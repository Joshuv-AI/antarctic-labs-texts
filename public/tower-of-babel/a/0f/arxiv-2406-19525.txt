An Energy Stable Incompressible Multi-Phase Flow Formulation
Jan Nordström, Arnaud.G. Malan

Abstract
We show that a reformulation of the governing equations for incompressible multi-phase flow in the volume of fluid setting leads to a well defined energy rate. New nonlinear inflow-outflow and solid wall boundary conditions bound the energy rate and lead to an energy estimate in terms of only external data. The new formulation combine perfectly with summation-by-parts operators and leads to provable energy stability.

Introduction

Initial boundary value problems (IBVPs) for nonlinear flow problems including boundary conditions are notoriously difficult to bound.
We have previously

,

,

,

]

such that energy estimates
were obtained.
We also discretized the new formulations and arrived at provably stable nonlinear schemes

In this note we provide a theoretical background for energy stability of the IBVP for incompressible multi-phase liquid-gas flows in the volume-of-fluid (VOF) formulation

The VOF formulation is applicable to complex interface motions, it is mass conservative and tracks the interface

[
9
,
10
]
results.

In
[

,

,
14
]
, the original equations are similar, but the dependent variables and bounds differ and most importantly: boundary conditions are essentially ignored. Here we reformulate the one-fluid VOF equations into a new set of skew-symmetric equations and derive new boundary conditions that lead to an energy bound. By discretizing using summation-by-parts (SBP) operators, energy stability follows.

2
The reformulation

We consider an incompressible viscous liquid (
l
) and gas (
g
) mixture in two dimensions (2D)
(with trivial extension to 3D). Using Einstein’s summation convention, the classical one-fluid VOF formulation
[
6
]
reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\alpha+u_{j}\partial_{j}\alpha

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{i}+u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}\tau_{ij}/\rho,\quad i=1,2

(2.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{j}u_{j}

\displaystyle=0.

In (
2
),
\alpha
is the volume fraction of the liquid,
u_{j}
is velocity in direction
x_{j}
,
\tau_{ij}=\tau_{ij}^{*}-\delta_{ij}p
is the stress tensor,
\tau_{ij}^{*}=\left[\mu\left(u_{i,j}+u_{j,i}\right)\right]
is the viscous stress tensor and
p
is pressure. Furthermore,
\rho=\alpha\rho_{l}+(1-\alpha)\rho_{g}
and
\mu=\alpha\mu_{l}+(1-\alpha)\mu_{g}
are the volume-averaged density and viscosity, respectively. The derivatives are denoted
\partial_{t}\psi=\psi_{,t}=\partial\psi/\partial t
and
\partial_{j}\psi=\psi_{,j}=\partial\psi/\partial x_{j}
, We neglected the external gravity forces which have no impact on stability. To get at an energy bound, the formulation (
2
) will be modified in two steps.

In the first step we replace the volume fraction by the density as dependent variable
and move the divergence relation to the righthand side leading to (
S_{ij}=\tau_{ij}
and
S_{3j}=-u_{j}
) the equivalent formulation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+u_{j}\partial_{j}\rho

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u_{i}+u_{j}\partial_{j}u_{i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}S_{ij}/\rho,\quad i=1,2

(2.2)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}S_{3j}.

In the second step we aim for a scaling of the viscous terms and introduce the new variables
[\phi_{0},\phi_{j},\phi_{3}]^{T}=[\sqrt{\rho},\sqrt{\rho}u_{j},p]
(see
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
, for similar but not identical choices) into (
2
) to yield the new equation set

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{0}+u_{j}\partial_{j}\phi_{0}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi_{i}+u_{j}\partial_{j}\phi_{i}

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}S_{ij}/\phi_{0},\quad i=1,2

(2.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{j}S_{3j}.

Introducing
\Phi=[\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3}]^{T}
,
\tilde{I}=diag[1,1,1,0]
,
\tilde{A}_{j}=diag[u_{j},u_{j},u_{j},0]
,
\tilde{B}=diag[0,1/\phi_{0},1/\phi_{0},1]
,
{S_{j}}=(0,S_{1j},S_{2j},S_{3j})^{T}
and noting that
\tilde{A}_{j,j}
vanish, we cast (
2
) in the final matrix-vector form

\tilde{I}\partial_{t}\Phi+\frac{1}{2}\Big[\partial_{j}(\tilde{A}_{j}\Phi)+\tilde{A}_{j}\partial_{j}\Phi\Big]=\tilde{B}\partial_{j}S_{j}.

(2.4)

Remark 2.1
.

The first step leading to (
2
) produces the skew-symmetric lefthand side of (
2.4
) suitable for Green’s theorem. The second step leading to (
2
) produces a scaling of the righthand side in (
2.4
) again opening up for Green’s theorem. Reformulating (
2
) into (
2.4
) simplifies the upcoming energy analysis.

3
Energy analysis

Focusing first on the skew-symmetric lefthand side of (
2.4
), we multiply with
\Phi^{T}
and integrate to get

\int_{\Omega}\Phi^{T}\tilde{I}\partial_{t}\Phi d\Omega+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\Big[\Phi^{T}\partial_{j}(\tilde{A}_{j}\Phi)+\Phi^{T}\tilde{A}_{j}\partial_{j}\Phi\Big]d\Omega=\int_{\Omega}\Phi^{T}\tilde{B}\partial_{j}S_{j}d\Omega.

(3.1)

Letting
\int_{\Omega}\Phi^{T}\tilde{I}\Phi d\Omega=||\Phi||_{\tilde{I}}^{2}
and
\Phi^{T}\partial_{j}(\tilde{A}_{j}\Phi)=\partial_{j}(\Phi^{T}\tilde{A}_{j}\Phi)-(\partial_{j}\Phi)^{T}\tilde{A}_{j}\Phi
followed by Green’s theorem yields

\frac{d}{dt}||\Phi||_{\tilde{I}}^{2}+\oint\limits_{\partial\Omega}\Phi^{T}\left(n_{j}\tilde{A}_{j}\right)\Phi ds=2\int_{\Omega}\Phi^{T}\tilde{B}\partial_{j}S_{j}d\Omega.

(3.2)

In (
3.2
),
\partial\Omega
,
ds
and
n_{j}
are respectively the boundary, its surface element and outward pointing unit normal.

Focusing secondly on the rescaled righthand-side of (
2.4
), we see that the righthand side of (
3.2
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Phi^{T}\tilde{B}\partial_{j}S_{j}d\Omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[\phi_{i}\partial_{j}S_{ij}/\phi_{0}+\phi_{3}\partial_{j}S_{3j}\Big]d\Omega=\int_{\Omega}\Big[u_{i}\tau_{ij,j}-pu_{i,i}\Big]d\Omega=\int_{\Omega}\Big[(u_{i}\tau_{ij})_{,j}-u_{i,j}\tau_{ij}-pu_{i,i}\Big]d\Omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[(u_{i}\tau_{ij})_{,j}-u_{i,j}\left(\tau^{*}_{ij}-\delta_{i,j}p\right)-pu_{i,i}\Big]d\Omega=\oint\limits_{\partial\Omega}u_{i}\tau_{ij}n_{j}ds-\int_{\Omega}u_{i,j}\tau^{*}_{ij}d\Omega,

(3.3)

where we again used Green’s theorem.
Inserting (
3
) into (
3.2
) and rearranging leads to the energy rate

\frac{d}{dt}||\Phi||_{\tilde{I}}^{2}+2\int_{\Omega}u_{i,j}\tau^{*}_{ij}d\Omega+\oint\limits_{\partial\Omega}\mathbb{BT}ds=0,

(3.4)

where the
2^{nd}
term (
u_{i,j}\tau^{*}_{ij}=2u_{i,i}^{2}+(u_{1,2}+u_{2,1})^{2}\geq 0
) provides dissipation and the boundary term
\mathbb{BT}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{BT}=\Phi_{1}^{T}A\Phi_{1}-2\left[u_{n}(\tau_{n}-p)+u_{t}\tau_{t}\right].

(3.5)

In (
3.5
),
A=diag[u_{n},u_{n},u_{n}]
and
\Phi_{1}^{T}=[\phi_{0},\phi_{n},\phi_{t}]=[\phi_{0},\phi_{0}u_{n},\phi_{0}u_{t}]
.
The subscripts
n,t
denote components normal and tangential to
\partial\Omega
.
For the stress term in (
3.5
) we use
{\bf{\tau}}^{T}=[\tau_{1},\tau_{2}]=[\tau^{*}_{1j}n_{j},\tau^{*}_{2j}n_{j}]
and the rotation matrix
N
(given in (
3.20
below) to obtain

{\bf{u}}^{T}{\bf{\tau}}={\bf{u}}^{T}\left(N^{T}N\right){\bf{\tau}}=(N{\bf{u}})^{T}\left(N{\bf{\tau}}\right)=[u_{n},u_{t}]\cdot[\tau_{n},\tau_{t}]^{T}
.

3.1
The boundary conditions

The unrestrained boundary term in (
3.5
) can be written in vector-matrix-vector form as

\mathbb{BT}=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
B^{T}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{where}\quad\Phi_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}p\\
\tau_{n}\\
\tau_{t}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad B=\frac{1}{\phi_{0}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
1&-1&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}

(3.6)

while
\Phi_{1}
and
A
are given above.
Next, we introduce the non-singular block rotation matrix
\mathbb{R}
as

\mathbb{R}\mathbb{R}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}I&R\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}I&-R\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix},

(3.7)

where the blocks are
3\times 3
matrices. The rotation matrix (
3.7
) introduced into (
3.6
) yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{BT}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{T}\mathbb{R}\mathbb{R}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
B^{T}&0\end{pmatrix}\mathbb{R}\mathbb{R}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbb{R}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right)^{T}\begin{pmatrix}A&AR+B\\
R^{T}A+B^{T}&R^{T}(AR+B)+B^{T}R\end{pmatrix}\left(\mathbb{R}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\Phi_{1}\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right).

(3.8)

With matrix
A
being non-singular, we cancel the off-diagonal matrices in (
3.1
) with
R=-A^{-1}B
to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{BT}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}A^{-1}[A\Phi_{1}+B\Phi_{2}]\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}A&0\\
0&-B^{T}A^{-1}B\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A^{-1}[A\Phi_{1}+B\Phi_{2}]\\
\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}A\Phi_{1}+B\Phi_{2}\\
B\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}I/u_{n}&0\\
0&-I/u_{n}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}A\Phi_{1}+B\Phi_{2}\\
B\Phi_{2}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}W_{1}\\
W_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda_{1}&0\\
0&\Lambda_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}W_{1}\\
W_{2}\end{pmatrix},\ \text{where}

(3.9)

\Lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{u_{n}}I,\Lambda_{2}=-\Lambda_{1},W_{1}=A\Phi_{1}+B\Phi_{2}=\frac{1}{\phi_{0}}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{n}\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{n}^{2}-(\tau_{n}-\phi_{3})\\
\phi_{n}\phi_{t}-\tau_{t}\end{pmatrix},W_{2}=B\Phi_{2}=\frac{1}{\phi_{0}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-(\tau_{n}-\phi_{3})\\
-\tau_{t}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.10)

The details in (
3.1
) and (
3.10
) reveal that five independent variables are involved in the boundary conditions.

) with standard eigenvalue techniques leads in general to very complex eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and hence complicated non-physical boundary conditions

]

3.1.1
Boundary conditions of inflow-outflow type with nonzero external data

We start by determining the number of boundary conditions
[
16
]
required at inflow-outflow boundaries.

1.

At inflow
u_{n}<0
,
W_{1}^{T}\Lambda_{1}W_{1}<0
and we need three conditions since
W_{1}
has three components.

2.

At outflow
u_{n}>0
,
W_{2}^{T}\Lambda_{2}W_{2}<0
and we need two conditions since
W_{2}
has two nonzero components.

Following
[
5
]
, the boundary conditions are applied weakly by inserting (
3.1
) and a lifting operator into (
3.4
):

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}||\Phi||_{\tilde{I}}^{2}+

\displaystyle 2\int_{\Omega}u_{i,j}\tau^{*}_{ij}d{\Omega}+\oint\limits_{\partial\Omega}\begin{pmatrix}W_{1}\\
W_{2}\end{pmatrix}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}\Lambda_{1}&0\\
0&\Lambda_{2}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}W_{1}\\
W_{2}\end{pmatrix}ds+2\int_{\Omega}\Phi^{T}L(\Sigma\mathbb{BC})d{\Omega}=0.

(3.11)

Here
\mathbb{BC}
denotes the boundary conditions,
\Sigma
is a penalty matrix and
L
is a lifting operator implementing boundary conditions weakly. It is defined by
\int_{\Omega}\psi^{T}L(\phi)d{\Omega}=\oint\limits_{\partial\Omega}\psi^{T}\phi ds
, where
\psi
and
\phi
are smooth vectors.

We will apply boundary conditions such that the boundary terms in (
3.11
) are bounded by external data only.
Following
[
3
]
, we consider the
nonlinear characteristic-like boundary condition on weak form

\mathbb{BC}=\sqrt{|\Lambda^{-}|}W^{-}-{\bf{G}}=0,

(3.12)

where
\Lambda^{-}
and
W^{-}
are functions of the solution and
\bf{G}
is external data.

To implement inflow boundary conditions weakly where
u_{n}<0
, we need an operator
T_{1}
so that

W^{-}=W_{1}=A\Phi_{1}+B\Phi_{2}=\frac{1}{\phi_{0}}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{n}\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{n}^{2}-(\tau_{n}-\phi_{3})\\
\phi_{n}\phi_{t}-\tau_{t}\end{pmatrix}=T_{1}\Phi=(\Phi^{T}T_{1}^{T})^{T}.

(3.13)

At an outflow boundary where
u_{n}>0
we require an operator
T_{2}
so that

W^{-}=W_{2}=\\
B\Phi_{2}=\frac{1}{\phi_{0}}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-(\tau_{n}-\phi_{3})\\
-\tau_{t}\end{pmatrix}=T_{2}\Phi=(\Phi^{T}T_{2}^{T})^{T}.

(3.14)

Implementing the boundary condition (
3.12
) weakly into (
3.11
) yield the augmented boundary term:

\displaystyle\mathbb{BT}

\displaystyle=W^{+^{T}}\Lambda^{+}W^{-}-W^{-^{T}}|\Lambda^{-}|W^{-}+2\Phi^{T}\Sigma\left(\sqrt{|\Lambda^{-}|}W^{-}-{\bf{G}}\right).

(3.15)

Next we choose
\Sigma=T^{T}\sigma_{0}\sqrt{|\Lambda^{-}|}
, where
T=T_{1}
or
T_{2}
depending on the signs of
\Lambda_{1,2}
, which gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{BT}

\displaystyle=W^{+^{T}}\Lambda^{+}W^{-}-(1-2\sigma_{0})W^{-^{T}}|\Lambda^{-}|W^{-}-2\sigma_{0}W^{-^{T}}\sqrt{|\Lambda^{-}|}{\bf{G}}.

(3.16)

Choosing
\sigma_{0}=1
as well as adding and subtracting
{\bf{G}}^{T}
leads to an estimate in terms of data only since

\displaystyle\mathbb{BT}=W^{+^{T}}\Lambda^{+}W^{-}+\left(W^{-}\sqrt{|\Lambda^{-}|}-\bf{G}\right)^{T}\left(W^{-}\sqrt{|\Lambda^{-}|}-\bf{G}\right)-{\bf{G}}^{T}{\bf{G}}\geq-{\bf{G}}^{T}{\bf{G}}.

(3.17)

Remark 3.2
.

The term related to
W^{+}
in (
3.15
) is positive, dissipative and requires no modification.

Remark 3.3
.

IBVPs with estimates in terms of only external data are cited as strongly energy bounded
[
17
]
.

3.1.2
Boundary conditions of solid wall type with zero external data

At solid wall boundaries, where
u_{n}=0
we work directly on the
\mathbb{BT}
in (
3.6
) which we group as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{BT}=\left[\Phi_{1}^{T}A\Phi_{1}+2\Phi^{T}\Sigma_{1}\mathbb{BC}_{1}\right]+\left[2\Phi_{1}^{T}B\Phi_{2}+2\Phi^{T}\Sigma_{2}\mathbb{BC}_{2}\right].

(3.18)

We will remove the terms within brackets on the righthand-side of (
3.18
) by appropriately selecting the penalty terms
\Sigma_{1}\mathbb{BC}_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}\mathbb{BC}_{2}
. Only the two boundary conditions
u_{n}=u_{t}=0
are available at a solid wall. For consistency reasons they must cancel both bracketed terms in (
3.18
) when imposed weakly.

For the first term in brackets in (
3.18
), we set
\Sigma_{1}=R_{1}^{T}\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}
with
R_{1}\Phi=\Phi_{1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}^{T}A\Phi_{1}+2\Phi^{T}\Sigma_{1}\mathbb{BC}_{1}=\Phi_{1}^{T}A\Phi_{1}+2\Phi_{1}^{T}\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}\mathbb{BC}_{1},

(3.19)

where

R_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0&0\\
0&n_{1}&n_{2}&0\\
0&-n_{2}&n_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{pmatrix}n_{1}&n_{2}\\
-n_{2}&n_{1}\end{pmatrix}=N

(3.20)

is the rotation matrix used in (
3.5
) above. Next, we insert
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}=\sigma_{1}
and
\mathbb{BC}_{1}=A\Phi_{1}
into (
3.19
) to give

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}^{T}A\Phi_{1}+2\Phi_{1}^{T}\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}\mathbb{BC}_{1}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{1}^{T}A\Phi_{1}+2\Phi_{1}^{T}\left(\sigma_{1}A\Phi_{1}\right)=\Phi_{1}^{T}A\Phi_{1}\left[1+2\sigma_{1}\right].

(3.21)

Equating
\sigma_{1}=-1/2
yields stability. Consistency is proven by noting that
u_{n}
is forced to zero by
\Sigma_{1}\mathbb{BC}_{1}
,
since

\Sigma_{1}\mathbb{BC}_{1}=\sigma_{1}R_{1}^{T}A\Phi_{1}=\sigma_{1}R_{1}^{T}\Phi_{1}u_{n}.

For the second term in brackets in (
3.18
), we set
\Sigma_{2}=R_{2}^{T}\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}
with
R_{2}\Phi=\Phi_{2}
to obtain

\displaystyle 2\Phi_{1}^{T}B\Phi_{2}+2\Phi^{T}\Sigma_{2}\mathbb{BC}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(B\Phi_{2}\right)^{T}\Phi_{1}+2\Phi^{T}_{2}\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}\mathbb{BC}_{2},

(3.22)

where
R_{2}=P_{e}T_{r}S_{c}
,
S_{c}=diag[1,1/\phi_{0},1/\phi_{0},1]
and

P_{e}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},~T_{r}=\begin{pmatrix}0&T_{r_{11}}&T_{r_{11}}&0\\
0&T_{r_{21}}&T_{r_{22}}&0\\
0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},~T_{r}=\mu N\left[\begin{pmatrix}2n_{1}&n_{2}\\
0&n_{1}\end{pmatrix}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+\begin{pmatrix}n_{2}&0\\
n_{1}&2n_{2}\end{pmatrix}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right].

(3.23)

Setting
\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}=\sigma_{2}
and
\mathbb{BC}_{2}=B^{T}\Phi_{1}
into (
3.22
) leads to,

\displaystyle 2\left(B\Phi_{2}\right)^{T}\Phi_{1}+2\Phi^{T}_{2}\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}\mathbb{BC}_{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(B\Phi_{2}\right)^{T}\Phi_{1}+2\sigma_{2}\left(B\Phi_{2}\right)^{T}\Phi_{1}=2(1+\sigma_{2})\left(B\Phi_{2}\right)^{T}\Phi_{1}.

(3.24)

Equating
\sigma_{2}=-1
yields stability. Consistency is proven by noting that
\Sigma_{2}\mathbb{BC}_{2}
forces
u_{n}
and
u_{t}
to zero, since

\Sigma_{2}\mathbb{BC}_{2}=R_{2}^{T}\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}\mathbb{BC}_{2}=\sigma_{2}R_{2}^{T}B^{T}\Phi_{1}=\sigma_{2}R_{2}^{T}B^{T}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{0}\\
\phi_{n}\\
\phi_{t}\end{pmatrix}=\sigma_{2}R_{2}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}u_{n}\\
-u_{n}\\
-u_{t}\end{pmatrix}.

Remark 3.4
.

The new inflow-outflow and solid wall boundary conditions leads to a strongly energy bounded VOF formulation with bounds on density, velocities and volume fraction in terms of external data only.

4
A stable straightforward nonlinear numerical approximation

The focus in this short note is on the continuous analysis, but for clarity we now sketch how the continuous formulation can be mimicked discretely, leading to
a stable scheme.
The semi-discrete version of (
2.4
) (ignoring boundary conditions) using summation-by-parts (SBP) operators
{\bf D_{x_{j}}}

[
5
,
18
,
19
]
can be written

{\bf\tilde{I}}\dfrac{d\vec{\Phi}}{dt}+\frac{1}{2}\Big[{\bf D_{x_{j}}}({\bf A_{j}}\vec{\Phi})+{\bf A_{j}}{\bf D_{x_{j}}}\vec{\Phi}\Big]={\bf B}{\bf D_{x_{j}}}{\bf S_{j}}.

(4.1)

In (
4.1
),
{\bf\tilde{I}}=\tilde{I}\otimes I_{x}\otimes I_{y}
where
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product, the vector
\vec{\Phi}=(\vec{\phi}_{0}^{T},\vec{\phi}_{1}^{T},\vec{\phi}_{2}^{T},\vec{\phi}_{3}^{T})^{T}
approximates
\Phi=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})^{T}
and the vector
{\bf S_{j}}=(0,\sec S_{1j}^{T},\vec{S}_{2j}^{T},\vec{S}_{3j}^{T})^{T}
approximates
{S_{j}}=(0,S_{1j},S_{2j},S_{3j})^{T}
in each node.
The matrix elements of
{\bf A_{j}}
and
{\bf B}
are matrices with node values of the matrix elements in
\tilde{A}_{j}
and
\tilde{B}
injected on the diagonal as exemplified below in matrix
{\bf C}

C=\begin{pmatrix}c_{11}&\ldots&c_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
c_{n1}&\ldots&c_{nn}\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf C}=\begin{pmatrix}{\bf c_{11}}&\ldots&{\bf c_{1n}}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
{\bf c_{n1}}&\ldots&{\bf c_{nn}}\end{pmatrix},\quad{\bf c_{ij}}=diag(c_{ij}(x_{1},y_{1}),\ldots,c_{ij}(x_{N},y_{M})).

(4.2)

Moreover,
{\bf D_{x_{j}}}=I_{4}\otimes\mathbb{D}_{x_{j}}
,
\mathbb{D}_{x_{1}}=D_{x}\otimes I_{y}
and
\mathbb{D}_{x_{2}}=I_{x}\otimes D_{y}
where

D_{x,y}
are 1D SBP difference operators.
All matrices have appropriate sizes such that the matrix-matrix and matrix-vector operations are defined.

The discrete energy method (multiply (
4.1
) from the left with
2\vec{\Phi}^{T}{\bf P}
) yields

2\vec{\Phi}^{T}{\bf P}\tilde{\bf I}\dfrac{d\vec{\Phi}}{dt}+\vec{\Phi}^{T}({\bf P}{\bf D_{x_{j}}}{\bf A_{j}}+{\bf A_{j}}{\bf P}{\bf D_{x_{j}}})\vec{\Phi}=2\vec{\Phi}^{T}{\bf B}{\bf P}{\bf D_{x_{j}}}{\bf S_{j}},

(4.3)

since
{\bf A_{j}}
and
{\bf B}
commute with the diagonal symmetric positive definite integration operator
{\bf P}=I_{4}\otimes\mathbb{P}
. Using the notation
\|\vec{\Phi}\|_{\tilde{\bf P}}^{2}=\vec{\Phi}^{T}{\bf P}\tilde{\bf I}\vec{\Phi}
, noting that only the symmetric part of
{\bf P}{\bf D_{x_{j}}}{\bf A_{j}}+{\bf A_{j}}{\bf P}{\bf D_{x_{j}}}
remains, and after applying the SBP properties (see
[
5
,
18
,
19
]
for details) we obtain the semi-discrete energy rate

\dfrac{d}{dt}\|\vec{\Phi}\|_{\tilde{\bf P}}^{2}+2(\mathbb{D}_{x_{j}}\vec{u}_{i})^{T}\mathbb{P}\vec{\mathbb{\tau^{*}}_{ij}}+\vec{\Phi}^{T}({\bf B_{x_{j}}}{\bf A_{j}})\vec{\Phi}-2\vec{u}_{i}^{T}\mathbb{B}_{x_{j}}\vec{\mathbb{\tau}_{ij}}=0.

(4.4)

The semi-discrete energy rate (
4.4
) mimicks the continuous energy rate (
3.4
) perfectly on a rectangular domain. The second term from the left in (
4.4
) is the dissipation numerically integrated over the domain (using the volume integrator
\mathbb{P}
). The next two terms, contain the boundary terms numerically integrated along the boundary (using the boundary integrators
{\bf B_{x_{j}}}=I_{4}\otimes\mathbb{B}_{x_{j}}
and
\mathbb{B}_{x_{j}}
).

5
Summary and outlook

A new formulation of the incompressible multi-phase liguid-gas flow equations that leads to a well defined energy rate was derived. It was complemented with new weak boundary conditions of inflow-outflow and solid wall types that lead to a unique result for nonlinear VOF formulations: energy bounds of density, velocities and volume fraction in terms of external data only.
The paper was concluded with a short illustration of how to construct a semi-discrete energy stable scheme by combining the new formulation with summation-by-parts operators. In future work we will develop nonlinear strongly energy stable schemes based on this new provably strongly energy bounded continuous formulation.

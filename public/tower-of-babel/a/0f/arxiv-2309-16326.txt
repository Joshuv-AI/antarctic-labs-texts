Numerical schemes for a multi-species quantum BGK model Thanks: Received date, and accepted date (The correct dates will be entered by the editor).
Gi-Chan Bae, Marlies Pirner, Sandra Warnecke

Abstract
This work is devoted to the numerical implementation of the quantum Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK) model for gas mixtures consisting of classical and quantum particles (fermions, bosons). We consider the model proposed by Bae, Klingenberg, Pirner, and Yun in 2021 and implement an Implicit-Explicit (IMEX) scheme due to the stiffness of the collision operator. A major obstacle is updating the parameters of quantum local equilibrium, which requires computing by inverting the relation between density and energy at every grid point in space and time. We address this difficulty by using the Lagrange multiplier method to minimize a potential function subject to constraints defined by specific moment equalities. Moreover, we analyze the convergence of mean velocity and temperature between the species both analytically and numerically. When a quantum component is included, we observe that the converging quantity is physical temperature, not the kinetic temperature. This differs from the mixture of classical species.

Introduction

In a kinetic description, the state of a dilute gas or plasma is given by a distribution function that prescribes the density of particles at each point in position-momentum phase space. The evolution of this distribution function is governed by the quantum Boltzmann equation which describes a balance of particle advection and binary collisions. The Boltzmann collision operator representing the interaction between particles preserves collision invariants (mass, momentum, and energy) and dissipates the mathematical entropy of the system. Unfortunately, the expense of evaluating this operator can be prohibitive. Indeed, its evaluation requires the calculation of a five-dimensional integral at every point in phase-space. Thus even with fast spectral methods
[

[

and electrons on energy band structure in semiconductor
[
23

In the classical case, the Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK) operator is a widely used surrogate for the Boltzmann operator, modeling collisions through a simple relaxation mechanism. This simplification provides significant computational advantages while maintaining the conservation and entropy dissipation properties of the Boltzmann operator. Similarly, quantum BGK models are widely used in place of the quantum Boltzmann equation

The multi-species quantum BGK model

We consider two distribution functions
f_{1}=f_{1}(x,p,t)\geq 0
and
f_{2}=f_{2}(x,p,t)\geq 0
for species with masses
m_{1}>0
and
m_{2}>0
, respectively, with the phase space variables (position and momentum)
x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and time
t\geq 0
. To be as general as possible, we generalize the quantum mixture BGK model in
[
6
]
describing a mixture of bosons and fermions to the mixture of gases including the interaction of quantum-classical particles:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{t}f_{1}+\frac{p}{m_{1}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{1}=\nu_{11}n_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{11}-f_{1})+\nu_{12}n_{2}(\mathcal{K}_{12}-f_{1}),\\
\partial_{t}f_{2}+\frac{p}{m_{2}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{2}=\nu_{22}n_{2}(\mathcal{K}_{22}-f_{2})+\nu_{21}n_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{21}-f_{2}),\end{split}

(2.2)

where
\mathcal{K}_{ij}
is the local equilibrium describing the interactions of
i
-th and
j
-th component and
\nu_{ij}n_{j}
for
i,j=1,2
are the collision frequencies. In the following, we always assume
\nu_{12}n_{2}=\nu_{21}n_{1}
as it is also done for example in
[
24
]
. More explicitly, the local equilibria read for fermion
\tau=+1
, for boson
\tau=-1
, and for classical particle
\tau=0

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{11}=\frac{1}{e^{m_{1}a_{1}\big|\frac{p}{m_{1}}-b_{1}\big|^{2}+c_{1}}+\tau},\quad\mathcal{K}_{12}=\frac{1}{e^{m_{1}a\big|\frac{p}{m_{1}}-b\big|^{2}+c_{12}}+\tau},\cr\mathcal{K}_{22}=\frac{1}{e^{m_{2}a_{2}\big|\frac{p}{m_{2}}-b_{2}\big|^{2}+c_{2}}+\tau^{\prime}},\quad\mathcal{K}_{21}=\frac{1}{e^{m_{2}a\big|\frac{p}{m_{2}}-b\big|^{2}+c_{21}}+\tau^{\prime}}.\end{split}

(2.3)

We denote
\mathcal{K}
as Fermi-Dirac distribution, Bose-Einstein distribution and Maxwellian for the case
\tau=+1,-1,0
, respectively. The equilibrium parameters
(a_{i},b_{i},c_{i})
and
(a,b,c_{12},c_{21})
will be determined uniquely in a way such that the conservation laws and the entropy principle are satisfied.
Note that the model includes the following cases depending on the types of the particles

\displaystyle(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(+1,+1)&\text{(fermion-fermion)}\cr(-1,-1)&\mbox{(boson-boson)}\cr(+1,-1)&\mbox{(fermion-boson)}\cr(+1,~0~)&\mbox{(fermion-classical)}\cr(~0,-1~)&\mbox{(classical-boson)}\cr(~0~,~0~)&\mbox{(classical-classical)}\end{array}\right.

We define the number density of particles
n_{i}
, momentum
P_{i}
, energy
E_{i}
of each species as

\displaystyle n_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}dp,\quad P_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}p~dp,\quad E_{i}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{i}\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{i}}dp,

(2.4)

and
m_{i}n_{i}=N_{i}
.
The parameters
(a_{i},b_{i},c_{i})
in
\mathcal{K}_{ii}
and
(a,b,c_{12},c_{21})
of
\mathcal{K}_{ij}
for
(ij)=(12),(21)

can be chosen to satisfy conservation of mass, momentum and energy in the intra-species interactions

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}_{ii}dp=n_{i},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}_{ii}p~dp=P_{i},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}_{ii}\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{i}}dp=E_{i},\quad(i=1,2).

(2.5)

and conservation of mass, total momentum and total energy in the inter-species interactions

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}_{12}dp=n_{1},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}_{21}dp=n_{2},\cr&\nu_{12}n_{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}_{12}p~dp-P_{1}\right)+\nu_{21}n_{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}_{21}p~dp-P_{2}\right)=0,\cr&\nu_{12}n_{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}_{12}\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{1}}dp-E_{1}\right)+\nu_{21}n_{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\mathcal{K}_{21}\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{2}}dp-E_{2}\right)=0,\end{split}

(2.6)

see Theorem 2.1 in
[
6
]
for quantum-quantum mixture case with unit collision frequencies. The proof can be extended for any choice of mixture between classical particles, fermions and bosons for more general velocity independent collision frequencies in a straight-forward way. For later references, we restate that
c_{i}
is determined by inverting the following relation

\displaystyle\frac{\int\frac{1}{e^{|p|^{2}+c_{i}}+\tau}dp}{\left(\int\frac{|p|^{2}}{e^{|p|^{2}+c_{i}}+\tau}dp\right)^{3/5}}=\frac{n_{i}^{\frac{2}{5}}}{\left(\frac{2m_{i}E_{i}}{n_{i}}-\frac{|P_{i}|^{2}}{n_{i}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{3}{5}}},

(2.7)

and
a_{i}
,
b_{i}
determined by

\displaystyle a_{i}=\left({\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{e^{|p|^{2}+c_{i}}+\tau}dp}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}n_{i}^{-\frac{2}{3}},\qquad b_{i}=\frac{P_{i}}{n_{i}},

(2.8)

for
(i=1,2).
The quantities
a
and
b
are given by

\displaystyle a=\left(\frac{m_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|p|^{2}}{e^{|p|^{2}+c_{12}}+\tau}dp+m_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|p|^{2}}{e^{|p|^{2}+c_{21}}+\tau^{\prime}}dp}{2E_{1}+2E_{2}-\frac{|P_{1}+P_{2}|^{2}}{N_{1}+N_{2}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{5}},\quad b=\frac{P_{1}+P_{2}}{N_{1}+N_{2}},

(2.9)

and
c_{12}
and
c_{21}
are determined by a more complex implicit condition than (
2.7
), see Theorem 2.1 in
[
6
]
for the detailed expression.

Moreover,

can be proven to satisfy the following relationship

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\eta_{\tau}(c_{12})}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}m_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\eta_{\tau^{\prime}}(c_{21})}=\frac{n_{1}}{n_{2}},

(2.10)

In
[
6
]
, it is shown that the distribution function in the fermion case remains bounded from above by

for all times

if it holds true for
t=0
. The model also satisfies an H-Theorem for the entropy

\displaystyle H_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}(f_{1},f_{2})

\displaystyle=H_{\tau}(f_{1})+H_{\tau^{\prime}}(f_{2})

with

\displaystyle H_{\tau}(f)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\ln fdpdx\quad\mbox{for}\quad\tau=0\\
\int_{\Omega}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f\ln f+\tau^{-1}(1-\tau f)\ln(1-\tau f)dpdx\quad\mbox{for}\quad\tau=\pm 1\end{cases}

(
\tau=+1
for fermion,
\tau=-1
, for boson), see Theorem 2.1 in
[
6
]

For further purposes, we denote the integrand by

,
i.e.

\displaystyle h_{\tau}(z)=z\ln z+\tau^{-1}(1-\tau z)\ln(1-\tau z),

(2.11)

for

and

if
\tau=+1
. Eventually, we highlight that the equilibrium distributions

solve an entropy minimization problem with contraints which ensure

[
10
]

Convergence rate for the velocities and kinetic temperatures in the space-homogeneous case

Theorem 3.5
.

Assume
\nu_{12}n_{2}=\nu_{21}n_{1}
and
\nu_{12},\nu_{21}
independent of

2.2

\frac{P_{1}(t)}{N_{1}}-\frac{P_{2}(t)}{N_{2}}=e^{-\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu_{12}n_{2}N_{2}+\nu_{21}n_{1}N_{1}}}{N_{1}+N_{2}}t}\left(\frac{P_{1}(0)}{N_{1}}-\frac{P_{2}(0)}{N_{2}}\right).

Proof 3.6
.

We start with calculating

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{P_{1}}{N_{1}}\right)=\frac{1}{N_{1}}\partial_{t}P_{1}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu_{12}n_{2}}\frac{1}{N_{1}}(P_{12}-P_{1}).

The first equality holds true because

is constant in the space-homogeneous case (
3.16

from (
3.16

3.14
) for the exchange of momentum leads to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{P_{1}}{N_{1}}\right)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu_{12}n_{2}}\frac{N_{2}}{N_{1}+N_{2}}\left(\frac{P_{2}}{N_{2}}-\frac{P_{1}}{N_{1}}\right).

(3.19)

In a similar way, we can compute

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{P_{2}}{N_{2}}\right)={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu_{21}n_{1}}\frac{N_{1}}{N_{1}+N_{2}}\left(\frac{P_{1}}{N_{1}}-\frac{P_{2}}{N_{2}}\right).

Substracting both equations yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\frac{P_{1}}{N_{1}}-\frac{P_{2}}{N_{2}}\right)=-\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\nu_{12}n_{2}N_{2}+\nu_{21}n_{1}N_{1}}}{N_{1}+N_{2}}\left(\frac{P_{1}}{N_{1}}-\frac{P_{2}}{N_{2}}\right),

and it gives the result (

Using the relationship

\displaystyle b_{1}-b_{2}=e^{-\frac{\nu_{12}n_{1}N_{2}+\nu_{21}n_{2}N_{1}}{N_{1}+N_{2}}t}(b_{1}(0)-b_{2}(0)).

We continue with deriving the convergence rates of the quantities

4
Numerical scheme

4.1
Time discretization

Let
k,j=1,2
and
k\neq j
. We write (
2.2
) as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{k}+\mathcal{T}_{k}(f_{k})=\mathcal{R}_{k}(f_{k},f_{j})

(4.22)

with the combined relaxation operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(f_{k},f_{j})=\mathcal{R}_{kk}+\mathcal{R}_{kj}=\nu_{kk}n_{k}\left(\mathcal{K}_{kk}-f_{k}\right)+\nu_{kj}n_{j}\left(\mathcal{K}_{kj}-f_{k}\right)

and the transport operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}(f_{k})=\frac{p}{m_{k}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{k}.

In the following, for simplicity, we assume that the collision frequencies
\tilde{\nu}_{kk}:=\nu_{kk}n_{k}
and
\tilde{\nu}_{kj}:=\nu_{kj}n_{k}
are constant in
x
and
t
. But an extension to an
x
and
t
dependence of the collision frequency would also be possible. Large collision frequencies result in a stiff relaxation operator such that an implicit time discretization for the relaxation part is a convenient choice. We pursue implicit-explicit (IMEX) schemes where
\mathcal{R}_{k}
is treated implicitly and
\mathcal{T}_{i}
is treated explicitly.

Given
t_{\ell}=\ell\Delta t
for
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, a simple update of
f_{k}^{\ell}\approx f_{k}(x,p,t_{\ell})
from
t_{\ell}
to
t_{\ell+1}
uses the approximation

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(f_{k}^{\ell+1},f_{j}^{\ell+1})\approx\tilde{\nu}_{kk}\left(\mathcal{K}_{kk}^{\ell+1}-f_{k}^{\ell+1}\right)+\tilde{\nu}_{kj}\left(\mathcal{K}_{kj}^{\ell+1}-f_{k}^{\ell+1}\right),

where
\mathcal{K}_{kk}^{\ell+1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{kj}^{\ell+1}
are discrete target functions depending on
f_{k}^{\ell+1}
and
f_{j}^{\ell+1}
via the solution of a convex minimization problem that is inspired by the work in
[
19
]
. We discuss it in Section
4.1.3
. By this procedure,
\mathcal{K}_{kk}
and
\mathcal{K}_{kj}
are evaluated exactly at the next time step (up to numerical tolerances) which results in the preservation of conservation properties, and the first-order version inherits additional properties from the continuum model.

4.1.1
First-order splitting

We split the relaxation and transport operators in
(
4.22
)
.

Relaxation.

We perform the relaxation step in each spatial cell by a backward Euler method

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k}^{\ast}-f_{k}^{\ell}}{\Delta t}=\mathcal{R}_{k}(f_{k}^{\ast},f_{j}^{\ast}),

(4.23)

which can be rewritten into the convex combination

\displaystyle f_{k}^{\ast}=d_{k}f_{k}^{\ell}+d_{k}\Delta t(\tilde{\nu}_{kk}\mathcal{K}_{kk}^{\ast}+\tilde{\nu}_{kj}\mathcal{K}_{kj}^{\ast}),

(4.24)

with

\displaystyle d_{k}=\frac{1}{1+\Delta t(\tilde{\nu}_{kk}+\tilde{\nu}_{kj})}.

The equation (
4.24
) represents an explicit update formula for
f_{k}^{\ast}

provided that
\mathcal{K}_{kk}^{\ast}
and
\mathcal{K}_{kj}^{\ast}
can be expressed as functions of
f_{k}^{\ell}
. In Section
4.1.3
we show how to determine
\mathcal{K}_{kk}^{\ast}
and
\mathcal{K}_{kj}^{\ast}
in a structure-preserving way.

Transport.

We compute the transport in
x
for
f_{k}^{\ell+1}
by a forward Euler method with initial data
f_{k}^{\ast}
:

\displaystyle\frac{f_{k}^{\ell+1}-f_{k}^{\ast}}{\Delta t}+\mathcal{T}_{k}(f_{k}^{\ast})=0.

(4.25)

Details on the numerical approximation of
\mathcal{T}_{k}
are presented in section
4.2
.

4.1.2
Second-order IMEX Runge-Kutta

We use the following Butcher tableaux
[
2
]
for a second-order approach

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}0\\
\gamma&0&\gamma\\
1&0&1-\gamma&\gamma\\
\hline\cr&0&1-\gamma&\gamma\end{array}\hskip 56.9055pt\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}0\\
\gamma&\gamma\\
1&\delta&1-\delta&0\\
\hline\cr&\delta&1-\delta&0\end{array}

with

\gamma=1-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta=1-\frac{1}{2\gamma}.

The left table applies to the relaxation part, and the right table applies to the transport terms.
This IMEX Runge-Kutta scheme is L-stable and globally stiffly accurate.

Applying this method to
(
4.23
) and (
4.25
)
and using the constants

\displaystyle d_{k}=\frac{1}{1+\gamma\Delta t(\tilde{\nu}_{kk}+\tilde{\nu}_{kj})},

(4.26)

we can write the stages in the scheme as convex combination of three terms

\displaystyle f_{k}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=d_{k}G_{k}^{(1)}

\displaystyle+d_{k}\gamma\Delta t\,\tilde{\nu}_{kk}\mathcal{K}_{kk}^{(1)}

\displaystyle+d_{k}\gamma\Delta t\,\tilde{\nu}_{kj}\mathcal{K}_{kj}^{(1)}

(4.27a)

\displaystyle f_{k}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=d_{k}G_{k}^{(2)}

\displaystyle+d_{k}\gamma\Delta t\,\tilde{\nu}_{kk}\mathcal{K}_{kk}^{(2)}

\displaystyle+d_{k}\gamma\Delta t\,\tilde{\nu}_{kj}\mathcal{K}_{kj}^{(2)},

(4.27b)

\displaystyle f_{k}^{\ell+1}

\displaystyle=f_{k}^{(2)}

(4.27c)

where

\displaystyle G_{k}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=f_{k}^{\ell}-\Delta t\,\gamma\,\mathcal{T}_{k}(f_{k}^{\ell})

(4.28a)

\displaystyle G_{k}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=f_{k}^{\ell}-\Delta t\,\delta\,\mathcal{T}_{k}(f_{k}^{\ell})-\Delta t\,(1-\delta)\mathcal{T}_{k}(f_{k}^{(1)})+\Delta t\,(1-\gamma)\mathcal{R}_{k}(f_{k}^{(1)},f_{j}^{(1)})

(4.28b)

depend on known data. For each stage, we have to determine the corresponding values of the target functions in order to update the distribution functions. In the following section, we explain how this can be achieved.

General implicit solver

We write the implicit updates in (

The functions

In Section

in Appendix

Applying the conservation properties (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\hskip 7.11317pt\overset{(\ref{eq:update_general})}{=}\int\tilde{\nu}_{11}d_{1}\left[G_{1}+\Delta t\,\gamma\tilde{\nu}_{11}\mathcal{K}_{11}+\Delta t\,\gamma\tilde{\nu}_{12}\mathcal{K}_{12}\right]\,\mathbf{p}_{1}dp\\

4.2
Space discretization

We assume a slab geometry, i.e.
\partial_{x^{2}}f_{k}=\partial_{x^{3}}f_{k}=0
. So we reduce the physical space dimension to one dimension and set
x:=x^{1}
while the momentum domain remains three dimensional
(p=(p^{1},p^{2},p^{3}))
. We divide the spatial domain
[x_{\rm min},x_{\rm max}]
into uniform cells
I_{i}=[x_{i}-\frac{\Delta x}{2},x_{i}+\frac{\Delta x}{2}]
for
i\in\{0,\dots,I\}
.

We employ a second-order finite volume framework using approximate cell-averaged quantities

f_{k,i}^{\ell}\approx\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int_{I_{i}}f_{k}(x,p,t^{\ell})dx.

The relaxation operators are approximated to second order by

\mathcal{R}_{k,i}^{\ell}=\mathcal{R}_{k}(f_{k,i}^{\ell},f_{j,i}^{\ell})\approx\frac{1}{\Delta x}\int_{I_{i}}\mathcal{R}\left(\,f_{k}(x,p,t^{\ell}),f_{j}(x,p,t^{\ell})\,\right)dx.

Whereas the transport operator
\mathcal{T}_{k}
is discretized with numerical fluxes
\mathscr{F}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{k}\left(g\right)\approx\mathcal{T}_{i;k}(g)=\frac{1}{\Delta x}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathscr{F}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}(g)}-\mathscr{F}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}(g)\right)

for any grid function
g=\{g_{i}\}
. We follow
[
30
]
and use

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}(g)=\frac{p^{1}}{2m}\left(g_{i+1}+g_{i}\right)-\frac{|p^{1}|}{2m}\left(g_{i+1}-g_{i}-\phi_{i+\frac{1}{2}}(g)\right)

where
\phi_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
is a flux limiter. The choice

Momentum discretization

Eventually, we discretize the momentum variable. We center the discrete momenta

6.1
Extension to N-Species

The two-species model can be extended to a system of
N
-species that undergo binary interactions. For ease in notation, we illustrate here the 3-species case. Each distribution function
f_{k}
,
k=1,\dots,3
, represents the solution to

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f_{k}+\frac{p}{m_{k}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f_{k}=\tilde{\nu}_{k1}(\mathcal{K}_{k1}-f_{k})+\tilde{\nu}_{k2}(\mathcal{K}_{k2}-f_{k})+\tilde{\nu}_{k3}(\mathcal{K}_{k3}-f_{k})

with
\tilde{\nu}_{kj}=\nu_{kj}n_{j}
.
Since we still consider only binary interactions, the properties in section
2
are still satisfied.

The presented numerical scheme is based on the general implicit solver in Section
4.1.3
. Since the transport operators act only on the individual species, we focus on and shortly illustrate the scheme of the relaxation process.

As above, we write the implicit updates of the distribution functions in a generic steady state form

\displaystyle f_{k}=d_{k}G_{k}+d_{k}\gamma\Delta t(\tilde{\nu}_{kk}\mathcal{K}_{kk,\tau_{k}}+\tilde{\nu}_{kj}\mathcal{K}_{kj,\tau_{j}}+\tilde{\nu}_{kl}\mathcal{K}_{kl,\tau_{l}})

(6.51)

for
k,j,l\in\{1,2,3\}
, each of
k,j,l
distinct, where
\mathcal{K}_{kk,\tau_{k}}
,
\mathcal{K}_{kj,\tau_{j}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{kl,\tau_{l}}
are the unique attractors associated to
f_{k}
,

d_{k}=\frac{1}{1+\gamma\Delta t(\tilde{\nu}_{kk}+\tilde{\nu}_{kj}+\tilde{\nu}_{kl})},

and
G_{k}
is a known function.
When we can express
\mathcal{K}_{kk}
,
\mathcal{K}_{kj}
and
\mathcal{K}_{kl}
as functions of
G_{k}
,
G_{j}
and
G_{l}
, (
6.51
) provides an explicit update formula for
f_{k}
.

We apply the conservation properties to (
6.51
). An analogous calculation as in the 2-species case leads to a set of constraints to determine the attractors from the given data:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int d_{1}\left(\tilde{\nu}_{11}\mathcal{K}_{11,\tau_{1}}+\tilde{\nu}_{12}\mathcal{K}_{12,\tau_{1}}+\tilde{\nu}_{13}\mathcal{K}_{13,\tau_{1}}\right)\mathbf{p}_{1}dp\\
&+\int d_{2}\left(\tilde{\nu}_{21}\mathcal{K}_{21,\tau_{2}}+\tilde{\nu}_{22}\mathcal{K}_{22,\tau_{2}}+\tilde{\nu}_{23}\mathcal{K}_{23,\tau_{2}}\right)\mathbf{p}_{2}dp\\
+&\int d_{3}\left(\tilde{\nu}_{31}\mathcal{K}_{31,\tau_{3}}+\tilde{\nu}_{32}\mathcal{K}_{32,\tau_{3}}+\tilde{\nu}_{33}\mathcal{K}_{33,\tau_{3}}\right)\mathbf{p}_{3}dp\\
=&\int d_{1}\left(\tilde{\nu}_{11}+\tilde{\nu}_{12}+\tilde{\nu}_{13}\right)G_{1}\mathbf{p}_{1}dp+\int d_{2}\left(\tilde{\nu}_{21}+\tilde{\nu}_{22}+\tilde{\nu}_{23}\right)G_{2}\mathbf{p}_{2}dp\\
&+\int d_{3}\left(\tilde{\nu}_{31}+\tilde{\nu}_{32}+\tilde{\nu}_{33}\right)G_{3}\mathbf{p}_{3}dp.\end{split}

(6.52)

These constraints (
6.52
) represent first-order optimality conditions associated to the minimization of the convex function

\displaystyle\varphi_{\rm tot}(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{12},\alpha_{13},\alpha_{23})=\varphi_{1}(\alpha_{1})+\varphi_{2}(\alpha_{2})+\varphi_{3}(\alpha_{3})+\varphi(\alpha_{12})+\varphi(\alpha_{13})+\varphi(\alpha_{23})

with

\displaystyle\varphi_{k}(\alpha_{k})

\displaystyle=\int d_{k}\tilde{\nu}_{kk}w[\mathcal{K}_{kk,\tau_{k}}]dp+\mu_{kk}\cdot\alpha_{k}

and

\displaystyle\varphi(\alpha_{kj})

\displaystyle=\int\left(d_{k}\tilde{\nu}_{kj}w[\mathcal{K}_{kj,\tau_{k}}]+d_{j}\tilde{\nu}_{jk}w[\mathcal{K}_{jk,\tau_{j}}]\right)dp+\mu_{kj}\cdot\alpha_{kj},

where

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}w[\mathcal{K}_{kj,\tau_{k}}]=\begin{cases}-\mathcal{K}_{kj,\tau_{k}}\qquad&\text{for }\tau_{k}=0,\\
\log(1-\mathcal{K}_{kj,+1})\quad&\text{for }\tau_{k}=+1,\\
-\log(1+\mathcal{K}_{kj,-1})\quad&\text{for }\tau_{k}=-1.\end{cases}}

Moreover,
\alpha_{k}=(\alpha_{k}^{0},\alpha_{k}^{1},\alpha_{k}^{2})^{\top}
;

\displaystyle\mu_{kk}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{kk}^{0}\\
\mu_{kk}^{1}\\
\mu_{kk}^{2}\end{pmatrix}=\int d_{k}\tilde{\nu}_{kk}G_{k}\mathbf{p}_{k}dp

for
k=1,2,3
; for
k\neq j:

\alpha_{kj}=(\alpha_{kj}^{0},\alpha_{jk}^{0},\alpha_{kj}^{1},\alpha_{kj}^{2})^{\top}
; and

\displaystyle\mu_{kj}=\begin{pmatrix}\mu_{kj}^{0}\\
\mu_{jk}^{0}\\
\mu_{kj}^{1}\\
\mu_{kj}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\int\left[\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
p\\
\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{k}}\end{pmatrix}d_{k}\nu_{kj}G_{k}+\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\\
p\\
\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{j}}\end{pmatrix}d_{j}\nu_{jk}G_{j}\right]dp.

6.2
Entropy minimization problem of the mixture equilibria

In this section,
we prove that the local equilibria in (
2.3
) are the unique entropy minimizing solution. We note that we choose the same equilibrium parameter
(b,c)
in
\mathcal{K}_{12}
and
\mathcal{K}_{21}
. This is also reflected in the choice of the equilibrium parameters in our numerical scheme.

For this, it will be convenient to define the following notations. We denote

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{11}=(e^{-\alpha_{11}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{1}}+\tau)^{-1},\quad\mathcal{K}_{22}=(e^{-\alpha_{22}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{2}}+\tau^{\prime})^{-1},\\
\mathcal{K}_{12}=(e^{-\alpha_{12}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{1}}+\tau)^{-1},\quad\mathcal{K}_{12}=(e^{-\alpha_{21}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{2}}+\tau^{\prime})^{-1}\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{k}(p):=(1,p,\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{k}})^{\top},\quad k=1,2

and the parameters
(a_{1},a_{2},a,b_{1},b_{2},b,c_{1},c_{2},c_{12},c_{21})
can be mapped one-to-one to
\alpha_{kj}=({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\alpha^{0}_{kj}},\alpha^{1}_{kj},\alpha^{2}_{kj}),~k,j=1,2
. As the function

h_{\tau}
defined by (
2.11
) is convex, it follows

h_{\tau}(z)\geq h_{\tau}(y)+\ln(y)(z-y),

(6.53)

for all
y,z>0
if
\tau=0,-1
and
0<y,z<1
if
\tau=+1
.

For interactions between different species, we seek a solution of the entropy minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{g_{1},g_{2}\in\chi_{12}}\int h_{\tau}(g_{1})dp+\int h_{\tau^{\prime}}(g_{2})dp,

(6.54)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\chi_{12}=\Bigg\{(g_{1},g_{2})~\Big|&~g_{1},g_{2}>0,\,(1+|p|^{2})g_{1},\,(1+|p|^{2})g_{2}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\
&\int g_{1}dp=\int f_{1}dp,\quad\int g_{2}dp=\int f_{2}dp,\\
&\int\begin{pmatrix}p\\
\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{1}}\end{pmatrix}(g_{1}-f_{1})dp+\int\begin{pmatrix}p\\
\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{2}}\end{pmatrix}(g_{2}-f_{2})dp=0\Bigg\}.\end{split}

(6.55)

Here,
\chi_{12}
is chosen such that the constraints (
2.6
) for inter-species collisions are satisfied.
Similar to the case of intra-species collisions, we consider the Lagrange functional
L\colon\chi_{12}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\begin{split}L(g_{1},g_{2},\alpha_{12}^{0},\alpha_{21}^{0},\alpha^{1},\alpha^{2})&=\int h(g_{1})dp+\int h(g_{2})dp\\
&+\alpha_{12}^{0}\int(g_{1}-f_{1})dp+\alpha_{21}^{0}\int(g_{2}-f_{2})dp\\
&+\alpha^{1}\cdot\left(\int p(g_{1}-f_{1})dp+\int p(g_{2}-f_{2})dp\right)\\
&+\alpha^{2}\left(\int\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{1}}(g_{1}-f_{1})dp+\int\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{2}}(g_{2}-f_{2})dp\right).\\
\end{split}

Any critical point
(\mathcal{K}_{12},\mathcal{K}_{21},\lambda_{12}^{0},\lambda_{21}^{0},\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2})
of
L
satisfies the first-order optimality conditions

\displaystyle\frac{\delta L}{\delta g_{1}}(\mathcal{K}_{12},\mathcal{K}_{21},\lambda^{0}_{12},\lambda^{0}_{21},\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2})=\ln\frac{\mathcal{K}_{12}}{1-\tau\mathcal{K}_{12}}+\lambda_{12}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{1}(p)=0,

\displaystyle\frac{\delta L}{\delta g_{2}}(\mathcal{K}_{12},\mathcal{K}_{21},\lambda^{0}_{12},\lambda^{0}_{21},\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2})=\ln\frac{\mathcal{K}_{21}}{1-\tau\mathcal{K}_{21}}+\lambda_{21}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{2}(p)=0,

where
\lambda_{12}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\lambda^{0}_{12},\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2})}
and
\lambda_{21}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(\lambda^{0}_{21},\lambda^{1},\lambda^{2})}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{K}_{12}&=(\exp(\lambda_{12}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{1}(p))+\tau)^{-1},\cr\mathcal{K}_{21}&=(\exp(\lambda_{21}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{2}(p))+\tau^{\prime})^{-1}.\end{split}

(6.56)

We highlight again that we only require conservation of the combined momentum and kinetic energy so that there is only one Lagrange multiplier for the momentum constraint and one Lagrange multiplier for the energy constraint. Hence,
\lambda^{1}_{12}=\lambda^{1}_{21}
and
\lambda^{2}_{12}=\lambda^{2}_{21}
in (
6.56
). When we are in the classical case (
\tau=0
), this restriction is the same as the one used in
[
20
]
, but more restrictive than the model in
[
24
]
.

Theorem 2.1 in
[
6
]
shows the existence of functions of the form (
6.56
) which satisfy the constraints in (
2.5
) and (
2.6
). It follows that these functions are unique minimizer of the corresponding minimization problem.

Theorem 6.1
.

(\mathcal{K}_{12},\mathcal{K}_{21})
as defined in (
6.56
) is the unique minimizer of (
6.54
).

Proof 6.2
.

According to (
6.53
)

h_{\tau}(g)\geq h_{\tau_{k}}(\mathcal{K}_{kj})+\lambda_{kj}\cdot\mathbf{p}_{k}(g-\mathcal{K}_{kj}),

point-wise in
p
, for any measurable function
g
and
k,j\in\{1,2\}
.
Therefore it follows that for any measureable functions
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
,

\displaystyle\int h_{\tau}(g_{1})dp+\int

\displaystyle h_{\tau^{\prime}}(g_{2})dp\geq\int h_{\tau}(\mathcal{K}_{12})dp+\int h_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathcal{K}_{21})dp

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda_{12}\cdot\int\mathbf{p}_{1}(g_{1}-\mathcal{K}_{12})dp+\lambda_{21}\cdot\int\mathbf{p}_{2}(g_{2}-\mathcal{K}_{21})dp.

(6.57)

Since
\lambda^{1}_{12}=\lambda^{1}_{21}
and
\lambda^{2}_{12}=\lambda^{2}_{21}
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda_{12}\cdot\int\mathbf{p}_{1}&(g_{1}-\mathcal{K}_{12})dp+\lambda_{21}\cdot\int\mathbf{p}_{2}(g_{2}-\mathcal{K}_{21})dp=\lambda^{0}_{12}\int(g_{1}-\mathcal{K}_{12})dp+\lambda^{0}_{21}\int(g_{2}-\mathcal{K}_{21})dp\\
&\quad+\lambda^{1}_{12}\cdot\left(\int p(g_{1}-\mathcal{K}_{12})dp+\int p(g_{2}-\mathcal{K}_{21})dp\right)\\
&+\lambda^{2}_{12}\left(\int\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{1}}(g_{1}-\mathcal{K}_{12})dp+\int\frac{|p|^{2}}{2m_{2}}(g_{2}-\mathcal{K}_{21})dp\right).\end{split}

If
(g_{1},g_{2})
and
(\mathcal{K}_{12},\mathcal{K}_{21})
are elements of
\chi_{12}
, then the constraints in (
6.55
) imply that each of the terms above is zero.
In such cases, (
6.2
) reduces to

\displaystyle\int h_{\tau}(g_{1})dp+\int h_{\tau^{\prime}}(g_{2})dp\geq\int h_{\tau}(\mathcal{K}_{12})dp+\int h_{\tau^{\prime}}(\mathcal{K}_{21})dp,

which shows that
(\mathcal{K}_{12},\mathcal{K}_{21})
solves (
6.54
). Since
h_{\tau_{k}}
is strictly convex, it follows that this solution is unique.

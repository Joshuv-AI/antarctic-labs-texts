Abstract
. The Monster Lie algebra 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} is a quotient of the physical space of the vertex algebra V = V ♮ ⊗ V 1 , 1 V=V^{\natural}\otimes V_{1,1} , where V ♮ V^{\natural} is the Moonshine module vertex operator algebra of Frenkel, Lepowsky, and Meurman, and V 1 , 1 V_{1,1} is the vertex algebra corresponding to the rank 2 even unimodular lattice II 1 , 1 \textrm{II}_{1,1} . We construct vertex algebra elements that project to bases for subalgebras of 𝔪 \mathfrak{m} isomorphic to 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{gl}_{2} , corresponding to each imaginary simple root, denoted ( 1 , j ) (1,j) for j > 0 j>0 . Our method requires the existence of pairs of primary vectors in V ♮ V^{\natural} satisfying some natural conditions, which we prove. We show that the action of the Monster finite simple group 𝕄 \mathbb{M} on the subspace of primary vectors in V ♮ V^{\natural} induces an 𝕄 \mathbb{M} -action on the set of 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 2 \mathfrak{gl}_{2} subalgebras corresponding to a fixed imaginary simple root. We use the generating function for dimensions of subspaces of primary vectors of V ♮ V^{\natural} to prove that this action is non-trivial for small values of j j .

(22)

\displaystyle g\cdot h_{1}

\displaystyle={\bf 1}\otimes t_{1}(-1)\iota(1)+R=h_{1},

\displaystyle g\cdot h_{2}

\displaystyle={\bf 1}\otimes t_{2}(-1)\iota(1)+R=h_{2},

(23)

where in line (
23
) we have used that the action of
\mathbb{M}
on
V^{\natural}
is by vertex operator algebra automorphisms and vertex operator algebra automorphisms fix the vacuum vector (
[
FLM88
]
, (8.10.22)).
Since
P^{\natural}_{j+1}
is an
\mathbb{M}
-module (Corollary
5.8
), we have
g\cdot u
and
g\cdot v\in P_{j+1}^{\natural}.
Again using that the action of
\mathbb{M}
on
V^{\natural}
is by vertex operator algebra automorphisms,

(g\cdot u)_{2j+1}(g\cdot v)=g\cdot(u_{2j+1}v)=g\cdot{\bf 1}={\bf 1},

(
[
FLM88
]
, (8.10.20), (8.10.22)).
Thus,
g\cdot e_{j,u},

g\cdot f_{j,v}
,
g\cdot h_{1}
, and
g\cdot h_{2}
are of the form given in Theorem
6.2
and therefore generate
\mathfrak{gl}_{2}(j,g\cdot u,g\cdot v)\in\mathcal{G}_{j}
. ∎

Lemma 7.2
.

For fixed
j>0
and fixed

such that
\bar{c_{j}}=(1,j)
,

\displaystyle E_{j}

\displaystyle:=\left\{e_{j,u}=u\otimes\iota({c_{j}})+R\mid u\in P^{\natural}_{j+1}\right\}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{(1,j)}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle F_{j}

\displaystyle:=\left\{f_{j,u}=u\otimes\iota({c_{j}}^{-1})+R\mid u\in P^{\natural}_{j+1}\right\}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}_{(-1,-j)}

are
\mathbb{M}
-submodules of
\mathfrak{m}_{(1,j)}
and
\mathfrak{m}_{(-1,-j)}
, respectively.

Proof.

Given any
g\in\mathbb{M}
and
e_{j,u}=u\otimes\iota({c_{j}})+R\in E_{j}
we have

g\cdot e_{j,u}=g\cdot(u\otimes\iota({c_{j}})+R)=(g\cdot u)\otimes\iota(c_{j})+R.

By Corollary
5.8
,
P_{j+1}^{\natural}
is an
\mathbb{M}
-submodule of
V_{j+1}^{\natural}
, so
g\cdot u\in P_{j+1}^{\natural}
and hence
g\cdot e_{j,u}\in E_{j}
. Therefore,
E_{j}
is an
\mathbb{M}
-module. A similar proof shows that
F_{j}
is an
\mathbb{M}
-module.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

The
\mathbb{M}
-modules
P_{j+1}^{\natural}
,
E_{j}
, and
F_{j}
are isomorphic.

Proof.

We will prove that
E_{j}
is isomorphic to
P_{j+1}^{\natural}
. A similar proof shows that
F_{j}
is isomorphic to
P_{j+1}^{\natural}.

Let

\Psi:P_{j+1}^{\natural}\rightarrow E_{j}

be given by

\Psi(u)=u\otimes\iota(c_{j})+R.

If
u\in P_{j+1}^{\natural}
and
u\neq 0,
then Proposition
6.4
implies that
u\otimes\iota(c_{j})\notin R.
Therefore,
\Psi
is injective. The map
\Psi
is also clearly surjective. Furthermore, for all
g\in\mathbb{M}
and for all
u\in P_{j+1}^{\natural},

\Psi(g\cdot u)=(g\cdot u)\otimes\iota(c_{j})+R=g\cdot(u\otimes\iota(c_{j})+R)=g\cdot\Psi(u).

Therefore,
\Psi
is also an
\mathbb{M}
-module homomorphism and thus is an isomorphism.
∎

. Since

is a proper
\mathbb{M}
-submodule of

,

.
∎

Theorem 7.5
.

Suppose that

is such that

is strictly greater than the multiplicity of the trivial
\mathbb{M}
-module in the decomposition of

as a direct sum of irreducible
\mathbb{M}
-modules.

\mathbb{M}
on

By Theorem
7.3
,
E_{j}

are isomorphic as

, each

Appendix A
Generating series

In this section, we recall the generating series for the dimensions of the subspaces
P_{j}^{\natural}
of primary vectors of homogeneous weight
j
in
V^{\natural}
(Proposition
A.2
). We use this series to prove that given any nonnegative integer
j
not equal to
1
, there exist primary vectors of weight
j
in
V^{\natural}
(Theorem
A.3
).

Let
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
, where
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}
with Im
(\tau)>0
. Recall that the modular
J
-function can be expressed as

J(q)=\frac{(1+240\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{3}(k)q^{k})^{3}}{q\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{k})^{24}}-744,

(24)

where
\sigma_{3}(k):=\displaystyle\sum_{d|k}d^{3}
is the sum of cubes of the positive integers that divide
k
. Define

\eta(q)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{j}).

(25)

It will also be helpful to recall the generating series for the partitions of
n
,

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}p(j)q^{j}={\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{j})^{-1}},

(26)

where
p(j)
denotes the number of partitions of
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We recall the Euler identity,

Proposition A.1
.

q^{-\frac{1}{24}}\eta(q)=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{j})=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}(q^{\frac{j(3j+1)}{2}}+q^{\frac{j(3j-1)}{2}}).

The following result can be obtained using the results of
[
HL98
]
.
It is stated explicitly in
[
DH17
]
, with a minor typo. For the reader’s convenience, we include a proof here.

Proposition A.2
.

[
HL98
]
(see also
[
DH17
]
)
Let
P_{j}^{\natural}
be the subspace of weight
j
primary vectors in
V_{j}^{\natural}
. Then

\sum^{\infty}_{j=0}\dim P_{j}^{\natural}\,q^{j-1}=q^{-\frac{1}{24}}J(q)\eta(q)+1.

Proof.

We denote by
W_{1},W_{2},\cdots,W_{194}
the irreducible representations of
\mathbb{M}
, with corresponding irreducible characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\cdots,\chi_{194}.
Then, since
V^{\natural}
is an
\mathbb{M}
-module, we can write it as a sum of these irreducible modules. In fact, since the action of the Virasoro algebra commutes with the action of
\mathbb{M}
, we can decompose
V^{\natural}_{j}
, for each
j
, in the following way:

V_{j}^{\natural}=\bigoplus^{194}_{k=1}W_{k}^{\mathrm{mult}_{k}(j)},

(27)

where
\mathrm{mult}_{k}(j)
is the
multiplicity
of
W_{k}
in
V_{j}^{\natural}.
Following the notation in
[
HL98
]
, we write
s^{k}_{j}
for the number of linearly independent primary vectors in
V^{\natural,k}_{j}:=W_{k}^{\mathrm{mult}_{k}(j)}
,
where
j\geq 0
and
1\leq k\leq 194
.
For
j\not=1,
the dimension of
P_{j}^{\natural}
is the sum over
k
of the
s^{k}_{j}
:

s_{j}:=\dim P_{j}^{\natural}=\sum^{194}_{k=1}s^{k}_{j}.

Following
[
HL98
]
, we set
s^{1}_{1}=-1
for convenience. We define

G^{k}(q)=\sum_{j\geq 0}s^{k}_{j}q^{j},

and by
[
HL98
]
, we have:

q^{-\frac{23}{24}}G^{k}(q)=(\dim{W_{k}})t_{k}(q)\eta(q),

where,

t_{k}(q)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{M}|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{M}}\chi_{k}(g)T^{\natural}_{g}(q).

Here,
T^{\natural}_{g}(q)
is the McKay-Thompson series for
V^{\natural}
corresponding to the element
g\in\mathbb{M}.
In particular, for
g=1
, the identity element in
\mathbb{M},
we have
T^{\natural}_{1}(q)=J(q).

Combining the above two equations, we get:

q^{-\frac{23}{24}}G^{k}(q)=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{M}|}\left(\sum_{g\in\mathbb{M}}\dim{W_{k}}\chi_{k}(g)\right)T^{\natural}_{g}(q)\eta(q)

Since we are interested in
s_{j}=\sum^{194}_{k=1}s^{k}_{j}
, we sum the above equation over
k
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}q^{-\frac{23}{24}}\sum^{194}_{k=1}G^{k}(q)&=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{M}|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{M}}\left(\sum^{194}_{k=1}\dim{W_{k}}\chi_{k}(g)\right)T^{\natural}_{g}(q)\eta(q)\\
q^{-\frac{23}{24}}\sum^{194}_{k=1}\sum_{j\geq 0}s^{k}_{j}q^{j}&=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{M}|}\sum_{g\in\mathbb{M}}\chi_{\reg}(g)T^{\natural}_{g}(q)\eta(q)\\
q^{-\frac{23}{24}}\sum_{j\geq 0}s_{j}q^{j}&=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{M}|}|\mathbb{M}|T^{\natural}_{1}(q)\eta(q)=J(q)\eta(q)\end{split}

where
\chi_{\reg}
is the character of the
regular representation
of the Monster and we have used:

\chi_{\reg}(g)=\sum^{194}_{k=1}(\dim{W_{k}})\chi_{k}(g)\quad\text{ and }\quad\chi_{\reg}(g)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }g\not=\mathrm{id}\\
|\mathbb{M}|&\text{ if }g=\mathrm{id}\end{cases}.

Thus, adjusting for the fact that we set
s_{1}=-1
whereas
\dim P^{\natural}_{1}=0,
we get:

\displaystyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\dim\,P^{\natural}_{j}q^{j}

\displaystyle=q^{\frac{23}{24}}J(q)\eta(q)+q

which proves our desired equality. ∎

Applying Proposition

Appendix B
Non-triviality of the
\mathbb{M}
-action

We denote by
W_{1},W_{2},\cdots,W_{194}
the irreducible representations of
\mathbb{M}
, with corresponding irreducible characters
\chi_{1},\chi_{2},\cdots,\chi_{194}.
Then, for
j\in\{-1,1,2,\dots\},
we have the decomposition (
27
):

V_{j+1}^{\natural}=\bigoplus^{194}_{k=1}W_{k}^{\mathrm{mult}_{k}(j+1)},

where
\mathrm{mult}_{k}(j+1)
is the
multiplicity
of
W_{k}
in
V_{j+1}^{\natural}.
As remarked at the end of Section

We let

and

]

\displaystyle~C(g,2j+1)+\frac{C(g,j)^{2}-C(g^{2},j)}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}C(g,i)\cdot C(g,2j-i),

\displaystyle C(g,4j+1)=

\displaystyle~C(g,2j+3)-C(g,2)\cdot C(g,2j)+\frac{C(g,2j)^{2}+C(g^{2},2j)}{2}+

\displaystyle\frac{C(g,j+1)^{2}-C(g^{2},j+1)}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}C(g,i)\cdot C(g,2j-i+2)+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}C(g^{2},i)\cdot C(g,4j-4i)+\sum_{i=1}^{2j-1}(-1)^{i}\cdot C(g,i)\cdot C(g,4j-i),

\displaystyle C(g,4j+2)=

\displaystyle~C(g,2j+2)+\sum_{i=1}^{j}C(g,i)\cdot C(g,2j-i+1),

\displaystyle C(g,4j+3)=

\displaystyle~C(g,2j+4)-C(g,2)\cdot C(g,2j+1)-\frac{C(g,2j+1)^{2}-C(g^{2},2j+1)}{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{j+1}C(g,i)\cdot C(g,2j-i+3)+\sum_{i=1}^{j}C(g^{2},i)\cdot C(g,4j-4i+2)+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2j}(-1)^{i}\cdot C(g,i)\cdot C(g,4j-i+2).

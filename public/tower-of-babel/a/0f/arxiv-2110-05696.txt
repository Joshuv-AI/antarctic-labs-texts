Poisson summation for Hankel transforms
Taiwang DENG

Abstract
. In this article we study the Poisson summation for Hankel transform in the sense of Braverman-Kazhdan-Ngo in the special case of L \displaystyle L -embedding ρ : GL 1 → GL 2 \displaystyle\rho:\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{1}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{2} . We view such a summation formula as the generalization of the classical Voronoi summation formula.



1.
Introduction

The Voronoi summation formula proved by Voronoi is
the following: Let
\displaystyle d(n)=\sum_{d|n}1
be the classical divisor
function and
\displaystyle\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a smooth
compactly supported function in
\displaystyle(0,\infty)
. Then

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)\varphi(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(x)(\mathop{\mathrm{log}}\nolimits x+2\gamma)dx+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}d(n)\widehat{\varphi}(n),

where
\displaystyle\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant and the function
\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}
is the Hankel transform given by

\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(y)(4K_{0}(4\pi|xy|^{1/2})-2\pi Y_{0}(4\pi|xy|^{1/2})dy,

where
\displaystyle K_{0}
and
\displaystyle Y_{0}
are Bessel functions. Later in 1927, Oppenheim gave the following generalization
for
\displaystyle\sigma_{s}=\sum_{d|n}d^{s}
and
\displaystyle 1/4<\Re(s)<3/4
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{2s-1}(n)n^{1/2-s}\varphi(n)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{2s-1}(n)n^{1/2-s}{\mathcal{H}}_{s}{\varphi}(n)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(x)(\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(2s)x^{s-1/2}+\zeta_{\mathbb{Q}}(2-2s)x^{1/2-s})dx

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{s}{\varphi}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(y)[-2\pi\cos(\pi s)J_{1-2s}(4\pi\sqrt{xy})-2\pi\sin(\pi s)Y_{1-2s}(4\pi\sqrt{xy})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+4\sin(\pi s)K_{1-2s}(4\pi\sqrt{xy})]dy

In particular, letting
\displaystyle s\rightarrow 1/2
we obtain the original Voronoi formula.

Following Beineke and Bump
[
2
, Discussion after Proposition 6]
, we can write the above transform in the following way:
although the integral

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w|^{-2s}\psi(-w-w^{-1}a)dw

is never absolutely convergent, it is conditionally convergent if
\displaystyle 0<\Re(s)<1/2
.
Moreover, if we take
\displaystyle\psi(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w|^{-2s}\psi(-w-w^{-1}a)dw

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\\
&-2\pi a^{1/2-s}[\cos(\pi s)J_{1-2s}(4\pi\sqrt{a})+\sin(\pi s)Y_{1-2s}(4\pi\sqrt{a})],\text{ if }a>0;\\
&4|a|^{1/2-s}\sin(\pi s)K_{1-2s}(4\pi\sqrt{a}),\text{ if }a<0.\end{array}\right.

Letting
\displaystyle s\rightarrow 1/2
, we get(this is indeed valid in distribution sense)

K(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\times}}\psi(-w-w^{-1}x)d^{\times}w=4K_{0}(4\pi|xy|^{1/2})-2\pi Y_{0}(4\pi|xy|^{1/2}),

where
\displaystyle d^{\times}x=\frac{dx}{|x|}
. Note that the kernel function appears in the work of
Ngo
[
12
, §6]
which is termed
\displaystyle\rho
-Bessel function for the representation of
\displaystyle L
-groups:

\rho:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{C}),\quad t\mapsto\begin{bmatrix}t&0\\
0&t\end{bmatrix}.

This is our starting point of developing an adelic version of the Voronoi summation formula
as a Poisson summation formula in the sense of Braverman-Kazhdan-Ngo.

More precisely, we consider the Hankel transform associated to the adelic
\displaystyle\rho
-Bessel function

K_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}(x,s)=\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}}|w|^{1-2s}\psi(-w-w^{-1}x)d^{\times}w.

We introduce the
\displaystyle\rho
-Schwartz spaces(local and global)
in our setup and show that they are stable under Hankel transform. Our main result is Theorem
4.1
,
which is both a Poisson summation in the sense of Braverman-Kazhdan-Ngo and an adelic generalization of the
Voronoi summation formula. We will continue along this line to
investigate the Poisson summation as well as the generalization of the Voronoi summation formula
in later work. Also, a general Poisson summation formula is established for representations of torus in
[
10
]
,
we hope to address the relation to our approach in future work.

Definition 2.4
.

We define for
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(M_{\rho})

Proposition 2.5
.

There exists
\displaystyle P\in\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]
such that

P(f_{\rho})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(M_{\rho})

Proof.

We first consider the integration

L(q^{-s})=\int_{M_{\rho}(F)}f_{\rho}(X)\nu_{s}(X)d\mu(X),

where
\displaystyle L(X)\in\mathbb{C}(X)
is a rational function.
By our assumption that
\displaystyle f_{\rho}
is constant along the fiber of
\displaystyle\nu_{1}
, we have

\int_{M_{\rho}(F)}f_{\rho}(X)\nu_{s}(X)d\mu(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}c_{i}\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}\nolimits(\nu_{1}^{-1}(q^{i}))q^{-is},\quad c_{i}=f_{\rho}|_{\nu_{1}^{-1}(q^{i})}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{M_{\rho}(F)}\epsilon(f_{\rho})(X)\nu_{s}(X)d\mu(X)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{M_{\rho}(F)}f_{\rho}(\pi_{F}^{-1}X)\nu_{s}(X)d\mu(X)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{M_{\rho}(F)}f_{\rho}(X)\nu_{s}(\pi_{F}X)d\pi_{F}\mu(X)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=q^{-n_{\rho}(s+r_{\rho})}\int_{M_{\rho}(F)}f_{\rho}(X)\nu_{s}(X)d\mu(X)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=q^{-n_{\rho}(s+r_{\rho})}L(q^{-s}).

Similarly,

\int_{M_{\rho}(F)}\epsilon^{n}(f_{\rho})(X)\nu_{s}(X)d\mu(X)=q^{-nn_{\rho}(s+r_{\rho})}L(q^{-s})

By assumption,

L(q^{-s})=\frac{R_{1}(q^{-s})}{R_{2}(q^{-s})},\quad R_{i}(q^{-s})\in\mathbb{C}[q^{-s}](i=1,2)

and we further have

R_{2}(q^{-s})=\prod_{i}(\alpha_{i}-q^{-s}).

Consequently, for
\displaystyle P\in\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]

\int_{M_{\rho}(F)}P(f_{\rho})(X)\nu_{s}(X)d\mu(X)=P(q^{-n_{\rho}(s+r_{\rho})})L(q^{-s}).

\displaystyle P\in\mathbb{C}[\epsilon]
such that
\displaystyle P(q^{-n_{\rho}(s+r_{\rho})})L(q^{-s})\in\mathbb{C}[q^{-s}]
.
Since
\displaystyle P(f_{\rho})(X)
is also constant along fibers of
\displaystyle\nu_{1}
, we have

\int_{M_{\rho}(F)}P(f_{\rho})(X)\nu_{s}(X)d\mu(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}d_{i}\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}\nolimits(\nu_{1}^{-1}(q^{i}))q^{-is},\quad d_{i}=P(f_{\rho})|_{\nu_{1}^{-1}(q^{i})}.

It follows that
\displaystyle P(f_{\rho})(X)
is compactly supported with support contained in
\displaystyle M_{\rho}(\mathcal{O}_{F})
.

\displaystyle F
. For
\displaystyle v

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle V_{\rho}=\mathbb{C}^{2}

\displaystyle\rho:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits(V_{\rho})

Corollary 3.18
.

Assume that
\displaystyle\Re(s)\leq 1/2
. Then
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(F_{v}^{\times})
is a subspace of
\displaystyle H^{\delta}(F_{v})
for
\displaystyle 0\leq\delta<3/2-2\Re(s)
. In particular,
we can extend the definition of Hankel transform to
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(F_{v}^{\times})
by applying (
3.7
).

Proof.

We note that for
\displaystyle\Re(s)\leq 1/2
,
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(F_{v})
is a subspace of
\displaystyle L^{2}(F_{v},dx)
.
We first consider the case where
\displaystyle 1-2s\neq 0
. Note that we only need to show that the space
\displaystyle|u|_{v}^{1-2s}{\mathcal{S}}(F_{v})
is contained
in
\displaystyle H^{1-2s}(F_{v})
. Let
\displaystyle\sigma\in|u|_{v}^{1-2s}{\mathcal{S}}(F_{v})
, we write

\sigma=|u|_{v}^{1-2s}f(u),\quad f\in{\mathcal{S}}(F_{v}).

And

\widehat{\sigma}(x)=\int_{F_{v}}|u|_{v}^{1-2s}f(u)\psi_{v}(xu)du.

First we note that for
\displaystyle v
non-archimedean,
\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}(x)
is of compact support, hence

||{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\sigma||_{H^{\delta}(F_{v})}=||(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}\widehat{\sigma}(x)||_{L^{2}(F_{v},dx)}<\infty

for any
\displaystyle\delta>0
.
It remains to consider the case when
\displaystyle v
is archimedean. Since

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\widehat{\sigma}(x)=C\int_{F_{v}}|u|_{v}^{1-2s}(u^{n}f(u))\psi_{v}(xu)du,\quad C\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

and
\displaystyle u^{n}f(u)
is a Schwartz function, the above integration is always well defined.
Therefore
\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}(x)
is in the class
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(F_{v})
.
We only need to know its asymptotic behavior when
\displaystyle|x|_{v}\rightarrow\infty
.
Let us show by using dyadic decomposition that
\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}(x)
is of decay

\displaystyle|x|_{v}^{2\Re(s)-2}
as
\displaystyle|x|_{v}\rightarrow\infty
. In fact let us consider the
decomposition

F_{v}=\coprod_{k=-1}^{\infty}\Delta_{k},\quad\Delta_{-1}=\{|x|_{v}\geq 1\},\Delta_{k}=\{x:\frac{1}{2^{k}}<|x|_{v}\leq\frac{1}{2^{k-1}}\}(k\geq 0).

Let us chose smooth functions
\displaystyle\{\eta_{k}:k\geq-1\}
on
\displaystyle F_{v}
satisfying

\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\eta_{i})\subseteq\Delta_{k}\cup\Delta_{k+1}(k\geq 0),\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits(\eta_{-1})\subseteq\Delta_{-1}

and

\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}\eta_{k}=1,\text{ and }\eta_{k}\geq 0.

Then

\widehat{\sigma}(x)=\sum_{k=-1}^{\infty}\int_{F_{v}}|u|_{v}^{1-2s}\eta_{k}(u)f(u)\psi_{v}(xu)du

Note that
\displaystyle|u|^{1-2s}\eta_{-1}f
is a Schwartz function, so is its Fourier transform, which is of rapid decay at infinity.
Therefore we can drop the term
\displaystyle k=-1
and only consider
\displaystyle k\geq 0
. For simplicity, let us assume that

\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
, but the case
\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
is similar. We have

\int_{F_{v}}|u|_{v}^{1-2s}\eta_{k}(u)f(u)\psi_{v}(xu)du=C_{\ell}|x|_{v}^{-\ell}\int_{F_{v}}\psi_{v}(xu)\frac{d^{\ell}}{du^{\ell}}(|u|_{v}^{1-2s}\eta_{k}(u)f(u))du

for
\displaystyle C_{\ell}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\displaystyle\ell\geq 0
. The derivative here can be written as

\sum_{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=\ell}C_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}}|u|^{1-2s-m_{1}}\eta_{k}^{(m_{2})}(u)\phi^{(m_{3})}(u).

Recall that the support of
\displaystyle\eta_{k}
is
\displaystyle\Delta_{k}\cup\Delta_{k+1}
,
hence
\displaystyle|u|^{1-2s-m_{1}}
is bounded by
\displaystyle 2^{k(m_{1}+2\Re(s)-1)}
.
We further bound
the derivatives
\displaystyle\eta_{k}
and
\displaystyle\phi
by additional constants. In all we obtain a bound for the derivative term

|\sum_{m_{1}+m_{2}+m_{3}=\ell}C_{m_{1},m_{2},m_{3}}2^{k(m_{1}+2\Re(s)-1)}|\leq D_{\ell,s}2^{k(\ell+2\Re(s)-1)}

Now the integral term is bounded by

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\int_{F_{v}}|u|_{v}^{1-2s}\eta_{k}(u)f(u)\psi_{v}(xu)du|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq D_{\ell,s}|x|_{v}^{-\ell}\int_{\Delta_{k}\cup\Delta_{k+1}}2^{k(\ell+2\Re(s)-1)}du

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|x|_{v}^{-\ell}D_{\ell,s}2^{k(\ell+2\Re(s)-2)}.

Note that although the above bound holds for any
\displaystyle\ell\geq 0
, but for
\displaystyle\ell=0
we get a bound
of order
\displaystyle 2^{-k(2-2\Re(s))}
. We see that taking derivatives only provides improvement on bounds
if
\displaystyle|x|_{v}\geq 2^{k}
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{F_{v}}|u|_{v}^{1-2s}\eta_{k}(u)f(u)\psi_{v}(xu)du|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|x|_{v}\geq 2^{k}}D_{\ell,s}|x|_{v}^{-\ell}2^{k(\ell+2\Re(s)-2)}+\sum_{|x|_{v}\leq 2^{k}}D^{\prime}2^{k(2\Re(s)-2)}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq D_{\ell,s}|x|_{v}^{-\ell}|x|_{v}^{\ell+2\Re(s)-2}+D^{\prime}|x|_{v}^{2\Re(s)-2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=D_{s}|x|_{v}^{2\Re(s)-2}.

Finally we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle||{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\sigma||^{2}_{H^{\delta}(F_{v})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=||(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta/2}\widehat{\sigma}(x)||_{L^{2}(F_{v},dx)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\int_{|x|_{v}\leq 1}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx|+|\int_{|x|_{v}\geq 1}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq|\int_{|x|_{v}\leq 1}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx|+D_{s}\int_{|x|_{v}\geq 1}|x|_{v}^{2\delta}|x|_{v}^{4\Re(s)-4}dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{|x|_{v}\leq 1}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx+D_{s}\int_{|x|_{v}\geq 1}|x|_{v}^{2\delta+4\Re(s)-4}dx.

The condition
\displaystyle\delta<3/2-2\Re(s)
implies that
\displaystyle 2\delta+4\Re(s)-4<-1
, hence the above integral
converges absolutely.
This finishes the proof of the fact that
\displaystyle||{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\sigma||_{H^{1-2s}(F_{v})}<\infty
.

Next consider the case when
\displaystyle s=1/2
. We repeat the above computation with
\displaystyle s=1/2

and obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\int_{F_{v}}|\mathop{\mathrm{log}}\nolimits(|u|_{v})|\eta_{k}(u)f(u)\psi_{v}(xu)du|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|x|_{v}^{-\ell}D_{\ell,s}2^{k(\ell-1)},

for
\displaystyle\ell\geq 1
. For
\displaystyle\ell=0
we obtain a bound of order
\displaystyle 2^{-k}k
. And as before,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{F_{v}}|\mathop{\mathrm{log}}\nolimits(|u|_{v})|\eta_{k}(u)f(u)\psi_{v}(xu)du|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|x|_{v}\geq 2^{k}}D_{\ell,s}|x|_{v}^{-\ell}2^{k(\ell-1)}+\sum_{|x|_{v}\leq 2^{k}}D^{\prime}2^{-k}k

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq D_{\ell,s}|x|_{v}^{-\ell}|x|_{v}^{\ell-1}+D^{\prime}|x|_{v}^{-1}|\mathop{\mathrm{log}}\nolimits(|x|_{v})|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq D^{\prime}|x|_{v}^{-1+\epsilon}.

for any
\displaystyle\epsilon>0
.
Now for
\displaystyle 0\leq\delta<1/2
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle||{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\sigma||^{2}_{H^{\delta}(F_{v})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=||(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta/2}\widehat{\sigma}(x)||_{L^{2}(F_{v},dx)}^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\int_{|x|_{v}\leq 1}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx|+|\int_{|x|_{v}\geq 1}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq|\int_{|x|_{v}\leq 1}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx|+D_{s}\int_{|x|_{v}\geq 1}|x|_{v}^{2\delta}|x|_{v}^{-2+2\epsilon}dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{|x|_{v}\leq 1}(1+|x|_{v}^{2})^{\delta}|\widehat{\sigma}(x)|^{2}dx+D_{s}\int_{|x|_{v}\geq 1}|x|_{v}^{2\delta-2+2\epsilon}dx.

This finishes the proof of the fact that
\displaystyle||{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\sigma||_{H^{\delta}(F_{v})}<\infty
.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(F_{v}^{\times})
.

Proof.

In view of Lemma
3.11
,
it remains to show the Proposition for
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F_{v}^{\times})
. Assume that
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F_{v}^{\times})
.
It follows from
[
13
, Theorem 13]
that for
\displaystyle|x|>>1
, we have

{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)(x,s)=0.

In fact by definition,

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)(x,s)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}|y|_{v}^{2s}f(y)K_{F_{v}}(xy,s)d^{\times}y

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}f(y)|w|_{v}^{1-2s}|y|_{v}^{2s}\psi(-w-w^{-1}xy)d^{\times}wd^{\times}y

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}}\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}f(y)|w|_{v}^{1-2s}\psi(-wy-w^{-1}x)d^{\times}wdy,

which is exactly the Hankel transform studied in
[
13
, §5]
. Next we study the behavior of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)(x,s)
near
\displaystyle x=0
.
Since
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}(F_{v}^{\times})
is generated by the set of characteristic functions
\displaystyle 1_{a+\pi_{v}^{r}\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(a\in\mathcal{O}_{v}\text{ but }a\not\equiv 0\mathop{\mathrm{mod}}\nolimits\pi_{v}^{r})
,
we can restrict ourselves to consider
\displaystyle f=1_{a+\pi_{v}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{v}}
with
\displaystyle a\not\equiv 0\mathop{\mathrm{mod}}\nolimits\pi_{v}^{r}
. Moreover, replacing

\displaystyle f
by
\displaystyle a^{-1}.f
we can further reduce to the case where
\displaystyle f=1_{1+\pi_{v}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{v}}

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)(x,s)=\int_{1+\pi_{v}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{v}}\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}|w|_{v}^{1-2s}\psi_{v}(-w-w^{-1}xy)d^{\times}wd^{\times}y

Note that it suffices to show that for
\displaystyle|x|_{v}<<1
, we have

{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)(x,s)=c(s){\mathbb{L}}_{v}(x,s),\quad c(s)\in\mathbb{C}(q_{v}^{-s}).

In fact, by
[
13
, Theorem 8]
, if
\displaystyle|xy|=|x|<|\omega_{v}|^{2}
(note the difference on the conductor of the additive character chosen), we have

\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}|w|_{v}^{1-2s}\psi(-w-w^{-1}xy)d^{\times}w=|x\omega_{v}^{-1}|_{v}^{1-2s}\gamma_{v}(2-2s)+\gamma_{v}(2s)|\omega_{v}|_{v}^{1-2s}.

Hence

{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)(x,s)=\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}\nolimits(1+\pi_{v}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{v},d^{\times}y)(|x\omega_{v}^{-1}|_{v}^{1-2s}\gamma_{v}(2-2s)+\gamma_{v}(2s)|\omega_{v}|_{v}^{1-2s}).

On the other hand,
it follows from the formula (
3.2
) that for
\displaystyle|x|_{v}<1
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}t(\nu(z))^{2-2s}\psi(zx)dz

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{1-|x|_{v}^{1-2s}q_{v}^{2s-1}}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)(1-q_{v}^{2s-1})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-|\pi_{F}|_{v}^{2s-1}\gamma_{v}(2-2s)+|x|_{v}^{1-2s}\gamma_{v}(2-2s).

Now it is clear that the function

{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)-\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{vol}}\nolimits(1+\pi_{v}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{v},d^{\times}y)|\omega_{v}|_{v}^{2s-1}}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}.

is constant around
\displaystyle x=0
. It remains to show that such functions are in
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(F_{v}^{\times})
.
Finally, we observe that

|\pi_{v}|^{2s-1}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}-\pi_{v}.{\mathbb{L}}_{v}

is constant around
\displaystyle x=0
.

Lemma 3.27

Assume that
\displaystyle v

Proof.

3.15
,
we have for

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(|x|_{v}^{2s-2}(-\widehat{\varphi}-\partial\widehat{\varphi})(1/x))^{\widehat{}}(u)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(\varphi)-(|x|_{v}^{2s-3}\frac{d\widehat{\varphi}}{dx}(1/x))^{\widehat{}}(u)

Note that

Proposition 3.28
.

The space
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}^{\circ}(F_{v}^{\times})
is stable under Hankel transform.

Although we may not need it, we make a detail computation of the the Fourier-Mellin transform of elements
of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}^{\circ}(F_{v}^{\times})
along the proof of the proposition.

Proof.

Here we employ Fourier transform over
\displaystyle F_{v}^{\times}
to show that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}^{\circ}(F_{v}^{\times})
is stable under Hankel transform.
In fact, the connected component of the unitary dual of
\displaystyle F_{v}^{\times}
are indexed by the characters

\chi_{n}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&(\frac{z}{|z|_{v}^{1/2}})^{n},n\in\mathbb{Z},\text{ if }F_{v}=\mathbb{C},\\
&(\frac{z}{|z|_{v}})^{n},n\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\text{ if }F_{v}=\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

Note that we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\chi,\mu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\chi(u)|u|_{v}^{\mu}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}(u)d^{\times}u

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\zeta_{F_{v}}(\mu)\zeta_{F_{v}}(1-2s+\mu)\text{ if }\chi=1,\\
0,\quad\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

As a consequence if
\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(u^{n}{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\chi_{m},\mu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\chi_{m}(u)|u|_{v}^{\mu}u^{n}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}(u)d^{\times}u

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu+n)\text{ if }n=m(\mathop{\mathrm{mod}}\nolimits 2),\\
0,\quad\text{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

Similarly, if
\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
and
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have for
\displaystyle n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(u^{n_{1}}\bar{u}^{n_{2}}{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\chi_{m},\mu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\chi_{m}(u)|u|_{v}^{\mu}u^{n_{1}}\bar{u}^{n_{2}}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}(u)d^{\times}u

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu+(n_{1}+n_{2})/2)\text{ if }m=n_{2}-n_{1},\\
0,\quad\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Furthermore, we have for
\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(u^{n}\partial{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\chi_{m},\mu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\chi_{m}(u)|u|_{v}^{\mu}u^{n}\partial{\mathbb{L}}_{v}(u)d^{\times}u

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-(\mu+n){\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu+n)\text{ if }n=m(\mathop{\mathrm{mod}}\nolimits 2),\\
0,\quad\text{ otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

and if
\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
and
\displaystyle m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have for
\displaystyle n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(u^{n_{1}}\bar{u}^{n_{2}}\partial{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\chi_{m},\mu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}\chi_{m}(u)|u|_{v}^{\mu}u^{n_{1}}\bar{u}^{n_{2}}\partial{\mathbb{L}}_{v}(u)d^{\times}u

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}-(\mu+(n_{1}+n_{2})/2){\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu+(n_{1}+n_{2})/2)\text{ if }m=n_{2}-n_{1},\\
0,\quad\text{ otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

For
\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
, it is clear that under the decomposition into even and odd part:
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[u]=\mathbb{C}[u]_{e}\oplus\mathbb{C}[u]_{o}

we have isomorphism of vector spaces

\mathbb{C}[u]_{e}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mu],\quad(P_{1},P_{2})\mapsto\frac{{\mathcal{M}}(P_{1}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}+P_{2}\partial{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu)}{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu)}

\mathbb{C}[u]_{o}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mu],\quad(P_{1},P_{2})\mapsto\frac{{\mathcal{M}}(P_{1}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}+P_{2}\partial{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\chi_{1},\mu)}{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu+1)}.

As for
\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
, let us introduce the weight on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[z,\bar{z}]:\mathop{\mathrm{wt}}\nolimits(z)=-1,\mathop{\mathrm{wt}}\nolimits(\bar{z})=1
.
Let
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[z,\bar{z}]_{m}
be the weight
\displaystyle m
-part of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}[z,\bar{z}]
.
It follows from the above computation that we have isomorphism of vector spaces

\mathbb{C}[z,\bar{z}]_{m}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mu],\quad(P_{1},P_{2})\mapsto\frac{{\mathcal{M}}(P_{1}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}+P_{2}\partial{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\chi_{m},\mu)}{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu+m/2)},\quad\text{ if }m\geq 0.

\mathbb{C}[z,\bar{z}]_{m}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mu],\quad(P_{1},P_{2})\mapsto\frac{{\mathcal{M}}(P_{1}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}+P_{2}\partial{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\chi_{m},\mu)}{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu-m/2)},\quad\text{ if }m\leq 0.

Finally, let us compute the Fourier(Mellin) transform of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)
for
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}^{\circ}(M_{\rho}(F_{v}))
. We have
by Proposition
3.23
,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}({\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f))(\chi,\mu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\gamma_{v}(2s-\mu,\chi^{-1})\gamma_{v}(1-\mu,\chi^{-1}){\mathcal{M}}(f)(\chi^{-1},2s-\mu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\epsilon(1-\mu,\chi^{-1})\epsilon(2s-\mu,\chi^{-1})\frac{L_{v}(\mu,\chi)L_{v}(1-2s+\mu,\chi)}{L_{v}(1-\mu,\chi^{-1})L_{v}(2s-\mu,\chi^{-1})}{\mathcal{M}}(f)(\chi^{-1},2s-\mu).

Of course, this is just the local functional equation for Hankel transform.

\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
, if
\displaystyle\chi=\chi_{n}
, we have(cf.
[
9
, Proposition 3.5]
)

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{v}(\mu,\chi_{n})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&\zeta_{v}(\mu-n/2),\text{ if }n\leq 0,\\
&\zeta_{v}(\mu+n/2),\text{ if }n\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

Hence

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&\epsilon(1-\mu,\chi_{-n})\epsilon(2s-\mu,\chi_{-n})\frac{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu+n/2)}{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(2s-\mu+n/2)}{\mathcal{M}}(f)(\chi_{-n},2s-\mu),n\geq 0\\

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}

Combining our explicit computation shows that

Let us recall the theorem of Hamburger(cf.

\displaystyle dm_{N}(x)

.
In our case, we take

dm(t)=|{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(1/2+it)|dt

which satisfies the assumption of Hamburger’s theorem due to the Stirling estimate:

(3.17)

|\Gamma(x+iy)|=\sqrt{2\pi}|y|^{x-1/2}e^{-\pi|y|/2}[1+O(1/y)],\quad|y|\rightarrow\infty,

and (cf.
[
18
, (1.5)]
)

|\Gamma(z)|\leq|\Gamma(\Re(z))|.

Let

h(t)=\overline{{\mathcal{M}}(f)(1/2+it)}\frac{{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(1/2+it)}{|{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{L}}_{v})(1/2+it)|}.

Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(t)|^{2}dm(t)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}}|{\mathcal{M}}(f)(1/2+it)|^{2}dt,\quad C\in\mathbb{R}.

Now apply Hamburger’s theorem to deduce
\displaystyle g(t)=0
. Hence
\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(f)(1/2+it)=0
. Similar proof works to prove
\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(f)(\chi_{1},1/2+it)
=0.
Hence
\displaystyle f(x)=0
for almost all
\displaystyle x
.

Now consider the case
\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{C}
.
Then the connected components of the unitary dual of
\displaystyle F_{v}^{\times}
is indexed by
\displaystyle\chi_{n}=(\frac{z}{|z|})^{n}
.
Let
\displaystyle g=P(z,\bar{z},\partial){\mathbb{L}}_{v}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}^{\circ}(M_{\rho}(F_{v}))
as
in (
3.14
),
we still have
\displaystyle g=\sum_{m>0}P_{m}(\partial)z^{m}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}+\sum_{m\geq 0}Q_{m}(\partial)\bar{z}^{m}{\mathbb{L}}_{v}
. And
following the computation in the proof of Proposition
3.28
, we have,

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(g)(\chi_{m},\mu)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}|u|_{v}^{\mu}\chi_{m}(u)g(u)d^{\times}u={\mathcal{M}}(\bar{z}^{m}{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu)Q_{m}(-\mu)(m\geq 0),

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(g)(\chi_{m},\mu)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}^{\times}}|u|_{v}^{\mu}\chi_{m}(u)g(u)d^{\times}u={\mathcal{M}}(z^{-m}{\mathbb{L}}_{v})(\mu)P_{-m}(-\mu)(m<0).

Now Plancherel’s theorem implies that

\sum_{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{{\mathcal{M}}(f)(1/2+it)}{\mathcal{M}}(g)(\chi_{m},1/2+it)dt=0,

the left hand side is a finite sum as we remark before.
Now arguing as in the case
\displaystyle F_{v}=\mathbb{R}
gives the result.

∎

Proposition 3.31
.

The Hankel transform is self-inversive: for
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(F_{v}^{\times})
,

{\mathcal{H}}_{s}^{2}(f)(y)=f(y).

Proof.

Note that we already know that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{s}
is an isometry of
\displaystyle H^{1-2s}(F_{v}^{\times})
by Proposition
3.16

and that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(F_{v}^{\times})\subseteq H^{1-2s}(F_{v}^{\times})
by Corollary
3.18
.
We apply Proposition
3.15
. For
\displaystyle f\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(F_{v}^{\times})
,

(3.18)

{\mathcal{H}}_{s}^{2}(f)=(|x|_{v}^{2s-2}\widehat{{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)}(1/x))^{\widehat{}}.

Since

\widehat{{\mathcal{H}}_{s}(f)}(x)=|x|_{v}^{2s-2}\widehat{f}(1/x),

we obtain

{\mathcal{H}}_{s}^{2}(f)=f.

∎

4.
Poisson Summation

We keep the same notation as in previous section.

Theorem 4.1

(Poisson summation)
.

For any
\displaystyle\phi\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times})
we have

\sum_{a\in F^{\times}}\phi(a)=\sum_{a\in F^{\times}}{\mathcal{H}}_{s}{\phi}(a)+\text{other terms}

Remark:
The full formula including the extra terms will be determined in the process of the proof.

We first recall the special Eisenstein series we need and then deduce the Poisson summation formula
from the modular relation of Eisenstein series. This is the classical approach to Voronoi type summation formula
taken up by Bochner(
[
3
]
).

Our construction of Eisenstein series follows
[
2
, §9]
.
Let
\displaystyle\phi=\otimes_{v}\phi_{v}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\rho}(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times})
.
From
\displaystyle\phi
we construct

\displaystyle f_{\phi}=\otimes_{v}f_{\phi,v}
as follows. Let
\displaystyle B
be the Borel subgroup of
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{2}
defined over
\displaystyle F
which consists of lower triangular matrices.
Consider the Bruhat decompostion of
\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{2}=B\coprod BwB
with
\displaystyle w=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
.
First of all, we require that

f_{\phi}(x\begin{bmatrix}t_{1}&0\\
\star&t_{2}\end{bmatrix},s)=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|^{1-s}f_{\phi}(x,s).

Secondly, we let

f_{\phi,v}(\nu(u),s)=\frac{1}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}\int_{F_{v}}\phi_{v}(x,s)\psi_{v}(-ux)dx.

By Bruhat decomposition, this uniquely defines
\displaystyle f
.
Note that for all but finitely many
\displaystyle v
,
\displaystyle\phi_{v}(u,s)={\mathbb{L}}_{v}(u,s)
is basic. In this case
we have

f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)=t(\nu(x))^{2-2s}.

Note that by Corollary
3.19
,
\displaystyle f_{v}(\nu(x),s)
is of decay order

\displaystyle|x|_{v}^{2\Re(s)-2}
as
\displaystyle|x|_{v}\rightarrow\infty
for
\displaystyle v
archimedean.

Finally, by Fourier inversion formula

\phi_{v}(u,s)=\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)\psi_{v}(ux)dx.

We follow
[
17
, §6]
to define our Eisenstein series. Then we define

E(f_{\phi},s,g)=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{2}(F)/B(F)}f_{\phi}(g\gamma,s),\quad g\in\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F}).

The basic properties are sumarized
in the following. For related results of Eisenstein series, we refer
[
17
, Lemma 6.1]
,
[
16
, §6.2]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

(i)

\displaystyle E(f_{\phi},s,g)
converges absolutely and locally uniformly for
\displaystyle\Re(s)<0
and
\displaystyle g\in G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.

(ii)

For
\displaystyle\Re(s)<1/2
, the Eisenstein series has the following convergent Fourier
expansion:

E(f_{\phi},s,a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x_{0}))=a_{0}(\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}})+\frac{|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|^{s}}{Z_{F}(2-2s)}\sum_{\alpha\in F^{\times}}{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\phi(\alpha t_{1}/t_{2},s)\psi_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}(\alpha x_{0}),

where

a_{0}(t)=|t|^{1-s}f_{\phi}(\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}\nolimits,s)+\frac{|t|^{s}}{Z_{F}(2-2s)}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}|u|^{2s-1}\phi(u,s)du,

with
\displaystyle Z_{F}(s)
the completed zeta function of
\displaystyle F

Z_{F}(s)=\prod_{v}\zeta_{v}(s).

(iii)

For
\displaystyle\Re(s)<1/2
, the Eisenstein series has the following convergent Fourier
expansion:

E(f_{\phi},s,wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x_{0}))=b_{0}(\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}})+\frac{|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|^{s}}{Z_{F}(2-2s)}\sum_{\alpha\in F^{\times}}\phi(-\alpha t_{1}/t_{2},s)\psi_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}(\alpha x_{0}),

where

b_{0}(t)=|t|^{1-s}f_{\phi}(w,s)+\frac{|t|^{s}}{Z_{F}(2-2s)}\phi(0,s).

Proof.

The convergence follows from the general theorems cited above.
Let us determine the constant term.
We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{0}(f_{\phi},s,a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x_{0}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=f_{\phi}(a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x_{0}),s)+\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}f_{\phi}(a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x+x_{0})w,s)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|^{1-s}f_{\phi}(\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}\nolimits,s)+\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}f_{\phi}(a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x)w,s)dx.

Since

a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x)w=\begin{bmatrix}0&t_{1}\\
t_{2}&t_{2}x\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&t_{1}t_{2}^{-1}x^{-1}\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}-x^{-1}t_{1}&0\\
t_{2}&t_{2}x\end{bmatrix}

we have

f_{\phi}(a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x)w,s)=|\frac{t_{1}}{x^{2}t_{2}}|^{1-s}f_{\phi}(\nu(t_{1}t_{2}^{-1}x^{-1}),s).

Hence

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x)w,s)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{1-s}\int_{F_{v}}|x|_{v}^{2s-2}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(t_{1}t_{2}^{-1}x^{-1},s))dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}\int_{F_{v}}|x|_{v}^{-2s}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)dx.

Moreover, we know

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}|x|_{v}^{-2s}\psi_{v}(ux)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|u|_{v}^{2s-1}\gamma_{v}(2s),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)\psi_{v}(ux)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{v}(u,s)}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}.

Using the fact that Fourier transform sends product to convolution, we have

\int_{F_{v}}|x|_{v}^{-2s}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)dx=\frac{\gamma_{v}(2s)}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}\int_{F_{v}}|u|_{v}^{2s-1}\phi_{v}(u,s)du.

Note that when
\displaystyle v
is non-archimedean and
\displaystyle\phi_{v}(u,s)
is basic (cf. (
3.2
)), i.e.,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\phi_{v}(u,s)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&|\mathfrak{D}_{v}|_{v}^{s-1/2}\sum_{i=0}^{\nu_{v}(u\omega_{v})}q_{v}^{i(2s-1)},\text{ if }\nu_{v}(u\omega_{v})\geq 0,\\
&0,\quad\text{ otherwise }\end{array}\right.

we have

\int_{F_{v}}|u|_{v}^{2s-1}\phi_{v}(u,s)du=\zeta_{F_{v}}(2s).

Back to genera case, taking product, we obtain

E_{0}(f_{\phi},s,a(t_{1},t_{2}))=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{1-s}f_{\phi}(\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}\nolimits,s)+\frac{|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}}{Z_{F}(2-2s)}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}|u|_{v}^{2s-1}\phi(u,s)du.

Similarly, for positive terms we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x+x_{0})w,s)\psi_{v}(-ux)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|^{1-s}\psi_{v}(ux_{0})\int_{F_{v}}|x|_{v}^{2s-2}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(t_{1}t_{2}^{-1}x^{-1},s))\psi_{v}(-ux)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}\psi_{v}(ux_{0})\int_{F_{v}}|x|_{v}^{-2s}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)\psi_{v}(-ut_{1}t_{2}^{-1}x^{-1})dx.

Plug in the formula

f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)=\frac{1}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}\int_{F_{v}}\phi_{v}(z,s)\psi_{v}(-zx)dz,

we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi}(a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x+x_{0})w,s)\psi_{v}(-ux)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}\psi_{v}(ux_{0})}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}\int_{F_{v}}\int_{F_{v}}|x|_{v}^{-2s}\phi_{v}(z,s)\psi_{v}(-zx-ut_{1}t_{2}^{-1}x^{-1})dzdx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}\psi_{v}(ux_{0})}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}\int_{F_{v}}\int_{F_{v}}|w|_{v}^{-2s}|z|_{v}^{2s-1}\phi_{v}(z,s)\psi_{v}(-w-ut_{1}t_{2}^{-1}zw^{-1})dzdw

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}\psi_{v}(ux_{0})}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}{\mathcal{H}}_{s}\phi_{v}(ut_{1}t_{2}^{-1}).

Taking product over all places yields the result.

We continue to prove (iii).

Let us determine the constant term.
We have

\displaystyle\displaystyle E_{0}(f_{\phi},s,wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x_{0}))

\displaystyle\displaystyle=f_{\phi}(wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x_{0}),s)+\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}f_{\phi}(wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x+x_{0})w,s)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|^{1-s}f_{\phi}(w,s)+\int_{\mathbb{A}_{F}}f_{\phi}(wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x)w,s)dx.

Since

wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x)w=\begin{bmatrix}t_{2}&t_{2}x\\
0&t_{1}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1&t_{2}t_{1}^{-1}x\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}t_{2}&0\\
0&t_{1}\end{bmatrix}

we have

f_{\phi}(wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x)w,s)=|\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}|^{1-s}f_{\phi}(\nu(t_{2}t_{1}^{-1}x),s).

Hence

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x)w,s)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}|_{v}^{1-s}\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(t_{2}t_{1}^{-1}x,s))dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)dx.

Moreover, we know

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)\psi_{v}(ux)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{\phi_{v}(u,s)}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}.

We deduce that

\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x)w,s)dx=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}\frac{\phi_{v}(0,s)}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}.

Taking product, we obtain

E_{0}(f_{\phi},s,a(t_{1},t_{2})n(x))=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|^{1-s}f_{\phi}(w,s)+\frac{|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|^{s}}{Z_{F}(2-2s)}\phi(0,s).

Similarly, for positive terms we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi}(wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x+x_{0})w,s)\psi_{v}(-ux)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}|_{v}^{1-s}\psi_{v}(ux_{0})\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(t_{2}t_{1}^{-1}x,s))\psi_{v}(-ux)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}\psi_{v}(ux_{0})\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)\psi_{v}(-ut_{2}^{-1}t_{1}x)dx.

Plug in the formula

\phi_{v}(u,s)=\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(\nu(x),s)\psi_{v}(ux)dx.

we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{F_{v}}f_{\phi,v}(wa(t_{1},t_{2})n(x+x_{0})w,s)\psi_{v}(-ux)dx

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\frac{|\frac{t_{1}}{t_{2}}|_{v}^{s}\psi_{v}(ux_{0})}{\zeta_{F_{v}}(2-2s)}\phi_{v}(-ut_{1}t_{2}^{-1},s)

Taking product over all places yields the result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

From Proposition
4.2
we have

E(f_{\phi},s,\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}\nolimits)=a_{0}(1)+\frac{1}{Z_{F}(2-2s)}\sum_{\alpha\in F^{\times}}{\mathcal{H}}_{s}{\phi}(\alpha,s),

and

E(f_{\phi},s,w)=b_{0}(1)+\frac{1}{Z_{F}(2-2s)}\sum_{\alpha\in F^{\times}}\phi(\alpha,s).

But we have
\displaystyle E(f_{\phi},s,\mathop{\mathrm{Id}}\nolimits)=E(f_{\phi},s,w)
, this gives

Z_{F}(2-2s)a_{0}(1)+\sum_{\alpha\in F^{\times}}{\mathcal{H}}_{s}{\phi}(\alpha,s)=Z_{F}(2-2s)b_{0}(1)+\sum_{\alpha\in F^{\times}}\phi(\alpha,s),

which is our desired Poisson summation formula.
∎

On endosplit p p -permutation resolutions and Broué’s conjecture for p p -solvable groups
Sam K. Miller

Abstract
. Endosplit p p -permutation resolutions play an instrumental role in verifying Broué’s abelian defect group conjecture in numerous cases. We give a new characterization of all endosplit p p -permutation resolutions and reduce the question of Galois descent of an endosplit p p -permutation resolution to the Galois descent of the module it resolves. This is shown using techniques from the study of endotrivial complexes, the invertible objects of the bounded homotopy category of p p -permutation modules. As an application, we show that a refinement of Broué’s conjecture proposed by Kessar–Linckelmann holds for certain blocks of groups G G satisfying G = O p ′ , p , p ′ ​ ( G ) G=O_{p^{\prime},p,p^{\prime}}(G) with abelian Sylow p p -subgroup, the key reduction step in Harris–Linckelmann’s verification of Broué’s conjecture for all p p -solvable groups.

Definition 3.1

Remark 3.2

It is straightforward to see via the Künneth formula that a bounded chain complex

Proposition 3.3

Let

(M^{*}\otimes_{k}M)(P)=0

Now, suppose condition (b) holds. Suppose for contradiction that

-permutation resolution.
∎

[

The kernel of

-endotrivial chain complexes

has nonzero homology only at degree 0 for any

-subgroup

. Suppose for contradiction that there exists an indecomposable

-endotrivial chain complex

with

such that

for some

-module

. Without loss of generality suppose that there exists

Remark 3.16

We are about to characterize all

-permutation resolutions themselves. This may be seen as the closest one may get to a classification theorem in the spirit of Dade, as there is no group structure that parameterizes all endo-

Similar to how capped endopermutation modules can be thought of as the invertible endopermutation modules, and thus can be given a group structure, the

-permutation module (resp. endosplit

-permutation resolution) can be built from invertible endo-

-permutation modules (resp. endosplit-

).

-permutation

-module.

has an endosplit

-endotrivial

, where

is a chain tensor of relative syzygies which resolves

The reverse direction of the first statement is straightforward: since both

for all

-permutation modules.

Now, we have

. Finally, if

is not permutation, there exists another
p
-permutation
X^{\prime}
for which
X\oplus X^{\prime}
is permutation (in fact,
X^{\prime}
can be chosen such that
X\oplus X^{\prime}
has stabilizers only
p
-subgroups of
G
, since every trivial source
kG
-module with vertex
P
is a direct summand of
k[G/P]
), and replacing
X
with
X\oplus X^{\prime}
completes the proof of the first statement.

Now, suppose that
C_{M}
has no contractible direct summands. It follows from the homotopy equivalence
C_{M}\oplus(N[0]\otimes_{k}C_{M})\simeq C_{E}\otimes_{k}C_{E}^{*}\otimes_{k}C_{M}
that
C_{M}
is a direct summand of
C_{E}\otimes_{k}C_{E}^{*}\otimes_{k}C_{M}
. Now, we have

C_{E}\otimes_{k}C_{E}^{*}\otimes_{k}C_{M}\cong C_{E}\otimes_{k}(X[0]\oplus K)\cong C_{E}\otimes_{k}X[0]\oplus C_{E}\otimes_{k}K,

where
K
is a contractible complex. Note that
C_{E}\otimes_{k}K
is also contractible. Combining these statements,
C_{M}
is a direct summand of
C_{E}\otimes_{k}X[0]\oplus C_{E}\otimes_{k}K
, but since
C_{M}
was assumed to have no contractible direct summands, we conclude
C_{M}
is a direct summand of
C_{E}\otimes_{k}X[0]
.

The final statement follows since we assumed in the proof that
C_{M}
was an arbitrary endosplit
p
-permutation resolution, and from the observation that the permutation module
X
can be chosen to have
p
-subgroup stabilizers.
∎

In particular, every endosplit
p
-permutation resolution is a direct summand of some chain complex which comes from the base field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. If one wishes to construct an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution of a module, it is not necessary to enlarge the base field.

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
k
be a finite field, and
M
be an endo-
p
-permutation
kG
-module. Set
k^{\prime}:=\overline{k}
. The module
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}M
has an endosplit
p
-permutation over
k^{\prime}
if and only if
M
has an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution over
k
.

Proof.

The reverse implication is clear. Suppose
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}M
has an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution. By Theorem
3.17
,
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}M
is a direct summand of
E\otimes_{k}X
, where
E
is a module tensor of relative syzygies and
X
is a permutation module, both over
k^{\prime}
. By construction, both
E
and
X
have corresponding
kG
-modules
\tilde{E}
and
\tilde{X}
for which
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{E}\cong E
and
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{X}\cong X
. Then

k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}(\tilde{E}\otimes_{k}\tilde{X})\cong(k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{E})\otimes_{k}^{\prime}(k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{X})\cong E\otimes_{k}X.

By Theorem
3.17
, it suffices to show that

is a direct summand of
\tilde{E}\otimes_{k}\tilde{X}
. However, this follows by the Noether-Deuring theorem since

is a direct summand of

.

∎

The classification can be restated in a slightly more compact manner, which is reminiscent of Dade’s original work on endopermutation modules. The following corollary also gives another moral reason why
V(\mathcal{F}_{G})
-endotrivial complexes may be seen as the “capped” endosplit

-permutation resolutions.

Corollary 3.19
.

Let

be an endosplit

-permutation resolution with no contractible direct summands of a

is a direct summand of the endosplit
p
-permutation resolution

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{P_{i}}\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{P_{i}}Y
for some chain tensor of relative syzygies

and family of

-subgroups
\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{l}
of

.

Proof.

We see from Theorem

that there exists a chain tensor of relative syzygies

and a permutation

-module

with

-subgroup stabilizers such that

is a direct summand of

. Frobenius reciprocity asserts that

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{P_{i}}((\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{P_{i}}Y)\otimes_{k}k[0])\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}Y\otimes_{k}(\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{P_{i}}k)[0]\cong Y\otimes_{k}\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{l}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{P_{i}}k\right)[0],

and the result follows.
∎

Remark 3.20
.

Dade described how all indecomposable endopermutation

.

[

Let

4.
Galois descent of endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions

In this section, we consider the question of Galois descent of endosplit

p
-permutation resolutions. First, we reduce the question of Galois descent of relatively endotrivial complexes to Galois descent of the module that they resolve. Using this, and the classification of endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions, we extend this reduction to all endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions.

Notation 4.1
.

We adapt the notation conventions from
[
16
]
. For arbitrary extensions of commutative rings
R\subseteq R^{\prime}
, if
A
is an
\mathcal{O}
-algebra, one can form the
R^{\prime}
-algebra
A^{\prime}:=R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}A
. Similarly, given any
A
-module
M
, one can form an
A^{\prime}
-module
R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}M
. In general, not every
A^{\prime}
-module arises in this way. Given a ring automorphism
\sigma
of
R^{\prime}
which restricts to the identity map on
R
and an
A^{\prime}
-module
M^{\prime}
, we denote by
{}^{\sigma}M^{\prime}
the
A^{\prime}
-module which is equal to
M^{\prime}
over the subalgebra
1\otimes A
of
A^{\prime}
, and such that
\lambda\otimes a\in A^{\prime}
acts on
M^{\prime}
as
\sigma^{-1}(\lambda)\otimes a
. Note that if
f\colon M^{\prime}\to N^{\prime}
is an
A^{\prime}
-module homomorphism, then
f
is also an
A^{\prime}
-module homomorphism
f\colon{}^{\sigma}M^{\prime}\to{}^{\sigma}N^{\prime}
. Thus, the Galois twist
{}^{\sigma}(-)
induces an
R
-linear (but in general not
R^{\prime}
-linear), exact autoequivalence of
{}_{A^{\prime}}\mathbf{mod}
.

We have functors

-_{R}\colon{}_{A^{\prime}}\mathbf{mod}\to{}_{A}\mathbf{mod}\text{ and }R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}-\colon{}_{A}\mathbf{mod}\to{}_{A^{\prime}}\mathbf{mod},

restriction and extension of scalars, respectively. These are both
R
-linear exact functors. Moreover,
R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}-
is left adjoint to
-_{R}
. Moreover, these functors induce functors over the bounded chain complex categories
Ch^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod})
and
Ch^{b}({}_{A^{\prime}}\mathbf{mod})
and the same adjunction holds.

We say an
A^{\prime}
-module
M
(resp. chain complex of
A^{\prime}
-modules
C
)
descends to
A
or
descends to
R
if there exists a
A
-module
\tilde{M}
(resp. chain complex of
A
-modules
\tilde{C}
) such that
R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}\tilde{M}\cong M
(resp.
R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}\tilde{C}\cong C
). We may also say
M

is realized in
R
. Another way of viewing this (see
[
8
, Remark after 7.15]
) is that there exists a matrix representation of
M
over
R^{\prime}
with coefficients in
R
. We have the same notion for homomorphisms of
R^{\prime}G
-modules, by considering them as intertwiners of matrix representations.

Let
(K,\mathcal{O},k)\subseteq(K^{\prime},\mathcal{O}^{\prime},k^{\prime})
be an extension of
p
-modular systems, i.e. a pair of
p
-modular systems such that
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
and
J(\mathcal{O})\subseteq J(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})
. Furthermore, we assume that
k
and
k^{\prime}
are finite fields, and that
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
are absolutely unramified, i.e.
J(\mathcal{O})=p\mathcal{O}
and
J(\mathcal{O}^{\prime})=p\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
. This is always achievable by setting
\mathcal{O}=W(k)
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}=W(k^{\prime})
, the Witt vectors of
k
and
k^{\prime}
. Set
d=[k^{\prime}:k]
. Then
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is free of rank
d
as an
\mathcal{O}
-module. Let
\sigma\colon k^{\prime}\to k^{\prime}
be a generator of
\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
. Then there exists a unique ring homomorphism
\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
lifting
\sigma\colon k^{\prime}\to k^{\prime}
, since
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is absolutely unramified, and the composite
(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}-)_{\mathcal{O}}
is naturally isomorphic to the functor
(-)^{\oplus d}
since
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is free over
\mathcal{O}
of rank
d
. As a result, since the Krull-Schmidt theorem for
\mathcal{O}G
-modules and complexes holds, Galois descent is unique up to isomorphism. That is, if
M_{1},M_{2}
are
\mathcal{O}G
-modules for which
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}M_{1}\cong\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}M_{2}
, then
M_{1}\cong M_{2}
, and similarly for chain complexes. This also holds over the residue field
k
.

Our main tool will be the following theorem, which describes exactly when Galois descent occurs for chain complexes for algebras over absolutely unramified complete discrete valuation rings.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
25
, Theorem 6]

Let
(K,\mathcal{O},k)\subseteq(K^{\prime},\mathcal{O}^{\prime},k^{\prime})
be an extension of
p
-modular systems with
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
absolutely unramified, and let
A
be a finitely generated
\mathcal{O}
-free
\mathcal{O}
-algebra. Set
A^{\prime}:=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}A
.

(a)

Suppose
C\in Ch^{b}({}_{A^{\prime}}\mathbf{mod})
indecomposable satisfies
{}^{\sigma}C\cong C
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
, where we regard
\sigma
as the unique ring homomorphism of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
lifting
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
. Then there exists an indecomposable chain complex
\tilde{C}\in Ch^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod})
such that
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}\cong C
. Moreover,
\tilde{C}
is unique up to isomorphism.

(b)

Conversely, let
\tilde{C}\in Ch^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod})
and define
C:=\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}
. Then
C
satisfies
{}^{\sigma}C\cong C
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
,
V\in{}_{k^{\prime}G}\mathbf{mod}
,
M
an indecomposable relatively
V
-endotrivial
k^{\prime}G
-module, and
C
an indecomposable endosplit
p
-permutation for
M
, i.e.
C
is a
V
-endotrivial complex. We have
{}^{\sigma}M\cong M
if and only if
{}^{\sigma}C\cong C
.

Proof.

First, assume
{}^{\sigma}M\cong M
. Since
C
is an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution for
M
and
{}^{\sigma}(-)
is exact,
{}^{\sigma}C
is an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution for
{}^{\sigma}M
. Therefore,
C^{*}\otimes_{k}{}^{\sigma}C
is an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution for
M^{*}\otimes_{k}{}^{\sigma}M\cong M^{*}\otimes_{k}M\cong k\oplus N
for some
V
-projective
N
.

Notice that
h_{C}(P)=h_{{}^{\sigma}C}(P)
for all
P\in s_{p}(G)
. Therefore,
C^{*}\otimes_{k}{}^{\sigma}C
has
h
-marks entirely 0 and homology
{}^{\sigma}M\otimes_{k}M^{*}\cong k\oplus N
. Therefore,
C^{*}\otimes_{k}{}^{\sigma}C\simeq(k\oplus N)[0]
, so
[C]=[{}^{\sigma}C]\in\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
. However, both
C
and
{}^{\sigma}C
are indecomposable, and every equivalence class in
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{V}(G)
has a unique indecomposable representative, thus
C\cong{}^{\sigma}C
. The reverse implication is clear since
{}^{\sigma}(-)
is exact.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
k^{\prime}/k
be an extension of finite fields and
V\in{}_{k^{\prime}G}\mathbf{mod}
. Suppose
C
is an indecomposable endosplit
p
-permutation for a
V
-endotrivial module
M\in{}_{k^{\prime}G}\mathbf{mod}
, i.e.
C
is
V
-endotrivial. Then
M
descends to
k
if and only if
C
does.

Proof.

Fix
(K,\mathcal{O},k)\subseteq(K^{\prime},\mathcal{O}^{\prime},k^{\prime})
an extension of
p
-modular systems with
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
absolutely unramified (for instance,
\mathcal{O}:=W(k),\mathcal{O}^{\prime}:=W(k^{\prime})
). The reverse direction of the if and only if statement is clear. Suppose
M
descends to a
kG
-module
\tilde{M}
. Then
{}^{\sigma}M\cong M
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
. By the previous lemma,
{}^{\sigma}C\cong C
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
as well. Now, there exists a unique lift
\hat{C}
, a chain complex of
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}G
-modules, such that
k^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\hat{C}\cong C
. It follows that
{}^{\sigma}\hat{C}\cong\hat{C}
for all
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(k^{\prime}/k)
by unique lifting of
\sigma
. By Theorem
4.2
, there exists a chain complex of
p
-permutation
\mathcal{O}G
-modules
\tilde{C}
for which
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}\cong\hat{C}
. Then
k\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}
. All together, we have

k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}(k\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C})\cong k^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C}\cong k^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}}\tilde{C})\cong C,

thus
C
descends to
k
, as desired.

∎

Remark 4.5
.

If the previous theorem holds, then if
M
descends to the
kG
-module
\tilde{M}
and
C
descends to a chain complex of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules
\tilde{C}
, then
\tilde{C}
is an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution for
\tilde{M}
, since extension of scalars is exact. This result also holds over complete discrete valuation rings, even if we make no assumptions about absolute unramification, due to unique lifting of endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions.

Because endotrivial complexes are particular examples of relatively endotrivial complexes, we can discern which fields they descend to rather easily. In particular, up to a twist by a degree one character, endotrivial complexes descend to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We denote the group of endotrivial complexes by
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
, and
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
the
homology-normalized
subgroup of
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
, which consists of equivalence classes of endotrivial complexes with nonzero homology isomorphic to the trivial
kG
-module.

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
C
be an indecomposable endotrivial complex of
kG
-modules with
H_{h_{C}(1)}(C)\cong k_{\omega}
for some
\omega\in\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}).
Then there exists an endotrivial complex
\tilde{C}\in Ch^{b}({}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}\mathbf{triv})
for which
k\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\tilde{C}\cong C
. In particular,

\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G)\times\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times})\cong CF_{b}(G,p)\times\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times}),

and
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)
is independent of the field
k
.

Proof.

The first statement follows immediately from the previous results. This asserts that
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G)
. The isomorphism
\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\cong\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(G)\times\operatorname{Hom}(G,k^{\times})
follows from
[
24
, Remark 3.8]
, and the isomorphism
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\cong CF_{b}(G,p)
follows from
[
27
, Corollary 6.4]
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

The fact that all endotrivial complexes live in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
up to a twist by a degree one Brauer character is not that surprising, since they are determined almost entirely by the Borel-Smith functions which exist independently of scalars. Since endotrivial complexes are, in a sense, the one-sided analogue of a splendid Rickard equivalence, this may provide a heuristic reason for why one may place faith in the refined abelian defect group conjecture. Additionally, the previous corollary gives another proof of non-surjectivity in general of the Lefschetz homomorphism
\Lambda\colon\mathcal{E}_{k}(G)\to O(T(kG))
, which was first considered in
[
24
, Section 7]
.

Now we extend our results to all endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions by using the characterization of endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions from Theorem
3.17
.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
k^{\prime}/k
be an extension of finite fields. Let
C
be an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution for an endo-
p
-permutation
k^{\prime}G
-module
M
which contains no contractible summands. Then
C
descends to
k
if and only if
M
does.

Proof.

The forward direction is clear since extension of scalars is exact, therefore a descendant of
C
is a resolution for a descendant of
M
. Suppose
M
descends to
k
. By Theorem
3.17
,
C
is a direct summand of the tensor product
C_{E}\otimes_{k^{\prime}}X[0]
of a chain tensor of relative syzygies
C_{E}
and a permutation module
X
, regarded as a chain complex in degree zero. Now, observe
C_{E}
and
X
both in fact descend to
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(this is clear from their constructions), therefore they both descend to any intermediate extension
k^{\prime}\supseteq k\supseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Hence the same holds for
C_{E}\otimes_{k^{\prime}}X[0]
.

Similarly,
M
is a direct summand of
E\otimes_{k^{\prime}}X
which also descends to
k
, say with multiplicity
m
. Let
Y
be the unique up to isomorphism
kG
-module for which
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}Y\cong E\otimes_{k^{\prime}}X
and let
C_{Y}
be its corresponding endosplit
p
-permutation resolution containing no contractible direct summands. Both complexes are tensors of relative syzygies, and we have
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}C_{Y}\simeq C_{E}\otimes_{k^{\prime}}X[0]
.

Let
\tilde{M}
be the
kG
-module for which
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}\tilde{M}\cong M
, then
\tilde{M}
is a direct summand of
Y
, and also occurs with multiplicity
m
.

Let
Y=Y_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus Y_{l}
be a direct sum decomposition of
Y
into indecomposables. By Proposition
3.4
, this corresponds to a direct sum decomposition
C_{Y}=C_{Y_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{Y_{l}}
where each
C_{Y_{i}}
is an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution for
Y_{i}
with no contractible summands. Therefore,

C_{E}\otimes_{k^{\prime}}X[0]\simeq k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}(C_{Y_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{Y_{l}})\cong k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}C_{Y_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}C_{Y_{l}}.

Note that in this sum, there must be exactly
m
endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions of
M
present (with respect to this decomposition into not necessarily indecomposable parts), since
\tilde{M}
occurs in
Y
with multiplicity
m
by the Noether-Deuring theorem. Since there must be a total of
m
endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions of
M
in any decomposition of
C_{E}\otimes_{k^{\prime}}X[0]
, by Krull-Schmidt one of these resolutions must be
C
up to isomorphism, since
C
is a direct summand of
C_{E}\otimes_{k^{\prime}}X[0]
as well. Thus
C
also descends to
k
.

∎

The above theorem also holds for complete discrete valuation rings with finite residue field by the unique lifting of endosplit
p
-permutation resolutions.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
k
be a finite field and set
k^{\prime}:=\overline{k}
. Suppose
M
is an endo-
p
-permutation
kG
-module for which
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}M
has an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution
\hat{C}
of
p
-permutation
k^{\prime}G
-modules. Then there exists an endosplit
p
-permutation resolution
C
of
M
such that
k^{\prime}\otimes_{k}C\cong\hat{C}
.

Proof.

This follows since both every component
\hat{C}_{i}
or differential
d_{i}\colon\hat{C}_{i}\to\hat{C}_{i-1}
has some finite field
l
that realizes
\hat{C}_{i}
or
d_{i}
, since
k^{\prime}
is the colimit of the diagram
\mathbb{F}_{p}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hookrightarrow\dots
, so any matrix representation or intertwiner will have values contained in some finite field. Therefore, there exists a finite field

which realizes
\hat{C}
, i.e. there exists a chain complex
C^{\prime}
of
lG
-modules for which
k^{\prime}\otimes_{l}C^{\prime}\cong\hat{C}
. By Theorem
4.8
,
C^{\prime}
descends to
k
, as desired.
∎

Constructing splendid Rickard equivalences for blocks of

-solvable groups over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

-solvable groups

with abelian defect groups.

Conjecture 5.1
.

[

“Given a categorical equivalence, say a Morita equivalence or a Rickard equivalence between

and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}Hc
for some complete discrete valuation ring
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
for

and

finite groups,

and

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}G
and

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}

\mathcal{O}^{\prime}Gb
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}Hc

\mathcal{O}Gb

Remark 5.9

The assumption that

\tilde{X}\otimes_{l}\tilde{V}
is splendid. From this we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.12

Let

Proposition

verifies that the construction of

∎

Remark 5.14
.

The key obstruction to verifying Kessar–Linckelmann’s conjecture for all
p
-solvable groups by proving the reduction step
[
10
, Proposition 3.1]
is that
[
10
, Lemma 3.3]
may not hold for fields not large enough for
G
. Indeed, a counterexample is as follows. Consider
\mathbb{F}_{2}S_{3}=\mathbb{F}_{2}(C_{3}\rtimes C_{2})
, which has two blocks. The non-principal block idempotent

is a block of
\mathbb{F}_{2}C_{3}
. However, in
\mathbb{F}_{4}C_{3}
,

decomposes as

, where the two blocks

correspond to the two nontrivial characters of

, and

swaps

and

e
is a
S_{3}
-stable block of
\mathbb{F}_{2}O_{2^{\prime}}(S_{3})=\mathbb{F}_{2}C_{3}
, but

has trivial defect group, not defect group the Sylow 2-subgroup

This also demonstrates that not all block idempotents of

, where

satisfies

-subgroup, satisfy condition
(\ast)
. Indeed, with the above notation, there does not exist a
G
-stable block

of

and

-stable.

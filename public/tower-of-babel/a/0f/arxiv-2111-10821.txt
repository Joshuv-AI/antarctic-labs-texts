The voter model with a slow membrane
Xiaofeng Xue, Linjie Zhao

Abstract
. We introduce the voter model on the infinite lattice with a slow membrane and investigate its hydrodynamic behavior and nonequilibrium fluctuations. The model is defined as follows: a voter adopts one of its neighbors’ opinion at rate one except for neighbors crossing the hyperplane { x : x 1 = 1 / 2 } \{x:x_{1}=1/2\} , where the rate is α ​ N − β \alpha N^{-\beta} . Above, α > 0 , β ≥ 0 \alpha>0,\,\beta\geq 0 are two parameters and N N is the scaling parameter. The hydrodynamic equation turns out to be heat equation with various boundary conditions depending on the value of β \beta . For the nonequilibrium fluctuations, the limit is described by generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with certain boundary condition corresponding to the hydrodynamic equation.



1.
Introduction

One of the main issues in statistical physics is to derive partial differential equations from microscopic systems. Roughly speaking, for symmetric systems the macroscopic behaviors are usually governed by diffusive equations, and for asymmetric systems usually by hyperbolic equations. We refer the readers to
[
10
,
15
]
for a comprehensive understanding of the above subject.
Recently, it has been a popular topic to establish PDEs with various boundary conditions from interacting particle systems. For example, Gonçalves, Franco and their collaborators have obtained the heat equation with Dirichlet/Robin/Neumann boundary conditions from the symmetric simple exclusion processes in one dimension
[
1
,
6
,
7
]
. The microscopic models they considered are either defined on a ring with a slow site/slow bond, or defined on a segment with slow boundaries. The results have also been extended to higher dimensions in
[
8
,
14
,
16
]
.

The voter model also plays an important role in the theory of interacting particle systems, and its hydrodynamic behavior has been considered by Presutti and Spohn in
[
13
]
. For each
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, imagine there is a voter at site
x
. Each voter has one of two possible opinions on an issue, and the possible opinions are denoted by
0
or
1
. The dynamics is quite simple: a voter adopts one of its neighbors’ opinion at rate one. Although the voter model has the same macroscopic behavior as the symmetric exclusion process, it differs from the later in essential points: the magnetization of the voter model is not locally conserved and the static correlations decay slowly. We refer the readers to
[
13
,
12
]
for details of the above properties. A natural question is to consider the impact of the slow dynamics introduced in
[
1
,
6
,
7
]
on the voter model, which is the main aim of this article.

In order to obtain boundary conditions, we let the voters at sites
x
and
x+e_{1}
adopt the other’s opinion at rate
\alpha N^{-\beta}
if
x_{1}=0
. Above,
x_{1}
is the first coordinate of site
x
, and
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The two parameters
\alpha>0,\beta\geq 0
denote the strength of the boundary interaction, and
N\in\mathbb{N}
is a scaling parameter. We consider the voter model in dimensions
d\geq 4
, and derive Robin boundary conditions if
\beta=1
and Neumann boundary conditions if
\beta>1
. There are no boundary conditions in the case
0\leq\beta<1
. Moreover, for nonequilibrium fluctuations, we prove that if
\beta=1
, the limit is given by generalized Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with Robin boundary condition, and if
\beta>3/2
, the limit has Neumann boundary condition. We remark that the other cases remain open.

Although the results are similar to the exclusion process, the proof differs a lot from the latter because of the two properties we addressed above. The standard approach to prove a hydrodynamic limit result is as follows: we first write down a martingale formula for the empirical measure of the process by using Dynkin’s formula, and then prove the so called replacement lemmas to close the equation. To ensure uniqueness of solution to hydrodynamic equations, an energy estimate is usually also needed. For exclusion process with boundary dynamics, the replacement lemmas and energy estimates are proved by investigating the entropy production of the process. This is not an easy task for the voter model with boundary terms since the invariant measures of the voter model in this case are not explicitly known. Instead, we adopt the duality method and investigate the correlation functions of the model. It is well known that the duality of the voter model is coalescing random walks,
cf.
[
12
]
for example. For the voter model with boundary dynamics we introduced above, the duality turns out to be coalescing random walks with slow bonds. Along the proof, we exploit an invariance principle for the random walk with slow bonds, proved very recently by Erhard
et al.
in
[
3
]
. We also remark that no replacement lemmas are needed in Presutti and Spohn’s paper
[
13
]
since the process is linear.

We believe the results should also hold for dimension
d=3
. The main issue is to investigate the meeting probabilities of two independent random walks with slow bonds in dimension
d=3
, which has long been a hard problem and has its own interest. This case is presently out of our reach and we leave it open. For dimensions
d\leq 2
, locally the voter model is in a superposition of the configuration with all sites occupied and the one with all sites empty, see
[
13
, Section 2]
for details.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we define the model rigorously and state our main results. We state duality of the voter model and invariance principle for the random walk with slow bounds in Section
3
. Hydrodynamic limits and fluctuations for the voter model are proved respectively in Sections
4
and
5
.

2.
Notation and Results

2.1.
The voter model.

The state space of the voter model is
\Omega_{d}:=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
,
d\geq 1
. In this article, we focus on the case
d\geq 4
. We use
x,y
(resp.
u,v
) to denote points in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(resp.
\mathbb{R}^{d}
), and
x_{i}
(resp.
u_{i}
),
1\leq i\leq d
, to denote the
i
-th component of the point
x
(resp.
u
). Let
\{e_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
i.e.
,
e_{i}
is the vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with the
i
–th component equal to one and the others equal to zero. For a point
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, denote
|x|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|
. Fix parameters
\alpha>0,\,\beta\geq 0
. We use
N\in\mathbb{N}
to denote the scaling parameter. The generator of the process
\mathcal{L}_{N}
, which acts on local functions
f:\Omega_{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, is given by

(2.1)

\mathcal{L}_{N}f(\eta)=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\atop|x-y|=1}\xi^{N}_{x,y}\big(f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)\big),

where
\eta^{x,y}
is the configuration obtained from
\eta
by flipping the value of
\eta(x)
to
\eta(y)
,
i.e.
,
\eta^{x,y}(x)=\eta(y)
and
\eta^{x,y}(z)=\eta(z)
for
z\neq x
. The flipping rates
\xi^{N}_{x,y}
are symmetric,
\xi^{N}_{x,y}=\xi^{N}_{y,x}
for
|x-y|=1
, and are given by

(2.2)

\xi^{N}_{x,y}=\begin{cases}\alpha N^{-\beta}&\text{if\;}x_{1}=0,y_{1}=1\\
1\quad&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

We refer the readers to
[
12
]
for a construction of the process.

2.2.
Hydrodynamic limit.

In this subsection, we state the first main result of the article. To this aim, we first introduce weak solutions of heat equations with/without boundary conditions. Hereafter, we fix a time horizon
T>0
.

Definition 2.1

(Hydrodynamic equation for
0\leq\beta<1
)
.

Let
\rho_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]
be continuous. A bounded function
\rho:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be a weak solution of the heat equation

(2.3)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,u)=\Delta\rho(t,u),\quad&t>0,\;u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\rho(0,u)=\rho_{0}(u),\quad&u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

if for any
H\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,u)H(u)du=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(u)H(u)du+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(s,u)\Delta H(u)\,du\,ds.

For the case
\beta\geq 1
, we first introduce the notion of Sobolev spaces. For an open subset
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
{\mathcal{H}^{1}}(U)
be the Sobolev space which consists of locally integrable functions with
weak derivatives
in
L^{2}(U)
,
i.e.
, for any
\varrho\in{\mathcal{H}^{1}}(U)
, there exist
d
elements in
L^{2}(U)
, denoted by
\partial_{u_{1}}\varrho,\ldots,\partial_{u_{d}}\varrho
, such that

\int_{U}\varrho(u)\partial_{u_{i}}H(u)du=-\int_{U}\partial_{u_{i}}\varrho(u)H(u)du

for any
H\in C_{c}^{\infty}(U)
and for any
1\leq i\leq d
. Denote by
L^{2}([0,T],{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(U))
the space of measurable functions
\rho:[0,T]\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(U)
such that the norm defined by

\|\rho\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(U))}:=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\big(\partial_{u_{i}}\rho(t,u)\big)^{2}\,du\,dt

is finite. We refer the readers to
[
5
, Chapter 5]
for properties of Sobolev spaces.

In this article, we are concerned with the case where
U=\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:u_{1}=0\}
. For simplicity, from now on we write
\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:~u_{1}\in A\}
as
\{u_{1}\in A\}
for any subset
A\subset\mathbb{R}
. For any
f\in{\mathcal{H}}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u_{1}=0\}\right)
, we have that
f\big|_{u_{1}>0}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{1}\left(\{u_{1}>0\}\right)
and
f\big|_{u_{1}<0}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{1}\left(\{u_{1}<0\}\right)
. Then, we use
f(\cdot^{+})
to denote the trace of
f\big|_{u_{1}>0}
on
\{u_{1}=0\}
and use
f(\cdot^{-})
to denote the trace of
f\big|_{u_{1}<0}
on
\{u_{1}=0\}
. The following property of
f(\cdot^{+})
and
f(\cdot^{-})
is crucial. For any bounded
D\subset\{u_{1}=0\}
, according to the trace theorem (
cf.

[
5
, Theorem 1, Section 5.5]
for example), it is easy to check that

(2.4)

{\rm Aver}_{\varepsilon,+}f\big|_{D}\rightarrow f(\cdot^{+})\big|_{D}\text{~and~}{\rm Aver}_{\varepsilon,-}f\big|_{D}\rightarrow f(\cdot^{-})\big|_{D}

in
L^{2}(D)
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, where

{\rm Aver}_{\varepsilon,+}f(u)=\frac{1}{(2\varepsilon)^{d-1}\varepsilon}\int\limits_{-\varepsilon<v_{i}<\varepsilon\text{~for~}2\leq i\leq d,\atop 0<v_{1}<\varepsilon}f(u+v)dv

and

{\rm Aver}_{\varepsilon,-}f(u)=\frac{1}{(2\varepsilon)^{d-1}\varepsilon}\int\limits_{-\varepsilon<v_{i}<\varepsilon\text{~for~}2\leq i\leq d,\atop-\varepsilon<v_{1}<0}f(u+v)dv

for any
u\in\{u_{1}=0\}
.

We use
\mathcal{C}
to denote the set of functions
H
such that

H(u)=H^{+}(u)1_{\{u_{1}>0\}}+H^{-}(u)1_{\{u_{1}\leq 0\}}

for some
H^{+},H^{-}\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, for any
H\in\mathcal{C}
and
u\in\{u_{1}=0\}
, define

H(u^{+})=H^{+}(u),\;H(u^{-})=H^{-}(u),\;\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}H(u)=\partial_{u_{1}}H^{+}(u),\;\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}H(u)=\partial_{u_{1}}H^{-}(u).

Note that functions in
\mathcal{C}
may be discontinuous at the boundary
\{u_{1}=0\}
.

Definition 2.2

(Hydrodynamic equation for
\beta=1
)
.

Let
\rho_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]
be continuous. A bounded function
\rho:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be a weak solution of the heat equation with Robin boundary condition

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,u)=\Delta\rho(t,u),\quad&t>0,\;u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u_{1}=0\}\\
\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}\rho(t,u)=\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}\rho(t,u)=\alpha(\rho(t,u^{+})-\rho(t,u^{-})),\quad&u_{1}=0,\\
\rho(0,u)=\rho_{0}(u),\quad&u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

if
\rho\in L^{2}([0,T],{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u_{1}=0\}))
, and for any function
H\in\mathcal{C}
, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,u)H(u)du=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(u)H(u)du\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(s,u)\sum_{i=2}^{d}\partial_{u_{i}}^{2}H(u)du\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{u_{1}\neq 0\}}\rho(s,u)\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(u)du\,ds\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{u_{1}=0\}}\big\{\rho_{s}(u^{+})\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}H(u)-\rho_{s}(u^{-})\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}H(u)+\alpha[\rho_{s}(u^{-})-\rho_{s}(u^{+})][H(u^{+})-H(u^{-})]\big\}\,dS\,ds.

Definition 2.3

(Hydrodynamic equation for
\beta>1
)
.

Let
\rho_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]
be continuous. A bounded function
\rho:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be a weak solution of the heat equation with Neumann boundary condition

(2.6)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\rho(t,u)=\Delta\rho(t,u),\quad&t>0,\;u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u_{1}=0\}\\
\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}\rho(t,u)=\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}\rho(t,u)=0,\quad&u_{1}=0,\\
\rho(0,u)=\rho_{0}(u),\quad&u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

if
\rho\in L^{2}([0,T],{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u_{1}=0\}))
, and for any function
H\in\mathcal{C}
, for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,u)H(u)du=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(u)H(u)du\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(s,u)\sum_{i=2}^{d}\partial_{u_{i}}^{2}H(u)du\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{u_{1}\neq 0\}}\rho(s,u)\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(u)du\,ds\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\{u_{1}=0\}}\big\{\rho_{s}(u^{+})\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}H(u)-\rho_{s}(u^{-})\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}H(u)\big\}\,dS\,ds.

Remark 2.4

(Uniqueness of weak solutions)
.

It is well known that the weak solution to the heat equation (
2.3
) is unique. Following the ideas in
[
6
, Section 7.2]
, it is easy to prove the weak solutions to the above PDEs (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) are unique. We also refer the readers to
[
8
, Section 7]
for the uniqueness of weak solutions to the above PDEs if the underlying space is the torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
instead of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Throughout the article, we assume the initial distribution
\mu_{N}
of the process satisfies the following condition.

Assumption (A)
.

Fix an initial density profile
\rho_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]
such that
\rho_{0}
has continuous and bounded partial derivative with respect to the first coordinate
u_{1}
, and that
\rho_{0}
is a global Lipschitz function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Assume that under
\mu_{N}
,
\{\eta(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
are independent, and that

\mu_{N}(\eta(x)=1)=\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)

for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Let
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the accelerated process with generator
N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}
and with initial condition
\mu_{N}
. To make notations short, we omit the dependence of the process
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
N
. Denote by
\mathbb{P}
the probability measure on the path space
D([0,T],\Omega_{d})
associated with the process
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and the initial distribution
\mu_{N}
, and by
\mathbb{E}
the corresponding expectation. Note that we also omit the dependence of
\mathbb{P}
(resp.
\mathbb{E}
) on
N
and
\mu_{N}
for short.

Now we are ready to state the hydrodynamic limits for the voter model with a slow membrane.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume
d\geq 4
. For any
t\in[0,T]
, any
\varepsilon>0
and any
H\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\eta_{t}(x)H(\tfrac{x}{N})-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,u)H(u)du\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=0,

where
\rho(t,u)
is the unique weak solution

(i)

to the heat equation (
2.3
) if
0\leq\beta<1
;

(ii)

to the heat equation with Robin boundary condition (
2.5
) if
\beta=1
;

(iii)

to the heat equation with Neumann boundary condition (
2.6
) if
\beta>1
.

2.3.
Fluctuations.

In this subsection, we state nonequilibrium fluctuations for the voter model with a slow membrane. We first introduce the space of test functions for the density fluctuation field.

Definition 2.6

(The space of test functions)
.

(1) For
0<\beta<1
, let
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the usual Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(2) For
\beta=1
, let
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the family of functions
H\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u_{1}=0\})
such that

\partial_{u_{1}}^{+,2k+1}H(t,u)=\partial_{u_{1}}^{-,2k+1}H(t,u)=\alpha\big[\partial_{u_{1}}^{+,2k}H(t,u)-\partial_{u_{1}}^{-,2k}H(t,u)\big],\;\forall u_{1}=0,\;\forall k\geq 0.

(3) For
\beta>1
, let
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the family of functions
H\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u_{1}=0\})
such that

\partial_{u_{1}}^{+,2k+1}H(t,u)=\partial_{u_{1}}^{-,2k+1}H(t,u)=0,\;\forall u_{1}=0,\;\forall k\geq 0.

Note that, according to above definitions, the semigroup
T_{\beta,t}
associated to the corresponding hydrodynamic equations can be considered as an operator from
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\mathcal{S}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
\beta>0
(
cf.

[
4
, Proposition 2.3]
), which is crucial for us later to define a generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process as the fluctuation limit of our process.

Define the fluctuation field
{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t}
, which acts on functions
H\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as

{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t}(H)=\frac{1}{N^{1+d/2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\bar{\eta}_{t}(x)H(\tfrac{x}{N})=N^{d/2-1}\Big(\pi^{N}_{t}(H)-\mathbb{E}\big[\pi^{N}_{t}(H)\big]\Big),

where
\bar{\eta}_{t}(x)=\eta_{t}(x)-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{t}(x)]
. Next, we introduce the limit of the fluctuation field, which is given by a martingale problem.

Definition 2.7

(The limiting fluctuation field)
.

We say
\mathcal{Y}_{\cdot}\in C([0,T],\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is a solution to the following generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process

(2.7)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}=\Delta{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}+\dot{{\mathcal{W}}}_{t},\\
{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}=0,\end{cases}

if for any
H\in\mathcal{S}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(H)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Y}}_{t}(H)-\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{Y}}_{s}(\Delta H)ds,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{t}(H)

\displaystyle=\big[{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(H)\big]^{2}-4d(1-\gamma_{d})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(s,u)(1-\rho(s,u))H^{2}(u)duds

are both martingales. Above,
\rho(t,u)
is the unique solution to the corresponding hydrodynamic equation defined in the last subsection, and
\dot{{\mathcal{W}}}_{t}
is inhomogeneous space-time white noise with mean zero and covariance given by

E[{\mathcal{W}_{t}}(H){\mathcal{W}}_{s}(G)]=4d(1-\gamma_{d})\int_{0}^{t\wedge s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\tau,u)(1-\rho(\tau,u)H(u)G(u)dud\tau

for
H,G\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\gamma_{d}
is the probability that the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
starting at
0
returns to
0
again.

Remark 2.8
.

We refer the readers to
[
4
, Proposition 2.7]
for a detailed proof of the uniqueness of the solution to the generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (
2.7
). Here we give an outline. Roughly speaking, since
\{{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(H)\}_{t\geq 0},\{{\mathcal{N}}_{t}(H)\}_{t\geq 0}
are martingales, via Itô’s formula we can deduce that

\displaystyle E\left(e^{\sqrt{-1}{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}(H)}\Big|{\mathcal{Y}}_{r},r\leq s\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sqrt{-1}{\mathcal{Y}}_{s}\left(T_{\beta,t-s}H\right)-\frac{4d(1-\gamma_{d})}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\tau,u)(1-\rho(\tau,u))\left(T_{\beta,t-\tau}H(u)\right)^{2}dud\tau\right)

for any
s<t
, where
\{T_{\beta,t}H\}_{t\geq 0}
is the solution to the heat equation with corresponding boundary condition and initial condition
H
. Hence
\{{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is uniquely distributed.

The following is the second main result of the article.

Theorem 2.9
.

Assume
\beta=1
or
\beta>3/2
. The sequence of the processes
\{{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
, converges in distribution, as
N\rightarrow\infty
, with respect to the Skorohod topology of
D([0,T],\mathcal{S}_{\beta}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to
\{{\mathcal{Y}}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}
, which is the unique solution to the generalized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process (
2.7
).

Remark 2.10
.

The above results should also hold for
0<\beta<1
and
1<\beta\leq 3/2
. However, this is beyond the techniques of the present article. See Remark
5.1
and
5.3
for details.

According to Remark
2.8
, we have the following corollary of Theorem
2.9
.

Corollary 2.11
.

Assume
\beta=1
or
\beta>3/2
. For any
H\in\mathcal{S}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
t>0
,
{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t}(H)
converges in distribution to

{\mathcal{N}}\left(0,4d(1-\gamma_{d})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(\tau,u)(1-\rho(\tau,u))\left(T_{\beta,t-\tau}H(u)\right)^{2}dud\tau\right)

as
N\rightarrow+\infty
, where
{\mathcal{N}}(\mu,\sigma^{2})
is the normal distribution with mean
\mu
and variance
\sigma^{2}
.

3.
Preliminaries

3.1.
Duality

In this subsection we recall the duality relationship introduced in
[
9
]
. Let
\{Y_{t,\beta}^{N}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the random walk on the one dimensional integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}
with generator
\Omega_{N,\beta}
given by

\Omega_{N,\beta}f(i)=\begin{cases}f(i+1)-f(i)+f(i-1)-f(i)&\text{~if~}i\neq 0,1,\\
\alpha N^{-\beta}\left[f(1)-f(0)\right]+f(-1)-f(0)&\text{~if~}i=0,\\
\alpha N^{-\beta}\left[f(0)-f(1)\right]+f(2)-f(1)&\text{~if~}i=1\end{cases}

for every
f:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
. Roughly speaking, the above random walk jumps to its neighbor at rate one everywhere except across the bond
(0,1)
, where the rate is
\alpha N^{-\beta}
. We further denote by
\{U_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
the usual simple symmetric random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
, i.e., the generator of
U_{t}
is given by
\Omega_{N,\beta}
with
(\alpha,\beta)
replaced by
(1,0)
. Let
\{X_{t,\beta}^{N}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that
\{\{X_{t,\beta}^{N}(i)\}_{t\geq 0}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
are independent,
\{X_{t,\beta}^{N}(1)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a copy of
\{Y_{t,\beta}^{N}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{X_{t,\beta}^{N}(i)\}_{t\geq 0}
is a copy of
\{U_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
for
i=2,3,\ldots,d
, where
X_{t,\beta}^{N}(i)
is the
i
-th coordinate of
X_{t,\beta}^{N}
. We write
X_{t,\beta}^{N}
as
X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}
when
X_{0,\beta}^{N}=x
.

For given
s>0
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we denote by
\{\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,x,s}\}_{t\geq 0}
the
frozen
random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that
\hat{X}_{u,\beta}^{N,x,s}=x
for
u\leq s
and
\{\hat{X}_{s+t,\beta}^{N,x,s}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an independent copy of
\{{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,x}\}_{t\geq 0}
. Intuitively, the random walk
\{\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,x,s}\}_{t\geq 0}
stays frozen at site
x
until time
s
, and then performs the random walk as
\{{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,x}\}_{t\geq 0}
after time
s
. For given
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},s>0
and
\{X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}\}_{t\geq 0},\{\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,s}\}_{t\geq 0}
which are independent, we define

\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,s}=\inf\{u\geq s:~X_{u,\beta}^{N,x}=\hat{X}^{N,y,s}_{u,\beta}\}-s,

i.e.,
\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,s}
is the time it takes for
X^{N,x}_{\cdot,\beta}
and
\hat{X}^{N,y,s}_{\cdot,\beta}
to meet after
\hat{X}^{N,y,s}_{\cdot,\beta}
is unfrozen from
y
.

For given
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
s>0
, we denote by
\{\tilde{X}_{t,\beta,x}^{N,y,s}\}_{t\geq 0}
the random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that

\tilde{X}_{t,\beta,x}^{N,y,s}=\begin{cases}\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,s}&\text{~if~}t\leq s+\tau^{N,\beta,s}_{x,y},\\
X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}&\text{~if~}t>s+\tau^{N,\beta,s}_{x,y}.\end{cases}

Note that
\{\tilde{X}_{t,\beta,x}^{N,y,s}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,s}\}_{t\geq 0}
have the same distribution but
\{\tilde{X}_{t,\beta,x}^{N,y,s}\}_{t\geq 0}
is not independent of
\{X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}\}_{t\geq 0}
. The process
\left\{\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x},\tilde{X}_{t,\beta,x}^{N,y,s}\right)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is the so-called
coalescing
random walk.

To distinguish from the accelerated voter model, let
\{\tilde{\eta}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the process with generator given by
\mathcal{L}_{N}
. According to the duality-relationship between the voter model and the coalescing random walk given in
[
9
]
, for given
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
t>s
,

(3.1)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{t}(x)=1\right)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=u\right){\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\eta}_{0}(u)=1),

and

(3.2)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{t}(x)=\tilde{\eta}_{s}(y)=1\right)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=u,\tilde{X}_{t,\beta,x}^{N,y,t-s}=v\right){\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\eta}_{0}(u)=\tilde{\eta}_{0}(v)=1)\\
=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=u,\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,t-s}\leq s\right)P(\tilde{\eta}_{0}(u)=1)\\
+\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{v\neq u}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=u,\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,t-s}=v,\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,t-s}>s\right){\mathbb{P}}(\tilde{\eta}_{0}(u)=\tilde{\eta}_{0}(v)=1).

Above, we also use
{\mathbb{P}}
to denote the law of the process
\{\tilde{\eta}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
and the random walks since this will not cause confusion. We refer the readers to
[
9
]
or
[
12
, Section 3.4]
for proofs of the above duality relations.

3.2.
Invariance principle

In this subsection we recall the invariance principle given in
[
3
]
of the random walk
\{Y_{t,\beta}^{N}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{Z}
with slow bond
(0,1)
. We denote by
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
the
1
-dimensional standard Brownian motion and write
B_{t}
as
B_{t}^{u}
when
B_{0}=u
. For any
\beta\geq 0
, we define
\{B_{t,\beta}\}_{t\geq 0}
as follows. If
0\leq\beta<1
, then
B_{t,\beta}=B_{t}
, i.e., the standard Brownian motion. When
\beta>1
, then
B_{t,\beta}=|B_{t}^{u}|
when
B_{0,\beta}=u\in[0^{+},\infty)
and
B_{t,\beta}=-|B_{t}^{u}|
when
B_{0,\beta}=u\in(-\infty,0^{-}]
, i.e.,
B_{t,\beta}
is the reflected Brownian motion. When
\beta=1
, then
B_{t,\beta}
is the
snapping out
Brownian motion with parameter
2\alpha
introduced in
[
11
]
. More precisely, for every
u\in(-\infty,0^{-}]\cup[0^{+},+\infty)
and
f\in C_{b}\left((-\infty,0^{-}]\cup[0^{+},+\infty)\right)
,

\mathbb{E}_{u}[f(B_{t,\beta})]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{t}}}{2}\right)f\left({\rm sgn}(u)|B_{t}^{u}|\right)+\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\alpha L_{t}}}{2}\right)f\left(-{\rm sgn}(u)|B_{t}^{u}|\right)\right],

where
L_{t}
is the local time of
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
at point
0
. We write
B_{t,\beta}
(resp.
Y_{t,\beta}^{N}
) as
B_{t,\beta}^{u}
(resp.
Y_{t,\beta}^{N,u}
) when
B_{0,\beta}=u
(resp.
Y_{0,\beta}^{N}=u
). The following invariance principle is proved by Erhard
et al.
in
[
3
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
3
, Theorem 2.2]
)
.

The following invariance principle holds for the one dimensional symmetric random walk
\{Y_{t,\beta}^{N}\}_{t\geq 0}
with a slow bond
(0,1)
,

(i)

for every
u\neq 0
and
t\geq 0
,
\frac{Y_{tN^{2},\beta}^{N,\lfloor uN\rfloor}}{N}
converges weakly to
B_{2t,\beta}^{u}
as
N\rightarrow+\infty
;

(ii)

for every
x\in\{1,2,\ldots\}
and
t\geq 0
,
\frac{Y_{tN^{2},\beta}^{N,x}}{N}
converges weakly to
B_{2t,\beta}^{0^{+}}
as
N\rightarrow+\infty
;

(iii)

for every
x\in\{-1,-2,\ldots\}
and
t\geq 0
,
\frac{Y_{tN^{2},\beta}^{N,x}}{N}
converges weakly to
B_{2t,\beta}^{0^{-}}
as
N\rightarrow+\infty
.

Note that although statements
(ii)
and
(iii)
in the above theorem are not listed in the main theorem of
[
3
]
, they are direct corollaries of Lemma 5.2 and Eq. (5.11) of
[
3
]
.

4.
Hydrodynamic limits

4.1.
Proof Outline

In this subsection, we outline the proof of Theorem
2.5
. The procedures are quite standard (
cf.

[
10
, Chapter 4]
for example), and the main ingredients are the replacement lemmas proved in Subsection
4.2
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of Radon measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with the vague topology, that is, for a sequence
\{\nu^{N}\}_{N\geq 1},\,\nu\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\nu^{N}\rightarrow\nu
as
N\rightarrow\infty
if for every
f\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\big\langle\nu^{N},f\big\rangle=\big\langle\nu,f\big\rangle.

For a configuration
\eta\in\Omega_{d}
, define the empirical measure
\pi^{N}(\eta)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

\pi^{N}(\eta;du)=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\eta(x)\delta_{x/N}(du).

Put
\pi^{N}_{t}(du)=\pi^{N}(\eta_{t};du)
. Let
Q^{N}
be the distribution on the path space
D([0,T],{\mathcal{M}}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
associated to the process
\pi^{N}_{t}
and the initial measure
\mu_{N}
.

By Lemma
4.2
, the sequence
\{Q^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
is tight. Whence, any subsequence of
Q^{N}
further has a subsequence that converges as
N\rightarrow\infty
, whose limit is denoted by
Q^{*}
. Moreover,
Q^{*}
is concentrated on trajectories which are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Denote by
\rho(t,u)
the corresponding density.

Now we characterize the limit. Fix a test function
H:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which will be specified later depending on whether
\beta<1
or
\beta\geq 1
. By Dynkin’s martingale formula,

(4.1)

\pi^{N}_{t}(H)=\pi^{N}_{0}(H)+\int_{0}^{t}N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}\big\langle\pi^{N}_{s},H\big\rangle ds+M^{N}_{t}(H),

where
M^{N}_{t}(H)
is a martingale, whose quadratic variation is given by

\int_{0}^{t}\big\{N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}\big\langle\pi^{N}_{s},H\big\rangle^{2}-2N^{2}\big\langle\pi^{N}_{s},H\big\rangle\mathcal{L}_{N}\big\langle\pi^{N}_{s},H\big\rangle\big\}\,ds.

A simple computation shows that the last line is bounded by
C_{H}N^{2-d}
for some finite constant
C_{H}
, hence, by Doob’s inequality,

(4.2)

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(M^{N}_{t}(H)\big)^{2}\Big]=0.

A tedious but elementary computation shows that the integrand in Eq. (
4.1
) is equal to

(4.3)

\displaystyle N^{-d}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=2}^{d}\eta_{s}(x)\partial_{u_{i}}^{2}H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)+N^{-d}\sum_{x_{1}\neq 0,1}\eta_{s}(x)\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)+o_{N}(1)

(4.4)

\displaystyle+N^{1-d}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\eta_{s}(x)\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)-N^{1-d}\sum_{x_{1}=0}\eta_{s}(x)\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)

(4.5)

\displaystyle+\alpha N^{2-d-\beta}\sum_{x_{1}=0}\eta_{s}(x)\Big(H\big(\tfrac{x+e_{1}}{N}\big)-H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)\Big)+\alpha N^{2-d-\beta}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\eta_{s}(x)\Big(H\big(\tfrac{x-e_{1}}{N}\big)-H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)\Big).

For a configuration
\eta
and a positive integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define the space average of
\eta
over a box of size
k
centered at
x
as

\bar{\eta}^{k}(x)=\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{d}}\sum_{|y-x|\leq k}\eta(y).

Similarly, the space averages over the right/left boxes are defined as

\bar{\eta}^{k,+}(x)=\frac{1}{|\Lambda^{+}_{x,k}|}\sum_{y\in\Lambda^{+}_{x,k}}\eta(y),\quad\bar{\eta}^{k,-}(x)=\frac{1}{|\Lambda^{-}_{x,k}|}\sum_{y\in\Lambda^{-}_{x,k}}\eta(y),

where

\Lambda^{+}_{x,k}=\{y:|y-x|\leq k,y_{1}\geq x_{1}\},\quad\Lambda^{-}_{x,k}=\{y:|y-x|\leq k,y_{1}\leq x_{1}\}.

Next we discuss the three cases respectively.

4.1.1.
The case
0\leq\beta<1

Take
H\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Whence

\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)=\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big).

By Lemma
4.1

(i)
, we could replace
\eta_{s}(x)
by
\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N}(x)
in (
4.4
). Note also that if
|x-y|=1
, then

|\bar{\eta}^{\varepsilon N}(x)-\bar{\eta}^{\varepsilon N}(y)|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon N}

for some finite constant
C
. Whence, the time integral of the term (
4.4
) converges in probability to zero as
N\rightarrow\infty
. The term (
4.5
) vanishes in the limit in the same way.
Therefore, the limit
Q^{*}
concentrates on trajectories whose densities
\rho(t,u)
satisfy

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,u)H(u)du=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(u)H(u)du+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(s,u)\Delta H(u)du\,ds

4.1.2.
The case
\beta=1

Take
H\in\mathcal{C}
. By Lemma
4.1

(ii)
, we could replace the time integral of the term (
4.4
) with the time integral of

N^{1-d}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,+}(x)\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)-N^{1-d}\sum_{x_{1}=0}\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,-}(x)\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big).

The term (
4.5
) is handled in the same way. Observe that

\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,+}(x)=\big\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\iota_{\varepsilon,x/N}^{+}\big\rangle+o_{N}(1),\quad\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,-}(x)=\big\langle\pi^{N}_{s},\iota_{\varepsilon,x/N}^{-}\big\rangle+o_{N}(1),

where

\displaystyle\iota_{\varepsilon,u}^{+}(v)=2^{-d+1}\varepsilon^{-d}\mathbbm{1}\{0\leq v_{1}-u_{1}\leq\varepsilon,|v_{i}-u_{i}|\leq\varepsilon,2\leq i\leq d\},

\displaystyle\iota_{\varepsilon,u}^{-}(v)=2^{-d+1}\varepsilon^{-d}\mathbbm{1}\{-\varepsilon\leq v_{1}-u_{1}\leq 0,|v_{i}-u_{i}|\leq\varepsilon,2\leq i\leq d\}.

Therefore, by Eq. (
2.4
), as
N\rightarrow\infty
and
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, the limit density
\rho(t,u)
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,u)H(u)du=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(u)H(u)du\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(s,u)\sum_{i=2}^{d}\partial_{u_{i}}^{2}H(u)du\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{u_{1}\neq 0}\rho(s,u)\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(u)du\,ds\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{u_{1}=0}\rho_{s}(u^{+})\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}H(u)-\rho_{s}(u^{-})\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}H(u)+\alpha(\rho_{s}(u^{-})-\rho_{s}(u^{+}))(H(u^{+})-H(u^{-}))\,dS\,ds.

4.1.3.
The case
\beta>1

Take
H\in\mathcal{C}
. This case is similar to the case
\beta=1
. The difference is that the term (
4.5
) converges in probability to zero as
N\rightarrow\infty
since
\beta>1
.
Therefore, the limit density
\rho(t,u)
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,u)H(u)du=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho_{0}(u)H(u)du\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(s,u)\sum_{i=2}^{d}\partial_{u_{i}}^{2}H(u)du\,ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{u_{1}\neq 0}\rho(s,u)\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(u)du\,ds\\
+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{u_{1}=0}\rho_{s}(u^{+})\partial_{u_{1}}^{+}H(u)-\rho_{s}(u^{-})\partial_{u_{1}}^{-}H(u)\,dS\,ds.

In Lemma
4.3
, we show that any limit
Q^{*}
of the sequence
\{Q^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
is concentrated on trajectories whose densities belong to the space
L^{2}([0,T],{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u_{1}=0\}))
if
\beta\geq 1
. Together with the above observations,
Q^{*}
is concentrated on trajectories whose densities are weak solutions to the corresponding hydrodynamic equations. Since the weak solution is unique, the limit
Q^{*}
is uniquely determined. This concludes the proof.

4.2.
Replacement Lemma

The aim of this subsection is to prove the following replacement lemma.

Lemma 4.1

(Replacement Lemma)
.

For every
\delta>0
,

(i)

if
0\leq\beta<1
, then for every
H\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{t}N^{1-d}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\big(\eta_{s}(x)-\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N}(x)\big)H\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)\,ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=0.

The same result holds with the summation over
\{x_{1}=1\}
replaced by over
\{x_{1}=0\}
.

(ii)

if
\beta\geq 1
, then for every
H\in\mathcal{C}

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\,\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\,\mathbb{P}\Big(\Big|\int_{0}^{t}N^{1-d}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\big(\eta_{s}(x)-\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,+}(x)\big)H\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)\,ds\Big|>\delta\Big)=0.

The same result holds with the summation over
\{x_{1}=1\}
replaced by over
\{x_{1}=0\}
, and with
\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,+}(x)
replaced by
\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,-}(x)
.

We only prove statement
(ii)
since
(i)
could be handled with in the same way.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
,
(ii)
.

By Chebyshev’s inequality, we only need to show that

(4.6)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(N^{1-d}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\left(\eta_{s}(x)-\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,+}(x)\right)H\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)ds\right)^{2}\right]=0.

We start with writing the expectation above as

{\rm I}^{2}+{\rm II}
, where

{\rm I}=\mathbb{E}\left[N^{1-d}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\left(\eta_{s}(x)-\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,+}(x)\right)H\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)ds\right]

and

{\rm II}={\rm Var}\left(N^{1-d}\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\left(\eta_{s}(x)-\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,+}(x)\right)H\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)ds\right).

Then, Eq. (
4.6
) holds if we can check that

(4.7)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm I}=0

and

(4.8)

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm II}=0

for every
\varepsilon>0
.

To check Eq. (
4.7
), we define

W_{t,\beta}=\left(W_{t,\beta}(1),W_{t,\beta}(2),\ldots,W_{t,\beta}(d)\right),

as the
\left((-\infty,0^{-}]\cup[0^{+},+\infty)\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
-valued stochastic process, where
\{\{W_{t,\beta}(i)\}_{t\geq 0}\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
are independent,
\{W_{t,\beta}(1)\}_{t\geq 0}
is an independent copy of
B_{2t,\beta}
introduced in the last subsection, and
W_{t,\beta}(i)
is an independent copy of
\{B_{2t}\}_{t\geq 0}
for
2\leq i\leq d
. We write
W_{t,\beta}
as
W_{t,\beta}^{u}
when
W_{0,\beta}=u
. By Eq. (
3.1
) and
Assumption (A)
,

\mathbb{E}[\eta_{s}(x)]=\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{X_{sN^{2},\beta}^{N,x}}{N}\right)\right]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[\bar{\eta}_{s}^{\varepsilon N,+}(x)]=\frac{1}{|\Lambda^{+}_{x,\varepsilon N}|}\sum_{y\in\Lambda^{+}_{x,\varepsilon N}}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{X_{sN^{2},\beta}^{N,y}}{N}\right)\right]

for every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Hence, by Theorem
3.1
,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm I}=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}H(0,u)\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(W_{s,\beta}^{(0^{+},u)}\right)\right]duds\\
-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}H(0,u)\left\{\frac{1}{\varepsilon(2\varepsilon)^{d-1}}\int_{v\in[0,\varepsilon]\times[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]^{d-1}}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(W_{s,\beta}^{(0^{+},u)+v}\right)\right]dv\right\}\,du\,ds,

According to the definition of
W_{t,\beta}
,

\lim_{v_{1}\downarrow 0,\atop v_{i}\rightarrow 0\text{~for~}2\leq i\leq d}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(W_{s,\beta}^{(0^{+},u)+v}\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(W_{s,\beta}^{(0^{+},u)}\right)\right]

for every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and hence Eq. (
4.7
) holds.

Now we check Eq. (
4.8
). According to the fact that the covariance operator is bilinear and
\eta_{t}=\tilde{\eta}_{tN^{2}}
, it is easy to check that Eq. (
4.8
) is a direct corollary of the following claim.

Claim
. For every
s>0
,

(4.9)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{N\geq 1,x,y\in Z^{d}}{\rm Cov}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{t}(x),\tilde{\eta}_{s}(y)\right)=0.

Now we prove the claim. By Eq. (
3.1
) and
Assumption (A)
, for
s<t
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{t}(x)=1\right){\mathbb{P}}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{s}(y)=1\right)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=u\right){\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{s,\beta}^{N,y}=v\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{u}{N}\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{v}{N}\right)\\
=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=u\right){\mathbb{P}}\left(\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,t-s}=v\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{u}{N}\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{v}{N}\right)\\
=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=u,\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,t-s}=v\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{u}{N}\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{v}{N}\right).

Then, by Eq. (
3.2
),

{\rm Cov}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{t}(x),\tilde{\eta}_{s}(y)\right)={\rm III}+{\rm IV}+{\rm V},

where

{\rm III}=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=u,\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,t-s}\leq s\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{u}{N}\right),

{\rm IV}=-\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{v\neq u}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=u,\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,t-s}=v,\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,t-s}\leq s\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{u}{N}\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{v}{N}\right),

and

{\rm V}=-\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}=\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,t-s}=u\right)\rho^{2}_{0}\left(\tfrac{u}{N}\right).

Since
\tilde{\eta}_{t}(x),\tilde{\eta}_{s}(y)
are positive correlated under
Assumption (A)
and
{\rm IV},{\rm V}\leq 0
,

0\leq{\rm Cov}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{t}(x),\tilde{\eta}_{s}(y)\right)\leq{\rm III}\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,t-s}<+\infty\right).

According to Markov property,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,t-s}<+\infty\right)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t-s,\beta}^{N,x}=u\right){\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau_{u,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right).

For any
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define
x^{\bot}=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
. Denote by
\{V_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
the symmetric simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
. For
\omega,\varpi\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
, let

p_{t}(\omega,\varpi)={\mathbb{P}}\left(V_{t}=\varpi\big|V_{0}=\omega\right),\quad\Gamma(\omega)={\mathbb{P}}\left(V_{t}=0\text{~for some~}t\geq 0\big|V_{0}=\omega\right).

According to the definition of
\{X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}\}_{t\geq 0}
,
\left\{\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}-\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,0}\right)^{\bot}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is a copy of
\{V_{2t}\}_{t\geq 0}
with
V_{0}=(x-y)^{\bot}
. As a result,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau_{u,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)\leq\Gamma\left((u-y)^{\bot}\right)
and hence

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,t-s}<+\infty\right)\leq\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t-s,\beta}^{N,x}=u\right)\Gamma\left((u-y)^{\bot}\right).

Without confusion, we also use
|\cdot|
to denote the
l^{1}
-norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
. Then
\lim_{|\omega|\rightarrow+\infty}\Gamma(\omega)=0
since
d-1\geq 3
. Therefore, for every
\delta>0
, there exists
M=M(\delta)
such that
\Gamma(\omega)\leq\delta
if
|\omega|>M
. As a result,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,t-s}<+\infty\right)\leq\delta+\sum_{u:|(u-y)^{\bot}|\leq M}{\mathbb{P}}\left(X_{t-s,\beta}^{N,x}=u\right).

Since
\left\{\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}\right)^{\bot}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is a copy of
\{V_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
with
V_{0}=x^{\bot}
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,t-s}<+\infty\right)\leq\delta+{\mathbb{P}}\left(|V_{t-s}-y^{\bot}|\leq M\big|V_{0}=x^{\bot}\right)\leq\delta+(2M+1)^{d-1}p_{t-s}(0,0).

As a result,

\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{N\geq 1,x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\rm Cov}\left(\tilde{\eta}_{t}(x),\tilde{\eta}_{s}(y)\right)\leq\delta+(2M+1)^{d-1}\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}p_{t-s}(0,0)=\delta.

Since
\delta
is arbitrary, Eq. (
4.9
) holds. As we have pointed out above, Eq. (
4.9
) implies Eq. (
4.8
) and the proof is completed.
∎

4.3.
Tightness.

The aim of this subsection is to prove the following tightness result.

Lemma 4.2

(Tightness)
.

The sequence
\{Q^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
is tight. Moreover, any limit point of
Q^{N}
is concentrated on trajectories which are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure.

Proof.

The proof of tightness is standard, and so we only sketch the proof. The second statement in the lemma follows directly from the fact that
|\eta(x)|\leq 1
for all
x
. To prove tightness, by
[
10
, Chapter 4]
, we only need to prove for any
H\in C^{2}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(4.10)

\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|\big\langle\pi^{N}_{t},H\big\rangle|\geq M\Big)=0

and for every
\varepsilon>0
,

(4.11)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t-s\leq\delta}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\big\langle\pi^{N}_{\tau},H\big\rangle d\tau\Big|\geq\varepsilon\Big)=0.

Eq. (
4.10
) follows from Chebyshev’s inequality and the fact that
|\big\langle\pi^{N}_{t},H\big\rangle|\leq C_{H}
for some finite constant
C_{H}
. To prove Eq. (
4.11
), we only need to prove it separately for the martingale
M^{N}_{t}(H)
defined in Eq. (
4.1
) and the terms (
4.3
)-(
4.5
). The martingale term satisfies (
4.11
) by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Eq. (
4.2
). Observe that all the terms (
4.3
)-(
4.5
) are bounded by
C_{H}
for some finite constant
C_{H}
, whence satisfy Eq. (
4.11
). This completes the proof.
∎

4.4.
Energy estimates.

In this subsection, we shall prove the following result.

Lemma 4.3

(Energy estimate)
.

Fix
\beta\geq 1
. Any limit
Q^{*}
of the sequence
\{Q^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
is concentrated on paths
\pi_{t}(du)=\rho(t,u)du
such that
\rho(t,u)\in L^{2}([0,T],{\mathcal{H}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{u_{1}=0\}))
.

By Lemma
4.2
,

Q^{*}(\pi_{t}(du)={\rho(t,u)}\,du\;\text{for all $0\leq t\leq T$})=1.

By
[
8
, Section 5.3]
, to prove Lemma
4.3
, we only need to prove the following result.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
M>1
, there exists a constant
K=K(M)<+\infty
such that

E_{Q^{*}}\left[\sup_{H}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{u_{1}}H)(s,u)\rho(s,u)duds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H^{2}(s,u)duds\right)\right]\leq K,

where the supremum is carried over all functions
H\in C^{0,1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with compact support contained in
[0,T]\times([-M,M]^{d}\backslash\{|u_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{M}\})
.

Based on the above lemma, the proof of Lemma
4.3
follows from the same analysis as that given in the proof of
[
8
, Lemma 5.7]
, where a crucial step is the utilization of Riesz’s representation theorem, the details of which we omit here.

To prove Lemma
4.4
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Q^{*}\left(\rho(t,u)=E_{Q^{*}}[\rho(t,u)]\text{~for all~}0\leq t\leq T\right)=1.

Proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Since
Q^{*}
is concentrated on càdlàg paths, we only need to show that

(4.12)

Q^{*}\left(\pi_{t}(H)=E_{Q^{*}}[\pi_{t}(H)]\right)=1

for every
0<t\leq T
and
H\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. To prove Eq. (
4.12
), we claim that

(4.13)

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm Var}\left(\pi_{t}^{N}(H)\right)=0

for every
0<t\leq T
and
H\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We first use Eq. (
4.13
) to prove Eq. (
4.12
) and then check Eq. (
4.13
). Note that
\pi_{\cdot}
is the weak limit of a subsequence of
\{\pi^{N}_{\cdot}\}_{N\geq 1}
but we still write this subsequence as
\{\pi^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
for simplicity. Since
\eta_{t}(x)\leq 1
,

|\pi_{t}^{N}(H)|\leq C\|H\|_{\infty}

for every
N\geq 1
, where
\|H\|_{\infty}=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|H(u)|
and
C<+\infty
is a constant only depending on the compact support of
H
. As a result, by dominated convergence theorem,

(4.14)

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}[\pi_{t}^{N}(H)]=E_{Q^{*}}[\pi_{t}(H)].

Then, Fatou’s lemma and Eq. (
4.13
) imply that

{\rm Var}_{Q^{*}}\left(\pi_{t}(H)\right)\leq\liminf_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\pi_{t}^{N}(H)\right)^{2}\right]-\left(E_{Q^{*}}[\pi_{t}(H)]\right)^{2}\\
=\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\pi_{t}^{N}(H)\right)^{2}\right]-\left(\mathbb{E}[\pi_{t}^{N}(H)]\right)^{2}\right)=\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm Var}(\pi_{t}^{N}(H))=0

and hence Eq. (
4.12
) holds.

Now we check Eq. (
4.13
). According to an analysis similar with that leading to Eq. (
4.9
),

{\rm Var}\left(\pi_{t}^{N}(H)\right)\leq\frac{1}{N^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|H\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right|\left|H\left(\frac{y}{N}\right)\right|{\rm Cov}(\eta_{t}(x),\eta_{t}(y))\\
\leq\frac{1}{N^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|H\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right|\left|H\left(\frac{y}{N}\right)\right|P\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)\\
\leq\frac{1}{N^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|H\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right|\left|H\left(\frac{y}{N}\right)\right|\Gamma\left((y-x)^{\bot}\right).

Since
d-1\geq 3
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
M=M(\varepsilon)<+\infty
that
\Gamma(u)<\varepsilon
when
|u|>M
. Since
H
is with compact support, there exists
C=C(H)\in[1,+\infty)
that
H(u)=0
when
|u_{1}|>C
. As a result, for sufficiently large
N
, the last formula is bounded by

\frac{\varepsilon}{N^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y:|(y-x)^{\bot}|>M}\left|H\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right|\left|H\left(\frac{y}{N}\right)\right|+\frac{1}{N^{2d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y:|(y-x)^{\bot}|\leq M,\|y_{1}\|\leq CN}\left|H\left(\frac{x}{N}\right)\right|\left|H\left(\frac{y}{N}\right)\right|\\
\leq C_{H}\varepsilon+C_{H}(2M+1)^{d-1}N^{1-d}.

This is enough to prove Eq. (
4.13
).
∎

At last, we prove Lemma
4.4
.

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

By Lemma
4.5
, we only need to show that

(4.15)

\sup_{H}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{u_{1}}H)(s,u)E_{Q^{*}}[\rho(s,u)]duds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H^{2}(s,u)duds\right)<+\infty

for each
M>0
, where the supremum is carried over all functions
H\in C^{0,1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
with compact support contained in
[0,T]\times([-M,M]^{d}\backslash\{|u_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{M}\})
.

Since
Q^{*}
is any weak limit of the sequence
\{Q^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
along some subsequence, which we still denote by
\{Q^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
for simplicity,

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{u_{1}}H)(s,u)E_{Q^{*}}[\rho(s,u)]duds=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{H\left(s,\frac{x}{N}+\delta e_{1}\right)-H\left(s,\tfrac{x}{N}\right)}{\delta}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{s}(x)]ds\\
=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}H\left(s,\tfrac{x}{N}\right)\frac{\mathbb{E}[\eta_{s}(x-N\delta e_{1})]-\mathbb{E}[\eta_{s}(x)]}{\delta}ds,

where
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
. Above, the first identify follows from Eq. (
4.14
) and the second from summation by parts.
By Eq. (
3.1
),

\mathbb{E}[\eta_{s}(x)]=\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{X_{sN^{2},\beta}^{N,x}}{N}\right)\right].

By Theorem
3.1
,

(4.16)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\partial_{u_{1}}H)(s,u)E_{Q^{*}}[\rho(s,u)]duds=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H(s,u)\frac{\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(W^{u-\delta e_{1}}_{s,\beta})]-\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(W^{u}_{s,\beta})]}{\delta}duds,

where
W_{s,\beta}
is defined as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
.

For all
\beta\geq 1
, we claim that there exists
C=C(\beta,\rho_{0},M)<+\infty
that

(4.17)

\left|\frac{\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(W^{u-\delta e_{1}}_{s,\beta})]-\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(W^{u}_{s,\beta})]}{\delta}\right|\leq C

for every
(u,s,\delta)
satisfying
|u_{1}|\in\left[\frac{1}{M},M\right],0\leq s\leq T
and
\delta<\frac{1}{2M}
. This is enough to prove Eq. (
4.15
) since by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we may bound the right-hand side of (
4.16
) by

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}H^{2}(s,u)duds+\frac{C^{2}}{2}T(2M)^{d}.

It remains to prove Eq. (
4.17
). Without loss of generality, we assume that
u_{1}\in\left[\frac{1}{M},M\right]
since the other part can be checked in the same way. If
\beta>1
, since
u_{1},u_{1}-\delta>0
when
\delta<\frac{1}{2M}
,

W_{s,\beta}^{u}=\left(|B_{2s}^{u_{1}}(1)|,B_{2s}^{u_{2}}(2),\ldots,B_{2s}^{u_{d}}(d)\right),\quad W_{s,\beta}^{u-\delta e_{1}}=\left(|B_{2s}^{u_{1}-\delta}(1)|,B_{2s}^{u_{2}}(2),\ldots,B_{2s}^{u_{d}}(d)\right)

according to the definition of
W_{s,\beta}
given in Section
4.2
, where
\{B_{t}(i):t\geq 0\}_{1\leq i\leq d}
are independent copies of standard Brownian motions and
B_{t}^{a}(i)
means
B_{0}(i)=a
for
1\leq i\leq d
. By translation invariance,

B_{2s}^{u_{1}}(1)=_{d}B_{2s}^{0}(1)+u_{1},\quad B_{2s}^{u_{1}-\delta}(1)=_{d}B_{2s}^{0}(1)+u_{1}-\delta.

By Lagrange’s mean value theorem,

\left|\frac{\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(W^{u-\delta e_{1}}_{s,\beta})]-\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(W^{u}_{s,\beta})]}{\delta}\right|\leq\|\partial_{u_{1}}\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}

and hence Eq. (
4.17
) holds for
\beta>1
.

When
\beta=1
,

W_{s,\beta}^{u}=\left(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}},B_{2s}^{u_{2}}(2),\ldots,B_{2s}^{u_{d}}(d)\right),\quad W_{s,\beta}^{u-\delta e_{1}}=\left(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}-\delta},B_{2s}^{u_{2}}(2),\ldots,B_{2s}^{u_{d}}(d)\right),

where
\{B_{t,1}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the snapping out Browning motion with parameter
2\alpha
. For any
u\in\mathbb{R}^{d},v\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
, let
f_{u^{\bot}}(t,v)
be the density function of
\left(B_{t}^{u_{2}}(2),B_{t}^{u_{3}}(3),\ldots,B_{t}^{u_{d}}(d)\right)
at
v
, then

(4.18)

\frac{\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(W^{u-\delta e_{1}}_{s,\beta})]-\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(W^{u}_{s,\beta})]}{\delta}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}-\delta},v\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}},v\right)\right]}{\delta}f_{u^{\bot}}(2s,v)dv.

As we have recalled in Section
4.2
,

\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}},v)]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{u_{1}}|,v\right)+\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(-|B_{2s}^{u_{1}}|,v\right)\right]\\
=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1})}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)+\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1})}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(-|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)\right]

where
L_{t}
is the local time of
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
at point
0
and
L_{t}(a)
is the local time of
\{B_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
at point
a
. Note that in the above equations we utilize the fact that
B_{t}^{a}
hits
0
if and only if
B_{t}^{0}
hits
-a
when we write
B_{t}^{a}
as
B_{t}^{0}+a
in the sense of coupling. Similarly, we may write
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{0}(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}-\delta},v)]
as

\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1}+\delta)}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}-\delta|,v\right)+\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1}+\delta)}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(-|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}-\delta|,v\right)\Bigg].

Hence,

\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}-\delta},v\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}},v\right)\right]={\rm I}+{\rm II},

where

{\rm I}=-\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1})}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1}+\delta)}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}-\delta|,v\right)\right]

and

{\rm II}=-\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1})}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(-|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1-e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1}+\delta)}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(-|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}-\delta|,v\right)\right].

We only calculate
{\rm I}
since
{\rm II}
can be calculated in the same way. For any
a\neq 0
, we define
\tau_{a}=\inf\{t:~B_{t}^{0}=a\}.
Let
p(t,a)
be the density function of
\tau_{a}
, then by strong Markov property,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1})}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1})}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{-u_{1}}\leq 2s\}}\right]\\
+\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s}(-u_{1})}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{-u_{1}}>2s\}}\right]\\
=\int_{0}^{2s}p(\theta,-u_{1})\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s-\theta}}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s-\theta}^{0}|,v\right)\right]d\theta+\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{-u_{1}}>2s\}}\right],

where we utilize the facts that
L_{t}(a)=0
when
t<\tau_{a}
and that
(L_{t}(-a),B_{t}^{-a}+a)
and
(L_{t},B_{t}^{0})
have the same distribution. As a result,

{\rm I}={\rm III}+{\rm IV},

where

{\rm III}=\int_{0}^{2s}\left(p(\theta,-u_{1}+\delta)-p(\theta,-u_{1})\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\frac{1+e^{-2\alpha L_{2s-\theta}}}{2}\right)\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s-\theta}^{0}|,v\right)\right]d\theta

and

{\rm IV}=\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}-\delta|,v\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{-u_{1}+\delta}>2s\}}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{-u_{1}}>2s\}}\right].

It is shown in
[
2
, Chapter 8]
that

p(\theta,a)=\frac{a}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-\frac{a^{2}}{2\theta}}\theta^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

As a result, there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(M)<+\infty
independent of
0\leq s\leq T
that

\int_{0}^{r}\left|\frac{d}{da}p(\theta,a)\right|d\theta<C_{1}

for any
0\leq r\leq 2T
and
\frac{1}{2M}\leq a\leq M
. Then, by Lagrange’s mean value theorem, for
\delta<\frac{1}{2M}
,

\frac{\left|{\rm III}\right|}{\delta}\leq C_{1}\|\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}

Now we deal with
{\rm IV}
. Since
\tau_{-u_{1}+\delta}<\tau_{-u_{1}}
for
\{B_{t}^{0}\}_{t\geq 0}
,

{\rm IV}={\rm V}+{\rm VI},

where

{\rm V}=-\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}-\delta|,v\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{-u_{1}}>2s>\tau_{-u_{1}+\delta}\}}\right]

and

{\rm VI}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}-\delta|,v\right)-\rho_{0}\left(|B_{2s}^{0}+u_{1}|,v\right)\right)\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{-u_{1}+\delta}>2s\}}\right].

According to the expression of
{\rm V}
and the definition of
p(t,a)
,

|{\rm V}|\leq\|\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{2s}\left|p(\theta,-u_{1}+\delta)-p(\theta,-u_{1})\right|d\theta

and hence
\frac{|{\rm V}|}{\delta}\leq C_{1}\|\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}
when
\delta<\frac{1}{2M}
by Lagrange’s mean value theorem.
Since
\left||x|-|y|\right|\leq|x-y|
, by Lagrange’s mean value theorem,

\frac{|{\rm VI}|}{\delta}\leq\|\partial_{u_{1}}\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}.

As a result,

(4.19)

\frac{|{\rm I}|}{\delta}\leq\frac{|{\rm III}|+|{\rm IV}|}{\delta}\leq 2C_{1}\|\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\partial_{u_{1}}\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}

when
\delta<\frac{1}{2M}
. Note that Eq. (
4.19
) still holds when
|{\rm I}|
is replaced by
|{\rm II}|
according to the same analysis. Therefore,

\left|\frac{\mathbb{E}\Big[\rho_{0}\left(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}-\delta},v\right)\Big]-\mathbb{E}\Big[\rho_{0}\left(B_{2s,1}^{u_{1}},v\right)\Big]}{\delta}\right|\leq 2\big(2C_{1}\|\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}+\|\partial_{u_{1}}\rho_{0}\|_{\infty}\big)

when
\delta<\frac{1}{2M}
and hence Eq. (
4.17
) follows from Eq. (
4.18
) when
\beta=1
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Nonequilibrium fluctuations

5.1.
Proof outline.

By Dynkin’s martingale formula,

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{t}(H)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t}(H)-{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{0}(H)-\int_{0}^{t}(N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}+\partial_{s}){\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{s}(H)ds,

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}^{N}_{t}(H)

\displaystyle=\big[{\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{t}(H)\big]^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}\big[{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{s}(H)\big]^{2}-2{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{s}(H)N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{s}(H)\Big\}ds

are both martingales. Similar to (
4.3
), (

) and (

), the integrand in the martingale

equals

\displaystyle(N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}+\partial_{s}){\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{s}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{1+d/2}}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{i=2}^{d}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)\partial_{u_{i}}^{2}H(\tfrac{x}{N})+\frac{1}{N^{1+d/2}}\sum_{x_{1}\neq 0,1}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(\tfrac{x}{N})+o_{N,p}(1)

Direct calculations show that the integrand in the expression of the martingale

In Subsection
5.3
, we prove the tightness of the sequences
\{{\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
and
\{{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\cdot}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{\cdot}
any limit points of
{\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{\cdot}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{\cdot}
along some subsequence respectively. For convenience, we still denote this subsequence by
\{N\}
. Next, we characterize the limit point
\mathcal{M}_{\cdot}
in different regimes of
\beta
.

5.1.1.
The case
\beta=1

Recall in this case, the function
H
satisfies

\partial_{u_{1}}^{+,2k+1}H(u)=\partial_{u_{1}}^{-,2k+1}H(u)=\alpha[\partial_{u_{1}}^{+,2k}H(u)-\partial_{u_{1}}^{-,2k}H(u)],\quad\forall u_{1}=0,\;\forall k\geq 0.

We claim that the time integral of the sum of (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) converges to zero in
L^{2}
. Indeed, by Taylor’s expansion,

\frac{N}{N^{d/2}}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)\big[H(\tfrac{x+e_{1}}{N})-H(\tfrac{x}{N})\big]-\frac{\alpha}{N^{d/2}}\sum_{x_{1}=0}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x+e_{1})\big[H(\tfrac{x+e_{1}}{N})-H(\tfrac{x}{N})\big]\\
=\frac{N}{N^{d/2}}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)\big[H(\tfrac{x+e_{1}}{N})-H(\tfrac{x}{N})\big]-\frac{\alpha}{N^{d/2}}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)\big[H(\tfrac{x}{N})-H(\tfrac{x-e_{1}}{N})\big]\\
=\frac{1}{N^{d/2}}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)\Big[\partial^{+}_{u_{1}}H(\tfrac{x-e_{1}}{N})-\alpha\big(H^{+}(\tfrac{x-e_{1}}{N})-H^{-}(\tfrac{x-e_{1}}{N})\big)\Big]+\frac{1}{2N^{d/2+1}}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)R_{1,H}(x),

where
R_{1,H}(x)=[4\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(\tfrac{\theta(x-e_{1},x+e_{1})}{N})-\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(\tfrac{\theta(x-e_{1},x)}{N})]-\alpha N[H(\tfrac{x}{N})-H^{+}(\tfrac{x-e_{1}}{N})]
for
x_{1}=0
. In the expression of
R_{1,H}
, we use
\theta(x,y)
to denote some point between
x
and
y
. Note that
R_{1,H}
is of order one. With the above choices of
H
, the first term in the last equation is zero, and by (
5.8
), the last term converges to zero in
L^{2}
. This proves the claim. Moreover, since
\eta(x)
is bounded, by (
5.2
),

\sup_{N\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big({\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{t}\big)^{2}\Big]\leq C

for some constant
C
. Thus, the martingale
{\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{t}
is uniformly integrable. This implies the limit
{\mathcal{M}}_{t}
is a martingale. Moreover, passing to the limit, we have

(5.6)

{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(H)={\mathcal{Y}}_{t}(H)-{\mathcal{Y}}_{0}(H)-\int_{0}^{t}{\mathcal{Y}}_{s}(\Delta H)ds.

For the martingale
{\mathcal{N}}^{N}_{t}(H)
, in Theorem
5.4
, we prove that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},\atop|x-y|=1}\xi^{N}_{x,y}(\eta_{s}(x)-\eta_{s}(y))^{2}H^{2}\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)ds\\
=4d(1-\gamma_{d})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(s,u)(1-\rho(s,u))H^{2}(u)duds

in
L^{2}
. Since
{\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{t}(H)
converges to
\mathcal{M}_{t}(H)
in distribution,
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{N}(H)
also converges in distribution, and denote the limit by
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}
. Passing to the limit,

(5.7)

{\mathcal{N}}_{t}(H)=\big[{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(H)\big]^{2}-4d(1-\gamma_{d})\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(s,u)(1-\rho(s,u))H^{2}(u)duds.

Similarly, one could also prove that
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{N}(H)
is uniformly integrable, implying
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}(H)
is a martingale. This concludes the proof for the case
\beta=1
.

5.1.2.
The case
\beta>3/2

Recall in this case we take
H
to be such that

\partial_{u_{1}}^{+,2k+1}H(t,u)=\partial_{u_{1}}^{-,2k+1}H(t,u)=0,\quad\forall u_{1}=0,\;\forall k\geq 0.

Then, by Taylor’s expansion, we could write (
5.4
) as

\frac{{1}}{2N^{1+d/2}}\sum_{x_{1}=1}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)R_{2,H}(x)+\frac{1}{2N^{1+d/2}}\sum_{x_{1}=0}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)R_{3,H}(x),

where
R_{2,H}(x)=[4\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(\tfrac{\theta(x-e_{1},x+e_{1})}{N})-\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(\tfrac{\theta(x-e_{1},x)}{N})]
for
x_{1}=1
, and
R_{3,H}(x)=\partial_{u_{1}}^{2}H(\tfrac{\theta(x-e_{1},x)}{N})
for
x_{1}=0
. By (
5.8
), the term (
5.4
) vanishes in the limit. Using (
5.8
) again, the variance of the time integral of the term (
5.5
) is bounded by
CN^{3-2\beta}
. Thus, the term (
5.5
) also vanishes in the limit since
\beta>3/2
. Thus, passing to the limit,
{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(H)
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}(H)
are still given by (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) respectively. Similar to the case
\beta=1
, one could also check directly that
{\mathcal{M}}_{t}^{N}(H)
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}^{N}(H)
are uniformly integrable, implying
{\mathcal{M}}_{t}(H)
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{t}(H)
are martingales. This concludes the proof for the case
\beta>3/2
.

Remark 5.1
.

For the case
0<\beta<1
, one needs to prove that for any

,

5.2.

The main aim of this subsection is to prove Theorems

Theorem 5.2
.

Under
Assumption (A)
,

(5.8)

{\rm Var}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\sum_{x_{1}=0}\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)H\big(\tfrac{x}{N}\big)ds\Big)\leq CN^{d+1}.

Remark 5.3
.

We believe the above bound is not sharp, and the correct bound should be
CN^{d}
. If this is the case, Theorem
2.9
also holds for
1<\beta\leq 3/2
.

Proof.

We first develop the left side in (
5.8
) as

2\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}\sum_{x,y:x_{1}=0,y_{1}=0}H(\tfrac{x}{N})H(\tfrac{y}{N})\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)\bar{\eta}_{s^{\prime}}(y)\big]\,ds^{\prime}\,ds.

For
s^{\prime}<s
, following the proof of (
4.9
),

\mathbb{E}\big[\bar{\eta}_{s}(x)\bar{\eta}_{s^{\prime}}(y)\big]\leq\mathbb{P}\big(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,(s-s^{\prime})N^{2}}<+\infty\big)\leq\Gamma\big((x-y)^{\bot}\big).

Recall for
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
,
\Gamma(z)
is the probability of the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
ever hitting the origin when starting from
z
. It is well known that
\Gamma(z)\leq C|z|^{2-(d-1)}
for some constant
C
. Thus, we could bound the left side in (

) by

Ct^{2}\sum_{x,y:x_{1}=0,y_{1}=0}H(\tfrac{x}{N})H(\tfrac{y}{N})|(x-y)^{\bot}|^{3-d}\leq Ct^{2}N^{d+1}

for some constant
C=C(H)
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

It is easy to check that Theorem

Lemma 5.5
.

Under

, for any

Lemma 5.6
.

Under
Assumption (A)
, for any
\beta\geq 0
,

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm Var}\left(\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\eta_{s}(x)\eta_{s}(y)H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)\right)=0.

Proof of Lemma
5.5
.

According to
Assumption (A)
and Equation (

),

\mathbb{E}\left[\eta_{s}(x)\eta_{s}(y)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\Big(\tfrac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\Big)1_{\{\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}\leq sN^{2}\}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{\hat{X}^{N,y,0}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)1_{\{\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}>sN^{2}\}}\right].

Thus,

(5.9)

\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\mathbb{E}[\eta_{s}(x)\eta_{s}(y)]H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)={\rm I}+{\rm II},

where

{\rm I}=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\Big(\tfrac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\Big)1_{\{\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}\leq sN^{2}\}}\right]H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)

As a result, to complete this proof, we only need to show that

(5.10)

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm I}=2d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\gamma_{d}\rho_{s}(u)H^{2}(u)du

(5.11)

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm II}=2d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\gamma_{d})\rho_{s}^{2}(u)H^{2}(u)du.

We first check Equation (
5.10

4.2
,
\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}-\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,0}\right)^{\perp}=V_{2t}^{(x-y)^{\perp}}
, where
\{V_{t}^{v}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
with
V_{0}=v
for each
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
. As a result, for any
K>0
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|y-x|=1}{\mathbb{P}}\left(K\leq\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)\leq\sup_{|v|\leq 1}{\mathbb{P}}\left(V_{2t}^{v}=0\text{~for some~}t\geq K\right).

Since
d-1\geq 3
,
\{V_{2t}\}_{t\geq 0}

1

\lim_{K\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{|v|\leq 1}{\mathbb{P}}\left(V_{2t}^{v}=0\text{~for some~}t\geq K\right)=0,

\lim_{K\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|y-x|=1}{\mathbb{P}}\left(K\leq\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)=0.

Therefore, for any given
\varepsilon>0

K_{1}=K_{1}(\varepsilon)>0

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|y-x|=1}\mathbb{P}\left(K_{1}\leq\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)\leq\varepsilon.

N

{\rm I}=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\Big(\tfrac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\Big)1_{\{\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}\leq K_{1}\}}\right]H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)+\varepsilon_{1},

Since

jumps at rate at most

K>0

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq K_{1}}\left|X^{N,x}_{t,\beta}-x\right|\geq K\right)\leq{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{K}T_{i}\leq K_{1}\right),

where

. Therefore,

\lim_{K\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq K_{1}}\left|X^{N,x}_{t,\beta}-x\right|\geq K\right)=0.

K_{2}=K_{2}(\varepsilon)>0

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq K_{1}}\left|X^{N,x}_{t,\beta}-x\right|\geq K_{2}\right)\leq\varepsilon.

On the event
\{\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}\leq K_{1}\}

. Thus,

(5.12)

{\rm I}=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\tfrac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)1_{\left\{\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}\leq K_{1}\right\}}1_{\left\{\left|X^{N,x}_{\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0},\beta}-x\right|\leq K_{2}\right\}}\right]H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)+\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}

when

Since

is integrable, we can choose

. Since
\rho_{0}
is global Lipschitz, according to Theorem

, let
X_{t,0}^{N,x},\hat{X}_{t,0}^{N,y,0}
be defined as in Section
4.2
with
\alpha=1
, i.e.,
\{X_{t,0}^{N,x}\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\hat{X}_{t,0}^{N,y,0}\}_{t\geq 0}
are independent simple random walks on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
starting at
x
and
y
respectively. Since
\{X_{t,0}^{N,x}-\hat{X}_{t,0}^{N,y,0}\}_{t\geq 0}
is an accelerated version of the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
starting at a neighbor of
0
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,0,0}<+\infty\right)=\gamma_{d},

where
\tau_{x,y}^{N,0,0}
is the collision time of
X_{t,0}^{N,x}
and
\hat{X}_{t,0}^{N,y,0}
as defined in Section
4.2
. Hence,

(5.16)

\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)-\gamma_{d}\right|=\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,0,0}<+\infty\right)\right|.

Let

\psi_{x,y}^{N}=\inf\left\{t:~\left(X_{t,0}^{N,x}\right)_{1}\in\{0,1\}\text{~or~}\left(\tilde{X}^{N,y,0}_{t,0,x}\right)_{1}\in\{0,1\}\right\},

then
\left\{\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x},\tilde{X}_{t,\beta,x}^{N,y,0}\right)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\left\{\left(X_{t,0}^{N,x},\tilde{X}_{t,0,x}^{N,y,0}\right)\right\}_{t\geq 0}
can be coupled in the same probability space such that

\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x},\tilde{X}_{t,\beta,x}^{N,y,0}\right)=\left(X_{t,0}^{N,x},\tilde{X}_{t,0,x}^{N,y,0}\right)

for
0\leq t\leq\psi_{x,y}^{N}
. As a result, for any
K>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<K,\psi_{x,y}^{N}\geq K\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,0,0}<K,\psi_{x,y}^{N}\geq K\right).

Therefore,

(5.17)

\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)-\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,0,0}<+\infty\right)\right|\leq{\rm III}+{\rm IV},

where

{\rm III}=\mathbb{P}\left(K\leq\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(K\leq\tau_{x,y}^{N,0,0}<+\infty\right)

and

{\rm IV}=2\mathbb{P}\left(\psi_{x,y}^{N}<K\right).

Let
\{U_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{1}
defined as in Section
3
with
U_{0}=0
, then for
x,y
such that
|x-y|=1
,

\mathbb{P}\left(\psi_{x,y}^{N}<K\right)\leq 2\mathbb{P}\left(\max_{0\leq t\leq K}|U_{t}|\geq|x_{1}|-2\right),

which converges to
0
as
|x_{1}|\rightarrow+\infty
. Hence,

(5.18)

\lim_{|x_{1}|\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{N\geq 1,x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}{\rm IV}=0.

Since
\left(X_{t,\beta}^{N,x}-\hat{X}_{t,\beta}^{N,y,0}\right)^{\perp}=V_{2t}^{(x-y)^{\perp}}
for any
\beta\geq 0
as we have explained,

{\rm III}\leq 2\sup_{|v|\leq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(V_{2t}^{v}=0\text{~for some~}t\geq K\right).

As a result, by Equations (
5.16
)-(
5.18
),

(5.19)

\limsup_{|x_{1}|\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{|y-x|=1,N\geq 1}\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}<+\infty\right)-\gamma_{d}\right|\leq 2\sup_{|v|\leq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(V_{2t}=0\text{~for some~}t\geq K\right)

for any
K>0
. As we have explained,
\{V_{2t}\}_{t\geq 0}
visits
0
for finite times with probability
1
and hence
\lim_{K\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{|v|\leq 1}\mathbb{P}\left(V_{2t}=0\text{~for some~}t\geq K\right)=0
. Therefore, let
K\rightarrow+\infty
in Equation (
5.19
) and then Equation (
5.15
) holds.

Now we check Equation (
5.11
). The proof of Equation (
5.11
) is similar with that of Equation (
5.10
) and hence we only give an outline. According to the definition of
{\rm II}
,

{\rm II}={\rm V}-{\rm VI},

where

{\rm V}=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\frac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)\rho_{0}\left(\frac{\hat{X}^{N,y,0}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)\right]H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)

and

{\rm VI}=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\frac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)\rho_{0}\left(\frac{\hat{X}^{N,y,0}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)1_{\{\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}\leq sN^{2}\}}\right]H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right).

Since
X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}
and
\hat{X}^{N,y,0}_{sN^{2},\beta}
are independent, it is easy to show that

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm V}=2d\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}(s,u)H^{2}(u)du

according to Theorem
3.1
and
[
3
, Proposition 2.3 ]
. Hence, to prove Equation (
5.11
) we only need to show that

(5.20)

\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm VI}=2d\gamma_{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{2}(s,u)H^{2}(u)du.

Let
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}
be defined as in the proof of Equation (
5.10
), then according to the strong Markov property and an analysis similar with that given in the proof of Equation (
5.10
),

\displaystyle{\rm VI}=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\atop\varepsilon N\leq|x|\leq K_{3}N,|x-y|=1}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|X^{N,x}_{\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0},\beta}-x\right|\leq K_{2},\tau_{x,y}^{N,\beta,0}\leq K_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\text{\quad\quad\quad\quad}\times\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\frac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\frac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)\right]H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)+\varepsilon_{8},

where
\varepsilon_{8}=\varepsilon_{8}(\varepsilon)
such that
\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon_{8}=0
. Then, according to definitions of
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}
, Equation (
5.15
) and an analysis similar with that given in the proof of Equation (
5.10
),

\displaystyle{\rm VI}=\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\gamma_{d}\mathbb{E}\left[\rho_{0}\left(\frac{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta}}{N}\right)\right]^{2}H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)+\varepsilon_{9},

where
\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow+\infty}\varepsilon_{9}=0
. Equation (
5.20
) follows from Theorem
3.1
and
[
3
, Proposition 2.3 ]
and then the proof of Equation (
5.11
) is complete.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.6
.

We claim that

(5.21)

\lim_{K\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{N\geq 1,x,y,z,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\atop|x-y|=1,|z-w|=1,|(x-z)^{\perp}|>K}\left|{\rm Cov}\left(\eta_{s}(x)\eta_{s}(y),\eta_{s}(z)\eta_{s}(w)\right)\right|=0,

where
x^{\perp}=(x_{2},\ldots,x_{d})
as we have defined. We prove Equation (
5.21
) at the end of this proof. By Equation (
5.21
), for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
K_{5}=K_{5}(\varepsilon)>0
such that

\left|{\rm Cov}\left(\eta_{s}(x)\eta_{s}(y),\eta_{s}(z)\eta_{s}(w)\right)\right|\leq\varepsilon

for any
N\geq 1
and
x,y,z,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
|x-y|=|z-w|=1,|(x-z)^{\perp}|>K_{5}
. Then, since
\varepsilon N>K_{5}
when
N
is sufficiently large,

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\eta_{s}(x)\eta_{s}(y)H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\frac{1}{N^{2d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\sum_{z,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|z-w|=1,|(z-x)^{\perp}|>\varepsilon N}H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)H^{2}\left(\tfrac{z}{N}\right)

\displaystyle\text{\quad}+\frac{1}{N^{2d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\sum_{z,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|z-w|=1,|(z-x)^{\perp}|\leq\varepsilon N}H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)H^{2}\left(\tfrac{z}{N}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{10}

for sufficiently large
N
, where

\varepsilon_{10}=\varepsilon_{10}(\varepsilon)=8\varepsilon d^{2}\left(\int_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}H^{2}(u)du\right)^{2}+8d^{2}\int_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{v\in\mathbb{R}^{d},|(v-u)^{\perp}|\leq\varepsilon}H^{2}(u)H^{2}(v)dudv.

As a result,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm Var}\left(\frac{1}{N^{d}}\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},|x-y|=1}\eta_{s}(x)\eta_{s}(y)H^{2}\left(\tfrac{x}{N}\right)\right)\leq\varepsilon_{10}(\varepsilon).

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary and
\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\varepsilon_{10}(\varepsilon)=0
, let
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and then Lemma
5.6
holds.

At last we only need to check Equation (
5.21
). For any
N\geq 1
and
x,y,z,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, let

\left\{\left\{\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,x},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,y},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,z},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,w}\right\}\right\}_{t\geq 0}

be the coalescing random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

\left\{\pi_{0,\beta}^{N,x},\pi_{0,\beta}^{N,y},\pi_{0,\beta}^{N,z},\pi_{0,\beta}^{N,w}\right\}=\{x,y,z,w\}

and
\left\{\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,x},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,y},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,z},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,w}\right\}
jumps to
\left\{\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,x},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,y},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,z},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,w}\right\}\cup\{b\}\setminus\{a\}
at rate
\xi^{N}_{a,b}
given in (
2.2
) for any
t\geq 0
and
a\in\{\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,x},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,y},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,z},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,w}\}
,
|b-a|=1
. Then, according to
Assumption (A)
and the duality relationship between the voter model and the coalescing random walk,

\displaystyle{\rm Cov}\left(\eta_{s}(x)\eta_{s}(y),\eta_{s}(z)\eta_{s}(w)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{a\in\{\pi_{sN^{2},\beta}^{N,x},\pi_{sN^{2},\beta}^{N,y},\pi_{sN^{2},\beta}^{N,z},\pi_{sN^{2},\beta}^{N,w}\}}\rho_{0}(a)\right)

(5.22)

\displaystyle\text{\quad}-\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{a\in\{X^{N,x}_{sN^{2},\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,y,0}_{sN^{2},0,x}\}}\rho_{0}(a)\right)\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{a\in\{X^{N,z}_{sN^{2},\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,w,0}_{sN^{2},0,z}\}}\rho_{0}(a)\right).

We couple
\left\{\{X^{N,x}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,y,0}_{t,0,x}\}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\left\{\{X^{N,z}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,w,0}_{t,0,z}\}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
in the same probability space such that
\left\{\{X^{N,x}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,y,0}_{t,0,x}\}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\left\{\{X^{N,z}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,w,0}_{t,0,z}\}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
are independent. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{x,y,z,w}_{N,\beta}=\inf\left\{t:~\left\{X^{N,x}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,y,0}_{t,0,x}\right\}\cap\left\{X^{N,z}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,w,0}_{t,0,z}\right\}\neq\emptyset\right\},

then we can further couple
\left\{\{\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,x},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,y},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,z},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,w}\}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
and

\left\{\{X^{N,x}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,y,0}_{t,0,x},X^{N,z}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,w,0}_{t,0,z}\}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
in the same probability space such that

\{\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,x},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,y},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,z},\pi_{t,\beta}^{N,w}\}=\{X^{N,x}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,y,0}_{t,0,x},X^{N,z}_{t,\beta},\tilde{X}^{N,w,0}_{t,0,z}\}

for
0\leq t\leq\mathcal{T}^{x,y,z,w}_{N,\beta}
. Therefore, by Equation (
5.2
),

(5.23)

\left|{\rm Cov}\left(\eta_{s}(x)\eta_{s}(y),\eta_{s}(z)\eta_{s}(w)\right)\right|\leq 2\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}^{x,y,z,w}_{N,\beta}\leq sN^{2}\right)\leq 2\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}^{x,y,z,w}_{N,\beta}<+\infty\right).

Since
\left\{\left(X^{N,a}_{t,\beta}\right)^{\perp}\right\}_{t\geq 0}
is a simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
starting at
a^{\perp}
for any
a\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

(5.24)

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{T}^{x,y,z,w}_{N,\beta}<+\infty\right)\leq 4\sup_{\omega\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1},|\omega|\geq K-2}\Gamma(\omega)

for any
x,y,z,w\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that
|x-y|=1,|z-w|=1,|(x-z)^{\perp}|\geq K
, where
\Gamma(\omega)
is defined as in Subsection
4.2
. As we have shown in Subsection
4.2
,

\lim_{|\omega|\rightarrow+\infty}\Gamma(\omega)=0

and hence Equation (
5.21
) follows from Equation (
5.24
). This completes the proof.
∎

5.3.
Tightness.

In this subsection, we prove the tightness of the sequence of processes
\{{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t},0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
. To this end, we only need to prove the tightness of the sequence of processes
\{{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t}(H),0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
for any
H\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

As in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we only need to check (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) also hold for
\{{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t}(H),0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
. Since

(5.25)

\sup_{N\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\big({\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t}(H)\big)^{2}\Big]<\infty,

by Chebyshev’s inequality, (
4.10
) holds for
\{{\mathcal{Y}}^{N}_{t}(H),0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
. By (
5.1
), it suffices to prove (
4.11
) holds for
\{{\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{t}(H),0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
and
\{\int_{0}^{t}N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}{\mathcal{Y}}_{s}^{N}(H)ds,0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
. By (
5.2
) and Theorem
5.4
,

\sup_{N\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big({\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{t}(H)\big)^{2}\Big]<\infty.

From the above inequality, as in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, one could easily prove that
\{{\mathcal{M}}^{N}_{t}(H),0\leq t\leq T\}_{N\geq 1}
satisfies (
4.11
). To conclude the proof of the tightness, we only need to prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(5.26)

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{0\leq t-s\leq\delta}\Big|\int_{s}^{t}N^{2}\mathcal{L}_{N}{\mathcal{Y}}_{\tau}^{N}(H)d\tau\Big|>\varepsilon\Big)=0

From the computations in Subsection
5.1
, we only need to prove the above equation for the three terms (
5.3
), (
5.4
) and (
5.5
). By (
5.25
), it is true for (
5.3
). In Subsection
5.1
, we have shown that the sum of the two terms (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) converges to zero in
L^{2}
, thus (
5.26
) is also true for (
5.4
) and (
5.5
). This concludes the proof of the tightness.

The Best of Both Worlds: Reinforcement Learning with Logarithmic Regret and Policy Switches
Grigoris Velegkas, Zhuoran Yang, Amin Karbasi

Abstract
In this paper, we study the problem of regret minimization for episodic Reinforcement Learning (RL) both in the model-free and the model-based setting. We focus on learning with general function classes and general model classes, and we derive results that scale with the eluder dimension of these classes. In contrast to the existing body of work that mainly establishes instance-independent regret guarantees, we focus on the instance-dependent setting and show that the regret scales logarithmically with the horizon T T , provided that there is a gap between the best and the second best action in every state. In addition, we show that such a logarithmic regret bound is realizable by algorithms with O ⁡ ( log ⁡ T ) O(\log T) switching cost (also known as adaptivity complexity ). In other words, these algorithms rarely switch their policy during the course of their execution. Finally, we complement our results with lower bounds which show that even in the tabular setting, we cannot hope for regret guarantees lower than o ⁡ ( log ⁡ T ) o(\log T) .



1
Introduction

The main goal of Reinforcement Learning (RL) is the design and analysis
of algorithms for automated
decision making in complex and unknown environments. The environment
is modeled as a
state space
and the available decisions
are modeled as an
action space
. In recent years, RL has
seen tremendous success in practical applications including, but not
limited to, games and robotics
(
Mnih et al. 2015
;
Silver et al. 2016
;
Duan et al. 2016
;
Silver et al. 2017
;
Vinyals et al. 2019
)
.
Despite this success, a theoretical understanding of the
algorithms that are
deployed in these settings remains elusive. Traditionally,
theoretical RL approaches have focused on the
tabular

setting where the complexity of the algorithms depends
on the cardinality of the
aforementioned spaces
(
Sutton & Barto 2018
)
.
Thus, they are not suitable for applications where
the state-action space is very large.

A different approach that has gained a lot of attention recently,
is the
function approximation
regime where the cumulative
reward of the algorithm is modeled through a function, such
as linear functions over some feature space.
The advantage of this approach is that the algorithm
can perform its search over a lower-dimensional space.
There is a long line of work that provides regret
guarantees for RL in the function approximation setting
(
Osband & Van Roy 2014
;
Osband et al. 2016
;
Yang & Wang 2020
;
Jin et al. 2020
;
Ayoub et al. 2020
;
Cai et al. 2020
;
Kakade et al. 2020
;
Zanette, Brandfonbrener, Brunskill, Pirotta & Lazaric 2020
;
He et al. 2021
;
Kong et al. 2021
;
Zhou et al. 2021
)
.

Most of these works have focused on establishing worst-case

\sqrt{T}
-regret guarantees, where
T
is the number
of interactions with the environment. The caveat with these guarantees
is that they are pessimistic since they neglect benign
settings where even an
exponential
improvement
over these bounds is achievable. To address this issue,
there are some works that obtain instance-dependent
regret bounds for RL in the tabular setting and
in the linear function approximation
setting
(
Simchowitz & Jamieson 2019
;
Yang & Wang 2020
;
He et al. 2021
)
.
However, getting logarithmic regret bounds in the context of

general
function approximation remains open. Hence,
a natural question is the following:

Can we establish instance-dependent logarithmic regret
bounds with general function approximation?

In this paper, we provide an affirmative answer to this
question. Following the assumptions in the existing
literature, we model the RL problem as an MDP that enjoys
the property that the optimal
policy
is at least

{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}
better than any other policy,
where
{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}>0
is a parameter that
captures the hardness of the underlying problem.
We focus both on the
model-free
and
model-based

settings with general function approximation, where
we represent the value function or the transition model
by a given function class, respectively.

In the model-free setting, we study the algorithm
proposed in
Kong et al. 2021
, which is a variant
of the least-squares value iteration (LSVI) with upper confidence
bound (UCB) bonuses
that guide exploration. Here, the bonus functions
are given by the width of a data-dependent confidence region
for LSVI. For the model-based setting, we develop
a similar algorithm which combines value-targeted
regression with UCB bonuses. On top of the
logarithmic regret
guarantees they enjoy, our algorithms feature
lazy policy updates, in the sense that policy
is updated rarely and only when certain conditions are met.

For both settings, we establish
O({\rm poly}(\log T)\cdot{\rm poly}(H)\cdot{\rm poly}(d_{{\mathcal{F}}})\cdot 1/{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min})

regret guarantees, where
T
is
the number of interactions with the environment,

H
is the
planning horizon
,
d_{{\mathcal{F}}}

is a term that captures the complexity of the function class
{\mathcal{F}}
which is used to approximate either the value function or the transition model. In particular,
d_{{\mathcal{F}}}
involves both the

eluder dimension

(
Russo & Van Roy 2013
)
and the log-covering numbers of the
function classes.
That is, for benign
MDPs where
{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}>0
these RL algorithms
achieve logarithmic regret, which is exponentially
better than the worst-case
O(\sqrt{T})
-regret.
Moreover, we show that the
adaptivity complexity
,
meaning the number of different policies our algorithms
use, is also logarithmic in
T
.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first
work that establishes a logarithmic
instance-dependent regret guarantee for RL
with general function approximation.

1.1
Related Work

Logarithmic regret bounds for bandits.

There is a
long line of work that establishes logarithmic
regret guarantees in bandit problems. Essentially,
bandits are a special case of RL where the transition to the
next state does not depend on the action that was taken by
the agent. An extensive list of such algorithms can
be found in
Bubeck & Cesa-Bianchi 2012
;
Slivkins 2019
;
Lattimore & Szepesvári 2020
.

Logarithmic regret bounds for RL.

A series of works are devoted to proving instance-dependent
logarithmic regret bounds in tabular RL.
Ok et al. 2018
;
Simchowitz & Jamieson 2019
prove lower bounds that show
that a logarithmic dependence on
T
is unavoidable.
Considering the upper bounds,
Auer & Ortner 2007
;
Tewari & Bartlett 2007
establish logarithmic regret guarantees in the
average reward setting. Both of these guarantees are asymptotic
as they require the number of interactions
T
with the MDP to
be large enough. Regarding non-asymptotic bounds,
Jaksch et al. 2010

provide such an algorithm that achieves

O(D^{2}|{\mathcal{S}}|^{2}|{\mathcal{A}}|\log(T)/{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min})
regret for the
average-reward MDP, where
D
is the diameter of the MDP.
For episodic MDPs, logarithmic regret upper bounds
are established in
Simchowitz & Jamieson 2019
;
Yang et al. 2021
.
The work that is probably the most closely related to ours
is
He et al. 2021
. It provides instance-dependent
logarithmic regret guarantees both in the model-free
and model-based setting with
linear
function
approximation. Moreover, the proposed algorithms update
the policy in every episode.
Our work generalizes these results since the linear
regime is a special case
of the setting we are studying. Furthermore, we
achieve an exponential improvement on the adaptivity complexity
over their algorithms.

Bandits with limited adaptivity complexity.

There is a lot of interest in
obtaining bandit algorithms that update their policies
rarely
(
Abbasi-Yadkori et al. 2011
;
Perchet et al. 2016
;
Agarwal et al. 2017
;
Gao et al. 2019
;
Dong et al. 2020
;
Chen et al. 2020
;
Ruan et al. 2021
)
.
Notably,
Dong et al. 2020
study rare policy
switching constraints for a broader class of online
learning and decision making problems such as logit
bandits.

RL with limited adaptivity complexity.

Recently, there has been a lot of interest in developing
RL algorithms that achieve sub-linear regret and have
low adaptivity complexity
(
Bai et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2020
;
Wang et al. 2021
;
Kong et al. 2021
;
Gao et al. 2021
)
.
We develop an algorithm with low adaptivity complexity
that works
in the model-based, general function approximation setting.

RL with general function approximation.

As we have alluded to already, because of the enormous size
of the state-action space in real world applications, it
is important to develop and analyze algorithms in the
function approximation regime. So far, the most commonly studied
setting is RL with linear function approximation
(
Yang & Wang 2020
;
Jin et al. 2020
;
Du et al. 2020
;
Wang et al. 2020
;
Zanette, Lazaric, Kochenderfer & Brunskill 2020
;
Agarwal et al. 2020
)
.
Recently, there are also important results in RL
with general function approximation. To be specific,
Jiang et al. 2017
design
an efficient algorithm whose sample complexity is bounded in terms
of the Bellman rank of the function class.
Ayoub et al. 2020

develop an algorithm for model-based RL, whose regret bound
depends on the eluder dimension of the underlying class of models.

Jin et al. 2021
propose an algorithm for problems where
the underlying class has bounded Bellman eluder dimension.
Recent works such as
Wang et al. 2020
;
Kong et al. 2021
develop algorithms
in the model-free setting whose regret scale with
the eluder dimension of the functions.
Foster et al. 2020
also
propose an LSVI-based algorithm whose regret depends on a disagreement
coefficient, which is upper bounded by the eluder dimension of the function
class. The caveat with their approach is that it requires the block MDP
assumption.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we present the notation and the important
definitions we
use throughout the paper.

2.1
Notation

We use the common notation
[N]=\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
.
We also define the infinity norm of some function
f:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
where
{\mathcal{X}}
is some domain, to be
||f||_{\infty}=\sup_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}|f(x)|
.
For a dataset
{\mathcal{D}}=\{(x_{i},q_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{X}}\times\mathbb{R}
and a function
f:{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
we define the following norm

\displaystyle||f||_{{\mathcal{D}}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(f(x_{i})-q_{i})^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Given a set
{\mathcal{Z}}=\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq{\mathcal{X}}
we let

\displaystyle||f||_{{\mathcal{Z}}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_{i})^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

be the data-dependent norm.
Given a measurable set
{\mathcal{X}}
, we denote with

\Delta({\mathcal{X}})
the
probability simplex over
{\mathcal{X}}
.
We also denote by
\mathbbm{1}[{\cal E}]
the indicator function
of the event
{\cal E}
.
We denote by
{\rm poly}(x)
a function that is a polynomial in
x
.

2.2
Episodic Markov Decision Processes

The learning agent interacts with the environment over a sequence
of
K
rounds which we call
episodes
.
We model the interaction of the agent with the environment in every episode as a

Markov Decision Process
(MDP). We denote an MDP by

M=({\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}},P,r,H,s_{1})
, where
{\mathcal{S}}
is the
state space
,
{\mathcal{A}}
is the
action space
,
P=\{P_{h}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\Delta({\mathcal{S}})\}_{h=1}^{H}
are the
transition kernels
,

r=\{r_{h}:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow[0,1]\}_{h=1}^{H}

are the
reward functions
which we assume to be deterministic,
H
is
the
planning horizon
, which is the length of every episode,
and
s_{1}
is the initial state of every episode. During every episode,
the agent uses a
policy

\pi=\{\pi_{h}:{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}\}_{h=1}^{H}
, to take an action at a given state.
We use the Q-function and V-function to evaluate the expected total
reward
generated by a policy
\pi
. More specifically,
we define

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{\pi}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\E\left[\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}r_{h^{\prime}}(s_{h^{\prime}},a_{h^{\prime}})\big|s_{h}=s,a_{h}=a,\pi\right]

\displaystyle V_{h}^{\pi}(s)

\displaystyle=\E\left[\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}r_{h^{\prime}}(s_{h^{\prime}},a_{h^{\prime}})\big|s_{h}=s,\pi\right],

where the actions are picked according to
\pi
and
s_{h^{\prime}+1}\sim P_{h^{\prime}}(\cdot|s_{h^{\prime}},a_{h^{\prime}})
. For simplicity, we denote

\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V\rangle=\E_{s^{\prime}\sim P_{h}(\cdot|s,a)}[V(s^{\prime})]
.
We denote the optimal policy for a given MDP with
\pi^{*}
.
Similarly, for the optimal Q-function and V-function
we use

Q^{*}_{h}(s,a)=Q^{\pi^{*}}_{h}(s,a),V^{*}_{h}(s)=V^{\pi^{*}}_{h}(s)
, respectively.

The goal of the learner is to improve her performance as she interacts
with the unknown environment.
In the episodic setting, the agent commits to a policy at the beginning
of every episode.
We let
\pi^{k}
denote the policy that the agent uses in the
k
-th episode.
At each step
h\in[H]
, the agent observes the state
s_{h}^{k}
,
chooses an action according to
\pi^{k}
, and then observes the reward

r_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})
and the next state
s^{k}_{h+1}\sim P_{h}(\cdot|s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})
.
In this work, to measure the performance
of the agent we use the notion of
regret
,
defined as

\displaystyle{\textnormal{Regret}}(K)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V_{1}^{*}(s_{1})-V_{1}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1})\right).

The regret measures the difference between the total reward that the agent would have accumulated if she was following the optimal policy and the reward she actually accumulates.
The intuition behind striving for algorithms that guarantee sub-linear regret
is that we want, as
K\rightarrow\infty
, the average
reward of the learner who follows the algorithm to approach
that of the optimal policy.

We now describe the tabular MDP setting, which is arguably the simplest
setting one can work on. The lower bounds on the regret that we state
apply to this setting, whereas both of the upper bounds we derive capture
the tabular
MDP setting. In this regime, there are
|{\mathcal{S}}|
states,
|{\mathcal{A}}|
actions, and for
each step
h\in[H]
the transition probability is given by
P_{h}(s^{\prime}|s,a)
, where
\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}P_{h}(s^{\prime}|s,a)=1
, and
the reward is denoted by
r_{h}(s,a)
. The caveat with this setting is that in practical applications
the state space and the action space can be very large, so the bounds that depend explicitly
on the cardinality of these quantities are not very useful.

An important assumption we make in order
to achieve logarithmic regret guarantees is that the minimum
sub-optimality
gap is positive.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the sub-optimality gap of a state-action pair
(s,a)
at step
h
to be

\displaystyle{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s,a)=V^{*}_{h}(s)-Q^{*}_{h}(s,a).

The minimum sub-optimality gap is defined to be

\displaystyle{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}=\min_{h,s,a}\{{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s,a):{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s,a)\neq 0\}.

It is well-known that if we do not make any assumptions the best
regret guarantee we can hope for is
O(\sqrt{T})

(
Jaksch et al. 2010
)
.
In this work, we derive instance-dependent
regret guarantees that achieve an exponential improvement
on
T
when
{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}>0
.

2.3
Model-Free Assumption

In this paper, we deal with general function classes.
In the model-free setting we assume that we have access to a function class

{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq\{f:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow[0,H+1]\}
.
Our goal is to use the functions in
{\mathcal{F}}
to approximate
the optimal Q-function. In order to derive
meaningful results
we assume that this class has some structure.
We follow the same assumption as in
Wang et al. 2020
;
Kong et al. 2021
.

Assumption 2.2

(Bellman Operator Assumption)
.

For any
h\in[H]
and
V:{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow[0,H]
there exists some
f_{V}\in{\mathcal{F}}

such that for all
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
we have

\displaystyle f_{V}(s,a)=r_{h}(s,a)+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}P_{h}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V(s^{\prime}).

The intuition behind this assumption is that if we apply the one-step Bellman backup operator
to some value function
V
, i.e.

\displaystyle r_{h}(s,a)+\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}P_{h}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V(s^{\prime}),

the result will remain in the
function class. Thus, it implicitly poses some constraints both
on the transition probabilities and the reward function.
It is known that both the tabular setting and the
linear MDP setting
(
Yang & Wang 2019
;
Jin et al. 2020
)
satisfy this assumption.

Another assumption we make is that the function class and the state-action space
have bounded covering numbers. We will show that the dependence of the regret on the covering number
is poly-logarithmic. This
assumption has also appeared in other works
(
Russo & Van Roy 2013
;
Wang et al. 2020
;
Jin et al. 2021
;
Kong et al. 2021
)
.

Assumption 2.3

(Bounded Covering Number)
.

We say that
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)
is a bound on the
\varepsilon
-covering number of
{\mathcal{F}}
, if
for any
\varepsilon>0
there is an
\varepsilon
-cover
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}

with size
|{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)|\leq{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)
, so that
for all
f\in{\mathcal{F}}
there is some
f^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)
such that

||f-f^{\prime}||_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
.
Similarly, we say that
{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
is a
bound on the
\varepsilon
-covering number of
{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
with
respect to
{\mathcal{F}}
, if for any
\varepsilon>0
there is
an
\varepsilon
-cover
{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)\subseteq{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
with size
|{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)|\leq{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
, so that
for all
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
there is some
(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},\varepsilon)
such that

\sup_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}}|f(s,a)-f(s^{\prime},a^{\prime})|\leq\varepsilon
.

The intuition behind this assumption is straightforward: even
if the function class or the state-action space are infinite,
we can approximate them using a small number of points.

2.4
Model-Based Assumption

The assumptions in Section
2.3
are
model-free
since they impose some structure on the function class
that approximates the Q-function instead of the transition kernel.
In order to derive our results, we can also
follow a different route and impose some structure directly
on the transition kernel
(
Ayoub et al. 2020
)
.

Assumption 2.4

(Known Transition Model Family)
.

For all
h\in[H]
, the transition model
P_{h}
belongs to a family of
models
{\cal P}_{h}
which is known to the learner. The elements of
{\cal P}_{h}

are transition kernels that map state-action pairs to signed distributions
over the state space
{\mathcal{S}}
.

We allow signed distributions in our model class to increase
its generality. For example, this is useful when we are given
access to a model class that can be compactly represented
only when it includes non-probability kernels. For an extensive
discussion about this, the reader is referred to
Pires & Szepesvári 2016
.

Transition kernels, either parametric or non-parametric, have been
used to model complex stochastic controlled systems. For instance,
transitions in robotics systems are often modelled using
parameters of the environment, such as friction.

An important class that satisfies this assumption are the

linear mixture models
.

Definition 2.5
.

The class of models
{\cal P}
with
feature mapping
\phi(s^{\prime}|s,a):{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}

and some
\theta^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},||\theta^{*}||_{2}\leq C_{\theta},
is called linear mixture model if:

•

P(s^{\prime}|s,a)=\left\langle\phi(\cdot|s,a),\theta^{*}\right\rangle.

•

For any bounded function
V:{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow[0,H]
and any pair
(s,a)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}
, we have
||\phi_{V}(s,a)||_{2}\leq\sqrt{H}
, where

\phi_{V}(s,a)=\left\langle\phi(\cdot|s,a),V\right\rangle.

One way to interpret the linear mixture model is as an aggregation of
some basis models which are known to the designer
(
Modi et al. 2020
)
.
Another interesting way to think about it comes from large-scale
queuing networks where both the arrival rate of jobs and
the processing speed for the queues are unknown. If we approximate
this system in discrete time, then the transition matrix
from timestep
t
to timestep
t+\Delta t
approaches that
of a linear function with respect to the arrival rate
and the processing time
(
Gnedenko & Kovalenko 1989
)
.

Another interesting setting that satisfies this assumption
is the linear-factored MDP
(
Yang & Wang 2020
)
.
Assuming that the state space is discrete, we have that

\displaystyle P(s^{\prime}|s,a)

\displaystyle=\phi(s,a)^{T}M\psi(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{2}}M_{ij}[\psi_{j}(s^{\prime})\phi_{i}(s,a)].

2.5
Complexity Measure: Eluder Dimension

Our results depend on the complexity of the function classes and the model
classes that we consider.
To measure this complexity, we use the
eluder dimension
of
these classes
(
Russo & Van Roy 2013
)
.

Definition 2.6
.

Fix some
\varepsilon\geq 0
and a sequence of
n
points
\mathcal{Z}=\{(x_{i})\}_{i\in[n]}\subseteq{\mathcal{X}}
. Then:

1.

A point
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
is
\varepsilon
-dependent on
\mathcal{Z}
with respect to
\mathcal{F}
if for all
f,f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
||f-f^{\prime}||_{\mathcal{Z}}\leq\varepsilon
it holds that
|f(x)-f^{\prime}(x)|\leq\varepsilon
.

2.

A point
x
is
\varepsilon
-independent of
\mathcal{Z}
with respect to
\mathcal{F}
if
x
is not
\varepsilon
-dependent on
\mathcal{Z}
.

3.

The
\varepsilon
-eluder dimension of
\mathcal{F}
, which is denoted by
\text{dim}_{E}(\mathcal{F},\varepsilon)
, is the length of the longest sequence of elements in
{\mathcal{X}}
such that every element in this sequence is
\varepsilon^{\prime}
-independent of its predecessors, for some
\varepsilon^{\prime}\geq\varepsilon
.

Intuitively, the eluder dimension of
{\mathcal{F}}
quantifies the
largest set of elements
{\mathcal{Z}}\subseteq{\mathcal{X}}
so that if all
f\in{\mathcal{F}}

are close with respect to
{\mathcal{Z}}
, then they are close
on all elements of
{\mathcal{X}}
.

It is known that when
{\mathcal{X}}={\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},f:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow[0,H]
, and
{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}}
are finite, we have that
\dim_{E}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)\leq|{\mathcal{S}}|\cdot|{\mathcal{A}}|
, for all
\varepsilon>0

(
Russo & Van Roy 2013
;
Wang et al. 2020
)
. Moreover,
when
{\mathcal{F}}
is the class of linear functions, i.e.,
f_{\theta}(s,a)=\theta^{T}\phi(s,a)
, for a given feauture vector
\phi(s,a)
, the eluder dimension of

{\mathcal{F}}
is bounded by
\dim_{E}({\mathcal{F}},\varepsilon)=O(d\log(1/\varepsilon))
, for all
\varepsilon>0
.
We also remark that more classes including generalized
linear functions and bounded degree polynomials
have bounded eluder dimension
(
Russo & Van Roy 2013
;
Osband & Van Roy 2014
;
Li et al. 2021
)
.

2.6
Switching Cost

Essentially,
the
switching cost
or the
adaptivity complexity
measures the number of episodes the algorithm
has to update its policy in order to achieve the guaranteed regret bound
(
Bai et al. 2019
;
Kong et al. 2021
)
. More formally:

Definition 2.7
.

We define the switching cost of an algorithm
A
over
K
episodes to be

\displaystyle N_{\textnormal{switch}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}\mathbbm{1}[\pi_{k}\neq\pi_{k+1}].

3
Overview of the Algorithms and Main Results

In this section, we present our main results and
give a high-level description of the techniques
we use. We treat both the model-free and the model-based setting in a unified way.
Our algorithm, inspired by
Kong et al. 2021
, is presented
in Algorithm
1
. The only differences
between the two settings are the different sampling routine
and Q-function estimator used by Algorithm
1
. In a nutshell, our low-switching cost algorithm works as follows:

•

After each round of the interaction with the MDP,
Algorithm
1
adds elements to the current dataset
with some probability that depends
on their significance
and updates the policy only if the dataset has changed.
This guarantees that the adaptivity of the algorithm
depends logarithmically on
T
, without hurting the
regret guarantee.

•

We use a least-squares estimate of the Q-function
(transition kernel) in the model-free (model-based) setting.

•

We add a bonus to this estimate which encourages exploration
and, with high probability, guarantees that the current estimate of
the Q-function serves as an element-wise upper bound of
Q^{*}
. This
bonus is based on a sub-sampled dataset that we have accumulated
from previous interactions with the MDP.

Before we delve deeper into the two settings separately,
we describe a parameter that is crucial for both of the algorithms
we are using.
Following
Kong et al. 2021
,
we define the
sensitivity
of an element
z
with respect to
a dataset
{\mathcal{Z}}
and a function class
{\mathcal{F}}
to be

\displaystyle\textnormal{sensitivity}_{{\mathcal{Z}},{\mathcal{F}}}(z)=\min\left\{\sup_{f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{\left(f_{1}(z)-f_{2}(z)\right)^{2}}{\min\{||f_{1}-f_{2}||_{{\mathcal{Z}}}^{2},T(H+1)^{2}\}+\beta}\right\}.

Intuitively, this parameter captures the importance
of the current element
z
relative to the dataset we are working
with.
We will elaborate on the choice of the parameter
\beta

for each of the two settings separately.
To establish the regret guarantee,
we propose a novel regret decomposition where we utilize
the “peeling technique” that has been applied in prior
works in
local Rademacher complexities
(
Bartlett et al. 2005
)

and in RL
(
He et al. 2021
;
Yang et al. 2021
)
.
By doing that, we show how the regret of the algorithm
relates to the suboptimality gap.

To establish the lower bound on the regret of any algorithm in the settings
we are interested in, we utilize a result that was proved in
Ok et al. 2018
;
Simchowitz & Jamieson 2019
. It states that for all algorithms
that achieve sublinear regret, there exists a tabular MDP
where its regret is at least
\Omega({\rm poly}(\log(T))\cdot{\rm poly}(H)\cdot 1/{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min})
.

The dataset includes pairs of the form

The Q-function routine is a least-squares
estimator that is based on all the previous interactions with the MDP. Notice that the bonus function is based only on the sub-sampled
dataset and depends on a hardcoded parameter
\beta

the Q-function is an optimistic estimate of the actual one
and the bonus we add is not too large. Since we are working

our choice of

A crucial part of the algorithm is the online sub-sampling routine.
The reason we are using this routine is twofold.
Firstly, if we use the entire dataset there will be a huge
number of distinct elements in it, which can make the exploration
bonus unstable since it changes constantly and can take
infinitely many different values. In order to establish
the optimism of the Q-function estimation, namely,

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{*}(s,a)\leq Q_{h}^{k}(s,a)\leq\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h}^{k}\rangle+2b_{h}^{k}(s,a),

Kong et al. 2021
show that it is crucial to bound the complexity of the exploration bonus.
Secondly, if we sub-sample the dataset based on the
importance of the elements, we can achieve the regret guarantees that
we are aiming for by switching the policy only when an important element has been added.
Notice that whenever an element is added to the dataset,
multiple copies are included.
This is to make the
sub-sampled dataset behave like an unbiased estimator of the orignal one.
Then, using concentration bounds one can show that it
approximates the original one with high probability.
The full description of this procedure is presented in Algorithm

.
For a more detailed discussion about the importance of sub-sampling
the dataset, the interested reader is referred to
Wang et al. 2020
;

3.2

In this regime, we assume that the MDP satisfies
Assumption
2.4
. We also
assume that the reward function is known to the learner similar
to
Ayoub et al. 2020
. If the reward is unknown, we just
estimate it and construct a confidence region.

Before we discuss the details of our approach, we need to describe an important set of functions that show up in our algorithm
and in the regret guarantee.
Let
{\cal V}
be the set of all measurable functions that are bounded by
H
.
Let
{\cal P}_{h}
be the set of potential models in step
H
.
We also let
f:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and define the following
set:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\biggl\{f:\exists\tilde{P}_{h}\in{\cal P}_{h}\text{ so that }f(s,a,V)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}\tilde{P}_{h}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime},\forall(s,a,V)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\cal V}\biggr\}.

The bounds we state scale with the complexity of
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
.
The Q-function estimator and the sampling routine
we use that are specific to this setting are presented in Algorithm
4
and Algorithm

, respectively.
The dataset includes elements of the form
z_{h}^{k}=(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},V^{k}_{h+1}(\cdot))
.
The Q-function routine works in the following way.
We first estimate a model
\widehat{P}_{h}\in{\cal P}_{h}
using a least-squares
estimator that is based on all the previous interactions with the MDP.
Using a concentration argument for this estimator of the model,
similar to
Russo & Van Roy 2013
;
Ayoub et al. 2020
,
we can show that for an appropriate choice of
\beta
, the estimated model lies
in a data-dependent ball centered at

in the Appendix). Thus,
we can set the bonus function to be the diameter of this ball in order
to ensure that

is an optimistic estimate of
Q_{h}^{*}
.
In addition, the choice of
\beta
ensures
that the bonus we add is not very large. Notice also
that since the concentration argument in this setting
differs with that in the model-free setting, we do not
need to round the elements that we are adding to the
sub-sampled dataset.

In this setting, the main reason
we sub-sample the dataset is to achieve logarithmic adaptivity.
Moreover, not using the entire dataset to compute the bonus function
improves the computational complexity of our algorithm.
To bound the adaptivity complexity, we use a similar approach as
in
Kong et al. 2021

4
Proof Sketch of Main Results

In this section, we sketch the proof of our main results.
Due to space limitation, we only discuss the model-free setting.
The full proofs in the model-free, model-based setting can be found
in Appendix
A
,
Appendix
B
, respectively.

The first step in our analysis is the regret decomposition of the algorithm.
Lemma
A.1
shows that

\E\left[{\textnormal{Regret}}(K)\right]=\E\left[\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\right]
. Thus, we see that to bound
the regret it is enough to bound
\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})
for every
h\in[H]
.
Towards this end, notice that
{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\in[{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min},H]
. We apply the “peeling technique” that
has also been used in local
Rademacher complexities
(
Bartlett et al. 2005
)
and in
He et al. 2021
;
Yang et al. 2021
. The idea is to split the interval
[0,H]

into
\log(H/{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min})
intervals, where the
i
-th interval is

[2^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min},2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}]
. Hence, for every
{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h},a_{h})

that falls in the
i
-th interval its contribution
to the regret is at most
2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}
.
Thus, to bound the regret it suffices
to bound the number of suboptimalities that fall into every interval.
Notice that for some
{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})
in this interval we have that

\displaystyle V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min},

so it suffices to bound the number of sub-optimalities
V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})

that fall into the
i
-th interval.
Both for the model-free and the model-based setting, we
can derive such a bound.
Finally, notice that once we have bounded the number of suboptimilaties
in every interval, it is not difficult to bound the total regret. Let

C_{i}=[2^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min},2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min})
and
N=\log(H/{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min})
.
Then, we know that
{\textnormal{Regret}}(K)
can be upper bounded by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\in C_{i}}{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K}2^{i}\mathbbm{1}\left[{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\in C_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}2^{i}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}\left[V^{*}_{h}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\right].

Hence, deriving the regret guarantee boils down to bounding

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}\left[V^{*}_{h}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\right]
. We provide such a bound in Lemma
A.9
,
which depends polynomially on
\log T,1/{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}
,
and the complexity parameters of the function class.
The outline of the proof is the following. We fix some
episode
h\in[H]
and let
K^{\prime}
be
the set of rounds where
V^{*}_{h}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}
.
To get a bound on
|K^{\prime}|
, our approach is to lower bound and upper bound
the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}},a_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}})
by
f_{1}(|K^{\prime}|),f_{2}(|K^{\prime}|)
, respectively. Then, we use the fact that

f_{1}(|K^{\prime}|)\leq f_{2}(|K^{\prime}|)
to establish our bound.
For the lower bound, using the definition of
K^{\prime}
we get

f_{1}(|K^{\prime}|)=2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}|K^{\prime}|
. For the upper bound,
we leverage the fact that
Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\leq\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}}),V_{h+1}^{k_{i}}\rangle+2b_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})
(cf. Lemma
A.8
) and obtain
f_{2}(|K^{\prime}|)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}\varepsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}b_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}})
, where

\varepsilon_{h}^{k_{i}}
forms a bounded martingale difference sequence.
We bound each term on the RHS separately.
For the first one, we use the Azuma-Hoeffding inequality which
can be found in Lemma
A.7
.
For the second term, we generalize the bound
on the summation of the bonus functions over all the episodes
from
Kong et al. 2021
, and show that a similar bound
holds for the summation of the bonus over
any
set
of episodes
K^{\prime}
(see Lemma
A.6
).
Putting everything together, we get that

|K^{\prime}|=O\left(1/(4^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min})\cdot H^{4}\cdot\log^{4}T\cdot{\rm poly}(d_{{\mathcal{F}}})\right)
,
where
d_{{\mathcal{F}}}
is the complexity parameter of the class.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we consider episodic RL with general
function approximation. We prove
that there are algorithms with logarithmic adaptivity complexity
both in the model-free and model-based
settings that achieve logarithmic instance-dependent
regret guarantees.
An interesting open question
is to establish the optimal dependence of the regret
guarantees on the planning horizon
H
.

Lemma A.6

For any set

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h=1}^{H}b_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\leq H+H(H+1)\cdot\textnormal{dim}_{E}(\mathcal{F},1/T)+CH\sqrt{\textnormal{dim}_{E}(\mathcal{F},1/T)|K^{\prime}|\cdot\beta},

Throughout the proof, we condition on the event defined in Proposition

\displaystyle b_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\leq\bar{b}_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})=\sup_{||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\leq 100\beta}|f_{1}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})-f_{2}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})|.

\sum_{i=1}^{K^{\prime}}\bar{b}_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})

Given some
\epsilon>0

Our goal is to show that there is some

\epsilon

L_{h}/\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},\epsilon)-1

\mathcal{L}_{h}^{j}

We use the following procedure. Initially we set
{\mathcal{L}}_{h}^{j}=\emptyset

For each such

\epsilon

{\mathcal{L}}_{h}^{j}\leftarrow{\mathcal{L}}_{h}^{j}\cup z_{h}^{k}

{\mathcal{L}}_{h}^{N+1}\neq\emptyset

This is because every set
{\mathcal{L}}_{h}^{j},1\leq j\leq N

Furthermore, since
\bar{b}_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})>\epsilon

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\bar{b}_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})

for some constant

Lemma A.9
.

If we pick

Proof.

We keep
h
fixed.

We denote by
K^{\prime}
the set of episodes where the gap at step

is at least
2^{n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle K^{\prime}=\left\{k\in[K]:V^{*}_{h}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\right\}.

The goal is to bound the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)
from below and above with functions

and then use the fact that
f_{1}(|K^{\prime}|)\leq f_{2}(|K^{\prime}|)
to derive an upper bound on

.

For the lower bound, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},\pi_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k_{i}}))-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},\pi_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k_{i}}))-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)\geq 2^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}|K^{\prime}|,

where the first inequality holds by the definition of the policy
\pi_{k_{i}}
, the second one follows because

is an optimistic estimate of

which happens with probability at least

(see Lemma
A.8
) and the third one by the definition of

.

We get the upper bound on this quantity in the following way. For any
h^{\prime}\in[H]
we have

\displaystyle Q_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})-Q_{h^{\prime}}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}P_{h^{\prime}}(s^{\prime}|s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})V^{k}_{h^{\prime}+1}(s^{\prime})+r_{h^{\prime}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})+2b_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})-Q_{h^{\prime}}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

\displaystyle=\left\langle P_{h^{\prime}}(\cdot|s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k}),V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k}-V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{k}}\right\rangle+2b_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

\displaystyle=V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})-V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})+\epsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k}+2b_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

\displaystyle=Q_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})-Q_{h^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})+\epsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k}+2b_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

where we define

and the inequality follows from Lemma
A.8
.

We now take the summation over all
k\in|K^{\prime}|,h\leq h^{\prime}\leq H
and we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}\epsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}b_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}).

We will bound each of the two terms on the RHS separately.

For the first term, we notice that
x_{j}=\left\langle P_{j}(\cdot|s_{j}^{k_{i}},a_{j}^{k_{i}}),V_{j+1}^{k_{i}}-V_{j+1}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}\right\rangle-(V_{j+1}^{k_{i}}(s_{j+1}^{k_{i}})-V_{j+1}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{j+1}^{k_{i}}))
forms a martingale difference sequence with zero mean and
|x_{j}|\leq 2H
. Hence, we can use Lemma
A.7
and that for each
k\in K^{\prime}
, with probability at least
1-\delta
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{H}\left(\left\langle P_{j}(\cdot|s_{j}^{k_{i}},a_{j}^{k_{i}}),V_{j+1}^{k_{i}}-V_{j+1}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}\right\rangle-\left(V_{j+1}^{k_{i}}(s_{j+1}^{k_{i}})-V_{j+1}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{j+1}^{k_{i}})\right)\right)\leq\sqrt{8kH^{3}\log(1/\delta)}.

If we take the union bound over all
k\in[K]

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}\epsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}\leq\sqrt{8|K^{\prime}|H^{3}\log(1/\delta)}.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}b_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}})\leq H+H(H+1)\dim_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)+CH\sqrt{\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)|K^{\prime}|\beta}

gives us that

\displaystyle|K^{\prime}|\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}H^{4}\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)\log^{2}T\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},\delta/T^{2})/\delta)\log({\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},\delta/T^{2})T/\delta)}{4^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}^{2}}.

Proof of Lemma
A.2
:

Throughout this proof we condition on the event described in Lemma
A.9
which happens with probability at least
1-2K\delta
. Since
{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}>0
whenever we do not take the optimal action, we have that either
V_{h}^{*}(s_{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})=0
or
V_{h}^{*}(s_{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}

[0,H]

N=\lceil\log(H/{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min})\rceil

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}\left[2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\geq V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\right]\cdot 2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}\left[V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\right]\cdot 2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\widetilde{C}H^{4}\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)\log^{2}T\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},\delta/T^{2})/\delta)\log({\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},\delta/T^{2})T/\delta)}{4^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}^{2}}\cdot 2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\widetilde{C}H^{4}\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)\log^{2}T\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},\delta/T^{2})/\delta)\log({\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},\delta/T^{2})T/\delta)}{2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CH^{4}\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)\log^{2}T\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},\delta/T^{2})/\delta)\log({\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}},\delta/T^{2})T/\delta)}{{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}}

where the first inequality holds by the definition of the intervals, the second due to the properties of the indicator function, the third because of Lemma
A.9
and in the last two steps we just manipulate the constants.

A.1
we have that

\displaystyle\text{Regret}(K)\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})+\frac{16H^{2}\tau}{3}+2.

We can bound the first term on the RHS using Lemma
B.1
as follows

\displaystyle 2\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}{\textnormal{gap}}_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})=2\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\right)\leq\frac{2CH^{5}\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)/\delta)\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)}{{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}}.

This gives us the result.

∎

B.1
Supporting Lemmas: Theorem
3.3

In this section our goal is to prove the supporting lemmas of Theorem
3.3
.

Recall that our approach
is to modify the algorithm in
Kong et al. 2021
to work in this
setting and use a similar analysis as in
Appendix
A
. Unlike
Appendix
A

where we approximate the optimal Q-function, here we try to
estimate
the true transition kernel.
Let

{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}=\{\left(s_{h}^{\tau},a_{h}^{\tau},V_{h+1}^{\tau}(\cdot)\right)\}_{\tau\in[k-1]}
be the dataset up to episode
k
and
\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{k}
the sub-sampled dataset.
In each episode
k
, we update our policy whenever we add an element
in the dataset for some
h\in[H]
. Recall that whenever we perform an update our policy becomes:

\displaystyle Q_{H+1}^{k}(s,a)=0,

\displaystyle V_{H+1}^{k}(s)=0,

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{k}(s,a)=\min\{r_{h}(s,a)+\langle\widehat{P}^{k}_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V^{k}_{h+1}\rangle+b_{h}^{k}(s,a),H\},

\displaystyle V_{h}^{k}(s)=\max_{a\in{\mathcal{A}}}Q_{h}^{k}(s,a)

for some
\widehat{P}_{h}^{k},b_{h}^{k}(\cdot,\cdot)
that we will define shortly.
We get the policy
\pi^{k}_{h}(s)
by picking greedily the action that maximizes
the estimate
Q_{h}^{k}(s,a)
.

The least-squares estimate of the model is

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{h}^{k+1}=\arg\min_{P\in{\cal P}_{h}}\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k}\left(\langle P(\cdot|s_{h}^{k^{\prime}},a_{h}^{k^{\prime}}),V_{h+1}^{k^{\prime}}\rangle-y_{h}^{k^{\prime}}\right)^{2},y_{h}^{k^{\prime}}=V_{h+1}^{k^{\prime}}(s_{h+1}^{k^{\prime}}).

Recall the definition of the function class
that we use in the derivation of our results.

Definition B.4
.

Let
{\cal V}
be the set all measurable functions that are bounded by
H
. We now let
f:{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and define the following set:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{h}=\left\{f:\exists P_{h}\in{\cal P}_{h}\text{ so that }f(s,a,V)=\int_{\mathcal{S}}P_{h}(s^{\prime}|s,a)V(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime},\forall(s,a,V)\in{\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\cal V}\right\}.

(1)

Recall that in this setting the norm of a function
with respect to a dataset
{\mathcal{Z}}
is

||f||_{{\mathcal{Z}}}=\sqrt{\sum_{z=(s_{z},a_{z},V_{z}(\cdot))\in{\mathcal{Z}}}\left(f(s_{z},a_{z},V_{z}(\cdot)\right)^{2}}.

Recall also that the bonus function is

\displaystyle b_{h}^{k}(s,a)=\sup_{f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:\min\{||f_{1}-f_{2}||_{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{k}},T(H+1)^{2}\}\leq\beta}|f_{1}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot))-f_{2}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot))|.

The parameter
\beta
will be defined later in a way that
will ensure optimism.

First, we need to show that at for every
k\in[K],h\in[H]

is inspired by
(
Kong et al. 2021
)
.

We
define the following quantities

\displaystyle\underline{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\left\{(f_{1},f_{2})\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\times{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\leq\alpha/100\right\}

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\left\{(f_{1},f_{2})\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\times{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:\min\{||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{k}},T(H+1)^{2}\}\leq\alpha\right\}

\displaystyle\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\left\{(f_{1},f_{2})\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}\times{\mathcal{F}}_{h}:||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\leq 100\alpha\right\}.

\displaystyle\underline{b}_{h}^{k}(s,a)=\sup_{f_{1},f_{2}\in\underline{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\beta)}|f_{1}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot))-f_{2}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot))|

\underline{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\alpha)\subseteq\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\alpha)\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\alpha)

Lemma B.5

The probability that all the events

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}\bigcap_{h=1}^{H}{\cal E}_{h}^{k}\right)\geq 1-\delta.

Lemma B.6

Let

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\E[X_{i}^{2}|Y_{0},\ldots,Y_{i-1}]\leq\sigma^{2}.

Then, for all

we have that

\displaystyle\Pr(|Y_{n}-Y_{0}|\geq t)\leq 2\exp{\left\{-\frac{t^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+Rt/3}\right\}}.

Moreover, we need a bound on the number of elements that are in the sub-sampled dataset.
This is established in
Kong et al. 2021

Lemma B.7
.

(

We have that with probability at least

The subsequent lemma shows that, indeed, whenever

Lemma B.8
.

(

Whenever the event

\displaystyle\frac{1}{10000}||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\leq\min\{||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{k}},T(H+1)^{2}\}\leq 10000||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}},\textnormal{ if }||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}>100\beta

\displaystyle\min\{||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{k}},T(H+1)^{2}\}\leq 10000\beta,\textnormal{ if }||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\leq 100\beta.

To establish our result, we need the following lemma. The proof follows the approach of

Lemma B.9
.

For any
\alpha\in[\beta,T(H+1)^{2}]

Let
C_{1}

We consider a fixed pair of functions

We also define

\displaystyle Y_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{p_{z_{h}^{i-1}}}(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}&\text{$z_{h}^{i-1}$ is added to $\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{i}$ and $Z_{i}\leq 2000000\alpha$}\\
0,&\text{$z_{h}^{i-1}$ is not added to $\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{i}$ and $Z_{i}\leq 2000000\alpha$}\\
(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Let
\mathbb{F}_{i}
be the filtration that
Y_{i}
is adapted to. Our goal is to use Freedman’s inequality (i.e. Lemma
B.6
) for
Y_{i}
.
Notice that
\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i}]=(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}

Y_{i}
. Notice that if
p_{z_{h}^{i-1}}=1

Y_{i}
is deterministic so
Y_{i}-\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i-1}]=\text{Var}[Y_{i}-\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i-1}]]=0

p_{z_{h}^{i}}=\min\{1,C^{\prime}\cdot\text{sensitivity}_{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{i-1},{\mathcal{F}}_{h}}(z_{h}^{i})\cdot\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},\sqrt{\delta/64T^{3}})/\delta)\}=\min\{1,C_{1}\cdot\text{sensitivity}_{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{i-1},{\mathcal{F}}_{h}}(z_{h}^{i})\}

\displaystyle\text{sensitivity}_{{\mathcal{Z}},{\mathcal{F}}}(z)=\min\left\{\sup_{f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{F}}}\frac{(f_{1}(z)-f_{2}(z))^{2}}{\min\{||f_{1}-f_{2}||_{\mathcal{{\mathcal{Z}}}},T(H+1)^{2}\}+\beta},1\right\}

Since
p_{z_{h}^{i-1}}<1\implies C_{1}\cdot\text{sensitivity}_{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}^{i-1}_{h},{\mathcal{F}}_{h}}(z_{h}^{i})<1

Y_{i}\neq 0

Y_{i}\geq\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i}]
so
|Y_{i}-\E[Y_{i}]|\leq Y_{i}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle Y_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}}{p_{z_{h}^{i-1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}}{C_{1}\sup_{f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}}\frac{(f_{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}}{\min\{||f_{1}-f_{2}||_{\mathcal{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}}_{h}^{i-1}},T(H+1)^{2}\}+\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq 2000001\alpha/C_{1}<3000000\alpha/C_{1}

Thus, we see that

. On the other hand, we can see that if
Y_{i}=0

|\E_{i-1}[Y_{i}]-Y_{i}|=(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}
and the inequality
we derived above still holds.

For the variance, we can see that

\displaystyle\text{Var}[Y_{i}-\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i}]|\mathbb{F}_{i}]

\displaystyle=p_{z_{h}}^{i-1}\left(\frac{1}{p_{z_{h}^{i-1}}}(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}\right)^{2}+(1-p_{z_{h}}^{i-1})\cdot 0

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p_{z_{h}^{i-1}}}(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{4}

\displaystyle\leq 3000000\alpha\left(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1})\right)^{2}/C_{1}

where the first equality follows from the definition, the first inequality is trivial

k^{\prime}
be the maximum number
\leq k

\displaystyle\sum_{i=2}^{k}\text{Var}[Y_{i}-\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i}]|\mathbb{F}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=2}^{k^{\prime}}\text{Var}[Y_{i}-\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i}]|\mathbb{F}_{i}]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3000000\alpha}{C_{1}}\sum_{i=2}^{k^{\prime}}(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3000000\alpha\cdot 2000000\alpha}{C_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(3000000\alpha)^{2}}{C_{1}}

where the the first equality follows from the fact that for

the random variable is deterministic, the first inequality follows by the summation of the previous one and the second one by the fact that
\sum_{i=2}^{k^{\prime}}(f_{h}^{1}(z_{h}^{i-1})-f_{h}^{2}(z_{h}^{i-1}))^{2}\leq||f_{h}^{1}-f_{h}^{2}||_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k^{\prime}}}\leq Z_{k^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}(Y_{i}-\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i}])\right|\geq\alpha/100\right)

\displaystyle=\Pr\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k^{\prime}}(Y_{i}-\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i}])\right|\geq\alpha/100\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\exp\left\{-\frac{(\alpha/100)^{2}/2}{(3000000\alpha)^{2}/C_{1}+\alpha^{2}3000000/300C_{1}}\right\}

\displaystyle=2\exp\left\{-\frac{C(\log((T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},\sqrt{\delta/64T^{3}})/\delta)^{2}))}{40000(3000000+10000)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq(\delta/64T^{2})/({\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},\sqrt{\delta/64T^{3}}))^{2}

for some choice of

. Now we can take a union bound over all the functions in the discretized set and conclude that with probability at least

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=1}^{k}(Y_{i}-\E[Y_{i}|\mathbb{F}_{i}])\right|\leq\alpha/100

for all pairs of functions in this set.

B.7
. We first show that when this event happens, we have that
\underline{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\alpha)\subseteq\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\alpha)
. Consider
f_{1},f_{2}\in\underline{{\mathcal{C}}}_{h}^{k}(\alpha)

f_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{F}},\sqrt{\delta/(64T^{3})})\times{\mathcal{C}}({\mathcal{F}},\sqrt{\delta/(64T^{3})})

\displaystyle||f_{1}^{\prime}-f_{2}^{\prime}||_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(||f_{1}-f_{1}^{\prime}||_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}+||f_{2}-f_{2}^{\prime}||_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}+||f_{1}-f_{2}||_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(||f_{1}-f_{2}||_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}+2\sqrt{\delta|{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}|/(64T^{3})}\right)^{2}\leq\alpha/50

Y_{i}

.

.

Thus, in any case we have that
||f_{1}^{\prime}-f_{2}^{\prime}||_{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{k}}^{2}>40\alpha>\alpha
, so we get the result.

Proof of Lemma
B.5
:

We know that for all
k\in[K],k\neq 1,h\in[H]
it holds that

\displaystyle\Pr({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k-1})-\Pr({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k})

\displaystyle=\Pr\left({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k-1}({\cal E}_{h}^{k})^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\Pr\left({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k-1}\left(\cap_{n=0}^{\infty}{\cal E}_{h}^{k}(100^{n}\beta)\right)^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\Pr\left({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k-1}\cup_{n=0}^{\infty}{\cal E}_{h}^{k}(100^{n}\beta)^{c}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Pr\left({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k-1}({\cal E}_{h}^{k}(100^{n}\beta))^{c}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0,100^{n}\beta\leq T(H+1)^{2}}\Pr\left({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k-1}({\cal E}_{h}^{k}(100^{n}\beta))^{c}\right).

Thus, using Lemma
B.9
we see that
\Pr({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k-1})-\Pr({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k})\leq\delta/(32T^{2})(\log(T(H+1)^{2}/\beta)+2)\leq\delta/32T
.

Hence, for any fixed
h\in[H]
we get

\displaystyle\Pr\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{K}{\cal E}_{h}^{k}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\Pr({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k-1})-\Pr({\cal E}_{h}^{1}{\cal E}_{h}^{2}\ldots{\cal E}_{h}^{k})\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-K(\delta/32T)=1-\delta/(32H)

and by taking a union bound over
h\in[H]
we get the result.

\Box

Now that we have shown that the sub-sampled dataset approximates well the original one, we shift our attention back to showing that our approach
achieves optimism.

We first need a definition and a concetration lemma
that is related to least-squares-estimators from prior
work.

Definition B.10
.

A random variable
X
is conditionally
\sigma
-subgaussian
with respect to some filtration
\mathbb{F}
if for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

it holds that
\mathbb{E}[\exp(\lambda X)]\leq\exp(\lambda^{2}\sigma^{2}/2)
.

Lemma B.11

(
(
Russo & Van Roy 2013
)
,
(
Ayoub et al. 2020
)
)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathbb{F}_{p}\}_{p=0,1,\ldots}
be a filtration,
\{(X_{p},Y_{p})\}_{p}
measurable random variables where
X_{p}\in\mathcal{X},Y_{p}\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
be a set of measurable functions from
\mathcal{X}
to
\mathbb{R}
and assume that
\E[Y_{p}|\mathbb{F}_{p-1}]=f^{*}(X_{p})
for some
f^{*}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}
. Assume that
\{Y_{p}-f^{*}(X_{p})\}_{p=1,\ldots}
is conditionally
\sigma
-subgaussian given
\mathbb{F}_{p-1}
. Let
\widehat{f}_{t}=\arg\min_{f\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}}\sum_{p=1}^{t}\left(f(X_{p})-Y_{p}\right)^{2}
and
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{t}(\beta)=\left\{f\in\widetilde{F}:\sum_{p=1}^{t}\left(f(X_{p})-\widehat{f}(X_{p}))\right)^{2}\leq\beta\right\}
. Then, for any
\alpha>0
, with probability
1-\delta
, for all
t\geq 1
it holds that
f^{*}\in\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}_{t}(\beta_{t}(\delta,\alpha))
, where

\displaystyle\beta_{t}(\delta,\alpha)=8\sigma^{2}\log(2{\mathcal{N}}(\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}},\alpha)/\delta)+4t\alpha\left(C+\sqrt{\sigma^{2}\log(4t(t+1)/\delta)}\right).

We are now ready to prove that our algorithm ensures optimism.

Lemma B.12
.

With probability at least
1-2\delta
, we have that for all
h\in[H],k\in[K],s\in{\mathcal{S}},a\in{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{k}(s,a)-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s,a)\leq\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)-V_{h+1}^{\pi_{k}}(\cdot)\rangle+2b_{h}^{k}(s,a).

Moreover, it holds that
Q_{h}^{k}(s,a)\geq Q_{h}^{*}(s,a)
.

Proof.

Fix some
h\in[H],k\in[K],s\in{\mathcal{S}},a\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Throughout the proof, we condition on the events in Lemma
B.5
and Lemma
B.11
.
We assume that
k
is a round that we perform an update.

We define
{\mathcal{X}}={\mathcal{S}}\times{\mathcal{A}}\times{\cal V},X_{h}^{k}=(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)),Y_{h}^{k}=V_{h+1}^{k}(s^{k}_{h+1})
. We also pick
\widetilde{{\mathcal{F}}}={\mathcal{F}}_{h}
, where
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
is defined
in Definition
B.4
. Then, we see that
\mathbb{E}[Y_{h}^{k}|\mathbb{F}_{k-1}]=f^{*}_{h}(X_{h}^{k})
, where
f^{*}_{h}
is the function
that corresponds to the true model
P_{h}
, and we know that
f^{*}_{h}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
.
Recall that the optimization problem we solve in Algorithm
1
for every round
k
we
update our policy is

\widehat{P}_{h}^{k}=\arg\min_{P\in{\cal P}_{h}}\sum_{p=1}^{k}\left(\langle P(\cdot|s^{p}_{h},a^{p}_{h}),V_{h+1}^{p}\rangle-V_{h+1}^{p}(s^{p}_{h+1})\right)^{2}.

Based on the definition of
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
, we can see that this is equivalent to

f_{h}^{k}=\arg\min_{f\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}}\sum_{p=1}^{k}\left(f(s_{h}^{p},a_{h}^{p},V_{h+1}^{p})-V_{h+1}^{p}(s^{p}_{h+1})\right)^{2}.

Moreoever,
Y_{h}^{k}\in[0,H]
, so
Z_{h}^{k}=Y_{h}^{k}-f_{h}^{*}(X_{h}^{k})
is
H/2
-conditionally subgaussian.
Thus, if we pick
\alpha=1/T
and

\beta_{h}^{k}=4H^{2}\log(2{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/T)/\delta)+4k/T\left(C+\sqrt{H^{2}/4\log(4(k(k+1)/\delta)})\right)

then Lemma
B.11
gives us that
||f^{*}_{h}-\widehat{f}_{h}^{k}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\leq\beta_{h}^{k}
.
In particular,
can pick

\widetilde{\beta}=\beta_{h}^{K}=4H^{2}\log(2{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/T)/\delta)+4K/T\left(C+\sqrt{H^{2}/4\log(4(K(K+1)/\delta)})\right)

and get a parameter that is independent of
k
. Moreover, Lemma
B.5

||f_{h}^{*}-\widehat{f}_{h}^{k}||_{\widehat{{\mathcal{Z}}}_{h}^{k}}\leq 100\widetilde{\beta}=\beta.

This implies that for our bonus function we have that
|\widehat{f}_{h}^{k}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot))-f_{h}^{*}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot))|\leq b_{h}^{k}(s,a)
.

Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{P}_{h}^{k}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle-\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}_{h}^{k}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k})-f^{*}_{h}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq|\widehat{f}_{h}^{k}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k})-f^{*}_{h}(s,a,V_{h+1}^{k})|

\displaystyle\leq b_{h}^{k}(s,a).

Now we use the definition of
Q_{h}^{k}(s,a),Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s,a)
to get that

\displaystyle Q^{k}_{h}(s,a)

\displaystyle\leq r_{h}^{k}(s,a)+\langle\widehat{P}_{h}^{k}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle+b_{h}^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle\leq r_{h}^{k}(s,a)+\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle+2b_{h}^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle Q^{\pi_{k}}_{h}(s,a)

\displaystyle=r_{h}^{k}(s,a)+\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{\pi_{k}}(\cdot)\rangle.

Combining these two, we get that

\displaystyle Q^{k}_{h}(s,a)-Q^{\pi_{k}}_{h}(s,a)\leq\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)-V_{h+1}^{\pi_{k}}(\cdot)\rangle+2b_{h}^{k}(s,a)

which proves the first part of the result.

For the second part, notice that if
Q_{h}^{k}(s,a)=H
then the statement holds trivially since
Q^{*}_{h}(s,a)\leq H
. So we can assume without loss of generality that
Q_{h}^{k}(s,a)=r_{h}^{k}(s,a)+\langle\widehat{P}_{h}^{k}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle+b_{h}^{k}(s,a)
. The Bellman optimality condition gives us that
Q^{*}_{h}(s,a)=r_{h}^{k}(s,a)+\langle P_{h}^{*}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{*}(\cdot)\rangle
. Hence, we have that

\displaystyle Q_{h}^{k}(s,a)-Q_{h}^{*}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{P}_{h}^{k}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle-\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{*}(\cdot)\rangle+b_{h}^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{P}_{h}^{k}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle-\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle+\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle-\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{*}(\cdot)\rangle+b_{h}^{k}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{P}_{h}^{k}(\cdot|s,a)-P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle+\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)-V_{h+1}^{*}(\cdot)\rangle+b_{h}^{k}(s,a).

Now from our previous discussion it follows that
b_{h}^{k}(s,a)+\langle\widehat{P}_{h}^{k}(\cdot|s,a)-P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)\rangle\geq 0
. Hence, it suffices to show that
\langle P_{h}(\cdot|s,a),V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)-V_{h+1}^{*}(\cdot)\rangle\geq 0
. To do that, we can just prove that
V_{h+1}^{k}(s^{\prime})-V_{h+1}^{*}(s^{\prime})\geq 0,\forall s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
. Since
V_{H+1}^{k}(s^{\prime})=V_{H+1}^{*}(s^{\prime})=0,\forall s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
we get that
Q_{H}^{k}(s,a)\geq Q_{H}^{*}(s,a)
. Thus, if we combine this with the update rule for
V_{h}^{k},V_{h}^{*}
we get the claim by induction.
∎

Now that we have established the previous lemma, we need
to bound the bonus that we are using in every round.
The issue is that we do not update our policy in every round.

To do that, we follow a similar approach as in Appendix
A
.

Lemma B.13
.

For every set
K^{\prime}\subseteq[K]
With probability at least
1-\delta
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h=1}^{H}b_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\leq H+H(H+1)\dim_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)+CH\sqrt{\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)|K^{\prime}|\beta}

where
\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)=\max_{h\in[H]}\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/T)
.

Proof.

We condition on the event described in Lemma
B.5
. From the definition of the bonus function, we have that for any
k\in[K]

\displaystyle b_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\leq\bar{b}_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})=\sup_{||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\leq 100\beta}|f_{1}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})-f_{2}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})|.

We bound
\sum_{i=1}^{K^{\prime}}\bar{b}_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})
for each
h\in[H]
separately.

Given some
\epsilon>0
, we define
\widetilde{K}=\{k\in K^{\prime}:\bar{b}_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})>\epsilon\}
,
i.e. the set of episodes in
K^{\prime}
where the bonus function at
h

has value greater than
\epsilon
. Consider some
k\in K^{\prime}
. We denote

{\mathcal{L}}_{h}=\{(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot)):k\in\widetilde{K}\},L_{h}=|{\mathcal{L}}_{h}|
, and
N=L_{h}/\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},\epsilon)-1
.
Our goal is to show that there is some
z_{h}^{k}=(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot))\in{\mathcal{L}}_{h}

that is
\epsilon
-dependent on at least
N
disjoint subsequences in
{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}\cap{\mathcal{L}}_{h}
.

To do that, we decompose
{\mathcal{L}}_{h}
into
N+1
disjoint subsets and we denote the
j
-th subset by
{\mathcal{L}}_{h,j}
.
We use the following procedure. Initially we set
{\mathcal{L}}_{h,j}=\emptyset
for all
j\in[N+1]

and consider every
z_{h}^{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{h}
in a sequential manner.
For each such
z_{h}^{k}
we find the smallest index
j,1\leq j\leq N
,
such that
z_{h}^{k}
is
\epsilon
-independent of the elements in
{\mathcal{L}}_{h,j}
with
respect to
{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
. If there is no such
j
, we set
j=N+1
.
Then, we update
{\mathcal{L}}_{h,j}\leftarrow{\mathcal{L}}_{h,j}\cup z_{h}^{k}
.
Notice that after we go through all the elements of
{\mathcal{L}}_{h}
,
we must have that
{\mathcal{L}}_{h,N+1}\neq\emptyset
.
This is because every set
{\mathcal{L}}_{h,j},1\leq j\leq N
, contains at most

\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},\epsilon)
elements.
Moreover, by definition, every element
z_{h}^{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{h,N+1}
is

\epsilon
-dependent on at least
N
disjoint
subsequences in
{\mathcal{L}}_{h}
.

Furthermore, since
\bar{b}_{h}^{k}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})>\epsilon
for all
z_{h}^{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{h}

there must exist
f_{1},f_{2}\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}
such that
|f_{1}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot))-f_{2}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},V_{h+1}^{k}(\cdot))|>\epsilon
and

||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\leq 100\beta
. Hence, since
z_{h}^{k}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{h,N+1}
is

\epsilon
-dependent on
N
disjoint subsequences
{\mathcal{L}}_{h}
and for each such
subsequence
{\mathcal{L}}
, by the definition of
\epsilon
-dependence, it holds that
||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{L}}}>\epsilon^{2}
we have that

\displaystyle N\epsilon^{2}\leq||f_{1}-f_{2}||^{2}_{{\mathcal{Z}}_{h}^{k}}\leq 100\beta

\displaystyle\implies(L_{h}/\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},\epsilon)-1)\epsilon^{2}\leq 100\beta

\displaystyle\implies L_{h}\leq\left(\frac{100\beta}{\epsilon^{2}}+1\right)\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},\epsilon).

We now pick a permutation
\bar{b}_{1}\geq\bar{b}_{2}\geq\ldots\geq\bar{b}_{|K^{\prime}|}
of the bonus
functions
\{\bar{b}_{h}^{k}(s^{k}_{h},a^{k}_{h})\}_{k\in K^{\prime}}
. For all
\bar{b}_{k}\geq 1/|K^{\prime}|
it holds
that

\displaystyle k\leq\left(\frac{100\beta}{\bar{b}_{k}^{2}}+1\right)\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},\bar{b}_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{100\beta}{\bar{b}_{k}^{2}}+1\right)\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/K^{\prime})\implies

\displaystyle\bar{b}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{k}{\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/K^{\prime})}-1\right)^{-1/2}\sqrt{100\beta}.

Moreover, notice that we get by definition that
\bar{b}_{k}\leq H+1
. Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\bar{b}_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i:\bar{b}_{k_{i}}<1/|K^{\prime}|}\bar{b}_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})+\sum_{i:\bar{b}_{k_{i}}\geq 1/|K^{\prime}|}\bar{b}_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq|K^{\prime}|\cdot 1/|K^{\prime}|+\sum_{i:\bar{b}_{k_{i}}\geq 1/|K^{\prime}|,i\leq\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/|K^{\prime}|)}\bar{b}_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})+\sum_{i:\bar{b}_{k_{i}}\geq 1/|K^{\prime}|,|K^{\prime}|\geq i>\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/|K^{\prime}|)}\bar{b}_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq 1+(H+1)\dim_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/|K^{\prime}|)+\sum_{|K^{\prime}|\geq i>\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/|K^{\prime}|)}\left(\frac{k}{\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/|K^{\prime}|)}-1\right)^{-1/2}\sqrt{100\beta}

\displaystyle\leq 1+(H+1)\dim_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/|K^{\prime}|)+C\sqrt{\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/|K^{\prime}|)|K^{\prime}|\beta}

\displaystyle\leq 1+(H+1)\dim_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/T)+C\sqrt{\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/T)|K^{\prime}|\beta}

for some constant
C>0
, where the second to last inequality can be obtained by bounding the summation by the integral and the last one by the definition of the eluder dimension. We get the final result by summing up all the inequalities over
H
.
∎

The next step in our proof, is to bound the number of episodes that our policy
can be worse than the optimal one
by
2^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This
is inspired by
He et al. 2021
.

Lemma B.14
.

If we pick

\beta=4H^{2}\log(2{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)/\delta)+4/H\left(C+\sqrt{H^{2}/4\log(4(K(K+1)/\delta)})\right),

then for every
h\in[H]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with probability at least
1-(K+3)\delta
, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}\left[V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\right]\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}H^{4}\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}}_{h},1/T)/\delta)\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)}{4^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}^{2}}.

Proof.

We keep
h
fixed.

We denote by
K^{\prime}
the set of episodes where the gap at step
h
is at least
2^{n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle K^{\prime}=\left\{k\in[K]:V^{*}_{h}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\right\}.

The goal is to bound the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)
from below and above with functions

f_{1}(|K^{\prime}|),f_{2}(|K^{\prime}|)
and then use the fact that
f_{1}(|K^{\prime}|)\leq f_{2}(|K^{\prime}|)
to derive an upper bound on
|K^{\prime}|
.

For the lower bound, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},\pi_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k_{i}}))-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},\pi_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k_{i}}))-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}|K^{\prime}|

where the first inequality holds by the definition of the policy
\pi_{k_{i}}
, the second one follows because
Q^{k_{i}}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
is an optimistic estimate of
Q^{*}_{h}(\cdot,\cdot)
which happens with probability at least
1-2\delta
(see Lemma
B.12
) and the third one by the definition of
k_{i}
.

We get the upper bound on this quantity in the following way. For any
h^{\prime}\in[H]
we have

\displaystyle Q_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})-Q_{h^{\prime}}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}}P_{h^{\prime}}(s^{\prime}|s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})V^{k}_{h^{\prime}+1}(s^{\prime})+r_{h^{\prime}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})+2b_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})-Q_{h^{\prime}}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

\displaystyle=\left\langle P_{h^{\prime}}(\cdot|s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k}),V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k}-V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{k}}\right\rangle+2b_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

\displaystyle=V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})-V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})+\epsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k}+2b_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

\displaystyle=Q_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})-Q_{h^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})+\epsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k}+2b_{h^{\prime}}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k})

where we define
\epsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k}=\left\langle P_{h^{\prime}}(\cdot|s_{h^{\prime}}^{k},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k}),V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k}-V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{k}}\right\rangle-\left(V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})-V_{h^{\prime}+1}^{\pi_{k}}(s_{h^{\prime}+1}^{k})\right)
and the inequality follows from Lemma
B.12
.

We now take the summation over all
k\in|K^{\prime}|,h\leq h^{\prime}\leq H
and we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}\left(Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}\epsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}b_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}).

We will bound each of the two terms on the RHS separately.

For the first term, we notice that
x_{j}=\left\langle P_{j}(\cdot|s_{j}^{k_{i}},a_{j}^{k_{i}}),V_{j+1}^{k_{i}}-V_{j+1}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}\right\rangle-\left(V_{j+1}^{k_{i}}(s_{j+1}^{k_{i}})-V_{j+1}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{j+1}^{k_{i}})\right)
forms a martingale difference sequence with zero mean and
|x_{j}|\leq 2H
. Hence, we can use Lemma
A.7
and that for each
k\in K^{\prime}
, with probability at least
1-\delta
we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{H}\left(\left\langle P_{j}(\cdot|s_{j}^{k_{i}},a_{j}^{k_{i}}),V_{j+1}^{k_{i}}-V_{j+1}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}\right\rangle-\left(V_{j+1}^{k_{i}}(s_{j+1}^{k_{i}})-V_{j+1}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{j+1}^{k_{i}})\right)\right)\leq\sqrt{8kH^{3}\log(1/\delta)}.

If we take the union bound over all
k\in[K]
we have that with probability at least
1-|K^{\prime}|\delta

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}\epsilon_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}\leq\sqrt{8|K^{\prime}|H^{3}\log(1/\delta)}.

We now focus on the second term. Using Lemma
B.13
we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\sum_{h^{\prime}=h}^{H}b_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}}(s_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}},a_{h^{\prime}}^{k_{i}})\leq H+H(H+1)\dim_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)+CH\sqrt{\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)|K^{\prime}|\beta}

and this happens with probability at least
1-\delta
. Hence, combining the upper and lower bound of

\sum_{i=1}^{|K^{\prime}|}\left(Q_{h}^{k_{i}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})-Q_{h}^{\pi_{k_{i}}}(s_{h}^{k_{i}},a_{h}^{k_{i}})\right)

we get that

\displaystyle 2^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}|K^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{8|K^{\prime}|H^{3}\log(1/\delta)}+H+H(H+1)\dim_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)+CH\sqrt{\textnormal{dim}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)|K^{\prime}|\beta}

\displaystyle\implies|K^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\widetilde{C}H^{4}\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)/\delta)\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)}{4^{n}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}^{2}}.

∎

We are now ready to prove Lemma
B.1
.

Proof of Lemma
B.1
:

Throughout this proof we condition on the event described in Lemma
B.14
which happens with probability at least
1-(K+3)\delta
. Since
{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}>0
whenever we do not take the optimal action, we have that either
V_{h}^{*}(s_{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})=0
or
V_{h}^{*}(s_{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}
. Our approach is to divide the interval
[0,H]
into
N=\lceil\log(H/{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min})\rceil
intervals and count the number of
V_{h}^{*}(s_{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})
that fall into each interval. Notice that for every
V_{h}^{*}(s_{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})
that falls into interval
i
we can get an upper bound of
V_{h}^{*}(s_{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\leq 2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}
and this upper bound is essentially tight. Hence, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}\left[2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\geq V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\right]\cdot 2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbbm{1}\left[V_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k})-Q_{h}^{*}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\geq 2^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}\right]\cdot 2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\widetilde{C}H^{6}\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)/\delta)\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)}{4^{i-1}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}^{2}}\cdot 2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{C^{\prime}H^{4}\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)/\delta)\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)}{2^{i}{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{CH^{4}\log(T{\mathcal{N}}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)/\delta)\dim^{2}_{E}({\mathcal{F}},1/T)}{{\textnormal{gap}}_{\min}}

where the first inequality holds by the definition of the intervals, the second due to the properties of the indicator function, the third because of Lemma
B.14
and in the last two steps we just manipulate the constants.

\Box

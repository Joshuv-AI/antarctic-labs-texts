Anomalous gravitomagnetic moment and non-universality of the axial vortical effect at finite temperature
M. Buzzegoli, Dmitri E. Kharzeev

Abstract
The coupling between the spin of a massive Dirac fermion and the angular momentum of the medium, i.e. the gravitomagnetic moment, is shown here to be renormalized by QED interactions at finite temperature. This means that the anomalous gravitomagnetic moment (AGM) does not vanish, and implies that thermal effects can break the Einstein equivalence principle in quantum field theory, as argued previously. We also show that the AGM causes radiative corrections to the axial current of massive fermions induced by vorticity in quantum relativistic fluids, similarly to the previous findings for massless fermions. The radiative QCD effects on the AGM should significantly affect the production of polarized hadrons in heavy-ion collisions.

Introduction

Recent experiments

;
Adam et al. 2018
;
Adam et al. 2019
at the Relativistic
Heavy Ion Collider (RHIC) have opened the possibility to study the effects of vorticity and magnetic field on the production of polarized hadrons
in relativistic heavy-ion collisions, see
Becattini and Lisa 2020
for a recent review.
The coupling between the spin and vorticity of the quark gluon
plasma
Liang and Wang 2005
;
Kharzeev 2006
;
Becattini et al. 2008
induces a polarization of the hadrons emitted
from the fluid, which can be measured in experiments. The spin and the polarization of
a spin
1/2
particle, like the
\Lambda
hyperon, is deeply connected to the axial
current of the particle. In a rotating medium, the coupling between the spin of a fermion and rotation of the medium
Vilenkin 1979
also induces a thermal effect on the axial current, known as the Axial Vortical Effect (AVE).

The AVE is a macroscopic quantum effect describing the axial
charge separation along the rotation of the fluid
Landsteiner et al. 2011a
;
Gao et al. 2012
;
Kharzeev et al. 2016
.
In the case of a system composed of massless fermions, one can give a simple intuitive picture
of the phenomenon. Indeed, as a result of the coupling between the fermions’ spin and the rotation of the medium, the spins of the
fermions are preferably aligned along the angular momentum vector of the medium. Then, since for
massless fermions chirality coincides with helicity (note that for antifermions, chirality and helicity are opposite), most of the right-handed particles
will move along the direction of the vorticity (pseudo)vector, while most of the left-handed particles will
move in the opposite direction. This flow causes the net separation between the right- and
left-handed particles that we refer to as the AVE. Since the AVE is
driven by spin-rotation coupling, it can be realized even in a global thermal equilibrium, where the expectation value of the axial current is given by
Buzzegoli and Becattini 2018

\langle\widehat{j}^{\mu}_{\textup{A}}\rangle=n_{\textup{A}}u^{\mu}+W^{\textup{A}}\frac{\omega^{\mu}}{T}.

where
n_{A}
is the density of axial charge,
u
is the fluid velocity,
\omega
is the vorticity of the medium, and
T
is the temperature.
For free massless fermions the AVE conductivity
W^{\textup{A}}
is found to
be
Vilenkin 1979
;
Landsteiner et al. 2011a
;
Gao et al. 2012

W^{\textup{A}}=\frac{T^{3}}{6}+\frac{\mu^{2}+\mu_{\textup{A}}^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}T,

where
\mu
is the vector chemical potential and

\mu_{\textup{A}}
is the axial chemical potential.

such as the Chiral Magnetic Effect (CME)

;
Kharzeev et al. 2008

First, both the axial current

Second, the AVE conductivity is not protected against radiative
corrections: explicit calculations in massless quantum electrodynamics (QED) and
quantum chromodynamics (QCD) show that AVE conductivity is renormalized by interactions

;
Golkar and Son 2015

This correction only affects the finite-temperature part of the AVE conductivity, while the parts
proportional to the axial chemical potential and the vector chemical potential are

The radiative corrections to AVE in QED can be
interpreted as driven by the interaction of a graviton with the photon cloud surrounding
the fermion. Indeed, we may understand the coupling between the chirality of fermions and rotation of the medium
induced by this
diagram as follows. Suppose the lower fermion line inside the loop in
Fig.
1
has a right chirality. Then it couples more effectively to
photons having a polarization parallel to the fermion spin. Following the loop in the
figure, those photons then interact with the rotation of the medium (represented by the
insertion of stress-energy tensor in the diagram) forcing their polarization to rotate – so when
they couple to the fermion in the loop again, they can induce the chirality flip of the fermion, and transform it into
a left-handed one. Note that this requires the presence of photons in the rotating fluid, which in thermal equlibrium implies a finite temperature. At zero temperature, the photon cloud is part of the coherent state of a charged fermion, and thus rotates together with it. At finite temperature, the thermal bath possesses its own photons, and mixing between these photons and the photons that form the coherent cloud of the fermion can cause the rotation of the fermion’s polarization.

In analogy with the magnetic moment, the quantity that describes the coupling of a fermion’s spin to rotation is
a gravitomagnetic moment. The picture discussed above thus suggests that we can
describe the radiative correction to this quantity as an Anomalous Gravitomagnetic Moment (AGM), as Fig.
1
is analogous to the diagram describing the anomalous magnetic moment in QED.
In this work we validate the connection between the AGM and the radiative corrections to the AVE for the case of a massive fermion. We show
that the gravitomagnetic moment of a massive fermion in thermal equilibrium
is indeed anomalous, and that the radiative corrections to the AVE
in the low temperature regime are due to the gravitomagnetic moment.

The emergence of the AGM may seem surprising, as the Einstein equivalence principle
forbids the appearance of an anomalous spin-rotation coupling
Kobzarev and Okun 1962
;
Teryaev 2016
.
In the
language of quantum field theory, the equivalence principle is translated into
the Lorentz invariance of the local coupling of gravitational fields to the
stress-energy tensor
Cho and Hari Dass 1976
. This is why the presence of an AGM is excluded
for both elementary
Cho and Hari Dass 1976
and composite
Teryaev 2016
;
Teryaev 2007
particles.
However, an explicit renormalization of stress-energy tensor for massive QED at
finite temperature has demonstrated that the effects of temperature break the weak equivalence
principle (i.e., gravitational and inertial mass are no longer
equivalent)
Donoghue et al. 1984
;
Donoghue et al. 1985
. The equivalence principle violation
can be ascribed to the fact that a finite temperature breaks the Lorentz invariance of the
vacuum and that, in the presence of a thermal bath, one can genuinely discern if the
system is under acceleration or under the effect of a gravitational field by making measurements in
reference to the thermal bath. Thus, we can expect, and indeed have found, the emergence of the AGM at finite
temperature.

Radiative corrections to the axial vortical effect for a massive field

In this section we evaluate the radiative corrections to the axial vortical

As any quantum operator can be written in terms of its matrix elements by expanding it
in multi-particle states

can be obtained by considering the radiative corrections to the following matrix elements:

annihilation operators and can be readily written down. An equivalent approach to
evaluating statistical averages is described in the textbook

where the density matrix is written in terms of its “in-picture” matrix elements.

III
Gravitomagnetic moment

In this section we introduce the gravitomagnetic moment and present the result
for the Anomalous Gravitomagnetic Moment (AGM) in finite temperature QED. The AGM can be
easily understood in analogy to the anomalous magnetic moment. The Dirac equation
in an external magnetic field and in the presence of rotation can be written using rotating coordinates
and takes the form

\left[\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}(D_{\mu}+\Gamma_{\mu})-m\right]\psi=0

with the covariant derivative
D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-\mathrm{i}eA_{\mu}
, the spin connection
\Gamma_{\mu}=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{4}\omega_{\mu ij}\sigma^{ij}

and
\sigma^{ij}=\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}[\gamma^{i},\gamma^{j}]
. Setting the magnetic field
\textstyle\mathbf{B}
and
the rotation
\textstyle\mathbf{\Omega}
along the
z
axis we find

\left[\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}+\mathrm{i}\gamma^{0}\Omega(y\partial_{x}-x\partial_{y}-\mathrm{i}\sigma^{12})-m\right]\psi=\left[\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}+\gamma^{0}\Omega(\widehat{L}_{z}+\widehat{S}_{z})-m\right]\psi=0.

Acting on this equation with
\left(\mathrm{i}\gamma^{\mu}(D_{\mu}+\Gamma_{\mu})+m\right)
we obtain
the second order Dirac equation

\left[\partial_{t}^{2}-\nabla^{2}-e\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{B}$}}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{L}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{L}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{L}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{L}$}}+2\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{S}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{S}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{S}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{S}$}})-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{L}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{L}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{L}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{L}$}}+\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{S}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{S}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{S}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{S}$}})+m^{2}\right]\psi=0.

The quantity which couples the magnetic field to the spin
\textstyle\mathbf{S}
is called the magnetic
moment
g_{B}
, while the quantity
g_{\Omega}
that couples the rotation to the spin is the
gravitomagnetic moment. Therefore, Dirac equation alone predicts that the spin couples
to the magnetic moment and rotation with

g_{B}=2,\qquad g_{\Omega}=1.

This is exactly what we expect for Dirac particles, as discussed
in
de Oliveira and Tiomno 1962
;
Mashhoon 1988
;
Hehl and Ni 1990
; spin-rotation coupling
has been reviewed in
Mashhoon 2000
;
Silenko and Teryaev 2007
.

However, one of the most precise predictions of QED is that the magnetic moment of
fermions is anomalous, i.e. the value given by the Dirac equation receives radiative corrections. The anomalous magnetic
moment can be found using scattering theory in QED in the non-relativistic limit.
Interactions with magnetic field are described by the Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}=J^{\mu}A_{\mu}
,
therefore radiative corrections to magnetic moment are obtained by evaluating the matrix
element of the electromagnetic current, which can be written as:

\langle p^{\prime},s^{\prime}|J^{\mu}(0)|p,s\rangle=\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\left\{\frac{P^{\mu}}{2m}F_{1}(q^{2})+\frac{\mathrm{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu}}{2m}\left[F_{1}(q^{2})+F_{2}(q^{2})\right]\right\}u(p,s)

where
|p,s\rangle
denotes a state with a fermion of momentum
p
and polarization

s
,
u(p,s)
denotes the Dirac spinor and we defined the momenta
P=p^{\prime}+p
and
q=p^{\prime}-p
;

F_{1},F_{2}
are the form factors. At first order in the transferred momentum

the matrix element is

\langle p^{\prime},s^{\prime}|J^{\mu}(0)|p,s\rangle=\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\left\{\frac{P^{\mu}}{2m}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\sigma^{\mu\nu}q_{\nu}}{2m}\left[1+F_{2}(0)\right]\right\}u(p,s)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}).

The from factor
F_{2}(0)
gives directly the anomalous magnetic moment, which was first evaluated
by Schwinger

\alpha=e^{2}/4\pi
from the vertex correction.
He obtained the famous result
1
1

1

Peressutti and Skagerstam 1982

a_{B}\equiv\frac{g_{B}-2}{2}=F_{2}(0)=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}.

Following a similar argument using scattering theory (as explained in detail in

In the weak gravitational field limit, the gravitational coupling is described

coupling as dictated by the Einstein equivalence principle

We are now interested in finite temperature effects, therefore we consider a Dirac fermion

,
which implies

q_{0}=0,\quad p_{0}=p_{0}^{\prime}=\sqrt{m^{2}+(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}^{2}+\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}^{2})/4},\quad|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{p}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{p}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{p}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{p}$}}^{\prime}|=|\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{p}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{p}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{p}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{p}$}}|.

In a generic frame, we take advantage of orthogonality relation
q\cdot P=0
and introduce

\omega_{P}\equiv P\cdot u
, so that we can define a space-like four-vector
\tilde{P}
orthogonal to
u
,
and a scalar
P_{s}
:

\tilde{P}_{\mu}\equiv(\eta_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu})P^{\nu}=P_{\mu}-\omega_{P}u_{\mu},\quad P_{s}\equiv\sqrt{\omega_{P}^{2}-P^{2}}.

To close the tetrad, we define the space-like unit four-vector
\hat{l}
orthogonal to
u,\,\tilde{P}

and
q
(it is also orthogonal to
P
):

l^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}u_{\nu}\tilde{P}_{\rho}q_{\sigma},\quad\hat{l}^{\mu}=\frac{l^{\mu}}{\sqrt{-l^{2}}}.

Note that
\tilde{P}\cdot q=0
and
q\cdot u=0
also hold true, meaning that
the tetrad
\{u,\,\tilde{P},\,q,\,\hat{l}\}
is an orthogonal non-normalized basis.
Using scattering theory (see Sec.
IV.2
), the gravitomagnetic moment is obtained by

g_{\Omega}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\to 0\\
P_{s}\to 0\end{subarray}}4\left(I_{P\gamma}(P,q)+\frac{I_{u\gamma}(P,q)}{\omega_{P}}-I_{Pl}(P,q)-\frac{I_{ul}(P,q)}{\omega_{P}}\right),

(15)

where the functions are the following form factors of the stress-energy tensor matrix element

\begin{split}\langle p^{\prime},s^{\prime}|T_{\mu\nu}(0)|p,s\rangle=\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\Big\{&I_{P\gamma}(P,q)\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+I_{u\gamma}(P,q)\left(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)\\
&+I_{Pl}(P,q)\not{\hat{l}}\left(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right)+I_{ul}(P,q)\not{\hat{l}}\left(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right)+\cdots\Big\}u(p,s)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}).\end{split}

(16)

Notice that in writing eq. (
16
) we have tacitly chosen to decompose the matrix
element with terms that do not contain more than one gamma matrix. At zero temperature the
decomposition (
16
) reads:

\begin{split}\langle p^{\prime},s^{\prime}|T_{\mu\nu}(0)|p,s\rangle=\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\Big\{&I_{P\gamma}(q^{2})P_{(\mu}\gamma_{\nu)}+I_{PP}(q^{2})\frac{P_{\mu}P_{\nu}}{m}\Big\}u(p,s)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}).\end{split}

(17)

However, there is a certain freedom in the choice of the form factors of the
stress-energy tensor stemming from the Gordon identity:

\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\gamma^{\mu}u(p,s)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\left[\frac{P^{\mu}}{2m}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\sigma^{\mu\alpha}q_{\alpha}}{2m}\right]u(p,s).

(18)

Different forms of this decomposition are used in the literature, for instance
in
Kobzarev and Okun 1962
the decomposition is written as:

\langle p^{\prime},s^{\prime}|T_{\mu\nu}(0)|p,s\rangle=\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\left[f_{1}(q^{2})\frac{P_{\mu}P_{\nu}}{m}+f_{2}(q^{2})\frac{\mathrm{i}\sigma_{(\mu\alpha}q^{\alpha}P_{\nu)}}{2m}+\cdots\right]u(p,s)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}),

(19)

while in
Teryaev 2016
the following decomposition is chosen:

\langle p^{\prime},s^{\prime}|T_{\mu\nu}(0)|p,s\rangle=\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s^{\prime})\left[A(q^{2})\gamma_{(\mu}P_{\nu)}+B(q^{2})\frac{\mathrm{i}\sigma_{(\mu\alpha}q^{\alpha}P_{\nu)}}{2m}\right]u(p,s)+\mathcal{O}(q^{2}).

(20)

By using the Gordon identity (
18
) it is straightforward to show that the
form factors in eq.s (
17
), (
19
)
and (
20
) are related to each other by:

I_{P\gamma}(q^{2})=A(q^{2})+B(q^{2})=f_{2}(q^{2}),\qquad I_{PP}(q^{2})=-B(q^{2})=f_{1}(q^{2})-f_{2}(q^{2}).

The presence of a finite temperature allows the introduction of additional form factors, which are
also not uniquely defined. However the argument presented in Sec.
IV.2
allows to
unambiguously identify the gravitomagnetic moment once a consistence choice of the decomposition
is made, in particular the decomposition in eq. (
16
) results in the
gravitomagnetic moment in eq. (
15
).

In App.
C
we evaluate the form factors in (
16
) by renormalizing
the stress-energy tensor in finite temperature QED. The full result at one-loop and at first
order in
q
is reported in App.
C.3
. In the limit
q\to 0
and
P_{s}\to 0
, the thermal
contributions to the form factors are

\begin{split}I_{P\gamma}=&-\frac{10+3\theta_{\textup{HT}}}{72}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{4m^{2}};\\
\frac{I_{u\gamma}}{\omega_{P}}=&-\frac{1}{9}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{4m^{2}};\\
I_{Pl}=&\frac{2-\theta_{\textup{HT}}}{72}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{4m^{2}};\\
\frac{I_{ul}}{\omega_{P}}=&-\frac{2+\theta_{\textup{HT}}}{18}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{4m^{2}},\\
\end{split}

(21)

where we introduced the function
\theta_{\textup{HT}}

\theta_{\textup{HT}}\equiv\begin{cases}0&T\ll m\\
1&T\gg m\end{cases}

which turns on the high temperature contributions. It is now straightforward to
compute the AGM from the relation (
15
) which gives

g_{\Omega}-1=-\frac{6-\theta_{\textup{HT}}}{36}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{m^{2}}=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{6}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{m^{2}}&T\ll m\\
-\frac{5}{36}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{m^{2}}&T\gg m,\,m>eT\end{cases}.

(22)

It should also be taken into account that in order to define one particle states and
the form factors of the stress-energy tensor, the mass of the particle must be larger
than
eT
. Then, the high temperature limit can only be taken in the weak coupling limit.
The presence of an anomalous gravitomagnetic moment is to be ascribed to the violation
of Einstein equivalence principle. At finite temperature the vacuum is not Lorentz
invariant. On the contrary, we can always distinguish a preferred reference frame,
which is the one where the thermal bath is at rest. This means that we can distinguish
between acceleration and the genuine effects of gravity. Similar thermal effects
in QED affect modify the values of inertial and gravitational mass of a Dirac fermion
providing an explicit breaking of the weak equivalence
principle
Donoghue et al. 1984
;
Donoghue et al. 1985
;
Donoghue and Holstein 1983
;
Mitra et al. 2001
;
Nieves and Pal 1998
, see
App.
A
for details.

IV
Gravitomagnetic moment in Linearized gravity

In this section we identify the gravitomagnetic moment of a fermion using the scattering theory in linearized gravity.
The fermion interacts with an external gravitational field
g_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}+h_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}
through
the linearized Hamiltonian

\widehat{H}_{\textup{int}}=\frac{1}{2}\int{\rm d}^{3}x\,\widehat{T}_{\mu\nu}h_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}.

The corresponding scattering amplitude is

\mathcal{A}=-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p\cdot u-p^{\prime}\cdot u)\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_{2}(p)Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}\frac{1}{2}\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})M_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})u_{\beta}(p)h_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}(p^{\prime}-p),

where
Z_{2}
is the wave-function renormalization constant.
To study the effect of rotation, we then just have to use the proper metric accounting for rotation. Using the linear approximation of
gravito-electromagnetism
Mashhoon 2000
, the metric can be written in terms of a gravitational gauge potential

A_{g}^{\mu}=(2\phi_{g},\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{A}$}}_{g})
. Let
h_{\mu\nu}
be the perturbation of metric
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+h_{\mu\nu}
;
we then define
\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}h^{\alpha}_{\alpha}
.
This definition is related to the gravitation gauge potential by
\bar{h}^{00}=4\phi_{g}
and
\bar{h}^{0i}=2A^{i}_{g}
.
For the case of rotation around an axis, we have
h^{\alpha}_{\alpha}=0
and therefore
h_{\mu\nu}=\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}

and
\phi_{g}=0
. The only non-vanishing components of metric perturbation are
\bar{h}^{0\mu}=2(2\phi_{g},\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{A}$}}_{g})
.
We can also define a gravitomagnetic field via
B_{g}^{i}=\epsilon^{ijk}\nabla^{j}A_{g}^{k}
, or in terms
of Fourier transform
B_{g}^{i}=-\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{ijk}q^{j}A_{g}^{k}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})
.
Gravito-electromagnetism is particularly well suited for describing a pure rotation.
For instance, consider a constant rotation around the
z
axis. In rotating cartesian coordinates, the deviation from
flat space-time is

h_{\mu\nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-(x^{2}+y^{2})\Omega^{2}&y\Omega&-x\Omega&0\\
y\Omega&0&0&0\\
-x\Omega&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0\end{array}\right)\simeq\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&y\Omega&-x\Omega&0\\
y\Omega&0&0&0\\
-x\Omega&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0\end{array}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{2}).

(23)

For the metric in Eq. (
23
), we simply have
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{B}$}}_{g}=\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}
.
For our system at thermal equilibrium, the rotation is taken in reference to the thermal bath velocity
u

and the metric deviation is therefore given by
h^{\mu\nu}=2u^{\mu}A^{\nu}+2u^{\nu}A^{\mu}
.
With this metric, the scattering amplitude is

\mathcal{A}=-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p\cdot u-p\cdot u)\frac{2}{\sqrt{Z_{2}(p)Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})M_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})u_{\beta}(p)u^{\mu}A_{g}^{\nu}(p^{\prime}-p).

(24)

To identify the gravitomagnetic moment
g_{\Omega}
of the fermion, we then compare the previous amplitude to the one obtained
from the potential containing the spin-rotation coupling:

V=-\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\mu}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\mu}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\mu}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\mu}$}}_{g}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{B}$}}_{g}=-g_{\Omega}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{S}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{S}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{S}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{S}$}}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}.

This potential, in the non-relativistic limit, leads to the amplitude

\mathcal{A}=-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})\left[-g_{\Omega}\,\xi^{\prime\dagger}\frac{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}}{2}\xi\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}\right],

(25)

where
\xi
is the two component spinor, normalized such that
\xi^{\dagger}\xi=1

and
\textstyle\mathbf{\sigma}
are the Pauli matrices.
By matching the explicit form of the amplitude in Eq. (
24
) to the one in Eq. (
25
)
coming from the spin-rotation coupling, we can read off the gravitomagnetic moment obtained in
finite temperature field theory.

IV.1
Gravitomagnetic moment at leading order

We now obtain the leading order gravitomagnetic moment by computing
the amplitude (
24
).
First, we go to the rest frame
u=(1,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{0}$}})
where the scattering amplitude becomes

\mathcal{A}=-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})\frac{2}{\sqrt{Z_{2}(p)Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})M_{0i}(p,p^{\prime})u(p)A_{g}^{i}(p^{\prime}-p).

At leading order,
u(p)
is the usual Dirac spinor and the matrix elements of stress
energy tensor are

M^{0}_{0i}(p,p^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4}\left[\gamma_{0}(p^{\prime}+p)_{i}+\gamma_{i}(p^{\prime}+p)_{0}\right],\quad Z_{2}(p)=Z_{2}(p^{\prime})=1.

In the limit
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}\to 0
and
P_{s}\to 0
we take advantage of the spinor identities

\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\gamma_{0}u(p)=\xi^{\prime\dagger}\xi,\quad\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\gamma^{i}u(p)=\xi^{\prime\dagger}\left[\frac{(p^{\prime}+p)^{i}}{2m}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{ijk}(p^{\prime}-p)^{j}\sigma^{k}}{2m}\right]\xi

and find

\begin{split}\lim_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}\to 0}\mathcal{A}=&-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})(-1)\frac{1}{2}\xi^{\prime\dagger}\left[(p^{\prime}+p)^{i}+\frac{(p^{\prime}+p)_{0}}{2m}(p^{\prime}+p)^{i}-\frac{(p^{\prime}+p)_{0}}{2m}\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{ijk}(p^{\prime}-p)^{j}\sigma^{k}\right]\xi A_{g}^{i}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}).\end{split}

This expression can be simplified using the following approximation valid in the non relativistic limit

\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})(p_{0}+p^{\prime}_{0})=\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})2p_{0}=\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})2\sqrt{m^{2}+(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}^{2}+\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}^{2})/4}=\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})2m+\mathcal{O}\left(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}^{2},\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}^{2}/m^{2}\right).

The amplitude is then

\begin{split}\lim_{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}\to 0}\mathcal{A}=&-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})(-1)\xi^{\prime\dagger}\left[(p^{\prime}+p)^{i}A_{g}^{i}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\epsilon^{ijk}q^{j}\sigma^{k}A_{g}^{i}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})\right]\xi\\
=&-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})(-1)\xi^{\prime\dagger}\left[(p^{\prime}+p)^{i}A_{g}^{i}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})+\frac{1}{2}\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{B}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{B}$}}_{g}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})\right]\xi\\
=&-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})\left[-2(p^{\prime}+p)^{i}A_{g}^{i}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})\xi^{\prime\dagger}\xi-\xi^{\prime\dagger}\frac{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}}{2}\xi\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\Omega}$}}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})\right].\end{split}

Comparing with the amplitude in Eq. (
25
), the gravitomagnetic moment of a fermion is

g_{\Omega}=1,

which, as expected, is the value predicted by the Dirac equation.

IV.2
Anomalous gravitomagnetic moment at one loop

Here we consider the radiative corrections to gravitomagnetic moment.
First, we deal with the zero temperature part.
At zero temperature, the one-loop renormalized stress-energy tensor is
Milton 1977

M_{\mu\nu}=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)I_{P\gamma}^{0}(Q)-P_{\mu}P_{\nu}I_{PP}^{0}(Q)-\left(q_{\mu}q_{\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}Q^{2}\right)I_{qq}^{0}(Q)\right]u(p)

where in the limit of
Q\equiv\sqrt{-q^{2}}\to 0
the form factors are

I_{P\gamma}^{0}(Q)=\frac{1}{4}\left(1-\frac{\alpha\pi}{8}\frac{Q}{m}\right);\quad I_{PP}^{0}(Q)=\frac{\alpha\pi}{64m}\frac{Q}{M};\quad I_{qq}^{0}(Q)=-\frac{\alpha\pi}{16m}\frac{m}{Q}.

Since the spin coupling can only come from a gamma matrix, only the
I_{P\gamma}^{0}(Q)
formfactor can contribute
to gravitomagnetic moment. However in the limit of vanishing
\textstyle\mathbf{q}
this term does not affect the gravitomagnetic moment;
therefore there is no AGM for QED at zero temperature. This is what we expect from the Einstein equivalence principle,
which is still valid in interacting quantum field theory at zero temperature.

We now move to temperature modifications. The finite temperature renormalization we adopt is described in App.
C
.
First, we address the corrections which might come from thermal spinors, see App.
C.1
.
We should repeat the previous calculation of the leading order gravitomagnetic moment, except that we
must now employ thermal on-shell condition and thermal Dirac spinors:

\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})M^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}u_{\beta}(p)=\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})\frac{1}{4}\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)u_{\beta}(p).

In the rest frame where
u=(1,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{0}$}})
, the thermal spinors describe the spin interaction, as can be seen by using the identity

\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}u_{\beta}(p)=\xi^{\prime\dagger}\left[\frac{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}_{T}}{2m_{p}}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}\wedge\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}_{T}}{2m_{p}}\right]\xi+\mathcal{O}(e^{4}),

where
P_{T},q_{T}
and
m_{p}
contain temperature modifications according to the notation used in Sec.
C.1
.
Here the spin-rotation coupling is divided by the thermal mass, but this thermal mass is canceled out by the term

p^{\prime}_{0T}+p_{0T}\simeq 2m_{p}
; the gravitomagnetic moment is therefore unaffected.

Now we include the radiative corrections coming from the stress-energy tensor matrix elements
evaluated from the diagrams considered in Sec.
C
.
First, we select the terms of the stress-energy tensor matrix element
M_{\mu\nu}(p^{\prime},p)

that actually contributes to the anomalous gravitomagnetic moment (AGM).
In App.
B
we show that only the following terms can bring contribution to AGM:

\begin{split}M^{\mu\nu}_{\textup{Relevant}}(p,p^{\prime})=&I_{P\gamma}(P,q)\left(\gamma^{\mu}P^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}P^{\mu}\right)+I_{u\gamma}(P,q)\left(\gamma^{\mu}u^{\nu}+\gamma^{\nu}u^{\mu}\right)+\\
&+I_{Pl}(P,q)\not{\hat{l}}\left(\hat{l}^{\mu}P^{\nu}+\hat{l}^{\nu}P^{\mu}\right)+I_{ul}(P,q)\not{\hat{l}}\left(\hat{l}^{\mu}u^{\nu}+\hat{l}^{\nu}u^{\mu}\right),\end{split}

which are the same one that contribute to the axial vortical effect.

By comparison with the contribution to AGM from
P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}
evaluated in Sec.
IV.1
,
the formfactor

I_{P\gamma}(P_{s},q^{2})\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)

leads to the gravitomagnetic moment

g_{\Omega}=+\lim_{P_{s}\to 0}\lim_{q\to 0}4I_{P\gamma}(P_{s},q^{2}).

Similarly, it is straightforward to show that the other formfactors contribute to AGM as

\begin{split}I_{u\gamma}(P_{s},q^{2})&\left(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)\to g_{\Omega}=+\lim_{P_{s}\to 0}\lim_{q\to 0}4\frac{I_{u\gamma}(P_{s},q^{2})}{\omega_{P}};\\
I_{ul}(P_{s},q^{2})&\not{\hat{l}}\left(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right)\to g_{\Omega}=-\lim_{P_{s}\to 0}\lim_{q\to 0}4\frac{I_{ul}(P_{s},q^{2})}{\omega_{P}};\\
I_{Pl}(P_{s},q^{2})&\not{\hat{l}}\left(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right)\to g_{\Omega}=-\lim_{P_{s}\to 0}\lim_{q\to 0}4I_{Pl}(P_{s},q^{2}).\end{split}

Summing all contributions, the anomalous gravitomagnetic moment is

g_{\Omega}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\to 0\\
P_{s}\to 0\end{subarray}}4\left(I_{P\gamma}(P,q)+\frac{I_{u\gamma}(P,q)}{\omega_{P}}-I_{Pl}(P,q)-\frac{I_{ul}(P,q)}{\omega_{P}}\right).

Those form factors are computed in App.
C
and lead to the result
quoted in Sec.
III
:

g_{\Omega}-1=-\frac{6-\theta_{\textup{HT}}}{36}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{m^{2}}=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{6}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{m^{2}}&T\ll m\\
-\frac{5}{36}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{m^{2}}&T\gg m\end{cases}.

V
Summary and Discussion

In summary, we showed that in a system at thermal equilibrium the interactions with
photons change the gravitomagnetic moment of a massive fermion, i.e. the coupling
between the spin and the rotation of the medium. Using the scattering theory, in analogy to
the magnetic moment, we obtained the gravitomagnetic moment from the form factors
of the stress-energy tensor, see Eq. (
15
). We then renormalized the
stress-energy tensor at one-loop level in the finite temperature QED. The resulting
gravitomagnetic moment, given by (
22
), receives
radiative corrections only in the presence of thermal effects. We argued that this is
possible because the thermal bath destroys the Lorentz invariance of stress-energy tensor
and consequently violates the Einstein equivalence principle. To the best of our knowledge, the
possibility of an anomalous gravitomagnetic moment (AGM) in these settings and the
calculation of it are new results.

The effect of spin-rotation coupling has been already observed in the non-vanishing global
polarization of particles emitted by the rotating quark-gluon plasma
Adamczyk et al. 2017

and has been investigated in several studies
Gao et al. 2008
;
Sorin and Teryaev 2017
;
Karpenko and Becattini 2017
;

Therefore, in principle, polarization measurements in heavy

extend the result (

, we expect that it is

with

g_{\Omega}^{\textup{QCD}}-1=-\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2}\frac{6-\theta_{\textup{HT}}}{36}\frac{g^{2}T^{2}}{m^{2}}=\begin{cases}-\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2}\frac{1}{6}\frac{g^{2}T^{2}}{m^{2}}&T\ll m\\
-\frac{N_{c}^{2}-1}{2}\frac{5}{36}\frac{g^{2}T^{2}}{m^{2}}&T\gg m\end{cases}.

(26)

In a simple recombination picture based on the quark model, the
\Lambda

gT\simeq 270-450

. Because it depends on the temperature, the AGM contribution may be detected in the data on
\Lambda

Note that the effect of the AGM on polarization has the same sign for fermions and antifermions, so we expect it to contribute equally to the polarization of both
\Lambda
and
{\bar{\Lambda}}
hyperons.

We also established the connection
between the AGM and the axial vortical effect (AVE). In the limit of the large
m/T
ratio,
we can separate the short distance interactions that renormalize the
angular momentum of the system, and the long distance thermal contributions which result in the
AVE. In this way we obtained the formula in Eq. (
14
) which relates the
radiative correction to the AVE for a massive fermion to the momentum-dependent AGM.

While we showed here that the thermal interpretation of the AVE together with
spin-rotation coupling is able to describe the radiative corrections to the AVE, this does
not exclude a connection to the mixed gauge-gravitational anomaly
Landsteiner et al. 2011b
.
The presence of radiative corrections itself does not conflict with an interpretation based on the anomaly
because the AVE is obtained from the matrix elements of the axial current and the
non-renormalization theorems apply to the operators, and not to the matrix elements, as has been established for the case of chiral anomaly in massless QED
Anselm and Johansen 1989
. However the anomalous
origin of the effect is not yet completely clear; this is particularly true for
massive fermions
Buzzegoli 2020
.

Radiative corrections to the AVE were presented previously in
Golkar and Son 2015
;
Hou et al. 2012

for massless fermions and can be linked to the gravitational anomaly of
photons
Prokhorov et al. 2020
;
Dolgov et al. 1987
. Unfortunately we cannot compare these corrections with
those presented above, since our derivation requires massive fermions, and a
massless limit cannot be performed. Even our definition of the gravitomagnetic moment can not
be applied to a massless particle, as it requires going to the rest frame of the
particle. Therefore, the connection between the AVE and the AGM for a massless Dirac field still has to be clarified. Nevertheless, we believe that the link between the anomalous gravitomagnetic moment and the axial vortical effect for massive fermions established above will help to understand the origin of chiral currents induced by rotation.

Appendix A
Equivalence principle at finite temperature

It has already been demonstrated
Donoghue et al. 1984
;
Donoghue et al. 1985
;
Donoghue and Holstein 1983
;
Mitra et al. 2001
;
Nieves and Pal 1998

that radiative corrections at finite temperature lead to a difference between the inertial and
gravitational masses, thus providing an explicit breaking of weak equivalence principle.
Indeed, one can define three distinct kinds of mass for a particle. The phase-space mass is
the position of the pole in the propagator of the field. The inertial mass is the
response to acceleration caused by an external force, such as an external electric field.
And lastly, the gravitational mass is a measure of how the fermion responds to the gravitational force.
At finite temperature it has been found that the inertial mass and the phase-space masses coincide but the
gravitational mass is different
Donoghue et al. 1984
.

A.1
Phase space mass

Consider a massive Dirac fermion in a QED-like theory at finite temperature.
As in
Donoghue et al. 1984
, we refer to phase-space mass as the location of the
pole in the propagator of the fermion field. The full fermion propagator is given by:

S(p)=\frac{1}{\not{p}-m-\Sigma(p)}.

The self-energy can be written in covariant form as
Weldon 1982b
;
Mitra et al. 2001

\Sigma(p)=a\not{p}+b\not{u}+c,

where
a,b,c
are Lorentz invariant functions. These functions can depend on
m,\,T
and on the
following Lorentz scalars:

\omega\equiv p^{\alpha}u_{\alpha},\quad p_{s}\equiv\sqrt{(p^{\alpha}u_{\alpha})^{2}-p^{2}};

since
p^{2}=\omega^{2}-p_{s}^{2}
, one may interpret
\omega
and
p_{s}
as Lorentz invariant energy
and three momentum. It is useful to define a tensor and a vector orthogonal to
u_{\mu}
by

\tilde{\eta}_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu},\quad\tilde{p}_{\mu}=\tilde{\eta}_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}=p_{\mu}-\omega u_{\mu}.

The vector
\tilde{p}
is automatically space-like:

\tilde{p}^{\mu}\tilde{p}_{\mu}=-p_{s}^{2}<0.

Inverting the matrices, the full propagator becomes:

S(p)=\frac{(1-a)\not{p}-b\not{u}+m+c}{[(1-a)p-bu]^{2}-(m+c)^{2}}.

Therefore, the location of the pole is determined by the vanishing of the denominator:

(1-a)^{2}(\omega^{2}-p_{s}^{2})+b^{2}-2(1-a)b\omega=(m+c)^{2}.

The positive solution for the pole is

\omega=\frac{b}{1-a}+\sqrt{p_{s}^{2}+\frac{(m+c)^{2}}{(1-a)^{2}}}.

Since we are interested in corrections of order
e^{2}
in the coupling constant
e
, it suffices to linearize
in the
a,b,c
functions, which are already
e^{2}
order. The phase-space mass is then

m_{p}^{2}\equiv\omega^{2}-p_{s}^{2}=m^{2}+2\left[a(\omega^{2}-p_{s}^{2})+b\omega+mc\right].

(27)

At a given a momentum
p
with scalars
\omega
and
p_{s}
, the pole of the propagator is situated at

\omega^{2}=E_{\beta}^{2}\equiv p_{s}^{2}+m_{p}^{2}.

The functions
a,b,c
are obtained with the traces over spinor indices

P_{T}={\rm tr}\left[\not{p}\Sigma^{\beta}(p)\right],\quad U_{T}={\rm tr}\left[\not{u}\Sigma^{\beta}(p)\right],\quad T={\rm tr}\left[\Sigma^{\beta}(p)\right],

which give

a=\frac{(p\cdot u)U_{T}-P_{T}}{4[(p\cdot u)^{2}-p^{2}]},\quad b=\frac{(p\cdot u)P_{T}-p^{2}U_{T}}{4[(p\cdot u)^{2}-p^{2}]},\quad c=\frac{T}{4}.

(28)

Replacing the Eq.s (
28
) into Eq. (
27
) we find

m_{p}^{2}=m^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(P_{T}+mT\right).

In real-time formalism the self energy is given by the one-loop diagram

\Sigma(p)=\mathrm{i}e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}D_{\mu\nu}(k)\gamma^{\mu}S_{F}(p+k)\gamma^{\nu}=\Sigma(T=0)+\Sigma^{\beta},

where
S_{F}
and
D_{\mu\nu}
are the fermion and the photon propagator in momentum space
(see Sec.
C
) and we split the zero and the finite temperature part of self-energy.
With standard techniques we can evaluate the self-energy in covariant form and the explicit
form of the functions
a,b
and
c
.
We find that the phase-space mass can be approximated by
Weldon 1982b
;
Donoghue et al. 1984
;
Mitra et al. 2001

m^{2}_{p}-m^{2}=\begin{cases}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{6}&T\ll m,\\
\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{8}&T\gg m\end{cases},

where the different behavior at high temperature arises because the fermion thermal distribution becomes
comparable to the contribution from the photon thermal distribution only when the temperature is much larger
than the mass of the particle.

A.2
Inertial mass

We refer, as usual, to inertial mass of the particle
m_{I}
as the proportionality term between a force and the
acceleration caused by it. To test the inertial mass of a charged Dirac particle, the most natural force
to consider is a constant electric field
\textstyle\mathbf{E}
. In this way, it is easy to include it in the Dirac equation as a
minimal coupling with an external gauge field
A^{\mu}=(\phi,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{0}$}})
, where
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{E}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{E}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{E}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{E}$}}=-\nabla\phi
.
The corrections to this coupling given by temperature and interactions are the corrections to the
vertex
\Gamma^{\mu}
. It is found that, when the vertex is contracted between thermal spinors
u_{\beta}(p)

(see Sec.
C.1
), the modifications exactly compensate each other
Yee 1984

e\bar{u}_{\beta}(p)\Gamma^{\mu}u_{\beta}(p)=e\frac{p_{\mu}}{E_{\beta}},

or in other words, the charge is not renormalized by finite temperature effects.
This suggests that the inertial mass is to be identified with the phase-space mass.

To properly evaluate the inertial mass, we just need to consider the modified Dirac equation

\left(\not{p}_{T}-m\right)\psi=e\Gamma^{\mu}A_{\mu}\psi.

In the non-relativistic limit, one can transform the previous equation into a Schrodinger equation
via a Foldy–Wouthuysen transformation. In that form, one can easily identify the Hamiltonian
H

of the system, then the acceleration of the particle is identified via
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{a}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{a}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{a}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{a}$}}=-[H,[H,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{r}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{r}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{r}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{r}$}}]]

and hence one can infer the value of inertial mass. It is indeed found
Donoghue et al. 1984
that
inertial mass
m_{I}
and phase-space mass
m_{p}
coincide.

A.3
Gravitational mass

Using scattering theory in linearized gravity, we can identify the gravitational mass
of a fermion. We indicate the matrix element of the stress-energy tensor with

\langle p^{\prime},s^{\prime}|T^{\mu\nu}(0)|p,s\rangle\equiv\bar{u}(p^{\prime})M_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})u(p),

where
p
and
p^{\prime}
are the external momenta and
s,\,s^{\prime}
the spin of the fermion.
Consider an external gravitational field
g_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}+h_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}
;
the interaction Hamiltonian in linearized gravity is therefore given by

\widehat{H}_{\textup{int}}=\int{\rm d}^{3}x\frac{1}{2}\widehat{T}_{\mu\nu}h_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}
.
In the leading order of perturbation theory, the S-matrix element for scattering is:

\mathrm{i}\mathcal{A}(2\pi)\delta(p\cdot u-p^{\prime}\cdot u)=-\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})M^{0}_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})u(p)h_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}(p^{\prime}-p),

where
h_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}(p^{\prime}-p)
is the Fourier transform of

h_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}(x)
and
M^{0}_{\mu\nu}
is the tree-level vertex function,
which is given by

M^{0}_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4}\left[\gamma_{\mu}(p^{\prime}+p)_{\nu}+\gamma_{\nu}(p^{\prime}+p)_{\mu}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\left[(\not{p}-m)+(\not{p}^{\prime}-m)\right].

The radiative corrections modify this expression to

\mathcal{A}=-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p\cdot u-p^{\prime}\cdot u)\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_{2}(p)Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}\frac{1}{2}\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})M_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})u_{\beta}(p)h_{\textup{Ext}}^{\mu\nu}(p^{\prime}-p),

where we divided by
\sqrt{Z_{2}}
- the wave-function renormalization constant - for each fermionic leg,
and
u_{\beta}(p,s)
is the Dirac thermal spinor which satisfies the Dirac equation including radiative and
thermal corrections and all perturbative diagrams are summed in
M
.

To identify the gravitational mass of the fermion
m_{g}
, following
Mitra et al. 2001
, we consider the scattering
of a fermion from a static gravitational potential produced by a static mass density
\rho_{\textup{Ext}}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{x}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{x}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{x}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{x}$}})
.
The resulting metric is the linearized solution of Einstein field equations with a matter
stress-energy tensor given by
T^{\mu\nu}=\rho_{\textup{Ext}}u^{\mu}u^{\mu}
. Taking advantage
of the Poisson equation
-2\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}^{2}\phi_{\textup{Ext}}=\rho_{\textup{Ext}}
the Fourier
transform of Einstein equation solution reads:

h^{\mu\nu}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})=2\phi_{\textup{Ext}}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})(2u^{\mu}u^{\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}).

Therefore, inserting this in the scattering amplitude, we find

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}=&-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p_{0}-p_{0}^{\prime})\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_{2}(p)Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})M_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})u_{\beta}(p)(2u^{\mu}u^{\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu})\delta(p_{0}^{\prime}-p_{0})\phi_{\textup{Ext}}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})\\
=&-\mathrm{i}(2\pi)\delta(p_{0}^{\prime}-p_{0})\mathcal{M}(p^{\prime},p)\phi_{\textup{Ext}}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}),\end{split}

where we have defined

\mathcal{M}(p^{\prime},p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_{2}(p)Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})M_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})u_{\beta}(p)(2u^{\mu}u^{\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}).

If the gravitational field is very slowly varying over a large (macroscopic) region,

\phi_{\textup{Ext}}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}})
will be concentrated around
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}=0
; then we can take the
limit
\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}\to 0
. In this way, by comparison with the scattering amplitude of a potential

V(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{x}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{x}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{x}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{x}$}})=m_{g}\phi(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{x}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{x}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{x}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{x}$}})
in the Born approximation, which is

\mathcal{A}=-\mathrm{i}m_{g}\phi(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}),

we can identify the gravitational mass of the fermion. From the previous expression, we see that
the gravitational mass is obtained when the spatial momenta of the fermion are vanishing:

m_{g}=\lim_{p_{s}\to 0}\left[\frac{(2u^{\lambda}u^{\rho}-\eta^{\lambda\rho})}{\sqrt{Z_{2}(p)Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}\left(\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime},s)M_{\lambda\rho}(p,p^{\prime})u_{\beta}(p,s)\right)_{\textup{On-shell}}\right]\Big|_{p^{\prime}\to p},

where
E_{\beta}=E_{\beta}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{p}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{p}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{p}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{p}$}})
is the on-shell energy of the particle, i.e. the position of the pole
of the self-energy, and
p_{s}=\sqrt{(p\cdot u)^{2}-p^{2}}
.

At leading order, as we are now showing, gravitational mass coincides with inertial and phase space mass.
At leading order
Z_{2}=1+\mathcal{O}(e^{2})
, the thermal Dirac spinor reduces to the usual free Dirac spinor
and the matrix element is simply the tree-level diagram

M^{0}_{\lambda\rho}(p,p^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4}\left[\gamma_{\lambda}(p+p^{\prime})_{\rho}+\gamma_{\rho}(p+p^{\prime})_{\lambda}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\lambda\rho}\left(\not{p}-m+\not{p}^{\prime}-m\right).

Proceeding to evaluate the gravitational mass step by step, we first find

\left(\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s)M^{0}_{\lambda\rho}(p,p^{\prime})u(p,s)\right)_{p^{\prime}=p}=\bar{u}(p,s)\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left[\gamma_{\lambda}p_{\rho}+\gamma_{\rho}p_{\lambda}\right]-\eta_{\lambda\rho}\left(\not{p}-m\right)\right\}u(p,s).

Then, since
p
is taken on-shell, we take advantage of spinor properties (see Sec.
C.1
for
the conventions used), which in the limit of
p_{s}
going to zero give

\bar{u}(p,s)u(p,s)|_{p_{s}=0}=1+\mathcal{O}(e^{2}),\quad\bar{u}(p,s)\gamma_{\mu}u(p,s)|_{p_{s}=0}=\frac{p_{\mu}}{m}+\mathcal{O}(e^{2}),

and we obtain

\begin{split}\lim_{p_{s}\to 0}\left(\bar{u}(p^{\prime},s)M_{\lambda\rho}(p,p^{\prime})u(p,s)\right)_{p^{\prime}=p}=&\lim_{p_{s}\to 0}\frac{1}{m}\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left(p_{\lambda}p_{\rho}+p_{\rho}p_{\lambda}\right)-\eta_{\lambda\rho}\left(p^{2}-m^{2}\right)\right\}\\
&=\lim_{p_{s}\to 0}\left\{\frac{1}{2m}\left(p_{\lambda}p_{\rho}+p_{\rho}p_{\lambda}\right)\right\}.\end{split}

At last, we find

m_{g}=\lim_{p_{s}\to 0}\left[\frac{1}{2m}(2u^{\lambda}u^{\rho}-\eta^{\lambda\rho})\left(p_{\lambda}p_{\rho}+p_{\rho}p_{\lambda}\right)\right]=\lim_{p_{s}\to 0}\frac{2(u\cdot p)^{2}-p^{2}}{m}=m,

where we used the on-shell condition
p\cdot u=E_{\beta}(p_{s}=0)=m+\mathcal{O}(e^{2})
.
At leading order, gravitational and inertial mass are indeed equivalent
m_{g}=m_{I}=m_{p}=m
.
Instead, for 1-loop QED it has been proved
Donoghue et al. 1984
;
Mitra et al. 2001
that gravitational mass is different from inertial
mass, in particular for small temperature
T\ll m
their ratio is

\frac{m_{I}}{m_{g}}=1+\frac{e^{2}}{3}\frac{T^{2}}{m^{2}}.

This is a manifest breaking of the weak equivalence principle caused by finite temperature effects.

\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\not{\hat{l}}u(p)(w_{\mu}v_{\nu}+w_{\nu}v_{\mu})u^{\mu}A_{g}^{\nu}=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{l}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{l}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{l}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{l}$}}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}})u(p)w_{0}(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{v}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{v}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{v}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{v}$}}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{A}$}}_{g}).

Since the gravitomagnetic moment is the coupling of spin and rotation, i.e.

\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{A}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{A}$}}_{g}

By definition of

(\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{v}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{v}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{v}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{v}$}}\cdot\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{l}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{l}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{l}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{l}$}})

Then, only the terms with

In order to evaluate the radiative corrections to gravitomagnetic moment at finite temperature
we have to consider the renormalization of the stress-energy tensor at finite temperature and
at first order on momentum transfer
q
. The zero temperature renormalization is performed with
usual techniques and we do not discuss it here. To address thermal corrections to the
matrix-element of stress-energy tensor we use the real-time formalism of thermal field theory.
The major modification of QFT at finite temperature is the value of the vacuum of the theory,
which is not empty but contain a number of bosons and fermions given
respectively by the Bose-Einstein and the Fermi-Dirac distribution functions:

\widehat{a}_{\textup{B}}^{\dagger}\widehat{a}_{\textup{B}}(p)|0\rangle=n_{\textup{B}}(E)|0\rangle,\quad\widehat{a}_{\textup{F}}^{\dagger}\widehat{a}_{\textup{F}}(p)|0\rangle=n_{\textup{F}}(E)|0\rangle,

where as usual we indicate

n_{\textup{B}}(x)=\frac{1}{\exp(\beta x)-1},\quad n_{\textup{F}}(x)=\frac{1}{\exp(\beta x)+1}.

The resulting propagators of the gauge field and of the fermionic field in real-time formalism are

\begin{split}\mathrm{i}S_{F}(p)&=\left(\not{p}+m\right)\left[\frac{\mathrm{i}}{p^{2}-m^{2}}-2\pi\delta\left(p^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(p)\right],\\
\mathrm{i}D_{\mu\nu}(k)&=-\eta_{\mu\nu}\left[\frac{\mathrm{i}}{k^{2}}+2\pi\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\right].\end{split}

Perturbation theory and Feynman diagrams at finite temperature are unmodified compared to usual quantum field
theory except for the previous propagators. We see that the propagators in real-time formalism are naturally
separated into a temperature part and into a
T=0
part. The
T=0
part has been addressed as usual
and from now on we just consider the thermal part. The diagrams responsible for radiative
corrections to the stress-energy tensor matrix element

\langle p^{\prime},s^{\prime}|T^{\mu\nu}(0)|p,s\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_{2}(p)Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})M_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})u_{\beta}(p),

are reported in Fig.
2
. Furthermore, since we are only interested in the thermal part,
each integrals corresponding to a Feynman diagram is weighted with a thermal function and it is therefore
ultraviolet convergent and finite. All the divergent part are already been taken care of with the
T=0

renormalization.

Note that we can distinguish between two different regimes of temperature. Indeed, finite-temperature
modification may arise both from the boson or the photon propagators. However, for low temperatures
such that
T\ll m
, the fermion distribution function is suppressed by a factor
\exp(-m/T)
, which
is negligible compared to contributions of order
(T/m)^{2}
coming from the photon distribution.
In the opposite regime, when
T\gg m
, the photon contribution is still the same as low temperatures,
while the fermion distribution can now also contribute with terms of order
(T/m)^{2}
. Therefore, as
in the case of the pole of the propagator, we can expect two different values of AGM valid in the
two regimes of low and high temperatures.

As first step, we need to identify the mass shift and the wave-function renormalization constant.
These quantities are obtained starting from the self-energy of the fermion, which is discussed
in Sec.
A.1
. We found that the self-energy can be written as
\Sigma(p)=a\not{p}+b\not{u}+c

and that the full fermion propagator becomes

S(p)=\frac{(1-a)\not{p}-b\not{u}+m+c}{[(1-a)p-bu]^{2}-(m+c)^{2}},

(30)

which has a pole in
\omega^{2}=E_{\beta}^{2}=p_{s}^{2}+m_{p}^{2}
, with
m_{p}
given by Eq. (
27
).

C.1
Thermal Dirac spinor

The Dirac equation in momentum space is modified according to the self-energy, which also include thermal modifications.
The thermal Dirac spinors
u_{\beta}(p)
satisfy the modified Dirac equation corresponding to the
new propagator (
30
):

\left[\not{p}-m_{R}-\Sigma^{\beta}(p)\right]u_{\beta}(p)=\left[(1-a)\not{p}-b\not{u}-m_{R}-c\right]u_{\beta}(p)=0,

where
m_{R}
indicates the zero temperature renormalized mass. The thermal spinor
u_{\beta}(p)
satisfies
the previous equation when
p
is the pole of the propagator, i.e. such that
p\cdot u=E_{\beta}(p_{s})
.
The thermal Dirac spinors are actually required to properly account for stress-energy tensor
renormalization at finite temperature
Donoghue et al. 1984
;
Weldon 1982b
.

In thermal bath rest frame, we choose the normalization

u^{\dagger}_{\beta}(p)u_{\beta}(p)=1.

For convenience, we furthermore define a four vector and a scalar

p_{T}\equiv(1-a)p-bu,\quad m_{T}\equiv m_{R}+c,

so that the modified thermal Dirac equation is written as

\left[\not{p}_{T}-m_{T}\right]u_{\beta}(p)=0.

Therefore, it follows from the modified thermal Dirac equation that the thermal spinors satisfy the following identities:

\bar{u}_{\beta}(p,s)u_{\beta}(p,s)=\frac{m_{T}}{(1-a)E_{\beta}-b},\quad\bar{u}_{\beta}(p,s)\gamma_{\mu}u_{\beta}(p,s)=\frac{{p_{T}}_{\mu}}{(1-a)E_{\beta}-b},\quad\sum_{s}u_{\beta}(p,s)\bar{u}_{\beta}(p,s)=\frac{\not{p}_{T}+m_{T}}{2(1-a)E_{\beta}-2b}.

In the non relativistic limit and in the frame where
u=(1,\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{0}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{0}$}})
, we can also show the
validity of the following identities:

\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})\gamma_{0}u_{\beta}(p)=\xi^{\prime\dagger}\xi+\mathcal{O}(e^{2}),\quad\bar{u}_{\beta}(p^{\prime})\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\gamma}$}}u_{\beta}(p)=\xi^{\prime\dagger}\left[\frac{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{P}$}}}{2m_{p}}+\mathrm{i}\frac{\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{\sigma}$}}\wedge\mathchoice{\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\textstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\scriptscriptstyle\mathbf{q}$}}}{2m_{p}}\right]\xi+\mathcal{O}(e^{4}),

where
\xi
is the two component spinor, normalized such that
\xi^{\dagger}\xi=1
.
We used the identities above to compute the gravitational mass in Sec.
A.3

and the gravitomagnetic moment in Sec.
IV
.

C.2
Wave-function renormalization constant
Z_{2}

The wave-function renormalization constant
Z_{2}
is obtained by requesting that
the fermion field is properly renormalized

\psi_{\textup{R}}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{Z_{2}}}\psi_{0}(x).

The temperature part of the wave-function renormalization constant is
Donoghue and Holstein 1983
;
Mitra et al. 2001

{}^{\beta}Z_{2}(\omega,p_{s})=\frac{2\left[(1-a)E_{\beta}-b\right]}{\frac{\partial D}{\partial\omega}\Big|_{\omega=E_{\beta}}},

where
E_{\beta}
is the pole of the propagator and
D
is the denominator of the propagator (
30
)

D(\omega,p_{s})=(1-a)^{2}(\omega^{2}-p_{s}^{2})+b^{2}-2(1-a)b\omega-(m+c)^{2}.

At order
e^{2}
we find

^{\beta}Z_{2}=1+\left(a+\frac{m^{2}}{\omega}\frac{\partial a}{\partial\omega}+\frac{\partial b}{\partial\omega}+\frac{m}{\omega}\frac{\partial c}{\partial\omega}\right)\Big|_{\omega=\sqrt{p_{s}^{2}+m^{2}}}.

(31)

For the computation of AGM we are interested to the quantity
(\sqrt{Z_{2}(p)Z_{2}(p^{\prime})})^{-1/2}
in the limits of
q\to 0

and of
P_{s}=\sqrt{(P\cdot u)^{2}-P^{2}}\to 0
. Therefore, we just have to evaluate
Z_{2}(p)
with
p
an on-shell momentum and then perform the
p_{s}\to 0
limit.
Using the prescription in Eq. (
31
) and the behavior of the functions
a,b,c
we find:

\lim_{p_{s}\to 0}\,^{\beta}Z_{2}(p)=1-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{12m^{2}}+\frac{e^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}k\,\frac{n_{\textup{B}}(k)}{k}-\theta_{\textup{HT}}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{24m^{2}},

where the
\theta_{\textup{HT}}
is defined such that it turns on the high temperatures contribution:

\theta_{\textup{HT}}\equiv\begin{cases}0&T\ll m\\
1&T\gg m\end{cases}.

The low-temperature part is in agreement with
Donoghue and Holstein 1983
;
Donoghue et al. 1984
;
Mitra et al. 2001
.
Therefore we have

\lim_{p_{s}\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\,{}^{\beta}Z_{2}(p)\,^{\beta}Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}=1+\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{12m^{2}}-\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{3}}I_{\textup{A}}+\theta_{\textup{HT}}\,\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{24m^{2}},

where we denoted

I_{\textup{A}}\equiv 8\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}k\,\frac{n_{\textup{B}}(k)}{k}.

For future convenience, noticing that for
p_{s}=0
we have
\omega_{P}=2m
, we can write the factor coming
from wave-function renormalization constants using
\omega_{P}\equiv u\cdot P
instead of the mass
m
:

\lim_{P_{s}\to 0}\lim_{q\to 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\,{}^{\beta}Z_{2}(p)\,^{\beta}Z_{2}(p^{\prime})}}=1+\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{3\omega_{P}^{2}}-\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{3}}I_{\textup{A}}+\theta_{\textup{HT}}\,\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{6\omega_{P}^{2}}.

Renormalization of stress-energy tensor at finite temperature

As last step to renormalize the stress-energy tensor we have to calculate the temperature contribution of the diagrams in Fig.
2
.
Here we write the general procedure and we leave the details in Appendix
D
.

First, we recap the notation used. We indicate with
q
and
P
the momenta

P=p^{\prime}+p,\quad q=p^{\prime}-p

where
p
and
p^{\prime}
are the momenta of external legs of the diagrams in Fig.
2
.
Notice that
q\cdot P=0
. Moreover, scattering theory imposes the conservation of the time component
of
p
and
p^{\prime}
in the thermal bath rest frame, meaning
q\cdot u=0
. We are using this constraint
when evaluating all the diagrams. It is then convenient to define the following scalar and four-vectors:

\omega_{P}=P\cdot u,\quad P_{s}=\sqrt{\omega_{P}^{2}-P^{2}},\quad\tilde{P}_{\mu}=(\eta_{\mu\nu}-u_{\mu}u_{\nu})P^{\nu}=P_{\mu}-\omega_{P}u_{\mu},\quad l^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}u_{\nu}\tilde{P}_{\rho}q_{\sigma},\quad\hat{l}^{\mu}=\frac{l^{\mu}}{\sqrt{-l^{2}}},

we also denote with
a
the ratio

a\equiv\frac{\sqrt{\omega_{P}^{2}-P^{2}}}{\omega_{P}}=\frac{P_{s}}{\omega_{P}},\quad 0<a<1.

Since we are considering 1-loop corrections, a generic diagrams of Fig.
2
, which we label with
X
,
can be written as

M_{\mu\nu}^{X}(p,p^{\prime})=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\frac{f^{X}(p,p^{\prime},k)}{D^{X}(p,p^{\prime},k)}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{X}_{\mu\nu}u(p),

(32)

where all the spinorial structure is contained inside the numerator
N^{X}
.
Therefore
N^{X}
can be simplified using Dirac equation and it can be decomposed in the following terms:

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{X}_{\mu\nu}u(p)=&\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big[N_{kk}^{X}\not{k}\,k_{\mu}k_{\nu}+N_{Pk}^{X}\not{k}\,(P_{\mu}k_{\nu}+P_{\nu}k_{\mu})+\\
&+(N_{P\gamma}^{X(s)}+N_{P\gamma}^{X(k)}\not{k})(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})+(N_{k\gamma}^{X(s)}+N_{k\gamma}^{X(k)}\not{k})(k_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+k_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})+\cdots\Big]u(p)\end{split}

(33)

where each term
N_{yy}^{X}
can depend on the scalars
\{k^{2},\,P^{2},\,q^{2},k\cdot P,\,k\cdot q,\,P\cdot q,\,m\}

and the dots stands for terms that to do not contribute to AGM. Indeed, we show in App.
B
that
the only terms that are relevant for AGM are

\begin{split}u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu},&\quad P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu},\quad\not{\hat{l}}\left(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right),\quad\not{\hat{l}}\left(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right).\end{split}

Using the orthogonal non-normalized basis
\{u,\,\tilde{P},\,q,\,\hat{l}\}

k
can be decomposed into:

\begin{split}k_{\mu}=&(k\cdot u)u_{\mu}+\frac{(k\cdot\tilde{P})}{\tilde{P}^{2}}\tilde{P}_{\mu}+\frac{(k\cdot q)}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}-(k\cdot\hat{l})\hat{l}_{\mu}\\
=&\left[(k\cdot u)-\frac{\omega_{P}(k\cdot\tilde{P})}{\tilde{P}^{2}}\right]u_{\mu}+\frac{(k\cdot\tilde{P})}{\tilde{P}^{2}}P_{\mu}+\frac{(k\cdot q)}{q^{2}}q_{\mu}-(k\cdot\hat{l})\hat{l}_{\mu}\\
\equiv&A_{u}\,u_{\mu}+A_{P}\,P_{\mu}+A_{q}\,q_{\mu}+A_{l}\,\hat{l}_{\mu}.\end{split}

This decomposition is used to write the diagrams in a covariant form.
Consider the term
\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\not{k}u(p)v_{\mu}w_{\mu}
, where
v
and
w
are any vector between
\{u,P,q,l\}
,
the term is decomposed into

\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\not{k}u(p)v_{\mu}w_{\nu}=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[A_{u}\,\not{u}+A_{l}\,\not{\hat{l}}+2mA_{P}\right]u(p)v_{\mu}w_{\nu},

and for what we show in Sec.
IV.2

\not{l}
can contribute to AGM:

\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\not{k}u(p)v_{\mu}w_{\nu}\to A_{l}\,\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\,\not{\hat{l}}\,v_{\mu}w_{\nu}\,u(p)\quad\text{ with }(v,w)=(u,l)\text{ or }(v,w)=(P,l).

With the same argument, we can select only the parts relevant to AGM of all the possible terms:

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\not{k}u(p)\left(k_{\mu}v_{\nu}+k_{\nu}v_{\mu}\right)\to&\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big[A_{u}A_{l}\not{\hat{l}}(u_{\mu}v_{\nu}+u_{\nu}v_{\mu})+A_{P}A_{l}\not{\hat{l}}(P_{\mu}v_{\nu}+P_{\nu}v_{\mu})\Big]u(p)\text{ if }v=l\\

q

k

D.1
Self-energy and Counter-terms

The fermion-self energy diagram, Fig.

\begin{split}M_{\mu\nu}^{CT}=&-\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\,\left(a\not{p^{\prime}}+b\not{u}+\delta m\right)\mathrm{i}S_{F}(p^{\prime})V_{\mu\nu}(p^{\prime},p)u(p).\end{split}

\begin{split}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{2}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}=\frac{8\pi}{2a}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right),\quad\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{2\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}=\frac{8\pi}{3}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right)\end{split}

we obtain

I_{u\gamma}^{P}=I^{P}_{u}=-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{9\omega_{P}}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{2}}-\frac{3(1-a^{2})}{4a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right).

The part proportional to
(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})
at first order in
\epsilon
is the integral

\begin{split}I_{P\gamma}^{P}=&e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{\left(2(k\cdot\tilde{P})A_{P}+(k\cdot q)\right)}{D_{1}}+\frac{\left(2(k\cdot\tilde{P})A_{P}-(k\cdot q)\right)}{D_{2}}\right]\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\\
\simeq&e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{\omega_{P}}\frac{1}{k_{0}-ak_{x}}-\frac{2k_{x}}{a\omega_{P}^{2}(k_{0}-ak_{x})}-\frac{k_{x}}{a\omega_{P}k_{z}^{2}}\right]\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\\
=&\frac{e^{2}}{2\omega_{P}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[\frac{1}{k}\frac{1}{s-a\hat{k}_{x}}-\frac{2\hat{k}_{x}}{a\omega_{P}(s-a\hat{k}_{x})}-\frac{1}{k}\frac{\hat{k}_{x}}{a\hat{k}_{z}^{2}}\right]\\
=&\frac{e^{2}}{2\omega_{P}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[\frac{1}{k}\frac{2a\hat{k}_{x}}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}-\frac{4\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}{\omega_{P}(1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})}-\frac{2}{k}\frac{\hat{k}_{x}}{a\hat{k}_{z}^{2}}\right]\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{2\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}{(1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})},\end{split}

where we first integrated
k_{0}
with the delta and then we performed the angular integration and removed the manifestly vanishing angular integrations.
After integration we obtain:

I_{P\gamma}^{P}=I^{P}_{P}=-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{18\omega_{P}^{2}}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right).

Summing all the relevant terms we found that the diagram can be written as:

M^{P}_{\mu\nu}=I^{P}_{u}\left(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+I^{P}_{P}\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+I^{P}_{u}\not{\hat{l}}\left(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right)+I^{P}_{P}\not{\hat{l}}\left(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right).

Using this result, the contribution to gravitomagnetic moment from the photon polarization diagram
at low temperatures is vanishing

g_{\Omega}^{P}=\lim_{a\to 0}4\left(I^{P}_{P\gamma}+\frac{I^{P}_{u\gamma}}{\omega_{P}}-I^{P}_{Pl}-\frac{I^{P}_{ul}}{\omega_{P}}\right)=0.

D.2.1
Fermionic part: High temperature

The fermionic part is negligible at low temperature but it is comparable to the bosonic part at high temperatures.
We obtain the fermionic part from

\begin{split}\text{Re}^{\beta}M^{P}_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\gamma_{\beta}\,\left(\not{p}-\not{k}+m\right)\gamma_{\alpha}u(p)T^{\alpha\beta}_{\mu\nu}(k,q+k)\frac{\delta\left((p-k)^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(p-k)}{(q+k)^{2}k^{2}}.\end{split}

Changing variables into
k\to p-k
we have

\begin{split}\text{Re}^{\beta}M^{P}_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\gamma_{\beta}\,\left(\not{k}+m\right)\gamma_{\alpha}u(p)T^{\alpha\beta}_{\mu\nu}(p-k,q+p-k)\frac{\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k)}{(q+p-k)^{2}(p-k)^{2}}\\
=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{P}u(p)}{D^{P}}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k).\end{split}

The numerator can be simplified into:

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{P}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{&\left(4\not{k}-8m\right)k_{\mu}k_{\nu}+\not{k}\,q_{\mu}q_{\nu}+\left[(k\cdot P)-2m^{2}\right](P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})-\not{k}P_{\mu}P_{\nu}+q^{2}(k_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+k_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\\
&-(k\cdot q)(q_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+q_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})+2m(k_{\mu}P_{\nu}+k_{\nu}P_{\mu})+\left(4m^{3}-2(k\cdot P)m-mq^{2}\right)\eta_{\mu\nu}\Big\}u(p).\end{split}

and the part relevant to AGM at first order in
q
is

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{P}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{&4\not{k}\,k_{\mu}k_{\nu}+\left[(k\cdot P)-2m^{2}\right](P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p).\end{split}

and after
k
decomposition the relevant part is

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{P}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{&4A_{u}A_{l}^{2}\not{\hat{l}}(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})+4A_{P}A_{l}^{2}\not{\hat{l}}(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})+\left(k\cdot P-2m^{2}\right)(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p).\end{split}

The denominator is

D^{P}=(q+p-k)^{2}(p-k)^{2}=\left(2m^{2}-(k\cdot P)\right)^{2}-(k\cdot q)^{2}=(2m^{2}-\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x}))^{2}-\epsilon^{2}k_{z}^{2}.

For the term in
\not{\hat{l}}(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})
we have

\begin{split}I^{P}_{ul,f}=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{4A_{u}A_{l}^{2}}{D}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k)\\
\simeq&-4e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{(k_{0}-k_{x}/a)k_{y}^{2}}{[\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x})-2m^{2}]^{2}}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k)\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\frac{k^{3}}{E_{k}}\,n_{F}(E_{k})\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{(sE_{k}/k-\hat{k}_{x}/a)\hat{k}_{y}^{2}}{[(sE_{k}/k-a\hat{k}_{x})-\frac{2m^{2}}{k\omega_{P}}]^{2}}\end{split}

Using the high temperature expansion described at the beginning of this appendix we find:

\begin{split}I^{P}_{ul,f}\simeq&-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}m^{2}}{12\pi\omega_{P}^{3}}\left(I_{\Omega}(0,2,0,3)+3(a^{2}-1)I_{\Omega}(2,2,0,3)-a^{2}I_{\Omega}(4,2,0,3)\right)\end{split}

and at the end

I^{P}_{ul,f}\simeq-\frac{1}{45}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{\omega_{P}}\frac{4m^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\left[\frac{15(3-a^{2})}{8a^{5}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{45}{4a^{4}}\right].

For the term in
(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})
we have

\begin{split}I^{P}_{Pl,f}=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{4A_{P}A_{l}^{2}}{D}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k)\\
\simeq&-\frac{4e^{2}}{a\omega_{P}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{k_{x}k_{y}^{2}}{[\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x})-2m^{2}]^{2}}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k)\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{4\pi^{3}a\omega_{P}^{3}}\int{\rm d}k\,\frac{k^{3}}{E_{k}}\,n_{F}(E_{k})\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{\hat{k}_{x}\hat{k}_{y}^{2}}{\left[(sE_{k}/k-a\hat{k}_{x})-\frac{2m^{2}}{k\omega_{P}}\right]^{2}}.\end{split}

At high temperatures it becomes

I^{P}_{Pl,f}=-\frac{e^{2}m^{2}T^{2}}{12\pi\omega_{P}^{4}}\left(3I_{\Omega}(2,2,0,3)+a^{2}I_{\Omega}(4,2,0,3)\right)

and hence

\begin{split}I^{P}_{Pl,f}=-\frac{1}{30}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\frac{4m^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\left[\frac{15(3-a^{2})}{8a^{5}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{45}{4a^{4}}\right].\end{split}

The part proportional to
(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})
at first order in
\epsilon
is the integral

\begin{split}I_{P\gamma,f}^{P}=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{(k\cdot P)-2m^{2}+A_{P}q^{2}}{D}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k)\\
\simeq&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x})-2m^{2}}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k)\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{3}\omega_{P}}\int{\rm d}k\frac{k}{E_{k}}\,n_{F}(E_{k})\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{1}{sE_{k}/k-a\hat{k}_{x}-\frac{2m^{2}}{k\omega_{P}}}.\end{split}

The high temperature expansion give a contribution of the form

I_{P\gamma,f}^{P}=\int{\rm d}k\,n_{F}(k)\sum_{n=1}k^{-n}f_{n}(a),

which gives logarithmic and sub-leading contributions in temperature that we can neglect. Notice that there is no IR divergence, because the previous
integral is just an approximation for high
k
, at low
k
when the divergence would occur the mass of the particle prevent the divergence.

Summing all the relevant terms we found that the diagram can be written as:

M^{P}_{\mu\nu,f}=I^{P}_{u,f}\not{\hat{l}}\left(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right)+I^{P}_{P,f}\not{\hat{l}}\left(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right),

with

\begin{split}I^{P}_{u,f}=&-\frac{4e^{2}T^{2}m^{2}}{45\omega_{P}^{3}}\left[\frac{15(3-a^{2})}{8a^{5}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{45}{4a^{4}}\right],\\
I^{P}_{P,f}=&-\frac{2e^{2}m^{2}T^{2}}{15\omega_{P}^{4}}\left[\frac{5(3-2a^{2})}{2a^{4}(1-a^{2})}-\frac{15}{4a^{5}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right].\end{split}

Using this result to evaluate the amplitude
\mathcal{A}\propto M^{P}_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}A_{g}^{\nu}
we find
that the gravitomagnetic moment coming from photon polarization diagram is

g_{\Omega}^{P}=-\lim_{P_{s}\to 0}\lim_{q\to 0}4\left(\frac{I^{P}_{u,f}}{\omega_{P}}+I^{P}_{P,f}\right)\theta_{\textup{HT}}=\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{18m^{2}}\theta_{\textup{HT}}.

Only at high temperatures the polarization diagram contribute to AGM.

D.3
Electromagnetic vertex

From electromagnetic vertex correction, Fig.
2
(d), we have

M_{\mu\nu}^{V}=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})(-\mathrm{i}e\gamma_{\alpha})\mathrm{i}S_{F}(p^{\prime}-k)V_{\mu\nu}(p-k,p^{\prime}-k)\mathrm{i}S_{F}(p-k)(-\mathrm{i}e\gamma_{\beta})\mathrm{i}D^{\alpha\beta}(k)u(p),

where
V_{\mu\nu}
is the stress-energy tensor fermion coupling:

V_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})=\frac{1}{4}\left[\gamma_{\mu}(p+p^{\prime})_{\nu}+\gamma_{\nu}(p+p^{\prime})_{\mu}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}\left[\not{p}-m+\not{p}^{\prime}-m\right].

As before, we evaluate the temperature part and the relevant part at low temperature is only given by the Bose distribution term:

\begin{split}{}^{\beta}M_{\mu\nu}^{V}=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\gamma_{\alpha}(\not{p}^{\prime}-\not{k}+m)V_{\mu\nu}(p-k,p^{\prime}-k)(\not{p}-\not{k}+m)\gamma^{\alpha}u(p)}{[(p^{\prime}-k)^{2}-m^{2}][(p-k)^{2}-m^{2}]}\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\\
\equiv&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{V}u(p)}{D^{V}}.\end{split}

After simplification we obtain

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{V}u(p)=-\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{&\left(4\not{k}+4m\right)\,k_{\mu}k_{\nu}+2\not{k}P_{\mu}P_{\nu}-\left(m+3\not{k}\right)(k_{\mu}P_{\nu}+k_{\nu}P_{\mu})\\
&+\left(m^{2}-q^{2}/2-(k\cdot P)\right)(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})+\left(-2m^{2}+q^{2}+2(k\cdot P)\right)(k_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+k_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\\
&+2\left(m+\not{k}\right)(k\cdot P)\eta_{\mu\nu}\Big\}u(p).\end{split}

The denominator is

D^{V}=\left[(k\cdot P)+(k\cdot q)\right]\left[(k\cdot P)-(k\cdot q)\right]=\omega_{P}^{2}(k_{0}-ak_{x})^{2}-k_{z}^{2}\epsilon^{2}.

The part of the nominator that brings contribution to AGM is then

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{V}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{&4\not{k}\,k_{\mu}k_{\nu}-3\not{k}(k_{\mu}P_{\nu}+k_{\nu}P_{\mu})+\left(m^{2}-q^{2}/2-(k\cdot P)\right)(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\\
&+\left(-2m^{2}+q^{2}+2(k\cdot P)\right)(k_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+k_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p).\end{split}

After decomposing
k
and removing odd terms in
A_{l}
, we have to consider:

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{V}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{&4A_{u}A_{l}^{2}\not{\hat{l}}(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})+(4A_{P}-3)A_{l}^{2}\not{\hat{l}}(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})\\
&+\left[\left(m^{2}-q^{2}/2-(k\cdot P)\right)+\left(-2m^{2}+q^{2}+2(k\cdot P)\right)A_{P}\right](P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\\
&+\left(-2m^{2}+q^{2}+2(k\cdot P)\right)A_{u}(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p).\end{split}

The part in
\not{\hat{l}}(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})
is

\begin{split}I^{V}_{ul}=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{4A_{u}A_{l}^{2}}{D^{V}}\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\simeq-4e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{(k_{0}-k_{x}/a)k_{y}^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}(k_{0}-ak_{x})^{2}}\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\\
=&-\frac{2e^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\,k^{2}\,n_{B}(k)\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{(s-\hat{k}_{x}/a)\hat{k}_{y}^{2}}{(s-a\hat{k}_{x})^{2}}\\
=&-\frac{2e^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\,k^{2}\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{2\left(a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{3}-2a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}+\hat{k}_{x}\right)\hat{k}_{y}^{2}}{a(1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})^{2}}=0.\end{split}

The part in
\not{\hat{l}}(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})
is

\begin{split}I^{V}_{Pl}=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{(4A_{P}-3)A_{l}^{2}}{D^{V}}\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\simeq e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{(3-4k_{x}/a\omega_{P})k_{y}^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}(k_{0}-ak_{x})^{2}}\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\\
=&\frac{e^{2}}{2(2\pi)^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{(3-4k\hat{k}_{x}/a\omega_{P})\hat{k}_{y}^{2}}{(s-a\hat{k}_{x})^{2}}=\frac{3e^{2}}{2(2\pi)^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{2(1+a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})\hat{k}_{y}^{2}}{(1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})^{2}}\\
=&\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{12\omega_{P}^{2}}\left[\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right].\end{split}

The term in
P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}
gives

\begin{split}I^{V}_{P\gamma}\simeq&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{m^{2}}{(k_{0}-ak_{x})^{2}}-\frac{\omega_{P}}{k_{0}-ak_{x}}\right]\frac{a\omega_{p}-2k_{x}}{a\omega_{P}^{3}}\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{2(2\pi)^{3}a\omega_{P}^{3}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[\frac{m^{2}}{k^{2}}\frac{a\omega_{p}-2k\,\hat{k}_{x}}{(s-a\hat{k}_{x})^{2}}-\frac{\omega_{P}}{k}\frac{a\omega_{p}-2k\,\hat{k}_{x}}{s-a\hat{k}_{x}}\right]\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{3}a\omega_{P}^{3}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[\frac{m^{2}}{k^{2}}\frac{2(a\omega_{p}-2k\,\hat{k}_{x})(1+a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})}{(1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})^{2}}-\frac{\omega_{P}}{k}\frac{(a\omega_{p}-2k\,\hat{k}_{x})2a\hat{k}_{x}}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}\right]\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[\frac{m^{2}}{k^{2}}\frac{2(1+a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})}{(1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})^{2}}+2\frac{2\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}\right]\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[m^{2}\frac{I_{A}}{8\pi}\frac{2(1+a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})}{(1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{2\pi^{2}T^{2}}{6}\frac{2\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}\right]\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}m^{2}}{16\pi^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\frac{I_{A}}{1-a^{2}}-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{18\omega_{P}^{2}}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right).\end{split}

The term in
u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}
gives

\begin{split}I^{V}_{u\gamma}\simeq&2e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{m^{2}}{(k_{0}-ak_{x})^{2}}-\frac{\omega_{P}}{k_{0}-ak_{x}}\right]\frac{k_{0}-k_{x}/a}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\delta\left(k^{2}\right)n_{B}(k)\\
=&\frac{e^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[\frac{m^{2}}{k}\frac{(s-\hat{k}/a)}{(s-a\hat{k}_{x})^{2}}-\frac{\omega_{p}(s-\hat{k}_{x}/a)}{s-a\hat{k}_{x}}\right]\\
=&\frac{e^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[\frac{m^{2}}{k}\frac{2(\hat{k}_{x}-2a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}+a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{3}))}{a(1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2})^{2}}-\omega_{P}\frac{2(1-\hat{k}_{x}^{2})}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}\right]\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{(2\pi)^{3}\omega_{P}}\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{6}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{2(1-\hat{k}_{x}^{2})}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{9\omega_{P}}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right).\end{split}

Summing all the relevant terms we found that the diagram can be written as:

M^{V}_{\mu\nu}=I^{V}_{u\gamma}\left(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+I^{V}_{P\gamma}\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+I^{V}_{Pl}\not{\hat{l}}\left(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu}\right).

D.3.1
Fermionic part: High temperature

The fermionic part is:

\begin{split}\text{Re}^{\beta}M^{V}_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})=&e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\gamma_{\alpha}(\not{p}^{\prime}-\not{k}+m)V_{\mu\nu}(p-k,p^{\prime}-k)(\not{p}-\not{k}+m)\gamma^{\alpha}u(p)}{k^{2}}\times\\
&\times\left[\frac{\delta\left((p-k)^{2}-m^{2}\right)}{(p^{\prime}-k)^{2}-m^{2}}n_{F}(p-k)+\frac{\delta\left((p^{\prime}-k)^{2}-m^{2}\right)}{(p-k)^{2}-m^{2}}n_{F}(p^{\prime}-k)\right].\end{split}

We change
k
to
p-k
in the first term and
k\to p^{\prime}-k
in the second one:

\begin{split}\text{Re}^{\beta}M^{V}_{\mu\nu}(p,p^{\prime})=&e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\gamma_{\alpha}\left[\frac{(\not{p}^{\prime}-\not{p}+\not{k}+m)V_{\mu\nu}(k,p^{\prime}-p+k)(\not{k}+m)}{[(p^{\prime}-p+k)^{2}-m^{2}](p-k)^{2}}\right.\\
&\left.+\frac{(\not{k}+m)V_{\mu\nu}(p-p^{\prime}+k,k)(\not{p}-\not{p}^{\prime}+\not{k}+m)}{[(p-p^{\prime}+k)^{2}-m^{2}](p^{\prime}-k)^{2}}\right]\gamma^{\alpha}u(p)\,n_{F}(k)\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})\\
\equiv&e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[\frac{N^{V}_{1}}{D_{1}}+\frac{N^{V}_{2}}{D_{2}}\right]u(p)n_{F}(k)\delta(k^{2}-m^{2}).\end{split}

After simplification the relevant parts for numerators are

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{V}_{1}u(p)=&\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{-4\not{k}\,k_{\mu}k_{\nu}-\not{k}(k_{\mu}P_{\nu}+k_{\nu}P_{\mu})+\left(k\cdot P+k\cdot q\right)(k_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+k_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p);\\
\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{V}_{2}u(p)=&\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{-4\not{k}\,k_{\mu}k_{\nu}-\not{k}(k_{\mu}P_{\nu}+k_{\nu}P_{\mu})+\left(k\cdot P-k\cdot q\right)(k_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+k_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p).\end{split}

After decomposing
k

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{V}_{1}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{&-4A_{u}A_{l}^{2}\not{\hat{l}}(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})-(4A_{P}+1)A_{l}^{2}\not{\hat{l}}(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})\\
&+\left(k\cdot P+k\cdot q\right)A_{P}(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})+\left(k\cdot P+k\cdot q\right)A_{u}(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p);\\
\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N^{V}_{2}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{&-4A_{u}A_{l}^{2}\not{\hat{l}}(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})-(4A_{P}+1)A_{l}^{2}\not{\hat{l}}(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})\\
&+\left(k\cdot P-k\cdot q\right)A_{P}(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})+\left(k\cdot P-k\cdot q\right)A_{u}(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p).\end{split}

The denominators are

\begin{split}D^{V}_{1}=&(4m^{2}-2k\cdot P)(k\cdot q)+2(k\cdot q)^{2}+q^{2}(2m^{2}-k\cdot P+k\cdot q)\\
=&2k_{z}\epsilon\left(\omega_{P}k_{0}-a\omega_{P}k_{x}-2m^{2}\right)+\epsilon^{2}\left(\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x})+2k_{z}^{2}-2m^{2}\right)+k_{z}\epsilon^{3};\\
D^{V}_{2}=&-(4m^{2}-2k\cdot P)(k\cdot q)+2(k\cdot q)^{2}+q^{2}(2m^{2}-k\cdot P-k\cdot q)\\
=&-2k_{z}\epsilon\left(\omega_{P}k_{0}-a\omega_{P}k_{x}-2m^{2}\right)+\epsilon^{2}\left(\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x})+2k_{z}^{2}-2m^{2}\right)-k_{z}\epsilon^{3}.\end{split}

Consider the term in
P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}
:

\begin{split}I^{V}_{P\gamma}=&e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{\omega_{P}(k_{0}-k_{x}a)-k_{z}\epsilon}{D^{V}_{1}}+\frac{\omega_{P}(k_{0}-k_{x}a)+k_{z}\epsilon}{D^{V}_{2}}\right]\frac{k_{x}}{a\omega_{P}}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k),\end{split}

after expanding at first order in
\epsilon
and in high temperature, we find

\begin{split}I^{V}_{P\gamma}\simeq&-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{96\pi\omega_{P}^{2}}I_{\Omega}(2,0,0,1)\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{36\omega_{P}^{2}}\left[\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right].\end{split}

Similarly the term in
u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}

\begin{split}I^{V}_{u\gamma}\simeq&e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{\omega_{P}(k_{0}-k_{x}a)-k_{z}\epsilon}{D^{V}_{1}}+\frac{\omega_{P}(k_{0}-k_{x}a)+k_{z}\epsilon}{D^{V}_{2}}\right](k_{0}-k_{x}/a)\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k),\end{split}

and at high temperature it becomes

\begin{split}I^{V}_{u\gamma}\simeq&-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{96\pi\omega_{P}}\left(I_{\Omega}(2,0,0,1)-I_{\Omega}(0,0,0,1)\right)\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{18\omega_{P}}\left[\frac{3}{2a^{2}}-\frac{3(1-a^{2})}{4a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right].\end{split}

The term in
\not{l}(P_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})
:

\begin{split}I^{V}_{Pl}\simeq&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{D^{V}_{1}}+\frac{1}{D^{V}_{2}}\right]\frac{(4k_{x}+a\omega_{P})k_{y}^{2}}{a\omega_{P}}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k)\end{split}

at first order in
\epsilon
and at high temperature is

\begin{split}I^{V}_{Pl}\simeq&\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{192\pi\omega_{P}^{4}}\left(16m^{2}\left(3I_{\Omega}(2,2,0,3)+a^{2}I_{\Omega}(4,2,0,3)\right)+\omega_{P}^{2}\left(I_{\Omega}(0,2,0,3)+2I_{\Omega}(2,2,-2,3)-4a^{2}I_{\Omega}(4,2,-2,3)+\right.\right.\\
&\left.\left.-a^{4}I_{\Omega}(4,2,0,3)+2a^{4}I_{\Omega}(6,2,-2,3)\right)\right)\\
=&\frac{2e^{2}m^{2}T^{2}}{15\omega_{P}^{4}}\left[\frac{5(3-2a^{2})}{2a^{4}(1-a^{2})}-\frac{15}{4a^{5}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right]-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{72\omega_{P}^{2}}\left[\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right]\\
=&\frac{7}{360}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\left[\frac{12}{7}\frac{4m^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\left(\frac{5(3-2a^{2})}{2a^{4}(1-a^{2})}-\frac{15}{4a^{5}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right)-\frac{5}{7}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right)\right].\end{split}

Lastly, the term in
\not{l}(u_{\mu}\hat{l}_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\hat{l}_{\mu})
:

\begin{split}I^{V}_{ul}\simeq&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{1}{D^{V}_{1}}+\frac{1}{D^{V}_{2}}\right]4(k_{0}-k_{x}/a)k_{y}^{2}\delta\left(k^{2}-m^{2}\right)n_{F}(k)\end{split}

gives

\begin{split}I^{V}_{ul}\simeq&\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{96\pi\omega_{P}^{3}}\left(8m^{2}\left(I_{\Omega}(0,2,0,3)+3(a^{2}-1)I_{\Omega}(2,2,0,3)-a^{2}I_{\Omega}(4,2,0,3)\right)+\omega_{P}^{2}\left(I_{\Omega}(0,2,-2,3)+\right.\right.\\
&\left.\left.-(1+2a^{2})I_{\Omega}(2,2,-2,3)+2a^{2}I_{\Omega}(4,2,-2,3)+a^{4}I_{\Omega}(4,2,-2,3)-a^{4}I_{\Omega}(6,2,-2,3)\right)\right)\\
=&\frac{4e^{2}m^{2}T^{2}}{45\omega_{P}^{3}}\left[\frac{15(3-2a^{2})}{8a^{5}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{45}{4a^{4}}\right]-\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{18\omega_{P}}\left[\frac{3}{2a^{2}}-\frac{3(1-a^{2})}{4a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right]\\
=&-\frac{1}{30}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{\omega_{P}}\left[-\frac{2}{3}\frac{4m^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\left(\frac{15(3-2a^{2})}{8a^{5}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{45}{4a^{4}}\right)+\frac{5}{3}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{2}}-\frac{3(1-a^{2})}{4a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right)\right].\end{split}

D.4
Contact term

The contact diagram, Fig.
2
(e), is

M_{\mu\nu}^{C}=\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e\,a_{\mu\nu\rho\kappa}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[\gamma^{\kappa}\mathrm{i}S_{F}(p-k)(-\mathrm{i}e\gamma^{\alpha})\mathrm{i}D^{\rho}_{\alpha}(k)+(p^{\prime}\leftrightarrow p)\right]u(p),

where the contact vertex is:

a_{\mu\nu}^{\beta\rho}=\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta^{\beta\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta^{\beta}_{\mu}\eta^{\rho}_{\nu}+\eta^{\beta}_{\nu}\eta^{\rho}_{\mu}\right).

The temperature part given by the Bose distribution is:

\begin{split}{}^{\beta}M_{\mu\nu}^{C}(p^{\prime},p)=&-e^{2}a_{\mu\nu\rho\kappa}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(k^{2})n_{B}(k)\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[\frac{\gamma^{\kappa}(\not{p}-\not{k}+m)\gamma^{\rho}}{(p-k)^{2}-m^{2}}+(p^{\prime}\leftrightarrow p)\right]u(p)\\
\equiv&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[\frac{N_{1}^{C}}{D_{1}^{C}}+\frac{N_{2}^{C}}{D_{2}^{C}}\right]u(p)n_{B}(k)\delta(k^{2}).\end{split}

We refer to “1” as the first term and with “2” to the term with
p^{\prime}\leftrightarrow p
:
The numerator of the Bose part:

\begin{split}\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N_{1}^{C}u(p)=&\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+(k_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+k_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})+\frac{1}{2}\left(q_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+q_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+\left(2m+\not{k}\right)\eta_{\mu\nu}\Big\}u(p),\\
\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N_{2}^{C}u(p)=&\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+(k_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+k_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})-\frac{1}{2}\left(q_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+q_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+\left(2m+\not{k}\right)\eta_{\mu\nu}\Big\}u(p),\end{split}

therefore the contribution to AGM is the same for the terms “1” and “2”:

\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N_{1}^{C}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N_{2}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+(k_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+k_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p).

Decomposing
k
in the numerators we have

\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N_{1}^{C}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})N_{2}u(p)=\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\Big\{\left(A_{P}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+A_{u}(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu})\Big\}u(p)

and the denominators are

\begin{split}D_{1}^{C}=&-\left[(k\cdot P)-(k\cdot q)\right]=-\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x})-k_{z}\epsilon,\\
D_{2}^{C}=&-\left[(k\cdot P)+(k\cdot q)\right]=-\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x})+k_{z}\epsilon.\end{split}

The part in
\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)
at leading order in
\epsilon
is

\begin{split}I_{P\gamma}^{C}=&-e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\frac{N_{1}^{C}}{D_{1}^{C}}+\frac{N_{2}^{C}}{D_{2}^{C}}\right]n_{B}(k)\delta(k^{2})\simeq-\frac{e^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(k^{2})n_{B}(k)\frac{\omega_{P}-2k_{x}/a}{k_{0}-ak_{x}}\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[\frac{\omega_{P}}{k}\frac{1}{s-a\hat{k}_{x}}-\frac{2}{a}\frac{\hat{k}_{x}}{s-a\hat{k}_{x}}\right]\\
=&-\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\left[\frac{\omega_{P}}{k}\frac{2a\hat{k}_{x}}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}-2\frac{2\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}\right].\end{split}

The first term in square bracket is vanishing and the second one gives

\begin{split}I_{P\gamma}^{C}=&+\frac{e^{2}}{16\pi^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}2\frac{8\pi}{3}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right)\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\\
=&\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{18\omega_{P}^{2}}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right).\end{split}

The part in
\left(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)
at leading order in
\epsilon
is

\begin{split}I_{u\gamma}^{C}\simeq&\frac{2e^{2}}{\omega_{P}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(k^{2})n_{B}(k)\frac{k_{0}-k_{x}/a}{k_{0}-ak_{x}}=\frac{e^{2}}{8\pi^{3}\omega_{P}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{s-\hat{k}_{x}/a}{s-a\hat{k}_{x}}\\
=&\frac{e^{2}}{8\pi^{3}\omega_{P}}\int{\rm d}k\,k\,n_{B}(k)\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{2(1-\hat{k}_{x}^{2})}{1-a^{2}\hat{k}_{x}^{2}}=\frac{e^{2}}{8\pi^{3}\omega_{P}}\frac{\pi^{2}T^{2}}{6}\frac{16\pi}{3}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{2}}-\frac{3(1-a^{2})}{4a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right)\\
=&\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{9\omega_{P}}\left(\frac{3}{2a^{2}}-\frac{3(1-a^{2})}{4a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right).\end{split}

D.4.1
Fermionic part: High temperature

The temperature part given by the Fermi distribution is:

\begin{split}{}^{\beta}M_{\mu\nu}^{C}(p^{\prime},p)=&e^{2}a_{\mu\nu\rho\kappa}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})n_{F}(k)\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[\frac{\gamma^{\kappa}(\not{k}+m)\gamma^{\rho}}{(p-k)^{2}}+(p^{\prime}\leftrightarrow p)\right]u(p)\\
=&e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})n_{F}(k)\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\gamma^{\kappa}(\not{k}+m)\gamma^{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{(p-k)^{2}}+\frac{1}{(p^{\prime}-k)^{2}}\right]u(p)\\
\equiv&e^{2}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})n_{F}(k)\bar{u}(p^{\prime})\left[\frac{N^{C}}{D_{1}^{C}}+\frac{N^{C}}{D_{2}^{C}}\right]u(p).\end{split}

The numerator is

N^{C}=-\left(\gamma_{\mu}k_{\nu}+\gamma_{\nu}k_{\mu}\right)\to N^{C}_{\textup{AGM}}=-A_{u}\left(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)-A_{P}\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right).

The denominators are

\begin{split}D_{1}^{C}=&\left[2m^{2}-(k\cdot P)+(k\cdot q)\right]=2m^{2}-\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x})-k_{z}\epsilon,\\
D_{2}^{C}=&\left[2m^{2}-(k\cdot P)-(k\cdot q)\right]=2m^{2}-\omega_{P}(k_{0}-ak_{x})+k_{z}\epsilon.\end{split}

The part in
\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)
at leading order in
\epsilon
is

\begin{split}I_{P\gamma,f}^{C}\simeq&\frac{2e^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}a}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})n_{F}(k)\frac{k_{x}}{k_{0}-ak_{x}-2m^{2}/\omega_{P}}\\
=&\frac{e^{2}}{8\pi^{3}\omega_{P}^{2}}\frac{1}{a}\int{\rm d}k\,\frac{k^{2}}{E_{k}}\,n_{F}(k)\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\frac{\hat{k}_{x}}{s\frac{E_{k}}{k}-a\hat{k}_{x}-\frac{2m^{2}}{\omega_{P}k}}.\end{split}

At high temperature the leading term is

I_{P\gamma,f}^{C}\simeq\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{96\pi\omega_{P}^{2}}I_{\Omega}(2,0,0,1)=\frac{1}{36}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{\omega_{P}^{2}}\left[\frac{3}{2a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)-\frac{3}{a^{2}}\right].

The part in
\left(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)
at leading order in
\epsilon
and expanding for high temperatures is

\begin{split}I_{u\gamma,f}^{C}\simeq&\frac{2e^{2}}{\omega_{P}}\int\frac{{\rm d}^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(k^{2}-m^{2})n_{F}(k)\frac{k_{0}-k_{x}/a}{k_{0}-ak_{x}-2m^{2}/\omega_{P}}\\
=&\frac{e^{2}}{8\pi^{3}\omega_{P}}\int{\rm d}k\,n_{F}(k)\sum_{s=\pm}\int{\rm d}\Omega_{k}\,\frac{k^{2}}{E_{k}}\frac{sE_{k}/k-\hat{k}_{x}/a}{sE_{k}/k-a\hat{k}_{x}-\frac{2m^{2}}{\omega_{P}k}}\\
\simeq&\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{96\pi\omega_{P}}\left(I_{\Omega}(0,0,0,1)-I_{\Omega}(2,0,0,1)\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{18}\frac{e^{2}T^{2}}{\omega_{P}}\left[\frac{3}{2a^{2}}-\frac{3(1-a^{2})}{4a^{3}}\log\left(\frac{1+a}{1-a}\right)\right].\end{split}

Summing all the relevant terms we found that the diagram can be written as:

M^{C}_{\mu\nu}=\left(I^{C}_{P\gamma}+\theta_{\textup{HT}}I^{C}_{P\gamma,f}\right)\left(P_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+P_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right)+\left(I_{u\gamma}^{C}+\theta_{\textup{HT}}I^{C}_{u\gamma,f}\right)\left(u_{\mu}\gamma_{\nu}+u_{\nu}\gamma_{\mu}\right).

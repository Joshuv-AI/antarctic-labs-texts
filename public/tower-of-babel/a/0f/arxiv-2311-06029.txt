Nonlocal quantum state ensembles and quantum data hiding
Donghoon Ha, Jeong San Kim

Abstract
We consider the discrimination of bipartite quantum states and establish a relation between a nonlocal quantum state ensemble and quantum data-hiding processing. Using a bound on the optimal local discrimination of bipartite quantum states, we provide a sufficient condition for a bipartite quantum state ensemble to be used to construct a quantum data-hiding scheme. Our results are illustrated with examples in multidimensional bipartite quantum systems.

Introduction

Quantum nonlocality is a key feature of quantum information processing and quantum communication
horo2009
;
chid2013
;
brun2014
;
uola2020
.
Quantum entanglement is a well-known quantum nonlocal correlation which can be used as crucial resources for quantum key distribution and quantum teleportation
eker1991
;
benn1993
;
chit2019
.
Quantum steering is another quantum nonlocality which can be used in various quantum information-processing tasks such as quantum cryptography, randomness certification, and quantum channel discrimination
uola2020
.
Recently, it was shown that the nonlocality of quantum coherence can provide
advantages for coherence steerability
stre2017
;
mond2017
;
hu2018
.

A quantum nonlocal phenomenon can also arise in discriminating multipartite quantum states.
In general, orthogonal quantum states can be discriminated with certainty,
whereas nonorthogonal quantum states cannot
chef2000
;
berg2007
;
barn20091
;
bae2015
.
However, some multipartite orthogonal quantum states exist that cannot be perfectly discriminated by only
local operations and classical communication
(LOCC)
benn19991
;
ghos2001
;
walg2002
.
Moreover, some multipartite nonorthogonal quantum states exist that cannot be optimally discriminated by only LOCC
pere1991
;
chit2013
;
chit20142
.

The nonlocality of multipartite quantum state discrimination can be useful in quantum communications
terh20011
;
divi2002
.
Quantum data hiding is one of the typical applications that use the nonlocality of multipartite quantum state discrimination
terh20011
;
divi2002
.
The first quantum data-hiding scheme was shown through the nonlocal quantum state ensemble consisting of Bell states
terh20011
; a classical bit is accessible globally but not locally, and the bit is perfectly hidden asymptotically.
Several quantum data-hiding schemes were also introduced using sets of quantum states
egge2002
;
mori2013
;
pian2014
;
peng2021
;
lami2021
.
However, it is still natural to ask which kind of nonlocal quantum state ensemble can be used in quantum data-hiding schemes.

Here, we consider the discrimination of bipartite quantum states and establish a relation between the nonlocal quantum state ensemble and quantum data-hiding processing.
We first introduce a quantity realizing the optimal distinguishability of
partially transposed
(PT) states
ha20221
.
By using this quantity, we show that a bipartite orthogonal quantum state ensemble with small PT-state distinguishability can be turned into a globally distinguishable ensemble with arbitrarily small local distinguishability.
Based on this relation, we provide a sufficient condition for a bipartite quantum state ensemble to be used to construct a quantum data-hiding scheme.
Using our quantum data-hiding scheme, classical data of arbitrary size can be perfectly hidden, asymptotically, if the users are restricted to LOCC measurements, whereas the data can be completely recovered if they can collaborate to use global measurements.
Our results are illustrated by examples in multidimensional bipartite quantum systems.

In Sec.

We further recall a bound on local minimum-error discrimination and provide some properties which are useful in quantum data hiding.
In Sec.

In Sec.
IV
, our results are illustrated with examples in multidimensional bipartite quantum systems.
In Sec.
V

II
Bounds of local minimum-error discrimination

For bipartite quantum systems held by two parties, Alice and Bob, a quantum state is described by a density operator
\rho
, that is, a positive-semidefinite operator
\rho\succeq 0
with unit trace
\Tr\rho=1
, acting on a bipartite Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{d_{\rm A}}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{d_{\rm B}}
.
A measurement is represented by a positive operator-valued measure
\{M_{i}\}_{i}
, that is, a set of positive-semidefinite operators
M_{i}\succeq 0
acting on
\mathcal{H}
satisfying
the completeness relation
\sum_{i}M_{i}=\mathbbm{1}
, where
\mathbbm{1}
is the identity operator acting on
\mathcal{H}
.
When a measurement
\{M_{i}\}_{i}
is performed on a quantum state
\rho
, the probability of obtaining the measurement outcome with respect to
M_{j}
is
\Tr(\rho M_{j})
.

In this section, we consider the situation of discriminating
n
bipartite quantum states
\rho_{0},\ldots,\rho_{n-1}
, where the state
\rho_{i}
is prepared with the probability
\eta_{i}\in[0,1]
for
i=0,\ldots,n-1
. We denote this situation as the
quantum state discrimination of the ensemble
,

\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}.

(1)

In discriminating the states from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
using a measurement
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
,
we decide that the prepared state is
\rho_{i}

if the measurement outcome is from
M_{i}
.
In this case, the average probability of correctly guessing the prepared state is

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}).

(2)

The
minimum-error discrimination
of
\mathcal{E}
is to achieve the optimal success probability

p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\mathcal{M}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}),

(3)

where the maximum is taken over all possible measurements
hels1969
;
bae2013
.

A measurement is called a
LOCC measurement
if it can be realized by LOCC.
When the available measurements are limited to LOCC measurements, we denote the maximum success probability by

p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rm LOCC\\
\mathcal{M}\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}M_{i}).

(4)

From the definitions of
p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
and
p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})
in Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
), respectively,
p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
is obviously an upper bound of
p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})
; therefore,

p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}).

(5)

Moreover, guessing the prepared state as the state with largest prior probability is obviously a LOCC measurement; thus, we have

\max\{\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{n-1}\}\leqslant p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}).

(6)

Now, we consider an upper bound of
p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})
in Eq. (
4
) based on
partial transposition
.
We further provide some properties of the upper bound which are useful in quantum data hiding.

Definition 1
.

A Hermitian operator
E
on a bipartite Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called
positive partial transpose
(PPT) if

E^{\PT}\succeq 0
,
where the superscript
\PT
indicates
the partial transposition
pere1996
;
pptp
. Otherwise,
E
is called
negative partial transpose
(NPT).

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
,
let us consider

q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})=\max_{\mathcal{M}}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\eta_{i}\Tr(\rho_{i}^{\PT}M_{i}),

(7)

where the maximum is taken over all possible measurements.
This quantity is known as an upper bound of
p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})
; that is,

p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})

(8)

for any bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}

ha20221
.
The following proposition provides a necessary and sufficient condition of a measurement realizing
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})

ha20221
.

Proposition 1
.

For a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}

and a measurement
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
,

\mathcal{M}
realizes
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
if and only if

\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\eta_{j}\rho_{j}^{\PT}M_{j}-\eta_{i}\rho_{i}^{\PT}\succeq 0

(9)

for each
i=0,\ldots,n-1
.

Although Proposition
1
provides a necessary and sufficient condition of the optimal measurement realizing
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
,
the definition of
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
in Eq. (
7
) is based on the optimization over all possible measurements. However, the following theorem provides the closed-form expression of
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
if the ensemble consists of two states.
The proof of Theorem
1
is given in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 1
.

For a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{1}
,

q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Tr|\eta_{0}\rho_{0}^{\PT}-\eta_{1}\rho_{1}^{\PT}|,

(10)

where
|E|
is the positive square root of
E^{\dagger}E
, that is,

|E|=\sqrt{E^{\dagger}E}.

(11)

For an ensemble
\mathcal{E}
with an arbitrary number of states, the following theorem provides an upper bound of
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
.
The proof of Theorem
2
is given in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 2
.

For a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
and a Hermitian operator
H
, if

H-\eta_{i}\rho_{i}^{\PT}\succeq 0

(12)

for each
i=0,\ldots,n-1
, then the trace of
H
is an upper bound of
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
, that is,

\Tr H\geqslant q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}).

(13)

III
Quantum data hiding

In this section, we show that a bipartite orthogonal quantum state ensemble with small
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
, the optimal PT-state distinguishability, can be turned into a globally distinguishable ensemble with arbitrary small local distinguishability.
Based on this relation, we provide a sufficient condition for a bipartite quantum state ensemble that can be used to construct a quantum data-hiding scheme. In our scheme, the hidden data is globally accessible, and LOCC measurements can provide arbitrarily little information about the data.
To analyze how reliably the data is hidden, we first introduce the notion of a
multifold ensemble
.

For positive integers
n
and
L
, we denote

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
as the set of all integers from
0
to
n-1
and
\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{L}
as the Cartesian product of
L
copies of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
.
For the ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
in Eq. (
1
) and each vector

\vec{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{L})\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{L},

(14)

we define

\eta_{\vec{c}}=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\eta_{c_{l}},~\rho_{\vec{c}}=\bigotimes_{l=1}^{L}\rho_{c_{l}},

(15)

where
\rho_{c_{l}}
is the state in the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
whose index is the
l
th coordinate of the vector
\vec{c}
, and
\eta_{c_{l}}
is the corresponding probability for
l=1,\ldots,L
.
We further use
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}
to denote the
L
-fold ensemble
of
\mathcal{E}
, that is,

\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}=\{\eta_{\vec{c}},\rho_{\vec{c}}\}_{\vec{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{L}}.

(16)

For each
\vec{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{L}
, we define
\omega_{n}(\vec{c})

as the modulo
n
summation of all entries in
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{L})
, that is,

\omega_{n}(\vec{c})=\sum_{l=1}^{L}c_{l}~(\mathrm{mod}~n).

(17)

Clearly, we have
\omega_{n}(\vec{c})\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
for any
\vec{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{L}
.

For the state
\rho_{\vec{c}}
prepared from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}
, let us consider the situation of guessing
\omega_{n}(\vec{c})
of the prepared state
\rho_{\vec{c}}
from
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}
.
This situation is equivalent to discriminating the states
\rho_{0}^{(L)},\ldots,\rho_{n-1}^{(L)}
prepared with the probabilities
\eta_{0}^{(L)},\ldots,\eta_{n-1}^{(L)}
, respectively, where

\displaystyle\eta_{i}^{(L)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{L}\\
\omega_{n}(\vec{c})=i\end{subarray}}\eta_{\vec{c}},~\rho_{i}^{(L)}=\frac{1}{\eta_{i}^{(L)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\vec{c}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{L}\\
\omega_{n}(\vec{c})=i\end{subarray}}\eta_{\vec{c}}\rho_{\vec{c}}.

(18)

In other words, quantum state discrimination of the ensemble

\mathcal{E}^{(L)}=\{\eta_{i}^{(L)},\rho_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}.

(19)

For a given two-state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{1}
, the following theorem gives the closed-form expression of
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
.
The proof of Theorem
3
is given in Appendix
B
.

Theorem 3
.

For a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{1}
and any positive integer
L
,

q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big(\Tr|\eta_{0}\rho_{0}^{\PT}-\eta_{1}\rho_{1}^{\PT}|\big)^{L}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\big[2q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})-1\big]^{L}.

(20)

Moreover, we have

p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})=q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})

(21)

if
p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})=q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
.

Theorem
3
provides the closed-form expression of
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
for the case of the two-state ensemble
\mathcal{E}
.
The following theorem provides an upper bound of
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
for a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}
with an arbitrary number of states.
The proof of Theorem
4
is given in Appendix
C
.

Theorem 4
.

For a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
and any positive integer
L
, we have

q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\leqslant\frac{1}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}\big[nq_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})-1\big]^{L}.

(22)

From Theorem
4
, we have

\frac{1}{n}\leqslant p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\leqslant q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\leqslant\frac{1}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}\big[nq_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})-1\big]^{L},

(23)

where the first inequality is due to inequality (
6
), the second inequality is from inequality (
8
), and the last inequality is from Theorem
4
.
For the case of
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})<\frac{2}{n}
,
the last term in Eq. (
23
) decreases to
\frac{1}{n}
as
L
gets larger.
Thus, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

For a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
with

q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})<\frac{2}{n}
,

\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty}p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})=\frac{1}{n}.

(24)

We further note that the last term in Eq. (
23
) decreases to
\frac{1}{n}
exponentially fast with respect to
L
.
Thus, the convergence of
p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
to
\frac{1}{n}
is also exponentially fast with respect to
L
.

In information theory, data hiding is the protection of a data by sharing it with multiple users so that the data becomes accessible only as a consequence of their cooperative action.
By using particular bipartite quantum states, several quantum data-hiding schemes have been proposed to share data between two parties, Alice and Bob
terh20011
;
divi2002
;
egge2002
;
mori2013
;
pian2014
;
peng2021
;
lami2021
.
If Alice and Bob can perform only LOCC measurements, they can obtain arbitrarily little information about the hidden data.
They can access the data only by global measurements, which require either a quantum channel, shared quantum entanglement, or direct interaction between them.

Here, we establish a relation between a bipartite quantum state ensemble and its role in quantum data hiding.
For a given bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}
, we first provide a condition in terms of
p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
and
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
defined in Eqs. (

) and (

We show that

satisfies the condition.
The data is globally accessible, and LOCC measurements can provide arbitrarily little information about the data. In other words, the data is perfectly hidden asymptotically.

For any integer

, let us consider a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
satisfying the following condition:

p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})=1,~~q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})<\frac{2}{n}.

(25)

By using the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
, we construct a quantum data-hiding scheme that can hide an
n
-ary classical data, that is, one in
\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
.

The hider, the third party, first chooses a bipartite quantum state
\rho_{c_{1}}
from
\mathcal{E}
with the corresponding probability
\eta_{c_{1}}
for
c_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, and shares it with Alice and Bob.
The hider repeats this process
L
times, so that
\rho_{c_{l}}
is chosen with probability
\eta_{c_{l}}
in the

th repetition for
l=1,\ldots,L
.
This whole procedure is equivalent to the situation in which the hider first prepares the state

\rho_{\vec{c}}=\rho_{c_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{c_{L}}

from the
L
-fold ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{\otimes L}
in Eq. (
16
)
with the corresponding probability
\eta_{\vec{c}}
for
\vec{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{L})\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{L}
, and sends it to Alice and Bob.

.

To hide classical data

, the hider broadcasts the classical information
z
to Alice and Bob,

where

for the prepared state

IV
Examples

In this section, we provide examples of bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}
satisfying condition (
25
) to illustrate our results.
First, we present the following example of the two-state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{1}
.

Example 1
.

For a NPT quantum state
\sigma
, let us consider the two-state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{1}
consisting of

\displaystyle\eta_{0}=\frac{\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|+1}{2\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|},

\displaystyle~~\rho_{0}=\frac{|\sigma^{\PT}|+\sigma^{\PT}}{\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|+1},

\displaystyle\eta_{1}=\frac{\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|-1}{2\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|},

\displaystyle~~\rho_{1}=\frac{|\sigma^{\PT}|-\sigma^{\PT}}{\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|-1}.

(30)

We first note that
\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|>1
because
\sigma
is a NPT state.

It is straightforward to verify

\Tr|\eta_{0}\rho_{0}^{\PT}-\eta_{1}\rho_{1}^{\PT}|=\frac{1}{\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|}.

(31)

From Theorem
1
together with Eqs. (
1
) and (
31
), we have

q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|+1}{2\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|}=\eta_{0}.

(32)

We also note that

\eta_{0}\leqslant p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})\leqslant q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})=\eta_{0},

(33)

where the first inequality is from inequality (
6
),
the second inequality is due to inequality (
8
),
and the last equality is from Eq. (
32
).
Thus, we have

p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E})=q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})=\eta_{0}.

(34)

From Theorem
3
together with Eqs. (
31
) and (
34
), we have

p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})=q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2(\Tr|\sigma^{\PT}|)^{L}}

(35)

for any positive integer
L
.

Because
|E|^{2}=E^{2}
for any Hermitian operator
E
, we can easily check that
\rho_{0}
and
\rho_{1}
in Eq. (
1
) are orthogonal; therefore, the condition
p_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})=1
in Eq. (
25
) holds.
Moreover, the condition
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})<1
in Eq. (
25
) holds due to Eq. (

).
Thus, the ensemble
\mathcal{E}
in Example

can be used to construct a data-hiding scheme that hides one classical bit.

Now, we provide another example of an ensemble with an arbitrary number of states in multidimensional bipartite quantum systems.

Example 2
.

For positive integers
d
,
m
, and
n
with
d\geqslant 2
and
2^{m-1}<n\leqslant 2^{m}
,
let us consider the
d^{m}\otimes d^{m}

\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
consisting of
m
-fold tensor products of
d\otimes d
quantum states
(\mathbbm{1}\pm\mathcal{F})/(d^{2}\pm d)

\eta_{i}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big[d^{2}+(-1)^{b_{k}(i)}d\big],~\rho_{i}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\mathbbm{1}+(-1)^{b_{k}(i)}\mathcal{F}}{d^{2}+(-1)^{b_{k}(i)}d},

where

is the
k
th digit in the

-digit binary representation of

\sum_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}(i)2^{k-1}=i,

(37)

\mathcal{F}
is the flip operator in a two-qudit system,

\mathcal{F}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{d-1}\ket{ij}\!\bra{ji},~

(38)

and
\mathcal{N}

\mathcal{N}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\prod_{k=1}^{m}\big[d^{2}+(-1)^{b_{k}(i)}d\big].

Note that
(\mathbbm{1}+\mathcal{F})/(d^{2}+d)

(\mathbbm{1}-\mathcal{F})/(d^{2}-d)

It is straightforward to verify

\displaystyle(\mathbbm{1}+\mathcal{F})^{\PT}=s_{0}^{(0)}\Pi_{0}+s_{1}^{(0)}\Pi_{1},~s_{0}^{(0)}=1+d,~s_{1}^{(0)}=1,

\displaystyle(\mathbbm{1}-\mathcal{F})^{\PT}=s_{0}^{(1)}\Pi_{0}+s_{1}^{(1)}\Pi_{1},~s_{0}^{(1)}=1-d,~s_{1}^{(1)}=1,

where
\Pi_{0}

V
Discussion

We considered bipartite quantum state discrimination and established a relation between a nonlocal quantum state ensemble and quantum data-hiding processing.
Using the bound
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
on local minimum-error discrimination of bipartite quantum states, we provided a sufficient condition for a bipartite orthogonal quantum state ensemble that can be used to construct a data-hiding scheme to make classical data globally but not locally accessible, in which the data is perfectly hidden asymptotically (Corollary
1
).
Our results were illustrated with examples from multidimensional bipartite quantum systems (Examples
1
and
2
).

For any integers

,
L\geqslant 1

satisfying condition (
25
), let us consider the
n
bipartite quantum states
\rho_{0}^{(L)},\ldots,\rho_{n-1}^{(L)}
in Eq. (

).

To hide
n
-ary classical data
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, the hider sends the state
\rho_{x}^{(L)}
to Alice and Bob.

, which is equivalent to discrimination of the states
\rho_{0}^{(L)},\ldots,\rho_{n-1}^{(L)}
with the same prior probability

.

In the data-hiding scheme presented in Figs.

and
2
, the hidden data

is independent of the prepared state
\rho_{y}^{(L)}
from the ensemble
\mathcal{E}^{(L)}
before the information

is broadcast to Alice and Bob.
On the other hand, the data-hiding scheme presented in Fig.

uses only the states
\rho_{i}^{(L)}
without the corresponding probabilities
\eta_{i}^{(L)}
; the hidden data
x
is directly encoded into
\rho_{x}^{(L)}
.
Because Alice and Bob have no information about the hidden data

, the preparation of
\rho_{i}^{(L)}
is completely random for Alice and Bob. Thus, the discrimination of the ensemble
\{\tfrac{1}{n},\rho_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}

From the argument before Eq. (
29
), the condition

in Eq. (

) means that the states
\rho_{0}^{(L)},\ldots,\rho_{n-1}^{(L)}
are mutually orthogonal.
Thus, we have

p_{\rm G}(\{\tfrac{1}{n},\rho_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1})=1.

(51)

In other words, the data

On the other hand, from Theorem
5

In other words, the globally accessible data

n

can be used to construct a data-hiding scheme that hides
n

Our results tell us that any bipartite orthogonal quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}
satisfying
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})<\frac{2}{n}
is useful in quantum data hiding.
Therefore, it is natural to ask whether
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})<\frac{2}{n}
is also necessary for
\mathcal{E}
to be used in quantum data hiding.
This question is related to the research on the operational meaning of
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})
in terms of quantum data hiding.

The results as well as the method of our paper can also be applied to construct a secure information network by extending them to multiparty quantum systems with more than two players.
In particular, it would be interesting in the future to investigate
(m,k)
-threshold
m
-player data-hiding schemes in which the hidden classical data can be recovered if
k
or more players collaborate.

Theorem 5
.

For a bipartite quantum state ensemble
\mathcal{E}=\{\eta_{i},\rho_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
with

q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})<\frac{2}{n}
,

\lim_{L\rightarrow\infty}p_{\rm L}(\{\tfrac{1}{n},\rho_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1})=\frac{1}{n}.

(79)

Proof.

For the quantum states
\rho_{0}^{(L)},\ldots,\rho_{n-1}^{(L)}
in Eq. (
18
), we will show that

p_{\rm L}(\{\tfrac{1}{n},\rho_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1})\leqslant\frac{1}{n}+\frac{(n-1)(n^{2}-n+2)}{2n}\big[nq_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})-1\big]^{L}.

(80)

Because
q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})<\frac{2}{n}
,
the right-hand side of inequality (
80
) converges to
\tfrac{1}{n}
as
L
gets larger.
Moreover,
p_{\rm L}(\{\tfrac{1}{n},\rho_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1})
is bounded below by
\frac{1}{n}
from inequality (
6
). Therefore, inequality (
80
) leads us to
Eq. (
79
).

Let
\mathcal{M}=\{M_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
be a LOCC measurement realizing
p_{\rm L}(\{\tfrac{1}{n},\rho_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1})
.
From the definition of
p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})
in Eq. (
4
), we have

p_{\rm L}(\{\tfrac{1}{n},\rho_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1})-p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{n}\Tr(\rho_{i}^{(L)}M_{i})-p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\leqslant\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big(\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big)\Tr(\rho_{i}^{(L)}M_{i}).

(81)

Since
0\leqslant\Tr(\rho_{i}^{(L)}M_{i})\leqslant 1
for any
i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
and
r\leqslant\frac{1}{2}(|r|+r)
for any real number
r
, we have

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big(\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big)\Tr(\rho_{i}^{(L)}M_{i})\leqslant\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2}\Bigg[\Big|\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big|+\Big(\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big)\Bigg]=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big|\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big|,

(82)

where the last equality holds from
\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\eta_{i}^{(L)}=1
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle p_{\rm L}(\{\tfrac{1}{n},\rho_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big|\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big|

(83)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big|\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}\big[nq_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})-1\big]^{L}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big|\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big|,

where the first inequality is due to inequalities (
81
) and (
82
),
the second inequality is from inequality (
8
),
and the last inequality is from Theorem
4
.

To obtain an upper bound of
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}|\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}|
, let us first consider

\max\{\eta_{0}^{(L)},\ldots,\eta_{n-1}^{(L)}\}\leqslant p_{\rm L}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\leqslant q_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E}^{(L)})\leqslant\frac{1}{n}+\frac{n-1}{n}\big[nq_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})-1\big]^{L},

(84)

where the first inequality is from inequality (
6
),
the second inequality is due to inequality (
8
),
and the last inequality is from Theorem
4
.
Moreover, we have

\min\{\eta_{0}^{(L)},\ldots,\eta_{n-1}^{(L)}\}\geqslant 1-(n-1)\max\{\eta_{0}^{(L)},\ldots,\eta_{n-1}^{(L)}\}\geqslant\frac{1}{n}-\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n}\big[nq_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})-1\big]^{L},

(85)

where the first inequality is because
\{\eta_{i}^{(L)}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
is a probability distribution, and the second inequality follows from Eq. (
84
).

From inequalities (
84
) and (
85
), we have

\Big|\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big|\leqslant\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{n}\big[nq_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})-1\big]^{L}

(86)

for each
i=0,\ldots,n-1
, which implies

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big|\frac{1}{n}-\eta_{i}^{(L)}\Big|\leqslant\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}\big[nq_{\rm G}(\mathcal{E})-1\big]^{L}.

(87)

Inequalities (
83
) and (
87
) lead us to inequality (
80
).
∎

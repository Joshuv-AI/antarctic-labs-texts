𝖪 {\sf K} -orbit closures and Barbasch-Evens-Magyar varieties
Laura Escobar, Benjamin J. Wyser, Alexander Yong

Abstract
. We define the Barbasch-Evens-Magyar varieties . We show they are isomorphic to the smooth varieties defined in [D. Barbasch-S. Evens ’94] that map generically finitely to symmetric orbit closures, thereby giving resolutions of singularities in certain cases. Our definition parallels [P. Magyar ’98]’s construction of the Bott-Samelson varieties [H. C. Hansen ’73, M. Demazure ’74]. From this alternative viewpoint, one deduces a graphical description in type A A , stratification into closed subvarieties of the same kind, and determination of the torus-fixed points. Moreover, we explain how these manifolds inherit a natural symplectic structure with Hamiltonian torus action. We then express the moment polytope in terms of the moment polytope of a Bott-Samelson variety.

This paper introduces and initiates our study of the

Just as P. Magyar

In type

Let

Let

Let

Given

is the standard parabolic subgroup
of
{\sf G}
corresponding to
I

(1)

{\sf P}_{I}={\sf B}\cup\left(\bigcup_{w\in W_{I}}{\sf B}w{\sf B}\right),

where

is the set of

is a

{\sf P}_{i}

As described in
[
40
, Section 3.10]

Richardson-Springer monoid

{\mathcal{M}}({\sf W})

of
{\sf W}

, together with the braid relations of
\sf W
. As a set, this monoid may be canonically identified with

{\sf W}
, with the ordinary product on
{\sf W}
being replaced by the
Demazure product

s_{i}\star w=\begin{cases}s_{i}w&\text{ if $\ell(s_{i}w)>\ell(w)$,}\\
w&\text{ otherwise,}\end{cases}

where

denotes ordinary Coxeter length, and where the juxtaposition
s_{i}w
denotes the ordinary product in
{\sf W}
.
A
word

Q=(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{N})
is an ordered tuple of numbers from

\{1,2,\ldots,r\}

Let

\text{Dem}(Q):=s_{j_{1}}\star s_{j_{2}}\star\ldots\star s_{j_{N}}
.
If
\text{Dem}(Q)=w
, then

is a
Demazure word
for
w
.

Consider the natural projection

\pi_{i}:{\sf G}/{\sf B}\rightarrow{\sf G}/{\sf P}_{i}
.
Given a
{\sf K}
-orbit closure
Y
on
{\sf G}/{\sf B}
and a simple reflection

{\sf K}
-orbit closure.
This extends to an
{\mathcal{M}}({\sf W})
-action on the set of
{\sf K}
-orbit closures: given a Demazure word

Q=(s_{j_{1}},\ldots,s_{j_{N}})
for
w
, define

The
{\sf K}
-orbit closure

is independent of the choice of Demazure word
Q
for
w

[
40
, Section 4.7]
.

The

on the set of
{\sf K}
-orbit closures is defined by

.
The minimal elements of this order are the

.
The following is well-known; see, e.g.,
[
7
, Proposition 2.2(i)]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Each closed orbit is isomorphic to
{\sf K}/{\sf B}^{\prime}

{\sf B}^{\prime}
is a Borel subgroup of
{\sf K}
. In particular, every closed orbit is smooth.

Remark 2.2
.

If

is disconnected, then
[
7
, Proposition 2.2(i)]
says that

is isomorphic to a finite union of flag manifolds

, where
{\sf K}^{0}
is the connected component of

{\sf B}^{\prime}
is a Borel subgroup of

Theorem 6.8

(Dimension of
{\mathcal{P}}_{Y_{0},Q}
)
.

For
({\sf G},{\sf K})=({\sf GL}_{p+q},{\sf GL}_{p}\times{\sf GL}_{q})
,

\dim({\mathcal{P}}_{Y_{0},Q})=\begin{cases}p+q-1,\ \text{ if }\ p\text{ is in }Q,\text{ and}\\
p+q-2,\ \text{ if }\ p\text{ is not in }Q.\end{cases}

Proof.

A
T
-action on a space
X
is
effective
if each element of
T
, other than the identity, moves at least one point of
X
.
In the proof of
[
9
, Corollary 27.2]
it is shown that for an effective Hamiltonian
T
-action the dimension of the corresponding moment polytope equals the dimension of the torus.
If the
T
-action is not effective it is known that it can be reduced it to an effective action with the same moment polytope. The
stabilizer
of the
T
-action is the normal subgroup

S_{T}(X):=\{t\in T\mid t\cdot x=x\text{ for all }x\in X\}.

The
T
-action on
X
reduces to the effective action of
T/S_{T}(X)
given by
tS_{T}(X)\cdot x:=t\cdot x
.
To prove the Theorem we will consider the cases in which
p
is in
Q
and when it isn’t separately.
In each case we will explicitly compute the stabilizer of the
\sf T
-action on
\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q}
.
First, note that

{\sf T}_{1}=\{{\sf t}\in{\sf T}\mid t_{1}=\ldots=t_{n}\}

acts trivially on
\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q}
and therefore it is a subgroup of
S_{\sf T}(\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q})
.

In view of Theorem
6.2
, we assume without loss of generality, that
Y_{0}=Y_{+\cdots+-\cdots-}
, i.e.

Y_{0}=\{(F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{n-1})\in{\sf G}/{\sf B}\mid F_{p}=E_{p}\}.

Consider the projection
\varphi_{0}:\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q}\to Y_{0}
that maps a point in the configuration space to
the leftmost flag (corresponding to the leftmost border) in the diagram.
Since the projection is
\sf T
-equivariant,
S_{\sf T}(\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q})
is a subgroup of
S_{\sf T}(Y_{0})
.
Let us first describe
S_{\sf T}(Y_{0})
.
Note that

{\sf T}_{2}=\{{\sf t}\in{\sf T}\mid t_{1}=\ldots=t_{p},\ t_{p+1}=\ldots=t_{n}\}

acts trivially on
Y_{0}
.
Furthermore, since
Y_{0}
is isomorphic to
{\sf GL}_{p}/{\sf B}\times{\sf GL}_{q}/{\sf B}
then
S_{\sf T}(Y_{0})
is isomorphic to
S_{\sf T}({\sf GL}_{p}/{\sf B})\times S_{\sf T}({\sf GL}_{q}/{\sf B})
.
It follows that
S_{\sf T}(Y_{0})={\sf T}_{2}
and therefore
S_{\sf T}(\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q})
is either equal to
{\sf T}_{1}
or
{\sf T}_{2}
.

First suppose that
p
is in
Q
and let
k
be such that
j_{k}=p
, where
Q=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{N})
.
It is straightforward from Section
5
that if we set

V_{k}={\rm span}\{\vec{e}_{1},\ldots,\vec{e}_{p-1},\vec{e}_{p}+\vec{e}_{p+1}\},

then the following holds:

(E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{n-1},E_{j_{1}},E_{j_{2}},\ldots E_{j_{k-1}},V_{k},E_{j_{k+1}},\ldots,E_{j_{N}})\in\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q}.

Furthermore, for
{\sf t}\in{\sf T}_{2}

\displaystyle{\sf t}\cdot V_{k}

\displaystyle={\rm span}\{\vec{e}_{1},\vec{e}_{2},\ldots,\vec{e}_{p-1},\vec{e}_{p}+t_{n}\vec{e}_{p+1}\}\neq V_{k},

so
{\sf T}_{2}
does not act trivially on
\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q}
and
S_{\sf T}(\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q})={\sf T}_{1}
.
From this and the first paragraph it follows that
\dim({\mathcal{P}}_{Y_{0},Q})=p+q-1
.

Now suppose that
p

Q
.
This implies that every vector space appearing in

(F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{n-1},V_{1},V_{2},\ldots V_{N})\in\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q}

So

acts trivially on
\mathcal{BEM}^{Y_{0},Q}
and

We conclude that

∎

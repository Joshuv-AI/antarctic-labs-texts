On the iterates of the Laguerre operator
Smiljana Jakšić, Stevan Pilipović, Nenad Teofanov, Đorđe Vučković

Abstract
. We use the iterates of the Laguerre operator to introduce Pilipović spaces on positive orthants. It is shown that such spaces coincide with G − G- type spaces g α α ​ ( ℝ + d ) g_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}) and G α α ​ ( ℝ + d ) G_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}) , when α > 1 \alpha>1 , and α ≥ 1 \alpha\geq 1 , respectively. However, in contrast to G G -type spaces, Pilipović spaces are nontrivial below the critical index α = 1 \alpha=1 . We also remark that there is a natural isomorphism between subspaces of Pilipović spaces on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} consisting of even functions, and Pilipović spaces on positive orthants.

1.
Introduction

Counterparts of Schwartz functions and of Gelfand-Shilov type functions on
(0,\infty)
were studied in
[
1
,
2
]
. These results were thereafter generalized in
[
6
]
, were the test spaces of ultradifferentiable functions of Roumieu and Beurling type, denoted by
G_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, and
g_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
,
\alpha\geq 1
, respectively, are introduced and characterized via decay properties of their coefficients in Laguerre expansions.

Toft in
[
10
]
studied Pilipović spaces
\mathbfcal S_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\alpha>0
, by considering the iterates of the linear Harmonic oscillator. These spaces are non trivial even if
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, whereas when
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
they coincide with the Gelfand-Shilov spaces usually denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, see e.g.
[
4
]
.
Recently, Toft, Bhimani and Manna demonstrated that Pilipović spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and their distribution spaces naturally appear in the study of well-posednedness of certain Schrödinger equations, which are ill-posed in the class of Gelfand-Shilov type distributions, see
[
11
]
.

In this report we use the iterates of the Laguerre operator to introduce Pilipović spaces on positive orthants, and relate them with with

G-
type spaces
g_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and
G_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
of ultradifferentiable functions when
\alpha>1
, and
\alpha\geq 1
, respectively.

The proofs will be given in our forthcoming contribution
[
8
]
, where we also study natural isomorphisms between Pilipović spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
consisting of even functions and Pilipović spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
. Related questions in the context of
G
-type spaces were considered in
[
7
]
.

1.1.
Notation

By
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
the sets of positive
integers, integers, real and complex numbers, respectively;

\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}=(\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})^{d}
,

\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,\infty)
,
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}=(0,\infty)^{d}
and
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}=[0,\infty)^{d}
.
The standard multi-index
notation will be used: for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(or
x\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
),
|\alpha|:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}
,
x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}...x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
, and
\partial^{\alpha}=\partial^{\alpha_{1}}/{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}}...\partial^{\alpha_{d}}/\partial x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
. If
x,\gamma\in\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
, then
x^{\gamma}=x_{1}^{\gamma_{1}}...x_{d}^{\gamma_{d}}
, and if

x_{j}=0
and
\gamma_{j}=0
,
j=1,\dots,d
, we use the convention
0^{0}=1
.

We use the standard notation for common spaces of functions, distributions, and sequences.
For example,
\ell_{p}
,
p\geq 1
, denotes the Banach space of Lebesgue sequences, endowed with the usual norm:

\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}\in\ell_{p}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\|a_{n}\|_{\ell^{p}}=(\sum_{n}|a_{n}|^{p})^{1/p}<\infty,

and with the usual modification when
p=\infty.

A measurable function belongs to the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
if

\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}:=(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|f(x)|^{2}dx)^{1/2}<\infty,

and the scalar product is given in the usual way.
By
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
we denote the space of all smooth functions on positive orthant,
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
such that all of its derivatives
D^{p}f
,
p\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
, extend to continuous functions on

\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
, and

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}x^{k}|D^{p}f(x)|<\infty\;,\qquad k,p\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}.

With this system of seminorms
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
becomes an
(F)
-space, see e.g.
[
5
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Sequence spaces

We use the notation from
[
10
]
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
h>1
, and put
\vartheta_{h,\alpha}(n)=e^{h|n|^{1/(2\alpha)}},

n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
. By
\ell^{p}_{[\vartheta_{h,\alpha}]}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
,
p\geq 1
,
we denote the space of all complex sequences
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}

for which

(1)

\|\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}}\|_{\ell^{p}_{[\vartheta_{h,\alpha}]}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})}:=\|\{|a_{n}|\cdot\vartheta_{h,\alpha}(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}\|_{l^{p}}<\infty.

Equipped with this norm
\ell^{p}_{[\vartheta_{h,\alpha}(n)}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
becomes a Banach space, with the usual modification when
p=\infty
.

We also introduce

\ell_{\alpha}(\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0})=\bigcup_{h>0}\ell^{\infty}_{[\vartheta_{h,\alpha}]}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}),\qquad\ell_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})=\bigcap_{h>0}\ell^{\infty}_{[\vartheta_{h,\alpha}]}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}),

and endow these locally convex spaces with inductive and projective limit topology, respectively.

For consistency, if
\alpha=0
, then
\ell_{0}(\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0})
denotes the space of all sequences

\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}}
such that
a_{n}\not=0
for only finitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, and
\ell_{0,0}(\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0})=\{0\}.

Observe that different choices of
p\in[1,\infty]
give rise to equivalent norms in (
1
), cf

[
10
]
. In particular, if

\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}}\in\ell^{\infty}_{[\vartheta_{h_{1},\alpha}]}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
then there exists
h_{1}>0
and
C_{1}>0
such that

\|\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}\}}\|_{\ell^{2}_{[\vartheta_{h_{1},\alpha}]}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})}=\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}|a_{n}|^{2}\vartheta_{h_{1},\alpha}(n)\Big)^{1/2}\leq C_{1}\|\{a_{n}\cdot\vartheta_{h,\alpha}(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}\|_{l^{\infty}},

and conversely,
if

\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}}\in\ell^{2}_{[\vartheta_{h,\alpha}]}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})

then there exist
C_{2},h_{2}>0
such that

\|\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}^{d}_{0}\}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{[\vartheta_{h_{2},\alpha}]}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})}\leq C_{2}\|\{a_{n}\cdot\vartheta_{h,\alpha}(n)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}\|_{l^{2}}.

2.2.
Laguerre functions and Laguerre operator

For
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\gamma\geq 0
, the
j
-th Laguerre
polynomial of order
\gamma
is defined by

L_{j}^{\gamma}(x)=\frac{x^{-\gamma}e^{x}}{j!}\frac{d^{j}}{dt^{j}}(e^{-x}x^{\gamma+j}),\qquad x\geq 0.

The
j
-th Laguerre function is then given by

l_{j}(x)=L_{j}(x)e^{-x/2}
,
x\geq 0
.
The
d
-dimensional Laguerre polynomials and Laguerre
functions, are given by tensor products

L_{n}(x)=\prod_{l=1}^{d}L_{n_{l}}(x_{l})
, and

l_{n}(x)=\prod_{l=1}^{d}l_{n_{l}}(x_{l})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
,
respectively.
We are interested in the case
\gamma=0
, and we write
L_{n}
and
l_{n}
instead of
L_{n}^{0}
and
\l_{n}^{0}
, respectively.

Laguerre functions
\{l_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}
form an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, cf. e.g.
[
9
,
12
]
. Thus every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
can be expressed via its Laguerre series expansion,
and by
a_{n}(f)

we denote the corresponding Laguerre coefficients:

a_{n}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}f(x)l_{n}(x)dx,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}.

Similarly as it is done in
[
10
]
,
we may consider the spaces of formal Laguerre series expansions

f=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}a_{n}l_{n}
that correspond to the sequence
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
from
\ell_{\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
or
\ell_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
,
\alpha\geq 0
.
These spaces will be denoted by
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
and
\mathcal{G}_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
, respectively, and the natural mapping

(2)

T:\ell_{\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})\quad(T:\ell_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})\rightarrow\mathcal{G}_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})),\\
T(\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}a_{n}l_{n}

induces topology on the target space.

Next we introduce the Laguerre operator:

E=-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Big(x_{j}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{x}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\Big).

Then the Laguerre functions are the eigenfunctions of
E
:

E(l_{n}(x))=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{d}n_{j}\Big)\cdot l_{n}(x)=|n|\cdot l_{n}(x),\qquad x>0,

see
[
3
, (11), p.188]
, and for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
we have

(3)

E^{N}(l_{n}(x))=|n|^{N}l_{n}(x).

3.
Pilipović spaces on positive orthants

Before we define spaces of ultradifferentiable functions in terms of the iterates of the Laguerre operator
we give a simple but useful lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
u
be a smooth and square integrable function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}
, i.e.

u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
. If
E^{N}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
then
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d}).

The proof follows from (
3
) and the characterization of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
via coefficients in Laguerre expansions of its elements.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
h>0
, and
\alpha>0
. Then a smooth functions
f
belongs to the space
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha,h}_{\alpha,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, if

(4)

\eta_{h}^{\alpha}(f):=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\|E^{N}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}}{h^{|N|}N!^{\alpha}}<\infty.

From Lemma
3.1
it immediately follows that
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha,h}_{\alpha,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
.
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha,h}_{\alpha,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
is a Banach space with the norm
\eta_{h}^{\alpha}
, and clearly, if
0<\alpha<\beta
, then
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha,h}_{\alpha,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
is continuously injected into
\mathbf{G}^{\beta,h}_{\beta,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
.

The Laguerre functions
l_{p}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
, satisfy (
4
) for every
h>0
and
\alpha>0
,
since

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\|E^{N}l_{p}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}}{h^{N}N!^{\alpha}}=\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{(\frac{|p|}{h})^{N}}{N!^{\alpha}}<\infty.

It follows that
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha,h}_{\alpha,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
are non-trivial spaces since they contain at least finite linear combinations of the Laguerre functions.

We notice that even if
\alpha=0
, and if
h>1
, then
l_{p}\in\mathbf{G}^{0,h}_{0,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})

for all
p\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
such that
|p|\leq h
.

Now we are ready to introduce Pilipović spaces on positive orthants.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
. Then the Pilipović spaces on positive orthant
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}
,
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and
\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
are defined to be the union, respectively the intersection of
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha,h}_{\alpha,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, with respect to
h>0
:

\mathbf{G}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=\bigcup_{h>0}\mathbf{G}^{\alpha,h}_{\alpha,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}),\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=\bigcap_{h>0}\mathbf{G}^{\alpha,h}_{\alpha,h}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}).

The spaces
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and
\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
are
endowed with projective and inductive limit topologies, respectively.
Thus,
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and
\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
are
(F)
-spaces continuously injected into
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, and if
0<\alpha<\beta
we have

(5)

\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\hookrightarrow\mathbf{G}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})\hookrightarrow\mathbf{g}^{\beta}_{\beta}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{G}^{\beta}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}),

where
\hookrightarrow
denotes dense and continuous inclusion.

Next we state our main result.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d})
, and let

\{a_{n}(f)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}
be the sequence of Laguerre coefficients of
f
. Then the following is true:

(i)

f\in\mathbf{G}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
if and only if

\{a_{n}(f)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}\in\ell_{\alpha/2}(\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0})

(ii)

f\in\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
if and only if

\{a_{n}(f)\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}\in\ell_{0,\alpha/2}(\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0})
.

The proof will be given in a separate contribution,
[
8
]

By Theorem
3.4
it follows that
\mathbf{G}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and
\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=\mathcal{G}_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
where

\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and
\mathcal{G}_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
are intriduced in subsection
2.2
.
Moreover, the mapping
T:{\ell_{\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
(resp.
T:{\ell_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})}\rightarrow\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
) is a well defined topological isomorphism, see (
2
) for the definition of
T
.

3.1.
G
-type spaces as Pilipović spaces

Following
[
6
]
, we define
G
-type spaces of ultradifferentiable functions
G^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and
g^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
as follows:

Let
A>0
. By
g^{\alpha,A}_{\alpha,A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, we denote the
space of all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{p,k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}\frac{\|x^{(p+k)/2}\partial^{p}f(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}}{A^{|p+k|}k^{(\alpha/2)k}p^{(\alpha/2)p}}<\infty.

With the following seminorms

\displaystyle\sigma_{A,j}^{\alpha,\alpha}(f)=\sup_{p,k\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}}\frac{\|x^{(p+k)/2}\partial^{p}f(x)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})}}{A^{|p+k|}k^{(\alpha/2)k}p^{(\beta/2)p}}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}|p|\leq j\\
|k|\leq j\end{subarray}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}|x^{k}D^{p}f(x)|,\,\,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

it becomes an
(F)
-space.

The spaces
G^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and

g^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
are then defined as union, respectively intersection of

g^{\alpha,A}_{\alpha,A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
with respect to
A
:

(6)

G^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=\bigcup_{A>0}g^{\alpha,A}_{\alpha,A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}),\qquad g^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=\bigcap_{A>0}g^{\alpha,A}_{\alpha,A}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}).

Then
G^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
and

g^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
are endowed with inductive, respectively projective limit topology.

We note that
G^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
is non-trivial when
\alpha\geq 1
, and

g^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
is non-trivial when
\alpha>1
.

Next, we rephrase and summarize results from
[
2
, Theorem 3.6]
and
[
6
, Theorem 5.7]
as follows:

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
.
Then the following is true.

(i)

Let
\alpha\geq 1
. If
f\in G_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, and

\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
is the sequence of its Laguerre coefficients,
then
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\in\ell_{\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
.

Conversely if
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\in\ell_{\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
,
then there exists
f\in G_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})

such that
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
is the sequence of its Laguerre coefficients.

(ii)

Let
\alpha>1
. If
f\in g_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, and

\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
is the sequence of its Laguerre coefficients,
then

\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\in\ell_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
.

Conversely if
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\in\ell_{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d})
,
then there exists
f\in g_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})

such that
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
is the sequence of its Laguerre coefficients.

By Theorems
3.5
and
3.4
we have the following:

Corollary 3.6
.

G
-type spaces coincide with Pilipović spaces whenever the former are non-trivial. More precisely,

\mathbf{G}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=G_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, when
\alpha\geq 1
and

\mathbf{g}^{\alpha}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})=g_{\alpha}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
, when
\alpha>1
.

Therefore,
G
-type spaces can be also defined by the iterates of the Laguerre operator, which gives a new insight
into their structure.

It should be noticed that
\mathbf{g}_{1}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+})
is of a particular interest, since
it is the largest Pilipović space on orthant which is not a
G
-type space.

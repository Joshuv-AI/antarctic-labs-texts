Abstract
Herschend-Liu-Nakaoka introduced the notion of n n -exangulated categories. It is not only a higher dimensional analogue of extriangulated categories defined by Nakaoka-Palu, but also gives a simultaneous generalization of n n -exact categories and ( n + 2 ) (n+2) -angulated categories. In this article, we give an n n -exangulated version of Auslander’s defect and Auslander-Reiten duality formula. Moreover, we also give a classification of substructures (=closed subbifunctors) of a given skeletally small n n -exangulated category by using the category of defects. Keywords: n n -exangulated categories; defects; Auslander’s defect formula; substructures. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 18G80; 18E10; 18E05.



1
Introduction

The notion of extriangulated categories was introduced in
[
NP
]
, which can be viewed
as a simultaneous generalization of exact categories and triangulated categories.
The data of such a category is a triplet
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
, where
\mathscr{C}
is an additive category,
\mathbb{E}:\mathscr{C}^{\rm op}\times\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
is an additive bifunctor and
\mathfrak{s}
assigns to each
\delta\in\mathbb{E}(C,A)
a class of
3
-term sequences with end terms
A
and
C
such that certain axioms hold. Recently, Herschend-Liu-Nakaoka
[
HLN
]
introduced an
n
-analogue of this notion called
n
-exangulated category. Such a category is a similar triplet
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
, with the main distinction being that the
3
-term sequences mentioned above are replaced by
(n+2)
-term sequences. It should be noted that the case
n=1
corresponds to extriangulated categories. As typical examples we have that
n
-exact and
(n+2)
-angulated categories are
n
-exangulated, see
[
HLN
, Propositions 4.34 and 4.5]
. However,
there are some other examples of
n
-exangulated categories which are neither
n
-exact nor
(n+2)
-angulated, see
[
HLN
, Remark 4.39]
and
[
LZ
, Remark 4.5]
.

The notion of defects over abelian categories was introduced by Auslander in
[
A1
]
, see also

[
ARS
, IV. 4]
. Recently, this notion was generalized by Jasso-Kvamme
[
JK
]
over
n
-exact categories and by Ogawa
[
O
]
over extriangulated categories. In this paper, we define defects over
n
-exangulated categories. More precisely,
let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an
n
-exangulated category.
If

X^{0}\xrightarrow{d_{X}^{0}}X^{1}\xrightarrow{d_{X}^{1}}\cdots\xrightarrow{}X^{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{X}^{n-1}}X^{n}\xrightarrow{d_{X}^{n}}X^{n+1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\dashrightarrow}},

is a distinguished
n
-exangle in
\mathscr{C}
, then the contravariant defect of
\delta
, denoted by
\widetilde{\delta}
, is defined by

\mathscr{C}(-,X^{n})\xrightarrow{\mathscr{C}(-,\ d_{X}^{n})}\mathscr{C}(-,X^{n+1})\rightarrow\widetilde{\delta}\rightarrow 0

the exact sequence of the functors. The covariant defect of
\delta
, denoted by
\check{\delta}
, is defined dually.

One of interesting applications of defects, due to Jasso-Kvamme
[
JK
, Theorem 3.8]
, is that one can get higher version of Auslander’s defect formula using a minor modification of Krause’s proof of the classical formula
[
K
]
. As an immediate consequence, one can obtain the higher Auslander-Reiten duality formula; see
[
JK
, Corollary 3.9]
for instance. Recall that Auslander’s defect formula appeared as
[
A2
, Theorem III.4.1]
for the first time and the Auslander-Reiten duality formula appeared at about the same time as Proposition 3.1 in Auslander and Reiten’s classical paper on the existence of almost split sequences
[
AR2
]
. Recently, some analogues of the above formulas can be seen in
[
I
,
J
,
L1
,
L2
]
. Our first main result is the following, which provides a higher counterpart of Auslander’s defect and Auslander-Reiten duality formula over
n
-exangulated categories.

Theorem 1.1
.

(see Theorem
3.12
and Corollary
3.13
for details)

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E},\mathfrak{s})
be an
n
-exangulated category with enough projectives and enough injectives. Assume that
\mathscr{C}
is a dualizing
k
-variety. Then there exists an equivalence
\tau\colon\underline{\mathscr{C}}\rightarrow\overline{\mathscr{C}}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

D\check{\delta}=\widetilde{\delta}\tau^{-1},

D\widetilde{\delta}=\check{\delta}\tau
;

(2)

D\mathbb{E}(-,X)\cong\underline{\mathscr{C}}(\tau^{-1}X,-),

D\mathbb{E}(X,-)\cong\overline{\mathscr{C}}(-,\tau X),

where
\underline{\mathscr{C}}
(resp.
\overline{\mathscr{C}}
) is the projectively (resp. injectively) stable category of
\mathscr{C}
and
D
is the
k
-dual.

There are two remarks on Theorem
1.1
: (1) It generalizes Jasso-Kvamme’s results on
the category of finitely generated left
\Lambda
-modules over an Artin algebra
\Lambda
and Lin’s results on an exact category and is new for
n
-cluster tilting subcategories of an extriangulated category (see Corollary

(n+2)
-angulated category has Auslander-Reiten
(n+2)
-angles (see Corollary

Another interesting application of defects, due to Enomoto
[
E
]
, is that one can give a classification of substructures of a given skeletally small extriangulated category. We show that this result has a higher counterpart:

Theorem 1.2
.

(see Theorem

Let

be a skeletally small

the full subcategory of finitely presented functors isomorphic to contravariant defects.
Then the map

gives an isomorphism of the following posets, where the poset structures are given by inclusion.

(1)

The poset of closed subbifunctors of
\mathbb{E}
;

(2)

{\rm def}\mathbb{E}

Note that, in
[

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x}
\textstyle{B\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we first review some definitions and basic facts.

2.1 Morphism categories.
Let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category. The
morphism category
of
\mathscr{C}
is the category
{\rm Mor}\mathscr{C}
defined by the following data. The objects of
{\rm Mor}\mathscr{C}
are all the morphisms
f:X\rightarrow Y
in
\mathscr{C}
. The morphism from
f:X\rightarrow Y
to
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
are pairs
(a,b)
where
a:X\rightarrow X^{\prime}
and
b:Y\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
satisfies
bf=f^{\prime}a
. The composition of morphisms is componentwise.

2.2 Functor categories.
For an additive category
\mathscr{C}
, a
right
\mathscr{C}
-module
is defined to be a contravariant functor
\mathscr{C}^{op}\rightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
and a morphism between right
\mathscr{C}
-modules is just a natural transformation of functors. Thus we define an abelian category
{\rm Mod}\mathscr{C}
. We denote by
{\rm mod}\mathscr{C}
the full subcategory of finitely presented
\mathscr{C}
-module in
{\rm Mod}\mathscr{C}
and by
\mathrm{proj}\text{-}\mathscr{C}
(resp.
\mathrm{inj}\text{-}\mathscr{C}
) the full subcategory of
{\rm mod}\mathscr{C}
consisting of
projective (resp. injective) objects. For an additive category
\mathscr{C}
, the subcategory
{\rm mod}\mathscr{C}
is not always abelian since it is not necessarily closed under kernels. Indeed, we have following well-known result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category. Then the full subcategory
{\rm mod}\mathscr{C}
is an exact abelian subcategory in
{\rm Mod}\mathscr{C}
if and only if the category
\mathscr{C}
admits weak-kernels.

2.3 Quotient categories.
Let
\mathscr{C}
be an additive category and
\mathcal{J}
an ideal of
\mathscr{C}
. The
quotient category

\mathscr{C}/\mathcal{J}
has the same objects as
\mathscr{C}
, and the morphisms in
\mathscr{C}/\mathcal{J}
are the residue classes of homomorphisms in
\mathscr{C}
modulo
\mathcal{J}
. Suppose that
\mathscr{X}
is a full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
, we denote by
[\mathscr{X}]
the ideal of
\mathscr{C}
given by all morphisms factors through an object in
\mathscr{X}
, thus we have a quotient category
\mathscr{C}/[\mathscr{X}]
.

Let
F:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
be an additive functor. An object
A
of
\mathscr{C}
is called a
kernel object
for
F
provided
F(A)=0
. The functor
F:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
will be said to be
objective
provided any morphism
f:A\rightarrow A^{\prime}
with
F(f)=0
factors through a kernel object for
F
, see
[
RZ
, Appendix]
. If
F:\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
is a full, dense, objective functor, there is an equivalence
\mathscr{C}/[{\rm Ker}F]\cong\mathcal{D}
, where
{\rm Ker}F
is the full subcategory of
\mathscr{C}
formed by
X
with
F(X)=0
.

Definition 2.3
.

[
HLN
, Definition 2.7]

be the category of complexes in
\mathscr{C}
. As its full subcategory, define

to be the category of complexes in

whose components are zero in the degrees outside of

Remark 2.6
.

[
HLN
, Remark 2.18]

Let

, Definition 4.2]
. An
n
-exact category
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{X})
can be regarded as
n
-exangulated category as follows: For
A,C\in\mathscr{C}
, let
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}}(C,A)
be the subclass of
\Lambda^{n+2}_{(A,C)}
consisting of all
[X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}]
such that
[X^{\hbox{\boldmath$\cdot$}}]\in\mathscr{X}
, where
\Lambda^{n+2}_{(A,C)}
denote the class of all homotopy equivalence class of
n
-exact sequences in
\mathbf{C}^{n+2}_{(A,C)}
. Then
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}}(C,A)
is an abelian group, hence there is a biadditive functor
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}}:\mathscr{C}^{\rm op}\times\mathscr{C}\rightarrow\mathsf{Ab}
by
[
HLN
, Proposition 4.32]
. Define the realization
\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{X}}(\delta)
of
\delta\in\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}}(C,A)
to be itself, then
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}},\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{X}})
is an
n
-exangulated category, see
[
HLN
, Proposition 4.36]
.

Now we have the following result on closed subbifunctors and
n
-exact structures.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{X})
be a skeletally small
n
-exact category. Then the map
\mathscr{Y}\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{Y}}
gives an isomorphism of posets between two posets, where poset structures are given by inclusion.

(1)
The poset of
n
-exact structures which is contained in
\mathscr{X}
.

(2)
The poset of closed subbifunctors of
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}}
.

Proof.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}},\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{X}})
be an
n
-exangulated category coming from an
n
-exact category
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{X})
as above. Then every
\mathfrak{s}|_{\mathscr{X}}
-inflation (resp.
\mathfrak{s}|_{\mathscr{X}}
-deflation) is a monomorphism (resp. an epimorphism). It follows from
[
HLN
, Proposition 4.23]
that
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{X})
satisfies condition (EI), see
[
HLN
, Definition 4.21]
. Assume that
\mathscr{Y}
is an
n
-exact structure on
\mathscr{C}
with
\mathscr{Y}\subseteq\mathscr{X}
, then we have an
n
-exangulated category
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{Y}},\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{Y}})
corresponding to
n
-exact structure
\mathscr{Y}
. Hence
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{Y}}
is a closed subbifunctor of
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}}
by Proposition
4.4
, thus we obtain a map
\mathscr{Y}\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{Y}}
from (1) to (2). It is easy to show that this map is order-preserving, and includes an embedding of posets (1) into (2).

Next we claim that every closed subbifunctor
\mathbb{F}
of
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}}
is of the form
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{Y}}
for some
n
-exact structure
\mathscr{Y}
on
\mathscr{C}
satisfying
\mathscr{Y}\subseteq\mathscr{X}
.
Assume that
\mathbb{F}
is a closed subbifunctor of
\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}}
, then
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{F},\mathfrak{s}|_{\mathbb{F}})
is an
n
-exangulated category by Proposition
4.4
. Let
\mathscr{Y}
denote the class of all
\mathfrak{s}|_{\mathbb{F}}
-conflation. It is easy to see that
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{Y})
satisfies condition (EI) by
[
HLN
, Definition 4.21]
. Since every
\mathfrak{s}|_{\mathbb{F}}
-inflation (resp.
\mathfrak{s}|_{\mathbb{F}}
-deflation) is an
\mathfrak{s}|_{\mathscr{X}}
-inflation (resp.
\mathfrak{s}|_{\mathscr{X}}
-deflation), it is a monomorphism (resp. an epimorphism). Therefore,
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{Y})
is an
n
-exact category by
[
HLN
, Propositions 4.37 and 4.23]
. Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{Y}},\mathfrak{s}_{\mathscr{Y}})
be an
n
-exangulated category corresponding to
n
-exact structure
\mathscr{Y}
. It is easy to see that
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{Y}}
, as desired.
∎

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.6
and Lemma
4.10
, we have the following classification of
n
-exact substructures of a given skeletally small
n
-exact category.

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},\mathscr{X})
be a skeletally small
n
-exact category. Then there exists an isomorphism of posets between two posets, where poset structures are given by inclusion.

(1)
The poset of
n
-exact structures which is contained in
\mathscr{X}
.

(2)
The poset of Serre subcategories of
{\rm def}\mathbb{E}_{\mathscr{X}}
.

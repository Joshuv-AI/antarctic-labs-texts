Abstract
. We defined the hyperelliptic Heisenberg algebra as the Heisenberg subalgebra of a hyperlliptic Krichever-Novikov algebra. Then we gave a explicit irreducibility criteria for φ \varphi -Verma modules for these algebras.



Introduction

If
R
is an commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra and
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple Lie
\mathbb{C}
-algebra, it is well known from the work of Kassel and Loday (see
[
KL82
]
, and
[
Kas84
]
) that the universal central extension
\mathscr{G}
of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes R
is the vector space
(\mathfrak{g}\otimes R)\oplus\Omega^{R}_{1}/dR
where
\Omega^{R}_{1}/dR
is the space of Kähler differentials module exact forms. The space
\mathscr{G}
is made into a Lie algebra by defining

\displaystyle{[x\otimes f,y\otimes g]=[xy]+(x,y)\overline{fdg}},

\displaystyle[x\otimes f,\omega]=0

for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
,
f,g\in R
,
\omega\in\Omega_{R}^{1}/dR
,
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the Killing form on
\mathfrak{g}
, and
\bar{a}
denotes the image of
a\in\Omega_{R}^{1}
in the quotient
\Omega_{R}^{1}/dR
.

Consider the Riemann sphere
\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
and fix a set composed by
n>2
distinct points on this Riemann sphere
P=\{p_{1},p_{2},...,p_{n}\}
. Bremner gave
\mathscr{G}
type algebras the name
n
point algebras
when
R
is the ring of rational functions with poles allowed only at
P
. The
n
point Lie algebras are examples of Krichever-Novikov algebras.

Probably the simplest example of Krichever-Novikov algebra beyond an affine Kac-Moody algebra is the three point algebra. If we look at the three point algebra case when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, Cox and Jurisich showed at
[
CJ13
]
that the three point ring is isomorphic to
R_{1}=\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1},u]
with
u^{2}=t^{2}+4t
and gave a free field realization for the three point affine Lie algebra. Furthermore,
[
CJ13
]
defined the subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\otimes{R_{1}}\oplus\Omega_{1}^{R_{1}}/d{R_{1}}
as the three point Heisenberg algebra through generators and relations.

The four point algebra was studied by Bremner at
[
Bre95
]
and, when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
, by Cox at
[
Cox08
]
. Bremner showed that the four point ring is isomorphic to
{R_{2}}=\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1},u]
where
u^{2}=t^{2}-2bt+1
with
b\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{\pm 1\}
and gave a free field realization of the four point affine Lie algebra in terms of the ultraspherical Gengenbauer polynomials. Cox gave a realization for the four point algebra where the center acts nontriavially, furthermore
[
Cox08
]
defined the subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\otimes{R_{2}}\oplus\Omega_{1}^{R_{2}}/d{R_{2}}
as the four point Heisenberg algebra.

We can find another examples of Krichever-Novikov algebras beyond the
n
point algebras such as the elliptic algebra and Date-Jimbo-Kashiwara-Miwa algebra. Bueno, Cox and Futorny showed at
[
BCF09
]
two realizations of the elliptic algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})\otimes R_{3}\oplus\Omega_{1}^{R_{3}}/d{R_{3}}
where
{R_{3}}=\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1},u]
with
u^{2}=t^{3}-2bt+t
and defined the subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\otimes R\oplus\Omega_{1}^{R}/dR
as the elliptic Heisenberg algebra.

The DJKM algebra introduced by Date, Jimbo, Kashiwara and Miwa in

[
DJKM83
]
are nothing but a one dimensional central extension of
\mathfrak{g}\otimes R_{3}
with
R_{3}=\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1},u]
with
u^{2}=(t^{2}-b^{2})(t^{2}-c^{2})
where
b\neq\pm c
are complex constants and
\mathfrak{g}
is a simple finite dimensional Lie
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. Cox, Futorny and Martins constructed a realization of the DJKM algebra when
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
in terms of sums of partial differential operators in
[
CFM14
]
, furthermore defined the subalgebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})\otimes R_{3}\oplus\Omega_{1}^{R_{3}}/d{R_{3}}
as the DJKM Heisenberg algebra.

It is a natural question how to classify irreducible modules for these Heisenberg subalgebras defined above. Inspired by
[
BBFK13
]
, in this paper we give an irreducibility criteria for
\varphi
-Verma modules over the hyperelliptic Heisenberg algebras.

Given an affine Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, the subspace
L=\mathbb{C}c\oplus\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\mathfrak{g}_{n\delta}
is a Heisenberg subalgebra. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow{\pm}
be an arbitrary function defined on
\mathbb{N}
. The following spaces are abelian subalgebras

L_{\varphi}^{\pm}=\left(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N},\varphi(n)=\pm}\mathfrak{g}_{n\delta}\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{N},\varphi(m)=\mp}\mathfrak{g}_{-m\delta}\right)

and

L=L_{\varphi}^{-}\oplus\mathbb{C}c\oplus L_{\varphi}^{+}

is a triangular decomposition. Let
\mathbb{C}v
be an one-dimensional representation of
\mathbb{C}c\oplus L_{\varphi}^{+}
, where
cv=av
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
and
L_{\varphi}^{+}=0
. The corresponding
\varphi
-Verma module is the induced module

M_{\mathfrak{g},\varphi}=\mathfrak{U}(L)\otimes_{\mathfrak{U}(\mathbb{C}c\oplus L_{\varphi}^{+})}\mathbb{C}v

where
\mathfrak{U}(A)
denote the universal enveloping algebra of
A
.

The main result of this paper is Theorem
4.2
which estabilishes the irreducibility criteria for
\varphi
-Verma modules over hyperelliptic Heisenberg algebras.

1.
The universal central extension of a Lie algebra

Let
\mathfrak{c}
be a Lie algebra. The central extension of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{c}
is an exact sequence of Lie algebras

(1.1)

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{c}\xrightarrow{i}\mathfrak{e}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow 0

such that
\mathfrak{c}
is the centre of
\mathfrak{e}
.

A morphism from one existing central extension to another central extension

(1.2)

0\longrightarrow\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}\xrightarrow{i^{\prime}}\mathfrak{e}\xrightarrow{\pi^{\prime}}\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow 0

a pair
(\phi,\phi_{0})
of Lie algebra homomorphisms such that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{e}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{g}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{e}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\prime}}

is commutative.

The central extension is said to be a
covering
of
\mathfrak{g}
in case
\mathfrak{e}
is perfect. A covering of
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be
universal central extension
if for every central extension of
\mathfrak{g}
there exists a unique morphism from the covering to the central extension.

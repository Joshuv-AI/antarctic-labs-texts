MULTIDEGREES OF BINOMIAL EDGE IDEALS
Jacob Cooper, Ethan Leventhal

Abstract
. Let G G be a simple graph with binomial edge ideal J G J_{G} . We prove how to calculate the multidegree of J G J_{G} based on combinatorial properties of G G . In particular, we study the set 𝒮 min ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G) defined as the collection of subsets of vertices whose prime ideals have minimum codimension. We provide results which assist in determining 𝒮 min ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G) , then calculate 𝒮 min ⁡ ( G ) \operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G) for star, horned complete, barbell, cycle, wheel, and friendship graphs, and use the main result of the paper to obtain the multidegrees of their binomial edge ideals.



1.
Introduction

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
G
be a simple, connected graph with vertex set
V(G)=[n]:=\left\{1,\ldots,n\right\}
and edge set
E(G)
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field and let
T
be the polynomial ring
T:=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
. The
binomial edge ideal
of
G
is defined as

J_{G}:=(x_{i}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{i}:i<j\text{ and }\left\{i,j\right\}\in E(G)).

These ideals have been intensely studied during the past few decades, in particular because of their connection to the class of ideals of 2-minors of a generic
2\times n
matrix, as well as their connection to conditional independence
[
1
,
5
,
8
,
13
]
. Similarly, the multidegree is a potent geometric invariant that has been used across domains of algebra, geometry, and even statistics
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
9
,
11
]
. We begin this section with the necessary notations to state the main result, then we include the statement of our main theorem, and finally we provide an outline of the rest of the paper.

Notation 1.1
.

Let
G
be a graph. For a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
, let
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
denote
V(G)\setminus S
and consider the induced subgraph
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
. Let
c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
be the number of connected components of
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
. We denote
h_{\operatorname{min}}:=\min_{S\in V(G)}\{|S|-c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\}
. Then we define

\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G):=\left\{S\subseteq V(G):|S|-c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)=h_{\operatorname{min}}\right\}.

By labeling the connected components
G_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
, we establish the notation
N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu):=\bigl(|V(G_{1})|,\dots,|V(G_{c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)})|\bigr)
as the multiset containing the number of vertices of each connected component. The following is our main theorem.

Theorem A

(
Theorem

4.2
)
.

The multidegree of the binomial edge ideal of a graph
G
is

\mathcal{C}\left(R(G);t_{1},t_{2}\right)=\sum_{S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)}\left[(t_{1}t_{2})^{|S|}\cdot\prod_{n\in N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}\frac{t_{1}^{n}-t_{2}^{n}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\right].

This theorem makes the computation of the multidegree of
J_{G}
very straightforward once
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
is known.

We begin
Section

2
with some background information on multidegrees. We define the ring of a graph
G
to be
R(G):=T/J_{G}
so that we can follow the notation of Miller and Sturmfels in
[
12
]
and denote the multidegree of
J_{G}
with
\mathcal{C}\left(R(G);t_{1},t_{2}\right)
. The multidegree is a polynomial with integer coefficients, which can be interpreted in different ways. Geometrically, for example, its coefficients correspond to the number of intersection points between the variety determined by the ideal and a suitable product of general hyperplanes in a product of projective spaces; algebraically, these coefficients can be read from the top degree of the Hilbert polynomial of the ring
[
4
,
14
]
.

The ideal
J_{G}
is radical. In
[
8
]
, Herzog, Hibi, Hreinsdottir, Kahle, and Rauh give a complete description of the prime decomposition of
J_{G}
; in
Section

3
, we provide the algebraic tools that allow us to use this description to find multidegrees. In
Section

4
, we compute some foundational multidegrees that serve as ‘building blocks’ for our final computations. Then we arrive at our main result,
Theorem

4.2
, which is a closed formula for the multidegree of
J_{G}
that only involves combinatorial properties of the graph.

We provide some results to speed up the process of determining
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
and then spend
Section

5
demonstrating the usefulness of these results by calculating the multidegrees for six different families of graphs. Finally,
Section

6
concludes the paper with closing thoughts and ideas for further research.

2.
Background Information

The multidegree was originally defined by van der Waerden in 1929
[
14
]
. It carries tremendous information about the algebraic and geometric properties of an ideal and its associated variety. This has made it a useful tool to approach multiple problems in algebra, geometry, and combinatorics
[
3
,
4
,
7
,
9
,
11
]
. Here we use the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-grading of
T
induced by
x_{i}=(1,0)
and
y_{i}=(0,1)
that carries over the variables
t_{1},t_{2}=t_{1},t_{2}
. We follow the notation from
[
12
]
and denote the Hilbert series of a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded module
M
by
H(M;t_{1},t_{2})
. By
[
12
, Theorem 8.20]
, there exists a
K
-polynomial

\mathcal{K}(M;t_{1},t_{2})
such that

H(M;t_{1},t_{2})=\frac{\mathcal{K}(M;t_{1},t_{2})}{(1-t_{1})^{n}(1-t_{2})^{n}}.

We define the
multidegree
of a
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-graded module
M
to be the sum
\mathcal{C}(M;t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},t_{2}]
of all terms in
\mathcal{K}(M;1-t_{1},1-t_{2})
of smallest degree, i.e., those of total degree
\text{codim}(M):=\dim(T)-\dim(M)
. Therefore, one has
\mathcal{K}(T;t_{1},t_{2})=1
.
Despite the popularity of binomial edge ideals and the usefulness of the multidegree, to the best of or knowledge there is a lack of literature studying both together. Our paper aims to investigate connections between the multidegree of a binomial edge ideal and the combinatorial information of its underlying graph. We note that a recent paper by Kumar and Sarkar studies the Hilbert series of decomposable graphs in the
\mathbb{Z}
-graded setting
[
10
]
.

The final piece of introductory information, found in
[
8
]
, is the computation of the prime decomposition of
J_{G}
. Recall that we use
c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
to denote the number of connected components of
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
. Call these components
G_{1},\ldots,G_{c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}
. For each
G_{i}
, let
\tilde{G_{i}}
be the complete graph on
V(G_{i})
. Then for a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
we define the prime ideal

P_{S}(G):=\left(\bigcup_{i\in S}\left\{x_{i},y_{i}\right\},J_{\tilde{G}_{1}},\ldots,J_{\tilde{G}_{c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}}\right).

In
[
8
, Theorem 3.2]
, it is shown that
J_{G}=\bigcap_{S\subseteq V(G)}P_{S}(G)
. We expand on this theorem in the following sections to obtain a direct calculation of the multidegree of
J_{G}
.

3.
Relationships Between Ideals and Multidegrees

In this section we include some preparatory results that simplify the computation of the multidegree of
J_{G}
. Our first proposition narrows down which multidegrees we need for our calculations. It is proven in
[
8
]
that the height (codimension) of
P_{S}(G)
for a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
is given by
0pt{P_{S}(G)}=|S|+n-c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)

[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
. This makes it clear that
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
, defined in
Notation

1.1
, is the collection of all subsets of
V(G)
whose corresponding prime ideals have minimum height.

Proposition 3.1
.

For a graph
G
we have

\mathcal{C}\Big(T/\bigcap_{S\subseteq V(G)}P_{S}(G);t_{1},t_{2}\Big)=\mathcal{C}\Big(T/\bigcap_{S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)}P_{S}(G);t_{1},t_{2}\Big).

Moreover, if
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}
are homogeneous ideals of
T
with the same height, then

\mathcal{C}\Big(R/\bigcap_{i\in[m]}I_{i};t_{1},t_{2}\Big)=\sum_{i\in[m]}\left(\mathcal{C}\Big(R/I_{i};t_{1},t_{2}\Big)\right).

Proof.

These follow from
[
12
, Definition 8.43, Theorem 8.53, and Corollary 8.54]
.
∎

In other words, the only prime ideals that contribute to the multidegree of
J_{G}
are those with minimum height. Additionally, this allows us to work with the sum of multidegrees instead of an intersection of ideals.

Next, we present a similar proposition for the sum of ideals.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
I_{1},\ldots,I_{n}
be homogeneous ideals of
T
such that for distinct
i
and
j
, the variables used in a minimal set of generators of
I_{i}
are not used in that of
I_{j}
. Moreover, assume all the variables in
T
are used in the ideals
I_{i}
. Let
T_{i}
be the polynomial ring in the variables involved in the generators of
I_{i}
. Then

\mathcal{C}\left(T/\sum_{i\in[n]}I_{i};t_{1},t_{2}\right)=\prod_{i\in[n]}(\mathcal{C}(T_{i}/(I_{i}\cap T_{i});t_{1},t_{2})).

Proof.

By assumption one has a graded isomorphism

\frac{T}{\sum_{i\in[n]}I_{i}}\cong\frac{T_{1}}{I_{1}\cap T_{1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\cdots\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}\frac{T_{n}}{I_{n}\cap T_{n}}.

Therefore

H\Big(T/\sum_{i\in[n]}I_{i};t_{1},t_{2}\Big)=\frac{\prod_{i\in[n]}\mathcal{K}(T_{i}/(I_{i}\cap T_{i});t_{1},t_{2})}{(1-t_{1})^{n}(1-t_{2})^{n}}

and the result follows.
∎

The following proposition computes the multidegree of ideals of variables.

Proposition 3.3
.

For a graph
G
and subset of vertices
S
,

\mathcal{C}(T/\bigcup_{i\in S}\left\{x_{i},y_{i}\right\};t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{1}t_{2})^{|S|}.

Proof.

Note that

H(T/\bigcup_{i\in S}\left\{x_{i},y_{i}\right\});t_{1},t_{2})=\frac{1}{(1-t_{1})^{n-|S|}(1-t_{2})^{n-|S|}}=\frac{(1-t_{1})^{|S|}(1-t_{2})^{|S|}}{(1-t_{1})^{n}(1-t_{2})^{n}}.

The result clearly follows.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two disjoint graphs and suppose
H
is their union. Then by
Proposition

3.2
,
\mathcal{C}\left(R(H);t_{1},t_{2}\right)=\mathcal{C}\left(R(G_{1});t_{1},t_{2}\right)\cdot\mathcal{C}\left(R(G_{2});t_{1},t_{2}\right)
, meaning the multidegree of the binomial edge ideal of the union of two disjoint graphs is the product of their multidegrees. For this reason, we assume all graphs are connected graphs for the rest of the paper.

4.
Computing the Multidegree of the Binomial Edge Ideal of a Graph

In
Section

3
we showed how the prime decomposition of
J_{G}
can be used to find the multidegree
\mathcal{C}\left(R(G);t_{1},t_{2}\right)
. We see from the definition that each
P_{S}(G)
is constructed from two types of ideals, so in this section we first obtain the two corresponding multidegrees.

The first multidegree we need is that of an ideal of the form
\bigcup_{i\in S}\left\{x_{i},y_{i}\right\}
, which we showed in
Proposition

3.2
to be

\mathcal{C}(T/\bigcup_{i\in S}\left\{x_{i},y_{i}\right\};t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{1}t_{2})^{|S|}.

The other multidegree that we need is the multidegree of the binomial edge ideal of a complete graph. For this we turn to a theorem proved by Bruns, Conca, Raicu, and Varbaro in
[
2
]
: Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field,
X
be the
n\times m
matrix of variables and
2\leq t\leq\text{min}(n,m)
. For a subset
L
of elements of a ring, define
h_{v}(L)
to be the sum of all power products of elements of
L
whose exponents sum to
v
. Let
I_{t}
be the ideal generated by
t
-minors of
X
. Then the multidegree of the ring
R/I_{t}(X)
with respect to the
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
-graded structure given by columns is

\mathcal{C}\Big(R/I_{t}(X);t_{1},t_{2}\Big)=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})^{t-2}\det(h_{\ell-i+j}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}))_{i,j=1,2,\ldots,t-1},

where
\ell=n+1-t

[
2
, Theorem 4.6.8]
. Therefore in our setting we obtain the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

The multidegree of the binomial edge ideal of a complete graph
K_{n}
is

\mathcal{C}\left(R(K_{n});t_{1},t_{2}\right)=\frac{t_{1}^{n}-t_{2}^{n}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}.

Proof.

The binomial edge ideal of a complete graph on
n
vertices is simply the ideal generated by the
2
-minors of a
2\times n
matrix of variables. This is an application of
[
2
, Theorem 4.6.8]
where
n
is the order of the complete graph and
m=2
. Following the theorem, we calculate
t=2
and
\ell=n+1-2=n-1
. Then the multidegree is

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\left(R(K_{n});t_{1},t_{2}\right)=\text{det}(h_{n-1}(t_{1},t_{2}))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}t_{1}^{i}t_{2}^{j}=\frac{t_{1}^{n}-t_{2}^{n}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}.

∎

Recall that to compute
J_{G}
, we need to find
J_{\tilde{G}_{1}},\ldots,J_{\tilde{G}_{c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}}
. Since
\tilde{G}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{G}_{c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}
are complete graphs, we can use
Proposition

4.1
to find their multidegrees very easily once we know the orders of these complete graphs, i.e., the number of vertices in the connected components of
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
. This motivates our notation
N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu):=\bigl(|V(G_{1})|,\dots,|V(G_{c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)})|\bigr)
.

Now we present the main result of the paper, a formula to combinatorially compute the multidegree of the binomial edge ideal of a graph
G
. Since it relies on determining
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
, we follow the theorem with a discussion of shortcuts for finding
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For a graph
G
, the multidegree of
J_{G}
is

\mathcal{C}\left(R(G);t_{1},t_{2}\right)=\sum_{S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)}\left[(t_{1}t_{2})^{|S|}\cdot\prod_{n\in N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}\frac{t_{1}^{n}-t_{2}^{n}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\right].

Proof.

Let
G
be a simple, connected graph. We have the following chain of equalities:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\left(R(G);t_{1},t_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}\Big(T/J_{G};t_{1},t_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle(\text{By definition})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}\Big(T/\bigcap_{S\subseteq V(G)}P_{S}(G);t_{1},t_{2}\Big)

[
8
, Theorem 3.2]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}\Big(T/\bigcap_{S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)}P_{S}(G);t_{1},t_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle(\text{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{prop:only min heights}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)}\mathcal{C}\Big(T/P_{S}(G);t_{1},t_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle(\text{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{prop:only min heights}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)}\mathcal{C}\Big(T/\Big(\bigcup_{i\in S}\left\{x_{i},y_{i}\right\},J_{\tilde{G}_{1}},\ldots,J_{\tilde{G}_{c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}}\Big);t_{1},t_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle(\text{By definition})

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)}\left[\mathcal{C}\Big(T/\bigcup_{i\in S}\left\{x_{i},y_{i}\right\};t_{1},t_{2}\Big)\cdot\prod_{i=1,\ldots,c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}\left(\mathcal{C}\left(R(\tilde{G}_{i});t_{1},t_{2}\right)\right)\right]

\displaystyle(\text{\lx@cref{creftype~refnum}{prop:product of multidegrees}})

(
Proposition

3.3
and
Proposition

4.1
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)}\left[(t_{1}t_{2})^{|S|}\cdot\prod_{n\in N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)}\frac{t_{1}^{n}-t_{2}^{n}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\right].

∎

Theorem

4.2
explicitly states how to compute the multidegree of
J_{G}
once we know
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
. The obvious goal now is to determine
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
. We could calculate
0pt{P_{S}(G)}
for all
S
, but since
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
is a subset of the power set
\mathcal{P}(V(G))
, this quickly becomes impractical. Here we provide our most relevant results to help find
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
efficiently, starting with some book-keeping notation.

Notation 4.3
.

For a set
X
and an element
y
we denote
X+y:=X\cup\left\{y\right\}
and
X-y:=X\setminus\left\{y\right\}
. Similarly, for a graph
G
and vertex
v
we denote
G-v:=G[V(G)-v]
.

Our first result explains how
cut vertices
(vertices
v
for which
c(G-v)>c(G)
) affect the height of prime ideals corresponding to the relevant subsets.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
G
be a graph,
S\subseteq V(G)
, and
v\in V(G)\setminus S
. From
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
we get the following:

a)

If
v
is not a cut vertex of
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
then
0pt{P_{S+v}(G)}>0pt{P_{S}(G)}
.

b)

If
v
is a cut vertex
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
which cuts a component of
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
into exactly two connected components then
0pt{P_{S+v}(G)}=0pt{P_{S}(G)}
.

c)

If
v
cuts a component of
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
into three or more connected components then
0pt{P_{S+v}(G)}<0pt{P_{S}(G)}
.

Next, recall that a
simplicial vertex
of a graph is a vertex whose open neighborhood forms a clique, i.e., whose neighbors are all adjacent. This includes vertices of degree 1, called
leaves
. We prove that simplicial vertices can be ignored when finding
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
G
be a graph. For all
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
,
S
contains no simplicial vertices.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph with simplicial vertex
v
and let
v\not\in S\subseteq V(G)
. We will show
S+v\notin\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
. Since
v
is a simplicial vertex, its neighbors in
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
are adjacent (regardless of
S
). This means
v
is not a cut vertex of
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
. By
Remark

4.4
,
0pt{P_{S+v}(G)}>0pt{P_{S}(G)}
. We have found a subset of vertices whose prime ideal has height less than that of
S+v
, thus
S+v\notin\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
.
∎

The last result we present here requires
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
to be a
separating set
, a vertex subset of a (connected) graph
G
whose removal disconnects
G
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
G
be a graph. For all nonempty
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
,
S
is a separating set of
G
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
and suppose
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
is nonempty. For all
T\subseteq V(G)
we have
0pt{P_{S}(G)}\leq 0pt{P_{T}(G)}
, so in particular
0pt{P_{S}(G)}\leq 0pt{P_{\varnothing}(G)}
. By
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
,

\displaystyle|S|+n-c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)

\displaystyle\leq n-1

\displaystyle|S|+c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)

\displaystyle\leq-1

\displaystyle|S|+1

\displaystyle\leq c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu).

Since
S
is nonempty,
|S|\geq 1
. This implies
2\leq c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
, thus
S
is a separating set of
G
.
∎

5.
Example Applications

To demonstrate the usefulness of
Theorem

4.2
, we take this section to work through some examples involving basic families of graphs. First, a bit of notation.

Notation 5.1
.

Recall that
N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
records the sizes of the connected components of
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
. When describing
N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
in these examples, we will use the convenient notation of
n:x
to denote
n
copies of
x
.

The first example we consider is a star graph, which gives a great first look at our proposed process for calculating multidegree, as well as showing off
Proposition

4.5
for reductions in cases with simplicial vertices.

Example 5.2

(Star Graph)

Example 5.3

(Barbell Graph)
.

We will use the notation of

\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(B_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\left\{v_{1},v_{2}\right\})

Example 5.4

(Horned Complete Graph)
.

Let
\check{K}_{n}
be a
horned complete graph
, which we define as the complete graph
K_{n}
on vertices
U:=\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right\}
with two leaves (‘horns’) attached to each (see
Figure

2
). By
Proposition

4.5
, we know
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(\check{K}_{n})
will not include subsets that contain a ‘horn’ vertex, which narrows it down to
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(\check{K}_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right\})
. To determine it exactly, first note that
v_{1}
cuts the graph into three connected components, so by
Remark

4.4
,
0pt{P_{\left\{v_{1}\right\}}(\check{K}_{n})}<0pt{P_{\varnothing}(\check{K}_{n})}
. Now in
\check{K}_{n}-v_{1}
, we once again have a vertex
v_{2}
that cuts one component into three, so
0pt{P_{\left\{v_{1},v_{2}\right\}}(\check{K}_{n})}<0pt{P_{\left\{v_{1}\right\}}(\check{K}_{n})}
. This pattern continues until
\check{K}_{n}-\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}\right\}
, in which
v_{n}
cuts one component into two, so
0pt{P_{\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\right\}}(\check{K}_{n})}=0pt{P_{\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}\right\}}(\check{K}_{n})}
. Because of the graph’s symmetry, we can repeat the argument above for all
v_{i}\in U
and conclude
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(\check{K}_{n})=\left\{U-v_{i}:i\in[n]\right\}\cup\left\{U\right\}
.

See that
N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU-v_{i}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)=\left\{2n-2:1,3\right\}
for
i\in[n]
and
N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)=\left\{2n:1\right\}
, so by
Theorem

4.2
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\left(R(\check{K}_{n});t_{1},t_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=n\cdot\left[(t_{1}t_{2})^{n-1}\cdot\left(\frac{t_{1}^{1}-t_{2}^{1}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\right)^{2n-2}\cdot\frac{t_{1}^{3}-t_{2}^{3}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\right]+(t_{1}t_{2})^{n}\cdot\left(\frac{t_{1}^{1}-t_{2}^{1}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\right)^{2n}

\displaystyle=n\cdot\left[(t_{1}t_{2})^{n-1}\cdot\frac{t_{1}^{3}-t_{2}^{3}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\right]+(t_{1}t_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle=n(t_{1}t_{2})^{n-1}(t_{1}^{2}+t_{1}t_{2}+t_{2}^{2})+t_{1}^{n}t_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle=n(t_{1}^{n+1}t_{2}^{n-1}+t_{1}^{n}t_{2}^{n}+t_{1}^{n-1}t_{2}^{n+1})+t_{1}^{n}t_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle=nt_{1}^{n+1}t_{2}^{n-1}+(n+1)t_{1}^{n}t_{2}^{n}+nt_{1}^{n-1}t_{2}^{n+1}.

\mathcal{C}\left(R(\check{K}_{n});t_{1},t_{2}\right)
is
n
rather than a constant, so it scales with the order of the graph. Based on our data collection in
Section

Example 5.5

(Cycle Graph)

Let

\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(C_{n})=\left\{\varnothing\right\}

Finally, we demonstrate
Proposition

4.5
by computing the multidegree of the binomial edge ideals of friendship graphs and then briefly explain how the strategy can generalize to windmill graphs.

Example 5.7

(Friendship Graph)
.

Let
F_{n}
be the
friendship graph
constructed by connecting
n
copies of
K_{3}
at a common vertex
v_{0}
, as in
Figure

3
. By
Proposition

4.5
,
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(F_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\left\{v_{0}\right\})
. By
Remark

4.4
we see that
0pt{P_{\left\{v_{0}\right\}}(F_{n})}<0pt{P_{\varnothing}(F_{n})}
, so
\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(F_{n})=\left\{\left\{v_{0}\right\}\right\}
.

We compute
N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\left\{v_{0}\right\}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)=\left\{n:2\right\}
. By
Theorem

4.2
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}\left(R(F_{n});t_{1},t_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(t_{1}t_{2})^{1}\cdot\left(\frac{t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}}{t_{1}-t_{2}}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle=t_{1}t_{2}\cdot(t_{1}+t_{2})^{n}

\displaystyle=t_{1}t_{2}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{i}t_{1}^{i}t_{2}^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{n}{i}t_{1}^{i+1}t_{2}^{j+1}.

This strategy works equally well for
windmill graphs

W_{n,m}
, a generalization of friendship graphs that combine
m
copies of
K_{n}
. The only difference in calculations is that
N(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\left\{v_{0}\right\}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)=\left\{m:n-1\right\}
, so the algebra requires the multinomial theorem instead of the binomial theorem.

Let
P_{n}
denote a
path graph
with
n
vertices, a graph whose edges are
\{i,i+1\}
for
1\leq i<n
. We were not able to obtain a satisfying closed formula for the multidegree of path graphs using our main theorem. However, since
J_{P_{n}}
is generated by a regular sequence, in the following proposition we are able to make this computation using alternative methods.

Proposition 5.8
.

For every positive integer
n
we have

\mathcal{C}\left(R(P_{n});t_{1},t_{2}\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=n-1\\
i,j\geq 0\end{subarray}}\binom{n-1}{i}t_{1}^{i}t_{2}^{j}.

Proof.

Let
f_{i}=x_{i}y_{i+1}-x_{i+1}y_{i}
for
1\leq i<n
be the generators of
J_{P_{n}}
. Notice that each
f_{i}
is homogeneous of degree
(1,1)
and that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1}
form a regular sequence. Therefore we have graded short exact sequences

0\to T/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1})[(-1,-1)]\xrightarrow{f_{i}}T/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1})\to T/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i})\to 0,

for each
i
, where
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1})
is the zero ideal if
i=1
. Thus

H\Big(T/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i});t_{1},t_{2}\Big)=(1-t_{1}t_{2})H\Big(T/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1});t_{1},t_{2}\Big)

for each
i
, and so

H\Big(R(P_{n});t_{1},t_{2}\Big)=H\Big(T/(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n-1});t_{1},t_{2}\Big)=\frac{(1-t_{1}t_{2})^{n-1}}{(1-t_{1})^{n}(1-t_{2})^{n}}.

Hence
\mathcal{C}\left(R(P_{n});t_{1},t_{2}\right)=(t_{1}+t_{2})^{n-1}
as desired.
∎

Notice that the table only goes up to graphs of order 9. This is because the number of calculations needed to study each graph is immense. One improvement we achieved comes from the following proposition, which effectively halves the number of subsets needed to be checked when finding

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
G
be a graph. For all
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
,
|S|\leq\left\lfloor{\frac{n-1}{2}}\right\rfloor
.

Proof.

Let
G
be a graph of order
n
and let
S\in\operatorname{\mathcal{S}_{min}}(G)
. This means for all
T\subseteq V(G)
we have
0pt{P_{S}(G)}\leq 0pt{P_{T}(G)}
. In particular,
0pt{P_{S}(G)}\leq 0pt{P_{\varnothing}(G)}
. By
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
,
|S|+n-c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\leq n-1
. Notice that
c(\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)
is at most
n-|S|
(the case where all vertices not in
S
are isolated in
G[\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muS\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu]
), so

\displaystyle|S|+n-(n-|S|)

\displaystyle\leq n-1

\displaystyle 2|S|

\displaystyle\leq n-1

\displaystyle|S|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n-1}{2}.

Since
|S|
must be an integer, we get
|S|\leq\left\lfloor{\frac{n-1}{2}}\right\rfloor
as desired.
∎

Despite this improvement, our machines could not finish the computations for graphs of order 10 after a week of nonstop computing.

Another area that could be explored further is generalizations of some of the graphs presented in
Section

5
. For example, it is very natural to consider barbell graphs with more than two ‘bells’, or bells of different sizes. Our methods easily provide the multidegrees of these graphs in terms of products of polynomials, but despite the approachable appearance of these products, we could not find satisfying simplifications or acquire exact expressions for the final coefficients.

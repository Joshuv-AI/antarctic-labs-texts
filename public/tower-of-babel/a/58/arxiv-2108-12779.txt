Parameterizations of Logarithmic-Space Reductions, Stack-State Complexity of Nonuniform Families of Pushdown Automata, and a Road to the LOGCFL ⊆ \subseteq LOGDCFL/poly Question


Abstract
The complexity class LOGCFL \mathrm{LOGCFL} (resp., LOGDCFL \mathrm{LOGDCFL} ) consists of all languages that are many-one reducible to context-free (resp., deterministic context-free) languages using logarithmic space. These complexity classes have been studied over five decades in connection to parallel computation since they are located between Nick’s classes NC 1 \mathrm{NC}^{1} and NC 2 \mathrm{NC}^{2} . In contrast, the state complexity of nonuniform finite-automaton families was first discussed in the 1970s and it has been extensively explored lately for various finite-automata families. We extend this old subject to the stack-state complexity (i.e., the total number of inner states plus simultaneously pushable stack symbol series) of nonuniform families of various pushdown automata. We introduce reasonable “parameterizations” of LOGCFL and LOGDCFL and apply them as a technical tool to establish a close connection between the LOGCFL ⊆ LOGDCFL / poly \mathrm{LOGCFL}\subseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly} question and the polynomial stack-state complexity of nonuniform families of two-way pushdown automata. We also discuss the precise computational complexity of polynomial-size one-way pushdown automata. Key words. parameterized decision problem, promise problem, LOGCFL, LOGDCFL, logarithmic-space reduction, stack-state complexity, pushdown automata, polynomial-size advice

Context-free languages are defined by context-free grammars. Those languages are also characterized by

of

If

x\in\overline{L}
,
M
rejects
x
. To express this language
L
, we often use the notation
L(M)
. At this moment, we formally introduce two fundamental families
\mathrm{CFL}
and
\mathrm{DCFL}
as the collections of all languages recognized by 1npda’s and by 1dpda’s, respectively.

As for two-way versions of 1npda’s and 1dpda’s, which are succinctly called 2npda’s and 2dpda’s, we modify their aforementioned definition of
M
as follows. A new transition function
\delta
maps
(Q-Q_{halt})\times\check{\Sigma}_{\lambda}\times\Gamma
to
{\cal P}(Q\times\Gamma^{\leq e}\times D)
, where
D=\{-1,0,+1\}
.
Assume that
M
is in inner state
q
, scanning
\sigma
on an input tape and
a
on a topmost stack cell. A transition of the form
(q,z,d)\in\delta(q,\sigma,a)
causes
M
to change
q
to
p
, replace
a
by
z
, and move an input-tape head in direction
d
. Note that, when
M
reads
\lambda
, the tape head must stay still, i.e., must take the value
d=0
.

2.4
Advice Extensions of LOGCFL and LOGDCFL

Let us define
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
, which are respectively advice versions of
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}
and
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
, and state a few important characterizations of them.
We first review an advice version of
\mathrm{CFL}
.

Karp-Lipton style advice for pushdown automata was discussed in
[
17
]
and the language family
\mathrm{CFL}/n
was introduced there by splitting an input tape of each underlying 1npda into two separate tracks, one of which holds a standard input string
x
and the other holds an advice string of
length equal to

x
.
For distinction, when advice is given to an underlying 1npda, we call such a machine an
advised-1npda
to emphasize the use of advice. Since an advised-1npda moves its tape head only in one direction until it either reads the right endmarker or enters a halting state before the endmarker, the 1npda reads the advice string only once from left to right.
This advice model is essentially different from the one equipped with “separate” advice tapes whose heads
can freely move in two directions. We will discuss this two-tape model later.

Consider a compound alphabet
\Sigma_{\Theta}=[\Sigma,\Theta,\#]
composed of two alphabets
\Sigma
and
\Theta
.
Given a language
L
over
\Sigma_{\Theta}
and an advice function
h:\mathbb{N}\to\Theta^{*}
, we define
L[h]
as the language
\{x\mid[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}]\in L\}
over
\Sigma
.
By extending this notation, for a given function
f
, we write
f[h]
to denote the function
g
defined as
g(x)=f([\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}])
for all
x
.
With the help of these notations,
\mathrm{CFL}/n
is precisely composed of all languages
L[h]
for length-preserving advice functions
h
and languages
L\in\mathrm{CFL}
.
Similarly, we define
\mathrm{DCFL}/n
using a deterministic version of advised-1npda’s, which are called
advised-1dpda’s
(
with no separate advice tape
).

Now, we are ready to define
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
using advised-
\mathrm{L}
-m-reductions.

Definition 2.2

The advised complexity class
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
(resp.,
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
) is defined to be
\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL})
(resp.,
\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{DCFL})
).

The advised families
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
are quite robust classes in the following sense. We further strengthen this robustness in Lemma
2.4
.

Lemma 2.3

\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}=\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL}/n)
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}=\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{DCFL}/n)
.

Proof.

We show only the first statement because the second one is similarly proven. Since
\mathrm{CFL}\subseteq\mathrm{CFL}/n
, we instantly obtain
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL}/n)
.
Conversely, let
L
denote any language over alphabet
\Sigma
in
\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL}/n)
. Take a function
f\in\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
, a polynomial
p_{1}
, and a language
A\in\mathrm{CFL}/n
over alphabet
\Xi
such that
L=\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\mid f(x)\in A\}

and
|f(x)|\leq p_{1}(|x|)
for all
x\in\Sigma^{*}
.
By Lemma
2.1
, we further take a function
g\in\mathrm{FL}
, a polynomial
p_{2}
, and an advice function
\ell:\mathbb{N}\to\Gamma^{*}
for advice alphabet
\Gamma
satisfying
f=g[\ell]
and
|\ell(n)|\leq p_{2}(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that
g
maps
(\Sigma_{\Gamma})^{*}
to
\Xi^{*}
, where
\hat{\Sigma}_{\Gamma}=[\Sigma,\Gamma,\#]
.
Moreover, we take a language
B\in\mathrm{CFL}
, a polynomial
p_{3}
, and an advice function
h:\mathbb{N}\to\Theta^{*}
for advice alphabet
\Theta
such that
A=B[h]
and
|h(n)|\leq p_{3}(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Here, we abbreviate as
\hat{h}(n)
the string
h(0)\natural h(1)\natural\cdots\natural h(n)
with a separator
\natural
and we intend to set
k(n)
to be
[\>\begin{subarray}{c}\ell(n)\\
\hat{h}(n)\end{subarray}]
, which is a string over the compound alphabet
\hat{\Gamma}_{\Theta}=[\Gamma,\Theta\cup\{\natural\},\#]
.
Next, we define
s([\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
w\end{subarray}])=[\>\begin{subarray}{c}g(z)\\
u_{|g(z)|}\end{subarray}]
, where
w=[\>\begin{subarray}{c}y\\
u_{0}\natural u_{1}\natural\cdots\natural u_{|x|}\end{subarray}]
in
(\hat{\Gamma}_{\Theta})^{*}
and

z=[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
y\end{subarray}]
in
(\hat{\Sigma}_{\Gamma})^{*}
.
Finally, let
\hat{\Xi}_{\Theta}=[\Xi,\Theta\cup\{\natural\},\#]
and define
t=s[k]
, which is a new reduction function from
\Sigma^{*}
to
(\hat{\Xi}_{\Theta})^{*}
.
Since
s\in\mathrm{FL}
and
k
is polynomially bounded,
t\in\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
follows immediately. Since
A=\{y\mid[\>\begin{subarray}{c}y\\
h(|y|)\end{subarray}]\in B\}
, we conclude that
x\in L
iff
t(x)=s([\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
k(|x|)\end{subarray}])=[\>\begin{subarray}{c}f(x)\\
h(|f(x)|)\end{subarray}]\in B
. Therefore,
L
belongs to
\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL})=\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

\Box

Unlike the advised-1npda’s with no separate advice tape, let us consider another model of advised-1npda that holds standard input strings and advice strings on two separate tapes and move its advice-tape head in two directions.
Formally, a language
L
over alphabet
\Sigma
is in
\mathrm{CFL}/\mathrm{poly}
if there exist an advice alphabet
\Theta
, a polynomially-bounded advice function
h:\mathbb{N}\to\Theta^{*}
, and an advised-1npda
M
(equipped with two separate tapes) such that, for any
x\in\Sigma^{*}
,

iff
M
starts with
x
on an input tape and
h(|x|)
on an advice tape and
M
eventually accepts

by moving an advice-tape head freely in two directions. Obviously,
\mathrm{CFL}/n\subseteq\mathrm{CFL}/\mathrm{poly}
follows.

Lemma 2.4

\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
coincide with
\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL}/\mathrm{poly})
and
\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{DCFL}/\mathrm{poly})
, respectively.

For technical reason, we first prove the following characterization lemma.

Lemma 2.5

Let
L
be any language. The following statements are logically equivalent.

1.

L\in\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

2.

There exist a polynomially-bounded advice function
h
and a language
K\in\mathrm{LOGCFL}
such that
L=K[h]
.

The same statements also hold for
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

Proof.

(1
\Rightarrow
2)
Assume that
L\in\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
. Since
L
is in
\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL})
, there exist a function
f\in\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
and a 1npda
M
such that
L=\{x\mid\text{ $M$ accepts $f(x)$ }\}
.
By Lemma
2.1
, there exist a function
g\in\mathrm{FL}
and a polynomially-bounded function
h
satisfying
f(x)=g([\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}])
for all

. We define
K
as the set
\{z\mid\text{ $M$ accepts $g(z)$ }\}
. It then follows that

L=\{x\mid\text{ $M$ accepts $g([\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}])$ }\}=\{x\mid[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}]\in K\}=K[h]
.

(2
\Rightarrow
1)
We assume that
L=K[h]
for a polynomially-bounded advice function

and a language
K\in\mathrm{LOGCFL}
.
Take a function
f\in\mathrm{FL}
and a language
A\in\mathrm{CFL}
satisfying

. We define

h(|x|)\end{subarray}])
for all

.
By Lemma
2.1
,

is in
\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
. It then follows that
L=\{x\mid[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}]\in K\}=\{x\mid g(x)\in A\}
. Thus,

is in
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.
∎

Let us return to the proof of Lemma
2.4
.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

Hereafter, we intend to verify that
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}=\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL}/\mathrm{poly})
. Notice that the deterministic case is similarly proven. Since
\mathrm{CFL}\subseteq\mathrm{CFL}/\mathrm{poly}

\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL}/\mathrm{poly})

For the other inclusion, let

denote any language in
\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL}/\mathrm{poly})

Take an advised-

-m-reduction function

, and an advised-1npda

starts with input string

.

Since

,

is polynomially bounded, and thus there exists a polynomial

satisfying

for all

.
By Lemma
2.1
, there exists a function

and a polynomially-bounded advice function

Take another polynomial
p_{2}
for which
\ell(n)\leq p_{2}(n)
for all

.
In a way similar to the proof of Lemma

\hat{h}(n)=h(0)\natural h(1)\natural\cdots\natural h(p_{2}(n))

as
\{[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\

w\end{subarray}]\wedge\text{ $M$ accepts $(g([\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\

We define another advice function

for any

\Box

2.3
and

leave unstated the use of another plausible complexity class

Concerning

Lemma 2.6

\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
if and only if
\mathrm{LOGCFL}\subseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

Proof.

The implication from left to right is trivial since
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
is properly included in
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
. Conversely, assume that
\mathrm{LOGCFL}\subseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
. Let
L
denote any language over alphabet
\Sigma
in
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
. By Lemma
2.5
, there are an advice alphabet
\Theta
, a polynomially-bounded advice function

, and a language

satisfying

. We then apply Lemma

for

and obtain an advice alphabet

2.5
Two-Way Auxiliary Pushdown Automata

With the use of reductions in
\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
, we have introduced the advised complexity classes
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
in Section
2.4
. Here, we provide another characterization of them
with no use
of advised
\mathrm{L}
-m-reduction. This will be quite useful in the proof of our main theorem (Theorem
4.1
) in Section
4
.
Recall that
Sudborough
[
15
]
characterized
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
(as well as
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}
) in terms of Cook’s auxiliary pushdown automata
[
3
]
. A
two-way nondeterministic auxiliary pushdown automaton
(or an aux-2npda, for short) is an extension of a 2npda by attaching an additional two-way rewritable work tape.
As demonstrated in
[
15
]
, a language
L
belongs to
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
iff there exists an aux-2npda that recognizes
L
in polynomial time using logarithmic work space.
We further expand such an aux-2npda by augmenting Karp-Lipton style advice as follows. For the sake of convenience, we call such a machine an
advised-aux-2npda
. An advised-aux-2npda uses an extra read-only tape called an
advice tape
on which an advice string is written. Note that all tape heads of the advised-aux-2npda can move in two directions.

Given an advised-aux-2npda
M
, an advice function
h
, and a language
L
, we say that
M

recognizes
L
with the help of

h
if (i)
M
takes standard input
x
and advice string
h(|x|)
and (ii) for any
x
, if
x\in L
, then
M
accepts, and otherwise,
M
rejects.

The following is an advice version of Sudborough’s characterization of
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
in terms of aux-2npda’s.

Lemma 2.7

Let
L
be any language. The following statements are logically equivalent.

1.

L\in\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

2.

There exist a polynomial-time, logarithmic-space advised-aux-2npda
M
and a polynomially-bounded advice function
h
such that
M
recognizes
L
with the help of
h
.

The same statements hold for
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
as well.

Proof.

(1
\Rightarrow
2)
This follows from
[
15
]
, in which
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
is characterized by logarithmic-space aux-2npda’s running in polynomial time. Let
K
denote any language in
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
. By Lemma
2.5
, there exist a language
K
in
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
and an advice function
h
for which
L=K[h]
. Since
K\in\mathrm{LOGCFL}
, by
[
15
]
, there exists an aux-2npda
M
that recognizes
K
in polynomial time using logarithmic work space.
We thus conclude that, if
x\in L
, then
M
accepts
[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}]
, and otherwise,
M
rejects
[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}]
.
Notice that
M
uses a single input tape, which is made up of two tracks. We split these two tracks of the input tape of
M
into two separate tapes, one of which is an advice tape for an advice string. We denote by
N
the obtained machine. Clearly,
N
is an advised-aux-2npda and recognizes
L
with the help of
h
.

(2
\Rightarrow
1)
Assume that
L
is recognized by a certain advised-aux-2npda
M
with a polynomially-bounded advice function
h
in polynomial time using logarithmic space.
Recall that
M
has both an input tape and an advice tape with two separate tape heads along them
other than an auxiliary tape as well as a stack.
Since all tape heads of
M
freely move in two directions, it is possible to treat an input tape and an advice tape as two tracks of a single input tape equipped with a single tape head. It is important to note that this modification requires additional
O(\log|x|)
memory bits to remember the locations of the two tape heads of the input and the advice tapes. We therefore obtain a new aux-2npda
N^{\prime}
that takes an input of the form
[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}]
and simulates
M
on the pair
(x,h(|x|))
of input strings.
We define
f(x)=[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}]
for any
x
and set
K=\{z\mid\text{ $N^{\prime}$ accepts $z$ }\}
. By the characterization of
[
15
]
,
K
belongs to
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
. Since
L=K[h]
, Lemma
2.5
concludes that
L
is in
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

\Box

2.6
Families of Promise Problems and Stack-State Complexity Classes

Given an alphabet
\Sigma
, a
promise decision problem
over
\Sigma
is a pair
(A,B)
of sets satisfying that
A,B\subseteq\Sigma^{*}
and
A\cap B=\varnothing
, where
A
is viewed as a set of “positive” instances and
B
represents a set of “negative” instances of the promise decision problem.
Naturally, we expand a single promise decision problem to a “family” of promise decision problems over a single alphabet.
Fix an alphabet
\Sigma
and let
{\cal L}=\{(L^{(+)}_{n},L^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
denote such a family of promise decision problems over
\Sigma
. It is important to remark that
\Sigma
does not depend on the choice of
n
(see, e.g.,
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
).
All strings in
\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(L^{(+)}_{n}\cup L^{(-)}_{n})
are distinguished as
valid
or
promised
strings.
At this moment, we demand neither
L^{(+)}_{n}\cap L^{(+)}_{m}=\varnothing
nor
L^{(-)}_{n}\cap L^{(-)}_{m}=\varnothing
for any distinct pair
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We say that
{\cal L}
has a
polynomial ceiling
if there exists a polynomial
r
satisfying
L^{(+)}_{n}\cup L^{(-)}_{n}\subseteq\Sigma^{\leq r(n)}
for all indices
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

To solve a family
{\cal L}=\{(L^{(+)}_{n},L^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of promise decision problems, we use a “family” of pushdown automata. Such a family is expressed as
{\cal M}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where each
M_{n}
has the form
(Q_{n},\Sigma,{\{\vdash,\dashv\}},\Gamma_{n},\delta_{n},q_{0},\bot,Q_{acc,n},Q_{rej,n})
with
\delta_{n}:(Q_{n}-Q_{halt,n})\times\check{\Sigma}_{\lambda}\times\Gamma\to{\cal P}(Q_{n}\times\Gamma^{\leq e_{n}})
, where
Q_{halt,n}=Q_{acc,n}\cup Q_{rej,n}
.
This machine family
{\cal M}
is said to
solve

{\cal L}
if, for any index
n\in\mathbb{N}
, (i) for all
x\in L^{(+)}_{n}
,
M_{n}
accepts
x
and (ii) for all
x\in L^{(-)}_{n}
,
M_{n}
rejects
x
. For any other string outside of
L^{(+)}_{n}\cup L^{(-)}_{n}
,
M_{n}
may possibly neither accept nor reject it.
moreover, we do not demand any “uniformity” of
{\cal M}
, that is, any existence of a fixed algorithmic procedure that generates from
1^{n}
the description of each machine
M_{n}
.

In Section
1
, we have already discussed families of 2nfa’s and 2dfa’s of polynomial state complexities.
In stark contrast to the state complexity of 2nfa’s and 2dfa’s, we need to consider the
stack-state complexity
of 2npda’s and 2dpda’s because we can reduce the number of inner states of pushdown automata at will by increasing their stack alphabet size.
In this work, we are interested in families of 2npda’s and 2dpda’s having polynomial stack-state complexities. Analogously to the nonuniform classes
2\mathrm{N}
and
2\mathrm{D}
, we introduce two complexity classes,
\mathrm{2NPD}
and
\mathrm{2DPD}
, where the suffix “PD” stands for “pushdown”.

Definition 2.8

The nonuniform stack-state complexity class
\mathrm{2NPD}
is composed of all nonuniform families of promise decision problems solvable by appropriate families of 2npda’s whose stack-state complexities are bounded from above by a fixed polynomial. Moreover,
\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
consists of all families in
\mathrm{2NPD}
that have polynomial ceilings. In a similar manner, we define
\mathrm{2DPD}
using 2dpda’s instead of 2npda’s.

If we use 1npda’s and 1dpda’s in place of 2npda’s and 2dpda’s, we analogously obtain
\mathrm{1NPD}
and
\mathrm{1DPD}
, respectively. These nonuniform complexity classes will be extensively discussed in Section
5
.

3
Parameterizations of LOGCFL/poly and LOGDCFL/poly

Toward the main goal of this work, we intend to parameterize the complexity classes
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}
as well as their advised counterparts
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

3.1
Parameterized Complexity Classes

Similarly to
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{L}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{NL}/\mathrm{poly}
defined in
[
21
,
22
]
, we wish to seek proper “parameterizations” of
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
, including
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}
as their special cases.
For readability, we will follow the basic terminology used in
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
. A
parameterized decision problem
is a pair
(L,m)
of a language
L
and a size parameter
m
. We are particularly interested in size parameters computable using logarithmic space.
A
log-space size parameter

m
is a function from
\Sigma^{*}
to
\mathbb{N}
for a given alphabet
\Sigma
for which its associated function mapping a string
x\in\Sigma^{*}
to the string of the form
1^{m(x)}\in\{1\}^{*}
belongs to
\mathrm{FL}

[
18
]
; that is, there is a DTM
M
(equipped with a read-only input tape, a rewritable work tape, and a write-once output tape) such that, for any string
x\in\Sigma^{*}
,
M
takes
x
as an input and produces
1^{m(x)}
on its output tape in
|x|^{O(1)}
time using
O(\log|x|)
work space.

How can we define
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
in a reasonable and systematic way? Since
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
are defined in terms of
\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
-functions in Definition
2.2
, we first need to look for a natural parameterization of
\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
-functions. Parameterizations of logarithmic-space computation was also discussed in, e.g.,
[
2
,
4
]
.
Given a function
f:\Sigma^{*}\to\Gamma^{*}
for two alphabets
\Sigma
and
\Gamma
and a size parameter
m
, the pair
(f,m)
belongs to
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
if
m
is logarithmic-space computable and there exists an advised DTM
M
, an advice function
h:\mathbb{N}\to\Theta^{*}
for an advice alphabet
\Theta
, and a polynomial
p
such that
M
takes an input string
x
on its input tape and an advice string
h(|x|)
on its advice tape, and
M
produces
f(x)
on its output tape within time
p(m(x)|x|)
using space
O(\log{m(x)|x|})
, provided that
h
satisfies
|h(|x|)|\leq p(m(x)|x|)
.
With the use of
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
, we introduce
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}({\cal C})
for each language family
{\cal C}
as the collection of all parameterized problems
(L,m)
such that there exist a function
f
and a language
A\in{\cal C}
satisfying: (i)
(f,m)\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
and (ii)
L=\{x\mid f(x)\in A\}
.
When we do not use advice, we naturally obtain
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{FL}
as a special case of
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

Definition 3.1

The parameterized complexity classes
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
are defined as
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL})
and
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{DCFL})
, respectively.

Is there any a close relationship between
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and appropriately-parameterized advised-aux-2npda’s?
We answer this question by proving the following characterization lemma.

Lemma 3.2

Let
(L,m)
be any parameterized problem. The following three statements are logically equivalent.

1.

(L,m)\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

2.

There exists an advice function
h
and an advised-aux-2npda
M
such that
M
takes any input
x
and an advice string
h(|x|)
of length
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
and then correctly determines whether or not
x\in L
in time
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
using space
O(\log{(m(x)|x|)})
, where
x
is a “symbolic” input.

3.

There exist an aux-2npda
M
(with no advice tape) and an advice function
h
such that
L=L(M)[h]
, where each advice string
h(|x|)
has length
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
and
M
runs in time
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
using space
O(\log{(m(x)|x|)})
, where
x
is a “symbolic” input.

The same statements hold for
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and advised-aux-2dpda’s.

Proof.
We note that the following argument works also for
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and advised-aux-2dpda’s.

(1
\Rightarrow
2) Assume that
(L,m)\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
. Take a parameterized function
(f,m)\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
and a language
A\in\mathrm{CFL}
satisfying
L=\{x\mid f(x)\in A\}
. We choose an advice function
h
and a DTM
M
such that
M
takes input string
x
on an input tape and advice string
h(|x|)
of length
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
on an advice tape and produces
f(x)
in time
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
using space
O(\log{(m(x)|x|)})
. We also take a 1npda
N
recognizing
A
.

We wish to construct an aux-2npda
F
as follows. On input
w
of the form
[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}]
, we simulate
N
step by step.
For any
i\in[|w|]
, whenever
N
tries to scan the
i
th tape cell of
N
’s input tape, we first run

on

to compute the
(i+1)
th symbol of
f(x)
, and then we simulate one step of
N
’s move. The execution time of
F
is obviously
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
since so is
M
. The space usage of
F
is
O(\log{(m(x)|x|)})
. Since
F
correctly solves
L
, (2) is true.

(2
\Rightarrow
3) Take
(h,M)
given in (2). We combine an input tape and an advice tape of
M
into a single tape made up of two separate tracks.
To simulate the two tape heads of the original tapes of
M
by a single tape head, we need to use extra
O(\log(m(x)|x|))
bits to remember the head locations. We denote by
N
the resulting machine.
By the definition of
N
, it follows that
L(M)
coincides with
L(N)[h]
.

(3
\Rightarrow
1) Take an advice function
h
and an aux-2npda
M
given in (2).
Write
K
for
L(M)
. Note that
L=\{x\mid[\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}]\in K\}
. By Sudborough’s characterization
[
15
]
,

belongs to
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
(
=\mathrm{LOG}(\mathrm{CFL})
). Take a function
f\in\mathrm{FL}
and a language
A\in\mathrm{CFL}
for which
K=\{z\mid f(z)\in A\}
. We then define
g(x)=f([\>\begin{subarray}{c}x\\
h(|x|)\end{subarray}])
for any
x
.
It suffices to verify that
(g,m)
is in
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
. Since length
|h(|x|)|
is
O(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
,

h(|x|)\end{subarray}])
can be computed in time
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
and space

(g,m)
belongs to
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{FL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

\Box

3.2
Polynomially-Honest Size Parameters

A log-space size parameter
m
is, by definition, polynomially bounded but not necessarily polynomially honest.
Here, we wish to pay special attention to polynomially-honest log-space size parameters and their associated parameterized decision problems.
For convenience, we use the notation
\mathrm{PHSP}
to mean the collection of all parameterized decision problems whose size parameters are polynomially honest (but not necessarily logarithmic-space computable).

Concerning the role of
\mathrm{PHSP}
, let us demonstrate the following property, stated as Proposition
3.3
, which will turn out to be crucial in proving a key proposition (Proposition
4.3
) in Section
4.2
. This property bridges between
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
(resp.,
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
) and its parameterization
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
(resp.,
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
) if its associated log-space size parameters are restricted to be polynomially-honest.

Proposition 3.3

\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\cap\mathrm{PHSP}\subseteq\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
if and only if
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

To prove this proposition, we utilize Lemmas
2.5
,
2.7
, and
3.2
as well as Sudborough’s important characterizations of
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}
in terms of polynomial-time aux-2npda’s and aux-2dpda’s working with only logarithmic space
[
15
]
.

Proof of Proposition
3.3
.

(If – part) Assume that
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
. Take an arbitrary parameterized decision problem
(L,m)
from
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\cap\mathrm{PHSP}
.
Since
(L,m)\in\mathrm{PHSP}
, there exists a constant

satisfying

for all
x

By Lemma
3.2
, there exist an advice function
h
and an advised-aux-2npda
N
such that

and
N
runs in time
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}\subseteq|x|^{O(1)}
and space
O(\log{(m(x)|x|)})\subseteq O(\log|x|)
with the help of

whose length
|h(|x|)|
is upper-bounded
by
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}\subseteq|x|^{O(1)}
.
Instead of giving a fixed advice string

, we provide an arbitrary string to the lower track of an single input tape of

and then obtain the non-advised language

. Write
K
for this language

for simplicity.

Using Sudborough’s characterization of
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
in terms of logarithmic-space aux-2npda’s
[
15
]
,

belongs to
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
.
Since

by Lemma

. Our assumption then derives
L\in\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}

is polynomially honest, we conclude that
(L,m)\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

(Only If – part) We start with the assumption of
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\cap\mathrm{PHSP}\subseteq\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
. Take any language

in
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and set

for all

. Notice that
m_{bin}

.

By Lemma
2.7
, there exist a polynomially-bounded function
h
and a polynomial-time, logarithmic-space advised-aux-2npda
N
satisfying
L=L(N)[h]
.
Consider the parameterized decision problem
(L,m_{bin})
. Since
N
solves
L
with the help of
h
in time
(m_{bin}(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
using space
O(\log{(m_{bin}(x)|x|)})
,

(L,m_{bin})
belongs to
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\cap\mathrm{PHSP}
.
Our assumption thus implies that
(L,m_{bin})\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.
Since
m_{bin}(x)=|x|
, Lemmas
2.7
and
3.2
for
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and

\Box

4
A Road to the LOGCFL
\subseteq
LOGDCFL/poly Question

In Sections
2
–
3
, we have already discussed fundamental properties necessary to prove the following main theorem of this work: two characterizations of the
\mathrm{LOGCFL}\subseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
question in terms of nonuniform stack-state complexity through the parameterizations of
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

Theorem 4.1

The following three statements are logically equivalent.

1.

\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{2DPD}
.

2.

\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\cap\mathrm{PHPS}\subseteq\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

3.

\mathrm{LOGCFL}\subseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

The proof of the above theorem is quite involved with the proper use of the parameterizations of
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
discussed in Section
3
.
In Section
4.1
, we will give the formal proof of Theorem
4.1
, which requires a supporting statement (Proposition
4.3
) verified specifically in Section
4.2
.

4.1
How to Verify Theorem
4.1

Our goal of the rest of this section is to prove Theorem
4.1
. To accomplish this goal, another key statement, Proposition
4.3
, is needed. To describe the proposition, nonetheless, we need to explain important terminology from
[
21
]
.

Given a promise decision problem
(L,m)
over alphabet
\Sigma
, we define
L^{(+)}_{n}=L\cap\Sigma^{(n)}
and
L^{(-)}_{n}=\overline{L}\cap\Sigma^{(n)}
, where
\Sigma^{(n)}
denotes
\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\mid m(x)=n\}
.
This special notation
\Sigma^{(n)}
is intentionally used here to differentiate it from
\Sigma^{n}
(
=\{x\in\Sigma^{*}\mid|x|=n\}
). We further define
{\cal L}=\{(L^{(+)}_{n},L^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. This family
{\cal L}
is said to be
induced from

(L,m)
.

On the contrary, given two families
{\cal L}=\{(L^{(+)}_{n},L^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\hat{{\cal L}}=\{(\hat{L}^{(+)}_{n},\hat{L}^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of promise decision problems,
\hat{{\cal L}}
is called an
extension
of
{\cal L}
if
L^{(+)}_{n}\subseteq\hat{L}^{(+)}_{n}
and
L^{(-)}_{n}\subseteq\hat{L}^{(-)}_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. When the set
\{1^{n}\#x\mid n\in\mathbb{N},x\in L^{(+)}_{n}\cup L^{(-)}_{n}\}
belongs to
\mathrm{L}
,
{\cal L}
is said to be
\mathrm{L}
-good
. Finally, a collection
{\cal F}
of families of promise problems is
\mathrm{L}
-good
if every element in
{\cal F}
has an
\mathrm{L}
-good extension in
{\cal F}
.
Given an
\mathrm{L}
-good family
{\cal L}=\{(L^{(+)}_{n},L^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of promise decision problems over alphabet
\Sigma
, we define
K^{(+)}_{n}=\{1^{n}\#x\mid x\in L^{(+)}_{n}\}
and
K^{(-)}_{n}=\{1^{n}\#x\mid x\notin L^{(+)}_{n}\}\cup\{z\#x\mid z\in\Sigma^{n}-\{1^{n}\},x\in(\Sigma_{\#})^{*}\}\cup\Sigma^{n}
, where
\Sigma_{\#}=\Sigma\cup\{\#\}
. We further define
K=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K^{(+)}_{n}
, from which
\overline{K}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}K^{(-)}_{n}

immediately follows. We then define
m:(\Sigma_{\#})^{*}\to\mathbb{N}
by setting
m(w)=n
if
w=1^{n}\#x
for a certain string
x\in L^{(+)}_{n}\cup L^{(-)}_{n}
, and
m(w)=|w|
otherwise. The obtained parameterized problem
(K,m)
is said to be
induced from

{\cal L}
.

Example 4.2

As a concrete example, we intend to demonstrate that
\mathrm{2NPD}
is
\mathrm{L}
-good. Take an arbitrary family
{\cal L}=\{(L_{n}^{(+)},L_{n}^{(-)})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of promise problems over alphabet
\Sigma
in
\mathrm{2NPD}
. Consider a family
{\cal M}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of polynomial-size 2npda’s that solves
{\cal L}
. Let
\Sigma^{(n)}=L_{n}^{(+)}\cup L_{n}^{(-)}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Although
M_{n}
is not even required to halt on inputs outside of
\Sigma^{(n)}
, we can easily modify

to halt on all of its computation paths on any input.
We then define

is recognized deterministically using only logarithmic space, and thus

\mathrm{L}
.

O(\log{(m(x)|x|)})\subseteq O(\log|x|)
with advice strings
h(|x|)
of size
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
, where
\Theta
is an alphabet for an auxiliary work tape. Let
e
denote the push size of
N
.
We wish to define a family
{\cal M}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of polynomial-size 2npda’s for
{\cal L}
since this concludes that
{\cal L}\in\mathrm{2NPD}
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
arbitrarily and let us define the desired machine
M_{n}
. For convenience, we prepare a pair
(l,w)
, which expresses a content
w
of an auxiliary work tape and a location
l
of its tape head.
The
M_{n}
’s inner states are of the form
(q,l,w)
for
q\in Q
,
l\in[0,k\log{n}]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
w\in\Gamma^{k\log{n}}
for a sufficiently large constant
k\geq 1
.
On input
x\in\Sigma^{(n)}
and advice string
z=h(|x|)
,
M_{n}
tries to simulate
N
on
(x,z)
in such a way that, whenever
N
changes
q
to
q^{\prime}
and
(l,w)
to
(l^{\prime},w^{\prime})
in a single step,
M_{n}
changes its inner state from
(q,l,w)
to
(q^{\prime},l^{\prime},w^{\prime})
accordingly. Since
|Q\times[0,k\log{n}]_{\mathbb{Z}}\times\Gamma^{k\log{n}}|=n^{O(1)}
,
M_{n}
has polynomially many inner states together with a constant size of
\Gamma^{\leq e}
, and thus
{\cal M}
has polynomial stack-state complexity.
Therefore,
{\cal L}
belongs to
\mathrm{2NPD}
. Since
\Sigma^{(n)}=\Sigma^{(m(x))}\subseteq\Sigma^{\leq p(|x|)}
,
we further conclude that
{\cal L}\in\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
.

Conversely, we assume that
{\cal L}\in\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
. Our goal is to obtain
(L,m)\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\cap\mathrm{PHSP}
.
Let us take a family
{\cal M}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of polynomial-size 2npda’s solving
{\cal L}
. For each index
n\in\mathbb{N}
, assume that
M_{n}
has the form
(Q_{n},\Sigma,{\{\vdash,\dashv\}},\Gamma_{n},\delta_{n},\bot,q_{0},Q_{n,acc},Q_{n,rej})
with push size
e_{n}
.
Note that there exist two polynomials
p
and
s
such that
L_{n}^{(+)}\cup L_{n}^{(-)}\subseteq\Sigma^{\leq s(n)}
and
|Q_{n}|\leq p(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. This yields
|x|\leq s(m(x))
for all
x
since
\Sigma^{(n)}=L_{n}^{(+)}\cup L_{n}^{(-)}
.
As a result,
(L,m)\in\mathrm{PHSP}
follows.
Next, we wish to simulate all 2npda’s
M_{n}
using an appropriate advised-aux-2npda, say,
N
and an appropriate advice function
h
to ensure that
(L,m)
is indeed in
\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

Since each
M_{n}
may use completely different sets
Q_{n}
and
\Gamma_{n}
, we first need to “identify”
Q_{n}
and
\Gamma_{n}
as sets of strings; i.e.,
Q_{n}=\{0,1\}^{k(n)}
and
\Gamma_{n}=\{0,1\}^{l(n)}
for polynomials
k(n)
and
l(n)
, where
q_{0}=0^{k(n)}
and
\bot=0^{l(n)}
.
This helps us switch
\Gamma_{n}
to a new stack alphabet
\Gamma=\{0,1,\#,\bot\}
of fixed size. Take another polynomial
r
satisfying
2^{l(n)(e_{n}+1)}\leq r(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We modify
M_{n}
to design a new machine
M^{\prime}_{n}
as follows. For readability, we intentionally use multiple auxiliary work tapes for
M^{\prime}_{n}
because it is possible to combine those tapes into a single one with extra
O(\log{|x|})
bits to remember the locations of work-tape heads.
As for an inner state
q\in Q_{n}
, we translate it into the corresponding
k(n)
-bit string

. We use the first auxiliary work tape of
O(\log{n})
space to store

in the form of

.
Next, we translate a stack content
\bot a_{1}\cdots a_{m}
into
\bot\#w(a_{1})\#w(a_{2})\cdots\#w(a_{m})
, where each symbol

of
l(n)
symbols together with the designated separator

.
One step of the simulation of
M_{n}
goes as follows. We scan a stack to remove a topmost block of symbols

into the 2nd work tape.
We scan both the 1st and the 2nd work tapes to recover
(q,a)
and apply a transition of
\delta_{n}
.
Whenever

replaces symbol

by

in a single step, we change it into
\#w(b_{1})\#w(b_{2})\cdots\#w(b_{m^{\prime}})
.
If the 2nd work tape does not become empty, then we push a series of newly written symbols back into the stack starting with
\#
.
This newly obtained machine
M^{\prime}_{n}
uses a transition function of the form
\delta^{\prime}_{n}:(\{0,1\}^{k(n)}-Q_{halt,n})\times\check{\Sigma}_{\lambda}\times\Gamma\times\Theta_{1}\times\Theta_{2}\to{\cal P}(\{0,1\}^{k(n)}\times\Gamma^{\leq\lceil\log{r(n)}\rceil}\times\Theta_{1}\times\Theta_{2})
, where
\Theta_{1}
and
\Theta_{2}
are appropriate alphabets used for the 1st and the 2nd auxiliary work tapes.

Furthermore, we translate
\delta^{\prime}_{n}
into a

, in which each row is indexed by
(q,\sigma,a,\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in\{0,1\}^{k(n)}\times\check{\Sigma}_{\lambda}\times\Gamma\times\Theta_{1}\times\Theta_{2}
and each column enumerates all values in

in a predetermined order. We take an appropriate encoding of this transition table into a single string

of length proportional to

so that we can recover necessary transitions
at any time simply by scanning this advice string

from left to right by a two-way tape head.
This provides an advised-aux-2npda
that simulates
M^{\prime}_{m(x)}
on input
x
in time
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
and space
O(\log{(m(x)|x|)})
with the help of
h
. In the end, we obtain
(L,m)\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
.

(2) We start with an
\mathrm{L}
-good family
{\cal L}=\{(L^{(+)}_{n},L^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of promise decision problems and

denotes the parameterized decision problem with a log-space size parameter

, which is induced from
{\cal L}
.

Assuming that
(K,m)\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}\cap\mathrm{PHSP}
, we aim at showing that
{\cal L}\in\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
.
Lemma
3.2
implies the existence of an advice function

and an advised-aux-2npda

that together solve
(L,m)
in time
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
and space
O(\log(m(x)|x|))
using advice strings
h(|x|)
of size
(m(x)|x|)^{O(1)}
.
Moreover, we assume that an auxiliary work tape of
N
has space at most
k\log{(m(x)|x|)}+k
for a fixed constant

on all inputs

. Since

is induced from
{\cal L}
, it follows that
m(w)=n
if

and

, and
m(w)=|w|
otherwise.
Since

is polynomially honest, we take a polynomial
p

|x|\leq p(m(x))
for all

. Therefore, if

, then
|1^{n}\#x|\leq p^{\prime}(n)
holds, where
p^{\prime}(n)=n+p(n)+1
.

Let us define a family
{\cal M}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of 2npda’s for
{\cal L}
as follows. To cope with the auxiliary work tape of
N
, we treat a pair
(l,w)
of a tape content

and a tape head location

as a part of extra inner states of
M_{n}
.
Each advice string
h(|w|)
is embedded into the inside of
M_{n}
as part of inner states.
Since
(K,m)\in\mathrm{PHSP}
,
x\in L^{(+)}_{n}\cup L^{(-)}_{n}
implies
|x|\leq p(m(x))=p(n)
. On input
x
,
M_{n}
reads

and simulates
N
on
(w,h(|w|))
with
w=1^{n}\#x
.
This is possible because
h(|1^{n}\#x|)
consists of at most
p(n)
symbols if

. If

changes

at any step, then

changes its associated inner states. This shows that
{\cal M}
solves
{\cal L}
correctly. Thus,
{\cal L}\in\mathrm{2NPD}
follows.
Since
|1^{n}\#x|\leq p^{\prime}(n)
for all

, we further conclude that
{\cal L}\in\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
.

To show the converse, we assume that
{\cal L}\in\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
. There is a polynomial
t
such that, for any

and

,

implies
|x|\leq t(n)
. By the definition of
m
, we obtain
|x|\leq t(m(1^{n}\#x))
. Thus,
m
is polynomially honest.
Take a family
{\cal M}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of polynomial-size 2npda’s solving
{\cal L}
.
Our goal is to show that
(K,m)\in\mathrm{para}\mbox{-}\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
by constructing an appropriate advised-aux-2npda, say,
N
.
In a way similar to (1), for each index

,
we encode all transitions of

into a single string, say,

.
Let

. We then define an advice string

. The desired machine

5
Case of One-Way Models

We have discussed the two nonuniform stack-state complexity classes
\mathrm{2NPD}
and
\mathrm{2DPD}
in Section
4
.
Unfortunately, it still remains open whether or not
\mathrm{2NPD}
coincides with
\mathrm{2DPD}
and
\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
is included in
\mathrm{2DPD}
. However, when we move away from the two-way model of polynomial-size families of pushdown automata
to the
one-way model
(that is, 1npda’s and 1dpda’s), it is actually possible to prove the clear difference between determinism and nondeterminism.

We begin with formally defining
\mathrm{1DPD}
and
\mathrm{1NPD}
. The class
\mathrm{1NPD}
is composed of all families of promise problems
{\cal L}=\{(L^{(+)}_{n},L^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that there exist families of 1npda’s
{\cal M}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for which each
M_{n}
solves
(L^{(+)}_{n},L^{(-)}_{n})
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The deterministic counterpart of
\mathrm{1NPD}
is denoted by
\mathrm{1DPD}
.
With these notations, we claim that
\mathrm{1DPD}
and
\mathrm{1NPD}
are indeed different. In the following two propositions, we actually show that
1\mathrm{N}
and
\mathrm{1DPD}
are
incomparable
. Since
1\mathrm{D}\subseteq\mathrm{1DPD}
and
1\mathrm{N}\subseteq\mathrm{1NPD}
, this incomparability leads to the desired separation
\mathrm{1DPD}\neq\mathrm{1NPD}
.

Proposition 5.1

\mathrm{1DPD}\nsubseteq 1\mathrm{N}
.

Proof.

We define
{\cal PAL}=\{(Pal^{(+)}_{n},Pal^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
over the ternary alphabet
\Sigma=\{0,1,\#\}
by setting
Pal^{(+)}_{n}=\{w\#w^{R}\mid w\in\{0,1\}^{n}\}
(even-length marked palindromes) and
Pal^{(-)}_{n}=\Sigma^{2n+1}-Pal^{(+)}_{n}
.
In what follows, we claim that (1)
{\cal PAL}\in\mathrm{1DPD}
and (2)
{\cal PAL}\notin 1\mathrm{N}
.

(1) Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
arbitrarily. Assuming
x=u\#v
for two strings
u,v\in\{0,1\}^{*}
, we first push
u
into a stack with the help of the separator
\#
and then check that
v
matches
u^{R}
by popping
u
from the stack in the reverse order. At the same time, we check that
|u|=n
by using polynomially many inner states. It is possible to check whether
x
is of the form
u\#v
for
u,v\in\{0,1\}^{*}
without using any stack. Thus, the entire procedure can be implemented on an appropriate 1dpda. As a consequence,
{\cal PAL}
belongs to
\mathrm{1DPD}
.

(2) The following argument loosely follows
[
11
]
by way of contradiction. Assume that there is a family
{\cal M}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of 1nfa’s having polynomial state complexity that solves
{\cal PAL}
. Let
p
denote an appropriate polynomial such that
M_{n}
has at most
p(n)
inner states for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Fix
n
arbitrarily and consider the
\{0,1\}^{\leq n}\times\{0,1\}^{\leq n}
matrix
A
whose indices
(u,v)
are of the form with
u,v\in\{0,1\}^{\leq n}
and their entries are
1
if
u\#v\in Pal^{(+)}_{n}
, and
0
otherwise. This matrix
A
is clearly a diagonal matrix (according to an appropriate index ordering). For each index
u\in\{0,1\}^{\leq n}
, we fix an accepting computation path of
M_{n}
on
u\#u
and denote by
q_{u}
an inner state that
M_{n}
takes while
M_{n}
’s tape head is crossing the boundary between
u\#
and
u
.

Since there are
2^{n+1}
diagonal entries in
A
and
M_{n}
has at most
p(n)
inner states, there must be two distinct indices
u_{1},u_{2}\in\{0,1\}^{\leq n}
for which
q_{u_{1}}=q_{u_{2}}
. Let us consider the input string
u_{1}\#u_{2}^{R}
. Since
q_{u_{1}}=q_{u_{2}}
,
M_{n}
must accepts
u_{1}\#u_{2}^{R}
, implying
u_{1}=u_{2}
. However, this contradictions
u_{1}\neq u_{2}
. Therefore,
{\cal PAL}
is outside of
1\mathrm{N}
.
∎

Proposition 5.2

1\mathrm{N}\nsubseteq\mathrm{1DPD}
.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that
\mathrm{co}\mbox{-}\mathrm{1DPD}=\mathrm{1DPD}
by exchanging between accepting states and rejecting states of underlying 1dpda’s. Thus, it suffices to show that
\mathrm{co}\mbox{-}1\mathrm{N}\nsubseteq\mathrm{1DPD}
.

Our example family of promise problems is
{\cal DUP}=\{(Dup^{(+)}_{n},Dup^{(-)}_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
(duplication) over the binary alphabet
\Sigma=\{0,1\}
, where

Dup^{(+)}_{n}=\{ww\mid w\in\Sigma^{n}\}

and
Dup^{(-)}_{n}=\Sigma^{2n}-Dup^{(+)}_{n}
.
To separate
\mathrm{co}\mbox{-}1\mathrm{N}
from
\mathrm{1DPD}
, we wish to show that (1)
{\cal DUP}
belongs to
\mathrm{co}\mbox{-}1\mathrm{N}
and (2)
{\cal DUP}
is not in
\mathrm{1DPD}
.

(1) Given each promise problem
(Dup^{(+)}_{n},Dup^{(-)}_{n})
, we design a machine
M_{n}
as follows. On input
x
of the form
uv
with
u,v\in\{0,1\}^{n}
,
universally
(i.e., co-nondeterministically) choose indices
i\in[|u|]
and check that
u_{(i)}=v_{(i)}
. Splitting
x
into
u
and
v
is possible by
M_{n}
using polynomially many inner states. This machine universally accepts the input
x
exactly when
x
is in
Dup^{(+)}_{n}
. Therefore,
{\cal DUP}\in\mathrm{co}\mbox{-}1\mathrm{N}
follows.

(2) We show this assertion by way of contradiction. Assume that
{\cal DUP}
is in
\mathrm{1DPD}
and take a family
{\cal M}=\{M_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of 1dpda’s that solves
{\cal DUP}
with a set
Q_{n}
of inner states. Furthermore, we choose a polynomial
p
satisfying
|Q_{n}|\leq p(n)
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is possible to assume that, without loss of generality, that
M_{n}
empties its stack at the end of computation and that there is only one accepting state, say,
q_{acc}
.

Let us consider configurations of each machine
M_{n}
. We define the set
A_{n}
to be
\{(x,q_{1},y,q_{2},z)\mid xyz\in Dup^{(+)}_{n},\exists\gamma[(q_{0},{\vdash{xyz}\dashv},\bot)\vdash^{*}(q_{1},{yz\dashv},\gamma)\vdash^{*}(q_{2},{z\dashv},\gamma)\vdash^{*}(q_{acc},\lambda,\bot)]\}
, provided that
M_{n}
’s stack height does not go below
|\gamma|
while reading
y
. Given
(q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
with
q_{1},q_{2}\in Q_{n}
and
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in[0,n+1]_{\mathbb{Z}}
, we set
B_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}=\{(x,q_{1},y,q_{2},z)\in A_{n}\mid|x|=\ell_{1},|y|=\ell_{2}\}
.
We claim that (*) there exists a quadruple
(q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
satisfying
|B_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}|\geq 2
. If this is true, then we take such a quadruple
(q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
and two distinct elements
(x_{1},q_{1},y_{1},q_{2},z_{1})
and
(x_{2},q_{1},y_{2},q_{2},z_{2})
from
B_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
. By the definition,
M_{n}
must accept the inputs
x_{1}y_{1}z_{1}
and
x_{2}y_{2}z_{2}
. Because of
B_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
,
x_{1}y_{2}z_{1}
and
x_{2}y_{1}z_{2}
are also accepted by
M_{n}
, implying
x_{1}y_{2}z_{1},x_{2}y_{1}z_{2}\in Dup_{n}^{(+)}
. However, this is impossible.
As a consequence, we obtain
{\cal DUP}\notin\mathrm{1DPD}
.

We still need to prove the pending claim (*). Assume otherwise; namely,
|B_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}|\leq 1
for any quadruple
(q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
. We define a partial function
g
by setting
g(q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=(x,y,z)
if
(x,q_{1},y,q_{2},z)\in B_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}
, and
g(q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
is undefined otherwise. Note that the size of the domain
dom(g)
is at most
|Q_{n}|^{2}(n+1)^{2}
. This implies that
|\bigcup_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}B_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}|\leq p(n)^{2}(n+1)^{2}<2^{n}
for a sufficiently large
n
. This contradicts the fact that
|\bigcup_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}B_{q_{1},q_{2},\ell_{1},\ell_{2}}|\geq 2^{n}
because
g
must produce all triplets
(x,y,z)
satisfying
xyz\in Dup^{(+)}_{n}
.

\Box

6
A Brief Discussion and Future Directions

Throughout this work, we have expanded to pushdown automata the scope of “nonuniform state complexity” classes based on finite automata, such as
2\mathrm{D}
and
2\mathrm{N}
, which were initiated in the 1970s by Berman and Lingas
[
1
]
and Sakoda and Sipser
[
14
]
.
We have introduced the notion of nonuniform stack-state complexity and have defined two important complexity classes
\mathrm{2DPD}
and
\mathrm{2NPD}
using 1dpda’s and 2npda’s, respectively.

As a main theorem, we have established an exact relationship between the
\mathrm{LOGCFL}\subseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
question and the
\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{2DPD}
question by way of introducing a reasonable “parameterization” of
\mathrm{LOGCFL}
and
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
. This relationship ensures the importance of the study of nonuniform stack-state complexity classes in automata theory. Using the one-way machine models of 1dpda’s and 1npda’s, we have defined two more natural complexity classes
\mathrm{1DPD}
and
\mathrm{1NPD}
. Unlike the case of two-way models, we have shown that
\mathrm{1DPD}\neq\mathrm{1NPD}
.

For the sake of the avid reader, we intend to raise a few open problems associated with the results of this work.

1.

The most important question left open in this work is the
\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}\subseteq\mathrm{2DPD}
question. At this moment, we speculate that
\mathrm{2NPD}/\mathrm{poly}
is not included in
\mathrm{2DPD}
, implying
\mathrm{LOGCFL}\nsubseteq\mathrm{LOGDCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
by Theorem
4.1
, but there seems no apparent evidence that strongly supports our speculation. Therefore, it would be desirable to find such an evidence to ensure the correctness of our speculation. Another relevant open question is whether or not
\mathrm{2DPD}\neq\mathrm{2NPD}
holds.

2.

Numerous nonuniform state complexity classes based on the one-way machine models are discussed in
[
21
]
but not all relationships among these classes are determined. We wonder how
\mathrm{1DPD}
and
\mathrm{1NPD}
fit into the landscape of all such complexity classes. Can we prove clear separations of
\mathrm{1DPD}
and
\mathrm{1NPD}
from those complexity classes?

3.

Lately, the language families, denoted by
\mathrm{LOG}k\mathrm{SDA}
for all indices
k\geq 2
, based on deterministic depth-
k
storage automata were introduced in
[
24
]
to extend
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}
. It is proven in
[
24
]
that
\mathrm{LOGDCFL}\subseteq\mathrm{LOG}k\mathrm{SDA}\subseteq\mathrm{SC}^{k}
for any
k\geq 2
, where
\mathrm{SC}^{k}
is the
k
th Steve’s class. Is it possible to expand our main result (Theorem
4.1
) to these intriguing classes?

4.

We have defined
\mathrm{LOGCFL}/\mathrm{poly}
as
\mathrm{LOG}/\mathrm{poly}(\mathrm{CFL})
in Section
2.4
. However, we have left open the question of whether
\mathrm{LOG}(\mathrm{CFL}/\mathrm{poly})
coincides with

On Modular Gray Map
Manish K Gupta

Abstract
This paper introduces an isometry between the modular rings ℤ 2 s {\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s}} and ℤ 2 s − 1 {\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s-1}} with respect to the homogeneous weights. Certain product of these maps gives Carlet’s generalised Gray map and also Vega’s Gray map. For s = 2 s=2 this reduces to popular Gray map. Several interesting properties of these maps are studied. Towards the end we list several interesting problems to work on.



Keywords:
Codes over rings, Gray isometry, Gray map.

Preliminaries And Notations

A
code

{\cal C},
of length
n
, over
\;{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}
is simply a
subset of
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}^{n}
. If in addition it is an additive
subgroup of
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}^{n}
we call
{\cal C}
a
linear code
of
length
n
. An element of
{\cal C}
is called a
codeword of

{\cal C}
and a
generator matrix
of a linear code
{\cal C}
is a
matrix whose rows generate
{\cal C}
. The
Hamming weight

w_{H}(x)
of a vector
x
in
\mbox{\msbm Z}_{p^{s}}^{n}
is the number of
non-zero components. The
Lee weight

w_{L}(x)
of a
vector
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
is
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min\{|x_{i}|,|p^{s}-x_{i}|\}
. The
Euclidean weight

w_{E}(x)
of a vector
x
is
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\min\{x_{i}^{2},(p^{s}-x_{i})^{2}\}
. The Euclidean weight is useful in connection
with lattice constructions. The
Chinese Euclidean weight

w_{CE}(x)
of a vector
x\in{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{m}^{n}
is
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\{2-2\;\mbox{cos}\;\left(\frac{2\pi x_{i}}{m}\right)\right\}
.
This is useful for
\!m\!-\!PSK
coding. In
[
9
]
, Constantinescu
and Werner introduced the
homogeneous weight
on the residue class
rings of integers which was later extended to finite Frobenius rings.
The homogeneous weight on
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}
is defined as follows. For
\;u\neq 0,u\in{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}

w_{HW}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}&p^{s-2}(p-1),u\notin p^{s-1}{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}\\
&p^{s-1},u\in p^{s-1}{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}\end{array}\right.\;\mbox{and}\;w_{HW}(0)=0.

The Hamming, Lee, Euclidean and Homogeneous distances
d_{H}(x,y)
,
d_{L}(x,y)
,

d_{E}(x,y)
and
d_{HW}(x,y)
between two vectors
x
and
y
are

w_{H}(x-y)
,
w_{L}(x-y)
,
w_{E}(x-y)
and
w_{HW}(x-y)
respectively. The minimum
Hamming, Lee, Euclidean and Homogeneous weights,
d_{H},d_{L}
,
d_{E}
and
d_{HW}

of
{\cal C}
are the smallest Hamming, Lee, Euclidean and Homogeneous weights
among all non-zero codewords of
{\cal C},
respectively.

Carlet’s defenition of generalized Gray map can be extended
to
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}
naturally. The
Generalized Gray map

\gamma:\;{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}^{n}\rightarrow\;{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p}^{p^{s-1}n}
is
the coordinate-wise extension of the function
\gamma_{g}

from
\;{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}
to
\;{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p}^{p^{s-1}}
defined as follows.
Let
u=u_{1}+pu_{2}+\cdot+p^{s-1}u_{s}\in{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}
be
the finite
p
-adic expansion of
u
, where
u_{i}\in{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p}

also let
y_{i}\in{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p}
. The map
\gamma_{g}:(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{s-1})\mapsto u_{s}+\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}u_{i}y_{i}\in{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p}
defined as a generalized Boolean function
evaluated on
GF(p^{s-1})
. It is easy to see that this
map is equivalent to the generalized Gray map introduced
by Greferath and Schmidt
[
14
]
using tensor product.
In particular for
s=2
this map is also equivalent
to a generalized Gray map from
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{2}}
to
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p}^{p}

introduced recently by Asch and Tilborg
[
5
]
. The image

\gamma({\cal C})
, of a linear code
{\cal C}
over
\;{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}

of length
n
by the generalized Gray map, is a
p
-ary
code of length
p^{s-1}n
. The
dual code

{\cal C}^{\perp}

of
{\cal C}
is defined as
\{x\in{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{p^{s}}^{n}\mid x\cdot y=0\;\mbox{for all}\;y\in{\cal C}\}
where
x\cdot y
is the standard inner product of
x
and
y
.
{\cal C}

is
self-orthogonal
if
{\cal C}\subseteq{\cal C}^{\perp}
and
{\cal C}

is
self-dual
if
{\cal C}={\cal C}^{\perp}
. Two codes are said
to be
equivalent
if one can be obtained from the other
by permuting the coordinates and (if necessary) changing the
signs of certain coordinates. Codes differing by only a
permutation of coordinates are called
permutation-equivalent
.

{\cal C}
over

Modular Gray Map

Now we define a natural Gray map as follows.

Let

\begin{array}[]{ccc}A_{1}&=&\{0,1,2,3,\ldots,2^{s-2}-1\},\\
A_{2}&=&\{2^{s-2},2^{s-2}+1,\ldots,2^{s-1}-1\},\\
A_{3}&=&\{2^{s-1},2^{s-1}+1,\ldots,2^{s-1}+2^{s-2}-1\}\;\mbox{and}\\
A_{4}&=&\{2^{s-1}+2^{s-2},2^{s-1}+2^{s-2}+1,\ldots,2^{s-1}+2^{s-1}-1\}\end{array}

be the four consecutive disjoint partitions of the
integers of
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s}}
. Define the modular generalized Gray map

\eta^{s}_{g}
on
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s}}
to
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s-1}}
as

\eta^{s}_{g}(i)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(i,i)&i\in A_{1}\\
(i-2^{s-2},i)&i\in A_{2}\\
(i-2^{s-2},i-2^{s-2})&i\in A_{3}\\
(i-2^{s-1},i-3\cdot 2^{s-2})&i\in A_{4}\end{array}\right.

{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s}}

2

]
generated by the
following generator matrix

0&2^{s-2}\end{array}\right]_{2\times 2}.

For

Theorem 1

The generalized Gray map of Carlet
\Phi_{G}:{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s}}\mapsto{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2}
can written as a product of
s-1
modular Gray maps i.e,

\Phi_{G}=\eta^{s}_{g}\eta^{s-1}_{g}\eta^{s-2}_{g}\ldots\eta^{2}_{g}
.

Proof.
By remark
1
, if we apply the modular gray map
\eta^{s}_{g}
to the
2
-basis
generator matrix of the first order Reed Muller code over
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s}}
we get the
2
-basis
generator matrix of the first order Reed Muller code over
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s-1}}
which is the image set of

\eta^{s-1}_{g}
and so on. Thus successive applications yields Carlet’s generalized Gray map

[
15
,
16
]
.

\Box

Proposition 1

The modular Gray map
\eta^{s}_{g}
is an isometry between
{\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s}}\mapsto({\mbox{\msbm Z}}_{2^{s-1}})^{2}

Proof.

Notes on the norm of pre-Schwarzian derivatives on bi-univalent functions of order α \alpha
H. Mahzoon and R. Kargar

Abstract
. In the present paper we estimate the norm of the pre-Schwarzian derivative of bi-starlike functions of order α \alpha where α ∈ [ 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in[0,1) . Initially this problem was handled by Rahmatan et al. in [Bull Iran Math Soc 43 : 1037-1043, 2017]. We pointed out that the proofs and bounds by Rahmatan et al. are incorrect and present correct proofs and bounds.

30C45

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{H}
be the family of analytic functions
f
on the open unit disc
\Delta=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
and
\mathcal{A}
be a subclass of
\mathcal{H}
that its members are normalized by the condition
f(0)=0=f^{\prime}(0)-1
. This means that each
f\in\mathcal{A}
has the following form

(1.1)

f(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\quad(z\in\Delta).

We denote by
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{A}
the family of univalent (one–to–one) functions
f
in
\Delta
. Let
f
and
g
belong to the class
\mathcal{H}
. A function
f
is called to be
subordinate
to
g
, written as

f(z)\prec g(z)\quad{\rm or}\quad f\prec g,

if there exists a Schwarz function
\phi:\Delta\rightarrow\Delta
with
the following properties

\phi(0)=0\quad{\rm and}\quad|\phi(z)|<1\quad(z\in\Delta),

such that
f(z)=g(\phi(z))
for all
z\in\Delta
. If

g\in\mathcal{U}
, then the following geometric equivalence
relation holds true:

f(z)\prec g(z)\Leftrightarrow f(0)=g(0)\quad{\rm and}\quad f(\Delta)\subset g(\Delta).

It is known that the
Koebe
function

k(z):=\frac{z}{(1-z)^{2}}=z+2z^{2}+3z^{3}+\cdots+nz^{n}+\cdots\quad(z\in\Delta),

maps the open unit disc
\Delta
onto the entire plane minus the interval
(-\infty,-1/4]
.
Also, the well-known
Koebe One-Quarter Theorem
states that the image of the open unit disc
\Delta
under every function
f\in\mathcal{U}
contains the disc
\{w:|w|<\frac{1}{4}\}
, see
[
3
, Theorem 2.3]
. Therefore every function
f
in the class
\mathcal{U}
has an inverse
f^{-1}
which satisfies the following conditions:

f^{-1}(f(z))=z\quad(z\in\Delta)

and

f(f^{-1}(w))=w\quad(|w|<r_{0}(f);\ \ r_{0}(f)\geq 1/4),

where

(1.2)

f^{-1}(w)=w-a_{2}w^{2}+(2a_{2}^{2}-a_{3})w^{3}-(5a_{2}^{3}-5a_{2}a_{3}+a_{4})w^{4}+\cdots=:g(w).

A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is
bi-univalent
in
\Delta
if, and only if, both
f
and
f^{-1}
are univalent in
\Delta
. We denote by
\Sigma
the class of bi-univalent functions in
\Delta
. The functions

f_{1}(z)=\frac{z}{1-z},\quad f_{2}(z)=-\log(1-z)\quad{\rm and}\quad f_{3}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right),

with the corresponding inverse functions, respectively

f_{1}^{-1}(w)=\frac{w}{1+w},\quad f_{2}^{-1}(w)=\frac{\exp(w)-1}{\exp(w)}\quad{\rm and}\quad f_{3}^{-1}(w)=\frac{\exp(2w)-1}{\exp(2w)+1},

belong to the class
\Sigma
.

A function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is called starlike (with respect to
0
) if
tw\in f(\Delta)
whenever
w\in f(\Delta)
and
t\in[0,1]
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}^{*}
the class of the starlike functions in
\Delta
. Also, we say that a function
f\in\mathcal{A}
is starlike of order
\alpha
(
0\leq\alpha<1
) if, and only if,

(1.3)

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}>\alpha\quad(z\in\Delta).

The class of the starlike functions of order
\alpha
in
\Delta
is denoted by

\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
. Now by definition of the starlike functions of order
\alpha
we recall the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1)
. A function
f\in\Sigma
given by (
1.1
) is said to be in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{\Sigma}(\alpha)
if the conditions (
1.3
) and

(1.4)

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{wg^{\prime}(w)}{g(w)}\right\}>\alpha\quad(w\in\Delta),

hold true where
w=f(z)
and
g=f^{-1}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{V}(\alpha)
the class of all
f\in\mathcal{A}
satisfying the following condition

(1.5)

{\rm Re}\left\{\left(\frac{z}{f(z)}\right)^{2}f^{\prime}(z)\right\}>\alpha\quad(0\leq\alpha<1,z\in\Delta).

The class
\mathcal{V}(\alpha)
was considered by Obradović and Ponnusamy in
[
5
]
. Now we recall the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1)
. A function
f\in\Sigma
given by (
1.1
) is said to be in the class
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}(\alpha)
if the conditions (
1.5
) and

(1.6)

{\rm Re}\left\{\left(\frac{w}{g(w)}\right)^{2}g^{\prime}(w)\right\}>\alpha\quad(w\in\Delta),

hold true where
w=f(z)
and
g=f^{-1}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{LU}
the subclass of
\mathcal{H}

consisting of all locally univalent functions, namely,
\mathcal{LU}:=\{f\in\mathcal{H}:f^{\prime}(z)\neq 0,\ \ z\in\Delta\}
. For a
f\in\mathcal{LU}
the pre–Schwarzian and Schwarzian derivatives of
f
are defined as follows

T_{f}(z):=\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\quad{\rm and}\quad S_{f}(z):=T^{\prime}_{f}(z)-\frac{1}{2}T^{2}_{f}(z),

respectively. We note that the quantity
T_{f}
(resp.
S_{f}
) is analytic on
\Delta
precisely when
f
is analytic (resp. meromorphic) and locally univalent on
\Delta
. Since
\mathcal{LU}
is a vector space over
\mathbb{C}
(see
[
4
]
), thus we can define the norm of
f\in\mathcal{LU}
by

||f||=\sup_{z\in\Delta}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|.

This norm has significance in the theory of Teichmüller spaces, see
[
1
]
. It is known that
||f||<\infty
if and only if
f
is uniformly locally univalent. Also, notice that if
||f||\leq 1
, then
f
is univalent in
\Delta
and conversely, if
f
univalent in
\Delta
, then
||f||\leq 6
and the equality is attained for the Koebe function and its rotation. In fact, both of these bounds are sharp, see
[
2
]
. Also, if
f
is starlike of order
\alpha\in[0,1)
, then we have the sharp estimate
||f||\leq 6-4\alpha
(see e.g.
[
7
]
).

The following theorems were wrongly proven by Rahmatan et al.
[
6
, Theorem 2.2 and Theorem 2.4]
.

Theorem A.
Let the function
f(z)
given by (
1.1
) be in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{\Sigma}(\alpha)
where
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Then

(1.7)

||f||\leq\min\{6-4\alpha,4\alpha+2\}.

Theorem B.
Let the function
f(z)
given by (
1.1
) be in the class
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}(\alpha)
where
\alpha\in[0,1)
. Then

(1.8)

||f||\leq\min\{10-8\alpha,6-8\alpha\}.

We notice that
6-8\alpha\in(-2,6]
when
\alpha\in[0,1)
.

In the present paper we give a correct proof for Theorem A and some remarks on the Theorem B.

2.
On the Theorem A

The correct version of Theorem A is contained in the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\alpha\in[0,1)
.
If the function
f
of the form (
1.1
) is in the class
\mathcal{S}^{*}_{\Sigma}(\alpha)
, then

(2.1)

||f||\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}6,&\hbox{$\alpha=0$;}\\
\min\left\{6-4\alpha,\frac{4(1-\alpha)}{\alpha}\right\},&\hbox{$0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}$;}\\
4,&\hbox{$\alpha=1/2$;}\\
2,&\hbox{$1/2<\alpha<1$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}_{\Sigma}(\alpha)
be given by (
1.1
). Then by Definition
1.1
the following conditions hold true:

(2.2)

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}>\alpha\quad(z\in\Delta)

and

(2.3)

{\rm Re}\left\{\frac{wg^{\prime}(w)}{g(w)}\right\}>\alpha\quad(w\in\Delta),

where
w=f(z)
and
g=f^{-1}
. Since (
2.2
) holds true by
[
7
]
we conclude that
||f||\leq 6-4\alpha
where
\alpha\in[0,1)
. On the other hand, since the mapping

(2.4)

F(w):=\frac{1+(1-2\alpha)w}{1-w}\quad(w\in\Delta)

maps
\Delta
onto the right half-plane having real part greater than
\alpha
, thus (
2.3
) implies that

(2.5)

\frac{wg^{\prime}(w)}{g(w)}\prec F(w)\quad(w\in\Delta).

By the identity
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}w}f^{-1}(w)=\frac{1}{f^{\prime}(z)}
(see
[
6
, Eq. (1.2)]
) (
2.5
) is equivalent to

(2.6)

\frac{f(z)}{zf^{\prime}(z)}\prec\frac{1+(1-2\alpha)z}{1-z}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Now by (
2.6
) and definition of subordination there exists a Schwarz function
\phi
with
\phi(0)=0
and
|\phi(z)|<1

(z\in\Delta)
such that

\frac{f(z)}{zf^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z)}{1-\phi(z)}\quad(z\in\Delta),

or equivalently

(2.7)

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\frac{1-\phi(z)}{z(1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z))}\quad(0\neq z\in\Delta).

Taking logarithm on both sides of (
2.7
) and differentiating, after simplification, we obtain

(2.8)

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=\frac{2(\alpha-1)\phi(z)}{z(1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z))}+\frac{2(\alpha-1)\phi^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\phi(z))(1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z))}\quad(0\neq z\in\Delta).

The well-known Schwarz-Pick lemma states that for a Schwarz function
\phi
the following inequality holds true:

(2.9)

|\phi^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{1-|\phi(z)|^{2}}{1-|z|^{2}}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Now by (
2.9
) and since
|\phi(z)|\leq|z|
(see
[
3
]
), the relation (
2.8
) implies that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{2(\alpha-1)\phi(z)}{z(1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z))}+\frac{2(\alpha-1)\phi^{\prime}(z)}{(1-\phi(z))(1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z))}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|\alpha-1||\phi(z)|}{|z|(1-|1-2\alpha||\phi(z)|)}+\frac{2|\alpha-1||\phi^{\prime}(z)|}{(1-|\phi(z)|)(1-|1-2\alpha||\phi(z)|)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{1-|1-2\alpha||z|}+\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{1-|1-2\alpha||z|}.\frac{1+|z|}{1-|z|^{2}}.

However we get

\displaystyle||f||

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\Delta}(1-|z|^{2})\left|\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{z\in\Delta}\left\{\frac{2(1-\alpha)(1-|z|^{2})}{1-|1-2\alpha||z|}+\frac{2(1-\alpha)(1+|z|)}{1-|1-2\alpha||z|}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{4(1-\alpha)}{1-|1-2\alpha|}=:\varphi(\alpha).

Now we consider the following cases.

Case 1.

\alpha=0,1/2
. In this case we have
\varphi(\alpha)=\infty
. Therefore

\min\{6-4\alpha,\varphi(\alpha)\}=6-4\alpha.

Case 2.

0<\alpha<1/2
. In this case
\varphi(\alpha)
becomes
\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{\alpha}\in(2,\infty)
. Also we have
6-4\alpha\in(2,4)
when
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
. Thus

||f||\leq\min\left\{6-4\alpha,\frac{4(1-\alpha)}{\alpha}\right\}.

Case 3.

1/2<\alpha<1
. In this case we have
\varphi(\alpha)=2
. Thus

\min\{6-4\alpha,\varphi(\alpha)\}=2.

Because
2<6-4\alpha
when
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
On the Theorem B

In this section we give some remarks on the Theorem B. Let a function
f
be of the form (
1.1
) belongs to the class
\mathcal{V}_{\Sigma}(\alpha)
. Then the conditions (
1.5
) and (
1.6
) hold true. From the geometric meaning of the function
(1+(1-2\alpha)z)/(1-z)
, subordination principle and (
1.5
) we have

\left(\frac{z}{f(z)}\right)^{2}f^{\prime}(z)\prec\frac{1+(1-2\alpha)z}{1-z}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Using definition of subordination there exists a Schwarz function
\phi
so that

(3.1)

\left(\frac{z}{f(z)}\right)^{2}f^{\prime}(z)=\frac{1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z)}{1-\phi(z)}\quad(z\in\Delta).

With a simple calculation (
3.1
) yields

(3.2)

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=2\left(\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-\frac{1}{z}\right)+\frac{(1-2\alpha)\phi^{\prime}(z)}{1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z)}+\frac{\phi^{\prime}(z)}{1-\phi(z)}\quad(z\in\Delta).

Again, from (
3.1
) we get

(3.3)

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}-\frac{1}{z}=\frac{f(z)}{z}\left(\frac{1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z)}{z(1-\phi(z))}-1\right)\quad(z\in\Delta).

By substituting (
3.3
) into (
3.2
), we have

(3.4)

\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}=2\frac{f(z)}{z}\left(\frac{1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z)}{z(1-\phi(z))}-1\right)+\frac{(1-2\alpha)\phi^{\prime}(z)}{1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z)}+\frac{\phi^{\prime}(z)}{1-\phi(z)}\quad(z\in\Delta).

To estimate the above relationship (
3.4
), we need to estimate
|f(z)/z|
, but so far this is an open question. So this question seems to be more difficult than what is given in
[
6
]
. Also, we remark that in
[
6
, proof of Theorem 2.4]
Rahmatan et al. mistakenly used the following relation

\frac{f^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}=\frac{1+(1-2\alpha)\phi(z)}{z(1-\phi(z))}

and this means that
f\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\alpha)
. Thus Theorem 2.4 and its proof in
[
6
]
is incorrect.

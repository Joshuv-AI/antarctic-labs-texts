On some properties of weak solutions to the Maxwell equations
Joachim Naumann

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with weak solutions { e , h } ∈ L 2 × L 2 \{e,h\}\in L^{2}\times L^{2} of the time-dependent Maxwell equations. We show that these solutions obey an energy equality. Our method of proof is based on the approximation of { e , h } \{e,h\} by its Steklov mean with respect to time t. This approximation technique is well-known for establishing integral estimates for weak solutions of parabolic equations. In addition we prove the uniqueness of { e , h } \{e,h\} .

Let
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

The evolution of an electromagnetic field in a medium at rest occupying the region

1.2.
Balance of electromagnetic energy

Let

We multiply scalarly (

Appendix I
The Steklov mean of an
L^{p}
function

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 2
) be any open set, let
0<T<+\infty
and put
Q_{T}=\Omega\times]0,T[
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(Q_{T})
(
1\leq p<+\infty
).
We extend
f
by zero a.e. onto
\Omega\times]T,+\infty[
and denote this function by
f
again.
For
\lambda>0
, the function

\displaystyle f_{\lambda}(x,t)\,=\,\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{t}^{t+\lambda}f(x,s)\,\mathrm{d}s\hskip 11.74988pt\text{for a.e. }(x,t)\in Q_{T}.

is called
Steklov mean of
f
.

Proposition I.1

For every
f\in L^{p}(Q_{T})
and every
\lambda>0
there holds

I.1
.1

\displaystyle\left\lVert f_{\lambda}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(Q_{T})}\,\leq\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(Q_{T})}
;

I.1
.2

\displaystyle\int\limits_{Q_{T}}f_{\lambda}(x,t)\partial_{t}\zeta(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\,=\,-\,\frac{1}{\lambda}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}(f(x,t+\lambda)-f(x,t))\zeta(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for every
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{T})
, i.e.,
f_{\lambda}
possesses the weak derivative

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f_{\lambda}(x,t)\,=\,\frac{1}{\lambda}(f(x,t+\lambda)-f(x,t))

for a.e.
(x,t)\in Q_{T}
.

I.1
.3

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}f(x,t)\left(\int_{t-\lambda}^{t}\alpha(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\,=\,\int\limits_{Q_{T}}f_{\lambda}(x,t)\alpha(t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

for any
\alpha\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\alpha(t)=0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[t_{0},t_{1}]

(
0<t_{0}<t_{1}<T
depending on
\alpha
)
.

Proposition I.2

For every
f\in L^{p}(Q_{T})
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\left\lVert f_{\lambda}-f\right\rVert_{L^{p}(Q_{T})}\,=\,0.

Prop.
.1
and Prop.
I.2
are special cases of well-known results about the mollification of
L^{p}
-functions.
The properties of the Steklov mean presented in Prop.
.2
,
.3
are used in
[
14
, Chap. III, §2, Lemma 2.1]
to establish energy inequalities for weak solutions of parabolic equations.
For reader’s convenience we present the proofs.

Proof of Proposition
.2
.

Let
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(Q_{T})
.
Then

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{Q_{T}}f_{\lambda}(x,t)\frac{\partial\zeta}{\partial t}(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\,=\,\lim_{h\to 0}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}f_{\lambda}(x,t)\frac{1}{h}(\zeta(x,t-h)-\zeta(x,t))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

On the other hand, for
0<h<\lambda
we have

\displaystyle\int\limits_{Q_{T}}f_{\lambda}(x,t)\frac{1}{h}(\zeta(x,t-h)-\zeta(x,t))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{\lambda h}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\int_{t+\lambda}^{t+\lambda+h}f(x,s)\,\mathrm{d}s-\int_{t}^{t+h}f(x,s)\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\zeta(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{\lambda h}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\int_{t+\lambda}^{t+\lambda+h}(f(x,s)-f(x,t+\lambda))\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\zeta(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 11.74988pt\,-\frac{1}{\lambda h}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}\left(\int_{t}^{t+h}(f(x,s)-f(x,t))\,\mathrm{d}s\right)\zeta(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\hskip 11.74988pt\,+\frac{1}{\lambda}\int\limits_{Q_{T}}(f(x,t+\lambda)-f(x,t))\zeta(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Here, the first and the second term on the right hand side converge to zero when
h\to 0
.
This can be easily seen by combinig Fubini’s theorem and continuity of
f
with respect to the integral mean.

Whence the claim.

Proof of Proposition

\lambda>0

Introduction

Many probabilistic models, estimating likelihoods of certain events, are based on the sequence of sums
\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},\,n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
, where
X_{i}
are some random variables. This sequence is called
the random walk
(r. w.). Random walks are usually visualized as branching trees or certain paths in plane or space; their occurrence spreads from pure mathematics to many applied sciences. For instance, one may refer to Case–Shiller home pricing index
[

arises is insurance mathematics. In ruin theory one may assume that insurer’s wealth
W(n)
in discrete time moments

consists of incoming cash premiums and outgoing payoffs (claims), and
W(n)
admits the following representation:

\displaystyle W(n)=u+\kappa n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}=u-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right),

(1)

where

is interpreted as initial surplus

,

denotes the arrival rate of premiums paid by customers and the subtracted sum of random variables represents claims. Here we assume that random variables

are independent, non-negative and integer-valued but not necessarily identically distributed. More precisely,
X_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}X_{i+N}
for all

and some fixed
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The model (

2
Recursive nature of ultimate time survival probability, basic notations and the net profit condition

This section starts with deriving the basic recurrent relation for the ultimate time survival probability. The definition (
4
), the law of total probability and rearrangements imply

\displaystyle\varphi(u)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\{W_{u}(n)>0\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{N}\left\{u+\kappa n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}>0\right\}\cap\bigcap_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\left\{u+\kappa n-\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}>0\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{N}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\leqslant u+\kappa n-1\right\}\cap\bigcap_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\left\{u+\kappa N-\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}+\kappa(n-N)-\sum_{i=N+1}^{n}X_{i}>0\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}\leqslant u+\kappa-1\\
i_{1}+i_{2}\leqslant u+2\kappa-1\\
\vdots\vskip 2.84526pt\\
i_{1}+i_{2}+\ldots+i_{N}\leqslant u+\kappa N-1\end{subarray}}\hskip-14.22636pt\mathbb{P}(X_{1}=i_{1})\mathbb{P}(X_{2}=i_{2})\cdots\mathbb{P}(X_{N}=i_{N})\,\varphi\left(u+\kappa N-\sum_{j=1}^{N}i_{j}\right).

(5)

Substituting
u=0
into the recursive formula (
2
), we notice that in order to find
\varphi(\kappa N)
we need to know the values of
\varphi(0),\,\varphi(1),\,\ldots,\,\varphi(\kappa N-1)
. Moreover, if we know the values of
\varphi(0),\,\varphi(1),\,\ldots,\,\varphi(\kappa N-1)
, the same recurrence (
2
) allows us to calculate
\varphi(u)
for any
u\geqslant\kappa N
by substituting
u=0,\,1,\ldots
there. Thus, as mentioned in the introduction, we only need a way to calculate these initial values.

We now define a series of notations. Recalling that we aim to know the distribution of
\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)
, we define
N
random variables:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{1}:=\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(X_{i}-\kappa)\right)^{+},\,\mathcal{M}_{2}:=\sup_{n\geqslant 2}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{n}(X_{i}-\kappa)\right)^{+},\,\ldots\,,\,\mathcal{M}_{N}:=\sup_{n\geqslant N}\left(\sum_{i=N}^{n}(X_{i}-\kappa)\right)^{+}\hskip-5.69046pt,

(6)

where
x^{+}=\max\{0,\,x\}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
is the positive part function. Obviously, same as
X_{1},\,X_{2},\,\ldots,\,X_{N}
, every random variable
\mathcal{M}_{1},\,\mathcal{M}_{2},\,\ldots,\,\mathcal{M}_{N}
attains the values from the set {0, 1, …}.

Let us denote the probability mass functions of
\mathcal{M}_{j}
, their generators
X_{j}
and the sum
S_{N}=X_{1}+X_{2}+\ldots+X_{N}
:

m_{i}^{(j)}:=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{M}_{j}=i\right),\ \ x_{i}^{(j)}:=\mathbb{P}\left(X_{j}=i\right),\ \ s_{i}^{(N)}:=\mathbb{P}\left(S_{N}=i\right),

(7)

where
j\in\{1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,N\}
and
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Let
F_{X_{j}}(i)
be the distribution function of the random variable
X_{j}
, i. e.

F_{X_{j}}(u)=\mathbb{P}(X_{j}\leqslant u)=\sum_{i=0}^{u}x^{(j)}_{i},\,j\in\{1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,N\},\,u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Also, for the complex number
s\in\mathbb{C}
let us denote the probability-generating function of some non-negative and integer-valued random variable
X

G_{X}(s)=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}s^{i}\mathbb{P}(X=i)=\mathbb{E}s^{X},\,|s|\leqslant 1.

The definition of the survival probability (
4
) and the definition of random variable
\mathcal{M}_{1}
imply

\varphi(u+1)=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{1}\leqslant u)=\sum_{i=0}^{u}m^{(1)}_{i}\textit{ for all }u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(8)

Thus, the survival probability calculation turns into the setup of the distribution function of
\mathcal{M}_{1}
. It is simple to explain the core idea of the paper, i. e. how the probabilities
m_{i}^{(1)}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
are attained. Let us refer to Feller’s book
[
14
, Theorem on page 198]
. The referenced Theorem states that if
N=1
in model (
1
), i. e. the random walk
\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i},\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is generated by independent and identically distributed random variables, which are the copies of
X
, then
(\mathcal{M}_{1}+X-\kappa)^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}\mathcal{M}_{1}
. For arbitrary number of periods
N\in\mathbb{N}
the mentioned distribution property is as follows:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}_{1}+X_{N}-\kappa)^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}\mathcal{M}_{N}\text{ and }(\mathcal{M}_{j}+X_{j-1}-\kappa)^{+}\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{d}}\mathcal{M}_{j-1},\text{ for all }j=2,\,3,\,\ldots,\,N,

(9)

see Lemma
2
in Section
5
below. Metaphorically, the distributions’ equalities in (
9
) mean that the random variables
\mathcal{M}_{1},\,\mathcal{M}_{2},\,\ldots,\,\mathcal{M}_{N}
”can see each other”, and, more precisely, based on the equalities in (
9
), we can set up a system of corresponding equalities of probability-generating functions

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&\mathbb{E}s^{(\mathcal{M}_{1}+X_{N}-\kappa)^{+}}=\mathbb{E}s^{\mathcal{M}_{N}}\\
&\mathbb{E}s^{(\mathcal{M}_{2}+X_{1}-\kappa)^{+}}=\mathbb{E}s^{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\\
&\vdots\\
&\mathbb{E}s^{(\mathcal{M}_{N}+X_{N-1}-\kappa)^{+}}=\mathbb{E}s^{\mathcal{M}_{N-1}}\end{cases}.

(10)

The system (
10
) contains the desired information on
m^{(1)}_{i},\,i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

In general, the random variables
\mathcal{M}_{1},\,\mathcal{M}_{2},\,\ldots,\,\mathcal{M}_{N}

can be extended, i. e.
\lim_{u\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{j}=u)>0
,
j=1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,N
. However,
\lim_{u\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{j}<u)=1
for all
j=1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,N
if
\mathbb{E}S_{N}<\kappa N
, see Lemma
1
in Section
5
below.

The condition
\mathbb{E}S_{N}<\kappa N
is called
the net profit condition
and it is crucially important for the survival probability
\varphi(u)
. Intuitively, an insurer has no chance to survive in long-term activity, if threatening amounts of claims on average are greater or equal to the collected premiums. This can also be well illustrated by the expected value of
W(n)
in (
1
). For instance,

\mathbb{E}W(nN)=u+n(-\mathbb{E}S_{N}+\kappa N)<0

if
\mathbb{E}S_{N}>\kappa N
and
n
is sufficiently large. Consequently, the negative value of
W(n)
is unavoidable. See Theorem
3
in Section
3
below for precise statements on the survival probability
\varphi(u),\,u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
when the net profit condition is violated, i. e.
\mathbb{E}S_{N}\geqslant\kappa N
.

Lemma 1
.

If the net profit condition is satisfied, i. e.
\mathbb{E}S_{N}<\kappa N
, then

\lim_{u\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{j}<u)=1,

for all
j\in\{1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,N\}
.

Proof.

We prove the case
j=1
only and note that the other cases can be proven similarly.

According to the law of large numbers, almost surely

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{N}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i=1}\\
i\equiv 1\,{\rm mod}\,N\end{subarray}}^{n}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)+\ldots+\frac{N}{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{i=1}\\
i\equiv N\,{\rm mod}\,N\end{subarray}}^{n}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\frac{1}{N}\left((\mathbb{E}X_{1}-\kappa)+\ldots+(\mathbb{E}X_{N}-\kappa)\right)=\frac{\mathbb{E}S_{N}-\kappa N}{N}=:-\mu<0.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{j\geqslant n}\left|\frac{1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)+\mu\right|<\frac{\mu}{2}\right)=1.

Consequently, for any arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
, there exists such number
N_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{j=n}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)\leqslant 0\right\}\right)\geqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{j=n}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)<-\frac{\mu}{2}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{j=n}^{\infty}\left\{\left|\frac{1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)+\mu\right|<\frac{\mu}{2}\right\}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{j\geqslant n}\left|\frac{1}{j}\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)+\mu\right|<\frac{\mu}{2}\right)\geqslant 1-\varepsilon

for all
n\geqslant N_{\varepsilon}
.

It follows that for any such
\varepsilon
and any
u\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{1}<u)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)<u\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\left\{\bigcap_{j=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}-1}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)<u\right\}\right\}\cap\left\{\bigcap_{j=N_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)\leqslant 0\right\}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}-1}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)<u\right\}\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{j=N_{\varepsilon}}^{\infty}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)\leqslant 0\right\}\right)-1

\displaystyle\geqslant\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}-1}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{j}\left(X_{i}-\kappa\right)<u\right\}\right)-\varepsilon.

The last inequality implies

\lim_{u\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}_{1}<u)\geqslant 1-\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon>0
is as small as we want and the assertion of Lemma follows.
∎

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa N-1}\varphi(i)\left(\sum_{u=0}^{v}s^{(N)}_{u+\kappa N-i}-\sum_{u=0}^{v}\mu_{i}(u)\right)-\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa N-1}\varphi(i)\sum_{u=i-\kappa N}^{v}s_{u+\kappa N-i}^{(N)}

or

\displaystyle\sum_{i=v+1}^{v+\kappa N}\varphi(i)-\sum_{i=0}^{v+\kappa N}\varphi(i)\left(1-\sum_{u=0}^{v+\kappa N-i}s_{u}^{(N)}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa N-1}\varphi(i)\left(\sum_{u=0}^{\kappa N-i-1}s^{(N)}_{u}+\sum_{u=0}^{v}\mu_{i}(u)\right),

which implies

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt\sum_{i=v+1}^{v+\kappa N}\varphi(i)-\sum_{i=0}^{v+\kappa N}\varphi(i)\mathbb{P}(S_{N}>v+\kappa N-i)=\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa N-1}\varphi(i)\left(\mathbb{P}(S_{N}\leqslant\kappa N-i-1)+\sum_{u=0}^{v}\mu_{i}(u)\right).

(37)

Clearly, the definition of the survival probability (
4
) implies that
\varphi(u)
is a non-decreasing function, i. e.
\varphi(u)\leqslant\varphi(u+1)
for all
u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Thus, there exists a non-negative limit
\varphi(\infty):=\lim_{u\to\infty}\varphi(u)
and
\varphi(\infty)=1
if the net profit condition
\mathbb{E}S_{N}<\kappa N
holds, see Lemma
1
. We now let
v\to\infty
in both sides of (
37
). For the first sum in (
37
) we obtain

\lim_{v\to\infty}\sum_{i=v+1}^{v+\kappa N}\varphi(i)=\lim_{v\to\infty}\left(\varphi(v+1)+\ldots+\varphi(v+\kappa N)\right)=\varphi(\infty)\kappa N,

(38)

and for the second

\displaystyle\lim_{v\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{v+\kappa N}\varphi(i)\mathbb{P}(S_{N}>v+\kappa N-i)=\varphi(\infty)\mathbb{E}S_{N}.

(39)

Indeed, let us recall that
\mathbb{E}X=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(X>i)
, when
X
is some non-negative and integer-valued random variable. Then, the upper bound of (
39
) is

\displaystyle\lim_{v\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{v+\kappa N}\varphi(i)\mathbb{P}(S_{N}>v+\kappa N-i)\leqslant\lim_{v\to\infty}\varphi(v+\kappa N)\sum_{i=0}^{v+\kappa N}\mathbb{P}(S_{N}>v+\kappa N-i)

\displaystyle=\lim_{v\to\infty}\varphi(v+\kappa N)\sum_{i=0}^{v+\kappa N}\mathbb{P}(S_{N}>i)=\varphi(\infty)\mathbb{E}S_{N},

while the lower bound is the same due to inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{v+\kappa N}\varphi(i)\mathbb{P}(S_{N}>v+\kappa N-i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{M}\varphi(i)\mathbb{P}(S_{N}>v+\kappa N-i)+\sum_{i=M+1}^{v+\kappa N}\varphi(i)\mathbb{P}(S_{N}>v+\kappa N-i)

\displaystyle\geqslant\inf_{i\geqslant M+1}\varphi(i)\sum_{i=0}^{v+\kappa N-M-1}\mathbb{P}(S_{N}>i),

where
M
is some fixed and sufficiently large natural number. Thus, when
v\to\infty
, the equality in (
37
) is

\displaystyle\varphi(\infty)(\kappa N-\mathbb{E}S_{N})=\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa N-1}\varphi(i)\left(\mathbb{P}(S_{N}\leqslant\kappa N-i-1)+\sum_{u=0}^{\infty}\mu_{i}(u)\right).

(40)

If
\mathbb{E}S_{N}>\kappa N
, as under the case of consideration, the non-negative right-hand-side of (
40
) implies
\varphi(\infty)=0
and consequently
\varphi(u)=0
for all
u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The first case that
\mathbb{E}S_{N}>\kappa N
makes survival impossible is proved.

Let us now consider the case when
\mathbb{E}S_{N}=\kappa N
and
\mathbb{P}(S_{N}=\kappa N)<1
. If
\mathbb{E}S_{N}=\kappa N
and
\mathbb{P}(S_{N}=\kappa N)<1
, then at least one probability
s_{0}^{(N)},\,s_{1}^{(N)},\,\ldots,\,s_{\kappa N-1}^{(N)}
is larger than zero, because otherwise
\mathbb{E}S_{N}>\kappa N
.
Then, from (
40
),

\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa N-1}\varphi(i)\left(\sum_{u=0}^{\kappa N-i-1}s_{u}^{(N)}+\sum_{u=0}^{\infty}\mu_{i}(u)\right)=0.

(41)

If
s_{0}^{(N)}>0
, then (
41
) implies
\varphi(0)=\varphi(1)=\ldots=\varphi(\kappa N-1)=0
. Using recurrence (
2
) we can show that
\varphi(u)=0
for all
u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
If
s_{0}^{(N)}=0
and
s_{1}^{(N)}>0
, then (
41
) implies that
\varphi(0)=\varphi(1)=\ldots=\varphi(\kappa N-2)=0
and once again, using recurrence (
2
) we can show that
\varphi(u)=0
for all
u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Arguing the same we proceed up to
s_{0}^{(N)}=s_{1}^{(N)}=\ldots=s_{\kappa N-2}^{(N)}=0,\,s_{\kappa N-1}^{(N)}>0
and observe that in all of these cases (
41
) and recurrence (
2
) yields
\varphi(u)=0
for all
u\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Finally, let us consider the case when
\mathbb{P}(S_{N}=\kappa N)=1
. If
S_{N}=\kappa N
with probability one, the random variables
X_{1},\,X_{2},\,\ldots,\,X_{N}
are degenerate, meaning that
X_{i}\equiv c_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,N\}
, where
c_{i}\in\{0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,\kappa N\}
and
c_{1}+c_{2}+\ldots+c_{N}=\kappa N
. Thus, the model (
1
) becomes fully deterministic. Moreover,
W(n)=W(n+N)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and it is sufficient to check if the lowest of values among
W(1),\,\ldots,\,W(N)
is larger than zero.
∎

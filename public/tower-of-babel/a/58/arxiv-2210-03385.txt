Bounds on the Hermite spectral projection operator
Eunhee Jeong, Sanghyuk Lee, Jaehyeon Ryu

Abstract
. We study L p L^{p} – L q L^{q} bounds on the spectral projection operator Π λ \Pi_{\lambda} associated to the Hermite operator H = | x | 2 − Δ H=|x|^{2}-\Delta in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . We are mainly concerned with a localized operator χ E ​ Π λ ​ χ E \chi_{E}\Pi_{\lambda}\chi_{E} for a subset E ⊂ ℝ d E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} and undertake the task of characterizing the sharp L p L^{p} – L q L^{q} bounds. We obtain sharp bounds in extended ranges of p , q p,q . First, we provide a complete characterization of the sharp L p L^{p} – L q L^{q} bounds when E E is away from λ ​ 𝕊 d − 1 \sqrt{\lambda}\mathbb{S}^{d-1} . Secondly, we obtain the sharp bounds as the set E E gets close to λ ​ 𝕊 d − 1 \sqrt{\lambda}\mathbb{S}^{d-1} . Thirdly, we extend the range of p , q p,q for which the operator Π λ \Pi_{\lambda} is uniformly bounded from L p ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) to L q ​ ( ℝ d ) L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}) .

Introduction

Let
H
denote the Hermite operator
-\Delta+|x|^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d\geq 2
. The operator
H
has a discrete spectrum
\lambda\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}+d
, where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. For
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
, let
\Phi_{\alpha}
be the
L^{2}
–normalized Hermite function which is
an eigenfunction of
H
with eigenvalue
2(\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{d})+d
. The set
\{\Phi_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\}
forms an orthonormal basis in
L^{2}
. We consider the spectral projection operator

\displaystyle\Pi_{\lambda}f=\underset{\alpha:d+2|\alpha|=\lambda}{\sum}\langle f,\Phi_{\alpha}\rangle\Phi_{\alpha},

which is the orthogonal projection to the vector space spanned by eigenfunctions with the eigenvalue
\lambda
. Then,
f=\sum_{\lambda\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}+d}\Pi_{\lambda}f
for
f\in L^{2}
.

L^{p}
–
L^{q}
bounds on the spectral projection operators associated to differential operators have been studied by various authors (see, for example,
[
33
,
34
,
37
,
24
,
28
,
20
]
).
Let
\|T\|_{p\to q}
denote the operator norm of an operator
T
from
L^{p}
to
L^{q}
. Concerning the Hermite operator, the bounds

(1.1)

\|\chi_{E}\Pi_{\lambda}\chi_{E}\|_{p\to q}\leq\mathrm{B}(\lambda,p,q)

with suitable subsets
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
has been of interest and studied by some authors.
The estimates are related to Bochner-Riesz summability of the Hermite expansion
[
21
,
41
]
and the unique continuation properties for the parabolic operators
[
10
,
12
]
.
When
E=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we call (
1.1
) a global estimate. In such a case, the bound (
1.1
) with

p=2
and
2\leq q\leq\infty
was studied by Thangavelu
[
40
]
, Karadzhov
[
21
]
, and Koch–Tataru
[
25
]
. Especially, Koch and Tataru obtained the optimal
L^{2}
–
L^{q}
bound for
2\leq q\leq\infty
except
q=2(d+3)/(d+1)
. Recently, the missing endpoint estimate was proved by the authors
[
19
]
for
d\geq 3
.

In this paper, we are mainly concerned with local estimates for the projection
\Pi_{\lambda}
, i.e., the estimate (
1.1
) with bounded sets
E
. As shown in the earlier works
[
40
,
21
,
41
,
25
]
,
\Pi_{\lambda}
exhibits different behaviors across the sphere
\sqrt{\lambda}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}=\{x:|x|=\sqrt{\lambda}\}
.

Estimate over
\sqrt{\lambda}\mathbb{B}

We first consider the case
E
is distanced away from
\sqrt{\lambda}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
Let
\mathbb{B}=\{x:|x|<1/2\}
. In view of the transplantation result due to Kenig, Stanton, and Tomas
[
23

that the

–
L^{q}

The sharp bounds on

–

2.

and
TT^{*}
argument

We make use of an observation in
[
19
, Section 2.1]
.
The Hermite-Schrödinger propagator
e^{-itH}
is given by

(2.1)

e^{-itH}f=\sum_{\lambda\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}+d}e^{-it\lambda}\Pi_{\lambda}f,\quad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Clearly,
e^{it(\lambda-H)}
is periodic in
t
with period
\pi
if
\lambda\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}+d
.
If
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
are eigenvalues of
H
,
\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}\in 2\mathbb{Z}
, so
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{I}e^{i\frac{t}{2}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})}dt

=\delta(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})
whenever
I
is an interval of length
2\pi
. It follows from (
2.1
) that

\Pi_{\lambda}f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi/2}^{3\pi/2}e^{i\frac{t}{2}(\lambda-H)}f\,dt,\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

More details can be found in
[
19
, Section 2.1]
.

2.1.
Decomposition of
\Pi_{\lambda}

Let
\eta_{\circ}
be an even function in
C_{c}^{\infty}((-\pi/2-2^{-7},\pi/2+2^{-7}))
such that
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta_{\circ}(t-j\pi)=1
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, it follows that
\eta_{0}(t):=\eta_{\circ}(t+\pi)+\eta_{\circ}(t)+\eta_{\circ}(t-\pi)+\eta_{\circ}(t-2\pi)=1
on
[-\pi/2,3\pi/2]
.
So, we can write
\Pi_{\lambda}f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-{\pi}/{2}}^{{3\pi}/{2}}\eta_{0}(t)e^{i\frac{t}{2}(\lambda-H)}fdt
.
Since
e^{i(t+\pi)(\lambda-H)}=e^{it(\lambda-H)}
, changing variables we see

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Pi_{\lambda}f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\eta_{\circ}(t)+\eta_{\circ}(t-\pi)\big)e^{i\frac{t}{2}(\lambda-H)}fdt.\end{aligned}

The operator
e^{-itH}
also has an explicit kernel representation based on Mehler’s formula
(e.g., see
[
32
]
and
[
39
, p.11]
). Combining the formula and (
2.2
),
we obtain an integral representation of
\Pi_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
19
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}(t)=(2\pi i\>\sin t)^{-\frac{d}{2}}{e^{i\pi d/4}}(\eta_{\circ}(t)+\eta_{\circ}(t-\pi)).

For
\lambda\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}+d
, set

\displaystyle\phi_{\lambda}(x,y,t)=\frac{\lambda t}{2}+\frac{|x|^{2}+|y|^{2}}{2}\cot t-\langle x,y\rangle\csc t.

Then, for all
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

(2.3)

\Pi_{\lambda}f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\mathfrak{a}(t)\int e^{i\phi_{\lambda}(x,y,t)}f(y)\,dy\,dt.

The function
\mathfrak{a}(t)
has the singularities at
t=0
and
t=\pi
. So, we make decomposition away from them.
Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([2^{-1},2])
be a nonnegative function such that
\sum_{j\geq 0}\psi(2^{j}t)=1
for
t\in(0,\pi/2+2^{-7}]
.
We set

\psi_{j}(t)=\psi(2^{j}t)\eta_{\circ}(t),

and

\psi_{j}^{-}(t)=\psi_{j}(-t),\quad\psi_{j}^{\pm\pi}(t)=\psi_{j}(\pm(t-\pi)).

For a bounded function
\eta
and
\lambda\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}+d
, we consider the operator

(2.4)

\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\eta(t)e^{i\frac{t}{2}(\lambda-H)}dt.

Clearly, the definition makes sense for any real number
\lambda
. By (
2.4
) and the isometry
\|e^{-itH}f\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}
it follows that

(2.5)

\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\|_{2\to 2}\leq 2\|\eta\|_{1}.

Since
\sum_{j}(\psi_{j}+\psi^{-}_{j})=\eta_{\circ}
, using (
2.2
), we now have

(2.6)

\textstyle\Pi_{\lambda}=\sum_{j\geq 0}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]+\sum_{\kappa=-,\pm\pi}\sum_{j\geq 0}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}^{\kappa}].

The decomposition is clearly valid since the right hand side converges to
\Pi_{\lambda}
as a bounded operator on
L^{2}
since (
2.5
) gives
\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim 2^{-j}
and
\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}^{\kappa}]\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim 2^{-j}
,
\kappa=-,\pm\pi.
We recall a symmetric property observed in
[
19
]
.
Note
\phi_{\lambda}(x,y,-t)=-\phi_{\lambda}(x,y,t)
and
\phi_{\lambda}(x,y,\pi\pm t)=(\lambda\pi/2)\pm\phi_{\lambda}(x,-y,t)
.
Considering the kernels of the operators (see
[
19
, (2.8) & (2,9) in p. 5]
), by a simple change of variables one can easily show

\textstyle\|\chi_{E}\sum_{j\geq 0}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\chi_{E}\|_{p\to q}=\|\chi_{E}\sum_{j\geq 0}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}^{\kappa}]\chi_{E}\|_{p\to q},\quad\kappa=-,\pm\pi

whenever
E
is a measurable subset such that
E=-E
.
(See
[
19
, Section 2]
.) Thus, we get the following which reduces the desired estimates for
\Pi_{\lambda}
to those for
\sum_{j}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
E
is a measurable subset such that
E=-E
. Then,

\textstyle\|\chi_{E}\Pi_{\lambda}\chi_{E}\|_{p\to q}\leq 4\|\chi_{E}\sum_{j\geq 0}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\chi_{E}\|_{p\to q}.

The inequality continues to hold when the spaces
L^{p}
and
L^{q}
are replaced by the Lorentz spaces
L^{p,r}
and
L^{q,s}
, respectively.

2.2.
TT^{\ast}
argument: Proof of Theorem
1.6

Lemma
2.3
below allows us to deduce off-diagonal estimates from an
L^{r,1}
–
L^{r^{\prime},\infty}
bound.
The following can be regarded as a variant of the usual
TT^{\ast}
argument (see
[
22
]
).

For
j\geq 0
, we say
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{j}
if
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}\big((-2^{1-j},2^{1-j})\big)
and
|\eta^{(l)}|\leq C_{l}2^{jl}
for some constant
C_{l}
and any
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
In particular, note that
\psi_{j}\in\mathcal{C}^{j}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
b>0
and
1\leq r<r_{b}:={2(b+1)}/(b+2)
. Set

\mathfrak{Q}(b,r)=[(1/2,1/2),(1/2,1/r_{b}^{\prime}),(1/r_{b},1/2),(1/r,1/{r^{\prime}})]\setminus\{(1/r,1/{r^{\prime}})\}.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a measurable set and suppose

(2.7)

\|\chi_{E}\Pi_{\lambda}[\tilde{\eta}]\chi_{E}\|_{L^{r,1}\to L^{r^{\prime},\infty}}\lesssim\beta^{\delta(r,r^{\prime})}2^{(-1+(b+1)\delta(r,r^{\prime}))j},\quad j\geq 0

holds whenever
\tilde{\eta}\in\mathcal{C}^{j}
and
\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\eta}\subset(2^{-1-j},2^{1-j})
.
Then, for
j\geq 0
and
(1/p,1/q)\in\mathfrak{Q}(b,r)
we have

(2.8)

\|\chi_{E}\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\chi_{E}\|_{p\to q}\lesssim\beta^{\delta(p,q)}2^{(-1+(b+1)\delta(p,q))j}

if
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{j}
and
\operatorname{supp}\eta\subset(2^{-1-j},2^{1-j})
.

Proof.

By (
2.5
) we have
\|\chi_{E}\Pi_{\lambda}[\tilde{\eta}]\chi_{E}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim 2^{-j}
. The estimate (
2.7
) and interpolation give

(2.9)

\|\chi_{E}\Pi_{\lambda}[\tilde{\eta}]\chi_{E}\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\lesssim\beta^{\delta(p,p^{\prime})}2^{j(-1+(b+1)(\frac{2}{p}-1))},\quad r<p\leq 2

whenever
\tilde{\eta}\in\mathcal{C}^{j}
and
\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\eta}\subset(2^{-1-j},2^{1-j})
.
Thus, it is sufficient to show (
2.8
) with
q=2
and
p=r_{b}
because the other estimates follow by duality and interpolation.
We claim that the estimate

(2.10)

\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\chi_{E}f\|_{2}\lesssim 2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\beta^{\frac{1}{r_{b}}-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{r_{b}}

holds.
The inequality clearly implies (
2.8
) with
q=2
and
p=r_{b}
.

By (
2.4
) we note

\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\chi_{E}f\|_{2}^{2}=\langle\chi_{E}\iint\eta(t)\eta(s)e^{i\frac{t-s}{2}(\lambda-H)}\chi_{E}fdsdt,f\rangle.

Thus, we decompose

(2.11)

\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\chi_{E}f\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k\geq j-2}\left\langle\chi_{E}P_{k}\chi_{E}f,f\right\rangle,

where

P_{k}=\iint\psi(2^{k}|t-s|)\eta(t)\eta(s)e^{i\frac{t-s}{2}(\lambda-H)}dsdt.

After a simple change of variables we observe

(2.12)

\chi_{E}P_{k}\chi_{E}f=\int\eta(s)\chi_{E}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi(2^{k}|\cdot|)\eta(\cdot+s)]\chi_{E}fds.

Note
\psi(|\cdot|)=\psi+\psi(-\,\cdot)
. Since
k\geq j-2
,
\psi(2^{k}\cdot)\eta(\cdot+s)\in\mathcal{C}^{k}
. Thus, we have (
2.9
) with
\tilde{\eta}=\psi(2^{k}\cdot)\eta(\cdot+s)
and
j=k
. By the aforementioned symmetric property of the kernels (
[
19
, p. 5]
) the same estimate holds for

\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi(-2^{k}\cdot)\eta(\cdot+s)]
. Therefore, taking integration in
s
, we have

\|\chi_{E}P_{k}\chi_{E}\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}\lesssim\beta^{\delta(p,p^{\prime})}2^{-j}2^{k(-1+(b+1)(\frac{2}{p}-1))}

for
r<p\leq 2
.
This gives
|\left\langle\chi_{E}P_{k}\chi_{E}f,g\right\rangle|\lesssim\beta^{(\frac{2}{p}-1)}2^{-j}2^{k(-1+(b+1)(\frac{2}{p}-1))}\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{p}
by Hölder’s inequality.
If we combine this and (
2.11
), summation over
k
yields

\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\chi_{E}f\|_{2}\lesssim\beta^{(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2})}2^{j(-1+(b+1)(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}))}\|f\|_{p}

for
r_{b}<p\leq 2
. (Note
r<r_{b}
.) Hence, we have (
2.8
) when
q=2
and
r_{b}<p\leq 2
. Duality gives (
2.8
) for
p=2
and
2\leq q<r_{b}^{\prime}
. Thus, interpolation between those estimates and
(
2.9
) gives (
2.8
) if
(1/p,1/q)
is contained in
\mathfrak{Q}(b,r)
but not on the line segments

[(1/2,1/r_{b}^{\prime}),(1/r,1/{r^{\prime}})]
,
[(1/r_{b},1/2),(1/r,1/{r^{\prime}})]
.

However, using the estimates above, we can obtain (
2.10
). Indeed, using (
2.12
) and (
2.8
) which now holds for
(1/p,1/q)
contained in the interior of
\mathfrak{Q}(b,r)
, by
Hölder’s inequality we have

(2.13)

|\left\langle\chi_{E}P_{k}\chi_{E}f,g\right\rangle|\lesssim\beta^{\delta(p,q)}2^{-j}2^{k(-1+(b+1)\delta(p,q))}\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q^{\prime}}

for
(1/p,1/q)\in\operatorname{int}\mathfrak{Q}(b,r)
. This allows us to apply the bilinear interpolation argument
(e.g., Keel and Tao
[
22
]
). Therefore, we obtain

\textstyle\sum_{k\geq j-2}|\left\langle\chi_{E}P_{k}\chi_{E}f,g\right\rangle|\lesssim\beta^{\delta(p,q)}2^{-j}\|f\|_{p}\|g\|_{q^{\prime}}

provided that
(1/p,1/q)\in\operatorname{int}\mathfrak{Q}(b,r)
and
(b+1)(1/p-1/q)=1
. In particular, taking
q^{\prime}=p
, by (
2.11
) we obtain (
2.10
).
∎

The following lemma is useful for obtaining some endpoint estimates.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1\leq p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1}\leq\infty
and
\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1}>0
.
Let
T_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, be sublinear operators satisfying
\|T_{j}\|_{p_{k}\to q_{k}}\leq B_{k}2^{j(-1)^{k}\epsilon_{k}}
for
k=0,1
. Let
\theta={\epsilon_{0}}/(\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon_{1})
,

1/p_{\ast}=\theta/{p_{1}}+(1-\theta)/{p_{0}},
and
1/q_{\ast}=\theta/{q_{1}}+(1-\theta)/{q_{0}}.

Then, the following hold
:

(a)

If
p_{0}=p_{1}=p
and
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
, then
\|\sum_{j}T_{j}f\|_{{q_{\ast},\infty}}\lesssim B_{0}^{1-\theta}B_{1}^{\theta}\|f\|_{p}
,

(b)

If
q_{0}=q_{1}=q
and
p_{0}\neq p_{1}
, then
\|\sum_{j}T_{j}f\|_{{q}}\lesssim B_{0}^{1-\theta}B_{1}^{\theta}\|f\|_{p_{\ast},1}
,

(c)

If
p_{0}\neq p_{1}
and
q_{0}\neq q_{1}
, then
\|\sum_{j}T_{j}f\|_{{q_{\ast},\infty}}\lesssim B_{0}^{1-\theta}B_{1}^{\theta}\|f\|_{p_{\ast},1}
.

The third assertion
(c)
is known as
‘Bourgain’s summation trick’
(see
[
5
, Section 6.2]
for a formulation in abstract setting).
The first
(a)
and the second
(b)
give better estimates than the restricted weak type estimate. As far as the authors are aware, this observation first appeared in
[
2
]
(see also
[
31
, Lemma 2.3]
).

Remark 1
.

Thanks to Lemma
2.4
and the estimate (
2.13
), which is equivalent to
\|\chi_{E}P_{k}\chi_{E}\|_{p\to q}\lesssim\beta^{\delta(p,q)}2^{-j}2^{k(-1+(b+1)\delta(p,q))}
, an elementary approach to the estimate (
2.10
)
is possible. By
(c)
in Lemma
2.4
we have
\|\sum_{k}\chi_{E}P_{k}\chi_{E}f\|_{q,\infty}\lesssim\beta^{\delta(p,q)}2^{-j}\|f\|_{p,1}
for
(1/p,1/q)\in\operatorname{int}\mathfrak{Q}(b,r)
satisfying
(b+1)\,\delta(p,q)=1
. Interpolation gives, in particular, (
2.8
) with
p=r_{b}
and
q=r_{b}^{\prime}
, which is equivalent to (
2.10
).

By using Lemma
2.3
, we can prove Theorem
1.6
.

1.6
.

To show (
1.10
) for
(1/p,1/q)\in\mathscr{P}
, by interpolation it suffices to show the restricted weak type
(p,q)
estimate for
(1/p,1/q)=\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}

By duality we need only to show

(2.14)

\|\Pi_{\lambda}f\|_{q_{\circ},\infty}\leq C\|f\|_{p_{\circ},1},

where
(1/p_{\circ},1/q_{\circ})=\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}.
Indeed, once we have (
2.14
), duality and interpolation give (
1.10
) for

(1/p,1/q)

\in(\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{F}^{\prime})

\Pi_{\lambda}^{*}\Pi_{\lambda}=\Pi_{\lambda}
and
(\tfrac{d+2}{2d},\tfrac{d-2}{2d})\in(\mathfrak{F},\mathfrak{F}^{\prime})
. So, we get (
1.9
) since

\|\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{2\to p^{\prime}}^{2}=\|\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{p\to p^{\prime}}
. Besides, duality gives
\|\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{\frac{2d}{d+2}\to 2}\leq C
.
Interpolation between those estimates and
\|\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{2\to 2}\leq 1
gives (
1.10
) for
(1/p,1/q)\in\mathscr{P}
(see Figure
4
).

By Lemma
2.2
it is sufficient for (
2.14
) to show

(2.15)

\textstyle\|\sum_{j\geq 0}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]f\|_{q_{\circ},\infty}\leq C\|f\|_{p_{\circ},1}.

By Lemma
2.1
we have (
2.7
) with
E=\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\beta=1
,
r=1
, and
b=(d-2)/{2}
, provided that

\tilde{\eta}\in\mathcal{C}^{j}
and
\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\eta}\subset(2^{-1-j},2^{1-j})
.
Using this and Lemma
2.3
, we have

(2.16)

\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\|_{p\to q}\lesssim 2^{j(\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q})-1)},\quad j\geq 0,

for
(1/p,1/q)
contained in
\mathfrak{Q}((d-2)/2,1)
which is a quadrangle with vertices
(1/2,1/2)
,
(1/2,(d-2)/(2d))
,

((d+2)/(2d),1/2)
, and
(1,0)
. To show (

and make use of the summation trick (

in Lemma

).
Using (
2.16
), we get restricted weak type

2.3.
L^{2}
–
L^{\infty}
estimate

We now consider
L^{2}
–
L^{\infty}
estimate for
\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]
, which plays a significant role in what follows.
For a given operator
T
, by
T(x,y)
we denote the kernel of
T
. A simple duality argument shows

(2.17)

\|T\|_{2\to\infty}=\|T\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}(L_{y}^{2})}.

We also observe that

(2.18)

\textstyle\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]f=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\widehat{\eta}(2^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda))\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}f

for
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}
, which follows from (
2.1
) and (
2.4
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\lambda^{-\frac{2}{3}}\lesssim\mu\leq 1/4
and
2^{-j}\gtrsim(\lambda\mu)^{-1}
.
If
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{j}
, then there is a constant
C
, independent of
\lambda
and
\mu
, such that

(2.19)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\|_{2\to\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\frac{j}{2}}(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{d-2}{4}},

(2.20)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\|_{2\to\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{4}}.

Proof.

We first show (
2.19
).
The proof follows an argument similar to that of Lemma 2.9 in
[
19
]
.

By orthogonality,
\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta](x,\cdot)\|_{2}^{2}=\pi^{-2}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}|\widehat{\eta}(2^{-1}(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda))|^{2}|\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}(x,x)|
. Also note that
|\widehat{\eta}(\tau)|\lesssim 2^{-j}(1+2^{-j}|\tau|)^{-N}
for any
N
. Thus, by (
2.18
) and (
2.17
), we have

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\|_{2\to\infty}^{2}\lesssim\sup_{x\in A_{\lambda,\mu}}\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}2^{-2j}\big(1+2^{-j}|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\big)^{-N}\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}(x,x).

Since
\|T\|_{1\to\infty}=\|T\|_{L^{\infty}_{x,y}}
for an operator
T
, this reduces the proof of (
2.19
) to showing

(2.21)

\textstyle\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}2^{-2j}\big(1+2^{-j}|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\big)^{-N}\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\|_{1\to\infty}\lesssim 2^{-j}(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}

for
2^{-j}\gtrsim(\lambda\mu)^{-1}
. Using

\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}^{2}=\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}
and duality, we note
\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\|_{1\to\infty}\leq\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}\|_{2\to\infty}^{2}
.
Thus, the estimate (
2.21
) follows from a stronger estimate

(2.22)

\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\big(1+2^{-j}|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\big)^{-N}\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}^{e}\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}\|_{2\to\infty}^{2}\leq C2^{j}(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{d-2}{2}},

where
\chi_{\lambda,\mu}^{e}=\chi_{A_{\lambda,\mu}^{e}}
and
A_{\lambda,\mu}^{e}=\{x:|x|\geq\lambda^{1/2}(1-\mu)\}
.

To handle the sum above, we use the estimates

(2.23)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{2\to\infty}\lesssim(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{d-2}{4}},

\|\chi_{A_{\lambda}^{\circ}}\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{2\to\infty}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{12}}
, and
\|\chi_{A_{\lambda,\mu}^{-}}\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{2\to\infty}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{12}}(\lambda\mu^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-N}
, where
A_{\lambda}^{\circ}:=\{\sqrt{\lambda}x:|1-|x||\leq\lambda^{-2/3}\}
and
A_{\lambda,\mu}^{-}:=\{\sqrt{\lambda}x:\mu\leq|x|-1<2\mu\}
. The first estimate (
2.23
) follows from (
1.4
).
For the second and third estimates, the reader may find their proofs in
[
19
]
or
[
25
]
.
Since
\lambda^{-\frac{2}{3}}\lesssim\mu
, summation over annuli gives

(2.24)

\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}^{e}\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{2\to\infty}\lesssim(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{d-2}{4}}.

We define
\ell(\rho)=\ell(\rho,\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})
by setting

\ell(\rho)=(\lambda/\lambda^{\prime})^{\frac{2}{3}}\rho
if
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}
and

\ell(\rho)=(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)/\lambda+\rho
otherwise. Since
\lambda^{-\frac{2}{3}}\lesssim\mu
, we have
\ell(\mu)\gtrsim(\lambda^{\prime})^{-\frac{2}{3}}
. Note that
(\lambda^{\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\ell(\mu))\leq\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}(1-\mu)
. Thus, it follows
that
\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}^{e}\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}\|_{2\to\infty}\leq\|\chi_{\lambda^{\prime},\ell(\mu)}^{e}\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}\|_{2\to\infty}
.
By (
2.24
) the left hand side of (
2.22
) is bounded above by

\displaystyle\textstyle C\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}\big(1+2^{-j}|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|\big)^{-N}(\lambda^{\prime}\ell(\mu))^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

To prove (
2.22
) it is sufficient to show the above sum is bounded by
C2^{j}(\lambda\mu)^{(d-2)/2}
.
Indeed, considering separately the cases
\lambda\geq\lambda^{\prime}
and
\lambda<\lambda^{\prime}
, we only have to show

\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}\leq\lambda}\big(1+2^{-j}(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})\big)^{-N}(\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda)^{\frac{d-2}{6}}\lesssim 2^{j}
and

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}>\lambda}\big(1+2^{-j}(\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)\big)^{-N}

\displaystyle(\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\big((\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda)/(\lambda\mu)+1\big)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\lesssim 2^{j}.

Both follow from a simple computation. Particularly, we use
2^{-j}\gtrsim(\lambda\mu)^{-1}
for the second inequality.

One can easily show the estimate (
2.20
) in the same manner using
\|\Pi_{\lambda}\|_{2\to\infty}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{4}}
. So, we omit the detail.
∎

The marginal case
2^{j}\geq\lambda\mu

Making use of the previous estimates, we obtain estimates for
\sum_{2^{j}\geq\lambda\mu}\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\chi_{\lambda,\mu}
, whose contribution turns out to be less significant.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{D}^{-1}:=\{2^{-k}:\,-k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
2^{j_{\circ}-1}\leq\lambda\mu<2^{j_{\circ}}
. Suppose
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{j_{\circ}}
. Then, for
(1/p,1/q)\in{{\square}}
, we have

(2.25)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\|_{p\to q}\lesssim\lambda^{\beta(p,q)}\mu^{\gamma(p,q)}.

Proof.

Note
(\lambda\mu)^{-1+\frac{d}{2}\delta(p,q)}\leq\lambda^{\beta(p,q)}\mu^{\gamma(p,q)}
. So, (
2.25
) follows once we have

(2.26)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\|_{p\to q}\lesssim(\lambda\mu)^{-1+\frac{d}{2}\delta(p,q)}.

In view of interpolation and duality it is sufficient to show (
2.26
) for
(p,q)=(2,2)
,
(2,\infty)
,
(1,\infty)
.
The estimate (
2.26
) for
(p,q)=(2,2)
is clear from (
2.5
) since
\|\eta\|_{1}\lesssim 2^{-j_{\circ}}
. Since
2^{j_{\circ}}\sim\lambda\mu
, (
2.26
) for
(p,q)=(2,\infty)
follows from (
2.19
).

Note that
\widehat{\eta}(\tau)=O(2^{-j_{\circ}}(1+2^{-j_{\circ}}|\tau|)^{-N})
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Using (
2.18
), we obtain

\textstyle\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta]\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\|_{1\to\infty}\lesssim\sum_{\lambda^{\prime}}2^{-j_{\circ}}(1+2^{-j_{\circ}}|\lambda^{\prime}-\lambda|)^{-N}\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda^{\prime}}\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\|_{1\to\infty}.

Since
2^{j_{\circ}}\sim\lambda\mu
, (
2.26
) for
(p,q)=(1,\infty)
follows from (
2.21
).
∎

2.4.
Estimates for the kernel of
\Pi_{\lambda}

In this subsection we are concerned with estimates for the kernel of
\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]
.
To do so, it is more convenient to consider a rescaled operator. For
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, by
\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\eta]
we denote the operator whose kernel is given by

\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\eta](x,y)=\Pi_{\lambda}[\eta](\sqrt{\lambda}x,\sqrt{\lambda}y).

As before, by Mehler’s formula (cf. (
2.3
)) and scaling one can see

(2.27)

\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\eta](x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\eta(t)\mathfrak{a}(t)e^{i\lambda\phi_{1}(x,y,t)}dt.

To obtain estimates for
\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]
we examine the phase function of the oscillatory integral.
A calculation shows

(2.28)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)=-\frac{\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cos s)}{2\sin^{2}s},

where

(2.29)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\tau)

\displaystyle:=(\tau-\langle x,y\rangle)^{2}-\mathcal{D}(x,y),

(2.30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=1+\langle x,y\rangle^{2}-|x|^{2}-|y|^{2}.

The stationary point of
\phi_{1}(x,y,\cdot)
is given by the zeros of
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cos\cdot)
.

\mathcal{D}(x,y)
, which is the discriminant of the quadratic equation
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\tau)=0
, regulates the nature of stationary point of the phase function
\phi_{1}(x,y,\cdot)
.
In fact, we can obtain bounds on
\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)
in terms of
|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|
, which are to be used later.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{D}^{-1}
and let
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfy that
1-C_{1}\mu\leq|x|,|y|\leq 1-c_{1}\mu
for some
0<c_{1}<C_{1}
. If
1+\langle x,y\rangle\geq 10^{-2}
, then for any
N>0
there exists a constant
\mathrm{B}=\mathrm{B}(c_{1},C_{1},N,d)
such that the following hold for
j\geq 0
:

(a)

If
-\mathcal{D}(x,y)\gtrsim\mu^{2}
, then

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)|\leq\mathrm{B}\begin{cases}2^{\frac{d-2}{2}j}(\lambda 2^{j}|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|+1)^{-N},&\ 2^{-j}\lesssim|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|^{\frac{1}{4}},\\[2.0pt]
2^{\frac{d-2}{2}j}(\lambda 2^{-3j}+1)^{-N},&\ 2^{-j}\gg|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|^{\frac{1}{4}}.\end{cases}

(b)

If
|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|\ll\mu^{2}
, then

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)|\leq\mathrm{B}\begin{cases}2^{\frac{d-2}{2}j}(\lambda 2^{j}\mu^{2}+1)^{-N},&\ 2^{-j}\ll\mu^{\frac{1}{2}},\\[2.0pt]
2^{\frac{d-2}{2}j}(\lambda 2^{-3j}+1)^{-N},&\ 2^{-j}\gg\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{cases}

(c)

If
\mathcal{D}(x,y)\sim\mu^{2}
, then

\displaystyle|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)|\leq\mathrm{B}\begin{cases}2^{\frac{d-1}{2}j}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}},&\ 2^{-j}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}},\\[2.0pt]
2^{\frac{d-2}{2}j}(\lambda 2^{-3j}+1)^{-N},&\ 2^{-j}\gg\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{cases}

If
1+\langle x,y\rangle<10^{-2}
, then
|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{d-2}{2}j}(\lambda 2^{j})^{-N}
for
j\geq 0
.

Proof.

We first consider the case
1+\langle x,y\rangle\geq 10^{-2}
and show
(a)
–
(c)
. The estimates
(a)
and
(b)
can be shown in a similar way.
We begin with observing that
0<1-\langle x,y\rangle\sim 1-\langle x,y\rangle^{2}
since
1+\langle x,y\rangle\geq 10^{-2}
. So, we have

(2.31)

1-\langle x,y\rangle\sim 2-|x|^{2}-|y|^{2}-\mathcal{D}(x,y)\sim\mu

if
-\mathcal{D}(x,y)\gtrsim\mu^{2}
or
|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|\ll\mu^{2}
.

Note that
1-\cos s\sim 2^{-2j}
if
s\in\operatorname{supp}\psi_{j}
. When

|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|\gtrsim\mu^{2}
, recalling (
2.29
) and using (
2.31
) we see
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cos s)\gtrsim 2^{-4j}
for

2^{-j}\gg|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|^{1/4}
and
s\in\operatorname{supp}{\psi_{j}}
. Thus, from (
2.28
) we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{s}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)|\gtrsim\begin{cases}2^{2j}|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|,&2^{-j}\lesssim|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|^{\frac{1}{4}},\\
2^{-2j},&2^{-j}\gg|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|^{\frac{1}{4}}\end{cases}

for
s\in\operatorname{supp}{\psi_{j}}
if
-\mathcal{D}(x,y)\gtrsim\mu^{2}
.
We also note that
|(d/ds)^{n}((\sin s)^{-2})|\lesssim 2^{(2+n)j}
and
|\partial_{s}^{n}(\cos s-\langle x,y\rangle)|\lesssim 2^{nj}\max(\mu,2^{-2j})
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Being combined with (
2.28
), these bounds yield

\displaystyle|\partial_{s}^{n}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)|\lesssim\begin{cases}2^{(1+n)j}|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|,&2^{-j}\lesssim|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|^{\frac{1}{4}}\\
2^{(n-3)j},&2^{-j}\gg|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|^{\frac{1}{4}}\end{cases},\quad s\in\operatorname{supp}{\psi_{j}}

if
\mathcal{D}(x,y)\lesssim-\mu^{2}
.
Also, we have
|(d/ds)^{n}(2^{-dj/2}\mathfrak{a}\psi_{j})|\lesssim 2^{nj}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Thus, integration by parts for the integral
\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)
(recall (
2.27
)) gives the estimate in
(a)
(see, for example,
[
19
, Lemma 2.5]
).

We can show
(b)
in the same manner as above. Since
1-\cos s\sim 2^{-2j}
and
|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|\ll\mu^{2}
, we have
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cos s)\sim\mu^{2}
if
2^{-j}\ll\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}

and
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cos s)\sim 2^{-4j}
if
2^{-j}\gg\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}.
Thus, using (
2.28
) we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{s}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)|\gtrsim\begin{cases}2^{2j}\mu^{2},&2^{-j}\ll\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
2^{-2j},&2^{-j}\gg\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{cases},\quad s\in\operatorname{supp}{\psi_{j}}.

Similarly as in the proof of
(a)
, we get

\displaystyle|\partial_{s}^{n}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)|\lesssim\begin{cases}2^{(1+n)j}\mu^{2},&2^{-j}\ll\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
2^{(n-3)j},&2^{-j}\gg\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\end{cases},\quad s\in\operatorname{supp}{\psi_{j}}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Combining those and
|(d/ds)^{n}(2^{-dj/2}\mathfrak{a}\psi_{j})|\lesssim 2^{nj}
, by routine integration by parts we obtain the desired estimate in
(b)
(
[
19
, Lemma 2.5]
).

We now prove
(c)
. From (
2.31
) we note
1-\langle x,y\rangle\lesssim\mu
since
\mathcal{D}(x,y)\sim\mu^{2}
. Using this, we have
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cos s)\sim 2^{-4j}
if
2^{-j}\gg\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Thus, the estimate for the case
2^{-j}\gg\mu^{1/2}
can be obtained in the same manner as in the proof of
(b)
.
Therefore, we only need to show
(c)
assuming
that

2^{-j}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In this case
\operatorname{supp}\psi_{j}
may contain at least one of stationary points of
\phi_{1}(x,y,\cdot)
.
The equation
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cdot)=0
has two roots
\tau_{\pm}=\langle x,y\rangle\pm\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(x,y)}
.
Since
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,1)=|x-y|^{2}
and
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,-1)=|x+y|^{2}
,
\tau_{\pm}\in[1,-1]
.
Thus, the function
\partial_{s}\phi_{1}(x,y,\cdot)
has two zeros
s_{-}
,
s_{+}
on
[0,\pi]
such that

\cos s_{\pm}=\langle x,y\rangle\pm\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(x,y)}.

Since
\cos s_{+}-\cos s_{-}=2\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(x,y)}\sim\mu
, we have
s_{-}-s_{+}\gtrsim\mu^{1/2}
.
We dyadically decompose the integral
\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)
((
2.27
)) away from
s_{\pm}
.
To do this, we let

\eta_{k}^{\kappa}(s)=\psi(2^{k}|s-s_{\kappa}|),\quad\kappa=\pm,

and
k_{0}
be an integer such that
\mu^{1/2}/(2C)\leq 2^{-k_{0}}<\mu^{1/2}/C
for a large constant
C>0
.
We also set

\textstyle\eta^{*}(s)=1-\sum_{k>k_{0}}\eta_{k}^{+}(s)-\sum_{k>k_{0}}\eta_{k}^{-}(s)

so that
\textstyle\eta^{\ast}+\sum_{k>k_{0}}\eta_{k}^{+}+\sum_{k>k_{0}}\eta_{k}^{-}=1
.

We first estimate the sum
\sum_{k>k_{0}}\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}\eta_{k}^{+}](x,y)
.
From (
2.28
), note that

\partial_{s}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)=-\frac{(\cos s-\cos s_{-})}{2\sin^{2}s}\int_{s_{+}}^{s}\sin u\,du.

Since
\cos s_{+}-\cos s_{-}\sim\mu
, we have
|\cos s-\cos s_{-}|\sim\mu
on
\operatorname{supp}{(\psi_{j}\eta_{k}^{+})}
. We also note
\big|\int_{s_{+}}^{s}\sin udu\big|\sim 2^{-j}2^{-k}
for
s\in\operatorname{supp}{(\psi_{j}\eta_{k}^{+})}
.
Hence,
|\partial_{s}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)|\gtrsim 2^{j}2^{-k}\mu
for
s\in\operatorname{supp}{(\psi_{j}\eta_{k}^{+})}
.
By the van der Corput lemma (for example,
[
35
, pp. 332–334]
) we get
|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}\eta_{k}^{+}](x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{d-2}{2}j}\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-1}2^{k}
.
Combining this and a trivial bound
|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}\eta_{k}^{+}](x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{d}{2}j}2^{-k}
, we obtain

\textstyle\big|\sum_{k>k_{0}}\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}\eta_{k}^{+}](x,y)\big|\lesssim\sum_{k>k_{0}}2^{\frac{d}{2}j}\min(2^{k}\lambda^{-1}2^{-j}\mu^{-1},\,2^{-k})\lesssim 2^{\frac{d-1}{2}j}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

In the same manner, one can show
|\sum_{k>k_{0}}\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}\eta_{k}^{-}](x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{d-1}{2}j}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
.

To complete the proof of
(c)
, it remains to show
|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}\eta^{\ast}](x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{d-1}{2}j}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Note that
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cos s)\sim\mu^{2}
for
s\in\operatorname{supp}(\psi_{j}\eta^{*})
.
This gives
|\partial_{s}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)|\gtrsim 2^{2j}\mu^{2}
. Thus, by the van der Corput lemma we get
|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}\eta^{\ast}](x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{d-4}{2}j}\lambda^{-1}\mu^{-2}
. Combining this and a trivial bound
|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}\eta^{\ast}](x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{d}{2}j}\mu^{1/2}
, we obtain the desired estimate since
2^{-j}\lesssim\mu^{1/2}
.

To prove the last assertion, we observe that
|\cos s-\langle x,y\rangle|\geq 10^{-1}
for
s\in\operatorname{supp}{\psi_{j}}
and
\mathcal{D}(x,y)=(1+\langle x,y\rangle)^{2}-|x+y|^{2}\leq 10^{-4}
if
1+\langle x,y\rangle<10^{-2}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cos s)\sim 1
for
s\in\operatorname{supp}{\psi_{j}}
. Combining this and (
2.28
), we have
|\partial_{s}\phi_{1}|\gtrsim 2^{2j}
.
Therefore, repeated integration by parts, as before, gives the desired estimate.
∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Suppose that
|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|\geq c_{0}
for a constant
c_{0}>0
whenever
x,y\in E
. Then, there is a constant

C=C(c_{0})
such that

(2.32)

\|\chi_{E_{\lambda}}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\chi_{E_{\lambda}}\|_{1\to\infty}\leq C2^{\frac{d-1}{2}j}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad j\geq 0.

Proof.

By rescaling
(x,y)\to(\sqrt{\lambda}x,\sqrt{\lambda}y)
, (
2.32
) is equivalent to

(2.33)

|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)|\leq C2^{\frac{d-1}{2}j}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}

for
x,y\in E
and
j\geq 0
. Since
\mathcal{D}(x,x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{2}
, we have
|x|\leq 1-c
or
|x|\geq 1+c
for a small constant
c=c(c_{0})>0
if
x\in E
.

Let
x,y\in E.
Then, by symmetry we need only to consider the cases
|x|,|y|\leq 1-c
;
|x|\leq 1-c,1+c\leq|y|
; and

1+c\leq|x|,|y|
. For the first case, by choosing
C_{1}=2
and
c_{1}=2c
in Lemma
2.7
, we may assume that
1-C_{1}\mu\leq|x|,|y|\leq 1-c_{1}\mu
with
\mu=1/2
. Thus,
(
2.33
) follows from
(a)
or
(c)
in Lemma
2.7
. When
|x|\leq 1-c
and
1+c\leq|y|
, we have
\mathcal{D}(x,y)\leq(1-|x|^{2})(1-|y|^{2})\leq-c^{2}
.
By (
2.28
) and (
2.29
) this gives
|\partial_{s}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)|\gtrsim 2^{2j}
for
s\in\operatorname{supp}\psi_{j}
. Using van der Corput’s lemma, we get

|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{d}{2}j}\min(\lambda^{-1}2^{-2j},\,2^{-j})\lesssim 2^{\frac{d-1}{2}j}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

For the third case
1+c\leq|x|,|y|
, we may assume
\mathcal{D}(x,y)\geq c_{0}
since the estimate (
2.33
) follows by the same argument as above if
\mathcal{D}(x,y)\leq-c_{0}
.
Thus, we have
1+c_{0}<\langle x,y\rangle^{2}
. If
\langle x,y\rangle>(1+c_{0})^{1/2}
, the two distinct roots
r_{1}<r_{2}
of the equation
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\tau)=0
are bigger than or equal to
1
because
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,1)=|x-y|^{2}\geq 0
.
Since
r_{2}>(1+c_{0})^{1/2}
,
|\mathcal{Q}(x,y,\cos s)|=|(r_{1}-\cos s)(r_{2}-\cos s)|\gtrsim(1-\cos s)
. Using (
2.28
), we see

|\partial_{s}\phi_{1}(x,y,s)|\gtrsim 1.

The same lower bound holds if
\langle x,y\rangle<-(1+c_{0})^{1/2}
. In fact,
r_{1},r_{2}\leq-1
because
\mathcal{Q}(x,y,-1)=|x+y|^{2}\geq 0
. Therefore, van der Corput lemma gives
|\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x,y)|\lesssim 2^{\frac{d}{2}j}\min(\lambda^{-1},\,2^{-j})\lesssim 2^{\frac{d-1}{2}j}\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}.
This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Estimate away from
\sqrt{\lambda}\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
: Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
and show the failure of the estimate (
1.3
) for
(1/p,1/q)\in[\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{D}]\cup[\mathfrak{C}^{\prime},\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}]
. This and the lower bounds on
\|\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\Pi_{\lambda}\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\|_{p\to q}
in Proposition
5.1
below show that the bounds in Theorem
1.2
are sharp.

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Making use of Lemma
2.2
and
2.6
, we see that it is sufficient to consider

\textstyle\tilde{\Pi}_{\lambda}:=\sum_{0\leq j\leq j_{\circ}}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]

in place of
\Pi_{\lambda}
where
2^{j_{\circ}}\sim\lambda
. That is to say,
the same estimates hold for
\tilde{\Pi}_{\lambda}
as those for
\Pi_{\lambda}
in Theorem
1.2
.

Since

|\mathcal{D}(x,y)|\geq 1/2
for
x,y\in\mathbb{B}
, we have the estimate (
2.32
). Applying Lemma
2.3
, we get

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\|_{p\to q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta(p,q)}2^{(\frac{d+1}{2}\delta(p,q)-1)j},

provided that
(1/p,1/q)
is contained in the close quadrangle
with vertices

(1/2,1/2)
,
\mathfrak{A}
,
\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
and
(1,0).

Thus, summation over
j
gives

(3.2)

\textstyle\|\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\tilde{\Pi}_{\lambda}\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\|_{p\to q}\lesssim\lambda^{\beta(p,q)},

for
(1/p,1/q)\in\mathcal{R}_{1}\setminus[\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}]
(see Figure
1
). It is convenient for our purpose to note
\beta(1,2)=\frac{d-2}{4}
,
\beta(p,q)=\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{d-1}{2d})-1
if
(1/p,1/q)\in(\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{D}]
, and
\beta(p,q)=-\frac{1}{d+1}
if
(1/p,1/q)=\mathfrak{C}
. By
(c)
in Lemma
2.4
and (
3.1
) we have

(3.3)

\textstyle\|\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\tilde{\Pi}_{\lambda}\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{p,1}\to L^{q,\infty}}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}\delta(p,q)},

for
(1/p,1/q)=\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{C}^{\prime}
, which satisfy
\delta(p,q)=2/(d+1)
. Interpolation yields (
3.2
) for
(1/p,1/q)\in\mathcal{R}_{1}
.
Using the estimate (
3.1
) and taking sum over
0\leq j\leq j_{\circ}
, we get (
3.2
) for
(p,q)=(1,\infty).

Now, in view of interpolation, to complete the proof we need only to show (
3.2
) with
(p,q)=(1,2)
and
the weak type estimate

(3.4)

\textstyle\|\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\tilde{\Pi}_{\lambda}\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{\frac{2d}{d-1},\infty}}\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{d-1}{2d})-1},

for
1\leq p<{2d(d+1)}/(d^{2}+4d-1)
, which corresponds to the estimate
(i)
in Theorem
1.2
for
(1/p,1/q)\in(\mathfrak{C},\mathfrak{D}]
.
Duality and interpolation provide
all the
L^{p}
–
L^{q}
estimates asserted in Theorem
1.2
(Figure
1
).

By the estimate (
2.20
) we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\frac{j}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{d-2}{4}}\|f\|_{1},\quad 2^{j}\lesssim\lambda.

Summation over
j
clearly yields (
3.2
) with
(p,q)=(1,2)
. It now remains to show (
3.4
).
Interpolation of the estimates (
3.5
) and (
3.1
) with
(1/p,1/q)=(1,0),

\mathfrak{C}
gives

(3.6)

\|\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\|_{p\rightarrow q}\lesssim 2^{jd(\frac{d-1}{2d}-\frac{1}{q})}\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q})-\frac{d+1}{2}}

for
(1/p,1/q)
contained in the closed triangle
\mathscr{T}
with vertices
(1,1/2),\mathfrak{C},(1,0)
.
Now, fixing
p\in[1,{2d(d+1)}/{(d^{2}+4d-1)})

q_{0}<2d/(d-1)<q_{1}
and

(
3.6
) with

and

. Then, we apply
(a)
in Lemma
2.4
to these two estimates to get the weak type estimate for

p,q
such that

. (Figure

is helpful here.) Hence, for

).

.

Remark 2
.

As can be easily seen from the proof, the same bounds remain to hold on
\chi_{E_{\lambda}}\Pi_{\lambda}\chi_{E_{\lambda}}

(
in place of
\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}\Pi_{\lambda}\chi_{\mathbb{B}_{\lambda}}
)

as long as
E

\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

Let us define an oscillatory integral operator

Note that

To obtain (

Lemma 4.6

(
[

To apply the lemma, we verify that the mixed Hessian of

Let

\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda}\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\|_{p\to q}\geq C\lambda^{\beta(p,q)}\mu^{\gamma(p,q)}.

For the purpose, it is sufficient to show the lower bounds (

Comparing those lower bounds immediately yields (

Proposition 5.1

Let

S_{l}=\{x\in A_{\lambda,\mu}:|x-x_{0}|\in C\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}\mu[2^{l-1},2^{l})\},

To prove the above estimate, it is enough to show

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\sum_{j\geq 0}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x_{0},\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(S_{l})}\lesssim(\mu 2^{l})^{-\frac{d-2}{4}}(\lambda^{\frac{d}{2}}\mu^{d}2^{dl})^{\frac{1}{p}}(\lambda(\mu 2^{l})^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-N},\quad l\geq 1.

Summation over
l
gives the estimate (
5.6
) because
\lambda\mu^{3/2}\gtrsim 1
. Using (
4.6
) and scaling, we observe

\textstyle\|\sum_{j\geq 0}\Pi_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}](x_{0},\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(S_{l})}\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 0}\sup_{k\sim_{\nu}k^{\prime}}\|\chi_{k}^{\nu}\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\chi_{k^{\prime}}^{\nu}\|_{L_{x,y}^{\infty}}\,|S_{l}|^{1/p},

where
\nu
satisfies
2^{-\nu}\sim C\mu 2^{l}
. Note that
\mathcal{D}\sim-(\mu 2^{l})^{2}
if
(x,y)\in\operatorname{supp}{\chi_{k}^{\nu}}\times\operatorname{supp}{\chi_{k^{\prime}}^{\nu}}
,
k\sim_{\nu}k^{\prime}
.
Thus, using
(a)
in Lemma
2.7
,
we obtain

\textstyle\sum_{j\geq 0}\sup_{k\sim_{\nu}k^{\prime}}\|\chi_{k}^{\nu}\mathfrak{P}_{\lambda}[\psi_{j}]\chi_{k^{\prime}}^{\nu}\|_{L_{x,y}^{\infty}}\lesssim(\mu 2^{l})^{-\frac{d-2}{4}}(\lambda(\mu 2^{l})^{\frac{3}{2}})^{-N},

for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, we get the desired estimate.
∎

To show (
5.4
), we first claim that there is a point
x_{0}\in A_{\lambda,\mu}
such that

(5.7)

\int_{A_{\lambda,\mu}}\Pi_{\lambda}(x_{0},y)^{2}\,dy\gtrsim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}.

Combined with (
2.17
), this shows sharpness of the bound (
1.6
) for
p=2
and
q=\infty
.
Assuming (
5.7
) for the moment we prove (
5.4
).
Let us set

f(x)=\Pi_{\lambda}(x_{0},x)\chi_{A_{\lambda,\mu}}(x).

By (
5.7
) we have
\Pi_{\lambda}f(x_{0})\gtrsim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}
.
We now recall the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

[
30
, Lemma 4.5]

Let
\lambda\in 2\mathbb{N}_{0}+d
and
\mu\in[\lambda^{-\frac{2}{3}},\frac{1}{2}]
.
Suppose that
h\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is an eigenfunction of
H
with eigenvalue
\lambda
, i.e.,
Hh(x)=\lambda h(x)
. If

y_{0}\in A_{\lambda,\mu},

then for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
we have

(5.8)

|\partial_{y}^{\alpha}h(y_{0})|\leq C(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{|\alpha|}{2}}\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B(y_{0},2(\lambda\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}}))}

with
C
independent of
\lambda
,
\mu
and
h
.

By this lemma we also have

\|\nabla\,\Pi_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{\lambda,\mu})}\lesssim\mu^{1/2}(\lambda\mu)^{(d-1)/{2}}
.
By the mean value theorem we see that
\Pi_{\lambda}f(x)\gtrsim\mu^{1/2}(\lambda\mu)^{(d-2)/{2}}
if
x\in B(x_{0},c(\lambda\mu)^{-1/2})
for a constant
c>0
small enough. Thus,
we have

\|\chi_{\lambda,\mu}\Pi_{\lambda}\chi_{\lambda,\mu}f\|_{q}\geq\|\Pi_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{q}(B(x_{0},c(\lambda\mu)^{-1/2})\cap A_{\lambda,\mu})}\gtrsim\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{d-2}{2}-\frac{d}{2q}}.

Combining this and the estimate
\|f\|_{p}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{d}{2p}}\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}+d(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{4})}
, which follows from Lemma
5.3
, we obtain (
5.4
).
It remains to show (
5.7
).

Proof of (
5.7
).

Let
\lambda=2N+d
. We set

J=\big\{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}\,:\,|\alpha|=N,\;{N\mu}/(2^{10}d)\leq\alpha_{j}\leq{N\mu}/(2^{9}d),\ \ 2\leq j\leq d\big\},

\ell={(2\sqrt{d})^{-1}}{\sqrt{\lambda\mu}}
, and
Q_{\ell}=[\sqrt{\lambda}(1-2\mu),\sqrt{\lambda}(1-3\mu/2)]\times[-\ell,\ell\,]^{d-1}
.
Noting that
Q_{\ell}\subset A_{\lambda,\mu}
and
|J|\sim(\lambda\mu)^{d-1}
, we have

\sum_{\alpha\in J}\int_{B_{d-1}(0,(\lambda\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}})}\int_{\sqrt{\lambda}(1-2\mu)}^{\sqrt{\lambda}(1-3\mu/2)}\left|\Phi_{\alpha}(x_{1},\bar{x})\right|\,dx_{1}d{\bar{x}}\gtrsim\lambda^{\frac{d}{4}}\mu^{\frac{d+2}{4}}.

This is an easy consequence of Lemma
5.2
.
Thus, there exists
x_{0}\in[\sqrt{\lambda}(1-2\mu),\sqrt{\lambda}(1-3\mu/2)]\times B_{d-1}(0,(\lambda\mu)^{-\frac{1}{2}})
such that
\sum_{\alpha\in J}|\Phi_{\alpha}(x_{0})|\gtrsim(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{3}{4}d-1}
.
We consider

g(x)=\chi_{Q_{\ell}}(x)\sum_{\alpha\in J}\,c_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}(x),

where
c_{\alpha}\in\{-1,1\}
such that
c_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}(x_{0})=|\Phi_{\alpha}(x_{0})|
.
By (
1.4
) with
q=2
, we have

\textstyle\|g\|_{2}\leq\|\sum_{\alpha\in J}c_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}(x)\|_{L^{2}(A_{\lambda,\mu})}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\sum_{\alpha\in J}c_{\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}(x)\|_{2}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}.

We now set

\displaystyle a_{u,v}:=\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}h_{u}(t)h_{v}(t)dt,\quad a^{\ast}_{u,v}:=\int_{\sqrt{\lambda}(1-2\mu)}^{\sqrt{\lambda}(1-3\mu/2)}h_{u}(t)h_{v}(t)dt,

and
A_{\alpha,\beta}=\int_{Q_{\ell}}\Phi_{\alpha}(x)\Phi_{\beta}(x)dx
.
Note that
A_{\alpha,\beta}=a^{\ast}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{d}a_{\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}}

and
\Pi_{\lambda}g(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in J}\sum_{\beta:|\beta|=N}c_{\alpha}A_{\alpha,\beta}\Phi_{\beta}(x)
. Thus, we write

(5.9)

\Pi_{\lambda}g(x)={\mathrm{I}}(x)+\mathrm{I\!I}(x),

where

\textstyle{\mathrm{I}}(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in J}c_{\alpha}A_{\alpha,\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}(x),\quad\mathrm{I\!I}(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in J}\sum_{\beta:|\beta|=N,\alpha\not=\beta}c_{\alpha}A_{\alpha,\beta}\Phi_{\beta}(x).

Using Lemma
5.2
, it is easy to see that
a_{u,u}\sim 1
and
a^{\ast}_{u,u}\sim\mu^{1/2}
if
u\sim N
. Consequently, it follows that

A_{\alpha,\alpha}\sim\mu^{1/2}\ \text{ if }\ \alpha\in J.

However, if
u\neq v
,
a_{u,v}
is exponentially decaying, thus we may regard
\mathrm{I\!I}
as a minor error.
More precisely,

(5.10)

A_{\alpha,\beta}\lesssim e^{-c\lambda\mu}

if
\alpha\in J
,
|\beta|=N
, and
\alpha\neq\beta
. Assuming this for the moment, we prove (
5.7
).

By (
5.10
) it follows that
\mathrm{I\!I}(x)=O\big((\lambda\mu)^{a}e^{-b\lambda\mu}\big)
for some
a,b>0
.
Hence, our choices of
c_{\alpha}
and
x_{0}
ensures that there exists
C>0
such that
{\mathrm{I}}(x_{0})=\sum_{\alpha\in J}c_{\alpha}A_{\alpha,\alpha}\Phi_{\alpha}(x_{0})\geq C\mu^{1/2}(\lambda\mu)^{3d/4-1}
.
Since
Q_{\ell}\subset A_{\lambda,\mu}
and
x_{0}\in A_{\lambda,\mu}
, recalling (
5.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\chi_{\lambda,\mu}(x_{0})\Pi_{\lambda}(x_{0},\cdot)\chi_{\lambda,\mu},g\rangle=\Pi_{\lambda}g(x_{0})\geq C\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}(\lambda\mu)^{\frac{3}{4}d-1}-O((\lambda\mu)^{a}e^{-b\lambda\mu}).

Since
\|g\|_{2}\lesssim\mu^{1/4}(\lambda\mu)^{(d-1)/2}
, by duality we get (
5.7
) as desired.

We now show (
5.10
). Recalling the identity
2(u-v)h_{u}h_{v}=h_{u}h_{v}^{\prime\prime}-h_{u}^{\prime\prime}h_{v}
(for example, see
[
40
, p. 2]
), we have

a_{u,v}=\frac{1}{2(u-v)}\int_{-\ell}^{\ell}h_{u}(s)h^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{v}(s)-h_{u}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(s)h_{v}(s)ds.

Thus, integration by parts gives

\displaystyle a_{u,v}=\frac{1}{2(u-v)}\Big(h_{u}(\ell)h^{{}^{\prime}}_{v}(\ell)-h_{u}(-\ell)h^{{}^{\prime}}_{v}(-\ell)-h_{u}^{{}^{\prime}}(\ell)h_{v}(\ell)+h_{u}^{{}^{\prime}}(-\ell)h_{v}(-\ell)\Big)

if
u\neq v
. Note that
h_{u}
is odd if
u
is odd and
h_{u}
is even otherwise. So,
h_{u}h^{{}^{\prime}}_{v}
is even if
u+v
is odd and
h_{u}h^{{}^{\prime}}_{v}
is odd otherwise.
Hence,
a_{u,v}=0
if
u+v
is odd. Using the identity
h_{u}^{\prime}(s)=sh_{u}(s)-\sqrt{2u+2}\,h_{u+1}(s)
(
[
40
, p. 5]
), we obtain

(5.11)

a_{u,v}=\frac{1+(-1)^{u+v}}{\sqrt{2}(u-v)}\big(\sqrt{u+1}\,h_{u+1}(\ell)h_{v}(\ell)-\sqrt{v+1}\,h_{u}(\ell)h_{v+1}(\ell)\big).

Note that
\ell-\sqrt{2u+1}\gtrsim\sqrt{\lambda\mu}\sim\ell
for
2^{-10}N\mu/d\leq u\leq 2^{-9}N\mu/d
. Thus, we have
s^{+}_{\sqrt{2u+1}}(\ell)\gtrsim\lambda\mu
as long as
2^{-10}N\mu/d\leq u\leq 2^{-9}N\mu/d
. By Lemma
5.2
it now follows that

|h_{u}(\ell)|\lesssim e^{-s^{+}_{\sqrt{2u+1}}(\ell)}\lesssim e^{-c\lambda\mu}

for some
c>0
if
2^{-10}N\mu/d\leq u\leq 2^{-9}N\mu/d
.
Combining this with (
5.11
) and
[
40
, Lemma 1.5.2]
, we have
|a_{u,v}|\lesssim e^{-c\lambda\mu}

for
2^{-10}N\mu/d\leq u\leq 2^{-9}N\mu/d
and
v\leq N
if
u\neq v
. Note that
|a_{u,v}|\lesssim 1
and
|a^{\ast}_{u,v}|\lesssim\mu^{1/2}
for any
u,v
.
We also note that there is at least one
j\in\{2,\dots,d\}
such that
\alpha_{j}\neq\beta_{j}
if
\alpha\neq\beta
,
\alpha\in J
, and
|\beta|=N
. Therefore, we
get (
5.10
) because
A_{\alpha,\beta}=a^{\ast}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}\prod_{i=2}^{d}a_{\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}}
.
∎

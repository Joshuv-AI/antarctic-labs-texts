Lev Borisov, Vernon Chan, Chengxi Wang

Abstract




Abstract.
Let
V
be a
6

\sigma
of trace
0

Introduction

Derived categories are a fascinating invariant of algebraic varieties. Two algebraic varieties are called derived equivalent if their bounded derived categories of coherent sheaves are equivalent as triangulated categories
[
13
]
. Bondal and Orlov
[
1
]
showed that a smooth projective variety
X
whose canonical class
K_{X}
is ample or anti-ample is determined by its bounded derived category
\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{Coh}-X)
, so in these cases derived equivalence implies isomorphism. However in the Calabi-Yau case of
K_{X}=0
there are multiple constructions of non-trivial derived equivalences
[
2
]
. In a number of cases, derived equivalences are related to string theory: for Calabi-Yau varieties
X,Y
and
Z
, homological mirror symmetry predicts that if
X
and
Y
are both “mirror” to
Z
(we say that
X
and
Y
are a double mirror pair), then
X
and
Y
are derived equivalent.

Many examples of derived equivalences come from the Homological Projective Duality construction of Kuznetsov
[
16
]
. In these examples, the derived partners of a Calabi-Yau variety can be slightly non-commutative and involve a DM-stack structure or a Brauer class. Of particular interest to us is the example of complete intersections of quadrics
[
15
]
, especially in dimension 2, which we describe briefly here. For
V\cong\mathbb{C}^{6}
and
W\subset S^{2}V^{\vee}
a dimension
3
subspace of homogeneous quadratic forms on
V
such that the intersection
X
of quadrics parametrized by
W
is a complete intersection, there exists a sheaf of algebras
A
on a double cover
Y
of
\mathbb{P}W
ramified over the locus of singular quadrics, such that the derived category
(Y,A)
is equivalent to the derived category of
X
:

(1)

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{Coh}-Y,A)\quad\simeq\quad\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{Coh}-X).

In
[
3
]
, Borisov and Li proposed a toric framework to generalize the above construction. In particular, they argued for the existence of a derived partner of Enriques surfaces (see
[
3
, Section 9.2]
), making use of the fact that generic complex Enriques surfaces can be obtained as a quotient of
(2,2,2)
-complete intersections in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
by a fixed-point-free involution
[
7
]
. In this paper, we look into the case of Enriques surfaces in more detail.

As above, let
V
be a
6
-dimensional complex vector space. However, it is now equipped with an involution
\sigma
of trace zero. Let
X
be a K3 surface obtained as a
(2,2,2)
-complete intersection in
\mathbb{P}V\cong\mathbb{CP}^{5}
, with the defining space of quadrics
W
being a generic dimension
3
subspace of the eigenvalue one subspace of
S^{2}V^{\vee}
. Then
\sigma
induces a fixed-point-free involution on

such that

is an Enriques surface.

On the dual side, the locus of singular quadrics in

Our main results are the following.

Theorem
2.7

There exists a sheaf of Azumaya algebras
\mathcal{A}
on

\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{Coh}-\mathscr{Y},\,\mathcal{A})\quad\simeq\quad\mathcal{D}^{b}(\operatorname{Coh}-X).

Structure of the paper.
In Section 2 we define the notations and list some properties of the quotient stacks involved. In Section 3 we construct the Azumaya algebras in question in terms of Clifford algebras and provide the proofs of the main theorems. In Section 4 we construct the associated Severi-Brauer varieties as geometric realizations of the Brauer classes. In Section 5 we discuss further research directions and open problems.

In the original setting, Kuznetsov considered the double cover

Specifically, let us define the stacky resolution
\mathscr{Y}
of the ramified double cover

\mathbb{C}[u]:=\mathbb{C}[u_{1},u_{2},u_{3}]

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{C}[u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},y_{+},y_{-}]/\langle y_{+}^{2}-f_{+},\,y_{-}^{2}-f_{-}\rangle,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{C}[u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},y]/\langle y^{2}-f_{+}f_{-}\rangle.

These algebras are related by the injective algebra homomorphism

which sends
u_{i}\mapsto u_{i}
and
y\mapsto y_{+}y_{-}
. Also note that both

and

are free
{\mathbb{C}}[u]

\{1,y_{+},y_{-},y_{+}y_{-}\}
and

\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}^{*}

\displaystyle\lambda\cdot(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},y_{+},y_{-})

\displaystyle:=(\lambda^{2}u_{1},\lambda^{2}u_{2},\lambda^{2}u_{3},\lambda^{3}y_{+},\lambda^{3}y_{-}),

\displaystyle t\cdot(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},y)

\displaystyle:=(tu_{1},tu_{2},tu_{3},t^{3}y).

Here we added subscripts
\lambda

[(\operatorname{Spec}A\backslash 0)/\mathbb{C}_{\lambda}^{*}]\to[(\operatorname{Spec}B\backslash 0)/\mathbb{C}_{t}^{*}].

Here and for the remainder of the paper, we abbreviate

\operatorname{Spec}A\backslash 0:=(\operatorname{Spec}A)\,\backslash\,\{(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})=0\}=(\operatorname{Spec}A)\,\backslash\,\{(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},y_{+},y_{-})=0\},

The

Then the ramified double cover

We now explore the smoothness conditions on

Proposition 2.2
.

The variety

\operatorname{Spec}A\backslash 0
is smooth if and only if
E_{+}
,
E_{-}
are smooth and intersect transversely. In particular, it is smooth for generic choices of

Proof.

The curves
E_{+}
,
E_{-}
are smooth if and only if their Jacobians
[\partial f_{+}/\partial x_{i}^{\pm}]
and
[\partial f_{-}/\partial x_{i}^{\pm}]
are non-vanishing on themselves, and
E_{+}
intersects
E_{-}
transversely means that the
2\times 6
matrix

\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial f_{+}}{\partial x_{1}^{+}}&\frac{\partial f_{+}}{\partial x_{2}^{+}}&\dots&\frac{\partial f_{+}}{\partial x_{3}^{-}}\\
\frac{\partial f_{-}}{\partial x_{1}^{+}}&\frac{\partial f_{-}}{\partial x_{2}^{+}}&\dots&\frac{\partial f_{-}}{\partial x_{3}^{-}}\end{bmatrix}

has rank
2
at the points of
E_{+}\cap E_{-}
. The Jacobian for
\langle y_{+}^{2}-f_{+},\,y_{-}^{2}-f_{-}\rangle
is the

\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc|cc}-\frac{\partial f_{+}}{\partial x_{1}^{+}}&-\frac{\partial f_{+}}{\partial x_{2}^{+}}&\dots&-\frac{\partial f_{+}}{\partial x_{3}^{-}}&2y_{+}&0\\
-\frac{\partial f_{-}}{\partial x_{1}^{+}}&-\frac{\partial f_{-}}{\partial x_{2}^{+}}&\dots&-\frac{\partial f_{-}}{\partial x_{3}^{-}}&0&2y_{-}\end{array}\right]

so it has rank
2
at the points of

because of the second block matrix. Looking at the first block matrix, we see that it has rank

at the points of

precisely when

E_{-}
are smooth and intersect transversely.

Proposition 2.3

The

To compute the stabilizer, we require that

, whose projectivizations are projective bundles that are locally trivial in the étale topology, namely the Severi-Brauer varieties. We will now construct projective bundles on
\mathscr{Y}

To construct the Brauer class on
\operatorname{Spec}A\backslash 0

(1)

Construct two

(2)

Construct a
\mathbb{P}^{3}
-bundle on

In the second half of this section, we will also relate the

Recall from Section 2 that
W
parametrizes quadratic forms

and each

can be decomposed into

. Consider the conic fibration
C
over
W\backslash 0
whose fiber over a point

is the conic
\{q_{u}^{+}=0\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}V_{+}
. Because we restrict to a general family of such quadratic forms

is smooth by Bertini’s Theorem, because

is a hyperplane of degree
(1,2)
in
(W\backslash 0)\times\mathbb{P}V_{+}
. These are nonsingular conics for
u\in W\backslash\operatorname{cone}(E_{+})
so we have a generic
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration, while the fibers over
u\in\operatorname{cone}(E_{+})
are pairs of lines. It is not possible to somehow replace the pairs of lines by a smooth
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
over

, but it becomes possible to do so on the double cover

\operatorname{Spec}A_{+}\backslash 0
.
We will present 2 ways to construct such

-bundles.

First method is the following: take the conic fibration, denoted
\operatorname{adj}(C)
, over
W\backslash 0
whose fiber over a point
u\in W\backslash 0
is the conic
\{\operatorname{adj}(q_{u}^{+})=0\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}V_{+}^{\vee}
in the dual projective space, where
\operatorname{adj}(q_{u}^{+})
denotes the quadratic form whose associated matrix is the adjugate matrix of
q_{u}^{+}
. The general fibers are then dual conics and the fibers over
u\in E_{+}
become double lines. Over
W\backslash\operatorname{cone}(E_{+})
, we have a birational map
C\to\operatorname{adj}(C)
arising from the dual curve map. For convenience, denote the pullback of
\operatorname{adj}(C)
to the ramified double cover
\operatorname{Spec}A_{+}\backslash 0
by

\operatorname{adj}(C_{+}):=\operatorname{adj}(C)\times_{(W\backslash 0)}(\operatorname{Spec}A_{+}\backslash 0).

Take the normalization
P_{+}
of

will be a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle, as the next proposition shows.

Proposition 4.1
.

The variety
P_{+}
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
\operatorname{Spec}A_{+}\backslash 0
.

Proof.

To see this, restrict to a Zariski neighborhood
U
of a point
u_{0}
lying over
\operatorname{cone}(E_{+})
in
\operatorname{Spec}A_{+}\backslash 0
. The quadratic forms can be simultaneously diagonalized to have the form (abusing notations):

q_{u}^{+}=\begin{bmatrix}a(u)&0&0\\
0&b(u)&0\\
0&0&\epsilon(u)\end{bmatrix}

where
\epsilon(u_{0})=0
and
a(u)
,
b(u)\neq 0
for
u\in U
, i.e.
a
and
b
are units in
\mathbb{C}[U]
. Its adjugate matrix is

\operatorname{adj}(q_{u}^{+})=\begin{bmatrix}b(u)\epsilon(u)&0&0\\
0&a(u)\epsilon(u)&0\\
0&0&a(u)b(u)\end{bmatrix}

so at
u_{0}
it is

\operatorname{adj}(q_{u_{0}}^{+})=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&a(u_{0})b(u_{0})\end{bmatrix}

and in the coordinates
x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},x_{3}^{+}
of
V_{+}
the fiber is the double line
(x_{3}^{+})^{2}=0
.

Now we verify that the normalization is smooth. By picking complementary coordinates
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
to the local coordinate
\epsilon
, we identify the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[U]
with
\mathbb{C}[u_{1},u_{2},\epsilon]
. The coordinate ring of
\operatorname{adj}(C)
over
U
is thus

\mathbb{C}[U][y_{+},x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},x_{3}^{+}]/\langle\,y_{+}^{2}-ab\epsilon,\,b\epsilon(x_{1}^{+})^{2}+a\epsilon(x_{2}^{+})^{2}+ab(x_{3}^{+})^{2}\,\rangle.

We observe that

\left(\frac{x_{3}^{+}}{y_{+}}\right)^{2}=\frac{(x_{3}^{+})^{2}}{(ab\epsilon)^{2}}=-\frac{(x_{1}^{+})^{2}}{a^{2}b}-\frac{(x_{2}^{+})^{2}}{ab^{2}}

belongs to the coordinate ring but
x_{3}^{+}/y_{+}
does not. We adjoin
\widetilde{x_{3}}^{+}=x_{3}^{+}/y_{+}
and show that the resulting ring

\mathbb{C}[U][y_{+},x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},\widetilde{x_{3}}^{+}]\bigg/\left\langle\,y_{+}^{2}-ab\epsilon,\,\frac{(x_{1}^{+})^{2}}{a^{2}b}+\frac{(x_{2}^{+})^{2}}{ab^{2}}+(\widetilde{x_{3}}^{+})^{2}\,\right\rangle

is regular. The Jacobian matrix of partial derivatives of the relations with respect to
u_{1}
,
u_{2}
,
\epsilon
,
y_{+}
,
x_{1}^{+}
,
x_{2}^{+}
,
\widetilde{x_{3}}^{+}
is the following.

\begin{bmatrix}-\epsilon\frac{\partial(ab)}{\partial u_{1}}&-\epsilon\frac{\partial(ab)}{\partial u_{2}}&-ab-\epsilon\frac{\partial(ab)}{\partial\epsilon}&2y_{+}&0&0&0\\
*&*&*&0&\frac{2x_{1}^{+}}{a^{2}b}&\frac{2x_{2}^{+}}{ab^{2}}&2\widetilde{x_{3}}^{+}\end{bmatrix}

Since
x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},\widetilde{x_{3}}^{+}
are coordinates on
\mathbb{P}V_{+}
and cannot be all zero, the matrix has rank
1
precisely when the first row is a multiple of the second row. This forces it to be the zero row, so
y_{+}=0
and thus
\epsilon=0
by the relation, but then
ab\neq 0
in the third entry. So there is no point at which the matrix has rank
1
.

Since all the fibers of
P_{+}
are smooth conics given by
\frac{(x_{1}^{+})^{2}}{a^{2}b}+\frac{(x_{2}^{+})^{2}}{ab^{2}}+(\widetilde{x_{3}}^{+})^{2}=0
, we obtain a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle.
∎

In the second method, we start from the pullback

The singular locus of

Observe that

In the other direction, we likewise locally diagonalize

So it suffices to show the following.

Proposition 4.7

The bundle

Let

\mathbb{P}M_{0}\to P^{\prime}\cup P^{\prime\prime}
sending
\mathbb{C}m\mapsto\operatorname{Ann}(m)
. By symmetry, there is also a map from
\mathbb{P}M_{1}\to P^{\prime}\cup P^{\prime\prime}
sending
\mathbb{C}m\mapsto\operatorname{Ann}(m)
.

It follows from the continuity of the map that two
m,m^{\prime}
from the same
M_{i}
will be sent to the same ruling, i.e. to the same
P^{\prime}
or
P^{\prime\prime}
.
For the inverse, and send an isotropic subspace
\mathbb{P}V^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}
in
\mathbb{P}V
to

\ker(V^{\prime})=\{m\in M:V^{\prime}\cdot m=0\text{ in }M\}.

Pick a complementary isotropic subspace
\mathbb{P}V^{\prime\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{P}V
from the other ruling of the quadric, then
V=V^{\prime}\oplus V^{\prime\prime}
. We can then identify
M_{0}=(\wedge V^{\prime\prime})_{ev}=(\wedge V^{\prime})_{odd}
,
M_{1}=(\wedge V^{\prime\prime})_{odd}=(\wedge V^{\prime})_{ev}
(note that we cannot distinguish
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
and the codomain of this map depends on the choice of the roles of
V^{\prime}
and
V^{\prime\prime}
), and from this description we see that
\ker(V^{\prime})=\wedge^{3}V^{\prime}\subseteq M_{0}
has dimension
1
, hence we get an element in
\mathbb{P}M_{0}
. This gives a map
P^{\prime}\cup P^{\prime\prime}\to\mathbb{P}M_{0}
. It is then evident that the two maps are inverses to each others.

Again from continuity, two
V^{\prime},V^{\prime\prime}
from the same ruling will both be sent to the same one of
\mathbb{P}M_{0}
or
\mathbb{P}M_{1}
.
∎

Let
Y^{\prime}\to W\backslash\operatorname{cone}(E_{+}\cup E_{-})
be the restriction of the ramified double cover
\operatorname{Spec}B\to W
. Then
Y^{\prime}
is unramified and we have the following:

Proposition 4.9
.

P^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime\prime}
can be combined into one
\mathbb{P}^{3}
-bundle
P_{d}
over
Y^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The two irreducible modules of
Cl_{ev}
are classified by the action of the central element, which can be one of the two roots
y_{1}
,
y_{2}
of
y^{2}-\det(q)=y^{2}-f_{+}f_{-}
. Once we made a choice of correspondence between
P^{\prime}
,
P^{\prime\prime}
and the two modules as in Proposition
4.8
, say
P^{\prime}\xleftrightarrow{}y_{1}
and
P^{\prime\prime}\xleftrightarrow{}y_{2}
, we can define the
\mathbb{P}^{3}
-bundle
P_{d}
for which the fiber over
(u,y_{1})\in\operatorname{Spec}B
is the fiber of
P^{\prime}
, and the fiber over
(u,y_{2})
is the fiber of
P^{\prime\prime}
. It is well-defined as we can choose a fixed square root over an étale neighborhood, showing that it is a
\mathbb{P}^{3}
-bundle in the étale topology.
∎

Let
C^{\prime}_{\pm}
denote the quadric fibration
\{q_{u}^{\pm}=0\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}V_{\pm}
over the unramified double cover
Y^{\prime}
.

Proposition 4.10
.

There exist embedding of
C^{\prime}_{+}\times_{Y^{\prime}}C^{\prime}_{-}
into
P_{d}
such that fiber-wise it is the Segre embedding
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Proof.

For
(u,y)\in Y^{\prime}
,
p_{+}\in\{q_{u}^{+}=0\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}V_{+}
and
p_{+}\in\{q_{u}^{+}=0\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}V_{-}
, the line
\ell
joining
p_{+}
and
p_{-}
in
\mathbb{P}V
is isotropic with respect to
q_{u}
:

q_{u}(ap_{+}+bp_{-},ap_{+}+bp_{-})=q_{u}(ap_{+},ap_{+})+2q_{u}(ap_{+},bp_{-})+q_{u}(bp_{-},bp_{-})=0.

The line
\ell
is contained in the two isotropic subspaces of dimension
2
with respect to
q_{u}
. From here we obtain a globally defined map
C^{\prime}_{+}\times_{Y^{\prime}}C^{\prime}_{-}\to P_{d}
by sending
\ell
to the point in the fiber of
P_{d}
over
(u,y)
which corresponds to the isotropic subspace that contains
\ell
. Now we show that étale locally they are Segre embeddings.

Fix an
u
. We can model the full rank quadric as
q_{u}=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}^{2}-x_{4}^{2}+x_{5}x_{6}
where
V_{+}
has coordinates
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
and
V_{-}
has coordinates
x_{4},x_{5},x_{6}
. By the change of variables

x_{1}=z_{12},\quad x_{2}=z_{34},\quad x_{3}=\frac{z_{13}-z_{24}}{2},\quad x_{4}=\frac{z_{13}+z_{24}}{2},\quad x_{5}=z_{14},\quad x_{6}=z_{23},

we can identify the quadric as the image
z_{12}z_{34}-z_{13}z_{24}+z_{14}z_{23}=0
of the Plücker embedding
G(2,4)\xhookrightarrow{}\wedge^{2}\mathbb{C}^{4}\simeq\mathbb{C}^{6}
where we use the basis
\{e_{ij}=e_{i}\wedge e_{j}:i<j\}
. For the Plücker embedding we can parametrize one
\mathbb{P}^{3}
-family of isotropic subspaces of dimension
2
by

(y_{1}:y_{2}:y_{3}:y_{4})\in\mathbb{P}^{3}\to\mathbb{P}\operatorname{Span}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{4}y_{i}e_{ij}:j=1,\dots,4\right\}.

Points
p_{+}\in\{q_{u}^{+}=x_{1}x_{2}+x_{3}^{2}=0\}

p_{+}=(a_{0}^{2}:-a_{1}^{2}:a_{0}a_{1}:0:0:0)

p_{-}=(0:0:0:a_{2}a_{3}:a_{2}^{2}:a_{3}^{2})
for

. Switching back to the coordinates
z_{ij}
’s, we have
p_{+}=a_{0}^{2}e_{12}-a_{1}^{2}e_{34}+a_{0}a_{1}e_{13}-a_{0}a_{1}e_{24}
and
p_{-}=a_{2}a_{3}e_{13}+a_{2}a_{3}e_{24}+a_{2}^{2}e_{14}+a_{3}^{2}e_{23}

\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&0&0&a_{1}\\

0&0&a_{3}&a_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

We are then looking for
(y_{1}:y_{2}:y_{3}:y_{4})
whose associated isotropic subspace contains both
p_{+}
and
p_{-}

\mathbb{C}^{4}

p_{+}
and
p_{-}

The same is true after pulling

5.
Generalizations and Future Work

We can ask whether some conditions on the root stacks and the corresponding Brauer class ensure certain properties on the Enriques surfaces, and vice versa:

(i)

If we look at the blowup of
Y
instead of its stacky desingularization, then the analog of the quotient
\widehat{\mathscr{Y}}
appears to be the root stack of
the rational elliptic surface at two smooth fibers. How is it related to the construction of Enriques surfaces via logarithmic transformation along these fibers, in particular how does the Brauer class come into play?

(ii)

When is the Enriques surface nodal, i.e. contains a nonsingular rational curve?

(iii)

How do the elements of the Picard group of the Enriques surface corresponds to (complexes of) sheaves on the root stack? What are the corresponding auto-equivalences?

(iv)

In our setup, the 2 cubic curves come from determinants of
3\times 3
matrices. If two quadratic forms give rise to the same cubic curves, i.e. if the root stacks are identical, is there any relation between the corresponding Enriques surfaces?

(v)

What can be said about the Enriques surface if the Brauer class is trivial?

(vi)

Is the orbifold cohomology of the two sides related? There are no twisted sectors in the Enriques surface, but the root stack should have the cubic curves and their intersection points as twisted sectors.

In another direction, direct generalizations of the tensor product construction of the Clifford algebras of invariant subspaces to other dimensions can be studied. For example, one can look at the the double mirrors of a dimension
3
intersection of
4
quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{7}
quotient by free actions of finite groups
[
12
]
.

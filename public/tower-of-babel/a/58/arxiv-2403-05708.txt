Dmitrii Karp, Alexey Kuznetsov

Abstract
. By replacing the Euler gamma function by the Barnes double gamma function in the definition of the Meijer G G -function, we introduce a new family of special functions, which we call K K -functions. This is a very general class of functions, which includes as special cases Meijer G G -functions (thus also all hypergeometric functions F q p {}_{p}F_{q} ) as well as several new functions that appeared recently in the literature. Our goal is to define the K K -function, study its analytic and transformation properties and relate it to several functions that appeared recently in the study of random processes and the fractional Laplacian. We further introduce a generalization of the Kilbas-Saigo function and show that it is a special case of K K -function.

G
-function, double gamma function, Mellin-Barnes integral, Mellin transform, Kilbas-Saigo function

1.
Introduction

Meijer
G
-function (see
[
28
]
and
[
36
]
[Chapter 9.3]) is defined as a Mellin-Barnes type integral

(1)

G_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}a_{1},\dots,a_{p}\\
b_{1},\dots,b_{q}\end{matrix}\Big|z\Big)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\int_{{\mathcal{L}}}\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(b_{j}-s)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(1-a_{j}+s)}{\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{q}\Gamma(1-b_{j}+s)\prod\limits_{j=n+1}^{p}\Gamma(a_{j}-s)}z^{s}{\textnormal{d}}s.

Here
m,n,p,q
are integers such that
0\leq m\leq q
and
0\leq n\leq p
and
a_{j}
,
b_{j}
are complex numbers such that
a_{k}-b_{j}\notin{\mathbb{N}}
for
1\leq k\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
. The contour
{\mathcal{L}}
runs either from
-{\textnormal{i}}\infty
to
{\textnormal{i}}\infty
or it is a loop beginning and ending at
+\infty
(or
-\infty
). In each case the contour
{\mathcal{L}}
separates the points

\{b_{j}+l,\;1\leq j\leq m,\;l\geq 0\},

which are the poles of the functions
\Gamma(b_{j}-s)
,
j=1,2,\dots,m

from the points

\{a_{k}-l,\;1\leq k\leq n,\;l\geq 1\},

which are the poles of
\Gamma(1-a_{j}+s)
,
j=1,2,\dots,n
. We refer the reader to
[
28
]
and
[
36
]
[Chapter 9.3] for complete description of the contour
{\mathcal{L}}
and various properties of the Meijer
G
-function.

The Meijer
G
-function unifies and generalizes most of the standard functions of mathematical physics. The family of
G
-functions is very useful and versatile due to a number of stability properties: it is preserved by taking integrals and derivatives (of integer or fractional order), multiplication by powers, taking reciprocal of the variable, Laplace and Euler transforms and multiplicative convolution. This is the main reason why these functions play an important role in solving the hypergeometric differential equation (of order greater than
2
)
[
17
]
and in fractional calculus
[
20
]
. Meijer’s
G
-function is indispensable in probability and statistics: it represents the density of arbitrary products of beta and gamma distributed random variables
[
8
]
, distribution of many likelihood ratio tests
[
27
,
34
]
, stationary distribution of certain Markov chains
[
7
,
11
]
. Moreover, it has been observed that some universality laws in random matrix theory are represented by
G
-function kernels
[
18
]
. Some optical transfer functions have been recently shown to be expressible in terms of Meijer’s
G
-function
[
10
]
.
When the Meijer
G
-function has a well-defined Mellin transform, multiplying or dividing that Mellin transform by gamma functions and taking the inverse Mellin transform often leads to another Meijer
G
-function. This is the main idea behind results in
[
12
]
, where it was shown that the fractional Laplace operator maps certain Meijer
G
-functions into other Meijer
G
-functions.

In the last fifteen years a number of papers

[
16
,
21
,
23
,
24
,
31
]
appeared in the literature on probability theory, where certain random variables had densities given by functions defined by Mellin-Barnes integrals similar to (
1
), except that the gamma functions are replaced by double gamma functions
G(z;\tau)
or
\Gamma_{2}(z;a,b)
. The first such example is the density function of the supremum of a stable process, it was studied in detail in
[
14
,
15
,
22
]
and was found to have very unusual series representation properties.

Our main goal in this paper is to define an extension of the Meijer
G
-function that would cover all the examples that appeared so far in the literature and to study some basic properties of this new special function. Thus, in Section
2
we introduce the Barnes double gamma function
G(z;\tau)
and present its main properties that will be used later in the paper. In Section
3
we define the extension of the Meijer
G
-function, which we call a
K
-function. In this section we also state analyticity properties of the
K
-function and several transformation identities. In Sections
4
we present results on the asymptotics of
K
-function when
z\to 0
or
z\to\infty
. Section
5
is devoted to Mellin transform properties of this function and to deriving integral equations satisfied by the
K
-function. In section
6
we give examples of various functions that have already appeared in the literature and show how to express them in terms of
K
-function. In
Appendix
A
we discuss an alternative definition of the
K
-function, where one would use

\Gamma_{2}(z;a,b)
(a symmetric version of the double gamma function) instead of Barnes’ version
G(z;\tau)
and we show that this would lead to essentially the same
K
-function, modulo a parameter change.

2.
Preliminaries: Barnes double gamma function

Everywhere in this paper we will use the following notation. We denote by

{\mathbb{C}}^{+}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\;:\;\textnormal{Im}(z)>0\},\;\;\;{\mathbb{C}}^{-}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\;:\;\textnormal{Im}(z)<0\}

the upper and lower complex half-planes. We take the principal branch of the logarithm function (with a branch cut along the negative half-line). For
z
on the Riemann surface of the logarithm function we allow
\arg(z)\in(-\infty,\infty)
and if
z\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
then
\arg(z)\in(-\pi,\pi)
.

The double gamma function
G(z;\tau)
was introduced by Alekseevsky in 1888-1889 (see
[
29
]
) and a decade later it was studied extensively by Barnes
[
3
,
4
]
. Now it is usually called Barnes (or Alekseevsky-Barnes) double gamma function and we will follow this convention in this paper. The function
G(z;\tau)
is an entire function of
z
of order two defined via the Weierstrass infinite product representation:

(2)

G(z;\tau)=\frac{z}{\tau}e^{a(\tau)\frac{z}{\tau}+b(\tau)\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau}}\prod\limits_{m\geq 0}\prod\limits_{n\geq 0}{}^{{}^{\prime}}\left(1+\frac{z}{m\tau+n}\right)e^{-\frac{z}{m\tau+n}+\frac{z^{2}}{2(m\tau+n)^{2}}},\;\;\;|\arg(\tau)|<\pi,\;\;\;z\in{\mathbb{C}},

where the prime in the second product means that the term corresponding to
m=n=0
is omitted.
The functions
a(\tau)
and
b(\tau)
in (
2
) can be expressed in terms of
the gamma modular forms

C(\tau)
and
D(\tau)

\displaystyle C(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\lim\limits_{m\to\infty}\bigg[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}\psi(k\tau)+\frac{1}{2}\psi(m\tau)-\frac{1}{\tau}\ln\left(\frac{\Gamma(m\tau)}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)\bigg],

\displaystyle D(\tau)

\displaystyle:=\lim\limits_{m\to+\infty}\bigg[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}\psi^{\prime}(k\tau)+\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime}(m\tau)-\frac{1}{\tau}\psi(m\tau)\bigg],

as follows

(3)

a(\tau):=-\gamma\tau+\frac{\tau}{2}\ln(2\pi\tau)+\frac{1}{2}\ln(\tau)-\tau C(\tau),\;\;b(\tau):=-\frac{\pi^{2}\tau^{2}}{6}-\tau\ln(\tau)-\tau^{2}D(\tau),

where
\psi(z)=\Gamma^{\prime}(z)/\Gamma(z)
is the digamma function (see
[
36
]
) and
\gamma=-\psi(1)=0.577215...
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

Barnes also gave another infinite product representation for
G(z;\tau)
:

(4)

G(z;\tau)=\frac{1}{\tau\Gamma(z)}e^{\tilde{a}(\tau)\frac{z}{\tau}+\tilde{b}(\tau)\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau^{2}}}\prod\limits_{m\geq 0}\frac{\Gamma(m\tau)}{\Gamma(z+m\tau)}e^{z\psi(m\tau)+\frac{z^{2}}{2}\psi^{\prime}(m\tau)},

where the functions
\tilde{a}(\tau)
and
\tilde{b}(\tau)
are given by

(5)

\tilde{a}(\tau)=a(\tau)-\gamma\tau,\qquad\tilde{b}(\tau)=b(\tau)+\frac{\pi^{2}\tau^{2}}{6}.

The double gamma function has a number of useful properties. First of all, we have a normalization condition
G(1;\tau)=1
. Second, the double gamma function is quasi-periodic with periods
1
and
\tau

(6)

G(z+1;\tau)=\Gamma\left(\frac{z}{\tau}\right)G(z;\tau),\;\;\;G(z+\tau;\tau)=(2\pi)^{\frac{\tau-1}{2}}\tau^{-z+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(z)G(z;\tau).

It is clear from the Weierstrass product (
2
) that the function
G(z;\tau)
has zeros on the lattice
m\tau+n
,
m\leq 0
,
n\leq 0
and that the zeros are all simple if and only if
\tau
is not a rational number.
The double gamma function satisfies other important identities:

(i)

Modular transformation

(7)

G(z;\tau)=(2\pi)^{\frac{z}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{z-z^{2}}{2\tau}+\frac{z}{2}-1}G\left(\frac{z}{\tau};\frac{1}{\tau}\right).

(ii)

A multiplication formula: for any integers
p,q\in{\mathbb{N}}

(8)

G(z;p\tau/q)=q^{\frac{1}{2p\tau}(z-1)(qz-p\tau)}(2\pi)^{-\frac{q-1}{2}(z-1)}\prod\limits_{i=0}^{p-1}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{q-1}\frac{G((z+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)}{G((1+i)/p+j\tau/q;\tau)}.

(iii)

A product identity

(9)

G(z;\tau)=\Big(\frac{1+\tau}{\tau}\Big)^{\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau}-(1+\tau)\frac{z}{2\tau}+1}(2\pi)^{-\frac{z}{2\tau}}G(z+1;1+\tau)G(z/\tau;1+1/\tau).

The proofs of (
7
) and
(
8
) can be found in
[
4
]
. For the proof of (iii) see
[
1
]
.

The double gamma function
G(z;\tau)
is closely related to the symmetric version of this function, denoted by
\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
, see
[
4
]
(note that this function is denoted by
\Gamma^{B}
in
[
32
]
and some other papers). We consider this function only for
\omega_{1},\omega_{2}>0
. It is known that
\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
is symmetric in
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
and
satisfies the functional identities

(10)

\Gamma_{2}(z+\omega_{1};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{\omega_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{z}{\omega_{2}}}}{\Gamma(\frac{z}{\omega_{2}})}\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),\;\;\;\Gamma_{2}(z+\omega_{2};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\sqrt{2\pi}\frac{\omega_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{z}{\omega_{1}}}}{\Gamma(\frac{z}{\omega_{1}})}\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2}),

and the normalization condition

\Gamma_{2}(\omega_{1};\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\sqrt{2\pi/\omega_{2}}
. As Barnes established in §26 in

[
4
]
, the function
\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
can be expressed in terms of
G(z;\tau)
as follows:

(11)

\Gamma_{2}(z;\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=(2\pi)^{\frac{z}{2\omega_{1}}}\omega_{2}^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}+\frac{z(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})}{2\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}-1}G\Big(\frac{z}{\omega_{1}};\frac{\omega_{2}}{\omega_{1}}\Big)^{-1}.

There exists a Binet type integral representation for the double gamma function, which we state in the next proposition. This representation is implicitly contained in the results of Barnes

[
4
]
, however, we were not able to locate this formula in this exact form in the existing literature and thus we provide a proof here.

Proposition 1
.

For
\textnormal{Re}(z)>0
and
\textnormal{Re}(\tau)>0
we have

(12)

\displaystyle\ln G(z;\tau)

\displaystyle=(a_{2}(\tau)z^{2}+a_{1}(\tau)z+a_{0}(\tau))\ln(z)+b_{2}(\tau)z^{2}+b_{1}(\tau)z+b_{0}(\tau)-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zx}f_{3}(x){\textnormal{d}}x,

where

f_{3}(x):=\frac{1}{x^{3}}\Big(f(x)-f(0)-f^{\prime}(0)x-\frac{1}{2}f^{\prime\prime}(0)x^{2}\Big),\;{\textnormal{ and }}\;f(x):=\frac{x^{2}}{(1-e^{-x})(1-e^{-\tau x})}.

In the above formula, we have

a_{1}(\tau)=-(1+\tau)/(2\tau)
,
a_{2}(\tau)=1/(2\tau)
,
b_{2}(\tau)=-(3/2+\ln(\tau))/(2\tau)
and the explicit expressions for other coefficients can be found in
[
1
,
5
]
.

Proof.

Assume first that
\tau>0
. Formulas (3.13), (3.18), (3.19) in
[
32
]
with
N=M=2
and
a_{1}=1
,
a_{2}=\tau
imply that (note that our
\Gamma_{2}
is
\Gamma_{2}^{B}
in
[
32
]
, up to a multiplicative constant)
for
\textnormal{Re}(z)>0

\ln\Gamma_{2}(z;1,\tau)=\tilde{P}(z;\tau)\ln(z)+\tilde{Q}(z;\tau)+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zx}f_{3}(x){\textnormal{d}}x,

where
\tilde{P}(z;\tau)
and
\tilde{Q}(z;\tau)
are polynomials of degree two in
z
-variable (with coefficients depending on
\tau
). Using the above result and formula (
11
) we conclude that for
\textnormal{Re}(z)>0

(13)

\ln G(z;\tau)=P(z;\tau)\ln(z)+Q(z;\tau)-\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zx}f_{3}(x){\textnormal{d}}x,

for some polynomials

\displaystyle P(z;\tau)

\displaystyle=a_{2}(\tau)z^{2}+a_{1}(\tau)z+a_{0}(\tau),

\displaystyle Q(z;\tau)

\displaystyle=b_{2}(\tau)z^{2}+b_{1}(\tau)z+b_{0}(\tau).

Using the fact that
f_{3}(x)
is analytic in the neighbourhood of
x=0
and applying Watson’s Lemma, we conclude that

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zx}f_{3}(x){\textnormal{d}}x=O(1/z)

as
z\to+\infty
along positive half-line
z\in(0,\infty)
. Thus formula (
13
) gives an asymptotic representation of
\ln G(z;\tau)
as
z\to+\infty
, and comparing the coefficients of this asymptotic expansion with those obtained by Billingham and King in
[
5
]
, we find the values of the coefficients
a_{1}(\tau)
,
a_{2}(\tau)
and
b_{2}(\tau)
. This ends the proof of (
12
) for
\tau>0
and the general case when
\textnormal{Re}(\tau)>0
follows by analytic continuation.

\sqcap\kern-8.0pt\hbox{$\sqcup$}

We will also require the following asymptotic expansion of
\ln G(z;\tau)
.

Proposition 2
.

For any fixed
\tau>0
, we have

(14)

\ln G(z;\tau)=\Big(\frac{1}{2\tau}z^{2}-\frac{1+\tau}{2\tau}z+a_{0}(\tau)\Big)\ln(z)-\frac{1}{2\tau}\Big(\frac{3}{2}+\ln(\tau)\Big)z^{2}+b_{1}(\tau)z+b_{0}(\tau)+O(|z|^{-1}),

as
z\to\infty
, uniformly in any sector
|\arg(z)|<\pi-\epsilon<\pi
.

This asymptotic result follows from the asymptotic formula for
\Gamma_{2}(z;a,b)
, derived by Barnes in
[
4
]
.
This asymptotic relation was rediscovered by Billingham and King in
[
5
]
(see also
[
1
]
, where explicit formula for all terms in the complete asymptotic expansion is given). Neither Barnes nor Billingham and King state explicitly that the asymptotic relation (
14
) holds uniformly in the sector
|\arg(z)|<\pi-\epsilon<\pi
, though this fact does follow implicitly from Barnes’ proof of (
14
). The proof that (
14
) is uniform in the given sector can be obtained fairly quickly from formula (
12
). We will only sketch the main steps here. We fix
\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
and rotate the contour of integration in the integral on the right-hand side of (
12
), so that after a change of variable
x=e^{{\textnormal{i}}\theta}y
we obtain

(15)

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zx}f_{3}(x){\textnormal{d}}x=e^{{\textnormal{i}}\theta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-e^{{\textnormal{i}}\theta}zy}f_{3}(e^{{\textnormal{i}}\theta}y){\textnormal{d}}y.

The rotation of the contour of integration is justified, since for
\tau>0
the function
f_{3}(x)
is analytic and bounded in any sector
|\arg(x)|<\pi/2-\epsilon
.
It is clear that the integral on the right-hand side of
(
15
) is
O(1/z)
as
z\to\infty
, uniformly in any sector
|\theta+\arg(z)|<\pi/2-\epsilon
. Since
\theta
can be an arbitrary number in the interval
(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
, we obtain that the desired result (
14
) is uniform in
|\arg(z)|<\pi-\epsilon
.

3.
Definition of the Meijer-Barnes
K
function

We assume that
m,n,p,q
are non-negative integers and denote
{\bf a}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{p})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p}
and

{\bf b}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{q})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{q}
.
If
{\bf a}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{p}
is a vector and
c
is a complex number, we will denote by
c{\bf a}
and
c+{\bf a}
vectors of length
p
having elements
ca_{i}
and
c+a_{i}
, respectively.
For
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
we define

(16)

\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}(s)=\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s;\tau\Big):=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}G(b_{j}-s;\tau)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}G(1+\tau-a_{j}+s;\tau)}{\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{q}G(1+\tau-b_{j}+s;\tau)\prod\limits_{j=n+1}^{p}G(a_{j}-s;\tau)}.

This function is an analogue of the product/ratio of gamma functions in (
1
). We define the two sets

(17)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{+}:

\displaystyle=\{a_{j}+l\tau+k\;:\;n+1\leq j\leq p,\;l\geq 0,\;k\geq 0\},

\displaystyle\Lambda^{-}:

\displaystyle=\{b_{j}-l\tau-k\;:\;m+1\leq j\leq q,\;l\geq 1,\;k\geq 1\}.

From the properties of the double gamma function, it is clear that
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}(s)
is a meromorphic function having poles precisely at the points in
\Lambda^{+}\cup\Lambda^{-}
.

Next we introduce the parameters:

(18)

\begin{split}N&:=2(m+n)-p-q,\\
\nu&:=\sum_{j=1}^{p}a_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{q}b_{j},\\
\mu&:=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}-\sum\limits_{j=n+1}^{p}a_{j}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}-\sum\limits_{j=m+1}^{q}b_{j},\\
\xi&:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j=n+1}^{p}a_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{q}b_{j}^{2}.\end{split}

In the following proposition we collect several transformation properties of
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}(s)
.

Proposition 3
.

For
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
and
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
the following identities hold:

(19)

\mathcal{G}_{q,p}^{n,m}\Big(\begin{matrix}1+\tau-{\bf b}\\
1+\tau-{\bf a}\end{matrix}\Big|-s;\tau\Big)=\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s;\tau\Big)=\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}c+{\bf a}\\
c+{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|c+s;\tau\Big),

and

(20)

\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s;\tau\Big)=A\times B^{-s}\times\tau^{-\frac{Ns^{2}}{2\tau}}\times\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}\tau^{-1}{\bf a}\\
\tau^{-1}{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s\tau^{-1};\tau^{-1}\Big),

where

(21)

A:=(2\pi)^{\frac{1-\tau}{2\tau}(\nu-(1+\tau)(n+m-q))}\tau^{\frac{1}{2\tau}((1+\tau)\mu-\xi)-N},\;\;\;B:=(2\pi)^{\frac{1-\tau}{2\tau}(p-q)}\tau^{\frac{1}{2\tau}(N(1+\tau)-2\mu)}.

Proof.

The two identities in (
19
) follow immediately from
(
16
). The proof of (
20
) follows from modular transformation (
7
)
and can be found in Appendix
B
.

\sqcap\kern-8.0pt\hbox{$\sqcup$}

For
\epsilon\in(0,\pi)
we introduce two sectors

{\mathcal{S}}^{\pm}_{\epsilon}:=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\;:\;z\neq 0,\;\epsilon\leq\pm\arg(z)\leq\pi-\epsilon\}.

The next proposition states the asymptotic behavior of
\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s;\tau\Big)
as
s\to\infty
in sectors
{\mathcal{S}}^{\pm}_{\epsilon}
.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\pi)
and
\tau>0
. Denote
\chi=\sign(\textnormal{Im}(s))
. Then as
s\to\infty
in sectors
{\mathcal{S}}^{\pm}_{\epsilon}
we have

(i)

if
N\neq 0
, then

(22)

\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s;\tau\Big)=\exp\Big(\frac{N}{2\tau}s^{2}\ln(s)+O(|s|^{2})\Big);

(ii)

if
N=0
, we have

(23)

\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s;\tau\Big)=\exp\Big(\frac{\pi{\textnormal{i}}\chi}{4\tau}(p-q)s^{2}-\frac{1}{\tau}\mu s\ln(s)+O(|s|)\Big);

(iii)

if
N=p-q=0
, we have

(24)

\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s;\tau\Big)=\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{\tau}\mu s\ln(s)+\frac{1}{\tau}(1+\ln(\tau))\mu s+\frac{\pi{\textnormal{i}}\chi}{2\tau}(\mu-\nu)s+O(\ln|s|)\Big);

(iv)

If
N=p-q=\mu=\nu=0
, then

(25)

\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s;\tau\Big)=\exp\Big(\frac{1}{2\tau}\xi\ln|s|+O(1)\Big).

All these asymptotic results hold uniformly as
s\to\infty
in the corresponding sectors.

Proof.

We denote the coefficients of the asymptotic expansion in (
14
) by
A_{1}=1/(2\tau)
,
A_{2}=-(3/2+\ln(\tau))/(2\tau)
,
A_{3}=-(1+\tau)/(2\tau)
,
A_{4}=a_{0}(\tau)
and
A_{5}=b_{1}(\tau)
. Using the asymptotic expansion (
14
) and the following result

\ln(c+s)=\ln(s)+c/s+O(s^{-2}),\;\;\;s\to\infty,

we find that

(26)

\displaystyle\ln(G(c+s;\tau))

\displaystyle=A_{1}s^{2}\ln(s)+A_{2}s^{2}+(A_{3}+2cA_{1})s\ln(s)

\displaystyle+(A_{5}+c(A_{1}+2A_{2}))s+(A_{1}c^{2}+A_{3}c+A_{4})\ln(s)+O(1),

as
s\to\infty
staying in
{\mathcal{S}}^{+}_{\epsilon}
. We recall that we consider the principal branch of the logarithm, for which we have

\ln(-s)=\ln(s)-\pi{\textnormal{i}},\;\;\;s\in{\mathbb{C}}^{+},

From this formula and from (
26
) we obtain

\displaystyle\ln(G(c-s;\tau))

=(2\pi)^{(\tau-1)(p-q)/2}\tau^{\mu-N(\tau+1/2)}\frac{e^{\pi\alpha\tau}}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}{z^{\tau}}}\int\limits_{\gamma-\tau}H(s)\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\

6.
Examples of
K
function in applications

Next we present several examples where
K
-functions appear in applications. All examples in this section will be stated in terms of the function
K_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|z;\tau\Big)
(for which
\alpha=0
), therefore in this section
\alpha
will be used in different meaning.

6.1.
Extrema of stable processes

Let
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be an
\alpha
-stable Lévy process started from
X_{0}=0
, see
[
26
]
.
Stable processes are parameterized by a pair
(\alpha,\rho)
, where
\alpha\in(0,2)
is called the stability parameter (we emphasize again that this
\alpha
plays different role than
\alpha
in (
28
)). The parameter
\rho:={\mathbb{P}}(X_{1}>0)
is called the positivity parameter and it satisfies the inequalities
\rho\in(0,1)
and
\alpha-1<\alpha\rho<1
.
Stable distributions are closely related to gamma functions via Mellin transform. For example, it is well known (see
[
26
, Theorem 1.13]
) that

{\mathbb{E}}[(X_{1})^{z}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{X_{1}>0\}}]=\frac{1}{\pi}\sin(\pi\rho z)\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z/\alpha),\;\;\;-1<\textnormal{Re}(z)<\alpha.

Let us define the running supremum of the process
X
as follows

S_{t}:=\sup\limits_{0\leq s\leq t}X_{s}.

It was shown in
[
21
]
that the Mellin transform of the random variable
S_{1}
is given by

(47)

{\mathbb{E}}[(S_{1})^{z}]=\alpha^{z}\frac{G(\alpha\rho;\alpha)}{G(\alpha(1-\rho)+1;\alpha)}\times\frac{G(\alpha(1-\rho)+1-z;\alpha)}{G(\alpha\rho+z;\alpha)}\times\frac{G(\alpha+z;\alpha)}{G(\alpha-z;\alpha)},

where
-\alpha\rho<\textnormal{Re}(z)<\alpha
.
Writing the probability density function
p_{S_{1}}(x)
(of the random variable
S_{1}
) via the inverse Mellin transform and applying (
32
) we obtain the following representation (this corresponds to
\tau=\alpha
and
\alpha=0
in (
30
))

(48)

p_{S_{1}}(x)=\frac{G(\alpha\rho;\alpha)}{G(\alpha(1-\rho)+1;\alpha)}x^{-1}K_{2,2}^{1,1}\Big(\begin{matrix}1,\alpha\\
1+\alpha(1-\rho),1+\alpha(1-\rho)\end{matrix}\Big|x/\alpha;\alpha\Big).

We compute
N=p-q=0
,
\mu=1-\alpha
and
\nu=\alpha(2\rho-1)-1
. If
\alpha\neq 1
the parameters correspond to a balanced case and if
\alpha=1
then necessarily

\nu=2\rho-2<0
and this becomes a linear case.
When
\alpha
is irrational, this function can be expanded as a convergent series of powers of
x
, but the convergence of this series is very delicate and depends on subtle number theoretic
properties of
\alpha
, see
[
14
,
15
,
22
]
. We will summarize some of the results on this series representation here.
First of all, for any irrational
\alpha
we define sequences
\{a_{m,n}\}_{m\geq 0,n\geq 0}
and
\{b_{m,n}\}_{m\geq 0,n\geq 1}
as
follows

(49)

a_{m,n}:=\frac{(-1)^{m+n}}{\Gamma\left(1-\rho-n-\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)\Gamma(\alpha\rho+m+\alpha n)}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{\alpha}\left(\alpha\rho+j-1\right)\right)}{\sin\left(\frac{\pi j}{\alpha}\right)}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\sin(\pi\alpha(\rho+j-1))}{\sin(\pi\alpha j)},

(50)

b_{m,n}:=\frac{\Gamma\left(1-\rho-n-\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)\Gamma(\alpha\rho+m+\alpha n)}{\Gamma\left(1+n+\frac{m}{\alpha}\right)\Gamma(-m-\alpha n)}a_{m,n}.

We also introduce a set
{\mathcal{A}}
, which consists of all of all real irrational numbers
x
, for which there exists a constant
b>1
such that the inequality

(51)

\Big|x-\frac{p}{q}\Big|<\frac{1}{b^{q}}

is satisfied for infinitely many integers
p
and
q
. It is known
[
15
]
that this set is dense in
{\mathbb{R}}
and has Lebesgue measure zero (and Hausdorff dimension zero).
The following result was proved in

[
15
]
.

Theorem 8
.

Assume that
\alpha\notin{\mathcal{A}}\cup{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then for all
x>0

(52)

\displaystyle p_{S_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{-1-\alpha}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}b_{m,n+1}x^{-m-\alpha n},\;\;{\textnormal{ if }}\alpha\in(0,1),

(53)

\displaystyle p_{S_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{\alpha\rho-1}\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\sum\limits_{m\geq 0}a_{m,n}x^{m+\alpha n},\;\;\;\;\;\;\;{\textnormal{ if }}\alpha\in(1,2).

The series converges absolutely and uniformly on compact subsets of
(0,\infty)
.
Moreover, for every
\alpha\notin{\mathbb{Q}}
the series (
52
)
(
resp. (
53
)
)
provides complete asymptotic expansion as
x\to+\infty

(
resp.
x\to 0^{+}
)
.

It was established in
[
22
]
that the series (
52
) diverges for a dense set of irrational numbers
\alpha\in(0,1)
. It is also known that for every irrational
\alpha
this series can be made to converge conditionally, if one performs summation over triangles
m+1+\alpha(n+1/2)<q_{k}
for a carefully chosen sequence
q_{k}\to+\infty
, see
[
14
]
for details.

6.2.
Eigenfunctions of fractional Laplacian on
(0,\infty)

Let us denote by
F
the eigenfunction of the fractional Laplace operator

(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
on
(0,\infty)
with Dirichlet condition on

(-\infty,0]
. This problem is related to an
\alpha
-stable process killed at the first exit from
(0,\infty)
, see
[
23
]
. It was first established in

[
25
]
that
F
can be expressed as follows

F(x)=\sin(x+\pi(2-\alpha)/8)-g(x),

for certain completely monotone bounded function
g
. The Mellin transform of
F
can be obtained from Theorem 1.5 in
[
23
]
by setting
\rho=1/2
:

(54)

\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x)x^{z-1}{\textnormal{d}}x=\frac{\Gamma(z)S_{2}(z;\alpha)}{2S_{2}(\alpha/2+z;\alpha)},\;\;\;-\alpha/2<\textnormal{Re}(z)<0.

Here
S_{2}
is the double sine function
[
3
,
23
]
:

(55)

S_{2}(z;\alpha):=(2\pi)^{(1+\alpha)/2-z}\frac{G(z;\alpha)}{G(1+\alpha-z;\alpha)}.

This function satisfies the two functional equations

(56)

S_{2}(z+1;\alpha)=\frac{S_{2}(z;\alpha)}{2\sin(\pi z/\alpha)},\;\;\;S_{2}(z+\alpha;\alpha)=\frac{S_{2}(z;\alpha)}{2\sin(\pi z)},

and the normalizing condition
S_{2}((1+\alpha)/2;\alpha)=1
.
Expressing the double sine functions in (
54
) in terms of double gamma functions, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}F(x)x^{z-1}{\textnormal{d}}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(2\pi)^{\alpha/2}\Gamma(z)\frac{G(z;\alpha)G(1+\alpha/2-z;\alpha)}{G(1+\alpha-z;\alpha)G(\alpha/2+z;\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\alpha^{z}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2\alpha}}\frac{G(z+\alpha;\alpha)G(1+\alpha/2-z;\alpha)}{G(1+\alpha-z;\alpha)G(\alpha/2+z;\alpha)},

where in the last step we used the second functional equation from (
6
).
From the above formula we obtain the desired representation of the eigenfunction of fractional Laplacian

(57)

F(x)=\sqrt{\frac{\pi\alpha}{2}}x^{-1}K_{2,2}^{1,1}\Big(\begin{matrix}0,\alpha\\
\alpha/2,\alpha/2\end{matrix}\Big|x/\alpha;\alpha\Big).

In this case
N=p-q=0
and
\mu=-\alpha
, which corresponds to a balanced case.

6.3.
Exponential functionals of hypergeometric processes

Let
X=\{X_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a hypergeometric Lévy process, defined via the Laplace exponent

\psi(z)=\ln{\mathbb{E}}[\exp(zX_{1})]=-\frac{\Gamma(1-\beta+\gamma-z)\Gamma(\hat{\beta}+\hat{\gamma}+z)}{\Gamma(1-\beta-z)\Gamma(\hat{\beta}+z)}.

Here the parameters satisfy
\beta\leq 1
,
\hat{\beta}\geq 0
and
\gamma,\hat{\gamma}\in(0,1)
.
Such processes were studied
[
24
]
, where it was shown that they are generalizations of the Lamperti-stable processes
[
26
]
. A very important random variable that is used in the study of
\alpha
-stable processes
is the exponential functional of hypergeometric processes, defined as

I=I(\alpha,X)=\int_{0}^{\zeta-}e^{-\alpha X_{t}}{\textnormal{d}}t,

where
\alpha>0
and
\zeta
is the lifetime of the process
X
.
The Mellin transform of
I
is defined as
M(s):={\mathbb{E}}[I^{s-1}]
and it is known
[
24
]
that
M
satisfies the equation

M(s+1)=-M(s)/\psi(\alpha s)
. Given that
\psi
is a product of gamma functions, this equation can be easily solved in terms of the double gamma functions (via (
6
)) and a certain uniqueness argument
[
24
, Proposition 2]
then shows that

(58)

M(s)=C\Gamma(s)\frac{G((1-\beta)\delta+s;\delta)G((\hat{\beta}+\hat{\gamma})\delta+1-s;\delta)}{G((1-\beta+\gamma)\delta+s;\delta)G(\hat{\beta}\delta+1-s;\delta)},

where
\delta:=1/\alpha
and
C
is a normalizing constant which makes the expression in the right-hand side equal to
1
when
s=1
.
Expressing the gamma function in (
58
) as a ratio of two double gamma functions via (
31
) and writing the probability density function
p_{I}(x)
(of random variable
I
) as the inverse Mellin transform, we obtain the following expression

(59)

p_{I}(x)=C\sqrt{\delta}(2\pi)^{(1-\delta)/2}x^{-1}K_{3,3}^{1,2}\Big(\begin{matrix}0,\beta\delta,\hat{\beta}\delta\\
(\hat{\beta}+\hat{\gamma})\delta,(\beta-\gamma)\delta,\delta\end{matrix}\Big|x/\delta;\delta\Big).

In deriving the above formula we also used (
32
).
Some results on series representation of
p_{I}(x)
can be found in
[
24
, Section 4]
. In this case we have
N=p-q=0
,

\mu=\delta(\gamma+\hat{\gamma}-1)
and
\nu=\delta(\gamma-\hat{\gamma}-1)
, thus this example falls either in the balanced case (when
\gamma+\hat{\gamma}\neq 1
) or the linear case (when

\gamma+\hat{\gamma}=1
).

6.4.
Generalized Kilbas-Saigo function

Following
[
6
]
we introduce a Pochhammer type symbol

[a;\tau]_{z}:=\frac{G(a+z;\tau)}{G(a;\tau)}.

Note that in view of the first relation in (
6
) for
n=0,1,2,\dots
we have

[a;\tau]_{0}=1,~~[a;\tau]_{n}=\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\Gamma((a+k)/\tau).

Assume that
\tau>0
,
a_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{-m\tau-n|m,n\geq 0\}
,
b_{j}>0
for
j=1,\dots,p
, and

(60)

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(b_{j}-a_{j})>0.

Define the function

(61)

f(x)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{[a_{1},\dots,a_{p};\tau]_{n}}{[b_{1},\dots,b_{p};\tau]_{n}}x^{n},

where

[a_{1},\dots,a_{p};\tau]_{n}:=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}[a_{j};\tau]_{n}

From Proposition
2
we find that

(62)

\frac{[a;\tau]_{z}}{[b;\tau]_{z}}=\frac{G(b;\tau)}{G(a;\tau)}\exp\Big(\frac{a-b}{\tau}z\ln(z)-\frac{(a-b)}{\tau}\Big(1+\ln(\tau)\Big)z+O(\ln|z|)\Big),\;\;\;z\to\infty,

which holds uniformly in any sector
|\arg(z)|<\pi-\epsilon.

The above asymptotic result and our assumption
(
60
) guarantee that
f(x)
is an entire function. This function is a natural generalization of the Kilbas-Saigo function (see

[
13
, Chapter 5.2]
and
[
6
]
), defined as

E_{\alpha,m,l}(x)=1+\sum\limits_{n\geq 1}\Big[\prod\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\Gamma(1+\alpha(km+l))}{\Gamma(1+\alpha(km+l+1))}\Big]x^{n}=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{[\tilde{a};\tilde{\tau}]_{n}}{[\tilde{b};\tilde{\tau}]_{n}}x^{n},

with
\tilde{\tau}=1/(\alpha m)
,
\tilde{a}=(\alpha l+1)\tilde{\tau}
and
\tilde{b}=\tilde{a}+1/m
.

As we show in the next result, the function

f
defined in (
61
) is a special case of the Meijer-Barnes
K
-function.

Proposition 5
.

Assume that
0<\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(b_{j}-a_{j})<2\tau
. Then for
x>0

(63)

f(-x)=C\times K^{p+1,1}_{p+2,p+2}\Big(\begin{matrix}1,1,b_{1},\dots,b_{p}\\
a_{1},\dots,a_{p},1+\tau,1+\tau\end{matrix}\Big|x;\tau\Big),

where

C:=(2\pi)^{1-\tau}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\frac{G(b_{j};\tau)}{G(a_{j};\tau)}.

Proof.

Let us denote

\varphi(z):=\frac{[a_{1},\dots,a_{p};\tau]_{z}}{[b_{1},\dots,b_{p};\tau]_{z}}.

We have

f(-x)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\varphi(n)(-x)^{n}

and using (
62
) one can check that function
\varphi
satisfies the conditions of Ramanujan’s Master Theorem (see
[
2
]
), thus formula (3.4) in
[
2
]
gives us

(64)

f(-x)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\textnormal{i}}}\int_{c+{\textnormal{i}}{\mathbb{R}}}\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}\varphi(-s)x^{-s}{\textnormal{d}}s,

where
0<c<\min_{1\leq{j}\leq{p}}(b_{j})
. In view of (
31
)
we have

(65)

\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi{s})}=\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)=(2\pi)^{1-\tau}\frac{G(s+\tau;\tau)G(1-s+\tau;\tau)}{G(s;\tau)G(1-s;\tau)}.

Given the asymptotics in (
62
), we can change the contour of integration in (
64
) to
{\mathcal{L}}_{-\pi+\epsilon,\pi-\epsilon}
and now,
using (
65
) and comparing (
64
) with our definition of Meijer-Barnes
K
-function, we obtain the desired result
(
63
).

\sqcap\kern-8.0pt\hbox{$\sqcup$}

Using exactly the same method one can establish the following result.

Proposition 6
.

The function

(66)

\tilde{f}(x)=\sum\limits_{n\geq 0}\frac{[a_{1},\dots,a_{p};\tau]_{n}}{[b_{1},\dots,b_{p};\tau]_{n}}\frac{x^{n}}{n!},

is entire if
0<\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(b_{j}-a_{j})
. If, in addition, we have
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p}(b_{j}-a_{j})<\tau
then

(67)

f(-x)=C\times K^{p,1}_{p+1,p+1}\Big(\begin{matrix}1,b_{1},\dots,b_{p}\\
a_{1},\dots,a_{p},1+\tau\end{matrix}\Big|x/\tau;\tau\Big),\;\;\;x>0,

where

C:=(2\pi)^{(1-\tau)/2}\tau^{-1/2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{p}\frac{G(b_{j};\tau)}{G(a_{j};\tau)}.

One can check that both of the examples in this section belong to the balanced case of parameters. The functions of the form (
66
) appeared in
[
31
]
as the Laplace transforms of Barnes beta distributions, to which we now turn our attention.

6.5.
Barnes beta distributions

Ostrovsky
[
31
]
introduced a family of higher-order beta distributions, called Barnes beta distributions (similar distributions appeared later in
[
6
]
). A particularly interesting example in that family is the random variable
\beta_{2,2}(\tau,{\mathbf{w}})
, defined via Mellin transform

{\mathbb{E}}[\beta_{2,2}(\tau,{\mathbf{w}})^{s}]=\frac{\Gamma_{2}(s+w_{0};1,\tau)\Gamma_{2}(w_{0}+w_{1};1,\tau)\Gamma_{2}(w_{0}+w_{2};1,\tau)\Gamma_{2}(s+w_{0}+w_{1}+w_{2};1,\tau)}{\Gamma_{2}(w_{0};1,\tau)\Gamma_{2}(s+w_{0}+w_{1};1,\tau)\Gamma_{2}(s+w_{0}+w_{2};1,\tau)\Gamma_{2}(w_{0}+w_{1}+w_{2};1,\tau)}.

Here
\textnormal{Re}(s)>-w_{0}
and
{\mathbf{w}}=(w_{0},w_{1},w_{2})
with
w_{i}>0
. It is known that the random variable
\beta_{2,2}(\tau,{\mathbf{w}})
is supported on
(0,1)
, it has absolutely continuous distribution and that its logarithm is infinitely divisible (see Theorem 2.4 and Corollary 2.5 in
[
31
]
). The random variable
\beta_{2,2}(\tau,{\mathbf{w}})
has interesting connection to the celebrated Selberg’s integral (see also
[
30
]
). It turns out that a product of five independent random variables, three of which are equal in distribution to
1/\beta_{2,2}(\tau,{\mathbf{w}})
(with different parameters
{\mathbf{w}}
) and the remaining two have Frechet and lognormal distributions, has moments given by the Selberg’s integral (see Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 in
[
31
]
).

Using (
11
) we can rewrite the above Mellin transform in the form

{\mathbb{E}}[\beta_{2,2}(\tau,{\mathbf{w}})^{s}]=\frac{G(w_{0};\tau)G(s+w_{0}+w_{1};\tau)G(s+w_{0}+w_{2};\tau)G(w_{0}+w_{1}+w_{2};\tau)}{G(s+w_{0};\tau)G(w_{0}+w_{1};\tau)G(w_{0}+w_{2};\tau)G(s+w_{0}+w_{1}+w_{2};\tau)}.

Writing the density
q(x)
of the random variable
\beta_{2,2}(\tau,{\mathbf{w}})
as the inverse Mellin transform and applying
(
32
) and (
33
) we obtain the following result: for
w_{1}w_{2}>\tau
and
x>0

q(x)=Cx^{w_{0}-1}K_{2,2}^{2,0}\Big(\begin{matrix}0,w_{1}+w_{2}\\
w_{1},w_{2}\end{matrix}\Big|x^{-1};\tau\Big),

where

C=\frac{G(w_{0};\tau)G(w_{0}+w_{1}+w_{2};\tau)}{G(w_{0}+w_{1};\tau)G(w_{0}+w_{2};\tau)}.

This is an example of the logarithmic case, as here we have
N=p-q=\mu=\nu=0
and
\xi=-2w_{1}w_{2}
.
Theorem
3
tells us that
q(x)=0
for
x>1
, as previously stated in
[
31
]
.

Appendix A
Alternative definition in terms of

Here we will follow

We define
\tilde{K}

Appendix B
Proof of (
20
)

We denote
C:=(2\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\tau}\right)}\tau^{\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{1}{\tau})}
and recall that
N
,
\mu
,
\nu
and
\xi
are defined in (
18
). From (
7
) we have

(B.1)

G(z;\tau)=C^{z}\tau^{-\frac{z^{2}}{2\tau}-1}G\left(\frac{z}{\tau};\frac{1}{\tau}\right).

We apply the above formula to every double gamma factor in (
16
) and obtain

(B.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}{\bf a}\\
{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s;\tau\Big)

\displaystyle=C^{D_{1}}\tau^{-N-D_{2}/(2\tau)}\ \frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}G(b_{j}/\tau-s/\tau;1/\tau)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}G(1+1/\tau-a_{j}/\tau+s/\tau;1/\tau)}{\prod\limits_{j=m+1}^{q}G(1+1/\tau-b_{j}/\tau+s/\tau;1/\tau)\prod\limits_{j=n+1}^{p}G(a_{j}/\tau-s/\tau;1/\tau)}

\displaystyle=C^{D_{1}}\tau^{-N-D_{2}/(2\tau)}\mathcal{G}_{p,q}^{m,n}\Big(\begin{matrix}\tau^{-1}{\bf a}\\
\tau^{-1}{\bf b}\end{matrix}\Big|s\tau^{-1};\tau^{-1}\Big),

where we denoted for
r=1,2
,

\displaystyle D_{r}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(b_{j}-s)^{r}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1+\tau-a_{j}+s)^{r}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{q}(1+\tau-b_{j}+s)^{r}-\sum_{j=n+1}^{p}(a_{j}-s)^{r}.

Now our goal is to simplify the expressions for
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
. The first one is easy: it is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle D_{1}=s(p-q)+(1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\nu.

Simplifying an expression for
D_{2}
takes more work:

\displaystyle D_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}(b_{j}-s)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1+\tau-a_{j}+s)^{2}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{q}(1+\tau-b_{j}+s)^{2}-\sum_{j=n+1}^{p}(a_{j}-s)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j=n+1}^{p}a_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{q}b_{j}^{2}+Ns^{2}+(1+\tau)^{2}(n+m-q)

\displaystyle+2s\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(1+\tau-a_{j})+\sum_{j=n+1}^{p}a_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{q}(1+\tau-b_{j})\bigg)

\displaystyle-2(1+\tau)\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{q}b_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j=n+1}^{p}a_{j}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{q}b_{j}^{2}+Ns^{2}+(1+\tau)^{2}(n+m-q)

\displaystyle+2s\Big((1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}+\sum_{j=n+1}^{p}a_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}+\sum_{j=m+1}^{q}b_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle-2(1+\tau)\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}-\sum_{j=m+1}^{q}b_{j}\Big)

\displaystyle=\xi+Ns^{2}+(1+\tau)^{2}(n+m-q)+2s\big((1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\mu\big)-(1+\tau)(\mu+\nu).

Thus we have

\displaystyle C^{D_{1}}\tau^{-N-D_{2}/(2\tau)}

\displaystyle=C^{s(p-q)+(1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\nu}\tau^{-N-\frac{1}{2\tau}\big(\xi+Ns^{2}+(1+\tau)^{2}(n+m-q)+2s((1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\mu)-(1+\tau)(\mu+\nu)\big)}

\displaystyle=e^{-s^{2}N\frac{\ln(\tau)}{2\tau}}\times\Big(C^{q-p}\tau^{\frac{1}{\tau}((1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\mu)}\Big)^{-s}

\displaystyle\times C^{(1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\nu}\tau^{-N-\frac{1}{2\tau}\big(\xi+(1+\tau)^{2}(n+m-q)-(1+\tau)(\mu+\nu)\big)}.

Recall that
C=(2\pi)^{\frac{\tau-1}{2\tau}}\tau^{\frac{1+\tau}{2\tau}}
,
thus

C^{q-p}\tau^{\frac{1}{\tau}((1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\mu)}=(2\pi)^{\frac{1-\tau}{2\tau}(p-q)}\tau^{\frac{1}{2\tau}(N(1+\tau)-2\mu)},

and

\displaystyle C^{(1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\nu}\tau^{-N-\frac{1}{2\tau}\big(\xi+(1+\tau)^{2}(n+m-q)-(1+\tau)(\mu+\nu)\big)}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{\tau-1}{2\tau}((1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\nu)}\times\tau^{\frac{1}{2\tau}((1+\tau)((1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\nu)-\xi-(1+\tau)^{2}(n+m-q)+(1+\tau)(\mu+\nu)-N}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{\tau-1}{2\tau}((1+\tau)(n+m-q)-\nu)}\times\tau^{\frac{1}{2\tau}((1+\tau)\mu-\xi)-N}.

Combining (
B.2
) and the above three formulas we
finally obtain the desired result (
20
).

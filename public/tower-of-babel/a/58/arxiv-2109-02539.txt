Heavy quark spin partners of the Y ⁡ ( 4260 ) Y(4260) in coupled-channel formalism
Muhammad Naeem Anwar 1,2, Yu Lu 3

Abstract
The charmoniumlike state Y ⁡ ( 4260 ) Y(4260) is described as predominantly a D 1 ​ D ¯ D_{1}\bar{D} molecule in a coupled-channel quark model [Phys. Rev. D 96 , 114022 (2017)]. The heavy quark spin symmetry (HQSS) thus implies the possible emergence of its heavy quark spin partners with molecular configurations as D 1 ​ D ¯ ∗ D_{1}\bar{D}^{*} and D 2 ∗ ​ D ¯ ∗ D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*} below these charmed mesons’ thresholds. We analyze the probabilities of various intermediate charmed meson loops for J P ​ C = 1 − ⁣ − J^{PC}=1^{--} exotic state Y ⁡ ( 4360 ) Y(4360) and find that the channel D 1 ​ D ¯ ∗ D_{1}\bar{D}^{*} couples more strongly around its mass regime, and the coupling behavior remains the same even if the mass of Y ⁡ ( 4360 ) Y(4360) is pushed closer to D 1 ​ D ¯ ∗ D_{1}\bar{D}^{*} threshold. This enlightens that the most favorable molecular scenario for the Y ⁡ ( 4360 ) Y(4360) could be D 1 ​ D ¯ ∗ D_{1}\bar{D}^{*} , and hence it can be interpreted as HQSS partner of the Y ⁡ ( 4260 ) Y(4260) . We also find the strong coupling behavior of D 2 ∗ ​ D ¯ ∗ D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*} channel with the ψ ⁡ ( 4415 ) \psi(4415) , which makes it a good candidate for a dominant D 2 ∗ ​ D ¯ ∗ D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*} molecule. We discuss the important decay patterns of these resonances to disentangle their long- and short-distance structures.

II
Coupled-Channel Formalism

The quenched quark model described the spectrum of the low-lying states reasonably well,
but its predictions for the states nearby or above open-flavor thresholds are questionable.
It lacks the influence of “sea-quarks”.
Heavy quarkonium can couple to intermediate
heavy mesons through the creation of light quark-antiquark pair.
This enlarges the Fock space of heavy quarkonium and manifests
the presence of multiquark components in its wave function.
Such components will change the Hamiltonian of the potential model,
causing a mass shift due to self-energy corrections, and may also
give direct contributions to strong and electromagnetic decays
Lu:2016mbb
;
Anwar:2018yqm
.
The probability of such multiquark (molecular) components can be worked out
(see e.g., Ref.
Lu:2017yhl
).

In UQM, a physical or experimentally observed hadron
\ket{A}
can be expressed as

\ket{A}=c_{0}\ket{\psi_{0}}+\sum_{BC}\int d^{3}p\,c_{BC}(p)\ket{BC;p},

(2)

where
c_{0}
and
c_{BC}
stand for the normalization constant of the bare state
and the
BC
components, respectively.
In this work,
B
and
C
refer to charmed and anti-charmed mesons,
and the summation over
BC
is carried out up to ground state
P
-wave charmed mesons
Lu:2017hma
.
The bare state
\ket{\psi_{0}}
is normalized to 1, and the physical state
\ket{A}
is also normalized to 1
if it lies below
D\bar{D}
threshold.
\ket{BC;p}
is normalized as
\langle{BC;p_{1}}|{B^{\prime}C^{\prime};p_{2}}\rangle=\delta^{3}(p_{1}-p_{2})\delta_{BB^{\prime}}\delta_{CC^{\prime}}
, where
p
is the momentum of meson
B
in
\ket{A}
’s rest frame.
The effects from the
BC
components are referred to as coupled-channel effects.

The full Hamiltonian of the physical state reads as

H=H_{0}+H_{BC}+H_{I},

(3)

where
H_{0}
is the Hamiltonian of the bare state (see Appendix
A
for details), the continuum Hamiltonian is
H_{BC}\ket{BC;p}=E_{BC}\ket{BC;p}
with
E_{BC}=\sqrt{m_{B}^{2}+p^{2}}+\sqrt{m_{C}^{2}+p^{2}}
is the energy of the continuum state (the interaction between
B
and
C
mesons is neglected here and transition between one continuum to another is not included), and
H_{I}
is the interaction Hamiltonian which triggers the mixing of the bare state to the continuum.

For the bare-continuum mixing, which is an important dynamical pieces of the UQM, we adopt the
widely used
{}^{3}P_{0}
model
Micu:1968mk
. In this model, the nonperturbative creation of light
quark-antiquark pairs is triggered from the vacuum of quantum chromodynamics (QCD) with
J^{PC}=0^{++}
,
which in the spectroscopical notation
{}^{2S+1}L_{J}
can be written as
{}^{3}P_{0}

Lu:2016mbb
;
Ferretti:2020civ
.
The interaction Hamiltonian can be expressed as

H_{I}=2m_{q}\gamma\int d^{3}x\bar{\psi}_{q}\psi_{q},

(4)

where
m_{q}
is the produced quark mass,
and
\gamma
is the dimensionless coupling constant.
The
\psi_{q}
(
\bar{\psi}_{q}
) is the spinor field to generate anti-quark (quark).
Since the probability to generate heavier quarks is suppressed,
we use the effective strength
\gamma_{s}=\frac{m_{q}}{m_{s}}\gamma
in the following calculation,
where
m_{q}=m_{u}=m_{d}
is the constituent quark mass of up (or down) quark and
m_{s}
is strange quark mass.
Their numerical values are listed in Appendix
A
(Table
5
).

The mass shift caused by the
BC
components and their probabilities are obtained after
solving the Schrödinger equation with the full Hamiltonian
H
.
They are expressed as

\displaystyle\Delta M

\displaystyle:=M-M_{0}=\sum_{BC}\int d^{3}p\,\frac{|\bra{BC;p}H_{I}\ket{\psi_{0}}|^{2}}{M-E_{BC}-i\epsilon},

(5)

\displaystyle P_{BC}

\displaystyle:=\int d^{3}p|c_{BC}|^{2}=\int d^{3}p\,\frac{|\bra{BC;p}H_{I}\ket{\psi_{0}}|^{2}}{(M-E_{BC})^{2}},{}

(6)

where

H

P_{BC}

The major part of the coupled-channel calculation is encoded in the wave function overlap integration,

\displaystyle\bra{BC;p}H_{I}\ket{\psi_{0}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}k~\phi_{0}(\vec{k}+\vec{p})~\phi_{B}^{*}(\vec{k}+x\vec{p})

(7)

\displaystyle\times\phi_{C}^{*}(\vec{k}+x\vec{p})~|\vec{k}|~Y_{1}^{m}(\theta_{\vec{k}},\phi_{\vec{k}}){}

where

The

These wave functions are in momentum space, and they are obtained by the Fourier transformation of the eigenfunctions of the bare Hamiltonian

For the heavy quarkonium decays, the heavy quarks are treated as spectators in the
{}^{3}P_{0}
model.
Their polarizations will not change after the generation of the light quark-antiquark pairs.
This leads us to conclude that the
{}^{3}P_{0}
model itself respects the HQSS.
Nevertheless, some HQSS breaking effects can still slip into the coupled-channel calculation.
The breaking effects mainly lie in the input of the charmed mesons masses
and can be noticed from their mass splitting in the same
j_{l}^{P}
multiplet,
where
j_{l}
is the total spin of the light-quark cloud (with parity
P
) in the charmed meson.
For example,
D_{2}^{*}
and
D_{1}
belong to the same
j_{l}^{+}=3/2
multiplet, but they do not degenerate
experimentally as claimed by the HQSS.

The HQSS further leads to configuration mixings of charmed mesons having different
j_{l}
.
For example, the experimentally observed
D_{1}(2420)
(or
D_{1}
in short) and
D_{1}(2430)
(or
D_{1}^{\prime}
)
are not the pure
j_{l}^{+}=3/2
and
j_{l}^{+}=1/2
(or the quark model
{}^{3}P_{1}
and
{}^{1}P_{1}
) states, respectively,
but are their linear combinations.
Therefore, the wave functions from the quark model should be modified accordingly.
The mixture can be formulated as

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\ket{D_{1}}\\
\ket{D_{1}^{\prime}}\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\,\theta&\sin\,\theta\\
-\sin\,\theta&\cos\,\theta\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\ket{{}^{3}P_{1}}\\
\ket{{}^{1}P_{1}}\\
\end{array}\right),

(8)

where
\theta
is the mixing angle.
The HQSS predicts it to be
\theta_{0}=\arctan(\sqrt{2})\approx 54.7^{\circ}
,
which is called the ideal mixing angle

;
Qin:2016spb
.
Experimentally, the deviation from ideal mixing is observed (
5.7^{\circ}\pm 4^{\circ}
)
Belle:2003nsh
,
which is very small compared with
\theta_{0}\approx 54.7^{\circ}
.
As reported earlier
Lu:2017yhl
, our results have minor changes if this deviation is added to
\theta_{0}
.
Hence, for simplicity, we will use ideal mixing of HQSS in this study.

Our bare mass value for the
\psi(3D)
is nearly 250 MeV higher than the
Y(4360)
, as listed in Table
5
.
Hadron loops can shift bare mass down to
Y(4360)
to compensate such a large mass gap

.
This requires the fitting of the charmonium spectrum which involves much
ab initio
calculations
(such as fixing of
{}^{3}P_{0}

\gamma

Therefore,
\gamma

in Eq. (
6

As a consequence, the absolute probabilities of the various charmed meson components are not determined.
Instead, by analyzing the
P_{BC}

as demonstrated in the following sections.

III.1
Coupling Strengths for

We compute the probabilities of intermediate charmed meson components by
solving the Schrödinger equation with the full Hamiltonian

Note that the

\psi(2D)\sim\psi(3D)

The unnormalized coupling strengths of

Due to higher mass of

one
P

D_{1}\bar{D}^{*}

The channel
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}

\psi(3S)

However, due to several reasons, discussed in the following,

a dominant

For the coupled channels which involve one

As a result, the contribution of all those channels which involve charmed meson form the same

In such a scenario, the largest coupling will naturally come from the
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}

since the spin configurations for this channel are at the maximum.
We refer this as

However, if the physical masses are applied to these charmed mesons,
the

Y(4360)
and is expected to have
a smaller coupling strength.

For
Y(4360)

Y(4360)
as the PDG average value
4368\pm 13
MeV. However, a recent analysis by the BESIII Collaboration for the process

4383.8\pm 4.2\pm 0.8

Y(4360)
might have a larger mass. The coupling of
Y(4360)

to the channel

The couplings to those channels that are far above 4.368 GeV are generally small—a universal conclusion from the coupled-channel effects.
The asymptotic behavior of

If the coupled channels are further from

For a solid conclusion,

We will come to this point in the next subsection.
If the
D_{s}

It is worth mentioning that, even though the non-

when the charmonium core is

\psi(4415)
and
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}

For the case of
\psi(4415)
,
since it is extremely close to the
D_{1}\bar{D}^{*}
threshold, one can expect that
this state will show dominant coupling to the aforementioned channel.
However, it is found that the spin-enhancement mechanism takes over here and the
\psi(4415)
has shown the largest coupling
to the
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
channel (see Table
4
).
The coupling of this channel with the
\psi(4S)
and
\psi(2D)
cores is almost the same.
The behavior of the
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
coupling strength as a function of mass of
the
\psi(4415)
resonance is shown in Fig.
3
.

Since the next higher channels above
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
, such as
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}_{0}
or
D^{*}_{2}\bar{D}_{2}^{*}

(both of these channels couple to
J^{PC}=1^{--}
in
P
-wave),
are further from the
\psi(4415)
, and the mass suppression mechanism implies that the
contributions from all these higher components will be highly suppressed,
which makes the name of the
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
molecule more reasonable.

HQSS also implies the presence of other molecular component in the wave function of
\psi(4415)

with different heavy quark spin structure such as
D_{1}\bar{D}
and
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}
;
the latter channel couples to
J^{PC}=1^{--}
through
D
-wave only.
However, these channels are open for
\psi(4415)
because the mass of these
thresholds are below the experimental mass of
\psi(4415)
.
This is merely the reason for zero coupling strengths in the Table
4
.

An important observation is that,
if the charmonium core of the
\psi(4415)
is in the
S
-wave,
the two mechanisms (mass suppression and the spin enhancement) will be of similar importance.
As a consequence, non-negligible contributions other than the
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
channel may exist.
This indeed showed up in our results and can be seen from Table
4
.
The coupling strength of
\psi(4415)
to the
D_{1}\bar{D}^{*}
is very close to the
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
channel
for the case of
\psi(3S)
and
\psi(4S)
.
In such a scenario, one can argue that the
\psi(4415)
is dominant by
D_{1}\bar{D}^{*}
rather than
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
.
However, the sizable coupling of the
\psi(4415)
with the channel
D_{1}\bar{D}^{*}
is merely
an artifact of just being extremely close to this threshold.

It is most likely that the charmonium core of
\psi(4415)
is in
S
-wave which results the same order of coupling for all initial core wave functions, as can be seen from Table
4
. This is further supported by the
R
-value measurement that the production cross section is enhanced significantly around
\psi(4415)
’s mass which normally is the case for
\psi(nS)
cores. The mass difference between
\psi(4415)
and the
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
threshold is just close enough to have the same binding energy as
Y(4260)
which reflects an important consequence of HQSS.

This analysis leads us to conclude that
\psi(4415)
is likely to be a mixture of a short-distance core
\psi(nS)
and the dominant long-distance
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
component. However, coupling strengths alone are not enough to conclude the structure of these resonances. An important way to disentangle the molecular configuration of
Y(4360)
and
\psi(4415)
is to look for suggested decay patterns, as discussed in the following section.

IV
Remarks on Decays

The open-charm decays of
Y(4360)
provide an important pathway to disentangle its long-distance structure.
The natural decay of a molecular state is into its constituents
Guo:2017jvc
, and the strongest decay channel of the
D_{1}
is
D^{*}\pi
.
Hence, in the
D_{1}\bar{D}^{*}
molecular configuration, we argue that the dominant decay mode of the
Y(4360)
is likely to be the
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}\pi
,
and the decay of
Y(4360)
into the
D\bar{D}^{*}\pi
final state is not possible. This is the reason that the
Y(4360)
has not shown up in the

e^{+}e^{-}\to D\bar{D}^{*}\pi
final state at Belle
Belle:2009dus
.

In fact, the
D\bar{D}^{*}\pi
final state
is expected to be the dominant decay mode of the
Y(4260)
in the molecular picture
Cleven:2013mka
,
which is supported by the recent precise measurements of BESIII Collaboration
BESIII:2018iea
.
It is interesting to mention that the
Y(4360)
is again invisible in this recent BESIII data
BESIII:2018iea
.
Hence, it is very important to measure
e^{+}e^{-}\to D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}\pi
cross sections
to prove/exclude the existence (as a hadronic molecule) of
Y(4360)
which we claim is a heavy quark spin partner of the
Y(4260)
.

In contrast to above, the
D_{2}^{*}
meson can decay into
D^{*}\pi
and
D\pi
. Therefore, a
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
molecular state must leave strong imprints in the
D\bar{D}^{*}\pi
and
D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}\pi
final states. For
\psi(4415)\to D\bar{D}^{*}\pi
, an upper limit for the partial decay width has been extracted by the Belle to be smaller than
11\%
ParticleDataGroup:2020ssz
;
Belle:2009dus
.
The broad enhancement around
4.40
GeV in the recent BESIII measurement of
e^{+}e^{-}\to D\bar{D}^{*}\pi

BESIII:2018iea
indicates clear contributions from
\psi(4415)
. If the same pattern is observed in the
e^{+}e^{-}\to D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}\pi
process, it will indicate that the
\psi(4415)
is likely to have a dominant
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
component which would provide a quantitative support to our conclusion.

The precise evaluation of the decay widths of the above discussed decays requires an accurate knowledge of the coupling of a molecular state with its components. One can benefit from the recent BESIII data
BESIII:2018iea
to extract the
Y(4260)
-
D_{1}\bar{D}
effective coupling and use HQSS to relate it to other molecular configurations. This would be the topic of our future explorations
anwar&dong
.

V
Summary

We computed and analyzed the probabilities of various charmed meson molecular components for
Y(4360)

under the coupled-channel formalism by assigning
\psi(3S)
,
\psi(4S)
,
\psi(2D)
and
\psi(3D)
initial wave functions.
We found that the channel
D_{1}\bar{D}^{*}
couples more strongly around the
Y(4360)
mass regime,
and the coupling behavior is the same for all considered initial charmonium wave functions.
This enlightens that the most favorable molecular scenario for
Y(4360)
is the
D_{1}\bar{D}^{*}
,
and hence, it can be interpreted as a heavy quark spin partner of the
Y(4260)
.

We also analyze the coupling of
\psi(4415)
with several nearest charmed meson channels.
It shows the largest coupling to the
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
channel in all four initial charmonium wave functions,
which makes
\psi(4415)
a good candidate for a prominent
D_{2}^{*}\bar{D}^{*}
molecule.
By this means, we argue that the heavy quark spin multiplet involving one
P
- and one
S
-wave
charmed meson is considered as completed.

However, the hitherto unobserved decays of these resonances are highly decisive and demanding,
such as
\Psi\to D^{*}\bar{D}^{*}\pi
, where
\Psi\in\{Y(4360),\psi(4415)\}
.
Once the predicted pattern of heavy quark spin multiplet is confirmed by future experiments,
it will serve to deepen our understanding of how QCD forms hadronic matter by arranging multiquarks.

Appendix A
Bare Hamiltonian

Bare charmonium states are obtained by solving the Schrödinger
equation with the well-known Cornell potential
Eichten:1978tg
;
Eichten:1979ms
,
which incorporates a spin-independent color Coulomb plus
linear confined (scalar) potential.
In the quenched limit, the potential can be written as

V(r)=-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\alpha}{r}+\lambda r+c,

(9)

where
\alpha,\lambda
and
c
stand for the strength of the color Coulomb potential,
the strength of linear confinement, and mass renormalization, respectively.
The hyperfine and fine structures are generated by the spin-dependent interactions,

\displaystyle V_{s}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m^{2}_{c}}\bigg[\left(\frac{2\alpha_{s}}{r^{3}}-\frac{\lambda}{2r}\right)\bm{\mathbf{L}}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{S}}+\frac{32\pi\alpha_{s}}{9}~\tilde{\delta}(r)~\bm{\mathbf{S}}_{c}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{S}}_{\bar{c}}

(10)

\displaystyle+\frac{4\alpha_{s}}{r^{3}}\left(\frac{\bm{\mathbf{S}}_{c}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{S}}_{\bar{c}}}{3}+\frac{(\bm{\mathbf{S}}_{c}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{r}})(\bm{\mathbf{S}}_{\bar{c}}\cdot\bm{\mathbf{r}})}{r^{2}}\right)\bigg]~,{}

where
\bm{\mathbf{L}}
denotes the relative orbital angular momentum,

\bm{\mathbf{S}}=\bm{\mathbf{S}}_{c}+\bm{\mathbf{S}}_{\bar{c}}
is the total spin of the charm quark pairs,
and
m_{c}
is the charm quark mass.
The smeared
\tilde{\delta}(r)
function can be read from Refs.
Barnes:2005pb
;
Li:2009ad
.
These spin dependent terms are treated as perturbations.

The Hamiltonian of the Schrödinger equation in the quenched limit is represented as

H_{0}=2m_{c}+\frac{p^{2}}{m_{c}}+V(r)+V_{s}(r).

(11)

The spatial wave functions and bare mass
M_{0}
are obtained by
solving the Schrödinger equation numerically using the Numerov method
Numerov:1927
.

,

)] from Ref.
Li:2009ad

and the bare-mass spectrum predicted by

JT Gravity Limit of Liouville CFT and Matrix Model
Kenta Suzuki

Abstract
In this paper we study a connection between Jackiw-Teitelboim (JT) gravity on two-dimensional anti de-Sitter spaces and a semiclassical limit of c < 1 c<1 two-dimensional string theory. The world-sheet theory of the latter consists of a space-like Liouville CFT coupled to a non-rational CFT defined by a time-like Liouville CFT. We show that their actions, disk partition functions and annulus amplitudes perfectly agree with each other, where the presence of boundary terms plays a crucial role. We also reproduce the boundary Schwarzian theory from the Liouville theory description. Then, we identify a matrix model dual of our two-dimensional string theory with a specific time-dependent background in c = 1 c=1 matrix quantum mechanics. Finally, we also explain the corresponding relation for the two-dimensional de-Sitter JT gravity.

2
JT Gravity

In this section, we study the partition function of JT gravity on a fixed background.
In particular, we are interested in the hyperbolic disk and double-trumpet geometries.

We write the action of JT gravity on a two dimensional manifold
M
as follows:

\displaystyle I_{\textrm{JT}}\,=\,-2\int_{M}\sqrt{g}\,\Phi(R+2)\,-\,4\int_{\partial M}\sqrt{\gamma}\,\Phi K\,+\,I_{\textrm{ct}}\,,

(2.1)

where
\gamma
and
K
are the induced metric and trace of the extrinsic curvature on the boundary
\partial M
.
One can also add the Einstein-Hilbert action to the above action, but it is just topological and gives a constant contribution
-S_{0}\chi(M)
to the on-shell action,
where
S_{0}
is a coefficient of the Einstein-Hilbert action and
\chi(M)
is the Euler characteristic of the manifold
M
.
In this case, the dialtion field must satisfy
S_{0}\gg\Phi
at any position.
However, for a fixed background geometry as we will consider in the rest of the paper, this topological suppression factor does not play any important role.
Therefore, we will omit the Einstein-Hilbert action in the following.

The counter term is added to make the on-shell action finite and it reads
[
64
]

\displaystyle I_{\textrm{ct}}\,=\,4\int_{\partial M}\sqrt{\gamma}\Phi\,.

(2.2)

2.1
Disk geometry

Let us first consider the disk topology.
The variation of the dilation field
\Phi
in the bulk action of (
2.1
) simply sets
R=-2
, so the background solution is (Euclidean) AdS
2
.
Still there is a non-trivial equation for the dilation field
\Phi
coming from the variation of the metric.
The the global coordinates of the (Euclidean) AdS
2
solution, which we are interested in, is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{4}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\theta^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\Phi\,

\displaystyle=\,A\cdot\frac{1+r^{2}}{1-r^{2}}\,,

(2.3)

where
A
is an integration constant of the solution.
As in the usual angular coordinates,
r
is the radial coordinate
0<r<1
, where the boundary is located at
r=1
,
and
\theta
is an angular coordinate with periodicyty
2\pi
.

We regulate the UV divergence coming from the AdS asymptotic boundary
r\to 1
by introducing a UV cutoff
\delta
as
[
5
]

\displaystyle r\,=\,1-\delta\,,\qquad(0<\delta\ll 1)\,,

(2.4)

which leads to the asymptotic behaviour of the background solutions

\displaystyle ds^{2}\,

\displaystyle\simeq\,\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\theta^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\Phi\,

\displaystyle\simeq\,\frac{A}{\delta}\,.

(2.5)

Eventually we will be interested in the
\delta\to 0
limit.
We can choose the boundary length to be
\beta
by redefining the angular coordinate

\displaystyle u\,=\,\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\,\theta\,,

(2.6)

where
u
can be understood as the time of the boundary theory.
Then the corresponding UV cut off
\epsilon
is now defined as
\epsilon=\frac{\beta}{2\pi}\,\delta
, such that

\displaystyle ds^{2}\,\simeq\,\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\,du^{2}\,,\qquad\ \Phi\,\simeq\,\frac{\bar{\Phi}}{\epsilon}\,,

(2.7)

where we set the renormalized value of the dilaton:
\bar{\Phi}=A\cdot\frac{\beta}{2\pi}
.

Since the background solution satisfies
R=-2
, the bulk action of (
2.1
) vanishes on-shell.
For the remaining boundary action, by substituting the background solution, the on-shell action is evaluated as

\displaystyle I^{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}_{\textrm{JT}}\,

\displaystyle=\,-4A\int d\theta\,\frac{1+r^{2}}{(1+r)^{2}}\bigg|_{r=1-\delta}

\displaystyle\simeq\,-4\pi A=-8\pi^{2}\frac{\bar{\Phi}}{\beta}\,,

(2.8)

where for the second line, we neglected order
\delta
contributions.

2.2
Euclidean wormhole geometry

Next we would like to study two-boundary Euclidean wormhole geometry (i.e. “double-trumpet”).
As shown in
[

Also it is known that single-trumpet geometry is related to the punctured disk (i.e. disk with a conical singularity) by analytical continuation of its conical deficit angle

It seems more convenient to take this detour route via punctured disk (in particular for comparison with the later Liouville theory discussion),
where we just need to insert a bulk “vertex operator”, instead of directly studying the single-trumpet geometry.

Effectively, this means that we need to study a bulk one-point function of the form
\big\langle e^{-4\pi b\alpha\Phi(0)}\big\rangle
,

6

The minus sign compared to the Liouville vertex operator comes from the overall sign difference of the bulk action.

where the operator is inserted at the center of disk and the defect angle is
\pi b\alpha

Now this modifies the JT bulk action as

\displaystyle I_{\textrm{JT}}^{\textrm{bulk}}\,=\,-2\int_{M}\sqrt{g}(R+2)\Phi\,+\,4\pi b\alpha\Phi(0)\,,

while the boundary action and counterterm do not change.
The corresponding background solution is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{4\nu^{2}\,r^{2\nu-2}}{(1-r^{2\nu})^{2}}\,(dr^{2}+r^{2}d\theta^{2})\,,

\displaystyle\Phi\,

\displaystyle=\,A\cdot\frac{1+r^{2\nu}}{1-r^{2\nu}}\,,

(2.10)

where we defined

\displaystyle\nu\,=\,1-\frac{b\alpha}{2}\,.

(2.11)

The on-shell action can be computed from the boundary action as before

\displaystyle I^{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}_{\textrm{JT}}\,

\displaystyle=\,-4\nu A\int d\theta\frac{1+r^{2\nu}}{(1+r^{\nu})^{2}}\bigg|_{r=1-\delta}

\displaystyle\simeq\,-4\pi\nu^{2}A=-8\pi^{2}\nu^{2}\frac{\bar{\Phi}}{\beta}\,.

(2.12)

This indeed agrees with the tree-level punctured disk partition function
[
55
]
.
The single-trumpet partition function is obtained by

\displaystyle 2\pi\nu\,\to\,i\tilde{\alpha}\,,

(2.13)

where
\tilde{\alpha}
is the geodesic length of the other end.

Finally the tree-level contribution to the double-trumpet partition function is given by
[

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\tilde{\alpha}\,\tilde{\alpha}\,e^{-2\tilde{\alpha}^{2}\frac{\bar{\Phi}}{\beta_{1}}}e^{-2\tilde{\alpha}^{2}\frac{\bar{\Phi}}{\beta_{2}}}\,=\,\frac{\beta_{1}\beta_{2}}{4\bar{\Phi}(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})}\,.

(2.14)

2.3
One-loop partition function

Next, we would like to study one-loop contributions to the partition function.
Since the pure JT gravity is one-loop exact
[
65
,
13
]
, this completes the partition function.
To this end, we promote the bulk coordinates to functions of the “boundary time”

as
r\to r(u)
and
\theta\to\theta(u)

[
5
]
.
This is simply a reparametrization between the bulk Euclidean time near the boundary and the physical boundary time

Now, because of the boundary condition (

), the two functions are not independent, but

\displaystyle\qquad g_{uu}\,=\,\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\,=\,\frac{4(r^{\prime 2}+r^{2}\theta^{\prime 2})}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad r(u)\,=\,1-\epsilon\,\theta^{\prime}(u)\,+\,\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\,,

(2.15)

where the prime denotes a derivative respect to the boundary time

Using this relation, we would like to evaluate the on-shell action (
2.8
) up to
\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{0})
order.
To do this, we need to be careful because the boundary cutoff is now located at
r(u)=1-\epsilon\theta^{\prime}(u)
.
Detail evaluation of the extrinsic curvature on this cutoff can be found in Appendix A of
[
8
]
, and this leads to

\displaystyle K\,=\,1\,+\,\epsilon^{2}\,\textrm{Sch}\big(\tan(\tfrac{\theta}{2})\,,u\big)\,+\,\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{3})\,,

(2.16)

where the Schwarzian derivative is defined by

\displaystyle\textrm{Sch}(F,x)\,=\,\frac{F^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)}{F^{\prime}(x)}\,-\,\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{F^{\prime\prime}(x)}{F^{\prime}(x)}\right)^{2}\,.

(2.17)

Therefore, we find the Schwarzian action

\displaystyle I^{\textrm{bdy}}_{\textrm{JT}}\,=\,-4\bar{\Phi}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\,\textrm{Sch}\big(\tan(\tfrac{\theta}{2})\,,u\big)\,.

(2.18)

From this action, expanding around the saddle-point solution as
\theta(u)=2\pi u/\beta+\epsilon(u)
, the quadratic action is obtained as

\displaystyle I^{(2)}_{\textrm{JT}}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{\bar{\Phi}\beta^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{\beta}du\,\left[\epsilon^{\prime\prime}{}^{2}(u)\,-\,\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta}\right)^{2}\epsilon^{\prime}{}^{2}(u)\right]

\displaystyle=\,\frac{8\pi^{2}\bar{\Phi}}{\beta}\sum_{m}\,m^{2}(m^{2}-1)\,\epsilon_{m}^{2}\,,

(2.19)

where we used Fourier expansion
\epsilon(u)=\sum_{m}e^{2\pi imu/\beta}\,\epsilon_{m}
.
For the disk geometry, among the Fourier modes, we have to exclude the zero modes corresponding
m=0,\pm 1

[
65
]
.
Combining with the appropriate measure factor
[
13
]
, now the one-loop partition function is obtained as

\displaystyle Z_{\scriptsize\mbox{one-loop}}\,=\,\left(\frac{\bar{\Phi}}{2\pi\beta}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\,.

(2.20)

Liouville CFT

In this section, we define our double Liouville theory by a combination of a space-like and a time-like Liouville theory
and show that such theory agrees with JT gravity in the semiclassical limit.

3.1
Liouville action

We consider the CFT consists of a space-like Liouville field
\phi
and the time-like one
\chi
, defined by the bulk action

\displaystyle I^{\textrm{bulk}}_{\rm L}\,=\,\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\,\Big[(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}-(\partial_{\mu}\chi)^{2}+\mu e^{2b\phi}-\mu e^{-2b\chi}\Big]\,,

(3.1)

on a flat space R
2

\displaystyle ds^{2}\,=\,dr^{2}\,+\,r^{2}d\theta^{2}\,.

(3.2)

Here we choose the renormalized bulk cosmological constant of the time-like Liouville theory as
-\mu
in order to agree with JT gravity
[
30
,
31
]
.
The background charges are fixed as
Q=b+1/b
for the space-like Liouville field
\phi
and
q=1/b-b
for the time-like field
\chi
with a parameter
0<b\leq 1
.
Therefore, each theory has its central charge

and
c_{\chi}=1-6q^{2}

so that the total central charge is

, consistent with the anormaly cancellation condition of the bosonic string theory.

The string coupling behaves as
g_{s}=e^{Q\phi+q\chi}
,
and it is useful to note that the conformal dimensions of bulk vertex operators read

\displaystyle h\big(e^{2\alpha\phi}\big)\,=\,\alpha(Q-\alpha)\,,\qquad h\big(e^{2\alpha\chi}\big)\,=\,\alpha(\alpha-q)\,.

(3.3)

This shows that the bulk vertex operator of the form
e^{Q\phi+q\chi+2iP\phi\pm 2iP\chi}
is dimension one.

In the presence of boundary we need to add the boundary term

\displaystyle I^{\textrm{bdy}}_{\rm L}\,=\,2\int d\theta(Q\phi+q\chi)K^{(0)},

(3.4)

where
K^{(0)}
is the trace of the extrinsic curvature in the flat space at
r=1
.

JT gravity as a semiclassical limit of Liouville action

\displaystyle I^{\textrm{bulk}}_{\rm L}+I^{\textrm{bdy}}_{\rm L}\,=\,-2\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g_{\textrm{JT}}}\,\Big[\Phi R+\mu\sinh(2b^{2}\Phi)\Big]\,-\,4\int d\theta\sqrt{\gamma_{\textrm{JT}}}\,\Phi K\,,

3.3

In the previous description (

Instead, it is also useful to regularize the solution itself with the boundary condition
\phi_{0}=\phi(r=1)
and
\chi_{0}=\chi(r=1)

This is expected to correspond to the semi-classical limit of FZZT-brane.
9
9

9

More precisely this type of boundary condition is the Legendre transform of the FZZT-brane boundary condition with a fixed boundary length.
Original FZZT-brane (at least classically) corresponds to a fixed energy boundary condition of JT gravity
[
69

.
We will summarize boundary conditions of classical Liouville theory in appendix

We can find appropriate solutions by solving the Liouville equation as follows:

\displaystyle e^{2b\phi}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{p_{\phi}^{2}e^{2b\phi_{0}}}{(1-(1-p_{\phi})r^{2})^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle e^{-2b\chi}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{p_{\chi}^{2}e^{-2b\chi_{0}}}{(1-(1-p_{\chi})r^{2})^{2}}\,,

(3.19)

where
p_{\phi}
and

satisfy
p_{\phi}^{2}e^{2b\phi_{0}}=4(1-p_{\phi})
and
p_{\chi}^{2}e^{-2b\chi_{0}}=4(1-p_{\chi})

Explicitly we find

\displaystyle p_{\phi}\,

\displaystyle=\,-2e^{-2b\phi_{0}}+2e^{-b\phi_{0}}\sqrt{1+e^{-2b\phi_{0}}}\,\simeq\,2e^{-b\phi_{0}}-2e^{-2b\phi_{0}}\,>\,0\,,

(3.20)

\displaystyle p_{\chi}\,

\displaystyle=\,-2e^{2b\chi_{0}}+2e^{b\chi_{0}}\sqrt{1+e^{2b\chi_{0}}}\,\simeq\,2e^{b\chi_{0}}-2e^{2b\chi_{0}}\,>\,0\,.

(3.21)

We are interested in the limit
b\to 0
with

\displaystyle\delta=e^{-\frac{b}{2}(\phi_{0}-\chi_{0})}\ll 1,

(3.22)

\displaystyle\eta\equiv b(\phi_{0}+\chi_{0})=-\frac{2b^{2}A}{\delta}\ll 1,

(3.23)

while we require
\Phi_{0}=-\frac{1}{2b}(\phi_{0}+\chi_{0})\gg 1
.
Then the on-shell action is evaluated as

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{L}\simeq\int d\theta\left[\eta\left(\frac{2}{b^{2}\delta}-\frac{2}{b^{2}}+\frac{\delta}{b^{2}}\right)\right]\simeq A\int d\theta\left[-\frac{4}{\delta^{2}}+\frac{4}{\delta}-2\right],

(3.24)

\displaystyle I^{bdy}_{L}\simeq\frac{2}{b}\int d\theta(\phi_{0}+\chi_{0})\simeq-\frac{4A}{\delta}\int d\theta.

(3.25)

The counter term is evaluated as

\displaystyle I_{ct}=4\int d\theta\left[-\frac{1}{2b}(\phi+\chi)e^{\frac{b}{2}(\phi-\chi)}\right]\simeq\frac{4}{\delta}\int d\theta\left(-\frac{1}{2b}(\phi_{0}+\chi_{0})\right)\simeq\frac{4A}{\delta^{2}}\int d\theta.

(3.26)

Therefore the total on-shell action becomes

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{L}+I^{bdy}_{L}+I_{ct}=-2A\int d\theta=-4\pi A,

(3.27)

which reproduces the JT gravity result (
2.8
).

It is also useful to evaluate the contributions from
\phi
and
\chi
sector, separately.
First we note

\displaystyle\phi_{0}=-\frac{1}{b}\log\delta+\frac{\eta}{2b},

(3.28)

\displaystyle\chi_{0}=\frac{1}{b}\log\delta+\frac{\eta}{2b}.

(3.29)

Then, the evaluation of the on-shell action of each sector (detail evaluations are presented in appendix
C
) is given by

\displaystyle I^{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}_{\phi}\,=\,\int d\theta\left[-A+\frac{2}{b^{2}}(\log 2-1){-\frac{\delta}{b^{2}}}\right]\,,

(3.30)

and

\displaystyle I^{\scriptsize\mbox{on-shell}}_{\chi}\,=\,\int d\theta\left[-A-\frac{2}{b^{2}}(\log 2-1)+\frac{\delta}{b^{2}}\right]\,.

(3.31)

The total of two sectors agrees with the above result (
3.27
).

Minisuperspace wavefunctions

3.6
Boundary Schwarzian theory

In this subsection, we study the boundary theory of our double Liouville theory defined in section
3.1
.
For this discussion, it’s more convenient to use complex coordinates:
z=re^{i\theta}
and
\bar{z}=re^{-i\theta}
.
Therefore, now the metric is given by
ds^{2}=dzd\bar{z}
and the bulk action becomes

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{L}\,=\,\int dzd\bar{z}\Big[4\partial\phi\bar{\partial}\phi\,-\,4\partial\chi\bar{\partial}\chi\,+\,\mu(e^{2b\phi}-e^{-2b\chi})\Big]\,.

(3.52)

This action is invariant under the conformal transformation

\displaystyle z\,\to f(z)\,,\qquad\bar{z}\,\to\,\bar{f}(\bar{z})\,,

(3.53)

together with

\displaystyle\phi(z,\bar{z})\,

\displaystyle\to\,\phi(f(z),\bar{f}(\bar{z}))\,-\,\frac{1}{2b}\,\log(\partial f\bar{\partial}\bar{f})\,,

(3.54)

\displaystyle\chi(z,\bar{z})\,

\displaystyle\to\,\chi(f(z),\bar{f}(\bar{z}))\,+\,\frac{1}{2b}\,\log(\partial f\bar{\partial}\bar{f})\,.

(3.55)

We note that the bulk action does not have independent transformation for each Liouville theory, because this is a coordinate transformation (
3.53
).

At the boundary, the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic functions are related by the condition
f(\bar{z})=\bar{f}(z)
.
This means that at the boundary, we have the diffeomorphism of the angular coordinate as
\theta\to f(\theta)
.
This symmetry is explicitly broken by the boundary term (
3.4
).
Taking
b\to 0
limit and combining with the counterterm (
3.10
), this boundary action is

\displaystyle I_{L}^{\textrm{bdy}+\textrm{ct}}\,=\,\frac{2}{b}\int d\theta\,(\phi+\chi)(K^{(0)}-1)\,.

(3.56)

In order to evaluate this action, we can again go back to the polar coordinates and the boundary condition gives
r(\theta)=1-\epsilon f^{\prime}(\theta)
as in (
2.15
).
This leads to

\displaystyle\phi+\chi\,=\,-\frac{2Ab}{\epsilon}\,+\,\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{0})\,,

(3.57)

\displaystyle K^{(0)}\,=\,1\,+\,\left(\textrm{Sch}\big(\tan(\tfrac{f}{2})\,,\theta\big)\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\right)\epsilon\,+\,\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2})\,.

(3.58)

Therefore, at the order
\epsilon^{0}
, we find the Schwarzain action up to a constant as

\displaystyle I_{\textrm{Sch}}\,=\,-4A\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\theta\,\textrm{Sch}\big(\tan(\tfrac{f}{2})\,,\theta\big)\,.

(3.59)

This agrees with (
2.18
).
Since all results of JT gravity with a fixed background topology is obtained by the Schwarzain theory,
this confirms that our double Liouville theory indeed gives JT gravity results in the semiclassical limit (
b\to 0
).

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we argued an equivalence between the JT gravity on an anti de-Sitter space
and the two dimensional string theory defined by a time-like Liouville CFT coupled to the space-like Liouville one. We confirmed that their actions, disk partition functions and annulus amplitudes perfectly agree with each other, where the presence of boundary terms plays an important role. We also
reproduced the boundary Schwarzian theory from the Liouville
theory description. Our two dimensional string theory looks different from the minimal string theory in that the latter assumes the truncation to a rational CFT, while the former does not. Nevertheless, as we explicitly worked out in this paper, our two dimensional string theory which is equivalent to the JT gravity, has a non-perturbative description in terms of a time-dependent background in the
c=1
matrix quantum mechanics. It will be an important future problem to prove the proposed correspondence between the JT gravity and the matrix quantum mechanics by explicitly evaluating the disk amplitudes of the latter. For this we need to precisely identify the form of loop operator in the matrix quantum mechanics, which fits with our
c<1
two dimensional string theory. It will be also intriguing to explore what we can learn about holography in de-Sitter space

Appendix A
Boundary
\rho K^{(0)}
contribution

As we commented in the footnote
7
, if we use (
3.5
) directly in (
3.4
), what we obtain is

\displaystyle I^{\textrm{bdy}}_{\rm L}\,=\,4\int d\theta\,(\rho-\Phi)K^{(0)}\,.

(A.1)

Therefore, we find an additional term containing
\rho K^{(0)}
in the boundary action.
In this appendix we make some comments on the effects of this additional term.

First let us consider the contribution of this term to the on-shell action

\displaystyle I^{\textrm{bdy}}_{\rm L}\,\supset\,4\int d\theta\,\rho K^{(0)}\,\simeq\,2b\int d\theta\,(\phi_{0}-\chi_{0})\,\simeq\,-8\pi\log\delta\,.

(A.2)

This is diverging, so it does not give any finite contribution to the on-shell action,
but we need an additional counterterm to eliminate this diverging contribution.
The additional counterterm can be taken as the following expression

\displaystyle I^{\textrm{ct}}_{\rm extra}\,=\,-4\int d\theta\,\rho\,\simeq\,8\pi\log\delta\,,

(A.3)

which indeed eliminates the diverging contribution coming from the additional boundary term.

Next, we consider the derivation of the Schwarzian action discussed in section
3.6
.
If we include the additional boundary and counter terms, instead of (
3.56
), we now obtain

\displaystyle I_{\rm L}^{\textrm{bdy}+\textrm{ct}}\,=\,4\int d\theta\,(\rho-\Phi)(K^{(0)}-1)\,.

(A.4)

Using the expansion
r(\theta)=1-\epsilon f^{\prime}(\theta)
, we find

\displaystyle\rho\,=\,\frac{b}{2}(\phi-\chi)\,=\,-\log\epsilon\,+\,\mathcal{O}(\epsilon)\,.

(A.5)

Combining with (
3.58
), we can see that the additional boundary term does not lead to any finite contribution in the
\epsilon\to 0
limit.
Therefore, even if we include the additional boundary term, the effective action is still given by the Schwarzian action (
3.59
).

It might be interesting to further clarify this discrepancy between the double Liouville theory and JT gravity,
but we leave this study to future work and we will not further comment on this additional boundary term in this paper.

Appendix B
Boundary conditions of classical Liouville theory

In this appendix, we study classical solutions of Liouville theory defined on a disk with all possible boundary conditions.
Since all discussion of this appendix applies to the timelike Liouville theory in the same manner, here we focus only on the spacelike Liouville theory:

\displaystyle S\,=\,\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\,\Big[g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi\,+\,\mu\,e^{2b\phi}\Big]\,.

(B.1)

We use the coordinates (
3.2
) and in the following we consider static solutions. (Here by static, we mean
\partial_{\theta}\phi=0
.)
Variation of the action on this coordinates can be written explicitly as

\displaystyle\delta S\,=\,2\int_{r=1}d\theta\,(\partial_{r}\phi)\,\delta\phi\,+\,2\int drd\theta\,r\,\left[-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r}\phi)\,+\,b\mu\,e^{2b\phi}\right]\delta\phi\,.

(B.2)

In order for the boundary term to vanish, we can take either boundary condition

\displaystyle{\rm Dirichlet}:\qquad\phi(r=1)\,=\,\phi_{0}\quad(=\,{\rm constant})

(B.3)

\displaystyle{\rm Neumann}:\qquad\partial_{r}\phi\big|_{r=1}\,=\,0\,.

(B.4)

If we include an additional boundary action

\displaystyle S_{\rm bdy}\,=\,\int_{r=1}d\theta\,4\pi\mu_{B}\,e^{b\phi}\,,

(B.5)

we can have the FZZT brane (modified Neumann) boundary condition
[
59
,
60
]
:

\displaystyle{\rm FZZT}:\qquad\partial_{r}\phi\,+\,2\pi\mu_{B}b\,e^{b\phi}\,=\,0\,,\qquad({\rm at}\ r=1)

(B.6)

The ZZ brane boundary condition
[
80
]
is a special case of the Dirichlet boundary condition with
\phi_{0}=\infty
.

The equation of motion can be read off from the second term of (
B.2
) as

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(r\partial_{r}\phi)\,+\,b\mu\,e^{2b\phi}\,=\,0\,,

(B.7)

and the most general solution is given by

\displaystyle e^{2b\phi(r)}\,=\,\frac{1}{\mu b^{2}}\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{r^{2}\sinh[c_{1}(c_{2}-\log r)]^{2}}\,,

(B.8)

where
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are integration constants.
We first require a regularity condition at the center of the disk (
r\to 0
).
Since

\displaystyle e^{2b\phi(0)}\,\sim\,\frac{1}{\mu b^{2}}\frac{4c_{1}^{2}}{r^{2-2c_{1}}}\,,

(B.9)

the regularity condition fixes
c_{1}=1
.
Now the solution (
B.8
) becomes

\displaystyle e^{2b\phi(r)}\,=\,\frac{1}{\mu b^{2}}\frac{4c^{2}}{(1-c^{2}r^{2})^{2}}\,,

(B.10)

where we set
c=e^{-c_{2}}
.

The Dirichlet boundary condition requires

\displaystyle e^{2b\phi(1)}\,=\,\frac{1}{\mu b^{2}}\frac{4c^{2}}{(1-c^{2})^{2}}\,=\,e^{2b\phi_{0}}\,.

(B.11)

Therefore, if we set
c^{2}=1-p
(with
\mu b^{2}=1
), we find the solution (
3.19
).
The ZZ brane boundary condition is a special case of this result with
c=1
, which gives

\displaystyle e^{2b\phi(r)}\,=\,\frac{1}{\mu b^{2}}\frac{4}{(1-r^{2})^{2}}\,.

(B.12)

The FZZT brane boundary condition fixes the remaining constant as

\displaystyle c\,=\,-\frac{2\pi\mu_{B}b^{2}}{\sqrt{\mu b^{2}}}\,.

(B.13)

Therefore, the standard Neumann boundary condition (
\mu_{B}=0
) leads to the trivial solution (
c=0
).

Appendix C
On-shell actions of Liouville theory

In this appendix, we present some detail of the on-shell actions for the disk geometry discussed in section
3.3

and for the punctured disk geometry discussed in section
3.5
.

C.1
Disk geometry

The on-shell action for
\phi
reads

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\phi}=\int d\theta\left[\left(\frac{2}{b^{2}\delta}+\frac{2}{b^{2}}(\log 2-1)+\frac{2}{b^{2}}\log\delta-\frac{\delta}{b^{2}}\right)+\eta\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}\delta}-\frac{1}{b^{2}}+\frac{\delta}{2b^{2}}\right)\right],

\displaystyle I^{bdy}_{\phi}=\frac{2}{b}\int\phi_{0}d\theta=\int d\theta\left[-\frac{2}{b^{2}}\log\delta+\frac{\eta}{b^{2}}\right],

\displaystyle I^{ct}_{\phi}=-\frac{2}{b^{2}}\int d\theta e^{b\phi_{0}}=-\frac{2}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\left[\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{\eta}{2\delta}\right],

(C.1)

which give the total action

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\phi}+I^{bdy}_{\phi}+I^{ct}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\theta\left[\frac{\delta}{2b^{2}}\eta+\frac{2}{b^{2}}(\log 2-1)-\frac{\delta}{b^{2}}\right]

(C.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\theta\left[-A+\frac{2}{b^{2}}(\log 2-1){-\frac{\delta}{b^{2}}}\right].

On the other hand, for the field
\chi
we find

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\chi}=\int d\theta\left[\left(-\frac{2}{b^{2}\delta}-\frac{2}{b^{2}}(\log 2-1)-\frac{2}{b^{2}}\log\delta+\frac{\delta}{b^{2}}\right)+\eta\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}\delta}-\frac{1}{b^{2}}+\frac{\delta}{2b^{2}}\right)\right],

\displaystyle I^{bdy}_{\chi}=\frac{2}{b}\int\chi_{0}d\theta=\int d\theta\left[\frac{2}{b^{2}}\log\delta+\frac{\eta}{b^{2}}\right],

\displaystyle I^{ct}_{\chi}=-\frac{2}{b^{2}}\int d\theta e^{-b\chi_{0}}=-\frac{2}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\left[-\frac{1}{\delta}+\frac{\eta}{2\delta}\right],

(C.3)

which give the total action

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\chi}+I^{bdy}_{\chi}+I^{ct}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\theta\left[\frac{\delta}{2b^{2}}\eta-\frac{2}{b^{2}}(\log 2-1)+\frac{\delta}{b^{2}}\right]

(C.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\theta\left[-A-\frac{2}{b^{2}}(\log 2-1)+\frac{\delta}{b^{2}}\right].

C.2
Punctured disk geometry

As we consider bulk one-point function, we include the contribution from the vertex operator into the bulk actions as

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\phi}\,

\displaystyle=\,\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\,\Big[(\partial_{a}\phi)^{2}+\mu e^{2b\phi}\Big]\,-\,\alpha\int d\theta\,\phi(\epsilon)\,,

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\chi}\,

\displaystyle=\,\int d^{2}x\sqrt{g}\,\Big[-(\partial_{a}\chi)^{2}-\mu e^{-2b\chi}\Big]\,-\,\alpha\int d\theta\chi(\epsilon)\,.

(C.5)

Since the bulk fields
\phi
,
\chi
are diverging at
r=0
, we regularize this by integrating
\epsilon\leq r\leq 1
and inserting the vertex operators at
r=\epsilon
.
Then, we simply eliminate all diverging contribution in
\epsilon\to 0
limit.
Now the on-shell action for
\phi
reads

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\phi}\,=\,\frac{1}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\left[\left(\frac{2}{\delta}-\nu(2+\nu\delta)+2\nu\log 2\nu+2\log\delta\right)\,+\,\eta\left(\frac{1}{\delta}-1+\frac{\nu^{2}\delta}{2}\right)\right]\,,

\displaystyle I^{bdy}_{\phi}\,=\,\frac{2}{b}\int\phi_{0}d\theta\,=\,\frac{1}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\Big[-2\log\delta+\eta\Big]\,,

\displaystyle I^{ct}_{\phi}\,=\,-\,\frac{2}{b^{2}}\int d\theta e^{b\phi_{0}}\,=\,-\frac{1}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\left[\frac{2}{\delta}+\frac{\eta}{\delta}\right],

(C.6)

which give the total action

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\phi}+I^{bdy}_{\phi}+I^{ct}_{\phi}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\left[\frac{\nu^{2}\delta\eta}{2}+2\nu\log 2\nu-\nu(2+\nu\delta)\right]\,.

(C.7)

On the other hand, for the field
\chi
we find

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\chi}\,=\,\frac{1}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\left[\left(-\frac{2}{\delta}+\nu(2+\nu\delta)-2\nu\log 2\nu-2\log\delta\right)\,+\,\eta\left(\frac{1}{\delta}-1+\frac{\nu^{2}\delta}{2}\right)\right]\,,

\displaystyle I^{bdy}_{\chi}\,=\,\frac{2}{b}\int\chi_{0}d\theta\,=\,\frac{1}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\Big[2\log\delta+\eta\Big]\,,

\displaystyle I^{ct}_{\chi}\,=\,-\frac{2}{b^{2}}\int d\theta e^{-b\chi_{0}}\,=\,-\frac{1}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\left[-\frac{2}{\delta}+\frac{\eta}{\delta}\right],

(C.8)

which give the total action

\displaystyle I^{bulk}_{\chi}+I^{bdy}_{\chi}+I^{ct}_{\chi}\,

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{b^{2}}\int d\theta\left[\frac{\nu^{2}\delta\eta}{2}-2\nu\log 2\nu+\nu(2+\nu\delta)\right]\,.

(C.9)

Therefore, the total of the spacelike and timelike contributions is

\displaystyle I_{\phi}\,+\,I_{\chi}\,=\,-2\nu^{2}A\int d\theta\,=\,-4\pi\nu^{2}A\,.

(C.10)

Kempe Equivalent List Edge-Colorings of Planar Graphs
Daniel W. Cranston

Abstract
For a list assignment L L and an L L -coloring φ \varphi , a Kempe swap in φ \varphi is L L -valid if it yields another L L -coloring. Two L L -colorings are L L -equivalent if we can form one from another by a sequence of L L -valid Kempe swaps. And a graph G G is L L -swappable if every two of its L L -colorings are L L -equivalent. We consider L L -swappability of line graphs of planar graphs with large maximum degree. Let G G be a planar graph with Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 9 \Delta(G)\geq 9 and let H H be the line graph of G G . If L L is a ( Δ ⁡ ( G ) + 1 ) (\Delta(G)+1) -assignment to H H , then H H is L L -swappable. Let G G be a planar graph with Δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 15 \Delta(G)\geq 15 and let H H be the line graph of G G . If L L is a Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) -assignment to H H , then H H is L L -swappable. The first result is analogous to one for L L -choosability by Borodin, which was later strengthened by Bonamy. The second result is analogous to another for L L -choosability by Borodin, which was later strengthened by Borodin, Kostochka, and Woodall.

Proofs of Structural Lemmas

In this section, we prove our two structural lemmas. For convenience, we
restate them.
Recall, for a plane graph

G_{3}

†
margin:

is
induced by all edges incident to vertices of degree 3.
If two vertices of degree 3 are adjacent, then

Structural Lemma 1

If

(C1)

Our proof is similar to a proof by Cohen and Havet

In fact, their proof implicitly contains an analogous structural lemma.
The main difference is that now we require either (C2) or (C3), whereas

contain an even cycle, our version of the structural lemma is stronger.

Proof.

Suppose the lemma is false, and let

.
We use discharging with initial charge

(R1)

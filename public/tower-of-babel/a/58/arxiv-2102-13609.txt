Leading-Color Two-Loop QCD Corrections for Three-Jet Production at Hadron Colliders Preprint: CERN-TH-2021-023 FR-PHENO-2021-06 MPP-2021-20
S. Abreu, F. Febres Cordero, H. Ita, B. Page, and V. Sotnikov

Abstract
We present the complete set of leading-color two-loop contributions required to obtain next-to-next-to-leading-order (NNLO) QCD corrections to three-jet production at hadron colliders. We obtain analytic expressions for a generating set of finite remainders, valid in the physical region for three-jet production. The analytic continuation of the known Euclidean-region results is determined from a small set of numerical evaluations of the amplitudes. We obtain analytic expressions that are suitable for phenomenological applications and we present a C++ library for their efficient and stable numerical evaluation.

Introduction

Multi-jet events are ubiquitous at high-energy hadron
colliders such as the Large Hadron Collider (LHC). Their precise theoretical
description including higher-order quantum corrections offers a robust path to
test the Standard Model of particle physics.
This has been demonstrated, for example, by comparing measurements of the strong coupling
\alpha_{s}

extracted from ratios between three- and two-jet production cross sections with theoretical predictions
computed at next-to-leading order (NLO) in QCD
Abazov:2012lua
;
Chatrchyan:2013txa
;
CMS:2014mna
;
ATLAS:2015yaa
.
Given the amount of experimental data that has been collected since these studies were conducted and
the amount of data forecast to be collected in the near future at the LHC, there is great interest in
obtaining theoretical predictions that will allow these types of studies to be carried out at a new
level of precision. In this paper, we provide a key missing ingredient required to reach
this goal, namely the leading-color two-loop contributions to
the three-jet production cross section at hadron colliders at
next-to-next-to-leading order (NNLO) in QCD. These are the so-called

double-virtual

2\rightarrow 3
contributions, which together with the

real-virtual

2\rightarrow 4
and the
double-real

2\rightarrow 5
contributions complete the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{2})
corrections
to the production of three jets at hadron colliders.

The calculation of NNLO QCD corrections to two-jet production at hadron
colliders has been a major success of the field in recent years. Results were obtained
first at leading color
Ridder:2013mf
;
Currie:2016bfm
;
Currie:2017eqf

and later subleading-color terms were incorporated
Czakon:2019tmo
(see
also
Currie:2013dwa
). Three-jet production is a more complex process.
Results are currently known at NLO in QCD
Nagy:2001fj
;
Nagy:2003tz
(see
also
Kilgore:1996sq
;
Kilgore:2000dr
) and also including electroweak
corrections
Frederix:2018nkq
;
Reyer:2019obz
. Work on the NNLO QCD
corrections to three-jet production has so far been reported only for the
leading-color two-loop scattering amplitudes, first through numerical
evaluations
Badger:2013gxa
;
Badger:2017jhb
;
Abreu:2017hqn
;
Badger:2018gip
;
Abreu:2018jgq
and then in analytic
form
Gehrmann:2015bfy
;
Badger:2018enw
;
Abreu:2018zmy
;
Abreu:2019odu
;
DeLaurentis:2020qle
.
Progress in including subleading-color contributions has been made for related
supersymmetric theories
Abreu:2018aqd
;
Chicherin:2018yne
;
Caron-Huot:2020vlo
as well as for special
external gluon states
Badger:2019djh
.
In this work we present the leading-color double-virtual contributions
to the scattering cross section, starting from the known analytic
amplitudes
Abreu:2019odu
which we analytically continue from the
(non-physical) Euclidean region to the physical region of phase space.
Furthermore, we implement our expressions in a publicly available

C++
library that allows one to evaluate these contributions efficiently
and in a stable manner, making our results readily available for a future full
calculation of the corresponding NNLO QCD corrections.

Our work builds on a number of recent technical advances that have paved the way to the calculation of two-loop scattering amplitudes for
2\rightarrow 3

Abreu:2020cwb

Chawdhry:2020for
;
Chawdhry:2019bji

We employ the known pure two-loop master

;
Chicherin:2020oor

In ref.
Chicherin:2020oor

in terms of a basis of functions for all assignments of the incoming
momenta required for the double-virtual corrections we compute, and they
can be efficiently evaluated in the physical region corresponding to three-jet production.

We obtain the analytic form of a generating set of partial amplitudes in the physical region
by analytically continuing the known amplitudes from the Euclidean region
Abreu:2019odu
.
Based on considerations on the analytic continuation of the pentagon functions,
the continuation of each helicity amplitude is reduced to a linear-algebra problem

evaluations of the amplitudes in a finite field

;
Peraro:2016wsq
;
Abreu:2017hqn

Caravel

region to results valid in other regions.
Finally, we assemble all partial amplitudes into squared color- and
helicity-summed finite remainders, which can be numerically evaluated over
phase space with the provided
C++
library
FivePointAmplitudes
.
We perform several checks on our results and verify
the efficiency and numerical stability of our implementation.

2.1
Helicity Amplitudes

We consider all the channels contributing to the production of three partons
at hadron colliders. We label the initial-state particles
with indices 1 and 2, and the final-state particles with indices 3, 4 and 5.
There is a single channel with five gluons,

\mathrm{g}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to\mathrm{g}^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\,,

(1)

three channels with a pair of quark and anti-quark,

\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}q^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to\mathrm{g}^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\,,\quad\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to\bar{q}^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\,,\quad\mathrm{g}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to q^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\bar{q}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\,,

(2)

and four channels with two distinct pairs of quark and anti-quark

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}q^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to Q^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\bar{Q}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\,,\quad\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}Q^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to Q^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\bar{q}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\,,\\
&\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}\bar{Q}^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to\bar{q}^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\bar{Q}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\,,\quad\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to\bar{q}^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}Q^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\bar{Q}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}.\end{split}

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}q^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to q^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\bar{q}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}=\left(\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}q^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to Q^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\bar{Q}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\right)-\left(\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}Q^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to Q^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\bar{q}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\right)\,,\\

(4)

In order to obtain them we thus also compute the (redundant) channels

\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}\bar{Q}^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to\bar{Q}^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}\bar{q}^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}\,,\quad\bar{q}^{h_{1}}_{p_{1}}\mathrm{g}^{h_{2}}_{p_{2}}\to\bar{Q}^{h_{3}}_{p_{3}}Q^{h_{4}}_{p_{4}}\bar{q}^{h_{5}}_{p_{5}}.

We will consider each of the channels above in the leading-color approximation.
Let us denote the set of helicities by a vector
{\bf\it h}
and
the set of momenta by a vector
{\bf\it p}
. The amplitudes
\mathcal{M}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})

describing each of the channels above,
which are vectors in color space,
can be expanded in powers of the bare strong
coupling
\alpha_{s}^{0}=(g_{s}^{0})^{2}/(4\pi)

\mathcal{M}=\left(g_{s}^{0}\right)^{3}\left(\mathcal{M}^{(0)}+\frac{\alpha^{0}_{s}}{2\pi}\mathcal{M}^{(1)}+\left(\frac{\alpha^{0}_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\mathcal{M}^{(2)}+\mathcal{O}\left((\alpha^{0}_{s})^{3}\right)\right)\,,

(6)

where we drop the dependence on
{\bf\it h}

{\bf\it p}

The order of
\alpha^{0}_{s}
is aligned with the number of loops of the Feynman diagrams contributing
to the associated coefficient, so we refer to
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}

\mathcal{M}^{(2)}

\mathcal{M}^{(0)}

All calculations will be done in the ’t Hooft-Veltman (HV) scheme of dimensional regularization
with

\epsilon

For our definition of helicity amplitudes with external quarks, we refer the reader
to the detailed discussion in ref.
Abreu:2018jgq
.

For each channel, the coefficients
\mathcal{M}^{(i)}
can be decomposed in terms of different color structures,
and at leading color this decomposition is independent of the loop order. We write

\mathcal{M}^{(i)}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})=\left(\frac{N_{c}}{2}\right)^{i}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\mathcal{C}\left(\sigma\right)\Phi\left(\sigma({\bf\it h}),\sigma({\bf\it p})\right)\mathcal{A}^{(i)}\left(\sigma({\bf\it h}),\sigma({\bf\it p})\right)\,,

2.2
Finite Remainders

Amplitudes contribute to physical observables only through so-called finite
remainders (see e.g.
Weinzierl:2011uz
),
which we denote
\mathcal{R}\left({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}\right)
.
We define a remainder for each partial amplitude
\mathcal{A}\left({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}\right)
.
They are obtained from the renormalized amplitudes by removing the remaining singularities
of infrared origin that can be determined from lower-loop amplitudes and well-known
universal factors
Catani:1998bh
;
Becher:2009cu
;
Gardi:2009qi
.
The finite remainders can be expanded in powers
of
\alpha_{s}
,

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{R}^{(0)}+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\mathcal{R}^{(1)}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\mathcal{R}^{(2)}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\,,

(15)

with the
\mathcal{R}^{(i)}
defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{R}^{(0)}&=\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(0)},\\
\mathcal{R}^{(1)}&=\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(1)}-\mathbf{I}^{(1)}\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(0)}~+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),\\
\mathcal{R}^{(2)}&=\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(2)}-\mathbf{I}^{(1)}\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(1)}-\mathbf{I}^{(2)}{\cal A}_{R}^{(0)}~+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon),\end{split}

(16)

The operators
{\bf I}^{(i)}
are channel specific, and we collect them in

appendix

A
for all the channels in
eqs.

1
,
2
and
3
.
In these expressions, we extend the expansion of

{\bf I}^{(1)}\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(1)}
and

{\bf I}^{(2)}\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(0)}
to also include
terms of order
\epsilon^{0}
. This subtracts non-trivial contributions from
the finite term of
\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(1)}
and
\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(2)}
that are related to
the lower-loop amplitudes. It is clear that the finite remainders
\mathcal{R}^{(k)}
contain
the genuine new information at order
k
.
We stress that even though our calculations are performed in the HV scheme of dimensional regularization,
the remainders computed in this scheme agree with the ones computed in the
conventional dimensional regularization (CDR) scheme, see e.g.
Broggio:2015dga
.
Finally, the finite remainders can also be
decomposed into powers of
{N_{f}}
, similarly to
eq.

11
,

\mathcal{R}^{(i)}\left({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\left(\frac{N_{f}}{N_{c}}\right)^{j}\mathcal{R}^{(i)[j]}\left({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}\right)\,.

(17)

Since the functions
\mathcal{R}^{(i)[j]}
are dimensionless by construction, their scale dependence
can be restored at any point by rescaling the momenta as
{\bf\it p}\to{\bf\it p}/\mu
(we set the factorization scale
\mu_{F}

to be equal to the renormalization scale,
\mu_{F}=\mu
).
Unless it happens to be important for the point we wish to make, we will keep this dependence implicit.

The color-dressed remainder
\mathcal{R}^{(i)}_{\mathcal{M}}\left({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}\right)
is
trivially obtained from the decomposition of the color-dressed amplitude
\mathcal{M}^{(i)}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})
into partial
amplitudes, see

7
:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{M}}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})&=g_{s}^{3}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{M}}^{(0)}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{M}}^{(1)}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{M}}^{(2)}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3})\right)\,,\\
\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{M}}^{(i)}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})&=\left(\frac{N_{c}}{2}\right)^{i}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\mathcal{C}\left(\sigma\right)\Phi\left(\sigma({\bf\it h}),\sigma({\bf\it p})\right)\mathcal{R}^{(i)}\left(\sigma({\bf\it h}),\sigma({\bf\it p})\right)\,.\end{split}

(18)

Whenever the distinction is important, we will call the
\mathcal{R}^{(i)}\left(\sigma({\bf\it h}),\sigma({\bf\it p})\right)

partial remainders, in analogy with the concept of partial amplitudes. We also recall that, even though this is kept implicit,
the color-dressed remainders are vectors in color space (in general, the
\mathcal{C}\left(\sigma\right)
have open (anti-)fundamental
and adjoint color indices, see
section

3.2
for their explicit form).

2.3
Squared Finite Remainders

When computing physical observables, we are interested in the squared remainder,
summed over color states and, in the case of unpolarized observables, over helicity states.
We denote this object as
H
. It admits an expansion in powers of
\alpha_{s}
as

H=H^{(0)}+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}H^{(1)}+\left(\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\right)^{2}H^{(2)}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s}^{3}).

(19)

In terms of the quantities in
eq.

18
,
H
is defined as

H=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}_{H}}\,\sum_{{\bf\it h}}\mathcal{R}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{M}}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{M}}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})\,,

(20)

where the normalization
\mathcal{N}_{H}
is fixed such that
H^{(0)}=1
,

\mathcal{N}_{H}\coloneqq(4\pi\alpha_{s})^{3}\sum_{{\bf\it h}}\absolutevalue{\mathcal{R}^{(0)}_\mathcal{M}(\vb*{h},\vb*{p})}^{2}\,.

(21)

To keep the notation light, in this equation and the following
we do not write explicitly the sum over contracted colour indices which is understood to be performed
in the squaring procedure.
At order
\alpha_{s}
we have

H^{(1)}=\frac{1}{\sum_{{\bf\it h}}\absolutevalue{\mathcal{R}^{(0)}_\mathcal{M}(\vb*{h},\vb*{p})}^{2}}\sum_{{\bf\it h}}\,2\real\left[\mathcal{R}^{(0)\dagger}_{\mathcal{M}}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{\mathcal{M}}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})\right]\,,

(22)

and at order
\alpha^{2}_{s}

\displaystyle H^{(2)}=\frac{1}{\sum_{{\bf\it h}}\absolutevalue{\mathcal{R}^{(0)}_\mathcal{M}(\vb*{h},\vb*{p})}^{2}}\left(\sum_{{\bf\it h}}\absolutevalue{\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_\mathcal{M}(\vb*{h},\vb*{p})}^{2}+\sum_{{\bf\it h}}2\real\left[\mathcal{R}^{(0)\dagger}_{\mathcal{M}}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})\mathcal{R}^{(2)}_{\mathcal{M}}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})\right]\right)\,,

(23)

with each of the two contributions depicted in
fig.

2
.
We note that, when summing over contracted color indices, we have

\mathcal{C}^{\dagger}(\sigma^{\prime})~\mathcal{C}(\sigma)\sim\delta_{\sigma\sigma^{\prime}}
in the leading-color approximation
we adopt. This greatly simplifies the construction of the squared remainders.

Figure 2
:
Schematic representation for the two kinds of contributions to
H^{(2)}
: the square of one-loop amplitudes,
and the interference between tree-level and two-loop amplitudes.

To be clear about which terms we keep in the leading-color approximation,
we explicitly write the decomposition of our remainders in powers of
{N_{f}}/{N_{c}}
.
For
H^{(1)}
, we have

H^{(1)}=\frac{{N_{c}}}{2}\left(H^{(1)[0]}+\frac{{N_{f}}}{{N_{c}}}H^{(1)[1]}\right)\,.

(24)

For
H^{(2)}
,

H^{(2)}=\left(\frac{{N_{c}}}{2}\right)^{2}\left(H^{(2)[0]}+\frac{{N_{f}}}{{N_{c}}}H^{(2)[1]}+\left(\frac{{N_{f}}}{{N_{c}}}\right)^{2}H^{(2)[2]}\right)\,.

(25)

For most channels the subleading-color corrections are suppressed by a factor of
1/N_{c}^{2}
. However, for the channels
in
eq.

4
with two identical quark lines the suppression is by a single power of
N_{c}
.

3.1
Partial Amplitudes in the Physical Region

The first step towards the construction of the squared finite remainder in the physical region is to
obtain the amplitudes computed in ref.
Abreu:2019odu
in such region.
Here we consider only the basis set of partial helicity amplitudes of
ref.
Abreu:2019odu
, and we will return to their permutations required to assemble
eq.

23
in
section

3.2
.
The amplitudes of ref.
Abreu:2019odu
were computed in the
(non-physical) Euclidean region where

s_{12},s_{23},s_{34},s_{45},s_{15}<0\,,\qquad{\rm tr}_{5}^{2}>0\,.

(26)

It is our aim to construct results in the physical region associated with the channels in

eqs.

1
,
2
,
3
,
5
and
4
,
where
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are initial-state momenta and
p_{3}
,
p_{4}
and
p_{5}

are final-state momenta. This region is characterized by

s_{12},s_{34},s_{45}>0,\qquad s_{23},s_{15}<0,\qquad{\rm tr}_{5}^{2}<0.

(27)

The latter condition is trivially satisfied by real-valued momenta.
In this section, for clarity, we will denote a phase-space point in the physical region as

{\bf\it p}_{\varphi}
and a phase-space point in the Euclidean region as
{\bf\it p}_{E}
.
For region-agnostic statements we simply denote a phase-space point as

{\bf\it p}
.

In order to explain how we convert the results of ref.
Abreu:2019odu
to the kinematic region
in
eq.

27
, let us briefly summarize how they were obtained.
The discussion below holds for each power of
{N_{f}}
, that is for each

\mathcal{A}^{(i)[j]}
, but for simplicity of the expressions we will
suppress this dependence in intermediate steps.
The first step is a computation of the decomposition of the bare partial amplitudes
at one and two loops into a set of master integrals:

\mathcal{A}^{(i)}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p};\epsilon)=\sum_{k}c^{(i)}_{k}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p};\epsilon)\,m^{(i)}_{k}({\bf\it p};\epsilon)\,,\qquad i=1,2\,,

(28)

where we make the dependence on the dimensional regulator
\epsilon
explicit.
This decomposition was obtained using the two-loop numerical unitarity
approach
Ita:2015tya
;
Abreu:2017xsl
;
Abreu:2017hqn
;
Abreu:2018jgq
.
At this stage, the master integrals were replaced by their expansion in terms of
Euclidean-region pentagon functions.
3
3

3

These functions are
a basis for the transcendental
functions appearing in five-point massless one- and two-loop master integrals.
We refer the reader to ref.
Gehrmann:2018yef
for more
details on their definition.

Denoting the vector space spanned by relevant pentagon-function monomials in this region as
\vec{f}({\bf\it p}_{E})=\{f_{l}({\bf\it p}_{E})\}_{l\in B}
,
with
B
denoting the set of multi-indices
l
, we write

m^{(i)}_{k}({\bf\it p}_{E};\epsilon)=\sum_{k=0}^{4}\sum_{l\in B}\epsilon^{k}d^{(i)}_{k,l}f_{l}({\bf\it p}_{E})+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{5})\,.

(29)

In this decomposition, we assume that we have a pure basis of master integrals
ArkaniHamed:2010gh
at one
and two loops, in which case the coefficients
d^{(i)}_{k,l}
are rational numbers.
Furthermore, we assume all integrals are normalized so that their Laurent expansion around

\epsilon=0
has no poles, and stress that the set of pentagon functions is common
to one and two loops.
By inserting the decomposition (
29
) into
eq.

28

for both one- and two-loop amplitudes, and then using these expressions in
the definitions for the finite remainders given in
eq.

16
,
we obtain a decomposition of the two-loop remainders into pentagon functions:

\mathcal{R}^{(i)[j]}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}_{E})=\sum_{k\in B}r^{(i)[j]}_{k}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}_{E})\,f_{k}({\bf\it p}_{E})\,,

(30)

where we have reintroduced the superscript
[j]
to stress that the decomposition holds
for each power of
{N_{f}}
. Obtaining the analytic form of the coefficients

r^{(i)[j]}_{k}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})
was the main result of ref.
Abreu:2019odu
.
Let us finish this summary by commenting on the structure of the coefficients

r^{(i)[j]}_{k}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})
. They can be written as

r_{k}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})=r^{+}_{k}({\bf\it h},\vec{s})+r^{-}_{k}({\bf\it h},\vec{s})\,{\rm tr}_{5}\,,

(31)

where we briefly suppress the
i
and
j
indices, we recall
\vec{s}=\{s_{12},s_{23},s_{34},s_{45},s_{15}\}
, and

r^{\pm}_{k}({\bf\it h},\vec{s})
are rational functions of the Mandelstam variables.
The analytic continuation of the
r_{k}
from Euclidean to physical
region is therefore trivial.
For a given
i
and
j
, the
r_{k}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})
are not
all linearly independent of each other (over
\mathbb{Q}
) for different values of
k
, and
we can define a basis of rational functions

\vec{v}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})=\{v_{1}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}),\ldots\}
such
that
all
r_{k}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})
live in the

\mathbb{Q}
-linear span of
\vec{v}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})
. We can
then write

\mathcal{R}^{(i)[j]}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}_{E})=\vec{v}^{(i)[j]}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}_{E})\cdot M^{(i)[j]}_{E}({\bf\it h})\cdot\vec{f}({\bf\it p}_{E})\,,

(32)

where
M_{E}^{(i)[j]}
is a matrix of rational numbers.

To understand our approach to the analytic continuation of
eq. (
32
), let us begin by
considering what would happen if we had chosen a different basis of pentagon functions

\vec{f}^{\prime}({\bf\it p}_{E})
, such that
\vec{f}^{\prime}({\bf\it p}_{E})=N\cdot\vec{f}({\bf\it p}_{E})
where

N
is a matrix of rational numbers. It is clear that to write
eq.

32

in this new basis of functions we do not need to determine the
basis

\vec{v}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}_{E})
of rational functions. The remainders
would have
exactly the same form, but with the matrix
M_{E}
replaced by

M^{\prime(i)[j]}_{E}=M^{(i)[j]}_{E}\cdot N^{-1}
, which is just a different matrix of rational numbers.

Let us now discuss what happens with the pentagon functions under analytic continuation,
which we will do through an example.
Consider the simplest non-trivial pentagon function,
\log(-s_{12})
.
The choice to write it in this form follows from the fact that in the Euclidean region
s_{12}<0
and
the master integrals are real valued.
We note that, in this region, we could also have written
\log(\abs{s_{12}})
.
Let us now continue this function to the physical region in
eq.

27
, where
s_{12}>0
.
We find

\log(-s_{12})=\log(\abs{s_{12}})-\,\mathrm{i}\,\pi\,

(33)

where the sign is determined by the usual Feynman
\,\mathrm{i}\,\varepsilon
-prescription of
the propagators. We see that the analytic continuation into the physical region introduces
a new pentagon “function”, the transcendental constant
\,\mathrm{i}\,\pi
. Crucially, however, the coefficient
of this function is generated by the analytic continuation of
\log(-s_{12})
. This implies
that the coefficient of
\,\mathrm{i}\,\pi

is trivially related to the coefficient of
\log(-s_{12})

when analytically continuing
eq.

29
or
eq.

30
, or more precisely that it is
in the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear span of

\vec{v}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}_{\varphi})
(we recall that
{\bf\it p}_{\varphi}
is used to denote
a point in the physical region).
We assume that this
is a general feature of the analytic continuation of pentagon
functions: while the dimension of the vector space in
eq.

30

might increase, the coefficients of the new pentagon functions
are not independent
from the coefficients of the pentagon functions in the Euclidean region.
4
4

4

In general, the two will be related by the (integer) winding numbers that naturally appear
when analytically continuing multi-valued functions.

Even though we do not prove this statement, as we shall see below we can explicitly check that
it holds for the remainders in
eq.

32
.

To analytically continue the remainders computed in ref.
Abreu:2019odu
,
we use the set of pentagon functions defined in ref.
Chicherin:2020oor
, and we denote the vector space of their monomials
as
\vec{g}({\bf\it p}_{\varphi})
.
These are valid precisely in the kinematic region of
eq.

27
, and are related to the set

\vec{f}({\bf\it p}_{E})
used in ref.
Abreu:2019odu
by a
change of basis and by analytic continuation.
Given the points raised above
about these two operations, this means that remainders in the physical
region can be written in terms of the same bases of rational functions that were found for
the Euclidean region. Hence we can write the partial remainder in the
physical region as

\mathcal{R}^{(i)[j]}_{\varphi}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}_{\varphi};\mu)=\vec{v}^{(i)[j]}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p}_{\varphi})\cdot M^{(i)[j]}_{\varphi}({\bf\it h})\cdot\vec{g}({\bf\it p}_{\varphi}/\mu)\,,

(34)

where for completeness we made the dependence on
\mu
explicit.
We stress that this representation is valid only when
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
are in the initial state
and
p_{3}
,
p_{4}
and
p_{5}
are in the final state, because that is the region of validity of the pentagon
functions
\vec{g}({\bf\it p}_{\varphi})
.
We discuss the generalization to
other configurations in
section

3.2
.
The
M^{(i)[j]}_{\varphi}({\bf\it h})
can be determined from a small number of
numerical evaluations of the amplitudes. To do this, we take equation

eq.

34
as an ansatz for the form of the remainder when expressed in
terms of the physical region pentagon functions
\vec{g}({\bf\it p}_{\varphi})
, with
the entries of the
M^{(i)[j]}_{\varphi}({\bf\it h})
as unknowns.
We then constrain the unknowns by performing the analogous procedure of
eqs. (
29
) and (
30
)
at a sufficient number of phase-space points.
5
5

5

This
number is dictated by
the number of independent functions in
\vec{v}^{(i)[j]}({\bf\it h},{\bf\it p})
.
The most complicated case, the five-gluon remainder

\mathcal{R}^{(2)[0]}_{\varphi}(\{-1,+1,-1,+1,+1\},{\bf\it p})
, requires 57 evaluations.
We note that a single numerical evaluation gives more than one constraint.

Linear algebra then allows us to extract the values of

M^{(i)[j]}_{\varphi}({\bf\it h})
.

In practice, we use the implementation of two-loop numerical unitarity in

Caravel

Abreu:2020xvt
to generate all the required numerical data. For
efficiency reasons, the procedure is performed over finite fields and we
construct the
\mathbb{Q}
-valued
M^{(i)[j]}_{\varphi}({\bf\it h})
via the
Chinese remainder theorem and standard rational reconstruction techniques.
We remark that we used at most three finite fields of cardinality
\mathcal{O}(2^{32})
.
Following these steps, we obtain the analytic continuation of the
partial amplitudes computed in ref.
Abreu:2019odu
to the physical region
defined in
eq.

27
, corresponding to the channels
in
eqs.

1
,
2
,
3
,
5
and
4
.
Crucially, this linear algebra exercise would not allow one to compute the
matrices
M^{(i)[j]}_{\varphi}({\bf\it h})
in the ansatz (
34
) if the assumption on the analytic
continuation of the pentagon functions did not hold.

4.2
Numerical Evaluation

Having phenomenological applications in mind, we implemented a
C++
library
that allows for the efficient evaluation of finite remainders and squared finite remainders.
In this section we comment on this numerical implementation.

Let us start by describing the quantities which can be evaluated with our
C++
library
FivePointAmplitudes
.
The code performs the numerical evaluation of one- and two-loop (partial) remainders
for each channel, each helicity configuration and each power of
N_{f}
. That is, we evaluate each of the

\mathcal{R}^{(i)[j]}\left(\sigma({\bf\it h}),\sigma({\bf\it p})\right)
for
i=1,2
and present each power
of
(N_{f}/N_{c})^{j}
separately. We stress that we have implemented all permutations, and not only the generating set
for which we provide analytic expressions. For physical applications, one might be interested
in the squared finite remainders defined in
eqs.

22
and
23
. Our code also outputs the numerical
values for these quantities, once again presenting each power of
N_{f}/N_{c}
separately, that is the

H^{(i)[j]}
in
eqs.

24
and
25
.

We employ
PentagonFunctions++

Chicherin:2020oor
for the numerical evaluation of the pentagon functions.
For the efficient evaluation of the rational functions, we generate optimized code with
FORM

Ruijl:2017dtg
;
Kuipers:2013pba
.
In any of the helicity remainders
\mathcal{R}^{(2)[j]}\left(\sigma({\bf\it h}),\sigma({\bf\it p})\right)
that we compute numerically,
the dominant contribution to the evaluation time is that of the pentagon functions.
When computing
H^{(1)}
and
H^{(2)}
, we have thus organized our numerical code such that the pentagon functions are
evaluated only once per phase-space point,
independently of the number of permutations contributing to the sums in
eqs.

22
and
23
.
We note nevertheless that, compared to e.g. the case of triphoton production
Abreu:2020cwb
,
the evaluation of
H^{(2)}
for five-gluon channels receives contributions from a large number of partial amplitudes, and therefore
the time spent in the evaluation of the other components in
eq.

39
becomes noticeable.
We find that the typical double-precision evaluation time for any
H^{(2)}
is
\mathcal{O}(1\text{s})
on a single CPU,
while the evaluation time for
H^{(1)}
is negligible at
\mathcal{O}(10\text{ms})
.

To be suitable for phenomenological applications the numerical evaluations should not only be fast but also stable across phase space.
To guarantee the latter, we develop a precision-rescue strategy that detects and corrects unstable numerical evaluations.
The main source of numerical instabilities is the presence of large cancellations between different contributions in
eq.

39
, when
a phase-space point gets close to the zero sets of the rational functions’ denominators.
As we have already argued in refs.
Abreu:2018zmy
;
Abreu:2019odu
;
Abreu:2020cwb
,
these are determined by a set of 25 quantities
\{W_{i}(\vec{s})\}
, with
i=1,\ldots,25
, which are
a subset of the so-called alphabet associated with the five-point
two-loop master integrals (see e.g. ref.
Chicherin:2017dob
for the definition of these quantities).
This subset is rational in the Mandelstam variables
\vec{s}
defined in
eq.

9
and all
elements have the same dimensions.
We find that potentially unstable points can be efficiently identified by computing a quantity that characterizes the spread of scales for a given phase-space point
\vec{s}
,
8
8

8

We attempted to further reduce the subset of letters to only include those that correspond to spurious singularities, as these had been identified
in ref.
Abreu:2020cwb

the five-parton channels considered in this paper this subset covers most of the unstable points, but we must also include non-spurious singularities to detect all unstable points.

\kappa(\vec{s})\coloneqq\frac{\min_{i}\{\absolutevalue{W_i(\vec s)}\}}{\max_{i}\{\absolutevalue{W_i(\vec s)}\}}\,.

(40)

We introduce a threshold
\kappa_{\text{thr}}
,

4.3
Validation

The main analytic results of our paper are the matrices
described in
section

4.1
. These are obtained by
building on the analytic results of ref.
Abreu:2019odu
,
which have undergone very stringent checks. Compared to
ref.
Abreu:2019odu
, our current results are written in terms of
a different basis of transcendental functions
Chicherin:2020oor

which have also undergone several direct checks (e.g., comparison
with the functions of ref.
Gehrmann:2018yef
)
and indirect checks
Kallweit:2020gcp
;
Abreu:2020cwb
;
Chawdhry:2020for
;
Agarwal:2021grm
. Furthermore, we have verified
that we reproduce the results independently obtained in
ref.
Badger:2018gip

for the numerical evaluation of five-gluon amplitudes in the
physical region defined in
eq.

27
.
Finally, we compared our results for the function
H^{(2)[0]}_{\mathrm{g}\mathrm{g}\to\mathrm{g}\mathrm{g}\mathrm{g}}
in the purely gluonic channel against an independent computation
Badger:correspondence
and found agreement.

We have also performed several consistency checks on our numerical results.
Since we obtain all the helicity partial remainders required for the functions
H^{(1)}
and
H^{(2)}

from sums over the permutations of the generating sets of remainders introduced in
section

3.2
,
it is important to check that these operations
are correctly implemented in the
C++
library we have developed.
We have made three types of consistency checks to validate this procedure.

Symmetries.

We have checked that the remainders and squared remainders satisfy all expected charge, parity conjugation and permutation symmetries.

Validation of
H^{(1)}
.

We have compared the one-loop squared remainders
H^{(1)}
of all the channels in
eqs.

1
,
2
,
3
,
5
and
4

against the evaluations by the
BlackHat
library
Berger:2008sj
and found full agreement.
10
10

10

After adjusting the definitions of the leading-color squared matrix elements in
BlackHat
such that all subleading-color contributions are discarded.

The color-dressed remainders
\mathcal{R}^{(1)}_{\mathcal{M}}
in
eq.

18
are constructed from generating sets of partial
remainders, as described in
section

3.2
, and then are directly fed into the definition of
H^{(1)}
, see
eq.

22
.
The procedure for assembling the squared remainders is independent of the number of loops, hence
this check validates the corresponding assembly of the contributions for the calculation of
H^{(2)}
.

Scale dependence of remainders.

Given that we evaluate the finite remainders directly, the correct pole structure of the underlying scattering amplitudes
is satisfied by construction. We note nevertheless that
finite remainders retain a memory of the poles of the
amplitudes and of their explicit (scheme-dependent) definition.
This information is encoded in the scale dependence of the finite remainders.
Quite generally, we find that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\partial\,\mathcal{R}^{(1)}(\mu)}{\partial\log\mu}~=~&\frac{2\beta_{0}\lambda}{N_{c}}\mathcal{R}^{(0)}\,,\\
\frac{\partial\,\mathcal{R}^{(2)}(\mu)}{\partial\log\mu}~=~&2\left(\frac{\beta_{1}\lambda}{N_{c}^{2}}+{\bf H}_{[n]}\right)\mathcal{R}^{(0)}+\frac{2\beta_{0}\left(2+\lambda\right)}{N_{c}}\mathcal{R}^{(1)}(\mu)\,,\end{split}

(45)

\lambda
the power of
g_{s}^{0}
in the tree-level amplitude (for five-parton amplitudes
\lambda=3
, see
eq.

6
) and
the
\beta_{i}
are the coefficients of the QCD
\beta

eq.

13
.
{\bf H}_{[n]}

is given explicitly in
eqs.

51
and
52

and controls the collinear singularities of scattering amplitudes. We have
verified that the scale dependence of the remainders obtained from our numerical implementation is described by
eq.

45

independently checked that, starting from this

H^{(1)}
and
H^{(2)}
, which again validates the correct assembly of these
functions.

5
Conclusion

In this paper we present the NNLO double-virtual contributions to three-jet production at hadron colliders
at leading color. While all required amplitudes were already available in the literature in analytic form,
they were only available in the non-physical Euclidean region, and as such could not be directly
used for phenomenological applications. Aside from analytically continuing those expressions
to the physical region, we also provide a
C++
library that allows to efficiently
evaluate these contributions and obtain stable results across phase space.

The analytic continuation of the results of ref.
Abreu:2019odu
to the physical
region corresponding to three-jet production is performed
by rewriting them in terms of the pentagon functions defined in ref.
Chicherin:2020oor
.
By noticing that this rewriting amounts to computing a matrix of rational numbers,
we obtained the physical-region expressions from a few numerical evaluations of the amplitudes
(57 in the most complicated case). These evaluations were performed with the
implementation of the two-loop numerical unitarity approach in
Caravel

Abreu:2020xvt
.
We provide analytic expressions for a generating set of partial remainders.
For all channels in
eqs.

1
,
2
,
3
,
5
and
4
,
these allow one to evaluate the color-dressed remainders defined in
eq.

18
as well as
the squared remainders
H^{(1)}
and
H^{(2)}
defined in
eqs.

24
and
25

in the physical region.

Our driving motivation was to provide results that are ready to be used for phenomenological
applications. To this end, we developed a
C++
library which
efficiently evaluates individual partial remainders as well as the one- and
two-loop squared remainders. The numerical evaluations can be performed in double or quadruple
precision. We find that double precision is sufficient for the vast majority of phase-space points,
and have implemented a rescue system which detects unstable points
and reevaluates them in quadruple precision. With the rescue system enabled, we find
very good overall numerical stability and our evaluation time for the two-loop squared remainders is

\mathcal{O}(1\text{s})
. This shows that our results and the numerical library
we provide are ready to be used in phenomenological studies.

Appendix A
Infrared Subtraction

The divergences of renormalized two-loop amplitudes obey a universal structure
Catani:1998bh
;
Becher:2009cu
;
Gardi:2009qi
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(1)}&={\bf I}^{(1)}_{[n]}(\epsilon)\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(0)}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{0})\,,\\
\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(2)}&={\bf I}^{(2)}_{[n]}(\epsilon)A_{R}^{(0)}+{\bf I}^{(1)}_{[n]}(\epsilon)\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(1)}+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{0})\,,\end{split}

(46)

with the renormalized amplitudes
\mathcal{A}_{R}^{(i)}
related to the bare amplitudes

\mathcal{A}^{(i)}
as in
eq.

14
.
For amplitudes in the leading-color approximation the operators

\mathbf{I}^{(1)}_{[n]}
and
\mathbf{I}^{(2)}_{[n]}
are
diagonal in color space.
The operator
\mathbf{I}^{(1)}_{[n]}
is given by

{\bf I}^{(1)}_{[n]}(\epsilon)=-\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}\epsilon}}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{a_{i},a_{i+1}}\left(-\frac{s_{i,i+1}}{\mu^{2}}-\,\mathrm{i}\,\varepsilon\right)^{-\epsilon}\,,

(47)

where
s_{i,j}=(p_{i}+p_{j})^{2}
, with the indices defined cyclically,
and we made explicit the
\,\mathrm{i}\,\varepsilon
associated with Mandelstam
variables that follows from the Feynman
\,\mathrm{i}\,\varepsilon
-prescription
of the propagators.
The index
a_{i}
denotes a type of particle,
i.e.
a_{i}\in\{\mathrm{g},q,\bar{q},Q,\bar{Q}\}
.
The symbols
\gamma_{a,b}
are symmetric
under exchange of indices,

\gamma_{a,b}=\gamma_{b,a}
, and given by:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma_{\mathrm{g},\mathrm{g}}&=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\frac{\beta_{0}}{{N_{c}}}\,,\qquad\gamma_{q,Q}=\gamma_{q,\bar{Q}}=\gamma_{\bar{q},Q}=\gamma_{\bar{q},\bar{Q}}=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{3}{2\epsilon}\,,\\
\gamma_{\mathrm{g},q}&=\gamma_{\mathrm{g},\bar{q}}=\gamma_{\mathrm{g},Q}=\gamma_{\mathrm{g},\bar{Q}}=\frac{\gamma_{\mathrm{g},\mathrm{g}}+\gamma_{q,Q}}{2}\,,\qquad\gamma_{q,\bar{q}}=\gamma_{Q,\bar{Q}}=0\,.\end{split}

(48)

The operator
{\bf I}^{(2)}_{[n]}
is

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bf I}^{(2)}_{[n]}(\epsilon)=&-\frac{1}{2}{\bf I}^{(1)}_{[n]}(\epsilon){\bf I}^{(1)}_{[n]}(\epsilon)-\frac{2\beta_{0}}{{N_{c}}\epsilon}{\bf I}^{(1)}_{[n]}(\epsilon)+\frac{e^{-\gamma_{E}\epsilon}\Gamma(1-2\epsilon)}{\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}\left(\frac{2\beta_{0}}{{N_{c}}\epsilon}+K\right){\bf I}^{(1)}_{[n]}(2\epsilon)\\
&+\frac{e^{\gamma_{E}\epsilon}}{\epsilon\Gamma(1-\epsilon)}{\bf H}_{[n]}\,,\end{split}

(49)

where

K=\frac{67}{9}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3}-\frac{10}{9}\frac{{N_{f}}}{{N_{c}}}\,,

(50)

and
{\bf H}_{[n]}
is a diagonal operator at
leading color that depends on the number of external quarks
and gluons,

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\bf H}_{[n]}&=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\delta_{a_{i},\mathrm{g}}H_{\mathrm{g}}+(\delta_{a_{i},q}+\delta_{a_{i},\bar{q}}+\delta_{a_{i},Q}+\delta_{a_{i},\bar{Q}})H_{q}\right)\,,\end{split}

(51)

with

\displaystyle\begin{split}H_{\mathrm{g}}&=\left(\frac{\zeta_{3}}{2}+\frac{5}{12}+\frac{11\pi^{2}}{144}\right)-\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{72}+\frac{89}{108}\right)\frac{N_{f}}{{N_{c}}}+\frac{5}{27}\left(\frac{N_{f}}{{N_{c}}}\right)^{2}\,,\\
H_{q}&=\left(\frac{7\zeta_{3}}{4}+\frac{409}{864}-\frac{11\pi^{2}}{96}\right)+\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{48}-\frac{25}{216}\right)\frac{N_{f}}{{N_{c}}}\,.\end{split}

(52)

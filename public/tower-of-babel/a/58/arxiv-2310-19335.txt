Abstract

The problem is known to have a polynomial time algorithm on the real RAM model of computation, however no sub-exponential time algorithm is known in the bit or Turing model of computation. The precise computational complexity of SSR has been a notorious open problem [ GGJ76 ] over the last four decades. Proof of Theorem

via Linear Threshold Functions

A Boolean function

AB09
]

The real numbers
w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{m}
are said to be a

[
Mur65
]
says that any LTF over a
bounded discrete domain

, can also be realized with integer weights of
small
4
4

4

It is easy to see that there always exists

integer realization for any LTF. Indeed, consider
\varepsilon=min_{x\in\mathbb{B}^{m}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}x_{i}-w_{0}\right)
, and for every
i\in[m]
, pick a rational number

in the interval
(w_{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{10m},w_{i}+\frac{\varepsilon}{10m})
. Clearly, for every
x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
,
\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}x_{i}-w_{0}>0
if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}^{\prime}x_{i}-w_{0}^{\prime}>0

depend on
\varepsilon

{\cal O}(m\log{m})
-bits).
In fact the theorem shows that such a representation is only dependent on

(Muroga

)

Let

The proof of Theorem

We invoke Theorem

We now present a fundamentally different

approximate magnitudes
of (
m+1
)

UUSSR

UUSSR

with a

in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}

m+1

\mathbb{B}^{m+1}
which spans
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
Could it be possible that the sign of this inner product can be estimated from the sign/value of the inner products of the basis elements? While this is not possible for an arbitrary basis, we show that there exists a
nice

First we set up some notation. As alluded to earlier, any

\displaystyle A=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\Delta_{j}\sqrt{s_{j}}=\langle\vec{\Delta},\vec{S}\rangle

where
\vec{\Delta}=(\Delta_{0},\dots,\Delta_{m})\in[-k^{2},k^{2}]^{m+1}
and
\vec{S}=(1,\sqrt{s_{1}},\dots,\sqrt{s_{m}})\in\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
. By Proposition
4
, none of them evaluate to
0
except the inner product with the zero vector.
In what follows, we consider only those vectors
\vec{\Delta}
which have a

on
\vec{S}
, i.e.,
W=\{\vec{\Delta}\in[-k^{2},k^{2}]^{m+1}\mid\langle\vec{\Delta},\vec{S}\rangle>0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
. Let
|W|=t
. Let
\{v_{i}\}_{i=1}^{t}
be the magnitude of the inner product of the elements of

. By our assumption we have

and each of these numbers are distinct since if

it gives rise to a non-trivial linear dependence between the square roots, which is impossible by Proposition

.

We now construct the unique
lightest

\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
from the vectors in

by picking the least (ordered by magnitude of inner product)

[-k^{2},k^{2}]^{m+1}

\mathbb{R}^{m+1}

4
). We denote these vectors by
{\bf b_{0}},{\bf b_{1}},\dots,{\bf b_{m}}
, where
{\bf b_{i}}=(b_{i0},\dots,b_{im})\in[-k^{2},k^{2}]^{m+1}
. Let
v_{0}=\langle{\bf b_{0}},\vec{S}\rangle,v_{1}=\langle{\bf b_{1}},\vec{S}\rangle,\dots,v_{m}=\langle{\bf b_{m}},\vec{S}\rangle
. Given an instance of
UUSSR
of the form
A=\langle\vec{\Delta},\vec{S}\rangle
, it is clear that we can express
\vec{\Delta}
in the lightest basis as
\vec{\Delta}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}{\bf b_{i}}
. The

|c_{i}|\geq\frac{1}{2^{{\cal O}(m\log{km})}}
. Now, we have

A=\langle\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}{\bf b_{i}},\vec{S}\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}\langle{\bf b_{i}},\vec{S}\rangle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}v_{i}

.

Remark 9
.

Note that just by observing that
\frac{1}{2^{{\cal O}(m\log{km})}}\leq c_{i}\leq 2^{{\cal O}(m\log{km})}
we can conclude that there exists
i\in\{0,1,\dots,m\}
such that
|v_{i}|>\frac{1}{2^{{\cal O}(m\log{km})}}
. That is, all the basis vectors cannot have double exponentially small inner product with
\vec{S}
. Because, otherwise, they cannot express
large
instance of
USSR
which are guaranteed to exist, for e.g.
A=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\sqrt{s_{i}}
whose magnitude is larger than
m
.

Notice that expressing the
UUSSR
instance in the lightest basis immediately yields the following simple lower bound.

Proposition 10
.

|A|=|\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}\langle{\bf b_{i}},\vec{S}\rangle|=|\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}v_{i}|>v_{m_{1}}

where
m_{1}\in[m]
is the largest index such that
c_{m_{1}}\neq 0
.

Proof.

If
|A|<v_{m_{1}}
, then by definition (of the lightest basis) the basis vector
{\bf b_{m_{1}}}
can be replaced by
\vec{\Delta}
.
∎

From the preceeding discussion, it seems like in order to find the sign of the quantity
A
, it suffices to know the magnitude and sign of all the
v_{i}
. The best bounds for
v_{i}
are obtained through root separation bounds (Proposition
2
), which turn out to be doubly exponentially small as a function of
m
in magnitude (Proposition
5
). Therefore, they need exponential (in
m
) number of bits to express them unambiguously and any arithmetic with such numbers is bound to result in an exponential time algorithm!

However the lower bound from Proposition
10
gives us some leeway to use approximations to
v_{i}
instead of
v_{i}
themselves. We will now show that if we have a suitable
rational approximation

\widetilde{v}_{i}
to the
v_{i}
’s the resulting error is small.
Towards this we now assume we work with
\widetilde{v}_{i}=\beta_{i}2^{-e_{i}}
, where
\beta_{i}
is a
{\cal O}(m^{2})
-bit rational number in the interval
(1,2)
. Notice that by standard root separation bounds (see Proposition
2
and
5
), one can infer that
|e_{i}|\leq 2^{{\cal O}(m)}
and hence expressible using
{\cal O}(m)
bits. The
\{\widetilde{v}_{i}=\beta_{i}2^{-e_{i}}\}_{i=0}^{m}
are approximations to
\{\langle{\bf b_{i}},\vec{S}\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_{ij}\sqrt{s_{j}}\}_{i=0}^{m}
. We have the following

Proposition 11
.

\widetilde{A}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}\widetilde{v_{i}}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}\beta_{i}2^{-e_{i}}>0
if and only if
A=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}v_{i}>0

Proof.

If we use
\widetilde{v_{i}}
instead of
v_{i}
, we can upper bound the total accumulated error incurred as follows:

\displaystyle|\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}(v_{i}-\widetilde{v_{i}})|\hskip 8.5359pt

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}|v_{i}-\widetilde{v_{i}}|\hskip 8.5359pt=\hskip 8.5359pt\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}\left|v_{i}-\beta_{i}2^{-e_{i}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{m}(2^{m\log{m}})\cdot(2^{-m^{2}}\cdot 2^{-e_{i}})\hskip 8.5359pt\leq\hskip 8.5359ptm\cdot(2^{m\log{m}})\cdot(2^{-m^{2}}2^{-e_{m_{1}}})

where the inequality
|v_{i}-\beta_{i}2^{-e_{i}}|\leq(2^{-m^{2}}\cdot 2^{-e_{i}})
above is obtained by noticing that by definition the approximation
\widetilde{v_{i}}
agrees with
v_{i}
on the mantissa. The last inequality is obtained by noting that
v_{m_{1}}
is the basis vector with the largest magnitude that is used in expressing
A
in terms of the lightest basis. We can now lower bound the ratio of value of the given instance of
UUSSR
to the error accrued is at least

\frac{|\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}v_{i}|}{|\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}(v_{i}-\widetilde{v_{i}})|}\hskip 8.5359pt\geq\hskip 8.5359pt\frac{2^{-e_{m_{1}}}}{m2^{m\log{m}}2^{-m^{2}}2^{-e_{m_{1}}}}\hskip 8.5359pt=\hskip 8.5359pt2^{\Omega(m^{2})}

(1)

This means that the total error accrued by using
\widetilde{v_{i}}
instead of
v_{i}
is a very small fraction of the absolute value of
A
and hence the
{\cal O}(m^{2})
-bit approximation to each
v_{i}
, namely
\widetilde{v_{i}}=\beta_{i}2^{-e_{i}}
suffices to compute the correct sign of
A
.
∎

We are now ready to give our second proof of the
{\bf P/poly}
upper bound.

Proof.

We have as our
{\bf poly}(k)
-length advice string:

1.

The lightest basis
\{{\bf b_{i}}\in[-k^{2},k^{2}]^{m}\}_{i=0}^{m}
.

2.

\{\widetilde{v}_{i}=\beta_{i}2^{-e_{i}}\}_{i=0}^{m}
, which are approximations to
\{\langle{\bf b_{i}},\vec{S}\rangle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}b_{ij}\sqrt{s_{j}}\}_{i=0}^{m}
.

Given this advice, our
{\bf P}/{\bf poly}
algorithm for the
UUSSR
instance
A=\langle\vec{\Delta},\vec{S}\rangle
is straight forward: Firstly notice that
A\neq 0
by Proposition
4
and this can be checked in polynomial time
[
Blo91
,
HBM+10
]
as well. If
\vec{\Delta}
is present in the lightest basis or a multiple of one of the basis elements, then the sign is just obtained directly from the advice string in conjuction with the polynomial time tests mentioned above. Therefore, let us assume that
\Delta
is a non-trivial linear combination of the basis vectors. We proceed as follows:

1.

Find
c_{i}
such that
\vec{\Delta}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}{\bf b_{i}}
.

2.

Return the sign of the appropriate linear combination
\widetilde{A}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}\widetilde{v}_{i}
.

Finding
c_{i}
can be done by solving a system of linear equations, and hence is in
P
. However, it is not clear how to return the sign of the linear combination
\widetilde{A}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}\widetilde{v}_{i}
, because this involves arithmetic with exponential-bit numbers! Recall that by the root separation bound, we only ensured that
e_{i}\leq 2^{{\cal O}(m)}
, so
\widetilde{v_{i}}
’s are still exponetial-bit approximations to
v_{i}
which admit a succinct representation. We are now left with the task of ensuring that (approximately) computing the linear combination can be done efficiently. For this, let
m_{1}\in[m]
be the largest index such that
c_{m_{1}}\neq 0
. Also let
m_{0}\leq m_{1}
be the smallest index such that
e_{m_{0}}-e_{m_{1}}\leq m^{2}
.
Let

\widetilde{V}=\sum_{i=m_{0}}^{m_{1}}{c_{i}\beta_{i}2^{-e_{i}}}=\left(\sum_{i=m_{0}}^{m_{1}}{c_{i}\beta_{i}2^{-(e_{i}-e_{m_{1}})}}\right)2^{-e_{m_{1}}}

(2)

where the quantity inside the large parentheses is a sum of at most
m

rationals each with at most a
2m^{2}
-bit representation, and hence can be computed
in
TC
0
. Essentially we drop all those terms whose exponents are larger than
m_{0}
.
It follows that
\widetilde{V}
is a good approximation
to
\widetilde{A}
and hence also to
A
. To this end, notice that we introduce two
kinds of errors while making this approximation:

1.

The error due to truncation (as argued in Proposition
11
). This error is upper bounded in absolute value by

(m_{1}-m_{0}+1)2^{-{(e_{m_{1}}+m^{2})}}
.

2.

The error due to dropping of all terms preceding
m_{0}
in Equation
2
. This error is upper bounded in absolute value by
(m_{0}-1)2^{(-e_{m_{0}-1}+m^{2})}
.

But we know from the definition of
m_{0}
that
|e_{m_{0}-1}|>|e_{m_{1}}|+m^{2}
. Thus the total error is upper bounded in magnitude by
m_{1}2^{-{(e_{m_{1}}+2m^{2})}}
. As a fraction of the magnitude of the
UUSSR
instance, this error is small and as argued previously via Equation
1
, our approximation using
\widetilde{v_{i}}
always returns the right answer.
∎

Remark 12
.

Note that our second proof seems weaker than the first proof since it seems to require solving a system of linear equations which is not known to be implementable in
TC
0
. However, notice that if we provide the inverse of the
(m+1)\times(m+1)
matrix
[{\bf b_{0}},{\bf b_{1}},\dots,{\bf b_{m}}]
as advice, the algorithm above can be implemented in
TC
0
. Note that this is only an additional
{\bf poly}(m)={\bf poly}(k)
bits long since each entry is a
{\bf poly}(k)
-bit rational number.

4
Summary and Conclusion

We have presented two non-uniform algorithms for
USSR
. Both can be viewed as “compressing” exponentially many input instances into a polynomial-length advice string such that each instance can be solved
easily
given the advice. Moreover, it is easy to see that the unary nature of
USSR
does not play an important role here. In other words, given any set of
k

fixed
positive integers the corresponding
2^{k}
instances of
SSR
can be similarly compressed into a polynomial length advice string from which extracting the solution to an instance is easy. In fact, the compression is possible for the signed sum of any
k

fixed
real numbers.

A notable point about the numerical approximation proof is that the number of basis elements
b_{i}
is only
m+1
while by the root separation bound their exponents
e_{i}
are distributed over an exponential range in
m
. Thus if the
e_{i}
’s are “well-distributed” i.e. consecutive
e_{i}
’s differ by
\Omega(m^{2})
then we just need to consult the value of
one
basis element to decide on an instance.
Prior to our work, there was no difference in the computational complexity between
USSR
and
SSR
. Our work raises a few natural questions:

1.

Is there a polynomial time algorithm for
USSR
? Is there a way to use both the proofs in tandem to
verify
the advice? This would give an
NP
algorithm for
USSR
. Even a
PH
algorithm will be a significant improvement to the current state of affairs.

2.

Consider the proof via LTFs: Is it possible to estimate the number of vertices in the polytope of the LTF obtained from the
UUSSR
instance? Can we characterize when the vertices will be determined by
easy

UUSSR
instances?

3.

Is it possible to eliminate either the values
\{v_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m}
or the basis
\{{\bf b_{i}}\}_{i=0}^{m}
from the advice string in the numerical approximation proof?

4.

Is
SSR

\in{\bf P}/{\bf poly}
?

5.

Can reasoning similar to Remark
9
be used to give better bounds on distribution on roots of a univariate polynomial, or give improved root separation bounds?

Appendix A
Muroga’s Proof

Let
\mathbb{B}
be any bounded discrete domain.

Theorem 13
.

Let
b
be the maximum magnitude of elements in
\mathbb{B}
. For any LTF
f:\mathbb{B}^{m}\to\{\pm 1\}
realized using weights
w_{0},w_{1},\dots,w_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists an equivalent integer realization with weights
u_{0},u_{1}\dots,u_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
1\leq|u_{0}|,\dots,|u_{m}|\leq{\cal O}((m+1)!b^{m})
.

Proof.

Given
w_{1},\dots,w_{n},\theta
, let
X
be that subset of
\mathbb{B}^{n}
for which
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}x_{i}\geq 1
. Now consider the linear program with variables
z_{1},\dots,z_{n}
and the following
|\mathbb{B}|^{n}
constraints

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}x_{i}

\displaystyle\geq 1\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{ for }(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}x_{i}

\displaystyle\leq-1\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{ for }(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\notin X

Clearly, this is a feasible LP because
z_{i}=w_{i}/\theta
is a feasible solution. Therefore it should also have a solution that is a vertex, i.e., a solution with
n
tight LP constraints of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}x_{i}=\pm 1
. We can solve such a system of linear equations
X{\bf z}={\bf b}
where
X\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n\times n}
and
{\bf b}\in\{\pm 1\}^{n}
. By Cramer’s rule, this should give a solution which is the ratio of two determinants. An upper bound on the determinant (and hence any minor) of
X
is
{\cal O}(n!b^{n})
. This gives a rational solution for
z_{i}
. By clearing the denominators, we get the claimed integer solution. It is clear that such a solution will satisfy all the constraints since it is a vertex of the polytope.
∎

Analytical solutions of virus propagation model in blockchain networks
Youness Chatibi

Abstract
The main goal of this paper is to find analytical solutions of a system of nonlinear ordinary differential equations arising in the virus propagation in blockchain networks. The presented method reduces the problem to an Abel differential equation of the first kind and solve it directly.

3
Analytical resolution of the model

Theorem 1

The system (
1
) can be reduced to an ordinary differential equation (called Liénard equation) determined by

\frac{d^{2}x_{1}}{dt^{2}}+A\frac{dx_{1}}{dt}+B(x_{1})=0,

(2)

where
A
and
B
are polynomial functions.

Proof 1

In this model, the population is constant (i.e.)
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}=N
. Then
\frac{dx_{1}}{dt}+\frac{dx_{2}}{dt}+\frac{dx_{3}}{dt}=0
, therefore, for all
t\geq 0
the resolution of this system is based on the determination of the function
x_{1}=x_{1}(t)
because after that

\displaystyle x_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{b_{2}}\bigg(\dfrac{dx_{1}}{dt}+d_{1}x_{1}\bigg),

(3)

\displaystyle x_{3}(t)

\displaystyle=N-x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t).

(4)

Substitution of (
4
) into (
1
) gives us the following ordinary differential equations:

\displaystyle\frac{dx_{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=(b_{1}-k_{2}N)x_{1}+k_{2}x_{1}^{2}-d_{2}x_{2}+k_{2}x_{1}x_{2},

(5)

\displaystyle\dfrac{dx_{3}}{dt}

\displaystyle=d_{3}(x_{1}+x_{2})+k_{1}x_{1}x_{2}-d_{3}N.

(6)

Therefore,

\displaystyle k_{2}\dfrac{dx_{1}}{dt}+(k_{1}+k_{2})\dfrac{dx_{2}}{dt}

\displaystyle=k_{1}\dfrac{dx_{2}}{dt}-k_{2}\dfrac{dx_{3}}{dt}

\displaystyle=[(b_{1}-k_{2}N)k_{1}-d_{3}k_{2}]x_{1}-(d_{2}k_{1}+d_{3}k_{2})x_{2}+k_{1}k_{2}x_{1}^{2}+d_{3}k_{2}N.

By differentiating Eq. (
3
) and use it, we get

a\frac{d^{2}x_{1}}{dt^{2}}+b\frac{dx_{1}}{dt}+cx_{1}^{2}+dx_{1}+e=0,

(7)

where

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\dfrac{k_{1}+k_{2}}{b_{2}},\quad b=k_{2}+\dfrac{k_{1}(d_{1}+d_{2})+k_{2}(d_{1}+d_{3})}{b_{2}},\quad c=-k_{1}k_{2},

\displaystyle d

\displaystyle=-b_{1}k_{1}+k_{2}(d_{3}+k_{1}N)+\dfrac{d_{1}(d_{2}k_{1}+d_{3}k_{2})}{b_{2}},\quad\text{and}\quad e=-d_{3}k_{2}N.

If we denote
A=\frac{b}{a}
and
B(x_{1})=\frac{cx_{1}^{2}+dx_{1}+e}{a}
, we obtain the Liénard ordinary differential equation
[
37
]

\frac{d^{2}x_{1}}{dt^{2}}+A\frac{dx_{1}}{dt}+B(x_{1})=0.

(8)

Lemma 1

The solutions of the Liénard equation (
2
) can be obtained by transforming it to an equivalent first kind first order Abel type equation given by

\frac{dv}{dx_{1}}=Av^{2}+B(x_{1})v^{3}.

(9)

Proof 2

By denoting
u=\frac{dx_{1}}{dt}
, Eq. (
2
) can be expressed as

u\frac{du}{dx_{1}}+Au+B(x_{1})=0.

(10)

By introducing a new dependent variable
v=\frac{1}{u}
, Eq. (
10
) takes the form of an Abel differential equation of the first kind (
9
).

Theorem 2

The solution of the Abel equation (
9
) is given by

v=\bigg(\dfrac{dx_{1}}{dt}\bigg)^{-1}=-(Ax_{1}+C)^{-1}\pm\big[Dx_{1}^{3}+Ex_{1}^{2}+Fx_{1}+G\big]^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(11)

where
C,D,E,F
and
G
are constants.

Proof 3

For resolving Eq. (
9
), we put
v=v_{1}+v_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\dfrac{dv_{1}}{dx_{1}}

\displaystyle=Av_{1}^{2},

(12)

\displaystyle\dfrac{dv_{2}}{dx_{1}}

\displaystyle=B(x_{1})v_{2}^{3}.

(13)

For Eq. (
12
), we have the solution

v_{1}=-(Ax_{1}+C)^{-1}\quad\text{where}\quad C\quad\text{is constant.}

(14)

Otherwise, for (
13
), we put
v_{2}=P(x_{1})^{r}
where
P\in\mathbb{R}[x_{1}]^{*}
and
r\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}
. We have

\frac{dv_{2}}{dx_{1}}=rP^{\prime}(x_{1})[v_{2}^{3}]^{s}

where
s=\frac{1}{3}\big(1-\frac{1}{r}\big)
. For
s=1
and
rP^{\prime}(x_{1})=B(x_{1})
, we get the solution

v_{2}=\big[-2\int B(x_{1})dx_{1}+C^{\prime}\big]^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\big[Dx_{1}^{3}+Ex_{1}^{2}+Fx_{1}+G\big]^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(15)

where
D=\frac{-2c}{3a},E=\frac{-d}{a},F=\frac{-2e}{a}
and
G=-2C^{\prime\prime}+C^{\prime}
(
C^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime\prime}
are integration’s constants).

Note that
\tilde{v}_{2}=-v_{2}
is also a solution. Then Eq. (
9
) admits two solutions that are

v=\bigg(\dfrac{dx_{1}}{dt}\bigg)^{-1}=-(Ax_{1}+C)^{-1}\pm\big[Dx_{1}^{3}+Ex_{1}^{2}+Fx_{1}+G\big]^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(16)

Theorem 3

The general solutions
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
of the system (
1
) is given by

x_{1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{n}^{\pm}t^{n},\quad x_{2}(t)=\dfrac{1}{b_{2}}\bigg(\dfrac{dx_{1}}{dt}+d_{1}x_{1}\bigg),\quad x_{3}(t)=N-x_{1}(t)-x_{2}(t),

(17)

where the coefficients
\rho_{n}^{\pm}
are defined by

\displaystyle\rho_{n}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{n(\sigma_{1}^{\pm})^{n}}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},\cdots}(-1)^{s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+\cdots}

(18)

\displaystyle\quad\times\dfrac{n(n+1)\cdots(n-1+s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots)}{s_{1}!s_{2}!s_{3}!\cdots}\bigg(\dfrac{\sigma_{2}^{\pm}}{\sigma_{1}^{\pm}}\bigg)^{s_{1}}\bigg(\dfrac{\sigma_{3}^{\pm}}{\sigma_{1}^{\pm}}\bigg)^{s_{2}}\cdots,

(19)

and the sum over
s
values is restricted to partitions of
n-1
,

s_{1}+2s_{2}+3s_{3}+\cdots=n-1.

Proof 4

By integrating (
9
) we obtain

t=-\frac{1}{A}\log{(Ax_{1}+C)}\pm\int\big[Dx_{1}^{3}+Ex_{1}^{2}+Fx_{1}+G\big]^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(20)

Now, letting the equation

P(x_{1})=0\quad\text{where}\quad P(x_{1})=Dx_{1}^{3}+Ex_{1}^{2}+Fx_{1}+G.

(21)

Note that, if we apply the Tschirnhaus method
[
38
]
by putting
y_{1}=x_{1}+\frac{E}{3D}
,

Eq. (
21
) become

\tilde{P}(y_{1})=0\quad\text{where}\quad\tilde{P}(y_{1})=y_{1}^{3}+Hy_{1}+I,

(22)

where
H=\frac{3DF-E^{2}}{3D^{2}}
and
I=\frac{2E^{3}-9DEF+27D^{2}G}{27D^{3}}
.

Eq. (
22
), can be solve by Cardano’s method
[
39
]
which one of its solutions take the form

y_{1}^{(1)}=\bigg(\dfrac{-I-\sqrt{\Delta_{1}}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}}+\bigg(\dfrac{-I+\sqrt{\Delta_{1}}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{3}},

(23)

where
\Delta_{1}=I^{2}+\dfrac{4H^{3}}{27}\geq 0
. As
y_{1}-y_{1}^{(1)}
divide
\tilde{P}(y_{1})
we have

\tilde{P}(y_{1})=(y_{1}-y_{1}^{(1)})Q(y_{1}),

(24)

where

Q(y_{1})=y_{1}^{2}+(y_{1}+y_{1}^{(1)})y_{1}^{(1)}+H.

(25)

Consequently, the two remaining roots of the equation
Q(y_{1})=0
are

y_{1}^{(2)}=\dfrac{-y_{1}^{(1)}-\sqrt{\Delta_{2}}}{2},\qquad y_{1}^{(3)}=\dfrac{-y_{1}^{(1)}+\sqrt{\Delta_{2}}}{2},

(26)

where
\Delta_{2}=y_{1}^{(1)2}-4(H+y_{1}^{(1)2})\geq 0
. Whence,

\tilde{P}(y_{1})=(y_{1}-y_{1}^{(1)})(y_{1}-y_{1}^{(2)})(x_{1}-y_{1}^{(3)}).

(27)

As
P(x_{1})=D\cdot\tilde{P}(x_{1}+\frac{E}{3D})
where
D=\frac{2b_{2}k_{1}k_{2}}{3(k_{1}+k_{2})}>0
, we have

\int v_{2}dx_{1}=\int\dfrac{dx_{1}}{\sqrt{D(x_{1}+\theta_{1})(x_{1}+\theta_{2})(x_{1}+\theta_{3})}},

(28)

with
\theta_{k}=\frac{E}{3D}-y_{1}^{(k)}\neq 0
for
k=1,2,3
.

By applying

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x+\theta}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\theta}}\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{p}\bigg(\dfrac{x}{\theta}\bigg)^{p},

(29)

where

\binom{x}{m}=\dfrac{1}{m!}\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}(x-i)=\dfrac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(m+1)\cdot\Gamma(x-m+1)},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}

(30)

is the generalized binomial coefficient, we get

\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{D(x_{1}+\theta_{1})(x_{1}+\theta_{2})(x_{1}+\theta_{3})}}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu_{n}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})x_{1}^{n},

(31)

where

\mu_{n}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})=\displaystyle\sum_{p+q+r=n}\dfrac{\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{p}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{q}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{r}}{\theta_{1}^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\theta_{2}^{q+\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\theta_{3}^{r+\frac{1}{2}}}.

(32)

Consequently, the integration implies that

\int v_{2}dx_{1}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mu_{n}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})}{n+1}x_{1}^{n+1}.

(33)

Otherwise, since
\dfrac{1}{1+x}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-x)^{n}
for
|x|<1
and by integration we achieve:

-\dfrac{1}{A}\log{(Ax_{1}+C)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}(A,C)x_{1}^{n+1},

(34)

where

\lambda_{n}(A,C)=\dfrac{(-1)^{n+1}}{(n+1)C^{n+1}}A^{n}.

(35)

Then, Eq. (
20
) become

t=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n-1}(A,C)x_{1}^{n}\pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{\mu_{n-1}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})}{n}x_{1}^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{n}^{\pm}x_{1}^{n},

(36)

where

\sigma_{n}^{\pm}=\lambda_{n-1}(A,C)\pm\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{D}}\dfrac{\mu_{n-1}(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})}{n}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 1.

(37)

Since, the series expansion of the inverse series is given by

x_{1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_{n}^{\pm}t^{n},

(38)

where the coefficients
\rho_{n}^{\pm}
are defined by (for details see
[
40
]
)

\displaystyle\rho_{n}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{n(\sigma_{1}^{\pm})^{n}}\sum_{s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},\cdots}(-1)^{s_{1}+s_{2}+s_{3}+\cdots}

(39)

\displaystyle\quad\times\dfrac{n(n+1)\cdots(n-1+s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots)}{s_{1}!s_{2}!s_{3}!\cdots}\bigg(\dfrac{\sigma_{2}^{\pm}}{\sigma_{1}^{\pm}}\bigg)^{s_{1}}\bigg(\dfrac{\sigma_{3}^{\pm}}{\sigma_{1}^{\pm}}\bigg)^{s_{2}}\cdots,

(40)

and the sum over
s
values is restricted to partitions of
n-1
,

s_{1}+2s_{2}+3s_{3}+\cdots=n-1.

We note that if
0<\theta_{1}\theta_{2}\theta_{3}\neq\frac{C^{2}}{D}
,
\sigma_{1}^{\pm}\neq 0
. The first few
\rho_{n}^{\pm}
given by (
19
) are

\displaystyle\rho_{1}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{\sigma_{1}^{\pm}},

\displaystyle\rho_{2}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{(\sigma_{1}^{\pm})^{3}}\sigma_{2}^{\pm},

\displaystyle\rho_{3}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{(\sigma_{1}^{\pm})^{5}}(2(\sigma_{2}^{\pm})^{2}-\sigma_{1}^{\pm}\sigma_{3}^{\pm}).

Finally, we use (
38
), Eq. (
3
) and Eq. (
4
) to get
x_{2}
and
x_{3}
.

Uniqueness of solutions to a class of isotropic curvature problems
Mohammad N. Ivaki, Emanuel Milman

Abstract
. Employing a local version of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality, we give a new and simple proof of a result due to Andrews, Choi and Daskalopoulos that the origin-centred balls are the only closed, self-similar solutions of the Gauss curvature flow. Extensions to various non-linearities are obtained, assuming the centroid of the enclosed convex body is at the origin. By applying our method to the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality, we also show that origin-centred balls are the only solutions to a large class of even Christoffel-Minkowski type problems.

1.
Introduction

The Gauss curvature flow in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
was proposed by Firey
[
Fir74
]
as a model for the changing shape of smooth, strictly convex stones as they tumble on a beach in an idealized situation. Assuming that the solutions exist and are regular, he showed centrally-symmetric stones become round. Firey conjectured that the resulting shapes would be rounded stones even without the symmetry assumption. The existence and regularity of solutions and convergence to a point were settled later by Chou
[
Tso85
]
, and in
[
And99
]
Andrews succeeded in proving Firey’s conjecture. One of the key ingredients in Andrews’ proof was showing that the difference of the principal curvatures decreases along the flow. The question of whether the asymptotic shape in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
n\geq 3
, is a sphere remained open until Guan and Ni
[
GN17
]
showed that the normalized solution converges to a self-similar solution (i.e. a smooth, closed hypersurface whose support function is positive and equal to its Gauss curvature), and Choi and Daskalopoulos
[
CD16
]
proved that these self-similar solutions are, in fact, round. Their argument relied on applying the maximum principle to a peculiar combination of principal curvatures and the position vector. In
[
Sar22
]
, Saroglou announced a second approach based on the Steiner symmetrization and extended the uniqueness results of
[
Cho85
,
And99
,
AC12
,
CD16
,
BCD17
]
to a non-homogeneous case. See also
[
McC18
]
(the last remark), and
[
ACGL20
]
*Chap. 15-17 for a more detailed account.

Our first contribution is a new proof of the following theorem; the uniqueness of closed, self-similar solutions of the Gauss curvature flow (for curves the theorem was proved by Gage
[
Gag84
]
; see also
[
And03
]
). Throughout the paper, all hypersurfaces
\mathcal{M}^{n}
are assumed to be closed smooth hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
bounding a convex compact set
K
with strictly positive Gauss curvature
\mathcal{K}
and having the origin in its interior. We denote the support function of
K
by
h
.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
Gag84
,
And99
,
CD16
]

Let
\mathcal{M}^{n}
be a smooth, strictly convex hypersurface. If
\mathcal{K}=h
, then
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is the origin-centred unit sphere.

We use a local version of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality applied to the position vector of
\mathcal{M}^{n}
to provide a surprisingly short proof of this theorem that differs from all the previous approaches discussed above. Our approach also yields (see Theorem
4.1
) the uniqueness of solutions to
\mathcal{K}^{\alpha}=h
when
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{n+2},\frac{1}{2}]
(see also
[
BCD17
]
for a different argument). In particular, we give a new proof of the following classical theorem due to Jörgens, Calabi, and Pogorelov
[
Jör54
,
Cal58
,
Pog72
,
CY86
]
. See also
[
ACGL20
]
*Sec. 16.4 for another proof based on the Steiner symmetrization.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{n}
be a smooth, strictly convex hypersurface. If
\mathcal{K}=h^{n+2}
, then
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is an ellipsoid centred at the origin.

We shall say that
\mathcal{M}^{n}=\partial K
is origin-centred if the centroid of
K
is at the origin; in particular, an origin-symmetric
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is origin-centred. The following theorem, when
\varphi
is
only
a function of the support function (i.e.
\partial_{2}\varphi\equiv 0
)
but
without the origin-centred assumption, was proved in
[
Sar22
]
. Otherwise, in this general form, it seems to be new.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
\varphi:(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is
C^{1}
-smooth with
\partial_{1}\varphi\geq 0,\partial_{2}\varphi\geq 0
, and at least one of these inequalities is strict. If
\mathcal{M}^{n}
be a smooth, strictly convex, origin-centred hypersurface with
\varphi(h,|Dh|)\mathcal{K}=h^{n+2}
, then
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is an origin-centred sphere.

An immediate corollary of
Theorem 1.3
is the following uniqueness which confirms a conjecture in
[
CHLZ23
]
on the isotropic Gaussian Minkowski problem in the class of origin-centred convex bodies.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is a smooth, strictly convex, origin-centred hypersurface such that
ce^{\frac{1}{2}|Dh|^{2}}\mathcal{K}=1
for some
c>0
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is an origin-centred sphere.

There are several known results about the uniqueness of solutions to the isotropic
L_{p,q}
Minkowski problem
h^{p-1}|Dh|^{n+1-q}\mathcal{K}=c
with
c>0
:

•

[
HZ18
]
, uniqueness of solutions for
p>q
;

•

[
CHZ19
]
, uniqueness of origin-symmetric solutions for

-(n+1)\leq p<q\leq\min\{n+1,n+1+p\};

•

[
CL21
]
, uniqueness of solutions for
1<p<q\leq n+1
, or
-(n+1)\leq p<q<-1
, or
p=q
(up-to rescaling);

•

[
LW22
]
, complete classification for
n=1
.

Here, as another corollary of
Theorem 1.3
, we state the following uniqueness result.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
n\geq 2
and assume that
-(n+1)\leq p
and
q\leq n+1
, with at least one of these being strict.
Suppose
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is a smooth, strictly convex, origin-centred hypersurface such that
h^{p-1}|Dh|^{n+1-q}\mathcal{K}=c
with
c>0
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is an origin-centred sphere.

Let
\sigma_{k}
denote the
k
th elementary symmetric function of principal radii of curvature. It is known that the only smooth, strictly convex solution to the isotropic
L^{p}
-Christoffel-Minkowski problem
h^{1-p}\sigma_{k}=1
,
1-k\leq p<1,\,1\leq k<n
, is the unit sphere; see
[
Che20
]
for the case
1-k<p<1
and
[
McC11
]
for
p=1-k
. Here, applying our method to a local form of the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality, we extend these previous results to the following general formulation.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
k<n
. Suppose
\varphi:(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is a
C^{1}
-smooth function with
k-1+x\partial_{1}(\log\varphi)(x,y)\geq 0,\,\partial_{2}\varphi\geq 0
. Let
\mathcal{M}^{n}
be a smooth, strictly convex, origin-symmetric hypersurface such that
h\sigma_{k}=\varphi(h,|Dh|)
. Then
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is an origin-centred sphere.

2.
Background

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\delta:=\langle\,,\rangle,D)
be the Euclidean space with its standard inner product and flat connection.

(\mathbb{S}^{n},\bar{g},\bar{\nabla})
denotes the unit sphere equipped with its standard round metric and Levi-Civita connection. Moreover,
\mu
is the spherical Lebesgue measure of the unit sphere.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a smooth, strictly convex body with the origin in its interior. We write
\mathcal{M}:=\partial K
for the boundary of
K
. The Gauss map of
K
,
\nu:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{S}^{n}
, takes
p\in\mathcal{M}
to its unique outer unit normal vector.
The support function and Gauss curvature of
\mathcal{M}
are defined as

\displaystyle h(x)=\langle\nu^{-1}(x),x\rangle,\quad\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}(x)}=\frac{\det(\bar{\nabla}^{2}h+\bar{g}h)}{\det(\bar{g})}\Big|_{x},\quad\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

The inverse Gauss map
X=\nu^{-1}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
is given by

X(x):=Dh(x)=h(x)x+\bar{\nabla}h(x)\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Note that
A[h]:=\bar{\nabla}^{2}h+\bar{g}h=D^{2}h|_{T\mathbb{S}^{n}}
is positive-definite.
We set

\sigma_{n}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}},\quad dV=h\sigma_{n}d\mu.

The measure
\sigma_{n}d\mu
is the surface-area measure of
K
, obtained as the push-forward of
\mathcal{H}^{n}|_{\partial K}
via the Gauss map; the measure
\frac{1}{n+1}V
is the cone-volume measure of
K
, whose mass is equal to the volume of
K
. We refer to
[
Sch14
,
KM22
]
,
[
ACGL20
]
*Sec. 18.7 for additional background.

For real symmetric
n\times n
matrices
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}
, write
Q(M_{1},\ldots,M_{n})
for their mixed discriminant; see
[
Sch14
]
*(2.64),(5.117). Let
P_{n}
be the group of all permutations of
\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\varepsilon:P_{n}\to\{-1,1\}
be defined by
\varepsilon(\sigma)=1

(-1)
if
\sigma
is even (odd). The mixed discriminant of
f_{k}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n}),1\leq k\leq n,
is a multilinear operator defined as

\displaystyle Q\left(A[f_{1}],\ldots,A[f_{n}]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{a,b\in P_{n}}\varepsilon(a)\varepsilon(b)\prod_{k=1}^{n}(A[f_{k}])_{a(k)b(k)},

where in a local orthonormal frame of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
the entries of the matrix
A[f_{k}]
are given by
(A[f_{k}])_{ij}=\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{i,j}f_{k}+\bar{g}_{ij}f_{k}
. We define the mixed volume of
f_{k}\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n}),1\leq k\leq n+1,
by

\displaystyle V(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n+1})=\frac{1}{n+1}\int f_{1}Q\left(A[f_{2}],\ldots,A[f_{n+1}]\right)d\mu.

It is known that
V
is invariant under the permutation of its arguments.
We also set

\displaystyle V_{k+1}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k+1})=V(f_{1},\ldots,f_{k+1},1,\ldots,1),

where
1
appears
(n-k)
-times on the right-hand side.
Due to
[
Sch14
]
*Thm. 7.6.8 (cf.
[
An97
]
*Lem. 8), for all
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
we have the following local version of (a particular case of) the Alexandrov–Fenchel inequality:

(2.1)

\displaystyle V_{k+1}(fh,h,\ldots,h)^{2}\geq V_{k+1}(fh,fh,h,\ldots,h)V_{k+1}(h,\ldots,h).

Equality holds if and only if for some vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
we have

f(x)=\langle\frac{x}{h(x)},v\rangle+c\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Let us put
\tau:=A[h]
and write
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
for its eigenvalues. Define

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}=\sigma_{k}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\lambda_{i_{2}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}},\quad\sigma_{k}^{ij}=\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\tau_{ij}},\quad 1\leq k\leq n.

Note that
\sigma_{k}^{ij}\tau_{ij}=k\sigma_{k}
by Euler’s identity, and that
\sigma_{n+1}=0
and
\sigma_{1}=\bar{\Delta}h+nh
. We also introduce the measures

dV_{k}:=h\sigma_{k}d\mu,

so that
V=V_{n}
. It is known that

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{k}V_{k+1}(fh,h,\ldots,h)

\displaystyle=\int fh\sigma_{k}d\mu=\int fdV_{k},

\displaystyle c_{k}V_{k+1}(fh,fh,h,\ldots,h)

\displaystyle=\int fh\sigma_{k}^{ij}(A[fh])_{ij}d\mu.

3.
Spectral estimate

The following is a spectral interpretation of (
2.1
); the case
k=n
is the spectral formulation of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality originating in Hilbert’s work and studied e.g. in
[
KM22
,
Mil21
]
.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
An97
]
*Lem. 8,
[
ACGL20
]
*Prop. 18.35)
.

Let
f\in C^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
with
\int fh\sigma_{k}d\mu=0
. Then we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle k\int f^{2}h\sigma_{k}d\mu\leq\int h^{2}\sigma_{k}^{ij}\partial_{i}f\partial_{j}fd\mu.

Equality holds if and only if for some vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
we have

f(x)=\langle\frac{x}{h(x)},v\rangle\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Proof.

Due to
\sigma_{k}^{ij}\tau_{ij}=k\sigma_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle fh\sigma_{k}^{ij}(\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{i,j}(fh)+\bar{g}_{ij}fh)=kf^{2}h\sigma_{k}+fh^{2}\sigma_{k}^{ij}\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{i,j}f+2fh\sigma_{k}^{ij}\partial_{i}f\partial_{j}h.

Hence, using
\bar{\nabla}_{i}\sigma_{k}^{ij}=0
(see
[
ACGL20
]
*Lem. 18.30) and integrating by parts we obtain

\displaystyle c_{k}V_{k+1}(fh,fh,h,\ldots,h)

\displaystyle=\int fh\sigma_{k}^{ij}(\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{i,j}(fh)+\bar{g}_{ij}fh)d\mu

\displaystyle=k\int f^{2}h\sigma_{k}d\mu-\int h^{2}\sigma_{k}^{ij}\partial_{i}f\partial_{j}fd\mu.

Since
V_{k+1}(fh,h,\ldots,h)=0
and
V_{k+1}(h,\ldots,h)>0
, the claim follows from (
2.1
), its equality cases, and the fact that
\int x\sigma_{k}d\mu=0
; see
[
Sch14
]
*(5.30).
∎

When
K
is an ellipsoid centred at the origin, for some symmetric, positive definite matrix
M
, we have

\langle Dh(x),v\rangle=\langle\frac{x}{h(x)},Mv\rangle\quad\forall v\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Moreover,
\int Dh\;dV=0,
and
hence, for
f=\langle Dh,v\rangle
we have equality in (
3.1
). In the next lemma, which is the main new ingredient in this work, we derive an inequality from (
3.1
) by substituting such functions for
f
.

Lemma 3.2

(Main Lemma)
.

Let
X=Dh:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\partial K
. Then we have

\displaystyle k\int|X|^{2}dV_{k}\leq\int h\left(\sigma_{1}-(k+1)\frac{\sigma_{k+1}}{\sigma_{k}}\right)dV_{k}+k\frac{|\int XdV_{k}|^{2}}{\int dV_{k}}.

In particular, for
k=n
we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle n\int|X|^{2}dV\leq\int h(\bar{\Delta}h+nh)dV+n\frac{\left|\int XdV\right|^{2}}{\int dV}.

Equivalently, for
k=n
there holds

(3.3)

\displaystyle\int\langle hX,\bar{\nabla}\log\frac{h^{n+2}}{\mathcal{K}}\rangle dV=\int(n|\bar{\nabla}h|_{\bar{g}}^{2}-h\bar{\Delta}h)dV\leq n\frac{|\int XdV|^{2}}{\int dV}.

Proof.

Let
\{E_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{n+1}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Suppose
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is a local orthonormal frame for
\mathbb{S}^{n}
that diagonalizes
\tau
, say at
x_{0}
, and
\tau(e_{i},e_{i})|_{x_{0}}=\lambda_{i}(x_{0})
. For
\ell=1,\ldots,n+1
, define the functions

\displaystyle f_{\ell}:\mathbb{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R},\quad f_{\ell}=\langle X,E_{\ell}\rangle-\frac{\int\langle X,E_{\ell}\rangle dV_{k}}{\int dV_{k}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\int f_{\ell}dV_{k}=0,\quad\ell=1,\ldots,n+1.

Since
e_{i}X=\tau(e_{i},e_{j})e_{j}
(cf.
[
CY76
]
*(4.15)) and hence
\partial_{i}f_{\ell}=\lambda_{i}\langle e_{i},E_{\ell}\rangle
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}^{ij}\partial_{i}f_{\ell}\partial_{j}f_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{i}^{2}\langle e_{i},E_{\ell}\rangle^{2},

and therefore (cf.
[
HS99
]
*Prop. 2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\sigma_{k}^{ij}\partial_{i}f_{\ell}\partial_{j}f_{\ell}=\frac{\partial\sigma_{k}}{\partial\lambda_{i}}\lambda_{i}^{2}=\sigma_{1}\sigma_{k}-(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}.

In addition,

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\int f_{\ell}^{2}dV_{k}=\int|X|^{2}dV_{k}-\frac{|\int XdV_{k}|^{2}}{\int dV_{k}}.

Applying
3.1
to
f_{\ell}
and summing over
\ell
we obtain the first inequality, and as a particular case when
k=n
the second inequality. Statement (
3.3
) follows from (
3.2
) after recalling that
|X|^{2}=h^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}_{\bar{g}}
,
dV=h\sigma_{n}d\mu
and integrating by parts.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

By the divergence theorem, for any vector
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,

\displaystyle\int\langle X,w\rangle dV

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial K}\langle p,w\rangle\langle p,\nu(p)\rangle\mathcal{H}^{n}(dp)

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\operatorname{div}_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}}(\langle x,w\rangle x)dx=(n+2)\int_{K}\langle x,w\rangle dx.

Therefore, the vector
\int XdV
appearing in (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) is a multiple of the centroid of
K
, and is equal to
0
whenever
\mathcal{M}^{n}=\partial K
is origin-centred.

Remark 3.4
.

Let
K
be a smooth, strictly convex body, and

M
be any
(n+1)\times(n+1)
matrix. By
[
LW13
]
*Prop. 2.1), we have

\displaystyle\int\langle\bar{\nabla}\log\frac{h^{n+2}}{\mathcal{K}},\xi_{M}\rangle dV=0,

where

\xi_{M}(x)=Mx-(x^{T}Mx)x,\quad x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

In particular, for an ellipsoid
E
whose support function is given by
h_{E}(x)=\sqrt{x^{T}Mx}
(with
M
symmetric and positive-definite), we have

\xi_{M}=D\frac{h_{E}^{2}}{2}(x)-h_{E}^{2}(x)x=\frac{1}{2}\bar{\nabla}h_{E}^{2}(x),\quad\int\langle\bar{\nabla}\log\frac{h^{n+2}}{\mathcal{K}},\bar{\nabla}h_{E}^{2}\rangle dV=0.

Compare this with (
3.3
): if
K
has its centroid at the origin, then

\int\langle\bar{\nabla}\log\frac{h^{n+2}}{\mathcal{K}},\bar{\nabla}h^{2}\rangle dV\leq 0.

4.
Proofs of main results

Proof of
Theorem 1.1
.

By
3.2
, the identity
|X|^{2}=h^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}h|_{\bar{g}}^{2}
,
dV=d\mu
, and integration by parts we find

(4.1)

\displaystyle(n+1)\int|\bar{\nabla}h|_{\bar{g}}^{2}d\mu\leq n\frac{\left|\int Xd\mu\right|^{2}}{\int d\mu}.

By
3.3
, if
K
is origin-centred, then the right-hand of (
4.1
) is zero and the proof is completed. In the general case, in order to estimate the right-hand side, note that

\displaystyle\int Xd\mu

\displaystyle=\int\left((h(x)-\frac{\int hd\mu}{\int d\mu})x+\bar{\nabla}h(x)\right)d\mu(x),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left|\int Xd\mu\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int d\mu\int\left((h-\frac{\int hd\mu}{\int d\mu})^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}h|_{\bar{g}}^{2}\right)d\mu.

Inequalities (
4.1
) and (
4
) together yield

\displaystyle\int|\bar{\nabla}h|_{\bar{g}}^{2}d\mu\leq n\int(h-\frac{\int hd\mu}{\int d\mu})^{2}d\mu.

On the other hand, by the (sharp) Poincaré inequality on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle n\int(h-\frac{\int hd\mu}{\int d\mu})^{2}d\mu\leq\int|\bar{\nabla}h|_{\bar{g}}^{2}d\mu.

So (
4.3
) is an equality, and hence
h=c+\langle x,v\rangle
and
\mathcal{M}^{n}
must be a sphere. Since
\mathcal{K}=h
,
\mathcal{M}^{n}
must be the origin-centred unit sphere.
∎

The initial part of the above argument immediately extends to yield a simple proof of the following theorem regarding the uniqueness in the isotropic
L^{p}
-Minkowski problem, originally established in
[
BCD17
]
(where the range
p\in(-1,1)
was also treated via a separate argument):

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{n}
be a smooth, strictly convex hypersurface. If
\mathcal{K}=h^{1-p}
with
p>-(n+1)
, and either
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is origin-centred or
p\leq-1
, then
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is an origin-centred ball.

Proof.

When
dV=h^{p}d\mu
,
3.2
and integration by parts yield

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{n+1+p}{n}\int|\bar{\nabla}h|_{\bar{g}}^{2}dV\leq\frac{\left|\int XdV\right|^{2}}{\int dV}.

By
3.3
, we conclude
h
is constant when
p>-(n+1)
and
\mathcal{M}^{n}
is origin-centred. In the general case, by the divergence theorem,

(4.5)

\displaystyle\int XdV=\frac{n+1+p}{n}\int h^{p}\bar{\nabla}hd\mu.

Plugging (
4.5
) into (
4.4
), we deduce when
p>-(n+1)
:

\int\left|\bar{\nabla}h-\frac{\int\bar{\nabla}hdV}{\int dV}\right|_{\delta}^{2}dV\leq\frac{p+1}{n}\frac{|\int\bar{\nabla}hdV|_{\delta}^{2}}{\int dV}.

Consequently, when in addition
p\leq-1
, we conclude that
h
is constant.
∎

Proof of
Theorem 1.2
.

In the case
p=-(n+1)
, due to (
4.5
), we have
\int XdV=0
. Thus we have, in fact, equality in (
3.2
):

\displaystyle n\int|X|^{2}h^{-(n+1)}d\mu=\int(\bar{\Delta}h+nh)h^{-n}d\mu.

By the characterization of the equality cases of (
3.1
), we deduce that for every
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, there exists a vector
w\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, such that

\langle X(x),v\rangle=\langle\frac{x}{h(x)},w\rangle\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Therefore, for some matrix
M
, we have

\langle Dh(x),v\rangle=\langle\frac{x}{h(x)},Mv\rangle\quad\forall x,v\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

That is,

Dh^{2}(x)=2M^{T}x.

Thus
h^{2}
is a quadratic function, and
K
is an origin-centred ellipsoid. The proof of
Theorem 1.2
is completed.
∎

Proof of
Theorem 1.3
.

Since
\int XdV=0
, from (
3.3
) it follows that

\displaystyle\int\langle X,\bar{\nabla}\frac{h^{n+2}}{\mathcal{K}}\rangle h^{-n}d\mu=\int\langle hX,\bar{\nabla}\log\frac{h^{n+2}}{\mathcal{K}}\rangle dV\leq 0.

Note that

|X|\langle\bar{\nabla}|X|,\bar{\nabla}h\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle D|Dh|^{2},\bar{\nabla}h\rangle=\tau(\bar{\nabla}h,\bar{\nabla}h)\geq c|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2},

where
c>0
depends on
\mathcal{M}^{n}
. Due to
h^{n+2}=\varphi(h,|X|)\mathcal{K}
,
\partial_{1}\varphi\geq 0
and
\partial_{2}\varphi\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle\langle X,\bar{\nabla}\frac{h^{n+2}}{\mathcal{K}}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}\partial_{1}\varphi+\langle\bar{\nabla}|X|,\bar{\nabla}h\rangle\partial_{2}\varphi\geq c^{\prime}|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2},

Proof of
Theorem 1.6
.

In view of
3.2
, and
\int XdV_{k}=0,

\displaystyle k\int|X|^{2}\varphi d\mu\leq\int(h\varphi\sigma_{1}-(k+1)h^{2}\sigma_{k+1})d\mu.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle(k+1)\sigma_{k+1}=\sigma_{k+1}^{ij}\tau_{ij}=\sigma_{k+1}^{ij}(\bar{\nabla}^{2}_{i,j}h+\bar{g}_{ij}h).

Using
\bar{\nabla}_{i}\sigma_{k+1}^{ij}=0
,
\partial_{2}\varphi\geq 0
, and integrating by parts we find

\displaystyle k\int(h^{2}+|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}_{\bar{g}})\varphi d\mu\leq

\displaystyle\int(nh^{2}\varphi-(\varphi+h\partial_{1}\varphi)|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}_{\bar{g}})d\mu

\displaystyle-\int h^{3}\sigma_{k+1}^{ij}\bar{g}_{ij}d\mu+2\int h\sigma_{k+1}^{ij}\partial_{i}h\partial_{j}hd\mu.

Choose a local orthonormal frame for
\mathbb{S}^{n}
that diagonalizes
\tau
at
x_{0}
, so that
\tau(e_{i},e_{i})|_{x_{0}}=\lambda_{i}(x_{0})
.
Now due to the identities (cf.
[
HS99
]
*Prop. 2.2)

\displaystyle\forall i\;\;\sigma_{k+1}^{ii}=\sigma_{k}-\lambda_{i}\sigma_{k}^{ii},\quad\sigma_{k+1}^{ij}\bar{g}_{ij}=(n-k)\sigma_{k}=(n-k)\frac{\varphi}{h},

we obtain

\displaystyle\int(k-1+h\partial_{1}(\log\varphi))\varphi|\bar{\nabla}h|^{2}_{\bar{g}}d\mu+2\int\varphi\lambda_{i}\frac{\sigma_{k}^{ii}}{\sigma_{k}}(\partial_{i}h)^{2}d\mu\leq 0.

Stable finiteness of endomorphism rings
Simone Virili

Abstract
. We combine a combinatorial idea of Benjamin Weiss and some localization theory of Grothendieck categories to give a short and completely self-contained proof of the following recent result of Hanfeng Li and Bingbing Liang: Given a left Noetherian ring R R and a sofic group G G , the group-ring R ⁡ [ G ] R[G] is stably finite.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 20C07, 18E15; secondary 16D10, 20C99, 18E35.

1.
Introduction

A function between two sets is said to be
surjunctive
if it is non-injective or surjective.
Let now
G
be a group,
A
a finite set and equip the product
A^{G}
with the product of the discrete topologies on each copy of
A
(so that
A^{G}
is a compact space with a continuous
G
-action). A continuous and
G
-equivariant map
\phi\colon A^{G}\to A^{G}
is also called a
cellular automaton
(on the finite alphabet
A
). A long standing open problem by Gottschalk
[
7
]
is that of determining whether or not all cellular automata on finite alphabets are surjunctive; we refer to this problem as the
Surjunctivity Conjecture
. When
G
is amenable this problem has been known for a long time to have a positive solution, but it was just in 1999 when Gromov
[
8
]
came out with a general theorem solving the problem in the positive for the large class of sofic groups (see also
[
14
]
). The general case remains open.

A related (or, actually, dual) problem is that of the stable finiteness of group rings (see
[
3
]
for connections between surjunctivity, “linear surjunctivity” and stable finiteness).
Indeed, a ring
R
is
directly finite
if
xy=1
implies
yx=1
for all
x,y\in R
. Furthermore,
R
is
stably finite
if the ring of square
k\times k
matrices
\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(R)
is directly finite for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
. In other words, a ring
R
is stably finite if, for each positive integer
k
, an endomorphism
\phi\colon R^{k}\to R^{k}
is (split) surjective if and only if it is bijective.
A long standing open problem due to Kaplansky
[
9
]
is to determine whether the group ring
\mathbb{F}[G]
is stably finite for any field
\mathbb{F}
, we refer to this problem as the
Stable Finiteness Conjecture
.
In case the field
\mathbb{F}
has characteristic
0
, then the problem was solved in the positive by Kaplansky. There was no progress in the positive characteristic case until the year 2002, when Ara, O’Meara and Perera
[
1
]
proved that a group algebra
D[G]
is stably finite whenever
G
is residually amenable and
D
is any division ring. This last result was generalized by Elek and Szabó
[
4
]
, that proved the same result for
G
a sofic group (see also
[
3
]
and
[
2
]
for alternative proofs).

Recently, the author
[
13
]
proved that
R[G]
is stably finite whenever
R
is left Noetherian and
G
is amenable using a notion of “algebraic entropy” for modules over
R[G]
. Soon after, Li and Liang
[
10
]
were able to extend this entropy theory to modules over
R[G]
with
G
sofic and, with this new invariant at hand, they could use similar ideas to
[
13
]
to verify that
R[G]
is stably finite whenever
R
is left Noetherian and
G
sofic.
In this short note, we show that the main ideas of
[
14
]
, combined with some localization theory for Grothendieck categories, can be used to give a direct and completely self-contained proof of this last result. In fact, we obtain an even more general statement for representations of a sofic group
G
on a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
(see Corollary
3.4
). The announced result then follows just specializing to the case when
\mathcal{G}\cong R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
for a left Noetherian ring
R
(see Corollary
3.5
).

2.
The main ingredients of the proof

In this section we recall some basic definitions and facts that will be needed for the proofs of the main results. In particular,
we start fixing some notations for labeled directed graphs and we recall a combinatorial result proved by Weiss (see Lemma
2.2
): this simple-looking result is key for the proofs in Section
3
. After this, we introduce Cayley graphs and we use these to define the class of sofic groups. In the second part of the section, we focus instead on Grothendieck categories, on their localizations, and on the so-called Gabriel filtration of a Grothendieck category. Finally, in the third and last part of the section, we concentrate on the category of actions of a group on the objects of a given Grothendieck category.

2.1.
Cayley Graphs and sofic groups

A
labeled directed graph (digraph)

\Gamma
is a pair
(V,E)
, where
V
is a set of
vertices
and
E\subseteq V\times V
is a set of ordered pairs of vertices, called
edges
, together with a map

\phi\colon E\to B
, from the set of edges to a set of
labelings

B
. If we need to make explicit the set of labelings
B
we say that
\Gamma
is a
B
-labeled digraph.

Let
B
be a set of labelings and consider two
B
-labeled digraphs
\Gamma_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1})
and
\Gamma_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
, with labelings defined by
\phi_{1}\colon E_{1}\to B
and
\phi_{2}\colon E_{2}\to B
, respectively. A
morphism of
B
-labeled digraphs

\alpha\colon\Gamma_{1}\to\Gamma_{2}
is a map
\alpha\colon V_{1}\to V_{2}
that sends edges to edges (i.e.,
(v,v^{\prime})\in E_{1}
implies
(\alpha(v),\alpha(v^{\prime}))\in E_{2}
, for all
v,\,v^{\prime}\in V_{1}
) and it respects the labels (i.e.,
\phi_{1}(v,v^{\prime})=\phi_{2}(\alpha(v),\alpha(v^{\prime}))
, for all
(v,v^{\prime})\in E_{1}
). In particular, such an
\alpha
is an
isomorphism
if it is bijective and its inverse also sends edges to edges or, equivalently, (
(\alpha(v),\alpha(v^{\prime}))\in E_{2}
implies
(v,v^{\prime})\in E_{1}
, for all
v,\,v^{\prime}\in V_{1}
).

Our main example of a labeled digraph is the following: given a finitely generated group
G
, fix a finite symmetric set of generators
B
of
G
. The
Cayley graph

\Gamma(G,B)
of
G
with respect to
B
is a
B
-labeled digraph
\Gamma(G,B)=(V,E)
such that the set of vertices
V
coincides with
G
and the edges are the pairs of the form
(g,gb)
, for
g\in G
and
b\in B
; such an edge is labeled by
b
.

Given a (labeled) digraph
\Gamma=(V,E)
we define the distance between vertices

d\colon V\times V\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\},

where
d(v,v^{\prime})
is the number of edges in a shortest directed path between
v
and
v^{\prime}
, if some directed path exists; and
d(v,v^{\prime})=\infty
otherwise. For all
v\in V
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we let

N_{n}(v):=\{v^{\prime}\in V:d(v,v^{\prime})\leq n\}

be the
n
-th neighborhood of
v
in
\Gamma
. In the case of a Cayley graph
\Gamma(G,B)
, the distance function is denoted by
d_{B}(-,-)
. For all
g\in G
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
N_{n}(B,g)

n
-th neighborhood of
g
in
\Gamma(G,B)
. If
g=1
we usually denote
N_{n}(B,1)
simply by
N_{n}(B)
.

Example 2.1
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}
be the additive group of integers, with the set of generators
B=\{-1,0,1\}
. Then
N_{n}(B)=\{-n,\dots,-1,0,1,\dots,n\}
. More generally, if we take

G=\mathbb{Z}^{k}=\mathbb{Z}e_{1}\times\dots\times\mathbb{Z}e_{k}
for some positive integer
k
, the canonical choice for the set of generators is
B=\{-e_{1},\dots,-e_{k},0,e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\}
. Thus we have

(1)

N_{n}(B)=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}e_{i}:\lambda_{i}\in\{-n,\dots,0,\dots,n\}\text{ and }\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\lambda_{i}|\leq n\right\}\,.

The following combinatorial lemma (which is Lemma 3.1 in
[
14
]
) will be extremely important later on.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group,
B
a finite symmetric set of generators of
G
and
\Gamma(G,B)
its Cayley graph. If
\Lambda=(V,E)
is a finite
B
-labeled digraph and, for at least half the vertices of
\Lambda
, their
2r_{0}+1
-neighborhoods in
\Lambda
are isomorphic to
N_{2r_{0}+1}(B)
(the
2r_{0}+1
-neighborhood of
1
in
\Gamma(G,B)
), then there is a subset
V_{1}
of these vertices such that

(1)

|V_{1}|/|V|\geq 1/2|N_{2r_{0}+1}(B)|
;

(2)

the minimal distance between any two vertices in
V_{1}
is at least
2r_{0}+1
.

Let us conclude this subsection with the definition of sofic group. Indeed, a finitely generated group
G
is
sofic
if, for each finite symmetric set of generators
B
of
G
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a finite
B
-labeled digraph
\Gamma=(V,E)
, which has a finite subset of its vertices
V_{0}\subseteq V
satisfying:

(1)

|V_{0}|\geq(1-\varepsilon)|V|
;

(2)

for each
v\in V_{0}
, the
r
-neighborhood
N_{r}(v)
of
v
in
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
N_{r}(B)
.

An arbitrary group
G
is
sofic
if each of its finitely generated subgroups is sofic.

It is an easy exercise to prove that each finite and each Abelian group is sofic. In fact, one can even prove that the class of sofic groups contains both the class of amenable and the class of residually finite (or, more generally, that of residually amenable) groups. It is still not known whether any group is sofic (for more details see
[
14
]
and
[
5
]
).

2.2.
Grothendieck categories, localization and Gabriel dimension

An Abelian category
\mathcal{G}
is said to be a
Grothendieck
category if it is cocomplete, it has a generator, and directed colimits are exact in
\mathcal{G}
. One can prove that Grothendieck categories are also complete and that they have injective envelopes.

A
torsion theory
in a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
is a pair
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
of full subcategories of
\mathcal{G}
such that

•

\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
is closed under taking quotients, extensions and coproducts;

•

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(T,F)=0
, for each
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
;

•

for each
X\in\mathcal{G}
, there are
T_{X}\in\mathcal{T}
,
F_{X}\in\mathcal{F}
and a short exact sequence:

0\to T_{X}\to X\to F_{X}\to 0.

A torsion theory
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is said to be
hereditary
provided
\mathcal{T}
is closed under taking subobjects.
Given a torsion theory
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
in a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
, the inclusion
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{G}
has a right adjoint
\mathbf{T}_{\tau}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{T}
, which is a subfunctor of the identity and it is called the
torsion functor
. For each
X\in\mathcal{G}
, we have that
X/\mathbf{T}_{\tau}X\in\mathcal{F}
.

Recall now that a
Giraud subcategory
of a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
, is a reflective subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
such that the inclusion functor
\mathbf{S}\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{G}
has an exact left adjoint
\mathbf{Q}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{C}
. To such a Giraud subcategory, one can associate a unique hereditary torsion theory, whose torsion class is:

\ker(\mathbf{Q}):=\{X\in\mathcal{G}:\mathbf{Q}(X)=0\}.

On the other hand, given a hereditary torsion theory
\tau=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
one can construct a Giraud subcategory of
\mathcal{G}
as follows. We say that an object
X\in\mathcal{G}
is
\tau
-local
if both
X\in\mathcal{F}
and
E(X)/X\in\mathcal{F}
. The full subcategory of
\mathcal{G}
of all the
\tau
-local objects is denoted by
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}
and the inclusion functor by
\mathbf{S}_{\tau}\colon\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{G}
; one can give an explicit construction of the left adjoint
\mathbf{Q}_{\tau}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}
(called the
\tau
-quotient functor
) to
\mathbf{S}_{\tau}
, and prove that this functor is exact. Let us remark that we adopt the notation “
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{T}
” because this category is equivalent to the Gabriel quotient of
\mathcal{G}
over
\mathcal{T}
(for more details on these constructions see
[
12
]
,
[
6
]
or
[
11
]
).

Let us now recall that the
Gabriel filtration
of a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
is a transfinite chain

0=\mathcal{G}_{-1}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{0}\subseteq\dots\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\subseteq\dots

of hereditary torsion classes, where:

•

\mathcal{G}_{-1}:=0
;

•

suppose that
\alpha
is an ordinal for which
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
has already been defined. An object
X\in\mathcal{G}
is said to be
\alpha
-cocritical
if
X
is
\tau_{\alpha}
-torsion free and every proper quotient of
X
is
\tau_{\alpha}
-torsion, where
\tau_{\alpha}
is the unique torsion theory whose torsion class is
\mathcal{G}_{{\alpha}}
. We let
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}
be the smallest hereditary torsion class containing
\mathcal{G}_{{\alpha}}
and all the
{\alpha}
-cocritical objects;

•

if
\lambda
is a limit ordinal,
\mathcal{G}_{\lambda}
is the smallest hereditary torsion class containing
\bigcup_{\alpha<\lambda}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
.

For any ordinal
\alpha
, we let
\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
and
\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
be the
\tau_{\alpha}
-torsion and the
\tau_{\alpha}
-quotient functor, respectively. Note that the ascending chain of subcategories
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
eventually stabilizes (one way to see this is to note that
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
is generated by the
\tau_{\alpha}
-torsion quotients of a fixed generator
G
of
\mathcal{G}
). Hence, it makes sense to consider
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}:=\bigcup_{\alpha}\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
. Let us remark that, even for
\mathcal{G}=R\text{-}{\mathrm{Mod}}
a category of modules, it may happen that
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}\neq\mathcal{G}
(e.g., take
R
to be a non-discrete valuation domain).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category, and
X\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
. Then,
X=0
if, and only if,
\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha+1}(X))=0
, for all
\alpha
.

Proof.

By definition of
\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, there is an ordinal
\delta\geq-1
such that
X\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
. We proceed by transfinite induction on such a
\delta
. Indeed, if
\delta=-1
, then
X=0
and so there is nothing to prove. If
\delta=\alpha+1
(so that
X=\mathbf{T}_{\alpha+1}(X)
), then there is a short exact sequence

0\to\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(X)\to X\to X/\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(X)\to 0.

Now,
\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(X)=0
by inductive hypothesis, while
X/\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(X)\leq\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha+1}(X))=0
, by hypothesis. Hence,
X=0
as desired. Finally, suppose that
\delta
is a limit ordinal. In this case,
X\cong\bigcup_{\alpha<\delta}\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(X)
and, by inductive hypothesis,
\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(X)=0
for each
\alpha<\delta
, showing that
X=0
.
∎

Recall that a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
is said to be
semi-Artinian
if, and only if, every non-trivial object
X\in\mathcal{G}
has a simple (i.e.,
(-1)
-cocritical) subobject. In particular, given an object
X
in a semi-Artinian Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
, there exists an ordinal
\alpha
and a continuous chain
\{X_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}
of subobjects of
X
such that
X=\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}X_{\beta}
and where
X_{\beta+1}/X_{\beta}
is a simple object for all
\beta<\alpha
.
Note that a Grothendieck category is semi-Artinian if, and only if,
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{0}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Given a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
, the category
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
is semi-Artinian for each ordinal
\alpha
.

Proof.

By construction, we have that
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}\cong(\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha})_{0}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category,
X\in\mathcal{G}
, and denote by
\mathcal{L}(X)
the lattice of subobjects of
X
. We say that
X
is
Noetherian
,
Artinian
or of
finite length
if the lattice
\mathcal{L}(X)
has the ascending chain condition, the descending chain condition or both, respectively. In case
X
is of finite length, we denote by
\ell(X)\in\mathbb{N}
the composition length of the lattice
\mathcal{L}(X)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category, and
0\neq N\in\mathcal{G}
a Noetherian object. Then, the following statements hold true:

(1)

N\in\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
;

(2)

\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(N)
is a Noetherian object in
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
, for each ordinal
\alpha
;

(3)

\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(N)
is a Noetherian object in
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
, for each ordinal
\alpha
;

(4)

\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha+1}(N))
is an object of finite length in
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
, for each ordinal
\alpha
.

Proof.

(1). Suppose, looking for a contradiction, that
N\notin\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
. By Noetherianity, we can select a maximal subobject
K\leq N
such that
N/K\notin\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
. Then, for each
K\lneq H\leq N
there exists an ordinal
\alpha_{H}
such that
N/H\in\mathcal{G}_{\alpha_{H}}
, let
\alpha:=\sup_{H}\alpha_{H}
. By construction,
N/K
is
\alpha
-cocritical, so
N/K\in\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}\subseteq\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}
, which is a contradiction.

(2). The lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(N))
of subobjects of
\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(N)
in
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
can be identified with the sublattice of
\mathcal{L}(N)
of those
K\in\mathcal{L}(N)
such that
K\leq\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(N)
. In particular, any ascending chain in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha}(N))
induces an ascending chain in
\mathcal{L}(N)
and, therefore, it stabilizes after a finite number of steps.

(3). The lattice
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(N))
of subobjects of
\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(N)
in
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
can be identified with the sublattice of
\mathcal{L}(N)
of those
K\in\mathcal{L}(N)
such that
N/K
is
\tau_{\alpha}
-torsion free (this is proved in
[
12
, Corollary IX.4.4]
for categories of modules, and the argument carries over to general Grothendieck categories). In particular, any ascending chain in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}}(\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(N))
induces an ascending chain in
\mathcal{L}(N)
and, therefore, it stabilizes after a finite number of steps.

(4). By (2) and (3) we deduce that
\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha+1}(N))
is a Noetherian object in
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
. As we have previously observed,
\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
is semi-Artinian and, therefore,
\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha+1}(N))
can be written as the union of a continuous chain whose subfactors are simple objects. By Noetherianity, any such ascending chain has to be finite.
∎

2.3.
Group actions on objects of a Grothendieck category

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category and consider a group
G
as a category with one object. The
category of representations
of
G
on
\mathcal{G}
is the functor category
\mathcal{G}^{G}:=\mathrm{Fun}(G,\mathcal{G})
. An alternative, but equivalent, description of this category can be given as follows:

•

each object of
\mathcal{G}^{G}
can be thought of as a pair
{}_{G}X:=(X,\lambda\colon G\to\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(X))
, with
X
in
\mathcal{G}
and
\lambda
a homomorphism to the automorphism group of
X
;

•

given two objects
{}_{G}X_{i}:=(X_{i},\lambda_{i}\colon G\to\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(X_{i}))
(with
i=1,2
), a morphism
\phi\colon{}_{G}X_{1}\to{}_{G}X_{2}
in
\mathcal{G}^{G}
is a
G
-
equivariant
morphism
\phi\colon X_{1}\to X_{2}
in
\mathcal{G}
, that is, the following square commutes for all
g\in G
:

\textstyle{X_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda_{1}(g)}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{X_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\lambda_{2}(g)}
\textstyle{X_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{X_{2}.}

As an example, one can take the trivial group
G=\mathbf{1}
and, in that case, it is easy to verify that
\mathcal{G}^{\mathbf{1}}\cong\mathcal{G}
.
Consider a group
G
and take the obvious inclusion
\iota\colon\mathbf{1}\to G
. This induces a
restriction functor
(also called a
forgetful
functor)

\downarrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{G}\colon\mathcal{G}^{G}\to\mathcal{G},

mapping a
G
-representation
{}_{G}X\mapsto X
. The left adjoint to
\downarrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{G}
is the
extension functor

\uparrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{G}\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{G}^{G}.

Given an object
X\in\mathcal{G}
, we have that
\uparrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{G}(X)\cong(X^{(G)},\beta\colon G\to\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathcal{G}}(X^{(G)}))
, where
X^{(G)}
is the coproduct of
|G|
-many copies of
X
in
\mathcal{G}
, and
\beta
is the usual
Bernoulli shift
on
X^{(G)}
, that is, for each
g\in G
,
\beta^{g}\colon X^{(G)}\to X^{(G)}
can be represented by the column-finite square matrix
\beta^{g}=(\beta^{g}_{g_{2},g_{1}})_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\in\mathrm{Mat}_{|G|}(\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}}(X))
:

\beta^{g}_{g_{2},g_{1}}:=\begin{cases}\mathrm{id}_{X}&\text{if $g_{2}=gg_{1}$;}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Example 2.6
.

Let
R
be a ring and
G
a group. The
group-ring

R[G]
is defined as the Abelian group
\bigoplus_{g\in G}\underline{g}R
, with the sum defined componentwise, and product defined as follows: given
x=\sum_{g\in G}\underline{g}x_{g}
and
y=\sum_{g\in G}\underline{g}y_{g}\in R[G]
,

x\cdot y:=\sum_{g\in G}\underline{g}\left(\sum_{h_{1}h_{2}=g}x_{h_{1}}y_{h_{2}}\right).

With these operations,
R[G]
becomes an associative and unitary ring. Consider now the category of left
R[G]
-modules
R[G]\text{-}{\mathrm{Mod}}
. Essentially by definition,
R[G]\text{-}{\mathrm{Mod}}
is equivalent to the category of additive functors
\mathrm{Add}(R[G],\mathrm{Ab})
from
R[G]
, viewed as a one-object preadditive category, to the category of Abelian groups
\mathrm{Ab}
. Similarly, these categories are both equivalent to
\mathrm{Add}(\mathbb{Z}[G],R\text{-}{\mathrm{Mod}})
and also to the category of (non-necessarily additive) functors
\mathrm{Fun}(G,R\text{-}{\mathrm{Mod}})
, that is,
R[G]\text{-}{\mathrm{Mod}}\cong(R\text{-}{\mathrm{Mod}})^{G}
. In this special example, the restriction and induction functors are called, respectively, the forgetful functor and the extension of scalars:

\downarrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{G}\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{R[G]}({}_{R[G]}R[G]_{R},-)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\uparrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{G}\cong{}_{R[G]}R[G]_{R}\otimes_{R}-.

Take now a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
and a group
G
. Limits and colimits in
\mathcal{G}^{G}
can be computed “as in
\mathcal{G}
”. More explicitly, given a small category
I
and a functor
F\colon I\to\mathcal{G}^{G}
, its (co)limit is the (co)limit of
\downarrow^{G}_{\mathbf{1}}\circ F\colon I\to\mathcal{G}
, endowed with the unique compatible
G
-action. In particular, the fact that
\mathcal{G}
is a cocomplete Abelian category with exact directed colimits implies that
\mathcal{G}^{G}
has the same properties. Furthermore, given a generator
X\in\mathcal{G}
, the induced representation
\uparrow^{G}_{\mathbf{1}}X
is a generator of
\mathcal{G}^{G}
, which is therefore a Grothendieck category.

An object
X\in\mathcal{G}
is said to be
compact
if the functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(X,-)\colon\mathcal{G}\to\mathrm{Ab}
commutes with coproducts. In particular, if
X
is Noetherian (or, more generally, finitely generated, that is,
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(X,-)
commutes with monomorphic directed colimits) then it is compact. Observe that, given a compact object
X\in\mathcal{G}
, an object
Y\in\mathcal{G}
and a set
I
, we have the following natural isomorphisms:

\textstyle{\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(X^{(I)},Y^{(I)})\cong\prod_{I}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(X,Y^{(I)})\cong\prod_{I}\bigoplus_{I}\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(X,Y),}

that is, a morphism
\phi\colon X^{(I)}\to Y^{(I)}
can be naturally considered as a column-finite matrix with coefficients in
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{G}}(X,Y)
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category,
X,\,Y\in\mathcal{G}
compact objects, and let

be a group. Consider a morphism

\phi_{g^{-1}g_{2},g_{1}}=\phi_{g_{2},gg_{1}},\qquad\text{for all $g,\,g_{1},\,g_{2}\in G$.}

Furthermore, given a morphism

\sum_{h\in G}\phi_{g_{2},h}\psi_{h,g_{1}}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{id}_{Y}&\text{if $g_{1}=g_{2}$;}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For the first claim about

\displaystyle(\beta^{g}\phi)_{g_{2},g_{1}}=\sum_{h\in G}\beta^{g}_{g_{2},h}\phi_{h,g_{1}}=\phi_{g^{-1}g_{2},g_{1}}\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle(\phi\beta^{g})_{g_{2},g_{1}}=\sum_{h\in G}\phi_{g_{2},h}\beta^{g}_{h,g_{1}}=\phi_{g_{2},gg_{1}}.

It is also clear that

if, and only if,
(\phi\circ\psi)_{g_{2},g_{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{Y}
if
g_{1}=g_{2}
and
(\phi\circ\psi)_{g_{2},g_{1}}=0
if
g_{1}\neq g_{2}\in G

(\phi\circ\psi)_{g_{2},g_{1}}=\sum_{h\in G}\phi_{g_{2},h}\psi_{h,g_{1}}
∎

Let us conclude with a brief discussion about the behavior of representations with respect to torsion and localization:

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category,

be a subobject of

\mathbf{Q}^{G}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{T}^{G}_{\alpha+1}(K))=0

By the above proposition it is clear that

3.
Stable finiteness of endomorphism rings

3.1.
The semi-Artinian case

This subsection contains the main application of Weiss’ idea to representations of a sofic group
G
on a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
. The other results in this section will be proved by reducing to this case with a combination of suitable torsion and quotient functors.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category,
G
a finitely generated sofic group,
X\in\mathcal{G}
an object of finite length, and let
{}_{G}M:=\uparrow^{G}_{\mathbf{1}}X\in\mathcal{G}^{G}
. Then,
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}^{G}}({}_{G}M)
is directly finite.

Proof.

Let
\phi=(\phi_{g_{2},g_{1}})_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}
and
\psi=(\psi_{g_{2},g_{1}})_{g_{1},g_{2}\in G}\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}^{G}}({}_{G}M)
be two endomorphisms such that
\phi\circ\psi=\mathrm{id}_{M}
, and let us verify that
\phi
is a monomorphism.
Indeed, we suppose, looking for a contradiction, that
\ker(\phi))\neq 0
, and we choose a sufficiently big
r_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
and a sufficiently small
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
in order to apply the definition of sofic group (Step 1 below) to obtain a statement about objects of finite length (Step 2), from which the contradiction can be seen (Step 3).

Step 1
.
The choice of
r_{0}
and
\varepsilon
.
Consider the following finite subset of
G
:

S:=\{1\}\cup\{g\in G:\phi_{g,1}\neq 0\}\cup\{g\in G:\psi_{g,1}\neq 0\}.

We denote by

the minimal positive integer such that

(where

with a sub-coproduct of

and note that

is an increasing direct union. Therefore,
\ker(\phi)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(X^{N_{n}(B)}\cap\ker(\phi))
and so there exists a minimal positive integer

such that

for all

. We let

•

•

and we choose

such that
\varepsilon\lneq 1/(2\,\ell(X)|N_{2r_{0}+1}(B)|)

The reason for this specific choice of

Note that, by the choice of

and Proposition

(2)

\sum_{g\in N_{r_{0}}(B)}\phi_{gh,1}\psi_{1,g}=\sum_{g\in N_{r_{0}}(B)}\phi_{h,g^{-1}}\psi_{g^{-1},1}=\sum_{g\in G}\phi_{h,g}\psi_{g,1}=\begin{cases}\mathrm{id}_{X}&\text{if $h=1$;}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Step 2

•

By (

\displaystyle\sum_{v^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}}\bar{\phi}_{v_{2},v^{\prime}}\bar{\psi}_{v^{\prime},v_{1}}

.

3.2.
The general result for Grothendieck categories

At this point, we know that, given a Grothendieck category
\mathcal{G}
, a finitely generated sofic group
G
, and an object
N\in\mathcal{G}
, the endomorphism ring of
\uparrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{G}N
is directly finite as far as
N
is of finite length. In the following corollary we show that, in fact, this last hypothesis can be weakened a lot, as it is enough to assume that
N
is Noetherian.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category,
G
a finitely generated sofic group,
N\in\mathcal{G}
a Noetherian object and
{}_{G}M:=\uparrow^{G}_{\mathbf{1}}N
. Then,
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}^{G}}({}_{G}M)
is directly finite.

Proof.

Let
\phi
,
\psi\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}^{G}}({}_{G}M)
such that
\phi\circ\psi=\mathrm{id}_{M}
,
let
{}_{G}K:=\ker(\phi)\in\mathcal{G}^{G}
and suppose, looking for a contradiction, that
K\neq 0
. By Corollary
2.9
, there is an ordinal
\alpha
such that
\bar{K}:=\mathbf{Q}^{G}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{T}^{G}_{\alpha+1}(K))\neq 0
. Let now
\bar{\mathcal{G}}:=\mathcal{G}_{\alpha+1}/\mathcal{G}_{\alpha}
, that is semi-Artinian,
\bar{N}:=\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}(\mathbf{T}_{\alpha+1}(N))\in\bar{\mathcal{G}}
, that is of finite length (being both Noetherian and semi-Artinian) and
\bar{M}:=\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}^{G}(\mathbf{T}^{G}_{\alpha+1}(M))\cong\uparrow^{G}_{\mathbf{1}}\bar{N}\in\bar{\mathcal{G}}^{G}
. Consider also the two morphisms
\bar{\phi}:=\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}^{G}(\mathbf{T}^{G}_{\alpha+1}(\phi))
and
\bar{\psi}:=\mathbf{Q}_{\alpha}^{G}(\mathbf{T}^{G}_{\alpha+1}(\psi))\in\mathrm{End}_{\bar{\mathcal{G}}^{G}}({}_{G}\bar{M})
and note that
{}_{G}\bar{K}=\ker(\bar{\phi})
in
\bar{\mathcal{G}}
. Since
\bar{\mathcal{G}}
is semi-Artinian, Proposition
3.1
tell us that
\bar{K}=0
, which is a contradiction.
∎

In the above corollary we have weakened the hypotheses on the base object
N
. In the following corollary we show that also the hypotheses on the acting group
G
can be weakened, in fact, we can take

to be an arbitrary sofic group.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category,
G
an arbitrary sofic group (not necessarily finitely generated),
N\in\mathcal{G}
a Noetherian object and
{}_{G}M:=\uparrow^{G}_{\mathbf{1}}N
. Then,
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}^{G}}({}_{G}M)
is directly finite.

Proof.

Suppose that we have two maps
\phi,\,\psi\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}^{G}}({}_{G}M)
such that
\phi\circ\psi=\mathrm{id}_{M}
. We suppose, looking for a contradiction, that
\ker(\phi)\neq 0
. Since
\mathcal{G}
is a Grothendieck category,
\ker(\phi)=\sum\{\ker(\phi)\cap N^{(F)}:F\subseteq G\text{ finite}\}
, so we can find a finite subset
F\subseteq G
such that
\ker(\phi)\cap N^{(F)}\neq 0
. Consider the following finite subset of
G
:

S:=F\cup\{g\in G:\phi_{g,1}\neq 0\}\cup\{g\in G:\psi_{g,1}\neq 0\},

and let
H\leq G
be the subgroup generated by
S
, which is clearly finitely generated and sofic. We claim that
N^{(H)}
is both a
\phi
- and a
\psi
-invariant subobject of
M
; to see this, consider
h\in H
,
g\in G
, and suppose that
\phi_{g,h}\neq 0
. Since
\phi
is
G
-equivariant,
\phi_{g,h}=\phi_{h^{-1}g,1}
, so
h^{-1}g\in S\subseteq H
that, together with the fact that
h\in H
, implies that
g=h(h^{-1}g)\in H
; the argument for
\psi
is completely analogous. We can then consider
M^{\prime}:=\uparrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{H}N
and the two maps
\phi^{\prime}:=\phi_{\restriction M^{\prime}}
and
\psi^{\prime}:=\psi_{\restriction M^{\prime}}
, that are clearly
H
-equivariant, that is, they are morphisms in
\mathcal{G}^{H}
. Finally, note that
\phi^{\prime}\circ\psi^{\prime}=\mathrm{id}_{M^{\prime}}
and
0\neq\ker(\phi)\cap N^{(F)}\leq\ker(\phi^{\prime})
, and this is absurd by Corollary
3.2
.
∎

Finally, let us show that the above statements about direct finiteness can be easily reformulated in terms of stable finiteness.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Grothendieck category,
G
a sofic group,
N\in\mathcal{G}
a Noetherian object and
{}_{G}M:=\uparrow^{G}_{\mathbf{1}}N
. Then,
R:=\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}^{G}}({}_{G}M)
is stably finite.

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and consider the following isomorphism:

\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(R)\cong\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}^{G}}(\uparrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{G}(N^{(k)})),

where
N^{k}
is still a Noetherian object. Hence, to verify that
R
is stably finite, that is, that
\mathrm{Mat}_{k}(R)
is directly finite for each
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, it is enough to verify that
\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{G}^{G}}(\uparrow_{\mathbf{1}}^{G}X)
is directly finite for each Noetherian object
X\in\mathcal{G}
, and we already know this by Corollary
3.3
.
∎

To conclude, let us reformulate the above results in the special case when the category
\mathcal{G}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
is the category of left
R
-modules for some ring
R
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
R
be a ring,
G
a sofic group and
{}_{R}N
a Noetherian left
R
-module. Then,
\mathrm{End}_{R[G]}(R[G]\otimes_{R}N)
is a stably finite ring.
In particular, if
R
is a left Noetherian ring, then
R[G]\cong\mathrm{End}_{R[G]}(R[G]\otimes_{R}R)
is stably finite.

Finding unavoidable colorful patterns in multicolored graphs
Matthew Bowen, Ander Lamaison, Alp Müyesser

Abstract
We provide multicolored and infinite generalizations for a Ramsey-type problem raised by Bollobás, concerning colorings of K n K_{n} where each color is well-represented. Let χ \chi be a coloring of the edges of a complete graph on n n vertices into r r colors. We call χ \chi ε \varepsilon -balanced if all color classes have ε \varepsilon fraction of the edges. Fix some graph H H , together with an r r -coloring of its edges. Consider the smallest natural number R ε r ​ ( H ) R_{\varepsilon}^{r}(H) such that for all n ≥ R ε r ​ ( H ) n\geq R_{\varepsilon}^{r}(H) , all ε \varepsilon -balanced colorings χ \chi of K n K_{n} contain a subgraph isomorphic to H H in its coloring. Bollobás conjectured a simple characterization of H H for which R ε 2 ​ ( H ) R_{\varepsilon}^{2}(H) is finite, which was later proved by Cutler and Montágh. Here, we obtain a characterization for arbitrary values of r r , as well as asymptotically tight bounds. We also discuss generalizations to graphs defined on perfect Polish spaces, where the corresponding notion of balancedness is each color class being non-meagre.

Introduction

Graph Ramsey Theory refers to mathematical results that attempt to find large patterns in colored graphs. The pattern in question is most often a monochromatic clique, a complete subgraph whose edges are all the same color. The
Ramsey number

R(k)
is the smallest integer
n
for which every
2
-edge-coloring of the complete graph
K_{n}
contains a monochromatic clique on
k
vertices. It is known that
2^{k/2}\leq R(k)\leq 2^{2k}
, where the constants that appear in the exponents resisted improvements for decades. For an overview, we refer the reader to the 2015 survey by Conlon et al.
[
2
]
.

To find patterns that are not monochromatic, we need to assume that all color classes are sufficiently represented in the colorings we consider.

Definition 1.1
.

We call
\chi:E(K_{n})\rightarrow[r]

\varepsilon
-balanced if each color class has at least
\varepsilon\binom{n}{2}
edges. When it is clear from context, we call
K_{n}

\varepsilon
-balanced if it comes equipped with an
\varepsilon
-balanced coloring.

A result by Erdős and Szemerédi shows that graphs which are not
\varepsilon
-balanced contain monochromatic cliques larger than the general bounds would be able to provide
[
7
]
. So in some sense, even if we were not interested in finding bi-colored patterns,
\varepsilon
-balanced graphs are the natural graphs to study from a Ramsey theoretic standpoint.

The natural function to look at is the
\varepsilon
-balanced Ramsey number
, which we introduce below. Note that by a color-consistent copy, we mean a subgraph which also preserves the color structure up to relabelling of the colors.

Definition 1.2

Let

\varepsilon

.

When

denotes
R_{\varepsilon}^{2}(H)
. Also, if

\varepsilon

In the other direction, Bollobás conjectured that

A natural question is to similarly classify the

Definition 1.3

Let

Proof of Theorem
1.4

We start with the simple observation that if we can find a complete subgraph
H
which is the disjoint union of monochromatic cliques of size
k
, the edge set between any two such monochromatic clique is also monochromatic (call
H
“blockwise monochromatic” if it is of this form), and further, all
r
colors appear somewhere in
H
, then
H
must contain
(r,k)
-unavoidable graph.

We should note that if one is not interested in asymptotically tight bounds, getting a blockwise monochromatic graph which uses all
r
colors is not difficult. Indeed, a celebrated theorem by Kővari, Sós and Turán
[
12
]
states that if a color class has an
\varepsilon
-fraction of the edge set, then we can find a monochromatic subgraph
K_{s,s}
where
s=c_{\varepsilon}\log{n}
and
c_{\varepsilon}>0
depends only on
\varepsilon
. Invoking the Kővari-Sós-Turán theorem on each of the
r
color classes in an
\varepsilon
-balanced
K_{n}
, we obtain already obtain a subgraph that uses all
r
colors. Now, we can apply Ramsey’s theorem to all
2r
parts to obtain monochromatic cliques, and afterwards simply use that between any two subset of the vertices, at least one color class has
1/r
-fraction of the edge set, and iteratively invoke the Kővari-Sós-Turán theorem to get a smaller subgraph where the edge-set between all
2r
monochromatic cliques is also monochromatic. This already proves:

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon
with
0<\varepsilon<1/r
,
R_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\mathcal{F}^{r}_{k})<\infty

In fact, since all elements of
\mathcal{F}^{r}_{k}
are
\varepsilon_{r}
-balanced for some
\varepsilon_{r}<1/(4r^{2})
(as any element of
\mathcal{F}^{r}_{k}
has at most
2r-2
vertices), we can state:

Proposition 2.2
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists some
0<\varepsilon_{r}<1/(4r^{2})
, such that for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{r}
, we have that for
r
-edge-colored graph
H
on
k

R_{\varepsilon}^{r}(H)<\infty
if any only if for all
F\in\mathcal{F}^{r}_{k}
,
F
contains a color-consistent copy of
H
.

When
r=2
, the argument we have sketched above seems to give the simplest proof of Bollobas’s conjecture. However, for arbitrary
r
, it gives a tower-type bound where the height of the tower is
\Theta(r)
. On the other hand, our Theorem
1.4
, combined with a simple probabilistic construction, will establish that the correct order of magnitude of
R_{\varepsilon}^{r}(\mathcal{F}^{r}_{k})
at
\varepsilon^{-ck}
, where
c
is some positive constant that depends only on

However, the layout of the better proof will be very similar in spirit to the simpler argument we provided above. In particular, we will again aim to find a fully-complete multipartite subgraph that uses all
r
colors. The trick will be to use the dependent random choice technique, which recently proved to be a powerful tool in combinatorics. We refer the reader to the survey by Fox and Sudakov for an overview
[
8
]
. We should also note that Fox and Sudakov also used the dependent random choice technique to obtain the asymptotically sharp bound on

when

, in
[
9
]
. However, their method also relied on the fact that in an
\varepsilon
-balanced graph with just two colors, there exists a large subset of vertices which have high degree in red as well as blue. For
\varepsilon

1
), sets with high degree on every color do not necessarily exist.

The following lemma is the most basic form of the technique, a proof can be found in
[
8
]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a graph with average degree
\varepsilon n
. Then, there exists a subset
W
with
|W|=w
such that all
k_{0}
sized subsets of
W
have a common neighborhood of size
\beta n
, provided that there exists a positive integer

satisfying:

n\varepsilon^{t}-n^{k_{0}}\beta^{t}\geq w

We will need a bipartite version of this lemma, which we state and prove below. We remark that the proof of the below lemma is very similar to the proof of the preceding lemma that can be found in
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a graph partitioned into vertex sets
A
and
B
, with
|A|=m
and
|B|=m^{\prime}
, and at least
\varepsilon mm^{\prime}
edges between
A
and
B
. Then, there exists
W\subseteq A
, with
|W|=w
, such that every subset
K\subset W
with
|K|=k_{0}
has
\beta m^{\prime}
common neighbors in
B
, provided that there exists a positive integer
t
satisfying:

m\varepsilon^{t}-m^{k_{0}}\beta^{t}\geq w

Proof.

N_{X}(v)
denotes the neighborhood of
v
in
X
, and
N_{X}(Y)
denotes the
common
neighborhood of
Y
in
X
.
Sample a subset
T\subseteq B
of size
t
uniformly at random with repetition. Let
U=N_{A}(T)
. Note that a vertex
a\in A
is in
U
if and only if
T\subseteq N_{B}(a)
. Using this fact and linearity of expectation, we calculate:

\mathbb{E}[\,|U|\,]=\sum_{a\in A}\left(\frac{|N_{B}(a)|}{m^{\prime}}\right)^{t}\geq\sum_{a\in A}\left(\frac{\varepsilon m^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}\right)^{t}=m\varepsilon^{t},

where the inequality holds by convexity of
x^{t}
. Now, if we remove from
U
an element from every
k_{0}
-element subset

which fails to have
|N_{B}(R)|\geq\beta m^{\prime}
, the resulting set will have the desired properties. Let
Z
be the random variable denoting how many such
k_{0}
-element subsets are there in

\mathbb{E}\left[Z\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\binom{A}{k_{0}}\\
N_{B}(R)<\beta m^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{N_{B}(R)}{m^{\prime}}\right)^{t}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\binom{A}{k_{0}}\\
N_{B}(R)<\beta m^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\beta^{t}\leq m^{k_{0}}\beta^{t}

Thus, on average, we would have to remove at most
m^{k_{0}}\beta^{t}
vertices to modify

to create a set
W
with the desired properties (removing one vertex from each “bad” set

). Using linearity of expectation one last time, we derive
\mathbb{E}[W]\geq\mathbb{E}[\,|U|-Z]\geq m\varepsilon^{t}-m^{k_{0}}\beta^{t}
, so in particular, a

with at least this size must exist, as claimed. ∎

The below corollary simply follows by iterating the above argument, and will be convenient when we establish connections between multipartite graphs. If the edges of a graph are colored, define

to be the set of common neighbors of

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
r
and

be positive integers,
r\geq 2
. There exists

with the following: let

be disjoint vertex sets, all of size

\frac{1}{8}\log_{r}n

|N_{B_{i}}^{c_{i}}(X)|\geq\sqrt{n}
.

Proof.

We will construct a nested sequence of sets

, with

such that every

. Apply Lemma
2.4
to the graph formed by the edges of color

, with the values

,
m^{\prime}=n
,
\epsilon=r^{-1}
,

,
\beta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}
,

, and denote by

m\epsilon^{t}-m^{k_{0}}\beta^{t}=n^{\frac{2\cdot 2^{-(i-1)}}{3}}-n^{-\frac{2^{-(i-1)}}{24}\log_{r}n}>n^{2^{-i}}

This proves the statement, with

.∎

Corollary 2.6

Let

Induction on

Note that for

As mentioned to previously, our goal is to find a fully-complete multipartite graph, which uses all
r

Given a color

2.3

k_{0}=\frac{-\log_{\varepsilon}n}{8}
,
\beta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}

W^{\prime}_{i}\subset W_{i}

|W^{\prime}_{i}|=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{r}
. We can now apply Corollary
2.6

4
Infinite Balanced Graphs

We now begin considering what natural restrictions we can put on our colorings to generalize our previous results to the infinite case. The main difficulty here is that it is no longer clear how the notion of being
\varepsilon
-balanced should generalize. For example, we cannot obtain an

(a countably infinite analogue of an

to vertices in

are blue and half red) this is still not enough. The following construction applies to both restrictions.

Proposition 4.1
.

There exist back-degree balanced

\omega

contains no

Proof.

Consider the graph with vertex set consisting of two disjoint copies of

. Color all edges between vertices in the right copy blue and all edges between vertices in the left copy red. For each vertex
i
in the left copy and
j
in the right copy, color edge

and red otherwise. Notice that this coloring has an

for any

yet no

. To see that we may choose this coloring to be back-degree balanced, one may start with evens and odds as the partitions.
∎

On the other hand, if we deal with the graphs defined on Polish spaces the notion of being non-meagre seems to correspond rather well with the density results from the finite version. For convenience, we recall the necessary definitions and results. The reader may see

]
for a more extensive overview.
X

Definition 4.2
.

We say

Definition 4.3
.

A set

Definition 4.4

is meagre, and we call

Baire measurable if the preimages of open sets have the BP.

We now restate Theorem

in more generality:

Theorem 4.5
.

Suppose

is a perfect Polish Space and

is Baire measurable and each color class is non-meagre. Then we find some color consistent member of

where each blown-up clique is a Cantor set.

The following easy consequences of the above definitions will be useful in the proof. Proofs of the following two lemmas can be found in Chapter 8 of

Lemma 4.6

(Localization)

Suppose

Let

Recall that using the axiom of choice

Suppose

Now, before beginning the proof of Theorem

In the finite setting we were able to get around this issue by utilizing Corollary

Here, we we collect some open problems and future directions of research. Firstly, even though the upper bound provided by Theorem

An extension of Bollobás’s conjecture for hypergraphs was given in
[

]
(Theorem 4.2). It would not be difficult to extend this result to arbitrary many colors, using an appropriate family of multicolored unavoidable hypergraphs as we did here for multicolored graphs. However, as far as we are aware there are no practical bounds, already for two colors and uniformity three. We state the result for uniformity three below informally. A more precise statement for arbitrary uniformities can be found in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
and positive integer

, any two-coloring of

(the

edges in both colors contains a subgraph on

such that the color of any

A proof for the above Theorem in full generality was given in the appendix of
[
14
]
, as it was used to prove another result in that paper. However, no explicit bound on

was cited, and from the proof it is clear that the dependence of

on

(uniformity) is a tower of length much larger than

. For

, as stated in the above Theorem, we pose the following problem that was essentially also raised in
[

]
.

Question 1

Can we take

in Theorem 5.1, for

some absolute constant?

For the convenience of the reader, we sketch a short proof of Theorem 5.1 that is somewhat different than the proof that was given in
[

]
, which we believe is more direct. Like in
[

]
, we will not get any sensible bounds because of a reference to the Product Ramsey Theorem (
[

]

Sketch of Theorem 5.1.

Consider an
\varepsilon
-balanced

-uniform complete hypergraph on

[

For any fixed integer

Let

Proposition 3.4

Let

]
is over the complex numbers, but that the theory carries over to any algebraically closed field.
, the Brill-Noether variety
W_{3}^{1}(X_{k})
either has 1 or 2 points over
\overline{k}
. The case
W_{3}^{1}(X)
being isomorphic to
X
mentioned there cannot happen since we assumed
X
to not be hyperelliptic. If
\#W_{3}^{1}(X_{k})(\overline{k})=1
then
\#W_{3}^{1}(X_{k})(k)=1
and
X_{k}
is trigonal by the above discussion. On the other hand if
\#W_{3}^{1}(X_{k})(\overline{k})=2
, then there are exactly two possibilities. Namely:

(a)

\#W_{3}^{1}(X_{k})(k)=2
and hence
X_{k}
is trigonal,

(b)

\#W_{3}^{1}(X_{k})(k)=0
and hence
X_{k}
is not trigonal but becomes trigonal over the quadratic extension of
k
over which the two points of
\#W_{3}^{1}(X)
are defined.

We let
F_{X_{k}}
denote the smallest field extension of
k
for which
\#W_{3}^{1}(X_{k})(F_{X_{k}})>0
. So either
F_{X_{k}}=k
or it is the quadratic field over which the two points of
\#W_{3}^{1}(X)
are defined in case (b) above.

Since
X_{k}
is not hyperelliptic, the canonical model of
X
is a smooth complete intersection of a cubic and a quadric
Q_{k}
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
. Each
g_{3}^{1}
corresponding to one of the points in
W_{3}^{1}(X)(k)
is a family of lines in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}
intersecting
X_{k}
three times, counting multiplicities. Let
L
denote one of these lines. By Bezout’s theorem this line
L
lies on the quadric
Q_{k}
. In particular, if
Q_{k}
is nonsingular then the lines of the
g_{3}^{1}
actually form a ruling of
Q
(see
[
3
, p.206]
again). In the nonsingular case, the field
F_{X_{k}}
defined above is actually the field of definition of these rulings. If
\Char k>2
, then this field is obtained by adjoining to
k
the square root of the discriminant of the polynomial defining
Q
(see e.g
[
5
, p.45-46]
).

Part (1) immediately follows from this discussion. Indeed,
F_{X_{\mathbb{Q}}}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})=\mathcal{O}(D)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
. So for a field extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
we have
\#W_{3}^{1}(X_{\mathbb{Q}})(K)>0
if and only if
K
contains
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})
. Both of these equivalent conditions are equivalent to
X_{K}
being trigonal.

Part
(3)
follows similarly since
F_{X_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}
is either
\mathbb{F}_{p}
or
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
. So in particular,
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
contains
F_{X_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}
if
n
is even.

Now we prove part
(2)
in the case where
p
splits or ramifies in
\mathcal{O}(D)
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
\mathcal{O}(D)
lying over
p
. Then by the maximality assumption on
\mathcal{O}(D)
at
p
, the ring
\mathcal{O}(D)_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a discrete valuation ring and the residue field of
\mathcal{O}(D)_{\mathfrak{p}}
is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. By part (1) we have
\gon X_{\mathcal{O}(D)\otimes\mathbb{Q}}=3
. Since the gonality of a curve can only decrease under specialization and field extensions we have

\gon X_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}\leq\gon X_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\leq\gon X_{\mathcal{O}(D)_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes\mathbb{Q}}\leq\gon X_{\mathcal{O}(D)\otimes\mathbb{Q}}=3.

Hence
\gon X_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}}=3
by the non-hyperellipticity assumption.

What remains is to show part
(2)
when
p
is inert. Assume for the moment
p>2
. Since
p
is inert in
\mathcal{O}(D)
we know that
F_{X_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}(\sqrt{D})
is a quadratic extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and hence isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
. It follows that
X
is trigonal over
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
if and only if
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
contains
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
, which happens exactly when
n
is even. So if
n
is odd,
X_{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}
is not trigonal.

The case
p=2
is slightly more subtle. While it might be possible to deal with this case abstractly, we found it clearer to take a more explicit approach. Let’s write the quadric
Q
in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
as

Q=\sum_{i=0}^{3}\sum_{j=0}^{i}a_{i,j}x_{i}x_{j}.

For this quadric we will also use the matrix form notation

Q=\begin{bmatrix}a_{0,0}&a_{0,1}&a_{0,2}&a_{0,3}\\
*&a_{1,1}&a_{1,2}&a_{1,3}\\
*&*&a_{2,2}&a_{2,3}\\
*&*&*&a_{3,3}\end{bmatrix}.

Let
P\in X(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{3}(\mathbb{Z}[1/N])
be a point. By choosing a suitable set of coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
we may assume
P=(0:0:0:1)
and hence
a_{3,3}=0
. By a further change of coordinates we may assume
a_{1,3}=a_{2,3}=0
as well, so that
Q
looks like:

\begin{bmatrix}a_{0,0}&a_{0,1}&a_{0,2}&a_{0,3}\\
*&a_{1,1}&a_{1,2}&0\\
*&*&a_{2,2}&0\\
*&*&*&0\end{bmatrix}.

With
Q
as above, we have
D=a_{0,3}^{2}(a_{1,2}^{2}-4a_{1,1}a_{2,2})
. The assumption for
p
to be inert in
\mathcal{O}(D)
is equivalent to
D\equiv 5\mod 8.
In particular
Q_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
is nonsingular and
a_{0,3}\equiv a_{1,2}\equiv a_{1,1}\equiv a_{2,2}\equiv 1\mod 2
.

Since
Q_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
is nonsingular it has two rulings. These two rulings both contain a line passing through
P
. Additionally, starting from
P
one can let
T\subset P^{3}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
be the tangent space to
Q_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
at
P_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
, and
T\cap Q_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
will be exactly the union of these same two lines. In particular if these two lines are interchanged by the action of Galois, then these two rulings will be interchanged as well.

Now let’s compute
T
on the affine chart where
x_{3}\neq 0
and let
X_{0},X_{1},X_{2}
be the affine coordinates on
\mathbb{A}^{3}_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
. Then the tangent space
T
is given by
X_{0}=0
, so that the union of two lines on
Q_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
passing through
P_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
can be described by
X_{0}=0
and
a_{1,1}X_{1}^{2}+a_{1,2}X_{1}X_{2}+a_{2,2}X_{2}^{2}
. Since
a_{1,1}\equiv a_{2,2}\equiv a_{1,2}=1\pmod{2}
, the polynomial
a_{1,1}X_{1}^{2}+a_{1,2}X_{1}X_{2}+a_{2,2}X_{2}^{2}
, is irreducible and hence so is also the scheme
T\cap Q_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
. This can only happen if the geometric lines generating
T\cap Q_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
are Galois conjugates. In particular, the two rulings of
Q_{\mathbb{F}_{2}}
are swapped by the action of Galois,
W_{3}^{1}(X_{\mathbb{F}_{2}})
consist of two points whose field of definition is
\mathbb{F}_{2^{2}}
and hence
X_{\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}
is not trigonal when
n
is odd.

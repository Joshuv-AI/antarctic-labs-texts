Abstract
. When solving the Poisson equation on honeycomb hexagonal grids, we show that the P 1 P_{1} virtual element is three-order superconvergent in H 1 H^{1} -norm, and two-order superconvergent in L 2 L^{2} and L ∞ L^{\infty} norms. We define a local post-process which lifts the superconvergent P 1 P_{1} solution to a P 3 P_{3} solution of the optimal-order approximation. The theory is confirmed by a numerical test.

1.
Introduction

In this work, we study the

P_{1}
virtual element on honeycomb meshes for solving the following Poisson equation.

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=f&&\hbox{\rm{in }}\ \Omega,\\
u&=0&&\hbox{\rm{on }}\ \partial\Omega,\end{aligned}

where
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a bounded polygonal domain
which can be meshed by one-size honeycomb hexagons and a few additional polygons consisting
of equilateral triangles only at domain boundary, such as the
one illustrated in Figure
1
.

\Omega

In 2D, the virtual element functions are continuous
P_{k}
polynomials on
edges of a polygonal mesh and extended to inside polygons by the Poisson equation with

P_{k-2}
moments.
In particular, for the 2D
P_{1}
virtual elements studied in this paper,
the continuous
P_{1}
polynomials on the edges of the mesh are harmonically
extended into polygons.
Such non-polynomial virtual element functions are interpolated into
a computable polynomial space on each polygon.
As the interpolation is usually not a full-rank operator,
a stabilizer has to be added to the discrete equation.
However, simply raising the polynomial degree of the local interpolation space,
in order to eliminate the stabilizer,
does not work in general, cf.
[
36
]
.

The references
[
4

5
]
propose to use some high-degree polynomial
interpolation space only for 2D
P_{1}
virtual elements to get some
stabilizer-free virtual element methods.
The reference
[

proposes to use only high-
P_{k}
harmonic polynomials as
the interpolation functions in a stabilizer-free virtual element method.
The references
[
24

36
]
use multi-piece polynomials as the interpolation space on
each polygon or polyhedron, so that the resulting virtual element method
works for any
P_{k}
virtual element without using stabilizer.

In this work, we apply the stabilizer-free
P_{1}
virtual element method
[
24
,
36
]

to the Poisson equation (
1.1
) on 2D honeycomb meshes,
cf. Figure
1
.
Taking the advance of extreme symmetry, we show that the
P_{1}
virtual element solution

u_{h}
is
three-order superconvergent (three orders above the optimal order) in
H^{1}
-norm, i.e.,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(I_{h}u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}}\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}},

where
H^{4,\infty}
is the Sobolev space of functions with all 4-th
derivatives bounded, and
I_{h}
is the
P_{1}
nodal interpolation at corners
of honeycombs.
Additionally, the
P_{1}
virtual element
solution is two-order superconvergent in
L^{2}
-norm and in
L^{\infty}
norm, i.e.,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}.

We then define a local-lift post-process, which lifts the
P_{1}
virtual element
solution
u_{h}
to a piecewise
P_{3}
solution

\tilde{u}_{h}
of the optimal order approximation, i.e.,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}+h|u-\tilde{u}_{h}|_{H^{1},h}\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}},

where
|\cdot|_{H^{1},h}
is a piecewise
H^{1}

patch grid
\mathcal{R}_{h}
(defined in section 3.)
We remark that the
P_{1}
virtual element solution itself can only converge at the optimal order,
two orders lower than that of (
1.4

\displaystyle\|u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}+h|u-u_{h}|_{H^{1},h}\leq Ch^{2}|u|_{H^{2}}.

The theory is confirmed by a numerical test.

2.
The
P_{1}
honeycomb virtual element

We define the
P_{1}
honeycomb virtual element in this section and show
that the
P_{1}
honeycomb virtual element equation has a unique solution.

Figure 2.
Left: A regular hexagon inside
\Omega
, where

{\bf x}_{0}
is an auxiliary vertex of triangular mesh
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
;
Middle: A regular triangle which can be only at a corner of
\Omega
;
Right: A pentagon consisting of three regular triangles which can be at an edge of
\Omega

with
{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{4}\subset\partial\Omega
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\{T\}
be a uniform honeycomb mesh on the domain
\Omega
,
where
T
is either a regular hexagon of edge-size
h
inside
\Omega
,
or a regular triangle of edge-side
h
at a corner of the polygonal domain, or
a pentagon consisting of three regular triangles of edge-side
h
at an edge of domain,
cf. Figure
2
.
We note that there is a vertex added at the middle of straight edge
{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{4}
,
cf. Figure
2
.
For each hexagon
T
, we add the central point
{\bf x}_{0}
to it so that it is subdivided
into 6 regular triangles
\{T_{i}\}
, cf. the left diagram of Figure
2
.
For a pentagon
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we connect its center point
{\bf x}_{0}
with
two vertices
{\bf x}_{2}
and
{\bf x}_{3}
so that
it is subdivided into 3 regular triangles, cf. the right diagram in Figure
2
.
In addition to the regular triangles in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
(the
middle graph in Figure
2
), all theses regular triangles form a
equilateral triangular mesh
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
on domain
\Omega
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{h}
denote the set of edges
e
in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Let
E=\cup_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}e
and
\mathbb{B}_{1}(E)
be the space of
piecewise linear functions on domain
E
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{1}(E)=\{\tilde{v}\in C^{0}(E):\tilde{v}|_{e}\in P_{1}(e)\ \hbox{\rm{ for all }}e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}\}.

The virtual element space on the mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is defined as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{V}_{h}=\{\tilde{v}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)&:\tilde{v}\in\mathbb{B}_{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h}),\ \Delta\tilde{v}|_{K}=0\quad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\}.\end{aligned}

In computation, the non-polynomial virtual element function is interpolated to
a piecewise polynomial.
The piecewise polynomial space on each
T\in\mathcal{T}
is defined by, cf.
Figure
2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{1}(T)=\begin{cases}\{v_{h}\in H^{1}(T):v_{h}|_{T_{i}}\in P_{1}(T_{i})\ \hbox{\rm{if $T=\bigcup_{i=1}^{6}T_{i}$ is a hexagon}}\},\\
\{v_{h}\in H^{1}(T):v_{h}|_{T_{1}}\in P_{1}(T_{1})\ \hbox{\rm{if
$T=T_{1}$ is a triangle}}\},\\
\{v_{h}\in H^{1}(T):v_{h}|_{T_{i}}\in P_{1}(T_{i})\ \hbox{\rm{if $T=\bigcup_{i=1}^{4}T_{i}$ is a pentagon}}\},\end{cases}

where
T_{i}
is an edge-size
h
regular triangle.

The interpolated virtual element space on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
is defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle V_{h}=\{v_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{v}\ :\ v_{h}|_{T}\in\mathbb{V}_{1}(T),\ T\in\mathcal{T}_{h};\ \tilde{v}\in\tilde{V}_{h}\},

where
v_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{v}
is the local
H^{1}
-projection, i.e.,
v_{h}
is
the local solution on
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} (\nabla(v_{h}-\tilde{v}),\nabla w_{h})_{T}&=0\quad\forall w_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{w}\in\mathbb{V}_{1}(T),\\
\langle v_{h}-\tilde{v},w_{h}\rangle_{\partial T}&=0\quad\forall w_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{1}(T).\end{aligned}\right.

The stabilizer-free virtual element equation reads:
Find
u_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{u}\in V_{h}
such that

(2.3)

\displaystyle(\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})=(f,v_{h})\quad\forall\tilde{v}\in\tilde{V}_{h},\ v_{h}=\Pi_{h}^{\nabla}\tilde{v}.

Lemma 2.1
.

The
P_{1}
honeycomb virtual element equation (
2.3
) has a unique
solution.

Proof.

(
2.3
) is a finite dimensional square system of linear equations.
Its existence of solution is implied by the uniqueness of the solution.
Let
f=0
and
v_{h}=u_{h}
in (
2.3
), where
u_{h}
is a solution.
(
2.3
) becomes
\|\nabla u_{h}\|_{T}^{2}=0
on every
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Thus
u_{h}\in P_{0}(T)
. Because
u_{h}\in C^{0}(\Omega)
,
u_{h}=C
is a global constant on

\Omega
. By
u_{h}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
,
u_{h}=0
. The proof is complete.
∎

3.
The superconvergence in
H^{1}
-norm

We show in this section that the
P_{1}
honeycomb virtual element is
three-order superconvergent in
H^{1}
-norm first, and is two-order superconvergent in

L^{2}
-norm and in
L^{\infty}
-norm.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in H^{4,\infty}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
of (
2.2
)
be the solutions of Poisson’s equation (
1.1
) and
the finite element equation (
2.3
), respectively.
It holds that

\displaystyle|I_{h}u-u_{h}|_{H^{1}}\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}.

Proof.

For analysis, we introduce the auxiliary, conforming
P_{1}
finite element
on a sub-triangular mesh of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{6}
be the set of all hexagons in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
For each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{6}
, we introduce a new vertex/node
{\bf x}_{0}
, the center
of the regular hexagon.
Let
\mathcal{N}_{h}
be the set of all vertices/nodes in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Let

(3.1)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{h}=\mathcal{N}_{h}\bigcup\{{\bf x}_{0}:{\bf x}_{0}\in T\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{6}\}.

Subdividing all hexagons and pentagons in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
by
connecting
{\bf x}_{0}
with all
{\bf x}_{i}
, cf. Figure
2
,
we have an equilateral triangular mesh on
\Omega
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}=\{T:T\ \hbox{\rm{ is a regular triangle of edge-size $h$}}\}.

The auxiliary, conforming
P_{1}
finite element
space on triangular mesh
\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is defined by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{h}=\{v_{h}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega):v_{h}|_{T}\in P_{1}(T)\ \forall T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\}.

Let
e_{h}=I_{h}u-u_{h}
, where
I_{h}:H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to V_{h}
is the nodal interpolation
operator based on nodal set
\mathcal{N}_{h}
.
Let
\overline{I}_{h}:H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\overline{V}_{h}
(defined in (
3.3
)) be the nodal interpolation
operator based on nodal set
\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{h}
(defined in (
3.1
)), i.e.,

\displaystyle I_{u}u({\bf x}_{j})=u({\bf x}_{j})\quad\forall{\bf x}_{j}\in\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{h}.

Let

(3.4)

\displaystyle d_{h}=I_{h}u-\overline{I}_{h}u\in\overline{V}_{h}.

By the harmonic solution in (
2.2
),
d_{h}
is supported only inside all
hexagons
T\in\mathcal{T}^{6}_{h}
and, cf Figure
2
, due to a
regular hexagon,

\displaystyle d_{h}({\bf x}_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{i=1}^{6}u({\bf x}_{i})-u({\bf x}_{0}),

\displaystyle d_{h}({\bf x}_{i})

\displaystyle=0,\quad i=1,\dots,6.

Because

(3.5)

\displaystyle(\nabla u-\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h})=0\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h},

it follows that by the divergence theorem,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |I_{h}u-u_{h}|_{H^{1}}^{2}&=(\nabla(I_{h}u-u),\nabla e_{h})\\
&=(\nabla d_{h},\nabla e_{h})+(\nabla(\overline{I}_{h}u-u),\nabla e_{h})\\
&=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{6}}(\nabla d_{h},\nabla e_{h})_{T}+\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla(\overline{I}_{h}u-u),\nabla e_{h})_{T}\\
&=0+\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}(\overline{I}_{h}u-u)\partial_{{\bf t}_{i+2}^{\perp}}e_{h}d{\bf s},\end{aligned}

where
{\bf t}_{i}^{\perp}
is the
90
degrees clockwise rotation of
{\bf t}_{i}
,
and
{\bf x}_{i}
are three vertices of
T
depicted in Figure
3
.
Here
(\nabla d_{h},\nabla e_{h})_{T}=0
because
e_{h}
is discrete harmonic inside
a hexagon
T
by (
2.2
), and
d_{h}
is supported inside
T
.

Figure 3.
A equilateral triangle with three unit tangent vectors.

For linear polynomials, the normal derivative is a linear combination of two
tangential derivatives.
We have, due to equilateral triangles, cf. Figure
3
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad\ \sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}(\overline{I}_{h}u-u)\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}^{\perp}}e_{h}d{\bf s}\\
&=\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}(\overline{I}_{h}u-u)\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}(\partial_{{\bf t}_{i+1}}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}})e_{h}d{\bf s}\\
&=\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}(\overline{I}_{h}u-u)\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i+1}}e_{h}d{\bf s},\end{aligned}

where the second term cancels itself from the other side of triangle
because
{\bf t}_{i+1}
has an opposite direction on the other side
of edge
{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}
, or
e_{h}=0
if
{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}
has no
other neighbor triangle, i.e., a boundary edge.

A version of the Euler-Maclaurin formula is, cf.
[
22
]
,

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}f(t)dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=a}^{b}f(n)-\frac{f(a)+f(b)}{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{b_{i}}{i!}(f^{(i-1)}(b)-f^{(i-1)}(a))

\displaystyle\quad\ -\int_{a}^{b}P_{k+1}(t)f^{(k+1)}(t)dt,

where
b_{i}
is a Bernoulli number and
P_{k+1}(t)
is
a scaled periodic Bernoulli polynomial
B_{k+1}(\{1-t\})/((k+1)!)
.
Other than
b_{1}
, all odd Bernoulli numbers are zero.
Letting
k=3
,
a=0
and
b=1
,
we
have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}fdt

\displaystyle=h\frac{f(0)+f(1)}{2}+\frac{h^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{1}f^{\prime\prime}dt-h^{4}\int_{0}^{1}P_{4}(t)f^{(4)}(t)dt.

Noting that
\int_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}}\overline{I}_{h}ud{\bf s}=(u({\bf x}_{1})+u({\bf x}_{2}))h/2
,
we get from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
),

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |e_{h}|_{H^{1}}^{2}&=-\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\frac{h^{2}}{2\sqrt{3}}\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}}^{2}u\partial_{{\bf t}_{i+1}}e_{h}d{\bf s}\\
&\quad\ +\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}h^{4}\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}P_{4}({\bf x})\partial^{4}_{{\bf t}_{0}}u\partial_{{\bf t}_{i+1}}e_{h}d{\bf s},\\
&=:\frac{h^{2}}{2\sqrt{3}}I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{aligned}

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get, because
P_{4}
has a zero-point inside the edge
(
\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}P_{4}({\bf x})d{\bf s}=0
),

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |I_{2}|&\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}\bigg(\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|e_{h}|_{H^{1}(T)}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}=Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}|e_{h}|_{H^{1}}.\end{aligned}

For the first term in (
3.8
), we consider an area-integral, cf. Figure
3
,
by integration by parts, noting that
\partial_{{\bf t}_{2}}e_{h}
is a constant,

\displaystyle\quad\ \int_{T}(\partial_{{\bf t}_{1}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{0}}^{2}u)\partial_{{\bf t}_{2}}e_{h}d{\bf x}=\int_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{0}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{0}}^{2}u\partial_{{\bf t}_{2}}e_{h}d{\bf s}-\int_{{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{2}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{0}}^{2}u\partial_{{\bf t}_{2}}e_{h}d{\bf s}.

When summing the first term above, as
{\bf t}_{2}
is tangential to
{\bf x}_{1}{\bf x}_{0}
,
the term is canceled with the term on the other side of the triangle.
Thus, we have, from (
3.8
),

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\int_{T}(\partial_{{\bf t}_{i}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}}^{2}u)\partial_{{\bf t}_{i+1}}e_{h}d{\bf x}.

Taking integration by parts, we get, noting that the angle between two
{\bf t}_{i}
is

120^{0}
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{1}&=\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\bigg(-\int_{T}(\partial_{{\bf t}_{i}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i+1}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}}^{2}u)e_{h}d{\bf x}\\
&\ \quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}(\partial_{{\bf t}_{i}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}}^{2}u)e_{h}d{\bf s}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\bf x}_{i+1}{\bf x}_{i-1}}(\partial_{{\bf t}_{i}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}}^{2}u)e_{h}d{\bf s}\bigg)\\
&=\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}-\int_{T}(\partial_{{\bf t}_{i}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i+1}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{2}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i}}u\Big)e_{h}d{\bf x}\\
&=0,\end{aligned}

where
\sum\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}(\partial_{{\bf t}_{i}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}}^{2}u)e_{h}d{\bf s}=0
as
{\bf t}_{i}
are parallel but in
opposite directions on the two sides of the edge
{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}
,

\sum\int_{{\bf x}_{i+1}{\bf x}_{i-1}}(\partial_{{\bf t}_{i}}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i-1}}^{2}u)e_{h}d{\bf s}=0
as
{\bf t}_{i}
lies on the edge
but in opposite directions on the two sides of the edge
{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}
,
and
\sum_{i=0}^{2}\partial_{{\bf t}_{i}}u=0
as
\sum_{i=0}^{2}{{\bf t}_{i}}={\bf 0}
.
By (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), the theorem is proved.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u\in H^{4,\infty}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

and
u_{h}\in V_{h}
of (
2.2
)
be the solutions of Poisson’s equation (
1.1
) and
the finite element equation (
2.3
), respectively.
It holds that

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}.

Proof.

Let
g^{j}_{h}\in V_{h}
be the discrete Green function at an interior vertex

{\bf x}_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}

satisfying

\displaystyle(\nabla g^{j}_{h},\nabla v_{h})=v_{h}({\bf x}_{j})\quad\forall v_{h}\in{V}_{h}.

Because of (
3.5
), we have, similar to (
3.6
),

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} (I_{h}u-u_{h})({\bf x}_{j})&=(\nabla(I_{h}u-u),\nabla g^{j}_{h})\\
&=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{6}}(\nabla d_{h},\nabla g^{j}_{h})_{T}+\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(\nabla(\overline{I}_{h}u-u),\nabla g^{j}_{h})_{T}\\
&=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\int_{{\bf x}_{i}{\bf x}_{i+1}}(I_{h}u-u)\partial_{{\bf t}_{i+2}^{\perp}}g^{j}_{h}d{\bf s},\end{aligned}

where
d_{h}
is defined in (
3.4
), and other notations are
defined in (
3.6
).

Repeating the proof from (
3.8
) to (
3.10
), i.e., replacing
e_{h}
there by

g^{j}_{h}
, we get

(3.11)

\displaystyle|(I_{h}u-u_{h})({\bf x}_{j})|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{T\in\overline{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h^{4}\int_{\partial T}(P_{4})(\partial_{{\bf t}}^{4}u)(\partial_{{\bf t}}g^{j}_{h})d{\bf s}\bigg|

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}\bigg(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}|g^{j}_{h}|_{H^{1}(T)}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}

(3.13)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}|\overline{g}_{h}^{j}|_{H^{1}},

where
{\bf t}
is a unit tangential vector on each edge of
T
,
and
\overline{g}_{h}^{j}
is the discrete function in
\overline{V}_{h}
, i.e., for a
{\bf x}_{j}\in\overline{\mathcal{N}}_{h}
,

\displaystyle(\nabla\overline{g}_{h}^{j},\nabla v_{h})=v_{h}({\bf x}_{j})\quad\forall v_{h}\in\overline{V}_{h}.

Therefore, for a
{\bf x}_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
, as
V_{h}\subset\overline{V}_{h}
,

\displaystyle(\nabla{g}_{h}^{j},\nabla v_{h})=v_{h}({\bf x}_{j})=(\nabla\overline{g}_{h}^{j},\nabla v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in{V}_{h}.

It implies that
{g}_{h}^{j}
is an
H^{1}
-orthogonal projection of
\overline{g}_{h}^{j}

in
V_{h}
.
By (
3.11
) and the
H^{1,\infty}
stability of
\overline{g}_{h}^{j}
from

[
26
]
, we have

\displaystyle|I_{h}u-u_{h}|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\max_{{\bf x}_{j}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}|(I_{h}u-u_{h})({\bf x}_{j})|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}|{g}_{h}^{j}|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}|\overline{g}_{h}^{j}|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}|\overline{g}_{h}^{j}|_{H^{1,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\hbox{\rm{meas}}(\Omega)\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{\infty}\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

4.
Lift the
P_{1}
solution to a
P_{3}
solution

We can choose a larger patch, and/or several patterns of patches, for
locally lifting a solution.
For a simple way of computation, we choose only one patch
R
of shape of
one regular triangle of edge-size
2h
on one honeycomb
mesh
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, cf. Figure
4
.

Figure 4.
Left: A recovery patch
R
of an equilateral triangle;
Right: a recovery patch grid
\mathcal{R}_{h}=\{\hbox{\rm{regular triangle }}\ R\}

on a regular hexagon domain.

On each patch
R
, there are 11 vertices
\{{\bf x}_{j},\ j=1,\dots,11\}\subset\mathcal{N}_{h}

of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, cf. Figure
4
.
Let
\tilde{u}_{h}\in P_{3}(R)
be the least squares solution of following

11\times 10
linear system of equations,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}({\bf x}_{j})=u_{h}({\bf x}_{j}),\quad\ j=1,\dots,11,

where
u_{h}\in V_{h}
is the
P_{1}
solution of (
2.3
).

Lemma 4.1
.

The linear system (
4.1
) of equations has a unique solution.

Proof.

The coefficient matrix of (
4.1
) is of size
11\times 10
, as

\dim P_{3}(R)=10
. To show it is a full-rank matrix, we can show
\tilde{u}_{h}=0

if

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{h}({\bf x}_{j})=0,\quad\ j=1,\dots,11.

Because the
P_{3}
polynomial
\tilde{u}_{h}
vanishes at four points on edge
e_{1}
,
cf. Figure
4
, it vanishes on the whole edge
e_{1}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{h}=l_{1}p_{2},

where
p_{2}\in P_{2}(R)
and
l_{1}\in P_{1}(R)
which vanishes only on
e_{1}
.

Because
\tilde{u}_{h}
vanishes but
l_{1}
does not vanish at three top points
of edge
e_{2}
,
cf. Figure
4
,
p_{2}
vanishes on the whole edge
e_{2}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{h}=l_{1}l_{2}p_{1},

where
p_{1}\in P_{1}(R)
and
l_{2}\in P_{1}(R)
which vanishes only on
e_{2}
.

Because
\tilde{u}_{h}
vanishes but
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
do not vanish at two internal points
of edge
e_{3}
,
cf. Figure
4
,
p_{1}
vanishes on the whole edge
e_{3}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{h}=Cl_{1}l_{2}l_{3},

where
C\in P_{0}(R)
and
l_{3}\in P_{1}(R)
which vanishes only on
e_{3}
.

There are two internal
{\bf x}_{j}
inside
R
.
Because
\tilde{u}_{h}({\bf x}_{j})=0
,
l_{1}({\bf x}_{j})\neq 0
,
l_{2}({\bf x}_{j})\neq 0

and
l_{3}({\bf x}_{j})\neq 0
,
cf. Figure
4
,
C=0
, i.e.
\tilde{u}_{h}=0
.

Thus the matrix in (
4.1
) is of full rank and the
solution
\tilde{h}
is well defined uniquely by the least squares method.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap H^{4,\infty}(\Omega)
and
\tilde{u}_{h}

be the solutions of Poisson’s equation (
1.1
) and
the lifting equation (
4.1
), respectively.
It holds that

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}+h|u-\tilde{u}_{h}|_{H^{1},h}\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}},

where
|u-\tilde{u}_{h}|_{H^{1},h}
is defined piecewise on
\mathcal{R}_{h}
.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{I}_{h}u=\tilde{u}_{h}\in P_{3}(R)
be the least squares solution of
(
4.1
).
Because the interpolation
\tilde{I}_{h}u
preserves
P_{3}
polynomials and is stable
(cf.
[
28
]
), we have

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{I}_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}+h|u-\tilde{I}_{h}u|_{H^{1},h}\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4}}.

As
\tilde{u}_{h}\in P_{3}(R)
,
\tilde{I}_{h}\tilde{u}_{h}=\tilde{u}_{h}
.
By definition (
4.1
),
\tilde{I}_{h}u=\tilde{I}_{h}I_{h}u
.
Thus, as
\tilde{I}_{h}
is stable (cf.
[
28
]
),

\displaystyle\quad\ \|u-\tilde{u}_{h}\|_{L^{2}}+h|u-\tilde{u}_{h}|_{H^{1},h}

\displaystyle\leq\|u-\tilde{I}_{h}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\tilde{I}_{h}(I_{h}u-u_{h})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+h|u-\tilde{u}_{h}|_{H^{1},h}+h|\tilde{I}_{h}(I_{h}u-u_{h})\|_{H^{1},h}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}+C\|I_{h}u-u_{h}\|_{L^{2}}+Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}+Ch|I_{h}u-u_{h}|_{H^{1},h}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{4}|u|_{H^{4,\infty}}.

The theorem is proved.
∎

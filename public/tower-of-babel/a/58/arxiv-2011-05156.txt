Asymptotics of some generalised sine-integrals
R. B. Paris

Abstract
We obtain the asymptotic expansion for large integer n n of a generalised sine-integral ∫ 0 ∞ ( sin ⁡ x x ) n ​ 𝑑 x \int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\sin\,x}{x}\right)^{n}dx by utilising the saddle-point method. This expansion is shown to agree with recent results of J. Schlage-Puchta in Commun. Korean Math. Soc. 35 (2020) 1193–1202 who used a different approach. An asymptotic estimate is obtained for another related sine-integral also involving a large power n n . Numerical results are given to illustrate the accuracy of this approximation. We also revisit the asymptotics of Ball’s integral involving the Bessel function J ν ​ ( x ) J_{\nu}(x) , which reduces to the above integral when ν = 1 / 2 \nu=1/2 . 1. Introduction

The expansion of the generalised sine-integral

I_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\sin\,x}{x}\biggr)^{\!n}dx

(1.1)

for integer
n\to\infty
has recently been considered by Schlage-Puchta
[
7
]
. However the method used seems to be unnecessarily involved and our aim here is to present a more direct computation using the well-known saddle-point method for Laplace-type integrals.
The interest in the integral
I_{n}
stems from the fact that the intersection of the unit cube with a plane orthogonal to a diagonal and passing through the midpoint has
(n-1)
-measure equal to
2\sqrt{n}\,I_{n}/\pi
. These intersections arise naturally in certain probabilistic problems; see the references cited in
[
7
]
.

The second related sine-integral we consider is given by

K_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ax}\biggl(1-\frac{\sin^{2}x}{x^{2}}\biggr)^{\!n}dx\qquad(a>0)

(1.2)

for
n\to\infty
when the parameter
a=O(1)
. An integral of this type was communicated to the author by H. Kaiser
[
3
]
. We employ a two-term saddle-point approximation to estimate the growth of
K_{n}
for large
n
and present numerical calculations to verify the accuracy of the resulting formula. In the final section we revisit the expansion for large
n
of Ball’s integral involving the Bessel function
J_{\nu}(x)
, which reduces to (
1.1
) when
\nu=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}
.

2. The asymptotic expansion of
I_{n}

We begin by writing the integral in (
1.1
) as

I_{n}=\int_{0}^{\pi}\biggl(\frac{\sin\,x}{x}\biggr)^{\!n}dx+R_{n}(x),\qquad R_{n}(x)=\int_{\pi}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\sin\,x}{x}\biggr)^{\!n}dx.

It is easily seen that

|R_{n}(x)|<\int_{\pi}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^{n}}=\frac{\pi^{1-n}}{n-1}.

The remainder term
R_{n}(x)
is therefore bounded by O(
n^{-1}\pi^{-n}
)=O(
n^{-1}e^{-n}(\pi/e)^{-n}
) and so is exponentially small as
n\to\infty
.

Let
\psi(x)=\log\,(x/\sin x)
, where
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi(\pi)=\infty
. Then the integral over
[0,\pi]
becomes

{\hat{I}}_{n}=\int_{0}^{\pi}\biggl(\frac{\sin\,x}{x}\biggr)^{\!n}dx=\int_{0}^{\pi}e^{-n\psi(x)}dx.

This integral has a saddle point at
x=0
and the integration path
[0,\pi]
is the path of steepest descent through the saddle. If we now make the standard change of variable
\psi(x)=\tau^{2}
discussed, for example, in
[
2
, p. 66]
we obtain

{\hat{I}}_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-n\tau^{2}}\,\frac{dx}{d\tau}\,d\tau.

From the expansion

\tau^{2}=\log\biggl(\frac{x}{\sin x}\biggr)=\frac{1}{6}x^{2}+\frac{1}{180}x^{4}+\frac{1}{2835}x^{6}+\frac{1}{37800}x^{8}+\frac{1}{467775}x^{10}+\cdots\

valid for
|x|<\pi
,
we find by inversion of this series using
Mathematica
that

x=\sqrt{6}\biggl\{\tau-\frac{1}{10}\tau^{3}-\frac{13}{4200}\tau^{5}+\frac{9}{14000}\tau^{7}+\frac{17597}{77616000}\tau^{9}+\frac{4873}{218400000}\tau^{11}+\cdots\ \biggr\}

whence

\frac{dx}{d\tau}=\sqrt{6}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}\tau^{2k}\qquad(|\tau|<\tau_{0}).

(2.1)

The first few coefficients
b_{k}
are

b_{0}=1,\quad b_{1}=-\frac{3}{10},\quad b_{2}=-\frac{13}{840},\quad b_{3}=\frac{9}{2000},\quad b_{4}=\frac{17597}{862400},

b_{5}=\frac{53603}{218400000},\quad b_{6}=-\frac{124996631}{1629936000000},\quad b_{7}=-\frac{159706933}{4366252800000},\ldots\ .

The circle of convergence of the series (
2.1
) is determined by the nearest point in the mapping
x\mapsto\tau
where
dx/d\tau
is singular (the point
x=\pi
maps to
\infty
in the
\tau
-plane); that is, where
\psi^{\prime}(x)=0
. This yields
x=\tan\,x
, with the root
x_{0}\doteq 1.43030\pi
, and hence

\tau_{0}=|\log\,x_{0}/|\sin x_{0}|+\pi i|^{1/2}\doteq 1.86894
.
Then we have

{\hat{I}}_{n}\sim\sqrt{6}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-n\tau^{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{k}\tau^{2k}\,d\tau=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{b_{k}}{n^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-w}w^{k-1/2}dw

=\sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{2n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{c_{k}}{n^{k}}\qquad(n\to\infty),

(2.2)

where the coefficients
c_{k}
are defined by

c_{k}:=b_{k}\frac{\Gamma(k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})}{\Gamma(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})}.

It follows that since we have extended the integration path in (
2.2
) beyond the circle of convergence of (
2.1
) the resulting asymptotic series is divergent.

An integral of a similar nature is

J_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{1-\cos x}{\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x^{2}}\biggr)^{\!n}dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\sin\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x}{\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x}\biggr)^{\!2n}dx=2I_{2n}.

3. An asymptotic estimate of another sine-integral

In this section we consider the following integral

K_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-ax}\biggl(1-\frac{\sin^{2}x}{x^{2}}\biggr)^{\!n}dx\qquad(a>0)

(3.1)

for
n\to\infty
(not necessarily an integer) when the parameter
a=O(1)
.
We express
K_{n}
as a Laplace-type integral in the form

K_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-n\psi(x)}f(x)\,dx,

where

\psi(x)=-\log\biggl(1-\frac{\sin^{2}x}{x^{2}}\biggr),\qquad f(x)=e^{-ax}.

For large
n
the exponential factor in the integrand consists of a series of peaks situated at
x=k\pi
, (
k=1,2,\ldots
) of decreasing height controlled by the decay of
f(x)
; see Fig. 1 for a typical example. This is in marked contrast to the situation pertaining to the integral
I_{n}
in (
1.1
), where the second and successive peaks are of height O(
(k\pi)^{-n})

(
k\geq 1
) and so are exponentially smaller than the (half) peak in
[0,\pi]
.

Figure 1:
Plot of the integrand in (

) when
n=5000

a=1/6

Routine calculations show that

\psi^{\prime\prime}(k\pi)=\frac{2}{(k\pi)^{2}},\quad\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}(k\pi)=-\frac{12}{(k\pi)^{3}},\quad\psi^{iv}(k\pi)=\frac{82}{(k\pi)^{4}}-\frac{8}{(k\pi)^{2}}.

Application of the two-term saddle-point approximation to the
k
th peak then yields the approximate contribution
[
5
, p. 48]
,
[
6
, §1.2.3]

2\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2n\psi^{\prime\prime}(k\pi)}}\biggl\{1+\frac{c_{2}}{n}\biggr\}e^{-k\pi a}=k\pi\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{n}}\biggl\{1+\frac{c_{2}}{n}\biggr\}e^{-k\pi a},

where

c_{2}=\frac{1}{2\psi^{\prime\prime}}\biggl\{\frac{2f^{\prime\prime}}{f}-2\frac{\psi^{\prime\prime\prime}}{\psi^{\prime\prime}}\,\frac{f^{\prime}}{f}+\frac{5\psi^{\prime\prime\prime 2}}{6\psi^{\prime\prime 2}}-\frac{\psi^{iv}}{2\psi^{\prime\prime}}\biggr\}

with all derivatives being evaluated at
x=k\pi
. This yields

c_{2}=\frac{1}{4}\biggl\{2(1+a^{2})(k\pi)^{2}-12ak\pi+9\biggr\}.

Summing over all the peaks we then obtain

K_{n}\sim\pi\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{n}}\biggl\{\sigma_{1}+\frac{1}{8n}\biggl(2\pi^{2}(1+a^{2})\sigma_{3}-12\pi a\sigma_{2}+9\sigma_{1}\biggr)\biggr\},

where

\sigma_{m}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{m}e^{-k\pi a}.

We have

\sigma_{1}=\frac{1}{4\sinh^{2}\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a},\quad\sigma_{2}=\frac{\cosh\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a}{4\sinh^{3}\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a},\quad\sigma_{3}=\frac{2+\cosh\pi a}{8\sinh^{4}\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a}.

Hence we obtain our final estimate in the form

K_{n}\sim\frac{\pi^{3/2}}{4n^{1/2}}\biggl\{1+\frac{T_{1}}{8n}\biggr\}\mbox{cosech}^{2}\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a\qquad(n\to\infty),

(3.2)

where

T_{1}=9-12\pi a\,\mbox{coth}\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a+\pi^{2}(1+a^{2})\frac{(2+\cosh\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a)}{\sinh^{2}\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a}

with
a>0
fixed and of O(1).

In Table 2 we show computed values of
K_{n}
compared with the asymptotic estimate (
3.2
) for different values of
n
and the parameter
a
. It is seen that the agreement is quite good and improves with increasing
n
. However, since
\psi^{\prime\prime}(k\pi)
scales like
k^{-2}
, the peaks progressively broaden as
k
increases with the consequence that
the saddle-point approximation eventually breaks down. In addition, the parameter
a
cannot be too small on account of
the fact that the envelope of the minima of the integrand, given by
e^{-ax}(1-1/x^{2})^{n}
, presents a maximum value at

x\simeq(2n/a)^{1/3}
equal to approximately
\exp\,[-\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{3}{2}}$}(2na^{2})^{1/3}]
. We require this last quantity to be small for the satisfactory estimation of each peak. This results in the condition
a\gg(2n)^{-1/2}
.

A closely related integral is

{\hat{K}}_{n}=\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-ax}\biggl(1-\frac{\cos^{2}x}{x^{2}}\biggr)^{\!n}dx.

The peaks in the graph of the integrand are similar to those indicated in Fig. 1 but now occur at
x=(k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\pi
,
k=0,1,2,\ldots\
. The lower limit of integration is chosen and to lie in the interval
(\delta,\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi-\delta)
(with
\delta>0
so as to avoid the origin and
\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi
).
With
\psi(x)=-\log\,(1-\cos^{2}x/x^{2})
we find

\psi^{\prime\prime}((k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})\pi)=\frac{2}{(k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})^{2}\pi^{2}}.

Then by similar arguments we obtain the leading asymptotic approximation

{\hat{K}}_{n}\sim\pi e^{-\pi a/2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})e^{-k\pi a}=\frac{\pi^{3/2}\cosh\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a}{4n^{1/2}\sinh^{2}\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}\pi a}\qquad(n\to\infty).

4. An asymptotic expansion for Ball’s integral

Ball’s integral is given by
[
1
]

L(\nu;n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)|J_{\nu}(x)|}{(x/2)^{\nu}}\biggr)^{\!n}x^{2\nu-1}dx,\qquad n\geq 2,\ \nu\geq\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$},

(4.1)

where
J_{\nu}(x)
is the Bessel function of the first kind and
n
is not necessarily an integer. In the case
\nu=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}

and integer values of
n
the integral (
4.1
) (without the modulus signs) reduces to that in (
1.1
), since
\sqrt{\pi/(2x)}J_{1/2}(x)=\sin x/x
. The expansion of
L(\nu;n)
for
n\to\infty
has been derived in
[
4
]
; here we revisit this result by means of the transformation used in Section 1.

We divide the integration path into the intervals
[0,j_{\nu,1}]
and
[j_{\nu,1},\infty)
, where
j_{\nu,1}
denotes the first zero of
J_{\nu}(x)
. In
[0,j_{\nu,1}]
, we have
J_{\nu}(x)\geq 0
and the modulus signs may be dropped in this interval, where

\sigma(x):=\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)J_{\nu}(x)}{(x/2)^{\nu}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-x^{2}/4)^{k}}{k!(\nu+1)_{k}}

with
(a)_{k}=\Gamma(a+k)/\Gamma(a)
being Pochhammer’s symbol.
We set
\psi(x)=-\log\,\sigma(x)
in the interval
[0,j_{\nu,1}]
,
so that the integral (
4.1
) becomes

L(\nu;n)=\int_{0}^{j_{\nu,1}}e^{-n\psi(x)}x^{2\nu-1}dx+R_{n}(x),

(4.2)

where

R_{n}(x)=\int_{j_{\nu,1}}^{\infty}\biggl(\frac{\Gamma(1+\nu)|J_{\nu}(x)|}{(x/2)^{\nu}}\biggr)^{\!n}x^{2\nu-1}dx.

The tail of the integral
R_{n}(x)
satisfies the bound

|R_{n}(x)|<(2^{\nu}\Gamma(1+\nu))^{n}\int_{j_{\nu,1}}^{\infty}\frac{dx}{x^{(n-2)\nu+1}}=\biggl(\frac{2^{\nu}\Gamma(1+\nu)}{j_{\nu,1}^{\nu}}\biggr)^{n}\,\frac{j_{\nu,1}^{2}}{(n-2)\nu}

[j_{\nu,1},\infty)

is the second zero of
J_{\nu}^{\prime}(x)
. Defining the quantity

\xi(\nu):=\frac{2^{\nu}\Gamma(1+\nu)}{j_{\nu,1}^{\nu}},

and noting that

when
\nu=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}

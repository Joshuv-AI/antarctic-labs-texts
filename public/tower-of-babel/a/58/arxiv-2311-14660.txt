Vanishing Mach Number Limit of Stochastic Compressible Flows
Gui-Qiang G. Chen, Michele Coti Zelati, Chin Ching Yeung

Abstract
. We study the vanishing Mach number limit for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations with γ \gamma -type pressure laws, with focus on the one-dimensional case. We prove that, if the stochastic term vanishes with respect to the Mach number sufficiently fast, the deviation from the incompressible state of the solutions (for γ ≥ 1 \gamma\geq 1 ) and the invariant measures (for γ = 1 \gamma=1 ) is governed by a linear stochastic acoustic system in the limit. In particular, the critically sufficient decay rate for the stochastic term is slower than the corresponding results with deterministic external forcing due to the martingale structure of the noise term, and the blow-up of the noise term for the fluctuation system can be allowed.

1.
Introduction

We are concerned with the vanishing Mach number limit of the stochastic Navier-Stokes
equations with
\gamma
-type pressure laws:

(NS)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\rho+(\rho u)_{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=0,\\[2.84526pt]
\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}(\rho u)+(\rho u^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\rho^{\gamma})_{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=u_{xx}\,\mathrm{d}t+\rho\sigma_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\end{cases}

where
\rho=\rho(t,x)
and
u=u(t,x)
represent the density and velocity of the fluid
in the position
x\in(0,1)
at time
t\geq 0
, respectively.
The system is complemented with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions:

(1.1)

\displaystyle u(t,0)=u(t,1)=0\qquad\mbox{ for all $t>0$},

and initial data:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\rho(0,x)=\rho_{0}(x),\quad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\qquad\text{ for all $x\in(0,1)$}.

The conservation of mass guaranteed by the continuity equation in system (
NS
) allows to fix,
without loss of generality, the initial mass to
1
, implying that

(1.3)

\int_{0}^{1}\rho(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x=1\qquad\text{ for all $t>0$}.

The constant
\varepsilon>0
represents the Mach number of the flow, which is (proportional to) the ratio of the typical flow
speed and the speed of sound propagating in the flow.
Without forcing, in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
, the fluid behaves as an incompressible one.
In the one-dimensional case, we expect the velocity to vanish due to the boundary conditions,
and the density to approach a constant because of the continuity equation.
In the presence of forcing, this necessarily imposes
the coefficient
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
to vanish as
\varepsilon\to 0
: system (
NS
) is therefore forced
by a collection of independent white noise processes, suitably colored in space
in an
\varepsilon
-dependent fashion through the coefficient
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{\beta}\sigma
,
with
\beta>0
and
\sigma
fixed and independent of
\varepsilon
.

The goal of this paper is to analyze the higher-order fluctuations in the vanishing Mach number limit.
For this purpose, we asymptotically expand the solutions
in the form
1
1

1

The first-order term of the density
\rho
vanishes by the momentum equation.
:

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\rho=1+\varepsilon^{2}\rho_{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\rho_{3}+\cdots=1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\\[2.84526pt]
u=\varepsilon u_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}u_{2}+\cdots=\varepsilon\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}.\end{cases}

We can now rewrite the system in the new variables
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
as the following
fluctuation system
:

(FS)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}+\big(\varepsilon(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t=0,\\[4.2679pt]
\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}+\big(\varepsilon\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-2}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t=\frac{(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{xx}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon^{\beta-1}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\end{cases}

with initial data:

(1.5)

\bar{\rho}(0,x)=\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon}(x),\quad\bar{u}(0,x)=\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\qquad\text{ for all $x\in(0,1)$},

and boundary conditions:

(1.6)

\bar{u}(t,0)=\bar{u}(t,1)=0\qquad\text{ for all $t>0$}.

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, system (
FS
) is formally well-approximated by the following linear acoustic system:

(AS)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}r_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=0,\\[7.11317pt]
\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\,\mathrm{d}t=(v_{\varepsilon})_{xx}\,\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon^{\beta-1}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},\end{cases}

equipped with initial data:

(1.7)

r_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=r_{0,\varepsilon}(x),\quad v_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=v_{0,\varepsilon}(x)\qquad\text{ for all $x\in(0,1)$},

and boundary conditions:

(1.8)

v_{\varepsilon}(t,0)=v_{\varepsilon}(t,1)=0\qquad\text{ for all }t>0.

The acoustic system (
AS
) is formally a stochastically forced linear damped wave equation:

\,\mathrm{d}(r_{\varepsilon})_{t}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(r_{\varepsilon})_{xx}\,\mathrm{d}t=(r_{\varepsilon})_{txx}\,\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon^{\beta-2}\sigma_{x}\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

The acoustic system (
AS
) is mathematically easier to study, compared to the original nonlinear system
(
FS
), and it reveals the fine wave-like structure of the system in the vanishing Mach number limit.
Justifying this limit is the main goal of this paper.

1.1.
Stochastic Navier-Stokes equations

The mathematical literature concerning the stochastic

Navier-Stokes equations is vast,
which has seen tremendous growth in the last decades.
We limit ourselves to recording a few relevant results concerning the existence and long-time behavior of solutions.
In the two-dimensional incompressible setting, the existence and uniqueness
of solutions have been established in various instances (different domains, boundary conditions,
regularity of initial data, and structure of the noise term);
see
[
28
,
13
]

and the references therein.
Moreover, the ergodicity and uniqueness of an invariant measure for the corresponding Markov semigroup
are also by now well-established under minimal requirements on the noise structure
[
22
,
18
]
.
The picture in the three-dimensional case is complicated by the well-known well-posedness issues.
The existence of global weak solutions is known (see
[
20
]
and the references therein),
and it has also been shown that there exists a strong Feller Markov selection with a unique invariant
measure
[
14
,
19
]
.

In comparison, the theory for compressible fluids is much less developed.
Most of the results concern the barotropic cases.
The existence of solutions has been obtained by using a
pathwise approach in
[
38
]
in the one-dimensional case,
in
[
39
]
for a special two-dimensional case, and in
[
16
]

for the three-dimensional case.
Furthermore, the existence of global weak solutions with more general noise terms
and adiabatic exponent
\gamma>\frac{3}{2}
in the three-dimensional setting has
been obtained in
[
6
,
36
,
41
]
independently
by using a several-layer approximation scheme, similar to the one used in the deterministic
case in
[
17
]
.
In addition, the existence and uniqueness of local-in-time (up to a positive stopping time)
pathwise strong solutions have been obtained in
[
4
]

for the barotropic Navier-Stokes equations with multiplicative noise on a torus.
Besides, the incompressible limit of the stochastic isotropic Navier-Stokes has been studied
in
[
3
]
.

There are only a few results available in the study of invariant measures and stationary solutions.
In
[
1
]
, the existence of invariant measures has been proved for an approximated
version of the one-dimensional stochastic compressible Navier-Stokes equations in Lagrangian coordinates,
while
[
12
]
provides the construction of an invariant measure for system (
NS
)
for the ideal gas pressure law, with
\gamma=1
.
In the three-dimensional case, stationary solutions to the isentropic Navier-Stokes equations
have been constructed in
[
2
]
.
However, due to the uniqueness issues, stationarity is not intended in terms of an invariant measure
for a related Markov semigroup. In this regard, a Markov selection
for the three-dimensional system has been constructed in
[
5
]
.

1.2.
Vanishing Mach number limit

Extensive research has been dedicated to the investigation of the low Mach number limit within the realm
of smooth solutions for the deterministic compressible Euler system,
even extending to multidimensional scenarios.
This rich body of work includes seminal contributions by Klainerman-Majda
[
27
]
, Majda
[
31
]
,
Metivier-Schochet
[
34
]
, Schochet
[
35
]
, and a plethora of related references.

Chen-Christoforou-Zhang in
[
11
]
provided a rigorous mathematical foundation for the zero Mach limit,
specifically focusing on global discontinuous solutions, characterized by bounded variation,
of the compressible Euler equations. Their results illustrate the behavior of second-order coefficients
in the asymptotic expansion concerning the Mach number, highlighting the relevance of the linear acoustic
system within the Euler framework. In the context of the stochastic compressible Navier-Stokes equations,
the present study builds upon this framework, further developing and extending it.
Our work entails the derivation of the corresponding stochastic linear acoustic system,
as described in (
AS
), and the subsequent validation of the vanishing Mach limit.

For additional insights into the low Mach number limit for global weak solutions pertaining
to the isentropic Navier-Stokes equations, we
refer to the work of Lions-Masmoudi
[
30
]
for the deterministic setting
and to the work of Breit-Feireisl-Hofmanova
[
3
]
for the stochastic case.

1.3.
Main results

We fix a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\Sigma,\mathbb{P},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0})

satisfying the usual conditions such that it
supports a sequence of independent real-valued Wiener processes
(W^{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
.
Let
\sigma=(\sigma_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset H^{1}_{0}(0,1)\cap H^{2}(0,1)
such that

(1.9)

\|\sigma_{xx}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}|(\sigma_{k})_{xx}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty.

This regularity assumption is tacitly assumed throughout this paper.
The noise term is defined as in the time-integrated sense as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon^{\beta}\rho\sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon^{\beta}\rho\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s},

where integrals are understood in the Itô’s sense.

The basic energy functional for the compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
NS
) can be written as

(1.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}+H(\rho)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rho_{x}u}{\rho}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{\rho_{x}^{2}}{\rho^{3}}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
H(\rho)
is the pressure potential given by

(1.11)

\displaystyle H(\rho)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\begin{cases}\rho\log\rho-\rho+1&\text{ if }\gamma=1,\\[2.84526pt]
\frac{1}{\gamma-1}(\rho^{\gamma}-\gamma(\rho-1))&\text{ if }\gamma>1.\end{cases}

The energy structure revealed by
\mathcal{E}
is crucial in our analysis.
The first main result is a finite-time convergence of solutions of system (
FS
) to those of system (
AS
),
in the whole range of pressure exponents
\gamma\geq 1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\beta>\frac{1}{2}
and
T>0
. Assume
(\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}

and
(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
are collections of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable
random variables in
H^{1}(0,1)\times H^{1}_{0}(0,1)
such that

\int_{0}^{1}\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad 1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon}>0,\quad\int_{0}^{1}r_{0,\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=0\qquad\,\,\,\,\mbox{almost surely}.

Furthermore, assume that there exist
\alpha>1
and
\lambda>0
such that

(1.12)

\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{E}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\varepsilon\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})^{p}\right]\lesssim p!\,\lambda^{p}\varepsilon^{\alpha p}\qquad\text{ for any }p\geq 1\text{ and }\ \varepsilon\in(0,1].

Then there exists
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1]
such that the corresponding solutions

(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
of system (
FS
) and
(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})

of system (

) satisfy the convergence estimate

(1.13)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(\gamma\|\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-r_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\Big]\lesssim\Big(\mathbb{E}\big[\gamma\|\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon}-r_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}+\|\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon}-v_{0,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\big]\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}+\varepsilon^{2\beta-1}

We also translate the above result to the convergence of the invariant measures for

The phase space for system (

1.4.
Techniques and key points of the analysis

Before presenting the detailed analysis in the subsequent sections,
we summarize here the novel ingredients and key observations that constitute the foundation of our approach.

\diamond

Polynomial and exponential moment estimates for the energy for all
\gamma\geq 1
.

These generalize the results obtained in
[
12
]
where only the isothermal pressure law (
\gamma=1
)
is considered (see Proposition
2.3
).
Instead of trying to control the source terms from the random forcing
by using the dissipative terms (which are degenerate for
\gamma>1
)
in the energy balance (see (
2.2
) below) as in
[
12
]
,
we directly use
\mathcal{E}
to estimate them, resulting in
the bounds that grow exponentially in time.

\diamond

Polynomial moment estimates on the upper and lower bounds of the density at small Mach numbers.

These estimates are a consequence of the exponential moment
estimates for
\mathcal{E}
. The estimates allow us to obtain fundamental controls on the fluctuation variables,
which play a critical role on the finite-time convergence analysis
(see Proposition
2.7
).

\diamond

Exponential contraction property of the acoustic system due to the viscosity.

We prove that the acoustic system has a fine structure of global exponential contraction.
This property allows the analysis of the large-time behavior of the system and is of paramount importance
in translating the finite-time convergence analysis to the study of the convergence of
invariant measures (which could informally be considered as capturing the information
on the behavior of the system when
t=\infty
). There is a mismatch between the topology
used to prove the finite-time convergence and the contraction property:
In order to overcome this issue,
we employ an
\varepsilon
-weighted
H^{1}
norm that is stronger than the
L^{2}
norm,
but coincides with the
L^{2}
norm in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
.

\diamond

Novel estimates of the invariant measures.

We prove that statistically stationary solutions distributed as
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}

satisfy the assumption of Theorem
1.1
.
These are some novel estimates on the invariant measures of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.
In particular, we obtain the finite exponential moments of
\mathcal{E}
.
We believe these estimates are of independent interest.

2.
Energy Structure for the Navier-Stokes Equations

In this section, we study the properties of solutions of problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) for system (

) in detail.
Considering the pathwise transformation

\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon^{\beta}\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s},

we can transform system (
NS
) into a system with random coefficients but without stochastic integrals.
Then, using the ideas in
[
33
]
, we can obtain the existence and uniqueness
of strong pathwise solutions of problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
)
for system (
NS
) (see also
[
37
,
38
,

,

]

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume
(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is a collection of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable
random variables in
H^{1}\times H^{1}_{0}
such that

\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0,\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=1,\quad\rho_{0,\varepsilon}>0\qquad\,\,\mbox{almost surely}.

Then there exists a unique adapted strong pathwise solution
(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
of system (

) such that

\rho_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,\infty)\times[0,1])\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty;H^{1}),\,\,\,\,u_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}_{0})\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty;H^{2})\qquad\,\mbox{almost surely}.

Moreover, the map

is weakly continuous
and, for every

\rho(t,\cdot)^{-1}\in L^{\infty}(0,1)\qquad\,\mbox{almost surely}.

2.1.
The main energy functional and its properties

The classical
a priori
estimates of solutions of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations
usually begin with the physical energy. However, in the one-dimensional case, we can use
the energy
\mathcal{E}
defined in (
1.10
),
which is more powerful as it provides additional estimates
on the
H^{1}
-norm of the density.
A similar structure in Lagrangian coordinates appeared in
[
26
]
.
The energy has been used in
[
24
,
25
]
for deterministic isothermal flows to study
the large-time behavior of solutions. Its generalization
for the density-dependent viscosities, which even applies to some multidimensional cases,
is sometimes known as the BD-entropy that was introduced in
Bresch-Desjardins
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
7
]
.
The entropy has then been used critically to prove
various
a priori
estimates, particularly on the lower bound of the density
in the one-dimensional case (see e.g.
[
33
,
29
]
).
We now record some properties of
\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(\rho,u)\in H^{1}\times H^{1}_{0}
such that
\int_{0}^{1}\rho(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
and
\rho(x)>0
. Then

(i)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\rho u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 4\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)
,

(ii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\rho_{x}|^{2}}{\rho^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 8\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)
,

(iii)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}H(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)
,

(iv)

\displaystyle\|\rho^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\big(1+2\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)
,

(v)

\displaystyle\max\{\|\rho^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\}\leq\exp(\sqrt{8\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)})
.

Proof.

The first two estimates follow respectively from the pointwise inequalities

\left|\frac{\rho_{x}u}{\rho}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\rho_{x}|^{2}}{\rho^{3}},\qquad\left|\frac{\rho_{x}u}{\rho}\right|\leq\rho u^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{|\rho_{x}|^{2}}{\rho^{3}}.

The third estimate follows from the fact that

\frac{1}{2}\rho u^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rho_{x}u}{\rho}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{|\rho_{x}|^{2}}{\rho^{3}}\geq 0,

which can be seen from the above two inequalities.

To prove the last two estimates, we note that
there exists
x_{0}\in[0,1]
such that
\rho(x_{0})=1
, since
\int_{0}^{1}\rho(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
and
\rho(x)
is continuous.
Hence, for all
x\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\big|\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x)-1\big|

\displaystyle=\big|\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x)-\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}(x_{0})\big|=\Big|\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\big(\rho^{-\frac{1}{2}}(y)\big)_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\big|\rho^{-\frac{3}{2}}\rho_{x}\big|\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|\rho_{x}|^{2}}{\rho^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)}.

The triangle inequality then implies the desired estimate. Likewise, we have

\displaystyle|\log\rho(x)|

\displaystyle=|\log\rho(x)-\log\rho(x_{0})|=\Big|\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\big(\log\rho(y)\big)_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big|\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\rho(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho_{x}^{2}}{\rho^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The first integral in the inequality above is
1
due to the mass condition,
while the second integral is bounded by
8\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)
.
Exponentiating both sides gives the last estimate.
∎

2.2.
Energy estimates for the Navier-Stokes equations

For solutions of system (
NS

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume
(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}

\mathcal{F}_{0}

\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{0,\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=1,\quad\,\rho_{0,\varepsilon}>0\qquad\mbox{almost surely}.

Then the corresponding solution
(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
of system (
NS
) satisfies the following properties

(i)

For all
p\geq 2
, there exists
C_{p}>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
T\geq 1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\gamma-3}{2}}(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{p}\Big]

(2.1)

\displaystyle\,\leq 3^{p-1}{\rm e}^{T}\,\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})^{p}\big]+C_{p}\,{\rm e}^{T}\,T^{p+1}\varepsilon^{2\beta p}\|\sigma\|^{2p}_{L^{\infty}},

with
C_{p}=O(1296^{p}p^{p})
as
p\to\infty
.

(ii)

For

(2.2)

\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T):=(4000T\varepsilon^{2\beta}\|\sigma\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}})^{-1},

there exists
C>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
T\geq 1
, and
0<\eta<\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\eta\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{(\gamma-3)/2}(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\big)\Big)\Big]

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{T}}{3}\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(3\eta\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})\big)\big]+\frac{CT{\rm e}^{T}}{1-\eta/\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T)}.

Proof.

For the first estimate, we employ the Itô’s formula to perform a direct computation
to obtain the energy balance:

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma-3}|(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\Big)\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

Note that the last two terms are interpreted as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\Big)\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\Big)\sigma_{k}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t};

also see
[
33
]
for the similar computation in the deterministic case without the Itô’s correction
term and
[
12
]
for the computation in the stochastic case with
\gamma=1
.
Therefore, for all
p\in[2,\infty)
and
T\geq 1
, it holds almost surely that

\displaystyle\bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma-3}|(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)\bigg)^{p}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})+\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big)\bigg)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\bigg(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})^{p}+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big)^{p}\bigg).

We now estimate the last two terms in the bracket on the right-hand side. For the first term, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{\varepsilon^{2{p\beta}}}{2^{p}}\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2{p\beta}}}{2^{p}}\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2{p\beta}}}{2^{p}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p}T^{p}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2{p\beta}}}{2^{p}}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p}T^{p}.

Next, we estimate the second term involving a (local) martingale.
For a local martingale
(N_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, we write
(\langle N\rangle_{t})_{t\geq 0}

for its quadratic variation process.
Now, by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality, there exists
G_{p}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq G_{p}\,\varepsilon^{p\beta}\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big\langle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}\Big\rangle^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=G_{p}\,\varepsilon^{p\beta}\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq G_{p}\,\varepsilon^{p\beta}\,\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 12G_{p}\,\varepsilon^{p\beta}\,\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 12G_{p}\,\varepsilon^{p\beta}\,T^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{p}\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 3^{-(p-1)}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]+\frac{144^{p}}{4}3^{p-1}G_{p}^{2}T^{p-1}\varepsilon^{2p\beta}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p}.

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\gamma-3}{2}}|(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]+3^{p-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})^{p}\Big]+\Big(\frac{1296^{p}}{36}G_{p}^{2}T^{p-1}+\frac{3^{p-1}}{2^{p}}T\Big)T^{p-1}\varepsilon^{2\beta p}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p}.

The assertion now follows from the Gronwall inequality.

In order to
show the asymptotic estimate of the constant in (
2.1
), we first observe that

N_{t}:=\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

is a continuous martingale. To see this, we compute similar to the above that,
for all
t\geq\tau\geq 0
and
m\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|N_{t}-N_{\tau}|^{m}\big]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\beta m}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\tau}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}\Big)^{m}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq G_{m}\,\varepsilon^{\beta m}\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\tau}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}\bigg)^{\frac{m}{2}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 12G_{m}\,\varepsilon^{\beta m}\,\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m}\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{\tau}^{t}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 12G_{m}\,\varepsilon^{\beta m}\,\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{m}\,(t-\tau)^{\frac{m}{2}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{s\leq r\leq t}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot))\Big)^{\frac{m}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=C_{m}\varepsilon^{\beta m}(t-\tau)^{\frac{m}{2}}.

Since
m\geq 2
can be arbitrary large, by the Kolmogorov continuity theorem,

N_{t}
is almost surely Hölder continuous with Hölder exponent as any number less than
\frac{1}{2}
.
In particular, since
N_{t}
is continuous, the asymptotic estimate of the constant in (
2.1
)
follows from the fact that
G_{p}=O(p^{\frac{p}{2}})
as
p\to\infty
, thanks to
[
15
]
.

We now show (
(ii)
).
Define

Y_{t}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{\gamma-3}{2}}(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big).

Then

\displaystyle\exp(\eta Y_{t})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\eta^{k}}{k!}\mathbb{E}\big[Y_{t}^{k}\big]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\eta^{k}}{k!}\Big(3^{k-1}{\rm e}^{t}\,\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})^{k}\big]+C_{k}{\rm e}^{t}t^{k+1}\varepsilon^{2\beta k}\|\sigma\|^{2k}_{L^{\infty}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{t}}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(3\eta)^{k}}{k!}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})^{k}\big]+Ct{\rm e}^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big(1296t\eta\varepsilon^{2\beta}\|\sigma\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{k}}{k!}k^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{t}}{3}\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(3\eta\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})\big)\big]+Ct{\rm e}^{t}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big(4000t\eta\varepsilon^{2\beta}\|\sigma\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\big)^{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{t}}{3}\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(3\eta\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})\big)\big]+\frac{Ct{\rm e}^{t}}{1-4000t\eta\varepsilon^{2\beta}\|\sigma\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}},

if
0<\eta<\min\{\frac{\eta(\varepsilon)}{3},\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T)\}
.
Note that we have used the Stirling’s approximation
k^{k}\sim k!\,{\rm e}^{k}(2\pi k)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

for large
k
in the penultimate inequality.
This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

In the isothermal case
\gamma=1
, the estimates in Proposition
2.3

can be improved. In particular, the factor
{\rm e}^{T}
in the first estimate can be replaced by a
T
-independent
constant and the smallness of
\eta
needed in the second estimate can also be chosen to be independent of

;
see
[
12
]
.

2.3.
Estimates for the fluctuation system

The energy estimates established for system (
NS
) in §
2.2

constitute the basis for the analysis
of the fluctuation system (
FS
).
The estimates here will be critical to establish
the finite-time convergence results in Theorem
1.1
.
We first restate Proposition
2.1
in the new variables
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
defined in (
1.4
).

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume that
(\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is a collection of

\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable random variables in
H^{1}\times H^{1}_{0}
such that

\int_{0}^{1}\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\quad 1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon}>0\qquad\,\,\,\mbox{almost surely}.

Then there exists a unique adapted strong pathwise solution
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
to (
FS
) such that

\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,\infty)\times[0,1])\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty;H^{1}),\,\,\,\,\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}_{0})\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty;H^{2})\qquad\mbox{almost surely}.

Moreover, the map:
t\mapsto\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)\in H^{1}
is weakly continuous and, for each
t>0
,

(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot))^{-1}\in L^{\infty}(0,1)\qquad\,\,\mbox{almost surely}.

We now further assume that (
1.12
) holds.
In particular, this implies that there exists
C>0
such that, for all
p\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
for all
0\leq\eta<\lambda^{-1}\varepsilon^{-\alpha}
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\exp\big(\eta\mathcal{E}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\varepsilon\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})\big)\right]\leq\frac{C}{1-\eta\lambda\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\qquad\mbox{for any $p\geq 1,\varepsilon\in(0,1],\eta\in(0,\lambda^{-1}\varepsilon^{-\alpha})$}.

In fact, the assumption is equivalent to asserting that

\mathcal{E}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\varepsilon\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})
has an exponential tail.
That is, there exist
\tilde{C},\tilde{\lambda}>0
such that, for all
R>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{E}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\varepsilon\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})\geq R\right]\leq\tilde{C}{\rm e}^{-\frac{R}{\tilde{\lambda}\varepsilon^{\alpha}}}.

Remark 2.6
.

The assumption on the initial data covers some important cases of interest.
For example, if the initial data are deterministic and bounded
in
H^{1}\times H^{1}_{0}
uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
,
then (
1.12
) is satisfied with
\alpha=2
.
Also, as we will see later, for
\gamma=1
,
if
(\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})
is a statistically stationary solution of
the fluctuation system (
FS
), then the assumption is satisfied with
\alpha=2\beta
.

We now quantify better the estimates of solutions of system (
FS
).

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
T\geq 1
, and let
(\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})
be
satisfying (
1.12
) as above. Then

(i)

For all
p\geq 1
, there exists
C>0
, independent of
(\varepsilon,\alpha,\beta)
, such that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\max\big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{p}\|_{L^{\infty}},\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{-p}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\}\Big]\leq C

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
depending only on
p,T,\alpha,\beta
, and
\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

(ii)

For all
p\geq 1
, there exists
C_{p}=C_{p}(p,\gamma,\lambda,T)>0
, independent of
(\alpha,\beta,\varepsilon)
,
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]

(
for
\varepsilon_{0}>0
depending on
p,T,\alpha,\beta
,
and
\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}
)
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}\bigg]

(2.7)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\big(\varepsilon^{(\alpha-2)p}+\varepsilon^{(2\beta-2)p}\big),

(iii)

There exists
C=C(T)
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
\delta>0
with

(2.8)

\displaystyle\delta<\min\big\{\frac{1}{6\lambda\varepsilon^{\alpha-2}},\,\frac{\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T)\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\big\}

and
\eta_{0}
defined in
Proposition
2.3
,

(2.9)

\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\delta\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)\Big]\leq C.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps.

1. We first go back to the original variables
\rho_{\varepsilon}=1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}
and
u_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
, which solve (
NS
)
with the initial data
(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\varepsilon\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})
.

Fix
p\geq 1
. To prove (
2.6
),
since
\alpha,\beta>0
, there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
,

8p^{2}\leq\min\{(6\lambda\varepsilon^{\alpha})^{-1},\frac{\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T)}{2}\}
, where
\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T)
is defined in (
2.2
).
For all such
\varepsilon
, by Proposition
2.2
,
we have

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{p}\|_{L^{\infty}},\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{-p}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big\}\leq\exp\Big(\big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}8p^{2}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big).

We note that the function
\mu:[1,\infty)\to[1,\infty)
, defined
by
\mu(x)=\exp((\log x)^{\frac{1}{2}})
for all
x\geq 1
, is concave and strictly increasing.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\max\Big\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{p}\|_{L^{\infty}},\,\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho^{-p}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}8p^{2}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\mu(\exp(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}8p^{2}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})))\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\mathbb{E}[\exp(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}8p^{2}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}))])

\displaystyle\leq\mu(\frac{{\rm e}^{T}}{3}\mathbb{E}[\exp(\frac{3}{6\lambda\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon}))]+\frac{CT{\rm e}^{T}}{1-(\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T)/2)/\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T)})

\displaystyle=\mu(\frac{{\rm e}^{T}}{3}\mathbb{E}[\exp(\frac{1}{2\lambda\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon}))]+2CT{\rm e}^{T})\leq C,

by means of (
(ii)
) and (
2.5
).

2. We now tackle (
2.7
) and start
by estimating
\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{2p}]
.
By Taylor’s theorem, for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
\xi
between
1
and
1+z
such that

\displaystyle H(1+z)=H(1)+H^{\prime}(1)z+\frac{H^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{2}z^{2}.

It follows from
the fact that
H^{\prime\prime}
is monotone with
H^{\prime\prime}(1)=\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon^{2}}
that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}H(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\geq b(t)\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{4}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

with

(2.11)

\displaystyle b(t):=\frac{1}{2}\min\big\{\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon^{2}},\,\inf_{x\in[0,1]}H^{\prime\prime}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})\big\}.

Note that, by (
2.6
), there exists
C_{p,\gamma}>0
such that,
for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}b(t)^{-2p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg]\leq C_{p,\gamma}\varepsilon^{2p}.

We use (
2.10
) to compute that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{4}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}b(t)\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{4}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{p}\bigg]\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}b(t)^{-2p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\Big)^{p}\bigg]\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}b(t)^{-2p}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\gamma}\varepsilon^{2}\left(3^{2p-1}{\rm e}^{T}\,\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})^{2p}]+C_{2p}\,{\rm e}^{T}\,T^{2p+1}\varepsilon^{4\beta p}\|\sigma\|^{4p}_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\gamma}\varepsilon^{2p}\big(\varepsilon^{2p\alpha}+\varepsilon^{4p\beta}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,\gamma}\varepsilon^{2p}\big(\varepsilon^{p\alpha}+\varepsilon^{2p\beta}\big),

from which the bound on
\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{2p}]
follows.
Regarding
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
, we similarly have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\inf_{x\in[0,1]}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{2}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{p}\bigg]\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(\displaystyle\inf_{x\in[0,1]}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})\big)^{-1}\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\Big)^{\!2p}\Big]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\big(\displaystyle\inf_{x\in[0,1]}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}))^{-1}\Big)^{\!2p}\Big]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\big(\varepsilon^{p\alpha}+\varepsilon^{2p\beta}\big),

giving the required bound on
\mathbb{E}\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}^{2p}
.
To complete the proof of (
2.7
), we need two more estimates on
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}
. The first is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{4}|(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{3-\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)^{p}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma-3}|(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{3-\gamma}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)^{2p}\Big]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma-3}(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{2p}\Big]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\big(\varepsilon^{p\alpha}+\varepsilon^{2p\beta}\big),

while the second is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{4}|(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{0}^{1}|(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 8\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\Big)^{p}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 8\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{6p}\Big]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})^{2p}\Big]\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\varepsilon^{p\alpha}+\varepsilon^{2p\beta}\big).

We observe that, using estimate (
2.1
) again, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{2}|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}\bigg]=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{p}\bigg]\leq C_{p}\big(\varepsilon^{p\alpha}+\varepsilon^{2p\beta}\big),

which gives the desired bound on
(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}
.

3. Finally, we turn to (
2.9
),
and fix any
\delta>0
complying with (
2.8
).
Then, by (
(ii)
),
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp(\delta\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t)\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp(\frac{\delta}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{\rm e}^{T}}{3}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp(\frac{3\delta}{\varepsilon^{2}}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon}))\Big]+\frac{CT{\rm e}^{T}}{1-\delta/(\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C{\rm e}^{T}}{3(1-3\delta\lambda\varepsilon^{\alpha-2})}+\frac{CT{\rm e}^{T}}{1-\delta/(\varepsilon^{2}\eta_{0}(\varepsilon,T))}\leq C.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Analysis of the Acoustic System

The starting point of our analysis of the acoustic system (
AS
) is the existence and uniqueness of solutions
with the initial data
(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})
,
which are proved directly by using again a pathwise transformation:

\hat{r}_{\varepsilon}=r_{\varepsilon}-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon^{\beta-2}\sigma_{k}\,\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume that
(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
is a collection of
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable
random variables in
H^{1}\times H^{1}_{0}
such that

\int_{0}^{1}r_{0,\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=0\qquad\mbox{almost surely}.

Then there exists a unique adapted strong pathwise solution
(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
of system (
AS
) such that

r_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}),\quad v_{\varepsilon}\in C([0,\infty);H^{1}_{0})\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,\infty;H^{2})\qquad\mbox{almost surely}.

The energy structure of (
AS
) is captured by the energy functional:

\mathcal{G}(r,v)=\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\frac{1}{2}(\gamma r^{2}+v^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon vr_{x}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}|r_{x}|^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x,

which can be viewed as analogous to
\mathcal{E}
in (
1.10
).

3.1.
Energy estimates

We start by a series of the bounds on the solutions of (
AS
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
be the solution of system (
AS
) with initial data
(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})

as in
Proposition
3.1
, and let

\kappa_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{4\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}}.

For all
R>0
, the following exponential martingale estimate holds
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{t\geq 0}\Big(\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}t\Big)-\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})\geq R\Big]

(3.1)

\displaystyle\,\,\leq{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\varepsilon}R}.

As a consequence, for any
m\geq 1
, there exists
c_{m}>0
independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

such that, for all
T\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)^{m}\Big]

(3.2)

\displaystyle\leq c_{m}\left(\kappa_{\varepsilon}^{-m}+\varepsilon^{2m\beta-2m}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2m}T^{m}+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})^{m}\right]\right).

Moreover, for any
\delta\in(0,\frac{\kappa_{\varepsilon}}{2})
,
there exist a constant
C_{\delta}>0
independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
such that, for all
T\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\delta\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Big(\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)\Big)\Big]

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\delta}\exp\big(\frac{\delta}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}T\big)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(2\delta\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})\big)\big]}.

Proof.

By Itô’s formula, the two equations in (
AS
) imply that

\,\mathrm{d}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}(\gamma r_{\varepsilon}^{2}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)+\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t+\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{\beta-1}\sigma v_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

Again, the first integral on the right-hand-side is

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

and the last integral is a short form representing

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{\beta-1}\sigma_{k}v_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t}.

This equality, together with the observation that

\,\mathrm{d}\Big(\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\Big)=-vv_{xx}\,\mathrm{d}t-\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon^{\beta}(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\sigma\,\mathrm{d}W_{t},

gives

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\big(|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t+\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{\beta-1}\sigma\big(v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

Define

\displaystyle Z(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{\beta-1}\sigma\big(v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W_{s}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{\beta-1}\sigma_{k}\big(v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{s}.

Then
(Z(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a (local) martingale. It follows from (
3.4
)
that, for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+Z(t).

Also, the quadratic variation of
Z(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\langle Z\rangle(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\varepsilon^{\beta-1}\sigma_{k}\big(v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2(\beta-1)}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big\|v_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{2(\beta-1)}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\kappa_{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s.

Let

\Phi(t):=\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})(t)+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s.

Then

\displaystyle\Phi(t)-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}t-\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq Z(t)-\frac{\kappa_{\varepsilon}}{2}\langle Z\rangle(t)+\Big(\frac{\kappa_{\varepsilon}}{2}\langle Z\rangle(t)-\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\big(|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\Big)

\displaystyle\leq Z(t)-\frac{\kappa_{\varepsilon}}{2}\langle Z\rangle(t).

Thus, it follows from the exponential martingale estimates that, for all
R>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{t\geq 0}\big(\Phi(t)-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}t\big)-\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})\geq R\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{t\geq 0}\big(Z(t)-\frac{\kappa_{\varepsilon}}{2}\langle Z\rangle(t)(t)\big)\geq R\Big]

\displaystyle\leq{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\varepsilon}R},

which is (
3.1
).
To prove (
3.2
), fix
T>0
and denote

Y:=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}T+\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon}).

We also denote by
\chi
the characteristic function of the set:
\{\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Phi(t)>Y\}
.
Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Phi(t)^{m}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(\Phi(t)-Y)^{m}\chi\Big]+2^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[Y^{m}\chi\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Phi(t)^{m}(1-\chi)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(\Phi(t)-Y)^{m}\chi\Big]+2^{m-1}\mathbb{E}[Y^{m}]

\displaystyle=2^{m-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(\Phi(t)-Y)^{m}\chi\geq\lambda\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+2^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\big[Y^{m}\big]

\displaystyle\leq 2^{m-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{1/m}}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda+2^{m-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}T+\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})\Big)^{m}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq c_{m}\Big(\kappa_{\varepsilon}^{-m}+\varepsilon^{2m\beta-2m}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2m}T^{m}+\mathbb{E}\big[\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})^{m}\big]\Big),

as we want.
Turning to (
3.3
),
we fix
0<\delta<\frac{\kappa_{\varepsilon}}{2}
and argue similarly to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\big(\delta\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Phi(t)\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\big(\delta\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(\Phi(t)-Y)\chi\big)\exp(\delta Y\chi)\exp\big(\delta(1-\chi)\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Phi(t)\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp(\delta Y)\exp\big(\delta\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(\Phi(t)-Y)\chi\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[\exp(2\delta Y)\big]}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\big(2\delta\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(\Phi(t)-Y)\chi\big)\Big]}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[\exp(2\delta Y)\big]}\sqrt{\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\Big[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}(\Phi(t)-Y)\chi\geq\frac{1}{2\delta}\log\lambda\Big]\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\big(2\delta(\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}T+\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon}))\big)\Big]}\sqrt{\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\kappa_{\varepsilon}/(2\delta)}\,\mathrm{d}\lambda}

\displaystyle=C_{\delta}\exp\big(\frac{\delta}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta-2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}T\big)\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(2\delta\mathcal{G}(r_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})\big)\big]}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

3.2.
Exponential contraction and stability

Due to the linearity of system (
AS
) and the additivity of the noise term,
the stability of the acoustic system can be analyzed through the corresponding deterministic system.
The main result of this section is the following:

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.
Let
(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\in C([0,\infty);H^{1})\times C([0,\infty;H^{1}_{0})\cap L^{2}_{\text{\rm loc}}(0,\infty;H^{2})

be a solution to the deterministic system:

(3.6)

\begin{cases}(r_{\varepsilon})_{t}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}=0,\\
(v_{\varepsilon})_{t}+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}=(v_{\varepsilon})_{xx}.\end{cases}

Then the following exponential stability estimates hold
:
For every
t\geq 0
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot),v_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot))\leq\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot),v_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot)){\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{2}},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\|(r_{\varepsilon},\,v_{\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq 8\gamma\|(r_{\varepsilon},\,v_{\varepsilon})(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{4}},

\displaystyle\|(r_{\varepsilon},\,v_{\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|((r_{\varepsilon})_{x},(v_{\varepsilon})_{x})(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\quad\leq 6\gamma\Big(\|(r_{\varepsilon},\,v_{\varepsilon})(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|((r_{\varepsilon})_{x},(v_{\varepsilon})_{x})(0,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big){\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{2}}.

Proof.

First, notice the following pointwise inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\gamma r_{\varepsilon}^{2}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4}|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}r_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}.

Then, applying the Poincaré inequality for both
r_{\varepsilon}
and
v_{\varepsilon}
in the second inequality, we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma+\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3}{4}\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\gamma\|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.
By the equations, we have the following equality (see (
3.4
)):

(3.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\leq-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}).

The first estimate (
3.7
) then follows from the Gronwall inequality.

Now, differentiating the first equation in (
3.6
) with respect to
x
gives

\displaystyle((r_{\varepsilon})_{x})_{t}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})_{xx}=0.

With this, multiplying the second equation of (
3.6
) by
(v_{\varepsilon})_{xx}
and integrating in
x
,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{2}|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{0}^{1}|(v_{\varepsilon})_{xx}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}(v_{\varepsilon})_{xx}\,\mathrm{d}x=-\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\gamma}{2}|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

which implies

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\big(\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle=-2\int_{0}^{1}|(v_{\varepsilon})_{xx}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq-2\int_{0}^{1}|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The last inequality is due to the Poincaré inequality applied to
(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}
that is of zero mean.
Combining this with (
3.11
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\int_{0}^{1}\big(\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{5}{2}\|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{4}\Big(\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\int_{0}^{1}\big(\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big).

The second estimate (
3.8
) now follows from the Gronwall inequality and the bounds:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\gamma r_{\varepsilon}^{2}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\int_{0}^{1}\big(\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}\big(\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Similarly, (
3.11
)–(
3.12
) imply

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\Big).

The last estimate (
3.9
) now follows from the Gronwall inequality and the bounds:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\big(r_{\varepsilon}^{2}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\varepsilon|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+\varepsilon|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{G}(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\gamma|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3\gamma}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\big(r_{\varepsilon}^{2}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\varepsilon|(r_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}+\varepsilon|(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

This concludes the proof.
∎

A straightforward consequence of the above result is the stability and continuous dependence of solutions of problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
)
for system (
AS
),
which follows from the observation that the difference of
any two solutions of system (
AS
) is a solution to
the deterministic system (
3.6
).

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Let
(r_{1,\varepsilon},v_{1,\varepsilon})
and
(r_{2,\varepsilon},v_{2,\varepsilon})

be two solutions of problem (
1.7
)–(
1.8
) for the stochastic system (
AS
).
Then, for all
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\big\|(r_{1,\varepsilon}-r_{2,\varepsilon},\,v_{1,\varepsilon}-v_{2,\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq 8\gamma\big\|(r_{1,\varepsilon}-r_{2,\varepsilon},\,v_{1,\varepsilon}-v_{2,\varepsilon})(0,\cdot)\big\|_{H^{1}}^{2}{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{4}},

\displaystyle\big\|(r_{1,\varepsilon}-r_{2,\varepsilon},\,v_{1,\varepsilon}-v_{2,\varepsilon})(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\big\|((r_{1,\varepsilon})_{x}-(r_{2,\varepsilon})_{x},\,(v_{1,\varepsilon})_{x}-(v_{2,\varepsilon})_{x})(t,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\,\,\leq 6\gamma\Big(\big\|(r_{1,\varepsilon}-r_{2,\varepsilon},\,v_{1,\varepsilon}-v_{2,\varepsilon})(0,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\big\|((r_{1,\varepsilon})_{x}-(r_{2,\varepsilon})_{x},\,(v_{1,\varepsilon})_{x}-(v_{2,\varepsilon})_{x})(0,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big){\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{2}},

almost surely.

. We first estimate the difference between

Proposition 4.1
.

There exists a generic constant

\displaystyle\gamma\|\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(t)-r_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}(t)-v_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

(4.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+C|\gamma-2|\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\|(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{1}|\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}|^{4}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s+C\gamma^{2}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{t}\|(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\Big).

Proof.

Taking the difference of the first equation of (
FS
) and the first equation of (
AS
),
and multiplying it by
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-r_{\varepsilon})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big((\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-r_{\varepsilon})^{2}\big)_{t}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})_{x}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-r_{\varepsilon})=-\varepsilon(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-r_{\varepsilon}).

Likewise, taking the difference of the second equation of (

) and the second equation of (

),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big((\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}\big)_{t}+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-r_{\varepsilon})_{x}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon^{2}\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{xx}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon uu_{x}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}).

Summing the two equations and integrating in

, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\big(\gamma\|\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-r_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)+2\|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-2\gamma\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-r_{\varepsilon})(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\gamma\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-r_{\varepsilon})\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\gamma}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\big(1-(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-2}\big)(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\int_{0}^{1}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{2}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x-2\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{xx}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x

Notice that

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}

Next, we estimate
I_{3}
. For

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

where we used here the inequality:

For

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\gamma}{\varepsilon}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\big(1-(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-2}\big)(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{2\gamma}{\varepsilon^{3}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\Big(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-1}\Big)(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\,\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{6}}\int_{0}^{1}\Big(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}-\frac{1}{\gamma-1}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-1}\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C|\gamma-2|\varepsilon^{2}\|(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{1}|\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}|^{4}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Lastly, for
\gamma=2

I_{3}=0

In all cases, we have

I_{3}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C|\gamma-2|\varepsilon^{2}\,\|(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{\gamma-3}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{1}|\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}|^{4}\,\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle I_{5}

.

\displaystyle\qquad\,\times\bigg(\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(C\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}|\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}|^{4}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+C\varepsilon^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\|(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})^{-2}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{1}\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}^{4}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{2}\bigg]

Convergence of Invariant Measures

In this section, we consider the convergence of invariant measures of system (

5.1.
Invariant measures for the fluctuation system

Let

\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}=\Big\{(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\in H^{1}(0,1)\times H^{1}_{0}(0,1)\,:\,\int_{0}^{1}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x=0,\,\,1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}>0\Big\}

be the state space for system (
FS
).
Following
[
12
]
, we equip
\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}
with the
L^{2}\times L^{2}
metric (see Remark
5.1
).
The Markov semigroup
(\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
associated to
system (
FS
) is a collection of operators

\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{M}_{b}(\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon})\to\mathcal{M}_{b}(\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon})
on the set of all bounded Borel measurable functions on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}
given by

\displaystyle\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}(\phi)(\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})=\mathbb{E}\big[\phi(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot),\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot))\big]

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon})
and
(\rho_{0,\varepsilon},u_{0,\varepsilon})\in\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}
,
where
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
is the solution of problem (
1.5
)–(
1.6
) for system (
FS
)
with initial data
(\bar{\rho}_{0,\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{0,\varepsilon})\in\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}
.
A Borel probability measure
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}
is said to be an invariant measure of
semigroup
(\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
if

(\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}=\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\qquad\text{for all }t\geq 0,

where
(\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
is the push-forward measure of
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
by
(\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}
.
Equivalently, the probability measure
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}(\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\qquad\,\,\mbox{for all $t\geq 0$ and $\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon})$}.

An invariant measure represents the law of a statistically stationary solution.

We briefly describe the main result in
[
12
]
, which implies the existence of an invariant probability
measure
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
for
(\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
.
In
[
12
]
,
the Naiver-Stokes equations (
NS
) are considered in the phase space

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}=\Big\{(\rho,u)\in H^{1}(0,1)\times H^{1}_{0}(0,1)\,:\,\int_{0}^{1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x=1,\,\,\rho>0\Big\}

equipped with the
L^{2}\times L^{2}
norm. For each
\varepsilon>0
,
it has been proved that there exists an invariant measure
\mu_{\varepsilon}

for the corresponding Markov semigroup
(\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
.
Now we can define a bijection
\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}:\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}\to\mathcal{X}
by

\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})=(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\,\varepsilon\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}).

Then
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}:=(\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})^{*}\mu_{\varepsilon}
is an invariant measure for
semigroup
(\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
. Indeed, for all
\phi\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\phi(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\phi\big(\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\big(\phi\circ\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\big)(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi\circ\mathcal{H}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\phi(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}.

Remark 5.1
.

The state space
\mathcal{X}
considered in
[
12
]
, equipped with the
L^{2}\times L^{2}
norm, is a non-complete metric space.
Nonetheless, one can establish an
approximate Feller
property for
\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
, which in turn facilitates the
construction of an invariant measure. However, since the
L^{2}\times L^{2}
topology and the
H^{1}\times H^{1}_{0}
topology generate the same

\sigma
-algebra on
\mathcal{X}
, the invariance of a Borel probability measure with respect
to
\mathcal{P}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
is not affected by the norm chosen.

5.3.
Contraction property of the acoustic system semigroup

In this section, we prove a contraction property of semigroup

Since the solutions are not known to be pathwise contractive in the

we define an
\varepsilon

\varepsilon\to 0

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

\varepsilon

\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}
on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
by

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|(r,v)\|_{\varepsilon}^{2}=\|(r,\,v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|(r_{x},\,v_{x})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

We now define the Wasserstein metrics for probability measures on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}

by using the metrics on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}

For a metric
d

\bar{\mathcal{X}}

let
4
4

4

Here, we denote the set of all Borel probability measures on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
by
\mathfrak{P}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})

\mathfrak{P}_{d}=\Big\{\bar{\eta}\in\mathfrak{P}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})\,:\,\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}d((0,0),(r,v))\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\eta}(r,v)<\infty\Big\}.

be the collection of Borel probability measures on

Then the Wasserstein metric

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{d}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=\inf_{\bar{\eta}\in\mathcal{C}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}\times\bar{\mathcal{X}}}d\big((r_{1},v_{1}),(r_{2},v_{2})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\eta}((r_{1},v_{1}),(r_{2},v_{2})),

where the infimum is taken over the set,
\mathcal{C}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})

\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}

i.e.

\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}
with marginals
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}

Note that

If, in addition,

We denote by

,

[

Moreover, it follows from the Monge–Kantorovich duality that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{d}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=\sup_{\textrm{Lip}_{d}(\phi)\leq 1}\left|\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\eta_{1}-\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\eta_{2}\right|,

where the supremum is taken over the set of all Lipschitz functions

with Lipschitz constants less than

We can establish the following contraction property for semigroup

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Then, for all
t\geq 0

and for probability measures
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{H^{1}}((\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\eta_{1},(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\eta_{2})\leq 2\sqrt{2}{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{8}}\mathcal{W}_{H^{1}}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}),

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}((\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\eta_{1},(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\eta_{2})\leq\sqrt{6}{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{4}}\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2}),

where
(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\bar{\eta}
is the push-forward measure of
\bar{\eta}
by
\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon}

Proof.

We first show that the conclusion is true when
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are the Dirac measures.
Indeed, let
g_{1},g_{2}\in\bar{\mathcal{X}}
. Then, by the Monge–Kantorovich duality (

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{H^{1}}((\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{g_{1}},(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{g_{2}})=\sup_{\textrm{Lip}_{H^{1}}(\phi)\leq 1}\left|\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\phi(g_{1})-\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\phi(g_{2})\right|.

Denote by
(r_{\varepsilon}(t;g),v_{\varepsilon}(t,g))

at time

Taking any
\phi\in C(\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{H_{1}})

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\phi(g_{1})-\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\phi(g_{2})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}\big[\phi(r_{\varepsilon}(t;g_{1}),v_{\varepsilon}(t,g_{1}))-\phi(r_{\varepsilon}(t;g_{2}),v_{\varepsilon}(t,g_{2}))\big]\right|

Corollary 5.3
.

The semigroup
(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
admits the unique invariant measure
\omega_{\varepsilon}

on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{H^{1}}
such that
\omega_{\varepsilon}\in\mathfrak{P}_{H^{1}}
and, for all
\lambda\in\mathfrak{P}_{H^{1}}
,

(5.7)

\mathcal{W}_{H^{1}}((\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\lambda,\omega_{\varepsilon})\leq 2\sqrt{2}{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{8}}\mathcal{W}_{H^{1}}(\lambda,\omega_{\varepsilon})\qquad\,\,\mbox{for every $t\geq 0$}.

Proof.

Note that, by Proposition
5.2
,
map
(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}
is a contraction on
\mathfrak{P}_{H^{1}}

for all sufficiently large
t\geq 0
.
Since

is a complete norm on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
,

\mathfrak{P}_{H^{1}}
is a complete space.
By the contraction mapping principle, for all such
t

there exists a probability measure
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{t}\in\mathfrak{P}_{H^{1}}

such that
(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon}^{t}=\omega_{\varepsilon}^{t}
.
The time-continuity of
(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})
implies
that
\omega_{\varepsilon}^{t}=\omega_{\varepsilon}^{s}
for all these sufficiently large
t
and
s
.
By the semigroup property,
\omega_{\varepsilon}=\omega_{\varepsilon}^{t}
is invariant for all
t\geq 0
.
The attraction property (
5.7
) is a consequence of Proposition
5.2
.

To complete the proof, we still have to show that the uniqueness holds in the entire space
\mathfrak{P}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
,
not just in
\mathfrak{P}_{H^{1}}
.
To see this, let
\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}\in\mathfrak{P}(\bar{\mathcal{X}})
be an invariant measure
for
(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})_{t\geq 0}
.
Note that, for all
\delta>0
, there exists
M_{\delta}>0
such that

Define
\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\in\mathfrak{P}_{H^{1}}

\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(A)=\frac{1}{1-m_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\int_{A}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\|r_{x}\|_{L^{2}}+\|v_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\leq M_{\delta}\}}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}

for all Borel measurable set

.

\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}

\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}

.
On the other hand, since
\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\in\mathfrak{P}_{H^{1}}
,

(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}

\omega

.
This yields
\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}=\omega_{\varepsilon}
by exchanging the order of the two limits,
which is valid since the setwise (and hence weak) convergence of
(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}

to
\bar{\omega}_{\varepsilon}
as
\delta\to 0
is uniform in
t\geq 0
. This completes the proof.
∎

\omega_{\varepsilon}\in\mathfrak{P}_{H^{1}}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\mathcal{G}(r,v)\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{\varepsilon}=\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\mathcal{G}(r,v)\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{\varepsilon}<\infty.

Therefore, (
5.11
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}t\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\|(r_{x},\,v_{x})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2(\beta-1)}t\int_{0}^{1}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

from which (
5.8
) follows.
Next, let
\delta_{(0,0)}
be the delta measure on
\bar{\mathcal{X}}
centred at
(0,0)\in\bar{\mathcal{X}}
.
By Proposition
3.5
, we see that, for all
p\geq 1
,
there exists
C_{p}>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\mathcal{G}(r,v)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{(0,0)}=C_{p}\varepsilon^{2(\beta-1)p}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p}.

Likewise, for all
\eta\in(0,\,(8\varepsilon^{2(\beta-1)}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})^{-1})
,
there exists
C_{\eta}>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\exp(\eta\mathcal{G}(r,v)\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{(0,0)}=C_{\eta}\exp\big(2\eta\varepsilon^{2(\beta-1)}\big).

By Corollary
5.3
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{H^{1}}((\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{(0,0)},\omega_{\varepsilon})=0.

This can be rephrased in terms of the Monge–Kantorovich duality (
5.4
).
For all globally Lipschitz function
f:\bar{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathbb{R}
,

(5.12)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}f\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{(0,0)}=\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}f\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{\varepsilon}.

Let
p\geq 1
and
N>0
. Choosing
f(r,v)=\min\{\mathcal{G}(r,v)^{p},N\}
in the above gives

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\min\{\mathcal{G}(r,v)^{p},N\}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\min\{\mathcal{G}(r,v)^{p},N\}\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{(0,0)}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\mathcal{G}(r,v)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{(0,0)}=C_{p}\varepsilon^{2(\beta-1)p}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2p}.

Now, letting
N\to\infty
gives the first estimate (
5.9
).
Similarly, let
\eta\in(0,(8\varepsilon^{2(\beta-1)}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2})^{-1})
and
M>0
.
Choosing
f(r,v)=\min\{\exp(\eta\mathcal{G}(r,v)),M\}
in (
5.12
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\min\{\exp(\eta\mathcal{G}(r,v)),M\}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\min\{\exp(\eta\mathcal{G}(r,v)),M\}\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{(0,0)}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\exp(\eta\mathcal{G}(r,v))\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\delta_{(0,0)}=C_{\eta}\exp\big(2\eta\varepsilon^{2(\beta-1)}\big).

Now, letting
M\to\infty
gives the second estimate (
5.10
).
∎

5.4.
Estimates of the invariant measures

In order to apply the finite-time convergence result to study the convergence of invariant measures,
we have to estimate the statistically stationary solutions distributed as invariant measures.
In particular, we want to show that they satisfy (
1.12
),
which is the main goal of this section.

Since there is a natural correspondence between the invariant measures of systems (
FS
) and (
NS
),
we can confine our analysis to the latter system.
Recall that the energy balance (
2.2
) holds for system (
NS
):

(5.13)

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\gamma-3}|(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)\,\mathrm{d}t

(5.14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}.

To simplify the notation, we write that, for all
(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\|(\log\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma\,\mathrm{d}x,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})=\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

so that the above equality reads

\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})+\mathcal{K}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}t=\mathcal{M}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}+\mathcal{R}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}t.

Note here that the terms
\mathcal{M}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}
and
\mathcal{R}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}t

should be interpreted as

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\big)\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}W^{k}_{t},

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\varepsilon}\sigma_{k}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

We also define

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})^{2}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho u+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rho_{x}}{\rho}\big)\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{2}

for later use.

As a first step of the estimates, we show that
\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
can be controlled
by
\mathcal{K}(\rho_{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon})
, a distinctive feature that is true only for the isothermal pressure law
and has been observed and used in
[
24
,
25
]
to study the long-time behavior
of the solutions in the deterministic case.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
. Then, for all
(\rho,u)\in\mathcal{X}
,

(5.15)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\leq\big(\,\frac{3}{2}+2\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|\rho^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\mathcal{K}(\rho,u),

which implies

(5.16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\frac{3}{2}+2\exp(2\varepsilon\sqrt{2\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)})+\varepsilon^{2}\exp(\varepsilon\sqrt{2\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)})\big)\mathcal{K}(\rho,u).

Proof.

It suffices to control each term in the definition of
\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)

in (
1.10
) separately.
The first two terms are easily bounded by

\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\mathcal{K}(\rho,u),

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}(\rho\log\rho-p+1)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\rho(\rho-1)\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}(\rho^{2}-1)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{x}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|(\log\rho)_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho_{x}^{2}}{\rho^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\rho^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\,\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\|(\log\rho)_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\rho^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho_{x}u}{\rho}\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\rho u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\rho_{x}^{2}}{\rho^{3}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}\|\rho^{-1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u).

Combining the above, we obtain the first estimate (
5.15
).

The second estimate (
5.16
) follows from the first one and the fact that

\displaystyle\|\log\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|(\log\rho)_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\sqrt{2\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)}

for all
\rho\in H^{1}
with mass equal to 1.
Indeed, there exists
x_{0}\in[0,1]
such that
\rho(x_{0})=1
so that, for all
x\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle|\log\rho(x)|=|\log\rho(x)-\log\rho(x_{0})|=\Big|\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\big(\log\rho(y)\big)_{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\Big|\leq\|(\log\rho)_{y}\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

In light of the above proposition, we define
h:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
given by

(5.17)

h(x):=\big(\frac{3}{2}+2\exp(2\varepsilon\sqrt{2x})+\varepsilon^{2}\exp(\varepsilon\sqrt{2x})\big)x.

Note that
h
is strictly increasing, bijective and convex.
Using (
5.14
), we can obtain a first bound for the invariant measures.
The computation here is similar to the one in the incompressible case; see
[
20
]
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Let
F:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be of class
C^{1}
and increasing.
Let
\mu_{\varepsilon}
be an invariant measure of system (
NS
) defined on the state space
\mathcal{X}
. Then

(5.18)

\int_{\mathcal{X}}F(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\>\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}}F(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))\mathcal{R}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))(\mathcal{M}(\rho,u))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon},

where the last integral is to be understood as

\int_{\mathcal{X}}F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))(\mathcal{M}(\rho,u))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{X}}F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))\varepsilon^{2\beta}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho u+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\rho_{x}}{\rho}\big)\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Note that, for each
\delta>0
, there exists
N_{\delta}>0
such that

\displaystyle q_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}:=\mu_{\varepsilon}\left(\{(\rho,u)\in\mathcal{X}\,:\,\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)>N_{\delta}\}\right)<\delta.

Define a probability measure
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
on
\mathcal{X}
by

\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(A)=\frac{1}{1-q_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\int_{A}\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\leq N_{\delta}\}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

for all Borel measurable set
A\subseteq\mathcal{X}
.
Let
(\rho_{0,\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{0,\varepsilon}^{\delta})
be an
\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable random variable
distributed as
\mu_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}
.
Let
(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})
be a solution of the fluctuation
system (
FS
) with initial data
(\rho_{0,\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{0,\varepsilon}^{\delta})
.

Define the function
\tilde{F}:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
by

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}F(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

so that
\tilde{F}^{\prime}=F
. By Itô’ s formula, we have

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{F}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})=F(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})+\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}))\,\mathrm{d}\langle\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}),\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\rangle,

so that

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{F}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}))+F(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}))K(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=F(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}))M(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\,\mathrm{d}W_{t}+F(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}))R(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}))\big(M(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t,

where

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}))\big(M(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}))\Big(\varepsilon^{\beta}\int_{0}^{1}\big(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\rho_{\varepsilon})_{x}^{\delta}}{\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}}\big)\sigma_{k}\,\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Thus, for all
T>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{F}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(T,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(T,\cdot)))\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}F(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot)))K(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(T,\cdot))\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{F}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{0,\varepsilon}^{\delta}))\big]+\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}F(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot)))R(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot))\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot)))\big(M(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot))\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big].

Therefore, for all
T,P>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\min\{F\left(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\right)\mathcal{K}(\rho,u),P\}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\min\{F(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))\mathcal{K}(\rho,u),P\})\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\min\{F(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))\mathcal{K}(\rho,u),P\})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\leq N_{\delta}\}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{P}_{t}(\min\{F(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))\mathcal{K}(\rho,u),P\})\mathbbm{1}_{\{\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)>N_{\delta}\}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-q_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[\min\{F(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot)))K(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot)),P\}\big]\,\mathrm{d}t+P\delta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-q_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})}{T}\mathbb{E}\big[\tilde{F}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{0,\varepsilon}^{\delta},u_{0,\varepsilon}^{\delta}))\big]+\frac{(1-q_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})}{T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}F(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot)))R(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot))\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(1-q_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})}{2T}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{T}F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot)))\big(M(\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot),u_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}(t,\cdot))\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\Big]+P\delta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-q_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\tilde{F}(N_{\delta})}{T}+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{P}_{t}(F(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))\mathcal{R}(\rho,u))\chi_{\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\leq N_{\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{P}_{t}(F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))(M(\rho,u))^{2})\chi_{\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\leq N_{\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}t+P\delta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(1-q_{\varepsilon}^{\delta})\tilde{F}(N_{\delta})}{T}+\int_{\mathcal{X}}F(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))\mathcal{R}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}F^{\prime}(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))(M(\rho,u))^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}+P\delta.

The result follows by letting
T\to\infty
, then
\delta\to 0
, and finally
P\to\infty
.
∎

We also need to control the integrals of all the powers of
\mathcal{E}
in the following sense,
for which the dependence on
n
below is crucial for what comes next.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
R_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
and
B_{\varepsilon}=4\varepsilon^{2\beta}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
. Then, for all integers
n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq 2R_{\varepsilon}B_{\varepsilon}^{n}n!\exp(\frac{h(2R_{\varepsilon})}{B_{\varepsilon}}),

where
h(x)
is defined in (
5.17
).

Proof.

We first note that, for all
(\rho,u)\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\rho,u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho\sigma_{k}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}=R_{\varepsilon},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(\rho,u)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2\beta}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\sigma_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big(2\int_{0}^{1}\rho\,\mathrm{d}x\int_{0}^{1}\rho u^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(\log\rho)_{x}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 4\varepsilon^{2\beta}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\|u_{x}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon^{2}}\|(\log\rho)_{x}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)=B_{\varepsilon}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u).

Now, taking
F(z)\equiv 1
in (
5.18
) yields

(5.19)

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{R}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq R_{\varepsilon}.

Next, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, taking
F(z)=z^{n}
in (
5.18
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\mathcal{R}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n-1}\big(\mathcal{M}(\rho,u)\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq R_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}+\frac{B_{\varepsilon}n}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n-1}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq R_{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathcal{X}\cap\{\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\leq 2R_{\varepsilon}\}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}+\int_{\mathcal{X}\cap\{\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)>2R_{\varepsilon}\}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{B_{\varepsilon}n}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n-1}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq R_{\varepsilon}\big(h(2R_{\varepsilon})\big)^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}\cap\{\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)>2R_{\varepsilon}\}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}+\frac{B_{\varepsilon}n}{2}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n-1}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}.

The last inequality is due to the control:
\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\leq h(\mathcal{K}(\rho,u))
.
This implies

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq 2R_{\varepsilon}\big(h(2R_{\varepsilon})\big)^{n}+B_{\varepsilon}n\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n-1}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}.

Iterating the last inequality and using (
5.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq 2R_{\varepsilon}B_{\varepsilon}^{n}n!\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\big(\frac{h(2R_{\varepsilon})}{B_{\varepsilon}}\big)^{k}+R_{\varepsilon}B_{\varepsilon}^{n}n!\leq 2R_{\varepsilon}B_{\varepsilon}^{n}n!\exp(\frac{h(2R_{\varepsilon})}{B_{\varepsilon}}).

∎

The previous lemma allows us to prove new moment bounds of the invariant measures of system (
NS
).
These are fundamental to prove the subsequent convergence results for the invariant measures.

Proposition 5.8
.

There exist
C,\lambda>0
, depending only on
\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}
, such that

(i)

For all
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, the following polynomial moment bound holds
:

(5.20)

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq C\,p!\,\lambda^{p}\varepsilon^{2\beta p};

(ii)

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
0<\eta<(2\lambda\varepsilon^{2\beta})^{-1}
, the
following exponential moment
bound holds
:

(5.21)

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\exp\big(\eta(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u))\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq C.

Proof.

For the polynomial bound (
5.20
), we preliminarily note that it suffices to show the case when
p
is a positive integer.
In light of Lemma
5.7
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}\cap\{\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\leq B_{\varepsilon}\}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}+\int_{\mathcal{X}\cap\{\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)>B_{\varepsilon}\}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\big(h(B_{\varepsilon})\big)^{p}+\frac{1}{B_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\mathcal{X}\cap\{\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)>B_{\varepsilon}\}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{p}\mathcal{K}(\rho,u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\big(h(B_{\varepsilon})\big)^{p}+2R_{\varepsilon}B_{\varepsilon}^{p-1}p!\exp(\frac{h(2R_{\varepsilon})}{B_{\varepsilon}}).

Since
R_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\varepsilon^{2\beta}}{2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}

and
B_{\varepsilon}=4\varepsilon^{2\beta}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
,
we see that
0<B_{\varepsilon}\leq 4\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
and
0<R_{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}
,
so that

0\leq h(B_{\varepsilon})\leq CB_{\varepsilon}
and
0\leq h(2R_{\varepsilon})\leq CR_{\varepsilon}
for some
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
.
In addition,
\frac{h(2R_{\varepsilon})}{B_{\varepsilon}}
is bounded uniformly in
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

so that (
5.20
) follows.

For the exponential moment bound (
5.21
), we use the polynomial moment bounds and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}}\exp\big(\eta\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{\eta^{n}}{n!}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)^{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C\eta^{n}\lambda^{n}\varepsilon^{2\beta n}=\frac{C}{1-\eta\lambda\varepsilon^{2\beta}}\leq 2C,

provided that
0<\eta<(2\lambda\varepsilon^{2\beta})^{-1}
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.5.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

To prove Theorem
1.2
,
we follow the strategy of
[
21
]
.
Let
T>0
to be determined later.
For each
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, let
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon},\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})
be a statistically stationary solution
of system (
FS
) such that
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot),\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot))
is distributed
as
\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
for
t\geq 0
,
and let
(r_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
be the solution of system (
AS
) with initial conditions:

r_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(0,x),\quad v_{\varepsilon}(0,x)=\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}(0,x)\qquad\text{ for all }x\in(0,1).

By Proposition
5.8
,
(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot),\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}(0,\cdot))
satisfies (
1.12
)
with
\alpha=2\beta>1
so that
Theorem
1.1
applies.
As a result, we have the stability estimate:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},Q_{T}^{*}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\|(r_{\varepsilon}(T)-\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon}(T),\,v_{\varepsilon}(T)-\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}(T))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|((r_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T)-(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T),\,(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T)-(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T))\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2\beta-1}+C\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\big[\|((r_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T,\cdot),\,(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}}\big]+C\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\big[\|((\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T,\cdot),\,(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T,\cdot))\|_{L^{2}}\big].

Now, since the function:
(r,v)\mapsto\varepsilon\|(r_{x},\,v_{x})\|_{L^{2}})
is Lipschitz with
unit Lipschitz constant with respect to norm
\|\cdot\|_{\varepsilon}
,
the Monge–Kantorovich duality (
5.4
) implies

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\big[\|((r_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T),(v_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T))\|_{L^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varepsilon\|(r_{x},v_{x})\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}(\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}-\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varepsilon\|(r_{x},\,v_{x})\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{\varepsilon}\Big)+\int_{\mathcal{X}}\varepsilon\|(r_{x},\,v_{x})\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}((\mathcal{Q}_{t}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})+C\varepsilon\Big(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\|(r_{x},\,v_{x})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\omega_{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{4}}\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})+C\varepsilon^{\beta}.

The last inequality is due to the invariance of
\omega_{\varepsilon}

and Propositions
5.2
and
5.4
.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\big[\|(\bar{\rho}_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}\big]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\|\bar{\rho}_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\big\|1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\big\|(\log(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}))_{x}\big\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\big\|1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\big\|(\log(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}))_{x}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big(\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\exp\big((4\sqrt{2\mathcal{E}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho},\varepsilon\bar{u})}\big)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\big\|(\log(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}))_{x}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\Big(\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\exp\big(16\mathcal{E}(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho},\varepsilon\bar{u})\big)\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\big\|(\log(1+\varepsilon^{2}\bar{\rho}))_{x}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\exp\big(16\mathcal{E}(\rho,u)\big)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big\|(\log(\rho))_{x}\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\beta}

for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, by virtue of Lemma
5.7
and Proposition
5.8
.
Likewise, Proposition
5.8
also gives

\displaystyle\varepsilon\mathbb{E}\big[\big\|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}(T,\cdot)\big\|_{L^{2}}\big]=\varepsilon\int_{\bar{\mathcal{X}}_{\varepsilon}}\big\|(\bar{u}_{\varepsilon})_{x}\big\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\big\|(u_{\varepsilon})_{x}\big\|_{L^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\varepsilon}\leq C\varepsilon^{\beta}.

Finally, combining all these and making use of the triangle inequality and the invariance of
\omega_{\varepsilon}
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},Q_{T}^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},Q_{T}^{*}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon})+\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(Q_{T}^{*}\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},Q_{T}^{*}\omega_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2\beta-1}+C{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{4}}\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})+C\varepsilon^{\beta}+C{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{4}}\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon}),

where we have used again Proposition
5.2
in the last inequality.
By choosing
T
large enough such that
C{\rm e}^{-\frac{t}{4}}<\frac{1}{4}
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{L^{2}}(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathcal{W}_{\varepsilon}(\bar{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\omega_{\varepsilon})\leq C\varepsilon^{2\beta-1}.

This is the statement of Theorem
1.2
, and hence the proof is completed.

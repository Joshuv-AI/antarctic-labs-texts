Information scrambling and butterfly velocity in quantum spin glass chains
Venkata Lokesh K. Y 1 , Surajit Bera 2 and Sumilan Banerjee 2

Abstract
We make lattice generalization of two well-known zero-dimensional models of quantum spin glass, Sachdev-Ye (SY) and spherical quantum p p -spin glass model, to one dimension for studying crossovers in non-local scrambling dynamics due to glass transition, complex dynamics, and quantum and thermal fluctuations in paramagnetic (PM) and spin glass (SG) phases. In the SY chain of quantum dots, each described by infinite-range random Heisenberg model of N N spin- S S S ​ U ​ ( M ) SU(M) spins, we obtain the quantum Lyapunov exponent λ L \lambda_{\mathrm{L}} and butterfly velocity v B v_{B} as a function of temperature T T and the quantum parameter S S across the PM-SG phase boundary using a bosonic spinon representation in the large N , M N,M limit. In particular, we extract asymptotic T T and S S dependence, e.g., power laws, for λ L \lambda_{\mathrm{L}} and v B v_{B} in different regions deep inside the phases and near the replica symmetry breaking SG transition. We find the chaos to be non-maximal almost over the entire phase diagram. Very similar results for chaos indicators are found for the p p -spin glass chain as a function of temperature and a suitable quantum parameter Γ \Gamma , with some important qualitative differences. In particular, λ L \lambda_{\mathrm{L}} and v B v_{B} exhibit a maximum, coinciding with onset of complex glassy relaxation, above the glass transition as a function of T T and Γ \Gamma in the PM phase of the p p -spin glass model. In contrast, the maximum is only observed as a function of S S , but not with temperature, in the PM phase of SY model. The maximum originates from enhanced chaos due to maximal complexity in the glassy landscape. Thus, the results in the SY model indicate very different evolution of glassy complexity with quantum and thermal fluctuations.

To capture both (replica) symmetry broken ordered SG and disordered paramagnetic (PM) phases we use bosonic spinons to represent spin-

The disorder averaged Green’s function

\displaystyle(G^{-1})^{ab}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})

\displaystyle=\left(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}+\lambda\right)\delta_{ab}-\Sigma^{ab}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})

( 3.1a)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{ab}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\tilde{J}^{2}[G^{ab}(\tau)]^{2}G^{ba}(-\tau)

( 3.1b)

where

;

\displaystyle{\tilde{J}^{2}=J^{2}+2J^{\prime 2}}

3.1.1
Paramagnetic phase

In the PM phase one employs a replica diagonal and symmetric ansatz,
G^{ab}(\tau)=G(\tau)\delta_{ab}
. Thus, the saddle-point equations reduce to,

\displaystyle[G(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})]^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}+\lambda)-\Sigma(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})

( 3.3a)

\displaystyle\Sigma(\tau)

\displaystyle=\tilde{J}^{2}[G(\tau)]^{2}G(-\tau)

( 3.3b)

Analytic continuation from Matsubara to real frequency

gives the saddle point equation for retarded Green’s function

\displaystyle[G_{R}(\omega)]^{-1}=\omega+\lambda-\Sigma_{R}(\omega)

( 3.4)

where
\Sigma_{R}(\omega)=\Sigma(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}\to\omega+\mathfrak{i}0^{+})
and
\Sigma(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau e^{\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}\tau}\Sigma(\tau)

3.1.2
Spin glass phase

Following earlier works
Georges et al. 2001
, we describe the spin glass phase via the following one-step replica symmetry breaking (1RSB) ansatz for the bosonic Green’s function that appears in Eq.(
3.1

\displaystyle G^{ab}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\tilde{G}(\tau)\delta_{ab}-g\epsilon_{ab}

( 3.5)

or,
G^{ab}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})=\tilde{G}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})\delta_{ab}-\beta g\epsilon_{ab}\delta_{\omega_{k},0}

\Sigma^{ab}(\tau)=\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)\delta_{ab}-\tilde{J}^{2}g^{3}\epsilon_{ab},

( 3.6)

where
\epsilon_{ab}=1
in the diagonal block of size

is the regular part of the Green’s function such that
\tilde{G}(\tau\to\infty)\to 0

Working in the

at zero temperature.
Using
[G^{-1}]^{ab}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})=A(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})\delta_{ab}+B(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})\epsilon_{ab}
and the standard replica matrix inversion in
n\rightarrow 0
limit, we get

\displaystyle A(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tilde{G}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})},

( 3.7a)

\displaystyle B(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta g}{[\tilde{G}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})-m\beta g]\tilde{G}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})}.

( 3.7b)

The saddle point equation for
\tilde{G}

\displaystyle[\tilde{G}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})]^{-1}=\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}-\frac{\tilde{J}g}{\Theta}-[\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})-\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}=0)]

( 3.8a)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)=

\displaystyle\tilde{J}^{2}\Big[\tilde{G}^{2}(\tau)\tilde{G}(-\tau)-2g\tilde{G}(\tau)\tilde{G}(-\tau)-g\tilde{G}^{2}(\tau)

\displaystyle+2g^{2}\tilde{G}(\tau)+g^{2}\tilde{G}(-\tau)\Big]

( 3.8b)

In the above, we have eliminated
\lambda
using the equation for
\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}=0
and the parameterization
\tilde{G}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}=0)=-\Theta/\tilde{J}g
.
The saddle point equation for

3.7b

m\beta=\frac{1}{\tilde{J}g^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{\Theta}-\Theta\Big)

( 3.9)

The equations Eq.

3.8

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}[G^{ab},\lambda]=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{k}\mathrm{Tr}\ln\,[\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}+\lambda-\Sigma^{ab}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})]

( 3.10)

\displaystyle+\frac{3\tilde{J}^{2}}{4}\sum_{ab}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau[G^{ab}(\tau)G^{ab}(-\tau)]^{2}-\lambda S

in the replica space around the one-step solution and impose the condition that lowest eigenvalue of the fluctuation matrix in the replica space must vanish. The variations in free energy due to small variation
g^{ab}
in the saddle point Green’s function

\displaystyle\delta\mathbb{F}=\sum_{a>b,c>d}M_{ab,cd}\delta g^{ab}\delta g^{cd}

Although, in principle one has to consider the variation in diagonal component,

and

\displaystyle e_{1}=

\displaystyle 3\beta\tilde{J}^{2}g^{2}(1-3\Theta^{2})

( 3.12a)

\displaystyle e_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{3\beta\tilde{J}^{2}g^{2}}{\Theta^{2}}[\Theta^{2}-3+3\beta\tilde{J}g^{2}\Theta(1+\Theta)]

( 3.12b)

\displaystyle e_{3}=

\displaystyle 6\beta\tilde{J}^{2}g^{2}(3\beta\tilde{J}g^{2}\Theta-1)

( 3.12c)

Setting

\displaystyle\Theta=\Theta_{R}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}

( 3.13)

Coincidentally, the marginality criterion leads to a gapless

with

\displaystyle[G_{R}(\omega)]^{-1}=\omega-\frac{\tilde{J}g}{\Theta_{R}}-[\Sigma_{R}(\omega)-\Sigma_{R}(\omega=0)]

( 3.14)

where
\Sigma_{R}(\omega)=\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}\to\omega+\mathfrak{i}0^{+})
and
\tilde{\Sigma}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau e^{\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}\tau}\tilde{\Sigma}(\tau)

\mathcal{F}_{\mu,x}^{p}(t)\sim\exp{[2\pi Tt+\mathfrak{i}p_{1}x]}

\displaystyle v_{m}=\mathfrak{i}\frac{2\pi T}{p_{1}}.

( 5.9)

To give rise to a well defined light cone,
\mathcal{F}_{\mu,x}^{s}(t)
(
\mathcal{F}_{\mu,x}^{p}(t)
) should be non-oscillatory in space and time, implying that
p_{s}
(
p_{1}
) is purely imaginary, i.e.,
p_{s}=\mathfrak{i}|p_{s}|
(
p_{1}=\mathfrak{i}|p_{1}|
), and
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(\mathfrak{i}|p_{s}|)
is purely real. In
Appendix C
, we show that this is indeed the case by solving for
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(p)
for purely imaginary
p=\mathfrak{i}|p|
from Eqs.(
5.2
) using the exponential growth ansatz, as discussed earlier for the real
p
method.
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(\mathfrak{i}|p|)
is found to be a convex function of
|p|
as expected
Gu and Kitaev 2019
. The light cone and the associated
v_{B}
are determined by either the saddle-point or the pole contribution to
\mathcal{F}_{\mu,x}(t)
depending on whether
|p_{s}|<|p_{1}|
or
|p_{s}|>|p_{1}|
, respectively. The conditions also translate to
v_{s}(\equiv v(p_{s}))<v_{1}(\equiv v(p_{1}))
or
v_{s}>v_{1}
, respectively, where the velocity
v(p)=\mathfrak{i}\lambda^{\prime}_{\mathrm{L}}(p)
, since
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(\mathfrak{i}|p|)
is a convex (up) function of
p
(
Appendix C
).

For the condition
|p_{s}|<|p_{1}|
, the deformed contour, that goes through the saddle point
\mathfrak{i}|p_{s}|
on the imaginary axis in the evaluation of the integral in Eq.(
5.6
), does not enclose the pole and hence the latter does not contribute. As discussed in refs.
Gu and Kitaev 2019
;
Guo et al. 2019a
, this condition is realized when
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(0)\ll 2\pi T
, i.e., when the chaos is far away from maximal. In this case
\mathcal{F}_{\mu,x}(t)\approx\mathcal{F}^{s}_{\mu,x}(t)
and
v_{B}=v_{s}
. By making a small
p
approximation,
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(p)\simeq\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(0)-\tilde{D}p^{2}
(with
\tilde{D}>0
), we obtain
v_{B}=v_{s}=[4\tilde{D}\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(0)]^{1/2}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mu,x}(t)\sim e^{\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(0)t(1-x^{2}/v_{B}^{2}t^{2})}

( 5.10)

Here the quantity
\tilde{D}=v_{B}^{2}/4\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(0)
has the dimension of diffusion constant. For certain holographic theories
Blake 2016
and strongly interacting diffusive metals built from SYK-type quantum dots
Gu et al. 2017
;
Guo et al. 2019a
,
4\tilde{D}
has been found to be exactly equal to energy diffusion constant at low temperature.
\tilde{D}
has also been found to be same as spin diffusion constant at high temperature in a classical spin liquid
Bilitewski et al. 2018
, and even for classical XY model
Ruidas and Banerjee 2021
. Assuming that such relation between
\tilde{D}
and actual diffusion constant hold, at least, over a limited range of
(S,T)
in the phased diagram of SY chain, we use
\tilde{D}
as a proxy for diffusion constant and plot it as a function of
S
and
T
below.

For the other condition
|p_{s}|>|p_{1}|
, realized for
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(0)\approx 2\pi T
, the pole contributes and dominates over the saddle-point contribution, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\mu,x}(t)\approx\mathcal{F}^{p}_{\mu,x}(t)\sim e^{2\pi Tt(1-|x|/v_{B}t)}

( 5.11)

and
v_{B}=v_{m}
, as can be deduced from
\mathcal{F}_{\mu,x}(t)\approx 1
. The above corresponds to maximal chaos, realized for SYK-type non-Fermi liquids
Gu et al. 2017
;
Gu and Kitaev 2019
;
Guo et al. 2019a
.

For most part of the phase diagram [Fig.
3.1
] we have studied, we see that saddle point,
|p_{s}|
remains well below the pole,
|p_{1}|
and so the OTOC receives dominant contribution from the saddle point. See
Appendix C
for more details.
Therefore the chaos front travels with a speed
v_{B}=\mathfrak{i}\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{i}|p_{s}|)
and the Lyapunov exponent
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
is non-maximal, as was verified through direct calculation of
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}(S,T)
in Sec.
4
. The SY chain can be analytically shown to be maximally chaotic in the limit
S\to 0,~T\to 0
following the same methods

;
Maldacena and Stanford 2016
;
Banerjee and Altman 2017

6
Discussion

In this work, we have studied how quantum and thermal fluctuations influence propagation of quantum information, as diagnosed by butterfly velocity, along with chaotic growth rate characterized by a Lyapunov exponent, through one-dimensional generalizations of two paradigmatic zero-dimensional quantum spin glass models, Sachdev-Ye and
p
-spin glass models. These lattice models allow us to map out variations of
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
and
v_{B}
with temperature and quantum parameter deep inside the phases and near the non-trivial replica symmetry breaking spin glass transitions, as well as through crossovers between competing paramagnetic phases, e.g., spin liquid and local moment behaviors, and across the SG transition. We find distinct evolution of glassy complexity, indicated by maximum, or lack thereof, in the Lyapunov exponent and butterfly velocity in the PM phase, as a function of quantum and thermal fluctuations in the SY model. The maximum is seen only as a function of quantum parameter
S
, around the onset of two-step glassy relaxation, away from the glass transition from the PM side. The maximum is absent when the glass transition is approached by decreasing
T
. In contrast, the maximum occurs as function of both
T
and the quantum parameter
\Gamma
in the PM phase of
p
-spin glass. The maximum corresponds to intensified chaos due to maximal complexity arising from the sampling of exponentially large number of saddle points of the underlying glassy landscape. In the semiclassical limit, the maximal number of saddle points leads to extreme sensitivity to the initial condition
Correale et al. 2023
or chaos.

The spin glass models discussed here and other related models can be realized in cavity quantum electrodynamics (QED) platforms
Gopalakrishnan et al. 2011
;
Strack and Sachdev 2011
;
Guo et al. 2019b
;
Hosseinabadi et al. 2023a
;
Hosseinabadi et al. 2023b
, and even scrambling dynamics can be probed in this setup
Swingle et al. 2016
;
Marino and Rey 2019
. Our work needs to be extended in future in several directions. To better understand connection between glassy complexity
Correale et al. 2023
;
Anous and Haehl 2021
and quantum many-body chaos, it will be worthwhile to compute the complexity for the SY model using both semiclassical methods
Correale et al. 2023
and quantum Thouless-Anderson-Palmer (TAP) equations
Biroli and Cugliandolo 2001
, as have been done for quantum
p
-spin glass.
As
\tilde{D}\sim v_{B}^{2}/\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
often acts as a proxy to diffusion constant, it will be interesting to compare our predictions of
\tilde{D}
, from the asymptotic behavior of
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
and
v_{B}
from chaos calculations with actual transport calculations in a chain of spin glass quantum dots.

Our real-frequency saddle-point calculations of
\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}
and
v_{B}
, and corresponding bosonic spectral functions, in the SY model indicate subtle competition between spin liquid and local moment solutions, as well as strong
S
and
T
dependent corrections to the conformal solution for the SYK-like spin liquid maximal chaotic behavior, even at small values of
S\lesssim 0.05
and low temperature
T\sim 0.1
. This implies intriguing crossover in the small
S
region towards the expected maximally chaotic SYK spin liquid for
S\to 0
. We have not studied this crossover and the relative stability and intricate competition between spin liquid and local moment solutions in the small
S
region in much detail in this work. It will be important to study the chaos and spectral properties in the small-
S
regime in future work. Finally, it will be an exciting research direction to compute entanglement properties in various crossover regions and across the replica symmetry breaking spin glass transition in these large
N
models following methods
Haldar et al. 2020
;
Bera et al. 2023
similar to those applied for calculating entanglement entropy in lattice of SYK dots and in Hubbard model within dynamical mean-field theory.

\mathcal{Z}(\{J_{ij,x},J^{\prime}_{ij,x}\})=\mathrm{Tr}[\exp(-\beta\mathcal{H})]
(
\beta=1/T
) of the disordered model of Eq.(
2.1
) as an imaginary-time path integral using the bosonic representation
S_{i\alpha\beta,x}=b_{i\alpha,x}^{\dagger}b_{i\beta,x}-S\delta_{\alpha\beta}
with the constraint
\sum_{\alpha}b_{i\alpha,x}^{\dagger}b_{i\alpha,x}=SM
(
S\geq 0

S

are drawn from Gaussian distributions with zero mean and variances
J^{2}
and
J^{\prime 2}

to obtain

\overline{\mathcal{Z}^{n}}=\int\mathcal{D}(\bar{b},b)\mathcal{D}\lambda\mathrm{exp}[-S_{\mathrm{eff}}]

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{eff}}=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau\sum_{ix}\left[\bar{b}_{i\alpha,x}^{a}\partial_{\tau}b_{i\alpha,x}^{a}-\lambda^{a}_{i,x}(\tau)(\bar{b}_{i\alpha,x}^{a}b_{i\alpha,x}^{a}-SM)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2MN}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\Bigg[\frac{J^{2}}{2}\sum_{i<j,x}S_{i\alpha\beta,x}^{a}(\tau)S_{i\gamma\delta,x}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})S_{j\beta\alpha,x}^{a}(\tau)

\displaystyle S_{j\delta\gamma,x}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})+J^{\prime 2}\sum_{i,j,x}S_{i\alpha\beta,x}^{a}(\tau)S_{i\gamma\delta,x}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})S_{j\beta\alpha,x+1}^{a}(\tau)

\displaystyle S_{j\delta\gamma,x+1}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\Bigg]

(A.1)

Here the field
\lambda_{i,x}^{a}(\tau)
imposes the constraint on boson number and sum over repeated
\alpha,\beta,\dots
and
a,b
indices are assumed. In the
N\to\infty

Q_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta,x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
, and its conjugate

in the Wightmann correlation function indicates the presence of static spin glass order. Similar delta function term appears in the Fourier transform of other correlation functions, e.g.,
G^{aa}_{>}(t)=-(\mathfrak{i}/M)\sum_{\alpha}\langle b_{i\alpha,x}^{a}\bar{b}_{i\alpha,x}^{a}\rangle
, and similarly
G^{aa}_{<}(t)
.
We note that, since retarded Green’s function,
G^{aa}_{R}(t)=\theta(t)[G^{aa}_{>}(t)-G^{aa}_{<}(t)]
[
\theta(t)
is the Heaviside function] and
G^{aa}_{R}(\omega)
is obtained by analytically continuing
\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}\to\omega+\mathfrak{i}0^{+}
in Eq.(
3.5
),
G_{R}^{aa}(t)
is free of any static part even in the SG phase.

Appendix B
Imaginary-time path integral and saddle point equations in
p
-spin glass chain

As in the case of SY chain [
Appendix A
], we write the partition function
\mathcal{Z}(\{J_{ijk,x},J^{\pm}_{ijk,x}\})=\mathrm{Tr}[\exp(-\beta\mathcal{H})]
(
\beta=1/T
) of the disordered model of Eq.(
2.2
), where
J_{ijk,x}
and
J_{ijk,x}^{\pm}
are Gaussian random number with zero mean and variances
3J^{2}/(2N^{2})
and
3J^{\prime 2}/2N^{2}
, as an imaginary-time path integral. We calculate disorder-averaged quantities by introducing replicas
a=1,\dots,n
, and a Lagrangian multiplier
z(\tau)
to impose the spherical constraint, to obtain
\overline{\mathcal{Z}^{n}}=\int\mathcal{D}s\mathrm{exp}[-S_{\mathrm{eff}}]
, where

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum_{x}\bigg\{\int_{\tau}\left[\frac{1}{2\Gamma}\left(\frac{\partial s_{i,x}^{a}}{\partial\tau}\right)^{2}+\frac{z^{a}(\tau)}{2}\left((s_{i,x}^{a})^{2}-N\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}\sum_{ab}\left[\frac{J^{2}}{N^{2}}\left(\sum_{i}s_{i,x}^{a}(\tau)s_{i,x}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{3}\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{J^{\prime 2}}{N^{2}}\left(\sum_{i}s_{i,x}^{a}(\tau)s_{i,x}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\right)\left(\sum_{i}s_{i,x+1}^{a}(\tau)s_{i,x+1}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{J^{\prime 2}}{N^{2}}\left(\sum_{i}s_{i,x}^{a}(\tau)s_{i,x}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\right)^{2}\left(\sum_{i}s_{i,x+1}^{a}(\tau)s_{i,x+1}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\right)\right]\bigg\}

(B.1)

Here
\int_{\tau}=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau
. We now introduce the large
N
field
Q
and its conjugate
\Sigma
, where
Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i}\langle s_{i,x}^{a}(\tau)s_{i,x}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle
, through the identity

\displaystyle 1=

\displaystyle\int DQ_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\delta\Big(NQ_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-\sum_{i}s_{i,x}^{a}(\tau)s_{i,x}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int DQ_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})D\Sigma_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\mathrm{exp}\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}\Sigma_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Big(NQ_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-\sum_{i}s_{i,x}^{a}(\tau)s_{i,x}^{b}(\tau^{\prime})\Big)\Big\}

(B.2)

Inserting the identity and integrating over
s_{i,x}(\tau)
in
\overline{\mathcal{Z}^{n}}
leads to the effective action

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{eff}}=\sum_{x}\bigg\{\frac{N}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\ln\,\Big[\big(-\frac{1}{\Gamma}\partial_{\tau}^{2}+z^{a}(\tau)\big)\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\delta_{ab}

\displaystyle-\Sigma_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\Big]-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{a}\int_{\tau}z^{a}(\tau)

\displaystyle+\frac{N}{2}\sum_{ab}\int_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}\Big[\Sigma_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})-\frac{J^{2}}{4}Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{3}

\displaystyle-\frac{J^{\prime 2}}{4}\Big(Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})Q_{x+1}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{2}+Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{2}Q_{x+1}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\Big)\Big]\bigg\}

(B.3)

In the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
, we can take the saddle point with respect to
\Sigma_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
. This gives
\boldsymbol{Q}_{x}^{-1}=\boldsymbol{Q}_{0}^{-1}-\boldsymbol{\Sigma}
, where the matrix
\boldsymbol{Q}^{-1}_{0}
is given by
(Q_{0}^{-1})^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\big(-\frac{1}{\Gamma}\partial_{\tau}^{2}+z^{a}(\tau)\big)\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\delta_{ab}
.
Using this we eliminate
\Sigma_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})
from the action and obtain effective action in terms of
Q
,

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{eff}}=-\sum_{x}\bigg\{\frac{N}{2}\mathrm{Tr}\ln\,\boldsymbol{Q}_{x}-\frac{N}{2}\sum_{a}\int_{\tau}z^{a}(\tau)\big(Q_{x}^{aa}(\tau,\tau)-1{\big)}

\displaystyle+N\sum_{a,b}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau d\tau^{\prime}\Big(\frac{1}{2\Gamma}\partial_{\tau}^{2}Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\delta_{ab}

\displaystyle+\frac{J^{2}}{4}Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{3}+\frac{J^{\prime 2}}{4}Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})Q_{x+1}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{J^{\prime 2}}{4}Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{2}Q_{x+1}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\Big)\bigg\}

(B.4)

Taking the saddle point with respect to
\boldsymbol{Q}
gives the Dyson equation,

\displaystyle(Q_{x}^{-1})^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=\Big(-\frac{1}{\Gamma}\partial_{\tau}^{2}+z_{a}(\tau)\Big)\delta(\tau-\tau^{\prime})\delta_{ab}

\displaystyle-\frac{3J^{2}}{2}Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{2}-\frac{J^{\prime 2}}{2}\Big(Q_{x+1}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{2}+Q_{x-1}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle-J^{\prime 2}Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\Big(Q_{x+1}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime}){+Q_{x-1}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})}\Big)

(B.5)

Considering space and time translation invariant solutions for the Dyson equation, of the form
Q_{x}^{ab}(\tau,\tau^{\prime})=Q^{ab}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
and
z^{a}(\tau)=z^{a}
, the above equation reduces to,

\displaystyle(Q^{-1})^{ab}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k})

\displaystyle=(\frac{1}{\Gamma}\omega_{k}^{2}+z^{a})\delta_{ab}-\Sigma_{ab}(\omega_{k})

(B.6a)

\displaystyle\Sigma^{ab}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{3\tilde{J}^{2}}{2}Q^{ab}(\tau)^{2}

(B.6b)

where
\tilde{J}^{2}=J^{2}+2J^{\prime 2}
. The above large
N
saddle-point equations are the same as that of the zero-dimensional model
Cugliandolo et al. 2001
.

Saddle point equation in PM phase—

Using the replica diagonal ansatz and analytically continuing to real frequency, we obtain the saddle-point equation for the retarded Green’s function,

\displaystyle[Q_{R}(\omega)]^{-1}=-\frac{1}{\Gamma}\omega^{2}+z-\Sigma_{R}(\omega)

(B.7a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{R}(\omega)=\Sigma(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}\to\omega+\mathfrak{i}0^{+})

(B.7b)

Following a procedure similar to that in the case of SY model (
Appendix A
), the self-energy can be written as,

\displaystyle\Sigma_{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{i}\frac{3\tilde{J}^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\mathfrak{i}\omega t}\Big(n_{1}^{2}(t)-n_{2}^{2}(t)\Big),

(B.8a)

\displaystyle n_{1}(t)

\displaystyle=\int d\omega e^{-\mathfrak{i}\omega t}\rho(\omega)n_{B}(-\omega),

\displaystyle n_{2}(t)

\displaystyle=\int d\omega e^{-\mathfrak{i}\omega t}\rho(\omega)n_{B}(\omega)

where
\rho(\omega)=-(1/\pi)\mathrm{Im}\,Q_{R}(\omega)
.The saddle-point equations can be solved numerically, following steps similar to that discussed in the case of SY model.

Saddle point equation in SG phase—

Using 1RSB ansatz, after analytical continuation to real frequency
\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}\to\omega+\mathfrak{i}0^{+}
only the regular part (see Sec.
3.2
) contributes to the retarded Green’s function
Q_{R}^{ab}(\omega)=Q_{R}(\omega)\delta_{ab}=\tilde{Q}(\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}\to\omega+\mathfrak{i}0^{+})\delta_{ab}
such that

\displaystyle[Q_{R}(\omega)]^{-1}=-\frac{1}{\Gamma}\omega^{2}+z-\Sigma_{R}(\omega),

(B.9a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{R}(\omega)=\frac{3{J}^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau e^{\mathfrak{i}\omega_{k}\tau}[\tilde{Q}(\tau)]^{2}+3\tilde{J}^{2}q_{\mathrm{EA}}Q_{R}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{i}\frac{3\tilde{J}^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\mathfrak{i}\omega t}\Big(n_{1}^{2}(t)-n_{2}^{2}(t)\Big)+3\tilde{J}^{2}q_{\mathrm{EA}}Q_{R}(\omega),

(B.9b)

where

\displaystyle n_{1}(t)=\int d\omega e^{-\mathfrak{i}\omega t}\rho(\omega)n_{B}(-\omega),

(B.9c)

\displaystyle n_{2}(t)=\int d\omega e^{-\mathfrak{i}\omega t}\rho(\omega)n_{B}(\omega)

(B.9d)

The spherical constraint now takes the form,

\displaystyle-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega\rho(\omega)n_{B}(\omega)=1-q_{\mathrm{EA}}

(B.10)

The Wightmann functions in the PM phase are given by,

\displaystyle Q_{lr}(t)=Q(\mathfrak{i}t+\beta/2)=-\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-\mathfrak{i}\omega t}\frac{\pi\rho(\omega)}{\mathrm{sinh}\frac{\beta\omega}{2}},

\displaystyle Q_{lr}^{ab}(t)=\epsilon_{ab}q_{\mathrm{EA}}-\delta_{ab}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-\mathfrak{i}\omega t}\frac{\pi\rho(\omega)}{\mathrm{sinh}\frac{\beta\omega}{2}}

(B.12)

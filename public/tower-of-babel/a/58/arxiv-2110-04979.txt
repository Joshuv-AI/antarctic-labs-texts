Analysis on Tollmien-Schlichting wave in the Prandtl-Hartmann Regime
Cheng-Jie Liu, Tong Yang, Zhu Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the instability induced by the Tollmien-Schlichting wave governed by the MHD system in the Prandtl-Hartmann regime. The interaction of the inviscid mode and viscous mode that leads to the instability is analyzed by the introduction of a new decomposition of the Orr-Sommerfeld operator on the velocity and magnetic fields. The critical Gevrey index for the instability is justified by constructing the growing mode in the essential frequency and it is shown to be the same as the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations in the Prandtl regime. This result justifies rigorously the physical understanding that the transverse magnetic field to the boundary in the Prandtl-Hartmann regime has no extra stabilizing effect on the Tollmien-Schlichting wave.



1.
Introduction

It is a classical problem in fluid mechanics about the stability and instability of different hydrodynamic patterns in various physical settings, in particular in the transition region from laminar flow to turbulence that involves different
modes interaction in the boundary layer. The analysis study can be traced back to the early work by Lord Rayleigh and Heisenberg among many others. In fact, for inviscid flow, the classical criterion for stability was given by Rayleigh saying that a necessary condition for instability of shear flow profile is that the shear flow must have an inflection point, and it was later refined by Fjortoft, see
[
5
,
39
]
. On the other hand, for viscous flow,
except the case of the linear Couette flow, which is proved to be linearly stable for all Reynolds numbers by Romanov
[
37
]
, all other profiles (including those which are inviscid stable) are shown to be linearly unstable for large Reynolds numbers
[
5
,
9
,
39
]
.

Despite the tremendous progress on the mathematical theory on the high Reynolds number limit, in particular for the Navier-Stokes equations, there are still many challenging and unsolved mathematical problems. This paper aims to study
a well-known physical phenomenon of the instability induced by Tollmien-Schlichting wave for the electrically
conducting fluid in the transition region from laminar flow to turbulence. For this,
we consider the following 2D incompressible MHD system that is a fundamental model describing behavior of electrically conducting fluid:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\vec{U}+\vec{U}\cdot\nabla\vec{U}+\nabla p-\frac{1}{\hbox{Re}}\triangle\vec{U}=S\vec{H}\cdot\nabla\vec{H},\\
\partial_{t}\vec{H}-\hbox{curl}(\vec{U}\times\vec{H})+\frac{1}{\hbox{Rm}}\hbox{curl curl}\vec{H}=\vec{0},\\
\hbox{div}\vec{U}=0,\quad\hbox{div}\vec{H}=0,\quad(x,y)\in\Omega=\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\end{array}\right.

where the physical parameters
Re
and
Rm
are the hydrodynamic and magnetic Reynolds numbers respectively, and

S=\frac{\hbox{Ha}^{2}}{\hbox{Re}\hbox{Rm}}

with
Ha
being Hartmann number. Here
\vec{U}=(u,v)
and
\vec{H}=(h,g)
represent the velocity field and magnetic field respectively, and
p
stands for the total pressure.

Precisely, we consider
the problem in the Prandtl-Hartmann regime by taking the following scaling for large Reynolds number:

\displaystyle\hbox{Re}\sim\frac{1}{\varepsilon},\qquad\hbox{Ha}\sim\hbox{Rm}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},

where
\varepsilon
is a small parameter. Hence, the system (
1
) becomes

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\vec{U}+\vec{U}\cdot\nabla\vec{U}+\nabla p-\varepsilon\triangle\vec{U}=\sqrt{\varepsilon}\vec{H}\cdot\nabla\vec{H},\\
\partial_{t}\vec{H}-\hbox{curl}(\vec{U}\times\vec{H})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\hbox{curl curl}\vec{H}=\vec{0},\\
\hbox{div}\vec{U}=0,\quad\hbox{div}\vec{H}=0,\quad t>0,\quad(x,y)\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Many physical literatures predict that under this scaling, the Hartmann boundary layer develops near the boundary by the transversal magnetic field, cf. the original work by Hartmann
[
22
]
. For simplicity, we consider the case with the transverse background magnetic field
\vec{H}=(0,1)
. In this case, the classical Prandtl-Hartmann boundary layer profile

\displaystyle(\vec{U}_{s},\vec{H}_{s})=[(U_{s}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),0),(H_{s}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),1)]=[(u_{\infty}(1-e^{-\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}}),0),(h_{\infty}-u_{\infty}e^{-\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}},1)]

(1.7)

is an exact steady solution to the above system, where the far fields
u_{\infty}
and
h_{\infty}
are two given
constants.

Then it is very natural to investigate its stability/ instability properties along the dynamics of (
1
).
In the following discussion, we take
u_{\infty}=1
without loss of generality.

One of the powerful analytic tools initiated by Orr and Sommerfeld is the spectral analysis by studying the Fourier normal mode behavior through the famous Orr-Sommerfeld equation
derived from the linearization of the incompressible Navier-Stokes equations around a given shear flow profile. As for the stability and instability investigation, tremendous progress has been made since the pioneer work by Heisenberg, C.C. Lin, Tollmien and Schlichting (see
[
23
,
27
,
39
]
and the reference therein), after the fundamental study on the boundary layer around a solid body by Prandtl. The related theories influenced by the wing design for airplanes have crucial impact on the development of aerodynamics because of the importance in understanding on the transition from laminar flow to turbulence that is also related to the separation of boundary layer. A large number of literatures have been devoted to the estimation of the critical Reynolds number for stability
based on the Navier-Stokes equations for various shear flow pattens, such as Poiseuille flow, Blasius profile, exponential suction profile, etc. Without viscosity, the Orr-Sommerfeld equation is the Rayleigh equation for inviscid flow.
Hence, Orr-Sommerfeld equation can be viewed as a singular perturbation of Rayleigh equation in high Reynolds number.

In fact, there have been extensive mathematical studies on the construction of growing modes to the Navier-Stokes equation that is related to the stability of boundary layer profile and validity of Prandtl ansatz. There are two destabilizing mechanisms of the boundary layer.

(a)

Instability at the inviscid level. If the boundary layer profile possesses a spectral instability for the inviscid system, then such instability persists at the viscous level for small viscosity
\varepsilon\ll 1
. In fact, in this case one can show that the unstable eigenvalue for the rescaled viscous system is of order
O(1)
, which leads to a strong ill-posedness of linearized system below analytic regularity
[
7
]
as well as a nonlinear instability in
L^{\infty}
-space
[
13
,
12
]
.

(b)

Tollmien-Schlichting instability. Even though the boundary layer profile is stable for the inviscid system, there is still a spectral instability at the Navier-Stokes level due to the small viscosity. A recent important work by Grenier-Guo-Nguyen
[
10
]
gave the precise description on such an unstable
eigenvalue to the classical Orr-Sommerfeld equation by deriving the pointwise bounds on the Green functions
associated to the corresponding Rayleigh and Airy operators. Roughly speaking, they decompose

\displaystyle\mbox{ Orr-Sommerfeld operator}

\displaystyle=\mbox{Rayleigh operator+ Diffusion operator,}

\displaystyle=\mbox{Airy operator+ Regular operator}.

By careful estimation and iteration, they gave a rigorous description of the unstable mode by showing that
if the Reynolds number
Re
is sufficiently large, there exists an unstable solution to the rescaled Navier-Stokes equation around any monotone, concave boundary layer profile. The growing mode is localized in the frequency
[\text{Re}^{-\frac{1}{8}},\text{Re}^{-\frac{1}{12}}]
with growth rate of order
\text{Re}^{-\frac{1}{8}}
, which leads to an ill-posedness for the linearized system in the original variables below Gevrey regularity with index
\frac{3}{2}.
This
analysis is consistent with the formation of Tollmien-Schlichting wave near the boundary due to the small viscosity
that is well documented in physical literatures, e.g.
[
5
,
39
]
. See also the asymptotic analysis by Wasow
[
42
]
. Note that the instability in this case is weaker than the previous one in the sense that the unstable eigenvalue for the rescaled system vanishes in the high Reynold number limit. Therefore, it is more difficult to bootstrap this linear instability to the nonlinear instability. Recently there are several
important progress in this direction, see
[
11
,
14
]
, where the instability of order
\text{Re}^{-\frac{1}{4}}
has been justified in the nonlinear settings.

Note that the above instability mechanisms occur in the high tangential frequency regime. Hence,
if initially data in this high frequency regime has small amplitude so that the growth induced
by the instability is negligible over the time scale of order
O(1)
, one can still expect the stability of boundary layer profiles and hence the validity of Prandtl ansatz in short time. For the analytic data, the inviscid limit for the Navier-Stokes equation is well-understood and a lot of important progress has been made in this direction. In fact, the verification of the Prandtl ansatz was achieved by Sammartino and Caflisch
[
38
]
. See also the work
[
40
]
by Wang-Wang-Zhang for a new proof based on the energy approach. If the initial vorticity supported away from boundary, Maekawa
[
32
]
justified the inviscid limit for 2D Navier-Stokes equation and this result was generalized to 3D in
[
6
]
. Remarkably, by obtaining some uniform bounds on vorticity, Nguyen and Nguyen
[
35
]
directly justified the inviscid limit in
L^{2}
-topology, without using any boundary layer expansion. Very recently, this limit was proved in 2D by Kukavica, Vicol and Wang
[
26
]
by assuming analyticity only near the boundary. See also
[
41
]
for the 3D generalization. If the boundary layer profile is
assumed to be monotone and concave, Gerárd-Varet-Maekawa-Masmoudi
[
18
]
proved the Gevrey stability for Navier-Stokes equation with critical index
\frac{3}{2}
and then justified the general Prandtl ansatz
[
19
]
at the same level of regularity. See also a recent improvement
[
2
]
. There is also some important progress for the steady case. We refer to
[
17
,
21
,
24
,
25
]
and references therein. Finally, we refer to
[
3
,
4
,
16
,
29
]
for the instabilities in Prandtl boundary layer model and some other
physical models.

Back to MHD, it is interesting to understand the effect of magnetic fields on the hydrodynamic instability mechanisms mentioned above. In fact, it is known that a strong tangential magnetic field can stabilize the boundary layer
[
20
,
30
]
. For transversal magnetic field, however, there are many numerical experiments
[
15
,
28
,
31
,
33
,
36
]
show that the laminar Hartmann layer loses its stability through
a transition to turbulence in the high Reynold number limit. The main purpose of this paper is to investigate the mechanism of instability from the mathematical point of view. In particular, as the first step in this direction, we aim to justify the presence of Tollmien-Schlichting instability at the linear level in the Hartmann layer.

To start with, let us first derive the Orr-Sommerfeld system for the stream functions of the velocity and magnetic fields. Consider the linearization of the system (
1
) around a boundary layer profile
(\vec{U}_{s},\vec{H}_{s})=({U}_{s}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}),0,{H}_{s}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}),1)
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}\vec{U}+U_{s}\partial_{x}\vec{U}+v\partial_{y}U_{s}\vec{e}_{1}+\nabla p-\varepsilon\triangle\vec{U}-\sqrt{\varepsilon}(H_{s}\partial_{x}\vec{H}+g\partial_{y}H_{s}\vec{e}_{1})-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{y}\vec{H}=\vec{0},\\
\partial_{t}\vec{H}+U_{s}\partial_{x}\vec{H}+v\partial_{y}H_{s}\vec{e}_{1}-H_{s}\partial_{x}\vec{U}-g\partial_{y}U_{s}\vec{e}_{1}-\partial_{y}\vec{U}-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\triangle\vec{H}=\vec{0},\\
\hbox{div}\vec{U}=0,\quad\hbox{div}\vec{H}=0,\quad t>0,\quad(x,y)\in\Omega.\end{array}\right.

In coherence with physical literature
[
28
,
31
,
36
]
, we supplement (
1
) with the following no-slip boundary condition on velocity field and perfect conducting wall condition on magnetic field:

\displaystyle\vec{U}|_{y=0}=(\partial_{y}h,g)|_{y=0}=\vec{0}.

(1.11)

Notice that under the boundary conditions (
1.11
), the divergence free condition of
\vec{H}
is preserved along (
1
). To study the linear system (
1
), (
1.11
), we introduce the following rescaled variables:

\tau=\frac{t}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},\,X=\frac{x}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}},\,Y=\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}.

Then (
1
) reads

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{\tau}\vec{U}+U_{s}\partial_{X}\vec{U}+v\partial_{Y}U_{s}\vec{e}_{1}+\nabla_{X,Y}p-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\triangle_{X,Y}\vec{U}-\sqrt{\varepsilon}(H_{s}\partial_{X}\vec{H}+g\partial_{Y}H_{s}\vec{e}_{1})-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}\vec{H}=\vec{0},\\
\partial_{\tau}\vec{H}+U_{s}\partial_{X}\vec{H}+v\partial_{Y}H_{s}\vec{e}_{1}-H_{s}\partial_{X}\vec{U}-g\partial_{Y}U_{s}\vec{e}_{1}-\partial_{Y}\vec{U}-\triangle_{X,Y}\vec{H}=\vec{0},\\
\hbox{div}_{X,Y}\vec{U}=0,\quad\hbox{div}_{X,Y}\vec{H}=0,\quad t>0,\quad(X,Y)\in\Omega_{\varepsilon}\triangleq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\\
\vec{U}\mid_{Y=0}=(\partial_{Y}h,g)\mid_{Y=0}=\vec{0}.\end{array}\right.

Thanks to divergence free and boundary conditions of both
\vec{U}
and
\vec{H}
, it is convenient to introduce the following stream functions
\Phi
and
\Psi
of
\vec{U}
and
\vec{H}
respectively:

\displaystyle\vec{U}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X,Y}^{\perp}\Phi=(\partial_{Y}\Phi,-\partial_{X}\Phi),~\Phi|_{Y=0}=0,

\displaystyle\vec{H}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{X,Y}^{\perp}\Psi=(\partial_{Y}\Psi,-\partial_{X}\Psi),~\Psi|_{Y=0}=0.

We further consider the solutions to linearized system (
1
) taken the following normal modes form:

\displaystyle(\vec{U},\vec{H})=e^{i\alpha(X-c\tau)}(\partial_{Y}\Phi,-i\alpha\Phi,\partial_{Y}\Psi,-i\alpha\Psi)(Y),

(1.16)

which lead to the following Orr-Sommerfeld equations:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\sqrt{\varepsilon}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\Phi+i\alpha(U_{s}-{c})(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\Phi-i\alpha\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\Phi\\
&\qquad\quad-i\alpha\sqrt{\varepsilon}\left(H_{s}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\Psi-\partial_{Y}^{2}H_{s}\Psi\right)-\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}\left(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2}\right)\Psi=0,\\
&-(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\Psi+i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\Psi-i\alpha H_{s}\Phi-\partial_{Y}\Phi=0,\\
&\Phi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\Phi|_{Y=0}=\Psi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}^{2}\Psi|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.17)

From the second equation one has

-\partial_{Y}\left(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2}\right)\Psi=\partial_{Y}^{2}\Phi+i\alpha\partial_{Y}\left(H_{s}\Phi-(U_{s}-c)\Psi\right).

Then plugging it into (
1.17
), we get the following equivalent system to (
1.17
):

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{i}{n}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\Phi+(U_{s}-\hat{c})(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\Phi-\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\Phi\\
&\qquad\quad-\sqrt{\varepsilon}H_{s}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\Psi+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}^{2}H_{s}\Psi-\frac{\alpha}{n}\partial_{Y}\left((U_{s}-c)\Psi-H_{s}\Phi\right)-\frac{i\alpha^{2}}{n}\Phi=0,\\
&-(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\Psi+i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\Psi-i\alpha H_{s}\Phi-\partial_{Y}\Phi=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.18)

with the boundary condition

\Phi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\Phi|_{Y=0}=\Psi|_{Y=0}=0.

(1.19)

Here we have used the notations

\displaystyle n=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\text{ and }\hat{c}=c+\frac{i}{n},

where
n
is the frequency in the original variable. Note that by restricting the second equation in (
1.18
) on the boundary
\{Y=0\}
and using the boundary conditions
\Phi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\Phi|_{Y=0}=\Psi|_{Y=0}=0
, the condition
\partial_{Y}^{2}\Psi|_{Y=0}=0
automatically holds. Thus, we omit it in the formulation (
1.18
) and (
1.19
).
In the following, without confusion we denote the linear operator of the Orr-Sommerfeld system (
1.18
) by
\mbox{OS}(\Phi,\Psi)
. In some places we replace
\mbox{OS}(\Phi,\Psi)
by
\mbox{OS}_{c}(\Phi,\Psi)
to emphasize the dependence of
c
. In view of (
1.16
), if the system (
1.18
) together with (
1.19
) has a non-trivial solution
(\Psi,\Psi)
with
\text{Im}c>0
, then the linearized system (
1
) is spectral unstable.

With the above notations, we can now state the main result as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\vec{U}_{s},\vec{H}_{s})
be the Hartmann layer profile given in (
1.7
). There is a positive constant
A_{0}>1
such that for any
A\geq A_{0}
, there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
, such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
\alpha=A\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
, the linearized MHD system (
1
) together with (
1.11
) has a solution
(\vec{U},\vec{H})
in the form of

\displaystyle(\vec{U},\vec{H})=e^{\frac{i\alpha}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}(x-c_{\varepsilon}t)}(\partial_{Y}\Phi,-i\alpha\Phi,\partial_{Y}\Psi,-i\alpha\Psi)(Y),~Y:=\frac{y}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}

(1.20)

for some
c_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{C}
with
\alpha\text{Im}c_{\varepsilon}\approx\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Moreover, the result also holds for
\alpha=M\varepsilon^{\beta}
with
M>0
and
\beta\in(3/28,1/8).

Remark 1.2
.

In terms of instability, the wave solution (
1.20
) is localized in the frequency
\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\in(A_{0}\varepsilon^{-\frac{3}{8}},B_{0}\varepsilon^{-\frac{11}{28}})
and it grows exponentially in time with the rate
\frac{\alpha\text{Im}c_{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\approx\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{4}}
. Hence this shows
the instability of linearized MHD system around the Prandtl-Hartmann layer with critical Gevrey index
\frac{3}{2}
that is
the same as the classical Navier-Stokes equations. Therefore, it reveals that the transversal magnetic field to the
boundary in the Prandtl-Hartmann regime does not enhance the stability of the boundary layer.
This is in sharp contrast to the stabilizing effect of the tangential magnetic field in the strong nonlinear
boundary layer regime and the Shercliff regime
[
20
,
30
]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Let us comment why the critical index for instability is the same as for classical Navier-Stokes equations in the model considered in this paper. As observed in
[
20
,
43
]
, the magnetic field creates a damping term in the reduced boundary layer equation. It is natural to expect that such a feature persists in the full system (
1
). In fact, compared with the classical Navier-Stokes system, the Lorenz force in the current scaling contributes an extra term
\frac{i}{n}
in the spectrum parameter
\hat{c}
that corresponds to the damping effect of magnetic field. However, in view of the leading order dispersion relation (
3.15
), this damping term is negligible so that it has no
essential effect on the formation of Tollmien-Schlichting wave.
Nevertheless, in the full system the coupling of velocity and the magnetic fields complicates the analysis so that it is more involved to justify this intuitive understanding rigorously.

.

In the proof given in Section 5, all arguments work for
\beta\in(1/12,1/8)

On the other hand, in view of (

In the following, we will briefly present the key ingredients in the proof of Theorem
1.1

and the novelty of the new approach. For illustration, we only consider the problem in the regime
with
\alpha\sim\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
.

Strategy and novelty:

•

First of all, even though the equations on the stream functions for the velocity and magnetic fields are coupled, one can still estimate the stream function of the magnetic field in terms of the stream function of the velocity field by a given boundary condition.

•

To investigate the instability
induced by the Tollmien-Schlichting wave, as for the Navier-Stokes equations, we can start with an approximate solution to the Orr-Sommerfeld system by constructing the slow mode (inviscid mode) and fast mode (viscous mode). One can observe that in the Prandtl-Hartmann regime, the terms involving magnetic stream function
\Psi
in the first equation of (
1.18
) is formally of high order. Therefore, it is permissible to construct these modes near those of classical Orr-Sommerfeld system for Navier-Stokes system constructed in
[
10
]
. The interaction of these two modes by a linear combination gives an approximate growing mode solution
(\Phi_{\text{app}}(Y;c),\Psi_{\text{app}}(Y;c))
to the system (
1.18
) with zero boundary conditions
\Phi_{\text{app}}(0;c)=\Psi_{\text{app}}(0;c)=0
on the stream functions. Then to recover the no-slip boundary condition, we obtain the dispersion relation
\Gamma_{0}(c)=0
, where
\Gamma_{0}(c)=\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}(0;c)
is the boundary data of the first order derivative of approximate solution, see (
3.15
). A formal expansion of
\Gamma_{0}(c)
gives the asymptotic behavior of its zero point:

\displaystyle c\approx(A+A^{-1}e^{\frac{1}{4}\pi i})\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}+O(1)A^{-2}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}+C_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{4}}|\log\varepsilon|,\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+},

for suitably large but fixed
A>1
.
We will apply Rouché’s theorem to justify this formal expansion.

To use Rouché’s Theorem in finding the critical point for instability of fluid mechanics equation can be traced back to the early paper
[
34
]
and also the recent works
[
3
,
4
]
on some boundary layer models. In the present paper, given that the underlying unstable eigenvalue vanishes in the high Reynold number limit, the instability under consideration is of different nature compared with
[
3
,
4
,
34
]
. The novelty therefore comes from a suitable choice of loops that shrink to the original point as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
and in the meantime surround the possible zero points of
\Gamma_{0}(c)
. Then by establishing some sharp pointwise estimates of slow and fast modes, we are able to show that
\Gamma_{0}(c)
is approximated by its linearization around the leading part of the unstable eigenvalue. This concludes that
\Gamma_{0}
has a simple root. Moreover, we can show that
\Gamma_{0}(c)
has a lower bound of order
O(1)
on these loops, which survives in the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

This idea will be crucially used to justify the existence of exact growing mode for the full original system.

•

The next step is to show the solvability of Orr-Sommerfeld system (
1.18
) in order to obtain an exact growing mode near the approximate one. The key point is to obtain some energy estimate for the resolvent problem. Using the idea introduced in
[
1
,
2
,
17
,
19
]
, we solve the resolvent problem by imposing the Navier-slip boundary condition on velocity field, so that the multiplier
\frac{\omega}{\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}}
can be used to obtain a weighted
L^{2}
-estimate
\|\frac{\omega}{|\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|_{L^{2}}
on vorticity. One of the new observations used in this step is that due to the favorable boundary condition, the stability estimates obtained in
[
2
,
19
]
can be extended to Gevrey function spaces with arbitrarily large index,
at least for strongly concave shear flow, cf. Lemma
4.3
and Remark
4.4
. The motivation of studying the resolvent estimates in this step is that we can control the
H^{2}
-norm of the remainder in terms of the
L^{2}
-norms of the error terms generated by the slow and fast modes, together with a small factor in the order of
O(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{16}})
when compared with
L^{\infty}
-norms. This small factor
is used to obtain the desired results without higher order expansion.

•

\text{OS}_{d}-\text{OS}_{s}
iteration: Above procedure however is not enough to close the resolvent estimate. A difficulty arises from the magnetic coupling. In fact, the terms coming from the Lorentz force exhibit a slow decay in
Y
as
e^{-\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}Y}
, which are not compatible with above weighted estimate. To overcome this, we first observe that all of these slow decay terms in the equation of velocity stream function
\Phi
are in the form of total derivatives. This observation leads us to the introduction of
a new decomposition of the solution operator by taking account of the strong decay property of the background solution and the differential structure in the operator together with the smallness in the frequency regime under consideration. Then the exact solution to the Orr-Sommerfeld system can be obtained by a series of approximate solutions through interation.
To illustrate this, note that we can have two ways to represent
OS
. The first one is

\mbox{OS}=\mbox{OS}_{d}+L_{d},

(1.23)

where
\mbox{OS}_{d}
is defined later in (
4.5
) and it comes from (
1.18
) by retaining only Navier-Stokes part in the first equation. Notice that
\mbox{OS}_{d}
in principle behaves like a regular viscous perturbation of Rayleigh’s equation so that the weighted estimate on the vorticity can be obtained by applying the trick in
[
8

. However, the remaining term
L_{d}(\cdot)\triangleq(\partial_{Y}R_{1}(\cdot)+i\alpha R_{2}(\cdot),0)
where

and

are defined in (
4.17
) that can not be regarded as a source term of
\mbox{OS}_{d}
due to slow decay of magnetic field. Hence, we introduce another decomposition

\mbox{OS}=\mbox{OS}_{s}+L_{s},

(1.24)

where
\mbox{OS}_{s}
is defined in (
4.16

1.18
). On one hand, due to the elliptic structure of
\mbox{OS}_{s}

L_{d}
in (
1.23

\mbox{OS}_{s}

in (

as

With this decomposition, as an analogy of the celebrated Rayleigh-Airy iteration introduced in

\mbox{OS}_{s}

2.
Estimation on magnetic field

In this section, we will study the following magnetic equation with Dirichlet boundary condition

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi+i\alpha(U_{s}-c)\varphi=f,~Y>0,\\
&\varphi|_{Y=0}=\varphi_{b},\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

where
\varphi_{b}
is a given constant and
f
is a source.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exist positive constants
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
and
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
if
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
and
c
lies in the disk
D_{\gamma_{0}}=\{c\in\mathbb{C}\mid|c|<\gamma_{0}\}
, then for any
\eta\in(0,\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}}{4})
and
f\in L^{\infty}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),
there exists a unique solution
\varphi\in W^{2,\infty}_{\eta}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\varphi_{b}|+\alpha^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}|\varphi_{b}|+\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha|\varphi_{b}|+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}.

Moreover, if both
\varphi_{b}
and
f
are holomorphic in
c
in
D_{\gamma_{0}}
, then
\varphi
is holomorphic in
D_{\gamma_{0}}
.

Proof.

Noting that
U_{s}\rightarrow 1
as
Y\rightarrow+\infty
, we rewrite (
2.1
) as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\partial_{Y}^{2}\varphi+(i\alpha+\alpha^{2}-i\alpha c)\varphi=f+i\alpha(1-U_{s})\varphi,~Y>0,\\
&\varphi|_{Y=0}=\varphi_{b}.\end{aligned}\right.

Observe that
i\alpha+\alpha^{2}-i\alpha c\sim i\alpha
for small
\alpha,c
.
Let
\xi\triangleq\sqrt{i\alpha+\alpha^{2}-i\alpha c},
where the root is taken so that
\text{Re}\xi>0.
Then
\text{Re}\xi\sim\text{Re}\left(e^{\frac{\pi i}{4}}\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}}{2}.
Thus there exist positive constants
\alpha_{0}
and
\gamma_{0}
, such that for
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})
and
c\in D_{\gamma_{0}}
,
\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}}{3}<\text{Re}\xi<\frac{2\sqrt{2\alpha}}{3}

holds. Now we decompose

\displaystyle\varphi(Y)=e^{-\xi Y}\varphi_{b}+\tilde{\varphi}(Y),

(2.3)

where
\tilde{\varphi}(Y)
satisfies the following equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\partial_{Y}^{2}\tilde{\varphi}+\xi^{2}\tilde{\varphi}=\tilde{f}+i\alpha(1-U_{s})\tilde{\varphi},~Y>0,\\
&\tilde{\varphi}|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(2.4)

with
\tilde{f}\triangleq f+i\alpha(1-U_{s})e^{-\xi Y}\varphi_{b}.
To solve (
2.4
), we introduce the following iteration scheme:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\partial_{Y}^{2}\tilde{\varphi}^{k+1}+\xi^{2}\tilde{\varphi}^{k+1}=\tilde{f}+i\alpha(1-U_{s})\tilde{\varphi}^{k},~Y>0,\\
&\tilde{\varphi}^{k+1}|_{Y=0}=0,~\tilde{\varphi}^{0}\equiv 0.\end{aligned}\right.

The sequence
\{\tilde{\varphi}^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
can be solved inductively by the following mild formulation:

\displaystyle\tilde{\varphi}^{k+1}(Y)=\int_{0}^{Y}e^{-\xi(Y-Y^{\prime})}{\rm d}Y^{\prime}\int_{Y^{\prime}}^{\infty}e^{\xi(Y^{\prime}-Y^{\prime\prime})}\left(\tilde{f}+i\alpha(1-U_{s})\tilde{\varphi}^{k}\right)(Y^{\prime\prime}){\rm d}Y^{\prime\prime}.

(2.5)

For any
\eta\in(0,\frac{\sqrt{2\alpha}}{4})
, using the decay property of boundary layer profile
|1-U_{s}(Y)|\lesssim e^{-s_{0}Y}
, we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{\varphi}^{k+1}(Y)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{Y}e^{-\text{Re}\xi(Y-Y^{\prime})}{\rm d}Y^{\prime}\int_{Y^{\prime}}^{\infty}e^{\text{Re}\xi(Y^{\prime}-Y^{\prime\prime})}e^{-\eta Y^{\prime\prime}}\left(\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}+\alpha e^{-s_{0}Y^{\prime\prime}}\|\tilde{\varphi}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\right){\rm d}Y^{\prime\prime}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{Y}e^{-\text{Re}\xi(Y-Y^{\prime})-\eta Y^{\prime}}\times\left(\frac{\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}}{\text{Re}\xi+\eta}+\frac{\alpha\|\tilde{\varphi}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}}{\text{Re}\xi+\eta+s_{0}}e^{-s_{0}Y^{\prime}}\right){\rm d}Y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{e^{-\eta Y}}{(\text{Re}\xi)^{2}-\eta^{2}}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}+\alpha e^{-\eta Y}\|\tilde{\varphi}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\int_{0}^{Y}e^{-(s_{0}+\eta-\text{Re}\xi)Y^{\prime}}{\rm d}Y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{-1}e^{-\eta Y}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}+C\alpha e^{-\eta Y}\|\tilde{\varphi}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}.

Here we have used the fact that
(\text{Re}\xi)^{2}-\eta^{2}\geq\frac{2\alpha}{9}-\frac{\alpha}{8}>\frac{\alpha}{12}
. Taking
\alpha_{0}
smaller if necessary, we obtain from (
2.6
) the following uniform-in-
k
estimate:

\|\tilde{\varphi}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\leq 2C\alpha^{-1}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}},~k=0,1,\cdots.

Applying the similar estimate to
\tilde{\varphi}^{k+1}-\tilde{\varphi}^{k}
gives

\|\tilde{\varphi}^{k+1}-\tilde{\varphi}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\leq C\alpha\|\tilde{\varphi}^{k}-\tilde{\varphi}^{k-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\tilde{\varphi}^{k}-\tilde{\varphi}^{k-1}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}.

This implies that
\{\tilde{\varphi}^{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\infty}_{\eta}
that has a limit
\tilde{\varphi}.
Taking the limit
k\rightarrow\infty
in (
2.5
), we have that
\tilde{\varphi}
is a solution to (
2.4
) and
\tilde{\varphi}
satisfies

\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\lesssim\alpha^{-1}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\lesssim\alpha^{-1}\|{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}+|\varphi_{b}|.

Note that

\partial_{Y}\tilde{\varphi}(Y)=-\xi\tilde{\varphi}(Y)+\int_{Y}^{\infty}e^{\xi(Y-Y^{\prime})}\left(\tilde{f}+i\alpha(1-U_{s})\tilde{\varphi}\right)(Y^{\prime}){\rm d}Y^{\prime},

which implies

\|\partial_{Y}\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\lesssim\left(|\xi|+\alpha\right)\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}+\frac{1}{\text{Re}\xi+\eta}\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\lesssim\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}+\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}|\varphi_{b}|.

By using the equation (
2.4
), we have

\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\lesssim\alpha\|\tilde{\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}+\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}_{\eta}}+\alpha|\varphi_{b}|.

We therefore conclude the estimate (
2.2
) by noting (
2.3
).
Moreover, for
|c|\leq\gamma_{0}
with
\gamma_{0}
being sufficiently small,
\text{arg}(i\alpha+\alpha^{2}-i\alpha c)\sim\frac{\pi}{2}
, which implies that
\xi
is holomorphic in
c
. Then for any loop
\Gamma
inside
D_{\gamma_{0}}
, by using the analyticity of
f
and
\varphi_{b}
we can show from (
2.5
) inductively that
\int_{\Gamma}\tilde{\varphi}^{k}(Y;c){\rm d}c=0
. Since the convergence is uniform, we conclude that
\int_{\Gamma}\tilde{\varphi}(Y;c){\rm d}c=0
. Therefore, by Morera’s Theorem we know that
\tilde{{\varphi}}(Y;c)
is analytic and so is
{{\varphi}}(Y;c)
. The proof of Proposition
2.1
is then completed.
∎

Next we will give some
L^{2}
-type estimates on the solution of (
2.1
) with homogeneous boundary data
\varphi_{b}=0
for use in Section 4.

Proposition 2.2
.

There exist positive constants
\alpha_{1}\in(0,1)
and
\gamma_{1}\in(0,1)
, such that if
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{1})
and
c
lies in the disk
D_{\gamma_{1}}=\{c\in\mathbb{C}\mid|c|<\gamma_{1}\}
,
then for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, equation (
2.1
) with
\varphi_{b}=0
has a unique solution
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
\varphi
satisfies

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,~\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.8)

Moreover,
\varphi(\cdot~;c)
is analytic in
c
with value in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Proof.

The solvability of elliptic problem (
2.1
) can be obtained via the similar idea in the proof of Proposition
2.1
. We only show the a priori estimates. Taking the
L^{2}
-inner product on both sides of (
2.1
) with
\varphi
, we obtain by integration by parts that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,~\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+i\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}(U_{s}-c)|\varphi|^{2}{\rm d}Y=\langle f,\varphi\rangle.

(2.9)

We take the real and imaginary part of (
2.9
) respectively to get

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\text{Im}c\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\left(\langle f,\varphi\rangle\right)\leq|\langle f,\varphi\rangle|,

(2.10)

and

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}(U_{s}-\text{Re}c)|\varphi|^{2}{\rm d}Y=\text{Im}\left(\langle f,\varphi\rangle\right)\leq|\langle f,\varphi\rangle|.

(2.11)

Let
\gamma_{1}\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
. Then for any
|c|<\gamma_{1}
, by using the fact
\|Y^{2}(1-U_{s})\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
, the left-hand side of (
2.11
) yields

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}(U_{s}-\text{Re}c)|\varphi|^{2}{\rm d}Y&=\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-\text{Re}c)|\varphi|^{2}{\rm d}Y+\alpha\int_{0}^{\infty}(U_{s}-1)|\varphi|^{2}{\rm d}Y\\
&\geq\alpha(1-\text{Re}c)\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\alpha\|Y^{2}(1-U_{s})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|Y^{-1}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\geq\alpha(1-\text{Re}c)\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-C\alpha\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{aligned}

(2.12)

where we have used the Hardy inequality in the last inequality.
We plug (
2.12
) into (
2.11
), and combine it with (
2.10
) to obtain

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,~\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha(1-|c|)\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\alpha\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\langle f,\varphi\rangle|\lesssim\alpha\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.

Then by the smallness of
\alpha_{1}
, this implies

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,~\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\alpha^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},~\text{for any }\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{1}).

Thus we obtain (
2.7
). Then, by (
2.1
) and (
2.7
) it is straightforward to obtain

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\|(U_{s}-c)\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{2}}

that is (
2.8
). Finally, we will show the analyticity. Define an operator

\displaystyle T:H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\mapsto L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})

\displaystyle T(\varphi)=\frac{i}{\alpha}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi+U_{s}\varphi.

We have shown that
D_{\gamma_{1}}
belongs to the resolvent set of
T
. From (
2.7
) and (
2.8
), we obtain that

\|(-c+T)^{-1}f\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\alpha^{-1}\|i\alpha f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, for any
c_{0}\in D_{\gamma_{1}}
and any
|c-c_{0}|<\min\{\frac{1}{2C},\frac{1}{2}d(c_{0},\partial D_{\gamma_{1}})\}
, we can write

\varphi(c)=(-c+T)^{-1}f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(c-c_{0})^{n}(-c_{0}+T)^{-n-1}f,

which is absolutely convergent in
H^{2}
. Therefore,
\varphi
is analytic in
c
and the proof of Proposition
2.1
is completed.

3.
Approximation of unstable modes

3.1.
Slow mode

In this subsection, we will construct the slow mode
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})
for (
1.18
) that captures the inviscid motion in
the fluid. This mode can be approximated by a solution to the Rayleigh equation:

\displaystyle\text{Ray}_{\alpha}(\psi)\triangleq(U_{s}-\hat{c})(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\psi-\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\psi=0,~Y>0,~\hat{c}=c+\frac{i}{n}.

(3.1)

The solution to (
3.1
) has been well studied, for example in
[
10
]
for small
\alpha
. For completeness,
we list some key points in the construction and one can refer to
[
10
]
for more details.
For
\alpha=0
and Im
c>0
, (
3.1
) has two independent solutions

\displaystyle\psi_{0,1}(Y;c)=U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c},~\psi_{0,2}(Y;c)=(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{2}}{\rm d}X.

(3.2)

For
\alpha>0
but small, the corresponding approximate solutions to (
3.1
) can be given by

\displaystyle\psi_{\alpha,j}(Y)=e^{-\alpha Y}\psi_{0,j}(Y),~j=1,2.

It is straightforward to check that these two solutions satisfy

\displaystyle\text{Ray}_{\alpha}(\psi_{\alpha,j})=-2\alpha(U_{s}-\hat{c})\partial_{Y}\psi_{0,j}e^{-\alpha Y},~j=1,2.

To obtain a better approximation, as
[
10
]
, one can define the approximate Green’s kernel by

G_{\alpha}(X,Y)\triangleq-(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{-1}\left\{\begin{aligned} &e^{-\alpha(Y-X)}\psi_{0,1}(Y)\psi_{0,2}(X),~Y>X,\\
&e^{-\alpha(Y-X)}\psi_{0,1}(X)\psi_{0,2}(Y),~Y<X,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

and introduce the corrector

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}^{s}(Y;{c})\triangleq 2\int_{0}^{\infty}G_{\alpha}(X,Y)(U_{s}-\hat{c})\partial_{Y}\psi_{0,1}(X)e^{-\alpha X}{\rm d}X

(3.4)

to absorb
O(\alpha)
error generated by
\psi_{\alpha,1}
. Thus, the slow mode
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})=\psi_{\alpha,1}(Y;{c})+\alpha\Phi_{1}^{s}(Y;{c}).

(3.5)

A straightforward calculation yields that

\displaystyle\text{Ray}_{\alpha}(\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s})=

\displaystyle-2\alpha^{2}(U_{s}-\hat{c})(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{1}^{s}+\alpha\Phi_{1}^{s})

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\alpha^{2}\psi_{\alpha,1}\partial_{Y}\psi_{0,1}\int_{0}^{Y}U_{s}^{\prime}(X)\psi_{0,2}(X){\rm d}X+4\alpha^{2}\psi_{\alpha,1}\partial_{Y}\psi_{0,2}\int_{Y}^{\infty}U_{s}^{\prime}(X)\psi_{0,1}(X){\rm d}X.

From (
3.6
),
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}
formally solves (
3.1
) up to
O(\alpha^{2})
. In the following lemma, we state some properties and estimates on the boundary data of the slow mode
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}
which will be used later.

Lemma 3.1
.

1. For each
Y\geq 0
,
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})
is holomorphic in the upper-half complex plane
\{c\in\mathbb{C}\mid\text{Im}c>0\}
.

2. At
Y=0
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})

\displaystyle=-\hat{c}-\alpha\psi_{0,2}(0)(1-2\hat{c}),

(3.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})

\displaystyle=1+\alpha\hat{c}+\alpha(1-2\hat{c})(\alpha\psi_{0,2}(0)-\partial_{Y}\psi_{0,2}(0)).

(3.8)

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
\gamma_{2}\in(0,\tilde{\gamma})
where
\tilde{\gamma}
is given in Lemma
6.2
, such that if
c\in\{\text{Im}c>0,~|\hat{c}|\leq\gamma_{2}\}
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})

\displaystyle=-\hat{c}+\alpha+O(1)\alpha|\hat{c}\log\text{Im}\hat{c}|,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})

\displaystyle=1+O(1)\alpha|\log\text{Im}\hat{c}|.

(3.10)

Proof.

Note that
\hat{c}=c+\frac{i}{n}
. For
c
lies in the upper-half plane, the denominators in the formula (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) do not vanish. This gives the analyticity of
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})
. To show (
3.7
), we compute

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}^{s}(Y;{c})=-2\psi_{0,1}(Y;{c})e^{-\alpha Y}\int_{0}^{Y}U_{s}^{\prime}(X)\psi_{0,2}(X){\rm d}X-2\psi_{0,2}(Y;{c})e^{-\alpha Y}\int_{Y}^{\infty}U_{s}^{\prime}(X)\psi_{0,1}(X){\rm d}X.

(3.11)

Taking (
3.11
) at
Y=0
gives

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}^{s}(0;{c})=-2\psi_{0,2}(0)\int_{0}^{\infty}(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})U_{s}^{\prime}(X){\rm d}X=-\psi_{0,2}(0)(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{2}\big|_{X=0}^{X=\infty}=-\psi_{0,2}(0)(1-2\hat{c}).

Thus we have
\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)=\psi_{\alpha,1}(0;c)+\alpha\Phi_{1}^{s}(0;c)=-\hat{c}-\alpha\psi_{0,2}(0)(1-2\hat{c})
, which is (
3.7
). Moreover, for
Y\in[0,1]
,
U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)
does not vanishes. Thus integration by parts yields that

\displaystyle\psi_{0,2}(0)

\displaystyle=\hat{c}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{2}}{\rm d}X=\hat{c}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}Y^{\prime}}\left(\frac{-1}{U_{s}(X)-\hat{c}}\right)\frac{1}{U_{s}^{\prime}(X)}{\rm d}X

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{U_{s}^{\prime}(0)}-\frac{\hat{c}}{U_{s}^{\prime}(1)(U_{s}(1)-\hat{c})}-\hat{c}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{U_{s}(X)-\hat{c}}\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(X)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(X))^{2}}{\rm d}X.

Since
U_{s}^{\prime}(0)=1
, the first term is equal to
-1
. For
|\hat{c}|\leq\frac{U_{s}(1)}{2}
, we have
|U_{s}(1)-\hat{c}|\geq\frac{U_{s}(1)}{2}.
So the second term satisfies

\left|\frac{\hat{c}}{U_{s}^{\prime}(1)(U_{s}(1)-\hat{c})}\right|\leq\frac{2|\hat{c}|}{U_{s}^{\prime}(1)U_{s}(1)}\lesssim|\hat{c}|.

For the last term, by using (
6.5
), we obtain for
|\hat{c}|<\tilde{\gamma}
that

\left|\hat{c}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{U_{s}(X)-\hat{c}}\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(X)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(X))^{2}}{\rm d}X\right|\lesssim|\hat{c}|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{|U_{s}(x)-\hat{c}|}{\rm d}X\lesssim|\hat{c}\log\text{Im}\hat{c}|.

Let
\gamma_{2}=\min\{\frac{U_{s}(1)}{2},\tilde{\gamma}\}
. Then for any
c\in\{\text{Im}{c}>0,~|\hat{c}|\leq\gamma_{2}\}
, we have

\psi_{0,2}(0)=-1+O(1)|\hat{c}\log\text{Im}\hat{c}|.

Substituting it into (
3.7
), we obtain (
3.9
). By using the explicit formula (
6.7
) and integral estimate in Lemma
6.2
, the estimates (
3.8
) and (
3.10
) can be obtained similarly. We omit the detail for brevity. The proof of Lemma
3.1
is completed.
∎

3.2.
Fast mode

To capture the viscous effect and the no-slip boundary data, one needs to investigate the fast mode in
the sub-layer with respect to the Prandtl layer scale.
For this, set
z=\delta^{-1}Y
as the boundary sub-layer variable, where
\delta=e^{-\frac{1}{6}\pi i}n^{-\frac{1}{3}}
. Then we
look for an approximate solution to (
1.18
) in the form of

\displaystyle(\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f},\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f})(Y)=(\phi^{f},\delta\varphi^{f})(\delta^{-1}Y).

(3.12)

Plugging this ansatz into the Orr-Sommerfeld equations (
1.18
) and taking the leading order, we obtain

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{z}^{4}\phi^{f}-(z+z_{0})\partial_{z}^{2}\phi^{f}=0,\\
&-\partial_{z}^{2}\varphi^{f}-\partial_{z}\phi^{f}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

for
z=e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}n^{\frac{1}{3}}Y\in e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Here we have used

U(Y)-\hat{c}\sim Y-\hat{c}=\delta(z+z_{0}),~\text{ where }z_{0}\triangleq-\delta^{-1}\hat{c}=e^{-\frac{5}{6}\pi i}n^{\frac{1}{3}}\hat{c}.

Therefore, we can expect that the profile
(\phi^{f},\varphi^{f})
satisfies:

\partial_{z}^{2}\phi^{f}(z)\sim Ai(z+z_{0}),~\partial_{z}^{2}\varphi^{f}(z)\sim-\partial_{z}\phi^{f}(z),

where
Ai(z)
is the classical Airy function defined in (
6.1
). Inspired by the above formal argument, we define

\displaystyle\phi^{f}(z)\triangleq\frac{Ai(2,z+z_{0})}{Ai(2,z_{0})},~\varphi^{f}(z)\triangleq-\frac{Ai(3,z+z_{0})}{Ai(2,z_{0})},

(3.13)

where
Ai(2,z)
and
Ai(3,z)
given in (
6.2
) are the second and third order primitive functions of classical Airy function respectively. By a straightforward computation, we know that the fast mode
(\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f},\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{i}{n}\partial_{Y}^{4}\Phi^{f}_{\text{app}}+(Y-\hat{c})\partial_{Y}^{2}\Phi^{f}_{\text{app}}=0,\\
&-\partial_{Y}^{2}\Psi^{f}_{\text{app}}-\partial_{Y}\Phi^{f}_{\text{app}}=0,\quad Y>0.\end{aligned}\right.

In addition, at
Y=0
it holds that

\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}(Y;{c})|_{Y=0}\equiv 1.

3.3.
Approximate eigenvalue

We are now ready to construct the approximate growing mode that represents the Tollmien-Schlichting wave in the
boundary layer profile. Set

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{app}}(Y;{c})\triangleq\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})-\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}(Y;{c}),

\displaystyle\Psi_{\text{app}}(Y;{c})\triangleq\Psi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})-\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f}(Y;{c}).

(3.14)

Here
\Psi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})
is the solution to (
2.1
) with

\varphi_{b}=\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f}(0;{c})\text{ and }f=i\alpha H_{s}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})+\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c}).

From the construction, the approximate solution satisfies the zero Dirichlet boundary conditions on the stream functions:

\Phi_{\text{app}}(0;{c})=\Psi_{\text{app}}(0;{c})=0.

To recover the no-slip boundary condition, we define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}({c})\triangleq\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}(0;{c})=\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;c)-\delta^{-1}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})\frac{Ai(1,z_{0}({c}))}{Ai(2,z_{0}({c}))}.

(3.15)

Then
(\Phi_{\text{app}},\Psi_{\text{app}})(Y;c)
satisfies the boundary conditions in (
1.19
) if and only if
\Gamma_{0}({c})=0
.
Let

\displaystyle c_{*}\triangleq(A+A^{-1}e^{\frac{1}{4}\pi i})\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}.

(3.16)

The following proposition gives the existence of approximate eigenvalue near
c_{*}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
be a fixed number. There is a positive constant
A_{0}>1
such that if
A\geq A_{0}
, then there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
, such that for any
\alpha=A\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
with
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
, the function
\Gamma_{0}({c})
defined in (
3.15
) admits a unique zero point
{c}_{\text{app}}
in the disk
D_{*}=\{{c}\in\mathbb{C}\mid|\hat{c}-c_{*}|\leq A^{-1-\theta}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}\}
.
Moreover, on the circle
\partial D_{*},
it holds that

\displaystyle|\Gamma_{0}(c)|\geq\frac{1}{2}A^{-\theta}.

(3.17)

Proof.

For convenience, we zoom in the disk
D_{*}
by introducing
\hat{c}=\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}h
,
h_{*}=A+A^{-1}e^{\frac{1}{4}\pi i}
and
\hat{\Gamma}_{0}(h)\triangleq\Gamma_{0}(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}h-\frac{i}{n})
.
It suffices to study the zeros of
\hat{\Gamma}_{0}(h)
in the disk
D_{h}=\{h\in\mathbb{C}\mid|h-h_{*}|\leq A^{-1-\theta}\}.
Recall that

z_{0}=-\delta^{-1}\hat{c}=e^{-\frac{5}{6}\pi i}A^{\frac{1}{3}}h.

Then by taking
A_{0}\geq 1
sufficiently large, it is straightforward to check that for
A\geq A_{0}
,

\displaystyle|z_{0}|=A^{\frac{4}{3}}\left(1+O(1)A^{-2}\right),~h\in D_{h},

(3.18)

and there exists a positive constant
\tau_{0}>0
, such that

\displaystyle-\frac{5}{6}\pi<\arg z_{0}\leq-\frac{5}{6}\pi+\tau_{0}A^{-2}.

(3.19)

Therefore, from the asymptotic behavior (
6.3
) of Airy profiles we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{Ai(1,z_{0})}{Ai(2,z_{0})}=-z_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}+O(1)|z_{0}|^{-1}=-A^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{-\frac{5\pi i}{12}}+O(1)A^{-\frac{4}{3}}.

(3.20)

Thus by taking the leading order in (
3.15
) and using the asymptotic behavior (
3.9
) and (
3.10
) in Lemma
3.1
for small
\hat{c}
, we have

\hat{\Gamma}_{0}(h)\sim 1+\delta^{-1}(\alpha-\hat{c})A^{\frac{2}{3}}e^{-\frac{5\pi i}{12}}=1+e^{-\frac{1}{4}\pi i}A(A-h),~\text{for }h\in D_{h}.

Based on this, we define a reference mapping:

\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)=1+e^{-\frac{1}{4}\pi i}A(A-h).

On one hand, we know that
\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)
has a unique zero point
h=h_{*}
in the whole complex plane. Moreover, on the circle
\{h\in\mathbb{C}\mid|h-h_{*}|=A^{-1-\theta}\}
it holds that

|\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)|=A^{-\theta}.

On the other hand, we can estimate the difference between
\hat{\Gamma}_{0}
and
\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}
as follows.

\displaystyle\left|\hat{\Gamma}_{0}(h)-\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\left|1-e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}A^{\frac{1}{3}}(A-h)\frac{Ai(1,z_{0})}{Ai(2,z_{0})}-\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)\right|+C_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}|\log\varepsilon|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|1+e^{\frac{1}{6}\pi i}A^{\frac{1}{3}}(A-h)z_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}-\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)\right|+CA^{-2}+C_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}|\log\varepsilon|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}A^{-2}+C_{1,A}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}|\log\varepsilon|.

Here the positive constant
C_{1}
does not depend on either
\varepsilon
or
A
and
C_{1,A}
may depend on
A
but not on
\varepsilon
. Since
\theta\in(0,1)
, taking
A_{0}
suitably large and then taking
\varepsilon_{1}
sufficiently small such that

C_{1}A^{-2}+C_{1,A}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}|\log\varepsilon|\leq\frac{1}{2}A^{-\theta}

for
A\geq A_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
, we obtain that
\left|\hat{\Gamma}_{0}(h)-\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)\right|<|\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)|
on the circle
\{h\in\mathbb{C}\mid|h-h_{0}|=A^{-1-\theta}\}
. In particular, we have

|\Gamma_{0}(c)|=|\hat{\Gamma}_{0}(h)|\geq|\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)|-|\hat{\Gamma}_{0}(h)-\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}(h)|\geq\frac{1}{2}A^{-\theta},

that is (
3.17
). Moreover, note that
Ai(k,z),k=0,1,2
are entire functions and
Ai(2,z_{0})\neq 0
due to (
3.20
). Both
\hat{\Gamma}_{0}
and
\hat{\Gamma}_{\text{ref}}
are holomorphic in the disk
D_{h}
. Therefore, Proposition
3.2
is concluded by Rouché’s Theorem.
∎

3.4.
Error estimates

In this subsection, we will give the precise representation of the error terms generated by the approximate growing mode
(\Phi_{\text{app}},\Psi_{\text{app}})(Y;{c})
defined in (
3.3
) and then
provide some estimates.
By a straightforward computation,
(\Phi_{\text{app}},\Psi_{\text{app}})(Y;{c})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\text{OS}_{c}(\Phi_{\text{app}},\Psi_{\text{app}})=(E^{s}(Y;c)+E^{f}(Y;c),F^{f}(Y;c)),~Y>0,\\
&\Phi_{\text{app}}(Y;c)|_{Y=0}=\Psi_{\text{app}}(Y;c)|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(3.21)

Moreover, from Proposition
3.2
we know that there exists a unique
c_{\text{app}}\in D_{*}
, such that
\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}(0;c)=0.
In what follows we specify the error terms on the right hand side of (
3.21
). First of all, let us focus on the error term
E^{s}(Y;{c})
generated by slow mode
(\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s},\Psi_{\text{app}}^{s})(Y;{c})
. For latter use, we divide
E^{s}(Y;{c})
into following three groups: the first two groups consist of terms that can be written either in derivatives of
Y
or
x
, and the remaining one consists of terms with strong decay in
Y.
That is,

\displaystyle E^{s}(Y;{c})=\partial_{Y}E^{s}_{1}(Y;{c})+i\alpha E^{s}_{2}(Y;{c})+E^{s}_{3}(Y;{c}),

(3.22)

where

\displaystyle E_{1}^{s}(Y;{c})\triangleq

\displaystyle\frac{i}{n}\left(\partial_{Y}^{3}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})-2\alpha^{2}\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})\right)-\sqrt{\varepsilon}H_{s}\partial_{Y}\Psi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha}{n}\left((U_{s}-{c})\Psi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})-H_{s}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})\right),

\displaystyle E_{2}^{s}(Y;{c})\triangleq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{3}}{n}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})-i\alpha\sqrt{\varepsilon}H_{s}\Psi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})-\frac{\alpha}{n}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c}),

\displaystyle E_{3}^{s}(Y;{c})\triangleq

\displaystyle-2\alpha^{2}(U_{s}-\hat{c})(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{1}^{s}(Y;{c})+\alpha\Phi_{1}^{s}(Y;{c}))+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}H_{s}\partial_{Y}\Psi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c})+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}^{2}H_{s}\Psi_{\text{app}}^{s}(Y;{c}).

The error terms
(E^{f}(Y;c),F^{f}(Y;c))
generated by the approximate fast mode are also written in the similar form:

E^{f}(Y;{c})=\partial_{Y}E_{1}^{f}(Y;{c})+i\alpha E_{2}^{f}(Y;c)+E_{3}^{f}(Y;{c}),

(3.23)

where

\displaystyle E^{f}_{1}(Y;{c})\triangleq

\displaystyle-\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})\left(\frac{-2\alpha^{2}i}{n}\partial_{Y}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}-\sqrt{\varepsilon}H_{s}\partial_{Y}\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f}-\frac{\alpha}{n}(U_{s}-{c})\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f}+\frac{\alpha}{n}H_{s}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}\right),

\displaystyle E^{f}_{2}(Y;{c})\triangleq

\displaystyle-\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})\left(\frac{\alpha^{3}}{n}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}+i\alpha(U_{s}-\hat{c})\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}-\frac{\alpha}{n}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}-i\alpha\sqrt{\varepsilon}H_{s}\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f}\right),

\displaystyle E^{f}_{3}(Y;{c})\triangleq

\displaystyle-\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})\left((U_{s}(Y)-U_{s}^{\prime}(0)Y)\partial_{Y}^{2}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}-\partial_{Y}^{2}U_{s}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}H_{s}\partial_{Y}\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f}+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\partial_{Y}^{2}H_{s}\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f}\right),

and

F^{f}(Y;{c})\triangleq-\Phi_{\text{app}}^{s}(0;{c})\left(\alpha^{2}\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f}+i\alpha(U_{s}-{c})\Psi_{\text{app}}^{f}-i\alpha H_{s}\Phi_{\text{app}}^{f}\right).

(3.24)

The main purpose in this subsection is to establish the following two Propositions for some estimates on these error terms.
Recall that
A_{0}>1
and
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0,1)
are the numbers given in Proposition
3.2
in which the disk
D_{*}
has been defined.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
A\geq A_{0}
be a fixed number. There exists
\varepsilon_{2}\in(0,\varepsilon_{1}),
such that for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{2}),

\alpha=A\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{8}}
and
{c}\in D_{*}
, we have

\displaystyle\|E_{1}^{s}(\cdot~;{c})\|_{L^{2}}+\|E_{2}^{s}(\cdot~;{c})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{16}},

(3.25)

\displaystyle\||U_{s}^{\prime\prime}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}E_{3}^{s}(\cdot~;{c})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{16}}.

(3.26)

Proposition 3.4
.

Under the same assumption as in Proposition
3.3
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|E_{1}^{f}(Y;{c})\|_{L^{2}}+\|E_{2}^{f}(Y;{c})\|_{L^{2}}+\|F^{f}(Y;{c})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{5}{16}},

(3.27)

\displaystyle\||U_{s}^{\prime\prime}|^{-\frac{1}{2}}E_{3}^{f}(Y;{c})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{A}\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{16}}.

(3.28)

To prove Proposition
3.3
and
3.4
, we need some preparation.
First, we prove the following lemma about some pointwise estimate on
\psi_{0,2}
defined in (
3.2
) which will be frequently used in the error estimates.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
{c}\in\{\text{Im}c>0,~|\hat{c}|\leq\gamma_{2}\}
where
\gamma_{2}\in(0,1)
is the number given in Lemma
3.1
, the following pointwise estimates on
\psi_{0,2}
hold.

(1)

For
Y\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle

\displaystyle|\psi_{0,2}(Y)|\lesssim(1+Y),~|\partial_{Y}\psi_{0,2}(Y)|\lesssim 1,

(3.29)

\displaystyle|\partial_{Y}^{2}\psi_{0,2}(Y)|+|\partial_{Y}^{3}\psi_{0,2}(Y)|\lesssim U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)(1+Y).

(2)

For
Y\in[0,1]
, we have

\displaystyle

\displaystyle|\psi_{0,2}(Y)|\lesssim 1,~\partial_{Y}\psi_{0,2}(Y)=-\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(Y)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(Y))^{2}}\log(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})+O(1),

(3.30)

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}^{2}\psi_{0,2}(Y)=-\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(Y)}{U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})}-\frac{(U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(Y))^{2}}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(Y))^{3}}\log(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})+O(1),

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}^{3}\psi_{0,2}(Y)=\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(Y)}{(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})^{2}}-\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}(Y)}{U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})}-\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(Y)U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}(Y)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(Y))^{3}}\log(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})+O(1).

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\psi_{0,2}(Y)

\displaystyle=U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{2}}{\rm d}X+\frac{1}{U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c}},

(3.31)

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}^{2}\psi_{0,2}(Y)

\displaystyle=U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(Y)\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{2}}{\rm d}X,

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}^{3}\psi_{0,2}(Y)

\displaystyle=U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}(Y)\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{2}}{\rm d}X+\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(Y)}{(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})^{2}}.

For
Y\geq 1,
we have
U_{s}(Y)\geq U_{s}(1)>0
. Thus, for
c\in\{\text{Im}c>0,~|\hat{c}|\leq\gamma_{2}\}
, we have

|\hat{c}|\leq\frac{U_{s}(1)}{2}
, which further implies that

\frac{1}{|U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c}|}\lesssim 1,~\text{for }Y\geq 1.

From (
3.31
) and using the structural condition of boundary layer
|\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}{U_{s}^{\prime}}|+|\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{U_{s}^{\prime}}|\lesssim 1,
we can directly obtain (
3.29
). For
Y\in[0,1]
, it suffices to consider the integration
\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{2}}{\rm d}X.
Since
U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)>0
, we can use integration by parts to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{2}}{\rm d}X

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}X}\left(\frac{-1}{U_{s}(X)-\hat{c}}\right)\frac{1}{U_{s}^{\prime}(X)}{\rm d}X

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})}+\frac{1}{U_{s}^{\prime}(1)(U_{s}(1)-\hat{c})}-\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{U_{s}(X)-\hat{c}}\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(X)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(X))^{2}}{\rm d}X.

For the second term, it is straightforward to have

\left|\frac{1}{U_{s}^{\prime}(1)(U_{s}(1)-\hat{c})}\right|\lesssim 1,~\text{for }c\in\{\text{Im}c>0,~|\hat{c}|\leq\gamma_{2}\}.

For the last term, we use integration by parts again to get

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{U_{s}(X)-\hat{c}}\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(X)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(X))^{2}}{\rm d}X=\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{{\rm d}\left(\log(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})\right)}{{\rm d}X}\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(X)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(X))^{3}}{\rm d}X

\displaystyle=\left(\log(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})\right)\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(Y)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(Y))^{3}}-\left(\log(U_{s}(1)-\hat{c})\right)\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(1)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(1))^{3}}-\int_{1}^{Y}\left(\log(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})\right)\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}X}\left(\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(X)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(X))^{3}}\right){\rm d}X.

By using (
6.4
) with
\kappa=1
and the fact that
U_{s}^{\prime}\sim 1
and
|U_{s}^{\prime\prime}|,|U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}|\lesssim 1
for
Y\in[0,1]
, we have

\left|\int_{1}^{Y}\left(\log(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})\right)\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}X}\left(\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(X)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(X))^{3}}\right){\rm d}X\right|\lesssim\int_{0}^{1}|\log(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})|{\rm d}X\lesssim 1,

and

\left|\left(\log(U_{s}(1)-\hat{c})\right)\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(1)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(1))^{3}}\right|\lesssim|\log U_{s}(1)|+1\lesssim 1.

Therefore, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{Y}\frac{1}{(U_{s}(X)-\hat{c})^{2}}{\rm d}X=-\frac{1}{U_{s}^{\prime}(Y)(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})}-\left(\log(U_{s}(Y)-\hat{c})\right)\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}(Y)}{(U_{s}^{\prime}(Y))^{3}}+R(Y;{c}),

(3.32)

where
|R(Y;{c})|\lesssim 1
uniformly for
Y\in[0,1]
and
{c}\in\{\text{Im}c>0,~|\hat{c}|\leq\gamma_{2}\}
. With (
3.32
), we can obtain (
3.30
) directly from (
3.31
). The proof of Lemma
3.5
is then completed.
∎

With Lemma
3.5
, we can obtain some estimates on the corrector
\Phi_{1}^{s}(Y;{c})
defined in (
3.4
).

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
c\in

\{\text{Im}c>0,~|\hat{c}|\leq\gamma_{2}\}
, we have

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|\Phi_{1}^{s}(\cdot~;{c})\|_{L^{\infty}_{\alpha}}\lesssim 1,

(3.33)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}\Phi_{1}^{s}(\cdot~;{c})\|_{L^{\infty}_{\alpha}}\lesssim 1+|\log\text{Im}\hat{c}|,~\|\partial_{Y}\Phi_{1}^{s}(\cdot~;{c})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\Phi_{1}^{s}(\cdot~;{c})\|_{L^{\infty}_{\alpha}}\lesssim 1+|\text{Im}\hat{c}|^{-1},~\|\partial_{Y}^{2}\Phi_{1}^{s}(\cdot~;\hat{c})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1+|\text{Im}\hat{c}|^{-\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}^{3}\Phi_{1}^{s}(\cdot~;{c})\|_{L^{\infty}_{\alpha}}\lesssim 1+|\text{Im}\hat{c}|^{-2},~\|\partial_{Y}^{3}\Phi_{1}^{s}(\cdot~;{c})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1+|\text{Im}\hat{c}|^{-\frac{3}{2}},

Remark 4.4

Proof of Lemma
4.3
:
We first show the a priori estimates. Denote
\omega=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi.

Note that
U_{s}^{\prime\prime}<0
from the strong concave condition in (

4.5
) and

, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\phi,~\alpha\phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(U_{s}-\hat{c})}{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}|\omega|^{2}{\rm d}Y+\langle\frac{i}{n}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\omega,\frac{\omega}{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle=\langle q_{1},\frac{\omega}{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle.

(4.9)

We use integration by parts and the boundary condition

\langle\frac{i}{n}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\omega,\frac{\omega}{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle=\frac{i}{n}\left\|(\partial_{Y}\omega,~\alpha\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\frac{i}{n}\langle\partial_{Y}\omega,~\frac{\omega U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{(U_{s}^{\prime\prime})^{2}}\rangle.

Then the imaginary and real part of (

) give respectively

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left\|(\partial_{Y}\omega,~\alpha\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\text{Im}\hat{c}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\text{Im}\left(\langle q_{1},\frac{\omega}{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\right)+\text{Im}\left(-\frac{i}{n}\langle\partial_{Y}\omega,~\frac{\omega U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{(U_{s}^{\prime\prime})^{2}}\rangle\right),

(4.10)

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\phi,~\alpha\phi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{U_{s}-\text{Re}\hat{c}}{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}|\omega|^{2}{\rm d}Y

\displaystyle=\text{Re}\left(\langle q_{1},\frac{\omega}{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\right)+\text{Re}\left(-\frac{i}{n}\langle\partial_{Y}\omega,~\frac{\omega U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{(U_{s}^{\prime\prime})^{2}}\rangle\right).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left|\langle\partial_{Y}\omega,~\frac{\omega U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{(U_{s}^{\prime\prime})^{2}}\rangle\right|\leq\frac{1}{n}\left\|\frac{U_{s}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{U_{s}^{\prime\prime}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{Y}\omega\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\lesssim\frac{1}{n}\|\partial_{Y}\omega\|_{L^{2}_{w}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{w}}.

Then from (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left\|(\partial_{Y}\omega,~\alpha\omega)\right\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}+\text{Im}\hat{c}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{w}}^{2}\lesssim

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
be an open subset and
J,K
be two Banach spaces. Denote by
B(J,K)
the space of bounded linear operators from
J
to
K
. Assume that

L(\cdot~;c):\Omega\mapsto B(J,K)

is analytic in
c
with values in
B(J,K)
and

f(c):\Omega\mapsto J

is analytic in
c
with values in
J
. Then
L(f;c)
is analytic in
c
with values in
K
.

Proof.

Let
c_{0}\in\Omega
be a fixed number. Since
L(\cdot~;c)
is analytic at
c_{0}
, there exists
r_{1}>0
and a sequence of bounded linear operator
\{L_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset B(J,K)
, such that

L(\cdot~;c)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}(c-c_{0})^{n}L_{n}(\cdot),

with

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|c-c_{0}|^{n}\|L_{n}\|_{B(J,K)}<\infty~\text{ for }|c-c_{0}|<r_{1}.

Also by analyticity of
f
we can find
r_{2}>0
and a sequence of elements
f_{n}\in J
such that

f(c)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}(c-c_{0})^{n}f_{n},

with

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|c-c_{0}|^{n}\|f_{n}\|_{J}<\infty~\text{ for }|c-c_{0}|<r_{2}.

By linearity, for
|c-c_{0}|<\min\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
we deduce that

\displaystyle L\left(f(c);c\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}(c-c_{0})^{n}L_{n}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}(c-c_{0})^{m}f_{m}\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}(c-c_{0})^{n}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}L_{m}f_{n-m}\right).

(6.8)

For
|c-c_{0}|<\min\{r_{1},r_{2}\}
, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|c-c_{0}|^{n}\left\|\sum_{m=0}^{n}L_{m}f_{n-m}\right\|_{K}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|c-c_{0}|^{n}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n}\|L_{m}\|_{B(J,K)}\|f_{n-m}\|_{J}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|c-c_{0}|^{n}\|L_{n}\|_{B(J,K)}\right)\times\left(\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}|c-c_{0}|^{n}\|f_{n}\|_{J}\right)<\infty,

which implies that the series on the right hand side of (
6.8
) absolutely converges in
K
. Therefore,
L(f;c)
is analytic and the proof of Lemma
6.3
is completed.
∎

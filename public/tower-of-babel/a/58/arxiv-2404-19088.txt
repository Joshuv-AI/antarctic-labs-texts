New look at Milnor Spheres
Leonardo F. Cavenaghi, Lino Grama, Ludmil Katzarkov

Abstract
. This paper investigates the nature of exotic spheres from two novel perspectives, addressing the fundamental question: “What makes an exotic sphere exotic?” First, we explore spherical T-duality in the context of S 3 \mathrm{S}^{3} -bundles over S 4 \mathrm{S}^{4} . We demonstrate that the distinct smooth structures of exotic spheres Σ 7 \Sigma^{7} correspond to spherical T-dual pairs for 16 out of the 28 diffeomorphism classes of smooth structures on seven-dimensional homotopy spheres. Furthermore, we show that spherical T-dual pairs can be encoded in ⋆ \star -diagrams. These diagrams impose equivalences that constrain their S 3 \mathrm{S}^{3} -invariant geometries. Second, we introduce homotopy Hopf manifolds Σ 7 × S 1 \Sigma^{7}\times\mathrm{S}^{1} and examine their complex structures using logarithmic transformations. This analysis links their smooth structures to the algebraic and topological invariants of Fano orbifolds. Additionally, we relate these constructions to the Homological Mirror Symmetry program and propose connections to the theory of topological modular forms, aiming to unify geometric topology, elliptic cohomology, and modular forms.

2.3.
\star
-diagrams and Morita equivalence

A more categorical description of
\star
-diagrams can be recognized through the lens of Lie groupoids. A crucial aspect of this approach is the Morita equivalence of the two
G
-manifolds,
M
and
M^{\prime}
, that serve as the base manifolds in a
\star
-diagram, denoted as
M\leftarrow\mathcal{P}\rightarrow M^{\prime}
. This fact was pointed out by O. Brahic and C. Ortiz in 2015 to L. Sperança, who then communicated it to the authors.

In the following discussion, we will examine two action groupoids, using the notation
X//G
to indicate that
X
is a smooth manifold with a smooth action by a Lie group
G
. Here,
X//G
is understood as the Lie action groupoid. Given the
\star
-diagram
M\leftarrow\mathcal{P}\rightarrow M^{\prime}
with structure group

, we recall that

can be viewed as a
G
-manifold with the induced
\star
-action, while
M^{\prime}
is a
G
-manifold with the restriction of the
\bullet
-action. We will establish a Morita equivalence between the action groupoids

and

Definition 2.12

(Morita equivalence of Lie groupoids. Definition 2.25 in
[
34
]

Two Lie groupoids

and

[
34

– are equivalent in
\mathcal{P}
.

To avoid introducing further details, we note that any
\star
-diagram satisfies Definition 2.1 in
[
34
]
, considering the singular foliation induced by the

-orbits on

and the
\bullet
-orbits on

. It is important to observe that

-diagrams also encapsulate what is known as a Hausdorff–Morita equivalence – see Corollary 2.17 in

]
. This leads us to the following:

Theorem 2.13
.

Any
\star

As stated in Corollary 2.17 in

with commuting actions, the singular foliation on

arising from the induced

and

.
∎

3.
Spherical T-duality and
\star
-diagrams

In the series of works
[
11
,
12
,
15
,
16
]
, it is demonstrated that for each pair
(\mathcal{P},H)
—consisting of a manifold
\mathcal{P}
with a free circle action and an integral 3-cocycle
H
on
\mathcal{P}
—there exists a unique associated T-dual pair
(\widehat{\mathcal{P}},\widehat{H})
. Here,
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
denotes a manifold with a free circle action and a cocycle
\widehat{H}
, such that the space of orbits for both circle actions is identical. Although
\mathcal{P}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
are generally not homeomorphic, it has been proven that T-duality induces various degree-shifting isomorphisms between different structures, such as twisted cohomology and twisted K-theory on
\mathcal{P}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{P}}
. Subsequent works
[
43
,
20
]
established that T-duality also induces isomorphisms on Dirac structures, Courant algebroids, generalized complex structures, and generalized Kähler structures. Later, a higher-dimensional version of T-duality was introduced in
[
14
,
13
]
, focusing on sphere bundles.

In this section, we delve into the work presented in
[
14
]
and its extension in
[
13
]
, offering a comprehensive approach to geometrically constructing spherical T-duality. Our primary goal is to establish topological relationships between spherical T-dual pairs and to realize them geometrically. To this end, we employ
\star
-diagrams as our central framework. Specifically, for
\mathrm{S}^{3}
-bundles over
\mathrm{S}^{4}
, we demonstrate that
\star
-diagrams can realize the manifolds arising in every spherical T-dual pair and vice versa (see Theorems
2.9
and
3.14
). Consequently, many results in
[
14
,
13
]
follow from the fact that
\star
-diagrams establish Morita equivalence (as demonstrated in Theorem
2.13
). Additionally, we observe a potential correspondence between
\mathrm{S}^{1}
-bundles fitting into corresponding
\star
-diagrams and T-duality for principal torus bundles. While we do not prove this correspondence here, we highlight the problem and leave it for formal proof and further exploration. Investigating the applicability of the results in
[
43
,
20
]
within this context appears promising, supported by insights from Theorem
2.11
, which suggests an explicit “transportation” of complex structures.

4.
Higher-Dimensional Logarithmic Transformation

In this section, we propose an extension of the logarithmic transformation into higher dimensions, specifically targeting its definition for homotopy Hopf manifolds.

Following the work of
[

, we present a topological construction of homotopy spheres in any dimension in Section

and revisit the definition of the

similar to the role of the connected sum in the
\theta
-group:

(4.2)

\theta^{7}:=\{\text{7-dimensional smooth manifolds }M\text{ that are homotopy equivalent to }\mathrm{S}^{7}\}/\langle\text{diffeomorphism}\rangle.

In Lemma

[
58
]
) and establish its relation to
\star
-diagrams. We introduce a bilinear map

(4.3)

\pi_{3}(\mathrm{SO}(3))\otimes\pi_{3}(\mathrm{SO}(3))\rightarrow\theta^{7},

which associates the Gromoll–Meyer exotic sphere

with the pair

. Consequently, its image generates
\theta^{7}
. In Theorem

demonstrating that its image generates
\sigma^{8}

Two questions arise: Why do we focus on

while relying on 7-dimensional homotopy spheres? The reason is that
\theta^{7}
can be identified with

, which represents the group of
\mathrm{h}
-cobordism of parallelizable manifolds. This identification is crucial for defining the Milnor pair and is fundamental to our extension into

Why not consider Calabi–Eckmann manifolds
\Sigma^{2n-1}\times\Sigma^{2m-1}

\mathrm{S}^{2n-1}\times\mathrm{S}^{2m-1}
(

). Notably, even for
\mathrm{S}^{2}

in dimension

.

In Section

, we revisit the fundamentals of classical logarithmic transformations for elliptic fibrations, emphasizing their topological aspects while intentionally overlooking their algebraic nature. We recall the concept and role of nuclei in logarithmic transformations. Subsequently, we explore the potential for an analogous concept in homotopy Hopf manifolds and establish their correlation with logarithmic transformations.

4.1.

This section presents a straightening angle description of some classical manifolds obtained from logarithmic transformations. We adhere to the methodology outlined in
[

Let
\pi:X\rightarrow\Sigma

(the upper half of the complex plane) to a

Since

Consequently, we have

\nu F
of

, and further discussed in

Definition 4.1

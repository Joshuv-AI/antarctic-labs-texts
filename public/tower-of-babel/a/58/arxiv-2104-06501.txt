Abstract
. Here is a direct problem for Sidon sets: Given a linear form φ = c 1 ​ x 1 + ⋯ + c h ​ x h \varphi=c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{h}x_{h} , construct and describe sets A A that are Sidon sets for φ \varphi . 1.
Sidon sets for linear forms

A classical Sidon set is a set
A
of positive integers such that every integer has at most one representation
as the sum of two elements of
A
. Equivalently,
A
has the property that if
a_{1},a_{2},a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2}\in A
and

a_{1}+a_{2}=a^{\prime}_{1}+a^{\prime}_{2}
, then
\{a_{1},a_{2}\}=\{a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2}\}
.
In this paper we consider an analogue of Sidon sets for linear forms.

Let
\mathbf{F}
be a field, let
\mathbf{F}^{\times}=\mathbf{F}\setminus\{0\}
,
and let
h
be a positive integer.
We consider linear forms

\varphi=\varphi(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{h})=c_{1}x_{1}+c_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+c_{h}x_{h}

where
c_{i}\in\mathbf{F}^{\times}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
.
The linear form
\varphi
is
monic
if
c_{1}=1
.
A linear form with 2 variables is
binary
.
A linear form with
h
variables is called
h
-ary
.

Let
A
be a nonempty subset
\mathbf{F}
.
For
c,t\in\mathbf{F}
, the
c
-dilate
of
A
is the set

c\ast A=\{ca:a\in A\}

and the
t
-translate
of
A
by
t
is the set

A+t=\{a+t:a\in A\}.

Let

A^{h}=\left\{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}):a_{i}\in A\text{ for all }i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}\right\}

be the set of all
h
-tuples of elements of
A
.
The
\varphi
-image of
A
is the set

\displaystyle\varphi(A)

\displaystyle=\left\{\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h}):(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{c_{1}a_{1}+\cdots+c_{h}a_{h}:(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}\right\}

\displaystyle=c_{1}\ast A+\cdots+c_{h}\ast A.

Thus,
\varphi(A)
is a sum of dilates.
We define
\varphi(\emptyset)=\{0\}
.

A nonempty subset
A
of
\mathbf{F}
is a

Sidon set for the linear form
\varphi
or, simply, a

\varphi
-Sidon set

if it satisfies the following property:
For all
h
-tuples
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}
and
(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})\in A^{h}
,
if

\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=\varphi(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})

then
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})
,
that is,
a_{i}=a^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,h\}
.
Thus,
A
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set if the linear form
\varphi
is one-to-one on
A^{h}
.
If
A
is a finite set with
k
elements, then
A
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set for the
h
-ary linear form

\varphi
if and only if
|\varphi(A)|=k^{h}
.

Of special interest is the case
\mathbf{F}=\mathbf{Q}
with
\varphi
-Sidon sets of integers or positive integers.

A
direct problem
for Sidon sets for linear forms is the following:
Given a linear form
\varphi
, describe the subsets of
\mathbf{F}
that are
\varphi
-Sidon sets.
In this paper we consider an
inverse problem
:
Given a subset
A
of
\mathbf{F}
, describe the linear forms
\varphi
such that

A
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set.

Sidon sets for linear forms were introduced by Nathanson
[
1
,
2
]
.

2.
Monic forms and normalized sets

Associated with the linear form

\varphi=c_{1}x_{1}+c_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+c_{h}x_{h}

is the monic linear form

\psi=x_{1}+c^{\prime}_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+c^{\prime}_{h}x_{h}

where

c^{\prime}_{i}=\frac{c_{i}}{c_{1}}\qquad\text{for $i=2,3,\ldots,h$.}

Let
A
be a nonempty subset of
V
.
For all
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})

\displaystyle=c_{1}a_{1}+c_{2}a_{2}+\cdots+c_{h}a_{h}

\displaystyle=c_{1}\left(a_{1}+\frac{c_{2}}{c_{1}}a_{2}+\cdots+\frac{c_{h}}{c_{1}}a_{h}\right)

\displaystyle=c_{1}\psi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})

and so

\varphi(A)=c_{1}\ast\psi(A).

Because
c_{1}\neq 0
, the set
A
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set if and only if
A
is a
\psi
-Sidon set.
Thus, it suffices to consider only monic linear forms.

A subset
A
of
\mathbf{F}
is
normalized
if
\{0,1\}\subseteq A
.

Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbf{F}
of cardinality
|A|\geq 2
.
Let
a_{0},a_{1}\in A
with
a_{0}\neq a_{1}
. For all
a\in A
, let

a^{\prime}=\frac{a-a_{0}}{a_{1}-a_{0}}

and

A^{\prime}=\left\{\frac{a-a_{0}}{a_{1}-a_{0}}:a\in A\right\}.

We have

a=(a_{1}-a_{0})a^{\prime}+a_{0}

and

A=(a_{1}-a_{0})\ast A^{\prime}+a_{0}.

Note that
a^{\prime}_{0}=0
and
a^{\prime}_{1}=1
, and so

\{0,1\}\subseteq A^{\prime}
, that is,
A^{\prime}
is normalized.
For all
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})\in A^{h}
we have

\displaystyle\varphi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}a_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}\left((a_{i}-a_{0})a^{\prime}_{i}+a_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=(a_{1}-a_{0})\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}a^{\prime}_{i}+a_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}

\displaystyle=(a_{1}-a_{0})\varphi\left(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h}\right)+a_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}.

Thus,

\varphi(A)=(a_{1}-a_{0})\ast\varphi(A^{\prime})+a_{0}\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}.

Because
a_{1}-a_{0}\neq 0
, the set
A
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set if and only if
A^{\prime}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set.
Thus, it suffices to consider only normalized subsets of
\mathbf{F}
.

3.
Binary linear forms

Consider monic binary linear forms
\varphi=x_{1}+cx_{2}
with
c\in\mathbf{F}^{\times}
.
For every nonempty subset
A
of
\mathbf{F}
, let

\mathcal{E}(A)=\left\{c\in\mathbf{F}^{\times}:\text{$A$ is not a $\varphi$-Sidon set for
the form $\varphi=x_{1}+cx_{2}$}\right\}.

We shall prove that
\mathcal{E}(A)
is finite if
A
is finite.
Equivalently, every nonempty finite subset
A
of
\mathbf{F}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set
for all but finitely many monic binary linear forms
\varphi
.
Moreover, we compute
\mathcal{E}(A)
explicitly for sets with at most 4 elements.

Let
A
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbf{F}
.
Define the
difference set

D(A)=A-A=\{a^{\prime}-a:a,a^{\prime}\in A\}

and the
set of difference quotients

D^{\ast}(A)=\left\{\frac{d^{\prime}}{d}:d,d^{\prime}\in D(A)\text{ and }d\neq 0\right\}.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A
be a nonempty subset of the field
\mathbf{F}
. Then

\mathcal{E}(A)\subseteq D^{\ast}(A).

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathbf{F}^{\times}
and
\varphi=x_{1}+cx_{2}
. If
c\in\mathcal{E}(A)
,
then there exist
(a_{1},a_{2}),(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})\in A^{2}

with
(a_{1},a_{2})\neq(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})
such that

a_{1}+ca_{2}=\varphi(a_{1},a_{2})=\varphi(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})=a^{\prime}_{1}+ca^{\prime}_{2}.

Equivalently,

c(a^{\prime}_{2}-a_{2})=a_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1}.

If
a_{2}=a^{\prime}_{2}
, then
a_{1}=a^{\prime}_{1}
and
(a_{1},a_{2})=(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})
, which is absurd.
Therefore,
a_{2}\neq a^{\prime}_{2}
and

c=\frac{a_{1}-a^{\prime}_{1}}{a^{\prime}_{2}-a_{2}}\in D^{\ast}(A).

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Every nonempty finite subset of a field is a
\varphi
-Sidon set for all
but finitely many monic binary forms.

Proof.

If
A
is finite, then the sets
D(A)
and
D^{\ast}(A)
are also finite,
and so
\mathcal{E}(A)
is finite.
∎

We shall explicitly compute
\mathcal{E}(A)
for sets with 2, 3, or 4 elements.

Lemma 1
.

If
A
and
B
are nonempty subsets of a field and
A\subseteq B
, then
\mathcal{E}(A)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(B)
.

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathcal{E}(A)
. There exist distinct pairs
(a_{1},a_{2}),(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})\in A^{2}

such that
a_{1}+ca_{2}=a^{\prime}_{1}+ca^{\prime}_{2}
. If
A\subseteq B
, then
(a_{1},a_{2}),(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})

are also distinct pairs in
B^{2}
and so
c\in\mathcal{E}(B)
. This completes the proof.
∎

For
c\in\mathbf{F}^{\times}
, let

E(c)=\left\{\pm c,\pm\frac{1}{c}\right\}.

Lemma 2
.

Let
A
be a nonempty subset of the field
\mathbf{F}
.
If
c\in\mathcal{E}(A)
, then
E(c)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(A)
.

Proof.

We associate to every
c\in\mathbf{F}^{\times}
the monic binary linear forms

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}

\displaystyle=x_{1}+cx_{2}

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}

\displaystyle=x_{1}-cx_{2}

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}

\displaystyle=x_{1}+\frac{1}{c}x_{2}

\displaystyle\varphi_{4}

\displaystyle=x_{1}-\frac{1}{c}x_{2}.

Let
(a_{1},a_{2}),(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})\in A^{2}
. We have

\varphi_{1}(a_{1},a_{2})=a_{1}+ca_{2}=a^{\prime}_{1}+ca^{\prime}_{2}=\varphi_{1}(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})

if and only if

\varphi_{2}(a_{1},a_{2}^{\prime})=a_{1}-ca^{\prime}_{2}=a^{\prime}_{1}-ca_{2}=\varphi_{2}(a^{\prime}_{1},a_{2})

if and only if

\varphi_{3}(a_{2},a_{1})=a_{2}+\frac{1}{c}a_{1}=a^{\prime}_{2}+\frac{1}{c}a^{\prime}_{1}=\varphi_{3}(a^{\prime}_{2},a^{\prime}_{1})

if and only if

\varphi_{4}(a_{2},a^{\prime}_{1})=a_{2}-\frac{1}{c}a^{\prime}_{1}=a^{\prime}_{2}-\frac{1}{c}a_{1}=\varphi_{4}(a^{\prime}_{2},a_{1}).

Also,

(a_{1},a_{2})\neq(a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})

if and only if

(a_{1},a^{\prime}_{2})\neq(a^{\prime}_{1},a_{2})

if and only if

(a_{2},a_{1})\neq(a^{\prime}_{2},a^{\prime}_{1})

if and only if

(a_{2},a^{\prime}_{1})\neq(a^{\prime}_{2},a_{1}).

Thus, the set
A
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set for some linear form

\varphi\in\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3},\varphi_{4}\}

if and only if
A
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set for all

\varphi\in\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\varphi_{3},\varphi_{4}\}
.
Equivalently,
c\in\mathcal{E}(A)
implies
E(c)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(A)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathbf{F}
be a field and let
A=\{0,1\}
be the normalized subset of
\mathbf{F}
of cardinality 2.
Then

\mathcal{E}(0,1)=\{0,\pm 1\}=\{0\}\cup E(1).

Proof.

For
c\in\mathbf{F}
and
\varphi=x_{1}+cx_{2}
, we have

\varphi(A)=\{0,1,c,1+c\}
, and
|\varphi(A)|=4
if and only if
c\neq 0,\pm 1
.
Therefore,
\mathcal{E}(A)=\{0,\pm 1\}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathbf{F}
be a field and let
A=\{0,1,a\}
be a normalized subset of
\mathbf{F}
of cardinality 3.
Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(0,1,a)

\displaystyle=\{0,\pm 1\}\cup\left\{\pm a,\pm\frac{1}{a}\right\},\left\{\pm(a-1),\pm\frac{1}{a-1}\right\},\left\{\pm\frac{a-1}{a},\pm\frac{a}{a-1}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}(0,1)\cup E(a)\cup E(a-1)\cup E\left(\frac{a-1}{a}\right)

Proof.

For the monic binary form
\varphi=x_{1}+cx_{2}
,
the set
A=\{0,1,a\}
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set if and only if the set

\varphi(A)=\{0,1,c,1+c,a,ac,a+c,1+ac,a+ac\}

has cardinality 9. There are
\binom{9}{2}=36
pairs of distinct elements
of
\varphi(A)
.
We obtain the set
\mathcal{E}(A)
by equating pairs of distinct elements
of
\varphi(A)
and solving for
c
.
For example, for the elements
c\in\varphi(A)
and
1+ac\in\varphi(A)
, we have

c=1+ac
if and only if
c=-1/(a-1)
, and so
c=-1/(a-1)\in\mathcal{E}(A)
.
Lemma
2
implies that
E(a-1)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(A)
.
Computing
c
for all 36 pairs determines
\mathcal{E}(A)
.
This completes the proof.
∎

\displaystyle\cup E(a-1)\cup E\left(\frac{a-1}{a}\right)=

Theorem 4
.

Let
\mathbf{F}
be a field and let
A=\{0,1,a,b\}
be a normalized subset of
\mathbf{F}
of cardinality 4.
Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(0,1,a,b)=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(0,1,a)\cup\mathcal{E}(0,1,b)

\displaystyle\cup E(a-b)\cup E\left(\frac{a-b}{a}\right)\cup E\left(\frac{a-b}{b}\right)

\displaystyle\cup E\left(\frac{a-1}{b}\right)\cup E\left(\frac{b-1}{a}\right)\cup E\left(\frac{a-1}{b-1}\right)

\displaystyle\cup E\left(\frac{a-1}{a-b}\right)\cup E\left(\frac{b-1}{a-b}\right)\cup E\left(\frac{a}{b}\right).

Proof.

The set
A
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set if and only if the set

\displaystyle\varphi(A)=

\displaystyle\{0,1,a,b,c,ca,cb,1+c,a+c,b+c,

\displaystyle\qquad ac+1,ac+a,bc+1,bc+b,bc+a,ac+b\}

has cardinality 16.

3
, we determine
the elements

.

4.
Sidon sets for linear forms in
h\geq 2
variables

Here are two simple constructions of
h
-ary linear forms
\varphi

for which a finite set
A
is a
\varphi
-Sidon set.

Theorem 5
.

Let
A
be a finite set of complex numbers with
|A|\geq 2
. Let

\delta=\min\{|a-a^{\prime}|:a,a^{\prime}\in A\text{ and }a\neq a^{\prime}\}

and

\Delta=\max\{|a-a^{\prime}|:a,a^{\prime}\in A\text{ and }a\neq a^{\prime}\}.

Let
(c_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be sequence of complex numbers
such that

|c_{j}|>\frac{\Delta}{\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}|c_{i}|

for all
j\geq 2
. The set
A
is a
\varphi_{h}
-Sidon set for the

h
-ary linear form

\varphi_{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{h}c_{i}x_{i}

for all
h\geq 2
.

Proof.

If
A
is not a
\varphi_{h}
-Sidon set, then there exist distinct
h
-tuples

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})
and
(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h})
in
A^{h}
such that

\varphi_{h}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{h})=\varphi_{h}(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{h}).

There is a largest integer
j\in\{1,\ldots,h\}

such that
a_{j}\neq a^{\prime}_{j}
. It follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i}a_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{j}c_{i}a^{\prime}_{i}

and so

c_{j}\left(a^{\prime}_{j}-a_{j}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}c_{i}(a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}).

Therefore,

|c_{j}|\delta\leq\left|c_{j}\right|\left|a^{\prime}_{j}-a_{j}\right|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\left|c_{i}\right|\left|a_{i}-a^{\prime}_{i}\right|\leq\Delta\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\left|c_{i}\right|<\delta|c_{j}|

which is absurd.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
A
be a nonempty finite set of positive integers and let

a^{*}=\max(A)

For all integers

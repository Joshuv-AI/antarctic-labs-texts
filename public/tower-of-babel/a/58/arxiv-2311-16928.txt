Convergence of Time-Average along Uniformly Behaved in ℕ \mathbb{N} Sequences on Every Point
Yunping Jiang and Jessica Liu

Abstract
. We define a uniformly behaved in ℕ \mathbb{N} arithmetic sequence 𝐚 \mathbf{a} and an 𝐚 \mathbf{a} -mean Lyapunov stable dynamical system f f . We consider the time-average of a continuous function ϕ \phi along the 𝐚 \mathbf{a} -orbit of f f up to N N . The main result we prove in the paper is that this partial time-average converges for every point in the space if 𝐚 \mathbf{a} is uniformly behaved in ℕ \mathbb{N} and f f is minimal and uniquely ergodic and 𝐚 \mathbf{a} -mean Lyapunov stable. In addition, if 𝐚 \mathbf{a} is also completely additive, we then prove that the time-average of a continuous function ϕ \phi along the square-free 𝐚 \mathbf{a} -orbit of f f up to N N converges for every point in the space as well. All equicontinuous dynamical systems are 𝐚 \mathbf{a} -mean Lyapunov stable for any sequence 𝐚 \mathbf{a} . When 𝐚 \mathbf{a} is a subsequence of ℕ \mathbb{N} with positive lower density, we give two non-trivial examples of 𝐚 \mathbf{a} -mean Lyapunov stable dynamical systems. We give several examples of uniformly behaved in ℕ \mathbb{N} sequences, including the counting function of the prime factors in natural numbers, the subsequence of natural numbers indexed by the Thue-Morse (or Rudin-Shapiro) sequence, and the sequence of even (or odd) prime factor natural numbers. We also show that the sequence of square-free natural numbers (or even (or odd) prime factor square-free natural numbers) is rotationally distributed in ℕ \mathbb{N} but not uniformly distributed in ℤ \mathbb{Z} , thus not uniformly behaved in ℕ \mathbb{N} . We derive other consequences from the main result relevant to number theory and ergodic theory/dynamical systems.

1.
Introduction

One of the most celebrated theorems in ergodic theory is the Birkhoff ergodic theorem. It concerns the relationship between the time and the space averages for an integrable function on an ergodic measurable dynamical system. Suppose
f:X\to X
is a measure-preserving transformation on a probability measure space
(X,\mathcal{B},\nu)

and suppose
\phi
is a
\nu
-integrable function. Then, we can consider the time average,

\widehat{\phi}(x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\phi(f^{n}x),\quad x\in X,

if the limit exists,
and the space average

\widetilde{\phi}=\int_{X}\phi d\nu.

It is clear that
\widehat{\phi}(fx)=\widehat{\phi}(x)
and
\widehat{\phi}
is a constant on any orbit
\{f^{n}x\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. The Birkhoff ergodic theorem says that

Theorem A
.

If
f
is ergodic, then

\widehat{\phi}(x)=\widetilde{\phi}\quad\hbox{for $\nu$-almost all $x\in X$}

This theorem only guarantees that for almost all points in
X
, the time average equals the space average.
For every point, we need to assume the unique ergodicity. That is, if
X
is a compact metric space,
f
is uniquely ergodic, and
\phi
is a continuous function, then the above theorem holds for every point in
X
, extending the applicability of the Birkhoff ergodic theorem.

Motivated by Sarnak’s conjecture, we began the study of a new branch in ergodic theory/dynamical systems in
[
10
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
. In this new branch, we defined oscillating sequences and oscillating sequences of order
d\geq 2
and used these oscillating properties to study the classification of dynamical systems with zero topological entropy.

In this paper, we branch this project into studying the convergence of the partial time-average,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\phi(f^{a_{n}}x),\quad x\in X,

of a continuous function
\phi
along a sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})
of natural numbers for a dynamical system
f
on a compact metric space
X
, and its relationship with the space average.
The study of the convergence of a partial time-average along a sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})
has been studied by many prominent mathematicians. However, they concentrated on the convergence for almost all points or the
L^{2}
-convergence with respect to an invariant probability measure. We give a partial list in the literature,
[
4
,
5
,
27
,
18
]
. In this paper, we concentrate on the convergence for every point in the space along a uniformly behaved in
\mathbb{N}
sequence. A recent interesting paper concentrates on the convergence for every point in the space is
[
26
]
(see also its enlarged version
[
3
]
). However,
[
26
,
3
]
only considers the convergence of the partial time-average along the prime big omega sequence, which is a uniformly behaved in
\mathbb{N}
sequence as defined in our paper due to the combination of the work Pillai and Selberg
[
25
,
30
]
in1940s and the work of Delange
[
9
]
in 1950s in number theory. Note that
[
26
]
gives a dynamical system proof of Pillai, Selberg, and Delange’s results. Before this paper, the prime big omega sequence was the only uniformly behaved in
\mathbb{N}
sequence as we knew. In this paper, we will discover many other examples of uniformly behaved in
\mathbb{N}
sequences by using the oscillating property of their characteristic sequences. These examples have a common property: subsequences of
\mathbb{N}
with density
1/2
in
\mathbb{N}
. It is still an important problem for us to discover an example of a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
, which is uniformly behaved in
\mathbb{N}
but whose density in
\mathbb{N}
is not
1/2
.

2.
Uniformly Behaved in
\mathbb{N}
Sequences

Let
{\mathbb{N}}
denote the set of all natural numbers.
Suppose
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})
, where
n
runs in
{\mathbb{N}}
, is a sequence in
{\mathbb{N}}
or called an arithmetic function
\mathbf{a}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 1
.

We say
\mathbf{a}
is uniformly behaved in
\mathbb{N}
(u.b. in
\mathbb{N}
) if for any
0<\theta<1
,

(1)

~\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}=0.

This definition is derived from two definitions of uniform distribution in number theory. The first one is the definition of uniformly distributed modulo
1
.

Definition 2
.

Let
x_{n}
be a sequence of real numbers. We say that
x_{n}
is uniformly distributed modulo 1 (u.d.1) if for all
0\leq s<t\leq 1
, we have:

(2)

~\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#\{1\leq n\leq N\;|\;s\leq x_{n}\pmod{1}\leq t\}}{N}=t-s.

Definition 3
.

We say
\mathbf{a}
is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{Z}
(u.d. in
\mathbb{Z}
) if for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
r\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}
, the set
\{n\;|\;a_{n}=r\pmod{m}\}
has asymptotic density

1/m
, that is,

(3)

~\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#(\{1\leq n\leq N\;|\;a_{n}=r\pmod{m}\}}{N}=\frac{1}{m}.

We introduce the following definition:

Definition 4
.

Let
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})
be a sequence in
\mathbb{N}
. We say that
\mathbf{a}
is rotationally distributed in
\mathbb{N}
(r.d. in
\mathbb{N}
) if for all irrational
0<\theta<1
, the sequence
(a_{n}\theta)
is u.d.1.

From the Weyl criterion (see
[
20
, Chapter 1, Theorem 2.1]
), we have

Theorem 1
.

For any irrational number
0<\theta<1
, the sequence
x_{n}=a_{n}\theta
of real numbers is u.d.1 if and only if

(4)

~\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi iha_{n}\theta}=0\hbox{ for all integers $h\not=0$}.

The following theorem is found in
[
20
, Chapter 5, Corollary 1.1]
.

Theorem 2
.

A necessary and sufficient condition that
\mathbf{a}
be u.d. in
\mathbb{Z}
is that

(5)

~\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}=0\hbox{ for all rational numbers $0<\theta<1$}.

Thus (
3
) for all
m
implies (
1
) for all rational number
0<\theta<1
.
Therefore, r.d. in
\mathbb{N}
together with u.d. in
\mathbb{Z}
. implies u.b. in
\mathbb{N}
.

3.
\mathbf{a}
-Mean Lyapunov Stable Dynamical Systems

Suppose
X
is a compact metric space with a metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)
. Suppose
f:X\to X
is a continuous map. Then we can consider the
n
-composition

f^{n}=\underbrace{f\circ\cdots\circ f}_{n}

and the discrete dynamical system
\{f^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
with
f^{0}=Id
. Following the notation in number theory (refer to
[
28
,
29
]
), we sometimes call
f
a flow to simplify the notation.
For any
x\in X
,

Orb(x)=\{f^{n}x\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

denotes the orbit of
x
under
f
.
The flow
f
is said to be minimal if for every
x\in X
, the closure of the orbit of
x
under
f
is
X
, that is,

\overline{Orb(x)}=X.

Let
M(X)
be the space of all finite Borel measures
\nu
on
X
and let
C(X)
be the space of all continuous functions
\phi
on
X
with the maximum norm

\|\phi\|=\max_{x\in X}|\phi(x)|.

By the Riesz representation theorem,
M(X)
is the dual space of
C(X)
. We have

<\phi,\nu>=\int_{X}\phi d\nu,\quad\phi\in C(X),\;\nu\in M(X),

which gives a weak topology on
M(X)
. We say
\nu\in M(X)
is a probability measure if
\nu(X)=1
.
Let
M_{1}(X)
be the set of all probability measures. We say
\nu\in M_{1}(X)
is
f
-invariant if
\nu(f^{-1}(A))=\nu(A)
for all Borel subsets
A
in
X
. Let
M_{1,f}(X)
be the set of all
f
-invariant probability measures.
The set
M_{1,f}(X)
is a non-empty convex compact subset in
M(X)
. The set of all extremal points on
M_{1,f}(X)
are those ergodic
f
-invariant probability measures. Then,
f
is uniquely ergodic if
M_{1,f}(X)
contains only one point.

Let
\mathbf{a}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{N}
, and let
E
be a subset of
\mathbb{N}
. The
\mathbf{a}
-upper density and the
\mathbf{a}
-lower density of
E
are

\overline{D}_{\mathbf{a}}(E)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#\{a_{n}\in E\;|\;1\leq n\leq N\}}{N},

and

\underline{D}_{\mathbf{a}}(E)=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#\{a_{n}\in E\;|\;1\leq n\leq N\}}{N},

where
\{a_{n}\in E\;|\;1\leq n\leq N\}
is counted with multipicity, and the
\mathbf{a}
-density of
E
is

D_{\mathbf{a}}(E)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#\{a_{n}\in E\;|\;1\leq n\leq N\}}{N}

if the limit exists.
In number theory, the upper density and the lower density of
E
are defined as

\overline{D}(E)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#(E\cap[1,N])}{N}

and

\underline{D}(E)=\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#(E\cap[1,N])}{N}

and the density of
E
is also defined as

D(E)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#(E\cap[1,N])}{N}

if the limit exists.

Definition 5

(
\mathbf{a}
-Mean Lyapunov Stable)
.

Given a sequence
\mathbf{a}=(a_{n})
in
\mathbb{N}
, we say
f
is
\mathbf{a}
-mean Lyapunov stable (
\mathbf{a}
-MLS)
if for every
\epsilon>0
, there is a
\delta>0
and
E=E_{\epsilon}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that

d(x,y)<\delta
implies
d(f^{a_{n}}x,f^{a_{n}}y)<\epsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}\backslash E
with
\overline{D}_{\mathbf{a}}(E)<\epsilon
.

Remark 1
.

When
{\bf n}=(n)
,
{\bf n}
-mean Lyapunov stable is equivalent to mean Lyapunov stable (MLS), which is defined in
[
11
]
(see
[
10
]
as well).

Definition 6

(Equicontinuity)
.

We say
f
is equicontinuous if for every
\epsilon>0
,
there is a
\delta>0
such that whenever
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\delta
,
we have
d(f^{n}x,f^{n}y)<\epsilon
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

It is clear that if
f
is equicontinuous, then it is
\mathbf{a}
-MLS for any
\mathbf{a}
. It is well known that a surjective
f
is equicontinuous if and only if there exists a compatible metric
\widetilde{d}(\cdot,\cdot)
on
X
such that
f
is an isometry with respect to
\widetilde{d}(\cdot,\cdot)
, that is,
\widetilde{d}(fx,fy)=\widetilde{d}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
. Thus, if
f
is surjective and equicontinuous, then
f
must be a homeomorphism. Moreover, it is also known if
f
is a homeomorphism and equicontinuous,
then
f
is minimal on the closure
\overline{Orb(x)}
of the forward orbit for any
x\in X
(see
[
24
]
). Thus, an equicontinuous transitive homeomorphism
f
is always conjugate to a minimal rotation on a compact abelian metric group. In this case, let
\nu
be the unique Haar probability measure on
X
. Then, the measurable dynamical system
(X,f,\nu)
has discrete spectrum. An equicontinuous homeomorphism
f
can be decomposed into minimal subsystems. Equicontinuous flows are trivial examples of flows which are
\mathbf{a}
-MLS for any
\mathbf{a}
.

To present some non-trivial examples, we first need to show the connection between
\mathbf{a}
-MLS systems and MLS systems.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose
\mathbf{a}
is a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
. Consider it as a subset of
\mathbb{N}
. If
\underline{D}(\mathbf{a})>0
. Then for any subset
E\subset\mathbb{N}
,

\overline{D}_{\mathbf{a}}(E)\leq\frac{\overline{D}(E)}{\underline{D}(\mathbf{a})}.

Proof.

Consider

\frac{\#\{a_{n}\in E\;|\;1\leq n\leq N\}}{N}.

Since
\mathbf{a}
is a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
, we have

\frac{\#\{a_{n}\in E\;|\;1\leq n\leq N\}}{N}=\frac{\#\{\mathbf{a}\cap E\;|\;1\leq n\leq N\}}{N}

=\frac{\#(E\cap[1,a_{N}])}{N}=\frac{\#(E\cap[1,a_{N}])}{a_{N}}\frac{a_{N}}{N}.

By taking the upper limit, we get

\overline{D}(E)=\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#\{a_{n}\in E\;|\;1\leq n\leq N\}}{N}

\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#(E\cap[1,a_{N}])}{a_{N}}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}}{N}

=\frac{\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#(E\cap[1,a_{N}])}{a_{N}}}{\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{N}{a_{N}}}=\frac{\overline{D}(E)}{\underline{D}(\mathbf{a})}.

∎

Corollary 1
.

Suppose
f
is MLS. Then
f
is
\mathbf{a}
-MLS for any subsequence
\mathbf{a}
with
\underline{D}(\mathbf{a})>0
.

Proof.

Since
f
is MLS, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for any
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\delta
, we have a subset
E\subset\mathbb{N}
with
\overline{D}(E)<\underline{D}(\mathbf{a})\epsilon
, such that
d(f^{n}x,f^{n}y)<\epsilon
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus E
. Thus we have that
\overline{D}_{\mathbf{a}}(E)<\epsilon
and
d(f^{a_{n}}x,f^{a_{n}}y)<\epsilon
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\setminus E
.
This implies that
f
is
\mathbf{a}
-MLS.
∎

Suppose
\mathbf{a}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{N}
. For a given
\phi\in C(X)
, denote

S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi(x)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\phi\circ f^{a_{n}}(x)

as the time-average of
\phi
along
\mathbf{a}
at point
x\in X
up to
N
.

Proposition 2
.

Suppose
f
is
\mathbf{a}
-MLS. Then for any
\phi\in C(X)
,

\{S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\}

is an equicontinuous sequence in
C(X)
.

Proof.

By uniform continuity of
\phi
, for any
\epsilon>0
there exists
\eta>0
such that for any
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\eta
,

(6)

|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

We can take
\eta\leq\frac{\epsilon}{4\|\phi\|_{\infty}}
.
Since
f
is
\mathbf{a}
-MLS, there is a
\delta^{\prime}>0
such that
x,y\in X
with

d(x,y)<\delta^{\prime}
implies
d(f^{a_{n}}x,f^{a_{n}}y)<\eta
for all positive integers
n
except a set, denoted
E
, of
\mathbf{a}
-upper density less than
\eta
.
There exists an integer
N^{*}
such that

\frac{\#\{a_{n}\;|\;n\in E,1\leq n\leq N\}}{N}<\eta,\qquad\forall N\geq N^{*}

Thus if
N\geq N^{*}
we have

(7)

|S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi(x)-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi(y)|<2\|\phi\|_{\infty}\eta+\frac{\epsilon}{2}=\epsilon.

The family
\{S_{1,\mathbf{a}}\phi,\cdots,S_{N^{*},\mathbf{a}}\phi\}
of a finite number of continuous functions on the compact metric space
X
is equicontinuous. Thus there exists
\delta^{\prime\prime}

such that

(8)

d(x,y)<\delta^{\prime\prime}\Rightarrow\max_{1\leq N\leq N^{*}}|S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi(x)-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi(y)|<\epsilon.

Therefore, we prove the proposition by combining (
7
) and (
8
).
∎

In view of the proof of Proposition
2
, we give the following concept.

Definition 7

(
\mathbf{a}
-Mean Equicontinuity)
.

We say
f
is
\mathbf{a}
-mean equicontinuous (
\mathbf{a}
-MEQ for short) at a point

if for every
\epsilon>0
, there is

\delta>0
such that for every

with

we have

(9)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}d(f^{a_{n}}x,f^{a_{n}}y)<\epsilon.

We say that
f
is
\mathbf{a}
-MEQ if it is
\mathbf{a}
-MEQ at every point

.
By the compactness of
X
,
\mathbf{a}
-MEQ is equivalent to say (
9
) holds for all
x,y\in X
with
d(y,x)<\delta
.

Proposition 3
.

If
f
is
\mathbf{a}
-MLS, then it is
\mathbf{a}
-MEQ.

The proof of this proposition is similar to that of Proposition
2
.

We devote the rest of this section to describing two non-trivial examples of
\mathbf{a}
-MLS flows when
\mathbf{a}
is a subsequence of
\mathbb{N}
with
\underline{D}(\mathbf{a})>0
. The first example is a Denjoy counter-example in circle homeomorphisms.

Suppose
\mathbb{C}
is the complex plane. Let
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\;|\;|z|=1\}
be the unit circle in the complex plane.
Consider a continuous map
f:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
. We know (see, for example,
[
7
,
31
]
) that if
f
is equicontinuous, then either

(1)

\deg(f)=0
and for every
z\in\mathbb{T}
,
f^{n}z\to E
as
n\to\infty
, where
E
is the fixed point set of
f^{2}
,

(2)

\deg(f)=1
and
f
is topologically conjugate to a rigid rotation
R_{\rho}(z)=e^{2\pi i\rho}z
for some
0\leq\rho<1
, or

(3)

\deg(f)=-1
and
f^{2}
is topologically conjugate to the identity.

The above three cases are simple and
\mathbf{a}
-MLS for any
\mathbf{a}
.

We call a homeomorphism
f
of
\mathbb{T}
a circle homeomorphism. If
f
is orientation-reversing, then it has two fixed points. In this case
f^{2}
is orientation-preserving, conjugate to the identity, equicontinuous and thus
\mathbf{a}
-MLS for any
\mathbf{a}
.

Consider a circle homeomorphism
f
which is orientation preserving. Every such an
f
is semi-conjugate to a rigid rotation
R_{\rho}(z)=e^{2\pi i\rho}z:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{T}
for some

due a theory developed by Poincaré (see, for example,
[
14
, Chapter 1]
or
[

4.
Convergence on Every Point

In this section, we state and prove the main result in this paper.

Theorem 5

(Main Theorem)
.

Suppose
\mathbf{a}
is a uniformly behaved in
\mathbb{N}
sequence and suppose
f:X\to X
is minimal and uniquely ergodic and
\mathbf{a}
-MLS.
Then for any
\phi\in C(X)
and
x\in X

(10)

~\lim_{N\to\infty}S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi(x)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\phi(f^{a_{n}}x)=\int_{X}\phi d\nu,

where
\nu
is the unique
f
-invariant probability measure.

Proof.

Consider the sequence of functions
\{\phi\circ f^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
C(X)
. Let
{\mathbb{T}}=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}\;|\;|z|=1\}
be the unit circle in the complex plane
{\mathbb{C}}
.
From the spectral lemma (see
[
19
, pp.94-95]
) in ergodic theory, we have a probability measure
\nu_{\phi}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
such that

<\phi\circ f^{n},\phi>=\int_{X}\phi\circ f^{n}\phi d\nu=\int_{\mathbb{T}}e^{2\pi in\theta}d\nu_{\phi},\quad n\geq 1.

This gives that

<(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi,\phi>=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)d\nu_{\phi}.

The
f
-invariance of
\nu
is equivalent to

\int_{X}\phi\circ f\;d\nu=\int_{X}\phi\;d\nu,\quad\forall\;\phi\in C(X).

Consider the square of the
L^{2}
-norm of
(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi
on
(X,\nu)
: we have

\|(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}=\int_{X}\Big((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\Big)\Big(\overline{(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi}\Big)\;d\nu

=\int_{X}\Big((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\Big)\Big((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\Big)\;d\nu

since
(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi
is a real-valued function. For each term
\phi\circ f^{b_{m}}
in
(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi
, where
b_{m}=a_{m}+1
or
a_{m}
,
we have

\int_{X}\Big((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\Big)\phi\circ f^{b_{m}}\;d\nu=\int_{X}\Big((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f\circ f^{-b_{m}}-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\circ f^{-b_{m}}\Big)\phi\;d\nu

=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big(e^{2\pi i(a_{n}+1-b_{m})\theta}-e^{2\pi i(a_{n}-b_{m})\theta}\Big)d\nu_{\phi}

=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)\overline{e^{2\pi ib_{m}\theta}}d\nu_{\phi}.

Summing up every term, we get

\int_{X}\Big((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\Big)\Big((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\Big)\;d\nu

=\int_{\mathbb{T}}(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)\Big(\overline{(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}}\Big)d\nu_{\phi}

=\|(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)\|_{L^{2}(\nu_{\phi})}^{2},

which is the square of the
L^{2}
-norm of
(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)
on
(\mathbb{T},\nu_{\phi})
.
Therefore, we have that

\|(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}=\|(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)\|_{L^{2}(\nu_{\phi})}.

Since

\sup_{x\in X}|S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi(fx)-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi(x)|\leq 2\sup_{x\in X}|\phi(x)|\quad\forall\;N\geq 1

and

\sup_{0\leq\theta<1}|(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)|\leq 2,\quad\forall\;N\geq 1,

Fatou’s lemma implies that

\|\lim_{N\to\infty}((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\|(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}

=\lim_{N\to\infty}\|(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)\|_{L^{2}(\nu_{\phi})}

=\|\lim_{N\to\infty}(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)\|_{L^{2}(\nu_{\phi})}.

Since
\mathbf{a}
is u.b. in
\mathbb{N}
, we have that for any
0\leq\theta<1
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}(e^{2\pi i\theta}-1)\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)=0.

Thus,

\lim_{N\to\infty}((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)=0,\quad\nu-a.e..

From Proposition
2
, the sequence
\{(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\}_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is equicontinuous and uniformly bounded, thus the Ascoli-Arzela theorem implies that it is a compact subset in
C(X)
. Therefore, every subsequence of

\{(S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi\}_{N\in{\mathbb{N}}}

has a convergent subsequence. Suppose that
\{(S_{N_{k},\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N_{k},\mathbf{a}}\phi\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is a convergent subsequence with limit
\psi\in C(X)
. Since
f:X\to X
is minimal, the support of
\nu
is all of
X
. This implies that
\psi\equiv 0
on
X
. Therefore,

\lim_{N\to\infty}((S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)\circ f-S_{N,\mathbf{a}}\phi)=0\hbox{ uniformly on $X$}.

For any
x\in X
, let
\delta_{x}
be the
\delta
-measure on
X
, that is
\delta_{x}(A)=1
if
x\in A
and
\delta_{x}(A)=0
if

for any subset

of
X
. The push-forward measures of

are

. Consider

This is a probability Borel measure on

and it’s push-forward measures are

For any continuous function
\phi

<\phi,f_{*}\nu_{N,x}-\nu_{N,x}>=\int_{X}\phi d(f_{*}\nu_{N,x}-\nu_{N,x})=

Since the space of all probability measures on

for all

.

Applications to Number Theory

Let
\mathcal{SF}
be the set of square-free natural numbers that is,

\mathcal{SF}=\{n\;\;|\;\;p^{2}\not|n\text{ for all primes }p\}.

The indicator (or characteristic function) of
\mathcal{SF}
is
\mu^{2}(n),
where
\mu(n)
is the Möbius function defined on
{\mathbb{N}}
as
\mu(1)=1
and

\mu(n)=(-1)^{r}
if
n=p_{1}\cdots p_{r}
as a product of
r
distinct prime numbers
p_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq r
, and
\mu(n)=0
if
n\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\mathcal{SF}
.
It is well know that the density of
\mathcal{SF}
is,

D(\mathcal{SF})=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu^{2}(n)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{m^{2}|n}\mu(m)\Big)

=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{1\leq m\leq\sqrt{n}}1_{m^{2}|n}(m)\mu(m)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{[\sqrt{N}]}\mu(m)\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}1

=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{[\sqrt{N}]}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{2}}=\frac{1}{\zeta(2)}=\frac{1}{\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}}=\frac{6}{\pi^{2}},

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann zeta function.

Definition 10
.

We say a sequence

Theorem 6
.

Suppose
\mathbf{a}
is u.b. in
\mathbb{N}
and completely additive. Then it is also s.f.u.b. in
\mathbb{N}

Proof.

Let
0<\theta<1
.
Using the identity
\mu^{2}(n)=\sum_{m^{2}|n}\mu(m)
, we have that

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n\in\mathcal{SF}\leq N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\mu^{2}(n)e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}

=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Big(\sum_{m^{2}|n}\mu(m)\Big)e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{1\leq m\leq\sqrt{n}}1_{m^{2}|n}(m)\mu(m)e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}

=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{[\sqrt{N}]}\mu(m)\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}e^{2\pi ia_{m^{2}n}\theta}+o(1)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{m=1}^{[\sqrt{N}]}\mu(m)\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}e^{2\pi i(a_{n}+a_{m^{2}})\theta}+o(1)

=\sum_{m=1}^{[\sqrt{N}]}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)e^{2\pi ia_{m^{2}}\theta}+o(1)

For any
\epsilon>0
, since
A=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}1/m^{2}
converges, there is some
m_{0}
such that for all
N>m_{0}^{2}
,

\Big|\sum_{m=m_{0}+1}^{[\sqrt{N}]}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)e^{2\pi ia_{m^{2}}\theta}+o(1)\Big|

\leq\sum_{m=m_{0}+1}^{[\sqrt{N}]}\frac{|\mu(m)|}{m^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\right||e^{2\pi ia_{m^{2}}\theta}|+o(1)

\leq\sum_{m=m_{0}+1}^{[\sqrt{N}]}\frac{1}{m^{2}}+o(1)<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Since

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}=0,

we have an integer
N_{0}>m_{0}^{2}
such that for all
N>N_{0}
and for all
1\leq m\leq m_{0}
,

\left|\frac{1}{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\right|<\frac{\epsilon}{2A}.

Thus for all
N>N_{0}
,

\Big|\sum_{m=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)e^{2\pi ia_{m^{2}}\theta}\Big|\leq\sum_{m=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{|\mu(m)|}{m^{2}}\Big|\frac{1}{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big||e^{2\pi ia_{m^{2}}\theta}|<

<\frac{\epsilon}{2A}\sum_{m=1}^{m_{0}}\frac{1}{m^{2}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

Thus, we have that for all
N>N_{0}
,

\Big|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n\in\mathcal{SF}\leq N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big|=\Big|\sum_{m=1}^{[\sqrt{N}]}\frac{\mu(m)}{m^{2}}\Big(\frac{1}{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}\sum_{n=1}^{[\frac{N}{m^{2}}]}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}\Big)e^{2\pi ia_{m^{2}}\theta}+o(1)\Big|<\epsilon.

Therefore,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n\in\mathcal{SF}\leq N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}=0.

We have proved the theorem.

An example of u.b. in
\mathbb{N}
sequences is given by the prime omega functions, which count the number of prime factors of a natural number.
From Euler’s theorem, any natural number

can be factored into the product of prime numbers (up to ordering), that is,

n=p_{1}^{k_{1}}p_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots p_{m}^{k_{m}},

where
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
,
\cdots
,
p_{m}
are distinct prime numbers and
k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m}\geq 1
.
The counting function of prime factors with multiplicity is

\Omega(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}

and the counting function of prime factors without multiplicity is

\omega(n)=m.

Pillai and Selberg proved in the 1940s that
{\bf\Omega}=(\Omega(n))
is u.d. in
\mathbb{Z}
in
[
25
,
30
]
. From the paper
[
9
]
of Delange in 1950s, we know that
{\bf\Omega}
is r.d. in
\mathbb{N}
.
Therefore,
{\bf\Omega}
is u.b. in
\mathbb{N}
. We also know that
{\bf\Omega}
is completely additive. Thus we have the following consequences of Theorem
5
and Corollary
5
:

Corollary 7
.

Suppose
f
is
{\bf\Omega}
-MMA and
{\bf\Omega}
-MUEMLS. Then for any
\phi\in C(X)
,

(16)

~\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\phi(f^{\Omega(n)}x)=\int_{K}\phi d\nu_{K}

for any
x\in Basin(K)
where
\nu_{K}
is the unique
f|K
invariant probability measure.

.

f

Remark 3

In a recent paper

for any bounded function

.
This formula gives an alternative proof of Corollary

However, the ideas of the proof are very different from ours.

The Liouville function is defined as

Since

\mathbb{N}

Another way to see (

Definition 15

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}\phi(f^{n}x)=0,\quad\forall\phi\in C(X),\;\;\forall x\in X,

Suppose

if

Suppose the density

exists. The following theorem is easily to prove.

As a consequence of our main result, we have that

Corollary 9

Suppose
\mathbf{a}

\mathbb{N}

\mathbf{a}
-MMA and

-MUEMLS. Then

.

Definition 16
.

Suppose
\mathbf{c}
is a sequence of complex numbers. We say
\mathbf{c}

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}e^{2\pi in\theta}=0,\quad\forall\;\;0\leq\theta<1,

and

Theorem 9
.

Let
\mathbf{c}
be the sequence defined by the indicator
(t_{n})
for

. Then
\mathbf{c}
is an oscillating sequence if and only if
\mathbf{a}

\mathbb{N}

Proof.

Suppose
\mathbf{c}
is an oscillating sequence. Since

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{n}=0,

the density of
A

D(A)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#(A\cap[1,N])}{N}=\frac{1}{2}.

Thus we have a constant

such that

for all

. This implies that

\frac{a_{N}}{N}\leq C=\frac{1}{\epsilon},\quad\forall\;\;N\geq 1.

For every

Since
\mathbf{c}
is an oscillating sequence, we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{a_{N}}c_{n}e^{2\pi in\theta}=0.

We also know

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{a_{N}}e^{2\pi in\theta}=0.

Since
a_{N}/(2N)\leq C/2
for all

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}=0.

Thus
\mathbf{a}
is u.b in
\mathbb{N}
.

On the other hand, if
\mathbf{a}
is u.b. in
\mathbb{N}
, then the above calculation shows that for every

∎

Recall that

Theorem 10

Proof.

Let
t_{n}=0
for

and

t_{n}=\frac{1+\mu(n)}{2},\quad n\in\mathcal{SF}.

Then
t_{n}
is the indicator of
EF\cap\mathcal{SF}
.

For any
0<\theta<1
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}e^{2\pi ia_{n}\theta}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n\in\mathcal{SF}\leq a_{N}}t_{n}e^{2\pi in\theta}

=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1\leq n\in\mathcal{SF}\leq a_{N}}\frac{1+\mu(n)}{2}e^{2\pi in\theta}

=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}}{2N}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{1\leq n\in\mathcal{SF}\leq a_{N}}\mu(n)e^{2\pi in\theta}+\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}}{2N}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{1\leq n\in\mathcal{SF}\leq a_{N}}e^{2\pi in\theta}

=\Big(\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}}{2N}\Big)\Big(\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{a_{N}}\mu(n)e^{2\pi in\theta}\Big)+\Big(\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}}{2N}\Big)\Big(\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{a_{N}}\mu^{2}(n)e^{2\pi in\theta}\Big)

=\frac{1}{2D(EF\cap{\mathcal{SF}})}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{a_{N}}\mu^{2}(n)e^{2\pi in\theta}

since
\mu(n)
is an oscillating sequence and since
D(EF\cap{\mathcal{SF}})\not=0
.
As we have seen in Theorem
7
, when
0<\theta<1
is irrational,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{a_{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{a_{N}}\mu^{2}(n)e^{2\pi in\theta}=0

This implies that
EF\cap\mathcal{SF}=(a_{n})
is r.d. in
\mathbb{N}
.

There are rational numbers

Thus,

\mathbb{N}

For

The rest of the proof is the same as that for

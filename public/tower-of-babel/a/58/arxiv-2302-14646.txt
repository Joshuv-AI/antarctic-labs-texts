Construction of general forms of ordinary generating functions for more families of numbers and multiple variables polynomials
Yilmaz SIMSEK

Abstract
The aim of this paper is to construct general forms of ordinary generating functions for special numbers and polynomials involving Fibonacci type numbers and polynomials, Lucas numbers and polynomials, Chebyshev polynomials, Sextet polynomials, Humbert-type numbers and polynomials, chain and anti-chain polynomials, rank polynomials of the lattices, length of any alphabet of words, partitions, and other graph polynomials. By applying the Euler transform and the Lambert series to these generating functions, many new identities and relations are derived. By using differential equations of these generating functions, some new recurrence relations for these polynomials are found. Moreover, general Binet’s type formulas for these polynomials are given. Finally, some new classes of polynomials and their corresponding certain family of special numbers are investigated with the help of these generating functions. Keywords. Generating function, Special functions, Fibonacci polynomials and numbers, Special polynomials and numbers, Special functions MSC2020. 05A15, 33-XX, 11B39, 11B83

Introduction

Special polynomials and numbers with their generating functions play a vital
role not only in many branches of mathematics, but also in almost many
applied sciences, thanks to their important applications. Therefore, the development of these functions is always destined to be up to date. As a result, many researchers have been doing very deep research on these functions for more than a century in order to solve many different problems involving mathematical models. Modified generating functions for the Fibonacci type polynomials, the Lucas type polynomials, and partitions have been given by many researchers for almost regularly every year. The Fibonacci polynomials, which are a polynomial sequence, are defined by the

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{Y}_{n}\left(P_{1}(x_{1}),P_{2}(x_{2})\right)w^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{w}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{w}{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients of
w^{n}
on both sides of the above equation,
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{n}\left(P_{1}(x_{1}),P_{2}(x_{2})\right)=\frac{\left(a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)^{n}-a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\left(\frac{1}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)^{n}\right)}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}.

After some elementary calculations in the above equation, the following
Binet type formula is obtained:

Theorem 12

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{n}\left(P_{1}(x_{1}),P_{2}(x_{2})\right)

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n-1}\left(P_{2}(x_{2})\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}}\left(\frac{P_{1}(x_{1})+\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}}{\left(-P_{1}(x_{1})+\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}\right)^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{2^{n-1}\left(P_{2}(x_{2})\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}}\left(\frac{P_{1}(x_{1})-\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}}{\left(-P_{1}(x_{1})-\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}\right)^{n}}\right).

Substituting
m=2
and
k=1
into (
2
), we now give Binet type
formula for the polynomials
\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{2}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{1}})\right)=\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P_{1}(x_{1}),P_{2}(x_{2});Q_{0}(x_{0}),Q_{1}(x_{1})\right)
as follows:

\frac{Q_{0}(x_{0})+Q_{1}(x_{1})w}{1+P_{1}(x_{1})w+P_{2}(x_{2})w^{2}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{2}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{1}})\right)w^{n}.

The partial fraction decomposition of the left side of above equation is
given by

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{2}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{1}})\right)w^{n}=\frac{Q_{0}(x_{0})+Q_{1}(x_{1})w}{\left(1-\frac{w}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)\left(1-\frac{w}{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)},

where

a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{-P_{1}(x_{1})+\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}}{2P_{2}(x_{2})}

and

a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=\frac{-P_{1}(x_{1})-\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}}{2P_{2}(x_{2})}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P_{1}(x_{1}),P_{2}(x_{2});Q_{0}(x_{0}),Q_{1}(x_{1})\right)w^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\left(\frac{Q_{0}(x_{0})}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})}+Q_{1}(x_{1})\right)}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)^{n}w^{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\left(\frac{Q_{0}(x_{0})}{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}+Q_{1}(x_{1})\right)}{\left(a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\right)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)^{n}w^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients of
w^{n}
on both sides of the above equation,
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P_{1}(x_{1}),P_{2}(x_{2});Q_{0}(x_{0}),Q_{1}(x_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\left(\frac{Q_{0}(x_{0})}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})}+Q_{1}(x_{1})\right)}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\left(\frac{1}{a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)^{n}

\displaystyle+\frac{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})\left(\frac{Q_{0}(x_{0})}{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}+Q_{1}(x_{1})\right)}{\left(a_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})-a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\right)}\left(\frac{1}{a_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})}\right)^{n}.

After some elementary calculations in the above equation, the following Binet type formula is obtained:

Theorem 13

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P_{1}(x_{1}),P_{2}(x_{2});Q_{0}(x_{0}),Q_{1}(x_{1})\right)

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n-1}\left(P_{2}(x_{2})\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}}\left(\frac{\left(P_{1}(x_{1})+\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}\right)Q_{0}(x_{0})-2Q_{1}(x_{1})}{\left(-P_{1}(x_{1})+\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}\right)^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2^{n-1}\left(P_{2}(x_{2})\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}}\left(\frac{\left(-P_{1}(x_{1})+\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}\right)Q_{0}(x_{0})+2Q_{1}(x_{1})}{\left(-P_{1}(x_{1})-\sqrt{P_{1}^{2}(x_{1})-4P_{2}(x_{2})}\right)^{n}}\right).

Substituting
P_{1}(x_{1})=-1
,
P_{2}(x_{2})=-1
,
Q_{0}(x_{0})=0
and
Q_{1}(x_{1})=1
into (
22
), we get the following explicit formula for the Fibonacci numbers:

4
Higher order of the polynomials
\mathbb{Y}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)
and
\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)

In this section, we define higher order of the polynomials
\mathbb{Y}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)
and
\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)
. We give some
properties of these polynomials with their special values.

The polynomials
\mathbb{Y}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)
of order
\beta
, which is denoted by
\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)
, are defined by the following generating function

F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});\beta\right)=\frac{1}{\left(1+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}P_{j}(x_{j})w^{j}\right)^{\beta}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)w^{n}.

(26)

Here we note that

\mathbb{Y}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right):=\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(1)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right).

Some special values of the function
F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});\beta\right)
are given as follows:

F\left(w,-1;\beta\right)=\frac{1}{\left(1-w\right)^{\beta}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(-1\right)w^{n}.

For
\left|w\right|<1
, using binomial theorem, we get

\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(-1\right)=(-1)^{n}\binom{-\beta}{n}=\binom{\beta+n-1}{n}=\frac{\beta\left(\beta+1\right)\cdots\left(\beta+n-1\right)}{n!}=\frac{\left(\beta\right)_{n}}{n!},

where
\left(\beta\right)_{n}
is denoted the rising factorial (the Pochhammer function). Few values of
\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(-1\right)
are given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(1)}\left(-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1\right)_{n}}{n!}=1,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(2)}\left(-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(2\right)_{n}}{n!}=n+1,

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(3)}\left(-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(3\right)_{n}}{n!}=\frac{\left(n+1\right)\left(n+2\right)}{2},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(4)}\left(-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(4\right)_{n}}{n!}=\frac{\left(n+1\right)\left(n+2\right)(n+3)}{6}

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(k)}\left(-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(k+1\right)_{n}}{n!}=\binom{k+n}{n},

and so on.

Substituting
P_{1}(x)=-2x
, and
P_{2}(x)=1
, into (
26
), we get the
most important family of orthogonal polynomials which are so-called the
ultraspherical polynomials or the Gegenbauer polynomials, for
0\leq\left|x\right|<1
,
\beta>0
,

C_{n}^{(\beta)}(x):=\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(-2x,1\right),

which are particular solutions of the Gegenbauer differential equation, and
these polynomials are also represented by the following Gaussian
hypergeometric series:

\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(-2x,1\right)=\frac{\left(2\beta\right)_{n}}{n!}\text{ }_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,2\beta+n;\frac{1}{2}+\beta;\frac{1-x}{2}\right),

and also special cases of the Jacobi polynomials are given by

P_{n}^{\left(-\frac{1}{2}+\beta,-\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)}(x):=\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}+\beta\right)_{n}}{\left(2\beta\right)_{n}}\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(-2x,1\right).

For detail about these
polynomials see also
[
41
]
, and
[
44
]
.

With the aid of the Rodrigues formula for the Gegenbauer polynomials, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(-2x,1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(n+2\beta)(1-x^{2})^{-\beta+\frac{1}{2}}\binom{n-\frac{1}{2}}{n}}{2^{n}\Gamma(2\beta)\Gamma\left(n+\beta+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left\{(1-x^{2})^{n+\beta-\frac{1}{2}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n}\sqrt{\pi}(n+1)C_{n}\Gamma(n+2\beta)(1-x^{2})^{-\beta+\frac{1}{2}}}{2^{3n}\Gamma(2\beta)\Gamma\left(n+\beta+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left\{(1-x^{2})^{n+\beta-\frac{1}{2}}\right\},

where
C_{n}
denotes the Catalan numbers,
\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\pi}
and

\Gamma\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)=\binom{n-\frac{1}{2}}{n}n!\sqrt{\pi}=\frac{(2n)!}{4^{n}n!}\sqrt{\pi}.

When
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
and
\beta=1
, the Gegenbauer polynomials reduce
to the Legendre polynomials and the Chebyshev polynomials of the second
kind, respectively.

The Rogers polynomials or the Rogers-Askey-Ismail polynomials, which are so
called continuous
q
-ultraspherical polynomials, are a family of orthogonal
polynomials introduced by Rogers (1892, 1893, 1894) in the course of his
work on the Rogers–Ramanujan identities, are defined in terms of the
q
-Pochhammer symbol and the basic hypergeometric series

C_{n}^{(\beta\left|q\right.)}(x):=\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta\left|q\right.)}\left(-2x,1\right)=\frac{\left(\beta;q\right)}{\left(q;q\right)}e^{in\theta}\text{ }_{2}\phi_{1}\left(q^{-n},\beta;\beta^{-1}q^{1-n};q,q\beta^{-1}e^{-2i\theta}\right),

where
x=\cos\left(\theta\right)
,

{}_{d}\phi_{c}\left(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d};b_{1},\ldots,b_{c};q,z\right)=\sum\frac{(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d};q)_{n}}{(b_{1},\ldots,b_{c};q)_{n}}\left((-1)^{n}q^{\binom{n}{2}}\right)^{1+c-d}z^{n}

and

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{d};q)_{n}=(a_{1};q)_{n}(a_{2};q)_{n}\cdots(a_{d};q)_{n},(a_{1};q)_{n}

denotes the
q
-Pochhammer symbol (
q
-shifted factorial) or
q
-analog of
the Pochhammer symbol
(x)_{n}
,

(a_{j};q)_{n}=\left(1-a_{j}\right)\left(1-a_{j}q\right)\cdots\left(1-a_{j}q^{n-1}\right)

and
(a_{j};q)_{0}=1
and also

\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}\frac{(q^{x};q)_{n}}{(1-q)^{n}}=(x)_{n}

(
cf
.
[
2
]
).

Substituting
\beta=\frac{1}{2}
,
P_{1}(x)=-2x
and
P_{2}(x)=1
into (
26
), we get other most important family of orthogonal polynomials which
are so-called the Legendre polynomials

P_{n}(x):=\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\frac{1}{2})}\left(-2x,1\right).

Substituting
\beta=-\frac{1}{2}
,
P_{1}(x)=-3x,P_{2}(x)=0
, and
P_{3}(1)=1
into (
26
), we get the Pincherle polynomials introduced
by Humbert in 1921,

P_{n}(x):=\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(-\frac{1}{2})}\left(-3x,0,1\right)

and

\mathbb{Y}_{2n}^{(-\frac{1}{2})}\left(-3x,0,1\right)=\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}\left\{x^{n}(x^{2}-1)^{n}\right\}

(
cf
.
[

).

Substituting
P_{1}(x)=-mx,P_{2}(x)=P_{m}(x)=0,\ldots,P_{m}(1)=1

26
), we get the Humbert polynomials, which are generalization of the
Pincherle polynomials introduced by Humbert in 1921,

The polynomials
\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)
of order
\alpha,\beta
, which is denoted by
\mathbb{S}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)

\displaystyle H\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}});\alpha,\beta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}Q_{j}(x_{j})w^{j}\right)^{\alpha}}{\left(1+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}P_{j}(x_{j})w^{j}\right)^{\beta}}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)w^{n}.

It is clear that

\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right):=\mathbb{S}_{n}^{(1,1)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right).

Combining (
27
) with (
26

H\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}});1,\beta\right)=\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}Q_{j}(x_{j})w^{j}\right)F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});\beta\right).

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{(1,\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)w^{n}=\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}Q_{j}(x_{j})w^{j}\right)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)w^{n}.

Therefore

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{(1,\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)w^{n}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{n=j}^{\infty}Q_{j}(x_{j})\mathbb{Y}_{n}^{(\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)w^{n}.

Comparing the coefficients of
w^{n}
on both sides of the above
equality, we arrive at the following theorem:

Theorem 17

\mathbb{S}_{n}^{(1,\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}Q_{j}(x_{j})\mathbb{Y}_{n-j}^{(\beta)}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right).

(28)

5
Recurrence relation of the polynomials
\mathbb{Y}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)
and
\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)

In this section, using partial derivative equations of the generating functions for the polynomials
\mathbb{Y}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)
and
\mathbb{S}_{n}\left(P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)
, we give recurrence relations of these polynomials.

Some differential equations of the functions
F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)
and
G\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)
are given as follows:

\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\left\{F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)\right\}=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}jP_{j}(x_{j})w^{j-1}F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});2\right),

(29)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\left\{G\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}lQ_{l}(x_{l})w^{l-1}F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)

(30)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k}Q_{l}(x_{l})w^{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\left\{F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)\right\},

and

\displaystyle F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});2\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\left\{G\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)\right\}

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}lQ_{l}(x_{l})w^{l-1}+\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}lQ_{l}(x_{l})P_{j}(x_{j})w^{j+l-1}-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}jQ_{l}(x_{l})P_{j}(x_{j})w^{l+j-1}.

The above differential equation can also be given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\left\{G\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});Q(\overrightarrow{X_{k}})\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{l=1}^{k}lQ_{l}(x_{l})w^{l-1}F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}})\right)-

(32)

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{l=0}^{k}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}jQ_{l}(x_{l})P_{j}(x_{j})w^{l+j-1}F\left(w,P(\overrightarrow{X_{m}});2\right).

or

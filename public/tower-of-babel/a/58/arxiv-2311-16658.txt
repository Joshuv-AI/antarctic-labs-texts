Quantum steering for two-mode states with Continuous-variable in laser channel
Kaimin Zheng 1,∗ , Jifeng Sun 2,∗ , Liyun Hu 2,† , Lijian Zhang 1,‡

Abstract
The Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen steering is an important resource for one-sided device independent quantum information processing. This steering property will be destroyed during the interaction between quantum system and environment for some practical applications. In this paper, we use the representation of characteristic function for probability to examine the quantum steering of two-mode states with continuous-variable in laser channel, where both the gain factor and the loss effect are considered. Firstly, we analyse the steering time of two-mode squeezed vacuum state under one-mode and two-mode laser channel respectively. We find the gain process will introduce additional noise to the two-mode squeezed vacuum state such that the steerable time is reduced. Secondly, by quantising quantum Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen steering, it shows that two-side loss presents a smaller steerability than one-side loss although they share the same two-way steerable time. In addition, we find the more gained party can steer the other’s state, while the other party cannot steer the gained party in a certain threshold value. In this sense, it seems that the gain effect in one party is equivalent to the loss effect in the other’s party. Our results pave way for the distillation of Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen steering and the quantum information processing in practical quantum channels.



I
Introduction

The Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen (EPR) steering was first proposed by Schrödinger in 1935 in response to the famous paper by Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen on the completeness of quantum mechanics
Schrödinger 1935
. It allows the subsystems Alice steer the quantum state of a distant subsystem Bob during a local measurement
Reid et al. 2009
;
Gallego and Aolita 2015
;
He et al. 2015
;
Cavalcanti and
Skrzypczyk 2016
;
Uola et al. 2020
. In terms of the degree of quantum correlation, EPR steering is intermediate between Bell inequality and quantum entanglement
Wiseman et al. 2007
. Unlike Bell inequality and quantum entanglement, EPR steering is inherently asymmetric for two subsystems, i.e., the ability of Alice to steer Bob may be different from the ability of Bob to steer Alice, and there may even be a one-way EPR steering situation
Armstrong et al. 2015
;
Sun et al. 2016
;
Deng et al. 2017
;
Tischler et al. 2018
;
Cavaillès et al. 2018
;
Weston et al. 2018
. EPR steering has received increasing attention since Wiseman et al. have analysed quantum entanglement, EPR steering and Bell inequality separately from the perspective of quantum information tasks
Wiseman et al. 2007
.

One important element of research on quantum EPR steering is to demonstrate the existence of one-way steering. In terms of discrete-variable systems, the one-way EPR steering was first demonstrated with Werner states and have been proved by using the ”all-or-nothing proof” criterion and quantum storage
Saunders et al. 2010
;
Sun et al. 2014
. Pan et al. have demonstrated the true four-body EPR steering and its application to one-way quantum computing
Li et al. 2015
. For the continuously variable systems, the existence of one-way EPR steering based on Reid’s criterion was first demonstrated by introducing the loss of one-sided paths in two-mode squeezed state
Händchen et al. 2012
.
On this basis, Su et al. have successfully realized the free control of EPR steering by using noise, and the many-body Gaussian EPR steering have been observed by introducing asymmetric losses
Qin et al. 2017
. On the other hand, the interaction between quantum system and environment is inevitable for the quantum information processing in practical quantum channels. Thus it is crucial to investigate the effect of decoherence on EPR steering in quantum channels, which can help to design effective distillation programmes. The decoherence of EPR steering have been theoretical analysed and experimentally demonstrated in recent years
Rosales-Zárate
et al. 2015
;
Deng et al. 2021
;
Wang et al. 2022
;
Liu et al. 2022
. However, most of them only take account in lossy and noisy channels. To avoid the decoherence of EPR steering and accomplish the quantum information processing in practical quantum channels, we should
analyse the evolution of EPR steering in more general environments, such as laser channel and phase-sensitive environment.

We first derive the relation between the probability distribution functions and the characteristic function in Sec. II. The relation provide a relative easy calculation for the analytical expressions of EPR steering criterion for two-mode squeezed vacuum state. In Sec. III, we analyse the steering conditions of two-mode squeezed vacuum state under laser channel. In Sec. IV, we further analyse the relationship between magnitude of EPR steering and the parameters of the laser channel.
In Sec. V, we make a comparison about the decoherence between quantum steering and
entanglement in laser channel. In Sec. VI, we extend our discussions to the evolution of the TMSV in the presence of phase-sensitive environment. Lastly, in Sec. VII, we give concluding remarks.

II
The EPR steering criterion

II.1
Relation between PDFs and CF

In quantum steering criterion, the probability distribution functions (PDFs)
are usually used in the calculation of original Reid criterion, entropic steering criterion, and Adesso’s steering low bound for Gaussian states. In addition, it is worth noting that the characteristic function (CF) provides a relative easy
calculation for the analytical expressions of the average values, especially
for the systems in noisy environments. Here, thus we first derive the relation
between PDFs and CF.

For a two-mode quantum system
\rho
, its Weyl expression of density operator
is given by

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dq_{1}dp_{1}dq_{2}dp_{2}}{\pi^{2}}\chi\left(q_{1},p_{1};q_{2},p_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\times D_{1}\left(-q_{1},-p_{1}\right)D_{2}\left(-q_{2},-p_{2}\right),

(1)

where
\chi\left(q_{1},p_{1};q_{2},p_{2}\right)=\mathtt{tr}\left[\rho D_{1}\left(q_{1},p_{1}\right)D_{2}\left(q_{2},p_{2}\right)\right]

is the CF and
D_{l}\left(q_{l},p_{l}\right)
(
l=1,2
) are the
displacement operators. In addition,
D_{l}\left(q_{l},p_{l}\right)
can
also be shown as

\displaystyle D_{l}\left(q_{l},p_{l}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{i(p_{l}Q_{l}-q_{l}P_{l})}

\displaystyle=e^{-iq_{l}p_{l}}e^{ip_{l}Q_{l}}e^{-iq_{l}P_{l}}

(2)

\displaystyle=e^{iq_{l}p_{l}}e^{-iq_{l}P_{l}}e^{ip_{l}Q_{l}},

(3)

where
Q_{l}=a_{l}+a_{l}^{{\dagger}}
and
P_{l}=(a_{l}-a_{l}^{{\dagger}})/i
are the
coordinate and momentum operators, respectively, satisfying
\left[Q_{l},P_{l}\right]=2i
. From Eqs. (
2
) and (
3
) and noticing
that
e^{-iq_{l}P_{l}}\left|\bar{q}_{l}\right\rangle=\left|\bar{q}_{l}+\sqrt{2}q_{l}\right\rangle
as well as
e^{ip_{l}Q_{l}}\left|\bar{p}_{l}\right\rangle=\left|\bar{p}_{l}+\sqrt{2}p_{l}\right\rangle
,
it is ready to see

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{q}_{l}\right|D_{l}\left(q_{l},p_{l}\right)\left|\bar{q}_{l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{i\sqrt{2}\bar{q}_{l}p_{l}}\delta\left(\sqrt{2}q_{l}\right),

(4)

\displaystyle\left\langle\bar{p}_{l}\right|D_{l}\left(q_{l},p_{l}\right)\left|\bar{p}_{l}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-i\sqrt{2}q_{l}\bar{p}_{l}}\delta\left(\sqrt{2}p_{l}\right),

(5)

where
\left|\bar{q}_{l}\right\rangle
and
\left|\bar{p}_{l}\right\rangle
are respectively the eigenstates of
Q_{l}
and
P_{l},

i.e.,
Q_{l}\left|\bar{q}_{l}\right\rangle=\sqrt{2}\bar{q}_{l}\left|\bar{q}_{l}\right\rangle
,
P_{l}\left|\bar{p}_{l}\right\rangle=\sqrt{2}\bar{p}_{l}\left|\bar{p}_{l}\right\rangle
.

After substituting Eqs.(
4
) and (
5
) into Eq.(
1
), then the
joint PDFs and its marginal and conditional PDFs can be easily calculated by,
for instance,

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2}\right|\rho\left|\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dp_{1}dp_{2}}{2\pi^{2}}e^{-i\sqrt{2}\left(\bar{q}_{1}p_{1}+\bar{q}_{2}p_{2}\right)}\chi\left(0,p_{1};0,p_{2}\right),

(6)

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}\right|\rho\left|\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dq_{1}dq_{2}}{2\pi^{2}}e^{i\sqrt{2}\left(\bar{p}_{1}q_{1}+\bar{p}_{2}q_{2}\right)}\chi\left(q_{1},0;q_{2},0\right),

and

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{q}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\bar{q}_{2}P\left(\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dp_{1}e^{-i\sqrt{2}\bar{q}_{1}p_{1}}\chi\left(0,p_{1};0,0\right),

(7)

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{p}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\bar{p}_{2}P\left(\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dq_{1}e^{i\sqrt{2}\bar{p}_{1}q_{1}}\chi\left(q_{1},0;0,0\right),

as well as

P\left(\left.\bar{q}_{2}\right|\bar{q}_{1}\right)=P\left(\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2}\right)/P\left(\bar{q}_{1}\right).

(8)

Thus the inferred variance of
q
can be derived by

\displaystyle\Delta_{\inf}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\bar{q}_{1}P\left(\bar{q}_{1}\right)\Delta_{\inf}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}=\bar{q}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\bar{q}_{1}P\left(\bar{q}_{1}\right)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\bar{q}_{2}P\left(\left.\bar{q}_{2}\right|\bar{q}_{1}\right)\left(\bar{q}_{2}-q_{est}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\bar{q}_{1}d\bar{q}_{2}P\left(\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2}\right)\left(\bar{q}_{2}-q_{est}\right)^{2}.

(9)

Here we should note that
q_{est}
is the function of variable
\bar{q}_{1}
.

II.2
Reid criterion

Using the linear inferences, i.e., taking
q_{est}=d-\lambda\bar{q}_{1}
,
where the two parameters
\lambda
and
d
will be chosen to minimize the
inferred variance
\Delta_{\inf}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)
, after a straight calculation then one can get

\displaystyle\Delta_{\inf}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{2}\left\langle Q_{1}^{2}\right\rangle+\lambda\left\langle Q_{1}Q_{2}\right\rangle-\sqrt{2}\lambda d\left\langle Q_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+d^{2}-\sqrt{2}d\left\langle Q_{2}\right\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\left\langle Q_{2}^{2}\right\rangle,

(10)

where we have used the relation between the CF and the average values, for
instance,

\displaystyle\left\langle Q_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial}{\partial\left(ip_{2}\right)}\chi\left(0,0;0,p_{2}\right)\right|_{p_{2}=0},

(11)

\displaystyle\left\langle Q_{2}^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\left(ip_{2}\right)^{2}}\chi\left(0,0;0,p_{2}\right)\right|_{p_{2}=0},

(12)

\displaystyle\left\langle Q_{1}Q_{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{-\partial^{2}}{\partial p_{1}\partial p_{2}}\chi\left(0,p_{1};0,p_{2}\right)\right|_{p_{1},p_{2}=0}.

(13)

The values of the two parameters
\lambda
and
d
are chosen by minimizing

\Delta_{\inf}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)
such that

\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\Delta_{\inf}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)=\frac{\partial}{\partial d}\Delta_{\inf}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)=0
, from which it follows
that

\lambda=-\frac{E_{Q_{1}Q_{2}}}{V\left(Q_{1}\right)},d=\frac{\lambda\left\langle Q_{1}\right\rangle+\left\langle Q_{2}\right\rangle}{\sqrt{2}},

(14)

where
E_{Q_{1}Q_{2}}=\left\langle Q_{1}Q_{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle Q_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle Q_{2}\right\rangle
and
V\left(Q_{l}\right)=\left\langle Q_{l}^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle Q_{l}\right\rangle^{2}
is the voriance for
Q_{l}
,
l=1,2
. Substituting
Eq. (
14
) into Eq. (
10
), the minimized inferred variance

\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)
is given
by

\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left[V\left(Q_{2}\right)-\frac{\left(E_{Q_{1}Q_{2}}\right)^{2}}{V\left(Q_{1}\right)}\right],

(15)

The minimized inferred variance
\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)
can be similarly derived as

\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left[V\left(P_{2}\right)-\frac{\left(E_{P_{1}P_{2}}\right)^{2}}{V\left(P_{1}\right)}\right],

(16)

where
V\left(P_{l}\right)=\left\langle P_{l}^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle P_{l}\right\rangle^{2}
and
E_{P_{1}P_{2}}=\left\langle P_{1}P_{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle P_{1}\right\rangle\left\langle P_{2}\right\rangle
. Combing Eqs. (
15
) and (
16
) and employing
the Heisenberg uncertainty relation, yields the Reid inequality (RI)

V_{B|A}\equiv\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)\geqslant\frac{1}{4},

(17)

where the communtation relations
\left[Q_{l},P_{l}\right]=2i
are used.
Thus the EPR paradox—or steering occurs when the RI is violated.

In a similar way, if Bob wants to infer Alice’s measurement results, then the
RI is

V_{A|B}\equiv\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{1}\right|Q_{2}\right)\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.P_{1}\right|P_{2}\right)\geqslant\frac{1}{4},

(18)

with

\displaystyle\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{1}\right|Q_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[V\left(Q_{1}\right)-\frac{\left(E_{Q_{1}Q_{2}}\right)^{2}}{V\left(Q_{2}\right)}\right],

(19)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.P_{1}\right|P_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[V\left(P_{1}\right)-\frac{\left(E_{P_{1}P_{2}}\right)^{2}}{V\left(P_{2}\right)}\right].

(20)

From Eqs. (
15
)-(
20
), it is clear that when the CF is known, then

V_{B|A}
and
V_{A|B}
can be directly calculated using Eqs. (
11
)-(
13
). In addition, for Guassian states which is fully described by
its covariance matrix, these elements involved in the inferred variance can be
directly extracted from the covariance matrix.

II.3
RI violation of two-mode squeezed vacuum state

Next, let us consider a two-mode squeezed vacuum (TMSV) state
\left|TMSV\right\rangle
, which can be generated by a nondegenerate optical
parameter process, i.e.,

\left|TMSV\right\rangle=S\left(r\right)\left|00\right\rangle,

(21)

where
S\left(r\right)=\exp\{r(a_{1}^{{\dagger}}a_{2}^{{\dagger}}-a_{1}a_{2})\}
is
the two-mode squeezing operator. The corresponding CF can be calculated as

\displaystyle\chi_{TMSV}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}(q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2}+p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})\cosh 2r\right\}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left\{(q_{1}q_{2}-p_{1}p_{2})\sinh 2r\right\}.

(22)

Using Eqs. (
11
)-(
13
), it is ready to have

\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)=\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)=\frac{1}{2\cosh 2r},

(23)

which leads to

V_{B|A}=\frac{1}{4\cosh^{2}2r}<\frac{1}{4}.

(24)

Thus the TMSV shows EPR steering for any nonzero squeezing. Here
we should mention that Reid criterion actually depends on the second-order
moments of observables, which implies that Reid criterion is effective for
Gaussian states because Gaussian states can be fully described by second-order
moments or covariance matrix. However, this case is not true for non-Gaussian
states which also depends on its higher order moments.

II.4
Entropic steering criterion

To more effectively demonstrate the EPR steering especially for non-Gaussian
states, correlations in all orders should be involved for steering criterion.
Based on the entropic uncertainty relation
Białynicki-Birula and
Mycielski 1975
,

H\left(Q\right)+H\left(P\right)\geqslant\ln\left(e\pi\right),

(25)

where
H\left(Y\right)
(
Y=Q,P
representing observables) is the Shannon
entropy for
y\left(=q,p\right)
PDF, i.e.,

H\left(Y\right)=-\int dyP\left(y\right)\ln P\left(y\right),

(26)

Walborn
et al
. have derived an entropic
steering inequality
Walborn et al. 2011
, i.e., for instance, for the position and momentum
distribution,

H_{B|A}\equiv H\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)+H\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)\geqslant\ln\left(e\pi\right),

(27)

where the conditional entropies
H\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)
and
H\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)
are
respectively given by

\displaystyle H\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=H\left(Q_{1},Q_{2}\right)-H\left(Q_{1}\right),

(28)

\displaystyle H\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=H\left(P_{1},P_{2}\right)-H\left(P_{1}\right),

(29)

with

\displaystyle H\left(x_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int dx_{1}P\left(x_{1}\right)\ln P\left(x_{1}\right),

(30)

\displaystyle H\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-\int dx_{1}dx_{2}P\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)\ln P\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right).

(31)

Next, we consider the inferred Shannon entropy
H_{B|A}
for the TMSV shown in
Eq. (
21
) as an example. Using the CF of the TMSV in Eq. (
22
) and
Eqs. (
6
) and (
7
), it is ready to have

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{q}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\cosh 2r}}e^{-\bar{q}_{1}^{2}\operatorname{sech}2r},

(32)

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{p}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\cosh 2r}}e^{-\bar{p}_{1}^{2}\operatorname{sech}2r},

(33)

and

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-(\bar{q}_{1}^{2}+\bar{q}_{2}^{2})\cosh 2r+2\bar{q}_{1}\bar{q}_{2}\sinh 2r},

(34)

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{p}_{1},\bar{p}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}e^{-(\bar{p}_{1}^{2}+\bar{p}_{2}^{2})\cosh 2r-2\bar{p}_{1}\bar{p}_{2}\sinh 2r},

(35)

then we have

\displaystyle H\left(Q_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=H\left(P_{1}\right)=\ln\sqrt{\pi e\cosh 2r}

(36)

\displaystyle H\left(Q_{1},Q_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=H\left(P_{1},P_{2}\right)=\ln e\pi,

(37)

and

H\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)=H\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{e\pi}{\cosh 2r},

(38)

as well as

H\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)+H\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)=\ln\frac{e\pi}{\cosh 2r}<\ln e\pi,

(39)

where we have used the following derived formula

P\left(x\right)=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\pi a}}e^{-\frac{1}{a}x^{2}},H\left(X\right)=\ln\sqrt{\pi ae},

(40)

and

\displaystyle P\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2}}}{\pi}e^{-a(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})+2x_{1}x_{2}b},

(41)

\displaystyle H\left(X_{1},X_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\ln\frac{e\pi}{\sqrt{a^{2}-b^{2}}},a>b.

(42)

Comparing Eq. (
39
) with Eq. (
24
), it is ready to see that Reid
and entropic criteria lead to the same result for the TMSV. Actually, for the
Gaussian states, both the entropic and Heisenberg uncertainty relation is
equivalent, thus the same results can be shared by both of them for
position-momentum measurement
Wolf et al. 2006
. Here we should mention
that for non-Gaussian states, the case is not always true. Generally, the
entropic steering criterion performs better than the Reid one for non-Gaussian
states
Lee et al. 2013
;
Chowdhury et al. 2014
.

III
EPR Steering under laser channel

In this section, we consider the quantum steering of quantum states with
continuous variable, such as the TMSVs, when both modes are going
through laser channel which includes both gain and loss effects. For this
purpose, here we start with the master equation and establish the output-input
relation of the CF.

III.1
Laser channel and the output-input CF relation

For single-mode system, the master equation describing the laser channel is
given by
Da and Fan 2019

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\rho\left(t\right)

\displaystyle=g\left[2a^{{\dagger}}\rho a-aa^{{\dagger}}\rho-\rho aa^{{\dagger}}\right]

\displaystyle+\kappa\left[2a\rho a^{{\dagger}}-a^{{\dagger}}a\rho-\rho a^{{\dagger}}a\right],

(43)

where
g
and
\kappa
are the gain and the loss factors, respectively. In
particular, when
g=0
then Eq. (
43
) reduces to the master equation
describing the amplitude decay channel (photon-loss channel); while for the
case with
g\rightarrow\kappa\bar{n}
and
\kappa\rightarrow\kappa\left(\bar{n}+1\right),
then Eq. (
43
) becomes the master equation
describing the thermal channel
Gardiner and Zoller 2000
.

To establish the output-input relation, here we can employ the entangled state
representation to obtain (see Appendix A )

\chi_{out}\left(\alpha,\alpha^{\ast}\right)=e^{-\frac{A}{2}|\alpha|^{2}}\chi_{in}\left(\alpha e^{-\left(\kappa-g\right)t},\alpha^{\ast}e^{-\left(\kappa-g\right)t}\right),

(44)

where

A=\Omega\left(1-R\right),\Omega=\frac{\kappa+g}{\kappa-g},R=e^{-2\left(\kappa-g\right)t}.

(45)

Eq. (
44
) is just the output-input relation for any single-mode quantum
state through the laser channel. When both modes of two-mode quantum states
independently go through the laser channel, from Eq. (
44
) it is ready
to have

\displaystyle\chi_{out}\left(\alpha,\alpha^{\ast};\beta,\beta^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{A}{2}(|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2})}\chi_{in}\left(\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\alpha}^{\ast};\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\beta}^{\ast}\right),

(46)

\displaystyle\left.\left(\tilde{\alpha}=\alpha e^{-\left(\kappa-g\right)t},\tilde{\beta}=\beta e^{-\left(\kappa-g\right)t}\right)\right.,

where for simplicity, the symmetrical laser channels are assumed, which are
characteristics of same gain and loss factors for two-mode.

III.2
TMSV under two-mode laser channel

Here, we first consider the effects of symmetrical laser channels on the
steerability of the decohered TMSV. Combining Eqs. (
22
) and
(
46
), the output CF of the TMSV under the laser channel can be readily
gotten

\displaystyle\chi_{out}\left(q_{1},p_{1};q_{2},p_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\frac{B}{2}(q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2}+p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})\right\}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left\{C(q_{1}q_{2}-p_{1}p_{2})\right\}.

(47)

where we have taken
\alpha=q_{1}+ip_{1}
,
\beta=q_{2}+ip_{2},
and

B=A+R\cosh 2r,C=R\sinh 2r.

(48)

Thus, using Eqs. (
6
), (
7
) and (
47
), the joint position
and its marginal PDFs can be given by

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{q}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi B}}\exp\left\{-\frac{\bar{q}_{1}^{2}}{B}\right\},

(49)

\displaystyle P\left(\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{B^{2}-C^{2}}}e^{-\frac{B\left(\bar{q}_{1}^{2}+\bar{q}_{2}^{2}\right)-2C\bar{q}_{1}\bar{q}_{2}}{B^{2}-C^{2}}}.

(50)

The joint momentum and its marginal PDFs have the same functional form as Eqs.
(
50
) and (
49
), respectively, just making a replacement with

\bar{q}_{1},\bar{q}_{2}
by
\bar{p}_{1},-\bar{p}_{2}.
From Eqs. (
50
)
and (
49
), it is ready to have

H\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)=H\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\pi e(B^{2}-C^{2})}{B},

(51)

which leads to

H\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)+H\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)=\ln\frac{\pi e(B^{2}-C^{2})}{B}.

(52)

On the other hand, from Eq. (
47
) and using Eqs. (
11
)-(
13
), the inferred variances are derived as

\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)=\Delta_{\min}^{2}\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)=\frac{B^{2}-C^{2}}{2B},

(53)

and consequently the steerable condition is given by

\Delta_{\min}\left(\left.Q_{2}\right|Q_{1}\right)\Delta_{\min}\left(\left.P_{2}\right|P_{1}\right)=\frac{B^{2}-C^{2}}{2B}.

(54)

Comparing Reid and entropic steering conditions by Eqs. (
52
) and
(
54
), the same steerable condition is shared, as expected, i.e.,

B^{2}-C^{2}<B.

(55)

Substituting Eqs. (
45
) and (
48
) into Eq. (
55
) and
noticing
A=\Omega(1-R)>0
, we can rewritte the steerable condition as

aA^{2}+bA-c<0,

(56)

where we have set

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega^{2}}\left(\Omega^{2}+1-2\Omega\cosh 2r\right),

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Omega}\left(2\Omega\cosh 2r+\cosh 2r-2-\Omega\right),

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\cosh 2r-1=2\sinh^{2}r>0.

After a straight discussion, it shown that the steerable condition of two-way is

t<t_{c}=\frac{1}{2\left(\kappa-g\right)}\ln\left\{1+\frac{b-\sqrt{b^{2}+4ac}}{2\Omega a}\right\}^{-1}.

(57)

Here we obtained
a general expression (
57
) that applies to linear amplifiers as well.
In generally, the upper bound of evolution time
t_{c}
not only depends on
the gain and loss factors, but also on the squeezing parameter. The
photon-loss, photon-gain and thermal noise channels can be seen as three
special case. In particular, for the photon-loss channel,
g=0
, then the
steerable condition is given by

\kappa t<\frac{1}{2}\ln 2.

(58)

It is interesting to notice that this steerable condition is independent of
the TMSV only with
r>0.
From Eq. (
58
), the decohered TMSV is
steerable provided the photon-loss is less than 50%, i.e.,
e^{-2\kappa t}>1/2
. While for the thermal channel,
g\rightarrow\kappa\bar{n}
and

\kappa\rightarrow\kappa\left(\bar{n}+1\right),
thus when
0<\bar{n}<\left(e^{2r}-1\right)/2,
then the steerable condition is

\kappa t<\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{2\left|\alpha\right|}{\beta+\sqrt{\beta^{2}+4\left|\alpha\right|\delta}},

(59)

where
\alpha=(N+1)^{2}-4N\cosh^{2}r
,

(

At the end of this section, we consider the case with

gt<\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{3+\sqrt{1+8\tanh^{2}r}}{4}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{3}{2}.

(60)

From Eq. (

exists but no loss,
the steerable condition depends on the squeezing parameter
r
, which is
different from the case for photon-loss in Eq. (

to notice that, however, for any squeezing parameter, the steerable condition

. This indicates that the gain will introduce
additional noise to the TMSV such that the steerable condition is destroyed.
In addition, comparing Eqs. (

) with (

), it is shown that the
gain process will provide a shorter steerable time if taking

the
limit difference between both is
\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{4}{3}

III.3
One way steering

In fact, the quantum steering is characteristized by asymmetry, i.e., system A
can steer system B, but the case is not true vice verse. In order to clearly
show the asymmetry of steering, we examine the effect of gain and loss on the
steering parameters with respect to system A and system B when the TMSV is
under one-mode laser channel.
Let us assume that system B (mode
b

this case, according to Eq. (
46

\chi_{out}\left(\alpha,\alpha^{\ast};\beta,\beta^{\ast}\right)=e^{-\frac{A}{2}|\beta|^{2}}\chi_{in}\left(\alpha,\alpha^{\ast};\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\beta}^{\ast}\right),

(61)

and the output CF of the TMSV under the one-side laser channel is

\displaystyle\chi_{TMSV}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{-\frac{\cosh 2r}{2}(q_{1}^{2}+p_{1}^{2})\right\}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left\{-\frac{B}{2}(q_{2}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})\right\}

\displaystyle\times\exp\left\{\frac{C}{\sqrt{R}}(q_{1}q_{2}-p_{1}p_{2})\right\}.

(62)

From Eq. (

\Sigma

i.e.,

\Sigma=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccc}\cosh 2r&0&\frac{C}{\sqrt{R}}&0\\

which becomes the covariance matrix of the TMSV under ideal condition of

t=0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(A+\frac{R}{\cosh 2r}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle V_{A|B}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{R+A\cosh 2r}{A+R\cosh 2r}\right)^{2}.

In particular, when

For the steering of

(
A

B

R>0

A>0

t_{A\rightarrow B}<\frac{1}{2\left(\kappa-g\right)}\ln\frac{\kappa\sinh^{2}r+g\cosh^{2}r}{g\cosh 2r},

Gerenally, the upbound time for the steering of
B

(gain) factor and squeeing parameter. In particular, there are three special

\displaystyle\kappa t_{A\rightarrow B}

\displaystyle<\infty,\text{
\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ loss,}

(67)

\displaystyle gt_{A\rightarrow B}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(2-\operatorname{sech}^{2}r\right)\text{$<$}\frac{1}{2}\ln 2,\text{ \ gain,}

(68)

\displaystyle\kappa t_{A\rightarrow B}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{N-\operatorname{sech}2r}{2\bar{n}},\text{thermal noise,}

(69)

where

)-(

\bar{n}

In a similar way, for the steering of
A
(

A

t_{B\rightarrow A}<\frac{1}{2\left(\kappa-g\right)}\ln\frac{2\kappa}{\kappa+g},\left(r>0\right),

(70)

and

\displaystyle\kappa t_{B\rightarrow A}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\ln 2,\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ loss,}

(71)

\displaystyle gt_{B\rightarrow A}

\displaystyle<\infty,\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ gain,}

(72)

\displaystyle\kappa t_{B\rightarrow A}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{2\bar{n}+2}{2\bar{n}+1},\text{thermal noise.}

(73)

By comparing Eq. (

When

\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(2-\operatorname{sech}^{2}r\right)

\frac{1}{2}\ln 2

In this sense, the gain channel seems beneficial for the steering of
A,

while the loss one is for the steering of

The regions of

\left\langle n\right\rangle

Next, we compare the cases of both one-side and two-side decoherence. (i) For

depending on

Qualification of quantum steering in laser channel

In these discussions above, we have employed two kinds of classical steering
criteria: Reid and entropy criteria. As discussed above, for Gaussian states,
these two criteria are equivalent, which are qualitative steering criteria. In
order to further quantifying the steerability, some measurement methods are
proposed
Skrzypczyk et al. 2014
;
Kogias et al. 2015
;
Jevtic et al. 2015

In particular, for two-mode Gaussian states, whose covariance matrix is

\Sigma_{AB}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}\mathcal{A}&\mathcal{C}\\

\displaystyle=\max\left\{0,\ln\frac{A+R\cosh 2r}{R+A\cosh 2r}\right\}.

V
Comparing with the decoherence of quantum entanglement

Quantum steering is a kind of quantum correlation, which is closely related to
quantum entanglement and Bell nonlocality. For Gaussian states, the relation
between quantum steering and entanglement has been discussed in Refs.
He et al. 2015
. In this section, we further make a
comparison about the decoherence between quantum steering and entanglement in
laser channel.

For two-mode Gaussian states, the degree of entanglement can be calculated by
the logarithmic negativity ,

E_{N}=\max[0,-\ln\nu_{s}],

(86)

where
\nu_{s}
is the smallest symplectic eigenvalue of partially transposed
state. Here the symplectic eigenvalues can be defined as the modulus of the
eigenvalues of a matrix
i\Omega\Sigma
, where
\Sigma
is the covariance
matrix (CM) of partially transposed state and
\Omega=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)\oplus\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
.

Next, we compare the decoherence of entanglement and steering under two-side
and one-side channels. First, we consider the two-side case. For this purpose,
from Eq. (
47
) the CM is given by

\Sigma_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccc}B&0&C&0\\
0&B&0&-C\\
C&0&B&0\\
0&-C&0&B\end{array}\right).

(87)

The symplectic eigenvalues can be calculated as
\nu_{1}=B+C,\nu_{2}=B-C,

then
\nu_{s}=\min[\nu_{1},\nu_{2}]=B-C
, such that
E_{N}=\max[0,-\ln(B-C)]
.
Thus the unseperable condition is given by
B-C<1.
On the other hand, under
the condition of two-side channels, from Eq. (
54
) the steerable
condition is given by
B-C<B/(B+C)<1
. Comparing these two conditions, it is
interesting to notice that the inseperable condition must be satisfied when
the steerable condition is satisfied, while the case is not true vice verse.
This implies that the steerable condition is stronger than the unseperable
condition for describing quantum correlation, or the steerable time is shorter
than the unseperable time. In fact, it is ready to have a critical time for
the inseperablity

t<t_{c}=\frac{1}{2\left(\kappa-g\right)}\ln\frac{g+\kappa\tanh r}{g\left(1+\tanh r\right)},

(88)

as expected
Poon and Law 2007
. In particular, for photon-loss, photon-gain and
thermal channels, Eq. (
88
) reduces to, respectively,

\displaystyle\kappa t_{c}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\infty,

(89)

\displaystyle gt_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1+\tanh r\right),

(90)

\displaystyle\kappa t_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(1+\frac{1/\bar{n}\tanh r}{1+\tanh r}\right),

(91)

Eq. (
91
) is also obtained in Ref.
Duan et al. 2000
. These critical
thresholds in Eqs. (
89
)-(
91
) are longer than those for
steerability, as expected.

Now, we further examine the single-side case. In a similar way, the
corresponding degree of entanglement is given by

E_{N}=\max\left\{0,-\ln\left[B_{+}-\left(B_{-}^{2}+\frac{C^{2}}{R}\right)^{1/2}\right]\right\},

(92)

where
B_{\pm}=(B\pm\cosh 2r)/2
. At initial time
t=0
, Eq. (
92
)
becomes
\ln e^{2r}
, as expected. Thus the inseperable condition can be
derived as

t<t_{c}=\frac{1}{2\left(\kappa-g\right)}\ln\frac{\kappa}{g}.

(93)

It is interesting to notice that the inseperable condition is independent of
squeezing parameter
r>0
when
B
-mode passes the laser channel. This case is
similar to the case of
A\rightarrow B
steering but different from that of

B\rightarrow A
steering. In addition, the inseperable threshold decreases as
the increase of gain factor
g
. In particular, for photon-loss, photon-gain
and thermal channels, Eq. (
93
) reduces to
\kappa t_{c}\rightarrow\infty,gt_{c}\rightarrow\infty,
and
\kappa t_{c}=\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\bar{n}+2}{\bar{n}}
, respectively. Compared with Eqs. (
67
)-(
69

Discussions under phase-sensitive enviornment

Actually, our dicsussions can be directly extended to the general noise
channel, i.e., next we consider the evolution of the TMSV in the presence of
phase-sensitive enviornments, which can be described as the following master
equation for single-mode case
Serafini et al. 2004

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho}{dt}

\displaystyle=\kappa\left(\bar{n}+1\right)\left(2a\rho a^{{\dagger}}-a^{{\dagger}}a\rho-\rho a^{{\dagger}}a\right)

\displaystyle+\kappa\bar{n}\left(2a^{{\dagger}}\rho a-aa^{{\dagger}}\rho-\rho aa^{{\dagger}}\right)

\displaystyle-\kappa M\left(2a^{{\dagger}}\rho a^{{\dagger}}-a^{{\dagger}2}\rho-\rho a^{{\dagger}2}\right)

\displaystyle-\kappa M^{\ast}\left(2a\rho a-a^{2}\rho-\rho a^{2}\right),

(94)

where
M
is a complex parameter related to the squeezed enviornments,
satisfying
\left|M\right|^{2}\leqslant\bar{n}\left(\bar{n}+1\right)
for a qualified density operator. In a similar way to Eq.
(
44
), using the entangled state representation, one can obtain the
evolution of the CF
Chen and Fan 2012
(see Appendix B) as

\chi\left(\alpha,t\right)=e^{-\frac{R}{2}(N\left|\alpha\right|^{2}-M\alpha^{\ast 2}-M^{\ast}\alpha^{2})}\chi(\alpha\sqrt{T},0)

(95)

with
N=2\bar{n}+1,R=1-e^{-2\kappa t},T=e^{-2\kappa t}
, and
\chi\left(\alpha,0\right)
is the initial CF. In particular, for the initial Gaussian
state, the CF can be given by
Weedbrook et al. 2012

\chi(\alpha,0)=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}\xi\Omega V_{0}\Omega^{T}\xi^{T}\right\},

(96)

where
\xi=

\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}q&p\end{array}\right)
and
V_{0}
is the covariance matrix of the initial Gaussian state,
as well as
\Omega=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right).
Here for simplicity, we have assumed
\bar{\xi}=0
. In this case,
after substituting Eq. (
96
) into Eq. (
95
), the evolution of the
covariance matrix is shown as

V_{out}=RV_{\infty}+TV_{0},

(97)

where
V_{\infty}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}N+2\operatorname{Re}M&2\operatorname{Im}M\\
2\operatorname{Im}M&N-2\operatorname{Re}M\end{array}\right)
is the covariance matrix of the output state when
t\rightarrow\infty

Lütkenhaus et al. 1998
;
Paris et al. 2003
;
Kim and Imoto 1995
.

Next, we consider the cases that the TSMV pass through single-side and
two-side phase-sensitive channels, reespctively. Using Eq. (
97
), we can
get the covariance matrix of the output state for these two cases above, which
has the following form

V_{j}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}\mathcal{A}_{j}&\mathcal{C}_{j}\\
\mathcal{C}_{j}^{T}&\mathcal{B}_{j}\end{array}\right),

(98)

where, the subscripts
j=1
,
2
represent the cases of single-side and
two-side channels, respectively. The entanglement degree described by Eq.
(
98
) is given by Eq. (
86
), where
2\nu_{s}^{2}=\Delta-\sqrt{\Delta^{2}-4\det V_{j}}
, and
\Delta=\det\mathcal{A}_{j}+\det\mathcal{B}_{j}-2\det\mathcal{C}_{j}
.

Specifically speaking, for single- and two-side decoherence, the CMs are,
respectively, given by

V_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccc}a_{1}&0&d_{1}&0\\
0&a_{1}&0&-d_{1}\\
d_{1}&0&b_{1}+c_{1}&f_{1}\\
0&-d_{1}&f_{1}&b_{1}-c_{1}\end{array}\right),

(99)

with
a_{1}=\cosh 2r,b_{1}=T\cosh 2r+RN,c_{1}=2R\operatorname{Re}M,d_{1}=\sqrt{T}\sinh 2r,f_{1}=2R\operatorname{Im}M
, and

V_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cccc}a_{2}&c_{2}&d_{2}&0\\
c_{2}&b_{2}&0&-d_{2}\\
d_{2}&0&a_{2}&c_{2}\\
0&-d_{2}&c_{2}&b_{2}\end{array}\right),

(100)

with
a_{2}=R\left(N+2\operatorname{Re}M\right)+T\cosh 2r
,
b_{2}=R\left(N-2\operatorname{Re}M\right)+T\cosh 2r
,
c_{2}=2R\operatorname{Im}M
,

d_{2}=T\sinh 2r
. Thus the steerability can be calculated as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}^{A\rightarrow B}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{a_{1}^{2}}{\allowbreak\det V_{1}}\right\},

(101)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}^{B\rightarrow A}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{\allowbreak b_{1}^{2}-c_{1}^{2}-f_{1}^{2}}{\allowbreak\det V_{1}}\right\},

(102)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}^{A\leftrightarrow B}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{0,\frac{1}{2}\ln\frac{a_{2}b_{2}-c_{2}^{2}}{\det V_{2}}\right\}.

(103)

In particular, when
\bar{n}=0
(
M=0

M=0,
both entanglement and

VII
Conclusions

In summary, we have studied the quantum EPR steering for two-mode states with
continuous-variable in laser channel by using the representation of characteristic function. By setting different channel parameters, we can simultaneously analyse the evolution of EPR Steering in loss, gain, and thermal channels. Concretely, it shows that the bound of two-way steering time not only depends on the gain
and loss factors, but also on the squeezing parameter in laser channel, while the squeezing parameter does not influence the bound in the case of loss channel. Furthermore, we found the gain process will introduce additional noise to the two-mode squeezed vacuum states such that the steerable time is reduced when they under one-mode and two-mode laser channel respectively. When
B
system passes only the gain channel, we found that
B
can always steer
A
for any long decoherence time but
A
can only steer
B
within a threshold value, which is opposite with loss channel. In addition, by comparing the steerable and inseparable conditions, it is shows that the steerable condition can be seen as a subset of the inseparable condition. At last, we extended our discussions to the phase-sensitive
environment. Our results are of great significance for the design of effective EPR steering distillation schemes in practical quantum channels.

Appendix A: Deriviation of Eq. (
44
)

Here we derive Eq. (
44
) by using the thermal entangled state
representation method. For this purpose, we intorduce a state vector, denoted
as
\left|I\right\rangle=\exp\left\{a^{{\dagger}}\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\right\}\left|0\tilde{0}\right\rangle=\sum_{m}^{\infty}\left|m,\tilde{m}\right\rangle
, where
m=\tilde{m},

a^{{\dagger}}
and
\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}
are
the creation operators of real mode and fictitious mode, respectively,
satisfying
[\tilde{a},\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}]=1
and
\tilde{a}\left|0\tilde{0}\right\rangle=0
, then the thermal entangled state representation
can be obtained by the displacement for real mode, i.e.,

\displaystyle\left|\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=D_{a}\left(\eta\right)\left|0\tilde{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-\left|\eta\right|^{2}/2+\eta a^{{\dagger}}-\eta^{\ast}\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}+a^{{\dagger}}\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}}\left|0\tilde{0}\right\rangle,

(A1)

where
D_{a}\left(\eta\right)=\exp\left\{\eta a^{{\dagger}}-\eta^{\ast}a\right\}
is the displacement operator for mode
a.
It is not difficult
to get the following eigen-equations:

\displaystyle\left(a-\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\right)\left|\eta\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\eta\left|\eta\right\rangle,\left(a^{{\dagger}}-\tilde{a}\right)\left|\eta\right\rangle=\eta^{\ast}\left|\eta\right\rangle,

(A2)

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta\right|\left(a^{{\dagger}}-\tilde{a}\right)

\displaystyle=\eta^{\ast}\left\langle\eta\right|,\left\langle\eta\right|\left(a-\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\right)=\eta\left\langle\eta\right|,

(A3)

with
\left[\left(a-\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\right),\left(a^{{\dagger}}-\tilde{a}\right)\right]=0
. When
\eta=0,
from Eqs. (A2) and (A3) it is ready
to have

a\left|I\right\rangle=\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\left|I\right\rangle,\tilde{a}\left|I\right\rangle=a^{{\dagger}}\left|I\right\rangle,a^{{\dagger}}a\left|I\right\rangle=\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\tilde{a}\left|I\right\rangle.

(A4)

Next, we bridge the relation between the characteristic function (CF) and

\left\langle\eta\right|
. According to the CF definition of density
operator
\rho
, i.e.,
\chi\left(\lambda\right)=\mathtt{tr}(e^{\lambda a^{{\dagger}}-\lambda^{\ast}a}\rho),
then the CF can be reformed to the following
form

\displaystyle\chi\left(\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n}^{\infty}\left\langle n,\tilde{n}\right|e^{\lambda a^{{\dagger}}-\lambda^{\ast}a}\rho\left|m,\tilde{m}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle I\right|D_{a}\left(\lambda\right)\left|\rho\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\eta_{=-\lambda}\right|\left.\rho\right\rangle,

(A5)

where
\left|\rho\right\rangle\equiv\rho\left|I\right\rangle,

m=\tilde{m},
and
n=\tilde{n}
.

In order to derive Eq. (
44
), operating Eq. (
43
) on
\left|I\right\rangle
and using Eq. (A4), we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left|\rho\left(t\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=[g\left(2a^{{\dagger}}\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}-aa^{{\dagger}}-\tilde{a}\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\right)

\displaystyle+\kappa\left(2a\tilde{a}-a^{{\dagger}}a-\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\tilde{a}\right)]\left|\rho\left(t\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=[\left(\kappa+g\right)\left(\tilde{a}-a^{{\dagger}}\right)\left(a-\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\kappa-g\right)\left(a\tilde{a}-a^{{\dagger}}\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}+1\right)]\left|\rho\left(t\right)\right\rangle.

(A6)

After operating Eq. (A6) from left-side on
\left\langle\eta_{=-\lambda}\right|
, and using Eqs. (A2)-(A3) as well as

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta\right|\tilde{a}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}+\frac{\eta^{\ast}}{2}\right)\left\langle\eta\right|,

(A7)

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta\right|\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta^{\ast}}-\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\left\langle\eta\right|,

(A8)

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta\right|a

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta^{\ast}}+\frac{\eta}{2}\right)\left\langle\eta\right|,

(A9)

\displaystyle\left\langle\eta\right|a^{{\dagger}}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}-\frac{\eta^{\ast}}{2}\right)\left\langle\eta\right|,

(A10)

it is ready to have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\chi\left(\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=[-\left(\kappa-g\right)(\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}+\eta^{\ast}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta^{\ast}})

\displaystyle-\left(\kappa+g\right)\left|\eta\right|^{2}]\left\langle\eta\right.\left|\rho\left(t\right)\right\rangle,

(A11)

whose solution is given by

\chi\left(\lambda,t\right)=e^{-\left(\kappa+g\right)t\left|\eta\right|^{2}-\left(\kappa-g\right)t(\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}+\eta^{\ast}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta^{\ast}})}\left\langle\eta\right.\left|\rho\left(0\right)\right\rangle,

(A12)

where
\eta=-\lambda,
and
\rho\left(0\right)
is the density operator at
initial time.

Noting
[\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}+\eta^{\ast}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta^{\ast}},\eta^{\ast}\eta]=2\eta\eta^{\ast},
and using the
following operator identity

e^{f\left[A+\sigma B\right]}=e^{\sigma B\left(e^{f\tau}-1\right)/\tau}e^{fA},

(A13)

when
\left[A,B\right]=\tau B
is hold, Eq. (A12) can be put into the form

\displaystyle\chi\left(\lambda,t\right)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\kappa+g}{\kappa-g}(1-e^{-2\left(\kappa-g\right)t})\left|\eta\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-\left(\kappa-g\right)t\left(\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}+\eta^{\ast}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta^{\ast}}\right)}\left\langle\eta\right.\left|\rho\left(0\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\kappa+g}{\kappa-g}(1-e^{-2\left(\kappa-g\right)t})\left|\eta\right|^{2}}\chi(\lambda e^{-\left(\kappa-g\right)t},0),

(A14)

where
\left\langle\eta\right.\left|\rho\left(0\right)\right\rangle=\chi\left(\lambda,0\right)
is the initial CF, and we have

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-f\left(\eta\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}+\eta^{\ast}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta^{\ast}}\right)\right\}\left\langle\eta\right|

\displaystyle=e^{f}\left\langle\eta\right|\exp\left\{f\left(a\tilde{a}-a^{{\dagger}}\tilde{a}^{{\dagger}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\eta e^{-f}\right|.

(A15)

Eq. (A14) is just Eq. (
44
), as expected.

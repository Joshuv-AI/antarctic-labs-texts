Riemann surface of the Riemann zeta function
S. Ivashkovich

Abstract
. In this paper we treat the classical Riemann zeta function as a function of three variables: one is the usual complex 𝟣 {\mathsf{1}} -dimensional, customly denoted as s s , another two are complex infinite dimensional, we denote it as 𝖻 = { b n } n = 1 ∞ {\mathsf{b}}=\{b_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty} and 𝗓 = { z n } n = 1 ∞ {\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty} . When 𝖻 = { 1 } n = 1 ∞ {\mathsf{b}}=\{1\}_{n=1}^{\infty} and 𝗓 = { 1 n } n = 1 ∞ {\mathsf{z}}=\{\frac{1}{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty} one gets the usual Riemann zeta function. Our goal in this paper is to study the meromorphic continuation of ζ ⁡ ( 𝖻 , 𝗓 , s ) \zeta({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s) as a function of the triple ( 𝖺 , 𝗓 , s ) ({\mathsf{a}},{\mathsf{z}},s) .

1.
Introduction.

1.1. Euler-Riemann zeta function

For a real
\sigma
let
H_{\sigma}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{s\in{\mathbb{C}}:{\mathsf{Re}}\,s>\sigma\}
be the halph-plane. For a complex variable

s\in H_{1}
the classical Riemann or, better Euler-Riemann zeta function is defined as

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{s}=\zeta\left(\frac{{\mathsf{1}}}{{\mathrm{n}}},s\right).

(1.1)

In the right hand side of (
1.1
) we interpret
\frac{{\mathsf{1}}}{{\mathrm{n}}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{\frac{1}{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

as the distinguished element of the following complex
sequence space

{\mathsf{l}}_{\frac{{\mathsf{1}}}{{\mathrm{n}}}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{{\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:\left\|{{\mathsf{z}}}\right\|_{\frac{{\mathsf{1}}}{{\mathrm{n}}}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}{\mathsf{sup}}\,_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in{\mathbb{N}}\end{subarray}}\left(n|z_{n}|\right)<\infty\}.

(1.2)

We shall also use the notation
{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}
for
\frac{{\mathsf{1}}}{{\mathrm{n}}}
as well as
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}
for

{\mathsf{l}}_{\frac{{\mathsf{1}}}{{\mathrm{n}}}}
. More generally:

Definition 1
.

Let
{\mathrm{r}}=\{r_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of positive reals. The following space of complex sequences

{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\left\{{\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:\left\|{{\mathsf{z}}}\right\|_{{\mathrm{r}}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}{\mathsf{sup}}\,_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in{\mathbb{N}}\end{subarray}}\left|\frac{z_{n}}{r_{n}}\right|<\infty\right\}

(1.3)

we shall call the
{\mathrm{r}}
-sequence space or, simply an
{\mathrm{r}}
-space.

{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
is a Banach space with respect to the norm
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{{\mathrm{r}}}
. In fact it is isometrically
isomorphic to
{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}
. Actually
{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}
appears to be
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{1}}}
according to this definition,
here
{\mathsf{1}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{1\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. By
B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}({\mathsf{z}}^{0},\beta)
denote the open ball of radius
\beta>0
in
the Banach space
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
centered at
{\mathsf{z}}^{0}
. Notice that for
{\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{n}\}\in B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}({\mathrm{n}}^{-1},{\mathsf{1}})
we have
that
{\mathsf{Re}}\,z_{n}>0
and therefore we can fix that
{{\mathsf{Arg}}}z_{n}\in]-\pi/2,\pi/2[
.

For given
{\mathsf{b}}=\{b_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}
,
{\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}({\mathrm{n}}^{-1},{\mathsf{1}})

and
s\in H_{1}
set (with arguments of
z_{n}
taken as above):

\zeta^{ER}({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z_{n}^{s}

(1.4)

and call it still the Euler-Riemann zeta function. It is not difficult to observe, see Lemma
2.4
,
that
\zeta^{ER}
is holomorphic as the function of the triple
({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)
on
{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}\times B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}({\mathrm{n}}^{-1},{\mathsf{1}})\times H_{1}
. Variable
{\mathsf{z}}
we shall call a
vectorial variable
of
\zeta^{ER}
and
{\mathsf{b}}
a (complex)
parameter
.

\zeta^{ER}({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)
will be denoted simply as
\zeta({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)
whenever this will not lead to a confusion. Notice that

\zeta({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)
is certainly
not
well defined on the coordinate cross
{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}=\{{\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

\in{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}:z_{n}=0\text{ for some }n\}
at least for a general

{\mathsf{b}}\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}
. The closure of
{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}
in
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}
is

\bar{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}=\left\{{\mathsf{z}}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}:\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in{\mathbb{N}}\end{subarray}}n|z_{n}|=0\right\},

see Lemma
2.1
. We prove in Lemma
2.5
that
\zeta
can be
continued to a (multivalued) analytic function on
{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}\times\left({\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\setminus\bar{\mathsf{C}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\right)\times H_{1}
, again as a function of the triple
({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)
.

Remark 1
.

One should be a bit carefull when speaking about multivalued analyticity and universal covers in infinite dimensions.
In our case the space
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\setminus\bar{C}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}
admits the universal
cover since it is semi-locally simply connected, see Corollary 14 in
[
Sp
]
.

Denote by
{\mathcal{M}}
the maximal set over

{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}\times\left({\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\setminus\bar{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\right)
which consists of
such
({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}})
that
\zeta({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},\cdot)
can be analytically continued to a meromorphic function on

{\mathbb{C}}
. By the classical theorem of Riemann
({\mathsf{1}},{\mathrm{n}}^{-1})\in{\mathcal{M}}
, here
{\mathsf{1}}

and
{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}
are sequences defined as above. Furthermore denote by
{\mathcal{Z}}
the maximal set over

{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}\times\left({\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\setminus\bar{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\right)\times{\mathbb{C}}
to which

\zeta({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)
can be analytically continued as a meromorphic function,
i.e.,
{\mathcal{Z}}
is the “Riemann surface”
of
\zeta
. Notice that
{\mathcal{Z}}\supset{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.
In this paper we are interested in the following:

Question:
What can be said about the structure of
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}
?

Our first result states that
{\mathcal{M}}
is “quite thick” and, moreover, possesses a certain
Banach analytic structure. Denote by
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}

the sequence space defined by the sequence
{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{e^{-n}\}_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
,
i.e.,

{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:\left\|{{\mathsf{z}}}\right\|_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\Sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in{\mathbb{N}}\end{subarray}}\left(e^{n}|z_{n}|\right)<\infty\}.

(1.5)

Notice that
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}
is obviously densily imbedded into
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}
.
We prove the following:

Theorem 1
.

For every
({\mathsf{b}}^{0},{\mathsf{z}}^{0})\in{\mathcal{M}}
the following holds:

i)

({\mathsf{b}}^{0},{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+B_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}^{\infty}(0,\left\|{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}}\right\|_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}))\subset{\mathcal{M}}
,
or in other words

\{({\mathsf{b}}^{0},{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+B_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}^{\infty}(0,\left\|{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}}\right\|_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}))\}\times{\mathbb{C}}\subset{\mathcal{Z}}
;

ii)
the restriction
\zeta({\mathsf{b}}^{0},\cdot,\cdot)
of
\zeta
to
\{{\mathsf{b}}^{0}\}\times\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+B_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}^{\infty}(0,\left\|{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}}\right\|_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}})\}\times{\mathbb{C}}
can be analytically

continued as a multivalued meromorphic function to

\{{\mathsf{b}}^{0}\}\times\left(\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\}\setminus\bar{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\right)\times{\mathbb{C}}
.

Remark 2
.

1.
First of all let’s make clear that for
{\mathsf{z}}^{0}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}
one has

B^{\infty}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}(0,\left\|{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}}\right\|_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}})=\{{\mathsf{z}}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}:\left\|{{\mathsf{z}}}\right\|_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}<\left\|{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}}\right\|_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\}.

2.
When writing
({\mathsf{b}}^{0},{\mathsf{z}}^{0})\in{\mathcal{M}}
we mean, in particular, that
({\mathsf{b}}^{0},{\mathsf{z}}^{0})\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}\times({\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\setminus\bar{\mathsf{C}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}})
. Therefore due to Lemma
2.3

we have that
\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\}\setminus\bar{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}=\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\}\setminus{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}
and, moreover, in the same lemma we prove that
\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\}\cap{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}
is a countable union of hyperplanes such that every bounded subset of the affine
plane
\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\}
meets only finitely many of them. Therefore an analytic continuation within
its complement makes well sense.

3.
Let
L_{n}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{{\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{e^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}:z_{n}+z_{n}^{0}=0\}

be one of these hyperplanes. Then it is easy to see that the monodromy of
\zeta({\mathsf{b}}^{0},\cdot,s)
around
this hyperplane along a loop
\gamma_{n}
in
z_{n}
-plane starting at point
z^{0}_{n}+z_{n}\not=0
and going in the
positive direction is
b^{0}_{n}(z^{0}_{n}+z_{n})^{s}\left(e^{2\pi is}-1\right)
. Since
\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+{\mathsf{l}}_{e^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\}\cap{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}=\bigcup_{n}L_{n}
the fundamental group of
\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+{\mathsf{l}}_{e^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\}\setminus{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}

is generated by such
\gamma_{n}
-s and is abelian. This shows that
\zeta({\mathsf{b}}^{0},\cdot,\cdot)
lifts to the
universal cover of
\{{\mathsf{b}}^{0}\}\times\left(\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+{\mathsf{l}}_{e^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\}\setminus{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\right)\times{\mathbb{C}}

and separates points in the fiber (for a non-integer
s
).

4.
Since
\{{\mathsf{z}}^{0}+{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{e}}^{-{\mathrm{n}}}}\}\times{\mathbb{C}}
is a dense affine subspace
of
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
this statement means that the Riemann surface of the function
\zeta
contains a huge
quantity of domains spread over parallel dense affine subspaces of the total space
{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}\times{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
. Indeed, apart from
({\mathsf{b}}^{0},{\mathsf{z}}^{0})=({\mathsf{1}},{\mathrm{n}}^{-1})
we know quite
a number of pairs
({\mathsf{b}}^{0},{\mathsf{z}}^{0})\in{\mathcal{M}}
. If, for example,
\alpha=\sqrt{D}
is a quadratic irrationality
then
(\{n\alpha\}_{n=1}^{\infty},{\mathrm{n}}^{-1})\in{\mathcal{M}}
due to results of Hecke and Hardy-Littlewood,
see
[
He
,
HL
]
. More examples can be found in
[
Pa
]
,
[
KL
]
and many other places.

5.
Notice as well that the result of this theorem can be reformulated in terms
of the general Dirichlet series, see subsection
2.5. Dirichlet series
.

6.
It should be noticed that the analytic continuation in this theorem preserves
the poles of
\zeta({\mathsf{b}}^{0},{\mathsf{z}}^{0},s)
.

1.2. Zeta function in the form of Lerch-Lipschitz

In order to produce new pairs
({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}})\in{\mathcal{M}}
let us make the
following substitution to
\zeta
:

\begin{cases}b_{n}=e^{2\pi ina_{n}}\cr z_{n}=\frac{1}{n+\xi_{n}}.\end{cases}

(1.6)

We obtain the function
\zeta
in the form of Lerch-Lipschitz

\zeta^{LL}({\mathsf{a}},{\mathsf{z}},s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi ina_{n}}}{(n+z_{n})^{s}}.

(1.7)

Variable
\xi
we immeediately renamed as
{\mathsf{z}}
, but parameter
{\mathsf{a}}
will distinguish the Lerch-Lipschits form
of the function zeta from that of Euler-Riemann and
\zeta^{LL}
will be renamed back to
\zeta
. This function
is certainly well defined and holomorphic for
{\mathsf{a}}=\{a_{n}\}\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}_{{\mathsf{Im}}\,^{+}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{{\mathsf{a}}\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}:{\mathsf{Im}}\,a_{n}>0\}
,
{\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{n}\}\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}_{{\mathsf{Re}}\,^{+}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{{\mathsf{z}}\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}:{\mathsf{Re}}\,z_{n}>0\}
and
s\in H_{1}
,
see Corollary
3.1
. Consider the following “
k
-dimensional cut“
\zeta_{k}
of function
\zeta
:

\zeta_{k}({\mathsf{a}},{\mathsf{z}},s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi ina_{[n-1]_{k}+1}}}{(n+z_{[n-1]_{k}+1})^{s}}=\frac{e^{2\pi ia_{1}}}{(1+z_{1})^{s}}+...+\frac{e^{2\pi ika_{k}}}{(k+z_{k})^{s}}+\frac{e^{2\pi i(k+1)a_{1}}}{(k+1+z_{1})^{s}}+...

(1.8)

Here
{\mathsf{a}}=(a_{1},...,a_{k})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathsf{Im}}\,^{+}}
,
{\mathsf{z}}=(z_{1},...,z_{k})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathsf{Re}}\,^{+}}
and
[m]_{k}
is the remainder of
the division of
m
by
k
. I.e.,
\zeta_{k}
is, in fact,
\zeta
itself but restricted to the finite dimensional
subspace of
”
k
-periodic sequences“

\left\{\left(a_{[n-1]_{k}+1},z_{[n-1]_{k}+1}\right)\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Our second result is the following:

Theorem 2
.

Function
\zeta_{k}
admits an analytic continuation to a single-valued meromorphic function
\tilde{\zeta}_{k}
on

\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}}^{k,ab}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[1/k]}\times\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}}^{k,ab}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}}\times{\mathbb{C}}.

(1.9)

Here we denote by
{\mathbb{Z}}[1/k]
the subring of
{\mathbb{Q}}
which consists
from rationals of the form
\{l/k:l\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
for a fixed
k
.
\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}}^{k,ab}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[1/k]}
stands for
the abelian cover of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[1/k]}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\left({\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}\left[1/k\right]\right)^{k}
.

\widetilde{{\mathbb{C}}}^{k,ab}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}}
is the abelian cover of
{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}({\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}_{1})\times...\times({\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}_{k})
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}={\mathbb{Z}}_{<0}
is the set of negative integers and
{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}_{j}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}:[z]_{k}=-j\}
.
The statement of the theorem means that the monodromy of
\zeta_{k}
along any loop generating the commutator
of
\pi_{1}({\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[1/k]}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}})
is trivial and that
\tilde{\mathcal{Z}}_{k}
is the genuine Riemann surface
of
\zeta_{k}
. Moreover, this theorem shows that for any
k
the set
{\mathcal{M}}
is infinitely sheeted over the space
of
k
-periodic sequences. This provides us a huge quantity of new pais
({\mathsf{a}},{\mathsf{z}})\in{\mathcal{M}}
.

Remark 3
.

Periods of

-s and
z
-s in Theorem

and

2.1. Vectorial topology on sequence spaces

Throughout this paper we consider various complex vector spaces of infinite sequences of complex numbers

{\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{k}\}
, they are customly called the
sequence spaces
. If, for some reasons, we shall need
to consider real sequences in the complex sequence space
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
then the corresponding real vector space
will be denoted as
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}},{\mathbb{R}}}
. The standard basis of
any
sequence space is
e_{1}=(1,0,...,0,...),...,e_{k}=(0,...,0,k,0,...)
etc. The ball of radius
\beta
centered at
{\mathsf{z}}
in
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
will be denoted as

B_{{\mathrm{r}}}^{\infty}({\mathsf{z}},\beta)
. The corresponding topology will be called the standard one or, an
{\mathrm{r}}
-topology.

A
polyradius
is a sequence
{\mathrm{\rho}}=\{\rho_{k}\}
such that all
\rho_{k}>0
. If
{\mathrm{\rho}}=\{\rho_{k}\}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{r}

we call it an
{\mathrm{r}}
-
polyradius
. Fix an polyradius
{\mathrm{\rho}}=\{\rho_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
. A polydisk of polyradius

{\mathrm{\rho}}
is the set
\Delta^{\infty}(z^{0},{\mathrm{\rho}}):=\{{\mathsf{z}}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}:|z_{k}-z_{k}^{0}|<\rho_{k}\text{ for all }k\}
. It is not
an open subset of
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
in general. The “closed” polydisk is
\bar{\Delta}^{\infty}(z^{0},{\mathrm{\rho}}):=\{z\in{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}:|z_{k}-z_{k}^{0}|\leqslant\rho_{k}\}
and it is a closed subset of
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
. For a real positive
\beta
and a polyradius

\rho
we set
\beta\rho=\{\beta\rho_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. Note that for the special polyradius
{\mathrm{r}}

we have that
\Delta^{\infty}(0,\beta{\mathrm{r}})\subset\bar{B}_{{\mathrm{r}}}^{\infty}(0,\beta)\subset\Delta^{\infty}(0,(\beta+\varepsilon){\mathrm{r}})
for any
\varepsilon>0
. We also denote
\Delta^{\infty}\left(0,\beta\frac{{\mathsf{1}}}{{\mathrm{n}}}\right)

as
\Delta^{\infty}\left(0,\frac{\beta}{{\mathrm{n}}}\right)
. We call the polydisk
\Delta^{\infty}(0,\beta{\mathrm{r}})

congruent
to
\Delta^{\infty}(0,{\mathrm{r}})
, the same for
\Delta^{\infty}(z^{0},\beta{\mathrm{r}})=z^{0}+\Delta^{\infty}(0,\beta{\mathrm{r}})
with
\Delta^{\infty}(z^{0},{\mathrm{r}})=z^{0}+\Delta^{\infty}(0,{\mathrm{r}})
.

By
{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}
or by
\frac{{\mathsf{1}}}{{\mathrm{n}}}
we denoted the sequence/polyradius

\{\frac{1}{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and by
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}
or by
{\mathsf{l}}_{\frac{{\mathsf{1}}}{{\mathrm{n}}}}
the corresponding
sequence space. We set
{\mathsf{1}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{1\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, and therefore
{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}
( =
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathsf{1}}}
) is
the sequence space of bounded complex sequences with the norm
\left\|{z}\right\|_{\infty}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}{\mathsf{sup}}\,_{n}|z_{n}|
. We shall also
consider polyradiuses
{\mathrm{r}}^{\eta}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{r_{n}^{\eta}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\beta{\mathrm{r}}^{\eta}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{\beta r_{n}^{\eta}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
for
\beta,\eta>0
. Notice that these polyradiuses are not in
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}

in general.

Now let us turn to the coordinate cross
{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{\{z_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}:z_{n}=0\text{ for some }n\}
in
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The closure of the cross
{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
in
{\mathrm{r}}
-topology is equal to

\bar{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}=\left\{{\mathsf{z}}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}:\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in{\mathbb{N}}\end{subarray}}\frac{|z_{n}|}{r_{n}}=0\right\},

(2.1)

and the open set
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}\setminus\bar{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
is pathwise
connected.

Proof.
Let
\bar{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
be the set defined by (
2.1
). Take some
{\mathsf{z}}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}\setminus\bar{\mathsf{C}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}^{\infty}
,
i.e.,
such that
\varepsilon\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in{\mathbb{N}}\end{subarray}}\frac{|z_{n}|}{r_{n}}>0
. The ball

Lemma 2.2
.

Every continuous path in

Proof.
First let us make a remark about paths in the complement of the cross. Notice that for a point

in

provided

Which shows that

for all

. This proves the assertion of the lemma.

\square

can be supposed to vary in

only.

2.3. Taylor expansion of function zeta

For
({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}\times B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}({\mathrm{n}}^{-1},{\mathsf{1}})\times H_{1}

we have

\zeta({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z_{n}^{s},

and therefore we can easily compute all partial derivatives of
\zeta
with respect to
{\mathsf{z}}
to get

\frac{\partial^{m}\zeta}{\partial z_{n}^{m}}({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)=s(s-1)...(s-m+1)b_{n}z_{n}^{s-m}\quad\text{ for all }n,m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Corresponding
m
-homogeneous polynomial in the Taylor expansion of
\zeta
at

is

P_{m}({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},{\mathrm{w}},s)=\frac{s(s-1)...(s-m+1)}{m!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z_{n}^{s-m}w_{n}^{m}.

(2.21)

This gives the Taylor expansion of zeta function at
({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}\times B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{n}}^{-1}}({\mathrm{n}}^{-1},{\mathsf{1}})\times H_{1}
with respect to the variable
{\mathsf{z}}

\zeta({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}}+{\mathrm{w}},s)-\zeta({\mathsf{b}},{\mathsf{z}},s)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{s(s-1)...(s-m+1)}{m!}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}z_{n}^{s-m}w_{n}^{m}.

(2.22)

It occurs that the right hand side of (

) makes sense and converge for

with the silent assumption that arguments of

are chosen to be bounded,
i.e.,

\Pi\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}{\mathsf{sup}}\,\{|{{\mathsf{Arg}}}z_{n}|\}<\infty.

(2.23)

For example
{\mathsf{z}}
is an endpoint of a path with bounded argument.

3.1. Zeta function in the form of Lerch-Lipschitz

(3.1)

One immediately sees that this series converge absolutely for
({\mathsf{a}},\xi)\in\bar{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}_{{\mathsf{Im}}\,^{+}}\times\bar{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}_{{\mathsf{Re}}\,^{+}}

\zeta^{LL}

|z_{n}|\leqslant\frac{1}{n+{\mathsf{Re}}\,\xi_{n}}\leqslant\frac{1}{n},

{\mathsf{z}}=\{z_{n}\}\in{\mathsf{l}}_{\frac{1}{{\mathrm{n}}}}

\zeta^{LL}
is obtained
as a composition of
\zeta
with mapping (
1.6
) on the whole domain in question.
Notice that the “cross”

{\mathsf{C}}^{\infty}_{-}\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}\{{\mathsf{z}}\in{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}:z_{n}=-n\text{ for some }n\}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\{z_{n}=-n\},

(3.2)

is a closed subset of
{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}
.
Set

(3.4)

i.e.,
to the fact that (

{\mathsf{z}}
at points of
{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}

From now on we rename variable

in variable

Theorem 3.1
.

i)
Let
{\mathsf{a}}^{0}=\{a^{0}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\bar{\mathsf{l}}^{\infty}_{{\mathsf{Im}}\,^{+}}

function

to

Then function

continued to

Proof.
Take any
{\mathsf{z}}^{1}

.
Notice that

on

3.2. Lerch-Lipschitz zeta function of finitely many variables

For
{\mathsf{a}}=(a_{1},...,a_{k})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathsf{Im}}\,^{+}}
and
{\mathsf{z}}=(z_{1},...,z_{k})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathsf{Re}}\,^{+}}
we considered in
the Introduction the following ”cut“ of the Lerch-Lipschitz zeta function
\zeta^{LL}
by
functions of finitely many variables

\zeta_{k}({\mathsf{a}},{\mathsf{z}},s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{e^{2\pi ina_{[n-1]_{k}+1}}}{(n+z_{[n-1]_{k}+1})^{s}}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{e^{2\pi i(pk+j)a_{j}}}{(pk+j+z_{j})^{s}}.

(3.7)

In more details
\zeta_{k}({\mathsf{a}},{\mathsf{z}},s)
is the sum of blocks indexed by
p=0,1,...
. Each
of these blocks depends on
k
variables
z_{1},...,z_{k}
and
k
parameters

as follows

\zeta_{k}(a,z,s)=\underbrace{\frac{e^{2\pi ia_{1}}}{(1+z_{1})^{s}}+...+\frac{e^{2\pi ika_{k}}}{(k+z_{k})^{s}}}_{k\text{ terms }}+...+\underbrace{\frac{e^{2\pi i(pk+1)a_{1}}}{(pk+1+z_{1})^{s}}+...+\frac{e^{2\pi i(pk+k)a_{k}}}{(pk+k+z_{k})^{s}}}_{k\text{ terms }}+...

(3.8)

When

functions
\zeta_{k}(\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k},\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{k},s)
in some sense approximate
the function
\zeta({\mathsf{a}},{\mathsf{z}},s)=\zeta(\{a_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},s)
. From (
3.8
)
it is well seen that
\zeta_{k}
is well defined for
{\mathsf{z}}=(z_{1},...,z_{k})
with
z_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{z\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}:[z]_{k}=-j\}={\mathbb{C}}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}_{j}
and
s\in H_{1}
. It is clear as well that
\zeta_{k}
is continuous in
parameter
{\mathsf{a}}
up to
{\mathsf{Im}}\,a_{j}=0
.

Function
\zeta_{k}
can be obtained from the Euler-Riemann form of
\zeta
by the substitution

\begin{cases}b_{n}=e^{2\pi ina_{[n-1]_{k}+1}},\cr z_{n}=\frac{1}{n+z_{[n-1]_{k}+1}},\end{cases}

(3.9)

where
{\mathsf{Im}}\,a_{j}>0
and
{\mathsf{Re}}\,z_{j}>0
for
j=1,...,k
, and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Therefore it follows also from Corollary
3.1
, which will be proved later, that
\zeta_{k}
admits a multivalued analytic continuation to

{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathsf{Im}}\,^{+}}\times{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}}\times H_{1}
continuous in parameter
{\mathsf{a}}
up to
\bar{\mathbb{C}}^{k}_{{\mathsf{Im}}\,^{+}}
. We shall
not stop on this here.

.

Proof.
The proof follows the classical line of arguments, which are due to Riemann, see
[
Rm
]

-psh itself. Now remark that for

\equiv-\infty
.

Proposition 0.4
.

the following unequality

Definition 0.8
.

Call a subset

of an

{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
-thick (plurithick) if for any sequence

-psh functions on

\mathop{{\mathsf{lim\,sup}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\to\infty\end{subarray}}u_{k}|_{E}\equiv-\infty\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mathop{{\mathsf{lim\,sup}}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\to\infty\end{subarray}}u_{k}\equiv-\infty.

(0.50)

Example 0.4
.

(a) For every

and every

-thick in

.
By the pathwise

,

Take any

Lemma 0.4
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic function on
B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(z^{0},\beta)\times\Delta
. Suppose that for every
z
in
some
{\mathcal{P}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
-thick subset
E
of
B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(z^{0},\beta)
the restriction
f_{z}(s)\mathrel{\raise 0.3pt\hbox{\rm:}{=}}f(z,s)
extends
holomorphically to
{\mathbb{C}}
. Then
f
extends to a holomorphic function on
B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(z^{0},\beta)\times{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.
Write the Taylor expansion of
f
in
B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(z^{0},1)\times\Delta
in the form

f(z^{0}+z,s)=f(z^{0},0)+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}a_{k}(z)s^{k},

(0.51)

where
a_{k}
are
{\mathrm{r}}
-holomorphic in
B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(0,1)
. To get this from (
0.36
) represent vectors
w

from
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
as
w=z+se_{0}
, where
e_{0}
spans the subspace
\{0\}\times{\mathbb{C}}
of
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}\times{\mathbb{C}}
.
With
w^{0}=z^{0}+0e_{0}=z^{0}
write (
0.36
) in coordinates
w
as

f(w^{0}+w)-f(w^{0})=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}P_{m}(w)=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\hat{P}_{m}(\underbrace{w,...,w}_{m-\text{times}})=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\hat{P}_{m}(\underbrace{z+se_{0},...,z+se_{0}}_{m-\text{times}})=

=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{m}C_{m}^{k}\hat{P}_{m}(\underbrace{se_{0},...,se_{0}}_{k-\text{times}},\underbrace{z,...,z}_{(m-k)-\text{times}})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}C_{m}^{k}\hat{P}_{m}(\underbrace{e_{0},...,e_{0}}_{k-\text{times}},\underbrace{z,...,z}_{(m-k)-\text{times}})\right)s^{k},

(0.52)

which gives us (
0.51
). To estimate the coefficients

a_{k}(z)=\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}C_{m}^{k}\hat{P}_{m}(\underbrace{e_{0},...,e_{0}}_{k-\text{times}},\underbrace{z,...,z}_{(m-k)-\text{times}})

(0.53)

and justify the change of summution in (
0.52
) remark that from (
0.42
) we get that

|\hat{P}_{m}(w,...,w)|\leqslant\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\right)^{m}M_{\beta_{2}}(f)\quad\text{ on }\quad\Delta^{\infty}(0,\beta_{1}{\mathrm{r}})\times\Delta(\beta_{1})

for every choice of
0<\beta_{1}<\beta_{2}<1
. This implies the uniform on
\Delta^{\infty}(0,\beta_{1}{\mathrm{r}})
estimate

|a_{k}(z)|\leqslant\frac{1}{\beta_{1}^{k}}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\right)^{m}M_{\beta_{2}}=\beta_{2}^{k}\sum_{m=k}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}\right)^{m-k}M_{\beta_{2}},

(0.54)

which implies the normal convergence of the series (
0.52
). Therefore

\ln\root{\mathsf{K}} \of{|a_{k}(z)|}\leqslant 1/k\ln\left(\frac{\beta_{2}}{(1-\beta_{2})(\beta_{2}-\beta_{1})}M_{\beta_{2}}\right)\quad\text{ on }\quad\Delta^{\infty}(0,\beta_{1}{\mathrm{r}}).

(0.55)

Functions
\ln\root{\mathsf{K}} \of{|a_{k}(z)|}
are
{\mathrm{r}}
-psh on
B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(0,1)
and tend to
-\infty
on
E
.
Due to the assumption of our lemma they tend to
-\infty
also on
\Delta^{\infty}(0,\beta_{1}{\mathrm{r}})
. We conclude that series
(
0.51
)
converge on
{\mathbb{C}}
for every
z\in B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(0,1)
. This gives us an extension of
f
to
z\in B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(z_{0},1)\times{\mathbb{C}}
.

To see that this extension (denoted as
\tilde{f}
) is Gâteaux differentiable take any two vectors
w^{0}=z^{0}+s^{0}e_{0},w=z+se_{0}\in B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(0,1)\times{\mathbb{C}}
, take
0<\beta<1
such that
z^{0},z\in B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(0,\beta)
and consider
the two (or one)-dimensional subspace
L
of
{\mathsf{l}}_{{\mathrm{r}}}
spanned by
z^{0}
and
z
. For
t
in some neighborhood of zero
vectors
z^{0}+tz
belong to
L\cap B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(0,1)
. Applying the calssical Hartogs theorem to the restriction of

\tilde{f}
to
\left(L\cap B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(0,1)\right)\times{\mathbb{C}}
we get the holomorphicity of this restriction. In
particular
\tilde{f}(z^{0}+tz)
is holomorphic in
t
. Gâteaux differentiability is proved.

Our extension
\tilde{f}
is Gâteaux differentiable on
B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(z_{0},1)\times{\mathbb{C}}
and holomorphic in

B^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{r}}}(z_{0},1)

\times\Delta
, therefore by Theorem 36.5 from
[
Mu
]

\tilde{f}
is holomorphic everywhere.
Lemma is proved.

\square

A Novel Catalyst Scheme for Stochastic Minimax Optimization
Guanghui Lan, Yan Li

Abstract
This paper presents a proximal-point-based catalyst scheme for simple first-order methods applied to convex minimization and convex-concave minimax problems. In particular, for smooth and (strongly)-convex minimization problems, the proposed catalyst scheme, instantiated with a simple variant of stochastic gradient method, attains the optimal rate of convergence in terms of both deterministic and stochastic errors. For smooth and strongly-convex-strongly-concave minimax problems, the catalyst scheme attains the optimal rate of convergence for deterministic and stochastic errors up to a logarithmic factor. To the best of our knowledge, this reported convergence seems to be attained for the first time by stochastic first-order methods in the literature. We obtain this result by designing and catalyzing a novel variant of stochastic extragradient method for solving smooth and strongly-monotone variational inequality, which may be of independent interest.

Our essential objective in this manuscript is to design a single method that, with proper parameter specifications, obtain optimal iteration complexity in the deterministic setting, and optimal reduction of both deterministic and stochastic errors in the stochastic setting.
Such a development bears the potential of several practical benefits.
For instance, one can reduce the iteration complexity in the stochastic setting by using mini-batches;
improve the communication complexity in the distributed or federated settings;
and take advantage of variance reduction techniques available for smooth and finite-sum problems.

Our main contributions can be summarized into the following aspects.
First, we propose a novel inexact accelerated proximal point framework for both convex optimization and minimax optimization.
The proposed framework is referred to as a catalyst scheme as it is capable of accelerating simple non-optimal methods and subsequently obtaining optimal complexities.
In a nutshell, within the catalytic process, the to-be-catalyzed non-optimal methods are used by the proposed framework as a subroutine for solving the inexact proximal step.
Compared to the inexact accelerated proximal point framework considered in
[
31
]
, the proposed catalyst framework properly modifies the search sequences within the acceleration scheme using the outputs of the inexact proximal update, leading to its optimal performances in both deterministic and stochastic regimes.

Second, we show that for smooth and convex problems, by catalyzing a simple variant of (stochastic) gradient descent method, the proposed framework obtains an
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{L/\epsilon})
iteration complexity in the deterministic setting, and an

\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{L/\epsilon}+\sigma^{2}/\epsilon^{2})
sample complexity in the stochastic setting.
Similarly, for smooth and strongly-convex problems, the proposed framework can catalyze (stochastic) gradient descent and obtain an
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{L/\mu}\log(1/\epsilon))
iteration complexity in the deterministic setting, and

\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{L/\mu}\log(1/\epsilon)+\sigma^{2}/(\mu\epsilon))
sample complexity in the stochastic setting.
Notably, these obtained complexities are optimal for smooth and (strongly)-convex optimization.
In particular, even when restricting to the deterministic setting, the obtained iteration complexity improves over existing catalyst scheme for convex optimization
[
35
]
by removing the additional logarithmic factor.

Third, we apply the proposed catalyst scheme to the minimax problem considered in (
1.1
).
En route, we develop a novel variant of extragradient method, named regularized extragradient (REG), applicable to strongly-monotone VI.
REG method obtains
\mathcal{O}(L/\mu\log(1/\epsilon))
iteration complexity in the deterministic setting and

\mathcal{O}(L/\mu\log(1/\epsilon)+\sigma^{2}/(\mu\epsilon))
sample complexity in the stochastic setting.
Notably, REG simultaneously controls the gap value and the distance to the reference point (determined a posteriori), a property that makes it an ideal candidate for the catalyst scheme compared to existing alternatives that only consider the distance to the solution (e.g.,
[
23
,
20
,
19
]
).
We then further catalyze the proposed REG method and establish that, in the deterministic setting, to obtain an
\epsilon
-optimal solution of (
1.1
), the iteration complexity can be bounded by
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(L/\sqrt{\mu_{d}\epsilon})
for convex-strongly-concave problems,
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(L/\sqrt{\mu_{d}\mu_{p}}\log(1/\epsilon))
for strongly-convex-strongly-concave problems.
In the stochastic setting, we obtain sample complexities that are optimal up to logarithmic factors.
Specifically, for convex-strongly-concave problems, we establish an

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(L/\sqrt{\mu_{d}\epsilon}+\sigma^{2}/\epsilon^{2})

sample complexity.
For strongly-convex-strongly-concave problems, we obtain an

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(L/\sqrt{\mu_{d}\mu_{p}}\log(1/\epsilon)+\sigma^{2}/(\mu\epsilon))

sample complexity.
To the best of our knowledge, the proposed catalyst scheme seems to be the first method that attains optimal complexities (up to logarithmic factors) in both deterministic and stochastic regimes for smooth and strongly-convex-strongly-concave problems.

Finally, it should be noted that the catalyst scheme can also be applied to convex-concave problems where
\mu_{p}=\mu_{d}=0
, with a black-box reduction technique considered in
[
31
]
. In a nutshell, one can perturb (
1.1

We do not explicitly delve into this reduction process in detail to maintain the simplicity of our presentation.

2
Catalyst Scheme for Convex Optimization

In this section, we consider the convex programming given in the form of

f^{*}:=\min_{x\in X}f(x),

(2.1)

where
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a closed convex set and
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
is
a convex function such that

\displaystyle f(\gamma x_{1}+(1-\gamma)x_{2})\leq\gamma f(x_{1})+(1-\gamma)f(x_{2})-\tfrac{\mu\gamma(1-\gamma)}{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2},~\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in X,

(2.2)

for some
\mu\geq 0
. In particular, we say that the problem is strongly convex
if
\mu>0
.
In addition, we assume
f
is smooth with modulus
L

\displaystyle\left|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})-\langle\nabla f(x_{1}),x_{2}-x_{1}\rangle\right|\leq\tfrac{L}{2}\lVert x_{1}-x_{2}\rVert^{2},~~\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in X.

The general framework of the proposed catalyst scheme is stated in Algorithm
1
, which can be viewed as a meta algorithm that wraps around an optimization method
\mathcal{A}
and improves its computational efficiency.
At each iteration, for a given prox-center
\widehat{x}_{k}
and some
\beta_{k}\geq 0

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\left\{\phi_{k}(x):=f(x)+\tfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\lVert x-\widehat{x}_{k}\rVert^{2}\right\}.

(2.3)

We let
(\widetilde{x}_{k},x_{k})=\mathcal{A}(\phi_{k})
denote the output of the to-be-catalyzed method
\mathcal{A}
for solving the above proximal update (
2.3
) while initialized at
\widehat{x}_{k}

As will be clarified later in our analysis, this particular choice of initial point when calling
\mathcal{A}(\phi_{k})
is of particular importance.
In addition, we assume

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\phi_{k}(\widetilde{x}_{k})-\phi_{k}(\widetilde{x})+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{x}-x_{k}\|^{2}]\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{x}-\widehat{x}_{k}\|^{2}+\delta_{k},~\forall\widetilde{x}\in X,

for some
\alpha_{k},\delta_{k},\varepsilon_{k}\geq 0

Here, the expectation is taken with respect to the possible randomness introduced when solving problem (

It should be noted that the implementation of the catalyst scheme only requires

Clearly, when the proximal step is computed exactly, we have

2.1
Catalyst for Stochastic Gradient Descent

Up to now, the proposed catalyst scheme is a conceptual method, as it requires the to-be-catalyzed method
\mathcal{A}
to satisfy certain error condition (
2.4
) when solving the proximal step (
2.3
).
We now introduce a simple first-order method with this desired capability.
To proceed, we assume the access to a stochastic first-order oracle, defined as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

The stochastic first-order oracle
\texttt{SFO}(f;x,\xi)
for a differentiable function
f
outputs the stochastic gradient
\nabla f(x;\xi)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\left[\nabla f(x;\xi)\right]=\nabla f(x),~\mathbb{E}_{\xi}\lVert\nabla f(x;\xi)-\nabla f(x)\rVert_{*}^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}.

Clearly,
\texttt{SFO}(f;x,\xi)
outputs the deterministic gradient almost surely when
\sigma=0
.
Going forward, for any method, we refer to its total number of calls to
SFO
as its sample complexity.
The proposed Procedure
1
, termed
SGD
, can be viewed a simple adaptation of the stochastic gradient descent method for solving

\displaystyle\min_{u\in X}\{\phi(u)\coloneqq f(u)+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\lVert u-\widehat{x}\rVert^{2}\}.

(2.19)

Upon termination, Procedure
1
returns both an ergodic mean of historical iterates and the last iterate.
As will be clarified later, setting proper weights in the ergodic mean suffices to certify condition (
2.4
).
We use
L_{\phi}
and
\mu_{\phi}
denote the smoothness and strong-convexity modulus of
\phi
, respectively.

Procedure 1

SGD
(\phi)
: stochastic gradient descent for
\min_{u\in X}\{\phi(u)\coloneqq f(u)+\tfrac{\beta}{2}\lVert u-\widehat{x}\rVert^{2}\}

Input:
stepsizes
\left\{\eta_{t}\right\}
, total number of steps
T>0
, initial point
u_{0}=\widehat{x}
.

for
t = 1, 2, …, T
do

Form
g_{t-1}=\nabla f(u_{t-1};\xi_{t-1})+\beta(u_{t-1}-\widehat{x})
, where
\nabla f(u_{t-1};\xi_{t-1})=\texttt{SFO}(f;u_{t-1},\xi_{t-1})

u_{t}=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{w\in X}\langle g_{t-1},u\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2\eta_{t}}\lVert u-u_{t-1}\rVert^{2}.

end

for

Compute
\overline{u}_{T}=\tfrac{\Lambda_{T}}{(1-\Lambda_{T})}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{\eta_{t}\mu_{\phi}}{\Lambda_{t}}u_{t}
,

defined in as (
2.20
).

(\overline{u}_{T},u_{T})
.

We next establish the generic convergence properties of Procedure

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\eta_{t}<1/L_{\phi}
and define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}=\begin{cases}(1-\eta_{t}\mu_{\phi})\Lambda_{t-1},~&t\geq 1,\\
1~&t=0.\end{cases}

Then for any

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(\overline{u}_{T})-\phi(u)\right]+\tfrac{\mu_{\phi}}{2(1-\Lambda_{T})}\mathbb{E}\lVert u-u_{T}\rVert^{2}\leq\tfrac{\Lambda_{T}\mu_{\phi}}{2(1-\Lambda_{T})}\lVert u_{0}-u\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{\Lambda_{T}}{2(1-\Lambda_{T})}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{\mu_{\phi}\eta_{t}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\Lambda_{t}(1-L_{\phi}\eta_{t})}.

The proof of Lemma
2.2
is deferred to Appendix
A
.
With Lemma
2.2
in place, we can proceed to establish that Procedure

produces solutions satisfying condition (

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(\overline{u}_{T},u_{T})
be the output of

(\phi)
(Procedure

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\phi(\overline{u}_{T})-\phi(u)+\tfrac{\alpha\mu_{\phi}}{2}\lVert\widetilde{u}-u_{T}\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon\mu_{\phi}}{2}\lVert u-\widehat{x}\rVert^{2}+\delta,~\forall u\in X,

\displaystyle\varepsilon=\tfrac{\Lambda_{T}}{1-\Lambda_{T}},~\alpha=\tfrac{1}{1-\Lambda_{T}}\equiv 1+\varepsilon,~\delta=\tfrac{\Lambda_{T}}{2(1-\Lambda_{T})}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=1}^{T}\tfrac{\mu_{\phi}\eta_{t}^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\Lambda_{t}(1-L_{\phi}\eta_{t})}.

In particular, let

The proof of Proposition

3
Extragradient for Minimax Optimization

In this section we turn our attention back to the minimax problem (
1.1
).
For the purpose of our discussion, we assume within this section that the minimax problem is strongly-convex-strongly-concave.
That is, for some
\mu_{p},\mu_{d}>0
,

\displaystyle F(x_{1},y)-F(x_{2},y)-\langle\nabla_{x}F(x_{2},y),x_{1}-x_{2}\rangle\geq\tfrac{\mu_{p}}{2}\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|^{2},\ \forall x_{1},x_{2}\in X,

(3.1)

\displaystyle F(x,y_{1})-F(x,y_{2})-\langle\nabla_{y}F(x,y_{2}),y_{1}-y_{2}\rangle\leq-\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|^{2},\ \forall y_{1},y_{2}\in Y.

(3.2)

Let us define
\mu\coloneqq\min\left\{\mu_{p},\mu_{d}\right\}>0
.
We next introduce a variant of extragradient method for problem (
1.1
), which we term as regularized extragradient (REG) in the ensuing discussion.
In particular, we will establish in this section that REG obtains optimal complexities in both deterministic and stochastic settings for solving smooth and strongly-monotone variational inequalities.
More importantly, it turns out that REG and its stochastic variant come with a convergence characterization
that simultaneously controls the gap value and the distance to any reference point chosen a posteriori, which
naturally aligns with the error condition required by the catalyst scheme for problem (
1.1
).
This property subsequently makes them ideal candidates as the to-be-catalyzed methods for the catalyst scheme, which we detail in Section
4
and
5
.
Notably, the aforementioned convergence characterization has not been established or reported before for other methods applied to smooth and strongly-monotone VIs (e.g.
[
23
,
20
,
19
]
), which solely focus on the distance to the solution (saddle point).

3.1
Deterministic Regularized Extragradient

The deterministic regularized extragradient (REG) method is presented in Algorithm
3
.
Similar to the SGD variant discussed in Section
2.1
,
REG returns the the latest iterate and an ergodic average of historical iterates.
With a slight overload of notations, for the remainder of the section we will denote the weights in the construction of the ergodic average as
\left\{\Lambda_{t}\right\}
, defined by

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t}=\begin{cases}1,&~t=0;\\
\Lambda_{t-1}(1+\mu\eta_{t-1}),&~t\geq 1.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Clearly, REG differs from the original extragradient method
[
6
]
by the additional strongly-convex term
\lVert z-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert^{2}
in the “extragradient” step (
3.5
).
As will be clarified later, the introduction of this element substantially improves the convergence of the extragradient method to linear rate for strongly-convex-strongly-concave problems.

Algorithm 3

REG
(F)
: regularized extragradient for
\min_{x\in X}\max_{y\in Y}F(x,y)

Input:
stepsizes
\left\{\eta_{t}\right\}
, total number of steps
T>0
, initial point
z_{0}\in Z
.

for
t = 0, 1, …, T-1
do

Define
G(z)=[\nabla_{x}F(z);-\nabla_{y}F(z)]
for any
z\in Z
, and consequently perform update

\displaystyle\widehat{z}_{t}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{z\in Z}\eta_{t}\langle G(z_{t}),z\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2};

(3.4)

\displaystyle z_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{z\in Z}\eta_{t}\left[\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t}),z\rangle+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert z-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

(3.5)

end

for

Construct
\overline{z}_{T}={\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}\widehat{z}_{t}}/({\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}})
, with
\left\{\Lambda_{t}\right\}
defined in (
3.3
).

Output:

(\overline{z}_{T},z_{T})
.

We proceed to establish the generic convergence properties of the REG method.

Lemma 3.1
.

Take stepsize
\eta_{t}\leq 1/L
.
Then for any
T\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle F(\overline{x}_{T},y)-F(x,\overline{y}_{T})+\tfrac{\mu\Lambda_{T}}{2(\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0})}\lVert z-z_{T}\rVert^{2}\leq\tfrac{\mu\Lambda_{0}}{2(\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0})}\lVert z-z_{0}\rVert^{2}.

(3.6)

Proof.

First, the optimality condition of (
3.4
) yields

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\langle G(z_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rVert^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

(3.7)

Similarly, the optimality condition of (
3.5
) yields

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t}),z_{t+1}-z\rangle+\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}+1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}}{2}\lVert z_{t+1}-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z_{t+1}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}}{2}\lVert z-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

(3.8)

By taking
z=z_{t+1}
in (
3.7
), and combining with (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\langle G(z_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t}),z_{t+1}-z\rangle+\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}+1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}}{2}\lVert z-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

(3.9)

In addition, one also has

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[\langle G(z_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rangle+\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t}),z_{t+1}-z\rangle\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle+\langle G(z_{t})-G(\widehat{z}_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rangle\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rVert^{2}-L\lVert z_{t}-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\geq}

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rVert^{2}\right],

(3.10)

where
(a)
follows from
G
being
L
-Lipschitz, and
F
being

\mu
-strongly-convex w.r.t.
x
and strongly-concave w.r.t.
y
;
(b)
follows from Hölder’s inequality together with
L\eta_{t}\leq 1
.
By combining (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})\right]+\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}+1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

Multiplying both sides by
\Lambda_{t}
, and making using of its definition, we obtain

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})\right]+\tfrac{\Lambda_{t+1}}{2}\lVert z-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}\leq\tfrac{\Lambda_{t}}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

Taking the telescopic sum of the above inequality from
t=0
to
T-1
yields

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})\right]+\tfrac{\Lambda_{T}}{2}\lVert z-z_{T}\rVert^{2}\leq\tfrac{\Lambda_{0}}{2}\lVert z-z_{0}\rVert^{2}.

Further dividing both sides of the above relation by
\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}
, while noting that
\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}=\left(\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0}\right)/{\mu}
from (
3.3
)
and making use of the definition of
\overline{z}_{T}
, we obtain the desired claim.
∎

We are now ready to establish the linear convergence of REG with concrete parameter specifications.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose
\mu>0
. Let
z^{*}
be the unique saddle point of (
1.1
).
Choose
\eta_{t}=1/L
in the REG method, then

\displaystyle\lVert z_{T}-z^{*}\rVert^{2}\leq\left(1+\tfrac{\mu}{L}\right)^{-T}\lVert z_{0}-z^{*}\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

The desired claim is an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.1
.
∎

It is worth mentioning that the above convergence of REG generalizes trivially to solving variational inequality

\displaystyle\langle G(z),z-z^{*}\rangle\geq 0,~\forall z\in Z,

when operator
G
is smooth and strongly-monotone.
Notably, the obtained iteration complexity by REG is optimal for this problem class.

3.2
Stochastic Regularized Extragradient

The stochastic variant of the REG method, which we term SREG (Algorithm

Algorithm 4

(F)

\left\{\eta_{t}\right\}

\displaystyle\widehat{z}_{t}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{z\in Z}\eta_{t}\langle G(z_{t},\xi_{t}),z\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2};

(3.11)

\displaystyle z_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{z\in Z}\eta_{t}\left[\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t},\widehat{\xi}_{t}),z\rangle+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert z-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

(3.12)

end

Construct
\overline{z}_{T}={\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}\widehat{z}_{t}}/({\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}})

\left\{\Lambda_{t}\right\}

.

For notational simplicity going forward let us denote

\widehat{\zeta}_{t}=G(\widehat{z}_{t},\widehat{\xi}_{t})-G(\widehat{z}_{t})

The next lemma provides a basic characterization on each step of the SREG method.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose

\displaystyle L\eta_{t}\leq 1/2,~t\geq 0.

Then for any

, we have

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})\right]+\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}+1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}\langle\widehat{\zeta}_{t},\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+4\eta_{t}^{2}\left(\lVert\zeta_{t}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\widehat{\zeta}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right).

(3.13)

Proof.

First, the optimality condition of (
3.11
) yields

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\langle G(z_{t},\xi_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rVert^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

(3.14)

Similarly, the optimality condition of (
3.12
) yields

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t},\widehat{\xi}_{t}),z_{t+1}-z\rangle+\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}+1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z_{t+1}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}\leq\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}}{2}\lVert z-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

(3.15)

By taking
z=z_{t+1}
in (
3.14
), and combining with (
3.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\langle G(z_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t}),z_{t+1}-z\rangle+\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}+1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle~~~+\eta_{t}\langle G(z_{t},\xi_{t})-G(z_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rangle+\eta_{t}\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t},\widehat{\xi}_{t})-G(\widehat{z}_{t}),z_{t+1}-\widehat{z}_{t}\rangle+\eta_{t}\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t},\widehat{\xi}_{t})-G(\widehat{z}_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}}{2}\lVert z-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

Then further applying Young’s inequality to the above relation yields

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\langle G(z_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t}),z_{t+1}-z\rangle

\displaystyle~~~+\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}+1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}\langle\widehat{\zeta}_{t},\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu\eta_{t}}{2}\lVert z-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+2\eta_{t}^{2}\left(\lVert\zeta_{t}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\widehat{\zeta}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right).

(3.16)

Following similar lines as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, one also has

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[\langle G(z_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rangle+\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t}),z_{t+1}-z\rangle\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle+\langle G(z_{t})-G(\widehat{z}_{t}),\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rangle\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{\geq}

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rVert^{2}-L\lVert z_{t}-\widehat{z}_{t}\rVert\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\geq}

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rVert^{2}\right],

(3.17)

where
(a)
follows from
G
being
L
-Lipschitz, and
F
is strongly-convex-strongly-concave with modulus
\mu
;
(b)
follows from Hölder’s inequality together with
L\eta_{t}\leq{1}/{2}
.
The desired claim follows by combining (
3.16
) and (
3.17
).
∎

With Lemma
3.2
in place, we are ready to establish the generic convergence properties of SREG.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\left\{\Lambda_{t}\right\}
be defined as in (
3.3
).
Choose in the SREG method that

\displaystyle L\eta_{t}\leq 1/2,~t\geq 0.

(3.18)

Then for any
T\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(\overline{x}_{T},y)-F(x,\overline{y}_{T})+\tfrac{\mu\Lambda_{T}}{2(\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0})}\lVert z-z_{T}\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu\Lambda_{0}}{2(\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0})}\lVert z-z_{0}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{8\mu\sigma^{2}}{\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0}}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}\Lambda_{t}.

Proof.

Multiplying both sides of (
3.13
) by
\Lambda_{t}
yields

\displaystyle\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})\right]+\tfrac{(\mu\eta_{t}+1)\Lambda_{t}}{2}\lVert z-z_{t+1}\rVert^{2}+\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}\langle\widehat{\zeta}_{t},\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle\leq\tfrac{\Lambda_{t}}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}+4\eta_{t}^{2}\Lambda_{t}\left(\lVert\zeta_{t}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\widehat{\zeta}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right).

Applying the definition of
\left\{\Lambda_{t}\right\}
, we can now take the telescopic sum of the above relation and obtain

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}\left[F(\widehat{x}_{t},y)-F(x,\widehat{y}_{t})\right]+\tfrac{(\mu\eta_{T-1}+1)\Lambda_{T-1}}{2}\lVert z-z_{T}\rVert^{2}+\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}\langle\widehat{\zeta}_{t},\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\Lambda_{0}}{2}\lVert z-z_{0}\rVert^{2}+4\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}\Lambda_{t}\left(\lVert\zeta_{t}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\widehat{\zeta}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right).

Dividing both sides of the above relation by
\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}=\tfrac{1}{\mu}\left(\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0}\right)
,
and making use of
F
being convex-concave,
we obtain

\displaystyle F(\overline{x}_{T},y)-F(x,\overline{y}_{T})+\tfrac{\mu\Lambda_{T}}{2(\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0})}\lVert z-z_{T}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu\Lambda_{0}}{2(\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0})}\lVert z-z_{0}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{4\mu}{\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0}}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}\Lambda_{t}\left(\lVert\zeta_{t}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\widehat{\zeta}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right)-\tfrac{\mu}{\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0}}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}\Lambda_{t}\langle\widehat{\zeta}_{t},\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle.

The desired claim follows after taking expectation on both sides of the above relation,
and noting that
\mathbb{E}[\langle\widehat{\zeta}_{t},\widehat{z}_{t}-z\rangle]=0
.
∎

We now specify detailed choice of
\left\{\eta_{t}\right\}
and obtain the concrete convergence characterization of SREG as an immediate consequence of Lemma
3.3
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Define
t_{0}=4\lceil{L}/{\mu}\rceil
, and choose

\eta_{t}=\tfrac{2}{\mu(t+t_{0}+1)}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert z^{*}-z_{T}\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\tfrac{6t_{0}^{2}}{T^{2}}\lVert z^{*}-z_{0}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{768\sigma^{2}}{\mu^{2}T}.

Proof.

Clearly, (
3.18
) is satisfied by the choice of
\left\{\eta_{t}\right\}
.
Direct computation also yields

\Lambda_{t}=\tfrac{(t+t_{0}+1)(t+t_{0}+2)}{(t_{0}+1)(t_{0}+2)}.

Applying Lemma
3.3
with
(x,y)=(x^{*},y^{*})
then yields

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[F(\overline{x}_{T},y^{*})-F(x^{*},\overline{y}_{T})+\tfrac{\mu}{2}\lVert z^{*}-z_{T}\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\tfrac{\mu\Lambda_{0}}{2(\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0})}\lVert z^{*}-z_{0}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{8\mu\sigma^{2}}{\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0}}\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}\Lambda_{t}.

The desired claim then follows from the above relation and the following simple observation:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{T}-\Lambda_{0}=\tfrac{(T+t_{0}+1)(T+t_{0}+2)}{(t_{0}+1)(t_{0}+2)}-1\geq\tfrac{T^{2}}{6t_{0}^{2}},~\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{t=0}^{T-1}\eta_{t}^{2}\Lambda_{t}=\tfrac{4(t+t_{0}+2)}{\mu^{2}(t_{0}+1)(t_{0}+2)(t+t_{0}+1)}\leq\tfrac{8T}{\mu^{2}t_{0}^{2}}.

∎

Theorem
3.2
implies an
\mathcal{O}(\tfrac{L\lVert z^{*}-z_{0}\rVert}{\mu\sqrt{\epsilon}}+\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{\mu^{2}\epsilon})
iteration and sample complexities for SREG to obtain an
\epsilon
-optimal solution.
We next proceed to show that with a proper restarting technique, one can indeed substantially improve the reduction of the deterministic error within the SREG method.

Theorem 3.3
.

Consider the multi-epoch version of SREG defined as follows.
For any
e>0
, within the
e
-th epoch, run SREG starting from
z_{(e-1)}
for
T_{e}
steps with parameters

\displaystyle T_{e}=\tfrac{6t_{0}^{2}}{T_{e}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\lVert z^{*}-z_{(e)}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{768\sigma^{2}}{\mu^{2}T_{e}},~\eta_{t}=\tfrac{2}{\mu(t+t_{0}+1)},~t_{0}=4\lceil\tfrac{L}{\mu}\rceil,

and output
z_{(e)}\coloneqq z_{T_{e}}
.
Then for any
z_{(0)}\in X\times Y
, by choosing
E=\log_{2}\left(\tfrac{\lVert z_{(0)}-z^{*}\rVert^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert z_{(E)}-z^{*}\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\epsilon.

The total iteration and sample complexities can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{L}{\mu}\log_{2}\left(\tfrac{\lVert z^{*}-z_{0}\rVert}{\epsilon}\right)+\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{\mu^{2}\epsilon}\right).

Proof.

In view of Theorem
3.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert z^{*}-z_{(e+1)}\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\tfrac{6t_{0}^{2}}{T_{e}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\lVert z^{*}-z_{(e)}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{768\sigma^{2}}{\mu^{2}T_{e}}.

It can be readily seen that from an induction argument, by choosing

T_{e}=6t_{0}+\tfrac{768\cdot 2^{e+3}\sigma^{2}}{\mu^{2}\lVert z^{*}-z_{0}\rVert^{2}},

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert z^{*}-z_{(e)}\rVert^{2}\right]\leq 2^{-e}\lVert z^{*}-z_{(0)}\rVert^{2},\forall e\geq 0.

The total number of iterations to obtain
\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert z^{*}-z_{(e)}\rVert^{2}\right]\leq\epsilon
is thus bounded by

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}\left(t_{0}\log_{2}\left(\tfrac{\lVert z^{*}-z_{0}\rVert}{\epsilon}\right)+\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{\mu^{2}\epsilon}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left(\tfrac{L}{\mu}\log_{2}\left(\tfrac{\lVert z^{*}-z_{0}\rVert}{\epsilon}\right)+\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{\mu^{2}\epsilon}\right).

The proof is then completed.
∎

A few remarks are in order before we conclude this section.
In view of Theorem
3.3
, the proposed REG method obtains the optimal iteration and sample complexities for solving smooth and strongly monotone variational inequalities.
It should be noted that similar performances have been obtained recently in
[
23
]
and
[
19
]
.
Nevertheless, given our discussions in Section
1
, these methods are clearly non-optimal for our problem of interest (
1.1
) even in the deterministic setting.
In following sections, we show that the error condition associated with the REG method makes it natural candidate for the catalyst scheme to be developed for (
1.1
).
Consequently, one can accelerate the REG method and obtain optimal iteration and sample complexities up to logarithmic factors.

4
Deterministic Minimax Catalyst Scheme

In this section, we formally introduce the catalyst scheme for the minimax problem (
1.1
).
Compared to Section
3
, we no longer require the problem to be strongly-convex-strongly-concave.
Instead, going forward we only assume that (
1.1
) is always strongly-concave w.r.t.
y
.
It should be noted that when
\mu_{d}=0
and subsequently
\mu_{p}=0
(since
\mu_{d}\geq\mu_{p}
given our discussion in Section
1
), one can simply add a proper strongly-convex-strongly-concave perturbation before applying the catalyst scheme.
As will be clarified by our ensuing discussion, this simple reduction technique again would yield optimal iteration and sample complexities up to logarithmic factors.

The proposed catalyst scheme for minimax problem (
Catalyst-Minimax
) is presented in Algorithm
5
.
Similar to (
2.3
), at the
k
-th iteration,
for a given prox-center
\widehat{x}_{k}
and some
\beta_{k}\geq 0
, we
denote

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(x,y):=F(x,y)+\tfrac{\beta_{k}}{2}\|x-\widehat{x}_{k}\|^{2},

(4.1)

and define the proximal subproblem as

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\left\{\phi_{k}(x):=\max_{y\in Y}\Phi_{k}(x,y)\right\}.

(4.2)

We assume the catalyst scheme has access to a to-be-catalyzed method
\mathcal{A}
, which can find a pair of solution
(\widetilde{z}_{k},z_{k})\in X\times X
for (
4.2
) such that

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{y})-\Phi_{k}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y}_{k})+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{z}-z_{k}\|^{2}\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{z}-z_{k}^{0}\|^{2},

(4.3)

for any
\widetilde{z}\in Z
, where
z_{k}^{0}=(x_{k}^{0},y_{k}^{0})

is a given pair of starting points for method
\mathcal{A}
.

Algorithm 5

Catalyst-Minimax
(\mathcal{A})
: catalyst scheme for minimax optimization

Input:
initial points
\bar{z}_{0}=\widetilde{z}_{0}=z_{0}
, and algorithmic parameters
\{\alpha_{k}\}
,
\{\beta_{k}\}
,
\{\gamma_{k}\}
, and
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
.

for

k=1,2,\ldots,

do

\displaystyle\widehat{x}_{k}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{k}\bar{x}_{k-1}+(1-\gamma_{k})\widetilde{x}_{k-1}.

(4.4)

\displaystyle(\widetilde{z}_{k},z_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}(\Phi_{k})~\text{s.t. \eqref{eq:cp_inexact_sd} holds with $x_{k}^{0}=\widehat{x}_{k}$ and $y_{k}^{0}=y_{k-1}$.}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\bar{x}_{k}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\alpha_{k}\gamma_{k}+\mu_{p}(1-\gamma_{k})}\left[\alpha_{k}x_{k}+(\mu_{p}-\alpha_{k})(1-\gamma_{k})\widetilde{x}_{k-1}\right].

(4.6)

end

for

It should be noted that although both
\alpha_{k}
and
\varepsilon_{k}
are involved in defining the approximate update (
4.3
), the catalyst scheme only requires
\alpha_{k}
to perform subsequent updates (
4.4
) and (
4.6
).
Clearly, when
\alpha_{k}=\beta_{k}
and
\varepsilon_{k}=0
, the proximal step (
4.1
) is computed exactly, and the catalyst scheme reduces to the accelerated proximal point method applied to (
1.1
).
Consequently, (
4.3
) can be viewed as characterizing the approximate update of the proximal step.
For the remainder of our discussion in this section, we establish the convergence of the catalyst scheme with proper conditions imposed on (
4.3
), and subsequently invoke the REG method developed in Section
3
to certify such conditions.
As our initial step, we first make the following technical observation that relates the distance on the dual space with the primal optimality gap.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(x^{*},y^{*})
be a pair of optimal primal and dual solutions of (
1.1
).
Define

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}:=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{y\in Y}\Phi_{k}(\widetilde{x}_{k},y).

(4.7)

Then

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y^{*}-\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}\|^{2}\leq f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(x^{*}).

Proof.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{y\in Y}\Phi_{k}(\widetilde{x}_{k},y)=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmax}}_{y\in Y}F(\widetilde{x}_{k},y).

Given the optimality condition of
\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}
, we further have

\displaystyle f(\widetilde{x}_{k})=F(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*})\geq F(\widetilde{x}_{k},y^{*})+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y^{*}-\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}\|^{2}\geq F(x^{*},y^{*})+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y^{*}-\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}\|^{2}=f(x^{*})+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{2}\|y^{*}-\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}\|^{2}.

The proof is then completed.
∎

With Lemma
4.1
in place, we proceed to characterize each step of the proposed catalyst scheme.

Lemma 4.2
.

Within Algorithm
5
,
for any
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\tfrac{4\varepsilon_{k}}{\mu_{d}}\right)\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})\right]+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}\gamma_{k}^{2}+\gamma_{k}(1-\gamma_{k})\mu_{p}}{2}\|x^{*}-\bar{x}_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\gamma_{k}+\tfrac{4\varepsilon_{k}}{\mu_{d}}\right)\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{k-1})-f(x^{*})\right]+\tfrac{(\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k})\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2}\|x^{*}-\bar{x}_{k-1}\|^{2}+\varepsilon_{k}\|\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.

(4.8)

Proof.

By (
4.3
) and the selection of initial points in (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{k}(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{y})-\Phi_{k}(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y}_{k})+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{z}-z_{k}\|^{2}\leq\tfrac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2}[\|\widetilde{x}-\widehat{x}_{k}\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{y}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}],~\forall\widetilde{z}\in Z,

which, in view of the definition of
\Phi_{k}
in (
4.1
), implies that

\displaystyle F(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{y})-F(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y}_{k})+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{z}-z_{k}\|^{2}\leq\tfrac{\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{x}-\widehat{x}_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}-y_{k-1}\|^{2},~\forall\widetilde{z}\in Z.

Using the above inequality and the fact that
F(\widetilde{x},\widetilde{y}_{k})\leq f(\widetilde{x})
, we obtain

\displaystyle F(\widetilde{x}_{k},\widetilde{y})-f(\widetilde{x})+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{z}-z_{k}\|^{2}\leq\tfrac{\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{x}-\widehat{x}_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}-y_{k-1}\|^{2},~\forall\widetilde{z}\in Z.

Setting
\widetilde{y}=\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}

in the above relation yields

\displaystyle f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(\widetilde{x})+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}[\|\widetilde{x}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k}\|^{2}]\leq\tfrac{\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{x}-\widehat{x}_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}

for any
\widetilde{x}\in X
.
In addition, we also have

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}\|^{2}+2\|\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4(\|\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y^{*}\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}-y^{*}\|^{2})+2\|\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{8}{\mu_{d}}\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})+f(\widetilde{x}_{k-1})-f(x^{*})\right]+2\|\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.

Combining the previous two relations, we have

\displaystyle f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(\widetilde{x})+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}[\|\widetilde{x}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{x}-\widehat{x}_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{4\varepsilon_{k}}{\mu_{d}}\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})+f(\widetilde{x}_{k-1})-f(x^{*})\right]+\varepsilon_{k}\|\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.

Setting
\widetilde{x}=\gamma_{k}x^{*}+(1-\gamma_{k})\widetilde{x}_{k-1}
and using the (strong) convexity
of
f
, we obtain

\displaystyle f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-\gamma_{k}f(x^{*})-(1-\gamma_{k})f(\widetilde{x}_{k-1})+\tfrac{\mu_{p}\gamma_{k}(1-\gamma_{k})}{2}\|x^{*}-\widetilde{x}_{k-1}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle~~~+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}[\|\gamma_{k}x^{*}+(1-\gamma_{k})\widetilde{x}_{k-1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\|\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\tfrac{\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}}{2}\|\gamma_{k}x^{*}+(1-\gamma_{k})\widetilde{x}_{k-1}-\widehat{x}_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{4\varepsilon_{k}}{\mu_{d}}\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})+f(\widetilde{x}_{k-1})-f(x^{*})\right]+\varepsilon_{k}\|\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2},

which, after substituting the definition of
\widehat{x}_{k}
in (
4.4
), becomes

\displaystyle\left(1-\tfrac{4\varepsilon_{k}}{\mu_{d}}\right)\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})\right]+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\|\gamma_{k}x^{*}+(1-\gamma_{k})\widetilde{x}_{k-1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{p}\gamma_{k}(1-\gamma_{k})}{2}\|x^{*}-\widetilde{x}_{k-1}\|^{2}+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1-\gamma_{k}+\tfrac{4\varepsilon_{k}}{\mu_{d}})\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{k-1})-f(x^{*})\right]+\tfrac{(\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k})\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2}\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}_{k-1}\rVert^{2}+\varepsilon_{k}\|\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.

It remains to make use of observation (

\displaystyle\left(1-\tfrac{4\varepsilon_{k}}{\mu_{d}}\right)\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})\right]+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}\gamma_{k}^{2}+\gamma_{k}(1-\gamma_{k})\mu_{p}}{2}\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}_{k}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1-\gamma_{k}+\tfrac{4\varepsilon_{k}}{\mu_{d}}\right)\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{k-1})-f(x^{*})\right]+\tfrac{(\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k})\gamma_{k}^{2}}{2}\lVert x^{*}-\overline{x}_{k-1}\rVert^{2}+\varepsilon_{k}\|\widetilde{y}_{k-1}^{*}-y_{k-1}\|^{2}.

The proof is then completed.
∎

We now turn our attention to the global convergence of the proposed catalyst scheme under proper requirements of the approximate proximal update (
4.3
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose (
4.3
) holds, and

\displaystyle\tfrac{\alpha_{k}\gamma_{k}^{2}}{1-4\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{d}}

\displaystyle\geq\tfrac{(\beta_{k+1}+\varepsilon_{k+1})\gamma_{k+1}^{2}}{1-\gamma_{k+1}+4\varepsilon_{k+1}/\mu_{d}},

(4.9)

\displaystyle\tfrac{\varepsilon_{k+1}}{\alpha_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1-\gamma_{k+1}+4\varepsilon_{k+1}/\mu_{d}}{2(1-4\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{d})}.

(4.10)

Define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}=\begin{cases}1,~&k=1;\\
\Gamma_{k-1}\tfrac{1-\gamma_{k}+4\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{d}}{1-4\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{d}},~&k\geq 2.\end{cases}

(4.11)

Then we have

\displaystyle{f(\widetilde{x}_{k})-f(x^{*})}+\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2(1-4\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{d})}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{k}^{*}-y_{k}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Gamma_{k}\left[\tfrac{1-\gamma_{1}+4\varepsilon_{1}/\mu_{d}}{(1-4\varepsilon_{1}/\mu_{d})}\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{0})-f(x^{*})\right]+\tfrac{(\beta_{1}+\varepsilon_{1})\gamma_{1}^{2}}{2(1-{4\varepsilon_{1}}/{\mu_{d}})}\lVert x-\widetilde{x}_{0}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{(1-{4\varepsilon_{1}}/{\mu_{d}})}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{0}^{*}-y_{0}\rVert^{2}\right].

\varepsilon_{k}=\beta_{k}\varepsilon

Combining the above observations, we can now invoke Lemma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{K})-f(x^{*})+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{12}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{K}^{*}-y_{K}\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{K})-f(x^{*})+\tfrac{\alpha_{K}}{2(1-4\varepsilon_{K}/\mu_{d})}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{K}^{*}-y_{K}\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\tfrac{12}{K^{2}}\big[\tfrac{1-\gamma_{1}+4\varepsilon_{1}/\mu_{d}}{(1-4\varepsilon_{1}/\mu_{d})}\left[f({x}_{0})-f(x^{*})\right]+\tfrac{(\beta_{1}+\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime})\gamma_{1}^{2}}{2(1-{4\varepsilon_{1}}/{\mu_{d}})}\lVert x-{x}_{0}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{(1-{4\varepsilon_{1}}/{\mu_{d}})}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{0}^{*}-y_{0}\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle~~~+\textstyle\sum\nolimits_{l=1}^{K}\tfrac{\delta}{(1-4\varepsilon_{l}/\mu_{d})\Gamma_{l}}\big]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\tfrac{12}{K^{2}}\big[\tfrac{1-\gamma_{1}+4\varepsilon_{1}/\mu_{d}}{(1-4\varepsilon_{1}/\mu_{d})}\left[f({x}_{0})-f(x^{*})\right]+\tfrac{(\beta_{1}+\varepsilon_{1}^{\prime})\gamma_{1}^{2}}{2(1-{4\varepsilon_{1}}/{\mu_{d}})}\lVert x-{x}_{0}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{\varepsilon_{1}}{(1-{4\varepsilon_{1}}/{\mu_{d}})}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{0}^{*}-y_{0}\rVert^{2}\big]+64K\delta,

(5.9)

where
(a)

\varepsilon\leq\tfrac{1}{12}

\displaystyle\tfrac{\alpha_{k}}{2(1-4\varepsilon_{k}/\mu_{d})}=\tfrac{\beta_{k}(1+\varepsilon^{\prime})}{2(1-4\beta_{k}\varepsilon/\mu_{d})}\geq\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{12}.

In addition,

Now simplifying (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{K})-f(x^{*})+\tfrac{\mu_{d}}{12}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{K}^{*}-y_{K}\rVert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\tfrac{12}{K^{2}}\left[2\varepsilon\left[f(\widetilde{x}_{0})-f(x^{*})\right]+\mu_{d}\lVert x^{*}-\widetilde{x}_{0}\rVert^{2}+\mu_{d}\lVert\widetilde{y}_{0}^{*}-y_{0}\rVert^{2}\right]+64K\delta.

∎

5.1
Catalyst Scheme with Stochastic Regularized Extragradient

Before we proceed to technical discussions in this subsection, it might be worth reiterating that within the approximate proximal update (
5.1
), the dual reference point can be chosen a posteriori (see Lemma
4.2
).
In the stochastic setting this creates a unique challenge as such a selection can depend on the noise when solving the proximal step (
4.2
).
To address this challenge, we will modify the SREG method discussed in Section
3
, and adopt a novel analysis by introducing an auxiliary sequence defined in the dual space to handle the correlation with noise when selecting the dual reference point.

We now present a simple modification of the SREG method for solving the proximal step (
4.2
).
The resulting method, presented in Algorithm
7
, considers the problem of
\min_{x\in X}\max_{y\in Y}\psi(x,y)
where
\psi
is
L_{\psi}
-smooth,
\mu_{X}
-strongly-convex w.r.t.
x
,
and
\mu_{Y}
-strongly-concave w.r.t.
y
.
With a slight terminology overload, we refer to this method also as the SREG method within our discussion in this subsection.
It can be readily seen that the only modification we make is the separation of strong-convexity/concavity modulus in defining the “extragradient” step (
5.15
) for the primal and dual variables.
As will be clarified in the ensuing Lemma
5.6
, this separation turns out to be essential for the efficiency of the catalyst scheme in the stochastic setting.

Algorithm 7

SREG
(\psi)
: extragradient for
\min_{x\in X}\max_{y\in Y}\psi(x,y)

Input:
stepsizes
\left\{\eta_{t}\right\}

\displaystyle\widehat{z}_{t}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{z\in Z}\eta_{t}\langle G(z_{t},\xi_{t}),z\rangle+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2};

(5.14)

\displaystyle z_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{z\in Z}\eta_{t}\left[\langle G(\widehat{z}_{t},\widehat{\xi}_{t}),z\rangle+\tfrac{\mu_{X}}{2}\lVert x-\widehat{x}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{Y}}{2}\lVert y-\widehat{y}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2},

(5.15)

where
\mu_{X}
(resp.
\mu_{Y}
) is the strong-convexity (resp. strong-concavity) modulus of
\psi
w.r.t.
x
(resp.
y

end

for

Construct

(5.16)

.

Lemma 5.5
.

Define

\displaystyle u_{t+1}=\textstyle\mathop{\mathrm{argmin}}_{y\in Y}\eta_{t}{\langle-\widehat{\zeta}^{Y}_{t},y\rangle}+\tfrac{\vartheta_{t}}{2}\lVert y-y_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert y-u_{t}\rVert^{2}.

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert x-x_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1+\vartheta_{t}}{2}\lVert y-y_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert y-u_{t}\rVert^{2}+4\eta_{t}^{2}\left(\lVert\zeta_{t}\rVert^{2}+\lVert\widehat{\zeta}_{t}\rVert^{2}\right).

First, the optimality condition of (

\displaystyle\tfrac{\mu_{X}\eta_{t}}{2}\lVert x-\widehat{x}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{\mu_{Y}\eta_{t}}{2}\lVert y-\widehat{y}_{t}\rVert^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert z-z_{t}\rVert^{2}.

By taking

6
Concluding Remarks

This manuscript presents a catalyst scheme applicable to both smooth convex optimization and convex-concave minimax problems.
We establish the optimal iteration and sample complexities of the catalyst scheme when accelerating a simple variant of stochastic gradient method for convex optimization.
We further develop a novel variant of the stochastic extragradient method for solving strongly-monotone variational inequalities with optimal complexities.
By catalyzing the proposed extragradient variant, the catalyst scheme further obtains optimal iteration and sample complexities for strong-convex-strongly-concave minimax problems, up to a potential logarithmic factor.
It remains highly rewarding to further investigate whether such a logarithmic factor is indeed unavoidable for minimax problems or could be removed with refined methods.

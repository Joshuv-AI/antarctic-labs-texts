Performance analysis of MUSIC-type imaging without diagonal elements of multi-static response matrix
Won-Kwang Park

Abstract
Generally, to apply the MUltiple SIgnal Classification (MUSIC) algorithm for the rapid imaging of small inhomogeneities, the complete elements of the multi-static response (MSR) matrix must be collected. However, in real-world applications such as microwave imaging or bistatic measurement configuration, diagonal elements of the MSR matrix are unknown. Nevertheless, it is possible to obtain imaging results using a traditional approach but theoretical reason of the applicability has not been investigated yet. In this paper, we establish mathematical structures of the imaging function of MUSIC from an MSR matrix without diagonal elements in both transverse magnetic (TM) and transverse electric (TE) polarizations. The established structures demonstrate why the shape of the location of small inhomogeneities can be retrieved via MUSIC without the diagonal elements of the MSR matrix. In addition, they reveal the intrinsic properties of imaging and the fundamental limitations. Results of numerical simulations are also provided to support the identified structures.

In this study, we consider the MUSIC algorithm for imaging two-dimensional small inhomogeneities in TM and TE polarization from MSR matrix when the diagonal elements are cannot be handled. In order to show the feasibility, we carefully investigate the mathematical structure of a MUSIC-type imaging function by identifying a connection with the Bessel function of integer order of the first kind. In order to support the theoretical results, simulation results with synthetic data polluted by random noise are exhibited.

D

\omega=2\pi f
. Here,

denotes the ordinary frequency measured in

Let
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{a}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{a}}
denote the value of dielectric permittivity and magnetic permeability of
D
, respectively, and we denote
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}
and
\mu_{\mathrm{b}}

\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\overline{D}

\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{a}}&\text{for}&\mathbf{x}\in D\\
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}&\text{for}&\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\overline{D}\end{array}\right.\quad\text{and}\quad\mu(\mathbf{x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mu_{\mathrm{a}}&\text{for}&\mathbf{x}\in D\\
\mu_{\mathrm{b}}&\text{for}&\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\overline{D}.\end{array}\right.

With this, we denote

In this paper, we consider the illumination of plane waves with the direction of propagation

u_{\inc}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})=e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\mathbf{x}},

u(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})
be the time-harmonic total field; then, it satisfies

\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{1}{\mu(\mathbf{x})}\nabla u(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)+\omega^{2}\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})u(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})=0\quad\text{for}\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\overline{{D}}

(1)

with transmission conditions at the boundaries of
{D}
. We denote
u_{\scat}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})=u(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})-u_{\inc}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})
as the scattered field, which is required to satisfy the Sommerfeld radiation condition

\lim_{|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty}|\mathbf{x}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\frac{\partial u_{\scat}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})}{\partial|\mathbf{x}|}-ik_{\mathrm{b}}u_{\scat}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})\right)=0

uniformly in all directions
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=\mathbf{x}/\left|\mathbf{x}\right|\in\mathbb{S}^{1}

Let
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\boldsymbol{\theta})
be the far-field pattern of the scattered field
u_{\scat}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})
with observation direction
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
that satisfies

u_{\scat}(\mathbf{x},\boldsymbol{\theta})=\frac{e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}|\mathbf{x}|}}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{x}|}}u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta},\boldsymbol{\theta})+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathbf{x}|}}\right)\quad\text{uniformly in all directions}\quad\boldsymbol{\vartheta}=\frac{\mathbf{x}}{|\mathbf{x}|},\quad|\mathbf{x}|\longrightarrow\infty.

(2)

Then, by virtue of

3
Traditional MUSIC Algorithm

In this section, we introduce the traditional MUSIC algorithm for imaging
{D}
in dielectric permittivity (or TM polarization) and magnetic permeability (or TE polarization) cases. For the sake of simplicity, suppose that we have
N-
different number of incident and observation directions
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}
and
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}
, respectively, for
n,m=1,2,\cdots,N
, and that the incident and observation directions are the same (i.e.,
\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{n}=-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}
). In this paper, we consider the full-view inverse problem. Therefore, we assume that
\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}
is uniformly distributed in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that

\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n}=\left(\cos\frac{2\pi n}{N},\sin\frac{2\pi n}{N}\right).

(4)

Traditionally, the following MSR matrix is used in the MUSIC algorithm:

\mathbb{M}=\begin{pmatrix}u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2})&\cdots&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N-1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N})\\
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2})&\cdots&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N-1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2})&\cdots&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N-1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N})\\
\end{pmatrix}.

First, let us assume that
\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})\neq\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}
and
\mu(\mathbf{x})=\mu_{\mathrm{b}}
. Based on (
3
), since
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n})
can be approximated as

u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n})\approx\alpha^{2}\pi\frac{k_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}(1+i)}{4\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{b}}\pi}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{a}}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}}\right)e^{-ik_{\mathrm{b}}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n})\cdot\mathbf{z}},

\mathbb{M}
can be written as

\mathbb{M}=\alpha^{2}\pi\frac{k_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}(1+i)}{4\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{b}}\pi}}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{a}}-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}}\right)\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})^{T}\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z}),

(5)

where

\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})=\bigg(e^{-ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{z}},e^{-ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{z}},\cdots,e^{-ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{z}}\bigg)\bigg|_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}=-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{m}}=\bigg(e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{z}},e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{z}},\cdots,e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{z}}\bigg).

(6)

Based on the factorization of the MSR matrix, the range of
\mathbb{M}
is determined by the span of
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})
corresponding to
{D}
; that is, we can define a signal subspace using a set of singular vectors corresponding to the nonzero singular values of
\mathbb{M}
.

Now, to introduce the imaging function of MUSIC, let us perform the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the MSR matrix
\mathbb{M}
:

\mathbb{M}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma_{n}\mathbf{E}_{n}\mathbf{F}_{n}^{*}\approx\sigma_{1}\mathbf{E}_{1}\mathbf{F}_{1}^{*},

where superscript
*
is the mark of Hermitian,
\mathbf{E}_{n}
and
\mathbf{F}_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
are respectively the left- and right-singular vectors of
\mathbb{M}
, and
\sigma_{n}
denotes singular values that satisfy

\sigma_{1}>0\quad\text{and}\quad\sigma_{n}\approx 0\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 2.

Then,
\left\{\mathbf{E}_{1}\right\}
and
\left\{\mathbf{E}_{2},\mathbf{E}_{3},\cdots,\mathbf{E}_{N}\right\}
are the (orthonormal) basis of the signal and null (or noise) space of
\mathbb{M}
, respectively. Therefore, one can define the projection operator onto the noise subspace. This projection is given explicitly by

\mathbb{P}_{\noise}=\mathbb{I}_{N}-\mathbf{E}_{1}\mathbf{E}_{1}^{*},

where
\mathbb{I}_{N}
denotes the
N\times N
identity matrix. By regarding the structure of
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{z})
of (
6
), we introduce the following unit test vector
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})
: for
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\bigg(e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x}},e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{x}},\ldots,e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\bigg)^{T},

(7)

where
\Omega
denotes the region of interests (ROI). Then, there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
N\geq N_{0}
, the following statement holds:

\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})\in\text{Range}(\mathbb{M})\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{z}\in{D}.

This signifies that
\mathbb{P}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}))=0
when
\mathbf{z}\in{D}
. Thus, we can design a MUSIC-type imaging function such that

\mathfrak{F}_{\tm}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}))|}.

(8)

Then, the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\tm}(\mathbf{x})
will have peaks of large (
+\infty
in theory) and small amplitude at
\mathbf{x}\in{D}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\backslash\overline{{D}}
, respectively.

Next, we assume that
\varepsilon(\mathbf{x})=\varepsilon_{\mathrm{b}}
and
\mu(\mathbf{x})\neq\mu_{\mathrm{b}}
. Based on (
3
), since
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n})
can be approximated as

\displaystyle u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n})

\displaystyle=-\alpha^{2}\pi\frac{k_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}(1+i)}{4\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{b}}\pi}}\frac{2\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{a}}+\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta})e^{-ik_{\mathrm{b}}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}-\boldsymbol{\theta})\cdot\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=-\alpha^{2}\pi\frac{k_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}(1+i)}{4\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{b}}\pi}}\frac{2\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{a}}+\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{s})(\boldsymbol{\theta}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{s})\right)e^{-ik_{\mathrm{b}}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}-\boldsymbol{\theta})\cdot\mathbf{z}},

\mathbb{M}
can be written as

\mathbb{M}=\alpha^{2}\pi\frac{k_{\mathrm{b}}^{2}(1+i)}{4\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{b}}\pi}}\frac{2\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{a}}+\mu_{\mathrm{b}}}\sum_{s=1}^{2}\mathbb{F}_{s}(\mathbf{z})^{T}\mathbb{F}_{s}(\mathbf{z}),

(9)

where

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{F}_{s}(\mathbf{z})&=\bigg((-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{s})e^{-ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{z}},(-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{s})e^{-ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{z}},\ldots,(-\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{s})e^{-ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{z}}\bigg)\bigg|_{\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{m}=-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{m}}\\
&=\bigg((\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{s})e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{z}},(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{s})e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{z}},\ldots,(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{s})e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{z}}\bigg).\end{aligned}

(10)

Based on the factorization of the MSR matrix, the range of
\mathbb{M}
is determined by the span of
\left\{\mathbb{F}_{1}(\mathbf{z}),\mathbb{F}_{2}(\mathbf{z})\right\}
corresponding to
{D}
. Thus, to introduce the imaging function of MUSIC, let us perform the singular value decomposition (SVD) of the MSR matrix
\mathbb{M}
:

\mathbb{M}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma_{n}\mathbf{E}_{n}\mathbf{F}_{n}^{*}\approx\sum_{n=1}^{2}\sigma_{n}\mathbf{E}_{n}\mathbf{F}_{n}^{*}.

Then,
\left\{\mathbf{E}_{1},\mathbf{E}_{2}\right\}
and
\left\{\mathbf{E}_{3},\mathbf{E}_{4},\ldots,\mathbf{E}_{N}\right\}
are the (orthonormal) basis of the signal and null (or noise) space of
\mathbb{M}
, respectively. Therefore, one can define the projection operator onto the noise subspace. This projection is given explicitly by

\mathbb{P}_{\noise}=\mathbb{I}_{N}-\sum_{n=1}^{2}\mathbf{E}_{n}\mathbf{E}_{n}^{*}.

By regarding the structure of
\mathbb{F}_{s}(\mathbf{z})
of (
10
), we introduce the following unit test vector
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})
: for
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
,

\mathbf{f}_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})=\sqrt{\frac{2}{N}}\bigg((\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi})e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{x}},(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi})e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2}\cdot\mathbf{x}},\ldots,(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi})e^{ik_{\mathrm{b}}\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N}\cdot\mathbf{x}}\bigg)^{T},

(11)

where
\Omega
denotes the region of interests (ROI). Then, there exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
N\geq N_{0}
, the following statement holds:

\mathbf{f}_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})\in\text{Range}(\mathbb{M})\quad\text{if and only if}\quad\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{z}\in{D}.

This signifies that
\mathbb{P}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}))=0
when
\mathbf{z}\in{D}
. Thus, we can design a MUSIC-type imaging function such that

\mathfrak{F}_{\te}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}_{\mu}(\mathbf{x}))|}.

Then, the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\te}(\mathbf{x})
will have peaks of large (
+\infty
in theory) and small amplitude at
\mathbf{x}\in{D}
and
\mathbf{x}\in\Omega\backslash\overline{{D}}
, respectively. A more detailed description is provided in
[
3
]
.

4
MUSIC algorithm without diagonal elements of MSR matrix: analysis and discussion

Hereinafter, we assume that we have no information of
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{n},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n})
for
n=1,2,\cdots,N
. That is, the obtained MSR matrix must be of the following form:

\mathbb{K}=\begin{pmatrix}\text{unknown}&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2})&\cdots&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N-1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N})\\
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1})&\text{unknown}&\cdots&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N-1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2})&\cdots&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N-1})&\text{unknown}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

It should be noted that we have no any a priori information of the crack, and it is thus difficult to approximate the diagonal terms of the MSR matrix. Throughout this paper, we set the diagonal terms to be zero and consider the following MSR matrix:

\mathbb{K}=\begin{pmatrix}0&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2})&\cdots&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N-1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N})\\
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1})&0&\cdots&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N-1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{2},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{1})&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{2})&\cdots&u_{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\vartheta}_{N},\boldsymbol{\theta}_{N-1})&0\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(12)

The remaining part of the algorithm is identical to the traditional one. For TM case, since the SVD of
\mathbb{K}
can be written as

\mathbb{K}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma_{n}\mathbf{U}_{n}\mathbf{V}_{n}^{*}\approx\sigma_{1}\mathbf{U}_{1}\mathbf{V}_{1}^{*},

(13)

we can define the projection operator onto the noise subspace

\mathbb{P}_{\noise}=\mathbb{I}_{N}-\mathbf{U}_{1}\mathbf{U}_{1}^{*},

(14)

and introduce the MUSIC-type imaging function
\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}
,

\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))|},

(15)

where
\mathbf{f}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})
is defined in (
7
). Then surprisingly, the location of
\mathbf{z}\in{D}
can be identified through the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}(\mathbf{x})
when the total number of incident/observation directions
N
is sufficiently large.

Remark 4.1
.

Although, the diagonal elements of the
\mathbb{K}
are missing, total number of nonzero singular values is same as the total number of cracks but structure of singular values is quietly different from the ones of
\mathbb{M}
. This fact has been examined heuristically in
[
21
]
.

For TE case, since the SVD of
\mathbb{K}
can be written as

\mathbb{K}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma_{n}\mathbf{U}_{n}\mathbf{V}_{n}^{*}\approx\sum_{n=1}^{2}\sigma_{n}\mathbf{U}_{n}\mathbf{V}_{n}^{*},

(16)

we can define the projection operator onto the noise subspace

\mathbb{P}_{\noise}=\mathbb{I}_{N}-\sum_{n=1}^{2}\mathbf{U}_{n}\mathbf{U}_{n}^{*},

(17)

and corresponding imaging function of the MUSIC can be introduced

\mathfrak{F}_{\de}^{(\varepsilon)}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x}))|}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{F}_{\de}^{(\mu)}(\mathbf{z})=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{P}_{\noise}(\mathbf{f}_{\mu}(\mathbf{x}))|},

(18)

respectively. Here,
\mathbf{f}_{\mu}(\mathbf{z})
is defined in (
11
).

To confirm this applicability in both TM and TE cases, we establish mathematical structure of
\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}(\mathbf{x})
by identifying a relationship with Bessel functions.

Now, we can obtain the following results about the structure of

Theorem 4.3

(TM polarization case)

For sufficiently large

Remark 4.3
.

Based on the identified structure (
), we can observe several properties of the imaging function.

1.

The imaging function
\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}(\mathbf{x})
consists of
J_{1}(k_{\mathrm{b}}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}_{m}|)
. Therefore, as in traditional MUSIC-type imaging, the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}(\mathbf{x})
will contain two peaks of large large magnitude in the neighborhood of
{D}
and many artifacts with small magnitude.

2.

Following
[
41
, Section 3.5]
,
f_{\fm}(\mathbf{x})
in the TE case can be represented as

f_{\fm}(\mathbf{x})=\Big(1-J_{1}(k_{\mathrm{b}}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|)^{2}\Big)^{-1/2}.

Therefore, similar to TM polarization, the obtained result should be poorer than the result obtained via
f_{\fm}(\mathbf{x})
i.e., a result of poor resolution will be obtained if
N
is small.

3.

Since

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}(\mathbf{x})=f_{\fm}(\mathbf{x}),

it can be said that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigg[(1-C_{\mu})^{2}-2N(1-C_{\mu})C_{\mu}\left(\frac{N}{2}-2\right)J_{1}(k_{\mathrm{b}}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|)^{2}+\frac{N^{2}}{2}C_{\mu}^{2}\left(\frac{N}{2}-2\right)^{2}J_{1}(k_{\mathrm{b}}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|)^{2}\bigg]\\
=1-J_{1}(k_{\mathrm{b}}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|)^{2}.

In this case, similar to the TM polarization case,
C_{\mu}=O(N^{-2})
but complete form of the
C_{\mu}
is unknown. Notice that if
C_{\mu}
satisfies

N\left(\frac{N}{2}-2\right)C_{\mu}=(2+\sqrt{2})(1-C_{\mu})\quad\text{or equivalently}\quad C_{\mu}=\frac{4+2\sqrt{2}}{N^{2}-4N+4+2\sqrt{2}}=O\left(\frac{1}{N^{2}}\right),

then similar to the TM polarization case,
\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}(\mathbf{x})
can be written as

\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{1-C_{\mu}}\Big(1-J_{1}(k_{\mathrm{b}}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|)^{2}\Big)^{-1/2}=\left(\frac{N^{2}-4N+4+2\sqrt{2}}{N^{2}-4N}\right)\Big(1-J_{1}(k_{\mathrm{b}}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}|)^{2}\Big)^{-1/2}.

4.

Opposite to the Corollary
4.4
, the unique determination in TE polarization cannot be guaranteed via the map of
\mathfrak{F}_{\dm}(\mathbf{x})
.

6
Conclusion

In this study, we considered the MUSIC algorithm for localizing two-dimensional small inhomogeneity modeled via TM and TE polarization when the diagonal elements of MSR matrix cannot be determined. We investigated a mathematical structure of the imaging functions by establishing a relationship with the Bessel function of order
0
(TM polarization) and
1
(TE polarization). Based on the investigated structures, we confirmed that MUSIC can be applied to retrieve location of small inhomogeneity without the diagonal elements of the MSR matrix in both TM and TE polarizations.

Unfortunately, exact expression of
C_{\mu}
in Theorem
4.6
is still unknown. Derivation of exact structure of the imaging function in TE polarization will be an interesting research subject. In this study, the structures were derived in the presence of single inhomogeneity but MUSIC can be applied to the identification of multiple, small inhomogeneities on the basis of simulation results. Extension to the multiple, small inhomogeneities will be the forthcoming work. Finally, extension to the three-dimensional inverse scattering problem will be an interesting research topic.

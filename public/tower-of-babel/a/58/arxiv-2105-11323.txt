Two-to-one mappings and involutions without fixed points over 𝔽 2 n {\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
Mu Yuan, Dabin Zheng and Yan-Ping Wang

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, two-to-one mappings and involutions without any fixed point on finite fields of even characteristic are investigated. First, we characterize a closed relationship between them by implicit functions and develop an AGW-like criterion for
2
-to-
1
mappings. Using this criterion, some new constructions of
2
-to-
1
mappings are proposed and eight classes of
2
-to-
1
mappings of the form
(x^{2^{k}}+x+\delta)^{s}+cx
are obtained. Finally, a number of classes of involutions without any fixed point are derived from the known
2
-to-
1
mappings by the relation between them.

Keywords.
Finite field, involution, two-to-one mapping, symbolic computation

Introduction

Let
q
be a power of a prime
p

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements. A polynomial
f(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
is called a permutation polynomial if
f:c\mapsto f(c)
from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
to itself is a bijection. In particular,
f
is called an
involution
if the compositional inverse of
f
is itself. If involutions are used as part of a block cipher, they are very suitable for use in devices with limited resources since the implementation of the inverse does not require additional resources. Involutions have been used in block ciphers such as AES
[
1
]
, Khazad, Anubis
[
4
]

and PRINCE
[
5
]
. Furthermore, to resist reflection cryptanalysis, the involutions used in PRINCE-like ciphers should not have fixed points
[
6
,
31
]
. The reference
[
9
]
first provided a systematic study of involutions over finite field of even characteristic and recent results on involutions can be found in
[
10
,
9
,
15
,
28
,
30
,
43
]
.
A polynomial
f(x)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
is said to be a two-to-one (
2
-to-
1
) mapping on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
if the equation
f(x)=a
has either two or no solutions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
for any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Two-to-one mappings over finite fields are used in design special important primitives in symmetric cryptography such as APN functions
[
29
,
34
]
, bent functions
[
8
]
, planar functions
[
11
,
36
]
and good linear codes
[
22
]
in coding theory. For example, Carlet and Mesnager constructed new bent Boolean functions in bivariate representation with

-to-
1
mappings over finite fields of even characteristic
[
8
]
. In addition, the hyperoval in finite geometry can be defined by o-polynomials, which are closed related to 2-to-1 mappings. As far as we know, Charpin and Kyureghyan in
[

,
19
]
first obtained some
2
-to-
1
mappings of the special forms in their study of permutation polynomials by using linear translators. Very recently, Mesnager and Qu in
[

first systematically studied
2
-to-
1
mappings over finite fields as a specific goal, then Li et al.
[

]
further constructed some

-to-
1
trinomials and quadrinomials over finite fields of even characteristic. Almost all

-to-

{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
in
[

,

]
exist only when

In this paper, we first characterize a relationship between

-to-

(1)

2
Preliminaries

Let
q
be a power of
2
,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
the finite field with
q
elements and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
its multiplicative group. Let
{\mathbb{N}}
denote the set of all positive integers.
For
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
d\,|\,(q-1)
, let
\mu_{d}
denote the subgroup of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
with order
d
and
\mu_{d}^{*}=\mu_{d}\setminus\{1\}
.
For
k,\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
\ell\,|\,k
, the trace function
{\rm Tr}^{k}_{\ell}(\cdot)
is defined as

{\rm Tr}^{k}_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k/\ell-1}x^{2^{\ell\cdot i}}.

In particular,
{\rm Tr}^{k}_{1}(x)
is called the absolute trace function defined on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{k}}
, and it is denoted by
{\rm Tr}_{k}(x)
for short.
A positive integer
s
is called a
Niho exponent
with respect to the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{2m}}
if
s\equiv 2^{j}\,\,({\rm mod}\,\,2^{m}-1)
for some

j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Now, we give the definition of 2-to-1 mappings over any finite set.

Definition 2.1

[
27
]

Let
A
and
B
be two finite sets, and let
f
be a mapping from
A
to
B
. Then
f
is called a 2-to-1 mapping if one of the following two cases holds:

(1)

If
\sharp A
is even, then for any
b\in B
, it has either
2
or
0
preimages of
f
;

(2)

If
\sharp A
is odd, then for all but one
b\in B
, it has either
2
or
0
preimages of
f
, and the exception element has exactly one preimage.

Next, we recall the resultant of two polynomials over fields. Let
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n-i}x^{i}
and
g(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}b_{n-i}x^{i}
be two polynomials of positive degree over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
a_{0}\neq 0
and
b_{0}\neq 0
.
The Sylvester resultant of
f(x)
and
g(x)
, denoted by
Res(f,g,x)
, is the following determinant:

\displaystyle Res(f,g,x)=\begin{array}[]{|cccccccc|}a_{0}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{n}&0&&\cdots&0\\
0&a_{0}&a_{1}&\cdots&a_{n}&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&&&&&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&a_{0}&a_{1}&&\cdots&a_{n}\\
b_{0}&b_{1}&\cdots&&b_{m}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&b_{0}&b_{1}&\cdots&&b_{m}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&&&&&&&\vdots\\
0&\cdots&0&b_{0}&b_{1}&&\cdots&b_{m}\\
\end{array}

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{l}\left.\rule{0.0pt}{28.45274pt}\right\}$m$~\text{rows}\\
\\
\left.\rule{0.0pt}{28.45274pt}\right\}$n$~\text{rows}\end{array}

There are two polynomials
A,B\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
such that
Af+Bg=Res(f,g,x)
, and so
Res(f,g,x)=0
if and only if
f(x)
and
g(x)
have a common divisor of positive degree.
For two polynomials
F(x,y),\,G(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x,y]
of positive degree in
y
, the resultant
Res(F,G,y)
with respect to
y
is a polynomial in
x
, and is in the elimination ideal

\langle F,G\rangle\cap{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
. So,
Res(F,G,y)
vanishes at any common solution of
F=G=0
. For more information on resultants, the reader can refer to
[
12
]
.

For later usage we recall some results on the number of the solutions of quadratic and cubic equations over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
.

Lemma 2.2

[
26
]

Let
a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
and
a\neq 0
. Then the quadratic equation
x^{2}+ax+b=0
has solutions in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
if and only if
{\rm Tr}_{n}(\frac{b}{a^{2}})=0
.

Lemma 2.3

[
3
]

Let
a,b\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
and
b\neq 0
. Then the cubic equation
x^{3}+ax+b=0
has a unique solution in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
if and only if
{\rm Tr}_{n}(\frac{a^{3}}{b^{2}}+1)\neq 0
.

Lemma 2.4

[
27
]

Let
f(x)=x^{4}+a_{3}x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}[x]
. Then
f(x)
is
2
-to-
1
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
if and only if one of the following holds:

•

a_{3}=a_{1}=0
,
a_{2}\neq 0
;

•

a_{3}=0
,
a_{1}\neq 0
and
{\rm Tr}_{n}\left(a_{2}^{3}/a_{1}^{2}+1\right)\neq 0
;

•

n
is odd,
a_{3}\neq 0
and
a_{2}^{2}=a_{1}a_{3}
.

3
A relation between
2
-to-
1
mappings and involutions

In this section, a closed relationship between
2
-to-
1
mappings and involutions over finite fields is investigated. This relation is useful for us to obtain
involutions without fixed points from 2-to-1 mappings over finite fields. We also study the relation between two involutions derived from two
2
-to-
1
mappings
which are differed by a bijection.

Theorem 3.1

Let
D
be a subset of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with even cardinality and
\mathcal{D}=\{(x,y)\in D^{2}|\,\,\,\,x\neq y\}
. Let
f
be a
2
-to-
1
mapping on
D
. Then the equation
G(x,y)=f(x)-f(y)=0
on
\mathcal{D}
determines an involution
y=\mathcal{I}_{f}(x)
on
D
. Furthermore,
y=\mathcal{I}_{f}(x)
has no fixed point on
D
.

Proof.
Since
f
is a
2
-to-
1
mapping on
D
, for any
x\in D
, there exists a unique
y\in D
with
y\neq x
satisfying
G(x,y):=f(x)-f(y)=0
, that is, the equation
G(x,y)=0
on
\mathcal{D}
defines a mapping
\mathcal{I}_{f}:x\rightarrow y
from
D
to
D
. Since
D
is a finite set, by Lagrange’s interpolation formula the expression of this mapping can be represented as follows,
\mathcal{I}_{f}(x)=\sum_{a\in D}\mathcal{I}_{f}(a)(1+(x+a)^{q-1})
.
Moreover, for any
a\in D
, there exists
b\in D
with
b\neq a
such that
G(b,a)=0
and
G(a,b)=0
, simultaneously. So
\mathcal{I}_{f}(a)=b
and
\mathcal{I}_{f}(b)=a
. That is to say,
y=\mathcal{I}_{f}(x)
is an involution on
D
without any fixed point.

\Box

Remark 3.2

We call
\mathcal{I}_{f}(x)
the involution derived from a
2
-to-
1
mapping
f
on
D
. The following are two examples.

(1)
Let
L(x)
be a linearized
2
-to-
1
mapping on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}
be a root of
L(x)
. For any
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, the equation
G(x,y)=L(x)+L(y)=L(x+y)=0
has two solutions
y=x

and
y=x+\alpha
, and so the involution derived from
L(x)
is
\mathcal{I}_{L}(x)=x+\alpha
.

(2)
For any APN function
F(x)
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, the derivative
D_{a}F(x)=F(x)+F(x+a)
is a
2
-to-
1
mapping on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
for any
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}^{*}
. Analysis similar to that in (1) shows that the involution derived from
D_{a}F(x)
is
\mathcal{I}_{D_{a}F}(x)=x+a
.

Let
D
and
S
be two finite sets with the same even cardinality, and let
f
be a
2
-to-
1
mapping on
D
and
p
be a bijection from
S
to
D
.
It is easy to see that
y=\mathcal{I}_{f}(x)
is the unique solution of the equation
f(x)-f(y)=0
in
\{(x,y)\in D^{2}|\,\,x\neq y\}
if and only if
y=p^{-1}\circ\mathcal{I}_{f}\circ p(x)
is the unique solution of the equation
f\circ p(x)-f\circ p(y)=0
in
\{(x,y)\in S^{2}|\,\,x\neq y\}
. So, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.3

Let
D
and
S
be two subsets of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with the same even cardinality. Let
f
be a
2
-to-
1
mapping on
D
and
p
be a bijection from
S
to
D
. Then the involution derived from
f
on
D
is
\mathcal{I}_{f}
if and only if the involution derived from
f\circ p
on
S
is
\mathcal{I}_{f\circ p}=p^{-1}\circ\mathcal{I}_{f}\circ p
.

Let
f
be a
2
-to-
1
mapping, and
p
be a injection over
{\rm Im}(f)
. We have the following proposition about the
2
-to-
1
mappings
p\circ f
and
f
.

Proposition 3.4

Let
A,B,C
be three finite sets with
\sharp A=2\sharp B=2\sharp C
,
f
be a
2
-to-
1
mappings from
A
to
B
, and
\bar{f}
be a
2
-to-
1
mapping from
A
to
C
. Then the involutions derived from
f
and
\bar{f}
respectively both are
\mathcal{I}
if and only if there exists a bijection
p
from
B
to
C
such that
\bar{f}=p\circ f
.

Proof.
The sufficiency holds since the equations
f(x)-f(y)=0
and
p\circ f(x)-p\circ f(y)=0
have the same roots in
\{(x,y)\in A^{2}|x\neq y\}
.

Next, we prove the necessity. Assume that the involutions derived from
f
and
\bar{f}
respectively both are
\mathcal{I}
. Since
f
is a
2
-to-
1
mapping over
A
, we have
A=\cup_{b\in B}A_{b}
, where
A_{b}=\{x\in A|f(x)=b\}
and

\sharp A_{b}=2
. Pick one element from each
A_{b}
for
b\in B
to form a set
\bar{A}
. Let
f_{\bar{A}}
and
\bar{f}_{\bar{A}}
denote the mappings of
f
and
\bar{f}
restricted on
\bar{A}
, respectively. By the definition of

\bar{A}
, one can verify that
f_{\bar{A}}
and
\bar{f}_{\bar{A}}
are bijective from
\bar{A}
to
B
and
C
, respectively, and
\mathcal{I}(\bar{A})=A\setminus\bar{A}
.
Similarly,
f
and
\bar{f}
are also bijective from
A\setminus\bar{A}
to
B
and
C
, respectively. Hence,

f
and
\bar{f}
can be rewritten as

f(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &f_{\bar{A}}(x),&&x\in\bar{A};\\
&f_{\bar{A}}(\mathcal{I}(x)),&&x\in A\setminus\bar{A}\end{aligned}\right.{\,\,\text{a}nd\,\,}\bar{f}(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bar{f}_{\bar{A}}(x),&&x\in\bar{A};\\
&\bar{f}_{\bar{A}}(\mathcal{I}(x)),&&x\in A\setminus\bar{A}.\end{aligned}\right.

Thus, there exists a bijection
p=\bar{f}_{\bar{A}}\circ f_{\bar{A}}^{-1}
from
B
to
C
such that
\bar{f}=p\circ f
.

\Box

For a given involution
\mathcal{I}
without fixed points over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, we have
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}=\bigcup_{a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}}S_{a}
, where
S_{a}=\{a,\mathcal{I}(a)\}
. Pick one element from each
S_{a}
for
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
to form a new set
S
.
It is easy to see that

f(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &x,&&x\in S;\\
&\mathcal{I}(x),&&x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\setminus S\end{aligned}\right.

is a
2
-to-
1
mapping over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
and the involution derived from it is
\mathcal{I}
. By Proposition
3.4
, all
2
-to-
1
mappings which derive the involution
\mathcal{I}
can be obtained by composing
f
with a bijection on Im
(f)
. Note that there are
\binom{2^{n}}{2^{n-1}}
subsets in
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
with cardinality
2^{n-1}
. For each such subset
T
, there are
2^{n-1}!
bijections from
S
to
T
. So, we have the following result.

Corollary 3.5

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, for a given involution
\mathcal{I}
without fixed points on
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
, there are
\frac{2^{n}!}{2^{n-1}!}
distinct
2
-to-
1
mappings from which
\mathcal{I}
can be derived.

4
AGW-like criterion for
2
-to-
1
mappings

The AGW criterion
[
2
]
transforms the problem of proving the permutation property of a polynomial on a finite set
A
into showing that another polynomial is a permutation on a subset of
A
.
This technique facilitates the proof and computer experiments of finding new permutation polynomials. Recently, this method has been generalized to construct
2
-to-
1
mappings over
{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}
by Mesnager and Qu as follows.

Lemma 4.1

[
27
]

Let
A
,
S
and
\bar{S}
be finite sets with
\sharp S=\sharp\bar{S}
. Let
f,\bar{f},\lambda,\bar{\lambda}
be four mappings defined as the following diagram such that
\bar{\lambda}\circ f=\bar{f}\circ\lambda
. If
\bar{f}
is a bijection from
S
to
\bar{S}
,
f|_{\lambda^{-1}(s)}
is
2
-to-
1
for any
s\in S
, and there is at most one
s\in S
such that
\sharp\lambda^{-1}(s)
is odd, then
f
is a
2
-to-
1
mapping over
A
.

\begin{CD}A@>{f}>{}>A\\
@V{}V{\lambda}V@V{}V{\bar{\lambda}}V\\
S@>{\bar{f}}>{}>\bar{S}\end{CD}

In Remark 7 of
[
27
]
, authors also wanted to derive
2
-to-
1
mappings
f
on
A
by the assumption that
\bar{f}
was a
2
-to-
1
mapping on
S
and
f|_{\lambda^{-1}(s)}
was injective. But they found that it seems not easy to add a condition such that
f
was a
2
-to-
1
mapping on
A
. Here, we find a restriction on
f
and then propose another AGW-like criterion for constructions of new
2
-to-
1
mappings.

Proposition 4.2

Let
A,\bar{A},S,\bar{S}
be four finite sets and
f,\bar{f},\lambda,\bar{\lambda}
be four surjective mappings defined as the following diagram such that
\bar{\lambda}\circ f=\bar{f}\circ\lambda
. Then
f
is a
2
-to-
1
mapping on
A
if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

\bar{f}
is a
2
-to-
1
mapping from
S
to
\bar{S}
;

(2)

\sharp S
is even, and
f
is bijective from
\lambda^{-1}(s)=\{x\in A|\lambda(x)=s\}
to
\bar{\lambda}^{-1}(\bar{f}(s))=\{x\in\bar{A}|\bar{\lambda}(x)=\bar{f}(s)\}
for any
s\in S
.

\begin{CD}A@>{f}>{}>\bar{A}\\
@V{}V{\lambda}V@V{}V{\bar{\lambda}}V\\
S@>{\bar{f}}>{}>\bar{S}\end{CD}

Proof.
For any
b\in\bar{A}
, let
\bar{s}=\bar{\lambda}(b)
. Since
f
is surjective, there exist some
a\in A
such that
f(a)=b
. From above diagram we have
\bar{\lambda}\circ f(a)=\bar{f}\circ\lambda(a)=\bar{s}
. Since
\bar{f}
is a
2
-to-
1
mapping from
S
to
\bar{S}
, there exist exactly two elements
s_{1},s_{2}
in
S
such that
\bar{f}(s_{1})=\bar{f}(s_{2})=\bar{s}
. Hence, we must have
a\in\lambda^{-1}(s_{1})
or
\lambda^{-1}(s_{2})
. Note that
f
is bijective from
\lambda^{-1}(s_{i})
to
\bar{\lambda}^{-1}(\bar{s})
, where
i=1,2
. Thus,
b
has exactly two preimages
a_{1}\in\lambda^{-1}(s_{1})
and
a_{2}\in\lambda^{-1}(s_{2})
.

\Box

By applying Proposition
4.2
, we can propose some general constructions of
2
-to-
1
mappings as follows.

Theorem 4.3

Let
q=2^{k}
and
k,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let
g,\phi,\psi,\bar{\psi},h\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}[x]
be such that
\phi
and
\psi
are
2
-linearized,
\bar{\psi}
is
q
-linearized,
\phi\circ\psi=\bar{\psi}\circ\phi
, and
h(\psi({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}))\subset{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
. Set
f=h(\psi(x))\phi(x)+g(\psi(x))
. Then
f
is a
2
-to-
1
mapping over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

\bar{f}=h(x)\phi(x)+\bar{\psi}\circ g(x)
is a
2
-to-
1
mapping over
\psi({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}})
;

(2)

ker(\phi)\cap ker(\psi)=\{0\}
and
{\rm dim\,\,}ker(\psi)={\rm dim\,\,}ker(\bar{\psi})
.

Proof.

For
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}\circ f(x)

\displaystyle=\bar{\psi}(h(\psi(x))\phi(x))+\bar{\psi}(g(\psi(x)))

\displaystyle=h(\psi(x))\phi(\psi(x))+\bar{\psi}(g(\psi(x)))

\displaystyle=\bar{f}\circ\psi(x).

The second equality holds since
h(\psi({\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}))\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
,
\bar{\psi}
is
q
-linearized and
\phi\circ\psi=\bar{\psi}\circ\phi

-to-

2
-to-

Proof.
The sufficiency is derived directly from Theorem

. Next, we show the necessity.
Let

\begin{CD}{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}@>{f}>{}>{\mathbb{F}}_{2^{n}}\\

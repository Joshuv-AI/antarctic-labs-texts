Algorithms for Weighted Pushdown Automata
Alexandra Butoi, Brian DuSell, Tim Vieira, Ryan Cotterell David Chiang, [1ex] ETH Zürich

Abstract
Weighted pushdown automata (WPDAs) are at the core of many natural language processing tasks, like syntax-based statistical machine translation and transition-based dependency parsing. As most existing dynamic programming algorithms are designed for context-free grammars (CFGs), algorithms for PDAs often resort to a PDA-to-CFG conversion. In this paper, we develop novel algorithms that operate directly on WPDAs. Our algorithms are inspired by Lang’s algorithm, but use a more general definition of pushdown automaton and either reduce the space requirements by a factor of | Γ | |\Gamma| (the size of the stack alphabet) or reduce the runtime by a factor of more than | Q | |Q| (the number of states). When run on the same class of PDAs as Lang’s algorithm, our algorithm is both more space-efficient by a factor of | Γ | |\Gamma| and more time-efficient by a factor of | Q | ⋅ | Γ | |Q|\cdot|\Gamma| . https://github.com/rycolab/wpda

Introduction

Weighted pushdown automata (WPDAs) are widespread in natural language processing (NLP), primarily in syntactic analysis.
For instance, WPDAs have found use in syntax-based statistical machine translation

2.2
Definition

Our definition of weighted PDA is more general than usual definitions, in order to accommodate the top-down and bottom-up variants introduced in

Definition 3
.

A

(WPDA) over a semiring

\mathcal{P}=(Q,\Sigma,\Gamma,\delta,(\iota,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{I}),(f,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{F}))

•

•

•

•

•

(\iota,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{I})
is called the initial configuration and
(f,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{F})
is called the final configuration, where
\iota,f\in Q

\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{I},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{F}\in\Gamma^{*}

Stacks are represented as strings over
\Gamma

\boldsymbol{\gamma}=X_{1}X_{2}\cdots X_{n}

Definition 4
.

A

is the current state and

The initial and final configurations of a WPDA are examples of configurations; it is possible to generalize the initial and final stacks to (say) regular expressions over

A WPDA moves from configuration to configuration by following transitions of the form

to state

\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{1}\in\Gamma^{*}
from the top of the stack and pushing the sequence
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{2}\in\Gamma^{*}
.

Definition 5
.

If

Definition 7

A

3.3
Unary Removal

The final step in conversion to normal form is removal of
unary
transitions, so called by analogy with unary productions in a CFG.

Definition 17
.

A transition is called
unary
if it is of the form
p,Y\xrightarrow{\varepsilon/w}q,X
.

We assume that
\mathcal{W}
is equipped with a star operation satisfying
a^{*}=\textbf{1}\oplus a\otimes a^{*}=\textbf{1}\oplus a^{*}\otimes a
. If
\mathcal{W}
is continuous, then
a^{*}=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}a^{i}
.

Unary transitions can form cycles that can be traversed an unbounded number of times, which is problematic for a dynamic programming algorithm. Therefore, we precompute the weights of all runs of unary transitions.
Define the matrix
U\in\mathcal{W}^{(Q\times\Gamma)\times(Q\times\Gamma)}
:

\displaystyle U_{pY,qX}=\delta(p,Y\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}q,X)

and form its transitive closure
U^{*}

(
Lehmann 1977
)
. Then
U^{*}_{pY,qX}
is the total weight of all runs of unary transitions from configuration
(p,Y)
to
(q,X)
.

Then we remove all unary transitions and modify every non-unary transition as follows:

\begin{array}[]{@{}ll@{}}\text{For each non-unary:}&\text{Replace with:}\\
p,\boldsymbol{\gamma}\xrightarrow{a/w}q,X&p,\boldsymbol{\gamma}\xrightarrow{a/w\otimes U^{*}_{qX,rY}}r,Y\end{array}

We give details on the complexity of this transformation in
appendix

A
.

In this section, we give dynamic programming algorithms for computing the stringsum of an input string

We give a basic version of the algorithm, which has the same runtime as Lang’s algorithm but improved space requirements, and a fast version that has the same space complexity and runs asymptotically faster. On simple PDAs (for which Lang’s algorithm was designed), the latter version has both improved space and time complexity.

4.2
Correctness

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a WPDA and
\boldsymbol{s}\in\Sigma^{*}
an input string. The weight
{\textbf{w}}[i,p,X,j,q]
is the total weight of all push computations of
\mathcal{P}
of type
\left[i,p,X,j,q\right]
.

Proof.

By induction on the span length,
\ell=j-i
.

Base Case.

Assume that
j-i=1
.
The only push computations from state
p
to
q
that push
X
and scan
\boldsymbol{s}(i\mathord{:}j]
are ones that have the single transition

\tau=p,\varepsilon\xrightarrow{\boldsymbol{s}_{j}/w}q,X
.
There cannot exist others, because normal form requires that any additional non-scanning transitions would decrease the stack height.
So the total weight of all such push computations is
w
, and the algorithm correctly sets
{\textbf{w}}[i,p,X,j,q]=w
at line
7
.

Inductive Step.

Assume that the statement holds for any
spans of length at most
(\ell-1)
and consider a span of length
\ell
. For such spans, the algorithm computes the total weight of all push computations
\boldsymbol{\pi}
of
type
\left[i,p,X,j,q\right]
, for all
X\in\Gamma
,
p,q\in Q
, and
j-i=\ell
.
This weight must be the sum of weights of three types of push computations: those that end with 0-pop transitions, with 1-pop transitions, and with 2-pop transitions.

But ending with a 0-pop transition is impossible, because such push computations must have only one transition and therefore
j-i\leq 1
.
The 1-pop and 2-pop parts of the sum are computed at lines
12
–
14
and
16
–
19
of the algorithm, respectively.

Ending with 1-pop transition.

The algorithm sums over all possible ending transitions
\tau_{\text{end}}=r,Y\xrightarrow{\boldsymbol{s}_{j}/w}q,X
. (Normal form requires that this transition be scanning.)
Let
\Pi
be the set of all push computations of type
\left[i,p,X,j,q\right]
ending in
\tau_{\text{end}}
, and let
\Pi^{\prime}
be the set of all push computations of type
\left[i,p,Y,j-1,r\right]
.
Every push computation in
\Pi
must be of the form
\boldsymbol{\pi}=\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\circ\tau_{\text{end}}
, where
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\in\Pi^{\prime}
, and conversely, for every
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\in\Pi^{\prime}
, we have
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\circ\tau_{\text{end}}\in\Pi
.
By the induction hypothesis, the total weight of
\Pi^{\prime}
was computed in a previous iteration.
Then, by distributivity, we have:

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\pi}\in\Pi}\bigotimes_{\tau\in\boldsymbol{\pi}}\delta(\tau)

\displaystyle=\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\in\Pi^{\prime}}\bigotimes_{\tau\in\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}}\delta\left(\tau\right)\otimes\delta\left(\tau_{\text{end}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\in\Pi^{\prime}}\bigotimes_{\tau\in\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}}\delta\left(\tau\right)\right)\otimes\delta\left(\tau_{\text{end}}\right)

\displaystyle={\textbf{w}}[i,p,Y,j-1,r]\otimes\delta\left(\tau_{\text{end}}\right).

Ending with 2-pop transition.

The algorithm sums over all possible ending transitions
\tau_{\text{end}}=s,YZ\xrightarrow{a/w}q,X,a\in\left\{\boldsymbol{s}_{j},\varepsilon\right\}
.
Every push computation
\boldsymbol{\pi}
that ends with
\tau_{\text{end}}
decomposes uniquely into
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\circ\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime\prime}\circ\tau_{\text{end}}
, where
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}
and
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime\prime}
are push computations of type
\left[i,p,Y,k,r\right]
and
\left[k,r,Z,j-|a|,s\right]
, respectively, for some
k\in[i+1\mathord{:}j-|a|-1]
and
r\in Q
. We call
(k,r)
the
split point
of
\boldsymbol{\pi}
.

The algorithm sums over all split points
(k,r)
.
Let
\Pi
be the set of all push computations of type
\left[i,p,X,j,q\right]
ending in
\tau_{\text{end}}
with split point
(k,r)
, and let
\Pi^{\prime}
and
\Pi^{\prime\prime}
be the sets of all push computations of type
\left[i,p,Y,k,r\right]
and
\left[k,r,Z,j-|a|,s\right]
, respectively.
Every
\boldsymbol{\pi}\in\Pi
must be of the form
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\circ\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime\prime}\circ\tau_{\text{end}}
, where
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\in\Pi^{\prime}
and
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime\prime}\in\Pi^{\prime\prime}
, and conversely, for every
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\in\Pi^{\prime}
and
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime\prime}\in\Pi^{\prime\prime}
,
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime}\circ\boldsymbol{\pi}^{\prime\prime}\circ\tau_{\text{end}}\in\Pi

By the induction hypothesis, the total weight of
\Pi^{\prime}

\Pi^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\pi}\in\Pi}\bigotimes_{\tau\in\boldsymbol{\pi}}\delta\left(\tau\right)={\textbf{w}}[i,p,Y,k,r]

\displaystyle\quad\otimes{\textbf{w}}[k,r,Z,j-|a|,s]\otimes\delta\left(\tau_{\text{end}}\right).

4.3
Stack Automaton

The distribution over possible configurations that
\mathcal{P}
can be in after reading
\boldsymbol{s}(0\mathord{:}m]
can be generated by a weighted finite-state automaton
M
. The states of
M
are of the form
(i,q)
, with start state
(0,s)
and accept states
(m,q)
for all
q\in Q
. There is a transition
(i,q)\xrightarrow{X/w}(j,r)
for every item
\left[i,q,X,j,r\right]
with weight
w
. Then if an accepting run of
M
scans
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
and ends in state
(m,q)
with weight
w
, then
\mathcal{P}
can be in configuration
(q,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
with weight
w
.

5.1
Comparison with Lang’s algorithm

Since top-down PDAs are more standard, and the only direct PDA stringsum algorithm in the literature is Lang’s algorithm, it might have seemed natural to extend Lang’s algorithm to top-down PDAs, as is done by
DuSell and Chiang 2020
. Like Lang’s algorithm, their algorithm has items of the form
\left[i,q,XY,j,r\right]
, but unlike Lang’s algorithm, it requires the
X
in order to support 1-pop, 2-push transitions.
As a result, their algorithm has space complexity
\mathcal{O}(n^{2}|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|^{2})
and time complexity
\mathcal{O}(n^{3}|Q|^{4}|\Gamma|^{3})
, like Lang’s algorithm.
But if they had used our algorithm for top-down WPDAs, using pop computations, they would have saved a factor of
|\Gamma|
space, and because their 1-pop, 2-push transitions never change the popped symbol, they would have also saved a factor of
|Q|\cdot|\Gamma|
time.

Allsums in Bottom-up WPDAs

We can use a notion of push computation similar to
14
to derive a space-efficient algorithm for computing allsums in bottom-up WPDAs. The item
[p,X,q]
stands for runs from state
p
to state
q
that push the symbol
X
on top of the stack while leaving the symbols underneath unchanged.

Definition 19

(Push computation)
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a bottom-up WPDA. A
push computation
of type
[p,X,q]
, where
p,q\in Q
, and
X\in\Gamma
, is a run
\boldsymbol{\pi}=(q_{0},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{0}),\ldots,(q_{n},\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n})
, where
q_{0}=p
,
q_{n}=q
,
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}=\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{0}X
, and for all
i>0
,
|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{i}|\geq|\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{n}|
.

These items closely resemble those used for computing stringsums, but discard the two variables
i,j
that we used for indexing substrings of the input, as we are interested in computing the total weight of runs that scan
any
string.

Definition 20
.

Let
\Pi\left(p,X,q\right)
be the set containing all push computations from state
p
to state
q
that push
X
. The allsum
\mathbf{w}[p,X,q]
is defined as

\mathbf{w}[p,X,q]=\bigoplus_{\boldsymbol{\pi}\in\Pi\left(p,X,q\right)}\mathbf{w}(\boldsymbol{\pi}).

The allsum of a set of push computations can be expressed in terms of other allsums:

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}[p,X,q]=\bigoplus_{\mathclap{a\in\Sigma\cup\left\{\varepsilon\right\}}}\delta(p,\varepsilon\xrightarrow{a}q,X)

\displaystyle\oplus\bigoplus_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in\Gamma\\
r\in Q\\
a\in\Sigma\cup\left\{\varepsilon\right\}\end{subarray}}}\mathbf{w}[p,Y,r]\otimes\delta(r,Y\xrightarrow{a}q,X)

\displaystyle\oplus\bigoplus_{\mathclap{\begin{subarray}{c}Y,Z\in\Gamma\\
r,s\in Q\\
a\in\Sigma\cup\left\{\varepsilon\right\}\end{subarray}}}\mathbf{w}[p,Y,r]\otimes\mathbf{w}[r,Z,s]\otimes\delta(s,YZ\xrightarrow{a}q,X)

In general, allsums cannot be computed recursively, as
\mathbf{w}[p,X,q]
may rely on allsums that are yet to be computed. Instead, we assume that
\mathcal{W}
is continuous and solve the system of nonlinear equations using fixed-point iteration or the semiring generalization of Newton’s method
(
Esparza et al. 2007
)
.

This algorithm computes
\mathcal{O}(|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|)
items. An allsum algorithm based on Lang’s algorithm would have computed
\mathcal{O}(|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|^{2})
items; thus we have reduced the space complexity by a factor of
|\Gamma|
.

7
Conclusion

Our study has contributed several results and algorithms whose weighted CFG analogues have long been known, but have previously been missing for weighted PDAs—a normal form analogous to Chomsky normal form and a stringsum algorithm analogous to weighted CKY. But it has also revealed some important differences, confirming that the study of weighted PDAs is of interest in its own right. Most notably, we identified two different normal forms and two corresponding stringsum algorithms (and two allsum algorithms). Since the only existing PDA stringsum algorithm we are aware of, Lang’s algorithm, is better suited to bottom-up PDAs, whereas the more standard definition of PDAs is of top-down PDAs, our algorithm for top-down WPDAs fills a significant gap.

Limitations

Removal of nullary transitions, while similar to removal of nullary rules from a WCFG, is conceptually more complicated, and while it has the same asymptotic complexity, it’s currently unknown how the two would compare in practice. Additionally, our nullary removal construction requires a commutative semiring, while removal of nullary productions from a WCFG does not.

Our algorithm for top-down WPDAs processes a string from right to left, which may be undesirable in some NLP applications and in models of human sentence processing.

Details of Unary Removal

Since
U
is a
|Q|\times|\Gamma|
matrix, computing its transitive closure takes
\mathcal{O}((|Q||\Gamma|)^{3})
time.
However, if we perform nullary removal first, the stack alphabet could grow by a factor of
|Q|^{2}
because of the stack symbols
\prescript{}{rs}{Z}
, which would seem to make the transitive closure take
\mathcal{O}((|Q|^{3}|\Gamma|)^{3})
time.

For comparison, if we converted the PDA to a CFG, it would have
\mathcal{O}(|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|)
nonterminals, so computing the transitive closure of the unary rules would take
\mathcal{O}((|Q|^{2}|\Gamma|)^{3})
time.

But the graph formed by the unary transitions can be decomposed into several strongly connected components (SCCs), many of which are identical, so the transitive closure can be sped up considerably.
Define three matrices for three different forms of unary transitions:

\displaystyle U^{1}_{ptZ,qsX}

\displaystyle=\delta(p,\prescript{}{rt}{Z}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}q,\prescript{}{rs}{X})

\displaystyle U^{2}_{qpX,qX}

There are no transitions of the form

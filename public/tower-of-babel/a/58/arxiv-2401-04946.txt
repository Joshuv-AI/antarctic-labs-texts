Abstract
We investigate a second-order accurate time-stepping scheme for solving a time-fractional diffusion equation with a Caputo derivative of order α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) . The basic idea of our scheme is based on local integration followed by linear interpolation. It reduces to the standard Crank–Nicolson scheme in the classical diffusion case, that is, as α → 1 \alpha\to 1 . Using a novel approach, we show that the proposed scheme is α \alpha -robust and second-order accurate in the L 2 ​ ( L 2 ) L^{2}(L^{2}) -norm, assuming a suitable time-graded mesh. For completeness, we use the Galerkin finite element method for the spatial discretization and discuss the error analysis under reasonable regularity assumptions on the given data. Some numerical results are presented at the end.



1
Introduction

We shall approximate the solution of the time-fractional diffusion equation

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u({\boldsymbol{x}},t)+\mathcal{A}u({\boldsymbol{x}},t)=f({\boldsymbol{x}},t)\quad\text{for $({\boldsymbol{x}},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T]$,}

(1.1)

subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions, that is,
u({\boldsymbol{x}},t)=0

on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T]
, with
u({\boldsymbol{x}},0)=u_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})
at the initial
time level
t=0.
The spatial domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

(with
d=1
,
2
,
3
) is a convex polyhedron,
0<\alpha<1
, the time
fractional Caputo derivative

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v(t):=\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}v^{\prime}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)v^{\prime}(s)\,ds,\quad{\rm with}\quad\omega_{1-\alpha}(t):=\frac{t^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)},

where
v^{\prime}=\partial v/\partial t
and
\Gamma
denotes the gamma function. We use the notation
\mathcal{I}v(t)
for the standard time integral of
v
from
0
to
t.
In (
1.1
),
{\mathcal{A}}
is an elliptic operator in the spatial variables, defined by
{\mathcal{A}}w({\boldsymbol{x}})=-\nabla\cdot(\kappa({\boldsymbol{x}})\nabla w)({\boldsymbol{x}})
. The diffusivity
\kappa\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfies

0<\kappa_{\min}\leq\kappa
on
\Omega,
for some constant
\kappa_{\min}
. For the error analysis, we also require that

\kappa\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
.

The presence of the nonlocal time fractional (Caputo) derivative
in (
1.1
) and the fact that the solution
u
suffers from a weak
singularity near
t=0
have a direct impact on the accuracy, and consequently
the convergence rates, of numerical methods. To overcome this difficulty,
different approaches have been applied including corrections, graded meshes,
and convolution quadrature
[
6
,
8
,
14
,
25
,
30
,
31
]
. Indeed, the numerical
solutions for model problems of the form (
1.1
), including

a priori
and
a posteriori
error analyses and fast algorithms,
were studied by various authors over the past fifteen years using multiple
approaches
[
1
,
3
,
5
,
7
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
18
,
28
,
32
,
33
,
34
,
36
,
37
]
. For more references and details, see the
recent monograph by Jin and Zhou
[
11
]
.

In this work, we investigate rigorously the error from approximating the solution of the initial-boundary value problem (
1.1
) using a uniform second-order accurate time-stepping method. The latter is defined via a local time-integration of problem (
1.1
) on each subinterval of the time mesh combined with continuous piecewise linear interpolation. The proposed scheme is identical to the piecewise-linear case of a discontinuous Petrov–Galerkin method proposed in
[
22
]
. Therein, with
\tau
being the maximum time mesh step size, a suboptimal convergence rate of order
O(\tau^{(3-\alpha)/2})
was proved. A time-graded mesh (
2.1
) was employed to compensate for the singular behaviour of the continuous solution at
t=0
. In the limiting case as
\alpha\to 1
, the problem (
1.1
) reduces to the classical diffusion equation, and the considered numerical scheme reduces to the classical Crank–Nicolson method. In this case,
O(\tau^{(3-\alpha)/2})
reduces to
O(\tau)
which is far from the optimal
O(\tau^{2})
rate achieved in practice.

By using an innovative approach that relies on interesting implicit polynomial interpolations and duality arguments, we show
O(\tau^{2})
convergence, whilst at the same time relaxing the imposed regularity assumptions from the earlier
analysis
[
22
]
. This convergence rate is
\alpha
-robust in the sense that the constant in the error bound remains bounded as
\alpha\to 1
. Implementation wise, although the proposed scheme is uniformly second-order accurate, the computational cost is comparable to the well-known backward Euler or
L1

[
16
,
25
,
29
]
methods, which are not even first-order accurate.

For completeness, we discretize the problem (
1.1
) over the spatial domain
\Omega
using the standard Galerkin finite element method (FEM), thereby defining a fully discrete approximation to
u
. An additional error of order
O(h^{2})
is anticipated under certain regularity assumptions on the continuous solution, where
h
is the maximum spatial finite element mesh size. This is proved via a concise approach that relies on the discrete version of the earlier error analysis. To make this feasible, the solution of the semidiscrete Galerkin finite element solution of problem (
1.1
) plays the role of the comparison function.

2
Time-stepping scheme

This section is devoted to discretizing the model problem (
1.1
) over
the time interval
[0,T]
through a second-order accurate method, and stating
our main convergence results. We begin by introducing some notations.

For
\ell\geq 0,
the norm on
H^{\ell}(\Omega)
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{\ell}
.
The Sobolev spaces
H^{\ell}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
are defined as usual,
and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{r}(\Omega)}
in the (fractional-order) Sobolev
space
\dot{H}^{r}(\Omega)
is defined in the usual way via the Dirichlet
eigenfunctions of the self-adjoint elliptic operator
\mathcal{A}
on
\Omega
.
The inner product in
L^{2}(\Omega)
is denoted by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, and the
associated norm by
\|\cdot\|
. The generic constant
C
remains bounded for
0<\alpha\leq 1,
and is independent of the time mesh and the finite element mesh, but may depend on

\Omega
,
T
, and other quantities, including
\kappa
,
u_{0}
and
f
.

Define the time mesh
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<t_{2}<\ldots<t_{N}=T
by

t_{n}=(n\,\tau)^{\gamma},\quad\text{with $\tau=T^{1/\gamma}/N$ and $\gamma\geq 1$,}\quad\text{for $0\leq n\leq N$,}

(2.1)

and let
\tau_{n}=t_{n}-t_{n-1}
. Such a time-graded mesh has the
properties
[
19
]

t_{n}\leq 2^{\gamma}t_{n-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma\tau t_{n-1}^{1-1/\gamma}\leq\tau_{n}\leq\gamma\tau t_{n}^{1-1/\gamma},\quad\text{for $n\geq 2$.}

(2.2)

Integrating problem (
1.1
) over
I_{n}:=(t_{n-1},t_{n})
and then dividing by
\tau_{n}

yields

\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{I_{n}}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\,dt+\mathcal{A}\bar{u}_{n}=\bar{f}_{n},\quad{\rm for}~~1\leq n\leq N,

(2.3)

where
\bar{f}_{n}=\tau_{n}^{-1}\int_{I_{n}}f(t)\,dt
denotes the average value of a function
f
over the time interval
I_{n}
. Motivated by (
2.3
), for
t\in I_{n}
and for
1\leq n\leq N,
our semidiscrete approximate solution
U(t)\approx u(t
is defined by requiring that

U(t)=\frac{t_{n}-t}{\tau_{n}}U^{n-1}+\frac{t-t_{n-1}}{\tau_{n}}U^{n},\quad U^{n}:=U(t_{n}),

with

\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{I_{n}}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}U\,dt+\mathcal{A}U^{n-1/2}=\bar{f}_{n},\quad{\rm with}~~U^{0}=U(0)=u_{0},

(2.4)

where
U^{n-1/2}=\bar{U}_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}(U^{n}+U^{n-1}).

At the
n
th time step, with
\tau_{n,\alpha}=\Gamma(3-\alpha)\tau_{n}^{\alpha}
, we have to solve an elliptic problem
for
W^{n}=U^{n}-U^{n-1}
, namely,

(I+\tfrac{1}{2}\tau_{n,\alpha}\mathcal{A})W^{n}=\tau_{n,\alpha}\bar{f}_{n}-\tau_{n,\alpha}\mathcal{A}U^{n-1}-\tau_{n,\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(\tau_{n}\tau_{j})^{-1}b_{n,j}W^{j},

where
b_{n,j}=a_{n,j}-a_{n,j+1},
with

a_{n,j}=\omega_{3-\alpha}(t_{n}-t_{j-1})-\omega_{3-\alpha}(t_{n-1}-t_{j-1}).

If
\alpha\to 1
, then

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u\to u^{\prime}
and
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}U\to U^{\prime}
, implying that our scheme reduces to the Crank–Nicolson scheme for the classical diffusion equation.

Our convergence analysis relies on decomposing the error
as

\eta=u-U=\psi-\theta\quad\text{with}\quad\psi=u-\widehat{u}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta=U-\widehat{u},

(2.5)

where
\widehat{u}
is a continuous piecewise-linear function in time satisfying

\int_{I_{n}}\widehat{u}(t)\,dt=\int_{I_{n}}u(t)\,dt\quad\text{for $1\leq n\leq N$,}\quad\text{with $\widehat{u}(0)=\widehat{u}^{0}=u_{0}$.}

(2.6)

Alternatively,
\widehat{u}
can be defined via
\mathcal{I}\widehat{u}(t_{n})=\mathcal{I}u(t_{n})
for
1\leq n\leq N
, with
\widehat{u}(0)=u_{0}
, and we say that

\widehat{u}
interpolates
u

implicitly
. The
decomposition (
2.5
) of the error
\eta
follows a
well-known pattern, but the novel choice of the piecewise linear
function
\widehat{u}
makes possible our improved error analysis under reasonable regularity assumption.
The continuous average of
u
equals both the continuous and the discrete
average of
\widehat{u}
on each time subinterval
I_{n}
. For comparison,
let
u_{I}
denote the usual continuous piecewise-linear interpolant to
u
, that
is,

u_{I}(t)=\frac{t_{n}-t}{\tau_{n}}\,u(t_{n-1})+\frac{t-t_{n-1}}{\tau_{n}}u(t_{n})\quad\text{for $t\in I_{n}$,}

(2.7)

and observe that
u_{I}
and
u
have the same
discrete

average
\tfrac{1}{2}(u(t_{n})+u(t_{n-1}))
on each
I_{n}
, but their continuous averages
will differ unless
u
is linear on
I_{n}
.

Subtracting (
2.4
) from (
2.3
) and using (
2.6
), we obtain

\int_{I_{n}}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(\psi-\theta)\,dt-\int_{I_{n}}\mathcal{A}\theta\,dt=0.

Taking the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-inner product with a test
function
\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and applying the divergence theorem, it follows
that

\int_{I_{n}}\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\theta^{\prime},\varphi\rangle\,dt+\int_{I_{n}}\langle\kappa\nabla\theta,\nabla\varphi\rangle\,dt=\int_{I_{n}}\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\varphi\rangle\,dt.

(2.8)

Choosing
\varphi=\theta^{\prime}
and summing over
n
yields

\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n})+\mathcal{I}(\langle\kappa\nabla\theta,\nabla\theta^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n})=\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n}).

Since
\mathcal{I}(\langle\kappa\nabla\theta,\nabla\theta^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\|\sqrt{\kappa}\nabla\theta(t_{n})\|^{2}-\|\sqrt{\kappa}\nabla\theta(0)\|^{2})=\tfrac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\kappa}\nabla\theta(t_{n})\|^{2}
,

\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n})\leq\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n}).

(2.9)

To proceed in our analysis, we make use of the following technical lemma.
For the proof, we refer to Mustapha and Schötzau
[
23
, Lemma 3.1
(iii)]
.

Lemma 2.1

For
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}v,w\rangle)(t)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\Big(\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}v,v\rangle)(t)\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}w,w\rangle)(t)\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}v,v\rangle)(t)+\frac{1}{4\epsilon\alpha^{2}}\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}w,w\rangle)(t).

For later use, by expanding
\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}(v+w),v+w)\rangle
then applying Lemma
2.1
with
\epsilon=1/(2\alpha)
we deduce the inequality in the next lemma.

Lemma 2.2

For
0<\alpha\leq 1
,

\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}(v+w),v+w\rangle)(t)\leq(1+\alpha^{-1})\Big(\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}v,v\rangle)(t)+\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}w,w\rangle)(t)\Big).

We now apply Lemma
2.1
to the right-hand side
of (
2.9
) with
\epsilon=1/(2\alpha^{2})
. Multiplying
through by
2
, and then cancelling the similar terms, leads to the estimate below that will be used later in our convergence analysis.

\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\theta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n})\leq\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n}),

(2.10)

Under reasonable regularity assumptions, a novel error analysis involving implicit interpolations and a duality argument leads to the convergence results in the next theorem. With
J=(0,T)
, an optimal

-rate of
convergence is achieved in the

Our numerical results illustrate this in the stronger

The developed error analysis requires the following regularity property

[
10

,

[

[

t\|u^{\prime}(t)\|+t^{2}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t)\|+t^{3}\|u^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)\|\leq Ct^{\sigma}\quad\text{for $t>0$.}

(2.11)

For example, if
f\equiv 0
and

with

, then (

holds true for

.

For a given time interval

\|w\|_{Q}=\sup_{t\in Q}\|w(t)\|

Let

The desired estimate follows from Lemma

3
Errors from implicit interpolations

In preparation for our convergence analysis, we now study the error from
approximating
u
by
\widehat{u}
, and proceed to estimate
\|\psi\|
and

\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\rangle)
. These estimates assume that the
regularity property (
2.11
) holds. For ease of reference, we
here introduce the parameter
\delta=\sigma-\frac{2}{\gamma}

which will subsequently appear repeatedly. We start this section with the following
representation of the implicit interpolation error in the approximation
u\approx\widehat{u}
at
t=t_{n}
.

Lemma 3.1

For
1\leq n\leq N
,
\psi^{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+j+1}\Delta_{j}
where

\Delta_{j}=\frac{2}{\tau_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}(u-u_{I})\,dt=-\frac{1}{\tau_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}(t_{j}-t)(t-t_{j-1})u^{\prime\prime}(t)\,dt.

Proof.

Since
\int_{I_{n}}u_{I}\,dt=\tfrac{1}{2}\tau_{n}(u^{n}+u^{n-1})
, we find using (
2.6
) that

\psi^{n}=-\psi^{n-1}-\frac{2}{\tau_{n}}\int_{I_{n}}(u-u_{I})\,dt.

The formula for
\psi^{n}
then follows by induction on
n
, after noting that
\psi^{0}=0
.
Recalling the Peano kernel for the trapezoidal rule, we see that

\int_{I_{j}}(u-u_{I})\,dt=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{I_{j}}(t_{j}-s)(s-t_{j-1})u^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds,

implying the second expression for
\Delta_{j}
.

\quad\Box

Lemma 3.2

For
n\geq 1
,

\|\psi^{n}\|\leq\int_{I_{1}}t\|u^{\prime\prime}(t)\|\,dt+\frac{1}{12}\biggl(\tau_{2}^{2}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t_{1})\|+2\tau_{n}^{2}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t_{n})\|+3\sum_{j=2}^{n}\tau_{j}^{2}\int_{I_{j}}\|u^{\prime\prime\prime}\|\,dt\biggr)

and
\|\psi(t)\|\leq\|u(t)-u_{I}(t)\|+\max\bigl(\|\psi^{n}\|,\|\psi^{n-1}\|\bigr)
for
t\in I_{n}
.

Proof.

For
t\in I_{j}
, we have the identity

-u^{\prime\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{t_{j}}u^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t}u^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\,ds-(u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})+u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j-1}))/2.

Multiply both sides by
(t_{j}-t)(t-t_{j-1})
and integrate to obtain

\Delta_{j}=-\frac{\tau_{j}^{2}}{12}[u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})+u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j-1})]+R_{j},

where

R_{j}=\frac{1}{2\tau_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}(t_{j}-t)(t-t_{j-1})\biggl(\int_{t}^{t_{j}}u^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\,ds-\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t}u^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)\,ds\biggr)\,dt.

Thus, by Lemma
3.1
,

(-1)^{n}\psi^{n}=\Delta_{1}+\frac{1}{12}\sum_{j=2}^{n}(-1)^{j}\tau_{j}^{2}[u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})+u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j-1})]-\sum_{j=2}^{n}(-1)^{j}R_{j}.

Shifting the summation index, so

\sum_{j=2}^{n}(-1)^{j}\tau_{j}^{2}u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j-1})=\tau_{2}^{2}u^{\prime\prime}(t_{1})-\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}\tau_{j+1}^{2}u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j}).

Since
\Delta_{1}=-\tau_{1}^{-1}\int_{I_{1}}(t_{1}-s)su^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds
and since
\|R_{j}\|\leq\frac{\tau_{j}^{2}}{6}\int_{I_{j}}\|u^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)\|\,dt,

\displaystyle\|\psi^{n}\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I_{1}}t\|u^{\prime\prime}(t)\|\,dt+\frac{1}{12}\biggl(\tau_{2}^{2}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t_{1})\|+\tau_{n}^{2}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t_{n})\|

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}(\tau_{j+1}^{2}-\tau_{j}^{2})\|u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})\|+2\sum_{j=2}^{n}\tau_{j}^{2}\int_{I_{j}}\|u^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)\|\,dt\biggr).

Using

\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}\tau_{j+1}^{2}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})\|=\sum_{j=3}^{n}\tau_{j}^{2}\|u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j-1})\|\leq\sum_{j=3}^{n}\tau_{j}^{2}\Big(\|u^{\prime\prime}(t_{j})\|+\int_{I_{j}}\|u^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)\|\,dt\Big),

and the bound for
\|\psi^{n}\|
follows after canceling the common terms.

The interpolant
\psi_{I}
, defined as in (
2.7
), satisfies
\psi_{I}=u_{I}-\widehat{u}
, leading to the
representation

\psi=u-\widehat{u}=\psi_{I}+u-u_{I},

(3.1)

which implies the bound for
\|\psi\|
because

\|\psi_{I}\|\leq\max\bigl(\|\psi^{n}\|,\|\psi^{n-1}\|\bigr)
on
I_{n}
.

\quad\Box

Corollary 3.1

Under the regularity assumption
in (
2.11
) and for a time mesh of the
form (
2.1
) with grading parameter
\gamma\geq 1
we have,
for
n\geq 1
,

\|\psi^{n}\|\leq\|\psi\|_{I_{n}}\leq C\times\begin{cases}\tau^{2}\log(t_{n}/t_{1}),&\text{if $\gamma=2/\sigma$,}\\
\tau^{\min(\gamma\sigma,2)}t_{n}^{\max(0,\delta)},&\text{if $\gamma\neq 2/\sigma$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

First we show that for
\gamma\geq 1,

\|u-u_{I}\|_{I_{n}}\leq C\tau^{\min(\gamma\sigma,2)}t_{n}^{\max(0,\delta)}.

(3.2)

Since the interpolation
error
u-u_{I}
vanishes if
u
is a polynomial of degree
1
, by computing the
Peano kernel one finds that for
t\in I_{n}
,

u(t)-u_{I}(t)=-\frac{t_{n}-t}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t}(s-t_{n-1})u^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds-\frac{t-t_{n-1}}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)u^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds.

(3.3)

The right-side is bounded by

\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t}(s-t_{n-1})\|u^{\prime\prime}(s)\|\,ds+\int_{t}^{t_{n}}(t-t_{n-1})\|u^{\prime\prime}(s)\|\,ds\leq\int_{I_{n}}(s-t_{n-1})\|u^{\prime\prime}(s)\|\,ds

and so, by using the time mesh properties in (
2.2
), we get

\|u-u_{I}\|_{I_{n}}\leq C\tau_{n}t_{n}^{-1}\int_{I_{n}}t\|u^{\prime\prime}(t)\|\,dt\leq C\tau_{n}t_{n}^{-1}\int_{I_{n}}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt\leq C\tau_{n}^{2}t_{n}^{\sigma-2},~~n\geq 1.

Since
\tau_{n}\leq C\tau t_{n}^{1-1/\gamma}
, the proof of (
3.2
) is completed after noting that

\tau_{n}^{2}t_{n}^{\sigma-2}\leq C\tau^{2}t_{n}^{\delta}\leq C\tau^{2}\max(t_{n}^{\delta},t_{1}^{\delta})\leq C\tau^{\min(\gamma\sigma,2)}t_{n}^{\max(0,\delta)}.

(3.4)

Turning to the estimate for
\psi^{n}
, Lemma
3.2
and (
2.11
) imply that

\|\psi^{n}\|\leq C\int_{I_{1}}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt+C\tau_{2}^{2}t_{1}^{\sigma-2}+C\tau_{n}^{2}t_{n}^{\sigma-2}+C\sum_{j=2}^{n}\tau_{j}^{2}\int_{I_{j}}t^{\sigma-3}\,dt,

for
1\leq n\leq N.
Since
t_{1}=\tau^{\gamma}
and
\tau_{2}\leq 2^{\gamma}\tau^{\gamma},

\int_{I_{1}}t^{\sigma-1}\,dt+\tau_{2}^{2}t_{1}^{\sigma-2}\leq C\tau^{\gamma\sigma},

and we again bound
\tau_{n}^{2}t_{n}^{\sigma-2}
using (
3.4
).
For the sum over
j
,

\sum_{j=2}^{n}\tau_{j}^{2}\int_{I_{j}}t^{\sigma-3}\,dt\leq C\sum_{j=2}^{n}\tau^{2}t_{j}^{2-2/\gamma}\int_{I_{j}}t^{\sigma-3}\,dt\leq C\tau^{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{n}}t^{\delta-1}\,dt,

and the estimate for
\|\psi^{n}\|
follows.

\quad\Box

The next target is to estimate

\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n})
. Preceding this, we need to
bound
\|\psi^{\prime}\|
in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.3

We have
\|\psi^{\prime}(t)\|\leq t^{\sigma-1}
for
t\in I_{1}
. Moreover,

\|\psi^{\prime}(t)\|\leq C\tau^{2}\tau_{n}^{-1}t_{n}^{\delta}
for
t\in I_{n}
with
n\geq 2,
and for
\delta>0
.

Proof.

Differentiating (
3.1
) and (
3.3
), we see
for
t\in I_{n}
that

\psi^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t}(s-t_{n-1})u^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds-\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{t}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)u^{\prime\prime}(s)\,ds+\tau_{n}^{-1}(\psi^{n}-\psi^{n-1}).

Thus, if
t\in I_{1}
then, by (
2.11
), Corollary
3.1
, and the fact that
\psi^{0}=0,

\|\psi^{\prime}(t)\|\leq C\tau^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}s^{\sigma-1}\,ds+C\tau_{1}^{-1}\int_{t}^{t_{1}}(t_{1}-s)s^{\sigma-2}\,ds+C\tau_{1}^{\sigma-1}\leq Ct^{\sigma-1}\,.

If
\delta>0
,
n\geq 2
and
t\in I_{n}

then, recalling (
3.4
) and using again (
2.11
),

\tau_{n}\|\psi^{\prime}(t)\|\leq C\tau_{n}\int_{I_{n}}t^{\sigma-2}\,dt+C\tau^{2}t_{n}^{\delta}\leq C\tau_{n}^{2}t_{n}^{\sigma-2}+C\tau^{2}t_{n}^{\delta}\leq C\tau^{2}t_{n}^{\delta},

showing that
\|\psi^{\prime}(t)\|\leq C\tau^{2}\tau_{n}^{-1}t_{n}^{\delta}
.

\quad\Box

Lemma 3.4

Assume
\sigma>\alpha/2
. Then

\big|\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n})\big|\leq C\tau^{3-\alpha}
for
n\geq 1
, provided that
\gamma>\max\{2/\sigma,(3-\alpha)/(2\sigma-\alpha)\}
.

Proof.

For
n=1
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Lemma
3.3
, and the assumption
\sigma>\alpha/2
give

\Big|\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{1})\Big|\leq C\int_{0}^{t_{1}}t^{\sigma-1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\alpha}s^{\sigma-1}\,ds\,dt\leq C\tau_{1}^{2\sigma-\alpha}.

(3.5)

To deal with the case
n\geq 2
, we make the splitting

\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime}=T_{1}+T_{2}
where

T_{1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t_{j-1}}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)\psi^{\prime}(s)\,ds\quad\text{and}\quad T_{2}(t)=\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)\psi^{\prime}(s)\,ds

for
t\in I_{j}
and
j\geq 2
. Using Lemma
3.3
and
(
2.2
), we observe that

\|T_{2}(t)\|\leq C\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)\,\frac{\tau^{2}s^{\delta}}{\tau_{j}}\,ds\leq C\frac{\tau^{2}t^{\delta}}{\tau_{j}}\omega_{2-\alpha}(t-t_{j-1})\leq C\tau^{2}t^{\delta}\tau_{j}^{-\alpha}.

For estimating
T_{1}(t)
, integrate by parts recalling
\psi^{0}=0
,

T_{1}(t)=\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-t_{j-1})\psi^{j-1}+\int_{0}^{t_{j-1}}\omega_{-\alpha}(t-s)\psi(s)\,ds,

where
\omega_{-\alpha}(t)=\omega_{1-\alpha}^{\prime}(t)=-\alpha t^{-\alpha-1}/\Gamma(1-\alpha)
.
We apply Corollary
3.1
to conclude that

\|T_{1}(t)\|\leq C\tau^{2}t_{j-1}^{\delta}\biggl(\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-t_{j-1})-\int_{0}^{t_{j-1}}\omega_{-\alpha}(t-s)\,ds\biggr)\leq C\tau^{2}t_{j}^{\delta}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-t_{j-1}).

Lemma
3.3
and above estimates for
\|T_{1}\|
and
\|T_{2}\|
yield

\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{I_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}\bigl(\|T_{1}\|+\|T_{2}\|\bigr)\,dt\leq C(\tau^{2}t_{j}^{\delta}\tau_{j}^{-1})\tau^{2}t_{j}^{\delta}(\tau_{j}^{1-\alpha})\leq\tau^{4}t_{j}^{2\delta}\tau_{j}^{-\alpha},~~{\rm for}~~j\geq 2\,.

By using this and (
3.5
),
and noting that
\gamma(2\sigma-\alpha)>3-\alpha
, we reach

\displaystyle\Big|\mathcal{I}(

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\rangle)(t_{n})\Big|\leq\biggl|\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\rangle\,dt\biggr|+\biggl|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{n}}\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\rangle\,dt\biggr|

\displaystyle\leq C\tau^{3-\alpha}+\sum_{j=2}^{n}\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{I_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}\bigl(\|T_{1}\|+\|T_{2}\|\bigr)\,dt\leq C\tau^{3-\alpha}+C\sum_{j=2}^{n}\tau^{4}t_{j}^{2\delta}\tau_{j}^{-\alpha},

for
j\geq 2.
By (
2.2
), if
j\geq 2
then

\tau^{1+\alpha}\leq C\tau_{j}^{1+\alpha}t_{j}^{-(1+\alpha)(1-1/\gamma)}
so

\tau^{1+\alpha}\sum_{j=2}^{n}t_{j}^{2\delta}\tau_{j}^{-\alpha}\leq C\sum_{j=2}^{n}t_{j}^{2\delta-(1+\alpha)(1-1/\gamma)}\tau_{j}\leq C\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{n}}t^{2\sigma-\alpha-(3-\alpha)/\gamma-1}\,dt\leq C,

and therefore the desired bound holds.

\quad\Box

4
Errors from the time discretizations

This section is devoted to estimating the error
\eta=u-U
from the time
discretization in the norm of
L^{2}(J;L^{2}(\Omega))
. To achieve an
optimal convergence rate, we employ a duality argument in addition to the usage of the time graded meshes. By reversing the order
of integration, we find that

\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}v,w\rangle)(T)=\mathcal{I}(\langle v,\mathcal{J}_{T}^{\alpha}w\rangle)(T)\quad\text{where}\quad(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{\alpha}w)(t)=\int_{t}^{T}\omega_{\alpha}(s-t)w(t)\,ds.

Using
\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v,w\rangle)(T)=\mathcal{I}(\langle v^{\prime},\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}w\rangle)(T)
and integrating by parts yield

\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v,w\rangle)(T)=-\langle v(0),(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}w)(0)\rangle-\mathcal{I}(\langle v,(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}w)^{\prime}\rangle)(T),

(4.1)

and since
\partial_{t}^{\alpha}v=\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}v^{\prime}=(\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}v)^{\prime}-v(0)\omega_{1-\alpha}
,

\mathcal{I}(\langle(\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}v)^{\prime},w\rangle)(T)=-\mathcal{I}(\langle v,(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}w)^{\prime}\rangle)(T).

(4.2)

We remark that Zhang et al.
[
35
, Equation (89)]
have
recently exploited this dual operator
w\mapsto-(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}w)^{\prime}
in the
error analysis of a discontinuous Galerkin scheme for (
1.1
).

Suppose that
\varphi
satisfies the final-value problem

-(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}+\mathcal{A}\varphi=\eta(t)\quad\text{for $0<t<T$,}\quad\text{with $\varphi(T)=0$,}

(4.3)

subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. Let

y(t)=\varphi(0)+\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(s)\,ds
so that
y
solves the initial-value
problem

y^{\prime}=\varphi\quad\text{for~$0<t<T$,}\quad\text{with $y(0)=\varphi(0)$,}

(4.4)

and with
y_{I}
denotes the continuous piecewise-linear function that
interpolates
y
at the time levels
t_{j}
, put

Y=y-y_{I}.

(4.5)

Lemma 4.1

With the notation above,
\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\eta^{\prime}+\mathcal{A}\eta\rangle)(T).

Proof.

Using (
4.1
), (
4.3
),
\eta(0)=0
and (
4.4
),

\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}=\mathcal{I}(\langle-(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}+\mathcal{A}\varphi,\eta\rangle)(T)=\mathcal{I}(\langle y^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\eta^{\prime}+\mathcal{A}\eta\rangle)(T).

At the same time, (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) imply that

\frac{1}{\tau_{n}}\int_{I_{n}}\bigl(\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\eta+\mathcal{A}\eta\bigr)\,dt=0\quad\text{so}\quad\int_{I_{n}}\langle y_{I}^{\prime},\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\eta+\mathcal{A}\eta\rangle\,dt=0,

because
y_{I}^{\prime}
is constant on
I_{n}
. Since
Y^{\prime}=y^{\prime}-y_{I}^{\prime}
, the inequality
follows at once.

\quad\Box

We will show below in Theorem
4.2
that the interpolation error
Y

satisfies

\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}Y^{\prime}\rangle)(T)+\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{A}Y,\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq C\tau^{1+\alpha}\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

(4.6)

Assuming this fact for now, we can derive an estimate for
\eta
in terms
of
\psi^{\prime}
. For convenience, we use the notations:
F(g)=\bigl(\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}g^{\prime},g\rangle)(T)\bigr)^{1/2}
and
G(g)=\bigl(\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}g^{\prime},g^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\bigr)^{1/2}
.

Theorem 4.1

We have
\alpha^{2}\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq C\,\tau^{\alpha+1}\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime},\psi^{\prime}\rangle)(T).

Proof.

It suffices to estimate the right-hand side of the inequality in
Lemma
4.1
. By Lemma
2.1
,

\alpha\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq G(Y)G(\psi)~~{\rm and}~~\alpha\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\theta^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq G(Y)G(\theta)\,.

After using (
2.10
) and
\eta^{\prime}=\psi^{\prime}-\theta^{\prime}
, we conclude that

\alpha^{2}\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\eta^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq 2G(Y)G(\psi).

(4.7)

Since
Y(t_{j})=0
for
0\leq j\leq N
, integrating by parts, using

\mathcal{A}Y=\mathcal{I}\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}=\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime})
, and applying Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\alpha\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{A}\theta\rangle)(T)

\displaystyle=-\alpha\mathcal{I}(\langle\theta^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}(\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime})\rangle)(T)\leq G(\theta)F({\mathcal{A}}Y).

The same estimate holds with
\theta
replaced by
\psi
, so because

\eta=\psi-\theta
, and using (
2.10
) again,

\alpha^{2}\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{A}\eta\rangle)(T)\leq 2F({\mathcal{A}}Y)G(\psi).

(4.8)

Adding (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), we see

\alpha^{2}\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq 2G(\psi)(G(Y)+F({\mathcal{A}}Y))
by
Lemma
4.1
.
Squaring both sides, we have

\alpha^{4}\|\eta\|^{4}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq 4(G(\psi))^{2}(G(Y)+F({\mathcal{A}}Y))^{2}\leq 8(G(\psi))^{2}((G(Y))^{2}+(F({\mathcal{A}}Y))^{2}).

Since
(G(Y))^{2}+(F({\mathcal{A}}Y))^{2}
is just the left-hand side
of (
4.6
), the desired inequality followed after cancelling the common factor

\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}
.

\quad\Box

It remains to prove (
4.6
). We start by showing
preliminary bounds for
\big|\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}Y^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\big|
and

\big|\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{A}Y,\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\big|

in the next two lemmas.

Lemma 4.2

\Big|\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}Y^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\Big|\leq C(1-\alpha)\sum_{j=1}^{N-2}\tau_{j}^{-\alpha}\|Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}+C\tau^{1-\alpha}\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

Proof.

For
t\in I_{j}
with
j\geq 2
, we write
\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}Y^{\prime}(t)=S_{1}(t)+S_{2}(t)
where

S_{1}(t)=\int_{t_{j-2}}^{t}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)Y^{\prime}(s)\,ds~\text{and}~S_{2}(t)=\int_{0}^{t_{j-2}}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)Y^{\prime}(s)\,ds.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, integrating, changing the order
of integration, and integrating again, yields

\displaystyle\|S_{1}(t)

\displaystyle\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\leq\int_{I_{j}}\biggl(\int_{t_{j-2}}^{t}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)\,ds\biggr)\biggl(\int_{t_{j-2}}^{t}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)\|Y^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\,ds\biggr)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C(\tau_{j}+\tau_{j-1})^{1-\alpha}\biggl(\int_{I_{j}}\int_{s}^{t_{j}}+\int_{I_{j-1}}\int_{I_{j}}\biggr)\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)\,dt\,\|Y^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(\tau_{j}+\tau_{j-1})^{2(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{j-2}}^{t_{j}}\|Y^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\,ds\leq C\tau_{j}^{2(1-\alpha)}\int_{t_{j-2}}^{t_{j}}\|Y^{\prime}(s)\|^{2}\,ds.

For
t\in I_{1}
, let
S_{1}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t-s)Y^{\prime}(s)\,ds
and

S_{2}(t)=0
. Following the steps above, we find that
\|S_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{1})}\leq C\tau_{1}^{2(1-\alpha)}\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{1})}
. Thus,

\|S_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq C\tau^{2(1-\alpha)}\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)},
and consequently

\mathcal{I}(|\langle Y^{\prime},S_{1}\rangle|)(T)\leq\|Y^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(J)}\|S_{1}\|_{L^{2}(J)}\leq C\tau^{1-\alpha}\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

(4.9)

Turning to the second term
S_{2}(t)
, we integrate by parts (noting that

Y^{j-2}=0=Y^{0}
) and obtain

\|S_{2}(t)\|=\biggl\|\int_{0}^{t_{j-2}}\omega_{-\alpha}(t-s)Y(s)\,ds\biggr\|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j-2}\|Y\|_{I_{i}}\int_{I_{i}}|\omega_{-\alpha}(t-s)|\,ds.

Since
|\omega_{-\alpha}(t-s)|\leq|\omega_{-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-s)|
for
t\in I_{j}
,

\int_{I_{j}}|\langle Y^{\prime},S_{2}\rangle|\,dt\leq\sum_{i=1}^{j-2}\int_{I_{j}}\|Y^{\prime}(t)\|\|Y\|_{I_{i}}\,dt\int_{I_{i}}|\omega_{-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-s)|\,ds,

with

\int_{I_{j}}\|Y^{\prime}(t)\|\|Y\|_{I_{i}}\,dt\leq\frac{1}{2}\biggl(\|Y\|_{I_{i}}^{2}+\tau_{j}\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\biggr),

and, remembering that
\omega_{-\alpha}(t)=-\alpha\,t^{-1}\omega_{1-\alpha}(t)
,

\int_{I_{i}}|\omega_{-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-s)|\,ds=\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-t_{i})-\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-t_{i-1}).

Since
t_{j}-t_{i}\geq t_{j-1}-t_{i-1}
,
\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-t_{i-1})\geq\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{j}-t_{i})
,
and thus,

\int_{I_{j}}|\langle Y^{\prime},S_{2}\rangle|\,dt\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{j-2}\|Y\|_{i}^{2}[\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-t_{i})-\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{j}-t_{i})]\\
+\frac{\tau_{j}}{2}\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\int_{0}^{t_{j-2}}|\omega_{-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-s)|\,ds,

and in the second term, noting that

1/\Gamma(1-\alpha)=(1-\alpha)/\Gamma(2-\alpha)
,

\int_{0}^{t_{j-2}}|\omega_{-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-s)|\,ds=\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{j-1}-t_{j-2})-\omega_{1-\alpha}(t_{j})\leq C(1-\alpha)\tau_{j-1}^{-\alpha}.

By interchanging the order of the double sum,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(|\langle Y^{\prime},S_{2}\rangle|)(T)=\sum_{j=2}^{N}\int_{I_{j}}|\langle Y^{\prime},S_{2}\rangle|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C(1-\alpha)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}\|Y\|_{I_{i}}^{2}\sum_{j=i+2}^{N}[(t_{j-1}-t_{i})^{-\alpha}-(t_{j}-t_{i})^{-\alpha}]+\Bigl(\max_{2\leq j\leq N}\tau_{j}\tau_{j-1}^{-\alpha}\Bigr)\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(1-\alpha)\biggl(\sum_{i=1}^{N-2}\tau_{i+1}^{-\alpha}\|Y\|_{I_{i}}^{2}+\tau^{1-\alpha}\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\biggr),

which, combined with (
4.9
), yields the desired estimate.

\quad\Box

Lemma 4.3

\Big|\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{A}Y,\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\Big|\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}^{\alpha}\biggr[\biggl(\int_{I_{j}}\|\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}\|\,dt\biggr)^{2}+\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\biggr].

Proof.

Integrating by parts,

\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{A}Y,\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}\rangle)(T)=-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime},S_{3}\rangle\,dt-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime},S_{4}\rangle\,dt,

(4.10)

where we used the splitting
\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}{\mathcal{A}}Y=S_{3}+S_{4}
with

S_{3}(t)=\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)\mathcal{A}Y(s)\,ds~~\text{and}~~S_{4}(t)=\int_{0}^{t_{j-1}}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)\mathcal{A}Y(s)\,ds

for
t\in I_{j}
. Since
\|S_{3}(t)\|\leq\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t}\omega_{\alpha}(t-s)\,ds\leq C\tau_{j}^{\alpha}\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}
,

\biggl|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime},S_{3}\rangle\,dt\biggr|\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}^{\alpha}\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}\|\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}(t)\|\,dt.

(4.11)

For the estimate involving
S_{4}
, we reverse the order of integration and then
integrate by parts, to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{I_{j}}\langle\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime},S_{4}\rangle\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t_{j-1}}\Big\langle\mathcal{A}Y(s),\int_{I_{j}}\omega_{\alpha-1}(t-s)\mathcal{A}Y(t)\,dt\Big\rangle\,ds,

and thus, applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and using
\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{i}}\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}\leq\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{i}}^{2}+\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2},
we get

\biggl|\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime},S_{4}\rangle\,dt\biggr|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{j-1}\bigl(\|{\mathcal{A}}Y\|_{I_{i}}^{2}+\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\bigr)\int_{I_{i}}\int_{I_{j}}|\omega_{\alpha-1}(t-s)|\,dt\,ds\\
=\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{i}}^{2}\int_{I_{i}}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{n}}|\omega_{\alpha-1}(t-s)|\,dt\,ds+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\int_{I_{j}}\int_{0}^{t_{j-1}}|\omega_{\alpha-1}(t-s)|\,ds\,dt\\
\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\|{\mathcal{A}}Y\|_{I_{i}}^{2}\int_{I_{i}}\omega_{\alpha}(t_{i}-s)\,ds+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|{\mathcal{A}}Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\int_{I_{j}}\omega_{\alpha}(t-t_{j-1})\,dt\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}^{\alpha}\|{\mathcal{A}}Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}.

The proof is concluded by inserting this and (
4.11
) into the
splitting (
4.10
).

\quad\Box

By using the achieved estimates in the previous two lemmas, we are now able in
the next theorem to provide the missing part in the proof of
Theorem
4.1
.

Theorem 4.2

The inequality (
4.6
) is satisfied by the function
Y
defined via
(
4.3
), (
4.4
) and (
4.5
).

Proof.

Recall that
Y=y-y_{I}
where
y_{I}
is the piecewise linear polynomial that
interpolates
y
at the time nodes, and
y^{\prime}=\varphi
. Thus, if
t\in I_{j}
then

Y(t)=y(t)-y_{I}(t)=\frac{t_{j}-t}{\tau_{j}}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t}\varphi(s)\,ds-\frac{t-t_{j-1}}{\tau_{j}}\int_{t}^{t_{j}}\varphi(s)\,ds

(4.12)

so
\|Y(t)\|\leq\int_{I_{j}}\|\varphi\|\,ds
. Similarly, replacing
u
with
y
in (
3.3
), we have
\|Y(t)\|\leq\tau_{j}\int_{I_{j}}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|\,ds
. Therefore

\|Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\leq\tau_{j}\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\leq\tau_{j}^{3}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}.

(4.13)

Consider the linear operator
B
defined by

(B\varphi)(t)=\tau_{j}^{-\alpha/2}\|Y\|_{I_{j}}
for
t\in I_{j}
and
1\leq j\leq N
. The estimates (
4.13
) give

\|B\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq C\tau^{1-\alpha}\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|B\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq C\tau^{3-\alpha}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)},

and, since
\varphi(T)=0

and
(\tau^{1-\alpha})^{1-\alpha}(\tau^{3-\alpha})^{\alpha}=\tau^{1+\alpha}
, we
may apply Corollary
7.1
from the Appendix to deduce that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}^{-\alpha}\|Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}=\|B\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq C\tau^{1+\alpha}\|(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

(4.14)

Furthermore, by differentiating (
4.12
) and (
3.3
),

Y^{\prime}(t)=\varphi(t)-\frac{1}{\tau_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}\varphi(s)\,ds=\frac{1}{\tau_{j}}\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t}(s-t_{j-1})\varphi^{\prime}(s)\,ds-\frac{1}{\tau_{j}}\int_{t}^{t_{j}}(t_{j}-s)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\,ds

for
t\in I_{j}
, so

\|Y^{\prime}(t)\|\leq\|\varphi(t)\|+\frac{1}{\tau_{j}}\int_{I_{j}}\|\varphi(s)\|\,ds\quad\text{and}\quad\|Y^{\prime}(t)\|\leq\int_{I_{j}}\|\varphi^{\prime}(s)\|\,ds,

implying that

\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\leq 4\|\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\quad\text{and}\quad\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\leq\tau_{j}^{2}\|\varphi^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}.

(4.15)

After summing over
j
and once again applying
Corollary
7.1
, we arrive at

\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq C\tau^{2\alpha}\|(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

(4.16)

Now take the inner product of (
4.3
)
with
-(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}
in
L_{2}(\Omega)
, and then integrate in time
to obtain

\displaystyle\|(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}-\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{A}\varphi,(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}\rangle)(T)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{I}(\langle\eta,(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}\rangle)(T)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}+\frac{1}{2}\|(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

By (
4.2
),

-\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{A}\varphi,(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}\rangle)(T)=\mathcal{I}(\langle(\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}\mathcal{A}^{1/2}\varphi)^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{1/2}\varphi\rangle)(T)\geq 0

(4.17)

and therefore

\|(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi)^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

Combining this with (
4.14
), (
4.16
), we conclude that

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}^{-\alpha}\|Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\leq C\tau^{1+\alpha}\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq C\tau^{2\alpha}\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}

and so, applying Lemma
4.2
,

\mathcal{I}(\langle Y^{\prime},\mathcal{I}^{1-\alpha}Y^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq C\tau^{1+\alpha}\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

(4.18)

By taking the inner product of (
4.3
) with
\mathcal{A}\varphi
and proceeding as above, we deduce that

\|\mathcal{A}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

(4.19)

Repeating the arguments leading to the first estimate
in (
4.15
) but with
Y^{\prime}
replaced by
\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}
, we see that

\|\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}\leq\|\mathcal{A}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}
and so

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}^{\alpha}\biggl(\int_{I_{j}}\|\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}\|\,dt\biggr)^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}^{1+\alpha}\|\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\leq C\tau^{1+\alpha}\|\mathcal{A}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

Likewise,
\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\leq\tau_{j}\|\mathcal{A}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}
by the arguments leading
to (
4.12
), so

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}^{\alpha}\|\mathcal{A}Y\|_{I_{j}}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\tau_{j}^{1+\alpha}\|\mathcal{A}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{j})}\leq C\tau^{1+\alpha}\|\mathcal{A}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

Hence, by Lemma
4.3
and (
4.19
),

\mathcal{I}(\langle\mathcal{A}Y,\mathcal{I}^{\alpha}\mathcal{A}Y^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq C\tau^{1+\alpha}\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(J)}.

(4.20)

Together, (
4.18
) and (
4.20
) imply the desired
estimate (
4.6
).

\quad\Box

5
A fully discrete scheme and error analysis

In this section, we discretize the time-stepping scheme (
2.4
) in
space using the continuous piecewise-linear Galerkin FEM and hence define
a fully-discrete method. Thus, we introduce a family of regular (conforming)
triangulations
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of the domain
\overline{\Omega}
indexed by

h=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(h_{K})
, where
h_{K}
denotes the diameter of the
element
K
. Let
V_{h}
denote the space of continuous, piecewise-linear
functions with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{h}
that vanish on
\partial\Omega
. Let
{\mathcal{W}}(V_{h})\subset{\mathcal{C}}([0,T];V_{h})
denote the space of linear polynomials on
\overline{I}_{n}
for
1\leq n\leq N
, with coefficients in
V_{h}
. Motivated by the weak formulation of (
2.4
), our fully-discrete
solution
U_{h}\in{\mathcal{W}}(V_{h})
is defined by requiring

\Big\langle\int_{I_{n}}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}U_{h}\,dt,v_{h}\Big\rangle+\tau_{n}\langle\kappa\nabla U_{h}^{n-1/2},\nabla v_{h}\rangle=\tau_{n}\langle\bar{f}_{n},v_{h}\rangle\quad\text{for all $v_{h}\in V_{h}$,}

(5.1)

and for
1\leq n\leq N
, where
U_{h}^{n}:=U_{h}(t_{n})
and

U_{h}^{n-1/2}=\tfrac{1}{2}(U_{h}^{n}+U_{h}^{n-1})
. For the discrete
initial data, we choose
U_{h}^{0}=R_{h}u_{0}\in V_{h}
, where
R_{h}:H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\mapsto V_{h}
is the Ritz projection defined by
\langle\kappa\nabla(R_{h}w-w),\nabla v_{h}\rangle=0
for all
v_{h}\in V_{h}
.

In the next theorem, we prove that the numerical solution defined
by (
5.1
) is second order accurate in both time and space, provided that
the time mesh exponent
\gamma
is chosen appropriately. In comparison, under
heavier regularity assumptions and stronger graded meshes, convergence of
order
h^{2}+\tau^{(3-\alpha)/2}
was proved by Mustapha et
al.
[
22
]
. Furthermore, the proof therein is more
technical and lengthy. Use of the piecewise-linear polynomial
function
\widehat{u}
, see (
2.6
), and a duality argument allowed us to improve the convergence rate, simplify the proof and also relax the regularity assumptions. In addition to
the regularity assumption in (
2.11
), for
t>0,
we impose

\|u^{\prime}(t)\|_{2}+t\|u^{\prime\prime}(t)\|_{2}\leq C\,t^{\upsilon-1},\quad\text{for some~$\upsilon>0.$}

(5.2)

Theorem 5.1

Let
u
be the solution of (
1.1
) and let
U_{h}^{n}
be the approximate
solution defined by (
5.1
). Assume that the regularity assumptions in (
2.11
) and (
5.2
) are satisfied for
\sigma,\upsilon>\alpha/2
, and choose the mesh grading
exponent
\gamma>\max\{2/\sigma,1/\upsilon,(3-\alpha)/(2\sigma-\alpha)\}
.
Then,

\|u-U_{h}\|_{L^{2}(J)}\leq C(\tau^{2}+h^{2}).

Proof.

Decompose the error as
u-U_{h}=(u-u_{h})+(u_{h}-U_{h})
, where
u_{h}
is the Galerkin finite element solution of (
1.1
) defined by

\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u_{h},v_{h}\rangle+\langle\kappa\nabla u_{h},\nabla v_{h}\rangle=\langle f,v_{h}\rangle\quad\text{for all $v_{h}\in V_{h}$,}

(5.3)

for each fixed
t>0,
with
u_{h}(0)=U_{h}^{0}=R_{h}u_{0}.
From this, the weak formulation of (
1.1
), and the orthogonality property of the Ritz projection, we have

\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u_{h}-R_{h}u),v_{h}\rangle+\langle\kappa\nabla(u_{h}-R_{h}u),\nabla v_{h}\rangle=\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-R_{h}u),v_{h}\rangle\quad\text{for $v_{h}\in V_{h}$,}

Choose
v_{h}=(u_{h}-R_{h}u)^{\prime}
, integrate over
(0,t)
and apply Lemma
2.1
to the right-hand side with
\epsilon=1/(4\alpha^{2})
. After canceling the common terms,

4\alpha^{2}\|\sqrt{\kappa}\nabla(u_{h}-R_{h}u)(t)\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}e_{h},e_{h}^{\prime}\rangle)(t),~~{\rm with}~~e_{h}=u-R_{h}u\,.

The error bound for the Ritz projection and the regularity assumption in (
5.2
) yield

\|e_{h}^{\prime}(t)\|\leq Ch^{2}\|u^{\prime}(t)\|_{2}\leq Ch^{2}t^{\upsilon-1}
. Hence,

\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}e_{h}(t)\|\leq Ch^{2}t^{\upsilon-\alpha}
and consequently,
\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}e_{h},e_{h}^{\prime}\rangle)(t)\leq Ch^{4}
for
\upsilon>\alpha/2
. Inserting this estimate in the above equation, we obtain

\|\nabla(u_{h}-R_{h}u)(t)\|\leq Ch^{2}

for
\upsilon>\alpha/2
, and thus, by applying the Poincaré and triangle inequalities, we get

\|u(t)-u_{h}(t)\|\leq Ch^{2}.

The remaining target now is to estimate
U_{h}-u_{h}.
By analogy with our earlier splitting (
2.5
), we let

\eta_{h}=u_{h}-U_{h},\quad\psi_{h}=u-R_{h}\widehat{u}\quad{\rm and}\quad\theta_{h}=U_{h}-R_{h}\widehat{u}.

From (
5.1
) and (
5.3
), and with
\chi_{h}=u_{h}-R_{h}\widehat{u},
we have

\int_{I_{n}}[\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\theta_{h}(t),v_{h}\rangle+\langle\kappa\nabla\theta_{h}(t),\nabla v_{h}\rangle]\,dt=\int_{I_{n}}[\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\chi_{h}(t),v_{h}\rangle+\langle\kappa\nabla\chi_{h}(t),\nabla v_{h}\rangle]\,dt,

for
v_{h}\in V_{h}
. Using the orthogonality property of the Ritz projection and the definition
of
\widehat{u}
, in addition to (
5.3
),

\int_{I_{n}}\langle\kappa\nabla\chi_{h}(t),\nabla v_{h}\rangle\,dt=\int_{I_{n}}\langle\kappa\nabla(u_{h}-u)(t),\nabla v_{h}\rangle\,dt=\int_{I_{n}}\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}(u-u_{h})(t),v_{h}\rangle\,dt,

and hence

\int_{I_{n}}[\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\theta_{h}(t),v_{h}\rangle+\langle\kappa\nabla\theta_{h}(t),\nabla v_{h}\rangle]\,dt=\int_{I_{n}}\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\psi_{h}(t),v_{h}\rangle\,dt,\quad\text{for $v_{h}\in V_{h}$.}

By repeating the steps from (
2.8
) to (
2.10
),
we deduce that

\alpha^{2}\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\theta_{h},\theta_{h}^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\psi_{h},\psi_{h}^{\prime}\rangle)(T).

(5.4)

Applying Lemma
2.2
with
v=\widehat{e}_{h}^{\prime}:=(\widehat{u}-R_{h}\widehat{u})^{\prime}
and

w=\psi^{\prime}
so that
\psi_{h}^{\prime}=v+w
,

\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\psi_{h},\psi_{h}^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq(1+\alpha^{-1})\Big(\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\widehat{e}_{h},\widehat{e}_{h}^{\prime}\rangle)(T)+\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\psi,\psi^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\Big).

For
t\in I_{j}
, since
\widehat{u}^{\prime}(t)=\tau_{j}^{-1}(\widehat{u}^{j}-\widehat{u}^{j-1}),
the Ritz projection error bound gives

\|\widehat{e}_{h}^{\prime}(t)\|\leq Ch^{2}\|\widehat{u}^{\prime}(t)\|_{2}\leq Ch^{2}\tau_{j}^{-1}\|\psi^{j}-\psi^{j-1}\|_{2}+Ch^{2}\tau_{j}^{-1}\int_{I_{j}}\|u^{\prime}(s)\|_{2}\,ds\,.

From Lemma
3.1
, (
5.2
) and the time mesh property (
2.2
), we have

\|\psi^{j}-\psi^{j-1}\|_{2}\leq\|\psi^{j}\|_{2}+\|\psi^{j-1}\|_{2}\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{j}\int_{I_{j}}(t-t_{j-1})\|u^{\prime\prime}(t)\|_{2}\,dt\\
\leq C\tau_{1}^{\upsilon}+C\tau\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{j}}t^{\upsilon-1/\gamma-1}\,dt\leq C\tau_{1}^{\upsilon}+C\tau t_{j}^{\upsilon-1/\gamma}\leq C\tau t_{j}^{\upsilon-1/\gamma}\leq C\tau_{j}t_{j}^{\upsilon-1},

for
\gamma>1/\upsilon
with
j\geq 1.
Combining the above two estimates, we conclude that

\|\widehat{e}_{h}^{\prime}(t)\|\leq Ch^{2}t_{j}^{\upsilon-1}\leq Ch^{2}t^{\upsilon-1}
for
t\in I_{j}
. This leads to

\|\widehat{e}_{h}^{\prime}(t)\|\leq Ch^{2}\omega_{\upsilon}(t)
and

\|\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\widehat{e}_{h}(t)\|\leq Ch^{2}\omega_{1-\alpha+\upsilon}(t).

Thence,

\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\widehat{e}_{h},\widehat{e}_{h}^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq Ch^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\omega_{1-\alpha+\upsilon}\,\omega_{\upsilon}\,dt\leq Ch^{4}\int_{0}^{T}t^{2\upsilon-\alpha-1}\,dt\leq Ch^{4},

for
\upsilon>\alpha/2
, so by Lemma
3.4
,

\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\psi_{h},\psi_{h}^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\leq C\Big(h^{4}+\mathcal{I}(\langle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\psi,\psi^{\prime}\rangle)(T)\Big)\leq C(h^{4}+\tau^{3-\alpha}).

(5.5)

Adapting (
4.3
), suppose that
\varphi_{h}(t)\in V_{h}
satisfies the discrete final-value problem

-(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\alpha}\varphi_{h})^{\prime}(t)+\mathcal{A}_{h}\varphi_{h}(t)=\eta_{h}(t)\quad\text{for $0<t<T$,}\quad\text{with $\varphi_{h}(T)=0$,}

(5.6)

where the discrete elliptic operator
{\mathcal{A}}_{h}:V_{h}\to V_{h}
is defined by
\langle{\mathcal{A}}_{h}v_{h},q_{h}\rangle=\langle\kappa\nabla v_{h},\nabla q_{h}\rangle
for all
v_{h},q_{h}\in V_{h}
. We now repeat the step in the error analysis of Section
4
, with
\eta_{h},

\theta_{h}
,
\psi_{h}
and
\mathcal{A}_{h}
playing the roles of
\eta,

\theta

\psi
and
\mathcal{A}
, respectively, and using (
5.1
) and (
5.4
) instead of (
2.4
) and (
2.10
), and (
5.5
) instead of Lemma
3.4
. We notice for

\gamma>\max\{2/\sigma,1/\upsilon,(3-\alpha)/(2\sigma-\alpha)\}
and for
\sigma,\upsilon>\alpha/2,
that

\|u_{h}-U_{h}\|_{L^{2}(J)}^{2}\leq C\tau^{1+\alpha}(h^{4}+\tau^{3-\alpha}).

The proof of this theorem is completed.

\quad\Box

7
Appendix: An
\alpha
-robust interpolation estimate

The purpose of this appendix is to prove Corollary
7.1
, which was used in the proof of Theorem
4.2
. Suppose that
Y
is a complex Hilbert space with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{Y}
and norm
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
. Put

X_{0}=L_{2}(0,T;Y)\quad\text{and}\quad X_{1}=\{\,u\in X_{0}:\text{$u^{\prime}\in X_{0}$ and $u(0)=0$}\,\},

(7.1)

equipped with the inner products

(u,v)_{X_{0}}=\int_{0}^{T}\bigl(u(t),v(t)\bigr)_{Y}\,dt\quad\text{and}\quad(u,v)_{X_{1}}=\int_{0}^{T}\bigl(u^{\prime}(t),v^{\prime}(t)\bigr)_{Y}\,dt.

We define an unbounded operator
D_{0}
on
X_{0}
with domain
X_{1}
, by setting

D_{0}u=u^{\prime}
on the interval
(0,T)
. Let
\Re z
denote the real part of a
complex number
z
. Since

\Re\bigl(u(t),D_{0}u(t)\bigr)_{Y}=\tfrac{1}{2}(d/dt)\|u(t)\|_{Y}^{2}
, we have

\Re(u,D_{0}u)_{X_{0}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|u(T)\|_{Y}^{2}\geq 0
for all
u\in X_{1}
. In addition, one easily verifies that

(\lambda+D_{0})^{-1}u(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\lambda(t-s)}u(s)\,ds\quad\text{for $0\leq t\leq T$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$,}

(7.2)

showing that
D_{0}
is m-accretive
[
17
, p. 9]
, and therefore a positive
operator
[
17
, pp. 1, 11]
with

\sup_{\lambda>0}\|\lambda(\lambda+D_{0})^{-1}\|_{X_{0}\to X_{0}}=1
, where
\|\cdot\|_{X_{0}\to X_{0}}
denotes the operator norm induced by the norm of
X_{0}
.

Suppose that
0<\Re\beta<1
. The operator
D_{0}^{-\beta}
is defined via the integral
[
2
, Equation (1.3)]

D_{0}^{-\beta}u=\frac{\sin\pi\beta}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{-\beta}(\lambda+D_{0})^{-1}u\,d\lambda\quad\text{for $u\in X_{0}$.}

A short calculation using (
7.2
) then
shows
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
that

D_{0}^{-\beta}u=\mathcal{I}^{\beta}u
for
0<\Re\beta<1
. One may then
define
[
2
, Equation 1.4]
,
[
17
, Equation (3.1)]

D_{0}^{\beta}u=D_{0}^{\beta-1}D_{0}u=\frac{\sin\pi\beta}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{\beta-1}(\lambda+D_{0})^{-1}D_{0}u\,d\lambda\quad\text{for $u\in X_{1}$,}

and
D_{0}^{\beta}u=\mathcal{I}^{1-\beta}u^{\prime}=(\mathcal{I}^{1-\beta}u)^{\prime}
, that is,
D_{0}^{\beta}
coincides with
both the Caputo and Riemann–Liouville fractional derivatives of order
\beta
.

For
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, let
X_{\alpha}
denote the complex interpolation
space
[
4
, Chap. 4]
arising from
X_{0}
and
X_{1}
. The
next result is known
[
17
, Theorem 11.6.1]
, but
we sketch the proof in order to verify that the constant does not depend on
\alpha
.

Theorem 7.1

Let
0<\alpha<1
. For the spaces (
7.1
) and any complex Hilbert
space
Z
, if the linear operator
B
satisfies
\|Bu\|_{Z}\leq M_{j}\|u\|_{X_{j}}
for
u\in X_{j}
and
j\in\{0,1\}
, then

\|Bu\|_{Z}\leq e^{1+(\pi/4)^{2}}M_{0}^{1-\alpha}M_{1}^{\alpha}\|D_{0}^{\alpha}u\|_{X_{0}}\quad\text{for $D_{0}^{\alpha}u\in X_{0}$.}

Proof.

If we set
Z_{0}=Z_{1}=Z
, then
Z_{\alpha}=Z
with equal norms

[
4
, Theorem 4.2.1]
, so

\|Bu\|_{Z}\leq M_{0}^{1-\alpha}M_{1}^{\alpha}\|u\|_{X_{\alpha}},
and our task is to estimate the interpolation norm
\|u\|_{X_{\alpha}}
in terms of the fractional derivative
D_{0}^{\alpha}u
. Define the closed strip
S=\{\,z\in\mathbb{C}:0\leq\Re z\leq 1\,\}
in the complex plane, and let
\mathcal{F}
denote the space of functions
f:S\to X_{0}+X_{1}
that are bounded and continuous on
S
, analytic in the interior of
S
,
and such that
f(iy)
and
f(1+iy)
tend to zero as
|y|\to\infty
. It can be
shown
[
4
, Lemma 4.1.1]
that
\mathcal{F}
is a Banach
space with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}=\max\Bigl(\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\|f(iy)\|_{X_{0}},\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\|f(1+iy)\|_{X_{1}}\Bigr).

The space
X_{\alpha}
then consists of those
u\in X_{0}+X_{1}
such that

u=f(\alpha)
for some
f\in\mathcal{F}
, with the interpolation norm defined by

\|u\|_{X_{\alpha}}=\inf\{\,\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}:\text{$u=f(\alpha)$ and $f\in\mathcal{F}$}\,\}.

If we define
f(z)=e^{(z-\alpha)^{2}}D_{0}^{\alpha-z}u
, then
f(\alpha)=u
with

\|f(iy)\|_{X_{0}}=e^{\alpha^{2}-y^{2}}\|D_{0}^{-iy}D_{0}^{\alpha}u\|_{X_{0}}
and

\|f(1+iy)\|_{X_{1}}=e^{(1-\alpha)^{2}-y^{2}}\|D_{0}(D_{0}^{\alpha-1-iy}u)\|_{X_{0}}=e^{(1-\alpha)^{2}-y^{2}}\|D_{0}^{-iy}D_{0}^{\alpha}u\|_{X_{0}}.

It is known
[
26
, Example 2]
that

\|D_{0}^{iy}u\|_{X_{0}}\leq e^{\pi|y|/2}\|u\|_{X_{0}}
for
u\in X_{0}

because
D_{0}
is m-accretive. Thus

\|u\|_{X_{\alpha}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\max\Bigl(\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}e^{\alpha^{2}-y^{2}+\pi|y|/2},\sup_{y\in\mathbb{R}}e^{(1-\alpha)^{2}-y^{2}+\pi|y|/2}\Bigr)\|D_{0}^{\alpha}u\|_{X_{0}},

and since
-y^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\pi|y|=(\tfrac{1}{4}\pi)^{2}-(|y|-\tfrac{1}{4}\pi)^{2}\leq(\tfrac{1}{4}\pi)^{2},
the proof is completed.

\quad\Box

The interpolation estimate used in the proof of Theorem
4.2
now follows
by repeating the preceding arguments with
D_{0}
and
X_{1}
replaced by their
time-reversed equivalents

D_{T}u(t)=-u^{\prime}(t)\quad\text{and}\quad X_{T,1}=\{\,u\in X_{0}:\text{$u^{\prime}\in X_{0}$ and $u(T)=0$}\,\}.

In fact,
\Re(u,D_{T}u)_{X_{0}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\|u(0)\|_{Y}^{2}\geq 0
for all
u\in X_{T,1}
,
and

(\lambda+D_{T})^{-1}u(t)=\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\lambda(s-t)}u(s)\,ds\quad\text{for $0\leq t\leq T$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$,}

so
D_{T}
is m-accretive. We find that if
0<\Re\beta<1
then
D_{T}^{-\beta}u=\mathcal{J}_{T}^{\beta}u
, with

D_{T}^{\beta}u=D_{T}D_{T}^{\beta-1}u=(\mathcal{J}_{T}^{1-\beta}u)^{\prime}\quad\text{for $u\in X_{T,1}$.}

Inhomogeneous approximation for systems of linear forms with primitivity constraints
Demi Allen, Felipe A. Ramírez

Abstract
. We study (inhomogeneous) approximation for systems of linear forms using integer points which satisfy additional primitivity constraints. The first family of primitivity constraints we consider were introduced in 2015 by Dani, Laurent, and Nogueira, and are associated to partitions of the coordinate directions. Our results in this setting strengthen a theorem of Dani, Laurent, and Nogueira, and address problems posed by those same authors. The second primitivity constraints we consider are analogues of the coprimality required in the higher-dimensional Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture, posed by Sprindžuk in the 1970’s and proved by Pollington and Vaughan in 1990. Here, with attention restricted to systems of linear forms in at least three variables, we prove a univariate inhomogeneous version of the Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture for systems of linear forms, the multivariate homogeneous version of which was stated by Beresnevich, Bernik, Dodson, and Velani in 2009 and recently proved by the second author.

1.
Introduction

We are concerned here with the problem of determining whether for a
given sequence
(B_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
of balls in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and a typical

\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}(\mathbb{R})
there are infinitely many

(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that

(1)

\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}\in B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert},

where
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=\max\{|q_{1}|,\dots,|q_{n}|\}
denotes the maximum norm,
subject to the integer vectors
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
satisfying additional
primitivity conditions. We consider two different kinds of conditions:
the first were introduced in work of Dani, Laurent, and
Nogueira
[
7
]
, and the second are relevant to a version of the
Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture for systems of linear forms.

The first type of primitivity conditions we consider—those
introduced by Dani, Laurent, and Nogueira—are associated to
partitions
\pi
of
\{1,2,\dots,m+n\}
. They are natural
refinements of the condition
\gcd(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})=1
which arises often in the
classical setting. For an integer vector
\mathbf{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
we write

\gcd(\mathbf{q})
to mean
\gcd(q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
. In this first setting
(which is described in more detail in
Section
3
), we consider the set

P(\pi)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
consisting of points
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
whose
coordinates corresponding to each partition element of
\pi
are coprime. We
seek solutions to (
1
) with
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi)
. In this
setting, Dani, Laurent, and Nogueira proved a doubly-metric
Khintchine–Groshev-type theorem
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
with certain
assumptions on the partition
\pi
and a monotonicity assumption on
the sequence of the radii of the balls
(B_{q})_{q=1}^{\infty}
.

Theorem

(Dani–Laurent–Nogueira
[
7
]
)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and suppose
\pi=\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}\}
is a
partition of
\{1,\dots,m+n\}
with
\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert\geq m+1
for
each
j=1,\dots,k
. (Here, the notation
|\pi_{j}|
denotes the number
of elements in the partition component
\pi_{j}
.) If

\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a mapping such that

x\mapsto x^{n-1}\psi(x)^{m}
is non-increasing and the series

(2)

\sum_{q=1}^{\infty}q^{n-1}\psi(q)^{m}

diverges, then for almost every pair

(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, there
exist infinitely many points
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi)
such that

(3)

\lvert\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{y}\rvert<\psi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert).

Conversely, if (
2
) converges, then for almost every
pair
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

there are only finitely many
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi)
such
that (
3
) holds.

Remark
.

To phrase this in terms of (
1
), take the balls

B_{|\mathbf{q}|}
to be centered at
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
with radius

\psi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)
.

Remark
.

In the case of the trivial partition

\pi=\left\{\{1,2,\dots,m+n\}\right\}
this theorem is classical. For
a discussion of the state of the art regarding the classical case,
we refer the reader to
[
2
,
5
]
and references therein. The
above theorem, and also Theorems
1
and

2
which follow, can be viewed as refinements of
the classical setting allowing for non-trivial partitions.

Regarding the assumptions in this theorem, Dani, Laurent, and Nogueira
ask the following questions
[
7
,
16
]
:

Question 1

(
[
7
]
/
[
16
, Problem

)
.

Can the result be made
singly-metric? It is expected that this result should hold for every

, as it does in the

Question 2

[

)
.

Can

Question 3

(

)

2.
Results

2.1.
Main results

The following theorem addresses Questions
1

and
2
. In particular, a singly metric version

Theorem 1
.

Let

and fix
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Suppose

\pi=\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}\}

\{1,\dots,m+n\}

Conversely, if

converges, then for almost

\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}(\mathbb{R})
there are only
finitely many
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi)
such that (
3
) holds.

Remark

In fact, we prove a stronger theorem (Theorem
7

The following theorem shows that, further to

willing to impose a mild assumption on the partition.

Theorem 2
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that

and fix
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Suppose

\pi=\{\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k}\}
is a partition of

\{1,\dots,m+n\}
such that
\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert\geq 2
for each

j=1,\dots,k
. Furthermore, suppose that there exists some

\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}
for which

\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\sum q^{n-1}\psi(q)^{m}
diverges, then for almost every

\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}(\mathbb{R})
there exist infinitely
many points
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi)
such that (
3
)
holds.

Conversely, if
\sum q^{n-1}\psi(q)^{m}
converges, then for
almost every
\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}(\mathbb{R})
there are
only finitely many
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi)
such that (
3
) holds.

Remark
.

As with the previous result, this one also follows from a stronger

8
) where the target ball’s center can

Next, we turn our attention to the following univariate inhomogeneous
analogue of the Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture for systems of linear
forms. A
homogenenous multivariate
Duffin–Schaeffer
conjecture for systems of linear forms, i.e. where
\psi
is a
multivariate function depending on
\mathbf{q}
rather than
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert
, and

\mathbf{y}=0
, was posed in
[
3
]
and has recently been proved by the
second author in
[
19
]
. We complement this recent work with the
following
inhomogeneous univariate
statement. Of course,
the univariate case is a special case of the multivariate case. The
novelty of the following statement therefore is the inhomogeneity
which is allowed.

Theorem 3

(Univariate inhomogeneous Duffin–Schaeffer
conjecture for systems of linear forms)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
n>2
and fix
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. If

\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is a function such that

(4)

\sum_{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left\lparen\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\mathbf{q}))\psi(\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert)}{\gcd(\mathbf{q})}\right\rparen^{m}=\infty,

then for almost every
\mathbf{x}\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times m}(\mathbb{R})

there exist infinitely many points
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with

\gcd(p_{i},\mathbf{q})=1
for every
i=1,\dots,m
and such
that (
3
) holds.

Remark
.

Again, this follows from a more general statement
(Theorem
9
) where
\mathbf{y}
may vary.

We conjecture that the result holds without the condition on
n
.

Conjecture 1
.

Theorem
3
also holds when
n\leq 2
.

In the case when
m=n=1
and
\mathbf{y}=0
, Conjecture
1
is
exactly the Duffin–Schaeffer conjecture
[
8
]
, which was proved
in 2020 by Koukoulopoulos and Maynard
[
15
]
. In 1990, Pollington
and Vaughan
[
18
]
proved Conjecture
1
in the cases
(m,1)

with
m\geq 2
and
\mathbf{y}=0
, thus verifying a higher-dimensional
simultaneous version of the classical Duffin–Schaeffer Conjecture as
postulated by Sprindžuk
[
21
]
.

2.2.
Hausdorff measure statements

In Diophantine Approximation, in addition to considering Lebesgue
measure, one is often interested in studying the Hausdorff measure and
dimension of sets. This is particularly pertinent for sets which have
zero Lebesgue measure as Hausdorff measures and dimensions can often
provide a means for distinguishing such sets. For example, to observe
this phenomenon, one can compare the classical works of Khintchine

[
14
]
, Jarník
[
12
,
13
]
, and Besicovitch
[
6
]
. For
definitions of Hausdorff measures and dimension, we refer the reader
to
[
9
]
.

Below we record Hausdorff measure analogues of Theorems

1
,
2
, and
3
. For the
Hausdorff measure analogues of Theorems
1
and

2
, the statements we give follow immediately from

[
1
, Theorem 7]
, which itself is deduced from the mass
transference principle for systems of linear forms proved in

[
1
, Theorem 1]
. Given a function
\psi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
, a
partition
\pi=\{\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{k}\}
of
[m+n]
with

|\pi_{j}|\geq 2
for each
j=1,\dots,k
, fixed
\Phi\in{\mathbb{I}}^{mm}
,
and
\mathbf{y}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{m}

{\mathcal{M}}_{n,m}^{\pi,\mathbf{y},\Phi}(\psi)
to be the
set of
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
such that

|\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}\Phi-\mathbf{y}|<\psi(|\mathbf{q}|)

holds for
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi)
with
arbitrarily large
|\mathbf{q}|
. When
\Phi
is the

identity
matrix, we will omit the superscript

and simply write

.

Throughout, we define a

to be a
continuous function
f:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
with

as

. For a subset

Theorem

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
\psi:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}

\left|{\mathcal{M}}_{n,m}^{\pi,\mathbf{y},\Phi}(\theta)\right|=1\qquad\text{implies}\qquad{\mathcal{H}}^{f}\left({\mathcal{M}}_{n,m}^{\pi,\mathbf{y},\Phi}(\psi)\right)={\mathcal{H}}^{f}({\mathbb{I}}^{nm}).

3.
Partition reduction

Fix
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. We are concerned with partitions of

[m+n]:=\{1,2,\dots,m,m+1,\dots,m+n\}
. For example,
[m+n]

itself can be regarded as the trivial partition, more correctly
written as
\{[m+n]\}
. Another important partition is

\{[m],m+[n]\}
, where

m+[n]=m+\{1,\dots,n\}=\{m+1,\dots,m+n\}
.

Let
\pi=(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})
be a partition of
[m+n]
such
that
\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert\geq 2
for each
j=1,\dots,k
. By re-ordering
the partition components if necessary, we may suppose that there exist

0\leq a\leq b\leq k
such that

•

For each
j\in[1,a]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
we have
\pi_{j}\cap[m]\neq\emptyset
and

\pi_{j}\cap m+[n]\neq\emptyset
.

•

For each
j\in(a,b]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
we have
\pi_{j}\subset[m]
.

•

For each
j\in(b,k]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
we have
\pi_{j}\subset m+[n]
.

For each
j=1,\dots,k
it is convenient to abuse notation and let

\pi_{j}
also denote the projection of
\mathbb{Z}^{m+n}
onto the coordinates
corresponding to
\pi_{j}
, regarded as a vector in

\mathbb{Z}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert}
. (The context will disambiguate.) For example, if
we use
\pi_{1}=[m]
and
\pi_{2}=m+[n]
, and elements of

\mathbb{Z}^{m+n}
are written
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
with
\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
and

\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})=\mathbf{p}
and
\pi_{2}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})=\mathbf{q}
.

Indeed, let us keep the convention of writing
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})
for elements
of
\mathbb{Z}^{m+n}=\mathbb{Z}^{m}\times\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then

P(\pi)=\left\{(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m+n}:\text{ for each }j=1,\dots,k,\quad\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}))=1\right\}.

Note that
P(\{[m+n]\})
is the set of primitive points in

\mathbb{Z}^{m+n}
. For
\mathbf{q}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
, let

P(\pi,\mathbf{q})=\left\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}:(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi)\right\}.

For example,
P(\{[m+n]\},\mathbf{q})=\mathbb{Z}^{m+n}
if
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1
, but in
general
P(\{[m+n]\},\mathbf{q})
is a proper subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{m+n}
. Finally, let

Q(\pi)=\{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:P(\pi,\mathbf{q})\neq\emptyset\}.

These are the vectors
\mathbf{q}
such that for all
j\in(b,k]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
,

\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{q}))=1
, with
\mathbf{0}
denoting the
m
-dimensional

0
-vector.

4.
Arithmetic lemmas

In this section we collect some useful arithmetic sums, many of which
are well-known. The main purpose of this section is to prove
Lemma
4
, a sum which is crucial later in the proof of
Lemma
7
.

We will often use the Vinogradov symbols. Suppose that

f:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
and
g:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
are
functions. We say that
f\ll g
if there exists a constant
C>0
such
that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for all
x\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
. If
f\ll g
and

g\ll f
, we write
f\asymp g
and say that
f
and
g
are

comparable
. We write
f\sim g
if

\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\to 1
as
x\to\infty
and we write
f=o(g)
if

\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\to 0
as
x\to\infty
.

Throughout, we use
\varphi
to denote the
Euler totient
function
and
\mu
to denote the
Möbius
function
. For definitions and properties, the reader is referred to

[
10
]
.

Lemma 1
.

For each integer
D\geq 2
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1\end{subarray}}1\geq Cq^{D}

for all
q\geq 1
.

Proof.

It is well-known that for all
D\geq 2
we have the asymptotic equality

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1\end{subarray}}1\sim 2^{D}\zeta(D)^{-1}q^{D}

as
q\to\infty
, where
\zeta
denotes the Riemann zeta
function. (See, for example,
[
7
, Lemma 4.2]
.) In particular,
there is some
Q_{D}
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1\end{subarray}}1\geq 2^{D-1}\zeta(D)^{-1}q^{D}

holds for all
q\geq Q_{D}
. We may take
C
as the minimum of the finite set

\left\{2^{D-1}\zeta(D)^{-1}\right\}\cup\left\{q^{-D}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1\end{subarray}}1:q\leq Q_{D}\right\}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For each integer
D\geq 1
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q},q)=1\end{subarray}}1\geq\begin{cases}C\varphi(q)&\textrm{if }D=1,\\
Cq^{D}&\textrm{if }D\geq 2\end{cases}

for all
q\geq 1
.

Proof.

The case
D=1
is exactly the definition of
2\varphi(D)
, so let us
assume
D\geq 2
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q},q)=1\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1\end{subarray}}1\geq Cq^{D},

for all
q\geq 1
, by Lemma
1
.
∎

Lemma 3
.

For each integer
D\geq 1
there is a constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\mathbf{q}))}{\gcd(\mathbf{q})}\geq Cq^{D}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\mathbf{q},q))}{\gcd(\mathbf{q},q)}\geq Cq^{D}

both hold for all
q\geq 1
.

Proof.

For the first expression, if
D\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\mathbf{q}))}{\gcd(\mathbf{q})}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\,\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\mathbf{q}))}{\gcd(\mathbf{q})}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\,\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1\end{subarray}}1\gg q^{D},

where the last inequality follows from
Lemma
1
. On the other hand, if
D=1
, we may
write

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\mathbf{q}))}{\gcd(\mathbf{q})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\lvert q^{\prime}\rvert\leq q\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(q^{\prime})}{q^{\prime}}\gg q.

The last estimate is well-known. It follows from the
fact that the average order of
\varphi(q)
is
6q/\pi^{2}
(see,
for example,
[
10
, Theorem 330]
).

For the second expression in the lemma, if
D\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\mathbf{q},q))}{\gcd(\mathbf{q},q)}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\,\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\mathbf{q},q))}{\gcd(\mathbf{q},q)}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{D}\,\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(\mathbf{q})=1\end{subarray}}1\gg q^{D},

where the final inequality again follows by
Lemma
1
. If
D=1
, we write

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}\\
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq q\end{subarray}}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\mathbf{q},q))}{\gcd(\mathbf{q},q)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}\,\\
\lvert q^{\prime}\rvert\leq q\\
\gcd(q^{\prime},q)=d\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}\,\\
\lvert q^{\prime}\rvert\leq q/d\\
\gcd\left(q^{\prime},\frac{q}{d}\right)=1\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=2\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\varphi(q/d).

Meanwhile, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{\varphi(d)\varphi(q/d)}{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid q}\varphi(q/d)\sum_{j\mid d}\frac{\mu(j)}{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\mid q}\frac{\mu(j)}{j}\sum_{i\mid(q/j)}\varphi(q/ij)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\mid q}\frac{\mu(j)}{j}\left\lparen\frac{q}{j}\right\rparen

\displaystyle=q\sum_{j\mid q}\frac{\mu(j)}{j^{2}}

\displaystyle=q\prod_{p\mid q}\left\lparen 1-p^{-2}\right\rparen.

Noting that (see
[
10
, Theorem 280]
) for every
q\geq 1
we have

\zeta(2)^{-1}\leq\prod_{p\mid q}\left\lparen 1-p^{-2}\right\rparen\leq 1,

we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid q}\frac{\varphi(d)\varphi(q/d)}{d}=q\prod_{p\mid q}\left\lparen 1-p^{-2}\right\rparen

\displaystyle\asymp q.

This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

5.
Counting

The main purpose of this section is to prove Lemma
7

which states that, in a sense, the integer points
(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi)

with
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q
are uniformly distributed for large
q
, where

\pi
is a partition of
[m+n]
as in
Section
3
. The lemma is important later in
our proof of Lemma
8
, where we show that the sets

A^{\pi}(\mathbf{q})
(defined in the next section) also enjoy a
kind of uniform distribution in
{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
where

{\mathbb{I}}=[0,1]
.

The following lemma can be deduced from
[
17
, Lemma 1]
. We include its proof for completeness.

Lemma 5
.

For any
0\leq\alpha<\beta\leq 1
with
\beta-\alpha=\gamma
,
there exists some integer
Q_{\gamma}>0
such that

(6)

\frac{1}{2}\varphi(q)\gamma\leq\#\left\{p\in\mathbb{N}:\gcd(p,q)=1,\quad\alpha q\leq p\leq\beta q\right\}\leq\frac{3}{2}\varphi(q)\gamma

whenever
q\geq Q_{\gamma}
.

Proof.

Let
\theta(q)=\#\left\{p/q\in(\alpha,\beta)\right\}
, and notice that

\lfloor\gamma q\rfloor\leq\theta(q)\leq\lfloor\gamma q\rfloor+1
. We have

\theta(q)=\sum_{d\mid q}\#(P_{d}\cap(\alpha,\beta)),

where
P_{d}
denotes the reduced fractions in
[0,1]
with
denominator
d
. The Möbius inversion formula (see
[
10
, Theorem 266]
) gives

\#(P_{q}\cap(\alpha,\beta))=\sum_{d\mid q}\mu\left\lparen\frac{q}{d}\right\rparen\theta(d)

For a lower bound, we have

\displaystyle\#(P_{q}\cap(\alpha,\beta))

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid q}\mu\left\lparen\frac{q}{d}\right\rparen\theta(d)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{d\mid q}\mu\left\lparen\frac{q}{d}\right\rparen\gamma d-\sum_{d\mid q}\{\gamma d\}\mu\left\lparen\frac{q}{d}\right\rparen

\displaystyle=\gamma\varphi(q)-\sum_{d\mid q}1,

and for an upper bound, we have

\displaystyle\#(P_{q}\cap(\alpha,\beta))

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\mid q}\mu\left\lparen\frac{q}{d}\right\rparen\theta(d)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{d\mid q}\mu\left\lparen\frac{q}{d}\right\rparen\gamma d+\sum_{d\mid q}1

\displaystyle=\gamma\varphi(q)+\sum_{d\mid q}1.

This last term is the number of divisors of
q
, which is

o(q^{\varepsilon})
for any
{\varepsilon}>0

[
10
, Theorem 315]
, and in
particular
o(\varphi(q))
(since

\varphi(q)\gg q/\log\log q
by
[
10
, Theorem 328]
).
Therefore, there exists
Q_{\gamma}>0
such that for all

q\geq Q_{\gamma}
we have

\sum_{d\mid q}1\leq\frac{1}{2}\gamma\varphi(q).

Combining this with the previous two bounds gives (
6
) for
all
q\geq Q_{\gamma}
, proving the lemma.
∎

Lemma 6
.

Suppose
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
and, for
1\leq i\leq d
, suppose that
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in[0,1]
are such that

0\leq\alpha_{i}<\beta_{i}\leq 1\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}\quad\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq d.

Then there exist
C,Q_{\gamma}>0
such that

(7)

\displaystyle\#\left\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{d})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:\gcd(p_{1},\dots,p_{d})=1\text{ and }\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,d\right\}

\displaystyle\geq C\gamma^{d}q^{d}

holds for all
q\geq Q_{\gamma}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction, first establishing (
7
) in the case when
d=2
. Notice that

\#\left\{(p_{1},p_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}:\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})=1\text{ and }\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\quad\text{for }i=1,2\right\}=\sum_{p_{1}=\lceil\alpha_{1}q\rceil}^{\lfloor\beta_{1}q\rfloor}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}=\lceil\alpha_{2}q\rceil\\
\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})=1\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor\beta_{2}q\rfloor}{1}}.

For a fixed
p_{1}
, by Lemma
5
, the inner sum is

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}=\lceil\alpha_{2}q\rceil\\
\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})=1\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor\beta_{2}q\rfloor}{1}

\displaystyle=\#\left\{p_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}:\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})=1\text{ and }\alpha_{2}q\leq p_{2}\leq\beta_{2}q\right\}

\displaystyle=\#\left\{p_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}:\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})=1\text{ and }\left(\frac{\alpha_{2}q}{p_{1}}\right)p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\left(\frac{\beta_{2}q}{p_{1}}\right)p_{1}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\varphi(p_{1})\left(\frac{\beta_{2}q}{p_{1}}-\frac{\alpha_{2}q}{p_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\varphi(p_{1})}{p_{1}}q\gamma.

Thus, recalling that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}}\sim\frac{6}{\pi^{2}}N

(see
[
10
]
), we have that there exists some
C>0,Q>0
such that
if
q\geq Q
we have

\sum_{p_{1}=\lceil\alpha_{1}q\rceil}^{\lfloor\beta_{1}q\rfloor}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{2}=\lceil\alpha_{2}q\rceil\\
\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})=1\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor\beta_{2}q\rfloor}{1}}\geq\frac{q\gamma}{2}\left(\sum_{p_{1}=\lceil\alpha_{1}q\rceil}^{\lfloor\beta_{1}q\rfloor}{\frac{\varphi(p_{1})}{p_{1}}}\right)\geq Cq\gamma(\beta_{1}q-\alpha_{1}q)=Cq^{2}\gamma^{2}.

This completes the proof of (
7
) in the case that
d=2
.

Next suppose that (
7
) has been established for

d=k
. We will now show that it also holds when
d=k+1
, and so the
proof is then completed by induction. In the case when
d=k+1
, we are
interested in

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(q):=\left\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},p_{d+1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d+1}:\gcd(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},p_{d+1})=1\text{ and }\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,d+1\right\}.

However, notice that

\displaystyle\left\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},p_{d+1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d+1}:\gcd(p_{1},\dots,p_{d})=1\text{ and }\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,d+1\right\}\subset\mathcal{P}(q).

Now, by our inductive hypothesis,

\displaystyle\#\left\{(p_{1},\dots,p_{d},p_{d+1})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d+1}:\gcd(p_{1},\dots,p_{d})=1\text{ and }\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,d+1\right\}

\displaystyle\phantom{==================}=\sum_{p_{d+1}=\lceil\alpha_{d+1}q\rceil}^{\lfloor\beta_{d+1}q\rfloor}\left(\sum_{p_{1}=\lceil\alpha_{1}q\rceil}^{\lfloor\beta_{1}q\rfloor}{\sum_{p_{2}=\lceil\alpha_{2}q\rceil}^{\lfloor\beta_{2}q\rfloor}{\dots{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{d}=\lceil\alpha_{d}q\rceil\\
\gcd(p_{1},\dots,p_{d})=1\end{subarray}}^{\lfloor\beta_{d}q\rfloor}{1}}}}\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{==================}\geq C\sum_{p_{d+1}=\lceil\alpha_{d+1}q\rceil}^{\lfloor\beta_{d+1}q\rfloor}{q^{d}\gamma^{d}}

\displaystyle\phantom{==================}\geq Cq^{d}\gamma^{d}(\beta_{d+1}q-\alpha_{d+1}q)

\displaystyle\phantom{==================}\geq Cq^{d+1}\gamma^{d+1}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 7
.

Suppose
\pi=(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})
is a partition of
[m+n]
such
that for every
j=1,\dots,k
we have
\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert\geq 2
, as in
Section
3
. Suppose there is some

\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}
such that

\pi_{\ell}\cap(m+[n])\neq\emptyset
and
\lvert\pi_{\ell}\rvert\geq 3
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that the following
holds. For every
0<\gamma\leq 1
there exists
Q_{\gamma}>0
, such
that for every choice of
0\leq\alpha_{i}<\beta_{i}\leq 1

(
i=1,\dots,m
) with
\beta_{i}-\alpha_{i}=\gamma
, we have

\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q}\#\left\{\mathbf{p}\in P(\pi,\mathbf{q}):\forall i\in[m],\quad\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\right\}\geq C\gamma^{m}q^{m+n-1}

as long as
q\geq Q_{\gamma}
. If no such
\ell
exists, then the sum
above is bounded below by
C\gamma^{m}q^{m+n-2}\varphi(q)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathbf{q}\in Q(\pi)
with
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q
. Let us estimate

\#\left\{\mathbf{p}\in P(\pi,\mathbf{q}):\forall i\in[m],\quad\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\right\}.

We will do this by analysing our freedom of choice of
\mathbf{p}
in the
different components defined by
\pi
. For
j\in[1,b]
, let

N_{j}(\mathbf{q})=\#\left\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}:\gcd(\mathbf{p},\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))=1,\quad\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\quad(i=1,\dots,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert)\right\},

and note that for
j\in(a,b]
this is equivalent to

N_{j}(\mathbf{q})=\#\left\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert}:\gcd(\mathbf{p})=1,\quad\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\quad(i=1,\dots,\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert)\right\}.

Then we have

\#\left\{\mathbf{p}\in P(\pi,\mathbf{q}):\forall i\in[m],\quad\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\right\}=\prod_{j=1}^{b}N_{j}(\mathbf{q}).

For
j\in(a,b]
, Lemma
6
tells us that there
exists
C_{j},Q_{j,\gamma}>0
such that

N_{j}(\mathbf{q})\geq C_{j}q^{\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert}\gamma^{\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert}

whenever
q\geq Q_{j,\gamma}
.

For
j\in[1,a]
, we consider two sub-cases. First, if
\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert\geq 2
, then

N_{j}(\mathbf{q})\geq\#\left\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}:\gcd(\mathbf{p})=1,\quad\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}q\quad(i=1,\dots,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert)\right\},

so, as above, Lemma
6
tells us that there exists

C_{j},Q_{j,\gamma}>0
such that

N_{j}(\mathbf{q})\geq C_{j}\gamma^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}q^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}

whenever
q\geq Q_{j,\gamma}
. Otherwise, we have

\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert=1
, in which case,

\displaystyle N_{j}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle=\#\{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\gcd(p,\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))=1,\quad\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}(q)\quad(i=1,\dots,|\pi_{j}\cap[m]|)\}

\displaystyle=\#\{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\gcd(p,\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q})))=1,\quad\alpha_{i}q\leq p_{i}\leq\beta_{i}(q)\quad(i=1,\dots,|\pi_{j}\cap[m]|)\}

\displaystyle=\#\left\{p\in{\mathbb{Z}}:\gcd(p,\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q})))=1,\phantom{\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}q}{\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q})}\right)}\right.

\displaystyle\phantom{=======}\left.\left(\frac{\alpha_{i}q}{\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))}\right)\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))\leq p_{i}\leq\left(\frac{\beta_{i}q}{\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))}\right)\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))\quad(i=1,\dots,|\pi_{j}\cap[m]|)\right\}.

Thus, by Lemma
5
, there exists

C_{j},Q_{j,\gamma}>0
such that

N_{j}(\mathbf{q})\geq C_{j}\gamma q\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q})))}{\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))}

for all
q\geq Q_{j,\gamma}
.

We conclude that there exists
C>0
and
Q_{\gamma}>0
such that

\prod_{j=1}^{b}N_{j}(\mathbf{q})\geq C\gamma^{m}q^{m}\prod_{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert=1}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q})))}{\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))}

holds for all
q\geq Q_{\gamma}
. Therefore, by Lemma
4
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q\\
\mathbf{q}\in Q(\pi)\end{subarray}}\prod_{j=1}^{b}N_{j}(\mathbf{q})

\displaystyle\geq C\gamma^{m}q^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q\\
\mathbf{q}\in Q(\pi)\end{subarray}}\prod_{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert=1}\frac{\varphi(\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q})))}{\gcd(\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))}

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}C\gamma^{m}q^{m+n-1}&\textrm{if $\ell$ exists,}\\[4.30554pt]
C\gamma^{m}q^{m+n-2}\varphi(q)&\textrm{if not,}\end{cases}

and the lemma is proved.
∎

6.
Uniformity

Suppose
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. For
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and a ball

B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, let

A_{n,m}(\mathbf{q},B)=A(\mathbf{q},B)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}:\exists\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},\quad\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{p}\in B\right\}

and

A_{n,m}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},B)=A^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},B)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}:\exists\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},\quad(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q})\in P(\pi),\quad\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{p}\in B\right\}

A_{n,m}^{\prime}(\mathbf{q},B)=A^{\prime}(\mathbf{q},B)=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}:\exists\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{m},\forall i\in[m],\quad\gcd(p_{i},\mathbf{q})=1,\quad\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{p}\in B\right\}.

Subscripts will be dropped when the context is clear. Notice that

(8)

\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\rvert=\min\{\lvert B\rvert,1\}.

Suppose
(B_{q})_{q\in\mathbb{N}}
is a
sequence of balls in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
. For
\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we will write

A(\mathbf{q})=A(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}),\quad A^{\pi}(\mathbf{q})=A^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}),\quad\text{and}\quad A^{\prime}(\mathbf{q})=A^{\prime}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert}).

Theorems
1
,
2
, and
3

can be phrased in terms of sets of the type we have just defined. It

measures of these sets.

The following lemmas show that for partitions
\pi
as in our main

A^{\pi}
and

have comparable measures, even
when intersected with arbitrary open sets, and that the same goes for

and
A
.

Lemma 8
.

Suppose
\pi=(\pi_{1},\dots,\pi_{k})
is a partition of
[m+n]
such
that for every
j=1,\dots,k
we have

\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert\geq 2
, as in Section
3
.

a)

Suppose there is some
\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}

such that
\pi_{\ell}\cap(m+[n])\neq\emptyset

\lvert\pi_{\ell}\rvert\geq 3
. Then there exists a constant

b)

If, on the other hand, no such

for all

Lemma 9
.

Let
n>2
. There exists a constant
C>0
such that for every open set

U\subset{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
there is some
Q_{U}>0
such that for all

q\geq Q_{U}
we have

\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q}\left\lvert A^{\prime}(\mathbf{q},B)\cap U\right\rvert\geq C\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\right\rvert\left\lvert U\right\rvert

for every ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Proof of Lemma
8
.

First, find a finite union
V
of disjoint balls contained in
U

such that
\lvert V\rvert\geq\lvert U\rvert/2
. (In principle, we could get as
close to the measure of
U
as we want.) Without loss of generality,
we assume all the balls in
V
have the same radius,
\gamma>0
.

Now let
W\subset{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
be any ball of radius
\gamma
. It will be
enough to show that

(9)

\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q}\left\lvert A^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},B)\cap W\right\rvert\geq C^{\prime}\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\right\rvert\left\lvert W\right\rvert

for all
q\geq Q_{\gamma}
, where
C^{\prime}>0
is some absolute constant which
may depend on
n,m
, and
\pi
, and
Q_{\gamma}
only depends on

\gamma
. Importantly for us,
Q_{\gamma}
does not depend on
B
and so, given
(
9
), one can deduce the lemma with

C=C^{\prime}/2
.

Let us write
\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{nm}
as
\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{x}_{m})

where
\mathbf{x}_{j}
are column vectors. Then for any
\mathbf{q}\in Q(\pi)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we have
that

\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{X}_{a},\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{X}_{a+1},\dots,\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{X}_{b})\in{\mathbb{I}}^{m},

where
\mathbf{X}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{a}
are the projections to the components
corresponding to
\pi_{1}\cap[m],\dots,\pi_{a}\cap[m]
, respectively,
and
\mathbf{X}_{a+1},\dots,\mathbf{X}_{b}
are the projections to the components
corresponding to
\pi_{a+1},\dots,\pi_{b}
, respectively. For

j\in[1,a]
,
\mathbf{X}_{j}
is an
n\times\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert
-matrix, and
for
j\in(a,b]
,
\mathbf{X}_{j}
is an
n\times\lvert\pi_{j}\rvert
-matrix.

The condition that

\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{p}\in B\qquad\textrm{for}\qquad\mathbf{p}=(p_{1},\dots,p_{m})\in P(\pi,\mathbf{q})

is equivalent to
\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{X}_{j}+\pi_{j}(\mathbf{p})\in\pi_{j}(B)
for each
j
. Therefore, we
have

(10)

A_{n,m}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},B)\cap W=\left\lparen\prod_{j=1}^{b}A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))\right\rparen\cap W=\prod_{j=1}^{b}A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))\cap W_{j},

noting that when
j\in(a,b]
we have
\pi_{j}\cap[m]=\pi_{j}
, and
where
W_{j}
is the projection of
W
to the copy of

{\mathbb{I}}^{n(\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert)}
corresponding to the
j
th component of
the above product.

Let us now find the (
n(\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert)
-dimensional Lebesgue)
measure of

A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))\cap W_{j}.

We will accomplish the task in one fell swoop, but it is worth
bearing in mind that there are two interpretations of what follows,
depending on whether
j\in[1,a]
or
j\in(a,b]
. In the latter case,
we have
\pi_{j}\cap[m]=\pi_{j}
and the condition

\gcd(\mathbf{p},\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))
is the same as
\gcd(\mathbf{p})=1
.

Proceeding, we have

A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))\cap W_{j}=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{n\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}:\exists\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert},\quad\gcd(\mathbf{p},\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))=1,\quad\mathbf{q}\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{p}\in\pi_{j}(B)\right\},

having noted that in this case the condition
\mathbf{p}\in P(\pi_{j},\mathbf{q})
is

\gcd(\mathbf{p},\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))=1
. Now we express points

\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{n\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}
as matrices with columns

\mathbf{x}=(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert})
(having relabelled
coordinates), so that the condition of interest is that

\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{i}-p_{i}
lies in an interval of side-length
2r
, the
projection of
W_{j}
to the
i
th coordinate. Suppose that

\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert
is achieved in the first coordinate,
q_{1}=q
. For any

\mathbf{z}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{(n-1)\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}
(representing the coordinates in the
rows
2,\dots,n
of
{\mathbb{I}}^{n\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}
), let

S_{\mathbf{z}}=\left\lparen A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))\cap W_{j}\right\rparen_{\mathbf{z}}=A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))_{\mathbf{z}}\cap(W_{j})_{\mathbf{z}}

be the cross-section through
\mathbf{z}
parallel to the

\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert
-dimensional space spanned by the coordinates in the
first row. Then

\displaystyle\left\lvert A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))\cap W_{j}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{I}}^{(n-1)\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}}\lvert S_{\mathbf{z}}\rvert\,d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=\int_{Y_{j}}\lvert S_{\mathbf{z}}\rvert\,d\mathbf{z}

where
Y_{j}
is the projection of
W_{j}
to the last
n-1
rows’
coordinates. Meanwhile,
(W_{j})_{\mathbf{z}}
is a
\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert
-dimensional
ball and

\displaystyle A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))_{\mathbf{z}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}:\exists\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert},\quad\gcd(\mathbf{p},\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))=1,\quad\mathbf{q}\binom{\mathbf{x}}{\mathbf{z}}-\mathbf{p}\in\pi_{j}(B)\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathbb{I}}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}:\exists\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert},\quad\gcd(\mathbf{p},\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))=1,\quad q\mathbf{x}+\mathbf{q}\binom{\mathbf{0}}{\mathbf{z}}-\mathbf{p}\in\pi_{j}(B)\right\}.

It is a union of disjoint balls of diameter

\frac{\lvert B\rvert^{1/m}}{q}
with centers at the points
\frac{\mathbf{p}}{q}

such that
\gcd(\mathbf{p},\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))=1
in

({\mathbb{I}}^{nm})_{\mathbf{z}}\cong{\mathbb{I}}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}
, translated by

\mathbf{q}\binom{\mathbf{0}}{\mathbf{z}}+\operatorname{center}(\pi_{j}(B))
. Let

N_{j}(\mathbf{q},W)
be the number of such center points which are also
contained in the ball
\frac{1}{2}W_{j}
in
{\mathbb{I}}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}
,
that is,

N_{j}(\mathbf{q},W)=\#\left\{\mathbf{p}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}:\gcd(\mathbf{p},\pi_{j}(\mathbf{q}))=1,\quad\mathbf{p}/q\in\frac{1}{2}W_{j}+t\right\},

where
t
is the translation vector as above; its precise value
does not matter. The reason for bringing our attention to the
shrunken ball
\frac{1}{2}W_{j}
is that each relevant
\mathbf{p}/q
is the
center of a diameter-
\lvert B\rvert^{1/m}/q
sub-ball of

A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))_{\mathbf{z}}
which is fully contained in

W_{j}
. We can therefore bound

\lvert S_{\mathbf{z}}\rvert\geq N_{j}(\mathbf{q},W)\frac{\lvert B\rvert^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert/m}}{q^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}}

and

\displaystyle\left\lvert A_{n,\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B))\cap W_{j}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\int_{Y_{j}}\lvert S_{\mathbf{z}}\rvert\,d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle\geq N_{j}(\mathbf{q},W)\frac{\lvert B\rvert^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert/m}}{q^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}}\int_{Y_{j}}d\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=N_{j}(\mathbf{q},W)\frac{\lvert B\rvert^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert/m}}{q^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}}\lvert Y_{j}\rvert

\displaystyle=N_{j}(\mathbf{q},W)\frac{\lvert B\rvert^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert/m}}{q^{\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}}(2\gamma)^{(n-1)\lvert\pi_{j}\cap[m]\rvert}

Note that the argument in this paragraph did not depend on the
supposition that
\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert
was achieved in the first of the
n

coordinates, so the measure calculation would have come out the same
regardless.

Now, combining (
10
) with the calculation above, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert A^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},B)\cap W\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{b}\left\lvert{A_{n,|\pi_{j}\cap[m]|}^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},\pi_{j}(B)})\cap W_{j}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lvert B\rvert}{q^{m}}(2\gamma)^{m(n-1)}\prod_{j=1}^{b}N_{j}(\mathbf{q},W).

Finally, Lemma
7
tells us that

\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q}\prod_{j=1}^{b}N_{j}(\mathbf{q},W)\geq\begin{cases}C\gamma^{m}q^{m+n-1}\\[4.30554pt]
C\gamma^{m}q^{m+n-2}\varphi(q),\end{cases}

as long as
q\geq Q_{\gamma}
, depending on whether there exists

\ell
as in the lemma’s statement. If there does exist such an

\ell
, then for
q\geq Q_{\gamma}
we will have

\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q}\left\lvert A^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},B)\cap W\right\rvert\geq\bar{C}\lvert B\rvert\gamma^{mn}q^{n-1}\overset{(\ref{eq:Ameasure})}{\gg}\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert=q}\left\lvert A(\mathbf{q},B)\right\rvert\left\lvert W\right\rvert,

where
\bar{C}
is a constant absorbing
C
and all of the powers of

2
appearing above. If there does not exist such an
\ell
, but

\lvert B_{q}\rvert
is non-increasing, then for large
Q
we will have

\displaystyle\sum_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\leq Q}\left\lvert A^{\pi}(\mathbf{q},B_{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert})\cap W\right\rvert

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{q\leq Q}\bar{C}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\gamma^{mn}q^{n-2}\varphi(q)

\displaystyle\gg\sum_{q\leq Q}\lvert B_{q}\rvert\gamma^{mn}q^{n-1}.

The last estimate comes from the fact that the average order of

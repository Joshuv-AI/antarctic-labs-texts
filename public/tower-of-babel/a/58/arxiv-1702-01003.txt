New results on sum-product type growth over fields Partially supported by the Leverhulme Trust Grant RPG 2017-371. This work was supported in part by the Program of the Presidium of the Russian Academy of Sciences 01 ”Fundamental Mathematics and its Application” under grant PRAS-18-01.
Brendan Murphy, Giorgis Petridis, Oliver Roche-Newton

Abstract
We prove a range of new sum-product type growth estimates over a general field 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , in particular the special case 𝔽 = 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p} . They are unified by the theme of “breaking the 3 / 2 3/2 threshold”, epitomising the previous state of the art. This concerns two pivotal for the sum-product theory questions, which are lower bounds for the number of distinct cross-ratios determined by a finite subset of 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , as well as the number of values of the symplectic form determined by a finite subset of 𝔽 2 \mathbb{F}^{2} . We establish the estimate | R ⁡ [ A ] | ≳ | A | 8 / 5 |R[A]|\gtrsim|A|^{8/5} for cardinality of the set R ⁡ [ A ] R[A] of distinct cross-ratios, defined by triples of elements of a (sufficiently small if 𝔽 \mathbb{F} has positive characteristic, similarly for the rest of the estimates) set A ⊂ 𝔽 A\subset\mathbb{F} , pinned at infinity. The cross-ratio bound enables us to break the threshold in the second question: for a non-collinear point set P ⊂ 𝔽 2 P\subset\mathbb{F}^{2} , the number of distinct values of the symplectic form ω \omega on pairs of distinct points u , u ′ u,u^{\prime} of P P is | ω ⁡ ( P ) | ≳ | P | 2 / 3 + c , |\omega(P)|\gtrsim|P|^{2/3+c}, with an explicit c c . Symmetries of the cross-ratio underlie its local growth properties under both addition and multiplication, yielding an onset of growth of products of difference sets, which is another main result herein. Our proofs strongly use specially suited applications of new incidence bounds over 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , which apply to higher moments of representation functions. The technical thrust of the paper is using additive combinatorics to relate and adapt these higher moment bounds to growth estimates. A particular instance of this is breaking the threshold in the few sums, many products question over any 𝔽 \mathbb{F} , by showing that if A A is sufficiently small and has additive doubling constant M M , then | A ​ A | ≳ M − 2 ​ | A | 14 / 9 |AA|\gtrsim M^{-2}|A|^{14/9} . This result has a second moment version, which allows for new upper bounds for the number of collinear point triples in the set A × A ⊂ 𝔽 2 A\times A\subset\mathbb{F}^{2} , the quantity often arising in applications of geometric incidence estimates.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field.
We assume throughout that
\mathbb{F}
has positive characteristic
p
, let
\mathbb{F}_{p}
denote the corresponding prime residue field and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
the field with
q
elements and characteristic
p
. We view
p
as a large asymptotic parameter. In the case of small
p
, the constraints in terms of
p
we impose trivialise the results; these results extend to zero characteristic by removing the latter constraints and other terms containing
p
.

For a finite
A\subset\mathbb{F}
, we use the standard notation

A\pm A=\{a\pm a^{\prime}:\,a,a^{\prime}\in A\}

for the sum or difference set of
A
, respectively; similarly we use
AA
or
A/A
for the product set or ratio set of
A
.
The cardinality
|A|
is also viewed as an asymptotic parameter, however it has to be sufficiently small relative to
p>0
for the questions below to be valid.

This paper studies a range of questions in arithmetic and geometric combinatorics, arising directly in the context of the Erdős-Szemerédi sum-product conjecture. The inter-relation of these questions accounts for its length. We first outline heuristically these questions and make a brief case why they are important. We then present the formal list of questions and main results. They are paralleled to what is known in the special case of
\mathbb{F}
being the real (or complex) field, implying that in many instances what we can prove for a general
\mathbb{F}
is not much worse, making one believe that the keys to the sum-product conjecture may be independent of the order properties of
\mathbb{R}
.

A central problem in arithmetic combinatorics is the
sum-product problem
, which asks for estimates of the form

\max(|A+A|,|AA|)\geq C_{\varepsilon}|A|^{1+\varepsilon}

(1)

for some
\varepsilon>0
and some constant
C_{\varepsilon}
, independent of
A
.

This question was originally posed for finite sets of integers
[
10
]
: Erdős and Szemerédi conjectured that (
1
) holds for all
\varepsilon<1
.
The sum-product problem has since been studied over a variety of fields and rings.
In positive characteristic, the first qualitative estimate (i.e., without an explicit lower bound for
\varepsilon
) of the form (
1
) was proved by Bourgain, Katz, and Tao
[
8
]
for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Today (for sufficiently small
A
if
p>0
), the best known results are that
\varepsilon
can be taken as any constant smaller than
2/9
for the general
\mathbb{F}

[
36
]
and smaller than
1/3+5/5277
for the special case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}

[
37
,
41
]
.

Until recently sum-product type estimates, dealing with sufficiently small sets in positive characteristic, mostly pertained to the case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, based on the arithmetic approach founded in
[
8
]
. They were quite a way off the real case, where the main tools were the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem, see e.g.
[
9
]
, and an order-based argument of Solymosi
[
51
]
. This changed recently after some geometric incidence theorems over the general
\mathbb{F}
have been developed, based on the fourth author’s point-plane incidence theorem
[
35
]
. See
[
26
]
for a review.

Already qualitative sum-product estimates over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
have had remarkable consequences as a fountainhead for the major theme of growth and pseudo-randomness throughout mathematics, as well as computer science and complexity theory. See, for example, a fairly recent review
[
16
]
by Helfgott for growth in Lie type groups. Here we do not address further the issue of external applications of sum-product bounds.

Notation
.

Since this paper is about inequalities, let us describe notation to this end.
We use the standard notation
f=O(g)
,
f\ll g
,
g=\Omega(f)
and
g\gg f
, meaning that there exists an absolute constant
C
such that
f\leq Cg
.
Both statements
f=\Theta(g)
and
f\approx g
stand for
f=O(g)
and
f=\Omega(g)
.

In the inequalities in question, our primary concern is powers of set cardinalities, first and foremost of
|A|
. However, at
two
instances throughout the paper our key estimates inadvertently acquire the term
\log|A|
: Theorems
11
and
34
below.
We decided not to keep track of the powers of
\log|A|
throughout most of the ensuing estimates, but for writing
\log|A|
explicitly in a few further estimates that then proliferate throughout, hoping that this will be sufficient for an interested reader to easily calculate powers of logarithms elsewhere.
Notation-wise, for positive quantities
f
and
g
, by
f\lesssim g
we mean that there is a constant
C>0
such that
f\ll(\log g)^{C}g
and the notation
f\gtrsim g
means that
g\lesssim f
, or
f\gg(\log f)^{-C}g
. If both
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
hold, then we write
f\sim g
.

The heuristic behind the sum-product problem is that a subset
A
of
\mathbb{F}
cannot simultaneously have strong additive and multiplicative structure (unless
A
is close to a sub-field). This suggests weaker formulations than (
1
). One is the
few sums, many products
question: is it true that if
|A+A|=M|A|
, then
|AA|\gtrsim M^{-C}|A|^{2}
, for some
C
? It was shown by Elekes and Ruzsa
[
9
]
that this question over the real/complex field is within reach of applications of the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem; this is even true in the stronger
L^{2}
-sense
[
37
]
. However, the known proofs of the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem
[
53
]
,
[
55
]
do not extend beyond
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
. In the context of the few sums, many products over a general
\mathbb{F}
, we establish the best known bound in the forthcoming Theorem
3
.

However, the key sum-product issue is the
weak Erdős-Szemerédi conjecture
, or the
few products, many sums
question: is this true that if
|AA|=M|A|
, then
|A+A|\gtrsim M^{-C}|A|^{2}
, for some
C
? This central question is wide open over any field but the rationals, where it was resolved by Bourgain and Chang
[
6
]
, the proof relying strongly on integers being a Principal Ideal Domain. For some best known results in the real category see
[
45
]
.

It was shown
[
18
]
that the weak Erdős-Szemerédi conjecture would easily follow from a sharp
L^{2}
-bound on the number of realisations function of the quantity
\omega(u,u^{\prime})
, where
u,u^{\prime}\in A\times A
and
\omega
is the symplectic form; such a bound was erroneously claimed in
[
17
]
in the wake of the resolution of the celebrated Erdős distinct distance conjecture by Guth and Katz
[
13
]
. Moreover, the Erdős-type question of the minimum number of distinct values of a non-degenerate bilinear form on a plane non-collinear set
P
now stands out as the major unsolved problem in plane discrete geometry, the forthcoming Conjecture
4
. Remarkably, unless the form is skew-symmetric, nothing better than a threshold lower bound
|P|^{2/3}
is known for any
\mathbb{F}
. But if
\omega
is the symplectic form and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
, there is a better bound
[
18
]
,
[
34
]
.
However, the proofs in the latter two papers strongly rely on Szemerédi-Trotter type theorems. It turns out that the ideas in these proofs can embrace slightly weaker incidence results. The forthcoming Theorem
5
breaks the above threshold bound in the general
\mathbb{F}
context.

The sum-product phenomenon concerns the affine group action on
\mathbb{F}
. This insight was key to the Cayley graph expansion theory founded by Helfgott, see
[
16
]
. Not surprisingly, the set of invariants under affine group action, generated by a finite set
A
and defined as
R[A]
by (
7
), plays a vital role in the quantitative study of the sum-product phenomenon. The forthcoming Theorem
6
provides threshold-breaking lower bounds for cardinality of
R[A]
in the general
\mathbb{F}
context. These bounds become key to establishing the above-mentioned Theorem
5
. It is well known but nonetheless remarkable that in the special case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
a near-sharp bound
|R[A]|\gtrsim|A|^{2}
follows from the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem, essentially underlying the
few sums-many products
argument of Elekes and Ruzsa
[
9
]
.

Thus the set
R[A]
, over a general
\mathbb{F}
, is the true protagonist in this paper. Members of
R[A]
can be viewed as cross-ratios defined by quadruples of pair-wise distinct elements of
A
, one of which elements has been pinned at infinity. The full cross-ratio is invariant under
SL_{2}
, acting on the projective line
\mathbb{F}P
as Möbius transformations. This is why the cross-ratio plays the central role in the proof of Theorem
5
: the symplectic form
\omega(u,u^{\prime})
is invariant to
SL_{2}
action on
\mathbb{F}^{2}
as linear transformations. Studying
SL_{2}
action on
\mathbb{F}P
therefore arises as another pivotal issue, closely related to the sum-product conjecture.

Lower bounds for cardinality of the set
C[A]
of distinct cross-ratios generated by
A\subset\mathbb{F}
constitute another unsolved central issue apropos of the sum-product phenomenon. The bound
|C[A]|\gtrsim|A|^{2+2/11}
over
\mathbb{C}
was recently obtained by the fourth author
[
34
]
(where further discussion of the cross-ratio and its role throughout arithmetic growth may give a sceptical reader additional motivation to the scope of this paper). For general fields the best results we can so far prove for
C[A]
are those for its subset
R[A]
.

1.1
Key questions and theorems

We start out with a question not yet mentioned, yet also directly dependent on the symmetry properties of the set
R[A]
, as well as its cardinality.

Question 1

(Difference sets are not multiplicatively closed)
.

Given a finite subset
A
of a field, let
D=A-A
denote its difference set.
Do we have an estimate of the form

|DD|\geq|D|^{1+\varepsilon}

(2)

for some
\varepsilon>0
?

When
A
is a subset of
\mathbb{R}
, fifth listed author
[
44
]
proved

|DD|\gtrsim{|D|^{1+\frac{1}{12}}}.

See
[
34
]
for additional discussion as to taking more products of
D
with itself. We claim a bound in the form (
2
) over a general
\mathbb{F}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}
, with
|A|\leq p^{5/12}
and
|A|^{16}|A-A|^{30}\leq p^{25}
. Then

|(A-A)(A-A)|\gtrsim|A-A|^{4/5}|A|^{\frac{32}{75}}\geq|A-A|^{1+\frac{1}{75}}.

Note that these two conditions are satisfied for sufficiently small sets, and certainly hold if
|A|<p^{25/76}
. The first inequality of Theorem
1
implies that for sufficiently small
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}

|(A-A)(A-A)|\ll|A|^{2}\Rightarrow|A-A|\lesssim|A|^{2-\frac{1}{30}}.

This is the first partial result in the finite field setting towards a conjecture in
[
33
]
. See also
[
48
]
for related results.

More generally, it was conjectured in
[
3
]
that over the reals, for any
n
there is a sufficiently large
m
, so that

|D^{m}|\geq|D|^{n}.

That is, the difference set expands ad infinitum under multiplication.
It was shown in
[
3
]
that

|D^{3}|\gtrsim|A|^{17/8}.

We are also able to prove something similar here in the finite field setting, giving a six-variable expander with superquadratic growth.

Theorem 2
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
|A|\leq p^{25/77}
and write
D=A-A
. Then

|DD/D|\gtrsim|A|^{2+\frac{1}{25}}

The proofs of Theorem
2
and Theorem
1
are similar. In fact, it is possible to derive a quantitatively weaker but still non-trivial version of Theorem
1
as a consequence of Theorem
2
, via an extra application of the Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality.

Question 2

(Few sums, many products)
.

If
|A+A|\leq|A|^{1+\varepsilon}
for a small
\varepsilon>0
, is
|AA|\geq|A|^{2-\delta}
for
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon)>0
?
If so, can we take
\delta\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
?

As we have mentioned, this is a weaker version of the sum-product conjecture of Erdős and Szemerédi.
Over
\mathbb{R}
, Question
2
has been completely resolved by Elekes
and Ruzsa
[
9
]
, who showed that if
|A+A|\leq|A|^{1+\varepsilon}
, then

|AA|\gg\frac{|A|^{2-4\varepsilon}}{\log|A|}.

In
[
37
]

4\varepsilon
gets replaced by
3\varepsilon
, and the formulation is true involving
A-A
as well as in the stronger
L^{2}
-sense. Solymosi’s well-known result
[
51
]
replaces in the above inequality
4\varepsilon
with
2\varepsilon
but is specific to
A+A
.

In this vein
[
1
]
, it was shown that if
A
is a subset of a field of characteristic
p
satisfying
|A|<p^{3/5}
and
|A+A|\leq|A|^{1+\varepsilon}
then

|AA|\gg|A|^{\frac{3}{2}(1-\varepsilon)}.

Here we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
.
Then if
|A|\leq p^{5/9}

|A+\lambda A|^{21}|A/A|^{10}\gtrsim|A|^{37}

(3)

and if
|A|\leq p^{9/16}

|A+\lambda A|^{18}|AA|^{9}\gtrsim|A|^{32}

(4)

In particular, if
|A+\lambda A|=M|A|
then
|A/A|\gtrsim M^{-21/10}|A|^{8/5}
and
|AA|\gtrsim M^{-2}|A|^{14/9}
.

The most natural instances of Theorem
3
occur by taking
\lambda=\pm 1
, in which case
|A\pm A|\leq|A|^{1+\varepsilon}
implies

|AA|\gtrsim|A|^{\frac{14}{9}-2\varepsilon}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad|A/A|\gtrsim|A|^{\frac{8}{5}-\frac{21\varepsilon}{10}}.

Both exponents
14/9
and
8/5
exceed
3/2
, which was a “natural” threshold appearing in many earlier estimates
[
26
]
.

The third question we consider is geometric.
Let
\omega
be a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form on
\mathbb{F}^{2}
. Since the vector space of such forms is one-dimensional, without loss of generality
\omega
is the standard symplectic (or area) form

\omega[(u_{1},u_{2}),\,(u_{1}^{\prime},u_{2}^{\prime})]=u_{1}u_{2}^{\prime}-u_{2}u_{1}^{\prime}.

For a point set
P\subset\mathbb{F}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, what is the minimum cardinality of

\omega(P):=\{\omega(u,u^{\prime}):\,u,u^{\prime}\in P\}\setminus\{0\}?

(5)

In the exceptional case where
P
is supported on a single line through the origin, then
\omega(P)
can be empty.
If this is not the case, one can make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4
.

Suppose
|P|\leq p
.
Either
\omega(P)
is empty or

|\omega(P)|\geq|P|^{1-\varepsilon}

(6)

for all
\varepsilon>0
.

The estimate
|\omega(P)|\gg\min\{|P|^{2/3},p\}
holds over any field
\mathbb{F}
, see
[
35
, Section 6.1]
, which establishes (
6
) for
\varepsilon=1/3
.
This implies that if
|P|\gg p^{3/2}
, then
|\omega(P)|\gg p
; again, the exponent
3/2
appears as a natural threshold.
It is difficult to surpass this threshold even in characteristic zero, where the best known bound
[
18
]
is
|\omega(P)|\gg|P|^{96/137}
.

We break the threshold now in positive characteristic, which provides some evidence towards Conjecture
4
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\omega
be a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form on
\mathbb{F}^{2}
.
If
P\subset\mathbb{F}^{2}
is not supported on a single line through the origin and
|P|\leq p^{161/162}
, then

|\omega(P)|\gtrsim|P|^{108/161}.

Remarks
.

If the form
\omega
is symmetric, rather than skew-symmetric, nothing better than
|\omega(P)|\gg|P|^{2/3}
(with
|P|\leq p^{3/2}
if
p>0
) is known for any field
\mathbb{F}
, including the rationals; the latter estimate over the reals arises as a one-step application of the Szemerédi-Trotter theorem.

Conjecture
4
appears at the first glance to be similar to the renown Erdős distinct distance problem, which asked for the minimum size of the set
\Delta(P)
of pairwise distances between points of
P
.
Erdős conjectured that
|\Delta(P)|\gg|P|/\sqrt{\log|P|}
.
The conjecture was resolved (up to a power of
\log|P|
, which was at the mercy of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality) by Guth and Katz
[
13
]
who showed that

|\Delta(P)|\gg\frac{|P|}{\log|P|}.

An attempt to mimic the Guth-Katz approach to the Erdős distance problem to deal with Conjecture
4
fails: see
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
.
Today Conjecture
4
is a major open question in geometric combinatorics.

1.2
Key estimates

The following results form the backbone of the paper. In particular, they contain the estimates of cardinality the above-mentioned set
R[A]
, underpinning the above Theorems
3
and
5
.

Estimates for collinear triples and quadruples

The first pair of key estimates bound the number
T(A)
of
collinear triples
and the number
Q(A)
of
collinear quadruples
of
A
.
Geometrically,
T(A)
and
Q(A)
are the number of ordered collinear triples and quadruples in the point set
A\times A\subset\mathbb{F}^{2}
. They represent respectively the third and fourth moment of the representation function
i(l)
, where
l
is a line defined by a pair of points in
A\times A
.

Algebraically,
T(A)
is the number of solutions to

(a_{1}-a_{2})(a_{3}-a_{4})=(a_{1}-a_{5})(a_{3}-a_{6})

with all the
a_{i}\in A
, and
Q(A)
is the number of solutions to

(a_{1}-a_{2})/(a_{3}-a_{4})=(a_{1}-a_{5})/(a_{3}-a_{6})=(a_{1}-a_{7})/(a_{3}-a_{8})

with all the
a_{i}\in A
.
In the formula above we mean that if a denominator is zero, then all others denominators are zeros as well.
If
A
is chosen uniformly at random from
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, then we expect
T(A)\approx|A|^{6}/p
and
Q(A)\approx|A|^{8}/p^{2}
.
In Section
2.1
we show that the expected values hold for large subsets
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
In particular, we show that

\left|Q(A)-\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}\right|\ll|A|^{5}\log|A|,

which is sharp (up to the logarithmic factor).
These results, which we state formally as Theorem
11
in Section
2.2
, are based on an incidence bound of Stevens and de Zeeuw
[
52
]
, which adapts the bound of Rudnev
[
35
]
for point-plane incidences.
Theorem
11
and the results leading to it will be used throughout the paper.

Expansion properties for
R[A]

The second key estimate is a growth result for the set
R[A]
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}
. Set

R[A]=\left\{\frac{b-a}{c-a}:\,a,b,c\in A;\,b,c\neq a\right\}.

(7)

This set was studied by Jones
[
20
]
in the real setting, where it was proven that
|R[A]|\gg|A|^{2}/\log|A|
. Geometrically,
R[A]
is the set of “pinned” cross-ratios for the set
A\cup\{\infty\}
on the projective line in the form
[a,b,c,\infty]
, where
a,b,c\in A
. Recall that the cross-ratio of four pair-wise distinct points
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}
is defined as

[a,b,c,d]:=\frac{(a-b)(c-d)}{(a-c)(b-d)}.

For the set
R[A]
, the last variable is fixed at infinity.

The following result shows that any subset of
\mathbb{F}
determines a large number of pinned cross-ratios.

Theorem 6
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}
. The following expansion properties hold for
R[A]
.

1.

(Expansion for small sets) If
|A|\leq p^{5/12}
, then
\displaystyle|R[A]|\gg\frac{|A|^{8/5}}{\log^{8/15}|A|}.

2.

(Expansion for large sets) If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p},
then
|R[A]|\gg\min\left\{p,\frac{|A|^{5/2}}{p^{1/2}}\right\}.
In particular, if
|A|\geq p^{3/5}
, then
\displaystyle|R[A]|\gg p
.

3.

(Uniform expansion) If
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p},
then
|R[A]|\gg\min\{p,|A|^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{22}}\log^{-\frac{4}{9}}|A|\}
.

The bound

|R[A]|\gtrsim\min\{p,|A|^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{22}}\}.

improves the lower bound
|R[A]|\gg\min\{p,|A|^{\frac{3}{2}}\}
established in
[
1
]
.
Theorem
6
underlies all of the results in Section
3
.

An alternative interpretation of Theorem
6
is that it offers strong expansion for the rational function
R(x,y,z)=\tfrac{y-x}{z-x}
.
Pham, Vinh, and de Zeeuw
[
30
]
showed that a large class of quadratic polynomials
f
in three variables satisfy the expansion bound
|f(A)|\gg\min\{p,|A|^{3/2}\}
.
Theorem
6
offers stronger expansion for
R[A]
, though
R
is a rational function in three variables.
Such expanding polynomials have applications to theoretical computer science
[
4
,
57
,
58
]
.
See
[
30
]
for further discussion.

Notation

By
\mathbb{F}^{*}
we mean the multiplicative group of
\mathbb{F}
, by
\mathbb{F}^{d}
the
d
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}
, and by
\mathbb{F}\mathbb{P}^{d}
the
d
-dimensional projective space over
\mathbb{F}
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{F}
we write
\tfrac{x}{y}
instead of
xy^{-1}
and implicitly assume
y\neq 0
. We use representation function notations like
r_{RR}(x)
, which counts the number of ways
x\in\mathbb{F}
can be expressed as a product
r_{1}r_{2}
with
r_{1},r_{2}
in some finite set
R
.

Given a family
L
of lines in
\mathbb{F}^{2}
,
\mathcal{I}(A\times A,L)
is the number of point-line incidences between
A\times A
and
L
; that is, the number of ordered pairs
(u,\ell)\in(A\times A)\times L
such that
u\in\ell
.

We denote the additive energy of finite sets
A,B
in an abelian group
(G,+)
by

\mathsf{E}(A,B):=|\{(a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime})\in A\times A\times B\times B:\,a+b=a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}\}|,

and write
\mathsf{E}(A)
when
A=B
.
We have the trivial bounds

|A||B|\leq\mathsf{E}(A,B)\leq|A|^{2}|B|,|A||B|^{2}.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have the lower bounds

|A+A|,|A-A|\,\geq\,\frac{|A|^{4}}{\mathsf{E}(A)}.

(8)

Thus if
\mathsf{E}(A)=O(|A|^{2})
, then
|A+A|\gg|A|^{2}.

Note that
A+A
is the support of the convolution
A\ast A
of the characteristic function of
A
with itself and
\mathsf{E}(A)
is the second moment of
A\ast A
.
To this end, we will also use the third moment of convolution.
We define the
third moment
, alias third, or cubic, energy of finite sets
A,B
in an abelian group by

\mathsf{E}_{3}(A,B)=|\{(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in A^{3}\times B^{3}:\,a_{1}-b_{1}=a_{2}-b_{2}=a_{3}-b_{3}\}|=\sum_{x}r^{3}_{A-B}(x),

with the shorthand
\mathsf{E}_{3}(A)=\mathsf{E}_{3}(A,A)
.
We have the trivial bounds
|A|^{3}\leq\mathsf{E}_{3}(A)\leq|A|^{4}
.

By Hölder’s inequality, there is a lower bound for
|A-A|
in terms of
\mathsf{E}_{3}(A)
analogous to (
8
). However, if
\mathsf{E}_{3}(A)=O(|A|^{3})
this lower bound only implies that
|A-A|\geq|A|^{3/2}
.
See
[
39
]
for more on higher moments of convolution.

Since we are working over a field, we use the notations
\mathsf{E}^{+}
and
\mathsf{E}^{\times}
to distinguish between additive and multiplicative energy.

1.4
Structure of the rest of the paper

The rest of the paper is taken up by proofs of the above formulated main results and some corollaries. Section

, with a self-explanatory title, is dedicated to the proof of Theorem

Section

uses Theorem

to prove Theorem

.

Section

uses Theorem

to establish a new bound on the size of a set

Section

is exclusively dedicated to proving a slightly cheaper version of Theorem

Section

for some absolute

Remark
.

We would like to point out that as far as the set
\frac{A-A}{A-A}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}
is concerned, a much stronger, optimal up to a constant bound

\left|\frac{A-A}{A-A}\right|\geq\frac{|A|^{2}+3}{2},\;\;\;\mbox{for}\;\;\;|A|\leq\sqrt{p}

(11)

has been known since at least as early as the late 1990s. See, e.g., a review
[
54
]
by T. Szőnyi and the references contained therein. This is due to the fact that
\frac{A-A}{A-A}
(on the projective line) is the set of directions, determined by the point set
A\times A\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
. For a non-collinear point set
P\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
, with
|P|\leq p
,
[
54
, Theorem 5.2]
claims the general optimal lower bound
\frac{|P|+3}{2}
for the number of distinct directions, determined by pairs of points of
P
. It is easy to see that if
|P|>p
, then all
p+1
directions are determined.

We will use (
11
) in the proof of Theorem
2
. One may think of Theorem
2
as an extension of the above bound with additional variables.

The proof of
[
54
, Theorem 5.2]
belongs to the realm of what today is generally called
the polynomial method
. The polynomial method, in fact, also underlies the incidence results used in this paper, stemming from Theorem
7
.

Finally, Appendices A and B present, respectively, additional discussion and the proof of a somewhat technical Lemma
23
.

2.1
Incidence bounds

We will use the following version of the fourth listed author’s point-plane incidence bound
[
35
]
that is specialized to the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-setting and the case of equal number of points and planes. See
[
26
, Corollary 2]
for a proof.

Theorem 7
.

Let
p
be an odd prime,
P\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
and a collection
\Pi
of planes in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{3}
. Suppose that
|P|=|\Pi|
and that
k
is the maximum number of collinear points in
P
. The number of point-plane incidences satisfies

\mathcal{I}(P,\Pi)\ll\frac{|P|^{2}}{p}+|P|^{3/2}+k|P|.

We will also need bounds on point-line incidences in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
. For a line
\ell
and a point set
P
in the plane
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
,
i(\ell)
represents the number of points in
P
incident to
\ell.
In particular, for
P=A\times A
,

i(\ell)=|\ell\cap(A\times A)|.

(12)

The number of point-line incidences is defined by

\mathcal{I}(A\times A,L)=\sum_{\ell\in L}i(\ell).

The state of the art on point-line incidences is due to Stevens and de Zeeuw
[
52
]
.
See
[
26
, Theorem 7]
for the following version.

Theorem 8
.

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{F}
with
|A|\leq|B|
and let
L
be a collection of lines in
\mathbb{F}^{2}
.

If
\mathbb{F}
has positive characteristic
p
, assume that

|A||L|\leq p^{2}.

(13)

Then the number of incidences

\mathcal{I}(P,L)
between the point set
P=A\times B
and
L
is bounded by

\mathcal{I}(P,L)\ll|A|^{3/4}|B|^{1/2}|L|^{3/4}+|P|+|L|.

If we omit (
13
), then the following estimate holds for
A,B\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}

\mathcal{I}(P,L)\ll\frac{|A||B|^{1/2}|L|}{p^{1/2}}+|A|^{3/4}|B|^{1/2}|L|^{3/4}+|P|^{2/3}|L|^{2/3}+|L|.

It was shown in
[
35
]
that Theorem
7
generally cannot be improved. This is also the case with the Stevens-de-Zeeuw theorem, modulo factors of
\log|A|
.

The following lemma is based on a refinement of
[
8
, Lemma 2.1]
; see also
[
22
,
7
]
. We reformulate it in terms of the incidence function
i
defined in (
12
), c.f.
[
25
, Lemma 1]
. Sums are over all lines in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
and not just those incident to some point of
A\times A
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

\sum_{\text{ all lines }\ell}i(\ell)^{2}=|A|^{4}+p|A|^{2}.

In particular

\sum_{\text{ all lines }\ell}\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\leq p|A|^{2}.

This simple yet powerful lemma was implicitly extended to general point sets
P\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
by Vinh
[
56
]
. The paper
[
25
]
contains a proof and many applications.

Now we formulate the main lemma needed for the proof of Theorem
11
.
Let
M
be a parameter and set

L_{M}=\{\ell\in L:M<i(\ell)\leq 2M\}

(14)

to be the collection of lines from
L
that are incident to between
M
and
2M
points in
A\times A
.

Lemma 10
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
let
\displaystyle 2|A|^{2}/p\leq M\leq|A|
be an integer that is greater than 1. The set
L_{M}
defined in (
14
) satisfies

|L_{M}|\ll\min\left(\frac{p|A|^{2}}{M^{2}},\frac{|A|^{5}}{M^{4}}\right).

In particular, if
M\geq 2|A|^{2}/p>1
then

\sum_{\ell\in L_{M}}i(\ell)^{3}\ll\frac{|A|^{5}}{M}\text{ and }\sum_{\ell\in L_{M}}i(\ell)^{4}\ll|A|^{5}.

The bound
|A|^{5}/M^{4}
is superior when
M\geq|A|^{3/2}/p^{1/2}
.

Proof.

The second part of the lemma follows by the first claim because, say,

\sum_{\ell\in L_{M}}i(\ell)^{3}\leq 8M^{3}|L_{M}|.

First we show that for all
M\geq 2|A|^{2}/p>1

|L_{M}|\leq\frac{4p|A|^{2}}{M^{2}}.

(15)

The hypothesis
\displaystyle i(\ell)\geq\tfrac{2|A|^{2}}{p}
implies that
\displaystyle i(\ell)-\tfrac{|A|^{2}}{p}\geq\tfrac{i(\ell)}{2}\geq\tfrac{M}{2}
.

Equation (
9
) now implies

\frac{M^{2}}{4}|L_{M}|\leq\sum_{\ell\in L_{M}}\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{\text{ all lines }\ell}\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\leq p|A|^{2}.

Thus (
15
) follows.

To show that
|L_{M}|\ll|A|^{5}/M^{4}
for
M\geq 2|A|^{2}/p
, we consider two cases.
First, suppose that
M<\frac{2|A|^{3/2}}{p^{1/2}}
.
By Lemma
15
, we have

|L_{M}|\ll\frac{p|A|^{2}}{M^{2}}<\frac{p|A|^{2}}{M^{2}}\frac{4|A|^{3}}{pM^{2}}=\frac{4|A|^{5}}{M^{4}}.

Next, suppose that
M\geq 2|A|^{3/2}/p^{1/2}
; in this case,
we apply Theorem
8
. Therefore we must confirm the condition
|A||L_{M}|\leq p^{2}
. The hypothesis
M\geq 2|A|^{3/2}/p^{1/2}
implies
M\geq 2|A|^{2}/p
. Lemma
15
can therefore be applied and in conjunction with the hypothesis
M^{2}\geq 4|A|^{3}/p
gives

|L_{M}|\leq\frac{4p|A|^{2}}{M^{2}}\leq\frac{p^{2}}{|A|}.

Hence,
|A||L_{M}|\leq p^{2}
and Theorem
8
may be applied. It gives

M|L_{M}|\leq\sum_{\ell\in L_{M}}i(\ell)=\mathcal{I}(A\times A,L_{M})\ll|A|^{5/4}|L_{M}|^{3/4}+|A|^{2}+|L_{M}|.

If
2\leq M\leq 2C
, where
C
is the implicit constant in Theorem
8
, then

|L_{M}|\leq|A|^{4}\ll|A|^{5}/M^{4}.

On the other hand, if
M>2C
, it follows that

M|L_{M}|\ll|A|^{2}+|A|^{5/4}|L_{M}|^{3/4}.

Hence, using
M\leq|A|
,

|L_{M}|\ll\frac{|A|^{5}}{M^{4}}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{M}\ll\frac{|A|^{5}}{M^{4}}.

∎

Note that conditions
1<M\leq|A|
are not restrictive; the only condition of significance in the statement of Lemma
10
is
M\geq 2|A|^{2}/p
.

2.2
Collinear triples and quadruples

Many of the facts stated in this subsection are justified in detail in Appendix
A
. Let us first define ordered triples and quadruples of a Cartesian product point set.

Definition
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}
.
T(A)
is the number of ordered
collinear triples
in
A\times A
. That is ordered triples
(u,v,w)\in(A\times A)\times(A\times A)\times(A\times A)
such that
u
,
v
and
w
are all incident to the same line.
Q(A)
is the number of ordered
collinear quadruples
in
A\times A
.

In this section, we combine Theorem
8
with a generalisation of a lemma of Bourgain, Katz, and Tao
[
8
]
, to prove an improved bound for
T(A)
for large sets (here we use the assumption that
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
); and an optimal bound for
Q(A)
(here our result is stated and proved in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, but the argument works with small modifications in any field
\mathbb{F}
; if the characteristic of the field is zero then the
|A|^{8}/p^{2}
term disappears; if
\mathbb{F}
has positive characteristic, then the
|A|^{8}/p^{2}
disappears provided that
|A|
is small enough in terms of the characteristic
p
).

We begin with an formula for
T(A)
and
Q(A)
in terms of the incidence function defined in (
12
).

T(A)=\sum_{\ell:i(\ell)>1}i(\ell)^{3}+O(|A|^{4})\quad\text{ and }\quad Q(A)=\sum_{\ell:i(\ell)>1}i(\ell)^{4}+O(|A|^{4}).

(16)

Alternative expressions for
T(A)
and
Q(A)
involve ratios of differences of elements of
A
. To this end we define two functions

t(x)=|\{(a,b,c)\in A\times A\times A\colon x=\tfrac{b-a}{c-a}\}|

(17)

and

q(x,y)=|\{(a,b,c,d)\in A\times A\times A\times A\colon\tfrac{b-a}{c-a}=x\,,\,\tfrac{d-a}{c-a}=y\}|.

(18)

We have the following two identities

T(A)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}t(x)^{2}+|A|^{4}

(19)

and

Q(A)=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}q(x,y)^{2}+|A|^{5}.

(20)

Finally, there are formulas for
T(A)
and
Q(A)
in terms of the multiplicative energies of additive shifts of
A
.

T(A)=\sum_{a,b\in A}\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A-a,A-b)\,,

(21)

and

Q(A)=\sum_{a,b\in A}\mathsf{E}_{3}^{\times}(A-a,A-b)\,.

(22)

Next, we state what is known for collinear triples. The contribution to collinear triples coming from the
|A|
horizontal lines incident to
|A|
points in
A\times A
is
|A|^{4}
. All other collinear triples can be counted by the number of solutions to

(b-a)(c^{\prime}-a^{\prime})=(b^{\prime}-a^{\prime})(c-a)\;,\;a,b,\dots,c^{\prime}\in A.

(23)

It follows from this that the expected number of (solutions to (
23
) and therefore of) collinear triples in
A\times A
where the elements of
A
are chosen uniformly at random is
\displaystyle\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}+O(|A|^{4})
.

Another interesting example is that of sufficiently small arithmetic progressions: if
A=\{1,\dots,\sqrt{p}/3\}\subset\mathbb{Z}
then
T(A)\gg|A|^{4}\log^{\gamma}|A|
, for some absolute
0<\gamma<1
, due to Ford
[
11
]
.

Over the reals, Elekes and Ruzsa observed in
[
9
]
that the Szemerédi-Trotter point-line incidence theorem
[
53
]
implies

T(A)\ll|A|^{4}\log|A|.

Because of this and the two examples discussed above, it is possible that the inequality

T(A)\ll\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}+|A|^{4}\log|A|

is correct up to logarithmic factors in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
.

Far less is currently known. It is straightforward to obtain, say from the forthcoming Lemma
9
,

\left|T(A)-\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}\right|=O(p|A|^{3}).

(24)

It follows that if
|A|\gg p^{2/3}
, then
T(A)\approx|A|^{6}/p.

Combining this bound with
[
1
, Proposition 5]
gives

T(A)\ll\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}+|A|^{9/2},

(25)

however this does not improve the range where
T(A)\ll\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}
.

Similar results are true for collinear quadruples. The expected number of collinear quadruples is
\displaystyle\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+O(|A|^{5})
. The above mentioned result of
[
1
]
implies that
Q(A)\ll|A|^{11/2}
when
|A|\leq p^{2/3}
. One expects that the correct order of magnitude up to logarithmic factors is

Q(A)\ll\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+|A|^{5}\log|A|.

Once again, large random sets and small arithmetic progressions offer (nearly) extremal examples.

We present an improvement on (
25
) when
|A|\gg p^{1/2}
and establish a nearly best possible bound for
Q(A)
.

Theorem 11
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

1.

The number of collinear triples in
A\times A
satisfies

T(A)=\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}+O(p^{1/2}|A|^{7/2}).

So there is at most a constant multiple of the expected number of collinear triples when
|A|\gg p^{3/5}
.

2.

The number of collinear quadruples in
A\times A
satisfies

Q(A)=\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+O(|A|^{5}\log|A|),

which is optimal up to perhaps logarithmic factors.

The theorem leads to improvements on some of the exponential sum estimates in
[
24
,
29
]
. We do not pursue this direction in the current paper.

The first step in the proof of Theorem
11
is the proof of inequality (
24
).

Proof of Inequality (
24
).

Using Lemma
9
and the formula
\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)=(p+1)|A|^{2}
, one has

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}i^{3}(\ell)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}+\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}+\frac{2|A|^{2}}{p}\sum_{\ell}i^{2}(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}}\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}+\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}+2|A|^{4}-\frac{|A|^{6}}{p^{2}}\,.

Thus by (
16
)

\left|T(A)-\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}-2|A|^{4}+\frac{|A|^{6}}{p^{2}}\right|=\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}+O(|A|^{4}).

(26)

Since any line contains at most
|A|
points of
A\times A
, we have
i(\ell)\leq|A|
for all
\ell
.
Thus by Lemma
9
,

\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\leq|A|\sum_{\ell}\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\leq p|A|^{3},

which proves Equation
24
.
∎

To prove Theorem
11
, we will improve the last step of the preceding proof by splitting into cases where
i(\ell)\leq\Delta
and
i(\ell)>\Delta
for some parameter
\Delta>0
.

Proof of Theorem
11
.

We prove the following asymptotic estimates for the number of collinear triples and quadruples, from which the theorem follows from
|A|\leq p
:

\left|T(A)-\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}-2|A|^{4}+\frac{|A|^{6}}{p^{2}}\right|\ll p^{1/2}|A|^{7/2}

(27)

and

\left|Q(A)-\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}\right|\ll|A|^{5}\log|A|.

(28)

We begin with (
27
). By equation (
26
), we have

\left|T(A)-\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}-2|A|^{4}+\frac{|A|^{6}}{p^{2}}\right|=\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}+O(|A|^{4}).

We will show that

\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\ll p^{1/2}|A|^{7/2}.

(29)

This will finish the proof of (
27
), because
|A|\leq p
.

Let
\Delta>0
be a parameter, which we will specify later.
Split the sum on the left-hand side of (
29
) into two parts:

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i(\ell)\leq\Delta}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i(\ell)>\Delta}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=I+II.

To bound term
I
, we use Lemma
9
:

I=\sum_{i(\ell)\leq\Delta}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\leq\Delta\sum_{\ell}\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}=\Delta p|A|^{2}.

To bound term
II
, we first suppose that
\Delta\geq 2|A|^{2}/p
, an assumption that we will show is satisfied by our choice of
\Delta
.
This allows us to write

II=\sum_{i(\ell)>\Delta}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\ll\sum_{i(\ell)>\Delta}i(\ell)^{3}.

Now we use dyadic decomposition and Lemma
10
to bound the right-hand side of the previous equation:

\displaystyle\sum_{i(\ell)>\Delta}i(\ell)^{3}

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{k\geq 0}(2^{k}\Delta)^{3}|L_{2^{k}\Delta}|\ll\sum_{k\geq 0}(2^{k}\Delta)^{3}\frac{|A|^{5}}{(\Delta 2^{k})^{4}}\ll\frac{|A|^{5}}{\Delta}.

(Note that our application of Lemma
10
is justified, since
2^{k}\Delta\geq 2|A|^{2}/p
for all
k\geq 0
.)

All together, we have

\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\ll\Delta p|A|^{2}+\frac{|A|^{5}}{\Delta}.

Choosing
\Delta=|A|^{3/2}/p^{1/2}
yields

\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\ll p^{1/2}|A|^{7/2},

assuming that
|A|^{3/2}/p^{1/2}\geq 2|A|^{2}/p
.
If not, we have
p<4|A|
, thus by (
24
), we have

\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\ll p|A|^{3}\ll p^{1/2}|A|^{7/2}.

Thus in any case, we have (
29
).

Next we prove (
28
). Arguing as in the proof of (
24
), by (
16
), Lemma
9
and the asymptotic formula for
T(A)
, we have

\displaystyle Q(A)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)^{4}+O(|A|^{4})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)^{2}\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}-\frac{2|A|^{2}}{p}T(A)+\frac{|A|^{4}}{p^{2}}(|A|^{4}+p|A|^{2})+O(|A|^{4})

\displaystyle=\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+O\left(\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}\right)+O\left(\frac{|A|^{11/2}}{p^{1/2}}\right)+\sum_{\ell}i(\ell)^{2}\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\,.

Now we estimate the sum, first by removing the portion where
i(\ell)<2|A|^{2}/p

\sum_{i(\ell)<2|A|^{2}/p}i(\ell)^{2}\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{4|A|^{4}}{p^{2}}p|A|^{2}=\frac{4|A|^{6}}{p},

and then by applying the second part of Lemma
10
to each of the
O(\log|A|)
remaining dyadic sums of the form
\displaystyle\sum_{2^{i}\leq i(\ell)\leq 2^{i+1}}i(\ell)^{4}
with
2|A|^{2}/p\leq 2^{i}\leq|A|

\sum_{i(\ell)\geq 2|A|^{2}/p}i(\ell)^{2}\left(i(\ell)-\frac{|A|^{2}}{p}\right)^{2}\ll\sum_{i(\ell)\geq 2|A|^{2}/p}i(\ell)^{4}\ll|A|^{5}\log|A|.

Since the terms
|A|^{6}/p
and
|A|^{11/2}/p^{1/2}
are both smaller than
|A|^{5}
, we have

Q(A)=\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+O(|A|^{5}\log|A|).

∎

2.3
Proof of part 2 of Theorem
6

Recall that part 2 of Theorem
6
states that
|R[A]|\gg\min\left\{p,\frac{|A|^{5/2}}{p^{1/2}}\right\}.

Proof of Part 2 of Theorem
6
.

R[A]
is the support of the function
t(x)
defined in (
17
). Note also that

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}t(x)=|A|^{2}(|A|-1)

because each ordered triplet
(a,b,c)\in A\times A\times A
with
c\neq a
contributes precisely 1 to the sum.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

|R[A]|=|\mathrm{supp}(t)|\geq\frac{\left(\sum_{x}t(x)\right)^{2}}{\sum_{x}t(x)^{2}}\gg\frac{|A|^{6}}{\sum_{x}t(x)^{2}}.

By identity (
19
) and Part 1 of Theorem
11
we have

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}t(x)^{2}\leq T(A)\ll\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}+p^{1/2}|A|^{7/2}.

Substituting above yields

|R[A]|\gg\min\left\{p,\frac{|A|^{5/2}}{p^{1/2}}\right\}.

(30)

When
|A|\geq p^{3/5}
,
p\leq\tfrac{|A|^{5/2}}{p^{1/2}}
and the claim follows.
∎

Also note that if
|A|>p^{3/5+\varepsilon}
, then
|R[A]|=p(1-\varepsilon)
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

2.4
Proof of part 1 and part 3 of Theorem
6

Recall that Part 1 states that if
|A|\leq p^{5/12}
, then
|R[A]|\gtrsim|A|^{8/5}
; and Part 3 states that
|R[A]|\gtrsim\min\{p,|A|^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{22}}\}
.

Proof of part 1 and part 3 of Theorem
6
.

We begin with some preliminaries. We denote
R[A]
by
R
. Recall also the functions
t
and
q
defined in (
17
) and (
18
) respectively.

t(x)=|\{(a,b,c)\in A^{3}\colon\tfrac{b-a}{c-a}=x\}|\text{ and }q(x,y)=|\{(a,b,c,d)\in A^{4}\colon\tfrac{b-a}{c-a}=x,\tfrac{d-a}{c-a}=y\}|.

(31)

It follows from the definitions that

\sum_{x\in R}\sum_{y\in R}q(x,y)=|A|\sum_{x\in R}t(x)=|A|^{3}(|A|-1)\leq|A|^{4}.

(32)

We will exploit a symmetry of the set
R
, namely that
R=1-R
.
To prove this, simply note that

1-\frac{b-a}{c-a}=\frac{c-b}{c-a}=\frac{b-c}{a-c}.

(33)

This shows that
t(x)=t(1-x)
and
q(x,y)=q(1-x,1-y)
as well.

Let
G\subset R\times R
denote the set of pairs
(x,y)
such that
q(x,y)\not=0
.
Applying Cauchy-Schwarz to the inequality in (
32
) yields

|A|^{8}\ll\left(\sum_{(x,y)\in G}q(x,y)\right)^{2}\leq|G|\left(\sum_{x,y}q(x,y)^{2}\right).

(34)

To bound
|G|
, we use the elementary observation that if
(x,y)\in G
, then there exist
a,b,c,d\in A
such that
x=\tfrac{b-a}{c-a}
and
y=\tfrac{d-a}{c-a}
.
It follows that
\tfrac{x}{y}=\tfrac{b-a}{d-a}\in R
.
Since
q(x,y)=q(1-x,1-y)
, we also have
(1-x,1-y)\in G
, hence
\tfrac{1-x}{1-y}\in R
.

Setting
G_{x}=\{y\colon(x,y)\in G\}
we have

|G|=\sum_{x\in R}|G_{x}|\leq|R|^{1/2}\left(\sum_{x\in R}|G_{x}|^{2}\right)^{\tfrac{1}{2}}=|R|^{1/2}|\{(x,y,y^{\prime})\in R^{3}\colon(x,y),(x,y^{\prime})\in G\}|^{1/2}.

If
(x,y),(x,y^{\prime})\in G
, then there exist elements
r,r^{\prime}\in R
such that
x=ry
and
1-x=r^{\prime}(1-y^{\prime})
.
Thus

|\{(x,y,y^{\prime})\in R^{3}\colon(x,y),(x,y^{\prime})\in G\}|\leq|\{(r,r^{\prime},y,y^{\prime})\in R\colon 1-ry=r^{\prime}(1-y^{\prime})\}|.

The quantity on the right-hand side can be interpreted as the number of incidences between the pairs
(y,y^{\prime})
in
R\times R
and the set of
|R|^{2}
lines of the form

1-r^{\prime}=ry-r^{\prime}y^{\prime}.

Applying Theorem
8
yields

|\{(r,r^{\prime},y,y^{\prime})\in R\colon 1-ry=r^{\prime}(1-y^{\prime})\}|\ll\frac{|R|^{7/2}}{p^{1/2}}+|R|^{11/4}.

Thus

|G|\ll|R|^{15/8}+\frac{|R|^{9/4}}{p^{1/4}}.

(35)

The term in the second parenthesis of (
34
) is, by (
18
), bounded above by the number of collinear quadruples in
A\times A
.
By Part 2 of Theorem
11
we have

\sum_{x,y}q(x,y)^{2}\ll\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+|A|^{5}\log|A|.

(36)

The first term dominates only when
|A|^{3}\gg p^{2}\log|A|
. This range is out of the scope of Part 1. As for Part 3, Part 2 (proved above) guarantees that
|R|\gg p
and the conclusion follows. So we may assume that the second term dominates. Therefore,

\sum_{x,y}q(x,y)^{2}\ll|A|^{5}\log|A|.

(37)

Thus by (
34
), (
35
), and (
37
) we have

\frac{|A|^{3}}{\log|A|}\ll\frac{|R|^{9/4}}{p^{1/4}}+|R|^{15/8}.

This implies

•

if
|R|\leq p^{2/3}
, then the second term on the right dominates and

|R|\gg\frac{|A|^{8/5}}{\log^{8/15}|A|};

(38)

•

if
|R|\geq p^{2/3}
, then the first term on the right dominates and

|R|\gg\frac{p^{1/9}|A|^{4/3}}{\log^{4/9}|A|}.

(39)

Conclusion of proof of Part 1:

By (
38
), either
|R|\gg|A|^{8/5}\log^{-8/15}|A|
or
|R|\geq p^{2/3}
. The hypothesis
|A|\leq p^{5/12}
implies that
p^{2/3}\geq|A|^{8/5}
.

Conclusion of the proof of Part 3:

We combine the lower bounds in (
30
) and (
39
).
They are roughly equal when
|A|=p^{11/21}
.
Using (
30
) for
|A|\geq p^{11/21}
and (
39
) for
|A|\leq p^{11/21}
gives
|R|\gg|A|^{17/11}\log^{-4/9}|A|,
as claimed.

∎

3
Applications of Theorem
6

This section contains three applications of Theorem
6
.
The first application is Theorem
1
, which states that difference sets are not multiplicatively closed.
The second application is Theorem
16
, which shows that multiplicative subgroups of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
can only contain the difference set of small sets.
The third application is Theorem
5
, which is a lower bound for the size of the image of a point set
P\subset\mathbb{F}^{2}
under a skew-symmetric form
\omega
.

3.1
Products of difference sets

In this subsection we need three additional lemmata. The first lemma is a consequence of adapting the proof
[
12
, Theorem 3]
by applying Theorem
8
in place of the Szemerédi-Trotter Theorem.

Lemma 12
.

Let
A,B,C\subset\mathbb{F}
. Suppose,
|AC|\leq|(A-1)B|
and
|AC||B||C|\leq p^{2}
. Then

|AC|^{3}|(A-1)B|^{2}\gg|A|^{4}|B||C|.

Proof.

Without loss of generality
0\not\in C
. Let
l_{b,c}
be the line with equation
y=b(c^{-1}x-1)
and let
L=\{l_{b,c}:b\in B,c\in C\}
, so that
|L|=|B||C|
. Let
P=AC\times(A-1)B
.
Each line of
L
is incident to at least
|A|
points of
P
.

We will apply Theorem
8
with these sets of points and lines.
By assumption we have
|AC||B||C|\leq p^{2}
, and so (
13
) is satisfied.
Thus

|A||B||C|\leq\mathcal{I}(P,L)\ll|AC|^{3/4}|(A-1)B|^{1/2}|B|^{3/4}|C|^{3/4}+|AC||(A-1)B|+|B||C|.

If the second term dominates, then the desired lower bound follows from

|AC|,|(A-1)B|\gg|A|.

Thus we may assume that

|A||B||C|\leq\mathcal{I}(P,L)\ll|AC|^{3/4}|(A-1)B|^{1/2}|B|^{3/4}|C|^{3/4}+|B||C|.

Rearranging this inequality completes the proof.
∎

The second lemma is the Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality. A simple proof of the following formulation of the inequality can be found in
[
27
]
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
A
be a subset of an abelian group
(G,+)
. Then

|kA-lA|\leq\frac{|A+A|^{k+l}}{|A|^{k+l-1}}.

We will also use the following adaptation of the Plünnecke-Ruzsa inequality. See
[
21
, Corollary 1.5]
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
X,B_{1},\dots,B_{k}
be subsets of an abelian group
(G,+)
. Then there exists
X^{\prime}\subset X
such that
|X^{\prime}|\geq|X|/2
and

|X^{\prime}+B_{1}+\dots B_{k}|\ll\frac{|X+B_{1}|\cdots|X+B_{k}|}{|X|^{k-1}}.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Recall that Theorem
1
states that for
A\subset\mathbb{F}
with
|A|^{16}|A-A|^{30}\leq p^{25}
and
|A|<p^{5/12}
, we have

|(A-A)(A-A)|\gtrsim|A-A|^{4/5}|A|^{\frac{32}{75}}\geq|A-A|^{1+\frac{1}{75}}.

The second inequality follows from the trivial inequality
|A|\geq|A-A|^{1/2}
, and so it suffices to prove the first bound only.

We use the shorthand
R=R[A]
and
D=A-A
. The proof is split into two cases.

Case 1
:

Suppose that

|RD||D|^{2}\leq p^{2}.

(40)

Apply Lemma
12
with
A=R
and
B=C=D
. Note that, since it is assumed that
|RD||D|^{2}\leq p^{2}
, the conditions of Lemma
12
in the positive characteristic case are satisfied. Then, recalling the property that
R-1=-R
, we have

|RD|^{5}\gg|R|^{4}|D|^{2}.

We can apply the bound
|R|\gtrsim|A|^{8/5}
from Theorem
6
. This is valid by our assumption that
|A|<p^{5/12}
. It follows that

|RD|^{5}\gtrsim|A|^{\frac{32}{5}}|D|^{2}.

On the other hand, we can copy arguments from
[
44
]
to bound the left side of this inequality from above. Indeed, applying Lemma
13
in the multiplicative setting, we have

|RD|\leq|DD/D|\leq\frac{|DD|^{3}}{|D|^{2}}.

Therefore,

|DD|^{15}\geq|RD|^{5}|D|^{10}\gtrsim|A|^{32/5}|D|^{12},

as required.

Case 2
:

Suppose that

|RD||D|^{2}\geq p^{2}.

(41)

Similarly to the previous case, it follows from Lemma
13
that

p^{2}\leq|RD||D|^{2}\leq|DD/D||D|^{2}\leq|DD|^{3}

and so

|DD|^{75}\geq p^{50}\geq|A|^{32}|D|^{60},

where the last inequality follows from the assumption that
|A|^{16}|D|^{30}\leq p^{25}
.

This completes the proof in the case when
\mathbb{F}
has characteristic
p
. For fields with characteristic zero the arguments in Case 1 suffice to complete the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

Recall that Theorem
2
states that, for any
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
|A|\leq p^{25/77}
,

|DD/D|\gtrsim|A|^{2+\frac{1}{25}},

where again
D=A-A
.
The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
1
. Write
Q=(A-A)/(A-A)
and
R=R[A]
. At the outset, apply Lemma
14
to obtain a subset
Q^{\prime}\subset Q
with

|Q^{\prime}|\gg|Q|\gg|A|^{2}

(42)

and

\left|\frac{Q^{\prime}D}{D}\right|\ll\frac{|QD||Q/D|}{|Q|}=\frac{|DD/D|^{2}}{|Q|}.

(43)

The bound
|Q|\gg|A|^{2}
is a consequence of (
11
), a result which is essential for this proof to work. This is valid here because of our assumption that
|A|\leq p^{25/77}<p^{1/2}
.

We will prove, under our assumption on the size of
A
, that

|RQ^{\prime}|\gtrsim|A|^{2+\frac{2}{25}}.

(44)

Suppose that (
44
) holds. Then in particular, by (
43
) we have

|A|^{2+\frac{2}{25}}\lesssim|RQ^{\prime}|\leq\left|\frac{Q^{\prime}D}{D}\right|\ll\frac{|DD/D|^{2}}{|Q|}.

Rearranging this inequality and applying the bound
|Q|\gg|A|^{2}
, we obtain the desired result that

|DD/D|\gtrsim|A|^{2+\frac{1}{25}}.

It remains to prove (
44
). We may assume that
|RQ^{\prime}|\leq|A|^{2+\frac{2}{25}}
, as otherwise we are done. Also, we may assume that
|Q|\leq|A|^{2+\frac{1}{25}}
, as otherwise there is nothing to prove. With these two assumptions and the assumption that
|A|\leq p^{25/77}
, we have

|RQ^{\prime}||Q^{\prime}|^{2}\leq|A|^{6+\frac{4}{25}}\leq p^{2},

and so Lemma
12
can be applied to obtain the bound

|RQ^{\prime}|^{5}=|RQ^{\prime}|^{3}|(R-1)Q^{\prime}|^{2}\gg|R|^{4}|Q^{\prime}|^{2}.

We can apply the bound
|R|\gtrsim|A|^{8/5}
from Theorem
6
. This is valid by our assumption that
|A|<p^{5/12}
. In addition, we can apply the bound on
|Q^{\prime}|
from (
42
). Putting everything together, it follows that

|RQ^{\prime}|^{5}\gtrsim|A|^{52/5}.

This proves (
44
), and thus completes the proof of Theorem
2
.
∎

3.2
Application to multiplicative subgroups

In this section we show that Theorem
6
implies a new bound for the size of a set
A
such that
A-A
is contained in the union of a multiplicative subgroup and
\{0\}
.
Our Theorem
16
below improves Theorem 25 from
[
44
]
. In
[
46
]
Theorem
11
is used to prove that small multiplicative subgroups are additive irreducible.

We need a result on intersections of additive shifts of subgroups, e.g., see
[
47
]
.

Theorem 15
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a multiplicative subgroup,

k\geq 1
be a positive integer, and
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
be different nonzero elements.
Also, let

32k2^{20k\log(k+1)}\leq|\Gamma|\,,\quad p\geq 4k|\Gamma|(|\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{2k+1}}+1)\,.

Then

|\Gamma\cap(\Gamma+x_{1})\cap\dots(\Gamma+x_{k})|\leq 4(k+1)(|\Gamma|^{\frac{1}{2k+1}}+1)^{k+1}\,.

(45)

Further

|\Gamma\cap(\Gamma+x_{1})\cap\dots\cap(\Gamma+x_{k})|=\frac{|\Gamma|^{k+1}}{(p-1)^{k}}+\theta k2^{k+3}\sqrt{p}\,,

(46)

where
|\theta|\leq 1
.
The same holds if one replaces
\Gamma
in (
45
) by any cosets of
\Gamma
.

Now let us formulate the main result of this section.

Theorem 16
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
be a multiplicative subgroup and
\xi\neq 0
be an arbitrary residue.
Suppose that for some
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
one has

A-A\subset\xi\Gamma\sqcup\{0\}\,.

(47)

Then

•

If
|\Gamma|<p^{3/4}
then
|A|\lesssim|\Gamma|^{5/12}
.

•

If
p^{3/4}\leq|\Gamma|<p^{5/6}
then
|A|\lesssim p^{-5/8}|\Gamma|^{5/4}
.

•

If
|\Gamma|\geq p^{5/6}
, then

|A|\lesssim p^{-1}|\Gamma|^{5/3}
.

In particular, for any
\varepsilon>0
and sufficiently large
\Gamma
,
|\Gamma|\leq p^{6/7-\varepsilon}
the following holds

A-A\neq\xi\Gamma\sqcup\{0\}\,.

Proof.

We can assume that
|A|>1
.
Put
\Gamma_{*}=\Gamma\sqcup\{0\}
and
R=R[A]
.
Then in light of our condition (
47
), we have

R\subset(\xi\Gamma\sqcup\{0\})/\xi\Gamma=\Gamma_{*}\,.

(48)

Suppose that
|\Gamma|\leq p^{3/4}
.
Applying (
33
) and formula (
45
) with
k=1
, we obtain

|R|=|R\cap(1-R)|\leq|\Gamma_{*}\cap(1-\Gamma_{*})|\leq|\Gamma\cap(1-\Gamma)|+2\ll|\Gamma|^{2/3}\leq p^{1/2}\,.

Since
|A|\leq|R|<p^{1/2}<p^{5/12}
, we may apply formula (
38
) of Theorem
6
to get
|R|\gtrsim|A|^{8/5}
.
Hence
|A|^{8/5}\lesssim|\Gamma|^{2/3}
,
and we obtain the required result.

Now let us suppose that
|\Gamma|\geq p^{3/4}
but
|\Gamma|\leq p^{5/6}
.
In this case, by formula (
46
) and the previous calculations, we get

|R|\ll|\Gamma|^{2}/p\leq p^{2/3}\,.

Applying Theorem
6
(namely, formula (
38
)) one more time, we obtain

|A|^{8/5}\lesssim|\Gamma|^{2}/p
,
which gives the required bound for the size of
A
, namely
|A|\lesssim p^{-5/8}|\Gamma|^{5/4}
.

Now suppose that
|\Gamma|\geq p^{5/6}
.
We use the second formula from (
31
) and note that if
q(x,y)>0
, then
x-y\in\Gamma_{*}
.
By (
34
)–(
36
) and inclusion (
48
), we obtain

\displaystyle|A|^{8}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+|A|^{5}\log|A|\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{x,y}R(x)R(y)\Gamma_{*}(x-y)\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+|A|^{5}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{x}\Gamma_{*}(x)\Gamma_{*}(1-x)\sum_{y}\Gamma_{*}(y)\Gamma_{*}(1-y)\Gamma_{*}(x-y)\right)\,.

It is easy to see that the summand with
x=1
is negligible in the last inequality.
Using formula (
46
) with
k=2
and the condition
|\Gamma|\geq p^{5/6}
, we get

|A|^{8}\lesssim\left(\frac{|A|^{8}}{p^{2}}+|A|^{5}\right)\cdot\frac{|\Gamma|^{5}}{p^{3}}\ll|A|^{5}\cdot\frac{|\Gamma|^{5}}{p^{3}}\,.

It follows that
|A|\lesssim p^{-1}|\Gamma|^{5/3}
, as claimed.

Finally, it is known that if
A+B=\xi\Gamma
or
A+B=\xi\Gamma\sqcup\{0\}
, then
|A|\sim|B|\sim|\Gamma|^{1/2+\varepsilon}
for all
\varepsilon>0
, see
[
49
]
,
[
47
]
.
Thus, if
p^{5/6}\leq|\Gamma|\leq p^{6/7-\varepsilon}
,
we have
|A|\leq|\Gamma|^{1/2-\varepsilon/2}
which is a contradiction.
Another two bounds which were obtained before give us the same for smaller
\Gamma
. Thus, we have
\xi\Gamma\sqcup\{0\}\neq A-A
for sufficiently large
\Gamma
.

∎

3.3
Proof of a weaker version of Theorem
5

Recall that we assume
\omega
is defined by

\omega[(u_{1},u_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})]=u_{1}v_{2}-u_{2}v_{1},

and that
\omega(P)=\{\omega(p,p^{\prime})\colon p,p^{\prime}\in P\}\setminus{0}
.

In this section, we will show that
if
|P|\leq p^{191/192}
and
P
is not supported on a line through the origin, then

|\omega(P)|\gg|P|^{128/191},

(49)

which is slightly weaker than Theorem
5
.

We have chosen to do so to fix the ideas. The full proof of Theorem
5
is somewhat more technical, so we have relegated it to Appendix C.

We split the intermediate claims into short lemmata.

Lemma 17
.

Consider a point set
P\subset\mathbb{F}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
that is not contained in a single line.
If
P
contains at most
w
points per line, where
w\geq 1
, and
|P|<p\sqrt{w}
, then
|\omega(P)|\gg|P|/\sqrt{w}
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
T\subset\mathbb{F}
, with
|T|\leq p^{2/3}
. Then the number of solutions of the equation

\omega(u,u^{\prime})=\omega(v,v^{\prime}),\qquad u,u^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in T\times T

is
O(|T|^{13/2})
and the number of solutions to

\omega(u,u^{\prime})=s

for
s\not=0
fixed is
O(|T|^{11/4})
.

Let

r_{TT-TT}(s)=|\{(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})\in T^{4}\colon t_{1}t_{2}-t_{3}t_{4}=s\}|.

Then Lemma
18
states that

\sum_{s}r^{2}_{TT-TT}(s)\ll|T|^{13/2}

(50)

and for
s\not=0

r_{TT-TT}(s)\ll|T|^{11/4}.

(51)

For

|A||B||C|\leq p^{2}

|A(B+C)|\gg(|A||B||C|)^{1/2}.

Proof of Theorem
27
.

Let
a\in A
be arbitrary. Suppose that
|A+\lambda A|\leq|A|^{1+\frac{1}{45}}
. Then
|(A+\lambda a)+\lambda(A+\lambda a)|\leq|A|^{1+\frac{1}{45}}
. By Theorem
3
, which can be applied because of the assumption that
|A|\leq p^{9/16}
, we have

|(A+\lambda A)(A+\lambda A)|\geq|(A+\lambda a)(A+\lambda a)|\gtrsim|A|^{\frac{14}{9}-\frac{2}{45}}=|A|^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{90}}.

On the other hand, suppose that
|A+\lambda A|\geq|A|^{1+\frac{1}{45}}
. Then by Lemma
28

|(A+\lambda A)(A+\lambda A)|\gg|A||A+\lambda A|^{1/2}.

Therefore,

|(A+\lambda A)(A+\lambda A)|\gg|A|^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{90}},

as required.

We must verify that we have applied Lemma
28
correctly in the case when
\mathbb{F}
has positive characteristic. That is, we must verify the condition that
|A+\lambda A||A|^{2}\leq p
. Note that we may assume that
|A+\lambda A|\leq|A|^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{90}}
, otherwise
|(A+\lambda A)(A+\lambda A)|\geq|A+\lambda A|\geq|A|^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{90}}
and there is nothing to prove. Therefore,

|A|^{2}|A+\lambda A|\leq|A|^{\frac{158}{45}}\leq(p^{\frac{9}{16}})^{\frac{158}{45}}<p^{2}.

∎

Of particular interest is the case when
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
\lambda=-1
because it ties well with collinear triples. Bennett, Hart, Iosevich, Pakianathan, and the fourth listed author proved in
[
5
]
that if
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfies
|A|\gg q^{2/3}
, then
|(A-A)(A-A)|\gg q
. Their result holds in non-prime order fields. In prime order fields the condition was relaxed to
|A|\gg p^{5/8}
in
[
28
]
. We offer the following improvement.

Theorem 29
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
. There exists
a,b\in A
such that

|(A-a)(A-b)|\gg\min\left\{p,\frac{|A|^{5/2}}{p^{1/2}}\right\}

In particular, if
|A|\geq p^{3/5}
, then
\displaystyle|(A-A)(A-A)|\gg p
.

Moreover,
|(A-A)(A-A)|\gg\min\{p,|A|^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{90}-\varepsilon}\}
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

We follow closely an argument of Jones
[
20
]
, see also
[
31
]
. Combining (
21
) and Theorem
11
gives

\sum_{a,b\in A}\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A-a,A-b)=\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}+O(p^{1/2}|A|^{7/2}).

Therefore, by averaging, there exist
a,b\in A
such that
\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A-a,A-b)\leq\tfrac{|A|^{4}}{p}+O(p^{1/2}|A|^{3/2}).
The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality then gives

|(A-a)(A-b)|\geq\frac{|A|^{4}}{\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A-a,A-b)}\gg\min\{p,|A|^{5/2}p^{-1/2}\}.

For the last part we may assume that
|A|\leq p^{3/5}
. Next note that if
|A|\leq p^{9/16}
, then Theorem
27
with
\lambda=-1
gives
|(A-A)(A-A)|\gg|A|^{\frac{3}{2}+\frac{1}{90}-\varepsilon}
. When
p^{9/16}\leq|A|\leq p^{3/5}
we just proved

|(A-A)(A-A)|\gg\frac{|A|^{5/2}}{p^{1/2}}\geq|A|^{3/2+1/9},

which is asymptotically bigger.
∎

It is straightforward to modify this argument to obtain the same lower bound for
(A+A)(A+A)
. By considering the number collinear triples
T(A\cup-A)
, it follows that there exist
a,b\in A
such that
E^{\times}(A+a,A+b)\ll|A|^{4}/p+p^{1/2}|A|^{3/2}
, and the rest of the proof is almost identical.

4.3
Four-fold products of differences

Theorem
29
states that if
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
has cardinality
|A|\gg p^{3/5}
then
(A-A)(A-A)
contains a positive proportion of the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. A complementary question is to seek conditions on
|A|
that guarantee that
(A-A)(A-A)=\mathbb{F}_{p}
. The strategy followed in the proof of Theorem
29
cannot work in this context. Hart, Iosevich, and Solymosi used additive character sum estimates in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}
to show that if
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}
satisfies
|A|\gg q^{3/4}
, then
(A-A)(A-A)=\mathbb{F}_{q}

[
14
]
. Note here that their results hold in non-prime order finite fields too. They also obtained conditions for
d
-fold products of differences
\displaystyle\underbrace{(A-A)\dots(A-A)}_{d\textrm{ times}}
to equal the whole of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
d\geq 2
. Balog
[
2
]
improved their bounds for sufficiently large
d
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

We combine the collinear triples bound of Theorem
11
with standard multiplicative character arguments to improve the state of the art for 4-fold products of differences in prime order finite fields only.

Theorem 30
.

Let
p
be a prime and
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
. If
|A|\gg p^{3/5}
, then

(A-A)(A-A)(A-A)(A-A)=\mathbb{F}_{p}.

Proof.

Note that
0\in(A-A)(A-A)(A-A)(A-A)
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
and set
r(\lambda):=r_{(A-A)(A-A)(A-A)(A-A)}(\lambda)
be the number of ways
\lambda
can be expressed as a product
\lambda=(a_{1}-b_{1})(a_{2}-b_{2})(a_{3}-b_{3})(a_{4}-b_{4})
with all the
a_{i},b_{i}\in A
. The orthogonality of multiplicative characters, which we denote by
\chi
, implies (see
[
19
]
for details)

\displaystyle r(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{\chi}\sum_{a_{1},b_{2},\dots,a_{4},b_{4}\in A:a_{i}\neq b_{i}}\chi\left(\frac{(a_{1}-b_{1})(a_{2}-b_{2})(a_{3}-b_{3})(a_{4}-b_{4})}{\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{\chi}\chi(\lambda^{-1})\sum_{a_{1},b_{2},\dots,a_{4},b_{4}\in A:a_{i}\neq b_{i}}\chi((a_{1}-b_{1})(a_{2}-b_{2})(a_{3}-b_{3})(a_{4}-b_{4}))

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{\chi}\chi(\lambda^{-1})\left(\sum_{a_{1},b_{1}\in A:a_{1}\neq b_{1}}\chi(a_{1}-b_{1})\right)^{4}.

The contribution to the sum coming from the principal character
\chi_{0}
is
\displaystyle\frac{|A|^{4}(|A|-1)^{4}}{p-1}
. Using the fact that
|\chi(\lambda)|=1
for all
\chi
and applying the triangle inequality we get

\displaystyle\left|r(\lambda)-\frac{|A|^{4}(|A|-1)^{4}}{p-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\left|\sum_{a_{1},b_{1}\in A:a_{1}\neq b_{1}}\chi(a_{1}-b_{1})\right|^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p-1}\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\,\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{4},b_{1},\dots,b_{4}\in A}\chi\left(\frac{(a_{1}-b_{1})(a_{2}-b_{2})}{(a_{3}-b_{3})(a_{4}-b_{4})}\right).

The orthogonality of characters implies that the right side equals the number of non-zero solutions to
(a_{1}-b_{1})(a_{2}-b_{2})=(a_{3}-b_{3})(a_{4}-b_{4})\neq 0
with all the
a_{i},b_{i}\in A
minus the
\tfrac{|A|^{4}(|A|-1)^{4}}{p-1}
contribution that comes from
\chi_{0}
. A typical application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality (see the proof of Lemma 2.7 in
[
29
]
, for example) bounds the number of non-zero solutions by
|A|^{2}T(A)
.

Combining all the above with the first part of Theorem
11
yields

\left|r(\lambda)-\frac{|A|^{4}(|A|-1)^{4}}{p-1}\right|\leq|A|^{2}\left(\frac{|A|^{6}}{p}+O(p^{1/2}|A|^{7/2})\right)-\frac{|A|^{4}(|A|-1)^{4}}{p-1}

Note now that
\displaystyle\tfrac{|A|^{4}(|A|-1)^{4}}{p-1}=\displaystyle\tfrac{|A|^{8}}{p}+O(\tfrac{|A|^{7}}{p})
and so the above becomes

\left|r(\lambda)-\frac{|A|^{4}(|A|-1)^{4}}{p-1}\right|=O\left(p^{1/2}|A|^{11/2}+\frac{|A|^{7}}{p}\right)=O(|A|^{11/2}p^{1/2}).

One more application of
\displaystyle\tfrac{|A|^{4}(|A|-1)^{4}}{p-1}=\displaystyle\tfrac{|A|^{8}}{p}+O(\tfrac{|A|^{7}}{p})
gives

r(\lambda)=\frac{|A|^{8}}{p}+O\left(|A|^{11/2}p^{1/2}+\frac{|A|^{7}}{p}\right)=\frac{|A|^{8}}{p}+O(|A|^{11/2}p^{1/2}).

The first term dominates when
|A|\gg p^{3/5}
, in which range
r(\lambda)>0
and so
\lambda\in(A-A)(A-A)(A-A)(A-A)
.
∎

Once again, a small modification of this argument gives the same result for the set
(A+A)(A+A)(A+A)(A+A)
. The only change comes when we need an upper bound for the number of solutions to
(a_{1}+b_{1})(a_{2}+b_{2})=(a_{3}+b_{3})(a_{4}+b_{4})\neq 0
. The number of such solutions is at most
O(|A|^{2}T(A\cup-A))
.

Note here that using the well-known bound

\left|\sum_{a_{1},a_{2}\in A}\chi(a_{1}-a_{2})\right|\leq p^{1/2}|A|

for
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
one may extend the result for
d
-fold products of differences with
d\geq 4
. Modifying the proof of Theorem
30
in a straightforward manner gives

\underbrace{(A-A)\dots(A-A)}_{d\textrm{ times}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}

whenever
|A|\gg p^{\frac{d-1}{2d-3}}=p^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2(2d-3)}}.
This improves the bounds of Hart, Iosevich, and Solymosi
[
14
]
, but is worse than the bounds of Balog
[
2
]
for sufficiently large
d
.

4.4
Variant of Theorem
3
for ratios and products of shifts

In this subsection, we give another sum-product type estimate in which we present some quantitative progress beyond the
3/2
threshold, in the form of the following theorem.

Theorem 31
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
. Then if, respectively,
|A|\leq p^{13/24}
and
|A|\leq p^{23/42}
one has

\displaystyle|AA|^{13}\left|\frac{A-\alpha}{A-\alpha}\right|^{5}

\displaystyle\gtrsim|A|^{21},

(70)

\displaystyle|AA|^{23}|(A-\alpha)(A-\alpha)|^{9}

\displaystyle\gtrsim|A|^{37}

(71)

In particular, if
|AA|=M|A|
then
\left|\frac{A-\alpha}{A-\alpha}\right|\gtrsim M^{-13/5}|A|^{8/5}
and
|(A-\alpha)(A-\alpha)|\gtrsim M^{-23/9}|A|^{14/9}
.

The proof is similar to the proof of Theorem
3
. First we need an analogue of Lemma
23
— a bound for third energy which is near-optimal.

Lemma 32
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F}
and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
. Suppose
|AA|\leq p^{2/3}
. Then

\mathsf{E}_{3}^{\times}(A+\alpha)\ll\frac{|AA|^{5}\log|A|}{|A|^{2}}.

We have chosen use a slightly simpler argument, using the bound on the number of collinear quadruples in a Cartesian product point set. This accounts for the slightly worse exponents in Theorem
31
. However we get the same result in the most relevant case when
|AA|\ll|A|
.

Proof.

Let
P=(AA\cup-\alpha A)\times(AA\cup-\alpha A)
. The idea is to bound the number of collinear quadruples from above and below, much in the same way as Elekes and Ruzsa
[
9
]
did for collinear triples.

Let
d,d^{\prime}\in A
be arbitrary and suppose that
\frac{a+\alpha}{a^{\prime}+\alpha}=\frac{b+\alpha}{b^{\prime}+\alpha}=\frac{c+\alpha}{c^{\prime}+\alpha}
for some
a,\ldots,c^{\prime}\in A
. Then observe that the four points
(-\alpha d,-\alpha d^{\prime}),(da,d^{\prime}a^{\prime}),(db,d^{\prime}b^{\prime}),(dc,d^{\prime}c^{\prime})
are collinear. This implies that the number of collinear quadruples in
P
is at least

|A|^{2}\mathsf{E}_{3}^{\times}(A+\alpha).

Applying Theorem
11
and our assumption that
|AA|\leq p^{2/3}
, we have

|A|^{2}\mathsf{E}_{3}^{\times}(A+\alpha)\leq Q(AA\cup-\alpha A)\ll|AA|^{5}\log|A|.

A rearrangement of this inequality completes the proof.
∎

We will also use an analogue of Lemma
25
, implicit in
[
1
, proof of Proposition 2]
.

Lemma 33
.

Let
A,B\subset\mathbb{F}
such that
|A||AA||B|\leq p^{2}
and also
\max(|AA|,|B|)\leq(|A||AA||B|)^{1/2}.
Then for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
,

\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+\alpha,B)\ll\frac{|AA|^{3/2}|B|^{3/2}}{|A|^{1/2}}.

Proof of Theorem
31
.

The remainder of the proof of Theorem
31
is very similar to that of Theorem
3
, and so we present only the full proof of inequality (
70
).

Apply respectively (
67
) in the multiplicative setting, Lemma
32
and Lemma
33
as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}|A|^{8}&\leq\mathsf{E}_{3}^{\times}(A+\alpha)\left|\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right|\mathsf{E}^{\times}\left(A+\alpha,\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right)\\
&\ll\frac{|AA|^{5}\left|\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right|\mathsf{E}^{\times}\left(A+\alpha,\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right)}{|A|^{2}}\log|A|\\
&\ll\frac{|AA|^{13/2}\left|\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right|^{5/2}}{|A|^{5/2}}\log|A|.\end{split}

(72)

Rearranging this inequality gives the inequality (
70
). However, when applying Lemmata
32
and
33
in the finite characteristic setting, we must ensure that the relevant conditions are met.

We may assume that
|AA|^{13}\left|\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right|^{5}\leq|A|^{21}
, or else there is nothing to prove. Therefore, we may assume that
|AA|\leq|A|^{16/13}
and
\left|\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right|\leq|A|^{8/5}
. The conditions of Lemma
32
hold, since
|AA|\leq|A|^{16/13}\leq p^{2/3}
, where the last inequality uses the condition
|A|\leq p^{13/24}
.

The first condition of Lemma
33
holds, since

\displaystyle|A||AA|\left|\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right|

\displaystyle=|A|\left(|AA|^{13}\left|\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right|^{5}\right)^{1/13}\left|\frac{A+\alpha}{A+\alpha}\right|^{8/13}

\displaystyle\leq|A|^{1+21/13+64/65}

\displaystyle\leq p^{2},

where the last inequality uses the condition
|A|\leq p^{13/24}
. Finally, the second condition of Lemma
33
holds trivially in this application.
∎

4.5
A bound for multiplicative energy in terms of
|A+A|

To describe the last main result of this section we need some preparations.
The following theorem may be extracted from
[
42
]
.

Theorem 34
.

Let
A
be a finite subset of an abelian group
G
.
Suppose there are parameters
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
such that

\mathsf{E}_{3}(A)\leq D_{1}|A|^{3}

and for any finite set
B\subset G

\mathsf{E}(A,B)\leq D_{2}|A||B|^{3/2}.

Then

\mathsf{E}(A)\ll D_{1}^{6/13}D_{2}^{2/13}|A|^{32/13}\log^{12/13}|A|.

This implies the following bound for the multiplicative energy of a subset of
\mathbb{F}
with small sumset.
It is implicit in the proof of Theorem
34
that the bound for
\mathsf{E}(A,B)
only needs to hold for
|B|\leq 4|A|^{4}/\mathsf{E}(A)
.

Theorem 35
.

Suppose
A
is a subset of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that
|A+A|=M|A|
and
|A|\leq p^{13/23}M^{25/92}
.
Then

\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A)\lesssim M^{51/26}|A|^{32/13}.

Note that
32/13=5/2-1/26
, so for
M
sufficiently small, this improves the bound
\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A)\ll M^{3/2}|A|^{5/2}
provided by Lemma
25
.

Proof.

We will apply Theorem
34
where
G
is the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}^{*}
.
Thus we require bounds on the third multiplicative energy of
A
and the multiplicative energy of
A
with another set
B
.

By Lemma
25
we have

\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A,B)\ll\frac{|A+A|^{3/2}}{|A|^{1/2}}|B|^{3/2},

so we may take
D_{2}=M^{3/2}
whenever
M|A|^{2}|B|\leq p^{2}
.
Since we may assume that
|B|\leq 4|A|^{4}/\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A)
, and the theorem is trivial if
\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A)<4M^{51/26}|A|^{32/13}
, without loss of generality,

|B|\leq\frac{|A|^{20/13}}{M^{51/26}},

hence
M|A|^{2}|B|\leq p^{2}
whenever

\frac{|A|^{46/13}}{M^{25/26}}\leq p^{2},

which follows from the assumption
|A|\leq p^{13/23}M^{25/92}
.

By Lemma
23

\mathsf{E}^{\times}_{3}(A)\ll\frac{|A+A|^{15/4}}{|A|^{3/4}}\log|A|,

whenever
|A|<p^{2/3}/M^{1/12}
, so we may take
D_{1}=M^{15/4}\log|A|
in this range.

The desired bound follows from plugging
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
into Theorem
34
.

To check the constraint, first note that
M^{51/26}{|A|^{32/13}}>M^{3/2}|A|^{5/2}
unless
M<|A|^{1/12}
.
For
M<|A|^{1/12}
, we have

p^{13/23}M^{25/92}\leq\frac{p^{2/3}}{M^{1/12}}.

∎

Corollary 36
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{F},
such that
|A+A|=M|A|
and

|A|\leq p^{13/23}M^{25/92}
. Then

T(A)\lesssim M^{\frac{51}{26}}|A|^{\frac{9}{2}-\frac{1}{26}}.

Proof.

We use the expression

T(A)=\sum_{a,b\in A}\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+a,A+b).

By Cauchy-Schwarz,
\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+a,A+b)\leq\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+a)^{1/2}\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+b)^{1/2}
, so

T(A)\leq\left(\sum_{a\in A}\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+a)^{1/2}\right)^{2}.

By a second application of Cauchy-Schwarz, we have

T(A)\leq|A|\sum_{a\in A}\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+a).

Now, since
|A+A|=M|A|
, we have
|(A+a)+(A+a)|=M|A|
, so by Theorem
35

\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+a)\lesssim M^{51/26}|A|^{32/13}.

Thus

T(A)\lesssim|A|^{2}\cdot M^{51/26}|A|^{32/13},

which is the claimed bound.
∎

Let us obtain a “dual” version of the results above.

Theorem 37
.

Suppose
A\subset\mathbb{F}
such that
|AA|=M|A|
and
|A|\leq p^{13/23}M^{10/23}
.
Then for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}^{*}
one has

\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+\alpha)\lesssim M^{33/13}|A|^{32/13}.

Note that
32/13=5/2-1/26
, so for
M
sufficiently small, this improves the bound
\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A)\ll M^{3/2}|A|^{5/2}
provided by Lemma
33
.

Proof.

By Lemma
33
we have

\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+\alpha,B)\ll\frac{|AA|^{3/2}}{|A|^{1/2}}|B|^{3/2},

so we may take
D_{2}=M^{3/2}
whenever
M|A|^{2}|B|\leq p^{2}
.
Since we may assume that
|B|\leq 4|A|^{4}/\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+\alpha)
, and the theorem is trivial if
\mathsf{E}^{\times}(A+\alpha)<4M^{33/13}|A|^{32/13}
, without loss of generality,

|B|\leq\frac{|A|^{20/13}}{M^{33/13}},

hence
M|A|^{2}|B|\leq p^{2}
whenever

\frac{|A|^{46/13}}{M^{20/13}}\leq p^{2},

which reduces to the assumption
|A|\leq p^{13/23}M^{10/23}
.

By Lemma

\mathsf{E}^{\times}_{3}(A+\alpha)\ll\frac{|AA|^{5}}{|A|^{2}}\log|A|,

whenever
|A|<p^{2/3}/M

D_{1}=M^{5}\log|A|
in this range.

The desired bound follows from plugging
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
into Theorem
34
.

To check the constraint, first note that
M^{33/13}{|A|^{32/13}}>M^{3/2}|A|^{5/2}
unless
M<|A|^{1/27}
.
For
M<|A|^{1/27}
, we have

p^{13/23}M^{10/23}\leq\frac{p^{2/3}}{M}.

∎

Repeating the arguments of the proof of Corollary

, we obtain

Corollary 38
.

Let
A
be a subset of
\mathbb{F}
such that
|AA|=M|A|
,

|A|\leq p^{13/23}M^{10/23}
.
Then

T(A)\lesssim M^{\frac{33}{13}}|A|^{\frac{9}{2}-\frac{1}{26}}.

Appendix A
Discussion on collinear triples and quadruples

In this section we elaborate on the some of the remarks made in Section
2.2
.

First, we justify the formula (
16
) for
T(A)
and
Q(A)
in terms of the incidence function:

T(A)=\sum_{\ell:i(\ell)>1}i(\ell)^{3}+O(|A|^{4})\quad\text{ and }\quad Q(A)=\sum_{\ell:i(\ell)>1}i(\ell)^{4}+O(|A|^{4}).

Let us first justify the expression for
T(A)
. We may suppose that
|A|>1
and so
A\times A
is not a singleton because for singleton
A
the number of collinear triples is
O(|A|^{4})
. Any collinear triple with at least two distinct points from
A\times A
determines a unique line
\ell
with
i(\ell)>1
and so every such triple is counted exactly once in the third moment of
i(\ell)
. We are left with collinear triples of the form
(u,u,u)
. Any such triple may be counted in the third moment more than once. The total number of times it is counted equals the number of lines with
i(\ell)>1
that
(u,u,u)
is incident to. Since every line with
i(\ell)>1
determines at least one distinct point of
(A\times A)\setminus\{u\}
, we see that the contribution to the third moment coming from
(u,u,u)
is
O(|A|^{2})
. There are
|A|^{2}
collinear triples
(u,u,u)
so the difference between
T(A)
and the third moment is
O(|A|^{4})
. A similar argument works for
Q(A)
.

Next we justify the alternative expressions for
T(A)
and
Q(A)
in terms ratios of differences of elements of
A
.
Recall that we defined
t(x)
and
q(x,y)
by

t(x)=|\{(a,b,c)\in A\times A\times A\colon x=\tfrac{b-a}{c-a}\}|

and

q(x,y)=|\{(a,b,c,d)\in A\times A\times A\times A\colon\tfrac{b-a}{c-a}=x\,,\,\tfrac{d-a}{c-a}=y\}|.

We claim in (
19
) and (
20
) that

T(A)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}t(x)^{2}+|A|^{4}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad Q(A)=\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}q(x,y)^{2}+|A|^{5}.

We verify only the second identity as it is probably the most complicated of the two. The sum
\sum q(x,y)^{2}
is the number of solutions to

\frac{b-a}{c-a}=\frac{b^{\prime}-a^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}-a^{\prime}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\frac{d-a}{c-a}=\frac{d^{\prime}-a^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}-a^{\prime}}\;,\;a,b,\dots,d^{\prime}\in A

Fixing
(a,a^{\prime})

counts the number of collinear quadruples on non-vertical lines. There are
|A|
vertical lines, each incident to

points of

Next, we elaborate on what is known for collinear triples. The contribution to collinear triples coming from the

horizontal lines incident to

points in

is

. All other collinear triples can be counted by the number of solutions to

Another interesting example is that of sufficiently small arithmetic progressions. First note that in general
T(A)
equals

\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{x}f_{a,a^{\prime}}^{2}(x)+O(|A|^{4}),

where

is the number of ways one can express

as a product

with

. The support of

is the set
(A-a)(A-a^{\prime})\subset(A-A)(A-A)
.

Observe that for all

, one has
\displaystyle\sum_{x}f_{a,a^{\prime}}(x)=|A|^{2}
because each pair
(b,c^{\prime})\in A\times A
contributes 1 to the sum.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

T(A)=\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\sum_{x}f_{a,a^{\prime}}^{2}(x)\geq\sum_{a,a^{\prime}\in A}\frac{\left(\sum_{x}f_{a,a^{\prime}}(x)\right)^{2}}{|\mathrm{supp}(f_{a,a^{\prime}})|}\geq\frac{|A|^{6}}{|(A-A)(A-A)|}.

Now take

. For all

the product
(b-a)(c^{\prime}-a^{\prime})
belongs to
\{-p/2,\dots,p/2\}
. This means that
|(A-A)(A-A)|
is the same whether

is taken to be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}
or of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Ford has shown in
[

]
that

(in
\mathbb{Z}
and hence) in

is

for some absolute constant
\gamma<1
. Substituting in the lower bound

T(A)\geq|A|^{6}/|(A-A)(A-A)|

implies that for
A=\{1,\dots,\sqrt{p}/3\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we have
T(A)\gg|A|^{4}\log^{\gamma}|A|
.

Over the reals, Elekes and Ruzsa observed in
[
9
]
that the Szemerédi-Trotter point-line incidence theorem
[

]
implies

Because of this and the two examples discussed above, it is possible that the inequality

is correct up to logarithmic factors in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
.

Infrared finite scattering theory: Amplitudes and soft theorems
Kartik Prabhu, Gautam Satishchandran

Abstract
Any non-trivial scattering with massless fields in four spacetime dimensions will generically produce an out-state with memory. Scattering with any massless fields violates the standard assumption of asymptotic completeness — that all “in” and “out” states lie in the standard (zero memory) Fock space — and therefore leads to infrared (IR) divergences in the standard S S -matrix amplitudes. In this paper we define an infrared finite scattering theory which assumes only (1) the existence of in/out algebras and (2) that Heisenberg evolution is an automorphism of these algebras. The resulting “superscattering” map $ \$ allows for transitions between different in/out memory states and agrees with the standard S S -matrix when it is defined. We construct $ \$ -amplitudes by defining (3) a “generalized asymptotic completeness” which accommodates states with memory in the space of asymptotic states and (4) a complete basis of improper states which generalize the usual n n -particle momentum basis to account for states with memory. Using only general properties of $ \$ , we prove an analog of the Weinberg soft theorems in quantum gravity and QED which imply that all $ \$ -amplitudes are well-defined in the infrared. We comment on how one must generalize this framework to consider $ \$ -amplitudes for theories with collinear divergences (e.g., massless QED and Yang-Mills theories).

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\limits_{\mathscr{I}}d^{3}x~\left[N_{1}^{AB}\sigma_{2AB}-N_{2}^{AB}\sigma_{1AB}\right]

In the following, it will be convenient to instead use the Fourier space representation. For a test tensor, we define the Fourier transform by

\displaystyle s^{AB}(\omega,\hat{p})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int\limits_{\mathscr{I}}d^{3}x~e^{i\omega u}\delta_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(\hat{x},\hat{p})s^{AB}(u,\hat{x})

2
Memory Fock space representations

We now recall the construction of the memory Fock spaces which are representations of the asymptotic algebra
\mathscr{A}
, including the News, the memory and the supermomentum charge. The Lorentz charges cannot be defined as operators on these memory Fock spaces, except when the memory vanishes, but as shown in
memory-orbits
one can construct Hilbert spaces on which the Lorentz charges are represented as operators by taking direct integrals over the memory Fock spaces. This construction is summarized at the end of this section.

We start with the BMS invariant vacuum state
\omega_{0}
, which is a Gaussian algebraic state with vanishing
1
-point function of the News and
2
-point function given by
eq.

2.10
with
S_{ABCD}=0
and the normalization
\omega_{0}(\boldsymbol{1})=1
. Further
\omega_{0}
is also an eigenstate with vanishing eigenvalue of the memory and all the BMS charges. The corresponding Hilbert space, constructed by applying the creation operators
\boldsymbol{a}(s)^{*}
to the vacuum
\ket{\omega_{0}}
, is the Fock space
\mathscr{F}_{0}
:

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{0}=\mathbb{C}\oplus\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\underbrace{\big(\mathscr{H}_{0}\otimes_{S}\dots\otimes_{S}\mathscr{H}_{0}\big)}_{n\text{ times}}.

(2.23)

Here,
\otimes_{S}
denotes the symmetrized tensor product, and the one-particle Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{0}
is the completion of the space of positive frequency test functions
s^{AB}
with respect to the inner product:

\displaystyle\braket{s_{1}|s_{2}}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\omega_{0}(\boldsymbol{a}(s_{1})\boldsymbol{a}(s_{2})^{*})=16\pi\int\limits_{C_{+}}d^{3}p~\omega\,\overline{s}_{1}^{AB}(p)s_{2,AB}(p)\,.

(2.24)

It is well-known that the standard Fock space of gravitons provides a dense set of Hadamard states with finite energy, (super)momentum, and angular momentum. The action of the BMS charges on states in
\mathscr{F}_{0}
can be found by using their commutation relations (
eq.

2.18
) and noting that they annihilate the vacuum.

14

14

The Lorentz charge operators are well-defined on
\mathscr{F}_{0}
and can be used to define the helicity of the graviton states. The helicity of a
1

\mathscr{F}_{0}
can be computed to be
\pm 2
as expected; see
asymp-quant
for details.
However, the standard Fock space does not encompass all states relevant for scattering theory. Specifically, since the memory operator
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
annihilates
\ket{\omega_{0}}
, the commutation of
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
with
\boldsymbol{N}(s)
implies that
all
states in
\mathscr{F}_{0}
have zero memory.

As discussed in the introduction, the memory is generically not conserved in scattering and thus states in the standard zero-memory Fock space are not sufficient to formulate an infrared finite scattering theory. A large supply of states with non-zero memory can be constructed following the procedure in
asymp-quant
. Choose a classical News tensor
\nu_{AB}(p)
with memory
\Delta_{AB}(\hat{p})
and consider the map

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\nu}[\boldsymbol{a}(s)]=\boldsymbol{a}(s)+\nu(\overline{s})\boldsymbol{1}\,,\quad\mathfrak{a}_{\nu}[\boldsymbol{\Delta}(f)]=\boldsymbol{\Delta}(f)+\Delta(\nu,f)\boldsymbol{1}

(2.25)

where

\displaystyle\nu(\overline{s})=\int\limits_{C_{+}}d^{3}p~\nu_{AB}(p)\overline{s}^{AB}(p)

(2.26)

and
\Delta(\nu,f)
denotes the chosen classical memory smeared with the function
f
. This map can be extended to an automorphism
\mathfrak{a}_{\nu}:\mathscr{A}\to\mathscr{A}
of the algebra containing the News and memory by demanding that it preserves the identity
\boldsymbol{1}
, the product and the
*
-relation in the algebra. Then, define a new algebraic state
\omega_{\nu}
by

\displaystyle\omega_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{O})=\omega_{0}(\mathfrak{a}_{\nu}[\boldsymbol{O}])\,,\quad\text{for all }\boldsymbol{O}\in\mathscr{A}

(2.27)

From
eqs.

2.25
and
2.27

\omega_{\nu}
is an eigenstate of the memory operator with eigenvalue
\Delta(\nu;f)
. If the News tensor is chosen so that
\Delta_{AB}=0
, this automorphism can be represented by a unitary operator on
\mathscr{F}_{0}

\omega_{\nu}
is a coherent state in
\mathscr{F}_{0}
(see

\Delta_{AB}
is non-vanishing, this defines a new Gaussian state which does not lie in
\mathscr{F}_{0}
and is an eigenstate of the memory operator with eigenvalue equal to the chosen memory
\Delta_{AB}

Next we explain our regularity conditions on the News tensor
\nu_{AB}

\displaystyle\omega_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{\mathcal{J}}(f))=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int\limits_{C_{+}}d^{3}p~f(\hat{p})\overline{\nu}^{AB}(p)\nu_{AB}(p)=\frac{1}{32\pi}\int\limits_{\mathscr{I}}d^{3}x~f(\hat{x})\nu^{AB}(x)\nu_{AB}(x)

(2.28)

. Note that for

Acting on the state
\omega_{\nu}
with the smeared creation operators generates an entire new Fock space
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
which is
unitarily inequivalent
to
\mathscr{F}_{0}
. The choice of the “vacuum” state
\omega_{\nu}
in this construction is not unique — if a different News tensor
\nu^{\prime}_{AB}
is picked with identical memory, i.e.,
\Delta(\nu^{\prime},f)=\Delta(\nu,f)
for all
f
, then our regularity conditions above ensure that the two representations will be unitarily equivalent. For this reason, the memory Fock spaces
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
are labeled by the memory instead of the chosen News tensor.

The memory of the radiative field is invariant under BMS supertranslations, i.e., for any chosen classical News
\nu_{AB}
the supertranslated News tensor
\nu^{\prime}_{AB}
has the same memory. Thus, any BMS supertranslation maps a choice of vacuum
\omega_{\nu}
to another equivalent one
\omega_{\nu^{\prime}}
within the same memory Fock space. Using the commutation relations with the creation operators, this action can be extended to the entire memory Fock space. Consequently, the BMS supermomentum is well-defined on any memory Fock space associated with a square-integrable memory tensor.

On the other hand, the Lorentz charges, in general, cannot be defined on a memory Fock space. Recall that the memory tensor is not Lorentz-invariant unless it vanishes, and the Lorentz charges do not commute with the memory operator. Since all the states in the memory Fock spaces are eigenstates of the memory operator, it follows that the Lorentz charges cannot be densely-defined operators on the Fock spaces of definite memory. Only a subgroup of the Lorentz transformations which preserve the memory tensor — the ‘little group” of the memory — have well-defined charges on the given Fock space
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
. However, since the memory is not preserved under scattering, the little groups of the in and out memory will not coincide in general.

It was recently shown in
memory-orbits
that plenty of states with finite Lorentz charges can be constructed by taking direct integrals over the memory Fock spaces. One could consider taking the direct integral over the infinite-dimensional space of all allowed memories. This scenario was studied in
Herdegen_1997
and § 7 of
PSW-IR
and requires a choice of an infinite-dimensional measure. Though this choice isn’t unique,
PSW-IR
argued that there is no Gaussian measure over the memory space yielding a representation with well-defined energy and angular momentum. The alternative construction in
memory-orbits
takes a direct integral over a finite-dimensional subspace of memories. This construction can summarized as follows. The space of memories obtained by applying the complete Lorentz group to a given memory
\Delta_{AB}
forms a finite-dimensional manifold — the
orbit space

\mathscr{O}
associated with the given memory
\Delta_{AB}
. If
y\in\mathscr{O}
represents a point in the orbit space, the Lorentz transformation gives a set of Fock spaces
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta(y)}
corresponding to each point in
\mathscr{O}
. Each orbit space has a unique, Lorentz invariant measure
\mu(y)
. Consequently, for each memory
\Delta_{AB}
, we obtain a
direct integral
Hilbert space

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\textrm{DI},\Delta}=\int\limits_{\mathscr{O}}^{\oplus}d\mu(y)\mathscr{F}_{\Delta(y)}

(2.29)

where the subscript “
\textrm{DI},\Delta
” indicates a direct integral. States in this direct integral Hilbert space have continuous memory distributions across the orbit space
\mathscr{O}
. These Hilbert spaces admit a unitary representation of the complete asymptotic BMS symmetry group, and contain a dense set of states with non-zero memory, along with well-defined energy and angular momentum.

Thus, while there is no single, preferred Hilbert space which includes all the states that can arise in scattering, the memory Fock spaces and direct integrals obtained from them, provide plenty of physical states to use in an infrared finite scattering theory.

3
Improper eigenstates of supermomentum

In this section, we construct the eigenstates of the supermomenta in every memory Fock space
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
. The operator
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}_{i^{0}}(f)
is a self-adjoint operator on
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
and so the existence of such a complete set of eigenstates is guaranteed by the spectral theorem which yields a decomposition of all states in terms of projection-valued measures

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}_{i^{0}}(f)
for all supertranslation functions
f
. In terms of using the spectral theorem, this would require performing the daunting task of finding projection-valued measures on each
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
for all functions
f
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
. Thus purely for practical computational convenience, we will instead use the rigorous rigged Hilbert space formalism

.

As we will see, the construction of eigenstates of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}_{i^{0}}(f)
on
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
for
\Delta_{AB}\neq 0
, is far more non-trivial than the construction of eigenstates on
\mathscr{F}_{0}
. Indeed, in the standard Fock space
\mathscr{F}_{0}
, the supermomentum eigenstates can be straightforwardly constructed by noting that the vacuum
\ket{\omega_{0}}
is a proper state annihilated by
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}_{i^{0}}(f)
for all
f
(see remark
3.2
). Using the fact that
\ket{\omega_{0}}
has vanishing supermomentum and vanishing memory,
eq.

2.20
together with
eq.

2.22
imply that, formally, the
n
-particle momentum states

\ket{p_{1}\dots p_{n}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!}}\boldsymbol{a}(p_{1})^{*}\cdots\boldsymbol{a}(p_{n})^{*}\ket{\omega_{0}}

(3.1)

are (improper) eigenstates of the supermomentum in
\mathscr{F}_{0}
with eigenvalue

\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}_{i^{0}}(f)\ket{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}=\bigg(\sum_{i=0}^{n}\omega_{i}f(\hat{p}_{i})\bigg)\ket{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}\quad\quad\textrm{ (in $\mathscr{F}_{0}$)}

(3.2)

However, this procedure cannot be directly generalized to construct eigenstates of
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}_{i^{0}}(f)
when the memory is non-zero. Indeed, as we will explicitly see, there is
no

\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
that is annihilated by
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{Q}}_{i^{0}}(f)
and, furthermore,

In
section

3.1

1
Rigged Hilbert spaces

As we have already stated, the eigenstates of the supermomentum are generically not proper states but will have a well-defined “inner product” with a dense set of states in the Hilbert space. Thus the eigenstates lie in the dual of this dense space. In this subsection, we will introduce the rigged Hilbert space, following
Gelfand4
, which allows one to generally define these improper states as well as the notion of improper eigenstates.

Consider a separable Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
and let
\mathscr{D}
be a dense vector subspace of
\mathscr{H}
. Furthermore, let
\mathscr{D}
also have the structure of a nuclear space (see
Gelfand4
;

be the dual of
\mathscr{D}
in the nuclear topology, i.e., the space of all continuous linear maps from
\mathscr{D}
to
\mathbb{C}
. The dual of
\mathscr{H}
in the Hilbert topology is isomorphic to

by the

\displaystyle\mathscr{D}\subset\mathscr{H}\subset\mathscr{D}^{\prime}

(3.3)

The triple of spaces

is known as a

or a
Gelfand triple
.

An

is then defined as an element of

\displaystyle F(\ket{\psi})\in\mathbb{C}\text{ for all }\ket{\psi}\in\mathscr{D}

(3.4)

In the oft-used abuse of Dirac notation we will denote improper states also as
\ket{F}
, and make the identification
16
16

16

The identification

\alpha
.

\displaystyle F(\ket{\psi})=\braket{F|\psi}\in\mathbb{C}\text{ for all }\ket{\psi}\in\mathscr{D}

(3.5)

is viewed as the “overlap” of the improper state

with the state
\ket{\psi}
in the dense subspace
\mathscr{D}
. Note that in general

\ket{\psi}
is a state in
\mathscr{H}
but not in
\mathscr{D}
. Similarly any expression of the form

Now, let

An example of the rigged Hilbert space construction, with relevance to quantum mechanics, is where

Infrared finite scattering theory: Amplitudes and soft theorems

To this end, we must generalize the notion of asymptotic completeness used to define traditional

1

In the usual formulation of scattering theory, one assumes that all in/out-states lie in the standard Fock space

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{\nu}}{e^{-\tfrac{1}{128\pi^{2}}\left\|\,\sigma(\epsilon)\,\right\|^{2}_{0}}}\braket{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n};0|\psi(\epsilon)}

holds for all
\ket{\psi}\in\mathscr{D}_{\Delta}
, where
\sigma_{AB}(p;\epsilon)
is the trucation of the shear. In the last line we have used
A_{0}=1
for the invariant vacuum
\ket{\omega_{0}}
(
remark

3.2
), we get
A_{\nu(\epsilon)}=e^{-\tfrac{1}{128\pi^{2}}\left\|\,\sigma(\epsilon)\,\right\|^{2}_{0}}\neq 0
for
\epsilon>0
(from
eq.

3.20
). Now we obtain the sought for formula for the inner product on
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
.

Theorem 4

(Inner product on
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
in terms of BMS particles)
.

The inner product on the memory Fock spaces
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
, restricted to the dense subspace
\mathscr{D}_{\Delta}
is given by

\displaystyle\braket{\psi^{\prime}|\psi}_{\Delta}=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{e^{-\tfrac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left\|\,\sigma(\epsilon)\,\right\|_{0}^{2}}}{\left|\,A_{\nu}\,\right|^{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{1}{(16\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{C_{+}^{n}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}d^{3}p_{i}\omega_{i}^{-1}\theta_{\epsilon}(\omega_{i})\right)\braket{\psi^{\prime}|p_{1},\ldots,p_{n};\Delta}\braket{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n};\Delta|\psi}

(B.5)

for all
\ket{\psi},\ket{\psi^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{D}_{\Delta}
. In the above formula, the shear
\sigma_{AB}
and the corresponding News
\nu_{AB}
have memory
\Delta_{AB}
,
A_{\nu}=\braket{\varnothing;\Delta|\omega_{\nu}}\neq 0
(as in
eq.

3.12
), and
\sigma_{AB}(\epsilon)
is the truncation of the shear as explained above. Further, the formula
eq.

B.5
is independent of the chosen vacuum
\ket{\omega_{\nu}}\in\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
.

Proof.

First we prove the final claim that the formula
eq.

B.5
is independent of the choice of vacuum
\ket{\omega_{\nu}}
. Let
\ket{\omega_{\nu^{\prime}}}
be another choice of vacuum with News tensor
\nu^{\prime}_{AB}
which has the same memory
\Delta_{AB}
. The the corresponding shears are related by
\sigma^{\prime}_{AB}=\sigma_{AB}+s_{AB}
where
s_{AB}
is a test tensor with zero memory. By the arguments already given in remark
3.1
the overlap of the BMS particle states is invariant under the choice of vacuum state in
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
; thus we only need to consider the prefactor in
eq.

B.5
. We compute the ratio of the prefactors with different choice of vacua using
eq.

3.20

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\left|\,\tfrac{A_{\nu^{\prime}}}{A_{\nu}}\,\right|^{2}e^{\tfrac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left(\left\|\,\sigma^{\prime}(\epsilon)\,\right\|_{0}^{2}-\left\|\,\sigma(\epsilon)\,\right\|_{0}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}e^{\left(\tfrac{1}{4\pi}{\rm Im\,}\nu(\overline{s})-\tfrac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left\|\,s\,\right\|_{0}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left\|\,\sigma(\epsilon)\,\right\|_{0}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left\|\,\sigma^{\prime}(\epsilon)\,\right\|_{0}^{2}\right)}

(B.6)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}e^{\left(\tfrac{1}{4\pi}{\rm Im\,}\nu(\overline{s}(1-\theta_{\epsilon}))-\tfrac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left\|\,s(1-\theta_{\epsilon})\,\right\|_{0}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=1

where the second line uses the identity

\displaystyle-\left\|\,\sigma(\epsilon)\,\right\|_{0}^{2}+\left\|\,(\sigma+s)\theta_{\epsilon}\,\right\|_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\,s\theta_{\epsilon}\,\right\|_{0}^{2}+2{\rm Re\,}\braket{s|\sigma(\epsilon)}_{0}=\left\|\,s\theta_{\epsilon}\,\right\|_{0}^{2}-16\pi{\rm Im\,}\nu(\overline{s}\theta_{\epsilon})

(B.7)

Thus, the inner product formula is independent of the choice of vacuum state.

Now, we prove that this formula is the inner product on
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
. Let
\boldsymbol{O}(\psi)
be the smeared sum over product of creation operators that maps the chosen vacuum
\ket{\omega_{\nu}}
to the state
\ket{\psi}=\boldsymbol{O}(\psi)\ket{\omega_{\nu}}\in\mathscr{D}_{\Delta}
according to
eq.

3.10
, and similarly,
\ket{\psi(\epsilon)}=\boldsymbol{O}(\psi)\ket{\omega_{\nu(\epsilon)}}\in\mathscr{F}_{0}
be the corresponding truncated state in
\mathscr{F}_{0}
as explained above. Then from
eq.

B.3
we have

\displaystyle\braket{\omega_{\nu}|\boldsymbol{O}(\psi^{\prime})^{*}\boldsymbol{O}(\psi)|\omega_{\nu}}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\braket{\omega_{\nu(\epsilon)}|\boldsymbol{O}(\psi^{\prime})^{*}\boldsymbol{O}(\psi)|\omega_{\nu(\epsilon)}}_{0}

(B.8)

\displaystyle\braket{\psi^{\prime}|\psi}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\braket{\psi^{\prime}(\epsilon)|\psi(\epsilon)}_{0}

Now we write the inner product on the right-hand side in terms of the zero memory
n
-particle states, as in
eq.

B.1
, to get

\displaystyle\braket{\psi^{\prime}|\psi}_{\Delta}=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(16\pi)^{-n}\int\limits_{C_{+}^{n}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}d^{3}p_{i}\omega_{i}^{-1}\right)\braket{\psi^{\prime}(\epsilon)|p_{1},\ldots,p_{n};0}\braket{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n};0|\psi(\epsilon)}

(B.9)

Finally, we use
eq.

B.4
to replace the zero memory
n
-particle states with the BMS
n
-particle states with memory which proves
eq.

B.5
.
∎

When the memory vanishes we can take the limit inside the sum in
eq.

B.5
since each term has a finite limit, to get the zero-memory inner product
eq.

B.1
. But for non-zero memory,
\Delta_{AB}\neq 0
, we must take the limit last since the individual integrals in the formula will not converge in the limit. In fact, the integrals along with the infinite sum together give an overall factor which precisely cancels the exponential factor in
eq.

B.5
, so that the final expression converges as
\epsilon\to 0
.

Next, we give a plausible interpretation of
eq.

B.5
in terms of a direct integral. Define
C_{+}^{0}=\set{0}
as the one element set and take the set
\mathfrak{C}_{+}
defined as the disjoint union

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{+}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\bigsqcup_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{+}^{n}=\set{(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}):n\geq 0\text{ and }(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\in C_{+}^{n}}

(B.10)

At each point
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
of
\mathfrak{C}_{+}
, let
\mathscr{H}_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}
be the Hilbert space of complex-valued test tensors
s_{A_{1}B_{1}\ldots A_{n}B_{n}}
which are symmetric and traceless in each pair of indices
A_{i}B_{i}
and totally symmetric with respect to interchange of any such pair with another. The inner product on
\mathscr{H}_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}
is simply the contraction of their indices

\displaystyle{s^{\prime}}^{A_{1}B_{1}\ldots A_{n}B_{n}}s_{A_{1}B_{1}\ldots A_{n}B_{n}}

(B.11)

Now, for
\epsilon>0
, consider the family of measures
\mu_{\Delta,\epsilon}((p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))
on
\mathfrak{C}_{+}
defined by

\displaystyle d\mu_{\Delta,\epsilon}((p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\frac{e^{-\tfrac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left\|\,\sigma(\epsilon)\,\right\|^{2}_{0}}}{\left|\,A_{\nu}\,\right|^{2}}(16\pi)^{-n}~\delta_{n}~\prod_{i=1}^{n}d^{3}p_{i}\omega_{i}^{-1}\theta_{\epsilon}(\omega_{i})

(B.12)

where
\nu_{AB}(p)
is a News tensor with memory
\Delta_{AB}
,
\sigma_{AB}
is the corresponding shear, and
\sigma_{AB}(\epsilon)
is the truncated shear are explained above. The
\delta_{n}
is the discrete Dirac measure supported at
n
. The same computation as in
eq.

B.6
above, without taking the final limit
\epsilon\to 0
, shows that the measures defined with different News tensors with the same memory are equivalent; hence we have labeled the measure with the memory
\Delta
instead of the News tensor. Now we construct a direct integral Hilbert space over
\mathfrak{C}_{+}
by integrating the Hilbert spaces
\mathscr{H}_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}
with the above family of measures,

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\Delta,\epsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int\limits_{\mathfrak{C}_{+}}^{\oplus}d\mu_{\Delta,\epsilon}\mathscr{H}_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}

(B.13)

A state in
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta,\epsilon}
is then represented as a function
\psi
on
\mathfrak{C}_{+}
such that at
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
we have

\displaystyle\psi((p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\braket{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n};\Delta|\psi}\in\mathscr{H}_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}

(B.14)

where
\ket{\psi}\in\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
and two functions are equivalent if they differ only on a set of measure zero with respect to
\mu_{\Delta,\epsilon}
.

Now, when
\Delta=0
, the limit of the measures as
\epsilon\to 0
can be taken and the limiting measure is

\displaystyle d\mu_{0}((p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0}d\mu_{0,\epsilon}((p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))=(16\pi)^{-n}~\delta_{n}~\prod_{i=1}^{n}d^{3}p_{i}\omega_{i}^{-1}

(B.15)

Then, the direct integral above gives the usual direct sum structure of the zero memory Fock space in terms of
n
-particle states (
eq.

2.23
)

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{0}\,\cong\,\int\limits_{\mathfrak{C}_{+}}^{\oplus}d\mu_{0}((p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))\mathscr{H}_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}\,\cong\,\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\left[(16\pi)^{-n}\int\limits_{C_{+}^{n}}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}d^{3}p_{i}\omega_{i}^{-1}\right)~\mathscr{H}_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}\right]

(B.16)

In fact, for any memory the direct integral
eq.

B.13
is unitarily equivalent to the zero memory Fock space when
\epsilon>0
; this is just a restatement of our truncation procedure described above. However for any non-zero memory, corollary
3.1
implies that any subset of
\mathfrak{C}_{+}
containing only finitely-many BMS particle numbers must have measure zero, and thus the direct sum structure of
\mathscr{F}_{0}
cannot be generalized to
\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}
in terms of the BMS particles. We conjecture that for
\Delta\neq 0
the limit of the measures
\mu_{\Delta,\epsilon}
as
\epsilon\to 0
exists as a measure
\mu_{\Delta}
on
\mathfrak{C}_{+}
. Since, the sets of measure zero for
\mu_{\Delta}
and
\mu_{0}
do not agree, the limiting measure
\mu_{\Delta}
must be inequivalent to
\mu_{0}
. If this conjectured limit measure exists, the inner product formula
eq.

B.5
can be given a precise measure theoretic meaning and the memory Fock spaces can be given the structure of a direct integral

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}_{\Delta}\,\cong\,\int\limits_{\mathfrak{C}_{+}}^{\oplus}d\mu_{\Delta}((p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}))\mathscr{H}_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}}

(B.17)

However, proving the existence of such a limit of measures would require considerable mathematical machinery, so we leave the existence of
\mu_{\Delta}
as purely conjecture.

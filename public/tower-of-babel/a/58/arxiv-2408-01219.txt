Anti-cyclotomic Euler system of diagonal cycles
Shilin Lai, Christopher Skinner

Abstract
. We construct split anti-cyclotomic Euler systems for Galois representations attached to certain RACSDC automorphic representations on the group GL n × GL n + 1 \mathrm{GL}_{n}\times\mathrm{GL}_{n+1} . As a result, we make progress towards certain rank 1 cases of the Beilinson–Bloch–Kato conjecture for those representations.

1.2.
Idea of proof

Our construction is an integral version of the zeta integral strategy first developed in the papers
[
LSZ22b
,
LSZ22a
]
. We outline the strategy in a loose way. One first constructs a map of the form

C_{c}^{\infty}(G,\mathbb{Q}_{p})\text{ ``$\to$'' }\mathrm{H}^{1}(E,V_{\Pi})

by taking Hecke translates of the diagonal cycle class. By construction, it is Hecke equivariant and invariant under a subgroup
H(\mathbb{A})
. Now write down some well-chosen test functions on the source. General Hecke-cyclicity results (Proposition
4.2
) imply the norm relation holds for some Hecke operator, and a zeta integral calculation (Proposition
4.4
) identifies the Hecke operator as the one required for an Euler system.

One subtle issue is that in most settings, the map does not preserve the natural integral structures, and one needs to formulate the correct integral structure on the source, cf.
[
LSZ22a
, Definition 3.2.1]
. In this paper, we take
H
-coinvariants immediately and constructs a map

C_{c}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\text{ ``$\to$'' }\mathrm{H}^{1}(E,T_{\Pi})

where
X=H\backslash G
is the spherical variety underlying the setting, and
T_{\Pi}
is a fixed lattice in
V_{\Pi}
(cf. Proposition
2.7
). This provides a natural explanation of the integrality requirements appearing in previous works. Moreover, all of the norm relations are proven directly in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(E,T_{\Pi})
, so we bypass some technical Iwasawa-theoretic arguments used in previous works, for example in the proof of
[
LSZ22b
, Theorem 10.5.4(b)]
. Thus, we can completely remove all conditions on
p
when dealing with tame norm relations.

We also systematically use the augmented group
{\widetilde{G}}=G\times\mathrm{U}(1)
, which was used in the work of Graham–Shah on Euler systems in the Friedberg–Jacquet setting
[
GS23
]
. Our use of the augmentation is generally cosmetic, but it allows us to fix the level of the Shimura variety for
G
in the steps where we construct Galois cohomology classes from cycles. This somewhat simplifies the exposition.

1.3.
Future work

To relate the Euler system to classical
L
-values (instead of derivatives of
p
-adic
L
-functions), one would hope for an explicit reciprocity law relating the diagonal cycle class
z_{\mathrm{GGP}}
to a certain
p
-adic
L
-function outside of the range of interpolation, as in the classical work
[
BDP13
]
. In our case, it is likely that one has to consider variations of
\Pi
in a Hida family, instead of just character twists.

In a different direction, we have reduced the Euler system norm relation to certain relations in the function space
C_{c}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{C})
. This becomes a question of unramified harmonic analysis on spherical varieties, and it is possible to avoid much of the ad hoc calculations of Section
4
using this point of view. In a future joint work of the first named author with Li Cai and Yangyu Fan, we will explain this approach in the twisted Friedberg–Jacquet setting.

\mathcal{O}
be its ring of integers, and let
t
be a uniformizer. Finally, let
\nu_{\Phi}
denote the additive valuation on
\Phi
normalized so that
\nu_{\Phi}(t)=1
. Fix a positive Weyl chamber of
\mathbf{H}\times_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}\Phi
containing
\Delta^{+}
. Given a dominant character
\mu
of
\mathbf{H}
, write its associated highest weight representation as

\xi_{\mu}:\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mu}).

Further fix an
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
\Lambda_{\mu}\subseteq V_{\mu}
which is invariant under
\xi_{\mu}(K_{H})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
a\in A^{-}
, then
t^{-\nu_{\Phi}(\mu(a))}\xi_{\mu}(a)\in\End_{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{\mu})
. Moreover, this extends to a monoid homomorphism

(4)

\xi_{\mu}^{\circ}:H_{p}^{-}\to\End_{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda_{\mu}).

Proof.

The extension part is clear in view of equation (
2
) and the comment following it. For integrality, note that for a vector
v
of weight
\mu^{\prime}
, we have

t^{-\nu_{\Phi}(\mu(a))}\xi_{\mu}(a)v=t^{-\nu_{\Phi}(\mu(a))}\mu^{\prime}(a)v=t^{\nu_{\Phi}(\mu^{\prime}\mu^{-1}(a))}\cdot(\text{unit})\cdot v.

Since
a\in A^{-}
and
\mu
is the highest weight, the exponent of
t
is non-negative, as required.
∎

2.3.4.
Branching law

In the standard pinning for
\mathrm{GL}_{n}
, the dominant characters can be labelled by non-increasing tuples
\underline{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by the formula

\mu_{\underline{a}}:\diag(t_{1},\cdots,t_{n})\mapsto\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{i}}.

Denote the corresponding highest weight representation by
(\xi_{\underline{a}},V_{\underline{a}})
. Define
-\underline{a}=(-a_{n},-a_{n-1},\cdots,-a_{1})
, so
V_{-\underline{a}}=V_{\underline{a}}^{\vee}
.

Using the basis
(e_{1},\cdots,e_{n})
for the Hermitian space, we have an identification

\mathbf{H}\times_{\mathbb{Q}}\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\simeq\prod_{\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}\ni\tau:E\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}\mathrm{GL}_{n},

so algebraic representations of
\mathbf{H}
are external tensor products of
V_{\underline{a}}
for various choices of
\underline{a}
. We write the dominant characters for
\mathbf{H}
as a
g
-tuple
\underline{\boldsymbol{a}}=(\underline{a}^{\tau})
, where
\tau
ranges over all embeddings
E\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
in the fixed CM type
\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}
. The same discussion applies to the group
\mathbf{G}=\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}G
, so its dominant characters is written as a pair

(\underline{\boldsymbol{a}},\underline{\boldsymbol{b}})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times g}\times\mathbb{Z}^{(n+1)\times g}.

Applying the classical branching law one embedding at a time gives the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
\underline{\boldsymbol{a}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n\times g}
and
\underline{\boldsymbol{b}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{(n+1)\times g}
satisfy the inequalities

b_{1}^{\tau}\geq a_{1}^{\tau}\geq b_{2}^{\tau}\geq\cdots\geq a_{n}^{\tau}\geq b_{n+1}^{\tau}

for all
\tau:E\hookrightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
in
\Sigma_{\infty}^{+}
,
then the space

\Hom_{\mathbf{H}}(1,V_{-\underline{\boldsymbol{a}}}\boxtimes V_{\underline{\boldsymbol{b}}})

is one-dimensional.

Definition 2.4
.

An algebraic representation
\xi_{\mu}
with highest weight
\mu=(\underline{\boldsymbol{a}},\underline{\boldsymbol{b}})
is
perfectly interlacing
if the condition of the above proposition holds.

In this case, the lattice
\Lambda_{\mu}
fixed in the previous subsection contains an
\mathbf{H}
-fixed vector
v_{*}
that is non-vanishing in
\Lambda_{\mu}/t\Lambda_{\mu}
. Such a
v_{*}
is unique up to
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
-multiple. Define the branching map by

\mathtt{br}:\mathcal{O}\to\Lambda_{\mu},\quad 1\mapsto v_{*}.

Choose the open compact subgroups
K_{H}
and
K_{G}
to be compatible under the diagonal embedding. Then for
h\in H_{p}^{-}
, its image
\triangle(h)
lies in
G_{p}^{-}
. Let
H_{p}^{-}
act on
\mathcal{O}
via the monoid homomorphism

H_{p}^{-}\to(\mathcal{O},\times),\quad h\mapsto t^{-(\nu_{\Phi}\circ\mu\circ\triangle)(h)}.

By construction, this makes the branching morphism
H_{p}^{-}
-equivariant. This additional constant factor will not be relevant to us, since we will never consider Hecke operators from
H
except in the proof of Proposition
4.2
.

Finally, we extend a representation of
G
to one for
{\widetilde{G}}
by making the augmentation factor act trivially. The branching law and branching morphism goes through without any change.

2.4.
Shimura varieties

2.4.1.
Shimura data

Define an algebraic homomorphism

h_{n}:\Res_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}\mathbf{G}_{m}\to\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{n})\times_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R},\quad z\mapsto\left(\big(\begin{smallmatrix}{z}^{\mathtt{c}}/z&\\
&\mathrm{id}_{n-1}\end{smallmatrix}\big)_{\infty_{*}},\mathrm{id}_{n},\cdots,\mathrm{id}_{n}\right).

Let
X_{n}
be the conjugacy class of
h_{n}
, then the pair
(\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{n}),X_{n})
is a Shimura datum of abelian type with reflex field
E
, viewed as a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
by
\iota_{\infty}
. This is the Shimura datum used in
[
LTX+22
]
and conjugate to the one in
[
GGP12
]
.

If
U\subseteq\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{n})(\mathbb{A}_{f})
is a neat open compact subgroup, then denote the associated Shimura variety by
\mathrm{Sh}_{n}^{\circ}(U)
. It is a smooth algebraic variety over
E
of dimension
n-1
. As
U
varies, we obtain an inverse system of varieties with finite étale transition maps. This system will be denoted by
\mathrm{Sh}_{n}^{\circ}
. Let
g\in\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{n})(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, then we have a right translation map

T_{g}:\mathrm{Sh}_{n}^{\circ}(K)\to\mathrm{Sh}_{n}^{\circ}(g^{-1}Kg)

satisfying
T_{gh}=T_{h}\circ T_{g}
. This gives a right action of
\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{n})(\mathbb{A}_{f})
on the inverse system
\mathrm{Sh}_{n}^{\circ}
.

Remark 2.5
.

In what follows, we will sometimes implicitly assume the level structure is neat. This can be arranged by choosing a sufficiently small open compact subgroup at finitely many places and requiring our level to be contained in them
[
Lan13
, Remark 1.4.1.9]
. This does not impact the discussion.

The Shimura data
(\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{n+1}),X_{n+1})
and
(\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{1}),X_{1})
can be defined similarly. By taking products, we get Shimura data
(G,X_{G})
and
(\widetilde{G},X_{\widetilde{G}})
. Let
(H,X_{H})=(\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{n}),X_{n})
, then the embeddings defined above induces embeddings of Shimura data

(H,X_{H})\to(G,X_{G})\to(\widetilde{G},X_{\widetilde{G}}).

We adopt similar notations for Shimura varieties. Their reflex fields are all contained in
E
, so we view them as varieties over
E
. If
K_{{\widetilde{G}}}\subseteq{\widetilde{G}}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
and
K_{H}\subseteq H(\mathbb{A}_{f})
are both open compact, and
K_{H}\subseteq K_{\widetilde{G}}\cap H(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, then we get a morphism

(5)

\iota_{K_{H},K_{\widetilde{G}}}:\mathrm{Sh}_{H}^{\circ}(K_{H})\to\mathrm{Sh}_{{\widetilde{G}}}^{\circ}(K_{\widetilde{G}}),

which is still defined over
E
. Moreover, for each
K_{H}
, there exists a
K_{\widetilde{G}}
such that this morphism is a closed embedding, by
[
Del71
, Proposition 1.15]
. We summarize these properties by saying there is a closed embedding of inverse systems
\mathrm{Sh}_{H}^{\circ}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Sh}_{{\widetilde{G}}}^{\circ}
.

2.4.2.
Extensions of the base field

We now consider the Shimura set
\mathrm{Sh}_{1}
more carefully. By Hilbert’s theorem 90, we have the following isomorphism

E^{\times}\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}/E_{\infty}^{\times}\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\mathrm{Sh}_{1}(\mathbb{C})=E^{\times,1}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E,f}^{\times,1},\quad s\mapsto\frac{{s}^{\mathtt{c}}}{s},

where the superscript 1 means the norm one elements. Given an ideal
\mathfrak{c}
of
F
, recall that
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{c}}=\mathcal{O}_{F}+\mathfrak{c}\mathcal{O}_{E}
. Assume that
\mathfrak{c}
is coprime to 2 times the discriminant ideal of
E/F
, then under the above isomorphism,
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{c}}^{\times}
gets mapped to the open compact subgroup

J_{\mathfrak{c}}=\{x\in\mathbb{A}_{E,f}^{\times,1}\,|\,x\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{c}}\}\subseteq\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{1})(\mathbb{A}_{f}).

It follows that we have an isomorphism.

E^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}/\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathfrak{c}}^{\times}E_{\infty}^{\times}\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\mathrm{Sh}_{1}(J_{\mathfrak{c}})(\mathbb{C})=E^{\times,1}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E,f}^{\times,1}/J_{\mathfrak{c}}.

By class field theory, the left hand side can be identified with
\spec E[\mathfrak{c}]
as schemes over
E
. The resulting action of
\Aut(\mathbb{C}/E)
agrees with the one given by the Shimura reciprocity law on the right hand side. Therefore, we obtain an isomorphism of schemes over
E

\mathrm{Sh}_{1}(J_{\mathfrak{c}})\simeq\spec E[\mathfrak{c}].

Consequently,

(6)

\mathrm{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Sh}_{1}(J_{\mathfrak{c}})_{/\bar{E}},\mathbb{Z}_{p})=\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\mathrm{Sh}_{1}(J_{\mathfrak{c}})]=\Ind_{E}^{E[\mathfrak{c}]}\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Moreover, if
K\subseteq G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
is an open compact subgroup, then

(7)

\mathrm{Sh}_{\widetilde{G}}(K\times J_{\mathfrak{c}})\simeq\mathrm{Sh}_{G}(K)\times_{E}E[\mathfrak{c}].

This will be used to interpret cycles on
\mathrm{Sh}_{\widetilde{G}}
as cycles on
\mathrm{Sh}_{G}
defined over field extensions of
E
.

The Hecke algebra for
{\widetilde{G}}
decomposes as

C_{c}^{\infty}({\widetilde{G}}(\mathbb{A}_{f}),\mathbb{Z})\simeq C_{c}^{\infty}(G(\mathbb{A}_{f}),\mathbb{Z})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathrm{U}(\mathtt{V}_{1})(\mathbb{A}_{f}),\mathbb{Z}).

Under the isomorphism (
7
), the action of a Hecke operator
\mathcal{T}\otimes\mathbf{1}[xJ_{\mathfrak{c}}]
on the left hand side becomes
\mathcal{T}\otimes\Art_{E}(s)
on the right hand side, where
s\in\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
is any element such that
x={s}^{\mathtt{c}}/s
.

2.4.3.
Coefficients

We follow the formalism in
[
HT01
, §III.2]
to construct
p
-adic étale local systems on Shimura varieties attached to the lattices considered in Subsection

. The discussion only assumes
H
is a reductive group over
F
with a Shimura variety. In particular, it also applies to
{\widetilde{G}}
.

Fix an open compact subgroup of
\mathbf{H}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=\prod_{\mathfrak{q}|p}H(F_{\mathfrak{q}})
with an Iwahori factorization. Using it, define

H(\mathbb{A}_{f})^{\pm}:=H(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})\times H_{p}^{\pm},

where the notations are as in Subsection
2.3.3
. Let

be one of the monoid representations constructed in Lemma

. By letting
H(\mathbb{A}_{f}^{p})
act trivially, it can be extended to a monoid homomorphism
H(\mathbb{A}_{f})^{-}\to\End_{\mathcal{O}}(\Lambda)
, which we continue to denote by
\xi^{\circ}
.

Let
K\subseteq H(\mathbb{A}_{f})
be an open compact subgroup contained in
H(\mathbb{A}_{f})^{-}
. The inverse system of varieties
\{\mathrm{Sh}_{H}(K^{\prime})\}_{K^{\prime}\subseteq K}
forms a Galois covering of
\mathrm{Sh}_{H}(K)
whose Galois group is
K
. Let
\mathbb{L}_{\xi}^{(K)}
be the local system corresponding to the restriction
\xi^{\circ}|_{K}
. More precisely, it is defined by

\mathbb{L}_{\xi}^{(K)}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{L}_{\xi,n}^{(K)},

where each
\mathbb{L}_{\xi,n}^{(K)}
is the
p^{n}
-torsion local system corresponds to the torsion representation on
\Lambda/p^{n}\Lambda
. We will often drop the superscript if the level is clear. If
g\in H(\mathbb{A}_{f})^{-}
and
K,K^{\prime}
are two level structures as above such that
g^{-1}Kg\subseteq K^{\prime}
, then we have morphisms

T_{g}:\mathrm{Sh}_{H}(K)\to\mathrm{Sh}_{H}(K^{\prime}),\quad\vec{T}_{g}:T_{g}^{*}\mathbb{L}_{\xi}^{(K^{\prime})}\to\mathbb{L}_{\xi}^{(K)}.

They satisfies the usual cocycle condition
\vec{T}_{gh}=\vec{T}_{g}\circ T_{g}^{*}\vec{T}_{h}
. We may pass to the infinite level and define

(8)

\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathrm{Sh}_{H/\bar{E}},\mathbb{L}_{\xi}):=\varinjlim_{K\subseteq H(\mathbb{A}_{f})^{-}}\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}\big(\mathrm{Sh}_{H}(K)_{/\bar{E}},\mathbb{L}_{\xi}^{(K)}\big),

where the transition maps are the pullbacks induced by
(T_{g},\vec{T}_{g})
. Moreover, write

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\bullet}\big(\mathrm{Sh}_{H}(K),\mathbb{L}_{\xi}^{(K)}\big)

for the continuous étale cohomology of the inverse system
(\mathbb{L}_{\xi}^{(K)}/p^{n}\mathbb{L}_{\xi}^{(K)})_{n\geq 1}
defined in
[
Jan88
]
. We can then define

(9)

\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{Sh}_{H},\mathbb{L}_{\xi}):=\varinjlim_{K\subseteq H(\mathbb{A}_{f})}\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\bullet}\big(\mathrm{Sh}_{H}(K),\mathbb{L}_{\xi}^{(K)}\big).

Both
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}^{\bullet}
and
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\bullet}
carry natural left
H(\mathbb{A}_{f})^{-}
-actions given by the pullback
T_{g}^{*}
.

All the constructions are naturally functorial when there is a morphism of representations
\xi_{1}^{\circ}\to\xi_{2}^{\circ}
. Moreover, if
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and
\xi^{\circ}
is trivial, then the local systems are just the constant sheaf
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and we have transition morphisms for all
g\in H(\mathbb{A}_{f})
.

Let
\xi
be a perfectly interlacing representation for
G
, which we extend trivially to
{\widetilde{G}}
. The goal of this subsection is to construct a collection of classes in
\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{cont}}^{2n}(\mathrm{Sh}_{{\widetilde{G}}},\mathbb{L}_{\xi}(n))

and

Let

Remark 2.6
.

It is not true in general that

is a closed embedding, but it always factors as a closed embedding followed by an étale covering map, so we may still define the cycle class map

In Subsection

\mathtt{br}_{g,*}:\mathrm{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}_{H}(K_{H}^{(g)}),\mathbb{Z}_{p})\to\mathrm{H}^{0}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\mathrm{Sh}_{H}(K_{H}^{(g)}),\mathbb{L}_{\xi|_{H}^{\circ}}).

The left hand side contains the fundamental class

\displaystyle=[\tau^{t}N(\mathcal{O})\tau^{-t}:\tau^{t+1}N(\mathcal{O})\tau^{-(t+1)}]

\displaystyle=[\tau^{-1}N(\mathcal{O})\tau:N(\mathcal{O})]

\displaystyle=[\tau^{-1}K_{G}^{\mathrm{Iw}}\tau:K_{G}^{\mathrm{Iw}}\cap\tau^{-1}K_{G}^{\mathrm{Iw}}\tau],

where the final equality again follows from the Iwahori decomposition. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

This is essentially the same as the construction formulated in
[
Loe21
]
.

4.
Tame norm relation

This section will prove the tame norm relation. The proof is a technical local computation, and it is independent of the rest of the paper. One can skip to Section
4.6
, which contains a statement of the main result in a form that immediately applies to the construction of Euler systems.

We will continue using the local notations introduced in Section
3.1
. Moreover, fix an additive character
\psi:F/\mathcal{O}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\psi(\varpi^{-1})\neq 1
. For psychological reasons, we will denote
\#k_{F}
by
\ell
instead of
q
.

4.1.
Whittaker models

For each positive integer
n
, let
N_{n}(F)
denote the subgroup of unipotent upper triangular matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, then
\psi
extends to a character of
N_{n}(F)
by

\psi(u)=\psi(u_{12}+\cdots+u_{n-1,n}).

The space of Whittaker functions on
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
with character
\psi
is defined by

\mathcal{W}_{n}(\psi)=\{W:\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\to\mathbb{C}\text{ smooth}\,|\,W(ug)=\psi(u)W(g)\text{ for all }u\in N_{n}(F),\ g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\}.

For
\underline{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, define
\varpi^{\underline{a}}=\diag(\varpi^{a_{1}},\cdots,\varpi^{a_{n}})
. By the Iwasawa decomposition,
W\in\mathcal{W}_{n}(\psi)
is uniquely determined by its values on matrices of the form
\varpi^{\underline{a}}k
with
k\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. The group
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
acts on
\mathcal{W}_{n}(\psi)
by right translation, and we let
\mathcal{W}_{n}(\psi)^{\circ}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{n}(\psi)
be the subspace of (right)
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
-invariant functions.

If
\pi
is a smooth admissible representation of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)
, then it is classical that

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Hom_{\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)}(\pi,\mathcal{W}_{n}(\psi))\leq 1

and the dimension is 1 for a dense set of irreducible unramified representations. In the dimension one case, let
\mathcal{W}_{n}(\pi,\psi)
denote the image of any non-trivial map in the Hom-space. This is the Whittaker model of
\pi
.

4.2.
Local Birch lemma

Let
\varphi
denote the diagonal matrix
\varpi^{(-1,\cdots,-n)}
. Let

\mathcal{N}_{n}:=\varphi^{-1}N_{n}(\mathcal{O})\varphi/N_{n}(\mathcal{O}).

Given integers
j>i>0
, let
R_{ij}
denote a set of representatives for
\varpi^{i-j}\mathcal{O}/\mathcal{O}
in
F
, then

\mathscr{N}_{n}:=\{\eta\in N_{n}(F)\,|\,\eta_{ij}\in R_{ij}\text{ if }1\leq i<j\leq n\}

is a set of coset representatives for
\mathcal{N}_{n}
. It has cardinality
\prod_{1\leq j\leq i\leq n}\ell^{j-i}=\ell^{\frac{1}{6}n(n+1)(n-1)}
. Let
s:F\to\{0,1\}
be the indicator function of
\mathcal{O}
. By an abuse of notation, also define
s:\mathcal{N}_{n}\to\mathbb{N}
by
s(\eta)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}s(\eta_{i,i+1})
. This counts the number of integral off-diagonal elements of
\eta
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
W_{n+1}\in\mathcal{W}_{n+1}(\psi)^{\circ}
, then the function

h\mapsto\widetilde{W_{n+1}}(h):=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{n+1}}(1-\ell)^{s(\eta)}W_{n+1}(\iota_{n,n+1}(h)\eta)

is supported on
N_{n}(F)\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. Moreover, if
k\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
, then

\widetilde{W_{n+1}}(k)=\begin{cases}(-1)^{n}\ell^{\frac{1}{6}n(n+1)(n+2)}W_{n+1}(1)&\text{if }v(k_{ij})>i-j\text{ whenever }i>j\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

First observe that
\widetilde{W_{n+1}}
is well-defined since
W_{n+1}
is spherical, so we can write the sum over
\mathscr{N}_{n+1}
instead. Given
\eta\in\mathscr{N}_{n+1}
, it can be partitioned into

\eta=\begin{pmatrix}\xi&v\\
&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\xi\in\mathscr{N}_{n},\ v\in C_{n}:=R_{n}\times\cdots\times R_{1}.

By performing the sum over
v\in C_{n}
first, we get

\widetilde{W_{n+1}}(h)=\sum_{\xi\in\mathscr{N}_{n}}(1-\ell)^{s(\xi)}\sum_{v\in C_{n}}(1-\ell)^{s(v_{n})}W_{n+1}(\iota_{n,n+1}(h)\eta).

It is clear that
\widetilde{W_{n+1}}\in\mathcal{W}_{n}(\psi)
, so
\widetilde{W_{n+1}}
is determined by its values on matrices of the form
\varpi^{\underline{a}}k
, where
\underline{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
k\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. The inner sum is then

(\dagger):=\sum_{v\in C_{n}}(1-\ell)^{s(v_{n})}W_{n+1}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\varpi^{\underline{a}}k&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\xi&v\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right).

Observe that

\begin{pmatrix}\varpi^{\underline{a}}k&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\xi&v\\
&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{n}&\varpi^{\underline{a}}kv\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\varpi^{\underline{a}}k\xi&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}.

Since
W_{n+1}\in\mathcal{W}_{n+1}(\psi)
, we get that

W_{n+1}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\varpi^{\underline{a}}k&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\xi&v\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)=\psi(\varpi^{a_{n}}(kv)_{n})W_{n+1}\left(\begin{pmatrix}\varpi^{\underline{a}}k\xi&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\right)=\psi(\varpi^{a_{n}}(kv)_{n})W_{n}(\varpi^{\underline{a}}k\xi),

where
W_{n}:=W_{n+1}\circ\iota_{n,n+1}
. The first term can be expanded as

\psi(\varpi^{a_{n}}(kv)_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\psi(\varpi^{a_{n}}k_{ni}v_{i}).

Therefore,

(\dagger)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{v_{i}\in R_{n-i+1}}(\ast)\,\psi(\varpi^{a_{n}}k_{ni}v_{i})\right)W_{n}(\varpi^{\underline{a}}k\xi),

where
(\ast)=1
if
i\neq n
, and
(\ast)=(1-\ell)^{s(v_{n})}
if
i=n
.

For an arbitrary
x\in\mathcal{O}
and index
i
, replacing
v_{i}
by
v_{i}+x
multiplies this product by
\psi(\varpi^{a_{n}}x)
. On the other hand, we have observed that this does not change the value of
(\dagger)
. Therefore,
(\dagger)=0
unless
a_{n}\geq 0
. Assuming this, we get
\varpi^{a_{n}}k_{ni}\in\mathcal{O}
for all
i
, so standard orthogonality relations gives

\sum_{v_{i}\in R_{n-i+1}}\psi(\varpi^{a_{n}}k_{ni}v_{i})=\begin{cases}\ell^{n-i+1}&\text{if }v(k_{ni})+a_{n}\geq n-i+1\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For
i=n
, the inclusion of the factor
(1-\ell)^{s(v_{n})}
gives

\sum_{v_{n}\in R_{1}}(1-\ell)^{s(v_{n})}\psi(\varpi^{a_{n}}k_{nn}v_{n})=\begin{cases}-\ell&\text{if }v(k_{nn})+a_{n}=0\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The expression
(\dagger)
is a product of the above terms. For it to be non-zero, we must have
v(k_{nn})=a_{n}=0
and
v(k_{ni})\geq n-i+1
for all
i<n
. Therefore,

(17)

(\dagger)=\begin{cases}-\ell^{\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)}W_{n}(\varpi^{\underline{a}}k\xi)&\text{if }a_{n}=0\text{ and for all }i<n,\ v(k_{ni})>n-i\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Assuming the above conditions are met, we are left with

\widetilde{W_{n+1}}(\varpi^{\underline{a_{n}}}k)=-\ell^{\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)}\sum_{\xi\in\mathscr{N}_{n}}(1-\ell)^{s(\xi)}W_{n}(\varpi^{\underline{a}}k\xi).

Decompose
k
as follows:

k=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{n}&x\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\cdot\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{n}&\\
y&u\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}k^{\prime}+xy&ux\\
y&u\end{pmatrix}.

By comparing entries, we see that
u=k_{nn}
is a unit, and for all
i\leq n-1
,
y_{i}=k_{ni}\in\varpi^{n-i+1}\mathcal{O}
and
x_{i}=u^{-1}k_{in}\in\mathcal{O}
. Moreover,

(18)

v((k^{\prime})_{ij}-k_{ij})=v(x_{i})+v(y_{j})\geq n-j+1.

We also have
a_{n}=0
, so

W_{n}(\varpi^{\underline{a}}k\xi)=\psi(\varpi^{a_{n-1}}k_{n-1,n})W_{n}\left(\iota_{n-1,n}(\varpi^{\underline{a}^{\prime}}k^{\prime})\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{n}&\\
y&u\end{pmatrix}\xi\right),

where
\underline{a}^{\prime}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n-1})
.

Let
b=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{n}&\\
y&u\end{smallmatrix}\big)
and
B=\varphi b\varphi^{-1}
. The valuation bounds on
y
implies that
B\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
and
B\equiv\mathrm{id}_{n}\pmod{\varpi}
. Write
\xi=\varphi^{-1}\Xi\varphi
, then
\Xi\in N_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. By performing column operations on the matrix
B\Xi
, we obtain a factorization

B\Xi=\Xi^{\prime}C,

where
\Xi^{\prime}\in N_{n}(\mathcal{O})
and
C
a lower triangular matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. Moreover,
C\equiv\mathrm{id}_{n}\pmod{\varpi}
. Conjugating by
\varphi
gives

b\xi=\xi^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}C\varphi),\quad\xi^{\prime}:=\varphi^{-1}\Xi^{\prime}\varphi\in\varphi^{-1}N_{n}(\mathcal{O})\varphi.

Since
C
is lower triangular, we still have
\varphi^{-1}C\varphi\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. Therefore, this is the Iwasawa decomposition for
b\xi
, and
\xi\mapsto\xi^{\prime}
is a permutation of
\mathcal{N}_{n}
. By comparing the
(i,i+1)
-th entry of both sides, we see that

\xi_{i,i+1}=(\xi^{\prime})_{i,i+1}C_{i+1,i+1}+\sum_{j>i+1}(\xi^{\prime})_{ij}C_{j,i+1}\varpi^{j-(i+1)}.

Since
C\equiv\mathrm{id}_{n}\pmod{\varphi}
, each term in the sum is integral, and
C_{i+1,i+1}
is a unit. Therefore,
\xi_{i,i+1}\in\mathcal{O}
if and only if
(\xi^{\prime})_{i,i+1}\in\mathcal{O}
. It follows that
s(\xi)=s(\xi^{\prime})
.

Combining everything together, we see that when the non-vanishing condition in equation (
17
) is satisfied,

\displaystyle\widetilde{W_{n+1}}(\varpi^{\underline{a}}k)

\displaystyle=-\ell^{\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)}\sum_{\xi\in\mathscr{N}_{n}}(1-\ell)^{s(\xi)}W_{n}(\varpi^{\underline{a}}k\xi)

\displaystyle=-\ell^{\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)}\psi(\varpi^{a_{n-1}}k_{n-1,n})\sum_{\xi\in\mathscr{N}_{n}}(1-\ell)^{s(\xi)}W_{n}\left(\iota_{n-1,n}(\varpi^{\underline{a}^{\prime}}k^{\prime})\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{n}&\\
y&u\end{pmatrix}\xi\right)

\displaystyle=-\ell^{\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)}\psi(\varpi^{a_{n-1}}k_{n-1,n})\sum_{\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}(1-\ell)^{s(\xi^{\prime})}W_{n}(\iota_{n-1,n}(\varpi^{\underline{a}^{\prime}}k^{\prime})\xi^{\prime}).

By induction, we may assume this lemma has been proven for
n
. Therefore, the sum is 0 unless
\underline{a}^{\prime}=0
and
v((k^{\prime})_{ij})>i-j
for all
1\leq j<i\leq n-1
. In particular,
a_{n-1}=0
, so the character term is trivial. Moreover, equation (
18
) implies that

v((k^{\prime})_{ij})>i-j\iff v(k_{ij})>i-j.

Combined with equation (
17
), we obtain the lemma.
∎

4.3.
Hecke algebra and cyclicity

Let
K_{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})\times\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathcal{O})
and
K_{H}=K_{G}\cap H=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. They are hyperspecial maximal compact subgroups of
G
and
H
respectively. In the rest of this section, fix the Haar measure on
G
(resp.
H
) so that
K_{G}
(resp.
K_{H}
) has volume 1.

The spherical Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}_{G}
is the set
C_{c}^{\infty}(K_{G}\backslash G/K_{G},\mathbb{C})
of compactly supported
K_{G}
-bi-invariant functions on
G
with multiplication given by convolution

(f_{1}\ast f_{2})(g):=\int_{G}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x^{-1}g)dx,

For the augmented group
\widetilde{G}=G\times F^{\times}
, take the maximal compact subgroup
K_{G}\times\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. Defining
\mathcal{H}_{{\widetilde{G}}}
analogously, then we have a relation

\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}=\mathcal{H}_{G}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}C_{c}^{\infty}(F^{\times}/\mathcal{O}^{\times},\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{H}_{G}[\mathtt{T}^{\pm 1}],

where
\mathtt{T}
is the indicator function of
\varpi\mathcal{O}^{\times}
in
F^{\times}/\mathcal{O}^{\times}
. On both
\mathcal{H}_{G}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
, we can define the involution
f\mapsto f^{\vee}
by
f^{\vee}(g):=f(g^{-1})
.

Given a smooth unramified representation
\widetilde{\pi}
of
\widetilde{G}
, the space
\widetilde{\pi}^{K_{\widetilde{G}}}
has the usual left
\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
-action. The trace of
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
will be denoted by
\Tr\widetilde{\pi}(f)
. Of course, if
\widetilde{\pi}
is irreducible, then
\widetilde{\pi}^{K_{\widetilde{G}}}
is 1-dimensional, and
\widetilde{\pi}(f)v=\Tr\widetilde{\pi}(f)v
for any
v\in\widetilde{\pi}
.

The space
C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{C})^{K_{\widetilde{G}}}
has a natural left
\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
-action, inherited from the right
\widetilde{G}
-action on
\widetilde{X}
. Let

\delta_{0}=\mathbf{1}[HK_{\widetilde{G}}]\in C_{c}^{\infty}\big(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{C}\big)^{K_{\widetilde{G}}}

be the basic element. With our choice of the Haar measure,
I_{H}(\mathbf{1}[K_{\widetilde{G}}])=\delta_{0}
. The following proposition follows easily from some classical results.

Proposition 4.2
.

The
\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
-module
C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{C})^{\widetilde{G}}
is generated by
\delta_{0}
.

Proof.

This follows from
[
Sak13
, Corollary 8.0.4(a)]
, since the restriction map (8.2) at
loc. cit.
is actually the identity in our setting. It is also an easy consequence of the calculations of
[
MS96
, Section 3]
.
∎

Finally, we need a special Hecke operator whose trace gives the inverse of a local
L
-factor.

Proposition 4.3
.

There exists a unique
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
such that

\Tr\widetilde{\pi}(\mathcal{L})=L\Big(\frac{1}{2},\widetilde{\pi}\Big)^{-1}

for all generic irreducible unramified representations
\widetilde{\pi}
of
\widetilde{G}

Proof.

We have the Satake isomorphism

\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}\simeq\mathbb{C}[A_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,A_{n}^{\pm 1}]^{S_{n}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}[B_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,B_{n+1}^{\pm 1}]^{S_{n+1}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[T^{\pm 1}],

where the symmetric groups
S_{n}
and
S_{n+1}
act by permuting their respective variables, and
\mathtt{T}
is sent to
T
. Let
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
be the Hecke operator whose image is the polynomial

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{j=1}^{n+1}\big(1-A_{i}B_{j}T\ell^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big).

Comparing this with the definition of the local
L
-factor, we see that
\mathcal{L}
satisfies the condition. Uniqueness follows since the set of Satake parameters for generic unramified representations is dense.
∎

4.4.
Zeta integral computation

Let
K_{H}^{\mathrm{Iw}}
be the Iwahori subgroup of
K_{H}
of matrices which reduce to an upper triangular matrix mod
\varpi
. Recall from the previous subsection that
\varphi=\varpi^{(-1,\cdots,-n)}
. Define

K_{H}^{\varphi}=K_{H}^{\mathrm{Iw}}\cap\varphi K_{H}^{\mathrm{Iw}}\varphi^{-1}.

This is the subgroup of
K_{H}
defined by the valuation condition
v(k_{ij})>i-j
whenever
i>j
. Let
\mathcal{N}_{n}
and
s:\mathcal{N}_{n}\to\mathbb{N}
be as in the Section
4.2
. Define

(19)

\delta^{\prime}=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})^{-1}(1-\ell)^{s(\eta)}\mathbf{1}[(1,\eta)K_{G}\times\mathcal{O}^{\times}]\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{G},\mathbb{C})^{K_{G}\times\mathcal{O}^{\times}}.

By the general cyclicity result (Proposition
4.2
), we know that
I_{H}(\delta^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}\cdot\delta_{0}
for some
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
. We will now determine
\mathcal{P}
using a zeta integral.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
be the element defined in Proposition
4.3
, then

(-1)^{n}\ell^{-\frac{1}{6}n(n+1)(n+2)}I_{H}(\delta^{\prime})=\mathcal{L}^{\vee}\cdot\delta_{0}.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\pi}
be an irreducible generic unramified representation of
\widetilde{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(F)\times\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(F)\times F^{\times}
. With respect to this decomposition, it factors as
\widetilde{\pi}=\pi_{n}\otimes\pi_{n+1}\otimes\chi
. Fix a spherical vector
v^{\circ}\in\widetilde{\pi}^{K_{\widetilde{G}}}
. Given a map
\mathfrak{z}\in\Hom_{H}(\widetilde{\pi},\mathbb{C})
, form the relative matrix coefficient

\mathcal{S}:C_{c}^{\infty}({\widetilde{G}},\mathbb{C})^{K_{\widetilde{G}}}\to\mathbb{C},\ \phi\mapsto\mathfrak{z}(\widetilde{\pi}(\phi)v^{\circ}).

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{G}}
and
v\in\widetilde{\pi}^{K_{\widetilde{G}}}
, then

\displaystyle\widetilde{\pi}(f\cdot\phi)v

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{G}}\int_{\widetilde{G}}\phi(xg)f(g)\widetilde{\pi}(x)v\,dgdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{G}}\int_{\widetilde{G}}\phi(y)f(g)\widetilde{\pi}(yg^{-1})v\,dgdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\widetilde{G}}\phi(y)\pi(y)\int_{\widetilde{G}}f(g)\widetilde{\pi}(g^{-1})vdgdy

\displaystyle=\big(\Tr\pi(f^{\vee})\big)\widetilde{\pi}(\phi)v.

In particular, we get
\mathcal{S}(f\cdot\phi)=\Tr\widetilde{\pi}(f^{\vee})\mathcal{S}(\phi)
.

The map
\mathcal{S}
is invariant under left translation by
H
, so it defines a quotient map

\overline{\mathcal{S}}:C_{c}^{\infty}({\widetilde{X}},\mathbb{C})^{K_{\widetilde{G}}}\to\mathbb{C},\quad\overline{\mathcal{S}}(I_{H}(\phi))=\mathcal{S}(\phi).

Applying this to the relation
I_{H}(\delta^{\prime})=\mathcal{P}\cdot\delta_{0}
gives

\mathfrak{z}(\widetilde{\pi}(\delta^{\prime})v^{\circ})=\Tr\widetilde{\pi}(\mathcal{P}^{\vee})\mathfrak{z}(v^{\circ}).

To prove this proposition, it remains to compute both sides for an explicit choice of
\mathfrak{z}
and
v^{\circ}
.

Let
\mathcal{W}_{\widetilde{\pi}}:=\mathcal{W}_{n}(\pi_{n},\psi^{-1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{n+1}(\pi_{n+1},\psi)\otimes\chi
denote the Whittaker model for
\widetilde{\pi}
. Note the difference in additive characters between the two terms. Define
\mathfrak{z}\in\Hom_{H}(\widetilde{\pi},\mathbb{C})
by the following zeta integral

\mathfrak{z}\big(W_{n}\otimes W_{n+1}\big)=\int_{N_{n}\backslash H}W_{n}(h)W_{n+1}(\iota(h))\chi(\det h)dh.

Let
W^{\circ}=W_{n}^{\circ}\otimes W_{n+1}^{\circ}
be the spherical Whittaker function in
\mathcal{W}_{\pi}
, normalized so that
W_{n}^{\circ}(1)=W_{n+1}^{\circ}(1)=1
. The classical calculation of Jacquet–Shalika gives

\mathfrak{z}(W^{\circ})=L\Big(\frac{1}{2},\widetilde{\pi}\Big).

On the other hand, by Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{z}\big(\pi({\delta}^{\prime})W^{\circ}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}(1-\ell)^{s(\eta)}\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})^{-1}\mathfrak{z}((1,\eta)W^{\circ})

\displaystyle=\int_{N_{n}\backslash H}\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})^{-1}W_{n}(h)\sum_{\eta\in\mathcal{N}_{n}}(1-\ell)^{s(\eta)}W_{n+1}(\iota(h)\eta)\chi(\det h)dh

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\ell^{\frac{1}{6}n(n+1)(n+2)}.

The proposition follows.
∎

4.5.
Integrality

The following groups were defined in Section
3.1
:

J_{0}=\mathcal{O}^{\times},\quad J_{1}=\{x\in\mathcal{O}^{\times}\,|\,x\equiv 1\pmod{\varpi}\}.

We have constructed an element
I_{H}(\delta^{\prime})\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{C})^{K_{G}\times J_{0}}
. For Euler system applications, we need to know that it lands in the image of

C_{c}^{\infty}\big(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{Z}[\ell^{-1}]\big)^{K_{G}\times J_{1}}

under the trace map
\Tr_{K_{G}\times J_{0}}^{K_{G}\times J_{1}}
. This will be done by another explicit computation.

Let
\mathscr{N}_{n}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{N}_{n}
denote the set of
\eta
such that
\varpi\eta_{i,i+1}\equiv 0\text{ or 1}\pmod{\varpi}
for all
i
. Define

(20)

{\delta}_{1}^{\prime}=\sum_{\eta\in\mathscr{N}^{\prime}_{n}}\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})^{-1}(-1)^{s(\eta)}(\ell-1)^{n}\mathbf{1}[(1,\eta)K_{G}\times J_{1}]\in C_{c}^{\infty}\big(\widetilde{G},\mathbb{C}\big)^{K_{G}\times J_{1}}.

Proposition 4.5
.

I_{H}(\delta^{\prime}_{1})\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{X},\mathbb{Z}[\ell^{-1}])^{K_{G}\times J_{1}}
and
\Tr_{K_{G}\times J_{0}}^{K_{G}\times J_{1}}I_{H}(\delta_{1}^{\prime})=I_{H}(\delta^{\prime})
.

Proof.

We have

I_{H}(\delta^{\prime}_{1})=\sum_{\eta\in\mathscr{N}^{\prime}_{n}}(-1)^{s(\eta)}\frac{\mu_{H}(H_{\eta})}{\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})}(\ell-1)^{n}\mathbf{1}[H((1,\eta)K_{G,1}\times J_{1})],

where
H_{\eta}
is the stabilizer

H_{\eta}:=H\cap\big((1,\eta)K_{G}(1,\eta^{-1})\times J_{1}\big).

We first show that the coefficients are in
\mathbb{Z}[\ell^{-1}]
. Let

K_{H,1}^{\varphi}=\{h\in K_{H}^{\varphi}\,|\,h_{ii}\equiv 1\ (\text{mod }\varpi)\text{ for all }i\}.

Then
K_{H,1}^{\varphi}
is a pro
\ell
-group, and
\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})=(\ell-1)^{n}\mu_{H}(K_{H,1}^{\varphi})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{H}(H_{\eta})}{\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})}(\ell-1)^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{H}(H_{\eta})}{\mu_{H}(K_{H,1}^{\varphi})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{H}(H_{\eta})}{\mu_{H}(H_{\eta}\cap K_{H,1}^{\varphi})}\cdot\frac{\mu_{H}(H_{\eta}\cap K_{H,1}^{\varphi})}{\mu_{H}(K_{H,1}^{\varphi})}

\displaystyle=\frac{[H_{\eta}:H_{\eta}\cap K_{H,1}^{\varphi}]}{[K_{H,1}^{\varphi}:H_{\eta}\cap K_{H,1}^{\varphi}]}\in\mathbb{Z}[\ell^{-1}].

By multiplying the columns and rows of an element
\eta\in\mathscr{N}_{n}
by appropriate units, we can define a map
\mathtt{pr}:\mathscr{N}_{n}\to\mathscr{N}_{n}^{\prime}
such that
H(1,\eta)K_{G}=H(1,\mathtt{pr}(\eta))K_{G}
for all
\eta
. The map
\mathtt{pr}
also does not change the value of
s(\eta)
. The sizes of the fibres are
\#\mathtt{pr}^{-1}(\eta^{\prime})=(\ell-1)^{n-s(\eta^{\prime})}
, so we can decompose
\delta^{\prime}
as follows:

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta\in\mathscr{N}_{n}}\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})^{-1}(-1)^{s(\eta)}(\ell-1)^{s(\eta)}\mathbf{1}[(1,\eta)K_{G}\times J_{0}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\eta^{\prime}\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{\prime}}\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})^{-1}(-1)^{s(\eta^{\prime})}(\ell-1)^{n}\cdot(\ell-1)^{-(n-s(\eta^{\prime}))}\sum_{\mathtt{pr}(\eta)=\eta^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}[(1,\eta)K_{G}\times J_{0}].

Therefore,

\begin{split}\Tr_{K_{G}\times J_{0}}^{K_{G}\times J_{1}}({\delta}^{\prime}_{1})-{\delta}^{\prime}=\sum_{\eta^{\prime}\in\mathscr{N}_{n}^{\prime}}\mu_{H}(K_{H}^{\varphi})^{-1}&(-1)^{s(\eta^{\prime})}(\ell-1)^{n}\\
&\times\left(\mathbf{1}[(1,\eta^{\prime})K_{G}\times J_{0}]-\frac{1}{(\ell-1)^{n-s(\eta^{\prime})}}\sum_{\mathtt{pr}(\eta)=\eta^{\prime}}\mathbf{1}[(1,\eta)K_{G}\times J_{0}]\right).\end{split}

After applying
I_{H}
, the term in the parenthesis becomes 0, giving the required result.
∎

for all unramified representations

of
{\widetilde{G}}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

There exists an element

Let

5.3.

Fix a non-zero vector

Theorem 5.5
.

If

\Tr_{E[\mathfrak{m}]}^{E[\mathfrak{m}\ell]}z_{\mathfrak{m}\ell}=\begin{cases}\lambda_{\mathfrak{p}}z_{\mathfrak{m}}&\text{if }\ell=\mathfrak{p}\\

P_{\lambda}(\mathbf{N}\ell^{-s})=L\Big(s+\frac{1}{2},\pi_{\ell}^{\vee}\Big)^{-1}

and

Proof.

Under the identification (

to
E[\mathfrak{m}]

where the final equality follows from the equivariance of

The traces on the right hand side are given by Proposition
5.3

\tau^{-1}

Comparing with the definition of the local

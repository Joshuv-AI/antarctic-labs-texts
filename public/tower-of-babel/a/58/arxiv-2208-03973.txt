An analytical backtracking method for electron beam longitudinal phase space shaping Preprint: APS/123-QED
N. Sudar and Y. Ding

Abstract
Many applications of high brightness, highly relativistic electron beams carry strict requirements on longitudinal phase space quality. To meet these requirements, accelerator systems typically utilize dispersive elements to manipulate the correlated energy spread acquired during acceleration or via collective effects. The many free variables in these systems make finding an optimal configuration difficult. We present here an analytical method for tracking a desired final longitudinal phase space backwards through an accelerator section composed of an acceleration or drift section followed by a dispersive section, including analytical models of pertinent collective effects. This backtracking can serve as a fast and accurate tool for optimization and study of the longitudinal phase space dynamics of an accelerator system. Here, we consider the LCLS-II accelerator as an example case, using this method to find an accelerator configuration and longitudinal phase space at the injector exit that lead to a significant increase in peak current while maintaining a narrow energy spread at the accelerator exit. The validity of this method is tested by comparison with Elegant simulations, showing good agreement.

III
Chirps and collective effects

In the previous section, we considered the transport through an acceleration section and/or drift, describing the chirp acquired by the beam with the polynomial coefficients,
H_{n}
. Here we describe the calculation of this chirp in terms of the parameters of the accelerator and the coefficients of the final current profile.

III.1
Chirp from RF curvature

For an acceleration section the chirp is given by the expansion of:

\displaystyle\Delta\eta_{a}(s)=\frac{eVN_{c}}{mc^{2}\gamma_{f}}\cos{(ks+\phi)}

(16)

Where m is the electron mass, e is the magnitude of the electron charge, c is the speed of light,

is the number of cavities in the accelerator section, V is the voltage in each cavity, k is the RF frequency and

is the acceleration phase, and
\gamma_{f}=\gamma_{i}+\frac{eVN_{c}}{mc^{2}\gamma_{f}}\cos{(\phi)}
is the central energy of the electron beam at the exit of the accelerator section

The chirp coefficients

are then given by:

\displaystyle H_{a(n)}=\frac{eVN_{c}}{mc^{2}\gamma_{f}}\frac{k^{n}}{n!}\cos{(\phi+n\frac{\pi}{2})}

III.2
Chirp from cavity wakefields

We next consider the chirp coming from longitudinal wakefields from the cavities or from the resistive walls of the beam pipe. The chirp here is given by the convolution of the beam current distribution with the wake kernel associated with the cavity or beam pipe:

III.4
Chirp from longitudinal space charge

The chirp from longitudinal space charge (LSC) can be found considering the LSC impedance in free space

;

\displaystyle Z_{LSC}(k)=\frac{ikZ_{0}}{\pi\gamma^{2}}\frac{1-\zeta K_{1}(\zeta)}{\zeta^{2}}

\displaystyle Z_{LSC}(k)\sim\frac{ikZ_{0}}{4\pi\gamma^{2}}[1.232-2\log(\zeta)]

III.5
Chirp from coherent synchrotron radiation

In order to find an analytical expression for the coherent synchrotron radiation (CSR) energy modulation generated by the beam passing through a bend magnet, we consider only the short beam-long magnet case where the electron beam experiences energy modulation from the entrance transient, a steady state regime, and the exit transient. This is true in many cases where the electron beam peak current or bend strength is large enough to generate significant energy modulation. For instance, in the case of the LCLS-II accelerator significant energy modulation from CSR is generated in dog-leg magnets located in the long bypass line after the beam reaches full compression. V
conclusion

The analytical approach to backtracking outlined in this paper offers a fast, effective method to investigate the longitudinal phase space requirements at various points along the accelerator, relating the final and initial longitudinal phase spaces and current profiles with analytical expressions. Comparison with forward tracking in Elegant shows that the method is robust and could be potentially applied to more exotic configurations. The extension of this method to backtrack to the cathode is made complicated by the need to include transverse space charge effects and requires additional study. However combining this with backtracking in the particle tracking code Opal
adelmann2008opal
, offers a possible path forward.

The method is limited in scope to piecewise continuous distributions, however in many cases the associated caustics are what we seek to avoid. To avoid these caustics, a recommended starting point is to match the non-linear chirp acquired in the final drift/acceleration section, linearizing the phase space at the exit of the dispersive section. Given target final and initial phase spaces it may be possible to use this backtracking method to solve directly for an accelerator configuration, however we leave this for further study. Furthermore, combining this backtracking solution with current machine learning techniques may be advantageous for swiftly finding a suitable initial phase space and accelerator configuration.

Appendix B
CSR chirp derivation

The derivation of the CSR chirp follows
saldin1997coherent
;
stupakov2002csr
, using the Lienard Wiechert potentials to find the electric field experienced by a particle as it traverses a bend magnet.

First, for the calculations throughout, we flip the sign of the current distribution from Eq. 1 such that the head of the beam is on the right.

\displaystyle\bar{I}(s)=I_{0}(1-I_{1}s+I_{2}{s}^{2}-

(32)

\displaystyle I_{3}{s}^{3}+\ldots+(-1)^{N}I_{N}{s}^{N})\quad-S_{2}<s<-S_{1}

\displaystyle\bar{I}(s)=0\quad s<-S_{2}\quad\&\quad s>-S_{1}

For case A in
saldin1997coherent
, where the observation point,
s
, is inside the magnet and the source point,
s^{\prime}
, is outside of the magnet entrance, the CSR wake kernel is given by:

w_{A}[s-s^{\prime}]=-\frac{4}{\rho^{2}\phi}\delta\bigg[\frac{s-s^{\prime}}{\rho}-\frac{\phi^{3}}{6}\bigg]

(33)

Here
\phi
is the angle of the observation point relative to the magnet entrance. The instantaneous change of energy is given by integrating over the source points:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{d\eta_{A}}{d\phi}=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\int ds^{\prime}w_{A}[s-s^{\prime}]\bar{I}[s^{\prime}]

(34)

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\frac{4}{\rho\phi}\bar{I}\bigg[s-\frac{\rho\phi^{3}}{6}\bigg]

The total energy change is given by integrating over the observation point’s path through the magnet up until the formation length of the radiation exceeds the tail of the beam.

\displaystyle\Delta\eta_{A}=\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\int_{0}^{\phi_{f}}d\phi\frac{4}{\phi}\bar{I}\bigg[s-\frac{\rho\phi^{3}}{6}\bigg]

(35)

\displaystyle\phi_{f}=\bigg[\frac{6}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg]^{1/3}

For case B in
saldin1997coherent
, where the observation point,
s
, is inside the magnet and the source point,
s^{\prime}
is inside the magnet, the CSR wake kernel is given by:

w_{B}[s-s^{\prime}]=-\frac{2}{(3\rho^{2})^{1/3}}\frac{d}{ds^{\prime}}\frac{1}{(s-s^{\prime})^{1/3}}

(36)

The instantaneous change of energy is given by integrating over the source points:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{d\eta_{B}}{d\phi}=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\int ds^{\prime}w_{B}[s-s^{\prime}]\bar{I}[s^{\prime}]

(37)

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\bigg(\frac{4}{\rho\phi}\bar{I}\bigg[s-\frac{\rho\phi^{3}}{24}\bigg]+

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(3\rho^{2})^{1/3}}\int_{s-\frac{\rho\phi^{3}}{24}}^{s}ds^{\prime}\frac{1}{(s-s^{\prime})^{1/3}}\frac{d\bar{I}[s^{\prime}]}{ds^{\prime}}\bigg)

The total energy change is given by integrating over the observation point’s path through the magnet up until the tail of the beam enters the magnet.

\displaystyle\Delta\eta_{B}=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\int_{0}^{\phi_{f}}d\phi\bigg(\frac{4}{\phi}\bar{I}\bigg[s-\frac{\rho\phi^{3}}{24}\bigg]+

(38)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(3\rho^{2})^{1/3}}\int_{s-\frac{\rho\phi^{3}}{24}}^{s}ds^{\prime}\frac{1}{(s-s^{\prime})^{1/3}}\frac{d\bar{I}[s^{\prime}]}{ds^{\prime}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\phi_{f}=\bigg[\frac{24}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg]^{1/3}

The sum of the integrals in Eq. 35 and 38 can be done explicitly, cancelling the logarithmic dependence at
\phi=0
. Rewriting the energy modulation such that the head of the beam is again on the left, the polynomial chirp coefficients associated with this entrance transient are given by:

\displaystyle H_{A+B(n)}=-\frac{e(-1)^{n}}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\times

(39)

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{4}{3}\log{(4)}(-1)^{n}\chi_{n}+\sum_{k=n}^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Xi_{n,k,i}(-1)^{k}\chi_{k}{S_{2}}^{k-n}+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n+1}^{N}\Xi_{n,k,n}(-1)^{k}\chi_{k}{S_{2}}^{k-n}\bigg)

The coefficient,
\Xi
, is independent of the beam and magnet parameters and are given by:

\Xi_{n,k,i}=\frac{8^{\frac{1}{3}}k(\frac{2}{3})^{(\overline{k-1-i})}(1-k)^{(\overline{k-1-i})}}{(k-n)!(n-i)!(\frac{5}{3})^{(\overline{k-1-i})}}

(40)

Here
x^{\overline{n}}
is the pochhammer function or rising factorial,
x^{\overline{n}}=x(x+1)(x+2)...(x+n-1)
.

Provided the beam satisfies the condition
\Phi>\bigg[\frac{24}{\rho}(S_{2}-S_{1})\bigg]^{1/3}
, the entire beam will experience a steady state CSR wake. For the case where this condition is not satisfied, some observation points in the beam will exit the magnet before the tail enters, case C in
saldin1997coherent
. In this regime, parts of the beam will experience a different CSR wake, complicating the analytical expression of the polynomial coefficients of the CSR chirp. Thus for simplicity, we assume the steady state condition is satisfied for the magnets in consideration. The CSR wake kernel for the steady state regime is given by the wake kernel for case B.

w_{SS}[s-s^{\prime}]=-\frac{2}{(3\rho^{2})^{1/3}}\frac{d}{ds^{\prime}}\frac{1}{(s-s^{\prime})^{1/3}}

(41)

The instantaneous change of energy is again given by integrating over the source points:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{d\eta_{SS}}{d\phi}=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\int ds^{\prime}w_{SS}[s-s^{\prime}]\bar{I}[s^{\prime}]

(42)

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\bigg(\frac{4}{\rho\big[\frac{24}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\big]^{1/3}}\bar{I}[-S_{2}]+

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(3\rho^{2})^{1/3}}\int_{-S_{2}}^{s}ds^{\prime}\frac{1}{(s-s^{\prime})^{1/3}}\frac{d\bar{I}[s^{\prime}]}{ds^{\prime}}\bigg)

The total energy change is given by the instantaneous energy change at the point the tail enters the magnet multiplied the distance travelled through the magnet until the the observation point exits.

\displaystyle\Delta\eta_{SS}=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\rho(\phi_{f}-\phi_{i})\bigg(\frac{4}{\rho\big[\frac{24}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\big]^{1/3}}\bar{I}[-S_{2}]+

(43)

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(3\rho^{2})^{1/3}}\int_{-S_{2}}^{s}ds^{\prime}\frac{1}{(s-s^{\prime})^{1/3}}\frac{d\bar{I}[s^{\prime}]}{ds^{\prime}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\phi_{i}=\bigg[\frac{24}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg]^{1/3}\quad\quad\phi_{f}=\Phi

Again rewriting the energy modulation such that the head of the beam is on the left, the polynomial chirp coefficients associated with the steady state regime are given by:

\displaystyle H_{SS(n)}=-\frac{e(-1)^{n}}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\times

(44)

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{N}\Lambda_{n,k}^{0}\Phi{\rho}^{\frac{1}{3}}(-1)^{k}\chi_{k}{S_{2}}^{k-n-\frac{1}{3}}+

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=m+1}^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\Lambda_{n,m,k,i}^{1}\Phi{\rho}^{\frac{1}{3}}(-1)^{k}\chi_{k}{S_{2}}^{k-n-\frac{1}{3}}-

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n+1}^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\Lambda_{n,k,i}^{2}(-1)^{k}\chi_{k}{S_{2}}^{k-n}-\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Lambda_{n,n,i}^{2}(-1)^{n}\chi_{n}\bigg)

The coefficients
\Lambda
are independent of the beam and magnet parameters and are given by:

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n,k}^{0}=\frac{(-1)^{k}4}{n!(24)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\bigg(-\frac{1}{3}\bigg)^{(\underline{n})}

(45)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n,m,k,i}^{1}=\frac{3^{\frac{2}{3}}k(\frac{2}{3})^{(\underline{n-m})}(\frac{2}{3})^{(\overline{k-1-i})}(1-k)^{(\overline{k-1-i})}}{(n-m)!(m-i)!(k-m-i)!(\frac{5}{3})^{(\overline{k-1-i})}}

\displaystyle\Lambda_{n,k,i}^{2}=\frac{6k(k-i)(\frac{2}{3})^{(\overline{k-1-i})}(1-k)^{(\overline{k-1-i})}}{(n-i)!(k-n)!(\frac{5}{3})^{(\overline{k-1-i})}}

Here
x^{\overline{n}}
is again the pochhammer function or rising factorial, and
y^{\underline{n}}
is the falling factorial
y^{\underline{n}}=y(y-1)(y-2)...(y-n+1)
.

For case D in
saldin1997coherent
, where the observation point,
s
, is outside the magnet exit and the source point,
s^{\prime}
, is inside the magnet, the CSR wake kernel is given by:

w_{D}[s-s^{\prime}]=-\frac{4}{\rho}\frac{d}{ds^{\prime}}\frac{1}{\psi[s^{\prime}]+2x}

(46)

Here
\psi
is the angle of the source point from the magnet exit and
x
is the distance of the observation point from the magnet exit. The distance between source point and observation point is related by
\psi
and
x
by:

s-s^{\prime}=\frac{\rho\psi^{3}}{24}\frac{\psi+4x}{\psi+x}

(47)

The instantaneous change of energy is given by integrating over the source points:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{d\eta_{D}}{d\phi}=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\int ds^{\prime}w_{D}[s-s^{\prime}]\bar{I}[s^{\prime}]

(48)

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\frac{4}{\rho}\bigg(\frac{\bar{I}[-S_{2}]}{\psi[-S_{2}]+2x}+

\displaystyle\int_{-S_{2}}^{s}ds^{\prime}\frac{1}{\psi[s^{\prime}]+2x}\frac{d\bar{I}[s^{\prime}]}{ds^{\prime}}\bigg)

In order for us to solve the above integral we first define the angle of the tail of the beam,
\psi_{0}
, when the observation point is at distance
x
:

s+S_{2}=\frac{\rho{\psi_{0}}^{3}}{24}\frac{\psi_{0}+4x}{\psi_{0}+x}

(49)

With the above relation
x
can be defined in terms of
\psi_{0}
.

x[\psi_{0}]=\frac{\rho{\psi_{0}}^{4}-24(s+S_{2})\psi_{0}}{24(s+S_{2})-4\rho{\psi_{0}}^{3}}

(50)

The integral giving the instantaneous change of energy can be done changing variables and integrating over the angle of the source points:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{d\eta_{D}}{d\phi}=-\frac{e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\frac{4}{\rho}\bigg(\frac{\bar{I}[-S_{2}]}{\psi_{0}+2x[\psi_{0}]}-

(51)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\psi_{0}}d\psi\frac{1}{\psi+2x[\psi_{0}]}\frac{d}{d\psi}\bar{I}\bigg[s-\frac{\rho\psi^{3}}{24}\frac{\psi+4x[\psi_{0}]}{\psi+x[\psi_{0}]}\bigg]\bigg)

The total energy change is given by integrating over the angle of the beam tail:.

\displaystyle\Delta\eta_{D}=\frac{4e}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\int_{\psi_{0}i}^{\psi_{0f}}d\psi_{0}\bigg(\frac{\bar{I}[-S_{2}]}{\psi_{0}+2x[\psi_{0}]}-

(52)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\psi_{0}}d\psi\frac{1}{\psi+2x[\psi_{0}]}\frac{d}{d\psi}\bar{I}\bigg[s-\frac{\rho\psi^{3}}{24}\frac{\psi+4x[\psi_{0}]}{\psi+x[\psi_{0}]}\bigg]\bigg)

\displaystyle\psi_{0i}=\bigg[\frac{6}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg]^{1/3}\quad\quad\psi_{0f}=\bigg[\frac{24}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg]^{1/3}

In general the above integral can not be done analytically. However, we can proceed by approximating the integral term in Eq. 51 associated with each polynomial coefficient of the current profile as being quadratic in
\psi_{0}
. Referring to these integral terms as,
F_{D(n)}
, we choose a quadratic approximation,
f_{D(n)}
:

\displaystyle F_{D(n)}[\psi_{0}]=-\int_{0}^{\psi_{0}}d\psi(-1)^{n}n\times

(53)

\displaystyle\bigg(s-\frac{\rho\psi^{3}}{24}\frac{\psi+4x[\psi_{0}]}{\psi+x[\psi_{0}]}\bigg)^{n-1}\frac{\rho\psi^{2}(\psi+2x[\psi_{0}])}{8(\psi+x[\psi_{0}])^{2}}

\displaystyle\sim f_{D(n)}[\psi_{0}]=A_{(n)}+B_{(n)}\bigg(\psi_{0}-\bigg[\frac{24}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg]^{1/3}\bigg)+

\displaystyle C_{(n)}\bigg(\psi_{0}-\bigg[\frac{24}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg]^{1/3}\bigg)^{2}

Here we choose
A_{(n)}
,
B_{(n)}
, and
C_{(n)}
such that
F_{D(n)}=f_{D(n)}
as x goes to 0,
f_{D(n)}=0
as x goes to infinity, and
F_{D(n)}=f_{D(n)}
at
{\psi_{0}}^{3}=\frac{1}{2}{\psi_{0f}}^{3}

\displaystyle A_{(n)}=F_{D(n)}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{24}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg)^{1/3}\bigg]

(54)

\displaystyle f_{D(n)}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{6}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg)^{1/3}\bigg]=0

\displaystyle f_{D(n)}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{12}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg)^{1/3}\bigg]=F_{D(n)}\bigg[\bigg(\frac{12}{\rho}(s+S_{2})\bigg)^{1/3}\bigg]

\displaystyle H_{D(n)}\sim-\frac{e(-1)^{n}}{mc^{3}4\pi\epsilon_{0}\gamma}\times

(55)

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{N-n}\sum_{m=0}^{n}(-1)^{k+n}\chi_{k+n}{S_{2}}^{k}\bigg[\Upsilon_{n,m,k}^{0}+\sum_{i=0}^{m+k-1}\Upsilon_{n,i,m,k}^{1}-

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m+k-1}\sum_{l=0}^{m+k-i-2}\Upsilon_{n,l,i,m,k}^{2}-\sum_{i=0}^{m+k-1}\sum_{l=m+k-i+1}^{3(m+k)-1}\Upsilon_{n,l,i,m,k}^{2}\bigg]\bigg)

(56)

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{n,i,m,k}^{1}=(-1)^{k+m-i}\times

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\frac{1}{3}}12^{m+k-1}4[2i(4+\log{(27)})+(k+m)(10+\log{(27)})]}{2^{6(m+k)-1}3^{i}i!m!k!}\times

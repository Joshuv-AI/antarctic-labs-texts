Zero-dimensional Shimura varieties and central derivatives of Eisenstein series
Siddarth Sankaran

Abstract
We formulate and prove a version of the arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula for (zero dimensional) Shimura varieties attached to tori, equipped with some additional data. More precisely, we define a family of “special” divisors in terms of Green functions at archimedean and non-archimedean places, and prove that their degrees coincide with the Fourier coefficients of the central derivative of an Eisenstein series. The proof relies on the usual Siegel-Weil formula to provide a direct link between both sides of the identity, and in some sense, offers a more conceptual point of view on prior results in the literature.



1
Introduction

In this paper, we formulate and prove a version of the “arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula” for zero-dimensional Shimura varieties. Broadly speaking, the arithmetic Siegel-Weil formula, which is a conjectural formula due to Kudla, predicts that the arithmetic heights of certain “special” cycles on Shimura varieties are related to the Fourier coefficients of derivatives of Eisenstein series. For zero-dimensional varieties, a number of special cases have been worked out in the literature; see for example
[
KRY99
,
How12
,
HY12
]
as well as
[
AGHMP18
]
, where an identity of this form was a crucial ingredient in their proof of the average Colmez conjecture.

Here we take a more general, abstract point of view. Our starting point is an arbitrary Shimura datum
(T,h)
, where
T
is a rational torus, and
h\colon\Res_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{G}_{m}\to T_{/{\mathbb{R}}}
is a morphism. Upon fixing a compact open subgroup
K\subset T(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, this data determines a zero-dimensional Shimura variety that admits a canonical model over the reflex field
E(T,h)
. Choose a CM field
E
containing
E(T,h)
and let
\mathcal{M}
denote the base change of the canonical model to
\Spec(E)
; then the theory of complex multiplication provides for an action of
E^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
on the geometric points
\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})
for any place
v
of
E
.

We also fix the following collection of data, which we describe in loose terms here, and refer to
Section

3
for more precise details:

•

an “incoherent family”
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{v})_{v}
whose members are one-dimensional local
E_{v}
-Hermitian spaces, one for each place
v
of
F
(here
F\subset E
is the maximal totally real subfield);

•

a family of lattices
\mathcal{L}_{v}\subset\mathcal{V}_{v}
for non-archimedean places
v
; and

•

a family of pairs
\left\{(\mathbb{V}^{(v)},\beta^{(v)})\right\}
indexed by the places of
F
that are non-split in
E
; here
\mathbb{V}^{(v)}
is a local system of one-dimensional
E
-Hermitian spaces on the base change
\mathcal{M}_{/E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}}
, and
\beta^{(v)}
is a map relating this local system to the incoherent collection
\mathcal{V}
.
In particular, at each geometric point
z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})
, the map
\beta^{(v)}
identifies the fibre of
\mathbb{V}^{(v)}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{E}
at
z
with the coherent collection that differs from
\mathcal{V}
at precisely the place
v
.

In addition,
the action of
E^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
on the geometric points of
\mathcal{M}
restricts to an action of
H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, where, for an
F
-algebra
R
, we write
H(R)=\{x\in R\otimes_{F}E\mid N_{E/F}(x)=1\}
; we further require that the data above satisfies a certain equivariance property with respect to the action of
H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}_{F})
.

With this data in place, and for a fixed
\alpha\in F^{\times}
, we construct a family of “Green functions”

g_{v}(\alpha,\tau)\colon\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})\to\mathbb{R}

(1.1)

indexed by the places
v
of
F
, and depending on an additional parameter
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{E}
, the Hilbert upper-half space attached to
E
. When
v
is an archimedean place, this coincides with the Green function defined by Kudla. The novelty of the present article is an analogous construction at non-archimedean places, which can be roughly described as a non-archimedean factor of an incomplete Mellin transform.
Packaging these Green functions together as
v
varies over all the places of
F
yields a “special cycle”

\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)\in\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathcal{M})

(1.2)

where
\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathcal{M})
is the group of isomorphism classes of adelically metrized line bundles.

On the other hand, we use the same data to define a Hilbert modular Eisenstein series
E(\tau,s,\Phi^{*})
of parallel weight one that vanishes at the centre
s=0
of its functional equation. Our main theorem relates the two constructions:

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
\alpha\in F^{\times}
, we have

\widehat{\mathrm{deg}}\,\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)=-\#\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})\cdot E^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\tau,0,\Phi^{*})

(1.3)

where
E^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\tau,0,\Phi)
is the
\alpha
’th Fourier coefficient of the central derivative

E^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{*}):=\frac{d}{ds}E(\tau,s,\Phi^{*})\big|_{s=0}.

(1.4)

The aforementioned special cases can be placed in the context of our main theorem (though the translation is not entirely trivial), and in particular we obtain new proofs of those results. It should be emphasized that our approach circumvents the explicit deformation-theoretic calculations that figure prominently in the prior literature. Instead, in these cases, the
p
-adic Green functions we construct encode arithmetic degrees in a natural way (see
Proposition

4.8
), and our main identity is proved via the Siegel-Weil formula, without requiring an explicit computation of the left hand side. In particular, this line of argument provides a more conceptual explanation for such identities. We present a few examples illustrating this point in the last section of the paper.

Preliminaries

2.1
Adelic line bundles on zero-dimensional spaces and arithmetic degrees

In this section, we give a very naive description of the theory of adelic line bundles, as introduced by Zhang, see e.g.
[
Zha95
]
: as our interest lies solely in the zero-dimensional case, we will give an ad-hoc presentation here relevant to this setting.

Suppose
X
is a zero dimensional variety over a number field

, and
\omega
is a line bundle on
X
.
For a place
v
of

, choose an algebraic close
L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}
of

. A
v-adic metric

\|\cdot\|_{v}=\{\|\cdot\|_{x}\}_{x\in X(L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})}

(2.1)

on

is a collection of (real-valued) metrics

\|\cdot\|_{x}\colon\omega_{x}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\qquad x\in X(L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})

(2.2)

on the fibres
\omega_{x}
of
\omega
at geometric points. These metrics are required to satisfy

\|\lambda s\|_{x}=|\lambda|_{v}\cdot\|s\|_{x},

(2.3)

for all
\lambda\in L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}},s\in\omega_{x}
, where
|\cdot|_{v}
is the unique extension of the norm on
L_{v}
to
L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}
.

At a non-archimedean place

, a key role is played by
model metrics
, defined as follows. Suppose
X_{v}=X\times_{L}\Spec(L_{v})
admits a projective model
\mathcal{X}
over
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{L,v})
and that
\omega
extends to a line bundle
\tilde{\omega}
over
\mathcal{X}
. Then an
L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}
valued point
z\in X(L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})
lifts uniquely to a point
\tilde{z}\in\mathcal{X}(\mathcal{O}_{L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}})
, and hence we obtain an
\mathcal{O}_{L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}}
-lattice

\widetilde{\omega}_{\tilde{z}}\subset\omega_{z}.

(2.4)

This inclusion determines a metric, called the
model metric relative to
(\mathcal{X},\tilde{\omega})
, by the formula

\|s\|_{z,(\mathcal{X},\tilde{\omega})}:=\inf\{|a|_{v}\ |\ a\in(L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})^{\times},\ a^{-1}s\in\widetilde{\omega}_{\tilde{z}}\}

(2.5)

Definition 2.1
.

An adelic line bundle
\widehat{\omega}=(\omega,(\|\cdot\|_{v})_{v})
consists of a line bundle
\omega
together with
v
-adic metrics
\|\cdot\|_{v}
for every place
v
, such that the following condition holds: there exists an integer
N
and a model
(\mathcal{X},\tilde{\omega})
over
\Spec\,\mathcal{O}_{L}[1/N]
such that the metric
\|\cdot\|_{v}
is induced by this model at every non-archimidean place induced by a prime ideal relatively prime to
N
.

Two adelic line bundles
\widehat{\omega}
and
\widehat{\omega}^{\prime}
are
isomorphic
if there is an isomorphism
\omega\simeq\omega^{\prime}
that induces an isometry
(\omega_{x},\|\cdot\|_{x})\simeq(\omega^{\prime}_{x},\|\cdot\|^{\prime}_{x})
at every geometric point
x
.

Of course, for zero dimensional schemes these definitions are overkill, as all line bundles are isomorphic to the trivial one. When
\omega=\mathcal{O}_{X}
is the trivial bundle, specifying an adelic metric
(\|\cdot\|_{v})_{v}
is equivalent to choosing a family
(g_{v})
of “Green functions”
g_{v}\colon X(L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})\to\mathbb{R}
whose members are identically zero for almost all
v
; this equivalence is encoded by the relation

\|1\|^{2}_{z}=e^{-g_{v}(z)},\qquad z\in X(L_{v}^{alg}).

(2.6)

Let
\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(X)
denote the group of isomorphism classes of adelic line bundles; as every bundle on
X
is isomorphic to the trivial one, every element of
\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(X)
is determined uniquely by a set of Green functions
g_{v}\colon X(L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})\to\mathbb{R}
as above.

Given an adelic bundle
\widehat{\omega}
, we define its arithmetic degree

\widehat{\deg}\,\widehat{\omega}=\sum_{v\leq\infty}\sum_{z\in X(L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})}-\log\|\eta\|_{z}^{2}

(2.7)

where
\eta
is a non-vanishing section of
\omega
. The product formula implies that this map is invariant on isomorphism classes, and therefore defines a function on
\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(X)
. In particular, if
\widehat{\omega}=(\mathcal{O}_{X},(g_{v})_{v})\in\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(X)
is given by a collection of Green functions as above, then

\widehat{\deg}\,\widehat{\omega}=\sum_{v\leq\infty}\sum_{z\in X(L_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})}g_{v}(z).

(2.8)

2.2
Hermitian spaces

Let
E
be a CM field, with maximal totally real subfield
F
. Suppose that
v
is a place of
F
, let
E_{v}:=E\otimes_{F}F_{v}
and suppose that
V_{v}
is a Hermitian space of dimension
m
over
E_{v}
. We define the local invariant

\mathrm{inv}_{v}(V_{v}):=((-1)^{m(m-1)/2}\det V_{v},\Delta_{E/F})_{v}\in F_{v}^{\times}/N_{E_{v}/F_{v}}(E_{v}^{\times})

(2.9)

where
\Delta_{E/F}
is the discriminant, and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{v}
is the Hilbert symbol. At a non-archimedean place, two
E_{v}
-Hermitian spaces of the same dimension are isomorphic if and only if they have the same invariants. In particular, at split places, there is only one isometry class of
E_{v}
-Hermitian space of a given dimension, while at non-split places, there are two. At archimedean places, the isometry class of a Hermitian space over
E_{v}\simeq\mathbb{C}
is determined by its signature.

Now suppose
V
is a global Hermitian space over
E
. Almost all of its local invariants are equal to one, and together they satisfy the product formula

\prod_{v\leq\infty}\mathrm{inv}_{v}(V)=1.

(2.10)

Conversely, suppose we have a collection of signs
\epsilon_{v}\in\{\pm 1\}
for
v\leq\infty
, almost all of which are 1, and a collection of pairs of non-negative integers
(r_{v},s_{v})
for
v|\infty
, with
r_{v}+s_{v}=n
independent of
v
. Then there exists a Hermitian space
V
of signature
(r_{v},s_{v})
at each
v|\infty
and
\mathrm{inv}_{v}(V)=\epsilon_{v}
if and only if
\prod_{v\leq\infty}\epsilon_{v}=1
and
\epsilon_{v}=(-1)^{s_{v}+1}
for each
v|\infty
.

Following the terminology of Kudla
[
Kud97
]
, we say that a collection

\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{v})_{v\leq\infty}

(2.11)

of local Hermitian spaces is
incoherent
if there is no global Hermitian space that is locally isometric to
\mathcal{V}_{v}
for all
v
.

(2.39)

where

3
Description of the data

In this section, we give a detailed description of the data and assumptions that go into our main theorem.

3.1
Zero dimensional Shimura varieties

Let
T
be a rational torus, and
h\colon\mathbb{S}\to(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
a homomorphism, where
\mathbb{S}=\Res_{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is Deligne’s torus.

For a neat compact open subgroup
K\subset T(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
, we have a zero dimensional Shimura variety

Sh_{K}(T,h):=T(\mathbb{Q})\big\backslash\{h\}\times T(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})/K.

(3.1)

Suppose that
E\subset\mathbb{C}
is a CM field that contains the reflex field
E(T,h)
, so that
Sh_{K}(T,h)
admits a canonical model
\mathcal{M}
over
\Spec(E)
. As per the theory of canonical models, there is an action of
E^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
on
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})=\mathcal{M}(E^{\mathrm{alg}})=Sh_{K}(T,h)
that factors through the Galois group
\Gal(E^{\mathrm{alg}}/E)^{\mathrm{ab}}
, where
E^{\mathrm{alg}}
is a fixed algebraic closure of
E
, see e.g.
[

]
for details. For any place
v
of
E
, we fix an embedding
E^{\mathrm{alg}}\subset E^{\mathrm{alg}}_{v}
, so we may view
E^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
acting on
\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})=\mathcal{M}(E^{\mathrm{alg}})
as well.

Let
F\subset E
denote the maximal totally real subfield, and consider the algebraic group
H
over
F
defined by (
2.37
); viewing
H(\mathbb{A}_{F})
and
H(F)
as a subgroups of
\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
and

, respectively, we have a natural inclusion
H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}_{F})\hookrightarrow E^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}
, and so in this way, we obtain an action of

on

.

3.2
Linear algebraic data

Let
v
be a place of
F
, and set
E_{v}:=E\otimes_{F}F_{v}
. We say that the place
v
is
non-split
if
E_{v}
is a field; in particular, the archimedean places of
F
are all non-split.

Our next piece of data is an
incoherent collection

\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{v})_{v\leq\infty}

(3.2)

indexed by the places of
F
; each member
(\mathcal{V}_{v},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{v})
is a Hermitian space over
E_{v}
, and we assume that

\mathcal{V}_{v}\text{ is positive definite for all }v|\infty.

(3.3)

For each non-archimedean place
v<\infty
, we also fix an
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-stable lattice

\mathcal{L}_{v}\subset\mathcal{V}_{v}

(3.4)

and assume that
\mathcal{L}_{v}
is self-dual for almost all
v
. Note that we are not assuming that
\mathcal{L}_{v}
is integral for all
v
.

For each non-archimedean place
v
, we define the Schwartz function

\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v}\in S(\mathcal{V}_{v})

(3.5)

to be the characteristic function of
\mathcal{L}_{v}
. At an archimedean place
v
, we set

\varphi_{\mathcal{V},v}(x)=e^{-\pi\langle x,x\rangle_{v}}.

(3.6)

For convenience, we set

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathcal{L},f}

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{v<\infty}\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v},

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathcal{V},{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{v|\infty}\varphi_{\mathcal{V},v},

\displaystyle\varphi_{\mathcal{L},\mathbb{A}}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{\mathcal{V},\infty}\otimes\varphi_{\mathcal{L},f}.

(3.7)

Next, let
v
be a non-split place of
F
. Define an element
\varsigma_{v}\in F_{v}^{\times}
as follows: if
v
is non-archimedean and inert, let
\varsigma_{v}=\varpi
, where
\varpi
is a uniformizer of
F_{v}
. If
v
is ramified, choose
\varsigma_{v}\in\mathcal{O}_{F,v}^{\times}
such that
\varsigma_{v}\notin N(E^{\times}_{v})
. If
v
is archimedean, we take
\varsigma_{v}=-1
.

In any of the above cases, let
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}
denote the
E_{v}
-Hermitian space whose underlying space is
\mathcal{V}_{v}
, with Hermitian form given by

\langle x,y\rangle^{\prime}_{v}:=\varsigma_{v}\cdot\langle x,y\rangle_{v}

(3.8)

By construction,
\mathcal{V}_{v}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}
are representatives for the two distinct isomorphism classes of
E_{v}
Hermitian spaces of dimension 1.

Finally, we note that as
\mathcal{V}_{v}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}
share the same underlying vector space, we view
\mathcal{L}_{v}
as a lattice in either space; in particular, we may view
\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v}
as a Schwartz function for
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}
as well.

3.3
Local systems

For each non-split place
v
of
F
, fix an algebraically closed extension

of

, and set

\mathcal{M}_{/E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}}:=\mathcal{M}\times_{E}\Spec(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}).

(3.9)

Our final piece of data is a family of pairs

\left\{(\mathbb{V}^{(v)},\,\beta^{(v)})\ |\ v\text{ non-split }\right\}

(3.10)

whose entries are as follows:

(a)

\mathbb{V}^{(v)}
is a local system of one-dimensional
E
-Hermitian spaces over
\mathcal{M}_{/E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}}
equipped with an
H(\mathbb{A}_{F})
action lifting the action on
\mathcal{M}_{/E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}}
.

(b)

Let

\mathcal{V}^{v}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}=\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\leq\infty\\
w\neq v\end{subarray}}\mathcal{V}_{w}\right)\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}

(3.11)

which we view as a Hermitian
\mathbb{A}_{E}
-module, and denote by
\underline{\mathcal{V}^{v}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}
the constant local system on

\mathcal{V}^{v}\times V^{\prime}_{v}

The datum

is an isometry

\beta^{(v)}\colon\,\mathbb{V}^{(v)}\otimes_{E}\mathbb{A}_{E}\to\underline{\mathcal{V}^{v}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}

(3.12)

of Hermitian
\mathbb{A}_{E}
-local systems, for which we require the following equivariance property to hold: given

and

, we have an isometry

(3.13)

arising from part (a) of the definition, and we require that the diagram

\begin{CD}\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\otimes_{E}\mathbb{A}_{E}@>{\beta^{(v)}_{z}}>{}>\mathcal{V}^{v}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}\\
@V{r(t)\otimes 1}V{}V@V{}V{\times t}V\\
\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{t\cdot z}\otimes_{E}\mathbb{A}_{E}@>{\beta^{(v)}_{t\cdot z}}>{}>\mathcal{V}^{v}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}\end{CD}

commutes up to multiplication by an element of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}{}^{\times}_{E}
. In other words, for any

\beta_{z}^{(v)}\circ\left(r(t^{-1})\otimes t\right)\circ\left(\beta^{(v)}_{t\cdot z}\right)^{-1}\ \in\ \End\left({\mathcal{V}^{v}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}\right)

(3.15)

is given by multiplication by an element of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}{}^{\times}_{E}
.

4
Eisenstein series and Green functions

We fix a collection of data

\left(\mathcal{M}\to\Spec(E),\,\,\mathcal{V},\,(\mathcal{L}_{v}),\,\left(\mathbb{V}^{(v)},\beta^{(v)}\right)_{v}\right)

(4.1)

as described in
Section

3
. In the following three subsections, we show how this data defines a section
\Phi^{*}(g,s)\in I(s,\chi)
, a collection of “special cycles”
\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)\in\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathcal{M}_{K})
, and then state and prove our main theorem.

4.1
The section
\Phi^{*}(s)

We begin with the non-archimedean setting. Fix a non-archimedean place
v
of
F
that is non-split in
E
, and let
E_{v}=E\otimes_{F}F_{v}
, which is a field. Then
\mathcal{V}_{v}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}
are the two non-isomorphic one dimensional Hermitian spaces over
E_{v}
.

Suppose for the moment that
\mathcal{V}_{v}
represents
1
, i.e. there exists a vector
x\in\mathcal{V}_{v}
such that
\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}=1
. Then there are isometries

\mathcal{V}_{v}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}_{v}^{+}:=(E_{v},x\overline{y})\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}^{-}_{v}:=(E_{v},\varsigma_{v}x\overline{y})

(4.2)

where for
a\in F_{v}^{\times}
, the space
(E_{v},ax\overline{y})
denotes the Hermitian space with underlying space
E_{v}
and Hermitian form
\langle x,y\rangle=ax\overline{y}
.

If
\mathcal{V}_{v}
does not represent 1, then
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}
does, and so there are isometries

\mathcal{V}_{v}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}^{-}_{v},\qquad\mathcal{V}_{v}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}^{+}_{v}.

(4.3)

In either case, these isomorphisms carry the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{v}\subset\mathcal{V}_{v}=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}
to some lattice
\Lambda\subset E_{v}
. It follows easily from definitions that

\Phi_{v}(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L}_{v}}))=\Phi_{v}(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\epsilon}_{v}}(\varphi_{\Lambda})).

(4.4)

where

\epsilon=\chi_{v}(\det\mathcal{V}_{v})=\pm 1.

(4.5)

Similarly, we have

\Phi_{v}\left(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L}_{v}})\right)=\Phi_{v}\left(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{-\epsilon}_{v}}(\varphi_{\Lambda})\right)

(4.6)

Now let
\varphi_{v}^{0}(x)
denote the characteristic function of
\mathcal{O}_{E,v}
. Write
\Lambda=t\cdot\mathcal{O}_{E,v}
for some
t\in E_{v}^{\times}
, so that

\varphi_{\Lambda}(x)=\varphi_{v}^{0}(t^{-1}x)=|t|_{v}\,\chi(t)\left(\omega_{\mathcal{V}^{\pm}_{v}}\left(m(t^{-1})\right)\varphi_{v}^{0}\right)(x).

(4.7)

This in turn implies that the difference

\delta_{v}(g,s):=\Phi_{v}(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L}}))-|t|^{1-s}_{v}\chi(t)\Phi_{v}\left(gm(t)^{-1},s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\epsilon}_{v}}(\varphi^{0}_{v})\right)

(4.8)

vanishes identically at
s=0
.

Remark 4.1
.

If
v
corresponds to a prime of
F
that is unramified in
E
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{v}=\mathcal{O}_{E,v}
is self-dual, then we may take
t=1
above, and
\delta_{v}(g,s)\equiv 0
. In particular,
\delta_{v}(g,s)=0
for almost all
v
. In this case,
\Phi_{v}(g,s,\lambda_{V_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L}}))
equals
\Phi_{v}(g,s,\lambda_{V^{+}_{v}}(\varphi^{0}_{v}))
, which is the standard spherical section.

Next, recall that

I_{v}(0,\chi)=R(\mathcal{V}_{v})\oplus R(\mathcal{V}_{v}^{\prime}),

(4.9)

so there are Schwartz functions
\phi_{v},\phi_{v}^{\prime}\in S(E_{v})=S(\mathcal{V}_{v})=S(\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v})
such that

\delta_{v}(g,s)=s\cdot\left(\Phi\left(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}(\phi_{v})\right)+\Phi\left(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}(\phi^{\prime}_{v})\right)\right)+O(s^{2}).

(4.10)

Definition 4.2
.

We define the modified section
\Phi_{v}^{*}(g,s)\in I_{v}(g,s)
by the formula

\Phi_{v}^{*}(g,s)\ :=\ \Phi_{v}(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v}))-s\cdot\left(\Phi\left(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}(\phi_{v})\right)+\Phi\left(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}(\phi^{\prime}_{v})\right)\right).

(4.11)

Note that in general, the value at
s=0
is given by

\Phi_{v}^{*}(g,0)=\Phi_{v}(g,0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v})).

(4.12)

Moreover, for almost all
v
, we have that
\Phi_{v}^{*}(g,s)
is the standard spherical section, as per
Remark

4.1
.

For future use, we will gather some information about the Whittaker functionals
W_{m,v}(g,s,\Phi_{v}^{*})
near
s=0
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose
\Phi(g,s)\in I_{v}(s,\chi)
is an arbitrary section,
m\in F_{v}^{\times}
and
t\in E_{v}^{\times}
. Then

W_{m,v}\left(m(t)^{-1},s,\Phi\right)=|t|^{s-1}_{v}\chi(\overline{t})W_{N(t)^{-1}m,v}(e,s,\Phi).

(4.13)

where
N(t)=N_{E_{v}/F_{v}}(t)
.

Proof.

By definition, we have

W_{m,v}\left(m(t)^{-1},s,\Phi\right)=\int_{b\in F_{v}}\Phi\left(wn(b)m(t)^{-1},s\right)\,\psi_{p}(-mb)\,db.

(4.14)

A straightforward computation shows that

w\,n(b)\,m(t^{-1})\ =\ m({\overline{t}})\,w\,n(bN(t)).

(4.15)

so

\Phi\left(wn(b)m(t)^{-1},s\right)=\Phi\left(m(\bar{t})\,w\,n(bN(t)),s\right)=|t|_{v}^{s+1}\chi(\bar{t})\Phi\left(w\,n(bN(t)),s\right).

(4.16)

Substituting this equation into (
4.14
) yields

\begin{split}W_{m,v}\left(m(t)^{-1},s,\Phi\right)&=|t|_{v}^{s+1}\chi(\bar{t})\int\Phi\left(wn(N(t)b),s\right)\,\psi_{p}(-mb)\,db\\
&=|t|_{v}^{s+1}\chi(\bar{t})\int\Phi\left(wn(b),s\right)\psi_{p}(-mb/N(t))\,\frac{db}{|t|^{-2}_{v}}\\
&=|t|_{v}^{s-1}\chi(\bar{t})W_{N(t)^{-1}m,v}(e,s,\Phi)\end{split}

(4.17)

where in the second line we applied the change of variables
b\mapsto N(t)^{-1}b
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose
v
is a non-split non-archimedean place of

(i)

W_{m,v}(e,0,\Phi_{v}^{*})=W_{m,v}(e,0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v}))

(ii)

If

(iii)

where
\mathfrak{p}_{E}

\varpi

Proof.

Part
(i)

4.12
). Part
(ii)

.

For the third part, using (

W_{m,v}\left(e,0,\lambda_{V^{\prime}_{v}}(\varphi_{L})\right)=|t|_{v}\chi(t)W_{m,v}\left(m(t)^{-1},0,\lambda_{V^{-\epsilon}_{v}}(\varphi^{0}_{v})\right)

\begin{split}W_{m,v}\left(e,0,\lambda_{V^{\prime}_{v}}(\varphi_{L})\right)&=\chi(t\overline{t})W_{N(t)^{-1}m,v}\left(e,0,\lambda_{V^{-\epsilon}_{v}}(\varphi^{0}_{v})\right)\\

On the other hand, applying

The archimedean analogue is as follows. For later purposes, it will be convenient to introduce an additional parameter

Proposition 4.5
.

Suppose

, and let

\Phi^{+}_{v}(g,s)=\Phi(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}(\varphi_{v}))\qquad{\text{and}}\qquad\Phi^{-}_{v}(g,s)=\Phi(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}(\varphi_{v})).

(4.23)

where
\varphi_{v}(x)=e^{-\pi\langle x,x\rangle_{\mathcal{V}_{v}}}

Recall here that
\mathcal{V}_{v}
and
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}
have signatures

and

For

\mathcal{W}_{m,v}(y_{v},s,\Phi^{\pm}):=y_{v}^{-1/2}W_{m,v}(m(\sqrt{y_{v}}),s,\Phi^{\pm}_{v})\cdot e^{2\pi y_{v}m_{v}}.

where

with

denoting the real embedding corresponding to the place
v
.

Suppose that
m_{v}<0
. Then for

, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{W}_{m,v}^{\prime}(ty_{v},0,\Phi^{+})\ =\ t^{-1}\mathcal{W}_{m,v}(ty_{v},0,\Phi^{-})

(4.25)

\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}_{m,v}(ty,0,\Phi^{+})=0.

Proof.

At this stage, we have defined a local section
\Phi^{*}_{v}(s)

.

\Phi^{*}(s):=\otimes\Phi_{v}^{*}(s)\in I(s,\chi).

(4.27)

Remark 4.7
.

Let
\Sigma^{\mathrm{bad}}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
denote the set of non-archimedean places of

For each
v_{i}\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{bad}}

, so there exists an

-Hermitian space

A moment’s contemplation of the definition of the local section

\begin{split}\Phi^{*}(g,s)&=\Phi\left(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}}\left(\varphi_{L}\right)\right)+s\left[\sum_{v_{i}\in\Sigma^{\mathrm{bad}}}\Phi(g,s,\lambda_{W_{i}}(\varphi_{i}))+\Phi(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}}(\varphi_{i}^{\prime}))\right]\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+O(s^{2})\end{split}

E(g,s,\Phi^{*})=E\left(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}}\left(\varphi_{L}\right)\right)+s\left[\sum_{v_{i}}E(g,s,\lambda_{W_{i}}(\varphi_{i}))+E(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}}(\varphi_{i}^{\prime}))\right]+O(s^{2})

(4.31)

of coherent Eisenstein series attached to totally positive definite spaces.

\diamond

4.2
Green functions and special cycles

We now turn our attention to the construction of a family of elements
\widehat{Z}(m,\tau)\in\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathcal{M})
.

We begin with a local construction. Let
v
be a non-archimedean place of
F
that is non-split in
E
, and recall that we had fixed a local system
\mathbb{V}^{(v)}
on
\mathcal{M}_{/E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}}
of one-dimensional Hermitian spaces over
E
, as in
Section

3
.

As part of our data, there is an isometry

\beta^{(v)}_{z}\colon\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}^{v}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v},

(4.32)

at each point
z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})
. Pulling back the Schwartz function
\varphi_{\mathcal{L},\mathbb{A}}
along this map, we obtain a Schwartz function

\varphi_{z}=\bigotimes_{w\leq\infty}\varphi_{z,w}\in S(\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{E})

(4.33)

on the adelification of the fibre of
\mathbb{V}^{(v)}
at
z
.

For
z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})
and
x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}
, we define a function

g(x,z):=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in E_{v}^{\times}\\
|\lambda|_{v}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\varphi_{z,v}(\lambda x)\,d^{\times}\lambda\cdot\log N_{E/{\mathbb{Q}}}(\mathfrak{p})

(4.34)

where
d^{\times}\lambda
is the multiplicative Haar measure on
E_{v}^{\times}
such that the volume of
\mathcal{O}_{E,v}^{\times}
with respect to
d^{\times}\lambda
is one, and
\mathfrak{p}
is the prime ideal of
E
corresponding to the place
v
.

There are two main motivations for this construction. The first is that it strongly parallels Kudla’s construction in the archimedean place, cf. (
4.46
) below. The more important point is that
g(x,z)
naturally encodes the Arakelov degrees of divisors locally on
\mathcal{M}
.

We make the second point more precise. Suppose the point
z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})
is defined over a finite extension
L
of
E_{v}
, and admits an integral extension
\widetilde{z}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{O}_{L})
. Suppose further that there is a line bundle
\tilde{\omega}
over
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and a map

s\colon\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\otimes_{E_{v}}L\to\tilde{\omega}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}L;

(4.35)

we may normalize
s
to have the property that

s(x)\in\pi\,\widetilde{\omega}\iff\varphi_{z,v}(x)=1

(4.36)

for every
x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\otimes_{E}E_{v}
, where
\pi
is a uniformizer of
L
.

For any
x
with
s(x)\in\widetilde{\omega}
, its vanishing locus
Z(s(x))
defines a divisor on
\Spec\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Let
I=(f)
denote the (principal) ideal that cuts out
Z(s(x))
; in other words,
I
is the largest ideal such that the image of
s(x)
in
\widetilde{\omega}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{L}}\mathcal{O}_{L}/I
vanishes. We define the normalized
Arakelov degree
of the divisor
Z(s(x))
to be

\widehat{\deg}\,Z(s(x)):=\frac{1}{[L:E_{v}]}\mathrm{length}(\mathcal{O}_{L}/I)\,\log|\kappa(L)|=\frac{1}{[L:E_{v}]}\ord_{L}(f)\,\log|\kappa(L)|

(4.37)

where
\kappa(L)
is the residue field of
L
. Note that the Arakelov degree is unchanged if
L
is replaced by a larger field.

Proposition 4.8
.

With the notation as in the previous paragraph, we have

g(x,z)=\deghat Z(s(x))

(4.38)

for all
x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}
with
s(x)\in\widetilde{\omega}
.

Proof.

If
\pi
is a uniformizer of
L
, and
I=(f)
is the ideal defining
Z(s(x))
, then
\ord_{L}(f)
is, by construction, the largest integer such that
\pi^{-\ord_{L}(f)}s(x)\in\widetilde{\omega}
.

On the other hand, suppose
\varpi
is a uniformizer of
E_{v}
such that
\varpi=\pi^{e}
, with
e=e(L/E_{v})
the ramification index. Then, using the normalization (
4.36
), we may write

\begin{split}g(x,z)&=\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in E_{v}^{\times}\\
|\lambda|_{v}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\varphi_{z,v}(\lambda x)\,d^{\times}\lambda\cdot\log N(\mathfrak{p})\\
&=\sum_{m\geq 0}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{E,v}^{\times}}\varphi_{z,v}(\varpi^{-m}ux)d^{\times}u\cdot\log N(\mathfrak{p})\\
&=\max\left(m\ |\ \varpi^{-m}s_{x}\in\widetilde{\omega}\right)\cdot\log N(\mathfrak{p})\\
&=(\ord_{\pi}(f)/e)\cdot\log N(\mathfrak{p})\\
&=\frac{\ord_{\pi}(f)}{[L:E_{v}]}\cdot\log|\kappa(L)|\end{split}

(4.39)

where in the last line, we used the identity
[L:E_{v}]=e(L/E_{v})\cdot f(L/E_{v})
. This last expression is
\widehat{\deg}\,Z(s(x))
, by definition.
∎

In practice, one often has a global line bundle
\omega
, for which the vanishing locus of a section represents a moduli or deformation-theoretic problem; some examples of this phenomenon are presented in
Section

5
.

We record another crucial property of this construction:

Lemma 4.9
.

Suppose

and

. For every

, there is an isometry

such that

\varphi_{z}(x)=\varphi_{t\cdot z}(t^{-1}r(t)x)

(4.40)

Proof.

This follows immediately from the equivariance property of
\beta^{(v)}
, as in (
3.14
).
∎

Finally, for
\alpha\in F^{\times}
, we define a function

on

by the formula

\begin{split}g_{v}(\alpha)(z)&:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\\
Q(x)=\alpha\end{subarray}}\varphi^{v}(x)\cdot g_{v}(x,z)\\
&=\log N(\mathfrak{p})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\\
Q(x)=\alpha\end{subarray}}\varphi^{v}_{z}(x)\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in E_{v}\\
|\lambda|_{v}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\varphi_{z,v}(\lambda x)\,d^{\times}\lambda\end{split}

(4.41)

where

.

For later comparison to the Fourier expansion of a Hilbert modular form, it will be convenient to introduce an additional parameter

, where

is the Hermitian upper half space attached to

where

(4.47)

Lemma 4.10
.

For a given

, there is at most one place

Suppose

.

4.3
Proof of the main theorem

We are now ready to formulate our main theorem.

Let
E(g,s,\Phi^{*})
denote the Eisenstein series attached to the section
\Phi^{*}(g,s)
as defined in
Section

4.1
. It will be convenient to express
E(g,s,\Phi^{*})
in “classical” coordinates, as follows: let
\tau\in\mathbb{H}_{E}
, and set

g_{\tau}=(g_{\tau,\infty},1,\dots)\in G(\mathbb{A}_{F})

(4.50)

where
g_{\tau,\infty}
is as in (
4.44
). We then define

E(\tau,s,\Phi^{*}):=N(y)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,E(g_{\tau},s,\Phi^{*}),

(4.51)

where

N(y)=\prod_{v|\infty}y_{v};

(4.52)

then
E(\tau,s,\Phi^{*})
is a (non-holomorphic) Hilbert modular form of parallel weight one.

Write the Fourier expansion of
E(\tau,s,\Phi^{*})
as

E(\tau,s,\Phi^{*})=\sum_{\alpha\in F}E_{\alpha}(\tau,s,\Phi^{*}).

(4.53)

Theorem 4.12
.

For all
\alpha\in F^{\times}
, we have

\widehat{\deg}\,\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)=-\#\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})\cdot E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{*}).

(4.54)

Proof.

By the product formula (
2.34
), we have

E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{*})=N(y)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\sum_{v\leq\infty}W^{\prime}_{\alpha,v}(g_{\tau,v},0,\Phi^{*}_{v})\prod_{w\neq v}W_{\alpha,w}(g_{\tau,w},0,\Phi^{*}_{w}).

(4.55)

For
\alpha\in F^{\times}
, let

\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha):=\{v\leq\infty\mid\alpha\text{ is not represented by }\mathcal{V}_{v}\}.

(4.56)

We observe that
\#\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)\geq 1
. Indeed, if
\mathcal{V}_{v}
represents
\alpha
, then
\mathcal{V}_{v}\simeq(E_{v},\alpha xy^{\prime})
. If
\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)=\emptyset
, then this would hold true for all places, and so

\prod_{v\leq\infty}\mathrm{inv}_{v}(\mathcal{V}_{v})=1,

(4.57)

which contradicts the assumption that
\mathcal{V}
is an incoherent collection.

If
\#\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)\geq 2
, then the vanishing criterion in
Proposition

2.2
implies that
E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\tau,0,\Phi^{*})=0
. On the other hand, every space
\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}
differs from
\mathcal{V}
at exactly one place, so no such space can represent
\alpha
; it follows that
\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)=0
in this case is well.

Thus, it remains to consider the case
\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)=\{v\}
is a singleton. Note that
v
is necessarily non-split, so that
E_{v}=E\otimes_{v}F_{v}
is a field.

First, suppose
v
is non-archimedean, and let
\mathfrak{p}
denote the prime ideal of
E
inducing the valuation
v
of
E
. Then

N(y)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,E^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\tau,0,\Phi^{*})=W^{\prime}_{\alpha,v}(e,0,\Phi^{*}_{v})\cdot W_{\alpha,\infty}(g_{\tau,\infty},0,\Phi^{*}_{\infty})\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}w<\infty\\
w\neq v\end{subarray}}W_{\alpha,w}(e,0,\Phi^{*}_{w})

(4.58)

where

W_{\alpha,\infty}(g_{\tau,\infty},0,\Phi^{*}_{\infty})=\prod_{w|\infty}W_{\alpha,v}(g_{\tau,w},0,\Phi^{*}_{w}).

(4.59)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}N(y)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\widehat{\deg}\,\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)&=N(y)^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})}g_{v}(\alpha,\tau)(z)\\
&=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\\
Q(x)=\alpha\end{subarray}}\left(\omega_{\mathbb{V}^{(v)}_{z}}(g_{\tau,\infty})\varphi_{z,\infty}(x)\right)\varphi_{z,f}^{v}(x)\\
&\hskip 86.72377pt\times\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in E_{v}^{\times}\\
|\lambda|_{v}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\varphi_{z,v}(\lambda x)\,d^{\times}\lambda\,\log N(\mathfrak{p})\end{split}

(4.60)

with notation as in (
4.45
). Next, for
\lambda\in E_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
, let

\underline{\lambda}=(1,\dots,1,\underbrace{\lambda}_{\mathclap{\mathfrak{p}\text{'th component}}},1,\dots)\in\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}

(4.61)

and write

g=g_{\tau}\,m(\underline{\lambda})\in G(\mathbb{A}_{F})

(4.62)

so that

\deghat\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)=N(y)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\log N(\mathfrak{p})\\
\times\int_{|\lambda|_{v}\geq 1}|\lambda|_{v}^{-1}\chi(\lambda)^{-1}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\\
Q(x)=\alpha\end{subarray}}(\omega_{\mathbb{V}^{(v)}_{z}}(g)\varphi_{z})(x)\,d^{\times}\lambda.

(4.63)

We compute the double sum in the integrand using the Siegel-Weil formula, as follows.

Choose a subgroup
K_{H}\subset H(\mathbb{A}_{F})
of finite index in
H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}_{F})
such that
\varphi_{\mathcal{L},\mathbb{A}}
is
K_{H}
-stable, where
\varphi_{\mathcal{L},\mathbb{A}}
is the Schwartz function (
3.7
). Assume furthermore that the action of
H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}_{F})
on
\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})=\mathcal{M}(E^{\mathrm{alg}})
factors through the class group

\mathcal{C}:=H(F)\backslash H(\mathbb{A}_{F})/K_{H}.

(4.64)

Fix a set of representatives

z_{1},\dots,z_{r}

(4.65)

for the orbits of the action of
\mathcal{C}
on
\mathcal{M}(E^{\mathrm{alg}})
, and for each
i
, let
c_{i}
denote the order of the stabilizer of
z_{i}
with respect to this action.

Then, for any
g\in G(\mathbb{A}_{F})
, and
i=1,\dots r
, we have

\begin{split}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z}\\
Q(x)=\alpha\end{subarray}}(\omega_{\mathbb{V}^{v}_{z}}(g)\varphi_{z})(x)&=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{c_{i}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{t\cdot z_{i}}\\
Q(x)=\alpha\end{subarray}}(\omega_{\mathbb{V}^{v}_{t\cdot z_{i}}}(g)\varphi_{t\cdot z_{i}})(x)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{c_{i}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{z_{i}}\\
Q(x)=\alpha\end{subarray}}(\omega_{\mathbb{V}^{v}_{z_{i}}}(g)\varphi_{z_{i}})(t^{-1}x)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{1}{c_{i}}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}}\Theta_{\alpha}(g,t,\varphi_{z_{i}})\end{split}

(4.66)

where the second line follows from
Lemma

4.9
, and
\Theta_{\alpha}(g,t,\varphi_{z_{i}})
is the
\alpha
’th Fourier coefficient of the theta function defined in (
2.38
).

Now by the Siegel-Weil formula (
Proposition

2.3
), the product formula (
2.34
), and applying the definition of the Schwartz function
\varphi_{z_{i}}
, cf. (
4.33
), we can write

\begin{split}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{C}}\Theta_{\alpha}(g,t,\varphi_{z_{i}})&=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{H},dh)}\int_{[H]}\Theta_{\alpha}(g,h,\varphi_{z_{i}})dh\\
&=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{vol}(K_{H})}E_{\alpha}(g,0,\lambda_{\mathbb{V}^{v}_{z_{i}}}(\varphi_{z_{i}}))\\
&=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{vol}(K_{H})}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\leq\infty\\
w\neq v\end{subarray}}W_{\alpha,w}(g_{w},0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{w}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},w}))\cdot W_{\alpha,v}(g_{v},0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}\left(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v})\right).\end{split}

(4.67)

Note that this is independent of
i
. Moreover, we observe that

\#\mathcal{M}(E^{\mathrm{alg}})=\#\mathcal{C}\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}^{-1}=\left(\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{H})}\int_{[H]}dh\right)\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(K_{H})}\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{i}^{-1};

(4.68)

thus, setting

C:=\frac{\#\mathcal{M}(E^{\mathrm{alg}})}{2}

(4.69)

and taking
g=g_{\tau,\infty}\,m(\underline{\lambda})
, we have

\widehat{\deg}\,\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)=C\cdot N(y)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\cdot W_{\alpha,\infty}(g_{\tau,\infty},0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{\infty}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},\infty}))\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}w<\infty\\
w\neq v\end{subarray}}W_{\alpha,w}(g_{w},0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{w}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},w}))\\
\times\log N(\mathfrak{p})\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in E_{v}^{\times}\\
|\lambda|_{v}\geq 1\end{subarray}}|\lambda|_{v}^{-1}\chi(\lambda)^{-1}W_{\alpha,v}(m(\lambda),0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v}))\,d^{\times}\lambda.

(4.70)

Now applying
Lemma

4.3
, we may rewrite the second line above as

\begin{split}\log N(\mathfrak{p})\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in E_{v}^{\times}\\
|\lambda|_{v}\geq 1\end{subarray}}|\lambda|_{v}^{-1}\chi(\lambda)^{-1}&W_{\alpha,v}(m(\lambda),0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v}))\,d^{\times}\lambda\\
&=\log N(\mathfrak{p})\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in E_{v}^{\times}\\
|\lambda|_{v}\geq 1\end{subarray}}W_{N(\lambda)\alpha,v}(e,0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v}))\,d^{\times}\lambda.\end{split}

(4.71)

Note that since
\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v}
is the characteristic function of a lattice,

W_{u\alpha,v}(e,0,\Phi_{v})=W_{\alpha,v}(e,0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v}))

(4.72)

for all
u\in\mathcal{O}_{F,v}^{\times}
. Fixing a uniformizer
\varpi
of
E_{\mathfrak{p}}
and applying
Proposition

4.4
(iii), we have

\begin{split}\eqref{eqn:whittaker mellin integral}&=\log N(\mathfrak{p})\sum_{k\geq 0}W_{N(\varpi)^{-k}\alpha,v}\left(e,0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{v}^{\prime}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},v})\right)\\
&=-2\,W_{\alpha,v}^{\prime}(e,0,\Phi^{*}_{v}).\end{split}

(4.73)

As for the remaining places,
Proposition

4.4
(i) gives

W_{\alpha,w}(e,0,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}_{w}}(\varphi_{\mathcal{L},w}))=W_{\alpha,w}(e,0,\Phi^{*}_{w})

(4.74)

for non-archimedean places

, and, following definitions, we have

(4.75)

for

.

The case that

∎

Remark 4.13
.

The theorem above readily generalizes to the situation where the compact open subgroup
K\subset T(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q},f})
is not neat. Indeed, fix a neat subgroup
K_{0}\subset K
, let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
denote the corresponding zero-dimensional Shimura variety, and let
\mathcal{M}=[\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{M}_{0}]
denote the stack quotient, where
\Gamma=K/K_{0}
. Let
j\colon\mathcal{M}_{0}\to\mathcal{M}
denote the natural map.

Suppose
\widehat{\omega}
is an adelic line bundle on
\mathcal{M}
, given by a family of Green functions
(g_{v})_{v}
as in
Section

2.1
. If we define its arithmetic degree on
\mathcal{M}
to be

\deghat\,\widehat{\omega}=\sum_{v\leq\infty}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})}\frac{1}{\#\Aut(z)}g_{v}(z),

(4.76)

then one may verify that

\frac{\deghat j^{*}\widehat{\omega}}{\#\mathcal{M}_{0}(\mathbb{C})}=\frac{\deghat\widehat{\omega}}{\#\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})}.

(4.77)

where the left hand side is the “stacky” cardinality.
This in turn implies that the main theorem continues to hold in this case.

Alternatively, one can take the definition of the arithmetic degree above, and make the minor changes required in the proof of
Theorem

4.12
directly.
\diamond

5
Examples

5.1
The weight one Eisenstein series of Kudla-Rapoport-Yang

Suppose that
E\subset\mathbb{C}
is an imaginary quadratic field, and let
\mathcal{O}_{E}\subset E
denote the ring of integers.
Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the moduli stack over
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{E})
whose
S
-points, for a connected
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-scheme
S
, parametrizes pairs
(A,i)
where
A
is an elliptic curve over
S
, and
i\colon\mathcal{O}_{E}\to\End(A)
is an action of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
such that the induced action on
\Lie(A)
coincides with the structural morphism
\mathcal{O}_{E}\to\mathcal{O}_{S}
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is a zero-dimensional Shimura variety with

\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\left[E^{\times}\big\backslash\mathbb{A}_{E,f}^{\times}/\widehat{\mathcal{O}_{E}}{}^{\times}\right].

(5.1)

In
[
KRY99
]
, the authors assume that
E=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})
for a prime
p
, construct a family of special cycles on
\mathcal{M}
via a moduli problem, and prove that their arithmetic degrees coincide with the Fourier coefficients of an incoherent weight one Eisenstein series. In this section, we explain how their results can be viewed in the context of the present work.

To begin, we fix our incoherent collection
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{v})_{v}
as follows. If
v
corresponds to a rational prime
p
, we set
\mathcal{V}_{v}=(E\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{p},-2x\overline{y})
, while at the infinite place, we set
\mathcal{V}_{\infty}=(\mathbb{C},2x\overline{y})
; here we view
\mathbb{C}\simeq E\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{R}
via the fixed embedding
E\to\mathbb{C}
.
As for the lattice
\mathcal{L}
, we set
\mathcal{L}_{p}=\mathcal{O}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for each prime
p
.

Next, let
p
denote a rational prime that is non-split in
E
, and take a geometric point
z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{p}^{\mathrm{alg}})
corresponding to an elliptic curve
A_{z}
with its
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-action. Let
A_{\overline{z}}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathrm{alg}})
denote the reduction mod
p
, which is equipped with an induced
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-action
\iota\colon\mathcal{O}_{E}\to\End(A_{\overline{z}})
, and set

\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{z}:=\{x\in\End^{0}(A_{\overline{z}})\mid\iota(a)\circ x=x\circ\iota(\bar{a})\ \text{for all }a\in\mathcal{O}\};

(5.2)

this is the space of “special quasi-endomorphisms” of
A_{\overline{z}}
as in
[
KRY99
]
.

Since
p
does not split in
E
, the elliptic curve
A_{\overline{z}}
is necessarily supersingular, and so
\End^{0}(A_{\overline{z}})
is a rational quaternion algebra that is non-split precisely at
\infty
and
p
. Since
\End^{0}(A_{\overline{z}})
contains an embedded copy of
E
, there is a decomposition

\End^{0}(A_{\overline{z}})=\iota(E)\oplus\iota(E)\theta

(5.3)

where
\theta
is an element satisfying
\theta\iota(a)=\iota(\overline{a})\theta
for all
a\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
. In particular,
\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{z}=\iota(E)\theta
is a one-dimensional vector space over
E
. We equip
\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{z}
with the E-Hermitian form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{z}
such that
\frac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{z}=\mathrm{Nrd}(x)=-x^{2}
; here
\mathrm{Nrd}(x)
is the reduced norm.

We need to show that as
z
varies, these spaces form an
H(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
-equivariant local system, where
H(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})=\{t\in\mathbb{A}_{E}^{\times}\mid N(t)=1\}
. This is best seen via Serre’s tensor construction as follows. For
t\in H(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, let
\mathfrak{a}=t\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E}\cap E
denote the corresponding fractional ideal. Then we may identify
A_{t\cdot z}\simeq A_{z}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\mathfrak{a}
, and we obtain a natural
E
-linear quasi-isogeny

\varphi_{t}\colon A_{z}\to A_{z}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{E}}\mathfrak{a}\simeq A_{t\cdot z},\qquad a\mapsto a\otimes 1.

(5.4)

This induces the desired isometry

r(t)\colon\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{t\cdot z}\to\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{z},\qquad x\mapsto\varphi_{t}\circ x\circ\varphi_{t}^{-1}

(5.5)

of
E
-Hermitian spaces, where, abusing notation, we use the same symbol
\varphi_{t}
to denote the induced quasi-isogeny between the special fibres of
A_{z}
and
A_{t\cdot z}
.

Now let
z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{p}^{\mathrm{alg}})
as above, and let

\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}:=\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{z}\cap\End(A_{\overline{z}}).

(5.6)

At a prime
q\neq p
, we may identify the Tate module
\mathrm{Ta}_{q}(A_{\overline{z}})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{E,q}
, and so we obtain isomorphisms

\End(A_{\overline{z}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{q}\simeq\End(\mathrm{Ta}_{q}(A_{\overline{z}}))\simeq\End(\mathcal{O}_{E,q}).

(5.7)

The space of
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-antilinear endomorphisms of
\mathcal{O}_{E,q}
is generated, as an
\mathcal{O}_{E,q}
-module, by the endomorphism
a\mapsto\bar{a}
; pulling this back through the above identifications, we obtain a generator
f\in\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
with
f^{2}=-1
. This choice of basis vector induces an isometry
\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}\simeq\mathcal{L}_{q}
, or put differently, an isometry

\beta^{(p)}_{z,q}\colon\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathbb{Q}_{q}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}_{q}

(5.8)

identifying
\mathbb{L}^{(p)}_{z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{q}
with
\mathcal{L}_{q}
.

At the prime
p
, note that we may choose the element
\theta
such that its image in
\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a generator, so that

\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}\simeq(\mathcal{O}_{E,p},2\mathrm{Nrd}(\theta)\,x\overline{y})

(5.9)

On the other hand, because
\End^{0}(A_{\overline{z}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a division algebra,
\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{z,p}
cannot represent
-2
, so
2\mathrm{Nrd}(\theta)\equiv-2\varsigma_{p}\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times}/N(E_{p}^{\times})
, where
\varsigma_{p}
is the element chosen in
Section

3.2
. Moreover,
\End(A_{\overline{z}})\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the unique maximal order, consisting precisely of those endomorphisms whose reduced norms have non-negative
p
-adic valuation. Hence
\ord_{p}(\mathrm{Nrd}(\theta))=0
or
1
, depending on whether
p
is ramified or inert, respectively, and we find
\mathrm{Nrd}(\theta)=-N(u)\varsigma_{p}
for some
u\in\mathcal{O}_{E,p}^{\times}
. To summarize, we have an isometry

\beta^{(p)}_{z,q}\colon\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{z}\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{q}

(5.10)

identifying
\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
\mathcal{L}_{p}
.
Finally, at the archimedean place, we have that both
\mathbb{V}^{(p)}_{z,\infty}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\infty}
are positive definite one dimensional Hermitian spaces, and so we may fix an isometry
\beta^{(p)}_{z,\infty}
between them.

Putting these local factors together, we obtain an isometry

\beta^{(p)}|_{z}\colon\mathbb{V}^{(p)}\otimes_{E}\mathbb{A}_{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}^{p}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{p}

(5.11)

which at the non-archimedean places identifies
\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}\otimes\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
with
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}
; note that this latter condition determines the finite part of
\beta^{(p)}_{z}
up to multiplication by an element of
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E}^{\times}
of norm one. One can then check that for
t\in H(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
, the corresponding maps
\beta^{(p)}_{z}
and
\beta^{(p)}_{t\cdot z}
satisfy the required equivariance property (
3.14
).

Now consider the archimedean place
v=\infty
. For a point
z\in\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})=\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})
, the homology group
H_{1}(A_{z}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})
has an induced
\mathcal{O}_{E}
action, and we define

\mathbb{L}^{(\infty)}|_{z}=\{x\in\End\left(H_{1}(A_{z}(\mathbb{C}),\mathbb{Z})\right)\ |\ x\circ\iota(a)=\iota(\overline{a})\circ x\}

(5.12)

and

\mathbb{V}^{(\infty)}|_{z}=\mathbb{L}^{(\infty)}|_{z}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q},

(5.13)

equipped with the
E
-hermitian form determined by
\frac{1}{2}\langle x,x\rangle_{z}=-x^{2}
. A similar argument to the non-archimedean case reveals that there is an isometry

\beta^{(\infty)}\colon\mathbb{V}^{(\infty)}|_{z}\otimes_{E}\mathbb{A}_{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}^{\infty}\times\mathcal{V}_{\infty}^{\prime}

(5.14)

satisfying the equivariance (
3.14
).

At this point, we have specified the data
(\mathcal{V},\widehat{\mathcal{L}},(\mathbb{V}^{(v)},\beta^{(v)}))
as set out in
Section

3
.
Carrying out the construction in
Section

4.2
, we obtain a family of Green functions
g_{v}(\alpha,\tau)\colon\mathcal{M}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})\to\mathbb{R}
, and consequently a family of special cycles
\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)\in\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathcal{M})
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
and
\tau\in\mathbb{H}
.

On the other hand, in
[
KRY99
]
, the authors define a family of special cycles
\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)
via a moduli problem. When
\alpha>0
, the link between the two constructions is given by
Proposition

4.8
. To make this more precise, recall that
\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)
is defined by the following moduli problem: for a scheme
S
over
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{E})
, the
S
-points parametrize tuples
(A,i,x)
where
(A,i)
is a point of
\mathcal{M}(S)
and
x\in\End_{S}(A)
is an endomorphism satisfying
x\circ i(a)=i(\overline{a})\circ x
for all
a\in\mathcal{O}_{E}
and
-x^{2}=\alpha
.

Now suppose we have a point
\overline{z}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathrm{alg}})
, corresponding to a pair
(A_{\overline{z}},i_{\overline{z}})
. By Gross’ theory of canonical liftings, this pair admits a canonical lift to characteristic zero; more precisely, choosing a sufficiently large finite extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, we have a (canonical) lift to a pair
(A_{\tilde{z}},i_{\tilde{z}})
over
\mathcal{O}_{L}
.
The algebraic de Rham homology group
H_{1}^{dR}(A_{\tilde{z}})
is a free
\mathcal{O}_{L}
module of rank 2, equipped with a Hodge filtration

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\Fil^{1}(A_{\tilde{z}})@>{}>{}>H_{1}^{dR}(A_{\tilde{z}})@>{}>{}>\Lie(A_{\tilde{z}})@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

(5.15)

Furthermore, we may canonically identify
H_{1}^{dR}(A_{\tilde{z}})
as the value of the Grothendieck-Messing crystal of
A_{\overline{z}}
at
\mathcal{O}_{L}
; in particular, any element
x\in\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}
induces an endomorphism
\tilde{x}
of
H_{1}^{dR}(A_{\tilde{z}})
.
Consider the line bundle

\widetilde{\omega}=\Hom(\Fil^{1}(A_{\tilde{z}}),\Lie(A_{\tilde{z}})).

(5.16)

Given
x\in\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}
, we obtain a section
s(x)\in\widetilde{\omega}
by assigning to
x
the composition

s(x)\colon\Fil^{1}(A_{\tilde{z}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{x}}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{dR}_{1}(A_{\tilde{z}})\to\Lie(A_{\tilde{z}})

(5.17)

where the second arrow is the natural projection.
By Grothendieck-Messing theory, the deformation locus of
x
is precisely the vanishing locus of
s(x)
. In particular, since
x\in\mathbb{L}^{(p)}|_{z}
is an endomorphism of
A_{\overline{z}}
, we have
s(x)\in\pi\widetilde{\omega}
, where
\pi
is a uniformizer of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. Tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
, we obtain a map
s=s_{z}\colon\mathbb{V}^{(p)}|_{z}\to\tilde{\omega}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
satisfying the compatibility condition (
4.36
).

Now suppose
\overline{y}\in\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathrm{alg}})
lies above the point
\overline{z}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\mathrm{alg}})
so that
\overline{y}
corresponding to a tuple
(A_{\overline{z}},i_{\overline{z}},x)
with
x^{2}=-\alpha
. In light of the above observations,
Proposition

4.8
implies that

g_{v}(x,z)=\deghat Z(s(x))=\log\#(\mathcal{O}_{\mathscr{Z}(\alpha),\overline{y}})

(5.18)

and it follows that for
\alpha>0
, we have

\deghat\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)=\deghat\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)\,q^{\tau}.

(5.19)

When
\alpha<0
, a direct computation shows that the above identity continues to hold, where
\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)=\mathscr{Z}(\alpha,\tau)
is defined in
[
KRY99
, §6]
.

Thus, our main theorem
Theorem

4.12
specializes to the following identity, which is due to Kudla, Rapoport and Yang in the case that the discriminant of
E
is prime, and generalized to arbitrary discriminant by Kudla and Yang.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
KRY99
,
KY13
]
)
.

For any
\alpha\neq 0
, we have

\deghat\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)\,q^{\alpha}=-\#\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{C})\cdot E^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\tau,0,\Phi^{*}).

∎

5.2
The Bruinier-Kudla-Yang formula

In this section, we revisit some aspects of a paper by Andreatta, Goren, Howard, and Madapusi Pera
[
AGHMP18
]
in which they prove an averaged version of Colmez’ conjecture; at the heart of their proof is a formula (originally conjectured by Bruiner, Kudla, and Yang) for the arithmetic intersection number of a special divisor and a “big CM cycle” on an orthogonal Shimura variety. Our aim is to highlight the relevant parts of their setup in the context of our discussion.

Suppose that
E
is a CM field with
[E:\mathbb{Q}]=2d
and let
F\subset E
the maximal totally real subfield.
We define a zero-dimensional Shimura variety as follows; note that our notation here will differ slightly from previous sections, in order to align more closely with
loc. cit
. Consider the tori

T_{E}:=\Res_{E/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{G}_{m}),\qquad T_{F}:=\Res_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{G}_{m}),

(5.20)

and let

T:=T_{E}/\ker(N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\colon T_{F}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}).

(5.21)

We have
T_{E}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\prod_{\sigma\colon E\to\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}_{\sigma})^{\times}
, where
\mathbb{C}_{\sigma}:=E\otimes_{E,\sigma}\mathbb{C}
. Choose an embedding
\sigma_{1}\colon E\to\mathbb{C}
, and let
h_{E}\colon\mathbb{S}\to T_{E,\mathbb{C}}
denote the homomorphism given on
\mathbb{C}
-points by the formula
h_{E}(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1},z_{2},1,\dots,1)
where
z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}_{\sigma_{1}}
and
z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}_{\overline{\sigma}_{1}}
, and all other components are one; we then define
h\colon\mathbb{S}\to T_{\mathbb{R}}
to be the composition of
h_{E}
with the projection to
T_{\mathbb{R}}
.
The reflex field of the pair
(T,h)
is the field
\sigma_{1}(E)\subset\mathbb{C}
.

Given a compact open subgroup
K\subset T(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, the Shimura datum
(T,h)
described above gives rise to a zero-dimensional Shimura variety
Y_{K}
defined over
E
, with

Y_{K}(\mathbb{C}_{\sigma_{1}})=T(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\{h\}\times T(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K.

(5.22)

Our next step is to specify the requisite data, as in
Section

3
. Let
\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{d}\colon F\to\mathbb{R}
denote the set of real embeddings.
Fix an element
\xi\in F^{\times}
such that
\tau_{1}(\xi)<0
and
\tau_{i}(\xi)>0
for
i=2,\dots,d
.

We define our incoherent family
\mathcal{V}=(\mathcal{V}_{v})_{v}
as follows: if
v
corresponds to the embedding
\tau_{1}
, we set
\mathcal{V}_{v}=(E_{v},-\xi x\overline{y})
, while at every other place
v
, we set
\mathcal{V}_{v}=(E_{v},\xi x\overline{y})
.
In addition, we fix an
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{E}
-stable,
K
-invariant lattice
\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\subset\prod_{v<\infty}\mathcal{V}_{v}
.

Next, we need to construct the family of local systems
\{(\mathbb{V}^{(v)},\beta^{(v)})\}
, which will require a little bit of preparation. Let
(V,Q)
denote the rational quadratic space of signature
(2d-2,2)
whose underlying space is
V=E
with quadratic form
Q(x)=tr_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\xi x\overline{x})
. As explained in
[
AGHMP18
, §5]
, this data gives rise to an embedding

j_{/\mathbb{Q}}\colon Y_{K}\to\mathcal{M}

(5.23)

where
\mathcal{M}
is (the base change to
\Spec(\mathcal{O}_{E})
of) an integral model of the
\GSpin
Shimura variety associated to
V
; note that this model depends on the choice of a maximal lattice
\widehat{L}^{\max}\subset V
containing
\mathscr{L}
, which we suppress from the notation. This extends to a morphism

j\colon\mathcal{Y}_{K}\to\mathcal{M}

(5.24)

where
\mathcal{Y}_{K}
is the normalization of
Y_{K}
in
\mathcal{M}
.

The variety
\mathcal{M}
comes with an abelian scheme
\mathcal{A}^{KS}\to\mathcal{M}
, called the Kuga-Satake abelian scheme, which is of relative dimension
2^{d-1}
. Pulling back along
j
, we obtain an abelian scheme

\mathcal{A}:=j^{*}\mathcal{A}^{KS}

(5.25)

over
\mathcal{Y}_{K}
. For a connected
\mathcal{Y}_{K}
-scheme
S
, let

L(\mathcal{A}_{S})\subset\End(\mathcal{A}_{S})

(5.26)

denote the space of
special endomorphisms
, as defined in
[
AGHMP18
, §4.3, §4.5]
The precise definition is quite technical, so we content ourselves here with a summary of the relevant properties, beginning with the fact that
L(\mathcal{A}_{S})
admits the structure of an
\mathcal{O}_{E}
-module, together with a positive definite Hermitian form.

It will be convenient to work with “good” places, as in
[
AGHMP18
, Definition 5.3.3]
. Let
v
denote a non-split, non-archimedean place of
F
. We say that
v
is good if the level structure
K
is maximal at
v
and either
(a)
the extension
E_{v}/F_{v}
is unramified, the lattice
\mathcal{L}_{v}
is self-dual, and
L^{\max}_{v}=\mathcal{L}_{v}\subset E_{v}
; or
(b)
if
E_{v}/F_{v}
is ramified and
\mathcal{L}_{v}\subset L^{\max}_{v}\subsetneq\mathfrak{d}_{E_{v}/F_{v}}^{-1}\mathcal{L}_{v}
. Note all but finitely many places are good.

Now suppose
v
is a good (non-archimedean) place, and let
y\in\mathcal{Y}_{K}(E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})
denote a geometric point; by construction, there is unique lift
\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{Y}_{K}(\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}})
, and a reduction
\overline{y}\in\mathcal{Y}_{K}(\kappa(v)^{\mathrm{alg}})
, where
\kappa(v)^{\mathrm{alg}}
is the residue field of
E_{v}
. We define the local system
\mathbb{V}^{(v)}
by setting

\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{y}:=L(\mathcal{A}_{\overline{y}})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}.

(5.27)

By
[
AGHMP18
, Corollary 5.4.6, Proposition 7.6.2]
, this space is one-dimensional over
E
, and there is an isometry

\beta^{(v)}_{y}\colon\mathbb{V}^{(v)}|_{y}\otimes_{E}\mathbb{A}_{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{V}^{v}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}

(5.28)

that identifies
L(\mathcal{A}_{\overline{y}})_{w}
with
L^{\max}_{w}
at all non-archimedean places
w
. In addition,
[
AGHMP18
, Lemma 7.6.3]
implies that the map
\beta^{(v)}\colon\mathbb{V}^{(v)}\otimes\mathbb{A}_{E}\to\underline{\mathcal{V}^{v}\times\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{v}}
of local systems is
H(\mathbb{A}_{F})
-equivariant in the sense of
eq.

3.14
.

Applying the construction in
Section

4.2
, we obtain a Green function
g_{v}(\alpha,\tau)
for each good non-archimedean place
v
, and consequently a special cycle
\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)\in\widehat{\Pic}_{\mathbb{A}}(Y_{K})
for
\alpha\in F^{\times}
for any
\alpha
with
\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)=\{v\}
for some good place
v
, cf.
eq.

4.56
.

As with the previous example, the cycles in
[
AGHMP18
]
are defined in terms of a moduli problem, and are related to the cycles
\widehat{Z}(\alpha,\tau)
via
Proposition

4.8
. To make this connection precise, for
\alpha\in F^{\times}
, consider the moduli problem
\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)
over
\mathcal{Y}_{K}
whose points, for a connected
\mathcal{Y}_{K}
-scheme
S
, are given by

\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)(S)=\left\{x\in L(\mathcal{A}_{S})\ |\ \langle x,x\rangle=\alpha\right\}.

(5.29)

One has that
\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)=0
if
\alpha
is not totally positive or
\#\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)\geq 2
, and that when
\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)=\{v\}
, the moduli problem
\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)
defines a zero-cycle that is contained in the special fibre of
\mathcal{Y}_{K}
at
v
, see
[
AGHMP18
, §7.6]
for details.

To relate this moduli problem to the Green functions constructed in the present paper, we first observe that the space
V
(viewed as a representation
T
) together with the choice of lattice
L^{\max}
, gives rise to a filtered vector bundle
(V_{dR},\Fil^{\bullet}(V_{dR}))
over
\mathcal{Y}_{K}
, and moreover

\omega:=\Fil^{1}(V_{dR})^{\vee}

(5.30)

is a line bundle; see
[
AGHMP18
, Proposition 3.8.1]
. Now suppose
\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)=\{v\}
is a good place and let
\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{Y}_{K}(\mathcal{O}_{E_{v}^{\mathrm{alg}}})
with special fibre
\overline{y}\in\mathcal{Y}_{K}(\kappa(v)^{\mathrm{alg}})
. Each special quasi-endomorphism
x\in L(\mathcal{A}_{\overline{y}})\otimes\mathbb{Q}
gives rise to an element
s(x)\in\omega_{y}
. Roughly speaking,
x
induces an quasi-endomorphism
x[p^{\infty}]
of the
p
-divisible group attached to
\mathcal{A}_{\overline{y}}
, which, via Grothendieck-Messing theory, induces in turn a quasi-endomorphism
\xi=\xi(x)
of the algebraic de Rham homology group
H_{1}^{dR}(\mathcal{A}_{y})
of the lift to characteristic zero. By virtue of being special,
\xi
lands in a distinguished quadratic subspace of quasi-endomorphisms that is identified with
V_{dR,y}
; the section
s(x)
is obtained by pairing elements of
\Fil^{1}V_{dR,y}
against
\xi
. All this is explained in more detail in
[
AGHMP18
, §4.3]
, see also
[
MP16
]
.

Finally, by
[
AGHMP18
, Proposition 4.3.2]
, if
x\in L(A_{\overline{y}})
then the locus on which
x
deforms to a special endomorphism of
\mathcal{A}_{\tilde{y}}
is precisely the vanishing locus
Z(s(x))
of
s(x)
; in particular, applying
Proposition

4.8
and unwinding definitions, we have

\widehat{\deg}Z(\alpha,\tau)=\deghat\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)\,q^{\alpha}

(5.31)

for any totally positive
\alpha\in F^{\times}
such that
\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)=\{v\}
for a good place
v
.

On the automorphic side, we need to modify the construction of our Eisenstein series slightly to account for the bad places. Define a section
\widetilde{\Phi}(g,s)=\otimes_{v\leq\infty}\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}(g,s)\in I(s,\chi)
as follows:

•

If
v
is archimedean, we set
\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}(g,s)=\Phi^{*}_{v}(g,s)
as in
Section

4.1
.

•

If
v
is a non-archimedean place lying over the rational prime
p
, let
\varphi_{L^{\max},v}
denote the characteristic function of
L^{\max}_{p}\cap\mathcal{V}_{v}
, and let
\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}(g,s)=\Phi(g,s,\lambda_{\mathcal{V}}(\varphi_{L^{\max},v}))
denote the corresponding section.

Note that for good places
v
, we have that
\widetilde{\Phi}_{v}(g,s)=\Phi^{*}_{v}(g,s)
, where
\Phi^{*}_{v}(g,s)
is defined as in
Section

4.1
.

Then, after a mild modification of the proof of
Theorem

4.12
to account for the bad places, we have the following result, which appears as Theorem 7.8.1 in
[
AGHMP18
]
, and is the main ingredient in the proof of the Bruinier-Kudla-Yang formula; to reiterate the essential point, our proof of this result does not require the explicit computation of the left hand side, and relies instead on the Siegel-Weil formula to establish the link to the right hand side.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\alpha\in F^{\times}
, and suppose that
\mathrm{Diff}(\alpha)=\{v\}
for a good non-archimedean place
v
. Then

\deghat\mathscr{Z}(\alpha)q^{\alpha}=-\deg_{\mathbb{C}}(Y_{K})\,E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(\tau,0,\widetilde{\Phi}).

(5.32)

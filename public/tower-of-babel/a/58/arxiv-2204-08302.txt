Minimality conditions equivalent to the finitude of Fermat and Mersenne primes
Menachem Shlossberg

Abstract
. It is still open whether there exist infinitely many Fermat primes or infinitely many composite Fermat numbers. The same question concerning the Mersenne numbers is also unsolved. Extending some results from [ 9 ] , we characterize the Fermat primes and the Mersenne primes in terms of topological minimality of some matrix groups. This is done by showing, among other things, that if 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} is a subfield of a local field of characteristic ≠ 2 \neq 2 , then the special upper triangular group ST + ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) \operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}) is minimal precisely when the special linear group SL ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) \operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}) is. We provide criteria for the minimality (and total minimality) of SL ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) \operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}) and ST + ⁡ ( n , 𝔽 ) , \operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}), where 𝔽 {\mathbb{F}} is a subfield of ℂ . {\mathbb{C}}. Let ℱ π \mathcal{F}_{\pi} and ℱ c \mathcal{F}_{c} be the set of Fermat primes and the set of composite Fermat numbers, respectively. As our main result, we prove that the following conditions are equivalent for 𝒜 ∈ { ℱ π , ℱ c } : \mathcal{A}\in\{\mathcal{F}_{\pi},\mathcal{F}_{c}\}: • 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is finite; • ∏ F n ∈ 𝒜 SL ⁡ ( F n − 1 , ℚ ⁡ ( i ) ) \prod_{F_{n}\in\mathcal{A}}\operatorname{SL}(F_{n}-1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i)) is minimal, where ℚ ⁡ ( i ) {\mathbb{Q}}(i) is the Gaussian rational field; • ∏ F n ∈ 𝒜 ST + ⁡ ( F n − 1 , ℚ ⁡ ( i ) ) \prod_{F_{n}\in\mathcal{A}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(F_{n}-1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i)) is minimal. Similarly, denote by ℳ π \mathcal{M}_{\pi} and ℳ c \mathcal{M}_{c} the set of Mersenne primes and the set of composite Mersenne numbers, respectively, and let ℬ ∈ { ℳ π , ℳ c } . \mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{M}_{\pi},\mathcal{M}_{c}\}. Then the following conditions are equivalent: • ℬ \mathcal{B} is finite; • ∏ M p ∈ ℬ SL ⁡ ( M p + 1 , ℚ ⁡ ( i ) ) \prod_{M_{p}\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname{SL}(M_{p}+1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i)) is minimal; • ∏ M p ∈ ℬ ST + ⁡ ( M p + 1 , ℚ ⁡ ( i ) ) \prod_{M_{p}\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(M_{p}+1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i)) is minimal.

1.
Introduction

A Fermat number has the form
F_{n}=2^{2^{n}}+1,
where
n
is a non-negative integer while a Mersenne number has the form
M_{p}=2^{p}-1
for some prime
p.
Note that
2^{n}-1
is composite when
n
is composite. In other words, a
Mersenne prime is a prime number that is one less than a power of two.
There are several open problems concerning these numbers (e.g., see
[
6
]
). For example, it is still unknown whether the Fermat primes, the composite Fermat numbers, the Mersenne primes or the composite Mersenne numbers are infinitely many.

All topological groups in this paper are Hausdorff. Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a topological subfield of a local field. Recall that a
local field
is a non-discrete locally compact topological field.
Denote by
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
the special linear group over
{\mathbb{F}}
of degree
n
equipped with the pointwise topology
inherited from
{\mathbb{F}}^{n^{2}},
and by
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
its topological subgroup consisting of upper triangular matrices.
Megrelishvili and the author characterized the Fermat primes in terms of the topological minimality of some special linear groups. Recall that a
topological group
G
is
minimal

[
5
,
12
]
if
every continuous isomorphism
f\colon G\to H
, with
H

a topological group, is a topological isomorphism (equivalently, if
G
does not admit a strictly coarser group topology).

Theorem 1.1
.

[
9
, Theorem 5.5]

For an odd prime
p
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

p
is a Fermat prime;

(2)

\operatorname{SL}(p-1,({\mathbb{Q}},\tau_{p}))
is minimal, where
({\mathbb{Q}},\tau_{p})
is the field of rationals equipped with the
p
-adic topology;

(3)

\operatorname{SL}(p-1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
is minimal, where
{\mathbb{Q}}(i)\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is the Gaussian rational field.

A similar characterization for the Mersenne primes is provided in Theorem
3.1
.
Note that it follows from Gauss–Wantzel Theorem that an odd prime
p
is a Fermat prime if and only if a
p
-sided regular polygon can be constructed with compass and straightedge.

We prove in Theorem
2.7
that if
{\mathbb{F}}
is a subfield of a local field of characteristic distinct than
2
, then the special upper triangular group
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal if and only if the special linear group
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal. This result with some other tools yield criteria for the minimality (and total minimality) of
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
and
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}),
where
{\mathbb{F}}
is a subfield of
{\mathbb{C}}
(see Proposition
2.12
, Remark
2.13
and Corollary
2.14
).

As a main result, we prove in Theorem
3.6
that the finitude of Fermat and Mersenne primes as well as the finitude of composite Fermat and Mersenne numbers is equivalent to the minimality of some topological products of some matrix groups.

2.
Minimality of
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
and
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})

Let
\mathsf{N}:=\operatorname{UT}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
and
\mathsf{A}
be the subgroups of
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
consisting of upper unitriangular matrices and diagonal matrices, respectively. Note that
\mathsf{N}
is normal in
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
and
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})\cong\mathsf{N}\rtimes_{\alpha}\mathsf{A}
, where
\alpha
is the action by conjugations. It is known that
\mathsf{N}
is the derived subgroup of
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}).

Recall also that
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
has finite center (e.g., see
[
11
, 3.2.6]
)

Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))=\{\lambda I_{n}:\lambda\in\mu_{n}\},

where
\mu_{n}
is a finite group consisting of the
n
-th roots of unity in
{\mathbb{F}}
and
I_{n}
is the identity matrix of size
n.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a field and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
Then

Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))=Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))
and
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})/Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))
is center-free.

Proof.

Let
(C,D)\in Z(\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{A})
and
E\in\mathsf{A}
such that

e_{ii}\neq e_{jj}
whenever
i\neq j.
For every
i>j
it holds that

(CE)_{ij}=\sum_{t=1}^{n}c_{it}e_{tj}=c_{ij}e_{jj}

and

(EC)_{ij}=\sum_{t=1}^{n}e_{it}c_{tj}=c_{ij}e_{ii}

Then, the equality
(C,D)(I,E)=(I,E)(C,D)
implies that
CE=EC
and
c_{ij}e_{jj}=c_{ij}e_{ii}
. As
e_{ii}\neq e_{jj}
we deduce that
c_{ij}=0.
Since

C
is an upper unitriangular matrix it follows that
C=I.
To prove that
Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))=Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))
it suffices to show that the diagonal matrix
D
is scalar. To this aim, pick distinct indices
i,j
and a matrix
F\in\mathsf{N}
such that
f_{ij}\neq 0.
As
(I,D)\in Z(\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{A}),
it follows that
(I,D)(F,I)=(F,I)(I,D).
This implies that

DF=FD
and, in particular,
(DF)_{ij}=(FD)_{ij}.
This yields the equality
d_{ii}f_{ij}=d_{jj}f_{ij}
since
D
is diagonal. We conclude that
d_{ii}=d_{jj}
in view of the inequality
f_{ij}\neq 0.
This proves that
Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))=Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})).

Now, let
(B,D)Z(\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{A})\in Z(\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{A}/Z(\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{A}))
and
(C,E)\in\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{A}.
By what we proved, there exists a scalar
\lambda\in{\mathbb{F}}
such that
(B,D)(C,E)=(C,E)(B,D)(I,\lambda I).

Therefore,
DE=\lambda DE
and
\lambda=1.
This means that
(B,D)(C,E)=(C,E)(B,D)
for every
(C,E)\in\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{A}.
Therefore,
(\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{A})/Z(\mathsf{N}\rtimes\mathsf{A})
and its isomorphic copy
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})/Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))
are center-free.
∎

The following lemma will be useful in proving Theorem
2.7
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a subfield of a field
H
and let
n\geq 3
be a natural number. If
L
is a normal subgroup of
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,H)
that intersects
\operatorname{UT}(n,H)
non-trivially, then it intersects
\operatorname{UT}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
non-trivially.

Proof.

Since
L\cap\operatorname{UT}(n,H)
is a non-trivial normal subgroup of the nilpotent group
\operatorname{UT}(n,H)
it must non-trivially intersects the center
Z(\operatorname{UT}(n,H)).
Then there exists

I\neq B=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}1&0&\ldots&0&b\\
0&1&\ddots&\vdots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\vdots&\ldots&0&1&0\\
0&\ldots&\ldots&0&1\end{array}\right)\in L\cap Z(\operatorname{UT}(n,H))

for some
b\in H
(see
[
14
, p. 94]
for example). Since
n\geq 3
there exists a diagonal matrix
D\in\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,H)
such that
d_{11}=b^{-1}
and
d_{nn}=1
. This implies that

I\neq DBD^{-1}\in Z(\operatorname{UT}(n,{\mathbb{F}})).\qed

Definition 2.3
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of a topological group
G
. Then
H
is essential in
G
if
H\cap L\neq\{e\}

for every non-trivial closed normal subgroup
L
of
G
.

The following minimality criterion of dense subgroups is well-known (for compact
G
see also
[
10
,
12
]
).

Fact 2.4
.

[
2
, Minimality Criterion]

Let
H
be a dense subgroup of a topological group
G.
Then
H
is minimal if and only if
G
is minimal and
H
is essential in
G
.

Remark 2.5
.

If
{\mathbb{F}}
is a subfield of a local field
P,
then its
completion
\widehat{\mathbb{F}}
is a topological field that can be identified with the closure of
{\mathbb{F}}
in
P
. In case
{\mathbb{F}}
is infinite then
\widehat{\mathbb{F}}
is also a local field, as
the local field
P
contains no infinite discrete subfields (see
[
7
, p. 27]
).

Proposition 2.6
.

[
9
, Proposition 5.1]

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a
subfield of a local field.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal;

(2)

any non-trivial central subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
intersects
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
non-trivially (i.e., if
1\neq\lambda\in\mu_{n}(\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
, then there exists
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
1\neq\lambda^{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}
).

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a subfield of a local field of characteristic distinct than
2.
Then,
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal if and only if
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
{\mathbb{F}}
is infinite. Suppose first that
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal. By Lemma
2.1
,
Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))=Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))
. Since this center is finite it follows from the Minimality Criterion that any non-trivial central subgroup of
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
intersects
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
non-trivially. This implies that any non-trivial central subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
intersects
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
non-trivially. By Proposition
2.6
,
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal.

Conversely, let us assume that
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal. In case
n=2
then
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal by
[
9
, Theorem 3.4]
as an infinite subfield of a local field is locally retrobounded and non-discrete. So, we may assume that
n\geq 3.
By
[
9
, Theorem 3.19]
,
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
is minimal as
\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}}
is a local field (see Remark
2.5
). In view of the Minimality Criterion, it suffices to show that
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is essential in
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
. Let
L
be closed normal non-trivial subgroup of
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
. If

L\subseteq Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}}))=Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})),

then
L
intersects
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
non-trivially by Proposition
2.6
. Clearly, this implies that
L
intersects
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
non-trivially. If
L
is not central, then it must non-trivially intersect
\operatorname{UT}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}}),
the derived subgroup of
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
, in view of
[
15
, Lemma 2.3]
. Now, Lemma
2.2
implies that
L
intersects
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
non-trivially and we deduce that
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is essential in
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
.
∎

The following concept has a key role in the Total Minimality Criterion.

Definition 2.9
.

A subgroup
H
of a topological group
G
is totally dense if for every closed normal subgroup

L
of
G
the intersection
L\cap H
is dense in
L.

Fact 2.10
.

[
3
, Total Minimality Criterion]

Let
H
be a dense subgroup of a topological group
G
. Then
H
is
totally minimal if and only if
G
is totally minimal and
H
is totally dense in
G
.

Theorem 2.11
.

[
9
, Theorem 4.7]

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a subfield of a local field.
Then
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is totally minimal if and only if

Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))=Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}}))
(i.e.,
\mu_{n}({\mathbb{F}})=\mu_{n}(\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}})
).

Let
\rho_{m}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{m}}
be the
m
-th primitive root of unity. The next result extends
[
9
, Corollary 5.3]
, where
\rho_{4}=i
is considered.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a dense subfield of
{\mathbb{C}}.
Then,

(1)

\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is totally minimal if and only if
\rho_{n}\in{\mathbb{F}};

(2)

\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal if and only if
\langle\rho_{m}\rangle\cap{\mathbb{F}}
is non-trivial whenever
m
divides
n.

Proof.

(1) Necessity: follows from Theorem
2.11
. Indeed,
\lambda=\rho_{n}\in{\mathbb{C}}
is an
n
-th root of unity.

Sufficiency: if
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
\lambda^{n}=1
,
then
\lambda\in\langle\rho_{n}\rangle\subseteq{\mathbb{F}}.
So, we may use Theorem
2.11
again.

(2) Necessity: Let
1\neq\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
be an
n
-th root of unity. Then
\lambda
is an
m
-th primitive root of unity where
m
divides
n.
Since
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal it follows that there exists
k
such that

1\neq\lambda^{k}\in{\mathbb{F}}.
Clearly,
\lambda^{k}\in\langle\rho_{m}\rangle\cap{\mathbb{F}}.
So,
\langle\rho_{m}\rangle\cap{\mathbb{F}}
is non-trivial. Now use Proposition
2.6
.

Sufficiency: Let
1\neq\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}
be an
n
-th root of unity. Then
\lambda
is an
m
-th primitive root of unity where
m
divides
n.
This means that

\lambda=e^{\frac{2\pi ik}{m}}=(\rho_{m})^{k}
, where
1\leq k\leq m
with
\gcd(k,m)=1.

By our assumption,
\langle\rho_{m}\rangle\cap{\mathbb{F}}
is non-trivial. Hence, there exists
l
such that
1\neq(\rho_{m})^{l}\in\langle\rho_{m}\rangle\cap{\mathbb{F}}.
Since
\gcd(k,m)=1
and
\lambda=(\rho_{m})^{k}
, it follows that there exists
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
(\rho_{m})^{l}=\lambda^{t}.
This proves the minimality of
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}),
in view of Proposition
2.6
.
∎

Proof of the main result

By

,
\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))

where

Theorem 3.1
.

For a prime
p
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

p
is a Mersenne prime;

(2)

\operatorname{SL}(p+1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))

(3)

\operatorname{ST^{+}}(p+1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))

Theorem 3.2
.

(1)

If
{\mathbb{F}}
is a local
field, then
\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal.

(2)

If, in addition,
\mathrm{char}({\mathbb{F}})\neq 2,
then
\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal.

Proof.

(1) Since a compact group is minimal we may assume without loss of generality that
{\mathbb{F}}
is infinite. By
[
1
]
(see also
[
9
, Theorem 4.3]
), the projective special linear group
\operatorname{PSL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})=\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})/Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))
(equipped with the quotient topology) is minimal for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}.
Being algebraically simple (see
[
11
, 3.2.9]
),
\operatorname{PSL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
has trivial center. Therefore, the topological product
\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{PSL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal by
[
8
, Theorem 1.15]
. As

\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{PSL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})\cong\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})/Z\Big(\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})\Big),

where

is compact, it follows from
[
4
, Theorem 7.3.1]
that

\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal.

(2) By Lemma
2.1
,
the center of
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})/Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))
is trivial for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, where
Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))=Z(\operatorname{SL}(n,{\mathbb{F}})).

By
[
9
, Theorem 3.17]
,
\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})/Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))
is minimal. We complete the proof using the topological isomorphism

\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})/\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}}))\cong\prod_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})/Z(\operatorname{ST^{+}}(n,{\mathbb{F}})))

and similar arguments to those appearing in the proof of (1).
∎

Definition 3.3

.

{\mathbb{F}}

\operatorname{ST^{+}}(2^{k},{\mathbb{F}})

Let
{\mathbb{F}}
be a subfield of a local field and

By
[

, Corollary 5.2]
,
\operatorname{SL}(2^{k},{\mathbb{F}})
is minimal.
Being finite, the center

). Having perfectly minimal center, the minimal group

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(n_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be an increasing sequence of natural numbers.
Then, neither
\prod_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{SL}(2^{n_{k}},{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
nor
\prod_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(2^{n_{k}},{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
are minimal.

Proof.

We first prove that
G=\prod_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{SL}(2^{n_{k}},{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
is not minimal. In view of the Minimality Criterion, it suffices to show that
G
is not essential in
\widehat{G}=\prod_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{SL}(2^{n_{k}},{\mathbb{C}}).

To this aim, let

N=\{(\lambda_{k}I_{2^{n_{k}}})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\in\widehat{G}|\ (\lambda_{k+1})^{2}=\lambda_{k}\ \forall k\in{\mathbb{N}}\}.

The equality
\lambda_{k+1}^{2}=\lambda_{k}
implies that
N
is a closed central subgroup of
\widehat{G}.
Moreover,
N
is non-trivial as the sequence
(n_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is increasing. Let us see that
N
trivially intersects
G.
Otherwise, there exists a sequence
(\lambda_{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of roots of unity in
{\mathbb{Q}}(i)
such that
(\lambda_{k+1})^{2}=\lambda_{k}
for every
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\lambda_{k_{0}}\neq 1
for some
k_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}.
It follows that

\lambda_{k_{0}},\lambda_{k_{0}+1},\lambda_{k_{0}+2},\lambda_{k_{0}+3}
are different non-trivial roots of unity in
{\mathbb{Q}}(i),
contradicting the fact that
\pm 1,\pm i
are the only roots of unity in
{\mathbb{Q}}(i).

Now consider the group
H=\prod_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(2^{n_{k}},{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
. In view of Lemma
2.1
and what we have just proved,
N
is also a closed non-trivial central subgroup of
\widehat{H}
that trivially intersects
H.
This means that
H
is not essential in
\widehat{H}
. By the Minimality Criterion,
H
is not minimal.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\pi}
and
\mathcal{F}_{c}
be the set of Fermat primes and the set of composite Fermat numbers, respectively, and let
\mathcal{A}\in\{\mathcal{F}_{\pi},\mathcal{F}_{c}\}.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{A}
is finite;

(b)

\prod_{F_{n}\in\mathcal{A}}\operatorname{SL}(F_{n}-1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
is minimal;

(c)

\prod_{F_{n}\in\mathcal{A}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(F_{n}-1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
is minimal.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{M}_{\pi}
and
\mathcal{M}_{c}
be the set of Mersenne primes and the set of composite Mersenne numbers, respectively, and let
\mathcal{B}\in\{\mathcal{M}_{\pi},\mathcal{M}_{c}\}.

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(a)

\mathcal{B}
is finite;

(b)

\prod_{M_{p}\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname{SL}(M_{p}+1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
is minimal;

(c)

\prod_{M_{p}\in\mathcal{B}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(M_{p}+1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
is minimal.

Proof.

(1) Assume first that
\mathcal{A}
is finite. Note that
F_{n}-1
is a power of two for every Fermat number
F_{n}\in\mathcal{A}.
It is easy to see that a product of finitely many perfectly minimal groups is minimal. Therefore, both topological groups
\prod_{F_{n}\in\mathcal{A}}\operatorname{SL}(F_{n}-1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))

and
\prod_{F_{n}\in\mathcal{A}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(F_{n}-1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
are minimal, in view of Proposition
3.4
.
If
\mathcal{A}
is infinite, then
\prod_{F_{n}\in\mathcal{A}}\operatorname{SL}(F_{n}-1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
and
\prod_{F_{n}\in\mathcal{A}}\operatorname{ST^{+}}(F_{n}-1,{\mathbb{Q}}(i))
are not minimal by Theorem
3.5
.

(2) The proof is similar to (1). The only difference is that for every prime
p
it holds that
M_{p}+1
is a power of two.
∎

Abstract
Let λ ⁡ ( G ) \lambda(G) be the maximum number of subgroups in an irredundant covering of a finite group G G . We prove that the finite groups with λ ⁡ ( G ) = | G | − t \lambda(G)=|G|-t , where t ≤ 5 t\leq 5 , are solvable, and classify such groups. Keywords covering groups, irredundant covering, solvable groups 2010 Mathematics subject classification: 20D10, 20D15

Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group. A
cover
of
G
is a collection
of subgroups of
G
whose union is
G
. The cover is
irredundant

if no proper sub-collection is also a cover.
Many different aspects of covers have been studied.
One of these aspects is to study
the structure of groups by using the number of subgroups in a cover of a group.
Cohn
[
6
]

defined
\sigma(G)
to be the smallest integer
n
such that the group
G
is
covered by
n
proper subgroups. He proved some properties of
\sigma(G)

and described the structure of the groups with
small
\sigma(G)
.

Since then, many scholars study groups by
\sigma(G)
. On one hand,
some scholars calculated
\sigma(G)
for given groups.
For example, Cohn
[
6
]
proved

\sigma(A_{5})=10
and
\sigma(S_{5})=16
.
Tomkinson
[
13
]
proved that

\sigma(G)=1+p^{a}
, where
p^{a}
is the order of a particular chief factor of
G
, if
G
is a finite solvable group.
Holmes
[
7
]
determined
\sigma(G)

for
G=M_{11},M_{22}
and
M_{23}
respectively. Kappe et al.
[
8
]

determined
\sigma(S_{n})
for
n=8,9,10,12
.
On the other hand, some scholars determined which numbers can occur as

\sigma(G)

for groups, and, when possible, to characterize the
groups having the same value of
\sigma(G)
.
For example,
Cohn
[
6
]
described the structure of the groups with

\sigma(G)=3,4,5
. Abdollahi et al.
[
1
]
gave a description of finite groups with
\sigma(G)=6
.
Tomkinson
[
13
]
proved that
there is no finite groups
G
such that
\sigma(G)=7
.
Zhang
[
15
]
proved that there is no finite
groups
G
satisfying
\sigma(G)=11
or
\sigma(G)=13
.
For more results, the readers refer to
[
2
,
5
,
11
,
12
]
.

As the dual of
\sigma(G)
, Rog
{\rm\acute{e}}
rio

[
9
]
defined
\lambda(G)
as the maximum number of subgroups
in an irredundant covering of a group
G
.
In
[
9
]
, the author gave some basic properties of
\lambda(G)
,
calculated
\lambda(G)
for some groups and
classified the finite groups with
\sigma(G)=\lambda(G)
and
\lambda(G)=3,4,5
, respectively.
Bastos et al.
[
4
]
described the structure of groups
G

with
\lambda(G)=6
.

We continue to investigate the structure of finite groups
by
\lambda(G)
. In
[
4
,
9
]
, the authors studied the groups with small
\lambda(G)
.
In the paper, we investigate the groups with large
\lambda(G)
and classify the groups with
\lambda(G)=|G|-t
, where
t\leq 5
.

The notation and terminology are standard, see
[
3
]
.
We
use
{\rm C}_{n},{\rm D}_{2^{n}},{\rm Q}_{2^{n}}
and
{\rm C}_{p}^{n}
to denote the
cyclic group of order
n
, the dihedral group of order
2^{n}
, the
generalized quaternion group of order
2^{n}
and the elementary abelian group of order
p^{n}
,
respectively.

Let
G
be a finite
p
-group.
For any positive integer
s
, we define

\Omega_{s}(G)=\langle a\in G\mid a^{p^{s}}=1\rangle\ {\rm and}\ \mho_{s}(G)=\langle a^{p^{s}}\mid a\in G\rangle.

\Omega_{\{s\}}(G)=\{a\in G\mid a^{p^{s}}=1\}\ {\rm and}\ \mho_{\{s\}}(G)=\{a^{p^{s}}\mid a\in G\}.

If
a,b
are two elements of
G
, the commutator of
a,b

is defined as
[a,b]=a^{-1}b^{-1}ab
.
If
H
and
K
are subgroups of
G
with
G=HK
and
[H,K]=1
, we call
G
a
central product of
H
and
K
, denoted by
G=H\ast K
. Clearly,
H\cap K\leq Z(G)
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we list some results which often are used in this paper.
The following Lemma tells us
\lambda(G)
is the number of maximal cyclic subgroups of a finite group
G
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
9
, Proposition 4]

Let
G
be a finite group and
\langle x_{i}\rangle,i=1,2,\cdots,n
be the maximal cyclic
subgroups of
G
. Then
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\langle x_{i}\rangle
is an irredundant covering of
G
and
\lambda(G)=n
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
9
, Proposition 5]

Let
G
be a finite group.

(i)

\lambda(H)\leq\lambda(G)
for any subgroup
H
of
G
.

(ii)
If
N\unlhd G
, then
\lambda(G/N)\leq\lambda(G)
. For

N=\cap_{i=1}^{\lambda}H_{i}
,
\lambda(G/N)=\lambda(G),

where
\lambda=\lambda(G)
and
H_{1},H_{2},\cdots,H_{\lambda}
are the
maximal cyclic subgroups of
G
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
9
, Proposition 6 and Proposition 7]

(1)\ \lambda({\rm C}_{p}\times{\rm C}_{p^{k}})=kp-k+2;

(2)\ \lambda({\rm D}_{2n})=n+1;

(3)\ \lambda({\rm Q}_{2^{n}})=2^{n-2}+1.

Lemma 2.4
.

\lambda({\rm D}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2})=12.

Proof
Let
G={\rm D}_{8}\times{\rm C}_{2}
. Assume without loss generality

G=\langle a,b,c\mid a^{4}=b^{2}=c^{2}=1,[a,b]=a^{2},[b,c]=[a,c]=1\rangle.

It is clear that
\exp(G)=4
. Hence, the order of maximal cyclic subgroups is
2
or
4
.
By Lemma
2.5
, we have

(a^{i}b^{j}c^{k})^{2}=(a^{i}b^{j})^{2}=a^{2i}[a^{i},b^{-j}]=a^{2i(1-j)}.

[formula]

Hence,

(a^{i}b^{j}c^{k})^{2}=1\Longleftrightarrow a^{2i(1-j)}=1\Longleftrightarrow i\equiv 0(\hbox{\rm mod }2)\ \ {\mbox{or}}\ \ j\equiv 1(\hbox{\rm mod }2).

It follows that

\Omega_{\{1\}}(G)=\{1,b,c,bc,a^{2},a^{2}b,a^{2}c,a^{2}bc,ab,a^{3}b,abc,a^{3}bc\}.

Thus, the number of maximal cyclic subgroups of order
4
is

\frac{|G|-|\Omega_{\{1\}}(G)|}{2}=\frac{16-12}{2}=2
.

By
(*)
,
\mho_{\{1\}}(G)=\{1,a^{2}\}
. Hence, the
number of maximal cyclic subgroups of order
2
is

|\Omega_{\{1\}}(G)|-|\mho_{\{1\}}(G)|=12-2=10
.
Therefore,
\lambda(G)=10+2=12
.
\Box

Lemma 2.5
.

(
[
14
]
)

Assume
G
is a metabelian
p
-group. Let
a,b
be elements of

G
and
n

(ab^{-1})^{n}=a^{n}\prod_{i+j\leq n}[ia,jb]^{n\choose{i+j}}b^{-n},

where
[ia,jb]=[a,b,\underbrace{a,\ldots,a}_{i-1},\underbrace{b,\ldots,b}_{j-1}].

3
Some properties

By Lemma
2.1
,

\lambda(G)
is the number of maximal cyclic subgroups of
G
.
In the following, we assume that

G=\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{\lambda(G)}\rangle\ \ {\mbox{\rm and}}\ \ o(g_{1})\geq o(g_{2})\geq\cdots\geq o(g_{\lambda(G)}),

where
\langle g_{1}\rangle,\langle g_{2}\rangle,\cdots,\langle g_{\lambda(G)}\rangle
are all maximal cyclic subgroups of
G
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(1)

o(g_{1})=2
if and only if
\lambda(G)=|G|-1
.

(2)
If
o(g_{1})=3
, then
\lambda(G)=|G|-k-1
, where
k
is the number of subgroups of order
3
of
G
.

Proof
(1) It is clear that
o(g_{1})=2
if and only if
G\cong C_{2}^{n}
.
The result follows.

(2) Since
o(g_{1})=3
,
each subgroup of order
3
of
G
is maximal cyclic in
G
. Assume that
\langle g_{1}\rangle,\langle g_{2}\rangle,\cdots\langle g_{k}\rangle

are all subgroups of order
3
of
G
.
Let

G=\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\cdots\langle g_{k}\rangle\cup\langle g_{k+1}\rangle\cdots\langle g_{\lambda(G)}\rangle,

where
o(g_{1})=\cdots=o(g_{k})=3
. Then
o(g_{k+1})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2
.
It is clear that
\langle g_{i}\rangle\cap\langle g_{j}\rangle=1
for
i\neq j
.
Thus

|G|=1+k(3-1)+(\lambda(G)-k)=\lambda(G)+k+1.

It follows that
\lambda(G)=|G|-k-1
.
\Box

By Proposition
3.1
(1), we assume that
\lambda(G)<|G|-1
in following.

Proposition 3.2
.

o(g_{1})\leq|G|-\lambda(G)+1
.

Proof
By Lemma
2.1
,
\{\langle g_{i}\rangle\mid i=1,\cdots,\lambda(G)\}

is an irredundant covering of
G
.
We have
g_{2}\cdots,g_{\lambda(G)}
are different pairwise elements of
G\backslash\langle g_{1}\rangle
.
Hence
\lambda(G)-1\leq|G|-o(g_{1})
. It follows that
o(g_{1})\leq|G|-\lambda(G)+1
.

\Box

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite non-cyclic group. If
o(g_{1})=|G|-\lambda(G)+1
, then

(1)

o(g_{2})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2
.

(2)

G\cong{\rm D}_{2n}
,
where
n=|G|-\lambda(G)+1
.

Proof
(1) Since
G
is non-cyclic,
\lambda(G)\geq 2
.
If
o(g_{2})>2
, then, by
o(g_{1})=|G|-\lambda(G)+1
,

|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle|\geq|G|-\lambda(G)+1+2=|G|-\lambda(G)+3.

Since
g_{3},\cdots,g_{\lambda(G)}
are
\lambda(G)-2
elements in
G\backslash(\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle)
,

\lambda(G)-2\leq|G|-|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle|\leq|G|-(|G|-\lambda(G)+3)=\lambda(G)-3,

This is a contradiction.
Hence
o(g_{2})=2
.
Thus,
o(g_{3})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2
.

(2) Since
\lambda(G)<|G|-1
,
n>2
.
It follows by (1) that
\langle g_{1}\rangle
is the unique cyclic subgroup of order
n
.
Take any
x\in G\backslash\langle g_{1}\rangle
.
Then
o(x)=2
. Since
g_{1}x\not\in\langle g_{1}\rangle
,
o(g_{1}x)=2
,
i.e.
g_{1}xg_{1}x=1
.
It follows that
g_{1}^{x}=g_{1}^{-1}
and
\langle g_{1},x\rangle\cong{\rm D}_{2n}
.
We assert that
G=\langle g_{1},x\rangle
.
If not, then there exists an element
y\in G\backslash\langle g_{1},x\rangle
.
Since
y\not\in\langle g_{1}\rangle
,
o(y)=2
. Thus
g_{1}^{y}=g_{1}^{-1}
.
It follows that
g_{1}^{xy}=g_{1}
.
Thus
g_{1}
and
xy
are commute and
g_{1}xy
is an element of order
n
of
G
.
This is contrary to the uniqueness of
\langle g_{1}\rangle
.
Hence,
G=\langle g_{1},x\rangle\cong{\rm D}_{2n}
.
\Box

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
p
be a prime. If there exists a maximal cyclic subgroup of order
p

in
G
, then
\lambda(G)\leq|G|-k(p-2)-1
, where
k
is the number of maximal cyclic subgroups of order
p
of
G
.

Proof
Let
A=\langle g_{i_{1}}\rangle\cup\langle g_{i_{2}}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{i_{k}}\rangle
, where
\langle g_{i_{1}}\rangle,\langle g_{i_{2}}\rangle,\cdots,\langle g_{i_{k}}\rangle

are all the maximal cyclic subgroups of order
p
of
G
. Then
|A|=1+k(p-1)
.
Since there exist
\lambda(G)-k
elements in
G\backslash A
,
\lambda(G)-k\leq|G|-(1+k(p-1))
.
It follows that
\lambda(G)\leq|G|-k(p-2)-1
.
\Box

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
G
be a finite group. If
\lambda(G)=|G|-t
, where
t\leq 5
, then
G

is solvable.

Proof
By Proposition
3.2
,
o(g_{1})\leq t+1
.
Since
t\leq 5
,
o(g_{1})\leq 6
. If
o(g_{1})\leq 4
, then
G
is a
\{2,3\}
-group.
It follows by
p^{a}q^{b}
-theorem that
G
is solvable. If
o(g_{1})=6
, then

t=5
and
o(g_{2})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2

by Theorem
3.3
. Hence

G
is a
\{2,3\}
-group. Hence
G
is also solvable.

If
o(g_{1})=5
, then
t=4
or
5
by Proposition
3.2
.
We assert that
t=4
. If not, then
t=5
and
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
.
Notice that
|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle|=o(g_{2})+(5-1)=o(g_{2})+4
.
Since
g_{3},\cdots,g_{\lambda(G)}\in G\backslash(\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle)
,

\lambda(G)-2\leq|G|-|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle|
.
It follows by
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
that
o(g_{2})\leq 3
.
If
o(g_{2})=2
, then
o(g_{3})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2
.
Since
G=\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\langle g_{3}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{\lambda(G)}\rangle
,

|G|=5+\lambda(G)-1=\lambda(G)+4
. This is contrary to
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
. Hence,
o(g_{2})=3
.
It follows that
G
has only one cyclic subgroup
\langle g_{1}\rangle
of order
5
.
Hence,
\langle g_{1}\rangle\unlhd G
.
Clearly,
\langle g_{1}\rangle\langle g_{2}\rangle
is a subgroup of order
15
of
G
.
Notice that the group of order
15
is cyclic. However, the largest order of maximal cyclic subgroups of
G
is 5.
This is a contradiction. Hence,
t=4
, and hence
\lambda(G)=|G|-4
.
By Proposition
3.2
,

o(g_{2})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2
. Hence
|G|=2^{k}5
.
It follows that
G
is solvable.
\Box

Theorem 4.1

(2)

(1) It is straightforward.

(2)
(\Longleftarrow).
It is straightforward by a simple calculation.

. It is clear that

,

If

. Assume

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

be a finite group. Then
\lambda(G)=|G|-3
if and only if
G\cong{\rm C}_{4}
or
{\rm D}_{8}

Proof

(\Longleftarrow).
It is straightforward by a simple calculation.

(\Longrightarrow).

If
G
is cyclic, then

. Assume that
G
is non-cyclic.
By Proposition

, we have

.
We assert that

If not, then

.
This contradicts

If

of order

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group. Then
\lambda(G)=|G|-4

,

\lambda({\rm C}_{4}\times{\rm C}_{2})=4

\lambda({\rm D}_{10})=6

(\Longrightarrow).
If

. Assume that
G

By Proposition

, we have

. We assert
o(g_{1})=4
or
5
. In fact,

o(g_{1})=2
, then

. It is clear that
\lambda(G)=|G|-1
.
This contradicts
\lambda(G)=|G|-4
. If

.

, where

. By Sylow Theorem,

If

.

If

Step 1.

-group.

Step 2.

We assert that

By step 1,

Since

If

cyclic subgroups of order

Lemma 4.5
.

If
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
and
o(g_{1})=3
, then
G\cong A_{4}
,
{\rm C}_{3}^{2}
or

\langle a,b,c\mid a^{3}=b^{3}=c^{2}=1,a^{c}=a^{-1},b^{c}=b^{-1},a^{b}=a\rangle\cong{\rm C}_{3}^{2}\rtimes{\rm C}_{2}
.

Proof
By Proposition
3.1
, there are four cyclic subgroups of order
3
.
Let
P\in\hbox{\rm Syl}_{3}(G)
and
|P|=3^{k}
. Since
o(g_{1})=3
,
\exp(P)=3
.
Let
n_{3}
be the number of subgroup of order
3
of
P
.
Then
n_{3}=\frac{|P|-1}{3-1}=\frac{3^{k}-1}{2}
.
Clearly
n_{3}\leq 4
. It follows that
k\leq 2
.
Thus,
P\cong{\rm C}_{3}
or
{\rm C}_{3}^{2}
.

Case 1.

P\cong{\rm C}_{3}
.

Since
G
has four cyclic subgroups of order
3
,
|G:N_{G}(P)|=4
by Sylow theorem.
Let
H=N_{G}(P)
and
H_{G}=\bigcap\limits_{x\in G}H^{x}
be the core of
H
.
If
3\mid|H_{G}|
, then
P\leq H_{G}
. Thus,
G=N_{G}(P)H_{G}
by Frattini argument.
Since
H=N_{G}(P)
,
H_{G}\leq N_{G}(P)
.
It follows that
G=N_{G}(P)
.
Hence,
P\unlhd G
. This is a contradiction.
Therefore,
3\nmid|H_{G}|
.
Since
G
is a
\{2,3\}
-group and
3\nmid|H_{G}|
,
H_{G}
is a
2
-subgroup of
G
.
Noticing that
H_{G}\leq H=N_{G}(P)
, we have
H_{G}P=H_{G}\times P
.
It follows that
H_{G}=1
by
o(g_{1})=3
.
Now, consider the transform representation of
H
. Since
|G:H|=4
and
H_{G}=1
,

G/H_{G}=G\lesssim S_{4}
. Noticing that
3\mid|G|
and
4\mid|G|
, it
follows that
G\cong A_{4}
or
S_{4}
.
By
o(g_{1})=3
, we have
G\ncong S_{4}
. Therefore,
G\cong A_{4}
.

Case 2.

P\cong{\rm C}_{3}^{2}
.

Since
o(g_{1})=3
,
G
is a
\{2,3\}
-group.
If
2\nmid|G|
, then
G\cong{\rm C}_{3}^{2}
. Assume that
2\mid|G|
.
By Proposition
3.1
,
G
has only four cyclic subgroups of order
3
.
It follows that
P\unlhd G
. Notice that
o(g_{1})=3
.
It follows that
G/P
is an element abelian
2
-group.
Clearly,
there is no
3^{\prime}
-elements in
C_{G}(P)
. Thus
C_{G}(P)=P
.
By the
N/C
-theorem,
G/P
is isomorphic to an elementary abelian
2
-subgroup of
GL(2,3)
.
Let

\alpha=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right),\beta=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&-1\\
0&-1\end{array}\right),

By computation, we get
o(\alpha)=8,o(\beta)=2
and
\alpha^{\beta}=\alpha^{3}
. Let

S=\langle\alpha,\beta\mid\alpha^{8}=\beta^{2}=1,\alpha^{\beta}=\alpha^{3}\rangle.

Then

Hence
S
is a Sylow
2
-subgroup of
GL(2,3)
.
By computation,
\alpha^{4},\beta,\alpha^{2}\beta,\alpha^{4}\beta,\alpha^{6}\beta

2
of

Let
P=\langle a,b\rangle
and
1\neq c\in Q

. Since the largest order of elements of

c
is an element of order

Since

,

Let

\Box

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
and
o(g_{1})=4
, then
G\cong{\rm Q}_{8}
.

Proof
We prove the result by the following four steps.

Step 1.
G
is a
2
-group.

If not, then there exist maximal cyclic subgroups of order
3
in
G
.
Assume that
G
has
k
maximal cyclic subgroups of order
3
.
Let
A
be the union of the maximal cyclic subgroups of order
3
of
G
.
Then
|A|=1+2k
. Since there are at least
\lambda(G)-(k+1)
elements in
G\backslash(\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup A)
,
we have

\lambda(G)-(k+1)\leq|G|-|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup A|=|G|-(2k+4).

It follows by
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
that
k\leq 2
. By Sylow theorem, we have
k=1
.

Let
H
be the maximal cyclic subgroup of order
3
of
G
. Then
H\unlhd G
.
Since
o(g_{1})=4
,
C_{G}(H)=H
.
It follows that
G/H\lesssim\hbox{\rm Aut\,}(H)\cong{\rm C}_{2}
. Thus
|G|=3
or
6
.
This is contrary to
o(g_{1})=4
.
Therefore,
G
has no subgroup of order
3
.
Since
o(g_{1})=4
,
G
is a
2
-group.

Step 2.
G
has only three maximal cyclic subgroups of order
4
.

Let
\langle g_{1}\rangle,\langle g_{2}\rangle,\cdots,\langle g_{k}\rangle
be the maximal cyclic subgroups of order
4
of
G
.
Then
|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{k}\rangle|\geq 2k+2
.
Since
g_{k+1},\cdots,g_{\lambda(G)}
are the elements of
G\backslash(\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{k}\rangle)
,

\lambda(G)-k\leq|G|-|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{k}\rangle|\leq|G|-(2k+2).

It follows by
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
that
k\leq 3
.

If
k=1
, then
o(g_{2})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2
.
By
G=\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{\lambda(G)}\rangle
,
we have
|G|=4+\lambda(G)-1=\lambda(G)+3
, which is contrary to
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
.
If
k=2
, then
o(g_{1})=o(g_{2})=4
and
o(g_{3})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2
.
By
G=\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{\lambda(G)}\rangle
,
we have
|G|=|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle|+\lambda(G)-2
.
Since
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
,
|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle|=7
.
It follows that
\langle g_{1}\rangle\cap\langle g_{2}\rangle=1
.
By step 1,
G
is a
2
-group. Hence
Z(G)\neq 1
.
Take an element
a
of order
2
in
Z(G)
. Then
a\not\in\langle g_{1}\rangle

or
a\not\in\langle g_{2}\rangle
.
Without losing generality, assume that
a\not\in\langle g_{1}\rangle
.
Then
\langle ag_{1}\rangle
is a cyclic subgroup of order
4
.
Since
k=2
and

a\not\in\langle g_{1}\rangle
,
\langle ag_{1}\rangle=\langle g_{2}\rangle
.
Thus
g_{2}^{2}=(ag_{1})^{2}=g_{1}^{2}\in\langle g_{1}\rangle\cap\langle g_{2}\rangle
,
which is contrary to
\langle g_{1}\rangle\cap\langle g_{2}\rangle=1
.
To sum up,
k=3
. That is,
G
has only three maximal cyclic subgroups
\langle g_{1}\rangle,\langle g_{2}\rangle
and
\langle g_{3}\rangle
of order
4
.

Step 3.
\langle g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle\cong Q_{8}
.

If
|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cap\langle g_{2}\rangle\cap\langle g_{3}\rangle|=1
,
then
|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\langle g_{3}\rangle|\geq 9
.
Noticing that
G=\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{\lambda(G)}\rangle

and
o(g_{4})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2
,
we have

|G|=|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\langle g_{3}\rangle|+\lambda(G)-3\geq 9+\lambda(G)-3=\lambda(G)+6.

It follows that
\lambda(G)\leq|G|-6
, which is contrary to
\lambda(G)=|G|-5
.
Hence
|\langle g_{1}\rangle\cap\langle g_{2}\rangle\cap\langle g_{3}\rangle|\neq 1

and
g_{1}^{2}=g_{2}^{2}=g_{3}^{2}
.

We claim that
\langle g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle
is not abelian. Otherwise,
\langle g_{1}g_{2}g_{3}\rangle
is the 4th maximal cyclic subgroup of order 4, a contradiction. Without loss of generality, we assume that
[g_{1},g_{2}]\neq 1
. It is easy to see that
\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle\cong Q_{8}
and
g_{3}=g_{1}g_{2}
.

Step 4.
G\cong Q_{8}
.

By

G=\langle g_{1}\rangle\cup\langle g_{2}\rangle\cup\cdots\cup\langle g_{\lambda(G)}\rangle

and

o(g_{1})=o(g_{2})=o(g_{3})=4,o(g_{4})=\cdots=o(g_{\lambda(G)})=2,g_{1}^{2}=g_{2}^{2}=g_{3}^{2},

we have
\Phi(G)=\mho_{1}(G)=\langle g_{1}^{2}\rangle
.
It follows that
\langle g_{1}\rangle\unlhd G
.

We assert
C_{G}(g_{1})=\langle g_{1}\rangle
.
If not, there exists an element
a\in C_{G}(g_{1})\backslash\langle g_{1}\rangle
.
It is easy to see that
\langle g_{1}a\rangle
is the 4th maximal cyclic subgroup of order 4, a contradiction.
Hence
C_{G}(g_{1})=\langle g_{1}\rangle
.
By
N/C
-theorem,
G/C_{G}(g_{1})=G/\langle g_{1}\rangle\lesssim C_{2}
.
Thus,
|G|\leq 8
. By Step 3,
G=\langle g_{1},g_{2},g_{3}\rangle\cong{\rm Q}_{8}
.

\Box

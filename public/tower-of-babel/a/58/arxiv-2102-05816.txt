Numerical analysis of a new formulation for the Oseen equations in terms of vorticity and Bernoulli pressure
Verónica Anaya, David MoraAmiya K. Pani,Ricardo Ruiz-Baier

Abstract
A variational formulation is introduced for the Oseen equations written in terms of vorticity and Bernoulli pressure. The velocity is fully decoupled using the momentum balance equation, and it is later recovered by a post-process. A finite element method is also proposed, consisting in equal-order Nédélec finite elements and piecewise continuous polynomials for the vorticity and the Bernoulli pressure, respectively. The a priori error analysis is carried out in the L 2 \mathrm{L}^{2} -norm for vorticity, pressure, and velocity; under a smallness assumption either on the convecting velocity, or on the mesh parameter. Furthermore, an a posteriori error estimator is designed and its robustness and efficiency are studied using weighted norms. Finally, a set of numerical examples in 2D and 3D is given, where the error indicator serves to guide adaptive mesh refinement. These tests illustrate the behaviour of the new formulation in typical flow conditions, and they also confirm the theoretical findings.



Key words
: Oseen equations; vorticity-based formulation;
finite element methods;
a priori
error bounds;

a posteriori
error estimation; numerical examples.

1
Introduction

In this paper, we propose a reformulation of the Oseen equations using only vorticity and Bernoulli pressure. A similar splitting of the unknowns has been recently proposed in
[
9
]
for the Brinkman equations. We extend those results for the Oseen problem and propose a residual-based
a posteriori
error estimator whose properties are studied using a weighted energy norm, as well as the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm.

There is an abundant body of literature dealing with numerical methods for incompressible flow problems using the vorticity as a dedicated unknown. These include spectral elements
[
6
,
10
]
, stabilised and least-squares schemes
[
5
,
15
]
, and mixed finite elements
[
8
,
23
,
22
,
30
,
24
]
, to name a few.
Works specifically devoted to the analysis of numerical schemes for the Oseen equations in terms of vorticity include the non-conforming exponentially accurate least-squares spectral method for Oseen equations proposed in
[
27
]
, the least-squares method proposed in
[
32
]
for Oseen and Navier-Stokes equations, the family of vorticity-based first-order Oseen-type systems studied in
[
18
]
, the enhanced accuracy formulation in terms of velocity-vorticity-helicity investigated in
[
12
]
, and the recent mixed and DG discretisations for Oseen’s problem in velocity-vorticity-pressure form, proposed in
[
7
]
.

The method advocated in this article focuses on Nédélec elements of order
k\geq 1
for the vorticity and piecewise continuous polynomials of degree
k
, for the Bernoulli pressure.
An abridged version of the analysis for this formulation has been recently advanced in
[
3
]
. In contrast, here we provide details on the

a priori
error estimates rigorously derived for the finite element discretisations in the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm under enough regularity and under smallness assumption on the mesh parameter.
Furthermore, we prove error estimates for two post-processes for the velocity
field in the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm. The first one is similar to the one used in
[
9
]
for Brinkman equations, which exploits
the momentum equation and direct differentiation of the
discrete vorticity and Bernoulli pressure. For the second post-process
we solve an additional elliptic problem emanating from the constitutive equation defining vorticity, and it uses the continuity equation and
the discrete vorticity appears on the right-hand side. This problem is discretised with, e.g.,
piecewise linear and continuous polynomials.

On the other hand, we address the construction of residual
based
a posteriori
error estimators which are reliable and efficient.
Adaptive mesh refinement strategies based on
a posteriori
error indicators
have a significant role in computing numerical solutions to partial differential equations, and this
is of high importance in the particular context of incompressible flow problems. Robust and efficient error estimators permit to restore the optimal convergence of finite element methods, specifically when complex geometries or singular coefficients are present (which could otherwise lead to non-convergence or to the generation of spurious solutions)
[
33
]
, and they can provide substantial enhancement to the accuracy of the approximations
[
1
]
.
A posteriori
error analyses for vorticity-based equations are already available from the literature (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
,
8
,
16
]
),
but considering formulations substantially different to the one we put forward here. Our analysis complements these works by establishing upper and lower bounds in different
norms, and using an estimator that is scaled according to the expected regularity of the solutions (which in turn also depends on the
regularity of the domain).
Reliability of the
a posteriori
error estimator is proved
in the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm, and local efficiency of the
error indicator is shown by using a standard technique based on bubble functions.

We further remark that the present method has the advantage of direct computation of vorticity, and it is relatively competitive in terms of computational cost (for instance when compared with the classical MINI-element, or Taylor-Hood schemes). The type of vorticity-based formulations we use here can be of additional physical relevance in scenarios where boundary effects are critical, for example as in those discussed in
[
21
,
28
]
. Moreover, the corresponding analysis is fairly simple, only requiring classical tools for elliptic problems.

We have structured the contents of the paper in the following manner. We present the model problem as well as the two-field weak formulation and its solvability analysis in Section
2
. The finite element discretisation is constructed in Section
3
, where we also derive the stability,
convergence bounds and we present two post-processes for the velocity field. Section
4
is devoted to the analysis of reliability and efficiency of a weighted residual-based
a posteriori
error indicator, and we close in Section
5
with a set of numerical tests that illustrate the properties of the proposed numerical scheme in a variety of scenarios, including validation of the adaptive refinement procedure guided by the error estimator.

2
The continuous formulation of the Oseen problem

This section deals with some preliminaries, variational formulation for the Oseen problem given in terms of vorticity and Bernoulli pressure and its well-posedness.

2.1
Preliminaries

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a bounded and connected Lipschitz domain with
its boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
and further, let
\boldsymbol{n}
be
the outward unit vector normal to
\Gamma
. The starting point of our investigation is the following form of the equations, that use velocity, vorticity, and Bernoulli pressure (see, e.g.,
[
7
,
29
]
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sigma\boldsymbol{u}-\nu\Delta\boldsymbol{u}+\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}\times\boldsymbol{\beta}+\nabla p&=\boldsymbol{f}\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}&=0\qquad\,\mbox{ in }\Omega,\end{split}

(2.1)

where
\nu>0
is the kinematic viscosity, and a linearisation and backward Euler time stepping
explain the terms
\sigma>0
as the inverse of the time step, and
\boldsymbol{\beta}
as
an adequate approximation of velocity (representing for example the velocity
at a previous time step). The Bernoulli pressure relates to the true fluid pressure
P

as follows
p:=P+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}-\lambda
, where
\lambda
is the mean value of
\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{u}
.

The structure of (
2.1
) suggests to introduce the rescaled
vorticity vector
\boldsymbol{\omega}:=\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}
as a new
unknown. Thus, the Oseen problem can be formulated as: Find
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{\omega},p
such that

\displaystyle\sigma\boldsymbol{u}+\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}+\nu^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}}+\nabla p\,=

\displaystyle\,\boldsymbol{f}

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\omega}-\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}=

\displaystyle\,\boldsymbol{0}

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}=

\displaystyle\,0

\displaystyle\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}=

\displaystyle\,\boldsymbol{g}

\displaystyle\mbox{ on }\Gamma.

(2.5)

The vector of external forces
\boldsymbol{f}
absorbs the contributions
related to previous time steps and to the fixed states in
the linearisation procedure that leads from Navier-Stokes to Oseen equations.
Along with to the Dirichlet boundary condition
for the velocity on
\Gamma
,
the additional condition
(p,1)_{\Omega,0}=0
is required to have uniqueness of the Bernoulli pressure.
We will also assume that the data are
regular enough:
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)^{3}.

However, we do not restrict the behaviour of
\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits{\boldsymbol{\beta}}
.
For different assumptions on
\boldsymbol{\beta}
we refer to, e.g.,

[
11
,
19
,
20
,
32
]
.

For the sake of conciseness of the presentation,
the analysis in the sequel is carried out for homogeneous boundary conditions on velocity,
i.e.
\boldsymbol{g}=\boldsymbol{0}
on
\Gamma
. Non homogeneous boundary data, as well as mixed boundary conditions,
will be considered in the numerical examples in Section
5
, below.

2.2
Variational formulation

For any
s\geq 0
, the symbol

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{s,\Omega}
denotes
the norm of the Hilbert Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega)
or

\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega)^{3}
, with the usual convention
\mathrm{H}^{0}(\Omega):=\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
. For
s\geq 0
, we recall
the definition of the space

\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega):=\left\{\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega)^{3}:\ \mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega)^{3}\right\},

endowed with the norm

\left\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\right\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)}=\Big(\left\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\right\|_{s,\Omega}^{2}+\left\|\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}\right\|^{2}_{s,\Omega}\Big)^{1/2}
, and will denote

\mathrm{H}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)=\mathrm{H}^{0}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)
. Finally,
c
and

C
, with subscripts, tildes, or hats, will represent a generic constant
independent of the mesh parameter
h
.

We denote the function spaces

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}:=\mathrm{H}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega),\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\mathrm{Q}:=\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega),

which are endowed, respectively, with the following norms

\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{\mathbf{Z}}:=\left(\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\nu\|\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\hskip 20.00003pt\mbox{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\|q\|_{\mathrm{Q}}:=(\|q\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\|\nabla q\|^{2}_{0,\Omega})^{1/2}.

Here,
\mathrm{L}_{0}^{2}(\Omega)
represents the set of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
functions with mean value zero.

In addition, for sake of the subsequent analysis,
it is convenient to introduce the following space

\mathbb{V}:=\{(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}(\Omega):\ \sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\}.

Lemma 2.1

The space
\mathbb{V}
endowed with the norm defined by

\|(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\|_{\mathbb{V}}:=\Big(\sigma\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|q\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(2.6)

is a Hilbert space.

Proof.
Note that (
2.6
) is in fact a norm as
\|(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\|_{\mathbb{V}}=0
implies
(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)=(\boldsymbol{0},0)
a.e.
Now, it is easy to check that the norm satisfies the parallelogram identity and hence, it induces an inner product by
the polarisation identity. Therefore,
\mathbb{V}
equipped with this inner product is an inner product space. To complete the proof, it remains to show that this space is complete. To this end, let
\{(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n},q_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an arbitrary Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{V}.
From the completeness of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
and
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
, it follows that

(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{n},q_{n})\to(\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}},\hat{q})\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\times\mathrm{L}^{2}_{0}(\Omega)
and
\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n}+\nabla{q_{n}}\to\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}.

We now observe that

\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+\nabla\hat{q}\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)^{\prime}
, and for
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\langle\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+\nabla\hat{q},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\nu}\langle\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle+\langle\nabla\hat{q},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle=\sqrt{\nu}\langle\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}},\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle-\langle\hat{q},\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl[\sqrt{\nu}\langle\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n},\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle-\langle\hat{q}_{n},\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle\bigr]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigl[\sqrt{\nu}\langle\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{n},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle+\langle\nabla\hat{q}_{n},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle\bigr]

\displaystyle=\langle\boldsymbol{\varphi},\boldsymbol{\xi}\rangle.

Here we have employed integration by parts twice and the continuity of the involved operators in
passing to the limit. Therefore,
\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}+\nabla\hat{q}=\boldsymbol{\varphi}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}
and this
completes the rest of the proof.

\square

In order to derive a variational formulation of the problem, we
test (
2.3
) against a sufficiently smooth function
\sigma\boldsymbol{\theta}
.
Then, integrating by parts (using the classical curl-based Gauss theorems from, e.g.,
[
25
]
) and using the velocity boundary condition, we arrive at

\sigma\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}-\sigma\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}=0.

(2.7)

Next, from the momentum equation (
2.2
), we readily obtain the relation

\sigma\boldsymbol{u}=\boldsymbol{f}-\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}-\nu^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-\nabla p\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in}\hskip 10.00002pt\Omega,

(2.8)

and after replacing (
2.8
) in (
2.7
),
we find that

\displaystyle\sigma\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nu\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}=\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}.

Next for a given sufficiently smooth function

q
, we can test (
2.2
) against
\nabla q
.
Then, we integrate by parts and use again the velocity boundary condition, as well as (
2.4
)
to arrive at

\displaystyle\sigma\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\nabla q=0,

which leads to the variational form

\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\nabla q+\nu^{-1/2}\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\cdot\nabla q+\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot\nabla q=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot\nabla q.

Summarising, problem (
2.2
)-(
2.5
) is
written in its weak form as: Find
(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)\in\mathbb{V}

such that

\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega},p),(\boldsymbol{\theta},q))=\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\in\mathbb{V},

(2.9)

where the multilinear form
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}
and linear
functional
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}

are specified as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega},p),(\boldsymbol{\theta},q))

\displaystyle:=\sigma\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\omega}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}+\int_{\Omega}(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}+\nabla p)\cdot(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q)

(2.10)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\nu^{-1/2}\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\cdot(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q).

(2.11)

While our whole development will focus on this vorticity-pressure formulation, we stress that
from (
2.8
) we can immediately have an expression for velocity

\boldsymbol{u}=\sigma^{-1}\left(\boldsymbol{f}-\nu^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\omega}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}+\nabla p)\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in}\hskip 10.00002pt\Omega.

(2.12)

Remark 2.1

The reason for scaling the vorticity with
\sqrt{\nu}
is now apparent from the structure of the
variational form in (
2.10
). On the other hand, if we write instead
\bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}:=\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{u}
, then (
2.9
)
could be written as: Find
(\bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}},p)\in\mathbb{V}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{\nu}\int_{\Omega}\bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\theta}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}+\nabla p)\cdot(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q)+\nu^{-1}\int_{\Omega}(\bar{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\cdot(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{f}\cdot(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q)\;\;\;\;\forall(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\in\mathbb{V},

and the analysis of this problem
follows the same structure as that of (
2.9
).

Let us first provide an auxiliary result to be used in the derivation of

a priori
error estimates.

Lemma 2.2

The multilinear form
\mathcal{A}
satisfies the following bounds for all
(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\in\mathbb{V}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\theta},q),(\boldsymbol{\theta},q))

\displaystyle\geq\sigma\left(1-\frac{2\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}^{2}}{\nu\sigma}\right)\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q\|_{0,\Omega}^{2},

(2.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega},p),(\boldsymbol{\theta},q))

\displaystyle\leq\|(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)\|_{\mathbb{V}}\|(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

(2.14)

Proof.

From the definition of
\mathcal{A}(\cdot,\cdot),
we readily obtain the relation

\sigma\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\nu^{-1/2}\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\theta}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\cdot(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q)=\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\theta},q),(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)).

Subsequently, an appeal to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality leads to

\sigma\left(1-\frac{2\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}^{2}}{\nu\sigma}\right)\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\theta}+\nabla q\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\leq\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\theta},q),(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)).

(2.15)

Thus, (
2.13
) follows from (
2.15
), and
relation (
2.14
) follows directly form the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality. This completes the proof.

\square

As a consequence of Lemma
2.2
, we can readily derive the following result, stating the stability of problem (
2.9
).

Lemma 2.3

Assume that

2\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{\infty,\Omega}^{2}<\nu\sigma,

(2.16)

holds true.
Then, there exists
C>0
such that

\|(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq C\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}.

Proof.
Choose
(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)=(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)
in (
2.9
). From (
2.13
) with (
2.16
), and the bound

|\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)|\leq\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\;\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}+\nabla p\|_{0,\Omega}\leq\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)\|_{\mathbb{V}},

we obtain

\Big(\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}+\nabla p\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\Big)\leq C\;\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

Then, we note that

\|p\|_{0,\Omega}\leq C_{s}\;\|\nabla p\|_{{-1},\Omega},

and invoking the definition of the
\mathrm{H}^{-1}
-norm, it is observed that

\displaystyle\|\nabla p\|_{{-1},\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\{q\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\|\nabla q\|_{0,\Omega}=1\}}(\nabla p,\nabla q)_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\{q\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\|\nabla q\|_{0,\Omega}=1\}}\Big((\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}+\nabla p,\nabla q)_{0,\Omega}-(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega},\nabla q)_{0,\Omega}\Big).

Using integration by parts for the second term in this last relation, and using that

q\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as well as
\nabla\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}=0
,

(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega},\nabla q)=0
, we end up with

\displaystyle\|p\|_{0,\Omega}\leq C_{s}\|\nabla p\|_{{-1},\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}\;\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}+\nabla p\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

(2.17)

Altogether, it completes the rest of the proof.

\square

Theorem 2.1

Under the assumption (
2.16
), there exists a unique weak solution
(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)\in\mathbb{V}
to the problem (
2.9
), which depends continuously on
\boldsymbol{f}
.

The continuous dependence of the solution

. Likewise, a straightforward application of that result implies the uniqueness of solution.

with respect to

\square

3
Finite element discretisation and error estimates

This section focuses on finite element approximations and their
a priori
error estimates.

3.1
Galerkin scheme and solvability

Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)\}_{h>0}
be a shape-regular
family of partitions of the polyhedral region

\bar{\Omega}
, by tetrahedrons
T
of diameter
h_{T}
, with mesh size

h:=\max\{h_{T}:\;T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)\}
.
In what follows, given an integer
k\geq 1
and a subset

S
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
\mathcal{P}_{k}(S)
will denote the space of polynomial functions
defined locally in
S
and being of total degree
\leq k
.

Now, for any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)
we recall the definition of the local Nédélec space

\mathbb{N}_{k}(T):=\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(T)^{3}\oplus R_{k}(T),

where
R_{k}(T):=\{\boldsymbol{p}\in\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{k}(T)^{3}:\boldsymbol{p}(\boldsymbol{x})\cdot\boldsymbol{x}=0\}
, and where

\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
is the subset of homogeneous polynomials of degree
k
.
With this we define the discrete spaces for vorticity and Bernoulli pressure:

\displaystyle\mathbf{Z}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}:\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h}|_{T}\in\mathbb{N}_{k}(T)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)\},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Q}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\{q_{h}\in\mathrm{Q}:q_{h}|_{T}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(T)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)\},

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{V}_{h}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{Z}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h},

and remark that functions in
\mathbf{Z}_{h}
have continuous tangential components across
the faces of
\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)
.

Let us recall that for
s>1/2
, the Nédélec global interpolation operator

{\mathcal{N}}_{h}:\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)\to\mathbf{Z}_{h}
(cf.
[
2
]
), satisfies
the following approximation property:
For all
\boldsymbol{\theta}\in\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)
with
s\in(1/2,k]
,
there exists
C_{\mathrm{apx}}>0
independent of
h
, such that

\|\boldsymbol{\theta}-{\mathcal{N}}_{h}\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{\mathbf{Z}}\leq C_{\mathrm{apx}}\;h^{s}\|\boldsymbol{\theta}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)}.

(3.2)

On the other hand, for all
s>1/2
, the usual Lagrange interpolant

\Pi_{h}:\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)\cap\mathrm{Q}\to\mathrm{Q}_{h}
features a similar property. Namely:
For all
q\in\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)
,
s\in(1/2,k]

there exists
C_{\mathrm{apx}}>0
, independent of
h
, such that

\|q-\Pi_{h}q\|_{\mathrm{Q}}\leq C_{\mathrm{apx}}\;h^{s}\|q\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)}.

(3.3)

The Galerkin approximation of (
2.9
) reads:

Find
(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
such that

\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},q_{h}))=\mathcal{F}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},q_{h})\hskip 10.00002pt\forall(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

(3.4)

where the multilinear form
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\times\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
and the linear
functional
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
are specified as in (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), respectively.

Next, let us prove that the discrete formulation (
3.4
) is well-posed.

Before that, we address the stability of the
discrete problem.

Lemma 3.1

Under the assumption
(A)
, and
h>0
small enough,
there exists
C>0,
independent of
h,
such that

\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq C\;\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}.

Proof.

Choosing
(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},q_{h})=(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})
in (
3.4
), a use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with
the estimate

\displaystyle\nu^{-1/2}\int_{\Omega}(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}})\cdot(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}+\nabla p_{h})\leq

\displaystyle 2\;\nu^{-1/2}\;\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega},

yields

\displaystyle\sigma\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\leq\Big(\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+2\;\nu^{-1/2}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big)\;\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}.

(3.5)

By (
2.2
), it follows that

\displaystyle\|p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq C_{s}\;\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}.

And eventually we arrive at

\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq C\;\Big(\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\sigma^{-1/2}\;\nu^{-1/2}\sigma^{1/2}\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big).

(3.6)

In order to complete the proof, we require an estimate for
\sigma^{1/2}\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}
. For this we apply the Aubin-Nitsche duality argument
to the following adjoint problem:
Find
(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\widetilde{p})\in\mathbf{Z}\times\mathrm{Q}
such that

\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\theta},q),(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\widetilde{p}))=\sigma(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},\boldsymbol{\theta})_{0,\Omega}+(p_{h},q)_{0,\Omega}\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\forall(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\in\mathbf{Z}\times\mathrm{Q},

and whose solution (after assuming the natural additional regularity
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\mathrm{H}^{\delta}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)
and
\widetilde{p}\in\mathrm{H}^{1+\delta}(\Omega)
,
for some
\delta\in(1/2,1]
)
satisfies

\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\delta}{(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits,\Omega)}}+\|\widetilde{p}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1+\delta}{(\Omega)}}\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(\sigma^{1/2}\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}),

for
C_{\mathrm{reg}}>0
a uniform regularity constant.
Then, we set
(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)=(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})
and find out
that for all
(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
, the following relation holds:

\displaystyle\sigma\;\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}=\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h}),(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\widetilde{p}))=\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h}),(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},\widetilde{p}-q_{h}))-\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},\widetilde{p}-q_{h})+\mathcal{F}(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\widetilde{p})

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\sigma\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\Big(\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}+\nabla\widetilde{p}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h})+\nabla(\widetilde{p}-q_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}\Big)\;\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 10.00002pt+\Big(\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\nu^{-1/2}\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big)\;\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h})+\nabla(\widetilde{p}-q_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\;C_{\mathrm{apx}}\;\biggl(\sigma\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\Big(h^{\delta}\;\big(1+\sigma^{1/2}+\sigma^{-1/2}\;\nu^{-1/2}\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\big)\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big).

In this way,
we obtain

\biggl(\sigma\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}\;C_{\mathrm{apx}}\;\Big(h^{\delta}\;\Big(1+\sigma^{1/2}+\sigma^{-1/2}\;\nu^{-1/2}\|{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\Big)\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}+\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}\Big),

(3.7)

and on substitution of (
3.7
) into (
3.6
), we readily see that

\biggl(1-C_{s}\;C_{\mathrm{reg}}\;C_{\mathrm{apx}}\;\sigma^{-1/2}\;\nu^{-1/2}\;\Big(1+\sigma^{1/2}+\sigma^{-1/2}\;\nu^{-1/2}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\Big)\;h^{\delta}\biggr)\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\leq C\;\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}.

Therefore, there is a positive
h_{0}
such that for
0<h\leq h_{0}
, the following holds:

\biggl(1-C_{s}\;C_{\mathrm{reg}}\;C_{\mathrm{apx}}\;\sigma^{-1/2}\;\nu^{-1/2}\;\Big(1+\sigma^{1/2}+\sigma^{-1/2}\;\nu^{-1/2}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\Big)\;h^{\delta}\biggr)\;\geq\gamma_{0}>0,

for some positive
\gamma_{0},
independent of
h
. This completes the rest of the proof.

\square

Theorem 3.1

For
h>0
small enough,
the discrete problem (
3.4
) has a unique solution
(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
.

Proof.

Since the assembled discrete problem (
3.4
) is a square linear system,
it is enough to establish uniqueness of solution. Considering
\boldsymbol{f}=\boldsymbol{0}
and using
(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},q_{h}):=(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})
as a test
function in (
3.4
), the discrete stability result in Lemma
3.1
(which is valid assuming (
2.16
))
immediately implies that
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}=\boldsymbol{0}
and
p_{h}=0
, thus concluding the proof.

\square

Remark 3.1

When the condition (
2.16
) is satisfied, we modify the stability proof of Lemma
3.1
as follows:
From (
3.5
), using the Young inequality on the right-hand side

\displaystyle\sigma\Big(1-\frac{2\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}^{2}}{\sigma\nu}\Big)\|\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}+\nabla p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}\leq\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}.

In addition, applying Young’s inequality once again, and appealing to (
2.2
), we obtain the desired stability result.

Note that in this case, we do not need a smallness condition on the mesh parameter
h
.

3.2
A priori
error estimates

In this subsection, using a classical duality argument we
bound the error measured in the

\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm by the error in the norm
\|(\cdot,\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{V}}
. Then, we
establish an energy error estimate that eventually yields
an optimal bound in
\mathrm{L}^{2}
.

Let
(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)\in\mathbb{V}
and
(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
be the unique
solutions to the continuous and discrete problems (cf. (
2.9
) and (
3.4
)), respectively.
Then, we obtain

\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},q_{h}))=0\hskip 10.00002pt\forall(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},q_{h})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

(3.8)

Lemma 3.2
(An
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-estimate)

There exists
C>0
, independent of
h
, such that for

h
small enough, and

\delta\in(1/2,1]

\|\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq C\;h^{\delta}\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

Proof.

We appeal again to the Aubin-Nitsche duality argument.
For this, let us
consider the adjoint continuous problem:
Find
(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\widetilde{p})\in\mathbb{V}
such that

\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\theta},q),(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\widetilde{p}))=(\sigma(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}),\boldsymbol{\theta})_{0,\Omega}+(p-p_{h},q)_{0,\Omega}\hskip 10.00002pt\hskip 10.00002pt\forall(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\in\mathbb{V}.

(3.9)

In addition, let us suppose that (
3.9
) is well-posed
and that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\in\mathrm{H}^{\delta}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)

and
\widetilde{p}\in\mathrm{H}^{1+\delta}(\Omega)
, and there exists a constant
C_{\mathrm{reg}}>0
,
such that

\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{\delta}{(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits,\Omega)}}+\|\widetilde{p}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1+\delta}{(\Omega)}}\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}(\sigma^{1/2}\|\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}).

(3.10)

Next, we proceed to test the adjoint problem (
3.9
)
against
(\boldsymbol{\theta},q):=(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h})
and to use the
error equation (
3.8
) with

(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},q_{h})=({\mathcal{N}}_{h}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\Pi_{h}\widetilde{p})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}

to obtain that

\displaystyle\sigma\;\|\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}=\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}),(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}},\widetilde{p}))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}),(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},\widetilde{p}-q_{h}))

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+\sigma^{1/2}+\sigma^{-1/2}\;\nu^{-1/2}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\Big)\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\|(\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\omega}}-\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},\widetilde{p}-q_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\mathrm{reg}}C_{\mathrm{apx}}h^{\delta}\Big(1+\sigma^{1/2}+(\sigma\nu)^{-1/2}\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty,\Omega}\Big)\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\Big(\|\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}+\|p-p_{h}\|^{2}_{0,\Omega}\Big)^{1/2}.

Here, we have used (
3.2
) and (
3.3
)
with
s=\delta
and this completes the rest of the proof.

\square

An error estimate in the energy norm can also be derived
in the following manner.

Theorem 3.2

Assume
that problem (
2.9
) has a unique solution
(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)
satisfying
the additional regularity

\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)
and
p\in\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)
,
for some
s\in(1/2,k]
. Then, there exists
C>0
,
independent of
h
, such that the following error estimates hold for
h
small enough:

\displaystyle\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,h^{s}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)}+\|p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)}\right),

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\leq C\,h^{s+\delta}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)}+\|p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)}\right),

where
(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
is the unique
solution to (
3.4
) with
s=\delta,
if
s\in(1/2,1)
and
\delta=1,
if
s\in[1,k].

Proof.

Now rewrite (
3.8
), then use boundedness, (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) to arrive at

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}))=\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{\omega}-{\mathcal{N}}_{h}\boldsymbol{\omega},p-\Pi_{h}p))

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-{\mathcal{N}}_{h}\boldsymbol{\omega},p-\Pi_{h}p)\|_{\mathbb{V}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\hskip 20.00003pt\leq C\,h^{s}\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h})\|_{\mathbb{V}}\left(\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)}+\|p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)}\right).

Next, for the term on the left-hand side of (
3.11
), apply (
2.13
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}))

\displaystyle\geq 1/2\;\Big(\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}-(1+\sigma^{-1}\;\nu^{-1}\;\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty})\;(\sigma\|\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2}+\|p-p_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}^{2})\Big).

Then, a use of Lemma
3.2
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}((\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}),(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h}))

\displaystyle\geq 1/2\;\Big(1-C(1+\sigma^{-1}\;\nu^{-1}\;\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty})\;h^{2\delta}\Big)\;\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p-p_{h})\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}}.

Choosing
h
small, the term within brackets,
(1-C(1+\sigma^{-1}\;\nu^{-1}\;\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_{\infty})\;h^{2\delta})
, can be made positive; therefore, concluding the proof.

\square

As a consequence of Theorem
3.2
, with
e_{\boldsymbol{\omega}}:=\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
and
e_{p}:=p-p_{h}
, we obtain the following inf-sup condition:
There exists
\gamma_{0}>0,
independent of
h,
such that,

\sup_{(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\in\mathbb{V}}\;\frac{\mathcal{A}((e_{\boldsymbol{\omega}},e_{p}),(\boldsymbol{\theta},q))}{\|(\boldsymbol{\theta},q)\|_{\mathbb{V}}}\geq\gamma_{0}\;\|(e_{\boldsymbol{\omega}},e_{p})\|_{\mathbb{V}}.

(3.12)

3.3
Convergence of the post-processed velocity

Let
(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
be
the unique solution of the discrete problem (
3.4
). Then following (
2.12
), we can recover the
discrete velocity as the following element-wise discontinuous function for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)
:

\boldsymbol{u}_{h}|_{T}:=\sigma^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}-\nu^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}}-(\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}+\nabla p_{h})\right)|_{T},

(3.13)

where
\mathcal{P}_{h}:\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}\to\mathbf{U}_{h}
is the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-orthogonal projector, with

\mathbf{U}_{h}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{3}:\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|_{T}\in\mathcal{P}_{k-1}(T)^{3}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)\}.

(3.14)

Consequently, we can state an error estimate
for the post-processed velocity.

Theorem 3.3

Let
(\boldsymbol{\omega},p)\in\mathbb{V}

be the unique solution of (
2.9
), and
(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbb{V}_{h}

be the unique solution of (
3.4
). Assume that
\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)
,
p\in\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)

and
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega)^{3}
,
for some
s\in(1/2,k]
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C
,
independent of
h
, such that

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}\leq Ch^{s}\left(\|\boldsymbol{f}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s}(\Omega)}+\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|_{\mathrm{H}^{s}(\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits;\Omega)}+\|p\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1+s}(\Omega)}\right).

Proof.

From (
2.12
), (
3.13
), and triangle inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt\leq\frac{1}{\sigma}\bigl(\|\boldsymbol{f}-\mathcal{P}_{h}\boldsymbol{f}\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}-\boldsymbol{\omega})-\nabla(p-p_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\nu}}\|(\boldsymbol{\omega}-\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h})\times{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|_{0,\Omega}\bigr).

Then, the result follows from standard estimates
satisfied by
\mathcal{P}_{h}
, as well as from Theorem
3.2
.

\square

An issue with the post-process (
3.13
) is that it requires
numerical differentiation (taking the curl of
\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}
and the gradient of
p_{h}
).
A possible way to getting around this problem is to set

\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{h}:=\{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathrm{H}_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{3}:\boldsymbol{v}_{h}|_{T}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(T)^{3}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)\},

and recover the discrete velocity in this space, using
the discrete versions of (
2.3
), (
2.4
), and (
2.5
).

This results in finding
\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{h}
such
that

\nu\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h}+\nu\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\,\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\sqrt{\nu}\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{\omega}_{h}\cdot\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{U}}_{h}.

(3.15)

The discrete velocity produced by (
3.15
) gives not only

\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{u}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}+\|\sqrt{\nu}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\boldsymbol{u}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h})\|_{0,\Omega}=\mathcal{O}(h^{s}),

but also, thanks to the identity relating vector Laplacians with curl and divergence
-\Delta\Phi=\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\mathop{\mathbf{curl}}\nolimits\Phi-\nabla(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\Phi)
,
one can show, using duality arguments, that

\|\boldsymbol{u}-\tilde{\boldsymbol{u}}_{h}\|_{0,\Omega}=\mathcal{O}(h^{s+\delta}),

where
s
and
\delta
are given as in Theorem
3.2
.

4
A posteriori
error analysis for the 2D problem

In this section, we
propose a residual-based
a posteriori
error estimator. For sake of clarity, we restrict our
analysis to the two-dimensional case (the extension to 3D can be carried out in a similar fashion).
Therefore, the functional space
\mathbf{Z}
considered in the

,
and

\mathbf{Z}_{h}:=\{\theta_{h}\in\mathbf{Z}:\theta_{h}|_{T}\in\mathcal{P}_{k}(T)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}(\Omega)\}.

(4.1)

We note that in the 2D case, the duality arguments presented
in Section
3
, hold for any
\delta\in(0,1]
.
In particular, this fact will be considered in the definition
of the local
a posteriori
error indicator.
Moreover, to keep the notation clear, in this section
we will denote by
{\mathcal{N}}_{h}
the usual Lagrange interpolant in
\mathbf{Z}_{h}
.

For each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}

{\mathcal{E}}(T)
be the set of edges of

, and we denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}

.
In what follows,
h_{e}
stands for the diameter of a given edge

e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
,
\boldsymbol{t}_{e}=(-n_{2},n_{1})
, where
\boldsymbol{n}_{e}=(n_{1},n_{2})

is a fix unit normal vector of
e
.
Now, let
q\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
such that
q|_{T}\in C(T)
for each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,
then, given
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}(\Omega)
, we denote by
[q]
the jump of
q
across
e
,
that is
[q]:=(q|_{T^{\prime}})|_{e}-(q|_{T^{\prime\prime}})|_{e}
, where
T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime\prime}
are the triangles
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
sharing the edge
e
. Moreover,
let
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{2}
such that
\boldsymbol{v}|_{T}\in C(T)^{2}
for each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.
Then, given
e\in{\mathcal{E}}_{h}(\Omega)
, we denote by
[\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}]
the tangential
jump of
\boldsymbol{v}
across
e
,
that is,
[\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}]:=\left((\boldsymbol{v}|_{T^{\prime}})|_{e}-(\boldsymbol{v}|_{T^{\prime\prime}})|_{e}\right)\cdot\boldsymbol{t}_{e}
,
where
T^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime\prime}
are the triangles
of
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
sharing the edge
e
.

Next, let
k\geq 1
be an integer and let
\mathbf{Z}_{h},\mathrm{Q}_{h}
and
\mathbf{U}_{h}

be given by (
4.1
), (
3.1
), and (
3.14
), respectively.
Let
(\omega,p)\in\mathbf{Z}\times\mathrm{Q}
and

(\omega_{h},p_{h})\in\mathbf{Z}_{h}\times\mathrm{Q}_{h}
be the unique solutions
to the continuous and discrete problems (
2.9
) and
(

(4.12)

Remark 4.1

Note that the a posteriori lower bound derived in Theorem
4.3
is valid only upon the assumption of
\mathrm{H}^{2}
-regularity, that is, for
\delta=1
. When
\delta\in(0,1)
, obtaining an efficiency result for the a posteriori error indicator in the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm is much more involved, essentially due to the presence of corner singularities. For instance, a
reliable and efficient estimators using weighted
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norms is available for the Poisson equation in
[

]
. A similar analysis could eventually be carried out in the present case, provided an additional regularity is established using weighted Sobolev spaces and appropriate interpolation results. However here we restrict ourselves only to verifying these properties numerically in the next Section.

In addition, the result of Theorem
4.2
does indicate that the estimator is quasi-efficient, as the error in the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm,
\|(\sigma^{1/2}e_{\omega},e_{p})\|_{0,\Omega}

C\;\|(e_{\omega},e_{p})\|_{0,\Omega}.

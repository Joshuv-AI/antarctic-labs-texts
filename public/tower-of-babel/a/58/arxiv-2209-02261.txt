Character formulas in category 𝒪 p \mathcal{O}_{p}
Henning Haahr Andersen

Abstract
. Let 𝒪 p \mathcal{O}_{p} denote the characteristic p > 0 p>0 version of the ordinary category 𝒪 \mathcal{O} for a semisimple complex Lie algebra. In this paper we give some (formal) character formulas in 𝒪 p \mathcal{O}_{p} . First we concentrate on the irreducible characters. Here we give explicit formulas for how to obtain all irreducible characters from the characters of the finitely many restricted simple modules as well as the characters of a small number of infinite dimensional simple modules in 𝒪 p \mathcal{O}_{p} with specified highest weights. We next prove a strong linkage principle for Verma modules which allow us to split 𝒪 p \mathcal{O}_{p} into a finite direct sum of linkage classes. There are corresponding translation functors and we use these to further cut down the set of irreducible characters needed for determining all others. Then we show that the twisting functors on 𝒪 \mathcal{O} carry over to twisting functors on 𝒪 p \mathcal{O}_{p} , and as an application we prove a character sum formula for Jantzen-type filtrations of Verma modules with antidominant highest weights. Finally, we record formulas relating the characters of the two kinds of tilting modules in 𝒪 p \mathcal{O}_{p} .



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple complex Lie algebra. Consider a field
K
of characteristic
p>0
,
and let
G
be the semisimple algebraic group over
K
corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}
. Denote by
U_{K}
the hyperalgebra over
K
for
G
, see
[
15
]
, I.7.7. In
[
3
]
we proved some basic properties of the category
\mathcal{O}_{p}
for
U_{K}
as well as for the corresponding quantum version
\mathcal{O}_{q}
, when
q
is a root of unity in
K
. Here we have chosen to focus on
\mathcal{O}_{p}
although many (maybe all) of our results work for
\mathcal{O}_{q}
as well. We shall use the notation and setup from
[
3
]
, but simplify notation by dropping the bars, i.e. writing just
\mathcal{O}_{p},L_{p}(\lambda)
, etc. instead of
\bar{\mathcal{O}_{p}},\bar{L}_{p}(\lambda)
, etc. Along the way we recall some of the key results from
[
3
]
that we need, referring to that paper for further statements and details. Additional background and results can be found in
[
11
]
and
[
13
]
.

We shall use in our context the general concept of (formal) characters introduced in
[
10
]
, Section 3. This involves in particular working with a completion of the character ring for finite dimensional modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
, cf. also
[
13
]
, Section 2.5. Our aim is to deduce some explicit formulas which allow us to determine characters for general modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
from the knowledge of the characters of a finite number of simple modules. The necessary background and methods are dealt with in Section 2 where we have also deduced the mentioned formulas for irreducible characters.

Then in Section 3 we explore further the linkage principle for Verma modules from
[
3
]
. We prove a stronger version of this principle using the concept from
[
10
]
of components (or composition factors) of modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
. This allow us to decompose
\mathcal{O}_{p}
into a direct sum of linkage classes, one for each of the (finitely many) weights in the closure of the fundamental alcove
\bar{C}
for the affine Weyl group. We can then introduce corresponding translation functors on
\mathcal{O}_{p}
. As a consequence we then demonstrate that when combined with our formulas in Section 2 we can obtain all irreducible characters from the knowledge of one such character for each facet in
\bar{C}
.

Another tool available in the study of
\mathcal{O}
is the socalled twisting functors, see
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
. In Section 4 we show that we have analogous twisting functors on
\mathcal{O}_{p}
and in Section 5 we use these to deduce a character sum formula for Jantzen-type filtrations of Verma modules with antidominant highest weights. Finally, in Section 6 we give some formulas for tilting characters. In particular, we record the fact, that at least for
p\geq 2h-2
the characters of
\infty
-tilting modules are determined by the characters of finite dimensional tilting modules.

2.
Characters of simple modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}

Let
n
denote the rank of
\mathfrak{g}
and set
X={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
. We write
R\subset X
for the root system for
\mathfrak{g}
and choose a set of positive roots
R^{+}
. Then we denote by
S=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\}
the set of simple roots in
R^{+}
. We let
W
denote the Weyl group for
R
. Then
W
acts naturally on
X
. We shall also consider the “dot”-action of
W
on
X
given by
w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho
,
\lambda\in X
. Here
\rho
is half the sum of all positive roots.

The order on
X
induced by
S
is denoted
\leq
and if
\mu\in X
we set

X(\leq\mu)=\{\lambda\mid\lambda\leq\mu\}.

Observe that for any
\lambda,\mu\in X
the interval
\{\nu\in X\mid\lambda\leq\nu\leq\mu\}
is always finite (possibly empty).

2.1.
The category
\mathcal{O}_{p}

Recall that
U_{K}
has a triangular decomposition
U_{K}=U_{K}^{-}U_{K}^{0}U_{K}^{+}
, cf.
[
3
]
, and set
B_{K}=U_{K}^{0}U_{K}^{+}
. Using the above notation we may define
\mathcal{O}_{p}
as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

The category
\mathcal{O}_{p}
is the full subcategory of the category of
U_{K}
-modules consisting of those
M
, which satisfy

(1)

M
is a weight module, i.e.
M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in X}M_{\lambda}
as a
U_{K}^{0}
-module.

(2)

\dim_{k}(M_{\lambda})<\infty
for all
\lambda
, and there exist finitely many
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\cdots,\mu_{r}\in X
such that if
M_{\lambda}\neq 0
then
\lambda\in X(\leq\mu_{1})\cup X(\leq\mu_{2})\cup\cdots\cup X(\leq\mu_{r})
.

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

This is equivalent to
[
3
]
, Definition 2.1 once one observes that condition (3) in that definition is contained in conditions (1) and (2) above. In fact, let
m\in M
be non-zero. To prove that
\dim(B_{K}m)<\infty
it is by (1) enough to verify this for
m\in M_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in X
. However,
B_{K}m\subset\oplus_{\lambda\leq\mu}M_{\mu}
and if
M_{\mu}\neq 0
we know by (2) that
\mu\leq\mu_{i}
for some
i
. Hence the non-zero terms in this direct sum only involves
\mu
’s belonging to the finitely many intervals
[\lambda,\mu_{i}],\;i=1,2,\cdots r
. By (2) all weight spaces
M_{\mu}
for
M
are finite dimensional, so we see that
\dim_{K}B_{K}m
is indeed finite dimensional.

(2)

In Definition 2.1 (2) we may always choose the
\mu_{i}
’s in different equivalences classes for
{\mathbb{Z}}R
(cf. the arguments verifying Remark
3.9
below). In particular, the number
r
can always be taken to be at most equal to the index of connection
|X:{\mathbb{Z}}R|
for all
M
.

Observe that
\mathcal{O}_{p}
is an abelian category. Moreover, it is also closed under taking (finite) tensor products. If
M,N\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
have weights contained in
\cup_{i=1}^{r}X({\leq\mu_{i}})
and
\cup_{j=1}^{s}X({\leq\nu_{j}})
, respectively, then the weights of
M\otimes_{K}N
are contained in
\cup_{i,j}X(\leq\mu_{i}+\nu_{j})
.

Among the modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
we have the finite dimensional modules. These form a subcategory
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{O}_{p}
, which identifies with the category of finite dimensional rational
G
-modules (studied in full details in
[
15
]
).

2.2.
Formal characters

Using the general concept “formal characters” introduced in
[
10
]
, Section 3 we shall now define characters of the modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
. The same approach is also used in
[
13
]
, Section 2.5. Note that here and in the future we write just characters (dropping the word “formal”).

If
f:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is a map then we set
\mathrm{supp}(f)=\{\lambda\in X\mid f(\lambda)\neq 0\}
. We define

\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}=\{f:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\mid\text{ there exist }\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}\in X\text{ such that }\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset X(\leq\mu_{1})\cup\cdots\cup X(\leq\mu_{r})\}.

Let
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
. Then we define the character of
M
to be the map
\mathrm{ch}M:X\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
given by

\mathrm{ch}M(\lambda)=\dim_{K}M_{\lambda},\,\lambda\in X.

Note that the finiteness condition (2) in Definition
2.1
ensures that
\mathrm{ch}M\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
. We have that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathrm{ch}M)
is the set of weights of
M
.

Pointwise addition makes
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
into an abelian group.
Clearly, taking characters is additive, i.e. if

0\to M_{1}\to M\to M_{2}\to 0

is a short exact sequence in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
, then
\mathrm{ch}M=\mathrm{ch}M_{1}+\mathrm{ch}M_{2}
.

We also have a product
\star
on
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
. It is defined by

f\star g(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\in X}f(\mu)g(\lambda-\mu),\;\lambda\in X.

Observe that when
f,g\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
the sum on the right hand side is finite. Hence
f\star g
is a well defined element of
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
. In this way
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
becomes a commutative ring. Its
1
-element is
\mathrm{ch}K
where
K
denotes the trivial
1
-dimensional
U_{K}
-module.
Note also that if
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
then we have

\mathrm{ch}(M_{1}\otimes M_{2})=(\mathrm{ch}M_{1})\star(\mathrm{ch}M_{2}).

For later use we also record the fact that
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
is closed under certain infinite sums. Namely, suppose
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of elements in
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
which satisfies

(1)

there exist
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}\in X
such that
\mathrm{supp}(f_{i})\subset X(\leq\mu_{1})\cup\cdots\cup X(\leq\mu_{r})
for all
i\in I,

(2)

for any
\lambda\in X
we have
\lambda\in\mathrm{supp}(f_{i})
for at most finitely many
i\in I
.

If (1) and (2) are satisfied the family
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
is called
summable
. In that case
the (possibly infinite) sum
\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}
equals the map
f\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
defined by

f(\lambda)=\sum_{i\in I}f_{i}(\lambda),\;\lambda\in X.

Let
\lambda\in X
. Define
e^{\lambda}
to be the characteristic function on
\{\lambda\}\subset X
, i.e.
e^{\lambda}(\mu)=\delta_{\mu,\lambda}
. Then for any
f\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
the family

(f(\lambda)e^{\lambda})_{\lambda\in X}
is summable (and has sum
f
). In fact, this family is summable if and only if
f\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. Then for any map
f:X\to{\mathbb{Z}}
we define
f^{(r)}:X\to{\mathbb{Z}}
by

f^{(r)}(\lambda)=f(p^{r}\lambda),\;\lambda\in X.

Clearly,
\mathrm{supp}f^{(r)}=p^{r}\mathrm{supp}(f)
. In particular, we see that
f\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
if and only if
f^{(r)}\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
.

Recall from
[
3
]
, 3.6 that for each
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
we have the Frobenius twists
M^{(r)}\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
,
r\geq 0
. Clearly, the characters of these twisted modules are given by

\mathrm{ch}M^{(r)}=(\mathrm{ch}M)^{(r)}.

We shall often write
\mathrm{ch}^{(r)}M
instead of
\mathrm{ch}(M)^{(r)}
.

2.3.
Verma modules and simple modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}

Recall that the Verma module
\Delta_{p}(\lambda)
in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
with highest weight
\lambda\in X
is defined by

\Delta_{p}(\lambda)=U_{K}\otimes_{B_{K}}\lambda,

where
\lambda
is considered a
B_{K}
-module via the natural projection
B_{K}\to U_{K}^{0}
.
It follows immediately from this definition that as a
U^{-}_{K}U^{0}_{K}
-module we have
\Delta_{p}(\lambda)\simeq U_{K}^{-}\otimes_{K}\lambda
. Hence we get from the PBW-basis of
U_{K}^{-}
the following character formula (with
e^{\mu}
as in Section
2.2
)

(2.1)

\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\leq\lambda}P(\lambda-\mu)e^{\mu},

where
P
is the Kostant partition function.

Each Verma module
\Delta_{p}(\lambda)
has a unique simple quotient which we denote
L_{p}(\lambda)
. Then clearly the weights of
L_{p}(\lambda)
are also
\leq\lambda
, and the multiplicity of
\lambda
as a weight of
L_{p}(\lambda)
is
1
. Up to isomorphisms the set
\{L_{p}(\lambda)\mid\lambda\in X\}
is the family of all simple modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
.

2.4.
Steinberg’s tensor product theorem and character formulas

As in
[
3
]
we set

X^{+}=\{\lambda\in X\mid\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle\geq 0\text{ for all }i\},

and for
r\geq 0

X_{r}=\{\lambda\in X\mid 0\leq\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle<p^{r}\text{ for all }i\}.

X^{+}
is the set of dominant weights and the elements of
X_{r}
are called the
p^{r}
-restricted weights (here we have deviated from the notation used in
[
3
]
).

For a fixed
r
we can for each
\lambda\in X
write
\lambda=\lambda^{0}+p^{r}\lambda^{1}
for unique
\lambda^{0}\in X_{r}
and
\lambda^{1}\in X
. Note that
\lambda^{0}
and
\lambda^{1}
depend on
r
. In the formulas below it should be clear from the context which
r
we are working with (special care should be taken in Theorem
2.3
below where we have abused notation further).

Steinberg’s tensor product theorem, Theorem 3.6 in
[
3
]
, for
\mathcal{O}_{p}
then says that we have an isomorphism

(2.2)

L_{p}(\lambda)\simeq L_{p}(\lambda^{0})\otimes L_{p}(\lambda^{1})^{(r)}.

Recall that
L_{p}(\lambda)
is finite dimensional if and only if
\lambda\in X^{+}
. For such
\lambda
we write
\lambda=\lambda^{0}+p\lambda^{1}+\cdots+p^{s}\lambda^{s}
with
\lambda^{i}\in X_{1}
for all
i=1,\cdots,s
and get the following well known formula for the characters of finite dimensional simple modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
(i.e for the finite dimensional simple
G
-modules), cf.
[
18
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\lambda\in X^{+}
. Then with the above notation

\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\lambda)=\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\lambda^{0})\star\mathrm{ch}^{(1)}L_{p}(\lambda^{1})\star\cdots\star\mathrm{ch}^{(s)}L_{p}(\lambda^{s}).

Proof.

Iterate the
s=1
case of (
2.2
) and use the formulas from Section
2.2
.
∎

Suppose next
\lambda\in X
is arbitrary. Set

Y=\{\omega\in X\mid\langle\omega,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle\in\{-1,0\}\text{ for all }i=1,\cdots,n\}.

Then we have

Theorem 2.4
.

For
\lambda\in X
we have for all
r\geq\mathrm{min}\{s\mid-p^{s}>\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle>p^{s}\text{ for all }i\}
that
\lambda=\lambda^{0}+p^{r}\omega
for unique
\lambda^{0}\in X_{r}
and
\omega\in Y
, and

\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\lambda)=\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\lambda^{0})\star\mathrm{ch}^{(r)}L_{p}(\omega).

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of (
2.2
).
∎

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\omega\in Y
. Then
(1-p^{r})\omega\in X_{r}
and hence Steinberg’s tensor product theorem tells us that
L_{p}(\omega)\simeq L_{p}((1-p^{r})\omega)\otimes L_{p}(\omega)^{(r)}
. Hence

\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\omega)=\mathrm{ch}L_{p}((1-p^{r})\omega)\star\mathrm{ch}^{(r)}\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\omega)
.

Observe that if
\lambda
is antidominant (i.e.
\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle<0
for all
i=1,\cdots,n
), then the
\omega\in Y
appearing in Theorem
2.4
equals
-\rho
. According to
[
3
]
, Theorem 6.1 we have
L_{p}(-\rho)=\Delta_{p}(\-\rho)
. Hence in this case Theorem
2.4
gives the following formula.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\lambda\in X
be antidominant and take
r
so large that
p^{r}\rho+\lambda\in X^{+}
. Then we have

\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\lambda)=\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(p^{r}\rho+\lambda)\star\mathrm{ch}^{(r)}L_{p}(-\rho)=\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(p^{r}\rho+\lambda)\star(\sum_{\mu\geq 0}P(\mu)e^{-p^{r}(\rho+\mu)}).

Remark 2.7
.

(1)

Assume that
\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\nu)
are known for all
\nu
in the finite set
X_{1}
(this is equivalent to knowing all irreducible
G_{1}T
characters,
G_{1}
being the Frobenius subgroup scheme of
G
and
T
is a maximal torus in
G
). Then Theorem
2.3
, respectively Corollary
2.6
, gives explicit formulas for how to obtain from this the characters of all simple modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
with dominant, respectively antidominant, highest weights.

(2)

Assume that
\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\nu)
are known for all
\nu
in the finite set
X_{1}\cup Y
. Then Theorem
2.4
gives explicit formulas for how to obtain from this the characters of all other simple modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
.

(3)

Recall that
[
3
]
, Corollary 3.10 gives recipes for determining all the remaining irreducible characters in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
from the assumption in (1) above, cf. also the periodicy theorem in
[
13
]
. However, in contrast to our formulas above, these recipes work one weight at a time.

(4)

The recent breakthrough, see
[
16
]
, determines for
p\leq 2h-1
the characters
\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\nu))
for all
\nu\in X^{+}

in terms of the
p
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials. Hence by (3) this leads to the determination of all simple characters in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
.

3.
The linkage principle in
\mathcal{O}_{p}

In this section we shall show that
\mathcal{O}_{p}
breaks up into a direct sum of subcategories corresponding to the orbits in
X
of the affine Weyl group. This result is analogous to the splitting of the ordinary category
\mathcal{O}
, and it is an even closer analogue of the situation in the category
\mathcal{C}
of finite dimensional rational representations of
G
, see
[
15
]
, II.6. As an immediate consequence of this result we get translation functors in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
(as in
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{C}
).

3.1.
Local composition series

Modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
do in general not have composition series in the usual sense, see e.g. the examples in
[
3
]
, Section 5. However, at any given weight we have a local composition series in the following sense:

Proposition 3.1
.

(
[
10
]
, Proposition 3.2)
Let
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
and
\lambda\in X
. Then there exists a finite filtration

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{r}=M

of submodules of
M
and a subset
J\subset\{1,2,\cdots,r\}
such that

(1)

if
i\in J
then
M_{i}/M_{i-1}\simeq L_{p}(\lambda_{i})
for some
\lambda_{i}\in X
,

(2)

if
i\not\in J
then
(M_{i}/M_{i-1})_{\mu}=0
for all
\mu\geq\lambda
.

A filtration like the one in this proposition is called a
local composition series
for
M
at
\lambda
.

Example 1
.

Let
G=SL_{2}(K)
. In this case
X={\mathbb{Z}}
. Take
r\geq 0
. Then the results in
[
3
]
, Section 5.2 give

(1)

The Verma module
\Delta_{p}(0)
has a filtration
0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{r+2}=\Delta_{p}(0)
with
M_{i}/M_{i-1}=L_{p}(-2p^{i-1}),\;i=1,2,\cdots,r
and
M_{r+1}=\mathrm{rad}\Delta_{p}(0).

This is a local composition series for
\Delta_{p}(0)
at
-2p^{r}+2
. The associated subset consisting of all
i
for which
M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is simple is
J=\{1,2,\cdots r,r+2\}.

(2)

The Verma module
\Delta_{p}(-2)
has a filtration
0=N_{0}\subset N_{1}\subset\cdots\subset N_{r+1}=\Delta_{p}(2)
with
N_{1}=\Delta_{p}(-2)^{(r)},\;N_{i}/N_{i-1}=L_{p}(-2p^{r+1-i}),\;i=2,\cdots,r+1
. This is a local decomposition series for
\Delta_{p}(-2)
at
-2p^{r}+2
with associated subset
J=\{2,\cdots,r+1\}.

3.2.
Composition factor multiplicities

Recall from Section
2.2
the concept of summability of certain families of elements in
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
. Using this it follows as in
[
10
]
, Section 3 that for each
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
there exist unique non-negative numbers
([M:L_{p}(\mu)])_{\mu\in X}
such that the family
([M:L_{p}(\mu)]\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\mu))_{\mu\in X}
is summable and the following formula holds in
\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}

\mathrm{ch}M=\sum_{\mu\in X}[M:L_{p}(\mu)]\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\mu).

Suppose
\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{r}
are elements of
X
such that all weights of
M
are contained in the set
X(\leq\mu_{1})\cup\cdots\cup X(\leq\mu_{r})
. Then we have

[M:L_{p}(\mu)]>0\text{ implies }\mu\in X(\leq\mu_{1})\cup\cdots\cup X(\leq\mu_{r}).

We shall call
[M:L_{p}(\mu)]
the composition factor multiplicity of
L_{p}(\mu)
in
M
and say that
L_{p}(\mu)
is a composition factor of
M
whenever this number is non-zero. Note that this is slightly misleading as
M
may not have a composition series in the usual sense, cf. Example
1
above. In
[
10
]
the term ‘simple component’ is used instead of ‘composition factor’.

Composition factors clearly have the following property.

Remark 3.2
.

For all
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
the simple module
L_{p}(\lambda)
is a composition factor of
M
if and only if
L_{p}(\lambda)
is a subquotient of
M
.

Let now
\lambda,\mu\in X
and consider
M=\Delta_{p}(\lambda)
. Set
a_{\mu,\lambda}=[\Delta_{p}(\lambda):L_{p}(\mu)]\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
. Then
a_{\mu,\lambda}=0
unless
\mu\leq\lambda
,
a_{\lambda,\lambda}=1
, and we have

\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu}a_{\mu,\lambda}\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\mu).

Fix now
\eta\in X
and consider the (infinite) matrix
(a_{\mu,\lambda})_{\mu,\lambda\in X(\leq\eta)}
. Then by the above observations this is an upper triangular matrix (wrt the order
\leq
on
(X(\leq\eta)
) with
1
’s on the diagonal. Hence it is invertible and we denote its inverse by
(c_{\mu,\lambda})_{\mu,\lambda\in X(\leq\eta)}
. This is also an upper triangular matrix with
1
’s on the diagonal and with all entries in
{\mathbb{Z}}
. If we take
\eta\geq\lambda
then we have

\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu}c_{\mu,\lambda}\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\mu).

These equations make explicit how we can obtain the irreducible characters in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
once we know the composition factor multiplicities of Verma modules - and vice versa. Note that the integers

a_{\mu,\lambda}
and
c_{\mu,\lambda}
are independent of
\eta
(as long as
\lambda\leq\eta
).

3.3.
The strong linkage principle for Verma modules

Recall the following result from
[
3
]
, Theorem 4.5

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\lambda\in X
and
r\in Z_{\geq 0}
. Then
\Delta_{p}(\lambda)
has a finite filtration with subquotients isomorphic to
L_{p}(\mu^{0})\otimes\Delta_{p}(\mu^{1})^{(r)}
for some
\mu\in X
. Moreover, if
L_{p}(\mu^{0})\otimes\Delta_{p}(\mu^{1})^{(r)}
occurs at least once as such a subquotient, then
\mu
is strongly linked to
\lambda
.

We deduce the following corollary

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in X
. If
[\Delta_{p}(\lambda):L_{p}(\mu)]\neq 0
then
\mu
is strongly linked to
\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
0=F_{0}\subset F_{1}\subset\cdots\subset F_{s}=\Delta_{p}(\lambda)
be a filtration as in the
r=1
case of Theorem
3.3
with
F_{i}/F_{i-1}\simeq L_{p}(\mu_{i}^{0})\otimes\Delta_{p}(\mu_{i}^{1})^{(1)},i=1,\cdots,s
.
Then the additivity of
\mathrm{ch}
gives

\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mathrm{ch}(L_{p}(\mu_{i}^{0})\otimes\Delta_{p}(\mu_{i}^{1})^{(1)}).

As each
\mu_{i}
is strongly linked to
\lambda
(by Theorem
3.3
) we are done if we prove

[L_{p}(\mu_{i}^{0})\otimes\Delta_{p}(\mu_{i}^{1})^{(1)}:L_{p}(\mu)]\neq 0\text{ implies }\mu\text{ is strongly linked to }\mu_{i}.

However, the composition factors of
L_{p}(\mu_{i}^{0})\otimes\Delta_{p}(\mu_{i}^{1})^{(1)}
have the form
L_{p}(\mu_{i}^{0})\otimes L_{p}(\nu)^{(1)}
where
L_{p}(\nu)
is a composition factor of
\Delta_{p}(\mu_{i}^{1})
. The last condition implies
\nu\leq\mu_{i}^{1}
so that we have
\mu_{i}^{0}+p\nu
is strongly linked to
\mu_{i}^{0}+p\mu_{i}^{1}=\mu_{i}
.
∎

3.4.
The duality functor

Recall that just as in ordinary category
\mathcal{O}
we have a duality functor
D:\mathcal{O}_{p}\to\mathcal{O}_{p}
, see
[
3
]
, Remark 4.11. As
D
preserves weight spaces we have

\mathrm{ch}DM=\mathrm{ch}M\text{ for all }M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}.

and hence also

[DM:L_{p}(\lambda)]=[M:L_{p}(\lambda)]\text{ for all }\lambda\in X.

As in
[
3
]
we write
\nabla_{p}(\lambda)
instead of
D\Delta_{p}(\lambda)
. By the above observations we see that the strong linkage principle holds just as well for the dual Verma modules.

For later use we record the following result, cf.
[
3
]
, Lemma 8.7(2),

Lemma 3.5
.

For all
\lambda,\mu\in X
we have
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\lambda),\nabla_{p}(\mu))=0
.

3.5.
The decomposition of
\mathcal{O}_{p}
into linkage classes

Denote by
W_{p}
the affine Weyl group corresponding to
R
. This group acts on
X
by the dot action
w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho
, see
[
15
]
, II.6.1. Clearly, if
\mu
is strongly linked to
\lambda
, then we have in particular
\mu\in W_{p}\cdot\lambda
.

Let
C=\{\nu\in X^{+}|\langle\nu+\rho,\beta^{\vee}\rangle<p\text{ for all }\beta\in R^{+}\}
be the bottom alcove in
X^{+}
and set

\bar{C}=\{\nu\in X|0\leq\langle\nu+\rho,\beta^{\vee}\rangle\leq p\text{ for all }\beta\in R^{+}\}.

Then
\bar{C}
is a fundamental domain for the action of
W_{p}
on
X
. If
\nu\in\bar{C}
we define
\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
to be the full subcategory of
\mathcal{O}_{p}
given by

\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)=\{M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}|[M:L_{p}(\mu)]=0\text{ unless }\mu\in W_{p}\cdot\nu\}.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
. Then there exists a unique set
(M(\nu))_{\nu\in\bar{C}}
of submodules of
M
with

M(\nu)\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)\text{ for all }\nu\in\bar{C}\text{ and }M=\bigoplus_{\nu\in\bar{C}}M(\nu).

The proof that we present below is obtained by specializing/simplifying the arguments in
[
10
]
, Section 4. First we prove

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\nu\in\bar{C}
and
\mu\in W_{p}\cdot\nu
. If
N\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
satisfies
[N:L_{p}(w\cdot\nu)]=0
for all
w\in W_{p}
then

(1)

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\mu),N)=0
,

(2)

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\mu),N)=0

Proof.

(1)
follows from the observation that if
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\mu),N)\neq 0
then
L_{p}(\mu)
is a subquotient of
N
contradicting our assumption on
N
.

To prove
(2)
we begin by noting that

(3.1)

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\mu),L_{p}(\lambda))=0\text{ for all }\lambda\text{ for which }[N:L_{p}(\lambda)]\neq 0.

In fact, we get from Lemma
3.5
a surjection
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\mu),\nabla_{p}(\lambda)/L_{p}(\lambda))\to\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\mu),L_{p}(\lambda))
. Then (
3.1
) follows from the first part of our proposition combined with (the “dual” of) Corollary
3.4
.

Take now a local composition series

0=N_{0}\subset N_{1}\subset\cdots\subset N_{r}=N

of
N
at
\mu
, cf. Proposition
3.1
and let
J
be the subset of
\{1,2,\cdots,r\}
as in loc. cit. By (
3.1
) we have
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\mu),N_{i}/N_{i-1})=0
for all
i\in J
. However, we also have
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\mu),N_{i}/N_{i-1})=0
for
i\notin J
because by the universal property of Verma modules we have
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\mu),N^{\prime})=0
for all
N^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
which have no weights bigger than
\mu
.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\nu
and
N
be as in Proposition
3.7
. Then

(1)

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(M,N)=0
for all
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
,

(2)

if
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
is a highest weight module then
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(M,N)=0
.

Proof.

To prove
(1)
we consider a non-zero element
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(M,N)
. Pick a maximal weight
\lambda
in
\mathrm{Im}(f)
. Then
0\neq\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\lambda),\mathrm{Im}(f))\subset\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(\Delta_{p}(\lambda),N)
. Note that
L_{p}(\lambda)
is a subquotient of
\mathrm{Im}(f)
and hence also a subquotient of
M
. As
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
it follows that
\lambda\in W_{p}\cdot\nu
. Hence we have a contradiction to Proposition
3.7
(1).

We now turn to
(2)
. Denote the highest weight of
M
by
\mu\in W_{p}\cdot\nu
and let
R
denote the kernel of the surjection
\Delta_{p}(\mu)\to M
. Then Proposition
3.7
(2) gives us a surjection
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(R,N)\to\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(M,N)
. However, because of Corollary
3.4
we have
R\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
and therefore
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{p}}(R,N)=0
by Proposition
3.7
(1).
∎

With this we are now ready to prove Theorem
3.6
:

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
. We first construct a “highest weight module” filtration of
M
as follows. Choose first a maximal weight
\lambda_{1}
among the weights of
M
together with a non-zero vector
v_{1}\in M_{\lambda_{1}}
. Set
M_{1}=U_{K}v_{1}
and choose then a maximal weight
\lambda_{2}
among the weights of
M/M_{1}
together with a non-zero vector
v_{2}\in(M/M_{1})_{\lambda_{2}}
. Define
M_{2}
as the preimage in
M
of the submodule
U_{K}v_{2}\subset M/M_{1}
. Continue this procedure to obtain a (possibly infinite) filtration

0=M_{0}\subset M_{1}\subset M_{2}\subset M_{3}\subset\cdots\subset M,

which satisfies (compare
[
10
]
, Proposition 3.1)

(1)

M=\cup_{i}M_{i}
,

(2)

M_{i}/M_{i-1}
is a highest weight module with highest weight
\lambda_{i}
,

(3)

if
\lambda_{i}>\lambda_{j}
then
i<j
,

(4)

for each
\lambda\in X
there exists
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
such that
(M_{r})_{\lambda}=M_{\lambda}
.

We claim that the theorem holds for each term
M_{i}
in this filtration. This is certainly true for the highest weight module
M_{1}
. In fact, Corollary
3.4
ensures that
M_{i}/M_{i-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu_{i})
where
\nu_{i}
is the unique element in
\bar{C}\cap W_{p}\cdot\lambda_{i}
. Suppose the claim is true for
i-1
, i.e. that there are submodules
M_{i-1}(\nu)\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
of
M_{i-1}
with
M_{i-1}=\oplus_{\nu\in\bar{C}}M_{i-1}(\nu)
.
Then it follows from Corollary
3.8
that
M_{i}=(\oplus_{\nu\in\bar{C}\setminus\{\nu_{i}\}}M_{i-1}(\nu))\oplus M_{i}(\nu_{i})
, where
M_{i}(\nu_{i})
is the extension
0\to M_{i-1}(\nu_{i})\to M_{i}(\nu_{i})\to M_{i}/M_{i-1}\to 0
. Note that each
M_{i}(\nu)
is the maximal submodule of
M_{i}
belonging to
\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
.

Set now
M(\nu)=\cup_{i}M_{i}(\nu),\;\nu\in\bar{C}
. It then follows that
M=\oplus_{\nu\in\bar{C}}M(\nu)
as desired.
∎

Remark 3.9
.

Let
\nu\in\bar{C}
. The component
\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
may then be described as the subcategory of all
U_{k}
-modules
M
which satisfy

(1)

M
is a weight module,

(2)

\dim M_{\lambda}<\infty
for all
\lambda\in X
, and the set of weights of
M
are bounded from above by some
\mu\in\nu+{\mathbb{Z}}R
,

(3)

[M:L_{p}(\lambda)]=0
unless
\lambda\in W_{p}\cdot\nu

It is clear that if
M
satisfies these conditions then
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
. The only thing we have to prove to check the reverse statement is that the weights of
M
are bounded by a single element in
\nu+{\mathbb{Z}}R
. However, if
\lambda
is a weight of
M
then by (3)
\lambda
is also a weight of
L_{p}(w\cdot\nu)
for some
w\in W_{p}
. Then
\lambda\leq w\cdot\nu
, and since
w\cdot\nu\in\nu+{\mathbb{Z}}R
we also get
\lambda\in\nu+{\mathbb{Z}}R
. Thus
\mathrm{supp}M\cap X(\leq\mu)=\emptyset
unless
\mu\in\nu+{\mathbb{Z}}R
.
As
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
there exist finitely many
\mu_{i}\in X
such that
\mathrm{supp}(\mathrm{ch}M)\subset\cup_{i}X(\leq\mu_{i})
. By our observations we may assume that
\mu_{i}\in\nu+{\mathbb{Z}}R
for all
i
. But if
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\nu+{\mathbb{Z}}R
we may write
\mu_{2}=\mu_{1}+\sum_{j}n_{j}\alpha_{j}
for some
n_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We note that then
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\leq\mu_{1}+\sum_{j}|n_{j}|\alpha_{j}
. It follows that we can find
\mu\in\nu+{\mathbb{Z}}R
such that
\mu_{i}\leq\mu
for all
i
. Hence this
\mu
is an upper bound for the set of weights of
M
.

3.6.
Translation functors

The decomposition of
\mathcal{O}_{p}
in Theorem
3.6
allows us to define translation functors like those in category
\mathcal{O}
and category
\mathcal{C}
, cf.
[
15
]
, II.7.

Below we give the main definitions and state the main properties of translation functors. For proofs we refer to
[
15
]
, Chapter II.7.

Let
C
and
\bar{C}
be as in Section
3.5
. If
\lambda\in\bar{C}
and
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
then the summand of
M
belonging to
\mathcal{O}_{p}(\lambda)
(when decomposing
M
as in Theorem
3.6
) is the maximal submodule
pr_{\lambda}(M)
of
M
with composition factors belonging to
\{L_{p}(w\cdot\lambda)\mid w\in W_{p}\}
. The functor
\mathcal{O}_{p}\to\mathcal{O}_{p}(\lambda)
given by the assignment
M\mapsto pr_{\lambda}(M)
on objects (and by the obvious restriction map on morphisms, cf. Corollary
3.8
) is denoted
pr_{\lambda}
.

Definition 3.10
.

Suppose
\lambda,\mu\in\bar{C}
. Then the translation functor
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}:\mathcal{O}_{p}(\mu)\to\mathcal{O}_{p}(\lambda)
is given by

T_{\mu}^{\lambda}(M)=pr_{\lambda}(M\otimes L_{p}((\lambda-\mu)^{+}))),\;M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\mu),

where
(\lambda-\mu)^{+}
is the unique weight belonging to
X^{+}\cap W(\lambda-\mu)
.

Remark 3.11
.

In
[
15
]
, Chapter II.7 the corresponding translation functor is considered as the endofunctor on
\mathcal{C}
given (in our notation) as the composite
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}\circ pr_{\mu}
.

Among the main properties of translation functors we have

(1)

T_{\mu}^{\lambda}
is an exact functor.

(2)

T_{\mu}^{\lambda}
is left and right adjoint to
T_{\lambda}^{\mu}
.

Suppose
\mu\in C
and
w\in W_{p}
. Then

(3)

T_{\mu}^{\lambda}(\Delta_{p}(w\cdot\mu))\simeq\Delta_{p}(w\cdot\lambda)
and
T_{\mu}^{\lambda}(\nabla_{p}(w\cdot\mu))\simeq\nabla_{p}(w\cdot\lambda)
.

(4)

T_{\mu}^{\lambda}(L_{p}(w\cdot\mu))\simeq\begin{cases}{L_{p}(w\cdot\lambda)\text{ if }w\cdot\lambda\in\widehat{w\cdot C},}\\
{0\text{ otherwise.}}\end{cases}

Here
\widehat{w\cdot C}
denotes the upper closure of
w\cdot C
, see
[
15
]
, II.6.2.

Remark 3.12
.

There are more general versions of (3) and (4) applying to arbitrary facets of
C
, see
[
15
]
, II.7.14

We shall now show that the above properties of translation functors allow us when
p\geq h
to reduce the problem of determining the irreducible characters in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
to solving the same problem in
\mathcal{O}_{p}(0)
. When
p\geq h
we have
0\in C
. The linkage principle (Corollary
3.4
) ensures then that if
w\in W_{p}
then
[\Delta_{p}(w\cdot 0):L_{p}(\mu)]=0
unless
\mu=y\cdot 0
for some
y\in W_{p}
. This means that we have
\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(w\cdot 0)=\sum_{y\in W_{p}}a_{y,w}\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(y\cdot 0)
, where
a_{y,w}=[\Delta_{p}(w\cdot 0):L_{p}(y\cdot 0)]
. We note that
a_{y,w}=0
unless
y\cdot 0\leq w\cdot 0
, and
a_{w,w}=1
. Arguing as in Section
3.2
we can therefore write

\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(w\cdot 0)=\sum_{\{y\in W_{p}\mid y\cdot 0\leq w\cdot 0\}}c_{y,w}\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(y\cdot 0),

where
(c_{y,w})_{y,w}
is the inverse matrix of
(a_{y,w})_{y,w}
, see Section
3.2
.

Using the above notation we then have

Proposition 3.13
.

Assume
p\geq h
and suppose
\lambda\in\bar{C}
has
w\cdot\lambda\in\widehat{w\cdot C}
. Then
\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(w\cdot\lambda)=\sum_{y}c_{y,w}\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(y\cdot\lambda)
.

Proof.

This follows by the exactness of the translation functor
T_{0}^{\lambda}
combined with its properties (3) and (4) above.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

(1)

Note that when we apply Proposition
3.13
to the set of restricted weights we get that to determine the irreducible characters for all such weights it is enough to know the set
\{\mathrm{ch}L_{p}(w\cdot 0)|w\cdot 0\in X_{1}\}
. This is (for
R
irreducible) a set of cardinality equal to
|W|/f
, see
[
14
]
4.9, where
f
is the index of connection for
R
. Note that this number is (for
p\geq h
) much less than the number of weights in
X_{1}
(which is
p^{n}
).

(2)

If
p<h
then the more general versions referred to in Remark
3.12
gives a similar reduction to a single weight in each facet contained in
X_{1}
.

(3)

The reductions in (1) and (2) then determine all irreducible characters in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
via the Steinberg tensor product theorem, see Section 2. This generalizes the well known reductions in
\mathcal{C}
(based on the classical Steinberg theorem for finite dimensional simple
G
-modules and the translation principles in
\mathcal{C}
).

4.
Twisting functors

In this section we shall show that the twisting functors on
\mathcal{O}
, cf.
[
6
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
, carry over to
\mathcal{O}_{p}
. In fact, we shall see that
twisting functors even have
{\mathbb{Z}}
-versions. This will be important for our applications in the next section.

4.1.
Twisting functors in characteristic
0

We start by briefly recalling the definition and some key properties of twisting functors on
\mathcal{O}
.

Consider the complex semisimple algebra
\mathfrak{g}
from the introduction. We can write
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}
,
where
\mathfrak{h}
is a Cartan subalgebra, and
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
, respectively
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
, is the nilpotent Lie subalgebra corresponding to
R^{+}
, respectively
-R^{+}
.
So we have
\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in R^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}
, respectively
\mathfrak{n}^{-}=\bigoplus_{\beta\in R^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{-\beta}
. We have correspondingly for the universal enveloping algebra
U=U(\mathfrak{g})=U^{-}U^{0}U^{+}
where
U^{-}=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-}),U^{0}=U(\mathfrak{h})
, and
U^{+}=U(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
. These algebras are
X
-graded with finite dimensional graded pieces. In particular we have

U^{-}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\leq 0}U^{-}_{\lambda}
where
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}U^{-}_{\lambda}=P(-\lambda)
.

Let
W
denote the Weyl group for
R
. Then
W
acts naturally on
\mathfrak{g}
. For each
w\in W
we denote by
\mathfrak{n}_{w}
the Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{n}^{-}
given by

\mathfrak{n}_{w}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\cap w^{-1}(\mathfrak{n}^{+})=\bigoplus_{\{\beta\in R^{+}\mid w(\beta)<0\}}\mathfrak{g}_{-\beta}.

We then set
N_{w}=U(\mathfrak{n}_{w})
, the (complex) universal enveloping of
\mathfrak{n}_{w}
. This is a graded subalgebra of
U^{-}
and we have
N_{w}=\bigoplus_{\{\lambda\leq 0\mid w(\lambda)\geq 0\}}U^{-}_{\lambda}
.

Let now
N_{w}^{*}
denote the restricted dual of
N_{w}
, i.e.
N_{w}^{*}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\leq 0}((N_{w})_{\lambda})^{*}
. Then right, respectively left, multiplication by
N_{w}
on itself give a left, respectively right
N_{w}
-module structure on
N_{w}^{*}
. We define the semiregular
(U,N_{w})
-bimodule
S_{w}
by

S_{w}=U\otimes_{N_{w}}N_{w}^{*}.

The left
U
-structure is given by left multiplication on
U
and the right
N_{w}
-structure comes from the right
N_{w}
-module structure on
N_{w}^{*}
.

It turns out that
S_{w}
is also a right
U
-module and that as such we have
S_{w}\simeq N_{w}^{*}\otimes_{N_{w}}U
with the right
U
-module structure on
N_{w}^{*}\otimes_{N_{w}}U
coming from right multiplication on
U
. This allows us to define the twisting functor
T_{w}
as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
w\in W
. Then
T_{w}
is the endofunctor on the category of
U
-modules given by

T_{w}(M)=\;^{\varphi_{w}}(S_{w}\otimes_{U}M),

where
\varphi_{w}
denotes the twist by the automorphism on
U
induced by the action of
w
on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Clearly,
T_{w}
is right exact. The reason for the twist by
\varphi_{w}
in this definition is that this makes the restriction of
T_{w}
to
\mathcal{O}
into an endofunctor on the category
\mathcal{O}
with the following properties, see
[
4
]
, Section 6.2:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
w\in W
. Then

(1)

T_{w}
restricts to an endofunctor on
\mathcal{O}
.

(2)

T_{ws}=T_{w}\circ T_{s}
for all simple reflections
s
with
ws>w
.

(3)

\mathrm{ch}T_{w}\Delta(\lambda)=\mathrm{ch}\Delta(w\cdot\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in X
.

(4)

T_{w_{0}}\Delta(\lambda)\simeq\nabla(w_{0}\cdot\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in X
.

Here the order relation on
W
appearing in (2) is the Bruhat order, and in (4)
w_{0}
is as usual the longest element in
W
.

4.2.
Twisting functors over
{\mathbb{Z}}

In this section we shall check that the construction of twisting functors has a version over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Choose a Chevalley basis for
\mathfrak{g}
. This is a basis
\{f_{\beta},h_{i},e_{\beta}\mid\beta\in R^{+},i\in\{1,2,\cdots,n\}\}
for
\mathfrak{g}
with particular nice properties. For instance, it makes

\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}\{f_{\beta},h_{i},e_{\beta}\mid\beta\in R^{+},i\in\{1,2,\cdots,r\}\}

into a Lie algebra over
{\mathbb{Z}}
. Among the relations we have
[e_{\alpha_{i}},f_{\alpha_{i}}]=h_{i},i=1,2,\cdots,n
(with
\{\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{n}\}
denoting the simple roots in
R^{+}
). Then the Kostant
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form
U_{\mathbb{Z}}\subset U
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-subalgebra generated by the divided powers of
e_{\alpha_{i}},f_{\alpha_{i}},i=1,2,\cdots,n
. We have a PBW-type
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
U_{\mathbb{Z}}
consisting of
products (in appropriate order) of divided powers of the basis-elements
f_{\beta}
and
e_{\beta}
together with the elements
\binom{h_{i}}{s}\in U^{0}_{\mathbb{Z}}=U_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathfrak{h})
.

In analogy with Section
4
for each
w\in W

we then define
N_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}
as the graded
{\mathbb{Z}}
-subalgebra of
U_{\mathbb{Z}}^{-}
generated by
\{f_{\beta}^{(s)}\mid\beta\in R^{+},w(\beta)<0,s\geq 0\}
. If
w=s_{i_{r}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}
is a reduced expression of
w
(
s_{j}
being the reflection in
W
associated with the simple root
\alpha_{j}
)
and
\beta_{j}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}(\alpha_{i_{j}})
, then
\{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{r}\}=\{\beta\in R^{+}\mid w(\beta)<0\}
and if
\lambda\leq 0
then the
\lambda
-graded piece of
N_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}
is a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module with basis
\{f_{\beta_{r}}^{(a_{r})}\cdots f_{\beta_{2}}^{(a_{2})}f_{\beta_{1}}^{(a_{1})}\mid a_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0},\sum_{j=1}^{r}a_{j}\beta_{j}=-\lambda\}
.

The above means that
N_{\mathbb{Z}}^{*}:=\bigoplus_{\lambda>0,\;w(\lambda)<0}(N_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}})_{(-\lambda)})^{*}
is also a free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module. It is moreover, in analogy with
N_{w}^{*}
above, a left and right
N_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}
-module, so that we can define the semiregular
(U_{\mathbb{Z}},N_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}})
-bimodule by

S_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}=U_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{N_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}}(N_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}})^{*}.

In analogy with the situation in Section 4.1 we get that
S_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}
is also a right
U_{\mathbb{Z}}
-module and as such isomorphic to
N^{*}_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes_{N_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}}U_{\mathbb{Z}}
.
So this leads to the twisting functor
T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}
on the category of
U_{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules given by

T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}M=\;^{\varphi_{w}}(S_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes_{U_{\mathbb{Z}}}M),

where
\varphi_{w}
now denotes the restriction of
\varphi_{w}
to
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Consider the subcategory
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z}}
consisting of
U_{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules
M
which satisfy

(1)

M
has an
X
-grading, i.e.
M=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in X}M_{\lambda}
as a
U_{Z}^{0}
-module, where
\binom{h_{i}}{s}m=\binom{\langle\lambda,\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\rangle}{s}m,\;m\in M_{\lambda},i=1,,\cdots,n,s\geq 0
.

(2)

All
M_{\lambda}
are free over
{\mathbb{Z}}
of finite rank and there exist
\mu_{1},\cdots\mu_{r}\in X
such that
M_{\lambda}=0
unless
\lambda\in X(\leq\mu_{1})\cup\cdots\cup X(\leq\mu_{r})
.

Note that each
M\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z}}
has a well defined character
\mathrm{ch}M\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
given by

\mathrm{ch}M(\lambda)=\sum_{\mu\in X}rk(M_{\lambda}).

Clearly,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is not closed under taking quotients, because these may have torsion. In the next section we shall need to consider some purely torsion
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules which occur as quotients of twisted Verma modules. For use in this context we make the following definition

Definition 4.3
.

Let
N
be a
U_{\mathbb{Z}}
-module which is
X
-graded just as the objects in
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z}}
but with each graded piece
N_{\lambda}
being a finite
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module. We assume that the set of weights of
N
are bounded just as in (2) above. Then we define
\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}N\in\widehat{{\mathbb{Z}}[X]}
by

\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}N:\lambda\mapsto\nu_{p}(|N_{\lambda}|),

where
\nu_{p}(m)
denotes the highest power of
p
dividing
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Clearly,
\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}
is additive on exact sequences whose terms all have the same properties as
N
.

Let
\lambda\in X
and set

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)=U_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{B_{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda.

We call this the Verma module over
{\mathbb{Z}}
with highest weight
\lambda
. Clearly,
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z}}
for all
\lambda
. We have also dual Verma modules over
{\mathbb{Z}}
which we denote
\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)
.

A direct analogue of Proposition
4.2
is then

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
w\in W
. Then

(1)

T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}
restricts to an endofunctor on
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

(2)

T_{ws,{\mathbb{Z}}}=T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}\circ T_{s,{\mathbb{Z}}}
for all simple reflections
s
with
ws>w
.

(3)

\mathrm{ch}T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)=\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(w\cdot\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in X
.

(4)

T_{w_{0},{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)\simeq\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(w_{0}\cdot\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in X
.

4.3.
Twisting functors in characteristic
p

It is straightforward to pass from the integral twisting functor
T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}
to its analogue on the category of
U_{K}
-modules. In fact, we have by definition that
U_{K}=U_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K
. Moreover, if we for
w\in W
define
S_{w,K}
in analogy with the procedure in Section
4
, then we also have

S_{w,K}=S_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K.

The twisting functor
T_{w,K}
on the category of
U_{K}
-modules is then given by

T_{w,K}M=\;^{\varphi_{w}}(S_{w;K}\otimes_{U_{K}}M).

Again this is a right exact functor, it restricts to an endofunctor on
\mathcal{O}_{p}
, we have
T_{ws,K}=T_{w,K}\circ T_{s,K}
for all simple reflections
s
for which
ws>w
,
\mathrm{ch}T_{w,K}\Delta_{p}(\lambda)=\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(w\cdot\lambda)
, and
T_{w_{0},K}\Delta_{p}(\lambda)\simeq\nabla_{p}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in X
. In addition, we have

Proposition 4.5
.

The twisting functors on
\mathcal{O}_{p}
preserve each of the subcategories
\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu),\;\nu\in\bar{C}
.

Proof.

Let
\nu\in\bar{C}
. If
w\in W
and
\lambda\in W_{p}\cdot\nu
then we have (as observed above)
\mathrm{ch}T_{w,K}\Delta_{p}(\lambda)=\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(w\cdot\lambda)
. This implies that
T_{w,K}\Delta_{p}(\lambda)\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
. The right exactness of
T_{w,K}
then gives that also
T_{w,K}L_{p}(\lambda)\in\mathcal{O}_{p}(\nu)
. The proposition follows.
∎

We say that
M\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
has a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form if there exists
M_{\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z}}
with
M_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K=M
. If
M
has a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form
M_{\mathbb{Z}}
then it is clear from the definitions that we have

T_{w,K}M=T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}M_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K.

Note that if
\lambda\in X
then
\Delta_{p}(\lambda)
has a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form, namely
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)
. Likewise
\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-form of
\nabla_{p}(\lambda)
.

5.
Filtrations and sum formulas for antidominant Verma modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}

As an application of the twisting functors introduced in Section 5 we shall in this section use them to construct Jantzen-type filtrations of all antidominant Verma modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
and prove that they satisfy sum formulas analogous to the Weyl modules for
G
. Our approach is related to the one used in
[
4
]
for category
\mathcal{O}
, but since we are in characteristic
p
there are closer similarities to the modular case in
[
2
]
(or
[
15
]
, Chapter II.8).

5.1.
The
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
case

We begin with the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
. Here we choose the usual basis
\{f,h,e\}
for this Lie algebra. This is also a Chevalley basis of
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and we shall work with the corresponding divided power basis of
U_{\mathbb{Z}}
. The Weyl group has order
2
and we denote its generator by
s
.

Let
\lambda\in X={\mathbb{Z}}
and consider the twisted Verma module
T_{s,{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)
which we by Proposition
4.4
can identify with
\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(-\lambda-2)
. Write
\mu=-\lambda-2
and observe that since
\mu
is the maximal weight of
\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)
we have
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu),T_{s,{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda))\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}c_{\mu}
for some (unique up to sign) homomorphism
c_{\mu}:\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)\to T_{s,{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)\simeq\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)
.

We now choose a generator
v_{0}
of
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)_{\mu}
. As this weight space equals
{\mathbb{Z}}
we have that
v_{0}
is unique up to sign. Then for
i\geq 0
we set
v_{i}=f^{(i)}v_{0}
, so that
\{v_{i}|i\geq 0\}
is a basis of
\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)_{\mu}
and we have a corresponding dual basis
\{v^{*}_{i}\mid i\geq 0\}
of
\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)
. An elementary computation shows then that
c_{\mu}
is given by (up to sign)

c_{\mu}:v_{i}\mapsto\binom{\mu}{i}v_{i}^{*},\;i\geq 0.

Let
C(\mu)
denote the cokernel of
c_{\mu}
. The above formula for
c_{\mu}
shows that
C(\mu)=\bigoplus_{i\geq 0}C(\mu)_{\mu-2i}
with

(5.1)

C(\mu)_{\mu-2i}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/(\binom{\mu}{i}){\mathbb{Z}}.

as
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
\mu<0
and set
\lambda=-\mu-2
. Then

\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}C(\mu)=\sum_{i>0}\nu_{p}(\binom{\mu}{i})e^{\mu-2i}=\sum_{a>\lambda+1}\nu_{p}(a)\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\lambda-2a)-\sum_{b>0}\nu_{p}(b)\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\mu-2b).

Proof.

The first equality is immediate from (
5.1
) and the second drops out when we write the Verma characters in terms of the
e^{\mu-2i}
’s.
∎

5.2.
The minimal parabolic case

We return to general
\mathfrak{g}
and fix a simple root
\alpha
. Associated with
\alpha
we have the minimal parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha}=\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\oplus\mathfrak{b}
.

Consider
\lambda\in X
and set
\mu=s\cdot\lambda
where
s=s_{\alpha}
denotes the simple reflection in
W
associated with
\alpha
.
Then in analogy with the
\mathfrak{s}l_{2}
case in the previous section we take a generator
c_{\mu}^{\alpha}
for the hom-space from
\Delta_{\alpha,{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mu)
to
T_{s,{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\alpha,{\mathbb{Z}}}(\lambda)
,
where for any
\nu\in X
we have denoted by
\Delta_{\alpha,{\mathbb{Z}}}(\nu)
the Verma module for
U_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha})
, i.e.
\Delta_{\alpha,{\mathbb{Z}}}(\nu)=U_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha})\otimes_{B_{\mathbb{Z}}}\nu
.

If
\langle\mu,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle<0
then we have with
\lambda=s\cdot\mu
(using the same computations as in Section
5.1
) a short exact sequence

(5.2)

0\to\Delta_{\alpha,{\mathbb{Z}}}(\mu)\to T_{s,{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\alpha,{\mathbb{Z}}}(\lambda)\to C_{\alpha}(\mu)\to 0

where the first map is
c_{\mu}^{\alpha}
and
C_{\alpha}(\mu)
is a torsion module with

\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}C_{\alpha}(\mu)=\sum_{a>-\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}\nu_{p}(a)\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{\alpha,p}(\lambda-a\alpha)-\sum_{b>0}\nu_{p}(b)\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{\alpha,p}(\mu-b\alpha).

Here
\Delta_{\alpha,p}(\nu)
is the Verma module for
U_{K}(\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha})
with highest weight
\nu
.

When we apply the exact functor
U_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{U_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha})}-
to (
5.2
) we get the short exact sequence

(5.3)

0\to\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)\to T_{s,{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)\to C_{s}(\mu)\to 0

where
C_{s}(\mu)=U_{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{U_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha})}C_{\alpha}(\mu)
is a torsion module with

(5.4)

\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}C_{s}(\mu)=\sum_{a>-\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha^{\vee}\rangle}\nu_{p}(a)\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\lambda-a\alpha)-\sum_{b>0}\nu_{p}(b)\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\mu-b\alpha).

Note that we have seriously abused notation: in (
5.2
) the symbol
T_{s,{\mathbb{Z}}}
denotes the twisting functor on
U_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathfrak{p}_{\alpha})
-modules while in (
5.3
) the same symbol denotes the twisting functor on
U_{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules.

5.3.
The general case

Consider now an element
w\in W
for which
ws>w
. Then by Proposition
4.4
(2) we have
T_{ws,{\mathbb{Z}}}=T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}\circ T_{s,{\mathbb{Z}}}
so that when we apply the right exact functor
T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}
to (
5.3
) we obtain a short exact sequence

0\to T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)\to T_{ws,{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\lambda)\to T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}C_{s}(\mu)\to 0.

In fact, when tensored by
{\mathbb{C}}
the two first terms are both isomorphic to the simple Verma module
\Delta(\mu)\in\mathcal{O}
. This gives the injectivity of the first map. The last term in this sequence is a torsion module and when we combine Proposition
4.4
(3) and (
5.4
) we get

\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}T_{w,{\mathbb{Z}}}C_{s}(\mu)=\sum_{a>-\langle\mu+\rho,\beta^{\vee}\rangle}\nu_{p}(a)\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(ws\cdot\mu-a\beta)-\sum_{b>0}\nu_{p}(b)\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(w\cdot\mu-b\beta),

where
\beta=w(\alpha)\in R^{+}
.

5.4.
Filtrations of antidominant Verma modules in
\mathcal{O}_{p}

The results in the preceding subsections lead to the following result.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mu\in X^{-}
. The Verma module
\Delta_{p}(\mu)\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
has a filtration

\cdots\subset\Delta_{p}^{2}(\mu)\subset\Delta_{p}^{1}(\mu)\subset\Delta_{p}(\mu)

in which the submodules
\Delta_{p}^{j}(\mu)
have the following properties

(1)

\Delta_{p}(\mu)/\Delta_{p}^{1}(\mu)\simeq L_{p}(\mu)
.

(2)

The family
(\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}^{j}(\mu))_{j\geq 1}
is summable and we have

\sum_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}^{j}(\mu)=\sum_{\beta\in R^{+}}(\sum_{a_{\beta}>-\langle\mu+\rho,\beta^{\vee}\rangle}\nu_{p}(a_{\beta})\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(s_{\beta}\cdot\mu-a_{\beta}\beta)-\sum_{b_{\beta}>0}\nu_{p}(b_{\beta})\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\mu-b_{\beta}\beta)).

Proof.

We have
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Z}}}(\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu),\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu))\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
. Choose a generator
c(\mu)
of this
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module. Note that
c(\mu)
is unique up to sign and an isomorphism on the
\mu
weight space. We then define for
j\geq 0

\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{j}(\mu)=c(\mu)^{-1}(p^{j}\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu))

and

\Delta_{p}^{j}(\mu)=\text{ the $K$-span of the image in }\Delta_{p}(\mu)=\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K\text{ of }\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}^{j}(\mu).

If
\overline{c(\mu)}=c(\mu)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K
, then the image of
\overline{c(\mu)}
equals
L_{p}(\mu)
and we have
\Delta_{p}^{1}(\mu)=\ker(\overline{c(\mu)})
. Hence (1) is immediate.

To check (2) we shall now see that we may factor
c(\mu)
into a composition of the homomorphisms occuring in the previous section. We let
w_{0}=s_{i_{1}}s_{i_{2}}\cdots s_{i_{N}}
be a reduced expression for the longest element in
W
and set
y_{j}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j}},\;j=0,1,,\cdots,N
. Then
y_{0}=1
and
y_{N}=w_{0}
. Moreover,
\beta_{j}=y_{j-1}(\alpha_{i_{j}})\in R^{+}
and in fact
R^{+}=\{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{N}\}
.

By Section
5.3
we have for each
j=1,\cdots,N
a homomorphism

c_{j}(\mu):T_{y_{j-1}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(y_{j-1}^{-1}\cdot\mu)\to T_{y_{j}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(y_{j}^{-1}\cdot\mu),

which is an isomorphism on the highest weight space (a rank
1
space with weight
\mu
). Moreover, if
C_{j}(\mu)
denotes the cokernel of
c_{j}(\mu)
then
C_{j}(\mu)
is a torsion
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module and we have

(5.5)

\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}C_{j}(\mu)=\sum_{a_{j}>-\langle\mu+\rho,\beta_{j}^{\vee}\rangle}\nu_{p}(a_{j})\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(s_{\beta_{j}}\cdot\mu-a_{j}\beta_{j})-\sum_{b_{j}>0}\nu_{p}(b_{j})\mathrm{ch}\Delta_{p}(\cdot\mu-b_{j}\beta_{j}).

The composite
c_{N}(\mu)\circ c_{N-1}(\mu)\circ\cdots c_{1}(\mu):\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)\to T_{w_{0},{\mathbb{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(w_{0}\cdot\mu)\simeq\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)
is an isomorphism on the
\mu
weight space and hence coincides up to sign with
c(\mu)
. Via the short exact sequences
0\to T_{y_{j-1}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(y_{j-1}^{-1}\cdot\mu)\to T_{y_{j}}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(y_{j}^{-1}\cdot\mu)\to C_{j}(\mu)\to 0
, cf. Section (
5.3
), we then get

\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}C(\mu)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}C_{j}(\mu).

Inserting the formulas from (
5.5
) we then see that
\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}C(\mu)
coincides with the right hand side of (2). Now standard arguments as in
[
2
]
(or
[
15
]
, II.8.18) give that
\mathrm{ch}_{p}^{t}C(\mu)
is also equal to the left hand side of (2).
∎

Remark 5.3
.

Note that the assumption that
\mu
is antidominant in Theorem
5.2
is needed in the proof to ensure that the homomorphisms
c_{j}(\mu)
are injective (and have cokernels which are torsion
{\mathbb{Z}}
-modules). In fact, the composite
c(\mu):\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)\to\nabla_{\mathbb{Z}}(\mu)
is injective iff
\mu
is antidominant.

Example 5.4
.

(1)

If in Theorem
5.2
we take
\mu=-\rho
then we see that the right hand side in the sum formula is zero (in fact, for each
\beta\in R^{+}
the
\beta
summand is
0
as
s_{\beta}\cdot(-\rho)=-\rho
). This is therefore another proof of the fact, that
\Delta_{p}(-\rho)
is irreducible, see
[
3
]
, Theorem 6.1. More generally, in the sum formula corresponding to an arbitrary
\mu\in X^{-}
the only
\beta\in R^{+}
that contribute non-trivially to the sum are those with
\langle\mu+\rho,\beta^{\vee}\rangle<0
.

(2)

Consider
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
and take
\mu=-\alpha
. We claim that the filtration in Theorem
5.2
in this case is
\Delta_{p}^{j}(-2)=\Delta_{p}(-2)^{(j)}
,
j\geq 0
. This follows from the considerations in Section 5.2 in
[
3
]
. In fact, the sequence (5.8) there shows that
\Delta_{p}(-2)^{(1)}=\Delta_{p}^{1}(-2)
. To check that the same is true for all
j
we just observe that the character sum formula for the submodules
(\Delta_{p}(-2)^{(j)})_{j\geq 1}
coincides with the one for
(\Delta_{p}^{(j)}(-2))_{j\geq 1}
given in Theorem
5.2
(2). Now all non-zero weight multiplicities in
\Delta_{p}(-2)
are
1
. Hence any two filtrations by submodules with the same character sum must coincide.

6.
Character formulas for indecomposable tilting modules

Recall from
[
3
]
that in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
we have two kinds of tilting modules: (1) the finite dimensional ones and (2) the
\infty
-dimensional ones defined as follows

(1)

A module
Q\in\mathcal{C}
is called a tilting module if it has a filtration by Weyl modules as well as by dual Weyl modules,

(2)

A module
Q\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
is called
\infty
-tilting if it has a finite filtration by Verma modules as well as by dual Verma modules.

Both kinds of tilting modules split into indecomposable modules. In this section we have collected some character formulas giving relations among indecomposable tilting modules. Our tilting character formulas are immediate consequences of results from
[
3
]
via our setup from the previous sections. Therefore we have just stated the results leaving the immediate proofs to the readers. In contrast to the situation in Section 2 our formulas does not reduce the problem of finding all indecomposable tilting characters to a finite set. We shall end the section (and the paper) by some remarks on how one nevertheless have some useful reductions.

6.1.
Characters for indecomposable finite dimensional tilting modules

Up to isomorphisms we have one indecomposable tilting module
T_{p}(\lambda)\in\mathcal{C}
for each
\lambda\in X^{+}
. The weights of
T_{p}(\lambda)
are all less than or equal to
\lambda
, and
\lambda
occurs with multiplicity
1
. These modules have been well studied and results can be found in the literature. For future use we just mention the following results

(1)

The linkage principle in
\mathcal{C}
shows that if
L_{p}(\mu)
is a composition factor (in the usual sense) of
T_{p}(\lambda)
for some
\mu,\lambda\in X^{+}
then
\mu\in W_{p}\cdot\lambda
.

(2)

Let
p\geq h
. Translation functor arguments show that in order to determine
\mathrm{ch}T_{p}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in X^{+}
it is enough to do so for
\lambda\in W_{p}\cdot 0\cap X^{+}
.

(3)

Let
r\geq 1
and
p\geq 2h-2
. Then for
\lambda\in(p^{r}-1)\rho+X_{r}
and arbitrary
\mu\in X^{+}
we have
\mathrm{ch}T_{p}(\lambda+p^{r}\mu)=\mathrm{ch}T_{p}(\lambda)\star\mathrm{ch}^{(r)}T_{p}(\mu)
.

The results in (1), (2) and (3) are “classical” and can all be found in
[
15
]
, II.E.

Remark 6.1
.

The result (3) above is called Donkin’s tensor product theorem and was proved by him in
[
12
]
, Proposition 2.1. It may hold for smaller values of
p
than the bound we have stated, but recently several examples by Bendel, Nakano, Pillen and Sobaje for
p=2
and
p=3
,
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
, show that there are exceptions. In a very recent preprint
[
9
]
these authors show that the bound on
p
can in general be lowered to
2h-4
.

We emphasize that although (3) gives the characters of indecomposable tilting modules for certain “large”
\nu\in X^{+}
in terms of smaller weights it does not reduce the problem of determining all tilting characters to a finite set of weights (as we saw was the case for irreducible characters in Section 2). There are large infinite subsets of
X^{+}
consisting of weights that cannot be expressed as in (3).

Remark 6.2
.

Note that by the recent results in
[
1
]
and
[
16
]
the characters of all finite dimensional tilting modules are determined by the
p
-Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials!

6.2.
Characters of indecomposable
\infty
-tilting modules

As demonstrated in
[
3
]
, Theorem 7.5 we have in analogy with the finite dimensional case that up to isomorphisms we have one indecomposable tilting module
T^{\infty}_{p}(\lambda)\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
for each
\lambda\in X^{+}-\rho
. The weights of
T_{p}^{\infty}(\lambda)
are all less than or equal to
\lambda
, and
\lambda
occurs with multiplicity
1
. We also have

(1)

The linkage principle in
\mathcal{O}_{p}
(combine Corollary
3.4
with Theorem 8.8 in

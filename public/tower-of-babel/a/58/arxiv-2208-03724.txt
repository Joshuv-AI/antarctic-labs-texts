Moment map, convex function and extremal point
King-Leung Lee, Jacob Sturm, Xiaowei Wang

Abstract
. The moment map μ \mu is a central concept in the study of Hamiltonian actions of compact Lie groups K K on symplectic manifolds. In this short note, we propose a theory of moment maps coupled with an Ad K \mathrm{Ad}_{K} -invariant convex function f f on 𝔨 ∗ \mathfrak{k}^{\ast} , the dual of Lie algebra of K K , and study the properties of the critical point of f ∘ μ f\circ\mu . Our motivation comes from Donaldson [ 2 ] which is an example of infinite dimensional version of our setting. As an application, we interpret Kähler-Ricci solitons as a special case of the generalized extremal metric.

3.1.
Calabi-Matsushima decomposition

In this section, prove a generalized version of classical Calabi-Matsushima decomposition
[
Calabi
]
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
and

\mathfrak{g}_{z}=\{\xi_{1}+\sqrt{-1}\xi_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}\,:\,\sigma(\xi_{1})+J\sigma(\xi_{2})=0\}\subset\mathfrak{g}.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
z\in Z
is a critical point of
f\circ\mu
, which will be called an
f
-extremal point
. Then

(1)

df\big|_{\mu(z)}\in\mathfrak{k}_{z}=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{k}\,:\,\sigma(\xi)=0\}

(2)

\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{df|_{\mu(z)}}\mu(z)=0

(3)

We have

\mathfrak{g}_{z}\ =\ \bigoplus_{\lambda\leq 0}\,\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\ \ {\rm where}\ \ \mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}\ =\ \{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}_{z}\,:\,\sqrt{-1}\mathrm{ad}_{df|_{\mu(z)}}\xi=\lambda\,\xi\,\}

(4)

In particular, we have

\mathfrak{g}_{0}\ =\ \mathfrak{k}_{z}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{\rm red}:=\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}_{\rm solv}

where
\mathfrak{g}_{\rm solv}\subseteq\mathfrak{g}
is the maximal solvable subalgebra.

Proof.

To prove (1) we assume
z
is a critical point of
f\circ\mu
so

for all tangent vectors

. Thus

df|_{\mu(z)}(d\mu_{z}(v))=\langle d\mu_{z}(v),df|_{\mu(z)}\rangle=0\text{ for all }v\in T_{z}Z.

Now (
3.13
) implies
\langle Iv,\sigma_{z}(df_{\mu(z)}\rangle=0
for all

, hence
\sigma_{z}(df\big|_{\mu(z)})=0
.

Part (2) follows from Part (1) of Proposition
3.14
.

To prove (3), we let
k=\exp(t\cdot df|_{\mu(z)})
. Then (1) implies
k\cdot z=z
so
\mu(z)=\mathrm{Ad}_{k}^{*}\mu(z)
by (
3.12
). Thus
if we substitute
k
and
\alpha^{\ast}=\mu(z)
into part (2) of Proposition
2.2
, we have

(\!(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})\!)_{\mu(z)}\ =\ (\!(\mathrm{Ad}_{k}^{*}\xi^{*},\mathrm{Ad}_{k}^{*}\eta^{*})\!)_{\mu(z)}

and differentiate with respect to

we get

(3.17)

(\!(\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{df|_{\mu(z)}}\xi^{*},\eta^{*})\!)_{\mu(z)}\ +(\!(\xi^{*},\mathrm{ad}^{*}_{df|_{\mu(z)}}\eta^{*})\!)_{\mu(z)}\ =\ 0

or equivalently, combining Proposition
2.2

\nabla^{2}f\big|_{\alpha^{\ast}}:\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\mathfrak{k}^{\ast}&\xrightarrow{\hskip 45.52458pt}&\mathfrak{k}\\
\xi^{\ast}&\xmapsto{\hskip 28.45274pt}&\xi:=\nabla^{2}f\big|_{\alpha^{\ast}}(\xi^{\ast})&\end{array}

Moreover,

.
To prove (3), we need the following:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a critical point of

\sqrt{-1}\mathrm{ad}_{df|_{\mu(z)}}\xi=\lambda\xi

for some
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}_{z}

Then

.

Proof.

Write

with
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathfrak{k}

\lambda\xi_{1}+\lambda\sqrt{-1}\xi_{2}=\lambda\xi\ =\ \sqrt{-1}\mathrm{ad}_{df|_{\mu(z)}}(\xi_{1}+\sqrt{-1}\xi_{2})\ \Longrightarrow\ \left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\mathrm{ad}_{df|_{\mu(z)}}\xi_{1}&=&\lambda\xi_{2}\\
&&\\
\mathrm{ad}_{df|_{\mu(z)}}\xi_{2}&=&-\lambda\xi_{1}.\end{array}\right.

Now (

\lambda(\!(\xi_{2},\xi_{2})\!)_{\nabla^{2}f|_{\mu(z)}}=(\!(\mathrm{ad}_{df|_{\mu(z)}}(\xi_{1}),\xi_{2})\!)_{\nabla^{2}f|_{\mu(z)}}\ \mathrel{{\mathop{=}\limits}}\ -\langle J\sigma(\xi_{1}),\sigma(\xi_{2})\rangle_{TZ}\ =\ -\langle\sigma(\xi_{2}),\sigma(\xi_{2})\rangle_{TZ}\ =\ -|\sigma(\xi_{2})|^{2}_{TZ}

with equality

∎

Part (3) and (4) follows from the Lemma above, our proof is thus completed.

5.3.
Legendre Transform

Let
\color[rgb]{0,0,0}F:\mathbb{R}\supset U\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a convex function and
\mathbf{D}_{F}:C^{\infty}(X,U)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a functional defined by

\mathbf{D}_{F}(\varphi):=\int_{X}F(\varphi(x))d\mathrm{vol}_{X}

for a fixed volume form
d\mathrm{vol}_{X}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
P:=\{t\in\mathbb{R}|t=f^{\prime}(x)\text{ for some }x\in X\}=\mathrm{Im}(dF)

for any
\psi\in C^{\infty}(X,P)
.
Then
the Legendre transform
\widehat{\mathbf{D}_{F}}:C^{\infty}(X,P)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\widehat{\mathbf{D}_{F}}(\psi)=\int_{X}\widehat{F}(\psi(x))d\mathrm{vol}_{X},

with
\widehat{F}
being the Legendre transform of
f
corresponding to the pairing

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle:=\int_{X}\varphi(x)\psi(x)d\mathrm{vol}_{X}.

Proof.

Fix
\psi
, then we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbf{D}_{F}}(\psi):=

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})}\{\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle-\mathbf{D}_{F}(\varphi)\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X,U)}\left(\int_{X}\varphi(x)\psi(x)d\mathrm{vol}_{X}-\int_{X}F(\varphi(x))d\mathrm{vol}_{X}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X,U)}\int_{X}\big(\varphi(x)\psi(x)-F(\varphi(x))\big)d\mathrm{vol}_{X}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(\sup_{u\in U}(u\cdot\psi(x)-F(u))\right)d\mathrm{vol}_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\widehat{F}(\psi(x))d\mathrm{vol}_{X}.

We also need to show that there is a
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(X,U)
satisfying:

\int_{X}(\varphi(x)\psi(x)-F(\varphi(x)))d\mathrm{vol}_{X}\geq\int_{X}\widehat{F}(\psi(x))d\mathrm{vol}_{X}.

For that, by our assumption
\psi\in C^{\infty}(X,P)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
there is a
unique

p_{x}
satisfying
\psi(x)=F^{\prime}(p_{x}).
We define
\varphi_{\psi}(x)=p_{x}
, we only need to show that
\varphi_{\psi}(x)
is continuous. However,
F
is convex and smooth implies
(dF)^{-1}
exists and smooth. Hence
\varphi_{\psi}(x)=(dF)^{-1}(\psi(x))
which is smooth, and for any
x
,
\widehat{F}(\psi(x))=\varphi(x)\psi(x)-F(\varphi(x))
, which implies the inequality is equality.
∎

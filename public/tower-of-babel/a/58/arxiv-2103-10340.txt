1.
Introduction

A
hypergraph
is a pair of sets
\left\langle V,\mathcal{E}\right\rangle
such that
the elements of
\mathcal{E}
are non-empty subsets of
V
.
We are interested in hypergraphs
\left\langle V,\mathcal{E}\right\rangle
whose edges cover
V
in
the sense that

V=\cup\mathcal{E}
. To shorten notations,
in this case the hypergraph
will be identified with the family
\mathcal{E}
of its edges.

Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{E}

a vertex set
Y\subset\cup\mathcal{E}
is a
vertex cover
of
\mathcal{E}
iff

Y\cap E\neq\emptyset
for each
E\in\mathcal{E}
.
We say that a vertex cover
Z
is a
minimal vertex cover
of
\mathcal{E}

iff no proper subset of
Z
is a vertex cover of
\mathcal{E}
.

Using the terminology of Erdős and Hajnal
[
2
]
we say that
a hypergraph
\mathcal{E}
possesses
property
\operatorname{C}(k,\rho)
iff

|\bigcap\mathcal{E}^{\prime}|<{\rho}
for each
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\in{[\mathcal{E}]}^{k}
.

Dominic van der Zypen
[
1
,
9
]
raised the following question:

Assume that
r\in{\omega}
. Does every hypergraphs which possesses property
\operatorname{C}(2,r)
have
a minimal vertex cover?

In
[
6
]
Komjáth gave some partial answers.
To formulate them precisely we introduce some more notations.

We define the
edge cardinality spectrum

of a hypergraph
\mathcal{E}
,
\|\mathcal{E}\|
, as the cardinalities of
the edges of the hypergraph:

\|\mathcal{E}\|=\{|E|:E\in\mathcal{E}\}.

If
A
is a set and
S
is a set of cardinals,
write

{[A]}^{S}=\{B\subset A:|B|\in S\}.

If
S=\{{\kappa}\}
, then we will use the standard notation
{[A]}^{{\kappa}}
instead of

{[A]}^{\{{\kappa}\}}
.

If

{\lambda}
and
{\rho}
are cardinals,
S
is a set of cardinals,

k\in{\omega}
, then we write

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{S},k,{\rho})\to\mathbf{MinVC}

iff
every hypergraph

\mathcal{E}\subset{[{\lambda}]}^{S}
possessing property
\operatorname{C}(k,{\rho})
has a minimal vertex cover.

Komjath (
[
6
, Corollary 8]
) proved that

(k1)

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{{\omega}\cup\{{\kappa}\}},2,k)\to\mathbf{MinVC}
for each

{\omega}\leq{\kappa}\leq{\lambda}
.

In this paper we prove a strengthening of this result. To formulate our theorem we need to introduce
the following notion.
A set
S
of ordinals is
nowhere stationary
iff

S\cap{\alpha}
is not stationary in
{\alpha}
for any ordinal
{\alpha}
with
cf({\alpha})>{\omega}
.
In particular, countable sets of cardinals, and sets of successor cardinals
are nowhere stationary.
In Section
3
we prove:

Theorem 1.1
.

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{S},2,r)\to\mathbf{MinVC}
for each cardinal
{\lambda}
and
r\in{\omega}

provided that
S
is a nowhere stationary set of cardinals.

Komjáth (
[
6
, Theorems 16 and 17]
) also proved

(k2)

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{{\omega}\cup\{{\kappa}\}},2,{\omega})\to\mathbf{MinVC}
for each

{\omega}_{1}\leq{\kappa}\leq{\lambda}
provided
V=L
,

(k3)

GCH does not imply that

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}\aleph_{{\omega}+1},{{\omega}_{1}},2,{\omega})\to\mathbf{MinVC}
.

In Section
2
we show that the assumption
V=L
can be relaxed to

GCH
provided we strengthen the assumption
{\kappa}\geq{\omega}_{1}
to

{\kappa}\geq{\omega}_{2}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

If GCH holds, then
\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{{\omega}\cup\{{\kappa}\}},2,{\omega})\to\mathbf{MinVC}

for each
{\omega}_{2}\leq{\kappa}\leq{\lambda}
.

One can conjecture that one can not obtain positive ZFC theorems
for hypergraphs which possesses property
\operatorname{C}(2,{\rho})
for some
{\rho}\geq{\omega}
.
But this is not true: in Section
2
,
using Shelah’s Revised CGH theorem, we prove in ZFC:

Theorem 1.3
.

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{{\omega}\cup\{{\kappa}\}},2,\rho)\to\mathbf{MinVC}

for each
{\rho}<\beth_{\omega}\leq{\kappa}\leq{\lambda}
.

In Section
4
we consider hypergraphs possessing property
\operatorname{C}(k,r)

for

, and we prove the following results.

Theorem 1.4
.

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{{\omega}},r,k)\to\mathbf{MinVC}

provided
{\omega}\leq{\lambda}
and
k,r\in{\omega}

Theorem 1.5
.

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{{\omega}_{1}},3,k)\to\mathbf{MinVC}

Notations and basic observations.

We use the standard notation of infinite combinatorics.

If
\mathcal{F}\subset\mathcal{P}(X)
,
x\in X
and
Y\subset X
, write

\mathcal{F}(x)=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:x\in F\},

\mathcal{F}[Y]=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:F\cap Y\neq\emptyset\}\text{ and }\mathcal{F}[-Y]=\{F\in\mathcal{F}:F\cap Y=\emptyset\},

and

\mathcal{F}\restriction Y=\{F\cap Y:F\in\mathcal{F}[Y]\}\text{ and }\mathcal{F}\sqcap Y=\mathcal{F}\cap\mathcal{P}(Y).

Observe that
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}\restriction Y
.

Observation 1.6
.

Assume that
\mathcal{E}
is a hypergraph and
Y
is a vertex cover of
\mathcal{E}
.
Then
Y
is a minimal vertex cover of
\mathcal{E}
iff there is a function

w:Y\to\mathcal{E}
such that
w(y)\cap Y=\{y\}
for each
y\in Y
.

We will say that
w
is a
witnessing function
, or that
w

witnesses the minimality
of
Y
.

If
\left\langle X_{\alpha}:{\alpha}<{\delta}\right\rangle
is a sequence of sets, and
{\beta}<{\delta}
, write

X_{<{\beta}}=\bigcup_{{\alpha}<{\beta}}X_{\alpha}
.
We define
X_{\leq{\beta}}
,
X_{>{\beta}}
, etc. analogously.

2.
Maximizing well ordering

Given a hypergraph
\mathcal{E}
a well-ordering
\preceq
of the vertex set of
\mathcal{E}
is called
a
maximizing well-ordering
iff every edge
E\in\mathcal{E}
has a
\preceq
-maximal element.

If

{\lambda}
and
{\rho}
are cardinals,
S
is a set of cardinals,

k\in{\omega}
, then
we write

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{S},k,\rho)\to\mathbf{MaxWO}

iff
every hypergraph

\mathcal{E}\subset{[{\lambda}]}^{S}
possessing property
\operatorname{C}(k,{\rho})
has a maximizing well-ordering.

In
[
6
]
Komjáth observed that Klimo
[
5
, Theorem 5]
practically proved that
if a hypergraph has a maximizing well order then
it has a minimal vertex cover, i.e.

\mathbf{MaxWO}\to\mathbf{MinVC}

In
[
6
, Theorem 7]
Komjáth actually proved that

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{{\kappa}},2,k)\to\mathbf{MaxWO}
for each

{\omega}\leq{\kappa}\leq{\lambda}
.
So if
\mathcal{E}\subset{[{\lambda}]}^{{\omega}\cup\{{\kappa}\}}
, then

\mathcal{E}\cap{[{\lambda}]}^{{\kappa}}
has a maximizing well-order
\preceq
,
which is also a maximizing well-order for
\mathcal{E}
because non-empty finite sets
have maximal elements in any ordering.

1.2
and
1.3
it is enough to prove
the following two theorems.

Theorem 2.1
.

If GCH holds, then
\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{{\kappa}},2,{\omega})\to\mathbf{MaxWO}

for each
{\omega}_{2}\leq{\kappa}\leq{\lambda}
.

\mathbf{M}\mathbb{(}{\lambda},{{\kappa}},2,\rho)\to\mathbf{MaxWO}

Definition 2.3
.

Let

be a hypergraph,

We say that a continuous, increasing sequence

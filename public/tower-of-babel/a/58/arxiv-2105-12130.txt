String correlators on AdS 𝟑 \boldsymbol{\text{AdS}_{3}} : Three-point functions
Andrea Dei, Lorenz Eberhardt

Abstract
We revisit the computation of string worldsheet correlators on Euclidean AdS 3 \text{AdS}_{3} with pure NS-NS background. We compute correlation functions with insertions of spectrally flowed operators. We explicitly solve all the known constraints of the model and for the first time conjecture a closed formula for three-point functions with arbitrary amount of spectral flow. We explain the relation of our results with previous computations in the literature and derive the fusion rules of the model. This paper is the first in a series with several installments.

Even though one can only study string theory perturbatively in this way, this already contains very non-trivial information and string scattering amplitudes quickly become too complicated to compute.

Let us clarify what we mean exactly by the worldsheet model. There has been some naming confusion in the literature. The analytic continuation of the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathds{R})_{k}

\mathrm{AdS}_{3}

\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathds{R})_{k}

\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathds{R})_{k}

We are thus motivated to revisit the worldsheet model (including spectrally flowed sectors) to address these problems. Since we want to apply the gained knowledge to string theory, we consider vertex operators in the so-called
x

\text{AdS}_{3}

Maldacena:1997re

David:2002wn

\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathds{R})_{k}
for

\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathds{R})_{k}

\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathds{R})
. Finally, vertex operators transform under global
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathds{R})
transformations with weight
(h,\bar{h})
, which is identified with the conformal dimension of the dual CFT. To summarize, the correlators we want to study take the form

\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{w_{i}}_{j_{i},h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\right\rangle\ .

(1.1)

This is to be compared to correlators of unflowed vertex operators, i.e. correlators of the form

\left\langle\prod_{i=1}^{n}V^{0}_{j_{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\right\rangle\ ,

(1.2)

Teschner:1997ft
;
Teschner:1999ug
;
Teschner:2001gi
.

We want to highlight a further subtlety that also has caused confusion. The choice of vertex operators that results from the
x
-basis corresponds to coherent states in the Verma module of
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathds{R})_{k}
. Such states are usually not considered in CFT, where one mostly only considers finite linear combinations of basis elements in the Verma module. It turns out that very important aspects of the model depend on this choice. In particular the fusion rules that one obtains in this way are less restrictive than for the more conservative choice where only finite linear combinations are considered. We are interested in string theory where such coherent states are clearly necessary to describe the correct physics. Therefore there are more three-point functions to be determined to give a complete solution of the model. We can always recover results that have been obtained without allowing for coherent states by taking suitable limits that correspond to the collision of vertex operators in the
x
-space. We explain this relation very carefully in this paper.

The unflowed vertex operators entering (
1.2
) carry no explicit dependence on
h_{i}
. This is because for
w=0

h

h=\bar{h}=j

\left\langle J^{+}_{p}V_{j_{i},h_{i},\bar{h}_{i}}^{w_{i}}(x_{i};z_{i})\prod_{\ell\neq i}V_{j_{\ell},h_{\ell},\bar{h}_{\ell}}^{w_{\ell}}(x_{\ell};z_{\ell})\right\rangle\ ,\qquad 0<p<w_{i}\ .

(1.3)

This issue has been solved in

\displaystyle(J_{0}^{+}V_{j,h}^{w})(x;z)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}V_{j,h}(x;z)\ ,

(2.11a)

\displaystyle(J_{\pm w}^{+}V_{j,h}^{w})(x;z)

\displaystyle=\left(h-\tfrac{k}{2}w\pm(1-j)\right)V_{j,h\pm 1}^{w}(x;z)\ .

(2.11b)

In the unflowed sector, these OPEs reduce to

\displaystyle J^{+}(\zeta)V_{j}^{0}(x;z)

(2.12a)

\displaystyle J^{3}(\zeta)V_{j}^{0}(x;z)

\displaystyle=\frac{(j+x\partial_{x})V_{j}^{0}(x;z)}{\zeta-z}+\mathcal{O}(1)\ ,

(2.12b)

\displaystyle J^{-}(\zeta)V_{j}^{0}(x;z)

2.3
Reflection symmetry

Unflowed continuous representations with spin
j
and
1-j
are equivalent and the associated
x
-basis vertex operators are identified
Teschner:1997ft
,

V^{0}_{1-j}(x;z)=R_{1-j}\frac{(1-2j)}{\pi}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}x^{\prime}\ |x-x^{\prime}|^{4j-4}\,V^{0}_{j}(x^{\prime};z)\ .

(2.13)

Here,
R_{j}
is the unflowed reflection coefficient.
For flowed continuous representation, the vertex operators
V^{w}_{j,h}(x;z)
and
V_{1-j,h}^{w}(x;z)
are identified and we write

V_{1-j,h,\bar{h}}^{w}(x;z)=R(1-j,h,\bar{h})\,V_{j,h,\bar{h}}^{w}(x;z)\ ,

(2.14)

where we have introduced the
reflection coefficient

R(j,h,\bar{h})
, allowing for an arbitrary relative normalization of the two vertex operators. We will refer to eq. (
2.14
) as
reflection symmetry
. Idempotency requires

R(j,h,\bar{h})\,R(1-j,h,\bar{h})=1\ .

(2.15)

The
h
and
\bar{h}
dependence of the reflection coefficient is constrained by the choice of the representation. In fact,

R(1-j,h,\bar{h})\,(h-\tfrac{k\,w}{2}+1-j)\,V_{j,h+1,\bar{h}}^{w}(x;z)=R(1-j,h,\bar{h})\,J^{+}_{w}V_{j,h,\bar{h}}^{w}(x;z)\\
=J^{+}_{w}V_{1-j,h,\bar{h}}^{w}(x;z)=(h-\tfrac{k\,w}{2}+j)\,R(1-j,h+1,\bar{h})\,V_{j,h+1,\bar{h}}^{w}(x;z)

(2.16)

implies a recursion relation for
R(j,h,\bar{h})
. It follows

R(j,h,\bar{h})=R(j,\lambda)\,\binom{h-\tfrac{k\,w}{2}+j-1}{2j-1}\,\binom{\bar{h}-\tfrac{k\,w}{2}+j-1}{2j-1}\ ,

(2.17)

where
R(j,\lambda)
is an arbitrary function of
j
and
\lambda=(h-\tfrac{kw}{2})\text{ mod }\mathds{Z}=(\bar{h}-\tfrac{kw}{2})\text{ mod }\mathds{Z}
.
In the following, we will find it useful to also specify the
\lambda
dependence of the reflection coefficient,

R(j,\lambda)=\frac{\pi\,R_{j}\,(1-2j)}{\sin(2\pi j)}\frac{\sin\left(\pi(\lambda+j)\right)}{\sin\left(\pi(\lambda-j)\right)}\ ,

(2.18)

where
R_{j}
is an arbitrary function of
j
satisfying

R_{j}\,R_{1-j}=1\ .

(2.19)

Notice that this is just a choice of convenience, simplifying many of the formulae appearing in the following sections.
It is convenient to write

R(j,h,\bar{h})=R_{j}(2j-1)\frac{\text{\textgamma}(h-\frac{kw}{2}+j)}{\text{\textgamma}(h-\frac{kw}{2}+1-j)\text{\textgamma}(2j)}\ ,

(2.20)

where

\text{\textgamma}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(1-\bar{x})}\ .

(2.21)

In this formula,
\bar{x}
is not the complex conjugate, but the corresponding quantity describing the right-movers. One can furthermore fix the reflection coefficient
R_{j}
to be
Teschner:1997ft

R_{j}=\frac{(k-2)\nu^{1-2j}}{(2j-1)\text{\textgamma}\left(\frac{2j-1}{k-2}\right)}\ .

(2.22)

This is the natural choice that comes from the normalization of the vertex operators using the free-field Wakimoto variables in the unflowed sector. See also appendix
B
for a slightly longer discussion on this.
\nu
is here an undetermined constant that can be thought of as the worldsheet cosmological constant. It is undetermined in the Wakimoto variables and we will leave it unfixed.

3
Global and local Ward identities

In this section we study the various constraints that correlators of the form

\left\langle\prod_{l=1}^{n}V_{j_{l},h_{l}}^{w_{l}}(x_{l};z_{l})\right\rangle

(3.1)

should obey. We mostly focus on symmetry constraints that apply equally to left- and right-movers. Consequently we shall often suppress the anti-holomorphic dependence of these correlators.
We start discussing global Ward identities on the worldsheet and in spacetime in Section
3.1
and proceed in Section
3.2
by reviewing local Ward identity constraints, mainly following
Eberhardt:2019ywk
.

3.1
Global Ward identities

Correlators satisfy global Ward identities both on the worldsheet and in spacetime. They respectively amount to

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}^{\ell+1}\partial_{x_{i}}+(\ell+1)\,h_{i}\,x_{i}^{\ell}\right)\left\langle\prod_{l=1}^{n}V_{j_{l},h_{l}}^{w_{l}}(x_{l};z_{l})\right\rangle=0\ ,

(3.2a)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(z_{i}^{\ell+1}\partial_{z_{i}}+(\ell+1)\ \Delta_{i}\ z_{i}^{\ell}\right)\left\langle\prod_{l=1}^{n}V_{j_{l},h_{l}}^{w_{l}}(x_{l};z_{l})\right\rangle=0\ ,

(3.2b)

for
\ell\in\{-1,0,1\}
. Here

\Delta_{i}=-\frac{j_{i}\,(j_{i}-1)}{k-2}-w_{i}\,h_{i}+\frac{k\,w_{i}^{2}}{4}

(3.3)

is the worldsheet conformal weight. See e.g.
Maldacena:2000hw
for an explanation of this formula.
Similar relations hold for the anti-holomorphic coordinates
\bar{x}_{i}
and
\bar{z}_{i}
. In terms of the
y
-transform introduced in Section
2.4
, eqs. (
3.2
) read

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(x_{i}^{\ell+1}\partial_{x_{i}}+(\ell+1)\,x_{i}^{\ell}\,\left(h_{i}^{0}+y_{i}\partial_{y_{i}}\right)\right)\left\langle\prod_{l=1}^{n}V_{j_{l}}^{w_{l}}(x_{l};y_{l};z_{l})\right\rangle=0\ ,

(3.4a)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(z_{i}^{\ell+1}\partial_{z_{i}}+(\ell+1)\,z_{i}^{\ell}\,\left(\Delta_{i}^{0}-w_{i}\,y_{i}\partial_{y_{i}}\right)\right)\left\langle\prod_{l=1}^{n}V_{j_{l}}^{w_{l}}(x_{l};y_{l};z_{l})\right\rangle=0\ ,

(3.4b)

where again
\ell\in\{-1,0,1\}
and we defined

\displaystyle h_{i}^{0}

\displaystyle=j_{i}+\frac{k\,w_{i}}{2}\ ,

(3.5a)

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}^{0}

\displaystyle=-\frac{j_{i}\,(j_{i}-1)}{k-2}-w_{i}\left(j_{i}+\frac{k\,w_{i}}{4}\right)\ .

(3.5b)

For two- and three-point functions global Ward identities specify the

\hskip 100.0pt+\frac{w_{i+1}}{N}a_{i+1}\left(h_{i+1}-\tfrac{k}{2}w_{i+1}-j_{i+1}+1\right)\left\langle h_{i+1}+1\right\rangle_{3}\\
\hskip 100.0pt+\frac{w_{i+2}}{N}a_{i+2}\left(h_{i+2}-\tfrac{k}{2}w_{i+2}-j_{i+2}+1\right)\left\langle h_{i+2}+1\right\rangle_{3}\\
+\left(-\frac{w_{i}}{N}\sum_{l=1}^{n}h_{l}+2h_{i}\right)\left\langle\,\dots\,\right\rangle_{3}\ ,

(3.15)

where indices are understood to be mod 3,
N=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}(w_{i}-1)+1
and

a_{i}=\frac{\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(w_{i}+w_{i+1}+w_{i+2}-1)\\
\frac{1}{2}(-w_{i}+w_{i+1}+w_{i+2}-1)\end{pmatrix}}{\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{2}(-w_{i}+w_{i+1}-w_{i+2}-1)\\
\frac{1}{2}(w_{i}+w_{i+1}-w_{i+2}-1)\end{pmatrix}}\ .

(3.16)

The
y
-transform of (

) will be useful in the following. It reads

\Bigl[-a_{i}^{-1}y_{i}\,(y_{i}\partial_{y_{i}}+2j_{i})+\left(\frac{w_{i}}{N}-1\right)a_{i}\,\partial_{y_{i}}+\\
\frac{w_{i+1}}{N}\,a_{i+1}\,\partial_{y_{i+1}}+\frac{w_{i+2}}{N}\,a_{i+2}\,\partial_{y_{i+2}}-\frac{w_{i}}{N}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\left(y_{l}\partial_{y_{l}}+j_{l}+\tfrac{k}{2}w_{l}\right)\\
+2\left(y_{i}\partial_{y_{i}}+j_{i}+\tfrac{k}{2}w_{i}\right)\Bigr]\left\langle V_{j_{1}}^{w_{1}}(0;y_{1};0)\,V_{j_{2}}^{w_{2}}(1;y_{2};1)\,V_{j_{3}}^{w_{3}}(\infty;y_{3};\infty)\right\rangle=0\ .

(3.17)

The fact that these constraints become partial differential equations in
y
-space is the principal reason that we introduce the
y
-transform in the first place.
Similarly, for all the configurations of
w_{i}
satisfying (

and reproduce them explicitly with the ancillary
Mathematica

Truncations.

Let us comment on the representation coefficients in (
2.5
). While this choice is legitimate — it gives rise to the Casimir (
2.4
) — some readers may find it unnatural and may prefer to flip the spins according to
j\to 1-j
. The choice made in (
2.5
) is however necessary in order to implement the correct truncation of discrete representations at the level of local Ward identities. Let us explain this by means of an example. Consider the local Ward identities of
Eberhardt:2019ywk
for a three-point function with spectral flow parameters
(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})=(1,1,1)
,

c_{j_{1}}^{-}\left\langle V^{1}_{j_{1},h_{1}-1}(0;0)V^{1}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(1;1)V^{1}_{j_{3},h_{3}}(\infty;\infty)\right\rangle\\
-(h_{1}-h_{2}-h_{3})\left\langle V^{1}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(0;0)V^{1}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(1;1)V^{1}_{j_{3},h_{3}}(\infty;\infty)\right\rangle\\
-c_{j_{3}}^{+}\left\langle V^{1}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(0;0)V^{1}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(1;1)V^{1}_{j_{3},h_{3}+1}(\infty;\infty)\right\rangle\\
=c_{j_{2}}^{+}\left\langle V^{1}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(0;0)V^{1}_{j_{2},h_{2}+1}(1;1)V^{1}_{j_{3},h_{3}}(\infty;\infty)\right\rangle\ ,

(3.18)

where
h_{i}=m_{i}+\frac{k}{2}

and we defined the representation coefficients
c_{j}^{\pm}
according to

J^{+}_{w}[\ket{j,m}]^{w}=c^{+}_{j}[\ket{j,m+1}]^{w}\ ,\qquad J^{-}_{-w}[\ket{j,m}]^{w}=c^{-}_{j}[\ket{j,m-1}]^{w}\ .

(3.19)

We shall consider the case where all vertex operators entering the correlator in the right-hand-side of (
3.18
) are lowest weight states of a
\mathcal{D}_{j}^{+}
representation, see eq. (
2.3a
). This corresponds to the choice

h_{1}=\frac{k}{2}+j_{1}\ ,\qquad h_{2}=-1+\frac{k}{2}+j_{2}\ ,\qquad h_{3}=\frac{k}{2}+j_{3}\ .

(3.20)

All correlators in the left-hand-side of (
3.18
) vanish, as they contain vertex operators with
h_{2}\leq-1+\frac{k}{2}+j_{2}
, which is outside the range (
2.3a
). It follows that
c_{j_{2}}^{+}
must also vanish for
h_{2}=-1+\frac{k}{2}+j_{2}
. Hence a consistent choice is

c_{j}^{+}=h-\frac{k}{2}+1-j\ ,

(3.21)

as in eq. (
2.5
). The representation coefficient
c_{j}^{-}
can be fixed by a similar argument or by requiring the Casimir to be as in eq. (
2.4
). On top of being the convention chosen by some of the standard references in the literature
Maldacena:2000hw
;
Maldacena:2000kv
;
Maldacena:2001km
, it will become clear in the next sections that this choice of representation coefficients also makes all formulas more uniform.

4
Two-point functions

As a warm up to three-point functions in the next section, we consider two-point functions first,

\left\langle V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle

(4.1)

with
w_{1},w_{2}>0
. We should mention that the following derivation is not necessarily the quickest route to the two-point function (
4.1
): the aim here is to introduce the reader to Section
5
, where three-point functions will be studied by similar techniques. We can be completely explicit in all the computations and show that (

) is entirely fixed by symmetries up to normalization. These conditions will be stronger than the solution of the global Ward identities (

). For instance they imply that the two-point function can be only non-vanishing for

. Let us look at the correlator with the insertion of a

\left\langle J^{a}(z)\,V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle\ .

(4.2)

One can derive a global Ward identity for this type of correlator as well, which relates the three correlators with
J^{+}(z)
,
J^{3}(z)
and
J^{-}(z)
insertions. Solvability of these constraints requires

h_{1}=h_{2}\qquad\text{or}\qquad h_{1}=h_{2}+1\qquad\text{or}\qquad h_{1}=h_{2}-1

(4.3)

and fixes the coordinate dependence together with the worldsheet global Ward identities to be

\displaystyle\left\langle J^{+}(z)\,V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{C\,(x_{1}-x_{2})^{-h_{1}-h_{2}-1}}{(z-z_{1})^{\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}+1}(z-z_{2})^{\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{1}+1}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1}}\ ,

(4.4a)

\displaystyle\left\langle J^{3}(z)\,V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(1-h_{1}+h_{2})\,x_{1}+(1+h_{1}-h_{2})\,x_{2}}{2}\,\left\langle J^{+}(z)\,V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle\ ,

(4.4b)

\displaystyle\left\langle J^{-}(z)\,V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(h_{1}-h_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2}-1)\,x_{1}^{2}-2((h_{2}-h_{1})^{2}-1)\,x_{1}x_{2}+(h_{1}-h_{2})(h_{1}-h_{2}+1)\,x_{2}^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 50.0pt\times\,\left\langle J^{+}(z)\,V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle\ ,

(4.4c)

where
C
is an arbitrary constant that may depend on
w_{1},w_{2},j_{1},j_{2},h_{1},h_{2}
. In the following we assume eq. (
4.3
) to be always satisfied. The analysis of
Eberhardt:2019ywk
suggests to consider for
z\to z_{i}
the combination

\left\langle\bigl(J^{-}(z)-2\,x_{i}\,J^{3}(z)+x_{i}^{2}J^{+}(z)\bigr)\,V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle\\
=(z-z_{i})^{w_{i}-1}\Bigl(h_{i}-\frac{k\,w_{i}}{2}+j_{i}-1\Bigr)\left\langle h_{i}-1\right\rangle_{2}+\mathcal{O}\bigl((z-z_{i})^{w}\bigr)\ ,

where the right-hand-side follows from the form of the OPE (

) and (

) and we used the notation (

). On the other hand, making use of (

(4.6)

where

is the index not equal to

) vanishes identically whenever

.
Comparing with eq. (
4.5
), this implies that also
\langle h_{i}-1\rangle=0

\left\langle\bigl(J^{-}(z)-2\,x_{i}\,J^{3}(z)+x_{i}^{2}J^{+}(z)\bigr)\,V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle\\
=\frac{C\,(x_{1}-x_{2})^{-2h_{1}+2}}{(z-z_{1})^{\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{2}+1}(z-z_{2})^{\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{1}+1}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-1}}\ .

Normalization.

We will now fix the normalization of the vertex operators. We choose
B\equiv(2\pi)^{2}i
. Reflection symmetry then also fully determines
A
, so that for continuous representations we obtain

\left\langle V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1},h_{1}}(x_{1};z_{1})\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2},h_{2}}(x_{2};z_{2})\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{2}i\,\delta^{(2)}(h_{1}-h_{2})\,\delta_{w_{1},w_{2}}\ (x_{1}-x_{2})^{-h_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})^{-h_{1}}\\
\times(\bar{x}_{1}-\bar{x}_{2})^{-\bar{h}_{1}}(\bar{x}_{2}-\bar{x}_{1})^{-\bar{h}_{1}}(z_{1}-z_{2})^{-\Delta_{1}}(z_{2}-z_{1})^{-\Delta_{1}}(\bar{z}_{1}-\bar{z}_{2})^{-\bar{\Delta}_{1}}(\bar{z}_{2}-\bar{z}_{1})^{-\bar{\Delta}_{1}}\\
\times\Bigl(R(j_{1},h_{1},\bar{h}_{1})\,\delta(j_{1}-j_{2})+\delta(j_{1}+j_{2}-1)\Bigr)\ .

(4.11)

Here,
\delta^{(2)}(h)\equiv\delta_{h,\bar{h}}\delta(h+\bar{h})

-basis where this result takes the form (putting

+\delta_{w_{1},w_{2}}\,\delta(j_{1}+j_{2}-1)|y_{1}|^{-2j_{1}}|y_{2}|^{-2j_{2}}\delta^{(2)}\left(1-y_{1}y_{2}\right)\ .

y_{2}
variables. Here,

) and is related to the reflection coefficient. This formula is of course consistent with the global Ward identities (

Eqs. (

5.1
The
\boldsymbol{y}
-dependence of the three-point function

It is convenient to divide the discussion of the configurations that satisfy (
5.2

\sum_{i}w_{i}
. We will refer to them as

and

case respectively. For each case we consider the

\left\langle V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0;y_{1};0)\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2}}(1;y_{2};1)\,V^{w_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty;y_{3};\infty)\right\rangle\ ,

where we used the global Ward identities (

-transform: the recursion relations turn into a system of three linear first order partial differential equations. We have solved this system in
Mathematica
for all choices of
(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
obeying (
5.2

\sum_{i}w_{i}\leq 25
. These are 1092 possible choices for
(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})

Our strategy is then to guess the general solution to the local Ward identities from the collected data. For the odd parity case we spot the following pattern (up to an overall constant and not including the right-movers),

\displaystyle\left\langle V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0;y_{1};0)\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2}}(1;y_{2};1)\,V^{w_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty;y_{3};\infty)\right\rangle=X_{123}^{\frac{k}{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\prod_{i=1}^{3}X_{i}^{-\frac{k}{2}+j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}-2j_{i}}\ ,

(5.4)

while for the even parity case we find

\displaystyle\left\langle V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0;y_{1};0)\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2}}(1;y_{2};1)\,V^{w_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty;y_{3};\infty)\right\rangle=X_{\emptyset}^{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}-k}\prod_{i<\ell}X_{i\ell}^{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}-2j_{i}-2j_{\ell}}\ ,

(5.5)

where for any subset

, we defined

X_{I}(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})=\sum_{i\in I:\ \varepsilon_{i}=\pm 1}P_{\boldsymbol{w}+\sum_{i\in I}\varepsilon_{i}e_{i}}\prod_{i\in I}y_{i}^{\frac{1-\varepsilon_{i}}{2}}\ .

(5.6)

Notice that the prefactor

In eq. (

\boldsymbol{w}=(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})

e_{1}=(1,0,0)\ ,\quad e_{2}=(0,1,0)\ ,\quad e_{3}=(0,0,1)\ .

(5.7)

The numbers

P_{\boldsymbol{w}}=0\qquad\text{for}\qquad\sum_{j}w_{j}<2\max_{i=1,2,3}w_{i}\quad\text{or}\quad\sum_{i}w_{i}\in 2\mathds{Z}+1

(5.8)

and otherwise

P_{\boldsymbol{w}}=S_{\boldsymbol{w}}\frac{G\left(\frac{-w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}}{2}+1\right)G\left(\frac{w_{1}-w_{2}+w_{3}}{2}+1\right)G\left(\frac{w_{1}+w_{2}-w_{3}}{2}+1\right)G\left(\frac{w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}}{2}+1\right)}{G(w_{1}+1)G(w_{2}+1)G(w_{3}+1)}\ ,

G(n)=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\Gamma(i)

(5.10)

for positive integer values. See Appendix
A

The function

is a phase depending on
\boldsymbol{w}\bmod 2

S_{\boldsymbol{w}}=(-1)^{\frac{1}{2}x(x+1)}\ ,

(5.11)

where

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{w}

\displaystyle\sim(0,0,0):\quad x=\frac{w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}}{2}\ ,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{w}

\displaystyle\sim(1,0,1):\quad x=\frac{w_{1}+w_{2}-w_{3}}{2}\ ,

(5.12a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{w}

\displaystyle\sim(0,1,1):\quad x=\frac{w_{1}-w_{2}+w_{3}}{2}\ ,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{w}

\displaystyle\sim(1,1,0):\quad x=\frac{-w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}}{2}\ .

(5.12b)

For example (again up to an overall constant and without including right-movers)

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{j_{1}}^{3}(0;y_{1};0)V_{j_{2}}^{3}(1;y_{2};1)V_{j_{3}}^{3}(\infty;y_{3};\infty)\right\rangle=\left(10-y_{1}\right){}^{-j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}-\frac{k}{2}}\left(10-y_{2}\right){}^{j_{1}-j_{2}+j_{3}-\frac{k}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1-6y_{3}\right){}^{j_{1}+j_{2}-j_{3}-\frac{k}{2}}\left(20-y_{1}-y_{2}-20y_{3}-4y_{1}y_{3}-4y_{2}y_{3}+y_{1}y_{2}y_{3}\right){}^{\frac{k}{2}-j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}}\ ,

(5.13)

\displaystyle\left\langle V_{j_{1}}^{4}(0;y_{1};0)V_{j_{2}}^{6}(1;y_{2};1)V_{j_{3}}^{4}(\infty;y_{3};\infty)\right\rangle=\left(35+y_{1}-50y_{2}+10y_{1}y_{2}\right){}^{-j_{1}-j_{2}+j_{3}}

Exchange symmetry.

Let us also mention another cross check that we performed on our conjectured solution for the three-point functions. The worldsheet CFT that we consider is a bosonic CFT and as such three-point functions should have bosonic exchange symmetry. It is by no means obvious that our formulae (
5.4
) and (

) satisfy this constraint. This requires that
P_{(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})}
and
P_{(w_{2},w_{1},w_{3})}
agree up to signs and those signs determine the statistics of the interchange. One can check that the perhaps peculiar looking signs (
5.11
) are exactly what is needed to give an answer that has bosonic exchange statistics.

5.3
Fusion rules

As a byproduct of our analysis we can read off the fusion rules of the theory (or rather the non-vanishing three-point functions from which we will deduce the fusion rules).
Let us analyze the case of even parity to illustrate how these fusion rules arise. We gave the corresponding solution in the
y
-basis representation in (

For illustration, let us consider the correlator with three
\mathcal{D}^{-}_{j}

1.

\mathcal{O}(x^{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}-\frac{k\delta}{2}})
as
x\to 0
.

We think that our investigation has clarified the origin of the spectral flow violation rule (
5.36
). In particular, this leads to more restrictive fusion rules than the ones we found in Section
5.3
. This is however an artifact of the too restrictive choice of states in the Verma module. What we have shown is that if one looks at coherent states obtained by acting with
\mathrm{e}^{xJ_{0}^{+}}
in the Verma module then more fusion channels are possible.
It is of course a logical possibility that we also still miss fusion channels because one should look at even more general states in the Verma module than the ones we did. We believe this not to be the case.

We should hasten to say that the bounds (
5.36
) have an important significance in holography and hence the study of these restricted fusion channels is well-motivated. In fact, there is an important class of operators with regular OPEs in the dual CFT (i.e.
x
-space) in the
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
supersymmetric setup. These are chiral primaries forming the chiral ring and the corresponding three-point functions are extremal three-point functions. Hence to study them it is sufficient to consider vertex operators at
x=0
or
x=\infty
.

5.6
A conjecture for the normalisation

We have seen in the examples of the previous section that the normalization that was unfixed by symmetry simply took the form

C(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3};j_{1},j_{2},j_{3};k)=\begin{cases}D(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})\ ,\quad w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}\in 2\mathds{Z}\ ,\\
\mathcal{N}(j_{1})D(\tfrac{k}{2}-j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})\ ,\quad w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{3}\in 2\mathds{Z}+1\ .\end{cases}

(5.49)

The second case looks asymmetric in the three variables, but this is actually not the case thanks to identity (
B.8a
). In fact, identities (
B.8a
) and (
B.8b
) imply that we could have replaced any number of spins
j_{i}\to\frac{k}{2}-j_{i}
, as long as we swap an even number for the even parity case and an odd number of the odd parity case. This makes our conjecture very natural and in fact this completely fixes the normalization constant
\mathcal{N}(j)
.
We should mention that we put an explicit normalization factor of
X_{\emptyset}^{j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}-k}
in the even parity solution (
5.5
). For the case
\boldsymbol{w}=(w,w,0)
that we have compared to the example, this factor is 1 and hence not observable. We nonetheless think that its presence is natural, because in this way both the even and odd parity cases have four factors. More convincingly, this factor is present together with a non-trivial function of the crossratios in the four-point function to which we return in a future publication
paper2
.
We conjecture that these formulae hold true in general, regardless of the values of
(w_{1},w_{2},w_{3})
. Below we collect some further evidence for this by showing that this factor is necessary for the result to be reflection symmetric. More evidence will follow in a future publication
paper2
.

To summarize, we conjecture that the three-point functions take the following form

\displaystyle\left\langle V^{w_{1}}_{j_{1}}(0;y_{1};0)\,V^{w_{2}}_{j_{2}}(1;y_{2};1)\,V^{w_{3}}_{j_{3}}(\infty;y_{3};\infty)\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\begin{cases}D(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})|X_{\emptyset}|^{2\sum_{l}j_{l}-2k}\displaystyle\prod_{i<\ell}^{3}|X_{i\ell}|^{2\sum_{l}j_{l}-4j_{i}-4j_{\ell}}\ ,&\sum_{i}w_{i}\in 2\mathds{Z}\ ,\\
\mathcal{N}(j_{1})D(\tfrac{k}{2}-j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})|X_{123}|^{k-2\sum_{l}j_{l}}\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{3}|X_{i}|^{2\sum_{l}j_{l}-4j_{i}-k}\ ,&\sum_{i}w_{i}\in 2\mathds{Z}+1\ .\end{cases}

(5.50)

This is the main result of our paper. Let us point the reader to the various definitions that enter this formula. The quantities

that are defined for any subset

are given in (

).

are the unflowed three-point functions that are given in eq. (
B.5
).
\mathcal{N}(j)
is defined in eq. (
5.42
) in terms of
B(j)
, which is the normalization of the unflowed two-point function (
B.2
), see eq. (
B.3
).

5.7
Reflection symmetry

We subject our proposal (
5.50
) to one further consistency check. If we consider a continuous representation, then it is a non-trivial check that our formula is reflection symmetric. We now show that this is the case.

Parity even case.

Let us discuss the reflection symmetry first in the parity-even case (i.e.
\sum_{i}w_{i}\in 2\mathds{Z}
). To demonstrate reflection symmetry, we work directly in the
y
-basis, where reflection symmetry acts according to (
2.30
). Let us reflect
j_{3}
, the argument for the other spins is similar.

So we want to compute the integral

\int\mathrm{d}^{2}y_{3}\ |y-y_{3}|^{4j_{3}-4}|X_{13}|^{2j_{1}-2j_{2}-2j_{3}}|X_{23}|^{-2j_{1}+2j_{2}-2j_{3}}\ ,

(5.51)

where we omitted all factors in (
5.50
) that do not depend on
y_{3}
. One can explicitly compute this integral by changing variables to

u=\frac{(A_{2}\,y+B_{2})(A_{1}\,y_{3}+B_{1})}{(A_{1}\,y_{1}+B_{1})(A_{2}\,y_{3}+B_{2})}\ ,

(5.52)

where we have temporarily written

X_{13}=A_{1}\,y_{3}+B_{1}\ ,\qquad X_{23}=A_{2}\,y_{3}+B_{2}\ .

(5.53)

This change of variables reduces the integral to

|A_{1}B_{2}-B_{1}A_{2}|^{2-4j_{3}}|A_{1}\,y+B_{1}|^{2j_{1}-2j_{2}+2j_{3}-2}|A_{2}\,y+B_{2}|^{-2j_{1}+2j_{2}+2j_{3}-2}\\
\times\int\mathrm{d}^{2}u|u|^{2j_{1}-2j_{2}-2j_{3}}|1-u|^{4j_{3}-4}\ .

(5.54)

We have
A_{i}\,y+B_{i}=X_{i3}(y)
, by which we mean
X_{i3}
where
y_{3}
has been replaced by

. Furthermore, we have the following identity:

A_{1}B_{2}-B_{1}A_{2}=\pm X_{\emptyset}X_{12}\ .

(5.55)

We have not tried to give a general proof of this identity, but it is easy to convince oneself numerically of its validity. We thus compute

\displaystyle R_{1-j_{3}}\frac{1-2j_{3}}{\pi}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}y_{3}\ |y-y_{3}|^{4j_{3}-4}\langle V_{j_{1}}^{w_{1}}(0;y_{1};0)V_{j_{2}}^{w_{2}}(1;y_{2};1)V_{j_{3}}^{w_{3}}(\infty;y_{3};\infty)\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=R_{1-j_{3}}\frac{(1-2j_{3})\text{\textgamma}(j_{1}-j_{2}-j_{3}+1)\text{\textgamma}(2j_{3}-1)}{\text{\textgamma}(j_{1}-j_{2}+j_{3})}D(j_{1},j_{2},j_{3})|X_{\emptyset}|^{2j_{1}+2j_{2}+2(1-j_{3})}

(5.58)

This is the statement of reflection symmetry in the unflowed sector and is eq. (
B.8c
). Hence reflection symmetry in the flowed sector is a consequence of reflection symmetry in the unflowed sector.

Parity odd case.

For the spins
j_{2}
and
j_{3}

6
Conclusions, discussion and open questions

In the following, we discuss various questions that we feel are not understood or deserve a better explanation. We keep this list short, since we will continue our exploration in
paper2
.

Path integral proof.

There is an alternative route to obtain results in the worldsheet theory, namely via the Wakimoto representation. This is the analogue of the Coulomb gas representation of Liouville theory. This formulation has the advantage that it possesses a free-field action with a particular screening charge operator. This representation was used successfully to derive the
H_{3}^{+}
/Liouville correspondence (for unflowed correlators) and to compute some correlators in the
m
-basis
Giribet:2000fy
;
Giribet:2001ft
;
Iguri:2007af
;
Hikida:2007tq
;
Giribet:2015oiy
. In light of this, it seems conceivable to give a direct path-integral derivation of our proposed formula.

Supersymmetric and extremal correlators.

It is straightforward to extend our analysis to the supersymmetric case. Superstrings on
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
are described in the RNS formalism by the
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathds{R})_{k+2}
model and three free fermions. The shift in the level and the contributions of the free fermions can be readily incorporated in our formulas. In the supersymmetric context, it is particularly interesting to consider extremal correlators. They are protected against marginal deformations
deBoer:2008ss
;
Baggio:2012rr
and are hence useful as probe to test the conjectured duality between superstring theory on
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}\times\mathrm{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
and the symmetric orbifold
\text{Sym}^{N}(\mathbb{T}^{4})
(or rather a deformation thereof in the case of higher NS-NS flux). It was confirmed in
Gaberdiel:2007vu
;
Dabholkar:2007ey
;
Pakman:2007hn
that the unflowed extremal three-point functions match with those of the symmetric orbifold. This was later also extended to extremal four-point functions
Cardona:2010qf
.
19
19

19

Somewhat surprisingly also a perfect match with the boundary correlator was found for certain non-extremal correlators of chiral primaries.
Extremal correlators precisely satisfy the relation
\sum_{i}j_{i}=\frac{k(n-2)}{2}-(n-3)
where the correlators universal behaviour is described by the Liouville answer (and is hence simple). Spectrally flowed extremal three-point functions were also matched with the boundary CFT in
Giribet:2007wp
;
Cardona:2009hk
. These correlators are much better explored than the ones away from extremality that we have considered in this paper. Thus it would be interesting to compare our results with these computations.

The spectral flow operator.

The traditional way to compute correlators with spectral flow is via the spectral flow operator
\Phi
.
\Phi
is a special unflowed vertex operator with spin
j=\frac{k}{2}
that possesses a null descendant. Similar to picture changing operators in superstring theory, one can then insert a number
\sum_{i}w_{i}
of these spectral flow operators in an unflowed correlator. Taking the collision limit of these spectral flow operators with the unflowed vertex operators yields the flowed correlator
Fateev
;
Maldacena:2001km
. This method of computation is rather cumbersome because the number of necessary spectral flow operators increases rapidly with the spectral flow which is why only correlators for very low amounts of spectral flow have been computed in this fashion. In section
5.5
, we compared our correlators to some that were computed using the spectral flow operator in
Maldacena:2001km
;
Minces:2005nb
;
Fukuda:2001jd
;
Satoh:2001bi
;
Cagnacci:2013ufa
.
Nevertheless, we were not able to make this connection direct and we feel that it would be important to do so in order to provide a cross check between the different methods. It seems conceivable to give a recursive proof in the spectral flow of our conjecture by using the spectral flow operator.

Relation to integrability.

Recently, the hexagon tessellation approach to correlation functions, originally developed for
\text{AdS}_{5}\times\text{S}^{5}

Basso:2015zoa
;
Eden:2016xvg
;
Fleury:2016ykk
, has been extended to
\text{AdS}_{3}\times\text{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}

Eden:2021xhe
and some
extremal
correlators
Gaberdiel:2007vu
;
Dabholkar:2007ey
;
Pakman:2007hn
have been reproduced by integrability techniques.
Non-extremal
correlators feature a much richer structure, in particular when spectral flow is present. In fact, from the point of view of the RNS formalism, it is clear that correlators with insertions of spectrally flowed states will be expressed in terms of the generalized hypergeometric functions we encountered in Section
5
. It would then be very interesting to see how this rich functional dependence emerges in the integrability approach to correlation functions.

We shall not need the numerous other interesting properties this function satisfies.

A.2

We need this function only to define the double Gamma-function below. We set

\zeta_{2}(s,z\,|\,a_{1},a_{2})=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\geq 0}(z+n_{1}a_{1}+n_{2}a_{2})^{-s}\ ,

A.3

The double gamma function is defined as

\log\Gamma_{2}(z\,|\,1,\omega)=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}\zeta_{2}(s,z\,|\,1,\omega)\ .

(A.4)

\Gamma_{2}(z\,|\,1,\omega)=\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\lambda_{1}z+\lambda_{2}z^{2}}}{z}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}\geq 0,\,n_{2}\geq 0\\

(A.5)

(A.6)

and the coefficients

are defined in terms of the Laurent series expansion of

at

and
s=2
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=[(s-1)^{0}]\,\zeta(s,0\,|\,1,\omega)\ ,

(A.7a)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[(s-2)^{0}]\,\zeta(s,0\,|\,1,\omega)+\frac{1}{2}[(s-2)^{-1}]\,\zeta(s,0\,|\,1,\omega)\ ,

(A.7b)

where
[(s-s_{0})^{n}]
denotes the corresponding Laurent series coefficient.
The double Gamma function satisfies the following fundamental functional identities

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(z\,|\,1,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma(z)\Gamma_{2}(z+\omega\,|\,1,\omega)\ ,

(A.8a)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(z\,|\,1,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega^{\frac{z}{\omega}-\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Gamma\left(\frac{z}{\omega}\right)\Gamma_{2}(z+1\,|\,1,\omega)\ .

(A.10)

Appendix C
Surface integral identities

In this appendix, we discuss, various surface integral identities that we frequently use. We adopt the following convention for the integral measure,

\text{d}^{2}y\equiv\text{d}\mathop{\text{Re}}(y)\,\text{d}\mathop{\text{Im}}(y)\ .

(C.1)

Two singular points.

The basic identity for
a-\bar{a}\in\mathds{Z}
is

\int\mathrm{d}^{2}y\ y^{a-1}\bar{y}^{\bar{a}-1}=\pm i(2\pi)^{2}\delta_{a,\bar{a}}\,\delta(a+\bar{a})\ ,

(C.2)

where
\delta_{a,\bar{a}}
is the discrete Kronecker delta and
\delta(a+\bar{a})
is the Dirac delta. We will frequently abbreviate
\delta_{a,\bar{a}}\,\delta(a+\bar{a})
with the more compact notation
\delta^{(2)}(a)
. The sign is ambiguous. To prove this identity, one has to use analytic continuation and similarly in all the subsequent identities. We illustrate this procedure in this simple case. First, change to radial coordinates

\int\mathrm{d}^{2}y\,y^{a-1}\bar{y}^{\bar{a}-1}=2\pi\delta_{a,\bar{a}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}r\ r^{a+\bar{a}-1}\ .

(C.3)

The latter integral is obviously never convergent and we use analytic continuation to give it a value. Changing coordinates to
x=\log(r)
, we need to define the integral

\int\mathrm{d}x\ \mathrm{e}^{x(a+\bar{a})}\ .

(C.4)

We can rotate the contour in
x
to obtain the analytically continued form

\pm i\int\mathrm{d}x\ \mathrm{e}^{ix(a+\bar{a})}=\pm 2\pi i\delta(a+\bar{a})\ .

(C.5)

The sign depends on the direction in which we rotate the contour.

Since the integral is subtle, we also mention a different way to obtain the same result. Introduce a regulator in the integral as follows:

\int\mathrm{d}x\ \mathrm{e}^{x(a+\bar{a})-\varepsilon x^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\varepsilon}}\ \mathrm{e}^{\frac{(a+\bar{a})^{2}}{4\varepsilon}}\ .

(C.6)

The integral converges for
\mathop{\text{Re}}(\varepsilon)>0
. We then analytically continue the result in
\varepsilon
. The resulting Gaussian provides an approximation of the Dirac delta for
\mathop{\text{Re}}(\varepsilon)<0
. Taking the square root is ambiguous and we obtain again the result

\int\mathrm{d}x\ \mathrm{e}^{x(a+\bar{a})}=\pm 2\pi i\delta(a+\bar{a})\ ,

(C.7)

corresponding to the two different analytic continuations.

Three singular points.

Another surface integral that we often encounter is

\int\mathrm{d}^{2}y\ y^{a-1}\bar{y}^{\bar{a}-1}(1-y)^{b-1}(1-\bar{y})^{\bar{b}-1}=\frac{\pi\text{\textgamma}(a)\text{\textgamma}(b)}{\text{\textgamma}(a+b)}\ ,

(C.8)

where

\text{\textgamma}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(x)}{\Gamma(1-\bar{x})}\ .

(C.9)

By
\bar{x}
we mean the corresponding right-moving quantity, which is not the complex conjugate of
x
.
The derivation of this formula is classic in the context of the Coulomb gas formalism
Dotsenko:1984nm
or the KLT formula
Kawai:1985xq
.

We also need the following higher-dimensional generalization of this result:

\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}^{2}y_{i}\ y_{i}^{a_{i}-1}\bar{y}_{i}^{\bar{a}_{i}-1}\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}\right)^{b-1}\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bar{y}_{j}\right)^{\bar{b}-1}=\frac{\pi^{n}\text{\textgamma}(b)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\text{\textgamma}(a_{i})}{\text{\textgamma}\left(b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right)}\ ,

(C.10)

which can be proved recursively by the substitution
y_{n}=x_{n}\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}y_{i}\right)
of
y_{n}
.

D.1
Twisted (co)homology

Let
u
be a multivalued function on
\mathds{C}^{m}
with singularities on a codimension 1 sublocus
S
. Let
M=\mathds{C}^{m}\setminus S
. We are interested in computing integrals of the form

\int_{\Delta}\varphi u\ ,

(D.3)

where
\varphi
is a single-valued
m
-form and
\Delta
a cycle. Since
u
is multivalued, we also have to specify the branch of
u
we want to use. This is done by
loading

\Delta
, resulting in a twisted cocycle of the form
\Delta\otimes u_{\Delta}
, where
u_{\Delta}
labels the branch.

One can develop a (co)homology theory for the twisted cocycle
\varphi
and the twisted cycle
\Delta
. Without going into details, we list here the most important definitions and properties.

Definition of the cohomology groups.

The space of cycles
\varphi
is equipped with a twisted differential

\nabla_{\omega}\varphi=(\mathrm{d}+\omega\wedge)\varphi\ ,

(D.4)

where
\omega=\mathrm{d}\log(u)
.
\nabla_{\omega}
squares to zero and hence defines twisted deRham groups:

H^{k}(M,\nabla_{\omega})=\frac{\ker\nabla_{\omega}}{\mathop{\text{im}}\nabla_{\omega}}\ .

(D.5)

Definition of the homology groups.

Similarly, there is a dual notion of twisted homology groups. For a twisted cycle
\Delta\otimes u_{\Delta}
one can define a boundary operator
\partial_{\omega}
, since a given branch of
u
on
\Delta
naturally restricts to a branch on its boundary. Since we also have
\partial_{\omega}^{2}=0
, this defines homology groups

H_{k}(M,\mathcal{L}_{\omega})=\frac{\ker\partial_{\omega}}{\mathop{\text{im}}\partial_{\omega}}\ .

(D.6)

Vanishing of homology groups.

Contrary to ordinary deRham (co)homology, most homology groups vanish. Generically, only the top homology group is non-vanishing, i.e.
H_{k}(M,\mathcal{L}_{\omega})=\{0\}
for
k<m
.

Poincaré duality.

There is a notion of Poincaré duality for twisted (co)homology. There is a natural pairing

\displaystyle H_{m}(M,\mathcal{L}_{\omega})\times H^{m}(M,\nabla_{\omega})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathds{C}\ ,

(D.7a)

\displaystyle\langle\Delta\otimes u_{\Delta},\varphi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\Delta\otimes u_{\Delta}}\varphi(z)\equiv\int_{\Delta}(\text{branch $u_{\Delta}$ of $u$})\varphi\ .

(D.7b)

It is known that this pairing is non-degenerate and consequently there is an isomorphism
H_{m}(M,\mathcal{L}_{\omega})\allowbreak\cong\allowbreak H^{m}(M,\nabla_{\omega})
.

Compactly supported cohomology.

The manifold
M
is non-compact, so in general the above pairing might not be well-defined. For the pairing to be well-defined, one needs that either
\Delta
is compact or
\varphi
is compactly supported. There is a map

\iota_{\omega}:H^{m}(M,\nabla_{\omega})\longrightarrow H^{m}_{c}(M,\nabla_{\omega})

(D.8)

from cohomology to compactly supported cohomology defined by picking another cocycle that is compactly supported, but cohomologous to the original one.

Dual bundle.

We can also define (co)homology groups obtained by replacing
\omega
with
\bar{\omega}
. We call the corresponding bundle
\mathcal{L}^{\vee}_{\omega}=\mathcal{L}_{\bar{\omega}}
. These are the corresponding dual (co)homology groups. There exists another notion of dual bundle that is employed more often in the literature and is obtained by replacing
\omega\to-\omega
. The notions are different, but many properties carry over.

Intersection forms.

There are intersection pairings between the groups
H_{m}(M,\mathcal{L}_{\omega})
and
H_{m}(M,\mathcal{L}_{\omega}^{\vee})
and between
H^{m}(M,\nabla_{\omega})
and
H^{m}(M,\nabla_{\bar{\omega}})
. The latter is given by

\langle\varphi_{i}(z)|\varphi_{j}^{\vee}(z)\rangle=\int_{M}|u(z)|^{2}\varphi_{i}(z)\wedge\overline{\varphi_{j}^{\vee}(z)}\ ,

(D.9)

provided that the integral converges. The intersection number between homologies is defined as

\langle\Delta\otimes u_{\Delta}|\Delta^{\vee}\otimes\overline{u_{\Delta^{\vee}}}\rangle=\sum_{z\in\Delta\cap\Delta^{\vee}}\langle\Delta|\Delta^{\vee}\rangle_{z}\frac{u_{\Delta}(z)\overline{u_{\Delta^{\vee}}(z)}}{|u(z)|^{2}}=\sum_{z\in\Delta\cap\Delta^{\vee}}\langle\Delta|\Delta^{\vee}\rangle_{z}u_{\Delta}(z)u_{\Delta^{\vee}}(z)^{-1}\ ,

(D.10)

where
\langle\Delta|\Delta^{\vee}\rangle_{z}
is the topological intersection number at
z
.

Homological splitting.

Let
\Delta_{\alpha}\otimes u_{\Delta_{\alpha}}
be a basis of
H_{m}(M,\mathcal{L}_{\omega})
. We collect the intersection products in the intersection matrix:

\mathbf{I}_{\alpha\beta}=\langle\Delta_{\alpha}\otimes u_{\Delta_{\alpha}}|\Delta_{\beta}^{\vee}\otimes u_{\Delta_{\beta}^{\vee}}\rangle\ .

(D.11)

For the purpose of intersection theory, the following combination is the identity:

\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{I}^{-1})_{\beta\alpha}|\Delta_{\beta}^{\vee}\otimes u_{\Delta_{\beta}^{\vee}}\rangle\langle\Delta_{\alpha}\otimes u_{\Delta_{\alpha}}|\ .

(D.12)

Thus we can insert it in the cohomological intersection pairing to homologically split the integral:

\langle\varphi|\varphi^{\vee}\rangle=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}(\mathbf{I}^{-1})_{\beta\alpha}\langle\varphi^{\vee},\Delta_{\beta}^{\vee}\otimes u_{\Delta_{\beta}^{\vee}}\rangle\langle\Delta_{\alpha}\otimes u_{\Delta_{\alpha}},\varphi\rangle\ .

(D.13)

This reduces the integral to contour integrals.

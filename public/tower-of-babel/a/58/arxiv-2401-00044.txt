Prospects of detection of subsolar mass primordial black hole and white dwarf binary mergers
Takahiro S. Yamamoto, Ryoto Inui, Yuichiro Tada, Shuichiro Yokoyama

Abstract
The subsolar mass PBH ( PBH ) attracts attention as robust evidence of its primordial origin against the astrophysical black hole. Not only with themselves, PBH can also form binaries with ordinary astrophysical objects, catching them by GW ( GW ) bremsstrahlung. We discuss the detectability of the inspiral GW from binaries consisting of a PBH and a WD ( WD ) by using space-borne gravitational wave interferometers like DECIGO. The conservative assessment shows the expected event number in three years by DECIGO is 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 − 6 ) \mathcal{O}(10^{-6}) for M PBH ∼ 0.1 ​ M ⊙ M_{\textrm{PBH}}\sim 0.1M_{\odot} . Possible enhancement mechanisms of WD - PBH binary formation may amplify this event rate. We discuss how large enhancement associated with WD is required to detect WD-PBH merger events without violating the existing constraints on the PBH - PBH merger by the ground-based detector.

II.2
Distinguishability between WD-PBH and WD-WD

In the previous subsection, we evaluated the detectability of WD-PBH binaries.
The
GW

\mathcal{M}_{\textrm{c}}
. Therefore, if a WD-WD binary and a WD-PBH binary have similar component masses, they can not be distinguished only from the chirp mass. However, the merger frequency can be different because of the difference in the radii of
\Acp
WD and
\Acp
PBH. We discuss, here, how we can distinguish WD-WD binaries and WD-PBH binaries by the difference in the merger frequencies.

The merger frequency of WD-WD binaries, which is denoted by
f_{\textrm{merge}}^{\textrm{BWD}}
, is estimated as well as the merger frequency of WD-PBH binaries. We consider the WD-WD binary with the masses of
M_{\textrm{WD,1}}
and
M_{\textrm{WD,2}}
. We use
R_{\textrm{tidal,1}}
to denote the tidal radius in which the first
WD
(
M_{\textrm{WD,1}}
and

) is disrupted by the companion
WD
(
M_{\textrm{WD,2}}
). It is estimated by

R_{\textrm{tidal,1}}=\left(\frac{2M_{\textrm{WD,2}}}{M_{\textrm{WD,1}}}\right)^{1/3}R_{\textrm{WD,1}}\,.

(21)

By changing the labels
1\leftrightarrow 2
, the tidal radius
R_{\textrm{tidal,2}}
can be defined.
When the two
WD
approach as close as the radius

R_{\textrm{tidal}}^{\textrm{BWD}}=\max[R_{\textrm{tidal,1}},R_{\textrm{tidal,2}}]\,,

(22)

the lighter one is tidally disrupted.
We define the frequency corresponding to
R_{\textrm{tidal}}^{\textrm{BWD}}

f_{\textrm{tidal}}^{\textrm{BWD}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{G(M_{\mathrm{WD,1}}+M_{\mathrm{WD,2}})}{\quantity(R_\textrm{tidal}^\textrm{BWD})^{3}}}\,.

(23)

When

could be smaller than the sum of the WD’s radii. In such cases, as the merger frequency, we should employ the Kepler frequency at the time when the binary separation is equal to the sum of the WD’s radii:

f_{\textrm{Kepler}}^{\textrm{BWD}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{G(M_{\textrm{WD,1}}+M_{\textrm{WD,2}})}{(R_{\textrm{WD,1}}+R_{\textrm{WD,2}})^{3}}}\,.

(24)

Thus, as in the case of the WD-PBH binary, the merger frequency of the WD-WD binary is given by the lower one:

f_{\textrm{merge}}^{\textrm{BWD}}=\min[f_{\textrm{Kepler}}^{\textrm{BWD}},f_{\textrm{tidal}}^{\textrm{BWD}}]\,.

(25)

Let us parametrize the enhancement of the event number by
Tsai et al. 2021

\tilde{N}^{\textrm{WD-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}=\alpha_{\textrm{WD}}\times\alpha_{\textrm{PBH}}\times f_{\textrm{PBH}}\times N^{\textrm{WD-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}\,,

(30)

where
\alpha_{\textrm{WD}}
and
\alpha_{\textrm{PBH}}
are the enhancement factors induced by some mechanisms related to the
\Acp
WD and the
\Acp
PBH, respectively.

Even forgetting about the realistic astrophysical processes,
\alpha_{\mathrm{PBH}}
cannot be enlarged arbitrarily because it would conflict with the current constraint on the
PBH
-
PBH
merger rate achieved by the ground-based
GW
detectors.
As we explained in Sec.
I
, PBH-PBH binaries are formed in two channels, and the early-time formation basically dominates the late-time formation. However, if the enhancement factor
\alpha_{\mathrm{PBH}}
for
WD
-
PBH
is too large, it would also enhance the late-time
PBH
-
PBH
formation, making it dominant and leading to the violation of the current constraint on the event rate of subsolar mass
PBH
binary coalescences.
To discuss it quantitatively, we calculate the event number of PBH-PBH mergers in the late-time formation scenario.
Because
\alpha_{\mathrm{PBH}}
phenomenologically parametrizes the enhancement due to the
PBH
properties, the
PBH
-
PBH
merger rate would also be enhanced in a similar way as

\tilde{N}^{\textrm{PBH-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}\sim(\alpha_{\textrm{PBH}}f_{\textrm{PBH}})^{2}N^{\textrm{PBH-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}

(31)

where
N^{\textrm{PBH-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}
is the expected number of PBH-PBH mergers estimated with
f_{\textrm{PBH}}=1
and
\alpha_{\textrm{PBH}}=1
. So far, binary mergers with masses of less than
1M_{\odot}
have not been detected.
Therefore,
\tilde{N}^{\textrm{PBH-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}
should be less than unity. For
M_{\textrm{PBH}}=10^{-1}M_{\odot}
as our interest, we find
N^{\textrm{PBH-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}\sim 1\times 10^{-5}
(see Fig.
10
in the appendix). Therefore, we get the upper bound

\tilde{N}^{\textrm{PBH-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}\lesssim 1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\alpha_{\textrm{PBH}}f_{\textrm{PBH}}\lesssim 3\times 10^{2}\,.

(32)

Substituting this into
\tilde{N}^{\textrm{WD-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}
and taking the value
N^{\textrm{WD-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}=2\times 10^{-6}
, the required enhancement factor
\alpha_{\textrm{WD}}
for the detectable event rate of
WD
-
PBH
binaries would be

\tilde{N}^{\textrm{WD-PBH}}_{\textrm{event}}\gtrsim 1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\alpha_{\textrm{WD}}\gtrsim 2\times 10^{3}\,.

(33)

If such an enhancement would be realized, one or more WD-PBH merger events could be detected by DECIGO without contradicting the current constraint on the
PBH
-
PBH
merger rate.

IV
Conclusions and future perspective

We have investigated the detectability of
WD
and subsolar mass
PBH
binaries formed by the
GW
bremsstrahlung.
To this end, in Sec.
II
, we first investigated the maximum distance of the detectable binaries as the horizon distance assuming the simple waveform model given by Eqs. (
7
)–(
9
) and
the three-year observation of DECIGO with its design sensitivity. We have shown that the
\Acp
GW from WD-PBH binaries at
\sim$1\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{c}$
are detectable when their mass satisfies
M_{\textrm{WD}}\gtrsim 0.8M_{\odot}
and
M_{\textrm{PBH}}\gtrsim 0.5M_{\odot}
. Second, we discussed the distinguishability between WD-PBH and WD-WD mergers by estimating the merger frequency from the comparison of their tidal and contact radii. We found that WD-PBH mergers can be distinguished from other binaries just by looking at the merger frequency unless the
WD
mass is less than
\sim 0.55M_{\odot}
. Finally, We evaluated the merger rate and the expected number of WD-PBH merger events in Sec.
III
. We assumed the standard halo properties and simplified
WD
distributions. The conservative expectation of the number of WD-PBH merger events is too small (
\sim\mathcal{O}(10^{-6})
) to be detected for
M_{\textrm{PBH}}\sim 10^{-1}M_{\odot}
.

In a specific situation such as clustering distribution or low velocity-dispersion of target objects, the expected number of WD-PBH merger events would be much enhanced. We should note that an arbitrarily large enhancement due to some mechanisms related to
PBH
would contradict the current constraint on the PBH-PBH merger rate in the ground-based detectors because the same mechanisms would also promote the
PBH
-
PBH
merger.
We conclude that
\mathcal{O}(10^{3})
enhancement due to WD-related mechanisms could enable the detection of
\mathcal{O}(1)
WD-PBH merger events by three-year DECIGO observation without violating the current
PBH
-
PBH
constraint.

One may include additional effects to improve the detectability and distinguishability of PBH-WD binaries. For example, the waveform modeling can be improved by, e.g., the tidal deformability of
\Acp
WD. As a binary becomes closer, the
WD
is gradually deformed, resulting in the modification in the gravitational waveform. This modification is significant around the merger.
Another example is the effect of mass transfer. The WD-WD binary under the stable mass transfer is discussed in the literature
Paczyński 1967
;
Kremer et al. 2017
. It is known that there is the possibility that the binary separation becomes wider as the mass transfers from the smaller star to the heavier star (called
outspiral
). The
\Acp
GW from such a binary are much different from that of the case without the mass transfer.
The synergy between
\Acp
GW and electromagnetic emissions can also be beneficial in identifying the source. WD-WD binary mergers are candidates of type Ia supernovae
Iben and Tutukov 1984
;
Webbink 1984
. WD-PBH binary mergers could emit electromagnetic emissions that are different from those of WD-WD binary mergers. On the analogy of NS-BH binaries, tidal disruption events can also be expected from the WD-PBH binary mergers.
Numerical simulations for these merger events would be interesting (see
Markin et al. 2023
for a numerical simulation of a binary coalescence of a
NS
and a subsolar mass
PBH
).
As well as concrete mechanisms for the merger rate enhancement discussed in Sec.
III
, these points should be worth studying in future work.

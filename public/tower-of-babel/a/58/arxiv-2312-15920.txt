Abstract
. We establish hyperweak boundedness of area functions, square functions, maximal operators and Calderón–Zygmund operators on products of two stratified Lie groups.

1.
Introduction and statement of main results

Since the 1980s, the development of multiparameter harmonic analysis has proceeded apace; recent contributions in the area include
[
1
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
16
,
17
,
19
,
21
,
22
,
23
]
.
Much of the product space theory on
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, including the duality of
\mathsf{H}^{1}
with
\mathsf{BMO}
, characterisation of
\mathsf{H}^{1}
by square functions and atomic decompositions, and interpolation, has been extended to more general products of spaces of homogeneous type.

In contrast to the classical theory of singular integrals, in multiparameter harmonic analysis product singular integrals are not of weak type
(1,1)
.
For functions supported on the unit cube, the classical weak type
(1,1)
estimate was replaced by a
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}
to
\mathsf{L}^{1,\infty}
estimate by R. Fefferman
[
8
]
.
More precisely, let
\mathcal{T}
be a product Calderón–Zygmund operator on
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
as in
[
17
]
or
[
8
]
; then for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle|\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|\mathcal{T}f(x_{1},x_{2})|>\lambda\}|\leq\frac{C}{\lambda}\|f\|_{\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(Q_{1}\times Q_{2})}

for all
f
supported in the product
Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
of the unit cubes in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(Q_{1}\times Q_{2})}=\iint_{Q_{1}\times Q_{2}}|f(x_{1},x_{2})|(1+\log^{+}(|f(x_{1},x_{2})|))\,\mathrm{d}x_{2}\,\mathrm{d}x_{1};

here
\log^{+}t:=\log(\max\{1,t\})
.
The proof in
[
8
]
relies on the boundedness of the strong maximal function and the area function from
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}
to
\mathsf{L}^{1,\infty}
, the local atomic decomposition of functions in
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}
produced using the
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}
to
\mathsf{L}^{1,\infty}
boundedness of the area function, and the boundedness of
\mathcal{T}
on
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}
atoms.

A natural question arises: is (
1.1
) true globally for area functions, square functions, maximal operators and singular integral operators, even in more general product settings?

In this paper, we answer the above question positively on product spaces
\mathbf{G}:=G_{1}\times G_{2}
, where
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are stratified Lie groups.
To state our results, we need a little notation.
Details may be found later.

In this introduction, the auxiliary functions
\varphi^{[i]}
on
G_{i}
satisfy standard decay and smoothness conditions and have integral
1
.
Likewise, the functions
\psi^{[i]}
on
G_{i}
satisfy standard decay and smoothness and have integral
0
.
We write
\zeta^{[1]}_{t_{1}}
and
\zeta^{[2]}_{t_{2}}
for normalised dilates of functions
\zeta^{[1]}
on
G_{1}
and
\zeta^{[2]}
on
G_{2}
, and
\varphi_{\mathbf{t}}
and
\psi_{\mathbf{t}}
for the product functions
\varphi^{[1]}_{t_{1}}\otimes\varphi^{[2]}_{t_{2}}
and
\psi^{[1]}_{t_{1}}\otimes\psi^{[2]}_{t_{2}}
.

We define
\mathbf{T}:=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
For
\mathbf{g}:=(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathbf{G}
,
\mathbf{t}\in\mathbf{T}
and
\eta\in[0,\infty)
, we denote by
P(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{t})
the product of open balls
B_{1}(g_{1},t_{1})\times B_{2}(g_{2},t_{2})
and by
\Gamma^{\eta}(\mathbf{g})
the product cone
\Gamma_{1}^{\eta}(g_{1})\times\Gamma_{2}^{\eta}(g_{2})
, where

\Gamma_{i}^{\eta}(g_{i}):=\{(h_{i},t_{i})\in G_{i}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\colon\rho_{i}(g_{i},h_{i})\leq\eta t_{i}\}.

For
\eta\in[0,\infty)
and
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathbf{G})
, we define the maximal function:

\mathcal{M}_{\varphi,\eta}(f)(\mathbf{g}):=\sup\Bigl\{\bigl|f\ast\varphi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{h})\bigr|\colon(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{t})\in\Gamma^{\eta}(\mathbf{g})\Bigr\}\qquad\forall\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}.

This maximal function is called radial when
\eta=0
and nontangential when
\eta>0
.

For
\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathbf{G})
, we define the Lusin area function
\mathcal{S}_{\psi,\eta}(f)
by

\mathcal{S}_{\psi,\eta}(f)(\mathbf{g}):=\left(\iint_{\Gamma^{\eta}(\mathbf{g})}\frac{\left|(f\ast\psi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{h})\right|^{2}}{\left|P(\mathbf{g},\eta\mathbf{t})\right|}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}\right)^{1/2}\qquad\forall\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}.

When
\eta=0
and
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathbf{G})
, we define the Littlewood–Paley square function
\mathcal{S}_{\psi,0}(f)
by

\mathcal{S}_{\psi,0}(f)(\mathbf{g}):=\left(\int_{\mathbf{T}}\left|(f\ast\psi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{g})\right|^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}\right)^{1/2}\qquad\forall\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}.

These are often given different symbols, but it is convenient to treat them together.

On a stratified Lie group
G
, we may take a basis
\{\mathcal{X}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{X}_{d}\}
for the space of left-invariant horizontal vector fields, and define the sublaplacian
\mathcal{L}
to be
-\sum_{j-1}^{d}\mathcal{X}_{j}^{2}
.
The Riesz transformations are then the operators
\mathcal{X}_{j}\mathcal{L}^{-1/2}
.
The double Riesz transformations
\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{[2]}_{j_{2}}
on
\mathbf{G}
are defined in the obvious way when
1\leq j_{i}\leq d_{i}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi,\eta}
or a Littlewood–Paley operator
\mathcal{S}_{\psi,\eta}
, where
\eta\geq 0
, or a double Riesz transformation
\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{[2]}_{j_{2}}
.
Then

(1.2)

\displaystyle\big|\big\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon|\mathcal{T}f(\mathbf{g})|>\lambda\big\}\big|\lesssim F_{\Phi}({f/\lambda})\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

for all
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
,
where
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
is the Orlicz space associated to the functional
F_{\Phi}
, given by

\displaystyle F_{\Phi}(f):=\iint_{\mathbf{G}}|f(\mathbf{g})|\log(\mathrm{e}+|f(\mathbf{g})|)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}.

It is not possible to replace
F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)
by
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})}/\lambda
, where
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})}
is the Luxemburg norm associated to
F_{\Phi}
.
We explain Orlicz spaces and Luxemburg norms later.
We say that the operator
\mathcal{T}
is hyperweakly bounded when an estimate of the form (
1.2
) holds.

It is easy to iterate estimates for one-parameter maximal, area or singular integral operators to prove a local version of this result, where the support of the function
f
is restricted to lie in a compact set.
However, iteration does not seem to be able to deal with the global case, and this is the main difficulty that we need to confront in this paper.

Our presentation has the following structure.
In Section 2 we review some background on stratified Lie groups, product spaces and Orlicz spaces.

In Section 3 we show that the strong maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{s}
is hyperweakly bounded, using a covering lemma, Theorem
3.2
, that goes back to
[
5
]
.
We then prove Theorem
1.1
for the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi,\eta}
, by using group properties to dominate the maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\varphi,\eta}
by the strong maximal operator.

In Section 4, we construct the atomic decomposition.
We use the gradient
\not{\nabla}p
of the Poisson kernel
p
as our auxiliary function, and then have a key result of
[
6
, (12) and following]
, namely, the good-
\lambda
inequality:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\bigl|\bigl\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}(f)(\mathbf{g})>\lambda\bigr\}\bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl|L_{\eta}^{c}\bigr|+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{L_{1}(\lambda)}\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}^{2}(f)(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g},

when
\eta
is sufficiently large; here
L_{\eta}(\lambda):=\left\{\mathbf{g}\in{\mathbf{G}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}(f)(\mathbf{g})\leq\lambda\right\}
.
This implies that the area operator
\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}
is hyperweakly bounded.
By using this boundedness and the Calderón reproducing formula, we can decompose global
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}
functions into atoms.

In Section 5, we apply our atomic decomposition and a version of Journé’s covering lemma for spaces of homogeneous type established in
[
12
]
.
We prove Theorem
1.1
for
general
operators
\mathcal{S}_{\psi,\eta}
and the double Riesz transformations
\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{[2]}_{j_{2}}
.
The same argument holds for general product Calderón–Zygmund operators, as in Journé
[
17
]
.

Most of the arguments rely only the theory of spaces of homogeneous type.
However, we need the setting of a stratified Lie group in two places.
First, it gives us the good
\lambda
inequality (
1.3
).
Second, it gives us the Calderón reproducing formula for
\mathsf{L}^{2}(G)
functions, which is needed for the atomic decomposition.

“Constants” are positive real numbers, depending only on the geometry of
\mathbf{G}
unless otherwise indicated; we write
A\lesssim B
when there exists a constant
C
such that
A\leq CB
.
We write
\chi_{E}
for the indicator function of a set
E
, and
o
denotes the identity of a group.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Stratified nilpotent Lie groups

Let
G
be a (real and finite dimensional) stratified nilpotent Lie group of step
s
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
This means that we may write
\mathfrak{g}
as a vector space direct sum
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\mathfrak{v}_{j}
, where
[\mathfrak{v}_{1},\mathfrak{v}_{j}]=\mathfrak{v}_{j+1}
when
1\leq j\leq s
; here
\mathfrak{v}_{s+1}:=\{0\}
.
Let
\nu
denote the homogeneous dimension of
G
; that is,
\sum_{j=1}^{s}j\dim\mathfrak{v}_{j}
.
There is a one-parameter family of automorphic dilations
\delta_{t}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, given by

\displaystyle\delta_{t}(\mathcal{X}_{1}+\mathcal{X}_{2}+\dots+\mathcal{X}_{s}):=t\mathcal{X}_{1}+t^{2}\mathcal{X}_{2}+\dots+t^{s}\mathcal{X}_{s};

here each
\mathcal{X}_{j}\in\mathfrak{v}_{j}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
The exponential mapping
\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}\to G
is a diffeomorphism, and we identify
\mathfrak{g}
and
G
.
The dilations extend to automorphic dilations of
G
, also denoted by
\delta_{t}
, by conjugation with
\exp
.
The Haar measure on
G
, which is bi-invariant, is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathfrak{g}
lifted to
G
using
\exp
.

By
[
13
]
, the group
G
may be equipped with a smooth subadditive homogeneous norm
\rho
, a continuous function from
G
to
[0,\infty)
that is smooth on
G\setminus\{o\}

and satisfies

(1)

\rho(g^{-1})=\rho(g)
;

(2)

\rho(g^{-1}h)\leq\rho(g)+\rho(h)
;

(3)

\rho({\delta_{t}(g)})=t\rho(g)
for all
g\in G
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
;

(4)

\rho(g)=0
if and only if
g=o
.

Abusing notation, we set
\rho(g,g^{\prime}):=\rho(g^{-1}g^{\prime})

for all
g,g^{\prime}\in G
; this defines a metric on
G
.
We write
B(g,r)
for the open ball with centre
g
and radius
r
with respect to
\rho
:

B(g,r)=gB(o,r)=g\{h\in G\colon\rho(h)<1\}.

The metric space
(G,\rho)
is
geometrically doubling
; that is, there exists
A\in\mathbb{N}
such that every metric ball
B(x,2r)
may be covered by at most
A
balls of radius
r
.

We remind the reader that a stratified Lie group is a space of homogenous type in the sense of Coifman and Weiss
[
3
,
4
]
, and analysis on stratified Lie groups uses much from the theory of such spaces.
In particular, we frequently deal with
molecules
, that is, functions
\zeta
that satisfy
standard decay and smoothness conditions
, by which we mean that there is a parameter
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, which we fix once and for all, such that

(2.1)

\begin{gathered}\left|\zeta(g)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+\rho(g))^{\nu+\varepsilon}},\\
\left|\zeta(g)-\zeta(g^{\prime})\right|\lesssim\frac{\rho(g^{\prime}g^{-1})^{\varepsilon}}{(1+\rho(g)+\rho(g^{\prime}))^{\nu+2\varepsilon}}\end{gathered}

for all
g,g^{\prime}\in G
.
We often impose an additional
cancellation condition
, namely

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{G}\zeta(g)\,\mathrm{d}g=0.

The
normalised dilate

f_{t}
of a function
f
on
G
by
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is given by
f_{t}:=t^{-\nu}f\circ\delta_{1/t}
, and the
convolution

f\ast f^{\prime}
of suitable functions
f
and
f^{\prime}
on
G
is defined by

\displaystyle f\ast f^{\prime}(g):=\int_{G}f(h)f^{\prime}(h^{-1}g)\,\mathrm{d}h=\int_{G}f(gh^{-1})f^{\prime}(h)\,\mathrm{d}h.

Take left-invariant vector fields
\mathcal{X}_{1}
, …,
\mathcal{X}_{n}
on
G
that form a basis of
\mathfrak{v}_{1}
, and define the
sub-Laplacian

\mathcal{L}:=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mathcal{X}_{j})^{2}
.
Observe that each
\mathcal{X}_{j}
is homogeneous of degree
1
and
\mathcal{L}
is homogeneous of degree
2
, in the sense that

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{j}\left(f\circ\delta_{t}\right)=t\left(\mathcal{X}_{j}f\right)\circ\delta_{t},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(f\circ\delta_{t}\right)=t^{2}\left(\mathcal{L}f\right)\circ\delta_{t}

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and all
f\in\mathsf{C}^{2}(G)
.

Associated to the sub-Laplacian, Folland
[
9
]
defined the Riesz potential operators
\mathcal{L}^{-\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
; these are convolution operators with homogeneous kernels.
The
Riesz transformation

\mathcal{R}_{j}:=\mathcal{X}_{j}\mathcal{L}^{-1/2}
is a singular integral operator, and is bounded on
\mathsf{L}^{p}(G)
when
1<p<\infty
as well as from the Folland–Stein Hardy space
\mathsf{H}^{1}(G)
to
\mathsf{L}^{1}(G)
.

The Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
\mathcal{M}
on
G
is defined using the metric balls:

\mathcal{M}f(g):=\sup\left\{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B(g^{\prime},r)}\left|f(g^{\prime\prime})\right|\,\mathrm{d}g^{\prime\prime}\colon g\in B(g^{\prime},r)\right\}.

where the “average integral”
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int
is defined by

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B}f(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}:=\frac{1}{\left|B\right|}\int_{B}f(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}.

For future use, we note that the layer cake formula implies that, if
\mu
is a radial decreasing function on
G
(that is,
\mu(g)
depends only on
\rho(g)
and decreases as
\rho(g)
increases), then

(2.3)

\left|f\right|\ast\mu_{\varepsilon}(g)\leq\left\|\mu\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(G)}\mathcal{M}f(g)\qquad\forall g\in G.

2.2.
Functional calculus for the sub-Laplacian

The sub-Laplacian
\mathcal{L}
has a spectral resolution:

\mathcal{L}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\lambda\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda)f\qquad\forall f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(G),

where
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda)
is a projection-valued measure on
[0,\infty)
, the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}
.
For a bounded Borel function
m\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
, we define the operator
F(\mathcal{L})
spectrally:

m(\mathcal{L})f:=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}m(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda)f\qquad\forall f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(G).

This operator is a convolution with a Schwartz distribution on
G
.

2.3.
The heat and Poisson kernels

Let
h_{t}
and
p_{t}
, where
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, be the heat and Poisson kernels associated to the sub-Laplacian operator
\mathcal{L}
, that is, the convolution kernels of the operators
e^{t\mathcal{L}}
and
e^{t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}}
on
G
.
We write
{{q}}_{t}
for
t\partial_{t}p_{t}
.
We warn the reader that
p_{t}
and
{{q}}_{t}
are the normalised dilates of
p_{1}
and
{{q}}_{1}
by the factor
t
, but
h_{t}
is the normalised dilate of
h_{1}
by a factor of
t^{1/2}
.
Let
\nabla
denote the subgradient on
G
and
\not{\nabla}
denote the gradient
(\nabla,\partial_{t})
on
G\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The kernels
h_{t}
and
p_{t}
are
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-valued.
Further,
h_{t}
and
p_{t}
have integral
1
, while
{{q}}_{t}
has integral
0

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Finally, there exists a constant
c
such that

\displaystyle h_{t}(g)

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-\nu/2}\exp\left(-{\rho^{2}(g)}/{ct}\right),

\displaystyle\left|\not{\nabla}h_{t}(g)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim t^{-(\nu+1)/2}\exp\left(-{\rho^{2}(g)}/{ct}\right),

\displaystyle p_{t}(g)

\displaystyle\eqsim\frac{t}{(t^{2}+\rho(g)^{2})^{(\nu+1)/2}},

\displaystyle\left|\not{\nabla}p_{t}(g)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{t}{(t^{2}+\rho(g)^{2})^{(\nu+2)/2}}

for all
g\in G
and
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

Proof.

For the heat kernel estimates, see
[
25
, Theorem IV.4.2]
.
Note that the first estimate has a version with the opposite inequality, with a different constant
c
.

The estimates for
p_{t}
and
{{q}}_{t}
follow from the subordination formula

e^{-t\sqrt{\mathcal{L}}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{te^{-{t^{2}}/{4v}}}{\sqrt{v}}e^{-v\mathcal{L}}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}v}{v}.

We leave the details to the reader.
∎

This lemma shows that the heat kernel
h_{1}
and the Poisson kernel
p_{1}
and their derivatives satisfy the standard decay and smoothness conditions (
2.1
); their derivatives also satisfy the cancellation condition (
2.2
).

, Remark 2.8]
, the number
\mathrm{T}
of adjacent systems of pseudodyadic cubes in Theorem
2.3
may be taken to be at most
A^{6}\kappa^{-\log_{2}(A)}
, where
A
is the geometric doubling constant of
G
.
The constants
c
,
C
,

and

2.5.
Products of stratified groups

We equip the product of two stratified groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
with a product structure.
We carry forward the notation from Section
2.1
, adding a subscript
i
or superscript
[i]
to clarify that we are dealing with
G_{i}
; the parameter
i
is always
1
or
2
.
To shorten the formulae, we often use bold face type to indicate a product object: thus we write
\mathbf{G}
,
\mathbf{T}
,
\mathbf{g}
,
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{t}
in place of
G_{1}\times G_{2}
,
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
(g_{1},g_{2})
,
(r_{1},r_{2})
and
(t_{1},t_{2})
.
For example,
B_{i}(g_{i},r_{i})
denotes the open ball in
G_{i}
with centre
g_{i}
and radius
r_{i}
, with respect to the homogeneous norm
\rho_{i}
, and we write
P(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{r})
for the product
B_{1}(g_{1},r_{1})\times B_{2}(g_{2},r_{2})
.
We write
\pi_{i}
for the projection of
\mathbf{G}
onto
G_{i}
.

Products of balls are basic geometric objects and we write
\mathscr{P}(\mathbf{G})
for the family of all such products.
In addition we deal with
rectangles
, by which we mean products of pseudodyadic cubes.
We write
\mathscr{R}(\mathbf{G})
for the family of all rectangles, and for adjacent pseudodyadic systems as constructed in Theorem
2.3
, we let
\mathscr{R}^{{\tau}_{1},{\tau}_{2}}(\mathbf{G})
be the family of rectangles
\{Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\colon Q_{i}\in\mathscr{Q}^{{\tau}_{i}},\ {\tau}_{i}=1,2,\dots,\mathrm{T}_{i}\}
.
We let
\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\colon\mathscr{R}(\mathbf{G})\to\mathbf{T}
be the function such that
\operatorname{\ell}_{i}(Q_{1}\times Q_{2})=\operatorname{\ell}(Q_{i})
, the “side-length” of
Q_{i}
.

The element of Haar measure on
\mathbf{G}
is denoted
\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}
, but is often written
\,\mathrm{d}g_{1}\,\mathrm{d}g_{2}
for calculations.
The convolution
f\ast f^{\prime}
of suitable functions
f
and
f^{\prime}
on
\mathbf{G}
is defined by

\displaystyle(f\ast f^{\prime})(\mathbf{g}):=\int_{\mathbf{G}}f(\mathbf{h})f^{\prime}(\mathbf{h}^{-1}\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}.

The strong maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{s}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{s}(f)(\mathbf{g}):=\sup\left\{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{P}\left|f(\mathbf{h})\right|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\colon P\in\mathscr{P}(\mathbf{G}),P\ni\mathbf{g}\right\}.

for all
f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{G})
.
It is evident that
\mathcal{M}_{s}
is dominated by the composition of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operators in the factors:

\mathcal{M}_{s}{f}\leq\mathcal{M}_{1}\mathcal{M}_{2}(f)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}_{s}{f}\leq\mathcal{M}_{2}\mathcal{M}_{1}(f).

When
1<p\leq\infty
, the operators
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
in the factors are
\mathsf{L}^{p}
-bounded, so the iterated maximal operators and hence the strong maximal operator are also
\mathsf{L}^{p}
-bounded.

Given an open subset
U
of
\mathbf{G}
with finite measure
\left|U\right|
, we define the enlargement
U^{*}
of
U
using the strong maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{s}
:

\displaystyle U^{*}:=\Bigl\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{M}_{s}\chi_{U}(\mathbf{g})>\alpha\Bigr\},

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
that varies from instance to instance.
We write
\mathscr{M}(U)
for the family of maximal rectangles contained in
U
.

If
\varphi^{[1]}
on
G_{1}
and
\varphi^{[2]}
on
G_{2}
both satisfy the decay and smoothness conditions (
2.1
) and have integral
1
, then

(2.5)

\left|f\ast\varphi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{g})\right|\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{s}(f)(\mathbf{g})\qquad\forall\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\quad\forall f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathbf{G}),

much as argued to prove (
2.3
), but with “biradial” in place of “radial”.

2.6.
Journé’s covering lemma

We recall a covering lemma for product spaces.
Journé
[
17
]
first established this result on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
.
The fourth author
[
23
]
extended it to higher dimensional Euclidean spaces and an arbitrary number of factors
\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{n_{m}}
.
This was extended to products of spaces of homogeneous type in
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathbf{G}
of finite measure and
\mathscr{M}_{i}(U)
denote the family of rectangles
R
in
U
which are maximal (in terms of inclusion) in the
i
th “direction”.
Recall that
\mathscr{M}(U)
is the set of all maximal subrectangles of
U
.
Given
R=Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\in\mathscr{M}_{1}(U)
, let
\tilde{Q}_{2}
be the biggest pseudodyadic cube containing
Q_{2}
such that

\big|\bigl(Q_{1}\times\tilde{Q}_{2}\bigr)\cap U\big|>\frac{1}{2}|Q_{1}\times\tilde{Q}_{2}|.

Similarly, given
R=Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\in\mathscr{M}_{2}(U)
, let
\tilde{Q}_{1}
be the biggest pseudodyadic cube
containing
Q_{1}
such that

\big|\bigl(\tilde{Q}_{1}\times Q_{2}\bigr)\cap U\big|>\frac{1}{2}|\tilde{Q}_{1}\times Q_{2}|.

We then set

\gamma_{1}(R):=\frac{\operatorname{\ell}(\tilde{Q}_{1})}{\operatorname{\ell}(Q_{1})}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gamma_{2}(R):=\frac{\operatorname{\ell}(\tilde{Q}_{2})}{\operatorname{\ell}(Q_{2})}.

Here is our Journé-type covering lemma on
\mathbf{G}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
U
is an open subset in
\mathbf{G}
of finite measure and
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}_{1}(U)}\left|R\right|\gamma_{1}(R)^{-\delta}\lesssim|U|\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}_{2}(U)}\left|R\right|\gamma_{2}(R)^{-\delta}\lesssim|U|.

Proof.

Apply the statement in
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
to
\mathbf{G}
.
∎

2.7.
The Orlicz space
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})

We recall the definition of an Orlicz space on
\mathbf{G}
.
A Young function is a continuous, convex, increasing bijective function
\Phi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
.
To a Young function
\Phi
, we associate the (usually nonlinear) functional
F_{\Phi}
, given by

F_{\Phi}(f)=\int_{\mathbf{G}}\Phi(|f(\mathbf{g})|)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}\qquad\forall f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{G}).

The convexity of
\Phi
implies that
\{f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{G})\colon F_{\Phi}(f)\leq 1\}
is a closed convex symmetric set, and so the Luxemburg norm, given by

\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\Phi}(\mathbf{G})}:=\inf\left\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\colon F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\right\},

is indeed a norm, and
\mathsf{L}^{\Phi}(\mathbf{G})
, the set of functions
f
for which
\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\Phi}(\mathbf{G})}
is finite, is a Banach space.
The sets
\{f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{G})\colon F_{\Phi}(f)\leq 1\}
and
\{f\in\mathsf{L}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{G})\colon\left\|f\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\Phi}(\mathbf{G})}\leq 1\}
coincide.

Suppose that
\Phi
and
\Psi
are Young functions such that

(2.6)

st\leq\Phi(s)+\Psi(t)\qquad\forall s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Then

(2.7)

\left|\int_{\mathbf{G}}f(\mathbf{g})h(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}\right|\leq\int_{\mathbf{G}}\Phi(f(\mathbf{g}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}+\int_{\mathbf{G}}\Psi(h(\mathbf{g}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g},

and it follows that the corresponding Luxemburg norms are related by the
generalized Hölder inequalities
, namely,

(2.8)

\int_{\mathbf{G}}f(\mathbf{g})h(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}\leq 2\|f\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\Phi}(\mathbf{G})}\|h\|_{\mathsf{L}^{\bar{\Phi}}(\mathbf{G})}.

We are particularly interested in a special pair of Young functions.
Henceforth,

(2.9)

\Phi(s):=s[\log(\mathrm{e}+s)]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Psi(t):=\exp(t)-1.

By maximising in
s
, it is straightforward to show that
1+st-s\log(\mathrm{e}+s)\leq\mathrm{e}^{t}
, so these functions satisfy (
2.6
), whence (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) hold.
For this choice of
\Phi
and
\Psi
, the corresponding spaces are denoted by
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
and
\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{L}}(\mathbf{G})
.
Clearly
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})\subseteq\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathbf{G})
.
Further,
\Phi(\lambda t)\leq\Phi(\lambda)\Phi(t)
, whence

(2.10)

F_{\Phi}(\lambda f)\leq\Phi(\lambda)F_{\Phi}(f)\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\quad\forall f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G}).

This implies that
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
if and only if
F_{\Phi}(f)
is finite.
Further, the hyperweak boundedness estimate (
1.2
) that we work with is equivalent to an estimate of the form

\displaystyle\big|\big\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon|\mathcal{T}f(\mathbf{g})|>\lambda\big\}\big|\lesssim\frac{\log(\mathrm{e}+1/\lambda)}{\lambda}\left\|f\right\|_{\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})}\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+},

for all
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
of norm
1
, that is,
\mathcal{T}f
decays a little slower at infinity that would occur if we had a weak type estimate.
Finally, since
\Psi
is a Young function,

(2.11)

\Psi(t/\lambda)\leq\Psi(t)/\lambda\qquad\forall\lambda\in[1,\infty).

We need a density result.

Proposition 2.5
.

\mathsf{L}^{2}\cap\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
is dense in
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
.

Proof.

For
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
, we define, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

f_{N}:=\chi_{\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon|f(\mathbf{g})|\leq N\}}f.

Then
f_{N}\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
,
|f_{N}|\leq|f|
, and
f_{N}\to f
almost everywhere as
n\to\infty
.
Next,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{G}}|f_{N}(\mathbf{g})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}\leq\int_{\mathbf{G}}N|f(\mathbf{g})|\log(\mathrm{e}+|f(\mathbf{g})|)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}=NF_{\Phi}(f),

which shows that
f_{N}\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})
for all
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
.
Finally, if
\lambda<1
, then

\displaystyle F_{\Phi}((f-f_{N})/\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbf{G}}|f(\mathbf{g})-f_{N}(\mathbf{g})|\log(\mathrm{e}+|(f(\mathbf{g})-f_{N}(\mathbf{g}))/\lambda)|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon|f(\mathbf{g})|>N\}}|f(\mathbf{g})|\left(\log(\mathrm{e}+|f(\mathbf{g})|)+\log(1/\lambda)\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle\to 0

as
N\to\infty
.
Consequently,
\left\|{f-f_{N}}\right\|_{\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})}
tends to zero as
N
tend to infinity.
∎

3.
The strong maximal function and Proof of Theorem
1.1

Before we tackle the main topic of this section, we state and prove a covering lemma.
We begin with a geometric observation.

Lemma 3.1
.

There is a geometric constant
C(\mathbf{G})
with the property that

R\subseteq\{g\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{M}_{s}(\chi_{U})(\mathbf{g})>C(\mathbf{G})\alpha\}

for all measurable subsets
U
of
\mathbf{G}
, all
\alpha\in(0,1)
and all rectangles
R\in\mathscr{P}
such that

|R\cap U|\geq\alpha\left|R\right|.

Proof.

By definition,
R
is a product of pseudodyadic cubes
Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
, and by Theorem
2.2
,

B(z(Q_{i}),c\ell(Q_{i})/3)\subseteq Q_{i}\subseteq B(z(Q_{i}),2C\ell(Q_{i})).

Define
P:=B(z(Q_{1}),2C\ell_{1}(R))\times B(z(Q_{2}),2C\ell_{2}(R))
.
Then
R\subseteq P
and

\displaystyle|P\cap U|

\displaystyle\geq|R\cap U|\geq\lambda\left|R\right|

\displaystyle\geq\lambda|B(z(Q_{1}),c\ell(Q_{1})/3)\times B(z(Q_{2}),c\ell(Q_{2})/3)|

\displaystyle=\lambda\Bigl(\frac{c}{6C}\Bigr)^{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}}|B(z(Q_{i}),2C\ell(Q_{i}))\times B(z(Q_{2}),2C\ell(Q_{2}))|

\displaystyle=:\lambda C(\mathbf{G})|P|.

Hence
R
is a subset of the set

U^{*}=\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{M}_{s}\chi_{U}(\mathbf{g})>C(\mathbf{G})\lambda\},

as required.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

[
5
]

Let
\{R_{j}\}_{j\in J}
be a family of rectangles in
\mathbf{G}
such that
\big|\bigcup_{j\in J}R_{j}\big|
is finite.
Then there is a sequence of rectangles
\{\tilde{R}_{k}\}\subset\{R_{j}\}_{j\in J}
such that

\big|\bigcup_{j\in J}R_{j}\big|\lesssim\big|\bigcup_{k}\tilde{R}_{k}\big|\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Bigl\|\sum_{k}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{k}}\Bigr\|_{\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{L}}(\mathbf{G})}\lesssim\big|\bigcup_{j\in J}R_{j}\big|.

Proof.

The proof is a generalisation of that in
[
5
]
.
However, the pseudodyadic case is somewhat trickier than the dyadic case in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and the argument in
[
5
]
is rather brief and not always precise, so it seems worthwhile to provide a complete proof.

Since there are countably many rectangles, we may assume that
J=\mathbb{N}
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
E
be the set
\bigcup_{j}R_{j}
.

Choose a subsequence
\{R_{\sigma(j)}\colon j\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\{R_{j}\colon j\in\mathbb{N}\}
, using the rules that
\sigma(1)=1
and
\sigma(k+1)
is the least
j>\sigma(k)
such that

|R_{j}\cap(R_{\sigma(1)}\cup\dots\cup R_{\sigma(k)})|\leq\frac{1}{4}|R_{j}|;

the construction terminates if this is not possible.
Let
E_{K}=\bigcup_{1\leq k\leq K}R_{\sigma(k)}\subseteq E
.
If the construction does not terminate, then by monotone convergence, we may choose
K
such that
|E_{K}|\geq|E_{\infty}|/2
; if the construction terminates, we take
K
to be the last index of the finite sequence.
The set
E
consists of rectangles
R_{j}
that are not one of the chosen rectangles
R_{\sigma(k)}
together with rectangles that are subsets of
E_{\infty}
if the construction did not terminate, or of
E_{K}
otherwise.
Suppose that the construction did not terminate.
Then by Lemma
3.1
,

R_{j}\subseteq E_{\infty}^{*}:=\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{M}_{s}\chi_{E_{\infty}}(\mathbf{g})>{C(\mathbf{G})}/{4}\}.

Hence
E\subseteq E_{\infty}^{*}\cup E_{\infty}
, and the boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{s}
on
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})
shows that

|E|\leq|E_{\infty}^{*}|+|E_{\infty}|\lesssim|E_{\infty}|\leq 2|E_{K}|.

When the construction terminates, a slightly easier argument shows that
|E|\lesssim|E_{K}|
.

We now relabel the finite family of rectangles
\{R_{\sigma(1)},\dots,R_{\sigma(K)}\}
as
R_{K},\dots,R_{1}
and repeat the construction, setting
\tau(1):=1
and choosing
\tau(l+1)
to be the least
l>\tau(l)
such that

|R_{k}\cap(R_{\tau(1)}\cup\dots\cup R_{\tau(l)})|\leq\frac{1}{4}|R_{k}|;

we end up with
L
terms, say.
Since

|R_{k}\cap(R_{k+1}\cup\dots\cup R_{K})|\leq\frac{1}{4}|R_{k}|

by construction, it follows that

(3.1)

|R_{\tau(l+1)}\cap((R_{\tau(1)}\cup\dots\cup R_{\tau(l)})\cup(R_{\tau(l+2)}\cup\dots\cup R_{\tau(L)}))|\leq\frac{1}{2}|R_{\tau(l+1)}|.

Write
\tilde{R}_{l}
for
R_{\tau(l)}
and
\tilde{E}
for
\bigcup_{l}R_{\tau(l)}
.
Much as before,
|E|\lesssim\bigl|\tilde{E}\bigr|
.
Note that

(3.2)

\bigl|\tilde{E}\bigr|\leq\sum_{l}\bigl|\tilde{R}_{l}\bigr|\leq 2\sum_{l}\biggl|\tilde{R}_{l}\setminus\biggl(\bigcup_{l^{\prime}\neq l}\tilde{R}_{l^{\prime}}\biggr)\biggr|=2\biggl|\bigcup_{l}\tilde{R}_{l}\setminus\biggl(\bigcup_{l^{\prime}\neq l}\tilde{R}_{l^{\prime}}\biggr)\biggr|\leq 2\bigl|\tilde{E}\bigr|.

We now claim that

(3.3)

\bigl|\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\sum_{l}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{l}}\geq n\}\bigr|\lesssim 2^{-n/2}|E|.

From this claim, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{G}}\Psi\Bigl(\sum_{l}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{L}}(\mathbf{g})/\lambda\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Psi(n/\lambda)2^{-n/2}|E|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp(n/\lambda)2^{-n/2}|E|

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\exp(n[1/\lambda-\log(2)/2])|E|

\displaystyle\lesssim|E|

as long as
1/\lambda<\log(2)/2
, and so
\sum_{l=1}^{L}\chi_{R_{l}}\in\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{L}}(G)
.
It remains to prove (
3.3
).

Consider
\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}
that lies in two distinct rectangles from the family
\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
,
R
and
S
say.
If
\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(S)=\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R)
, then
\pi_{1}(R)=\pi_{1}(S)
, since both
\pi_{1}(R)
and
\pi_{1}(S)
are pseudodyadic cubes containing
\pi_{1}(\mathbf{g})
; now
\pi_{2}(R)\subseteq\pi_{2}(S)
or
\pi_{2}(R)\supseteq\pi_{2}(S)
, so
R\cap S
is either
R
or
S
, and this contradicts (
3.1
).
Hence we may assume, without loss of generality, that
\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(S)<\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R)
.
A similar argument then shows that
\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(S)>\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(R)
.
Thus, if there are
n
distinct rectangles in
\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
that contain
\mathbf{g}
, then we may label them
R_{1}(\mathbf{g})
, …,
R_{n}(\mathbf{g})
, in such a way that
\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R_{j}(\mathbf{g}))
decreases with
j
and
\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(R_{j}(\mathbf{g}))
increases with
j
; then

R_{1}(\mathbf{g})\cap\dots\cap R_{n}(\mathbf{g})=\pi_{1}(R_{n}(\mathbf{g}))\times\pi_{2}(R_{1}(\mathbf{g})),

and

\left|R_{1}(\mathbf{g})\cap\dots\cap R_{n}(\mathbf{g})\right|=\left|\pi_{1}(R_{n}(\mathbf{g}))\times\pi_{2}(R_{1}(\mathbf{g}))\right|\lesssim\kappa^{-n\nu_{1}}\left|R_{1}(\mathbf{g})\right|.

We say that a rectangle
T\in\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
is a
descendant
of a rectangle
R\in\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
, and we write
T\succ R
, if
R\cap T\neq\emptyset
and both
\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(T)>\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R)
and
\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(T)<\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(R)
, and we say that
T
is a
child
of
R
if
T\succeq R
and if
S\in\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
and
T\succeq S\succeq R
then
S=T
or
S=R
.
We may define
ancestors
and
parents
similarly, with the relations reversed.

Fix a rectangle
R\in\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
, and consider
\mathbf{g}\in R
that lies in at least
n
distinct rectangles of

\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
.
Then
\mathbf{g}
lies in at least
\lceil n/2\rceil
distinct rectangles
S
such that
S\succeq R
, or

\mathbf{g}
lies in at least
\lceil n/2\rceil
distinct rectangles
S
such that
S\preceq R
.
Thus

\displaystyle\left|\left\{\mathbf{g}\in R\colon\sum\nolimits_{l}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{l}}(\mathbf{g})\geq n\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\{\mathbf{g}\in R\colon\sum\nolimits_{l}^{\succeq}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{l}}(\mathbf{g})\geq\frac{n}{2}\right\}\right|+\left|\left\{\mathbf{g}\in R\colon\sum\nolimits_{l}^{\preceq}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{l}}(\mathbf{g})\geq\frac{n}{2}\right\}\right|,

where
\sum^{\succeq}_{l}
indicates that we sum over
l
such that
\tilde{R}_{l}\succeq R
, and
\sum^{\preceq}_{l}
is defined analogously.
We estimate the measure of one of these two sets: the other may be estimated similarly.

Consider all
S\in\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
such that
S\succeq R
.
Inside this collection of rectangles, we may identify the children
S_{1}
, …,
S_{p}
of
R
, which are pairwise disjoint, the children of the children of
R
, which are again pairwise disjoint, and so on.
Observe that

R\cap(S_{1}\cup\dots\cup S_{p})=\pi_{1}(R)\times(\pi_{2}(S_{1})\cup\dots\cup\pi_{2}(S_{p}))\subseteq\pi_{1}(R)\times\pi_{2}(R),

and the near disjointness condition (
3.1
) implies that

\left|\pi_{2}(S_{1})\cup\dots\cup\pi_{2}(S_{p})\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left|\pi_{2}(R)\right|.

Then the measure of the set of all
\mathbf{g}\in R
that also belong to another rectangle
S\succ R
is at most
\left|R\right|/2
.

Likewise, similar inequalities hold for the children of the children of
R
, which we may label as
T_{1}
, …,
T_{q}
, and so

\left|\pi_{2}(T_{1})\cup\dots\cup\pi_{2}(T_{q})\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left|\pi_{2}(S_{1})\cup\dots\cup\pi_{2}(S_{p})\right|\leq\frac{1}{2^{2}}\left|\pi_{2}(R)\right|.

and the measure of the set of all
\mathbf{g}\in R
that also belong to two more rectangles
S,T\succ R
is at most
2^{-2}\left|R\right|
.
Continuing inductively, the measure of the set of all
\mathbf{g}\in R
that lie in at least
m
distinct rectangles
S\succ R
is at most
2^{-m}\left|R\right|
.

Combining this estimate with an almost identical estimate for the measures of sets defined using ancestors and parents, we conclude that the measure of the set of all
\mathbf{g}\in R
that belong to at least
n
rectangles of
\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
is at most

2\times 2^{-\lceil(n-1)/2\rceil}\left|R\right|\lesssim 2^{-n/2}\left|R\right|.

Then the measure of all
\mathbf{g}\in G
that belong to at least
n
rectangles of
\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}
is at most a multiple of

\sum_{l}2^{-n/2}\bigl|\tilde{R}_{l}\bigr|=2^{-n/2}\sum_{l}\bigl|\tilde{R}_{l}\bigr|\lesssim 2^{-n/2}|E|,

which is what we needed to prove.
∎

Now we can prove an endpoint estimate for the strong maximal function on
\mathbf{G}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

The strong maximal function satisfies the following:

\big|\big\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon|\mathcal{M}_{s}f(\mathbf{g})|>\lambda\big\}\big|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

for all
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
.

Proof.

We recall a fundamental estimate on the strong maximal function and pseudodyadic strong maximal functions associated with adjacent pseudodyadic systems, the prototype of which (in the setting of the product
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
) was proved in
[
20
, Theorem 6.1]
.
More explicitly, we define the pseudodyadic strong maximal function as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{s}^{{\tau}_{1},{\tau}_{2}}(f)(\mathbf{g}):=\sup\left\{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R}\left|f(\mathbf{h})\right|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\colon R\ni\mathbf{g},R\in\mathscr{Q}^{{\tau}_{1}}\times\mathscr{Q}^{{\tau}_{2}}\right\},

where
{\tau}_{i}=1,2,\dots,\mathrm{T}_{i}
.
Then

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{s}(f)(\mathbf{g})\lesssim\sum_{{\tau}_{1}=1}^{\mathrm{T}_{1}}\sum_{{\tau}_{2}=1}^{\mathrm{T}_{2}}\mathcal{M}_{s}^{{\tau}_{1},{\tau}_{2}}(f)(\mathbf{g})\qquad\forall\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}.

This inequality means that instead of considering general products of balls, it suffices to consider rectangles, and Theorem
3.2
may be applied.

In light of (
3.4
), sublinearity and Proposition
2.5
, it suffices to show that

(3.5)

\big|\big\{g\in\mathbf{G}\colon|\mathcal{M}_{s}^{{\tau}_{1},{\tau}_{2}}f(\mathbf{g})|>1\big\}\big|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f)\qquad\forall f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}\cap\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G}),

when
{\tau}_{i}=1,2,\dots,\mathrm{T}_{i}
; we may assume that
f
takes nonnegative real values.

Let
E=\big\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon|\mathcal{M}_{s}^{{\tau}_{1},{\tau}_{2}}f(\mathbf{g})|>1\big\}
.
Since
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})
and
\mathcal{M}_{s}^{{\tau}_{1},{\tau}_{2}}
is
\mathsf{L}^{2}
bounded,
|E|
is finite.
For every
\mathbf{g}\in E
there exists
R_{\mathbf{g}}\in\mathscr{R}^{{\tau}_{1},{\tau}_{2}}(\mathbf{G})
satisfying

(3.6)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{R_{\mathbf{g}}}f(\mathbf{h})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}>1.

Then
E=\bigcup_{\mathbf{g}\in E}R_{\mathbf{g}}
by definition.

By Theorem
3.2
, there is a sequence of rectangles
\{\tilde{R}_{l}\}\subseteq\{R_{\mathbf{g}}\}_{\mathbf{g}\in E}
such that

(3.7)

|E|=\Bigl|\bigcup_{\mathbf{g}\in E}R_{\mathbf{g}}\Bigr|\lesssim\Bigl|\bigcup_{l}\tilde{R}_{l}\Bigr|\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Bigl\|\sum_{l}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{l}}\Bigr\|_{\mathrm{e}^{\mathsf{L}(\mathbf{G})}}\lesssim\Bigl|\bigcup_{\mathbf{g}\in E}R_{\mathbf{g}}\Bigr|.

Write
\tilde{E}
for
\bigcup_{l}\tilde{R}_{l}
and
\tilde{E}_{n}
for
\left\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}:\sum_{l}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{l}}(\mathbf{g})=n\right\}
.
From (
3.6
), (
2.7
), (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we deduce that
|E|\lesssim|\tilde{E}|
and, for all
\lambda\in[1,\infty)
,

\displaystyle|\tilde{E}|

\displaystyle=\Bigl|\bigcup_{l}\tilde{R}_{l}\Bigr|\leq\sum_{l}|\tilde{R}_{l}|<\sum_{l}\int_{\tilde{R}_{l}}f(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{E}}\sum_{l}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{l}}(\mathbf{g})f(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\tilde{E}}\Phi(\lambda^{2}f(\mathbf{g}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}+\int_{\tilde{E}}\Psi(\sum_{l}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{l}}(\mathbf{g})/\lambda^{2})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(\lambda^{2})\int_{\mathbf{G}}\Phi(f(\mathbf{g}))\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}+\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\tilde{E}}\Psi(\sum_{l}\chi_{\tilde{R}_{l}}(\mathbf{g})/\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}.

Since
|\tilde{E}_{n}|\leq C_{1}2^{-n/2}|\tilde{E}|
, where
C_{1}
is a geometric constant,

\displaystyle|\tilde{E}|

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(\lambda^{2})F_{\Phi}(f)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{n/\lambda}\bigl|\tilde{E}_{n}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(\lambda^{2})F_{\Phi}(f)+\frac{C_{1}}{\lambda}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{n/\lambda}2^{-n/2}\bigl|\tilde{E}\bigr|.

We fix
\lambda
that is large enough that the series converges and the right hand term is less than
\bigl|\tilde{E}\bigr|/2
; then
\bigl|\tilde{E}\bigr|\leq 2\Phi(\lambda^{2})F_{\Phi}(f)
, as required.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

The maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{\zeta,\eta}
satisfies the endpoint estimate:

\bigl|\bigl\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\left|\mathcal{M}_{\zeta,\eta}(f)(\mathbf{g})\right|>\lambda\bigr\}\bigr|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

for all
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.5
) with a little care that
\mathcal{M}_{\zeta,\eta}(f)\lesssim\mathcal{M}_{s}f
pointwise.
∎

The following lemma is another well known consequence of the boundedness of the strong maximal operator.
Recall that
\mathscr{M}(U)
denotes the collection of all maximal rectangles contained in
U
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
U
be an open subset of
\mathbf{G}
of finite measure and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Given
R
in
\mathscr{M}(U)
, let
\beta R
be the set
\mathbf{z}(R)\delta_{\beta}(\mathbf{z}(R)^{-1}R)
.
Then

\biggl|\bigcup_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U)}\beta R\biggr|\lesssim(\beta+1)^{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}}\left|U\right|.

Proof.

Recall that
B(z(Q),c\operatorname{\ell}(Q)/3)\subseteq Q\subseteq B(z(Q),2C\operatorname{\ell}(Q))
for all
Q\in\mathscr{Q}(G)
and
P(\mathbf{z},\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\ell}})
is the product
B_{1}(z_{1},\operatorname{\ell}_{1})\times B_{2}(z_{2},\operatorname{\ell}_{2})
.
It follows that, if
R\in\mathscr{R}
and
\mathbf{g}\in\beta R
, then

P(\mathbf{z}(R),c\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(R)/3)\subseteq R\subseteq P(\mathbf{g},2C(\beta+1)\operatorname{\boldsymbol{\ell}}(R)),

and we deduce, much as in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, that

\beta R\subseteq\biggl\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{M}_{s}(\chi_{R})(\mathbf{g})\geq C(\mathbf{G})(\beta+1)^{-\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}\biggr\}.

Hence

\biggl|\bigcup_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U)}\beta R\biggr|\leq\biggl|\biggl\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{M}_{s}(\chi_{R})(\mathbf{g})\geq C(\mathbf{G})(\beta+1)^{-\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}}\biggr\}\biggr|,

and the hyperweak boundedness of
\mathcal{M}_{s}
implies that

\biggl|\bigcup_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U)}\beta R\biggr|\lesssim(\beta+1)^{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}}F_{\Phi}(\chi_{U})\lesssim(\beta+1)^{\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}}\left|U\right|,

as claimed.
∎

4.
The atomic decomposition

In this section, we first prove a hyperweak boundedness estimate for the Lusin area function
\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}(f)
, and then use this estimate to decompose
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
functions.

Recall that
p^{[i]}_{t_{i}}
and
q^{[i]}_{t_{i}}
denote the convolution kernels of the operators
e^{-t_{i}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{i}}}
and
t_{i}\partial_{t_{i}}e^{-t_{i}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{i}}}
; then
q^{[i]}_{t_{i}}=t_{i}\partial_{t_{i}}p^{[i]}_{t_{i}}
.
We write
p_{\mathbf{t}}:=p^{[1]}_{t_{1}}\otimes p^{[2]}_{t_{2}}
and
{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}:=q^{[1]}_{t_{1}}\otimes q^{[2]}_{t_{2}}
.

4.1.
Hyperweak boundedness and the area function
\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}(f)

The first step is to apply Corollary
3.4
.

Proposition 4.1
.

The nontangential maximal operator
\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}
is hyperweakly bounded.
That is,

\big|\big\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon|\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}f(\mathbf{g})|>\lambda\big\}\big|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

for all
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
.

This weak type estimate for the Poisson maximal operator implies a similar estimate for the Lusin area function
\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}
, which is the key to establishing the atomic decomposition for
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
functions.
More explicitly, we denote the tensor
\not{\nabla}^{[1]}p_{1}^{[1]}(g_{1})\otimes\not{\nabla}^{[2]}p_{1}^{[2]}(g_{2})
by
\not{\nabla}p(\mathbf{g})
and define

\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,\eta}f(\mathbf{g}):=\biggl(\int\!\!\!\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Gamma(\mathbf{g})}\left|(f\ast(\not{\nabla}p)_{\mathbf{t}})(\mathbf{h})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}\biggr)^{1/2},

where

(4.1)

\int\!\!\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Gamma(\mathbf{g})}f(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{t})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}:=\int_{\mathbf{T}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{P(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{t})}f(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{t})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}.

Theorem 4.2
.

The following estimate holds:

\left|\left\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}(f)(\mathbf{g})>\lambda\right\}\right|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

for all
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
.

Proof.

A recent result of Fan, Yan and the first and third authors
[
6
]
shows that for
f
such that
\left\|\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}f\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathbf{G})}<\infty
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}f\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathbf{G})}\lesssim\left\|\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}f\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathbf{G})}.

More precisely, define the sublevel set
L_{\eta}(\lambda):=\left\{\mathbf{g}\in{\mathbf{G}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}(f)(\mathbf{g})\leq\lambda\right\}
.
Then it is shown that, when
\eta
is sufficiently large,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\bigl|\bigl\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}(f)(\mathbf{g})>\lambda\bigr\}\bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl|L_{\eta}(\lambda)^{c}\bigr|+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{L_{\eta}(\lambda)}\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}^{2}(f)(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Now
\bigl|L_{\eta}(\lambda)^{c}\bigr|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)
by Proposition
4.1
, so the layer-cake formula and (
2.10
) show that

\displaystyle\bigl|\bigl\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}(f)(\mathbf{g})>\lambda\bigr\}\bigr|

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigl|L_{\eta}(\lambda)^{c}\bigr|+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{L_{\eta}(\lambda)}\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}^{2}(f)(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle=\bigl|L_{\eta}(\lambda)^{c}\bigr|+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\lambda}2\mu\Big|L_{\eta}(\mu)^{c}\Big|\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{0}^{\lambda}F_{\Phi}(f/\mu)\mu\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)+\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\log(\mathrm{e}+\lambda/\mu)F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda).

This implies that the right-hand side of (
4.2
) is finite.
By repeating the argument of
[
6
]
, we see that (
4.2
) also holds for
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
, and we conclude that

\displaystyle\big|\big\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon|\mathcal{S}_{\not{\nabla}p,1}(f)(\mathbf{g})|>\lambda\big\}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\big|L_{\eta}(\lambda)^{c}\big|+\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}}\int_{L_{\eta}(\lambda)}\mathcal{M}_{p,\eta}^{2}(f)(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

for all
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
, as required.
∎

Recall that
{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}
denotes
t_{1}\partial_{t_{1}}p_{t_{1}}^{[1]}\otimes t_{2}\partial_{t_{2}}p^{[2]}_{t_{2}}
and

\mathcal{S}_{q,1}(f)(\mathbf{g}):=\biggl(\int\!\!\!\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\Gamma(\mathbf{g})}\left|f\ast{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{h})\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}\biggr)^{1/2}.

Corollary 4.3
.

The area function
\mathcal{S}_{q,1}(f)
satisfies the estimate:

\bigl|\bigl\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{S}_{q,1}(f)(\mathbf{g})>\lambda\bigr\}\bigr|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

for all
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
.

Proof.

This holds as
f*{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}
is one of the components of the tensor
f*(\not{\nabla}p)_{\mathbf{t}}
.
∎

4.2.
The atomic decomposition for
\mathsf{L\,log\,L}

Recall that the
q^{[i]}_{1}
satisfy the standard smoothness, decay and cancellation conditions (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), and by
[
11
]
there exist compactly supported smooth functions
\varphi^{[i]}
on
G_{i}
with integral
0
such that

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{T}}f\ast{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}\ast\varphi_{\mathbf{t}}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}

for all
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})
, where
\varphi_{\mathbf{t}}=\varphi_{t_{1}}\otimes\varphi_{t_{2}}
.
This is the Calderón reproducing formula.
We suppose that
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi^{[i]})\subseteq B_{i}(o,1)
, by rescaling if necessary.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})\cap\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
.
Then we may write

f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k},

where the sum converges unconditionally in
L^{2}(\mathbf{G})
and the
atoms

a_{k}
satisfy:

(1)

a_{k}
vanishes outside a subset
U^{\dagger}_{k}
of
\mathbf{G}
such that
|U^{\dagger}_{k}|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(2^{-k}{f})
;

(2)

\|a_{k}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}^{2}\lesssim F_{\Phi}(2^{k}f)
;

(3)

each
a_{k}
can be further decomposed:
a_{k}=\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}a_{k,R}
, where the sum converges unconditionally in
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})
, and

(a)

\operatorname{supp}a_{k,R}\subseteq\beta R
, for a suitable
\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
;

(b)

\int_{G_{1}}a_{k,R}(g_{1},g_{2})\,\mathrm{d}g_{1}=0
for all
g_{2}\in G_{2}
;

(c)

\int_{G_{2}}a_{k,R}(g_{1},g_{2})\,\mathrm{d}g_{2}=0
for all
g_{1}\in G_{1}
;

(d)

\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}\left\|a_{k,R}\right\|^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}\lesssim 2^{2k}F_{\Phi}(2^{-k}f)
.

Proof.

From Corollary
4.3
,
\mathcal{S}_{q,1}
is hyperweakly bounded.
Assume that
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})\cap\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
.
Then
\mathcal{S}_{q,1}(f)\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})
.
Now for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we set

(4.3)

\displaystyle U_{k}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{S}_{q,1}(f)(\mathbf{g})>2^{k}\};

(4.4)

\displaystyle U^{*}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{M}_{s}(\chi_{U_{k}})(\mathbf{g})>\alpha\};

(4.5)

\displaystyle U^{\dagger}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\bigcup\nolimits_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}\beta R;

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{k}

\displaystyle:=\{R\in\mathscr{R}(\mathbf{G})\colon\left|R\cap U_{k}\right|\geq\left|R\right|/2,\left|R\cap U_{k+1}\right|<\left|R\right|/2\};

here
\alpha
and
\beta
are positive numbers that will be specified during the proof.
By definition
U_{k}\supseteq U_{k+1}
, so the sequence
\left|R\cap U_{k}\right|/\left|R\right|
is decreasing and the sets
\mathscr{B}_{k}
are pairwise disjoint.
If
\mathbf{g}\in R\in\mathscr{B}_{k}
, then
|R\cap U_{k}|/\left|R\right|>1/2
so
\mathbf{g}\in U^{*}_{k}
by Lemma
3.1
provided that
\alpha<C(\mathbf{G})/2
; coupled with (
4.6
), this shows that

(4.7)

\left|R\cap(U^{*}_{k}\setminus U_{k+1})\right|=\left|R\setminus(R\cap U_{k+1})\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|R\right|.

For each rectangle
R\in\mathscr{B}_{k}
, we define the tent
R_{+}
over
R
to be the set

(4.8)

\Bigl\{(\mathbf{g},\mathbf{t})\in\mathbf{G}\times\mathbf{T}\colon\mathbf{g}\in R,4C\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R)<t_{1}\leq 8C\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R),4C\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(R)<t_{2}\leq 8C\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(R)\Bigr\}.

This definition implies that, if
R\in\mathscr{B}_{k}
and
(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{t})\in R_{+}
, then

(4.9)

R\subseteq B_{1}(h_{1},4C\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R))\times B_{2}(h_{2},4C\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(R))\subseteq B_{1}(h_{1},t_{1})\times B_{2}(h_{2},t_{2})=P(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{t}).

For future use, we note that, for any measurable set
V
in
\mathbf{G}
,

(4.10)

\displaystyle\chi_{V}*\chi_{P(\mathbf{o},\mathbf{t})}(\mathbf{h})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{G}}\chi_{V}(\mathbf{g})\chi_{P(\mathbf{o},\mathbf{t})}(\mathbf{g}^{-1}\mathbf{h})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}=\int_{\mathbf{G}}\chi_{V}(\mathbf{g})\chi_{P(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{t})}(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle=\left|P(\mathbf{h},\mathbf{t})\cap V\right|.

Since
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})
, the reproducing formula implies that

\displaystyle f(\mathbf{g})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{T}}f\ast{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}\ast\varphi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{g})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{T}}\int_{\mathbf{G}}f\ast{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{h})\varphi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{h}^{-1}\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{R\in\mathscr{B}_{k}}\iint_{R_{+}}f\ast{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{h})\varphi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{h}^{-1}\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}(\mathbf{g}),

say.
Next, for all
R\in\mathscr{B}_{k}
, choose
\tilde{R}\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})
such that
R\subseteq\tilde{R}
.
Then for all
S\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})
, set
a_{k,S}=\sum_{R\in\mathscr{B}_{k}\colon\tilde{R}=S}b_{k,R}
,
where

b_{k,R}(\mathbf{g})=\iint_{R_{+}}f\ast{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{h})\varphi_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{h}^{-1}\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{h}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}.

By construction,

a_{k}=\sum_{S\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}a_{k,S}.

Now

\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{\mathbf{t}})\subseteq B(o,t_{1})\times B(o,t_{2})\subseteq B_{1}(o,8C\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R))\times B_{2}(o,8C\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(R)),

and hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}(b_{k,R})

\displaystyle\subseteq B_{1}(z_{1}(R),10C\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R))\times B_{2}(z_{2}(R),10C\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(R))

\displaystyle=B_{1}(z_{1}(R),\beta c\operatorname{\ell}_{1}(R)/3)\times B_{2}(z_{2}(R),\beta c\operatorname{\ell}_{2}(R)/3)

\displaystyle\subseteq\beta R,

where
\beta=30C/c
.
Thus
a_{k}
vanishes on
(U^{\dagger}_{k})^{c}
.
By Lemmas
3.5
and
3.1
and Theorem
4.4
,

(4.11)

|U^{\dagger}_{k}|\lesssim|U^{*}_{k}|\lesssim|U_{k}|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(2^{-k}f).

Next, we claim that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\|a_{k}\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}^{2}\lesssim 2^{2k}F_{\Phi}(2^{-k}f).

To see this, take
h\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})
such that
\|h\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}=1
; then, writing
\check{\varphi}
for the reflected version of
\varphi
, that is,
\check{\varphi}(\mathbf{g})=\varphi(\mathbf{g}^{-1})
, we see that

\displaystyle\big|\langle a_{k},h\rangle\big|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{R\in\mathscr{B}_{k}}\iint_{R_{+}}f\ast{{q}}_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{g})h\ast\check{\varphi}_{\mathbf{t}}(\mathbf{g})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}\mathbf{t}}{\mathbf{t}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\eqsim\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{1}})^{2\varepsilon}}{\bigl(\rho_{1}(g_{1}^{-1}z({Q_{1}}))\bigr)^{2\nu_{1}+2\varepsilon}}\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{2}})^{2\varepsilon}}{\bigl(\rho_{2}(g_{2}^{-1}z({Q_{2}}))\bigr)^{2\nu_{2}+2\varepsilon}}\,.

Our estimation of
\mathrm{I}_{2}(R)
concludes with the observation that

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{2}(R)

\displaystyle=\int_{(100\beta_{k}{\tilde{Q}_{1}})^{c}}\int_{(100{Q_{2}})^{c}}|\mathcal{S}_{\psi,\eta}(a_{k,R})(g_{1},g_{2})|\,\mathrm{d}g_{2}\,\mathrm{d}g_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|a_{k,R}\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{1}(\mathbf{G})}\int_{(100\beta_{k}{\tilde{Q}_{1}})^{c}}\int_{(100{Q_{2}})^{c}}\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{1}})^{\varepsilon}}{\bigl(\rho_{1}(g_{1}^{-1}z({Q_{1}}))\bigr)^{\nu_{1}+\varepsilon}}\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{2}})^{\varepsilon}}{\bigl(\rho_{2}(g_{2}^{-1}z({Q_{2}}))\bigr)^{\nu_{2}+\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}g_{2}\,\mathrm{d}g_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{1}})^{\varepsilon}}{(100\beta_{k}\operatorname{\ell}({\tilde{Q}_{1}}))^{\varepsilon}}\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{2}})^{\varepsilon}}{(100\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{2}}))^{\varepsilon}}\left|R\right|^{1/2}\left\|a_{k,R}\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}

\displaystyle\eqsim\beta_{k}^{-\varepsilon}\gamma_{1}(R)^{-\varepsilon}\left|R\right|^{1/2}\left\|a_{k,R}\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}.

Then, by combining the estimates of
\mathrm{I}_{1}(R)
and
\mathrm{I}_{2}(R)
, and then using Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.4
, we see that for all
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}\mathrm{I}_{1}(R)+\mathrm{I}_{2}(R)

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{k}^{-\varepsilon}\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}\gamma(R)^{-\varepsilon}\left|R\right|^{1/2}\left\|a_{k,R}\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{k}^{-\varepsilon}\biggl(\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}\gamma_{1}(R)^{-2\varepsilon}\left|R\right|\biggr)^{1/2}\biggl(\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}\left\|a_{k,R}\right\|^{2}_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}\biggr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{k}^{-\varepsilon}|U_{k}|^{1/2}\Bigl(2^{2k}F_{\Phi}(2^{-k}f)\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\beta_{k}^{-\varepsilon}2^{k}F_{\Phi}(2^{-k}f)

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-\varepsilon k/(2\nu_{1}+2\nu_{2})}F_{\Phi}(f).

Similar estimates hold for
\mathrm{I}_{3}(R)
and
\mathrm{I}_{4}(R)
, but with
\gamma_{2}
in place of
\gamma_{1}
, and hence (
5.7
) holds, and the proof is complete.

(5.8)

5.3.
Hyperweak boundedness of Riesz transformations

We write
\mathcal{R}
for a double Riesz transformation
\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}\otimes\mathcal{R}^{[2]}_{j_{2}}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The double Riesz transformations satisfy the endpoint estimate:

(5.9)

\big|\big\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{R}(f)(\mathbf{g})>\lambda\big\}\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f/\lambda)\qquad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

for all
f\in\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
.

Proof.

By density and sublinearity, we need only prove that, for all
f\in\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})\cap\mathsf{L\,log\,L}(\mathbf{G})
,

(5.10)

\big|\big\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{R}(f)(\mathbf{g})>1\big\}\big|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f).

From Theorem
4.4
, we may write

f=\sum_{k}a_{k}

where the
a_{k}
satisfy the conditions specified there.
By the
\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})
boundedness of
\mathcal{R}
,

(5.11)

\Bigl|\Bigl\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{R}\biggl(\sum_{k\leq 0}a_{k}\biggr)(\mathbf{g})>1\Bigr\}\Bigr|\lesssim\Bigl\|\sum_{k\leq 1}a_{k}\Bigr\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}^{2}\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f).

To handle
\mathcal{R}(\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k})
, we consider the
a_{k}
in more detail.
The atom
a_{k}
is supported in
U^{\dagger}_{k}
, where
|U^{\dagger}_{k}|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(2^{-k}f)
, and we may write
a_{k}=\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}a_{k,R}
.

Again, for all
R={Q_{1}}\times{Q_{2}}\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})
, let
{\tilde{Q}_{1}}
be the biggest pseudodyadic cube containing
{Q_{1}}
such that
{\tilde{Q}_{1}}\times{Q_{2}}\subset U^{**}_{k}
, where
U^{**}_{k}
is defined in (
5.1
).
Next, let
{\tilde{Q}_{2}}
be the biggest pseudodyadic cube containing
{Q_{2}}
such that
{\tilde{Q}_{1}}\times{\tilde{Q}_{2}}\subseteq U^{***}_{k}
, where
U^{***}_{k}
is defined in (
5.2
).
Now let
R^{\dagger}
be
100\beta_{k}({\tilde{Q}_{1}}\times{\tilde{Q}_{2}})
, where
\beta_{k}=2^{k/(2\nu_{1}+2\nu_{2})}
.
By Lemmas
3.5
and
3.1
and Theorem
4.4
,

\displaystyle\biggl|\bigcup_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}R^{\dagger}\biggr|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{k/2}|U_{k}^{***}|\lesssim 2^{k/2}|U_{k}^{**}|\lesssim 2^{k/2}|U_{k}^{*}|\lesssim 2^{k/2}|U_{k}|\lesssim 2^{k/2}F_{\Phi}(2^{-k}f).

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, to prove that

(5.12)

\bigg|\bigg\{\mathbf{g}\in\mathbf{G}\colon\mathcal{R}\biggl(\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\biggr)(\mathbf{g})>1\bigg\}\bigg|\lesssim F_{\Phi}(f),

it suffices to show that, for some
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

(5.13)

\sum_{R\in\mathscr{M}(U^{*}_{k})}\int_{(R^{\dagger})^{c}}|\mathcal{R}(a_{k,R})(\mathbf{g})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}\lesssim 2^{-\delta k}F_{\Phi}(f)

for every
k\geq 1
.
If (
5.13
) holds, then (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) imply (
5.10
).
We now prove (
5.13
).

\displaystyle\int_{(R^{\dagger})^{c}}|\mathcal{R}(a_{k,R})(\mathbf{g})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle\leq\iint_{g_{1}\notin 100\beta_{k}{\tilde{Q}_{1}}}|\mathcal{R}(a_{k,R})(\mathbf{g})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}+\iint_{g_{2}\notin 100\beta_{k}{\tilde{Q}_{2}}}|\mathcal{R}(a_{k,R})(\mathbf{g})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle=:\mathrm{I}_{1}+\mathrm{I}_{2},

say, where
\beta_{k}=2^{{k/(2\nu_{1}+2\nu_{2})}}
.
Since the estimates of
\mathrm{I}_{1}
and
\mathrm{I}_{2}
are symmetric, we only estimate
\mathrm{I}_{1}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{g_{1}\notin 100\beta_{k}{\tilde{Q}_{1}}}\int_{100{Q_{2}}}|\mathcal{R}(a_{k,R})(\mathbf{g})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}+\int_{g_{1}\notin 100\beta_{k}{\tilde{Q}_{1}}}\int_{(100{Q_{2}})^{c}}|\mathcal{R}(a_{k,R})(\mathbf{g})|\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{g}

\displaystyle=:\mathrm{I}_{11}+\mathrm{I}_{12},

say.
By Hölder’s inequality and the
\mathsf{L}^{2}(G_{2})
-boundedness of
\mathcal{R}^{[2]}_{j_{2}}
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{11}

\displaystyle\lesssim|{\tilde{Q}_{2}}|^{1/2}\int_{g_{1}\notin 100\beta_{k}{\tilde{Q}_{1}}}\biggl(\int_{Q_{2}}\bigl(\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}(a_{k,R})(g_{1},g_{2})\bigr)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}g_{2}\biggr)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}g_{1}.

By the cancellation condition on
a_{k,R}(\cdot,g_{2})
and the smoothness of
\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}(a_{k,R})(\mathbf{g})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{{G_{1}}}\bigl(\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}(g_{1},h_{1})-\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}(g_{1},z({Q_{1}}))\bigr)a_{k,R}(h_{1},g_{2})\,\mathrm{d}h_{1}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{1}})^{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{1}(g_{1},z({Q_{1}}))^{\nu_{1}+\varepsilon}}\int_{{G_{1}}}a_{k,R}(h_{1},g_{2})\,\mathrm{d}h_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{1}})^{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{1}(g_{1},z({Q_{1}}))^{\nu_{1}+\varepsilon}}|{Q_{1}}|^{1/2}\biggl(\int_{{G_{1}}}|a_{k,R}(h_{1},g_{2})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}h_{1}\biggr)^{1/2},

where
z({Q_{1}})
is the center of
{Q_{1}}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{11}\lesssim\beta_{k}^{-\varepsilon}\gamma_{1}(R)^{-\varepsilon}\left|R\right|^{1/2}\left\|a_{k,R}\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}.

To estimate
\mathrm{I}_{12}
, we use the cancellation of
a_{k,R}
to write
\mathcal{R}(a_{k,R})(\mathbf{g})
as

\displaystyle\int_{\beta_{k}R}\bigl(\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}(g_{1},h_{1})-\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}(g_{1},z({Q_{1}}))\bigr)\bigl(\mathcal{R}^{[2]}_{j_{2}}(g_{2},h_{2})-\mathcal{R}^{[2]}_{j_{2}}(g_{2},z({Q_{2}}))\bigr)a_{k,R}(h_{1},h_{2})\,\mathrm{d}h_{1}\,\mathrm{d}h_{2},

where
z({Q_{2}})
is the center of
{Q_{2}}
.
Using the smoothness of
\mathcal{R}^{[1]}_{j_{1}}
and
\mathcal{R}^{[2]}_{j_{2}}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{12}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{g_{1}\notin 100b{\tilde{Q}_{1}}}\int_{(100{Q_{2}})^{c}}\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{1}})^{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{1}(g_{1},z({Q_{1}}))^{\nu_{1}+\varepsilon}}\frac{\operatorname{\ell}({Q_{2}})^{\varepsilon}}{\rho_{2}(g_{2},z({Q_{2}}))^{\nu_{2}+\varepsilon}}\,\mathrm{d}g_{1}\,\mathrm{d}g_{2}\left|R\right|^{1/2}\left\|a_{k,R}\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}

\displaystyle\lesssim b^{-\varepsilon}\gamma_{1}(R)^{-\varepsilon}\left|R\right|^{1/2}\left\|a_{k,R}\right\|_{\mathsf{L}^{2}(\mathbf{G})}.

Then, by continuing as in Section
5.1
, we establish (
5.13
) and complete the proof. ∎

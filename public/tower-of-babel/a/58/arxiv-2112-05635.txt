Geometry of inhomogeneous Poisson brackets, multicomponent Harry Dym hierarchies and multicomponent Hunter-Saxton equations
Andrey Yu. Konyaev

Abstract
We introduce a natural class of multicomponent local Poisson structures 𝒫 k + 𝒫 1 \mathcal{P}_{k}+\mathcal{P}_{1} , where 𝒫 1 \mathcal{P}_{1} is local Poisson bracket of order one and 𝒫 k \mathcal{P}_{k} is a homogeneous Poisson bracket of odd order k k under assumption that is has Darboux coordinates (Darboux-Poisson bracket) and non-degenerate. For such brackets we obtain general formulas in arbitrary coordinates, find normal forms (related to Frobenius triples) and provide the description of the Casimirs, using purely algebraic procedure. In two-component case we completely classify such brackets up to the point transformation. From the description of Casimirs we derive new Harry Dym (HD) hierarchies and new Hunter-Saxton (HS) equations for arbitrary number of components. In two component case our HS equation differs from the well-known HS2 equation. MSC classes: 37K05, 37K10, 37K25, 53B20, 53B30, 53B50, 35Q53, 35Q35

Introduction

We start with a motivational example. Recall that Harry Dym (HD) equation is a non-linear partial differential equation on a function
u(t,x)
, usually written in compact form

u_{t}=\Bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{2u}}\Bigg)_{xxx}

(1)

It was discovered by Kruscal in an unpublished work of Harry Dym (see book
[
4
]
) and rediscovered by Sabatier in his series of papers dedicated to the classical strings
[
5
]
. It plays an important role in study of infinite dimensional integrable systems and bares a deep connection with KdV, Camassa-Holm, KP and other integrable hierarchies (see
[
2
]
,
[
1
]
for details and further references).

Harry Dym equation possesses bihamiltonian structure. Consider the space of real or complex-valued
C^{\infty}
functions on
S^{1}
. We denote the coordinate on the circle as
x\in[0,2\pi]
and the function itself as
u
, omitting the dependence in
x
. The
j-
th derivative of
u
in
x
is denoted as
u_{x^{j}}
.

We are interested in the functionals in the form
\mathcal{H}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}\mathcal{h}\mathrm{d}x
, where density
\mathcal{h}
is a finite sum of terms

\mathcal{h}_{j_{1}\dots j_{s}}u_{x^{j_{1}}}\dots u_{x^{j_{s}}},\quad j_{1}+\dots+j_{s}=k.

(2)

Here
\mathcal{h}_{j_{1}\dots j_{s}}
are smooth (may be complex-valued) functions of
u
and, possibly,
x
. The corresponding densities are called homogeneous differential polynomials of degree
k
.

Consider a pair of Poisson brackets, which act on functionals
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathcal{h}_{1}\mathrm{d}x
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathcal{h}_{2}\mathrm{d}x
as

\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\}_{1}=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u}D^{3}\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\delta u}\Big)\mathrm{d}x,\quad\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\}_{2}=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\Big(2u\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u}D\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\delta u}\Big)+u_{x}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\delta u}\Big)\mathrm{d}x.

(3)

Here
D
is total derivative in variable
x
and
\frac{\delta}{\delta u}
is variational derivative in field variable
u
. Brackets (
3
) are compatible in a usual sense: their linear combination with arbitrary constant coefficients is again a Poisson bracket.

The equation (
1
) is Hamiltonian with respect to both brackets. Moreover, there exists a sequence of functionals
\mathcal{H}_{i}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathcal{h}_{i}\mathrm{d}x,i\geq 1
, such that the corresponding flows have the form

\displaystyle u_{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=2uD\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u}\Big)+u_{x}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u}=0,

(4)

\displaystyle u_{t_{i}}

\displaystyle=2uD\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{i+1}}{\delta u}\Big)+u_{x}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{i+1}}{\delta u}=D^{3}\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{i}}{\delta u}\Big),\quad i\geq 1.

The flows (
4
) pairwise commute and Harry Dym equation (
1
) is
u_{t_{1}}
for the functional
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}\sqrt{2u}\mathrm{d}x
. Functionals
\mathcal{H}_{i},i\geq 1
are common conservation laws for all the equations (
4
). Usually the term hierarchy refers to the collection of
u_{t_{i}}
, but we use it for the collection of
\mathcal{H}_{i}
as well.

The densities
\mathcal{h}_{i}
for functionals
\mathcal{H}_{i}
can be found by purely algebraic means, without solving any differential equations.
Consider the identity

u=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{v}^{2}+\mathrm{v}_{x}.

(5)

We search the solution in a form of a series

\mathrm{v}=\mathcal{v}_{1}+\mathcal{v}_{2}+\mathcal{v}_{3}+\dots,

(6)

where
\mathrm{v}_{i}
is either zero or a homogeneous differential polynomial of degree
i-1
. Substituting
\mathrm{v}
into (
5
) yields the recursion formulas for the components of the series

\displaystyle\mathcal{v}^{2}_{1}

\displaystyle=2u,

\displaystyle\mathcal{v}_{i+1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{v}_{1}}\Bigg(\,\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{i}\mathcal{v}_{j}\mathcal{v}_{i+2-j}+(\mathcal{v}_{i})_{x}\Bigg),\quad i\geq 1.

The series is uniquely defined by the solution of the first equation, that is the choice of either
\mathcal{v}_{1}=\sqrt{2u}
or
\mathcal{v}_{1}=-\sqrt{2u}
. Taking
\mathcal{h}_{i}=\mathcal{v}_{2i-1}
we get a collection of functionals
\mathcal{H}_{i}
. This is exactly the Harry Dym hierarchy.

We can try and continue hierarchy for negative
i
. For
i=-1
and
i=-2
we get

\displaystyle u_{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=D^{3}\Big(\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}{\delta u}\Big)=0,

(7)

\displaystyle u_{t_{-1}}

\displaystyle=D^{3}\Big(\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u}\Big)=2uD\Big(\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}{\delta u}\Big)+u_{x}\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}{\delta u},

\displaystyle u_{t_{-2}}

\displaystyle=2uD\Big(\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u}\Big)+u_{x}\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u}

We take
\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}u\mathrm{d}x
. Introducing new variable
u=v_{xx}
we get
\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u}=v
. After renaming
t_{-2}
as
t
the third equation in (
7

v_{xxt}=2v_{xx}v_{x}+v_{xxx}v
. Integrating it by
x
and taking the integration constant to be zero, we arrive to the Hunter-Saxton equation

v_{xt}=v_{xx}v+\frac{1}{2}v_{x}^{2}.

(8)

This equation originally appeared in the study of nematic liquid crystals.

All the mentioned well-known results admit a natural interpretation in terms of inhomogeneous Poisson brackets. Consider a sum of the brackets (
3
), which is again a Poisson bracket (actually, it is one of KdV brackets)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\}

\displaystyle=\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\}_{1}+\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\}_{2}=

(9)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\Bigg(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u}D^{3}\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\delta u}\Big)+2u\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u}D\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\delta u}\Big)+u_{x}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\delta u}\Bigg)\mathrm{d}x.

Using Lemma
6.4
from section
6
one can show that that Poisson bracket (
9
) has no Casimirs in the category of differential polynomials, except constants.

At the same time one can extend the Poisson bracket (
9
) onto the space of the series of differential polynomials in the form (
6
). We treat such series as density, which depends on all derivatives
u_{x^{j}}
. The corresponding functional is

\mathrm{W}=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{w}\mathrm{d}x=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\mathcal{w}_{i}\mathrm{d}x=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{W}_{i}.

For Harry Dym hierarchy
\mathcal{H}_{i}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}\mathcal{h}_{i}\mathrm{d}x
consider the functional
\mathrm{H}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{h}\mathrm{d}x
for density
\mathrm{h}=\sum_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}\mathcal{h}_{i}
. Then for arbitrary functional
\mathrm{W}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{w}\mathrm{d}x
the conditions (
4
) yield

\{\mathrm{W},\mathrm{H}\}=0.

In other words, the Harry Dym hierarchy defines the Casimirs of the inhomogeneous Poisson bracket. This implies, that the geometry of such bracket (the description of bracket and Casimirs) contains all the necessary information, that allows one to write the equations in any given coordinates.

We apply this idea in case of arbitrary
n
. To do that we introduce a natural class of inhomogeneous Poisson brackets, for which the geometry can be completely described. This, in turn, leads to the natural (and new!) generalizations of Harry Dym hierarchies and Hunter-Saxton equations. The brackets itself are described by a purely geometric object: a pair of flat metrics with certain compatibility conditions. The Casimirs and corresponding hierarchies are found by purely algebraic means. In our work we use the language of Dubrovin-Novikov brackets as the most general one. The further results, associated with compatible inhomogeneous brackets of the given type are given in
[
3
]
.

First, we recall some basic definitions and results of the theory of Dubrovin-Novikov brackets (for more detailed exposure on the subject we refer the reader to
[
6
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
).

Let
\mathrm{M}^{n}
be the
n-
dimensional ball. Denote
\Omega\mathrm{M}^{n}
to be a space of maps from
S^{1}\to\mathrm{M}^{n}
. The functionals
\mathcal{H}:\Omega\mathrm{M}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}
(for
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
), we are interested in, are written (same as for
n=1
) in the form
\mathcal{H}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}\mathcal{h}\mathrm{d}x
. Here
\mathcal{h}
is a differential polynomial in
n
field variables.

The local homogeneous Poisson bracket of order
k
is a bilinear skew-symmetric operation, which acts on functional
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathcal{h}_{1}\mathrm{d}x
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathcal{h}_{2}\mathrm{d}x
as

\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\}=\int\limits^{2\pi}_{0}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u^{\alpha}}h^{\alpha\beta}D^{k}\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\delta u^{\beta}}\Big)\mathrm{d}x+\int\limits^{\infty}_{0}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u^{\alpha}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathrm{j}}D^{k-j}\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\delta u^{\beta}}\Big)\mathrm{d}x

(10)

and satisfies the Jacobi identity. The entries of matrix
h^{\alpha\beta}
are the functions of field variables
u^{\alpha}
only. The entries of matrices
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathrm{j}}
are homogeneous differential polynomials of degree
j
. The skew-symmetry condition and Jacobi identity provide differential conditions on components of
\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathrm{j}}
.

The symbolic expression

\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}=h^{\alpha\beta}D^{k}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}_{\mathrm{j}}D^{k-j}

(11)

is called a Hamiltonian operator. For Poisson bracket (
10
) it plays the same role as Poisson tensor plays for finite-dimensional Poisson bracket.

For a given functional
\mathcal{H}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}\mathcal{h}\mathrm{d}x
the corresponding Hamiltonian flow is defined as

u^{\alpha}_{t}=\mathcal{P}^{\alpha q}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta u^{q}}=h^{\alpha q}D^{k}\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta u^{q}}\Big)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{P}^{\alpha q}_{\mathrm{j}}D^{k-j}\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}}{\delta u^{q}}\Big).

For arbitrary test functional
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}\mathcal{h}_{1}\mathrm{d}x
it satisfies the obvious relation
\partial_{t}\mathcal{H}_{1}=\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}\}
.

Under the coordinate change
\bar{u}(u)=(\bar{u}^{1}(u^{1},\dots,u^{n}),\dots,\bar{u}^{n}(u^{1},\dots,u^{n}))
the components of the Hamiltonian operator are transformed as (formula 3.2
[
6
]
):

\bar{h}^{\alpha\beta}=h^{pq}\frac{\partial\bar{u}^{\alpha}}{\partial u^{p}}\frac{\partial\bar{u}^{\beta}}{\partial u^{q}},\quad\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{j}}^{\alpha\beta}=\sum\limits_{i=j}^{k}\binom{j}{i}\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{i}}^{pq}\frac{\partial\bar{u}^{\alpha}}{\partial u^{p}}D^{j-i}\Big[\frac{\partial\bar{u}^{\beta}}{\partial u^{q}}\Big].

Here
\binom{j}{i}
are binomial coefficients,
\bar{h}^{\alpha\beta}
and
\bar{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathrm{j}}^{\alpha\beta}
stand for the coefficients of Hamiltonian operator in coordinates
\bar{u}^{1},\dots,\bar{u}^{n}
.

As it was mentioned earlier, the term
h^{\alpha\beta}
does not depend on differential coordinates and under the coordinate transformation behaves like a contrvariant tensor of type
(2,0)
. Due to skew-symmetry condition of the Poisson bracket (formula 3.3,
[
6
]
) this tensor is symmetric for odd
k
and skew-symmetric for even
k
. The Hamiltonian operator is called non-degenerate if
\operatorname{det}h\neq 0
.

The analog of (
9
) is an inhomogeneous Poisson bracket, for which its Hamiltonian operator is a sum of two homogeneous parts
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
of orders
1
and
3
respectively. Due to Theorem 7.1 in
[
6
]
both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are Hamiltonian operators on their own. Thus, by definition, we have a pair of compatible Poisson brackets.

The local homogeneous Poisson bracket of order
k
is Darboux-Poisson if there exist coordinates
u^{1},\dots,u^{n}
, such that the Poisson bracket takes form

\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}}{\delta u^{\alpha}}h^{\alpha\beta}D^{k}\Big(\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}}{\delta u^{\beta}}\Big)\mathrm{d}x.

Following
[
6
]
we call such coordinates Darboux coordinates. The geometry of homogeneous Poisson brackets of order
k=3
was investigated by a number of authors (
[
6
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
). In our work we assume that the bracket of order

is Darboux-Poisson.

The non-degenerate homogeneous Poisson brackets of order one are called Poisson brackets of hydrodynamic type. The physicists used this type of brackets for a long time: Landau, for example, used them without any specific name in formulation of his theory of the superfluidity for hellium (formula 1.4 in
[
10
]
).

The geometric theory of these objects was developed by Dubrovin and Novikov in the 1980s (
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
, see also
[
9
]
). They showed, in particular, that these Poisson brackets always have Darboux coordinates and, thus, are in one-to-one correspondence with flat metrics. We will denote the Hamiltonian operator of non-degenerate homogeneous Poisson bracket of order one as
\mathcal{A}_{g}
, where
g
is the corresponding flat contrvariant metric.

The paper is organized as follows: in the next section
2
we formulate the main results and provide important examples. The Theorem
2.1
provides the formula for Darboux-Poisson brackets in local coordinates for arbitrary
k
. It generalizes the famous formula by Dobrovin and Novikov. In particular, it implies that for odd

Theorem
2.2
yields normal form for inhomogeneous Poisson brackets with the odd term of order

being homogeneous Darboux-Poisson bracket. For

the statement of the Theorem
2.2
was independently obtained by P. Lorenzoni and R. Vitolo in their unpublished paper.

For

[
21

. It implies that any such bracket corresponds to a Frobenius triple (see the exact definitions below) — that is a commutative associative algebra with a pair of non-degenerate invariant forms. This implies, that in general the description of such brackets is a lost cause. Yet, in small dimension it is possible. Theorem
2.3
provides the classification of Darboux-Poisson brackets in dimension two. This classification contains the inhomogeneous brackets of many famous integrable systems.

The last Theorem

provides an algebraic procedure to construct the conservation laws of such system in an implicit form. Similar to the case of dimension one we get an equation, bares similarities to both Balinskii-Novikov formula and Miura formula. This identity provides the recursion relation, which can be solved.

In the end of the section we provide the construction of Hunter-Saxton equation in case of arbitrary inhomogeneous Darboux-Poisson bracket with minor non-degeneracy conditions (formula (
31
)). We also describe two important examples, for which we write all the formulas explicitly. We compare them with the cases, known in the literature, and show, that they are, indeed, new.

All the proofs are provided in sections

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{q}S^{\alpha\beta}_{s}=0,\quad S^{\alpha\beta}_{q}S^{q\gamma}_{s}=S^{\gamma\beta}_{q}S^{q\alpha}_{s},\quad S^{\alpha\beta}_{q}h^{q\gamma}_{s}=S^{\gamma\beta}_{q}h^{q\alpha}_{s},\quad S^{\alpha\beta}_{q}b^{q\gamma}_{s}=S^{\gamma\beta}_{q}b^{q\alpha}_{s}.

(22)

In flat coordinates of
h^{\alpha\beta}
, the constants
a^{\alpha\beta}_{s}
from formula (
21
) are related to tensor
S^{\alpha\beta}_{s}
as
a^{\alpha\beta}_{s}=-S^{\alpha\beta}_{s}
.

The first statement of Theorem
2.2
shows, that in our line of investigation there are no non-trivial higher order analogs (for example, with fifth order derivative in
x
) of Harry Dym equation. The Hamiltonian operator for
k=3
in given dimension is defined by a finite collection of constants
a^{\alpha\beta}_{s},b^{\alpha\beta},h^{\alpha\beta}
. Let us explore the algebraic properties of these constants.

First, recall that flat coordinate system for
h
is defined up to an affine transformation. In case of
k=3
the translation by vector
c=(c^{1},\dots,c^{n})
changes
b^{\alpha\beta}
into
\bar{b}^{\alpha\beta}=b^{\alpha\beta}-a^{\alpha\beta}_{s}c^{s}
.

Let
\mathrm{p}
be a coordinate origin of the coordinate system
u^{1},\dots,u^{n}
, in which the inhomogeneou Poisson bracket is in the form (
21
). Restricting ourselves to the coordinate changes that preserve point
\mathrm{p}
, we observe that the constants
a^{\alpha\beta}_{s}
behave as tensor of type
(2,1)
, while
h^{\alpha\beta},b^{\alpha\beta}
behave as tensors of type
(2,0)
.

Consider a commutative associative algebra
\mathfrak{a},\star
over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. The symmetric bilinear form
h
is invariant form if

h(\xi\star\eta,\zeta)=h(\xi,\eta\star\zeta),\quad\forall\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\mathfrak{a}.

(23)

If an invariant form is non-degenerate, then it is called Frobenius form. A Frobenius triple
(\mathfrak{a},b,h)
is a commutative associative algebra
\mathfrak{a}
, equipped with two Frobenius forms
b
and
h
.

The formulas (
20
) and (
23
) imply that constants

are just components of some Frobenius triple, written in a given basis of

. Moreover, the coordinate change that preserves coordinate origin
\mathrm{p}
does not change the triple. So, in algebraic terms, the Theorem
2.2
establishes one-to-one correspondence between inhomogeneous Hamiltonian operators of the form (
18

\mathrm{p}\in\mathrm{M}
and Frobenius triples on
T^{*}_{\mathrm{p}}\mathrm{M}^{n}
.

Remark 2.2
.

In case of degenerate
h^{\alpha\beta}
even if the Poisson bracket is in the form (
21
) the constants
a^{\alpha\beta}_{s}
do not necessarily define commutative associative algebra. The following is a Hamiltonian operator of inhomogeneous Poisson bracket (
[
22
]
, the case of algebra N4)

\mathcal{P}^{\alpha\beta}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&0\end{array}\right)D^{3}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2u^{1}&u^{2}\\
u^{2}&0\end{array}\right)D+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u^{1}_{x}&0\\
u^{2}_{x}&0\end{array}\right).

The corresponding algebra is non-commutative, but still associative (it is, in fact, an associative Novikov algebra). At the same time coordinate change

transforms the Hamiltonian operator of the Poisson bracket into the form

\bar{\mathcal{P}}^{\alpha\beta}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)D^{3}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&2\bar{u}^{1}\\

\bar{u}^{1}_{x}&\bar{u}^{2}_{x}\end{array}\right).

Now the algebra, corresponding to
g^{\alpha\beta}
is commutative and associative. This Hamiltonian operator can be obtained as a limit
h_{1}\to 0
in family
(31)
(see Theorem
2.3
).
\blacksquare

The next theorem shows, that in coordinates, described in Theorem

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
a^{\alpha\beta}_{s},b^{\alpha\beta},h^{\alpha\beta}
be a Frobenius triple, written in a given basis. Define
r^{\beta}_{p}=\frac{1}{2}h^{\beta q}b_{qp}
and
c^{\beta}_{ps}=\frac{1}{2}b_{pq}a^{q\beta}_{s}
, where
b_{pq}
are components of the inverse of
b^{\alpha\beta}
. Denote
l^{\beta}_{s}
to be the components of a good square root of matrix
r^{\beta}_{p}
.

Consider a system of algebraic equations

u^{\beta}=\mathrm{v}^{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}c^{\beta}_{ps}\mathrm{v}^{p}\mathrm{v}^{s}+l^{\beta}_{s}\mathrm{v}^{s}_{x},\quad\beta=1,\dots,n.

(26)

The solution to this system is a collection of series
\mathrm{v}^{1},\dots,\mathrm{v}^{n}
in the form
\mathrm{v}^{\beta}=\mathcal{v}^{\beta}_{0}+\mathcal{v}^{\beta}_{1}+\mathcal{v}^{\beta}_{2}+\dots
. Here
\mathcal{v}^{\beta}_{i}
are either zero or a homogeneous differential polynomial of degree
i-1
. Take

\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{i}=\int\limits^{2\pi}_{0}v^{\alpha}_{2i-1}\mathrm{d}x,\quad i=1,\dots.

(27)

Then the following holds.

1.

For each
\alpha=1,\dots,n
the series
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{i}
defines a multicomponent Harry Dym hierarchy

\displaystyle u^{\beta}_{t_{0}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{\beta q}_{g}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{1}}{\delta u^{q}}=0,

(28)

\displaystyle u^{\beta}_{t_{i}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}^{\beta q}_{g}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{i+1}}{\delta u^{q}}=\mathcal{B}_{h}^{\beta q}\frac{\delta\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{i+1}}{\delta u^{q}},\quad i\geq 2.

for the Hamiltonian operators
\mathcal{A}^{\beta q}_{g}=2(b^{\beta q}+a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s})D+a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}_{x}
and
\mathcal{B}_{h}^{\beta q}=h^{\beta q}D^{3}

2.

For arbitrary
\alpha,\beta
and
i,j
the flows
u_{t^{\alpha}_{i}}
and
u_{t^{\beta}_{j}}
commute. The functional
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{i}
is a conservation law of
u_{t^{\beta}_{j}}

3.

Consider the sequence of functionals
\mathcal{H}_{i},i\geq 1
, which define the multicompomemt Harry Dym hierarchy with respect to the the Hamiltonian operators
\mathcal{A}^{\beta q}_{g}=2(b^{\beta q}+a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s})D+a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}_{x}
and
\mathcal{B}_{h}^{\beta q}=h^{\beta q}D^{3}
. Assume in addition, that each
\mathcal{H}_{i}
possesses the following property: it is either zero or a homogeneous differential polynomial of degree
2i-1
. Then there exists a collection of constants
c_{0},c_{1},\dots,c_{n}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=c_{0}+c_{1}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{1}+\dots+c_{n}\mathcal{H}^{n}_{1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{i}

\displaystyle=c_{1}\mathcal{H}^{1}_{i}+\dots+c_{n}\mathcal{H}^{n}_{i},\quad i>1.

In particular, the linear space of the multicomponent Harry Dym hierarchies with the property given above is finite-dimensional and sequences
\mathcal{H}^{\alpha}_{i},i\geq 1,\alpha=1,\dots,n
together with constant functional form a basis in this space.

4.

If in addition
a^{\alpha\beta}_{s}
has unity, then one can replace equation (
26
) with simpler Miura type equation

u^{\beta}=\frac{1}{2}c^{\beta}_{ps}\mathrm{v}^{p}\mathrm{v}^{s}+l^{\beta}_{s}\mathrm{v}^{s}_{x},\quad\beta=1,\dots,n.

(29)

and Hamiltonian operator of order one in formula (
28
) with
\mathcal{A}^{\beta q}_{g}=2a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}D+a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}_{x}
.

Assume now that in Frobenius triple
a^{\alpha\beta}_{s},b^{\alpha\beta},h^{\alpha\beta}
the commutative associative algebra has unity
e=(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
. For Hamiltonian operators
\mathcal{A}^{\beta q}_{g}=2a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}D+a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}_{x}
and
\mathcal{B}_{h}^{\beta q}=h^{\beta q}D^{3}
(these are the Hamiltonian operators from the fourth statement of the Theorem
2.4
) we write negative hierarchy for
i=0,-1,-2
we get:

\displaystyle u^{\beta}_{t_{0}}

\displaystyle=h^{\beta q}D^{3}\Big(\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}{\delta u^{q}}\Big)=0,

(30)

\displaystyle u^{\beta}_{t_{-1}}

\displaystyle=2a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}D\Big(\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}{\delta u^{q}}\Big)+a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}_{x}\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}}{\delta u^{q}}=h^{\beta q}D^{3}\Big(\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u^{q}}\Big),

\displaystyle u^{\beta}_{t_{-2}}

\displaystyle=2a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}D\Big(\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u^{q}}\Big)+a^{\beta q}_{s}u^{s}_{x}\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u^{q}}

Consider functional
\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\int^{2\pi}_{0}e_{\alpha}u^{\alpha}\mathrm{d}x
. The equation for
i=-1
takes form

u^{\alpha}_{t_{-1}}=u^{\alpha}_{x}=h^{\alpha q}D^{3}\Big(\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u^{q}}\Big).

We introduce new variables
v^{\alpha}
as

v^{p}_{xxx}=\mathcal{B}^{pq}\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u^{q}}.

In given coordinates this yields
h^{qp}\frac{\delta\bar{\mathcal{H}}_{-1}}{\delta u^{q}}=v^{p}
. We get, that

. The equation (
30
) for
i=-2
takes form

v^{\alpha}_{xxt_{-2}}=2a^{\alpha q}_{s}v^{s}_{xx}h_{qp}v^{p}_{x}+a^{\alpha q}_{s}v^{s}_{xxx}h_{qp}v^{p}.

Denote
\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{ps}=h_{pq}a^{\alpha q}_{s}
. Renaming
t_{-2}
as
t
, we integrate both sides of the equation, using property
\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{ps}=\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{sp}
(Lemma

). Taking the integration constants we arrive to the system

\displaystyle v^{\alpha}_{xt}=\frac{1}{2}\bar{c}^{\alpha}_{ps}\big(v^{p}_{xx}v^{s}+v^{p}_{x}v^{s}_{x}+v^{p}v^{s}_{xx}\big).

(31)

We call this system the multicomponent Hunter-Saxton equation.

By construction we see, that these equations are defined for arbitrary commutative associative algebra. The existence of Frobenius form is necessary and sufficient for this equation to be bihamiltonian with respect to

and
\mathcal{B}_{g}
. The conservation laws for this equation are obtained from Theorem

7
Appendix: some facts about commutative associative algebras with Frobenius forms

In this section we collect some basic facts about commutative associative algebras and their invariant forms. We always assume that algebra is over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. If the field is not specified, the result holds for both.

Proposition 7.1
.

Consider a commutative associative algebra
\mathfrak{a}
and arbitrary
m\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. Then
h_{m}(\xi,\eta)=m(\xi\star\eta)
is an invariant form. We call this form exact.

Proof.

We have

h_{m}(\xi\star\eta,\zeta)=m((\xi\star\eta)\star\zeta)=m(\xi\star(\eta\star\zeta))=h_{m}(\xi,\eta\star\zeta).

The Proposition is proved.
∎

Among others we deal with commutative associative algebras with unity

Proposition 7.2
.

Consider a commutative associative algebra
\mathfrak{a}
. Then

1.

If
\mathfrak{a}
has unity, then every invariant form is exact

2.

If there exists a non-degenerate exact invariant form, then
\mathfrak{a}
has unity

Proof.

First, assume that
h
is an invariant form for commutative associative algebra
\mathfrak{a}
with unity
e
. Define
m\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
as
m(\eta)=h(e,\eta)
. We have

h(\xi,\eta)=h(e,\xi\star\eta)=h_{m}(\xi,\eta).

The first part of Proposition is proved. Now proceed to the second part. Assume that
h_{m}
is non-degenerate exact invariant form for
m\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. It can be treated as map
h_{m}:\mathfrak{a}\to\mathfrak{a}^{*}
. We write

h_{m}(\xi,\zeta)=m(\xi\star\zeta)=h_{m}(\xi\star\zeta,h_{m}^{-1}m)=h_{m}(\xi\star h_{m}^{-1}m,\zeta).

(64)

Denote
\eta=h_{m}^{-1}m
and consider the operator
R_{\eta}\xi=\xi\star\eta
. The formula (
64
) takes form

h_{m}(\xi,\eta)=h_{m}(R_{\eta}\xi,\zeta)

By construction
R_{\eta}=h_{m}^{-1}h_{m}=\operatorname{Id}
and
\eta
is unity. The Proposition is proved.
∎

The commutative associative algebra
\mathfrak{a}
is called reducible if it can be written as
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\prime\prime}
for some commutative associative algebras
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{a}^{\prime\prime}
, and irreducible otherwise. By definition, every commutative associative algebra is a sum of irreducible algebras.

Proposition 7.3
.

Assume that a commutative associative algebra
\mathfrak{a}
is reducible, that is

\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}^{\prime}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{\prime\prime},

(65)

where
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{a}^{\prime\prime}
are both commutative associative algebras. If
\mathfrak{a}
has unity and invariant non-degenerate form
h
, then both
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{a}^{\prime\prime}
have unities, decomposition (
65
) is orthogonal with respect to
h
and restrictions of
h
on each summand are non-degenerate invariant forms.

Proof.

Denote unity in
\mathfrak{a}
as
e
. Consider the decomposition
e=e^{\prime}+e^{\prime\prime}
of the unity with respect to (
65
). By construction both
e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}
are unities for
\mathfrak{a}^{\prime},\mathfrak{a}^{\prime\prime}
respectively.

For arbitrary
\xi\in\mathfrak{a},\eta\in\mathfrak{a}^{\prime\prime}
we have
\xi\star\eta=0
and, thus,

h(\xi,\eta)=h(\xi,\eta\star e)=h(\xi\star\eta,e)=0.

Finally, if either of
h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}
has kernel, then this is the kernel of the entire
h
. The proposition is proved.
∎

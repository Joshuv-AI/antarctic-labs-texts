A family of interpolation inequalities involving products of low-order derivatives
Frédéric Marbach

Abstract
Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequalities relate Lebesgue norms of iterated derivatives of a function. We present a generalization of these inequalities in which the low-order term of the right-hand side is replaced by a Lebesgue norm of a pointwise product of derivatives of the function.



1
Introduction

The symbol
\lesssim
denotes inequalities holding up to a constant, which can depend on the parameters of the statement, but not on the unknown function.
By convenience, we restrict the statements to smooth functions, although they persist in appropriate Sobolev spaces, by usual regularization arguments.

1.1
The classical Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality

In their full generality, the following interpolation inequalities date back to Gagliardo’s and Nirenberg’s respective short communications at the 1958 International Congress of Mathematicians in Edinburgh, later published in
[
8
,
9
,
17
,
18
]
.
We refer to
[
7
]
for a recent proof with an historical perspective.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
,
0\leq k\leq j<m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\theta\in[\theta^{*},1]
where

\theta^{*}:=\frac{j-k}{m-k}.

(1.1)

Assume that

\frac{1}{p}-j=\theta\left(\frac{1}{r}-m\right)+(1-\theta)\left(\frac{1}{q}-k\right).

(1.2)

Then, for
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}^{\theta}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{1-\theta}.

(1.3)

In this 1D setting, estimate (
1.3
) had also been derived with optimal constants by Landau
[
15
]
, Kolmogorov
[
13
]
and Stein
[
21
]
for particular cases of the parameters and exponents.

Remark 1.1

(Role of
k
)
.

Usually, such inequalities are stated with
k=0
.
The less frequent case
k>0
can of course be reduced to the standard case
k=0
by applying the latter to the function
D^{k}u
.
We include it in the above statement to highlight the symmetry with our generalization in
Theorem

2
.

Remark 1.2

(Geometric setting)
.

We restrict the statements in this note to the one-dimensional case.

Mutatis mutandis
in (
1.2
),
Theorem

1
remains valid in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or sufficiently regular bounded domains
[
17
]
, or even exterior domains
[
5
]
, up to exceptional cases of the parameters.
In particular, the case
p=\infty
is only valid when
d=1
(see
[
7
, Theorem 1.1 and comments below]
).

Remark 1.3

(Fractional versions)
.

Although historical statements only involved integer orders of the derivatives, generalizations of
Theorem

1
to fractional Sobolev spaces
[
3
,
4
]
or Hölder spaces
[
14
]
are now correctly understood.

Remark 1.4

(Bounded domain)
.

Let
0\leq k_{0}\leq k
and
s\in[1,\infty]
.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
, which is not necessarily compactly supported in
(0,1)
,

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}^{\theta}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{1-\theta}+\|D^{k_{0}}u\|_{L^{s}(0,1)}.

(1.4)

The supplementary (inhomogeneous, low-order) term is necessary, as one could have
D^{m}u\equiv 0
on
(0,1)
but
D^{j}u\neq 0
(see
Section

3.1
).

1.2
Statement of the main result

Motivated by applications to nonlinear control theory (see
Section

4
), our main result is the following one-dimensional generalization of
Theorem

1
in which the low-order term
\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}
of the right-hand side is replaced by a Lebesgue norm of a pointwise product of low-order derivatives of the function.

Theorem 2
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and

0\leq k_{1}\leq\dotsb\leq k_{\kappa}\leq j<m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\bar{k}:=(k_{1}+\dotsb+k_{\kappa})/\kappa
.
Let
\theta\in[\theta^{*},1]
, where

\theta^{*}:=\frac{j-\bar{k}}{m-\bar{k}}.

(1.5)

Assume that

\frac{1}{p}-j=\theta\left(\frac{1}{r}-m\right)+(1-\theta)\left(\frac{1}{q\kappa}-\bar{k}\right).

(1.6)

Then, for
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}^{\theta}\|D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{(1-\theta)/\kappa}.

(1.7)

Remark 1.5
.

Heuristically, everything behaves as if the pointwise product term was replaced by
\|D^{\bar{k}}u\|_{L^{q\kappa}(\mathbb{R})}
, see also
Section

1.4
.

Remark 1.6
.

In the critical case
\theta=\theta^{*}
, relation (
1.6
) is equivalent to

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\theta}{r}+\frac{1-\theta}{q\kappa}.

(1.8)

Corollary 1.7

(Bounded domain)
.

Let
0\leq k_{0}\leq k_{1}
and
s\in[1,\infty]
.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
, which is not necessarily compactly supported in
(0,1)
,

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}^{\theta}\|D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{(1-\theta)/\kappa}+\|D^{k_{0}}u\|_{L^{s}(0,1)}.

(1.9)

Our proof is inspired by Nirenberg’s historical one, as rewritten recently in
[
7
]
.
Compared with the usual case, we encounter two difficulties.
First, the additive version of (
1.7
) now involves a compactness argument (see
Proposition

2.4
).
Second, and maybe more importantly, the pointwise product nature of the new term breaks the usual subdivision argument (see
Section

2.4
) since, within a small interval where
u
is a polynomial of low degree, this term could vanish identically.
To circumvent this difficulty, we introduce a notion of “nowhere-polynomial” function (see
Section

2.3
) and we prove that any smooth function can be approximated in this class.

An illustration of these difficulties is that pointwise multiplicative inequalities of the form
|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\lesssim|u(x)u^{\prime\prime}(x)|
usually require to subtract from
u
a local polynomial approximation, and to formulate the estimate using the Hardy–Littlewood maximal functions
Mu
,
Mu^{\prime}
and
Mu^{\prime\prime}
instead of the raw functions (see e.g.
[
11
, Theorem 1]
).

1.3
Some examples

As illustrations of
Theorem

2
for small values of the parameter
\kappa
, and in view of
Section

4
, we state two particular cases.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
For
u\in W^{2k,\infty}_{0}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
,

\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{6}(0,1)}^{6}\lesssim\|D^{2k}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}^{2}\|uD^{k}u\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.

(1.10)

Proof.

This follows from
Theorem

2
with
\kappa=2
,
\bar{k}=\frac{k}{2}
,
\theta=\theta^{*}=\frac{k-\bar{k}}{2k-\bar{k}}=\frac{1}{3}
, for which
\frac{1}{6}=\frac{\theta}{\infty}+\frac{1-\theta}{2\cdot 2}
so that (
1.8
) holds.
The estimate for non-smooth
u
follows by standard regularization arguments.
∎

Corollary 1.9
.

For
u\in W^{3,\infty}_{0}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
,

\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{12}(0,1)}^{12}\lesssim\|u^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}^{6}\|uu^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.

(1.11)

Proof.

This follows from
Theorem

2
with
\kappa=3
,
\bar{k}=1
,
\theta=\theta^{*}=\frac{2-1}{3-1}=\frac{1}{2}
, for which
\frac{1}{12}=\frac{\theta}{\infty}+\frac{1-\theta}{2\cdot 3}
so that (
1.8
) holds.
The estimate for non-smooth
u
follows by standard regularization arguments.

Incidentally, this particular estimate can also be checked from the usual Gagliardo–Nirenberg estimate of
Theorem

1

\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{12}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|u^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}

(1.12)

and the coercivity estimate (
1.16
) proved below.
∎

1.4
Some open problems

As mentioned in
Remark

1.2
, the usual Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities admit generalizations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It would be natural to investigate such generalizations of
Theorem

2
.
A difficulty in this direction might be that one has to determine the appropriate (symmetric?) generalizations of the product
D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u
with partial derivatives.

As mentioned in
Remark

1.3
, the usual Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities admit generalizations in fractional Sobolev spaces. It would be natural to investigate such generalizations of
Theorem

2
, especially since, as noted in
Remark

1.5
, even for integer values of the parameters, the product
D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u
already behaves as a fractional Sobolev norm when
\bar{k}\notin\mathbb{N}
.

Another particularly challenging problem concerns the possibility to relax the assumptions
k_{i}\leq j
of
Theorem

2
.
A natural (weaker) assumption would be
\bar{k}\leq j
.
In particular, one can wonder in which settings the following result holds (corresponding to
j=\bar{k}
and
\theta=\theta^{*}=0
).

Open problem 1.10
.

Let
q\in[1,\infty]
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
0\leq k_{1}\leq\dotsb\leq k_{\kappa}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\bar{k}:=(k_{1}+\dotsb+k_{\kappa})/\kappa
.
When is it true that, for
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,

\|D^{\bar{k}}u\|_{L^{q\kappa}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}},

(1.13)

where the left-hand side should be interpreted as the fractional
\dot{W}^{\bar{k},q\kappa}(\mathbb{R})
semi-norm of
u
when
\bar{k}
is not an integer.

As noted in
Remark

1.5
, positive answers to
1.10
imply
Theorem

2
(up to exceptional cases) thanks to the (fractional) Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality
\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R})}^{\theta}\|D^{\bar{k}}u\|_{L^{q\kappa}(\mathbb{R})}^{1-\theta}
(see e.g.
[
3
,
4
]
when
\bar{k}\notin\mathbb{N}
).

Unfortunately, the proofs of
Section

2
do rely on the assumptions
k_{i}\leq j
.
In particular, estimate (
2.10
) below is false if there exists
i
such that
k_{i}>j
.
Nevertheless,
ad hoc
arguments entail that (
1.13
) holds for some examples, hinting that
1.10
might have positive answers.

Lemma 1.11
.

For
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{2},4}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|uu^{\prime}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})},

(1.14)

\displaystyle\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|uu^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(1.15)

\displaystyle\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{6}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|uu^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{3}}.

(1.16)

Proof.

Estimate (
1.14
) can be derived from the remark that
(u^{2})^{\prime}=2uu^{\prime}
.
Hence

\|u\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{2},4}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\||u|\|_{\dot{W}^{\frac{1}{2},4}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|u^{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\|2uu^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{2}},

(1.17)

where the first estimate with the absolute value is derived in
[
16
, Théorème 2]
and the second estimate in
[
19
, Section 5.4.4]
.

Estimates (
1.15
) and (
1.16
) come from straightforward integrations by parts and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{\prime})^{4}=-3\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(u^{\prime})^{2}u^{\prime\prime}\leq 3\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{\prime})^{4}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(uu^{\prime\prime})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(1.18)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{\prime})^{6}=-5\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(u^{\prime})^{4}u^{\prime\prime}\leq 5\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(u^{\prime})^{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(uu^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(1.19)

which entail (
1.15
) and (
1.16
).
∎

Estimate (
1.15
) above is very classical, for example stated as Lemma 1 in
[
12
]
, which contains many interesting generalizations.

2
Proofs on the real line

2.1
Sobolev inequalities with localized low-order terms

In this paragraph, we start by proving the natural statement that low-order terms in Sobolev inequalities can be localized in arbitrarily small subdomains.

Lemma 2.1

(Usual Sobolev embedding)
.

Let
0\leq j<m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\omega\subset(0,1)
be a non-empty open interval.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
,

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(\omega)}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}

(2.1)

Lemma 2.2

(Localized
W^{1,1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
embedding)
.

Let
\omega\subset(0,1)
be a non-empty open interval.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
,

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\lesssim\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\|u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}.

(2.2)

Proof.

We write
\omega=(x_{1},x_{2})
with
0\leq x_{1}<x_{2}\leq 1
.
Let
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
.
For any
x\in[0,1]
and
x_{0}\in(x_{1},x_{2})
,

u(x)=u(x_{0})+\int_{x_{0}}^{x}u^{\prime}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

(2.3)

Hence, for any
x\in[0,1]
, averaging over
x_{0}\in(x_{1},x_{2})
,

u(x)=\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}u(x_{0})\,\mathrm{d}x_{0}+\frac{1}{x_{2}-x_{1}}\int_{x_{1}}^{x_{2}}\left(\int_{x_{0}}^{x}u^{\prime}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right)\,\mathrm{d}x_{0},

(2.4)

which entails (
2.2
).
∎

Lemma 2.3

(Localized Sobolev embedding)
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
.
Let
0\leq k\leq j<m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\omega\subset(0,1)
be a non-empty open interval.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
,

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}+\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{q}(\omega)}.

(2.5)

Proof.

By monotony of the Lebesgue spaces on the bounded domain
(0,1)
, it is sufficient to prove the result for
p=\infty
and
q=r=1
.
By
Lemma

2.1
,
\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}=\|D^{j-k}D^{k}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}\lesssim\|D^{m-k}D^{k}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}+\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}
, so it is sufficient to prove the result with
k=j
.
Hence, up to working with
D^{j}u
instead of
u
, it is sufficient to prove the result with
k=j=0
and
m\geq 1
. We will therefore prove

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\|u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}.

(2.6)

For
m=1
, this corresponds to
Lemma

2.2
.
Take
m>1
.
By
Lemma

2.2
, there exists
C>0
such that, for each
i=0,\dotsc,m-1
,

\|D^{i}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq C\left(\|D^{i+1}u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\|D^{i}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}\right).

(2.7)

Multiplying these inequalities by
C^{i}
and summing over
i
yields

\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}C^{i}\|D^{i}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq C\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}C^{i}\left(\|D^{i+1}u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\|D^{i}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}\right).

(2.8)

Thus, bounding the
L^{1}
norms by
L^{\infty}
and cancelling the terms on both sides,

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq C^{m}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}C^{i+1}\|D^{i}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}.

(2.9)

By
Lemma

2.1
, for each
i=0,\dotsc,m-1
,
\|D^{i}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}+\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}
, which concludes the proof of (
2.6
).
∎

2.2
Sobolev inequality involving a product of derivatives

In this paragraph, we prove the following localized additive version of (
1.7
), by induction on the length of the product and a compactness argument.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
0\leq k_{1}\leq\dotsb\leq k_{\kappa}\leq j<m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\omega\subset(0,1)
be a non-empty open interval.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
,

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}+\|D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{q}(\omega)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}.

(2.10)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, up to working with
D^{k_{1}}u
, one can assume that
k_{1}=0
.
When
m=1
,
k_{1}=\dotsb=k_{\kappa}=j=0
, so that the statement follows from
Lemma

2.3
.
Hence, one can assume that
m\geq 2
.
By monotony of the Lebesgue spaces on bounded domains, it is sufficient to prove the result with
p=\infty
and
q=r=1
.
By
Lemma

2.3
,
\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}
.
Hence, it is sufficient to prove the result with
p=1
.

We proceed by induction on
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
.
The case
\kappa=1
corresponds to
Lemma

2.3
.
Let
\kappa>1
.
Assume by contradiction that the lemma holds for products of up to
\kappa-1
terms, but not for
\kappa
terms.
One could therefore find a sequence
u_{n}\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
such that

\|D^{j}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}>n\left(\|D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\|u_{n}D^{k_{2}}u_{n}\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\right).

(2.11)

In particular,
\|D^{j}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}>0
.
Since (
2.10
) is linear in
u
, up to a rescaling, one can assume that

\|D^{j}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\|u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}=1.

(2.12)

By (
2.11
), this entails that
u_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{m,1}(0,1)
.
Hence, by the Rellich–Kondrachov compact embedding theorem, there exists
\bar{u}\in W^{m-1,1}(0,1)
such that
u_{n}\to\bar{u}
strongly in
W^{m-1,1}(0,1)
.
Since the sequence converges strongly in
W^{j,1}(0,1)
, the normalization (
2.12
) implies

\|D^{j}\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+\|\bar{u}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}=1

(2.13)

which ensures that
\bar{u}\neq 0
.
By Morrey’s inequality, since
m-1\geq 1
,
\bar{u}\in C^{0}([0,1])
and
u_{n}\to u
in
C^{0}([0,1])
.
Thus, since
\bar{u}\neq 0
, there exists a small non-empty open interval
\omega^{\prime}\subset(0,1)
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that, for
n
large enough
|u_{n}|\geq\delta
on
\omega^{\prime}
.
Hence,

\|u_{n}D^{k_{2}}u_{n}\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\omega)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\geq\delta^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\|D^{k_{2}}u_{n}\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\omega^{\prime})}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}.

(2.14)

Moreover, since
u_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{m,1}(0,1)
, the
D^{k_{i}}u_{n}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(0,1)
by
Lemma

2.1
.
Hence there exists
0<c\leq 1
such that

\delta^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\|D^{k_{2}}u_{n}\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\omega^{\prime})}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\\
\geq c\|D^{k_{2}}u_{n}\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\omega^{\prime})}^{\frac{1}{\kappa-1}}.

(2.15)

Hence, substituting in (
2.11
), and applying the induction hypothesis, there exists
C>0
such that,

\begin{split}\|D^{j}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}&>n\left(\|D^{m}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}+c\|D^{k_{2}}u_{n}\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(\omega^{\prime})}^{\frac{1}{\kappa-1}}\right)\\
&\geq\frac{nc}{C}\|D^{j}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)},\end{split}

(2.16)

which yields a contradiction for
n
large enough since
\|D^{j}u_{n}\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}>0
.
∎

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
0\leq k_{1}\leq\dotsb\leq k_{\kappa}\leq j<m\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
and
I\subset(0,1)
a non-empty interval of length
\ell
,

\ell^{j-\frac{1}{p}}\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(I)}\lesssim\ell^{m-\frac{1}{r}}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(I)}+\ell^{\bar{k}-\frac{1}{q\kappa}}\|D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{q}(I)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}},

(2.17)

where
\bar{k}:=(k_{1}+\dotsb+k_{\kappa})/\kappa
.

Proof.

This is a straightforward consequence of
Proposition

2.4
by a scaling argument.
Indeed, write
I=(x_{0},x_{0}+\ell)
for some
x_{0}\in[0,1)
.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
, let
v\in C^{\infty}([0,1],\mathbb{R})
defined by
v(t):=v(x_{0}+x\ell)
, so that (
2.17
) follows from (
2.10
) with the same constant.
∎

2.3
Nowhere-polynomial functions

In this paragraph, we introduce a notion of “nowhere-polynomial” function, as well as an approximation result by this subclass of smooth functions.
Our motivation is that we wish to interpret the pointwise product
D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u
as playing the role of the low-order term in the interpolation inequality and thus avoid that it vanishes on significant portions of the support of
u
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
I
be a (closed or open) non-empty interval of
\mathbb{R}
.
We say that
u\in C^{\infty}(I;\mathbb{R})
is
nowhere-polynomial
when

\mu\Big(\{u\neq 0\}\cap\left(\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\{D^{i}u=0\}\right)\Big)=0,

(2.18)

where
\mu
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
(0,1)
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
I\subset(0,1)
be a non-empty open interval with
\bar{I}\subset(0,1)
.
There exists a nowhere-polynomial
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that
\psi>0
on
I
.

Proof.

Let
\chi(t):=e^{-\frac{1}{t(1-t)}}
for
t\in(0,1)
, extended by
0
on
\mathbb{R}
.
It is classical that
\chi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
\operatorname{supp}\chi=[0,1]
,
\chi>0
on
(0,1)
and that, for every
i\geq 1
,
D^{i}\chi(t)=R_{i}(t)\chi(t)
where
R_{i}
is a (non-zero) rational function.
In particular,
R_{i}
vanishes at most a finite number of times on
[0,1]
.
Thus
(0,1)\cap\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\{D^{i}\chi=0\}
is countable, so of zero Lebesgue measure.
Given
I=(a,b)
with
0<a<b<1
,
\psi(t):=\chi((t-a)/(b-a))
satisfies the conclusions of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
.
There exist nowhere-polynomial functions
u_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
such that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{n}\to u
in
C^{k}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
.
Let
0<a<b<1
such that
\overline{\{u\neq 0\}}\subset(a,b)
.
Let
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
be a nowhere-polynomial function given by
Lemma

2.7
such that
\psi>0
on
(a,b)
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, set
u_{\varepsilon}:=u+\varepsilon\psi
.
As
\varepsilon\to 0
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
C^{k}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
.
We claim that there exists a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
such that the
u_{\varepsilon_{n}}
are nowhere-polynomial.

Otherwise, by contradiction, one could find
\varepsilon^{*}>0
such that, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*})
,
u_{\varepsilon}
is not nowhere-polynomial.
Hence

J_{\varepsilon}:=\{u_{\varepsilon}\neq 0\}\cap\left(\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\{D^{i}u_{\varepsilon}=0\}\right)\subset(a,b)

(2.19)

satisfies
\mu(J_{\varepsilon})>0
.
Let
J^{i}_{\varepsilon}:=\{D^{i}u_{\varepsilon}=0\}\cap(a,b)
.
Since
\mu(J_{\varepsilon})>0
, there exists
i_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that
\mu(J^{i_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon})>0
.
Hence,
(0,\varepsilon^{*})=\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}M_{i}
, where

M_{i}:=\{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*});\mu(J^{i}_{\varepsilon})>0\}.

(2.20)

Let
i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Let
\varepsilon\neq\varepsilon^{\prime}\in(0,\varepsilon^{*})
.
Since
J_{\varepsilon}^{i}\cap J^{i}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}}\subset\{D^{i}\psi=0\}\cap\{\psi>0\}
and
\psi
is nowhere-polynomial, one has
\mu(J^{i}_{\varepsilon}\cap J^{i}_{\varepsilon^{\prime}})=0
.
Hence, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
\{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon^{*});\mu(J^{i}_{\varepsilon})\geq 1/n\}
is finite.
Thus
M_{i}
is a countable union of finite sets, so is countable.
Hence
\cup_{i\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}M_{i}=(0,\varepsilon^{*})
is also countable, which contradicts the fact that
\mathbb{R}
is not countable.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
.
There exist nowhere-polynomial functions
u_{n}\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
such that, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
u_{n}\to u
in
C^{k}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
\bar{u}\in C^{\infty}_{c}((-1,2);\mathbb{R})
be a smooth compactly supported extension of
u
.
We apply
Lemma

2.8
to a rescaled version of
\bar{u}
to obtain a sequence
\bar{u}_{n}\in C^{\infty}_{c}((-1,2);\mathbb{R})
of nowhere-polynomial functions such that
\bar{u}_{n}\to\bar{u}
in
C^{k}([-1,2];\mathbb{R})
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then the sequence of restrictions
u_{n}:=(\bar{u}_{n})_{\rvert[0,1]}
satisfies the claimed properties.
∎

2.4
Subdivision argument

In this paragraph, we prove that, given a nowhere-polynomial function, we can find a subdivision of its support such that, on each interval, both terms of the right-hand side of (
2.17
) are equal.
The proof is inspired by
[
7
, Lemma 3.3]
and relies on the following version of Besicovitch’s covering theorem
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.10

(Besicovitch)
.

Let
E
be a bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}
and
r:E\to(0,+\infty)
.
For
x\in E
, consider the non-empty open interval
I_{x}:=(x-r_{x},x+r_{x})
.
There exists a countable (finite or countably infinite) collection of points
x_{n}\in E
such that
E\subset\cup_{n}I_{x_{n}}
and
\sum_{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{x_{n}}}\leq 4
on
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

This statement corresponds to the one-dimensional case of
[
6
, Theorem 18.1c, Chapter 2]
(see also
[
6
, Section 18, Chapter 2]
).
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
q,r\in[1,\infty]
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
0\leq k_{1}\leq\dotsb\leq k_{\kappa}<m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Assume that
\bar{k}<m-1
where
\bar{k}:=(k_{1}+\dotsb+k_{\kappa})/\kappa
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
be a nowhere-polynomial function.
Then there exists a countable family
(I_{n})_{n}
of non-empty open intervals
I_{n}\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle 1\leq\sum_{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}}\quad\mu\text{ a.e.\ on }\{u\neq 0\},

(2.21)

\displaystyle\sum\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}}\leq 4\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R},

(2.22)

and, for every
n
, denoting by
\ell_{n}
the length of
I_{n}
,

\ell_{n}^{m-\frac{1}{r}}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(I_{n})}=\ell_{n}^{\bar{k}-\frac{1}{q\kappa}}\|D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{q}(I_{n})}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}.

(2.23)

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
be nowhere-polynomial and

v:=D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u
(we implicitly consider their respective smooth extensions by
0
outside of
(0,1)
).
Let

E:=\left\{x\in(0,1);u(x)\neq 0\text{ and }v(x)\neq 0\right\}.

(2.24)

For
x\in E
and
h>0
, we define

\displaystyle\alpha_{x}(h)

\displaystyle:=h^{\bar{k}-\frac{1}{q\kappa}}\|v\|^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}_{L^{q}(x-h,x+h)},

(2.25)

\displaystyle\beta_{x}(h)

\displaystyle:=h^{m-\frac{1}{r}}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(x-h,x+h)}.

(2.26)

As
h\to 0
,
\alpha_{x}(h)\sim|v(x)|^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}2^{\frac{1}{q\kappa}}h^{\bar{k}}
and
\beta_{x}(h)\leq h^{m}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}
.
Thus, since
m>\bar{k}
,
\beta_{x}(h)<\alpha_{x}(h)
for
h
small enough.
Conversely, for
h\geq 1
,
\alpha_{x}(h)=h^{\bar{k}-\frac{1}{q\kappa}}\|v\|^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}_{L^{q}(0,1)}
and
\beta_{x}(h)=h^{m-\frac{1}{r}}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}
.
Since
m>\bar{k}+1
,
m-\frac{1}{r}>\bar{k}-\frac{1}{q\kappa}
and thus
\beta_{x}(h)>\alpha_{x}(h)
for
h
large enough.
Hence, we can define

r_{x}:=\inf\{h>0;\alpha_{x}(h)\leq\beta_{x}(h)\}\in(0,+\infty).

(2.27)

In particular, for every
x\in E
,
\alpha_{x}(r_{x})=\beta_{x}(r_{x})
.
By
Lemma

2.10
, there exists a countable collection of elements
x_{n}\in E
such that
E\subset\cup_{n}I_{n}
and
\sum_{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}}\leq 4
on
\mathbb{R}
, where
I_{n}=(x_{n}-r_{x_{n}},x_{n}+r_{x_{n}})
.
These intervals satisfy (
2.23
) by the definition of
r_{x}
.
Moreover, since
1\leq\sum_{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}}
on
E
, writing

\{u\neq 0\}=\left(\{u\neq 0\}\cap\{v=0\}\right)\cup E

(2.28)

and using the fact that
u
is nowhere-polynomial, we obtain that
1\leq\sum_{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}}
almost everywhere on
\{u\neq 0\}
, which proves (
2.21
).
∎

2.5
Proof of the main result

We start with a classical result from measure theory.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
and
j\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
,

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}^{p}=\int_{0}^{1}|D^{j}u|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{u\neq 0}.

(2.29)

Proof.

We write

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}^{p}=\int_{0}^{1}|D^{j}u|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{u\neq 0}+\int_{0}^{1}|D^{j}u|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{u=0}\mathbf{1}_{D^{j}u\neq 0}.

(2.30)

Thus, it is sufficient to prove that
E:=\{u=0\}\cap\{D^{j}u\neq 0\}
is of zero Lebesgue measure.
Let us show that
E
is a discrete subset of
[0,1]
.
Let
x\in E
.
Then
u(x)=0
.
Let
1\leq i\leq j
be the smallest integer such that
D^{i}u(x)\neq 0
.
For
h
small enough
u(x+h)=D^{i}u(x)h^{i}/i!+O(h^{i+1})
.
In particular, there exists
h
small enough such that
u(x+h)=0
if and only if
h=0
.
Thus
x
is isolated in
E
.
Hence
E
is discrete and
\mu(E)=0
, which concludes the proof.
∎

We now prove
Theorem

2
.
Since estimate (
1.7
) is invariant under translation and rescalings, one can assume that
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
.
We start with the most important case:
\theta=\theta^{*}
.
We postpone the generalization to
\theta\in(\theta^{*},1]
to the end of this section.

Proof in the critical case
\theta=\theta^{*}
.

Assume moreover, temporarily, that
\bar{k}<m-1
and
p,q,r<\infty
.
Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1);\mathbb{R})
.
As a first step, assume that
u
is nowhere-polynomial.
Let
v:=D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u
.
Let
(I_{n})_{n}
be a countable collection of non-empty open intervals such as in
Proposition

2.11
.
First, using
Lemma

2.12
and (
2.21
),

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}^{p}=\int_{0}^{1}|D^{j}u|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{u\neq 0}\leq\sum_{n}\int_{0}^{1}|D^{j}u|^{p}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}}

(2.31)

Second, using
Corollary

2.5
and (
2.23
), there exists
C>0
(independent of
u
) such that, for each
n
,

\begin{split}\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(I_{n})}^{p}&\leq C^{p}\ell_{n}^{1-pj}\left(\ell_{n}^{m-\frac{1}{r}}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(I_{n})}+\ell_{n}^{\bar{k}-\frac{1}{q\kappa}}\|v\|_{L^{q}(I_{n})}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\right)^{p}\\
&=C^{p}2^{p}\ell_{n}^{1-pj}\left(\ell_{n}^{m-\frac{1}{r}}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(I_{n})}\right)^{\theta p}\left(\ell_{n}^{\bar{k}-\frac{1}{q\kappa}}\|v\|_{L^{q}(I_{n})}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\right)^{(1-\theta)p}\\
&=(2C)^{p}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(I_{n})}^{\theta p}\|v\|_{L^{q}(I_{n})}^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{\kappa}}\end{split}

(2.32)

since the parameters are related by (
1.6
).
Since
\theta=\theta^{*}
, the relation (
1.8
) of

1.6
implies that the exponents
\alpha=\frac{r}{\theta p}
and
\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{q\kappa}{p(1-\theta)}
satisfy

.
Thus, by Hölder’s inequality,

\begin{split}\sum_{n}\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(I_{n})}^{p}&\leq(2C)^{p}\sum_{n}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(I_{n})}^{\theta p}\|v\|_{L^{q}(I_{n})}^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{\kappa}}\\
&\leq(2C)^{p}\left(\sum_{n}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(I_{n})}^{r}\right)^{\frac{\theta p}{r}}\left(\sum_{n}\|v\|_{L^{q}(I_{n})}^{q}\right)^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{q\kappa}}\\
&\leq(2C)^{p}4^{\frac{\theta p}{r}}4^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{q\kappa}}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}^{\theta p}\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{\frac{p(1-\theta)}{\kappa}}\end{split}

(2.33)

using (
2.22
).
Substituting this estimate in (
2.31
) proves (
1.7
).

If
u
is not nowhere-polynomial, then one applies (
1.7
) to the approximation sequence
u_{n}
of nowhere-polynomial functions given by
Lemma

2.8
.
Since
u_{n}\to u
in
C^{m}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
, the estimate passes to the limit.

When
q=\infty
or
r=\infty
, it suffices to replace the Hölder estimate in (

) involving a sum by the appropriate supremum over
n
.

When
p=\infty
, one writes

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}=\sup_{n}\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{n})},

(2.34)

where, similarly, using
Corollary

2.5
and (
2.23
), there exists
C>0
(independent of
u
) such that,

\begin{split}\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(I_{n})}&\leq C\ell_{n}^{-j}\left(\ell_{n}^{m-\frac{1}{r}}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(I_{n})}+\ell_{n}^{\bar{k}-\frac{1}{q\kappa}}\|v\|_{L^{q}(I_{n})}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\right)\\
&=C\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(I_{n})}^{\theta}\|v\|_{L^{q}(I_{n})}^{\frac{(1-\theta)}{\kappa}}.\end{split}

(2.35)

Eventually, when
\bar{k}=m-1
, the assumption
k_{i}\leq j<m
entail that
k_{1}=\dotsb=k_{\kappa}=j=m-1
.
Hence
\theta^{*}=0
and

by (

).
Thus (
1.7
) reduces to
\|D^{m-1}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{m-1}u\dotsb D^{m-1}u\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}=\|D^{m-1}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}
.

Proof in the case
\theta\in(\theta^{*},1]
.

When
\theta=1
, this simply corresponds to the embedding

for compactly supported functions.
Now let
\theta\in(\theta^{*},1)
and define

by

\frac{1}{p^{*}}=\frac{\theta^{*}}{r}+\frac{1-\theta^{*}}{q\kappa}.

(2.36)

Thanks to the critical case
\theta=\theta^{*}
, we know that

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p^{*}}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}^{\theta^{*}}\|v\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{\frac{1-\theta^{*}}{\kappa}}.

(2.37)

Define

by

\alpha:=\frac{\theta-\theta^{*}}{1-\theta^{*}}.

(2.38)

We apply the usual Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality of

1
to obtain

(2.39)

Combining (

) and (
2.39
) proves (

).

1
.

\begin{split}\alpha&\left(\frac{1}{r}-m\right)+(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{1}{p^{*}}-j\right)-\left(\frac{1}{p}-j\right)\\

since

).

3
The case of bounded domains

In this paragraph, we consider the case

, but not necessarily compactly supported in

The proofs rely on the distinction between two cases, depending on whether

3.1
A slight extension of the usual inequality

We prove
Remark

1.4
.
Estimate (
1.4
) is classical when
k_{0}=k
(it follows by applying the usual inequality to
D^{k}u
, see e.g.
[
17
, item 5, p. 126]

We build upon this case to give a short proof when
0\leq k_{0}<k\leq j
.
Up to working with
D^{k_{0}}u
, it is sufficient to treat the case
k_{0}=0
.

Case
0<k<j
.

Define
\alpha^{*}\in(0,1)
and
p_{\alpha}^{*}\in[1,\infty]
by

\alpha^{*}:=\frac{k}{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{1}{p_{\alpha}^{*}}:=\frac{\alpha^{*}}{p}+\frac{1-\alpha^{*}}{s}.

(3.1)

By
Remark

1.4
(in the classical case
k_{0}=k
), one has both

\displaystyle\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}^{\theta}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{1-\theta}+C_{1}\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{p_{\alpha}^{*}}(0,1)},

\displaystyle\|D^{k}u\|_{L^{p_{\alpha}^{*}}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}^{\alpha^{*}}\|u\|_{L^{s}(0,1)}^{1-\alpha^{*}}+C_{2}\|u\|_{L^{s}(0,1)}.

By Young’s inequality for products, for

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}^{\alpha^{*}}\|u\|_{L^{s}(0,1)}^{1-\alpha^{*}}\leq\alpha^{*}\varepsilon\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}+(1-\alpha^{*})\varepsilon^{-\frac{\alpha^{*}}{1-\alpha^{*}}}\|u\|_{L^{s}(0,1)}.

Choosing

and combining the three estimates proves (

).

Low-frequency case when
k=j
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})

Assume that

\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}\leq\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}.

By

1.4
(in the classical case

\displaystyle\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}^{\theta}\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{1-\theta}+\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p_{\beta}^{*}}(0,1)},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p_{\beta}^{*}}(0,1)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}^{\beta^{*}}\|u\|_{L^{s}(0,1)}^{1-\beta^{*}}+\|u\|_{L^{s}(0,1)}.

(3.8)

Combining both estimates with assumption (

) and using Young’s inequality as above proves (

).

High-frequency case when

.

Let

.

1.4

).

3.2
Proof of the main result for bounded domains

.
We start with the following modification of

2.11
(which removes the compact support assumption).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
q,r\in[1,\infty]
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
0\leq k_{1}\leq\dotsb\leq k_{\kappa}<m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\bar{k}:=(k_{1}+\dotsb+k_{\kappa})/\kappa

Let

\|D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\leq\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}.

(3.12)

There exists a countable family
(I_{n})_{n}
of non-empty open intervals
I_{n}\subset(0,1)
satisfying (
2.21
), (
2.22
) on
[0,1]
and (
2.23
).

Proof.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
be nowhere-polynomial and

v:=D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u
.
Let
E
as in (
2.24
).
For
x\in E
and
h>0
, we define

\displaystyle\alpha_{x}(h)

\displaystyle:=|J_{x}(h)|^{\bar{k}-\frac{1}{q\kappa}}\|v\|^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}_{L^{q}(J_{x}(h))},

(3.13)

\displaystyle\beta_{x}(h)

\displaystyle:=|J_{x}(h))|^{m-\frac{1}{r}}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(J_{x}(h))},

(3.14)

where
J_{x}(h):=(x-h,x+h)\cap(0,1)
.
Since
x\in(0,1)
, for
h
small enough
J_{x}(h)=(x-h,x+h)
and
|J_{x}(h)|=2h
.
As
h\to 0
,
\alpha_{x}(h)\sim|v(x)|^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}2^{\frac{1}{q\kappa}}h^{\bar{k}}
and
\beta_{x}(h)\leq h^{m}\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}
.
Thus, since
m>\bar{k}
,
\beta_{x}(h)<\alpha_{x}(h)
for
h
small enough.
Conversely, for
h\geq\max\{x,1-x\}
,
J_{x}(h)=(0,1)
and
\alpha_{x}(h)=\|v\|^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}_{L^{q}(0,1)}
and
\beta_{x}(h)=\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}
.
Thus, by (
3.12
),
\alpha_{h}(x)\leq\beta_{h}(x)
.
Hence, for every
x\in E
, we can define
r_{x}\in(0,\infty)
as in (
2.27
), which satisfies
\alpha_{x}(r_{x})=\beta_{x}(r_{x})
.

By
Lemma

2.10
, there exists a countable collection of elements
x_{n}\in E
such that
E\subset\cup_{n}I_{n}^{\prime}
and
\sum_{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}^{\prime}}\leq 4
on
\mathbb{R}
, where
I_{n}^{\prime}=(x_{n}-r_{x_{n}},x_{n}+r_{x_{n}})
.
Let

.
The intervals
I_{n}
satisfy (
2.23
) by the definitions of
r_{x}
and of
J_{x}(r_{x})
.
Moreover, since
1\leq\sum_{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}^{\prime}}
on
E
, writing

\{u\neq 0\}\cap(0,1)=\left(\{u\neq 0\}\cap\{v=0\}\cap(0,1)\right)\cup E

(3.15)

and using the fact that
u
is nowhere-polynomial, we obtain that
1\leq\sum_{n}\mathbf{1}_{I_{n}}
almost everywhere on

, which proves (
2.21
).
∎

In the situation where
D^{m}u
is small compared with
D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u
, the construction of the family of intervals of the previous lemma fails.
We will rely on the following estimate instead.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
,
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and

0\leq k_{1}\leq\dotsb\leq k_{\kappa}\leq j<m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\bar{k}:=(k_{1}+\dotsb+k_{\kappa})/\kappa
.
Let
0\leq k_{0}\leq k_{1}
and
s\in[1,\infty]
.
For
u\in C^{\infty}([0,1];\mathbb{R})
such that

\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(0,1)}\leq 2\|D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}},

(3.16)

there holds,

\displaystyle\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{k_{0}}u\|_{L^{s}(0,1)}.

(3.17)

Proof.

By monotony of the Lebesgue spaces on the bounded domain
(0,1)
, it is sufficient to prove the result for
p=\infty
and
s=1
.
Moreover, since
0\leq k_{0}\leq k_{1}\leq\dotsb\leq j<m
, up to working with
D^{k_{0}}u
instead of
u
, one can assume that
k_{0}=0
.
By the usual Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality on bounded domains of

1.4
(with
k=k_{0}=0
),

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{m}u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{r}(0,1)}\|u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}^{1-\alpha}+\|u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}

(3.18)

with
\alpha:=\frac{j+1}{m+1+\frac{1}{r}}\geq\frac{j}{m}
since
j<m
and
r\geq 1
.
Moreover, for each
0\leq k_{i}\leq j

\|D^{k_{i}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}+\|u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)},

(3.19)

which is immediate when
k_{i}=j
and follows from the usual Sobolev embedding
Lemma

when

.
Thus, by Hölder’s inequality,

\begin{split}\|D^{k_{1}}u\dotsb D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{q}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}&\leq\|D^{k_{1}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\dotsb\|D^{k_{\kappa}}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\\
&\lesssim\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}+\|u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}.\end{split}

(3.20)

Using (

) in (

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\lesssim\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}^{\alpha}\|u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}^{1-\alpha}+\|u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)}.

(3.21)

Since
j<m
,
\alpha<1
and Young’s weighted inequality for products entails that

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{1}(0,1)},

(3.22)

which is indeed (
3.17
) with
p=\infty
,
s=1
and
k_{0}=0
.
∎

.

4
An application to control theory

Our initial motivation concerns obstructions to small-time local controllability for nonlinear finite-dimensional scalar-input control-affine systems.
It is known that such obstructions are linked with interpolation inequalities (see
[
1
]
).
As an example, given
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, consider the following system on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
:

\begin{cases}\dot{x}_{1}=w\\
\dot{x}_{2}=x_{1}\\
\dot{x}_{3}=x_{2}\\
\dot{x}_{4}=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}x_{3}^{2}-x_{1}^{p}\end{cases}

(4.1)

with initial condition
x(0)=0
where
w\in L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
is the control to be chosen.
We are interested in the following local property.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that system (
4.1
) is
small-time locally controllable
when, for every
T,\eta>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for every
x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
with
|x^{*}|\leq\delta
, there exists
w\in L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
such that
\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq\eta
and the associated solution to (
4.1
) with initial condition
x(0)=0
satisfies
x(T)=x^{*}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

System (
4.1
) is small-time locally controllable if and only if
p\in\{3,5,7,8,9,10,11\}
.

Proof.

Let
T>0
.
If
w\in L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
is a control such that
x_{1}(T)=x_{2}(T)=x_{3}(T)=0
, then
u:=x_{3}\in W^{3,\infty}_{0}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
and

x_{4}(T)=\int_{0}^{T}(uu^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime})^{2}-\int_{0}^{T}(u^{\prime\prime})^{p}

(4.2)

so that the possibility to reach a target of the form
(0,0,0,\pm 1)
is linked with functional inequalities involving products of derivatives.
We study each case.

•

Case
p\geq 12
.
First,

\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}^{p}\leq\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{p-12}\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{12}(0,T)}^{12}

(4.3)

Moreover, since
u^{\prime\prime\prime}=x_{3}^{\prime\prime\prime}=w
and
u^{\prime\prime}(0)=x_{1}(0)=0
,

\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}\leq T\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}.

(4.4)

Thus, thanks to the interpolation inequality of
Corollary

1.9
,

\|u^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}(0,T)}^{p}\leq T^{p-12}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{p-6}\int_{0}^{T}(uu^{\prime}u^{\prime\prime})^{2}.

(4.5)

Substituting in (
4.2
) proves that
x_{4}(T)\geq 0
when
T^{p-12}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T)}^{p-6}\leq 1
.
Thus, choosing
0<\eta\ll 1
such that
T^{p-12}\eta^{p-6}\leq 1
negates the definition of small-time local controllability.

•

Case
7\leq p\leq 11
.
Let
0\neq\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
and consider
w(t):=\varepsilon\chi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)
for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.
As
\varepsilon\to 0
,
w\to 0
in
L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
.
Moreover, by (
4.2
),

x_{4}(T)=\varepsilon^{6}\int_{0}^{T}(\chi\chi^{\prime}\chi^{\prime\prime})^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{7}).

(4.6)

So one can move in the direction
(0,0,0,+1)
.

Conversely, set
u(t)=\varepsilon^{1+3a}\chi(t\varepsilon^{-a})
or equivalently
w(t):=\varepsilon\chi^{\prime\prime\prime}(t\varepsilon^{-a})
for
a>0
and
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
.
As
\varepsilon\to 0
,
w\to 0
in
L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathbb{R})
.
By (
4.2
),

x_{4}(T)=\varepsilon^{7+12a}\int_{0}^{T}(\chi\chi^{\prime}\chi^{\prime\prime})^{2}-\varepsilon^{p(1+a)+1}\int_{0}^{T}(\chi^{\prime\prime})^{p}.

(4.7)

If
\int_{0}^{T}(\chi^{\prime\prime})^{p}>0
and
p(1+a)<6+12a
(which is possible when
7\leq p\leq 11
), one can move in the direction
(0,0,0,-1)
.

From these elementary movements, it is classical to conclude that (
4.1
) is small-time locally controllable.

•

Case
p=1
.
Then
\dot{x}_{2}+\dot{x}_{4}=(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3})^{2}\geq 0
.
Hence, for every control,
(x_{2}+x_{4})(T)\geq 0
so targets with
x_{2}^{*}+x_{4}^{*}<0
are not reachable.

•

Case
p\in\{2,4,6\}
.
The system does not satisfy Stefani’s necessary condition for small-time local controllability (see
[
20
]
).

•

Case
p\in\{3,5\}
.
The system satisfies Hermes’ sufficient condition for small-time local controllability (see
[
10
]
).∎

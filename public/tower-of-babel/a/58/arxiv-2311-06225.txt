Optimal regularity in time and space for nonlocal porous medium type equations
Benjamin Gess, Jonas Sauer

Abstract
. A broad class of possibly non-unique generalized kinetic solutions to hyperbolic-parabolic PDEs is introduced. Optimal regularity estimates in time and space for such solutions to nonlocal, and spatially inhomogeneous variants of the porous medium equation are shown in the scale of Sobolev spaces. The optimality of these results is shown by comparison to the non-local Barenblatt solution. The regularity results are used in order to obtain existence of generalized kinetic solutions.

1.
Introduction

We prove optimal estimates on the time and space regularity in Sobolev spaces of solutions to equations of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+L\Phi(u)&=S&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

where
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
S\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
a\in(0,2)
, and
L
is a linear integro-differential operator given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle Lu(x):=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big(u(x+y)-u(x)-\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)\mathsf{1}_{|y|\leqslant 2}(y)y\cdot\nabla u(x)\big)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

In the recent contributions
[
25
,
27
]
, the optimal regularity in space and time for solutions to local, homogeneous porous media equations has been obtained.
Some of the key ingredients of these works were kinetic solution theory, singular moments of entropy dissipation measures, and velocity averaging in terms of micro-local Fourier analysis.
In the present work, we extend this theory in several directions:

(1) We treat non-local, degenerate PDEs, such as the non-local porous medium equation.

(2) We include spatially inhomogeneous degenerate diffusions.

(3) The previous works
[
25
,
27
]
relied on the uniqueness of solutions in order to derive singular moment estimates on the entropy dissipation measures. In this work, we introduce a new modification of the approach which completely avoids this.
As a consequence, the regularity statements apply to a large class of – possibly non-unique – solutions.

Notably, the regularity estimates obtained in the present work depend on
u_{0}
only via its
L^{1}
norm. In particular, they immediately generalize to measure-valued initial data.

We will always work under the following assumption.

Assumption 1.1
.

(i)

The kernel
k(x,y):\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})\to(0,\infty)
is
smooth in
x
and
y
, and such that for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
there are
C_{\alpha,\beta}>0
and
c>0
with

(1.3)

\displaystyle|\partial_{y}^{\beta}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leqslant C|y|^{-d-a-|\beta|},

\displaystyle\forall|y|>0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle k(x,y)

\displaystyle\geqslant c|y|^{-d-a},

\displaystyle\forall|y|>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Moreover, we assume
k(x,y)=k(x+y,-y)
, and in the case
a=1
also
k(x,y)=k(x,-y)
.

(ii)

For the nonlinearity we suppose that
\Phi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap W^{2,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
with
\Phi^{\prime}\geqslant 0
,
and there are
C>0
and
m\in(1,\infty)
such that for all
\delta>0

(1.5)

\displaystyle|\{\Phi^{\prime}(v)\leqslant\delta\}|

\displaystyle\leqslant C\delta^{\frac{1}{m-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\#\{\Phi^{\prime}(v)=\delta\}\leqslant C.

where we have written for short
\{\Phi^{\prime}(v)\leqslant\delta\}:=\{v\in\mathbb{R}\ \lvert\ \Phi^{\prime}(v)\leqslant\delta\}
.

The condition
k(x,y)=k(x+y,-y)
ensures that
L
is
L^{2}
-symmetric, and that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Lu(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=0
.

A prime example which satisfies these conditions is the case of a fractional porous medium equation,
i.e.

\Phi(v):=v^{[m]}:=|v|^{m-1}v
,
m>1
and
k(x,y)=c|y|^{-d-a}
, which corresponds to the fractional Laplacian
L=(-\Delta)^{\frac{a}{2}}
. The corresponding problem then takes the form

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+(-\Delta)^{\frac{a}{2}}u^{[m]}&=S&&\text{in }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0)&=u_{0}&&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{aligned}\right.

We discuss some further examples of admissible nonlinearities
\Phi
in Example
1.4
below.

The arguments of the present work rely on velocity averaging techniques and the kinetic formulation

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\chi+\Phi^{\prime}L_{x}\chi

\displaystyle=\partial_{v}q+S\delta_{v=u(t,x)},

for a non-negative measure
q
, where
\chi(u(t,x),v):=\mathsf{1}_{v<u(t,x)}-\mathsf{1}_{v<0}
. However, in contrast to
[
27
]
we can allow even for generalized kinetic solutions
f
which are not confined to the specific form of
\chi
, cf. Definition
5.4
below. Additionally, both of the key arguments, that is, the micro-localization and the treatment of the entropy dissipation measure proceed along different lines, which allows to significantly weaken the assumptions. In particular, we do not rely on singular moments of the measure
q
at all. Indeed, it will be sufficient to exploit that
q
is bounded in the velocity variable
v
, which we prove to be satisfied for all generalized kinetic solutions.

The non-locality and spatial inhomogeneity of the operator
L
, and the generality of the nonlinearity
\Phi
in (
1.1
) cause several challenges requiring new arguments, which we will comment on next:

(1) Due to the spatial inhomogeneity of (
1.1
), the established methods from
[
25
,
27
]
do not appear to be applicable, and a different line of arguments needs to be developed.
Indeed,
[
25
,
27
]
relied on the so-called truncation property for the kinetic operator
\Phi^{\prime}(v)L_{x}
in (
1.7
), which corresponds to a microlocal analysis relating the Fourier variable
\xi
with the velocity variable
v
.
However, in the setting of the present work, due to the spatial inhomogenity and non-locality of the operator
L
it is not possible anymore to directly link the velocity
v
to the Fourier variable
\xi
. Therefore, we develop a different approach to the microlocal analysis of the kinetic operator in Section
4
, which does not rely on a truncation property anymore.

(2) The
v
-dependency of the kinetic operator
\Phi^{\prime}(v)L_{x}
requires an extension and adaptation of the parametrix method for pseudodifferential operators providing precise bounds in terms of
v
(see Lemma
4.1
and
4.2
).

(3)
Since the parametrix does not provide an exact inverse, lower order terms emerge on the right-hand side of the estimates, which need to be controlled.
This is a delicate issue: Since we work in the broad class of (possibly non-unique) generalized kinetic solutions, only the properties contained in their definition can be used. This is different to previous work where the uniqueness of solutions was known, and, therefore, additional facts about solutions could be used. Moreover, the lower order terms contain the non-linearity
\Phi
, and, thus, are superlinear.
Therefore a careful interpolation and absorption argument is necessary to guarantee that these terms can be controlled for a large class of non-linearities (see Step
6
in the proof of Theorem
1.2
).

(4)
Generalizing the decomposition employed in
[
27
]
in terms of small and large velocities, we decompose in terms of level sets of
\Phi^{\prime}
, and we prove that this allows the treatment of more general nonlinearities than in
[
25
,
27
]
.

(5)
Finally, we identify a specific class of inhomogeneous Besov spaces of dominating mixed smoothness (see Lemma
2.3
) to be particularly well suited for the theory developed in this work. This allows to avoid the use of homogeneous spaces, which in
[
27
]
required a careful argument in order to return to standard inhomogeneous (Sobolev) spaces. The approach presented here is more direct and makes use of recent advances in interpolation theory based on wavelet descriptions
[
49
]
.
In the argument for the regularity in time, cf. Lemma
4.5
, interpolation theory is avoided altogether.

Our first main result is the following.

.

Let

,
S\in L^{1}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and let

and
\Phi

for some

,

. Let

be a generalized kinetic solution to (
1.1
) on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, cf. Definition
5.4
,
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{v})
such that
\eta\Phi^{\prime}f\in L^{1}_{t,x,v}
,

and
\eta^{\prime}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{v})
, and write
u^{\eta}(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v}}\eta(v)f(t,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}v
,

Let
p\in(1,m)
and define

\displaystyle\kappa_{t}:=\frac{m-p}{p}\frac{1}{m-1},\quad\kappa_{x}:=\frac{p-1}{p}\frac{a}{m-1}.

Then, for all
\sigma_{t}\in[0,\kappa_{t})

\displaystyle u^{\eta}\in W^{\sigma_{t},p}(0,T;W^{\sigma_{x},p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|u^{\eta}\|_{W^{\sigma_{t},p}(0,T;W^{\sigma_{x},p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{p}\lesssim(1+\|\eta^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}_{v}})(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x}}^{m}+\|S\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}^{m}+1)+\|\eta\Phi^{\prime}f\|_{L^{1}_{t,x,v}}.

Here, the implicit constant depends only on

,

,
m
,
p
, and the constants

,
C
and

from Assumption
1.1
with

If

for a.e.
(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{\sigma_{t},p}(0,T;W^{\sigma_{x},p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{p}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}_{x}}^{m+\varepsilon}+\|S\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}^{m+\varepsilon}+1,

where the implicit constant depends additionally on
\varepsilon

\sigma_{t}=0
and
\sigma_{x}\in(0,\frac{a}{m})
in case of

.

2.
Preliminaries, Notation and Function Spaces

We use the notation
a\lesssim b
if there is a universal constant
C>0
such that
a\leqslant Cb
. We introduce
a\gtrsim b
in a similar manner, and write
a\sim b
if
a\lesssim b
and
a\gtrsim b
.
For a Banach space
X
and
I\subset\mathbb{R}
we denote by
C(I;X)
the space of bounded and continuous
X
-valued functions endowed with the norm
\|f\|_{C(I;X)}:=\sup_{t\in I}\|f(t)\|_{X}
. If
X=\mathbb{R}
we write
C(I)
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, the space of
k
-times continuously differentiable functions is denoted by
C^{k}(I;X)
. The subspace of
C^{k}(I;X)
consisting of compactly supported functions is denoted by
C^{k}_{c}(I;X)
. Moreover, we write
{\mathcal{M}}_{TV}
for the space of all measures with finite total variation.
Throughout this article we use several types of
L^{p}
-based function spaces.
For a Banach space
X
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, we endow the Bochner-Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R};X)
with the usual norm

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};X)}

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lVert f(t)\rVert_{X}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

with the standard modification in the case of
p=\infty
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the corresponding
X
-valued Sobolev space is denoted by
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R};X)
. If
\sigma\in(0,\infty)
is non-integer (say
\sigma=k+r
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
r\in(0,1)
), then we define the
X
-valued Sobolev-Slobodeckiĭ space
W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R};X)
as the space of functions in
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R};X)
with

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lVert f\rVert_{\dot{W}^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R};X)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}\frac{\lVert D^{k}f(t)-D^{k}f(s)\rVert_{X}^{p}}{|t-s|^{rp+1}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty,

again with the usual modification in the case of
p=\infty
. Further, let
\dot{W}^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R};X)
be the space of all locally integrable
X
-valued functions
f
for which (
2.1
) is finite. If we factor out the equivalence relation
\sim
, where
f\sim g
if
\lVert f-g\rVert_{\dot{W}^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R};X)}=0
, the space
\dot{W}^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R};X)
equipped with the norm
\lVert\cdot\rVert_{\dot{W}^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{R};X)}
is a Banach space.

Moreover, in order to treat regularity results in both time and space efficiently, we introduce spaces with dominating mixed derivatives set in the framework of Fourier analysis, that is, corresponding Besov spaces. These spaces have a long history in the literature, beginning with the works of S. M. Nikol’skiĭ
[
38
,
39
,
40
]
. We refer the reader to the monograph of Schmeisser and Triebel
[
46
]
and the references therein. We adopt the notation of
[

]
for the non-homogeneous spaces.

Let
\varphi
be a smooth function supported in the annulus
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\frac{1}{2}\leqslant|\xi|\leqslant 2\}
and such that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi_{j}(\xi):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

Similarly, let
\eta
be a smooth function supported in
\left(-2,-\frac{1}{2}\right)\cup\left(\frac{1}{2},2\right)
with

\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta_{l}(\tau):=\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\eta(2^{-l}\tau)=1,\quad\forall\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

Moreover, define
\phi_{j}:=\varphi_{j}
for

and
\phi_{0}:=1-\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\phi_{j}
as well as
\psi_{l}:=\eta_{l}
for

and
\psi_{0}:=1-\sum_{l\geqslant 1}\eta_{l}
. We will use the shorthand notation

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\sigma_{i}\in\left(-\infty,\infty\right)
,

, and

. Set
\overline{\sigma}:=(\sigma_{t},\sigma_{x})

The non-homogeneous Besov space with dominating mixed derivatives

is given by

\displaystyle S^{\overline{\sigma}}_{p,q}B:=S^{\overline{\sigma}}_{p,q}B(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}):=\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})\ \lvert\ \|f\|_{S^{\overline{\sigma}}_{p,q}B}<\infty\},

with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{S^{\overline{\sigma}}_{p,q}B}

\displaystyle:=\left(\sum_{l,j\geqslant 0}2^{\sigma_{t}lq}2^{\sigma_{x}jq}\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{t,x}\psi_{l}\phi_{j}{\mathcal{F}}_{t,x}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad q<\infty,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{S^{\overline{\sigma}}_{p,\infty}B}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{l,j\geqslant 0}2^{\sigma_{t}l}2^{\sigma_{x}j}\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}_{t,x}\psi_{l}\phi_{j}{\mathcal{F}}_{t,x}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}.

Remark 2.2

The spaces

.

We will now introduce symbol classes for pseudodifferential operators. We will use the notation
\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
as well as
\,\textrm{\dj}(\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\mathrm{d}\xi
and
\,\textrm{\dj}(\tau,\xi):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d+1}}\,\mathrm{d}(\tau,\xi)
.

Definition 2.4
.

(i)

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\textsf{m}=(m_{\tau},m_{\xi})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
We say that
p:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{C}
belongs to
S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
if
p=p(x,\tau,\xi)
is smooth in
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
there holds

\displaystyle\|p\|_{S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0},N}:=\sup\langle\tau\rangle^{-m_{\tau}+\beta_{\tau}}\langle\xi\rangle^{-m_{\xi}+|\beta_{\xi}|}|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\tau}^{\beta_{\tau}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta_{\xi}}p(x,\tau,\xi)|<\infty.

where the supremum runs over all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
and all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
,
(\beta_{\tau},\beta_{\xi})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d+1}
with
|\alpha|,|\beta_{\tau}|,|\beta_{\xi}|\leqslant N
.
For
p\in S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
and
l,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we write
p_{lj}(x,\tau,\xi):=\psi_{l}(\tau)\phi_{j}(\xi)p(x,\tau,\xi)
.

(ii)

For
p\in S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
and
u\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
we introduce

\displaystyle p(x,D_{t,x})u(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}e^{i(t-s)\tau+i(x-y)\cdot\xi}p(x,\tau,\xi)u(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}(s,y)\,\textrm{\dj}(\tau,\xi),

\displaystyle p(D_{t,x},x)u(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}e^{i(t-s)\tau+i(x-y)\cdot\xi}p(y,\tau,\xi)u(s,y)\,\mathrm{d}(s,y)\,\textrm{\dj}(\tau,\xi).

We call
p(x,D_{t,x})
a pseudodifferential operator of order
m
in
x
-form, and
p(D_{t,x},x)
a pseudodifferential operator of order
m
in
y
-form.
If
p\in S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
does not depend on
\tau
, we also write
p(x,D_{x})
and
p(D_{x},x)
, respectively.

Equipped with the semi-norms
\|p\|_{S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0},N}
, the space
S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
turns into a Fréchet space.
Observe that for
p\in S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
and
l,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have

\displaystyle p_{lj}(x,D_{t,x})

\displaystyle=p(x,D_{t,x})\psi_{l}(D_{t})\phi_{j}(D_{x})=\psi_{l}(D_{t})p(x,D_{t,x})\phi_{j}(D_{x}),

\displaystyle p_{lj}(D_{t,x},x)

\displaystyle=\psi_{l}(D_{t})\phi_{j}(D_{x})p(D_{t,x},x)=\phi_{j}(D_{x})p(D_{t,x},x)\psi_{l}(D_{t}).

We associate to
p\in S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
the symbols

\displaystyle p_{L}(x,\tau,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-iy\cdot\eta}p(x+y,\tau,\xi+\eta)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{\dj}\eta,

\displaystyle p_{R}(x,\tau,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{iy\cdot\eta}p(x+y,\tau,\xi+\eta)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{\dj}\eta,

where the integrals are understood in the oscillatory sense.
Moreover, for
r\in S^{\textsf{m}_{1}}_{1,0}
and
q\in S^{\textsf{m}_{2}}_{1,0}
we introduce the oscillatory integral

\displaystyle(r\#q)(x,\tau,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-iy\cdot\eta}r(x,\tau,\xi+\eta)q(x+y,\tau,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\textrm{\dj}\eta.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\textsf{m},\textsf{m}_{1},\textsf{m}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
p\in S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
,
r\in S^{\textsf{m}_{1}}_{1,0}
and
q\in S^{\textsf{m}_{2}}_{1,0}
.
Then for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, the above oscillatory integrals are well-defined, and there holds
p_{L},p_{R}\in S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
as well as
r\#q\in S^{\textsf{m}_{1}+\textsf{m}_{2}}_{1,0}
.
The mappings
p\mapsto p_{L}
,
p\mapsto p_{R}
are bounded linear mappings on
S^{\textsf{m}}_{1,0}
, while
(r,q)\mapsto r\#q
is a bounded bilinear mapping from
S^{\textsf{m}_{1}}_{1,0}\times S^{\textsf{m}_{2}}_{1,0}
to
S^{\textsf{m}_{1}+\textsf{m}_{2}}_{1,0}
.
More precisely, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there is a
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\left\|r\#q-\sum_{|\alpha|<k}\frac{1}{\alpha!}(\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}r)(D_{x}^{\alpha}q)\right\|_{S^{\textsf{m}_{1}+\textsf{m}_{2}-k};N}\leqslant c\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}r\|_{S^{\textsf{m}_{1}}_{1,0};N}\|D_{x}^{\alpha}q\|_{S^{\textsf{m}_{2}}_{1,0};N}.

Finally, for
u\in{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
there holds

\displaystyle p_{L}(x,D_{t,x})u=p(D_{t,x},x)u,\quad p_{R}(D_{t,x},x)u=p(x,D_{t,x})u,\quad(r\#q)(x,D_{t,x})=r(x,D_{t,x})q(x,D_{t,x})u.

Proof.

Analogous to Theorem 3.16 and Theorem 3.32 in
[
1
]
. The boundedness of the mappings follows by an inspection of the proof of Theorem 3.15 in
[
1
]
.
∎

In particular, Lemma
2.5
states that the composition
r(x,D_{t,x})q(x,D_{t,x})
is a pseudodifferential operator of order
\textsf{m}_{1}+\textsf{m}_{2}
in
x
-form, and its symbol is given by
q\#p\in S^{\textsf{m}_{1}+\textsf{m}_{2}}_{1,0}
.

We record the following continuity result for symbols of pseudodifferential operators well-adapted to our Averaging Lemma
4.4
.
We recall the definition of the symbol class

in Definition

.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

Then there is

3.
Optimality of Estimates via Scaling

The following lemma based on a scaling argument extends the local case
[
27
, Lemma 3.1]
to the case of a fractional porous medium equation. Since the proof is virtually the same as in
[
27
]
, we omit it here.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
T>0
,
m>1
,
a\in(0,2)
,
\mu\in[1,m]
,
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\sigma_{t},\sigma_{x}\geqslant 0
. Assume that there is a constant
c=c(d,a,m,\mu,p,\sigma_{t},\sigma_{x})>0
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lVert u^{[\mu]}\rVert_{\dot{W}^{\sigma_{t},p}(0,T;\dot{W}^{\sigma_{x},p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{p}\leqslant c\left(\lVert u_{0}\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\lVert S\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T;L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right)

for all solutions
u
to (
1.6
). Then, necessarily,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}p&\leqslant\frac{m}{\mu+(m-1)\sigma_{t}}\leqslant\frac{m}{\mu},\\
\sigma_{t}&\leqslant\frac{m-\mu p}{p(m-1)}\leqslant\frac{m-\mu}{m-1},\quad\text{and }\\
\sigma_{x}&=\frac{\mu p-1}{p}\frac{a}{m-1}\leqslant\frac{a(\mu-\sigma_{t})}{m}\leqslant\frac{a\mu}{m}.\end{split}

In particular, if
\sigma_{t}=\frac{m-\mu}{m-1}
, then
p=1
and
\sigma_{x}=\frac{a(\mu-1)}{m-1}
.

Remark 3.2
.

As in the local case, Lemma
3.1
shows that in the whole space, the regularity exponent
\sigma_{x}\in[\frac{a(\mu-1)}{m-1},\frac{a\mu}{m}]
is in a one-to-one correspondence to the integrability exponent
p\in[1,\frac{m}{\mu}]
via

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}=\frac{\mu p-1}{p}\frac{a}{m-1},\quad\text{and }\quad p=\frac{a}{a\mu-\sigma_{x}(m-1)}.

For every
m\in(1,\infty)
and
a\in(0,2)
there is suitable bounded and Hölder continuous function
f:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle u_{BB}(t,x):=t^{-\alpha}f(|x|t^{-\beta}),

with
\alpha:=\frac{d}{d(m-1)+a}
and
\beta=\frac{\alpha}{d}
is a self-similar solution to (
1.6
), see
[
50
]
.
In the local case,
u_{BB}
is called the Barenblatt solution, and we continue to use this name in our non-local situation.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
m\in(1,\infty)
and
a\in(0,2)
, and let
u_{BB}
be given by (
3.3
).
Then, for
\mu\in[1,m]
,

\displaystyle u_{BB}^{[\mu]}\in L^{\frac{m}{\mu}}(0,T;\dot{W}^{\sigma,\frac{m}{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))

implies
\sigma<\frac{a\mu}{m}
.

Proof.

With
F(x):=f(x)^{\mu}
we have
u_{BB}^{[\mu]}(t,x)=t^{-\alpha\mu}F(xt^{-\beta})
. We next observe that, for
\sigma\in(0,1)
and each
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\lVert u_{BB}^{[\mu]}(t,\cdot)\rVert_{\dot{W}^{\sigma,\frac{m}{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{m}{\mu}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{|u_{BB}^{[\mu]}(t,x)-u_{BB}^{[\mu]}(t,y)|^{\frac{m}{\mu}}}{|x-y|^{\frac{\sigma m}{\mu}+d}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=t^{-\alpha m-\beta(\frac{\sigma m}{\mu}+d)+2d\beta}\lVert F\rVert_{\dot{W}^{\sigma,\frac{m}{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{m}{\mu}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\lVert u_{BB}^{[\mu]}\rVert_{L^{\frac{m}{\mu}}(0,T;\dot{W}^{\sigma,\frac{\mu}{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}^{\frac{m}{\mu}}=\lVert t^{-\alpha m-\beta(\frac{\sigma m}{\mu}+d)+2d\beta}\rVert_{L^{1}(0,T)}\lVert F\rVert_{\dot{W}^{\sigma,\frac{m}{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{\frac{m}{\mu}},

which is finite if and only if

\displaystyle-\alpha m-\beta(\frac{\sigma m}{\mu}+d)+2d\beta>-1\quad\text{and }\quad F\in\dot{W}^{\sigma,\frac{m}{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Hence, necessarily

\displaystyle m+\frac{1}{d}(\frac{\sigma m}{\mu}+d)-2<\frac{1}{\alpha}=\frac{d(m-1)+a}{d},

which is equivalent to
\sigma<\frac{a\mu}{m}
. In the case
\sigma\in(1,2)
we observe that it holds
\partial_{x_{i}}u_{BB}^{[\mu]}(t,x)=t^{-\alpha\mu+\beta}\partial_{x_{i}}F(xt^{-\beta})
, so that analogous arguments may be applied.
∎

4.
Averaging Lemmata

For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
let
h_{1}(\xi,y):=e^{iy\cdot\xi}-1
and
h_{2}(\xi,y):=e^{iy\cdot\xi}-1-i\xi\cdot y
. The formulae

\displaystyle u(x+y)-u(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}[h_{1}(\cdot,y){\mathcal{F}}u](x),

\displaystyle u(x+y)-u(x)-y\cdot\nabla u(x)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}[h_{2}(\cdot,y){\mathcal{F}}u](x),

show that the operator
L
given in (
1.2
) has a representation as a pseudodifferential operator in
x
-form,
i.e.

(4.1)

\displaystyle Lu(x)=p(x,D_{x})u(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}p(x,\xi)[{\mathcal{F}}u](\xi)\,\textrm{\dj}\xi,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where

(4.2)

\displaystyle p(x,\xi):=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(e^{iy\cdot\xi}-1-\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)i\xi\cdot y)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

Observe that there are different sign conventions for the operator
L
, cf.
[
3
,
4
]
, and we opt for the one of
[
4
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let Assumption
1.1
be valid for some
a\in(0,2)
and
m\in(1,\infty)
, and let
p
be given by (
4.2
).

(i)

There holds
p\in S^{(0,a)}_{1,0}
.

(ii)

There is
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}p(x,\xi)\geqslant c|\xi|^{a},\qquad\forall x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In particular there exists
M>0
with

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}p(x,\xi)}{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}p(x,\xi)}\right|\leqslant M,\qquad\forall x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(iii)

For
v,\tau\in\mathbb{R}
,
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
define

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{v}(x,\tau,\xi)

\displaystyle:=i\tau+\Phi^{\prime}(v)p(x,\xi).

Then there exists
c>0
such that we have

\displaystyle|{\mathcal{L}}_{v}(x,\tau,\xi)|\geqslant c(|\tau|+\Phi^{\prime}(v)|\xi|^{a}),\qquad\forall v,\tau\in\mathbb{R},\ x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proof.

We provide a proof along the lines of
[
3
, Lemma 2.13]
.

(i)

Observe that the function
h:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
,
h(s):=e^{is}-1-\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)is
admits the bound

\displaystyle h(s)\leqslant C\frac{|s|^{j}}{1+|s|},\quad s\in\mathbb{R},

where
j=1
if
a\in(0,1)
and
j=2
if
a\in[1,2)
.
We first give the proof in the case
a\neq 1
.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\alpha|\leqslant N
and
|\beta|=|\gamma|=\ell\leqslant N
.
Moreover, let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
0\leqslant\eta\leqslant 1
,
\eta(y)=1
for
y\in B_{1}(0)
, and
\supp\eta\subset B_{2}(0)
. For
R>0
define
\eta_{R}(y):=\eta(R^{-1}y)
.
Then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}&(\xi^{\gamma}h(\xi\cdot y))\eta_{R}(y)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{y}^{\gamma}(y^{\beta}h(\xi\cdot y))\eta_{R}(y)\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=(-1)^{\ell+1}\sum_{\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y^{\beta}h(\xi\cdot y)\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{1}}\eta_{R}(y)\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{2}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Whenever
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\gamma
and
\gamma_{1}\neq 0
, we have that
\partial_{y}^{\gamma}\eta
is supported on the closure of
B_{2}(0)\setminus B_{1}(0)
, and thus (
1.3
) yields

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y^{\beta}h(\xi\cdot y)\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{1}}\eta_{R}(y)\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{2}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant C\int_{B_{2R}(0)\setminus B_{R}(0)}|y|^{\ell}|y|^{j-1}R^{-|\gamma_{1}|}|\partial_{y}^{\gamma_{1}}\eta(R^{-1}y)||y|^{-d-a-|\gamma_{2}|}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad=CR^{j-1-a}\int_{B_{2}(0)\setminus B_{1}(0)}|z|^{\ell+j-1-d-a-|\gamma_{2}|}\eta(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\to 0\quad\text{as }R\to\infty.

For the term
\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\gamma
in (
4.4
) with
\gamma_{1}=0
we have by dominated convergence

\displaystyle(-1)^{\ell+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y^{\beta}

\displaystyle h(\xi\cdot y)\eta_{R}(y)\partial_{y}^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\to(-1)^{\ell+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y^{\beta}h(\xi\cdot y)\partial_{y}^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\ell+1}|\xi|^{-d-\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}y^{\beta}h(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot y)\partial_{y}^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,\frac{y}{|\xi|})\,\mathrm{d}y.

Since the left-hand side in (
4.4
) tends to
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\xi^{\gamma}p(x,\xi)
, we have

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\xi^{\gamma}p(x,\xi)|\leqslant C|\xi|^{-d-\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|^{\ell}\frac{|y|^{j}}{1+|y|}\left|\frac{y}{|\xi|}\right|^{-d-a-\ell}\,\mathrm{d}y=C^{\prime}|\xi|^{a}.

By induction on
\ell
we thus infer

\displaystyle|\xi^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}p(x,\xi)|\leqslant C^{\prime}|\xi|^{a}

for all
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}
with
|\alpha|\leqslant N
and
|\beta|=|\gamma|=\ell\leqslant N
.
This is the claimed estimate for
a\neq 1
.

In the case
a=1
we have the additional assumption

, which in particular implies by a similar cut-off argument as above

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\xi^{\gamma}p(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\beta|+1}|\xi|^{-d-\ell}\int_{|y|\geqslant 1}y^{\beta}h(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot y)\partial_{y}^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,\frac{y}{|\xi|})\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{|\beta|+1}|\xi|^{-d-\ell}\int_{|y|<1}y^{\beta}\left(e^{i\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot y}-1\right)\partial_{y}^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}k(x,\frac{y}{|\xi|})\,\mathrm{d}y.

Therefore we obtain also in the case

the estimate

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\xi^{\gamma}p(x,\xi)|\leqslant C|\xi|^{-d-\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|y|^{\ell}\frac{|y|^{2}}{1+|y|^{2}}\left|\frac{y}{|\xi|}\right|^{-d-1-\ell}\,\mathrm{d}y=C^{\prime}|\xi|.

The rest of the proof is identical to the case
a\neq 1
.

(ii)

By (
1.4
) and the change of variables formula we have for all
|\xi|>0

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}p(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1-\cos(\xi\cdot y))k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\geqslant c\int_{B_{1/|\xi|}(0)}(1-\cos(\xi\cdot y))|y|^{-d-a}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=c|\xi|^{a}\int_{B_{1}(0)}(1-\cos(\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}\cdot z))|z|^{-d-a}\,\mathrm{d}z\geqslant c^{\prime}|\xi|^{a},

where we used that the value of the integral
\int_{B_{1}(0)}(1-\cos(\omega\cdot z)|z|^{-d-a}\,\mathrm{d}z
is independent of the unit vector
\omega
.
The assertion about the quotient of the imaginary and real part now follows by combining the upper with the lower bound.

(iii)

Since
\frac{|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}p(x,\xi)|}{\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}p(x,\xi)}\leqslant M
for
|\xi|>0
, the symbol
p(x,\xi)
is contained in a sector of the complex plane of angle

, and thus so is

.

is at least of comparable size to both

\Phi^{\prime}(v)p(x,\xi)

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that Assumption
1.1

Define

Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{v}(x,\tau,\xi):=i\tau+\Phi^{\prime}(v)p(x,\xi)

with
\Phi^{\prime}(v)\neq 0
there are symbols
{\mathcal{M}}_{v},{\mathcal{R}}_{v}\in S^{(0,0)}_{1,0}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}_{v}(D_{t,x},x){\mathcal{L}}_{v}(x,D_{t,x})=\id-{\mathcal{R}}_{v}(D_{t,x},x)

and subject to the following additional smoothing properties:
For each
N\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{M}}_{v}\|_{S^{(0,-a)}_{1,0;N}}\leqslant c\Phi^{\prime}(v)^{-1},\quad\|\partial_{v}{\mathcal{M}}_{v}\|_{S^{(0,-a)}_{1,0;N}}\leqslant c\frac{|\Phi^{\prime\prime}(v)|}{\Phi^{\prime}(v)^{2}},\quad\|{\mathcal{R}}_{v}\|_{S^{(0,-a)}_{1,0;N}}\leqslant c,

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{M}}_{v}\|_{S^{(-1,0)}_{1,0;N}}\leqslant c,\quad\|\partial_{v}{\mathcal{M}}_{v}\|_{S^{(-2,a)}_{1,0;N}}\leqslant c|\Phi^{\prime\prime}(v)|,\quad\|{\mathcal{R}}_{v}\|_{S^{(-1,0)}_{1,0;N}}\leqslant c(1+\Phi^{\prime}(v)),

\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{v},\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{v}\in S^{(0,0)}_{1,0}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\overline{f}_{l,j}\|_{L^{m}_{t,x}}&\lesssim 2^{d(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{m})j}\|\overline{f}_{l,j}\|_{L^{m}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}\lesssim 2^{d(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{m})j}\|\overline{f}_{l,j}\|_{L^{m,q}_{t}L^{q}_{x}}\\

5.
Generalized kinetic solutions

In
[
4
]
, the kinetic theory for homogeneous, nonlocal parabolic-hyperbolic PDEs was studied. Here, we generalize this theory to spatially inhomogeneous kernels
k
, that is to kernels which depend on both
x
and
y
instead of just
y
.

In the following, we will first introduce the notion of entropy and kinetic solutions to PDEs of the type

(5.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+\mathrm{div}\,F(u)+L\Phi(u)&=S&&\text{on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}\\
u(0)&=u_{0}&&\text{on }\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d},\end{aligned}\right.

where
L
is of the pure jump type (
1.2
) and

(5.2)

\begin{split}&u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}),\,S\in L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}),\,T\geqslant 0,\\
&\Phi\in W^{1,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\text{ is nondecreasing},\\
&F\in W^{1,\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d}),\\
&k:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})\to(0,\infty)\text{ measurable with }k(x,y)=k(x+y,-y)\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}((\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)|y|^{2}+\mathsf{1}_{(0,1)}(a)|y|)\wedge 1)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y<\infty.\end{split}

Motivated from this, we introduce a broad class of so-called generalized kinetic solutions. This constitutes an extension to parabolic-hyperbolic, nonlocal PDEs of related concepts for scalar conservation laws by
[
42
, Chapter 4]
. Despite the breadth of the resulting class of solutions, we demonstrate in this section that it offers sufficient structure to be amenable to the regularity results derived in this work.

We start by recalling the motivation of the concept of entropy solutions. Let
s\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be convex and
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\beta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\eta^{\prime}=s^{\prime}F^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}=s^{\prime}\Phi^{\prime}
. Moreover let
\varphi
be smooth, compactly supported on
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and nonnegative. Then, we have for any smooth solution
u
to (
5.1
) that

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle s^{\prime}(u)S\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s^{\prime}(u)\varphi\big(\partial_{t}u+\mathrm{div}\,F(u)+L\Phi(u)\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(s(u)\partial_{t}\varphi+\eta(u)\cdot\nabla\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s(u_{0})\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s^{\prime}(u)\varphi\big(\tau_{y}\Phi(u)-\Phi(u)-\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)\mathsf{1}_{|y|\leqslant 2}(y)y\cdot\nabla\Phi(u)\big)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

We next use

\displaystyle s^{\prime}(a)(\Phi(b)-\Phi(a))

\displaystyle=\beta(b)-\beta(a)-\int_{a}^{b}s^{\prime\prime}(v)|\Phi(b)-\Phi(v)|\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\beta(b)-\beta(a)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}s^{\prime\prime}(v)|\Phi(b)-\Phi(v)|\mathsf{1}_{\mathrm{conv}\{a,b\}}(v)\,\mathrm{d}v,

which can be derived by Taylor’s formula and the fact that
\Phi
is non-decreasing in order to introduce the absolute value, see
[
4
, Lemma 22]
. Here
\mathrm{conv}\{a,b\}=(\min\{a,b\},\max\{a,b\})
.
Hence

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle s^{\prime}(u)\varphi(t,x)\big(\tau_{y}\Phi(u)-\Phi(u)-\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)\mathsf{1}_{|y|\leqslant 2}(y)y\cdot\nabla\Phi(u)\big)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\beta(u)L\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}s^{\prime\prime}(v)n(t,x,v)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

where we have used that
L
is
L^{2}
-symmetric due to
k(x,y)=k(x+y,-y)
in the last step, and where

(5.3)

\begin{split}n(t,x,v)&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Phi(u(t,x+y))-\Phi(v)|\mathsf{1}_{\mathrm{conv}\{\tau_{y}u(t,x),u(t,x)\}}(v)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\end{split}

is the nonlocal dissipation measure.
It follows

(5.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(s(u)\partial_{t}\varphi+\eta(u)\cdot\nabla\varphi-\beta(u)L\varphi\big)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s(u_{0})\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}s^{\prime\prime}(v)n(t,x,v)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s^{\prime}(u)S\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

This discussion motivates the following definition of entropy solutions.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
T\geqslant 0
,
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
and
S\in L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
.
We call a function
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
entropy solution to (
5.1
) if for all
(s,\eta,\beta)
with
s\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\beta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with
\eta^{\prime}=s^{\prime}F^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime}=s^{\prime}\Phi^{\prime}
and all nonnegative, smooth
\varphi
with compact support in
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}

there holds

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(s(u)\partial_{t}\varphi+\eta(u)\cdot\nabla\varphi-\beta(u)L\varphi)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s(u_{0})\varphi(0,x)\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\geqslant\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}s^{\prime\prime}(v)n(t,x,v)\varphi\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}s^{\prime}(u)S\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,\end{split}

with the nonlocal dissipation measure
n
defined in (
5.3
).

Formally choosing
s(u):=|u-v|
for
v\in\mathbb{R}
and then differentiating with respect to
v
yields the concept of kinetic equation in
\chi(u(t,x),v):=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{v}(|v|-|u(t,x)-v|)
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
T\geqslant 0
,
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
and
S\in L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
. We say that
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}))
is a kinetic solution to (
5.1
) if the corresponding kinetic function
\chi
satisfies the following:

(i)

There is a non-negative measure
m\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}_{t,x,v}
such that, in the sense of distributions,

\partial_{t}\chi+F^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}\chi-\Phi^{\prime}(v)L\chi=\partial_{v}q+\delta_{v=u(t,x)}S\quad\text{on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}

where
q=m+n
, and
n
is the nonlocal dissipation measure defined in (
5.3
).

(ii)

We have

(5.6)

\int q(t,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\mu(v)\in L_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),

where
L_{0}^{\infty}
is the space of
L^{\infty}
-functions vanishing for
|v|\to\infty
.

Remark 5.3
.

A function
u\in C([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
is a kinetic solution if and only if it is an entropy solution.

The proof of this remark follows along the same line as
[
4
, Theorem 12]
.

Since, in the present work, we are deriving regularity results for solutions to (
5.1
) with
F\equiv 0
, we next introduce a comparatively large class of solutions, requiring only what is needed in the proof of regularity. This notion of generalized kinetic solutions is inspired by
[
42
, Chapter 4]
.

A decisive difference to kinetic solutions is that for generalized kinetic solutions it is not required anymore that the solution takes the form of a kinetic function
f(t,x,v)=\chi(u(t,x),v)
. The reason is that the latter expression is unstable with respect to weak convergence of sequences
u^{n}
. As a result, the class of generalized kinetic solutions offers favorable stability properties with respect to weak convergence compared to kinetic solutions.

In contrast to
[
42
, Chapter 4]
, for the sake of the proof of regularity, no specific form for the dissipation measure
q=m+n
like in (
5.3
) is required. This leads to the following broad class of solutions.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
T\geqslant 0
,
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
and
S\in L^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
. We say that
f\in L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}))\cap L^{\infty}([0,T];L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{v}))
is a generalized kinetic solution to (
5.1
) if

(i)

For some non-negative measure
\nu

we have

\displaystyle|f(t,x,v)|=\sgn(v)f(t,x,v)

\displaystyle\leqslant 1,

\displaystyle\partial_{v}f=\delta_{v=0}-\nu(t,x,v).

(ii)

There is a non-negative measure
q\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}
such that,

\partial_{t}f+F^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\Phi^{\prime}(v)Lf=\partial_{v}q+\nu S\quad\text{on }(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{v},

in the sense that, for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{v})
we have that

-\int\partial_{t}\varphi f-\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot F^{\prime}(v)f-\int L\varphi\Phi^{\prime}(v)f=\int\varphi(0,x,v)\chi(u_{0}(x),v)-\int\partial_{v}\varphi q+\int\varphi\nu S.

(iii)

We have

(5.7)

\int q(t,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t\leqslant\mu(v)\in L_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),

where
L_{0}^{\infty}
is the space of
L^{\infty}
-functions vanishing for
|v|\to\infty
.

We note that analogously to
[
42
, Remark 4.1.4]
we have
\nu\in L^{\infty}_{t,x}\mathcal{M}_{v}
with

(5.8)

\int\nu(t,x,v)\,\mathrm{d}v=1\quad\text{for a.e. }(t,x).

Remark 5.5
.

For a nonnegative measure
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{t,x,v}
we set
[\nu](t,x,v):=\nu(t,x,(-\infty,v])
. Observe that by Fubini’s theorem we have
\partial_{v}[\nu]=\nu
, cf. Example 1.75 in
[
5
]
. Due to Definition
5.4
(i) and (ii), we then have that

(5.9)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}f+F^{\prime}(v)\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\Phi^{\prime}(v)Lf&=\partial_{v}q+\nu S=\partial_{v}(q+[\nu]S)=:\partial_{v}Q\end{split}

and, by (
5.8
),

(5.10)

\begin{split}\|Q\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}{\mathcal{M}}_{TV}}\leqslant\|q\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}{\mathcal{M}}_{TV}}+\|[\nu]S\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}{\mathcal{M}}_{TV}}\leqslant\|q\|_{L^{\infty}_{v}{\mathcal{M}}_{TV}}+\|S\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}.\end{split}

We further have that

(5.11)

\begin{split}Q-[\delta_{v=0}S]=q+[\nu-\delta_{v=0}]S=q-([\partial_{v}f]S)=q-fS.\end{split}

Lemma 5.6
.

For
\varphi\in C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
we have

(5.12)

\begin{split}\|L\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\mathsf{1}_{(0,1)}(a)\|D^{1}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.\end{split}

For
R>0
let
\varphi^{R}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
with
|\varphi^{R}|\leqslant 1
,
\varphi^{R}=1
on
B_{R}(0)
and
\supp\varphi^{R}\subset B_{R+1}(0)
. Then,
L\varphi^{R}
is uniformly bounded and
L\varphi^{R}(x)\to 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

We first prove (
5.12
) by noting that, using Taylor’s formula,

\begin{split}L\varphi(x)&=-\int_{|y|\leqslant 2}\big(\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)-\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)(y\cdot\nabla\varphi(x)\big)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{|y|>2}\big(\varphi(x+y)-\varphi(x)\big)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\lesssim\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|y|\leqslant 2}|y|^{2}k(x,y)\ \,\mathrm{d}y+\mathsf{1}_{(0,1)}(a)\|D^{1}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|y|\leqslant 2}|y|\ k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|y|>2}k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{C^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{y}}((\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)|y|^{2}+\mathsf{1}_{(0,1)}(a)|y|)\wedge 1)\ k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

In particular, by (
5.2
) this implies that
L\varphi^{R}
is uniformly bounded. We further have

(5.13)

\begin{split}L\varphi^{R}(x)=&-\int_{|x+y|\leqslant R}\big(\varphi^{R}(x+y)-\varphi^{R}(x)-\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)\mathsf{1}_{|y|\leqslant 2}(y)y\cdot\nabla\varphi^{R}(x)\big)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&-\int_{|x+y|>R}\big(\varphi^{R}(x+y)-\varphi^{R}(x)-\mathsf{1}_{[1,2)}(a)\mathsf{1}_{|y|\leqslant 2}(y)y\cdot\nabla\varphi^{R}(x)\big)k(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y.\end{split}

Therefore, for
r\geqslant 2
,
R>r
and
|x|<R-r
we have

(5.14)

\begin{split}L\varphi^{R}(x)&\leqslant\int_{|x+y|>R}k(x,y)\leqslant\int_{|y|>r}k(x,y)=\int_{|y|>r}(|y|^{2}\wedge 1)k(x,y).\end{split}

Hence, for fixed
r
and
R\to\infty
we observe the pointwise convergence to zero.
∎

Lemma 5.7
.

Let

In the following, we use for

Lemma 5.8
.

Let

be a generalized kinetic solution to (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v}}\eta^{\prime}(\overline{Q}-1_{v>0}\overline{S})\,\,\mathrm{d}v=&\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v}}\eta\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}}\varphi(0)\chi(u_{0}(x),v)\,\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}v\\

Proof.

By a standard approximation argument, dominated convergence, and Lemma
5.6
, the kinetic formulation yields (
5.15

\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{v})

\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{v})

\eta^{\prime}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{v})

for

Defining

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v}}(\eta\varphi_{R})^{\prime}\overline{Q}\,\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v}}\eta_{R}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}}\varphi(0)\chi(u_{0}(x),v)\,\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\mathrm{d}v+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v}}\eta_{R}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d}}\varphi^{\prime}f\,\,\mathrm{d}x\,\,\mathrm{d}r\right)\,\mathrm{d}v.

Since

is uniformly bounded in

locally uniformly, and since

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v}}(\eta\varphi_{R})^{\prime}\overline{Q}\,\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v}}(\eta\varphi_{R})^{\prime}\overline{(q+[\nu-\delta_{v=0}]S)}\,\,\mathrm{d}v+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{v}}(\eta\varphi_{R})^{\prime}\overline{[\delta_{v=0}]S}\,\,\mathrm{d}v

Lemma 5.9

Let

Continued Fractions for partition generating functions Thanks: Thanks are due to Professor Dr Henk Koppelaar, whose discussions and suggestions have been very helpful for the book for which this paper is essentially a chapter.
Geoffrey B Campbell

Abstract
. We derive continued fractions for partition generating functions, utilizing both Euler’s techniques and Ramanujan’s techniques. Although our results are for integer partitions there is scope to extend this work to vector partitions, including for binary and n-ary partitions.

Primary: 11J70; Secondary: 05A15, 05E40, 11Y11, 11P21

1.
Euler’s Continued Fraction

Almost 290 years ago in 1737, Leonhard Euler wrote
De fractionibus continuis dissertatio
, which gave mathematics a first ever comprehensive account of the properties of continued fractions, and included the first proof that the number
e
is irrational. (See Sandifer
[
50
]
) Later, but still 275 years ago in 1748, Euler, in his
Introductio in analysin infinitorum Vol. I, Chapter 18

[
28
]
, proved:

(a)

the equivalence of his continued fraction to a generalized infinite series,

(b)

every rational number can be written as a finite continued fraction, and

(c)

the continued fraction of an irrational number is infinite.

Euler’s continued fraction is the very nice identity, whose first few cases are:

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{0}a_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{0}/(1-a_{1}/(1+a_{1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{a_{0}}{1-\cfrac{a_{1}}{1+a_{1}}}\;;

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{0}a_{1}+a_{0}a_{1}a_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{0}/(1-a_{1}/(1+a_{1}-a_{2}/(1+a_{2})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{a_{0}}{1-\cfrac{a_{1}}{1+a_{1}-\cfrac{a_{2}}{1+a_{2}}}}\;;

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{0}a_{1}+a_{0}a_{1}a_{2}+a_{0}a_{1}a_{2}a_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{0}/(1-a_{1}/(1+a_{1}-a_{2}/(1+a_{2}-a_{3}/(1+a_{3}))))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cfrac{a_{0}}{1-\cfrac{a_{1}}{1+a_{1}-\cfrac{a_{2}}{1+a_{2}-\cfrac{a_{3}}{1+a_{3}}}}}\;.

Hence, we can state Euler’s Continued Fraction in the following

Theorem 1.1
.

If
a_{0}
,
a_{1}
,
a_{3}
, …
a_{n}
are defined functions such that no denominator is zero in the following equations then

(1.1)

\sum_{k=0}^{n}\prod_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}=a_{0}+a_{0}a_{1}+a_{0}a_{1}a_{2}+...+a_{0}a_{1}...a_{n}

=a_{0}/(1-a_{1}/(1+a_{1}-a_{2}/(1+a_{2}-a_{3}/(1+...\,a_{n-1}/(1+a_{n-1}-a_{n}/(1+a_{n}))))).

=\cfrac{a_{0}}{1-\cfrac{a_{1}}{1+a_{1}-\cfrac{a_{2}}{1+a_{2}-\cfrac{a_{3}}{1+a_{3}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{a_{n-1}}{1+a_{n-1}-\cfrac{a_{n}}{1+a_{n}}}}}}}}\;.

Obviously, this lends itself to many of the elementary series that arise in school and university analysis. However, we shall put this to good use in applying it to partition generating functions. The fact of this theorem involving a finite sum allows us to incrementally extend the number of terms until we can infer the infinite versions of the theorem.

Example 1: The exponential function is

(1.2)

\exp(z)=1+\frac{z}{1!}+\frac{z^{2}}{2!}+\frac{z^{3}}{3!}+...=1+\left(\frac{z}{1}\right)+\left(\frac{z}{1}\right)\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)+\left(\frac{z}{1}\right)\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)\left(\frac{z}{3}\right)+...

=1/\left(1-z/\left(1+z-\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)/\left(1+\left(\frac{z}{2}\right)-\left(\frac{z}{3}\right)/\left(1+\left(\frac{z}{3}\right)-\left(\frac{z}{4}\right)/\left(1+\left(\frac{z}{4}\right)-...\right)\right)\right)\right)\right).

Applying an “equivalence transformation” that consists of clearing the fractions, this example is simplified to

\exp(z)=1/(1-z/(1+z-z/(2+z-2z/(3+z-3z/(4+z-\ldots))))),

or the equivalent statement

\exp(z)=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{z}{1+z-\cfrac{z}{2+z-\cfrac{2z}{3+z-\cfrac{3z}{4+z-\dots}}}}}

and we know this continued fraction converges uniformly on every bounded domain in the complex plane because it is equivalent to the power series for
\exp(z)
.

Example 2: There is the well-known logarithmic function series

(1.3)

\log\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)=2z(\frac{1}{1}+\frac{z^{2}}{3}+\frac{z^{4}}{5}+...)

=2z(1+(\frac{z^{2}}{3})+(\frac{z^{2}}{3})(\frac{3z^{2}}{5})+(\frac{z^{2}}{3})(\frac{3z^{2}}{5})(\frac{5z^{2}}{7})+...).

Applying Euler’s continued fraction formula to this expression shows that:

\log\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)

=2z/(1-(\frac{z^{2}}{3})/(1+(\frac{z^{2}}{3})-(\frac{3z^{2}}{5})/(1+(\frac{3z^{2}}{5})-(\frac{5z^{2}}{7})/(1+(\frac{5z^{2}}{7})-(\frac{7z^{2}}{9})/(1+(\frac{7z^{2}}{9})-...))))).

Applying the “equivalence transformation” this example is simplified to

\log\left(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\right)=2z/(1-z^{2}/(z^{2}+3-(3z)^{2}/(3z^{2}+5-(5z)^{2}/(5z^{2}+7-(7z)^{2}/(7z^{2}+9-...)))))

=\cfrac{2z}{1-\cfrac{z^{2}}{z^{2}+3-\cfrac{(3z)^{2}}{3z^{2}+5-\cfrac{(5z)^{2}}{5z^{2}+7-\cfrac{(7z)^{2}}{7z^{2}+9-\dots}}}}}

Example 3: A continued fraction for
\pi
.
We can use the previous example involving the principal branch of the natural logarithm function to construct a continued fraction representation of
\pi
. First we note that

(i+1)/(i-1)=i,\quad so\quad then\quad\log((i+1)/(i-1))=i\pi/2.

Setting
z=i
in the previous result, and remembering that
i^{2}=-1
, we obtain immediately

\pi=\cfrac{4}{1+\cfrac{1^{2}}{2+\cfrac{3^{2}}{2+\cfrac{5^{2}}{2+\cfrac{7^{2}}{2+\dots}}}}}

2.
Euler’s continued fraction applied to partitions

In this section we will technically do no more than apply the previous section. However, the theory of partitions is full of generating functions that are emenable to the Euler continued fraction. In a subsequent section we will examine Ramanujan type continued fractions, but firstly we will gather some ”low hanging fruit” from some elementary series-product identities.

We begin with the well-known telescoping identities:

If
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
a_{3}
, … ,
a_{n}
, are functions chosen for nonzero denominators, then

(2.1)

1+\frac{a_{1}}{1-a_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{(1-a_{1})(1-a_{2})}+...+\frac{a_{n}}{(1-a_{1})(1-a_{2})...(1-a_{n})}

=\frac{1}{(1-a_{1})(1-a_{2})(1-a_{3})...(1-a_{n})};

and

(2.2)

1+a_{1}+a_{2}(1+a_{1})+a_{3}(1+a_{1})(1+a_{2})+...+a_{n}(1+a_{1})(1+a_{2})...(1+a_{n-1})

=(1+a_{1})(1+a_{2})(1+a_{3})...(1+a_{n}).

The series in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are already close to being in the required form to apply the Euler continued fraction since

(2.3)

1+\frac{a_{1}}{1-a_{1}}+\frac{a_{2}}{(1-a_{1})(1-a_{2})}+...+\frac{a_{n}}{(1-a_{1})(1-a_{2})...(1-a_{n})}

=1+\frac{a_{1}}{1-a_{1}}+\frac{a_{1}}{1-a_{1}}\frac{a_{2}(1-a_{1})}{a_{1}(1-a_{2})}+...+\frac{a_{1}}{1-a_{1}}\frac{a_{2}(1-a_{1})}{a_{1}(1-a_{2})}...\frac{a_{n}(1-a_{n-1})}{a_{n-1}(1-a_{n})};

and

(2.4)

1+a_{1}+a_{2}(1+a_{1})+a_{3}(1+a_{1})(1+a_{2})+...+a_{n}(1+a_{1})(1+a_{2})...(1+a_{n-1})

=1+a_{1}+a_{1}\frac{a_{2}(1+a_{1})}{a_{1}}+a_{1}\frac{a_{2}(1+a_{1})}{a_{1}}\frac{a_{3}(1+a_{2})}{a_{2}}+...+a_{1}\frac{a_{2}(1+a_{1})}{a_{1}}\frac{a_{3}(1+a_{2})}{a_{2}}...\frac{a_{n}(1+a_{n-1})}{a_{n-1}}.

Hence combining (
2.1
) with (
2.3
) and then (
2.2
) with (
2.4
) respectively, we obtain

(2.5)

\frac{1}{(1-a_{1})(1-a_{2})(1-a_{3})...(1-a_{n})}

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{\frac{a_{1}}{1-a_{1}}}{1+\frac{a_{1}}{1-a_{1}}-\cfrac{\frac{a_{2}(1-a_{1})}{a_{1}(1-a_{2})}}{1+\frac{a_{2}(1-a_{1})}{a_{1}(1-a_{2})}-\cfrac{\frac{a_{3}(1-a_{2})}{a_{2}(1-a_{3})}}{1+\frac{a_{3}(1-a_{2})}{a_{2}(1-a_{3})}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{\frac{a_{n-1}(1-a_{n-2})}{a_{n-2}(1-a_{n-1})}}{1+\frac{a_{n-1}(1-a_{n-2})}{a_{n-2}(1-a_{n-1})}-\cfrac{\frac{a_{n}(1-a_{n-1})}{a_{n-1}(1-a_{n})}}{1+\frac{a_{n}(1-a_{n-1})}{a_{n-1}(1-a_{n})}}}}}}}}\;;

and

(2.6)

(1+a_{1})(1+a_{2})(1+a_{3})...(1+a_{n})

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{a_{1}}{1+a_{1}-\cfrac{\frac{a_{2}(1+a_{1})}{a_{1}}}{1+\frac{a_{2}(1+a_{1})}{a_{1}}-\cfrac{\frac{a_{3}(1+a_{2})}{a_{2}}}{1+\frac{a_{3}(1+a_{2})}{a_{2}}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{\frac{a_{n-1}(1+a_{n-2})}{a_{n-2}}}{1+\frac{a_{n-1}(1+a_{n-2})}{a_{n-2}}-\cfrac{\frac{a_{n}(1+a_{n-1})}{a_{n-1}}}{1+\frac{a_{n}(1+a_{n-1})}{a_{n-1}}}}}}}}}\;.

After applying the “equivalence transformation” to both of (
2.5
) and then (
2.6
) to eliminate denominator terms, each continued fraction is simplified giving us the following two theorems.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
a_{3}
, … ,
a_{n}
, are functions chosen for nonzero denominators, then

(2.7)

\frac{1}{(1-a_{1})(1-a_{2})(1-a_{3})...(1-a_{n})}

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{a_{1}}{1-\cfrac{a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}-2a_{1}a_{2}-\cfrac{a_{1}a_{3}}{a_{2}+a_{3}-2a_{2}a_{3}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{a_{n-2}a_{n}}{a_{n-1}+a_{n}-2a_{n-1}a_{n}}}}}}}\;.

At first glance we can see this theorem as being applicable to generating functions for unrestricted partitions of various kinds. Similarly the next theorem applies for partitions of various sorts into distinct parts.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
a_{3}
, … ,
a_{n}
, are functions chosen for nonzero denominators, then

(2.8)

(1+a_{1})(1+a_{2})(1+a_{3})...(1+a_{n})

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{a_{1}}{1+a_{1}-\cfrac{(1+a_{1})a_{2}}{a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{1}a_{2}-\cfrac{(1+a_{2})a_{3}}{a_{2}+a_{3}+a_{2}a_{3}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{(1+a_{n-1})a_{n}}{a_{n-1}+a_{n}+a_{n-1}a_{n}}}}}}}\;.

There are many examples we could choose for substitution into theorems
2.2
and
2.2
. So, let’s start with the generating functions for unrestricted partitions, and for distinct partitions as follows.

Corollary 2.1
.

If
p_{n}(k)
, is the number of unrestricted partitions of
k
into integers no greater than
n
, then

(2.9)

\frac{1}{(1-q^{1})(1-q^{2})(1-q^{3})...(1-q^{n})}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(k)q^{k}

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{q^{1}}{1-\cfrac{q^{2}}{q^{1}+q^{2}-2q^{1}q^{2}-\cfrac{q^{1}q^{3}}{q^{2}+q^{3}-2q^{2}q^{3}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{q^{n-2}q^{n}}{q^{n-1}+q^{n}-2q^{n-1}q^{n}}}}}}}\;.

Corollary 2.2
.

If
p_{n}(\mathfrak{D},k)
, is the number of distinct partitions of
k
into integers no greater than
n
, then

(2.10)

(1+q^{1})(1+q^{2})(1+q^{3})...(1+q^{n})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(\mathfrak{D},k)q^{k}

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{q^{1}}{1+q^{1}-\cfrac{(1+q^{1})q^{2}}{q^{1}+q^{2}+q^{1}q^{2}-\cfrac{(1+q^{2})q^{3}}{q^{2}+q^{3}+q^{2}q^{3}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{(1+q^{n-1})q^{n}}{q^{n-1}+q^{n}+q^{n-1}q^{n}}}}}}}\;.

Next we choose the odd integer powers substituted into the two theorems.

Corollary 2.3
.

If
p_{n}(\mathfrak{O},k)
, is the number of unrestricted partitions of
k
into odd integers no greater than
2n-1
, then

(2.11)

\frac{1}{(1-q^{1})(1-q^{3})(1-q^{5})...(1-q^{2n-1})}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(\mathfrak{O},k)q^{k}

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{q^{1}}{1-\cfrac{q^{3}}{q^{1}+q^{3}-2q^{1}q^{3}-\cfrac{q^{1}q^{5}}{q^{3}+q^{5}-2q^{3}q^{5}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{q^{n-2}q^{n}}{q^{2n-3}+q^{2n-1}-2q^{2n-3}q^{2n-1}}}}}}}\;.

Corollary 2.4
.

If
p_{n}(\mathfrak{DO},k)
, is the number of distinct partitions of
k
into odd integers no greater than
2n-1
, then

(2.12)

(1+q^{1})(1+q^{3})(1+q^{5})...(1+q^{2n-1})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}p_{n}(\mathfrak{DO},k)q^{k}

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{q^{1}}{1+q^{1}-\cfrac{(1+q^{1})q^{3}}{q^{1}+q^{3}+q^{1}q^{3}-\cfrac{(1+q^{3})q^{5}}{q^{3}+q^{5}+q^{3}q^{5}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{(1+q^{2n-3})q^{2n-1}}{q^{2n-3}+q^{2n-1}+q^{2n-3}q^{2n-1}}}}}}}\;.

It is a well-known result due to Euler that
p_{\infty}(\mathfrak{DO},k)=p_{\infty}(\mathfrak{O},k)
. Explicitly, as
n\rightarrow\infty
equations (
2.12
) and (
2.11
) are equal to each other.

Next, let us give the cases covering binary partitions.

Corollary 2.5
.

If
b_{n}(\mathfrak{2},k)
, is the number of unrestricted binary partitions of
k
into non-negative powers of two no greater than
2^{n}
, then

(2.13)

\frac{1}{(1-q^{1})(1-q^{2})(1-q^{4})...(1-q^{2^{n}})}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}b_{n}(\mathfrak{2},k)q^{k}

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{q^{1}}{1-\cfrac{q^{2}}{q^{1}+q^{2}-2q^{1}q^{2}-\cfrac{q^{1}q^{4}}{q^{2}+q^{4}-2q^{2}q^{4}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{q^{2^{n-2}}q^{2^{n}}}{q^{2^{n-1}}+q^{2^{n}}-2q^{2^{n-1}}q^{2^{n}}}}}}}}\;.

The following distinct binary partitions example is completely solvable.

Corollary 2.6
.

If
p_{n}(\mathfrak{2D},k)
, is the number of binary partitions of
k
into distinct non-negative powers of two no greater than
2^{n}
, then

(2.14)

(1+q^{1})(1+q^{2})(1+q^{4})...(1+q^{2^{n}})=\frac{1-q^{2^{n+1}}}{1-q}=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n+1}-1}p_{n}(\mathfrak{2D},k)q^{k}

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{q^{1}}{1+q^{1}-\cfrac{(1+q^{1})q^{2}}{q^{1}+q^{2}+q^{1}q^{2}-\cfrac{(1+q^{2})q^{4}}{q^{2}+q^{4}+q^{2}q^{4}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{(1+q^{2^{n-1\emph{}}})q^{2^{n}}}{q^{2^{n-1}}+q^{2^{n}}+q^{2^{n-1}}q^{2^{n}}}}}}}}\;.

Note that from (
2.14
) we have directly that

p_{n}(\mathfrak{2D},k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{when $0\leq k<2^{n+1}$;}\\
0,&\hbox{when $k\geq 2^{n+1}$.}\end{array}\right.

The following distinct ternary partitions example is easily stated.

Corollary 2.7
.

If
p_{n}(\mathfrak{3D},k)
, is the number of ternary partitions of
k
into distinct non-negative powers of three no greater than
3^{n}
, then

(2.15)

(1+q^{1})(1+q^{3})(1+q^{9})...(1+q^{3^{n}})=\sum_{k=0}^{3^{n}-1}p_{n}(\mathfrak{3D},k)q^{k}

=\cfrac{1}{1-\cfrac{q^{1}}{1+q^{1}-\cfrac{(1+q^{1})q^{3}}{q^{1}+q^{3}+q^{1}q^{3}-\cfrac{(1+q^{3})q^{9}}{q^{3}+q^{9}+q^{3}q^{9}-\cfrac{\ddots}{\ddots\cfrac{(1+q^{3^{n-1\emph{}}})q^{3^{n}}}{q^{3^{n-1}}+q^{3^{n}}+q^{3^{n-1}}q^{3^{n}}}}}}}}\;.

Note that from (
2.15
) we have directly that

p_{n}(\mathfrak{3D},k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{for $0\leq k<3^{n+1}$; $k$ is a sum of distinct powers of 3.}\\
0,&\hbox{for $0\leq k<3^{n+1}$; $k$ not a sum of distinct powers of 3.}\\
0,&\hbox{for $k\geq 3^{n+1}$.}\end{array}\right.

Clearly this topic of Euler Continued Fractions applied to partition generating functions is an interesting elementary study for students, and a possible tool for researchers. The above results are old, and have probably been well-worked over time.

3.
Rogers-Ramanujan Continued Fractions for partition functions

The fraction given here was mentioned by Ramanujan in his second letter to Hardy (see Adiga et al.
[
2
, p. xxviii]
); namely

(3.1)

R(a,b)=1+\cfrac{bq}{1+aq+\cfrac{bq^{2}}{1+aq^{2}+\cfrac{bq^{3}}{1+aq^{3}+\cfrac{bq^{4}}{\ddots}}}}\;.

However, these now famous continued fractions, as with the Rogers-Ramanujan identities, were first discovered in 1894 by Rogers (see
[
49
]
).
We define the functions
G(q)
and
H(q)
in the context of the Rogers–Ramanujan identities,

(3.2)

G(q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{n})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}}}{(q:q)_{n}}

=\frac{1}{(q;q^{5})(q^{4};q^{5})}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{5n-4})(1-q^{5n-1})},

and

(3.3)

H(q)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\cdots(1-q^{n})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n^{2}+n}}{(q:q)_{n}}

=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{5})(q^{3};q^{5})}=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{5n-3})(1-q^{5n-2})}.

The Rogers–Ramanujan continued fraction is then,

(3.4)

R(q)=\frac{q^{\frac{11}{60}H(q)}}{q^{\frac{-1}{60}G(q)}}=q^{\frac{1}{5}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{5n-4})(1-q^{5n-1})}{(1-q^{5n-3})(1-q^{5n-2})}

=1+\cfrac{q^{\frac{1}{5}}}{1+\cfrac{q}{1+\cfrac{q^{2}}{1+\cfrac{q^{3}}{\ddots}}}}\;.

So, we note that
R(0,1)
leads us to the celebrated Rogers-Ramanujan continued fraction, which has been researched by many (see Andrews
[
4
, Chapter 7]
, for example). In the course of analyzing identities from Ramanujan’s Lost Notebook
[
7
]
, Andrews and Berndt have discussed the fraction
R(a,b)
, but mainly from the viewpoint of transformation formulas. Our emphasis here is on using (
3.1
) in a generalized approach to several partition identities, but there is a whole adjacent theory on particular values of these continued fractions determined from applying the theory of modular forms. Hence the examples, using
\varphi
as the golden ratio
(\sqrt{5}+1)/2
,

(3.5)

\cfrac{e^{-\frac{-\pi}{5}}}{1+\cfrac{e^{-\pi}}{1+\cfrac{e^{-2\pi}}{1+\cfrac{e^{-3\pi}}{\ddots}}}}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi(\sqrt{5}-\varphi^{3/2})(\sqrt[4]{5}+\varphi^{3/2}),

(3.6)

\cfrac{e^{-\frac{-2\pi}{5}}}{1+\cfrac{e^{-2\pi}}{1+\cfrac{e^{-4\pi}}{1+\cfrac{e^{-6\pi}}{\ddots}}}}=\sqrt[4]{5}\varphi^{1/2}-\varphi,

(3.7)

\cfrac{e^{-\frac{-4\pi}{5}}}{1+\cfrac{e^{-4\pi}}{1+\cfrac{e^{-8\pi}}{1+\cfrac{e^{-12\pi}}{\ddots}}}}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi(\sqrt{5}-\varphi^{3/2})(-\sqrt[4]{5}+\varphi^{3/2}).

So next we examine the continued fraction
R(a,b)
of Ramanujan and consider various restricted partition functions. For further reading, a good reference is Alladi and Gordon
[
3
]
. We use the continued fraction to give results for several partition identities, some of which generalize results of Bressoud
[
12
]
and Göllnitz
[
34
]
. We also give a combinatorial interpretation for the coefficients in the power series expansion of the reciprocal
\frac{1}{R(-a,-b)}
, extending a result of Odlyzko and Wilf
[
42
]
. The full description of this approach would add several more pages to our work, but
[
3
]
covers all of this very nicely.

It turns out that
Lebesgue’s identity
plays a major role in our analysis with respect to the numerators and denominators of the finite continued fractions we consider.

(3.8)

\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{q^{k(k+1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k}(1+bq^{j})}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})...(1-q^{k})}=\prod_{m\geq 1}(1+bq^{2m})(1+q^{m}).

It is known that Lebesgue’s identity implies Ramanujan’s fraction
R(a,b)
has a product representation when
a=1
. More precisely (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) (see below) yield

(3.9)

1+\cfrac{bq}{1+q+\cfrac{bq^{2}}{1+q^{2}+\cfrac{bq^{3}}{1+q^{3}+\cfrac{bq^{4}}{\ddots}}}}=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1+bq^{2m-1})}{(1+bq^{2m})}.

A neat case of (
3.9
) is obtained from
q\mapsto q^{2}
and
b\mapsto bq^{-1}
so then

(3.10)

1+\cfrac{bq}{1+q^{2}+\cfrac{bq^{3}}{1+q^{4}+\cfrac{bq^{5}}{1+q^{6}+\cfrac{bq^{7}}{\ddots}}}}=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1+bq^{4m-3})}{(1+bq^{4m-1})}.

For a continued fraction
F
, let
P_{n}/Q_{n}
denote its
n
th convergent, and suppose that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P_{n}=P
,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q_{n}=Q
in a suitable topology. We then say that
F
has numerator
P
and denominator
Q
, and write
P=F^{N}
,
Q=F^{D}
.
Consider the fraction

F(a,c)=1+a+\cfrac{acq}{1+aq+\cfrac{acq^{2}}{1+aq^{2}+\cfrac{acq^{3}}{1+aq^{3}+\cfrac{acq^{4}}{\ddots}}}}\;.

This can be written in the form

F(a,c)=\frac{f(a,c)}{f(aq,c)},\quad\textmd{where}\quad f(a,c)=\sum_{k\geq 0}A_{k}q^{k}.

We now compute the coefficients
A_{k}=A_{k}(c,q)
, observing that
f(a,c)
satisfies the recurrence

f(a,c)=(1+a)f(aq,c)+acq\,f(aq^{2},c).

Therefore the coefficients
A_{k}
satisfy

A_{k}=q^{k}\,A_{k}+q^{k-1}A_{k-1}\,q-cq^{2k-1}\,A_{k-1},

which is the same as

A_{k}=\frac{q^{k-1}(1+cq^{k})}{(1-q^{k})}A_{k-1}.

By iteration this yields

F(a,c)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a^{k}q^{\frac{k(k-1)}{2}}(-cq)_{k}}{(q)_{k}}.

Let
c=a^{-1}b
. Then

R(a,b)=\frac{f(a,a^{-1}b)}{f(aq,a^{-1}b)}-a

is Ramanujan’s fraction (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For the fraction
R(a,b)
, the numerator is

(3.11)

R^{N}(a,b)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a^{k}q^{k(k+1)/2}(-a^{-1}b)_{k}}{(q)_{k}},

and the denominator is

(3.12)

R^{D}(a,b)=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a^{k}q^{k(k+1)/2}(-a^{-1}bq)_{k}}{(q)_{k}}.

Proof
: The expansion (
3.12
) is an immediate consequence of

(3.13)

R^{D}(a,b)=f(aq,a^{-1}b).

The expansion (
3.11
) is more complicated. To obtain it, observe that

\displaystyle R^{N}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(a,a^{-1}b)-a\,f(aq,a^{-1}b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a^{k}q^{k(k-1)/2}(-a^{-1}bq)_{k}}{(q)_{k}}-\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a^{k+1}q^{k(k+1)/2}(-a^{-1}bq)_{k}}{(q)_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a^{k+1}q^{k(k+1)/2}(-a^{-1}bq)_{k}}{(q)_{k}}\left(\frac{1+a{-1}bq^{k+1}}{1-q^{k+1}}-1\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a^{k+1}q^{(k+1)(k+2)/2}(-a^{-1}bq)_{k}(1-a^{-1}b)}{(q)_{k+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a^{k}q^{k(k+1)/2}(-a^{-1}b)_{k}}{(q)_{k}}

as required.
\quad\blacksquare

Andrews (see

for

Lemma 3.2
.

For the fraction
R(a,b)
, we also have the expansions

(3.14)

R^{N}(a,b)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\frac{a^{i}b^{j}q^{(i^{2}+i)/2+ij+j^{2}}}{(q)_{i}(q)_{j}},

and the denominator is

(3.15)

R^{D}(a,b)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\frac{a^{i}b^{j}q^{(i^{2}+i)/2+ij+j^{2}+j}}{(q)_{i}(q)_{j}}.

Proof
: To obtain (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) from (
3.12
) and (
3.13

(-z)_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}z^{j}q^{j(j-1)/2}\binom{k}{j}_{q}

with

z=a^{-1}bq

for the

\displaystyle R^{N}(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{a^{k}q^{k(k+1)/2}}{(q)_{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{a^{-j}b^{j}q^{j(j-1)/2}(q)_{k}}{(q)_{j}(q)_{j-k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\geq 0}\frac{a^{i}b^{j}q^{(i+j)(i+j+1)/2}}{(q)_{i}(q)_{j}},

where

The following two theorems relate successively to the numerator and the denominator of the fraction (

Theorem 3.1
.

(Numerator)

Let

be the number of partitions of

be zero and every blue part is
\leq i+j-1

Let
B^{N}(n;i,j)
be the number of partitions of
n
into
i
distinct red parts and
j
distinct non-consecutive blue parts such that every red part is
>j
.

Let

be the number of partitions of
n
into
i
red parts and
j
blue parts such that all parts are distinct and after each blue part there is a gap of at least 2. Then

A^{N}(n;i,j)=B^{N}(n;i,j)=C^{N}(n;i,j).

Theorem 3.2
.

(Denominator)

Let
A^{D}(n;i,j)

>0

\leq i+j

Let
B^{D}(n;i,j)

Let
C^{D}(n;i,j)
be as in

A^{D}(n;i,j)=B^{D}(n;i,j)=C^{D}(n;i,j).

So reprising (
3.10
) namely

1+\cfrac{bq}{1+q^{2}+\cfrac{bq^{3}}{1+q^{4}+\cfrac{bq^{5}}{1+q^{6}+\cfrac{bq^{7}}{\ddots}}}}=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1+bq^{4m-3})}{(1+bq^{4m-1})}),

we have interesting cancellations in numerator-denominator equations. That is, the numerator is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{q^{k(k+1)}(-bq^{-1};q^{2})_{k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1+bq^{4m-3})(1+q^{2m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1+bq^{4m-3})(1+q^{4m-2})(1+q^{4m})

and the denominator is given by

\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{q^{k(k+1)}(-bq;q^{2})_{k}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{k}}=\prod_{m=1}^{\infty}(1+bq^{4m-1})(1+q^{4m-2})(1+q^{4m})

with right sides having common factors that eliminate.

This leads in particular to the continued fraction identity

(3.17)

1+\cfrac{q}{1+q^{2}+\cfrac{q^{3}}{1+q^{4}+\cfrac{q^{5}}{1+q^{6}+\cfrac{q^{7}}{\ddots}}}}=\frac{\prod_{j\equiv 2,3,7\,(\textmd{mod}8)}(1-q^{j})}{\prod_{j\equiv 1,5,6\,(\textmd{mod}8)}(1-q^{j})}.

4.

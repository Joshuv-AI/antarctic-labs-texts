Understanding the training of infinitely deep and wide ResNets with Conditional Optimal Transport
Raphaël Barboni, Gabriel Peyré, François-Xavier Vialard

Abstract
We study the convergence of gradient flow for the training of deep neural networks. If Residual Neural Networks are a popular example of very deep architectures, their training constitutes a challenging optimization problem due notably to the non-convexity and the non-coercivity of the objective. Yet, in applications, those tasks are successfully solved by simple optimization algorithms such as gradient descent. To better understand this phenomenon, we focus here on a “mean-field” model of infinitely deep and arbitrarily wide ResNet, parameterized by probability measures over the product set of layers and parameters and with constant marginal on the set of layers. Indeed, in the case of shallow neural networks, mean field models have proven to benefit from simplified loss-landscapes and good theoretical guarantees when trained with gradient flow for the Wasserstein metric on the set of probability measures. Motivated by this approach, we propose to train our model with gradient flow w.r.t. the conditional Optimal Transport distance: a restriction of the classical Wasserstein distance which enforces our marginal condition. Relying on the theory of gradient flows in metric spaces we first show the well-posedness of the gradient flow equation and its consistency with the training of ResNets at finite width. Performing a local Polyak-Łojasiewicz analysis, we then show convergence of the gradient flow for well-chosen initializations: if the number of features is finite but sufficiently large and the risk is sufficiently small at initialization, the gradient flow converges towards a global minimizer. This is the first result of this type for infinitely deep and arbitrarily wide ResNets. In addition, this work is the occasion to study in more detail the conditional Optimal Transport metric, particularly its dynamic formulation. Some of our results in this direction might be interesting on their own.

Introduction

Understanding the training dynamic of neural networks is an important problem in Machine Learning as it brings the hope of understanding the good performances of these models.
This training is however an involved optimization problem, usually solved by performing (stochastic) gradient descent for the training risk, an optimization procedure which, though simple, often manages to find a global minimum of the risk despite its non-convexity. This phenomenon is now correctly understood in some simple cases such as the one of linear networks
[
HM16
,
BHL18
,
ZLG19
,
Bah+22
]
.
In the more realistic case of non-linear architectures, most works have focused on
Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLP)

[
LY17
,
Du+19
,
ALS19
,
Zou+20
,
Lee+19
,
Che+20
,
Ngu21
]
and convergence towards a minimizer of the risk can be obtained with great probability over a random initialization provided that the network is sufficiently wide, a regime referred to as “overparameterization”.
Taking the limit of infinite width, many works have also studied the convergence gradient descent for the training of neural networks in the limit of an infinite number of parameters
[
CB18
,
MMN18
,
JMM20
,
Woj20
,
NP23
]
. In those works, the neural network is trained by modeling the parameters as a probability measure over the parameter space and performing a Wasserstein gradient flow over the set of probability measures. Notably,
[
CB18
]
establish a result of optimality at convergence: if the gradient flow converges then its limit is a global minimizer of the training risk.

We will focus in this work on the case of the
Residual Neural Network (ResNet)
architecture.
ResNets were first introduced by
[
He+16
]
for applications in computer vision but the architecture has since distinguished itself by obtaining state-of-the-art results in several other machine-learning applications. A key feature of ResNets is the extensive use of
skip connections
: each layer consists of the addition of a perturbation (called
residual
) to the output of the previous layer.
The presence of skip connections has indeed been identified to ease the training of deeper neural networks
[
RVL12
,
Sze+17
]
by mitigating the
vanishing / exploding gradient
phenomena, a common problem encountered when training deep neural networks
[
BSF94
,
GB10
]
. The ResNet architecture has thus permitted the training of neural networks of almost arbitrary depth
[
He+16a
]
.
Considering the limit where the depth tends to infinity
[
Che+18
]
introduced the
Neural Ordinary Differential Equation (
\mathrm{NODE}
)
architecture: ResNets being seen as the
explicit Euler
discretization scheme of an ODE, passing to the limit of infinite depth leads to a model performing the integration of an ODE with a parametric velocity field. An important contribution of
\mathrm{NODEs}
is to provide a theoretical framework upon which many other works have been based to study very deep neural network architectures.

[
Che+18
]
proposed a method based on
adjoint sensitivity analysis
to compute the gradient of
\mathrm{NODEs}
efficiently without automatic differentiation.

[
San+21
]
proposed a new architecture based on a second-order ODE which can be trained with reduced computational complexity.
Inspired by methods from medical imaging and shape analysis,
[
Via+20
]
proposed a new algorithm for the training of deep ResNets.

[
EHL19
,
EMW21
]
studied the training and generalization properties of deep ResNets borrowing tools from the mathematical theory of
Optimal Control
.

\mathcal{W}_{p}

[

,
San15
]

the

\mathcal{E}_{p}(\mu)\coloneqq\int_{X}|x|^{p}\mathrm{d}\mu(x)

\pi^{i}:(x^{1},...,x^{n})\in X\mapsto x^{i}\in X^{i}

1.1
Mean-field model of neural network

We consider in this paper neural networks parameterized by probability measures.
Such “mean-field models” can be thought of as neural networks of arbitrary width and were studied before by
[
CB18
,
MMN18
,
RV18
,
Woj20
,
NP23
]
.
Provided with the
parameter space

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the input space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and with a Borel map
\phi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
(the
feature map
), we consider mappings
F_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
parameterized by measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
and defined by:

\forall\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega),\quad F_{\mu}:x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\phi(\omega,x)\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega).

(1)

Single hidden layer perceptron

The above definition encompasses as a particular case standard neural network architectures. For example, for
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
and
\phi:((u,w,b),x)\mapsto u\sigma(w^{\top}x+b)
with a real-valued function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
(called
activation
), considering the atomic measure
\mu=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{(u_{i},w_{i},b_{i})}
one recovers the classical model of a
Single Hidden Layer (SHL) Perceptron
of width
m\geq 0
:

F_{\mu}:x\mapsto\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}\sigma(w_{i}^{\top}x+b_{i}).

Convolutional Layer

Closer to applications,
eq.

1
also encompasses the convolutional architecture originally used in ResNet for image recognition
[
He+16
]
. Consider integers
n,c,m,k\geq 0
and
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{c\times 1\times k\times k}\times\mathbb{R}^{1\times c\times k\times k}\times\mathbb{R}^{1\times n\times n}
, the set of parameters of the form
(u,w,b)
where
u
and
w
are convolutional kernels of size
c\times 1\times k\times k
and
1\times c\times k\times k
respectively and
b
is a bias term of size
1\times n\times n
. Then for an image input
x\in\mathbb{R}^{c\times n\times n}
of size
n\times n
with
c
channels, and
(u,w,b)\in\Omega
consider the feature map
\varphi:((u,w,b),x)\mapsto u\star\sigma(w\star x+b)\in\mathbb{R}^{c\times n\times n}
where
\star
denotes the discrete convolution operator and the activation
\sigma
is applied component-wise. Then for an empirical measure
\mu=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{(u_{i},w_{i},b_{i})}
,
eq.

1
gives on input
x
:

\displaystyle F_{\mu}(x)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}u_{i}\star\sigma(w_{i}\star x+b_{i}),

which is the output of a ResNet residual with
m
intermediary channels (without normalization)
[
He+16
]
. However, the definition in
eq.

1
does not model some popular architectures such as normalization layers which play an important role in the success of ResNets.

Attention layers

Finally,
eq.

1
also models
attention layers
at the heart of
Transformers
architectures
[
Vas+17
]
. Consider as parameter space
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{c\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{c\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, the set of triplets
(K,Q,V)
where
K\in\mathbb{R}^{c\times d}
is the
key
matrix,
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{c\times d}
is the
query
matrix and
V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the
value
matrix. One can define the feature map
\phi
as an
attention
layer with parameter
(K,Q,V)\in\Omega
. For an input sequence of
tokens

\mathbf{x}=(x^{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
of length
N\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\phi((K,Q,V),\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\mathrm{Attention}((K,Q,V),\mathbf{x})=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{e^{\langle Kx^{i},Qx^{j}\rangle}}{\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{\langle Kx^{i},Qx^{j}\rangle}}Vx^{j}\right)_{1\leq i\leq N}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}\,.

Then for a measure an empirical measure
\mu=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\delta_{(K_{k},Q_{k},V_{k})}
,
eq.

1
defines a
multi-head attention
layer with
m
heads:

\displaystyle F_{\mu}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathrm{Attention}((K_{k},Q_{k},V_{k}),\mathbf{x})\,.

Note that with this definition, we are only able to describe Transformer architectures taking as input sequences of tokens of fixed length
N
. However, our setting could be adapted to model Transformer architecture taking as inputs sequences of tokens of various finite lengths by considering different feature maps depending on the length of the input sequence.

Functional properties

[
WW22
]
study the class of mappings defined by
eq.

1
from a functional analysis viewpoint.
More precisely, they study the “Barron space”

\mathcal{B}\coloneqq\{f:x\mapsto\int u\sigma(w^{\top}x+b)\mathrm{d}\mu(u,w,b),\,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})\}

in the case
\sigma=\mathrm{ReLU}
and show
\mathcal{B}
can be endowed with a norm

\displaystyle\forall f\in\mathcal{B},\quad\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\coloneqq\inf\{\int|u|(\|w\|+|b|)\mathrm{d}\mu,\,\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}),\,f=F_{\mu}\},

for which it is a Banach space, continuously embedded in the space of Lipschitz functions.
In fact,
\mathcal{B}
is the smallest Banach space containing all single hidden layer neural networks of finite width
[
WW22
, Thm.3.7 and Thm.3.8]
and it is thus dense in the set of continuous functions provided with the compact-open topology
[
Cyb89
]
. Note however that the case of convolutional or attention layers is different as the maps
F_{\mu}
defined by
eq.

1
are then equivariant by translation of the input images or permutation of the tokens respectively.

In our case, due to
1
on
\phi
, we have that if
f=F_{\mu}
is given by
eq.

1
then
\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}}\leq\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu)
and the second-order moments
\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu)
will be a key ingredient in controlling the training dynamic of our model (
e.g.
by
eq.

4
). Finally, note that
eq.

1
is not the only way to represent neural networks of arbitrary width and
[
WW22
]
propose various equivalent representations of the functional space
(\mathcal{B},\|.\|_{\mathcal{B}})
.

1.2
Mean-field
\mathrm{NODEs}

We proceed then to the definition of
Neural ODEs (
\mathrm{NODEs}
)
modeling ResNets whose depth tends to infinity with a proper rescaling of the residual layers
[
Che+18
]
. Our
\mathrm{NODE}
model is an ODE whose velocity field (or
residual
) belongs to the class of mappings parameterized by measure defined in
eq.

1
. Similar models of “mean-field
\mathrm{NODEs}
” or “mean-field limit of ResNets” were studied by
[
Lu+20
,
Din+22
,
Iso23
]
.

Definition 1.1

(
\mathrm{NODE}
)
.

For a family of probability measures
\mu=\{\mu(.|s)\}_{s\in[0,1]}\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)^{[0,1]}
and input
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define the output of the
\mathrm{NODE}
model as
\mathrm{NODE}_{\mu}(x)\coloneqq x_{\mu}(1)
where
(x_{\mu}(s))_{s\in[0,1]}
satisfies the
Forward ODE
:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}x_{\mu}(s)=F_{\mu(.|s)}(x_{\mu}(s)),\quad x_{\mu}(0)=x.

(2)

When there is no ambiguity, we simply note
x(s)
.

The parameter set
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)

To justify the well-posedness of
eq.

2
it is first necessary to define the adequate set of parameters we will consider. Given a topological space
Z
, we define
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times Z)
as the set of probability measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}([0,1]\times Z)
whose marginal w.r.t.
[0,1]
is the Lebesgue measure
\mathrm{Leb}([0,1])
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times Z)\coloneqq\left\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}([0,1]\times Z)\;:\;\pi^{1}_{\#}\mu=\mathrm{Leb}([0,1])\right\}.

Given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega))
, using a result of disintegration of measures
[
ABM14
, Thm.4.2.4]
, there exists a
\mathrm{d}s
-a.e. uniquely determined family of probability measure
\mu(.|s)\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(Z)
such that for every measurable
f:[0,1]\times Z\to\mathbb{R}
:

s\in[0,1]\mapsto\int_{Z}f(s,z)\mathrm{d}\mu(z|s)
is measurable and
\int_{[0,1]\times Z}f(s,z)\mathrm{d}\mu(s,z)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{Z}f(s,z)\mathrm{d}\mu(z|s)\mathrm{d}s
.

In the following, we will consider as parameters probability measures
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Therefore, every parameter
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
is naturally associated with a (almost everywhere uniquely defined) family of probability measures
\{\mu(.|s)\}_{s\in[0,1]}
.
We will provide this set of parameters with a modification of the Wasserstein-
2
distance
[
Vil09
,
San15
]
that takes into account the marginal constraint by considering a restriction of Kantorovitch’s original optimal coupling problem to the set of couplings that are the identity on the first variable
s\in[0,1]
. The solution of this new optimization problem induces the
Conditional Optimal Transport
(COT) distance on the parameter set
[
HHT23
]
.

Well-posedness of
\mathrm{NODEs}

The following assumption on
\phi
will be sufficient to show the well-posedness of
eq.

2
for any parameter
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. This is the content of
1.1
.

Assumption 1
.

Assume
\phi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is measurable and

1.

(quadratic growth) grows at most quadratically w.r.t.
\omega
and linearly w.r.t.
x
: there exists a constant
C
s.t.

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\omega\in\Omega,\quad\|\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq C(1+\|x\|)(1+\|\omega\|^{2})\,.

2.

(local Lipschitzness) is locally Lipschitz with respect to
x
with a Lipschitz constant that grows at most quadratically with
\omega
: for every
R\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C(R)
s.t.

\displaystyle\forall x,x^{\prime}\in B(0,R),\,\forall\omega\in\Omega,\quad\|\phi(\omega,x)-\phi(\omega,x^{\prime})\|\leq C(R)(1+\|\omega\|^{2})\|x-x^{\prime}\|\,.

Proposition 1.1

(Well-posedness of the flow)
.

Assume
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
and
\phi
satisfies
1
. Then for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists a unique weak solution to
eq.

2
, that is an absolutely continuous path
(x(s))_{s\in[0,1]}
such that for every
s\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle x(s)=x+\int_{0}^{s}F_{\mu(.|r)}(x(r))\mathrm{d}r.

(3)

Proof.

The result follows Caratheodory’s theorem for the existence and uniqueness of absolutely continuous solutions
[
Hal69
, Sec.I.5]
. Indeed, given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
the map
(s,x)\mapsto F_{\mu(.|s)}(x)
is measurable w.r.t.
s
and, thanks to
1
(local Lipschitzness), locally Lipschitz w.r.t.
x
with a Lipschitz constant that is integrable w.r.t.
s
. Moreover, the solutions of
eq.

3
are defined up to time
s=1
thanks to the growth assumption in
1
, and if
C
is the growth constant we get the following bound on the solution:

\displaystyle\forall s\in[0,1],\quad\|x(s)\|\leq\exp(C(1+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu)))(\|x(0)\|+C(1+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu))).

(4)

1.3
Supervised learning problem

We consider training our
\mathrm{NODE}
model for a supervised learning task. That is, given a data distribution
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}\ni(x,y)\sim\mathcal{D}
and loss
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we associate to a parameterization
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
the risk:

\displaystyle L(\mu)\coloneqq\ \mathbb{E}_{x,y}\ell(\mathrm{NODE}_{\mu}(x),y)=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\ell(x_{\mu}(1),y).

(5)

In the following, we assume the data distribution
\mathcal{D}
has compact support and
\ell
is a smooth loss. In particular, remark that in the case of an empirical data distribution
\mathcal{D}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{(x_{i},y_{i})}
, we recover the empirical risk

\displaystyle L(\mu)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell(\mathrm{NODE}_{\mu}(x_{i}),y_{i}).

Training the
\mathrm{NODE}
model then amounts to find a parameterization
\mu
which minimizes the risk defined in
eq.

5

i.e.
to solve the following
risk minimization
task:

\text{Find}\quad\mu^{*}\in\argmin_{\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)}L(\mu)\,.

In this work, we consider solving the above risk minimization task by performing gradient flow over the parameter
\mu
. We are interested in providing sufficient conditions to ensure that such a gradient flow can “efficiently train” our
\mathrm{NODE}

i.e.
finds a minimizer
\mu^{*}
of the risk.

1.4
Related works and contributions

Due to the popularity and performance of the ResNet architecture, many works have studied its training dynamic and the convergence of this training dynamic toward a global minimizer of the risk.

ResNets and
\mathrm{NODEs}
of finite width

[
ALS19
,
Du+19
,
LZB20
]
give convergence results for the training of deep neural networks with gradient descent and their results can be applied to ResNets. However, their condition for convergence depends on the ResNet’s depth and their result can therefore not be applied to infinitely deep models of
\mathrm{NODEs}
.
[
Mar+23
]
give local convergence results for the training of
\mathrm{NODEs}
based on a local Polyak-Łojasiewicz (P-Ł) condition. They assume a model of finite width and their result therefore does not hold in the mean field limit where residuals are of the form
eq.

1
.
They also consider parameter initializations that are Lipschitz w.r.t. depth which is not consistent with applications where the parameters are initialized at random, independently at each layer. As a comparison
eq.

1
models residual of both finite and infinite width and we only assume the family
\{\mu(.|s)\}_{s\in[0,1]}
is measurable w.r.t.
s\in[0,1]

\mathrm{NODEs}
with linear parameterization of the residuals

Relying on a similar local P-Ł analysis,
[
BPV22
]
show a local convergence result for
\mathrm{NODEs}
where the residuals are linear functions on a Hilbert space of arbitrary dimension. For SHL residuals this amounts to only training the outer weights and keeping the inner weights unchanged. While preserving the expressivity of the model, one can see that this assumption does not model the way ResNets are trained in practice.
It also simplifies the analysis by naturally providing the set of residuals with a Hilbert space structure.
In contrast,
our model allows for residuals that are not necessarily linear w.r.t. their parameters and encompasses (among other) the case of SHL residuals where both the outer and inner weights are trained.
This is done at the cost of considering the parameter set
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
which has no Hilbert space structure and is a positively curved metric space when provided with the Conditional OT distance
[
AGS08
, Sec.7.3]
.
Another notable difference is that in
[
BPV22
]
the risk is shown to admit no spurious critical points (saddles, local minima, …) whereas in this work, the risk may admit saddles whenever
\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi
is not surjective. This is for example happening for the SHL architecture defined by
eq.

32
when the feature distribution is not sufficiently spread.

\mathrm{NODEs}
in the mean-field limit

Some works have proposed models for ResNets of infinite depth similar to
1.1
.
[
EMW21
]
study properties of the functional space induced by considering the flow of functions of the form
eq.

1
and define a notion of norm which they use to provide bounds on the Rademacher complexity of this class of function.

]

Closer to our work are the works of
[

,

,

]
studying gradient flow dynamics for the minimization of the risk

for the ResNet model of

As a comparison, a key contribution of our work is to provide the parameter set with the appropriate metric structure allowing us to identify the gradient flow equation, derived formally by

with a

of the risk. In this direction, closest to our work is the recent paper of
[

]

Though the work of
[
Iso23

of the risk to define its differential in the space of parameters and to define the gradient-flow equation. This semi-convexity is obtained by adding a positive regularization term to the risk whereas we show convergence for the
unregularized
risk (
eq.

5

For this reason, the two convergence results are fundamentally different:

and study its properties.
Importantly, the characterization of absolutely continuous curves in the metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega),d)
will be used in

.

In the rest of this work, we will assume for simplicity that
\Omega
is the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{p}
for some
p\geq 1
. However, the presented results could probably be adapted to the case where
\Omega
is a smooth manifold embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. In particular, we will extensively use the fact that
\Omega
is a complete, separable metric space.
We recall the definition of the
Wasserstein

\mathcal{W}_{2}
distance over the space
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
, obtained as the solution to Kantorovitch’s optimal transport problem:

\displaystyle\forall\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{p}),\quad\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\coloneqq\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\right)^{1/2},

(6)

where
\Gamma(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
is the set of
couplings
between
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
, that is the set of probability measures
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{p}\times\mathbb{R}^{p})
respecting the marginal conditions
\pi^{1}_{\#}\gamma=\mu
and
\pi^{2}_{\#}\gamma=\mu^{\prime}
. We note
\Gamma_{o}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\subset\Gamma(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
the subset of
optimal couplings
achieving the equality in
eq.

6
. We refer to the books of
[
Vil09
,
San15
]
for further properties of the Wasserstein distance.

2.1
Conditional Optimal Transport distance

The Conditional Optimal Transport distance
d

is a modification of the Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{W}_{2}
with the supplementary constraint that the transport plan should preserve the marginal over
[0,1]
. This constraint is introduced to closely model the training dynamic of ResNets where the gradients are computed over the weights of each layer independently. For this purpose, it is natural to define a “layer-wise-
L^{2}
” Wasserstein distance, that is a
L^{2}
-distance over the set of families of probability measures in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
index over
s\in[0,1]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Conditional OT distance)
.

Consider the function
d:\left(\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)\right)^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by:

\displaystyle d(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{1}\mathcal{W}_{2}\left(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{1/2}.

Then,
d
defines a metric on
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
.

Proof.

One essentially needs to justify the existence of the integral in the definition of
d
. That
d
is a metric then follows from the properties of the Wasserstein and
L^{2}
metrics respectively.

For a complete separable metric space
\Omega
and Borel probability measures
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega)
it is known
[
Vil09
, Thm.5.10]
that the Monge-Kantorovitch problem admits the dual formulation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\sup\{\int_{\Omega}\varphi\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\Omega}\psi\mathrm{d}\nu\}

where the supremum is taken over all pairs
(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{C}_{b}(\Omega)
such that
\varphi(x)+\psi(y)\leq\|x-y\|^{2}
.
We also have the alternate formulation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\Omega)}\{\int_{\Omega}\varphi\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\Omega}\varphi^{c}\mathrm{d}\nu\},

where for
\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
the
c
-transform
\varphi^{c}
of
\varphi
is defined as
[
San15
, Def.1.10]
:

\displaystyle\forall\omega\in\Omega,\quad\varphi^{c}(\omega)\coloneqq\inf_{\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega}\|\omega^{\prime}-\omega\|^{2}-\varphi(\omega^{\prime}).

Consider
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geq 0}
a sequence of functions in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(\Omega)
such that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\Omega)
we can find a subsequence
m(n)
with
\varphi_{m(n)}\to\varphi
for the compact-open topology (uniform convergence on compact subsets) and
\|\varphi_{m(n)}\|_{\infty}
is uniformly bounded. Then we also have
\varphi_{m(n)}^{c}\to\varphi^{c}
uniformly on compact subsets with
\|\varphi^{c}_{m(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\varphi_{m(n)}\|_{\infty}
uniformly bounded, whence:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\nu)^{2}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\{\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{n}\mathrm{d}\mu+\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{n}^{c}\mathrm{d}\nu\}.

Thus, for
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
, the application
s\mapsto\mathcal{W}_{2}\left(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s)\right)^{2}
is measurable as it can be expressed as the supremum of countably many measurable functions.
∎

Alternatively, the distance
d
can be viewed as an optimal transport distance with the additional constraint that the transport plans should be the identity on the first marginals.
This new formulation will show itself convenient for calculations and will in particular allow to estimate from above the distance
d
easily.
Given
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
it is convenient to work with the two following sets of “couplings” between
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega^{2}),\ \gamma(.|s)\in\Gamma(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))\ \text{for $\mathrm{d}s$-a.e. $s\in[0,1]$}\},

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\mathrm{diag}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\gamma\in\Gamma(\mu,\mu^{\prime}),\ \int f(s,s^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\gamma(s,\omega,s^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})=\int_{0}^{1}f(s,s)\mathrm{d}s\ \forall f\in\mathcal{C}([0,1]^{2})\}.

Note that these to sets are closely related as if
\gamma\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
then
\tilde{\gamma}\coloneqq(\pi^{1},\pi^{2},\pi^{1},\pi^{3})_{\#}\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{diag}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
and conversely, if
\tilde{\gamma}\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{diag}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
then
\gamma\coloneqq(\pi^{1},\pi^{2},\pi^{4})_{\#}\tilde{\gamma}\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
. In both cases, we have for any measurable
f:\Omega^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega^{2}}f(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\gamma(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})=\int_{([0,1]\times\Omega)^{2}}f(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\tilde{\gamma}(s,\omega,s^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}).

(7)

In the same way the Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
can be obtained of the solution of a minimization problem over the set
\Gamma(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
(
eq.

6
),
the Conditional OT distance
d
can be obtained as the solution of minimization problems over the sets
\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
and
\Gamma^{\mathrm{diag}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
then:

\displaystyle d(\mu,\mu^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle=\min_{\gamma\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\min_{\gamma\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{diag}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\int_{([0,1]\times\Omega)^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma(s,\omega,s^{\prime},\omega^{\prime}).

We note respectively
\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{o}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
and
\Gamma^{\mathrm{diag}}_{o}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
the set of optimal couplings in both minimization problems. Then for
\gamma\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{o}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
we have for
\mathrm{d}s
-a.e.
s\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle\gamma(.|s)\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))\quad\text{i.e.}\quad\int_{\Omega^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma(\omega,\omega^{\prime}|s)=\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))^{2}.

Proof.

Our proof technique is similar to the one of
[
HHT23
, Prop.3.3]
and relies on the possibility of choosing an optimal transport plan
\gamma(.|s)\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))
for every
s\in[0,1]
in a measurable way.

We show equality with the first minimization problem on
\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
, equality between the two minimization problems then comes from
eq.

7
. Assume there exists a Borel map
\gamma:s\mapsto\gamma(.|s)\in\mathcal{P}(\Omega^{2})
(where
\mathcal{P}(\Omega^{2})
is provided with the narrow topology) such that
\gamma(.|s)\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))
for every
s\in[0,1]
. With such a map one can define a Borel probability measure that we also note
\gamma
over
[0,1]\times\Omega
which is the measure whose disintegration w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
is
\{\gamma(.|s)\}_{s\in[0,1]}
. In other words the measure
\gamma
is defined as:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega^{2}}f(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\gamma(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega^{2}}f(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\gamma(\omega,\omega^{\prime}|s)\mathrm{d}s,\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}([0,1]\times\Omega^{2}).

Such
\gamma
will be a solution to our first optimization problem as we have:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})=d(\mu,\mu^{\prime})^{2}\leq\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime}).

To show the existence of such
\gamma
we use a measurable selection result, that is considering the set-valued mapping
s\in[0,1]\mapsto\Gamma_{o}(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))\subset\mathcal{P}(\Omega^{2})
we show it admits a measurable section. Consider the set:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{*}\coloneqq\{(s,\gamma),\,\gamma\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))\}\subset[0,1]\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega^{2}).

Using
[
Bog07
, Thm.6.9.6]
, as for every
s\in[0,1]
the set
\Gamma_{o}(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))
is narrowly compact, it is sufficient to show that
\mathcal{G}^{*}\in\mathcal{B}([0,1]\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega^{2}))
. Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be dense in
\mathcal{C}_{b}(\Omega)
for the compact-open topology. Then, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
as the mapping
(s,\gamma)\mapsto\int_{\Omega^{2}}f_{n}\mathrm{d}\gamma-\int_{\Omega}f_{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(.|s)
is measurable, so are the sets:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(s,\gamma),\,\int_{\Omega^{2}}f_{n}(\omega)\mathrm{d}\gamma(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|s)\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{(s,\gamma),\,\int_{\Omega^{2}}f_{n}(\omega^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\gamma(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=\int_{\Omega}f_{n}(\omega^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}(\omega^{\prime}|s)\}.

Also, as the mapping
(s,\gamma)\mapsto\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))^{2}-\int_{\Omega^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma
is measurable by
2.1
, so is the set:

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{o}\coloneqq\{(s,\gamma),\,\int_{\Omega^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma=\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu(.|s),\mu^{\prime}(.|s))^{2}\}\in\mathcal{B}([0,1]\times\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega^{2})).

Finally we have that
\mathcal{G}^{*}=\mathcal{G}_{o}\cap\left(\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{G}_{n}\cap\mathcal{G}_{n}^{\prime}\right)
is a Borel set, which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.1

(Comparison with
\mathcal{W}_{2}
)
.

Note that, for
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
, we have that
\Gamma^{\mathrm{diag}}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\subset\Gamma(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
. Hence from the previous result, it follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\leq d(\mu,\mu^{\prime})

and the topology induced by
d
on
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
is stronger than the Wasserstein topology. It is in fact strictly stronger and,
for example, the sequence
\mu_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\delta_{(-1)^{\lfloor 2ns\rfloor}}\mathrm{d}s
and the measure
\mu=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}(\delta_{1}+\delta_{-1})\mathrm{d}s
in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\mathbb{R})
are such that
\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu_{n},\mu)\to 0
but
d(\mu_{n},\mu)\geq 1
.

The following result states that the metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega),d)
is complete.

Proposition 2.3

(Completeness)
.

(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega),d)
is a complete metric space.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of completeness of the Wasserstein space
\mathcal{P}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
(see
[
Vil09
, Thm.6.18]
)

Let
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega))
. Then for any
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)

\displaystyle d(\nu,\mu_{n})\leq d(\nu,\mu_{0})+\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}d(\mu_{i},\mu_{i-1})\leq d(\nu,\mu_{0})+\sum_{i\geq 1}d(\mu_{i},\mu_{i-1})<\infty,

implying that the sequence
(\mu_{n})
is bounded. Hence it is tight and by Prokhorov’s theorem, it admits a subsequence
(\mu_{n_{k}})_{k\geq 0}
converging narrowly to some
\mu_{\infty}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Then by narrow-lower-semi-continuity of
d
(
2.1
) we have for every
l\geq 0
:

\displaystyle d(\mu_{\infty},\mu_{n_{l}})\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}d(\mu_{n_{k}},\mu_{n_{l}})

and by taking the
\limsup
w.r.t.
l
:

\displaystyle\limsup_{l\to\infty}d(\mu_{\infty},\mu_{n_{l}})\leq\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\to\infty\\
l\to\infty\end{subarray}}d(\mu_{n_{k}},\mu_{n_{l}})=0.

Hence
(\mu_{n_{k}})

d
-converges to
\mu_{\infty}
, implying that the whole sequence
(\mu_{n})

d
-converges to
\mu_{\infty}
.
∎

Lemma 2.1

(narrow-lower-semi-continuity of
d
)
.

Let
(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and
(\nu_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be sequences in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
such that
(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}(\mu,\nu)
narrowly for some
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Then:

\displaystyle d(\mu,\nu)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}d(\mu_{n},\nu_{n}).

Proof.

Up to extraction of a subsequence one can consider
d(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})\to\liminf d(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})
. Then for every
n\geq 0
consider some
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma_{o}^{\mathrm{diag}}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})
. In particular
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})
and by
[
Vil09
, Lem.4.4]
the sequence
(\gamma_{n})
is tight. Hence it admits a subsequence
(\gamma_{n_{k}})_{k\geq 0}
narrowly converging to some
\gamma
which is in
\Gamma^{\mathrm{diag}}(\mu,\nu)
by the properties of narrow convergence. Thus applying
[
Vil09
, Lem.4.3]
and using the characterization of
d
in
2.2
:

\displaystyle d(\mu,\nu)^{2}\leq\int_{([0,1]\times\Omega)^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{([0,1]\times\Omega)^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma_{n_{k}}=\liminf_{n\to\infty}d(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})^{2},

from which the result follows.
∎

2.2
Dynamical formulation of Conditional Optimal Transport

We analyze here the properties of absolutely continuous curves in the metric space
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
provided with the Conditional OT metric. Similarly to the (unconstrained) Wasserstein metric, we show that absolutely continuous curves obey a certain continuity equation. This characterization will be crucial to define the gradient flow equation used in the training of our
\mathrm{NODE}
model.

Absolutely continuous curves for the Wasserstein distance
\mathcal{W}_{2}

For
T>0
, consider
I=(0,T)
an open interval and
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
a family of probability measures over the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Given a Borel velocity field
v:(t,x)\in I\times\mathbb{R}^{p}\mapsto v_{t}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
such that
\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\|v_{t}\|\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathrm{d}t<\infty
, we say that
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
satisfies the continuity equation
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\mathrm{div}(v_{t}\mu_{t})
in the weak sense if:

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{p}}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi(t,x)+\langle\nabla\varphi(t,x),v_{t}(x)\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t=0,\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{p}).

(8)

Equivalently (
[
San15
, Prop.4.2]
), when the mapping
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
is narrowly continuous, this amounts to have that for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
the map
t\mapsto\mu_{t}(\varphi)\coloneqq\int\varphi\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}
is absolutely continuous and verifies:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}(\varphi)=\int\langle\nabla\varphi,v_{t}\rangle\mathrm{d}\mu_{t},\quad\text{for $\mathrm{d}t$-a.e. $t\in I$.}

An important property of the Wasserstein space
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
endowed with the distance
\mathcal{W}_{2}
is the characterization of absolutely continuous curves: a narrowly continuous curve
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
is absolutely continuous in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
if and only if it is solution to the continuity equation
eq.

8
for some velocity field
v
with
\int_{I}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\mathrm{d}t<\infty

[
AGS08
, Thm.8.3.1]
. We refer to the book by
[
AGS08
]
for a detailed study of absolutely continuous curves in
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{p}),\mathcal{W}_{2})
.

Absolutely continuous curves for the Conditional OT distance

Similarly to the characterization of absolutely continuous curves in the Wasserstein space
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
, an adaptation of
[
AGS08
, Thm.8.3.1]
provides an analogous characterization of absolutely continuous curves in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
provided with the Conditional OT distance
d
. This characterization allows us to (formally) provide the metric space
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega),d)
with a kind of “ differential structure” by seeing tangent vectors as velocity fields. This identification will be crucial for defining the gradient flow equation which will take the form of a continuity equation with an appropriate velocity field (
3.2
).

Proposition 2.4

(adapted from
[
AGS08
]
, Thm.8.3.1)
.

Assume
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{p}
. Let
I=(0,T)
for some
T>0
and
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
an absolutely continuous curve in
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Then there exists a unique Borel velocity field
v:(t,s,\omega)\in I\times[0,1]\times\Omega\mapsto v_{t}(s,\omega)\in\Omega
such that for a.e.
t\in I
:

\displaystyle v_{t}\in L^{2}(\mu_{t}),\quad\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\leq\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|,

and
\mu
is a weak solution of the continuity equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\mathrm{div}((0,v_{t})\mu_{t})=0\quad\text{over $I\times[0,1]\times\Omega$.}

(9)

We will refer to such
v_{t}
as the
tangent velocity field
of the curve
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
.
Conversely, if
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
is a narrowly continuous curve satisfying
eq.

9
for some Borel velocity field
v_{t}
with
\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\in L^{1}(I)
, then
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
is absolutely continuous and
|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}|\leq\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}
for a.e.
t\in I
.

Proof.

Part 1:
AC curve
\Rightarrow
Continuity equation.

Note that this part is the easiest as
d
-absolute continuity implies
\mathcal{W}_{2}
-absolute continuity for which the result is well-known, originally proven in
[
AGS08
, Thm.8.3.1]
.
Therefore we here only adapt this proof to our specific setting.

Up to reparameterization, one can assume without loss of generality that
\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|\in L^{\infty}(I)
.

First we show that for
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(I\times[0,1]\times\Omega)
the map
t\mapsto\mu_{t}(\varphi)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\varphi\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}
is absolutely continuous. Indeed, for
t,u\in I
, introducing a coupling
\gamma_{t,u}\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{o}(\mu_{t},\mu_{u})
we have:

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{t}(\varphi)-\mu_{u}(\varphi)\right|\leq\left|\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega^{2}}(\varphi(s,\omega)-\varphi(s,\omega^{\prime}))\mathrm{d}\gamma_{t,u}(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\|\nabla_{\omega}\varphi\|_{\infty}d(\mu_{t},\mu_{u}),

from which absolute continuity follows. Then considering the map:

\displaystyle H(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\|\nabla_{\omega}\varphi(s,\omega)\|&\text{if $\omega=\omega^{\prime}$},\\
&\\
\frac{|\varphi(s,\omega)-\varphi(s,\omega^{\prime})|}{\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|}&\text{else},\end{array}\right.

we have for every
t,u\in I
:

\displaystyle\frac{|\mu_{t}(\varphi)-\mu_{u}(\varphi)|}{|t-u|}\leq\frac{1}{|t-u|}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega^{2}}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|H(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\gamma_{t,u}(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\leq\frac{d(\mu_{t},\mu_{u})}{|t-u|}\|H\|_{L^{2}(\gamma_{t,u})}

As
u\to t
we have
d(\mu_{u},\mu_{t})\to 0
and by the properties of
L^{2}
spaces
[
CD15
, Prop.3.11]
we can take a sequence
u_{n}\to t
such that
\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu_{u_{n}}(.|s),\mu_{t}(.|s))\to 0
for
\mathrm{d}s
-a.e.
s\in[0,1]
. This implies for those
s\in[0,1]
that
\mu_{u_{n}}(.|s)\to\mu_{t}(.|s)
narrowly and that
\gamma_{t,u_{n}}(.|s)\to\gamma(.|s)\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu_{t}(.|s),\mu_{t}(.|s))
, i.e. the trivial transport plan
\gamma(.|s)=(\mathrm{Id},\mathrm{Id})_{\#}\mu_{t}(.|s)
.
Thus we have that
\gamma_{t,u_{n}}\to(\pi^{1},\pi^{2},\pi^{2})_{\#}\mu_{t}
narrowly since, by Lebesgue’s theorem, given a bounded continuous function
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}([0,1]\times\Omega\times\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\int f\mathrm{d}\gamma_{t,u_{n}}=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\Omega^{2}}f(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\mathrm{d}\gamma_{t,u_{n}}(\omega,\omega^{\prime}|s)\right)\mathrm{d}s\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\Omega}f(s,\omega,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(\omega|s)\right)\mathrm{d}s.

Hence, at a point where
t\mapsto\mu_{t}
is metrically differentiable:

\displaystyle\limsup_{u\to t}\frac{|\mu_{t}(\varphi)-\mu_{u}(\varphi)|}{|t-u|}\leq\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|\|\nabla_{\omega}\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}.

Consider
\bm{\mu}=\int_{I}\mu_{t}\mathrm{d}t\in\mathcal{P}(I\times[0,1]\times\Omega)
the measure whose disintegration w.r.t. Lebesgue’s measure on
I
is
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
. Then for
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(I\times[0,1]\times\Omega)
we have:

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi(t,s,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(s,\omega)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{I}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\frac{\varphi(t,s,\omega)-\varphi(t-h,s,\omega)}{h}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(s,\omega)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\int_{I}\frac{1}{h}\left(\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\varphi(t,s,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(s,\omega)-\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\varphi(t,s,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t-h}(s,\omega)\right)\mathrm{d}t.

Thus by the previous inequality and Fatou’s lemma:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{I}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi(t,s,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(s,\omega)\mathrm{d}t\right|\leq\left(\int_{I}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{I\times[0,1]\times\Omega}\|\nabla_{\omega}\varphi(t,s,\omega)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}(t,s,\omega)\right)^{1/2}.

Consider the subspace
V\coloneqq\{\nabla_{\omega}\varphi,\,\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(I\times[0,1]\times\Omega)\}
and
\mathcal{V}
its closure in
L^{2}(I\times[0,1]\times\Omega,\bm{\mu})
. Then by the previous inequality the linear functional
\mathcal{A}:V\to\mathbb{R}
defined by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\nabla_{\omega}\varphi)\coloneqq-\int_{I\times[0,1]\times\Omega}\partial_{t}\varphi(t,s,\omega)\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}(t,s,\omega)

is continuous on
V
and thus, by Hahn-Banach’s theorem, can be extended to a unique continuous linear functional on
\mathcal{V}
. Therefore, by Lax-Milgram’s theorem, the minimization problem:

\displaystyle\min\left\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{p+1}}\|w(t,s,\omega)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}(t,s,\omega)-\mathcal{A}(w),\,w\in\mathcal{V}\right\}

admits a unique solution
v\in\mathcal{V}
which is characterized by the property that:

\displaystyle\int_{I\times\mathbb{R}^{p+1}}\langle v(t,s,\omega),\nabla_{\omega}\varphi(t,s,\omega)\rangle\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}(t,s,\omega)=\mathcal{A}(\nabla_{\omega}\varphi),\quad\forall\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(I\times[0,1]\times\Omega),

which is the desired continuity equation by definition of
\mathcal{A}
.

Finally, let
(\nabla_{\omega}\varphi_{n})\subset V
be a sequence converging to
v\in L^{2}(\bm{\mu})
. Considering an interval
J\subset I
and some
\eta\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(J)
with
0\leq\eta\leq 1
we have by the previous arguments:

\displaystyle\int_{I\times[0,1]\times\Omega}\eta(t)\|v(t,s,\omega)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}(t,s,\omega)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{I\times[0,1]\times\Omega}\eta\langle v,\nabla_{\omega}\varphi_{n}\rangle\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{A}(\nabla_{\omega}(\eta\varphi_{n}))

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{J}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{J\times[0,1]\times\Omega}\|\nabla_{\omega}\varphi_{n}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{J}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{J\times[0,1]\times\Omega}\|v\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\bm{\mu}\right)^{1/2}.

Hence approximating the characteristic function of
J
with such an
\eta
we get:

\displaystyle\int_{J}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\|v_{t}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{J}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

implying
\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\leq\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|
for a.e.
t\in I
.

Part 2:
Continuity equation
\Rightarrow
AC curve.

This part of the proof is new as, according to
[
AGS08
, Thm.8.3.1]
, the continuity equation only ensures
\mathcal{W}_{2}
-absolute continuity as explained in
2.1
. We show here that the specific form of the velocity field ensures
d
-absolute continuity.

For
(t,s)\in I\times[0,1]
, we note
v_{t,s}
the Borel vector field
v_{t,s}:\omega\in\Omega\mapsto v_{t}(s,\omega)
. Note that by Jensen’s inequality:

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{0}^{1}\|v_{t,s}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t}(.|s))}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\leq\int_{I}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\mathrm{d}t<+\infty,

and we have that for
\mathrm{d}s
-a.e.
s\in[0,1]
,
t\mapsto\|v_{t,s}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t}(.|s))}\in L^{1}(I)
. Also if
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}(I\times\Omega)
and
\chi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}([0,1])
then by definition of the continuity equation:

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+\langle\nabla_{\omega}\varphi,v_{t,s}\rangle\right)\chi(s)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(.|s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t=0.

Hence if
J\subset[0,1]
is an interval, approaching the characteristic function of
J
with
\chi
we get:

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{J}\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+\langle\nabla_{\omega}\varphi,v_{t,s}\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(.|s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t=0,

and hence for
\mathrm{d}s
-a.e.
s\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle\int_{I}\int_{\Omega}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi+\langle\nabla_{\omega}\varphi,v_{t,s}\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(.|s)\mathrm{d}t=0.

Now if we consider
(\varphi_{n})
a countable dense sequence in
\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(I\times\Omega)
endowed with the usual topology then we can find a set
\Lambda\subset[0,1]
of full Lebesgue’s measure such that for every
s\in\Lambda
the above equation holds for every test function
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}(I\times\Omega)
. In other words we have shown that, for
\mathrm{d}s
-a.e.
s\in[0,1]
,
\mu_{t}(.|s)
solves the continuity equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu_{t}(.|s)+\mathrm{div}(v_{t,s}\mu_{t}(.|s))=0\quad\text{over $I\times\Omega$.}

Note that, without loss of generality, we can consider the curve
(\mu_{t}(.|s))_{t\in I}
to be narrowly continuous. Indeed, as it is a solution of the continuity equation we know that the curve
(\mu_{t}(.|s))_{t\in I}
admits a narrowly continuous representative
\tilde{\mu}_{t}(.|s)

[
AGS08
, Lem.8.1.2]
and that this representative is characterized by that for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
and every
t\in I
:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{t}(.|s)(\varphi)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\left(\chi^{\prime}(u)\varphi+\chi(u)\langle\nabla_{\omega}\varphi,v_{u,s}\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{u}(.|s)\mathrm{d}u,

where
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(I)
is any function such that
\chi=0
on a neighbourhood of
0
and
\chi(u)=1
for
u\geq t
(the definition does not depend on
\chi
by definition of the continuity equation). Then it follows that for any
t\in I
and any
f\in\mathcal{C}_{b}([0,1]\times\Omega)
the map
s\mapsto\tilde{\mu}_{t}(.|s)(f)
is measurable and integrating w.r.t.
s
we get that
\tilde{\mu}_{t}\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{\mu}_{t}(.|s)\mathrm{d}s
defines a probability measure over
[0,1]\times\Omega
whose disintegration is
\{\tilde{\mu}_{t}(.|s)\}_{s\in[0,1]}
. Moreover for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1}([0,1]\times\Omega)
we have:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mu}_{t}(\varphi)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left(\chi^{\prime}(u)\varphi+\chi(u)\langle\nabla_{\omega}\varphi,v_{u,s}\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{u}(.|s)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}u=\mu_{t}(\varphi)

and hence in fact the equality
\tilde{\mu}_{t}=\mu_{t}
.

Then using
[
AGS08
, Thm.8.3.1]
with the assumption that
t\mapsto\mu_{t}(.|s)
is narrowly continuous we hence have that for
\mathrm{d}s
-a.e.
s\in[0,1]
the curve
t\in I\mapsto\mu_{t}(.|s)
is absolutely continuous and

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu_{t_{1}}(.|s),\mu_{t_{2}}(.|s))^{2}\leq(t_{2}-t_{1})\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}\|v_{t,s}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(.|s)\mathrm{d}t,\quad\forall t_{1}<t_{2}\in I.

Hence integrating w.r.t.
s\in[0,1]
gives:

\displaystyle d(\mu_{t_{1}},\mu_{t_{2}})^{2}\leq(t_{2}-t_{1})\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\|v_{t}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathrm{d}t,\quad\forall t_{1}<t_{2}\in I,

which shows that
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
is
d
-absolutely continuous with
\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|\leq\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}
for a.e.
t\in I
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Note that to study absolutely continuous curves, we introduce the supplementary time variable
t\geq 0
. This time variable will model the optimization time in the
3.2
of the gradient flow equation. It is not to be interverted with the
\mathrm{NODE}
flow time
s\in[0,1]
.

As a consequence of
2.4
we recover two useful results about absolutely continuous curves in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Those are stated in the following
2.2
and
2.3
. The first result is a result of approximation along absolutely continuous curves. It states that the tangent velocity field
(v_{t})_{t\in I}
defined in
2.4
indeed furnishes a first-order approximation of the curve
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
at every time
t\in I
. It will be particularly useful to differentiate quantities related to
\mu_{t}
(
3.2
and
3.3
). The second result is an application and gives the differential of the square-distance
d(\mu_{t},\mu^{\prime})^{2}
along an absolutely continuous curve
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
.

Lemma 2.2

(Adapted from
[
AGS08
, Prop.8.4.6]
)
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
be an absolutely continuous curve in
(\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega),d)
and let
v:I\times\mathbb{R}^{p+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{p}
be the unique velocity field satisfying the conclusions of
2.4
. Then for
\mathrm{d}t
-a.e.
t\in I
it holds that for any choice of
\gamma^{h}_{t}\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{o}(\mu_{t+h},\mu_{t})
:

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\left(\pi^{1},\pi^{2},\frac{1}{h}(\pi^{3}-\pi^{2})\right)_{\#}\gamma^{h}_{t}=\left(\pi^{1},\pi^{2},v_{t})\right)_{\#}\mu_{t}\quad\text{in $\mathcal{W}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega\times\Omega)$}

and

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{d(\mu_{t+h},(\mathrm{Id}+h(0,v_{t}))_{\#}\mu_{t})}{|h|}=0.

Proof.

The proof only needs to be slightly adapted from the one of
[
AGS08
, Lem.8.4.6]
but we rewrite it here for completeness.

Let
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a countable dense sequence in
\mathcal{C}^{1}_{c}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Then for
\mathrm{d}t
-a.e.
t\in I
we have
\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{1}{|h|}d(\mu_{t+h},\mu_{t})=\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|
and for every
n\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{\mu_{t+h}(\varphi_{n})-\mu_{t}(\varphi_{n})}{h}=\int\langle\nabla_{\omega}\varphi_{n},v_{t}\rangle\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}.

Introducing some
\gamma^{h}_{t}\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{o}(\mu_{t+h},\mu_{t})
we consider:

\displaystyle\nu^{h}\coloneqq\left(\pi^{1},\pi^{2},\frac{1}{h}(\pi^{3}-\pi^{2})\right)_{\#}\gamma^{h}_{t}.

Then for any sequence
(h_{n})
converging to
0
the sequence
(\nu^{h_{n}})
is tight in
\mathcal{P}([0,1]\times\Omega\times\Omega)
and we can consider a narrow limit point
\nu^{0}
. The marginal of
\nu^{h}
, and hence of
\nu^{0}
, on
[0,1]\times\Omega
is
\mu_{t}
which allows to write by disintegration
\nu^{0}=\int\nu^{0}_{s,\omega}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(s,\omega)
. Then we have for every
n\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{t+h}(\varphi_{n})-\mu_{t}(\varphi_{n})}{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\int\left(\varphi_{n}(s,\omega^{\prime})-\varphi_{n}(s,\omega)\right)\mathrm{d}\gamma^{h}_{t}(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\int\left(\varphi_{n}(s,\omega+hz)-\varphi_{n}(s,\omega)\right)\mathrm{d}\nu^{h}(s,\omega,z)

and taking the limit
h\to 0
, by Lebesgue’s theorem:

\displaystyle\int\langle\nabla_{\omega}\varphi_{n},v_{t}\rangle\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}=\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\langle z,\nabla_{\omega}\varphi_{n}(s,\omega)\rangle\mathrm{d}\nu^{0}_{s,\omega}(z)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(s,\omega).

Let us note, for
(s,\omega)\in[0,1]\times\Omega
,
\tilde{v}_{t}(s,\omega)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}z\mathrm{d}\nu^{0}_{s,\omega}(z)
the first moment of
\nu^{0}_{s,\omega}
. Then from the last equality and by a density argument it follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}((0,\tilde{v}_{t}-v_{t})\mu_{t})=0,

and in particular the continuity equation
eq.

9
is satisfied with the vector field
(0,\tilde{v}_{t})
.
Let us now show:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\|z\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu^{0}_{s,\omega}(z)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(s,\omega)\leq\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|^{2}.

Indeed we have:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\|z\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu^{0}_{s,\omega}(z)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(s,\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega\times\Omega}\|z\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu^{h}(s,\omega,z)

\displaystyle=\liminf_{h\to 0}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{1}{h^{2}}\|\omega^{\prime}-\omega\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{h}_{t}(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\liminf_{h\to 0}\frac{d(\mu_{t+h},\mu_{t})^{2}}{h^{2}}=\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|^{2}.

Whence by definition of
\tilde{v}_{t}
and Jensen’s inequality:

\displaystyle\|\tilde{v}_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\leq\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|=\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}

from which it follows that
\tilde{v}_{t}=v_{t}
in
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
because of the minimality of
\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}
and the strict convexity of the
L^{2}
-norm. Moreover the above inequality is strict whenever
\nu^{0}_{s,\omega}
is not a Dirac mass in a set of
\mu_{t}
positive measure. This implies that
\nu^{0}_{s,\omega}
is a Dirac mass for
\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}
-a.e.
(s,\omega)\in[0,1]\times\Omega
and that
\nu^{0}=(\pi^{1},\pi^{2},v_{t})_{\#}\mu_{t}
. This proves the narrow convergence of
\nu^{h}
towards the desired measure and together with the convergence of the second moments we have
\mathcal{W}_{2}
convergence.

Let us now estimate the distance between
\mu_{t+h}
and
(\pi^{1},\pi^{2}+h(0,v_{t}))_{\#}\mu_{t}
with the coupling
\gamma\coloneqq(\pi^{1},\pi^{2}+h(0,v_{t}),\pi^{3})_{\#}\gamma^{h}_{t}\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}((\pi^{1},\pi^{2}+h(0,v_{t}))_{\#}\mu_{t},\mu_{t+h})
. By the preceding result:

\displaystyle\frac{d((\pi^{1},\pi^{2}+hv_{t})_{\#}\mu_{t},\mu_{t+h})^{2}}{h^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega\times\Omega}\frac{1}{h^{2}}\|\omega+hv_{t}(s,\omega)-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma^{h}_{t}(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega\times\Omega}\|v_{t}(s,\omega)-z\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu^{h}(s,\omega,z)\xrightarrow[h\to 0]{}0.

∎

Lemma 2.3

(Adapted from
[
AGS08
, Thm.8.4.7]
)
.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
be an absolutely continuous curve in
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
, let
v:I\times[0,1]\times\omega\to\Omega
be its

and let
\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)

:

Proof.

Having shown
2.2
, the proof is the same as the one of
[

, Thm.8.4.7]
.

3
Gradient flow dynamics

To train the
\mathrm{NODE}
model of
1.1
we consider performing
Gradient Flow
on the parameter
\mu
for the risk
L
and for the Conditional OT metric described in the previous section.
However the parameter set
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)

provided with the distance
d
lacks a proper differential structure. We will thus in this section give a sense to the notion of gradient flow of
L
. First, motivated by formal computations we will introduce a definition of gradient flow that is consistent with the one proposed by
[
Che+18
]
for the training of
\mathrm{NODEs}
of finite width. Then, we will show this definition to be equivalent to the notion of
curve of maximal slope
from the theory of gradient flow in metric spaces
[
AGS08
,
San17
]
. Finally, this equivalence will allow us to show well-posedness results for the gradient flow equation.

3.1
Backward equation and adjoint variables

The computation of the gradient will make use of a new ODE linked to
eq.

3
. This ODE should be understood as running backward over the time variable
s\in[0,1]
with the initial condition at
s=1
. In the same way
eq.

3
models the processing of the data by a ResNet of infinite depth, the
adjoint variables

p
solutions to this
backward ODE
should be considered as modeling the quantities calculated when performing back-propagation over a deep ResNet.

Definition 3.1

(Adjoint variable)
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
and
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}
. Let
(x_{\mu}(s))_{s\in[0,1]}
be the solution to
eq.

2
with parameter
\mu
and
x_{\mu}(0)=x
.
Then we call
adjoint variable associated to
\mu
,
x
and
y
the solution
(p_{\mu,x,y}(s))_{s\in[0,1]}
to the
backward ODE
:

\forall s\in[0,1],\quad p_{\mu,x,y}(s)=\nabla_{x}\ell(x_{\mu}(1),y)+\int_{s}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{x}F_{\mu(.|r)}(x_{\mu}(r))^{\top}p_{\mu,x,y}(r)\mathrm{d}r.

(10)

When no ambiguity, the dependence of
p
w.r.t.
\mu
,
x
and
y
is omitted and we simply write
p(s)
.

The following proposition states the well-posedness of the backward equation under suitable assumptions on the feature map
\phi
and gives a useful representation of the adjoint variables.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
and
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}
. Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
. Then there exists a unique solution to
eq.

10
which is given by:

\forall s\in[0,1],\quad p_{\mu,x,y}(s)=\Phi_{\mu,x}(s)^{-\top}\Phi_{\mu,x}(1)^{\top}\nabla_{x}\ell(x_{\mu}(1),y).

(11)

where we define
\Phi_{\mu,x}
to be the (matrix) solution of the linear ODE:

\forall s\in[0,1],\quad\Phi_{\mu,x}(s)=\mathrm{Id}+\int_{0}^{s}\mathrm{D}_{x}F_{\mu(.|r)}(x_{\mu}(r))\Phi_{\mu,x}(r)\mathrm{d}r.

(12)

When no ambiguity we simply note
\Phi_{\mu}(s)
or even
\Phi(s)
.

Proof.

Note that
eq.

10
is non-autonomous linear ODE w.r.t. the variable
p
. Thus, the representation
eq.

11
follows from the existence and uniqueness of
\Phi
and to prove the result it suffices to show the map
s\mapsto\mathrm{D}_{x}F_{\mu(.|s)}(x(s))
is integrable.

First, as
\phi
is continuously differentiable w.r.t. with integrable differential for almost every fixed
s\in[0,1]
the map
x\mapsto F_{\mu(.|s)}(x)
is continuously differentiable with differential given by:

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}_{x}F_{\mu(.|s)}(x)=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi_{\omega}(x)\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|s).

Moreover, by continuity of
s\mapsto x(s)
the integrand
\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi_{\omega}(x(t))
is measurable and so is the map
s\mapsto\mathrm{D}_{x}F_{\mu(.|s)}(x(s))
. Finally integrability follows as
\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi_{\omega}(x(s))
has 2-growth w.r.t.
\omega
and
\int_{\Omega}\|\omega\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|s)
is integrable on
[0,1]
.
∎

The following result gives an alternate point of view on the adjoint variable
p
. Geometrically, it follows from
eq.

11
that
p
lives in the co-tangent space of the flow
x
. Therefore in the case of a general (not necessarily with finite support) data distribution
\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}})
it is convenient to see
p
as the gradient of a potential
\psi
over the variables
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Then for every
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}
the associated adjoint variable
p
can be expressed for every
s\in[0,1]
as:

p_{\mu,x,y}(s)=\nabla_{x}\psi_{\mu}(s,x_{\mu}(s),y),

(13)

where
\psi_{\mu}
is the unique solution to the transport equation:

\partial_{s}\psi_{\mu}+\langle\nabla_{x}\psi_{\mu},F_{\mu(.|s)}\rangle=0,\quad\psi_{\mu}(1,x,y)=\ell(x,y),\,\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}.

(14)

Proof.

The solution to the transport equation can be given in the characteristic form:

\forall s\in[0,1],x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\psi_{\mu}(s,x_{\mu}(s),y)=\psi_{\mu}(1,x_{\mu}(1),y)=\ell(x_{\mu}(1),y).

One can then check that the r.h.s. of
eq.

13
is indeed a solution of
eq.

10
.
∎

3.2
The gradient flow equation

We motivate here by formal computations a definition of a gradient flow equation for the risk
L
. This
adjoint sensitivity analysis
consists in using a Lagrangian form of the risk minimization problem to obtain an expression of the gradient w.r.t. the parameter
\mu
.

As the inputs are processed by our model through
eq.

2
one can consider for a parameter
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
and every time
s\in[0,1]
the distribution
\rho_{\mu}(.|s)\coloneqq(x\mapsto x_{\mu}(s),\mathrm{Id})_{\#}\mathcal{D}
of the data at time
s
. Then
\{\rho_{\mu}(.|s)\}_{s\in[0,1]}
is a narrowly continuous solution to the continuity equation:

\partial_{s}\rho^{*}_{\mu}(.|s)+\mathrm{div}_{x}(F_{\mu(.|s)}\rho^{*}_{\mu}(.|s))=0

(15)

and the risk associated to
\mu
is
L(\mu)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}}\ell(x,y)\mathrm{d}\rho_{\mu}(x,y|1)=\rho_{\mu}(.|1)(\ell)
.
We introduce a Lagrange multiplier
\psi
to penalize the above continuity equation. For a parameter
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
, a measurable family
\rho=\{\rho(.|s)\}_{s\in[0,1]}
of probability measures over
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
and a smooth test function
\psi:[0,1]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}\to\mathbb{R}
, consider the lagrangian
\mathcal{L}
defined as:

\mathcal{L}(\mu,\rho,\psi)\coloneqq\rho(.|1)(\ell)-\rho(.|1)(\psi(1))-\rho(.|0)(\psi(0))+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}}\left(\partial_{s}\psi+\langle\nabla_{x}\psi,F_{\mu(.|s)}\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}\rho(.|s)\mathrm{d}s.

(16)

Using the definition of
F
and inverting integrals, the variation of
\mathcal{L}
w.r.t.
\mu
is given for every
\rho
and
\psi
by:

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\rho,\psi):(s,\omega)\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}}\langle\nabla_{x}\psi(s,x,y),\phi(\omega,x)\rangle\mathrm{d}\rho(x,y|s).

Also, if
\rho=\rho_{\mu}
is the solution of
eq.

15
for the parameter
\mu
, we have the relation
\mathcal{L}(\mu,\rho_{\mu},\psi)=L(\mu)
for any test function
\psi
. Hence the variation of
L
w.r.t.
\mu
is:

\displaystyle\frac{\delta L}{\delta\mu}(\mu)=\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\rho_{\mu},\psi)+\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\rho}(\mu,\rho_{\mu},\psi)\frac{\delta\rho_{\mu}}{\delta\mu}(\mu),

where the
Lagrange multiplier

\psi
can be chosen arbitrarily. Also the variation of
\mathcal{L}
w.r.t. the family of probability measures
\rho
, seen as the probability measure whose disintegration on
[0,1]
is
\{\rho_{s}\}_{s\in[0,1]}
(with the fixed initial condition
\rho(.|0)=\mathcal{D}
), can be formally given by:

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\rho}(\mu,\rho,\psi)=\left(\ell-\psi(1)\right)\delta_{s=1}+\partial_{s}\psi+\langle\nabla_{x}\psi,F_{\mu(.|s)}\rangle.

We see that taking
\psi=\psi_{\mu}
to be a solution of
eq.

14
cancels
\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\rho}
for every
\rho
and hence:

\displaystyle\frac{\delta L}{\delta\mu}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\rho_{\mu},\psi_{\mu}).

By
2.4
we know that for every absolutely continuous curve
(\mu_{t})
passing through
\mu
, its variation at
\mu
is given by
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}=-\mathrm{div}((0,v)\mu)
for some
v\in L^{2}(\mu)
.
A notion of gradient of
L
(for the “differential” structure of
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
) at
\mu
could thus be defined as the unique solution to the variational problem:

\displaystyle\nabla L(\mu)\in\,\argmin_{v\in L^{2}(\mu)}\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}-\langle\nabla_{\omega}\frac{\delta L}{\delta\mu}(\mu),v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

This problem admits a unique solution
v^{*}\in L^{2}(\mu)
, provided that
\nabla_{\omega}\frac{\delta L}{\delta\mu}(\mu)\in L^{2}(\mu)
, and using the relation
eq.

13
between the adjoint variable
p_{\mu}
and the potential
\psi_{\mu}
we have:

\displaystyle v^{*}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\omega}\frac{\delta L}{\delta\mu}(\mu)=\nabla_{\omega}\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta\mu}(\mu,\rho_{\mu},\psi_{\mu})

\displaystyle\text{that is}\quad v^{*}

\displaystyle:(s,\omega)\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x)^{\top}\nabla_{x}\psi_{\mu}(x,y)\mathrm{d}\rho_{\mu}(x,y|s)=\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{\mu}(s))^{\top}p_{\mu,x,y}(s).

If the above calculations are purely formal they motivate the following definition of gradient flow for
L
. In particular, this definition will be shown in the next section to be equivalent to the appropriate notion of gradient flow in metric spaces.

Definition 3.2

(Gradient flow)
.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval.
For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
let us define:

\displaystyle\nabla L[\mu]:(s,\omega)\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{\mu}(s))^{\top}p_{\mu,x,y}(s).

(17)

We say that a curve
t\in I\mapsto\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
is a
gradient flow
for
L
if it is locally absolutely continuous curve satisfying the continuity equation
eq.

9
with
v_{t}=-\nabla L[\mu_{t}]
for
\mathrm{d}t
-a.e.
t\in I
.

The following result is a useful representation formula for the gradient flow curves defined by
3.2
: for every
t\geq 0
the gradient flow
\mu_{t}
at time
t
is the pushforward of the initialization
\mu_{0}
by a flow-map. The proof relies on classical results from transport equation theory
[
Amb08
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
\phi
is twice continuously differentiable and satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
. Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a gradient flow for the risk
L
and consider for every
t\geq 0
the vector field:

\displaystyle V_{t}:(s,\omega)\mapsto\left(0,\nabla L[\mu_{t}](s,\omega)\right)=\left(0,\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{\mu_{t}}(s))^{\top}p_{\mu_{t},x,y}(s)\right)\in\mathbb{R}\times\Omega.

Then for every
t\geq 0
we have
\mu_{t}=(X_{t})_{\#}\mu_{0}
where
X_{t}
is the flow-map solution of the ODE:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}X_{t}(s,\omega)=V_{t}(X_{t}(s,\omega)),\quad X_{0}=\mathrm{Id}.

(18)

Proof.

The existence and uniqueness of the flow-map
X_{t}
for every
t\geq 0
follows from the assumptions on
\phi
(in particular linear growth and local-Lipschitzness of
\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi
w.r.t.
\omega
) and classical theory of ODEs. The flow-map representation of
\mu_{t}
then follows from
[
Amb08
, Thm.3.2]
as for any initial value
(s,\omega)\in[0,1]\times\Omega
the set of curves solutions to the ODE is the singleton
\{(X_{t}(s,\omega))_{t\geq 0}\}
.
∎

Consistency with the adjoint gradient flow

A case of particular interest for numerical applications is when the measure
\mu
is discretized and approximated at every time
s\in[0,1]
by an empirical distribution.
Given
m\geq 0
and
\theta=(\theta^{j})_{1\leq j\leq m}\in L^{2}([0,1],\Omega)^{m}
we define the associated empirical distribution
\mu_{\theta}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
by:

\displaystyle\text{for $\mathrm{d}s$-a.e. $s\in[0,1]$},\quad\mu_{\theta}(.|s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\delta_{\theta^{j}(s)}\mathrm{d}s.

(19)

i.e.

\mu_{\theta}
is the measure whose disintegration at any time
s\in[0,1]
is the empirical measure
\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\delta_{\theta^{j}(s)}
. Then we note
L(\theta)\coloneqq L(\mu_{\theta})
the risk associated to
\theta
.
In the original work of
[
Che+18
]
, the authors propose to train the Neural ODE parameterized by
\theta
and minimize
L(\theta)
by performing gradient descent for the
adjoint gradient
defined as:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\theta^{j}}L(\theta)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\theta^{j},x(s))^{\top}p(s),

where
x
and
p
are respectively the solutions of
eqs.

3
and
10
for the parameter
\mu_{\theta}
. One can observe that the adjoint gradient is the one calculated by
eq.

17
when
\mu=\mu_{\theta}
. Given sufficient regularity assumptions on the feature map
\phi
, we have by
3.2
that
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a gradient flow in the sense of
3.2
with
\mu_{0}=\mu_{\theta_{0}}
if and only if
\mu_{t}=\mu_{\theta_{t}}
for every
t\geq 0
and
(\theta_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a gradient flow for the above adjoint gradient.

3.3
Gradient flows as curves of maximal slope

There exists a large body of mathematical works devoted to the generalization of the classical theory of gradient flows to functionals over metric spaces.

[
AGS08
]
give an in-depth presentation of this theory. Complementary and more synthetic presentations are given by
[
Amb+13
,
San17
]
. Based on those works, we introduce here another definition of gradient flows for the risk
L
which is the one of
curves of maximal slope
and show it coincides with the definition from the previous section.

3.3.1
Curves of maximal slope in metric spaces

When
Z
is a Euclidean space, and
f
is a smooth function
the gradient flow of
f
is defined as the solution of the ODE
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}z_{t}=-\nabla f(z_{t})
. Then such a gradient flow satisfies:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f(z_{t})=-\|\nabla f(z_{t})\|^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}z_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla f(z_{t})\|^{2}\right),

whereas for any other smooth curve
(y_{t})
we have by Young’s inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f(y_{t})=-\langle\nabla f(y_{t}),\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y_{t}\rangle\geq-\frac{1}{2}\left(\|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y_{t}\|^{2}+\|\nabla f(y_{t})\|^{2}\right)

(20)

with equality if and only if
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y_{t}=-\nabla f(y_{t})
. Hence, we see that imposing equality in the above inequality gives a characterization of gradient flow curves in the Euclidean case.
The definition of
curves of maximal slope
is based on the generalization of this characterization to metric spaces. For example, a generalization of the speed’s norm
\|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}z_{t}\|
is given by the metric derivative
\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}z_{t}\right|
. To give a sense to the gradient’s norm
\|\nabla f(z)\|
we need to introduce the notion of
upper gradient
.

Definition 3.3

(Upper gradient
[
AGS08
, Def.1.2.1]
)
.

Let
(Z,d)
be a complete metric space and
f:Z\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. The map
g:Z\to[0,+\infty]
is a
upper gradient
for
f
if for every curve absolutely continuous curve
(z_{t})_{t\in I}
we have that
t\mapsto g(z_{t})
is measurable and:

\displaystyle\left|f(z_{t_{1}})-f(z_{t_{2}})\right|\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}g(z_{t})\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}z_{t}\right|\mathrm{d}t,\quad\forall t_{1}\leq t_{2}\in I.

When no ambiguity an upper gradient of
f
will simply be noted
|\nabla f|
.

Given an upper gradient for
f
, the definition of curves of maximal slope consists in imposing equality in
eq.

20
.

Definition 3.4

(Curve of maximal slope
[
AGS08
, Def.1.3.2]
)
.

Let
(Z,d)
be a complete metric space,
I\in\mathbb{R}
be an interval and
f:Z\to\mathbb{R}
a function with
|\nabla f|
an upper gradient for
f
. We say that
(z_{t})_{t\in I}
is a
curve a maximal slope
for
f
w.r.t.
|\nabla f|
if it satisfies:

1.

(z_{t})_{t\in I}
is locally absolutely continuous,

2.

the map
t\mapsto f(z_{t})
is non-increasing,

3.

for
\mathrm{d}t
-a.e.
t\in I
it holds
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}f(z_{t})\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}z_{t}\right|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2}(z_{t})\right)
.

Moreover, if
\lim_{t\to\inf I}z_{t}=z
exists then we say
(z_{t})
is a
curve of maximal slope starting at
z
.

Remark 3.1

(About the various definitions of
curves of maximal slope
)
.

There exist various definitions of the notion of curve of maximal slope in metric spaces, see for example
[
Amb+13
, Sec.4]
for a discussion about the various definitions and their relations. Our definition is the same as the one used in
[
HM19
, Def.2.12]
. In particular, it implies the following
Energy Dissipation Equality

[
HM19
, Prop.2.14]
:

\displaystyle f(z_{t_{1}})-f(z_{t_{2}})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\left(\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}z_{t}\right|^{2}+|\nabla f|^{2}(z_{t})\right)\mathrm{d}t,\quad\forall t_{1},t_{2}\in I.

(EDE)

This definition differs from the one exposed in
[
SMP23
, Def.2.2]
(see also
[
MS20
, Def.4.4]
) as the map
t\mapsto f(z_{t})
need not be locally absolutely continuous. The difference between these two definitions is discussed in
[
SMP23
, Rem.2.6]
but observe that for our purpose
(i)
the loss
L
will be shown to be locally Lipschitz in
3.1
, hence implying that
L(\mu_{t})
is locally absolutely continuous,
(ii)
the gradient norm
\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
will be shown to be an upper gradient in
3.4
. For these reasons, we need not here make the distinction between these two definitions and prefer weaker assumptions.

Note that there is
a priori
no reasons for
3.2
and
3.4
to define the same notion of gradient flow for the risk
L
. In particular, the first definition uses the existence of the adjoint variable
p
and thus some regularity on
\phi
. In contrast, the second definition requires an upper gradient which is yet unspecified for
L
. Taking appropriate assumptions on the feature map
\phi
, we show in the rest of this section that the risk
L
is sufficiently regular for the two definitions to coincide. This will be the content of
3.1
.

3.3.2
Curve of maximal slope for the risk
L

Provided with
3.4
, we seek to characterize the curves of maximal slope for the risk
L
in the metric space
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
.
We first show the
\mathrm{NODE}
’s output is a locally Lipschitz function of the parameter
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
.

Assumption 2

(local Lipschitzness w.r.t.
\omega
)
.

Assume that
\phi:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is locally Lipschitz w.r.t.
\omega
with a Lipschitz constant that grows at most linearly w.r.t.
\Omega
: for every
R\geq 0
there exists a constant
C(R)
s.t.:

\displaystyle\forall x\in B(0,R),\,\forall\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega,\quad\|\phi(\omega,x)-\phi(\omega^{\prime},x)\|\leq C(R)(1+\max(\|\omega\|,\|\omega^{\prime}\|))\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|.

Lemma 3.2

(local Lipschitzness of the flow)
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
and
2
and consider some input
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the map
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)\mapsto(x_{\mu}(s))_{s\in[0,1]}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
is locally Lipschitz. More precisely, for every
\mathcal{E}\geq 0
there exists a constant
C=C(\mathcal{E})
s.t.:

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\|x_{\mu}(s)-x_{\mu^{\prime}}(s)\|\leq Cd(\mu,\mu^{\prime}),

for every
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
with
\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu),\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu^{\prime})\leq\mathcal{E}
. Moreover, the constant
C
can be chosen uniformly over
x
in a compact set.

Proof.

Consider
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{E}\geq 0
and
\mu,\mu^{\prime}
such as in the statement.
We note
(x(s))_{s\in[0,1]}
and
(x^{\prime}(s))_{s\in[0,1]}
the flow associated to
x
and to the parameters
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
respectively.
Let
R\geq 0
be such that
\|x\|\leq R
.
Then by
1.1
the trajectories
x,x^{\prime}
are uniformly bounded by some
R^{\prime}=R^{\prime}(R,\mathcal{E})
. Then using
eq.

2
we have for every
s\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle\|x(s)-x^{\prime}(s)\|\leq

\displaystyle\|x(0)-x^{\prime}(0)\|+\|\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\phi(\omega,x(r))\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)\mathrm{d}r-\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}(\omega|r)\mathrm{d}r\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|x(0)-x^{\prime}(0)\|+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\|\phi(\omega,x(r))-\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))\|\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s}\|\int_{\Omega}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))\mathrm{d}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(\omega|r)\|\mathrm{d}r.

For the first integral note that using the local Lipschitzness of
\phi
w.r.t.
x
in
1
we have for every
r\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\|\phi(\omega,x(r))-\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))\|\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}(1+\|\omega\|^{2})\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)\|x(r)-x^{\prime}(r)\|,

where
C_{1}=C_{1}(R,\mathcal{E})
. For the second integral, note that at fixed
r\in[0,1]
, if
\gamma\in\Gamma_{o}(\mu(.|r),\mu^{\prime}(.|r))
is a (optimal) coupling between
\mu(.|r)
and
\mu^{\prime}(.|r)
then:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))\mathrm{d}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(\omega|r)=\int_{\Omega^{2}}(\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))-\phi(\omega^{\prime},x^{\prime}(r)))\mathrm{d}\gamma(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\,.

Using the local Lipschitzness of
\phi
w.r.t.
\omega
in
2
and the optimality of
\gamma
:

\displaystyle\|\int_{\Omega}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))\mathrm{d}(\mu-\mu^{\prime})(\omega|r)\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega^{2}}\|\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))-\phi(\omega^{\prime},x^{\prime}(r))\|\mathrm{d}\gamma(\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega^{2}}C_{2}(1+\max(\|\omega\|,\|\omega^{\prime}\|))\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|\mathrm{d}\gamma(\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{3}C_{2}\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu(.|r))+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu^{\prime}(.|r))\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu(.|r),\mu^{\prime}(.|r)),

where
C_{2}=C_{2}(R,\mathcal{E})
. Integrating those inequalities gives by Grönwall’s lemma that for
s\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle\|x(s)-x^{\prime}(s)\|\leq\sqrt{3}e^{C_{1}(1+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu))}C_{2}\int_{0}^{s}\left(1+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu(.|r))+\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu^{\prime}(.|r))\right)^{1/2}\mathcal{W}_{2}(\mu(.|r),\mu^{\prime}(.|r))\mathrm{d}r\leq C_{3}d(\mu,\mu^{\prime}),

where
C_{3}=C_{3}(R,\mathcal{E})
.
∎

As an immediate corollary of the above proposition we get that, provided the loss function
\ell
is itself locally Lipschitz then the risk
L
is also a locally Lipschitz function of
\mu
.

Corollary 3.1

(local Lipschitzness of the risk)
.

Assume that
\phi
satisfies
1
and
2
and
\ell
is locally Lipschitz w.r.t.
x
. Then the risk map
L:\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)\mapsto L(\mu)
is locally Lipschitz.

Assuming more regularity on the map
\phi
, one can express the first variation
\delta x
of the flow map with respect to a variation of the parameter transported by a velocity field
v\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

Assumption 3

(Differentiability of
\phi
)
.

Assume that
\phi
is continuously differentiable and s.t.

1.

\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi
grows at most quadratically with
\omega
: for every
R\geq 0
there exists a constant
C(R)
s.t.

\displaystyle\forall x\in B(0,R),\,\forall\omega\in\Omega,\quad\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq C(R)(1+\|\omega\|^{2}).

2.

\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi
grows at most linearly with
\omega
: for every
R\geq 0
there exists a constant
C=C(R)
s.t.

\displaystyle\forall x\in B(0,R),\,\forall\omega\in\Omega,\quad\|\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq C(R)(1+\|\omega\|).

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
. Consider
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
and a velocity field
v:[0,1]\times\Omega\to\Omega
in
L^{2}(\mu)
. For
t\in\mathbb{R}
note
\mu_{t}\coloneqq(\mathrm{Id}+t(0,v))_{\#}\mu
. Then, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
(x_{\mu_{t}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is differentiable in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
at
t=0
and
\delta x\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{\mu_{t}}|_{t=0}
is the solution to:

\displaystyle\forall s\in[0,1],\,\delta x(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\mathrm{D}F_{\mu(.|r)}(x_{\mu}(r))\delta x(r)\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{\mu}(r))v(r,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)\mathrm{d}r.

(21)

Proof.

First, thanks to
3
, for
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
the map
F_{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is differentiable with
\mathrm{D}F_{\nu}:x\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)
.
Also,
\delta x
is well-defined as the unique solution of
eq.

21
and:

\displaystyle\forall s\in[0,1],\quad\delta x(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\mu,x}(s)\Phi_{\mu,x}(r)^{-1}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x(r))v(r,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega).

For simplicity, in the rest of the proof we will note
x_{t}\coloneqq x_{\mu_{t}}
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let us then show that
\delta x
is the derivative of
x_{t}
at
t=0
. For
t\neq 0
, consider the normalized increment:

\displaystyle z_{t}\coloneqq\frac{1}{t}(x_{t}-x_{0})\in\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Then we have by definition of
x_{t}
and
x_{0}
that for every
s\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle z_{t}(s)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\phi(\omega,x_{t}(r))\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(r,\omega)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\phi(\omega,x_{0}(r))\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\left(\phi(\omega+tv(r,\omega),x_{t}(r))-\phi(\omega,x_{0}(r))\right)\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x_{0}(r)+utz_{t}(r))\mathrm{d}u\right)\cdot z_{t}(r)\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega+utv(r,\omega),x_{t}(r))\mathrm{d}u\right)\cdot v(r,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega).

Hence
z_{t}
is solution of the linear ODE
z_{t}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\left(A_{t}(r)\cdot z_{t}(r)+b_{t}(r)\right)\mathrm{d}r
where we defined for
\mathrm{d}r
-a.e.
r\in[0,1]
:

\displaystyle A_{t}(r)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x_{0}(r)+utz_{t}(r))\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)

\displaystyle b_{t}(r)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega+utv(r,\omega),x_{t}(r))\cdot v(r,\omega)\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)

and in order to prove that
z_{t}\to\delta x
in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
t\to 0
it suffices to show that
A_{t}
and
b_{t}
converge respectively in
L^{1}([0,1])
to:

\displaystyle A(r)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x_{0}(r))\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r),\quad\text{and}\quad b(r)\coloneqq\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{0}(r))\cdot v(r)\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r).

Indeed, note that
d(\mu_{t},\mu)\leq t\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
and the family
(z_{t})_{t\in[-1,1]}
is bounded
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
3.2
. Thus for
t\in\mathbb{R}
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left|A_{t}(r)-A(r)\right|\mathrm{d}r\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x_{0}(r)+utz_{t}(r))\mathrm{d}u-\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x_{0}(r))\right|\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega)\xrightarrow[t\to 0]{}0

where
3
allows to bound the integrand by an integrable function and to apply Lebesgue’s theorem, showing that
A_{t}\to A
as
t\to 0
in
L^{1}([0,1])
.
Similarly for
b_{t}
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\left|b_{t}(r)-b(r)\right|\mathrm{d}r\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega+utv(r,\omega),x_{t}(r))\mathrm{d}u-\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{0}(r))\right|\|v(r,\omega)\|\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega)\xrightarrow[t\to 0]{}0.

∎

A direct consequence of the previous result is the differentiability of the flow map and consequently of the risk along absolutely continuous curves.

Corollary 3.2

(Differentiability of the flow)
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
. Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval and consider
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
an absolutely continuous curve in
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
satisfying the continuity equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\mathrm{div}((0,v_{t})\mu_{t})=0\quad\text{over $I\times[0,1]\times\Omega$.}

Consider some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
(x_{\mu_{t}})_{t\in I}
is an absolutely continuous curve in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
and is differentiable in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
\mathrm{d}t
-a.e.
t\in I
with
\delta x_{t}\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{\mu_{t}}
the solution to:

\displaystyle\forall s\in[0,1],\,\delta x_{t}(s)=\int_{0}^{s}\mathrm{D}F_{\mu_{t}(.|r)}(x_{\mu_{t}}(r))\delta x_{t}(r)\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{\mu_{t}}(r))v_{t}(r,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(\omega|r)\mathrm{d}r.

(22)

Proof.

For
t\in I
we use the shortcut notation
x_{t}\coloneqq x_{\mu_{t}}
. The fact that
(x_{t})_{t\in I}
is absolutely continuous follows from
3.2
stating that the flow map is locally Lipschitz. To prove the result it hence suffices to show that
\delta x_{t}
is the derivative of
t\mapsto x_{t}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Note that, without loss of generality we can consider
v_{t}
to be the (uniquely defined) tangent velocity field of the curve
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
. Indeed if
\tilde{v}_{t}
is the tangent velocity field then we have by
2.4
that in the sense of distributions:

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}((0,v_{t}-\tilde{v}_{t})\mu_{t})=0.

Hence for every

and every

:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{t}(r))v_{t}(r,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(r,\omega)=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{t}(r))\tilde{v}_{t}(r,\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(r,\omega)

and the definition of
\delta x_{t}
stays unchanged.
Then, assuming
v_{t}
is the tangent velocity field to the curve
\mu_{t}
, we can consider a subset
\Lambda\subset I
of full Lebesgue measure such that the conclusions of
2.2
hold. For every
t\in\Lambda
and every

consider
\tilde{\mu}^{h}_{t}\coloneqq(\mathrm{Id}+h(0,v_{t}))_{\#}\mu_{t}
and
\tilde{x}^{h}_{t}
the associated flow. Then by

\displaystyle\|\frac{x_{t+h}-x_{t}}{h}-\delta x_{t}\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,1])}\leq\|\frac{\tilde{x}^{h}_{t}-x_{t}}{h}-\delta x_{t}\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,1])}+\|\frac{x_{t+h}-\tilde{x}^{h}_{t}}{h}\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,1])}\xrightarrow[h\to 0]{}0

where the first term goes to

by application of
3.3
.The second term also goes to
0
by the fact that the flow map is locally Lipschitz, thus
\|x_{t+h}-\tilde{x}^{h}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,1])}\leq Cd(\mu_{t+h},\tilde{\mu}^{h}_{t})
for some constant
C
and
\frac{1}{h}\|x_{t+h}-\tilde{x}^{h}_{t}\|_{\mathcal{C}([0,1])}\to 0
by
2.2
. Note that, as
\Lambda\subset I
is independent of
x
, it follows that the curve

is differentiable at every

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Corollary 3.3

(Differentiability of the loss)
.

Assume

satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
and

is continuously differentiable. Let

First, the fact that

. It remains to show the formula for its derivative.

For

we know that there exists a subset

Thanks to
3.2
(which can be seen as a chain-rule formula) one can show that the gradient norm

in the sense of

. Moreover the following

shows it corresponds to the notion of

(

Lemma 3.3

(Continuity of the adjoint variable

)
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
.
Then, for fixed

-continuous on

Proof.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)

(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)

(resp.
p\coloneqq p_{\mu,x,y}

3.2
we already have

in
\mathcal{C}([0,1])
and we show now that

in
\mathcal{C}([0,1])

Remark that by the assumptions on
\phi

,
x_{n}

and

stay in a bounded set

for some

. Also, as

(\mu_{n})
has uniformly integrable second moment and for every

Then for

10

\displaystyle\|p_{n}(s_{2})-p_{n}(s_{1})\|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\int_{\Omega}(1+\|\omega\|^{2})\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega)

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon+(1+k^{2})|s_{2}-s_{1}|),

where

. Hence the sequence

is equicontinuous and, up to a subsequence, we have

. Indeed using the initial condition we have for

and

\displaystyle p_{n}(s)=\nabla_{x}\ell(x_{n}(1),y)+\int_{s}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x_{n}(r))^{\top}p_{n}(r)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega).

-convergence, we can take the limit in the above equation to obtain:

\displaystyle\bar{p}(s)=\nabla_{x}\ell(x(1),y)+\int_{s}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x(r))^{\top}\bar{p}(r)\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega),

\bar{p}=p

10

(Continuity of

,

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)

(\mu_{n})_{n\geq 0}
in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
s.t.

(resp.

\mu_{n}
(resp.

Proposition 3.4

(
\|\nabla L(\mu)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
is an upper-gradient)
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
. Let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
, then
\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}}(\mu)
is the

of the risk
L
at
\mu
, that is:

\displaystyle\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\limsup_{\nu\to\mu}\frac{\left(L(\mu)-L(\nu)\right)^{+}}{d(\mu,\nu)}.

Moreover, it is an upper-gradient in the sense of
3.3
.

Proof.

The last part of the result follows from
2.4
, since if
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
is an absolutely continuous curve then it satisfies the continuity equation with a vector field
v
such that
\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\leq\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|
for a.e.

. Hence by
3.3
and Cauchy-Schwarz we have:

\displaystyle\forall t_{1}\leq t_{2}\in I,\quad\left|L(\mu_{t_{1}})-L(\mu_{t_{2}})\right|\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|\nabla L[\mu_{t}]\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|\mathrm{d}t.

Let us then show
eq.

23
.
Consider some parameter
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
and note
|\nabla L|(\mu)
the r.h.s. of
eq.

23
.
Then for
\varepsilon>0
, by continuity of
\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
(
3.4
)
and by definition of
|\nabla L|(\mu)

one can find a
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
s.t.:

\displaystyle\frac{\left(L(\mu)-L(\nu)\right)^{+}}{d(\mu,\nu)}\geq\left|\nabla L\right|(\mu)-\varepsilon,\quad\text{and}\quad\left|\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}-\|\nabla L[\nu^{\prime}]\|_{L^{2}(\nu^{\prime})}\right|\leq\varepsilon,\,\text{if $d(\mu,\nu^{\prime})\leq d(\mu,\nu)$}.

Consider
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
a constant speed geodesics with endpoints
\mu_{0}=\mu
and
\mu_{1}=\nu
(such a geodesic can easily be constructed by similarity with classical Wasserstein geodesics, see
[
AGS08
, Thm.7.2.2]
). Then by
2.4
the tangent velocity field
v
of the curve
(\mu_{t})
satisfies for
\mathrm{d}t
-a.e.
t\in[0,1]
,
\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\leq\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|=d(\mu,\nu)
and using
3.3
:

\displaystyle L(\nu)

\displaystyle=L(\mu)+\int_{0}^{1}\langle\nabla L[\mu_{t}],v_{t}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq L(\mu)+d(\mu,\nu)\int_{0}^{1}\|\nabla L[\mu_{t}]\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq L(\mu)+d(\mu,\nu)\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\varepsilon.

Similarly we have
L(\mu)\leq L(\nu)+d(\mu,\nu)\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\varepsilon
and hence
\left|\nabla L\right|(\mu)\leq\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\varepsilon
.

For the converse inequality consider for
t\in\mathbb{R}
the parameter
\mu_{t}=(\mathrm{Id}+t(0,\nabla L[\mu]))_{\#}\mu
. Then, by
3.3
with
v=\nabla L[\mu]
, the map
t\mapsto L(\mu_{t})
is differentiable at
t=0
and applying the same calculations as in
3.3
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}L[\mu_{t}]\Bigr|_{t=0}=\langle\nabla L[\mu],v\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\|\nabla L[\mu]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}.

Hence observing that
d(\mu_{t},\mu)\leq t\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}
we have
\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{(L(\mu_{t})-L(\mu))^{+}}{d(\mu_{t},\mu)}\geq\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
.
∎

As a consequence of the previous result, we will from now on only consider as upper gradient of
L
the one given for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
by:

\displaystyle|\nabla L|(\mu)\coloneqq\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}=\left(\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\|\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x(s))^{\top}p(s)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(s,\omega)\right)^{1/2}.

(24)

Note that the vector field
\nabla L[\mu]
was used in
3.2
to define the notion of
gradient flow
whereas the upper-gradient
|\nabla L|(\mu)
is used in the
3.4
of curves of maximal slope. The following theorem is the main result of this section and shows these two notions coincide.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
and
\ell
is smooth. Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open interval. Then a curve
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
is a
gradient flow
in the sense of
3.2
if and only if it is a
curve of maximal slope
for
L
in the sense of
3.4
.

Proof.

Part 3:
Gradient flows are curves of maximal slope.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
be a gradient flow for
L
in the sense of
3.2
. Then
(\mu_{t})
is a locally absolutely continuous curve satisfying the continuity equation
\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\mathrm{div}(v_{t}\mu_{t})=0
with
v_{t}=-\nabla L[\mu_{t}]
. Hence by
2.4
we have for a.e.
t\in I
:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|\leq\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}=\|\nabla L[\mu_{t}]\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}.

Also, by
3.2
,
(L(\mu_{t}))_{t\in I}
is absolutely continuous with for a.e.
t\in I
:

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}L(\mu_{t})=\langle v_{t},\nabla L[\mu_{t}]\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}=\|\nabla L[\mu_{t}]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}.

Thus recalling that
|\nabla L|(\mu)=\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
we get
3.4
by putting together the two previous equations.

Part 4:
Curves of maximal slope are gradient flows.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
be a curve of maximal slope for
L
in the sense of
3.4
. Then in particular
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
is locally abolutely continuous in
(\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega),d)
and by
2.4
there exists a Borel velocity field
v:I\times[0,1]\times\Omega\to\Omega
such that
\mu
satisfies the continuity equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mu_{t}+\mathrm{div}((0,v_{t})\mu_{t})=0,\quad\text{over $I\times[0,1]\times\Omega$,}

and such that the metric derivative satisfies
|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}|\geq\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}
for a.e.
t\in I
. Hence it follows from
3.3
that
(L(\mu_{t}))_{t\in I}
is absolutely continuous and for a.e.
t\in I
:

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}L(\mu_{t})=-\langle v_{t},\nabla L[\mu_{t}]\rangle.

Using the EDE condition we thus have:

\displaystyle-\langle v_{t},\nabla L[\mu_{t}]\rangle\geq\frac{1}{2}(|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}|^{2}+|\nabla L|^{2}(\mu_{t}))\geq\frac{1}{2}(\|v_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}+\|\nabla L[\mu_{t}]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})})

from which it follows by Young’s inequality that
v_{t}=-\nabla L[\mu_{t}]
in
L^{2}(\mu_{t})
for a.e.
t\in I
.
∎

Note that, although it does not appear in
3.2
, the above equivalence shows that if
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
is a gradient flow for
L
then
\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|=\|\nabla L[\mu_{t}]\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{t})}

i.e.

\nabla L[\mu_{t}]
is in fact the (uniquely defined) tangent velocity field of the curve
(\mu_{t})_{t\in I}
.

3.4
Existence, uniqueness, and stability of gradient flow curves

We show here the well-posedness result for the gradient flow equation of the risk
L
, namely we show the existence, uniqueness, and stability of gradient flow curves starting from any initialization
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. For the “existence” part we will rely on classical results from the theory of gradient flows in metric spaces showing the convergence of proximal sequences towards a curve known as
(Generalized) Minimising Movements

[
De 93
]
.
For the “uniqueness” part we will show that gradient flow trajectories are stable, that is if two initializations
\mu_{0},\mu_{0}^{\prime}
are close (in the sense of the metric
d
), then the emanating gradient flow curves
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0},(\mu_{t}^{\prime})_{t\geq 0}
stay close in finite time.

3.4.1
Existence

We proceed to show the existence of gradient flow curves as defined in
3.2
. For that purpose, we need a strengthening of
1
. Notably,
A
allows to show the flow map
\mu\mapsto x_{\mu}
is continuous for the topology of narrow convergence over
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
.

Assumption A
.

For some
\alpha\in[1,2)
we assume that:

1.

The feature map
\phi
has
\alpha
-growth w.r.t.
\omega
, locally w.r.t.
x
. That is for every compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists a constant
C=C(K)
s.t:

\displaystyle\forall x\in K,\forall\omega\in\Omega,\quad\|\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq C(1+\|\omega\|^{\alpha}).

2.

The feature map
\phi
is continuously differentiable and its differential
\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi
w.r.t.
x
has
\alpha
-growth w.r.t.
\omega
, locally w.r.t.
x
. That is for every compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
there exists a constant
C=C(K)
s.t.:

\displaystyle\forall x\in K,\forall\omega\in\Omega,\quad\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq C(1+\|\omega\|^{\alpha}).

Theorem 3.2

(Existence of curves of maximal slope)
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
and
A
. Let
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Then there exists a curve of maximal slope
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,+\infty)}
starting from
\mu_{0}
and
\left(\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}\right|\right)_{t\geq 0}\in L^{2}_{loc}([0,+\infty))
.

Proof.

The result follows from the successive application of
[
AGS08
, Thm.2.2.3 and Thm.2.3.3]
, the first result ensuring the existence of
Generalized Minimizing Movements
and the second result stating that these curves are curves of maximal slope for the local slope. The proof proceeds by verifying the assumptions of these theorems. We consider here
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
provided with the topology induced by the distance
d
and with the (weaker) topology of narrow convergence, noted
\tau
. Note that
(\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega),d)
is a complete metric space (
2.3
) and that the distance
d
is
\tau
-lower-semi-continuous (
2.1
).

Part 5:

d
-bounded sets are
\tau
-relatively compact.

This property is verified as
d
-bounded sets are tight and hence
\tau
-relatively compact by Prokhorov’s theorem.

Part 6:

L
is
\tau
-continuous on
d
-bounded sets.

Let
(\mu_{n})
be a
d
-bounded sequence in
\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
such that
\mu_{n}\xrightarrow{\tau}\mu
for some
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
and let us show that
L(\mu_{n})\to L(\mu)
. Take
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and note
x_{n}\coloneqq x_{\mu_{n}}
the flow trajectory starting from
x
and associated to
\mu_{n}
. By Lebesgue’s theorem, it suffices to show that
x_{n}(1)\to x_{\mu}(1)
. Using Ascoli’s theorem, we will proceed by showing that
x_{n}\to x_{\mu}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

From the
d
-boundedness and the proof of
1.1
, it follows that the trajectories
x_{n}
stay in a compact set. Moreover given
s_{1}<s_{2}\in[0,1]
we have using the
\alpha
-growth assumption:

\displaystyle\|x_{n}(s_{2})-x_{n}(s_{1})\|\leq\int_{[s_{1},s_{2}]\times\Omega}\|\phi(\omega,x_{n}(r))\|\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega)\leq\int_{[s_{1},s_{2}]\times\Omega}C(1+\|\omega\|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega).

Also as the sequence
(\mu_{n})
is
d
-bounded it has uniformly integrable
\alpha
-moments. Given
\varepsilon>0
we can thus find a
k\geq 0
such that, for every
n\geq 0
,
\int_{\|\omega\|\geq k}C(1+\|\omega\|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\leq\varepsilon
. Using this in the previous inequality and the fact that the marginal of
\mu_{n}
on
[0,1]
is the Lebesgue measure gives:

\displaystyle\forall s_{1}<s_{2}\in[0,1],\quad\|x_{n}(s_{2})-x_{n}(s_{1})\|\leq\varepsilon+C(1+k^{\alpha})(s_{2}-s_{1}).

Thus the trajectories
(x_{n})
are equicontinuous and, by Arzela-Ascoli’s theorem, we have (up to a subsequence) that
x_{n}\to\bar{x}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Let us show that
\bar{x}=x_{\mu}
is the flow generated by
\mu
. This will conclude this part of the proof as it will imply
x_{n}(1)\to x_{\mu}(1)
and then
L(\mu_{n})\to L(\mu)
by Lebesgue’s convergence theorem. Considering
s\in[0,1]
we have:

\displaystyle x_{n}(s)=x

\displaystyle+\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s}\phi(\omega,x_{n}(r))\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega)

\displaystyle=x

\displaystyle+\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s}\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega)

\displaystyle+\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s}\left(\phi(\omega,x_{n}(r))-\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(s))\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega)

(L1)

\displaystyle+\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s}\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))\mathrm{d}(\mu_{n}-\mu)(r,\omega).

(L2)

In this last equality, we need to show that
L1
and
L2
vanish as
n\to\infty
. In
L1
we have that the integrand has
\alpha
-growth, hence given
\varepsilon>0
we have using the uniform integrability of
\|\omega\|^{\alpha}
on the sequence
(\mu_{n})
that for every
n\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\|\int\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s}\left(\phi(\omega,x_{n}(r))-\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega)\|\leq\varepsilon+\int\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s,\|\omega\|\leq k}\|\phi(\omega,x_{n}(r))-\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))\|\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega).

Then as
\phi
is locally-Lipschitz w.r.t.
x
and
x_{n}\to\bar{x}
we have that the integrand on the r.h.s. converges uniformly to 0 and hence:

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s}\left(\phi(\omega,x_{n}(r))-\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(s))\right)\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega)\|\leq\varepsilon.

In
L2
the integrand is not continuous so we can’t simply apply the definition of narrow convergence and need to leverage the fact that
(\mu_{n})
is a
d
-bounded sequence in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Note that for every
(r,\omega)\in[0,1]\times\Omega
,
\|\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))\|\leq C(1+\|\omega\|^{\alpha})
. Given
\varepsilon>0
and using the uniform integrability of
\|\omega\|^{\alpha}
we can thus have a
k\geq 0
such that:

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 0}\int_{\|\omega\|\geq k}C(1+\|\omega\|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}\mu_{n},\,\int_{\|\omega\|\geq k}C(1+\|\omega\|^{\alpha})\mathrm{d}\mu\,\leq\varepsilon.

Then whenever
\delta>0
we can find a continuous function
\varphi:[0,1]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\|\varphi(r,\omega)\|\leq C(1+\|\omega\|^{\alpha})
,
\varphi(r,\omega)=\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))
for every
r\leq s
and
\varphi(r,\omega)=0
whenever
r\geq s+\delta
. Considering such a function
\varphi
we have for
L2
:

\displaystyle\|\int\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s}\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))\mathrm{d}(\mu_{n}-\mu)(r,\omega)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\int\varphi\mathrm{d}(\mu_{n}-\mu)\|+\int\|\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s}\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))-\varphi(r,\omega)\|\mathrm{d}(\mu_{n}+\mu)(r,\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\|\int\varphi\mathrm{d}(\mu_{n}-\mu)\|+4\varepsilon+C(1+k^{\alpha})\delta

where we used the fact that, in the second term, the integrand has
\alpha
-growth and is only non-zero for
r\in[s,s+\delta]
. Hence having chosen
\delta
sufficiently small gives:

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\int\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq s}\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))\mathrm{d}(\mu_{n}-\mu)(r,\omega)\|\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\|\int\varphi\mathrm{d}(\mu_{n}-\mu)\|+5\varepsilon\leq 5\varepsilon,

where the first
\limsup
is 0 by definition of narrow convergence. We have thus shown that for every
s\in[0,1]
, taking the limit as
n\to\infty
:

\displaystyle\bar{x}(s)=x+\int_{0}^{s}\phi(\omega,\bar{x}(r))\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)\mathrm{d}r,

i.e.

\bar{x}=x_{\mu}
is the flow trajectory associated to
\mu
and starting from
x
.

Part 7:

\|\nabla L(\mu)\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
is
\tau
-lower semi-continuous on
d
-bounded subsets.

We previously considered the upper-gradient
|\nabla L|
defined in
eq.

24
as
|\nabla L|(\mu)\coloneqq\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
. However,
[
AGS08
, Thm.2.3.3]
state that the obtained curve of maximal slope is a curve of maximal slope for another definition of gradient, referred to as
relaxed slope
. To show these two notions coincide here we show that the map
\mu\mapsto\|\nabla L[\mu]\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
is
\tau
-lower semi-continuous on
d
-bounded subsets (
c.f.

[
AGS08
, Rm.2.3.4]
).

As before consider a
d
-bounded sequence
(\mu_{n})
in
\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
which narrowly converges towards some
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Then we previously have shown that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
x_{\mu_{n}}\to x_{\mu}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Proceeding with similar arguments one could show the same for the adjoint variable
p
, that is
p_{n}\coloneqq p_{\mu_{n},x,y}\to p_{\mu,x,y}
in
\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then using a generalization of Fatou’s lemma with varying measures (
e.g.

[
FKL20
, Thm.2.4]
) we have:

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla L[\mu_{n}]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu_{n})}

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\|\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{n}(r))^{\top}p_{n}(r)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}(r,\omega)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega}\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\to\infty\\
(r^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})\to(r,\omega)\end{subarray}}\|\mathbb{E}_{x,y}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega^{\prime},x_{n}(r^{\prime}))^{\top}p_{n}(r^{\prime})\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(r,\omega)

\displaystyle=\|\nabla L[\mu]\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)},

which is the desired property.
∎

3.4.2
Uniqueness

We present here a uniqueness result for solutions of the gradient flow equation, which is the content of the following
3.3
. The proof is standard and relies on the lipschitzness of the gradient vector field
\nabla L[\mu]
w.r.t. the measure
\mu
. It uses the following
B
on the feature map
\phi
to ensure local lipschitzness of the adjoint variable
p
(
3.5
).

Assumption B
.

Assume that
\phi
is twice continuously differentiable with

\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\omega,\omega}\phi
uniformly bounded,
\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\omega,x}\phi
having linear growth and
\mathrm{D}^{2}_{x,x}\phi
having quadratic growth w.r.t.
\omega
. Namely, for every
R\geq 0
there exists a constant
C=C(R)
s.t. for every
x,x^{\prime}\in B(0,R)
and every
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega
:

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\omega,\omega}\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq C(R),\quad\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{\omega,x}\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq C(R)(1+\|\omega\|),\quad\|\mathrm{D}^{2}_{x,x}\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq C(R)(1+\|\omega\|^{2}).

Theorem 3.3

(Uniqueness of curves of maximal slope)
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
,
3
and
B
and that
\nabla_{x}\ell
is locally Lipschitz w.r.t.
x
.
Let
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Then the gradient flow for the risk
L
starting from
\mu_{0}
, if it exists, is unique.

Proof.

Let
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and
(\mu_{t}^{\prime})_{t\geq 0}
be two gradient flow curves for the risk
L
starting from
\mu_{0}
. We will proceed to show that
d(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime})=0
for every
t\geq 0
.

We use the shorter notations
v_{t}\coloneqq\nabla L[\mu_{t}]
,
v_{t}^{\prime}\coloneqq\nabla L[\mu_{t}^{\prime}]
to refer to the
tangent vector fields
of
\mu
and
\mu^{\prime}
respectively. Observe that the map
t\mapsto d(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime})^{2}
is locally absolutely continuous and by
2.3
its differential is given at almost every
t\geq 0
by:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}d(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime})^{2}=2\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\langle\omega^{\prime}-\omega,v_{t}^{\prime}(s,\omega^{\prime})-v_{t}(s,\omega)\rangle\mathrm{d}\gamma_{t}(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime}),

where
\gamma_{t}\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{o}(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime})
can be any optimal coupling.

Let
T\geq 0
and note
\mathcal{E}\coloneqq\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\max(\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu_{t}),\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu_{t}^{\prime}))<\infty
.
Fix some
t\in[0,T]
and consider
(x,y)
in the support of the data ditsribution
\mathcal{D}
with the shortcuts
x_{t}\coloneqq x_{\mu_{t}}
,
p_{t}\coloneqq p_{\mu_{t},x,y}
and similarly
x_{t}^{\prime},p_{t}^{\prime}
for
\mu_{t}^{\prime}
. As the data distribution has compact support, we have that there exists some
R=R(\mathcal{E})
such that
\|x_{t}(s)\|,\|x_{t}^{\prime}(s)\|,\|p_{t}(s)\|,\|p_{t}^{\prime}(s)\|\leq R
. Then using
3.2
and
3.5
as well as the assumptions on
\phi
we have for every
(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\in[0,1]\times\Omega^{2}
:

\|\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{t}(s))^{\top}p_{t}(s)-\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega^{\prime},x_{t}^{\prime}(s))^{\top}p_{t}^{\prime}(s)\|\\
\leq\|\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{t}(s))^{\top}p_{t}(s)-\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega^{\prime},x_{t}(s))^{\top}p_{t}(s)\|\\
+\|\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega^{\prime},x_{t}(s))^{\top}p_{t}(s)-\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega^{\prime},x_{t}^{\prime}(s))^{\top}p_{t}^{\prime}(s)\|\\
\leq\|\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x_{t}(s))-\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega^{\prime},x_{t}(s))\|\|p_{t}(s)\|\\
+\|\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega^{\prime},x_{t}(s))-\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega^{\prime},x_{t}^{\prime}(s))\|\|p_{t}(s)\|\\
+\|\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega^{\prime},x_{t}^{\prime}(s))\|\|p_{t}(s)-p_{t}^{\prime}(s)\|\\
\leq C_{1}\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|+C_{1}(1+\|\omega^{\prime}\|)d(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime})+C_{1}(1+\|\omega^{\prime}\|)d(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime}),

(25)

with
C_{1}=C_{1}(\mathcal{E})
some constant. Fixing some
\gamma_{t}\in\Pi^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{o}(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime})
, using that
2\langle a,b\rangle\leq\|a\|^{2}+\|b\|^{2}
and integrating the previous inequality over
(x,y)
and
(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})
we get:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}d(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,1]\times\Omega^{2}}\left(\|\omega-\omega^{\prime}\|^{2}+\|v_{t}(s,\omega)-v_{t}^{\prime}(s,\omega^{\prime})\|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\gamma_{t}(s,\omega,\omega^{\prime})\leq C_{2}d(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime})^{2},

for some constant
C_{2}=C_{2}(\mathcal{E})
. We can then conclude using Grönwall’s inequality to:

\displaystyle\forall t\in[0,T],\quad d(\mu_{t},\mu_{t}^{\prime})^{2}\leq e^{C_{2}t}d(\mu_{0},\mu_{0})^{2}=0.

∎

The above proof relied on the following lemma, showing that the adjoint variable map
\mu\mapsto p_{\mu,x,y}
is locally Lipschitz under
B
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
,
3
and
B
and that
\nabla_{x}\ell
is locally Lipschitz w.r.t.
x
. Then for fixed
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
the adjoint variable map
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)\mapsto p_{\mu,x,y}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{d})
is locally Lipschitz. Namely, for every
\mathcal{E}\geq 0
there exists a constant
C=C(\mathcal{E})
such that:

\displaystyle\sup_{s\in[0,1]}\|p_{\mu,x,y}(s)-p_{\mu^{\prime},x,y}(s)\|\leq Cd(\mu,\mu^{\prime})

such that

. Moreover, the constant

Proof.

Consider

,
\mathcal{E}\geq 0
and parameterizations

as in the proposition. We note
(x(s))

\mu

Let

\|p(s)-p^{\prime}(s)\|\leq\|p(1)-p^{\prime}(1)\|\\
+\int_{s}^{1}\|\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x(r))^{\top}p(r)\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))^{\top}p^{\prime}(r)\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}(\omega|r)\|\mathrm{d}r.

\displaystyle\|\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x(r))^{\top}p(r)\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)-\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))^{\top}p^{\prime}(r)\mathrm{d}\mu^{\prime}(\omega|r)\|

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x(r))-\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))\|\|p(r)\|\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)+\int_{\Omega}\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))\|\|p(r)-p^{\prime}(r)\|\mathrm{d}\mu(\omega|r)

\displaystyle+\|\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime}(r))p^{\prime}(r)\mathrm{d}(\mu^{\prime}-\mu)(\omega|r)\|

\displaystyle I_{1}(r)+I_{2}(r)+I_{3}(r).

Then using the assumptions on

\displaystyle\|p(s)-p^{\prime}(s)\|

\displaystyle\|p(1)-p^{\prime}(1)\|\leq C_{4}d(\mu,\mu^{\prime}),\quad\text{where $C_{4}=C_{4}(R,\mathcal{E})$.}

∎

4
Convergence analysis and (local) Polyak-Łojasiewicz property

This section is devoted to the analysis of the convergence of the gradient flow dynamic we defined in the previous section. The questions we will try to answer are the following:

Given an initial parametrization
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
, does the risk
L(\mu_{t})
converge to
0
as
t\to\infty
? Does the parameterization converge towards an optimal parameterization
\mu_{\infty}
?

4.1
Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality

Our approach to show convergence of the gradient flow is to show that the risk satisfies the following local Polyak-Łojasiewicz (P-Ł) property around well-chosen parameterizations. The P-Ł inequality provides a lower bound on the ratio between the square gradient’s risk
\left|\nabla L\right|^{2}
and the risk
L
. It thus prevents the existence of spurious critical points and guarantees that the risk decreases at a constant rate along gradient flow.

Definition 4.1

(Local P-Ł property)
.

We say that the risk
L
is
(R,m)
-P-Ł around a parameterization
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
if it satisfies:

\displaystyle\forall\mu^{\prime}\in B(\mu,R),\quad|\nabla L|^{2}(\mu^{\prime})\geq mL(\mu^{\prime}).

(26)

Remark 4.1
.

Different formulations of the
(R,m)
-P-Ł property have been introduced in the literature. For example
[
Cha22
]
introduced the local ratio:

\displaystyle\alpha(\mu_{0},R)\coloneqq\inf_{\mu^{\prime}\in B(\mu_{0},R),L(\mu^{\prime})>0}\frac{\|\nabla L(\mu^{\prime})\|}{L(\mu^{\prime})}.

A direct consequence of the
(R,m)
-P-Ł property
eq.

26
is that the risk admits no spurious critical points around
\mu
as all critical points are global minimizers. A second consequence is a local convergence result: if the loss at initialization is sufficiently small then both the risk and the parameterization converge, respectively to
0
and to some global minimizer
\mu_{*}
of the risk.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
SMP23
, Cor.1.5]
)
.

Assume that
L
is
(R,m)
-P-Ł around an initial parameterization
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. If the initial risk satisfies:

\displaystyle L(\mu_{0})<\frac{mR^{2}}{4}

(27)

then, for some
T\in(0,+\infty]
, if
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T)}
is a curve of maximal slope for
L
we have

\displaystyle L(\mu_{t})\leq e^{-mt}L(\mu_{0}),\quad\forall t\in[0,T).

Moreover
\mu_{T}\coloneqq\lim_{t\to T}\mu_{t}
exists and satisfies
d(\mu_{T},\mu_{0})\leq R
.

The above convergence result is open in the sense that if its assumptions are satisfied for some initialization
\mu_{0}
then it is also the case for any initialization
\mu_{0}^{\prime}
sufficiently close to
\mu_{0}
, this is the content of the following
4.1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
L:\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies the assumptions of
4.1
around some
\mu_{0}
.
Then there exists a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
\mu_{0}
such that any initialization
\mu_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{U}
also satisfies these assumptions.

Proof.

Note that, by definition of the
(R,m)
-P-Ł property, if it is satisfied around
\mu_{0}
then, for any
\delta\in(0,R)
a
(R-\delta,m)
-P-Ł property is satisfied around
\mu_{0}^{\prime}
for any
\mu_{0}^{\prime}\in B(\mu_{0},\delta)
. Moreover the condition
eq.

27
is open: if it is satisfied at
\mu_{0}
with
R
and
m
then it is satisfied at any
\mu_{0}^{\prime}\in B(\mu_{0},\delta)
with
R-\delta
and
m
provided
\delta
is sufficiently small.
∎

4.2
Convergence for general architecture

We explain here how one can prove convergence of the gradient flow towards a minimizer of the risk
L
when training the
\mathrm{NODE}
model. More precisely, we give sufficient conditions for
L
to satisfy a
(R,m)
-P-Ł property around the gradient flow initialization, and as a consequence of
4.1
, these ensure convergence towards a global minimizer of the risk. Those conditions are first described at a general level and later specified for practical examples of architectures and initializations.

Specifically, we will consider the case where the data distribution is the empirical distribution
\mathcal{D}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{(x^{i},y^{i})}
for a dataset
\{(x^{i},y^{i})\}_{1\leq i\leq N}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
. In this case, the risk
L
for a parameterization
\mu
is given by the
empirical risk
:

\displaystyle L(\mu)=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell(x^{i}_{\mu}(1),y^{i}),

where
x^{i}_{\mu}=x^{i}_{\mu}
is the flow of
eq.

2
starting at
x^{i}
and with parameterization
\mu
, which we will simply note
x^{i}
when no ambiguity. Similarly when note
p^{i}_{\mu}\coloneqq p_{\mu,x^{i},y^{i}}
, or simply
p^{i}
when no ambiguity, the associated adjoint variable of
eq.

10
. We also suppose that
\phi
satisfies at least
1
,
2
and
3
.
In this setting, using
eq.

24
we get for any
\mu\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathrm{Leb}}_{2}([0,1]\times\Omega)
:

\displaystyle|\nabla L|^{2}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x^{i}(s))^{\top}p^{i}(s)\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu(s,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}p^{i}(s)^{\top}K[\mu(.|s)](x^{i}(s),x^{j}(s))p^{j}(s)\right)\mathrm{d}s,

where for
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
we define the kernel
K[\nu]:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
as:

\displaystyle\forall x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad K[\nu](x,x^{\prime})\coloneqq\int_{\omega}\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x)\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x^{\prime})^{\top}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega).

(28)

For
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\Omega)
and a point cloud
\mathbf{z}=(z^{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
we will note
\mathbb{K}[\mu,\mathbf{z}]\in\mathbb{R}^{dN\times dN}
the
kernel matrix
associated to
K[\mu]
and defined as the block matrix:

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}[\mu,\mathbf{z}]\coloneqq\left(K[\mu](z^{i},z^{j})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.

(29)

In particular, we see that the conditioning
\lambda_{\min}\left(\mathbb{K}[\mu,\mathbf{z}]\right)
of the kernel matrix will play an important role in proving a local P-Ł property for the risk
L
. Indeed, in terms of the kernel matrix
\mathbb{K}
, the square gradient can be written :

\displaystyle|\nabla L|^{2}(\mu)=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\langle\mathbf{p}_{\mu}(s),\mathbb{K}[\mu(.|s),\mathbf{x}_{\mu}(s)]\mathbf{p}_{\mu}(s)\rangle\mathrm{d}s,

(30)

where for every
s\in[0,1]
we defined the point cloud
\mathbf{x}_{\mu}(s)\coloneqq(x^{i}_{\mu}(s))\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N}
and where we concatenated the adjoint variables into
\mathbf{p}_{\mu}(s)\coloneqq(p^{i}_{\mu}(s))_{1\leq i\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{dN}
. In the following, when no ambiguity we will simply note
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{\mu}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1],(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N})
and
\mathbf{p}=\mathbf{p}_{\mu}\in\mathcal{C}([0,1],\mathbb{R}^{dN})
.

Remark 4.2

(Kernels, RKHS and feature representation)
.

Due to its definition, the above function
K[\mu]
is obviously a (vector valued)
positive kernel
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in the sense that:

\displaystyle\forall(x^{i})_{1\leq i\leq N},(\alpha^{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{N},\quad\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\langle\alpha^{i},K[\mu](x^{i},x^{i})\alpha^{j}\rangle\geq 0.

It is a classical result that every such kernel defines a unique structure of
Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS)
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a Hilbert space of function for which the evaluation function is continuous
[
Car+10
]
.

Note that the kernel
K[\mu]
is here directly given by a
feature representation
, that is a representation of the form
K[\mu](x,y)=\chi(x)^{*}\chi(y)
with a map
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathcal{H})
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Here one can for example consider
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mu)
and
\chi:x\mapsto\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(.,x)^{\top}
. If such a representation always defines a positive kernel, one can conversely show that such a representation always exists whenever
K
is a positive kernel
[
Car+10
]
. This representation can however not be expected to be unique and corresponds with a certain square root of
K[\mu]
viewed as an integral operator
[
Bac17
]
.

To obtain a P-Ł inequality for the risk
L
we will from now on assume that the loss function
\ell
itself is P-Ł w.r.t. the
x
variable.

Assumption 4
.

We assume that the loss
\ell
satisfies a P-Ł inequality w.r.t.

that is:

\displaystyle\forall(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}},\quad\|\nabla_{x}\ell(x,y)\|^{2}\geq 2\ell(x,y).

This assumption is in particular satisfied in regression problems by the quadratic loss
\ell(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\|x-y\|^{2}
or locally by the cross entropy loss
\ell(x,y)=\log\left(\frac{\sum_{j}y[j]\exp(x[j])}{\sum_{j}\exp(x[j])}\right)
in classification.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\phi
satisfies
1
,
2
and
3
and
\ell
satisfies
4
. Consider
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu))
s.t.:

\displaystyle|\nabla L|^{2}(\mu)\geq\frac{2e^{-C}}{N}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{K}[\mu(.|s),\mathbf{x}_{\mu}(s)])\mathrm{d}s\right)L(\mu),

(31)

Proof.

Thanks to
3
and the definition of
p^{i}_{\mu}
there exists a constant
C=C(\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mu))
such that for every
1\leq i\leq N
we have the estimate:

\displaystyle\|p^{i}_{\mu}(s)\|^{2}\geq e^{-C}\|p^{i}_{\mu}(1)\|^{2},\quad\forall s\in[0,1].

Using that
p^{i}\mu(1)=\nabla_{x}\ell(x^{i}\mu(1),y^{i})
and with the previous
4
we have
\|\mathbf{p}_{\mu}(s)\|^{2}\geq e^{-C}\|\mathbf{p}_{\mu}(1)\|^{2}\geq 2Ne^{-C}L(\mu)
. Putting this lower bound in
eq.

30
then gives the result
∎

The above
4.1
shows that the conditioning
\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{K})
of the kernel matrix provides a lower bound on the ratio between the square gradient and the risk: having
\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{K})
strictly positive directly implies the P-Ł inequality
eq.

26
for the risk. The above quantity could for example be computed numerically during training. However, at this point, it is not clear how one can be sure, before training, that the P-Ł inequality will hold along the gradient flow. We investigate this problem in the next section for a special kind of architecture.

4.3
Convergence for SHL residuals

We study here in more detail the case of
Single Hidden Layer (SHL)
perceptrons for which we show the kernel matrix is well-conditioned and the risk satisfies a P-Ł property around well-chosen initializations. The SHL architecture is an instance of

1
which we define by setting the parameter space

and the map:

\displaystyle\phi:((u,w,b),x)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto u\sigma(w^{\top}x+b),

(32)

where
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is an activation function. We will generically make the following assumption on the activation

to ensure that our previous assumptions on the feature map

are satisfied.
5
is in particular satisfied for the popular choices that are
\sigma=\tanh
or any smooth approximation of
\mathrm{ReLU}
such as

or
\mathrm{Swish}

Assumption 5
.

The activation
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice continuously differentiable function with a uniformly bounded derivative.
Noting
M=M(\sigma)\coloneqq|\sigma(0)|+\|\sigma^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
we have for
(x,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\Omega
:

\displaystyle\|\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq M(1+\|x\|)(1+\|\omega\|^{2}),\quad\|\mathrm{D}_{\omega}\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq M(1+\|x\|)(1+\|\omega\|),\quad\|\mathrm{D}_{x}\phi(\omega,x)\|\leq M\|\omega\|^{2}.

(33)

Note that this assumption ensures that

,
2

Recall the definition of the kernel

in

28

we have

where we define for every

:

\displaystyle k^{1}[\mu](x,y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}\sigma(w^{\top}x+b)\sigma(w^{\top}y+b)\mathrm{d}\mu(u,w,b)

(34)

\displaystyle K^{2}[\mu](x,y)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}}\sigma^{\prime}(w^{\top}x+b)\sigma^{\prime}(w^{\top}y+b)(x^{\top}y+1)(u\otimes u)\mathrm{d}\mu(u,w,b).

Remark 4.3

(Feature distribution and representation of

in

.

Similarly to

in

here only depends the marginal of

. This representation bridges the gap with the work of

For a parameterization

As one can see in the previous condition, the better the conditioning of the kernel matrix, the better the constants in the local P-Ł property, and hence the easier it is to satisfy the condition for convergence. This conditioning will depend on the choice of activation and initialization and it is important to keep in mind that the P-Ł property is not expected to hold around any initialization. For example, there is a saddle at every initialization
\mu_{0}
with feature distribution
\mu_{0}^{2}=\delta_{(w,b)=0}
whenever
\sigma(0)=\sigma^{\prime}(0)=0
.
In the following, we provide examples of activations
\sigma
and initializations

for which the kernel matrix is well-conditioned.

Identity (or
FixUp
) initialization

It will be particularly convenient to consider initial parameterization of the form
\mu_{0}=\mathrm{Leb}([0,1])\otimes\delta_{0}\otimes\mu_{0}^{2}
for some
\mu_{0}^{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})
, i.e. parameterization whose disintegration
\mu_{0}(.|s)=\delta_{0}\otimes\mu_{0}^{2}
is independent of

and has support in

Such an initialization has been proposed for ResNets in
[
ZDM18
]
and is shown to be associated with robust training and good generalization performances. Moreover, note that such an initialization is particularly natural for
\mathrm{NODEs}

F_{\mu_{0}}
is identically

and the associated
\mathrm{NODE}
flow is the identity. As a consequence the kernel matrix
\mathbb{K}^{1}[\mu_{0}]
is independent of

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}^{1}[\mu_{0}]=\left(k^{1}[\mu_{0}](x^{i},x^{j})\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N},

only depending on the feature distribution
\mu_{0}^{2}
and on the input data distribution
\mathcal{D}_{x}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x^{i}}
.

Data separation and strictly positive kernel

In order to ensure the positivity of the kernel matrix
\mathbb{K}_{\mu_{0}}^{1}
we will assume that the input data
(x^{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
are

\delta>0

\displaystyle\min_{i\neq j}\|x^{i}-x^{j}\|\eqqcolon\delta>0.

(36)

Note that, when sampling
N

\mathbb{R}^{d}

\delta\sim N^{-1/d}
.
For a kernel

SFL11
]

In general, the feature distribution
\mu^{2}
having dense support is a sufficient condition to ensure strict positivity. The following proposition is a direct consequence of of
[

, Thm.III.4]

[

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume
\sigma
has linear growth and is not a polynomial. Then if the feature distribution
\mu^{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})
has dense support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
, the kernel

is strictly positive.

[

4.4
Satisfying the convergence condition by lifting and scaling

The previously derived condition for convergence of the gradient flow notably asks for the loss at initialization to be sufficiently low. We conclude the present work by showing how this condition can always be enforced, that is how, for a given training dataset, one can modify the ResNet architecture in a way such that the convergence conditions are satisfied. The modification we propose is inspired by the work of
[
COB19
]
and consists of embedding the data in a higher dimensional space and performing a rescaling.

As before, we consider an empirical data distribution
\mathcal{D}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{x^{i},y^{i}}
where
(x^{i},y^{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
. Consider also respectively the

and

\displaystyle A\coloneqq(\mathrm{Id}_{d},0_{d,d^{\prime}})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{(d+d^{\prime})\times d},\quad B\coloneqq(0_{d^{\prime},d},\mathrm{Id}_{d^{\prime}})\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}\times(d+d^{\prime})}.

Using the matrix

we embed the input variables

in the space
\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}
by noting

. We then consider the
\mathrm{NODE}
model of
1.1
with inputs
(z^{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}
and the feature map
\phi
of the form
eq.

32
with some activation
\sigma
. In this case we have

and for

\displaystyle\phi((u,w,b),x)=u\sigma(w^{\top}x+b).

For an input

the associated flow defined by

Also, for a rescaling factor

\displaystyle\forall(z,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+d^{\prime}}\times\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}},\quad\ell^{\alpha}(z,y)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\|\alpha Bz-y\|^{2}.

We consider training the parameter

\displaystyle L^{\alpha}(\mu)\coloneqq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\ell^{\alpha}(z^{i}_{\mu}(1),y^{i}).

Note that, due to its definition, the loss

\displaystyle\left|\nabla L^{\alpha}\right|^{2}(\mu)\geq\frac{2\alpha^{2}e^{-C}}{N}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\lambda_{\min}(\mathbb{K}[\mu(.|s),\mathbf{z}_{\mu}(s)])\mathrm{d}s\right)L^{\alpha}(\mu),

(38)

where

is the point cloud

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}^{\mathrm{Leb}}([0,1]\times\Omega)

By

, the map

5
Conclusion

We studied in this paper the convergence of the gradient flow optimization scheme for the training of a ResNet model of infinite depth and arbitrary width. Our model is a Neural ODE parameterized by measures over the product set
[0,1]\times\Omega
with the constraint to have its marginal on
[0,1]
to be the Lebesgue measure. Introducing the framework of Conditional Optimal Transport, we provided this set of parameters with a metric structure modeling the metric implicitly used when training ResNets with automatic differentiation. Leveraging results from the theory of gradient flows in metric spaces
[
AGS08
]
, this property of the Conditional OT metric allowed us to show that the gradient flow equation, derived formally by adjoint sensitivity analysis and used for the training of Neural ODEs, is equivalent to a curve of maximal slope of the risk. The well-posedness of the gradient flow equation follows from this result. Finally, we showed that convergence of mean field models of ResNet can be proved by using a Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality. This inequality is satisfied locally around well-chosen initializations for which the residuals have sufficiently (but possibly finitely) many features, ensuring their expressivity. As a consequence, assuming the risk is already sufficiently small at those initializations, the gradient flow provably converges towards a global minimizer of the risk with an exponential convergence rate. This is the first result of this type for mean-field models of ResNets with unregularized risk as previous works only showed results of optimality under the assumption of convergence.
Finally, for practical examples of architectures and parameter initialization, we quantified explicitly the convergence condition as a function of the number of data points.

We point out some limitations and possible extensions of our work:

•

We only consider training our ResNet model with gradient flow, an idealized version of the more realistic gradient descent algorithm. An extension of our results to the case of optimization with gradient descent should probably hold due to the local P-Ł property of the risk. However, this could be at the cost of considering stronger regularity assumptions on the feature map
\phi
to ensure the gradient descent dynamic stays close to the gradient flow dynamic for small step sizes.

•

We make regularity assumptions on the feature map
\phi
that might be improved on. In particular,
3
assumes
\phi
to be at least continuously differentiable which does not allow us to consider SHL residuals with
\mathrm{ReLU}
activations. This assumption might be weakened, for example by using the recently introduced notion of
conservative gradient

[
Bol+21
]
.

•

We only considered in our convergence analysis of
section

4
the case of an empirical data distribution
\mathcal{D}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{(x^{i},y^{i})}
. This assumption is crucial as the P-Ł constant in
eq.

31
scales as
N^{-1}
and become degenerate for large
N
. It would therefore be interesting to extend our analysis to the case of a data distribution with density.

•

An important feature of our convergence analysis is to only leverage information about the gradient w.r.t. the outer weights of the residuals (noted with the variable
u
) to obtain the Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality. In doing so, we are unable to provide information about the behavior of the feature distribution during training and unable to ensure that gradient flow will escape the “kernel regime”.
Therefore, leveraging information about the feature dynamic to improve our convergence proof is an exciting perspective.

Appendix A

Uniform resolvent and orthonormal Strichartz estimates for repulsive Hamiltonian
Akitoshi Hoshiya

Abstract
We consider the uniform resolvent and orthonormal Strichartz estimates for the Schrödinger operator. First we prove the Keel-Tao type theorem for the orthonormal Strichartz estimates, which means that the dispersive estimates yield the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for strongly continuous unitary groups. This result applies to many Schrödinger propagators which are difficult to treat by the smooth perturbation theory, for example, local-in-time estimates for the Schrödinger operator with unbounded electromagnetic potentials, the ( k , a ) (k,a) -generalized Laguerre operators and global-in-time estimates for the Schrödinger operator with scaling critical magnetic potentials including the Aharonov-Bohm potentials. Next we observe mapping properties of resolvents for the repulsive Hamiltonian and apply to the orthonormal Strichartz estimates. We prove the Kato-Yajima type uniform resolvent estimates with logarithmic decaying weight functions. This is new even when without perturbations. The proof is dependent on the microlocal analysis and the Mourre theory. We also discuss mapping properties on the Schwartz class and the Lebesgue space.

2
A refinement of the Keel-Tao theorem for strongly continuous unitary groups

2.1
Abstract theorem

In this subsection we prove a refinement of the Keel-Tao theorem, which states that the dispersive estimate implies the orthonormal Strichartz estimate. For
r\geq 2

First we assume

(2.11)

) for all

2.2
Applications to some operators

In this subsection we give some applications of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.3
. These examples do not follow from the perturbation theory in
[
H1
]
and
[
H2
]
due to the lack of the Kato smoothing estimate. First we consider the magnetic Schrödinger operator with unbounded electromagnetic potentials and partially improve the known results in
[
Y2
]
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
H=(D_{x}-A)^{2}+V
be the magnetic Schrödinger operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying the following conditions:

1.

Electric potential
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function satisfying
|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}V(x)|\lesssim 1
if
|\alpha|\geq 2
.

2.

Magnetic potential
A:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is smooth and satisfies
|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}A(x)|\lesssim 1
if
|\alpha|\geq 1
.

3.

Magnetic field
B:=(\frac{\partial A_{k}}{\partial x_{j}}-\frac{\partial A_{j}}{\partial x_{k}})_{j,k}
satisfies
|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}B(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1-\epsilon}
if
|\alpha|\geq 1
for some
\epsilon>0
.

Then it is well-known that
H
is essentially self-adjoint on
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore Yajima (
[
Y2
]
and
[
Y3
]
) proved the following expression of the propagator:

\displaystyle e^{-i(t-s)H}u=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}E(t,s,x,y)u(y)dy,\quad E(t,s,x,y)=\frac{e^{\mp id\pi/4}}{(2\pi|t-s|)^{\frac{d}{2}}}e^{iS(t,s,x,y)}e(t,s,x,y)

(2.12)

for all
u\in\mathcal{S}
and
0<\pm(t-s)<T\ll 1
. Here
S
is a real-valued function solving the Hamilton-Jacobi equation and
e
satisfies
|e(t,s,x,y)-1|\lesssim|t-s|
. Therefore by taking sufficiently small
T>0
,

\displaystyle\|e^{-i(t-s)H}u\|_{\infty}\lesssim|t-s|^{-\frac{d}{2}}\|u\|_{1}

holds for
t\neq s
satisfying
|t|,|s|<T
. By applying Corollary
1.3
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu_{j}|e^{-itH}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{T}L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

for any
\frac{d}{2}
-admissible pair
(q,r)
and
\beta
satisfying the assumption in Theorem
1.1
with
\sigma=\frac{d}{2}
. Here
L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{T}L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x}
denotes
L^{\frac{q}{2}}([-T,T];L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})
. For any
N\in\mathbb{N}
we calculate

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu_{j}|e^{-itH}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{NT}L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x}}\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{4N-1}\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu_{j}|e^{-itH}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}([-NT+\frac{k}{2}T,-NT+\frac{k+1}{2}T];L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}

by the triangle inequality. Since
\{e^{-i(-NT+\frac{k}{2}T)H}f_{j}\}_{j}
is an orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
N
and
k
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu_{j}|e^{-itH}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}([-NT+\frac{k}{2}T,-NT+\frac{k+1}{2}T];L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu_{j}|e^{-itH}(e^{-i(-NT+\frac{k}{2}T)H}f_{j})|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}([0,\frac{T}{2}];L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}.

Hence we have proved the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
H
be the magnetic Schrödinger operator introduced in Example
2.3
. For any bounded interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu_{j}|e^{-itH}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(I;L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

for any
(q,r,\beta)
satisfying the assumption in Theorem
1.1
with
\sigma=\frac{d}{2}
.

We notice that, in Corollary
2.4
, the implicit constant is dependent on the volume of
I
. Actually it is impossible to take
I=\mathbb{R}
in general. As a counterexample we have
H=-\Delta+x^{2}
, which has infinitely many eigenfunctions. Next we consider the
(k,a)
-generalized Laguerre operator. Recently the ordinary and orthonormal Strichartz estimates are considered in
[
GMS
]
,
[
MS1
]
and
[
TT
]
but the latter are restricted to
a=1
or
2
(see
[
MS1
]
).

Example 2.5
.

We recall the definition of the
(k,a)
-generalized Laguerre operator, which was introduced in
[
BKO
]
. Let
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
be a reduced root system. The finite Coxeter group associated with
\mathcal{R}
is a subgroup
C\subset O(d)
generated by
\{r_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}}
, where
r_{\alpha}(x):=x-2\frac{x\cdot\alpha}{|\alpha|^{2}}\alpha
denotes the reflection. If
k:\mathcal{R}\to\mathbb{C}
is invariant under the finite Coxeter group associated with
\mathcal{R}
,
k
is called a multiplicity function. For
\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
, a non-negative multiplicity function
k
and
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we define

\displaystyle T_{\xi}(k)f(x):=\partial_{\xi}f(x)+\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}k(\alpha)\alpha\cdot\xi\frac{f(x)-f(r_{\alpha}x)}{\alpha\cdot x}

where
\mathcal{R}^{+}
is a positive subsystem of
\mathcal{R}
and
\partial_{\xi}
denotes the directional derivative for
\xi
. It is known that this definition is independent of the choice of
\mathcal{R}^{+}
(see
[
BKO
]
pp.1275). The Dunkl Laplacian
\Delta_{k}
is defined by
\Delta_{k}:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\{T_{\xi_{j}}(k)\}^{2}
, where
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This is also independent of the choice of an orthonormal basis (see
[
BKO
]
pp.1278). Then the
(k,a)
-generalized Laguerre operator
H_{k,a}
is defined by

\displaystyle H_{k,a}:=\frac{-|x|^{2-a}\Delta_{k}+|x|^{a}}{a}

for
a>0
. We set
\langle k\rangle:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}k(\alpha)
,
\sigma_{k,a}:=\frac{a+2\langle k\rangle+d-2}{a}
and
\vartheta_{k,a}(x):=|x|^{a-2}\Pi_{\alpha\in\mathcal{R}^{+}}|\alpha\cdot x|^{2k(\alpha)}
. It is proved in
[
BKO
]
Corollary 3.22 that if
\sigma_{k,a}>0
then
H_{k,a}
is essentially self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\vartheta_{k,a}(x)dx)
and its spectrum is discrete. Recently Taira and Tamori (
[
TT
]
) proved that if
\sigma_{k,a}>0
and either of the following holds:

1.

d=1
and
a\geq 2-4\langle k\rangle

2.

d\geq 2
and
0<a\leq 1
or
d\geq 2
and
a=2

then the dispersive estimate holds:
\|e^{-itH_{k,a}}\|_{1\to\infty}\lesssim|t|^{-\sigma_{k,a}}
for
|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
. Then by using Corollary
1.3
and repeating a similar argument as in Example
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu_{j}|e^{-itH_{k,a}}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(I;L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

(2.13)

where
I\subset\mathbb{R}
is an arbitrary bounded interval and
(q,r,\beta)
satisfies the condition in Theorem
1.1
with
\sigma=\sigma_{k,a}
. We notice that
L^{p}_{x}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\vartheta_{k,a}(x)dx)
. Next we assume
d\geq 2
,
k\equiv 0
and
a\in(1,2)
. We take
\phi_{0}\in(0,2\pi)
and a finite partition of unity
\{\chi_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
for
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
satisfying
\omega,\eta\in\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\chi_{i}\Rightarrow\omega\cdot\eta\geq\cos\phi_{0}
. It is proved in
[
TT
]
that

\displaystyle\|\chi_{i}(\hat{x})e^{-i(t-s)H_{k,a}}\chi_{i}(\hat{x})\|_{1\to\infty}\lesssim|t-s|^{-\sigma_{k,a}}

holds for
t,s\in(-\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{\pi}{4})
, where
\hat{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}
. Then by repeating the proof of Proposition
2.2
and Corollary
1.3
with
\phi\equiv 1
and
\{T_{z}\}
replaced by
\{\chi_{i}(\hat{x})\chi_{I}(t)T_{z}\chi_{I}(t)\chi_{i}(\hat{x})\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu_{j}|\chi_{i}(\hat{x})e^{-itH_{k,a}}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(I;L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

where
I,(q,r,\beta)
are as above. By taking a sum with respect to

, we obtain (

). We notice that these are refinements of Theorem 1.1 in
[
TT
]
and an extension of
[
MS1
]
. It would be also possible to prove the global-in-time orthonormal Strichartz estimate for

[

The next example is concerned with the Schrödinger operator with scaling critical electromagnetic potentials. The orthonormal Strichartz estimate for the Schrödinger operator with scaling critical electric potentials (including inverse square potentials) are proved in

Example 2.6
.

Let
H_{A,a}
be the magnetic Schrödinger operator defined by

\displaystyle H_{A,a}:=\left(D_{x}+\frac{A(\hat{x})}{|x|}\right)^{2}+\frac{a(\hat{x})}{|x|^{2}}

on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Here

,

for all

. If

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\nu_{j}|e^{-itH_{A,a}}f_{j}|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{t}L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{l^{\beta}}

for all

.

Remark 2.7
.

We here give more comments and examples that are not considered before.

•

There seems to be no result on the orthonormal Strichartz estimate for variable coefficient operators or the Schrödinger operator on manifolds except for the Laplacian on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
(
[
N
]
). Concerning the Laplacian on compact Riemannian manifolds, the ordinary global-in-time Strichartz estimate never holds but the local-in-time Strichartz estimate holds with some derivative loss (
[
BGT
]
). This is also true for non-elliptic operators (
[
MT
]
). On non-compact manifolds, the ordinary (global-in-time or local-in-time) Strichartz estimate is proved by many authors under non-trapping conditions (
[
BT1
]
,
[
BT2
]
,
[
MMT
]
,
[
M3
]
,
[
M4
]
,
[
MY1
]
and
[
Ta2
]
). However derivative losses may appear without non-trapping conditions. We give examples of non-compact Riemannian manifolds with (infinitely many) trapped classical trajectories on which, however, we have the orthonormal Strichartz estimate without derivative losses. Let

be a convex co-compact subgroup and

. Then

is a hyperbolic manifold with infinitely many trapped geodesics (see
[

]
). However, if its limit set has Hausdorff dimension

, the global dispersive estimate:
\|e^{it\Delta_{X}}\|_{1\to\infty}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{3}{2}}
holds for all

(
[
BGH
]
Theorem 0.1). Therefore by using Theorem
1.1
we obtain the global-in-time orthonormal Strichartz estimate without derivative losses for
(q,r,\beta)
with
\sigma=\frac{3}{2}
.

•

also applies to the Schrödinger operator with the Delta potential:

,

We consider the Stark Hamiltonian with perturbations:

2.3
Refined Strichartz estimate

In this subsection we prove the refined Strichartz estimates. We refer to
[
FS1
]
for
-\Delta
and to
[
H1
]
and
[
H2
]
for perturbed Hamiltonians. First we consider
H
as in Example
2.3
.

Corollary 2.8
.

For
H
as in Example
2.3
, we further assume
V\geq 0
and
0\notin\sigma_{p}(H)
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|e^{-itH}u\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{r}_{x})}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H})}

for all bounded
I\subset\mathbb{R}
,
(q,r)
and
\beta
as in Example
2.3
. Here
\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H})}:=\|\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H})u\|_{2}\|_{l^{2\beta}}
denotes the homogeneous Besov space associated with
H
, where
\{\phi_{j}\}
denotes a homogeneous Littlewood-Paley decomposition.

Proof.

Since we assume
V\geq 0
and
0\notin\sigma_{p}(H)
, by the diamagnetic inequality, we have the following Gaussian upper bound for the Schrödinger semigroup:

\displaystyle|e^{-tH}(x,y)|\lesssim t^{-\frac{d}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{At}}

for some
A>0
and all
t>0
. This yields
\|u\|_{p}\approx\|u\|_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(\sqrt{H})}
, where
\|u\|_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(\sqrt{H})}:=\|\|\{\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H})u\}\|_{l^{2}}\|_{p}
is the homogeneous Triebel-Lizorkin space associated with
H
. We refer to Proposition 4.12 in
[
H1
]
for a similar proof. Then we have

\displaystyle\|e^{-itH}u\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{r}_{x})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|e^{-itH}\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H})u|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(I;L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|e^{-itH}\phi_{2j}(\sqrt{H})u|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(I;L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}+\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|e^{-itH}\phi_{2j+1}(\sqrt{H})u|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(I;L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\phi_{2j}(\sqrt{H})u\|^{2}_{2}\left|e^{-itH}\frac{\phi_{2j}(\sqrt{H})u}{\|\phi_{2j}(\sqrt{H})u\|_{2}}\right|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(I;L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left\|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\phi_{2j+1}(\sqrt{H})u\|^{2}_{2}\left|e^{-itH}\frac{\phi_{2j+1}(\sqrt{H})u}{\|\phi_{2j+1}(\sqrt{H})u\|_{2}}\right|^{2}\right\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(I;L^{\frac{r}{2}}_{x})}

where in the first line we have used
\|u\|_{p}\approx\|u\|_{\dot{F}^{0}_{p,2}(\sqrt{H})}
. By a support property of
\{\phi_{j}\}
,
\left\{\frac{\phi_{2j}(\sqrt{H})u}{\|\phi_{2j}(\sqrt{H})u\|_{2}}\right\}
and
\left\{\frac{\phi_{2j+1}(\sqrt{H})u}{\|\phi_{2j+1}(\sqrt{H})u\|_{2}}\right\}
are orthonormal systems in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore by using Corollary
2.4
we obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{-itH}u\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{r}_{x})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\{\|\phi_{2j}(\sqrt{H})u\|^{2}_{2}\}\|_{l^{\beta}}+\|\{\|\phi_{2j+1}(\sqrt{H})u\|^{2}_{2}\}\|_{l^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H})}

and this gives the desired estimate.
∎

We notice that, since
2\beta>2
, this includes a refinement of
[
Y2
]
, where the ordinary Strichartz estimate
\|e^{-itH}u\|_{L^{q}(I;L^{r}_{x})}\lesssim\|u\|_{2}=\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2}(\sqrt{H})}
is proved. Next we consider the Schrödinger operator with scaling critical electromagnetic potentials discussed in Example
2.6
. The following result is a refinement of
[
FFFP
]
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
H_{A,a}
be as in Example
2.6
. We further assume either
\Phi_{A}\notin\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
or
a(\pi-\theta)=a(\pi+\theta)
for
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|e^{-itH_{A,a}}u\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})}

for all
(q,r,\beta)
as in Example
2.6
. Notice that
\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})
is similarly defined as in Corollary
2.8
.

Proof.

By our assumption and Proposition 1.2 in
[
GYZZ
]
, the Gaussian upper bound:

\displaystyle|e^{-tH_{A,a}}(x,y)|\lesssim t^{-1}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{At}}

holds for some
A>0
and all
t>0
. Hence by repeating the argument in the proof of Corollary
2.8
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|e^{-itH_{A,a}}u\|_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H_{A,a}})}

for all
(q,r,\beta)
as in Example
2.6
. Now we employ the following Kato-Yajima estimate:

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}}\||x|^{-1}(H_{A,a}-z)^{-1}|x|^{-1}\|_{2\to 2}\lesssim 1

(2.14)

which is proved in
[
GWZZ
]
. Then

\displaystyle|\langle e^{itH_{A,a}}e^{-itH_{A,0}}u,v\rangle-\langle e^{isH_{A,a}}e^{-isH_{A,0}}u,v\rangle|

\displaystyle=\left|i\int_{s}^{t}\langle\frac{a(\hat{x})}{|x|}e^{-irH_{A,0}}u,\frac{1}{|x|}e^{-irH_{A,a}}v\rangle dr\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\frac{1}{|x|}e^{-irH_{A,0}}u\right\|_{L^{2}([s,t];L^{2}_{x})}\left\|\frac{1}{|x|}e^{-irH_{A,a}}v\right\|_{L^{2}_{r}L^{2}_{x}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v\|_{2}\left\|\frac{1}{|x|}e^{-irH_{A,0}}u\right\|_{L^{2}([s,t];L^{2}_{x})}

holds. This yields

\displaystyle\|e^{itH_{A,a}}e^{-itH_{A,0}}u-e^{isH_{A,a}}e^{-isH_{A,0}}u\|_{2}\lesssim\left\|\frac{1}{|x|}e^{-irH_{A,0}}u\right\|_{L^{2}([s,t];L^{2}_{x})}\to 0

as
s,t\to\infty
, where we have used (
2.14
) for
a=0
. Hence we have proved the existence of wave operators
W_{\pm}:=\slim_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{itH_{A,a}}e^{-itH_{A,0}}
. The completeness of
W_{\pm}
can be similarly proved. Then by the intertwining property

\displaystyle\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,a}})u\|_{2}=\|W_{\pm}\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})W^{*}_{\pm}u\|_{2}\leq\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})W^{*}_{\pm}u\|_{2}

holds. By taking the
l^{2\beta}
norm with respect to
j
, we obtain
\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H_{A,a}})}\lesssim\|W^{*}_{\pm}u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})}
. Though the ordinary Hardy inequality fails if
d=2
, we have the following Hardy inequality with magnetic potentials:

\displaystyle\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|k-\Phi_{A}|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{|u|^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left|\left(D_{x}+\frac{A(\hat{x})}{|x|}\right)u\right|^{2}dx

for all
u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\})
(see
[
GYZZ
]
). By our assumption and this inequality we have
\langle H_{A,a}u,u\rangle\approx\langle H_{A,0}u,u\rangle
and hence
H^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}_{A,a}H^{\mp\frac{1}{2}}_{A,0}
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Again by the intertwining property

\displaystyle\|H^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}_{A,0}W^{*}_{\pm}u\|_{2}=\|W^{*}_{\pm}H^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}_{A,a}u\|_{2}\leq\|H^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}_{A,a}u\|_{2}\lesssim\|H^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}_{A,0}u\|_{2}

holds. This implies that
W^{*}_{\pm}
are bounded on
\dot{H}^{\pm 1}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})
, where
\dot{H}^{\pm 1}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})
are defined similarly as the Besov spaces associated with
H_{A,0}
. Now we use the real interpolation:
(\dot{H}^{s_{0}}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}}),\dot{H}^{s_{1}}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}}))_{\theta,q}=\dot{B}^{s}_{2,q}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})
for
s_{0}<s<s_{1}
,
s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
q\in(2,\infty)
, which is proved in Appendix
A
. Then
W^{*}_{\pm}
are bounded on
\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})
. As a result we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Note that this argument does not hold if we replace
\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})
with
\dot{B}^{0}_{2,2\beta}
since we do not have the equivalence:
\langle-\Delta u,u\rangle\approx\langle H_{A,a}u,u\rangle
.

2.4
Mass critical NLS with scaling critical electromagnetic potentials

In this subsection we discuss about the
L^{2}
-critical NLS with scaling critical electromagnetic potentials:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u=H_{A,a}u+\lambda|u|^{2}u\\
u(0)=u_{0}\end{array}\right.

(NLS)

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. It is known (at least for
A=a=0
) that if the nonlinearity is
\lambda|u|^{p}u
with
p<2
, the corresponding NLS is globally well-posed in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Concerning (
NLS
), if
\|u_{0}\|_{2}
is sufficiently small, there exists a unique global solution to (
NLS
). By using the refined Strichartz estimate proved in subsection
2.3
, we refine this result and prove that a unique solution scatters.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
H_{A,a}
be as in Corollary
2.9
. Then for any
M>0
, there exists
\eta>0
such that if
\|u_{0}\|_{2}<M
and
\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})}<\eta
, then we have a unique global solution to (
NLS
) satisfying
u\in C(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{x})\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})
. Furthermore
u
scatters as
t\to\pm\infty
, i.e. there exists
u_{\pm}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\|u(t)-e^{-itH_{A,0}}u_{\pm}\|_{2}=0.

Proof.

The proof is based on a standard contraction mapping argument. We set

\displaystyle X:=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{x})\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})\mid\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}\leq K\eta^{\frac{1}{4}}\right\}.

where
K,\eta>0
are specified later.
Then
X
is a complete metric space with respect to
d(u_{1},u_{2})=\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{x})\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}
. We prove that

\displaystyle\Phi:u\mapsto e^{-itH_{A,a}}u_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)H_{A,a}}(|u|^{2}u)(s)ds

is a contraction on
X
if
\eta>0
is small enough. We take
u\in X
. Then

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{-itH_{A,a}}u_{0}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)H_{A,a}}(|u|^{2}u)(s)ds\right\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{-itH_{A,a}}u_{0}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}+C_{0}\||u|^{2}u\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle=\|e^{-itH_{A,a}}u_{0}\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}+C_{0}\|u\|^{3}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,\frac{8}{3}}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})}+C_{0}(K\eta^{\frac{1}{4}})^{3}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{4}}_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{3}{4}}_{2}+C_{0}(K\eta^{\frac{1}{4}})^{3}

\displaystyle\leq(C_{1}M^{\frac{3}{4}}+C_{0}K^{3}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}})\eta^{\frac{1}{4}}

holds, where in the second line we have used the inhomogeneous Strichartz estimate, in the fourth line we have used Corollary
2.9
and in the last line
\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{2,\infty}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})}<\eta
and
\|u_{0}\|_{2}<M
have been used. By a similar calculation we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{x})}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{2}+C_{2}\|u\|^{3}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}<\infty

Then by setting
K=2C_{1}M^{\frac{3}{4}}
and
\eta>0
sufficiently small, we obtain
\Phi:X\to X
. Concerning a difference we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)-\Phi(v)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{x})\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)H_{A,a}}(|u|^{2}u-|v|^{2}v)(s)ds\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{x})\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}\||u|^{2}u-|v|^{2}v\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}+\|v\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})})\|u-v\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle\leq 2C_{4}K^{2}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u-v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L^{2}_{x})\cap L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})}.

Therefore by taking
\eta<\min\{(\frac{C_{1}M^{\frac{3}{4}}}{C_{0}K^{3}})^{2},\frac{1}{(4C_{4}K^{2})^{2}}\}
,
\Phi:X\to X
is a contraction. This implies the unique existence of a global solution to (
NLS
). Next we prove that the soluion
u
scatters. By the inhomogeneous Strichartz estimate

\displaystyle\|e^{itH_{A,a}}u(t)-e^{isH_{A,a}}u(s)\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(r)-e^{-i(r-s)H_{A,a}}u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t];L^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{s}^{r}e^{-i(r-k)H_{A,a}}(|u|^{2}u)(k)dk\right\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t];L^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||u|^{2}u\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}([s,t];L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{x})}=\|u\|^{3}_{L^{4}([s,t];L^{4}_{x})}

holds. Since
u\in L^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x})
, this implies the existence of
\widetilde{u_{\pm}}=\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{itH_{A,a}}u(t)
. Furthermore, by the completeness of wave operators
W_{\pm}=\slim_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{itH_{A,a}}e^{-itH_{A,0}}
(see the proof of Corollary
2.9
), there exist
u_{\pm}
such that
u_{\pm}=\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}e^{itH_{A,0}}e^{-itH_{A,a}}\widetilde{u_{\pm}}
. Then

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{-itH_{A,0}}u_{\pm}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|u(t)-e^{-itH_{A,a}}\widetilde{u_{\pm}}\|_{2}+\|e^{-itH_{A,a}}\widetilde{u_{\pm}}-e^{-itH_{A,0}}u_{\pm}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow 0

holds as
t\to\pm\infty
.
∎

3
Uniform resolvent estimate for repulsive Hamiltonian

In this section we consider
H=H_{0}+V
,
H_{0}=-\Delta-x^{2}
and
V\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
d\geq 1
. First we introduce Fourier integral operators (FIOs) that make our analysis simpler. See
[
Dy
]
,
[
Ma
]
and
[
Hö
]
for Lagrangian distributions and FIOs. For a canonical transform
\kappa
on
T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we define
I(\kappa)
as the set of all the FIOs associated with
\kappa
. Since the symbol of
H_{0}
is
\xi^{2}-x^{2}
, we consider the canonical transform

\displaystyle\kappa:(x,\xi)\mapsto(y,\eta)=\left(\frac{x+\xi}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{\xi-x}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

(3.1)

By this transform,
\xi^{2}-x^{2}
changes into
2y\eta
. To look at
\kappa
more closely, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{h}:u\longmapsto\frac{1}{(2\pi h)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\xi/h}u(x)dx

\displaystyle J_{B}:u\longmapsto|\det B|^{\frac{1}{2}}u\circ B

\displaystyle K_{C}:u\longmapsto e^{-i\langle Cx,x\rangle/2h}u

where
B
is an invertible matrix and
C
is a non-degenerate symmetric matrix. Then it is well-known that
\mathcal{F}_{h}\in I(\ell)
,
J_{B}\in I(j_{B})
and
K_{C}\in I(k_{C})
holds, where
\ell:(x,\xi)\mapsto(-\xi,x)
,
j_{B}:(x,\xi)\mapsto(Bx,{}^{t}B^{-1}\xi)
and
k_{C}:(x,\xi)\mapsto(x,\xi+Cx)
are corresponding canonical transforms. Since we can decompose
\kappa:(x,\xi)\mapsto(x,x+\xi)\mapsto(-(x+\xi),x)\mapsto\left(\frac{x+\xi}{\sqrt{2}},-\sqrt{2}x\right)\mapsto\left(\frac{x+\xi}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{\xi-x}{\sqrt{2}}\right)
, we define

\displaystyle U_{h}:u\longmapsto K_{id}J_{-\sqrt{2}id}\mathcal{F}_{h}K_{id}u=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2}\pi h)^{\frac{d}{2}}}e^{-ix^{2}/2h}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{i\sqrt{2}xy/h}e^{-iy^{2}/2h}u(y)dy

where
id
denotes the
d\times d
identity matrix. Then
U_{h}
is a FIO associated with
\kappa
since

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\Phi}

\displaystyle:=\{(x,\partial_{x}\Phi,y,\partial_{y}\Phi)\mid(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\}

\displaystyle=\{(x,-x+\sqrt{2}y,y,-y+\sqrt{2}x)\mid(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2d}\}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(x,\xi,\frac{x+\xi}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{x-\xi}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\mid(x,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{d}\right\}

\displaystyle=\{(x,\xi,y,-\eta)\mid(y,\eta)=\kappa(x,\xi)\}

holds, where
\Phi(x,y)=-\frac{x^{2}}{2}-\frac{y^{2}}{2}+\sqrt{2}xy
is a phase function of the Schwartz kernel of
U_{h}
. By the unitarity of
\mathcal{F}_{h},J_{B}
and
K_{C}
,
U_{h}
is also a unitary operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Furthermore
U_{h}:\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is a homeomorphism. We set

[

[

\displaystyle U^{*}V_{n}U=V_{n}^{w}\left(\frac{x-D_{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)

\displaystyle V_{n}^{w}\left(\frac{x-D_{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)u(x)

3.1
Kato-Yajima estimate for the repulsive Hamiltonian

In this subsection we prove the Kato-Yajima type uniform resolvent estimate for
H
under weaker assumptions on the decay order of
V
than
[
KaYo
]
. Their result is based on the LAP bound by
[
BCHM
]
in the middle energy regime. On the other hand, in the high or low energy regime, they use the dispersive estimate for

and Hölder’s inequality to obtain a uniform bound. Due to the use of Hölder’s inequality, their weight functions and perturbations are assumed to be decaying with polinomial order. In order to gain a uniform resolvent estimate with logarithmic decaying weight functions and perturbations, we employ the weakly conjugate operator method, which is a version of the Mourre theory. It has been used for the Schrödinger operator with inverse square type potentials (
[

[

[

[

]
for more abstract result. However there seems to be no result on the Schrödinger operator with growing potentials. In this subsection, as a conjugate operator, we use

\displaystyle A=a^{w}(x,D_{x}),\quad a(x,\xi)=\log\langle x+\xi\rangle-\log\langle x-\xi\rangle\in S(\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle,g_{0})

. Since
|R_{N,4}|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-N+(2d+1)\rho}
holds, we obtain the desired estimate.
For

, by taking care of the fact that

\displaystyle L_{1}=\langle\langle x\rangle^{-\rho}\eta\rangle^{-2}(1+\langle x\rangle^{-2\rho}\eta D_{y})\quad satisfies\quad L_{1}e^{-iy\eta}=e^{-iy\eta},

\displaystyle L_{2}=-|y|^{-2}yD_{\eta}\quad satisfies\quad L_{2}e^{-iy\eta}=e^{-iy\eta}

and a support property of
\chi_{j}
, if we repeat integration by parts sufficiently many times, we obtain
|I_{2}(x,\xi)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-N}
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Again by changing the order of differentiation and integration, for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
, we obtain
|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}I_{2}(x,\xi)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-N}
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For
I_{3}
, we notice that
\epsilon_{1}\langle x\rangle^{\rho}\leq|\eta|
holds in the support of
\chi_{3}
. Then we use the fact

\displaystyle L_{3}=-|\eta|^{-2}\eta D_{y}\quad satisfies\quad L_{3}e^{-iy\eta}=e^{-iy\eta},

\displaystyle L_{4}=\langle y\rangle^{-2}(1-yD_{\eta})\quad satisfies\quad L_{4}e^{-iy\eta}=e^{-iy\eta}

and repeat integration by parts. As a result we get
|I_{3}(x,\xi)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-N}
for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
. As above, for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
,
|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}I_{3}(x,\xi)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-N}
holds for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We also calculate
c(x,\xi)
in the same manner. Then for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}_{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}(b(x,\xi)-c(x,\xi))|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-1}

in the remainder term large enough. This is because the

\displaystyle\left[V^{w}\left(\frac{x-D_{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\right),\log\langle\sqrt{2}x\rangle\right]\in OPS(\langle x\rangle^{-1},g_{0}).

Then by a symbolic calculus in

\displaystyle\left[\left[V^{w}\left(\frac{x-D_{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\right),\log\langle\sqrt{2}x\rangle\right],\log\langle\sqrt{2}x\rangle\right]\in OPS(1,g_{0}).

Next we consider the case where
(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=(x,D_{x})

\displaystyle[f^{w}(x,D_{x}),\log\langle\sqrt{2}D_{x}\rangle]\in OPS(1,g_{0})

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-2d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4d}}e^{-i(y\eta-z\zeta)}c(x+z/2,\xi+\eta)\log\left\langle\sqrt{2}(\xi+\zeta)\right\rangle dydzd\zeta d\eta

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}e^{iz\zeta}c(x+z/2,\xi)\log\left\langle\sqrt{2}(\xi+\zeta)\right\rangle dzd\zeta.

Instead of the cut-off functions used above, we set

\displaystyle\chi_{2}(\xi,z,\zeta)=\left\{1-\chi\left(\frac{z}{\epsilon_{0}\langle\xi\rangle^{\rho}}\right)\right\}\chi\left(\frac{\zeta}{\epsilon_{1}\langle\xi\rangle}\right),

\displaystyle\chi_{3}(\xi,z,\zeta)=1-\chi\left(\frac{\zeta}{\epsilon_{1}\langle\xi\rangle}\right)

Then we can estimate

are estimated by using integration by parts as above. Since the symbol of

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial^{\beta}_{\xi}\sigma(f^{w}(x,D_{x})\log\langle\sqrt{2}D_{x}\rangle-\log\langle\sqrt{2}D_{x}\rangle f^{w}(x,D_{x}))|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{-1}

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

We set
M_{1}=[H,iA]
,
M_{2}=[[H,iA],iA]
and
S=[H_{0},iA]^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Note that
M_{1},M_{2}
and
S
are bounded operators on

by Lemma
3.1
.

(Step1) For
(\epsilon,z)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{C}^{+}\cup(-1,0)\times\mathbb{C}^{-}
, by using the Mourre inequality
M_{1}\gtrsim S^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mp\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})=\pm\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z\pm\epsilon M_{1}\geq\pm\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z

where
\mp
corresponds to
\epsilon\in(0,1)\cup(-1,0)
. This yields

\displaystyle|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z|\|u\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim\langle\mp\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})u,u\rangle\lesssim\|(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})u\|_{2}\|u\|_{2}

and we obtain
|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z|\|u\|_{2}\lesssim\|(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})u\|_{2}
for all
u\in D(H)
. Similarly we have
\|(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})^{*}u\|_{2}\gtrsim|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z|\|u\|_{2}
. Since
(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})^{*}
is densely defined and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})=\mathop{\mathrm{Ker}}(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})^{*}\oplus\overline{\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})}
holds,
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore
R_{\epsilon}(z):=(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})^{-1}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to D(H)
exists and
\|R_{\epsilon}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z|^{-1}
holds.

(Step 2) We set
W=S(A+ik)^{-1}
and
F_{\epsilon}(z)=WR_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}
for sufficiently large
k>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|Se^{i\lambda A}u\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\|Su\|^{2}_{2}+\int_{0}^{\lambda}\frac{d}{d\mu}\langle S^{2}e^{i\mu A}u,e^{i\mu A}u\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle=\|Su\|^{2}_{2}+\int_{0}^{\lambda}\langle[S^{2},iA]e^{i\mu A}u,e^{i\mu A}u\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle=\|Su\|^{2}_{2}+\int_{0}^{\lambda}\langle[[H_{0},iA],iA]e^{i\mu A}u,e^{i\mu A}u\rangle d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\|Su\|^{2}_{2}+c\int_{0}^{\lambda}\|Se^{i\mu A}u\|^{2}_{2}d\mu

for some
c>0
and all
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where we have used
\langle[[H_{0},iA],iA]u,u\rangle\leq c\langle[H_{0},iA]u,u\rangle
, which is found in the proof of Lemma
3.1
. By the Gronwall inequality and density argument,
\|Se^{i\lambda A}u\|_{2}\leq e^{\frac{\lambda c}{2}}\|Su\|_{2}
holds for all
\lambda>0
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This yields

\displaystyle\|S(A+ik)^{-1}u\|_{2}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\|Se^{i\lambda(A+ik)}u\|_{2}d\lambda\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(-k+\frac{c}{2})\lambda}\|Su\|_{2}d\lambda\lesssim\|Su\|_{2}

if
k>\frac{c}{2}
. By the definition of
F_{\epsilon}(z)
, we obtain
\|F_{\epsilon}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z|^{-1}
. Now we show

\displaystyle\partial_{\epsilon}F_{\epsilon}(z)=2kiF_{\epsilon}(z)-SR_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}-WR_{\epsilon}(z)S-\epsilon WR_{\epsilon}(z)M_{2}R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}.

(3.6)

Note that
R_{\epsilon}(z)(D(A))\subset D(A)
holds. This inclusion is important in the Mourre theory and proofs may be well-known. However we cannot find a reference completely including our setting and we give a proof in Appendix
B
.
Hence we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\lambda}\langle We^{-i\lambda A}R_{\epsilon}(z)e^{i\lambda A}W^{*}u,v\rangle\Biggm|_{\lambda=0}

\displaystyle=\langle[R_{\epsilon}(z),iA]W^{*}u,W^{*}v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle R_{\epsilon}(z)[iA,H-z-i\epsilon M_{1}]R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,W^{*}v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle WR_{\epsilon}(z)(-M_{1}+i\epsilon M_{2})R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,v\rangle

for all
u,v\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. On the other hand
\frac{d}{d\lambda}e^{i\lambda A}W^{*}u|_{\lambda=0}=iAW^{*}u=(iS-kW^{*})u
yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\lambda}\langle R_{\epsilon}(z)e^{i\lambda A}W^{*}u,e^{i\lambda A}W^{*}v\rangle\Biggm|_{\lambda=0}

\displaystyle=\langle R_{\epsilon}(z)(iS-kW^{*})u,W^{*}v\rangle+\langle R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,(iS-kW^{*})v\rangle

\displaystyle=-2k\langle F_{\epsilon}(z)u,v\rangle+i\langle WR_{\epsilon}(z)Su,v\rangle-i\langle SR_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,v\rangle

since
S
is self-adjoint. Therefore

\displaystyle WR_{\epsilon}(z)(-M_{1}+i\epsilon M_{2})R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}=-2kF_{\epsilon}(z)+iWR_{\epsilon}(z)S-iSR_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}

holds. By using
\partial_{\epsilon}F_{\epsilon}(z)=W\partial_{\epsilon}R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}=iWR_{\epsilon}(z)M_{1}R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}
we obtain (
3.6
).

(Step 3) By Step 2, for
(\epsilon,z)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{C}^{+}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|F_{\epsilon}(z)u\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim\|SR_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle=\langle S^{2}R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle M_{1}R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\{\epsilon\langle M_{1}R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u\rangle+\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z\|R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u\|^{2}_{2}\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\langle(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\langle u,F_{\epsilon}(z)u\rangle\lesssim\frac{1}{|\epsilon|}\|u\|_{2}\|F_{\epsilon}(z)u\|_{2}

This yields
\|F_{\epsilon}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\epsilon|}
and
\|SR_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\epsilon|^{1/2}}\|F_{\epsilon}(z)\|^{1/2}_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}
. By a similar calculation, this is true for
(\epsilon,z)\in(-1,0)\times\mathbb{C}^{-}
. Then

\displaystyle\|WR_{\epsilon}(z)S\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=\|SR_{-\epsilon}(\bar{z})W^{*}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\epsilon|^{1/2}}\|F_{-\epsilon}(\bar{z})\|^{1/2}_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\epsilon|^{1/2}}\|F_{\epsilon}(z)^{*}\|^{1/2}_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\epsilon|^{1/2}}\|F_{\epsilon}(z)\|^{1/2}_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

holds for
(\epsilon,z)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{C}^{+}
. By these estimates, we have

\displaystyle|\langle WR_{\epsilon}(z)M_{2}R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,u\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle M_{2}R_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u,R_{\epsilon}(z)^{*}W^{*}u\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|SR_{\epsilon}(z)W^{*}u\|_{2}\|SR_{\epsilon}(z)^{*}W^{*}u\|_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{|\epsilon|}\|F_{\epsilon}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}

where we have used
|\langle M_{2}u,u\rangle|\lesssim\langle[H_{0},iA]u,u\rangle
. Hence by (
3.6
)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\epsilon}F_{\epsilon}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|F_{\epsilon}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\frac{1}{\epsilon^{1/2}}\|F_{\epsilon}(z)\|^{1/2}_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

holds for
(\epsilon,z)\in(0,1)\times\mathbb{C}^{+}
. By integrating in
(\epsilon,1)
we obtain

\displaystyle\|F_{\epsilon}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|F_{1}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\left(\|F_{t}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}+t^{-1/2}\|F_{t}(z)\|^{1/2}_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\right)dt

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\int_{\epsilon}^{1}\frac{1}{t}dt\lesssim 1-\log\epsilon.

By using this inequality and again integrating in
(\epsilon,1)
we obtain
\|F_{\epsilon}(z)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim 1
.

(Step 4) By Step 3, we have
\|S(A+ik)^{-1}R_{\epsilon}(z)(A-ik)^{-1}S\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim 1
. By taking
\epsilon\to 0
we obtain
\|S(A+ik)^{-1}(H-z)^{-1}(A-ik)^{-1}S\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim 1
. We transform

\displaystyle\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle^{-1}(H-z)^{-1}\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle^{-1}

\displaystyle=\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle^{-1}(A+ik)S^{-1}\cdot S(A+ik)^{-1}(H-z)^{-1}(A-ik)^{-1}S\cdot S^{-1}(A-ik)\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle^{-1}.

We recall that
\|u\|^{2}_{2}\lesssim\left\langle\left(\frac{4x^{2}}{\langle\sqrt{2}x\rangle^{2}}+\frac{4D^{2}_{x}}{\langle\sqrt{2}D_{x}\rangle^{2}}\right)u,u\right\rangle
and
S^{2}=[H_{0},iA]=U\left(\frac{4x^{2}}{\langle\sqrt{2}x\rangle^{2}}+\frac{4D^{2}_{x}}{\langle\sqrt{2}D_{x}\rangle^{2}}\right)U^{*}
yields

and hence

is bounded on

. Since

,

\displaystyle\|\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle^{-1}(A+ik)S^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle^{-1}(A+ik)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim 1,

\displaystyle\|S^{-1}(A-ik)\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\|(A-ik)\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle^{-1}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim 1

holds. Therefore we obtain the desired estimate.
∎

As a corollary of the Strichartz estimates, we consider the
L^{p}
-mapping property of resolvents called the uniform Sobolev estimates.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
r\in(2,\infty)
if
d=1
or
2
and
r\in(2,\frac{2d}{d-2})
if
d\geq 3
. Then

\displaystyle\|(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f\|_{r,2}\lesssim\|f\|_{r^{\prime},2}

holds for any
f\in\mathcal{S}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\pm}
.

Proof.

We mimick the argument due to Duyckaerts (see
[
BM
]
). We set
u(t)=e^{izt}(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f
for
f\in\mathcal{S}
. Since
u
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u=-H_{0}u+e^{izt}f\\
u(0)=(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f,\end{array}\right.

by the Strichartz estimate, we obtain

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}([-T,T];L^{r,2}_{x})}\lesssim\|(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f\|_{2}+\|e^{izt}f\|_{L^{2}([-T,T];L^{r^{\prime},2}_{x})}

where the implicit constant is independent of
T>0
. By the definition of
u
,

\displaystyle\|(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f\|_{r,2}\lesssim\|f\|_{r^{\prime},2}+\frac{1}{\|e^{izt}\|_{L^{2}([-T,T])}}\|(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f\|_{2}

holds. By using
\|e^{izt}\|_{L^{2}([-T,T])}\geq\sqrt{T}
and taking
T\to\infty
, we have the desired result.
∎

We extend the above lemma to
H
. The proof is based on the perturbation method in
[
BM
]
and our Kato-Yajima type estimates.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
r
be as in Lemma
3.3
and
V
be as in Theorem
1.8
. If
V\in L^{\frac{r}{r-2},\infty}
holds, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(H-z)^{-1}f\|_{r,2}\lesssim\|f\|_{r^{\prime},2}

(3.8)

for any
f\in\mathcal{S}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{\pm}
.

Proof.

By the resolvent identity and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle|\langle(H-z)^{-1}f,g\rangle|\lesssim|\langle(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f,g\rangle|+\||V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H-z)^{-1}f\|_{2}\||V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{0}-z)^{-1}g\|_{2}

holds for all
f,g\in\mathcal{S}
. By Theorem
1.6
,
|V(x)|\lesssim\langle\log\langle x\rangle\rangle^{-2}
and resolvent identity,

\displaystyle|\langle|V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H-z)^{-1}f,g\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle|V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f,g\rangle|+|\langle|V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f,|V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H-z)^{-1}|V|^{\frac{1}{2}}g\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{2}\||V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f\|_{2}

holds. This yields
\||V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H-z)^{-1}f\|_{2}\lesssim\||V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f\|_{2}
. Then Hölder’s inequality and Lemma

implies

\displaystyle\||V|^{\frac{1}{2}}(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f\|_{2}\lesssim\|(H_{0}-z)^{-1}f\|_{r,2}\lesssim\|f\|_{r^{\prime},2}.

By duality and Lemma
3.3
, we obtain (

).
∎

Lemma 3.5

(Local regularity)
.

Assume

holds for some

Proof.

The proof is based on a standard parametrix construction. Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d};[0,1])
satisfy
\chi(x)=1
if
|x|\leq M
and
\chi(x)=0
if
|x|\geq 2M
for some fixed

. We set
\bar{\chi}_{R}(x)=1-\chi_{R}(x)=1-\chi(x/R)
. Since
\chi(x)u=\chi(x)\chi_{R}(D)u+\chi(x)\bar{\chi}_{R}(D)u
and
\chi(x)\chi_{R}(D)u\in\mathcal{S}
hold, it suffices to show
\chi(x)\bar{\chi}_{R}(D)u\in\mathcal{S}
for sufficiently large

Next we prove that

Nonlinear Schrödinger equations for infinitely many particles under the effect of Aharonov-Bohm potentials

In this section we consider the Hartree equations for infinitely many particles:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}\gamma=[H_{A,a}+w*\rho_{\gamma},\gamma]\\
\gamma(0)=\gamma_{0}\end{array}\right.

Here

A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})

\rho_{A}(x):=k_{A}(x,x)
denotes the density function of
A
, where

is the integral kernel of

\rho(A)

N

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\partial_{t}u_{j}=H_{A,a}u_{j}+w*(\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{k=N}|u_{k}|^{2})u_{j}\\
u_{j}(0)=u_{0,j}\end{array}\right.

.
We refer to
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[

]
,
[
Ha
]
,
[

]
and references therein for well-posedness and scattering for infinitely many particle systems, though all of these results are considered for the free Hamiltonian. To state our result we recall the definition of the Schatten class.

Definition 4.1

(Schatten space)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be a Hilbert space. For a compact operator

A:\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}
, the singular values
\{\mu_{n}\}
of
A
is defined as the set of all the eigenvalues of
(A^{*}A)^{1/2}
. The Schatten space
\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2})
for
\alpha\in[1,\infty]
is the set of all the compact operators:
\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}
such that its singular values belong to
\ell^{\alpha}
. Its norm is defined by the
\ell^{\alpha}
norm of the singular values. We also use the following notation for simplicity:
\|S\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}_{0})}:=\|S\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{\alpha}(\mathcal{H}_{0}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{0})}
.

Concerning the global existence for large initial data, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose

is as in Example

\gamma_{0}\in\mathfrak{S}^{2q/q+1}
. Then there exists a unique global solution
\gamma
to (

The proof is based on the orthonormal Strichartz estimate for

and analogous to
[

]
and
[

. Hence we omit it here. Next we prove that, for a small initial data, unique solution

Suppose

Before proving Theorem

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

be a real-valued function. Then there exists a family of unitary operators

such that
U_{V}(s,s)=1

\displaystyle U_{V}(t,s)u_{0}=e^{-i(t-s)H_{A,a}}u_{0}-i\int_{s}^{t}e^{-i(t-\tau)H_{A,a}}V(\tau)U_{V}(\tau,s)u_{0}d\tau

(4.3)

Proof.

The proof is similar to Lemma 4.15 and Corollary 4.17 in
[
H1
]
(see also
[
Y1
]
). Therefore we only give modifications needed. By the Strichartz estimate we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)H_{A,a}}V(s)u(s)ds\right\|_{C(I;L^{2})\cap L^{4}(I;L^{4})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Vu\|_{L^{4/3}(I;L^{4/3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|V\|_{L^{2}(I;L^{2})}\|u\|_{C(I;L^{2})\cap L^{4}(I;L^{4})}

where

with

. Since

, there exists a small

such that

\displaystyle C(I;L^{2})\cap L^{4}(I;L^{4})\ni u\to e^{-itH_{A,a}}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)H_{A,a}}V(s)u(s)ds\in C(I;L^{2})\cap L^{4}(I;L^{4})

is a contraction on

. We set

, where

is a unique fixed point. We split

into a finite sum of intervals on which the above argument succeed (this is possible by

). Then by glueing solutions we obtain

. See the proof of Lemma 4.15 and Corollary 4.17 in
[

]
or
[
Y1
]
for more details. The proof of

is totally the same so we omit its proof
∎

We define

for the sake of simplicity. The next lemma is the orthonormal Strichartz estimates for

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

be a real-valued function and

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\nu_{n}|U_{V}(t)f_{n}|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{q/2}_{t}L^{r/2}_{x}}\lesssim\|\nu\|_{\ell^{\beta}}

holds, where

is as in Example
2.6
and the implicit constant is bounded if

is bounded. Furthermore this is equivalent to

\displaystyle\|fU_{V}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{2\beta^{\prime}}(L^{2}_{x}\to L^{2}_{t,x})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\tilde{q}}_{t}L^{\tilde{r}}_{x}}.

(4.4)

Proof.

The proof is just repeating the argument of Lemma 3.7 in
[

with

By using Lemma
4.4
and

, we prove the following estimates, which are used in the contraction mapping argument.

Lemma 4.6

Let
\gamma_{0}\in\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}

\displaystyle\|\rho(U_{V}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{V}(t)^{*})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}

\displaystyle\|\rho(U_{V}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{V}(t)^{*})-\rho(U_{W}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{W}(t)^{*})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|V-W\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}

and
\|W\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

Proof.

We compute as

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x)\rho(U_{V}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{V}(t)^{*})(x)dxdt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[f(t)U_{V}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{V}(t)^{*}]dt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}[U_{V}(t)^{*}f^{1/2}f^{1/2}U_{V}(t)\gamma_{0}]dt\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}\||f|^{1/2}U_{V}\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{S}^{8}(L^{2}_{x}\to L^{2}_{t,x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}\||f|^{1/2}\|^{2}_{L^{4}_{t,x}}=\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}

where we have used (
4.4
). By a duality argument we obtain
\|\rho(U_{V}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{V}(t)^{*})\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}
. Next we transform

\displaystyle\rho(U_{V}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{V}(t)^{*})-\rho(U_{W}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{W}(t)^{*})

\displaystyle=\rho((U_{V}(t)-U_{W}(t))\gamma_{0}U_{V}(t)^{*})+\rho(U_{W}(t)\gamma_{0}(U_{V}(t)^{*}-U_{W}(t)^{*})).

(4.5)

To estimate a difference, we use

\displaystyle U_{V}(t)-U_{W}(t)=-i\int_{0}^{t}U_{V}(t,\tau)(V(\tau)-W(\tau))U_{W}(\tau)d\tau.

(4.6)

If

are differentiable, this is easily verified (e.g.
[

). However in our case

are not necessarily differentiable since it is constructed by a solution to an integral equation. Hence we need a little technical computation. See
[
Y1

for a sufficient condition for differentiability of

\displaystyle U_{V}(t,s)=e^{-i(t-s)H_{A,a}}-i\int_{s}^{t}U_{V}(t,\tau)V(\tau)e^{i(s-\tau)H_{A,a}}d\tau

(4.7)

holds. This can be obtained by taking the adjoint of (

\displaystyle U_{V}(t)-U_{W}(t)=-i\underbrace{\left\{\int_{0}^{t}U_{V}(t,s)V(s)e^{-isH_{A,a}}ds-\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-s)H_{A,a}}W(s)U_{W}(s)ds\right\}}_{=:I(t)}

holds. By using (
4.3

\displaystyle I(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}U_{V}(t,s)V(s)\left\{U_{W}(s)+i\int_{0}^{s}e^{-i(s-r)H_{A,a}}W(r)U_{W}(r)dr\right\}ds

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\left\{U_{V}(t,s)+i\int_{s}^{t}U_{V}(t,\tau)V(\tau)e^{i(s-\tau)H_{A,a}}d\tau\right\}W(s)U_{W}(s)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}U_{V}(t,s)(V(s)-W(s))U_{W}(s)ds

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\underbrace{i\int_{0}^{t}U_{V}(t,s)V(s)\left\{\int_{0}^{s}e^{-i(s-r)H_{A,a}}W(r)U_{W}(r)dr\right\}ds}_{=:I_{1}(t)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\underbrace{i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}U_{V}(t,\tau)V(\tau)e^{i(s-\tau)H_{A,a}}d\tau W(s)U_{W}(s)ds}_{=:I_{2}(t)}.

By changing the order of integration in

holds. Therefore we have proved (

Then

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x)\rho((U_{V}(t)-U_{W}(t))\gamma_{0}U_{V}(t)^{*})(t,x)dtdx\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x)\rho\left(\int_{0}^{t}U_{V}(t,\tau)(V(\tau)-W(\tau))U_{W}(\tau)d\tau\gamma_{0}U_{V}(t)^{*}\right)dxdt\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}\left[U_{V}(t)^{*}f^{1/2}f^{1/2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}U_{V}(t,\tau)(V(\tau)-W(\tau))U_{W}(\tau)d\tau\right)\gamma_{0}\right]dt\right|

Proof of Theorem

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[g](t,x)=\rho(U_{w*g}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{w*g}(t)^{*})(t,x)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}[g]\|_{L^{2}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}},

\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}<\epsilon_{0}

\mathcal{L}

\phi\in X
be a unique fixed point of
\mathcal{L}
and set

\displaystyle\gamma(t)=U_{w*\phi}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{w*\phi}(t)^{*}.

Then we have
\rho(\gamma(t))=\rho(U_{w*\phi}(t)\gamma_{0}U_{w*\phi}(t)^{*})=\phi(t)
. Since
U_{w*\rho(\gamma)}
is strongly continuous and
\gamma_{0}\in\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}
, by Grümm’s convergence theorem (Theorem 2.19 in
[
Si
]
), we have
\gamma\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathfrak{S}^{4/3})
. Furthermore

\displaystyle\|e^{itH_{A,a}}\gamma(t)e^{-itH_{A,a}}-e^{isH_{A,a}}\gamma(s)e^{-isH_{A,a}}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|e^{itH_{A,a}}U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}(t)-e^{isH_{A,a}}U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}(s)\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{x})}\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\|U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}(t)^{*}e^{-itH_{A,a}}-U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}(s)^{*}e^{-isH_{A,a}}\|_{\mathcal{B}(L^{2}_{x})}\|\gamma_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}

and

\displaystyle\|e^{itH_{A,a}}U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}(t)u-e^{isH_{A,a}}U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}(s)u\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\int_{s}^{t}e^{i\tau H_{A,a}}w*\rho_{\gamma}(\tau)U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}(\tau)ud\tau\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{s}^{r}e^{-i(r-\tau)H_{A,a}}w*\rho_{\gamma}(\tau)U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}(\tau)ud\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}([s,t];L^{2}_{x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|w*\rho_{\gamma}U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}u\|_{L^{4/3}([s,t];L^{4/3}_{x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\rho_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}([s,t];L^{2}_{x})}\|U_{w*\rho_{\gamma}}u\|_{L^{4}_{t,x}}\lesssim\|\rho_{\gamma}\|_{L^{2}([s,t];L^{2}_{x})}\|u\|_{2}

implies
\|e^{itH_{A,a}}\gamma(t)e^{-itH_{A,a}}-e^{isH_{A,a}}\gamma(s)e^{-isH_{A,a}}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}\to 0
as
s,t\to\infty
. Therefore there exists
\eta_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}
such that
\|\gamma(t)-e^{-itH_{A,a}}\eta_{\pm}e^{itH_{A,a}}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}\to 0
as
t\to\pm\infty
. Then by the completeness of
W_{\pm}
(see the proof of Corollary
2.9
) and Grümm’s convergence theorem, there exists
\gamma_{\pm}\in\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\|\gamma(t)-e^{-itH_{A,0}}\gamma_{\pm}e^{itH_{A,0}}\|_{\mathfrak{S}^{4/3}}=0

holds and hence we are done.
∎

Appendix A
Interpolation spaces

Here we prove that real interpolation spaces of Sobolev spaces associated to
H_{A,0}
are the Besov spaces associated to
H_{A,0}
. This is used in the proof of Corollary
2.9
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
H_{A,0}
be the operator appeared in Corollary
2.9
. Then we have

\displaystyle(\dot{H}^{s_{0}}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}}),\dot{H}^{s_{1}}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}}))_{\theta,q}=\dot{B}^{s}_{2,q}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})

for
s_{0}<s<s_{1}
,
s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
,
\theta\in(0,1)
and
q\in(2,\infty)
.

This lemma would hold for any
q\in[1,\infty]
but we only consider
q\in(2,\infty)
since we do not use other cases. We mimic the argument for the ordinary Besov spaces in
[
T
]
.

Proof.

For simplicity we set
\dot{H}^{t}=\dot{H}^{t}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})
and
\dot{B}^{t}_{2,r}=\dot{B}^{t}_{2,r}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\in(2,\infty)
. First we prove
\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{2,q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{(\dot{H}^{s_{0}},\dot{H}^{s_{1}})_{\theta,q}}
for all
f\in E((\epsilon,\frac{1}{\epsilon}))L^{2}_{x}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, where
E
denotes the spectral measure associated with
H_{A,0}
. For such
f
, we set
K(t,f)=\inf_{f=f_{0}+f_{1}}(\|f_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{0}}}+t\|f_{1}\|_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}}})
for any
t>0
, where the infimum is taken for all
f=f_{0}+f_{1}\in\dot{H}^{s_{0}}+\dot{H}^{s_{1}}
. Let
\{\phi_{j}\}
be a homogeneous Littlewood-Paley decomposition. Then

\displaystyle\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f_{0}\|_{2}+\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f_{1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-s_{0}j}\|(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})^{s_{0}}f_{0}\|_{2}+2^{-s_{1}j}\|(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})^{s_{1}}f_{1}\|_{2}

holds. By taking the infimum, we obtain
\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|_{2}\lesssim 2^{-s_{0}j}K(2^{j(s_{0}-s_{1})},f)
. This implies

\displaystyle\|\{2^{js}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|_{2}\}\|_{l^{q}}\lesssim\|\{2^{-\theta j(s_{0}-s_{1})}K(2^{j(s_{0}-s_{1})},f)\}\|_{l^{q}}

Since
K(t,f)
increases monotonically with respect to
t>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle 2^{\theta(s_{0}-s_{1})}2^{-\theta(j+1)(s_{0}-s_{1})}K(2^{(j+1)(s_{0}-s_{1})},f)\leq t^{-\theta}K(t,f)\leq 2^{-\theta(s_{0}-s_{1})}2^{-\theta j(s_{0}-s_{1})}K(2^{j(s_{0}-s_{1})},f)

for all
t\in[2^{(j+1)(s_{0}-s_{1})},2^{j(s_{0}-s_{1})}]
. Therefore

\displaystyle(s_{1}-s_{0})\log 2\cdot 2^{\theta(s_{0}-s_{1})q}\{2^{-\theta(j+1)(s_{0}-s_{1})}K(2^{(j+1)(s_{0}-s_{1})},f)\}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2^{(j+1)(s_{0}-s_{1})}}^{2^{j(s_{0}-s_{1})}}(t^{-\theta}K(t,f))^{q}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq(s_{1}-s_{0})\log 2\cdot 2^{-\theta(s_{0}-s_{1})q}\{2^{-\theta j(s_{0}-s_{1})}K(2^{j(s_{0}-s_{1})},f)\}^{q}

holds. for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
. By taking the sum, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\{2^{-\theta j(s_{0}-s_{1})}K(2^{j(s_{0}-s_{1})},f)\}\|_{l^{q}}\approx\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}(t^{-\theta}K(t,f))^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\|f\|_{(\dot{H}^{s_{0}},\dot{H}^{s_{1}})_{\theta,q}}.

Hence we have proved
\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{2,q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{(\dot{H}^{s_{0}},\dot{H}^{s_{1}})_{\theta,q}}
. Next we prove the other inequality:
\|f\|_{(\dot{H}^{s_{0}},\dot{H}^{s_{1}})_{\theta,q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{2,q}}
. By the above argument we have

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q}_{(\dot{H}^{s_{0}},\dot{H}^{s_{1}})_{\theta,q}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\theta q}K(t,f)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\approx\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{-q\theta k(s_{0}-s_{1})}K(2^{k(s_{0}-s_{1})},f)^{q}.

We set
f_{0,k}=\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f
and
f_{1,k}=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k}\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f
. Then by a support property of
\{\phi_{j}\}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f_{0,k}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s_{0}}}\lesssim\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}2^{2js_{0}}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|^{2}_{2},\quad\|f_{1,k}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s_{1}}}\lesssim\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k}2^{2js_{1}}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|^{2}_{2}.

This yields

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q}_{(\dot{H}^{s_{0}},\dot{H}^{s_{1}})_{\theta,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{qk(s-s_{0})}\left[\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}2^{2js_{0}}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|^{2}_{2}+\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k}2^{2k(s_{0}-s_{1})}\cdot 2^{2js_{1}}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|^{2}_{2}\right]^{\frac{q}{2}}

by the definition of
K
. By taking
s_{0}<\chi_{0}<s<\chi_{1}<s_{1}
and
\sigma>0
such that
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f\|^{q}_{(\dot{H}^{s_{0}},\dot{H}^{s_{1}})_{\theta,q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{qk(s-s_{0})}\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}2^{(s_{0}-\chi_{0})\sigma j}\right)^{\frac{q}{\sigma}}\left(\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}2^{\chi_{0}qj}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|^{q}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{qk(s-s_{1})}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k}2^{(s_{1}-\chi_{1})\sigma j}\right)^{\frac{q}{\sigma}}\left(\sum_{j=-\infty}^{k}2^{\chi_{1}qj}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|^{q}_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\chi_{0}qj}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|^{q}_{2}\sum_{k\leq j-1}2^{qk(s-\chi_{0})}+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\chi_{1}qj}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|^{q}_{2}\sum_{k\geq j}2^{qk(s-\chi_{1})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{qjs}\|\phi_{j}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})f\|^{q}_{2}=\|f\|^{q}_{\dot{B}^{s}_{2,q}}.

Therefore we have
\|f\|_{(\dot{H}^{s_{0}},\dot{H}^{s_{1}})_{\theta,q}}\approx\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{2,q}}
for all
f\in E((\epsilon,\frac{1}{\epsilon}))L^{2}_{x}
and
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. By the density of such
f
, we have proved
(\dot{H}^{s_{0}}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}}),\dot{H}^{s_{1}}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}}))_{\theta,q}=\dot{B}^{s}_{2,q}(\sqrt{H_{A,0}})
.
∎

Appendix B
Lemma for Mourre theory

Here we give a supplementary lemma which is used when we apply the weakly conjugate operator method (see the proof of Theorem
1.6
). We follow the argument in
[
Mo
]
with some modifications. See also
[
ABG
]
and
[
GG
]
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
H
and
A
be as in Theorem
1.6
. We set
R_{\epsilon}(z)=(H-z-i\epsilon M_{1})^{-1}
, where
M_{1}
is as in the proof of Theorem
1.6
. Then we have

\displaystyle R_{\epsilon}(z)(D(A))\subset D(A).

Proof.

First we notice that
e^{itA}(D(H))\subset D(H)
holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. This is a consequence of
e^{itA}(D(H_{0}))\subset D(H_{0})
, which follows from
H_{0}\in C^{1}(A)
(see
[
BCHM
]
Lemma 3.9) and
D(H)=D(H_{0})
. Then by Proposition 3.2.5 in
[
ABG
]
,
e^{itA}\in\mathcal{B}(D(H))
and

\displaystyle\sup_{|t|<1}\|He^{itA}u\|_{2}<\infty

holds for all
u\in D(H)
. Since
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are bounded, the assumptions
(a)\sim(d)
in
[
Mo
]
are satisfied. Therefore by Proposition II.3 in
[
Mo
]
,
(A-z)^{-1}(D(H))\subset D(H)
holds if
|\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}z|
is sufficiently large. We set
A(\lambda)=i\lambda A(A+i\lambda)^{-1}=i\lambda+\lambda^{2}(A+i\lambda)^{-1}
for
\lambda>0
. Since
\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}R_{\epsilon}(z)\subset D(H)
holds, we have

\displaystyle\langle(A+i\lambda)^{-1}HR_{\epsilon}(z)u,v\rangle-\langle H(A+i\lambda)^{-1}R_{\epsilon}(z)u,v\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle-iM_{1}(A+i\lambda)^{-1}R_{\epsilon}(z)u,(A-i\lambda)^{-1}v\rangle

and this yields

\displaystyle R_{\epsilon}(z)[A(\lambda),H]R_{\epsilon}(z)=iR_{\epsilon}(z)i\lambda(A+i\lambda)^{-1}M_{1}i\lambda(A+i\lambda)^{-1}R_{\epsilon}(z).

Furthermore

\displaystyle[A(\lambda),M_{1}]=i\lambda(A+i\lambda)^{-1}iM_{2}i\lambda(A+i\lambda)^{-1}

holds. By using
[R_{\epsilon}(z),A(\lambda)]=R_{\epsilon}(z)[A(\lambda),H-z-i\epsilon M_{1}]R_{\epsilon}(z)
, for
u\in D(A)
,

\displaystyle A(\lambda)R_{\epsilon}(z)u

\displaystyle=[A(\lambda),R_{\epsilon}(z)]u+R_{\epsilon}(z)A(\lambda)u

\displaystyle\to-iR_{\epsilon}(z)M_{1}R_{\epsilon}(z)-\epsilon R_{\epsilon}(z)M_{2}R_{\epsilon}(z)+R_{\epsilon}(z)Au

as
\lambda\to\infty
. Since
i\lambda(A+i\lambda)^{-1}R_{\epsilon}(z)u\to R_{\epsilon}(z)u
, we obtain
R_{\epsilon}(z)u\in D(A)
.
∎

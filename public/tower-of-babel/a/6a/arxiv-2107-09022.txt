Abstract
. We give infinitely many examples of manifold-knot pairs ( Y , J ) (Y,J) such that Y Y bounds an integer homology ball, J J does not bound a non-locally-flat PL-disk in any integer homology ball, but J J does bound a smoothly embedded disk in a rational homology ball. The proof relies on formal properties of involutive Heegaard Floer homology.



1.
Introduction

Every knot
K
in
S^{3}
bounds a non-locally-flat PL-embedded disk in
B^{4}
, obtained by taking the cone over
K
. (Throughout, we will not require PL-disks to be locally-flat.) The analogous statement does not hold for knots in more general manifolds. Adam Levine
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
found examples of manifold-knot pairs
(Y,J)
such that
Y
bounds a contractible 4-manifold and
J
does not bound a PL-disk in any homology ball
X
with
\partial X=Y
; see also
[
6
]
.

The main result of this note concerns rationally slice knots in homology spheres bounding integer homology balls:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist infinitely many manifold-knot pairs
(Y,J)
where
Y
is an integer homology sphere and

(1)

Y
bounds an integer homology 4-ball,

(2)

J
does not bound a PL-disk in any integer homology 4-ball,

(3)

J
does bound a smoothly embedded disk in a rational homology
4
-ball.

Throughout, let
Y
be an integer homology sphere. Recall that a knot
J\subset Y
is
rationally slice
if
J
bounds a smoothly embedded disk in a rational homology
4
-ball
W
with
\partial W=Y
. Two manifold-knot pairs
(Y_{0},J_{0})
and
(Y_{1},J_{1})
are
integrally
(respectively
rationally
)
homology concordant
if
J_{0}
and
J_{1}
are concordant in an integral (respectively rational) homology cobordism between
Y_{0}
and
Y_{1}
. A knot
J\subset Y
is integrally (respectively rationally) homology concordant to a knot
K
in
S^{3}
if and only if
J\subset Y
bounds a PL-disk in an integer (respectively rational) homology ball.

Theorem
1.1
is an immediate consequence of the following theorem, where
\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}
and
\overline{V}_{0}
are the involutive knot Floer homology invariants of
[
5
]
and
V_{0}
the knot Floer homology invariant defined in
[
9
, Section 2.2]
(see also
[
12
]
,
[

]
):

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K
be a negative amphichiral rationally slice knot in
S^{3}
with
\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}\geq 1
and
V_{0}=\overline{V}_{0}=0
and let
\mu
be the core of surgery in
M=S^{3}_{-1/\ell}(K)
, where
\ell
is an odd positive integer. Consider
J=\mu\#U\subset M\#-M
, where
U
denotes the unknot in
-M
. Then
(M\#-M,J)
is rationally slice, hence rationally homology concordant to a knot in
S^{3}
, but
(M\#-M,J)
is not integrally homology concordant to any knot in
S^{3}
.

Remark 1.4
.

Note that
M
does not bound an integer homology ball (since, for instance,
\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu(M)=2\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}\neq 0
), but
M\#-M
does.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
is inspired by the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.1(1)]
. Our proof relies on the following result from
[
3
]
relating the involutive correction term
\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu

[
5
, Section 5]
with the ordinary Heegaard Floer correction term
d

[
10
, Section 4]
, for even denominator surgery on knots in
S^{3}
:

Proposition 1.5

(
[
3
, Proposition 1.7]
)
.

Let
K
be a knot in
S^{3}
and let
p,q>0
be relatively prime integers, with
p
odd and
q
even. Then

\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu(S^{3}_{p/q}(K),[p/2q])=d(S^{3}_{p/q}(K),[p/2q])

where
[p/2q]
denotes the unique self-conjugate
\Spin^{c}
structure on
S^{3}_{p/q}(K)
.

The key feature from the above proposition is that for even denominator surgery on a knot in
S^{3}
, we have that
\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu
is equal to
d
for the unique self-conjugate
\Spin^{c}
structure on the surgery. More generally, we have the following corollary of Proposition
1.5
:

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
J
be a knot in an integer homology sphere
Y
and let
p,q>0
be relatively prime integers, with
p
odd and
q
even. If
(Y,J)
is integrally homology concordant to a knot in
S^{3}
, then

\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu(Y_{p/q}(J),[p/2q])=d(Y_{p/q}(J),[p/2q])

where
[p/2q]
denotes the unique self-conjugate
\Spin^{c}
structure on
Y^{3}_{p/q}(J)
.

Proof.

If
(Y,J)
is integrally homology concordant to a knot
(S^{3},K)
, then
Y^{3}_{p/q}(J)
and
S^{3}_{p/q}(K)
are integrally homology cobordant; the homology cobordism is given by surgering along the concordance annulus from
(Y,J)
to
(S^{3},K)
. Since
\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu
and
d
are invariants of integer homology cobordism, the result follows from Proposition
1.5
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
1.2
relies on finding manifold-knot pairs
(Y,J)
where
\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu
and

of even denominator surgery along

1.6
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We first show that
(M\#-M,J)
is rationally slice. Since
K
is rationally slice, the core of surgery in
M=S^{3}_{-1/\ell}(K)
is rationally homology concordant to the core of surgery in
S^{3}_{-1/\ell}(U)
, which is the unknot in
S^{3}
; that is,
(M,\mu)
is rationally slice. Hence
(M\#-M,J)
is also rationally slice.

We now show that
(M\#-M,J)
is not integrally homology concordant to any knot in
S^{3}
. Since
\mu
is the core of surgery in
S^{3}_{-1/\ell}(K)
, we have that

M_{1/n}(\mu)=S^{3}_{1/(n-\ell)}(K).

Choose an even positive integer
n
such that
n>\ell
. Since
\ell
is odd,
n
is even, and
n-\ell>0
, by
[
3
, Proposition 1.7]
we have that

\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu(M_{1/n}(\mu))=\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu(S^{3}_{1/(n-\ell)}(K))=-2\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(K)\quad\textup{ and }\quad d(M_{1/n}(\mu))=d(S^{3}_{1/(n-\ell)}(K))=-2V_{0}(K)=0.

Since
J=\mu\#U\subset M\#-M
, we have that

(M\#-M)_{1/n}(J)=M_{1/n}(\mu)\#-M.

Note that
-M=S^{3}_{1/\ell}(-K)=S^{3}_{1/\ell}(K)
, where the last equality follows from the fact that
K
is negative amphichiral. Since
\ell>0
,
[
3
, Proposition 1.7]
implies that

\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu(-M)=-2\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(K)\quad\textup{ and }\quad d(-M)=\overline{d}(-M)=0.

Recall that
[
4
, Proposition 1.3]
states that if
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are integer homology spheres, then

\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu(Y_{1}\#Y_{2})\leq\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu(Y_{1})+\overline{d}(Y_{2}).

Hence
\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu(M_{1/n}(\mu)\#-M)\leq-2\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(K)
. Since
d
is additive under connected sum, we have that
d(M_{1/n}(\mu)\#-M)=0
.

We have shown that

\underline{d\mkern-2.0mu}\mkern 2.0mu((M\#-M)_{1/2}(J))\leq-2\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(K)\quad\textup{ and }\quad d((M\#-M)_{1/2}(J))=0.

Recall that
\underline{V\mkern-3.0mu}\mkern 3.0mu_{0}(K)\geq 1
. Now by Corollary
1.6
, it follows that
(M\#-M,J)
is not integrally homology concordant to any knot in
S^{3}
.
∎

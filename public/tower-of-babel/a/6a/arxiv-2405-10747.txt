Abstract
. In this paper we find the number of homogeneous polynomials of degree

Introduction

Let
N>1
,
m\geq 2
an even number and let
f_{0},...,f_{t-1}
be elements of the basis of the space of cuspidal modular forms
S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
of weight
m
with
\dim S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))=t
. Let
X_{0}(N)
be the modular curve for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. As in
[
10
]
we look at the holomorphic map
X_{0}(N)\to\mathbb{P}^{t-1}
defined by

\mathfrak{a}_{z}\mapsto(f_{0}(z):\cdots:f_{t-1}(z))

(1)

and we denote the image curve of this map by

\mathcal{C}(N,m)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{t-1}.

Let us set
g=f_{t-1}
. Then the map
(\ref{map})
can be written as

\mathfrak{a}_{z}\mapsto(f_{0}(z)/g(z):\cdots:f_{t-1}(z)/g(z))

(2)

and the map is a rational map of algebraic curves.

We gather the following facts about this image curve
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
:

Lemma 1.1
.

Assume that
m\geq 2
even. Let
t=\dim S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
,
f_{0},\dots,f_{t-1}
be
a basis of
S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
,
g
be the genus of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
and we denote by

\mathcal{C}(N,m)=C(f_{0},\cdots,f_{t-1})

the image of the map
1
. Then we have the following

i)

\mathcal{C}(N,m)
is an irreducible smooth projective curve in
\mathbb{P}^{t-1}
.

ii)

If
m\geq 4
, then we always have
t\geq g+2
and the degree of the curve is
t+g-1
.

iii)

If
m=2
then
t=g
and if
X_{0}(N)
is not hyperelliptic then the map
(\ref{map})
is the canonical embedding and the degree of the curve is
2g-2
.

iv)

If
m=2
then
t=g
and if
X_{0}(N)
is hyperelliptic, then the canonical map
(\ref{map})
is of degree 2 and the degree of the curve is
g-1
.

.

Proof.

The i) part of Lemma
1.1
follows from Chow’s theorem, ii) and its proof can be found in
[
10
]
, Corollary 3-4a and iii) and iv) for canonical map see
[
9
]
, Chapter 7.
∎

Planar curves, for instance planar models of modular curves
X_{0}(N)
are defined by one irreducible equation, defining equation for the curve, (see
[
2
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
5
]
). The degree of this minimal polynomial is the degree of the curve. But when we look at maps to higher dimensional projective spaces as in our case (
1
), then the image curve is a projective curve, a one-dimensional variety in
\mathbb{P}^{t-1}
, for
t\geq 3
. In this higher-dimensional space curves are no longer given by one equation, since hypersurfaces are of codimension
1
, yet algebraic sets whose ideal is finitely-generated.

We can compare our situation to results from
[
5
]
where the canonical map, given in terms of cusp forms of weight
2
for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
, is used to obtain models for modular curves. Then the map is to projective space
\mathbb{P}^{g}
, where
g
is the genus of the group
\Gamma_{0}(N)
,
g=\dim S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
, and the model for modular curve is the projective curve defined as intersection of finite number of hypersurfaces, given by system polynomial equations. The study of such projective curves are in domain of intersection theory (
[
7
]
,
[
15
]
) and ideals of commutative graded rings (
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
).

Let
\mathcal{P}=\mathbb{Q}\left[X_{0},\dots,X_{t-1}\right]
be the ring of polynomials in
t
variables and
\mathcal{P}_{d}=\mathbb{Q}\left[X_{0},\dots,X_{t-1}\right]_{d}
subring of homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
. We regard
\mathcal{P}
as the graded ring
\mathcal{P}=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}\mathcal{P}_{d}
.

Let
I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))\subseteq\mathcal{P}
be the ideal of the curve
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
, radical homogenous ideal consisting of all homogenous polynomials that vanish on
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
. Then
f\in I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))
defines a hypersurface
\mathcal{C}(N,m)\subset V(f)
in
\mathbb{P}^{t-1}
.
We also have a graded structure on the ideal
I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))
, if we set

I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}=\mathcal{P}_{d}\cap I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))

(3)

we get the vector space
I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}
of all homogenous polynomials of degree
d
which vanish on
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
.

Lemma 1.2
.

I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}

Proof.

A homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
only has monomials of degree
d
. Product of two homogeneous polynomials of degrees
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
is again a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d_{1}+d_{2}
.
We can see this graded structure as vector spaces or modules, because we have

\mathcal{P}_{j}I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}\subseteq I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{jd}

∎

Our goal is to compute all linearly independent homogeneous polynomials of certain given degree that vanish on the curve
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
and the dimension of the vector space
I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}
. In Section
2
we present the algorithm to compute homogeneous polynomials vanishing on
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
, in Section
3
we present the results of computation, and in Section
4
we find the formula for the number of polynomials, which we present here in the following two theorems.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
t=\dim S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))\geq 3
,
g
be genus of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.
Let
N\geq 2
be such that
g=0
or
g=t
and
X_{0}(N)
is hyperelliptic.
Then the number
N(t,d)
of homogeneous polynomials of degree
d\geq 0
is equal to

N(t,d)=\binom{t+d-1}{d}-(t-1)d-1.

(4)

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
t=\dim S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))\geq 3
,
g
be genus of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
.
Then the number
N(t,d)
of homogeneous polynomials of degree
d\geq 0
is equal to

•

g=\{1,2\}

N(t,d)=\binom{t+d-1}{d}-(t-1)d-1-(g+(d-2)g)

•

g>2

N(t,d)=\binom{t+d-1}{d}-(t-1)d-1-((g-2)+(d-2)(g-1))

We prove Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
in Section
5
by computing the Hilbert polynomial of the curve
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
. Hilbert polynomial is a great tool to record and handle information about dimensions of homogeneous components of graded modules, (
[
6
]
).

2
Computing homogeneous polynomials vanishing on cusp forms

Let
f_{0},\dots,f_{t-1}\in S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
be a basis of the space of cuspidal modular forms for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(N)
of weight
m\geq 2
.

Let
P\in\mathbb{Q}\left[x_{0},\dots,x_{t-1}\right]
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d

P(x_{0},\dots,x_{t-1})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}\leq d\\
i_{0}+\dots+i_{t-1}=d\end{subarray}}a_{i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}}x_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots x_{t-1}^{i_{t-1}}.

For a given degree
d\geq 0
, we are interested in those polynomials which vanish on the elements of the basis
f_{0},\dots,f_{t-1}
,

P(f_{0}(z),\cdots,f_{t-1}(z))=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}\leq d\\
i_{0}+\dots+i_{t-1}=d\end{subarray}}a_{i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}}f_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots f_{t-1}^{i_{t-1}}=0

(5)

for all
\mathfrak{a}_{z}\in X_{0}(N)
.

Vector space
\mathcal{P}_{d}
of all homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
is generated with monomials and its dimension can be viewed as the number of coefficients
a_{i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}}
with respect to the indexing set of the set of monomials of degree
d

I=\left\{(i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}):0\leq i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}\leq d,i_{0}+\dots+i_{t-1}=d\right\}.

The cardinality of
|I|
is known as the weak composition problem in combinatorics and the solution is

d^{\prime}=\dim\mathcal{P}_{d}=|I|=\binom{d+t-1}{d}.

(6)

We will order
I
using the lexicographical ordering,(see
[
4
]
) so that we consider a polynomial
P
as a finite linear array of its coefficients

P\longrightarrow(a_{0},\dots,a_{d^{\prime}-1})

(7)

satisfying the order of corresponding monomials, as basis representation of
P
.

We are interested in subspaces
I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{d}
containing polynomials that vanish on the basis of
S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
for certain choices of
d,N,m
and their dimensions,

I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}=\left\{P\in\mathcal{P}_{d}:\,P(f_{0},\cdots,f_{t-1})=0\right\}

(8)

where
f_{0},\dots,f_{t-1}
is a basis of
S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
. Each modular form is in practical computations given by a finitely many coefficients of its integral Fourier expansion in the cusp
\infty
.

The polynomial combination
P(f_{0},\dots,f_{t-1})
is again a modular form, of weight
md
, where
d
is the degree of the homogeneous polynomial
P
, since cuspidal forms on a given group also make a graded ring,
S(\Gamma_{0}(N))=\oplus_{m}S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
.

The condition of vanishing of the modular form
P(f_{0},\dots,f_{t-1})
is known as the Sturm bound saying that we only consider a finite number
B
of coefficients of the
q
-expansion of the form to distinguish forms,

B_{m}=\left\lfloor\dfrac{m\left[SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):\Gamma_{0}(N)\right]}{12}\right\rfloor.

(9)

Similar to
[
5
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
the algorithm for computing polynomials vanishing on a basis of
S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))
is based on the following linear algebra considerations: for fixed values of
d,N,m
we are solving a homogeneous system of equations, where the unknowns are coefficients of a polynomial
P
,
a_{0},\dots,a_{d^{\prime}-1}
as in
(\ref{coef})
and the coefficients of the system are values of
q
-expansions of evaluated monomials
f_{0}^{i_{0}}\dots f_{t}^{i_{t-1}}
over the indexing set
I
,

\displaystyle P(f_{0},\dots,f_{t-1})=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}\leq d\\
i_{0}+\dots+i_{t-1}=d\end{subarray}}a_{i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}}f_{0}^{i_{0}}\cdots f_{t-1}^{i_{t-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}\leq d\\
i_{0}+\dots+i_{t-1}=d\end{subarray}}a_{i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1}}\left(a_{0}^{(i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1})}+a_{1}^{(i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1})}q+\dots\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{0}+p_{1}q+p_{2}q^{2}+\cdots.

The homogeneous system is
p_{0}=p_{1}=\cdots=p_{B_{md}}=0

and its solutions are obtained as the basis of the right kernel of the transpose of
d^{\prime}\times B_{md}
matrix whose rows are made of coefficients of
f_{0}^{i_{0}}\dots f_{t-1}^{i_{t-1}}
, after ordering the index set
I
.

Here is the algorithm, for a given
N
and weight
m
, with the use of lexicographic ordering on the set of monomials of degree
d
:

Input:

q
-expansions of
f_{0},\dots,f_{t-1}
basis of
S_{m}(\Gamma_{0}(N))

•

for a degree
d\geq 0
:

–

for each monomial index
(i_{0},\dots,i_{t-1})\in I
in the ordered set of monomials of degree
d
:

compute
f_{0}^{i_{0}}\dots f_{t-1}^{i_{t-1}}

–

create a
d^{\prime}\times B_{md}
matrix
A
, whose rows are first
B_{md}
coefficients of
q
-expansion of
f_{0}^{i_{0}}\dots f_{t-1}^{i_{t-1}}

–

return the dimension (or the elements) of the right kernel of
A

Output:

beginning part of an array containing the number of linearly independent homogeneous polynomials of degree
d\geq 0
vanishing on all forms, i.e. such that
P(f_{0},\dots,f_{t-1})=0
.

We can also compute explicitly all of these polynomials.
In our computations we are using the SAGE software system
[
14
]
and the cusp form basis we are using is generated by command

CuspForms(Gamma_0(N),m).q_integral_basis(prec)

5
Hilbert function and Hilbert polynomial

In Section
1
we have seen that homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
vanishing on the curve
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
make the vector space

I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}=\mathcal{P}_{d}\cap I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))

and generate the graded ideal

I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))=\bigoplus_{d\geq 0}I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}.

From Lemma
4.1
we have:

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
2\leq d\leq 7
. Then
I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}
is a vector space of dimension

\dim I(\mathcal{C}(N,m))_{d}=\binom{t+d-1}{d}-(t-1)d-1.

(15)

For
d\geq 0
we define the Hilbert function
[
6
]
of the curve
\mathcal{C}_{N,m}
to be the Hilbert function of its coordinate ring:

HF_{\mathcal{C}_{N,m}}(d)=HF_{\mathcal{P}/I(\mathcal{C}_{N,m})}(d)=\dim\mathcal{P}_{d}-\dim I_{d}

(16)

For the polynomial ring
\mathcal{P}
we have

HF_{\mathcal{P}}(d)=\dim\mathcal{P}_{d}=\binom{t+d-1}{d}

(17)

By Hilbert-Serre theorem (see
[
7
]
, Thm. 7-5) for a projective curve there is a unique polynomial of degree one such that for
d\gg 0

HP_{\mathcal{C}_{N,m}}(d)=HF_{\mathcal{C}_{N,m}}(d).

We have calculated
\dim I_{d}
up to
d=7
, but we now use formulas from Section
4
to find this polynomial explicitly.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

In the case of genus
0
or a hyperelliptic
X_{0}(N)
from
(\ref{Hil_fun})
,
(\ref{Hil_fun_P})
and
15
we have:

\displaystyle HF_{I(C)}(d)

\displaystyle=\binom{t+d-1}{d}-\binom{t+d-1}{d}+(t-1)d+1

\displaystyle=(t-1)d+1

(18)

From
18
we can see that the Hilbert function is expressed as the Hilbert polynomial from the index
d\geq 2
and the degree of the polynomial is equal to one, as is always in the case of curves.

From this, we can conclude that this will be the Hilbert function for all
d
since, hence Theorem
1.3
follows from here.

∎

The coefficients and the degree of this linear Hilbert polynomial for our
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
as an embedded projective variety have following interpretation,
[
6
]
:

•

\dim\mathcal{C}(N,m)=1
is the degree of the Hilbert polynomial

•

the degree of
\mathcal{C}(N,m)
is the leading coefficient of the Hilbert polynomial.

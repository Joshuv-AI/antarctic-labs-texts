The uniqueness of vertex pairs in π \pi -separable groups Thanks: *Corresponding author
Lei Wang, Ping Jin*

Abstract
. Let G G be a finite π \pi -separable group, where π \pi is a set of primes, and let χ \chi be an irreducible complex character that is a π \pi -lift of some π \pi -partial character of G G . It was proved by Cossey and Lewis that all of the vertex pairs for χ \chi are linear and conjugate in G G if 2 ∈ π 2\in\pi , but the result can fail for 2 ∉ π 2\notin\pi . In this paper we introduce the notion of the twisted vertices in the case where 2 ∉ π 2\notin\pi , and establish the uniqueness for linear twisted vertices under the conditions that either χ \chi is an 𝒩 \mathcal{N} -lift for a π \pi -chain 𝒩 \mathcal{N} of G G or it has a linear Navarro vertex, thus answering a question proposed by them.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite
\pi
-separable group for a set
\pi
of primes,
and let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be an irreducible complex character of
G
.
Suppose that
Q
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
and
\delta\in\Irr(Q)
.
Following Cossey
[
4
]
, we say that the pair
(Q,\delta)
is a
vertex pair
for
\chi
if there exists a subgroup
U
of
G
such that
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
U

and
\delta=(\psi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}
, where
\psi\in\Irr(U)
is
\pi
-factored with
\psi^{G}=\chi
and
\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
is the
\pi^{\prime}
-special factor of
\psi
.
In particular, if
U
and
\psi
are chosen
so that the pair
(U,\psi)
is a normal nucleus of
\chi
(see
[
16
]
for details),
then
(Q,\delta)
is known as a
Navarro vertex
for
\chi
.
We remark that in general, all of the vertex pairs for
\chi
need not be conjugate,
but the Navarro vertex for
\chi
is unique up to conjugacy.
In this paper we are interested in
linear vertex pairs
,
that is, those vertex pairs
(Q,\delta)
with
\delta
a linear character.

Linear vertex pairs play an essential role in proving theorems about lifts of Brauer characters or more general Isaacs’
\pi
-partial characters; see, for example,
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
16
]
and especially the review paper
[
4
]
mentioned above. A crucial result is that in the case where
|G|
is odd or
2\in\pi
,
all of the vertex pairs for a
\pi
-lift are linear and conjugate
(see Theorem 1.1 of
[
2
]
, or Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.3 of
[
8
]
).
Recall that
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is said to be a
\pi
-lift
if its restriction
\chi^{0}
to the set
G^{0}
of
\pi
-elements of the
\pi
-separable group
G
is an irreducible
\pi
-partial character of
G
.
(For definition and properties of the
\pi
-partial characters, we refer to
[
13
]
;
here we mention that if
G
is
p
-solvable and
\pi=p^{\prime}
is the complement of the prime
p
,
then the set
\I(G)
of irreducible
\pi
-partial characters of
G
is exactly the set
\IBr_{p}(G)
of irreducible
p
-Brauer characters of
G
.)

The aim of the present paper is to explore the uniqueness of linear vertex pairs in the remaining case where
2\notin\pi
.
In order to study the behavior of a given
\pi
-lift with respect to a chain of normal subgroups,
Cossey and Lewis
[
5
]
introduced the notion of the inductive vertices
for
\mathcal{N}
-lifts, and showed that these vertex pairs are linear and ‘almost’ unique up to conjugacy (Theorem 1.2 of that paper; see also Theorem 5.4 of
[
4
]
).
Here
\mathcal{N}
is a
\pi
-chain
of
G
,
which by definition is a chain of normal subgroups of
G
:

\mathcal{N}=\{1=N_{0}\leq N_{1}\leq\cdots\leq N_{k-1}\leq N_{k}=G\}

such that
N_{i}/N_{i-1}
is either a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group for
i=1,\dots,k
,
and
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is an
\mathcal{N}
-lift
if every irreducible constituent of
\chi_{N}
is a
\pi
-lift for all
N\in\mathcal{N}
.
Inspired by their work, we define the
twisted vertex

for any
\chi\in\Irr(G)
to be
(Q,\epsilon\delta)

by adding some permutation sign character
\epsilon

to the original vertex pair
(Q,\delta)
of
\chi
(see Definition 2.1 below),
and we will study the uniqueness of such vertices.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group with
2\notin\pi
, and
suppose that
\chi\in\Irr(G)
is an
\mathcal{N}
-lift for some
\pi
-chain
\mathcal{N}
of
G
. Then all of the linear twisted vertices for
\chi
are conjugate in
G
.

This gives a positive answer to a question proposed by Cossey and Lewis
of whether or not the nilpotence assumption on the
\pi
-chain
\mathcal{N}
appeared in
Theorem 5.4 of
[
4
]
or Theorem 4.2 of
[
5
]
is really necessary.
We remark that the linear twisted vertices in Theorem A definitely exist
(see Corollary
2.4
below).

Using Navarro vertices, we can present another condition sufficient to guarantee the uniqueness for our linear twisted vertices.

Theorem B
.

Let
G
be
\pi
-separable with
2\notin\pi
, and let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be a
\pi
-lift.
If
\chi
has a linear Navarro vertex, then all of the linear twisted vertex pairs for
\chi
are conjugate in
G
.

In
[
17
]
, we will use Theorem B to study a conjecture of Cossey
[
1
]

on the number of lifts of a given irreducible Brauer character in characteristic two.
Observe that the assumption on Navarro vertices in Theorem B is trivially satisfied
if either
\chi
has
\pi
-degree or
G
has an abelian Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup.

We now present an immediate consequence of both Theorem A and Theorem B.
Recall that in order to obtain canonical lifts for
\I(G)
in a
\pi
-separable group
G
,
several important subsets of
\Irr(G)
were constructed,
such as
\B(G)
,
\D(G)
,
\N(G)
and
\B(G,{\mathcal{N}})
.
(See
[
10
,
12
,
16
,
14
]
in turn for the relevant definitions and properties.)
We mention that all characters in
\N(G)
satisfy the hypothesis in Theorem B,
and each member of
\B(G)
,

\D(G)
or
\B(G,{\mathcal{N}})
is an
\mathcal{N}
-lift for any
\pi
-chain
\mathcal{N}
of
G
, so that Theorem A applies.

Corollary C
.

Let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
, where
G
is a
\pi
-separable group with
2\notin\pi
.
If
\chi
is any member of
\B(G)
,
\D(G)
,
\N(G)
, or
\B(G,\mathcal{N})

for a
\pi
-chain
\mathcal{N}
of
G
,
then
\chi
has a linear vertex pair, and all of the linear twisted vertices for
\chi
are conjugate in
G
.

Most of our notation and terminology can be found in
[
9
]
and
[
13
]
.
In particular, if
N
is a normal subgroup of a finite group

and
\theta\in\Irr(N)
,
we always write
G_{\theta}
for the inertia group of
\theta
in
G

and
\chi_{\theta}
for the Clifford correspondent with respect to
\theta

of any
\chi\in\Irr(G)
lying over
\theta
.
Also, if a character
\chi
is
\pi
-factored, that is, if
\chi=\alpha\beta
,
where
\alpha
is
\pi
-special and
\beta
is
\pi^{\prime}
-special,
then
\alpha
and
\beta
are uniquely determined by
\chi
by a theorem of Gajendragadkar (see Theorem 2.2 of

]
),
so for notational convenience, we often use
\chi_{\pi}
and

to denote the
\pi
-special factor
\alpha

\pi^{\prime}
-special factor

, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin with the definition of twisted vertices mentioned in the introduction.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
, where
G
is a
\pi
-separable group with
2\notin\pi
,
and suppose that
Q
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
and
\delta\in\Irr(Q)
.
We say that the pair
(Q,\delta)
is a
twisted vertex
for
\chi

if there exists a subgroup
U
of
G
such that
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
U

and
\delta=(\det((1_{Q})^{U}))_{Q}(\psi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}
,
where
\psi\in\Irr(U)
is
\pi
-factored with
\psi^{G}=\chi
.
If
(Q,\delta)
is a twisted vertex pair for
\chi
and
\delta
is linear,
then
(Q,\delta)
is called a
linear twisted vertex pair
for
\chi
.

It is easy to see that
\epsilon=(\det((1_{Q})^{U}))_{Q}
is the permutation sign character of the action of
Q
on the right cosets of
Q
in
U
, so that
\epsilon
is a sign character,
which means that its values are
\pm 1
.
For any subgroup
H
of
G
, Isaacs
[
11
]
defined the
\pi
-standard sign character

\delta_{(G,H)}
(see also
[
13
]
), and for convenience we list some relevant properties as follows.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group, where
2\notin\pi
.

(1)
If
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
, then
\delta_{(G,Q)}=(\det((1_{Q})^{G}))_{Q}
.

(2)
If
Y\leq X\leq G
, then
\delta_{(G,Y)}=(\delta_{(G,X)})_{Y}\delta_{(X,Y)}
.

(3)
If
N\vartriangleleft G
, then
\delta_{(G,N)}=1_{N}
, the principal character of
N
.

Proof.

(1) is Lemma 2.10 of
[
11
]
,
and for (2) and (3), see Theorem 2.5 of that paper or Lemma 2.33 of
[
13
]
.
∎

By Lemma
2.2
(1),
we can modify Definition 2.1 by replacing the permutation sign character
(\det((1_{Q})^{U}))_{Q}
with the
\pi
-standard sign character
\delta_{(U,Q)}
,
and obtain a more convenient definition of twisted vertices.
From now on, we will always use this new definition.

We need some elementary properties of
\mathcal{N}
-lifts,
which appeared implicitly in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be an
\mathcal{N}
-lift,
where
\mathcal{N}
is a
\pi
-chain of a
\pi
-separable group
G
,
and let
N\in\mathcal{N}
.
Suppose that
\theta\in\Irr(N)
is a constituent of
\chi_{N}
. Then the following hold.

(1)

\chi_{\theta}\in\Irr(G_{\theta})
is an
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}
-lift, where
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}=\{M\cap G_{\theta}|M\in\mathcal{N}\}
is a
\pi
-chain for
G_{\theta}
.

(2)
If
\theta
is
\pi
-factored, then
\theta
has
\pi
-degree.

Proof.

(1) By definition, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}
is a
\pi
-chain for
G_{\theta}
.
To prove that
\chi_{\theta}
is an
\mathcal{N}_{\theta}
-lift,
we fix an arbitrary
M\in\mathcal{N}
and
\eta\in\Irr(M\cap G_{\theta})
lying under
\chi_{\theta}
,
and we want to show that
\eta
is a
\pi
-lift.
In this case, we have two possibilities: either
M\leq N
or
N\leq M
.

If
M\leq N
, then
M\cap G_{\theta}=M
,
and since
\chi
is an
\mathcal{N}
-lift and
\eta\in\Irr(M)
lies under
\chi
, we know that
\eta
is a
\pi
-lift. We may therefore assume that
M\geq N
,
and then
M\cap G_{\theta}=M_{\theta}
is the inertia of
\theta
in
M
.
Now
\eta
lies over
\theta
, so the Clifford correspondence implies that
\eta^{M}
is irreducible,
which clearly lies under
\chi
. It follows that
\eta^{M}
is a
\pi
-lift,
and thus
(\eta^{0})^{M}=(\eta^{M})^{0}\in\I(M)
.
This shows that
\eta^{0}
is irreducible, i.e.,
\eta
is also a
\pi
-lift, as wanted.

(2) We proceed by induction on
|N|
.
If
N=1
, there is nothing to prove, so we can assume that
N>1
,
and then there exists
M\in\mathcal{N}
such that

M<N
with
N/M
either a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group.
Let
\eta\in\Irr(M)
lie under
\theta
. Then
\eta
is also
\pi
-factored,
and the inductive hypothesis guarantees that
\eta(1)
is a
\pi
-number.
Since
\theta(1)
divides
|N:M|\eta(1)
, the result follows in the case where
N/M
is a
\pi
-group.

Now suppose that
N/M
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-group.
Then, since both
\theta
and
\eta
are
\pi
-factored, we have
(\theta_{\pi})_{M}=\eta_{\pi}
by Clifford’s theorem, and thus
\eta_{\pi}
is invariant in
N
.
Note that both
\theta
and
\eta
are
\pi
-lifts because
\chi
is assumed to be an
\mathcal{N}
-lift, so
\eta^{0}\in\I(M)
lies under
\theta^{0}\in\I(N)
.
But
\eta(1)
is a
\pi
-number, so
\eta_{\pi^{\prime}}
is linear,
and we have
\eta^{0}=(\eta_{\pi})^{0}(\eta_{\pi^{\prime}})^{0}=(\eta_{\pi})^{0}
. It follows that
\eta^{0}
is
N
-invariant, and by Clifford’s theorem for
\pi
-partial characters (see Corollary 5.7 of
[
13
]
), we deduce that
(\theta^{0})_{M}=\eta^{0}
.
This shows that
\theta(1)=\eta(1)
, which is a
\pi
-number, as required.
∎

Although the following result is known (see Theorem 1 or Lemma 2.4 of
[
15
]
),
as a simple application of Lemma
2.3
,
we present an alternative proof for completeness.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a
\pi
-chain of a
\pi
-separable group
G
,
and let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be an
\mathcal{N}
-lift.
Then
\chi=\psi^{G}
for some
\pi
-factored character
\psi
of a subgroup
U\leq G
,
where
\psi(1)
is a
\pi
-number. In particular,
\chi
has a linear twisted vertex.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\chi
is not
\pi
-factored.
Let
N\in\mathcal{N}
be maximal such that the constituents of
\chi_{N}
are
\pi
-factored,
and let
\theta\in\Irr(N)
lie under
\chi
.
We claim that
G_{\theta}<G
.
To see this, note that
N<G
, so there exists
M\in\mathcal{N}
such that
M/N>1
is a
\pi
-group or a
\pi^{\prime}
-group. If the
\pi
-factored character
\theta
is
G
-invariant, then both
\theta_{\pi}
and
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
are invariant in
G
, and by Lemma 4.2 of
[
13
]
,
every member of
\Irr(M|\theta)
is
\pi
-factored.
In particular, each irreducible constituent
\eta
of
\chi_{M}
lying over
\theta
is
\pi
-factored,
and this contradicts the maximality of
N
.
Thus
\theta
cannot be invariant in
G
, as claimed.

Now we work by induction on
|G|
.
By Lemma
2.3
(1) and the inductive hypothesis applied in
G_{\theta}
,
we have
\chi_{\theta}=\psi^{G_{\theta}}
, where
\psi
is a
\pi
-factored character of some subgroup
U\leq G_{\theta}
and
\psi(1)
is a
\pi
-number.
Since
\chi=(\chi_{\theta})^{G}=\psi^{G}
, the result follows in this case.

In the remaining case,
\chi
is
\pi
-factored.
Then
\chi(1)
is a
\pi
-number by Lemma
2.3
(2),
and the proof is complete by taking
(U,\psi)=(G,\chi)
.
∎

3.
The uniqueness of twisted vertices

In this section we will prove our main theorems.
The following ‘replacement lemma’ is key to our purpose,
which might be of independent interest on its own.
Note that the assumption that
2\notin\pi
is essential,
and the lifts of
\pi
-partial characters are not involved
(compare with Lemma 4.8 of
[
8
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
, where
G
is
\pi
-separable with
2\notin\pi
,
and suppose that
\chi=\psi^{G}
, where
\psi\in\Irr(U)
is
\pi
-factored and
U\leq G
.
Assume that
\chi_{N}
has a
\pi
-factored irreducible constituent for some
N\vartriangleleft G
.
Write
\xi=\psi^{NU}
.
If
|NU:U|
is a
\pi
-number, then
\xi
is
\pi
-factored,
and its
\pi
-special factor
\xi_{\pi}
and
\pi^{\prime}
-special factor
\xi_{\pi^{\prime}}

satisfy
\xi_{\pi}=(\delta_{(NU,U)}\psi_{\pi})^{NU}
and
(\xi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{U}=\delta_{(NU,U)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
, respectively.

Proof.

We proceed by double induction, first on
|G:U|
and then on
|N|
.
Note that all irreducible constituents of
\chi_{N}
are conjugate in
G
by Clifford’s theorem,
and since
\xi^{G}=\psi^{G}=\chi
, it follows that
\xi
lies under
\chi
and thus each irreducible constituent of
\xi_{N}
is also
\pi
-factored.
So it is no loss to assume that
G=NU
.

If
N=1
, then
G=NU=U
, and there is noting to prove.
So we assume that
N>1
, and take
N/K
to be a chief factor of
G
.
Write
D=N\cap U
, so that
D\leq KD\leq N
.
If
KD=N
, then
G=(KD)U=KU
, and since
K<N

and each irreducible constituent of
\chi_{K}
is
\pi
-factored,
the result follows by the inductive hypothesis applied in the group
N
.

We can assume, therefore, that
KD<N
.
Write
X=KD
. Then
|N:X|>1
divides
|G:U|
, which is a
\pi
-number,
so
N/K
is a
\pi
-group.
Since we are assuming that
2\notin\pi
,
it follows that the chief factor
N/K
has odd order and hence is abelian
(using the odd-order theorem).
This shows that
X\vartriangleleft N
, which forces
X=K
, and thus
D\leq K<N
.
In this case, we have two possibilities: either
D<K
or
D=K
.

Suppose first that
D<K
. Let
Y=KU
and
\zeta=\psi^{Y}
.
Then
U<Y<G
and
\chi=\zeta^{G}
, so that each irreducible constituent of
\zeta_{K}
lies under
\chi

and thus is
\pi
-factored.
Since
|Y:U|<|G:U|
, the inductive hypothesis (with
Y
in place of
G
)
implies that
\zeta
is
\pi
-factored with
\zeta_{\pi}=(\delta_{(Y,U)}\psi_{\pi})^{Y}
and
(\zeta_{\pi^{\prime}})_{U}=\delta_{(Y,U)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
Again, we have
|G:Y|<|G:U|
, so the inductive hypothesis guarantees that

\chi
is
\pi
-factored with
\chi_{\pi}=(\delta_{(G,Y)}\zeta_{\pi})^{G}
and
(\chi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Y}=\delta_{(G,Y)}\zeta_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
Combining these two cases and using the fact that

(\delta_{(G,Y)})_{U}\delta_{(Y,U)}=\delta_{(G,U)}
(see Lemma
2.2
(2)), we obtain

\chi_{\pi}=(\delta_{(G,Y)}(\delta_{(Y,U)}\psi_{\pi})^{Y})^{G}=((\delta_{(G,Y)})_{U}\delta_{(Y,U)}\psi_{\pi})^{Y})^{G}=(\delta_{(G,U)}\psi_{\pi})^{G}

and
(\chi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{U}=(\delta_{(G,Y)})_{U}\delta_{(Y,U)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}=\delta_{(G,U)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
, as required.

Now suppose that
D=K\vartriangleleft G
.
Let
\eta\in\Irr(D)
lie under
\psi
,
and choose some
\theta\in\Irr(N|\eta)
lying under
\chi
.
Then
\eta
is
\pi
-factored because
\psi
is
\pi
-factored and
D\vartriangleleft U
. Note that
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
restricts irreducibly to
D
(see Theorem 2.10 of
[
13
]
),
and by the normality of
D
in
N
, it is easy to see that
(\theta_{\pi^{\prime}})_{D}=\eta_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
Also, since
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
is the unique
\pi^{\prime}
-special character of
N
lying over
\eta_{\pi^{\prime}}
, both characters have the same inertia group in
G
.
Observe that
D\vartriangleleft G
is contained in the kernel of
\delta_{(G,U)}
(Lemma
2.2
(3))
and that
\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
lies over
\eta_{\pi^{\prime}}
, so
\delta_{(G,U)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
also lies over
\eta_{\pi^{\prime}}
and extends to
G
by Lemma 2.11 of
[
13
]
.
Furthermore, we can deduce from Corollary 3.15 of
[
13
]

(with the roles of
\pi
and
\pi^{\prime}
interchanged) that
there exists a
\pi^{\prime}
-special character
\beta
of
G
such that

\beta_{U}=\delta_{(G,U)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
Write
\alpha=(\delta_{(G,U)}\psi_{\pi})^{G}
. Then we have

\chi=\psi^{G}=(\delta_{(G,U)}\psi_{\pi}\beta_{U})^{G}=\alpha\beta,

so
\alpha
is irreducible, and by Theorem 7.25 of
[
13
]
, we see that
\alpha
is
\pi
-special.
This shows that
\chi
is
\pi
-factored with
\chi_{\pi}=\alpha
and
\chi_{\pi^{\prime}}=\beta
,
and the proof is now complete.
∎

The following technical result will be used twice later.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
G
be
\pi
-separable with
2\notin\pi
, and let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
.
If
\chi
is
\pi
-factored and
\chi(1)
is a
\pi
-number,
then all of the twisted vertices for
\chi
are conjugate in
G
.

Proof.

Let
(Q,\delta)
be a twisted vertex for
\chi
.
By definition,
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of some subgroup
U\leq G
,
and there is a
\pi
-factored character
\psi
of
U
such that
\psi^{G}=\chi

and
\delta=\delta_{(U,Q)}(\psi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}
.
Since
\chi
has
\pi
-degree, we see that
|G:U|
is a
\pi
-number and hence
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
G
. Also, by Lemma
3.1
(with
N=G
),
we know that
(\chi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{U}=\delta_{(G,U)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
, and thus

\delta=\delta_{(U,Q)}(\psi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}=\delta_{(U,Q)}(\delta_{(G,U)})_{Q}(\chi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}=\delta_{(G,Q)}(\chi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}.

Since the Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup
Q
is uniquely determined up to
G
-conjugacy,
it is clear that the twisted vertex
(Q,\delta)=(Q,\delta_{(G,Q)}(\chi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q})

is also unique up to conjugation in
G
, as desired.
∎

We consider the Clifford correspondents for
\pi
-factored characters.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a
\pi
-separable group with
2\notin\pi
,
and let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be
\pi
-factored with
\chi(1)
a
\pi
-number.
Assume that
N\vartriangleleft G
and
\theta\in\Irr(N)
lie under
\chi
.
Let
\psi\in\Irr(T)
be the Clifford correspondent of
\chi
with respect to
\theta
,
where
T=G_{\theta}
is the inertia group of
\theta
in
G
.
Then
\psi
is
\pi
-factored, and also
\chi_{\pi}=(\delta_{(G,T)}\psi_{\pi})^{G}

and
(\chi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{T}=\delta_{(G,T)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

By the hypothesis on
\chi
, it is easy to see that
\theta
is
\pi
-factored.
Write
\lambda=\chi_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
Then
\lambda
is linear because
\chi(1)
is a
\pi
-number,
and thus
\lambda_{N}=\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
In particular,
\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}
is invariant in
G
,
and we have
T=G_{\theta_{\pi}}\cap G_{\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}}=G_{\theta_{\pi}}
.
Now
\chi_{\pi}
lies over
\theta_{\pi}
, and we let
\xi\in\Irr(T)

be the Clifford correspondent of
\chi_{\pi}
with respect to
\theta_{\pi}
.
Then
\xi^{G}=\chi_{\pi}
, so
(\xi\lambda_{T})^{G}=\xi^{G}\lambda=\chi
.
Clearly
\xi\lambda_{T}
lies over
\theta_{\pi}\theta_{\pi^{\prime}}=\theta
,
and hence
\psi=\xi\lambda_{T}
.
By Theorem 2.37 of
[
13
]
, we see that
\delta_{(G,T)}\xi
is
\pi
-special.
On the other hand, since
\lambda
is a
\pi^{\prime}
-special linear character of
G
,
it follows that
\lambda_{T}
is also
\pi^{\prime}
-special.
Note that
\delta_{(G,T)}
is always
\pi^{\prime}
-special, and so
\delta_{(G,T)}\lambda_{T}
is
\pi^{\prime}
-special. This shows that
\psi
is
\pi
-factored with

\psi_{\pi}=\delta_{(G,T)}\xi
and
\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}=\delta_{(G,T)}\lambda_{T}
,
and thus
\chi_{\pi}=\xi^{G}=(\delta_{(G,T)}\psi_{\pi})^{G}

and
(\chi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{T}=\lambda_{T}=\delta_{(G,T)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
,
as desired.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorems A and B.
Since the arguments for both results are similar,
we combine them into a single theorem as follows.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
G
be
\pi
-separable with
2\notin\pi
, and let
\chi\in\Irr(G)
be a
\pi
-lift.
Assume one of the following conditions.

(a)

\chi
is an
\mathcal{N}
-lift for some
\pi
-chain
\mathcal{N}
of
G
.

(b)

\chi
has a linear Navarro vertex.

Then all of the linear twisted vertices for
\chi
are conjugate in
G
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
|G|
.
Fix an arbitrary linear twisted vertex
(Q,\delta)
for
\chi
,
so by definition,
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of some subgroup
U\leq G
,
and there is a
\pi
-factored character
\psi
of
U
such that
\psi^{G}=\chi

and
\delta=\delta_{(U,Q)}(\psi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}
.
Note that
\psi
has
\pi
-degree because
\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}(1)=\delta(1)=1
.

First, suppose that
\chi
is
\pi
-factored.
By Corollary
3.2
, it suffices to prove that
\chi(1)
is a
\pi
-number.
In case (a), this is true by Lemma
2.3
(2),
and in case (b), since
(G,\chi)
is itself a normal nucleus for
\chi
,
it follows by the definition of Navarro vertices that
\chi
has
\pi
-degree.

Now suppose that
\chi
is not
\pi
-factored.
We need to pick out an appropriate normal subgroup
N
of
G

such that each irreducible constituents of
\chi_{N}
is
\pi
-factored but not invariant in
G
.
In case (a), we can choose
N\in\mathcal{N}
maximal with the property that
an irreducible constituent
\theta
is
\pi
-factored,
and so the same reasoning as we did in the beginning of the proof of Corollary
2.4
,
yields
G_{\theta}<G
.
In case (b), we let
N
to be the unique normal subgroup of
G
such that
some
\theta\in\Irr(N)
lying under
\chi
is
\pi
-factored,
and by the construction of Navarro vertices, we have
G_{\theta}<G
, too.
Observe that in both cases the Clifford correspondent
\chi_{\theta}
of
\chi
with respect to
\theta
also satisfies the assumption on
\chi
(by Lemma
2.3
(1) in case (a)).
Since
G_{\theta}<G
, the inductive hypothesis implies that
all the linear twisted vertices for
\chi_{\theta}
are conjugate in
G_{\theta}
,
and we will complete the proof by showing that
(Q,\delta)
is
G
-conjugate to some linear twisted vertex for
\chi_{\theta}
.

We may assume that
N\leq U
.
To see this, note that
\theta(1)
is a
\pi
-number in both case (a) by Lemma
2.3
(2)
and case (b) by the construction of Navarro vertices.
It follows that
\theta^{0}\in\I(N)
is an irreducible constituent of
(\chi^{0})_{N}
with
\pi
-degree.
Recall that
\chi=\psi^{G}
, so
\chi^{0}=(\psi^{0})^{G}
and
\psi^{0}\in\I(U)
, and Lemma 5.21 of
[
13
]
applies.
We conclude that
|NU:U|
is a
\pi
-number, and thus
Q
is a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
NU
.
Write
\xi=\psi^{NU}
, so that
\xi^{G}=\chi
. By Lemma
3.1
,

\xi
is
\pi
-factored with
(\xi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{U}=\delta_{(NU,U)}\psi_{\pi^{\prime}}
, and we have

\delta=\delta_{(U,Q)}(\psi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}=\delta_{(U,Q)}(\delta_{(NU,U)})_{Q}(\xi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}=\delta_{(NU,Q)}(\xi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q}.

This shows that
(Q,\delta)
is also a linear twisted vertex for
\xi
,
and we can thus replace
U
by
NU
and
\psi
by
\xi
.
So it is no loss to assume that
N\leq U
, as wanted.

Furthermore, we may assume that
\theta
lies under
\psi
by replacing
\theta
with a conjugate if necessary.
For notational simplicity, we write
T=G_{\theta}
and
S=U_{\theta}
for the inertia groups of
\theta
in
G
and in
U
,
and let
\alpha=\chi_{\theta}
and
\beta=\psi_{\theta}
be the Clifford correspondents of
\chi
and
\psi
with respect to
\theta
, respectively.
By Lemma
3.3
, we know that
\beta\in\Irr(S)
is
\pi
-factored with
(\psi_{\pi^{\prime}})_{S}=\delta_{(U,S)}\beta_{\pi^{\prime}}
.
Now let
Q_{1}
be a Hall
\pi^{\prime}
-subgroup of
S
and let
\delta_{1}=\delta_{(S,Q_{1})}(\beta_{\pi^{\prime}})_{Q_{1}}
.
Since
\psi=\beta^{U}
and
\alpha=\beta^{T}
by the Clifford correspondence,
it follows by definition that
(Q_{1},\delta_{1})
is a common linear twisted vertex for
\psi

and
\alpha
.
But
\psi
is
\pi
-factored with
\pi
-degree,
so Corollary
3.2
implies that
(Q,\delta)
and
(Q_{1},\delta_{1})
are conjugate in
U
. This shows that
(Q,\delta)
is conjugate to a linear twisted vertex for
\alpha=\chi_{\theta}
, and the proof is now complete.
∎

Bessel Models for Representations of GSp ⁡ ( 4 , q ) {\rm GSp}(4,q)


Abstract
We compute the Bessel models of irreducible representations of the finite group GSp ⁡ ( 4 , q ) {\rm GSp}(4,q) .



Jonathan Cohen

1
Introduction

For
F
a local or global field, there has been significant work on Bessel models for
{\rm GSp}(4,F)
and their applications to Siegel modular forms and automorphic representations; see
[
Pi2011
]
,
[
Pr2011
]
,
[
Ro2016
]
, and
[
Sc1995
]
for some examples. In this article, we consider the analogous situation over finite fields, and our main result is the computation of the Bessel models for all irreducible representations of
{\rm GSp}(4,q)
; see Theorem
4.1
. One of our primary motivations is the determination of the (nonsplit) Bessel models for the depth zero supercuspidals of
p
-adic
{\rm GSp}(4,F)
, which we will carry out in a forthcoming paper. We also remark that there are some notable differences between the
p
-adic and finite field cases. For example, it is shown in
[
Ro2016
]
that all split Bessel models are unique (when they exist) over the
p
-adics, while a consequence of our computations is that the split Bessel models over finite fields can have multiplicity 2; see Corollary
4.2
below. On the other hand, over both
p
-adic and finite fields, the generic representations admit every split Bessel model and (if
q>3
in the finite field case) this characterizes genericity.

We now briefly outline the contents of this article. In section 2 we introduce the requisite notation, in section 3 we carry out a technical preliminary computation, and in section 4 we prove the main result.

2
Notations

Let
\zeta_{n}
denote a complex primitive
n
th root of unity and
\delta_{i,j}
denote the Kronecker delta for
i,j
varying over some set. Let
q
be a power of a prime
p
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
denote the field of order
q
. If
q
is even then the group homomorphism
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
given by
x\mapsto x^{2}+x
is two-to-one. Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\circ}
be its image and
\varepsilon:{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\to\{\pm 1\}
be the homomorphism with kernel
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\circ}
. Let
\xi
be a fixed element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
that is not in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\circ}
; so
\varepsilon(t^{2}+t)=1
and
\varepsilon(t^{2}+t+\xi)=-1
for all
t\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. If
q
is odd let
\xi
be a fixed nonsquare in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
.

Fix the symplectic form

J=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}&&&1\\
&&1\\
&-1\\
-1\end{smallmatrix}\right]

(1)

and define

G:={\rm GSp}(4,q)=\{g\in{\rm GL}(4,q)\mid\ ^{t}gJg=\mu(g)J\text{ for some }\mu(g)\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\}.

(2)

The kernel of the multiplier homomorphism
\mu:{\rm GSp}(4,q)\to{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
is the symplectic group
{\rm Sp}(4,q)
. The center
Z
of
{\rm GSp}(4,q)
consists of scalar invertible matrices. If
q
is even then
G={\rm Sp}(4,q)\times Z
.
We further define the Siegel parabolic subgroup

P=G\cap\left[\begin{smallmatrix}*&*&*&*\\
*&*&*&*\\
&&*&*\\
&&*&*\end{smallmatrix}\right]

(3)

and its unipotent radical
N
, given by

N=\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&y&z\\
&1&x&y\\
&&1\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mid x,y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\}.

(4)

If
A=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in{\rm GL}(2,q)
then let
A^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{ad-bc}\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&-b\\
-c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]
so
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}A&\\
&uA^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}\right]\in P
with multiplier
u\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}
.

Let
\psi
be a non-trivial character of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. For
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, let
\psi_{a,b,c}
be the character of
N
given by

\psi_{a,b,c}(\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&&y&z\\
&1&x&y\\
&&1\\
&&&1\end{smallmatrix}\right])=\psi(ax+by+cz)

(5)

for
x,y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. We denote by
{\mathbb{C}}_{a,b,c}
the one-dimensional representation of
N
given by the character
\psi_{a,b,c}
. We also define the group

T=\{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}r-bs&-as\\
cs&r\\
&&r-bs&as\\
&&-cs&r\end{smallmatrix}\right]\mid r,s\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q},\ r^{2}-brs+s^{2}ac\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\}.

(6)

The reader may readily check that
T
normalizes
N
and preserves the form
ax+by+cz
. Let
\chi:T\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
be a character. The map
tn\mapsto\chi(t)\psi_{a,b,c}(n)
defines a character of the
Bessel subgroup

R:=TN,

(7)

which we denote by
\chi\otimes\psi_{a,b,c}
. Let
{\mathbb{C}}_{\chi,a,b,c}
be the one-dimensional representation of
R
given by the character
\chi\otimes\psi_{a,b,c}
.

The irreducible characters of
G
have been determined in
[
Sh1982
]
(if
q
is odd) and
[
En1972
]
(if
q
is even). Some of the mistakes in Table IV-2 of
[
En1972
]
have been corrected in Table 7 of
[
Da2007
]
; we remark that the values for
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
there are transposed.

(8)

3.3 Lemma.

Let
q
be even and
a,b,c\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\

(12)

(x,z)=(0,0)\end{subarray}}\psi(-ax-by-cz)=\begin{cases}q-1&\text{if }a=b=c=0,\\
q-1&\text{if }b=0,\>(a,c)\neq(0,0),\\
-1&\text{if }b\neq 0,\>\varepsilon(acb^{-2})=1,\\

(13)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\
y^{2}-xz\neq 0\\
(x,z)\neq(0,0)\end{subarray}}\psi(-ax-by-cz)=\begin{cases}(q-1)(q^{2}-1)&\text{if }a=b=c=0,\\

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\
y^{2}-xz=0\\
(x,y,z)\neq(0,0,0)\end{subarray}}\psi(-ax-by-cz)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\\

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\\
y\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\end{subarray}}\psi(-x(a+by+cy^{2}))+\sum_{z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}}\psi(-cz).

If
c=0
, then we see easily that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\
y^{2}-xz=0\\
(x,y,z)\neq(0,0,0)\end{subarray}}\psi(-ax-by-cz)=\begin{cases}q-1&\text{if }b\neq 0,\\
-1&\text{if }a\neq 0,\>b=0,\\
q^{2}-1&\text{if }a=b=0.\end{cases}

Suppose that
c\neq 0
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\
y^{2}-xz=0\\
(x,y,z)\neq(0,0,0)\end{subarray}}\psi(-ax-by-cz)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{\times}\\

If

, this is easily seen to be
-1
. Suppose that

. Then

Suppose that

(and still

). Then

If

, then this equation has exactly two solutions, otherwise none. Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,z\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\\
y^{2}-xz=0\\
(x,y,z)\neq(0,0,0)\end{subarray}}\psi(-ax-by-cz)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}2\cdot(q-1)+(q-2)(-1)-1&\text{if }\varepsilon(cab^{-2})=1,\\

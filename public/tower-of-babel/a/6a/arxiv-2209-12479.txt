A complete family of Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for convex capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space Thanks: The research was surpported by National Key Research and Development Program of China 2021YFA1001800 and 2020YFA0713100, National Natural Science Foundation of China NSFC11721101 and NSFC12101027.
Yingxiang Hu, Yong Wei, Bo Yang, Tailong Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the locally constrained inverse curvature flow for hypersurfaces in the half-space with θ \theta -capillary boundary, which was recently introduced by Wang-Weng-Xia [ Wang-Weng-Xia2022 ] . Assume that the initial hypersurface is strictly convex with the contact angle θ ∈ ( 0 , π / 2 ] \theta\in(0,\pi/2] . We prove that the solution of the flow remains to be strictly convex for t > 0 t>0 , exists for all positive time and converges smoothly to a spherical cap. As an application, we prove a complete family of Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for convex capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space with the contact angle θ ∈ ( 0 , π / 2 ] \theta\in(0,\pi/2] . Along the proof, we develop a new tensor maximum principle for parabolic equations on compact manifold with proper Neumann boundary condition.

Introduction

The classical Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for convex domains in Euclidean space are important in convex geometry. For a smooth bounded domain
\widehat{\Sigma}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we denote
\Sigma=\partial\widehat{\Sigma}
the boundary of
\widehat{\Sigma}
. Let
\kappa(x)=(\kappa_{1}(x),\cdots,\kappa_{n}(x))
be the principal curvatures of
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
at the point
x
, and
H_{k}(\kappa)
is the
k
th normalized mean curvature of
\Sigma
. The quermassintegrals of
\widehat{\Sigma}
are given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle W_{0}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=|\widehat{\Sigma}|,\quad W_{k+1}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}dA,\quad 0\leq k\leq n.

The Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for quermassintegrals (see
[
Schn
]
) of a convex domain
\widehat{\Sigma}
state as

(1.2)

\displaystyle\frac{W_{k}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{|\mathbb{B}^{n+1}|}\geq\left(\frac{W_{\ell}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{|\mathbb{B}^{n+1}|}\right)^{\frac{n+1-k}{n+1-\ell}},\quad 0\leq\ell<k\leq n,

with equality holds if and only if
\widehat{\Sigma}
is a ball. The inequality (
1.2
) is a natural generalization of the classical isoperimetric inequality, which corresponds to the case
k=1,\ell=0
of (
1.2
). Using a quermassintegral preserving curvature flow, McCoy
[
McC05
]
provides a new proof of the inequality (
1.2
) for smooth convex domains. Later, Guan and Li
[
GL09
]
applied the inverse curvature flow
[
Ger1990
,
Urbas1991
]
to show that the inequality (
1.2
) holds for smooth
(k-1)
-convex and starshaped domains. Using the similar idea, there are many recent work on the Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities for smooth domains in the hyperbolic space
[
ACW20
,
AHL2020
,
BGL
,
GL-2021
,
GWW14
,
HL18
,
HLW2020
,
LWX14
,
Wang-Xia2014
]
and in the sphere
[
CS22
,
ChenGLS22
,
MS15
,
WeiX15
]
.

In this paper, we focus on the generalizations of (
1.2
) to the hypersurfaces with capillary boundary in the half-space
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
. Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
be a smooth properly embedded hypersurface in the half-space
\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
with boundary
\partial\Sigma
supported on
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
, where by proper embeddedness we mean that
\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
and
\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
. Let
\widehat{\Sigma}
be the bounded domain enclosed by
\Sigma
and the hyperplane
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
. Then the boundary of
\widehat{\Sigma}
consists of two parts: one is
\Sigma
and the other one lying on
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
is denoted by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
. See Figure
1
in §
2
. We also denote by
\partial\Sigma=\Sigma\cap\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
as the common boundary of
\Sigma
and
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
. Suppose that
\Sigma
intersects with
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
at a constant contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. Then the following relative isoperimetric inequality in the half-space holds (see
[
Maggi
, §19.3]
):

(1.3)

\displaystyle\frac{|\Sigma|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|}{|\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}|}\geq\left(\frac{|\widehat{\Sigma}|}{|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|}\right)^{\frac{n}{n+1}}.

The equality holds in (
1.3
) if and only if
\Sigma
is homothetic to a spherical cap
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}
with contact angle
\theta
. See (
2.2
) for the definition of the spherical caps, where
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}=C_{1,\theta}(e)
.

Recently, the following geometric functionals were introduced by Wang, Weng and Xia
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
]
, which can be considered as the suitable quermassintegrals for capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space
\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
with constant contact angle
\theta
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=

\displaystyle|\widehat{\Sigma}|,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}(|\Sigma|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|),

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}):=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}dA-\frac{\cos\theta\sin^{k}\theta}{n}\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{k-1}^{\partial\Sigma}ds,\quad 1\leq k\leq n,

where
H_{k-1}^{\partial\Sigma}
is the normalized
(k-1)
th mean curvature of
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In particular, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{n+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{1}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma}H_{n}dA-\cos\theta\sin^{n}\theta\frac{\omega_{n-1}}{n(n+1)}=(n+1)\omega_{\theta},

which can be considered as a Gauss-Bonnet type result for capillary hypersurfaces in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
with contact angle
\theta
, where
\omega_{n-1}=|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
and
\omega_{\theta}=|\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\mathbb{S}^{n}_{\theta}}|
.

The quermassintegrals defined above satisfy the following variational formulas (
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
, Theorem 1.1]
): Let
\Sigma_{t}\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
be a family of smooth, embedded, capillary hypersurfaces with a fixed contact angle
\theta
, which are given by the embeddings
x(\cdot,t):M\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
and satisfy

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}x\right)^{\bot}=\mathcal{F}\nu

for some speed function
\mathcal{F}
. Then for
0\leq k\leq n
,

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\mathcal{F}H_{k}dA_{t},

and in particular

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{V}_{n+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=0.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}:=\{x\in\mathbb{B}^{n+1}|\langle x,e_{n+1}\rangle>\cos\theta\}
. The following conjecture was proposed in
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
, Conjecture 1.5]
, which could be viewed as the relative Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities for quermassintegrals in the half-space.

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
]
)
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
be a convex hypersurface with capillary boundary supported on
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
at a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi)
. Then there holds

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|}\geq\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\ell,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|}\right)^{\frac{n+1-k}{n+1-\ell}},\quad 0\leq\ell<k\leq n

with equality holding in (
1.6
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap in the half-space with
\theta
-capillary boundary.

For this purpose, Wang, Weng and Xia
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
]
introduced a new locally constrained inverse curvature flows: Let
e=-e_{n+1}
, where
e_{n+1}
is the
(n+1)
th coordinate vector in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
. Let
x:M\rightarrow\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
be the embedding of
\Sigma=x(M)\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
with its boundary
x|_{\partial M}:\partial M\rightarrow\partial\Sigma\subset\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
and
\nu
its unit normal vector field. For
k=1,\cdots,n
, we consider a family of smooth immersions
x(\cdot,t)
of capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space with contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, which evolves by

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} (\partial_{t}x)^{\bot}=&\left((1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}-\langle x,\nu\rangle\right)\nu,&\text{in $M\times[0,T)$},\\
\langle\overline{N}\circ x,\nu\rangle=&\cos(\pi-\theta),&\text{on $\partial M\times[0,T)$},\\
x(\cdot,0)=&x_{0}(\cdot),&\text{on $M$}.\end{aligned}\right.

The introduction of this flow is motivated by a similar flow proposed earlier by Brendle, Guan and Li
[
BGL
]
for closed hypersurfaces in the space forms, and is based on the Minkowski type formula
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
, Proposition 2.5]
:

(1.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}H_{k-1}(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)dA=\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}\langle x,\nu\rangle dA,\quad 1\leq k\leq n,

for
\theta
-capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space. It follows from the variation formula (
1.5
) that

(1.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=\frac{n+1-k}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left((1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)H_{k-1}-\langle x,\nu\rangle H_{k}\right)dA_{t}=0,

and for
1\leq\ell<k

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{V}_{\ell,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma}_{t})=

\displaystyle\frac{n+1-\ell}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left((1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)\frac{H_{k-1}}{H_{k}}H_{\ell}-\langle x,\nu\rangle H_{\ell}\right)dA_{t}

(1.10)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{n+1-\ell}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}\left((1+\cos\theta\langle e,\nu)\rangle H_{\ell-1}-\langle x,\nu\rangle H_{\ell}\right)dA_{t}=0,

where we used the Newton-MacLaurin inequality (
2.4
). That is, along the flow (
1.7
),
\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}
is preserved and
\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}
is monotone increasing for
\ell<k
. Then the flow (
1.7
) could be used as a tool to establish the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for convex capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space. The special case
k=n
of the flow (
1.7
) has been considered in
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
]
, and as a consequence, the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequality between
\mathcal{V}_{n,\theta}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\ell,\theta}
for
0\leq\ell<n
is obtained.

The aim of this paper is to provide a solution to the Conjecture
1.1
for convex capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space with the contact angle
\theta\in(0,\pi/2]
. We shall prove the smooth convergence of the flow (
1.7
) for all
1\leq k\leq n
. As the key step, we prove the preservation of the convexity along the flow (
1.7
). For this purpose, we develop a generalized tensor maximum principle on compact manifolds with proper Neumann boundary condition.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth compact manifold with boundary
\partial\Sigma
and
\mu
be the outward pointing unit normal vector field of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
. Assume that
S_{ij}
is a smooth time-varying symmetric tensor field on
\Sigma
satisfying

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}S_{ij}=a^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\ell}S_{ij}+b^{k}\nabla_{k}S_{ij}+N_{ij}

on
\Sigma\times[0,T]
, where the coefficients
a^{k\ell}
and
b^{k}
are smooth,
\nabla
is a smooth symmetric connection, and
(a^{k\ell})
is positive definite everywhere. Suppose that

(1.11)

N_{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}+\sup_{\Gamma}2a^{k\ell}\left(2\Gamma^{p}_{k}\nabla_{\ell}S_{ip}\xi^{i}-\Gamma_{k}^{p}\Gamma_{\ell}^{q}S_{pq}\right)\geq 0\quad\mathrm{on}~\Sigma\times(0,T],

(1.12)

\left(\nabla_{\mu}S_{ij}\right)\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\geq 0\qquad\mathrm{on}~\partial\Sigma\times(0,T]

whenever
S_{ij}\geq 0
and
S_{ij}\xi^{i}=0
. If
S_{ij}\geq 0
everywhere on
\Sigma\times\{0\}
, then it remains so on
\Sigma\times[0,T]
.

The tensor maximum principle is a generalization of the scalar maximum principle so that it applies to symmetric
2
-tensors. This was introduced firstly by Hamilton
[
Hamilton1982
, Theorem 9.1]
on compact manifolds to prove that the positivity of Ricci curvature is preserved along
3
-dimensional Ricci flow. Since then, it has been a powerful tool in the geometric flows and has been applied to find curvature preserving conditions along many kinds of flows. Hamilton’s tensor maximum principle was refined by Andrews
[
And2007
, Theorem 3.1]
by weakening the null eigenvector condition
N_{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\geq 0
to the condition (
1.11
). The tensor maximum principle on compact manifolds with proper Neumann boundary condition was also obtained by Stahl
[
Stahl1996-2
, Theorem 3.3]
under Hamilton’s null eigenvector condition, in order to study the mean curvature flow with free boundary. Our Theorem
1.2
can be viewed as a generalization of the tensor maximum principle of Hamilton
[
Hamilton1982
, Theorem 9.1]
, Andrews
[
And2007
, Theorem 3.1]
and Stahl
[
Stahl1996-2
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Remark 1
.

Besides the null eigenvector condition
N_{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\geq 0
and the boundary condition (
1.12
), Stahl’s tensor maximum principle in
[
Stahl1996-2
, Theorem 3.3]
needs an extra condition
\langle b,\mu\rangle\geq 0
on the boundary
\partial\Sigma
. Theorem
1.2
refines Stahl’s result by removing this condition and weakening the null eigenvector condtion to (
1.11
).

The tensor maximum principle in Theorem
1.2
is sufficient for us to prove that the strict convexity is preserved along the flow (
1.7
). With the help of the strict convexity, we establish the convergence result of the flow (
1.7
) for strictly convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in the half-space.

Theorem 1.3
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
be a strictly convex hypersurface with capillary boundary supported on
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
at a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, which is given by the embedding
x_{0}:M\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
of a compact manifold
M
with non-empty boundary in
\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
. Then for each
k=1,\cdots,n
, there exists a unique smooth solution
x:M\times[0,\infty)\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
to the flow (
1.7
), which remains to be strictly convex and exists for all time
t\in[0,\infty)
. Moreover,
x(\cdot,t)
converges to
x_{\infty}(\cdot)
in the
C^{\infty}
-topology as
t\rightarrow\infty
, and the limit
x_{\infty}:M\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
is a spherical cap.

As an application of Theorem
1.3
, we establish a complete family of Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities for convex capillary boundary hypersurfaces in the half-space.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
n\geq 2
, let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
be a convex hypersurface with capillary boundary supported on
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
at a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Then there holds

(1.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|}\geq\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\ell,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|}\right)^{\frac{n+1-k}{n+1-\ell}},\quad 0\leq\ell<k\leq n.

Equality holds in (
1.13
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap in the half-space with
\theta
-capillary boundary.

.

The case
k=n
and

of (

Remark 3

For convex capillary hypersurfaces in the unit Euclidean ball

]

When

2.3.
Elementary symmetric polynomials

For
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial function is defined as

\sigma_{k}(\kappa)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\kappa_{i_{1}}\cdots\kappa_{i_{k}},\qquad k=1,\cdots,n

and its normalization is denoted by
H_{k}(\kappa)=\binom{n}{k}^{-1}\sigma_{k}(\kappa)
. We also take
\sigma_{0}(\kappa)=H_{0}(\kappa)=1
by convention. This definition can be extended to symmetric matrices. Let
W\in\mathrm{Sym}(n)
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix. Denote
\kappa=\kappa(W)
the eigenvalues of
W
. Set
\sigma_{k}(W)=\sigma_{k}(\kappa(W))
. We have

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(W)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k!}\delta_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}^{j_{1},\cdots,j_{k}}W_{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdots W_{i_{k}j_{k}},\quad k=1,\cdots,n,

where
\delta_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{k}}^{j_{1},\cdots,j_{k}}
is the generalized Kronecker delta.

Let
\Gamma_{k}^{+}
be the Garding cone defined by

\Gamma_{k}^{+}:=\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\sigma_{j}(\kappa)>0,\forall 1\leq j\leq k\}

and
\Gamma_{+}
be the positive cone

\Gamma_{+}:=\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\kappa_{i}>0,\forall 1\leq i\leq n\}.

We have the following Newton-MacLaurin inequalities (see e.g.
[
guan2014
, Lemma 2.5]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

For
1\leq\ell<k\leq n
, we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle H_{\ell}H_{k-1}\geq H_{\ell-1}H_{k},\quad\forall\kappa\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}.

Equality holds if and only if
\kappa=\lambda(1,\cdots,1)
for any constant
\lambda>0
.

In this paper, we will focus on the function
F={H_{\ell}}/{H_{\ell-1}}
, where
1\leq\ell\leq n
. Sometimes, during the calculation of some evolution equations, we write
F=F(W)=f(\kappa)
for
\kappa=\kappa(W)
, where we view
F
as a smooth function of the symmetric matrix
W
and
f
as a smooth function of the eigenvalues
\kappa
of
W
. Denote
\dot{F}^{k\ell},\ddot{F}^{k\ell,pq}
and
\dot{f}^{i},\ddot{f}^{ij}
as the first and second derivatives of
F
and
f
with respect to their arguments. The derivatives of
f
and
F
are related in the following way: if
W
is diagonal, then

\dot{F}^{k\ell}(W)=\dot{f}^{k}(\kappa)\delta_{k\ell}.

At any diagonal
W
with distinct eigenvalues, the second derivatives
\ddot{F}
of
F
in direction
B\in\mathrm{Sym}(n)
are given in terms of
\dot{f}
and
\ddot{f}
by (see
[
And2007
]
):

(2.5)

\ddot{F}^{ij,k\ell}(W)B_{ij}B_{k\ell}=\sum_{i,k}\ddot{f}^{ik}(\kappa)B_{ii}B_{kk}+2\sum_{i>k}\frac{\dot{f}^{i}(\kappa)-\dot{f}^{k}(\kappa)}{\kappa_{i}-\kappa_{k}}B_{ik}^{2}.

This formula makes sense as a limit in the case of any repeated values of
\kappa_{i}
. In what follows, we will drop the arguments when
F
and
f
, and their
derivatives, are evaluated at
W
or
\kappa=\kappa(W)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F={H_{\ell}}/{H_{\ell-1}}
, where
1\leq\ell\leq n
.

(1)

F
is homogeneous of degree one, so
F=\dot{F}^{k\ell}W_{k\ell}
.

(2)

F
is concave in
\Gamma_{+}
, and so
(\dot{f}^{i}-\dot{f}^{j})(\kappa_{i}-\kappa_{j})\leq 0
for
i\neq j
. If
W
is diagonal, we also have
(\dot{F}^{ii}-\dot{F}^{jj})(W_{ii}-W_{jj})\leq 0
for
i\neq j
. Moreover,
\sum_{i}\dot{f}^{i}\geq 1
on
\Gamma_{+}

(3)

If
\kappa\in\Gamma_{+}
, then there holds

(2.6)

\displaystyle F^{2}\leq\dot{F}^{k\ell}(W^{2})_{k\ell}\leq(n-\ell+1)F^{2}.

where
(W^{2})_{k\ell}=W_{kp}W_{p\ell}
.

Proof.

These properties are well known. See
[
guan2014
]
and
[
Andrews-McCoy-Zheng
]
. We include a proof for (iii) for convenience of the readers.

Firstly, we have

F=\frac{\sigma_{\ell}(\kappa)}{\sigma_{\ell-1}(\kappa)}\frac{\sigma_{\ell-1}(I)}{\sigma_{\ell}(I)},

where
I=(1,\cdots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Using the formula (see
[
guan2014
, Proposition 2.2]
)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{\sigma}_{\ell}^{i}(\kappa)\kappa_{i}^{2}=\sigma_{1}(\kappa)\sigma_{\ell}(\kappa)-(\ell+1)\sigma_{\ell+1}(\kappa),

we get

\displaystyle\dot{F}^{k\ell}(h^{2})_{k\ell}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{f}^{i}\kappa_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{\ell-1}(I)}{\sigma_{\ell}(I)}\frac{\dot{\sigma}_{\ell}^{i}(\kappa)\kappa_{i}^{2}\sigma_{\ell-1}(\kappa)-\sigma_{\ell}(\kappa)\dot{\sigma}^{i}_{\ell-1}(\kappa)\kappa_{i}^{2}}{\sigma_{\ell-1}^{2}(\kappa)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{\ell-1}(I)}{\sigma_{\ell}(I)}\frac{\ell\sigma_{\ell}^{2}(\kappa)-(\ell+1)\sigma_{\ell+1}(\kappa)\sigma_{\ell-1}(\kappa)}{\sigma_{\ell-1}^{2}(\kappa)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(n-\ell+1)H_{\ell}^{2}-(n-\ell)H_{\ell-1}H_{\ell+1}}{H_{\ell-1}^{2}}.

Then the inequality (

) follows from the Newton-MacLaurin inequality

and
H_{\ell-1}H_{\ell+1}\geq 0
since

.

\nabla_{\mu}h_{\alpha\alpha}=\cot\theta{h}_{\alpha\alpha}(h_{\mu\mu}-h_{\alpha\alpha}),\quad\alpha=1,\cdots,n-1,

which implies that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}H=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\dot{F}^{\mu\mu}}(\dot{F}^{\mu\mu}-\dot{F}^{\alpha\alpha})(h_{\mu\mu}-h_{\alpha\alpha}){h}_{\alpha\alpha}\cot\theta.

Since
F
is concave, there holds
(\dot{F}^{\mu\mu}-\dot{F}^{\alpha\alpha})(h_{\mu\mu}-h_{\alpha\alpha})\leq 0
. By the convexity of
\Sigma_{t}
and the condition
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, we also have

{h}_{\alpha\alpha}\cot\theta\geq 0
. Therefore, we conclude that
\nabla_{\mu}H\leq 0
on the boundary
\partial\Sigma_{t}
.

(v). Using (
3.5
) and (
3.21
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}x,\nu\rangle+\langle x,\partial_{t}\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F}-u-\frac{\cos\theta}{F}\langle x,e_{i}\rangle h_{i}^{k}\langle e_{k},e\rangle+\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\nabla F

\displaystyle+\langle x,\nabla u\rangle+\langle x,e_{i}\rangle h_{i}^{k}\langle e_{k},V\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle V+x-\frac{\cos\theta}{F}e,\nabla u\right\rangle+\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F}-u+\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\nabla F.

On the other hand, the equation (
3.23
) implies that

\dot{F}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\ell}u=F+\langle x,\nabla F\rangle-\dot{F}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}u.

Combining the above two equations, we obtain (
v
).

(vi). Similarly, by (
3.5
) and (
3.20
), we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\langle\nu,e\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}\nu,e\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\cos\theta}{F}\langle h_{i}^{k}e_{k},e\rangle\langle e_{i},e\rangle+\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\langle\nabla F,e\rangle

\displaystyle+\nabla_{i}\langle x,\nu\rangle\langle e_{i},e\rangle+\langle V,e_{k}\rangle h_{i}^{k}\langle e_{i},e\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\langle V+x-\frac{\cos\theta}{F}e,\nabla\langle\nu,e\rangle\right\rangle+\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\langle\nabla F,e\rangle.

The equation (

\dot{F}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\langle\nu,e\rangle=\langle\nabla F,e\rangle-\dot{F}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}\langle\nu,e\rangle.

Combining the above two equations, we obtain (
vi
).
∎

4.
Tensor maximum principle

In this section, we prove the tensor maximum principle in Theorem
1.2
.

Let
\Sigma
be a smooth compact manifold with boundary
\partial\Sigma
and
\mu
be the outward pointing unit normal vector field of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
.
Denote
T\Sigma
the tangent bundle of
\Sigma
, that is,
T\Sigma=\{(p,\xi),~p\in\Sigma,~\xi\in T_{p}\Sigma\}
. Let
S=(S_{ij})
be a smooth symmetric tensor on
\Sigma
. We consider a function on the tangent bundle of
\Sigma
:

(4.1)

Z(p,\xi)=S(p)(\xi,\xi)

where
(p,\xi)\in T\Sigma
. Then
S_{ij}\geq 0
is equivalent to an inequality
Z(p,\xi)\geq 0
for all
(p,\xi)\in T\Sigma
. Let
p\in\Sigma
be a point where
S(p)
has a null vector
\xi
, i.e.,
S(p)(\xi,\xi)=0
. The key of Andrew’s argument
[
And2007
, §3]
is that the minimality of
Z(p,\xi)
implies that the Hessian of
Z
is non-negative definite, which is obvious when
p
is an interior point of the manifold. We prove that this is also true on the boundary when
S_{ij}
satisfies a proper Neumann boundary condition.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
S_{ij}\geq 0
on
\Sigma
and

(4.2)

(\nabla_{\mu}S_{ij})\xi^{i}\xi^{j}\geq 0,\qquad\mathrm{on}~\partial\Sigma

whenever
S_{ij}\xi^{j}=0
for some tangent vector
\xi
. If the function
Z
defined in (
4.1
) attains its minimum
Z(p,\xi)=0
at
(p,\xi)\in T\Sigma
, then
DZ|_{(p,\xi)}=0
and
D^{2}Z|_{(p,\xi)}
is non-negative definite.

Proof.

We choose coordinates
x^{1},\cdots,x^{n}
for
\Sigma
near
p
such that the connection coefficients
\nabla
vanish at
p
. Then any vector in
T_{q}{\Sigma}
for
q
near
p
has the form
\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\dot{x}^{i}\partial_{i}}
, so
T\Sigma
is described locally by the
2n
coordinates
x^{1},\cdots,x^{n}
and
\dot{x}^{1},\cdots,\dot{x}^{n}
. We can rotate the coordinates such that
\xi=\partial_{1}
. In particular, we have
Z(p,\xi)=0
and
Z(q,\eta)\geq 0
for all
q\in\Sigma
and
\eta\in T_{q}\Sigma
.

If
p\in\operatorname{int}(\Sigma)
, then the conclusion follows easily. So in the following, we assume that
p\in\partial\Sigma
. For any
v\in T_{p}\partial\Sigma
, we have

0=DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot\left(v,0\right),

We need to show that
DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot\left(\mu,0\right)=0
as well, where
\mu\in T_{p}\Sigma
is the unit outward normal of
\partial\Sigma
in
\Sigma
. Since
Z(p,\xi)=S_{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}=0
, by the assumption (
4.2
), we have

0\leq\left(\nabla_{\mu}S_{ij}\right)\xi^{i}\xi^{j}=DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot\left(\mu,0\right).

If
DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot\left(\mu,0\right)>0
, we consider the geodesic
\gamma:[0,\delta]\rightarrow\Sigma
with
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=-\mu
. Extend
\xi\in T_{p}\Sigma
to a vector field
\xi(s)\in T_{\gamma(s)}\Sigma
along the geodesic
\gamma
by parallel transport, that is,
\xi^{\prime}(s)=\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}(s)}\xi(s)=0
and
\xi(0)=\xi
. Along the geodesic
\gamma(s)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}Z(\gamma(s),\xi(s))=

\displaystyle DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot(\gamma^{\prime}(0),\xi^{\prime}(0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot\left(\mu,0\right)<0

and hence there exists an interior point
\gamma(s_{0})\in\text{int}(\Sigma),0<s_{0}<\delta
such that
Z(\gamma(s_{0}),\xi(s_{0}))<Z(\gamma(0),\xi(0))=Z(p,\xi)=0
, which contradicts with
Z\geq 0
. Therefore,

DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot(v,0)=0,\quad\forall~v\in T_{p}\Sigma.

Since
\xi\in T_{p}\Sigma
is an interior point, we also have

DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot(0,\eta)=0,\quad\forall~\eta\in T_{\xi}(T_{p}\Sigma).

This means that
DZ|_{(p,\xi)}=0
. In local coordinates, we write this as

0=\frac{\partial Z}{\partial x^{k}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}S_{11}

and

0=\frac{\partial Z}{\partial\dot{x}^{k}}=2S_{k1}

for all
k=1,\cdots,n
.

Next, we show that
D^{2}Z
is non-negative definite, i.e., for any
a=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{n})
,
b=(b_{1},\cdots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, at the point
(p,\xi)
we have

D^{2}Z|_{(p,\xi)}\left((a,b),(a,b)\right)\geq 0.

Suppose not, then there exists
(a,b)
such that

D^{2}Z|_{(p,\xi)}\left((a,b),(a,b)\right)<0.

Note that for any fixed two vectors
\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle(D^{2}Z)|_{(p,\xi)}((0,\xi),(\tilde{a},\tilde{b}))=

\displaystyle 2\nabla_{\tilde{a}}S_{11}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}S_{1k}\tilde{b}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot\left(2\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\right)=0.

Hence there exits a constant
k\in\mathbb{R}
such that
b-k\xi\in T_{\xi}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and

D^{2}Z|_{(p,\xi)}\left((a,b-k\xi),(a,b-k\xi))\right)=D^{2}Z|_{(p,\xi)}\left((a,b),(a,b)\right)<0

at the point
(p,\xi)
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\langle a,\mu\rangle<0
, i.e.,
a\in T_{p}\Sigma
is pointing inward of
\Sigma
. Denote
UT(\Sigma)
the unit tangent bundle of
\Sigma
, that is,
UT(\Sigma)=\{(p,\xi)\in T\Sigma,~|\xi|=1\}
. Let
\tilde{\gamma}
be a curve in
UT(\Sigma)
such that
\tilde{\gamma}(s)=(p(s),\xi(s))
with
\tilde{\gamma}(0)=(p,\xi),\ \tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(0)=(a,b-k\xi)
. Then we have

\frac{d}{ds}\Big|_{s=0}Z(\tilde{\gamma}(s))=DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot(a,b-k\xi)=0,

and

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\Big|_{s=0}Z(\tilde{\gamma}(s))=

\displaystyle D^{2}Z|_{(p,\xi)}(\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(0),\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(0))+DZ|_{(p,\xi)}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D^{2}Z|_{(p,\xi)}\left((a,b-k\xi),(a,b-k\xi))\right)<0,

which contradicts with the minimality of
Z(\tilde{\gamma}(s))
at
s=0
.
∎

By Lemma
4.1
, we can use the argument in
[
And2007
, §3]
to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Recall that the coordinate is chosen such that
\partial_{1}=\xi
. Consider the second order conditions implied by the minimality of
Z
, the entries in the Hessian matrix
D^{2}Z
are as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial x^{k}\partial x^{\ell}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{k}\partial x^{\ell}}S_{11},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial x^{k}\partial\dot{x}^{q}}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{k}}S_{1q},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial\dot{x}^{p}\partial\dot{x}^{q}}

\displaystyle=2S_{pq},

where
1\leq k,\ell,p,q\leq n
. Note that in the coordinates chosen above,
\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\ell}S_{11}=\frac{\partial^{2}S_{11}}{\partial x^{k}\partial x^{\ell}}
at
p
. Since
S_{ij}
satisfies the boundary condition (
1.12
) whenever
S_{ij}\geq 0
and
S_{ij}\xi^{j}=0
, by Lemma
4.1
we have
D^{2}Z\geq 0
at
(p,\xi)
. Then for any matrix
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{k}^{p})_{n\times n}
, we have:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq a^{k\ell}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial x^{k}\partial x^{\ell}}-\Gamma_{k}^{p}\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial x^{\ell}\partial\dot{x}^{p}}-\Gamma_{\ell}^{q}\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial x^{k}\partial\dot{x}^{q}}+\Gamma_{k}^{p}\Gamma_{\ell}^{q}\frac{\partial^{2}Z}{\partial\dot{x}^{p}\partial\dot{x}^{q}}\right)

\displaystyle=a^{k\ell}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}S_{11}}{\partial x^{k}\partial x^{\ell}}-4\Gamma_{k}^{p}\frac{\partial S_{1p}}{\partial x^{\ell}}+2\Gamma_{k}^{p}\Gamma_{\ell}^{q}S_{pq}\right).

It follows that at the minimum point
(p,\xi)
, the function
Z
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial Z}{\partial t}=

\displaystyle a^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\ell}S_{ij}\dot{x}^{i}\dot{x}^{j}+b^{k}\nabla_{k}S_{ij}\dot{x}^{i}\dot{x}^{j}+N_{ij}\dot{x}^{i}\dot{x}^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a^{k\ell}\frac{\partial^{2}S_{11}}{\partial x^{k}\partial x^{\ell}}+N_{11}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma}2a^{k\ell}\left(2\Gamma_{k}^{p}\nabla_{\ell}S_{1p}-\Gamma_{k}^{p}\Gamma_{\ell}^{q}S_{pq}\right)+N_{11}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0,

by the condition (
1.11
). By the maximum principle (see the argument in Lemma 3.1 and Lemma 3.5 of
[
Hamilton
]
), we conclude that
Z\geq 0
is preserved.
∎

5.
Preserving of convexity

Since the initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
is strictly convex, we can choose a suitable point
o
on
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
as the origin of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
\Sigma_{0}
is star-shaped with respect to
o
. Moreover, there exists two constants
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\infty
such that

\Sigma_{0}\subset\widehat{C_{r_{2},\theta}}\backslash\widehat{C_{r_{1},\theta}},

where
\widehat{C_{r_{i},\theta}},i=1,2
are spherical caps of radius
r_{i}
. As the spherical caps are stationary along the flow (
1.7
), the avoidance principle implies that the solution
\Sigma_{t}
of the flow (
3.10
) satisfies

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t}\subset\widehat{C_{r_{2},\theta}}\backslash\widehat{C_{r_{1},\theta}},

for all time
t\in[0,T)
. This gives the
C^{0}
estimate of
\Sigma_{t}
.

The star-shapedness of
\Sigma_{t}
is preserved: this follows from applying maximum principle to a modified support function introduced in
[
WWX23
, §2]
:

\bar{u}=\frac{u}{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}.

Combining (
v
) and (
vi
),
\bar{u}
satisfies the evolution equation

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{u}=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\dot{F}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\bar{u}+\left\langle V+x-\frac{\cos\theta}{F}e,\nabla\bar{u}\right\rangle

(5.2)

\displaystyle+\bar{u}\left(\frac{\dot{F}^{ij}(h^{2})_{ij}}{F^{2}}-1\right)+\frac{2\cos\theta}{F^{2}}\dot{F}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\langle\nu,e\rangle\nabla_{j}\bar{u}.

On the boundary
\partial\Sigma_{t}
, using (
2.1
) we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}u=

\displaystyle\langle\mu,\nu\rangle+\langle x,h(\mu,\mu)\mu\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(\mu,\mu)\cos\theta\langle x,\bar{\nu}\rangle=h(\mu,\mu)\cot\theta u,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\langle\nu,e\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle h(\mu,\mu)\mu,e\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h(\mu,\mu)\langle\sin\theta e,e\rangle=-h(\mu,\mu)\tan\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle.

These imply that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\bar{u}=

\displaystyle\frac{uh(\mu,\mu)}{(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{2}}\left(\cot\theta+\frac{\langle\nu,e\rangle}{\sin\theta}\right)=0,\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\Sigma_{t}.

Applying maximum principle to (
5
) and using the boundary condition (
5.3
), we have that
\bar{u}\geq\min_{\Sigma_{0}}\bar{u}>0
on
\Sigma_{t}
and then
u\geq(1-\cos\theta)\bar{u}>0
. That is,
\Sigma_{t}
is star-shaped.

By considering
\bar{F}=\bar{u}F
, a positive lower bound on
F
is proved in
[
WWX23
, Prop. 2.6]
. We include the proof here for the convenience of readers.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that the initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
is star-shaped and satisfies
F>0
. Let
\Sigma_{t},t\in[0,T)
, be the smooth solution to the flow (
3.10
) starting from
\Sigma_{0}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
such that
F\geq C>0
on
\Sigma_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

As in
[
WWX23
, Prop. 2.6]
, we consider
\bar{F}=\bar{u}F
. The equations (
iii
) and (
5
) imply that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\bar{F}=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\dot{F}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\ell}\bar{F}+\left\langle V+x-\frac{\cos\theta}{F}e,\nabla\bar{F}\right\rangle

(5.4)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\cos\theta}{F^{2}}\dot{F}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\langle\nu,e\rangle\nabla_{j}\bar{F}-\frac{2(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{F^{3}}\dot{F}^{ij}\nabla_{i}F\nabla_{j}\bar{F}.

On the boundary
\partial\Sigma_{t}
, by (
3.15
) and (
5.3
) we also have

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\bar{F}=

\displaystyle 0,\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\Sigma_{t}.

The maximum principle implies that

Since

we conclude that

on

for a uniform positive constant

.
∎

Now we apply the tensor maximum principle in Theorem
1.2
to prove that the flow (
3.10
) preserves the strict convexity of

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume that the initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
is strictly convex. Let
\Sigma_{t},t\in[0,T)
, be the smooth solution to the flow (
3.10
) starting from
\Sigma_{0}
. Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\Sigma_{0})
such that
h_{ij}\geq\varepsilon Fg_{ij}
is preserved for positive times
t\in[0,T)
.

Proof.

Since the initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
is strictly convex, we have
F>0
on
\Sigma_{0}
and by the compactness there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\Sigma_{0})
such that
h_{ij}\geq\varepsilon Fg_{ij}
holds on
\Sigma_{0}
. Let

S_{ij}=h_{ij}-\varepsilon Fg_{ij}.

We will apply the tensor maximum principle in Theorem
1.2
to show that
S_{ij}\geq 0
is preserved. This together with Proposition
5.1
concludes the theorem.

Combining the evolution equations (
3.12
) – (
iii
), we calculate that the tensor
S_{ij}=h_{ij}-\varepsilon Fg_{ij}
evolves by

\displaystyle\partial_{t}S_{ij}=

\displaystyle\partial_{t}h_{ij}-\varepsilon\partial_{t}Fg_{ij}-\varepsilon F\partial_{t}g_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\dot{F}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}\nabla_{\ell}S_{ij}+\left\langle V+x-\frac{\cos\theta}{F}e,\nabla S_{ij}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\ddot{F}^{k\ell,pq}\nabla_{i}h_{k\ell}\nabla_{j}h_{pq}-\frac{2(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{F^{3}}\nabla_{i}F\nabla_{j}F

\displaystyle+\frac{\cos\theta}{F^{2}}(\nabla_{i}Fh_{j}^{k}+\nabla_{j}Fh_{i}^{k})\langle e,e_{k}\rangle-\frac{2\varepsilon\cos\theta}{F^{2}}\dot{F}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}Fh_{\ell}^{j}\langle e_{j},e\rangle g_{ij}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\varepsilon\left(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)}{F^{3}}\dot{F}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}F\nabla_{\ell}Fg_{ij}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F}-2\langle x,\nu\rangle\right)((S^{2})_{ij}+2\varepsilon FS_{ij})+S_{j}^{k}\nabla_{i}V_{k}+S_{i}^{k}\nabla_{j}V_{k}

\displaystyle+\left(1+\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\dot{F}^{k\ell}(h^{2})_{k\ell}-2\varepsilon(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)+2\varepsilon\langle x,\nu\rangle F\right)S_{ij}

\displaystyle+\frac{\varepsilon(2+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{F}\left(\dot{F}^{k\ell}(h^{2})_{k\ell}-\varepsilon F^{2}\right)g_{ij}.

We need to check both the conditions (
1.11
) and (

) in order to apply Theorem

.

). Assume that
S_{ij}\geq 0
, and

, where

is a null vector. Let
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}

\partial\Sigma_{t_{0}}\subset\Sigma_{t_{0}}

\{\mu\}\cup\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=1}^{n-1}

\Sigma_{t_{0}}
and we have either
\xi=\mu
or
\xi=e_{\alpha}
for some
\alpha
on
\partial\Sigma_{t_{0}}
. By Proposition
2.1
and
\nabla_{\mu}F=0
on
\partial\Sigma_{t_{0}}

(i)

If

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}h_{\alpha\alpha}-\varepsilon\nabla_{\mu}F

\displaystyle=

, where

) involving

can be ignored due to the null vector condition. We choose an orthonormal frame

around

such that

is diagonal with principal curvatures

in the increasing order and

corresponds to the principal direction of

. The the null vector

is the eigenvector corresponding to

. We need to show that

\displaystyle Q_{1}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\left(\ddot{F}^{k\ell,pq}\nabla_{1}h_{k\ell}\nabla_{1}h_{pq}-\frac{2}{F}|\nabla_{1}F|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2\varepsilon\left(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle\right)}{F^{3}}\dot{F}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}F\nabla_{\ell}F

\displaystyle+\frac{2\varepsilon\cos\theta}{F}\nabla_{1}F\langle e,e_{1}\rangle-\frac{2\varepsilon\cos\theta}{F^{2}}\dot{F}^{k\ell}\nabla_{k}Fh_{\ell}^{j}\langle e_{j},e\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{\varepsilon(2+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{F}\left(\dot{F}^{k\ell}(h^{2})_{k\ell}-\varepsilon F^{2}\right)

(5.7)

\displaystyle+\frac{2(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{F^{2}}\sup_{\Gamma}\dot{F}^{k\ell}(2\Gamma_{k}^{p}\nabla_{\ell}S_{1p}-\Gamma_{k}^{p}\Gamma_{\ell}^{q}S_{pq})\geq 0.

By continuity we can assume that the principal curvatures are mutually distinct at

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.4

Since the initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
is strictly convex, by choosing a suitable point lying in
\widehat{\partial\Sigma_{0}}
as the origin,
\Sigma_{0}
is star-shaped with respect to the origin. We have proved in §
5
that
\Sigma_{t}
,
t\in[0,T)
is star-shaped as well. In the class of star-shaped hypersurfaces, we can reduce the flow (
1.7
) to a scalar parabolic equation with an oblique boundary condition. This also ensures the short time existence of the flow (
1.7
).

6.1.
Radial graph parametrization

Suppose the capillary hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}_{+}
is star-shaped with respect to the origin, i.e.,
\langle x,\nu\rangle>0
, one can reparametrize it as a graph over
\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
. That is, there exists a positive radial function
r
defined on
\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle\Sigma=\{r(z)z~|~z\in\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}\},

where
z:=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})
is a local coordinate of
\overline{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
. We denote
\bar{\nabla}
to be the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
with respect to the round metric
\sigma:=g_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}
,
\partial_{i}=\partial_{z_{i}}
,
\sigma_{ij}=\sigma(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})
,
r_{i}=\bar{\nabla}_{i}r
and
r_{ij}=\bar{\nabla}_{i}\bar{\nabla}_{j}r
. It is convenient to use a new radial function
\varphi(z)=\log r(z)
and denote

v=\sqrt{1+|\bar{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}}.

Then the induced metric
g
, the inverse matrix of
g
, the unit normal
\nu
, the second fundamental form
h
and the Weingarten matrx
\mathcal{W}=(h_{i}^{j})
of
\Sigma
can be expressed as follows (see
[
guan2014
, §3]
):

\displaystyle g_{ij}=

\displaystyle r^{2}\delta_{ij}+r_{i}r_{j}=e^{2\varphi}(\sigma_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}),

\displaystyle g^{ij}=

\displaystyle e^{-2\varphi}\left(\sigma^{ij}-\frac{\varphi^{i}\varphi^{j}}{v^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle\nu=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{v}\left(\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{r^{2}}\bar{\nabla}r\right)=\frac{1}{v}(\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{r}\bar{\nabla}\varphi),

\displaystyle h_{ij}=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\varphi}}{v}(\sigma_{ij}+\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j}-\varphi_{ij}),

\displaystyle h_{i}^{j}=

\displaystyle g^{jk}h_{ki}=\frac{1}{e^{\varphi}v}\left(\delta_{i}^{j}-(\sigma^{jk}-\frac{\varphi^{j}\varphi^{k}}{v^{2}})\varphi_{ki}\right).

where
r^{i}=\sigma^{ij}r_{j}
,
\varphi^{i}=\sigma^{ij}\varphi_{j}
. The curvature function
F=\frac{H_{\ell}}{H_{\ell-1}}
is a smooth symmetric function of the Weingarten matrix
h_{i}^{j}
and thus can be expressed as a function of
\varphi,\varphi_{i},\varphi_{ij}
. We can also express the support function of
\Sigma
as

\displaystyle\langle x,\nu\rangle=\langle r\partial_{r},\nu\rangle=\frac{e^{\varphi}}{v}.

It’s well known that the flow (
3.10
) is equivalent to the scalar parabolic equation for the radial function
r
(and thus for
\varphi=\log r
) (see
[
Ger06
]
):

(6.1)

\partial_{t}\varphi=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{t}r=\frac{v}{e^{\varphi}}\mathcal{F},\qquad\text{in}~\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\times[0,T).

The capillary boundary condition can also be expressed by the radial function
\varphi
(see
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
, §4.1]
). We use the polar coordinate in the half-space. Let
x:=(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[0,\infty)
and
z:=(\beta,\xi)\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, we have

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=r\cos\beta,\quad|x^{\prime}|=r\sin\beta.

In this polar coordinates, the Euclidean metric is given by

dx^{2}=dr^{2}+r^{2}(d\beta^{2}+\sin^{2}\beta g_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}).

The vector
e_{n+1}=\cos\beta\partial_{r}-\frac{\sin\beta}{r}\partial_{\beta}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\langle\nu,e_{n+1}\rangle=\frac{1}{v}(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\bar{\nabla}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi).

On the boundary
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
, there holds

\displaystyle\overline{N}\circ x=-e_{n+1}=\frac{1}{r}\partial_{\beta},

and thus

\displaystyle-\cos\theta=\langle\nu,\overline{N}\circ x\rangle=-\frac{\bar{\nabla}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi}{v},

which we rewrite as

(6.2)

\bar{\nabla}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi=\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|\bar{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}},\qquad\mathrm{on}~\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\times[0,T).

Since
0\leq\cos\theta<1
, (
6.2
) is a uniform oblique boundary condition on
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
as in
[
Ural1991
]
. In fact, denote the boundary operator as

b(z,\varphi,p)=-\langle p,\partial_{\beta}\rangle+\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}},\quad p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then (
6.2
) is equivalent to
b(z,\varphi,\bar{\nabla}\varphi)=0
. As the unit inward normal of
\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
in
\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
is
\gamma=-\partial_{\beta}
, we have

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\frac{\partial b}{\partial p}\Big|_{p=\bar{\nabla}\varphi},\gamma\right)=

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\partial_{\beta},\partial_{\beta}-\cos\theta\frac{p}{\sqrt{1+|p|^{2}}}\Big|_{p=\bar{\nabla}\varphi}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\cos\theta\frac{\bar{\nabla}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi}{\sqrt{1+|\bar{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\cos^{2}\theta>0.

This means that oblique boundary condition (
6.2
) is non-degeneracy as in
[
Ural1991
]
.

In summary, under the radial graph parametrization, the flow (
1.7
) is transformed into a scalar parabolic equation with a uniform oblique boundary condition up to a tangential diffeomorphism:

(6.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\varphi=&\frac{v}{e^{\varphi}}\mathcal{F},\quad&\text{in $\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\times[0,T)$,}\\
\bar{\nabla}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi=&\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|\bar{\nabla}\varphi|^{2}},\quad&\text{on $\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\times[0,T)$},\\
\varphi(\cdot,0)=&\varphi_{0}(\cdot),\quad&\text{on $\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\varphi_{0}
is the radial function of
\Sigma_{0}
over
\overline{\mathbb{S}}_{+}^{n}
and

\mathcal{F}=\frac{H_{\ell-1}}{H_{\ell}}\left[1-\frac{\cos\theta}{v}\left(\cos\beta+\sin\beta\bar{\nabla}_{\partial_{\beta}}\varphi\right)\right]-\frac{e^{\varphi}}{v}.

6.2.
Curvature estimates

First, we deduce the uniform bound of
F
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Along the flow (
3.10
), if the initial hypersurface
\Sigma_{0}
is strictly convex, then there holds

(6.4)

\displaystyle 0<C\leq F(p,t)\leq\max_{M}F(\cdot,0),\quad\forall(p,t)\in M\times[0,T),

where
\varepsilon>0
is constant depending only on
\Sigma_{0}
.

Proof.

The lower bound of
F
has been proved in Proposition
5.1
. For the upper bound of
F
, we note that
\dot{F}^{k\ell}(h^{2})_{k\ell}\geq F^{2}
. By the evolution equation (
iii
) of

and the boundary condition
\nabla_{\mu}F=0
, the maximum principle (see
[
Stahl1996-2
, Theorem 3.1]
) implies that
F(p,t)\leq\max_{M}F(\cdot,0)
.
∎

Next, we prove the uniform upper bound of the mean curvature.

Proposition 6.2
.

There exists
C>0
depending only on
\Sigma_{0}
such that

\displaystyle H(p,t)\leq C,\quad\forall(p,t)\in[0,T).

Proof.

The proof is by applying maximum principle to the evolution equation (
iv
) of
H
and is essentially the same as
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
, Proposition 4.8]
. The only difference is that they used the fact
\dot{F}^{k\ell}(h^{2})_{k\ell}=F^{2}
due to that
F=H_{n}/H_{n-1}
, while for general
F=H_{\ell}/H_{\ell-1}
we instead use the inequality (
2.6
):

(6.5)

\dot{F}^{k\ell}(h^{2})_{k\ell}\leq(n-\ell+1)F^{2}.

We include the proof here for convenience of the readers.

By the concavity of
F
, we have

\displaystyle(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)F^{-2}\ddot{F}^{kl,pq}\nabla_{i}h_{kl}\nabla^{i}h_{pq}\leq 0.

Since
\nabla_{\mu}H\leq 0
on the boundary
\partial\Sigma_{t}
, arguing as Lemma
4.1
, we know that
\nabla H=0
and
\nabla^{2}H\leq 0
at the spatial maximum point of
H
, no matter the maximum point is an interior point or a boundary point. Then, by the evolution equation (
iv
) of
H
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\max_{M}H(p,t)\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{F^{2}}\dot{F}^{kl}(h^{2})_{kl}+1\right)H

\displaystyle-\frac{2(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{F^{3}}|\nabla F|^{2}+\frac{2\cos\theta}{F^{2}}\langle\nabla F,\nabla\langle\nu,e\rangle\rangle

(6.6)

\displaystyle-(2+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)F^{-1}|A|^{2}.

Using (
6.5
), the first term on the right hand side of (
6.2
) is bounded by
C_{1}H
for some positive constant
C_{1}
. The Weingarten equation and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality imply that

\langle\nabla F,\nabla\langle\nu,e\rangle\rangle\leq|\nabla F||A||e^{T}|,

where
e^{T}
denotes the tangential part of
e
. By completing the square, the second line of (
6.2
) satisfies

\displaystyle-\frac{2(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{F^{3}}|\nabla F|^{2}+\frac{2\cos\theta}{F^{2}}\langle\nabla F,\nabla\langle\nu,e\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{2(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{F^{3}}|\nabla F|^{2}+\frac{2\cos\theta}{F^{2}}|\nabla F||A||e^{T}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}{F^{3}}\left(|\nabla F|-\frac{F\cos\theta|A||e^{T}|}{2(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}\right)^{2}+\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{2F(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}|A|^{2}|e^{T}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{2F(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}|A|^{2}|e^{T}|^{2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\max_{M}H(p,t)\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}H-(2+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)F^{-1}|A|^{2}+\frac{\cos^{2}\theta}{2F(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}|A|^{2}|e^{T}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}H-\frac{\sin^{2}\theta+3(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)^{2}}{2F(1+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle)}|A|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}H-C_{2}|A|^{2},

where
C_{2}
depends on the uniform bound of
F
and
\theta
. The maximum principle implies that
H
is bounded from above by a uniform constant.
∎

and the scalar equation (
6.3
) is uniformly parabolic. Since
0\leq\cos\theta<1
, the boundary condition (
6.2
) satisfies the uniform oblique property. Then by the parabolic theory in
[
Ural1991
, Theorem 5]
(see also
[
Lieb96
, §14]
), we conclude the uniform
C^{\infty}
-estimates and the long-time existence of the solution to (
6.3
). The convergence to a uniquely determined spherical cap with
\theta
-capillary boundary as
t\rightarrow\infty
follows from the same argument as
[
Wang-Weng-Xia2022
, Proposition 4.12]
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

6.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

If the hypersurface
\Sigma
is strictly convex, we run the flow (
1.7
) starting from
\Sigma
. For
1\leq\ell<k\leq n
, by Theorem
1.3
, we know that the solution
\Sigma_{t}
of the (
1.7
) converges smoothly to a spherical cap
C_{r_{\infty},\theta}
. By (
1.9
) and (
1
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{C_{r_{\infty},\theta}}),\quad\mathcal{V}_{\ell,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\leq\mathcal{V}_{\ell,\theta}(\widehat{C_{r_{\infty},\theta}}).

Recall that

\mathcal{V}_{m,\theta}(\widehat{C_{r,\theta}})=|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|r^{n+1-m},\quad 0\leq m\leq n,

we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-k}}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{k,\theta}(\widehat{C_{r_{\infty},\theta}})}{|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-k}}=r_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\ell,\theta}(\widehat{C_{r_{\infty},\theta}})}{|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-\ell}}

(6.8)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathcal{V}_{\ell,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})}{|\mathbb{B}_{\theta}^{n+1}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1-\ell}}.

Equality holds if and only if
H_{k}H_{\ell-1}=H_{k-1}H_{\ell}
in (
1
), which is equivalent to that all
\Sigma_{t}
are spherical caps and in particular the initial hypersurface
\Sigma
is a spherical cap. When
\Sigma
is convex but not strictly convex, we can approximate
\Sigma
by a family of strictly convex hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Then the inequality (
1.13
) follows by approximation. The equality characterization can be proved by using an argument of
[
GL09
]
.

Finally, the inequality (
1.13
) for
0=\ell<k\leq n
follows from combining (
1.3
) and (
6.8
) for
1=\ell<k\leq n
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Appendix A
Approximation result

In this appendix, we prove that a convex hypersurface
\Sigma
with
\theta
-capillary boundary in the half-space can be approximated by a sequence of strictly convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in the half-space in the
C^{2,\alpha}
sense. Note that the free boundary case (i.e.
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
) in the unit ball was treated by Lambert and Scheuer in
[
Lambert-Scheuer2017
]
. We adapt their idea and include a proof for the case of capillary hypersurfaces in the half-space.

Let
\Sigma\subset\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
be a convex hypersurface with capillary boundary supported on
\partial\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
at a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, which is given by the embedding
x_{0}:M\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1}
of a compact manifold
M
with non-empty boundary. We consider the mean curvature flow

(A.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}x&=-H\nu+V,\qquad\text{in}\quad M\times[0,T),\\
\langle\bar{N}\circ x,\nu\rangle&=\cos(\pi-\theta),\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial M\times[0,T),\\
x(\cdot,0)&=x_{0}(\cdot)\qquad\text{on}\quad M,\end{aligned}\right.

and denote
\Sigma_{t}=x(M,t)
, where
V
is tangential component of
\partial_{t}x
and satisfies
V|_{\partial\Sigma_{t}}=-H\cot\theta\mu
.

The short time existence of flow (
A.1
) can be obtained by a similar argument as in
[
Stahl1996-2
]
by Stahl.

Theorem A.1
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the flow (
A.1
) admits a unique solution:

x(\cdot,t)\in C^{\infty}(M\times(0,\delta])\cap C^{2+\alpha,1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}(M\times[0,\delta]),

where
\delta>0
is a small constant.

Then we can prove the following approximation result:

Theorem A.2
.

Suppose that
\Sigma_{t}
,
t\in[0,\delta)
is a solution to the flow (
A.1
) starting from the convex hypersurface
\Sigma
. Then
\Sigma_{t}
is strictly convex for all time
t>0
as long as the flow exists.

Proof.

Note that the height function
\langle x,e_{n+1}\rangle
attains its global maximum in the interior point of
\Sigma
, we can find a strictly convex point in the interior by attaching a large sphere to
\Sigma
from above.

Let

\chi(x,t)=\min_{|\xi|=1}{h_{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}}.

Since
h_{ij}
is smooth, the function
\chi(x,t)
is Lipschitz continuous in space and by a cut-off function argument, we find a smooth function
\phi_{0}:M\to\mathbb{R}
such that
0\leq\phi_{0}\leq\chi(x,0)
and there exists an interior point
y
such that
\phi_{0}(y)>0
. We extend the function
\phi_{0}
to
\phi:M\times[0,\delta^{\prime})\to\mathbb{R}
by solving a linear parabolic PDE:

(A.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi&=\Delta\phi+\nabla_{V}\phi,\qquad\text{in}\quad M\times[0,\delta^{\prime}),\\
\nabla_{\mu}\phi&=0\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial M\times[0,\delta^{\prime}),\\
\phi(\cdot,0)&={\phi}_{0}(\cdot)\qquad\text{on}\quad M,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Delta
and
\nabla
are Laplacian operator and Levi-Civita connection with respect to the induced metric on
\Sigma_{t}=x(M,t)
of the flow (
A.1
). The solution
\phi
of (
A.2
) exists at least for a short time interval
[0,\delta^{\prime})
. By the strong maximum principle for scalar functions (see
[
Stahl1996-2
, Corollary 3.2]
), we have
\phi>0
for all
x\in M
and
t\in(0,\delta^{\prime})
.

We take
\tau=\frac{1}{2}\min\{\delta,\delta^{\prime}\}
and consider the tensor:

(A.3)

M_{ij}=h_{ij}-\phi g_{ij}

for the time interval
t\in[0,\tau)
. By the construction of
\phi_{0}
, we see that
M_{ij}\geq 0
at time
t=0
. We now apply the tensor maximum principle (i.e. Theorem
1.2
) to deduce that
M_{ij}\geq 0
is preserved along the flow (
A.1
).

By a direct computation using Proposition
3.1
for
\mathcal{F}=-H
, we have:

(A.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}M_{ij}=\Delta M_{ij}+\nabla_{V}M_{ij}+N_{ij},

where

\displaystyle N_{ij}=

\displaystyle|A|^{2}M_{ij}+|A|^{2}\phi g_{ij}-2HM_{i}^{k}M_{kj}

\displaystyle-2H\phi M_{ij}+M_{j}^{k}\nabla_{i}{V_{k}}+M_{i}^{k}\nabla_{j}{V_{k}}.

We easily see that whenever
M_{ij}\geq 0
and
M_{ij}\xi^{i}=0
at a point, we have

(A.5)

N_{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}=|A|^{2}\phi\geq 0,

and thus the condition (
1.11
) is satisfied. The boundary condition (
1.12
) can be checked similarly as in Theorem
5.2
, using Proposition
2.1
and the fact that

\nabla\mu H=\cot\theta h_{\mu\mu}H

on the boundary
\partial\Sigma_{t}
(see (
3.11
)). Hence Theorem
1.2
implies that
M_{ij}\geq 0
is preserved along the flow (
A.1
) for time
t\in[0,\tau)
, and it follows that
\Sigma_{t}
is strictly convex for time interval
(0,\tau)
.

To show the strict convexity for the whole time interval
(0,\delta)
, we fix a time
t_{0}\in(0,\tau)
. Since
\Sigma_{t}
is strictly convex at time
t_{0}
, then there exists a constant
\varepsilon>0
, such that
h_{ij}\geq\varepsilon g_{ij}
holds everywhere on
\Sigma_{t_{0}}
. A similar procedure as above can be used to show that
\tilde{M}_{ij}=h_{ij}-\varepsilon g_{ij}\geq 0
is preserved along the flow (
A.1
) for all time
t>t_{0}
as long as the flow (
A.1
) exists, which finishes the proof.

Variation of GIT and Variation of Lagrangian Skeletons I: Flip and Flop
Peng Zhou

Abstract
. Coherent-Constructible Correspondence for toric variety assigns to each n n -dimensional toric variety X Σ X_{\Sigma} a Lagrangian skeleton Λ Σ ⊂ T ∗ ​ T n \Lambda_{\Sigma}\subset T^{*}T^{n} , such that the derived category of coherent sheaves C ​ o ​ h ​ ( X Σ ) Coh(X_{\Sigma}) is equivalent to the (wrapped) constructible sheaves S ​ h w ​ ( T n , Λ Σ ) Sh^{w}(T^{n},\Lambda_{\Sigma}) . In this paper, we extend this correspondence, so that flip and flop between toric varieties corresponds to variation of Lagrangian skeletons. The main idea is to translate window subcategory in variation of GIT to a window skeleton.



Introduction

0.1.
Variation of Skeletons

Constructible sheaf has a nice balance between rigidity and flexibility. On one hand, to specify a constructible sheaf
F
on a manifold
M
with stratification
M=\sqcup_{\alpha}S_{\alpha}
, one only needs to specify locally constant sheaf on each strata and glue them together. On the other hand, constructible sheaves on
M
is equivalent to Lagrangians in
T^{*}M

[
NZ09
,
Nad09
,
GPS18a
]
. And all the flexibility of symplectic geometry, e.g. Hamiltonian isotopy, can be enjoyed by the constructible sheaves
[
GKS12
]
.

One manifestation of this flexibility is the existence of non-characteristic (NC) variation of skeleton, i.e, a family of Lagrangian skeleton
\Lambda_{t}\in T^{*}M
parameterized by
t
, such that the category of constructible sheaves with singular support in
\Lambda_{t}
, denoted as
Sh^{\diamondsuit}(M,\Lambda_{t})
, are invariant.

A natural source of NC variation of skeleton is isotopy of Weinstein pair
(W,\mathcal{H}_{t}\in\partial W)

[
GPS18b
,
Zho18
]
, where
W
is a Weinstein domain (here
W=D^{*}M
the unit disk bundle in
T^{*}M
),
\mathcal{H}_{t}
is a family of Weinstein hypersurfaces in the contact boundary
\partial W
, and the family of skeletons arises as

\Skel(W,\mathcal{H}_{t})=\Skel(W)\sqcup\\
R_{>0}\cdot(\Skel(\mathcal{H}_{t})),

where
\mathbb{R}_{>0}
acts by retracting Liouville flow.
However, in practice it is often hard to write down the skeleton of a Weinstein pair and its variations.

In this paper, we obtain a variation of skeleton not from symplectic geometry, but from a toric variation of GIT and coherent constructible correspondence (CCC). Here we only consider the simplest case of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and leave the general case for future work. There are two quotients toric smooth Deligne-Mumford stack
\mathcal{X}_{\pm}
, and correspondingly two skeletons
\Lambda_{\pm}\subset T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{N-1}
, where
\mathbb{T}=S^{1}
, with

\Coh(\mathcal{X}_{\pm})\simeq Sh^{w}(\mathbb{T}^{N-1},\Lambda_{\pm}),

(0.1)

Our goal is to find a variation of skeleton that interpolates
\Lambda_{\pm}
, then get the semi-orthogonal decomposition relation between
Coh(\mathcal{X}_{\pm})
. The answer comes naturally by translating the window subcategory in variation of GIT (VGIT) theory to Lagrangian skeleton.

Setup and Results

Let

0.3.
Related works

0.3.1.
Variation of Lagrangian Skeleton

Let
\Lambda\subset T^{*}M
be a conical Lagrangian, and assume
T_{M}^{*}M\subset\Lambda
. Let
\Lambda^{\infty}\subset T^{\infty}M
be the Legendrian in the contact infinity of
T^{*}M
. We speak of deformation of Legendrian
\Lambda^{\infty}
since
\Lambda^{\infty}
determines
\Lambda
.

The notion of non-characteristic variation of Lagrangian skeletons is introduced by Nadler
[
Nad15
]
. In loc.cit, he proves any Legendrian singularity admits a NC deformation to a ’standard’ (arboreal) singulairty, defined and studied in
[
Nad17
]
.

One sufficient and necessary condition for NC variation of skeleton, is the that the complement of the Legendrian at infinity remains invariant upto contactomorphism
[
GPS18b
,
Zho18
]
. Intuitively speaking, it is expected that, if the homotopy type of
\Lambda^{\infty}
is unchanged, and there is no birth or death of Reeb chords ending on
\Lambda^{\infty}
as it moves (for some fixed Reeb flow on
T^{\infty}M
), then the motion of
\Lambda^{\infty}
is non-characteristic. See also the recent work of Nadler-Shende on invariance of microlocal sheaf category under Weinstein isotopy
[
NS20
]
.

Deformation of Legendrians has been used to induce equivalences. Shende-Treumann-Williams-Zaslow have demonstrated beautifully how to understand cluster mutation from the point of view of Legendrian knot isotopy and the corresponding change of exact Lagrangian fillings
[
STW+19
,
STW16
]
.

Given a Laurent polynomial with the coefficient in some tropical limit, one can construct a Lagrangian skeleton for the zero loci of the polynomial
[
GS17
,
Zho20
]
. If we rotate the arguments of the coefficients at the vertex of the Newton polytope, then the corresponding skeleton undergoes a NC deformation as well
[
Zho18
,
Zho20
]
. As well known, such rotation of the argument corresponds convolution on the constructible sheaf side
[
FLTZ11
]
, and also corresponds to tensoring by line bundle on the toric variety side (see also
[
Han19
]
).

0.3.2.
Homological Minimal Model Program

The homological mirror symmetry for toric varieties has been understood from different point of views, from Abouzaid’s Lagrangian section
[
Abo09
,
Abo06
]
, through Fang-Liu-Treumann-Zaslow’s treatment through constructible sheaves
[
FLTZ11
,
FLTZ12
]
, to wrapped Fukaya categories or wrapped microlocal sheaves
[
Kuw16
]
.

There is an interesting refinement of homological mirror symmetry of toric variety, that not only matches the entire category of
Coh(X_{\Sigma})
to certain wrapped Fukaya category, but also matches certain semi-orthogonal decomposition of them. On the B-side (coherent side), it involves using a run of minimal model program on
X_{\Sigma}
to get a sequence of birational morphisms

X_{\Sigma}=X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{1}\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow X_{n}

as done by Kawamata
[
Kaw16
]
, or Ballard-Favero-Katzarkov
[
BFK19
]
. On the A-side, suppose we are given a Landau-Ginzburg model
W:(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, one may tropicalize
W
(by making a choice), and decompose the critical values of
W
into different sets, each lies approximately on a concentric circle
[
DKK16
]
. Conjecturally, the choices of the minimal model run on the B-side matches the choices of the tropicalization of
W
on the A-side, and the semi-orthogonal decompositions of
\Coh(X_{\Sigma})
matches that with the
\Fuk((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{n},W)
, and there has been progress towards it
[
BDF+15
,
Ker17
]
.

Here we are proposing a third player in the picture, the constructible sheaf category. Given a minimal model run
X_{0}\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow X_{n}
, we can use coherent-constructible correspondence
[
Bon06
,
FLTZ11
,
Kuw16
]
to get a sequence of Lagrangian skeleta
\Lambda_{0},\cdots,\Lambda_{n}
in
T^{*}T^{n}
, however it is not clear (as far as the author know) how to build functor between
Sh(T^{n},\Lambda_{i})
and
Sh(T^{n},\Lambda_{i+1})
except in the special case that
\Lambda_{i}\subset\Lambda_{i+1}
or
X_{i}
and
X_{i+1}
is related by blow-up. The more general flip / flop between
X_{i}
needs to be explained using variation of Lagrangian skeletons.

Our final goal is to build functors between
Sh(T^{n},\Lambda_{i})
and
Sh(T^{n},\Lambda_{i+1})
using a continuous family of skeletons interpolating from
\Lambda_{i}
to
\Lambda_{i+1}
. The general case will be studied in future work.

In the upcoming paper with Jesse Huang
[
HZ
]
, we will consider the quasi-symmetric toric VGIT setup, a special case of toric CY, and one obtain an interesting local system of categories over a higher dimensional base.

0.4.
Sketch of the Proof

Our main tool is the notion of variation of Lagrangian skeleton, which we now recall.

Let
M
be a real analytic compact manifold (we may relax the ’compact’ condition in practice),
B
a smooth manifold and
\pi:M\times B\to B
.
A
variation of Lagrangian skeleton
over
B
is a Lagrangian
\Lambda_{B}\subset T^{*}(M\times B)
containing the zero section, such that
1
1

1

Here we use notation that
T^{\infty}M=(T^{*}M\backslash T_{M}^{*}M)/\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the contact cosphere bundle at infinity, and
\Lambda^{\infty}=(\Lambda\backslash T_{M}^{*}M)/\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the (singular) Legendrian boundary of
\Lambda
.

\Lambda_{B}^{\infty}\cap T^{\infty}_{M\times\{b\}}(M\times B)=\emptyset,\quad\forall b\in B.

For
b\in B
, denote
M_{b}=\pi^{-1}(b)
and
\Lambda_{b}=\Lambda_{B}|_{M_{b}}
. If
Sh^{\diamondsuit}(M_{b},\Lambda_{b})
is invariant as we vary
b
, then this is called ’non-characteristic’ deformation by Nadler
[
Nad15
]
. In general, we are interested in how
Sh^{\diamondsuit}(M_{b},\Lambda_{b})
varies.

We study this problem locally in
M\times B
. We consider the sheaf of categories
Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda_{B}}
on
M\times B
, and define its singular support
SS(Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda_{B}})
. We also define the push-forward of
\pi_{*}Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda_{B}}
, then

SS(\pi_{*}Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda_{B}})\subset\pi_{*}(SS(Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda_{B}}))

where push-forward Lagrangian skeleton is by Lagrangian correspondence.

In general, given a skeleton
\Lambda\subset T^{*}Z
, we are interested in how to compute
SS(Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda})
from
\Lambda
. Let
V\subset U\subset Z
be nested open sets, he restriction functor

\rho_{U,V}:Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(U)\to Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(V)

is an equivalence if and only if
\rho_{U,V}
is fully-faithful and its left-adjoint
\rho_{U,V}^{L}
, i.e., the co-restriction functor is fully-faithful. Hence we defined two other singular supports
SS_{Hom}(\Lambda)
and
SS_{Hom}^{L}(\Lambda)
that measures the failure of fully-faithfulness. We have

SS(Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda})=SS_{Hom}^{L}(\Lambda)\cup SS_{Hom}(\Lambda).

In our case,
M\times B=\mathbb{T}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
, the variation of skeleton is
\Lambda_{W}\subset T^{*}(\mathbb{T}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R})
. Since the computation is local, we work in the universal cover
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{W}\subset T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The computation of
SS_{Hom}(\Lambda_{W})
boils down to an estimation of the singular support of the hom-sheaf.
A useful trick in the estimation is the following theorem.

Theorem 0.6

(Theorem
3.15
later)
.

Let
F,G
be constructible sheaves over
M
and
\{G_{t}\}_{t\in(0,\epsilon)}
a positive isotopy of
G
. Then

SS({\mathcal{H}om}(F,G))\subset\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}SS({\mathcal{H}om}(F,G_{t})).

In particular, if
SS^{\infty}(F)\cap SS^{\infty}(G_{t})=\emptyset
for
t\in(0,\epsilon)
then

SS({\mathcal{H}om}(F,G))\subset\limsup_{t\to 0^{+}}SS(F)^{a}+SS(G_{t}).

The computation of
SS_{Hom}^{L}(\Lambda_{W})
, in our case, is done by a straight-forward, if tedious, study on the skeleton and the generators of the sheaf category locally, i.e, the microlocal skyscrapers sheaves. The main result is that, if the window size is large enough, more precisely
|W|\geq\eta_{+}
, then
SS_{Hom}^{L}(\Lambda)
is the zero-section.

2.
Background

2.1.
Wrapped Microlocal Sheaves

We review the basic definitions and properties of wrapped microlocal sheaves, as introduced by Nadler in
[
Nad16
]
to mimic the definition of wrapped Fukaya categories. There is nothing original in this subsection, we only give an abridged account of loc.cit, section 1.1.

We fix
k=\mathbb{C}
, or any other characteristic zero field.

2.1.1.
Motivation

First we introduce the notion of Lagrangian skeleton of a Weinstein manifold or Weinstein pair. Since these heuristics are only for motivations, we will not give the precise definitions but refer the reader to the survey
[
Eli17
]
and references in there.

Let
(W,\omega=d\lambda)
be an exact symplectic manifold, with Liouville one-form
\lambda
and (retracting) Liouville vector field
X_{\lambda}
defined by
\iota_{X_{\lambda}}\omega=-\lambda
. Roughly speaking, if the Liouville vector field ’points inward’ near infinity and is gradient-like, then
W
is a Weinstein manifold. The skeleton
\Skel(W)
(or core) of a Weinstein manifold
W
is the union of the unstable manifolds for
X_{\lambda}
. Hence
\Skel(W)
is a deformation retract of
W
that is also compact and isotropic. We will only consider the case where the skeletons are Lagrangian.

A variant of Weinstein manifold is
Weinstein pair

(W,H)
, where
H
can be thought of as a Weinstein hypersurface in the contact boundary
\partial^{\infty}W
of
W
at infinity. Then
\Skel(W,H)
is the union of
\Skel(W)
and the flow-out of
\Skel(H)
under the retracting flow
X_{\lambda}
. We assume
\Skel(W,H)
is again a Lagrangian in
W
.

One may define the
(partially) wrapped Fukaya category

\mathcal{W}(W,H)
associated to the Weinstein pair
(W,H)
, where the objects are Lagrangian submanifolds, and morphisms
\Hom(L_{1},L_{2})
are computed by wrapping
L_{1}
positively near infinity.

Kontsevich outlined an approach to compute the category
\mathcal{W}(W,H)
as global section of a cosheaf of categories on the skeleton
L=\Skel(W,H)
. This idea is realized first in the sheaf-theoretic
approach in
[
Nad16
,
She17
]
as cosheaf of dg categories
\mu sh_{L}^{w}
of
wrapped microlocal sheaf
, then as wrapped Fukaya category of Liouville sectors in
[
GPS19
,
GPS18b
]
.

2.1.2.
Three categories of microlocal sheaves

Let
\Lambda\subset T^{*}Z
be a closed conical Lagrangian in a cotangent bundle of a real analytic manifold
Z
. To a conical open subset
\Omega\subset T^{*}Z
, we associate the following three dg categories.

(1)

\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)
: the category of
large
microlocal sheaves. It is a cocomplete dg category consists of microlocal sheaves with microstalks being arbitrary
k
-modules supported on
\Lambda
.

(2)

\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)
: the category of
wrapped
microlocal sheaves . By definition, this is the category of compact objects in
\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)
.

\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)=(\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega))_{c}.

(3)

\mu Sh_{\Lambda}(\Omega)
: the category of
traditional
microlocal sheaves . This is a full dg subcategory of
\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)
, consisting of objects whose microstalks are perfect
k
-modules (i.e. finite dimensional
k
-vector spaces) supported along
\Lambda
.

The wrapped microlocal sheaves
\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)
can recover the other two. The large microlocal sheaves is its ind-completion:

\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)=Ind(\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)).

The traditional microlocal sheaves is the category of exact functors on it valued in perfect modules:

\mu Sh_{\Lambda}(\Omega)=Fun^{ex}(\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)^{op},\Perf_{k}).

There is a distinguished set of wrapped microlocal sheaves, called
microlocal skyscrapers
, that co-represents the microstalk functors associated to smooth strata of
\Lambda\cap\Omega
. The collection of microlocal skyscrapers forms a compact generators of
\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)
, which split-generate
\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}(\Omega)

[
Nad16
]
[Lemma 1.4].

If
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}
are two conic open subsets in
T^{*}Z
, we have the restriction functor

\rho:\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega_{2})\to\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega_{1}).

\rho
has a left-adjoint
\rho^{L}
that preserves compact objects, we have then

\rho^{L}:\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega_{1})\to\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\Omega_{2}),\quad\rho^{w}:\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}(\Omega_{1})\to\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}(\Omega_{2}).

If
\Lambda_{1}\subset\Lambda_{2}
are two closed conic Lagrangians, then we have a fully-faithful embedding

i:\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda_{1}}(\Omega)\to\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda_{2}}(\Omega).

i
also has a left-adjoint that preserves compact objects, we have

i^{L}:\mu Sh^{L}_{\Lambda_{2}}(\Omega)\to\mu Sh^{L}_{\Lambda_{1}}(\Omega),\quad i^{w}:\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda_{2}}(\Omega)\to\mu Sh^{w}_{\Lambda_{1}}(\Omega).

The realization of microlocal sheaves are described in
[
Nad16
]
[Section 3]. Here we only use corresponding three versions for constructible sheaves,
Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}
,
Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}
and
Sh_{\Lambda}
. Given an open set
U\subset Z
, we have
Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(U)=\mu Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(\pi^{-1}(U))
, where
\pi:T^{*}Z\to Z
is the projection. All the statement about functors and left-adjoints applies here.

2.1.3.
Stalk Probe Sheaves

If
U\subset Z
is an open set, and
p\in U
, then the stalk functor
\phi_{p}:Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(U)\to Mod_{k}
is co-representable by an object
\mathcal{F}_{p}\in Sh^{w}_{\Lambda}(U)
, i.e.

\Hom(\mathcal{F}_{p},G)\simeq G_{p}
for all
\mathcal{G}\in Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(U).
. We call
\mathcal{F}_{p}
the
stalk probe sheaf
for point
p
in
Sh^{\diamondsuit}_{\Lambda}(U)
. This is an alias for Nadler’s microlocal skyscraper sheaf in the special case that the microstalk functor is a stalk functor.

In general, it is hard to explicitly describe the stalk probe sheaves. In this paper, we employ two methods to find the descriptions:

(1)

Geometric wrapping. We start from a sheaf
P_{0}=\mathbb{C}_{B}
supported on a small open ball around
p
, then we consider
positive isotopy

P_{t}
of
P_{0}
, such that

SS^{\infty}(P_{t})\cap\Lambda^{\infty}=\emptyset,\text{ and },\;SS^{\infty}(P_{1})\subset\Lambda^{\infty}.

Or more generally, an arbitrary isotopy
P_{t}
such that there exists a positive isotopy of
\Lambda
, satisfying
SS^{\infty}(P_{1})\subset\Lambda^{\infty}
and the
left non-characteristic (LNC)
condition

SS^{\infty}(P_{t})\cap\Lambda_{s}^{\infty}=\emptyset,\forall t\in[0,1],s\in(0,\epsilon).

(2)

Algebraic Constraint. If the stalk functor that we are interested in can be resolved using other stalks functors with known probe sheaves, then the probe sheaf that we are interested enjoys the same resolution. This is useful if we have know a closed embedding of skeleton
\Lambda_{1}\subset\Lambda_{2}
, and we know the probes for
\Lambda_{2}
and want to find probes for
\Lambda_{1}
.

Definition 3.1

Let

We also record the following results, where the window size is more general.

Proposition 8.7
.

Let
W=\{a,a+1,\cdots,b-1,b\}
be a window of size
|W|
. Let
\mathbb{C}^{*}
acts on
V=\mathbb{C}^{N}
and decompose
V=V_{+}\oplus V_{-}
according to the weights. Let
\eta_{\pm}
be the absolute values of weights of
\det(V_{\pm})
. Then

(1)

If
|W|\geq\eta_{-}
, then we have semi-orthogonal decomposition,

\mathcal{B}_{W}=\langle\mathcal{O}_{V_{+}}(a),\mathcal{O}_{V_{+}}(a+1),\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{V_{+}}(b-\eta_{-}),\underbrace{\langle\{\mathcal{O}_{V}(b-\eta_{-}+1),\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{V}(b)\}\rangle}_{Coh(\chi_{-})}\rangle.

(2)

If
|W|\geq\eta_{+}
, then we have semi-orthogonal decomposition

\mathcal{B}_{W}=\langle\mathcal{O}_{V_{-}}(b),\mathcal{O}_{V_{-}}(b-1),\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{V_{-}}(a+\eta_{+}),\underbrace{\langle\{\mathcal{O}_{V}(a+\eta_{+}-1),\cdots,\mathcal{O}_{V}(a)\}\rangle}_{Coh(\chi_{+})}\rangle.

Proof.

The proof for (1) is the same as above. For, we may consider a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
action on
V
with opposite weights, then the result follows from (1).
∎

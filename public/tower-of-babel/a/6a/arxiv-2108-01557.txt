Stable determination by a single measurement, scattering bound and regularity of transmission eigenfunction
Hongyu Liu, Chun-Hsiang Tsou

Abstract
. In this paper, we study an inverse problem of determining the cross section of an infinitely long cylindrical-like material structure from the transverse electromagnetic scattering measurement. We establish a sharp logarithmic stability result in determining a polygonal scatterer by a single far-field measurement. The argument in establishing the stability result is localised around a corner and can be as well used to produce two highly intriguing implications for invisibility and transmission resonance in the wave scattering theory. In fact, we show that if a generic medium scatterer possesses an admissible corner on its support, then there exists a positive lower bound of the L 2 L^{2} -norm of the associated far-field pattern. For the transmission resonance, we discover a quantitative connection between the regularity of the transmission eigenfunction at a corner and its analytic or Fourier extension. Keywords: Inverse medium scattering, single far-field pattern, logarithmic stability, corner singularity, invisibility, transmission resonance. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35R30, 78A46, 35Q60, 35P25,



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Mathematical setup and physical background

Initially focusing on the mathematics but not the physics, we introduce the scattering system for our study. Let
k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
be a wavenumber and
u^{i}
be an entire solution to the Helmholtz equation,

(1.1)

\Delta u^{i}+k^{2}u^{i}=0\quad\mbox{in}\ \ \mathbb{R}^{2}.

Let
\varepsilon,\mu\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be real-valued functions such that
\mbox{supp}(\varepsilon-1),\mbox{supp}(\mu-1)\subset D
and
\varepsilon,\mu\geq c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where
D
is a bounded Lipschitz domain such that
\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\overline{D}
is connected. Consider the following scattering system for
u=u^{i}+u^{s}\in H_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
:

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\mbox{div}(\varepsilon^{-1}\nabla u)+k^{2}\mu u=0&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
r^{1/2}(\partial_{r}-\mathrm{i}k)(u-u^{i})\rightarrow 0&\mbox{while }r:=|x|\rightarrow\infty,\end{cases}

where
\mathrm{i}:=\sqrt{-1}
.

In the physical context, (
1.2
) describes the transverse magnetic scattering due to the impingement of an incident field
u^{i}
on an infinitely long cylindrical material structure whose cross section is
D
; see e.g.
[
36
]
for more detailed discussion. Here,
\varepsilon
and
\mu
are respectively the electric permittivity and magnetic permeability, which characterize the medium parameters. The medium outside the material structure is uniformly homogeneous.
u^{s}
signifies the perturbation of the incident field due to the presence of the inhomogeneous medium and is referred to as the scattered field. The last limit in (
1.2
) is called the Sommerfeld radiation condition which characterizes the outgoing nature of the scattered field. The well-posedness of the scattering system (
1.2
) is known (cf.
[
44
,
51
]
), and moreover the following asymptotic expansion holds (cf.
[
18
]
):

(1.3)

u(x)=u^{i}(x)+\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k|x|}}{|x|^{1/2}}u_{\infty}(\hat{x};u^{i})+\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-1})\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ |x|\rightarrow+\infty,

which holds uniformly in the angular variable
\hat{x}:=x/|x|\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. The function
u_{\infty}:\mathbb{S}^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called the
far-field pattern
to the scattering problem.

It is noted that the transverse electric scattering can be described similarly with the first equation in (
1.2
) replaced by
{\rm div\;}(\mu^{-}\nabla u)+k^{2}\varepsilon u=0
. In order to unify our discussion, we introduce the following scattering system

(1.4)

\begin{cases}\mbox{div}(\sigma\nabla u)+k^{2}qu=0&\mbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
r^{1/2}(\partial_{r}-\mathrm{i}k)(u-u^{i})\rightarrow 0&\mbox{while }r\rightarrow\infty,\end{cases}

where
(\sigma,q)
may be either
(\varepsilon^{-1},\mu)
or
(\mu^{-1},\varepsilon)
. The inverse scattering problem that we are concerned with associated with the scattering system (
1.4
) in this article is to recover
D
, independent of
\sigma
and
q
, by knowledge of
u^{\infty}
corresponding a single incident field
u^{i}
, namely

(1.5)

u^{\infty}\ \mbox{corresponding to a fixed }u^{i}\longrightarrow D,\ \mbox{independent of }\sigma\mbox{ and }q.

1.2.
Summary of main results and connection to existing studies

The inverse problem (
1.5
) is a fundamental one in the inverse scattering theory for electromagnetic waves, and there are rich results in the literature (cf.
[
18
,
49
,
55
]
). However, the determination by a single far-field measurement in the general case still remains to be an open problem; see
[
40
]
and the references cited therein for more relevant discussions. For the convenience of the readers to have a global picture of our result, we briefly summarize the major discovery in this article in what follows.

Suppose that
\sigma
is of the form:

(1.6)

\sigma:=1+(\gamma-1)\chi_{D}

where
\gamma\neq 1
is a positive constant and
q\in L^{\infty}
with
\mbox{supp}(q-1)\subset D
. We write
(D;\gamma,q)
to signify such a medium scatterer. The main stability result established in this paper for the inverse problem (
1.5
) can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(D;\gamma,q)
and
(D^{\prime};\gamma^{\prime},q^{\prime})
be two medium scatterers as described above, where
D
and
D^{\prime}
are assumed to be convex polygons in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
u
and
u^{\prime}
the corresponding solutions to the scattering system (
1.4
) associated with
(D;\gamma,q)
and
(D^{\prime};\lambda^{\prime},q^{\prime})
respectively. Suppose that the solutions
u
and
u^{\prime}
as well as the incident field
u^{i}
satisfy the admissible assumptions, which will be detailed in Theorem
2.5
. Denote by
u_{\infty}
and by
u^{\prime}_{\infty}
the far-filed patterns respectively, and suppose that

(1.7)

\|u_{\infty}-u^{\prime}_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\varepsilon,

where
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\varepsilon\ll 1
.
Then there exist constants
C,\beta>0
which depend only on the a-priori parameters such that when
\varepsilon>0
is sufficiently small,

(1.8)

d_{\mathcal{H}}(D,D^{\prime})\leq C(\ln|\ln\varepsilon|)^{-\beta}.

Here

, defined later by (

), signifies the Hausdorff distance between two domains.

It is known that stability estimates of logarithmic type are generically optimal for inverse shape problems (cf.

The mathematical strategy developed in this article is partly inspired from the method used in
[
7
]
. It begins with an integral identity, which can be established by applying Green’s formula on a neighborhood of the scatterer. The second step is to construct a suitable family of functions, which are referred to as the Complex-Geometrical-Optics (CGO) solutions. The third step is to estimate the two sides of the integral identity in order to give an estimation of the geometrical parameters in terms of the estimation of the solutions near the scatterer. We shall use the quantitative estimation of the unique continuation property
[
1

to give an estimation of solutions near the scatterer in terms of the far-field patterns. We finally shall choose delicately the right CGO solution to derive our main stability result. It is particularly noted that there is a challenging point comes from the discontinuity of the leading-order coefficient (cf. (
1.6
)). To overcome this difficulty, we shall make use of the corner singularity decomposition theory (see Section
2
for more details). It is noted that the corner singularity in three dimensions can be much more complicated, which include both the edge and the vertex singularities. This makes the singular behaviours of the solutions to elliptic PDEs radically more complicated. This is also the main reason that we are confined within the two dimensions in the current study. It is also the technical reason that we assume that
\gamma

) is constant, since otherwise the induced singularity in the solution due to the singularity of the coefficient coupled with the geometric singularity can be highly complicated. That is, as long as the singularity decomposition theory is well understood, our study can be extended to more general scenarios in high dimensions with variable coefficients. Nevertheless, as can be seen from our subsequent analysis that in principle, we only make use of the fact that
\gamma
is a constant in a neighbourhood of any corner on
\partial D
and it can be variable in the rest part of
D
. However, in order to ease the exposition, we assume that
\gamma
is constant in the whole domain. Here, we would also like to mention in passing some related studies for the so-called Calderon’s inverse problem
[

,

]
and inverse obstacle problems
[

We make a novel discovery on a quantitative relation between the regularity of the transmission eigenfunctions at a corner and their analytic or Fourier extensions. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section

, we first discuss the corner singularity decomposition, which is a critical ingredient in our study. Then we introduce some admissibility assumptions on the geometric setup as well as the incident fields. In Section

2.
Geometrical setup and statement of the main results

2.1.
Corner singularity decomposition

One of the key ingredients to derive the stability theorem is the local decomposition of solutions to transmission problems in a neighborhood of each polygonal vertex. This is known from the pioneering works of Kondrat’ev
[
34
]
, Grisvard
[
32
]
and from Kozlov, Maz’ya, Rossemann
[
35
]
that the solution to a boundary value problem
\Delta u=f
in a domain with a polygonal corner can be written as the sum of a regular part and a singular part. The singular part is composed of a discrete family of special functions which are explicit in terms of the underlying geometrical parameters. This result is extended to transmission problems, and we refer to Kellogg
[
33
]
, Dauge and Nicaise
[
20
,
52
]
. For the boundary value problem associated with the Helmholtz equation system, the same decomposition as the Laplacian case holds
[
17
]
. The transmission problems for the Helmholtz systems are also studied by Costabel
[
19
]
in a framework of the layer potential techniques. We summarize those results into the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1

(Local decomposition of solutions to transmission problems)
.

Let
u\in H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be the solution to (
1.4
) with
\sigma
satisfying (
1.6
), in which
D
represents a convex polygon. We assume also that
u
satisfies the asymptotic behavior (
1.3
) at infinity with a nonzero incident field
u^{i}\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{D}
the set of vertices of
D
. Here the variables
r
,
\theta
are related to the polar coordinates in the neighborhood of each vertex. Then the following decomposition holds,

(2.1)

u=u_{reg}+\sum_{x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{D}}K_{i}r^{\eta_{i}}\phi_{i}(\theta)\zeta_{i}.

We have also the following properties:

(1)

Let
B_{R}
be the central ball with a radius
R>0
such that
D\Subset B_{R}
.
u_{reg}|_{D}\in H^{2}(D)
,
u_{reg}|_{B_{R}\setminus\overline{D}}\in H^{2}(B_{R}\setminus\overline{D})
, and
u_{reg}
is continuous across
\partial D
.

(2)

There holds

(2.2)

\|u_{reg}\|_{H^{2}(D)}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{1}(B_{R})},

where the constant
C
is independent of the shape of
D
. The same regularity estimate holds for
u_{reg}
in
B_{R}\setminus\overline{D}
.

(3)

There exists explicit formulas to calculate the coefficients
K_{i}
.

(4)

The exponent
\eta_{i}\in(0,1)
satisfies the following equation

(2.3)

\left(\frac{\sin\eta_{i}(\pi-a_{i})}{\sin\eta_{i}\pi}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{\gamma+1}{\gamma-1}\right)^{2},

where
a_{i}
stands for the opening angle at each vertex
x_{i}
.

(5)

The functions
\phi_{i}
admit the following form:
\phi_{i}(\theta)=\cos(\eta_{i}\theta+\Phi_{i,\pm})
, where the phase shits
\Phi_{i,\pm}
are constants determined by the transmission conditions.

(6)

\zeta_{i}
is a smooth cut-off function such that
\zeta_{i}(r)=1
if
r\leq\varrho_{i}
and
\zeta_{i}(r)=0
if
r\geq 2\varrho_{i}
. The radius
\varrho_{i}
is chosen such that the disks
(B_{2\varrho_{i}}(x_{i}))_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{D}}
do not intersect each other.

Proof.

The solution to equation (
1.4
) in the ball
B_{R}
can be rewritten as the solution to the following system,

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\mbox{div}(\sigma\nabla u)=\hat{f}&\mbox{in }B_{R},\\
u=\hat{g}&\mbox{on }\partial B_{R},\end{cases}

where
\hat{f}=-k^{2}qu\in L^{2}(B_{R})
,
\hat{g}=u|_{\partial B_{R}}\in H^{1/2}(B_{R})
.

Then it follows from Theorem 1.6 in
[
52
]
that the solution to the transmission problem (
2.4
) admits the decomposition (
2.1
). The equation (
2.3
) to determine the exponents
\eta_{i}
can be obtained by following similar calculations as those in
[
21
]
. From the equation (4.3bis) in
[
52
]
, we also have an estimation of the regular part
u_{reg}
,

(2.5)

\|u_{reg}\|_{H^{2}(D)}\leq C(\|\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}(B_{R})}+\|\hat{g}\|_{H^{1/2}(\partial B_{R})}),

with a constant
C
independent of the shape of
D
. It follows then from the trace operator on
\partial B_{R}
that the estimate (
2.2
) holds. Moreover, the coefficients
K_{i}
can be explicitly calculated by the formula
(1.24) in
[
52
]

∎

2.2.
Admissibility assumptions

Before announcing our main stability result, we introduce some admissibility conditions to clarify the framework of our study.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
D
be a convex polygon in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\gamma>0
and
q\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We say that
(D;\gamma,q)
belongs to the admissible class
\mathcal{D}
if the following conditions are fulfilled:

(1)

D
is a convex polygon and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
\gamma\neq 1
and
\gamma\in(\gamma_{m},\gamma_{M})
, where
\gamma_{m}
and
\gamma_{M}
are two positive constants;

(2)

D\Subset B_{R}
, where
B_{R}
stands for the central ball at origin with a radius
R>0
;

(3)

There exist
0<a_{m}<a_{M}<\pi
such that the opening of the angle at each vertex of
D
is in
(a_{m},a_{M})
;

(4)

The length of each edge of
D
is at least
l>0
;

(5)

The support of
q-1
is contained in the polygon
D
, which means
q\equiv 1
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{D}
;

(6)

\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\mathcal{Q}
where
\mathcal{Q}>0
is a constant.

Remark 2.3
.

In fact, the inhomogeneity on both
\sigma
and
q
could generate the scattering waves. It is shown in
[
7
]
that the support of
q-1
can be stably determined from a single far-field pattern under the assumption
\sigma\equiv 1
. In principle, it is possible to stably recover both the supports of
\sigma
and
q-1
by using in parallel the method introduced in this paper and the one in
[
7
]
. However, if we do so, the technical details will become more complicated and distract the meaning of introducing our new method. In order to have a focusing theme of our study, we are only concerned with the recovery of the support of
\sigma
in this paper. This is the main reason for us introducing item
(5)
in Definition
2.2
.

On the other hand, we shall impose a genetic condition on the incident field
u^{i}
such that the singular behaviors at each corner are guaranteed.

Assumption A
.

Let
u^{i}\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be an entire solution to (
1.1
). We denote by
S>0
the
amplitude
of the incident wave
u^{i}
, which is defined by

(2.6)

\|u^{i}\|_{H^{2}(B_{2R})}\leq S,

with
R>0
introduced in Definition
2.2
. We assume in the rest of this paper that for all
(D;\gamma,q)\in\mathcal{D}
, the corresponding solution
u
to the scattering problem (
1.4
)–(
1.3
) admits a nondegenerate singularity coefficient
K_{i}>0
in the decomposition (
2.1
) at each vertex
x_{i}
of
D
.

Remark 2.4
.

It is pointed out that (
2.6
) is a generic condition, which can be easily fulfilled, say e.g. by the plane wave of the form
\exp\{\mathrm{i}kx\cdot d\}
,
d\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

As we mentioned previously in Theorem
2.1
, the coefficients
K_{i}
in (
2.1
) can be determined by explicit formulas. The formulas can be found in
[
20
,
52
]
. In fact, each
K_{i}
depends linearly on the incident field
u^{i}
for fixed polygon
D
and vertex
x_{i}
. Assumption
A
is in fact a generic condition except for the case when
K_{i}=0
, which means the solution to (
1.4
) has
H^{2}
-regularity in a neighborhood of
x_{i}
, corresponding to a very limited class of scenarios from the practical point of view.

In what follows, for
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
, we define

(2.7)

d_{\mathcal{H}}(D,D^{\prime})=\max\big(\sup_{x\in D}\mathrm{dist}(x,D^{\prime}),\sup_{x^{\prime}\in D^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}(x^{\prime},D)\big),

to be Hausdorff distance between
D
and
D^{\prime}
. In order to simplify the expressions, we shall call the following parameters
(k,R,\gamma_{m},\gamma_{M},a_{m},a_{M},l,\mathcal{Q})
as the
a-priori
parameters. With those admissible assumptions and
a-priori
parameters, we are in a position to complete the statement of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
u^{i}\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a nontrivial solution to the Helmholtz equation (
1.1
). Let
(D;\gamma,q)
and
(D^{\prime};\gamma^{\prime},q^{\prime})
belong in the admissible class
\mathcal{D}
in Definition
2.2
. We consider the solutions
u,u^{\prime}\in H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to the Helmholtz equation (
1.4
) where the corresponding functions
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
are defined by (
1.6
). Suppose that
u^{i}
satisfies Assumption
A
. We define
\displaystyle K_{m}:=\min_{x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{D}\cup\mathcal{S}_{D^{\prime}}}|K_{i}|>0
as the smallest singularity coefficient among the vertices of
D
and
D^{\prime}
.

Assume that there holds

(2.8)

\|u_{\infty}-u^{\prime}_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\varepsilon.

Then there exist constants
C,\beta,\widetilde{\beta}>0
which depend only on the a-priori parameters such that when
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is sufficiently small, one has

(2.9)

3.
Propagation of smallness

The proof of Theorem
2.5
involves several technical ingredients. The objective of this section is to estimate the difference
u-u^{\prime}
of the solutions to the scattering problems near the polygonal scatterer
D
. The first step in our method is to estimate
u-u^{\prime}
in terms of the difference on their far-field patterns
u_{\infty}-u^{\prime}_{\infty}
in a near-field domain. The second step is to estimate
u-u^{\prime}
and its derivative
\nabla u-\nabla u^{\prime}
in a neighborhood of the polygonal scatterer
D
from the near-field estimation. Combining those results, we derive the estimations of
u-u^{\prime}
and
\nabla u-\nabla u^{\prime}
in terms of
u_{\infty}-u^{\prime}_{\infty}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
w^{s}\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a solution to (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{R}
and satisfy the Sommerfeld radiation condition (
1.3
) at infinity. We denote by
w^{s}_{\infty}
its far-field pattern and
\varepsilon=\|w^{s}_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}
.

We assume the a-priori bound
\|w^{s}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\setminus B_{R})}\leq\mathcal{S}
with
\mathcal{S}\geq 0
depending on the a-priori parameters. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a domain such that
\mathcal{A}\subset B_{2R}\setminus\overline{B_{R}}
. Then, for any smoothness index
p\in\mathbb{N}
there exists constants
c,C>0
depending only on
k,p,R,\mathcal{A}
such that

(3.1)

\|w^{s}\|_{H^{p}(\mathcal{A})}\leq C\max(\varepsilon,\mathcal{S}e^{-c\sqrt{\ln(\mathcal{S}/\varepsilon)}}).

Proof.

See Proposition 5.2 and Corollary 5.3 in
[
7
]
.
∎

We next present the propagation of smallness from a near field domain up to the boundary of the polygonal scatterer. The method we use here is mainly inspired by the one in
[
7
]
, and we shall adopt similar notations therein.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
Q\Subset B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a convex polygon,
x_{c}\in\partial Q
be a vertex of
Q
,
P\in\mathbb{N}
,
0<\alpha<1
and
w\in H^{1}(B_{2R}\setminus\overline{Q})
. We assume that
w
is a solution to (
1.1
) in
B_{2R}\setminus\overline{Q}
and the function
\widetilde{w_{P}}:x\mapsto|x-x_{c}|^{P}w(x)
belongs to the class
\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
in
B_{2R}\setminus\overline{Q}
with a norm at most
T\geq 1
.

We assume furthermore that
|w(x)|\leq\delta
in
B_{7R/4}\setminus\overline{B_{5R/4}}
. If

(3.2)

\delta\leq\delta_{m}=\left[\exp\exp\left(\frac{9R|\ln c_{2}|}{(1-\alpha)\min(R_{m},2R/5)}\right)\right]^{-1},

then there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
depending only on
k
,
\alpha
,
P
and
R
such that,

(3.3)

|w(x)|\leq\frac{C_{0}T}{\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial Q)^{P}}(\ln|\ln\delta|)^{-\alpha},

for
x\in B_{3R/2}\setminus\overline{Q}
. Here
R_{m}
and
c_{2}
are constants, which depend only on
k
and are given respectively in Lemmas 5.4 and 5.5 in
[
7
]
.

Proof.

5.
Implications to invisibility and transmission eigenvalue problem

In this section, we present two interesting implications to the wave scattering theory which are byproducts of our stability study. Let us consider the scattering problem (
1.4
) and (
1.3
). It is said that invisibility occurs if

(5.1)

u_{\infty}\equiv 0.

The first byproduct we shall establish is to show that if a general scattering medium possesses a corner on its support, then the
L^{2}
-energy of the corresponding far-field pattern possesses a positive lower bound. That is, a corner scatters stably and invisibility cannot be achieved. In fact, we have

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
D
be a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, not necessarily a convex polygon. We assume that
\partial D
admits a convex polygonal point, i.e. there exists

. Let

,

and

. We consider the scattering problem (
1.4
) where
\sigma
is defined by (

1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We suppose the following conditions are fulfilled:

(1)

for some

;

(2)

;

(3)

0<\gamma_{m}\leq\gamma\leq\gamma_{M}

(4)

\mathrm{supp}(q-1)\Subset B_{R}
;

(5)

\mathrm{supp}(q-1)\cap B_{l}(x_{c})\subset D\cap B_{l}(x_{c})

\|q\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\mathcal{Q}

(7)

\|u^{i}\|_{H^{2}(B_{2R})}\leq S

(8)

The solution

Then there exists a constant

,

(5.2)

\|u_{\infty}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\geq\frac{S}{\exp\exp\left(C(1+\frac{S}{|K|})^{\frac{2}{\eta(1-\eta)}}\right)}.

Proof.

The proof follows from similar arguments in the proof of Theorem

with necessary modifications. We take

,

Next, we consider the implication to the transmission eigenvalue problem. For the scattering problem (
1.4
), if invisibility occurs, namely (
5.1
) holds, one can directly verify by setting
v=u^{i}|_{D}
that

(5.7)

\begin{cases}\mbox{div}(\sigma\nabla u)+k^{2}qu=0&\text{in }D,\\
\Delta v+k^{2}v=0&\text{in }D,\\
u=v,\quad\sigma\partial_{\nu}u=\partial_{\nu}v&\text{on }\partial D.\end{cases}

On the other hand, if there exists nontrivial solutions
u\in H^{1}(D)
and
v\in H^{1}(D)
to (
5.7
), then
k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is called a transmission eigenvalue and
u,v
are the associated transmission eigenfunctions (cf.
[
10
]
). Let
v
be transmission eigenfunction associated with
k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then the following so-called Herglotz approximation holds, which represents a certain Fourier extension property (cf.
[
22
,
25
]
). For any
\varepsilon\ll 1
, there exists
g_{\varepsilon}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
such that (cf.
[
56
]
)

(5.8)

\|v_{g_{\varepsilon}}-v\|_{H^{1}(D)}\leq\varepsilon,\ \ v_{g_{\varepsilon}}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}e^{\mathrm{i}kx\cdot d}g_{\varepsilon}(d)\,ds(d),

where
v_{g_{\varepsilon}}
is referred to as a Herglotz wave function associated with the kernel density
g_{\varepsilon}
and is an entire solution to the Helmholtz equation (
1.1
).

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(D,\gamma,q)
be a convex polygonal scatter satisfying the assumptions in Theorem
2.5
. We consider the transmission eigenvalue problem (
5.7
) with nontrivial eigenfunctions
u,v\in H^{1}(D)
with
\|v\|_{H^{1}(D)}=1
. Then it holds that:

(1)

If
v
can be extended outside
D
to be an entire solution to (
1.1
), then
u\in H^{2}(D)
.

(2)

Let
(v_{g_{\varepsilon}})_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of Herglotz wave functions that are given in (
5.8
) and approximate the transmission eigenfunction
v
. There exist positive constants
A
and
b
which are independent of
g_{\varepsilon}
such that for any given function
\psi:\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying
\psi(\varepsilon)\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, if
\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\leq\psi(\varepsilon)\left(\ln\ln\frac{A}{\varepsilon}\right)^{b}
, then it holds that

(5.9)

\lim_{x\neq x^{\prime}\in D,x,x^{\prime}\rightarrow x_{c}}\frac{|u(x)-u(x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\eta}}=0

at each vertex
x_{c}
of
D
, where
\eta
is given by (
2.3
).

Remark 5.3
.

The Herglotz approximation property (
5.8
) can be regarded as a certain Fourier extension of the transmission eigenfunction
v
; see
[
22
,
25
]
for more related discussion. On the other hand, if
v
can be extended to an entire solution to (
1.1
), it is clear that
v
is analytic in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Hence, intriguingly, Theorem
5.2
connects the local regularity of the transmission eigenfunction
u
around a corner to the analytic or Fourier extension property of the transmission eigenfunction
v
.

Note that by the standard Sobolev embedding,
u\in H^{2}(D)
implies that
u
is Hölder continuous up to the boundary. Hence, the first result in the theorem indicates that if
u
is not Hölder continuous up to a corner vertex, then
v
cannot be analytically extended across the corner. Moreover, it further indicates that if
v
is approximated by the Herglotz sequence
v_{g_{\varepsilon}}
, then
\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}
must be unbounded, since otherwise
v_{g_{\varepsilon}}
converges, by passing to a subsequence if necessary, to an entire solution to (
1.1
), say
v_{0}
, which is an analytic extension of
v
into
\mathbb{R}^{2}
; see also
[
4
,
11
]
for more relevant discussion about this point.

The second result in Theorem
5.2
quantitatively reinforces the above assertion about the unboundedness of the Herglotz kernels
\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}
if the transmission eigenfunction
u
is not Hölder continuous up to any corner vertex of the domain
D
. To illustrate this point, let us take
\psi(\varepsilon)=\left(\ln\ln\frac{A}{\varepsilon}\right)^{-b/2}
in item (2) in the theorem. Then if
u
is not
C^{\eta}
up to a corner vertex, then one clearly has
\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})}\geq\psi(\varepsilon)\left(\ln\ln\frac{A}{\varepsilon}\right)^{b/2}\rightarrow+\infty
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow+0
.

It is also interesting to point out that Theorem
5.2
corroborates the studies in
[
22
,
25
]
, which make use of a similar Herglotz approximation property as a regularity criterion in a certain different setup from the current article.

Finally, we would like to point out that Theorem
5.2
still holds true for the case that
D
is a generic domain but possess an admissible corner. In fact, as point out earlier, our argument can be localised around the corner and is irrelevant to the rest part of
\partial D
. Nevertheless, in order to ease the exposition, we only consider the case that

is a convex polygon.

We proceed to prove the claim (2). Let

Let

and the far-field pattern of

approximates that of

. We apply again the standard scattering theory, there exists a constant

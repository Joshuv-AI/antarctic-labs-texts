On Computationally Efficient Subsystems of Propositional Logic
Inga Lev

Abstract
In this paper, we show that the derivability problem for the primal propositional logic remains solvable in polynomial time upon adding a certain form of the principle of equivalent form substitution; and that, upon adding another form of this principle, it becomes co-NP-hard.



Contents

1
Introduction

There are numerous real-world tasks that include determining whether a given formula is derivable
from given assumptions in a certain logic. One possible area is access control; the task of determining
whether certain access is to be granted based on some prior knowledge could be reduced to the task of
determining whether the proposition “Access should be granted” is derivable from the corresponding
assumptions.

Obviously, for such a system to be usable, the corresponding logic should conform to certain
desirable requirements, and the derivability problem should be reasonably easy (when expressed in
terms of the sequent length).

It is known that the derivability problem for classical propositional logic is co-NP-complete; and,
by a result of Statman
[
St
]
, intuitionistic logic is PSPACE-complete. Additionally, it is easy to
prove that any intermediate logic is co-NP-hard.

Y. Gurevich and I. Neeman proved, in the context of their research into infon logic and Distributed
Knowledge Authorization Language, that the derivability problem is solvable in polynomial time for
a certain subsystem of disjunction-free minimal logic, called
primal logic
(
PL
), which is, in a certain
sense, the smallest useful logic.

However,
PL
is so limited that it lacks certain properties one expects from the real-world logic. In
particular, it does not conform to the
principle of equivalent formula substitution
; that is, it is possible
to turn a derivable formula into a non-derivable one or vice versa by changing one of its subformulas
for an equivalent one (we consider two formulas
\phi
and
\psi
to be equivalent if they are derivable from
each other,
\phi\vdash\psi
, and
\psi\vdash\phi
).

The main objective of this paper is to show that, for every logic that extends
PL
and conforms to
the strong form of principle of equivalent formula substitution, the derivability problem is co-NP-hard;
yet that there is a logic that extends
PL
and conforms to the week form of principle of equivalent
formula substitution, for which the derivability problem is solvable in cubic time. It is achieved by
showing that
PL
with the strong form of principle of equivalent formula substitution could be used to
solve the derivability problem for classical logic, while
PL
itself could be used to solve the derivability
problem for
PL
with the weak form of this principle.

Additionally, we will show that the smallest logic containing the strong form of the principle of
equivalent substitution is different from minimal logic.

This work was partially done during my study at the Faculty of Mathematics, Higher School of Economics, Moscow, Russia.
I am grateful to my supervisor Lev Beklemishev for suggesting me to work on this problem and for his guidance and advice.

2.2
Equivalent sequents

Definition 2.1
.

We say that

Additionally, we say that

Note that both forms of equivalence relation are reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. That is,
\phi\sim_{\Gamma}\phi

2.3
Polynomial-time locality

A logic is said to have the
polynomial-time locality
property, if there is a polynomial-time algorithm
which, given the theorem
\sigma
, constructs a set of formulas
\phi_{i}
such that there exists a proof of
\sigma
in the
logic concerned such that every step of the proof contains only the
\phi_{i}
formulas.

Related to this concept is the
subformula
property. A logic is said to have the subformula property
if for any theorem
\sigma
there exists a proof such that every sequent of it contains only the subformulas
of
\sigma
.

The subformula property implies the polynomial-time locality property, as any sequent
\sigma
of the
length
n
has at most
n
subformulas, which all could be obtained in a linear time.

As noted in
[
GN
]
and
[
BG
]
,
ML
and
\texttt{ML}\left[\lor\right]
have the subformula property.

2.4
Ordering of logics

Definition 2.2
.

The inference rule

We say that

Note that this implies that all theorems in

Obviously, if logic

.

We say that logics

), if

is then equal to the set of all theorems in

We say that

B
.

2.5
Complexity of logic

We define the complexity of logic as the complexity of the algorithmic problem to decide whether a given sequent is a theorem in the logic concerned or not.

For example, we say that the complexity of the logic
A
is polynomial-time, if there are number

and

such that, given a sequent
\sigma
of the length
n

F
terminates after no more than

2.6
Compatibility

We say that, in context of the logic

If

The principle of equivalent formula substitution

The
principle of compositionality
states that
‘‘the meaning of a complex expression is fully determined
by its structure and the meanings of its constituents’’
(
[
SEP
]
).

This property is, understandably, desirable in the logics used in the real world.

We will study a similar property in this paper:

Definition 3.1
.

The logic

\texttt{E}_{F}

The weak form of this principle is defined by using the following family of inference rules instead:

\texttt{E}_{0,F}

F{\left[x_{0}/\phi\right]}\vdash F{\left[x_{0}/\psi\right]}\lx@proof@logical@and\phi\vdash\psi\quad\psi\vdash\phi
,

That is, if two formulas are equivalent, then they yield equivalent formulas when substituted in
any expression.

Lemma 5.3
.

\texttt{PEL}1_{0},\texttt{PEL}2_{0}\not=\texttt{PL}

Proof.

First, note that

6.1
The complexity of CL

Lemma 6.1
.

\texttt{CL}\left[\lor\right]
is reducible to
CL
by a polynomial-time mapping.

Proof.

Let us define the mapping
t
on both formulas and sequents as follows:

•

t{\left(x\right)}=x
, where
x
is a variable or constant

•

t{\left(\phi\land\psi\right)}={\left(t{\left(\phi\right)}\land t{\left(\psi\right)}\right)}

•

t{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}={\left({\left(t{\left(\phi\right)}\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(t{\left(\psi\right)}\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)}

•

t{\left(\phi\to\psi\right)}={\left(t{\left(\phi\right)}\to t{\left(\psi\right)}\right)}

•

t{\left(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}\vdash\psi\right)}=t{\left(\phi_{1}\right)},\ldots,t{\left(\phi_{n}\right)}\vdash t{\left(\psi\right)}

Note that
t
is idempotent.

We will first prove that
{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}\sim{\left({\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)}
in
\texttt{CL}\left[\lor\right]
. We get:

{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)},{\left(\phi\to\bot\right)},{\left(\psi\to\bot\right)},\phi\vdash\bot\quad{\left(\texttt{x2x},\to\texttt{E}\right)}

{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)},{\left(\phi\to\bot\right)},{\left(\psi\to\bot\right)},\psi\vdash\bot\quad{\left(\texttt{x2x},\to\texttt{E}\right)}

{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)},{\left(\phi\to\bot\right)},{\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\vdash\bot\quad{\left(\lor\texttt{I}\right)}

{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)},{\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\vdash{\left({\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\quad{\left(\to I\right)}

{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}\vdash{\left({\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)}\quad{\left(\to I\right)}

On the other hand, we get:

{\left({\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)},{\left(\phi\to\bot\right)},{\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\vdash\bot\quad{\left(\texttt{x2x},\to\texttt{E}\right)}

{\left({\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)},{\left(\phi\to\bot\right)},{\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\vdash{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}\quad{\left(\texttt{IL},\to\texttt{Cut}\right)}

{\left({\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)},{\left(\phi\to\bot\right)},\psi\vdash{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}\quad{\left(\texttt{x2x},\lor\texttt{I}_{r}\right)}

{\left({\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)},{\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\vdash{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}\quad{\left(\texttt{Law of excluded middle}\right)}

{\left({\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)},\phi\vdash{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}\quad{\left(\texttt{x2x},\lor\texttt{I}_{l}\right)}

{\left({\left(\phi\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(\psi\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)}\vdash{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}\quad{\left(\texttt{Law of excluded middle}\right)}

By the induction on the formula length, we get that, in
\texttt{CL}\left[\lor\right]
,
\phi\sim t{\left(\phi\right)}
.

This means that
t
is an equivalent formula substitution. From this immediately follows that
t
is
theoremhood-preserving.

Now, let us suppose that in the image of mapping there are theorems that are not
CL
-compatible.
Let us take simplest of these theorems (in terms of the shortest derivation length in
\texttt{CL}\left[\lor\right]
),
\sigma
. All the
theorems in that derivation are
CL
-compatible under the mapping (otherwise, it would not be simplest).

Let us consider the final step of that derivation. It could not involve any inference rule which does
not deal with
\lor
directly, as in that case, we could just apply the mapping
t
to both the premises and
conclusion, and obtain the derivation that is
CL
-compatible. It could not be
\lor\texttt{I}_{l}
or
\lor\texttt{I}_{r}
, since
\sigma
is the
result of the mapping, and does not contain
\lor
. Therefore, it should be
\lor\texttt{E}
.

Then, for some
\Gamma,\phi,\psi,\chi
, the theorem
\sigma
is equal to
\Gamma\vdash\chi
, and is obtained (in
\texttt{CL}\left[\lor\right]
) from theorems

\Gamma,\phi\vdash\chi
;
\Gamma,\psi\vdash\chi
; and
\Gamma\vdash{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}
. By the choice of
\sigma
, both
\Gamma
and
\chi
do not contain disjunction;
additionally,
t{\left(\Gamma\vdash{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}\right)}=\Gamma\vdash{\left({\left(t{\left(\phi\right)}\to\bot\right)}\to{\left({\left(t{\left(\psi\right)}\to\bot\right)}\to\bot\right)}\right)}
,
t{\left(\Gamma,\phi\vdash\chi\right)}=\Gamma,t{\left(\phi\right)}\vdash\chi
, and

t{\left(\Gamma,\psi\vdash\chi\right)}=\Gamma,t{\left(\psi\right)}\vdash\chi
are all theorems in
CL
. Yet these are enough to obtain
\sigma
in
CL
:

\Gamma,{\left(t{\left(\phi\right)}\to\bot\right)},{\left(t{\left(\psi\right)}\to\bot\right)}\vdash\bot

\Gamma,{\left(t{\left(\phi\right)}\to\bot\right)},{\left(t{\left(\psi\right)}\to\bot\right)}\vdash\chi

\Gamma,{\left(t{\left(\phi\right)}\to\bot\right)},t{\left(\psi\right)}\vdash\chi

\Gamma,{\left(t{\left(\phi\right)}\to\bot\right)}\vdash\chi

\Gamma,\phi\vdash\chi

\Gamma\vdash\chi

Therefore, our assumption was wrong, and there is no such sequent
\sigma
; all the resulting sequents
are
CL
-compatible.

∎

Note that there is one additional consequence: every disjunction-free sequent of
\texttt{CL}\left[\lor\right]
is
CL
-compatible;
disjunction-free theorems in
CL
do not require disjunction to prove them. Therefore,

CL
is reducible to
\texttt{CL}\left[\lor\right]
with respect to a trivial linear-time mapping, which preserves all sequents that
do not contain
\lor
intact, and translates all other sequents to
\vdash\bot
. From this, we immediately obtain
that
CL
is co-NP-complete.

Lemma 6.2

Proof.

Let us show that if sequent
\sigma
of

contains helper formula (
\bot\to x_{i}

mentioned in
\sigma

or not a theorem in

.

Let us assume that
\sigma

\sigma
(including all

(with

\bot

\bot

\sigma
is not a theorem in

.

Now, let us consider a mapping that turns sequent

\Gamma,\Delta\vdash\phi
, where

is

\sigma
. Such a mapping obviously turns theorems into theorems in every

Premise inflation
rule. It turns non-theorems into non-theorems in

, since it only adds

sequents to
ML
-compatible ones.

∎

Now it immediately follows that

is PSPACE-hard.

7.1
Framework

For the sake of convenience, in this chapter we will redefine
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
as the logic obtained from
PL
by
adding the following inference rule:

\texttt{E}_{0}

{\left(\phi_{1}\to\psi_{1}\right)}\vdash{\left(\phi_{2}\to\psi_{2}\right)}\phi_{1}\vdash\phi_{2}\qquad\phi_{2}\vdash\psi_{2}\qquad\psi_{1}\vdash\psi_{2}\qquad\psi_{2}\vdash\psi_{1}

It is easy to see that this new inference rule is equivalent to the combination of
\texttt{E1}_{0}
and
\texttt{E2}_{0}
.

We will define the set of
significant
implications for the certain derivation of theorem
\sigma
in
\texttt{PEL}_{0}

as the minimal set that:

•

contains all implication subformulas of
\sigma

•

for each application of the
\texttt{E}_{0}
rule, either contains both its left-hand and right-hand sides simultaneously or does not contain neither; and

•

contains all implication subformulas of all its elements.

We will say that the formula
\omega
is
substantial
for the certain derivation step, if its structure matters
for the corresponding inference rule. We will say that all other formulas are
auxiliary
for that step.
For example, if
\psi\vdash\phi\to\psi
was obtained by applying
\to\texttt{IW}
to
\psi\vdash\psi
(which, in turn, was obtained by

x2x
), both
\phi
and
\psi
are auxiliary formulas for this step, while
\phi\to\psi
is significant.

7.2
Elimination of insignificant formulas

Lemma 7.1
.

For every
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
theorem
\sigma
there is a derivation such that every its implication subformula
is significant.

Proof.

Let us take any derivation of
\sigma
.

Let
F
be the set of all significant formulas for the derivation.

Let us define the mapping
t_{\sigma}=t_{F}
as follows:

•

t_{F}{\left(x\right)}=x
, where
x
is a variable or constant

•

t_{F}{\left(\phi\land\psi\right)}={\left(t_{F}{\left(\phi\right)}\land t_{F}{\left(\psi\right)}\right)}

•

t_{F}{\left(\phi\to\psi\right)}={\left(t_{F}{\left(\phi\right)}\to t_{F}{\left(\psi\right)}\right)}={\left(\phi\to\psi\right)}
if
{\left(\phi\to\psi\right)}\in F
, and
t_{F}{\left(\psi\right)}
otherwise

•

t_{F}{\left(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}\vdash\psi\right)}=t_{F}{\left(\phi_{1}\right)},\ldots,t_{F}{\left(\phi_{n}\right)}\vdash t_{F}{\left(\psi\right)}

It is obvious that, for every
\phi\in F
,
t_{F}{\left(\phi\right)}=\phi
. In particular,
t_{\sigma}{\left(\sigma\right)}=\sigma
.

Now, let us apply
t_{F}
to all theorems of the derivation. We will prove that this application produced the derivation of
\sigma
such that every its implication subformula is
significant.

It is obvious that every implication subformula of the result is significant for the original derivation. Additionally, every implication subformula of the result is significant
for the result because for every
\phi\in F
,
\phi
is left intact by
t_{F}
and thus is the part of the result. Now it only remains to prove that the result is the correct derivation.

Let us suppose that
\tau
is the first incorrect derivation step of the result. Or, in other words, that
\tau

is the first sequent in the resulting pseudo-derivation such, that it could not be obtaining by applying
some
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
inference rule to the preceding steps (which are all theorems).

•

It obviously could not originally be obtained by one of the rules
\top,\texttt{x2x},\land\texttt{E},\land\texttt{I},\texttt{Cut}
, since every
implication subformula is auxiliary for these rules

•

It could not originally be obtained by the rule
\to\texttt{E}
or
\to\texttt{IW}
, where
{\left(\phi\to\psi\right)}\in F
, since both
\phi

and
\psi
remain intact under
t_{F}
transformation, and thus
\tau
is obtained as in the original derivation

•

It could not be originally obtained by the rule
\to\texttt{E}
, where
{\left(\phi\to\psi\right)}\notin F
, since
t_{F}{\left(\Gamma\vdash{\left(\phi\to\psi\right)}\right)}=t_{F}{\left(\Gamma\right)}\vdash t_{F}{\left(\psi\right)}
; the transformation turns both the latter of the premises and the conclusion into
the same sequent

•

It could not be originally obtained by the rule
\to\texttt{IW}
, where
{\left(\phi\to\psi\right)}\notin F
by the same argument;
the transformation turns both the premise and the conclusion into the same sequent

•

It could not be originally obtained by the rule
\texttt{E}_{0}
, where
{\left(\phi_{1}\to\psi_{1}\right)}\in F
, since that would imply

{\left(\phi_{2}\to\psi_{2}\right)}\in F
by the construction of
F
, and thus all of
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\psi_{1},\psi_{2}
remain intact under
t_{F}

transformation, and thus
\tau
is obtained as in the original derivation

•

It could not be originally obtained by the rule
\texttt{E}_{0}
, where
{\left(\phi_{1}\to\psi_{1}\right)}\notin F
, since that would imply

{\left(\phi_{2}\to\psi_{2}\right)}\notin F
by the construction of
F
, and thus
t_{F}{\left({\left(\phi_{1}\to\psi_{1}\right)}\vdash{\left(\phi_{2}\to\psi_{2}\right)}\right)}=t_{F}{\left(\psi_{1}\vdash\psi_{2}\right)}
;
the transformation turns both the latter of the premises and the conclusion into the same sequent,
yet the result does not contain duplicate sequents

We just have exhausted all the possibilities for how the preimage of
\tau
could be originally obtained.
Thus our assumption of
\tau
being the first incorrect derivation step is wrong; the result is indeed the correct
derivation.

∎

7.3

Lemma 7.2

\texttt{PEL}_{0}

Corollary 7.1
.

If formulas
\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n},\psi
do not contain different equivalent proper subformulas, then
the sequent
\sigma=\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{n}\vdash\psi
of
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
is
PL
-compatible.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
be a theorem of
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
. Let us consider its derivation that does not contain implication
subformulas that are not subformulas of
\sigma
.

This derivation does not employ
\texttt{E}_{0}
rule, as both parts of its conclusion are subformulas of
\sigma
, and
therefore all parts of its premises are proper subformulas of
\sigma
, and therefore equivalent parts of its
premises are actually equal, and therefore both parts of its conclusion are equal, and could as well be
obtained using
x2x
rule. Therefore, this derivation is a derivation in
PL
.

∎

7.4
Elimination of equivalent subformulas

We say that the set of formulas
\psi_{i}
is
free of equivalents
if they do not contain different subformulas
which are equivalent in
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
.

Lemma 7.3
.

Given set of formulas
\phi_{i}
with the combined length of
n
, it is possible to compute, in

O{\left(n^{3}\right)}
time, the set of formulas
\psi_{i}
free of equivalents such that, for every
i
,
\phi_{i}\sim\psi_{i}
in
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
; and
\psi_{i}

is no longer than
\phi_{i}
.

Proof.

Let us consider expression trees corresponding to the formulas
\phi_{i}
.

Now, let us mark and transform these trees in several steps as follows:

In the beginning, all the nodes are unmarked.

On every step, we will consider the shortest (in terms of the length of the corresponding formula)
unmarked node. If there is any marked node equivalent to the one under consideration, we’ll replace
the latter with the former. In any case, we’ll mark the resulting node.

We will continue doing such steps until all nodes are marked.

It is easy to see that the following invariants are satisfied on every step:

•

Every ancestor (subformula) of the marked node is marked. This is obviously true in the beginning (since
there are no marked nodes), and remains true on every step (since we only mark either shortest
unmarked node, or the one which already satisfies this condition).

•

Set of formulas represented by the marked nodes is free of equivalents. This is obviously true
in the beginning, and remains true on every step (since we only mark a new node if formula it
represents is not equivalent to any of formulas represented by the marked nodes, and all of its
ancestors are already marked).

•

Any unmarked node is not shorter than any marked node (in terms of the length of the corresponding subformulas). This is obviously true in the beginning, and remains true on every step
(since we only mark a new node if it is shortest among the unmarked nodes).

•

For every tree, the formula it represents is equivalent to, and not longer than the formula it
originally represented (since every change in its structure is caused by replacing unmarked node
with one not longer and equivalent to it).

Thus, in the end we get the set of formulas
\psi_{i}
satisfying the condition of lemma.

It remains to estimate computational complexity of the process.

There are at most
n
steps, since every step reduces the number of unmarked nodes by one, and
there was at most
n
unmarked nodes.

On every step, we have to find the shortest node (
O{\left(n\right)}
), and then, for every marked node (number
of which is less than
n
), to check whether the corresponding formulas are equivalent in
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
. As all
their ascendants are already marked (and thus corresponding subformulas are free of equivalents), by
corollary
7.1
the check could be performed in
PL
in linear time. Thus, the step could be performed in

O{\left(n^{2}\right)}
, and all the process could be performed in
O{\left(n^{3}\right)}
time.

∎

7.5
Multiple derivability problem complexity

Lemma 7.4
.

Multiple derivability problem for
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
is solvable in cubic time.

Proof.

Given hypotheses and queries, we could obtain the equivalent hypotheses and queries, free
of equivalents, such that the combined length of new hypotheses and queries is not greater than the
combined length of the original hypotheses and queries; the construction of new hypotheses and queries
would require cubic time, by lemma
7.3
. Thus, multiple derivability problem for original hypotheses
and queries in
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
is equivalent to the multiple derivability problem for new hypotheses and queries
in
PL
, which is, in turn, is solvable in linear time.

∎

Corollary 7.2
.

\texttt{PEL}_{0}<\texttt{PEL}1
,
\texttt{PEL}_{0}<\texttt{PEL}2

Proof.

As
\texttt{PEL}1
and
\texttt{PEL}2
are co-NP-hard, while
\texttt{PEL}_{0}
is cubic-time,
\texttt{PEL}1
and
\texttt{PEL}2
are not equal to

\texttt{PEL}_{0}
.

∎

All of the results above could be directly translated to the case of logics with disjunction, with the

\lor\texttt{E}

\Gamma\vdash\chi\lx@proof@logical@and\Gamma,\phi\vdash\chi\quad\Gamma,\psi\vdash\chi\quad\Gamma\vdash{\left(\phi\lor\psi\right)}

The only diffence is that, as

. As

is different from all of
\texttt{PEL}1\left[\lor\right]
,
\texttt{PEL}2\left[\lor\right]
,
\texttt{PEL}\left[\lor\right]
, or not.

9
Conclusion

In this work we have defined the principle of equivalent formula substitution and the closure of primal
logic for this principle, and established the following facts:

1.

\texttt{CL}\left[\lor\right]
is reducible to
CL
;
CL
is reducible to
\texttt{CL}\left[\lor\right]
;
CL
is co-NP-complete.

2.

IL
and
ML

3.

IL
is reducible to
ML

4.

ML
is reducible to
\texttt{PEL}1
and
\texttt{PEL}2
, and so the latter are PSPACE-complete. The same is true for
their disjunction-free counterparts.

5.

CL
is reducible to any
\texttt{PEL}1
and
\texttt{PEL}2

6.

\texttt{PEL}_{0}
is reducible to

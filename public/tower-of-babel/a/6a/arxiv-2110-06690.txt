Asymptotic expansion of the Wright function for large variable and parameter
R. B. Paris

Abstract
We consider the asymptotic expansion of the Wright function W λ , μ ​ ( z ) = ∑ n = 0 ∞ z n n ! ​ Γ ​ ( λ ​ n + μ ) ( λ > − 1 ) W_{\lambda,\mu}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n!\Gamma(\lambda n+\mu)}\qquad(\lambda>-1) for large (positive and negative) variable and large parameter μ \mu . The analysis is based on use of the method of steepest descents applied to a suitable integral representation and, in part, complements the recent work of Ansari and Askari. Numerical results are presented to illustrate the accuracy of the different expansions obtained. Mathematics subject classification (2020): 33C10, 34E05, 41A30, 41A60 Keywords: Asymptotic expansion, method of steepest descents, Wright function



1. Introduction

The Wright function under consideration (also known as a generalised Bessel function) is defined by

W_{\lambda,\mu}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{n!\Gamma(\lambda n+\mu)},

(1.1)

where
\lambda
is supposed real and
\mu
is, in general, an arbitrary complex parameter. The series
converges for all finite
z
provided
\lambda>-1
and, when
\lambda=1
, it reduces to the modified Bessel function
z^{(1-\mu)/2}I_{\mu-1}(2\sqrt{z})
. This function has found recent application in the theory of fractional calculus
[
2
,
3
]
.
The case corresponding to
\lambda=-\sigma
,
0<\sigma<1
arises in the analysis of time-fractional diffusion and diffusion-wave equations
[
4
]
; see also
[
6
]
.
The case
\mu=0
in (
1.1
) also finds application in probability theory and is discussed extensively in
[
7
]
, where it is denoted by

\phi(\lambda,0;z)=W_{\lambda,0}(z)

and referred to therein as a ‘reduced’ Wright function.

The asymptotics of this function were first studied by Wright
[
8
,
9
]
using the method of steepest descents applied to the integral representation

W_{\lambda,\mu}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}t^{-\mu}\exp\,[t+zt^{-\lambda}]\,dt\qquad(\lambda>-1,\ \mu\in{\bf C});

(1.2)

see also the summaries in
[
5
,
6
]
using a different approach. A recent paper by Ansari and Askari
[
1
]
has investigated the asymptotic expansion of (
1.1
) when the argument
z<0
and the parameter
\mu>0
are both large. More specifically, these authors considered the Wright function

{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x):=(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\nu}W_{\lambda,\nu+1}(-(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\lambda+1})=(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\nu}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-)^{n}(x/2)^{(\lambda+1)n}}{n!\Gamma(\lambda n+\nu+1)}

(1.3)

for
x\to+\infty
when
\nu=ax
, where
a>0
is fixed, by employing the method of steepest descents applied to a suitable Laplace-type integral representation. Here we consider the same problem in more detail using the same approach. In a certain domain of the integration variable it is found that there are two relevant saddle points, which can either be real or a complex conjugate pair. We also discuss a special case when the parameters
a
and
\lambda
are connected in a specific manner that corresponds to the formation of a double saddle point.

The function of positive argument

{\cal W}^{+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x):=(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\nu}W_{\lambda,\nu+1}((\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\lambda+1})=(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\nu}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(x/2)^{(\lambda+1)n}}{n!\Gamma(\lambda n+\nu+1)}

(1.4)

is also considered for
x\to+\infty
when
\nu=ax
, where
a>0
is fixed. This function is described by a similar Laplace-type integral which, in contrast to that defining
{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
, can involve a more elaborate saddle-point structure. There is always a single real saddle but, dependent on the values of
a
and
\lambda
, there can be several contributory complex saddles. In addition, it is possible to encounter
a Stokes phenomenon as the parameters
a
and
\lambda
are varied (with
x>0
). However, the additional complex saddle points yield contributions that are subdominant relative to that from the real saddle; these contributions may be neglected in certain applications.

2. The integral representation of
{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)

From (
1.2
), we find the integral representation

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x)^{\nu}}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}t^{-\nu-1}\exp\,[t-(x/2)^{\lambda+1}t^{-\lambda}]\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{(0+)}\tau^{-\nu-1}\exp\,[(x/2)(\tau-\tau^{-\lambda})]\,d\tau,

where
t=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}x\tau
. If we make the further change of variable
\tau=e^{u}
and put
\nu=ax
, we obtain

{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}e^{xh(u)}du,\qquad h(u):=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}(e^{u}-e^{-\lambda u})-au.

(2.1)

This last transformation causes all the Riemann sheets in the
\tau
-plane to appear as horizontal strips of width
2\pi
in the
u
-plane.
The loop in the
\tau
-plane can be taken to be a circle of radius
\rho
about the origin together with straight line segments on the upper and lower sides of the branch cut on the negative real axis. The map
C
of this path in the
u
-plane then consists of the three sides of the rectangle with vertices at
\infty-\pi i
,
\log\,\rho-\pi i
,
\log\,\rho+\pi i
and
\infty+\pi i
; see Fig. 1. An equivalent version of (
2.1
) was given in
[
1
]
.

3. Asymptotic expansions

In this section we consider the asymptotic expansions of
{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
in the three cases that can arise, namely a real contributory saddle, a complex conjugate pair of saddles and a real double saddle.

3.1 Real saddle point

When
-1<\lambda<0
and
\lambda>0
with
(a,\lambda)
situated above the curve in Fig. 2, the contributory saddle is real; see Fig. 3(a, b). We have from (
2.1
)

{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{e^{xh(u_{0})}}{2\pi i}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-xw^{2}/2}\,\frac{du}{dw}\,dw,

where we have introduced the new variable
w
by

-\frac{1}{2}w^{2}=h(u)-h(u_{0})=\frac{h_{0}^{\prime\prime}}{2!}(u-u_{0})^{2}+\frac{h_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{3!}(u-u_{0})^{3}+\cdots

and for brevity we denote the derivatives of
h(u)
evaluated at
u=u_{0}
by
h_{0}^{(n)}\equiv h^{(n)}(u_{0})
.
Inversion of this expansion using the
InverseSeries
command in
Mathematica
(essentially Lagrange inversion) yields

u-u_{0}=\frac{w}{\sqrt{-h_{0}^{\prime\prime}}}+\frac{h_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}w^{2}}{6h_{0}^{\prime\prime 2}}-\frac{(5h_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime 2}-3h_{0}^{\prime\prime}h_{0}^{iv})}{72h_{0}^{\prime\prime 3}\sqrt{-h_{0}^{\prime\prime}}}\,w^{3}+\cdots\ .

(3.1)

Upon differentiation this yields

\frac{du}{dw}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{=}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{-h_{0}^{\prime\prime}}}\sum_{k\geq 0}(-)^{k}A_{k}w^{2k},

where
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle e}}{{=}}
denotes the inclusion of
only the even powers of
w
, since odd powers will not enter into this calculation. We have

h_{0}^{\prime\prime}=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}(e^{u_{0}}-\lambda^{2}e^{-\lambda u_{0}})=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}(1+\lambda)e^{u_{0}}-a\lambda

(3.2)

and note that
0<h_{0}^{\prime\prime}<a
. The first few coefficients
A_{k}
are:

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,\qquad A_{1}=\frac{1}{24h_{0}^{\prime\prime}}(5{\bf h}_{3}^{2}-3{\bf h}_{4}),

\displaystyle A_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3456h_{0}^{\prime\prime 2}}(385{\bf h}_{3}^{4}-620{\bf h}_{3}^{2}{\bf h}_{4}+105{\bf h}_{4}^{2}+168{\bf h}_{3}{\bf h}_{5}-24{\bf h}_{6})

(3.3)

\displaystyle A_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6220800h_{0}^{\prime\prime 3}}(425425{\bf h}_{3}^{6}-1126125{\bf h}_{3}^{4}{\bf h}_{4}+675675{\bf h}_{3}^{2}{\bf h}_{4}^{2}-51975{\bf h}_{4}^{3}+360360{\bf h}_{3}^{3}{\bf h}_{5}

\displaystyle-249480{\bf h}_{3}{\bf h}_{4}{\bf h}_{5}+13608{\bf h}_{5}^{2}-83160{\bf h}_{3}^{2}{\bf h}_{6}+22680{\bf h}_{4}{\bf h}_{6}+12960{\bf h}_{3}{\bf h}_{7}-1080{\bf h}_{8}),

where we have defined

{\bf h}_{n}:=\frac{h_{0}^{(n)}}{h_{0}^{\prime\prime}},\qquad h_{0}^{(n)}=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}(1-(-\lambda)^{n-1})e^{u_{0}}+a(-\lambda)^{n-1}\qquad(n\geq 3).

Then

{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)\sim\frac{e^{xh(u_{0})}}{\pi\sqrt{2xh_{0}^{\prime\prime}}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-)^{k}A_{k}}{(x/2)^{k}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s}s^{k-1/2}ds

=\frac{e^{xh(u_{0})}}{\sqrt{2\pi xh_{0}^{\prime\prime}}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-)^{k}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}A_{k}}{(x/2)^{k}}\hskip 8.5359pt

(3.4)

as
x\to+\infty
with
\nu=ax
, where
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\ldots(a+n-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol. An expansion equivalent to this is given in Theorem 2.2 in
[
1
]
.

3.2 Complex saddle points

When
\lambda>0
and
(a,\lambda)
is situated below the curve in Fig. 2 the contributory saddles are complex as illustrated in Fig. 3(c). The contribution to
{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
from the upper steepest descent path is given by (compare (
3.4
))

\frac{e^{xh(u_{0})}}{i\sqrt{2\pi xh_{0}^{\prime\prime}}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-)^{k}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}A_{k}}{(x/2)^{k}},

where in this case
h_{0}^{\prime\prime}
given in (
3.2
) is complex-valued. Since the contribution
from the lower path yields the conjugate expression, we therefore obtain

{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi x}}\,\Re\biggl\{\frac{e^{xh(u_{0})}}{\sqrt{h_{0}^{\prime\prime}}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(-)^{k}(\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$})_{k}A_{k}}{(x/2)^{k}}\biggr\}

(3.5)

as
x\to+\infty
with
\nu=ax
. The leading term of this expansion is given in an equivalent form in Theorem 3.1 in
[
1
]
.

It is worth remarking that in §§3.1, 3.2 the exact location of the saddle
u_{0}
is not available in closed form. This necessitates the numerical solution of (
2.2
) to obtain
u_{0}
when the parameters
a
and
\lambda
have specific values. It is apparent that the presentation of higher coefficients
A_{k}
becomes prohibitive on account of their rapidly increasing complexity. This point is discussed further in Section 5 where it is indicated how more coefficients
A_{k}
can be generated by numerical reversion in specific cases.

3.3 Double saddle point

In the case of the double saddle point we are in the fortunate position of having an exact expression for the location of this saddle given in (
2.3
) combined with the condition (
2.4
). The contribution from the upper half of the steepest descent path illustrated in Fig. 3(d) is

I=\int_{u_{0}}^{\infty+\pi i}e^{xh(u)}du=e^{xh(u_{0})}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xw^{3}/3}\frac{du}{dw}\,dw,

where we have introduced the new variable
w
by

-\frac{w^{3}}{3}=h(u)-h(u_{0})=\frac{h_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{3!}(u-u_{0})^{3}+\frac{h_{0}^{(4)}}{4!}(u-u_{0})^{4}+\cdots\ .

Inversion of this expansion (using
Mathematica
) yields for the upper half of the integration path

u-u_{0}=\frac{2^{2/3}e^{\pi i/3}}{H^{1/3}}\,w+\frac{(\lambda-1)e^{2\pi i/3}}{2^{2/3}\cdot 3H^{2/3}}\,w^{2}-\frac{(1-6\lambda+\lambda^{2})}{60H}\,w^{3}+\cdots\ ,

where
H:=2h_{0}^{\prime\prime\prime}
.
Upon differentiation, we then obtain

\frac{du}{dw}=\frac{2^{2/3}}{H^{1/3}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{3}\pi i(k+1)}B_{k}}{H^{k/3}}\,w^{k},

where

\displaystyle B_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1,\quad B_{1}=\frac{\lambda-1}{2^{1/3}\cdot 3},\quad B_{2}=\frac{1-6\lambda+\lambda^{2}}{2^{2/3}\cdot 20},\quad B_{3}=\frac{1}{1620}(5+93\lambda-93\lambda^{2}-5\lambda^{3}),

\displaystyle B_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2^{1/3}\cdot 136080}(277+826\lambda-6114\lambda^{2}+826\lambda^{3}+277\lambda^{4}),

\displaystyle B_{5}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{2/3}\cdot 16800}(1-61\lambda-254\lambda^{2}+254\lambda^{3}+61\lambda^{4}-\lambda^{5}),

\displaystyle B_{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{10497600}(959+7098\lambda-2031\lambda^{2}-58708\lambda^{3}-2031\lambda^{4}+7098\lambda^{5}+959\lambda^{6}).

The contribution from the upper half of the steepest descent path then becomes

I\sim\frac{2^{2/3}e^{xh(u_{0})}}{3(Hx/3)^{1/3}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{3}\pi i(k+1)}B_{k}}{(Hx/3)^{k/3}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s}s^{k/2-2/3}\,ds

The contribution from the lower half of the integration path is given by the conjugate expansion. Hence, when
\nu=ax
,
\lambda>0
and the parameter
a
satisfies the condition (
2.4

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{-}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)\!\!

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{2\pi i}(I-{\overline{I}})

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{2^{2/3}e^{xh(u_{0})}}{3\pi(Hx/3)^{1/3}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{B_{k}}{(Hx/3)^{k/3}}\,\Gamma\biggl(\frac{k+1}{3}\biggr)\sin\frac{\pi}{3}(k+1)

H=\lambda^{2/(1+\lambda)}(1+\lambda),\qquad h(u_{0})=\frac{(\lambda^{2}-1)}{2\lambda^{2}}e^{u_{0}}-\lambda^{\gamma}\log\,\lambda,

with

4. The expansion of
{\cal W}^{+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)

We consider the function
{\cal W}^{+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)
defined in (
1.4
), which has the integral representation

{\cal W}^{+}_{\lambda,\nu}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C}e^{x{\tilde{h}}(u)}du,\qquad{\tilde{h}}(u):=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}(e^{u}+e^{-\lambda u})-au,

(4.1)

where
C
is the same path as in (
2.1
). Paths of steepest descent (resp. ascent) as
\Re(u)\to-\infty
asymptote to
\Im(u)=\pi n/\lambda
for odd (resp. even)
n
.
Saddle points are given by

e^{u_{0}}-\lambda e^{-\lambda u_{0}}=2a;

when
-1<\lambda<0

u_{0}
in the principal strip
-\pi\leq\Im(u)\leq\pi
. When
\lambda>0
, there is the real saddle
u_{0}
together with an infinite string of complex saddles
u_{k}=X_{k}\pm iY_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
approximately parallel to the imaginary axis with
X_{k}>0
. The saddle
u_{1}
has
\pi/\lambda<Y_{1}<3\pi/\lambda
while it is found that
Y_{k}\simeq(2k-1)\pi/\lambda
as
k
increases. As
\lambda
increases, more of these complex saddles enter the principal strip, some of which may, dependent on the values of
a
and
\lambda
, interact with the steepest descent path through
u_{0}
. We define the second derivative
{\tilde{h}}_{k}^{\prime\prime}
by

{\tilde{h}}_{k}^{\prime\prime}:=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}(e^{u_{k}}+\lambda^{2}e^{-\lambda u_{k}})=\mbox{${\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}$}(1+\lambda)e^{u_{k}}-a\lambda\qquad(k=0,1,2\ldots).

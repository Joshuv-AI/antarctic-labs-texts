Sharp weighted CR trace Sobolev inequalities
Gunhee Cho, Zetian Yan

Abstract
. We establish a sharp Sobolev trace inequality on the Siegel domain Ω n + 1 \Omega_{n+1} involving the weighted norm- W 2 , 2 ​ ( Ω n + 1 , ρ 1 − 2 ​ [ γ ] ) W^{2,2}(\Omega_{n+1},\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}) . The inequality is closely related the realization of fractional powers of the sub-Laplacian on ℍ n = ∂ Ω n + 1 \mathbb{H}^{n}=\partial\Omega_{n+1} as generalized Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators associated to the weighted poly-sublaplacian, generalizing observations of Frank–González–Monticelli–Tan.

1.
Introduction

In
[
Cas20
]
, Case recovered the fractional Laplacian
(-\overline{\Delta})^{\gamma}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator associated to the
weighted poly-Laplacian

L_{2k}:=\Delta_{m}^{k}
determined by
\gamma\in(0,\infty)\backslash\mathbb{N}
, where
\Delta_{m}:=\Delta+my^{-1}\partial_{y}
is the weighted Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}:=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
for
m=1-2[\gamma]
and
k:=\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+1
. Specifically, given functions
f^{(2j)}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{\gamma-2j}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor]
and
\phi^{(2j)}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap H^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-[\gamma]-2j}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor-1]
, if
V
is a solution of

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{2k}V=0,&~~\mbox{in}~~\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},\\
B_{2j}^{2\gamma}(V)=f^{(2j)},&~~\mbox{for}~~j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor],\\
B_{2[\gamma]+2j}^{2\gamma}(V)=\phi^{(2j)},&~~\mbox{for}~~j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor-1],\end{array}\right.

there exists constants
c_{\gamma,j}
and
d_{\gamma,j}
such that

(1.2)

\begin{split}B_{2\gamma-2j}^{2\gamma}(V)=c_{\gamma,j}(-\overline{\Delta})^{\gamma-2j}f^{(2j)},&~~\mbox{for}~~j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor],\\
B_{2\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-2j}^{2\gamma}(V)=d_{\gamma,j}(-\overline{\Delta})^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-[\gamma]-2j}\phi^{(2j)},&~~\mbox{for}~~j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor-1],\end{split}

where
B_{2j}^{2\gamma}
and
B_{2[\gamma]+2j}^{2\gamma}
are boundary operators constructed in
[
Cas20
, Definition 3.1]
.

Applying the Dirichlet principle, one deduces that

(1.3)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U)\geqslant&\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor}c_{\gamma,j}\oint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{(2j)}(-\overline{\Delta})^{\gamma-2j}f^{(2j)}dx\\
+&\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor-1}d_{\gamma,j}\oint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi^{(2j)}(-\overline{\Delta})^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-[\gamma]-2j}\phi^{(2j)}dx,\end{split}

for any
U\in\mathcal{C}^{2\gamma}\cap W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},y^{1-2[\gamma]})
, where the Dirichlet energy
\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U)
is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}UL_{2k}Uy^{m}dxdy&+\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor}\oint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}B_{2j}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{2\gamma-2j}^{2\gamma}(U)dx\\
&-\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor-1}\oint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}B_{2[\gamma]+2j}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{2\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-2j}^{2\gamma}(U)dx;\end{split}

see
[
Cas20
]
for more details. We may regard (
1.3
) as a functional inequality for the Sobolev trace embedding

W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},y^{1-2[\gamma]})\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor}H^{\gamma-2j}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor-1}H^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-[\gamma]-2j}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Combining (
1.3
) with Lieb’s sharp fractional Sobolev inequality
[
Lie83
]
yields the sharp Sobolev trace inequality for the embedding

W^{k,2}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+},y^{1-2[\gamma]})\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor}L^{\frac{2n}{n-2\gamma+4j}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\oplus\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor-1}L^{\frac{2n}{n-2\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+2[\gamma]+4j}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

when
n>2\gamma
.

The sharp version of (
1.3
) for
\gamma\in(0,1)
stems from Caffarelli and Silvestre’s seminal paper
[
CS07
]
. Furthermore, the special case
\gamma=\frac{3}{2}
was proven by Ache–Chang
[
AC17
]
; the special case
\gamma=\mathbb{N}_{0}+\frac{1}{2}
was prove by Yang
[
Yan19
]
; the general case
\gamma\in(0,\infty)\backslash\mathbb{N}
was proven by Case
[
Cas20
]
.

The remarkable breakthrough in
[
Cas20
]
is the construction of boundary operators associated to the weighted poly-Laplacian. They are defined recursively in terms of the weighted Laplacian
\Delta_{m}
and the weighted derivative
y^{m}\partial_{y}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
, and the induced Laplacian
\overline{\Delta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows:

\begin{split}\iota^{*}\circ\Delta_{m}^{j}&=\sum_{l=0}^{j}(-1)^{l}\binom{j}{l}\frac{(\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-l)!\Gamma(\gamma-j-l)}{(\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-j)!\Gamma(\gamma-2l)}\overline{\Delta}^{j-l}B_{2l}^{2\gamma},\\
\iota^{*}\circ y^{m}\partial_{y}\Delta_{m}^{j}&=(-1)^{j+1}\sum_{l=0}^{j}\binom{j}{l}\frac{(\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-l)!\Gamma(1+2l-\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+[\gamma])}{(\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-j)!\Gamma(1+j+l-\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+[\gamma])}\overline{\Delta}^{j-l}B_{2[\gamma]+2l}^{2\gamma},\end{split}

where
\iota^{*}:C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the restriction operator. These definitions are justified by three properties. First, they are such that the associated Dirichlet form
\mathcal{Q}_{2\gamma}(U,V):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U+V)-\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U)-\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(V)\right)
is symmetric. Second, the boundary operators are covariant with respect to the group of conformal isometries of
(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{n})
; see
[
Cas20
, Section 4]
for the precise statement. Third, as shown in (
1.2
), they are such that the generalized Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators recover the fractional Laplacians.

In the CR setting, fractional covariant operators of order
2\gamma
for
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, was defined from scattering theory on a Kähler–Einstein manifold
\mathcal{X}

[
GSB08
,
HPT08
,
EMM91
]
. They are pseudodifferential operators whose principal symbol agress with the pure fractional powers of the CR sub-Laplacian
(-\Delta_{b})^{\gamma}
on the boundary
M=\partial\mathcal{X}
. In the special case of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, they are simply the intertwining operators on the CR sphere calculated in
[
BFM13
]
using classical representation theory.

In the same spirit of Caffarelli and Silvestre, Frank, González, Monticelli and Tan formulated an extension problem on
\Omega_{n+1}
and constructed the pure fractional powers of the sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator of a degenerate elliptic equation using the Fourier transform and the spectral resolution of the operator. Moreover, we have the following sharp Sobolev trace inequality.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
FdMGMT15
]
)
.

Given
\gamma\in(0,1)
, for any
U\in\mathcal{C}_{D}^{2\gamma}
,
\phi\in S^{\gamma,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, we have

(1.4)

\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U)\geqslant 2^{1-2\gamma}\gamma\frac{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}{\Gamma(1+\gamma)}\oint_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\phi P_{\gamma}^{\theta}\phi dzdt.,\quad\phi=\iota^{*}U,

where
\iota^{*}:C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{n+1})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is the restriction operator, the corresponding function space
\mathcal{C}_{D}^{2\gamma}
is defined in Theorem
6.1
and the Dirichlet energy
\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U)
is given by

\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U)=\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}\left(|\partial_{\rho}U|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|X_{j}(U)|^{2}+|Y_{j}(U)|^{2}+\rho^{2}|\partial_{t}U|^{2}\right)\rho^{1-2\gamma}dzdtd\rho.

Equality is attained if and only if
U
is the unique solution for the extension problem

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\partial^{2}_{\rho}+(1-2\gamma)\rho^{-1}\partial_{\rho}+\rho^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta_{b}\right)U=0,&~~\mbox{in}~~\Omega_{n+1},\\
U=\phi,&~~\mbox{on}~~\mathbb{H}^{n}.\end{array}\right.

Our main result gives the sharp Sobolev trace inequality for
\gamma\in(1,2)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given
\gamma\in(1,2)
, for any
U\in\mathcal{C}_{D}^{2\gamma}
,
\phi\in S^{\gamma,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, we have

(1.6)

\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U)\geq 2^{3-2\gamma}\frac{\Gamma(2-\gamma)}{\Gamma(\gamma)}\oint_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\phi P^{\theta}_{\gamma}\phi dzdt,\quad\phi=\iota^{*}U,

where
\iota^{*}:C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{n+1})\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is the restriction operator, the corresponding function space
\mathcal{C}_{D}^{2\gamma}
is defined in Theorem
6.1
. The Dirichlet energy
\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U)
in (
1.6
) is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{2\gamma}(U):=&\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}UL_{4}U\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}dzdtd\rho\\
&+\oint_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}B_{0}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}(U)dzdt-\oint_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{2}^{2\gamma}(U)dzdt,\end{split}

where the associated boundary operators are

(1.7)

\begin{split}B_{0}^{2\gamma}=\iota^{*},\quad&B_{2}^{2\gamma}=-\iota^{*}\circ\mathcal{T}+\frac{1-[\gamma]}{[\gamma]}\Delta_{b}\circ B_{0}^{2\gamma},\\
B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma}=-\iota^{*}\circ\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho},\quad&B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}=\iota^{*}\circ\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho}\mathcal{T}-\frac{1+[\gamma]}{[\gamma]}\Delta_{b}\circ B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma},\end{split}

if we set

\mathcal{T}:=\partial^{2}_{\rho}+(1-2[\gamma])\partial_{\rho}+\rho^{2}\partial^{2}_{t}.

Equality in (
1.6
) is attained if and only if
U
is the unique solution for the extension problem

(1.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}L_{4}V=0,&~~\mbox{on}~~\Omega_{n+1},\\
B_{0}^{2\gamma}(V)=\phi,&~~\mbox{on}~~\mathbb{H}^{n},\\
B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma}(V)=0,&~~\mbox{on}~~\mathbb{H}^{n},\end{array}\right.

where

\begin{split}L_{4}&:=L_{[\gamma]}^{2}+T^{2},\\
L_{[\gamma]}=\partial^{2}_{\rho}+(1-2[\gamma])&\rho^{-1}\partial_{\rho}+\rho^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta_{b},\quad T=2\frac{\partial}{\partial t}.\end{split}

In particular,
P^{\theta}_{\gamma}
can be recovered from the solution of (
1.8
) by

P^{\theta}_{\gamma}\phi=2^{-3+2\gamma}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(2-\gamma)}\lim_{\rho\to 0^{+}}\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho}L_{[\gamma]}U.

The article is organized as follows:

In Section 2 we review some basic concepts on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
as a CR manifold and as the boundary of Siegel domain.

In Section 3 we recall the identification of fractional powers of the sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
via scattering theory for the hyperbolic Laplacian on the Siegel domain
\Omega_{n+1}

[
HPT08
]
. In the same spirit in
[
Cas20
]
, we define the weighted poly-sublaplacian on
\Omega_{n+1}
and prove a nice factorization in Lemma
3.5
. We also give a direct relationship between the weighted poly-sublaplacian on
\Omega_{n+1}
and the complex hyperbolic poly-Poisson operator in hyperbolic space.

In Section 4 we introduce the boundary operators associated to the weighted poly-sublaplacian for
\gamma\in(0,2)
and study their role in recovering fractional powers of the sub-Laplacian as generalized Dirichlet-to-Neumann operators. The key results here link our boundary operators to the asymptotics of solutions to a Poisson equation relevant to scattering theory
[
HPT08
]
and show that the Dirichlet form determined by our boundary operators is symmetric.

Due to the extra term
\rho^{2}\partial^{2}_{t}
in extension problems, it is hard to construct higher order boundary operators which satisfy the ‘mutual exclusion’ property; see Remark
4.4
for a precise statement.

In Section 5 we prove the existence and uniqueness of solutions to extension problems (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), which are degenerate elliptic boundary value problems. This enables us to prove the main result of this section, Theorem
5.3
.

In Section 6 we prove various sharp trace inequalities. First, Theorem
6.1
asserts a Dirichlet principle for solutions of the Dirichlet problem (
5.1
) and (
5.2
). As a result, we recover Theorem
1.1
in Corollary
6.2
and prove the sharp Sobolev trace inequality (
1.6
) as a direct consequence of Corollary
6.3
. Combining Corollary
6.2
and Corollary
6.3
with the sharp Sobolev inequalities on
\mathbb{H}^{n}

[
FL12
]
we obtain a sharp Sobolev trace inequality stated in Corollary
6.4
.

2.
Prelimiaries

2.1.
The Heisenberg group as a CR manifold

The
n
-dimensional Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is defined as the set
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
endowed with the group law

\left(\hat{z},\hat{t}\right)\cdot\left(z,t\right)=\left(\hat{z}+z,\hat{t}+t+2\mathrm{Im}\langle\hat{z},z\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\right)

for
\left(\hat{z},\hat{t}\right),\left({z},{t}\right)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}
is the standard inner product in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The CR structure on the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is inherited from the embedding
\mathbb{H}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. The holomorphic tangent bundle
T^{1,0}\mathbb{H}^{n}
is given by

T^{1,0}\mathbb{H}^{n}=T^{1,0}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\cap\mathbb{C}T\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\langle Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}\rangle,

where

(2.1)

Z_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+i\bar{z}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad\quad\bar{Z}_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}_{j}}-iz_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad j=1,\cdots,n.

We also set

(2.2)

X_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+2y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad Y_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}-2x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad j=1,\cdots,n,\quad T=2\frac{\partial}{\partial t},

which form a base of the Lie algebra of vector fields on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and are left invariant with respect to the group action. We immediately observe that the associated maximal complex distribution
H(\mathbb{H}^{n})=\mathrm{Re}\left\{T^{1,0}\mathbb{H}^{n}\oplus T^{0,1}\mathbb{H}^{n}\right\}
is simply

H(\mathbb{H}^{n})=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\langle X_{1},\cdots,X_{n},Y_{1},\cdots,Y_{n}\rangle,

which carries the complex structure
J_{b}:H(\mathbb{H}^{n})\to H(\mathbb{H}^{n})
defined by

J_{b}X_{j}=Y_{j},\quad J_{b}Y_{j}=-X_{j},\quad j=1,\cdots,n.

The real tangent bundle
T\mathbb{H}^{n}
satisfies

(2.3)

T\mathbb{H}^{n}=H(\mathbb{H}^{n})\oplus\mathbb{R}T.

The canonical contact form
\theta
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is precisely

(2.4)

\theta=\frac{1}{2}\left[dt+i\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(z_{j}d\bar{z}_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}dz_{j}\right)\right],

and the vector field
T
is the characteristic direction satisfying

\theta(T)=1,\quad\quad T\rfloor d\theta=0.

The Levi form
L_{\theta}
associated to
\theta
is given by

L_{\theta}=-id\theta=\sum_{j=1}^{n}dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j},

which is a positive definite matrix. This tells us that
(\mathbb{H}^{n},\theta)
is strictly pseudoconvex.

The sub-Laplacian associated to
\theta
is defined as

(2.5)

\Delta_{b}u:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[\bar{Z}_{j}Z_{j}+Z_{j}\bar{Z}_{j}\right]u=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left[X^{2}_{j}+Y^{2}_{j}\right]u;

the differential operator is linear, second order, degenerate elliptic, and it is hypoelliptic being the sum of squares of smooth vector fields satisfying the Hörmander condition.

2.2.
The Heisenberg group as the boundary of the Siegel domain

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
may be identified as the boundary of a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. Indeed, let
\Omega_{n+1}
be the Siegel domain, defined by

(2.6)

\Omega_{n+1}:=\left\{\zeta=(z,z_{n+1})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}|q(\zeta)>0\right\},

where

q(\zeta)=\mathrm{Im}z_{n+1}-|z|^{2}.

The boundary
\partial\Omega_{n+1}=\left\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}|q(\zeta)=0\right\}
is an oriented CR manifold of hypersurface type with the CR structure induced by
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
, which is CR equivalent to
\mathbb{H}^{n}
by the CR isomorphism
\mathcal{G}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\partial\Omega_{n+1}
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}(z,t)&=\left(z,t+i|z|^{2}\right),\quad(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n},\\
\mathcal{G}^{-1}(\zeta)&=(z,\mathrm{Re}z_{n+1}),\quad\zeta\in\partial\Omega_{n+1}.\end{split}

The boundary manifold
\partial\Omega_{n+1}
inherits a natural CR structure from the complex structure of the ambient manifold
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
,
-q
as a defining function. The pseudohermitian structure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is then given (via pullback) by the contact form

\theta=\frac{i}{2}\left(\bar{\partial}-\partial\right)(-q)=\frac{1}{4}(dz_{n+1}+d\bar{z}_{n+1})+\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(z_{j}d\bar{z}_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}dz_{j}\right),

which agrees with the definition given in (
2.4
).

In order to complete a basis for
T\Omega_{n+1}
, we define

\xi_{n+1}=\frac{1}{2}(N-iT),\quad\quad\bar{\xi}_{n+1}=\frac{1}{2}(N+iT),

where

T=2\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad\quad N=-2\frac{\partial}{\partial q}.

Then a frame in
\Omega_{n+1}
is given by
\left\{Z_{j},\bar{Z}_{j},\xi_{n+1},\bar{\xi}_{n+1}\right\}_{j=1}^{n}
, and the dual coframe of this basis is
\left\{\theta^{j},\theta^{\bar{j}},\theta,dq\right\}
with
dq=\partial q+\bar{\partial}q
. In particular, the dual coframe in
\mathbb{C}T\mathbb{H}^{n}
of
\left\{Z_{j},\bar{Z}_{j},T\right\}
is simply
\left\{\theta^{j},\theta^{\bar{j}},\theta\right\}
.

On the other hand, the functions
t=\mathrm{Re}Z_{n+1}\in\mathbb{R}
,
z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\rho=(2q)^{1/2}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
give coordinates in
\Omega_{n+1}\simeq\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. For the defining function
-q
, we can construct a Kähler form in
\Omega_{n+1}
as

\omega_{+}=-\frac{i}{2}\partial\bar{\partial}\log(q)=\frac{i}{2}\left(-\frac{\partial\bar{\partial}q}{q}+\frac{\partial q\wedge\bar{\partial}q}{q^{2}}\right).

By direct calculation, we observe that

(2.7)

-\partial\bar{\partial}q=\sum_{j=1}^{n}dz_{j}\wedge d\bar{z}_{j},\quad\quad\partial q\otimes\bar{\partial}q+\bar{\partial}q\otimes\partial q=\frac{1}{2}\left[(dq)^{2}+4\theta^{2}\right].

Then after the change
q=\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}
, the Riemannian metric
g^{+}
associated to
\omega_{+}
is given by

g^{+}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{d\rho^{2}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{2\delta_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}\theta^{\alpha}\otimes\theta^{\bar{\beta}}}{\rho^{2}}+\frac{4\theta}{\rho^{2}}\right).

Then
(\Omega_{n+1},\omega_{+})
is a Kähler manifold with constant holomorphic curvature. We recall from
[
FdMGMT15
]
that the Laplace operator on the Kähler manifold
(\Omega_{n+1},\omega_{+})
is calculated as

\Delta_{+}u=\mathrm{Tr}\left(i\partial\bar{\partial}u\right),

and the following formula decomposes the Laplacian into tangential and normal components relative to the level sets of
q
:

(2.8)

\Delta_{+}=q\left[q(\partial_{q}^{2}+\partial_{t}^{2})+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{b}-n\partial_{q}\right],

with
\Delta_{b}
the sub-Laplacian (
2.5
). The spectrum of the Laplacian
-\Delta_{+}
consists of an absolutely continuous part

\sigma_{ac}(-\Delta_{+})=\left[\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{4},\infty\right),

and the pure point spectrum satisfying

\sigma_{pp}(-\Delta_{+})\subset\left(0,\frac{(n+1)^{2}}{4}\right),

and moreover it consists of a finite set of
L^{2}
-eigenvalues.

3.
Scattering theory on the Siegel domain

Given
\gamma\in(0,m)\backslash\mathbb{N}
,
m=n+1
, for each
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\cap S^{\gamma,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, where
S^{\gamma,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
is the Folland–Stein space characterized in
[
BFM13
]
, there exists a unique solution
u_{s}
of the Poisson equation

(3.1)

\Delta_{+}u_{s}+s(m-s)u_{s}=0,\quad s=\frac{m+\gamma}{2}.

Then the Poisson map is defined as
\mathcal{P}(s):C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\to C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{n+1})
by
f\to u_{s}
; see
[
HPT08
]
for a Poisson kernel for (
3.1
). For our purposes, it suffices to know that there are functions
F,G\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{n+1})
such that

(3.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}u_{s}=\mathcal{P}(s)(f)=q^{m-s}F+q^{s}G,&\\
F|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}=f.&\end{array}\right.

Define the scattering operator
\mathcal{S}(s)(f):=G|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
. The CR
fractional GJMS operator
on
(\mathbb{H}^{n},\theta)
of order
2\gamma
is given by

(3.3)

P_{\gamma}^{\theta}:=2^{\gamma}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(-\gamma)}\mathcal{S}(\frac{m+\gamma}{2}),

which satisfies

(3.4)

P_{\gamma}^{e^{2w}\theta}f=e^{-(n+1+\gamma)w}P_{\gamma}^{\theta}\left(e^{(n+1-\gamma)w}f\right),\quad w\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n}).

It is known from
[
FdMGMT15
]
that

(3.5)

P_{\gamma}^{\theta}=\left(2|T|\right)^{\gamma}\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{2}+\frac{-\Delta_{b}}{2|T|}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1-\gamma}{2}+\frac{-\Delta_{b}}{2|T|}\right)},\quad\gamma\in(0,1).

The function
F
in (
3.2
) is determined modulo
O(q^{\infty})
by
f
by finding the Taylor series solution to

(3.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\left(\Delta_{+}+\frac{m^{2}-\gamma^{2}}{4}\right)\left(q^{\frac{m-\gamma}{2}}F\right)=O(q^{\infty}),&\\
F|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}=f.&\end{array}\right.

Similarly, the function
G
in (
3.2
) is determined modulo
O(q^{\infty})
by
P_{\gamma}^{\theta}f
by finding the Taylor series solution to

(3.7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\left(\Delta_{+}+\frac{m^{2}-\gamma^{2}}{4}\right)\left(q^{\frac{m+\gamma}{2}}G\right)=O(q^{\infty}),&\\
G|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}=\mathcal{S}(\frac{m+\gamma}{2})(f).&\end{array}\right.

For this reason, we would like to understand the formal solution of

(3.8)

\left(\Delta_{+}+s(m-s)\right)(V)=0,\quad s=\frac{m\pm\gamma}{2},\quad\gamma\in(0,m)\backslash\mathbb{N}

Because of a computational error in
[
HPT08
, Lemma 3.4]
, we recast the calculation for the correct expansion.

Proposition 3.1
.

Suppose that
u_{s}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{n+1})
satisfying
u_{s}=q^{m-s}F+q^{s}G
is the solution of (
3.2
). Then we have

F=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}q^{l}f_{l},\quad\quad f_{l}=(-1)^{l}\frac{\Gamma(m-2s+1)}{l!\Gamma(m-2s+l+1)}P^{s}_{l}(f_{0}),

and

G=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}q^{l}g_{l},\quad\quad g_{l}=(-1)^{l}\frac{\Gamma(2s-m+1)}{l!\Gamma(2s-m+l+1)}G^{s}_{l}(g_{0}),

where
P^{s}_{l}
and
G^{s}_{l}
are differential operators of order
2l
defined by (
3.18
) and (
3.20
), respectively, with leading symbol

(3.9)

\sigma(P^{s}_{l})=\sigma(G^{s}_{l})=\sigma(\Delta_{b}^{l}).

In particular, we have
P^{s}_{l}=G^{m-s}_{l}
.

Proof.

We think of (
2.8
) as a variable-coefficient differential operator with respect to vector field
q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}
and vector fields tangent to
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, i.e.,

(3.10)

\Delta_{+}=\sum_{i=0}^{2}q^{i}L_{i},

where

(3.11)

L_{0}=\left(q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}\right)^{2}-(n+1)q\frac{\partial}{\partial q},\quad\quad L_{1}=\frac{\Delta_{b}}{2},\quad\quad L_{2}=\frac{T^{2}}{4}.

We substitute
V=q^{m-s}F
into (
3.8
) and obtain that

(3.12)

\begin{split}&\left(\Delta_{+}+s(m-s)\right)(q^{m-s}F)=\left(\sum_{i=0}^{2}q^{i}L_{i}+s(m-s)\right)(q^{m-s}F)\\
&=\left(L_{0}+s(m-s)\right)(q^{m-s}F)+qL_{1}(q^{m-s}F)+q^{2}L_{2}(q^{m-s}F).\end{split}

We assume that
F
has the formal expansion
F=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}q^{l}f_{l}
. Direct calculation shows that

(3.13)

\begin{split}\left(L_{0}+s(m-s)\right)&(q^{m-s}F)=\left(m-2s+1\right)q^{m-s+1}\frac{\partial F}{\partial q}+q^{m-s+2}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial q^{2}}\\
&=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l\left(m-2s+1\right)q^{m-s+l}f_{l}+\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l\left(l-1\right)q^{m-s+l}f_{l}\\
&=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}l\left(m-2s+l\right)q^{m-s+l}f_{l}.\end{split}

Plugging (
3.13
) into (
3.12
) yields

(3.14)

f_{-1}=0,\quad f_{0}=f,\quad f_{l}=-\frac{L_{1}f_{l-1}+L_{2}f_{l-2}}{l\left(m-2s+l\right)},\quad l\geqslant 1.

We assume that

(3.15)

f_{l}=(-1)^{l}\frac{\Gamma(m-2s+1)}{l!\Gamma(m-2s+l+1)}P^{s}_{l}(f),

where
P^{s}_{l}
is an operator depending on
L_{1},L_{2}
and
s
.

Combining this with (
3.14
), we observe that

(3.16)

\begin{split}&P^{s}_{-1}=0,\quad P^{s}_{0}=\mathrm{id},\\
P^{s}_{l}=L_{1}\circ P^{s}_{l-1}-&(l-1)(m-2s+l-1)L_{2}\circ P^{s}_{l-2},\quad l\geqslant 1.\end{split}

By induction one sees that
P^{s}_{l}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
l
in the commuting variables
L_{1}
and
\sqrt{L_{2}}
. Hence, the recursion relation for
P^{s}_{l}
reduces to the recursion for the polynomials
p_{l}^{s}
of one variable
x
defined by

(3.17)

\begin{split}&p^{s}_{-1}=0,\quad p^{s}_{0}(x)=1,\\
p^{s}_{l}=xp^{s}_{l-1}-&(l-1)(m-2s+l-1)p^{s}_{l-2},\quad l\geqslant 1.\end{split}

Obviously,
p^{s}_{l}
is a polynomial of degree
l
with the leading coefficient
1
.
Moreover,
P^{s}_{l}
is recovered from
p^{s}_{l}
by

(3.18)

P^{s}_{l}=\left(\sqrt{L_{2}}\right)^{l}\circ p^{s}_{l}\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sqrt{L_{2}}}\right).

Similarly, we have

(3.19)

g_{l}=(-1)^{l}\frac{\Gamma(2s-m+1)}{l!\Gamma(2s-m+l+1)}G^{s}_{l}(\mathcal{S}(s)(f)),

where
G^{s}_{l}
is given by

(3.20)

G^{s}_{l}=\left(\sqrt{L_{2}}\right)^{l}\circ g^{s}_{l}\left(\frac{L_{1}}{\sqrt{L_{2}}}\right),

and the polynomial
g^{s}_{l}
is determined by

(3.21)

\begin{split}&g^{s}_{-1}=0,\quad g^{s}_{0}(x)=1,\\
g^{s}_{l}=xg^{s}_{l-1}-&(l-1)(2s-m+l-1)g^{s}_{l-2},\quad l\geqslant 1,\end{split}

which implies that
P^{s}_{l}=G^{m-s}_{l}
. Finally, (
3.9
) follows from (
3.18
), (
3.20
) immediately.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The analogue of the operator
P^{s}_{l}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is precisely
(-\overline{\Delta})^{l}
, independent of the value of
s
. However, from the recursion relation (
3.17
), we observe that
P^{s}_{l}
depends on the value of
s
when
l\geqslant 2
and it is hard to solve the recursion relation (
3.17
) explicitly.

There are two ways to study the CR fractional GJMS operators via an extension. The first approach is to identify solutions of (
3.2
) as elements of the kernel of a second-order “weighted” Laplacian on the Siegel domain.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,m)\backslash\mathbb{N}
. We define

(3.22)

L_{\gamma}=\partial^{2}_{\rho}+(1-2\gamma)\rho^{-1}\partial_{\rho}+\rho^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta_{b}.

Then

L_{\gamma}\circ\rho^{-m+\gamma}\circ\mathcal{P}(\frac{m+\gamma}{2})=0.

Proof.

After the change
q=\frac{\rho^{2}}{2}
, we have

(3.23)

\Delta_{+}=\frac{\rho^{2}}{4}\left(\partial^{2}_{\rho}-(2n+1)\rho^{-1}\partial_{\rho}+\rho^{2}\partial_{t}^{2}+\Delta_{b}\right).

A direct computation using (
3.23
) shows that

(3.24)

\frac{1}{4}L_{\gamma}\left(\rho^{-m+\gamma}U\right)=\rho^{-m+\gamma-2}\left(\Delta_{+}+\frac{m^{2}-\gamma^{2}}{4}\right)U,

for all
U\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{n+1})
. The conclusion follows from the definition of the Poisson operator
\mathcal{P}(\frac{m+\gamma}{2})
.
∎

The second approach to studying the CR fractional GJMS operators via an extension is to identify solutions of (
3.2
) as elements of the kernel of a product of
L_{\gamma}
. This can be done as follows.

First, let
\gamma\in(0,m)\backslash\mathbb{N}
. Set
k:=\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+1
. The
weighted poly-sublaplacian determined by

\gamma
is

(3.25)

L_{2k}:=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}L_{\gamma-2j}=L_{\gamma-2k+2}\circ\cdots\circ L_{\gamma},

where
L_{\mu}
is defined in (
3.22
).

Second, let
\gamma\in(0,m)\backslash\mathbb{N}
. Set
s:=\frac{m+\gamma}{2}
and

D_{s}:=\Delta_{+}+s(m-s).

The
complex hyperbolic poly-Poisson operator determined by

\gamma
is

(3.26)

L^{+}_{2k}:=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}D_{s-j}.

The following two lemmas capture the essential features of this relationship as needed to study CR sharp Sobolev trace inequalities on the Siegel domain.

First, to understand the boundary operators, it is useful to rewrite the factorization of
L_{2k}^{+}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,m)\backslash\mathbb{N}
and
L_{2k}^{+}
be the weighted poly-sublaplacian defined in (
3.26
). Then

(3.27)

L_{2k}^{+}=\prod_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor}D_{s_{j}}\circ\prod_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor-1}D_{\tilde{s}_{j}},

where
s_{j}=\frac{m+\gamma}{2}-j
and
\tilde{s}_{j}=\frac{m+\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-[\gamma]}{2}-j
.

Proof.

Separating (
3.26
) into terms with
s-j>\frac{m}{2}
and
s-j<\frac{m}{2}
, we compute that

L_{2k}^{+}=\prod_{j=0}^{\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor}D_{s_{j}}\circ\prod_{j=\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor+1}^{k-1}D_{s_{j}}

Observe that if
j+i=\lfloor\gamma\rfloor
for
\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor+1\leqslant j\leqslant\lfloor\gamma\rfloor
, we have

(3.28)

D_{\frac{m+\gamma}{2}-j}=D_{\frac{m+\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-[\gamma]}{2}-i}.

Using (
3.28
), the desired result follows by reindexing.
∎

Second, the operator
L_{2k}
defined as a product of
L_{\gamma-2j}
has a nice factorization (
3.29
) which implies that
L_{2k}
is formally self-adjoint with respect to the weighted measure
\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}dzdtd\rho
on
\Omega_{n+1}
. The following result can be deduced from Theorem 1.3
[
LY22
]
easily. In order to make this paper self-contained, we prove it in an alternative way.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,m)\backslash\mathbb{N}
and
L_{2k}
be the weighted poly-sublaplacian defined in (
3.25
). Then

(3.29)

L_{2k}=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\left(L_{[\gamma]}+i\left(k-1-2j\right)T\right),

where
L_{\mu}
is given in (
3.22
) and
T=2\partial_{t}
.

Proof.

We prove the claim by induction. Direct calculation shows that (
3.29
) holds for
\gamma\in(0,2)
. Suppose that (
3.29
) holds for
\gamma\in(0,k-2)
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
k>2
.

For convenience, we denote the operator
\rho^{-1}\partial_{\rho}
by
Y
. First observe the commutator identity

(3.30)

\left[Y,L_{\mu}\right]=2\left(Y^{2}+\partial_{t}^{2}\right),\quad\forall\mu\in\mathbb{R}.

A direct computation using (
3.25
) yields

(3.31)

\begin{split}L_{2k}=&L_{\gamma-2k+2}\circ\prod_{j=1}^{k-2}\left(L_{\gamma-k+1}+i\left(k-1-2j\right)T\right)\circ L_{\gamma}\\
=&\left(L_{[\gamma]}+2(k-1)Y\right)\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ\left(L_{[\gamma]}-2(k-1)Y\right)\\
=&L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}^{2}+2(k-1)\left[Y,L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right]\\
&-4(k-1)^{2}Y\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y.\end{split}

Hence, it suffices to show that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
k>2

(3.32)

\left[Y,L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right]=2(k-1)Y\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y+2(k-1)L_{2(k-2)}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}.

By induction, suppose that (
3.32
) holds for
k-2
, then

(3.33)

\begin{split}\left[Y,L_{2k}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right]&=\left[Y,\left(L_{[\gamma]}^{2}+(k-1)^{2}T^{2}\right)\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right]\\
&=\left[Y,L_{[\gamma]}^{2}+(k-1)^{2}T^{2}\right]\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\\
&+\left(L_{[\gamma]}^{2}+(k-1)^{2}T^{2}\right)\circ\left[Y,L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right].\end{split}

Notice that
T
can commute with
Y
and
L_{\mu}
for all
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence

(3.34)

\begin{split}\left[Y,L_{[\gamma]}^{2}+(k-1)^{2}T^{2}\right]&=\left[Y,L_{[\gamma]}\right]\circ L_{[\gamma]}+L_{[\gamma]}\circ\left[Y,L_{[\gamma]}\right]\\
&=2\left(Y^{2}\circ L_{[\gamma]}+L_{[\gamma]}\circ Y^{2}\right)+4L_{[\gamma]}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}.\end{split}

Plugging into (
3.34
) and (
3.32
) into (
3.33
) yields

(3.35)

\begin{split}\left[Y,L_{2k}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right]&=2Y^{2}\circ L_{[\gamma]}^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}+2L_{[\gamma]}\circ Y^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\\
&+2(k-1)\left(L_{[\gamma]}^{2}+(k-1)^{2}T^{2}\right)\circ Y\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\\
&+4L_{[\gamma]}^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}+2(k-1)L_{2k}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}.\end{split}

Besides, we have

(3.36)

Y^{2}\circ L_{[\gamma]}^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}=Y\circ L_{[\gamma]}^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y+Y\circ\left[Y,L_{[\gamma]}^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\right],

(3.37)

\begin{split}L_{[\gamma]}\circ Y^{2}\circ&L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}=Y\circ L_{[\gamma]}^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\\
&+Y\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ\left[Y,L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right]\\
&+\left[L_{[\gamma]},Y\right]\circ Y\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ L_{2(k-2)},\end{split}

and

(3.38)

\left(L_{[\gamma]}^{2}+(k-1)^{2}T^{2}\right)\circ Y\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y=Y\circ L_{2k}\circ Y+\left[L_{[\gamma]}^{2},Y\right]\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y.

Combining them together yields

(3.39)

\begin{split}\left[Y,L_{2k}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right]=&2(k+1)Y\circ L_{2k}\circ Y+\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III}\\
&+4L_{[\gamma]}^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}+2(k-1)L_{2k}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}\\
&-4(k-1)^{2}Y\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\circ T^{2},\end{split}

where

(3.40)

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}=&2Y\circ\left[Y,L_{[\gamma]}^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\right]\\
\mathrm{II}=&2Y\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ\left[Y,L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right]\\
&+2\left[L_{[\gamma]},Y\right]\circ Y\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\\
\mathrm{III}=&2(k-1)\left[L_{[\gamma]}^{2},Y\right]\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y.\end{split}

Using (
3.32
) and (
3.30
), we find that

(3.41)

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}=&8(k-1)Y\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ Y\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\\
&+8(k-1)Y\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}\\
\mathrm{III}=&-4(k-1)Y^{2}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\\
&-4(k-1)L_{[\gamma]}\circ Y^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\\
&-8(k-1)L_{[\gamma]}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\circ\partial_{t}^{2}\end{split}

Notice that

(3.42)

\begin{split}Y\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ Y\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y=&Y^{2}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\\
&-2Y\circ\left(Y^{2}+\partial_{t}^{2}\right)\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\\
-L_{[\gamma]}\circ Y^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y=&-Y^{2}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\\
&+4Y\circ\left(Y^{2}+\partial_{t}^{2}\right)\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y.\end{split}

We conclude that

(3.43)

\begin{split}\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III}&=8(k-1)\left[Y,L_{2(k-2)}\circ L_{[\gamma]}\right]\circ\partial_{t}^{2}\\
&=16(k-1)^{2}Y\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ Y\circ\partial_{t}^{2}+16(k-1)^{2}L_{2(k-2)}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}.\end{split}

Observe that

(3.44)

\begin{split}&4L_{[\gamma]}^{2}\circ L_{2(k-2)}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}+2(k-1)L_{2k}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}\\
&=2(k+1)L_{2k}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}-16(k-1)^{2}L_{2(k-2)}\circ\partial_{t}^{2}.\end{split}

Finally, (
3.32
) holds for
k
.
∎

We now prove that elements of the kernel
D_{s-j}
are also in the kernel of
L_{2k}
when weighted against a suitable power of
\rho
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,m)\backslash\mathbb{N}
and set
k:=\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+1
. Denote

\mathcal{I}^{\gamma}:=\left\{\gamma-2j|j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor]\right\}\cup\left\{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-[\gamma]-2j|j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma\rfloor-\lfloor\gamma/2\rfloor-1]\right\}.

For each
\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{I}^{\gamma}
it holds that

L_{2k}\circ\rho^{-m+\gamma}\circ\mathcal{P}(\frac{m+\tilde{\gamma}}{2})=0.

Proof.

It is clear from Lemma
3.6
that

L_{2k}^{+}\circ\mathcal{P}(\frac{m+\tilde{\gamma}}{2})=0,

for each
\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{I}^{\gamma}
. The desired result follows from a repeated application of the transformation law (

(3.45)

4^{-k}L_{2k}\circ\rho^{-m+\gamma}=\rho^{-m+\gamma-2k}\circ L^{+}_{2k}.

∎

4.
Boundary operators on the Siegel domain

In this section we introduce the boundary operators associated to the weighted ploy-sublaplacian
L_{2k}
determined by
\gamma\in(0,2)\backslash\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
3.6
, the kernel of
L_{2k}
contains solutions of the Poisson equation (
3.2
) for any
\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{I}^{\gamma}
. Our boundary operators are designed to pick out the functions
F(\cdot,0)
and
G(\cdot,0)
of solutions to (
3.2
). They also give rise to formally self-adjoint boundary value problems. To that end, it is convenient to introduce the space

\mathcal{C}^{2\gamma}=C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}(\overline{\Omega}_{n+1})+\rho^{2[\gamma]}C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}(\overline{\Omega}_{n+1})

associated to a given
\gamma\in(0,m)\backslash\mathbb{N}
, where
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{even}}
denotes the space of smooth functions on
\overline{\Omega}_{n+1}
whose Taylor series expansions in
\rho
at
\rho=0
contain only even terms. Note that

(1) if
\gamma\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
\mathcal{C}^{2\gamma}=C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{n+1})
; and

(2) for any
\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{I}^{\gamma}
, it holds that
\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{m+\tilde{\gamma}}{2}\right):C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\cap S^{\tilde{\gamma},2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\to\mathcal{C}^{2\gamma}
.

The second point means that the space
\mathcal{C}^{2\gamma}
is well-suited to studying all of the scattering problems formed from the factors of the complex hyperbolic poly-Poisson operator
L_{2k}^{+}
. Let
\iota^{*}:\mathcal{C}^{2\gamma}\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
denote the restriction operator,
\iota^{*}U=U|_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}
. Our boundary operators are elements of the set

\begin{split}\mathcal{B}^{2\gamma}:=&\left\{B_{2j}^{2\gamma}:\mathcal{C}^{2\gamma}\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})|j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma\rfloor]\right\}\\
&\cup\left\{B_{2[\gamma]+2j}^{2\gamma}:\mathcal{C}^{2\gamma}\to C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})|j\in[0,\lfloor\gamma\rfloor]\right\}\end{split}

defined as follows:

Definition 4.1
.

Set

\mathcal{R}:=\partial^{2}_{\rho}+(1-2[\gamma])\partial_{\rho},\quad\quad\mathcal{T}:=\mathcal{R}+\rho^{2}\partial^{2}_{t}.

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we define associated boundary operators by

(4.1)

B_{0}^{2\gamma}=\iota^{*},\quad B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}=-\iota^{*}\circ\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho}.

For
\gamma\in(1,2)
, we define associated boundary operators by

(4.2)

\begin{split}B_{0}^{2\gamma}=\iota^{*},\quad&B_{2}^{2\gamma}=-\iota^{*}\circ\mathcal{T}+\frac{1-[\gamma]}{[\gamma]}\Delta_{b}\circ B_{0}^{2\gamma},\\
B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma}=-\iota^{*}\circ\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho},\quad&B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}=\iota^{*}\circ\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho}\mathcal{T}-\frac{1+[\gamma]}{[\gamma]}\Delta_{b}\circ B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma}.\end{split}

The first goal of this section is to show that the boundary operators
\mathcal{B}^{2\gamma}
are relevant for picking out the Dirichlet data
F(\cdot,0)
and the Neumann data
G(\cdot,0)
of solutions of the Poisson equation
\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{m+\tilde{\gamma}}{2}\right)
for any
\tilde{\gamma}\in\mathcal{I}^{\gamma}
. This is accomplished by the following two propositions.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,2)\backslash\mathbb{N}
. Let
V=\rho^{-m+\gamma}\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{m+{\gamma}}{2}\right)(f)
for some
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\cap S^{\gamma,2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. It holds that

(4.3)

B_{0}^{2\gamma}(V)=2^{\frac{\gamma-m}{2}}f,

(4.4)

B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}(V)=(-1)^{\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+1}2^{-\frac{m+\gamma}{2}}2^{2\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+1}\lfloor\gamma\rfloor!\frac{\Gamma(\gamma+1)}{\Gamma([\gamma])}\hat{f}

where
\hat{f}=\mathcal{S}\left(s\right)f
with
s=\frac{m+{\gamma}}{2}
. Moreover,
B_{2\alpha}^{2\gamma}(V)=0
for all
B_{2\alpha}^{2\gamma}\in\mathcal{B}^{2\gamma}\backslash\{B_{0}^{2\gamma},B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}\}
.

Proof.

To begin, note that

(4.5)

\mathcal{R}(\rho^{2j})=4j(j-[\gamma])\rho^{2j-2},\quad\quad\mathcal{R}(\rho^{2[\gamma]+2j})=4j(j+[\gamma])\rho^{2[\gamma]+2j-2}.

By (
3.2
), it holds that

(4.6)

V=\rho^{-m+\gamma}\mathcal{P}\left(\frac{m+{\gamma}}{2}\right)f=\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{m-\gamma}{2}}F+\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{m+\gamma}{2}}\rho^{2\gamma}G,

where by Proposition
3.1
,

F=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\frac{\Gamma(1-\gamma)}{2^{l}l!\Gamma(l+1-\gamma)}\rho^{2l}P^{s}_{l}(f),

and

G=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\frac{\Gamma(1+\gamma)}{2^{l}l!\Gamma(l+1+\gamma)}\rho^{2l}G^{s}_{l}(\hat{f}).

We separate the proof into two cases:

First consider
B_{2l}^{2\gamma}
for
l\in[0,\lfloor\gamma\rfloor]
. By the definition of
B_{0}^{2\gamma}
, we know that (
4.3
) holds. When
\gamma\in(1,2)
and
l=1
, using (
4.3
) and (
3.18
),we compute that

(4.7)

Remark 4.4
.

Our boundary operators are designed to pick out the functions
F(\cdot,0)
and
G(\cdot,0)
of solutions of (
3.2
). To guarantee that
B_{2l}^{2\gamma}(V)=0
for

(see
[

, Propositon 3.2]
for more details), we need to make the cancellation by composing
B_{2j}^{2\gamma}
with
P^{s}_{l-j}=(-\overline{\Delta})^{l-j}
because the output of
\iota^{*}\circ T^{l}
is exactly
f_{l-j}
. However, in the CR setting, the presence of the term
\rho^{2}\partial^{2}_{t}
affects the output of leading terms
\iota^{*}\circ\mathcal{T}^{l}
and
\iota^{*}\circ\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho}\mathcal{T}^{l}
,
l\geqslant 2
, which implies that the operator to be composed is no longer
P^{s}_{l-j}
.

Let

(4.14)

\begin{split}\mathcal{Q}_{2\gamma}(U,V):=&-\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}UL_{2}V\rho^{1-2\gamma}dzdtd\rho+\oint_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}B_{0}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}(V)dzdt,\\
\mathcal{Q}_{2\gamma}(U,V):=&\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}UL_{4}V\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}dzdtd\rho\\
&+\oint_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}B_{0}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}(V)dzdt-\oint_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{2}^{2\gamma}(V)dzdt\end{split}

be the associated Dirichlet energy determined by
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
\gamma\in(1,2)
, respectively. The second goal of this section is to prove that
\mathcal{Q}_{2\gamma}
is symmetric. This implies that the boundary value problem involving
L_{2k}
and
\mathcal{B}^{2\gamma}
are variational. The proof that
\mathcal{Q}_{2\gamma}
is symmetric is essentially integration by parts on the Siegel domain
\Omega_{n+1}
with flat metric. To that end, it is useful to express
\iota^{*}\circ L_{[\gamma]}
and
\iota^{*}\circ\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho}L_{[\gamma]}
in terms of boundary operators.

Proposition 4.5
.

For
\gamma\in(1,2)
, it holds that

(4.15)

\begin{split}\iota^{*}=B_{0}^{2\gamma},\quad&\iota^{*}\circ L_{[\gamma]}=-B_{2}^{2\gamma}+\frac{1}{[\gamma]}\Delta_{b}\circ B_{0}^{2\gamma},\\
\iota^{*}\circ\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho}=-B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma},\quad&\iota^{*}\circ\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}\partial_{\rho}L_{[\gamma]}=B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}+\frac{1}{[\gamma]}\Delta_{b}\circ B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma}.\end{split}

Proof.

Note that

(4.16)

\iota^{*}\circ L_{[\gamma]}=\iota^{*}\circ\left(\mathcal{T}+\Delta_{b}\right)=\iota^{*}\circ\mathcal{T}+\iota^{*}\circ\Delta_{b}=\iota^{*}\circ\mathcal{T}+\Delta_{b}\circ\iota^{*}.

Then, (
4.15

directly.
∎

We now prove that

is symmetric by giving an explicit formula for

\mathcal{B}^{2\gamma}_{D}:=\left\{B^{2\gamma}_{2\alpha}\in\mathcal{B}^{2\gamma}|0\leqslant\alpha<\gamma/2\right\}

of the inputs.

Theorem 4.6
.

Given
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have

(4.17)

\begin{split}&\mathcal{Q}_{2\gamma}(U,V)\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}\left(\partial_{\rho}U\partial_{\rho}V+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}(U)X_{j}(V)+Y_{j}(U)Y_{j}(V)+\rho^{2}\partial_{t}U\partial_{t}V\right)\rho^{1-2\gamma}dzdtd\rho.\end{split}

Likewise, given
\gamma\in(1,2)
, we have

(4.18)

\begin{split}\mathcal{Q}_{2\gamma}(U,V)&=\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}\left(L_{[\gamma]}(U)L_{[\gamma]}(V)-T(U)T(V)\right)\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}dzdtd\rho\\
&-\frac{1}{[\gamma]}\oint_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\Delta_{b}\left(B_{0}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma}(V)+B_{2[\gamma]}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{0}^{2\gamma}(V)\right)dzdt\end{split}

Proof.

Given
\gamma\in(0,1)
, using the integration by parts on the Siegel domain
\Omega_{n+1}
with flat metric, we have

(4.19)

\begin{split}&-\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}U\left(\partial^{2}_{\rho}+(1-2\gamma)\rho^{-1}\partial_{\rho}\right)V\rho^{1-2\gamma}dzdtd\rho\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}\partial_{\rho}U\partial_{\rho}V\rho^{1-2\gamma}dzdtd\rho-\oint_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}B_{0}^{2\gamma}(U)B_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}(V)dzdt.\end{split}

(4.20)

-\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}U\Delta_{b}V\rho^{1-2\gamma}dzdtd\rho=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}X_{j}(U)X_{j}(V)+Y_{j}(U)Y_{j}(V)\rho^{1-2\gamma}dzdtd\rho.

(4.21)

-\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}U\rho^{2}\partial^{2}_{t}V\rho^{1-2\gamma}dzdtd\rho=\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}\rho^{2}\partial_{t}U\partial_{t}V\rho^{1-2\gamma}dzdtd\rho.

Combining (
4.19
), (
4.20
) and (

4.17

Similarly, given
\gamma\in(1,2)

, we know that

(4.22)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}UL^{2}_{[\gamma]}(V)\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}dzdtd\rho=\int_{\Omega_{n+1}}L_{[\gamma]}(U)L_{[\gamma]}(V)\rho^{1-2[\gamma]}dzdtd\rho\\

By Propositon

(4.23)

\begin{split}B_{0}^{2\gamma}\circ L_{[\gamma]}&=-B_{2}^{2\gamma}+\frac{1}{[\gamma]}\Delta_{b}\circ B_{0}^{2\gamma},\\

Plugging (

6.

The purpose of this section is to use the boundary operators to prove CR sharp Sobolev trace inequalities. A key tool in this endeavor is the Dirichlet energy

where

Our first result is a Dirichlet principle for solutions of (

Theorem 6.1

If

For

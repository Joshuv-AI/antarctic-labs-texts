Abstract
. In the work by M. C. Lee, A. Naber, and R. Neumayer [ 7 ] a beautiful ε \varepsilon -regularity theorem is proved under small negative scalar curvature and entropy bounds. In that paper, the d p d_{p} distance for Riemannian manifolds is introduced and the quantitative stability results are given in terms of this notion of distance, with important examples showing why other existing notions of convergence are not adequate in their setting. Due to the presence of an entropy bound, the possibility of long, thin splines forming along a sequence of Riemannian manifolds whose scalar curvature is becoming almost positive is ruled out. It is important to rule out such examples since the d p d_{p} distance is not well behaved in the presences of splines that persist in the limit. Since there are many geometric stability conjectures [ 5 , 8 ] where we want to allow for the presence of splines that persist in the limit, it is crucial to be able to modify the d p d_{p} distance to retain its positive qualities and prevent it from being sensitive to splines. In this paper we explore one such modification of the d p d_{p} distance and give a theorem which allows one to estimate the modified d p d_{p} distance, which we expect to be useful in practice.



1.
Introduction

In the work by M. C. Lee, A. Naber, and R. Neumayer
[
7
]
a beautiful
\varepsilon
-regularity theorem is proved under small negative scalar curvature and entropy bounds. In that paper, the
d_{p}
distance for rectifiable Riemannian manifolds is introduced and the quantitative stability results are given in terms of this notion of distance, with important examples showing why other existing notions of convergence are not adequate in their setting. In particular, many examples were given in dimension
n\geq 4
where shortcuts develop along the sequence, that are not well behaved along Gromov-Hausdroff or Sormani-Wenger Intrinsic Flat convergence, but which are well behaved under the
d_{p}
distance. More recently, D. Kazaras and K. Xu
[
6
]
showed that similarly examples, developing what they call drawstings, can exist in dimension
n=3
with scalar curvature bounds as well. In
[
7
]
, the presence of an entropy bound rules out the possibility of long, thin splines forming along a sequence of Riemannian manifolds whose scalar curvature is becoming almost positive. This is important since the
d_{p}
distance is not well behaved in the presences of splines that persist in the limit. In particular, the distance between points along the spline will become arbitrarily large along such a sequence.

Since there are many geometric stability conjectures stated, by M. Gromov
[
5
]
and C. Sormani
[
8
]
, where we want to allow for the presence of long thin splines which persist in the limit, it is important to be able to modify the
d_{p}
distance to retain its positive qualities and prevent it from being sensitive to splines. In this paper we explore one such possible modification of the
d_{p}
distance and give a theorem which allows one to estimate the modified
d_{p}
distance which we expect to be useful in practice.

Let
M_{0}=(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold. We can define the
d_{p,g}
distance of M. C. Lee, A. Naber, and R. Neumayer
[
7
]
to be

(1)

\displaystyle d_{p,g}(x,y)=\sup\left\{|f(x)-f(y)|:\int_{M}|\nabla^{g}f|_{g}^{p}dV_{g}\leq 1\right\}.

One can notice that if a spline develops along a sequence of Riemannian manifolds then the Sobolev constant for
p>n
of such a sequence cannot be well controlled. In particular, the constant which appears in the Morrey inequality will degenerate and hence the class of functions used to define the
d_{p,g}
metric will have unbounded differences
|f(x)-f(y)|
for points
x,y\in M
along the spline, despite having a controlled
L^{p}
norm of the gradient. This motivates our modification of the
d_{p}
distance where we control the distances
|f(x)-f(y)|
by imposing a Hölder bound in terms of a background metric:

Definition 1.1
.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold,
g_{0}
a smooth Riemannian manifold, and
g
a continuous Riemannian manifold. Then we define the modified
d_{p}
distance between points
x,y\in M

(2)

\displaystyle d

{}_{p,g,g_{0}}^{D}(x,y)=\sup\left\{|f(x)-f(y)|:\mathcal{H}_{p,g_{0}}(f)\leq D,\int_{M}|\nabla^{g}f|_{g}^{p}dV_{g}\leq 1\right\},

where

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{p,g_{0}}(f)=\sup_{x,y\in M}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{d_{g_{0}}(x,y)^{\frac{p-n}{p}}}.

Note that a reasonable choice of
D
is given by
D=\frac{\Diam(M,g)}{\Diam(M,g_{0})^{\frac{p-n}{p}}}
, but we have the freedom to choose this constant as we wish. We also immediately note that if
g_{0,\lambda}=\lambda^{2}g_{0}
and
g_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}g
then
d_{p,g_{\lambda},g_{0,\lambda}}^{D}=\lambda^{\frac{p-n}{p}}d_{p,g,g_{0}}^{D}
. Observe that the
\Diam(M,d_{p,g,g_{0}}^{D})\leq D
. In fact, due to the Hölder restriction on the admissible functions, we see that
d^{D}_{p,g,g_{0}}(x,y)\leq Dd_{g_{0}}(x,y)^{\frac{p-n}{p}}
. It follows by Arzela-Ascoli that the space of functions
d^{D}_{p,g,g_{0}}
is a priori compact as
g
varies, defining a pseudometric as a limit, but may not define a distance function in the limit if tending to zero (See Lemma
2.1
).

By considering admissible functions whose Hölder norm with respect to
g_{0}
is controlled, one can see that for an example with splines and bubbles forming along a sequence, the distance in (
2
) will not be sensitive to these splines or bubbles. In particular, the examples given by the first named author and C. Sormani
[
3
]
which are conformal to a sphere and develop a spline or bubble (Examples 3.5 and 3.7 of
[
3
]
, respectively) would converge to a round sphere in the modified
d_{p}
sense. In addition, the distance in (
2
) retains the positive features of the
d_{p}
distance not being sensitive to shortcuts developing along a sequence as in the examples of
[
6
,
7
]
. We will elaborate on these conclusions after the statement of the main theorem.

A natural class of Riemannian manifolds to study using the modified
d_{p}
distance is given by:

Definition 1.2
.

Given parameters
V_{1},V_{2},D>0
,
q_{1},q_{2}>1
, and a background Riemannian metric
(M^{n},g_{0})
,
n\geq 2
, we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{g_{0}}(q_{1},q_{2},V_{1},V_{2},D)
the collection of compact, connected, oriented Riemannian metric tensors
g
on
M
which satisfy the following:

(1)

\|g\|_{L^{\frac{q_{1}}{2}}(M,g_{0})}\leq V_{1}
,

(2)

\|g^{-1}\|_{L^{\frac{q_{2}}{2}}(M,g_{0})}\leq V_{2}
,

(3)

\Diam(M,g)\leq D
.

When studying geometric stability problems related to rigidity theorems in geometric analysis, a natural background metric presents itself from the rigidity problem one is studying. For instance, in the case of the stability of Geroch’s conjecture a flat torus is a natural background metric
g_{0}
, for the stability of the positive mass theorem Euclidean space is the natrual background metric
g_{0}
, and in the case of stability of Llarull’s theorem the round sphere would be the background metric
g_{0}
. Hence for each of these stability questions there is a natural way to define the class
\mathcal{N}_{g_{0}}(q_{1},q_{2},V_{1},V_{2},D)
where
q_{1}
and
q_{2}
are chosen depending on the problem. Our goal in this paper then is to provide a result which allows one to establish
L^{p}
convergence of a sequence of inverse metrics for Riemannian manifolds in the class
\mathcal{N}(q_{1},q_{2},V_{1},V_{2},D)
in order to conclude
d_{p,g_{i},g}^{D}
convergence.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M^{n}
be a compact, connected, and oriented manifold,
g_{i},g_{0}
continuous Riemannian metrics,
p>3n
,
n\geq 3
. Then there exists a
\bar{D}(g_{0})
so that for
D\geq\bar{D}
if

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|g_{j}|_{g_{0}}^{\frac{n}{2}}dV_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C,

and

(5)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|g_{j}^{-1}-g_{0}^{-1}|_{g_{0}}^{\frac{n(p-1)}{2}}dV_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,

then

(6)

\displaystyle d_{p,g_{j},g_{0}}^{D}\rightarrow d_{p,g_{0},g_{0}}^{D},

uniformly.

Remark 1.4
.

It was observed by the first named author in
[
2
]
that if one has an
L^{p}
bound
\int_{M}|g_{j}|_{g_{0}}^{\frac{p}{2}}dV_{g_{0}}\leq C
for
p>n
then one can obtain Hölder bounds on the distance function
d_{g_{j}}
in terms of
d_{g_{0}}
. Since examples involving splines and bubbles do not satisfy such Hölder bounds, it is important that assumption (
4
) is for the critical power so that examples involving splines and bubbles are allowed in Theorem
1.3
. In fact, the first named author and C. Sormani
[
3
]
give examples which show that under a
L^{\frac{n}{2}}
bound on
g_{j}
, splines and bubbles can form. So Theorem
1.3
shows that the modified
d_{p}
distance of (
2
) is not sensitive to such behavior. Since M-C Lee, A. Naber, and R. Neumayer
[
7
]
assume a entropy bound they are able to obtain
L^{p}
bounds on the metric tensors for high powers of
p
which is not surprising since an entropy bound will rule out splines and bubbles.

Remark 1.5
.

In
[
7
]
, many examples with various collapsing along a line phenomenon were given with
n\geq 4
which
d_{p}
converge to smooth Riemannian manifolds and are shown to satisfy
L^{p}
bounds and convergence, similar to the requirements of Theorem
1.3
. Recently, D. Kazaras and K. Xu
[
6
]
have shown that similar collapsing examples, called drawstrings, are possible in dimension
3
. One can check that the examples in
[
6
]
satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
. Hence the modified
d_{p}
distance given in this paper is well suited to problems where splines, bubbles, and drawstrings occur.

For example, in Theorem 1.19 of Lee, Naber, and Neumayer
[
7
]
we see a torus stability result where almost non-negative scalar curvature and a bound on the entropy is assumed. The entropy bound removes the possibility of splines and bubbles forming and is generally a rather strong assumption. Using the main theorem of this paper we can trade out the entropy bound for
q_{1}=n
and
q_{2}=n(p-1)
for
p>3n
which will allow for the possibility of splines and bubbles. Then one would need to figure out how to use the almost non-negativity of the scalar curvature in order to prove the inverse metric convergence of Theorem
1.3
in order to conclude modified
d_{p}
stability. Similar applications to the stability of the positive mass theorem should also be possible allowing for splines, bubbles, and drawstrings.

Remark 1.6
.

We note that it is also possible to choose
L^{p}
bounds in the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
which also work for
n=2
but those powers are not conveniently stated and the main purpose of these results is to apply them to the case where
n\geq 3
. We also note that by reworking the arguments given in the proof of Theorem
1.3
one is able to assume a power slightly under the critical power in (
4
) at the cost of more complicated powers in the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
.

Remark 1.7
.

In the course of proving Theorem
1.3
, in particular in Theorem
3.1
, we show that the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
also imply
L^{\frac{k}{2}}
,
k<n
convergence of
g_{j}
to
g_{0}
. If one is in a situation where adding
g_{j}\geq\left(1-\frac{C}{j}\right)g_{0}
is a natural hypothesis then by previous work of the authors
[
1
]
, we know that
d_{g_{j}}
converges pointwise almost everywhere to
d_{g_{0}}
with a Hausdorff measure estimate on the size of the set where pointwise convergence does not hold.

We now give a precise definition of what it means for a sequence of Riemannian manifolds to converge in the modified
d_{p}
sense.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
D>0
,
p>n
,
(M,g_{0})
be a smooth Riemannian manifold. We define the uniform modified
d_{p}
distance between two continuous Riemannian metric tensors on
M
:

(7)

\displaystyle d_{p,g_{0}}^{sup,D}((M,g_{1}),(M,g_{2}))=\sup_{x,y\in M}|d_{p,g_{1},g_{0}}^{D}(x,y)-d_{p,g_{2},g_{0}}^{D}(x,y)|.

2.
Background

In
[
7
]
, many useful properties of
d_{p}
distance are established. Since the only modification we make here is to further restrict the class of admissible functions in the definition we will see that these important properties are retained for the modified
d_{p}
distance.

2.1.
Properties of Modified
d_{p}
Distance

Let us begin by observing a simple, yet very useful, consequence of the definition of
d^{D}_{p,g,g_{0}}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,g_{0})
be a compact, connected, oriented Riemannian manifold,
p>n
, and
D>0
. Then, the space of distance functions
d^{D}_{p,g,g_{0}}
is pre-compact in the space of Riemannian metrics
g
on
M
.

Proof.

It follows from the definition that for any
g
we have

(8)

d^{D}_{p,g,g_{0}}(x,y)\leq Dd_{g_{0}}(x,y)^{\frac{p-n}{p}}.

Thus, the family satisfies the hypotheses of the Arzela-Ascoli theorem.
∎

We would like to be able to show that the distances themselves converge as
p\rightarrow\infty
. In Remark 2.33 of
[
7
]
they mention that for reasonable spaces one should find
d_{p}\rightarrow d
as
p\rightarrow\infty
. We start by showing that pointwise convergence always holds for
p\rightarrow\infty
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(M,g)
,
(M,g_{0})
be compact, connected, oriented Riemannian manifolds, and
(M,d_{p,g,g_{0}})
the corresponding modified
d_{p}
distance metric space. If
d_{g}\leq D_{0}d_{g_{0}}
then for
x_{1},x_{2}\in M
and every
D\geq D_{0}+2
we find

(9)

\displaystyle d_{p,g,g_{0}}^{D}(x_{1},x_{2})\rightarrow d_{g}(x_{1},x_{2}),

as
p\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

For each
q>n
consider a test function
f_{q}
so that

(10)

\displaystyle d_{q,g,g_{0}}^{D}(x_{1},x_{2})=|f_{q}(x_{1})-f_{q}(x_{2})|+\varepsilon.

By definition, we have that

(11)

\int_{M}\left\lvert\nabla f_{q}\right\rvert^{q}dV_{g}\leq 1

and

(12)

\mathcal{H}_{q,g_{0}}(f_{q})\leq D.

Now, by Hölder’s inequality for
1<p<q<\infty

(13)

\left(\int_{M}\left\lvert\nabla^{g}f_{q}\right\rvert^{p}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left\lvert M\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{M}\left\lvert\nabla^{g}f_{q}\right\rvert^{q}dV_{g}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

which implies that for
q>p
we have

(14)

\|\nabla^{g}f_{q}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lvert M\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}}

Therefore, we see that for any fixed
p
we have

(15)

\limsup_{q\rightarrow\infty}\|\nabla^{g}f_{q}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lvert M\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{p}}

Next, by the Poincare inequality we know

(16)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{M}|f_{q}-\bar{f}_{q}|^{p}dV_{g}\leq C_{p}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{M}|\nabla^{g}f_{q}|_{g}^{p}dV_{g}\leq C_{p},

and hence
\|f_{q}-\bar{f}_{q}\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C
. So we can extract a subsequence so that

(17)

\displaystyle f_{q}-\bar{f}_{q}\rightarrow f_{\infty},\quad\nabla^{g}f_{q}\rightharpoonup h_{\infty},\quad\text{ in }L^{p},\quad\text{for all $1<p<q$.}

By successively picking subsequences as
p\rightarrow\infty
and using the lower semi-continuity of the norm under weak convergence, it follows that for all
p
we have

(18)

\|h_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}}\leq\left\lvert M\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{p}}

Thus, using the fact that
\displaystyle\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\|h_{\infty}\|_{L^{p}}=\|h_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, we see that
\|h_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
. In particular, since the weak gradient of
f_{\infty}
is
h_{\infty}
, we see that
f_{\infty}
is 1-Lipschitz.
Next, as each
f_{q}-\bar{f}_{q}
converges to
f_{\infty}
in
W^{1,p}
for all
p
, by picking
p
to be large enough, we can ensure that this convergence is uniform by applying Morrey’s inequality to
f_{\infty}-\bigl(f_{q}-\bar{f}_{q}\bigr)
. In particular, the convergence is pointwise, so

(19)

\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}|f_{q}(x)-f_{q}(y)|=\lim_{q\rightarrow\infty}|f_{q}(x)-\bar{f}_{q}-(f_{q}(y)-\bar{f}_{q})|=|f_{\infty}(x)-f_{\infty}(y)|.

Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
we have that

(20)

\limsup_{q\rightarrow\infty}d_{q,g,g_{0}}^{D}(x,y)\leq d_{g}(x,y)+\varepsilon.

On the otherhand, it is well known that

(21)

d_{g}(x,y)=\sup\left\{\left\lvert f(x)-f(y)\right\rvert:\operatorname{Lip}_{d_{g}}(f)\leq 1\right\}.

Let
f_{\varepsilon}
be a 1-Lipschitz function with respect to
d_{g}
such that

(22)

d_{g}(x,y)\leq\left\lvert f_{\varepsilon}(x)-f_{\varepsilon}(y)\right\rvert+\varepsilon.

Then, for all
p
we have that
\tilde{f}_{p,\varepsilon}=\left\lvert M\right\rvert^{-\frac{1}{p}}f_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

(23)

\int\left\lvert\nabla\tilde{f}_{p,\varepsilon}\right\rvert^{p}dV_{g}\leq 1

and

(24)

\mathcal{H}_{p,g}(\tilde{f}_{p,\varepsilon})\leq\left\lvert M\right\rvert^{-\frac{1}{p}}\Diam_{g}(M)^{\frac{n}{p}}=\left(\frac{\Diam_{g}(M)^{n}}{\left\lvert M\right\rvert_{g}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

For large enough
p
, the term on the right hand side will be arbitrarily close to
1
. Since
d_{g}\leq D_{0}d_{g_{0}}
we have for
0<\delta<1
that
H_{p,g_{0}}\bigl(\tilde{f}_{p,\varepsilon}\bigr)\leq D_{0}^{\frac{p-n}{p}}+\delta\leq D
. Thus, our choice of
D
ensures that
\tilde{f}_{p,\varepsilon}
is a test function for every
p
large enough. Thus, we have that

(25)

d^{D}_{p,g,g_{0}}(x,y)\geq\bigl|\tilde{f}_{p,\varepsilon}(x)-\tilde{f}_{p,\varepsilon}(y)\bigr|\geq\left\lvert M\right\rvert^{-\frac{1}{p}}(d_{g}(x,y)+\varepsilon).

This gives us that

(26)

\liminf_{p\rightarrow\infty}d^{D}_{p,g,g_{0}}(x,y)\geq d_{g}(x,y)+\varepsilon,

and so the result follows.
∎

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
(M,g)
,
(M,g_{0})
be compact, connected, oriented Riemannian manifolds, and
(M,d_{p,g,g_{0}})
the corresponding modified
d_{p}
distance metric space. If
d_{g}\leq D_{0}d_{g_{0}}
and
D\geq D_{0}+2
, then
d^{D}_{p,g,g_{0}}
converges uniformly to
d_{g}
as
p
tends to infinity.

Proof.

Notice that the conclusion of Lemma
2.1
holds even if we let the exponent
p
approach infinity. This combined with the pointwise convergence in Lemma
2.2
shows that the convergence is actually uniform.
∎

We now make a simple observation about how the modified
d_{p,g,g_{0}}^{D}
distance scales.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
g_{\lambda}=\lambda^{2}g
and
g_{0,\lambda}=\lambda^{2}g_{0}
then we find that for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in M

(27)

\displaystyle d_{p,g_{\lambda},g_{0,\lambda}}(x_{1},x_{2})=\lambda^{\frac{p-n}{p}}d_{p,g,g_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2}).

Proof.

We first observe that

(28)

\displaystyle\int_{M}|\nabla^{g}f|_{g_{\lambda}}^{p}dV_{g_{\lambda}}=\lambda^{n-p}\int_{M}|\nabla^{g}f|_{g}^{p}dV_{g}.

Hence if
f
is the admissible function so that

(29)

\displaystyle d_{p,g,g_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2})=|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})|-\varepsilon,

we see that for
f_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\frac{p-n}{p}}f
is admissible for
g_{\lambda}
, since additionally
\mathcal{H}_{p,g_{0,\lambda}}(f_{\lambda})\leq D
, and so

(30)

\displaystyle d_{p,g_{\lambda},g_{0,\lambda}}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle\geq|f_{\lambda}(x_{1})-f_{\lambda}(x_{2})|

(31)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{p-n}{p}}|f(x_{1})-f(x_{2})|

(32)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{p-n}{p}}(d_{p,g,g_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2})+\varepsilon).

Since this is true for all
\varepsilon>0
we find

(33)

\displaystyle d_{p,g_{\lambda},g_{0,\lambda}}(x_{1},x_{2})\geq\lambda^{\frac{p-n}{p}}d_{p,g,g_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2}).

By reworking the argument from
g_{\lambda}
to
g
a second time one obtains the desired result.
∎

We now note a convergence result where we assume smooth convergence. This will be used in the following proposition.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(M,g_{i})
and
(M,h_{i})
be two sequences of compact, connected, oriented Riemannian manifolds such that
g_{i}
smoothly converges to
g_{\infty}
and
h_{i}
smoothly converges to
h_{\infty}
. Then, we have that
d^{D}_{p,g_{i},h_{i}}
uniformly converges to
d^{D}_{p,g_{\infty},h_{\infty}}
.

Proof.

First, notice that the conclusion of Lemma
2.1
holds in this scenario, so that the family of metric functions is pre-compact. Therefore, in order to establish the result, it suffice to establish pointwise convergence.

We begin by showing that for any
x,y
in
M
we have

(34)

\liminf_{i\rightarrow\infty}d^{D}_{p,g_{i},h_{i}}(x,y)\geq d^{D}_{p,g_{\infty},h_{\infty}}(x,y).

To see this, consider any test function, say
f
, for the distance
d^{D}_{p,g_{\infty},h_{\infty}}
. Since both
g_{i}
and
h_{i}
converge smoothly to
g_{\infty}
and
h_{\infty}
, respectively, it follows that one may pick a sequence of numbers
C_{i}
tending towards
1
such that
f_{i}=C_{i}f
are test functions for
d^{D}_{p,g_{i},h_{i}}
. Since
d^{D}_{p,g_{i},h_{i}}(x,y)\geq\left\lvert f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(y)\right\rvert
, we get (
34
).

Observe that since
g_{i}
smoothly converges
g_{\infty}
, so do the inverse metrics. In particular, we can choose a subsequence such that

(35)

(1-i^{-1})^{2}\leq\frac{g^{-1}_{i}(df,df)}{g^{-1}_{\infty}(df,df)}\leq(1+i^{-1})^{2}

for all
f
. Thus, we see that

(36)

(1-i^{-1})\leq\frac{\left\lvert\nabla^{g_{i}}f\right\rvert_{g_{i}}}{\left\lvert\nabla^{g_{\infty}}f\right\rvert_{g_{\infty}}}\leq(1+i^{-1})

for all
f
.
Similarly, we see that

(37)

(1+i^{-1})^{-n}\leq\frac{dV_{g_{i}}}{dV_{g_{\infty}}}\leq(1-i^{-1})^{-n}

Now, let
f_{i}
be a sequence of test functions for
d^{D}_{p,g_{i},h_{i}}
such that

(38)

d^{D}_{p,g_{i},h_{i}}(x,y)\leq\left\lvert f_{i}(x)-f_{i}(y)\right\rvert+i^{-1}.

Then the sequence
f_{i}
is uniformly bounded in
W^{1,p}(g_{\infty})
, and so a subsequence weakly converges to some limit
f_{\infty}
. Additionally, since the
h_{i}
smoothly converge to
h_{\infty}
, we see that
\mathcal{H}_{p,h_{\infty}}(f_{i})
is uniformly bounded and hence an additional subsequence converges uniformly to
f_{\infty}
. Finally, the lower-semicontinuity of the norm now implies that
f_{\infty}
is a test function. Hence we see that

(39)

\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}d^{D}_{p,g_{i},h_{i}}(x,y)\leq\left\lvert f_{\infty}(x)-f_{\infty}(y)\right\rvert\leq d^{D}_{p,g_{\infty},h_{\infty}}(x,y).

∎

Now we would like to know whether or not having isometric modified
d_{p}
distances is enough to know that the Riemannian metrics are isometric.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(M,g)
,
(M,h)
, and
(M,g_{0})
be compact , connected, oriented Riemannian manifolds. Then for

2.2.
Useful Technical Lemmas

Using the theory in
[
4
]
one finds a useful characterization of the norm of the gradient of a function
f\in W^{1,p}
as the essential infimum of upper gradients of
f
. We use this characterization as an important technical point in the proof of Theorem
3.3
. In fact, in the theory developed in
[
4
]
, it is shown that a function’s minimal upper gradient has this characterization. The result then follows by showing that for Sobolev functions, the minimal upper gradient and the norm of the weak gradient coincide almost everywhere. The proof of this result for Euclidean space is the content of Corollary 2.1.24 together with Proposition 2.1.36 and Theorem 2.1.37 in
[
4
]
. Although
[
4
]
only develops the theory of
W^{1,2}
explicitly, the machinery developed there allows one to define
W^{1,p}
for all
p\geq 1
. The proof below is a modification of the approach found in
[
4
]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

The Sobolev space defined in
[
4
]
and the standard Sobolev space agree on smooth Riemannian manifolds. In addition, this implies that the norm of the gradient of a function
f\in W^{1,p}
is the essential infimum of upper gradients of
f
.

Proof.

Let us first fix some notation. For
f\in W^{1,p}\cap C^{\infty}(M)
we let
\nabla f
denote the classical gradient of
f
. For
f
in the Sobolev space
S^{p}\cap L^{p}
as defined in
[
4
]
, we let
\left\lvert Df\right\rvert
denote the minimal weak upper-gradient of
f
.

We first claim that for
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, we have that
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert=\left\lvert Df\right\rvert
almost everywhere. To see this, first observe that by definition
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert
is an upper gradient for
f
, and so we must have that
\left\lvert Df\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert
almost everywhere. Let
X=\left\{x:\left\lvert Df\right\rvert<\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert\right\}
. Thus, we see that for almost every
x
such that
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert(x)=0
, we actually have equality. So, we may focus on the set
Y=X\cap\left\{x:\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert(x)>0\right\}
. For any
x
such that
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert(x)>0
, we may find a
\delta>0
and an open neighborhood
U
about
x
, with compact closure, such that for all
y\in U
we have
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert(y)>\delta
. Let
\Phi
be the flow generated by
\frac{\nabla f}{\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert}
with initial points in
U
. Since
U
has compact closure and
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert
is continuous, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
\Phi:U\times[0,\varepsilon]\rightarrow M
exists, and is smooth. In fact, by picking
\varepsilon>0
small enough we ensure that the Jacobian is non-vanishing, and so we may guarantee that the map
y\rightarrow\Phi(y,t)
defines a test family of curves with measure
\frac{1}{\left\lvert U\right\rvert_{g}}\Vol_{g}|_{U}
in the sense of
[
4
]
. Consider now that on one hand

(40)

\int_{U}f\left(\Phi(y,\varepsilon)\right)-f\left(\Phi(y,0)\right)dV_{g}=\int_{U}\int^{\varepsilon}_{0}\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert\left(\Phi(y,t)\right)dtdV_{g},

while on the other hand we have by definition of
\left\lvert Df\right\rvert
that

(41)

\int_{U}f\left(\Phi(y,\varepsilon)\right)-f\left(\Phi(y,0)\right)dV_{g}\leq\int_{U}\int^{\varepsilon}_{0}\left\lvert Df\right\rvert\left(\Phi(y,t)\right)dtdV_{g}.

Notice that
\Phi(y,t)
is the evaluation map on the family of curves as in
[
4
]
. Then, by subtracting Equation (
41
) from Equation (
40
) and using the fact that
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert\geq\left\lvert Df\right\rvert
almost everywhere, we see
Y\cap U
has measure zero. This in turn shows that
X
has measure zero. Thus,
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert=\left\lvert Df\right\rvert
almost everywhere.

Now, it follows directly from a partition of unity argument and the proof of Proposition 2.1.36 C) in
[
4
]
that for any
f\in W^{1,p}
we may find a sequence of smooth functions
f_{j}
and
G_{j}
such that
f_{j}\rightarrow f
in
W^{1,p}
,
G_{j}\rightarrow\left\lvert Df\right\rvert
in
L^{p}
and

(42)

\left\lvert Df_{j}\right\rvert\leq G_{j}.

Since
\left\lvert Df_{j}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\nabla f_{j}\right\rvert
almost everywhere, we actually have that

(43)

\left\lvert\nabla f_{j}\right\rvert\leq G_{j}

almost everywhere. Since
f_{j}\rightarrow f
and
\left\lvert\nabla f_{j}\right\rvert\rightarrow\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert
in
L^{p}
, it follows from
[
4
]
that
\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert
is an upper gradient for
f
, and so
\left\lvert Df\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\nabla f\right\rvert
. However, from Equation (
43
) the opposite inequality must also hold. Therefore, we have equality almost everywhere.
∎

We will often want to compute the norm of
g
and
g^{-1}
with respect to a background metric
g_{0}
so we review the definition of these norms.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
g,g_{0}
be Riemannian metrics so that
e_{1},...,e_{n}
is an orthonormal frame with respect to
g_{0}
that diagonalizes
g
, i.e.

(44)

\displaystyle g(e_{i},e_{i})=\lambda_{i}^{2},

for eigenvalues
0\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq...\leq\lambda_{n}
then

(45)

\displaystyle|g|_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{4}+...+\lambda_{n}^{4}},

(46)

\displaystyle|g^{-1}|_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{-4}+...+\lambda_{n}^{-4}},

and

(47)

\displaystyle\det(g)_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{2}...\lambda_{n}^{2},

(48)

\displaystyle\det(g^{-1})_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}^{-2}...\lambda_{n}^{-2}.

We now establish a simple way of estimating the determinant of
g
by the norm of
g
, with respect to
g_{0}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
g,g_{0}
be Riemannian metrics then

(49)

\displaystyle\det(g)_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq n^{-\frac{n}{2}}|g|_{g_{0}}^{n},

(50)

\displaystyle\det(g^{-1})_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq n^{-\frac{n}{2}}|g^{-1}|_{g_{0}}^{n},

Proof.

We will prove the first inequality and the second inequality will follow by a similar argument. By Definition
2.8
we can consider the diagonal matrix with entries
\lambda_{1}^{2},...,\lambda_{n}^{2}
and by the determinant trace inequality we find

(51)

\displaystyle\det(g)_{g_{0}}^{\frac{1}{n}}=(\lambda_{1}^{2}...\lambda_{n}^{2})^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{n}(\lambda_{1}^{2}+...+\lambda_{n}^{2}),

and now we use the fact that the
\ell_{1}
norm is bounded by
\sqrt{n}
times the
\ell_{2}
norm to find

(52)

\displaystyle\det(g)_{g_{0}}^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{n}\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{4}+...+\lambda_{n}^{4}}=n^{-\frac{1}{2}}|g|_{g_{0}},

and the result follows by raising both sides to the
n
th power.
∎

We now establish a useful observation for when one is comparing norms of gradients with respect to two different metrics.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
g
be a Riemannian metric,
f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a function, and
\omega
a
(0,2)
tensor then

(53)

\displaystyle\omega(df,df)\leq|\omega|_{g}|\nabla^{g}f|_{g}^{2}.

In addition, if
h
is another Riemannian metric then

(54)

\displaystyle|g^{-1}|_{h}=|h|_{g}.

Proof.

We define a
(0,2)
tensor
\nabla^{g}f\otimes\nabla^{g}f
so that if
\alpha,\beta
are
1-
forms then

(55)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}f\otimes\nabla^{g}f)(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha(\nabla^{g}f)\beta(\nabla^{g}f),

and hence for
dx_{i},dx_{j}
we find

(56)

\displaystyle(\nabla^{g}f\otimes\nabla^{g}f)(dx_{i},dx_{j})=dx_{i}(\nabla^{g}f)dx_{j}(\nabla^{g}f)=g^{il}\partial_{l}fg^{jk}\partial_{k}f.

Now we notice that

(57)

\displaystyle g(\omega,\nabla^{g}f\otimes\nabla^{g}f)\leq|\omega|_{g}|\nabla^{g}f\otimes\nabla^{g}f|_{g}=|\omega|_{g}|\nabla^{g}f|_{g}^{2},

and lastly we see that

(58)

\displaystyle g(\omega,\nabla^{g}f\otimes\nabla^{g}f)

\displaystyle=g_{pj}g_{qm}\omega^{pq}g^{ij}\partial_{i}fg^{lm}\partial_{l}f

(59)

\displaystyle=\delta_{p}^{i}\delta_{q}^{l}\omega^{pq}\partial_{i}f\partial_{l}f=\omega(df,df).

For the second result, we can calculate

(60)

\displaystyle|g^{-1}|_{h}=h_{ij}h_{pq}g^{ip}g^{jq}=|h|_{g}.

∎

We now also review a simple inequality which will be used repeatedly in the next section. We suspect this is a well known theorem but give a short proof for completeness.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
a,b
be in
\mathbb{R}
and let
t\in(0,1]
. Then, we have that

(61)

\left\lvert\left\lvert a\right\rvert^{t}-\left\lvert b\right\rvert^{t}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert a-b\right\rvert^{t}.

Proof.

Let
d(x,y)=\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert
. Then, it is well known that the snowflake metric
d_{t}(x,y)=d(x,y)^{t}
satisfies all of the properties of a metric. In particular, we have the reverse triangle inequality:

(62)

\left\lvert d_{t}(x,a)-d_{t}(x,b)\right\rvert\leq d_{t}(a,b).

Choosing
x=0
gives the desired result.
∎

3.

From Lemma

\displaystyle d_{p,g_{j},g_{0}}^{D}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle\geq|\varphi_{j}(x_{1})-\varphi_{j}(x_{2})|

\displaystyle=C_{j}|\varphi(x_{1})-\varphi(x_{2})|=C_{j}(d_{p,g_{0},g_{0}}^{D}(x_{1},x_{2})-\varepsilon).

\displaystyle C_{j}^{-1}d_{p,g_{j},g_{0}}^{D}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle\geq d_{p,g_{0},g_{0}}^{D}(x_{1},x_{2}).

Now by taking the liminf on both sides of (

∎

The second part of showing that pointwise convergence holds is to demonstrate an upper bound. This is more difficult, because now we must work with a sequence of test functions for the metrics
g_{i}
instead of a single test function for the metric

. Analyzing such sequences requires using the Arzela-Ascoli theorem in some form.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

be a compact, connected, oriented manifold,

a smooth Riemannian metric, and

a sequence of continuous Riemannian metrics. If

,
p>\frac{5n}{2}
,
\eta=\frac{5}{12}n
,

(105)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|g_{j}^{-1}|_{g_{0}}^{\frac{n\eta}{p-\eta}}dV_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{p-\eta}{2p}}

\displaystyle\leq C,

(106)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|g_{j}|_{g_{0}}^{\frac{n}{2}}dV_{g_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C,

and

(107)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|g_{j}^{-1}-g_{0}^{-1}|_{g_{0}}^{\frac{p}{(p-1)}}dV_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow 0,

then for
x_{1},x_{2}\in M
we find

(108)

\displaystyle\limsup_{i\rightarrow\infty}d_{p,g_{j},g_{0}}^{D}(x_{1},x_{2})\leq d_{p,g_{0},g_{0}}^{D}(x_{1},x_{2}).

We note that with the choice of

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

From Lemma
2.1
, a subsequence of
d_{p,g_{i},g_{0}}^{D}
converges uniformly to a symmetric function
d:M\times M\rightarrow[0,\infty)
which satisfies the triangle inequality. By combining Theorem
3.1
with Theorem
3.3
we see pointwise convergence of
d_{p,g_{i},g_{0}}^{D}
to
d_{p,g_{0}}=d_{p,g_{0},g_{0}}^{D}
for
D
chosen to be larger than
\bar{D}
, the Morrey constant for
g_{0}
. Hence
d=d_{p,g_{0},g_{0}}^{D}
and the original subsequence must not have been necessary since every convergence subsequence has the same limit. So we find that
d_{p,g_{i},g_{0}}^{D}
converges uniformly to
d_{p,g_{0},g_{0}}^{D}
, as desired.
∎

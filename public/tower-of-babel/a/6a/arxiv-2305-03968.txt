Quasilinear Dirichlet systems with competing operators and convection
Laura Gambera, Salvatore A. Marano, Dumitru Motreanu

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a quasi-linear Dirichlet system with possible competing ( p , q ) (p,q) -Laplacians and convections. Due to the lack of ellipticity, monotonicity, and variational structure, the standard approaches to the existence of weak solutions cannot be adopted. Nevertheless, through an approximation procedure and a corollary of Brouwer’s fixed point theorem we show that the problem admits a solution in a suitable sense.

1.
Introduction and main result

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 2
, be a bounded domain with a Lipschitz boundary
\partial\Omega
, let
1<q_{i}<p_{i}<N
, and let
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2
. Consider the system

(P)

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{p_{1}}u+\mu_{1}\Delta_{q_{1}}u&=f_{1}(x,u,v,\nabla u,\nabla v)&&\quad\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
-\Delta_{p_{2}}v+\mu_{2}\Delta_{q_{2}}v&=f_{2}(x,u,v,\nabla u,\nabla v)&&\quad\mbox{in}\;\;\Omega,\\
u=v&=0&&\quad\mbox{on}\;\;\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Delta_{r}u:=\mathrm{div}\,(|\nabla u|^{r-2}\nabla u)
, with
1<r<+\infty
, denotes the so-called
r
-Laplacian while
f_{i}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}\to\mathbb{R}
,
i=1,2
, are Carathéodory functions satisfying appropriate conditions; see

(

H
1

)

–

(

H
2

)

below. Since
q_{i}<p_{i}
, we seek solutions
(u,v)
to (
P
) in the space

(1.1)

X:=W^{1,p_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)\times W^{1,p_{2}}_{0}(\Omega).

The involved differential operator

A_{i,\mu_{i}}(w):=-\Delta_{p_{i}}w+\mu_{i}\Delta_{q_{i}}w,\;\;w\in W^{1,p_{i}}_{0}(\Omega),

exhibits completely different behaviors according to whether

\mu_{i}<0,\quad\mu_{i}=0,\quad\mbox{or}\;\;\mu_{i}>0.

If
\mu_{i}<0
then
A_{i,\mu_{i}}
is basically patterned
[
4
, Section 2.1]
after the well known
(p_{i},q_{i})
-Laplacian, namely
\Delta_{p_{i}}w+\Delta_{q_{i}}w
, which turns out non-homogeneous because
p_{i}\neq q_{i}
. When
\mu_{i}=0
it coincides with the classical negative
p_{i}
-Laplacian. Both cases have been widely investigated and meaningful results are by now available in the literature; see, e.g.,
[
5
, Chapter 6]
. On the contrary, for
\mu_{i}>0
the operator
A_{i,\mu_{i}}
contains the
difference
between the
q_{i}
-Laplacian and the
p_{i}
-Laplacian. It is usually called competitive and, as already pointed out in
[
3
,
6
]
, doesn’t comply with any ellipticity or monotonicity condition. In fact,
given
u_{0}\in W^{1,p_{i}}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}
and chosen
u:=tu_{0}
,
t>0
, the expression

\langle A_{i,\mu_{i}}(u),u\rangle=t^{p}\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{p}^{p}-\mu_{i}t^{q}\|u_{0}\|_{q}^{q}

turns out negative for
t
small and positive when
t
is large. Hence, if
\mu_{i}>0
, problem (
P
) gathers at least two challenging technical features:

•

driving operators are neither elliptic nor monotone. This prevents to use surjectivity results for pseudo-monotone operators, nonlinear regularity theory, besides comparison principles.

•

right-hand sides depend on the gradient of solutions, so that variational techniques cannot be enforced directly.

To overcome these difficulties we first exploit the Galerkin method, thus working in a sequence
\{X_{n}\}
of finite dimensional sub-spaces of
X
. A
finite dimensional approximate solution

(u_{n},v_{n})\in X_{n}
to (
P
) is obtained for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
via a corollary of the Brouwer fixed point theorem. Next, an appropriate passage to the limit yields a solution in a generalized sense; cf. Definitions
2.6
–
2.7
.

The idea of weakening the notion of solution to treat more general situations is classical; cf. for instance
[
9
, p. 183]
and
[
10
,
11
]
.

As far as we know, equations driven by competing operators have previously been investigated only in
[
3
,
6
]
. The main difference between them concerns right-hand sides. The paper
[
3
]
doesn’t treat convective reactions. Solutions are thus obtained through Galerkin’s method and Ekeland’s variational principle. In
[
6
]
, instead, any variational approach is forbidden, because the nonlinearity depends on the gradient of solutions.

This work continues the study started in
[
6
]
, treating
convective systems driven by competing operators
. Section 2, beyond some auxiliary results, contains the notions of generalized and strong generalized solution to (
P
), adapted from Definitions 2.3 and 2.5, respectively, in
[
6
]
. A sequence of finite dimensional approximate solutions is constructed in Section 3. Finally, Section 4 deals with a limit procedure that provides a generalized solution to (
P
). Under a growth condition stronger than

(

H
1

)

(see

(

H
1

′

)

below) we next show that (
P
) admits a strong generalized solution, which becomes a weak one as soon as
\mu_{1}\vee\mu_{2}<0
. The section ends by exhibiting a natural class of reactions that fulfill our hypotheses; cf. Example
4.3
.

2.
Preliminaries and hypotheses

Let
Y
be a real Banach space and let
Y^{*}
be its topological dual, with duality brackets
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
. An operator
B:Y\to Y^{*}
is said to be:

•

bounded
when it maps bounded sets into bounded sets.

•

monotone
if
\langle B(y)-B(z),y-z\rangle\geq 0
for all
y,z\in Y
.

•

pseudo-monotone
when
y_{n}\rightharpoonup y
in
Y
and
\displaystyle{\limsup_{n\to\infty}}\langle B(y_{n}),y_{n}-y\rangle\leq 0
imply

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle B(y_{n}),y_{n}-z\rangle\geq\langle B(y),y-z\rangle\;\;\forall\,z\in Y.

•

of type
(\mathrm{S})_{+}
provided

y_{n}\rightharpoonup y\;\;\mbox{in $Y$,}\;\;\limsup_{n\to\infty}\langle B(y_{n}),y_{n}-y\rangle\leq 0\implies y_{n}\to y\;\;\mbox{in $Y$.}

The following consequence of Brouwer’s fixed point theorem will play a basic role in the sequel. For the proof we refer to
[
9
, p. 37]

.

Let

Then the equation

admits a solution

.

The symbol

Given a real number

3.
Approximate solutions via Galerkin’s method

Since the Banach space

defined in (

\{X_{n}\}
of vector sub-spaces of

such that

•

\dim(X_{n})<\infty\;\;\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}

•

X_{n}\subseteq X_{n+1}\;\;\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}

•

\overline{\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}}=X

Evidently, we may suppose
X_{n}=U_{n}\times V_{n}
, with
U_{n}\subseteq W^{1,p_{1}}_{0}(\Omega)
and

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
{\rm(H_{1})}
–
{\rm(H_{2})}
be satisfied and let
(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

Then for every

&\langle B_{2}(u,v),\psi\rangle=\langle-\Delta_{p_{2}}v+\mu_{2}\Delta_{q_{2}}v,\psi\rangle-\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{N}_{f_{2}}(u,v)\psi\,{\rm d}x\end{split}\quad\forall\,(u,v),(\varphi,\psi)\in X_{n}.

Claim:

In fact,

(

)

&\phantom{PP}+\int_{\Omega}c_{2}\left(|\nabla\varphi|^{p_{1}}+|\nabla\psi|^{p_{2}}\right)\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}d_{2}\left(|\varphi|^{p_{1}}+|\psi|^{p_{2}}\right)\,{\rm d}x+\|\gamma_{2}\|_{1}\\
&\leq c_{1}\left(\|\varphi\|^{p_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}+\|\psi\|^{p_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}\right)+d_{1}\left(\lambda_{1,p_{1}}^{-1}\|\varphi\|^{p_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}+\lambda_{1,p_{2}}^{-1}\|\psi\|^{p_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}\right)\\
&\phantom{PP}+c_{2}\left(\|\varphi\|^{p_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}+\|\psi\|^{p_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}\right)+d_{2}\left(\lambda_{1,p_{1}}^{-1}\|\varphi\|^{p_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}+\lambda_{1,p_{2}}^{-1}\|\psi\|^{p_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}\right)+\|\gamma_{1}\|_{1}+\|\gamma_{2}\|_{1}\\
&=\left[c_{1}+c_{2}+(d_{1}+d_{2})\lambda_{1,p_{1}}^{-1}\right]\|\varphi\|^{p_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}+\left[c_{1}+c_{2}+(d_{1}+d_{2})\lambda_{1,p_{2}}^{-1}\right]\|\psi\|^{p_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}+C,\end{split}

where
C:=\|\gamma_{1}\|_{1}+\|\gamma_{2}\|_{1}
. Since
q_{i}<p_{i}
, using Hölder’s inequality we thus obtain

\begin{split}\langle&B(\varphi,\psi),(\varphi,\psi)\rangle\\
&\geq\left[1-c_{1}-c_{2}-(d_{1}+d_{2})\lambda_{1,p_{1}}^{-1}\right]\|\varphi\|^{p_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}+\left[1-c_{1}-c_{2}-(d_{1}+d_{2})\lambda_{1,p_{2}}^{-1}\right]\|\psi\|^{p_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}\\
&\phantom{PP}-|\mu_{1}||\Omega|^{\frac{p_{1}-q_{1}}{p_{1}}}\|\varphi\|^{q_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}-|\mu_{2}||\Omega|^{\frac{p_{2}-q_{2}}{p_{2}}}\|\psi\|^{q_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}-C,\\
\end{split}

and the claim easily follows from (
2.5
).

Now, Theorem
2.1
gives a pair
(u_{n},v_{n})\in X_{n}
such that
B(u_{n},v_{n})=0
, which entails (
3.1
). Finally, letting
(\varphi,\psi)=(u_{n},v_{n})
in (
3.1
) and arguing as done for (
3.2
) produces

\begin{split}\|u_{n}\|^{p_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}+&\|v_{n}\|^{p_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}\\
&\leq|\mu_{1}|\|u_{n}\|^{q_{1}}_{1,q_{1}}+|\mu_{2}|\|v_{n}\|^{q_{2}}_{1,q_{2}}+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{N}_{f_{1}}(u_{n},v_{n})u_{n}\,{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{N}_{f_{2}}(u_{n},v_{n})v_{n}\,{\rm d}x\\
&\leq|\mu_{1}||\Omega|^{\frac{p_{1}-q_{1}}{p_{1}}}\|u_{n}\|^{q_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}+|\mu_{2}||\Omega|^{\frac{p_{2}-q_{2}}{p_{2}}}\|v_{n}\|^{q_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}\\
&\phantom{PPPP}+\left[c_{1}+c_{2}+(d_{1}+d_{2})(\lambda_{1,p_{1}}\wedge\lambda_{1,p_{2}})^{-1}\right]\left(\|u_{n}\|^{p_{1}}_{1,p_{1}}+\|v_{n}\|^{p_{2}}_{1,p_{2}}\right)+C\end{split}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. At this point, the boundedness of
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\subseteq X
is an immediate consequence of
q_{i}<p_{i}
,
i=1,2
, and (
2.5
).
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Under hypotheses
{\rm(H_{1})}
–
{\rm(H_{2})}

Proof.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\rm(H_{1}^{\prime})}
–
{\rm(H_{2})}
be satisfied and let

Proof.

Since

(

′

)

1

)

′

)

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{N}_{f_{1}}(u_{n},v_{n})(u_{n}-u)\,{\rm d}x\right|\\

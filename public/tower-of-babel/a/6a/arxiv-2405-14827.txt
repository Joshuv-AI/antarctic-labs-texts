An augmented Lagrangian trust-region method with inexact gradient evaluations to accelerate constrained optimization problems using model hyperreduction
Tianshu Wen, Matthew J. Zahr

Abstract
We present an augmented Lagrangian trust-region method to efficiently solve constrained optimization problems governed by large-scale nonlinear systems with application to partial differential equation-constrained optimization. At each major augmented Lagrangian iteration, the expensive optimization subproblem involving the full nonlinear system is replaced by an empirical quadrature-based hyperreduced model constructed on-the-fly. To ensure convergence of these inexact augmented Lagrangian subproblems, we develop a bound-constrained trust-region method that allows for inexact gradient evaluations, and specialize it to our specific setting that leverages hyperreduced models. This approach circumvents a traditional training phase because the models are built on-the-fly in accordance with the requirements of the trust-region convergence theory. Two numerical experiments (constrained aerodynamic shape design) demonstrate the convergence and efficiency of the proposed work. A speedup of 12.7x (for all computational costs, even costs traditionally considered “offline” such as snapshot collection and data compression) relative to a standard optimization approach that does not leverage model reduction is shown.

Introduction

Optimization problems involving partial differential equations (PDEs) arise in many fields for product design, risk control, cost management, etc. Often, the optimization problem may have additional side constraints (i.e., constraints other than the PDE constraint), which increases the complexity of the problem and the computational cost required to solve it. These problems can be prohibitively expensive for PDE discretizations with many degrees of freedom (DoFs) due to repeated queries to the expensive PDE solver.

To mitigate the large computational cost of such problems, surrogate-based approaches have been developed to reduce the computational cost of expensive PDE-constrained optimization problems. Problem formulation

In this section, we formulate the governing high-dimensional optimization problem that we aim to accelerate using model hyperreduction in later sections. We consider general optimization problems constrained by a large-scale nonlinear system of equations, although our primary interest is fully discrete PDE-constrained optimization. To this end, we introduce the governing nonlinear system of equations (Section
2.1
) and the formulation of the complete optimization problem (Section
2.2
), including an augmented Lagrangian formulation of the problem and adjoint method to compute gradients.

2.1
Governing equations

We consider a parametrized, large-scale system of nonlinear equations that we will refer to as the high-dimensional model (HDM):
given a collection of system parameters

{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{\mu}}}
, find
{\bm{u}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}
such that

{\bm{r}}({\bm{u}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0},

(1)

where
{\bm{u}}^{\star}
is the primal solution implicitly defined as the solution of (
1
) and
{\bm{r}}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}\times\mathcal{D}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}
with
{\bm{r}}:({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto{\bm{r}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})
denotes the residual. We assume that for every
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
, there exists a unique primal solution
{\bm{u}}^{\star}={\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})
satisfying (
1
) and that the implicit map
{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto{\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})
is continuously differentiable. In most practical applications, the dimension

is large, which causes (

We focus on the case where the residual arises from the high-fidelity discretization of a parametrized, partial differential equation (PDE), although the developments in this work generalize to naturally discrete systems. Furthermore, we assume the system (
\Omega
) consists of
N_{\mathtt{e}}
elements (
\Omega_{e}
), i.e.,
\Omega=\cup_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}\Omega_{e}
, and the residual can be written as an assembly of element residuals

{\bm{r}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}{\bm{P}}_{e}{\bm{r}}_{e}\left({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}}\right),

(2)

where
{\bm{r}}_{e}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{\mathtt{e}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{\mathtt{e}^{\prime}}}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{\mathtt{e}}}
with

{\bm{r}}_{e}:({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto{\bm{r}}_{e}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}})

is the residual contribution of element
e
,
{\bm{u}}_{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{\mathtt{e}}}
are the DoFs associated with element

e
,
{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{\mathtt{e}^{\prime}}}
are the DoFs associated with elements neighboring element
e
(if applicable),
and
{\bm{P}}_{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times N_{\bm{u}}^{\mathtt{e}}}
is the assembly operator that maps element DoFs for
element
e
to global DoFs. The element and global DoFs are related via assembly operators

{\bm{u}}_{e}={\bm{P}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{u}}
and
{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}=({\bm{P}}_{e}^{\prime})^{T}{\bm{u}}
, where
{\bm{P}}_{e}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times N_{\bm{u}}^{\mathtt{e}^{\prime}}}

is an assembly operator that maps element DoFs from neighbors of element
e
to the corresponding
global DoFs. This structure will be used to facilitate hyperreduction of the nonlinear system of equations.

Remark 1
.

There are many PDE discretizations that possess the elemental decomposition

2

2.2
Optimization formulation

Now, we pose the constrained optimization problem considered in this work as

\displaystyle\underset{{\bm{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}},{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle j({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})

(3)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{c}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{r}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{\mu}}_{l}\leq{\bm{\mu}}\leq{\bm{\mu}}_{u},

where
j:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the quantity to be minimized,

{\bm{c}}:({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})\rightarrow{\bm{c}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})
is a vector of
N_{\bm{c}}

side constraints (i.e., constraints other than the governing equation
{\bm{r}}

involving the primal variable
{\bm{u}}
), and
{\bm{\mu}}_{l}
and
{\bm{\mu}}_{u}
are vectors
of lower and upper bounds, respectively, on the design parameters. In the setting
where the residual
{\bm{r}}
corresponds to a PDE discretization, this is a

discrete PDE-constrained optimization problem
(discretize-then-optimize).
We assume the objective function and side constraints can be written as a summation of
element contributions, i.e.,

j({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}j_{e}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{\mu}}),\qquad{\bm{c}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}{\bm{c}}_{e}({\bm{u}}_{e},{\bm{\mu}}),

(4)

where
j_{e}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{\mathtt{e}}}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and

{\bm{c}}_{e}:\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}^{\mathtt{e}}}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}

are the element contributions of element
e
to the objective function and
side constraints, respectively. Such a decomposition commonly arises in
PDE-constrained optimization applications where the objective and side constraints
are defined as integrals over the computational domain or its boundary.

.

The optimization problem (

) is sufficiently general to

In this case, the parameter vector

(5)

\displaystyle\underset{{\bm{u}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}},{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}}{\text{minimize}}

\displaystyle\ell({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)

(7)

\displaystyle\text{subject to}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{r}}({\bm{u}},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\bm{\mu}}_{l}\leq{\bm{\mu}}\leq{\bm{\mu}}_{u},

where
{\bm{\theta}}
and
\tau

3.1
Projection-based model reduction

Projection-based model reduction begins with the ansatz that the primal state lies in a low-dimensional subspace

\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{\Phi}}=\{{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}}\mid\hat{\bm{y}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}}
, where
{\bm{\Phi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{u}}\times n}
with
n\ll N_{\bm{u}}
is the reduced basis. That is, we approximate the primal state
{\bm{u}}^{\star}
as

{\bm{u}}^{\star}\approx\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\coloneqq{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}}^{\star},

(26)

where
\hat{\bm{u}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\bm{\mathcal{V}}_{\bm{\Phi}}
is the subspace approximation of the primal
state
{\bm{u}}_{\star}
and
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
contains the corresponding reduced coordinates.
The primal reduced coordinates are implicitly defined as the solution of the Galerkin reduced-order model:
given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
, find
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0},

(27)

where
\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto{\bm{\Phi}}^{T}{\bm{r}}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}),

(28)

which is obtained by substituting the ROM ansatz (
26
) into the governing equation (
1
)
and requiring the resulting residual to be orthogonal to the reduced subspace (Galerkin projection). We assume that for
every
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
, there exists a unique primal solution
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}=\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})

satisfying (
27

{\bm{\mu}}\mapsto\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}})
is continuously
differentiable. The reduced residual (
\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}

\hat{{\bm{r}}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}).

(29)

where

and

are the
reduced bases restricted to the elemental degrees of freedom.

Each optimization functional is reduced by substituting the ROM approximation into the original functional, including the augmented Lagrangian function.
That is, let

\hat{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)\mapsto\ell({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau),\quad\hat{\ell}^{L}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})\mapsto\ell^{L}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}}),\quad\hat{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)\mapsto\hat{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau),

(30)

\hat{\ell}^{\mathtt{L}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\hat{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}
, and
\hat{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

The reduced AL function can be expanded as

\hat{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\ell^{\mathtt{L}}_{{\bm{\Phi}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})+\frac{\tau}{2}\left\|{\bm{c}}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right\|^{2}_{2}

(31)

\hat{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\hat{\ell}^{\mathtt{L}}_{{\bm{\Phi}},e}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})+\frac{\tau}{2}\left\|\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}{\bm{c}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right\|^{2}_{2},

(32)

where the elemental contribution to the AL function,
\hat{\ell}^{\mathtt{L}}_{{\bm{\Phi}},e}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\hat{\ell}^{\mathtt{L}}_{{\bm{\Phi}},e}:(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})\mapsto j_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})-{\bm{\theta}}^{T}{\bm{c}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}).

(33)

The reduced adjoint residual,

, is defined as

\hat{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)\mapsto\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}\hat{\bm{z}}-\frac{\partial\hat{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)^{T}={\bm{\Phi}}^{T}{\bm{r}}^{\lambda}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{z}},{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau),

(34)

where the equality follows directly from the definitions in (
12
), (
28
), and (
30
).
Following (
13
) and (
14
), we let the Lagrangian component
{\bm{r}}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be

\hat{\bm{r}}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})\mapsto\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}\hat{\bm{z}}-{\bm{\Phi}}^{T}\frac{\partial j}{\partial{\bm{u}}}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}+\left[\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}}{\partial{\bm{u}}}({\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}){\bm{\Phi}}\right]^{T}{\bm{\theta}}={\bm{\Phi}}^{T}{\bm{r}}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{z}},{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}}),

(35)

and (
34
) becomes

\hat{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\hat{\bm{r}}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})-\tau{\bm{\Phi}}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}}{\partial{\bm{u}}}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{c}}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}).

(36)

The reduced adjoint problem is: given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
and the corresponding reduced primal solution

\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}
satisfying (
27
), find the reduced adjoint solution
\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

satisfying

\hat{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\mathbf{0}.

(37)

Assuming the reduced residual Jacobian is well-defined and invertible for every
(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})

pair with
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}

\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}})^{-T}\frac{\partial\hat{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}),{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)^{T},

(38)

making the implicit map
({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)\mapsto\hat{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)
well-defined for all
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}

The reduced adjoint residual inherits an unassembled structure from the HDM using

\hat{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\hat{\bm{r}}_{{\bm{\Phi}},e}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})-\tau\left[\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}\right]\left[\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}{\bm{c}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right].

(39)

where the elemental contribution,
\hat{\bm{r}}_{{\bm{\Phi}},e}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{r}}_{{\bm{\Phi}},e}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}:(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})

\displaystyle\mapsto-{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial j_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\theta}}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{z}}+

(40)

\displaystyle\left({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\right)^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{z}}.

From the primal-adjoint pair (

\hat{\bm{g}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\lambda}:(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)\mapsto\frac{\partial\hat{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)-\hat{\bm{z}}^{T}\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})={\bm{g}}^{\lambda}({\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{z}},{\bm{\Phi}}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau).

(42)

The Lagrangian part of the gradient reconstruction operator,

\hat{\bm{g}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}:(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})\mapsto\frac{\partial j}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})-{\bm{\theta}}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})-\hat{\bm{z}}^{T}\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}),

which allows the gradient operator to be rewritten as

\hat{\bm{g}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\lambda}({\bm{z}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\hat{\bm{g}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}(\hat{\bm{z}},{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})+\tau{\bm{c}}({\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}).

(44)

The reduced gradient operator also inherits an unassembled structure from the HDM in (
24

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{g}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\lambda}(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}},\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\hat{\bm{g}}_{{\bm{\Phi}},e}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})+\tau\left[\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}{\bm{c}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right]^{T}\left[\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right],

(45)

where the elemental contribution,
\hat{\bm{g}}_{{\bm{\Phi}},e}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{1\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}
, is defined as

\hat{\bm{g}}_{{\bm{\Phi}},e}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}(\hat{\bm{z}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\theta}})=\frac{\partial j_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})-{\bm{\theta}}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})-\hat{\bm{z}}^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}}^{\prime},{\bm{\mu}}).

(46)

Finally, we consider the reduced sensitivity residual as in
[
53
]
,
\hat{\bm{r}}^{\partial}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}
, which will be used to accelerate convergence of the optimization framework. The sensitivity residual is defined as

\hat{\bm{r}}^{\partial}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\hat{\bm{w}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\mapsto\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\hat{\bm{y}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\hat{\bm{w}}+\frac{\partial\hat{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}(\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}),

(47)

and its unassembled form

\hat{\bm{r}}^{\partial}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\hat{\bm{w}},\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})=\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\left[{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}){\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{w}}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}){\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{w}}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{\mu}}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\hat{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right].

(48)

The reduced sensitivity problem is: given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
and the corresponding reduced primal solution
\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}
, find the reduced sensitivity solution
\partial_{\bm{\mu}}\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times N_{\bm{\mu}}}
such that

\hat{\bm{r}}^{\partial}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\partial_{\bm{\mu}}\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},\hat{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}})=\mathbf{0}.

(49)

Despite the potentially significant reduction in degrees of freedom between the HDM system (
1
) and the
reduced system (
27
), computational efficiency will not necessarily be achieved due to the cost of
constructing the nonlinear terms. This can be clearly seen from the unassembled form of the primal residual
(
29
), objective function (
32
), adjoint residual (
39
), and AL gradient (
45
) as each of these operations requires elemental operations
for all elements
. For systems comprised of many elements, e.g., high-fidelity discretization of PDEs, which can be a serious bottleneck. To accelerate the formation of the nonlinear terms, we turn to EQP-based hyperreduction.

3.2
An optimization-aware empirical quadrature procedure

To accelerate the assembly of the nonlinear terms in the various reduced quantities introduced in
Section
3.1
, we use the empirical quadrature procedure
[
53
,
58
,
55
]
. In the adjoint-based
optimization setting, we use EQP to accelerate any operation that involves assembly over all elements (Section
3.2.1
),
i.e., evaluation of the primal and adjoint residuals, the optimization functionals, and gradient reconstruction.
To ensure all hyperreduced quantities are accurate with respect to their reduced counterparts, we include additional
constraints on the original EQP linear program introduced in
[
55
]
(Section
3.2.2
).

3.2.1
Formulation

The EQP construction replaces the unassembled form of the reduced primal residual with a weighted (hyperreduced) version,

\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where

\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})\mapsto\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}\rho_{e}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}{\bm{r}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})

(50)

and
{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{R}
is the vector of weights with
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}
the set of admissible weights
such that
\mathbf{1}\in\mathcal{R}
(
\mathbf{1}
is the vector with each entry equal to one). For each element
\Omega_{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
with
\rho_{e}=0
, the operations on the element can be completely skipped so computational efficiency is achieved when the vector of weights is highly sparse; construction of
{\bm{\rho}}
is deferred to Section
3.2.2
. The primal EQP problem reads: given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
and
{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{R}
, find
\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})=\mathbf{0}.

(51)

For each
{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{R}
, we assume there is a unique primal solution

\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}=\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})
satisfying (
51
) for every
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
.

In the optimization setting, computational efficiency is also required for the evaluation of the optimization functionals, the adjoint residual
(similar to
[
53
,
56
,
20
]
), and the gradient reconstruction. We use the same approach of introducing weights
(
{\bm{\rho}}
) into the unassembled form so efficiency is achieved when
{\bm{\rho}}
is sparse.
The hyperreduced AL,
\tilde{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, its Lagrangian part
\tilde{\ell}^{\mathtt{L}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and the restriction of the hyperreduced AL function to the EQP solution manifold,
\tilde{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
are defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{\ell}^{\mathtt{L}}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}})+\frac{\tau}{2}\left\|\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\rho_{e}{\bm{c}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right\|^{2}_{2},

(52)

\displaystyle\tilde{\ell}^{\mathtt{L}}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}})

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{e=1}^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}\rho_{e}j_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})-{\bm{\theta}}^{T}\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\rho_{e}{\bm{c}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}),

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{\bm{\Phi}}:({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)

\displaystyle\mapsto\tilde{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}}),{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau).

The hyperreduced adjoint residual,
\tilde{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is defined as

\tilde{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}:(\tilde{\bm{z}},\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)\mapsto\frac{\partial\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\tilde{\bm{y}}}(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})^{T}\tilde{\bm{z}}-\frac{\partial\tilde{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\tilde{\bm{y}}}(\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)^{T},

(53)

or, in unassembled form,

\tilde{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{z}},\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}(\tilde{\bm{z}},\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}})-\tau\left[\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\rho_{e}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}\right]\left[\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\rho_{e}{\bm{c}}_{e}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})\right].

(54)

where its Lagrangian part
\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{D}\times\mathcal{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N_{\bm{c}}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\mathtt{L},\lambda}:(\tilde{\bm{z}},\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}})

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum^{N_{\mathtt{e}}}_{e=1}\rho_{e}\left[-{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial j_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{c}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\theta}}+{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{z}}\right.+

(55)

\displaystyle\left.\left({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\right)^{T}\frac{\partial{\bm{r}}_{e}}{\partial{\bm{u}}_{e}^{\prime}}({\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}^{\prime}\tilde{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})^{T}{\bm{\Phi}}_{e}\tilde{\bm{z}}\right].

The hyperreduced adjoint problem is: given
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
and the corresponding hyperreduced primal solution

\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}
satisfying (
51
), find the hyperreduced adjoint solution
\tilde{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\tilde{{\bm{r}}}^{\lambda}_{\bm{\Phi}}(\tilde{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star},{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\mathbf{0}.

(56)

For each
{\bm{\rho}}\in\mathcal{R}
, we assume the hyperreduced Jacobian is well-defined and invertible for every
(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}}),{\bm{\mu}})
pair with
{\bm{\mu}}\in\mathcal{D}
so there exists a unique hyperreduced adjoint solution

\tilde{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)=\frac{\partial\tilde{\bm{r}}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\tilde{\bm{y}}}(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}}),{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}})^{-T}\frac{\partial\tilde{\ell}_{\bm{\Phi}}}{\partial\tilde{\bm{y}}}(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}}),{\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)^{T},

(57)

making the implicit map
({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)\mapsto\tilde{\bm{\lambda}}_{\bm{\Phi}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}};{\bm{\rho}},{\bm{\theta}},\tau)

5
Numerical experiments

In this section, we demonstrate the performance of the proposed method with two aerodynamic shape optimization problems with state-based constraints, e.g., drag, lift. To provide a baseline for comparison, we solve the optimization problem in (
3
) with only HDM evaluations (no model reduction) with a standard interior-point method. We compare the HDM performance with ROM/BTR and EQP/BTR methods embedded in the augmented Lagrangian framework.

To assess the performance of the proposed method, we study the computational cost required to achieve a given value of the objective function, i.e., given
\epsilon>0
, we investigate the costs for each algorithm to satisfy
S_{i}<\epsilon
, where
S_{i}
is the normalized distance from the optimal objective value at major iteration
i

S_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\left|j({\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}_{i}^{\star}),{\bm{\mu}}_{i}^{\star})-j_{\star}\right|}{j({\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}_{0}),{\bm{\mu}}_{0})},

(110)

{\bm{\mu}}_{0}
is the initial guess for the optimal parameter configuration, and
j_{\star}
is the optimal objective value determined by HDM optimization (interior-point) with optimality tolerance
\omega_{\star}=10^{-10}
. Furthermore, we introduce the following notation for the objective function, constraint function, and optimality measure at the end of major iteration

j_{i}\coloneqq j({\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}_{i}^{\star}),{\bm{\mu}}_{i}^{\star}),\qquad{\bm{c}}_{i}\coloneqq{\bm{c}}({\bm{u}}^{\star}({\bm{\mu}}_{i}^{\star}),{\bm{\mu}}_{i}^{\star}),\qquad\chi_{i}\coloneqq\chi({\bm{\mu}}_{i}^{\star}).

(111)

We quantify computational cost with the CPU time required to reach
S_{i}<\epsilon
for a given
\epsilon
and constraint violation tolerance.

In this work, there are only a few user-defined parameters because most of the EQP tolerances are prescribed in Section
4.3
directly from the current optimization state (

5.1
Inverse design of an airfoil in inviscid, subsonic flow with one side constraint

We consider an aerodynamic shape design problem that aims to recover an airfoil with a similar flow profile as the RAE2822 with lower drag starting from a NACA0012 at Mach number

{\bm{\mu}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(U;{\bm{\mu}})=0

{\bm{\mu}}
and flow field

6
Conclusion

In this work, we introduced an augmented Lagrangian trust-region method to efficiently solve constrained optimization problems governed by large-scale nonlinear systems. At each major augmented Lagrangian iteration, the expensive optimization subproblem involving the full nonlinear system is replaced by an EQP-based hyperreduced model (Section
3
) constructed on-the-fly. Convergence to a critical point of an augmented Lagrangian subproblem is guaranteed by a novel bound-constrained trust-region method that allows for inexact gradient evaluations that we developed (Section
4.1
) and specialized to our specific setting that leverages hyperreduced models (Section
4.3
). The approach circumvents a traditional training phase because the EQP models are built on-the-fly in accordance with the requirements of the trust-region convergence theory.

In our previous work
[
53
]
, we carefully studied the impact
of numerous user-defined parameter introduced by the trust-region
framework with inexact gradient evaluations. In this work, we
studied the impact of the user-defined augmented Lagrangian
parameters
(\tau_{0},a)
and found they do not impact convergence,
but have a large influence on convergence rate. For the problems
considered in this work, we found
\tau_{0}\in[10,25]
and
a\in[10,100]

lead to favorable convergence rates.
We also found that inheriting primal and dual solutions from previous major iterations
does not necessarily improve the convergence of the proposed method, but may be useful
in other problems or situations (e.g., unsteady problems).
Using these empirical rules, the proposed EQP/BTR method demonstrated speedups
of up to about 12.7x relative to a popular interior-point optimization method
using only HDM evaluations, even when accounting for all sources of computational
cost, i.e., HDM evaluations for snapshot generation and trust-region assessment,
reduced basis construction, EQP training, trust-region subproblem solves, etc.

HDM evaluations dominate the cost of the proposed EQP/BTR method. This suggests reduced models with improved prediction potential that could reduce the overall number of HDM evaluations would lead to future computational speedup. Additionally, there is likely overhead associated with the nested augmented Lagrangian approach and further speedup may be available from a trust-region method that directly handles the constrained problem (instead of a bound-constrained subproblem). Other interesting research directions include the extension to unsteady problems, the extension to quadrature-based (instead of element-based) EQP
[
20
,
21
]
for integration with higher order finite element discretization, and using the methodology to solve relevant engineering problems.

In this appendix, we revisit the proofs of the global convergence for the inexact, box-constrained trust-region framework, and they directly follow those set forth in

Lemma 1
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
hold and there
exists
\epsilon>0
and
K>0
such that
\chi({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\geq\epsilon
for all
k>K
.
Then the sequence of trust-region radii
\{\Delta_{k}\}

produced by Algorithm
3
satisfies

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{k}<\infty.

Proof.

We first consider the case with an infinite sequence of successful iterations
\{k_{i}\}
with the new gradient condition in
(AT6)
. In this case, for all
i
such that
k_{i}>K
,

\displaystyle F({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})-F({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i+1}})

\displaystyle\geq F({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})-F({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}+1})=F({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})-F(\check{\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})\geq\eta_{1}(m_{k_{i}}({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})-m_{k_{i}}(\check{\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}}))

\displaystyle\geq\eta_{1}\kappa\left\|\chi_{m}({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})\right\|\min\left[\frac{\left\|\chi_{m}({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})\right\|}{\beta_{k}},~\Delta_{k_{i}}\right]\geq\eta_{1}\kappa\epsilon\min\left[\frac{\epsilon}{\beta_{k}},~\Delta_{k_{i}}\right],

for some constant
\kappa\in(0,1)
. The subscript for the second
{\bm{\mu}}
in the first and second expressions are
k_{i+1}
and
k_{i}+1
, respectively.
Due to the step acceptance condition in Algorithm
1
, the sequence
\{F({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\}
is non-increasing, and thus the first inequality holds. The first equality holds because iteration
k_{i}
is successful (by construction). The remaining inequalities
follow from the step acceptance condition in Algorithm
1
, the fraction of Cauchy decrease, and the assumption that

\chi_{m}({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})\geq\epsilon
for all
k_{i}>K
.

Now, we sum over all
i>I
for
k_{I}=K
to yield

\eta_{1}\kappa\epsilon\sum_{i\geq I}\min\left[\frac{\epsilon}{\beta_{k_{i}}},~\Delta_{k_{i}}\right]\leq\sum_{i\geq I}(F({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})-F({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i+1}}))=F({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{I}})-\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}F({\bm{\mu}}_{k_{i}})<\infty,

where the equality follows from the telescoping series, and the finiteness of the limit follows from
F
being bounded below. Because
\epsilon/\beta_{k}
is bounded away from zero, the inequality above implies that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\Delta_{k_{i}}<\infty
. The result also holds for a finite number of successful iterations (Lemma A.2 of
[

]
), and the desired result follows.

∎

Lemma 2
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
hold and there exists
\epsilon>0
and
K>0
such that

\left\|\chi_{m}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\right\|\geq\epsilon
for all
k>K
. Then the ratios

\{\varrho_{k}\}
produced by Algorithm
1
converges to one.

Proof.

By Taylor’s theorem and Assumption
1
, we have

\left|\mathrm{ared}_{k}-\mathrm{pred}_{k}\right|\leq\left\|{\bm{s}}_{k}\right\|\left\|\nabla F({\bm{\mu}}_{k})-\nabla m_{k}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\right\|+\frac{1}{2}(\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}-1)\left\|{\bm{s}}_{k}\right\|^{2},

(116)

where
{\bm{s}}_{k}={\bm{\mu}}-{\bm{\mu}}_{k}
is the step from the current trust-region center
{\bm{\mu}}_{k}
. For all
k>K
, the gradient error bound (Assumption
(AT4)
) and the result of Lemma
1
(
\Delta_{k}\rightarrow 0
) imply

\left|\mathrm{ared}_{k}-\mathrm{pred}_{k}\right|\leq\xi\Delta_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}-1)\Delta_{k}^{2}.

(117)

Additionally, for
k>K
, the fraction of Cauchy decrease condition and
the result of Lemma
1
(
\Delta_{k}\rightarrow 0
) imply

\mathrm{pred}_{k}\geq\kappa\left\|\chi_{m}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\right\|\min\left[\frac{\left\|\chi_{m}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})\right\|}{\beta_{k}},\Delta_{k}\right]\geq\kappa\epsilon\Delta_{k}.

(118)

Combining these results yields

\left|\varrho_{k}-1\right|=\left|\frac{\mathrm{ared}_{k}}{\mathrm{pred}_{k}}-1\right|\leq\frac{\xi\Delta_{k}+\frac{1}{2}(\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2}-1)\Delta_{k}}{\kappa\epsilon}.

(119)

Thus, as
\Delta_{k}\rightarrow 0
,
\varrho_{k}
approaches

, which is the desired result.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

We prove by contradiction. Suppose there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\chi_{m}({\bm{\mu}}_{k})>\epsilon
for sufficiently large

. By Lemma

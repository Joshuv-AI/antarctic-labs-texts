Starobinsky-Type B − L B-L Higgs Inflation Leading Beyond MSSM


Abstract
Models of induced-gravity inflation are formulated within Supergravity employing as inflaton the Higgs field which leads to a spontaneous breaking of a U ​ ( 1 ) B − L U(1)_{B-L} symmetry at M GUT = 2 ⋅ 10 16 ​ GeV M_{\rm GUT}=2\cdot 10^{16}~{\mbox{\rm GeV}} . We use a renormalizable superpotential, fixed by a U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) R symmetry, and logarithmic or semi-logarithmic Kähler potentials with integer prefactors which exhibit a quadratic non-minimal coupling to gravity. We find inflationary solutions of Starobinsky type in accordance with the observations. The inflaton mass is predicted to be of the order of 10 13 ​ GeV 10^{13}~{\mbox{\rm GeV}} . The model can be nicely linked to MSSM offering an explanation of the magnitude of the μ \mu parameter consistently with phenomenological data. Also it allows for baryogenesis via non-thermal leptogenesis, provided that the gravitino is heavier than about 10 ​ TeV 10~{\mbox{\rm TeV}} .

\par\par\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{subsection}{toc}{Connection with the MSSM Phenomenology}
\par The SUSY breaking effects, considered in Eq.~(\ref{Vsoft}), explicitly
break $U(1)_R$ to a subgroup, $\mathbb{Z}_2^{R}$ which remains
unbroken by $\langle{S} \rangle$ in Eq.~(\ref{vevS}) and so no disastrous domain

symmetry prevents rapid proton decay and guarantees the stability
of the \emph{lightest SUSY particle} ({\sf\footnotesize LSP}), providing
thereby a well-motivated \emph{cold dark matter} ({\sf\footnotesize CDM})

\begin{equation}Y\_B=-0.35\cdot{5\over 2}{T_{\rm rh}\over\widehat{m}_{\rm\delta\phi}}\mbox{$\sum_i$}{\widehat{\Gamma}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow N_{i}^{c}N_{i}^{c}}\over\widehat{\Gamma}_{\rm\delta\phi}}\varepsilon\_i~~~\mbox{with}~~~\varepsilon\_i=\sum\_{j\neq i}\frac{{\sf Im}\left[(m_{[\rm D}]^{\dagger}m_{[\rm D}])\_{ij}^2\right]}{8\pi\langle{{H_{u}}}\rangle^2(m_{[\rm D}]^{\dagger}m_{[\rm D}])\_{ii}}\bigg(F\_{\rm S}\left(x\_{ij},y\_i,y\_j\right)+F\_{\rm V}(x\_{ij})\bigg).\end{equation}Here $\langle{{$H_{u}$}} \rangle\simeq 174~{\mbox{\rm GeV}}$, for large $\tan\beta$, $F_{\rm S}$
[$F_{\rm V}$] are the functions entered in the vertex and
self-energy contributions computed as indicated in Ref.~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{plum}{}{}]} and
$m_{\rm D}$ is the Dirac mass matrix of neutrinos, $\nu_i$,
arising from the forth term in Eq.~(\ref{wmssm}). Employing the seesaw
formula we can then obtain the light-neutrino masses $m_{i\nu}$ in
terms of $$m_{[\rm D}$i]$ and $$M_{[N^{c}}$i]$ given by Eq.~(\ref{Wrhn}). As a
consequence, nTL can be nicely linked to low energy neutrino data.
We take into account the recently updated best-fit values
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{valle}{}{}]} of that data listed in Table~\ref{tabn}. Furthermore, the
sum of $$m_{[\nu}$i]$'s is bounded from above at 95\% c.l. by the data
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{plcp, valle}{}{}]}
\begin{equation}\mbox{$\sum_i$}m_{[\nu}i]\leq 0.23~{{\mbox{\rm eV}}}~~~\mbox{for NO
$$m_{[\nu}$i]$'s}~~~\mbox{or}~~~\mbox{$\sum_i$}m_{[\nu}i]\leq 0.15~{{\mbox{\rm eV}}}~~~\mbox{for IO $$m_{[\nu}$i]$'s},\end{equation}where {\footnotesize\sf NO [IO]} stands for \emph{normal [inverted]
ordered} neutrino masses $$m_{[\nu}$i]$'s.

\par\par\par\par The validity of Eq.~(\ref{Yb}) requires that the $$\widehat{\delta\phi}$$ decay into a
pair of $$N^{c}_{i}$$'s is kinematically allowed for at least one species
of the $$N^{c}_{i}$$'s and also that there is no erasure of the produced
$Y_L$ due to $N^c_1$ mediated inverse decays and $\Delta L=1$
scatterings. These prerequisites are ensured if we impose
\begin{equation}{\sf\footnotesize(a)}\>\>\widehat{m}_{\rm\delta\phi}\geq 2M_{[N^{c}}1]\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>{\sf\footnotesize(b)}\>\>M_{[N^{c}}1]\gtrsim 10T_{\rm rh}.\end{equation}Finally, Eq.~(\ref{Yb}) has to reproduce the observational result
\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{plcp}{}{}]}
\begin{equation}Y\_B=\left(8.697\pm 0.054\right)\cdot 10^{-11}.\end{equation}The required $$T_{\rm rh}$$ in Eq.~(\ref{Yb}) must be compatible with
constraints on the $$\widetilde{G}$$ abundance, $Y_{3/2}$, at the onset of
\emph{nucleosynthesis} (BBN), which is estimated to be
\begin{equation}Y\_{3/2}\simeq 1.9\cdot 10^{-22}\ T_{\rm rh}/{\mbox{\rm GeV}},\end{equation}where we take into account only thermal production of $$\widetilde{G}$$, and
assume that $$\widetilde{G}$$ is much heavier than the MSSM gauginos. On the
other hand, $$Y_{3/2}$$ is bounded from above in order to avoid
spoiling the success of the BBN. For the typical case where $$\widetilde{G}$$
decays with a tiny hadronic branching ratio, we have
\begin{equation}Y_{3/2}\lesssim\left\{\begin{matrix}10^{-13}\hfill\cr 10^{-12}\hfill\cr\end{matrix}\right.\>\>\>\>\mbox{for}\>\>\>\>m_{3/2}\simeq\left\{\begin{matrix}10.6~{\mbox{\rm TeV}}\hfill\cr 13.5~{\mbox{\rm TeV}}\hfill\cr\end{matrix}\right.\>\>\>\>\mbox{implying}\>\>\>\>T_{\rm rh}\lesssim 0.53\cdot\left\{\begin{matrix}1~{\mbox{\rm EeV}}\,,\hfill\cr 10~{\mbox{\rm EeV}}\,.\hfill\cr\end{matrix}\right.\end{equation}The bounds above can be somehow relaxed in the case of a stable
$$\widetilde{G}$$.

\par\par\par Confronting $Y_B$ and $$Y_{3/2}$$ -- see Eqs.~(\ref{Yb}) and (\ref{Ygr}) -- with
observations we can constrain the parameters of neutrino sector.
This is because $Y_B$ and $$Y_{3/2}$$ depend on $$\widehat{m}_{\rm\delta\phi}$$, $$T_{\rm rh}$$,
$$M_{[N^{c}}$i]$ and $$m_{[\rm D}$i]$ and can interconnect IHI with neutrino
physics. We follow the bottom-up approach detailed in Ref.~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{ighi}{}{}]},
according to which we find the $$M_{[N^{c}}$i]$'s by using as inputs the
$$m_{[\rm D}$i]$'s, a reference mass of the $\nu_i$'s -- $$m_{[\nu}$1]$ for NO
$$m_{[\nu}$i]$'s, or $$m_{[\nu}$3]$ for IO $$m_{[\nu}$i]$'s --, the two Majorana
phases $\varphi_1$ and $\varphi_2$ of the PMNS matrix, and the
best-fit values for the low energy parameters of neutrino physics
shown in Table~\ref{tabn}.

\par\par The outcome of our computation is presented in Fig.~\ref{fmD}, where
we depict the allowed values of $m_{\rm 2D}$ versus $m_{\rm 1D}$
for $K=K_2$ with $${N_{X}}$=2$, $$\lambda_{\mu}$=10^{-6}$ and the remaining
parameters shown in the Table of Fig.~\ref{fmD}. The conventions
adopted for the various lines is depicted in the plot label. In
particular, we use solid, dashed and dot-dashed line when the
remaining inputs -- i.e. $$m_{[\nu}$i]$, $$m_{[\rm D}$3]$, $\varphi_1$, and
$\varphi_2$ -- correspond to the cases A, B and C of the Table of
Fig.~\ref{fmD} respectively. We consider NO (cases A and B) and IO
(case C) $$m_{[\nu}$i]$'s. In all cases, the current limit in
Eq.~(\ref{sumnu}) is safely met. The gauge symmetry considered here
does not predict any particular Yukawa unification pattern and so,
the $$m_{[\rm D}$i]$'s are free parameters. This fact offers us a
convenient flexibility for the fulfilment of all the imposed
requirements. Care is also taken so that the perturbativity of
$$\lambda$_{iN^c}$ holds, i.e., $$\lambda$_{iN^c}^2/4\pi\leq 1$. The inflaton
$$\widehat{\delta\phi}$$ decays mostly into $N_1^c$'s. In all cases
$$\widehat{\Gamma}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow N_{i}^{c}N_{i}^{c}}$<$\widehat{\Gamma}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow{H_{u}}{H_{d}}}$<$\widehat{\Gamma}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow XYZ}$$ and so the ratios $$\widehat{\Gamma}_{\delta\phi\rightarrow N_{i}^{c}N_{i}^{c}}$/$\widehat{\Gamma}_{\rm\delta\phi}$$ in Eq.~(\ref{Yb})
introduce a considerable reduction in the derivation of $$Y_{B}$$. For
the considered cases in Fig.~\ref{fmD} we obtain:
\lx@equationgroup@subnumbering@begin\begin{equation}0.01\lesssim M\_{1N^c}/10^{3}~{\mbox{\rm EeV}}\lesssim 6.4,\>\>\>2\lesssim M\_{2N^c}/10^{3}~{\mbox{\rm EeV}}\lesssim 447\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>0.1\lesssim M\_{2N^c}/10^{6}~{\mbox{\rm EeV}}\lesssim 9.5.\end{equation}As regards the other quantities, in all we obtain
\begin{equation}1.4\lesssim Y\_{\widetilde{G}}/10^{-13}\lesssim 1.7\>\>\>\mbox{with}\>\>\>0.75\lesssim T_{\rm rh}/{{\mbox{\rm EeV}}}\lesssim 0.9\,.\end{equation}\lx@equationgroup@subnumbering@end As a bottom line, nTL is a realistic possibility within our
setting provided that $$m_{3/2}$\sim 10~{\mbox{\rm TeV}}$ as deduced from
Eqs.~(\ref{Ygw}) and (\ref{res2}). As advertised in Sec.~\ref{pheno}, these values are
in nice agreement with the ones needed for the solution of the
$\mu$ problem within CMSSM in regions (I) and (IV) of Table~\ref{tab}.
\par\par\par\par\lx@frontmatter@fallback\@@numbered@section{section}{toc}{Conclusions}
\par We investigated the realization of IHI in the framework of a $B-L$
extension of MSSM endowed with the condition that the GUT scale is
determined by the renormalization-group running of the three gauge
coupling constants. Our setup is tied to the super-{} and K\"{a}hler potentials
given in Eqs.~(\ref{Wtot}) and (\ref{K1}) -- (\ref{K2}). Our
models exhibit the following features:
\par\begin{itemize}\par\itemize@item@[\sf\ftn(i)]they predict the correct $$n_{\rm s}$$ and low $r$
thanks to the induced-gravity and the GUT requirements;
\par\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@[\sf\ftn(ii)]they ensure the validity of the effective
theory up-to $$m_{\rm P}$$;
\par\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@[\sf\ftn(iii)]they inflate away cosmological defects;
\par\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@[\sf\ftn(iv)]they offer a nice solution to the $\mu$
problem of MSSM, provided that $$\lambda$_\mu$ is somehow small;
\par\par\vskip 4.0pt\vskip 4.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 1.0pt\itemize@item@[\sf\ftn(v)]they allow for baryogenesis via nTL
compatible with $$\widetilde{G}$$ constraints and neutrino data. In
particular, we may have $$m_{3/2}$\sim 10~{\mbox{\rm TeV}}$, with the inflaton
decaying mainly to $N^c_1$ and $N^c_2$ -- we obtain $$M_{[N^{c}}$i]$ in
the range $(10^{10}-10^{15})~{\mbox{\rm GeV}}$.
\par\end{itemize}
\par It remains to introduce a consistent soft SUSY breaking sector --
see, e.g., Ref.~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{susyb}{}{}]} -- to obtain a self-contained theory --
cf. Ref.~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{nsreview, lhclinde}{}{}]}. Moreover, since our main aim here
is the demonstration of the mechanism of IHI in SUGRA, we opted to
utilize the simplest GUT embedding. Extensions to more structured
GUTs are also possible -- see e.g. Ref.~\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{igsu5, nmh}{}{}]} -- with
similar inflationary observables.

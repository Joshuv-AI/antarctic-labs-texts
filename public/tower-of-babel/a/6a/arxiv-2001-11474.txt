Tomasz Łuczak, Joanna Polcyn, Christian Reiher

Abstract
. One of the oldest results in modern graph theory, due to Mantel, asserts that every triangle-free graph on n n vertices has at most ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ \lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor edges. About half a century later Andrásfai studied dense triangle-free graphs and proved that the largest triangle-free graphs on n n vertices without independent sets of size α ​ n \alpha n , where 2 / 5 ≤ α < 1 / 2 2/5\leq\alpha<1/2 , are blow-ups of the pentagon. More than 50 further years have elapsed since Andrásfai’s work. In this article we make the next step towards understanding the structure of dense triangle-free graphs without large independent sets. Notably, we determine the maximum size of triangle-free graphs G G on n n vertices with α ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 3 ​ n / 8 \alpha(G)\geq 3n/8 and state a conjecture on the structure of the densest triangle-free graphs G G with α ⁡ ( G ) > n / 3 \alpha(G)>n/3 . We remark that the case α ⁡ ( G ) ≤ n / 3 \alpha(G)\leq n/3 behaves differently, but due to the work of Brandt this situation is fairly well understood.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Ramsey-Turán theory

Mantel
[
13
]
proved in 1907 that balanced complete bipartite graphs maximise the number
of edges among all triangle-free graphs on a given set of vertices. Later
this result was generalized to
K_{\ell}
-free graphs by Turán
[
16
]
and, asymptotically,
to
H
-free graphs by Erdős and Stone
[
10
]
, and by Erdős and
Simonovits
[
7
]
. All of these works had a decisive impact on
the development of extremal graph theory.

Here we deal with one particular line of research systematically initiated by Vera T. Sós
and known as Ramsey-Turán theory (for a survey on this fascinating area
we refer to Simonovits and Sós
[
14
]
).
For
\ell\geq 3
and
n\geq s\geq 0
the
Ramsey-Turán number

\mathrm{ex}_{\ell}(n,s)
is the maximum number of edges in a
K_{\ell}
-free graph on
n

vertices which contains no independent set consisting of more than
s
vertices, i.e.,

\mathrm{ex}_{\ell}(n,s)=\max_{G=(V,E)}\bigl\{|E|\colon K_{\ell}\nsubseteq G,\;|V|=n,\textrm{ and }\alpha(G)\leq s\bigl\}\,.

One usually considers the case that
n
is large and thus,
instead of
\mathrm{ex}_{\ell}(n,s)
, one rather studies the

Ramsey-Turán density function

f_{\ell}\colon(0,1]\longrightarrow{\mathds{R}}
defined by

f_{\ell}(\alpha)=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\mathrm{ex}_{\ell}(n,\alpha n)}{\binom{n}{2}}\,.

For the existence of this limit we refer to
[
6
]
. Notice that Turán’s theorem implies

(1.1)

\mathrm{ex}_{\ell}(n,s)=\bigl(1-o(1)\bigr)\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}\binom{n}{2}\quad\text{\ for\ }s\geq\Big\lceil\frac{n}{\ell-1}\Big\rceil\,,

whence
f_{\ell}(\alpha)=\frac{\ell-2}{\ell-1}
for all
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{\ell-1}
.
The central problem to determine the limit

\varrho(K_{\ell})=\lim_{\alpha\to 0}f_{\ell}(\alpha)

was solved for odd
\ell
in
[
9
]
and proved to be very difficult for even
\ell
.
Following the ingenious contributions by Szemerédi
[
15
]
and by Bollobás
and Erdős
[
2
]
, this problem was finally solved by Erdős, Hajnal, Sós,
and Szemerédi
[
6
]
.

Recently Lüders and Reiher
[
12
]
determined the value of
f_{\ell}(\alpha)

whenever
\alpha
is sufficiently small depending on
\ell
, so the extremal behaviour
of
K_{\ell}
-free graphs with small (linear) independence number is now well understood.
Despite this fact, we firmly believe that the problem to determine
f_{\ell}(\alpha)

for all

\alpha\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{\ell-1}\bigr)
remains interesting.

1.2.
Results on triangle-free graphs

In this article we restrict our attention to the innocent looking case
\ell=3
,
which seems surprisingly intricate to us. In other words, we concentrate on
triangle-free graphs and, eliminating some indices, we study the behaviour of
the function
\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=\mathrm{ex}_{3}(n,s)
and its ‘scaled’ version
f(\alpha)=f_{3}(\alpha)
.
We also write

\mathscr{E}(n,s)=\bigl\{G=(V,E)\colon K_{3}\nsubseteq G,\;|V|=n,\;\alpha(G)\leq s,\textrm{ and }|E|=\mathrm{ex}(n,s)\bigr\}

for the corresponding families of extremal graphs.

In an early contribution from 1962 Andrásfai
[
1
]
studied the question of
how many edges a triangle-free graph on
n
vertices, whose independence number
is at most
\alpha n
for some given constant
\alpha
, can have.
As a special case of (
1.1
), for
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{2}
Mantel’s Theorem
yields
\bigl\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor
as an answer and for
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{3}

the “trivial” upper bound
\frac{1}{2}\alpha n^{2}
is essentially optimal
(see the discussion at the end of this subsection), so the question is most interesting when

\alpha\in\bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
.
Andrásfai settled this problem for all
\alpha\in\bigl[\frac{2}{5},\frac{1}{2}\bigr]

and conjectured for
\alpha\in\bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\bigr)
that the answer is describable
in terms of appropriate blow-ups of certain graphs nowadays bearing his name (see
Conjecture
1.4
below).

Theorem 1.1

(Andrásfai)
.

For every nonnegative integer
n
and every integer
s\in\bigl[\frac{2}{5}n,\frac{1}{2}n\bigr]

we have

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=n^{2}-4ns+5s^{2}\,.

In particular,
f(\alpha)=2-8\alpha+10\alpha^{2}
holds for
every
\alpha\in\bigl[\frac{2}{5},\frac{1}{2}\bigr]
.

Andrásfai
[

]
also determined the extremal families

for

We suspect that some of the tools we have developed for proving Theorem

will be relevant for a complete determination of the function

, even though some

Notice that every triangle-free graph

\Delta(G)\leq\alpha(G)\,,

for the neighbourhood of every vertex is an independent set.
Therefore

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}ns

holds for all
n\geq s\geq 0
and
f(\alpha)\leq\alpha
for
\alpha>0

We call these estimates the

on

and

, respectively.

In the regime
s<\frac{1}{3}n
Brandt
[

]
provided several constructions of

-regular
graphs on
n

, and his work implies

f(\alpha)=\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\bigl(0,\frac{1}{3}\bigr]
.
In view of this result
and the above theorems it remains to study the behaviour of
f(\alpha)

for
\alpha\in\bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{3}{8}\bigr)
. The next subsection offers a conjecture
for this range.

1.3.
A conjecture on triangle-free graphs

Let us introduce one more piece of terminology. By a
blow-up
of a graph
G
we mean
any graph
\hat{G}
obtained from
G
be replacing each of its vertices
v_{i}
by an
independent set
V_{i}
(that can be empty) and joining two subsets
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
of
vertices of
\hat{G}
by all
|V_{i}||V_{j}|
possible edges whenever the pair
\{v_{i},v_{j}\}

is an edge of
G
. For instance, Figure
1.1
shows a blow-up of the pentagon,
where three consecutive vertices are replaced by independent
(n-2s)
-sets (drawn red)
while the remaining two vertices are enlarged to (blue) independent
(3s-n)
-sets.

Let us recall that Andrásfai graphs are Cayley graphs
\bigl({\mathds{Z}}/(3k-1){\mathds{Z}},S\bigr)

with
k\geq 1
and
S\subseteq{\mathds{Z}}/(3k-1){\mathds{Z}}
being a sum-free subset of size
k
.
For definiteness, we denote the graph obtained for
S=\{k,\ldots,2k-1\}

by
\Gamma_{k}
. So explicitly two vertices
i
and
j
of
\Gamma_{k}
are adjacent if and only
if
i-j\in S
. Following
\Gamma_{1}=K_{2}
the first few Andrásfai graphs are depicted
in Figure
1.3
.

Notice that
\Gamma_{k}
is a triangle-free,
k

3k-1

whose independence number is exactly
k
. Therefore, balanced blow-ups of
\Gamma_{k}

show that the trivial bound on
f(\alpha)
is optimal if
\alpha
is of the form

\frac{k}{3k-1}
, i.e., that we have

(1.2)

f\Big(\frac{k}{3k-1}\Big)=\frac{k}{3k-1}\quad\text{\ for all\ }\ k\geq 1\,.

Like Andrásfai we believe that whenever
n\geq 0
and
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{3}n,\frac{1}{2}n\bigr]

there exists a graph
G\in\mathscr{E}(n,s)
which is a blow-up of an appropriate Andrásfai graph.
This leads to
f(\alpha)
being piecewise quadratic on
\bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\bigr]

with critical values at
\alpha_{k}=\frac{k}{3k-1}
for
k\geq 2
. By optimizing over all
blow-ups of Andrásfai graphs we were led to the following function.

Definition 1.3
.

For integers
n\geq s\geq 0
we set

g(n,s)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}ns\,,&\text{ if $s\leq\frac{1}{3}n$}\cr g_{k}(n,s)\,,&\text{ if $\frac{k}{3k-1}n\leq s<\frac{k-1}{3k-4}n$ for some $k\geq 2$}\cr\bigl\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\bigr\rfloor\,,&\text{ if $\frac{n}{2}\leq s\leq n$,}\end{cases}

where

(1.3)

g_{k}(n,s)=\tfrac{1}{2}k(k-1)n^{2}-k(3k-4)ns+\tfrac{1}{2}(3k-4)(3k-1)s^{2}\,.

Conjecture 1.4
.

For all integers
n\geq s\geq 0
we have
\mathrm{ex}(n,s)\leq g(n,s)
. In other words,
every triangle-free
n
-vertex graph
G
with
\alpha(G)\leq s
has at most
g(n,s)

edges.

Admittedly, one needs some time to get used to the functions
g_{k}(n,s)
introduced
in (
1.3
) but we believe that the motivation in terms of optimal blow-ups of
Andrásfai graphs renders the conjecture sufficiently natural (see also
[
18
]
*Lemma 3.3).
Observe that
the functions
g_{2}(n,s)
and
g_{3}(n,s)
are precisely the quadratic forms appearing
in the Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Therefore, Conjecture
1.4

is only open for
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{3}n,\frac{3}{8}n\bigr)
.
Let us briefly indicate one construction showing that, if true, Conjecture
1.4

is optimal for the most interesting range of
\frac{s}{n}
.

Fact 1.5
.

If
s\in\bigl(\frac{1}{3}n,\frac{1}{2}n\bigr)
, then
\mathrm{ex}(n,s)\geq g(n,s)
.

Proof.

Let
k\geq 2
be the unique integer with

s\in\bigl[\frac{k}{3k-1}n,\frac{k-1}{3k-4}n\bigr)
.
Take a blow-up
G
of
\Gamma_{k}
obtained by replacing the vertices
1
,
k
, and
2k

by sets of size
(k-1)n-(3k-4)s
and the remaining vertices by sets of size
3s-n
.
Clearly,
G
is triangle-free. One can check that
G
has
n

independence number
s
, and
g_{k}(n,s)
edges.
∎

Let us observe that Conjecture
1.4
yields a precise prediction on the
Ramsey-Turán density function
f
, namely the following.

Conjecture 1.6
.

The function
f\colon(0,1]\longrightarrow{\mathds{R}}
is given by

f(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\alpha\,,&\text{ if $\alpha\leq\frac{1}{3}$}\cr f_{k}(\alpha)\,,&\text{ if $\frac{k}{3k-1}\leq\alpha<\frac{k-1}{3k-4}$ for some $k\geq 2$}\cr\frac{1}{2},&\text{ if $\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1$},\end{cases}

where

(1.4)

f_{k}(\alpha)=k(k-1)-2k(3k-4)\alpha+(3k-4)(3k-1)\alpha^{2}\,.

Notice that at the critical values
\alpha_{k}=\frac{k}{3k-1}
this function
agrees with (
1.2
). The remainder of this introduction discusses
further evidence in support of Conjecture
1.4
.

1.4.
Minimum degree

There appears to be a mysterious analogy between the Ramsey-Turán problem for
triangles and the more thoroughly studied problem to describe the structure of
triangle-free graphs of large minimum degree. For instance, there is a similar
transition from chaos to structure occurring at
\frac{1}{3}n
. As reported in
[
8
]

Hajnal constructed triangle-free graphs
G
with

\delta(G)\geq\bigl(\frac{1}{3}-o(1)\bigr)|V(G)|

of arbitrarily large chromatic number. On the other hand, Łuczak
[
19
]

proved that for every
\varepsilon>0

all triangle-free graphs
G
with
\delta(G)\geq\bigl(\frac{1}{3}+\varepsilon\bigr)|V(G)|

admit a homomorphism into a triangle-free graph whose order can be bounded in terms
of
\varepsilon
. The ultimate variant of Łuczak’s result is due to
Brandt and Thomassé
[
4
]
, who proved that, actually, such graphs either admit
a homomorphism into some Andrásfai graph or into some
so-called
Vega graph
(see
[
3
]
).

We will not introduce Vega graphs properly here and only remark that they can be
obtained from Andrásfai graph by adding
8
vertices forming a cube as well as several
new edges, and possibly deleting at most two special vertices afterwards.
For
k\geq 10
with
k\equiv 1,2,3\pmod{9}
there are blow-ups of appropriate
Vega graphs with
3k-1
vertices and independence number
k
that furnish additional
extremal cases for (
1.2
).

Now several questions present themselves. Are Vega graphs so special that their
only appearances in extremal families
\mathscr{E}(n,s)
are the aforementioned regular ones?
Or, perhaps, on the contrary, because of their more sophisticated structure they
admit blow-ups falsifying Conjecture
1.4
? Both these speculations seem to be false.
There are non-trivial blow-ups of Vega graphs which are in
\mathscr{E}(n,s)
. Nonetheless, it can be
proved that, in a sense made precise in
[
18
]
, no ‘natural’ blow-up of an Andrásfai
graph or Vega graph
can be a counterexample to Conjecture
1.4
.

These facts have an interesting consequence. Together with the structure theorem of
Brandt and Thomassé they allow us to prove Conjecture
1.4
for

\alpha\in\bigl[\frac{k}{3k-1},\frac{k}{3k-1}+\varepsilon_{k}\bigr]
, where
k\geq 2
and
\varepsilon_{k}

is sufficiently small.
Further details and a conjectural explicit description of the extremal graph
families
\mathscr{E}(n,s)
will be presented in our forthcoming article
[
18
]
.

This article is organized as follows. In the next section we sketch some tools
and observations which we shall use later and which, hopefully, could be useful
in the quest of proving Conjecture
1.4
in full generality. The last section
is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Preliminaries

The goal of this section is to gather several results that we believe to be
relevant in general to the problem of determining
\mathrm{ex}(n,s)
for
s>n/3
.
These preliminaries fall naturally into three groups.
We start in Subsection
2.1
with some facts concerning matchings
and independent sets in arbitrary, not necessarily triangle-free, graphs.
Subsection

the structure of graphs that are extremal for the problem to determine

.

2.1.
Matchings and independent sets

Clearly if
A
and
Y
are two disjoint independent sets in a graph
G
with

|Y|\leq|A|
, then there is an injective map from
Y
to
A
. The following simple
consequence of Hall’s theorem, due to Andrásfai (see
[
1
]
*Lemma 2.3), ensures
that in case
|A|=\alpha(G)

one such injection is exemplified by a matching. For the reader’s
convenience we include a short proof.

Fact 2.1
.

Let
A
and
Y
be two disjoint independent sets in a graph
G
. If
|A|=\alpha(G)
,
then
Y
is matchable into
A
.

Proof.

In the light of Hall’s theorem
[
11
]
it suffices to
prove that for an arbitrary
D\subseteq Y
and its neighbourhood
A\cap\mathrm{N}(D)
in
A

we have
|D|\leq|A\cap\mathrm{N}(D)|
. Since
D\cup(A\smallsetminus\mathrm{N}(D))
is independent in
G
,
we have indeed

|D|+|A|-|A\cap\mathrm{N}(D)|=|D\cup(A\smallsetminus\mathrm{N}(D))|\leq\alpha(G)=|A|\,.\qed

In general, deleting edges from a graph may increase its independence number.
For our purposes it will be important to know that the following type
of edge deletions leave the independence number invariant.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given a graph
G
, suppose

\bullet

that
A\subseteq V(G)
is an independent set of size
\alpha(G)
,

\bullet

and that
M
is a matching in
G
from
V(G)\smallsetminus A
to
A
,
the size of which is as large as possible.

If
G^{\prime}
denotes the graph obtained from
G
by isolating the vertices in

A\smallsetminus V(M)
, i.e., by deleting all edges incident with them, then

\alpha(G^{\prime})=\alpha(G)
.

Proof.

Since
G^{\prime}
is a subgraph of
G
, we have
\alpha(G^{\prime})\geq\alpha(G)
.
For the converse direction we consider any set
U\subseteq V(G)
which is
independent in
G^{\prime}
.
Since
U\smallsetminus A
is independent in
G
, Fact
2.1
tells us that
in
G
there exists a matching
N
from
U\smallsetminus A
to
A
covering all vertices
of
U\smallsetminus A
. We contend that

M^{\prime}=\{e\in M\colon A\cap U\cap e\neq\varnothing\}\cup N

is a matching in
G
. Otherwise, there had to exist two edges,
e\in M
with

A\cap U\cap e\neq\varnothing
and
f\in N
, sharing a vertex
x
. By
x\in f\in N

we have either
x\in U\smallsetminus A
or
x\in A
.
In the former case the vertex of
e
distinct from
x
needs to be in
A\cap U
.
In particular, both ends of
e
are in
U
, contrary to
e\in M\subseteq E(G^{\prime})

and
U
being independent in
G^{\prime}
.

So we are left with the case
x\in A
. Now we have in fact
A\cap U\cap e=\{x\}

and both ends
of
f
are in
U
. Moreover,
x\in A\cap U\cap e\subseteq A\cap V(M)
shows that
f
does
not get deleted when we pass from
G
to
G^{\prime}
. This contradiction to
U
being
independent in
G^{\prime}
proves that
M^{\prime}
is indeed a matching in
G
.

Therefore the maximality of
M
yields

|N|+|V(M)\cap A\cap U|=|M^{\prime}|\leq|M|=|V(M)\cap A\cap U|+|V(M)\cap(A\smallsetminus U)|\,,

i.e.,

|U\smallsetminus A|=|N|\leq|V(M)\cap(A\smallsetminus U)|\,.

Thus

|U\smallsetminus A|\leq|A\smallsetminus U|\,,

and, consequently,
|U|\leq|A|=\alpha(G)
, as desired.
∎

To unleash the full power of the foregoing lemma it is useful to know
that certain subsets of
V(G)
can be forced to be
subsets of
V(M)
. For such purposes we shall employ the following observation.

Fact 2.3
.

Let
H
be a bipartite graph with vertex classes
R
and
S
in which the largest
matching has size
m
. If some set
R^{\prime}\subseteq R
is matchable into
S
,
then
H
contains a matching of size
m
saturating all vertices of
R^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be the set of all matchings in
H
having size
m
.
Choose first a matching
N
from
R^{\prime}
into
S
saturating all vertices in
R^{\prime}
,
and then a matching

M\in\mathscr{M}
for which
|M\cap N|
is maximal. We will show that
M

covers
R^{\prime}
. Otherwise there was a vertex
x\in R^{\prime}
not covered by
M
.
Let
z
be the unique vertex with
xz\in N
. By the maximality of
m
,
the
m+1
edges in
M\cup\{xz\}
cannot form a matching in
H

and, consequently, we have
z\in V(M)
. So there is a vertex
u
such
that
uz\in M
. But now
M^{\prime}=(M\smallsetminus\{uz\})\cup\{xz\}

is a matching in

satisfying

, contradicting

∎

2.2.
Symmetrisation

One of the standard proofs of Turán’s theorem
[
16
]
, due to
Zykov
[
17
]
, shows that every
K_{\ell}
-free graph
G
can be
transformed into an
(\ell-1)
-partite graph
on the same vertex set
by a sequence of symmetrisation operations, in such a way that throughout
the whole process the number of edges never decreases. Indeed, this implies that
G
does
have at most as many edges as the corresponding Turán graph. The

symmetrisation
step employed by Zykov consists in taking two non-adjacent
vertices
u
and
v
, deleting all edges incident with
u
, and then adding all edges
from
u
to the neighbours of
v
.

Our proof of Theorem
1.2
utilises a modest generalisation of this idea.
Given a graph
G

and two disjoint sets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, we say that
a graph
G^{\prime}
on the same vertex set as
G
arises from
G
by the
generalised Zykov
symmetrisation

\mathbf{Sym}(A,B)
if it is obtained by deleting all edges incident with
B

and afterwards adding all edges from
A
to
B
. Explicitly, this means

V(G^{\prime})=V(G)\quad\text{ and }\quad E(G^{\prime})=\bigl(E(G)\smallsetminus\{e\in E(G):e\cap B\neq\varnothing\}\bigr)\cup K(A,B)\,.

We will express this state of affairs by writing
G^{\prime}=\mathbf{Sym}(G\,|\,A,B)
.
In the special case where
B=\{v\}
is a singleton, we will often
abbreviate
\{v\}
to
v
, thus speaking, e.g. of the operation
\mathbf{Sym}(A,v)
.
For later use we record the following obvious properties of these operations.

Fact 2.4
.

Given a graph
G
and two disjoint sets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, let
G^{\prime}=\mathbf{Sym}(G\,|\,A,B)
.

(

)

If
|A|\geq\deg_{G}(b)
holds for all
b\in B
, then
e(G^{\prime})\geq e(G)
.
If equality occurs, then all vertices in
B
have degree
|A|
in
G

and
B
is an independent set in
G
.

(

)

If
A
is independent and
G
is triangle-free, then so is
G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Part
( )
‣
2.4
follows from the estimate

e(G^{\prime})-e(G)\geq\sum_{b\in B}\bigl(|A|-\deg_{G}(b)\bigr)\geq 0\,,

where the first “
\geq
” sign takes into account that edges both of whose
ends are in
B
are subtracted twice in the sum over
b\in B
. The statement
addressing the equality case should now be clear.

To prove part
( )
‣
2.4
we assume
for the sake of contradiction that
xyz
was a triangle in
G^{\prime}
. Owing to

G-B=G^{\prime}-B
we may suppose further that
x\in B
. Now
y
and
z
are neighbours
of
x
in
G^{\prime}
and, hence, both of them are in
A
. But
A
is still independent
in
G^{\prime}
and thus
yz
cannot be an edge of
G^{\prime}
.
∎

Our next result describes a case where symmetrization preserves the independence number.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A
and
B
be two disjoint independent sets in a graph
G
such
that
|A|=|B|=\alpha(G)
. If
M
is a matching from
V(G)\smallsetminus(A\cup B)

to
B
, whose size is as large as possible,
B^{\prime}\subseteq B\smallsetminus V(M)
,
and
G^{\prime}=\mathbf{Sym}(G\,|\,A,B^{\prime})
, then
\alpha(G^{\prime})=\alpha(G)
.

Proof.

Since
A
is independent in
G^{\prime}
, we have
\alpha(G^{\prime})\geq\alpha(G)
.
Now suppose conversely that
U\subseteq V(G)
is independent in
G^{\prime}
.
We are to prove
|U|\leq\alpha(G)
.

If
A
and
U
are disjoint it follows from Lemma
2.2
(applied
to
G-A
,
B
, and
M
here in place of
G
,
A
, and
M

there) that
|U|\leq\alpha(G-A)=\alpha(G)
, meaning that we are done.

It remains to consider the case
U\cap A\neq\varnothing
.
Now
K(A,B^{\prime})\subseteq E(G^{\prime})
implies that
U
is disjoint
to
B^{\prime}
. Therefore
U
is independent in
G
and so
|U|\leq\alpha(G)
.
∎

2.3.
Some general results

Recall that for

\displaystyle\mathrm{ex}(n,s)=\max\bigl\{|E|\colon G=(V,E)\text{ is a triangle-free graph with $|V|=n$ and

We begin by observing that the estimate

Fact 2.6
.

Let integers

and equality can only hold if
s\in\bigl\{\frac{k}{3k-1}n,\frac{k-1}{3k-4}n\bigr\}

Proof.

We check that under our assumption on

the trivial

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)\leq\frac{1}{2}ns
is at least as good as

. Since
for

,

\displaystyle ns

\displaystyle\leq ns+\bigl(kn-(3k-1)s\bigr)\bigl((k-1)n-(3k-4)s\bigr)

\displaystyle=k(k-1)n^{2}-2k(3k-4)ns+(3k-4)(3k-1)s^{2}=2g_{k}(n,s)

this is indeed the case and the statement about the equality case also easily follows.
∎

In combination with Fact

this leads to the following alternative

in case
\frac{s}{n}\in\bigl(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{2}\bigr)

Corollary 2.7

If
\frac{1}{3}n<s<\frac{1}{2}n
, then

\Box

\displaystyle\hfill g(n,s)=\min\bigl\{g_{k}(n,s)\colon k\geq 2\bigr\}\,.

Our first structural result on graphs in

Case 2.

Observe that the

Lemma 2.9

Given two integers

3.1.

Let us recall that

Lemma 3.1

Let

s^{*}=n-2s
,

G^{*}=G-(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime})
, and
A^{*}=B\smallsetminus A^{\prime}
,
Q^{*}=C\smallsetminus B
.

Claim 3.6
.

The numbers
n^{*}
and
s^{*}
as well as the triple
(G^{*},A^{*},Q^{*})

satisfy the hypothesis of Proposition
3.2
.

Proof.

A quick calculation based on
s\in\bigl[\frac{1}{3}n,\frac{2}{5}n\bigr]

shows that
n^{*}>0
and
s^{*}\in\bigl[\frac{1}{3}n^{*},\frac{1}{2}n^{*}]
, i.e., that the
size of
s^{*}
is in the appropriate range.
Owing to

|V(G^{*})|=n-(|A^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|+|C^{\prime}|)=n-3(3s-n)=4n-9s=n^{*}

and
|A^{*}|=|B|-|A^{\prime}|=s-(3s-n)=n-2s=s^{*}
the sets
V(G^{*})
and
A^{*}

have the correct size. Next, we would like to show
\alpha(G^{*}-A^{*})\leq s^{*}
.
If
J\subseteq V(G^{*})\smallsetminus A^{*}
is independent in
G^{*}
, then
J\cup B^{\prime}

is independent in
G
, and thus we have
|J|\leq s-|B^{\prime}|=s^{*}
, as desired.

Regarding the set

Z^{*}=\{v\in V(G^{*})\smallsetminus A^{*}\colon\deg_{G^{*}}(v)>s^{*}\}

we contend

(3.3)

Z^{*}\subseteq V(G)\smallsetminus(A\cup B\cup C)\,.

Indeed, if
v\in V(G^{*})\cap A
, then

\deg_{G^{*}}(v)=\deg_{G}(v)-|A^{\prime}|\leq s-(3s-n)=s^{*}

and the same reasoning applies to
B
and
C
in place of
A
as well.

As a consequence of (
3.3
) we have

Q^{*}\subseteq V(G^{*})\smallsetminus(A^{*}\cup Z^{*})
,
as required. The assumption
|B\cap C|\leq n-2s
implies

|Q^{*}|=s-|B\cap C|\geq 3s-n=3s^{*}-n^{*}
,
so
Q^{*}
is sufficiently large for our purposes.

Finally, if
Z^{\prime}\subseteq Z^{*}
is independent in
G^{*}
, then
Z^{\prime}
is also
independent in
G
and (
3.3
) shows that
Z^{\prime}
has to be disjoint to
A
.
Thus, Fact
2.1
tells us that in
G
there is a matching
M
from

Z^{\prime}
to
A
. By Claim
3.5
and (
3.3
) such a matching
can only use the part
A\smallsetminus(B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime})
of
A
, meaning that

Z^{\prime}
is indeed matchable into
V(G^{*})\smallsetminus(A^{*}\cup Z^{\prime}\cup Q^{*})
.
Thereby Claim
3.6
is proved.
∎

Now the foregoing claim and Proposition
3.2
result in

e(G^{*})\leq{n^{*}}^{2}-4n^{*}s^{*}+5{s^{*}}^{2}=5n^{2}-24ns+29s^{2}\,,

so

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=|B||B^{\prime}|+|C||C^{\prime}|+|A\smallsetminus(B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime})||A^{\prime}|+e(G^{*})

\displaystyle\leq 2s(3s-n)+(2n-5s)(3s-n)+(5n^{2}-24ns+29s^{2})

\displaystyle=3n^{2}-15ns+20s^{2}\,.\qed

Perhaps somewhat surprisingly, the previous result allows us to study a similar
configuration, where
C
is no longer disjoint to one of
A
and
B
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
n\geq s\geq 0
and let
G
be a triangle-free graph on
n
vertices
with
\alpha(G)=s
containing two disjoint independent sets
A
and
B

of size
s
, which in turn have subsets
A^{\prime}\subseteq B
,
B^{\prime}\subseteq A

with
|A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|=3s-n
and

K(A^{\prime},A)\cup K(B^{\prime},B)\subseteq E(G)\,.

If
G
contains a further independent set
C
of size
s
intersecting both
A

and
B
, then

e(G)\leq 3n^{2}-15ns+20s^{2}\,.

Proof.

Regarding
n
and
s
as being fixed we consider a counterexample with
|Q|
maximal,
where

Q=\{q\in V\colon\mathrm{N}(q)=C\}\,.

Let us start with a few basic observations. First of all, we have
C\cap A^{\prime}=\varnothing
,
because
K(A^{\prime},A)\subseteq E(G)
and
A
intersects
C
. Similarly, one checks

C\cap B^{\prime}=\varnothing
. Furthermore, since
\Delta(G)\leq\alpha(G)=s
, the sets
(C\smallsetminus A)\cup A^{\prime}

and
(C\smallsetminus B)\cup B^{\prime}
are independent. Hence they consist of at most
s=|C|
vertices each, which yields

(3.4)

|A\cap C|\geq|A^{\prime}|=3s-n\quad\text{and}\quad|B\cap C|\geq|B^{\prime}|=3s-n\,.

Finally, using the definition of
Q
, the independence of
A
,
B
, and
C
, and
the fact that
C
meets
A
and
B
, one obtains

(3.5)

Q\cap(A\cup B\cup C)=\varnothing\,.

The maximality of
Q
together with Lemma
3.4
leads to the following statement.

Claim 3.8
.

We have
V(G)=A\cup B\cup C\cup Q
.

Proof.

Assume contrariwise, that there exists a vertex

q\in V(G)\smallsetminus(A\cup B\cup C\cup Q)
and set
G^{\prime}=\mathbf{Sym}(G\,|\,C,q)
. The sets

A
,
B
,
A^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime}
, and
C
still satisfy the hypothesis of Lemma
3.7

in
G^{\prime}
. Moreover, Fact
2.4
tells us that
G^{\prime}
is a triangle-free graph
with
e(G^{\prime})\geq e(G)
. Due to the maximality of
|Q|
all this is only
possible if
\alpha(G^{\prime})>s
. This means that there exists an independent set

D^{\prime}=\{q\}\cup D\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
in
G^{\prime}
with
|D^{\prime}|>s
. As usual,
D
needs to be
an independent set in
G
with
|D|=s
and
C\cap D=\varnothing
.

The case
k=3
of Fact
2.6
allows us to assume
s>3n/8
,
which leads to

|A^{\prime}|+|B^{\prime}|+|C|+|D|=8s-2n>n\,.

Thus
D\cap(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime})\neq\varnothing
and without loss of generality
we may suppose
D\cap A^{\prime}\neq\varnothing
, which in turn implies
D\cap A=\varnothing
.
Moreover, we have

|B\cap D|\leq|B\smallsetminus C|=|B|-|B\cap C|\overset{\eqref{eq:CcapA}}{\leq}n-2s\,.

So altogether the graph
G
and the sets of vertices
A
,
B
, and
D
satisfy
the assumption of Lemma
3.4
with
D
here in place of
C
there.
But now
e(G)\leq 3n^{2}-15ns+20s^{2}
contradicts
G
being a counterexample
and, hence, establishes Claim
3.8
.
∎

Continuing the proof of Lemma
3.7
we observe that, owing to the definition
of
Q
, the set
(A\smallsetminus C)\cup Q
is independent, and so
|Q|\leq|A\cap C|
.
In combination with Claim
3.8
this yields

n\overset{\eqref{eq:QABC}}{=}|A\cup B\cup C|+|Q|\leq 3s-|A\cap C|-|B\cap C|+|A\cap C|=3s-|B\cap C|\overset{\eqref{eq:CcapA}}{\leq}n\,,

and equality holds throughout. In particular, we have
|Q|=|A\cap C|
and
consequently
E=(A\smallsetminus C)\cup Q
is an independent set consisting of
s
vertices.
To complete the proof it suffices to show that the assumptions of Lemma
3.4

are satisfied for
B
,
A
, and
E
here in place of
A
,
B
, and
C
there.
The condition
B\cap E=\varnothing
is clear and in the light of (
3.4
)
we obtain

|A\cap E|=|A\smallsetminus C|=|A|-|A\cap C|\overset{\eqref{eq:CcapA}}{\leq}s-(3s-n)=n-2s\,.\qed

We proceed to the main result of this article, which we reformulate as follows.

Theorem 3.9
.

If
n\geq s\geq 0
, then

(3.6)

\mathrm{ex}(n,s)\leq g_{3}(n,s)=3n^{2}-15ns+20s^{2}\,.

Moreover, for
s\in\bigl[\frac{3}{8}n,\frac{2}{5}n\bigr]
equality holds.

Proof.

The statement on equality follows from the first part in view of Fact
1.5

(see also Figure
1.2
), so it remains to establish (
3.6
).

Arguing indirectly we take a counterexample
(n,s)
with
n
minimum.
The case
k=3
of Fact
2.6
tells us

(3.7)

\frac{n}{3}<\frac{3n}{8}<s<\frac{2n}{5}<\frac{n}{2}\,.

By Lemma
2.9
there exists a graph
G\in\mathscr{E}(n,s)
which contains
two disjoint independent sets
A
and
B
of size
s
such that there are
sets
A^{\prime}\subseteq B
,
B^{\prime}\subseteq A
with
|A^{\prime}|=|B^{\prime}|=3s-n\overset{\eqref{eq:sn3}}{>}0

and

K(A^{\prime},A)\cup K(B^{\prime},B)\subseteq E(G)\,.

Pick two arbitrary vertices
a\in A^{\prime}
,
b\in B^{\prime}
and set
G^{\prime}=G-\{a,b\}
.
We shall consider two cases depending on the independence number of
G^{\prime}
.

Case 1.
\alpha(G^{\prime})=s
.

Take an independent set
C\subseteq V(G^{\prime})
of size
s
. Since the set
C\cup\{a\}

has size
s+1
and thus fails to be independent in
G
, we have

C\cap A\neq\varnothing
. A similar argument shows
C\cap B\neq\varnothing
and, hence,

C
is as demanded by Lemma
3.7
. Consequently we have

e(G)\leq 3n^{2}-15ns+20s^{2}
, which contradicts
(n,s)
being counterexample.

Case 2.
\alpha(G^{\prime})\leq s-1
.

Observe that the minimality of
n
leads to

\displaystyle e(G^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq 3(n-2)^{2}-15(n-2)(s-1)+20(s-1)^{2}

\displaystyle=(3n^{2}-15ns+20s^{2})+(3n-10s+2)\,.

Since (
3.7
) yields
3n+1\leq 8s
, this implies

e(G^{\prime})\leq(3n^{2}-15ns+20s^{2})+(1-2s)

and consequently we have

e(G)=e(G^{\prime})+(2s-1)\leq 3n^{2}-15ns+20s^{2}\,,

which again contradicts the assumption that
(n,s)
is a counterexample.
∎

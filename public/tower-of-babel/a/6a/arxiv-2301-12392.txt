Prismatization over 𝐙 \mathbf{Z}
Lance Gurney

Abstract
. The aim of this article is to given an extension of the prismatization functor for p p -adic formal schemes (whose construction was first sketched by Drinfeld in [ Dri20 ] and then given by Bhatt-Lurie in [ BL22b ] ) to all schemes over Spec ⁡ ( 𝐙 ) \mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Z}) . We then prove some basic properties of this extension (algebraicity, flatness for syntomic morphisms, perfectness of cohomology) and show that for smooth schemes over 𝐐 \mathbf{Q} this construction recovers (a version of) the filtered de Rham stack.

1.1.1.
Stacks

We denote by
\mathcal{S}
the
\infty
-category of animated sets (equivalently the
\infty
-category of spaces). We denote by
\mathrm{Aff}
the opposite of the 1-category of rings
\mathrm{Ring}
and by
\mathrm{dAff}
the opposite of the
\infty
-category of animated rings
\mathrm{aRing}

[

We denote by
\mathrm{dPSt}
the category of derived prestacks, i.e. the category of functors
X:\mathrm{dAff}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{S}

\mathrm{dAff}
with the fpqc topology, we have the full subcategory

of derived stacks.

Inside
\mathrm{dSt}
we have the full subcategory of classical stacks
\mathrm{St}
(which we will just call stacks) which is equivalent to the category of functors
\mathrm{Aff}^{\circ}\to\mathcal{S}
satisfying the sheaf condition for fpqc covers; equivalently they are those derived stacks which can be written as a colimit of non-derived affine schemes. Note that the inclusion
\mathrm{St}\to\mathrm{dSt}
commutes with colimits but not with limits (unless they are ‘Tor-independent’).

We will not really have any use for the rather wild generality above, but it is convenient to consider all of the objects which will appear: schemes, derived affine schemes, 1-stacks and so on, as living (fully faithfully) within a single larger category.

All limits will mean limits taken in
\mathrm{St}
and not their derived counterparts, unless explicitly stated.

1.1.2.
Algebraic stacks

A morphism of

of stacks is called schematic if for all schemes
Y

the fibre product
\mathscr{X}\times_{\mathscr{Y}}

has property
P

have property
P
for all maps
Y\to\mathscr{Y}
with

3
3

3

Foundationally speaking, schematic morphisms are not so useful; for instance the property of being schematic does not descend. However, this notion will suffice for our purposes.

Schematic morphisms which tend to arise in nature are affine morphisms and open immersions (and compositions thereof).

A stack

is called algebraic if there exists a schematic morphism

which is representable by faithfully flat morphisms of schemes which are covering maps for the fpqc topology; we call

A morphism of stacks

is called algebraic if for all schemes
Y\to\mathscr{Y}

\mathscr{X}\times_{\mathscr{Y}}Y
is algebraic. Any morphism of algebraic

-stacks is algebraic and algebraic morphisms are stable under composition.

An algebraic morphism of stacks

is called (faithfully) flat if for all maps
Y\to\mathscr{Y}
with
Y
a scheme, and all presentations

the composition

Finally, we note that if

(ii) This follows from (i).

(iii) By (i) the map of animated rings

Remark 2.3.3
.

There is another ‘stacky’ approach to the de Rham cohomology for schemes over
\mathbf{Q}
due to Simpson
[
Sim96
]
. In Simpon’s set up one works over the (uncompleted) Cartier stack
\mathbf{A}^{1}/\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
and instead of working with the ring stack
\mathrm{Cone}(\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{a}}(-1)\to\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{a}})
, one works with the ring stack
\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{a}}^{\mathrm{dR},\mathrm{Sim}}:=\mathrm{Cone}(\widehat{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{a}}(-1)\to\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{a}})
and, using the same formulas as in (

), defines stacks

. The major upshot of Simpson’s approach is that one can recover unfiltered de Rham cohomology via base change along the open point
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Q})\to\mathbf{A}^{1}/\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
which is missing in the story above. However, it comes with some drawbacks:

First, the stacks

are not algebraic over
\mathbf{A}^{1}/\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
due to the appearance of the formal group scheme
\widehat{\mathbf{G}}_{\mathrm{a}}(-1)
above and so the quasi-coherence of the direct images, while still true, must be proven directly.

Secondly, there is a general method (due to Mondal
[
Mon22
]
) by which one can try to recover stacky approaches to cohomology theories from purely cohomological information. This method can be applied in the setting outlined in this article to show that Hodge filtered de Rham cohomology of smooth affine schemes can be used to recover the stacks
X^{\mathrm{dR}}
, while this method fails in Simpson’s approach (and will fail for any approach which recovers unfiltered de Rham cohomology).

Lastly, the theory given in this article can be extended to work for all smooth schemes over
\mathbf{Z}
, while it is not clear that there is a sensible way to do this in Simpson’s approach (although we note that something probably can be done in this direction, see Appendice
[
Ber74
]
.)

4.1.

4.1.1.
Definition

Let

In terms of the decomposition in (

The following gives some alternative characterisations of Hodge-Tate elements:

Proposition 4.1.2
.

Let

be an affine scheme and let

. The following are equivalent:

(i)

W_{S}
-linear isomorphism

(ii)

\mathrm{im}_{S}(v)=VW_{S}
,

(iii)

\ker_{S}(v)=W_{S}[F]

Proof.

(i) implies (ii): Clear.

(ii) implies (iii): We must have

and therefore
W_{S}[F]\subset\ker_{S}(v)
. Thus,

induces a
W_{S}
-linear epimorphism

which, as

is an invertible

-module, must be an isomorphism. Therefore,

.

(iii) implies (i): As noted above, using (

) we see that

. Therefore,

induces a
W_{S}
-linear monomorphism

which as

is an invertible
\widetilde{W}_{S}
-module must be an isomorphism by (

).
∎

Corollary 4.1.3
.

Proof.

This is (i) of (

is a flat affine scheme over

f:Y\to X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}
be a morphism of algbraic stacks over
\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}
. Then
f
flat if and only if the composition
Y\times_{\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbf{Z}}\to X^{HT}\to X\times\Delta_{\mathbf{Z}}
is flat.

Proof.

It is clear that
f
is flat if and only if
Y\times_{\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}}\Delta_{\mathbf{Z}}\to X^{HT}
is flat. However,
X^{HT}\to X\times\Delta_{\mathbf{Z}}
is a torsor under the group stack
\Theta_{X/\mathbf{Z}}^{HT}
which, as
X
is smooth over
\mathbf{Z}
, is the classifying stack of a flat group scheme over
X\times\Delta_{\mathbf{Z}}
. The claim the follows from the general fact that if
G
is flat group scheme over a stack
S
and
T
is a gerbe banded by
G
, then a morphism
S^{\prime}\to T
is flat and only if
S^{\prime}\to S
is flat.
∎

5.4.
Presentations of
X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}

Given a scheme
X
we denote by
J(X)
the sheaf whose
R
-points are given by
J(X)(R)=\lim_{n}X(W_{n}(R))
. In general
J(X)
will no longer be a scheme, but if
X
is affine then
J(X)
is again an affine scheme. If
\mathrm{Spec}(R)\to\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}
is a map the homomorphisms
W_{n}(R)\to\overline{W}_{n}(R)
induce a map
J(X)\times\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}\to X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}
.

Proposition 5.4.1
.

If
X
is smooth and affine over
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})
the map
J(X)\times\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}\to X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}
is faithfully flat and affine.

Proof.

This follows from (
5.3.5
) as the map
J(X)\times\Delta_{\mathbf{Z}}\to X\times\Delta_{\mathbf{Z}}
is flat.
∎

Let us describe

X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}_{n}=(J(X)\times\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}})\times_{X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}}\cdots\times\times_{X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}}(J(X)\times\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}})

in the case that
X=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
is affine.

Let
I_{n}=\ker(A^{\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}n}\to A)
and consider the affine scheme over
W
:

J(\mathrm{Spec}(A^{\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}n}[t,t^{-1}I_{n}]))\to J(\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Z}[t]))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}W.

If we let
\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
act on
\mathrm{Spec}(A^{\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}n}[t,t^{-1}I_{n}])
by giving
t
degree one, then
J(\mathbf{G}_{\mathrm{m}})=W^{\times}
acts on
J(\mathrm{Spec}(A^{\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}n}[t,t^{-1}I_{n}]))
compatibly with the natural map to
W
. Therefore, we obtain a map

J(\mathrm{Spec}(A^{\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}n}[t,t^{-1}I_{n}]))/W^{\times}\to W/W^{\times}.

It is straightforward to prove (just as in the de Rham case) that we have

X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}J(\mathrm{Spec}(A^{\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}n}[t,t^{-1}I_{n}]))/W^{\times}\times_{W/W^{\times}}\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}.

Finally, if a smooth scheme
X
admits a
\delta
-structure, which is to say a family of commuting Frobenius lifts
(\varphi_{p})_{p}
, then there is a canonical morphism
X\to J(X)
. Composing this with the map above, induces a map

X\times\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}\to X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}.

Proposition 5.4.2
.

If
X\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})
is a smooth
\delta
-scheme then the map

X\times\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}\to X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}

is faithfully flat.

Proof.

Faithful flatness of the map follows from (
5.3.5
).
∎

If we write

X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}_{\delta,n}=(X\times\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}})\times_{X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}}\cdots\times_{X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}}(X\times\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}})

and
X=\mathrm{Spec}(A)
as above we have natural isomorphisms:

X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}_{\delta,n}=(J(\mathrm{Spec}(A^{\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}n}[t,t^{-1}I_{n}]))\times_{J(\mathrm{Spec}(A^{\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}n}))}\mathrm{Spec}(A^{\otimes_{\mathbf{Z}}n}))/W^{\times}\times_{W/W^{\times}}\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}.

5.5.
Comparisons and the cohomology of
X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}

For each prime
p
, we denote by
\Sigma_{p}
the
p
-adic formal stack, and by

\mathrm{Sch}_{\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})}\to\mathrm{St}_{\Sigma_{p}}:X\mapsto X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{p}}

the
p
-adic prismatization functor, defined by Drinfeld in
[
Dri20
]
and by Bhatt-Lurie in
[
BL22b
]
.

Theorem 5.5.1
.

Let
X\to\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})
be a scheme. Then we have:

(i)

\Sigma\times\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Q})^{\mathrm{dR}}
and
\Sigma\times\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbf{Z})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Sigma_{p}
.

(ii)

Under the equivalences in
(i)
we have natural isomorphisms

X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}\times\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Q})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{\mathbf{Q}}^{\mathrm{dR}}\quad\text{ and }\quad X^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}\times\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{p}}

where
X_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}=X\times\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
.

Proof.

This is just a restatement of (
5.1.4
) combined with the definition of
X\mapsto X^{\mathrm{dR}}
in (
2.2.2
) and definition of the prismatization functor in 1.4.2 of
[
Dri20
]
.
∎

Theorem 5.5.2
.

If
f:X\to Y
is syntomic, quasi-compact and quasi-separated then
Rf_{+}^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}(\mathscr{O}_{X})
is quasi-coherent. It is perfect whenever
f
is proper and smooth.

Proof.

Quasi-coherence of
Rf_{+}^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}(\mathscr{O}_{X})
follows from (ii) of (
5.3.2
) and (
1.1.5
).

Now assume that
f
is smooth and proper. In order to show that
Rf_{+}^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{\mathbf{Z}}}(\mathscr{O}_{X})
is perfect, is enough to show that
Rf^{HT}_{+}(\mathscr{O}_{X^{HT}})\in D(Y^{HT})
is perfect, since
\Delta_{\mathbf{Z}}\to\Sigma_{\mathbf{Z}}
is a nilpotent immersion. However, by (
5.3.4
) we know that
R\rho_{X/Y+}(\mathscr{O}_{X^{HT}})
is perfect and since the projection
f_{Y^{HT}}:Y^{HT}\times_{Y}X\to Y^{HT}
is smooth and proper it follows that
Rf^{HT}_{+}(\mathscr{O}_{X^{HT}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}Rf_{Y^{HT}+}(R\rho_{X/Y+}(\mathscr{O}_{HT}))
is perfect.
∎

For
X/\mathbf{Z}
denote by
f^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{p}}_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}:X_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}^{{{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}_{p}}\to\Sigma_{p}
the structure map of the
p
-adic prismatisation of
X_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}:=X\times\mathrm{Spf}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
.

Corollary 5.5.3
.

Let

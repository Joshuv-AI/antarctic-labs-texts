On linear diameter perfect Lee codes with diameter 6
Tao Zhang, Gennian Ge

Abstract
In 1968, Golomb and Welch conjectured that there is no perfect Lee codes with radius r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 and dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 . A diameter perfect code is a natural generalization of the perfect code. In 2011, Etzion (IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory, 57(11): 7473–7481, 2011) proposed the following problem: Are there diameter perfect Lee (DPL, for short) codes with diameter greater than four besides the D ​ P ​ L ​ ( 3 , 6 ) DPL(3,6) code? Later, Horak and AlBdaiwi (IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory, 58(8): 5490–5499, 2012) conjectured that there are no D ​ P ​ L ​ ( n , d ) DPL(n,d) codes for dimension n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and diameter d > 4 d>4 except for ( n , d ) = ( 3 , 6 ) (n,d)=(3,6) . In this paper, we give a counterexample to this conjecture. Moreover, we prove that for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , there is a linear D ​ P ​ L ​ ( n , 6 ) DPL(n,6) code if and only if n = 3 , 11 n=3,11 . Keywords : Diameter perfect code, Lee metric, group ring, lattice tiling. AMS subject classifications : 52C22, 11H31, 11H71.



1
Introduction

The Lee metric was first introduced in
[
16
,
29
]
for transmission of signals. For two vectors
u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{n}),v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, their
Lee distance
is defined by

d_{L}(u,v)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|u_{i}-v_{i}|.

A
Lee code

C
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
endowed with Lee distance. If
C
is also a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, then
C
is called a
linear Lee code
. Lee codes have been well studied due to many practical applications, for example, constrained and partial-response channel
[
24
]
, flash memory
[
14
,
26
]
, interleaving schemes
[
3
]
and multidimensional burst error correction
[
7
]
.

Let
S_{n,r}(u)
denote the
Lee sphere
of radius
r
centered at
u
, that is,

S_{n,r}(u)=\{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\ d_{L}(u,v)\leq r\}.

The
packing radius

r(C)
of a Lee code
C
is the greatest integer
r
such that
S_{n,r}(u)\cap S_{n,r}(v)=\emptyset
holds for all
u,v\in C
. The
covering radius

R(C)
of a Lee code
C
is the smallest integer
r
such that
\cup_{u\in C}S_{n,r}(u)=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. A Lee code
C\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is called
perfect
if
r(C)=R(C)=r
, and then
C
is called a
PL(n,r)
code.

Another way to define perfect codes is by tiling.
For a subset
V\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, if there exists
W\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that
\mathbb{Z}^{n}=V\bigoplus W
, then we say that
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
can be
tiled
by
V
. It is easy to see that there is a
PL(n,r)
code if and only if
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
can be tiled by
S_{n,r}(0)
. The well-known
Golomb-Welch conjecture
states that

Conjecture 1.1
.

[
8
]

For
n\geq 3
and
r\geq 2
, there is no
PL(n,r)
code.

In
[
8
]
, Golomb and Welch showed that there is no
PL(n,r)
code for
n\geq 3
and
e\geq e_{n}
, where
e_{n}
is not specified. Post
[
21
]
proved the nonexistence of
PL(n,r)
codes for
n\geq 6
and
r\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}n-\frac{1}{4}(3\sqrt{2}-2)
. Later, Lepistö
[
17
]
showed that
PL(n,r)
codes must satisfy
n\geq\frac{(r+2)^{2}}{2.1}
, where
r\geq 285
.

For some small dimensions, Golomb-Welch conjecture has been solved. Gravier et al.
[
9
]
settled the dimension 3 case. Dimension 4 was resolved in
[
27
]
, and dimension 5 was proved in
[
11
]
. Horak
[
10
]
showed the nonexistence of perfect Lee codes for
n=6
and
r=2
.

Researchers also considered the conjecture with small radius. Under certain conditions, the nonexistence of
PL(n,r)
(or linear
PL(n,r)
) codes has been proved in
[
15
,
23
,
31
,
32
]
for
r=2,3,4
. In
[
18
]
, Leung and Zhou completely solved the existence of linear
PL(n,2)
codes. In
[
30
,
33
]
, the authors showed that almost perfect linear Lee codes of radius 2 only exist for small dimensions. For more information about Golomb-Welch conjecture, we refer the readers to
a recent survey
[
13
]
.

A natural generalization of perfect code includes the diameter perfect code. Let
(V,d)
be a metric space. Then a set
C\subseteq V
is a
diameter
d
code
if
d(u,v)\geq d
for any
u,v\in C
, and a set
A\subseteq V
is a
diameter
d
anticode
if
d(u,v)\leq d
for all
u,v\in A
. In
[
1
]
, the authors proved that if
V
is a distance regular graph,
C
is a diameter
d
code and
A
is a diameter
d-1
anticode, then
|C||A|\leq|V|
. A diameter
d
code that attains above bound with equality is called a diameter
d
perfect code. For more about perfect codes (and diameter perfect codes), we refer the readers to
[
6
]
.

However, the above definition cannot be extended to infinite space. In order to define diameter perfect Lee codes, we need the following notations. Let
\mathcal{S}=\{S_{i}:i\in I\}
be a family of subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then a set
T\subset\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is called a transversal of
\mathcal{S}
if
|T\cap S_{i}|=1
for each
i\in I
, and
T\cap S_{i}\neq T\cap S_{j}
for all
i\neq j
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then
C
is a diameter
d
perfect Lee code if
C
is a diameter
d
code, and there is a tiling
\mathcal{T}=\{W_{i}:i\in I\}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by the anticode of diameter
d-1
of maximum size such that
C
is a transversal of
\mathcal{T}
. The diameter
d
perfect Lee code in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
will be denoted by
DPL(n,d)
.

For
d
odd, the anticode of diameter
d-1
of the maximum size is the Lee sphere
S_{n,\frac{d-1}{2}}
. Hence
DPL(n,d)
is the same as
PL(n,\frac{d-1}{2})
for
d
odd.

For
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with
d_{L}(u,v)=1
, the double Lee sphere is defined by

DS_{n,r}(u,v)=S_{n,r}(u)\cup S_{n,r}(v).

The size of double Lee sphere is
[
4
]

|DS_{n,r}|=\sum_{i=0}^{\min\{n-1,r\}}2^{i+1}\binom{n-1}{i}\binom{r+1}{i+1}.

It was pointed out in
[
2
]
that, for
d
even, the anticode of diameter
d-1
of maximum size is the double Lee sphere
DS_{n,r}
with
r=\frac{d-2}{2}
.

The following conjecture is an extension of Golomb-Welch conjecture, which was first proposed by Etzion
[
5
]
and formally presented in
[
12
]
.

Conjecture 1.3
.

There is no
DPL(n,d)
code for
n\geq 3
and
d>4
with the exception of the pair
(n,d)=(3,6)
.

In this paper, we give a counterexample to this conjecture. Moreover, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
n\geq 3
, there is a linear
DPL(n,6)
code if and only if
n=3,11
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we first give some basics about group rings and partial difference sets, then we provide a group ring representation of linear
DPL(n,6)
codes. In Section
3
, we prove our main result.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Group ring and partial difference set

Let
G
be a finite abelian group (written multiplicatively). The group ring
\mathbb{Z}[G]
is a free abelian group with a basis
\{g:\ g\in G\}
. For any
A\in\mathbb{Z}[G]
,
A
can be written as formal sum
A=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g
, where
a_{g}\in\mathbb{Z}
. For any
A=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g\in\mathbb{Z}[G]
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define

A^{(t)}=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g^{t}.

Addition, subtraction and multiplication in group rings are defined as:

\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g\pm\sum_{g\in G}b_{g}g=\sum_{g\in G}(a_{g}\pm b_{g})g,

and

\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g\sum_{g\in G}b_{g}g=\sum_{g\in G}(\sum_{h\in G}a_{h}b_{h^{-1}g})g.

For any set
A
whose elements belong to
G
(
A
may be a multiset), we can identify
A
with the group ring element
\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g
, where
a_{g}
is the multiplicity of
g
appearing in
A
.
For a finite abelian group
G
, its character group is denoted by
\widehat{G}
. For any
A=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}g\in\mathbb{Z}[G]
and
\chi\in\widehat{G}
, define

\chi(A)=\sum_{g\in G}a_{g}\chi(g).

Group rings are widely used in the study of difference sets and related topics, see
[
22
,
25
]
and the references therein.

We also need some results about partial difference sets. We first give its definition.

Definition 2.1
.

A
k
-element subset
D
of a finite group
G
(written multiplicatively) of order
v
is called a
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
-partial difference set in
G
if the multiset
\{d_{1}d_{2}^{-1}:\ d_{1},d_{2}\in D,d_{1}\neq d_{2}\}
contains each nonidentity element of
D
exactly
\lambda
times and each nonidentity element of
G\backslash D
exactly
\mu
times. When
e\not\in D
and
D^{(-1)}=D
, then
D
is called a regular partial difference set.

In the language of group ring,
D
is a
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
partial difference set in
G
if and only if

DD^{(-1)}=\mu G+(\lambda-\mu)D+\gamma e,

where
\gamma=k-\mu
if
e\notin D
and
\gamma=k-\lambda
if
e\in D
.
See
[
20
]
for the background on partial difference sets. The following result can be found in
[
19
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

[
19
]

If there exists a nontrivial regular
(v,k,\lambda,\mu)
partial difference set in an abelian group, then
v,\Delta
and
v^{2}/\Delta
must have the same prime divisors, where
\Delta=(\lambda-\mu)^{2}+4(k-\mu)
.

2.2
Group ring representation of linear
DPL(n,6)
codes

In this paper, we focus on the existence of linear
DPL(n,d)
codes. The following theorem says that there is a linear
DPL(n,d)
code if and only if there is a lattice tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by a fixed double Lee sphere
DS_{n,\frac{d-2}{2}}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
12
]

Let
d
be an even integer and
C
be a linear
DPL(n,d)
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Let
W
be a double Lee sphere
DS_{n,r}
with
r=\frac{d-2}{2}
. Then
\mathcal{T}=\{W+c:c\in C\}
is a lattice tiling of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by double Lee sphere
W
.

The following theorem can be found in
[
12
]
, which establishes the connection between lattice tilings of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and finite abelian groups.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
12
]

Let
V
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then there is a lattice tiling
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by
V
if and only if there are both an abelian group
G
of order
|V|
and a homomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow G
so that the restriction of
\phi
to
V
is a bijection.

The following lemma can be found in
[
28
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
28
, Theorem 3.19]

If a finite abelian group
G
is a direct sum of subgroups
H,K
of relatively prime orders and
G=A+B
is a factorization, where
|A|=|H|
,
|B|=|K|
, then
G=H+B=A+K
.

Now we translate the existence of linear
DPL(n,6)
codes into group ring equations.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
n\geq 3
, then there exists a linear
DPL(n,6)
code if and only if there exists a finite abelian group
H
of order
2n^{2}+1
and
T\subseteq H
satisfying

(1)

|T|=2n
,

(2)

T=T^{(-1)}
,

(3)

T^{2}=2H-T^{(2)}+(2n-2)e
.

Proof.

Let
e_{i}
,
i=1,2,\dots,n
, be a fixed orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.
By Theorem
2.4
, there exists a linear
DPL(n,6)
code if and only if there are both an abelian group
G
(written multiplicatively) of order
4n^{2}+2
and a homomorphism
\phi:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rightarrow G
such that the restriction of
\phi
to
DS_{n,2}(0,e_{1})
is a bijection. Since the homomorphism
\phi
is determined by the values
\phi(e_{i})
,
i=1,\dots,n
, then there exists a linear
DPL(n,6)
code if and only if there exists an
n
-subset
\{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}\}\subset G
(let
\phi(e_{i})=a_{i}
) such that

\displaystyle G=

\displaystyle\{e,a_{1}^{\pm 1},a_{1}^{\pm 2},a_{1}^{3}\}\cup\{a_{i}^{\pm 1},a_{1}^{\pm 1}a_{i}^{\pm 1},a_{1}^{2}a_{i}^{\pm 1},a_{i}^{\pm 2},a_{1}a_{i}^{\pm 2}:\ 2\leq i\leq n\}

\displaystyle\cup\{a_{i}^{\pm 1}a_{j}^{\pm 1},a_{1}a_{i}^{\pm 1}a_{j}^{\pm 1}:\ 2\leq i<j\leq n\}.

In the language of group ring, the above equation can be written as

\displaystyle G=

\displaystyle e+a_{1}+a_{1}^{2}+a_{1}^{-1}+a_{1}^{-2}+a_{1}^{3}+(e+a_{1}+a_{1}^{-1}+a_{1}^{2})\sum_{i=2}^{n}(a_{i}+a_{i}^{-1})+(e+a_{1})\sum_{i=2}^{n}(a_{i}^{2}+a_{i}^{-2})

\displaystyle+(e+a_{1})\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n}(a_{i}+a_{i}^{-1})(a_{j}+a_{j}^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e+a_{1})(e+a_{1}^{2}+a_{1}^{-2}+(a_{1}+a_{1}^{-1})\sum_{i=2}^{n}(a_{i}+a_{i}^{-1})+\sum_{i=2}^{n}(a_{i}^{2}+a_{i}^{-2})+\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n}(a_{i}+a_{i}^{-1})(a_{j}+a_{j}^{-1}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(e+a_{1})(e+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}^{2}+a_{i}^{-2})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(a_{i}+a_{i}^{-1})(a_{j}+a_{j}^{-1})).

Since
|G|=4n^{2}+2
and
\gcd(2,2n^{2}+1)=1
, then
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a subgroup of
G
. Let
H=G/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\varphi:G\rightarrow G/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be the natural homomorphism, then
|H|=2n^{2}+1
.
By Lemma
2.5
, we have

\varphi(e)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\varphi(a_{i})^{2}+\varphi(a_{i})^{-2})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\varphi(a_{i})+\varphi(a_{i})^{-1})(\varphi(a_{j})+\varphi(a_{j})^{-1})=H.

Let
T=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\varphi(a_{i})+\varphi(a_{i})^{-1})
, then we can compute to get that

\displaystyle T^{2}=

\displaystyle(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\varphi(a_{i})+\varphi(a_{i})^{-1}))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\varphi(a_{i})^{2}+\varphi(a_{i})^{-2})+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(\varphi(a_{i})+\varphi(a_{i})^{-1})(\varphi(a_{j})+\varphi(a_{j})^{-1})+2n\varphi(e)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2H-T^{(2)}+(2n-2)\varphi(e).\qed

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
H
be a finite abelian group of order
2n^{2}+1
and
T\subset H
satisfying conditions in Theorem
2.6
, then
for any nontrivial character
\chi\in\widehat{H}
,
\chi(T)\neq 0
.

Proof.

If
\chi(T)=0
, by Theorem
2.6
, we have
\chi(T^{(2)})=2n-2
. This is impossible since
|T^{(2)}|=2n
and
\gcd(2,|H|)=1
.
∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we will give an explicit construction of linear
DPL(11,6)
code and prove that
for any
n\geq 4
,
n\neq 11
, there does not exist any subset
T
in a finite abelian group
H
satisfying conditions in Theorem
2.6
.

We will first study
T^{(2)}T
, by modulo 3, we can get some restrictions on the coefficients of elements of
T^{(2)}T
. This can solve the cases
n\equiv 1\pmod{3}
and
n\equiv 2\pmod{3}
except for
n=5,8,11
. However, we cannot get a contradiction when
n\equiv 0\pmod{3}
. Hence we consider
TT^{(4)}\pmod{5}
and
TT^{(5)}\pmod{5}
. Small dimensions will be solved in the last subsection.

Let
n\geq 3
be an integer. Suppose that
H
is a finite abelian group with order
2n^{2}+1
, and
T\subset H
satisfying conditions in Theorem
2.6
. We write
T
as
T=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}+a_{i}^{-1})
, then

\displaystyle H=e+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}^{2}+a_{i}^{-2})+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(a_{i}+a_{i}^{-1})(a_{j}+a_{j}^{-1}).

(1)

We first prove some lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

T\cap T^{(3)}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

If there exist
a_{i},a_{j}\in T
with
j\neq i
such that
a_{i}=a_{j}^{3}\in T\cap T^{(3)}
, then
a_{j}^{2}=a_{i}a_{j}^{-1}
. By Equation (
1
), it can be seen that
a_{j}^{2}
appears twice in
H
, which is a contradiction.

If there exists
a_{i}\in T
such that
a_{i}=a_{i}^{3}\in T\cap T^{(3)}
, then
a_{i}^{2}=e
. Since
\gcd(2,|H|)=1
, then
a_{i}=e
, which contradicts to
e\notin T
.

If there exists
a_{i}\in T
such that
a_{i}=a_{i}^{-3}\in T\cap T^{(3)}
, then
a_{i}^{4}=e
. Similarly as above, we have
a_{i}=e
, which is also a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
t\neq e
, we have

|\{(t_{1},t_{2})\in T\times T:t_{1}t_{2}=t\}|=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }t\in T^{(2)};\\
2,&\mbox{if }t\notin T^{(2)}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof is directly from Condition (3) of Theorem
2.6
.
∎

Since
T^{2}=2H-T^{(2)}+(2n-2)e
, then

\displaystyle TT^{(2)}=4nH+(2n-2)T-T^{3}.

(2)

Writing
TT^{(2)}
as

\displaystyle TT^{(2)}=\sum_{i=0}^{M}iX_{i},

(3)

where
X_{i}

(i=0,1,\dots,M)
form a partition of group

, i.e.,

\displaystyle H=\sum_{i=0}^{M}X_{i}.

(4)

Then we have the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{M}i|X_{i}|=4n^{2}
,

(2)

\sum_{i=0}^{M}|X_{i}|=2n^{2}+1
,

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{M}|X_{i}|=4n-\beta+\sum_{i\geq 3}\frac{(i-1)(i-2)}{2}|X_{i}|
, where
2\beta=|T\cap T^{(2)}|
.

Proof.

The first two equations are directly from Equations (
3
) and (
4
). Now we count the size of distinct elements in
T^{(2)}T
.
Let
T^{(2)}=\sum_{i=1}^{2n}b_{i}
with
b_{i}^{-1}=b_{n+i}
for

.
By Lemma
3.2
, we have

|b_{i}T\cap b_{j}T|=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }b_{i}b_{j}^{-1}\in T^{(2)};\\
2,&\mbox{if }b_{i}b_{j}^{-1}\notin T^{(2)}.\end{cases}

Let
|T\cap T^{(2)}|=2\beta
and
\alpha=|\{(i,j):1\leq i<j\leq 2n,b_{i}b_{j}^{-1}\in T^{(2)}\}|

\alpha=\sum_{t\in T^{(2)}}N(t)

. By the definition of

.

By the inclusion-exclusion principle, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}|X_{i}|=

\displaystyle|T^{(2)}T|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2n}|b_{i}T|-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 2n}|b_{i}T\cap b_{j}T|+\sum_{r\geq 3}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\leq 2n}(-1)^{r-1}|b_{i_{1}}T\cap\dots\cap b_{i_{r}}T|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4n^{2}-\alpha-2(\binom{2n}{2}-\alpha)+\sum_{r\geq 3}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\leq 2n}(-1)^{r-1}|b_{i_{1}}T\cap\dots\cap b_{i_{r}}T|.

is

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}|X_{i}|=

\displaystyle 4n^{2}-\alpha-2(\binom{2n}{2}-\alpha)+\sum_{r\geq 3}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\leq 2n}(-1)^{r-1}|b_{i_{1}}T\cap\dots\cap b_{i_{r}}T|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4n^{2}-\alpha-2(\binom{2n}{2}-\alpha)+\sum_{i\geq 3}|X_{i}|\frac{(i-1)(i-2)}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4n-\beta+\sum_{i\geq 3}\frac{(i-1)(i-2)}{2}|X_{i}|.\qed

\begin{split}&(\pm a_{1})^{2}+(\pm 2a_{1})^{2}+(3a_{1})^{2}+\sum_{i=2}^{n}((\pm a_{i})^{2}+(\pm a_{1}\pm a_{i})^{2}+(2a_{1}\pm a_{i})^{2}+(\pm 2a_{i})^{2}\\

(19)

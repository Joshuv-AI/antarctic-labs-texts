On-Chip Characterization of High-Loss Liquids between 750 GHz and 1100 GHz
Juan Cabello-Sánchez, Vladimir Drakinskiy, Jan Stake, Helena Rodilla

Abstract
Terahertz spectroscopy is a promising tool for analyzing the picosecond dynamics of biomolecules, which is influenced by surrounding water molecules. However, water causes extreme losses to terahertz signals, preventing sensitive measurements at this frequency range. Here, we present sensitive on-chip terahertz spectroscopy of highly lossy aqueous solutions using a vector network analyzer, contact probes, and a coplanar waveguide with a 0.1 mm wide microfluidic channel. The complex permittivities of various deionized water/isopropyl alcohol concentration are extracted from a known reference measurement across the frequency range 750–1100 GHz and agrees well with literature data. The results prove the presented method as a high-sensitive approach for on-chip terahertz spectroscopy of high-loss liquids, capable of resolving the permittivity of water.

Introduction

Terahertz (THz) spectroscopy is an indispensable tool to analyze light-weight molecules with applications in astronomy and chemistry.
With new technological developments, the application of THz technology has extended to fields as diverse as security

The low SNR further plummets when measuring high-loss aqueous samples (around 100 dB/mm

A promising method for obtaining high SNR for on-chip applications is measuring
S-parameters
using vector network analyzers (VNA), a common measuring method at microwave and millimeter-wave frequencies.
This method is based on an electronic heterodyne technique, and benefits from having first-class frequency resolution (
\sim$1\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}$
), about
20\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}

1\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}

17

The frequency range of VNA measurements applied to biology has been typically restricted to microwave
[
18
]
and millimeter-wave frequencies
[

20
]

However, recent development in heterodyne technology has increased the maximum frequency of VNA analysis up to 1.5 THz

21

22

In this letter, we demonstrate the use of a vector network analyzer and contact probes for THz spectroscopy of high-loss aqueous samples contained in a chip.
We describe the design of the low-loss sensing waveguide and its fabrication, the measurement setup, and how the complex refractive index was extracted from the measured complex transmission coefficient.
This is a first step towards a miniaturized chip sensor for high-loss liquid samples at THz frequencies.

Method

For sensing liquid samples on a chip, we used a coplanar waveguide (CPW)

23
]

The CPW was designed to suit both the measurement probe’s pitch (
25\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}

50\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolOhm}

23.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}

1.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}

The CPW was designed on a ultra-thin
23\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}
thick polyethylene terephthalate (PET) film substrate (
\epsilon^{\prime}=3.15

[
24

[
25

to undesirable substrate modes
[
26

The CPWs and their dedicated calibration standards were fabricated using e-beam lithography and evaporation of
20\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
Ti and
350\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
Au on top of the PET substrate.

The PET substrate containing the CPWs is held on top of a polyethylene supporting substrate to avoid coupling with the probe station’s metal chuck during the measurements (Fig.
1

The polyethylene supporting substrate had a
1\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}

1\text{\,}\mathrm{mm}

On top of the PET substrate, an interchangeable polydimethylsiloxane (PDMS) microfluidic channel was clamped to the polyethylene supporting substrate.
The microfluidic channel was designed to have a
100\text{\,}\mathrm{\SIUnitSymbolMicro m}

1\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{m}

To deliver the sample into the microfluidic channel, input and output tubings were connected to the microfluidic channel.

We measured the complex transmission coefficient,
\hat{T}_{s}
, between
0.75\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}
and
1.1\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}

27

-40\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{m}

To couple the signal to the sensing chip, we used DMPI T-Wave ground-signal-ground probes
[
22
]

We used dedicated multi-line TRL
[
28
]

Illustration of the cross-section of the microfluidic channel filled with sample intersecting the CPW.

The aqueous samples consist of propan-2-ol (IPA) in deionized-water (DI-
{}{}\mathrm{H}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\mathrm{2}}}\mathrm{O}
) with different concentrations—0, 10, 20, 30, 40, and 50% of IPA in volume.
The samples were pumped into the microfluidic channel from higher to lower concentration of IPA through the input tube using a syringe and flushing air between samples.
The pressure was adjusted to atmospheric pressure by removing the syringe momentarily after introducing each sample.
The probes were kept in contact with the chip throughout all measurements to minimize probing position uncertainty.
Each sample was measured five times in consecutive VNA sweeps with an intermediate frequency bandwidth of
50\text{\,}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}

The effective refractive index of the sample-loaded CPW (
\hat{n}_{e}=n_{e}-j\kappa_{e}

\hat{T}_{r}

\begin{split}\hat{n}_{e}&=\hat{n}_{r}-\frac{\ln{\left[\hat{T_{s}}/\hat{T_{r}}\right]}}{jk_{0}l_{s}}\end{split}

(1)

where
k_{0}

l_{s}
is the effective sample length, and
\hat{n}_{r}

\hat{n}_{r}

[
29

[
9

Finally, the sample’s refractive index

was found from measured CPW’s effective refractive index,
\hat{n}_{e}

[
29
]

\begin{split}\hat{n}_{e}^{2}=\hat{\epsilon}_{e}=1+\frac{1}{2}(\hat{n}_{s}^{2}-1)\frac{K(k)K(k^{\prime}_{s})}{K(k^{\prime})K(k_{s})}+\\
+\frac{1}{2}(\hat{n}_{sub}^{2}-1)\frac{K(k)K(k^{\prime}_{sub})}{K(k^{\prime})K(k_{sub})}\end{split}

(2)

where
K
is the complete elliptical integral of the first kind, and
k
,
k^{\prime}
,
k_{s}
,
k^{\prime}_{s}
,
k_{sub}
,
k^{\prime}_{sub}

29

IV
Conclusion

This letter presents an on-chip dielectric spectroscopy method capable of measuring high-loss aqueous samples enabled by VNA and ground-signal-ground probes.
Similar attempts of broadband measurements of aqueous solutions with TDS had problems resolving the complex refractive index for high water content solutions at frequencies higher than
0.5\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\mathrm{H}\mathrm{z}

[
12
]
or lost sensitivity from not placing sample where the sensor’s field is strongest
[
11
]
.
The main reasons why the presented method could measure aqueous samples sensitively is due to (1) a higher coupling efficiency when exciting the CPWs using the contact probes compared to other methods which couple a free-space beam into the substrate via an antenna; (2) the higher average power of continuous-wave THz signals produced by the VNA compared to other pulsed methods, like TDS; (3) a low-loss design of the CPW.
The high dynamic range (typically
50\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
at calibration plane) allows to measure water with a sample length up to approximately
0.6\text{\,}\mathrm{m}\mathrm{m}
.
Some drawbacks of this method are that (1) is limited to frequencies up to 1.1 THz, due to lack of contact probes at higher frequencies; (2) being limited to rectangular waveguide bands for a given setup, and (3) a higher cost.
In perspective, this method is a first step towards a miniaturized system for sensing high-loss aqueous samples at THz frequencies, which could provide an integrated, accurate, and controlled platform to study fundamental biological phenomena in their native environment.

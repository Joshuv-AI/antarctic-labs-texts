A Riemann Hilbert approach to the study of the generating function associated to the Pearcey process
Thomas Chouteau

Abstract
Using Riemann-Hilbert methods, we establish a Tracy-Widom like formula for the generating function of the occupancy numbers of the Pearcey process. This formula is linked to a coupled vector differential equation of order three. We also obtain a non linear coupled heat equation. Combining these two equations we obtain a PDE for the logarithm of the the generating function of the Pearcey process.



Keywords:
Pearcey process, Riemann-Hilbert problems, Integrable operator, Generating function.

1
Introduction

The Pearcey process is a universal determinantal point process associated to the Pearcey kernel (see (
2.1
) for the definition of the kernel). It appeared for the first time in the spectral analysis of random matrices with external sources
[
7
]
. If
2N
is the size of matrices, the Pearcey process describes the behaviour, when
N\to\infty
, of eigenvalues near a point where the density of states admits a cusp-like gap. Another model linked with the Pearcey process are
2N
1-dimension non intersecting Brownian motions
[
6
]
starting from 0 at
t=0
and ending at
\pm a
(half particles at
a
and the others at
-a
) at time
t=1
. For this model, in the large
N
-limit, there exists a time
t_{c}
such that, for
t<t_{c}
the distribution of particles is supported in an interval and for
t>t_{c}
it splits in two disjoint intervals. The distribution of particles of the Brownian motion for
t
close to
t_{c}
is described by the Pearcey process. A last example which reveals the universality of the Pearcey process is the one of random skew plane partition
[
17
]
. Studying the limit shape associated to this model leads to different processes: Beta process, extended Airy process, and extended Pearcey process.

As known for the Airy process
[
19
,
2
]
, it is possible to express probabilities associated to the Pearcey process in terms of some partial differential equations by studying the Fredholm determinant of the Pearcey kernel operator (
2.1
). Studying the Brownian motion model at several times and deriving the kernel associated to the Pearcey process, Tracy and Widom
[
20
]
, obtained partial differential equations for the distribution associated to the Pearcey process. Using KP-tau functions and Hirota bilinear equations for the study of random matrices with external sources, Adler and van Moerbeke
[
3
]
introduced a non linear fourth order PDE for the Pearcey process. With Riemann-Hilbert methods Bertola and Cafasso
[
5
]
obtained the same PDE as Adler and van Moerbeke and introduced a new one for the gap probability for the Pearcey process.

The aim of this article is to present a Tracy-Widom formula for the generating function of the Pearcey process linked with two vector valued functions satisfying a system of coupled non linear third order differential equations and of non linear heat equation.

Let
N>1
be an integer,
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}}:=(a_{1},...,a_{N})
with
a_{1}<...<a_{N}
and
\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}=(k_{0},k_{1},...,k_{N-1},k_{N})
such that
k_{0}=k_{N}=0
,
k_{j}\in[0,1]
,
j=1,...,N-1
and
k_{j}\neq k_{j+1},j=0,...,N-1
.

Consider the Pearcey process associated to the kernel
K_{P}
(
2.1
) (which depends on an additional positive parameter
\tau
) and define its generating function

F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}},\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}):=\mathbb{E}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N-1}(1-k_{j})^{\sharp{(a_{j},a_{j+1})}}\right)

(1.1)

where
\sharp I
is the random variable counting the number of points of the process in the interval
I
.

This generating function is well defined for
(a_{i})_{1\leqslant i\leqslant N}
finites.

Computing derivatives with respect to
k_{j}
’s of
F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}},\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})
allows us to express the joint probability of occupancy numbers of particles as follows:

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{j=1}^{N-1}\sharp(a_{j},a_{j+1})=m_{j}\right)=\left.\dfrac{(-1)^{m_{1}+...+m_{N-1}}}{m_{1}!...m_{N-1}!}\dfrac{\partial^{m_{1}+...+m_{N-1}}}{\partial k_{1}^{m_{1}}...\partial k_{N-1}^{m_{N-1}}}F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}},\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})\right._{\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}=(1,...,1)}

(1.2)

(see for instance
[
15
]
for a similar computation).

Remark 1.1
.

Since for all
B\subset\mathbb{R}
bounded Borel set
\sharp B<\infty
almost surely,
F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}},\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})>0
.

Indeed, with Jensen’s inequality,

F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}},\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})\geqslant\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\log(1-k_{j})\mathbb{E}\left(\sharp(a_{j},a_{j+1})\right)\right)

and
\forall 1\leqslant j\leqslant N-1,\ \log(1-k_{j})\mathbb{E}\left(\sharp(a_{j},a_{j+1})\right)>-\infty
.

Charlier and Moreillon studied the generating function of the Pearcey process
[
10
]
on intervals of the form
(-x_{j},x_{j})
. They considered a parameter
r
of dilatation of their intervals and obtained an expression for the generating function of the Pearcey process linked with a Hamiltonian. Recently, Kimura and Zahabi
[
16
]
introduced higher order Pearcey kernels and obtained a Hamiltonian structure for the level spacing distribution by studying a different Lax Pair for the Pearcey process on intervals of the form
[-s,s]
.

In this article, we follow a different way. We consider the generating function (
1.1
) not necessarily on symmetric intervals, and we use a parameter
s
of translation of intervals instead of dilatation. This work is inspired by works of Claeys and Doeraene
[
11
]
and Charlier and Doeraene
[
9
]
where they studied the generating function for the Airy process and for the Bessel process. Recently Cafasso and Tarricone
[
8
]
obtained a Tracy-Widom type formula for the generating function associated to the higher order Airy process.

The generating function associated to the Pearcey process satisfies the following:

.

Let

Defining

2
From generating function of the Pearcey process to a Riemann-Hilbert Problem

In this section we establish a link between the generating function
F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}}+s,\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})
and the Fredholm determinant of an integrable operator and introduce the Riemann-Hilbert Problem (RHP) associated to this integrable operator.

The Pearcey kernel operator

The Pearcey process is a determinantal point process on
\mathbb{R}
associated to the Pearcey kernel operator.

For
(x,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
define
\theta_{x}(\mu):=\dfrac{\mu^{4}}{4}-\dfrac{\tau\mu^{2}}{2}-x\mu
and introduce the Pearcey Kernel

K_{P}(x,y;\tau):=\dfrac{1}{(2i\pi)^{2}}\int_{\Sigma}\int_{i\mathbb{R}}\dfrac{e^{\theta_{x}(\mu)-\theta_{y}(\lambda)}}{(\lambda-\mu)}d\lambda d\mu

(2.1)

where
\Sigma:=\Sigma_{-}\cup\Sigma_{+}
is as in the Figure
1
:

Figure 1:
The contour for the operator
\mathbb{K}_{P}

Introduce
\mathbb{K}_{P}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with kernel
K_{P}
. According to Theorem 2 of
[
18
]
, if
s
and
a_{i}
’s are all finite, then the generating function
F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}}+s,\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})
and the Fredholm determinant of the operator

are linked in the following way:

F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}}+s,\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})=\det\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}k_{j}\chi_{(a_{j}+s,a_{j+1}+s)}\mathbb{K}_{P}\right)

(2.2)

where
\chi_{I}
is the characteristic function of the interval
I
.

From Pearcey kernel operator to an integrable operator

We link the Fredholm determinant of the operator
\mathbb{K}_{P}
with the one of an integrable operator in the sense of
[
14
]
so that we study
F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}}+s,\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})
with Riemann-Hilbert problem associated to integrable operator.

Define

\tilde{f}(\mu)=\dfrac{1}{2i\pi}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}e^{\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\mu)}\chi_{\Sigma}(\mu)\\
\hline\cr\sqrt{k_{1}-k_{0}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\mu)+(a_{1}+s)\mu}\chi_{i\mathbb{R}}(\mu)\\
\vdots\\
\sqrt{k_{N}-k_{N-1}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\mu)+(a_{N}+s)\mu}\chi_{i\mathbb{R}}(\mu)\end{array}\right)

(2.3)

\tilde{g}(\mu)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\mu)}\chi_{i\mathbb{R}}(\mu)\\
\hline\cr\sqrt{k_{1}-k_{0}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\mu)-(a_{1}+s)\mu}\chi_{\Sigma}(\mu)\\
\vdots\\
\sqrt{k_{N}-k_{N-1}}e^{\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\mu)-(a_{N}+s)\mu}\chi_{\Sigma}(\mu)\end{array}\right)

(2.4)

We decompose
\tilde{f}
and
\tilde{g}
in a block

and a vector of size
N
because in what follows we will study a Riemann-Hilbert problem of size
(N+1)\times(N+1)
and matrices will be partitioned in four blocks of size

,

and

Define
\mathbb{K}:L^{2}(\Sigma\cup i\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\Sigma\cup i\mathbb{R})

[
14
]
with kernel

K(u,v)=\dfrac{{\tilde{f}}^{T}(u)\tilde{g}(v)}{u-v}

(2.5)

We establish the relation between
F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}}+s,\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})
and the Fredholm determinant of the operator
\mathbb{K}

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}}+s,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})
be as in Theorem

\mathbb{K}
be as in (

F(\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}}+s,\tau,\overrightarrow{\mathbf{k}})=\det(1-\mathbb{K})_{L^{2}(\Sigma\cup i\mathbb{R})}

Proof.

The idea of the proof is to compose the operator
\mathbb{K}
with multiplication operator and Fourier operator so that its Fredholm determinant will not change and to obtain an operator defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with kernel
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}k_{j}\chi_{(a_{j}+s,a_{j+1}+s)}(x)K_{P}(x,y)
.

The proof of this proposition is similar to the one of Theorem 4.1 in
[
5
]
and can be adapted as follows.

Since
i\mathbb{R}
and
\Sigma
are disjoint, one can decompose

L^{2}(i\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}(\Sigma)

\det(1-\mathbb{K})_{L^{2}(\Sigma\cup i\mathbb{R})}=\det\left(1-\begin{pmatrix}0&\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}_{j}\\
\mathcal{F}&0\end{pmatrix}\right)_{L^{2}(i\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}(\Sigma)}=\det\left(1-\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{G}_{j}\circ\mathcal{F}\right)_{L^{2}(i\mathbb{R})}

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}_{j}
are defined below with
\mathcal{G}_{j}
depending on
k_{j}
’s

\begin{array}[]{rrrcl}\mathcal{F}&:&L^{2}(i\mathbb{R})&\longrightarrow&L^{2}(\Sigma)\\
&&f&\longmapsto&\displaystyle\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\lambda)}}{2i\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\mu)}}{\lambda-\mu}f(\mu)d\mu\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{rrrcl}\mathcal{G}_{j}&:&L^{2}(\Sigma)&\longrightarrow&L^{2}(i\mathbb{R})\\
&&g&\longmapsto&\displaystyle(k_{j}-k_{j-1})\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\xi)+\xi(a_{j}+s)}}{2i\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{e^{\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\lambda)-\lambda(a_{j}+s)}}{\xi-\lambda}g(\lambda)d\lambda\end{array}

Then composing with a multiplication operator
\mathcal{M}
and using a Fourier operator
\mathcal{T}
(for example as in
[
12
]
) it is possible to relate Fredholm determinants of
\mathbb{K}
and
\mathbb{K}_{P}

to one on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

With
\mathcal{M}:=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\theta_{0}(\mu)}
and

\begin{array}[]{rrrcl}\mathcal{T}&:&L^{2}(i\mathbb{R})&\longrightarrow&L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\\

\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{M}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{G}_{j}\circ\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{M}\circ\mathcal{T}^{-1}

If
x<a_{1}+s
, then
x<a_{j}+s
for all

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{T}\circ\mathcal{M}^{-1}\circ\mathcal{G}_{j}\circ\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{M}\circ\mathcal{T}^{-1}

The same holds for
x>a_{N}+s
.

If
x\in(a_{j}+s,a_{j+1}+s)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{L}_{j}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}k_{\ell}-k_{\ell-1}}{(2i\pi)^{2}}\int_{i\mathbb{R}}\int_{\Sigma_{+}}\dfrac{e^{\theta_{x}(\lambda)-\theta_{y}(\mu)}}{(\mu-\lambda)}d\lambda d\mu

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{N}k_{\ell}-k_{\ell-1}}{(2i\pi)^{2}}\int_{i\mathbb{R}}\int_{\Sigma_{-}}\dfrac{e^{\theta_{x}(\lambda)-\theta_{y}(\mu)}}{(\mu-\lambda)}d\lambda d\mu

\displaystyle=k_{j}\chi_{(a_{j}+s,a_{j+1}+s)}(x)K_{P}(x,y)

This concludes the proof.

3
Study of the Riemann-Hilbert Problem associated to the Pearcey kernel operator

In this section, we obtain a Lax Pair associated to the previous RHP which leads to a system of coupled vector differential equation for

and

(see equation (

) for the definition of

and

). Studying the derivative with respect to

A Lax Pair for
\Psi

Let
\Gamma
be a solution of
RHP

2.2
and
T
be the gauge transformation:

T(\mu):=e^{\dfrac{1}{N+1}\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\theta_{a_{j}+s}(\mu)}\diag\left(1,e^{-\theta_{a_{1}+s}(\mu)},...,e^{-\theta_{a_{N}+s}(\mu)}\right).

Defining
\Psi(\mu):=\Gamma(\mu)T(\mu)

\Psi
.

Proposition 3.1

(Lax pair for
\Psi
)
.

If
\Gamma
is solution of RHP

then
\Psi
satisfy a system of partial differential equation.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{s}\Psi(\mu)=A(\mu)\Psi(\mu)\\
\partial_{\mu}\Psi(\mu)=B(\mu)\Psi(\mu)\\
\partial_{\tau}\Psi(\mu)=C(\mu)\Psi(\mu)\end{array}\right.\\

(3.1)

where

A(\mu)=\mu A_{1}+A_{0}=\dfrac{\mu}{N+1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}-N&(0)\\
\hline\cr(0)&I_{N}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&p^{T}\\
\hline\cr-q&(0)\end{array}\right)

(3.2)

B(\mu)=\mu^{3}\tilde{B}_{3}+\mu^{2}B_{2}+\mu(\tilde{B}_{1}+B_{1})+\tilde{B}_{0}+B_{0}

’s and

\tilde{B}_{3}=-A_{1},\ \tilde{B}_{1}=\tau A_{1}

\tilde{B}_{0}=sA_{1}+\dfrac{1}{N+1}\diag\left(-\sum_{j=1}^{N}a_{j},Na_{1}-\sum_{j\neq 1}a_{j},...,Na_{N}-\sum_{j\neq N}a_{j}\right)

C(\mu)=\mu^{2}\tilde{C}_{2}+\mu C_{1}+C_{0}=\dfrac{\mu^{2}}{2}A_{1}+\mu C_{1}+C_{0}

Proof.

It is easy to show that if
\Gamma
satisfies the previous

then

\overrightarrow{\mathbf{a}},\tau,s
and
\mu

Then
\Psi

\partial_{s}\Psi

A(\mu):=\partial_{s}\Psi(\mu)\Psi(\mu)^{-1}

\Psi
and
\partial_{s}\Psi

A

\mu

A(\mu)=\mu A_{1}+A_{0}=\dfrac{\mu}{N+1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}-N&(0)\\
\hline\cr(0)&I_{N}\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&p^{T}\\
\hline\cr-q&(0)\end{array}\right)

Using the same method we conclude that
B(\mu):=\partial_{\mu}\Psi(\mu)\Psi(\mu)^{-1}
(respectively
C(\mu):=\partial_{\tau}\Psi(\mu)\Psi(\mu)^{-1}
) is a polynomial of degree 3 (respectively degree 2).
We will not do all computations for

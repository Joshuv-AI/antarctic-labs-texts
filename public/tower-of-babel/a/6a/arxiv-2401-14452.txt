The Kalb-Ramond field and Gravitational Parity Violation
Tucker Manton, Stephon Alexander

Abstract
Gravitational wave physics can probe theories that extend beyond General Relativity. Motivated by recent attention on the Kalb-Ramond field as a dark matter candidate, in this work, we study a parity violating dimension four operator which couples the dual Riemann curvature to the 2-form field. After mapping the equations of motion for the right- and left-handed gravitational wave amplitudes to the novel parameterization recently presented in Jenks:2023pmk , we discuss various constraints on the model parameters in light of the coincident electromagnetic/gravitational wave signal of GW170817 and the GWTC-3 dataset.

Introduction

The advent of gravitational wave (GW) astronomy has ushered in a new era of modern physics, pioneered by the growing catalog of observations from the LIGO-Virgo-Kagra collaboration
LIGOScientific:2014pky
;
VIRGO:2014yos
;
LIGOScientific:2020ibl
;
KAGRA:2021vkt
with recent data supporting a stochastic GW background from the pulsar timing array collaborations
NANOGrav:2023gor
;
NANOGrav:2023hvm
;
EPTA:2023fyk
;
Xu:2023wog
;
Reardon:2023gzh
. GW signals provide a rich compliment to electromagnetic signals of astrophysical phenomena
LIGOScientific:2017zic
, as well as offering a new arena for testing General Relativity (GR) in strong field regimes
LIGOScientific:2021sio
. An immense outstanding question in the field is not if GR will fail to describe a phenomenon, but when and how. In particular, observing gravitational parity violation would be a smoking gun for physics beyond GR, as the Einstein-Hilbert term alone produces no parity violation. Parity violating gravitational theories have gathered interest in recent decades for a variety of reasons. Observing parity violation in CMB polarization measurements was considered as far back as 1998
Lue:1998mq
.

Dynamical Chern-Simons theory (dCS) is likely the most studied parity violating model for gravity. In addition to the two usual massless spin-2 degrees of freedom in GR, dCS includes a pseudoscalar field
\phi
with a canonical kinetic term coupled to the Pontryagin density
\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}R^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
, where
\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}_{\ \ \ \rho\sigma}
is the dual Riemann tensor, which arises in many contexts
Yunes:2009hc
(see
Alexander:2009tp
and references therein for a review). Interesting physics follows if the dCS pseudoscalar acts as the inflaton. This includes a preheating mechanism
Alexander:2014bsa
, leptogenesis
Alexander:2004us
, as well as the potential for parity violation to be detected in large-scale structure via galaxy correlations
Creque-Sarbinowski:2023wmb
. Perturbations to rotating black holes were also studied in dCS theory recently in
Wagle:2023fwl
, and in
Nojiri:2019nar
, the propagation of gravitational waves in dCS were compared to standard
f(R)
modified gravity theories.

There are numerous other parity violating theories on the market. One such class is the ghost-free higher derivative models
Crisostomi:2017ugk
;
Qiao:2021fwi
, which are characterized by Lagrangians of the form
\mathcal{L}(g,\partial g,\partial^{2}g)
, and individual terms are contractions of the metric, Riemann, and the Levi-Civita tensors. Higher derivative theories are generically plagued by Ostrogradsky instabilities
Woodard:2015zca
, therefore such theories can at best be valid up to the mass scale of the ghost degrees of freedom from an effective field theory standpoint. Adjacent to these are the ghost-free scalar tensor models, such as those discussed in
Nishizawa:2018srh
. Here, derivatives of a scalar field couple to contractions of the Riemann and Levi-Civita tensors. As in the higher order derivative theories, constraints on the Lagrangian coefficients must be imposed in order to avoid Ostrogradsky ghosts. There is also a parity violating addition to the Hor̂ava-Lifshitz approach to quantum gravity
Horava:2009uw
;
Wang:2017brl
, which was studied in the context of gravitational wave physics in
Zhu:2013fja
. A different construction was proposed in
Takahashi:2022mew
; here, the authors employed conformal transformations of the metric where the conformal factor is a function of the Pontryagin density. This produces a parity violating effect in the Einstein frame, which was recently used to study modifications of the Kerr metric
Tahara:2023pyg
.

Alternatively, one can relax the standard assumptions of metricity (the so-called teleparallel gravity theories) and vanishing torsion
Conroy:2019ibo
;
Iosifidis:2020dck
;
Freidel:2005sn
;
Mavromatos:2023wkk
;
Shapiro:2014kma
;
Szczachor:2012hh
. In theories of this type, parity violating couplings between the Levi-Civita and either non-metricity or torsion tensors are expected on general grounds, which has been known since the late 1970’s
Hojman:1980kv
. The authors of
Jenks:2023pmk
analyzed many of these examples, notably dynamical Chern-Simons, ghost-free scalar tensor gravity, (symmetric) teleparallel, and Hor̂ava-Lifshitz.

A common feature of many of these models is that the parity violating operators have dimension greater than four. Returning back to dCS, the interaction is a dimension five operator, and therefore necessitates a mass scale,

\mathcal{L}_{\text{dCS}}=\frac{\phi}{m_{\text{CS}}}R\tilde{R},

(1)

implying that its potential affect on gravitational parity violation is suppressed. Moreover, dCS being quadratic in curvature has the consequence that the field equations are higher than second order
PhysRevD.86.124031
. It is therefore well worth exploring the possibility of lower dimensional operators which will not come with an (inverse) mass scale and, idealistically, be manifestly safe from instabilities arising from higher derivatives. Studying such an interaction is precisely the purpose of this note.

The presence of the Levi-Civita symbol contracted with the Riemann tensor results in an overall sign difference between the equations of motion for gravitational wave amplitudes when written in the circular basis. That is, for a gravitational wave propagating in the
z

h_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\

0&i(h_{\text{L}}-h_{\text{R}})&h_{\text{L}}+h_{\text{R}}&0\\
0&0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

(2)

where the right- and left-handed strains are related to the usual

amplitudes by
h_{\text{R,L}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(h_{+}\pm ih_{\times}).
Varying the Einstein-Hilbert Lagrangian with respect to each amplitude results in simple wave equations for each. Parity violation will then follow provided an additional interaction term sources the left- and right-handed amplitudes with a different sign, say

\begin{split}\Box h_{\text{L}}&=+\Xi,\\
\Box h_{\text{R}}&=-\Xi,\end{split}

(3)

for some
\Xi
that is an expression involving additional matter fields and perhaps their derivatives

II
Features of the KR Lagrangian

The KR field is described by the Lagrangian
PhysRevD.9.2273

\mathcal{L}_{\text{B}}=\frac{1}{12}H_{\mu\nu\rho}H^{\mu\nu\rho}-V(B),

(7)

where

H_{\mu\nu\rho}=\partial_{[\mu}B_{\nu\rho]}=\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu\rho}+\partial_{\nu}B_{\rho\mu}+\partial_{\rho}B_{\mu\nu}

(8)

is the KR field strength and
V(B)
is some potential. When
V=0
, the theory enjoys a gauge symmetry with transformation
3
3

3

We use the anti-symmetrization convention
A_{[\mu}B_{\nu]}=A_{\mu}B_{\nu}-A_{\nu}B_{\mu}
.

B_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow B_{\mu\nu}+\partial_{[\mu}A_{\nu]}

(9)

for an arbitrary vector
A_{\mu}(x)
, corresponding to a shift in
B
by an exact 2-form. There is an additional transformation involving a scalar field,

A_{\mu}\rightarrow A_{\mu}+\partial_{\mu}\lambda.

(10)

Thus although
A_{\mu}
has four components, only three are independent. The subsidiary gauge transformation (
10
) is a shift involving an exact 1-form.

The gauge invariance (
9
), (
10
) is broken explicitly by the potential
V
and the interaction (
6
). This is analogous to the Proca theory of a massive vector boson, which is known to be dual to the massive KR field in certain regimes
Hell:2021wzm
;
Capanelli:2023uwv
. Regarding interactions with the gravitational waves, we will see that a simple and interesting scenario occurs when the KR field’s magnitude settles at a non-zero, constant (classical) value,
B^{2}=B_{(0)}^{2}
. This can be modeled by a potential of the form

V(B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}-v^{2}).

(11)

When the KR field relaxes to the minimum, we have

v^{2}\rightarrow\bar{g}^{\mu\alpha}\bar{g}^{\nu\beta}B_{\mu\nu}^{(0)}B_{\alpha\beta}^{(0)},

(12)

where
\bar{g}^{\mu\nu}
is the unperturbed metric. The explicit form of the potential (
11
) is not relevant for this work, beyond requiring that it possesses a nontrivial stable minimum. In theories of this type, such potentials have the consequence that the physics can display (spontaneous) Lorentz violation, despite the Lagrange density being Lorentz invariant
Altschul:2009ae
.

The KR field can be parameterized in a particularly simple form
Altschul:2009ae
;
Maluf:2021eyu
. In general, a single KR field in a constant background value breaks the isotropy of the cosmology
Maluf:2021eyu
, analogous to the case of a single vector field
Golovnev:2008cf
. Global isotropy can be recovered if multiple vector fields take on constant values of equal magnitude orthogonal to each other, and we expect a similar story to hold here. We will save a treatment of such a scenario for future work, and will solely consider the effect of the interaction on gravitational waves approximating an isotropic cosmology. We therefore parameterize the background field as
4
4

4

Note in difference from
Altschul:2009ae
;
Maluf:2021eyu
, we include the factor of
\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
for convenience.

B^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&-\mathcal{V}_{e}a^{2}&0&0\\
\mathcal{V}_{e}a^{2}&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&\mathcal{V}_{m}a^{2}\\
0&0&-\mathcal{V}_{m}a^{2}&0\end{pmatrix},

(13)

where
a(\eta)
is the scale factor and
\mathcal{V}_{e},\mathcal{V}_{m}
are taken to be real numbers, and can be thought of as the “electric” and “magnetic” parts of the KR field, respectively. It then follows that the minimum of the potential is at

v=\sqrt{\mathcal{V}_{m}^{2}-\mathcal{V}_{e}^{2}},

(14)

independent of the background evolution of the spacetime. It is also natural to expect a mass term for the KR field in this scenario. For the field to be frozen at the bottom of the minimum surrounding
v
, we require the mass to be slightly smaller than but on the order of
v
. This will guarantee that the symmetry breaking minimum exists while simultaneously ensuring the field is heavy enough such that no radial excitations occur, allowing us to treat the field as constant. However, as we look to make contact with observations in the late universe with this construction, this is not a requirement in early universe physics.

An alternative justification for considering a frozen KR background is by simply examining the background evolution in a flat FLRW spacetime without the interaction term. The field equation in this case is

B^{\prime\prime}_{\mu\nu}+\delta_{\eta[\mu}B^{\prime\prime}_{\nu]\eta}+2\mathcal{H}\delta_{\eta[\mu}B^{\prime}_{\nu]\eta}+a^{2}\frac{\partial V}{\partial B^{\mu\nu}}=0,

(15)

where we’ve assumed
B_{\mu\nu}=B_{\mu\nu}(\eta)
and
\mathcal{H}=a^{\prime}/a
is the Hubble parameter. As is standard in an expanding cosmological background, the presence of Hubble friction ultimately dampens the evolution of the unperturbed field until it rests at a constant value. This, of course, will be at the minimum of the potential where
\frac{\partial V}{\partial B^{\mu\nu}}=0.

III
Features of the interaction

The interaction (
6
) is a specific case of a more general coupling

\mathcal{L}\sim R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}P^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},

(16)

since here, we can move the Levi-Civita symbol over to one of the
B
-fields and equivalently write

\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=-\frac{\xi}{2}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}B^{\rho\sigma},

(17)

where
\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu\nu}_{\ \ \alpha\beta}B^{\alpha\beta}
. From (
16
), we see that in considering equations of motion, we have the derivative

\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}=P^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma},

(18)

which implies that
P
must inherit all of the symmetries of the Riemann tensor. That fact is not manifest as it is written in (
17
), therefore care must be taken when we derive the equations of motion covariantly. In particular, we have that

\begin{split}P^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}&=\frac{1}{3}\Big(\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}B^{\rho\sigma}+B^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}^{\rho\sigma}\Big)\\
&+\frac{1}{6}\Big(\tilde{B}^{\mu\rho}B^{\nu\sigma}+B^{\mu\rho}\tilde{B}^{\nu\sigma}+\tilde{B}^{\rho\nu}B^{\mu\sigma}+B^{\rho\nu}\tilde{B}^{\mu\sigma}\Big).\end{split}

(19)

It is tedious but straightforward to show that this indeed possesses all symmetries of the Riemann tensor,

P_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=-P_{\nu\mu\rho\sigma}=-P_{\mu\nu\sigma\rho}=P_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu},

(20)

along with the cyclic property

P_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+P_{\mu\rho\sigma\nu}+P_{\mu\sigma\nu\rho}=0.

(21)

However, when contracted with the Riemann tensor, all of the terms combine such that

R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}P^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}B^{\rho\sigma}.

(22)

From an effective field theory standpoint, there are additional dimension-four operators coupling the KR field to curvature tensors involving the Levi-Civita symbol that we could consider at linear order in the curvature;

\mathcal{L}_{B\tilde{B}R}=\xi_{2}B^{\mu\lambda}\tilde{B}^{\nu}_{\ \ \lambda}R_{\mu\nu}+\xi_{3}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}R.

(23)

Firstly, these terms are proportional due to the identity
B_{\mu}^{\ \lambda}\tilde{B}_{\lambda\nu}=\frac{1}{4}g_{\mu\nu}B_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{B}^{\alpha\beta},
but more relevant for our purposes, such operators do not contribute to parity violation. In this work we will therefore focus our attention on (
6
).

V
GW solution in FLRW background

We will look to make contact with the parameterization given in

Now, the authors of
Jenks:2023pmk
go on to write down the solutions for the polarization modes as a function of redshift
z
in terms of the parameters of (
29
), which take the general form

h_{\text{R,L}}=h^{\text{GR}}_{\text{R,L}}\text{exp}\Bigg\{\mp\frac{[k(1+z)]^{n}}{2}\Bigg(\frac{\alpha_{n_{0}}}{\Lambda_{\text{PV}}^{n}}z_{n}+\frac{\beta_{n_{0}}}{\Lambda_{\text{PV}}^{n-1}}D_{n+1}\Bigg)\Bigg\}\text{exp}\Bigg\{\pm i\frac{[k(1+z)]^{m}}{2}\Bigg(\frac{\gamma_{m_{0}}}{\Lambda_{\text{PV}}^{m}}z_{m}+\frac{\delta_{m_{0}}}{\Lambda_{\text{PV}}^{m-1}}D_{m+1}\Bigg)\Bigg\}.

(41)

Here, the summations over
m
and
n
are implicit,
h^{\text{GR}}
is the standard oscillating solution from GR,
D_{\alpha}
and
z_{\alpha}
are defined as

\begin{split}D_{\alpha}&=(1+z)^{1-\alpha}\int\frac{(1+z)^{\alpha-2}}{H(z)}dz,\\
z_{\alpha}&=(1+z)^{-\alpha}\int\frac{dz}{(1+z)^{1-\alpha}}.\end{split}

(42)

The
n_{0},m_{0}
subscript on
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta
denote their late time, slowly varying approximations. Our solution has
\beta_{1}=-\tfrac{1}{5}\gamma_{0}=\xi\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{m}^{2},
thus only
m=0,

n=1
are nontrivial. In the exponent, we therefore have
z_{0}=\text{ln}(1+z)
and
D_{2}=(1+z)^{-1}D_{C}\equiv D_{A},
where
D_{C}
and
D_{A}
are respectively the comoving and angular-diameter distances. Our solution is then

h_{\text{R,L}}=h^{\text{GR}}_{\text{R,L}}\text{exp}\Big\{\mp\tfrac{\xi\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{m}^{2}}{2}\Big(k(1+z)D_{2}+5iz_{0}\Big)\Big\}.

(43)

As we discussed in Sec.
I
, the interaction being a dimension-four operator implies that the coupling
\xi
is dimensionless, such that there is not a mass scale setting a clear UV cutoff. This manifests in (
43
) by way of only
n=1,m=0
contributions from (
37
) along with
\alpha_{n}=\delta_{m}=0
; no factors of
\Lambda_{\text{PV}}
survive in the parameterization (
29
).

The dispersion relation for any parity violating theory which can be classified by the parameterization (
29
) is given by equation (30) in
Jenks:2023pmk
. In our case, this becomes

\begin{split}\omega_{\text{R,L}}^{2}&=k^{2}\big\{1+\lambda_{\text{R,L}}k^{-1}\gamma_{0}\mathcal{H}\big\}\\
&=k^{2}\{1+\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}k^{-1}\},\end{split}

(44)

defining

\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}=-\lambda_{\text{R,L}}(5\xi\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{m}^{2}\mathcal{H}).

(45)

From this we easily obtain the phase
v_{p}=\omega/k
and group
v_{d}=d\omega/dk
velocities,

\begin{split}v_{p}&=\sqrt{1+\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}k^{-1}},\\
v_{g}&=\frac{1+\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}k^{-1}}{\sqrt{1+\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}k^{-1}}},\end{split}

(46)

which are clearly different between
h_{\text{R}}
and
h_{\text{L}}
. Taking a closer look at the group velocity, we have

\begin{split}v_{g}&=(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}k^{-1})(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}k^{-1}+\tfrac{3}{8}\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}^{2}k^{-2}+\cdots)\\
&\simeq 1+\tfrac{3}{8}\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}^{2}k^{-2}+O\Big(\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}^{3}k^{-3}\Big)\end{split}

(47)

for
(\xi\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{m}^{2}\mathcal{H})\ll 1.
Since the correction appears at
O(\tilde{\lambda}^{2})
, the sign difference between the left and right handed amplitudes squares away such that they propagate with equal group velocity to leading order. And because the correction is non-negative, the model predicts a propagation speed slightly larger than one. Comparing to the phase velocity, we see that

v_{p}\simeq 1+\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}k^{-1}-\tfrac{1}{8}\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}^{2}k^{-2}+O\Big(\tilde{\lambda}_{\text{R,L}}^{3}k^{-3}\Big).

(48)

The correction to the phase velocity due to the parity violating interaction clearly appears at a lower order than the group velocity, and the sign difference between
h_{\text{R}}
and
h_{\text{L}}
survives.

VI
Comments on constraints

The possibility of parity violation to be observed in gravitational wave detectors was suggested well over 10 years ago
Alexander:2007kv
;
Yunes:2010yf
. The data currently being gathered from merger processes
LIGOScientific:2014pky
;
VIRGO:2014yos
;
LIGOScientific:2020ibl
;
KAGRA:2021vkt
can in principle constrain parity violating parameters, although much more progress on this front will be made from third generation detectors due to their increased sensitivities
Maggiore:2019uih
;
Evans:2021gyd
. Additionally, very recently, an analysis to constrain parity violation was carried out in
Callister:2023tws
using data from pulsar timing arrays
NANOGrav:2023gor
;
NANOGrav:2023hvm
;
EPTA:2023fyk
;
Xu:2023wog
;
Reardon:2023gzh
. The two primary effects of interest are velocity birefringence and amplitude birefringence. The former refers to right- and left-handed waves propagating with different frequency dependent phase shifts, the latter corresponding to an attenuation or amplification of one polarization over another. It is also the case that modified theories of gravity generally predict a propagation speed different from unity. In what follows, we will focus on propagation speed and amplitude birefringence.

VI.1
Propagation speed

The observation of the coincident gamma ray burst (GRB)/gravitational wave signal GW170817 has constrained the GW propagation speed to be
LIGOScientific:2017zic

-3\times 10^{-15}<c_{\text{gw}}-1<7\times 10^{-16}.

(49)

The measurement was used to constrain parity violating effects in ghost-free scalar tensor models in
Nishizawa:2018srh
and in symmetric teleparallel gravity in
Conroy:2019ibo
. Using our result for the group velocity (
47
), this translates to
a bound on the parameter
\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{m}=\mathcal{V}_{m}/m_{p}
. If we take the dimensionless coupling to be
\xi\sim O(1)
, and use the upper bound on (
49
), then

\mathcal{V}_{m}^{4}\Bigg(\frac{\mathcal{H}^{2}}{k^{2}m_{p}^{4}}\Bigg)<7\times 10^{-17}.

(50)

This can be simplified in terms of the Hubble constant and proper wavenumber as

\mathcal{V}_{m}\sqrt{\frac{H_{0}}{ka}}\lesssim 10^{-4}m_{p}.

(51)

VI.2
Amplitude birefringence

In
Okounkova:2021xjv
, the authors studied the GWTC-2 data set to construct constraints on amplitude birefringence from binary black hole merger. They took the ansatz for the GW amplitudes
h_{\text{R,L}}=h^{\text{GR}}_{\text{R,L}}e^{\lambda_{\text{R,L}}\kappa D_{C}}
, where
\kappa
is referred to as the opacity parameter and
D_{C}
is again the coming distance. They showed that
\kappa\lesssim 0.74
Gpc
-1
, and the authors of
Jenks:2023pmk
utilized this bound to constrain the dimensionless parameter
\beta_{1}
to be less than
0.7\times 10^{-20}
at 90% confidence level. More recently, in
Ng:2023jjt
, GWTC-3 data was used to constrain the opacity parameter to be
\kappa<0.04
Gpc
-1
, which is currently the most stringent bound. Repeating the analysis in
Jenks:2023pmk
with the new bound on
\kappa
results in
\beta_{1}<3.8\times 10^{-22}
. We can easily translate this to a bound on the parameter
\mathcal{V}_{m}
in terms of the Planck mass. Again setting the coupling as
\xi\sim O(1)
, since
\xi\tilde{\mathcal{V}}_{m}^{2}=\beta_{1},
we find that
\mathcal{V}_{m}<1.9\times 10^{-11}m_{p}
, or

\mathcal{V}_{m}\lesssim 2.4\times 10^{8}\text{ GeV}.

(52)

That the upper bound on the
\mathcal{V}_{m}
parameter lies in the
O(100)
PeV range is quite interesting. From a purely theoretical standpoint, it has been known for some time
Wells:2004di
that the PeV scale is an attractive range for supersymmetry (SUSY), admitting unified gauge couplings, disallowing flavor changing neutral currents, and predicting a Higgs mass between 125-155 GeV
Bhattiprolu:2023lfh
. It has also gained attention as a candidate energy scale for dark matter
Berlin:2016vnh
, partially motivated by the detection of near-PeV gamma rays by the Tibet
\text{AS}_{\gamma}
collaboration
TibetASgamma:2021tpz
. Stringent constraints on PeV scale dark matter were derived in
Maity:2021umk
, and future data from Tibet
\text{AS}_{\gamma}
is expected to further shed light on the viability of PeV scale dark matter (see also
Cohen:2016uyg
;
Bhattacharya:2019ucd
;
Ishiwata:2019aet
;
Blanco:2018esa
for further relevant discussion of the gamma ray background).

VII
Discussion

Third generation gravitational wave detectors such as the Einstein Telescope
Maggiore:2019uih
and Cosmic Explorer
Evans:2021gyd
are anticipated to have sensitivities capable of probing beyond GR effects such as parity violation
Califano:2023aji
. Parity violation may also be observed in large scale structure
Vanzan:2023cze
;
Creque-Sarbinowski:2023wmb
. Any observation definitively signalling parity violation would be a smoking gun for gravitational physics beyond General Relativity. It is therefore of great interest to explore various models which predict gravitational parity violation in anticipation of such data. In this work, inspired by recent attention on the Kalb-Ramond field as a dark matter candidate, we considered parity violation from a dimension-four operator coupling the dual Riemann tensor to the KR fields,
\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=-\tfrac{\xi}{2}\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}B^{\mu\nu}B^{\rho\sigma}
. For simplicity, we constructed a scenario where the KR fields can be treated as being constant. This will happen provided the KR field experiences a potential that satisfies
\frac{\partial V}{\partial B^{\mu\nu}}\big|_{B^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}}=0
for
B^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}\neq 0.
Two parameters
\mathcal{V}_{e},\mathcal{V}_{m}
, characterize the degrees of freedom of
B_{\mu\nu}.
After writing down the field equations for the gravitational waves in the circular basis for arbitrary
\mathcal{V}_{m},\mathcal{V}_{e},
we restricted to the case
\mathcal{V}_{e}=0
before mapping the solution to the general parameterization presented in
Jenks:2023pmk
. Constraints from the GRB/gravitational wave signal GW170817
LIGOScientific:2017zic
and the GWTC-3
KAGRA:2021vkt
data set were considered, and the latter translated to the bound
\mathcal{V}_{m}\lesssim 10^{8}
GeV. As we discussed in Sec.
VI.2
, this upper bound for dark matter being near the PeV scale is interesting from theoretical
Wells:2004di
;
Bhattiprolu:2023lfh
and phenomenological
TibetASgamma:2021tpz
;
Maity:2021umk
standpoints.

We assumed a generic potential was responsible for the KR field settling into its background value, which triggers spontaneous Lorentz violation. As a result, (pseudo-) Nambu-Goldstone modes associated to the field fluctuations along the broken generators emerge
Bluhm:2004ep
;
Bluhm:2007bd
;
Higashijima:2001sq
;
Hernaski:2016dyk
. Consequences of this spontaneous symmetry breaking were studied extensively in
Altschul:2009ae
, where the authors derived reasonable constraints on the Lorentz violating coefficients present in certain non-minimal curvature couplings (see, e.g.
Cheng:2006us
;
Carroll:2004ai
;
Lambiase:2005kb
;
Colladay:1998fq
;
Kostelecky:1988zi
;
Kostelecky:2008ts
;
Kostelecky:2003fs
). However, such modes do not play an important role in our work here.

There are numerous next directions to pursue. On one hand, couplings to the Riemann tensor contribute nontrivially to gravitational entropy
Parikh:2009qs
;
LopesCardoso:1999cv
. Many parity violating theories, such as the interaction we studied in this work (
17
) and better studied theories such as dynamical Chern-Simons gravity, entail couplings to the Riemann tensor and therefore will modify the Wald entropy
Wald:1993nt
. Entanglement entropy in quantum field theory was studied in the context of parity violating theories in
Hughes:2015ora
, where the authors made contact with gravitational anomalies in flat space. It would be interesting to explore a connection between gravitational entropy corrections and parity violation in black hole physics. More generally, black hole solutions with KR hair modify the shadow and lensing properties in comparison to GR
Kumar:2020hgm
;
Lessa:2019bgi
. Repeating such an analysis would offer a different approach to constraining our model parameters, as we certainly expect additional modifications as a result of the interaction (
6
).

It is of significant importance to consider the consequences of the interaction (
6
) in early universe physics. The KR field and more general
p
-forms in inflationary scenarios have been been studied in Starobinsky inflation
Elizalde:2018now
,
F(R)
theories
Elizalde:2018rmz
, anisotropic inflation
Almeida:2019xzt
, and in general cosmological backgrounds
BeltranAlmeida:2018nin
. Each of these works assumed even parity and gauge invariance. Gauge invariance being preserved implies the KR field can be described in terms of one (pseudo)scalar degree of freedom, which is not an approach that we can take due to the non gauge invariance of (
6
). It is therefore interesting to explore the consequences of (
6
) in inflationary scenarios, in particular the deviations from GR for the primordial gravitational radiation, as well as consistency with other cosmological observables (for example, parity violation in the CMB was considered very recently in
Gaztaaga2024:2401.08288v1
). Allowing the KR fields to be fully dynamical opposed to frozen in a constant background value will be relevant in such scenarios. One approach would be to study the interactions with gravity in their polarization basis, as written in
Capanelli:2023uwv
. On the other hand, as mentioned in Section
II
, genuinely preserving cosmological isotropy when the KR field is in its background value should necessitate multiple fields, which may result in modifications to our results in this paper. We leave this and other explorations to future work.

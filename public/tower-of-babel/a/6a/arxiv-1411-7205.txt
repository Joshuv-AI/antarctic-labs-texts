Relative Hom-Hopf modules and total integrals
Shuangjian Guo, Xiaohui Zhang and Shengxiang Wang 1. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Guizhou University of Finance andEconomics, Guizhou 550025, China2. Department of Mathematics, Southeast University, Nanjing 210096, China3. School of Mathematics and Finance, Chuzhou University, Chuzhou 239000, China

Abstract




Abstract
Let
(H,\alpha)
be a monoidal Hom-Hopf algebra and
(A,\beta)
a right
(H,\alpha)
-Hom-comodule algebra.
We first investigate the criterion for the existence
of a total integral of
(A,\beta)
in the setting of monoidal Hom-Hopf algebras.
Also we prove that there exists a total integral
\phi:(H,\alpha)\rightarrow(A,\beta)
if and only if any representation
of the pair
(H,A)
is injective in a functorial way, as a corepresentation of
(H,\alpha)
,
which generalizes Doi’s result. Finally, we define a total quantum integral
\gamma:H\rightarrow Hom(H,A)
and prove the following affineness
criterion: if there exists a total quantum integral
\gamma
and the
canonical map
\psi:A\otimes_{B}A\rightarrow A\otimes H,\ \ a\otimes_{B}b\mapsto\beta^{-1}(a)b_{[0]}\otimes\alpha(b_{[1]})

is surjective, then the induction functor

A\otimes_{B}-:\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})_{B}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}

is an equivalence of categories.

Keywords
Monoidal Hom-Hopf algebra; total integral; relative Hom-Hopf module.

1
Introduction

Hom-algebras and Hom-coalgebras were introduced by Makhlouf and Silvestrov in

[
14
]
as generalizations of ordinary algebras and coalgebras in the following sense: the
associativity of the multiplication is replaced by the Hom-associativity and similar for
Hom-coassociativity. They also defined the structures of Hom-bialgebras and Hom-Hopf
algebras, and described some of their properties extending properties of ordinary bialgebras
and Hopf algebras in
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
.
Recently, many more properties and structures of Hom-Hopf algebras have been developed,
see
[
3
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
22
]
and references cited therein.

Caenepeel and Goyvaerts
[
1
]
studied Hom-bialgebras and Hom-Hopf algebras from
a categorical view point, and called them monoidal Hom-bialgebras and monoidal Hom-
Hopf algebras respectively, which are slightly different from the above Hom-bialgebras
and Hom-Hopf algebras.
In
[
2
]
, Chen, Wang and Zhang introduced integrals of monoidal Hom-Hopf algebras and investigated the existence and
uniqueness of integrals for finite-dimensional monoidal Hom-Hopf algebras,
they also applied the integrals to the Maschke type theorem for monoidal
Hom-Hopf algebras controlling the semisimplicity and separability of monoidal
Hom-Hopf algebras.

In
[
12
]
, the first author and Chen introduced the notion of relative Hom-Hopf modules and proved that the functor
F

from the category of relative Hom-Hopf modules to the category of right
(A,\beta)
-Hom-modules has a right
adjoint. We also introduced the notion of total integrals for
Hom-comodule algebras which has effective applications in
investigating properties of relative Hom-Hopf modules,
and proved a Maschke type theorem for the category of relative Hom-Hopf modules as an application our theory.

Total integral is a vital notion in representation theories.
Doi
[
7
]
introduced the notion of total integral for any
H
-comodule algebra
A
over a Hopf algebra
H
, which
has strong ties both to
\mathcal{M}^{H}
(i. e., the corepresentation of
H
) and to the representation of the
pair
(H,A)
(i. e., the category of relative Hopf modules
\mathcal{M}^{H}_{A}
),
and presented the famous necessary and sufficient criterion for the existence of a total integral.
Later, Menini and Militaru
[
17
]
interpreted
the criterion for the existence of a total integral with the help of forgetful functors

F:\mathcal{M}^{H}_{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{A}
and
G:\mathcal{M}^{H}_{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{H}
. Furthermore, the affineness criterion for affine
algebraic group schemes was studied by Cline et al.
[
5
]
and Oberst
[
18
]
.
Inspired by the ideas from Doi, Menini and Militaru, we want to investigate the
criterion for the existence of a total integral in the setting of monoidal Hom-Hopf algebras.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall some definitions and
properties relative to separable functors and monodial Hom-Hopf algebras which are needed later.
In Section 3, we present two pairs of important adjoint functors (see Proposition 3.1).
In Section 4, we show a necessary and
sufficient criterion for the existence of an integral on a monodial Hom-Hopf algebra (see Theorem 4.3).
In Section 5, we give the affineness criterion for relative Hom-Hopf
modules (see Theorem 5.7).

2
Preliminaries

In this section we recall some basic definitions and results related to our paper.
Throughout the paper, all algebraic systems are supposed to be over a commutative ring
k
.
The reader is referred to
[
1
]
and
[
12
]

as general references about Hom-structures.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category, there is a new category

\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathcal{C})
as follows: objects are
couples
(M,\mu)
, with
M\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mu\in Aut_{\mathcal{C}}(M)
. A morphism
f:(M,\mu)\rightarrow(N,\nu)

is a morphism
f:M\rightarrow N
in
\mathcal{C}
such that
\nu\circ f=f\circ\mu
.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{k}
denotes the category of
k
-modules.

\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
will be called the Hom-category
associated to
\mathscr{M}_{k}
. If
(M,\mu)\in\mathscr{M}_{k}
, then

\mu:M\rightarrow M
is obviously a morphism in

\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
. It is easy to show that

\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
=
(
\mathscr{H}(\mathscr{M}_{k}),~\otimes,~(I,I),~\widetilde{a},~\widetilde{l},~\widetilde{r}))
is a monoidal category by
Proposition 1.1 in
[
1
]
: the tensor product of
(M,\mu)
and
(N,\nu)
in
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
is given by the
formula
(M,\mu)\otimes(N,\nu)=(M\otimes N,\mu\otimes\nu)
.

Assume that
(M,\mu),(N,\nu),(P,\pi)\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
. The associativity and unit
constraints are given by the formulas

\displaystyle\widetilde{a}_{M,N,P}((m\otimes n)\otimes p)=\mu(m)\otimes(n\otimes\pi^{-1}(p)),

\displaystyle\widetilde{l}_{M}(x\otimes m)=\widetilde{r}_{M}(m\otimes x)=x\mu(m).

An algebra in
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
will be called a monoidal Hom-algebra.

Definition 2.1.

A monoidal Hom-algebra is an object
(A,\alpha)\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
together with a
k
-linear
map
m_{A}:A\otimes A\rightarrow A
and an element
1_{A}\in A
such that

\displaystyle\alpha(ab)=\alpha(a)\alpha(b);~\alpha(1_{A})=1_{A},

\displaystyle\alpha(a)(bc)=(ab)\alpha(c);~a1_{A}=1_{A}a=\alpha(a),

for all
a,b,c\in A
. Here we use the notation
m_{A}(a\otimes b)=ab
.

Definition 2.2.
A monoidal Hom-coalgebra is
an object
(C,\gamma)\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})

together with
k
-linear maps
\Delta:C\rightarrow C\otimes C,~~~\Delta(c)=c_{(1)}\otimes c_{(2)}
(summation implicitly understood) and

\gamma:C\rightarrow C
such that

\displaystyle\Delta(\gamma(c))=\gamma(c_{(1)})\otimes\gamma(c_{(2)});~~~\varepsilon(\gamma(c))=\varepsilon(c),

and

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}(c_{(1)})\otimes c_{(2)(1)}\otimes c_{(2)(2)}=c_{(1)(1)}\otimes c_{(1)(2)}\otimes\gamma(c_{(2)}),

\displaystyle\varepsilon(c_{(1)})c_{(2)}=\varepsilon(c_{(2)})c_{(1)}=\gamma^{-1}(c),

for all
c\in C
.

Definition 2.3.
A monoidal Hom-bialgebra

H=(H,\alpha,m,\eta,\Delta,\varepsilon)
is a bialgebra in
the category
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
. This means that
(H,\alpha,m,\eta)
is a monoidal Hom-algebra,
(H,\Delta,\alpha)
is a monoidal Hom-coalgebra
and that
\Delta
and
\varepsilon
are morphisms of monoidal Hom-algebras, that is,

\displaystyle\Delta(ab)=a_{(1)}b_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}b_{(2)};\Delta(1_{H})=1_{H}\otimes 1_{H},

\displaystyle\varepsilon(ab)=\varepsilon(a)\varepsilon(b),~\varepsilon(1_{H})=1_{H}.

Definition 2.4.
A monoidal Hom-Hopf algebra is
a monoidal Hom-bialgebra
(H,\alpha)
together with a linear map

S:H\rightarrow H
in
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
such
that

S\ast I=I\ast S=\eta\varepsilon,~S\alpha=\alpha S.

Definition 2.5.
Let
(A,\alpha)
be a monoidal
Hom-algebra. A right
(A,\alpha)
-Hom-module is an object

(M,\mu)\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
consists of a

k
-module and a linear map
\mu:M\rightarrow M
together with a
morphism
\psi:M\otimes A\rightarrow M,\psi(m\cdot a)=m\cdot a
in

\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
such that

\displaystyle(m\cdot a)\cdot\alpha(b)=\mu(m)\cdot(ab);~~~~m\cdot 1_{A}=\mu(m),

for all
a\in A
and
m\in M
. The fact that
\psi\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
means

\displaystyle\mu(m\cdot a)=\mu(m)\cdot\alpha(a).

A morphism
f:(M,\mu)\rightarrow(N,\nu)
in
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
is called right
A
-linear if it preserves the
A
-action, that is,
f(m\cdot a)=f(m)\cdot a
.
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})_{A}
will denote the category of right
(A,\alpha)
-Hom-modules and
A
-linear morphisms.

Definition 2.6.
Let
(C,\gamma)
be a monoidal
Hom-coalgebra. A right
(C,\gamma)
-Hom-comodule is an object

(M,\mu)\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
together with a

k
-linear map
\rho_{M}:M\rightarrow M\otimes C

(
\rho_{M}(m)=m_{[0]}\otimes m_{[1]}
) in

\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
such that

\displaystyle m_{[0][0]}\otimes(m_{[0][1]}\otimes\gamma^{-1}(m_{[1]}))=\mu^{-1}(m_{[0]})\otimes\Delta_{C}(m_{[1]});~m_{[0]}\varepsilon(m_{[1]})=\mu^{-1}(m),

for all
m\in M
. The fact that
\rho_{M}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})
means

\displaystyle\rho_{M}(\mu(m))=\mu(m_{[0]})\otimes\gamma(m_{[1]}).

Morphisms of right
(C,\gamma)
-Hom-comodule are defined in the obvious way. The
category of right
(C,\gamma)
-Hom-comodules will be denoted by
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{C}
.

Definition 2.7.
Let
(H,\alpha)
be a monoidal Hom-Hopf algebra. A monoidal Hom-algebra
(A,\beta)
is called a right

(H,\alpha)
-Hom-comodule algebra if
(A,\beta)
is a right
(H,\alpha)
Hom-comodule with a coaction
\rho_{A}:A\rightarrow A\otimes H,\rho_{A}(a)=a_{[0]}\otimes a_{[1]}

such that the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle\rho_{A}(ab)=a_{[0]}b_{[0]}\otimes a_{[1]}b_{[1]},\rho_{A}(1_{A})=1_{A}\otimes 1_{H},

for all
a,b\in A
.

3
Relative Hom-Hopf Modules: functors and structures

Let
(H,\alpha)
be a monoidal Hom-Hopf algebra and
(A,\beta)
a right

(H,\alpha)
-Hom-comodule algebra.
Recall from
[
12
]
,
a relative Hom-Hopf module
(M,\mu)

is a right
(A,\beta)
-Hom-module which is also a right

(H,\alpha)
-Hom-comodule with the coaction structure
\rho_{M}:M\rightarrow M\otimes H
defined by
\rho_{M}(m)=m_{[0]}\otimes m_{[1]}
such that the following compatible condition holds: for all

m\in M
and
a\in A
,

\displaystyle\rho_{M}(ma)=m_{[0]}\cdot a_{[0]}\otimes m_{[1]}a_{[1]}.

A morphism between two right relative Hom-Hopf modules is a
k
-linear map
which is a morphism in the categories
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})_{A}
and
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}
at the same time.
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}
will denote the category of right relative Hom-Hopf modules and morphisms
between them.

By
[
12
]
, the forgetful functor

F:\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})_{A}
has a right
adjoint
G:\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})_{A}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}
.

G
defined by

G(M)=M\otimes H

with structure maps

\displaystyle(m\otimes h)\cdot a=m\cdot a_{[0]}\otimes ha_{[1]},

\displaystyle\rho_{G(M)}(m\otimes h)=(\mu^{-1}(m)\otimes h_{(1)})\otimes\alpha(h_{(2)}),

for all
a\in A,m\in M
and
h\in H
.

Let us describe the
unit
\eta
and the counit
\delta
of the adjunction. The unit is described by the coaction:
for
M\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}
, we define

\eta_{M}:M\rightarrow M\otimes H
as follows:

\eta_{M}(m)=m_{[0]}\otimes m_{[1]},~m\in M.

We can check that
\eta_{M}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}
.
For any
N\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})_{A}
, define

\delta_{N}:N\otimes H\rightarrow N,~\delta_{N}(n\otimes h)=\varepsilon(h)n,~n\in N,h\in H.

It is easy to check that
\eta_{M}\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}
.
One may verify that
\eta
and
\delta
defined above are natural transformations and
satisfy

G(\delta_{N})\circ\eta_{G(N)}=I_{G(N)},~\delta_{F(M)}\circ F(\eta_{M})=I_{F(M)},

for all
M\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}
and
N\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})_{A}
.

Since
(A,\beta)
is a right
(A,\beta)
-Hom-module,
G(A)
is a relative Hom-Hopf module via

\displaystyle(a\otimes h)\cdot b=ab_{[0]}\otimes hb_{[1]};

(3.1)

\displaystyle\rho_{A\otimes H}(a\otimes h)=(\beta^{-1}(a)\otimes h_{(1)})\otimes\alpha(h_{(2)}),

(3.2)

for all
a,b\in A
and
h\in H
. Notice that

GFG(A)=A\otimes H\otimes H

is an object in
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}
, and we also have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(a\otimes h\otimes g)\cdot b=ab_{[0][0]}\otimes hb_{[0][1]}\otimes gb_{[1]};\\
\rho_{A\otimes H\otimes H}(a\otimes h\otimes g)=(\beta^{-1}(a)\otimes\alpha^{-1}(h)\otimes g_{(1)})\otimes\alpha(g_{(2)}),\end{array}\right.

for any
a,b\in A
and
h,g\in H
.

Similar to
[
12
]
, one can check that
\widetilde{F}:\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}

which forgets the
(A,\beta)
-action has a left adjoint given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}:\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A},\widetilde{G}(N)=A\otimes N,~N\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}.

The relative Hom-Hopf module structure on
\widetilde{G}(N)
is given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(a\otimes n)\cdot b=a\beta(b)\otimes\nu^{-1}(n),\\
\rho_{A\otimes N}(a\otimes n)=(a_{[0]}\otimes n_{[0]}\otimes)\otimes a_{[1]}n_{[1]},\end{array}\right.

for all
a\in A
and
n\in N
. Since
(H,\alpha)
is a right

(H,\alpha)
-Hom-comodule, then
\widetilde{G}(H)=A\otimes H
can be viewed as a relative Hom-Hopf
module via

\displaystyle(a\otimes h)\cdot b=a\beta(b)\otimes\alpha^{-1}(h),

(3.3)

\displaystyle\rho_{A\otimes H}(a\otimes h)=(a_{[0]}\otimes h_{(1)}\otimes)\otimes a_{[1]}h_{(2)},

(3.4)

for all
a\in A
and
h\in H
.

From Eqs. (3.1) and (3.2) or Eqs. (3.3) and (3.4), we have two types of relative Hom-Hopf modules structures on
A\otimes H
. The following proposition reveals their
relationship.

Proposition 3.1.
With the two types of relative Hom-Hopf modules on
A\otimes H
.
Then

\displaystyle G(A)\cong\widetilde{G}(H)

in
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}
.

Proof.
For any
a\in A
and
h\in H
, we construct two maps
u
and
v
as follows:

\displaystyle u:A\otimes H\rightarrow A\otimes H,u(a\otimes h)=\beta(a_{[0]})\otimes h\alpha^{-1}(a_{[1]}),

\displaystyle v:A\otimes H\rightarrow A\otimes H,u(a\otimes h)=\beta(a_{[0]})\otimes h\alpha^{-1}(S(a_{[1]})).

We claim that
u\circ v=id_{A\otimes H}
. In fact,

\displaystyle u\circ v(a\otimes h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(\beta(a_{[0]})\otimes h\alpha^{-1}(S(a_{[1]})))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{2}(a_{[0][0]})\otimes(h\alpha^{-1}(S(a_{[1]})))\alpha^{-1}(a_{[0][1]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(a_{[0]})\otimes(hS(a_{[1](2)}))\alpha^{-1}(a_{[1](1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(a_{[0]})\otimes\alpha^{-1}(h)(S(a_{[1](2)})a_{[1](1)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta(a_{[0]})\otimes\alpha^{-1}(h)\varepsilon(a_{[1]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\otimes h.

Similarly, one may verify that
v\circ u=id_{A\otimes H}
.
The proof that
u
is a
morphism in
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}
is straightforward.

4
Natural transformations versus integrals

Definition 4.1.
[
12
]
Let
(H,\alpha)
be a monoidal Hom Hopf algebra and
(A,\beta)
a right

(H,\alpha)
-Hom-comodule algebra. The map
\phi:(H,\alpha)\rightarrow(A,\beta)
is called a total integral if the following conditions
are satisfied:

\rho_{A}\phi=(\phi\otimes id_{H})\Delta_{H},~~~\phi\alpha=\beta\phi,~~~~~\phi(1_{H})=1_{A}.

Proposition 4.2.
Let
(A,\beta)
be a right

(H,\alpha)
-Hom-comodule algebra,

(M,\mu),(N,\nu)\in\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}

and
u:(N,\nu)\rightarrow(M,\mu)
a
k
-linear map. Suppose that there exists a
total integral
\phi:(H,\alpha)\rightarrow(A,\beta)
. Then

(1) The map

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}:(N,\nu)\rightarrow(M,\mu),~\widetilde{u}(n)=u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(n_{[0]})_{[1]})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1]})),~n\in N

is right
(H,\alpha)
-colinear.

(2) If
f:(M,\mu)\rightarrow(N,\nu)
is a morphism in
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}
of which is a
k
-split
injection (respectively, a
k
-split surjection), then
f
has an
(H,\alpha)
-colinear retraction
(respectively, a section).

Proof.
For any
n\in N
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{M}\circ\widetilde{u}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[0][0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(n_{[0]})_{[1]})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1]}))_{[0]}

\displaystyle\hskip 142.26378pt\otimes u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[0][1]}\phi(S(u(n_{[0]})_{[1]})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1]}))_{[1]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(n_{[0]})_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[1](2)})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1]}))_{[0]}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\otimes u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[1](1)}\phi(S(u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[1](2)})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1]}))_{[1]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(n_{[0]})_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[1](2)(2)})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1](1)}))

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\otimes u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[1](1)}S(u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[1](2)(1)})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1](2)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(n_{[0]})_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(n_{[0]})_{[1](2)})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1](1)}))

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\otimes u(\nu^{2}(n_{[0]}))_{[1](1)(1)}S(u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[1](1)(2)})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1](2)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(n_{[0]})_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(n_{[0]})_{[1](2)})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1](1)}))

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\otimes[u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[1](1)(1)}S(u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[1](1)(2)})]n_{[1](2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(n_{[0]})_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(\nu^{-1}(n_{[0]}))_{[1]})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1](1)}))\otimes\alpha(n_{[1](2)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(\nu(n_{[0][0]}))_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(n_{[0][0]})_{[1]})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1](1)}))\otimes n_{[1](2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\widetilde{u}\otimes id_{H})\circ\rho_{N}(n).

Hence
\widetilde{u}
is right
(H,\alpha)
-colinear.

Let
u:(N,\nu)\rightarrow(M,\mu)
be
k
-linear, then
\widetilde{u}:(N,\nu)\rightarrow(M,\mu)
is right

(H,\alpha)
-colinear. Assume that
u
is a retraction of
f
, for any
m\in M
, we have

\displaystyle(\widetilde{u}\circ f)(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(\nu(f(m)_{[0]}))_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(f(m)_{[0]})_{[1]})\alpha^{-1}(f(m)_{[1]}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u(\nu(f(m_{[0])}))_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(u(f(m_{[0]}))_{[1]})\alpha^{-1}(m_{[1]}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu(m_{[0][0]})\cdot\phi(S(m_{[0][1]})\alpha^{-1}(m_{[1]}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(m_{[1](1)})m_{[1](2)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{[0]}\varepsilon(m_{[1]})\cdot 1_{A}=m.

So
\widetilde{u}:(N,\nu)\rightarrow(M,\mu)
is a right

(H,\alpha)
-colinear retraction of
f
. On the other hand, if
u

is a section of
f
, for any
n\in N
, we have

\displaystyle(f\circ\widetilde{u})(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[0]})\cdot\phi(S(u(n_{[0]})_{[1]})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1]}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(u(\nu(n_{[0]}))_{[0]})\cdot\phi(S(u(n_{[0]})_{[1]})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1]}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu(n_{[0][0]})\cdot\phi(S(n_{[0][1]})\alpha^{-1}(n_{[1]}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{[0]}\cdot\phi(S(n_{[1](1)})n_{[1](2)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{[0]}\varepsilon(n_{[1]})\cdot 1_{A}=n.

Thus
\widetilde{u}:(N,\nu)\rightarrow(M,\mu)
is a right
H
-colinear section of
f
.

Theorem 4.3.
Let
(A,\beta)
be a right

(H,\alpha)
-Hom-comodule algebra. Then the following statements
are equivalent:

(1) There exists a total integral
\phi:(H,\alpha)\rightarrow(A,\beta)
;

(2) There exists a natural transformation
\rho:\widetilde{F}\circ 1_{\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}_{A}}\rightarrow\widetilde{F}\circ G\circ F
splits;

(3)
\rho_{A}:A\rightarrow A\otimes H
splits in
\widetilde{\mathscr{H}}(\mathscr{M}_{k})^{H}
.

Consequently, if one
of the equivalent conditions holds, then any relative Hopf module is injective
as a right
(H,\alpha)
-comodule.

Proof.

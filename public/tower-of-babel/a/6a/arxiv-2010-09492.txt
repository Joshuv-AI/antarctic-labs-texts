Examples on the sharpness of an inequality about multiplicities over hyperfields Thanks: This work is an undergraduate research under the instruction of Professor Matthew Baker in Georgia Institute of Technology. The author also thanks professor Oliver Lorscheid for helpful discussions and feedback in October, 2019. The main part of this work was finished in December, 2019 and the author made some modifies recently.
Ziqi Liu

Abstract
. In this paper, the author introduces hyperfields and give some facts about the roots and multiplicities of polynomials over hyperfield based on [ BB19 ] and [ BL18 ] . Then he tests the sharpness of an inequality in Baker’s former work [ BB19 ] about the behavior of multiplicities under homomorphisms between hyperfields and show that the inequality is not sharp under the natural homomorphisms ℂ → ℙ \mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P} and ℂ → 𝕍 \mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{V} while it is sharp under the natural homomorphisms ℂ → 𝕂 \mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{K} , ℝ → 𝕊 \mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{S} and ℝ → 𝕋 \mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{T} according to the previous work [ BB19 ] of Baker and Lorscheid.



1.
Some Backgrounds

1.1.
The Definition of Hyperfields

Definition 1.1.1
.

A
hyperoperation
on a set
S
is a map
\boxplus:S\times S\rightarrow 2^{S}\backslash\{\varnothing\}
. For given hyperoperation
\boxplus
on
S
and non-empty subsets
A,B
of
S
, define

A\boxplus B:=\bigcup_{a\in A,b\in B}(a\boxplus b)

A hyperoperation
\boxplus
is
commutative
if
a\boxplus b=b\boxplus a
for all
a,b\in S
. If not especially mentioned, hyperoperations in this paper will always be commutative.

A hyperoperation
\boxplus
is
associative
if
a\boxplus(b\boxplus c)=(a\boxplus b)\boxplus c
for all
a,b,c\in S
.

Definition 1.1.2
.

Given an associative hyperoperation
\boxplus
, the
hypersum
of
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m}
for
m\geq 2
is recursively defined as

x_{1}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus x_{n}:=\bigcup_{x^{\prime}\in x_{2}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus x_{n}}x_{1}\boxplus x^{\prime}

Definition 1.1.3
.

A
hypergroup
is a tuple
(G,\boxplus,0)
, where
\boxplus
is an associative hyperoperation on
G
such that:

(1)
0\boxplus x=\{x\}
for all
x\in G
;

(2) For every
x\in G
there is a unique element
-x
of
G
such that
0\in x\boxplus-x
;

(3)
x\in y\boxplus z
if and only if
z\in x\boxplus(-y)
.

Here
-x
is often called as the
hyperinverse
of
x
and (3) as the reversibility axiom.

Definition 1.1.4
.

A (Krasner)
hyperring
is a tuple
(R,\odot,\boxplus,1,0)
such that:

(1)
(R,\odot,1)
is a commutative monoid;

(2)
(R,\boxplus,0)
is a commutative hypergroup;

(3)
0\odot x=x\odot 0=0
for all
x\in R
;

(4)
a\odot(x\boxplus y)=(a\odot x)\boxplus(a\odot y)
for all
a,x,y\in R
;

(5)
(x\boxplus y)\odot a=(x\odot a)\boxplus(y\odot a)
for all
a,x,y\in R
.

In the following part, we often use the underlying set
R
to refer to a hyperring and may omit
\odot
if there is no likehood of confusion.

Definition 1.1.5
.

A hyperring
F
is called a
hyperfield
if
0\neq 1
and every non-zero element of
F
has a multiplicative inverse.

Example 1.1.6
.

If
(\mathbb{F},\cdot,+)
is a field, then
\mathbb{F}
can be trivially associated with a hyperfield
(\mathbb{F},\odot,\boxplus)
where
x\odot y=x\cdot y
and
x\boxplus y=\{x+y\}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{F}
.

In the following context, when we mention a field
\mathbb{F}
, we may actually refer to the hyperfield associated with
\mathbb{F}
.

Example 1.1.7
.

Consider
\mathbb{K}=(\{0,1\},\odot,\boxplus,1,0)
with the usual multiplication rule and a hyperaddition
\boxplus
defined by

0\boxplus 0=\{0\},\qquad 1\boxplus 0=0\boxplus 1=\{1\},\qquad 1\boxplus 1=\{0,1\}

then
\mathbb{K}
is a hyperfield, called the
Krasner hyperfield
.

Example 1.1.8
.

Consider
\mathbb{S}=(\{0,1,-1\},\odot,\boxplus,1,0)
with the usual multiplication rule and a hyperaddition
\boxplus
generated by

x\boxplus x=\{x\},\quad x\boxplus 0=\{x\},\quad 1\boxplus-1=\{-1,0,1\}

then
\mathbb{S}
is a hyperfield, called the
hyperfield of signs
.

Example 1.1.9
.

Let
\mathbb{T}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
as sets and define hyperoperation
\boxplus
as

x\boxplus y=\left\{\begin{aligned} \{\max\{x,y\}\}&,&x\neq y\\
\{z\in\mathbb{T}:z\leq x\}&,&x=y\end{aligned}\right.

and
\odot
as
x\odot y=x+y
. Then
\mathbb{T}
is a hyperfield, called the
tropical hyperfield
.

Remark 1.1.10
.

More generally, let
\Gamma
be a totally ordered abelian group (written multiplicatively) one can define a canonical hyperfield structure on set
\Gamma\cup\{0\}
where

•

the multiplication
\odot
is multiplication in
\Gamma
with
0\odot x=0
for all
x\in\Gamma\cup\{0\}

•

the hyperaddition
\boxplus
is defined as
x\boxplus x:=\{y:y\leq x\}
and
x\boxplus y:=\max\{x,y\}
for
x\neq y
.

with
0\leq x
for all
x\in\Gamma
. One such hyperfield is a
valuative hyperfield
.

Example 1.1.11
.

Let
\mathbb{P}=S^{1}\cup\{0\}
, where
S^{1}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
is the complex unit circle. Then one can define a hyperfield structure on
\mathbb{P}
where the multiplication is the usual one and the hyperaddition is defined as

x\boxplus y=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cll}\{x\},&&y=0\\
\{0,x,-x\},&&y=-x\\
\{\frac{\alpha x+\beta y}{||\alpha x+\beta y||}:\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\},&&\textup{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

This hyperfield structure on
S^{1}\cup\{0\}
is called the
phase hyperfield
.

Example 1.1.12
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be the set
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}=\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{0\}
of nonnegative real numbers with the usual multiplication and the hyperaddition is defined as

x\boxplus y=\{z\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}:|x-y|\leq z\leq x+y\}

Then
\mathbb{V}
is a hyperfield named the
Viro hyperfield
(or the triangle hyperfield).

Definition 1.1.13
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2}
be two hyperfields. A map
f:\mathbb{F}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
is called a
hyperfield homomorphism
if for all
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{1}

(1)
f(0)=0,f(1)=1
;

(2)
f(xy)=f(x)f(y)
and
f(x\boxplus y)\subseteq f(x)\boxplus f(y)
.

Example 1.1.14
.

Consider the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
and the hyperfield of signs
\mathbb{S}
, a natural hyperfield homomorphism can be defined as

f:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{S},\quad x\longmapsto\textup{sign}(x)

where
\textup{sign}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\{1,-1,0\}
is the function which maps a real number to its sign.

Example 1.1.15
.

Consider the field of complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
and the Viro hyperfield
\mathbb{V}
, one can see a natural hyperfield homomorphism

f:\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow\mathbb{V},\quad re^{i\theta}\longmapsto r

where
r\geq 0
and
\theta\in[0,2\pi]
.

Proposition 1.1.16
.

Given two hyperfields
\mathbb{F}_{1}
and
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and a homomorphism
f:\mathbb{F}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then for any
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
in
\mathbb{F}_{1}
, we have

f(x_{1}\boxplus x_{2}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus x_{n})\subseteq f(x_{1})\boxplus f(x_{2})\boxplus\cdots\boxplus f(x_{n})

holds, where
n\geq 2
is an arbitrary integer.

Proof.

We prove it by induction. The
n=2
case is obviously valid by definition.

Assume that our claim is valid for
n-1
, then for case
n
, we have

\displaystyle f(x_{1}\boxplus x_{2}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus x_{n})

\displaystyle=f(\bigcup_{x\in x_{2}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus x_{n}}x_{1}\boxplus x)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{x\in x_{2}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus x_{n}}f(x_{1}\boxplus x)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in x_{2}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus x_{n}}f(x_{1})\boxplus f(x)

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{y\in f(x_{2}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus x_{n})}f(x_{1})\boxplus y

\displaystyle\subseteq\bigcup_{y\in f(x_{2})\boxplus\cdots\boxplus f(x_{n})}f(x_{1})\boxplus y

\displaystyle=f(x_{1})\boxplus f(x_{2})\boxplus\cdots\boxplus f(x_{n})

where we use our inductive assumption for the last
\subseteq
.

Hence our claim is true for
n
and then we finish the induction and the whole proof.
∎

1.2.
The Definition of Multiplicities over Hyperfields

Definition 1.2.1
.

Given a hyperfield
\mathbb{F}
, a
polynomial
over
\mathbb{F}
(or with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
) is a map
p:\mathbb{F}\rightarrow 2^{\mathbb{F}}
that

a\longmapsto c_{n}a^{n}\boxplus c_{n-1}a^{n-1}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus c_{1}a\boxplus c_{0}

where
\{c_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\subset\mathbb{F}
and
c_{n}\neq 0
.

For such
p
, denote it by
p(T)=c_{n}T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
.

Example 1.2.2
.

The polynomial
p(T)=1T^{3}-2
over the tropical hyperfield
\mathbb{T}
is

p(a)=1a^{3}\boxplus(-2)=\left\{\begin{aligned} -2\quad&,\,\,a<-1\\
[-\infty,-2]&,\,\,a=-1\\
1a^{3}\quad&,\,\,a>-1\end{aligned}\right.

where
<
is the natural order in the real axis and
1a^{3}
means
1\odot a\odot a\odot a
in
\mathbb{T}
.

Proposition 1.2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a hyperfield, the set of all polynomials over
\mathbb{F}
can be naturally endowed with two hyperoperations

(1)
p\boxdot q=e_{mn}T^{mn}+\cdots+e_{1}T_{1}+e_{0}
where
e_{i}=\mathop{\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{\mathbb{F}}}\limits_{k+l=i}c_{k}d_{l}
;

(2)
p\boxplus q=e_{n}T^{n}+\cdots+e_{1}T_{1}+e_{0}
where
e_{i}=c_{i}\boxplus_{\mathbb{F}}d_{i}
for
i\leq m
and
e_{i}=c_{i}
for
i>m
.

for
p(T)=c_{n}T^{n}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
and
q(T)=d_{m}T^{m}+\cdots+d_{1}T+d_{0}
with
n\geq m
.

Therefore, one can get a new hyperstructure over that set, denoted by
\textup{Poly}(\mathbb{F})
.

Remark 1.2.4
.

In
[
BL18
]
, this hyperstructure is called a polynomial hyperring (but it is clearly not a hyperring). In some materials like
[
AEM19
]
, this hyperstructure is identified as a new concept: superring.

Definition 1.2.5
.

Let
p(T)=c_{n}T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial over a hyperfield
\mathbb{F}
, an element
a\in\mathbb{F}
is called a
root
of
p
if and only if either the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(1)
0\in p(a)=c_{n}a^{n}\boxplus c_{n-1}a^{n-1}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus c_{1}a\boxplus c_{0}
;

(2) there exist
q
in Poly
(\mathbb{F})
such that
p\in(T-a)\boxdot q
(the symbol
\boxdot
is sometimes omitted).

Definition 1.2.6
.

Let
\displaystyle p(T)=c_{n}T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial over a hyperfield
\mathbb{F}
, if
a
is not a root of
p
, set mult
{}_{a}(p)=0
. If
a
is a root of
p
, define

mult
{}_{a}(p)
=
1+\max\{\textup{mult}_{a}(q):p\in(T-a)q\}

as the
multiplicity
of the root
a
of
p
. Moreover, for a nonempty set
S
, define

mult
{}_{S}(p)
=
1+\max\{\textup{mult}_{S}(q):p\in(T-a)q
for some
a\in S\}

It is clearly that mult
{}_{S}(p)\leq\textup{deg}(p)
for any
S\subset\mathbb{F}
.

Example 1.2.7
.

Given a polynomial
p(T)=T^{2}+3T+1
over the Viro hyperfield
\mathbb{V}
.

(1) Since
p(3)=(3^{2})\boxplus(3^{2})\boxplus 1=9\boxplus 9\boxplus 1=[0,19]

one has
0\in p(3)
and hence mult
{}_{3}(p)\geq 1
. From

(T+3)\boxdot(T+3)=\{T^{2}+aT+9:a\in[0,6]\}

one can see
p(T)\notin(T+3)(T+3)
and then mult
{}_{3}(p)=1
.

(2) Given
S=[1,+\infty)
, from (1) one can see mult
{}_{S}(p)\geq 1
. Suppose mult
{}_{S}(p)=2
, then there exist an
s\in S
such that

p(T)\in(T+s)\boxdot(T+s)=\{T^{2}+aT+s^{2}:a\in[0,2s]\}

In this case,
s^{2}=1
and thus
s=1
, which is impossible. Therefore, mult
{}_{S}(p)=1
.

Definition 1.2.8
.

Given hyperfields
\mathbb{F}_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2}
and a polynomial
\overline{p}
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
if there exists a hyperfield homomorphism
f:\mathbb{F}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
, then a
corresponding polynomial
of
\overline{p}
with respect to
f
is a polynomial
p
in set

\{p(T)=c_{n}T+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}\,|\,f(c_{n})T+\cdots+f(c_{1})T+f(c_{0})=\overline{p}(T)\}

which is a subset of Poly
(\mathbb{F}_{1})
.

Also, for
p
in Poly
(\mathbb{F}_{1})
, one also denotes
f(c_{n})T+\cdots+f(c_{1})T+f(c_{0})
by
\overline{p}
.

Example 1.2.9
.

It is natural for us to consider if multiplicity of a polynomial can be preserved by hyperfield homomorphisms. Following are two simple examples.

(1) Consider
p(T)=T^{2}-3T+2
over the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
and the hyperfield homomorphism
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}
described in Example
1.1.14
, one is able to see that the corresponding polynomial
\overline{p}(T)=T^{2}-T+1
with respect to
f
satisfies

mult
{}_{1}(\overline{p})=2=\sum_{a>0}\textup{mult}_{a}(p)=\sum_{a\in f^{-1}(1)}\textup{mult}_{a}(p)
.

(2) Consider
q(T)=T^{3}+2T-3
over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
and the hyperfield homomorphism
g:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
described in Example
1.1.15
, it is not hard to check that the corresponding polynomial
\overline{q}(T)=T^{3}+4T+5
with respect to
g
satisfies

mult
{}_{2}(\overline{q})=2>0=\sum_{|a|=2}\textup{mult}_{a}(q)=\sum_{a\in g^{-1}(2)}\textup{mult}_{a}(q)
.

For (2), we have mult
{}_{2}(\overline{q})\leq 2
and

T^{3}+4T+5\in(T+2)(T^{2}+2T+2.5),\quad T^{2}+2T+2.5\in(T+2)(T+1.25)

where one should notice that
-x=x
holds for each
x\in\mathbb{V}
.

Proposition 1.2.10
.

For a monic polynomial
p(T)=T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{0}
over a hyperfield
\mathbb{F}
with
x\boxplus x=\{x\}
, the following two statements are equivalent:

(1)
a
is a root of
p
with mult
{}_{a}(p)=n
;

(2)
c_{n-k}=(-a)^{k}
for
k=1,\dots,n
.

Proof.

We prove our claim by induction on
n
. First, it is clear that our claim is true for
n=1
and we know it is true for
n=2
since

(T-a)\boxdot(T-a)=\{T^{2}+(-a)\boxplus(-a)T+(-a)^{2}\}=\{T^{2}+(-a)T+(-a)^{2}\}

Suppose our claim is valid for
n
, consider the case
n+1
.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2). If
a
is a root of
p(T)=T^{n+1}+c_{n}T^{n}+\cdots+c_{0}
with mult
{}_{a}(p)=n+1
, then there exist a polynomial
q(T)
such that mult
{}_{a}(q)=n
and
p\in(T-a)q
. By our inductive assumption, one knows that

q(T)=T^{n}-aT^{n-1}+\cdots+(-a)^{n-1}T+(-a)^{n}

Therefore, from
p\in(T-a)q
one has

c_{0}=(-a)^{n+1}
and
c_{n+1-k}\in(-a)(-a)^{k-1}\boxplus(-a)^{k}={(-a)^{k}}

this part is done.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1). If
c_{n+1-k}=(-a)^{k}
for
k=1,\dots,n+1
, one can get
p\in(T-a)q
where

q(T)=T^{n}+(-a)T^{n-1}+\cdots+(-a)^{n-1}T+(-a)^{n}

By our inductive assumption, we have mult
{}_{a}(q)=n
and it follows immediately that mult
{}_{a}(p)=n+1
.

In conclusion, our claim is still true for
n+1
and then we are done here.
∎

2.
The Inequality about Multiplicities over Hyperfields

2.1.
Introduction to the Inequality

Proposition 2.1.1
.

Given two hyperfields
\mathbb{F}_{1},\mathbb{F}_{2}
and a homomorphism
f:\mathbb{F}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Let

p(T)=T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}

be a polynomial with coefficients in a hyperfield
\mathbb{F}_{1}
, if
\alpha
is a root of
p
, then
f(\alpha)
is a root of

\overline{p}(T)=T^{n}+f(c_{n-1})T^{n-1}+\cdots+f(c_{1})T+f(c_{0})

with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

This proposition is a corollary of Proposition
1.1.16
and one can then obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1.2
.

Given a field
\mathbb{L}
, a hyperfield
\mathbb{F}
and a homomorphism
f:\mathbb{L}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
, we have

\textup{mult}_{b}(\overline{p})\geq\sum_{a\in f^{-1}(b)}\textup{mult}_{a}(p),\quad\textup{for all}\,\,b\in\mathbb{F}

for every
\overline{p}
and its corresponding polynomial
p
with respect to
f
.

Moreover, if
\sum_{b\in\mathbb{F}}\textup{mult}_{b}(\overline{p})\leq\textup{deg}(\overline{p})
and
p
splits into a product of linear factors over
\mathbb{L}
, then the equality holds in this theorem.

This theorem is Proposition B in
[
BL18
]
. In fact, we can write a more general theorem with homomorphisms from hyperfield
\mathbb{L}
to
\mathbb{F}
where polynomials in Poly
(\mathbb{L})
have unique decomposition. In the following part, We will focus on whether the inequality in Theorem
2.1.2
is sharp under different hyperfield homomorphisms, that is, whether we can always find a
p
for each
\overline{p}
such that the inequality holds.

Proposition 2.1.3
.

Let
p(T)=T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial with coefficients in a hyperfield
\mathbb{F}
, if mult
{}_{a}(p)=n
, then we have
c_{n}=(-a)^{n}
.

Proof.

We will prove it by induction, since our definition of multiplicity is inductive.

For
n=1
, if
0\in a\boxplus c_{0}
, then
c_{0}\in 0\boxplus(-a)=\{-a\}
which implies that
c_{0}=-a
.

Assume that our claim is true for
n-1
, we want to prove that it is also true for
n
.

Let
a
be a root of polynomial
p(T)
with mult
{}_{a}(p)=n
, then from

mult
{}_{a}(p)
=
1+\max\{\textup{mult}_{a}(q):p\in(T-a)q\}

we know that
\max\{\textup{mult}_{a}(q):p\in(T-a)q\}=n-1
. So there are some

q(T)=T^{n-1}+d_{n-2}T^{n-2}+\cdots+d_{1}T+d_{0}

such that
a
is a root of
q(T)
with mult
{}_{a}(p)=n-1
. By our assumption,
d_{0}=(-a)^{n}
, hence from
p\in(T-a)q
we get
c_{0}=-ad_{0}=(-a)^{n}
, our induction is done.
∎

Proposition 2.1.4
.

Let
p(T)=c_{n}T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}
, then
0
is a root of
p
if and only if
c_{0}=0
.

Proof.

If
0
is a root of
p
, then there exist
\{d_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}=\{c_{i+1}\}_{i=0}^{n-1}
such that

c_{0}=0,\,\,c_{i}\in 0\boxplus d_{i-1}
for
i=1,\dots,n-1
and
c_{n}=d_{n-1}

If
c_{0}=0
, then it is clear that
0\in p(0)=0\boxplus 0\boxplus\cdots\boxplus 0=\{0\}
.
∎

Proposition 2.1.5
.

Let
p(T)=T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
, if
c_{0}=0
then the set of nonzero roots of
p
is the same as the set of nonzero roots of
q
where
q(T)=T^{n-1}+c_{n-1}T^{n-2}+\cdots+c_{1}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient for us to prove that

0\in a^{n}\boxplus c_{n-1}a^{n-1}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus c_{1}a\Longleftrightarrow 0\in a^{n-1}\boxplus c_{n-1}a^{n-2}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus c_{1}

for none zero
a\in\mathbb{F}
, which is obvious.

Furthermore, the multiplicities of non-zero roots don’t change.
∎

Proposition 2.1.6
.

Let
p(T)=c_{n}T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{k}T^{k}
be a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}
and for some
0<k<n
, if
c_{k}\neq 0
, then
p
has 0 as its root with multiplicity of
k
.

Proof.

It is clear that
0
is a root of
p(T)
and then we will prove mult
{}_{0}(p)=k
by induction.

If
k=1
, consider the
p_{1}(T)=d_{n-1}T^{n-1}+d_{n-2}T^{n-2}+\cdots+d_{0}
in

mult
{}_{0}(p)
=
1+\max{\{\textup{mult}_{0}(p_{1}):p\in Tp_{1}\}}

We have
c_{n}=d_{n-1}
and

c_{i}\in 0\boxplus d_{i-1}=\{d_{i-1}\}
for
i=1,\dots,n-1

which implies that
p_{1}(T)\in\{c_{n}T^{n-1}+c_{n-1}T^{n-2}+\cdots+c_{1}\}
. According to Proposition
2.1.4
,
0
is not a root of
p_{1}
and then mult
{}_{0}(p)
=1, so our claim is true for
k=1
.

Suppose our claim is true for
k-1
, we consider the case with
k
. From our assumption,

mult
{}_{0}(p)
=
k+\max\{\textup{mult}_{0}(p_{k}):p_{k-1}\in Tp_{k}\}
.

where
p_{k-1}(T)=c_{n}T^{n-k+1}+\cdots+c_{k}T
. Let
p_{k}(T)=d_{n-k}T^{n-k}+\cdots+d_{0}
, we have

c_{n}=d_{n-k}
and
c_{i}\in 0\boxplus d_{i-k}=\{d_{i-k}\}
for
i=k,\dots,n-1

which implies that
p_{k}(T)\in\{c_{n}T^{n-k}+c_{n-1}T^{n-k-1}+\cdots+c_{k}\}
. According to Proposition
2.1.5
,
0
is not a root of
p_{k}
and then mult
{}_{0}(p)=k
, so we are done.
∎

Remark 2.1.7
.

Propositions
2.1.4
,
2.1.5
and
2.1.6
show that polynomials over hyperfields share some similar nature with respect to the root
0
. Moreover, Proposition
2.1.4
shows that the proposition can be an equivalent statement.

Notice that for given hyperfield homomorphism
f:\mathbb{F}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
f(a)=0
for some
a\neq 0
implies that
f(ax)\subseteq\{0\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{F}_{1}
. Set
x=a^{-1}y
, then
f(y)\subseteq\{0\}
for all
y\in\mathbb{F}_{1}
, which implies that
f
is trivial. Therefore, a nontrivial hyperfield homomorphism preserves
0
as well as the behaviours of root 0 of polynomials.

Corollary 2.1.8
.

For a nontrivial hyperfield homomorphism
f:\mathbb{F}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{2}
, if mult
{}_{0}(p)=k
for polynomial
p(T)=T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T_{1}+c_{0}
over
\mathbb{F}_{1}
, then we have mult
{}_{0}(\overline{p})=k
for

\overline{p}(T)=T^{n}+f(c_{n-1})+\cdots+f(c_{1})T+f(c_{0})

in
Poly
(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.

Remark 2.1.9
.

This corollary shows that when studying the behaviors of multiplicities of polynomials under nontrivial homomorphisms, one only needs to consider the nonzero roots.

2.2.
The Sharpness under Natural Hyperfield Homomorphism
\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}

Proposition 2.2.1
.

For
x,y\in\mathbb{V}
,
0\in x\boxplus y
if and only if
x=y
.

Proof.

If
0\in x\boxplus y=\{z:|x-y|\leq z\leq x+y\}
, then
|x-y|=0
and thus
x=y
.

If
x=y
, then
0\in x\boxplus y=x\boxplus x=\{z:0\leq z\leq 2x\}
.
∎

Proposition 2.2.2
.

Consider the polynomial
p(T)=T^{2}+c_{1}T+c_{0}
which coefficients in
\mathbb{V}
, then
p
has a root
a
with mult
{}_{a}(p)=2
if and only if
c_{0}=a^{2}
and
c_{1}^{2}\leq 4c_{0}
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.1.4
, if
a
is a root of
p
with mult
{}_{a}(p)=2
, we have
a=\sqrt{c_{0}}
and

0\in p(\sqrt{c_{0}})=c_{0}\boxplus c_{1}\sqrt{c_{0}}\boxplus c_{0}\Longrightarrow c_{1}\sqrt{c_{0}}\in c_{0}\boxplus c_{0}

which gives that
c_{1}^{2}\leq 4c_{0}
.

If
c_{0}=a^{2}
and
c_{1}^{2}\leq 4c_{0}
, then we have
p\in(T+a)q
where
q(T)=T+a
since

c_{1}\in a\boxplus a=\sqrt{c_{0}}\boxplus\sqrt{c_{0}}=[0,2\sqrt{c_{0}}]

Therefore, we are done.
∎

Proposition 2.2.3
.

The inequality in Theorem
2.1.2
is sharp under natural hyperfield homomorphism
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{V}
for polynomials of degree 2.

Proof.

According to Proposition
2.1.9
, here we discuss the different cases with nonzero
a
for given polynomial
\overline{p}=T^{2}+c_{1}T+c_{0}
in Poly
(\mathbb{V})
.

For
a\in\mathbb{V}
with mult
{}_{a}(\overline{p})=0
, it is clear that mult
{}_{f^{-1}(a)}(p)=0
for all its corresponding polynomials
p
with respect to
f
according to Theorem
2.1.2
. Therefore, the inequality is sharp in this case.

For
a\in\mathbb{V}
with mult
{}_{a}(\overline{p})=1
, one know that
c_{0}=ab
and
c_{1}\in a\boxplus b
for
b\in\mathbb{V}
and
b\neq a
, which implies that
|a-b|\leq c_{1}\leq a+b
. So there exists a
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
such that
c_{1}=|a+be^{i\theta}|
and then one has a corresponding polynomial

p(T)=T^{2}-(a+be^{i\theta})T+abe^{i\theta}\in\mathbb{C}[T]

of
\overline{p}
with respect to
f
to make the equality hold.

For
a\in\mathbb{V}
with mult
{}_{a}(\overline{p})=2
, we know that
c_{0}=a^{2}
and
c_{1}^{2}\leq 4c_{0}
according to Proposition
2.2.2
, then by calculation it is clear that
p(T)=T^{2}+c_{1}T+c_{0}\in\mathbb{C}[T]
is what we want.
∎

Example 2.2.4
.

For any corresponding polynomial
p
of
\overline{p}(T)=T^{3}+1.6T^{2}+0.512
with respect to the natural hyperfield homomorphism

\displaystyle\textup{mult}_{0.8}(\overline{p})=3>2\geq\sum_{|b|=0.8}\textup{mult}_{b}(p)

is not sharp in this case.

Note that

is true since

for

\overline{q}(T)=T^{2}+0.8T+0.64

and mult

by Proposition
2.2.2
.

However, one also has
\sum_{|b|=0.8}\textup{mult}_{b}(p)\leq 2
because for

, one can see

and then by Rouche’s theorem we know that

has exactly 2 roots inside the circle

Remark 2.2.5
.

Though the inequality in Theorem

is not always sharp, one can assert that it is sharp in some cases based on the nature of the hyperfield
\mathbb{V}
itself with the following propositions inspired by

Proposition 2.2.6
.

Let
p(T)=T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{V}
, if the inequality

c_{n-k}>1+c_{n-1}+\cdots+c_{n-k+1}+c_{n-k-1}+\cdots+c_{0}

.

Proof.

According to Proposition

, one can suppose

Notice that

∎

Proposition 2.2.7
.

Let
p(T)=T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{V}
, if the inequality

c_{n-1}>1+c_{n-2}+\cdots\cdots+c_{1}+c_{0}

has no roots in

Proof.

Suppose
p
has a root

with mult
{}_{a}(p)=n
, we want to find contradictions.

First, according to Proposition
2.1.4
, we have
a\neq 0
otherwise
c_{i}=0
for
i=1,2,\dots,n-1
which leads to a contradiction. Also, from Proposition
2.2.6
, this root
a
can’t be
1
.

When
a>1
, notice that

c_{n-1}a^{n-1}>(c_{n-2}+\cdots\cdots+c_{1}+c_{0})a^{n-1}\geq c_{n-2}a^{n-2}+\cdots+c_{1}a+c_{0}

and
c_{0}=a^{n}
(from Proposition
2.1.3
), one can get

S=\{x:c_{n-1}a^{n-1}-(c_{n-2}a^{n-2}+\cdots+a^{n})\leq x\leq c_{n-1}a^{n-1}+c_{n-2}a^{n-2}+\cdots+a^{n}\}

where
S
denote
c_{n-1}a^{n-1}\boxplus c_{n-2}a^{n-2}\boxplus\cdots\boxplus c_{1}a\boxplus a^{n}
. Then from

\displaystyle c_{n-1}a^{n-1}-(a^{n}+c_{n-2}a^{n-2}+\cdots+a^{n})

\displaystyle\!>

\displaystyle a^{n-1}(1+\sum^{n-2}_{i=0}c_{i})-(a^{n}+\sum^{n-2}_{i=0}c_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n-2}_{k=1}(a^{n-1}-a^{i})c_{i}+a^{n-1}(1+a^{n}-2a)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\sum^{n-2}_{k=1}(a^{n-1}-a^{i})c_{i}+a^{n-1}(1+a^{2}-2a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{n-2}_{k=1}(a^{n-1}-a^{i})c_{i}+a^{n-1}(1-a)^{2}\geq 0

one can see that

, which leads to a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 2.2.8
.

Let
p(T)=T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial with coefficients in
\mathbb{V}
, if the inequality

c_{n-1}>1+c_{n-2}+\cdots\cdots+c_{1}+c_{0}

holds, then
p
has no roots whose multiplicity is bigger or equal that 2 in
[1,+\infty)
.

Proof.

If
p
has no roots in
[1,\infty)
then we are done. Now we only consider a root
a\in(1,\infty)
of
p
by Proposition
2.2.6
. It’s enough to prove that for any
q
with
p\in(T+a)q
,
0\notin q(a)
.

Let
q(T)=T^{n-1}+d_{n-2}T^{n-2}+\cdots+d_{0}
be such polynomial, then one can see

c_{0}=ad_{0}
and
c_{i}\in ad_{i}\boxplus d_{i-1}
for
i=1,\dots,n-1

Therefore the inequality

\displaystyle d_{n-2}+a

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c_{n-1}>1+c_{n-2}+\cdots+c_{1}+c_{0}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1+|ad_{n-2}-d_{n-3}|+|ad_{n-3}-d_{n-4}|+\cdots+|ad_{1}-d_{0}|+ad_{0}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1+ad_{n-2}-d_{n-3}+ad_{n-3}-d_{n-4}+\cdots+ad_{1}-d_{0}+ad_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+ad_{n-2}+(a-1)(d_{n-2}+d_{n-3}+\dots+d_{1}+d_{0})

holds, which implies that

1>d_{n-2}+d_{n-3}+\dots+d_{1}+d_{0}

Furthermore, for any

we have

e^{n-1}>e^{n-1}(d_{n-2}+d_{n-3}+\dots+d_{1}+d_{0})\geq e^{n-2}d_{n-2}+e^{n-3}d_{n-3}+\dots+ed_{1}+d_{0}

which leads to
e^{n-1}\notin e^{n-2}d_{n-2}\boxplus e^{n-3}d_{n-3}\boxplus\dots\boxplus ed_{1}\boxplus d_{0}
and then

.

Specifically, one can set

to reach

∎

2.3.
The Sharpness under Natural Hyperfield Homomorphism
\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}

Proposition 2.3.1
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{P}
,
x\boxplus x=\{x\}
holds.

Proof.

It is clear when
x=0
. If
x\neq 0
, then one can see

x\boxplus x=\{\frac{(\alpha+\beta)x}{\|(\alpha+\beta)x\|}:\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}=\{x\}

so we are done.
∎

Example 2.3.2
.

The map
f:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}
with

re^{i\theta}\longmapsto e^{i\theta}
when
r\neq 0
and
0\longmapsto 0

is a hyperfield homomorphism.

Proof.

It is clear that
f(0)=f(0),f(1)=f(1)
and
f(xy)=f(x)f(y)
.

For any
xe^{i\alpha}
and
ye^{i\beta}
, one has
f(xe^{i\alpha}\boxplus ye^{i\beta})=0
when

and

f(xe^{i\alpha}\boxplus ye^{i\beta})=f(\{xe^{i\alpha}+ye^{i\beta}\})=f(\{ze^{i\gamma}\})=\{e^{i\gamma}\}

where

z=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}+2xy\cos(\alpha-\beta)},\,\gamma=\arctan\frac{x\sin{\alpha}+y\sin{\beta}}{x\cos{\alpha}+y\cos{\beta}}

when either
x
or
y
is nonzero. Therefore we have

f(xe^{i\alpha}\boxplus ye^{i\beta})=\{0\}\subset\{0\}=f(xe^{i\alpha})\boxplus f(ye^{i\beta})
when
x=y=0

f(xe^{i\alpha}\boxplus ye^{i\beta})=\{\textup{sign}(x-y)e^{i\theta}\}\subset\{\pm e^{i\theta},0\}=f(xe^{i\alpha})\boxplus f(ye^{i\beta})
when
e^{i\alpha}=-e^{i\beta}

and

f(xe^{i\alpha}\boxplus ye^{i\beta})=\{e^{i\gamma}\}\subseteq\{\frac{ae^{i\alpha}+be^{i\beta}}{\|ae^{i\alpha}+be^{i\beta}\|}:a,b>0\}

when
xy\neq 0
since
e^{i\gamma}
is a point on the major arc between
e^{i\alpha}
and
e^{i\beta}
.
∎

Proposition 2.3.3
.

For
\overline{p}(T)=T^{3}+c_{2}T^{2}+c_{1}T+c_{0}

(\mathbb{P})

According to Proposition

If mult

If mult
{}_{a}(\overline{p})=1
, there exists
\overline{q}(T)=T^{2}+d_{1}T+d_{0}

Example 2.3.4

Consider
\overline{p}(T)=T^{3}+e^{i\frac{\pi}{24}}T^{2}+e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}}T+e^{i\frac{23\pi}{24}}
in Poly
(\mathbb{P})

\overline{p}(T)\in(T+1)\boxdot(T^{2}+e^{i\frac{\pi}{16}}T+e^{i\frac{23\pi}{24}})

since

of

, then we have

3.1.
The Sharpness under Natural Hyperfield Homomorphism
\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
p(T)=c_{n}T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial over
\mathbb{S}
, the
number of sign changes
in coefficients

of

\sigma(p)=\texttt{\#}\{k\geq 0|c_{k}=-c_{k+l+1}\neq 0\textup{ and }c_{k+1}=\cdots=c_{k+l}=0\textup{ for some }l\geq 0\}

Example 3.1.2
.

For
p(T)=T^{7}-T^{5}-T^{4}+T^{2}-1
, one has
\sigma(p)=2
.

Lemma 3.1.3
.

Let
p(T)=c_{n}T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
be a polynomial over
\mathbb{S}
, then one has mult
{}_{1}(p)=\sigma(p)
.

Remark 3.1.4
.

This lemma cited from
[
BL18
]
gives us a good way to compute the multiplicity of roots in hyperfield of signs. In fact, one can also determine the multiplicity of
-1
and
0
by it with simple deduction.

Proposition 3.1.5
.

For every sequence
(s_{i})_{i=0}^{n}\subset\mathbb{S}
with
s_{n}>0
and
s_{0}\neq 0
, there exist a corresponding polynomial
p(T)=c_{n}T^{n}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}[T]
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{a>0}\textup{mult}_{a}(p)=\sigma(p)

and
\textup{sign}(c_{i})=s_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,n
.

Proof.

The proof is given by David J. Grabiner in
[
DG99
]
where he gives a polynomial

p(T)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{s_{i}}{(2n)^{i^{2}}}T^{i}

which satisfies that
\sum_{a>0}\textup{mult}_{a}(p)=\sigma(p)
. It is valid because, for two every consecutive
s_{k},s_{l}
with
s_{k}s_{l}<0
in such polynomial, one can check that
p((2n)^{2k})p((2n)^{2l})<0
and then know that
p
has a root between
(2n)^{2k}
and
(2n)^{2l}
.
∎

Remark 3.1.6
.

Here one knows that there always exist a
p(T)\in\mathbb{R}[T]
, such that

\sum_{a\in f^{-1}(1)}\textup{mult}_{a}(p)=\sigma(p)=\sigma(\overline{p})=\textup{mult}_{1}(\overline{p})

for any
\overline{p}(T)
with coefficients in
\mathbb{S}
. Notice that
f(a)=0
is equal to
a=0
, so one has

\textup{mult}_{0}(p)=\textup{mult}_{0}(\overline{p})

Additionally, for every consecutive
s_{k},s_{l}
with
s_{k}s_{l}>0
, one has
p(-(2n)^{2k})p(-(2n)^{2l})<0
and then get a negative root, so

\sum_{a\in f^{-1}(-1)}\textup{mult}_{a}(p)=\textup{mult}_{-1}(\overline{p})

where
\textup{mult}_{-1}(\overline{p})=\textup{mult}_{1}(\overline{q})
where
q(T):=p(-T)
in Proposition
3.1.5
.

Therefore, the inequality in Theorem
2.1.2
under
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}
is always sharp.

3.2.
The Sharpness under Valuative Hyperfield Homomorphisms
\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}

Definition 3.2.1
.

Given a field
\mathbb{K}
, a map
\nu:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is called a
(Krull) valuation
if

(1)
\nu^{-1}(-\infty)=0
;

(2)
\nu(ab)=\nu(a)+\nu(b)

(3)
\nu(a+b)\leq\max\{\nu(a),\nu(b)\}

for all
a,b\in\mathbb{K}
.

Example 3.2.2
.

Consider the map

f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{0\},x\longmapsto\log{|x|}

where
f(0)=\log{0}=-\infty
. Then it is clear that
f
is a valuation.

Proposition 3.2.3
.

A map
\nu:\mathbb{K}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is a valuation if and only if it is a homomorphism of hyperfields.

Theorem 3.2.4
.

(Fundamental theorem for the tropical hyperfield) Given a polynomial
p=T^{n}+c_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{1}T+c_{0}
in Poly
(\mathbb{T})
, then

(1) There is a unique sequence
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{T}
, up to permutation of the indices, such that

c_{n-i}\in\mathop{\vphantom{\bigoplus}\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{0}{}{$\boxplus$}}}}}\displaylimits_{e_{1}<\cdots<e_{i}}\!\!\!\!{}_{\mathbb{T}}\,\,a_{e_{1}}\cdots a_{e_{i}}

for all
i=1,\dots,n
where
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{i}\}
is a subset of
\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and
\boxplus_{\mathbb{T}}
is the hyperaddition in
\mathbb{T}
.

(2) The equalities

\textup{mult}_{a}(p)=\#\{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\,|\,a=a_{i}\}

hold for all
a\in\mathbb{T}
.

Corollary 3.2.5
.

Given a polynomial
p
in
\textup{Poly}(\mathbb{T})
, one has
\deg(p)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{T}}\textup{mult}_{a}(p)
.

Remark 3.2.6
.

This theorem cited from
[
BL18
]
is critical to the study of tropical hyperfield. In fact, the techniques used to prove this theorem can be also applied to prove similar results about general valuative hyperfields. Moreover, one can immediately get the sharpness of the inequality in Theorem
2.1.2
under all (valuative) hyperfield homomorphisms
\tau:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}
where
\mathbb{F}
is a valuative hyperfield.

It is clear that

Vortex Rings in Event-by-Event Relativistic Heavy-Ion Collisions
David Dobrigkeit Chinellato, Michael Annan Lisa, Willian Matioli Serenone, Chun Shen, Jun Takahashi, Giorgio Torrieri

Abstract
We present event-by-event simulations for central asymmetric light+heavy and Au+Au collisions to investigate the formation and evolution of vortex-ring structures in the longitudinal flow velocity profile. The production-plane polarization of Λ \Lambda hyperons, defined w.r.t. the Λ \Lambda momentum and the beam, can track the “vortex-ring” feature in the event, a characteristic vortical structure generated by longitudinal flow gradients. We make comprehensive model predictions for the rapidity-dependent vortex-ring observables for different collision system sizes at s NN = 200 \sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=200 and 72 GeV. Our predictions at the latter energy can be explored in the future LHCb fixed-target experiment at the Large Hadron Collider.



I
Introduction

One of the most common manifestations of hydrodynamics in everyday physics is the appearance of vortical structures originating from gradients
[
1
]
. Most people are familiar with “smoke rings”, in both air and water, generated by localized fast-moving currents embedded in a larger medium. Considering the seeming explanatory power of hydrodynamics in heavy ion collisions – including small-on-large systems such as p+Au collisions –, it is interesting to look for vortex ring phenomena in such systems.

The appearance of collective phenomena in proton+nucleus collisions
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
makes these systems ideal laboratories for the search for such a phenomenon.
The gradients produced by a “bullet” passing through a larger medium mimic the conditions associated with vortex ring formation. We have argued in Ref.
[
7
]
that the production-plane polarization of
\Lambda
hyperons can be used to measure the vortex-ring-like structure in the flow profile of produced deconfined matter (an analogous problem with jets was examined in
[
8
,
9
]
). We initialized a three-dimensional viscous hydrodynamic simulation with a completely central collision (with impact parameter
b=0
fm) between a smooth proton and a smooth gold nucleus.

This work extends our simulations to more realistic conditions, including lumpy initial conditions and impact parameter fluctuations in event-by-event hydrodynamics. These simulations produce similar flow profiles; the vortex ring formation and observable are robust.
We present systematic studies on this vortex-ring observable for different light+heavy asymmetric collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\rm NN}}=200
and 72 GeV. The system size scan allows us to study how the vortex ring observable approaches the limit of axially symmetric collision systems, such as Au+Au collisions. Our model predictions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=72
GeV would motivate future measurements in the LHCb fixed-target experiments with the System for Measuring Overlap with Gas (SMOG) setup. The simulations at low collision energy also allow a quantitative comparison between our collective-dominated model and the earlier experimental results interpreted via QCD spin-orbit couplings
[
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
.

Experimentally, the vortex ring structure can be quantified as
[
7
]
,

\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}\equiv 2\left\langle\frac{\vec{S}_{\Lambda}\cdot\left(\hat{z}\times\vec{p}_{\Lambda}\right)}{|\hat{z}\times\vec{p}_{\Lambda}|}\right\rangle_{\phi_{\Lambda}},

(1)

where
\hat{z}\equiv(0,0,1)
points in the direction of the light-ion beam, and the average is taken over the
\Lambda
momentum azimuthal angle
\phi_{\Lambda}
about the beam. The vectors
\vec{S}_{\Lambda}
and
\vec{p}_{\Lambda}
are the spin and momentum vectors for the
\Lambda
hyperons in the lab frame, respectively.

The
\Lambda
’s spin polarization can be computed using the following the Cooper-Frye like formula from a vortical medium
[
15
,
16
,
17
]
,

S^{\mu}(p)=-\frac{1}{8m}\epsilon^{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}\left\langle\omega_{\sigma\rho}p_{\tau}\right\rangle,

(2)

where
\omega_{\sigma\rho}\equiv-\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{\sigma}(u_{\rho}/T)-\partial_{\rho}(u_{\sigma}/T)]
is the thermal vorticity tensor.
The
\left\langle...\right\rangle
operator is defined as,

\left\langle X\right\rangle\equiv\frac{\int d\Sigma_{\lambda}p^{\lambda}n_{F}(1-n_{F})X}{\int d\Sigma_{\lambda}p^{\lambda}n_{F}},

(3)

where
n_{F}
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution, and
d\Sigma_{\mu}
is the normal vector on the hydrodynamic freeze-out hypersurface.

Equation (
2
) builds the connection between the vortex ring observable
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}
with the production plane fluid vorticity density at freeze-out

\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{fluid}}^{\hat{z}}\equiv\frac{\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\Omega_{\mu}n_{\nu}\hat{z}_{\rho}u_{\sigma}}{|\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}n_{\nu}\hat{z}_{\rho}u_{\sigma}|},

(4)

where
u_{\sigma}
is the fluid velocity and
\Omega^{\mu}\equiv\epsilon^{\mu\alpha\beta\gamma}\omega_{\alpha\beta}u_{\gamma}
is the vorticity vector orthogonal to
u^{\mu}
. The unit vectors
\hat{z}^{\rho}=(0,0,0,1)
and
n^{\mu}
points to the normal direction of the freeze-out surface,
n^{\mu}\equiv d\Sigma^{\mu}/|d\Sigma^{\mu}|
.

This paper will be laid out as follows. Section
II
will provide a concrete description of the (3+1)D dynamical model and computations of the
\Lambda
’s polarization vector. In Section
III
, we will discuss the ring observables in detail. We will conclude with some closing remarks in Sec.
IV

In this paper, we use the conventions for the metric tensor
g^{\mu\nu}=\mathrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
and the Levi-Civita symbol
\epsilon^{0123}=1

This work employs the geometric-based 3D initial conditions developed in Refs.
[

T_{A(B)}(\vec{x}_{\perp})=\sum_{i}\frac{1}{2\pi\omega^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{|\vec{x}_{\perp}-\vec{x}_{\perp,i}|^{2}}{2\omega^{2}}\right).

(5)

Here, the summation goes over all participant nucleons in the colliding nucleus. We assume a 2D Gaussian profile for each nucleon in the transverse plane with a width
\omega
as a model parameter

We follow the 3D initial model

\displaystyle T^{\tau\tau}

\displaystyle=e(\vec{x}_{\perp},\eta_{s})\cosh(y_{L}(\vec{x}_{\perp})),

(6)

\displaystyle T^{\tau\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\tau_{0}}e(\vec{x}_{\perp},\eta_{s})\sinh(y_{L}(\vec{x}_{\perp})).

\displaystyle e(\vec{x}_{\perp},\eta_{s},y_{\mathrm{CM}},f)=

\displaystyle\quad\mathcal{N}_{e}(\vec{x}_{\perp})\exp\bigg[-\frac{(|\eta_{s}-(y_{\mathrm{CM}}(1-f))|-\eta_{0})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\eta}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\theta(|\eta_{s}-(y_{\mathrm{CM}}(1-f))|-\eta_{0})\bigg],

(10)

where

f

[
18

\eta-y_{\mathrm{CM}}

is fixed by the total incoming collision energy, and it scales with
\sqrt{T_{A}(\vec{x}_{\perp})T_{B}(\vec{x}_{\perp})}
at high energies
[
18
]
. The values of the model parameters are listed in Table
1
. The initial baryon density distribution is set up as in Ref.
[
18
]
.

[

Now, we would like to understand the transverse momentum
p_{T}
dependence of the
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}
observable.
Based on Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
), we can show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(p_{T})

\displaystyle\propto\left\langle\frac{1}{m}(E\omega^{z\sigma}-p^{z}\omega^{t\sigma})\frac{p_{\sigma}}{p_{T}}+\frac{m}{p_{T}}\omega^{tz}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\propto\left\langle\frac{1}{m}(E\omega^{zi_{T}}-p^{z}\omega^{ti_{T}})\frac{p_{i_{T}}}{p_{T}}-\frac{p_{T}}{m}\omega^{tz}\right\rangle,

(11)

where the index
i_{T}

\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(p_{T})\propto\left\langle\frac{E}{m}(\hat{p}^{x}\omega^{xz}+\hat{p}^{y}\omega^{yz})-\frac{p_{T}}{m}\omega^{tz}\right\rangle,

(12)

where the unit 2D vector
\hat{p}^{i_{T}}\equiv p^{i_{T}}/p_{T}

p_{T}=\sqrt{p_{x}^{2}+p_{y}^{2}}

Eq. (
12
) shows that

at large transverse momenta, which is observed in our calculations, shown in Fig.
3

p_{T}\rightarrow 0

\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(p_{T})\rightarrow\left\langle(\hat{p}^{x}\omega^{xz}+\hat{p}^{y}\omega^{yz})\right\rangle
. Our calculations show
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(p_{T})\rightarrow 0
after the
\left\langle\cdot\right\rangle
average defined in Eq. (
3

For small systems like p+Au and
3

with

\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(p_{T})

\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(p_{T})

Additional gradient-induced polarization

In addition to the thermal vorticity, the
\Lambda
hyperons’ polarization also receives contribution from the thermal shear tensor and gradients of
\mu_{B}/T

[

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{SIP(type\,I)}}^{\mu}(p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4m}\epsilon^{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}\left\langle\frac{1}{p\cdot u}\hat{t}_{\rho}\xi_{\sigma\lambda}p^{\lambda}p_{\tau}\right\rangle,

(13)

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{SIP(type\,II)}}^{\mu}(p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4m}\epsilon^{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}\left\langle\frac{1}{p\cdot u}u_{\rho}\xi_{\sigma\lambda}p^{\lambda}_{\perp}p_{\tau}\right\rangle,

(14)

\displaystyle S_{\mu_{B}\mathrm{IP}}^{\mu}(p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4m}\epsilon^{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}\left\langle\frac{T}{p\cdot u}u_{\rho}\partial_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\mu_{B}}{T}\right)p_{\tau}\right\rangle.

(15)

Here, the thermal shear tensor
\xi^{\sigma\lambda}\equiv\frac{1}{2}[\partial^{\sigma}\left(\frac{u^{\lambda}}{T}\right)+\partial^{\lambda}\left(\frac{u^{\sigma}}{T}\right)]
. In the type I shear-induced polarization (SIP), the unit vector
\hat{t}^{\rho}=(1,0,0,0)

33

]

is the momentum vector transverse to the flow velocity

Using Eq. (
1
), we can obtain their contribution to the
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(p)
observables as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(\mathrm{SIP(type\,I)})\propto\left\langle\frac{p_{\lambda}}{m(p\cdot u)}[-\xi^{z\lambda}p_{T}-\xi^{i_{T}\lambda}\hat{p}_{i_{T}}p^{z}]\right\rangle

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(\mathrm{SIP(type\,II))}\propto

\displaystyle\qquad\left\langle\frac{p_{\lambda,\perp}}{m(p\cdot u)}\{-\xi^{z\lambda}[p^{t}(u^{i_{T}}\hat{p}_{i_{T}})+u^{t}p_{T}]\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 68.28644pt-\xi^{i_{T}\lambda}\hat{p}_{i_{T}}(u^{t}p^{z}-u^{z}p^{t})

\displaystyle\hskip 68.28644pt+\xi^{t\lambda}[(u^{i_{T}}\hat{p}_{i_{T}})p^{z}+u^{z}p_{T}]\}\bigg\rangle

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(\mu_{B}\mathrm{IP})\propto

\displaystyle\qquad\bigg\langle\frac{T}{m(p\cdot u)}\bigg\{-\partial^{z}\left(\frac{\mu_{B}}{T}\right)[p^{t}(u^{i_{T}}\hat{p}_{i_{T}})+u^{t}p_{T}]

\displaystyle\hskip 68.28644pt-\hat{p}_{i_{T}}\partial^{i_{T}}\left(\frac{\mu_{B}}{T}\right)(u^{t}p^{z}-p^{t}u^{z})

\displaystyle\hskip 68.28644pt+\partial^{t}\left(\frac{\mu_{B}}{T}\right)[(u^{i_{T}}\hat{p}_{i_{T}})p^{z}+u^{z}p_{T}]\bigg\}\bigg\rangle

IV
Conclusions

In conclusion, we have confirmed and extended the studies of the production plane polarization observable
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}
in asymmetric collisions, first discussed in
[
7
]
. We included hot spot and impact parameter fluctuations event-by-event and expanded our scope to study its system size dependence. The pseudo-rapidity dependence of the
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(\eta)
observable can serve as a sensitive probe for the initial longitudinal flow velocity. We predict a linear
p_{T}
dependence of
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}(p_{T})
for
p_{T}>1
GeV at mid-rapidity. Its slope can provide information about the relative sizes of individual components of the thermal vorticity tensor.
We further provide model predictions for asymmetric collision systems in the LHCb SMOG experiment setup. These asymmetric collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=72
GeV reveal more non-trivial longitudinal dynamics.
We systematically explored the sensitivity of the
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}
observable on various model parameters.

The proposed
\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}^{\hat{z}}
observable is a promising probe of hydrodynamic behavior in small asymmetric systems. Quantitative comparisons will set valuable constraints on the longitudinal dynamics and early-stage stopping mechanism.
We look forward to experimental investigations in this direction.

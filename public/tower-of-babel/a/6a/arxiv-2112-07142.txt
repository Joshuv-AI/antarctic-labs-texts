L 2 L^{2} -blowup estimates of the plate equation
Ryo Ikehata

Abstract
We consider the Cauchy problems in 𝐑 n {\bf R}^{n} for the plate equation with a weighted L 1 L^{1} -initial data. We derive optimal estimates of the L 2 L^{2} -norm of solutions for n = 1 , 2 , 3 , 4 n=1,2,3,4 . In particular, such obtained results express infinite time blowup property in the case when the 0 0 -th moment of the initial velocity does not vanish. The idea to derive them is strongly inspired from an already developed technique [ 6 , 11 , 7 ] .

Now, we consider the Cauchy problem of the plate equation:

\displaystyle u_{tt}+\Delta^{2}u=0,\ \ \ (t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\bf R}^{n},

(1.1)

\displaystyle u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{t}(0,x)=u_{1}(x),\ x\in{\bf R}^{n}.

(1.2)

Here, we assume, for the moment,
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
.

Concerning the existence of a unique energy solution to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), by the standard semi-group theory one can find that the problem (1.1)-(1.2) has a unique weak solution

u\in C([0,\infty);H^{2}({\bf R}^{n}))\cap C^{1}([0,\infty);L^{2}({\bf R}^{n}))

satisfying the energy conservation law such that

E(t)=E(0),\quad t\geq 0,

(1.3)

where the total energy
E(t)
for the solution to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) can be defined by

E(t):=\frac{1}{2}\left(\|u_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\|\Delta u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\right).

Here,
\|u\|
denotes the usual
L^{2}
-norm of
u\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
.

n\geq 5

Before going to introduce our Theorems, we set

I_{0,n}:=\|u_{0}\|+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}({\bf R}^{n})}+\|u_{1}\|+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1}({\bf R}^{n})}.

The following two theorems give a hint about a question why
n\geq 5
in the plate equation.

Theorem 1.1

Let
n=1,2,3
. Let
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. Then, the solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies the following properties under the additional regularity on the initial data:

[u_{0},u_{1}]\in L^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq C_{1}I_{0,n}t^{1-\frac{n}{4}},

[u_{0},u_{1}]\in L^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{1,1}({\bf R}^{n})\quad\Rightarrow\quad C_{2}\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx\right|t^{1-\frac{n}{4}}\leq\|u(t,\cdot)\|

for
t\gg 1
, where
C_{j}>0

(
j=1,2
)
are constants depending only on
n
.

Our next result is the case of
n=4
.

Theorem 1.2

Let
n=4
. Let
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{4})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{4})
. Then, the solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies the following properties under the additional regularity on the initial data:

[u_{0},u_{1}]\in L^{1}({\bf R}^{4})\times L^{1}({\bf R}^{4})\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq C_{1}I_{0,4}\sqrt{\log t},

[u_{0},u_{1}]\in L^{1}({\bf R}^{4})\times L^{1,1}({\bf R}^{4})\quad\Rightarrow\quad C_{2}\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{4}}u_{1}(x)dx\right|\sqrt{\log t}\leq\|u(t,\cdot)\|

for
t\gg 1
, where
C_{j}>0

(
j=1,2
)
are constants depending only on the space dimension
n=4
.

Remark 1.1

One of our advantages in the results is that we never use the compactness argument as is frequently developed in the wave equation case because of the non-Kowalewskian property of the equation (
1.1
). The method will be widely applicable to the other types of evolution equations. Indeed, it will be possible to generalize our results to the more general
\sigma
-evolution equations:

u_{tt}+(-\Delta)^{\sigma}u=0,

where
((-\Delta)^{\sigma}f)(x):={\cal F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}(|\xi|^{2\sigma}\hat{f}(\xi))(x)
and
\sigma>0
. A threshold number
n^{*}
on the dimension
n
to divide whether the blow up phenomenon occurs or not can be defined by
n^{*}=2\sigma
.

Remark 1.2

In both Theorems, the so-called infinite time blowup property can be observed in the case when
n<5
. One has to treat more delicately when we study decay estimates, asymptotic profiles, nonlinear problems and so on, of the plate equation considered in unbounded domains, and in particular, in
{\bf R}^{n}
.

Contrary to infinite time blowup results above, in the case when
\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx=0
, one has the
L^{2}
-boundedness property (at least) for
n=3,4
.

Theorem 1.3

Let
n=3,4
, and
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. Assume further that
u_{1}\in L^{1,\gamma}({\bf R}^{n})
with

n=4\quad\Rightarrow\quad\gamma\in(0,1],

n=3\quad\Rightarrow\quad\gamma\in(\frac{1}{2},1].

If

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx=0,

then the solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies

\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq C(\|u_{0}\|+\|u_{1}\|+\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}),

where
C>0
is a constant depending on the space dimension
n
and
\gamma
.

In the case of
n=1,2
, if one assumes an additional vanishing condition of the
1
th-order moment of the initial velocity
u_{1}
one can state the following
L^{2}
-boundedness result.

Theorem 1.4

Let
n=1,2
, and
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. Assume further that
u_{1}\in L^{1,\gamma}({\bf R}^{n})
with

n=2\quad\Rightarrow\quad\gamma\in(1,2],

n=1\quad\Rightarrow\quad\gamma\in(\frac{3}{2},2].

Under the following two conditions,

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx=0,\qquad\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}x_{j}u_{1}(x)dx=0\quad(j=1,2,\cdots,n),

(1.4)

the solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies

\|u(t,\cdot)\|\leq C(\|u_{0}\|+\|u_{1}\|+\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}),

where
C>0
is a constant depending on the space dimension
n
and
\gamma
.

Example.
Let
n=1
, and let
g\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R})
be an odd function, and choose
u_{1}(x):=g^{\prime}(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R})
. Then, one has

\int_{{\bf R}}u_{1}(x)dx=0,\quad\int_{{\bf R}}xu_{1}(x)dx=\int_{{\bf R}}xg^{\prime}(x)dx=-\int_{{\bf R}}g(x)dx=0.

This expresses an example of the initial velocity satisfying (
1.4
) for
n=1
.

Similarly, in the case when
n=2
, one can construct an example satsfying (
1.4
) by

u_{1}(x_{1},x_{2}):=g^{\prime}(x_{1})h^{\prime}(x_{2}),

where
g,h\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R})
.

As a counter part of Theorem 1.4 one can get the infinite time blowup result in the case when
n=1,2
. For simplicity, we mention only the case of
\gamma=2
in order to compare it with Theorem 1.4..

Theorem 1.5

(1)
Let
n=1
, and
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R})\times L^{2}({\bf R})
. Assume further that
u_{1}\in L^{1,2}({\bf R})
satisfies

\left|\int_{{\bf R}}u_{1}(x)dx\right|+\left|\int_{{\bf R}}xu_{1}(x)dx\right|>0.

(1.5)

Then the solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies

C\left(\left|\int_{{\bf R}}xu_{1}(x)dx\right|t^{\frac{1}{4}}+\left|\int_{{\bf R}}u_{1}(x)dx\right|t^{\frac{3}{4}}\right)\leq\|u(t,\cdot)\|,\quad t\gg 1,

where
C>0
is a constant.

(2)
Let
n=2
, and
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{2})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{2})
. Assume further that
u_{1}\in L^{1,2}({\bf R}^{2})
satisfies

\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}u_{1}(x)dx\right|+\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}x_{1}u_{1}(x)dx\right|+\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}x_{2}u_{1}(x)dx\right|>0.

(1.6)

Then the solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies

C\left(\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}x_{1}u_{1}(x)dx\right|+\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}x_{2}u_{1}(x)dx\right|\right)\sqrt{\log t}+C\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{2}}u_{1}(x)dx\right|\sqrt{t}\leq\|u(t,\cdot)\|,\quad t\gg 1,

where
C>0
is a constant.

Remark 1.3

As a result of Theorem 1.5, (for examle) in case of
n=1
, even if
\int_{{\bf R}}u_{1}(x)dx=0
, if
\int_{{\bf R}}xu_{1}(x)dx\neq 0
, then one has the infinite time blowup result with its rate
t^{1/4}
, however one can know nothing about the optimality of the blowup rate
t^{1/4}
. A corresponding result for
n=2
is true similarly. Of course, one should investigate (optimal) upper bound estimates of Theorem 1.5, however, this study will be left to the reader’s interest.

\|\cdot\|_{q}

\|\cdot\|

.
We also introduce the following weighted functional spaces.

L^{1,\gamma}({\bf R}^{n}):=\left\{f\in L^{1}({\bf R}^{n})\;\bigm|\;\|f\|_{1,\gamma}:=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(1+|x|^{\gamma})|f(x)|dx<+\infty\right\}.

One denotes the Fourier transform
{\cal F}_{x\to\xi}(f)(\xi)

{\cal F}_{x\to\xi}(f)(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi):=\displaystyle{\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx,\hskip 9.24994pt\xi\in{\bf R}^{n},

as usual with
i:=\sqrt{-1}
, and
{\cal F}_{\xi\to x}^{-1}
expresses its inverse Fourier transform. Finally, we denote the surface area of the
n
-dimensional unit ball by
\omega_{n}:=\displaystyle{\int_{|\omega|=1}}d\omega
, and we set
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n})\in{\bf R}^{n}
.

2
L^{2}
-upper bound estimates: higher dimensional case

In this section, we introduce a device to derive
L^{2}
-estimates of solutions to problem (1.1)-(1.2) by relying on the modified Morawetz method developed newly in
[
9
]
. For this we prepare the following Hardy type inequality (see Davies-Hinz
[
4
]
). The basic concept in
[
9
]
is that the Hardy type inequality implies the
L^{2}
-upper bound estimate of the solution in several types of linear evolution equations.

Lemma 2.1

Let
n\geq 5
. Then there exists a constant
C^{*}>0
such that

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\frac{|w(x)|^{2}}{|x|^{4}}dx\leq C^{*}\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\Delta w(x)|^{2}dx

for all
w\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
.

As in the idea
[
9
]
, for the solution
u(t,x)
to problem (1.1)-(1.2) we set

v(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}u(s,x)ds.

Then, the function
v(t,x)
satisfies the following equation and initial data.

\displaystyle v_{tt}+\Delta^{2}v=u_{1},\ \ \ (t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\bf R}^{n},

(2.1)

\displaystyle v(0,x)=0,\quad v_{t}(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\ x\in{\bf R}^{n}.

(2.2)

Multiplying the both sides of (
2.1
)-(
2.2
) by
v_{t}
, and integrating it over
[0,t]\times{\bf R}^{n}
one can get the following energy equality such that

\frac{1}{2}\|v_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta v(t,\cdot)\|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\|v_{0}\|^{2}+\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)v(t,x)dx.

(2.3)

Now we are in a position to use Lemma
2.1
to proceed the following computations based on the Schwarz inequality:

\left|\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)v(t,x)dx\right|\leq\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|u_{1}(x)||v(t,x)|dx=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\left(|x|^{2}|u_{1}(x)|\right)\left(\frac{|v(t,x)|}{|x|^{2}}\right)dx

\leq\left(\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|x|^{4}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\frac{|v(t,x)|^{2}}{|x|^{4}}dx\right)^{1/2}

\leq C_{\varepsilon}\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|x|^{4}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx+\varepsilon\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\frac{|v(t,x)|^{2}}{|x|^{4}}dx

\leq C_{\varepsilon}\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|x|^{4}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx+\varepsilon C^{*}\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\Delta v(t,x)|^{2}dx

(2.4)

with some constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
, which depends on each
\varepsilon>0
. Thus, from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) it follows that

\frac{1}{2}\|v_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\Delta v(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|x|^{4}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx+\varepsilon C^{*}\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|\Delta v(t,x)|^{2}dx,

which implies

\frac{1}{2}\|v_{t}(t,\cdot)\|^{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon C^{*}\right)\|\Delta v(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|u_{0}\|^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|x|^{4}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx

provided that

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|x|^{4}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx<+\infty.

By choosing
\varepsilon>0
small enough one can arrive at the crucial
L^{2}
-estimate by means of the multiplier method only because of
v_{t}(t,x)=u(t,x)
. The result below compensates the unknown information on
n\geq 5
.

Proposition 2.1

Let
n\geq 5
, and
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})\times L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that the solution
u(t,x)
to problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) satisfies

\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|x|^{4}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx\right)\quad t\geq 0,

provided additionally that

\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}|x|^{4}|u_{1}(x)|^{2}dx<+\infty.

(2.5)

Remark 2.1

An essential part of this argument above has already been developed previously in
[
2
]
for the plate equation with damping and the rotational inertia term by employing the idea due to
[
9
]
. This part is just a trial to observe the case of
n\geq 5
.

Remark 2.2

Note that in the case of
4\leq n
, the condition (
2.5
) together with
u_{1}\in L^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
does not necessarily imply
u_{1}\in L^{1}({\bf R}^{n})
.

So, one can apply the method due to
[
9
]
to get the
L^{2}
-bound in the case when
n\geq 5
, however, this method decisively depends on the existence of the Hardy type inequality. So, the results for
n=1,2,3,4
of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 are quite important since those are independent of any such inequalities. We have to derive
L^{2}
-bound estimates from the equation itself.

One can proceed the similar argument by using the following inequality in place of Lemma
2.1
(see
[
2
]
for its detail).

Lemma 2.2

Let
n\geq 5
. Then there exists a constant
C^{*}>0
such that

\|w\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-4}}}\leq C^{*}\|\Delta w\|

for all
w\in H^{2}({\bf R}^{n})
.

3
L^{2}
-lower bound estimates

In this section, we derive the lower bound estimates of the
t
-function
\|u(t,\cdot)\|
based on the Plancherel Theorem combined with low frequency estimates (cf.
[
6
]
). For the proof, it suffices to assume
[u_{0},u_{1}]\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})\times C_{0}^{\infty}({\bf R}^{n})
to have sufficient regularity of the solution
u(t,x)
because the density argument can be applied in the final estimates.

We first set

L:=\sup_{\theta\neq 0}\left|\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}\right|<+\infty.

(3.1)

On the other hand, because of

\lim_{\theta\to+0}\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}=1,

there exists a real number
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\left|\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}

(3.2)

for all
\theta\in(0,\delta_{0}^{2}]
. The following fundamental inequality will be useful, too:

|a+b|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}|a|^{2}-|b|^{2}

(3.3)

for all
a,b\in{\bf C}
.

In order to get the lower bound estimate for the quantity
\|u(t,\cdot)\|
, it suffices to treat
\|w(t,\cdot)\|
with
w(t,\xi):={\cal F}_{x\to\xi}(u(t,\cdot))(\xi)=\hat{u}(t,\xi)
because of the Plancherel Theorem.

Now we decompose the quantity
\|w(t,\cdot)\|
as follows: for each
n\geq 1

\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}=\left(\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}+\int_{|\xi|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\right)|w(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi=I_{low}^{(n)}(t)+I_{high}^{(n)}(t).

(3.4)

Here we have just chosen
t>0
large enough such that

\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}\leq 1.

By the way, in the Fourier space
{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n}
the problem (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and its solution
u(t,x)
can be transformed into the following ODE with parameter
\xi\in{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n}
:

\displaystyle w_{tt}+|\xi|^{4}w=0,\ \ \ t>0,\quad\xi\in{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n},

(3.5)

\displaystyle w(0,\xi)=w_{0}(\xi),\quad w_{t}(0,\xi)=w_{1}(\xi),\ \ \ \xi\in{\bf R}^{n},

(3.6)

where
w_{0}(\xi):=\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)
and
w_{1}(\xi):=\hat{u}_{1}(\xi)
. In addition, one can solve the problem (
3.5
)-(
3.6
) as follows:

w(t,\xi)=\frac{\sin(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{2}}w_{1}(\xi)+\cos(t|\xi|^{2})w_{0}(\xi).

(3.7)

Now, let us derive the lower bound estimates for
I_{low}^{(n)}(t)
because one has
\|w(t,\cdot)\|^{2}\geq I_{low}^{(n)}(t)
. One relies on a device coming from an idea in
[
6
]
. Indeed, it follows from (
3.7
) and (
3.3
) that

I_{low}^{(n)}(t)=\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\left|\frac{\sin(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{2}}w_{1}(\xi)+\cos(t|\xi|^{2})w_{0}(\xi)\right|^{2}d\xi

\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi-\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\cos^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})|w_{0}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

=:\frac{1}{2}J_{1}(t)-J_{2}(t).

(3.8)

Let us first estimate
J_{1}(t)
by using the decomposition of the initial data
w_{1}(\xi)
in the Fourier space:

w_{1}(\xi)=P+(A(\xi)-iB(\xi)),\quad\xi\in{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n},

where

P:=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}u_{1}(x)dx,

A(\xi):=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}(\cos(x\xi)-1)u_{1}(x)dx,\quad B(\xi):=\int_{{\bf R}^{n}}\sin(x\xi)u_{1}(x)dx.

It is known (see
[
5
]
) that there is a constant
M>0
such that

|A(\xi)-iB(\xi)|\leq M|\xi|\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,1}},\quad\xi\in{\bf R}_{\xi}^{n},

(3.9)

in case of
u_{1}\in L^{1,1}({\bf R}^{n})
. Then, from (
3.3
) we see that

J_{1}(t)=\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\geq\frac{P^{2}}{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}}d\xi-\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}|A(\xi)-iB(\xi)|^{2}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}}d\xi

=\frac{P^{2}}{2}K_{1}(t)-K_{2}(t).

(3.10)

K_{2}(t)
can be estimated from above by using and (
3.1
) and (
3.9
):

K_{2}(t)\leq M^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,1}}^{2}\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}t^{2}\left(\frac{\sin(t|\xi|^{2})}{t|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{2}|\xi|^{2}d\xi

\leq M^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,1}}^{2}L^{2}t^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}|\xi|^{2}d\xi=M^{2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,1}}^{2}\omega_{n}L^{2}t^{2}\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}r^{n+1}dr

=\frac{L^{2}}{n+2}M^{2}\omega_{n}\delta_{0}^{n+2}\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1,1}}^{2}t^{1-\frac{n}{2}},\quad t\gg 1.

(3.11)

On the other hand, one can obtain the lower bound estimate for
K_{1}(t)
because of (
3.2
):

As in Section 3, one again assumes

From (
3.4

for all

I_{low}^{(n)}(t)\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}|\frac{\sin(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{2}}w_{1}(\xi)+\cos(t|\xi|^{2})w_{0}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\leq 2\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi+2\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\cos^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})|w_{0}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

(4.1)

It is easy to obtain the estimate for
L_{2}(t)
as in the estimate for

in Section 2:

L_{2}(t)\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}|w_{0}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\omega_{n}\int_{0}^{\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}r^{n-1}dr

=\frac{\omega_{n}\delta_{0}^{n}}{n}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}t^{-\frac{n}{2}},

(4.2)

where one has just used the fat that

\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}d\xi=\frac{\omega_{n}\delta_{0}^{n}}{n}t^{-\frac{n}{2}},\quad t\gg 1.

(4.3)

Let us estimate
L_{1}(t)
. Indeed, from (
3.1
) and (
4.3
) one has

L_{1}(t)=\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}t^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{(t|\xi|^{2})^{2}}d\xi

\leq\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}L^{2}t^{2}\frac{\omega_{n}\delta_{0}^{n}}{n}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}=\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}L^{2}\frac{\omega_{n}\delta_{0}^{n}}{n}t^{2-\frac{n}{2}},\quad t\gg 1.

(4.4)

Thus, from (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.4
) one can obtain the low-frequency estimate

I_{low}^{(n)}(t)\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}t^{-\frac{n}{2}}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}t^{2-\frac{n}{2}}\right),\quad t\gg 1,

(4.5)

with some constant

Next, let us treat

to get the upper bound estimate. Indeed, similar to the above estimate one stars with the following inequalities.

I_{high}^{(n)}(t)\leq\int_{|\xi|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}|\frac{\sin(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{2}}w_{1}(\xi)+\cos(t|\xi|^{2})w_{0}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\leq 2\int_{|\xi|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi+2\int_{|\xi|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}}\cos^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})|w_{0}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

(4.6)

It is easy to treat

as follows. This can be derived for all

(4.7)

Let us estimate
N_{1}^{(n)}(t)
. First, for all

N_{1}^{(n)}(t)=\int_{|\xi|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{t^{1/8}}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi+\int_{\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{t^{1/8}}}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|^{2})}{|\xi|^{4}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

=:R_{1}^{(n)}(t)+R_{2}^{(n)}(t).

(4.8)

To begin with, we treat
R_{1}^{(n)}(t)

R_{1}^{(n)}(t)\leq\frac{t^{1/2}}{\delta_{0}^{4}}\int_{|\xi|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{t^{1/8}}}\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\leq\frac{t^{1/2}}{\delta_{0}^{4}}\int_{|\xi|\geq\frac{\delta_{0}}{t^{1/8}}}|w_{1}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leq\frac{\sqrt{t}}{\delta_{0}^{4}}\|u_{1}\|^{2},\quad t\gg 1.

(4.9)

On the other hand, in the case of

can be estimated as follows:

R_{2}^{(n)}(t)\leq\|u_{1}\|_{1}^{2}\int_{\frac{\delta_{0}}{\sqrt{t}}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{\delta_{0}}{t^{1/8}}}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{4}}d\xi

(4.10)

Thus, from (
4.6
), (
4.7
), (
4.8
), (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) one has the infinite time blowup estimate for

\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|u_{1}\|^{2}+\|u_{1}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\frac{1}{4-n}\right)t^{2-\frac{n}{2}},\quad t\gg 1.

Now, we introduce one more auxiliary function

By applying Lemma 3.1 of

|\hat{u}_{1}(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{\gamma}\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma},

(5.9)

, where

\int_{|\xi|\geq\delta}|\hat{u}(t,\xi)|^{2}\leq\|u_{0}\|^{2}+\|u_{1}\|^{2}+C\|u_{1}\|_{1,\gamma}^{2}\int_{1\geq|\xi|\geq\delta}|\xi|^{2\gamma-4}d\xi

provided that

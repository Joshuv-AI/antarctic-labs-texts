2.
Harmonic analysis on Homogeneous Trees

We begin this section by recapitulating some basic and important results of harmonic analysis on the homogeneous tree. For more details about homogeneous tree see
[
11
,

,

,

,

.

A
homogeneous tree

\mathfrak{X}
of degree
q+1
is a connected graph with no loops, in which every vertex is adjacent to
q+1
other vertices.
We assume
q\geq 2
. We fix an arbitrary reference point
o
in
\mathfrak{X}
and
|x|
will denote the distance from
o
of the vertex
x
. The tree
\mathfrak{X}
carries a natural measure, a counting measure, and a natural distance
d,
viz,
d(x,y)
is the number of edges between the vertices
x
and
y
. We denote
[x,y]
, the unique geodesic path joining two arbitrary vertices
x
and
y
.

An infinite geodesic ray
\omega
in
\mathfrak{X}
is a one-sided sequence
\{\omega_{n}:n=0,1,2\ldots\}
where the
\omega_{n}
are
in
\mathfrak{X}
. The boundary
\Omega
of the tree
\mathfrak{X}
is the set of all infinite geodesic rays starting at
o
. It is also the set of equivalence classes of one-sided geodesics, where two half geodesics
\{\omega_{n}:n=0,1,2\ldots\}
and
\{y_{n}:n=0,1,2\ldots\}
are equivalent if they meet at infinity, that is if there exists
k,N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq N,\omega_{n}=y_{n+k}.
The one-sided geodesic starting at a point
x
and equivalent to
\omega
will be denoted by
[x,\omega)
.

The height functions
h_{\omega}:\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined for all
\omega=\{x_{0},x_{1},\ldots\}
in
\Omega
with
x_{0}=o
by

h_{\omega}(x)=\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\left(m-d\left(x,x_{m}\right)\right).

These functions are normalized so that
h_{\omega}(o)=0.
Another definition of the height functions can be give in this way, for every
x\in\mathfrak{X}
and
\omega\in\Omega,
we denote
c(x,\omega)
be the confluence point of
x
and
\omega
i.e., the last point lying on
[o,\omega)
in the geodesic path
[o,x]
then

h_{\omega}(x)=2|c(x,\omega)|-|x|.

For
x\in\mathfrak{X},
let
E_{i}(x)=\{\omega\in\Omega:|c(x,\omega)|=i\}
for all
|x|\geq j\geq 0
. We use the notation
E(x)
for the set
E_{|x|}(x)
. Then there exist a unique
K
-invariant probability measure
\nu
such that

\nu(E_{j}(x))=\dfrac{q}{(q+1)q^{j}},\text{ where }j\leq|x|.

Therefore
(\Omega,\mathcal{M},\nu)
is a measure space with
\sigma
algebra
\mathcal{M}
being generated by the set
\{E(x):x\in\mathfrak{X}\}
.
\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the sub-algebra of
\mathcal{M}
generated by the sets
E(x),~|x|\leq n.
The conditional expectation or
\mathcal{E}_{n}(F)
of an integrable function
F
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{M},\nu)
relative to the sub-algebra
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is defined by

\mathcal{E}_{n}(F)(\omega)=\frac{1}{\nu(E_{n}(\omega))}\int\limits_{E_{n}(\omega)}F(\omega^{\prime})d\nu(\omega^{\prime}).

This
\mathcal{E}_{n}
are called the family of averaging operators.

2.1.
Change of reference point

One can define all the previous substance with respect to another reference point. For a new reference point
x_{0}
we would write
c^{x_{0}}(x,\omega),E_{i}^{x_{0}}(x)\mathcal{E}_{n}^{x_{0}}\ldots
to denote the change of reference point. Nonetheless, we will write
h_{\omega}(x)-h_{\omega}\left(x_{0}\right)
in place of
h_{\omega}^{x_{0}}(x)
. One also note the Radon-Nikodym derivative

\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{y}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{x}}(\omega)=q^{h_{\omega}(y)-h_{\omega}(x)}

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{X}
and
\omega\in\Omega
.

2.2.
The Fourier-Helgason transform

Let
f:\mathfrak{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a function of finite support. The Fourier-Helgason transform
of
f
is denoted by
\mathcal{H}f
defined by

\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)=\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}f(y)q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}

for every
\omega\in\Omega
and
s\in[0,\tau]
with
\tau=\frac{\pi}{\log q}.
Here
\log(t)
is the natural logarithm of
t\in\mathbb{R}.
We also have the inversion formula given by

f(x)=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

for every
x\in\mathfrak{X}
.
The measure
\mathrm{d}\mu(s)
is called the Plancherel measure and is defined by

\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=c_{P}|c(s)|^{-2},

where
c_{P}=\frac{q\log(q)}{4\pi(q+1)}
and for every
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\tau\mathbb{Z}
we have

c(z)=\frac{q^{1/2}}{q+1}\frac{q^{1/2+iz}-q^{-1/2-iz}}{q^{iz}-q^{-iz}}
.

Moreover the following version of Plancherel formula holds:
if
f
and
g
are two finitely supported functions on
\mathfrak{X},
then

\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}f(x)\overline{g(x)}=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\overline{\mathcal{H}g(\omega,s)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

Note that the Fourier-Helgason transform
\mathcal{H}
can be extended to an isometry from
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
to its image in
L^{2}(\Omega\times\mathbb{T})
.
Now we are in a position to define pseudo-differential operator on homogeneous tree using the inversion formula.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\sigma:\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function. Then for every finitely supported function
f
on
\mathfrak{X}
, we define
T_{\sigma}f
formally by

\displaystyle T_{\sigma}f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathcal{H}{f}(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s),~x\in\mathfrak{X}.

We call
T_{\sigma}
the pseudo-differential operator on
\mathfrak{X}
corresponding to the symbol
\sigma
.

For more details results related to pseudo differential operator on homogeneous tree and their symbolic calculus we refer
[
35
]
.

3.
Boundedness

This section is devoted to study boundedness of pseudo differential operators on
\mathfrak{X}
. We also study
r
-Schatten-von Neumann class of pseudo-differential operators on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is a complex Hilbert space, a linear compact operator
A:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
belongs to the
r
-Schatten-von Neumann class
S_{r}(\mathcal{H})
if

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(s_{n}(A)\right)^{r}<\infty,

where
s_{n}(A)
denote the singular values of
A,
i.e. the eigenvalues of
|A|=\sqrt{A^{*}A}

with multiplicities counted.

For
1\leq r<\infty
, the class
S_{r}(\mathcal{H})
is a Banach space
endowed with the norm

\|A\|_{S_{r}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(s_{n}(A)\right)^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

For
0<r<1
, the
\|\cdot\|_{S_{r}}
as above only defines a quasi-norm with respect to which

S_{r}(\mathcal{H})
is complete. An operator belongs to the class
S_{1}(\mathcal{H})
is known as
Trace class
operator. Also, an operator belongs to
S_{2}(\mathcal{H})
is known as
Hilbert-Schmidt
operator.

We begin this section by obtaining a necessary and sufficient condition on symbols for which these operators are in the class of Hilbert-Schmidt operators. Indeed we have the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\sigma:\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function and let
b:\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

be the measurable function defined by

b(x,\omega,s)=\overline{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)},\quad(x,\omega,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau].

Then the pseudo differential operator
T_{\sigma}:L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator if and only
if
b\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau])
. Moreover

\left\|T_{\sigma}\right\|_{s_{2}}=\|b\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau])}.

Proof.
For
x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}
, define the function
f_{x_{0}}
on
\mathfrak{X}
by

f_{x_{0}}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{0}&{\text{if }x_{0}\neq y,}\\
{1}&{\text{if }x_{0}=y.}\end{array}\right.

Then
\{f_{x_{0}}\}_{x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}}
be the standard orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X}).
For
x\in\mathfrak{X}
we have

\displaystyle(T_{\sigma}f_{x_{0}})(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)~\mathcal{H}f_{x_{0}}(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)~f_{x_{0}}(y)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x_{0})}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\overline{\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x_{0})}\left[q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}\overline{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}\right]\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)}

\displaystyle=\overline{\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x_{0})}b(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)}

\displaystyle=\overline{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}b(x,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(x_{0})},

where
b(x,\omega,s)=q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}\overline{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}
.
Using Plancherel formula we get

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\sigma}\right\|_{S_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}}\left\|T_{\sigma}f_{x_{0}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|\left(T_{\sigma}f_{x_{0}}\right)(x)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}b(x,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(x_{0})}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}b(x,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(x_{0})}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left\|{\mathcal{H}^{-1}b(x,\cdot,\cdot)}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|b(x,\omega,s)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\|b\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau])}^{2}.

∎

The following lemma is a consequence of the definition of Schatten classes (see
[
19
]
) which is needed to obtain our results,
r
-Schatten-von Neumann class of pseudo-differential operators on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X}).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a linear compact operator. Let
0<r,t<\infty.
Then

A\in S_{r}(\mathcal{H})
if and only if
|A|^{\frac{r}{t}}\in S_{t}(\mathcal{H}).
Moreover,
\|A\|_{S_{r}}^{r}=\||A|^{\frac{r}{t}}\|_{S_{t}}^{t}
.

Using the above lemma, we have a characterization of a pseudo-differential operator on
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
to be a Schatten class operator. The proof follows from Lemma
3.2
with
t=2
and Theorem
3.1
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
T:L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
be a continuous linear operator with symbol
\sigma
on
\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau].
Then
T\in S_{r}\left(L^{2}(G/H)\right)
if and only if

\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|\sigma_{1}(x,\omega,s)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)<\infty,

where
\sigma_{1}(x,\omega,s)
is the symbol of the operator
|T|^{\frac{r}{2}}
.

The next theorem gives a simple sufficient condition on the symbols
\sigma
such that the corresponding pseudo differential operators
T_{\sigma}:L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
is bounded. A sufficient condition in terms of symbol class is already given in
[
35
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\sigma:\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function such that there
exist a function
v\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
and a positive constant
C
for which

|q^{\frac{1}{2}h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)|\leq C|v(x)|,\quad x\in\mathfrak{X},

and almost all
(\omega,s)
in
\Omega\times[0,\tau]
. Then
T_{\sigma}:L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
is a bounded linear operator.
Moreover,

\left\|T_{\sigma}\right\|_{B(L^{2}(\mathfrak{X}))}\leq C^{\prime}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})}

for some constant
C^{\prime}.

Proof.
Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{X}).
Then using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Plancherel formula

\displaystyle\left\|T_{\sigma}f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|T_{\sigma}f(x)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)~\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\right|^{2}|\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left|q^{\frac{1}{2}h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\right|^{2}|\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}C^{2}\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|v(x)\right|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}|\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=C^{\prime 2}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})}^{2}

and the proof is complete.
∎

Since a Hilbert-Schmidt operator is a compact operator, using Theorem
3.1
we have the following result.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\sigma:\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function and let

Let

, then the pseudo differential operator

is a compact operator.

4.
Weak type
L^{p}
-estimate

In this section, we study the relation between weak continuity and continuity of a linear
operator defined on
L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
. In this context, we begin with the definition of
(p,q)
-weak continuity for general measure spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\mu)
and
(Y,\nu)
be two measure spaces and let
T
be a linear operator
from
L^{p}(X)
into the space of measurable functions from
Y
to
\mathbb{C}
. Then
T
is said to be a weak type
(p,q)
or
(p,q)
-weak operator,
1\leq q<\infty
, if there exists
C>0
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\nu(\{y\in Y:|Tf(y)|>\lambda\})\leq\left(C\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}}{\lambda}\right)^{q},~\quad\text{for all $\lambda>0$}

i.e.
T
maps
L^{p}(X,\mu)
boundedly into
L^{q,\infty}(Y,\nu)
.

We say that
T
is of strong type
(p,q)
if it is bounded from
L^{p}(X,\mu)
to
L^{q}(Y,\rho).
Let us also remind that if
(X,\mu)
is a measure space and
f:X\rightarrow C
is a measurable function on
X,

then

\|f\|_{L^{p}(X)}^{p}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda^{p-1}m(\lambda)d\lambda

for all
0<p<\infty,
where
m(\lambda)=\mu(\{x\in X:|f(x)|>\lambda\})
is the distribution function of
f
. From onwards
\vartheta
will stands for the counting measure defined on
\mathfrak{X}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let T be a linear operator from
L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
into the space of functions from
\mathfrak{X}
to
\mathbb{C}
. If
T
is a
(p,q)
-weak operator,
p>q\geq 1
, then

T:L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\rightarrow L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)

is bounded. Moreover

\|T\|_{B(L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right))}\leq 2^{\frac{p-q}{p}}C\left(\frac{p}{p-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.
Let
p>q\geq 1
. Let us assume that
T
is a
(p,q)
-weak operator. Then from (
4.1
),

\vartheta(\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:|Tf(x)|>\lambda\})\leq\left(C\frac{\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})}}{\lambda}\right)^{q}

for all
\lambda>0
and some
C>0.
Let
f\in L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)}=1.
Using the definition

\displaystyle\|Tf\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)}^{p}

\displaystyle=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\vartheta(\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:|(Tf)(x)|>\lambda\})\lambda^{p-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle\leq pC^{q}\int_{0}^{2C}\lambda^{p-q-1}d\lambda

\displaystyle=pC^{q}\frac{(2C)^{p-q}}{p-q}.

Therefore,

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)}\leq 2^{\frac{p-q}{p}}C\left(\frac{p}{p-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Thus for an arbitrary
f\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
with
\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)}\neq 0
, we get

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)}\leq 2^{\frac{p-q}{p}}C\left(\frac{p}{p-q}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)}.

∎

An immediate consequence of Theorem
4.2
gives boundedness of pseudo differential operator from
L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
into
L^{p}(\mathfrak{X}),2<p<\infty
. Indeed, we have the following result.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
2<p<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Let
\sigma
be a measurable function on
\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
satisfying

\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right)_{(x,y)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\mathfrak{X}}\in L^{q}\left(\mathfrak{X}\times\mathfrak{X}\right).

Then the corresponding pseudo differential operator
T_{\sigma}:L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\rightarrow L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
is bounded operator.

Proof.
Let
2<p<\infty
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
. Let
f\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{X})
. Using by Hölder’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\left|\left(T_{\sigma}f\right)(x)\right|^{q}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}f(x,\omega,s)\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right|^{q}

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\left(\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}f(y)\right)~\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right||f(y)|\right)^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right|^{q}\right)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})}^{q}

for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}.
Thus

\displaystyle\|T_{\sigma}f\|_{L^{q}(\mathfrak{X})}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right|^{q}\right)\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})}^{q}.

Hence
T_{\sigma}:L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\rightarrow L^{q}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
is a bounded operator. Moreover

\displaystyle\|T_{\sigma}\|_{B(L^{p}(\mathfrak{X}),L^{q}(\mathfrak{X}))}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Since
T_{\sigma}
is a bounded operator it follows that
T_{\sigma}
is a
(p,q)
-weak operator. By Theorem
4.2
and a standard density argument
T_{\sigma}:L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\rightarrow L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
bounded for
2<p<\infty
.
∎

The following theorem is a special case of Theorem
4.2
; however, to emphasize the definition of weak continuity, we provide proof for this theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let T be a linear operator from
L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
into the space of functions from
\mathfrak{X}
to
\mathbb{C}
. If
T
is a
(p,q)
-weak operator for
q\geq 1
, then

T:L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\rightarrow L^{r}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)

is a bounded operator for all
r>q+1
. Moreover

\|T\|_{B\left(L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right),L^{r}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\right)}\leq C^{\frac{q}{r}}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{k^{q}}{(k-1)^{r}}+(2C)^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Proof.
Let
f\in L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)}=1
and let
r>q+1
. Since
T
is a
(p,q)
-weak operator, so

\vartheta(\left\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:|(Tf)(x)|>\lambda\right\})\leq\left(\frac{C}{\lambda}\right)^{q}

for some
C>0
and for all
\lambda>0.
Observe that

\vartheta(\left\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:\left|\left(Tf\right)(x)\right|>2C\right\})\leq\frac{1}{2^{q}}<1,

so
\vartheta(\left\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:\left|\left(Tf\right)(x)\right|>2C\right\})=0,
which implies that
\left|T\left(f\right)(x)\right|\leq 2C
for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}=

\displaystyle\left\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:\left|\left(Tf\right)(x)\right|\leq 1\right\}\cup\left\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:\left|\left(Tf\right)(x)\right|>1\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{k=2}^{\infty}\left\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:\frac{1}{k}<\left|\left(Tf\right)(x)\right|\leq\frac{1}{k-1}\right\}\cup\left\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:1<\left|\left(Tf\right)(x)\right|\leq 2C\right\}

for all
f\in L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
such that
\|f\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)}=1.

Let us denote

A_{k}=\left\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:\frac{1}{k}<\left|\left(Tf\right)(x)\right|\leq\frac{1}{k-1}\right\},k\geq 2

and

B=\left\{x\in\mathfrak{X}:1<\left|\left(Tf\right)(x)\right|\leq 2C\right\}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}|(Tf)(x)|^{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\sum_{x\in\cup A_{k}}|(Tf)(x)|^{r}+\sum_{x\in B}|(Tf)(x)|^{r}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k-1)^{r}}\vartheta(A_{k})+(2C)^{r}\vartheta(B)

\displaystyle\leq C^{q}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{k^{q}}{(k-1)^{r}}+(2C)^{r}\right)

Since
r>q+1
, the series
\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{k^{q}}{(k-1)^{r}}
is converent. Thus for
f\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
we have

\|Tf\|_{L^{r}(\mathfrak{X})}\leq C^{\frac{q}{r}}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{k^{q}}{(k-1)^{r}}+(2C)^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})}.

∎

5.
Characterization of nuclear operators

We first recall the basic notions of nuclear operators on Banach spaces.
Let
T
be a bounded linear operator from a complex Banach space
X
into
another complex Banach space
Y
such that there exist sequences
\left\{x_{n}^{\prime}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
the dual space
X^{\prime}
of
X
and
\left\{y_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
Y
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|x_{n}^{\prime}\right\|_{X^{\prime}}\left\|y_{n}\right\|_{Y}<\infty

and

Tx=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{\prime}(x)y_{n},\quad x\in X.

Then we call
T:X\rightarrow Y
a nuclear operator and if
X=Y,
then its nuclear trace tr
(T)

is given by

\operatorname{tr}(T)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{\prime}\left(y_{n}\right).

It can be proved that the definition of a nuclear operator and the definition of
the trace of a nuclear operator are independent of the choices of the sequences

\left\{x_{n}^{\prime}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\left\{y_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}.

The following theorem proved by Delgado
[
15
]
present a characterization of nuclear operators in term of the conditions on the kernel of the operator.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\left(X_{1},\mu_{1}\right)
and
\left(X_{2},\mu_{2}\right)
be two
\sigma
-finite measure spaces. Then
a

bounded linear operator
T:L^{p_{1}}\left(X_{1},\mu_{1}\right)\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}\left(X_{2},\mu_{2}\right),1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty,
is
nuclear if and only if there exist sequences
\left\{g_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
L^{p_{1}}\left(X_{1},\mu_{1}\right)
and
\left\{h_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

in
L^{p_{2}}\left(X_{2},\mu_{2}\right)
such that for all
f\in L^{p_{1}}\left(X_{1},\mu_{1}\right)

(Tf)(x)=\int_{X_{1}}K(x,y)f(y)d\mu_{1}(y),\quad x\in X_{2},

where

K(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}(x)g_{n}(y),\quad x\in X_{2},y\in X_{1}

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{L^{{p_{1}}^{\prime}}\left(X_{1},\mu_{1}\right)}\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}\left(X_{2},\mu_{2}\right)}<\infty.

The function
K
on
X_{2}\times X_{1}
in Theorem
5.1
is called the kernel of the
nuclear operator
T:L^{p_{1}}\left(X_{1},\mu_{1}\right)\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}\left(X_{2},\mu_{2}\right).

Let
(X,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space. Let
T:L^{p}(X,\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(X,\mu)

1\leq p<\infty,
be a nuclear operator. Then by Theorem
5.1
, we can find sequences

\left\{g_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(X,\mu)
and
\left\{h_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
L^{p}(X,\mu)
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X,\mu)}\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}<\infty

and for all
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)

\displaystyle(Tf)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{X}K(x,y)f(y)d\mu(y),\quad x\in X,

where

K(x,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}h_{n}(x)g_{n}(y),\quad x,y\in X

and it satisfies

\int_{X}|K(x,y)|~d\mu(y)\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(X,\mu)}\left\|h_{n}\right\|_{L^{p}(X,\mu)}.

The trace
\operatorname{tr}(T)
of
T:L^{p}(X,\mu)\rightarrow L^{p}(X,\mu)
is given by

(5.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{tr}(T)=\int_{X}K(x,x)d\mu(x).

In the next theorem we give a sufficient and necessary condition on the symbol
\sigma
such that the corresponding pseudo-differential operator from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})
into
L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
is nuclear for
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty.
Indeed we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\sigma:\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function. Then the pseudo differential operator
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
is nuclear for
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
if and only if there exist sequences
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})

and
\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
such that

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty

and for every
(x,w,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
symbol has the decomposition

\sigma(x,\omega,s)=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)\overline{\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega,s)}.

Proof.
Suppose that
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
is nuclear, where
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty.
Then by Theorem
5.1
, there exist sequences
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})
and
\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
such that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty

and for all
f\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X}),
we have

\displaystyle(T_{\sigma}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}f(x,\omega,s)\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)~f(y)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right)f(y)

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)g_{k}(y)f(y).

Let
x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}
and the function
f
is given by

f(y)=f_{x_{0}}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{0}&{\text{if }x_{0}\neq y,}\\
{1}&{\text{if }x_{0}=y.}\end{array}\right.

Then from (
5
) we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)g_{k}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x_{0})}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\overline{\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x_{0})}\left[q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}\overline{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}\right]\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\overline{\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x_{0})}b(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)},

where
b(x,\omega,s)=q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}\overline{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}

and so

(5.4)

\displaystyle\overline{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}b(x,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(x_{0})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)g_{k}(x_{0}).

Now,

\displaystyle q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)

\displaystyle=\overline{b(x,\omega,s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x_{0})}\overline{\mathcal{H}^{-1}b(x,\cdot,\cdot)(x_{0})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)g_{k}(x_{0})\right)q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x_{0})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)\left(\sum_{x_{0}\in\mathfrak{X}}g_{k}(x_{0})q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x_{0})}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)\overline{\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega,s)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma(x,\omega,s)=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)\overline{\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega,s)},~~(x,\omega,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau].

Conversely, suppose that there exist sequences
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})
and
\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

in
L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
such that

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty

and for every
(x,w,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]

\sigma(x,\omega,s)=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)\overline{\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega,s)}.

Then, for all
f\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})

\displaystyle(T_{\sigma}f)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)~\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)\overline{\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega,s)}\right)\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}{g_{k}(y)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(y)}\right)\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x){g_{k}(y)}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(y)}\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x){g_{k}(y)}f(y)

for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}.
Therefore by Theorem
5.1
,
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
is a nuclear operator.
∎

In order to find the trace of a nuclear operator from
L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
into
L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
, in the next theorem, we give another characterization of nuclear operator from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})
into
L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X}),1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\sigma
be a measurable function on
\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
. Then the pseudo differential operator
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
is nuclear for
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
if and only if there exist sequences
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})

and
\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
such that

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty

and for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{X}
we have

\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)~\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x){g_{k}(y)}.

Proof.
Let
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
is nuclear operator for
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
. By Theorem
5.1
and equation (
5
) there exist sequences
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})

and
\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
such that

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty

and for every
x,y\in\mathfrak{X}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)g_{k}(y)=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

Conversely, suppose there exist sequences
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})

and
\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
such that

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty

and for every
x,y\in\mathfrak{X}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)g_{k}(y)=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

Now, for all
f\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})

\displaystyle\left(T_{\sigma}f\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)~\mathcal{H}f(\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)~f(y)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)g_{k}(y)f(y)

for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}.
Therefore by Theorem
5.1
,
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
is a nuclear operator.
∎

An immediate consequence of Theorem
5.3
gives the trace of a nuclear of pseudo differential operator from
L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
into
L^{p}(\mathfrak{X}),1\leq p<\infty
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Let
T_{\sigma}:L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
be a nuclear operator for
1\leq p<\infty
.
Then the nuclear trace
\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{\sigma}\right)
of
T_{\sigma}
is given by

\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{\sigma}\right)=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

Moreover,

\left\{\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{h_{\omega}(\cdot)}\sigma(\cdot,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right\}\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{X}).

Proof.
Using trace formula (
5.1
) and Theorem
5.3
we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}\left(T_{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)g_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

Using Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{h_{\omega}(x)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left|\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)g_{k}(x)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty

and this implies that

\left\{\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{h_{\omega}(\cdot)}\sigma(\cdot,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right\}\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{X}).

∎

5.1.
Adjoints

Let
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X}),1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty,
be a nuclear operator. In this sub-section we show that the adjoint operator
T_{\sigma}^{*}:L^{p_{2}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})
of
T_{\sigma}
is also nuclear. We also present a necessary and sufficient condition on the symbol
{\sigma}
so
that the corresponding muclear operator
T_{\sigma}
from
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
into
L^{2}(\mathfrak{X})
to be self-adjoint.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\sigma
be a measurable function on
\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
. Let
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
is nuclear for
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
. Then
T_{\sigma}^{*}
, the adjoint of
T_{\sigma},
is also a nuclear operator from
L^{p_{2}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})
into
L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})
with symbol
\sigma^{*}
given by

{\sigma^{*}(x,\omega,s)}=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{H}f_{k}(\omega,s)}~\overline{g_{k}(x)},\quad(x,\omega,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]

where
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
and
\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
are two sequences in
L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})

and
L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
respectively such that

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|h_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty.

Proof.
For all
f\in L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})
and
g\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
, we have

\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}(T_{\sigma}f)(x)\overline{g(x)}=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}f(x)\overline{T_{\sigma}^{*}g(x)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)f(y)\right)\overline{g(x)}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}f(x)\overline{\left(\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma^{*}(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)g(y)\right)}.

For all
a,b\in\mathfrak{X}
and for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
,
f
and
g
is defined by

f(x)=f_{a}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{0}&{\text{if }a\neq x,}\\
{1}&{\text{if }a=x,}\end{array}\right.\quad g(x)=g_{b}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{0}&{\text{if }b\neq x,}\\
{1}&{\text{if }b=x.}\end{array}\right.

Therefore from (
5.1
) we have,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(a)}[q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(b)}\sigma(b,\omega,s)]\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\overline{\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}[q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(a)}\sigma^{*}(a,\omega,s)]\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)}

and thus for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{X}
,

(5.6)

\displaystyle\overline{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}(b,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(a)}={\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(a,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(b)},

where
\sigma^{\prime}(b,\omega,s)=q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}\overline{\sigma(b,\omega,s)}
and
\sigma^{\prime\prime}(a,\omega,s)=q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(a)}\overline{\sigma^{*}(a,\omega,s)}
.
Since
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
is nuclear, from Theorem
5.2
, there exist sequences
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})

and
\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty
and for every
(x,w,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
symbol has the decomposition

\sigma(x,\omega,s)=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)\overline{\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega,s)}.

Using the relation (
5.6
) and the nuclearity of
T_{\sigma}
, we have

\displaystyle q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(a)}\overline{\sigma^{*}(a,\omega,s)}

\displaystyle=\sigma^{\prime\prime}(a,\omega,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\sigma^{\prime\prime}(a,\cdot,\cdot))(b)

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}\overline{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}(b,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(a)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(a)}\overline{\sigma^{\prime}(b,\omega_{1},s_{1})}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega_{1})\mathrm{d}\mu(s_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(a)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(b)}\sigma(b,\omega_{1},s_{1})\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega_{1})\mathrm{d}\mu(s_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(a)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(b)\overline{\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega_{1},s_{1})}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega_{1})\mathrm{d}\mu(s_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(b)\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(a)}\overline{\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega_{1},s_{1})}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega_{1})\mathrm{d}\mu(s_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(b)g_{k}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{b\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}f_{k}(a)g_{k}(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}f_{k}(\omega,s)g_{k}(a).

Therefore, for every
(x,w,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]

{\sigma^{*}(x,\omega,s)}=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{\mathcal{H}f_{k}(\omega,s)}~\overline{g_{k}(x)}.

∎

As a consequence of the above theorem and help of Theorem
5.2
, we can give a criterion in terms of symbol such that the corresponding pseudo differential operator on
L^{2}
to be self-adjoint.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
\sigma
be a measurable function on
\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
such that

and

Theorem 5.5
.

\sigma

Proof.
Let
T_{\sigma}:L^{p_{1}}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
be a nuclear operator for
1\leq p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
. Then by Theorem
5.2
there exist sequences
\left\{g_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})

and
\left\{f_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})
such that

\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{X})}\left\|f_{k}\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathfrak{X})}<\infty

and for every
(x,w,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
symbol has the decomposition

\sigma(x,\omega,s)=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}f_{k}(x)\overline{\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega,s)}.

Let
y\in\mathfrak{X}.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\overline{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(y)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(y)}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{f_{k}(x)}\mathcal{H}\overline{g_{k}}(\omega,s)\right)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{f_{k}(x)}~\overline{g_{k}(y)}.

Thus, using Theorem
5.4
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(x)}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\overline{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(y)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(x)}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{f_{k}(x)}~\overline{g_{k}(y)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\overline{(\mathcal{H}f_{k})(\omega_{1},s_{1})}~\overline{g_{k}(y)}={\sigma^{*}(y,\omega_{1},s_{1})}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(y)}.

we get

\displaystyle{\sigma^{*}(y,\omega_{1},s_{1})}

\displaystyle=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(y)}\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is_{1})h_{\omega_{1}}(x)}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}\overline{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(y)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s).

∎

Using the above theorem, we have another criterion in terms of symbol such that the corresponding pseudo differential operator to be self-adjoint.

Corollary 5.3
.

\displaystyle{\sigma(y,\omega_{1},s_{1})}

5.2.
Products

In this subsection, we show that the product of a nuclear pseudo-differential operator with a bounded operator is again a nuclear operator, and as a consequence, we give a necessary and sufficient condition on symbol so that the corresponding nuclear pseudo-differential operator to be normal.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
\sigma
and
\eta
be two measurable function on
\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
such that the corresponding pseudo differential operator

is a nuclear operator
T_{\lambda}:L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
with symbol
\lambda
is given by

\lambda(b,\omega,s)=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(b)}\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\eta^{\prime}(x,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(b)},

where
\eta^{\prime}(x,\omega,s)=q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}\overline{\eta^{*}(x,\omega,s)},~(x,\omega,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
.

Proof.
For all
f,g\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{X})
, we have

\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}(T_{\lambda}f)(x)\overline{{g(x)}}=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}(T_{\eta}T_{\sigma}f)(x)\overline{g(x)}=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}(T_{\sigma}f)(x)\overline{T_{\eta^{*}}g(x)}.

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\lambda(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)f(y)\right)\overline{g(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}(T_{\sigma}f)(x)\overline{\left(\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\eta^{*}(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)g(y)\right)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left(\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)f(y)\right)

(5.7)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\overline{\left(\sum_{y\in\mathfrak{X}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(y)}\eta^{*}(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)g(y)\right)}

For all
a,b\in\mathfrak{X}
and for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
,
f
and
g
is defined by

f(y)=f_{a}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{0}&{\text{if }a\neq y,}\\
{1}&{\text{if }a=y.}\end{array}\right.g(y)=g_{b}(y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{0}&{\text{if }b\neq y,}\\
{1}&{\text{if }b=y.}\end{array}\right.

Therefore from (
5.2
) we have,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(b)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(a)}\lambda(b,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(a)}\sigma(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\overline{\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\tau}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}\eta^{*}(x,\omega,s)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\right)}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\lambda^{\prime}(b,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(a)}=\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}(x,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(a)}\overline{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\eta^{\prime}(x,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(b)}

where

\lambda^{\prime}(b,\omega,s)=q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(b)}\overline{\lambda(b,\omega,s)},

\sigma^{\prime}(x,\omega,s)=q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}\overline{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}

and

\eta^{\prime}(x,\omega,s)=q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(x)}\overline{\eta^{*}(x,\omega,s)}.

So,

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}(b,\omega,s)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}+is)h_{\omega}(a)}(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\lambda^{\prime}(b,\cdot,\cdot))(a)

Therefore for all
(b,\omega,s)\in\mathfrak{X}\times\Omega\times[0,\tau]
we get

\lambda(b,\omega,s)=q^{-(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(b)}\sum_{x\in\mathfrak{X}}q^{(\frac{1}{2}-is)h_{\omega}(x)}{\sigma(x,\omega,s)}{\left(\mathcal{H}^{-1}\eta^{\prime}(x,\cdot,\cdot)\right)(b)}.

Since
T_{\sigma}
is nuclear operator and
T_{\eta}
is bounded operator, by applying Theorem
5.2
, the proof will be complete.
∎

\mathfrak{X}

.

Corollary 5.5
.

6.

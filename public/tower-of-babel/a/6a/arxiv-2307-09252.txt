Delocalized eta invariants of the signature operator on G-proper manifolds
Paolo Piazza, Hessel Posthuma, Yanli Song, Xiang Tang

Abstract
. Let G G be a connected, linear real reductive group and let X X be a cocompact G G -proper manifold without boundary. We define delocalized eta invariants associated to a L 2 L^{2} -invertible perturbed Dirac operator D X + A D_{X}+A with A A a suitable smoothing perturbation. We also investigate the case in which D X D_{X} is not invertible but 0 0 is isolated in the L 2 L^{2} -spectrum of D X D_{X} . We prove index formulas relating these delocalized eta invariants to Atiyah-Patodi-Singer delocalized indices on G G -proper manifolds with boundary. In order to achieve this program we give a detailed account of both the large and small time behaviour of the heat-kernel of perturbed Dirac operators, as a map from the positive real line to the algebra of Lafforgue integral operators. We apply these results to the definition of rho-numbers associated to G G -homotopy equivalences between closed G G -proper manifolds and to the study of their bordism properties. We also define delocalized signatures of manifolds with boundary satisfying an invertibility assumption on the differential form Laplacian of the boundary in middle degree and prove an Atiyah-Patodi-Singer formula for these delocalized signatures.

We show first of all that we can lift the perturbation

Let us consider the first property.

To this end we recall a classic property of eta invariants of Dirac operators on even dimensional manifolds, namely that they vanish.
Assume that

7.1.
Delocalized eta invariants associated to the gap case

7.1 Definition
.

For any
\Theta>0
, let us introduce the perturbed Dirac operator

D_{\Theta}=D+\Theta.

Let
\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}
be the smoothing kernel of the following operator

D_{\Theta}\cdot\exp(-tD^{2}_{\Theta}).

For any small number

, we split the integral

(7.2)

\begin{split}\int_{s}^{\infty}\tau^{X}_{g}(D_{\Theta}\exp(-tD_{\Theta}^{2})\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}=&\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{X}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\\
=&\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\\
+&\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{N^{c}(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}},\end{split}

where

is a properly chosen

-neighborhood of the fixed point submanifold
X^{g}
in

, c.f.
[
28
, Proposition 5.27 and Lemma 5.30]
.

Note that the integral

\int_{s}^{\infty}\tau^{X}_{g}(D_{\Theta}\exp(-tD_{\Theta}^{2})\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

might be divergent as
s\to 0
. The reason is that
D_{\Theta}
is no longer associated to a Clifford connection and thus

\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

will in general tend to infinity as

.

7.3 Proposition
.

For the terms in the righthand side of (
7.2
), the following holds

(1)

The integrand of the first summand has the asymptotic expansion
[
41
, Section 2]
:

\int_{N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\sim t^{-\frac{\dim X+1}{2}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}(r)\cdot t^{i}\right)

We define

A(r)\colon={\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

as the coefficient of

in this asymptotic expansion.

(2)

The integral

\int_{N^{c}(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy

is of exponential decay as
t\to 0
. And thus

\begin{split}B(r)\colon=&\lim_{s\to 0}\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{N^{c}(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\\
=&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{N^{c}(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\end{split}

is well defined.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from the similar arguments as in
[

, Lemma 5.28]
via asymptotic expansion
[

, Section 2]

[

∎

7.4 Definition
.

For the perturbed Dirac operator
D_{\Theta}
, we define the
regularized eta invariant
as

(7.5)

\begin{split}\eta_{g}(\Theta)=&{\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{s}^{\infty}\tau^{X}_{g}(D_{\Theta}\exp(-tD_{\Theta}^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\\
=&A(r)+B(r).\end{split}

7.6 Lemma
.

The regularized eta invariant
\eta_{g}(\Theta)
is independent of the choice of
r
.

Proof.

Suppose that
r^{\prime}>r>0
are two small constants. We want to show that

A(r)+B(r)=A(r^{\prime})+B(r^{\prime}).

By definition, we have

\begin{split}A(r^{\prime})=&{\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{N(r^{\prime})}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\\
=&{\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\\
+&{\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{N(r^{\prime})\setminus N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.\end{split}

Because

\int_{N(r^{\prime})\setminus N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy

is of exponential decay as
t\to 0
, the following limit is finite,

\begin{split}&{\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\int_{s}^{\infty}\int_{N(r^{\prime})\setminus N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\\
=&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{N(r^{\prime})\setminus N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}<+\infty.\end{split}

Thus,

A(r^{\prime})=A(r)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{N(r^{\prime})\setminus N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.

On the other hand, we compute

\begin{split}B(r)=&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{N^{c}(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\\
=&B(r^{\prime})+\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{N(r^{\prime})\setminus N(r)}\int_{G}c_{G}(h)c(hy){\rm tr}(\kappa^{\Theta}_{t}(y,gy)g)\;dhdy\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.\end{split}

This shows that

A(r)+B(r)=A(r^{\prime})+B(r^{\prime}).

∎

7.7 Lemma
.

If
0
is isolated in the
L^{2}
-spectrum of
D
, then

(1)

the delocalized eta invariant

\eta_{g}(D)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{X}_{g}(D\exp(-tD^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

is well defined;

(2)

the projection operator
\Pi_{\ker D}\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{G,s}(X)
and
\tau^{X}_{g}(\Pi_{\ker D})
is finite.

Proof.

We are assuming that
D
, and thus
D^{2}
, has a gap at
0
. Then there exists
\sigma>0
such that
{\rm spec}_{L^{2}}(D^{2})\cap[-2\sigma,2\sigma]=\{0\}
.
Let
\gamma

{\rm Im}z=\pm m{\rm Re}z
,
m={\rm tg}\phi>0
,
m
small, for
|z|>\sigma
and of
the portion of the circle of radius
\sigma
joining the points

\{\sigma\cos\phi+i\sigma\sin\phi,\sigma\cos\phi-i\sigma\sin\phi\}

and passing through the
point
-\sigma\equiv-\sigma+i0
in the negative real axis. We know that

\exp(-tD^{2})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}e^{-t\lambda}(D^{2}-\lambda)^{2}d\lambda\,.

Consider now
\gamma_{\sigma}
, the path obtained by taking the union of the two straight lines
{\rm Im}z=\pm m{\rm Re}z
,
m={\rm tg}\phi>0
for
|z|>\sigma
and
of the portion of the circle of radius
\sigma
joining the points

and passing through the
point
\sigma\equiv\sigma+i0
in the positive real axis. If we denote by

the circle of radius

\exp(-tD^{2})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{\sigma}}e^{-t\lambda}(D^{2}-\lambda)^{2}d\lambda+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{\sigma}}e^{-t\lambda}(D^{2}-\lambda)^{2}d\lambda.

See
[
32
]
. Thus

(7.8)

\exp(-tD^{2})=\Pi_{\ker D}+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma_{\sigma}}e^{-t\lambda}(D^{2}-\lambda)^{2}d\lambda.

This establishes that

\Pi_{\ker D}\,,\text{ which is equal to }\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{\sigma}}e^{-t\lambda}(D^{2}-\lambda)^{2}d\lambda\,,\text{ is in }\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{G,s}(M)

[

\exp(-tD^{2})\to\Pi_{\ker D}

\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{G,s}(M)
and
exponentially, as

In particular

\int_{1}^{\infty}\tau^{X}_{g}(D\exp(-tD^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

∎

7.9 Proposition
.

Let
(X,\mathbf{h})
be an odd dimensional
G
-proper manifold and let

D
be a
G
-equivariant Dirac operator.
If
0
is isolated in the
L^{2}
-spectrum of
D
and
\Theta>0
is not in the
L^{2}
-spectrum of

then

(7.10)

\eta_{g}(\Theta):={\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{s}^{\infty}\tau^{N}_{g}((D_{\Theta})\exp(-t(D+\Theta)^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

is well-defined. Moreover if
\Theta>0
belongs to the gap around

, then

\lim_{\Theta\downarrow 0}\eta_{g}(\Theta)
exists and we have

(7.11)

\lim_{\Theta\downarrow 0}\eta_{g}(\Theta)=\eta_{g}(D)+\tau^{X}_{g}(\Pi_{\ker D}).

Proof.

We shall adapt to the present context an argument due to Melrose, see
[
23
, Prop. 8.38]
. Let us break

\eta_{g}(\Theta)={\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{s}^{1}\tau^{X}_{g}((D_{\Theta})\exp(-sD_{\Theta}^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{1}^{\infty}\tau^{X}_{g}((D_{\Theta})\exp(-sD_{\Theta}^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.

Because
D_{\Theta}
is
L^{2}
-invertible, we know that the second summand is finite. For the first summand, we need to take the regularized limit,
{\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}
, because
D_{\Theta}
is a Dirac-type operator but it is not associated to a Clifford connection. In particular,
\eta_{g}(\Theta):=\eta_{g}(D_{\Theta})
.

Recall that the heat kernel and its asymptotic expansion for small time, are
C^{1}
in
\Theta
, for finite times. Thus, we have that.

(7.12)

\lim_{\Theta\downarrow 0}{\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{s}^{1}\tau^{X}_{g}(D_{\Theta}\exp(-tD_{\Theta}^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}={\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{s}^{1}\tau^{X}_{g}(D\exp(-tD^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}.

Again by
[
28
, Proposition 5.29]
, the righthand side is equal to

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{1}\tau^{X}_{g}(D\exp(-tD^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}<+\infty.

On the other hand, by the tracial property of
\tau_{g}^{X}
and Duhamel’s principle, we compute that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\Theta}\left(\int_{1}^{T}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(D_{\Theta}\exp(-tD_{\Theta}^{2})\right)\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\tau^{X}_{g}\left[\left(1-2t\cdot D_{\Theta}^{2}\right)\exp(-tD_{\Theta}^{2})\right]\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2T^{1/2}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\exp(-TD_{\Theta}^{2})\right)-2\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\exp(-D_{\Theta}^{2})\right).

Changing
\Theta
in
\phi
and integrating in
\phi
, from
\phi=0
to
\phi=\Theta
, we obtain:

(7.13)

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(D_{\Theta}\exp(-tD_{\Theta}^{2})\right)\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{T}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(D\exp(-tD^{2})\right)\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}+2\int_{0}^{\Theta}T^{1/2}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\exp(-TD_{\phi}^{2})\right)d\phi-2\int_{0}^{\Theta}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\exp(-D_{\phi}^{2})\right)d\phi.

We want to take the limit as
T\to+\infty
and then
\Theta\downarrow 0
. By Lemma
7.7
the first summand on the righthand side converges to

\int_{1}^{\infty}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(D\exp(-tD^{2})\right)\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}<+\infty,

and it is obviously independent of
\Theta
.
It is clear that

\lim_{\Theta\downarrow 0}\int_{0}^{\Theta}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\exp(-D_{\phi}^{2})\right)d\phi=0.

The difficult term is

\lim_{\Theta\downarrow 0}\lim_{T\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{\Theta}T^{1/2}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\exp(-TD_{\phi}^{2})\right)d\phi.

Note that
D_{\phi}
is a lower order invertible perturbation of
D
. For
\phi>0
small,
write

\exp(-TD_{\phi}^{2})=\exp\left(-T(D^{2}+2\phi D)\right)\exp(-T\phi^{2}).

Here the operator
D^{2}+2\phi D
is a generalized laplacian. Because
0
is isolated in the spectrum of
D
, we can choose
\Theta
small enough such that for each
\phi\in[0,\Theta]
,
0
is also isolated in the spectrum of
D^{2}+2\phi D
. In particular,

\ker(D^{2}+2\phi D)=\ker(D).

By the decomposition of the heat kernel,

(7.14)

\exp\left(-T(D^{2}+2\phi D)\right)=\Pi_{\ker D}+R(T,\phi)

with

going to 0 exponentially in

. We conclude that

(7.15)

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{\Theta}T^{1/2}\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\exp(-TD_{\phi}^{2})\right)d\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{\Theta}T^{1/2}e^{-T\phi^{2}}\cdot\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\exp\left(-T(D^{2}+2\phi D)\right)\right)d\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{T\to+\infty}\int_{0}^{\Theta}T^{1/2}e^{-T\phi^{2}}\cdot\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\Pi_{\ker D}\right)d\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\cdot\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\Pi_{\ker D}\right).

Combining the above computation, we conclude that

\lim_{\Theta\downarrow 0}\eta_{g}(\Theta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{X}_{g}((D)\exp(-sD^{2}))\frac{dt}{\sqrt{t}}+\tau^{X}_{g}\left(\Pi_{\ker D}\right)=\eta_{g}(D)+\tau^{X}_{g}(\Pi_{\ker D}).

∎

7.2.
The delocalized APS index theorem in the gap case

We discuss an extension of the 0-degree delocalized APS index theorem to the case in which the

We assume that

Let
D
be the

-Dirac operator on

-th order perturbation of

It is known that

(D^{\pm}_{\Theta})_{\partial}=D_{\partial}+\Theta\,;

\Theta+D_{\partial}

.
This does not depend on
\Theta
, for

.There is a well defined delocalized APS numeric index given by

Proceeding
as before, using in particular the relative index class, excision and the relative cyclic cocycle

\displaystyle\langle\Ind_{\infty}(D_{\Theta}),\tau^{Y}_{g}\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau^{Y,r}_{g}(e^{-s^{2}(D^{-}_{\Theta}D^{+}_{\Theta})})-\tau^{Y,r}_{g}(e^{-s^{2}(D^{+}_{\Theta}D^{-}_{\Theta}))})

Thus, adapting to our context the arguments in Melrose
[

\displaystyle\langle\Ind_{\infty}(D_{\Theta}),\tau^{Y}_{g}\rangle

\displaystyle={\rm LIM}_{s\downarrow 0}\left(\tau^{Y,r}_{g}(e^{-s^{2}(D^{-}_{\Theta}D^{+}_{\Theta})})-\tau^{Y,r}_{g}(e^{-s^{2}(D^{+}_{\Theta}D^{-}_{\Theta})})\right)

See (

\lim_{\Theta\downarrow 0}\eta(\Theta)=\eta_{g}(D_{\partial})+\tau^{\partial Y}_{g}(\Pi_{\ker D_{\partial}}).

G

8.
Large and short time behaviour

8.1.
Large time behaviour on

C_{s,t}>0

\sup_{x,z\in X\times X}||(D_{x}^{s}D_{z}^{t}l)(\lambda,x,z)||\leq\frac{C_{s,t}}{||\lambda||^{2}},

where
D_{x}
and
D_{z}
are partial differentiations along the

and

By (1) and (2), we conclude that the composition

belongs to
\mathcal{L}^{c}_{G}(X)\subset\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{G,s}(X)
. Furthermore, by (3), for any seminorm

on
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{G,s}(X)
, we have the bound
p(KF(\lambda))\leq C_{p}/||\lambda||^{2}
and accordingly the convergent Bochner integral as an element in
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{G,s}(X)
,

\int_{\gamma}KF(\lambda){\rm d}\lambda.

The dominated convergence theorem for the Bochner integral gives that

\lim_{\delta\to 0}p\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}e^{-\delta\lambda}KF(\lambda){\rm d}\lambda-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}KF(\lambda){\rm d}\lambda\right)=0.

Summarizing Part I and II, we conclude that in
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{G,s}(X)
,

\begin{split}\lim_{\delta\to 0}Ke^{-\delta D^{2}}&=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}e^{-\delta\lambda}KF(\lambda){\rm d}\lambda\right)+\lim_{\delta\to 0}\left(\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}e^{-\delta\lambda}KE(\lambda){\rm d}\lambda\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}KF(\lambda){\rm d}\lambda+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}KE(\lambda){\rm d}\lambda\\
&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\gamma}K(F+E)(\lambda){\rm d}\lambda.\end{split}

We conclude that
Ke^{-\delta D^{2}}
is a convergent sequence in
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{G,s}(X)
;
since we have proved that for the
L^{2}
-operator norm we do have

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\|Ke^{-\delta D^{2}}-K\|=0

we conclude that
Ke^{-\delta D^{2}}
converges to
K
in
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{G,s}(X)
∎

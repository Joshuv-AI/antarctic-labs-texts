Combinatorial curvature flows for generalized circle packings on surfaces with boundary
Xu Xu, Chao Zheng

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the deformation of generalized circle packings on ideally triangulated surfaces with boundary, which is the ( − 1 , − 1 , − 1 ) (-1,-1,-1) type generalized circle packing metric introduced by Guo-Luo [ 16 ] . To find hyperbolic metrics on surfaces with totally geodesic boundaries of prescribed lengths, we introduce combinatorial Ricci flow and combinatorial Calabi flow for generalized circle packings on ideally triangulated surfaces with boundary. Then we prove the longtime existence and global convergence for the solutions of these combinatorial curvature flows, which provide effective algorithms for finding hyperbolic metrics on surfaces with totally geodesic boundaries of prescribed lengths.



Keywords
: Combinatorial Ricci flow; Combinatorial Calabi flow; Circle packings; Hyperbolic metrics; Surfaces with boundary

Suppose
\Sigma
is a compact surface with boundary
\partial\Sigma
consisting of
N
connected components, which are topologically circles.
Let
\widetilde{\Sigma}
be the compact surface obtained by coning off each boundary component of
\Sigma
to be a point,
thus there are exactly
N
cone points
\{v_{1},...,v_{N}\}
in
\widetilde{\Sigma}
so that
\widetilde{\Sigma}-\{v_{1},...,v_{N}\}
is homeomorphic to
\Sigma-\partial\Sigma
.
An ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
of
\Sigma
is a triangulation
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
of
\widetilde{\Sigma}
such that the vertices of the triangulation are exactly the cone points
\{v_{1},...,v_{N}\}
.
The ideal edges and ideal faces of
\Sigma
in the ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
are defined to be the intersection
\widetilde{E}\bigcap\Sigma
and
\widetilde{F}\bigcap\Sigma
, where
\widetilde{E}
and
\widetilde{F}
are the sets of edges and faces in the triangulation
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}
of
\widetilde{\Sigma}
.
The intersection of an ideal face and
\partial\Sigma
are called boundary arcs.
For simplicity, we denote the connected components of the boundary as
B=\{1,2,...,N\}
, and the sets of ideal edges and ideal faces as
E
and
F
respectively.
The ideal edge between two adjacent boundary components
i,j\in B
is denoted by
\{ij\}

and the ideal face adjacent to boundary components
i,j,k\in B
is denoted by
\{ijk\}
.

The edge length function associated to
\mathcal{T}
is a vector
l:E\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
assigning each ideal edge
\{ij\}
a positive number
l_{ij}
.
For an ideal face
\{ijk\}
adjacent to boundary components
i,j,k\in B
, there exists a unique hyperbolic right-angled geodesic hexagon whose three non-pairwise adjacent edges having lengths
l_{ij},l_{jk},l_{ki}
.
Gluing all such geometric hexagons along the edges in pairs by hyperbolic isometries, one can construct a hyperbolic surface with totally geodesic boundary from the ideal triangulation
\mathcal{T}
.
Conversely, any ideally triangulated hyperbolic surface with totally geodesic boundary
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
produces a function
l:E\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
with
l_{ij}
given by the length of the shortest geodesic connecting the boundary components
i,j\in B
.
The edge length function

is called a discrete hyperbolic metric on
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
.
The length
K_{i}
of the boundary component

is called the generalized combinatorial curvature of the discrete hyperbolic metric

at
i\in B
, i.e.,

K_{i}=\sum_{\{ijk\}\in F}\theta^{jk}_{i},

where the summation is taken over all the hyperbolic right-angled hexagons with
i
as a boundary component and
\theta^{jk}_{i}
is the length of the boundary arc of the hyperbolic right-angled hexagon
\{ijk\}\in F
at
i\in B
.

Definition 1.2

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i},\\

Remark 1
.

In the special case of

The combinatorial Ricci flow defined by (

2

Preliminaries on generalized circle packings

Suppose
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
is an ideally triangulated surface with boundary.
Let
\Phi:E\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be the weight on
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
.
The admissible space of generalized circle packings
r
on
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
is defined to be

\mathcal{M}(\Phi)=\bigcap_{\{ij\}\in E}\mathcal{M}(\Phi_{ij}),

(6)

where

\mathcal{M}(\Phi_{ij})=\{(r_{i},r_{j})\in(0,+\infty)^{2}|\ \cosh\Phi_{ij}\sinh r_{i}\sinh r_{j}-\cosh r_{i}\cosh r_{j}>1\}.

(7)

By a change of variables, i.e.,
u_{i}=\log\tanh\frac{r_{i}}{2}
, the admissible space
\mathcal{M}(\Phi)
of generalized circle packings
r
is transferred to the following admissible space
\Omega(\Phi)
of discrete conformal factors
u
on
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})

\Omega(\Phi)=\bigcap_{\{ijk\}\in F}\Omega(\Phi_{ij},\Phi_{jk},\Phi_{ki}),

(8)

where

\Omega(\Phi_{ij},\Phi_{jk},\Phi_{ki})=\{(u_{i},u_{j},u_{k})\in(-\infty,0)^{3}|u_{r}+u_{s}>-\Phi_{rs},\{r,s,t\}=\{i,j,k\}\}.

(9)

The relationship of the admissible space
\mathcal{M}(\Phi)
and
\Omega(\Phi)
can be obtained directly from Lemma 4.2 in Guo-Luo
[
16
]
or derived by calculations in Lemma
4.2
.
It is obvious that the admissible space
\Omega(\Phi_{ij},\Phi_{jk},\Phi_{ki})
is a convex polyhedron.
As a result, the admissible space
\Omega(\Phi)
is a convex polyhedron.

Suppose
\{ijk\}\in F
is a right-angled hyperbolic hexagon adjacent to the boundary components
i,j,k\in B
.
Denote the edge lengths of three nonadjacent edges
\{ij\},\ \{ik\},\ \{jk\}\in E
as
l_{ij},\ l_{ik},\ l_{jk}
respectively and the lengths of the hyperbolic arc in the boundary components

as
\theta_{i}^{jk},\ \theta_{j}^{ik},\ \theta_{k}^{ij}
respectively.

.

(
[

]

Combinatorial curvature flows on surfaces with boundary

In this section, we first study some basic properties of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) and the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
).
Then we give some results on the longtime existence and convergence for the solutions of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) and the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) for initial
data with small energy.

3.1
Two important energy functions

Consider the following two energy functions

\mathcal{E}(u)=-\int_{0}^{u}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i})du_{i}

(10)

and

\mathcal{C}(u)=\frac{1}{2}||K-\overline{K}||^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i})^{2}.

(11)

Combining the fact that the admissible space
\Omega(\Phi)
of discrete conformal factors
u
defined by (
8
) is a convex polyhedron and Lemma
2.1
,
the function
\mathcal{E}(u)
is a well-defined strictly convex function defined on the admissible space
\Omega(\Phi)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) is a negative gradient flow of the function
\mathcal{E}(u)
defined by (
10
).
As a result, the function
\mathcal{E}(u)
defined by (
10
) is decreasing along the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
).
Furthermore, the combinatorial Calabi energy
\mathcal{C}(u)
defined by (
11
) is decreasing along the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
).

Proof.

By direct calculations, we have

\nabla_{u_{i}}\mathcal{E}(u)=-(K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i}),

which implies the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) is a negative gradient flow of the function
\mathcal{E}(u)
. Furthermore,

\frac{d\mathcal{E}(u(t))}{dt}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial u_{i}}\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i})^{2}\leq 0,

which implies that the function
\mathcal{E}(u)
is decreasing along the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
). Similarly,

\frac{d\mathcal{C}(u(t))}{dt}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\mathcal{C}}{\partial K_{i}}\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}\frac{du_{j}}{dt}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}(K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i})\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}(K_{j}-\overline{K}_{j})=(K-\overline{K})^{T}\Delta(K-\overline{K})\leq 0

by Lemma
2.1
, the right side of which is strictly negative unless
K=\overline{K}
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) is a negative gradient flow of the combinatorial Calabi energy
\mathcal{C}(u)
defined by (
11
).
As a result, the combinatorial Calabi energy
\mathcal{C}(u)
defined by (
11
) is decreasing along the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
).
Furthermore, the energy function
\mathcal{E}(u)
defined by (
10
) is decreasing along the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
).

Proof.

By direct calculations, we have

\nabla_{u_{i}}\mathcal{C}(u)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial K_{j}}{\partial u_{i}}(K_{j}-\overline{K}_{j})=\Delta(K-\overline{K})_{i}=-\frac{du_{i}}{dt},

which implies that the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) is a negative gradient flow of the combinatorial Calabi energy
\mathcal{C}(u)
.
Furthermore,

\frac{d\mathcal{C}(u(t))}{dt}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\mathcal{C}}{\partial u_{i}}\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\Delta(K-\overline{K})_{i})^{2}\leq 0,

the right side of which is strictly negative unless
K=\overline{K}
.
Similarly,

\frac{d\mathcal{E}(u(t))}{dt}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}}{\partial u_{i}}\frac{du_{i}}{dt}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(K-\overline{K})_{i}\Delta(K-\overline{K})_{i}=(K-\overline{K})^{T}\Delta(K-\overline{K})\leq 0,

where the last inequality follows from the strictly negative definiteness of the discrete Laplace operator
\Delta=(\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{j}})_{N\times N}
in Lemma
2.1
.
∎

3.2
Local convergence of combinatorial curvature flows

Note that the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) and the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) are ODE systems with smooth coefficients.
Therefore, the solutions always exist locally around the initial time
t=0
by the standard ODE theory.
We further have the following result on the longtime existence and convergence for the solutions of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
)
and the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) with small initial energy.

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
is an ideally triangulated surface with boundary.
Let
\Phi:E\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be the weight on
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
. And
\overline{K}\in(0,+\infty)^{N}
is a given function defined on
B=\{1,2,...,N\}
.
If the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) or the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) converges to
\overline{u}\in\Omega(\Phi)
, then
K(\overline{u})=\overline{K}
.
Furthermore, for any
\overline{K}\in(0,+\infty)^{N}
defined on
B=\{1,2,...,N\}
, there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that if
||K(u(0))-\overline{K}||=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}(K_{i}(u(0))-\overline{K}_{i})^{2}}<\delta
, then the solution of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) (the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) respectively) exists for all time and converges exponentially fast.

Proof.

Suppose
u(t)
is a solution of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
).
If
\overline{u}:=u(+\infty)=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}u(t)
exists in
\Omega(\Phi)
, then
K(\overline{u})=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}K(u(t))
exists by the
C^{1}
-smoothness of
K
.
Furthermore, there exists a sequence
\xi_{n}\in(n,n+1)
such that as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,

u_{i}(n+1)-u_{i}(n)=u^{\prime}_{i}(\xi_{n})=K_{i}(u(\xi_{n}))-\overline{K}_{i}\rightarrow 0,

which implies
K(\overline{u})=\overline{K}
.
Similarly, if the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) converges, then

K(\overline{u})=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}K(u(t))
exists by the
C^{1}
-smoothness of
K
.
Furthermore, there exists a sequence
\xi_{n}\in(n,n+1)
such that as
n\rightarrow+\infty
,

u_{i}(n+1)-u_{i}(n)=u^{\prime}_{i}(\xi_{n})=\Delta(K(u(\xi_{n}))-\overline{K})_{i}\rightarrow 0,

which implies
K(\overline{u})=\overline{K}
by the strictly negative definiteness of the discrete Laplace operator
\Delta
in Lemma
2.1
.

For the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
), set
\Gamma(u)=K-\overline{K}
.
Then

is negative definite by Lemma
2.1
, which implies
\overline{u}
is a local attractor of the combinatorial Ricci flow (

Then the conclusion follows from Lyapunov Stability Theorem (
[
22
]
, Chapter 5).
Similarly, for the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3

\Gamma(u)=-\Delta(K-\overline{K})
.

2.1
, which implies
\overline{u}
is a local attractor of the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
).
Then the conclusion follows from Lyapunov Stability Theorem (
[
22
]
, Chapter 5).
∎

Theorem
3.3
gives the longtime existence and convergence for the solutions of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) and the combinatorial Calabi flow (

) for initial value with small energy.
For general initial value, we can further prove that the solutions of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2

2
) and the combinatorial Calabi flow (

) can not develop singularities.

4
The longtime behavior of combinatorial Ricci flow

In this section, we prove the longtime existence and convergence of the solution of the combinatorial Ricci flow (

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
is an ideally triangulated surface with boundary.
Let
\Phi:E\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be the weight on
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
.
For any
\overline{K}\in(0,+\infty)^{N}
defined on
B=\{1,2,...,N\}
,
the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) stays in a bounded subset of
(-\infty,0)^{N}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.2
, there exists
\overline{u}\in\Omega(\Phi)
such that
K(\overline{u})=\overline{K}
, which implies
\nabla\mathcal{E}(\overline{u})=-(K-\overline{K})|_{u=\overline{u}}=0.

Note that
\mathcal{E}(u)
is a strictly convex function on
\Omega(\Phi)\subseteq(-\infty,0)^{N}
, we have

\lim_{u\rightarrow-\infty}\mathcal{E}(u)=+\infty.

(12)

Note that
\mathcal{E}(u)
is decreasing along the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) by Lemma
3.1
, then

\mathcal{E}(u(t))\leq\mathcal{E}(u(0)).

(13)

Combining (
12
) and (
13
), the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) stays in a bounded subset of
(-\infty,0)^{N}
.
∎

To prove the longtime existence of the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
),
we just need to prove that the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) stays in a compact subset of the admissible space
\Omega(\Phi)
, i.e., the solution
u(t)
can not reach the boundary of the admissible space
\Omega(\Phi)
.
By (
8
) and (
9
), the boundary of the admissible space
\Omega(\Phi)
in
[-\infty,0]^{N}
consists of the following three parts

(1)

\partial_{\infty}\Omega(\Phi)=\{u\in[-\infty,0]^{N}|\ \text{there exists at least}\ i\in B\ \text{such that}\ u_{i}=-\infty\}
,

(2)

\partial_{0}\Omega(\Phi)=\{u\in[-\infty,0]^{N}|\ \text{there exists at least}\ i\in B\ \text{such that}\ u_{i}=0\}
,

(3)

\partial_{l}\Omega(\Phi)=\bigcup_{\{ij\}\in E}\partial_{ij}\Omega(\Phi)=\bigcup_{\{ij\}\in E}\{u\in[-\infty,0]^{N}|u_{i}+u_{j}=-\Phi_{ij}\}
.

Lemma
4.1
shows that the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) can not reach the boundary
\partial_{\infty}\Omega(\Phi)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) can not reach the boundary
\partial_{l}\Omega(\Phi)
.

Proof.

Suppose
\{ijk\}\in F
is a right-angled hyperbolic hexagon adjacent to the boundary components
i,j,k\in B
.
For any fixed
(\Phi_{ij},\Phi_{jk},\Phi_{ki})\in(0,+\infty)^{3}
,
if
u_{i}+u_{j}=-\Phi_{ij}
, by
u_{i}=\log\tanh\frac{r_{i}}{2}
,
we have

\log\tanh\frac{r_{i}}{2}+\log\tanh\frac{r_{j}}{2}=-\Phi_{ij},

which is equivalent to

\tanh\frac{r_{i}}{2}\tanh\frac{r_{j}}{2}=e^{-\Phi_{ij}}.

Hence

\frac{1}{2}(\tanh\frac{r_{i}}{2}\tanh\frac{r_{j}}{2}+\frac{1}{\tanh\frac{r_{i}}{2}\tanh\frac{r_{j}}{2}})=\frac{1}{2}(e^{-\Phi_{ij}}+e^{\Phi_{ij}})=\cosh\Phi_{ij}.

(14)

Note that

\frac{1}{2}(\tanh\frac{r_{i}}{2}\tanh\frac{r_{j}}{2}+\frac{1}{\tanh\frac{r_{i}}{2}\tanh\frac{r_{j}}{2}})=\frac{1+\cosh r_{i}\cosh r_{j}}{\sinh r_{i}\sinh r_{j}},

and we have

\cosh\Phi_{ij}=\frac{\cosh l_{ij}+\cosh r_{i}\cosh r_{j}}{\sinh r_{i}\sinh r_{j}}

by (
1
).
This implies
l_{ij}=0
by (
14
).
Therefore, if
u_{i}+u_{j}=-\Phi_{ij}
, then the edge length
l_{ij}=0
.
Similarly, we can obtain the edge length
l_{ij}>0
is equivalent to
u_{i}+u_{j}>-\Phi_{ij}
,
which shows the equivalence of the admissible space
\mathcal{M}(\Phi)
and
\Omega(\Phi)
.

By the cosine law for a hyperbolic right-angled hexagon, we have

\cosh\theta^{jk}_{i}=\frac{\cosh l_{jk}+\cosh l_{ij}\cosh l_{ik}}{\sinh l_{ij}\sinh l_{ik}}>\frac{\cosh l_{ij}\cosh l_{ik}}{\sinh l_{ij}\sinh l_{ik}}>\frac{\cosh l_{ij}}{\sinh l_{ij}},

which implies that
\theta^{jk}_{i}\rightarrow+\infty
uniformly as
l_{ij}\rightarrow 0
.
Note that
K_{i}=\sum_{\{ijk\}\in F}\theta^{jk}_{i}
and the combinatorial Calabi energy
\mathcal{C}(u)
is decreasing along the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) by Lemma
3.1
, then

\mathcal{C}(u(t))\leq\mathcal{C}(u(0)),

which implies that for any
i\in B
,

|K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i}|
is bounded along the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
).
Since
\overline{K}
is a given function, then

K
is bounded from above along the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
).
As a result,
\theta^{jk}_{i}
can not tend to
+\infty
along the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
), which implies that the edge length
l_{ij}
has a positive lower bound along the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
).
This is equivalent to
u_{i}+u_{j}>-\Phi_{ij}
.
Therefore, the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) can not reach the boundary
\partial_{l}\Omega(\Phi)
.
∎

To prove the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (

[

]

Lemma 4.3
.

(
[
16
]

Suppose
\{ijk\}\in F
is a right-angled hyperbolic hexagon adjacent to the boundary components

.
For any fixed
(\Phi_{ij},\Phi_{jk},\Phi_{ki})\in(0,+\infty)^{3}

\lim_{r_{k}\rightarrow+\infty}\theta^{ij}_{k}(r_{i},r_{j},r_{k})=0

Lemma 4.4
.

The solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Ricci flow (
2
) can not reach the boundary
\partial_{0}\Omega(\Phi)
.

Proof.

The trick is taken from Ge-Xu
[
8
]
.
Suppose that
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}u_{i}(t)=0
for
T\in(0,+\infty]
.
By
u_{i}=\log\tanh\frac{r_{i}}{2}
, we have
\lim_{t\rightarrow T}r_{i}(t)=+\infty
.
By Lemma
4.3
,
\theta^{jk}_{i}\rightarrow 0
uniformly as

.
Therefore, there exists

such that whenever

\epsilon>0
,

Choose a time

Set

Note that

, which contradicts
u_{i}(t_{0})>c
.
Therefore,

is uniformly bounded from above in

of the combinatorial Ricci flow (

\partial_{0}\Omega(\Phi)

As a direct corollary of Lemma

, we have the following result.

Corollary 5.4
.

Suppose
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
is an ideally triangulated surface with boundary.
Let
\Phi:E\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be the weight on
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
.
Let
n
be the degree at the boundary component
i\in B
.
Then for any

, there exists a constant
M=M(C_{1},C_{2},...,C_{n})>0
such that if
r_{i}\geq M
, then

-\sum_{\{ijk\}\in F}\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}>\sum^{n}_{j=1,j\sim i}C_{j}\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
, suppose
\{ijk\}\in F
is a right-angled hyperbolic hexagon adjacent to the boundary components
i,j,k\in B
.
Then for any
C=|C_{j}|\in(0,+\infty)

such that if

-\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}=\bigg|\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}\bigg|>C\bigg(\bigg|\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}\bigg|+\bigg|\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{k}}\bigg|\bigg)\geq C_{j}\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}.

Therefore, for any

, there exists a constant

such that if

-\sum_{\{ijk\}\in F}\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}>\sum_{\{ijk\}\in F}C_{j}\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}=\sum^{n}_{j=1,j\sim i}C_{j}\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}.

Lemma 5.5
.

The solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Calabi flow (

) can not reach the boundary
\partial_{0}\Omega(\Phi)
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists at least

such that

,
then
r_{i}\rightarrow+\infty
by

,
K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i}<0

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{du_{i}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\Delta(K-\overline{K})_{i}

(20)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}(K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i})-\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}(K_{j}-\overline{K}_{j})

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\{ijk\}\in F}\frac{\partial\theta_{i}^{jk}}{\partial u_{i}}(K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i})-\sum_{j\sim i}\left(\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\theta^{jl}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}\right)(K_{j}-\overline{K}_{j})

\displaystyle=\bigg[-\sum_{\{ijk\}\in F}\frac{\partial\theta_{i}^{jk}}{\partial u_{i}}-\sum_{j\sim i}\left(\frac{\partial\theta^{jk}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}+\frac{\partial\theta^{jl}_{i}}{\partial u_{j}}\right)\frac{K_{j}-\overline{K}_{j}}{K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i}}\bigg](K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i}).

By Lemma
3.2
, the combinatorial Calabi energy
\mathcal{C}(u)
is decreasing along the combinatorial Calabi flow (

),
which implies that for any

is bounded along the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
), i.e., there exists a constant
M_{3}
, such that for any
i\in B
,

.
Therefore,
\big|\frac{K_{j}-\overline{K}_{j}}{K_{i}-\overline{K}_{i}}\big|\leq\frac{2M_{3}}{M_{2}}
,
j\neq i
.
By Corollary
5.4
, one can choose numbers

,

, specially

The following theorem gives the global convergence of the solution of the combinatorial Calabi flow (

Theorem 5.7
.

Suppose
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
is an ideally triangulated surface with boundary.
Let
\Phi:E\rightarrow(0,+\infty)
be the weight on
(\Sigma,\mathcal{T})
.
For any
\overline{K}\in(0,+\infty)^{N}
defined on
B=\{1,2,...,N\}
,
the solution of the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) converges exponentially fast.

Proof.

Note that the solution
u(t)
of the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
) stays in a compact subset of the admissible space
\Omega(\Phi)
by Corollary
5.6
and the discrete Laplace operator
\Delta=(\frac{\partial K_{i}}{\partial u_{j}})_{N\times N}
is strictly negative by Lemma
2.1
.
By the continuity of the eigenvalues of
\Delta
, there exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{\Delta}
of
\Delta
satisfies
\lambda_{\Delta}<-\sqrt{\lambda_{0}}
along the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
).
Therefore, along the combinatorial Calabi flow (
3
), we have

\frac{d\mathcal{C}(u(t))}{dt}=-(K-\overline{K})^{T}\Delta^{2}(K-\overline{K})\leq-\lambda_{0}\mathcal{C}(u(t)),

which implies

\mathcal{C}(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}||K(t)-\overline{K}||^{2}\leq e^{-\lambda_{0}t}||K(0)-\overline{K}||^{2}.

Combining Theorem
2.2
and Corollary
5.6
,
we have

||u(t)-\overline{u}||^{2}\leq C_{1}||K(t)-\overline{K}||^{2}\leq C_{1}e^{-\lambda_{0}t}||K(0)-\overline{K}||^{2}\leq C_{2}e^{-\lambda_{0}t}

for some positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
, which completes the proof.

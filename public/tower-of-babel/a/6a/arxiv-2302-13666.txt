Correlated Noise and Critical Dimensions Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Harukuni Ikeda

Abstract
In equilibrium, the Mermin-Wagner theorem prohibits the continuous symmetry breaking for all dimensions d ≤ 2 d\leq 2 . In this work, we discuss that this limitation can be circumvented in non-equilibrium systems driven by the spatio-temporally long-range anticorrelated noise. We first compute the lower and upper critical dimensions of the O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) model driven by the spatio-temporally correlated noise by means of the dimensional analysis. Next, we consider the spherical model, which corresponds to the large n n limit of the O ⁡ ( n ) O(n) model and allows us to compute the critical dimensions and critical exponents, analytically. Both results suggest that the critical dimensions increase when the noise is positively correlated in space and time, and decrease when anticorrelated. We also report that the spherical model with the correlated noise shows the hyperuniformity and giant number fluctuation even well above the critical point.



I
Introduction

The minimum dimension required for a phase transition to occur is known
as the lower critical dimension
d_{l}

Nishimori and Ortiz 2010
. For
systems with quenched randomness, Imry and Ma predicted that the lower
critical dimension is
d_{l}=2
for a discrete order parameter and
d_{l}=4

for a continuous order parameter
Imry and Ma 1975
. Recent studies have
reported that
d_{l}
can be reduced by introducing anticorrelation to the
quenched randomness. For example, in Ref.
Schwartz et al. 1993
, the
authors studied the random field Ising model with the anticorrelated
random field and showed that the ordered phase arises on the ground
state even in
d=2
. Ref.
Janke and Villanova 1995
reported a first-order
transition of the Potts model on a random Voronoi lattice in
d=2
. The
authors in Ref.
Barghathi and Vojta 2014
argued that this is a consequence
of the strong anticorrelation in the coordination number of the random
Voronoi lattices, which reduces the lower critical dimension. Similar
anticorrelation also appears in the Ising model in the aperiodic field,
which stabilizes the ferromagnetic ground state even in

d=1

Luck 1987
;
Sire 1993
.

For equilibrium systems without quenched randomness, the Mermin-Wagner
theorem claims that the lower-critical dimension of the continuous
symmetry breaking is
d_{l}=2

Mermin and Wagner 1966
. However, for
out-of-equilibrium systems, the continuous symmetry breaking can occur
even
d\leq 2
; some examples include the XY model driven by anisotropic
noise
Bassler and Rácz 1995
;
Reichl et al. 2010
,
O(n)
model driven by
shear
Corberi et al. 2002
;
Nakano et al. 2021
, Vicsek
model
Vicsek et al. 1995
;
Toner and Tu 1995
, and so
on
Loos et al. 2022
;
Dadhichi et al. 2020
. A recent numerical study suggests
that so-called hyperuniform states of
matter
Torquato 2018
may potentially be added to the
list above
Galliano et al. 2023
. The hyperuniform states of matter are
characterized by the anomalous suppression of the density fluctuation on
a large scale, which leads to the vanishing of the static structure
factor
S(q)
in the limit of the small wave number

\lim_{q\to}S(q)=0

Torquato 2018
. This property is
referred to as the hyperuniformity (HU), which was first introduced for
density fluctuation, but later has been extended to fluctuations of more
general quantities, such as spin variables and vector
fields
Torquato 2018
. In Ref.
Galliano et al. 2023
,
Galliano
et al.
proposed and numerically demonstrated that the
suppression of the density fluctuation also reduces
d_{l}
, leading to
the perfect crystalline phase even in
d=2
.

The HU is widely observed in various systems such as Vycor
glass
Crossley et al. 1991
, periodically driven
emulsions
Weijs et al. 2015
, chiral active
matter
Huang et al. 2021
;
Zhang and Snezhko 2022
, and so
on
Torquato 2018
. While no unified theory has been
found that can comprehensively explain the HU observed in all these
systems, if it exists, Hexner and Levine proposed that the HU can
universally appear for systems having certain
symmetries
Hexner and Levine 2017
. In Ref.
Hexner and Levine 2017
, they
derived a Langevin equation for a system conserving the total number of
particles and center of mass. The effective noise of the resultant
Langevin equation has spatial anticorrelation, which leads to the
HU
Hexner and Levine 2017
;
Lei and Ni 2019
. Other examples showing
the HU are chiral active
matter
Huang et al. 2021
;
Zhang and Snezhko 2022
, where the periodic
nature of the driving force leads to the temporal anticorrelation of the
effective noise, which results in the
HU
Kuroda and Miyazaki 2023
;
Ikeda and Kuroda 2023
. These results suggest
that spatial or temporal anticorrelation of the noise leads to HU, which
may reduce
d_{l}
.

Based on the above observations, it is tempting to conjecture that the
anticorrelation of the noise or quenched randomness generally reduces
the lower critical dimension. Our first goal is to test this conjecture.
The second goal is to investigate the effects of the long-range temporal
correlation of the noise on critical phenomena. The effects of the
temporally correlated noise on a single particle have been investigated
significantly in the context of anomalous
diffusion
Bouchaud and Georges 1990
;
Eliazar and Klafter 2009
. However, its
effects on many-body systems, in particular near the critical point,
have not been investigated sufficiently before. For those goals, here we
investigate the effects of the spatiotemporally correlated noise on the
second-order phase transition by using the
O(n)
and spherical
models. As discussed in the following paragraph, the noise encompasses
the time-independent random field and equilibrium white noise in certain
limits.

For concreteness, we consider model-A and B
dynamics

;
Nishimori and Ortiz 2010

\xi(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle\left\langle\xi(\bm{x},t)\xi(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right\rangle=2TD(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime},t-t^{\prime}),

III.1
Model

The effective free energy of the model is

\displaystyle\int d\bm{x}\left\langle\phi(\bm{x})^{2}\right\rangle=N.

We impose the spherical constraint for the mean value. One can, in

,
instead of Eq. (
20

For

\displaystyle S(q,\omega)

Correlation length and relaxation time

Since we are interested in the critical behaviors in large

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}(x,y)=\frac{2\Gamma x^{-2\rho}y^{-2\theta}}{y^{2}+\Gamma^{2}(x^{2}+1)^{2}}.

(39)

This is consistent with the result of the dimensional analysis
Eq. (
15
) for
\rho>-1/2
. On the contrary, the results
are inconsistent for
\rho<-1/2
. Further studies would be beneficial to
elucidate this point, but anyway, the qualitative result remains the
same: the positive correlation increases
d_{l}
, and the anticorrelation
reduces
d_{l}
.

The detailed analysis of Eq. (
36
) near
T_{c}

leads to (see Appendix.
A
)

\displaystyle\mu\sim(T-T_{c})^{\gamma}

(40)

with

\displaystyle\gamma=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{d-d_{l}}&d_{l}<d<d_{u}\\
1&d>d_{u},\end{cases},

(41)

where the upper critical dimension
d_{u}
is

\displaystyle d_{u}=4+2\rho+4\theta,(-1/2<\theta<1/2).

(42)

Again the result is consistent with the dimensional analysis for

\rho>-1/2
. Substituting this result into Eq. (
28
),
we can determine the critical exponent:

\displaystyle\xi\sim(T-T_{c})^{-\nu}

(43)

with

\displaystyle\nu=\begin{cases}1/(d-d_{l})&d_{l}<d<d_{u}\\
1/2&d>d_{u}\end{cases}.

(44)

The critical exponent differs from the equilibrium value if

2\rho+4\theta\neq 0
since
d_{l}\neq d_{l}^{\rm eq}=2
. In other words,
the long-range spatio-temporal correlation of the noise changes the
universality class.

IV
Conserved order parameter

Let
\vec{\phi}=\{\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{n}\}
be a conserved
n
-component order parameter. We
assume that the time evolution of
\phi_{a}(\bm{x},t)
is described by the
model-B dynamics
Chaikin et al. 1995
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{a}(\bm{x},t)}{\partial t}=\Gamma\nabla^{2}\frac{\delta F[\vec{\phi}]}{\delta\phi_{a}(\bm{x},t)}+\nabla\cdot\bm{\xi}_{a}(\bm{x},t),

(45)

where
\Gamma
denotes the damping coefficient,

{\bm{\xi}_{a}=\{\xi_{a,\mu}\}_{\mu=1,\dots,d}}
denotes the noise, and
d
denotes
the spatial dimension. The mean and variance of the noise
\xi_{a,\mu}(\bm{x},t)
are given
by

\displaystyle\left\langle\xi_{a,\mu}(\bm{x},t)\right\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\left\langle\xi_{a,\mu}(\bm{x},t)\xi_{b,\nu}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right\rangle=2T\delta_{ab}\delta_{\mu\nu}\Gamma D(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime},t-t^{\prime}),

(46)

where the Fourier transform of
D(\bm{x},t)
is given by
Eq. (
6
).

IV.1
Dimensional analysis for
O(n)
model

Substituting the free energy Eq. (
4
) into
Eq. (
45
), we get

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}_{a}=\Gamma\nabla^{2}(-\nabla^{2}\phi_{a}+\varepsilon\phi_{a}+g\phi_{a}|\vec{\phi}|^{2})+\nabla\cdot\bm{\xi}_{a}.

(47)

As before, we consider the scaling transformations:
x\to bx
,
t\to b^{z_{t}}t
,
\phi_{a}\to b^{z_{\phi}}\phi_{a}
,
g\to b^{z_{g}}g

Nishimori and Ortiz 2010
. Assuming the scaling invariance
of the dynamic equation Eq. (
47
), we get

\displaystyle z_{t}=4,

\displaystyle z_{g}=2-2z_{\phi}-z_{t},

\displaystyle z_{\phi}=1+\frac{2\rho^{\prime}-d}{2}+4\theta^{\prime},

(48)

where
\rho^{\prime}=\max[\rho,-1/2]
and
\theta^{\prime}=\max[\theta,-1/2]
, as
defined in Eq. (
12
). As before, the lower critical
dimension is calculated by setting
z_{\phi}=0
, leading to

\displaystyle d_{l}=2+2\rho^{\prime}+8\theta^{\prime}.

(49)

The upper critical dimension is obtained by setting
z_{g}=0
, leading to

\displaystyle d_{u}=4+2\rho^{\prime}+8\theta^{\prime}.

(50)

When
\theta=0
, the results are consistent with those of the model-A,
see Sec.
II
, while when
\theta\neq 0
, we get
different results. Aside from such a difference, the qualitative
conclusion remains the same: the positive correlation of the noise
(
\rho>0
and
\theta>0
) increases the critical dimensions
d_{l}
and

d_{u}
, while the anticorrelation (
\rho<0
and
\theta<0
) reduces

d_{l}
and
d_{u}
.

IV.2
Spherical model

The spherical model for the model-B dynamics is

\displaystyle\dot{\phi}=\Gamma\nabla^{2}(-\nabla^{2}\phi+\mu\phi)+\nabla\cdot\bm{\xi}.

(51)

This model neglects the non-linear term of the
O(n)
model and instead
impose the spherical constraint
{\int d\bm{x}\left\langle\phi^{2}\right\rangle=N}
. One
can solve it easily since this is a linear equation. For instance, the
static structure factor
S(\bm{q})
in the steady-state is calculated as

\displaystyle S(\bm{q})

\displaystyle=\frac{BTq^{-2\rho-4\theta}}{(q^{2}+\mu)^{1+2\theta}}

(52)

where
B
denotes a constant.

\displaystyle B=\frac{1}{\Gamma^{2\theta}\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\left|x\right|^{-2\theta}dx}{x^{2}+1}=\frac{\sec(\pi\theta)}{\Gamma^{2\theta}}.

(53)

As before, we restrict
\theta
to
-1/2<\theta<1/2
to keep
B
finite.

S(q)
shows the power low behavior for
q\ll\mu^{1/2}\approx\xi^{-1}
:

\displaystyle S(q)\sim q^{-2\rho-4\theta}.

(54)

For
2\rho+4\theta>0
,
S(q)\to\infty
for small
q
, leading to the
GNF
Narayan et al. 2007
. On the contrary, for
2\rho+4\theta<0
,

S(q)\to 0
for small
q
, leading to the
HU
Torquato 2018
. Interestingly, the GNF and HU appear
even without the spatial correlation of the noise
\rho=0
. To
visualize the above results, we show typical behaviors of
S(q)
in
Fig.

.

\displaystyle\nu=\frac{1}{d-d_{l}},

IV.3
Center of mass conserving dynamics

An interesting application is for the systems driven by the center of
mass conserving (COMC) dynamics, which was introduced to explain the
HU
Hexner and Levine 2017
;
Lei and Ni 2019
. The origin
of the HU may depend on the detail of the system, but
Hexner and Levine claimed that there is a universal mechanism to yield
HU for systems conserving the center of
mass
Hexner and Levine 2017
. Below we briefly summarize their argument
for the model-B dynamics. Let
\rho(\bm{x},t)
be the density following the
equation of continuity:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho(\bm{x},t)}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot\bm{J(\bm{x},t)},

(58)

where
\bm{J}
denotes the flux.
In the case of the standard model-B,

\bm{J}
is written as
Onuki 2002

\displaystyle\bm{J}=-\Gamma\nabla\mu+\bm{\xi},

(59)

where
\mu=\delta F/\delta\rho(\bm{x},t)
denotes the chemical potential and
\bm{\xi}
denotes the
noise. The conservation of the center of mass
requires
Hexner and Levine 2017

\displaystyle\frac{d\left\langle x_{a}\right\rangle}{dt}

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{x}x_{a}\frac{\partial\rho(\bm{x},t)}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\int d\bm{x}x_{a}\nabla\cdot\bm{J}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{x}J_{a}(\bm{x},t)

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{x}\xi_{a}=0.

(60)

To satisfy the last equality,
\xi_{a}
should be written in the form of
a divergence of other vector:

\displaystyle\xi_{a}(\bm{x},t)=\nabla\cdot\bm{\sigma}_{a}(\bm{x},t)=\sum_{b}\frac{\partial\sigma_{ab}(\bm{x},t)}{\partial x_{b}}.

(61)

The simplest choice of
\sigma_{ab}(\bm{x},t)
is an isotropic white noise:

\displaystyle\left\langle\sigma_{ab}(\bm{x},t)\sigma_{cd}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right\rangle\propto\delta_{ac}\delta_{bd}\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime}).

(62)

Then we get

\displaystyle\left\langle\xi_{a}(\bm{x},t)\xi_{b}(\bm{x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right\rangle\propto\delta_{ab}\nabla^{2}\delta(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime}),

(63)

which is tantamount to set
D(\bm{q},\omega)\propto q^{2}
in our model-B
dynamics,
i.e.
,
\rho=-1
and
\theta=0
, see
Eq. (
46
). In this case, the static structure factor
Eq. (
54
) behaves as
S(q)\sim q^{2}
for a small waver
number
q
. Therefore, the model exhibits the HU, as discussed in
previous work
Hexner and Levine 2017
. Also, the dimensional
analysis of the
O(n)
model and spherical model both predict that the
lower critical dimension becomes lower than the equilibrium value,

d_{l}<d_{l}^{\rm eq}=2
. This implies that the continuous symmetric
breaking can occur even in
d=2
or lower dimension in contrast with the
equilibrium systems for which the Mermin-Wagner theorem prohibits the
long-range order. This appears to be consistent with a recent numerical
simulation of a two-dimensional system driven by the COMC dynamics,
where the authors reported the emergence of the perfect crystal phase
even in
d=2

Galliano et al. 2023
. But strictly speaking, our model is
for the second-order phase transition, and thus it can not be directly
applied to the first-order phase transition such as the
crystaillization. Further numerical and theoretical studies would be
beneficial.

V.1
Summary

In this work, we calculated the lower and upper critical dimensions,

d_{l}
and
d_{u}

O(n)
and spherical models driven by the
model A and B dynamics with the correlated noise
\xi(\bm{x},t)

Table.
1
for a summary. The correlation of the noise
is written in the Fourier space as
D(\bm{q},\omega)=\left|\bm{q}\right|^{-2\rho}\left|\omega\right|^{-2\theta}
. Our results imply that the
positive correlation of the noise (
\rho>0
and
\theta>0
) increases
the critical dimensions,
d_{l}
and
d_{u}
, while the anticorrelation
(
\rho<0
and
\theta<0
) reduces
d_{l}
and
d_{u}
. We also found that
the static structure factor
S(q)
in the paramagnetic phase exhibits
the power-low behavior for small wave number
S(q)\sim q^{\alpha}
with

\alpha=-2\rho
for the non-conserved order parameter (model-A) and

\alpha=-2\rho-4\theta
for the conserved order parameter (model-B),
leading to the GNF for
\alpha<0
, and HU for
\alpha>0
. We summarize
those results in Fig.

.

V.2
Hyperuniformity, gian number fluctuation, and lower critical dimension

Our first expectation was that the HU would decrease
d_{l}
from the
equilibrium value
d_{l}^{\rm eq}=2
, and the GNF would increase

d_{l}
. This expectation is correct for the spatiotemporally positive
correlated noise (
\rho>0,\theta>0
) and anticorrelated noise (
\rho<0,\theta<0
), see Fig.
3
. However, for the intermediate
cases,
\rho<0,\theta>0
and
\rho>0,\theta<0
, the HU or GNF does not
always guaranty
d_{l}<2
or
d_{l}>2
, see
Fig.
3
. Further studies would be beneficial to
elucidate the relation between the HU, GNF, and
d_{l}
.

V.3
Perspective on temporally correlated noise

The temporally correlated noise for
-1/2<\theta<1/2
has been studied
extensively in the context of the anomalous diffusion in crowded
environments, because a free particle driven by the noise
\dot{x}=\xi

exhibits the sub-diffusion
\left\langle x(t)^{2}\right\rangle\sim t^{1+2\theta}
for

-1/2<\theta<0
and super-diffusion for
0<\theta<1/2
, see
Refs.
Bouchaud and Georges 1990
;
Höfling and Franosch 2013
for reviews.
However, relatively few studies have been done on the effects of the
temporal correlation on critical phenomena. For example, in
Refs.
García-Ojalvo and Sancho 1994
;
Sancho et al. 1998
;
Maggi et al. 2022
, the authors
studied the effect of exponentially correlated noise on the
\varphi^{4}

model and found the same universality as the equilibrium Ising model. In
Ref.
Bonart et al. 2012
, the authors studied the
O(n)
model
with the power-low correlated noise, but the noise was introduced in a
way that preserves the detailed balance. Thus, the critical dimensions
and the static critical exponents are unchanged from those in
equilibrium. On the contrary, non-equilibrium noises, such as the
1/f

noises, often show the power-low frequency dependence of the power
spectrum, naturally leading to the long-range temporal
correlation
Voss and Clarke 1976
;
Bak et al. 1987
;
Dutta and Horn 1981
;
Milotti 2002
;
Eliazar and Klafter 2009
.
Our research has demonstrated the emergence of novel phenomena, such as
the GNF, HU, and new universality classes
in systems driven by such long-range temporally correlated noise. We
hope that our findings will motivate further investigation into the
fascinating properties of these systems.

V.4
Systems driven by imperfect periodic or quasiperiodic forces

Tissues are often driven by periodic deformation of
cells
Zhang and Fodor 2022
. In chiral active matter, constituent
particles spontaneously rotate due to asymmetry of the driving
forces
Liebchen and Levis 2022
;
Kuroda and Miyazaki 2023
. The driving
force of those systems would be approximated by temporally periodic
functions. In previous work, we investigated a model driven by
temporally periodic but spatially uncorrelated driving forces and found
that the model exhibits the HU and smaller value of the lower critical
dimension than that in equilibrium
d_{l}<2

Ikeda and Kuroda 2023
.
However, the completely periodic function does not exist in reality due
to friction or other uncontrollable effects. The effects of the imperfection
of periodic patterns have been investigated extensively in the context
of the HU, and it is known that the Fourier spectrum often exhibits the
power-low with a positive exponent in these
cases
Kim and Torquato 2018
;
OÇ§uz et al. 2019
. For the simplest example, we
consider an imperfect periodic pulse
Kim and Torquato 2018
:

\displaystyle\xi(t)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{T}\delta(t-na-\eta_{n}),

(64)

where
\eta_{n}
represents a perturbation to a periodic
pulse. For simplicity, let us assume that
\eta_{n}
is an i.i.d Gaussian
random variable of zero mean and variance
\sigma
. Then, the power
spectrum of
\xi(t)
can be calculated as follows
Kim and Torquato 2018

\displaystyle D(\omega)

\displaystyle=\lim_{T\to\infty}\overline{\frac{1}{T}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{T}e^{i\omega(na+\eta_{n})}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=1+e^{-\sigma\omega^{2}}(D_{0}(\omega)-1),

(65)

where the overline denotes the average for
\eta_{n}
, and
D_{0}(\omega)

represents the spectrum of the periodic pulse:

\displaystyle D_{0}(\omega)=\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\left|\sum_{n=1}^{T}e^{i\omega na}\right|^{2}.

(66)

D_{0}(\omega)
is nothing but the static structure factor of a
one-dimensional lattice, and in particular
D_{0}(\omega)=0
for
sufficiently small
\omega

Kim and Torquato 2018
. For
\omega\ll 1
, we get

\displaystyle D(\omega)\sim 1-e^{-\sigma\omega^{2}}\sim\sigma\omega^{-2\theta}

(67)

with
\theta=-1
. This simple example demonstrated that the power-law
spectrum with negative
\theta
can naturally arise due to the
imperfection of the periodic pattern. More systematic studies for
various types of imperfections have been investigated in
Ref.
Kim and Torquato 2018
. The similar power law of the Fourier spectrum has
been also reported for one-dimensional quasi-periodic
sequences
Oğuz et al. 2017
;
OÇ§uz et al. 2019
. Do systems driven by
imperfect periodic or quasi-periodic forces exhibit the HU and symmetry
breaking transition in
d\leq 2
, as predicted by our theory? Further
theoretical and numerical studies would be beneficial to elucidate this
point.

Appendix A
Scaling of
\mu

To determine
\mu
, one should solve the following self-consistent equation:

\displaystyle 1=TG(\mu)\equiv TA\int_{0}^{q_{D}}dq\frac{q^{d-1+m}}{(q^{2}+\mu)^{n}},

(68)

where
A
,
n
, and
m
are constants.
We want to derive the scaling behavior of
\mu
near the critical point:

\displaystyle T_{c}=\left[A\int_{0}^{q_{D}}dqq^{d-1+m-2n}\right]^{-1}.

(69)

For
d+m-2n>0
, the denominator of Eq. (
69
) diverges,
and thus the model does not have the critical point at finite
T
.
This implies that the lower critical dimension is

\displaystyle d_{l}=2n-m.

(70)

When
d>2n-m+2
,
G(\mu)
can be expanded as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}

\displaystyle=G(0)+\mu G^{\prime}(0)+\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T_{c}}+\mu G^{\prime}(0)+\cdots,

(71)

leading to

\displaystyle\mu\sim(T-T_{c})^{1}.

(72)

On the contrary, if
d\in(2n-m,2n-m+2)
,
G^{\prime}(\mu)
for small
\mu

behaves as

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(\mu)\sim\mu^{\frac{d+m-(2n+2)}{2}},

(73)

implying

\displaystyle G(\mu)-G(0)=\int_{0}^{\mu}d\mu^{\prime}G^{\prime}(\mu^{\prime})\sim\mu^{\frac{d+m-2n}{2}},

(74)

leading to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}=G(\mu)=\frac{1}{T_{c}}-B\mu^{\frac{d+m-2n}{2}},

(75)

where
B
is a constant. Therefore, the scaling of
\mu
for
\mu\ll 1
is

\displaystyle\mu\sim(T-T_{c})^{\frac{2}{d+m-2n}}\sim(T-T_{c})^{\frac{2}{d-d_{l}}}.

(76)

The above results imply that the
upper critical dimension is

\displaystyle d_{u}=2n-m+2=d_{l}+2.

(77)

Quivers, Flow Trees, and Log Curves
Hülya Argüz, Pierrick Bousseau

Abstract
. Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants of a quiver with potential can be expressed in terms of simpler attractor DT invariants by a universal formula. The coefficients in this formula are calculated combinatorially using attractor flow trees. In this paper, we prove that these coefficients are genus 0 log Gromov–Witten invariants of d d -dimensional toric varieties, where d d is the number of vertices of the quiver. This result follows from a log-tropical correspondence theorem which relates ( d − 2 ) (d-2) -dimensional families of tropical curves obtained as universal deformations of attractor flow trees, and rational log curves in toric varieties.

Introduction

This paper relates two seemingly different ways to define enumerative invariants given a quiver with potential: the quiver Donaldson–Thomas (DT) invariants, defined using moduli spaces of quiver representations, and log Gromov–Witten invariants of toric varieties, defined as counts of rational log curves satisfying constraints specified by the combinatorics of the quiver.

To relate quiver DT invariants to log Gromov–Witten invariants, we use the combinatorial calculation of quiver DT invariants in terms of attractor flow trees following our previous work
[
5
]
. We explain how to interpret such trees as tropical curves, and then prove a new correspondence theorem between counts of tropical curves and log Gromov–Witten invariants of toric varieties. We show this log-tropical correspondence in a situation where one considers non-rigid tropical curves moving in non-trivial families.

After a brief description of DT
quiver invariants in §
0.1
and of log Gromov–Witten invariants in §
0.2
, we provide a more detailed overview of the log-tropical correspondence in §
0.3
, and the quiver DT-log Gromov–Witten correspondence in §
0.4
.

0.2.
Log Gromov–Witten invariants

Log Gromov–Witten theory, developed by Abramovich–Chen and Gross–Siebert
[
1
,
24
]
, provides a framework to enumerate algebraic curves in an algebraic variety
X
having prescribed tangency conditions along a divisor
D\subset X
which is allowed to have particular types of singularities – typically we consider normal crossing divisors. Generalizing the notion of a stable map
f:C\to X
to the log setting amounts to endowing all spaces with (fine, saturated) log structures and lift all morphisms to morphisms of log spaces – see §
2.1
for a brief review of log schemes and stable log maps.

The additional data of a log structure on a variety
X
encodes combinatorial information which is used to define a cone complex associated to
X
, referred to as the
tropicalization
of
X
. This makes it feasible to count log curves in terms of their tropical counterparts. In
[
40
]
, Nishinou-Siebert used toric degenerations and log deformation theory to prove
a correspondence theorem between counts of genus
0
complex curves in
d
-dimensional toric varieties and counts of tropical curves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. More precisely, the main result of
[
40
]
provides a correspondence between counts of stable log maps to counts of rigid tropical trees. To obtain such a correspondence, one considers log curves generically contained in the big torus orbit of the toric variety and with generically trivial log structure.

In the present paper, given a quiver with
d
vertices, we prove a correspondence theorem between counts of log curves in
d
-dimensional toric varieties and tropical counts associated to
(d-2)
-dimensional families of tropical curves in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
obtained as universal deformations of the attractor flow trees.
The main difference with the set-up of
[
40
]
is that for
d\geq 3
, the relevant log curves are entirely contained in the toric boundary divisor, have generically non-trivial log structures, and correspond to non-rigid tropical curves moving in non-trivial
(d-2)
-dimensional families.

Related works

Another way to construct a geometrical object out of a quiver is based on the theory of cluster algebras

[
16
]
and cluster varieties
[
15
]
.
From a quiver
Q
with
d
vertices, one can construct the corresponding
d
-dimensional cluster variety using cluster transformations in a way prescribed by the combinatorics of
Q
. A key result of Gross–Hacking–Keel–Kontsevich
[
21
]
shows that the algebra of regular functions of a cluster variety admits a canonical basis, which can be constructed using a combinatorial gadget, known as the cluster scattering diagram. A version of such a cluster scattering diagram is shown to capture quiver DT invariants in the work of Bridgeland
[
11
]
. On the other hand, Gross–Siebert in
[
25
]
show how to construct a mirror to a given log Calabi–Yau variety from a canonical scattering diagram, defined using counts of log curves. In our previous paper
[
6
]
we showed how the canonical scattering diagram for cluster varieties relates to the cluster scattering.

In a paper in preparation, we provide a correspondence between quiver DT invariants, in the situation when attractor DT invariants are trivial, and counts of log curves in cluster varieties, using the comparison between cluster and canonical scattering diagrams. This will generalize the Kronecker-Gromov–Witten correspondence which was shown earlier in the case when the cluster variety is of dimension two
[
9
,
22
,
23
,
46
,
47
]
. For variants of the Kronecker-Gromov–Witten correspondence in dimension two see also
[
10
,
48
]
. We will also show that such a correspondence between quiver DT invariants and log curve counts in cluster varieties is compatible with the results of the present paper, using that cluster varieties can be obtained by non-toric blow-ups of toric varieties as explained in
[
20
]
, and so admit degenerations into a toric variety and other simpler varieties, as studied in
[
7
,
23
]
. In particular, log curve counts in cluster varieties are naturally related to log curve counts in toric varieties considered in this paper.

-dimensional real vector space. We let
N:=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb{Z})
denote the dual abelian group.

1.1.
Tropical curves

We mainly follow the convention and notation of
[
40
]
.
In this paper, a
graph

\Gamma
is finite and connected, consisting of vertices, edges connecting pairs of vertices, and legs adjacent to single vertices. Moreover, we assume that legs are marked by distinct labels.
We denote by

\displaystyle V(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\text{the set of vertices of\,\,}\Gamma

\displaystyle E(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\text{the set of edges of\,\,}\Gamma

\displaystyle L(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\text{the set of legs of\,\,}\Gamma\,.

We denote the set of the two vertices adjacent to an edge
E
by
\partial E
, and similarly the vertex adjacent to a leg
L
by
\partial L
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
weighted graph
is a graph
\Gamma
together with a weight function
w\colon E(\Gamma)\cup L(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
assigning a weight
w(E)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
to every edge or leg
E
of
\Gamma
.

Definition 1.2
.

A
parametrized tropical curve in
M_{\mathbb{R}}

is a weighted graph
\Gamma
without divalent vertices, together with a proper continuous map
h:\Gamma\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}

satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

for every edge or leg
E
, the restriction
h|_{E}
is an embedding with image contained in an affine line with rational slope,

(ii)

for every vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
, the following
balancing condition
holds: Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}\in E(\Gamma)\cup L(\Gamma)
be the edges or legs adjacent to
v
, and let
\bar{u}_{i}\in M
be the primitive integral vector emanating from
h(v)
in the direction of
h(E_{i})
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{m}w(E_{i})\bar{u}_{i}=0\,.

An isomorphism of parameterized tropical curves
h:\Gamma\to M_{\mathbb{R}}
and
h_{0}:\Gamma_{0}\to M_{\mathbb{R}}

is a homeomorphism
\Psi:\Gamma\to\Gamma_{0}
respecting the marking of the legs, the weights of the edges and legs, and such that
h=h_{0}\circ\Psi
. A
tropical curve
is an isomorphism class of parameterized tropical
curves. The genus of a tropical curve
h:\Gamma\to M_{\mathbb{R}}
is the first Betti number of its domain
\Gamma
. A rational tropical curve is a tropical curve of genus zero. In what follows we focus attention to rational tropical curves.

Definition 1.3
.

A
tropical type
for
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is the data
(\Gamma,\bar{u})
of a weighted graph
\Gamma
and of a map

\displaystyle\bar{u}:F(\Gamma)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow M

\displaystyle(v,E)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\bar{u}_{v,E}

where
F(\Gamma)
is the set of flags of
\Gamma
, that is, of pairs
(v,E)
where
v\in V(\Gamma)
is a vertex and
e\in E(\Gamma)\subset L(\Gamma)
is an edge or leg adjacent to
v
.

Definition 1.4
.

The type of a tropical curve
h\colon\Gamma\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}
is the tropical type
(\Gamma,\bar{u})
where for every
(v,E)\in F(\Gamma)
,
\bar{u}_{v,E}
is the primitive integral in
M
emanating from
h(v)
in the direction of
h(E)
.

The moduli space of tropical curves of a given type
\tau=(\Gamma,\bar{u})
is naturally the interior of a polyhedral cone. For example, when
\Gamma
is of genus
0
, this moduli space is isomorphic to
M_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{|E(\Gamma)|}
, where
M_{\mathbb{R}}
parametrizes the position of a chosen vertex of
\Gamma
, and
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{|E(\Gamma)|}
parametrizes the affine lengths of the images
h(E)
of the edges
E\in E(\Gamma)
.

Definition 1.5
.

The
degree
of a tropical type
(\Gamma,\bar{u})
is the tuple

\Delta=(w(L)\bar{u}_{v,L})_{L\in L(\Gamma)}\in M^{L(\Gamma)}

of weighted directions of the legs, where
v
is the unique vertex of
\Gamma
adjacent to the leg
L
. The degree of a tropical curve is the degree of its type.

By
[
40
, Proposition 2.1]
, there are finitely many tropical types of given genus and degree which are realized by tropical curves. In particular, the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{trop}}(\Delta)
of rational tropical curves of given degree
\Delta
is naturally a finite cone complex, obtained by gluing together the cones obtained as the closure of the spaces of tropical curves of given types (when the length of an edge goes to
0
, a vertex of higher valency is produced).

1.2.
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curves

Let
\omega\in\bigwedge^{2}M
be a skew-symmetric form on
N
. For every
n\in N
, we denote by
\iota_{n}\omega\in M
the contraction of
\omega
by
n
, that is, the linear form
\omega(n,-)
on
N
.
Let
\boldsymbol{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{r})
be a
r
-tuple of elements
\gamma_{i}\in N
such that
\iota_{\gamma_{i}}\omega\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq r
and
\iota_{\gamma}\omega\neq 0
, where
\gamma:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\gamma_{i}
.

Definition 1.6
.

An
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curve in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is a rational tropical curve
h\colon\Gamma\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}
of
degree

\Delta_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}:=(\iota_{\gamma_{1}}\omega,\dots,\iota_{\gamma_{r}}\omega,-\iota_{\gamma}\omega)\,.

In other words,
\Gamma
is a genus
0
graph, with
r+1
legs
L_{1},\dots,L_{r}
,
L_{\mathrm{out}}
, such that

w(L_{i})\bar{u}_{v_{i},L_{i}}=-\iota_{\gamma_{i}}\omega
for all

1\leq i\leq r
, and
w(L_{\mathrm{out}})\bar{u}_{v_{\mathrm{out}},L_{\mathrm{out}}}=-\iota_{\gamma}\omega
, where
v_{i}
(resp.
v_{\mathrm{out}}
) is the unique vertex of
\Gamma
adjacent to
L_{i}
(resp.
L_{\mathrm{out}}
).

Given an
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curve
h\colon\Gamma\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}
, we usually view
\Gamma
as a tree with root leg
L_{\mathrm{out}}
and leaves
L_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq r
. Correspondingly, we say that an edge
E
is a child (resp. a parent) of a vertex
v
if
E
is adjacent to
v
and is not (resp. is) contained in the unique path in
\Gamma
between
v
and the root. We similarly define descendants and ancestors. For every edge or leg
E
of
\Gamma
,
we denote

(1.1)

u_{E}:=w(E)\bar{u}_{v,E}\,,

where
v
is the unique vertex such that
E
is a parent of
v
. In other words,
u_{E}
is the weighted direction of
E
following the flow on
\Gamma
starting at the leaves and ending at the root.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
h\colon\Gamma\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}
be an
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curve in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then for every edge or leg
E
of
\Gamma
, the
class of
E
is the element
\gamma_{E}\in N
defined by

\gamma_{E}=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}

where the sum is over the indices
1\leq i\leq r
such that the leg
L_{i}
is a descendant of
E
.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
h\colon\Gamma\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}
be an
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curve in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then for every edge or leg
E
of
\Gamma
, we have
u_{E}=-\iota_{\gamma_{E}}\omega
. In particular,
the class
\gamma_{E}
is non-zero.

Proof.

The equality
u_{E}=-\iota_{\gamma_{E}}\omega
is true for leaves by Definition
1.6
of a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-marked tropical curve and then follows for the other edges and the root leg by the balancing condition in
Definition
1.2
(ii). This implies that
\gamma_{E}\neq 0
because
u_{E}\neq 0
by definition of a tropical curve in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
(see (
1.1
) and Definition
1.2
(i)).

\blacklozenge

To simplify the notation, instead of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{trop}}(\Delta_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}})
, in what follows we use

{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
for the moduli space of
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curves in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. It follows from the discussion after Definition
1.4
that
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
is naturally a finite polyhedral cone.
For every cone
\sigma
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
, the relative interior
\operatorname{Int}(\sigma)
of
\sigma

parametrizes tropical curves of a given type
(\Gamma_{\sigma},\bar{u}_{\sigma})
. Moreover, the dimension of
\sigma
is given by

\dim\sigma=d+|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|\,,

which can also be written using that
\Gamma_{\sigma}
has genus
0
as

\dim\sigma=d-2+r-\mathrm{ov}(\Gamma_{\sigma})\,,

where
\mathrm{ov}(\Gamma_{\sigma})
is the overvalence of
\Gamma
, which is the non-negative integer defined by

(1.2)

\mathrm{ov}(\Gamma):=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}\mathrm{ov}(v)\,,

where
\mathrm{ov}(v):=k-3
if
v
is a
k
-valent vertex. In particular,
\sigma
is of dimension at most
d-2+r
, and is of dimension equal to
d-2+r
if and only if
\Gamma_{\sigma}
is trivalent.

1.3.
Tropical constraints

Definition 1.9
.

A
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint
is a
r
-tuple
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{r})
of affine hyperplanes
A_{i}
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
whose associated linear hyperplanes are given by
(\gamma_{i}^{\perp})_{\mathbb{R}}\subset M_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Choices of
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraints
\mathbf{A}
are in one-to-one correspondence with choices of points
[\mathbf{A}]=([A_{i}])_{i}\in\prod_{i=1}^{r}M_{\mathbb{R}}/(\gamma_{i}^{\perp})_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

Definition 1.10
.

An
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curve
h:\Gamma\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}

matches
a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint
\mathbf{A}
if
h(L_{i})\subset A_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq r
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
the moduli space of
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curves matching the
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint
\mathbf{A}
.
In other words,

{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}\coloneqq(\mathrm{ev}^{\mathrm{trop}})^{-1}([\mathbf{A}])
,
where
\mathrm{ev}^{\mathrm{trop}}
is the evaluation map at the legs
L_{i}
, defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ev}^{\mathrm{trop}}\colon{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\mathrm{trop}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{r}M_{\mathbb{R}}/(\gamma_{i}^{\perp})_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{r}

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle\longmapsto([h(L_{i})])_{i}\,,

which is well-defined because
\omega(\gamma_{i},\gamma_{i})=0
implies that
u_{L_{i}}=\iota_{\gamma_{i}}\omega\in\gamma_{i}^{\perp}
.
As the evaluation map is affine in restriction to each cone of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
, the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
is naturally a finite polyhedral complex. For every face
\sigma
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
, the relative interior
\operatorname{Int}(\sigma)
of
\sigma

parametrizes tropical curves of a given type
(\Gamma_{\sigma},\bar{u}_{\sigma})
and matching
\mathbf{A}
.

For every subset
I\subset\{1,\dots,r\}
, we denote
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{I}:=(\gamma_{i})_{i\in I}
and we consider the
\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{I}
-constraint
\mathbf{A}_{I}:=(A_{i})_{i\in I}
.

Definition 1.11
.

A
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint
\mathbf{A}=(A_{1},\dots,A_{r})
is
general
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

A_{i}\neq A_{j}
for
i\neq j
,

(ii)

for every
I\subset\{1,\dots,r\}
,
\dim{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{I},\mathbf{A}_{I}}^{\mathrm{trop}}\leq d-2
,

(iii)

for every
I\subset\{1,\dots,r\}
, if
\sigma
is a face of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{I},\mathbf{A}_{I}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
, then
\dim\sigma=d-2
if and only if
\Gamma_{\sigma}
is trivalent.

Lemma 1.12
.

The set of general
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraints is an open dense subset in the space
\prod_{i=1}^{r}M_{\mathbb{R}}/(\gamma_{i}^{\perp})_{\mathbb{R}}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{r}
of
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraints.

Proof.

As there are only finitely many subsets
I\subset\{1,\dots,r\}
, it is enough to prove that the set of
\mathbf{A}
satisfying Definition
1.11
(ii)-(iii) for a given
I
is open and dense.
Let
\tau=(\Gamma,\bar{u})
be the type of
an
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{I})
-marked tropical curve. As
\Gamma
is of genus
0
,
h
is not superabundant by
[
36
, Proposition 2.5]
. In this case,
[
36
, Proposition 2.10]
implies that, for
\mathbf{A}
in an open dense subset of the space of constraints, the space of
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curves of type
\tau
matching
\mathbf{A}
is a non-empty open convex polyhedron in a vector space of dimension

d+\overline{e}-\sum_{i\in I}\operatorname{codim}(A_{i})=d+\overline{e}-|I|\,,

where by
[
36
, §2, Eq.​ (2)]
, using that
\Gamma
has
|I|+1
legs,

\overline{e}=(|I|+1)-3-\mathrm{ov}(\Gamma)\,,

where the overvalence
\mathrm{ov}(\Gamma)
is given by (
1.2
) and is a non-negative integer.
Therefore, the space of
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma}_{I})
-marked tropical curves of type
\tau
matching
\mathbf{A}
is of dimension at most

d+(|I|+1)-3-\mathrm{ov}(\Gamma)-|I|=d-2-\mathrm{ov}(\Gamma)\,,

and of dimension equal to
d-2
if and only if
\mathrm{ov}(\Gamma)=0
, that is, if
\Gamma
is trivalent.

1.4.
Properties of
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curves

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint and let
\sigma
be a face of the moduli space

{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
of
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked tropical curves in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
matching
\mathbf{A}
.
For each vertex
v
of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
, we obtain a polyhedron

\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

(1.4)

\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}\coloneqq\{h(v)\,|\,h\in\operatorname{Int}(\sigma)\}^{\operatorname{cl}}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

and for each edge or leg
E
of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
, a polyhedron
\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

(1.5)

\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\coloneqq\left(\bigcup_{h\in\operatorname{Int}(\sigma)}h(E)\right)^{\operatorname{cl}}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

see Figure
1.1
.
For every polyhedron
\mathfrak{d}
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote by
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}}\subset M
the subspace of integral tangent vectors to
\mathfrak{d}
.

Figure 1.1.
Polyhedra
\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}
and
\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}
for
d=3
.

By Definition
1.9
, the affine hyperplanes
A_{i}
of
\mathbf{A}
have equations of the form
\gamma_{i}=\epsilon_{i}
for some
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
.
For every edge or leg
E
of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
, we denote by
A_{E}
the affine hyperplane in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
with equation

(1.6)

\gamma_{E}=\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}

where the sum is over the indices
1\leq i\leq r
such that the leg
L_{i}
is a descendant of
E
. We have
\dim A_{E}=d-1
because
\gamma_{E}\neq 0
by Lemma
1.8
.

Lemma 1.13
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint and let

\sigma
be a face of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
.
Then,

(i)

for every edge or leg
E
of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
, we have
\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\subset A_{E}
,
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}}\subset\gamma_{E}^{\perp}
, and in particular
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\leq d-1
,

(ii)

for every vertex
v
of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
, we have
\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}\subset\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}A_{E_{i}}
, and
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}}\subset\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
where
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
are the children edges of
v
.

Proof.

We prove the result by induction following the flow on
\Gamma_{\sigma}
starting at the leaves and ending at the root.

For the initial step of the induction, we consider a leg
E=L_{i}
of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
. Every tropical curve
h\in\operatorname{Int}(\sigma)
matches the
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint
\mathbf{A}
, and so
h(L_{i})\subset A_{i}
.
It follows that
\mathfrak{d}_{L_{i}}^{\sigma}\subset A_{i}
and so
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{L_{i}}^{\sigma}}\subset\gamma_{i}^{\perp}
because
\Lambda_{A_{i}}=\gamma_{i}^{\perp}
.

For the induction step, let
v
be a vertex of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
,
E
the parent edge of
v
and
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
the children edges of
v
. We assume that Lemma
1.13
holds for
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
, and we have to show that Lemma
1.13
holds for
v
and
E
. For every tropical curve
h\in\operatorname{Int}(\sigma)
, we have
h(v)\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}h(E_{i})
, and so
\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{\sigma}\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{d}_{E_{1}}^{\sigma}
.
By the induction hypothesis, we have

\mathfrak{d}_{E_{i}}^{\sigma}\subset A_{E_{i}}
and

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{E_{i}}^{\sigma}}=\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
for all
1\leq i\leq k
, and so we conclude that

\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}A_{E_{i}}
and

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}}\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
. Similarly, we have
h(E)\subset h(v)+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}u_{E}
. Using Lemma
1.8
, this implies that
\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\subset\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}-\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\iota_{\gamma_{E}}\omega
. As we have already showed that
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}}\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
, we conclude that

\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}A_{E_{i}}-\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\iota_{\gamma_{E}}\omega\subset A_{E}
and

\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}}\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}-\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\iota_{\gamma_{E}}\omega\subset\gamma_{E}^{\perp}
. Indeed, we have
\cap_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}\subset\gamma_{E}^{\perp}
because
\gamma_{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{E_{i}}
, and we
have
\iota_{\gamma_{E}}\omega\in\gamma_{E}^{\perp}
because
\omega(\gamma_{E},\gamma_{E})=0
.

We denote by
T_{v}\Gamma

Lemma 1.14
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint and let

\sigma
be a face of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
. Let
v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})
be a vertex such that
\dim\mathfrak{d}^{\sigma}_{E}=d-1
, where
E
is the unique parent edge of
v
. Then,
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}\leq 2
.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
be the children edges of
v
.
If
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}>2
, then, as
u_{E_{k}}=-\iota_{\gamma_{E_{k}}}\omega
by Lemma
1.8
, the
\mathbb{R}
-span in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
of the vectors
\gamma_{E_{k}}
is also

, and so
\dim\cap_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{E_{k}}^{\perp}<d-2
. By Lemma

, we have
\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
, and so
\dim\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}<d-1
. Finally, as
\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\subset\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}u_{E}
, this implies that
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}<d-1
, contradiction.

The following Lemma is similar to
[
34
, Lemma 3.2.4]
and

[
21
, Lemma C.2]
.

Lemma 1.15
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint and let

\sigma
be a face of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
. Let
v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})
be a vertex such that

\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}>1
and

\dim\mathfrak{d}^{\sigma}_{E}=d-1
, where
E
is the unique parent edge of
v
. Then, we have
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E_{1}}^{\sigma}=d-1
, for any child edge
E_{1}
of
v
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.13
(i), it is enough to prove that
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E_{1}}^{\sigma}\geq d-1
in order to show that
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}=d-1
.

Assume by contradiction that
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E_{1}}^{\sigma}\leq d-2
for a child edge or leg
E_{1}
of
v
. Denote by
E_{2},\dots,E_{k}
the other child edges or legs of
v
.
For every tropical curve
h\in\operatorname{Int}(\sigma)
, we have
h(v)\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}h(E_{i})
, and so
\mathfrak{d}_{v}^{\sigma}\subset\cap_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{d}_{E_{i}}^{\sigma}
. By assumption, we have
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E_{1}}^{\sigma}\leq d-2
. On the other hand, by Lemma
1.13
(i), we also have
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E_{i}}^{\sigma}\leq d-1
for all
2\leq i\leq k
.
If
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{E_{1}}^{\sigma}}
is not included in
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{E_{i}}^{\sigma}}
for some
2\leq i\leq k
, it follows that
\dim\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}\leq d-3
, and so, as
\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\subset\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}u_{E}
, we conclude that
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\leq d-2
, contradiction.

Therefore, to end the proof, it is enough to consider the case where
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{E_{1}}^{\sigma}}\subset\cap_{i=2}^{k}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{E_{i}}^{\sigma}}
. In this case, the direction
u_{E_{1}}
is contained in
\cap_{i=2}^{k}\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{E_{2}}^{\sigma}}
, and so in
\cap_{i=2}^{k}\gamma_{E_{2}}^{\perp}
by Lemma
1.13
(i). By Lemma
1.8
, we have
u_{E_{1}}=-\iota_{\gamma_{E_{1}}}\omega
, and so
u_{E_{1}}\in\cap_{i=2}^{k}\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}

implies
\omega(\gamma_{E_{1}},\gamma_{E_{i}})=0
for all
2\leq i\leq k
.

As we are assuming that
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}>1
and
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}=d-1
, we actually have
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}=2
by Lemma
1.14
. Hence, there exists
2\leq i\leq k
such that
u_{E_{1}}
and
u_{E_{i}}
generate
T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}
. Hence,
\omega(\gamma_{E_{1}},\gamma_{E_{i}})=0
implies that
T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}\subset\gamma_{E_{1}}^{\perp}\cap\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
.
In particular, we obtain that
u_{E}\in\gamma_{E_{1}}^{\perp}\cap\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
. On the other hand, by Lemma
1.13
(ii), we also have
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}}\subset\gamma_{E_{1}}^{\perp}\cap\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
. We conclude that

\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\subset\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}u_{E}\subset\gamma_{E_{1}}^{\perp}\cap\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
. On the other hand, as we are assuming that
u_{E_{1}}
and
u_{E_{i}}
span the 2-dimensional space
T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}
,
u_{E_{1}}
and
u_{E_{i}}
are not proportional, and so the charges
\gamma_{E_{1}}
and
\gamma_{E_{i}}
are also not proportional. Hence, the hyperplanes
\gamma_{E_{1}}^{\perp}
and
\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
are distinct, and so
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}\leq d-2
, contradiction.

Lemma 1.16
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint and let

\sigma
be a face of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}

such that
\dim\sigma=d-2
and
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{L_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\sigma}=d-1
.
Then, we have
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}=2
for all vertices
v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})
, and
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}=d-1
for all edges or legs
E\in E(\Gamma_{\sigma})\cup L(\Gamma_{\sigma})
.

Proof.

We first show that
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}>1
for all
v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})
.
Assume by contradiction that there exists
v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})
such that
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}=1
. Then, there exists at least one vertex
v^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\sigma}
such that
\dim T_{v^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\sigma}=1
and such that
\dim T_{v^{\prime\prime}}\Gamma_{\sigma}>1
for all vertices
v^{\prime\prime}\neq v^{\prime}
on the unique path in
\Gamma_{\sigma}
from
v^{\prime}
to the root.

If
v^{\prime}
is the unique vertex adjacent to
L_{\mathrm{out}}
, then, as
\dim T_{v^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\sigma}=1
, one can move
h(v^{\prime})
is the direction of
h(L_{\mathrm{out}})
, and so
\mathfrak{j}_{v^{\prime}}^{\sigma}=\mathfrak{d}_{L_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\sigma}
, so
\dim\mathfrak{j}_{v^{\prime}}^{\sigma}=d-1
. This contradicts the assumption that
\dim\sigma=d-2
because
\mathfrak{j}_{v^{\prime}}^{\sigma}
is the image of
\sigma
by the evaluation map
h\mapsto h(v^{\prime})
.

If
v^{\prime}
is not adjacent at
L_{\mathrm{out}}
, then
v^{\prime}
admits a unique parent vertex
v
. Denote by
E_{1}
the edge connecting
v^{\prime}
and
v
. As
\dim T_{v^{\prime\prime}}\Gamma_{\sigma}>1
for every vertex
v^{\prime\prime}
on the path from
v
to the root, applying Lemma
1.15
iteratively from the root down to
v
shows that the assumption
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{L_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\sigma}=d-1
implies
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E_{1}}^{\sigma}=d-1
, and so
\dim\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}\geq d-2
.
On the other hand, as
\dim T_{v^{\prime}}\Gamma_{\sigma}=1
, one can move
h(v^{\prime})
is the direction of
h(E_{1})
without moving
h(v)
, and so
\dim\sigma\geq 1+\dim\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}\geq d-1
, contradiction with the assumption that
\dim\sigma=d-2
.

Once one knows that
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}>1
for all
v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})
, applying Lemma
1.15
iteratively starting from the root shows that the assumption
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{L_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\sigma}=d-1
implies
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}=d-1
for all edges or legs
E
. We conclude that
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}=2
for all
v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})
by Lemma
1.14
.

Theorem 1.17
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint and let
\sigma
be a
(d-2)
-dimensional face of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}

such that
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{L_{\mathrm{out}}}^{\sigma}=d-1
.
Then,

(i)

for every edge or leg
E
of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
, we have
\dim\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}=d-1
and
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{d}_{E}^{\sigma}}=\gamma_{E}^{\perp}
,

(ii)

for every vertex
v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})
, we have
\dim T_{v}\Gamma_{\sigma}=2
,
\dim\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}=d-2
and
\Lambda_{\mathfrak{j}_{v}^{\sigma}}=\cap_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{E_{i}}^{\perp}
where

are the children edges of

(iii)

for every edge or leg

of

with parent vertex

.

Proof.

(i) follows from Lemma
1.16
and Lemma
1.13
(i).
The first part of (ii)

. Moreover, denoting by

1.5.1.
Tropical multiplicities

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint and let
\sigma
be a face of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
.
For every leg
L\in L(\Gamma_{\sigma})
, there exists a unique vertex
\partial^{-}L
adjacent to
L
.
For every edge

\partial^{+}E

Definition 1.18
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint.
For every face
\sigma
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
, the

gluing map

\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}
is the map

(1.7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}:\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})}M

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\prod_{e\in E(\Gamma_{\sigma})}M/\mathbb{Z}u_{E}\times\prod_{i=1}^{r}M/\gamma_{i}^{\perp}

\displaystyle(m_{v})_{v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})}

\displaystyle\longmapsto((m_{\partial^{+}E}-m_{\partial^{-}E})_{E\in E(\Gamma_{\sigma})},m_{\partial^{-}E})\,.

By construction, the kernel
T_{\sigma}:=\operatorname{ker}\,\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}
is the integral tangent space to
\sigma
(see
[
36
, Proposition 2.10]
): an element
(m_{v})_{v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})}\in\prod_{v\in V(\Gamma_{\sigma})}M
contained in the kernel of
\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}
defines an integral way to move the vertices of
\Gamma_{\sigma}
in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
while preserving the directions of the edges and the matching of the constraint
\mathbf{A}
.

Lemma 1.19
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a general
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint as in Definition
1.11
. For every
(d-2)
-dimensional face
\sigma
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}
, the cokernel
\operatorname{coker}\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}
of the gluing map
\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}
is finite.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}
is surjective after tensoring with
\mathbb{Q}
. This follows from a dimension count: the domain of
\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{Q}
is of dimension
d|V(\Gamma_{\sigma})|
, its codomain is of dimension
(d-1)|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|+r
, and its kernel is of dimension
d-2
.
Hence,

\dim\operatorname{coker}\,\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=(d-1)|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|+l-d|V(\Gamma_{\sigma})|+d-2

=d(|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|-|V(\Gamma_{\sigma})|+1)-|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|+r-2\,.

As
\Gamma_{\sigma}
is of genus
0
, we have
|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|-|V(\Gamma_{\sigma})|+1=0
, and so

(1.8)

\dim\operatorname{coker}\,\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|+r-2\,.

Moreover,
as
\mathbf{A}
is general and
\dim\sigma=d-2
, the graph
\Gamma_{\sigma}
is trivalent by Definition
1.11
(iii),
with
r+1
legs, and so we have
3|V(\Gamma_{\sigma})|=2|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|+r+1
. Combining these two relations, we obtain

3(|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})+1|)=2|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|+r+1

and so
|E(\Gamma_{\sigma})|=r-2
, which implies that
\dim\operatorname{coker}\,\mathrm{gl}_{\sigma}\otimes\mathbb{Q}=0
by (
1.8
).

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint and

(d-2)
-dimensional face of

Remark 1.25
.

The tropical problem, the coefficient
k_{\sigma}
, and the tropical multiplicity
N_{\sigma}^{\mathrm{trop}}
only depend on the vectors
\iota_{\gamma_{i}}\omega
and on the hyperplanes
\gamma_{i}^{\perp}
. In particular, they are invariant under a common rescaling of
\gamma_{i}
and
\omega
of the form
\gamma_{i}\mapsto t\gamma_{i}
and
\omega\mapsto t^{-1}\omega
. As a consistency check, one can verify directly that the right-hand side of (
1.18
) in Lemma
1.24
is invariant under such rescaling: a trivalent tree with

legs has

vertices, and so the right-hand side of (

) scales as
\frac{t(t^{-1}t^{2})^{r-1}}{t^{r}}=1
.

1.7.
Polyhedral decompositions and tropical multiplicities

Until now, we considered tropical curves in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
. In this section, we introduce tropical curves in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
endowed with a polyhedral decomposition.
The following definition can be found in
[
40
, Def 3.1]
.

Definition 1.26
.

A
polyhedral decomposition
of
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is a
covering
\mathscr{P}=\{\Xi\}
of
M_{\mathbb{R}}
by a finite number of strongly
convex polyhedra satisfying the following properties:

(i)

If
\Xi\in\mathscr{P}
and
\Xi^{\prime}\subset\Xi

is a face, then
\Xi^{\prime}\in\mathscr{P}
.

(ii)

If
\Xi,\Xi^{\prime}\in\mathscr{P}
, then
\Xi\cap\Xi^{\prime}

is a common face of
\Xi
and
\Xi^{\prime}
.

For the remaining of this section, we fix a polyhedral decomposition
\mathscr{P}
of
M_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 1.27
.

A
parametrized tropical curve in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})

is a weighted graph
\Gamma
together with a proper continuous map
h:\Gamma\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}

satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

for every edge or leg
E
, the restriction
h|_{E}
is an embedding with image contained in an affine line with rational slope,

(ii)

for every vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
, the following
balancing condition
holds: Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}\in E(\Gamma)\cup L(\Gamma)
be the edges or legs adjacent to
v
, and let
\bar{u}_{i}\in M
be the primitive integral vector emanating from
h(v)
in the direction of
h(E_{i})
, then
\sum_{i=1}^{m}w(E_{i})\bar{u}_{i}=0
.

(iii)

for every edge or leg
E
, there exists a cell of
\mathscr{P}
containing
h(E)
,

(iv)

for every divalent vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
, denoting by
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)
the smallest cell of
\mathscr{P}
containing
h(v)
, there exists an open subset of
\Gamma
containing
v
such that
h(U)\cap\boldsymbol{\sigma}(v)=\{h(v)\}
.

We define as in §1.1 a tropical curve in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
as an isomorphism class of parametrized tropical curves in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
.
Compared with tropical curves in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
of Definition
1.2
, tropical curves in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
are required to be compatible with the polyhedral decomposition
\mathscr{P}
in the sense of Definition
1.27
(iii), and can have divalent vertices, which are required to satisfy Definition
1.27
(iv).

Definition 1.28
.

A
tropical type
for
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
is the data
(\Gamma,\bar{u},\boldsymbol{\sigma})
of a weighted graph
\Gamma
, a map

\bar{u}:F(\Gamma)\longrightarrow M
as in
Definition
1.3
, and a map
\boldsymbol{\sigma}\rightarrow\mathscr{P}
assigning a cell of
\mathscr{P}
to every vertex, edge or leg of
\Gamma
.

Definition 1.29
.

The type of a tropical curve
h\colon\Gamma\rightarrow M_{\mathbb{R}}
in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
is the tropical type
(\Gamma,\bar{u},\boldsymbol{\sigma})
where for every
(v,E)\in F(\Gamma)
,
\bar{u}_{v,E}
is the primitive integral in
M
emanating from
h(v)
in the direction of
h(E)
, and for every vertex, edge or leg
x
of
\Gamma
,
\boldsymbol{\sigma}(x)
is the smallest cell of
\mathscr{P}
containing
x
.

One defines as in §
1.2
-
1.3
the notion of
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-tropical curves in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
, the notion of
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-tropical curves in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
matching a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint
\mathbf{A}
, and the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{trop}}(\mathscr{P})
the moduli space of
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-tropical curves in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
matching
\mathbf{A}
. As the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}
of tropical curves in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
, the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}(\mathscr{P})
is a polyhedral complex: the relative interior
\operatorname{Int}(\sigma)
of a face
\sigma\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}(\mathscr{P})
parametrizes
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-tropical curves

in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
matching

\mathbf{A}
and of a given type for
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
.

In fact, there is a natural homeomorphism
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}(\mathscr{P})\simeq{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}
realizing
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}(\mathscr{P})
as a polyhedral refinement of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}
: every face of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}
is a union of faces
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}(\mathscr{P})
. Indeed, given a tropical curve in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
, we obtain a tropical curve in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
by “erasing” the divalent vertices, and conversely, given a tropical curve in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
there is a unique minimal way to add divalent vertices so that Definition
1.27
(iii) is satisfied and we obtain a tropical curve in
(M_{\mathbb{R}},\mathscr{P})
.

However, the identification
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}(\mathscr{P})\simeq{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}

does not preserve the integral structures in general: if
\tilde{\sigma}
is a face of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}(\mathscr{P})
contained in a face
\sigma
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma},\mathbf{A}}

of the same dimension, then there is a natural lattice embedding of integral tangent spaces
T_{\tilde{\sigma}}\subset T_{\sigma}

which can have a non-trivial finite cokernel.
The issue is that when adding a divalent vertex on a edge in a family of tropical curves, the lengths of the newly created edges are affine functions on the base of the family which might not be integral with respect to the original integral structure on the base: one needs in general to coarsen the integral structure on the base to make all length functions integral affine.
In the general context of tropicalizations of stable log maps, this phenomenon is discussed in
[
27
, §4]
.
When
\mathbf{A}
is a general
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint,
\sigma

(d-2)

and

.

Recall from (

of the locus of

for

We define similarly

For

We assume basic familiarity with log

A

together with a homomorphism of sheaves of multiplicative monoids

\alpha_{X}:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}

. The standard notation we use for a log scheme is

, where by
\underline{X}
we denote the underlying scheme. By abuse of notation we also denote the underlying scheme just by
X
when it is clear from the context. Throughout this paper, we assume that all log schemes are fine and saturated (fs)

[
41
, I,§1.3]
. A
morphism of log schemes

consists of an
ordinary morphism

of schemes along with
a map

which is compatible with

via the structure homomorphisms

\alpha_{X}
and
\alpha_{Y}
.

The

to the description of the

of a log scheme

for details. Briefly,

which leads dually to a map

The condition of being fine and saturated implies this is an inclusion
of faces. Note that each cone

comes with a

Tropicalization is functorial, with

inducing

and

Then, it follows from

4.1.
Attractor flow trees and tropical curves

Let
\omega
be a skew-symmetric form on
N

r

\boldsymbol{\gamma}=(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{r})

\gamma_{i}\in N
such that

Definition 4.1
.

A
rooted tree
is a genus
0

root
and denoted by
R_{T}
.

Given a rooted tree

with

decorated by

, we define for every edge or leg

of
T

\gamma_{E}
of

by
\gamma_{E}=\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}
, where the sum is over the indices

such that the leg
L_{i}
is a descendant of

, that is such that
E
is in the path from the root to
L_{i}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Fix a point
\theta\in\gamma^{\perp}
and a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint
\mathbf{A}
.
A parametrized
(\omega,\boldsymbol{\gamma})
-marked

with root
\theta

(i)

is a rooted tree with
r

\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{r}
, such that, for every edge of leg

, and for every vertex

distinct from the root

,

and
E_{2}

adjacent to

such that

(ii)

is a proper continuous map such that
h(R_{T})=\theta
,
h(L_{i})\subset A_{i}
for every
1\leq i\leq r
, and for every edge or leg
E
, the restriction
h|_{E}
is an embedding with image contained in an affine line of oriented direction
\iota_{\gamma_{E}}\omega
, where the orientation is defined following the flow starting at the root of
T
and ending at the leaves.

An isomorphism of parameterized attractor flow tree

and

respecting the marking of the legs, the weights of the edges and legs, and such that

is an isomorphism class of parameterized attractor flow trees.

-marked attractor flow trees with root

.

-marked attractor flow tree with root

and matching
\mathbf{A}
. We denote by

be the graph obtained from

by removing the root and viewing the edge

of

of

. We extend the map

to a half-line in the direction of

By construction,

matching

, and such that

.

.

Fix a point
\theta\in\gamma^{\perp}
and a
\boldsymbol{\gamma}
-constraint
\mathbf{A}
.

Fix a
\mathbf{\gamma}
-constraint

\mathbf{A}

Appendix A
Generic log smoothness

The classical generic smoothness (or Bertini-Sard) theorem states that given
X
and
Y
two varieties over a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic 0, and a morphism
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
, if
X
is smooth over
\Bbbk
, then there exists an open dense subset
U
of
Y
such that for every
u\in U
, the fiber
f^{-1}(u)
is smooth over
\Bbbk
(see e.g.
[
26
, Corollary 10.7]
). We give below a logarithmic version of this result.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
\Bbbk
be a field of characteristic
0
. Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a morphism between fine and saturated log schemes over
\Bbbk
. Assume that
X
is log smooth over the trivial log point
(\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk,\Bbbk^{\star})
, that the scheme underlying
Y
is integral and that the log structure on
Y
is generically trivial. Then, there exists an open dense subset
U
of
Y
such that for every
u\in U
the fiber
f^{-1}(u)
, endowed with the log structure restricted from
X
, is log smooth over the trivial log point
(\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk(u),\Bbbk(u)^{\star})
, where
\Bbbk(u)
is the residue field of
u
.

Proof.

As
X
is log smooth over the trivial log point
(\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk,\Bbbk^{\star})
,
X
is log regular in the sense of
[
31
, (2.1)]
by
[
31
, Proposition 8.3 (1)]
. By
[
31
, Proposition 7.2]
the localization of a log regular log scheme is log regular. So, the generic fiber
X_{\eta}
of
X
over the generic point
\eta
of
Y
is also log regular. As
\Bbbk
is of characteristic
0
, the function field
\Bbbk(\eta)
of
Y
is also of characteristic zero, and so
X_{\eta}
log regular implies that
X_{\eta}
is log smooth over the trivial log point
(\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk(\eta),\Bbbk(\eta)^{\star})
by
[
31
, Proposition 8.3 (2)]
. As the log structure on
Y
is assumed to be generically trivial,
(\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk(\eta),\Bbbk(\eta)^{\star})
is the generic point of
Y
endowed with the log structure restricted from
Y
, and so there exists an open dense subset
V
of
Y
such that
f|_{f^{-1}(V)}
is log smooth by the generic smoothness result of
[
35
, §1.8]
.
On the other hand, applying
[
35
, §1.8]
to
Y
over
(\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk,\Bbbk^{\star})
, one deduces that there exists an open dense subset
W
of
Y
such that the log structure of
Y
is trivial in restriction to
W
. The intersection
U:=V\cap W
is an open dense subset of
Y
with trivial log structure such that
f|_{f^{-1}(U)}
is log smooth. Hence, for every
u\in U
, the fiber
f^{-1}(u)
is log smooth over the trivial log point
(\operatorname{Spec}\Bbbk(u),\Bbbk(u)^{\star})
.

Boundary representations of hyperbolic groups: the log–Sobolev case
Kevin Boucher, Ján Špakula

Abstract
. We study boundary representations of hyperbolic groups Γ \Gamma on the (compactly embedded) function space W log , 2 ​ ( ∂ Γ ) ⊂ L 2 ​ ( ∂ Γ ) W^{\log,2}(\partial\Gamma)\subset L^{2}(\partial\Gamma) , the domain of the logarithmic Laplacian on ∂ Γ \partial\Gamma . We show that they are not uniformly bounded, and establish their exact growth (up a multiplicative constant): they grow with the square root of the length of g ∈ Γ g\in\Gamma . We also obtain L p L^{p} –analogue of this result. Our main tool is a logarithmic Sobolev inequality on bounded Ahlfors–David regular metric measure spaces.

1.
Introduction

To state the main results of this paper, let us start with a Gromov hyperbolic group
\Gamma
, acting properly, cocompactly, and by isometries, on a Gromov hyperbolic space
(X,\rho)
. The Gromov boundary
\partial X
can be endowed with a visual metric
d
, and a Radon measure
\nu
, such that action of
\Gamma
on
\partial X
preserves the class of
\nu
(i.e. maps sets of measure zero to sets of measure zero), hence the Radon–Nikodym derivative of the action of any
g\in\Gamma
exists. Thus, for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
0\leq\re(z)\leq 1
, the formula

(1)

\pi_{z}(g)\phi(\xi)=\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}g_{*}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\nu}\right)^{z}\!(\xi)\cdot\phi(g^{-1}\xi)

where
\xi\in\partial X
,
\phi:\partial X\to\mathbb{C}
,
g\in\Gamma
, formally defines a representation of
\Gamma
on functions on
\partial X
. These are the so-called
boundary representations
.

In this paper, we focus on the case
\re(z)=\frac{1}{2}
— these are of course isometric on
L^{2}
; in the context of Lie groups they would be referred to as ”principal series representations”. The representation
\pi_{\frac{1}{2}}
is sometimes referred to as the Koopman representation, or the quasi–regular representation. Nevertheless, there is a (compactly embedded) dense subspace of
L^{2}(\partial X)
which is preserved under the representation, namely the domain of the so-called logarithmic Laplacian
[
8
]
; we denote this space
W^{\log,2}(\partial X)
in this paper. We are interested in norm bounds for
\pi_{z}
for the natural norm on
W^{\log,2}
(which makes it into a Hilbert space).
We also address the
L^{p}
–analogue of this setup,
p\geq 1
.

Let us provide some context for considering the space
W^{\log,2}
. On
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there are fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,2}
for
s\in(0,1)
which interpolate between
L^{2}
and the standard
W^{1,2}
. One way to describe the norm on
W^{s,2}
is by way of a non-local Dirichlet form given by the convolution kernel
|\cdot|^{-n-2s}
. See for example
[
5
, Section 2]
. When appropriately normalised, the norms indeed converge to the
L^{2}
and
W^{1,2}
norms for
s\to 0^{+}
and
s\to 1^{-}
respectively (see e.g.
[
5
, p.9]
and references therein). However when the norms are
not
normalised, for
s\to 0^{+}
they converge to the norm coming from the kernel
|\cdot-\cdot|^{-n}
, leading to the function space that we refer to
W^{\log,2}
in this paper, cf. (
3
) below.

In
[
3
]
the authors considered boundary representations on the fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(\partial X)
and proved that they are uniformly bounded. This piece deals with the ‘limit case’ of
s=0
.

For our results, we need the setting to be a little nicer than an arbitrary Gromov hyperbolic space
(X,\rho)
.
Any Gromov hyperbolic group
\Gamma
acts properly and cocompactly on a
strongly hyperbolic space

(X,\rho)
(with some parameter
\epsilon>0
), cf.
[
12
]
and references therein.
One of the main features of strong hyperbolicity that we need is its
\text{CAT}(-1)
–like behaviour at infinity: if
o,x,y\in X
, denote the Gromov product by
\langle x,y\rangle_{o}=\frac{1}{2}(\rho(o,x)+\rho(o,y)-\rho(x,y))
. Then
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{o}
extends continuously to
\partial X
and the formula
d(\xi,\eta)=\exp(-\epsilon\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{o})
,
\xi,\eta\in\partial X
, defines a metric on
\partial X
. This is not generally true when
X
is not-strongly hyperbolic.

Finally, let
D
be the Hausdorff dimension of
(\partial X,d)
, and
\nu
be the
D
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\partial X
. Then
\nu
is
Ahlfors–David
D
-regular
, i.e.
\nu(B(\xi,r))\asymp r^{D}
for
\xi\in\partial X
,
r\in[0,\diam(\partial X)]
and
B(\xi,r)
denoting the closed ball about
\xi
with radius
r
. This is true whenever
X
is hyperbolic
[
4
]
.

We record this setup as an Assumption for easy reference.

Let
\Gamma
be a non–elementary Gromov hyperbolic group, acting properly and cocompactly on a roughly geodesic, strongly hyperbolic space

(with parameter

, which is Ahlfors–David

Note that under this Assumption,

Theorem 1.1
.

In the setting of Assumption
(Hyp)
, let
1\leq p<\infty
and

. Then we have
\|\pi^{(p)}_{it}(g)\mid W^{\log,p}\to W^{\log,p}\|^{p}\asymp_{t}1+\rho(go,o)
for any

.

This is a precise estimate — however one can show that these representations can not be uniformly bounded by a more general argument, as follows.
We observe in Proposition
1.2
below that under mild hypotheses, a compactly embedded sub-representation of a uniformly bounded representation can not be uniformly bounded. Let us recall some terminology first:
We say that a representation
\pi:\Gamma\to\mathcal{B}(V)
of a group
\Gamma
on a Banach space
V
is
uniformly bounded

\sup_{g\in G}\|\pi(g)\|<\infty
. We say that
\pi
is
weakly mixing
, if for any
\theta\in V^{*}
(the topological dual of
V
), any
v\in V
, and any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
g\in\Gamma
such that
|\theta(\pi(g)v)|<\varepsilon
. We say that
\pi
is
mixing
, if
|\Gamma|=\infty
and for any
\theta\in V^{*}
and any
v\in V
, we have
\lim_{g\to\infty}\theta(\pi(g)v)=0
.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\pi:G\to\mathcal{B}(V)
be a weakly mixing, uniformly bounded representation of a group
G
on a Banach space
V
with a separable dual
V^{*}
.
Suppose that
W\subset V
is preserved under
\pi
, and is endowed with a norm such that the inclusion
W\hookrightarrow V
is compact.
Then
\pi
is not uniformly bounded on
W
.

Remark 1.3
.

By a similar argument as our proof of the above Proposition, one can prove the following variant: assume that
\pi
is mixing and uniformly bounded,
V
is an arbitrary Banach space, and
W\hookrightarrow V
is compact.
Then
\lim_{g\to\infty}\|\pi(g)\mid W\to W\|=\infty
.

The above Proposition applies to our setup for
1<p<\infty
, since
\pi_{it}^{(p)}
is isometric on

(cf. (
25
)), mixing, and
W^{\log,p}
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}
, as detailed in the Propositions below.
We discuss the case
p=1
in Section
9
.
We also note that while
\pi^{(p)}_{it}
is isometric on
L^{p}
, the representation grows exponentially when considered on
L^{r}
for

, see Proposition
8.3
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Under Assumption
(Hyp)
, for any
1<p<\infty
and

, the representation
\pi^{(p)}_{it}
is mixing on
L^{p}(\partial X)
.
The representation
\pi^{(1)}_{0}
is weakly mixing on the subspace

of

consisting of functions with zero average.

Proposition 1.5
.

In the setting of Assumption
(Hyp)
, let

. Then

is compactly embedded in

.

In fact this is true more generally, Assumption

Finally, as we require this for the proof of Theorem

Setup

The results in this paper hold in varying degree of generality, so let us record the assumptions here, in decreasing strength. The strongest setup is that of the above Assumption
(Hyp)
; however in some Sections we in fact do not need the ”inside” (i.e. the space
(X,\rho)
), but only a metric measure space
Z
thought of as ”a boundary” (i.e.
Z=\partial X
in
(Hyp)
).

Assumption (Haus)
.

Let
(Z,d)
be a bounded metric space of Hausdorff dimension
D
, such that its
D
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
\nu
is Ahlfors–David
D
-regular.

This is provides sufficient link between the measure and the metric to perform some calculations involving the Möbius self-maps of
Z
. (For example, the uniform boundedness results of
[
3
]
apply in this generality.) Let us recall the formulas we need.

Recall that a Möbius map is a function
g:(Z,d)\to(Y,d^{\prime})
between metric space which preserves cross-ratios, i.e. the quantity
[x,y;z,w]=\frac{d(x,z)d(y,w)}{d(x,w)d(y,z)}
, whenever this expression makes sense.
We denote by
\Mob(Z)
the group of self-homeomorphisms of
(Z,d)
. For any
g\in\Mob(Z)
there exists a
metric derivative
of
g
, that is, a Lipschitz function
|g^{\prime}|:Z\to(0,\infty)
satisfying the
geometric mean value property

(4)

d^{2}(g\xi,g\eta)=|g^{\prime}|(\xi)\,|g^{\prime}|(\eta)\,d^{2}(\xi,\eta)\qquad\text{for all }g\in\Mob(Z),\,\xi,\eta\in Z.

see e.g.
[
11
, Lemmas 6, 7]
(cf. also
[
15
]
). Now, if
\nu
is the
D
-dimensional Hausdorff measure which is also Ahlfors regular,
g
preserves the class of
\nu
and (a power of)
|g^{\prime}|
gives the Radon–Nikodym derivative of the action (see e.g.
[
11
, Lemma 8]
):

(5)

\mathrm{d}\nu(g\xi)=|g^{\prime}|^{D}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\qquad\text{for all }g\in\Mob(Z)\text{ and }\xi\in Z.

We further have a cocycle formula

(6)

|(gh)^{\prime}|(\xi)=|g^{\prime}|(h\xi)\,|h^{\prime}|(\xi)\qquad\text{for all }g,h\in\Mob(Z)\text{ and }\xi\in Z.

Finally, the function spaces, and the various Sobolev inequalities, work even more generally:

Assumption (Ahlf)

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a bounded metric measure space, which is Ahlfors–David
D
-regular (for some
D\geq 0
).

2.3.
Strong hyperbolicity

Let us return to the setting of Assumption
(Hyp)
and be more precise. Let
(X,\rho)
be a metric space. It is
roughly geodesic
if there exists
C\geq 0
such that for all
x,y\in X
there exists a function
\gamma:[a,b]\to X
with
\gamma(a)=x
,
\gamma(b)=y
, and for all
s,s^{\prime}\in[a,b]
one has
|s-s^{\prime}|-C\leq\rho(\gamma(s),\gamma(s^{\prime}))\leq|s-s^{\prime}|+C
.

A metric space
(X,\rho)
is
strongly hyperbolic
with parameter
\epsilon>0
if
\exp(-\epsilon\langle x,y\rangle_{o})\leq\exp(-\epsilon\langle x,z\rangle_{o})+\exp(-\epsilon\langle z,y\rangle_{o})
for all
x,y,z,o\in X

[
12
, Definition 4.1]
. Fix
o\in X
. As mentioned above, then
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{o}
extends continuously to
X\cup\partial X
, and
d(\xi,\eta)=\exp(-\epsilon\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{o})
defines a metric on
\partial X

[
12
, Theorem 4.2]
.

In this setting, we have a formula for
|g^{\prime}|
. First the Busemann function: for
\xi\in\partial X
,
x,o\in X
, denote
\beta(\xi,x,o)=-2\langle\xi,x\rangle_{o}+\rho(x,o)
(secretly thinking it ”equals
\rho(\xi,x)-\rho(\xi,o)
”). Then for
g\in\Gamma
we have
|g^{\prime}|(\xi)=\exp(-\epsilon\beta(\xi,go,o))=\exp(2\epsilon\langle\xi,go\rangle_{o}-\epsilon\rho(go,o))

[
12
, Section 6.2]
.

We record a fact well–known to experts, and for the sake of completeness also a short proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
(Hyp)
. Then there exist
M\geq 0
such that for any
g\in\Gamma
there exists
\xi\in\partial X
such that
|g|-M\leq\langle\xi,go\rangle_{o}\leq|g|
.

Proof.

The second inequality (for any
\xi\in\partial X
) follows immediately from the definition of Gromov product.

For the first inequality, fix
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\partial X
,
\eta_{1}\not=\eta_{2}
; this is possible since the action of
\Gamma
is assumed to be non-elementary.
A direct calculation shows that for any
g\in\Gamma,\omega\in X
, we have
\langle go,\omega\rangle_{o}+\langle g^{-1}o,g^{-1}\omega\rangle_{o}=|g|
, and by continuity of the Gromov product on
X\cup\partial X
this holds for
\omega\in\partial X
as well.
Hence

\max(\langle go,g\eta_{1}\rangle_{o},\langle go,g\eta_{2}\rangle_{o})=|g|-\min(\langle g^{-1}o,\eta_{1}\rangle_{o},\langle g^{-1}o,\eta_{2}\rangle_{o})\geq|g|-\langle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle_{o}-\delta,

where
\delta\geq 0
is the hyperbolicity constant. Hence, choosing
\xi=g\eta_{i}
which realises
\max_{i=1,2}\langle go,g\eta_{i}\rangle_{o}
, the claim follows with
M=\langle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle_{o}+\delta
.
∎

2.4.
Zygmund/Orlicz spaces

We shall need Zygmund spaces
L^{p}\log L
(
p\geq 1
) and
L_{\exp}
; they are also instances of more general Orlicz spaces. We recall the definitions and some facts about them. Let
(Z,\nu)
be a measure space, with
\nu(Z)<\infty
.

For
p\geq 1
, denote
\Phi_{p}(t)=|t|^{p}\log^{+}|t|^{p}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\log^{+}(s)=\max(0,\log_{e}(s))
,
\log^{+}(0)=0
. Also denote
\Psi(t)=|t|
for
t\in(-1,1)
and
\Psi(t)=\exp(|t|-1)
for
|t|\geq 1
. These are examples of the so-called Young functions in the theory of Orlicz spaces. Let
\Phi
be one of the functions defined in this paragraph.

The
Orlicz space

L_{\Phi}(\nu)
is the space of (a.e.–equivalence classes of) measurable functions
f:Z\to\mathbb{C}
, such that
\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\Phi(|f|/k)\,\mathrm{d}\nu<\infty
for some
k>0

[
13
, III]
. When
\Phi=\Phi_{1}
above, the resulting space is also referred to as a Zygmund space
L\log L
; we shall use this notation for it. When
\Phi=\Phi_{p}
, we will use the notation
L^{p}\log L
, and when
\Phi=\Psi
we write
L_{\exp}
; the measure space
(Z,\nu)
being understood from the context.

There are several ways to define (equivalent) norms on
L_{\Phi}(\nu)
, we shall use the Luxemburg norm
[
13
, Theorem III.2.3]
: for
f\in L_{\Phi}(\nu)
, we put

(7)

\|f|L_{\Phi}\|=\inf\big\{k\geq 0\mid\uprightintop\displaylimits\nolimits_{Z}\Phi(|f|/k)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq 1\big\}.

An immediate consequence of this definition is that if
f\in L^{p}\log L
, then
f^{p}\in L\log L
and

(8)

\|f^{p}|L\log L\|=\|f|L^{p}\log L\|^{p}.

Note that if
\nu(Z)<\infty
and
p>1
, then
L^{\infty}\subseteq L_{\exp}\subseteq L^{p}\log L\subseteq L^{p}\subseteq L\log L\subseteq L^{1}
. For example, the first inclusion when
\nu(Z)=1
follows by observing that
\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\Psi(|f|/k)\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq\Psi\!\left(\|f|L^{\infty}\|/k\right)
. Since
\Psi(1)=1
, we obtain that
\|f|L_{\exp}\|\leq\|f|L^{\infty}\|
.

Finally,
\Phi_{1}
and
\Psi
form a so-called (normalised)
complementary Young pair

[
13
, I.3 and II.4]
, and hence a generalised Hölder’s inequality holds
[
13
, Proposition III.3.1]
:

Proposition 2.2
.

If
f\in L\log L
and
g\in L_{\exp}
, then

(9)

\uprightintop\displaylimits\nolimits_{Z}|fg|\,\mathrm{d}\nu\leq\|f|L\log L\|\cdot\|g|L_{\exp}\|.

3.
Basic calculation

In this section we go through a calculation which shows how to reduce bounding the representation
\pi^{(p)}_{it}
to having an appropriate Sobolev inequality and bounding certain multipliers. (Somewhat similar calculations has been used before; the earliest place the authors are aware of is
[
10
]
.)

Proposition 3.1
.

In the setting of Assumption
(Haus)
, let
p\geq 1
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
\phi\in W^{\log,p}(Z)
and
g\in\Mob(Z)
. Then

(10)

\mathcal{E}_{\log,p}\big(\pi^{(p)}_{it}(g)\phi\big)\leq C_{1}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\log,p}(\phi)+C_{2}\cdot\left\|\Theta_{g,t}|L_{\exp}\right\|\cdot\left\|\phi|L^{p}\log L\right\|^{p},

where

(11)

\Theta_{g,t}(\omega)=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\frac{\left|\frac{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\zeta)}{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\omega)}-1\right|^{p}}{d^{D}(\omega,\zeta)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\zeta).

To proceed from this result towards a norm bound for the representation, the
L^{p}\log L
term will be controlled by
\|\phi|W^{\log,p}\|^{p}
using the logarithmic Sobolev inequality (Corollary
4.2
). We refer to the control on the
L_{\exp}
term as ”geometric control”, as it requires knowing something about the behaviour of metric derivatives
|g^{\prime}|
.
In the setting of Assumption
(Hyp)
, we obtain geometric control which is linear in
\rho(go,o)
in Section
5
, Corollary
5.2
.

Remark 3.2
.

For comparison, a very much analogous calculation can be performed to show that
\pi_{s+it}^{(p)}
is uniformly bounded on fractional Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(Z)
(for
sp<D
,
0<s<1
), cf.
[
3
, Section 7]
. In that context, for the final steps one uses a dual pair of Lorentz spaces
L(\frac{D}{sp},\infty)
and
L(\frac{D}{D-sp},1)
, fractional Sobolev inequality, and can establish a uniform geometric control.

Proof.

We calculate:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\log,p}\big(\pi^{(p)}_{it}(g)\phi\big)

\displaystyle=\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{|\pi^{(p)}_{it}(g)\phi(\xi)-\pi^{(p)}_{it}(g)\phi(\eta)|^{p}}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)

\displaystyle=\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{\big||(g^{-1})^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{p}-it}(\xi)\phi(g^{-1}\xi)-|(g^{-1})^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{p}-it}(\eta)\phi(g^{-1}\eta)\big|^{p}}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)

\displaystyle=\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{\big||g^{\prime}|^{-\frac{D}{p}+it}(\omega)\phi(\omega)-|g^{\prime}|^{-\frac{D}{p}+it}(\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\big|^{p}}{d^{D}(g\omega,g\zeta)}|g^{\prime}|^{D}(\omega)|g^{\prime}|^{D}(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\nu(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{\big||g^{\prime}|^{-\frac{D}{p}+it}(\omega)\phi(\omega)-|g^{\prime}|^{-\frac{D}{p}+it}(\zeta)\phi(\zeta)\big|^{p}}{d^{D}(\omega,\zeta)}|g^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{2}}(\omega)|g^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{2}}(\zeta)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\nu(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{\left|\frac{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\zeta)}{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\omega)}\phi(\omega)-\frac{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\omega)}{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\zeta)}\phi(\zeta)\right|^{p}}{d^{D}(\omega,\zeta)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\nu(\zeta).

Using that
|x+y+z|^{p}\leq 3^{p-1}(|x|^{p}+|y|^{p}+|z|^{p})
, we continue:

\displaystyle\leq 3^{p-1}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{\log,p}(\phi)+2\cdot 3^{p-1}\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\frac{\left|\frac{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\zeta)}{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\omega)}-1\right|^{p}}{d^{D}(\omega,\zeta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\zeta)\cdot|\phi(\omega)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega).

For the remaining integral, we use the notation
\Theta_{g,t}
from (
11
), and use Hölder’s inequality (
9

\displaystyle\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\frac{\left|\frac{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\zeta)}{|g^{\prime}|^{D/(2p)+it}(\omega)}-1\right|^{p}}{d^{D}(\omega,\zeta)}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\nu(\zeta)\cdot|\phi(\omega)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\Theta_{g,t}(\omega)|\phi(\omega)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Theta_{g,t}|L_{\exp}\right\|\cdot\left\||\phi|^{p}|L\log L\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\Theta_{g,t}|L_{\exp}\right\|\cdot\left\|\phi|L^{p}\log L\right\|^{p}.

This finishes the proof.

The results in this Section hold under Assumption

Theorem 4.1

Let

\uprightintop\displaylimits|f|^{p}\log^{+}\frac{|f|^{p}}{\|f|L^{p}\|^{p}}\mathrm{d}\nu\prec\left\|f|L^{p}\right\|^{p}+\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{\left|f(\xi)-f(\eta)\right|^{p}}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta).

By the definition of Luxemburg norm on the space

Corollary 4.2

(The logarithmic Sobolev inequality)

Under the assumptions of Theorem

To prove Theorem

, we start with an analogue of

, Lemma 6.1]

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a bounded Ahlfors–David
D
-regular metric measure space. Then for any
E\subset Z
and any
\xi\in Z
, we have

\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z\setminus E}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\geq-c\log(\nu(E))+cD\log(\diam(Z))+c\log(c),

where
0<c\leq 1
is the lower regularity constant, i.e.
cr^{D}\leq\nu(B(\cdot,r))
for all
0\leq r\leq\diam(Z)
.

Proof.

With
\alpha=c^{-1/D}
, we have
\nu(B(\xi,\alpha r))\geq c\alpha^{D}r^{D}=r^{D}
.
Denote
B=B(\xi,\alpha r)
with
r=\nu(E)^{\frac{1}{D}}
. Then
\nu(B)\geq\nu(E)
, and so

\displaystyle\nu\left((Z\setminus E)\cap B\right)

\displaystyle=\nu(B)-\nu(E\cap B)\geq\nu(E)-\nu(E\cap B)=\nu\left(E\cap(Z\setminus B)\right).

It follows that

\displaystyle\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z\setminus E}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}

\displaystyle=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{(Z\setminus E)\cap B}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}+\uprightintop\displaylimits_{(Z\setminus E)\cap(Z\setminus B)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}

\displaystyle\geq(\alpha r)^{-D}\nu\left((Z\setminus E)\cap B\right)+\uprightintop\displaylimits_{(Z\setminus E)\cap(Z\setminus B)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}

\displaystyle\geq(\alpha r)^{-D}\nu\left(E\cap(Z\setminus B)\right)+\uprightintop\displaylimits_{(Z\setminus E)\cap(Z\setminus B)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}

\displaystyle\geq\uprightintop\displaylimits_{E\cap(Z\setminus B)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}+\uprightintop\displaylimits_{(Z\setminus E)\cap(Z\setminus B)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}

\displaystyle=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z\setminus B}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)},

with Lemma
4.4
below, we continue:

\displaystyle\geq cD\left(\log(\diam(Z))-\log(\alpha r)\right)

\displaystyle=cD\log(\diam(Z))-c\log(\nu(E))-cD\log(c^{-1/D}).\qed

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a bounded Ahlfors–David
D
-regular metric measure space. Denote
0<c\leq 1\leq C
to be the regularity constants, i.e.
cr^{D}\leq\nu(B(\cdot,r))\leq Cr^{D}
for
0\leq r\leq\diam(Z)
. Then for any
\xi\in Z
and
0\leq r\leq\diam(Z)
, we have

cD\log\left(\frac{\diam(Z)}{r}\right)\leq\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z\setminus B(\xi,r)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\leq C\left(1+D\log\left(\frac{\diam(Z)}{r}\right)\right).

Proof.

Using the layer cake formula, we have

\displaystyle\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z\setminus B(\xi,r)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}

\displaystyle=\uprightintop\displaylimits\nolimits_{\diam(Z)^{-D}}^{r^{-D}}\nu\!\left(\{d^{-D}(\xi,\cdot)>t\}\right)\mathrm{d}t+\uprightintop\displaylimits\nolimits_{0}^{\diam(Z)^{-D}}\nu\!\left(Z\setminus B(\xi,r)\right)\mathrm{d}t.

The latter integral equals

\diam(Z)^{-D}\cdot\nu(Z\setminus B(\xi,r))\leq\diam(Z)^{-D}\cdot\nu(Z)\leq C.

For the former, we calculate:

\displaystyle\uprightintop\displaylimits\nolimits_{\diam(Z)^{-D}}^{r^{-D}}\nu\!\left(\{d^{-D}(\xi,\cdot)>t\}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\uprightintop\displaylimits\nolimits_{\diam(Z)^{-D}}^{r^{-D}}\nu\!\left(\{d(\xi,\cdot)<t^{-1/D}\}\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\asymp\uprightintop\displaylimits\nolimits_{\diam(Z)^{-D}}^{r^{-D}}t^{-1}\mathrm{d}t=\left[\log(t)\right]_{\diam(Z)^{-D}}^{r^{-D}}

\displaystyle=\log(\diam(Z)^{D})-\log(r^{D})=D\log(\diam(Z)/r),

where
\asymp
uses Ahlfors regularity.
The claim now follows.
∎

d(a,\eta)\leq d(a,\xi)+d(\xi,\eta)\leq 2d(\xi,\eta)

d(\xi,\eta)\leq d(a,\xi)+d(a,\eta)\leq\frac{3}{2}d(a,\eta)
. Summarising, we have

(21)

\xi\in U(a)\implies d(a,\xi)\prec d(a,\eta)\asymp d(\xi,\eta).

Symmetrically, we have

(22)

\xi\in U(\eta)\implies d(\xi,\eta)\prec d(a,\eta)\asymp d(a,\xi).

Finally, in
W:=Z\setminus(U(a)\cup U(\eta))
, a similar calculation with the triangle inequality gives

(23)

d(a,\eta)\leq 2d(a,\xi)\asymp d(\xi,\eta).

For easy reference, recall that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{C}
we have

(24)

|x-y|^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}(|x|^{p}+|y|^{p}).

We now estimate the integral in the statement of the Proposition on three subsets of
Z
.

On
W
, we have that
\frac{d(a,\eta)}{d(a,\xi)}\leq 2
. We use (
24
) and Lemma
4.4
to estimate

\displaystyle\uprightintop\displaylimits_{W}\frac{\left|1-\big[\frac{d(a,\eta)}{d(a,\xi)}\big]^{D/p+it}\right|^{p}}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\prec\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z\setminus U(\eta)}\frac{1}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\prec 1-\log d(a,\eta).

On
U(a)
, when nonempty, we start with (
24
) again, then use (
21
). For the last step we use Lemma
5.4
and Lemma
5.5
to get

\displaystyle\uprightintop\displaylimits_{U(a)}\frac{\left|1-\big[\frac{d(a,\eta)}{d(a,\xi)}\big]^{D/p+it}\right|^{p}}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\prec\frac{\nu(U(a))}{d^{D}(a,\eta)}+\uprightintop\displaylimits_{U(a)}\frac{1}{d^{D}(a,\xi)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\prec 1+|a|.

Finally, we deal with
U(\eta)
. Here
\frac{d(a,\eta)}{d(a,\xi)}\in[\frac{2}{3},2]
, and so the Mean Value Theorem will imply the first
\prec
in the calculation below. Next, the triangle inequality gives that

\left|1-\tfrac{d(a,\xi)}{d(a,\eta)}\right|\leq\tfrac{d(\xi,\eta)}{d(a,\eta)}.

This, followed by (
22
) and Lemma
5.3
finish the estimate:

\displaystyle\uprightintop\displaylimits_{U(\eta)}\frac{\left|1-\big[\frac{d(a,\eta)}{d(a,\xi)}\big]^{D+it}\right|^{p}}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\prec_{t}\uprightintop\displaylimits_{U(\eta)}\frac{\left|1-\frac{d(a,\eta)}{d(a,\xi)}\right|^{p}}{d^{D}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\uprightintop\displaylimits_{U(\eta)}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)}{d^{p}(a,\xi)d^{D-p}(\xi,\eta)}\prec_{t}1.\qed

Lemma 6.2
.

In the setting of Assumption

, there exists

, such that for any

\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\langle\omega,a\rangle_{o}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\leq C

7.
Compact embedding

We aim for showing that
W^{\log,p}(Z)
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(Z)
for
p>1
. We will use compactness of integral kernel operators given by off-diagonal cut-off kernels
K_{\epsilon}=\chi_{\{(\xi,\omega)\in Z^{2}|d(\xi,\omega)>\epsilon\}}\cdot d^{-D}
for
\epsilon>0
(Proposition
7.2
). Proposition
7.1
shows how can one use this to obtain a compact embedding result, but we need to set up some notation first.

The argument presented here has a similar structure as
[
8
, Subsection 3.1]
, but in a more general setting.

Suppose that
(Z,\nu)
is a probability measure space. For a measurable function
K:Z^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
, we denote the corresponding integral kernel operator by

T_{K}(\phi)(\xi)=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}K(\xi,\omega)\phi(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega).

Next, if
K\geq 0
, for
1\leq p<\infty
we denote the ”energy” by

\mathcal{E}_{p,K}(\phi)=\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\omega)|^{p}K(\xi,\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega),

and finally the ”domain” by

W^{p,K}(Z)=\left\{\phi\in L^{p}(Z)\mid\mathcal{E}_{p.K}(\phi)<\infty\right\}.

When appropriate, we endow
W^{p,K}(Z)
with a norm
\|\phi|W^{p,K}\|^{p}=\|\phi|L^{p}\|^{p}+\mathcal{E}_{p.K}(\phi)
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
(Z,\nu)
be a probability measure space, and let
K:Z^{2}\to[0,\infty)
be locally integrable on
Z^{2}\setminus\{\text{diagonal}\}
. Let
1\leq p<\infty
.

Suppose that there is a sequence
K_{n}:Z^{2}\to[0,\infty]
of kernels, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

(1)

K_{n}\leq K
(pointwise);

(2)

T_{K_{n}}
is a compact operator on
L^{p}(\nu)
;

(3)

denoting
N_{n}(\xi)=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}K_{n}(\xi,\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)
, we have
\ess\inf N_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
;

Then
W^{p,K}
is compactly embedded in
L^{p}(\nu)
.

Proof.

For a.e.
\xi\in Z
, we have that
K_{n}(\xi,\cdot)/N_{n}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\cdot)
is a probability measure on
Z
; in the next calculation, we use Hölder’s inequality for these measures. Let
\phi\in W^{p,K}(Z)\subseteq L^{p}(Z)
. Then

\displaystyle\big\|\phi-\tfrac{1}{N_{n}}T_{K_{n}}(\phi)\mid L^{p}\big\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\Big|\phi(\xi)-\tfrac{1}{N_{n}(\xi)}\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}K_{n}(\xi,\omega)\phi(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\Big|^{p}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\Big|\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\left(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\omega)\right)\cdot\frac{K_{n}(\xi,\omega)}{N_{n}(\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\Big|^{p}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\omega)|^{p}\cdot\frac{K_{n}(\xi,\omega)}{N_{n}(\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq(\ess\inf N_{n})^{-1}\cdot\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\omega)|^{p}K_{n}(\xi,\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\omega)\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq(\ess\inf N_{n})^{-1}\cdot\mathcal{E}_{p,K}(\phi).

Since the constant in the last formula tends to
0
as
n\to\infty
, and
\frac{1}{N_{n}}T_{K_{n}}
is compact, it follows that we can approximate any bounded set in
W^{p,K}(Z)
by a compact set in
L^{p}(Z)
, in
L^{p}
-norm. Hence the inclusion
W^{p,K}(Z)\to L^{p}(Z)
is compact.
∎

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
satisfy
(Ahlf)
, i.e. it is an Ahlfors–David
D
-regular bounded metric measure space. For
\epsilon>0
, denote by
K_{\epsilon}:Z^{2}\to[0,\infty)
the function
K_{\epsilon}=\chi_{\{(\xi,\omega)\in Z^{2}|d(\xi,\omega)>\epsilon\}}\cdot d^{-D}
. Then for any
1<p<\infty
,
T_{K_{\epsilon}}
is compact on
L^{p}(Z)
. Furthermore, the function
N_{\epsilon}
(see
7.1
/(
3
)) satisfies
\ess\inf N_{\epsilon}\to\infty
as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
.

Proof.

As each
K_{\epsilon}
is a bounded function on a finite measure space
Z^{2}
, the integral kernel operators
T_{K_{\epsilon}}
are
(L^{p}-L^{p})
-Hille–Tamarkin operators
[
9
, p. 403]
, and hence compact
[
9
, Theorem 15.15]
. This shows the first claim.

For the second claim, Lemma
4.4
gives
N_{\epsilon}\succ\log(\diam(Z)/\epsilon)\to\infty
as

.
∎

Proposition
1.5
now immediately follows from Propositions
7.1
and
7.2

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

Suppose, for contradiction, that

is uniformly bounded on
W
. Let
w\in W\setminus\{0\}
, and denote
\theta_{0},\theta_{1},\dots
a dense subset of
V^{*}

\theta
at the same time; and use this inductively to obtain a sequence
(g_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}\subset\Gamma
, such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
|\theta_{i}(\pi(g_{n})w)|<\frac{1}{n}
for all

.

As
\pi
is assumed to be uniformly bounded on
W
, the set
\{\pi(g_{n})w|n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded in

, hence pre-compact in

. Thus there exists a subsequence, continued to be denoted
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and a vector
v\in V
, such that
\pi(g_{n})w\to v
in norm in

. As

and
\pi
is uniformly bounded on

is bounded away from

On the other hand, we also have that

for all

. It follows that

by density, which is a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 8.1

Proposition 9.3
.

Proof.

Comment on the Analytical Bounds in the Rezzolla-Zhidenko Parametrization
Prashant Kocherlakota 1 {}^{\text{1}} and Luciano Rezzolla 1,2,3 {}^{\text{1,2,3}}

Abstract
In this short note, we briefly comment on the analytical bounds that must be imposed on the parameter space of the Rezzolla-Zhidenko (RZ) metric-parametrization approach introduced in Ref. Rezzolla and Zhidenko 2014 . We hope this will clarify some of the confusion recently emerged on this issue Heumann and Psaltis 2022 .



Introduction.
The Rezzolla-Zhidenko (RZ) metric parametrization
approach was introduced in Ref.
Rezzolla and Zhidenko 2014
to characterize
efficiently and accurately the exterior horizon geometry of
asymptotically-flat, spherically-symmetric and static black-hole (BH)
spacetimes. This approach has been employed successfully to model a large
number of generic BH spacetimes

As noted in
Ref.
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2020
, the RZ parametrization can trivially be
employed also for spherically symmetric static spacetimes that do not
contain BHs but other compact objects, including nonsingular and singular
black holes, boson stars, and naked singularities.
in terms of a series
of coefficients
\{\epsilon,a_{i},b_{i}\}
and by Padé approximants in
the form of continued fractions (see Ref.
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2020
and
references therein); the approach has also been extended to stationary BH
spacetimes in Ref.
Konoplya et al. 2016
.

Comment.
In the RZ-parametrization approach, the radial
coordinate
r=r_{0}
must,
by construction
, correspond to the
location of the outermost Killing horizon. We recall that spherically
symmetric BH spacetimes admit a time-translation Killing vector
field
\bm{K}
which, in the static (
\bm{K}\wedge\mathrm{\mathbf{d}}\bm{K}=\mathbf{0}
) exterior horizon geometry, can be
identified to be
\bm{K}=\bm{\partial_{t}}
.
\bm{K}
is asymptotically timelike,
i.e.,
g_{tt}<0
, and becomes null on Killing horizons located at

r=r_{\mathrm{kh};k}
, i.e.,
g_{tt}(r_{\mathrm{kh};k})=0
(with
k

indexing the roots of
g_{tt}
). As per the definition above, if
r_{0}
is
the largest, real, positive root, i.e.,
r_{0}=\mathrm{max}\{r_{\mathrm{kh};k},~\forall~k\}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, then
clearly for
\bm{K}
to remain timelike on the exterior horizon
geometry,
N^{2}(r):=-g_{tt}
must be positive-definite on
r>r_{0}
. Therefore, and as already noted in Ref.
Rezzolla and Zhidenko 2014
;
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2020
;
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2022
;
Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration et al. 2022
, this requires that
the following condition must be met

\displaystyle N^{2}=:xA(x,\epsilon,a_{i})>0\,,\qquad\mathrm{for}~0\leq x\leq 1\,,

(1)

where
x:=1-r_{0}/r
is a compactified coordinate such that
x=0

corresponds to the location of the event horizon (
N^{2}(x)=0
) and
x=1

to spatial infinity (we recall that
r_{0}:=2M/(1+\epsilon)
, where
M

is the BH mass). As pointed out already in 2014 (see Eq. (5) of
Ref.
Rezzolla and Zhidenko 2014
), Eq. (
1
) partitions the
relevant parameter space into mathematically “allowed” and
“disallowed” regions, and its impact on the relevant parameter spaces
for various slices of the general RZ metric has
already
been
reported in Table 2 of Ref.
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2022
, but also in
Figs. 3-5 of Ref.
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2022
, where the regions in which

r=r_{0}
does not locate the outermost Killing horizon are shown as gray
regions.

Recently, Ref.
Heumann and Psaltis 2022
has considered a two-parameter
realization of the RZ approach, that is, it has employed the space of
paramters
(r_{0},a_{1})
, or equivalently
(\epsilon,a_{1})
, to express the

g_{tt}
metric function of a generic BH spacetime and solved for all
possible roots of
N^{2}(r,r_{0},a_{1})=0
, which corresponds to a quartic
equation in this specific case. Finding that
r=r_{0}
does not locate the
outermost Killing horizon for arbitrary choices of the RZ parameters,
Ref.
Heumann and Psaltis 2022
partitions the relevant
(r_{0},a_{1})
RZ
parameter space into regions where
r=r_{0}
does in fact locate the
outermost Killing horizon and where it does not, essentially re-iterating
the computation reported in Ref.
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2022
. Indeed,
Eqs. (25) and (26) of Ref.
Heumann and Psaltis 2022
are not new and have
already been reported respectively in the seventh and eight rows of Table
2 of Ref.
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2022
(i.e., the case
\mathscr{M}(\epsilon,a_{1})
); the same table also provides the ranges for other choices of the
parametrization.

Conclusion.
Contrary to the recent claim in
Ref.
Heumann and Psaltis 2022
, the horizon radius in the RZ parameterization
does not change discontinuously. This can happen only if the
parameterization is performed incorrectly outside of the region of
validity required by Eq. (
1
). Such conditions were

already
discussed and published in Refs.
Rezzolla and Zhidenko 2014
;
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2020
;
Kocherlakota and Rezzolla 2022
;
Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration et al. 2022
. Finally, we note
that to ensure that the Ricci and Kretschmann scalars corresponding to
the RZ metric do not diverge outside or on the horizon, the metric
function
B^{2}(r):=g_{rr}(r)N^{2}(r)
must generically be nonvanishing
for
r\geq r_{0}
. This represents another nontrivial constraint on the

b_{i}
coefficients of the RZ parameter space.

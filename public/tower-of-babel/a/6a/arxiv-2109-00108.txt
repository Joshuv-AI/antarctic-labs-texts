Decay estimates for unitary representations with applications to continuous- and discrete-time models
S. Richard, R. Tiedra de Aldecoa

Abstract
We present a new technique to obtain polynomial decay estimates for the matrix coefficients of unitary operators. Our approach, based on commutator methods, applies to nets of unitary operators, unitary representations of topological groups, and unitary operators given by the evolution group of a self-adjoint operator or by powers of a unitary operator. Our results are illustrated with a wide range of examples in quantum mechanics and dynamical systems, as for instance Schrödinger operators, Dirac operators, quantum waveguides, horocycle flows, adjacency matrices, Jacobi matrices, quantum walks or skew products.

In recent papers

be a family of

\mathcal{H}
, let

, let

\lim_{j}|\langle\varphi,U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|=0\quad\hbox{for all $\varphi\in\ker(D)^{\perp}$ and $\psi\in\mathcal{H}$.}

(1.1)

In concrete situations, one usually seeks to get an explicit rate of decay in
estimates like (

) in order to quantify the time propagation of the wave
functions. This problem is a very broad and active field of research, with numerous
results in a variety of setups. Decay of correlations, local decay estimates,
pointwise decay estimates,
\mathrm{L}^{p}
decay estimates, Strichartz estimates,
microlocal estimates, propagation estimates, Morawetz estimates,… all are families
of results related to this problem. In this paper, we pursue the study initiated in

[
34
,
26
]
and determine conditions that guarantee a polynomial rate of
decay in (
1.1
). Our results are general, in the sense that they are stated
first for general nets of unitary operators, then for unitary representations of
topological groups, and finally for unitary operators given by the evolution group

(\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of a self-adjoint operator
H
or by the powers

(U^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of a unitary operator

. Moreover, they apply to a wide range
of models both in quantum mechanics and dynamical systems. And finally, they
generalise to some extent the results of
[
15
]
(see also
[
19
]
)
where the authors use commutator methods to establish abstract pointwise decay
estimates for certain classes of self-adjoint operators. We refer to

[
2

20

for related results about pointwise decay estimates
for self-adjoint and unitary operators.

Let us give a more detailed description of our results. In Section
2.1
,
we introduce our framework and determine sufficient conditions that guarantee a
polynomial decay estimate

|\langle\varphi,U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}^{n}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad\ell_{j}>0,~n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},

with

\varphi,\psi
(and

) but not on

are given by a unitary representation
\mathscr{U}
of a topological group

and the
scalars
\ell_{j}
are given by a proper length function on
X
, then we provide
conditions ensuring that
D
commutes with
\mathscr{U}
and that
\mathscr{U}
has no nontrivial
finite-dimensional unitary subrepresentation in
\ker(D)^{\perp}
(Proposition

These general results are then applied in Sections

In the former case, the operators

and our new results are the fact that

is decomposable in the spectral

In Section

For some of them, the decay estimates we obtain are known, while for others they are

striking feature of our approach does not really rely on any new result for a given
example, but on its broad applicability. The whole variety of examples introduced in
Section

Since Section

2
Decay estimates for unitary representations

2.1
General unitary representations

We start with a general theorem on decay estimates for the matrix coefficients of
unitary operators
U_{j}
in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. In the proof, we use standard results
about commutators of operators recalled in Appendix
A
.

Theorem 2.1

(Decay estimates)
.

Let
(U_{j})_{j\in J}
be a net in
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
, let
(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}\subset[0,\infty)

satisfy
\ell_{j}\to\infty
, assume there exists a self-adjoint operator
A
in
\mathcal{H}

such that
U_{j}\in C^{1}(A)
for each
j\in J
, and suppose that the strong limit

D:=\slim_{j}D_{j}\quad\hbox{with}\quad D_{j}:=\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}[A,U_{j}]U_{j}^{-1}

exists. Then

(a)

For each
\varphi=D\widetilde{\varphi}\in D\mathcal{D}(A)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
there
exists a constant
c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

|\langle\varphi,U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq\|(D-D_{j})\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad\ell_{j}>0.

In particular,
\lim_{j}\langle\xi,U_{j}\zeta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=0
for all
\xi\in\ker(D)^{\perp}

and
\zeta\in\mathcal{H}
.

(b)

Assume that
D=D_{j}
for all
j\in J
. Then for each
\varphi\in D\mathcal{D}(A)

and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
there exists a constant
c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

|\langle\varphi,U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad\ell_{j}>0.

(c)

Assume that
D=D_{j}
for all
j\in J
, that
D\in C^{1}(A)
, and that

[A,D]=DB
with
B\in C^{(n-1)}(A)
(
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
) and
[D,B]=0
. Then for each

\varphi\in D^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
there exists a constant

c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

|\langle\varphi,U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}^{n}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad\ell_{j}>0.

Proof.

(a) Take
\varphi=D\widetilde{\varphi}\in D\mathcal{D}(A)
,
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
, and
j\in J
such
that
\ell_{j}>0
. Then we have

\displaystyle|\langle\varphi,U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|

\displaystyle=\big|\langle(D-D_{j})\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}+\langle D_{j}\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|(D-D_{j})\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\!\;\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\big|\langle[A,U_{j}]U_{j}^{-1}\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|(D-D_{j})\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\big|\langle A\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|+\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\big|\langle\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}A\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|(D-D_{j})\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi}

with

c_{\varphi,\psi}:=\|A\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\|A\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}
.
This proves the first part of the claim. Since
D=\slim_{j}D_{j}
and
\ell_{j}\to\infty
,
we infer that
\lim_{j}\langle\varphi,U_{j}\psi\rangle=0
, and thus the second part of the
claim follows by the density of
D\mathcal{D}(A)
in
\overline{D\mathcal{H}}=\ker(D)^{\perp}
and the
density of
\mathcal{D}(A)
in
\mathcal{H}
.

(b) The claim is a direct consequence of point (a) in the case
D=D_{j}
for all

j\in J
.

(c) We prove the claim by induction on
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. For
n=1
, the claim is true due to
point (b). For
n-1\geq 1
, we make the induction hypothesis that the claim is true. For

n
, we take
\varphi\in D^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
,
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
and
j\in J
such that

\ell_{j}>0
. Then, since
D=D_{j}
and
\varphi=D\widetilde{\varphi}
with

\widetilde{\varphi}\in D^{n-1}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
, we get

\langle\varphi,U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\langle D_{j}\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\langle A\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\langle\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}A\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.1)

The induction hypothesis applies to the second term in (
2.1
) since

\widetilde{\varphi}\in D^{n-1}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})\subset D^{n-1}\mathcal{D}(A^{n-1})
and

A\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n-1})
. So there exists
c_{\widetilde{\varphi},A\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}A\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}^{n-1}}\;\!c_{\widetilde{\varphi},A\psi}.

(2.2)

For the first term in (
2.1
), we have

\widetilde{\varphi}=D^{n-1}\tilde{\tilde{\varphi}}
with

\tilde{\tilde{\varphi}}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
. So, using the relations
[A,D]=DB
and
[D,B]=0
,
we get that

\displaystyle A\widetilde{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\big(D^{n-1}A+[A,D^{n-1}]\big)\tilde{\tilde{\varphi}}

\displaystyle=\big(D^{n-1}A+\mathop{\textstyle\sum}\nolimits_{m=0}^{n-2}D^{n-2-m}[A,D]D^{m}\big)\tilde{\tilde{\varphi}}

\displaystyle=D^{n-1}(A+(n-1)B)\tilde{\tilde{\varphi}}

with
(A+(n-1)B)\tilde{\tilde{\varphi}}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n-1})
due to the inclusions

\tilde{\tilde{\varphi}}\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
and
B\in C^{(n-1)}(A)
. Therefore

A\widetilde{\varphi}\in D^{n-1}\mathcal{D}(A^{n-1})
and

\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})\subset\mathcal{D}(A^{n-1})
, and we infer from the induction hypothesis
that there exists
c_{A\widetilde{\varphi},\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle A\widetilde{\varphi},U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}^{n-1}}\;\!c_{A\widetilde{\varphi},\psi}.

(2.3)

Finally, combining (
2.1
), (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we obtain that

|\langle\varphi,U_{j}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|\leq\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}^{n-1}}\;\!c_{A\widetilde{\varphi},\psi}+\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}}\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}^{n-1}}\;\!c_{\widetilde{\varphi},A\psi}=\tfrac{1}{\ell_{j}^{n}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi}

with
c_{\varphi,\psi}:=c_{A\widetilde{\varphi},\psi}+c_{\widetilde{\varphi},A\psi}
.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

(a) If the operators
U_{j}
are given by a unitary representation, then the property of
Theorem
2.1
(a)
\lim_{j}\langle\xi,U_{j}\zeta\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=0
for

\xi\in\ker(D)^{\perp}
and
\zeta\in\mathcal{H}
amounts to a strong mixing property of the
unitary representation in
\ker(D)^{\perp}
. See
[
26
]
for more information on
this point.

(b) The set
D^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
in Theorem
2.1
(c) is always dense in

\ker(D)^{\perp}
, independently of the value of
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Indeed, since
\mathcal{D}(A^{n})

is dense in
\mathcal{H}
, we have that
D^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
is dense in

\overline{D^{n}\mathcal{H}}=\ker(D^{n})^{\perp}
. But
D
is self-adjoint. So
\ker(D^{n})=\ker(D)
,
and thus
D^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
is dense in
\ker(D)^{\perp}
.

(c) Sometimes the unitary operators
U_{j}
are given by the evolution group of a
self-adjoint operator
H
, namely,
(U_{j})_{j\in J}=(\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH})_{t>0}
. In this
situation, a convenient operator
A
in Theorem
2.1
is often of the form

\widetilde{A}=(H+i)^{-1}A(H-i)^{-1}
with
A
some self-adjoint operator such that

(H-i)^{-1}\in C^{1}(A)
. In that case, the estimates of Theorem
2.1
hold
for vectors
\varphi\in D^{n}\mathcal{D}((\widetilde{A})^{n})
and

\psi\in\mathcal{D}((\widetilde{A})^{n})
. However, since
A
is simpler than
\widetilde{A}
and
since
\mathcal{D}(A^{n})\subset\mathcal{D}((\widetilde{A})^{n})
, in concrete examples we will only
present the estimates for vectors
\varphi\in D^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
for
the sake of simplicity (see Sections
3.2
,
3.4
,

3.7
,
3.8
and
3.9
).

In the sequel, we assume that the unitary operators
U_{j}
are given by a unitary
representation
\mathscr{U}:X\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
of a topological group
X
. We also assume that the
scalars
\ell_{j}
are given by a proper length function on
X
, that is, a function

\ell:X\to[0,\infty)
satisfying the following properties (with
e
the identity of

X
):

(L1)

\ell(e)=0
,

(L2)

\ell(x^{-1})=\ell(x)
for all
x\in X
,

(L3)

\ell(xy)\leq\ell(x)+\ell(y)
for all
x,y\in X
,

(L4)

if
K\subset[0,\infty)
is compact, then
\ell^{-1}(K)\subset X
is
relatively compact.

Finally, we recall that a net
(x_{j})_{j\in J}
in a topological space
X
diverges to
infinity, with notation
x_{j}\to\infty
, if
(x_{j})_{j\in J}
has no limit point in
X
.
This implies that for each compact set
K\subset X
, there exists
j_{K}\in J
such that

x_{j}\notin K
for
j\geq j_{K}
. In particular,
X
is not compact.

In this situation, the existence of the strong limit
D
leads to additional
properties of the unitary operators given by
\mathscr{U}
. Namely,
\mathscr{U}
has no nontrivial
finite-dimensional unitary subrepresentation in
\ker(D)^{\perp}
, and the operator
D

commutes with
\mathscr{U}:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
X
be a topological group equipped with a proper length function
\ell
, let

\mathscr{U}:X\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
be a unitary representation of
X
, let
(x_{j})_{j\in J}
be a net in

X
with
x_{j}\to\infty
, assume there exists a self-adjoint operator
A
in
\mathcal{H}
such
that
\mathscr{U}(x_{j})\in C^{1}(A)
for each
j\in J
, and suppose that the strong limit

D:=\slim_{j}D_{j}\quad\hbox{with}\quad D_{j}:=\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}[A,\mathscr{U}(x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x_{j})^{-1}

exists. Then

(a)

\mathscr{U}
has no nontrivial finite-dimensional unitary subrepresentation in

\ker(D)^{\perp}
.

(b)

Let
x\in X
, assume that
\mathscr{U}(x)\in C^{1}(A)
, and suppose that the strong
limit

\widetilde{D}:=\slim_{j}\widetilde{D}_{j}\quad\hbox{with}\quad\widetilde{D}_{j}:=\tfrac{1}{\ell(x^{-1}x_{j})}[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}

exists and satisfies
D=\widetilde{D}
. Then
[D,\mathscr{U}(x)]=0
.

Proof.

(a) The claim follows from Theorem
2.1
(a) and the fact that matrix
coefficients of finite-dimensional unitary representations of a group do not vanish at
infinity (see for instance
[
3
, Rem. 2.15(iii)]
).

(b) First, note that the commutator
[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]
in the expression for

\widetilde{D}
is well-defined for each
j\in J
because

\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})=\mathscr{U}(x)^{-1}\mathscr{U}(x_{j})
with
\mathscr{U}(x)\in C^{1}(A)
and
\mathscr{U}(x_{j})\in C^{1}(A)
.
Next, we have

\displaystyle D\mathscr{U}(x)

\displaystyle=\slim_{j}\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}[A,\mathscr{U}(x)\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\slim_{j}\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}[A,\mathscr{U}(x)]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathscr{U}(x)\cdot\slim_{j}\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\lim_{j}\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}\cdot[A,\mathscr{U}(x)]+\mathscr{U}(x)\cdot\slim_{j}\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}

with the first term vanishing because
\lim_{j}\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}=0
and with the second
term satisfying

\displaystyle\mathscr{U}(x)\cdot\slim_{j}\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{U}(x)\widetilde{D}+\mathscr{U}(x)\cdot\slim_{j}\left(\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}-\tfrac{1}{\ell(x^{-1}x_{j})}\right)[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}.

Thus, to conclude the proof, it is sufficient to show that

\slim_{j}\left(\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}-\tfrac{1}{\ell(x^{-1}x_{j})}\right)[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}=0.

(2.4)

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\lim_{j}\left\|\left(\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}-\tfrac{1}{\ell(x^{-1}x_{j})}\right)[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}\varphi\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{j}\left|\tfrac{\ell(x^{-1}x_{j})-\ell(x_{j})}{\ell(x_{j})}\right|\cdot\left\|\tfrac{1}{\ell(x^{-1}x_{j})}[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}\varphi\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\|\widetilde{D}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+1\big)\lim_{j}\left|\tfrac{\ell(x^{-1}x_{j})-\ell(x_{j})}{\ell(x_{j})}\right|.

Since
|\ell(x^{-1}x_{j})-\ell(x_{j})|\leq\ell(x)
due to the triangle inequality for

\ell
, we infer that

\lim_{j}\left\|\left(\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}-\tfrac{1}{\ell(x^{-1}x_{j})}\right)[A,\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x^{-1}x_{j})^{-1}\varphi\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\big(\|\widetilde{D}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+1\big)\ell(x)\lim_{j}\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}=0,

which proves (
2.4
).
∎

2.2
Unitary representations with self-adjoint generator

In this section, we consider the important case where the representation is a strongly
continuous unitary representation
\mathscr{U}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
of the additive group
\mathbb{R}
. In
such a case, Stone’s theorem implies the existence of a self-adjoint operator
H
in

\mathcal{H}
such that
\mathscr{U}(t)=\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
. One could also consider the
higher-dimensional case of a strongly continuous unitary representation of the
additive group
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\geq 1
. But we refrained from doing it for the sake of
simplicity.

We use the notation
P_{\rm p}(H)
(resp.
P_{\rm c}(H)
,
P_{\rm ac}(H)
) for the
projection onto the pure point (resp. continuous, absolutely continuous) subspace

\mathcal{H}_{\rm p}(H)
(resp.
\mathcal{H}_{\rm c}(H)
,
\mathcal{H}_{\rm ac}(H)
) of
H
,
E^{H}(\cdot)
for the
spectral projections of
H
, and
\chi_{\mathcal{B}}
for the characteristic function
of a Borel set
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.4

(Properties of
D
)
.

Let
H
and
A
be self-adjoint operators in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with

(H-i)^{-1}\in C^{1}(A)
, and let

D_{t}:=\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}(H+i)^{-1}[iH,A](H-i)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H},\quad t>0.

Then

(a)

\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}P_{\rm p}(H)=0
.

(b)

If there exists
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that
(H+i)^{-1}([iH,A]-B)(H-i)^{-1}\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})

and

\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}(H+i)^{-1}B(H-i)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm c}(H)~~\hbox{exists,}

then

\slim_{n\to\infty}D_{t}=\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}(H+i)^{-1}B(H-i)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm c}(H).

Furthermore, if
D:=\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}
exists, then

(c)

[D,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{isH}]=0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular,
D
is decomposable in the
spectral representation of
H
.

(d)

D=DP_{\rm c}(H)
. In particular,
H|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}
has purely
continuous spectrum.

(e)

If
D\mathcal{D}(A)\subset\mathcal{D}(A)
and

\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\;\!\|(D-D_{t})\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
,
then
H|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}
has purely a.c. spectrum.

Point (c) implies that
\ker(D)^{\perp}
is a reducing subspace for
H
. Therefore the
operator
H|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}
in points (d)-(e) is a well-defined self-adjoint
operator (see
[
40
, Thm. 7.28]
).

Proof.

(a) Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
. Then
P_{\rm p}(H)\varphi=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}\varphi_{j}
with

(\varphi_{j})_{j\geq 1}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\rm p}(H)
,
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, and

H\varphi_{j}=\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j}
for some
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus we obtain

\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}P_{\rm p}(H)\varphi=\slim_{t\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}\left(\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau(H-\lambda_{j})}\right)(H+i)^{-1}[iH,A](H-i)^{-1}\varphi_{j}.

(2.5)

Now

\|\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau(H-\lambda_{j})}\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1

for all
t>0
, and

\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau(H-\lambda_{j})}=E^{H}(\{\lambda_{j}\})

due to von Neumann’s mean ergodic theorem. Therefore we can exchange the limit and the
sum in (
2.5
) to get

\displaystyle\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}P_{\rm p}(H)\varphi

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}E^{H}(\{\lambda_{j}\})(H+i)^{-1}[iH,A](H-i)^{-1}\varphi_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}\langle\lambda_{j}\rangle^{-2}E^{H}(\{\lambda_{j}\})[iH,A]E^{H}(\{\lambda_{j}\})\varphi_{j}.

But
E^{H}(\{\lambda_{j}\})[iH,A]E^{H}(\{\lambda_{j}\})=0
for each
\lambda_{j}
due to the
virial theorem for self-adjoint operators
[
1
, Prop. 7.2.10]
. Thus we
obtain that
\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}P_{\rm p}(H)\varphi=0
, which proves the claim.

(b) Let
K:=(H+i)^{-1}([iH,A]-B)(H-i)^{-1}\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Then it follows from point (a)
that

\displaystyle\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}

\displaystyle=\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}(H+i)^{-1}B(H-i)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm c}(H)

\displaystyle\quad+\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}K\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm c}(H).

with

\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}K\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm c}(H)=0

due to
[
30
, Thm. 5.9]
.

(c) The claim follows from Proposition
2.3
(b) in the case of the additive
group
X=\mathbb{R}
and the auxiliary operator

\widetilde{A}\varphi:=(H+i)^{-1}A(H-i)^{-1}\varphi,\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(A).

Indeed, we know from
[
26
, Cor. 2.7 & Rem. 2.8]
that
\widetilde{A}
is
essentially self-adjoint (with closure denoted by the same symbol) and that

\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\in C^{1}(\widetilde{A})
with
[\widetilde{A},\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}]=tD_{t}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}
for any

t>0
. Therefore, if we take the proper length function
\ell:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
given
by
\ell(t):=|t|
, the unitary representation
\mathscr{U}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
given by

\mathscr{U}(t):=\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}
, and the net
(x_{j})_{j\in J}=(t)_{t>0}
, then we get

\mathscr{U}(s)=\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-isH}\in C^{1}(\widetilde{A})
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}=\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t-s}[\widetilde{A},\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i(t-s)H}]\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i(t-s)H}=\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}[\widetilde{A},\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}]\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{itH}=\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}=D.

So all the assumptions of Proposition
2.3
(b) are verified, and thus

[D,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{isH}]=0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

Finally, since
[D,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{isH}]=0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

D\chi_{\mathcal{B}}(H)=\chi_{\mathcal{B}}(H)D
for each Borel set

\mathcal{B}\subset\mathbb{R}
, and thus
D
is decomposable in the spectral representation of

H

[
4
, Thm. 7.2.3(b)]
.

(d) The equality
D=DP_{\rm c}(H)
follows from point (a). As a consequence, we get
that
\ker(D)^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\rm c}(H)
, and thus the operator
H|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}

has purely continuous spectrum.

(e) Take
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
. Then

\psi=D\varphi\in D\mathcal{D}(\widetilde{A})\cap\mathcal{D}(\widetilde{A})
, and it follows from
Theorem
2.1
(a) that there exists
c_{\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\psi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\big\|(D-D_{t})\varphi\big\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{t}\;\!c_{\psi},\quad t>0.

So, we infer from the assumption and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}t\;\!|\langle\psi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}|^{2}<\infty
, and thus that

t\mapsto\langle\psi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}
belongs to
\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore,
Plancherel’s theorem for the group
X=\mathbb{R}

[
9
, Thm. 4.26]
implies that

\psi\in D\mathcal{D}(A)
belongs to the a.c. subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\rm ac}(H)
of
H
. Thus

H|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}
has purely a.c. spectrum, since
D\mathcal{D}(A)
is dense in

\overline{D\mathcal{H}}=\ker(D)^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\rm ac}(H)
is closed in
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

In the next proposition, we consider the particular case where
[iH,A]=f(H)
for some
Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. Our results in this case generalise the results of

[
36
, Cor. 4.3-4.4]
.

Proposition 2.5

(The case
[iH,A]=f(H)
)
.

Let
H
and
A
be self-adjoint operators in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, assume that

(H-i)^{-1}\in C^{1}(A)
with
[iH,A]=f(H)
for some Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, and set

g:=f\langle\cdot\rangle^{-2}
. Then

(a)

For each
\varphi\in g(H)\mathcal{D}(A)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
there exists a
constant
c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{t}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

(b)

If
g(H)\mathcal{D}(A)\subset\mathcal{D}(A)
, then
H|_{\ker(f(H))^{\perp}}
has purely
a.c. spectrum.

(c)

Suppose that
f\in C^{n}(\mathbb{R})
(
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
) with

g^{(k)}\in\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R})
for all
k=0,\dots,n
and
g^{(n)}
uniformly
continuous. Then for each
\varphi\in g(H)^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
there
exists a constant
c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{t^{n}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

Proof.

(a) We know from the proof of Lemma
2.4
(c) that the auxiliary operator

\widetilde{A}\varphi=(H+i)^{-1}A(H-i)^{-1}\varphi,\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(A),

is essentially self-adjoint (with closure denoted by the same symbol) and that

\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\in C^{1}(\widetilde{A})
with
[\widetilde{A},\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}]=tD_{t}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}
for any

t>0
. So the strong limit
D
exists and satisfies the equalities

D_{t}=\tfrac{1}{t}[\widetilde{A},\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}]\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{itH}=\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}(H+i)^{-1}[iH,A](H-i)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}=g(H)=D.

(2.6)

Therefore the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
(b) are satisfied for the net

(U_{j})_{j\in J}=(\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH})_{t>0}
, the set
(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}=(t)_{t>0}
, the
operator
\widetilde{A}
, and
D=g(H)
. Thus for each

\varphi\in g(H)\mathcal{D}(A)\subset g(H)\mathcal{D}(\widetilde{A})
and

\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)\subset\mathcal{D}(\widetilde{A})
there exists a constant

c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{t}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

(b) The claim follows from Lemma
2.4
(e) because
D=g(H)=D_{t}
for all

t>0
and

\ker(g(H))=\chi_{g^{-1}(\{0\})}(H)\mathcal{H}=\chi_{f^{-1}(\{0\})}(H)\mathcal{H}=\ker(f(H)).

(c) Since
g
is uniformly continuous and belongs to
\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R})\cap\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\big\|g(H)-g^{\varepsilon}(H)\big\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})}=0

(2.7)

with

g^{\varepsilon}(H):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}t\;\!(\mathcal{F}g)(t)\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-(\varepsilon t)^{2}}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{2\pi itH}\quad\hbox{(strong or Bochner integral)}

and
\mathcal{F}:\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R})\to\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R})
the Fourier transform (see

[
8
, Thm. 8.35(b)]
). Using successively the fact that
D=g(H)
, equation
(
2.7
), the inclusion
\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{2\pi itH}\mathcal{D}(A)\subset\mathcal{D}(\widetilde{A})
,
equation (
2.6
), the relation
2\pi it(\mathcal{F}g)(t)=(\mathcal{F}g^{\prime})(t)
, and
(
2.7
) with
g
replaced by
g^{\prime}
, we get for
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
the
equalities

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{A}\varphi,D\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\langle\varphi,D\widetilde{A}\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}t\,\overline{(\mathcal{F}g)(t)}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-(\varepsilon t)^{2}}\big(\langle\widetilde{A}\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{2\pi itH}\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{2\pi itH}\widetilde{A}\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}t\,\overline{(\mathcal{F}g)(t)}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-(\varepsilon t)^{2}}\langle\varphi,[\widetilde{A},\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{2\pi itH}]\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}t\,\overline{(\mathcal{F}g)(t)}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-(\varepsilon t)^{2}}\langle\varphi,(-2\pi t)D\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{2\pi itH}\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\overline{i(\mathcal{F}g^{\prime})(t)}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-(\varepsilon t)^{2}}\langle\varphi,D\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{2\pi itH}\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\langle\varphi,iDg^{\prime}(H)\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Since
g^{\prime}(H)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
due to the inclusion
g^{\prime}\in\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R})
, we infer that

D\in C^{1}(\widetilde{A})
with
[\widetilde{A},D]=iDg^{\prime}(H)
. Thus, in the present case,
the operator
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
appearing in the statement of Theorem
2.1
(c) is

B=ig^{\prime}(H)
. So we trivially get that
[D,B]=0
, and by reproducing
(n-1)
times the
previous argument we obtain that
B\in C^{n-1}(\widetilde{A})
.

Summing up, the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
(c) are satisfied for the net

(U_{j})_{j\in J}=(\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH})_{t>0}
, the set
(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}=(t)_{t>0}
, the
operator
\widetilde{A}
, and
D=g(H)
. Thus for each

\varphi\in g(H)^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})\subset g(H)^{n}\mathcal{D}((\widetilde{A})^{n})
and

\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})\subset\mathcal{D}((\widetilde{A})^{n})
there exists a constant

c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{t^{n}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

∎

Remark 2.6
.

If the operators
H
and
A
satisfy the commutation relation
[iH,A]=f(H)
for some
Borel function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, then they satisfy the relation
[iH,A]=\widetilde{f}(H)

for any Borel function
\widetilde{f}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\widetilde{f}|_{\sigma(H)}=f|_{\sigma(H)}
due to functional calculus. This basic
observation will be useful in some applications in which the initial function
f

fails to satisfy the assumptions of Proposition
2.5
(b) or

2.5
(c) (see for instance Section
3.3
).

2.3
Unitary representations with unitary generator

In this section, we consider the important case where the representation is a unitary
representation
\mathscr{U}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
of the additive group
\mathbb{Z}
. In such a case, the fact
that
\mathbb{Z}
has generator
1
implies the existence of a unitary operator
U
in
\mathcal{H}

such that
\mathscr{U}(m)=U^{m}
for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. One could also consider the
higher-dimensional case of a unitary representation of the additive group
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
for

d\geq 1
. But we refrained from doing it for the sake of simplicity.

We use the notation
P_{\rm p}(U)
(resp.
P_{\rm c}(U)
,
P_{\rm ac}(U)
) for the
projection onto the pure point (resp. continuous, absolutely continuous) subspace

\mathcal{H}_{\rm p}(U)
(resp.
\mathcal{H}_{\rm c}(U)
,
\mathcal{H}_{\rm ac}(U)
) of
U
,
E^{U}(\cdot)
for the
spectral projections of
U
, and
\chi_{\Theta}
for the characteristic function of a
Borel set
\Theta\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Lemma 2.7

(Properties of
D
)
.

Let
U
and
A
be a unitary and a self-adjoint operator in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with

U\in C^{1}(A)
, and let

D_{n}:=\tfrac{1}{n}[A,U^{n}]U^{-n},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}.

Then

(a)

\slim_{n\to\infty}D_{n}P_{\rm p}(U)=0
.

(b)

If there exists
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that
[A,U]U^{-1}-B\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})
and

\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}BU^{-m}P_{\rm c}(U)
exists, then

\slim_{n\to\infty}D_{n}=\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}BU^{-m}P_{\rm c}(U).

Furthermore, if
D:=\slim_{n\to\infty}D_{n}
exists, then

(c)

[D,U^{m}]=0
for all
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
D
is decomposable in the
spectral representation of
U
.

(d)

D=DP_{\rm c}(U)
. In particular,
U|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}
has purely
continuous spectrum.

(e)

If
D\mathcal{D}(A)\subset\mathcal{D}(A)
and

\sum_{n\geq 1}\|(D-D_{n})\varphi\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
, then

U|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}
has purely a.c. spectrum.

Point (c) implies that
\ker(D)^{\perp}
is a reducing subspace for
U
. Therefore the
operator
U|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}
in points (d)-(e) is a well-defined unitary operator
(see
[
40
, Example 5.39(b)]
).

Proof.

(a) Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
. Then
P_{\rm p}(U)\varphi=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}\varphi_{j}
with

(\varphi_{j})_{j\geq 1}
an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\rm p}(U)
,
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, and

U\varphi_{j}=\theta_{j}\varphi_{j}
for some
\theta_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Furthermore, we have for
any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
the equalities

D_{n}=\tfrac{1}{n}[A,U^{n}]U^{-n}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}([A,U]U^{-1})U^{-m}.

Therefore, we obtain

\slim_{n\to\infty}D_{n}P_{\rm p}(U)\varphi=\slim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(U\theta_{j}^{-1})^{m}\right)[A,U]U^{-1}\varphi_{j}.

(2.8)

Now

\|\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(U\theta_{j}^{-1})^{m}\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and

\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(U\theta_{j}^{-1})^{m}=E^{U}(\{\theta_{j}\})

due to von Neumann’s mean ergodic theorem. Therefore we can exchange the limit and the
sum in (
2.8
) to get

\displaystyle\slim_{n\to\infty}D_{n}P_{\rm p}(U)\varphi=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}E^{U}(\{\theta_{j}\})[A,U]U^{-1}\varphi_{j}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}E^{U}(\{\theta_{j}\})[A,U]U^{-1}E^{U}(\{\theta_{j}\})\varphi_{j}.

. Thus we obtain that

(b) Let

. Then it follows from point (a) that

\displaystyle\slim_{n\to\infty}D_{n}

\displaystyle=\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}([A,U]U^{-1})U^{-m}P_{\rm c}(U)

\displaystyle=\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}BU^{-m}P_{\rm c}(U)+\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}KU^{-m}P_{\rm c}(U).

with
\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}KU^{-m}P_{\rm c}(U)=0

Theorem

.

(c) The claim follows from Proposition
2.3
(b) in the case of the additive

and the operator

. Indeed, if we take the proper length function

, the unitary representation

, and the net

,
then we get

and

Finally, since

[
4

.

(d) The equality

, and thus the operator

has purely continuous spectrum.

(e) Take

. Then

, and it
follows from Theorem

. Thus

has purely a.c. spectrum, since

∎

In the next proposition, we consider the particular case where

Proposition 2.8

(The case

)
.

Let

(c)

Proof.

(a) The strong limit
D

the equalities

D_{n}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}([A,U]U^{-1})U^{-m}=\eta(U)=D.

Therefore the assumptions of Theorem

(b) are satisfied for the net

\varphi\in\eta(U)\mathcal{D}(A)
and

(b) The claim follows from Lemma

(e) because

(c) Since

, we have that

\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\big\|\eta(U)-\eta^{\varepsilon}(U)\big\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})}=0

(2.10)

with

\displaystyle\langle A\varphi,D\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\langle\varphi,A\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

3
Applications

In this section, we apply our results to various models in quantum mechanics and
dynamical systems. First, we consider an example of unitary representation with no
self-adjoint or unitary generator. Then we consider examples of unitary
representations having a self-adjoint generator. And finally we consider examples of
unitary representations having a unitary generator.

3.1
Left regular representation

This example is motivated by
[
26
, Ex. 2.9]
. Let
X
be a
\sigma
-compact
locally compact Hausdorff group with left Haar measure
\mu
and proper length
function
\ell
(see properties (L1)-(L4) in Section
2.1
). Let

\mathcal{H}:=\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(X,\mu)
, let
\mathscr{D}\subset\mathcal{H}
be the set of functions
X\to\mathbb{C}
with compact
support, let
\mathscr{U}:X\to\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{H})
be the left regular representation of
X
on
\mathcal{H}

\mathscr{U}(x)\varphi:=\varphi(x^{-1}\;\!\cdot),\quad x\in X,~\varphi\in\mathcal{H},

and let
A
be the maximal multiplication operator by
\ell
in
\mathcal{H}

A\varphi:=\ell\varphi,\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(A):=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}\mid\|\ell\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty\}.

For any
x\in X
, one has on
\mathscr{D}
the equality

A\mathscr{U}(x)-\mathscr{U}(x)A=\big(\ell(\cdot)-\ell(x^{-1}\;\!\cdot)\big)\mathscr{U}(x),

with
|(\ell(\cdot)-\ell(x^{-1}\;\!\cdot))|\leq\ell(x)
due to properties (L2)-(L3).
Since
\mathscr{D}
is dense in
\mathcal{D}(A)
, it follows that
\mathscr{U}(x)\in C^{1}(A)
with

[A,\mathscr{U}(x)]\mathscr{U}(x)^{-1}=\ell(\cdot)-\ell(x^{-1}\;\!\cdot).

(3.1)

In addition, we know from
[
26
, Ex. 2.9]
that for any net
(x_{j})_{j\in J}
in

X
with
x_{j}\to\infty
we have

D:=\slim_{j}D_{j}=\slim_{j}\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}[A,\mathscr{U}(x_{j})]\mathscr{U}(x_{j})^{-1}=-1.

Therefore, Theorem
2.1
(a) applies for the net

(U_{j})_{j\in J}=(\mathscr{U}(x_{j}))_{j\in J}
, the set

(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}=(\ell(x_{j}))_{j\in J}
, the operator
A
, and
D=-1
. Thus, for each

\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
there exists a constant
c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathscr{U}(x_{j})\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\big\|(D-D_{j})\varphi\big\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad\ell(x_{j})>0.

But (
3.1
) and the bound

|\ell(\cdot)-\ell(x_{j}^{-1}\;\!\cdot)+\ell(x_{j})|\leq 2\ell(\cdot)
(which follows from
properties (L2)-(L3)) imply that

\big\|(D-D_{j})\varphi\big\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\Big\|\tfrac{\ell(\cdot)-\ell(x_{j}^{-1}\;\!\cdot)+\ell(x_{j})}{\ell(x_{j})}\varphi\Big\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\tfrac{2}{\ell(x_{j})}\|\ell\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}}.

Therefore, by setting

\widetilde{c}_{\varphi,\psi}:=2\|\ell\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+c_{\varphi,\psi}
, we
obtain that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathscr{U}(x_{j})\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{\ell(x_{j})}\;\!\widetilde{c}_{\varphi,\psi},\quad\ell(x_{j})>0.

This estimate is similar to the estimates of Propositions
2.5
(a) and

2.8
(a), but with the interesting difference that in general the
representation
\mathscr{U}
doesn’t have neither a self-adjoint generator nor a unitary
generator. In addition, we note that one can even obtain higher order decay estimates
by carrying on the above calculations. But we refrained from presenting them here for
the sake of simplicity.

3.2
Schrödinger operator in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

In the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}:=\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, consider the Schrödinger operator and the
generator of dilations given by

H\varphi:=(-\Delta+V)\varphi\quad\hbox{and}\quad A\varphi:=\tfrac{1}{2}(Q\cdot P+P\cdot Q),\quad\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

with
V\in\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
,
Q:=(Q_{1},\dots,Q_{n})
the position operator,
P:=-i\nabla

the momentum operator and
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the Schwartz space on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Both operators are
essentially self-adjoint with closures denoted by the same symbols. If we assume that

x\mapsto x\cdot(\nabla V)(x)\in\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})\quad\hbox{and}\quad\lim_{|x|\to\infty}\big|2V(x)+x\cdot(\nabla V)(x)\big|=0,

then we have
(H-i)^{-1}\in C^{1}(A)
with
[iH,A]=2H-2V-Q\cdot\nabla V
, we have

(H+i)^{-1}(2V+Q\cdot\nabla V)(H-i)^{-1}\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})

due to the standard theorem
[
1
, Prop. 4.1.3]
, and

\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}(H+i)^{-1}2H(H-i)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm c}(H)=2H\langle H\rangle^{-2}P_{\rm c}(H).

Therefore, it follows from Lemma
2.4
that

D=\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}=2H\langle H\rangle^{-2}P_{\rm c}(H)

and that
H|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}
has purely continuous spectrum. Moreover, Theorem

2.1
(a) applies for the net
(U_{j})_{j\in J}=(\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH})_{t>0}
, the set

(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}=(t)_{t>0}
, the operator
\widetilde{A}=(H+i)^{-1}A(H-i)^{-1}
, and

D
as above. Therefore, for each
\varphi=D\widetilde{\varphi}\in D\mathcal{D}(A)
and

\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
there exists a constant
c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\|(D-D_{t})\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{t}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

(3.2)

In this example, one cannot easily improve the decay estimate (
3.2
)
when
V\neq 0
. Indeed, in order to establish the convergence

D_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm s}}{{\to}}D
, we used Lemma
2.4
(b). But the proof of
Lemma
2.4
(b) relies on the RAGE theorem for self-adjoint operators,
whose proof relies in turn on Wiener’s theorem. And as sad as it is, in general one
cannot infer an explicit rate of convergence from Wiener’s theorem.

On the other hand, if
V=0
, then we have
[iH,A]=2H
, and thus the stronger decay
estimates of Proposition
2.5
(c) are satisfied.

Dirac operator in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

1

1

V\in\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\nolimits(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
satisfying

(1\otimes\langle Q_{n}\rangle^{1+\varepsilon})V\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})\quad\hbox{and}\quad(1\otimes\langle Q_{n}\rangle^{1+\varepsilon})(\partial_{n}V)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})\quad\hbox{for some $\varepsilon>0$.}

Then
V\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{D}(H_{0}),\mathcal{H})
. Indeed, if we use the strongly commuting self-adjoint
operators
X_{1}:=1\otimes\langle P_{n}\rangle^{2}
and

X_{2}:=(-\Delta^{\Sigma}_{\rm D}+1)\otimes 1
, we get

V(H_{0}+2)^{-1}=(1\otimes\langle Q_{n}\rangle)V\cdot(-\Delta^{\Sigma}_{\rm D}+1)^{-1/2}\otimes\langle Q_{n}\rangle^{-1}\langle P_{n}\rangle^{-1}\cdot X_{1}^{1/2}X_{2}^{1/2}(X_{1}+X_{2})^{-1}.

But
(1\otimes\langle Q_{n}\rangle)V\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
by assumption,

X_{1}^{1/2}X_{2}^{1/2}(X_{1}+X_{2})^{-1}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
due to the bound

\big\|X_{1}^{1/2}X_{2}^{1/2}(X_{1}+X_{2})^{-1}\big\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})}\leq\sup_{x_{1},x_{2}\geq 1}(x_{1}x_{2})^{1/2}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{-1}<\infty,

and

(-\Delta^{\Sigma}_{\rm D}+1)^{-1/2}\otimes\langle Q_{n}\rangle^{-1}\langle P_{n}\rangle^{-1}\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})

because
-\Delta^{\Sigma}_{\rm D}
has compact resolvent and

\langle Q_{n}\rangle^{-1}\langle P_{n}\rangle^{-1}
is compact. Hence

V(H_{0}+2)^{-1}\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})
, and thus
V\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{D}(H_{0}),\mathcal{H})
. Therefore, the assumptions
of
[
32
]
are satisfied and the following holds true: The operator
H
has no
singular continuous spectrum, the eigenvalues of
H
(if any) are of finite
multiplicity and can accumulate only at the set of eigenvalues of

-\Delta^{\Sigma}_{\rm D}
, and the wave operators

W_{\pm}:=\slim_{t\to\pm\infty}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{itH}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH_{0}}
exist and are complete.

Now, set
A:=1\otimes A_{n}
with
A_{n}
the generator of dilations in
\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(\mathbb{R})
. Then

(H-i)^{-1}\in C^{1}(A)
with

[iH,A]=2(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})-(1\otimes Q_{n})(\partial_{n}V),

and an argument as above shows that the following three operators belong to
\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})
:

\displaystyle(H+i)^{-1}(1\otimes Q_{n})(\partial_{n}V)(H-i)^{-1},

\displaystyle\big((H+i)^{-1}-(H_{0}+i)^{-1}\big)(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})(H-i)^{-1},

\displaystyle(H_{0}+i)^{-1}(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})\big((H-i)^{-1}-(H_{0}-i)^{-1}\big).

Furthermore, since
P_{\rm c}(H)=P_{\rm ac}(H)
, one has

\displaystyle\slim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}(H_{0}+i)^{-1}2(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})(H_{0}-i)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm c}(H)

\displaystyle=2\;\!\slim_{\tau\to\infty}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H_{0}}(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})\langle H_{0}\rangle^{-2}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H_{0}}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm ac}(H)

\displaystyle=2W_{-}(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})\langle H_{0}\rangle^{-2}W_{-}^{*}.

And since strong convergence implies strong Cesaro convergence, this implies that

\displaystyle\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}(H_{0}+i)^{-1}2(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})(H_{0}-i)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm c}(H)

\displaystyle=2W_{-}(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})\langle H_{0}\rangle^{-2}W_{-}^{*}.

Thus it follows from Lemma
2.4
(b) that

D=\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}=2W_{-}(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})\langle H_{0}\rangle^{-2}W_{-}^{*}.

So, Theorem
2.1
(a) applies for the net
(U_{j})_{j\in J}=(\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH})_{t>0}
,
the set
(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}=(t)_{t>0}
, the operator

\widetilde{A}=(H+i)^{-1}A(H-i)^{-1}
, and
D
as above. Therefore, for each

\varphi=D\widetilde{\varphi}\in D\mathcal{D}(A)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
there exists a constant

c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\|(D-D_{t})\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{t}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

Of course, one obtains stronger decay estimates when
V=0
. Indeed, one gets in such a
case
[iH,A]=2(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})
and
D=D_{t}=2(1\otimes P_{n}^{2})\langle H\rangle^{-2}
for
all
t>0
, from which one can infer the estimates of Theorem
2.1
(c).

with electric field along

3.7
Horocycle flow

This example is motivated by
[
33
,
36
]
, but see also

[
34
,
37
]
. Let
\Sigma
be a finite volume Riemann surface of genus

\geq 2
and let
M:=T^{1}\Sigma
be the unit tangent bundle of
\Sigma
. The
3
-manifold

M
carries a probability measure
\mu_{\Omega}
, induced by a canonical volume form

\Omega
, which is preserved by two distinguished one-parameter groups of
diffeomorphisms ​: the horocycle flow
F_{1}:=(F_{1,t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
and the geodesic
flow
F_{2}:=(F_{2,t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. Each flow admits a self-adjoint generator
H_{j}
in

\mathcal{H}:=\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(M,\mu_{\Omega})
essentially self-adjoint on
C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M)
and given
by

H_{j}\varphi:=-i\mathscr{L}_{X_{j}}\varphi,\quad\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{\rm c}(M),

with
X_{j}
the divergence-free vector field associated to
F_{j}
and
\mathscr{L}_{X_{j}}
the
corresponding Lie derivative. Moreover, one has
(H_{1}-i)^{-1}\in C^{\infty}(H_{2})
with

[iH_{1},H_{2}]=H_{1}
, see
[
33
, Sec. 3]
. Thus Proposition
2.5

applies with
f(H_{1})=H_{1}
,
g(H_{1})=H_{1}\langle H_{1}\rangle^{-2}\in C^{\infty}(H_{2})
and

\ker(f(H_{1}))=\ker(H_{1})
. It follows that
H_{1}|_{\ker(H_{1})^{\perp}}
has purely a.c.
spectrum (which is standard knowledge) and for each
\varphi\in g(H_{1})^{n}\mathcal{D}(H_{2}^{n})

and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(H_{2}^{n})
(
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
) there exists a constant
c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0

such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\psi\circ F_{1,t}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|=\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH_{1}}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{t^{n}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

Roughly, this estimate shows that if the vectors
\varphi,\psi
are
n
times
differentiable along the geodesic flow, then their correlation along the horocycle
flow decays as
\tfrac{1}{t^{n}}
. It thus provides a new version of the polynomial decay
of correlations for the horocycle flow on the unit tangent bundle of a finite volume
surface of constant negative curvature. And its (rather short) proof did not use the
identification of
M
with a homogeneous space
\Gamma\setminus{\rm PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
and
the representation theory associated to it as is customary (see for instance

[
22
]
).

Now, assume that
\Sigma
is compact and consider a
C^{1}
vector field proportional to

X_{1}
, that is,
fX_{1}
with
f\in C^{1}(M,(0,\infty))
. The vector field
fX_{1}
admits a
complete flow
\widetilde{F}_{1}:=(\widetilde{F}_{1,t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
uniquely ergodic with
respect to the measure

\widetilde{\mu}_{\Omega}:=\frac{f^{-1}\mu_{\Omega}}{\int_{M}f^{-1}\mathrm{d}\mu_{\Omega}}
and with
generator
H:=fH_{1}
essentially self-adjoint on

C^{1}(M)\subset\mathcal{H}:=\mathop{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\nolimits(M,f^{-1}\mu_{\Omega})
. Furthermore, the following holds true

[
36
, Ex. 4.8]
: The operator
A:=f^{1/2}H_{2}f^{-1/2}
is essentially
self-adjoint on
C^{1}(M)\subset\mathcal{H}
, one has
(H-i)^{-1}\in C^{1}(A)
with

[iH,A]=H\xi+\xi H,\quad\xi:=\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}f^{-1}\mathscr{L}_{X_{2}}(f),

and
D=\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}=H\langle H\rangle^{-2}
. Thus Theorem
2.1
(a)
applies for the net
(U_{j})_{j\in J}=(\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH})_{t>0}
, the set

(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}=(t)_{t>0}
, the operator
\widetilde{A}=(H+i)^{-1}A(H-i)^{-1}
, and

D=H\langle H\rangle^{-2}
. Therefore, for each

\varphi=D\widetilde{\varphi}\in D\mathcal{D}(A)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
there exists a constant

c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\psi\circ\widetilde{F}_{1,t}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|=\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\|(D-D_{t})\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{t}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

(3.4)

In this case, one cannot easily improve the decay estimate (
3.4
) with
the tools of this paper. Indeed, in order to establish the convergence

D_{t}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm s}}{{\to}}D
, one uses the ergodic theorem for uniquely ergodic flows
(see
[
36
]
) which does not come with an explicit rate of convergence. We
refer to
[
10
, Thm. 19]
for a more quantitative decay estimate obtained using
the representation theory of
{\rm SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
.

3.8
Adjacency matrices

This example is motivated by
[
21
]
and
[
36
, Ex. 4.7]
. Let

(X,\sim)
be a graph of finite degree, with symmetric relation
\sim
, and with no
multiple edges or loops. Then the adjacency matrix of
(X,\sim)
is the bounded
self-adjoint operator in the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}:=\ell^{2}(X)
given by

(H\varphi)(x):=\sum_{y\sim x}\varphi(y),\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{H},~x\in X.

A directed graph
(X,<)
subjacent to
(X,\sim)
is the graph
(X,\sim)
together with
a relation
<
on
X
such that, for each
x,y\in X
,
x\sim y
is equivalent to
x<y

or
y<x
(and one cannot have both
x<y
and
y<x
). When using drawings, we draw an
arrow
x\leftarrow y
if
x<y
. For each
x\in X
, we define the sets

N^{-}(x):=\{y\in X\mid x<y\}
and
N^{+}(x):=\{y\in X\mid y<x\}
. Then a directed graph

(X,<)
is called admissible if
[
21
, Def. 5.1]
:

(i)

each closed path in
X
has the same number of positively and negatively
oriented edges,

(ii)

for each
x,y\in X
, one has

\#\{N^{-}(x)\cap N^{-}(y)\}=\#\{N^{+}(x)\cap N^{+}(y)\}
.

Figure 1:
Example of admissible graph

In the case of admissible graphs, it is shown in
[
21
, Secs. 3-5]
that there
exist a self-adjoint operator
A
and a bounded self-adjoint operator
K
in
\mathcal{H}
such
that
H\in C^{1}(A)
with
[iH,A]=K^{2}
,
K^{2}\in C^{1}(A)
with
[iK^{2},A]=-2HK^{2}
, and

[K,H]=0
. Thus Lemma
2.4
applies with

D=D_{t}=K^{2}\langle H\rangle^{-2}\in C^{1}(A)
for all
t>0
and
\ker(D)=\ker(K)
. So

H|_{\ker(K)^{\perp}}
has purely a.c. spectrum, as was first proved in

[
21
, Thm. 1.1]
with a more complicated method.

Now, since
H,K^{2}\in C^{1}(A)
, we have
(H\pm i)^{-1}\mathcal{D}(A)\subset\mathcal{D}(A)
and

K^{2}\mathcal{D}(A)\subset\mathcal{D}(A)
. Thus a calculation using the above commutation relations
and the operator
\widetilde{A}:=(H+i)^{-1}A(H-i)^{-1}
gives on
\mathcal{D}(A)
:

\displaystyle[\widetilde{A},D]

\displaystyle=[(H+i)^{-1}A(H-i)^{-1},K^{2}\langle H\rangle^{-2}]

\displaystyle=K^{2}(H+i)^{-1}[A,\langle H\rangle^{-2}](H-i)^{-1}+(H+i)^{-1}[A,K^{2}](H-i)^{-1}\langle H\rangle^{-2}

\displaystyle=K^{2}(H+i)^{-1}(-2iK^{2}H\langle H\rangle^{-4})(H-i)^{-1}+(H+i)^{-1}(-2iHK^{2})(H-i)^{-1}\langle H\rangle^{-2}

\displaystyle=-2iK^{2}H\langle H\rangle^{-4}(K^{2}\langle H\rangle^{-2}+1)

\displaystyle=-2iDH\langle H\rangle^{-2}(D+1).

Since
DH\langle H\rangle^{-2}(D+1)\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
\mathcal{D}(A)
is a core for

\widetilde{A}
, we infer that
D\in C^{1}(\widetilde{A})
with

[\widetilde{A},D]=-2iDH\langle H\rangle^{-2}(D+1)
. So, in the present case, the
operator
B\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
appearing in the statement of Theorem
2.1
(c) is

B=-2iH\langle H\rangle^{-2}(D+1)
and it satisfies
[D,B]=0
. Furthermore, a repeated
use of the information gathered so far shows that each factor appearing in the
expression for
B
belongs to
C^{(n-1)}(\widetilde{A})
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Thus

B\in C^{(n-1)}(\widetilde{A})
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. It follows that the assumptions of
Theorem
2.1
(c) are satisfied for the net

(U_{j})_{j\in J}=(\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH})_{t>0}
, the set
(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}=(t)_{t>0}
, the
operator
\widetilde{A}
, and
D=K^{2}\langle H\rangle^{-2}
. Thus for each

\varphi\in D^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
(
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
) there exists a constant

c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{t^{n}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

3.9
Jacobi matrices

This example is motivated by
[
27
]
. Let
\mathcal{H}:=\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}^{*})
, let

\ell^{2}_{0}\subset\mathcal{H}
be the set of functions
\mathbb{N}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
with compact support, and let

H
be the Jacobi matrix

(H\varphi)(n):=a_{n-1}\varphi(n-1)+b_{n}\varphi(n)+a_{n}\varphi(n+1),\quad\varphi\in\ell^{2}_{0},~n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},~\varphi(0):=0,

with coefficients

a_{n}:=n^{\alpha}+\eta_{n},\quad b_{n}:=\lambda(n^{\alpha}+(n-1)^{\alpha})+\beta_{n},\quad\alpha>0,~\lambda,\eta_{n},\beta_{n}\in\mathbb{R}.

For any sequence
(r_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, define by induction

(\partial^{k+1}r)_{n}:=(\partial\partial^{k}r)_{n}
with
(\partial r)_{n}:=r_{n+1}-r_{n}
, set

\beta_{n}^{\prime}:=\beta_{n}-\lambda(\eta_{n}+\eta_{n-1}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}^{*},~\eta_{0}:=0,

and assume the following:

Assumption 3.2
.

Suppose that
|\lambda|<1
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and assume that the sequences with
elements

\tfrac{\eta_{n}}{n^{\alpha}},\quad(\partial\eta)_{n},\quad n^{1-2\alpha}(\partial\eta)_{n},\quad n^{1-\alpha}(\partial^{2}\eta)_{n},\quad\beta_{n}^{\prime},\quad n^{1-\alpha}(\partial\beta^{\prime})_{n},\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}^{*})

vanish as
n\to\infty
.

Then it is shown in
[
27
, Thms 1.1-1.4]
that
H
is essentially self-adjoint
(with closure denoted by the same symbol), that
\sigma_{\rm ess}(H)=\mathbb{R}
, and that the
eigenvalues of
H
(if any) are of finite multiplicity and can accumulate only at

\pm\infty
. Furthermore, it is shown in the proof of
[
27
, Cor. 8.1]
that
there exists a self-adjoint operator
A
in
\mathcal{H}
such that
(H-i)^{-1}\in C^{1}(A)
with

[iH,A]=4(1-\lambda^{2})(1-\alpha)+K,\quad K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{D}(H),\mathcal{H}).

Since
(H+i)^{-1}K(H-i)^{-1}\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})
and

\displaystyle\slim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau\,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-i\tau H}(H+i)^{-1}4(1-\lambda^{2})(1-\alpha)(H-i)^{-1}\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{i\tau H}P_{\rm c}(H)

\displaystyle=4(1-\lambda^{2})(1-\alpha)\langle H\rangle^{-2}P_{\rm c}(H),

it follows from Lemma
2.4
that

D=\slim_{t\to\infty}D_{t}=4(1-\lambda^{2})(1-\alpha)\langle H\rangle^{-2}P_{\rm c}(H)

and that
H|_{\ker(D)^{\perp}}=H|_{P_{\rm c}(H)\mathcal{H}}
has (trivially) purely continuous
spectrum. Moreover, Theorem
2.1
(a) applies for the net

(U_{j})_{j\in J}=(\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH})_{t>0}
, the set
(\ell_{j})_{j\in J}=(t)_{t>0}
, the
operator
\widetilde{A}=(H+i)^{-1}A(H-i)^{-1}
, and
D
as above. Therefore, for each

\varphi=D\widetilde{\varphi}\in D\mathcal{D}(A)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A)
there exists a constant

c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\|(D-D_{t})\widetilde{\varphi}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\;\!\|\psi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\tfrac{1}{t}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

One can obtain stronger decay estimates in particular cases. For instance, it is shown
in
[
27
, Appx. A]
that for certain choices of
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
one gets the
relation
[iH,A]=aH+b
with
(a,b)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}
, from which one can infer
the estimates of Theorem
2.1
(c).

3.10
Schrödinger operators on Fock spaces

This example is motivated by
[
14
]
. Let
U
be an isometry in a Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}
and let
N
be a number operator for
U
, that is, a self-adjoint operator in

\mathcal{H}
such that
U^{*}\mathcal{D}(N)\subset\mathcal{D}(N)
and
UNU^{*}=N-1
on
\mathcal{D}(N)
. Number
operators do not always exist, but if
U
is completely non-unitary, namely if

\slim_{n\to\infty}(U^{*})^{n}=0
, then
N
exists and is unique. Next, consider the
bounded self-adjoint operators
H:=\mathop{\mathrm{Re}}\nolimits(U)
and
S:=\mathop{\mathrm{Im}}\nolimits(U)
, and set

A\varphi:=\tfrac{1}{2}(SN+NS)\varphi,\quad\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(N).

Then it is shown in
[
14
, Secs. 2-3]
that
A
is essentially self-adjoint
(with closure denoted by the same symbol) and that
H\in C^{\infty}(A)
with

[iH,A]=1-H^{2}
. Thus Proposition
2.5
applies with
f(H)=1-H^{2}
,

g(H)=(1-H^{2})\langle H\rangle^{-2}\in C^{\infty}(A)
and
\ker(f(H))=E^{H}(\{-1,1\})\mathcal{H}
.
It follows that
H
has purely a.c. spectrum in
(-1,1)
and for each

\varphi\in g(H)^{n}\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(A^{n})
(
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
) there exists a
constant
c_{\varphi,\psi}\geq 0
such that

\big|\langle\varphi,\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itH}\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{t^{n}}\;\!c_{\varphi,\psi},\quad t>0.

Operators like
H
(and additive perturbations of it) appear naturally in the
framework of Fock spaces and their applications to Schrödinger operators on trees.
Indeed, let
\mathfrak{h}
be a complex Hilbert space and

\mathcal{H}:=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathfrak{h}^{\otimes n}

Appendix A
Commutators and regularity classes

In this appendix, we recall the definitions of commutators of operators and regularity
classes associated with them that we use in this work. We refer to Chapters 5-6 of the
monograph
[
1
]
for more details.

Let
A
be a self-adjoint operator in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with domain
\mathcal{D}(A)
, and
take a bounded operator
S\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that
S
belongs to

C^{k}(A)
, with notation
S\in C^{k}(A)
, if the map

\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{-itA}S\mathop{\mathrm{e}}\nolimits^{itA}\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})

(A.1)

is strongly of class
C^{k}
. The sets
C^{k}(A)\subset\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
satisfy the inclusions

C^{\infty}(A):=\cap_{k\in\mathbb{N}}C^{k}(A)\subset\cdots\subset C^{2}(A)\subset C^{1}(A)\subset C^{0}(A)=\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H}).

In the case
k=1
, one has
S\in C^{1}(A)
if and only if the quadratic form

\mathcal{D}(A)\ni\varphi\mapsto\langle\varphi,iSA\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\langle A\varphi,iS\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathbb{C}

is continuous for the topology induced by
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathcal{D}(A)
. We denote by
[iS,A]
the
bounded operator associated with the continuous extension of this form, or
equivalently the strong derivative of the map (
A.1
) at
t=0
.

If
H
is a self-adjoint operator in
\mathcal{H}
with domain
\mathcal{D}(H)
and spectrum

\sigma(H)
, we say that
H
is of class
C^{k}(A)
if
(H-z)^{-1}\in C^{k}(A)
for some

z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma(H)
. In particular,
H
is of class
C^{1}(A)
if and only if the
quadratic form

\mathcal{D}(A)\ni\varphi\mapsto\langle\varphi,(H-z)^{-1}A\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}-\langle A\varphi,(H-z)^{-1}\varphi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\in\mathbb{C}

extends continuously to a bounded form with corresponding bounded operator denoted by

[(H-z)^{-1},A]\in\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
. In such a case, the set
\mathcal{D}(H)\cap\mathcal{D}(A)
is a core for

H
and the quadratic form

\mathcal{D}(H)\cap\mathcal{D}(A)\ni\varphi\mapsto\langle H\varphi,A\varphi\rangle-\langle A\varphi,H\varphi\rangle\in\mathbb{C}

is continuous in the graph norm topology of
\mathcal{D}(H)

[
1
, Thm. 6.2.10(a)]
.
This form then extends uniquely to a continuous quadratic form on
\mathcal{D}(H)
which can
be identified with a continuous operator
[H,A]
from
\mathcal{D}(H)
to the adjoint space

\mathcal{D}(H)^{*}
. In addition, the following relation holds in
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})

[
1
, Thm. 6.2.10(b)]
:

[(H-z)^{-1},A]=-(H-z)^{-1}[H,A](H-z)^{-1}.

Appendix B
RAGE-type theorem for unitary operators

In this appendix, we give the proof of a RAGE-type theorem for unitary operators that
we use in Section
2.3
. The theorem is surely well-known, but since we did
not find it in this form in the literature we present its proof for completeness.

We start by recalling the usual RAGE theorem for a unitary operator
U
. As in the
previous sections, we use the notation
P_{\rm p}(U)
for the projection onto the pure
point subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\rm p}(U)
of
U
and
P_{\rm c}(U)
for the projection onto the
continuous subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\rm c}(U)
of
U
.

Theorem B.1

(RAGE theorem, page 320 of
[
28
]
)
.

Let
U
be a unitary operator in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\|KU^{-m}\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\|KP_{\rm p}(U)\varphi\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\quad\hbox{for all $\varphi\in\mathcal{H}$.}

Theorem B.2
.

Let
U
be a unitary operator in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and
K\in\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}KU^{-m}=\sum_{\theta\in\scriptsize\{\hbox{eigenvalues of $U$}\}}E^{U}(\{\theta\})KE^{U}(\{\theta\}).

Proof.

We mimic the proof of the analogous theorem in the self-adjoint case

[
30
, Thm. 5.9]
. Any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
admits an orthogonal decomposition

\varphi=\varphi_{\rm p}+\varphi_{\rm c}
with
\varphi_{\rm p}\in\mathcal{H}_{\rm p}(U)
and

\varphi_{\rm c}\in\mathcal{H}_{\rm c}(U)
. For the component
\varphi_{\rm c}
, we get from
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Theorem
B.1
that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}KU^{-m}\varphi_{\rm c}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\tfrac{1}{n}\right)^{1/2}\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\big\|KU^{-m}\varphi_{\rm c}\big\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right)^{1/2}=0.

For the component
\varphi_{\rm p}
, we write

\varphi_{\rm p}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}\varphi_{j}
with
(\varphi_{j})_{j\geq 1}
an
orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\rm p}(U)
,
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
, and

U\varphi_{j}=\theta_{j}\varphi_{j}
for some
\theta_{j}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Then we get

\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}KU^{-m}\varphi_{\rm p}=\slim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}\left(\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(U\theta_{j}^{-1})^{m}\right)K\varphi_{j}.

(B.1)

Now we have

\|\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(U\theta_{j}^{-1})^{m}\|_{\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})}\leq 1

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and

\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(U\theta_{j}^{-1})^{m}=E^{U}(\{\theta_{j}\})

due to von Neumann’s mean ergodic theorem. Therefore we can exchange the limit and the
sum in (
B.1
) to obtain

\slim_{n\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}U^{m}KU^{-m}\varphi_{\rm p}=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}E^{U}(\{\theta_{j}\})K\varphi_{j}=\sum_{\theta\in\scriptsize\{\hbox{eigenvalues of $U$}\}}E^{U}(\{\theta\})KE^{U}(\{\theta\})\varphi,

as desired.
∎

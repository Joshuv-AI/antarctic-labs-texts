Electric-dual BPS Vortices in The Generalized Self-dual Maxwell-Chern-Simons-Higgs Model
Laurenzius Yudha Prasetya Tama 1, Bobby Eka Gunara 1, Ardian Nata Atmaja 2

Abstract
In this paper we show how to derive the Bogomolny’s equations of the generalized self-dual Maxwell-Chern-Simons-Higgs model presented in Bazeia et al. 2012a by using the BPS Lagrangian method with a particular choice of the BPS Lagrangian density. We also show that the identification, potential terms, and Gauss’s law constraint can be derived rigorously under the BPS Lagrangian method. In this method, we find that the potential terms are the most general form that could have the BPS vortex solutions. The Gauss’s law constraint turns out to be the Euler-Lagrange equations of the BPS Lagrangian density. We also find another BPS vortex solutions by taking other identification between the neutral scalar field and the electric scalar potential field, N = ± A 0 N=\pm A_{0} , which is different by a relative sign to the identification in Bazeia et al. 2012a , N = ∓ A 0 N=\mp A_{0} . Under this identification, N = ± A 0 N=\pm A_{0} , we obtain a slightly different potential terms and Bogomolny’s equations compared to the ones in Bazeia et al. 2012a . Furthermore we compute the solutions numerically, with the same configurations as in Bazeia et al. 2012a , and find that only the resulting electric field plots differ by sign relative to the results in Bazeia et al. 2012a . Therefore we conclude that these BPS vortices are electric-dual BPS vortices of the ones computed in Bazeia et al. 2012a .



I
Introduction

In the classical picture of some certain nonlinear field theories we may have a solution called soliton which differs from the usual elementary particles that are obtained from the quantization of the wave-like excitation of the fields. This object has topological structure and finite energy that is localized in space, see for example,
Manton and Sutcliffe 2004
. There are several kind of solitons: kink, vortex, domain walls, and monopole. Some of them are endowed with a potential that possess a nontrivial vacuum structure, see for example,
Weinberg 2012
.

Particularly, our interest turns to consider solitons with co-dimension two or known as vortices in the model with
U(1)
symmetry. These vortices can be classified into two categories, namely, global and gauged vortices that corresponds to the global and local
U(1)
transformations, respectively. The energy density for global vortices diverge at large
r
. However, the existence of these solutions are still physically relevant in three dimensions such as the cosmic strings
Kibble 1976
. In gauged vortices, the
U(1)
gauge field is added to the Lagrangian so that it remains invariant. With the addition of gauge field, the divergence that occurs in the energy density vanishes so that the total energy to this solution is proportional to the magnetic flux. Due to its finite energy solution, gauged vortices have interesting application in the superconductors of the second group
Abrikosov 1957
;
Nielsen and Olesen 1973
.

In the canonical model of gauged vortices, the kinetics of the gauge field is described by the Maxwell term. Nevertheless, there are another possible kinetic term to the gauge field that remains gauge invariant, that is, the Chern-Simons term. In general, this term could exist in any odd-dimensional spacetime. Particularly, in
2+1
-dimensional spacetime it has a quadratic form in the gauge field
Dunne 1999
. In the canonical model with only the Maxwell term, there are well-known stable vortices with some particular choice of coupling constants and potential, which turned out to be solutions of first-order differential equations known as the Bogomolny’s equations
Nielsen and Olesen 1973
;
Bogomol’nyi 1976
. On the other hand, the canonical model with only the Chern-Simons term also found to have stable vortices
Paul and Khare 1986
. For the canonical model with both Maxwell and Chern-Simons terms, the existance of additional neutral scalar filed is necessary in order to have stable vortices
Lee et al. 1990
. The first-order differential formulation in this study leads into a conclusion that there is an identification between the scalar gauge potential and the neutral scalar field. Moreover, there is a recent study that generalizes this idea by introducing coupling functions of the scalar fields to the Maxwell term and all kinetic terms of the scalar fields
Bazeia et al. 2012a
. In this generalization, the coupling functions between Maxwell term and the kinetic term of the neutral scalar field are identical. This allows identification between the neutral scalar field and the scalar gauge potential field to be hold which later give us the Bogomolny’s equations and their solutions, or known as BPS vortices.

In this paper, we investigate BPS vortices in the generalized self-dual Maxwell-Chern-Simons-Higgs model in
Bazeia et al. 2012a
using the BPS Lagrangian method developed in
Nata Atmaja 2017
. The BPS Langrangian method is used to find a set of first-order differential equations, called Bogomolny’s equations, that solve the second-order differential equations of the equations of motion in the aforementioned generalized self-dual Maxwell-Chern-Simons-Higgs model. We would like to answer a question if the BPS Lagrangian method could reproduce rigorously all the results in
Bazeia et al. 2012a
and furthermore if there are other possible potential terms that could lead to different Bogomolny’s equations and BPS vortices.

This paper consist of four sections including this one. In Sec.
II
, the radially symmetric ansatz is applied to the Lagrangian density of this model. In Sec.
III
, the analysis to obtain the BPS equations is presented. The solution to the obtained BPS equations is done by numerical analysis. We conclude this study in Sec.
IV
, where we give our final comment to the obtained solution and discuss the possibility for further research.

II
Generalized Self-dual Maxwell-Chern-Simons-Higgs Model

The model that has been presented in
Bazeia et al. 2012a
is described by the Lagrangian density of the form

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}&=-\frac{h(|\phi|,N)}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}-\frac{\kappa}{4}\epsilon^{\mu\rho\sigma}A_{\mu}F_{\rho\sigma}+w(|\phi|)|D_{\mu}\phi|^{2}+\frac{h(|\phi|,N)}{2}\partial_{\mu}N\partial^{\mu}N-V(|\phi|,N),\end{split}

(1)

where
h(|\phi|,N)
and
w(|\phi|)
are dimensionless positive functions of the scalar fields,
F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}
is the usual electromagnetic field strength tensor,
D_{\mu}\phi=\partial_{\mu}\phi+ieA_{\mu}\phi
is the covariant derivative of the Higgs field, and neutral scalar field
N
was added to stabilize the self-dual solutions
Bazeia et al. 2012a
. In this paper, we first set the potential
V(|\phi|,N)
to be arbitrary.

The dynamics of the fields in this model are described by the Euler-Lagrange equations. The equations of motion for the gauge fieds are given by

\partial_{\mu}\big(hF^{\mu\nu}\big)+J^{\nu}=\kappa F^{\nu}~,

(2)

where
J^{\nu}=iew\big((D^{\nu}\phi)^{*}\phi-\phi^{*}D^{\nu}\phi\big)
is the electromagnetic current and
F^{\nu}=(1/2)\epsilon^{\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\rho\sigma}
is the electromagnetic dual vector. From this equation, one can show that the temporal gauge,
A_{0}=0
, cannot be used since it will lead to the trivial solution. The remaining equations of motion for the scalar fields,
\phi
and
N
respectively, are

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\big(w(|\phi|)D^{\mu}\phi\big)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\phi}h\left(F_{\mu}F^{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}N\partial^{\mu}N\right)-\partial_{\phi}w|D_{\mu}\phi|^{2}+\partial_{\phi}V=0

(3)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\big(h\partial^{\mu}N\big)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{N}h\big(F_{\mu}F^{\mu}-\partial_{\mu}N\partial^{\mu}N\big)+\partial_{N}V=0.

(4)

In order to solve these equations of motion we impose the usual radially symmetric ansatz

\phi(r,\theta)=g(r)e^{in\theta}~,\quad N\equiv N(r)~,\quad A_{0}\equiv A_{0}(r)~,

(5)

\textbf{A}(r,\theta)=-\frac{\hat{\theta}}{r}\left(a(r)-n\right)~,

(6)

where
n=\pm 1,\pm 2,\pm 3,\ldots
is the winding number.
Hereafter, we will use the metric
\eta^{\mu\nu}=\text{diag}(+,-,-)
and a unit such that
e=v=\kappa=1
, where
v
is the vacuum expectation value of the Higgs scalar field. Under the ansatz
5
, the Lagrangian density of this model can be effectively written as

\mathcal{L}=\frac{h}{2}{A^{\prime}}_{0}^{2}-\frac{h}{2}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{2}-A_{0}\frac{a^{\prime}}{r}-w\left({g^{\prime}}^{2}+\frac{{a^{2}}{g^{2}}}{r^{2}}\right)+{A_{0}^{2}}{g^{2}}w-\frac{h}{2}{N^{\prime}}^{2}-V(g,N),

(7)

where
h\equiv h(g,N)
,
w\equiv w(g)
, and
{}^{\prime}\equiv{d\over dr}
.

III
BPS Lagrangian Method

It has been shown in
Nata Atmaja 2017
that the BPS Lagrangian method was able to rederive the Bogomolny’s equations of the Maxwell-Higgs and Born-Infeld-Higgs models. This method uses the fact that the (effective) Lagrangian density of a system must contains at most up to quadratic of first-derivative of the effective fields. Under this requirement the effective Lagrangian density of a model with
N
-scalar fields can be rewritten into the following form

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\partial_{\mu}{\phi^{i}}-f_{\mu}^{i}\left(\phi^{j},\partial_{\nu}\phi^{j};x^{\nu}\right)\right)^{2}+\mathcal{L}_{BPS}~,

(8)

where in general
f_{\mu}^{i}
is a function of fields
\phi^{j}
’s and their first-derivative
\partial_{\nu}\phi^{j}
’s, and may also explicit coordinates
x^{\nu}
, but not of
\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}
, with
j=1,\ldots,N
. Here we shall call
\mathcal{L}_{BPS}
as BPS Lagrangian density which in general is a function is a function of fields
\phi^{j}
’s and their first-derivative
\partial_{\nu}\phi^{j}
’s. For most of the known cases,
\mathcal{L}_{BPS}
contains at most first-derivative of the effective fields up to the power one. The Bogomolny’s equations are obtained in the limit where
\mathcal{L}-\mathcal{L}_{BPS}=0
, or also known as the BPS limit condition, which, from (
8
), are given by

\partial_{\mu}\phi^{i}=f_{\mu}^{i}\left(\phi^{j},\partial_{\nu}\phi^{j},x^{\nu}\right).

(9)

The BPS Lagrangian density plays an important role in the BPS Lagrangian method. Its Euler-Lagrange equations, which are called constraint equations, must be considered in finding solitonic solutions, in addition to the Bogomolny’s equations. Depending on the choice of terms in the BPS Lagrangian density, its Euler-Lagrange equations could be all trivial in the BPS limit. In this case the BPS Lagrangian density posses boundary terms such that it does not contribute to the equations of motion of the model. Type of boundary terms that could be included in the BPS Lagrangian density can be found in
Adam and Santamaria 2016
.
In general, the BPS Lagrangian density containing both boundary and non-boundary terms can be written as follows

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{BPS}=&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det(g_{mn})}}\left(D_{\mu}F^{\mu}(\phi^{a},x^{\nu})+\sum_{j=2}^{\min(N,d)}F^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{j}}_{{a_{1}}...{a_{j}}}(\phi^{a})~\phi^{a_{1}}_{,\mu_{1}}...\phi^{a_{j}}_{,\mu_{j}}\right)\\
&+\sum_{p,q=1}^{N}X_{pq}^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}(\phi^{a},x^{\nu}){\partial_{\mu_{1}}\phi^{p}}{\partial_{\mu_{2}}\phi^{q}}~,\end{split}

(10)

where
F^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{j}}_{{a_{1}}...{a_{j}}}
is totally anti-symmetric tensor in all indices,
D_{\mu}
is total derivative, and the notation
{}_{,\mu_{1}}\equiv\partial_{\mu_{1}}
. The function
X_{pq}^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}
is not totally anti-symmetric tensor which correspond to the non-boundary terms of the BPS Lagrangian density. Previous study has been made regarding this non-boundary term of the BPS Lagrangian in
Atmaja 2020
which results in non-zero stress tensor of the generalized Born-Infeld-Higgs model.

III.1
Bogomolny’s equations

For the purpose of rederiving the Bogomolny’s equations of this model as in
Bazeia et al. 2012b
, with regard to the defined ansatze in (

(11)

\begin{split}&w{g^{\prime}}^{2}-\frac{X_{1}}{r}{g^{\prime}}+\frac{h}{2}\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{r}\right)^{2}-({X_{2}}-{A_{0}})\frac{a^{\prime}}{r}+\left(\frac{h}{2}-X_{3}\right){N^{\prime}}^{2}-\left(\frac{h}{2}+X_{4}\right){A^{\prime}}_{0}^{2}\\

The above expression can be seen as a quadratic equation in the first-derivative of the fields. At first, we can consider it as a quadratic equation in

\displaystyle{g^{\prime}}_{\pm}=

\displaystyle\frac{{X_{1}}\pm r\sqrt{S_{1}}}{2wr}~,

&-\frac{1}{2}h\left(N^{\prime}\right)^{2}+X_{3}\left(N^{\prime}\right)^{2}-V+X_{0}\bigg)+\frac{X_{1}^{2}}{r^{2}}~.\end{split}

(14)

The Bogomolny’s equation for

\displaystyle\frac{4w({X_{2}}-{A_{0}})\pm r\sqrt{S_{2}}}{4hw}~,

(15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{S_{2}}=&\frac{8hw}{r^{2}}\bigg(2w{A^{\prime}_{0}}^{2}\left(2X_{4}+h\right)+2wN^{\prime 2}\left(2X_{3}-h\right)\bigg)+\frac{8hw}{r^{4}}\left(X_{1}^{2}-4a^{2}g^{2}w^{2}\right)\\

Here we can see that there are no linear terms of

IV
Conclusion

We have shown how a particular choice of BPS Lagrangian density (
11
) is sufficient to reproduce rigorously all the results in Ref.
Bazeia et al. 2012a
by using the BPS Lagrangian method
Nata Atmaja 2017
. We would like to point out that the potential (
32
) was derived rigorously under the BPS Lagrangian method and it is the most general potential, relative to the BPS Lagrangian density (
11
), that could bear the BPS vortices. Moreover we did not need to impose a priori the Gauss’s law constraint equation, such as in Ref.
Bazeia et al. 2012a
, since it is also derived rigorously as the constraint equation (
27
), or similarly (
30
), of the BPS Lagrangian density (
11
) in the BPS limit.

We also found another possible identification with
A_{0}=\pm N
, which is different by sign, that leads to potential (
33
). We calculated and compared our solutions with the ones in Ref.
Bazeia et al. 2012a
. As we observed from the FIG.
3
., our solutions describe BPS vortex solutions with negative electric charge which are opposite to the ones obtained in Ref.
Bazeia et al. 2012a
. One can simply check that the Bogomolny’s and constraint equations, along with the potential, can be obtained from the corresponding equations in Ref.
Bazeia et al. 2012a
by transforming the neutral scalar field
N\to-N
. This also the reason why the BPS vortex solutions have negative electric charge, even if
e=1
, since the electric charge density
J^{0}\propto N
. Here we may conclude that BPS vortices with identification
A_{0}=\pm N
are electrically dual to the BPS vortices with identification
A_{0}=\mp N
as found in Ref.
Bazeia et al. 2012a
.

The BPS Lagrangian density (
11
) is not the only possible BPS Lagrangian density that one could consider. The most general BPS Lagrangian density consist of all possible terms would be

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}{BPS}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle X{0}+\frac{X_{1}}{r}{a^{\prime}}+\frac{X_{2}}{r}{g^{\prime}}+\frac{X_{3}}{r}{N^{\prime}}+\frac{X_{4}}{r}{A^{\prime}_{0}}

(44)

\displaystyle+X_{5}{a^{\prime}}^{2}+X_{6}{a^{\prime}g^{\prime}}+X_{7}{a^{\prime}N^{\prime}}+X_{8}{a^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+X_{9}{g^{\prime}}^{2}+X_{10}{g^{\prime}N^{\prime}}+X_{11}{g^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+X_{12}{N^{\prime}}^{2}+X_{13}{N^{\prime}A_{0}^{\prime}}+X_{14}{A_{0}^{\prime}}^{2}.

Depending on the choice of terms in the BPS Lagrangian density, the resulting Bogomolny’s and constraint equations, as well as the form of potential terms and functions
h
and
w
, could be different from the ones obtained here. Other possibles BPS Lagrangian density will be studied elsewhere.

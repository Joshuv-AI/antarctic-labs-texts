CUP: A Conservative Update Policy Algorithm for Safe Reinforcement Learning L.Yang do partial work of this submission when studying at Zhejiang University, and L.Yang now is with Peking University.
Long Yang, Jiaming Ji, Juntao Dai, Linrui Zhang, Yu Zhang, Pengfei Li, Gang Pan

Abstract
Safe reinforcement learning (RL) is still very challenging since it requires the agent to consider both return maximization and safe exploration. In this paper, we propose CUP, a C onservative U pdate P olicy algorithm with a theoretical safety guarantee. We derive the CUP based on the new proposed performance bounds and surrogate functions. Although using bounds as surrogate functions to design safe RL algorithms have appeared in some existing works, we develop them at least three aspects: (i) We provide a rigorous theoretical analysis to extend the surrogate functions to generalized advantage estimator (GAE). GAE significantly reduces variance empirically while maintaining a tolerable level of bias, which is an efficient step for us to design CUP; (ii) The proposed bounds are tighter than existing works, i.e., using the proposed bounds as surrogate functions are better local approximations to the objective and safety constraints. (iii) The proposed CUP provides a non-convex implementation via first-order optimizers, which does not depend on any convex approximation. Finally, extensive experiments show the effectiveness of CUP where the agent satisfies safe constraints. We have opened the source code of CUP at https://github.com/RL-boxes/Safe-RL .



1
Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL)
[
Sutton and Barto, 1998
]
has achieved significant successes in many fields
[
Mnih et al., 2015
,
Silver et al., 2017
,
OpenAI, 2019
,
Afsar et al., 2021
]
,Dota
[
OpenAI, 2019
]
,
robotics
[
Deisenroth et al., 2013
]
, playing Go
[
Silver et al., 2016
,
Silver et al., 2017
]
,
recommendation system
[
Afsar et al., 2021
]
,
and Starcraft
[
Vinyals et al., 2019
]
.
However, most RL algorithms improve the performance under the assumption that an agent is free to explore any behaviors.
In real-world applications, only considering return maximization is not enough, and we also need to consider safe behaviors.
For example, a robot agent should avoid playing actions that irrevocably harm its hardware, and a recommender system should avoid presenting offending items to users.
Thus, it is crucial to consider
safe exploration
for RL, which is usually formulated as constrained Markov decision processes (CMDP)
[
Altman, 1999
]
.

It is challenging to solve CMDP since traditional approaches (e.g., Q-learning
[
Watkins, 1989
]
& policy gradient
[
Williams, 1992
]
) usually violate the safe exploration constraints, which is undesirable for safe RL.
Recently,
[
Achiam et al., 2017
,
Yang et al., 2020b
,
Bharadhwaj et al., 2021
]
suggest to use some surrogate functions to replace the objective and constraints.
However, their implementations involve some convex approximations to the non-convex objective and safe constraints,
which leads to many error sources and troubles.
Concretely,
[
Achiam et al., 2017
,
Yang et al., 2020b
,
Bharadhwaj et al., 2021
]
approximate the non-convex objective (or constraints) with first-order or second Taylor expansion,
but their implementations still lack a theory to show the error difference between the original objective (or constraints) and its convex approximations.
Besides, their approaches involve the inverse of a high-dimension Fisher information matrix, which causes their algorithms to require a costly computation for each update when solving high-dimensional RL problems.

1.1
Our Main Work

To address above problems, we propose the
conservative update policy
(CUP) algorithm with a theoretical safety guarantee.
We derive the CUP bases on new surrogate functions, and provide a practical implementation of CUP that does not depend on any convex approximation to adapt high-dimensional safe RL.

Concretely, in Section
3
, Theorem
3.1
shows generalized difference bounds between two arbitrary policies for the objective and constraints.
Those bounds provide principled approximations to the objective and constraints, which are theoretical foundations for us to use those bounds as surrogate functions to replace
objective and constraints to design algorithms.

Although using difference bound to replace objective or constraints has appeared in some existing works (e.g.,
[
Kakade and Langford, 2002
,
Schulman et al., 2015
,
Achiam et al., 2017
]
), Theorem
3.1
improves their bounds at least two aspects:

(i)
Firstly, our rigorous theoretical analysis extends the bound w.r.t. generalized advantage estimator (GAE)
[
Schulman et al., 2016
]
.
GAE significantly reduces variance while maintains a tolerable level of bias, which is one of the critical steps for us to design efficient algorithms in the later section.
Although
[
Zhang et al., 2020
,
Kang et al., 2021
]
have applied GAE to solve safe RL problems, their approaches are empirical and lack a theoretical analysis w.r.t. GAE.
Thus, our result provides a theory to illustrate the effectiveness of the work
[
Zhang et al., 2020
,
Kang et al., 2021
]
.

(ii)
Our new bounds in refine classic difference bounds.
For example, our bounds are more compact than
[
Achiam et al., 2017
]
, i,e., using our new bounds as surrogate functions are better local approximations to the objective and constraints.
Besides, the surrogate functions w.r.t. our new bounds are more accessible to be estimated from the samples than the approaches appears in
[
Kakade and Langford, 2002
,
Schulman et al., 2015
]
), for more discussions, please see Remark
3.1
.

In Section
4
, we provide the necessary details of the proposed CUP.
The CUP contains two steps: it performs a policy improvement at first, then it projects the policy back onto the safe region to reconcile the constraint violation.
Theorem
4.1
shows a lower bound on policy improvement and an upper bound on constraint violation for CPU at each update.
Notably, the result in Theorem
4.1
shows the bound of CUP is more compact than state-of-the-art safe RL algorithms: CPO
[
Achiam et al., 2017
, Proposition 1-2]
, PCPO
[
Yang et al., 2020b
, Theorem 1]
and FOCOPS
[
Zhang et al., 2020
]
, which provides a partial explanation for why CUP is so good in practice.
For more discussions, please refer to Remark
4.1
.
Finally, we provide a practical implementation of sample-based CUP. Such an implementation allows us to use deep neural networks to train a model.
Mainly, CUP does not depend on any convex approximation for objective and constraints, and it optimizes the objective according to the first-order optimizer.
Extensive high-dimensional experiments on continuous control tasks show the effectiveness of CUP where the agent satisfies safe constraints.

Preliminaries

Reinforcement learning (RL)
[
Sutton and Barto, 1998
]
is often formulated as
a
Markov decision process
(MDP)
[
Puterman, 2014
]
that is a tuple
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P},{r},\rho_{0},\gamma)
.
Here
\mathcal{S}
is state space,
\mathcal{A}
is action space.

\mathbb{P}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)
is probability of state transition from
s
to
s^{{}^{\prime}}
after playing
a
.

r(\cdot):\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
and
r(s^{\prime}|s,a)
denotes the reward that the agent observes when state transition from
s
to
s^{{}^{\prime}}
after it plays
a
.

\rho_{0}(\cdot):\mathcal{S}\rightarrow[0,1]
is the initial state distribution and
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

A stationary parameterized policy
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
is a probability distribution defined on
\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
,
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(a|s)
denotes the probability of playing
a
in state
s
.
We use
\Pi_{\bm{\theta}}
to denote the set of all stationary policies, where
\Pi_{{{\bm{\theta}}}}=\{\pi_{{{\bm{\theta}}}}:{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\}
, and
{{\bm{\theta}}}
is a parameter needed to be learned.
Let
\mathbf{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{S}|}
be a state transition probability matrix, and their components are:

\mathbf{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[s,s^{\prime}]=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(a|s)\mathbb{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a)=:\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s),

which denotes one-step state transformation probability from
s
to
s^{{}^{\prime}}
by executing
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
.
Let
\tau=\{s_{t},a_{t},r_{t+1}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a trajectory generated by
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
,
where
s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)
,
a_{t}\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s_{t})
,
s_{t+1}\sim\mathbb{P}(\cdot|s_{t},a_{t})
, and
r_{t+1}=r(s_{t+1}|s_{t},a_{t})
.
We use
\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)
to denote the probability of visiting the state
s^{{}^{\prime}}
after
t

time steps from the state
s
by executing
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
.
Due to the Markov property,
\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)
is
(s,s^{{}^{\prime}})
-th component of the matrix
\mathbf{P}^{t}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}
, i.e.,

\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)=\mathbf{P}^{t}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[s,s^{{}^{\prime}}].

Finally, let
d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{s_{0}}(s)=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})
be the stationary state distribution of the Markov chain (starting at
s_{0}
) induced by policy
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
.
We define

d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)}[d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{s_{0}}(s)]

as the discounted state visitation distribution on initial distribution
\rho_{0}(\cdot)
.

The
state value function
of
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
is defined as
V_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r_{t+1}|s_{0}=s],

where
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[\cdot|\cdot]
denotes a conditional expectation on actions which are selected by
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
.
Its
state-action value function
is
Q_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s,a)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}r_{t+1}|s_{0}=s,a_{0}=a]
,
and advantage function is
A_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s,a)=Q_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s,a)-V_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s)
.
The goal of reinforcement learning is to maximize
J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})
:

\displaystyle J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})=\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot)}[V_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s)].

(1)

2.1
Policy Gradient and GAE

Policy gradient
[
Williams, 1992
,
Sutton et al., 2000
]
is widely used to solve policy optimization, which maximizes the expected total reward by repeatedly estimating the gradient
\nabla J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})
.
The work

[
Schulman et al., 2016
]
summarize several different related expressions for the policy gradient:

\displaystyle\nabla J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})=\E\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\Psi_{t}\nabla\log\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(a_{t}|s_{t})\right],

(2)

where
\Psi_{t}
can be total discounted reward of the trajectory, value function, advantage function or temporal difference (TD) error.
As stated by
[
Schulman et al., 2016
]
, the choice
\Psi_{t}=A(s_{t},a_{t})
yields almost the lowest possible variance, which is consistent with the theoretical analysis
[
Greensmith et al., 2004
,
Wu et al., 2018
]
.
Furthermore,
[
Schulman et al., 2016
]
propose generalized advantage estimator (GAE)
\hat{A}^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})
to replace
\Psi_{t}
: for any
\lambda\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{t}(s_{t},a_{t})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\lambda)^{\ell}\delta^{V}_{t+\ell},

(3)

where
\delta^{V}_{t}=r_{t+1}+\gamma V(s_{t+1})-V(s_{t})
is TD error, and
V(\cdot)
is an estimator of value function.
GAE is an efficient technique for data efficiency and reliable performance of reinforcement learning.

2.2
Safe Reinforcement Learning

Safe RL is often formulated as
a constrained MDP (CMDP)
\mathcal{M}\cup\mathcal{C}

[
Altman, 1999
]
,
which is a standard MDP
\mathcal{M}
augmented with an additional constraint set
\mathcal{C}
.
The set
\mathcal{C}=\{(c_{i},b_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
,
where
c_{i}
are cost functions:
c_{i}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and limits are
b_{i}
,
i=1,\cdot,m
.
The
cost-return
is defined as:

J^{c_{i}}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})=\E_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c_{i}(s_{t},a_{t})\right]
,
the feasible policy set
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}
is defined as:

\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\left\{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}\in\Pi_{\bm{\theta}}~~\text{and}~~J^{c_{i}}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})_{\leq}b_{i}\right\}.

The goal of safe RL is to search the optimal policy
\pi_{\star}
s.t.

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}=\arg\max_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}\in\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}}J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}}).

(4)

Furthermore, we define value functions, action-value functions, and advantage functions for the auxiliary costs in analogy to
V_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}},Q_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}
, and
A_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}
,
with
c_{i}
replacing
r
respectively, we denote them as
V^{c_{i}}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}},Q^{c_{i}}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}
, and
A^{c_{i}}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}
.
For example,
V^{c_{i}}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}c_{i}(s_{t},a_{t})|s_{0}=s\right]
.
Without loss of generality, we will restrict our discussion to the case of one constraint with a cost function
c
and upper bound
b
.
Finally, we extend the GAE w.r.t. auxiliary cost function
c
:

\displaystyle\hat{A}^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{C,t}(s_{t},a_{t})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\lambda)^{\ell}\delta^{C}_{t+\ell},

(5)

where
\delta^{C}_{t}=r_{t+1}+\gamma C(s_{t+1})-C(s_{t})
is TD error, and
C(\cdot)
is an estimator of cost function
c
.

3
Generalized Policy Performance Difference Bounds

In this section, we show some generalized policy optimization performance bounds for
J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})
and
J^{c}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})
.
The proposed bounds provide some new certain surrogate functions w.r.t. the objective and cost function, which are theoretical foundations for us to design efficient algorithms to improve policy performance and satisfy constraints.
Additionally, those bounds refine or extend some existing works (e.g.,
[
Kakade and Langford, 2002
,
Schulman et al., 2015

Achiam et al., 2017
]
) to GAE case that significantly
reduces variance while maintains a tolerable level of bias, which is one of the key steps for us to propose efficient algorithms in the later section.

Before we present our new bounds, let us revisit a classic result about policy performance difference from
[

]
, i.e., the next Eq.(

\displaystyle J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}})-J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})=(1-\gamma)^{-1}\E_{s\sim d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}(s,a)\right].

(6)

Eq.(

) shows a difference between two arbitrary policies
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
and
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}
with different parameters
\bm{\theta}
and
\bm{\theta}^{{}^{\prime}}
.
According to (

4
) as follows

\displaystyle\pi_{\star}=\arg\max_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}\in\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}}\E_{s\sim d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}(s,a)\right].

(7)

However, Eq.(
6
) or (
7
) is very intractable for sampling-based policy optimization since it requires the data comes from the (unknown) policy
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
that needed to be learned.

In this section, our new bound refines the result (
6
), which provide the sights for surrogate functions to solve safe RL problem (
4
).
For more discussions about the difference between our new bound and Eq.(

3.1

\displaystyle J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})-J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})\geq\dfrac{1}{1-\tilde{\gamma}}\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{\emph{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}(s,a)-\frac{2\gamma(1-\lambda)\epsilon^{V}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})}{(1-\gamma\lambda)\left|1-2\gamma\lambda|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\right|}D_{\emph{TV}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right].

(11)

If
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, then the distribution
{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)
is reduced to
{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\rho_{0}}(\cdot)
and the bound (
11
) is reduced to

\displaystyle J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})-J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\rho_{0}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}(s,a)-2\gamma\epsilon^{V}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})D_{\text{TV}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right].

(12)

Let us review
[
Achiam et al., 2017
, Corollary 1]
, which shows

\displaystyle J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})-J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})\geq\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\rho_{0}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}(s,a)-2\frac{\gamma\epsilon^{V}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})}{1-\gamma}D_{\text{TV}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right].

(13)

Comparing (
12
) to (
13
), our new bound (
12
) is slightly tighter than the bound shown by the work
[
Achiam et al., 2017
]
, concretely, our result improves the bound (
13
) by a factor
\dfrac{1}{1-\gamma}
.
The bound (
13
) has been used as a surrogate function for
J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})-J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})
, and this idea has been developed as
constrained policy optimization
by extensive works (e.g.,
[
Koller et al., 2018
,
Zhang et al., 2020
,
Yang et al., 2020a
,
Yang et al., 2020b
,
Zanger et al., 2021
]
).
Since the refined bound (
11
) contains GAE technique that significantly reduces variance while maintains a tolerable level of bias
[
Schulman et al., 2016
]
, which implies using the bound (
11
) as a surrogate function could improve performance potentially.

Remark 3.1

(Comparison with
[
Kakade and Langford, 2002
]
)
.

The result (
11
) develops the classic performance difference (
6
) at least two aspects.
Firstly, the bound (
11
) extends from the advantage
A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}
(
6
) to GAE function
A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\emph{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}
.
Secondly, the following term in Eq.(
11
):

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{1-\tilde{\gamma}}\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{\emph{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}(s,a)\right]

(14)

is an approximation for the difference
J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})-J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})
,
while Eq.(
6
) shows an identity for difference
J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})-J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})
.
It seems that Eq.(
6
) is a natural objective for return maximization, however, Eq.(
6
) is very intractable for sampling-based policy optimization since Eq.(
6
) requires the data comes from a the policy
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
that needed to be learned.
The approximation (
14
) solves this problem by the expectation over the state distribution
d_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)
w.r.t. policy
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}
and action distribution of another policy
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
.
Thus, bound (
11
) provides a tractable objective for sample-based optimization.

Let
\varphi=V^{c}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}
, Theorem
3.1
implies an upper bound of cost function as presented in the next Proposition
3.3
, we will use it to make guarantee for safe policy optimization.

Proposition 3.3
.

For any two policies
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
and
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}
, let
\epsilon^{C}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})=:\sup_{t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}}\{\epsilon^{\varphi}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}},t}:\varphi=V^{c}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}\}
, then
the following bound holds

\displaystyle J^{c}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})-J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})\leq\dfrac{1}{1-\tilde{\gamma}}\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{\emph{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},C}(s,a)+\frac{2\gamma(1-\lambda)\epsilon^{C}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})}{(1-\gamma\lambda)\left|1-2\gamma\lambda|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\right|}D_{\emph{TV}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right],

(15)

where we calculate
A^{\emph{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},C}(s,a)
according to the data sampled from
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}
and (
5
).

All above bound results (
11
) and (
15
) can be extended for a total variational divergence to KL-divergence between policies, which are desirable for policy optimization.
We obtain

\displaystyle\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\left[D_{\text{TV}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right]\leq

\displaystyle\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\left[\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\text{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]}\right]\leq\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\left[\text{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right]},

(16)

where
\text{KL}(\cdot,\cdot)
is KL-divergence, and

\text{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]=\text{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}(\cdot|s),\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s));

the first inequality follows Pinsker’s inequality
[
Csiszár and Körner, 2011
]
and the second inequality follows Jensen’s inequality.
According to (
16
), we obtain the next Proposition
3.4
.

Proposition 3.4
.

All the bounds in (
11
) and (
15
) hold if we make the following substitution:

\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\left[D_{\emph{TV}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right]\leftarrow\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\left[\emph{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right]}.

4
CUP: Conservative Update Policy

According to the bounds in Proposition
3.2
-
3.4
, we develop new surrogate functions to replace the objective and constraints.
Inspired by two recent works
[
Yang et al., 2020b
,
Zhang et al., 2020
]
,
we propose the CUP (conservative update policy) algorithm that is a two-step approach contains
performance improvement
and
projection
.
Theorem
4.1
shows the proposed CUP guarantees the policy improvement and safe constraints.

4.1
Methodology

Step 1: Performance Improvement.

According to Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.4
, for an appropriate coefficient
\alpha_{k}
, we update policy as follows,

\displaystyle\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\Pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}}\left\{\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}[A^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}}(s,a)]-\alpha_{k}\sqrt{\E_{s\sim{d}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\left[\text{KL}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right]}\right\}

(17)

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\Pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}}\left\{\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}s\sim{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)\\
a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(\cdot|s)\end{subarray}}{\E}\left[\frac{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(a|s)}{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(a|s)}A^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}}(s,a)\right]-\alpha_{k}\sqrt{\E_{s\sim{d}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\left[\text{KL}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right]}\right\}.

(18)

We replace (
17
) with an importance sampling with respect to
\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}
to obtain the expectation (
18
), and all the remains is to replace the expectation (
18
) by sample averages according to the trajectories collected by
\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}
.

Step 2: Projection.

According to Proposition
3.3
and Proposition
3.4
, for an appropriate coefficient
\beta_{k}
, we project the policy
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
onto the safe constraint set.
Concretely, we use a measure
D(\cdot,\cdot)
(e.g., KL divergence or
\ell_{2}
-norm) to minimize distance between
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}
and
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
, and require the new policy satisfies the safe constraint:

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}}=\arg\min_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\Pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}}~D\left(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

(19)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})+\dfrac{1}{1-\tilde{\gamma}}\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}},C}(s,a)\right]+\beta_{k}\sqrt{\E_{s\sim{d}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\left[\text{KL}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}})[s]\right]}\leq b.

Until now, the particular choice of surrogate function is heuristically motivated, we show the policy and safe constraint guarantee of the proposed CUP in Theorem
4.1
, and its proof shown in Appendix
G
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\chi_{k}=\E_{s\sim{d}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\left[\emph{KL}\left(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}},\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)[s]\right]
, if
\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}
and

\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k+1}}
are related to (
18
)-(
19
),
then the lower bound on policy improvement, and upper bound on constraint violation are

\displaystyle J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k+1}})-J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}})\geq-\frac{\gamma(1-\lambda)\alpha_{k}\sqrt{2\chi_{k}}\epsilon^{V}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})}{(1-\gamma)\left|1-2\gamma\lambda|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\right|},J^{c}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k+1}})\leq b+\frac{\gamma(1-\lambda)\beta_{k}\sqrt{2\chi_{k}}\epsilon^{C}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})}{(1-\gamma)\left|1-2\gamma\lambda|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|\right|}.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\lambda\rightarrow 0
, according to Theorem
4.1
, the performance and cost constraint of CUP satisfies

\displaystyle J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k+1}})-J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}})\geq-\frac{\gamma\alpha_{k}\sqrt{2\chi_{k}}\epsilon^{V}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})}{(1-\gamma)},J^{c}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k+1}})\leq b+\frac{\gamma\beta_{k}\sqrt{2\chi_{k}}\epsilon^{C}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})}{(1-\gamma)}.

(20)

The bounds of CUP in (
20
) achieves at
\mathcal{O}(\frac{\alpha_{k}\gamma}{1-\gamma})
or
\mathcal{O}(\frac{\beta_{k}\gamma}{1-\gamma})
,
which is more tight than the bounds of CPO
[
Achiam et al., 2017
, Proposition 1-2]
, PCPO
[
Yang et al., 2020b
, Theorem 1]
and FOCOPS
[
Zhang et al., 2020
]
where their bounds achieve at
\mathcal{O}(\frac{\gamma}{(1-\gamma)^{2}})
.

Practical Implementation

Now, we present our sample-based implementation for CUP (
18
)-(
19
).
Our main idea is to estimate the objective and constraints in (
18
)-(
19
) with samples collected by current policy
\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}
, then solving its optimization problem via first-order optimizer.

We denote the empirical KL-divergence w.r.t
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
and
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}
as follows,

\hat{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}})=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\text{KL}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(a_{t}|s_{t}),\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}^{{}^{\prime}}}(a_{t}|s_{t})).

Let
\{(s_{t},a_{t},r_{t+1},c_{t+1})\}_{t=1}^{T}\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}
, we update performance improvement (
18
) step as follows,

\displaystyle\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}\in\Pi_{\theta}}\left\{\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{R}}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})\right\}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{R}}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{N}\dfrac{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(a_{t}|s_{t})}{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(a_{t}|s_{t})}\hat{A}_{t}-\alpha_{k}\sqrt{\hat{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}})},

where
\hat{A}_{t}
is an estimator of
A^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)
.

Then we update projection step by replacing the distance function
D
by KL-divergence, and we solve the constraint problem (
19
) by the following primal-dual approach,

\displaystyle(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}},\nu_{k+1})=\arg\min_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}\in\Pi_{\bm{\theta}}}\max_{\nu\geq 0}\left\{\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{c}}\left(\pi_{\bm{\theta}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}},{\bm{\theta}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{\text{c}}\left(\pi_{\bm{\theta}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}},{\bm{\theta}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\hat{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}})+\nu\hat{C}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}),

where the empirical constraint function

\hat{C}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})=\hat{J}^{C}+\frac{1}{1-\tilde{\gamma}}\cdot\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(a_{t}|s_{t})}{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(a_{t}|s_{t})}\hat{A}^{C}_{t}+\beta_{k}\sqrt{\hat{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}})}-b,

\hat{J}^{C}
and
\hat{A}^{C}_{t}
are estimators for cost-return and cost-advantage correspondingly.

Due to the limitation of space, we have presented all the details for the implementation of CUP in Appendix
B
and Algorithm
1
.

5
Related Work

This section reviews some typical ways to solve safe reinforcement learning: local policy search, Lagrangian approach, and constrained policy optimization (CPO).
We mainly focus on CPO since those algorithms also use surrogate functions to replace the objective and constraints, which resembles the proposed CUP.
We provide more comparisons and discussion in Appendix
C
and Table
2
.

Local Policy Search and Lagrangian Approach
.
A direct way to solve CMDP (
4
) is to apply
local policy search

[
Peters and Schaal, 2008
,
Pirotta et al., 2013
]
over the policy space
\Pi_{\mathcal{C}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\pi_{{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k+1}}=\arg\max_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\Pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}}J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}),~\text{s.t}.~J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}})\leq b,~\text{and}~D(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{{\bm{\theta}}}_{k}})<\delta,

(21)

where
\delta
is a positive scalar,
D(\cdot,\cdot)
is some distance measure.
For practice, the local policy search (
21
) is challenging to implement because it requires evaluation of the constraint function
c
to determine whether a proposed point
\pi
is feasible
[
Zhang et al., 2020
]
.
Besides, when updating policy according to samples, local policy search (
21
) requires off-policy evaluation
[
Achiam et al., 2017
]
, which is very challenging for high-dimension control problem
[
Duan et al., 2016
,
Yang et al., 2018
,
Yang et al., 2021a
]
.
Thus, local policy search (
21
) looks simple, but it is impractical for high-dimension policy optimization.

The standard way to solve CMDP (
4
) is Lagrangian approach
[
Chow et al., 2017
]
that is also known as primal-dual policy optimization
[
Chen et al., 2021
]
:

\displaystyle(\pi_{\star},\lambda_{\star})=\arg\min_{\lambda\geq 0}\max_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}\in\Pi_{\bm{\theta}}}\left\{J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})-\lambda(J^{c}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})-b)\right\}.

(22)

Although extensive canonical algorithms are proposed to solve problem (
22
), e.g.,
[
Liang et al., 2018
,
Tessler et al., 2019
,
Paternain et al., 2019
,
Le et al., 2019
,
Russel et al., 2020
,
Xu et al., 2020
,
Satija et al., 2020
,
Chen et al., 2021
]
,
the policy updated by Lagrangian approach may be infeasible w.r.t. CMDP (
4
).
This is hazardous in reinforcement learning when one needs to execute the intermediate policy (which may be unsafe) during training
[
Chow et al., 2018
]
.

Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO)
.
Recently, CPO
[
Achiam et al., 2017
]
suggests to replace the cost constraint with a surrogate cost function which evaluates the constraint
J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}})
according to the samples collected from the current policy
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}
:

\displaystyle\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}}=\arg\max_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\Pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}}~~~~\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]

(23)

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~~J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{c}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]\leq b,

(24)

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})=\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot)}[\text{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})[s]]\leq\delta.

(25)

Existing recent works (e.g.,
[
Achiam et al., 2017
,
Vuong et al., 2019
,
Yang et al., 2020b
,
Han et al., 2020
,
Bisi et al., 2020
,
Bharadhwaj et al., 2021
]
) try to find some convex approximations to replace the term
A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)
and
\bar{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})
Eq.(
23
)-(
25
).
Concretely, according to (
6
),
[
Achiam et al., 2017
]
suggest to use first-order Taylor expansion to replace (
23
)-(
24
), use second-oder approximation to replace (
25
).
Such first-order and second-order approximations turn a non-convex problem (
23
)-(
25
) to be a convex problem,
it seems to make a simple solution, but this approach results in many error sources and troubles in practice.
Firstly, it still lacks a theory analysis to show the difference between the non-convex problem (
23
)-(
25
) and its convex approximation.
Policy optimization is a typical non-convex problem
[
Yang et al., 2021b
]
; its convex approximation may introduce some error for its original issue.
Secondly, CPO updates parameters according to conjugate gradient
[
Süli and Mayers, 2003
]
, and its solution involves the inverse Fisher information matrix,
which requires expensive computation for each update.
Later,
[
Yang et al., 2020b
]
propose projected-based constrained policy optimization (PCPO) that also uses second-order approximation, which also results in an expensive computation.

Instead of using a convex approximation for the objective function, the proposed CUP algorithm improves CPO and PCPO at least two aspects.
Firstly, the CUP directly optimizes the surrogate objective function via the first-order method, and it does not depend on any convex approximation.
Thus, the CUP effectively avoids the expensive computation for the inverse Fisher information matrix.
Secondly, CUP extends the surrogate objective function to GAE. Although
[
Zhang et al., 2020
]
has used the GAE technique in experiments, to the best of our knowledge, it still lacks a rigorous theoretical analysis involved GAE before we propose CUP.

Experiment

In this section, we verify the effectiveness and stability of CUP in terms of policy improvement while satisfying safety.
We aim to answer the following three issues:

(I)
. Does CUP satisfy the safety constraints in different environments? For the same environment with different cost limit, Does CUP also performs well?

(II)
. How does CUP compare to the state-of-the-art safe RL algorithms? Does CUP achieve higher rewards under the constraint of cost threshold?

(III)
. Does CUP play a sensibility during the hyper-parameters (e.g., step-size
\nu
, the coefficient
\alpha
with respect KL-divergence) tuning processing?

Environments
: We train different robotic agents using five MuJoCo physical simulators
[
Todorov et al., 2012
]
which are open by OpenAI Gym API
[
Brockman et al., 2016
]
.

Learning from Samples.

Then, according to gradient decent method, we have

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}\leftarrow\bm{\theta}-\eta\dfrac{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta},\nu)}{\partial\bm{\theta}},~~~\nu\leftarrow\left\{\nu+\eta\dfrac{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta},\nu)}{\partial\nu}\right\}_{+},

(34)

where
\{\cdot\}_{+}
denote the positive part, i.e., if
x\leq 0
,
\{x\}_{+}=0
, else
\{x\}_{+}=x
.
Particularly,

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathcal{L}(\bm{\theta},\nu)}{\partial\nu}=J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})+\dfrac{1}{1-\tilde{\gamma}}\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}},C}(s,a)\right]-b,

(35)

where the term
\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}},C}(s,a)\right]
can be estimated following the idea as (
26
):

\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}},C}(s,a)\right]=\E_{s\sim d_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(\cdot|s)}\left[\dfrac{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(a|s)}{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(a|s)}A^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}},C}(s,a)\right].

But recall (
18
) is a MM-iteration, i.e.,
we require to minimize
\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot)}\text{KL}\left(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}\right)[s]
, which implies
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
is close to
\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}
.
Thus it is reasonable
\E_{s\sim{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}^{\lambda}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{\text{GAE}(\gamma,\lambda)}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}},C}(s,a)\right]\approx 0
, thus, in practice, we update
\nu
following a simple way

\nu\leftarrow\left\{\nu+\eta(J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})-b)\right\}_{+}.

Finally, according to (
34
), for each data sampled from

\cup_{i=1}^{M}\cup_{t=0}^{T}\left\{(s_{i,t},a_{i,t},r_{i,t+1},c_{i,t+1})\right\}
according to current policy
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}
,
we learn the parameter
\bm{\theta}_{k+1}
as follows,

\displaystyle{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{{\bm{\theta}}}\dfrac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\{\text{KL}\left({\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}}(\cdot|s_{i,t}),\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|s_{i,t})\right)+\nu_{k}\dfrac{1-\gamma\lambda}{1-\gamma}\dfrac{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(a_{i,t}|s_{i,t})}{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(a_{i,t}|s_{i,t})}\hat{A}^{C}_{i,t}\right\},

which can be solved via the first-order optimizer.

C.1
CPO
[
Achiam et al., 2017
]

For a given policy
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}
, CPO updates new policy
\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}}
as follows:

\displaystyle\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}}=\arg\max_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\Pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}}~~~~\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]

(36)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~\text{s.t.}~~J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{c}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]\leq b,

(37)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~\bar{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})=\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot)}[\text{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})[s]]\leq\delta.

(38)

It is impractical to solve the problem (
23
) directly due to the computational cost.

[
Achiam et al., 2017
]
suggest to find some convex approximations to replace the term
A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)
and
\bar{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})
Eq.(
23
)-(
25
).

Concretely, according to (
6
),
[
Achiam et al., 2017
]
suggest to use first-order Taylor expansion of
J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})
to replace the objective (
23
) as follows,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\E_{s\sim d_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}^{\rho_{0}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(\cdot|s)}\left[\dfrac{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(a|s)}{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(a|s)}A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]=J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}})-J(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})\approx({\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}_{k})^{\top}\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}}).

Similarly,
[
Achiam et al., 2017
]
use the following approximations to turn the constrained policy optimization (
23
)-(
25
) to be a convex problem,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\E_{s\sim d_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}^{\rho_{0}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(\cdot|s)}

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(a|s)}{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}(a|s)}A^{c}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]\approx({\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}_{k})^{\top}\nabla_{{\bm{\theta}}}J^{c}(\pi_{\bm{\theta}}),

(39)

\displaystyle\bar{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})

\displaystyle\approx({\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}_{k})^{\top}\mathbf{H}({\bm{\theta}}-{\bm{\theta}}_{k}),

(40)

where
\mathbf{H}
is Hessian matrix of
\bar{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})
, i.e.,

\mathbf{H}[i,j]=:\dfrac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{\bm{\theta}}_{i}\partial{\bm{\theta}}_{j}}\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot)}\left[\text{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})[s]\right],

Eq.(
40

25

Let
\lambda_{\star},\nu_{\star}
is the dual solution of the following problem

\lambda_{\star},\nu_{\star}=\arg\max_{\lambda\geq 0,\nu\geq 0}\left\{\dfrac{-1}{2\lambda}\left(\mathbf{g}^{\top}\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{g}-2\nu r+sv^{2}\right)+\nu c-\dfrac{\lambda\delta}{2}\right\};

where

\mathbf{g}=\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]
,
\mathbf{a}=\nabla_{\bm{\theta}}\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{c}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]

, and

Finally, CPO updates parameters according to conjugate gradient as follows:
if approximation to CPO is feasible:

\displaystyle\bm{\theta}_{k+1}=\bm{\theta}_{k}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{\star}}\mathbf{H}^{-1}(\mathbf{g}-\nu_{\star}\mathbf{a}),

C.2
PCPO
[

]

Projection-Based Constrained Policy Optimization (PCPO) is an iterative method for optimizing policies in a two-step process: the first step performs a local reward improvement update, while the second step reconciles any constraint violation by projecting the policy back onto the constraint set.

Reward Improvement:

\displaystyle\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}=\arg\max_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\Pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}}\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right],

\displaystyle\text{ s.t.}\bar{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})=\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot)}[\text{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})[s]]\leq\delta;

Projection:

\displaystyle\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+1}}=\arg\min_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}\in\Pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}}~D\left(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}}\right),

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}~J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}}(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{c}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]\leq b.

Then,
[

]
follows CPO
[

]
uses convex approximation to original problem, and calculate the update rule as follows,

\bm{\theta}_{k+1}=\bm{\theta}_{k}-\sqrt{\dfrac{2\delta}{\mathbf{g}^{\top}\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{g}}}\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{g}-\max\left(0,\dfrac{\sqrt{\dfrac{2\delta}{\mathbf{g}^{\top}\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{g}}}\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{g}+c}{\mathbf{a}^{\top}\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathbf{a}}\right)\mathbf{L}^{-1}\mathbf{a},

where
\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{I}
if

\ell_{2}

\mathbf{L}=\mathbf{H}
if
D
is KL-divergence.

C.3
FOCOPS
[
Zhang et al., 2020
]

[
Zhang et al., 2020
]
propose the First Order Constrained Optimization in Policy Space (FOCOPS) that is a two-step approach. We present it as follows.

Step1: Finding the optimal update policy.

Firstly, for a given policy
\pi_{\bm{\theta}k}
, we find an optimal update policy
\pi^{\star}
by solving the optimization problem (

)-(

\displaystyle\pi^{\star}=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}~~~~\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}\left[A_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]

(41)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~\text{s.t.}~~J^{c}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})+\frac{1}{1-\gamma}\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi(\cdot|s)}\left[A^{c}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(s,a)\right]\leq b,

(42)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~\bar{D}_{\text{KL}}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})=\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot)}[\text{KL}(\pi,\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}})[s]]\leq\delta.

(43)

If
\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}

\displaystyle\pi^{\star}(a|s)=\dfrac{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}(a|s)}{Z_{\lambda,\nu}(s)}\exp\left(\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\left(A_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}(s,a)-\nu A^{c}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}_{k}}}(s,a)\right)\right),

(44)

where

\lambda
and
\nu

\min_{\lambda,\nu\geq 0}\lambda\nu+\nu\tilde{b}+\lambda\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi^{\star}(\cdot|s)}\left[Z_{\lambda,\nu}(s)\right],

the term

Step 2: Projection

Then, we project the policy found in the previous step back into the parameterized policy space
\Pi_{\bm{\theta}}
by solving for the closest policy
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}\in\Pi_{\bm{\theta}}
to
\pi^{\star}
in order to obtain

\bm{\theta}_{k+1}=\arg\min_{\bm{\theta}}\E_{s\sim d^{\rho_{0}}_{\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}_{k}}}(\cdot)}[\text{KL}(\pi_{{\bm{\theta}}},\pi^{\star})[s]].

C.4
Comparison to CUP

Comparing to CPO and PCPO, the implementation of CUP does not depend on any convex approximations.
CPO learns its objective with the deep neural network via the first-order method (see Appendix
B
).

Concretely, CPO and PCPO approximate the non-convex objective (or constraints) with first-order or second Taylor expansion,
but their implementations still lack a theory to show the error difference between the original objective (or constraints) and its convex approximations.
Additionally, their approaches involve the inverse of a high-dimension Fisher information matrix, which causes their algorithms to require a costly computation for each update when solving high-dimensional RL problems.
While the proposed CUP does not depend on any convex approximations, it learns the policy via first-order optimization approaches.
Thus, CUP does not involve the inverse of a high-dimension Fisher information matrix, which implies CUP requires less memory than CPO and PCPO.

Although FOCOPS is also a non-convex implementation, it heavily depends on the current best-satisfied policy.
It is known that the current best policy may not be the optimal policy, and FOCOPS requires to project this policy back into the parametric policy space, which implies FOCOPS reduce the chances for an agent to explore the environment since it may lose in a locally optimal solution.
While the proposed CUP does not depend on the current optimal policy, in fact, CUP requires the agent to learn the policy according to (
18
), the numerical solution is not the current optimal policy, which helps CUP to explore the environment.

Appendix D
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some new notations about state distribution, policy optimization and
\lambda
-returns.

D.1
State Distribution

We use
\mathbf{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\times|\mathcal{S}|}
to denote the state transition matrix by executing
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
, and their components are:

\mathbf{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[s,s^{\prime}]=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(a|s)\mathbb{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a)=:\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s),~~s,s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S},

which denotes one-step state transformation probability from
s
to
s^{{}^{\prime}}
.

We use
\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})
to denote the probability of visiting
s
after
t

time steps from the initial state
s_{0}
by executing
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
.
Particularly, we notice if
t=0
,
s_{t}\neq s_{0}
, then
\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})=0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})=0,~~t=0~\text{and}~s\neq s_{0}.

(45)

Then for any initial state
s_{0}\sim\rho(\cdot)
, the following holds,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0})=

\displaystyle\sum_{s^{{}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}}(s_{t}=s|s_{t-1}=s^{{}^{\prime}})\mathbb{P}_{{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}}(s_{t-1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s_{0}).

(46)

Recall
d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{s_{0}}(s)
denotes the normalized discounted distribution of the future state
s
encountered starting at
s_{0}
by executing
\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
,

d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{s_{0}}(s)=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{t}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t}=s|s_{0}).

Furthermore, since
s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)
, we define

d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)}[d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{s_{0}}(s)]=\int_{s_{0}\in\mathcal{S}}\rho_{0}(s_{0})d^{s_{0}}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s)\text{d}s_{0}

as the discounted state visitation distribution over the initial distribution
\rho_{0}(\cdot)
. We use
\mathbf{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
to store all the normalized discounted state distributions, and its components are:

\mathbf{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}[s]=d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}(s),~~s\in\mathcal{S}.

We use
\bm{\rho}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
to denote initial state distribution vector, and their components are:

\bm{\rho}_{0}[s]=\rho_{0}(s),~~s\in\mathcal{S}.

Then, we rewrite
\mathbf{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}
as the following matrix version,

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}=(1-\gamma)\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}})^{t}\bm{\rho}_{0}=(1-\gamma)(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}})^{-1}\bm{\rho}_{0}.

(47)

D.2
Objective of MDP

Recall
\tau=\{s_{t},a_{t},r_{t+1}\}_{t\geq 0}\sim{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}
, according to
\tau
,
we define the expected return
J({\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}|s_{0})
as follows,

\displaystyle J({\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}|s_{0})=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[R(\tau)]=\dfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{s_{0}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s),s^{{}^{\prime}}\sim\mathbb{P}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[r(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)\right],

(48)

where
R(\tau)=\sum_{t\geq 0}\gamma^{t}r_{t+1}
, and the notation
J({\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}|s_{0})
is “conditional” on
s_{0}
is to emphasize the trajectory
\tau
starting from
s_{0}
.
Since
s_{0}\sim\rho_{0}(\cdot)
, we define the objective of MDP as follows,

\displaystyle J(\pi_{\bm{\theta}})=\dfrac{1}{1-\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{s\sim d_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{\rho_{0}}(\cdot),a\sim\pi_{\bm{\theta}}(\cdot|s),s^{{}^{\prime}}\sim\mathbb{P}(\cdot|s,a)}\left[r(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s,a)\right].

(49)

The goal of reinforcement learning is to solve the following optimization problem:

D.3

Let
\mathcal{B}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}
be the

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}:\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|},~~~~v\mapsto\mathbf{r}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}+\gamma\mathbf{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}v,

(51)

Let
\mathbf{v}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
be a vector that stores all the state value functions, and its components are:

\mathbf{v}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[s]=V_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s),~~s\in\mathcal{S}.

Then, according to Bellman operator (
51
), we rewrite Bellman equation
[
Bellman, 1957
]
as the following matrix version:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}\mathbf{v}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}=\mathbf{v}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}.

(52)

Furthermore, we define
\lambda

Bellman operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\lambda}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}:\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|},~~~~v\mapsto\mathbf{r}^{(\lambda)}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}+\tilde{\gamma}\mathbf{P}^{(\lambda)}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}v,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{(\lambda)}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}=(1-\gamma\lambda)\sum_{{t}=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\lambda)^{{t}}\mathbf{P}^{{t}+1}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}},~~\mathbf{r}^{(\lambda)}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}=\sum_{{t}=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\lambda\mathbf{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}})^{{t}}\mathbf{r}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}},~~\tilde{\gamma}=\dfrac{\gamma(1-\lambda)}{1-\gamma\lambda}.

(54)

Let

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{(\lambda)}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)=\mathbf{P}^{(\lambda)}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[s,s^{{}^{\prime}}]=:(1-\gamma\lambda)\sum_{{t}=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\lambda)^{{t}}\left(\mathbf{P}^{{t}+1}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[s,s^{{}^{\prime}}]\right),

-th component of matrix

s^{{}^{\prime}}
after

\pi_{\bm{\theta}}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}^{{t}+1}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}[s,s^{{}^{\prime}}]=\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t+1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s).

(56)

Thus, we rewrite
\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{(\lambda)}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}^{(\lambda)}(s^{{}^{\prime}}|s)=(1-\gamma\lambda)\sum_{{t}=0}^{\infty}(\gamma\lambda)^{{t}}\mathbb{P}_{\pi_{\bm{\theta}}}(s_{t+1}=s^{{}^{\prime}}|s),~~s\in\mathcal{S}.

Towards a mod- p p Lubin-Tate theory for GL 2 {\mathrm{GL}}_{2} over totally real fields
Debargha Banerjee, Vivek Rai

Abstract
. We show that the conjectural mod p p local Langlands correspondence can be realised in the mod p p cohomology of the Lubin-Tate towers. The proof utilizes a well known conjecture of Buzzard-Diamond-Jarvis [ BDJ10 , Conj. 4.9] , a study of completed cohomology of the ordinary and supersingular locus of the Shimura curves for a totally real field F F and of mod l ( ≠ p ) l(\neq p) local Langlands correspondence as given by Emerton-Helm [ EH14 ] . In the case of modular curves a similar theorem was obtained by Chojecki [ Cho15 ] .

1.
Introduction

The aim of this article is to study the relationship of the mod
p
local Langlands correspondence with the mod
p
completed cohomology of Shimura curves over a totally real field
F
and with the mod
p
cohomology of Lubin-Tate tower. Assuming a well known conjecture of Buzzard-Diamond-Jarvis
[
BDJ10
, Conj. 4.9]
, we prove in Theorem
1.1
that the conjectural mod
p
local Langlands correspondence lies in the mod
p
completed cohomology of the supersingular part of the Shimura curve.
By further assuming a Serre type Conjecture
2
, in Theorem
1.2
, we show that the conjectural mod
p
local Langlands correspondence lies in the mod
p
cohomology of the Lubin-Tate towers.

In recent years quite a progress have been made to establish the local Langlands correspondence (both
p
-adic and mod
p
) but unfortunately the complete picture is not clear even for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}
.
The mod
p
and
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence has been established for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
by Breuil, Berger, Colmez, Dospinescu, Paskunas and others. But for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)
, when
E(\neq\mathbb{Q}_{p})
is a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
the correspondence is not known. Roughly speaking the aim of the mod
p
local Langlands is to give a correspondence between the
n
-dimensional continuous mod
p
representation of
G_{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\mathrm{Gal}}(\bar{E}/E)
and smooth admissible mod
p
representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(E)
. The picture on the Galois side is much simpler and it is the automorphic side which should be understood better in order to give such a correspondence. For
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
the classification of irreducible smooth admissible representation was started by Barthel-Livné
[
BL94
]
and completed by Breuil
[
Bre03
]
wherein he classified all the supersingular representation for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
but for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)
the classification is still incomplete mainly because of the mysterious supersingular representations.

The following are some basic differences between the smooth representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)
:

•

Unlike
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
the complete classification of supersingular representations for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)
is not known. The study of such representations were carried out by Breuil-Paskunas
[
BP12
]
wherein they constructed infinite family of smooth admissible supersingular representations using the theory of diagrams.

•

Unlike
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
there exist smooth representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)
which are not admissible
[
Le19
]
,
[
GS20
]
.

•

Under the assumption of Buzzard-Diamond-Jarvis conjecture it was shown by Hu
[
Hu17
]
that for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)
there are non-trivial extensions between supersingular and principal series representations which is not the case for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
(we will come back to this point later in the introduction).

Because of these reasons among others its quite difficult to establish mod
p
local Langlands correspondence for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)
. However there has some been recent progress on the Gelfand-Kirillov dimension of the possible candidate for such a correspondence, see
[
HW20
]
,
[
BHH+20
]
.

Now coming to the global picture, Galois representations of
G_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\mathrm{Gal}}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
coming from geometry (for instance modular Galois representation) are often realised in the étale cohomology of modular curves. In
[
Eme11
, Theorem 1.2.6]
, Emerton showed that the mod
p
completed cohomology (constructed by taking direct limit over étale cohomology of modular curves for finite levels) of modular curve realises the mod
l
local Langlands correspondence for all primes
l
, in particular it realises mod
p
local Langlands correspondence (see
[
CEG+18
, Section 7]
for a different proof of this local-global compatibility result). This result is a special case of Buzzard-Diamond-Jarvis conjecture
[
BDJ10
, Conj. 4.9]
which states that the completed cohomology of Shimura curve (over a totally real field
F
) realises the mod
l
local Langlands correspondences for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F_{l})
where
F_{l}
denotes the completion of
F
at the prime
l
(for the precise statement see Conjecture
1
). The local factors appearing in Conjecture
1
is studied in
[
EH14
]
,
[
Hel13
]
for primes
l\nmid p
and in
[
Hu17
]
,
[
BHH+20
]
,
[
HW20
]
for primes lying above
p
.
Therefore to salvage information about mod
p
local Langlands correspondence its natural to study the completed cohomology of Shimura curves. In fact in this article we will be also be concerned with the completed cohomology of ordinary and supersingular locus of Shimura curves.

On the other hand the classical local Langlands correspondence for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}
by Harris-Taylor
[
HT01
]
was realised in the
l
-adic cohomology of Lubin-Tate towers. Also Carayol in
[
Car86b
]
,
[
Car90
]
using cohomology of vanishing cycles define ‘local fundamental representation’ and showed that it decomposes into a product of Jacquet-Langlands correspondence and local Langlands correspondence.
Hence one hopes that mod
p
local Langlands correspondence can also be realised inside the cohomology of Lubin-Tate towers (for details on Lubin-Tate tower see
[
Dat12
]
). In fact for the case
l=p
, Chojecki
[
Cho15
, Section 6]
defines an analogue of the ‘local fundamental representation’ and showed that the mod
p
local Langlands correspondence for
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
injects into the cohomology of Lubin-Tate tower. Following Chojecki we will define ‘local fundamental representation’ and show that it realises the conjectural mod
p
local Langlands correspondence for Shimura curves. For a study of the
p
-adic cohomology of the Lubin-Tate towers see
[
Sch18
]
.

To state our results we first define few notation. Fix an odd prime
p
, let
F
be a totally real number field of finite degree
d>1
. Let
D
be a quaternion algebra over
F
such that
D
splits at one real place of
F
and non-split at other real places, also assume that there is only one prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
F
lying above
p
and
D
splits at
\mathfrak{p}
. One can associate to
D
a system of quaternion Shimura curves

indexed by compact open subgroups

of
D_{f}=(D\otimes_{F}\mathbb{A}_{f})^{\times}
where
\mathbb{A}_{f}
is finite adele of

. Let
\rho
be an absolutely irreducible continuous two-dimensional representation of
G_{F}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\mathrm{Gal}}(\bar{F}/F)
over
\mathbb{\bar{F}}_{p}
and let

denotes the completed cohomology of Shimura curves

. Let

be the completion of

\mathfrak{p}
, also assume that
\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\rho|_{{\mathrm{Gal}}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/F_{\mathfrak{p}})}
is absolutely irreducible. Then the conjectural mod

to a supersingular representation of

say

.
We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}G_{F}\rightarrow{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
be continuous, irreducible and totally odd representation such that
\rho_{\mathfrak{p}}
is irreducible. Assuming Conjecture
1
we have a continuous
G_{F}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F_{\mathfrak{p}})\times\Pi_{w\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}}(D\otimes_{F}F_{w})^{\times}
- equivariant injection

\rho\otimes\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes(\otimes_{w\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}}\pi_{w})\hookrightarrow H^{1}_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p},ss}[\mathfrak{m}_{\rho}],

where
H^{1}_{\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p},\text{ss}}
denotes the mod
p
cohomology of the supresingular locus of

(see (
4.1
)).

Note that for
F=\mathbb{Q}
, the above theorem is proved by Chojecki
[
Cho15
, Theorem 6.3]
. Before we give the strategy of our proof let us mention one crucial difference between the proof of Theorem
1.1
and of
[
Cho15
, Theorem 6.3]
. Chojecki uses the localisation functor at a supersingular representation
\pi
of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
and it is a result of Paskunas
[
Paš13
]
that this functor decomposes the category of smooth admissible representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
into a direct sum of categories of smooth representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
such that the first direct summand has all those representations whose irreducible subquotients are isomorphic to
\pi
and the second summand consists of smooth representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
such that none of its irreducible subquotients are isomorphic to
\pi
. Such a decomposition does not occur for the category of smooth admissible mod

representations of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(E)

[
Hu17
]
that in tame case under the assumption of
[
BDJ10
, Conjecture 4.9]
, there exist non-trivial extensions between the principal series representations and supersingular representations.

We state Conjecture
2
in Section
7
which is a generalisation of
[
Ser96
, THÉORÈME, p.282]
, comparing the system of eigenvalues for the Hecke operators in the Hecke algebra for two related quaternion algebras for
D
and
\bar{D}
. Assuming Conjecture
1
and Conjecture

, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\rho
be as in Theorem
1.1
, then we have a continuous
G_{F}\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F_{\mathfrak{p}})\times\Pi_{w\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}}(D\otimes_{F}F_{w})^{\times}
-equivariant injection

\rho\otimes\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes(\otimes_{w\in\Sigma\setminus\Sigma^{\prime}}\pi_{w})\hookrightarrow\hat{H}^{1}_{\text{LT},\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}},

where
\hat{H}^{1}_{\text{LT},\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
is the first Lubin-Tate cohomology (see
7.1
).

Strategy of the proofs

The strategy for the proofs of above two theorems are summarised below:

(1)

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
we first study the ordinary and supersingular part of Shimura curves and show that the cohomology of the ordinary part can be induced from Borel subgroup of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{2}(F_{\mathfrak{p}})
. This would be crucial because it will help us to show that completed cohomology of ordinary part of Shimura curve does not contain any supersingular representations since by definition the supersingular representations are not subquotient of the representations induced from Borel. We follow this by a thorough study of the mod
l
local Langlands correspondence as given by Emerton-Helm
[
EH14
]
and Helm
[
Hel13
]
. We prove a crucial Theorem
5.1
which shows the existence of ‘new vectors’ for such a correspondence. Using this we construct a tame level such that invariance under this tame level along with Conjecture
1
gives us an injection of the conjectural mod
p
local Langlands correspondence in the completed cohomology of the supersingular part of the Shimura curve.

(2)

For the proof of Theorem
1.2
we define the cohomology of the Lubin-Tate towers following Chojecki
[
Cho15
, Section 6]
and then we show that the cohomology of the supersingular part of Shimura curve injects into the cohomology of the Lubin-Tate towers. Using the above step we get an injection of the of conjectural mod
p
local Langlands correspondence in the first cohomology of Lubin-Tate towers thereby completing the proof.

3.1.
Étale sheaves and exact sequences

Let
X
be a scheme and
j\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}U\hookrightarrow X
be an open immersion. Let
Z=X\setminus U
and
i\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}Z\to X
be the inclusion map. Let
\mathcal{F}
be an étale sheaf on
X
, we get a following exact sequence of sheaves on
X

0\to j_{!}j^{*}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\to i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{F}\to 0,

which gives the following long exact sequence of cohomology groups

\cdots\to H^{0}(X,i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{F})\to H^{1}(X,j_{!}j^{*}\mathcal{F})\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{F})\to H^{1}(X,i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{F})\to\cdots

The cohomology with compact support is defined by
H^{r}_{c}(U,\mathcal{G})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=H^{r}(X,j_{!}\mathcal{G})
, for
r\geq 0
and any étale sheaf
\mathcal{G}
on
U
. Also, by definition of
i^{*}
we have
H^{i}(X,i_{*}i^{*}\mathcal{F})=H^{i}(Z,i^{*}\mathcal{F})
for
i=0
,
1
. Therefore we get

\cdots\to H^{0}(Z,i^{*}\mathcal{F})\to H^{1}_{c}(U,j^{*}\mathcal{F})\to H^{1}(X,\mathcal{F})\to H^{1}(Z,i^{*}\mathcal{F})\to\cdots

We will also consider the cohomology with support on
Z
. For this let
Z
be a closed subvariety (or subscheme) of
X
. For any étale sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
we have the following long exact sequence of cohomology groups

\cdots\to H^{r}_{Z}(X,\mathcal{F})\to H^{r}(X,\mathcal{F})\to H^{r}(U,\mathcal{F})\to H^{r+1}(X,\mathcal{F})\to\cdots

By the general formalism of six operations for Berkovich spaces (see
[
Ber93
]
) and by the comparison results of étale cohomology of schemes and its analytification (see
[
Ber95
]

7.

In this section we connect the cohomology of the Lubin-Tate tower with the completed cohomology of the Shimura curves.

Coördinate transformations, metrics and black hole features in the collapsed phase of EDT
Jan Smit Institute for Theoretical Physics, University of Amsterdam, Science Park 904, P.O.Box 94485, 1090 GL Amsterdam, the Netherlands

Abstract
This is a companion article to Using massless fields for observing black hole features in the collapsed phase of Euclidean dynamical triangulations Smit 2023 . It clarifies a singular coördinate transformation of an S ​ O ​ ( 4 ) SO(4) invariant metric to the usual spherical coördinates in which, at an instant of time called zero, the metric takes the form of a black hole with an interior. Regular transformations are also studied and found to lead in the zero time limit to the same spatial components of the metric as with the singular one, whereas the time component ends up differently. Components of the Einstein tensor also end up the same. A regular black hole metric is inversely transformed and compared with simulation results in Smit 2023 .



I
Introduction

In Euclidean quantum field theory, configurations contributing to a lattice-regulated path integral are typically wildly varying on the lattice scale, whereas average propagators vary typically slowly. Likewise, in the Euclidean dynamical triangulation (EDT) approach to quantum gravity the simplicial configurations are wildly varying, but average scalar field propagators still vary slowly as a function of the geodesic lattice distance
Agishtein and Migdal 1992
;
de Bakker and Smit 1997
.
In
Smit 2023
we proposed using ‘measured’ (numerically computed quantum-averaged) massless propagators for defining an average metric. An intuitive idea supporting this is the fact that our experimental understanding of distance is essentially based on QED with its massless photon field.

In the collapsed phase of EDT, measurement of massless scalar field propagators led to the determination — apart from an integration constant — of the scale factor
a(\eta)
in an
SO(4)
rotation invariant metric with line element

ds^{2}=d\eta^{2}+a(\eta)^{2}\,d\Omega_{3}^{2}\,,\quad 0<\eta<\eta_{\rm max}\,.

(1)

Here
\eta
is a
radial coördinate in four dimensions and
d\Omega_{3}
the line element on the
3
-sphere
S^{3}
with unit radius;
a(\eta)
is defined to be positive. The scale factor was obtained in terms of a rational fit function

f_{\rm rat}(\eta)
:

a(\eta)=c_{G}\,f_{\rm rat}(\eta)=c_{G}\,\frac{p_{0}+p_{1}\,\eta^{2}}{1+q_{1}\,\eta^{2}}\,,

(2)

where
c_{G}>0
is the (dimensionless) integration constant.

Figure
1
shows
f_{\rm rat}(\eta)
fitted to an example of measured data. The fit ignored data in
0<\eta<5
suspected to be too much influenced by lattice artefacts, and also data in
\eta>24
suspected of being susceptible to uncontrolled statistics, or finite size effects or ‘polymer hair’.

The scale factor is taken to represent a smooth continuum geometry with a three-ball boundary at the origin
\eta=0
, of finite radius
a(0)
.
The constant
c_{G}
gets determined when fitting to a continuum formula.

The slope
a^{\prime}(\eta)=da(\eta)/d\eta
vanishes at the origin,
a^{\prime}(0)=0
, which led to black-hole features shown in
Smit 2023
.
It may well be that more refined simulations on larger lattices suggest vanishing scale factors with non-vanishing slope at the origin, but which still have a (possibly nearly) vanishing slope somewhere away from the origin, which would then lead again to features of (possibly remnants of) black holes. This will be illustrated near the end of this article by the Hayward model
Hayward 2006
.

The Einstein equations were assumed to hold in the effective sense and in the presence of a condensate of geometrical degrees of freedom.
The energy-momentum tensor of the condensate is determined by the Einstein tensor,

In the present article we present calculations that led the construction of the coördinate transformation and the computation of the Einstein tensor in
Smit 2023

II

The Einstein tensor is also calculated using results in

In section

An inverse transformation is applied to the Hayward model in section

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.+\partial_{y}\sqrt{F(\sqrt{r^{2}+y^{2}})}\,P(r,y)\right..

22
+

\displaystyle y(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}+r^{2}}\,\sinh\frac{t}{r_{0}}\,,\qquad\mbox{(EAdS)}

\displaystyle y(r,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r_{0}^{2}-r^{2}}\,\sin\frac{t}{r_{0}}\,,\quad\qquad\mbox{(EdS)}

II.1
Regular transformation to diagonal metric

In this section the condition of diagonality,
g_{rt}=0
is kept and the differential equation (
20
) will be integrated at fixed times.
Equation (
22
) will be used only at one reference
r
to obtain boundary conditions depending on time for the integration of (
20
) along
r
. Then there is no imperfectness of the coördinate transformation.

We concentrate on the fit function (
2
). It is convenient to rewrite its
a(\eta)
in the form

a(\eta)=h\,\frac{1+p\,\eta^{2}}{1+q\,\eta^{2}}\,,\quad h=c_{G}\,p_{0}\,,\;p=\frac{p_{1}}{p_{0}}\,,\;q=q_{1}\,.

(28)

The function
F(a)
introduced in (
14

\eta(a)
of
a(\eta)
; one finds

\displaystyle\eta^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{a-h}{ph-qa},\,\quad a^{\prime}(\eta)^{2}=\frac{4h^{2}(p-q)^{2}\eta^{2}}{(1+q\,\eta^{2})^{4}}\,

(29)

and

F(a)=\frac{4q^{3}(a-h)(hp/q-a)^{3}}{h^{2}(p-q)^{2}}\,.

(30)

The function

is positive between its zeros at

and

with a maximum at

somewhere in between, and only its monotonous branch in

is to be used. In the numerical simulation
Smit 2023

was only slightly larger than
2h
. In this section results will be shown for the generic case
c_{G}=1.5
(
h=3.3
) (cf. figure
2

Since an analytical treatment is already awkward in this case and might be prohibitive with more general fit functions, the following is a numerical exploration.

We choose a reference distance
r_{\rm p}

\dot{y}_{\rm p}(t;r_{\rm p})=\sqrt{F\left(\sqrt{r_{\rm p}^{2}+y_{\rm p}(t;r_{\rm p})^{2}}\right)}\,,

(31)

with initial conditions
y=y_{0}
at

chosen as follows.
Guided by (
25

(
y<0
) when

(

|y|

\displaystyle y_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,,\qquad\quad\quad h\leq r_{\rm p}\leq r_{\rm max}\,,\quad t_{0}=0\,,

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{h^{2}-r_{\rm p}^{2}}\,,\quad 0\leq r_{\rm p}\leq h\,,\quad t_{0}\to 0^{\pm}\,.

The resulting

is a monotonically rising function of

Next, equation (
20

is solved (numerically) with a boundary condition attaching

to the pole:

y^{\prime}(r,t;r_{\rm p})=P(r,y(r,t;r_{\rm p}))\,,\quad y(r_{\rm p},t;r_{\rm p})=y_{\rm p}(t;r_{\rm p})\,.

(33)

The solutions

map the

Consider erecting a second time pole

\bar{y}_{\rm p}(\bar{t};\bar{r}_{\rm p})

at a different position
\bar{r}_{\rm p}\neq r_{\rm p}
. This pole will be crossed by a foliation line

of the first pole,

, which determines
\bar{t}
in terms of

. This can be interpreted as a coördinate transformation of the time variable only:

\bar{t}=\bar{t}(t)

The second pole leads to a second foliation

. However, substituting
\bar{t}=\bar{t}(t)
will give just the original foliation.

In other words: results of different choices of the pole position are related by coördinate time transformations that depend only on time. We shall see evidence of this in tensor components that transform as a scalar field under such transformations, in particular
the spatial components of the metric (which is diagonal in this section)
and the diagonal up-down components of the Einstein tensor,
G^{\mu}_{\;\,\mu}(r,t)

(no summation).

II.2
Singular transformation implementing
g_{tt}=g^{rr}
at
t=0

The numerical integration of
y^{\prime}=P
in section
II.1
yielded diagonal metrics with spatial inverse components
1/g_{rr}=g^{rr}
that approached a robust envelope when
t\to 0
.
On the contrary, the magnitude of the component
g_{tt}
was highly sensitive to differing choices of boundary conditions (the two pole choices)
and the shape of
g_{tt}(r,t)
differed from
g^{rr}(r,t)
.

In this section we seek to obtain
g_{tt}=g^{rr}
in the limit
t\to 0
. We give up diagonality of the metric for general times but seek to recover it at zero time..
Consider erecting a pole as in the previous section but here at every
r\in(0,r_{\rm max})
. We wish to solve
\dot{y}=\sqrt{F}
analytically at small times and try

.
It is helpful to take a brief look at the solution of this equation in case of the cosh-model,

(38)

which is simpler to solve than the rat-model.
(This model fitted the simulation data less accurately and failed at smaller Newton couplings
Smit 2023
.
It approaches the exponential form of hyperbolic space at large
\eta
, but here
r_{0}
is meant to parametrize primarily the small to intermediate distance region. In particular,
1/r_{0}^{2}
may be thought to represent
2(p-q)
of the rat-model to
\mathcal{O}(\eta^{2})
.)
With

the function

in (

) turns out as

(39)

The solution of (
22
+
) with initial condition (

)
|_{r_{\rm p}\to r}
is

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm\sqrt{h^{2}-r^{2}}\,\cosh(t/r_{0})\,,\;0<r<h\,,\;t\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle>}}{{<}}0\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{r^{2}-h^{2}}\,\sinh(t/r_{0})\,,\quad\;\;\;r>h\,.

(trivially in the exterior as

), and the resulting metric becomes indeed diagonal,

\displaystyle 1-r^{2}/h^{2}\,,\quad r<h\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(r^{2}-h^{2})/r_{0}^{2}=F(r)\,,\quad r>h\,.

III
Transforming the Hayward model

The time-independence of the static version of Hayward’s model
Hayward 2006
allows a simple transformation to imaginary time, resulting in the Euclidean model:

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(r)\,dt^{2}+\frac{1}{f(r)}\,dr^{2}+r^{2}\,d\Omega_{2}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{2mr^{2}}{2ml^{2}+r^{3}}\,.

(58)

The two parameters
m
and
l
are positive and have the dimension of length. For small
r
the metric is like EdS,
f(r)\simeq 1-r^{2}/l^{2}
, and for large
r
it is ‘Newton-like’
f(r)\simeq 1-2m/r
. Figure
13
shows
f(r)
for several values of
m
at fixed
l
. With sufficiently large
m
there are two real zeros at
r_{+}>r_{-}>0
, which coincide,
r_{+}=r_{-}=r_{*}
, when
m
is reduced to a critical value
m_{*}
. Then also
f^{\prime}(r_{*})=0
, which together with
f(r_{*})=0
determines

m_{*}=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4}\,l\,,\quad r_{*}=\sqrt{3}\,l\,.

(59)

: top to bottom

,
0.96\,m_{*}
,
1.5\,m_{*}
and

IV
Conclusions

We studied the effect of two types of coördinate transformations,
a regular one producing a diagonal metric with
g_{tt}(r,t)\neq g^{rr}(r,t)
at all times and a singular one
Smit 2023
producing a metric which becomes diagonal only in the limit
t\to 0
with
g_{tt}(r,0)=g^{rr}(r,0)
. At time zero the component
g^{rr}(r,0)
is the same for both transformations As shown in figure
2
it is zero at
r=h
, as for a black hole with gravitational radius
h
. The singular transformation has a singularity at this point, which is akin to the transformation between the Schwarzschild-Droste black hole and its Kruskal-Szekeres extension

Schwarzschild 1916
;
Rothman 2002
;
McGruder and VanDerMeer 2018
;
Kruskal 1960
;
Szekeres 1960
.

The numerical study of the regular transformation showed that the left cuspy curve in figure
2
is the limiting envelope of smooth curves representing
g^{rr}(r,t)
(cf. figure
6
). The time component
g_{tt}(r,t)
differed from
g^{rr}(r,t)
, in shape and, depending on boundary conditions specifying the implementation of the transformation, also by many orders of magnitude.
These changes in boundary conditions correspond to transformations
t\to\bar{t}(t)

under which
y(r,t)
and
g^{rr}(r,t)
behave as scalar fields, but not
g_{tt}(r,t)
which may suffer large multiplicative factors. Diagonal mixed components of the Einstein tensor,
G^{\mu}_{\;\,\nu}(r,t)
,
\mu=\nu
, do also transform as a scalar field under
t\to\bar{t}(t)
and they approach the same zero time limit as with the singular transformation. Remarkably, the off-diagonal
G^{t}_{\;\,r}(r,t)
also does not suffer from the time ambiguity; it was found to vanish when
t\to 0
and arguments where given that this holds also in the distributional sense.

In
Smit 2023
a shell-like singular distribution was conjectured in the transverse pressure
\propto G^{\theta}_{\;\,\theta}(r,0)
at
r=h
as a consequence of the discontinuous first derivative
2\kappa_{\pm}=\lim_{r\to h^{\pm}}\partial_{r}g_{tt}(r,0)
.
However, with the regular transformation such a shell is absent due to a remarkable cancellation between singular contributions to
G^{\theta}_{\;\,\theta}
(cf. appendix
B
). One also notes that the local minimum in
g^{rr}(r,t)
approaches
r=h
when
t\to 0
(figure
6
), which
implies that the derivative at the minimum remains zero in the limit.
A gravastar-like shell
Mazur and Mottola 2015
is not supported in this study.

The inversely transformed Hayward model and its qualitative match to the EDT data sheds new light on the interpretation of the EDT results.

Appendix A
Einstein tensor for a time-dependent diagonal metric

In equation (14.48) of
Misner et al. 1973
, a time-dependent rotation-invariant diagonal Lorentzian metric depending on coördinates
T
,
R
,
\theta
and
\phi
is defined by its line element

(66)

and its Einstein tensor is given in (14.48), (14.51) and (14.52) of this book. Transforming coördinates
T=-i\,t
and choosing
R=r
we obtain the Einstein tensor for the Euclidean metric.
The transformed functions of
Misner et al. 1973
are given by

\Phi=\frac{1}{2}\,\ln g_{tt}\,,\quad\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}\,\ln g_{rr}\,,

(67)

and

\displaystyle E_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-2\Phi}(\ddot{\Lambda}+\dot{\Lambda}^{2}-\dot{\Lambda}\dot{\Phi})\,,

(68)

\displaystyle E_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{-2\Lambda}(\Phi^{\prime\prime}+\Phi^{\prime 2}-\Phi^{\prime}\Lambda^{\prime})\,,

(69)

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E_{1}+E_{2}\,,\quad\bar{E}=-\frac{1}{r}\,e^{-2\Lambda}\;\Phi^{\prime}\,,

(70)

\displaystyle F_{\mbox{\tiny{MTW}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r^{2}}\,(1-e^{-2\Lambda})\,,\quad\bar{F}_{\mbox{\tiny{MTW}}}=\frac{1}{r}\,e^{-2\Lambda}\;\Lambda^{\prime}\,,

(71)

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\frac{1}{r}\,e^{-\Phi-\Lambda}\;\dot{\Lambda}\,,

(72)

in terms of which the Euclidean components of the Einstein tensor are given by

\displaystyle G^{t}_{\;\,t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-F_{\mbox{\tiny{MTW}}}-2\bar{F}_{\mbox{\tiny{MTW}}}\,,\quad G^{r}_{\;\,r}=-2\bar{E}-F_{\mbox{\tiny{MTW}}}\,,

\displaystyle G^{\theta}_{\;\,\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G^{\phi}_{\;\,\phi}=-E-\bar{E}-\bar{F}_{\mbox{\tiny{MTW}}}\,,\quad G^{t}_{\;\,r}=2\,iH\,.

(73)

Note that
E_{1}
contains two time derivatives of
g_{rr}
,
E_{2}
two spatial derivatives of
g_{tt}
, and that their sum

enters in

.
It is convenient to treat

introduced in (

, writing

(74)

The functions
E
, …,
H
depend on derivatives of the metric components

(75)

(cf. (
15
), (
17
)). Indicating their explicit dependence on variables these are given by

\displaystyle j(r,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}\tilde{F}(r^{2}+y^{2})+y^{2}}{r^{2}+y^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle f(r,y,\dot{y})

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{y}^{2}}{\tilde{F}(r^{2}+y^{2})}\,j(r,y)\,.

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\,j

\displaystyle\frac{\partial j}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial j}{\partial y}\,P\,,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial r}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\,P+\frac{\partial f}{\partial\dot{y}}\,\partial_{r}\dot{y}\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\,\dot{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\partial_{t}y=\partial_{t}\partial_{r}y=\frac{d}{dt}\,P=\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\,\dot{y}\,,

(81)

and recursively for higher derivatives. In principle this gives

, …,

,

,
\dot{y}

, …,

, …,

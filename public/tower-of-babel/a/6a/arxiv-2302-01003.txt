Termination of linear loops under commutative updates
Ruiwen Dong

Abstract
. We consider the following problem: given d × d d\times d rational matrices A 1 , … , A k A_{1},\ldots,A_{k} and a polyhedral cone 𝒞 ⊂ ℝ d \mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} , decide whether there exists a non-zero vector whose orbit under multiplication by A 1 , … , A k A_{1},\ldots,A_{k} is contained in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . This problem can be interpreted as verifying the termination of multi-path while loops with linear updates and linear guard conditions. We show that this problem is decidable for commuting invertible matrices A 1 , … , A k A_{1},\ldots,A_{k} . The key to our decision procedure is to reinterpret this problem in a purely algebraic manner. Namely, we discover its connection with modules over the polynomial ring ℝ ⁡ [ X 1 , … , X k ] \mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}] as well as the polynomial semiring ℝ ≥ 0 ​ [ X 1 , … , X k ] \mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}] . The loop termination problem is then reduced to deciding whether a submodule of ( ℝ ⁡ [ X 1 , … , X k ] ) n \left(\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}]\right)^{n} contains a “positive” element.

1.
Introduction

We consider the termination problem for multipath (or branching) linear loops over the real numbers.
We are concerned with problems of the following form: given
d\times d
matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
and a subset
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, decide whether there exists a vector
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{C}
whose orbit under multiplication by
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
is contained in
\mathcal{C}
.
In other words, denote by
\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\rangle
the monoid generated by
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
, we want to decide whether there exists
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
A\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{C}
for all
A\in\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\rangle
.
Such a problem can be interpreted as the non-termination of the following linear loop:

(1)

\textit{while }\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{C}\textit{ do }(\boldsymbol{x}\coloneqq A_{1}\boldsymbol{x}\textit{ or }\cdots\textit{ or }\boldsymbol{x}\coloneqq A_{k}\boldsymbol{x}).

The problem is whether there exists an initial value
\boldsymbol{v}
such that the loop never terminates.
Analysis of loops of this form is an important part of analysing more complex programs
(
jeannet2014abstract
;
kincaid2019closed
)
.
Various tools such as computing polynomial invariants
(
hrushovski2018polynomial
)
, porous invariants
(
lefaucheux2021porous
)
, ranking functions
(
ben2014ranking
;
ben2019multiphase
)
have been developed to analyse properties of such multipath loops.

Termination of linear loops with a
single
update (
k=1
) has been intensely studied for the last twenty years.
Tiwari
(
tiwari2004termination
)
first showed its decidability in the case where the guard condition
\mathcal{C}
is a convex polyhedron.
Subsequently, Braverman
(
braverman2006termination
)
extended the decidability result to the case where
\mathcal{C}
is the set of rational points in a convex polyhedron; and Hosseini, Ouaknine, Worrell
(
HosseiniO019
)
further extended the result to integer points.
1
1

1

The original results all concern single
affine
updates. However, they are equivalent to the case of
linear
updates by increasing the dimension by one and homogenizing the coordinates.

In the above results, the decidability relies on the freedom of choosing the initial value
\boldsymbol{v}
to circumvent the difficulties inherent in deciding termination on a single initial value.
In fact, when
\mathcal{C}
is a closed halfspace, termination of single-path linear loops for a
fixed
initial value, that is,

(2)

\boldsymbol{x}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{v};\textit{ while }\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathcal{C}\textit{ do }(\boldsymbol{x}\coloneqq A\boldsymbol{x}),

subsumes the
Positivity Problem

(
ouaknine2014positivity
)
.
The Positivity Problem at dimension greater than five has been shown to be intrinsically hard; and its decidability would entail major breakthroughs in open problems in Diophantine approximation
(
ouaknine2014positivity
)
.
Therefore, the freedom of choice for the initial value is crucial in proving decidability results.

In this paper we consider termination of the multipath loop (
1
) under
commutative
updates, that is, when
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
are commuting matrices.
In
(
babai1996multiplicative
)
, Babai, Beals, Cai, Ivanyos and Luks proved various results on reachability problems of multipath linear loops under commutative updates.
A continuous analogue of the reachability problem has been studied in
(
ouaknine2019decidability
)
.
For the termination problem, we restrict to the case where
\mathcal{C}
is a
polyhedral cone
, that is,

\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\middle|\;\boldsymbol{c}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\geq 0,i=1,\ldots,n\right\}

for some
\boldsymbol{c}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{c}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We will also exclude the initial value
(0,\ldots,0)
since it is trivially invariant under linear updates.
For the sake of simplicity, we will suppose
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
to have rational entries and are invertible, and that
\boldsymbol{c}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{c}_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
.
Our main result (see Theorem
5
) is that termination of such multipath linear loops is decidable.

If one tries to adapt the proofs for the single update case to multiple updates, natural attempts will start by simultaneously diagonalizing the update matrices and analysing their actions on the cone geometrically.
Unfortunately such analysis depends on classifying the complex eigenvalues of all the update matrices.
Besides the unavoidable tedious case analysis, it is also not clear how to circumvent certain problems related to Diophantine approximation, as such circumvention appears ad hoc in the single update case.
Our main contribution is a completely algebraic approach that overcomes this difficulty.
In particular, using the duality of linear programming, we reduce the problem to deciding whether an orbit cone is
salient
.
We show that deciding whether an orbit is salient is equivalent to deciding whether a submodule of
\left(\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}]\right)^{n}
contains an element with only positive coefficients.
We then use a result of Einsiedler, Mouat, and Tuncel
(
einsiedler2003does
)
and the decidability of first order theory of the reals to deduce the desired result.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Convex geometry

A
convex cone
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a subset
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
that satisfies
r\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{C}\implies r\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{C}
and
\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathcal{C}\implies\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathcal{C}
.
In this article, we will use the word
cone
to imply convex cones.
A
polyhedral
cone is a cone of the form

\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\middle|\;\boldsymbol{c}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\geq 0,i=1,\ldots,n\right\}.

for finitely many given non-zero vectors
\boldsymbol{c}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{c}_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Given mutually commuting matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, and a vector
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denote by

\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\rangle\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\coloneqq\left\{A_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots A_{k}^{n_{k}}\boldsymbol{v}\;\middle|\;n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

the orbit of
\boldsymbol{v}
under multiplication by
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
.
Similarly, given a cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denote by

\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\left\{A_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots A_{k}^{n_{k}}\boldsymbol{v}\;\middle|\;n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in\mathbb{N},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{C}\right\}

the orbit of
\mathcal{C}
under multiplication by
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
.

Given an arbitrary subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denote by
\boldsymbol{cone}(\mathcal{S})
the smallest cone in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
\mathcal{S}
.
Similarly, denote by
\boldsymbol{lin}(\mathcal{S})
the smallest linear space in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
containing
\mathcal{S}
.

A
closed halfspace
of a
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a subset
\mathcal{H}
defined by
\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\{\boldsymbol{x}\mid\boldsymbol{v}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\geq 0\}
for some
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0^{d}\}
.
Here,
0^{d}
denotes the zero vector of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
By the Supporting Hyperplane Theorem
(
boyd2004convex
, Chapter 2.5.2)
(applied at the point
0^{d}
), every cone
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is either contained in some closed halfspace or it is
\mathbb{R}^{d}
itself.

A cone
\mathcal{C}
is called
salient
if
\mathcal{C}\cap-\mathcal{C}=\{0^{d}\}
, where
-\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\{-\boldsymbol{v}\mid\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{C}\}
.
An arbitrary set
\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called salient if
\boldsymbol{cone}(\mathcal{S})
is salient.
Obviously every salient cone (and hence every salient set) is contained in a closed halfspace because a salient cone cannot be
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

2.2.
Modules over polynomial rings

Fix an integer
k\geq 1
.
In this paper we consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{A}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}]
and its sub-semiring

\mathbb{A}^{+}=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}]\\
\coloneqq\Bigg\{\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}r_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}X_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots X_{k}^{n_{k}}\;\Bigg|\;r_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\\
r_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}=0\text{ for all but finitely many }r_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}}\Bigg\}.

That is,
\mathbb{A}^{+}\subset\mathbb{A}
is the sub-semiring of polynomials with non-negative coefficients.
Define additionally
\mathbb{A}^{++}\coloneqq\mathbb{A}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
.

Let
\mathbb{A}^{n}\coloneqq\{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\mid f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathbb{A}\}
.
An
\mathbb{A}
-
submodule
of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
is a subset
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{A}^{n}
such that
\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathcal{M}\implies\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathcal{M}
and
f\in\mathbb{A},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{M}\implies f\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathcal{M}
.
Here
f\cdot(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\coloneqq(ff_{1},\ldots,ff_{n})
.

For example, given
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}\in\mathbb{A}^{n}
, the set

\mathcal{M}=\boldsymbol{g}_{1}\mathbb{A}+\cdots+\boldsymbol{g}_{m}\mathbb{A}\\
\coloneqq\{f_{1}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{1}+\cdots+f_{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{g}_{m}\mid f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in\mathbb{A}\}

is an
\mathbb{A}
-submodule of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
.
In this case we will say that
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}
form a
basis
of
\mathcal{M}
.
The above definition still works when one replaces
\mathbb{A}
by any commutative ring.

Naturally, we define the following subset of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
:

\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{n}\coloneqq\{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\mid f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}\in\mathbb{A}^{+}\}.

The key ingredient of our paper is a deep result by Einsiedler, Mouat, and Tuncel
(
einsiedler2003does
)
that characterizes when an
\mathbb{A}
-submodule of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
contains an element of
\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{n}\setminus\{0^{n}\}
.
We will state this result in Section
6
.

3.
Overview of the decision procedure

In this section we give an overview of the procedure that decides (non-)termination of the multipath loops under commutative updates.
In rigorous terms, given commuting matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
and a polyhedral cone
\mathcal{C}
, we want to decide whether there exists a vector
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0^{d}\}
, such that
\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\rangle\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\subset\mathcal{C}
.

Let the cone
\mathcal{C}
be given by

(3)

\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\middle|\;\boldsymbol{c}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{x}\geq 0,i=1,\ldots,n\right\}.

We define its
dual cone

\mathcal{C}^{*}
as

(4)

\mathcal{C}^{*}\coloneqq\left\{\boldsymbol{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;\middle|\;\boldsymbol{x}=r_{1}\boldsymbol{c}_{1}+\cdots+r_{n}\boldsymbol{c}_{n},r_{1},\ldots,r_{n}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\right\}.

Our first lemma is a duality type argument that reduces the termination problem to deciding whether an orbit is contained in some halfspace.

Lemma 1.

The following two conditions are equivalent:

(i)

There exists
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0^{d}\}
, such that
\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\rangle\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\subset\mathcal{C}
.

(ii)

The orbit
\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}
is contained in a closed halfspace.

The proof of Lemma
1
is given in Section
4
.
Deciding whether an orbit has the property of being contained in a closed halfspace is challenging.
In particular, the smallest cone containing the orbit might not be finitely generated.
It is very unlikely that one can compute any precise description of this cone, otherwise one would be able to decide whether it is contained in a
given
halfspace, and hence solve the Positivity Problem, entailing breakthroughs in number theory
(
ouaknine2014positivity
)
.
Therefore, we will first devise a procedure that decides a similar property for the orbit, namely whether it is salient.
We will later use it as a sub-procedure to decide whether the orbit is contained in a closed halfspace.
The next lemma reduces deciding salient-ness to finding certain tuples of polynomials in
\mathbb{A}^{+}
.

Lemma 2.

The following two conditions are equivalent:

(i)

The orbit
\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}
is not salient.

(ii)

There exists a tuple of polynomials
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{n}\setminus\{0^{n}\}
, not all zero, such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top})\cdot\boldsymbol{c}_{i}=0^{d}
.

The proof of Lemma
2
is given in Section
4
.

Define the following
\mathbb{A}
-submodule of
\mathbb{A}^{n}
.

(5)

\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\left\{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})\in\mathbb{A}^{n}\;\middle|\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top})\cdot\boldsymbol{c}_{i}=\boldsymbol{0}\right\}.

One easily verifies that
\boldsymbol{g},\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathcal{M}\implies\boldsymbol{g}+\boldsymbol{h}\in\mathcal{M}
, as well as
f\in\mathbb{A},\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathcal{M}\implies f\cdot\boldsymbol{g}\in\mathcal{M}
; so
\mathcal{M}
is indeed an
\mathbb{A}
-module.
Observe that condition (ii) of Lemma
2
can be expressed as “
\mathcal{M}
contains an element of
\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{n}\setminus\{0^{d}\}^{n}
”.
The next proposition shows that a basis of
\mathcal{M}
is computable.

Proposition 3.

Given as input
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
and
\boldsymbol{c}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{c}_{n}
, one can compute a basis
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}\in\mathbb{A}^{n}
such that
\mathcal{M}=\boldsymbol{g}_{1}\mathbb{A}+\cdots+\boldsymbol{g}_{m}\mathbb{A}
.

The proof of Proposition
3
is given in Section
5
.
The next proposition shows that it is decidable whether the module
\mathcal{M}
contains an element of
\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{n}\setminus\{0^{n}\}
.
Hence condition (ii) of Lemma
2
is decidable.

Proposition 4.

There is a procedure that, given as input a basis
\boldsymbol{g}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{g}_{m}\in\mathbb{A}^{n}
with rational coefficients such that
\mathcal{M}=\boldsymbol{g}_{1}\mathbb{A}+\cdots+\boldsymbol{g}_{m}\mathbb{A}
, decides whether
\mathcal{M}
contains an element of
\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{n}\setminus\{0^{n}\}
,
and outputs this element
\boldsymbol{f}
in case it exists.

The proof of Proposition
4
is given in Section
6
.
Combining Lemma
2
, Proposition
3
and
4
, we conclude that it is decidable whether the orbit
\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}
is salient.
We now use this to show that it is decidable whether
\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}
is contained in a closed halfspace.
This together with Lemma
1
will give us the main result:

Theorem 5.

Given commuting invertible matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d\times d}
and vectors
\boldsymbol{c}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{c}_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
defining a polyhedral cone
\mathcal{C}
as in Equation (
3
), it is decidable whether there exists
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0^{d}\}
such that
\langle A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}\rangle\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\subset\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, it suffices to construct a procedure that decides whether the orbit
\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}
is contained in a closed halfspace.
We use induction on
d
.
When
d=1
, the decision procedure is easy:
\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}
is contained in a closed halfspace if and only if
\boldsymbol{c}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{c}_{n}
all have the same sign and
A_{1},\ldots,A_{k}
are all positive rationals.

Suppose we have a decision procedure for all dimensions smaller than
d
, we now construct a procedure for dimension
d
.
By Lemma
2
, Proposition
3
and
4
, it is decidable whether the orbit
\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}
is salient.

If the orbit is salient, then it is contained in some closed halfspace.
If the orbit is not salient, then
\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)
contains the vectors
\boldsymbol{w}
and
-\boldsymbol{w}
for some
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0^{d}\}
.
Such a
\boldsymbol{w}
can be effectively computed in the following way:
by Proposition
4
, one can compute an element
\boldsymbol{f}\in\mathcal{M}\cap\left(\mathbb{A}^{+}\right)^{n}\setminus\{0^{n}\}
.
Then, as in the proof of Lemma
2
(see Section
4
), one can obtain from
\boldsymbol{f}
a non-zero vector
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{Q}^{d}
such that
\pm\boldsymbol{w}\in\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)
.

We then compute (a basis of) the linear space

W\coloneqq\boldsymbol{lin}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\boldsymbol{w}\right).

This can be done by starting with
W\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\boldsymbol{w}
and repeatedly replacing
W
by
W+A_{1}W+\cdots+A_{k}W
until
W=W+A_{1}W+\cdots+A_{k}W
.
This process must terminate since the dimension of
W
is always growing.
The resulting
W=\boldsymbol{lin}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\boldsymbol{w}\right)
is invariant under the linear maps
A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}
.
Since
\mathbb{R}\boldsymbol{w}\subset\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)
we have
W\subset\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)
.

The quotient
\mathbb{R}^{d}/W
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{d-\dim(W)}
after fixing a basis.
We can suppose this basis admits only rational entries.
Composing with the canonical map
\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}/W
we have an (effectively computable) map

\pi\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d-\dim(W)}

with
\ker(\pi)=W
.
Since multiplication by the matrices
A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}
leaves
W
invariant, these matrices act on
\mathbb{R}^{d}/W\cong\mathbb{R}^{d-\dim(W)}
linearly.
We denote these actions by

B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}^{(d-\dim(W))\times(d-\dim(W))}.

These matrices are obviously invertible, commuting and effectively computable.

Using the induction hypothesis on the dimension
d
, we can decide whether the image

\pi\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)=\langle B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}\rangle\cdot\pi(\mathcal{C}^{*})

is contained in some closed halfspace (of
\mathbb{R}^{d}/W\cong\mathbb{R}^{d-\dim(W)}
).
If the image
\pi\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)
is contained in a closed halfspace
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-\dim(W)}
then
\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}
is contained in the closed halfspace
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{H})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Otherwise,

\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\pi\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)\right)=\mathbb{R}^{d-\dim(W)}.

Since
\pi(\boldsymbol{v}+\boldsymbol{w})=\pi(\boldsymbol{v})+\pi(\boldsymbol{w})
for all
\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the above equation yields

\pi\left(\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)\right)=\mathbb{R}^{d-\dim(W)}.

Thus, any
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be written as
\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime\prime}
where

\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}\in\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right),

and
\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime\prime}\in W
.
Recall that
W\subset\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)
.
Therefore,
\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime\prime}\in\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)
for all
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
So

\boldsymbol{cone}\left(\langle A_{1}^{\top},\ldots,A_{k}^{\top}\rangle\cdot\mathcal{C}^{*}\right)=\mathbb{R}^{d}

is not contained in any closed halfspace.
∎

7.
Conclusion and future work

In this paper we proved decidability of termination of linear loops under commutative updates, where the guard condition is a polyhedral cone.
A natural continuation of our work would be to generalize Theorem
5
to the case where
\mathcal{C}
is a polyhedron (instead of a polyhedral cone).
Unfortunately, the duality argument that is crucial in our proof stops working when we remove the homogeneity of the guard conditions.
New techniques, possibly combining geometric and algebraic arguments, might be needed to overcome this difficulty.
Another possible generalization is the removal of the commutativity assumption on the update matrices.
For this, one would need a generalization of Einsiedler, Mouat, and Tuncel’s result to left-modules over non-commutative polynomials rings, which would have deep consequences in the field of real algebra.

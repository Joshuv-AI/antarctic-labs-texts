Quotients of Severi–Brauer surfaces
Andrey Trepalin

Abstract
. We show that a quotient of a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface S S over arbitrary field 𝕜 \Bbbk of characteristic 0 0 by a finite group G ⊂ Aut ⁡ ( S ) G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(S) is 𝕜 \Bbbk -rational, if and only if | G | |G| is divisible by 3 3 . Otherwise, the quotient is birationally equivalent to S S .



Let
\Bbbk
be an arbitrary field of characteristic zero, and
\overline{\Bbbk}
be its algebraic closure.
A
d
-dimensional
variety
X
is called a
Severi–Brauer variety
if
\overline{X}=X\otimes\overline{\Bbbk}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{d}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
. If
d=1
then
X
is a conic, and if
d=2
then
X
is called a
Severi–Brauer surface
.

A Severi–Brauer variety is called
non-trivial
if it is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{d}_{\Bbbk}
. It is well known that a Severi–Brauer variety is trivial if and only if it has a
\Bbbk
-point. Moreover, if
X
is a non-trivial
d
-dimensional Severi–Brauer variety and
d+1
is a prime number, then degree of any point on
X
is divisible by
d+1
(see
[
Kol16
, Theorem 53]
).

Complete classification of finite subgroups of automorphism groups of non-trivial Severi–Brauer surfaces is obtained in works of C. Shramov and V. Vologodsky
[
ShV20
]
,
[
Sh20a
]
,
[
Sh20b
]
,
[
Sh21
]
. Let
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
be a cyclic group of order
n
. Then we have the following.

Theorem 1

(cf.
[
Sh21
, Theorem 1.3(ii)]
)
.

Let
S
be a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface over a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. Then any finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
is isomorphic to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
,
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3n}
,
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
or
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}\times\left({\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}\right)
, where
n
is a positive integer divisible only by primes congruent to
1
modulo
3
(including the case
n=1
), and
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
is a semidirect product corresponding to an outer automorphism of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
of order
3
acting non-trivially on each non-trivial element of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
.

Moreover, for any group
G
mentioned above there exists a field
\Bbbk
of charectiristic zero and a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface
S
over
\Bbbk
, such that
G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(S)
.

The aim of this paper is to obtain a birational classification of quotients of non-trivial Severi–Brauer surfaces by finite subgroups of automorphism groups. In particular, we want to answer a question, for which Severi–Brauer surfaces
S
and finite subgroups
G\subset\operatorname{Aut}(S)
the quotient
S/G
is
\Bbbk
-rational (i.e. birationally equivalent to
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
). Note that for an algebraically closed field
\overline{\Bbbk}
of characteristic zero quotients of
\overline{\Bbbk}
-rational surfaces by finite automorphism groups are always
\overline{\Bbbk}
-rational by Castelnuovo’s rationality criterion (see
[
Cast94
]
). Moreover, for arbitrary field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero quotients of
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
by finite automorphism groups are always
\Bbbk
-rational (see
[
Tr14
, Theorem 1.3]
). One can find more results about rationality of quotients of
\Bbbk
-rational surfaces by finite automorphism groups in
[
Tr19
]
. The main result of this paper is the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
S
be a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface over a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero, and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
. Then the quotient
S/G
is
\Bbbk
-rational, if and only if
|G|
is divisible by
3
. Otherwise, the quotient
S/G
is birationally equivalent to
S
.

This theorem is an analogue of the following proposition.

Proposition 3
.

Let
C
be a conic over a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero without
\Bbbk
-points, and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(C)
. Then the quotient
C/G
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, if and only if
G
has even order.

Proof.

The degree of any point on
C
is even. Assume that the order of
G
is odd. Then any
G
-orbit on the set of geometric points on
C
has odd cardinality. Thus the image of any such orbit on
C/G
is not defined over
\Bbbk
, and the quotient
C/G
does not have
\Bbbk
-points and is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
.

Now assume that the order of
G
is even. Therefore there is an element
g\in G
of order
2
. The anticanonical map given by the linear system
|-K_{C}|
gives an embedding
C\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
, such that the action of
g
on
C
induces an action of
g
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
. One can choose coordinates
(x:y:z)
on
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
such that the action of
g
is given by
(x:y:z)\mapsto(x:y:-z)
. Then the quotient map
C\rightarrow C/\langle g\rangle
is just a restriction of the projection
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
given by
(x:y:z)\mapsto(x:y)
.

Therefore some
\langle g\rangle
-orbits are defined over
\Bbbk
, and thus some
G
-orbits are defined over
\Bbbk
too. It means that there are
\Bbbk
-points on the quotient
C/G
, and therefore this quotient is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\Bbbk}
, since one has
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}/G\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
for any finite subgroup
G\subset\operatorname{Aut}\left(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}\right)
.

∎

The proof of Theorem
2
is more complicated, and we decompose it into several lemmas.

Lemma 4
.

Let
S
be a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface over a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
isomorphic to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
. Then the set of
G
-fixed points on
S
consists of three isolated geometric points. If
n>3
then these points are defined over extension
K/\Bbbk
of degree
3
.

Proof.

Consider the action of
G
on
\overline{S}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
. This action can be diagonalized in
\mathrm{PGL}_{3}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}\right)
. One can see that the set of fixed points of
G
either consists of an isolated fixed point and a line, or consists of three isolated points
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
and
p_{3}
. In the former case the isolated fixed point is unique, and therefore defined over
\Bbbk
, but there are no
\Bbbk
-points on
S
. Therefore
G
has three isolated fixed geometric points on
S
.

Isolated fixed geometric points of
G
are transitevely permuted by the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}/\Bbbk\right)
. Therefore these points are defined either over extension
K/\Bbbk
of degree
3
with
\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
, or over extension
K/\Bbbk
of degree
6
with
\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}
, where
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
is a non-abelian group of order
6
. In particular, there is an element
\gamma\in\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}/\Bbbk\right)
of order
3
, such that
\gamma p_{1}=p_{2}
,
\gamma p_{2}=p_{3}
,
\gamma p_{3}=p_{1}
.

If
n>3
then there is a subgroup
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{p}\subset G
for a prime number
p
such that
p
equals
1
modulo
3
. The action of a generator of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{p}
on
\overline{S}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
can be written as
\operatorname{diag}(\xi_{p};\xi_{p}^{a};1)
, where
\xi_{p}
is
a
p
-th
root of unity. This element acts on the tangent spaces
T_{p_{1}}\overline{S}
,
T_{p_{2}}\overline{S}
and
T_{p_{3}}\overline{S}
as
\operatorname{diag}\left(\xi_{p};\xi_{p}^{a}\right)
,
\operatorname{diag}\left(\xi_{p}^{a-1};\xi_{p}^{-1}\right)
and
\operatorname{diag}\left(\xi_{p}^{-a};\xi_{p}^{1-a}\right)
respectively.

One has
\gamma p_{1}=p_{2}
. Thus
\gamma\left(\xi_{p}\right)=\xi_{p}^{a-1}
, and

\xi_{p}^{-1}=\gamma\left(\xi_{p}^{a}\right)=\gamma\left(\xi_{p}\right)^{a}=\xi_{p}^{a(a-1)}.

Therefore
a^{2}-a+1=0
modulo
p
. In particular,
a\neq 1
and
a\neq-1
modulo
p
.

Assume that the Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}\left(\overline{\Bbbk}/\Bbbk\right)
contains an element
\delta
such that
\delta p_{1}=p_{1}
,
\delta p_{2}=p_{3}
and
\delta p_{3}=p_{2}
. Then
\delta\left(\xi_{p}\right)=\xi_{p}^{a}
, and
\delta\left(\xi_{p}^{2}\right)=\xi_{p}
. Therefore
a^{2}=1
modulo
p
. But
a\neq\pm 1
modulo
p
. We have a contradiction. Hence if
n>3
then the points
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
and
p_{3}
are defined over an extension
K/\Bbbk
of degree
3
.
∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
S
be a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface over a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
isomorphic to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
, where
n
is a positive integer divisible only by primes congruent to
1
modulo
3
. Then the quotient
S/G
is birationally equivalent to
S
.

Proof.

Any point on a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface has degree divisible by
3
. Therefore there are no
\Bbbk
-points on
S/G
, since cardinalities
of
G
-orbits
are not divisible by
3
.

For
n=1
the assertion of the lemma is trivial. Therefore assume that
n>1
.

By Lemma
4
the set of
G
-fixed points on
S
consists of three isolated geometric points defined over an extension
K/\Bbbk
of degree
3
. Any cyclic group acting on
\overline{S}\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\overline{\Bbbk}}
is a subgroup of a torus acting on
\overline{S}
. Therefore the quotient
S/G
is a
\Bbbk
-form of a toric surface with three singular points, that are images of
G
-fixed points on
S
. The Galois group
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}\cong\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)
acts on the fan of the corresponding toric surface. Let us resolve the singularities of
S/G
and run
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
-equivariant minimal model program. As a result we should obtain
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
-minimal del Pezzo surface or conic bundle
S^{\prime}
, such that
\overline{S}^{\prime}
is a toric surface. Any toric del Pezzo surface or conic bundle
X
has
K_{X}^{2}\geqslant 6
, and one can easily check that for such surface a group
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
can faithfully act only on the fan of a del Pezzo surface of degree
9
or
6
. Moreover, del Pezzo surface of degree
6
is not
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
-minimal, since one can
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
-equivariantly blow down a triple of disjoint
(-1)
-curves. Thus
S^{\prime}
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
9
without
\Bbbk
-points, i.e. non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface.

The existence of rational map
S\dashrightarrow S^{\prime}
implies that the class of
S^{\prime}
in the Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}\left(\Bbbk\right)
lies in the cyclic group generated by the class of
S
(see
[
GS18
, Exercise 3.3.8(iii)]
). Therefore
S^{\prime}
is either isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
, that is impossible since there are no
\Bbbk
-points on
S^{\prime}
, or isomorphic to
S
, or isomorphic to
S^{op}
, where
S^{op}
is the Severi–Brauer surface corresponding to the central simple algebra opposite to the one corresponding to
S
. The surface
S^{op}
is birationally equivalent to
S
(see
[
Ami55
]
). Thus
S/G
is birationally equivalent to
S
.

∎

Remark 6
.

Note that the proof of Lemma
5
can be easily generalized on the case where
N\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
is a normal subgroup of a finite group
G
, such that
G
is isomorphic to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3n}
,
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
or
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}\times\left({\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}\right)
. In this case the quotient
S/N
is
G/N
-birationally equivalent to a Severi–Brauer surface
S^{\prime}
, since the action of
G/N
on the fan corresponding to
S/N
is either trivial, or coincides with the action of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}\cong\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)
.

Now assume that a group
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
acts on a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface
S
. Then by Lemma
4
the group
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
has three isolated fixed geometric points defined either over extension
K/\Bbbk
of degree
3
with
\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
, or over extension
K/\Bbbk
of degree
6
with
\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}
. We consider these two cases separately, and show that in the both cases the quotient
S/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
is
\Bbbk
-rational.

Lemma 7
.

Let
S
be a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
isomorphic to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
. Assume that the isolated fixed points of
G
are defined over extension
K/\Bbbk
of degree
3
with
\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
. Then the quotient
S/G
is
\Bbbk
-rational.

Proof.

Let
p_{1}
,
p_{2}
and
p_{3}
be the isolated fixed geometric points of
G
on
S
. One can
G
-equivariantly
blow up this triple of points and get a del Pezzo surface
\widetilde{S}
of degree
6
. Note that each of six
(-1)
-curves is
G
-invariant, therefore the six points of intersection of these six curves are
G
-fixed. One can easily check that on the tangent space of
\overline{\widetilde{S}}
at each of these points the group
G
acts as
\langle\operatorname{diag}\left(\omega;\omega\right)\rangle
, where
\omega
is a third root of unity.

The group
\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
does not preserve any
G
-fixed point on
\widetilde{S}
. Therefore one can blow up any triple of
G
-fixed points defined over
\Bbbk
, and get a surface
\widetilde{X}
. One has
K_{\widetilde{X}}^{2}=3
, the exceptional divisor of the blowup
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\widetilde{S}
is a triple of pointwisely
G
-fixed
(-1)
-curves
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
, and the proper transforms of the six
(-1)
-curves on
\widetilde{S}
are
(-2)
-curves on
\widetilde{X}
. One can check that
\widetilde{X}
is a weak del Pezzo surface, and the anticanonical map

\varphi_{|-K_{\widetilde{X}}|}:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X

blows down the six
(-2)
-curves into three
A_{2}
-singularities. Moreover,
X
is a singular cubic surface, and the images of
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
and
E_{3}
are lines passing through pairs of singular points.

The linear systems
|-K_{\widetilde{S}}|
,
|-K_{\widetilde{X}}|
and
|-K_{X}|
are
G
-invariant, since the morphisms
\widetilde{S}\rightarrow S
,
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\widetilde{S}
and
\widetilde{X}\rightarrow X
are
G
-equivariant. Therefore the action of
G
on
X
induces an action of
G
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
. Moreover,
G
pointwisely fixes a plane in
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
since it fixes three lines meeting each other. Therefore one can choose coordinates
(x:y:z:t)
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}
such that the action of
G
is given by
(x:y:z:t)\mapsto(x:y:z:\omega t)
. Then the quotient map
X\rightarrow X/G
is just a restriction of the projection
\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\Bbbk}\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
given by

(x:y:z:t)\mapsto(x:y:z)

(cf. the proof of Proposition
3
). Therefore
X/G\cong\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\Bbbk}
, and
S/G
is
\Bbbk
-rational, since
S/G
and
X/G
are birationally equivalent.

∎

For the remaining case
\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}
we need the following lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Let
S^{\prime}
be a del Pezzo surface of degree
6
over arbitrary field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
0
. If
S^{\prime}
contains a point of degree
2
and a point degree
3
, then
S^{\prime}
is
\Bbbk
-rational.

Proof.

Let
L
be an extension of degree
2
of
\Bbbk
, such that there is a
L
-point on
S^{\prime}\otimes L
. Then
S^{\prime}\otimes L
is
L
-rational, therefore we can find a pair of geometric points
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
on
S^{\prime}
defined over
L
and not lying on
(-1)
-curves.

Let us consider the blowup
X\rightarrow S^{\prime}
at
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
. One can check that the surface
X
is a (possibly, weak) del Pezzo surface of degree
4
, and there is a point of degree
3
on
X
. The anticanonical map given by
|-K_{X}|
maps
X
to
X^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}_{\Bbbk}
, where
X^{\prime}
is a (possibly, singular) del Pezzo surface of degree
4
. The arguments of the proof of
[
Sh20c
, Lemma 2.4]
show that in this case
X^{\prime}
has a smooth
\Bbbk
-point. For convenience of the reader, we give the other proof of this result.

Indeed, let
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
and
q_{3}
be three geometric points on
X^{\prime}
, such that this triple is defined over
\Bbbk
. Such triple exists since
S^{\prime}
contains a point of degree
3
. Consider a plane
\Pi
defined over
\Bbbk
and passing through
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
and
q_{3}
. The intersection number
\Pi\cdot X^{\prime}=4
, and the multiplicity of the intersection is the same at the points
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
and
q_{3}
. Therefore, if
\Pi\cap X^{\prime}
is zero-dimensional, then there is an other smooth
\Bbbk
-point
q
on
\Pi\cap X^{\prime}
.

If
\Pi\cap X^{\prime}
is one-dimensional, then the intersection
C=X^{\prime}\cap\Pi
is a conic, since
X^{\prime}
is an intersection of two quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{4}_{\Bbbk}
. If
C
is smooth, then there are infinitely many
\Bbbk
-points on
C
, since there is a point of degree
3
on
C
. If
C
is a union of two lines then the points
q_{1}
,
q_{2}
and
q_{3}
have to lie on one of these lines. Therefore this line is defined over
\Bbbk
. If
C
is a double line, then this line is defined over
\Bbbk
. In each case we find infinitely many
\Bbbk
-points on
C
, thus there is a smooth
\Bbbk
-point on
X^{\prime}
.

The image of any
\Bbbk
-point on
X^{\prime}
under the map
X^{\prime}\rightarrow S^{\prime}
is a
\Bbbk
-point on
S^{\prime}
. Therefore
S^{\prime}
is
\Bbbk
-rational by
[
Isk96
, Chapter 4]
.

∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
S
be a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface and
G
be a finite subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
isomorphic to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
. Assume that the isolated fixed points of
G
are defined over extension
K/\Bbbk
of degree
6
with
\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{3}
. Then the quotient
S/G
is
\Bbbk
-rational.

Proof.

The group
G
has three isolated fixed points on
S
, that are defined over
K
. Therefore the quotient
S/G
is a
\Bbbk
-form of a singular toric surface, such that the three singular points are not defined over
\Bbbk
, and defined over
K
. The Galois group
\mathfrak{S}_{3}\cong\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)
acts on the fan of the corresponding toric surface. Let us resolve the singularities of
S/G
and run
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
-equivariant minimal model programm. As a result we should obtain
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
-minimal del Pezzo surface or conic bundle
S^{\prime}
, such that
\overline{S}^{\prime}
is a toric surface. Any toric del Pezzo surface or conic bundle
X
has
K_{X}^{2}\geqslant 6
, and one can easily check that for such surface a group
\mathfrak{S}_{3}
can faithfully act only on the fan of a del Pezzo surface of degree
9
or
6
.

Let us show that
S^{\prime}
is
\Bbbk
-rational. Note that there is a normal subgroup
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
in the group
\mathfrak{S}_{3}\cong\operatorname{Gal}\left(K/\Bbbk\right)
, therefore there exist an extension
L=K^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}}
of
\Bbbk
of degree
2
, such that the isolated fixed points of
G
are not defined over
L
, and defined over the extension
K/L
of degree
3
. Thus
S^{\prime}\otimes L\cong\left(S\otimes L\right)/G
is
L
-rational by Lemma
7
. So there is a point of degree
2
on
S^{\prime}
. If
S^{\prime}
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
9
then
S^{\prime}
is
\Bbbk
-rational, since the line passing through a point of degree
2
is defined over
\Bbbk
.

If
S^{\prime}
is a del Pezzo surface of degree
6
then there is a point of degree
3
on
S^{\prime}
, since the image of any point of degree
3
on
S
under the quotient map
S/G
is either a
\Bbbk
-point, or a point of degree
3
. Therefore
S^{\prime}
is
\Bbbk
-rational by Lemma
8
.

∎

Now we can prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

A finite subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{Aut}(S)
is isomorphic to
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
,
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3n}
,
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}

or
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}\times\left({\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}\rtimes{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}\right)
by Theorem
1
.

If there is a subgroup
N\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
in
G
, where
n
is a positive integer divisible only by primes congruent to
1
modulo
3
, then
N
is normal in
G
, and the quotient
S/N

is
G/N
-birationally
equivalent to a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface
S^{\prime}
with the action of
G/N
by Lemma
5
and Remark
6
. In particular, if
G\cong{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
, then
S/G
is birationally equivalent to
S
by Lemma
5
.

The other three cases of
G
are reduced to the case, where a group
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
or
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}^{2}
acts on a non-trivial Severi–Brauer surface, since
S/G
and
S^{\prime}/(G/N)
are birationally equivalent.

The quotient of Severi–Brauer surface
S
by
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{3}
is
\Bbbk
-rational by Lemmas
7

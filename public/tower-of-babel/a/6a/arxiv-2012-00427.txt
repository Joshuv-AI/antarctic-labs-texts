On the special affine representations for hyperbolic groups.


Abstract
. In this paper we extend the construction of special representations [ 35 ] [ 21 ] to Gromov hyperbolic groups which admits complementary series in the sense of [ 4 ] . We prove that these representations have a natural non-trivial reduced cohomology class [ c ] [c] . An analogue of Kuhn-Vershik’s formula [ 21 ] is established and as a by-product a characterisation of hyperbolic groups that admit complementary series. Investigating dynamical properties of the cohomology class [ c ] [c] we prove an cocycle equidistribution theorem á la Roblin-Margulis [ 32 ] [ 23 ] (see also [ 17 ] [ 8 ] [ 5 ] ) and deduce the irreducibility of the associated affine actions [ 26 ] [ 3 ] [ 1 ] . The irreducibility of the affine actions associated to the canonical class [ c ] [c] is original even in the case of uniform lattices in S ​ O ​ ( n , 1 ) SO(n,1) , S ​ U ​ ( n , 1 ) SU(n,1) or S ​ L 2 ​ ( ℚ p ) SL_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}) with n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 and p p prime.

Given a unitary representation

.
A cocycle with coefficients in

The Hilbert norm on the space of cocycles

]

-orthogonal decomposition holds:

Since

for all

\beta
-harmonic cocycles and thus:

The following criterion was proved by Erschler and Ozawa in
[

]
:

Lemma 4.6

Given a unitary representation

and

Corollary 4.7

\Gamma

Proof.

As a consequence of Kingman’s subadditive theorem (see
[
36
]
) together with Proposition
4.3
one has:

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\frac{1}{n}{\mathbb{E}}_{\beta}[\|c(X_{n})\|_{H_{1}^{\prime}}^{2}]

Appendix A
Positive operators on reflexive Banach spaces.

Let
V
be a real separable reflexive Banach space and
T
a bounded operator between
V
and
V^{\prime}
.

We call a bounded operator
T
on
V

self-adjoint
if for all
x,y\in V
,
T(x)(y)=T(y)(x)
, in other words
T^{\prime}:V\simeq(V^{\prime})^{\prime}\rightarrow V^{\prime}
identifies with
T
.

Remark A.1
.

Recall that if
W\subset V
denote a closed subspace of
V
, then
W
and
V/W
are also reflexive.
Moreover the dual of
V/W
is isomorphic to the annihilator of
W

[
12
]
.
As the adjoint of
T
takes value in the annihilator of the kernel of
T
, one can assume
T
has trivial kernel whenever
T
is self-adjoint.
Moreover it follows from Hahn-Banach that
T
has dense range in this case.

A self-adjoint operator
T
is
positive
if the quadratic form
v\in V\mapsto Q_{T}(v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}T(v)(v)
is positive definite on
V
.

Given a positive operator the non-degenerated bilinear form,
Q_{T}
, induces a real Hilbert space,
H_{T}(V)
, by completion of
V
with respect to
Q_{T}
.
As
T
is bounded the inclusion
v\in V\mapsto[v]\in H_{T}(V)
is bounded with norm at most
\|T\|_{\text{op}}
.

Conversely given a (injective) bounded operator,
S
, from
V
into a Hilbert space
H
, the operator
T\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}S^{\prime}S
is positive on
V
.

Remark A.2
.

Every separable Banach space embeds into a Hilbert space.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
T
be a positive operator on
V
.
There exists a dense Hilbert subspace,
H_{T}^{\prime}
, in
V^{\prime}
that contains the range of
T
such that
T:V\rightarrow V^{\prime}
extends as an isometry between
H_{T}
and
H_{T}^{\prime}
and duality between
V
and
V^{\prime}
as a non-degenerated bilinear form between
H_{T}
and
H_{T}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

The operator
T
is bijective between
V
and
R\subset V^{\prime}
with
R
the range of
T
.

Given
y\in R
and
x\in V
such that
T(x)=y
we define
Q(y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}T(x)(x)
.
The space
H_{T}^{\prime}
is obtained from the completion of
R
with respect to
Q
.
It follows from the definition that
\|T(x):H_{T}^{\prime}\|=\|x|H_{T}\|
, i.e
T
induces an isometry between
H_{T}^{\prime}
and
H_{T}
.
For all
x\in V
it follows from Hahn-Banach that one can find
x^{\prime}\in V
with
\|x^{\prime}\|=1
such that

\|T(x)|V^{\prime}\|=T(x)(x^{\prime})\leq\sqrt{T(x)(x)}\sqrt{T(x^{\prime})(x^{\prime})}\leq\|T\|_{\text{op}}\|x|H_{T}\|=\|T(x):H_{T}^{\prime}\|

and thus
R\subset H_{T}^{\prime}\subset V^{\prime}
.
On the other hand for all

and

\displaystyle|T(x^{\prime})(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq_{\text{C-S ineq.}}\overline{T(x^{\prime})}(x^{\prime})\overline{T(x)}(x)

\displaystyle=\|x|H_{T}\|^{2}\|T(x^{\prime})|H_{T}^{\prime}\|^{2}

which proves the extension of the duality.
∎

Limit Distribution of Two Skellam Distributions, Conditionally on Their Equality
Élie de Panafieu, François Durand

Abstract




This note provides a proof of the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Let
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
,
C_{n}
and
D_{n}
denote independent random Poisson variables of respective parameters
n\tau_{A}
,
n\tau_{B}
,
n\tau_{C}
and
n\tau_{D}
. We denote
X_{n}=(A_{n}-B_{n}\mid A_{n}-B_{n}=C_{n}-D_{n})
.

1.

If
\tau_{A}=\tau_{B}=0
, or
\tau_{B}=\tau_{C}=0
, or
\tau_{C}=\tau_{D}=0
, or
\tau_{D}=\tau_{A}=0
, then the distribution of
X_{n}
is a Dirac measure in 0.

2.

Otherwise,

\displaystyle\mathds{E}(X_{n})

\displaystyle=nE+E^{\prime}+o(1),

\displaystyle\mathds{V}(X_{n})

\displaystyle=nV+V^{\prime}+o(1),

where

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}-\tau_{B}\tau_{D}}{\sqrt{(\tau_{A}+\tau_{D})(\tau_{B}+\tau_{C})}},

\displaystyle E^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}-\tau_{B}\tau_{D}}{4(\tau_{A}+\tau_{D})(\tau_{B}+\tau_{C})},

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\frac{(\tau_{A}\tau_{C}+2\tau_{A}\tau_{B}+\tau_{B}\tau_{D})(\tau_{A}\tau_{C}+2\tau_{C}\tau_{D}+\tau_{B}\tau_{D})}{2\big[(\tau_{A}+\tau_{D})(\tau_{B}+\tau_{C})\big]^{\frac{3}{2}}},

\displaystyle V^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\tau_{A}\tau_{C}+\tau_{B}\tau_{D})(\tau_{A}\tau_{B}+\tau_{C}\tau_{D})+4\tau_{A}\tau_{B}\tau_{C}\tau_{D}}{4\big[(\tau_{A}+\tau_{D})(\tau_{B}+\tau_{C})\big]^{2}}.

And the distribution of
(X_{n}-nE)/\sqrt{nV}
is asymptotically Gaussian.

Since case
1
is trivial, we only need to prove case
2
and thus assume that
\tau_{A}+\tau_{B}>0
and
\tau_{B}+\tau_{C}>0
and
\tau_{C}+\tau_{D}>0
and
\tau_{D}+\tau_{A}>0
.

Up to exchanging roles between
A_{n}
and
B_{n}
, and between
C_{n}
and
D_{n}
, we also assume that
\tau_{A}\tau_{C}\geq\tau_{B}\tau_{D}
. Intuitively, it means that in
X_{n}=(A_{n}-B_{n}\mid A_{n}-B_{n}=C_{n}-D_{n})
, the “positive” forces
A_{n}
and
C_{n}
are stronger than the “negative” ones,
B_{n}
and
D_{n}
. It corresponds to the cases where
E
, the main term in the asymptotic development of the expectation, will be proved nonnegative.

1
Generic Case: All Coefficients Are Positive

For this section, we add the assumption that
\tau_{A}
,
\tau_{B}
,
\tau_{C}
and
\tau_{D}
are all positive. This assumption will be removed in the second section.

Let
P_{n}(u)
denote the probability generating function

P_{n}(u)=\sum_{m\in\mathds{Z}}\mathds{P}(A_{n}-B_{n}=m\mid A_{n}-B_{n}=C_{n}-D_{n})u^{m}.

Introducing the function

F_{n}(u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b,c,d\geq 0\\
a-b=c-d\end{subarray}}\mathds{P}(A_{n}=a)\mathds{P}(B_{n}=b)\mathds{P}(C_{n}=c)\mathds{P}(D_{n}=d)u^{a-b},

we obtain

P_{n}(u)=\frac{F_{n}(u)}{F_{n}(1)}.

Expressing the probabilities explicitly, the expression becomes

F_{n}(u)=e^{-n(\tau_{A}+\tau_{B}+\tau_{C}+\tau_{D})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b,c,d\geq 0\\
a-b=c-d\end{subarray}}\frac{(n\tau_{A})^{a}}{a!}\frac{(n\tau_{B})^{b}}{b!}\frac{(n\tau_{C})^{c}}{c!}\frac{(n\tau_{D})^{d}}{d!}u^{a-b}.

Introducing the variable
g=a-b=c-d
, we obtain

F_{n}(u)=e^{-n(\tau_{A}+\tau_{B}+\tau_{C}+\tau_{D})}G_{n}(u),

where

G_{n}(u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b,d\geq 0\\
g\geq max(-b,-d)\end{subarray}}\frac{(n\tau_{A})^{(b+g)}}{(b+g)!}\frac{(n\tau_{B})^{b}}{b!}\frac{(n\tau_{C})^{(d+g)}}{(d+g)!}\frac{(n\tau_{D})^{d}}{d!}u^{g}.

The Stirling approximation is introduced and we define the values

x=\frac{b}{n}
,
y=\frac{d}{n}
,
z=\frac{g}{n}
. Then:

G_{n}(u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b,d\geq 0\\
g\geq max(-b,-d)\end{subarray}}\psi_{n}(x,y,z)e^{-n\phi_{u}(x,y,z)},

where

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x,y,z)=\

\displaystyle\frac{(n(x+z))^{n(x+z)}e^{-n(x+z)}}{(n(x+z))!}\frac{(nx)^{nx}e^{-nx}}{(nx)!}\frac{(n(y+z))^{n(y+z)}e^{-n(y+z)}}{(n(y+z))!}\frac{(ny)^{ny}e^{-ny}}{(ny)!},

\displaystyle\phi_{u}(x,y,z)=\

\displaystyle x\log(x)-x(1+\log(\tau_{B}))+(x+z)\log(x+z)-(x+z)(1+\log(\tau_{A}))

\displaystyle+y\log(y)-y(1+\log(\tau_{D}))+(y+z)\log(y+z)-(y+z)(1+\log(\tau_{C}))-z\log(u).

The above expression is well defined because we assumed that
\tau_{A}
,
\tau_{B}
,
\tau_{C}
and
\tau_{D}
are all positive.

We will soon see that
the main contributions to
G_{n}(u)

come from the vicinity of the minimum of
\phi_{u}
.
We will first compute this main contribution,
then prove that the rest of the sum is negligible.

The function
\phi_{u}
is convex with a unique minimum.
Therefore, there is a small enough vicinity
\mathcal{V}
of this minimum such that
\phi_{u}

is larger anywhere outside this vicinity than anywhere inside.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\mathcal{V}

does not contain the origin.
Thus, uniformly on
\mathcal{V}
, we have

\psi_{n}(x,y,z)\sim\frac{\psi(x,y,z)}{(2\pi n)^{2}}\quad\text{where}\quad\psi(x,y,z):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{x(x+z)y(y+z)}}.

We apply the following classical lemma
(Laplace method) to extract the asymptotics.

Lemma 1

(Laplace Method)
.

Consider a compact set
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathds{R}^{d}
and the series

I_{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{k}\in\mathds{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}\\
\bm{k}/n\in\mathcal{C}\end{subarray}}\psi(\bm{k}/n)e^{-n\phi(\bm{k}/n)}

where
\psi,\phi
are differentiable functions from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathds{R}
.
Assume furthermore that

\phi
has a unique global minimum
\bm{\theta}^{*}

which is located in the interior of
\mathcal{C}
,

\phi
is three-times differentiable in a neighborhood of
\bm{\theta}^{*}
,

\operatorname{Hessian}_{\phi}(\bm{\theta}^{*})>0
and
\psi(\bm{\theta}^{*})\neq 0
.
Then

I_{n}\sim(2\pi n)^{d/2}\psi(\bm{\theta}^{*})\frac{e^{-n\phi(\bm{\theta}^{*})}}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Hessian}_{\phi}(\bm{\theta}^{*})}}.

Proof.

There are many variants of this classic result.
The one-dimensional case (
d=1
) is treated by
Masoero 2015
.
There, the approximation of the sum by a Riemann integral is justified.
The same transformation applies to the multivariate case.
The asymptotics of the triple integral is then obtained
by a multivariate Laplace method, see
e.g.

Pemantle and Wilson 2013
.
∎

Denoting
x^{*},y^{*},z^{*}
the minimal point of
\phi_{u}
, we conclude that the contribution from
\mathcal{V}
to the sum
G_{n}(u)
has an asymptotics of the form
e^{-n\phi(x^{\star},y^{\star},z^{\star})}

multiplied by a polynomial term.
Because
\phi_{u}
outside of
\mathcal{V}
is larger than

\phi_{u}(x^{\star},y^{\star},z^{\star})+\epsilon

for some positive
\epsilon
,
we conclude that the contribution of the rest of the sum
is exponentially small and therefore negligible in the asymptotics.
(Also: outside of
\mathcal{V}
, we use Stirling bounds to bound
\psi_{n}(x,y,z)
).

In order to get the asymptotics of
G_{n}(u)
, what remains to do is to evaluate
\psi(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*})
,
\phi_{u}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*})
and
\operatorname{Hessian}_{\phi_{u}}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*})
. The minimal point of
\phi_{u}
is characterized by the system

\displaystyle x^{*}(x^{*}+z^{*})

\displaystyle=\tau_{A}\tau_{B},

\displaystyle y^{*}(y^{*}+z^{*})

\displaystyle=\tau_{C}\tau_{D},

\displaystyle(x^{*}+z^{*})(y^{*}+z^{*})

\displaystyle=\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u.

The solution is given by:

\displaystyle x^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{B}}{\sqrt{u}}\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{A}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{D}}{\sqrt{u}}}{\tau_{C}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{B}}{\sqrt{u}}}},

\displaystyle y^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{D}}{\sqrt{u}}\sqrt{\frac{\tau_{C}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{B}}{\sqrt{u}}}{\tau_{A}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{D}}{\sqrt{u}}}},

\displaystyle z^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u-\frac{\tau_{B}\tau_{D}}{u}}{\sqrt{\left(\tau_{A}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{D}}{\sqrt{u}}\right)\left(\tau_{C}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{B}}{\sqrt{u}}\right)}}.

We have
x^{*}>0
and
y^{*}>0
. Moreover, the assumption
\tau_{A}\tau_{C}\geq\tau_{B}\tau_{D}
ensures that for
u
close enough to 1,
z^{*}>\max(-x^{*},-y^{*})
. As a consequence,
(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*})
is on the interior of the integration zone defining
G_{n}(u)
, which validates the approximation by a Riemann integral mentioned above.

Simple algebra leads to:

\displaystyle\psi(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau_{A}\tau_{B}\tau_{C}\tau_{D}}},

\displaystyle\phi_{u}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*})

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{\left(\tau_{A}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{D}}{\sqrt{u}}\right)\left(\tau_{C}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{B}}{\sqrt{u}}\right)},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hessian}_{\phi_{u}}(x^{*},y^{*},z^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\tau_{A}\tau_{B}\tau_{C}\tau_{D}}\sqrt{\left(\tau_{A}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{D}}{\sqrt{u}}\right)\left(\tau_{C}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{B}}{\sqrt{u}}\right)}.

Applying Lemma
1
, we then have:

G_{n}(u)\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi n}}\gamma(u)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\exp\left(2n\sqrt{\gamma(u)}\right),

where

\gamma(u)=\left(\tau_{A}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{D}}{\sqrt{u}}\right)\left(\tau_{C}\sqrt{u}+\frac{\tau_{B}}{\sqrt{u}}\right).

To obtain the convergence in distribution to a Gaussian law,
we will apply the
Quasi-powers Theorem
, due to
Hwang 1998
,
which proof is also given by
Flajolet and Sedgewick 2009
(Lemma IX.1)
(we use a slightly weaker version because we are not interested into the speed of convergence).

Lemma 2

(Quasi-powers)
.

Assume that the Laplace transform
\mathds{E}(e^{sX_{n}})

of a sequence of random variables
X_{n}

is analytic in a neighborhood of
0
,
and has an asymptotics of the form

\mathds{E}(e^{sX_{n}})\underset{n\to+\infty}{\sim}e^{\beta_{n}f(s)+g(s)},

with
\beta_{n}\to+\infty
as
n\to+\infty
,
and
f(s)
,
g(s)
analytic on a neighborhood of
0
.
Assume also the condition
f^{\prime\prime}(0)\neq 0
.
Under these assumptions, the mean and variance of
X_{n}
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathds{E}(X_{n})

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}f^{\prime}(0)+g^{\prime}(0)+o(1),

\displaystyle\mathds{V}(X_{n})

\displaystyle=\beta_{n}f^{\prime\prime}(0)+g^{\prime\prime}(0)+o(1),

and the distribution of
(X_{n}-\beta_{n}f^{\prime}(0))/\sqrt{\beta_{n}f^{\prime\prime}(0)}

is asymptotically Gaussian.

We apply Lemma
2
to
X_{n}=(A_{n}-B_{n}\ |\ A_{n}-B_{n}=C_{n}-D_{n})
. Using the asymptotics of
G_{n}
, we have:

\mathds{E}(e^{sX_{n}})=\frac{F_{n}(e^{s})}{F_{n}(1)}\underset{n\to+\infty}{\sim}\exp\left[2n\left(\sqrt{\gamma(e^{s})}-\sqrt{\gamma(1)}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\left(\log(\gamma(e^{s}))-\log(\gamma(1))\right)\right].

The result of Proposition
1

is then obtained by application of Lemma
2

with

\displaystyle\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=n,

\displaystyle f(s)

\displaystyle=2\left(\sqrt{\gamma(e^{s})}-\sqrt{\gamma(1)}\right),

\displaystyle g(s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\left(\log(\gamma(e^{s}))-\log(\gamma(1))\right).

The assumptions
\tau_{A}+\tau_{B}>0
,
\tau_{B}+\tau_{C}>0
,
\tau_{C}+\tau_{D}>0
and
\tau_{D}+\tau_{A}>0
ensure that

f^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{(\tau_{A}\tau_{C}+2\tau_{A}\tau_{B}+\tau_{B}\tau_{D})(\tau_{A}\tau_{C}+2\tau_{C}\tau_{D}+\tau_{B}\tau_{D})}{2\big[(\tau_{A}+\tau_{D})(\tau_{B}+\tau_{C})\big]^{\frac{3}{2}}}

is positive.

2
Degenerate Case: Some Coefficients Are Zero

We now consider the case where one or several coefficients
\tau
vanish. Considering our assumptions
\tau_{A}+\tau_{B}>0
and
\tau_{B}+\tau_{C}>0
and
\tau_{C}+\tau_{D}>0
and
\tau_{D}+\tau_{A}>0
and
\tau_{A}\tau_{C}\geq\tau_{B}\tau_{D}
, there are only two cases, up to symmetries:

•

\tau_{B}=0
and the other coefficients are positive,

•

\tau_{B}=\tau_{D}=0
and the other coefficients are positive.

In both cases, the proof is based on the same principle as in the first section. The main difference is that the triple sum is replaced by a double sum in the first case, and by a simple sum in the second case.

2.1
\tau_{B}=0
and the other coefficients are positive

The probability generating function becomes

P_{n}(u)=\sum_{m\in\mathds{Z}}\mathds{P}(A_{n}=m\mid A_{n}=C_{n}-D_{n})u^{m}.

Introducing the function

F_{n}(u)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,c,d\geq 0\\
a=c-d\end{subarray}}\mathds{P}(A_{n}=a)\mathds{P}(C_{n}=c)\mathds{P}(D_{n}=d)u^{a},

we obtain

P_{n}(u)=\frac{F_{n}(u)}{F_{n}(1)}.

Expressing the probabilities explicitly, the expression becomes

F_{n}(u)=e^{-n(\tau_{A}+\tau_{C}+\tau_{D})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,c,d\geq 0\\
a=c-d\end{subarray}}\frac{(n\tau_{A})^{a}}{a!}\frac{(n\tau_{C})^{c}}{c!}\frac{(n\tau_{D})^{d}}{d!}u^{a}

and we obtain

F_{n}(u)=e^{-n(\tau_{A}+\tau_{C}+\tau_{D})}G_{n}(u),

where

G_{n}(u)=\sum_{a,d\geq 0}\frac{(n\tau_{A})^{a}}{a!}\frac{(n\tau_{C})^{a+d}}{(a+d)!}\frac{(n\tau_{D})^{d}}{d!}u^{a}.

The Stirling approximation is introduced and we define the values

x=\frac{a}{n}
and
y=\frac{d}{n}
. Then:

G_{n}(u)=\sum_{a,d\geq 0}\psi_{n}(x,y)e^{-n\phi_{u}(x,y)},

where

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x,y)=\

\displaystyle\frac{(nx)^{nx}e^{-nx}}{(nx)!}\frac{(n(x+y))^{n(x+y)}e^{-n(x+y)}}{(n(x+y))!}\frac{(ny)^{ny}e^{-ny}}{(ny)!},

\displaystyle\phi_{u}(x,y)=\

\displaystyle x\log(x)-x(1+\log(\tau_{A}))+(x+y)\log(x+y)-(x+y)(1+\log(\tau_{C}))

\displaystyle+y\log(y)-y(1+\log(\tau_{D}))-x\log(u).

The minimum of
\phi_{u}
is obtained for:

\displaystyle x^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u}{\sqrt{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u+\tau_{C}\tau_{D}}},

\displaystyle y^{*}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau_{C}\tau_{D}}{\sqrt{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u+\tau_{C}\tau_{D}}}.

This leads to:

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x^{*},y^{*})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{(2\pi n)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{(\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u+\tau_{C}\tau_{D})^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\sqrt{\tau_{A}{\tau_{C}}^{2}\tau_{D}u}},

\displaystyle\phi_{u}(x^{*},y^{*})

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u+\tau_{C}\tau_{D}},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hessian}_{\phi_{u}}(x^{*},y^{*})

\displaystyle=2\frac{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u+\tau_{C}\tau_{D}}{\tau_{A}{\tau_{C}}^{2}\tau_{D}u}.

Applying the same reasonning as in the previous section
and Lemma
1
, we obtain

G_{n}(u)\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi n}}\gamma(u)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\exp\left(2n\sqrt{\gamma(u)}\right),

where
\gamma(u)
has the same expression as in Section
1
, applied to the particular case
\tau_{B}=0
. From this point, the end of the proof is the same as in Section
1
.

2.2
\tau_{B}=\tau_{D}=0
and the other coefficients are positive

In this case, we have

F_{n}(u)=e^{-n(\tau_{A}+\tau_{C})}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,c\geq 0\\
a=c\end{subarray}}\frac{(n\tau_{A})^{a}}{a!}\frac{(n\tau_{C})^{c}}{c!}u^{a},

which leads to a simple sum (instead of a double or triple sum):

G_{n}(u)=\sum_{a\geq 0}\frac{(n\tau_{A})^{a}}{a!}\frac{(n\tau_{C})^{a}}{a!}u^{a}.

As usual, we define the value
x=\frac{a}{n}
and obtain

G_{n}(u)=\sum_{a\geq 0}\psi_{n}(x)e^{-n\phi_{u}(x)},

where

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x,y)=\

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(nx)^{nx}e^{-nx}}{(nx)!}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\phi_{u}(x,y)=\

\displaystyle x\big(2\log(x)-2-\log(\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u)\big).

The minimum of
\phi_{u}
is obtained for
x^{*}=\sqrt{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u}
, which leads to:

\displaystyle\psi_{n}(x^{*})

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{2\pi n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u}},

\displaystyle\phi_{u}(x^{*})

\displaystyle=-2\sqrt{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u},

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hessian}_{\phi_{u}}(x^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\tau_{A}\tau_{C}u}}.

Applying Lemma
1
, we obtain

G_{n}(u)\sim\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi n}}\gamma(u)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\exp\left(2n\sqrt{\gamma(u)}\right),

where
\gamma(u)
has the same expression as in Section
1
, applied to the particular case
\tau_{B}=\tau_{D}=0
. We then conclude like in Section
1
.

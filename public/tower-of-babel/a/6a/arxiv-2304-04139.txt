1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the generalized energy-critical Hartree equations in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}

(NLH)

-{\Delta u}\left(x\right)-{\bm{\alpha}}\left(N,\lambda\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{u^{p}\left(y\right)}{\left|\,x-y\,\right|^{\lambda}}}\mathrm{d}y\,u^{p-1}\left(x\right)=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

where
\displaystyle u
is a real-valued function,
\displaystyle N\geqslant 3
,
\displaystyle 0<\lambda<N
,
\displaystyle p=\frac{2N-\lambda}{N-2}
and

(1.1)

{\bm{\alpha}}\left(N,\lambda\right)=\frac{N\left(N-2\right)\Gamma\left(N-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)}{\pi^{\frac{N}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{N-\lambda}{2}\right)}.

The equation (
NLH
) is left invariant by the scaling transform

u(x)\longmapsto u_{\delta}(x)=\delta^{\frac{N-2}{2}}u(\delta x),

which preserves the
\displaystyle\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm. That is the reason why the equation (
NLH
) is called the energy-critical equation.

The equation (
NLH
),
which is also called nonlinear Choquard or Choquard-Pekar equation,
has several physical motivations.
In the subcritical case
\displaystyle N=3,

\displaystyle p=2
and
\displaystyle\lambda=1
, the equation (
NLH
)
firstly appeared
in the context of Fröhlich and Pekar’s polaron model,
which describes the interaction between one single electron and the dielectric polarisable continuum, see
[
22
,
23
,
1
,
46
]
.
Later, Choquard proposed the equation (
NLH
) in order to describe
an approximation to the Hartree-Fock theory of a plasma, and then attracted the substantial attention in the field of nonlinear elliptic equations, see
[
37
,
40
,
43
]
. The equation (
NLH
) also arises as a model problem in the study of stationary solutions to nonlinear Schrödinger equation with nonlocal nonlinearity:

(1.2)

iu_{t}-{\Delta u}-{\bm{\alpha}}\left(N,\lambda\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{\left|u\left(y\right)\right|^{p}}{\left|\,x-y\,\right|^{\lambda}}}\mathrm{d}y\,\left|u\right|^{p-2}u=0,\quad p\geqslant 2.

Physically, the equation
(
1.2
) effectively describes the mean field limit of
quantum many-body systems, see e.g.,
[
25
,
34
,
24
]
, and references therein.

The existence and uniqueness of positive bubble solutions of (
NLH
) has been known for some time, see e.g.,
[
15
,
27
,
31
,
41
,
42
,
43
]
and references therein.
The existence of bubble solutions of (
NLH
)
is closely related to the sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\;\mathrm{C}\left(N,\lambda\right)\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2},

where
\displaystyle N\geqslant 3
,
\displaystyle 0<\lambda<N
and
\displaystyle p=\frac{2N-\lambda}{N-2}
. In fact, by use of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality and the Sobolev inequality in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
Du, Yang and Gao showed that sharp constant

\displaystyle\mathrm{C}\left(N,\lambda\right)
in (
1.3
) is obtained if and only if

(1.4)

f\left(x\right)=c\cdot\left(\delta^{2}+\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}

for some
\displaystyle c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\penalty\ \delta>0
and
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
in
[
15
,
27
]
, we can also refer to
[
41
]
for the case
\displaystyle p=2
,
\displaystyle\lambda=4<N
. The existence of the extremizer for sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (
1.3
) is more subtle than the fact that the inequality (
1.3
) holds. The rearrangement inequalities, the conformal transform and the stereographic projection are useful arguements to show the existence of the extremizer of (
1.3
), see
[
38
,
39
]
.

In this paper, without use of the rearrangement inequalities, we will firstly give an alternative proof of the existence of
extremizer for sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality (
1.3
) with
\displaystyle p=\frac{2N-\lambda}{N-2}\geqslant 2
by the stereographic projection and sharp Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality on the sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{N}
in
[
20
]
. That is,

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\displaystyle N\geqslant 3
,

\displaystyle 0<\lambda<N
,
and
\displaystyle p=\frac{2N-\lambda}{N-2}\geqslant 2
.
Then for any
\displaystyle f,g\in\dot{H}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, the inequality

(1.5)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{\left|f\left(x\right)\right|^{p}\left|g\left(y\right)\right|^{p}}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leqslant\mathrm{C}\left(N,\lambda\right)\left\|\nabla f\right\|_{2}\left\|\nabla g\right\|_{2}

holds with sharp constant

(1.6)

\mathrm{C}\left(N,\lambda\right)=\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(N\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\left(4\pi\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\right)\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{N-\lambda}{2}\right)}{\Gamma\left({N}\right)\Gamma\left(N-\frac{\lambda}{2}\right)}\left(4\pi\right)^{N}\right)^{\frac{N-2}{2N-\lambda}}.

Moreover, the equality in (
1.5
) holds if and only if

f\left(x\right)=c\cdot\left(\delta^{2}+\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N-2}{2}},\quad\text{and}\quad g\left(x\right)=c^{\prime}\cdot\left(\delta^{2}+\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N-2}{2}},

where
\displaystyle c,c^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
,
\displaystyle\delta>0
, and
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Remark 1.2
.

In fact, the estimate (
1.5
) holds for any
\displaystyle p\geqslant 1
. The restriction condition that
\displaystyle p\geqslant 2
comes from an algebraic inequality used in Lemma
3.1
.

As for the rigidity classification of the positive solution to (
NLH
) in
\displaystyle L^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, Du, Yang
[
15
]
and Guo, Hu, Peng, Shuai
[
31
]
independently showed that any solutions to (

) with constant sign must be the form of (

) by use of the Kelvin transform and the moving plane method (see also,
[

,

,
41
]
). More precisely, we have

Proposition 1.3

(
[
15
,
31
,
41
]
)
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle N\geqslant 3
,
\displaystyle 0<\lambda<N
and
\displaystyle p=\frac{2N-\lambda}{N-2}
. Let
\displaystyle u
be a solution of (
NLH
) with constant sign, then there exist
\displaystyle c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\penalty\ \delta>0
and
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

such that
\displaystyle u\left(x\right)=U_{c,\delta,x_{0}}\left(x\right)
, where

(1.7)

U_{c,\delta,x_{0}}\left(x\right)=c\cdot\left(\delta^{2}+\left|x-x_{0}\right|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}.

Motivated by the nondegeneracy results of eigenfunctions and ground state in
[
52
,
53
]
, a natural question arises
in the study of bubble solutions to (
NLH
) is that

Are the positive solutions of
(
NLH
) non-degenerate?

Since the equation (
NLH
) is invariant
under the scaling and space translations, i. e.,
any solution
\displaystyle{v}
solves (
NLH
) if and only if
\displaystyle v_{\delta,x_{0}}\left(x\right)=\delta^{\frac{N-2}{2}}v\left(\delta x+x_{0}\right)
satisfies

(NLH*)

-{\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}v_{\delta,x_{0}}}\left(x\right)-{\bm{\alpha}}\left(N,\lambda\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{v_{\delta,x_{0}}^{p}\left(y\right)}{\left|\,x-y\,\right|^{\lambda}}}\mathrm{d}y\,v_{\delta,x_{0}}^{p-1}\left(x\right)=0

for any
\displaystyle\delta>0
and
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Hence, for simplicity, it suffices to consider the solution in (
1.7
) with
the special parameters
\displaystyle c=1
,
\displaystyle\delta=1
and
\displaystyle x_{0}=0,
i.e.

(1.8)

u\left(x\right):=U_{1,1,0}\left(x\right)=\left(1+\left|x\right|^{2}\right)^{-\frac{N-2}{2}}.

That is the reason why we choose the normalized constant
\displaystyle{\bm{\alpha}}\left(N,\lambda\right)
in (
NLH
) and (
NLH*
). By differentiating (
NLH*
) with respect to

\displaystyle\delta
and
\displaystyle x_{0}
at
\displaystyle\left(\delta,x_{0}\right)=\left(1,0\right)
, we know that
the functions

(1.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varphi_{j}\left(x\right):=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\left(x\right)=\left(2-N\right)u\left(x\right)\frac{x_{j}}{1+\left|x\right|^{2}},\;\;\penalty\ 1\leqslant j\leqslant N,

and

(1.10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\varphi_{N+1}\left(x\right):=\frac{N-2}{2}u\left(x\right)+x\cdot\nabla u\left(x\right)=\frac{N-2}{2}u\left(x\right)\frac{1-\left|x\right|^{2}}{1+\left|x\right|^{2}},

are
\displaystyle N+1
linear independent bounded solutions with vanishing at infinity
to the linearized equation

(1.11)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-{\mathop{}\!\mathbin{\bigtriangleup}\varphi}={\bm{\alpha}}\left(N,\lambda\right)\left[{p\left(\frac{1}{\left|\,\cdot\,\right|^{\lambda}}\ast u^{p-1}\varphi\right)u^{p-1}+\left(p-1\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|\,\cdot\,\right|^{\lambda}}\ast u^{p}\right)u^{p-2}\varphi}\right].

Definition 1.4
.

The solution
\displaystyle u
defined by (
1.8
) to the equation (
NLH
) is said to be non-degenerate
if any bounded solution vanishing at infinity to the linearized equation (
1.11
) must be the linear combinations of
the functions
\displaystyle\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{N}
and
\displaystyle\varphi_{N+1}
defined by (
1.9
) and (
1.10
).

We can state the main result in this paper.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\displaystyle N\geqslant 3
,
\displaystyle\lambda\in\left(0,N\right)
and
\displaystyle p=\frac{2N-\lambda}{N-2}
. Then the nontrivial solution
\displaystyle U_{c,\delta,x_{0}}
of (
NLH
) with constant sign is non-degenerate. More precisely, any bounded solution with vanishing at infinity to the linearized equation (
1.11
) must be the linear combinations of
the functions
\displaystyle\varphi_{1},\cdots,\varphi_{N}
and
\displaystyle\varphi_{N+1}
defined by (
1.9
) and (
1.10
).

The result in Theorem
1.5
generalizes the nondegeneracy results in
[
15
,
28
,
35
,
41
]
to the full rang
\displaystyle\lambda\in(0,N)
, and confirms an open nondegeneracy problem in
[
26
]
. In fact, we firstly obtain an integral form in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{N}
of the corresponding linearized equation around the positive bubble solutions under suitable decay condition by the Green function, and its equivalent integral form on the sphere
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{N}
via the stereographic projection. The key observation is that the weighted pushforward map
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{*}
related to the stereographic projection
\displaystyle\mathcal{S}
is one-to-one maps between the null space of the linearized operator and the spherical harmonic function subspace
\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}^{N+1}
of order one. Lastly, together with the key spherical harmonic decomposition and the Funk-Hecke formula in
[

,
9
,
51
]
, we can obtain the nondegeneracy of positive bubble solutions for generalized energy-critical Hartree equation (NLH). The use of the stereographic projection and the Funk-Hecke formula is inspired by Frank and Lieb in
[

,
20
]
, and the argument here is different with those in
[

,
15
]
and
[

,

,

.

Appendix A
Some useful estimates

In this appendix, we collect several useful estimates.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\displaystyle\lambda\in\left(0,N\right)
and
\displaystyle\theta+\lambda>N
. Then

(A.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}}\frac{1}{\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\begin{cases}\langle x\rangle^{N-\lambda-\theta},&\quad\text{if }\quad\theta<N,\\
\langle x\rangle^{-\lambda}\left(1+{\log}{\langle x\rangle}\right),&\quad\text{if }\quad\theta=N,\\
\langle x\rangle^{-\lambda},&\quad\text{if }\quad\theta>N.\end{cases}

Proof.

We will divide the proof of
Appendix

A
into three cases.

Case 1:
\displaystyle\bm{x=0}
.

By a direct computation,
using the facts that
\displaystyle N>\lambda
and
\displaystyle N<\theta+\lambda
,
we obtain that,

(A.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\left|y\right|^{\lambda}}\frac{1}{\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim 1.

Case 2:
\displaystyle\bm{x\in\mathrm{B}\left(0,1\right)\setminus\left\{0\right\}}
.

On the one hand, for
\displaystyle y\in\mathrm{B}\left(x,2\left|x\right|\right)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle y\rangle\approx 1
, which together with
\displaystyle\left|x\right|<1
and
\displaystyle N>\lambda

implies that,

(A.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}\left(x,2\left|x\right|\right)}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{0}^{2}r^{N-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}r\lesssim 1.

On the other hand, for
\displaystyle y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(x,2\left|x\right|\right)
,
we have
\displaystyle\left|y\right|\approx\left|x-y\right|
,
which, together with
\displaystyle N<\lambda+\theta
,implies that,

(A.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(x,2\left|x\right|\right)}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(0,2\left|x\right|\right)}\frac{1}{\left|y\right|^{\lambda}\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{N-\lambda-\theta}.

By combining (
A.3
) and (
A.4
),
we obtain that,

(A.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim 1,\quad\text{for any}\quad x\in\mathrm{B}\left(0,1\right)\setminus\left\{0\right\}.

Case 3:
\displaystyle\bm{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(0,1\right)}
.

Firstly, noticing that for any

\displaystyle y\in\mathrm{B}\left(x,\frac{\left|x\right|}{2}\right)
,
we have
\displaystyle\langle y\rangle\approx\langle x\rangle
.
Hence,

(A.6)

\int_{\mathrm{B}\left(x,\frac{\left|x\right|}{2}\right)}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{\theta}}\int_{0}^{\frac{\left|x\right|}{2}}r^{N-\lambda-1}\mathrm{d}r\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{N-\lambda-\theta},

where we used that
\displaystyle N>\lambda
.

Secondly, for any

\displaystyle y\in\mathrm{B}\left(x,2{\left|x\right|}\right)\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(x,\frac{\left|x\right|}{2}\right)
,
we have

\left|y\right|\leqslant 3\left|x\right|,\quad\text{and}\quad\left|x-y\right|\approx\langle x\rangle,

which implies that,

(A.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{B}\left(x,2{\left|x\right|}\right)\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(x,\frac{\left|x\right|}{2}\right)}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\frac{1}{\langle x\rangle^{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{3\left|x\right|}\frac{r^{N-1}}{\langle r\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}r\lesssim\begin{cases}\langle x\rangle^{N-\lambda-\theta},&\text{if}\;\theta<N,\\
\langle x\rangle^{-\lambda}\left(1+{\log}{\langle x\rangle}\right),&\text{if}\;\theta=N,\\
\langle x\rangle^{-\lambda},&\text{if}\;\theta>N.\end{cases}

Thirdly, for any
\displaystyle y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(x,2{\left|x\right|}\right)
,
we have
\displaystyle\langle y\rangle\approx\left|y-x\right|
, which implies that,

(A.8)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(x,2{\left|x\right|}\right)}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(x,2{\left|x\right|}\right)}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda+\theta}\mathrm{d}y}\lesssim\left|x\right|^{N-\lambda-\theta}\approx\langle x\rangle^{N-\lambda-\theta},

where we used that
\displaystyle N<\lambda+\theta
.

Finally, using (
A.6
), (
A.7
) and
(
A.8
), we obtain that,
for any
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\mathrm{B}\left(0,1\right)
,

(A.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{1}{\left|x-y\right|^{\lambda}\langle y\rangle^{\theta}}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\begin{cases}\langle x\rangle^{N-\lambda-\theta},&\text{if }\theta<N,\\
\langle x\rangle^{-\lambda}\left(1+{\log}{\langle x\rangle}\right),&\text{if }\theta=N,\\

By combining (

) with (

),

) holds.

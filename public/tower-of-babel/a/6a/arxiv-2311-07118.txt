Abstract
. In this work, a boundary control problem for the following generalized Burgers-Huxley (GBH) equation: u t = ν ​ u x ​ x − α ​ u δ ​ u x + β ​ u ​ ( 1 − u δ ) ​ ( u δ − γ ) , u_{t}=\nu u_{xx}-\alpha u^{\delta}u_{x}+\beta u(1-u^{\delta})(u^{\delta}-\gamma), where ν , α , β > 0 , \nu,\alpha,\beta>0, 1 ≤ δ < ∞ 1\leq\delta<\infty , γ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \gamma\in(0,1) subject to Neumann boundary conditions is analyzed. Using the Minty-Browder theorem, standard elliptic partial differential equations theory, the maximum principle and the Crandall-Liggett theorem, we first address the global existence of a unique strong solution to GBH equation. Then, for the boundary control problem, we prove that the controlled GBH equation (that is, the closed loop system) is exponentially stable in the H 1 \mathrm{H}^{1} -norm (hence pointwise) when the viscosity ν \nu is known (non-adaptive control). Moreover, we show that a damped version of GBH equation is globally asymptotically stable (in the L 2 \mathrm{L}^{2} -norm), when ν \nu is unknown (adaptive control). Using the Chebychev collocation method with the backward Euler method as a temporal scheme, numerical findings are reported for both the non-adaptive and adaptive situations, supporting and confirming the analytical results of both the controlled and uncontrolled systems.

1.1.
The model

The
generalized Burgers-Huxley (GBH) equation
describes a prototype model for describing the interaction between reaction mechanisms, convection effects and diffusion transports. For the boundary control problem (stabilization), we investigate the following GBH (reaction-diffusion-convection) equation: for
x\in(0,1),\ t>0
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle u_{t}(x,t)=\nu u_{xx}(x,t)-\alpha u^{\delta}(x,t)u_{x}(x,t)+\beta u(x,t)(1-u^{\delta}(x,t))(u^{\delta}(x,t)-\gamma),

with
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),
using the following nonlinear Neumann boundary control:

(1.2)

\displaystyle u_{x}(0,t)=w_{1}(t)\ \text{ and }\ u_{x}(1,t)=w_{2}(t).

In (
1.1
),
\alpha>0
is the advection coefficient,
\nu,\beta>0,

1\leq\delta<\infty

\gamma\in(0,1)
are parameters. The model (
1.1
) with
\alpha=\delta=1
and
\beta=0
is the classical Burgers equation and the case
\delta=1
is known as the Burgers-Huxley equation. The global solvability (the existence and uniqueness of global weak as well as strong solution) of the GBH equation (
1.1
) with homogeneous Dirichlet’s boundary condition is established in
[

For the stabilization of the GBH equation (

\nu

Lemma 2.1

(Agmon’s inequality)
.

For any

, the following inequality holds:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\max_{x\in[0,1]}|w(x)|\leq C_{a}\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{1/2}\|w\|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}^{1/2},

where
C_{a}=2^{5/4}
.

Proof.

An application of Hölder’s inequality yields

(2.2)

\displaystyle w^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=w^{2}(0)+2\int_{0}^{x}w(\zeta)w_{x}(\zeta)d\zeta\leq w^{2}(0)+2\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{1}|w(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta}}\right)^{1/2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{1}|w_{x}(\zeta)|^{2}d\zeta}}\right)^{1/2},

for all
x\in[0,1]
. Note that

\displaystyle|w^{2}(0)|

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|w^{2}(x)-2\int_{0}^{x}w(\zeta)w_{x}(\zeta)d\zeta\leq w^{2}(0)}}\right|\leq|w(x)|^{2}+2\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\|w_{x}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}.

Integrating the above inequality from
0
to
1
, we deduce

(2.3)

\displaystyle|w^{2}(0)|\leq\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}(\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+2\|w_{x}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}).

Therefore, from (
2.2
), we infer

(2.4)

\displaystyle\max\limits_{x\in[0,1]}|w(x)|^{2}\leq\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}(\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}+4\|w_{x}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}})\leq 4\sqrt{2}\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\|w\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|w_{x}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}}}\right)^{1/2},

and (
2.1
) follows.
∎

2.2.
Classical solutions

To establish the existence and uniqueness of classical solutions of (

) subject to (
1.2
) given in the feedback form (

), one can proceed in the following way: We can consider the problem (

) as
\mathscr{L}u\equiv u_{t}+\nu u_{xx}+b(u,u_{x})=0
, where

. A simple application of Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle-ub(u,p)

\displaystyle=-\alpha u^{\delta+1}p+\beta(\gamma+1)u^{\delta+2}-\beta\gamma u^{2}-\beta u^{2\delta+2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\beta}{2}u^{2\delta+2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta}|p|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}u^{2\delta+2}+\frac{\beta}{2}(1+\gamma)^{2}u^{2}-\beta\gamma u^{2}-\beta u^{2\delta+2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta}|p|^{2}+\frac{\beta}{2}(1+\gamma^{2})u^{2}.

Therefore the existence of classical solutions follows from
[
30
, Theorem 7.4, Chapter V]
. This Theorem establishes, for a more general quasi-linear parabolic boundary value problem, the existence of a unique solution in the Hö1der space of functions
\mathcal{H}^{2+\ell,1+\ell}([0,1]\times[0,T])
for some
\ell>0
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{2+\ell,1+\ell}([0,1]\times[0,T])\hookrightarrow\mathrm{C}^{2,1}([0,1]\times[0,T])
, we obtain the existence of classical solutions for time intervals
[0,T]
, where
T>0
is arbitrarily large. The proof in
[
30
]
is based on a linearization of the system and an application of the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem.

Let us consider the following Green’s function (cf.
[
5
,
8
]
):

\displaystyle G(x,y,t,s)

\displaystyle=1+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\cos(n\pi x)\cos(n\pi y)e^{-\nu n^{2}\pi^{2}(t-\tau)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi\nu(t-\tau)}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(e^{-\frac{(x-y-2n)^{2}}{4\nu(t-\tau)}}+e^{-\frac{(x+y-2n)^{2}}{4\nu(t-\tau)}}}}\right),

corresponding to the heat operator

\mathcal{L}u\equiv u_{t}-\nu u_{xx},\ 0\leq x\leq 1,\ t>0,

with Neumann boundary conditions

u_{x}(0,t)=g_{0}(u(0,t)),\ u_{x}(1,t)=g_{1}(u(1,t)),\ t>0.

Let us take

Then, the function

is a classical solution of (

)-(

) with continuous initial data

if and only if

\displaystyle u(x,t)=F(u)(x,t)

\displaystyle\equiv\int_{0}^{1}G(x,y,t,0)u_{0}(y)dy+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}G(x,y,t,s)g(u(y,s))dyds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}G(x,1,t,s)g_{1}(u(1,s))ds+\int_{0}^{t}G(x,0,t,s)g_{0}(u(0,s))ds.

The local solvability of the above equation can be established by applying the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem to the iteration

with starting function

. Then by using apriori estimates (cf.
[

]

[
4
]
for the case of viscous Burgers equation). Smooth solutions of the system (

[

. We summarize the discussion as the following theorem:

Theorem 2.11

Consider the system (

It is important to note that a crucial step in the proof is establishing

for the problem (

3.
Boundary Control

In this section, we discuss the exponential stability for the Burgers-Huxley equation (

) subject to (

) for non-adaptive and adaptive control (a damped version) cases, that is, when the viscosity

is known and

The following form of Poincaré’s inequality is used frequently in the paper (see
[
21
, Lemma A.1]
).

Lemma 3.1

(Poincaré’s inequality)
.

For any
u\in\mathrm{C}^{1}([0,1])
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}(x)dx\leq 2u^{2}(0)+2\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{1}u_{x}^{2}(x)dx}}\right).

Proof.

For any
u\in\mathrm{C}^{1}[0,1]
, we know that

\displaystyle u(x)=u(0)+\int_{0}^{x}u_{x}(\zeta)d\zeta,

so that

\displaystyle u^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=u^{2}(0)+2u(0)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{x}u_{x}(\zeta)d\zeta}}\right)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{x}u_{x}(\zeta)d\zeta}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2u^{2}(0)+2\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{x}u_{x}(\zeta)d\zeta}}\right)^{2}\leq 2u^{2}(0)+2\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{1}u_{x}^{2}(x)dx}}\right).

Ingratiating the above inequality from
0
to
1
, we have the Poincaré inequality (
3.1
).
∎

.

(3.2)

Let

\displaystyle V(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}u^{2}(x,t)dx,\ t\geq 0.

\displaystyle\dot{V}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}u(x,t)u_{t}(x,t)dx

(3.4)

\displaystyle\quad+\nu u(1,t)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{w_{2}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\nu(\delta+2)}u^{\delta+1}(1,t)}}\right\},

Remark 3.3
.

One can use the control

\displaystyle u_{t}(x,t)-\nu u_{xx}(x,t)+\alpha u^{\delta}(x,t)u_{x}(x,t)-\beta u(x,t)(1-u^{\delta}(x,t))(u^{\delta}(x,t)-\gamma)=f(x,t),

.

For

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\delta+2}}\leq\|u(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2(\delta+1)}}\leq e^{-\frac{\varrho t}{2(\delta+1)}}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2(\delta+1)}},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{\varrho s}\|u^{\delta}(s)u_{x}^{2}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}ds\leq C_{\nu,\delta}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2(\delta+1)}}^{2(\delta+1)},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{\varrho s}\|u(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2(\delta+p)}}^{2(\delta+p)}ds\leq C_{\beta,\delta}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2(\delta+1)}}^{2(\delta+1)},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}e^{\varrho s}\|u_{x}^{2}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{\varrho s}\|u(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2(\delta+1)}}^{2(\delta+1)}ds\leq C_{\nu,\beta,\delta}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Substituting the above inequalities in (
3.1
), we deduce

(3.31)

\displaystyle\Psi(t)

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\Psi(0)+C_{\varrho,\hat{\eta}_{1},\hat{\eta}_{2},\nu,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2(\delta+1)}}^{\delta+2}+\|u_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2(\delta+1)}}^{2(\delta+1)}}}\right)}}\right\}e^{-\frac{\varrho}{2}t},

for all
t\geq 0
. From the definition of
\Psi
given in (
3.1
) and Sobolev’s inequality, one can immediately deduce (
3.17
).
∎

Remark 3.10
.

As we are not using any Gronwall’s inequality to obtain (
3.1
), we are able to establish global exponential stability in
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-norm. The author in
[
4
]
obtained a semi-global exponential stability only in
\mathrm{H}^{1}
-norm for the viscous Burgers equation.

Therefore an application of Agmon’s inequality (Lemma
2.1
) yields the following result on the pointwise exponential convergence:

Theorem 3.11
.

Consider the problem (
1.1
) with boundary control (
3.13
) for
p=\delta+1
. If
w_{0}\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,1)
and
\nu>\frac{2p}{2p-1}\beta(1+\gamma^{2})
,

(3.32)

\displaystyle\max_{x\in[0,1]}|u(x,t)|\leq C\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\varrho,\hat{\eta}_{1},\hat{\eta}_{2},\nu,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,\delta,\|(u_{0})_{x}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}}}\right)e^{-\frac{\varrho}{2}t},

for all
t\geq 0
, where
\varrho
is defined in (
3.18
).

3.2.
Adaptive control

In this subsection, we consider the adaptive control problem of the following damped gneralized Burgers-Huxley equation subject to (
1.2
) (that is, when
\nu>0
is unknown):

(3.33)

\displaystyle u_{t}(x,t)=\nu u_{xx}(x,t)-\kappa u(x,t)-\alpha u^{\delta}(x,t)u_{x}(x,t)+\beta u(x,t)(1-u^{\delta}(x,t))(u^{\delta}(x,t)-\gamma),

where
0<\kappa\leq\frac{\beta}{4}(1-\gamma)^{2}
is known.

Theorem 3.12
.

The solution
u(\cdot)
of the closed-loop system of the damped GBH equation (
3.33
) subject to (
1.2
) with unknown viscosity
\nu>0
, is asymptotically stable under the following control law:

(3.34)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} w_{1}(t)&=\eta_{1}(t)u^{\delta+1}(0,t)+u(0,t),\\
w_{2}(t)&=\eta_{2}(t)u^{\delta+1}(1,t),\end{aligned}}}\right.

where
\eta_{k}(t)
,
k=1,2,
are bounded for any
t\geq 0
with:

(3.35)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\begin{aligned} \dot{\eta}_{1}(t)&=r_{1}u^{\delta+2}(0,t),\ r_{1}>0,\\
\dot{\eta}_{2}(t)&=-r_{2}u^{\delta+2}(1,t),\ r_{2}>0.\end{aligned}}}\right.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{E}(t)
be a non-negative energy function defined by

(3.36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(t)=V(t)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{2\nu r_{1}}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\nu\eta_{1}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\delta+2}}}\right)^{2}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{2\nu r_{2}}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\nu\eta_{2}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\delta+2}}}\right)^{2},

where
V(t)
is defined in (
3.3
). Taking the time derivative of the energy function
\mathcal{E}(t)
, we get

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)

\displaystyle=\dot{V}(t)+\frac{1}{r_{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\nu\eta_{1}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\delta+2}}}\right)\dot{\eta}_{1}(t)+\frac{1}{r_{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\nu\eta_{2}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\delta+2}}}\right)\dot{\eta}_{2}(t)

\displaystyle\leq-\zeta V(t)-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\nu\eta_{1}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\delta+2}}}\right)u^{\delta+2}(0,t)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\nu\eta_{2}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\delta+2}}}\right)u^{\delta+2}(1,t)

(3.37)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{r_{1}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\nu\eta_{1}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\delta+2}}}\right)\dot{\eta}_{1}(t)+\frac{1}{r_{2}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\nu\eta_{2}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\delta+2}}}\right)\dot{\eta}_{2}(t),

where we have used (
3.1
) with
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\nu}{2}+\beta\gamma-\frac{\beta(1+\gamma)^{2}}{4\theta}}}\right)
replaced by
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\nu}{2}+\kappa+\beta\gamma-\frac{\beta(1+\gamma)^{2}}{4\theta}}}\right)
and
\zeta=\frac{\nu}{2}+\kappa+\beta\gamma-\frac{\beta(1+\gamma)^{2}}{4\theta}>0,
since
\kappa\leq\frac{\beta}{4}(1-\gamma)^{2}
. Substituting (
3.35
) in (
3.2
), we obtain

(3.38)

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{E}}(t)\leq-\zeta V(t)\leq 0,

since
\zeta>0
. Therefore, we have
\mathcal{E}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}(0)
, and it is immediate that
\eta_{1}(t)
and
\eta_{2}(t)
are bounded functions for any
t>0
. This also implies
u(0,t),u(1,t)\in\mathrm{L}^{\delta+2}(0,\infty)
.

Let now show the asymptotic stability result. From (
3.2
), we have

(3.39)

\displaystyle\dot{V}(t)\leq-\zeta V(t)-\nu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\eta_{1}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\nu(\delta+2)}}}\right)u^{\delta+2}(0,t)+\nu\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\eta_{2}(t)-\frac{\alpha}{\nu(\delta+2)}}}\right)u^{\delta+2}(1,t).

An application of variation of constants formula yields

\displaystyle V(t)

\displaystyle\leq V(0)e^{-\zeta t}

\displaystyle\quad+\nu\int_{0}^{t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{\alpha}{\nu(\delta+2)}-\eta_{1}(s)}}\right)u^{\delta+2}(0,s)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\eta_{2}(s)-\frac{\alpha}{\nu(\delta+2)}}}\right)u^{\delta+2}(1,s)}}\right\}e^{-\zeta(t-s)}ds

(3.40)

\displaystyle\leq V(0)e^{-\zeta t}+\nu\xi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\zeta(t-s)}|u^{\delta+2}(0,s)|ds+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\zeta(t-s)}|u^{\delta+2}(1,s)|ds}}\right),

where

\xi=\max\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sup_{s\in[0,\infty)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\frac{\alpha}{\nu(\delta+2)}-\eta_{1}(s)}}\right|,\sup_{s\in[0,\infty)}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\eta_{2}(s)-\frac{\alpha}{\nu(\delta+2)}}}\right|}}\right\}.

Exploiting Lemma
3.4
, one can easily obtain the required result.
∎

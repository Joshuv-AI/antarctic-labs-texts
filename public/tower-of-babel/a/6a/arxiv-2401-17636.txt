Kollár’s package for polystable locally abelian parabolic Higgs bundles
Junchao Shentu, Chen Zhao

Abstract
. We generalize Kollár’s package (including torsion freeness, injectivity theorem, vanishing theorem and decomposition theorem) to polystable locally abelian parabolic Higgs bundles twisted by a multiplier ideal sheaf associated with an ℝ \mathbb{R} -divisor. This gives a uniform treatment for various kinds of Kollár’s package in different topics in complex geometry. As applications, the weakly positivity (in the sense of Viehweg) and the generic vanishing property for higher direct image sheaves are deduced.



1.
Introduction

Everything is defined over the complex number field
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
f:X\rightarrow Y
be a proper surjective morphism from a projective variety
X
to a complex space
Y
. We say that a coherent sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
satisfies
Kollár’s package
with respect to
f
if the following statements hold.

Torsion Freeness:

R^{q}f_{\ast}(\mathscr{F})
is torsion free for every
q\geq 0
and vanishes if
q>\dim X-\dim Y
.

Injectivity Theorem:

If
L
is a semi-positive (Definition
3.5
) holomorphic line bundle on
X
so that
L^{\otimes l}
admits a nonzero holomorphic global section
s
for some
l>0
, then the canonical morphism

R^{q}f_{\ast}(\times s):R^{q}f_{\ast}(\mathscr{F}\otimes L^{\otimes k})\to R^{q}f_{\ast}(\mathscr{F}\otimes L^{\otimes(k+l)})

is injective for every
q\geq 0
and every
k\geq 1
.

Vanishing Theorem:

If
Y
is a projective algebraic variety and
L
is an ample line bundle on
Y
, then

H^{q}(Y,R^{p}f_{\ast}(\mathscr{F})\otimes L)=0,\quad\forall q>0,\forall p\geq 0.

Decomposition Theorem:

Rf_{\ast}(\mathscr{F})
splits in
D(Y)
the derived category of
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-modules, that is,

Rf_{\ast}(\mathscr{F})\simeq\bigoplus_{q}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\mathscr{F})[-q]\in D(Y).

As a consequence, the spectral sequence

E^{pq}_{2}:H^{p}(Y,R^{q}f_{\ast}(\mathscr{F}))\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(X,\mathscr{F})

degenerates at the
E_{2}
page.

These statements date back to J. Kollár
[
13
,
14
]
, who proved that the dualizing sheaf
\omega_{X}
satisfies Kollár’s package when
X
is smooth and
Y
is projective. Kollár’s results have since been generalized in two directions to aim for various geometric applications.

The first direction is Kollár’s package for the dualizing sheaf twisted by a
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor, or more generally, a multiplier ideal sheaf.
This particular type of Kollár’s package has significant applications in various areas of research. For example, E. Viehweg’s work on the quasi-projective moduli of polarized manifolds
[
37
,
38
]
, O. Fujino’s project on the minimal model program for log-canonical varieties
[
7
]
, and the Kollár-Kovács’ splitting criterion for du Bois singularities
[
12
]
all make use of this type of Kollár’s package. K. Takegoshi
[
35
]
has also established a proof of Kollár’s package for the dualizing sheaf twisted by a Nakano semi-positive vector bundle. In addition, S. Matsumura
[
15
]
and Fujino-Matsumura
[
6
]
have investigated the injectivity theorem for the dualizing sheaf twisted by a general multiplier ideal sheaf. Recently, Cao-Păun
[
3
,
2
]
extended Kollár’s injectivity theorem to the log canonical bundle twisted by a semi-positive line bundle, thus confirming a conjecture proposed by Fujino
[
7
]
. Subsequently, Chan-Choi-Matsumura
[
4
]
provided another proof and generalized Fujino’s conjecture to the scenario of log canonical pairs (also refer to
[
16
,
15
]
). However, a complete proof of Kollár’s package, especially the decomposition theorem, for the dualizing sheaf twisted by a multiplier ideal sheaf remains elusive.

The other direction is to generalize Kollár’s package to certain Hodge-theoretic objects, such as variations of Hodge structure and Hodge modules. Let’s assume that
f:X\to Y
is a morphism between projective varieties.
Suppose that
\mathbb{V}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-polarized variation of Hodge structure on some dense Zariski open subset
X^{o}\subset X_{\text{reg}}
of the regular locus
X_{\text{reg}}
. In his work, M. Saito
[
29
]
constructs a coherent sheaf
S_{X}(\mathbb{V})
(as the lowest Hodge piece of the Hodge module
IC_{X}(\mathbb{V})
) and shows that
S_{X}(\mathbb{V})
satisfies Kollár’s package with respect to
f
. When
\mathbb{V}
is the trivial variation of Hodge structure,
S_{X}(\mathbb{V})\simeq\omega_{X}
. Saito’s work provides an affirmative answer to Kollár’s conjecture
[
14
, §4]
. In addition to other deep results on Hodge modules, Kollár’s package for
S_{X}(\mathbb{V})
proves to be instrumental in the series of works by Popa-Schnell
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
.
Recently, the authors of this article give an
L^{2}
-theoretic proof to Saito’s result in
[
32
]
.

The purpose of the present article is to demonstrate that Kollár’s package holds for specific subsheaves of a polystable parabolic Higgs bundle, which is twisted by a multiplier ideal sheaf associated with an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor.
This approach provides a unified and systematic treatment for various versions of Kollár’s package. Notably, even when considering the dualizing sheaf twisted by a multiplier ideal sheaf, this package yields novel results. The main arguments rely on the
L^{2}
-theoretic method developed by Andreotti-Vesentini
[
1
]
and Hörmander
[
9
]
, as well as the non-abelian Hodge theory developed by Simpson
[
34
]
and Mochizuki
[
19
,
20
]
.

1.1.
Main results

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and
D
be a simple normal crossing divisor on
X
. Consider a locally abelian parabolic Higgs bundle
(H,\{{{}_{E}}H\}_{E\in{\rm Div}_{D}(X)},\theta)
on
(X,D)

•

A locally abelian parabolic vector bundle
(H,\{{{}_{E}}H\}_{E\in{\rm Div}_{D}(X)})
with parabolic structures on

\{{{}_{E}}H\}
is indexed by the set
{\rm Div}_{D}(X)

\mathbb{R}
-divisors whose supports lie in

•

A Higgs field
\theta:H|_{X\backslash D}\to H|_{X\backslash D}\otimes\Omega_{X\backslash D}
which has regular singularities along
D
, meaning that
\theta({{}_{E}}H)\subset{{}_{E}}H\otimes\Omega_{X}(\log D)
for every
E\in{\rm Div}_{D}(X)
.

This parabolic Higgs bundle is required to have vanishing first and second parabolic Chern classes and to be polystable with respect to an ample line bundle
A
on
X
. Readers may refer to §
2
for the detailed notations regarding parabolic Higgs bundles.

The main object of study is a specific extension, denoted as
P_{E,(2)}(H)
, of
H|_{X\backslash D}
. To define this extension, let
E
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor supported on
D
. We denote
\cup_{E^{\prime}<E}{{}_{E^{\prime}}}H
as
{{}_{<E}}H
.
The coherent sheaf
P_{E,(2)}(H)
is determined by the following conditions.

(1)

{{}_{<E}}H\subset P_{E,(2)}(H)\subset{{}_{E}}H
. In particular
P_{E,(2)}(H)|_{X\backslash D}=H|_{X\backslash D}
.

(2)

Take
x
to be a point on
D
, and let
(U;z_{1},\dots,z_{n})
be holomorphic local coordinates on an open neighborhood
U
of
x
in
X
, such that
D=\{z_{1}\cdots z_{r}=0\}
. Let
D_{i}=\{z_{i}=0\}
,
i=1,\dots,r
. Let
N_{i}
be the nilpotent part of the residue map
{\rm Res}_{D_{i}}(\theta)
of the Higgs field along
D_{i}
. For any subset
I\subset\{1,\dots,r\}
, let
\{W(\sum_{i\in I}N_{i})_{m}\}_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}
represent the monodromy weight filtration on
{{}_{E}}H|_{U}
at
x
with respect to
\sum_{i\in I}N_{i}
.
Then we have

P_{E,(2)}(H)={{}_{<E}}H+\sum_{\emptyset\neq I\subset\{1,\dots,r\}}{{}_{\leq_{I}E}}H\cap\bigcap_{J\subset I}W(\sum_{i\in J}N_{i})_{-\#(J)-1}

on
U
(please refer to §
2.4
for the notation
{{}_{\leq_{I}E}}H
).

When

,
P_{D,(2)}(H)
is the sheaf of
L^{2}
-holomorphic sections with coefficients in

(see Proposition
3.13
). Originally introduced by S. Zucker
[
39
]
on algebraic curves, this kind of construction involves Higgs bundles

L^{2}
-cohomology of a variation of Hodge structure. The construction by Zucker has been extended to higher dimensions by Kashiwara and Kawai
[
11

[
17

provided a further generalization of the characterization of the

de Rham complex to
\lambda

-modules). This advancement represents a significant step in establishing the hard Lefschetz theorem for the pushforward of a twistor
\mathscr{D}
-module within the framework of Kähler geometry. It is noteworthy that our construction

17

To generalize Zucker’s construction

According to the non-abelian Hodge theory of Simpson

]

\rho:H|_{X\backslash D}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X\backslash D}}\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{X\backslash D}=V|_{X\backslash D}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X\backslash D}}\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{X\backslash D}\quad(\lx@sectionsign\ref{section_Simpson_Mochizuki}).

In particular, the

has two complex structures. One is the complex structure

of the Higgs bundle

The main result of the present article is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K

H|_{X\backslash D}

•

Holomorphicity:

\nabla^{0,1}(K)=0
, meaning that
K

\bar{\partial}
and
\nabla^{0,1}

•

Weak transversality

Let

Together with Hacon’s criterion (

2.1.

be a proper surjective holomorphic morphism to a complex space. Then

\omega_{X}\otimes(P{{}_{D-N,(2)}}(H)\cap j_{\ast}K)\otimes F\otimes L

Proof.

Based on Theorem

∎

Lemma 4.4

Let us consider the quotient

4.1.3.
Twisted Kollár-Saito’s S-sheaf

In this section, we introduce a construction that combines Kollár-Saito’s
S_{X}(\mathbb{V})
and the multiplier ideal sheaf associated with an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor. Additionally, we generalize Kollár-Saito’s construction to complex variations of Hodge structure, without making assumptions on the local monodromy. This is interesting from the perspective of nonabelian Hodge theory, as complex variations of Hodge structure are precisely the
\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
fixed points on the moduli space of certain tame harmonic bundles (see
[
34
, Theorem 8]
,
[
19
, Proposition 1.9]
).

Let
X
be a projective variety and
X^{o}\subset X_{\rm reg}
be a dense Zariski open subset. Let
\mathbb{V}=(\mathcal{V},\nabla,\{\mathcal{V}^{p,q}\},Q)
be a polarized complex variation of Hodge structure on
X^{o}
. Let
h_{Q}
be the Hodge metric associated with
Q
and let
\left(H_{\mathbb{V}}:=\bigcup_{E\in{\rm Div}_{D}(X)}{{}_{E}}H_{h_{Q}},\{{{}_{E}}H_{h_{Q}}\}_{E\in{\rm Div}_{D}(X)},\theta\right)
be the
\mu_{A}
-polystable locally abelian parabolic Higgs bundle on
(X,D)
associated with
\mathbb{V}
.
Let
N
be an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on
X
. We define the coherent sheaf
S_{X}(\mathbb{V},-N)
as follows.

Log smooth case:

Assuming that
X
is smooth and
D:=X\backslash X^{o}
is a simple normal crossing divisor with
{\rm supp}(N)\subset D
, we denote the irreducible decomposition of
D
as
D=\cup_{i=1}^{l}D_{i}
, and we write
N=\sum_{i=1}^{l}{r_{i}}D_{i}
where
r_{1},\dots,r_{l}\in\mathbb{R}
. For
\bm{r}=(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})
, we let
\mathcal{V}_{>\bm{r}-1}
be the Deligne-Manin prolongation with indices
>\bm{r}-1
. This is a locally free
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module that extends
\mathcal{V}
in such a way that
\nabla
induces a connection with logarithmic singularities given by

\nabla:\mathcal{V}_{>\bm{r}-1}\to\mathcal{V}_{>\bm{r}-1}\otimes\Omega_{X}(\log D),

where the real part of the eigenvalues of the residue of
\nabla
along

belongs to
(r_{i}-1,r_{i}]
for each

We can now define

as

(4.2)

\displaystyle S_{X}(\mathbb{V},-N)

\displaystyle:=\omega_{X}\otimes\left(j_{\ast}S(\mathbb{V})\cap\mathcal{V}_{>\bm{r}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\omega_{X}\otimes\left(j_{\ast}S(\mathbb{V})\cap{{}_{<D-N}}H_{h_{Q}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\omega_{X}\otimes\big(P_{D-N,(2)}(H_{\mathbb{V}})\cap j_{\ast}S(\mathbb{V})\big)\quad\textrm{(Corollary \ref{cor_W_F})}.

Here

is the open immersion.

General case:

Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a resolution of singularities such that
\pi^{o}:=\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(X^{o}\backslash{\rm supp}(N))}:\pi^{-1}(X^{o}\backslash{\rm supp}(N))\to X^{o}\backslash{\rm supp}(N)
is biholomorphic and the exceptional loci

is a simple normal crossing divisor. Then

\displaystyle S_{X}(\mathbb{V},-N)\simeq\pi_{\ast}\left(S_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{o\ast}\mathbb{V},-\pi^{\ast}N)\right).

Proposition 4.6
.

We have
S_{X}(\mathbb{V},-N)\simeq S_{X}(S(\mathbb{V}),e^{-\varphi_{N}}h_{Q})
, where
\varphi_{N}
is a weight function associated with

. In particular,
S_{X}(\mathbb{V},-N)
is a torsion free coherent sheaf on

that is independent of the choice of the desingularization
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X

Proof.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a resolution of singularities such that
\pi^{o}:=\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(X^{o}\backslash{\rm supp}(N))}:\pi^{-1}(X^{o}\backslash{\rm supp}(N))\to X^{o}\backslash{\rm supp}(N)
is biholomorphic and the reduced exceptional loci

is a simple normal crossing divisor. It follows from (
4.2
) that

\displaystyle S_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{o\ast}\mathbb{V},-\pi^{\ast}(N))

\displaystyle\simeq\omega_{\widetilde{X}}\otimes\big(P_{E-\pi^{\ast}N,(2)}(H_{\pi^{o\ast}\mathbb{V}})\cap j_{\ast}S(\pi^{o\ast}\mathbb{V})\big)

\displaystyle\simeq S_{\widetilde{X}}(S(\pi^{o\ast}\mathbb{V}),e^{-\pi^{\ast}(\varphi_{N})}\pi^{o\ast}h_{Q})\quad(\textrm{Proposition \ref{prop_P(2)_L2}}),

where

is an immersion.
Applying

on both sides, we get

\displaystyle S_{X}(\mathbb{V},-N)

\displaystyle\simeq\pi_{\ast}(S_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{o\ast}\mathbb{V},-\pi^{\ast}(N)))

\displaystyle\simeq\pi_{\ast}(S_{\widetilde{X}}(S(\pi^{o\ast}\mathbb{V}),e^{-\pi^{\ast}(\varphi_{N})}\pi^{o\ast}h_{Q}))

\displaystyle\simeq S_{X}(S(\mathbb{V}),e^{-\varphi_{N}}h_{Q})\quad\textrm{(Lemma \ref{lem_L2ext_birational})}.

∎

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper surjective holomorphic morphism from a projective variety

Y
. Let
\mathbb{V}
be a polarized complex variation of Hodge structure on some regular dense Zariski open subset

is a semi-positive
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor and

is an
\mathbb{R}
-Cartier divisor on

be an arbitrary Nakano semi-positive vector bundle on

Proof.

Let
\pi:\widetilde{X}\to X
be a resolution of singularities such that
\pi^{o}:=\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(X^{o}\backslash{\rm supp}(N))}:\pi^{-1}(X^{o}\backslash{\rm supp}(N))\to X^{o}\backslash{\rm supp}(N)

Let

be an immersion. Equation (

\displaystyle\simeq\pi_{*}\left(S_{\widetilde{X}}(\pi^{o*}\mathbb{V},-\pi^{*}N)\otimes\pi^{*}F\otimes\pi^{*}L\right)

Log smooth case:

General case:

is smooth and

where
\mathscr{I}(-N)
is the multiplier ideal sheaf associated with

.
According to Theorem
1.3
, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper surjective holomorphic morphism from a projective variety
X
to a complex space
Y
. Let
L
be a line bundle on

\mathbb{R}

be a proper surjective holomorphic morphism from a smooth projective variety

be the canonical divisor of

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be the ideal sheaf of the base scheme of

From this, we have

.

4.3.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety and

be a torsion free sheaf on

]
) on some Zariski open subset

is generated by global sections at each point of

refers to the reflexive hull of the symmetric power

. We can say that

Notations as in Theorem
1.1

be a point. Let

be the components of
D
that contains
x
. For every
E\in{\rm Div}_{D}(X)
and every
i=1,\dots,r
let
N_{r}\in{\rm End}({{}_{E}}H/{{}_{<E}}H|_{D_{i}})
be the nilpotent part of the residue map
{\rm Res}_{D_{i}}(\theta)
of the Higgs field
\theta
. For every
I\subset\{1,\dots,r\}
, let
W(\sum_{i\in I}N_{i})_{\bullet}
be the monodromy weight filtration associated with
\sum_{i\in I}N_{i}
.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
K\subset H|_{X\backslash D}
be a locally free subsheaf. We say that
K

has non-negative weight at
x
if

(_{E}H\cap j_{\ast}(K))|_{x}\cap W(\sum_{i\in I}N_{i})_{-1}|_{x}=0

for every
E\in{\rm Div}_{D}(X)
and every
I\subset\{1,\dots,r\}
.
K

has non-negative weight along
D
if it has non-negative weight at every point of
D
.

There are two examples of
K
that has non-negative weight along
D
. The first example is presented in Example
1.1.1
, where the weight filtrations are trivial due to the vanishing of the Higgs fields. The other is example
1.1.2
, wherein
H
arises from a polarized complex variation of Hodge structure
\mathbb{V}
on
X\backslash D
, and
K=S(\mathbb{V})
represents the top non-zero Hodge bundle. In this context, Corollary
4.5
demonstrates that
S(\mathbb{V})
has non-negative weight along
D
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Notations as in Theorem
1.1
. Assume that
K
has non-negative weight along
D
. If
f:X\to Y
is a surjective morphism between smooth projective varieties, then
R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/Y}\otimes(P{{}_{D-N,(2)}}(H)\cap j_{\ast}K)\otimes F\otimes L)
is weakly positive.

Proof.

Let us denote the sheaf
(P_{D-N,(2)}(H)\cap j_{\ast}K)\otimes F\otimes L
by
\mathscr{E}
for simplicity. Additionally, let
A_{Y}
be a line bundle on
Y
that is very ample. Our goal is to demonstrate that, for every
m>0
, the sheaf

S^{[m]}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/Y}\otimes\mathscr{E})\otimes\omega_{Y}\otimes A_{Y}^{\dim Y+1}

is generated by global sections over a Zariski open subset of
Y
.

Step 1:

Let
\Delta_{Y}
be the set of critical values of
f
. By performing possibly blowing-ups
\rho:X^{\prime}\to X
, we can assume that
\rho^{\ast}(f^{\ast}\Delta_{Y})\cup\rho^{\ast}(D)
forms a divisor with simple normal crossings. Using Lemma
3.1
and Corollary
3.14
, we have:

\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E}\simeq\rho_{\ast}\left(\omega_{X^{\prime}}\otimes(P_{\rho^{\ast}(D-N),(2)}(\rho^{\ast}H)\cap j^{\prime}_{\ast}(\rho^{\ast}K))\otimes\rho^{\ast}F\otimes\rho^{\ast}L\right)

where
j^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\backslash\rho^{\ast}D\to X^{\prime}
is the open immersion. By applying Theorem
1.1
(torsion freeness) to the morphism
\rho:X^{\prime}\to X
, we can conclude that

\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E}\simeq R\rho_{\ast}\left(\omega_{X^{\prime}}\otimes(P_{\rho^{\ast}(D-N),(2)}(\rho^{\ast}H)\cap j^{\prime}_{\ast}(\rho^{\ast}K))\otimes\rho^{\ast}F\otimes\rho^{\ast}L\right).

Therefore, there exists an isomorphism:

R^{q}(f\circ\rho)_{\ast}\left(\omega_{X^{\prime}}\otimes(P_{\rho^{\ast}(D-N),(2)}(\rho^{\ast}H)\cap j^{\prime}_{\ast}(\rho^{\ast}K))\otimes\rho^{\ast}F\otimes\rho^{\ast}L\right)\simeq R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})

for every
q
. As a result, we can assume that
f^{\ast}(\Delta_{Y})\cup D
forms a divisor with a simple normal crossing on
X
for the rest of the proof. Specifically, we assume that
f
is flat in codimension
1
on
Y
.

Step 2: Viehweg’s trick.

We denote the main component of the
m
-th fiber product
X\times_{Y}\cdots\times_{Y}X
by
X^{[m]}
, and let
f^{[m]}:X^{[m]}\to Y
denote the projection map. Let
D^{[m]}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}^{\ast}(D)
, where
p_{i}:X^{[m]}\to X
is the projection map to the
i
-th component. Consider the largest Zariski open subset
U\subset Y
where
(X,D)
is log smooth over
U
. Then,
f^{[m]}:(X^{[m]},D^{[m]})\to Y
is log smooth over
U
. Let
X^{[m]o}:=(f^{[m]})^{-1}(U)
.
Let
\pi:X^{(m)}\to X^{[m]}
be a resolution of singularities such that
\pi^{\ast}(X^{[m]}\backslash X^{[m]o})
is a simple normal crossing divisor, and
\pi
is an isomorphism on
X^{(m)o}:=\pi^{\ast}(X^{[m]o})
.

According to Corollary
3.15
,
\mathscr{E}
is a locally free
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-module. Let
hh_{F}h_{L}
be the singular Hermitian metric on
\mathscr{E}
as introduced in §
3.3.1
. Given that
K
has non-negative weight along
D
, it follows from Theorem
2.8
that there exists a
C^{\infty}
Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
\mathscr{E}
such that
h_{0}\lesssim hh_{F}h_{L}
.
We define
\mathscr{E}^{[m]}:=\otimes_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}^{\ast}\mathscr{E}
and
\mathscr{E}^{(m)}:=\pi^{\ast}\mathscr{E}^{[m]}
. Let
V\subset Y
be a Zariski open subset such that
R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})
is locally free on
V
for every
q\geq 0
, and
f
is flat over
V
. Since
R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})
is torsion free for each
q\geq 0
(Theorem
1.1
), we can assume that
Y\backslash V
is of codimension
\geq 2
for simplicity.

Since
f:X\to Y
is a Gorenstein morphism, we can conclude that
f^{[m]}:X^{[m]}\to Y
is also a Gorenstein morphism. Moreover, there exists an isomorphism

\displaystyle\omega_{X^{[m]}/Y}\simeq\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}^{\ast}\omega_{X/Y}

over
f^{[m]\ast}(V)
,
where
\omega_{X^{[m]}/Y}
is the relative dualizing sheaf. Additionally, there is an injective morphism given by the trace map:

(4.3)

\displaystyle{\rm tr}:\rho_{\ast}(\omega_{X^{(m)}/Y})\to\omega_{X^{[m]}/Y}.

By applying the flat base change theorem, we obtain an isomorphism in the derived category
D(V)
:

\displaystyle Rf^{[m]}_{\ast}(\omega_{X^{[m]}/Y}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{[m]})|_{V}\simeq\bigotimes^{m}Rf_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})|_{V}.

Since every
R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})|_{V}
is locally free, it follows that
\bigotimes^{m}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})|_{V}
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
R^{mq}f^{[m]}_{\ast}(\omega_{X^{[m]}/Y}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{[m]})|_{V}
.
Hence, we have a surjective morphism

\displaystyle R^{mq}f^{[m]}_{\ast}(\omega_{X^{[m]}/Y}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{[m]})|_{V}\to\bigotimes^{m}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})|_{V}.

By taking reflexive hulls, we obtain a morphism:

(4.4)

\displaystyle\alpha:R^{mq}f^{[m]}_{\ast}(\omega_{X^{[m]}/Y}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{[m]})\to\bigotimes^{[m]}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})\to S^{[m]}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E}),

which is surjective over
V
. Here,
\otimes^{[m]}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})
denotes the reflexive hull of
\otimes^{m}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})
.

Let
h_{\mathscr{E}^{[m]}}=\otimes_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}^{\ast}(hh_{F}h_{L})
,
h^{[m]}_{0}=\otimes_{i=1}^{m}p_{i}^{\ast}(h_{0})
,
h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}=\rho^{\ast}(h_{\mathscr{E}^{[m]}})
, and
h^{(m)}_{0}=\rho^{\ast}(h^{[m]}_{0})
. Then
h^{(m)}_{0}
is a smooth Hermitian metric on
\mathscr{E}^{(m)}
, and
h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}
is a singular Hermitian metric on
\mathscr{E}^{(m)}
satisfying
h^{(m)}_{0}\lesssim h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}
. Therefore, we have the following inclusion:

\displaystyle S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}})\subset S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h^{(m)}_{0})\simeq\omega_{X^{(m)}}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{(m)}.

By combining this with the trace map (
4.3
), we obtain an injective morphism:

(4.5)

\displaystyle\beta:\rho_{\ast}(S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}))\to\omega_{X^{[m]}/Y}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{[m]}\otimes f^{[m]\ast}(\omega_{Y}).

Since
hh_{F}h_{L}
is Nakano semi-positive over some Zariski open subset of
X
and tame on
X
(Lemma
3.10
),
h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}
is Nakano semi-positive over some Zariski open subset of
X^{(m)}
and tame on
X^{(m)}
. Applying Theorem
3.4
, we have:

\rho_{\ast}(S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}))\simeq R\rho_{\ast}(S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}})).

Therefore, (
4.5
) induces a morphism:

(4.6)

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}:R^{mq}f^{(m)}_{\ast}(S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}))\to R^{mq}f^{[m]}_{\ast}(\omega_{X^{[m]}/Y}\otimes\mathscr{E}^{[m]})\otimes\omega_{Y},

where
f^{(m)}
denotes
f^{[m]}\circ\rho
.
By applying Corollary
3.14
on
X^{[m]o}
, it can be seen that
\beta
is also an isomorphism over
X^{[m]o}=(f^{[m]})^{-1}(U)
. Therefore,
\beta^{\prime}
is an isomorphism over
U
. By combining (
4.6
) with (
4.4
), we obtain a morphism

(4.7)

\displaystyle R^{mq}f^{(m)}_{\ast}(S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}))\to S^{[m]}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X}\otimes\mathscr{E})\otimes\omega_{Y}

which is surjective over
U\cap V
.

Step 3: Castelnuovo-Mumford’s criterion.

Applying Theorem
3.4
to
S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}})
yields the following result:

H^{i}(Y,R^{mq}f^{(m)}_{\ast}(S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}))\otimes A_{Y}^{\dim Y+1}\otimes A_{Y}^{-i})=0,\quad\text{for all }i>0.

This means that
R^{mq}f^{(m)}_{\ast}(S_{X^{(m)}}(\mathscr{E}^{(m)},h_{\mathscr{E}^{(m)}}))\otimes A_{Y}^{\dim Y+1}
is
0
-regular and therefore generated by global sections. Combining this with the generic surjective map (
4.7
), we can conclude that

S^{[m]}R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/Y}\otimes\mathscr{E})\otimes\omega_{Y}\otimes A_{Y}^{\dim Y+1}

is generated by global sections at every point of
V\cap U
. This demonstrates that
R^{q}f_{\ast}(\omega_{X/Y}\otimes\mathscr{E})
is weakly positive.
∎

4.4.
Generic vanishing theorem

Definition 4.13
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety. A coherent sheaf
F
on
A
is called a
GV-sheaf
if

{\rm codim}_{{\rm Pic}^{0}(A)}\left\{M\in{\rm Pic}^{0}(A)\mid H^{i}(A,F\otimes M)\neq 0\right\}\geq i

for every
i
.

Let us recall Hacon’s criterion of a GV-sheaf
[
8
]
(see also
[
31
, Theorem 25.5]
and
[
24
]
).

Lemma 4.14
.

Suppose that for every finite étale morphism
\varphi:B\to A
of abelian varieties, and every ample line bundle
L
on
B
, one has
H^{i}(B,\varphi^{*}(F)\otimes L)=0
for
i>0
. Then
F
is a GV-sheaf.

Theorem 4.15
.

Notations as in Theorem
1.1
. Let

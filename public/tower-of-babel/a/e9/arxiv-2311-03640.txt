Impact of the Ce ​ 4 ​ f \mathrm{Ce}~4f states in the electronic structure of the intermediate-valence superconductor CeIr 3 Journal: Electronic Structure
Shin-ichi Fujimori, Ikuto Kawasaki, Yukiharu Takeda, Hiroshi Yamagami, Norimasa Sasabe, Yoshiki J. Sato, Ai Nakamura, Yusei Shimizu, Arvind Maurya, Yoshiya Homma, Dexin Li, Fuminori Honda, Dai Aoki

Abstract
The electronic structure of the f f -based superconductor CeIr 3 \mathrm{CeIr_{3}} was studied by photoelectron spectroscopy. The energy distribution of the Ce ​ 4 ​ f \mathrm{Ce}~4f states were revealed by the Ce ​ 3 ​ d − 4 ​ f \mathrm{Ce}~3d-4f resonant photoelectron spectroscopy. The Ce ​ 4 ​ f \mathrm{Ce}~4f states were mostly distributed in the vicinity of the Fermi energy, suggesting the itinerant character of the Ce ​ 4 ​ f \mathrm{Ce}~4f states. The contribution of the Ce ​ 4 ​ f \mathrm{Ce}~4f states to the density of states (DOS) at the Fermi energy was estimated to be nearly half of that of the Ir ​ 5 ​ d \mathrm{Ir}~5d states, implying that the Ce ​ 4 ​ f \mathrm{Ce}~4f states have a considerable contribution to the DOS at the Fermi energy. The Ce ​ 3 ​ d \mathrm{Ce}~3d core-level and Ce ​ 3 ​ d \mathrm{Ce}~3d X-ray absorption spectra were analyzed based on a single-impurity Anderson model. The number of the Ce ​ 4 ​ f \mathrm{Ce}~4f states in the ground state was estimated to be 0.8 − 0.9 0.8-0.9 , which is much larger than the values obtained in the previous studies (i.e., 0 − 0.4 0-0.4 ).

Introduction

\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
is a
f
-based superconductor with a relatively high transition temperature (
T_{\mathrm{SC}}=3.1~\mathrm{K}
), and its superconducting properties have attracted much attention in recent years
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
.
Its thermodynamical properties and
\mu
SR measurements suggest
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
is a weak-coupling superconductor with an anisotropic
s
-wave gap
[
1
,
4
]
.
One of the key issues of this compound is the role of the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states in superconductivity.
Isostructural analogue compounds
\mathrm{LaIr_{3}}
(
T_{\mathrm{SC}}=2.5~\mathrm{K}

[
5
,
6
]
) and
\mathrm{ThIr_{3}}
(
T_{\mathrm{SC}}=4.41~\mathrm{K}

[
7
]
) also exhibit the superconductivity with similar or higher transition temperatures; hence, the role of the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states in the superconductivity of
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
is considered to be negligible
[
8
]
.
Experimentally, the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states in
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
are suggested as an intermediate-valence state, but the occupancy of the
4f
states
n_{4f}
is a matter of controversies.
Based on the relationship between the transition temperature and the valence states in
\mathrm{(La,Th)Ir_{3}}
alloys,
n_{4f}
is estimated as
n_{4f}\sim 0.4

[
9
]
.
Górnicka
et al
recently suggested that
n_{4f}\sim 0
based on the weak-temperature-dependent magnetic susceptibility of
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
. They argued that the contributions from the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states at the Fermi energy are negligible in this compound
[
2
]
.
On the contrary, the experimental specific heat coefficient (
\gamma_{\mathrm{exp}}=25.1~\mathrm{mJ/mol\cdot K^{2}}
) is much larger than that obtained by the GGA calculation (
\gamma_{\mathrm{GGA}}=10.2~\mathrm{mJ/mol\cdot K^{2}}
)
[
2
]
, suggesting that there should be a considerable contribution from the correlated
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states at the Fermi energy.
Gutowska
et al
recently calculated the electronic structure of
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
based on the dynamical mean field theory (DMFT) and reproduced the experimental specific heat coefficient and the electron–phonon coupling
[
10
]
.
Their calculation showed that the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states had a finite contribution at the Fermi energy, and its occupation number was
n_{4f}\sim 0.8
.
Thus, the nature of the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states of this compound is controversial.

In this study, we have investigated the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states of
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
by the
\mathrm{Ce}~3d-4f
resonant photoelectron spectroscopy (RPES), X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XAS), and core-level photoelectron spectroscopy.
Accordingly, the RPES study shows that the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states form a sharp peak at the Fermi energy, suggesting that the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states in
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
have a considerable contribution to the Fermi energy despite the stronger contribution from the
\mathrm{Ir}~5d
states.
The results of the analysis of the
\mathrm{Ce}~3d
XAS and core-level spectra based on the single-impurity Anderson model reveal that
n_{4f}=0.8-0.9
, contrast to the predictions of the previous studies
[
2
,
9
]
.
These results argue that the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states are in an intermediate-valence state, and the contribution from the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states to superconductivity is not negligible in
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
.

2
Experimental Procedure

Single crystals of
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
were prepared using the Czochralski method in a tetra arc furnace under Ar atmosphere.
The details of the sample growth and their characterizations were presented in Refs.
[
1
,
3
,
11
]
.
Photoemission experiments were performed at the soft X-ray beamline BL23SU of SPring-8
[
12
]
.
The total energy resolution at the on-resonant condition (
h\nu\sim 881~\mathrm{eV}
) was approximately
65~\mathrm{meV}
, while that in the
\mathrm{Ce}~3d
core-level measurements was approximately
190~\mathrm{meV}
.
Clean sample surfaces were obtained by cleaving high-quality single crystals
in situ
under an ultra-high vacuum condition.
The sample temperature was kept at
20~\mathrm{K}
during the measurements.

4
Conclusion

We studied the electronic structure of
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
by the
\mathrm{Ce}~3d-4f
resonant photoelectron,
\mathrm{Ce}~3d
XAS, and
\mathrm{Ce}~3d
core-level spectroscopies.
All experimental data suggested the well-hybridized nature of the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states with the occupation number of
n_{f}=0.8-0.9
, which was consistent with the DMFT calculation result
[
10
]
.
The comparison of the intensities at the Fermi energy of the on- and off-resonance spectra suggested that more than 30 % of the DOS at the Fermi energy was contributed by the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states.
These results claimed a finite contribution of the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states on the superconductivity of
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
.
Thus,
\mathrm{CeIr_{3}}
is an
s
-wave-type superconductor with a finite contribution from the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f
states.

In this appendix, we describe the influence of Auger signals in the resonant photoemission spectra.
Figures
4
(a) and (b) show the
\mathrm{Ce}~3d_{5/2}
XAS and the density plot of the valence band spectra measured at
h\nu=880-884~\mathrm{eV}
, respectively.
The gigantic resonant enhancement of the
\mathrm{Ce}~4f

\mathrm{Ce}~3d_{5/2}

h\nu=881-882~\mathrm{eV}
.
Meanwhile, two diagonal lines were recognized in Fig.

The resonant photoemission spectra measured at
h\nu=881-882~\mathrm{eV}
normalized by the peak height of each spectrum is displayed in Fig.

\mathrm{Ce}~4f

The spectral shapes of the resonance spectra measured at

We have used the incident photon energy of

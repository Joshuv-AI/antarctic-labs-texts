Recall that the concepts of Riesz bases and bases quadratically close to the orthonormal bases were introduced by N.K. Bari in

Definition 1.2

(i)
A sequence of vectors

(ii)
A sequence of vectors
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
in a separable Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
is called a

if it is quadratically close to some orthonormal basis
\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
\mathfrak{H}
, i.e.

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{n}-e_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}<\infty.

(1.10)

A.S. Markus in
[
36

studied in detail bases of subspaces with the property similar to (

[

,

,
3
]
.

. Namely, results of papers

,

,

One of our main results establishes the criterion for the system of root vectors of the operator

Theorem 1.3
.

Let boundary conditions (

(1.11)

In this case every normalized system of root vectors of the operator

2
Definitions and formulations of the main results

Let us recall the following abstract criterion for Bari basis property.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
17
, Theorem VI.3.2]

A complete system
\mathfrak{F}=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of unit vectors in a separable Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
forms a Bari basis if and only if there exists a sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
biorthogonal to
\mathfrak{F}
that is quadratically close to
\mathfrak{F}
:

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}<\infty,\qquad(f_{n},g_{m})_{\mathfrak{H}}=\delta_{nm},\quad n,m\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.1)

Based on this abstract criterion we will introduce a generalization of Bari basis concept. Let
p\in[1,2]
and
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)\in[2,\infty]
. It is well-known that for the dual space of
\ell^{p}:=\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
we have,

(\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}))^{*}\cong\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Z}),\quad p\in(1,2],\qquad\text{and}\qquad(\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z}))^{*}\cong c_{0}(\mathbb{Z}).

(2.2)

For simplicity we identify
(\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}))^{*}
with
\ell^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{Z})
for
p\in(1,2]
and with
c_{0}(\mathbb{Z})
for
p=1
, respectively. E.g.
\{a_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}))^{*}
for
p>1
means that
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|a_{n}|^{p^{\prime}}<\infty
.
With this in mind, we can extend Definition
1.2
(ii) using equivalence from Proposition
2.1
to more general concept of closeness of sequences
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
p\in[1,2]
, let
\mathfrak{F}:=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a complete minimal sequence of unit vectors in a separable Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
and let
\mathfrak{G}:=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be its (unique) biorthogonal sequence:
(f_{n},g_{m})_{\mathfrak{H}}=\delta_{nm}
,
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. A sequence
\mathfrak{F}
is called a
Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequence
if it is “(
\ell^{p})^{*}
-close” to its biorthogonal sequence
\mathfrak{G}
, i.e.
\left\{{\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
. In view of (
2.2
) it means that

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{p^{\prime}}<\infty\quad\text{if}\quad p\in(1,2],\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=0\quad\text{if}\quad p=1.

(2.3)

For brevity we will call
Bari
(\ell^{1})^{*}
-sequence
as
Bari
c_{0}
-sequence
and
Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequence
as
Bari
\ell^{p^{\prime}}
-sequence
for
p\in(1,2]
.

Proposition
2.1
implies that the notion of Bari
\ell^{2}
-sequence coincides with the notion of Bari basis. Note also that every Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequence is Bari
c_{0}
-sequence. We specifically chose the word “sequence” because it is not clear if Bari
c_{0}
-sequence is a Riesz basis or even a regular basis in general case.

Remark 2.3
.

Note that Bari
c_{0}
-property from definition
2.2
is not equivalent to more conventional formulation of
c_{0}
-closeness of
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
to a certain orthonormal basis
\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
even if
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is already a Riesz basis. Indeed, in this case
f_{n}=e_{n}+Ke_{n}
, where
K
and
(I+K)^{-1}
are bounded operators in
\mathfrak{H}
. Hence
\|f_{n}-e_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=\|Ke_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}
. It is easily seen that
g_{n}=\left((I+K)^{-1}\right)^{*}e_{n}=e_{n}-\left((I+K)^{-1}\right)^{*}K^{*}e_{n}
, and hence
\|g_{n}-e_{n}\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
is equivalent to
\|K^{*}e_{n}\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. If
K
is not compact then
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|Ke_{n}\|=0
is generally not equivalent to
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|K^{*}e_{n}\|=0
for a given orthonormal basis
\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
.

Let us also recall the notion of the system of root vectors of an operator with compact resolvent. Firt, we recall a few basic facts regarding the eigenvalues of a
compact, linear operator
T\in\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{H})
in a
separable complex Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
. The
geometric
multiplicity
,
m_{g}(\lambda_{0},T)
, of an eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}\in\sigma_{p}(T)
of
T
is given by

m_{g}(\lambda_{0},T):=\dim(\ker(T-\lambda_{0})).

The
root subspace
of
T
corresponding to
\lambda_{0}\in\sigma_{p}(T)
is given by

\mathcal{R}_{\lambda_{0}}(T)=\big\{f\in\mathfrak{H}\,:\,(T-\lambda_{0})^{k}f=0\ \ \text{for some}\ \ k\in\mathbb{N}\big\}.

(2.4)

Elements of
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda_{0}}(T)
are called
root vectors
.
For
\lambda_{0}\in\sigma_{p}(T)\backslash\{0\}
, the set

\mathcal{R}_{\lambda_{0}}(T)
is a closed linear subspace of

\mathfrak{H}
whose dimension equals to the
algebraic
multiplicity
,
m_{a}(\lambda_{0},T)
, of
\lambda_{0}
,

m_{a}(\lambda_{0},T):=\dim\big(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda_{0}}(T)\big)<\infty.

Denote by
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
the sequence of non-zero
eigenvalues of
T
and let
n_{j}
be the algebraic multiplicity
of
\lambda_{j}
. By the
system of root vectors
of the operator

T
we mean any sequence of the form

\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}\{e_{jk}\}_{k=1}^{n_{j}},

where
\{e_{jk}\}_{k=1}^{n_{j}}
is a basis in
\mathcal{R}_{\lambda_{j}}(T)
,

n_{j}=m_{a}(\lambda_{j},T)<\infty
. The system or root vectors of the operator
T
is called
normalized
if
\|e_{jk}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=1
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\in\{1,\ldots,n_{j}\}
.

We are particularly interested in the case where
A
is a
densely defined, closed, linear operator in

whose
resolvent is compact, that is,

R_{A}(\lambda):=(A-\lambda)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}_{\infty}(\mathfrak{H}),\ \lambda\in\rho(A).

Via the spectral mapping theorem all eigenvalues of

correspond to eigenvalues of its resolvent

,
\lambda\in\rho(A)
, and vice versa. Hence, we use the same notions of
root vectors, root subspaces, geometric and algebraic
multiplicities associated with the eigenvalues of
A
, and the
system of root vectors of
A
.

Now we are ready to formulate the main result of this paper, which involve notions of Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequences and
c_{0}
-sequences from Definition
2.2
above.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let boundary conditions (
1.6
) be strictly regular and let
Q\in{L^{p}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
for some
p\in[1,2]
. Then some normalized system of root vectors of the operator
L_{U}(Q)
is a Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequence in
{L^{2}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2})
if and only if the operator
L_{U}(0)
is self-adjoint, i.e. when relations (
1.11
) hold for the coefficients
a,b,c,d
in boundary conditions (
1.6
). In this case every normalized system of root vectors of the operator
L_{U}(Q)
is a Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequence in
{L^{2}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.4
we get Theorem
1.3
: the criterion of Bari basis property for Dirac-type operator
L_{U}(Q)
with
L^{2}
-potential and strictly regular boundary conditions.

Let us briefly comment on the proof of our main result, Theorem
2.4
.
First, we apply Theorem
1.1
to reduce the Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-property of the system of root vectors of operator
L_{U}(Q)
with strictly regular boundary conditions to a certain explicit condition in terms of the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operator
L_{U}(0)
, which reads as follows for the case
p=1
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
Q\in{L^{1}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
and boundary conditions (
1.3
) be strictly regular. Then some normalized systems of root vectors
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operator
L_{U}(Q)
is a Bari
c_{0}
-sequence in
{L^{2}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2})
if and only if:

b_{1}|c|+b_{2}|b|=0,\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\Im\lambda_{n}^{0}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n}=|bc|,

(2.5)

where

(2.6)

With condition (

1.11

In this connection, recall that the sequence

coincides with the sequence of zeros of characteristic determinant

(2.7)

If

The case

Namely, if

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

Recall that
x_{n}\asymp y_{n}

C_{2}>C_{1}>0
such that
C_{1}|y_{n}|\leqslant|x_{n}|\leqslant C_{2}|y_{n}|
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. We start the following simple property of zeros of

Lemma 4.1
.

Let boundary conditions (
3.14
) be regular and
\Lambda_{0}:=\{\lambda_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the sequence of zeros of
\Delta_{0}(\cdot)
counting multiplicity. Set

(4.1)

(i)
Let
bc\neq 0
. Then

1+ae_{1n}\asymp 1,\qquad 1+de_{2n}\asymp 1,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(4.2)

(ii)
Let boundary conditions (

\left|{1+ae_{1n}}\right|^{2}+\left|{1+de_{2n}}\right|^{2}\asymp 1,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(4.3)

Proof.

Note that

\Delta_{0}(\lambda)=\left(1+ae^{ib_{1}\lambda}\right)\left(d+e^{ib_{2}\lambda}\right)-bc\cdot e^{ib_{1}\lambda}=e^{ib_{2}\lambda}\left(1+ae^{ib_{1}\lambda}\right)\left(1+de^{-ib_{2}\lambda}\right)-bc\cdot e^{ib_{1}\lambda},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

(4.4)

Since
\Delta(\lambda_{n}^{0})=0
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then with account of notation (
4.1
) we have

\left(1+ae_{1n}\right)\left(1+de_{2n}\right)=bce_{1n}e_{2n},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(4.5)

According to Proposition
3.3
the relation (
3.10
) holds.
Hence

e_{jn}\asymp 1,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},\quad j\in\{1,2\}.

(4.6)

(i)
Since
bc\neq 0
, then combining (
4.5
) with (
4.6
) yields the following estimate with some
C_{3}>C_{2}>C_{1}>0
,

C_{3}>C_{2}\left|{1+ae_{1n}}\right|\geqslant\left|{(1+ae_{1n})(1+de_{2n})}\right|=2|bc|\cdot\left|{e_{1n}e_{2n}}\right|>C_{1},\quad|n|\in\mathbb{Z},

(4.7)

which proves the first relation in (
4.2
). The second relation is proved similarly.

(ii)
If
bc\neq 0
then (
4.3
) is implied by (
4.2
). Let
bc=0
. In this case
ad\neq 0
and
\Delta_{0}(\lambda)=e^{ib_{2}\lambda}\left(1+ae^{ib_{1}\lambda}\right)\left(1+de^{-ib_{2}\lambda}\right)
. It is clear that
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda_{0}^{1}\cup\Lambda_{0}^{2}
, where
\Lambda_{0}^{1}=\{\lambda_{1,n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\Lambda_{0}^{2}=\{\lambda_{2,n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
are the sequences of zeros of the first and second factor, respectively. Clearly, these sequences constitute arithmetic progressions lying on the lines, parallel to the real axis. More precisely,

\lambda_{1,n}^{0}=\frac{\arg(-a^{-1})+2\pi n}{b_{1}}+i\frac{\ln|a|}{b_{1}},\qquad\lambda_{2,n}^{0}=\frac{\arg(-d)+2\pi n}{b_{2}}-i\frac{\ln|d|}{b_{2}},

(4.8)

for

. Since boundary conditions (

) are strictly regular, then the union of these arithmetic progressions

is asymptotically separated. It is easily seen that, in fact,

is separated: if

then
\Lambda_{0}
is periodic and if

then arithmetic progressions
\Lambda_{0}^{1}
and
\Lambda_{0}^{2}
necessarily lie on different parallel lines.

1+de^{-ib_{2}\lambda_{2,n}^{0}}\asymp 1,\quad 1+ae^{ib_{1}\lambda_{1,n}^{0}}\asymp 1,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},

Throughout the rest of the section we will denote by

Lemma 4.2
.

Let

First, let us recall some simple properties of the sequences that have a finite set of limit points. For brevity we denote the cardinality of the limit points set of a bounded sequence

\left.\text{for some}\ \ \{n_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{Z}\ \ \text{such that}\ \ n_{j}\neq n_{k}\ \ \text{for}\ \ j\neq k\right\}.

If the set of limit points is infinite we set

Lemma 4.3
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

Let

(ii)

Let

(iii)

(iv)

\nlim\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}=m\in\mathbb{N}

The following result of Diophantine approximation nature plays crucial role in treating the tricky case of

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and
b_{2}/b_{1}\notin\mathbb{Q}
. Further, let

for some

.
Then one of the sequences
\{\sin(b_{1}\alpha_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{\sin(b_{2}\alpha_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

has an infinite set of limit points.

Proof.

Assume the contrary. Namely, let

\nlim\left\{{\sin(b_{1}\alpha_{n})}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}=m_{1}\in\mathbb{N}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nlim\left\{{\sin(b_{2}\alpha_{n})}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}=m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}.

Let us set

b_{1}\alpha_{n}=2\pi(k_{n}+\delta_{n}),\qquad k_{n}:=\left\lfloor{\frac{b_{1}\alpha_{n}}{2\pi}}\right\rfloor\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\delta_{n}=\left\langle{\frac{b_{1}\alpha_{n}}{2\pi}}\right\rangle\in[0,1).

(4.13)

It is clear that
\sin(2\pi\delta_{n})=\sin(b_{1}\alpha_{n})
. Hence by Lemma
4.3
(iii)

\nlim\{\delta_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\leqslant 2m_{1}+1.

(4.14)

It is clear from (

b_{2}\alpha_{n}=2\pi\left(\beta k_{n}+\beta\delta_{n}\right),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad\beta:=b_{2}/b_{1}\notin\mathbb{Q}.

The same reasoning as above shows that

\nlim\left\{{u_{n}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\leqslant 2m_{2}+1,\qquad u_{n}:=\left\langle{\beta k_{n}+\beta\delta_{n}}\right\rangle.

Further, combining (
4.14
) with by Lemma
4.3
(iv) implies that

\nlim\left\{{v_{n}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\leqslant 2m_{1}+2,\quad\text{where}\quad v_{n}:=\left\langle{\beta\delta_{n}}\right\rangle,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(4.15)

Finally, note that
\left\langle{\beta k_{n}}\right\rangle=\left\langle{u_{n}-v_{n}}\right\rangle
,

the sequence
\left\{{\left\langle{\beta k_{n}}\right\rangle}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

Let
\varepsilon>0

Since

4.2
implies that

\displaystyle\card(\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon,M})

\displaystyle\leqslant 6p\varepsilon M,\qquad M\geqslant M_{\varepsilon},\quad M\in\mathbb{N},\quad\text{where}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{\varepsilon,M}

\displaystyle:=\{m\in\{-M,\ldots,M\}:\left\langle{\beta m}\right\rangle\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\},\quad M\in\mathbb{N},

(4.18)

For
M_{\varepsilon}:=\max\bigl\{M_{x_{j}-\varepsilon,x_{j}+\varepsilon,\varepsilon}:j\in\{1,\ldots,p\}\bigr\}

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}

It is clear from (

Hence if

with

\widetilde{M}_{\varepsilon}\geqslant M_{\varepsilon}
.

4.16

) of

5
Bari
c_{0}
-property of the system of root vectors of the unperturbed operator

In this section assuming boundary conditions (
3.14
) to be strictly regular, we show that the system of root vectors of the operator
L_{U}(0)
is a Bari
c_{0}
-sequence in
{L^{2}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2})
if and only if the operator
L_{U}(0)
is selfadjoint. Since eigenfunctions of
L_{U}(0)
in their “natural form” are not normalized we need the following simple practical criterion of Bari
c_{0}
-property.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a complete minimal system of vectors in a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
. Let also
\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be “almost biorthogonal” to
\mathfrak{F}
. Namely,
(f_{n},g_{m})=0
,
n\neq m
,
(f_{n},g_{n})\neq 0
,
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then the normalized system

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}:=\left\{{f_{n}^{\prime}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\quad f_{n}^{\prime}:=\frac{1}{\|f_{n}\|}f_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

is a Bari
c_{0}
-sequence in
\mathfrak{H}
(see Definition
2.2
) if and only if

\frac{\|f_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}\cdot\|g_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}}{|(f_{n},g_{n})_{\mathfrak{H}}|}\to 1\quad\text{as}\quad n\to\infty.

(5.1)

Proof.

For brevity we set
\|\cdot\|:=\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{H}}
and
(\cdot,\cdot):=(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathfrak{H}}
.
It is clear that for the system
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}:=\{g_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
that is biorthogonal to
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
we have,

(f_{n}^{\prime},g_{m}^{\prime})=\delta_{nm},\quad n,m\in\mathbb{Z};\qquad g_{n}^{\prime}=\frac{\|f_{n}\|}{(f_{n},g_{n})}\cdot g_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(5.2)

Relations (
5.2
) imply that

\|f_{n}^{\prime}-g_{n}^{\prime}\|^{2}=\|f_{n}^{\prime}\|^{2}-(f_{n}^{\prime},g_{n}^{\prime})-\overline{(f_{n}^{\prime},g_{n}^{\prime})}+\|g_{n}^{\prime}\|^{2}=\|g_{n}^{\prime}\|^{2}-1=\frac{\|f_{n}\|^{2}\cdot\|g_{n}\|^{2}}{|(f_{n},g_{n})|^{2}}-1.

(5.3)

Hence, systems
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
are
c_{0}
-close if and only if condition (
5.1
) holds.
∎

The following simple property of compact operators with asymptotically simple spectrum will be also useful in the next section.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
T
be an operator with compact resolvent in a separable Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
and let
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a sequence of its eigenvalues counting multiplicities. Let also

Assume that for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
eigenvalues
\lambda_{n}
,
|n|\geqslant N
, are algebraically simple.
Then if some normalized system of root vectors of the operator
T
is a Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequence in
\mathfrak{H}
then every normalized system of root vectors of the operator
T
is a Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequence in
\mathfrak{H}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{F}=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}

be a normalized system of root vectors of the operator
T
, which is a Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequence in
\mathfrak{H}
. By definition,
the system
\mathfrak{F}
is complete and minimal in
\mathfrak{H}
. Let
\mathfrak{G}=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be its (unique) biorthogonal system. Further, let
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}=\{f_{n}^{\prime}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be any other normalized system of root vectors of the operator
T
. Since eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}
,
|n|\geqslant N
, is algebraically simple then
\dim(\mathcal{R}_{\lambda_{n}}(T))=1
,
|n|\geqslant N
. Hence

,
|n|\geqslant N
, for some

. It is clear that
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
is also complete and minimal and for its biorthogonal sysyem

,

\|f_{n}^{\prime}-g_{n}^{\prime}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=\|\alpha_{n}\cdot(f_{n}-g_{n})\|_{\mathfrak{H}}=\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}}

Remark 5.3
.

Let

be a selfadjoint operator with compact resolvent. Then every its normalized system of root vectors is an orthonormal basis in

and coincides with its biorthogonal sequence. This implies that every normalized system of root vectors of the operator

-sequence.

To study norms

and

of the eigenvectors of the operators

Lemma 5.4
.

Denote for

Then the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle E_{j}^{+}(\lambda)E_{j}^{-}(\lambda)-1\geqslant\frac{(b_{j}\Im\lambda)^{2}}{3},\qquad j\in\{1,2\},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}.

(5.5)

In particular,
E_{j}^{+}(\lambda)E_{j}^{-}(\lambda)-1>0

Proof.

Let

E_{j}^{\pm}(\lambda)=f(\mp 2b_{j}\Im\lambda),\quad\text{where}\quad f(x):=\frac{e^{x}-1}{x}=1+\frac{x}{2}+O(x^{2}),\quad|x|<h.

(5.6)

It follows from Taylor expansion of

x\in\mathbb{R}

(5.7)

Estimate (

Lemma 5.7

where

is defined in (

Then

Proof.

Since

|e_{1n}|\to 1

Further, since boundary conditions are regular,

are valid. Since

z_{n}=(1+de_{2n})\cdot\overline{(1+ae_{1n})}=\left(1-d\frac{1+ae_{1n}}{d+ue_{1n}}\right)\cdot(1+\overline{a}\overline{e_{1n}})\\

,

, as established in (

). Since

and

, then (

Now we are ready to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.8
.

3.14
) be strictly regular. Then some normalized system of root vectors of the operator
L_{U}(0)
is a Bari
c_{0}
-sequence in
{L^{2}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2})
(see Definition
2.2
) if and only the operator
L_{U}(0)
is self-adjoint. The latter holds if and only if coefficients
a,b,c,d
from boundary conditions (
3.14
) satisfy the following relations:

|a|^{2}+\beta|b|^{2}=1,\qquad|c|^{2}+\beta|d|^{2}=\beta,\qquad a\overline{c}+\beta b\overline{d}=0,\qquad\beta:=-b_{2}/b_{1}>0.

In this case every normalized system of root vectors of the operator
L_{U}(0)
is a Bari
c_{0}
-sequence in
{L^{2}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

Proof.

(i)
If conditions (
5.37
) hold then by Corollary
3.9
the operator
L_{U}(0)
with boundary conditions (
5.8
) is self-adjoint. Remark
5.3
now finishes the proof.

(ii)
Now assume that some normalized system of root vectors of the operator
L_{U}(0)
forms a Bari basis in
{L^{2}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2})
.

If
b=c=0
then Proposition
5.5
yields that
|a|=|d|=1
, in which case operator
L_{U}(0)
is self-adjoint. This finishes the proof in this case.

Now let
|b|+|c|\neq 0
. Proposition
5.6
implies that relations (
5.14
) take place. In particular,
|c|=\beta|b|
. Consider three cases.

Case A.
Let
b_{1}/b_{2}\in\mathbb{Q}
. In this case
b_{1}=-m_{1}b_{0}
,
b_{2}=m_{2}b_{0}
, where
b_{0}>0
,
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
. Set
m=m_{1}+m_{2}
. Since
ad\neq bc
, then
\Delta_{0}(\lambda)e^{-ib_{1}\lambda}
is a polynomial in
e^{ib_{0}\lambda}
of degree

with non-zero roots
e^{i\mu_{k}}
,
\mu_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
,
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, counting multiplicities. Hence, the sequence of zeros
\{\lambda_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
\Delta_{0}(\cdot)
is a union of arithmetic progressions
\left\{\frac{\mu_{k}+2\pi n}{b_{0}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. Clearly
\Im\lambda_{n}^{0}=\Im\mu_{k_{n}}/b_{0}
for some
k_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. It is clear, that if
\Im\mu_{k}\neq 0
, for some
k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, then
\Im\lambda_{n}^{0}
does not tend to

as
n\to\infty
.
Hence
\Im\lambda_{n}^{0}=0
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. This implies that

E_{jn}^{\pm}=\int_{0}^{1}\left|{e^{\pm 2ib_{j}\lambda_{n}^{0}x}}\right|dx=1,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad j\in\{1,2\}.

(5.38)

It is clear that
e^{-ib_{2}\lambda_{n}^{0}}=(e^{-i\mu_{k_{n}}})^{m_{2}}
for some
k_{n}\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\{k_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a periodic sequence. Hence the sequence
\{e^{-ib_{2}\lambda_{n}^{0}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is periodic. Similarly the sequence
\{e^{ib_{1}\lambda_{n}^{0}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is periodic. Hence, the sequence

\{z_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\qquad z_{n}=\left(1+de^{-ib_{2}\lambda_{n}^{0}}\right)\overline{\left(1+ae^{ib_{1}\lambda_{n}^{0}}\right)}=\left(1+de_{2n}\right)\overline{\left(1+ae_{1n}\right)},

(5.39)

is periodic. Since
z_{n}\to|bc|
as
n\to\infty
and
|c|=\beta|b|
, it implies that
z_{n}=|bc|=\beta|b|^{2}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. It now follows from (

)–(
5.27
) and (
5.38
) that

\|f_{n}^{0}\|_{2}^{2}\cdot\|g_{n}^{0}\|_{2}^{2}-|(f_{n}^{0},g_{n}^{0})|_{2}^{2}=\tau_{4,n}=\left|{z_{n}-\beta|b|^{2}}\right|^{2}=0,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(5.40)

Taking into account formula (
5.3
) we see that the normalized eigenvectors

and
g_{n}^{\prime}
of the operators
L_{U}(0)
and
L_{U}^{*}(0)
corresponding to the common eigenvalue

are equal for all

, which implies that
L_{U}(0)=L_{U}^{*}(0)
.

Case B.
Let

.
Then
z_{n}=1
,

. Since

as

, then
|bc|=1
. Combined with

, this implies the desired condition (
5.37
), and finishes the proof in this case.

Case C.
Finally, let

,
|a|+|d|>0
and
bc\neq 0
. Since
\Im\lambda_{n}^{0}\to 0
and
z_{n}\to|bc|
as

, then Lemma
5.7
implies condition (
5.32
). In particular,

. Since, in addition,
|c|=\beta|b|>0
, then

-ad\overline{bc}=|ad\cdot bc|=|d|^{2}\cdot\beta|b|^{2}=\beta b\overline{d}\cdot d\overline{b}.

(5.41)

Since
d\overline{b}\neq 0
, this implies that
a\overline{c}+\beta b\overline{d}=0
and coincides with the third condition in (
5.37
). Further, the second relation in (
5.32
), combined with relations (
5.41
),
|a|=|d|
and
|c|=\beta|b|
, implies that

0=\left(-ad+bc+d/\overline{a}\right)\overline{bc}=-ad\overline{bc}+|bc|^{2}+\frac{ad\overline{bc}}{|a|^{2}}=|ad|\cdot|bc|+|bc|^{2}-\frac{|ad|\cdot|bc|}{|a|^{2}}\\
=|bc|(|ad|+|bc|-1)=|bc|(|a|^{2}+\beta|b|^{2}-1)=|bc|(|d|^{2}+\beta^{-1}|c|^{2}-1).

(5.42)

Since
bc\neq 0
, relation (
5.42
) implies the first and second relations in (
5.37
), which finishes the proof.
∎

6
The proof of the main result

This section is devoted to the proof of the main result of the paper, Theorem
2.4
. Throughout the section we use the following notations:

\mathfrak{H}:={L^{2}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2}),\qquad\|\cdot\|:=\|\cdot\|_{2}=\|\cdot\|_{\mathfrak{H}}\quad\text{and}\quad(\cdot,\cdot):=(\cdot,\cdot)_{2}=(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathfrak{H}}.

(6.1)

First we need the following trivial corollary from Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 6.1
.

Let
Q\in{L^{p}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
for some
p\in[1,2]
and boundary conditions (
1.3
) be strictly regular. Let
\mathfrak{F}:=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a system of root vectors of the operator
L_{U}(Q)
such that
\|f_{n}\|\asymp 1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then there exists a system of root vectors
\mathfrak{F}_{0}:=\{f_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operator
L_{U}(0)
such that
\left\{{\|f_{n}-f_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
and
\|f_{n}\|=\|f_{n}^{0}\|
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Combining relations (
1.7
) and (
1.9
) from Theorem
1.1
applied with
\widetilde{Q}=0
, implies existence of normalized systems of root vectors
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}:=\{\widetilde{f}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{F}}_{0}:=\{\widetilde{f}_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operators
L_{U}(Q)
and
L_{U}(0)
, respectively, such that
\left\{{\|\widetilde{f}_{n}-\widetilde{f}_{n}^{0}\|_{\infty}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
. Hence

\left\{{\|\widetilde{f}_{n}-\widetilde{f}_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
. By Proposition
3.4
eigenvalues of
L_{U}(Q)
are asymptotically simple. Hence vectors
f_{n}
and
\widetilde{f}_{n}
,
|n|\geqslant N
, are proportional for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e.
f_{n}=\alpha_{n}\widetilde{f}_{n}
,
|n|\geqslant N
, for some
\alpha_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
.
Let us set

\mathfrak{F}_{0}:=\{f_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}},\qquad f_{n}^{0}:=\begin{cases}\alpha_{n}\widetilde{f}_{n}^{0},&|n|\geqslant N,\\
\|f_{n}\|\widetilde{f}_{n}^{0},&|n|<N.\\
\end{cases}

(6.2)

It is clear that
\mathfrak{F}_{0}
is a system of root vectors of the operator
L_{U}(0)
and
\|f_{n}^{0}\|=\|f_{n}\|
,

. Moreover,
\|f_{n}-f_{n}^{0}\|=|\alpha_{n}|\cdot\|\widetilde{f}_{n}-\widetilde{f}_{n}^{0}\|
,
|n|\geqslant N
. Since
|\alpha_{n}|=\|f_{n}\|\asymp 1
,
|n|\geqslant N
, and
\left\{{\|\widetilde{f}_{n}-\widetilde{f}_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
, then
\left\{{\|f_{n}-f_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove our main result on Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-property.

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

Recall that
Q\in{L^{p}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
for some
p\in[1,2]
. Also note that if
L_{U}(0)
is selfadjoint then Theorem
5.8
implies conditions (
1.11
) on the coefficients from boundary conditions (
3.14
).

(i)
First assume that the operator
L_{U}(0)
is selfadjoint and let
\mathfrak{F}:=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be some normalized system of root vector of the operators
L_{U}(Q)
. By Corollary
6.1
there exists normalized system of root vectors
\mathfrak{F}_{0}:=\{f_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operator
L_{U}(0)
such that
\left\{{\|f_{n}-f_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
. Since
L_{U}(0)
is selfadjoint, then
\{f_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an orthonormal basis in
\mathfrak{H}
. If
p=2
then the proof would be already finished since
\mathfrak{F}
is
\ell^{2}
-close to the orthonormal basis
\mathfrak{F}_{0}
. But as Remark
2.3
shows for
p\in[1,2)
, the
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-closeness to the orthonormal basis is not equivalent to the Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-property.

To this end, let
\mathfrak{G}:=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the system of vectors in
\mathfrak{H}
that is biorthogonal to the system
\mathfrak{F}
. We need to prove that
\left\{{\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
. Clearly,
\mathfrak{G}
is (not normalized) system of root vectors of the adjoint operator
L_{U}^{*}(Q)
. Since
L_{U}(0)
is self-adjoint then by Lemma
3.8
we have
L_{U}^{*}(Q)=L_{U}(Q^{*})
. Using Corollary
6.1
in the “opposite” direction we can find a normalized system of root vectors
\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}}:=\{\widetilde{g}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operator
L_{U}(Q^{*})
such that
\left\{{\|\widetilde{g}_{n}-f_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
. Therefore,
\left\{{\|f_{n}-\widetilde{g}_{n}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
.
Since both systems
\mathfrak{G}
and
\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}}
are root vector systems of the operator
L_{U}^{*}(Q)=L_{U}(Q^{*})
and eigenvalues of
L_{U}(Q^{*})
are asymptotically simple due to Proposition
3.4
, then vectors
g_{n}
and
\widetilde{g}_{n}
,
|n|\geqslant N
, are proportional for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
(f_{n},g_{n})=1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
\widetilde{g}_{n}=(f_{n},\widetilde{g}_{n})g_{n}
,
|n|\geqslant N
.
Note that if
f,g\in\mathfrak{H}
and
\|f\|=1
, then

|(f,g)-1|^{2}=|(f,g)|^{2}+1-2\Re(f,g)\\
\leqslant\|f\|^{2}\|g\|^{2}+1-2\Re(f,g)=\|f\|^{2}+\|g\|^{2}-2\Re(f,g)=\|f-g\|^{2}.

(6.3)

Since
\|f_{n}\|=1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then (
6.3
) implies that
|(f_{n},\widetilde{g}_{n})-1|\leqslant\|f_{n}-\widetilde{g}_{n}\|
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence for
|n|\geqslant N
we have,

\|\widetilde{g}_{n}-g_{n}\|=\|(f_{n},\widetilde{g}_{n})g_{n}-g_{n}\|=|(f_{n},\widetilde{g}_{n})-1|\cdot\|g_{n}\|\leqslant\|f_{n}-\widetilde{g}_{n}\|\cdot\|g_{n}\|.

(6.4)

By the main result of

[
25
,
27
,
40
]

the system
\mathfrak{F}=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a Riesz basis in
\mathfrak{H}
. Hence so is its biorthogonal system
\mathfrak{G}=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
. This in particular implies that
\|g_{n}\|\asymp 1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
\left\{{\|f_{n}-\widetilde{g}_{n}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
and
\|g_{n}\|\asymp 1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then inequality (
6.4
) implies that
\left\{{\|\widetilde{g}_{n}-g_{n}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
, which in turn implies the desired inclusion
\left\{{\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
.

(ii)
Now assume that some normalized system of root vectors
\mathfrak{F}:=\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operator
L_{U}(Q)
is a Bari
(\ell^{p})^{*}
-sequence in
\mathfrak{H}
. By definition
\left\{{\|f_{n}-g_{n}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
, where
\mathfrak{G}:=\{g_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a system biorthogonal to
\mathfrak{F}
in
\mathfrak{H}
. Clearly,
\mathfrak{G}
is a system of root vectors of the adjoint operator
L_{U}^{*}(Q)
.
By Corollary
6.1
there exists normalized system of root vectors
\mathfrak{F}_{0}:=\{f_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operator
L_{U}(0)
such that
\left\{{\|f_{n}-f_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
. Similarly there exists (possibly not normalized) system of root vectors
\mathfrak{G}_{0}:=\{g_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operator
L_{U}^{*}(0)=L_{U*}(0)
such that
\left\{{\|g_{n}-g_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
. It is clear, now that
\left\{{\|f_{n}^{0}-g_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}}_{0}:=\{\widetilde{g}_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a system biorthogonal to
\mathfrak{F}_{0}
. As in part (i),
\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}}_{0}
is a Riesz basis in
\mathfrak{H}
and
g_{n}^{0}=(f_{n}^{0},g_{n}^{0})\widetilde{g}_{n}^{0}
,
|n|\geqslant N
. Since
\|f_{n}^{0}\|=1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then (
6.3
) implies that
|(f_{n}^{0},g_{n}^{0})-1|\leqslant\|f_{n}^{0}-g_{n}^{0}\|
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence

\|g_{n}^{0}-\widetilde{g}_{n}^{0}\|=|(f_{n}^{0},g_{n}^{0})-1|\cdot\|\widetilde{g}_{n}^{0}\|\leqslant\|f_{n}^{0}-g_{n}^{0}\|\cdot\|\widetilde{g}_{n}^{0}\|,\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(6.5)

Since

\widetilde{\mathfrak{G}}_{0}
is a Riesz basis, then
\|\widetilde{g}_{n}^{0}\|\asymp 1
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Thus, inequality (
6.5
) and inclusion
\left\{{\|f_{n}^{0}-g_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
imply that
\left\{{\|f_{n}^{0}-\widetilde{g}_{n}^{0}\|}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\ell^{p})^{*}
, which means that the normalized root vectors system
\mathfrak{F}_{0}=\{f_{n}^{0}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the operator
L_{U}(0)
is a Bari

-sequence and, in particular, is a Bari

-sequence. Theorem

now implies that the operator

∎

7
Application to a non-canonical string equation

In this section we show the connection of
2\times 2
Dirac type operators with a non-canonical string equation with
u_{xt}
term, and apply our results on Riesz and Bari basis property.

Consider the following non-canonical hyperbolic equation on a complex-valued function
u(x,t)
defined for
x\in[0,1]
and
t\in[0,\infty)
:

u_{tt}-(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})u_{xt}+\beta_{1}\beta_{2}u_{xx}+a_{1}(x)u_{x}+a_{2}(x)u_{t}=0,

(7.1)

with the boundary conditions

u(0,t)=0,\qquad h_{0}u_{x}(0,t)+h_{1}u_{x}(1,t)+h_{2}u_{t}(1,t)=0,\qquad t\in[0,\infty),

(7.2)

and initial conditions

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\qquad u_{t}(x,0)=u_{1}(x),\qquad x\in[0,1].

(7.3)

Here
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
are constants and

\beta_{1}<0<\beta_{2},\quad a_{1},a_{2}\in L^{1}[0,1],\quad h_{0},h_{1},h_{2}\in\mathbb{C},\quad|h_{1}|+|h_{2}|>0.

(7.4)

If
-\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=\rho^{-1}>0
and
h_{0}=0
, the initial-boundary value problem (
7.1
)–(
7.3
) governs the small vibrations of a string of length
1

and density
\rho
with the presence of a damping coefficient
a_{2}(x)
;
the string is fixed at the left end (
x=0
), while the right end (
x=1
) is damped with the coefficient
h_{2}/h_{1}\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. Functions
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
represent the initial position and velocity of the string, respectively.

If
-\beta_{1}\neq\beta_{2}
one can use linear transform of the variables
x
and
t
to reduce it to a classical string equation, but with damping that depends on
t
and non-classical initial and boundary conditions: initial condition will be on a segment non-parallel to the
x
-axis (
t=0
), while boundary conditions will be on the rays non-parallel to the
t
-axis (
x=0
).

Recall that
W^{1,p}[0,1]
,
p\geqslant 1
, denotes the Sobolev space of absolutely continuous functions
with the finite norm

\|f\|_{W^{1,p}[0,1]}^{p}:=\int_{0}^{1}\bigl(|f(x)|^{p}+|f^{\prime}(x)|^{p}\bigr)dx<\infty.

(7.5)

For convenience, we introduce the following notations:

\widetilde{W}^{1,p}[0,1]:=\{f\in W^{1,p}[0,1]:f(0)=0\},\qquad\widetilde{H}^{1}_{0}[0,1]:=\widetilde{W}^{1,2}[0,1],

(7.6)

where
p\in[1,\infty]
.

The non-canonical initial-boundary value problem (
7.1
)–(
7.3
) of a damped string can be transformed into an abstract Cauchy problem in a Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
of the form

\mathfrak{H}:=\widetilde{H}^{1}_{0}[0,1]\times L^{2}[0,1],\qquad

(7.7)

with the inner product

\left\langle{f,g}\right\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}:=\int_{0}^{1}\left(f_{1}^{\prime}(x)\cdot\overline{g_{1}^{\prime}(x)}+f_{2}(x)\cdot\overline{g_{2}(x)}\right)\,dx,

(7.8)

where
f=\col(f_{1},f_{2})
,
g=\col(g_{1},g_{2})\in\mathfrak{H}
.

Now the new representation of the problem (
7.1
)–(
7.2
) reads as follows:

Y^{\prime}(t)=i\mathcal{L}Y(t),\quad Y(t):=\binom{u(\cdot,t)}{u_{t}(\cdot,t)},\quad t\geqslant 0,\qquad Y(0)=\binom{u_{0}}{u_{1}},

(7.9)

where the linear operator
\mathcal{L}:\dom(\mathcal{L})\to\mathfrak{H}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}y=\mathcal{L}\binom{y_{1}}{y_{2}}=-i\,\binom{y_{2}}{-\beta_{1}\beta_{2}y_{1}^{\prime\prime}+(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})y_{2}^{\prime}-a_{1}y_{1}^{\prime}-a_{2}y_{2}},

(7.10)

where
y=\col(y_{1},y_{2})\in\dom(\mathcal{L})
, with

\dom(\mathcal{L})=\{y=\col(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathfrak{H}:y_{1}^{\prime}\in W^{1,1}[0,1],\quad\mathcal{L}y\in\mathfrak{H},\quad h_{0}y_{1}^{\prime}(0)+h_{1}y_{1}^{\prime}(1)+h_{2}y_{2}(1)=0\}.

(7.11)

It is clear from the definition of
\mathcal{L}
and
\dom(\mathcal{L})
that for
y=\col(y_{1},y_{2})\in\dom(\mathcal{L})
we have:
y_{1}\in\widetilde{W}^{1,1}_{0}[0,1]
and
y_{2}\in\widetilde{H}^{1}_{0}[0,1]
. In particular,
y_{1}(0)=y_{2}(0)=0
.

Spectral properties of the operator
\mathcal{L}
play important role in the study of stability of solutions of the corresponding string equation. For example, Riesz basis property of the root vectors system of
\mathcal{L}
guarantees the exponential stability of the corresponding
C_{0}
-semigroup. The Riesz basis property and behavior of the spectrum of the operator
\mathcal{L}
have been studied in numerous papers (see
[
9
,
10
,
44
,
45
,
6
,
14
,
18
,
38
]
and references therein).

Let us show that the operator
\mathcal{L}
is similar to a certain
2\times 2
Dirac type operator
L_{U}(Q)
. Since many spectral properties are preserved under similarity transform, known spectral properties for
2\times 2
Dirac type operators will translate to corresponding properties of the dynamic generator
\mathcal{L}
.

To this end, we need to introduce some notations. Set

B:=\diag(b_{1},b_{2}),\qquad b_{1}:=\beta_{1}^{-1},\quad b_{2}:=\beta_{2}^{-1},

(7.12)

Q(x):=\frac{i}{b_{2}-b_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}0&w(x)\cdot\left(b_{2}^{2}a_{1}(x)+b_{2}a_{2}(x)\right)\\
\frac{-1}{w(x)}\cdot\left(b_{1}^{2}a_{1}(x)+b_{1}a_{2}(x)\right)&0\end{pmatrix},

(7.13)

where

w(x):=w_{1}(x)w_{2}(x),

(7.14)

w_{j}(x):=\exp\left(\frac{b_{1}b_{2}}{b_{2}-b_{1}}\int_{0}^{x}(b_{j}a_{1}(t)+a_{2}(t))dt\right),\qquad x\in[0,1],\quad j\in\{1,2\}.

(7.15)

Note, that
w_{1}(\cdot)
,
w_{2}(\cdot)
are well defined and
Q\in L^{1}([0,1],\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
in view of condition (
7.4
). Finally let

\displaystyle U_{1}(y)

\displaystyle:=y_{1}(0)+y_{2}(0)=0,

(7.16)

\displaystyle U_{2}(y)

\displaystyle:=b_{1}h_{0}y_{1}(0)+b_{2}h_{0}y_{2}(0)+(b_{1}h_{1}+h_{2})w^{-1}_{1}(1)y_{1}(1)+(b_{2}h_{1}+h_{2})w_{2}(1)y_{2}(1)=0,

(7.17)

be boundary conditions for a Dirac operator
L_{U}(Q)
. Here
w_{1}(\cdot)
,
w_{2}(\cdot)
are given by (

Proposition 7.1
.

Operator
\mathcal{L}
is similar to the
2\times 2

with the matrix
B
given by (

Proof.

We will transform the operator
\mathcal{L}
into the desired operator

via series of similarity transformations.

Step 1.
Define

\mathcal{V}_{0}:\mathfrak{H}\to{L^{2}}({[0,1]};\mathbb{C}^{2})\quad\text{as}\quad\mathcal{V}_{0}y:=\binom{y_{1}^{\prime}}{y_{2}},\quad y=\binom{y_{1}}{y_{2}}\in\mathfrak{H}.

(7.18)

Since
\frac{d}{dx}
isometrically maps
\widetilde{H}_{0}^{1}[0,1]=\{f\in W^{1,2}[0,1]:f(0)=0\}
onto

then the operator
\mathcal{V}_{0}

L_{1}y:=\mathcal{V}_{0}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{-1}y=-i\,\binom{y_{2}^{\prime}}{-\beta_{1}\beta_{2}y_{1}^{\prime}+(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})y_{2}^{\prime}-a_{1}y_{1}-a_{2}y_{2}},

(7.19)

where

y=\binom{y_{1}}{y_{2}}\in\dom(L_{1}):=\mathcal{V}_{0}\dom(\mathcal{L})=\{y\in W^{1,1}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2}):\\
L_{1}y\in L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2}),\quad y_{2}(0)=0,\ \ h_{0}y_{1}(0)+h_{1}y_{1}(1)+h_{2}y_{2}(1)=0\},

(7.20)

in view of (

) and definition of
\widetilde{H}_{0}^{1}[0,1]
. Thus, the operator
\mathcal{L}
is similar to the operator

with the domain

ia_{1}(x)&ia_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

(7.21)

Note, that

Next we diagonalize the matrix

1&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\
1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{and so}\quad V_{1}^{-1}=\frac{1}{b_{2}-b_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}-1&b_{2}\\
1&-b_{1}\end{pmatrix},

(7.22)

where

and

are defined in (

V_{1}^{-1}B_{1}V_{1}=\diag(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})=\diag(b_{1}^{-1},b_{2}^{-1})=B^{-1},

V_{1}^{-1}Q_{1}(x)V_{1}=\frac{i}{b_{2}-b_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}b_{2}a_{1}(x)+b_{2}a_{2}(x)&b_{2}^{2}a_{1}(x)+b_{2}a_{2}(x)\\
-b_{1}^{2}a_{1}(x)-b_{1}a_{2}(x)&-b_{1}b_{2}a_{1}(x)-b_{1}a_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}=:Q_{2}(x),\qquad x\in[0,1].

(7.24)

Note, that
Q_{2}\in L^{1}([0,1],\mathbb{C}^{2\times 2})
in view of condition (

). Introducing bounded operator
\mathcal{V}_{1}:y\to V_{1}y
in

, noting that it has a bounded inverse, and taking into account (

) and (

\displaystyle L_{2}y

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{V}_{1}^{-1}L_{2}\mathcal{V}_{1}y=-iV_{1}^{-1}B_{1}V_{1}y^{\prime}+V_{1}^{-1}Q_{1}(x)V_{1}y

\displaystyle=-iB^{-1}y^{\prime}+Q_{2}(x)y,\qquad y\in\mathcal{V}_{1}^{-1}\dom(L_{1})=:\dom(L_{2}),

(7.25)

where

\dom(L_{2})=\{y\in W^{1,1}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2}):\ L_{2}y\in L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2}),\ y_{1}(0)+y_{2}(0)=0,\\
b_{1}h_{0}y_{1}(0)+b_{2}h_{0}y_{2}(0)+(b_{1}h_{1}+h_{2})y_{1}(1)+(b_{2}h_{1}+h_{2})y_{2}(1)=0\},

(7.26)

with account of formula (
7.20
) for the domain
\dom(L_{1})
and the formula (
7.22
) for the matrix
V_{1}
.

Step 3.
On this step we make potential matrix
Q_{2}
to be off-diagonal. To this end, Let
\widetilde{Q}_{2}
be a diagonal of
Q_{2}
, i.e.

\widetilde{Q}_{2}(x):=\frac{i}{b_{2}-b_{1}}\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}b_{2}a_{1}(x)+b_{2}a_{2}(x)&0\\
0&-b_{1}b_{2}a_{1}(x)-b_{1}a_{2}(x)\end{pmatrix}.

Let
V_{2}(\cdot)
be a solution of the initial value problem

-iB^{-1}V_{2}^{\prime}(x)+\widetilde{Q}_{2}(x)V_{2}(x)=0,\qquad V_{2}(0)=I_{2}.

(7.27)

It is easily seen that

V_{2}(x):=\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(x)&0\\
0&w_{2}^{-1}(x)\end{pmatrix},\qquad x\in[0,1],

(7.28)

where
w_{1}(\cdot)
,
w_{2}(\cdot)
are defined in (
7.15
). Let us introduce operator
\mathcal{V}_{2}:y\to V_{2}(x)y
in
L^{2}([0,1];\mathbb{C}^{2})
. Since
a_{1},a_{2}\in L^{1}[0,1]
, the operator
\mathcal{V}_{2}
is bounded and has a bounded inverse. Combining relation (
7.27
), definition (
7.13
) of
Q
and definition (
7.14
) of
w
, we get

\displaystyle L_{3}y

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{V}_{2}^{-1}L_{2}\mathcal{V}_{2}y

\displaystyle=-i[V_{2}(x)]^{-1}B^{-1}V_{2}(x)y^{\prime}+[V_{2}(x)]^{-1}(-iB^{-1}V_{2}^{\prime}(x)+Q_{2}(x)V_{2}(x))y

\displaystyle=-iB^{-1}y^{\prime}+[V_{2}(x)]^{-1}(Q_{2}(x)-\widetilde{Q}_{2}(x))V_{2}(x))y

\displaystyle=-iB^{-1}y^{\prime}+Q(x)y,\qquad y\in\mathcal{V}_{2}^{-1}\dom(L_{2})=:\dom(L_{3}).

(7.29)

It is clear from the definition of
\mathcal{V}_{2}
that
\dom(L_{3})
coincides with
\dom(L_{U}(Q))
defined via (
7.16
)–(
7.17
). Hence
L_{3}=L_{U}(Q)
. Combining all the steps of the proof one concludes that
\mathcal{L}
is similar to
L_{U}(Q)
.
∎

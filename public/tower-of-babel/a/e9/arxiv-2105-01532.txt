Two Families of Unravelled Abstract Regular Polytopes in B n \mathrm{B}_{n}
Robert Nicolaides, Peter Rowley

Abstract
This paper exhibits two families of unravelled abstract regular polytopes in Coxeter groups of type B n \mathrm{B}_{n} . For one family they have rank 4 while the other family has arbitrarily large rank.



1
Introduction

Unravelled abstract regular polytopes were first introduced and studied in
[
4
]
. In that paper, among other things, certain unravelled polytopes for
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(q)\rtimes\langle t\rangle,

t
being the transpose inverse automorphism of
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(q)
, were analyzed. Here we continue investigating unravelled polytopes this time in Coxeter groups of type
\mathrm{B}_{n}
.

The study of abstract regular polytopes may be carried out entirely in a group theoretic environment. The parallel group theoretic concept is that of a C-string. See
[
3
]
for details of this reformulation. For a finite group
G
and
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
a set of involutions is
G
, set
I=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and for
J\subseteq I

G_{J}=\langle t_{j}|j\in J\rangle
, with
G_{\emptyset}=1
by convention. If
J=\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}\}
, then
G_{J}
is sometimes written as
G_{j_{1}\ldots j_{k}}
. A C-string of rank
n
for
G
is a set of involutions
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
in
G
such that

1.

G=\langle t_{1},\ldots t_{n}\rangle

2.

t_{i}
and
t_{j}
commute whenever
|i-j|\geq 2;

and

3.

for
J,K\subseteq I,

G_{J}\cap G_{K}=G_{J\cap K}.

We can now define unravelled C-strings and unravelled abstract regular polytopes.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose
G
is a finite group with
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
a C-string for
G
of rank
n
.

1.

For
N\trianglelefteq G,
set
\overline{G}=G/N
and use
\overline{g}
for the image of
g
in
\overline{G}
,
g\in G
. If either
|\{\overline{t_{1}},\ldots,\overline{t_{n}}\}|<n
or,
|\{\overline{t_{1}},\ldots,\overline{t_{n}}\}|=n
and
\{\overline{t_{1}},\ldots,\overline{t_{n}}\}
is not a C-string for
\overline{G},
we say that
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
is an
N
-unravelled C-string for
G
.

2.

If
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
is an
N
-unravelled
C-string for all non-trivial proper normal
subgroups
N
of
G
, we call
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}

an unravelled C-string for
G
, and refer to the
associated abstract regular polytope as being
unravelled.

Our first theorem, just as for those on
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(q)\rtimes\langle t\rangle,
yields unravelled C-strings of rank 4.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
G=\mathrm{B}_{n}
where
n
is odd and
n\geq 5
. Then
G
has a rank 4 C-string
\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\}
with Schlafli symbol

2n-4
6
4
t_{1}
t_{2}
t_{3}
t_{4}

,

which is unravelled when
n>5.
Further
G_{123}\cong\mathrm{Sym}({n})
and
G_{234}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathrm{Sym}({5}).

Our second theorem, by contrast, gives examples in
\mathrm{B}_{n}
of arbitrary large rank.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
G=\mathrm{B}_{n}
where
n\geq 8,
and set
m=n-4.
Then
G
has a rank
m
C-string
\{t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{m}\}
with Schlafli symbol

12
12
6
3
3
t_{1}
t_{2}
t_{3}
t_{4}
t_{5}
t_{m-1}
t_{m}

.

Further, when
n
is even, this C-string is unravelled.

1.2

2
Preliminary Results

This short section contains the results we need in the two following sections. First of these results is one which identifies the Coxeter groups of type
\mathrm{B}_{n}
. For a set
\Omega=\{1,\ldots,n\},

\mathrm{Sym}({n})=\mathrm{Sym}({\Omega})
denotes the symmetric group of degree
n
defined on
\Omega
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\Omega=\{1,2\ldots,n,n+1,\ldots,2n\}.
Let
\beta_{0}=(1,n+1)
and
\beta_{i}=(i,i+1)(n+i,n+i+1)
for
1\leq i<n.
Then
\langle\beta_{0},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n-1}\rangle
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}_{n}
.

Proof.

See (2.10) of
[
2
]
.
∎

In a similar vein to Lemma
2.1
, we have the well-known characterization of
\mathrm{Sym}({n})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
H
is a group with presentation
\langle r_{1},\ldots,r_{n-1}|(r_{i}r_{j})^{m_{ij}})\rangle
. If
m_{ii}=1
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1,m_{ij}=3
if
|i-j|=1
and
m_{ij}=2
if
|i-j|>1,
then
H\cong\mathrm{Sym}({n})
.

Proof.

See (6.4) of
[
2
]
.
∎

We make frequent use of the next lemma to verify that particular sets of involutions are C-strings.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G=\langle t_{1},\ldots t_{n}\rangle
where for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
we have
|i-j|\geq 2
implies that
(t_{i}t_{j})^{2}=1
and each
t_{i}
an involution. If
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1}\}
and
\{t_{2},\ldots,t_{n}\}
are C-strings for
\langle t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1}\rangle
and
\langle t_{2},\ldots,t_{n}\rangle
, then
\{t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\}
is a C-string for G if

\langle t_{1},\ldots,t_{n-1}\rangle\cap\langle t_{2},\ldots,t_{n}\rangle=\langle t_{2},\ldots,t_{n-1}\rangle.

Proof.

See 2E16(a) in
[
3
]
.
∎

Our group theoretic notation is standard, as given for example in
[
5
]
, with the addition that
\mathrm{Dih}({n})
denotes the dihedral group of order

.

Twisted categorical generic fiber
Hayato Morimura

Abstract
. After extending Orlov’s theorem, we prove specialization of derived equivalence for flat proper families of Azumaya varieties.



1.
Introduction

When working on a problem in
geometry
and
mathematical physics,
one often enters the realm of noncommutative algebraic geometry.
For instance,
algebraic varieties are tied with in general Azumaya varieties via homological projective duality established by Kuznetsov in

[
Kuz07
]
,
which categorifies classical projective duality.
In order to incorporate
B
-fields into homological mirror symmetry,
Kapstin–Orlov suggested to consider the derived category of twisted coherent sheaves

[
KP03
]
.

Noncommutativity of an Azumaya variety

(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})

comes from twisting the structure sheaf

\mathscr{O}_{X}

by a Brauer class

[\alpha_{X}]\in\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(X)
,
which corresponds to the equivalence class

[\mathscr{A}_{X}]\in\operatorname{Br}(X)

via the canonical inclusion of the Brauer group

\operatorname{Br}(X)

to the cohomological Brauer group

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(X)
.
More explicitly,
there is an equivalence

\operatorname{coh}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\simeq\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})

of categories of
coherent sheaves of right
\mathscr{A}_{X}
-modules
and
coherent
\alpha_{X}
-twisted sheaves.
On the other hand,
some
B
-fields define cohomological Brauer classes.

Considering the flexibility of the derived category
which allows us to find relations between nonisomorphic algebraic varieties,
it is natural to ask
whether an Azumaya variety

(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})

has its

geometric realization
,
i.e.,
an ordinary algebraic variety

X

with

D^{b}(X)\simeq D^{b}(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})
.
Via the derived equivalence

X

may be regarded as a resolution of noncommutativity of

(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})
.
The question makes sense for more general noncommutative spaces.
One of
the earliest
and
the most interesting
examples should be the following.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Căl02
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let

f\colon X\to S

be a generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-fold in the sense of

[
Căl00
, Definition 6.1.6]
,

\varpi\colon J\to S

its relative Jacobian
and

\bar{\varpi}\colon\bar{J}\to S

any analytic small resolution of singularities of

J
.
For the complement

U

of the discriminant locus

\Delta\subset S

of

f
,
let

\alpha

be a representative of the element in

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J_{U})

corresponding to the base change

f_{U}\colon X_{U}=X\times_{S}U\to U

and

\bar{\alpha}

its unique extension to

\bar{J}
.
Then there is an exact
S
-linear equivalence

\displaystyle D^{b}(X)\simeq D^{b}(\bar{J},\bar{\alpha}).

This can be regarded as a special case of our main result,
which gives a sort of sufficient condition for flat proper families of Azumaya varieties to be geometrically realized.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let

\tilde{\pi}\colon(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\to(S,\mathscr{A}_{S})

be a flat proper morphism of Azumaya varieties over
a field

\mathbf{k}

of characteristic

0
.
If its generic fiber in the sense of Definition

4.1

is geometrically realized by the generic fiber of some flat proper family,
then so is

\tilde{\pi}

up to shrinking

S
.

Indeed,
suppose that

f\colon X\to S

is any flat projective morphism from a complex
3
-fold
whose generic fiber

X_{\xi}

satisfies

D^{b}(X_{\xi})\simeq D^{b}(J_{\xi},\alpha_{\xi})

for the image

\alpha_{\xi}

of

\alpha

under the canonical map

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J_{U})\to\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J_{\xi})
.
This is the case for the generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-fold from
Theorem
1.1
.
By Corollary

1.2

there is an open subset

V\subset S

and

V
-linear equivalence

D^{b}(X_{V})\simeq D^{b}(J_{V},\alpha_{V})
.
From the argument in

[
Morc
, Section 4]

it follows that

f_{V}

is a generic elliptic
3
-fold with

\omega_{X_{V}}\cong\mathscr{O}_{X_{V}}

whose relative Jacobian is

\varpi_{V}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Via Ogg–Shafarevich theory

[
Căl00
, Section 4]
,
the fibration

f_{V}

corresponds to a coprime power

\alpha^{d}_{V}

to the order of

\alpha_{V}

in

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(J_{V})

[
Morb
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Remark 1.4
.

Since

\bar{J}

is analytic,

\bar{\varpi}

does not have the naive generic fiber.
However,
one can take the categorical generic fiber in the sense of Definition

4.7
.
Extending

[
Mora
]
,
it gives a description of the twisted derived category of the generic fiber as a Verdier quotient.
Here in the setting of
Theorem
1.1
,
by construction one may pass to the generic fiber of

\varpi_{U}
.

Remark 1.5
.

When

f

is originally a generic elliptic Calabi–Yau
3
-fold,
the moduli theoretic argument in

[
Căl02
]

allows us to obtain the derived equivalence for the whole families.
In other words,
among the lifts to

D^{b}(X\times_{S}\bar{J},\operatorname{pr}^{*}_{2}\bar{\alpha})

along the projection to the Verdier quotient of the Fourier–Mukai kernel associated with

D^{b}(X_{\xi})\simeq D^{b}(J_{\xi},\alpha_{\xi})
,
one can find the twisted pseudo-universal sheaf constructed in

[
Căl00
, Section 5]
.

Corollary

1.2

is a direct consequence of the following extension of specialization of derived equivalence established in

[
Mora
]
.

Theorem 1.6

(Corollary
4.18
)
.

Let

\tilde{\pi}\colon(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\to(S,\mathscr{A}_{S}),\tilde{\pi}^{\prime}\colon(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})\to(S,\mathscr{A}_{S})

be flat proper morphisms of Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-varieties.
Assume that
their generic fibers are derived-equivalent.
Then up to shrinking

S

they are
S
-linear derived-equivalent.
In particular,
their closed fibers are derived-equivalent.

Our proof is a simple adaptation of the argument in

[
Mora
]

to twisted case,
for which we need to know
whether any derived equivalence of the generic fibers is of Fourier–Mukai type.
Extending

[
CS07
]

to proper case,
we obtain the following stronger result
which should be of independent interest.

Theorem 1.7

(cf.
[
CS07
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let

(X,\mathscr{A}_{X}),(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})

be smooth proper Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-varieties with

\mathscr{A}_{X}\cong\mathscr{E}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X},\mathscr{A}_{Y}\cong\mathscr{E}_{Y}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{Y}}\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{Y}

for locally free sheaves

\mathscr{E}_{X},\mathscr{E}_{Y}

of finite ranks twisted by

\alpha_{X},\alpha_{Y}
.
If

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X,\alpha_{X})\to D^{b}(Y,\alpha_{Y})

is an exact
\mathbf{k}
-linear functor satisfying

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(Y,\alpha_{Y})}(\Phi(\mathscr{F}),\Phi(\mathscr{G})[l])=0,\ l<0

for any

\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}\in\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})
,
then there exist

P\in D^{b}(X\times Y,\alpha^{-1}_{X}\boxtimes\alpha_{Y})

and
an isomorphism

\Phi\cong\Phi^{X\to Y}_{P}

to the twisted Fourier–Mukai transform with kernel

P
.
Moreover,

P

is uniquely determined up to isomorphism.

This also extends to twisted case Orlov’s theorem

[
Orl97
]

for smooth proper varieties proved by
Olander in

[
Ola
]
.
We could have adapted his argument using point-like objects
whose result would require fully faithfullness of

\Phi
,
which is stronger than the above hypothesis.
Moreover,
we would need a refined version of Gabriel’s theorem

[
CG15
, Theorem 3.5]
,
instead of

[
Per08
, Theorem 1.1]
,
in adapting the final step of the proof of

[
Ola
, Proposition 2]
.
However,
the

pseudo-ample sequence

tacitly introduced in

[
Ola
]

is still useful for our purpose.

The significance of the categorical generic fiber might be clearer
when working with

noncommutative schemes

over

S
,
i.e.,
idempotent-complete stable
\infty
-categories equipped with

\operatorname{Perf}(S)
-actions.
Over characteristic

0
,
we may pass to the corresponding pretriangulated dg categories via

[
Coh
, Corollary 5.7]
.
Also the dg categorical generic fiber

[
Mord
, Definition 1.5]

extends to twisted case,
which we expect to be defined for more general noncommutative schemes.
It would be an interesting problem to extend
Corollary

1.2

to this setting.

2.
Derived categories of smooth proper Azumaya varieties

In this section,
we review
Azumaya varieties,
twisted sheaves
and
their categorical relation
following

[
Kuz06
]

and

[
Căl00
]
.
Then
we adapt to our setting
pseudo-ample sequences tacitly introduced in

[
Ola
]

and
some preparatory results from

[
CS07
]
.
Here
and
in the next section,
one may drop the assumption on

\mathbf{k}

to be of characteristic

0
.

2.1.
Categories of coherent sheaves on Azumaya varieties

Definition 2.1

(
[
Kuz06
, Definition D.1]
)
.

An

Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-variety

is a pair

(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})

of
an algebraic
\mathbf{k}
-variety
and
a sheaf

\mathscr{A}_{X}

of Azumaya algebras over

X
.
A

morphism

of Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-varieties

f\colon(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})

is a pair

(f_{\circ},f_{\mathscr{A}})

of
a morphism

f_{\circ}\colon X\to Y

of algebraic
\mathbf{k}
-varieties
and
a homomorphism

f_{\mathscr{A}}\colon f^{*}_{\circ}\mathscr{A}_{Y}\to\mathscr{A}_{X}

of
\mathscr{O}_{X}
-algebras.

Definition 2.2

(
[
Kuz06
, Definition D.1]
)
.

A morphism of Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-varieties

f\colon(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})

is

strict

if

\mathscr{A}_{X}\cong f^{*}_{\circ}\mathscr{A}_{Y}

and

f_{\mathscr{A}}

is the identity.
A morphism of Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-varieties

f\colon(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})

is an

extension

if

X\cong Y

and

f_{\circ}

is the identity.

Remark 2.3
.

Any morphism

f\colon(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\to(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})

of Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-varieties is canonically decomposed by
an extension

f^{e}

and
a strict morphism

f^{s}

as

\displaystyle(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\xrightarrow{f^{e}}(X,f^{*}_{\circ}\mathscr{A}_{Y})\xrightarrow{f^{s}}(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y}).

We denote by

\operatorname{qch}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})

and

\operatorname{coh}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})

the category of quasicoherent sheaves of right
\mathscr{A}_{X}
-modules
and
its full subcategory of coherent modules.
Up to equivalence,
these are independent of the choice of representatives of the Brauer class

[\mathscr{A}_{X}]\in\operatorname{Br}(X)
.

Definition 2.4

(
[
Căl00
, Definition 1.2.1]
)
.

For a Čech
2
-cocycle

\alpha_{X}=\{\alpha_{ijk}\}_{i,j,k\in I}\in\check{C}^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathscr{O}^{*}_{X})

with respect to an étale cover

\mathfrak{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}

of an algebraic
\mathbf{k}
-variety

X
,
an

\alpha_{X}
-twisted sheaf

is a pair

(\{\mathscr{F}_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{\varphi_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I})

of collections of

\mathscr{O}_{U_{i}}
-modules
and
isomorphisms

\varphi_{ij}\colon\mathscr{F}_{j}|_{U_{ij}}\to\mathscr{F}_{i}|_{U_{ij}}

on

U_{ij}=U_{i}\times_{X}U_{j}

satisfying

\displaystyle\varphi_{ii}=\operatorname{id},\ \varphi_{ji}=\varphi^{-1}_{ij},\ \varphi_{ij}\circ\varphi_{jk}\circ\varphi_{ki}=\alpha_{ijk}\cdot\operatorname{id}

for all

i,j,k\in I
.
It is

quasicoherent

if each

\mathscr{F}_{i}

is quasicoherent as an
\mathscr{O}_{U_{i}}
-module.
A

morphism

of
\alpha_{X}
-twisted sheaves

(\{\mathscr{F}_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{\varphi_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I}),(\{\mathscr{G}_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{\psi_{ij}\}_{i,j\in I})

is a collection of compatible morphisms

f_{i}\colon\mathscr{F}_{i}\to\mathscr{G}_{i}

with

\varphi_{ij},\psi_{ij}
.

Remark 2.5
.

In the above definition,

\mathscr{F}_{i}|_{U_{ij}}

denote the pullbacks instead of restrictions.

We denote by

\operatorname{qch}(X,\alpha_{X})

and

\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})

the category of quasicoherent
\alpha_{X}
-twisted sheaves on

X

and
its full subcategory of coherent sheaves.
Up to equivalence,
these are independent of the choices of
étale covers

\mathfrak{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}

and
representatives of the cohomological Brauer class

[\alpha_{X}]\in\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(X)=H^{2}_{\text{\'{e}t}}(X,\mathscr{O}^{*}_{X})

[
Căl00
, Lemma 1.2.3, 1.2.8]
.

Given an Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-variety

(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})
,
the Brauer class

[\mathscr{A}_{X}]

is represented by some Čech
2
-cocycle

\alpha_{X}

via the canonical inclusion

\operatorname{Br}(X)\subset\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(X)

from

[
Căl00
, Theorem 1.1.8]
.
Then by

[
Căl00
, Theorem 1.3.5]

there exists a locally free
\alpha_{X}
-twisted sheaf

\mathscr{E}_{X}

of finite rank with

\mathscr{A}_{X}\cong\underline{\operatorname{End}}(\mathscr{E}_{X})\cong\mathscr{E}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}
.
For the rest of the paper,
we fix such

\alpha_{X}

and

\mathscr{E}_{X}
.
The following result allows us to categorically pass from Azumaya varieties to twisted sheaves.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
Căl00
, Theorem 1.3.7]
)
.

The functors

\displaystyle\begin{gathered}(-)\otimes_{\mathscr{A}_{X}}\mathscr{E}_{X}\colon\operatorname{coh}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\to\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X}),\ (-)\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\colon\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})\to\operatorname{coh}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\end{gathered}

give mutually inverse
\mathbf{k}
-linear equivalences.

Remark 2.7
.

The above result holds without the coherent assumption.
Unless

X

is smooth quasiprojective over

\mathbf{k}
,
in general

\operatorname{Br}(X)

is strictly smaller than

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(X)
.
For any Brauer class in the complement

\operatorname{Br}^{\prime}(X)\setminus\operatorname{Br}(X)

there is no locally free twisted sheaf of finite rank representing the class.
Hence categories of coherent twisted sheaves

\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})

cover more variants of

\operatorname{coh}(X)

than that of coherent right modules

\operatorname{coh}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})

over sheaves of Azumaya algebras.

2.2.
Pseudo-ample sequences

We denote by

D^{b}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X}),D^{b}(X,\alpha_{X})

the derived categories of

\operatorname{coh}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X}),\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})
.
Under the assumptions imposed later,
all usual
derived functors
and
their relations
will have their twisted counterparts

[
Căl00
, Chapter 2]
.
We use the same symbols to denote derived functors
unless otherwise specified.

In the following three lemmas,
we assume

\dim X\geq 1
.
Take an affine open cover

X=\bigcup^{M}_{i=1}U_{i}

such that
the complements

D_{i}=X\setminus U_{i}

are proper nontrivial closed subscheme with reduced induced scheme structures for all

i
.
Consider the system of sheaves

(2.1)

\displaystyle\cdots\subset\mathscr{O}_{X}(-n_{i}D_{i})\subset\mathscr{O}_{X}(-(n_{i}-1)D_{i})\subset\cdots\subset\mathscr{O}_{X}(-D_{i}).

Lemma 2.8

(cf.
[
Ola
, Lemma A]
)
.

For any

\mathscr{F}\in\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})

there are surjections

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{i}\mathscr{O}_{X}(-n_{i}D_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}_{X}\to\mathscr{F}

when

n_{i}

are sufficiently large.
Moreover,
these are compatible with the system

(
2.1
)
.
Namely,
for any

m_{i}\geq n_{i}

the induced morphism

\bigoplus_{i}\mathscr{O}_{X}(-m_{i}D_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}_{X}\to\mathscr{F}

remains surjective.

Proof.

Choose a surjection

\varphi_{i}\colon\mathscr{O}^{\oplus r_{i}}_{U_{i}}\to(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F})|_{U_{i}}
.
By the formula

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{i}}(\mathscr{O}^{\oplus r_{i}}_{U_{i}},(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F})|_{U_{i}})=\operatorname{colim}_{n_{i}}\operatorname{Hom}_{X}(\mathscr{O}_{X}(-n_{i}D_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}},\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}),

for sufficiently large

n_{i}

there is a morphism

\mathscr{O}_{X}(-n_{i}D_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}}\to\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{F}

which coincides with

\varphi_{i}

when restricted to

U_{i}
.
∎

Lemma 2.9

(cf.
[
Ola
, Lemma B]
)
.

For any

F\in D^{b}(X,\alpha_{X})

with

\mathcal{H}^{0}(F)\cong 0

and
morphism

\bigoplus_{i}\mathscr{O}_{X}(-n_{i}D_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}_{X}\to F
,
there is

N\in\mathbb{N}

such that
when

m_{i}\geq N

the induced morphism

\bigoplus_{i}\mathscr{O}_{X}(-m_{i}D_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}_{X}\to F

becomes trivial.

Proof.

The claim follows from

\mathcal{H}^{i}((\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}F)|_{U_{i}})=(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathcal{H}^{i}(F))|_{U_{i}}

and
the spectral sequence

(2.2)

\displaystyle E^{p,q}_{2}=\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{i}}(\mathscr{O}^{\oplus r_{i}}_{U_{i}},\mathcal{H}^{q}((\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}F)|_{U_{i}})[p])\Rightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{i}}(\mathscr{O}^{\oplus r_{i}}_{U_{i}},(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}F)|_{U_{i}}[p+q]).

∎

Remark 2.10
.

In

[
Ola
, Lemma B]

the author assumed the vanishing of

\mathcal{H}^{j}(F)

also for

j>0
,
which is irrelevant.
Indeed,
whenever

q>0

the left term of
(
2.2
)
does not contribute to

\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{i}}(\mathscr{O}^{\oplus r_{i}}_{U_{i}},(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}F)|_{U_{i}})
,
as it becomes a negative Ext-group between coherent sheaves.
Moreover,
whenever

p>0

the left term of
(
2.2
)
does not contribute to

\operatorname{Hom}_{U_{i}}(\mathscr{O}^{\oplus r_{i}}_{U_{i}},(\mathscr{E}^{\vee}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}F)|_{U_{i}})
,
as

U_{i}

are affine.

Although the last one below does not play any role in the proof of
Theorem
1.6

nor
Theorem
1.7
,
we include it here for completeness.

Lemma 2.11

(cf.
[
Ola
, Lemma C]
)
.

For any

\mathscr{F}\in\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})

there is

N\in\mathbb{N}

such that
when

n_{i}\geq N

we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})}(\mathscr{F},\bigoplus_{i}\mathscr{O}_{X}(-n_{i}D_{i})^{\oplus r_{i}}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}_{X})=0.

Proof.

It suffices to show the vanishing
when the target is

\mathscr{O}_{X}(-nD)\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}_{X}

for
a nontrivial effective divisor

D

on

X

and
sufficiently large

n
.
By
Lemma
2.8

we may assume

\mathscr{F}

ro be

\mathscr{O}_{X}(-D^{\prime})\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{E}_{X}

for some effective divisor

D^{\prime}
.
Hence it suffices to show the vanishing of

H^{0}(X,\mathscr{O}_{X}(D^{\prime}-nD)\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\mathscr{A}_{X})
.
Then the argument in

[
Ola
, Lemma C]

carries over.
∎

2.3.
Some preparatory results

Lemma 2.12

(cf.
[
CS07
, Remark 2.1(i)]
)
.

Let

(X,\alpha_{X})

be a smooth proper Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-variety.
Then

\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})

has homological dimension

\dim X
.

Proof.

By Serre duality

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G})

vanishes for any

i>\dim X

and

\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}\in\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})
.
Then the claim follows from

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ext}^{\dim X}_{\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})}(\mathscr{E}_{X},\mathscr{E}_{X}\otimes_{\mathscr{O}_{X}}\omega_{X})\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{0}_{\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})}(\mathscr{E}_{X},\mathscr{E}_{X})^{\vee}\neq 0.

∎

Lemma 2.13

(cf.
[
CS07
, Remark 2.1(ii)]
)
.

Let

(X,\mathscr{A}_{X}),(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})

be smooth proper Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-varieties
and

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X,\alpha_{X})\to D^{b}(Y,\alpha_{Y})

an exact
\mathbf{k}
-linear functor.
Then

\Phi

admits
left
and
right
adjoints

\Phi^{L},\Phi^{R}
.

Proof.

By

[
Per
, Theorem 1.3]

any cohomological functor of finite type is representable.
Hence for any

G\in D^{b}(Y,\alpha_{Y})

the functor

\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(Y,\alpha_{Y})}(\Phi(-),G)

is representable by a unique

F\in D^{b}(X,\alpha_{X})
.
Setting

\Psi^{R}(G)=F
,
via Yoneda lemma we obtain a functor
which is right adjoint to

\Psi
.
Since by
Lemma
2.12

all Hom spaces of

D^{b}(X,\alpha_{X}),D^{b}(Y,\alpha_{Y})

are finite dimensional,

\Phi^{L}=S^{-1}_{X}\circ\Psi^{R}\circ S_{Y}

gives a left adjoint to

\Psi
.
∎

Lemma 2.14

(cf.
[
CS07
, Proposition 2.4]
,
[
Ola
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let

(X,\mathscr{A}_{X}),(Y,\mathscr{A}_{Y})

be smooth proper Azumaya
\mathbf{k}
-varieties
and

\Phi\colon D^{b}(X,\alpha_{X})\to D^{b}(Y,\alpha_{Y})

an exact
\mathbf{k}
-linear functor.
Then

\Phi

is bounded,
i.e.,
there exists

m\in\mathbb{N}

satisfying

\mathcal{H}^{i}(\Phi(\mathscr{F}))=0

for any

\mathscr{F}\in\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})

and

i\notin[-m,m]
.

Proof.

Consider the homological functor

\operatorname{Hom}_{D^{b}(Y,\alpha_{Y})}(\mathscr{E}_{Y},\Phi(-))
.
Since by

[
Per
, Proposition 25]

the triangulated category

D^{b}(X,\alpha)

has a strong generator,
the argument in

[
SP
, Tag 0FZ8]

shows that

Lemma 2.15

\mathbf{k}

Proof.

The locally free

the collection

Remark 2.16

When

Lemma 2.17
.

Let

(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})

4.4.
The categorical generic fiber

Now,

Definition 4.7

The

of

\displaystyle D^{b}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})/\operatorname{Ker}(\bar{\iota}^{*}_{\xi}),

\operatorname{Ker}(\bar{\iota}^{*}_{\xi})

\mathbf{k}
-linear functor

\bar{\iota}^{*}_{\xi}\colon D^{b}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\to D^{b}(X_{\xi},\bar{\iota}^{*}_{\xi,\circ}\mathscr{A}_{X})
.

Remark 4.8

Another candidate for the categorical generic fiber of

\tilde{\pi}

is the Verdier quotient

\displaystyle D^{b}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})/\operatorname{Ker}(\tilde{\iota}^{*}_{\xi}).

Recall that

\tilde{\iota}_{\xi}\colon(\operatorname{Spec}K,\iota^{*}_{\xi,\circ}\mathscr{A}_{S})\to(S,\mathscr{A}_{S})

is the strict morphism canonically induced by

\iota_{\xi}
.
Also here,

Lemma 4.9
.

Let

Z\subset X

be a closed
\mathbf{k}
-subvariety with complement

induces an exact

-linear equivalence

\displaystyle D^{b}(U,j^{*}_{U,\circ}\mathscr{A}_{X})\simeq D^{b}(U,j^{*}_{U}\alpha_{X}).

Proof.

By

[

, Proposition 4.3]

the pullback functor

induces an equivalence

\displaystyle\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})/\operatorname{coh}_{Z}(X,\alpha_{X})\simeq\operatorname{coh}(U,j^{*}_{U}\alpha_{X}).

As any

is supported on

so is

\displaystyle\operatorname{coh}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})/\operatorname{coh}_{Z}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})\simeq\operatorname{coh}(X,\alpha_{X})/\operatorname{coh}_{Z}(X,\alpha_{X}).

\displaystyle\operatorname{coh}(U,j^{*}_{U,\circ}\mathscr{A}_{X})\simeq\operatorname{coh}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})/\operatorname{coh}_{Z}(X,\mathscr{A}_{X})

The point is the fact that

as tacitly explained at the end of the proof of

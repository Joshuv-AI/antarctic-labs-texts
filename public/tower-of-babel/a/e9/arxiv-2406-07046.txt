A non-vanishing conjecture for cotangent bundles on elliptic surfaces
Haesong Seo

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the non-vanishing conjecture for cotangent bundles on isotrivial elliptic surfaces. Combined with the result by Höring and Peternell, it completely solves the question for surfaces with Kodaira dimension at most 1 1 .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification

14E05, 14J27.

1.
Introduction

The non-vanishing conjecture is one of the main ingredients in the minimal model program, which is stated as follows: if
X
is a smooth projective variety with
K_{X}
pseudoeffective, then
H^{0}(X,mK_{X})\neq 0
for some
m>0
.
Höring and Peternell
[
9
]
suggested its generalization to the cotangent bundles; recall that a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
is pseudoeffective (resp. big) if the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})}(1)
on the projectivization
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E})
is pseudoeffective (resp. big).

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety, and let
1\leq q\leq\dim X
.
If
\Omega_{X}^{q}
is pseudoeffective, then
H^{0}(X,S^{m}\Omega_{X}^{q})\neq 0
for some
m>0
.

The corresponding version for tangent bundles on surfaces has been widely studied.
For example, Höring, Liu and Shao
[
8
]
proved that for a smooth del Pezzo surface
S
, the tangent bundle
T_{S}
is pseudoeffective (resp. big) if and only if the degree
d=K_{S}^{2}
is at least
4
(resp. at least
5
).
Also, Höring and Peternell
[
10
]
provided a splitting structure of the tangent bundle of a smooth non-uniruled projective surface
S
when
T_{S}
is pseudoeffective.
Jia, Lee and Zhong
[
11
]
showed that if
S
is a smooth non-uniruled projective surface, then
T_{S}
is pseudoeffective if and only if
S
is minimal and
c_{2}(S)=0
.
In particular, the non-vanishing conjecture for tangent bundles holds for del Pezzo surfaces (cf.
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
) and for non-uniruled surfaces (cf.
[
11
, Corollary 1.3]
).
In addition, the tangent bundle on a surface of general type is not pseudoeffective by
[
11
, Proposition 3.2]
.

On the other hand, not much is known for cotangent bundles.
Höring and Peternell
[
9
]
showed that Conjecture
1.1
partially holds for a smooth projective surface
S
with
\kappa(S)\leq 1
, but the result remains open when
S
admits an isotrivial elliptic fibration.
Cao and Höring
[
5
]
proved that if a smooth projective variety
X
admits an abelian fibration
f:X\rightarrow C
onto a curve, then Conjecture
1.1
holds if
f_{\ast}\Omega_{X}^{1}
has rank
1
, which corresponds to the non-isotrivial one for elliptic surfaces.
For a surface
S
of general type, the Bogomolov vanishing theorem tells us that
\Omega_{S}^{1}
is big if
c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}>0
, but little is known for the case
c_{1}^{2}-c_{2}\leq 0
.

In this paper, we prove Conjecture
1.1
for the remaining case of isotrivial elliptic surfaces.
Together with
[
9
, Proposition 5.4]
, it fully answers the question for surfaces
S
with
\kappa(S)\leq 1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f:S\rightarrow B
be a relatively minimal isotrivial elliptic surface.
If
\Omega_{S}^{1}
is pseudoeffective, then
H^{0}(S,S^{m}\Omega_{S}^{1})\neq 0
for some
m>0
.

Our main theorem can be proven by applying
[
9
, Proposition 5.2 and 4.6]
to the following technical lemma:

Theorem 1.3

(cf.
[
9
, Theorem 6.7]
)
.

Let
f:S\rightarrow B
be a relatively minimal isotrivial elliptic surface.
Let
D=\sum_{b\in B}[f^{-1}(b)-f^{-1}(b)_{\mathrm{red}}]
, where
f^{-1}(b)_{\mathrm{red}}
is the reduction of the fibre
f^{-1}(b)
.
If
\Omega_{S}^{1}
is pseudoeffective, so is
f^{\ast}\Omega_{B}^{1}(D)
.

For a given relatively minimal elliptic surface
f:S\rightarrow B
, let
\lambda^{f}(B)
be the Iitaka dimension of the
\mathbb{Q}
-line bundle
K_{B}+\sum_{i=1}^{s}(1-\frac{1}{\nu_{i}})a_{i}
, where
f
has multiple fibres over
a_{i}\in B
with multiplicity
\nu_{i}
.
Notably, a (relatively minimal) isotrivial elliptic surface
f:S\rightarrow B
with
\lambda^{f}(B)=0
and that is not almost smooth has only one nontrivial symmetric differential (cf.
[
14
, Section 4, Table III and (H)]
).

In view of
[
9
, Lemma A.1]
, one can characterize the pseudoeffectivity of the cotangent bundle on an elliptic surface:

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
f:S\rightarrow B
be a smooth elliptic surface.
Then
\Omega_{S}^{1}
is pseudoeffective if and only if the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S)
is infinite.

The ‘if’ direction is due to
[
9
, Lemma A.1]
.
For the converse, suppose that
\Omega_{S}^{1}
is pseudoeffective.
We may assume that
f
is relatively minimal and not almost smooth.
Theorem
1.3
, as well as
[
9
, Proposition 5.4]
, states that either
g(B)\geq 1
or
f
has at least three multiple fibres.
Thus, by
[
6
, Theorem IV.9.12]
, there is a ramified covering
B^{\prime}\rightarrow B
that induces a finite étale cover
S^{\prime}\rightarrow S
such that the elliptic fibration
S^{\prime}\rightarrow B^{\prime}
has no orbifold divisor.
Now
\pi_{1}(S^{\prime})\simeq\pi_{1}(B^{\prime})
is infinite as
g(B^{\prime})\geq 1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.3
basically uses the same idea as that of
[
9
, Theorem 6.7]
.
However, to deal with singular fibres of types
II
,
III
and
IV
, we need to work with a birational model
S^{\prime}
of
S
which might not be relatively minimal.
Using an explicit computation, we analyze the local obstruction for symmetric differentials on
S^{\prime}
in terms of types of singular fibres.
Then we use the fact from
[
9
, Proposition 4.1]
that the pseudoeffectivity of
\Omega_{S}^{1}
is equivalent to that of
\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}
, which completes the proof.

Elliptic surfaces

We will work over
\mathbb{C}
and follow
[

for basic notations.
All the varieties are assumed to be reduced and irreducible.

An

(or an

We typically define elliptic surfaces to be

3.
The non-vanishing conjecture for cotangent bundles

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
.
Let
f:S\rightarrow B
be a relatively minimal isotrivial elliptic surface.
Write

D=\sum_{b\in B}[f^{-1}(b)-f^{-1}(b)_{\mathrm{red}}]=\sum_{i=1}^{s}(\nu_{i}-1)F_{i}+D_{0},

where
F_{i}
is the reduction of a multiple fibre,
\nu_{i}
is the multiplicity of
F_{i}
and
D_{0}
is the non-multiple, non-reduced part.
By Zariski’s lemma
[
2
, Lemma III.8.2]
, the intersection matrix of
D_{0}
is negative definite.
Thus, by
[
4
, Lemma 14.10]
,
f^{\ast}\Omega_{B}^{1}(D)
is pseudoeffective if and only if
f^{\ast}\Omega_{B}^{1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{S}(\sum_{i=1}^{s}(\nu_{i}-1)F_{i})
is pseudoeffective.

Assume that
f^{\ast}\Omega_{B}^{1}(D)
is not pseudoeffective, so that
B\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{s}(1-\frac{1}{\nu_{i}})<2
.
Under the setting (
2.1
), let
Z_{\ast}\subset C
be the set of points under which fibres are singular of type
\ast\in\{I_{0}^{\ast}
,
II
,
II^{\ast}
,
III
,
III^{\ast}
,
IV
,
IV^{\ast}\}
.
Let
e_{\ast}
be the ramification index of the quotient map
\pi:C\rightarrow B
at a point
x\in Z_{\ast}
, namely

(3.1)

e_{\ast}=|G_{x}|=\begin{cases}2,&\text{if }\ast=I_{0}^{\ast},\\
6,&\text{if }\ast=II\text{ or }II^{\ast},\\
4,&\text{if }\ast=III\text{ or }III^{\ast},\\
3,&\text{if }\ast=IV\text{ or }IV^{\ast}.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.1

(cf.
[
9
, Lemma 6.4]
)
.

Under the above setting, we have

(3.2)

\sum(e_{\ast}-1)|Z_{\ast}|\geq 2g(C)-1.

Proof.

By Riemann-Hurwitz formula, we have

2g(C)-2=d(2g(B)-2)+\sum_{x\in C}(e_{x}-1)

where
d=\deg(\pi)
and
e_{x}
is the ramification index at
x
.
Note that
e_{x}=|G_{x}|
.
If the stabilizer group
G_{x}
of
x\in C
acts on
E
by translation, then its order coincides with the multiplicity of the multiple fibre of
f
under
x
.
This property holds for every point in the orbit
G.x
.
It follows that

2g(C)-2=d(2g(B)-2)+\sum(e_{\ast}-1)|Z_{\ast}|+\sum_{i=1}^{s}d\left(1-\frac{1}{\nu_{i}}\right).

Since
B\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{s}(1-\frac{1}{\nu_{i}})<2
, this yields the desired inequality.
∎

3.1.
Logarithmic symmetric differentials

First, we will address the singular fibres of type
I_{0}^{\ast}
,
II
,
III
and
IV
.
Consider
x\in Z_{III}
, and write
G_{x}=\langle g\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}
.
Choose a small disc
x\in\Delta\subset C
such that
(C\times E)/G
is locally isomorphic to
(\Delta\times E)/G_{x}
.
Replacing the origin of
E
if necessary, the fixed points of
g
are
p_{0}=(x,[0])
and
p_{1}=(x,[\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}i])
; the fixed point of
g^{2}
is
p_{2}=(x,[\frac{1}{2}])
.
The quotient singularities of
(\Delta\times E)/G_{x}
at
q_{\nu}=q(p_{\nu})
for
\nu=0,1
are
A_{4,1}
-singularities, so they are resolved by
(-4)
-curves
\Theta_{\nu}
; that of
q_{2}=q(p_{2})
is an
A_{1}
-singularity, so it is resolved by a
(-2)
-curve
\Theta_{2}
.
Let
\Theta\subset S^{\prime}|_{\Delta}
be the proper transform of the central fibre of
(\Delta\times E)/G_{x}
under
x
.
As shown by Figure
2.1
, the
f^{\prime}
-fibre under
x
is
4\Theta+\Theta_{0}+\Theta_{1}+2\Theta_{2}
whose intersection numbers are

(\Theta^{2})=-1,\quad(\Theta_{0}^{2})=(\Theta_{1}^{2})=-4,\quad(\Theta_{2}^{2})=-2,\quad(\Theta.\Theta_{\nu})=1.

To calculate the symmetric differentials on the surface
S^{\prime}|_{\Delta}
, we first determine those admitting (at most) logarithmic poles along
\Theta_{\nu}
.
Let
E_{0}=\Theta_{0}+\Theta_{1}+\Theta_{2}
. Define

\mathcal{B}_{\mathbf{h}}=S^{m}(\Omega_{S^{\prime}}^{1}(\log E_{0}))(-\mathbf{h}\cdot E_{0})

where
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{3}
and
\mathbf{h}\cdot E_{0}=\sum_{\nu=0}^{2}h_{\nu}\Theta_{\nu}
.

(cf.

For

if and only if

.

\nu=2

\mathrm{ord}_{\Theta_{2}}

.

.

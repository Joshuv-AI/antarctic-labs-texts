Asymmetric Self-interacting Dark Matter via Dirac Leptogenesis
Manoranjan Dutta, Nimmala Narendra, Narendra Sahu, Sujay Shil

Abstract
The nature of neutrinos, whether Dirac or Majorana, is hitherto not known. Assuming that the neutrinos are Dirac, which needs B − L B-L to be an exact symmetry, we make an attempt to explain the observed proportionality between the relic densities of dark matter (DM) and baryonic matter in the present Universe i . e . , Ω DM ≈ 5 ​ Ω B {\it i.e.,}\,\,\Omega_{\rm DM}\approx 5\,\Omega_{\rm B} . We extend the Standard Model (SM) by introducing heavy scalar doublets X i , i = 1 , 2 X_{i},i=1,2 and η \eta , two singlet scalars Φ \Phi and Φ ′ \Phi^{\prime} , a vector-like Dirac fermion χ \chi representing the DM and three right-handed neutrinos ν R i , i = 1 , 2 , 3 \nu_{R_{i}},i=1,2,3 . Assuming B − L B-L is an exact symmetry of the early Universe, the CP-violating out-of-equilibrium decay of heavy scalar doublets: X i , i = 1 , 2 X_{i},i=1,2 to the SM lepton doublet L L and the right-handed neutrino ν R \nu_{R} , generate equal and opposite B − L B-L asymmetry among left ( ν L \nu_{L} ) and right ( ν R \nu_{R} )-handed neutrinos. We ensure that ν L − ν R \nu_{L}-\nu_{R} equilibration does not occur until below the electroweak (EW) phase transition during which a part of the lepton asymmetry gets converted to dark matter asymmetry through a dimension eight operator, which conserves B − L B-L symmetry and remains in thermal equilibrium above sphaleron decoupling temperature. A part of the remaining B − L B-L asymmetry then gets converted to a net B-asymmetry through EW-sphalerons which are active at a temperature above 100 GeV. To alleviate the small-scale anomalies of Λ \Lambda CDM, we assume the DM ( χ \chi ) to be self-interacting via a light mediator Φ \Phi , which not only depletes the symmetric component of the DM, but also paves a way to detect the DM at terrestrial laboratories through Φ − H \Phi-H mixing, where H H is the SM Higgs doublet.

Introduction

It is presumed that the early Universe has gone through a period of exponential expansion called inflation to solve
the cosmological problems. At the end of inflation, the Universe is reheated to give rise to a thermal bath with reheating
temperature
T_{R}\gtrsim 4

SU(3)_{c}\times SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}

dark matter, dark energy, non-zero but small neutrino mass etc.

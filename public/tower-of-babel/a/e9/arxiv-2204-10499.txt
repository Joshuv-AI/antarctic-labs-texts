Abstract
A sequence ( x n ) (x_{n}) in a lattice-normed space ( X , p , E ) (X,p,E) is statistical p p -convergent to x ∈ X x\in X if there exists a statistical p p -decreasing sequence q ​ ↓ st p ⁡ 0 q\stpd 0 with an index set K K such that δ ⁡ ( K ) = 1 \delta(K)=1 and p ⁡ ( x n k − x ) ≤ q n k p(x_{n_{k}}-x)\leq q_{n_{k}} for every n k ∈ K n_{k}\in K . This convergence has been investigated recently for ( X , p , E ) = ( E , | ⋅ | , E ) (X,p,E)=(E,|\cdot|,E) under the name of statistical order convergence and under the name of statistical multiplicative order convergence, and also, for taking E E as a locally solid Riesz space under the names statistically unbounded τ \tau -convergence and statistically multiplicative convergence. In this paper, we study the general properties of statistical p p -convergence.

Introduction

statistical
p

[

Riesz space that was introduced by F. Riesz in
[
17

As an active area of research, statistical convergence is a generalization of the ordinary convergence of a real sequence, and the idea of statistical convergence was firstly introduced by Zygmund

Definition 2.1
.

A real-valued vector space
E
with a partial order relation ”
\leq
” on
E
(i.e. it is an antisymmetric, reflexive and transitive relation) is called ordered vector space whenever, for every
x,y\in E
, we have

(a)

x\leq y
implies
x+z\leq y+z
for all
z\in E
,

(b)

x\leq y
implies
\lambda x\leq\lambda y
for every
0\leq\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

An ordered vector space
E
is called
Riesz space
or
vector lattice
if, for any two vectors
x,y\in E
, the infimum and the supremum

x\wedge y=\inf\{x,y\}\ \ \text{and}\ \ x\vee y=\sup\{x,y\}

exist in
E
, respectively. A Riesz space is called
Dedekind complete
if every nonempty bounded above subset has a supremum (or, equivalently, whenever every nonempty bounded below subset has an infimum). For an element
x
in a Riesz spaces
E
,
the positive part
,
the negative part
, and
module
of
x
, respectively

x^{+}:=x\vee 0,\ \ \ x^{-}:=(-x)\vee 0\ \ and\ \ |x|:=x\vee(-x).

In the present paper, the vertical bar
|\cdot|
of elements of the Riesz spaces will stand for the module of the given elements. A Riesz space
E
is called
Archimedean
whenever
\frac{1}{n}x\downarrow 0
holds in
E
for each
x\in E_{+}
. Every Riesz space need not be Archimedean. To see this, we give the following example.

Example 2.2
.

Consider the Riesz space
E:=\mathbb{R}^{2}
with lexicographical ordering:
(x_{1},x_{2})\leq(y_{1},y_{2})
if and only if
x_{1}<y_{1}
or
x_{1}=y_{1}
and
x_{2}\leq y_{2}
. Then
E
is not Archimedean because, for the positive element
(1,1)\in E
, we have
\frac{1}{n}(1,1)\downarrow
, but
\frac{1}{n}(1,1)\neq 0
.

Unless otherwise stated, we assume that all vector lattices are real and Archimedean. A sequence
(x_{n})
in a Riesz space
E
is said to be
increasing
whenever
x_{1}\leq x_{2}\leq\cdots
, and
decreasing
if
x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\cdots
. Then we denote them by
x_{n}\uparrow
and
x_{n}\downarrow
respectively. Moreover, if
x_{n}\uparrow
and
\sup x_{n}=x
then we write
x_{n}\uparrow x
. Similarly, if
x_{n}\downarrow
and
\inf x_{n}=x
then we write
x_{n}\downarrow x
. Then we call that
(x_{n})
is increasing or decreasing as monotonic.

One of the fundamental and crucial concepts in the study of Riesz space is the order convergence (cf.
[
16

Definition 2.3

A sequence

Definition 2.4

Let

If, in addition,

The lattice norm

and
p(x_{k})=e_{k}
for
k=1,2
. While dealing with

Example 2.5
.

Let

. Then
(X,\|\cdot\|,{\mathbb{R}})
is an
LNS
.

Example 2.6
.

Let

be a Riesz space. Then
(X,|\cdot|,X)
is an

.

.

Let
(X,p,E)

(x_{n})
in

holds in

An

Now, we recall some basic properties of the concepts related to statistical convergence. Consider a set
K

\delta(K):=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\left|\left\{k\leq n:k\in K\right\}\right|,

S-\lim x_{k}=L

Let

(b)

q_{n}\std 0

3
Statistical
p
-convergence

In this section, we introduce the statistical convergence on
LNS
s. Some notions and results of this section are direct analogies of well-known facts of the theory of normed lattices. The notion of statistical monotony was first introduced in
[
21
]
for real sequences. We extend the notions of statistical monotony and statistical convergence to statistical
p
-version of them in
LNS
s.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,p,E)
be an
LNS
and
(x_{n})
be a sequence in
X
. Then
(x_{n})
is said to be

(1)

statistical
p
-decreasing to
0
if there exists a set
K=\{n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\delta(K)=1
and
p(x_{n_{k}})\downarrow 0
on
K
, and so, we abbreviate it as
x_{n}\stpd 0
,

(2)

statistical
p
-convergence to
x
if there exists a sequence
q_{n}\stpd 0
in
X
with a set
K=\{n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots\}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\delta(K)=1
and
p(x_{n_{k}}-x)\leq q_{n_{k}}
for all
n_{k}\in K
, and so, we write
x_{n}\stpc x
.

Note that every
p
-convergent sequence is statistical
p
-convergent to its
p
-limit. However, the converse need not be true in general. To illustrate this, we give the following example.

Example 3.2
.

Consider the Riesz space
E:=c
of all convergent real sequences. Then define a norm on
E
by
p(x):=|x|+(\lim_{n\to\infty}|x_{n}|)\cdot\mathbb{1}

for every
x\in E
, where
\mathbb{1}
denotes the sequence identically equal to
1
. Thus,
(E,p,E)
is an
LNRS
. Now, take a sequence
(x_{n})
in
E
denoted by
x_{n}:=(x_{k}^{n})=(x_{1}^{n},x_{2}^{n},\cdots)\in E
such that

x_{k}^{n}:=\begin{cases}m,&k=m^{3}\ and\ k=n\\
0,&k=m^{3}\ and\ k\neq n\\
\frac{1}{k},&k\neq m^{3}\ and\ k=n\\
0,&k\neq m^{3}\ and\ k\neq n\end{cases}

for
n,k,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Next, take another sequence
q_{n}:=(q_{k}^{n})
in
E
denoted by
q_{k}^{n}:=m
whenever
k=m^{3}
and
k=n
, and otherwise, denoted by
q_{k}^{n}:=\frac{1}{k}
. Then it is clear that
q_{n_{k}}\downarrow 0
on the index set
J:=\{n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}:\forall m\in\mathbb{N},\ n_{j}\neq m^{3}\}
. Now, take
x_{k_{j}}
as
y_{k}^{n}
whenever
k\neq m^{3}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Also, we have

p(x_{n_{j}})=p(y_{k}^{n})=|y_{k}^{n}|+(\lim_{n\to\infty}|y_{k}^{n}|)\cdot\mathbb{1}=y_{k}^{n}\leq q_{n_{j}}

for all
n_{j}\in J
. Therefore, we get
x_{n}\stpc 0
in
(E,p,E)
. On the other hand,
(x_{n})
is not
p
-bounded, and so, it is not
p
-convergent sequence.

Remark 3.3
.

Consider an
LNRS

(E,|\cdot|,E)
for an arbitrary Riesz space
E
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

Statistical order decreasing and statistical
p
-decreasing of sequences coincide.

(ii)

Statistically order convergence and statistical
p
-convergence agree.

In the following examples, we give some relations between the classical statistical convergences and statistical

Example 3.4

Take the

(\mathbb{R},|\cdot|,\mathbb{R})

because order convergence implies the convergence in

Example 3.5

Let
(X,\lVert\cdot\rVert)
be a normed space. Then statistical

-convergence of sequence implies statistical norm convergence in
LNS

(X,\lVert\cdot\rVert,\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 3.6
.

(i)

Every statistical
p
-decreasing to
0

(ii)

A decreasing to

(iii)

Every

LNS
s.

(iv)

Any order convergent sequence in

-continuous

s

-converges to its order limit.

(v)

The statistical

-decreasing limit is linear.

(vi)

If

holds for all

q_{n}\stpd 0

r_{n}\stpd 0
in
LNRS

The convergences of the properties of Example
3.7
need not be true in general. To see one case of them, we give the following example.

Example 3.7
.

Let
E
be the Riesz space
c_{0}
of all convergent to zero real sequences. Consider a sequence
(x_{n})
in
LNRS

(E,|\cdot|,E)
denoted by
x_{n}:=(x_{k}^{n})=(x_{1}^{n},x_{2}^{n},\cdots,x_{k}^{n},\cdots)\in E
such that

x_{k}^{n}:=\begin{cases}1,&k=n^{3}\\
\frac{1}{k},&k\neq n^{3}\end{cases}

for all
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then it is clear that
x_{n}\stpd 0
. Observe that the whole sequence
(x_{n})
is not monotonic, but it is statistically

Proposition 3.8
.

The statistical
p
-limit of a sequence is uniquely determined.

Proof.

Assume that a sequence
(x_{n})
in an
LNS

(X,p,E)
satisfies
x_{n}\stpc x
and
x_{n}\stpc y
for some different element
x,y\in X
. Then it follows that there are two sequences
q_{n}\stpd 0
and
r_{n}\stpd 0
with index sets
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
\delta(K)=\delta(L)=1
and

p(x_{n_{k}}-x)\leq q_{n_{k}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ p(x_{n_{l}}-y)\leq r_{n_{l}}

holds for all
n_{k}\in K
and
n_{l}\in L
. Now, consider the set
M:=K\cap L
. Then we have
\delta(M)=1
and

p(x_{n_{m}}-x)\leq q_{n_{m}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ p(x_{n_{m}}-y)\leq r_{n_{m}}

holds for all
n_{m}\in M
. Hence, by the inequality

0\leq p(x-y)\leq p(x_{n_{m}}-x)+p(x_{n_{m}}-y)\leq q_{n_{m}}+r_{n_{m}},

and by
(q_{n_{m}}+r_{n_{m}})\downarrow 0
on
M
, we obtain the desired result, i.e.,
x=y
.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

The statistical
p
-limit is linear.

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{n}\stpc x
and
y_{n}\stpc y
holds in an
LNS

(X,p,E)
. Then there exist sequences
q_{n}\stpd 0
and
r_{n}\stpd 0
such that

\delta(\{n\in\mathbb{N}:p(x_{n}-x)\nleq q_{n}\})=0

and

\delta(\{n\in\mathbb{N}:p(y_{n}-y)\nleq r_{n}\})=0

hold. On the other hand, we observe the following inequality

\{n\in\mathbb{N}:p(x_{n}+y_{n}-x-y)\nleq q_{n}+r_{n}\}\subseteq\{n\in\mathbb{N}:p(x_{n}-x)\nleq q_{n}\}\cup\{n\in\mathbb{N}:p(y_{n}-y)\nleq r_{n}\}.

Hence, we have

\delta\{n\in\mathbb{N}:p((x_{n}+y_{n})-(x+y))\nleq q_{n}+r_{n}\}=0.

It follows from
(q_{n}+r_{n})^{st_{p}}\downarrow 0
that we get
x_{n}+y_{n}\stpc x+y
.
∎

The following result is a statistical
p
-version of the squeeze law.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(X,p,E)
be an
LNRS
and
(x_{n})
,
(y_{n})
and
(z_{n})
be sequences in
X
such that
x_{n}\leq y_{n}\leq z_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
x_{n}\stpc x
and
z_{n}\stpc x
implies
y_{n}\stpc x
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{n}\stpc x
and
z_{n}\stpc x
. Then we have some sequences
q_{n}\stpd 0
and
r_{n}\stpd 0
with an index set
\delta(M)=1
such that
p(x_{n_{m}}-x)\leq q_{n_{m}}
and
p(z_{n_{m}}-x)\leq r_{n_{m}}
for all
n_{m}\in M
. On the other hand, following from the inequality
x_{n}\leq y_{n}\leq z_{n}
for all
n
, we have
y_{n}-x\leq z_{n}-x
and
x-y_{n}\leq x-y_{n}
, and so,
p(y_{n}-x)\leq p(z_{n}-x)
and
p(y_{n}-x)\leq p(x_{n}-x)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
because of the monotony of
p
. So, it follows that

p(y_{n}-x)\leq q_{n_{m}}+r_{n_{m}}

for all
n_{m}\in M
. Therefore, the proof is completed by
(q_{n}+r_{n})\stpd 0
, i.e.,
y_{n}\stc x
.
∎

The following statements hold.

(i)

Every statistical

(ii)

A statistical

KB

Proof.

(i):
Assume that
x_{n}\stpc x

q_{n}\stpd 0
with a set
\delta(K)=1

p(x_{n_{k}}-x)\leq q_{n_{k}}

We remind that if
(x_{n})
is a sequence satisfying the property
P
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
except a set of natural density zero then we say that
(x_{n})
satisfies the property
P
for almost all

[

.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(X,p,E)
be an
LNS
and
(x_{n})
be a sequence in
X
. Then
x_{n}\stpc x
holds if and only if there is another sequence
(y_{n})
in
X
such that
x_{n}=y_{n}
for a.a.n and
y_{n}\stpc x
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a sequence
(y_{n})
in
X
such that
x_{n}=y_{n}
for a.a.n and
y_{n}\stpc x
. Then there exists a sequence
q_{n}\stpd 0
with a set
\delta(K)=1
such that
p(y_{n_{k}}-x)\leq q_{n_{k}}
for every
n_{k}\in K
. Since
x_{n}=y_{n}
for a.a.n, we observe that the index sets
\{n_{k}\in K:p(x_{n_{k}}-x)\leq q_{n_{k}}\}
and
\{n_{k}\in K:p(y_{n_{k}}-x)\leq q_{n_{k}}\}
are a.a.n. Thus, there exists a subset
M\subseteq K
such that
\delta(M)=1
and
p(x_{n_{m}}-x)\leq q_{n_{m}}
for every
n_{m}\in M
. Therefore, the desired result is to appear, i.e., we get
x_{n}\stpc x
.
∎

The lattice operations in
LNRS
s are statistically
p
-continuous
in the following sense.

Theorem 4.2
.

If
x_{n}\stpc x
and
y_{n}\stpc y
satisfy then
x_{n}\vee y_{n}\stpc x\vee y
and
x_{n}\wedge y_{n}\stpc x\wedge y
in an
LNRS
.

Proof.

Suppose that
x_{n}\stpc x
and
y_{n}\stpc y
holds in an
LNRS

(X,p,E)
. Then it is enough to show that
x_{n}\vee y_{n}\stpc x\vee y
because the other case can be obtained by applying both
[
16
, Thm.11.5(iv)]
and the linearity of the statistical
p
-limit. Since
x_{n}\stpc x
and
y_{n}\stpc y
, there are two sequences
q_{n}\stpd 0
and
r_{n}\stpd 0
with index sets
\delta(K)=\delta(L)=1
such that

p(x_{n_{k}}-x)\leq q_{n_{k}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ p(x_{n_{l}}-y)\leq r_{n_{l}}

hold for all
n_{k}\in K
and
n_{l}\in L
. It follows from the inequality
|a\vee b-a\vee c|\leq|b-c|
(cf.
[
3
, Thm.1.9(2)]
) in Riesz spaces that we have

\displaystyle p(x_{n}\vee y_{n}-x\vee y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(|x_{n}\vee y_{n}-x_{n}\vee y+x_{n}\vee y-x\vee y|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p(|x_{n}\vee y_{n}-x_{n}\vee y|)+p(|x_{n}\vee y-x\vee y|)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p(|y_{n}-y|)+p(|x_{n}-x|)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we obtain

p(x_{n_{m}}\vee y_{n_{m}}-x\vee y)\leq p(y_{n_{m}}-y)+p(x_{n_{m}}-x)\leq q_{n_{m}}+r_{n_{m}}

for each
n_{m}\in M
, where
M:=K\cap L
. It follows from
(q_{n_{k}m}+r_{n_{m}})\stpd 0
that
x_{n}\vee y_{n}\stpc x\vee y
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

If
x_{n}\stpc x
satisfies in an
LNRS
then the following statements hold

(i)

x_{n}^{+}\stpc x^{+}
,

(ii)

x_{n}^{-}\stpc x^{-}
,

(iii)

|x_{n}|\stpc|x|
.

Proof.

The straightforward proofs of
(i)
and
(ii)
are direct results of Theorem
4.2
. We show
(iii)
. Since
x_{n}\stpc x
, we have another sequence
q_{n}\stpd 0
with an index set
\delta(K)=1
such that
p(x_{n_{k}}-x)\leq q_{n_{k}}
for all
n_{k}\in K
. Then, by using the inequality
||a|-|b||\leq|a|+|b|
in Riesz spaces and by applying the monotony of

, we have

p(|x_{n_{k}}|-|x|)=p(||x_{n_{k}}|-|x||)\leq p(|x_{n_{k}}-x|)\leq q_{n_{k}}

for every
n_{k}\in K
. Therefore, we get

.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
(x_{n})
be a positive sequence in an
LNRS
. Then
x_{n}\stpc x
implies
x\geq 0
.

Proof.

Assume that
x_{n}\stpc x
holds in an
LNRS

(X,p,E)
. Then it follows from Theorem
4.2
that

0\leq x_{n}=x_{n}\vee 0\stpc x\vee 0=x^{+}\in X_{+}.

Thus, by considering Proposition
3.8
, we know that the statistical
p
-limit of a sequence is unique. Therefore, we obtain the desired result,
x=x_{+}\in X_{+}
.
∎

In the following theorem, we give a relation between statistical
p
-convergence and order convergence.

Theorem 4.5
.

Every monotone decreasing and statistical
p
-convergent sequence order converges to its statistical
p
-limit.

Proof.

Suppose that
(x_{n})
is a monotone decreasing and statistical

-convergent to
x
sequence in an

(X,p,E)
. Then consider an arbitrary

. Thus, we have
x_{m}-x_{n}\in X_{+}
for all

, and so, we get
x_{m}-x_{n}\stpc x_{m}-x\in X_{+}
by Proposition

. Hence,
x_{m}\geq x
. Since

is a lower bound of
(x_{n})
. Assume that
y

x_{n}-y\stpc x-y\in X_{+}

Abstract
. We consider a chain consisting of n + 1 n+1 pinned harmonic oscillators subjected on the right to a time dependent periodic force ℱ ⁡ ( t ) {\mathcal{F}}(t) while Langevin thermostats are attached at both endpoints of the chain. We show that for long times the system is described by a Gaussian measure whose covariance function is independent of the force, while the means are periodic. We compute explicitly the work and energy due to the periodic force for all n n including n → ∞ n\to\infty .

1.
Introduction

In this work we consider the conversion of work into heat in a simple
model system: a pinned harmonic chain of
n+1
particles on which
work is performed by an external periodic force acting at one of
the endpoints.
The system is also in contact with thermal reservoirs, placed at both
of its endpoints, which absorb the energy generated by the work. In
the absence of the reservoirs the response of the system to the external forcing depends entirely on whether the frequency
\omega
of the external force coincides with the normal frequencies of the
chain
\{\omega_{j},j=0,\ldots,n\}
. When
\omega\neq\omega_{j}

the system adjusts itself to be out of phase with the force
so that there is no work done on the average.
If on the other hand the system is in resonance
with the force, i.e.
\omega=\omega_{j}
for some
j
, then the amplitude of
the oscillation tends to infinity
as time
t\to\infty
.

The situation is different in the presence of the thermostats.
They cause the oscillations at resonance to be damped and as a
result the work done by the force is strictly finite for all values of
\omega
.

There is still a strong dependence on
\omega
, as far as the magnitude
of the work is concerned, when
n
gets large.
This difference becomes qualitative when
n\to\infty
and the spectrum of the harmonic chain
becomes dense in an interval
\cal I
. The work done and the internal energy of the chain
depend strongly on whether
\omega
lies in the interior of
\cal I
, or not.

Due to the linearity of the system there is a clear division, in the
long time properties of the system, between those due to temperatures
of the thermal reservoirs and those due to the external force.
The energy flowing through the system as a result of the presence of the thermal reservoirs
we call
thermal energy
. It is not influenced by the external force and its behavior
is the same as in
[
10
]
and
[
8
]
.
The energy flow due to the work of the external force we call
mechanical energy
.
It is independent of the temperatures of the reservoirs,
and it is influenced only by the corresponding damping.
For finite
n
and pure damping equal on both sites this
was computed in section 4 of
[
9
]

in terms of the Green function of the corresponding damped harmonic chain.
The main objective of the present note is the exact calculation of the asymptotic behavior
as
n\to\infty
of the work and the mechanical energy.
Calculation of these quantities, turns out to be quite complicated,
but leads to explicit expressions for their
asymptotics.
In particular we show that, for forcing frequency outside
\cal I
, the work,
the mechanical energy and its flow become negligible as
n\to\infty
. Inside
\cal I

these quantities oscillate fast and their asymptotic behavior can be described
in terms of Young measures.

The results of the present work remain also valid in the case of
unpinned harmonic chain. It suffices to set the pinning constant

\omega_{0}=0
in our formulas describing the work and energy
functionals. Obviously we now always have
\omega>\omega_{0}=0

and consider the motion relative to the center of mass positioned at zero.

For anharmonic interaction the situation is qualitatively very
different. The non-linearity produces many new effects described in
[
9
]
.
We have also studied the case of a harmonic chain with a random velocity flip
[
7
]
.

2.
Description of the System

The configurations of our system, consisting of
n+1
pinned harmonic oscillators, are described by

(2.1)

(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p})=(q_{0},\dots,q_{n},p_{0},\dots,p_{n})\in\Omega_{n}:={\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.

We should think of the positions
q_{x}
as the relative displacement of an
atom from a point

x
belonging to the integer lattice interval
{{\mathbb{I}}_{n}=\{0,\ldots,n\}}
and
p_{x}
as its respective momentum.

The total energy of the chain is given by the Hamiltonian:

(2.2)

\mathcal{H}_{n}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}):=\sum_{x=0}^{n}e_{x}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}),

where the microscopic energy density at
x
is given by

(2.3)

e_{x}(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p}):=\frac{p_{x}^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(q_{x}-q_{x-1})^{2}+\frac{\omega_{0}^{2}q_{x}^{2}}{2},\quad x\in{\mathbb{I}}_{n}.

Here we let
q_{-1}:=q_{0}
.

The microscopic dynamics of
the process
describing the total chain is given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle\dot{q}_{x}(t)

\displaystyle=p_{x}(t),\qquad\qquad x\in{\mathbb{I}}_{n},

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{x}(t)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{x}q_{x}-\omega_{0}^{2}q_{x},\quad x\in{\mathbb{I}}_{n}^{o}=\{1,\dots,n-1\}

and at the boundaries by

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}p_{0}(t)

\displaystyle=\;\Big(q_{1}(t)-q_{0}(t)-\omega_{0}^{2}q_{0}\Big)\mathrm{d}t-2\gamma_{-}p_{0}(t)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{4\gamma_{-}T_{-}}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{w}_{-}(t),\vphantom{\Big(}

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}p_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\;\Big(q_{n-1}(t)-q_{n}(t)-\omega_{0}^{2}q_{n}(t)\Big)\mathrm{d}t+\;{\mathcal{F}}(t/\theta)\mathrm{d}t-2\gamma_{+}p_{n}(t)\mathrm{d}t+\sqrt{4\gamma_{+}T_{+}}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{w}_{+}(t).\vphantom{\Big(}

Here
\Delta q_{x}=q_{x+1}+q_{x-1}-2q_{x}
,
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}
is the laplacian
on the integer lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\omega_{0}>0
is a pinning constant,

\widetilde{w}_{-}(t)
and
\widetilde{w}_{+}(t)
are two independent standard one dimensional Wiener
processes
and
\gamma_{\pm}
are non-negative constants that
describe the respective strengths of the Langevin thermostats.

We assume the force
{\mathcal{F}}(t)
to be a smooth periodic function
of period
1
and parameter
\theta
rescales the period. We will
suppose, without losing generality, that

(2.6)

\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{F}}(t)\mathrm{d}t=0,\qquad\int_{0}^{1}{\mathcal{F}}(t)^{2}\mathrm{d}t>0.

The generator of the dynamics is given by

(2.7)

\mathcal{G}_{t}=\mathcal{A}_{t}+2\sum_{\iota\in\{-,+\}}\gamma_{\iota}S_{\iota},

where

(2.8)

\mathcal{A}_{t}=\sum_{x=0}^{n}p_{x}\partial_{q_{x}}+\sum_{x=0}^{n}(\Delta q_{x}-\omega^{2}_{0}q_{x})\partial_{p_{x}}+{\mathcal{F}}(t/\theta)\partial_{p_{n}}.

By convention we let
q_{n+1}:=q_{n}
and
q_{-1}=q_{0}
.
Furthermore

(2.9)

S_{-}=T_{-}\partial_{p_{0}}^{2}-p_{0}\partial_{p_{0}},\qquad S_{+}=T_{+}\partial_{p_{n}}^{2}-p_{n}\partial_{p_{n}}

The energy currents are

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{t}e_{x}=j_{x-1,x}-j_{x,x+1},

\displaystyle j_{x,x+1}:=-p_{x}(q_{x+1}-q_{x}),\qquad\text{if }x\in\{0,...,n-1\},

and at the boundaries

(2.11)

j_{-1,0}:=2{\gamma}_{-}\left(T_{-}-p_{0}^{2}\right)\qquad j_{n,n+1}:=-2{\gamma}_{+}\left(T_{+}-p_{n}^{2}\right)-{\mathcal{F}}(t/\theta)p_{n}.

We are interested in the long time behavior of the system.
In the absence of the external forcing,
{\mathcal{F}}(t)\equiv 0
, this is just
the model considered in
[
10
]
, with
\omega_{0}=0
, and in

[
8
]
for
\omega_{0}>0
.
In the case when
{\mathcal{F}}(t)\equiv 0
, starting
with any initial configuration
(\mathbf{q}(0),\mathbf{p}(0))
(or any
initial probability distribution
\mu_{0}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q},\mathrm{d}\mathbf{p})
)
the system approaches a stationary Gaussian distribution

\mu_{\rm stat}(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{q},\mathrm{d}\mathbf{p})
, in which the expectation values
of
q_{x}
and
p_{x}
vanish, i.e.
\overline{q}_{x}(t)=0
and
\overline{p}_{x}(t)=0
, while the covariances between components
of
(\mathbf{q},\mathbf{p})
are given explicitly.

In particular the
expectation of the energy current
\overline{j}_{x,x+1}
between sites
x

and
x+1
, that is independent of
x
and
t
, is given by

(2.12)

\overline{j}_{x,x+1}=(c+o(1))(T_{-}-T_{+}),\quad\mbox{as }n\gg 1,

with

c=\frac{\gamma}{1+4\gamma^{2}+2\gamma\omega_{0}(\gamma\omega_{0}+\sqrt{1+4\gamma^{2}+(\gamma\omega_{0})^{2}})},

when
\gamma_{-}=\gamma_{+}=\gamma
, see
[
8
, formula (37), p. 240]
. In the
case
\omega_{0}=0
the term
o(1)
in the formula (
2.12
) can be
omitted (no dependence on
n
) and we have
c=\frac{\gamma}{1+4\gamma^{2}}
, see
[
8
, formula (40),
p. 241]
.

Eq. (
2.12
) implies that the thermal conductivity is proportional to
n

- the size of the system - and becomes
infinite in the limit
n\to+\infty
, see also
[
10
]
. In fact
the ”temperature”
T_{x}
, defined as the variance of
p_{x}^{2}
, is independent of
x
, except near the boundary points

x=0,n
. Adding now the periodic force of period
\theta
leads, as

t\to+\infty
, to a Gaussian, periodic stationary state
\{\mu_{t}^{P},t\in[0,+\infty)\}
,
whose covariances are
the same as in the case when no force is applied.
For any functions
F=F(\bf q,\bf p)
and
G=G(t)
define

(2.13)

\overline{F}(t)=\int_{\Omega_{n}}F\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{P}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle\langle G\rangle\rangle=\frac{1}{\theta}\int_{0}^{\theta}G(t)\mathrm{d}t.

The periodic stationary state has the property that
\langle\langle\overline{\mathcal{G}F}\rangle\rangle=0

for any
F
in the domain of
\mathcal{G}_{t}
.

The
expectation values of the position and momentum
\overline{q}_{x}(t)
and

\overline{p}_{x}(t)
are now

\theta
-periodic and independent of the temperature of the
reservoirs. They are given by

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\overline{\bf q}(t)\\
\overline{\bf p}(t)\end{array}\right)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}e^{-A(t-s)}\;{\mathcal{F}}(s/\theta){\rm e}_{p,n+1}\mathrm{d}s.

Here
A
is a
2\times 2
block matrix made of
(n+1)\times(n+1)

matrices of the form

A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-{\rm Id}_{n+1}\\
-\Delta_{\rm N}+\omega_{0}^{2}&\Gamma\end{array}\right),

where
{\rm Id}_{n+1}
is the
(n+1)\times(n+1)
identity matrix,

\Delta_{\rm N}
is the Neumann laplacian
on
{\mathbb{I}}_{n}
:

(2.14)

\Delta_{\rm N}f_{x}:=\Delta f_{x},\quad x\in{\mathbb{I}}_{n}^{o}\quad\mbox{and }\quad\Delta_{\rm N}f_{0}=f_{1}-f_{0},\quad\Delta_{\rm N}f_{n}=f_{n-1}-f_{n}.

Furthermore
\Gamma
is the diagonal matrix

\Gamma=2\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\gamma_{-}&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&0&\ldots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&0&0\\
0&0&\ldots&0&\gamma_{+}\end{array}\right).

The column vector
{\rm e}_{p,n+1}

is given by

{\rm e}_{p,n+1}^{T}=[\underbrace{0,\ldots,0}_{2n+1-\rm{times}},1].

Notice that the first of the conditions (
2.4
) implies that

\langle\langle\overline{p}_{x}\rangle\rangle=0
, while the second gives
\langle\langle\overline{q}_{x}\rangle\rangle=0
.

The expected value of energy, averaged over a period,
breaks up into the mechanical part,
coming from the averaged position
\overline{\bf q}(t)
and momentum

\overline{\bf p}(t)
, which is independent of the temperature of the
reservoirs, and the thermal part, which is independent of the external force. More
precisely

(2.15)

\langle\langle e_{x}\rangle\rangle=\langle\langle e_{x}^{\rm mech}\rangle\rangle+\langle\langle e_{x}^{\rm th}\rangle\rangle,

where the mechanical component of the energy is given by

(2.16)

e_{x}^{\rm mech}(t):=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\overline{p}_{x}^{2}(t)+\omega_{0}^{2}\overline{q}_{x}^{2}(t)+\big(\overline{q}_{x}(t)-\overline{q}_{x-1}(t)\big)^{2}\Big],\quad x\in{\mathbb{I}}_{n}.

and the thermal part is

(2.17)

e_{x}^{\rm th}(t):=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[p_{x}^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\omega_{0}^{2}q_{x}^{\prime}(t)^{2}+\big(q_{x}^{\prime}(t)-q_{x-1}^{\prime}(t)\big)^{2}\Big],\quad x\in{\mathbb{I}}_{n}.

where
q_{x}^{\prime}(t)=q_{x}(t)-\overline{q}_{x}(t)
and
p_{x}^{\prime}(t)=p_{x}(t)-\overline{p}_{x}(t)

and
{\mathbb{E}}
denotes the average with respect to the initial data and
the realizations of the Wiener processes in (
2.5
). As
before, we adopt the convention
\overline{q}_{-1}(t):=\overline{q}_{0}(t)
and
likewise
q_{-1}^{\prime}(t):=q_{0}^{\prime}(t)
.

As already mentioned in the Introduction one of the goals of
the present paper is to describe the work done by the force on the
system. It is given by

(2.18)

W(n)=\frac{1}{\theta}\int_{0}^{\theta}{\mathcal{F}}(t/\theta)\overline{p}_{n}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

W(n)
is always positive, generates energy fluxes into
the two heat reservoirs. Furthermore, we describe the
time average of the
mechanical energy functional given by eq. (
2.16
). Its
thermal counterpart does not depend on time and has been described in

[
8
,
10
]
. We mention here also that the case
n=0
, i.e. a
single oscillator in contact with a heat bath and driven by an
external unbiased time-periodic force, has been fully characterized in
[
11
]
.

3.
Results

In what follows we will use the dispersion relation of the
infinite chain given by

(3.1)

\omega(r)=\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}+4\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi r}{2}\right)},\qquad r\in[0,1]

and its inverse defined for
\omega\in\cal I:=[\omega_{0},\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}+4}]
by the formula

(3.2)

r(\omega)=\frac{2}{\pi}\arcsin\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\omega^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}\right),

3.1.
Work done by the force on the system

The work
W(n)
performed by the force on the system, see
(
2.18
), depends on the period
\theta
.
Considering for simplicity the simple mode case when

(3.3)

{\mathcal{F}}(t/\theta)=F\cos(\omega t),\quad\omega:=\frac{2\pi}{\theta}

the work done is given by (see Appendix):

(3.4)

W(\omega,n)=\big(\omega F\big)^{2}\frac{N(\omega,n)}{D(\omega,n)}.

Here

\displaystyle N(\omega,n)=\

\displaystyle\gamma_{-}G^{1}(\omega,n)^{2}+\gamma_{+}G^{0}(\omega,n)^{2}+4{\gamma_{-}^{2}\gamma_{+}}\omega^{2}\left(G^{0}(\omega,n)^{2}-G^{1}(\omega,n)^{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle D(\omega,n)=\

\displaystyle 1+8\gamma_{-}\gamma_{+}{\omega^{2}}G^{1}(\omega,n)^{2}+4\omega^{2}G^{0}(\omega,n)^{2}(\gamma_{-}^{2}+\gamma_{+}^{2})

(3.5)

\displaystyle+16\gamma_{-}^{2}\gamma_{+}^{2}\omega^{4}\left(G^{0}(\omega,n)^{2}-G^{1}(\omega,n)^{2}\right)^{2},

where

(3.6)

G^{s}(\omega,n)=G_{0,sn}(\omega,n),\quad s=0,1,

and

(3.7)

G_{x,y}(\omega,n)=\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot\frac{1}{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}}+\frac{2}{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\cos(\frac{\pi j(2x+1)}{2(n+1)})\cos(\frac{\pi j(2y+1)}{2(n+1)})}{\omega_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}},\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{I}}_{n}

is the Green’s functions of
-\Delta_{N}+\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}
,
and
\pm\omega_{j}
,
j=0,\ldots,n
are
the eigenvalues of
-\Delta_{N}+\omega_{0}^{2}
defined by
\omega_{j}=\omega\left(\frac{j}{n+1}\right)

where
\omega(r)
is given by (
3.1
).

It is easy to see from (
3.1
) that
4\omega^{2}\gamma_{-}N\leqslant D+\gamma_{-}^{2}G^{1}(\omega,n)^{2}
. Therefore, the following bound can be found

(3.8)

W(\omega,n)\leqslant\frac{\big(\omega F\big)^{2}}{4}\Big(\frac{1}{\gamma_{-}}+\frac{1}{\gamma_{+}}\Big),\quad n=1,2,\ldots.

The functions
G^{s}(\omega,n)
can be computed explicitly:

(3.9)

G^{s}(\omega,n)=\frac{1}{n+1}\cdot\frac{1}{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega^{2}}+\frac{2}{n+1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{js}\cos^{2}(\frac{\pi j}{2(n+1)})}{\omega_{j}^{2}-\omega^{2}}\quad,\quad s=0,1,

depending on whether
\omega
is in
the spectrum of the harmonic chain, or not, see Figure

4.
Current of mechanical energy

The currents of the mechanical energy are given by

(4.1)

\begin{split}\overline{j_{x,x+1}^{\text{mech}}}(t)&=-\overline{p}_{x}(t)\left(\overline{q}_{x+1}(t)-\overline{q}_{x}(t)\right),\qquad x=0,\dots,n-1\\
\overline{j_{-1,0}^{\text{mech}}}(t)&=-\gamma_{-}\overline{p}_{0}(t)^{2},\qquad\overline{j_{n,n+1}^{\text{mech}}}(t)=\gamma_{+}\overline{p}_{n}(t)^{2}-\mathcal{F}(t/\theta)\overline{p}_{n}(t).\end{split}

They have all the same time average over the period:

(4.2)

J^{\text{mech}}(n):=\langle\langle\overline{j_{x,x+1}^{\text{mech}}}\rangle\rangle.

Note that
W^{-}(n):=-J^{\text{mech}}(n)
is the amount of work that
goes into the left reservoir.
Of course when
\gamma_{+}=0
we have
W^{-}(n)=W(n)
.
If however
\gamma_{+}>0
, then some of the work, denoted by
W^{+}(n)=W(n)-W^{-}(n)
, goes into the right reservoir.

We compute first

W^{-}(n)
, using
\overline{j_{-1,0}^{\text{mech}}}(t)
,
as it involves simpler formulas.
From (
A.5
) we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}&\overline{p}_{0}(t)=\text{Re}\left(i\omega{e^{i\omega t}}\widetilde{q}_{0}(\omega)\right)\\
&=\frac{FG^{1}(\omega,n)\left[\text{Re}(\widetilde{D}(\omega,n))\omega\cos(\omega t)-\text{Im}(\widetilde{D}(\omega,n))\omega\sin(\omega t)\right]}{\left|\widetilde{D}(\omega,n)\right|^{2}}\end{split}

and, recalling that
\omega=\frac{2\pi}{\theta}
,

(4.4)

\begin{split}J^{\text{mech}}(n)=-\frac{\gamma_{-}}{\theta}\int_{0}^{\theta}\overline{p}_{0}(t)^{2}\mathrm{d}t=-\gamma_{-}\Big(F\omega G^{1}(\omega,n)\Big)^{2}\end{split}

As a result, combining with (
3.4
), we get

(4.5)

\displaystyle W^{-}(n)=\gamma_{-}\Big(F\omega G^{1}(\omega,n)\Big)^{2},\qquad W^{+}(n)=W(n)-W^{-}(n).

Notice that if
\omega\notin\cal I
, since
G^{1}(\omega,n)\mathop{\longrightarrow}_{n\to\infty}0
,
we have
J^{\text{mech}}(n)\mathop{\longrightarrow}_{n\to\infty}0
.
Comparing with (
3.17
) we deduce that if
\omega\notin\cal I
, all the work goes to
the right thermostat as
n\to\infty
.

If
\omega\in(\omega_{0},\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}+4})
, then

W^{-}(n)=\overline{W}\vphantom{1}^{-}\Big(r(\omega),(n+1)r(\omega)\Big)+o(1),

where the formula for
\overline{W}\vphantom{1}^{-}(r,u)
can be obtained from
(
4.5
) by replacing
G^{1}(\omega,n)
by the function

\overline{G}^{1}(r,u)
defined in (
B.13
). We also have
W^{+}(n)=\overline{W}\vphantom{1}^{+}\Big(r(\omega),(n+1)r(\omega)\Big)+o(1),
where
\overline{W}\vphantom{1}^{+}(r,u)=\overline{W}(r,u)-\overline{W}\vphantom{1}^{-}(r,u)
, where

\overline{W}(r,u)
is given by (
B.14
).

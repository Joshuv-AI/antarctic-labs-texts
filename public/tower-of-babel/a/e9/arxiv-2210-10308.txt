Abstract
. The 0 0 -surgeries of two knots K 1 K_{1} and K 2 K_{2} are homology cobordant rel meridians if there exists a ℤ \mathbb{Z} -homology cobordism X X between them such that the two knot meridians are in the same homology class in H 1 ​ ( X , ℤ ) H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}) . In this paper, we give a pair of rationally slice knots which are not smoothly concordant but whose 0 0 -surgeries are homology cobordant rel meridians. One knot in the pair is the figure eight knot, which has concordance order two; all previous examples of such pairs of knots are infinite order.



1.
Introduction

Two oriented knots
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
in
S^{3}
are
smoothly concordant
, denoted
K_{1}\sim K_{2}
, if they cobound a smooth annulus in
S^{3}\times[0,1]
. The set of isotopy classes of knots forms a monoid, and that monoid modulo this smooth concordance relation yields an abelian group with binary operation connected sum. We call this group the
smooth concordance group
, denoted
\mathcal{C}
, whose identity element is the class of
smoothly slice knots
. There are several variations on
\mathcal{C}
, including the
rational concordance group

\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{Q}}
, where two knots are equivalent if they cobound a smooth annulus in a rational homology cobordism, and the
topological concordance group

\mathcal{C}_{\text{TOP}}
, where we relax the conditions on the annulus the knots cobound to only require that it be locally flat.

We let
S^{3}_{n}(K)
denote the 3-manifold that results from performing
n
-framed surgery on a knot
K
in
S^{3}
. In general, two 3-manifolds
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-homology cobordant
if there exists a smooth, compact, oriented 4-manifold
X
such that
\partial X=(-Y_{1})\cup Y_{2}
and
H_{*}(X,Y_{i};\mathbb{Z})=0
for
i=1,2
. If we let
Y_{1}=S^{3}_{n}(K_{1})
and
Y_{2}=S^{3}_{n}(K_{2})
for some

knots in
S^{3}
and some

, then the
n
-surgeries of
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are
\mathbb{Z}
-homology cobordant rel meridians
if they are
\mathbb{Z}
-homology cobordant by cobordism
X
and the positively oriented meridians of the
K_{i}
are representatives of the same homology class in
H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.

It is a natural question to ask whether there is a relationship between the homeomorphism type of
S^{3}_{n}(K)
and the knot type of
K
; in the Kirby problem list
[
Kir97
]
, Akbulut and Kirby conjectured in Problem 1.19 and subsequent remarks that two knots with the same
0
-surgeries will be concordant knots. This conjecture inspires the following question:

Question 1.1
.

If
S^{3}_{0}(K_{1})
and
S^{3}_{0}(K_{2})
are
\mathbb{Z}
-homology cobordant rel meridians, are
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
smoothly concordant?

In
[
CFHH13
]
, Cochran-Franklin-Hedden-Horn give a negative answer to this question by providing a family of topologically slice knots which are not pairwise smoothly concordant, but whose
0
-surguries satisfy the hypothesis of
1.1
. All knots in this family have infinite order in
\mathcal{C}
, and are distinguishable as non-concordant in
\mathcal{C}
by Ozsváth-Szabó’s

invariant
[
OS04b
]
and Rasmussen’s

invariant
[
Ras03
]
.

In
[
Yas15
]
, Yasui gives a condition on knots such that their images under the Mazur pattern and under a second closely related pattern gives a pair of knots with the same 0-surgery which are not concordant for any orientations.

In this paper, we will expand on this work in the smooth category. We show the answer to Question 1.1 is “No” even when one knot in the pair is torsion in
\mathcal{C}
. Interestingly, most of the smooth concordance invariants coming from the knot Floer package vanish for our pair of knots, and both knots are rationally slice. We summarize our results thusly:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist rationally slice knots whose zero surgeries are smoothly
\mathbb{Z}
-homology cobordant rel meridians, but which are not smoothly concordant, and furthermore have different orders in
\mathcal{C}
.

2.1.
Knot Floer Homology

Knot Floer homology is a Heegaard Floer theory for null-homologous knots embedded in 3-manifolds. We will restrict our attention to knots in
S^{3}
. To such an oriented knot
K
, we associate a doubly pointed Heegaard diagram
\mathcal{H}=(\Sigma,\alpha,\beta,w,z)
, with base points
w
and
z
, where
\Sigma
is the genus-g closed, oriented Heegaard surface. This surface is decorated with
\alpha
, a g-tuple of
\alpha
-circles, and
\beta
, a g-tuple of
\beta
-circles, and these attaching circles satisfy a series of intersection conditions amongst same sets and between the two sets. Then, the
knot Floer complex
is the chain complex we build using the intersection data of two Lagrangian submanifolds
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}=\alpha_{1}\times\alpha_{2}\times\cdots\times\alpha_{g}
and
\mathbb{T}_{\beta}=\beta_{1}\times\beta_{2}\times\cdots\times\beta_{g}
in the symmetric product of
\Sigma_{g}
. Intersection points between the two tori give generators of our complex, and the differential counts the pseudoholomorphic discs with boundary on
\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cup\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
that connect these points. The knot Floer complex of
K
is a
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
-filtered chain complex, whose filtered chain homotopy type is a knot invariant of
K
.

We will use a variation on the full knot Floer complex described in
[
OS04b
]
and
[
Ras03
]
. In this setting, the
knot Floer complex over

\mathcal{R}
, denoted
\operatorname{CFK}_{\mathcal{R}}(K)
, is the complex finitely and freely generated over the polynomial ring
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}]/(\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})
.

Given a generator
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
, our chain complex admits a bigrading given by:

\text{gr}(\textbf{x})=(\text{gr}_{\mathcal{U}}(\textbf{x}),\text{gr}_{\mathcal{V}}(\textbf{x}))

Multiplication by
\mathcal{U}
lowers the
\mathcal{U}
-grading by 2, multiplication by
\mathcal{V}
lowers the
\mathcal{V}
-grading by 2, and the differential lowers both gradings by 1.
Now, given two generators
x
,
y

\in\mathbb{T}_{\alpha}\cap\mathbb{T}_{\beta}
, let
\pi_{2}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y})
be the space of homotopy classes of discs connecting
x
and
y
. Then, we can define a relative bigrading on
\operatorname{CFK}_{\mathcal{R}}(K)
as follows:

\text{gr}_{\mathcal{U}}(\textbf{x})-\text{gr}_{\mathcal{U}}(\textbf{y})=\mu(\phi)-2n_{w}(\phi)

\text{gr}_{\mathcal{V}}(\textbf{x})-\text{gr}_{\mathcal{V}}(\textbf{y})=\mu(\phi)-2n_{z}(\phi)

where
\phi\in\pi_{2}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y})
, and
\mu(\phi))
is the Maslov index of this homotopy class.

The set of generators of
\operatorname{CFK}_{\mathcal{R}}{(K)}
admits a
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
-bigrading
(M,A)
with
M
the
Maslov grading
equivalent to the
\mathcal{U}
-grading as described above, and
A
the
Alexander grading
defined as:

A=\frac{1}{2}(\text{gr}_{\mathcal{U}}-\text{gr}_{\mathcal{V}})

We note that it is possible in general to pin down absolute Maslov and Alexander gradings for the generators, but we will only need to make use of absolute Alexander and relative Maslov gradings for our purposes.

We recall some additional properties of Knot Floer homology. The knot Floer complex of a knot detects genus
[
OS04b
]
in the following sense. Let
g(K)
denote the genus of the knot
K
; then

g(K)=\text{max}\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:\widehat{\operatorname{HFK}}(K,i)\neq 0\}

Additionally, knot Floer homology categorifies the Alexander polynomial; that is, the graded Euler characteristic of the knot Floer homology of a knot
K
gives
\Delta_{K}(t)
, the Alexander polynomial of
K
, up to multiplication by a unit.

2.2.
Bordered Floer homology

Bordered Floer homology is a version of Heegaard Floer theory developed by Lipshitz-Ozsváth-Thurston
[
LOT18
]
for 3-manifolds with boundary. We equip the boundary surface with a basepoint and choose a parameterization of its handlebody decomposition. We then associate to it a differential graded algebra, which we will denote
\mathcal{A}
, which is determined by
\partial M
equipped with this data. We suppress from notation the choice of parameterization of
\partial M
, and note that our invariants will be independent of this choice up to homotopy equivalence.

Throughout this paper, all 3-manifolds with boundary are knot or link complements in
S^{3}
and thus all boundaries are toroidal. In this case,
\mathcal{A}
is called the
torus algebra
, which we can denote specifically as
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{T})
. The ring of idempotents of the torus algebra is given by
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{I})=(\iota_{0},\iota_{1})
where
1
=\iota_{0}+\iota_{1}
gives the unit of
\mathcal{A}
, and we provide an
\mathbb{F}_{2}
-basis for the algebra:

\{\rho_{1},\hskip 2.84544pt\rho_{2},\hskip 2.84544pt\rho_{3},\hskip 2.84544pt\rho_{12},\hskip 2.84544pt\rho_{23},\hskip 2.84544pt\rho_{123}\}

We describe multiplication in this algebra using the following quiver:

\iota_{0}
\iota_{1}
\rho_{2}
\rho_{1}
\rho_{3}

The nonzero products of this algebra are given as
\rho_{1}\rho_{2}=\rho_{12}
,
\rho_{2}\rho_{3}=\rho_{23}
, and
\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\rho_{3}=\rho_{123}
. All other products are zero.

Note that this algebra is 8-dimensional over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
and is in fact just a graded algebra; it has no differential. Note also that, as described in the quiver, there are compatibility conditions with the idemptotents; e.g., we have that
\rho_{1}=\iota_{0}\rho_{1}\iota_{1}
,
\rho_{2}=\iota_{1}\rho_{2}\iota_{0}
, etc. Further consideration of the torus algebra is given in Section 11.1 of
[
LOT18
]
.

With our algebra in hand, we can compute the
bordered Floer invariants
of
M
, which are differential graded modules over
\mathcal{A}
. The first type of invariant is called a
type D structure
, denoted
\widehat{\operatorname{CFD}}(M)
, which is a left differential module over
\mathcal{A}
equipped with a structure map:

\delta^{1}:\widehat{\operatorname{CFD}}(M)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\otimes\widehat{\operatorname{CFD}}(M)

that satisfies the condition:

(\mu_{1}\otimes\text{id}_{\widehat{\operatorname{CFD}}(M)})\circ(\text{id}_{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\delta_{1})\circ(\delta_{1})+(\mu_{2}\otimes\text{id}_{\widehat{\operatorname{CFD}}(M)})\circ\delta_{1}=0

where
\mu_{2}:\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is (associative) multiplication on
\mathcal{A}
, and
\mu_{1}
is the differential, in the cases it does not vanish.

The second type of invariant is called a
type A structure
, denoted
\widehat{\operatorname{CFA}}(M)
, which is a right
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
module over
\mathcal{A}
equipped with multiplication maps:

m_{i+1}:\widehat{\operatorname{CFA}}(M)\otimes\underbrace{\mathcal{A}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathcal{A}}_{\text{i copies}}\rightarrow\widehat{\operatorname{CFA}}(M)

These
m_{i}
maps satisfy a series of compatibility conditions, called the
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
relations. For any element
x\in\widehat{\operatorname{CFA}}(M)
and for
a_{1},\cdots a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have that:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}m_{n-i+1}(m_{i+1}(x\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i})\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{n+1}(x\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{i-1}\otimes d(a_{i})\otimes a_{i+1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}m_{n}(x\otimes a_{1}\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes a_{i+2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})

These modules are shown to recover the closed Heegaard Floer invariants via a series of pairing theorems. When a closed three-manifold
Y
is the union of two three-manifolds with boundary
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
identified along their boundaries, then the Heegaard Floer complex
\widehat{\operatorname{HF}}(Y)
can be recovered via the pairing theorems outlined in
[
LOT18
]
.
\widehat{\operatorname{HF}}(Y)
is homotopy equivalent to the
A_{\infty}
tensor product of the type A structure of
Y_{i}
with the type D structure of
Y_{i+1\hskip 2.84544pt\text{mod}\hskip 2.84544pt2}
.
When our modules satisfy appropriate conditions of boundedness, we can use the box tensor product
\boxtimes
in place of the
A_{\infty}
tensor product
\widetilde{\otimes}
. We describe the pairing theorem, formalized in the Theorem 1.3 of
[
LOT18
]
, via the following homotopy equivalence:

\widehat{\operatorname{CFA}}(M_{1})\hskip 5.69046pt\boxtimes\hskip 5.69046pt\widehat{\operatorname{CFD}}(M_{2})\hskip 5.69046pt\simeq\hskip 5.69046pt\widehat{\operatorname{CF}}(M_{1}\cup_{h}M_{2})

where
h
is an orientation-reversing homeomorphism,
h:\partial M_{1}\rightarrow\partial M_{2}
.

The bordered Floer invariants generalize for connected, oriented three-manifolds with
n
connected boundary components, where we choose an assignment of a type A or type D structure for each boundary component. For example, when
n=2
, we have three equivalent bimodules:
\widehat{\operatorname{CFDD}},\widehat{\operatorname{CFAA}},
or
\widehat{\operatorname{CFDA}}
. Where there are two boundary components, we will differentiate between the two copies of the torus algebra as
\mathcal{A}_{\rho}
with basis elements
\rho_{i}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\sigma}
with basis elements
\sigma_{j}
. In this paper, we will not consider manifold with more than two boundary components. The earlier pairing theorems extend across n-modules; relevant to this paper there is, for
M_{1}
a manifold with single boundary component and
M_{2}
a manifold with two boundary components, an
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
-homotopy equivalence [Theorem 11,
[
LOT11
]
]:

\widehat{\operatorname{CFA}}(M_{1})\hskip 5.69046pt\widetilde{\otimes}_{A_{\infty}}\hskip 5.69046pt\widehat{\operatorname{CFDD}}(M_{2})\hskip 5.69046pt\simeq\hskip 5.69046pt\widehat{\operatorname{CFD}}(M_{1}\cup_{h}M_{2})

where we match the symbols of the algebras to specify which boundary component of
M_{2}
is paired with
\partial M_{1}
. We note that this tensor product only makes sense when the boundary components being matched are surfaces of the same genus. As we will see in Section 3, performing such a computation gives us something homotopy equivalent to a differential module, which we can easily simplify until it more clearly resembles a type D structure.

A distinct advantage of working over the ring
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}](\mathcal{U}\mathcal{V})

is that

In section 3 we will provide a detailed overview of performing the tensor product described above; a reader interested in thoroughly understanding the intricacies of such a computation and the underlying algebraic theory is encouraged to consult

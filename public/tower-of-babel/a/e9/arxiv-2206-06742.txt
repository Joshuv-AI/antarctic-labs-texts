Elliptic inequalities with nonlinear convolution and Hardy terms in cone-like domains
Marius Ghergu, Zhe Yu

Abstract
We study the inequality − Δ ​ u − μ | x | 2 ​ u ≥ ( | x | − α ∗ u p ) ​ u q \displaystyle-\Delta u-\frac{\mu}{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}u\geq(|x|^{-\alpha}*u^{p})u^{q} in an unbounded cone 𝒞 Ω ρ ⊂ ℝ N \mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N} ( N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 ) generated by a subdomain Ω \Omega of the unit sphere S N − 1 ⊂ ℝ N , S^{N-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}, p , q , ρ > 0 p,q,\rho>0 , μ ∈ ℝ \mu\in\mathbb{R} and 0 ≤ α < N 0\leq\alpha<N . Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a subdomain of the unit sphere
S^{N-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}(N\geq 2)
such that
\Omega
is connected and relatively open in
S^{N-1}
. Set

\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}=\{(r,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:r>0\text{ and }\omega\in\Omega\}.

For any
\rho>0
and
0\leq\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}<\infty
, we define

\begin{split}\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}&=\{(r,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:r>\rho\text{ and }\omega\in\Omega\},\\
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}&=\{(r,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\rho_{1}<r<\rho_{2}\text{ and }\omega\in\Omega\}.\end{split}

This paper is concerned with the study of positive solutions to the following semi-linear elliptic inequality with convolution and Hardy terms:

{\mathscr{L}}_{H}u:=-\Delta u-\frac{\mu}{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}u\geq(|x|^{-\alpha}*u^{p})u^{q}\;\;\;\text{ in }\;\;\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}.

(1.1)

In the above inequality we assume
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
p,q>0
and

0\leq\alpha<N
. The quantity
|x|^{-\alpha}\ast u^{p}
represents the convolution operation in
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}
given by

|x|^{-\alpha}\ast u^{p}=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{u^{p}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\,,\quad x\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}.

In this paper we shall be interested in classical positive solutions of (
1.1
), that is, functions
u\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho})\cap C(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}})
such that

u(x)>0\;,\;\;|x|^{-\alpha}*u^{p}<\infty\quad\mbox{ for all }\;x\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\Omega}^{\rho},

(1.2)

and
u
satisfies (
1.1
) pointwise in
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}
. We do not prescribe any condition on
u
on the boundary
\partial\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}
of the cone.

The prototype model

-\Delta u=\big(|x|^{-1}\ast u^{2}\big)u\quad\mbox{ in }\;\mathbb{R}^{3},

(1.3)

has been around for nearly a century, being introduced in 1928 by D.R. Hartree
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
in the form of time-dependent equations
given by

i\psi_{t}+\Delta\psi+(|x|^{-1}\ast|\psi|^{2})\psi=\psi\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times(0,\infty).

(1.4)

We may regard (
1.4
) in connection with the Schrödinger-Newton (or Schrödinger-Poisson) equation, namely

i\psi_{t}+\Delta\psi+\phi\psi=\psi\quad\mbox{in }{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times(0,\infty),

(1.5)

where
\phi
is a gravitational potential representing the interaction of the particle with its own gravitational field and satisfies the Poisson equation

\Delta\phi=4\pi G|\psi|^{2}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Thus, one may write the gravitational potential as a convolution, namely

\phi(x)=4\pi G\big(|x|^{-1}\ast|\psi|^{2}\big),

which plugged into (
1.5
) leads us (modulo a scaling of coefficients) to (
1.4
). Looking for a solution
\psi
of (
1.4
) in the form
\psi(x,t)=e^{-it}u(x)
we see that
u
satisfies (
1.3
).

More recently, the equation (
1.3
) is encountered in the literature under the name of Choquard or Choquard-Pekar equation. Precisely, (
1.3
) was introduced in 1954 by S.I. Pekar
[
26
]
as a model in quantum theory of a Polaron at rest (see also

[
7
]
) and in 1976, P. Choquard used (
1.3
) in a certain approximation to Hartree-Fock theory of one component plasma (see
[
20
]
).
The first mathematical study of (
1.3
) appeared in the mid 1970s and is due to E.H. Lieb
[
20
]
, followed by P.-L. Lions’ works
[
21
,
22
]
.

The inequality

-\Delta u\geq(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\quad\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B}_{1},

(1.6)

was first discussed in
[
25
]
. Let us note that (
1.6
) corresponds to (
1.1
) in the cone
\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\Omega}
where
\Omega=S^{N-1}
. The approach in
[
25
]
relies on the so-called Agmon-Allegretto-Piepenbrink positivity principle.
Quasilinear versions of (
1.6
) (again in exterior domains) are studied in
[
9
]
while the polyharmonic case is discussed in
[
10
]
( see also
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
8
,
11
,
12
]
for more contexts in which (
1.6
) occurs).

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work dealing with semilinear elliptic inequalities in cone-like domains featuring convolution terms. The local cases

-\Delta u=u^{p}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad-\Delta u\geq u^{p}

in a cone-like domain have been studied in
[
1
,
2
,
16
,
17
,
23
]
. In the current work we derive an a priori estimate result which we provide in Proposition
3.1
below. Our method is inspired by
[
3
]
(see also
[
9
]
) where integral estimates are obtained to study local inequalities of type
-\Delta_{p}u\geq|x|^{\sigma}u^{q}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
. Due to the geometric features of our cone domain
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}
, we cannot handle
W^{1,1}_{loc}
-solutions as in
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
, and thus have to restrict ourselves to the class
C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho})\cap C(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}})
. The test function we construct (see (
3.2
) below) is not a proper
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho})
function, rather a mere element in the space
C^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega^{\prime}}^{R,4R}})
, where
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
is a subdomain and
R>\rho
is large. Nonetheless, Proposition
3.1
below provides us with the right tool to derive the nonexistence of a positive solution to (
1.1
) under some conditions on the exponents and parameters.

Apart from the a priori estimates described above, another crucial tool in our approach is the Hardy inequality on cone-like domains established in
[
23
]
:

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}\geq C_{H,\Omega}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{|x|^{2}}dx\quad\mbox{ for all }\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}),

(1.7)

where
C_{H,\Omega}=\lambda_{1}+(\frac{N-2}{2})^{2}
and
\lambda_{1}
denotes the first eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Omega
subject to Dirichlet boundary condition. As obtained in
[
23
]
the constant
C_{H,\Omega}
in (
1.7
) is optimal.
Throughout this paper, we denote by
\phi
the corresponding eigenfunction which we normalize as
\phi>0
in
\Omega
and
\max_{\overline{\Omega}}\phi=1
.

For
\mu\leq C_{H,\Omega}
the quadratic equation

\gamma(\gamma+N-2)=\lambda_{1}-\mu

(1.8)

has two real roots which satisfy
\gamma_{*}<0\leq\gamma^{*}
.

Our first result on (
1.1
) establishes conditions under which no positive solutions exist.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Nonexistence)

Assume
0\leq\alpha<N
,
p,q>0
and
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.

(a)

If
\mu>C_{H,\Omega}
then (
1.1
) has no positive solutions.

(b)

If
\mu\leq C_{H,\Omega}
and one of the following conditions hold, then (
1.1
) has no positive solutions.

(i)

\displaystyle p\leq\frac{N-\alpha}{|\gamma_{*}|}
.

(ii)

\displaystyle p+q<1+\frac{N-\alpha+2}{\lvert\gamma_{*}\rvert}
.

(iii)

\displaystyle p+q=1+\frac{N-\alpha+2}{|\gamma_{*}|}
and
q>1
.

We next turn to the existence of positive solutions to (
1.1
). Under the assumption

q>1+\max\left\{0,\frac{2-\alpha}{|\gamma_{*}|}\right\},

(1.9)

we provide optimal conditions in terms of
p,q,\alpha
and
\mu
for the existence of a positive solution.
Precisely, we have:

Theorem 1.2
.

(Existence)

Assume
\mu\leq C_{H,\Omega}
,
0\leq\alpha<N
and (
1.9
) holds.
Then, inequality (
1.1
) has a positive solution in
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}
if and only if

p>\frac{N-\alpha}{|\gamma_{*}|}\quad\mbox{ and }\;\quad p+q>1+\frac{N-\alpha+2}{|\gamma_{*}|}.

(1.10)

1.1
) in the positive quadrant of the
pq
-plane are depicted in Figure

below in the case

.

For
\alpha=0
the inequality (

) reads

\mathscr{L}_{H}u\geq\Big(\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}u^{p}(y)dy\Big)u^{q}\quad\mbox{ in }\;\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}.

(1.11)

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
we deduce the following result.

Corollary 1.3
.

(i)

If
\mu>C_{H,\Omega}
then inequality (
1.11
) has no positive solutions.

(ii)

If
\mu\leq C_{H,\Omega}
and

then (
1.11
) has a positive solution if and only if

.

Our approach can be extended to system of inequalities of type

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\mathscr{L}}_{H}u:=-\Delta u-\frac{\mu}{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}u\geq\big(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast v^{p}\big)v^{q}\\[7.22743pt]

(1.12)

where
p,q>0
and
s>1
. The system (
1.12
) contains a combination of the local nonlinearity
u^{s}
and nonlocal nonlinearities given by the convolution operator. As in the case of a single inequality, we say that
(u,v)
is a positive solution of (
1.12
) if:

•

u,v\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho})\cap C(\overline{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}})
and
\big(|x|^{-\alpha}\ast v^{p}\big)v^{q}<\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}
.

•

u,v>0
in
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}
satisfy (
1.12
) pointwise.

Semilinear elliptic systems of inequalities in cone-like domains
were discussed in
[
18
,
24
]
(see also
[

]
for time dependent inequalities).
In the case of (

1.12
).

The main result on the system (
1.12
) is stated below.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
p,q>0
and
0\leq\alpha<N
.

(a)

If
\mu>C_{H,\Omega}
then (
1.12
) has no positive solutions.

(b)

Assume
\mu\leq C_{H,\Omega}
.

(i)

If
s>1
,
p+q\geq 2
and

p\leq\frac{N-\alpha}{|\gamma_{*}|}\quad\mbox{or}\quad p+q<\frac{1}{s}\Big(1+\frac{2}{|\gamma_{*}|}\Big)+\frac{N-\alpha+2}{|\gamma_{*}|},

then (
1.12
) has no positive solutions.

(ii)

If
\displaystyle s>1+\frac{2}{|\gamma_{*}|}
,
\displaystyle q>1+\max\Big\{0,\frac{2-\alpha}{|\gamma_{*}|}\Big\}
and

p>\frac{N-\alpha}{|\gamma_{*}|}\quad\mbox{and}\quad p+q>\frac{1}{s}\Big(1+\frac{2}{|\gamma_{*}|}\Big)+\frac{N-\alpha+2}{|\gamma_{*}|},

then (
1.12
) has positive solutions.

Theorem
1.4
yields a new critical curve given by

p+q=\frac{1}{s}\Big(1+\frac{2}{|\gamma_{*}|}\Big)+\frac{N-\alpha+2}{|\gamma_{*}|},

which defines the regions of existence and nonexistence of a positive solution to (
1.12
). We may regard the quantity
p+q
as the global exponent of
v
in the first inequality of (
1.12
).

2
Preliminary Results

In this section we derive some preliminary results for our approach.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
u\in C^{2}(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho})
is positive and satisfies
\mathscr{L}_{H}u\geq 0
in
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}
, then
\mu\leq C_{H,\Omega}
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho})
. Multiply the inequality
\mathscr{L}_{H}u\geq 0
by
\frac{\varphi^{2}}{u}
and integrate over
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}
. We deduce

-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{u}\Delta udx\geq\mu\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}dx.

By Green’s formula, one has

\begin{split}-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{u}\Delta udx&=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\nabla u\nabla\Big(\frac{\varphi^{2}}{u}\Big)dx\\
&=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\left(\frac{2\varphi}{u}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla u-\frac{\varphi^{2}}{u^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}\right)dx\\
&\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}dx.\end{split}

It follows that

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\lvert\nabla\varphi\rvert^{2}dx\geq-\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{u}\Delta udx\geq\mu\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{\varphi^{2}}{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}dx.

Furthermore, by Hardy’s inequality (
1.7
) and the optimality of the constant
C_{H,\Omega}
we have

\mu\leq C_{H,\Omega}
.
∎

In the result below we derive various integral estimates on cone domains involving the kernel
|x|^{-\alpha}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset S^{N-1}
be a subdomain,
\rho>0
and
0\leq\alpha<N
.

(i)

If
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho})
is positive, then there exists
C>0
such that

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\geq C|x|^{-\alpha}\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{2\rho}.

(ii)

If
f:\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}\to[0,\infty)
is measurable and there exists a subdomain
\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega
and
c,\beta>0
such that
f(x)\geq c|x|^{-\beta}
in
\mathcal{C}_{\Omega_{0}}^{\rho}
, then

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy=+\infty&&\quad\mbox{ if }\;N\geq\alpha+\beta\\[3.61371pt]
&\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\geq C|x|^{N-\alpha-\beta}&&\quad\mbox{ if }\;N<\alpha+\beta\end{aligned}\right.\quad\mbox{ in }\;\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{2\rho},

where
C>0
is a constant.

(iii)

If
f:\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}\to[0,\infty)
is measurable and satisfies

\displaystyle f(x)\leq c|x|^{-\beta}\log^{\tau}(1+|x|)\quad\mbox{ in }\;\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho},

for some
c>0
,
\tau\geq 0
and
\beta>N-\alpha>0
, then there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C|x|^{N-\alpha-\beta}\log^{\tau}(1+|x|)&&\quad\mbox{ if }\;\beta<N\\
&C|x|^{-\alpha}\log^{1+\tau}(1+|x|)&&\quad\mbox{ if }\;\beta=N\\
&C|x|^{-\alpha}\log^{\tau}(1+|x|)&&\quad\mbox{ if }\;\beta>N\end{aligned}\right.\quad\mbox{ in }\;\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{2\rho}.

Proof.

(i) For any
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{2\rho}
and
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{3\rho/2,2\rho}
we have

|x-y|\leq|x|+|y|\leq 2|x|
. Thus,

|x|^{-\alpha}\ast f\geq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{3\rho/2,2\rho}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\geq C\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{3\rho/2,2\rho}}\frac{f(y)}{(2|x|)^{\alpha}}dy=C|x|^{-\alpha}.

(ii) We use the spherical coordinates: each
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega_{0}}
can be written as

\begin{cases}x_{1}=r\sin\theta_{1}\sin\theta_{2}\cdots\sin\theta_{N-2}\sin\theta_{N-1},\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle x_{k}=r\cos\theta_{k-1}\prod_{j=k}^{N-1}\sin\theta_{j},\;2\leq k\leq N-2,\\[7.22743pt]
x_{N-1}=r\cos\theta_{N-1},\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
r>0
. Because
\Omega_{0}
is a subdomain of
S^{N-1}
, each
\theta_{k}
(
1\leq k\leq N-1
) belongs to a nondegenerate interval
I_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Let
|y|\geq 2|x|\geq 2\rho
. Then
|x-y|\leq|x|+|y|\leq 3|y|/2
so that

\displaystyle|x|^{-\alpha}\ast f

\displaystyle\geq c\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega_{0}}^{2|x|}}\frac{|y|^{-\beta}}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\geq C\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega_{0}}^{2|x|}}|y|^{-\alpha-\beta}dy

\displaystyle=C\int_{2|x|}^{\infty}r^{N-1-\alpha-\beta}\,dr=\begin{cases}+\infty&\mbox{ if }N\geq\alpha+\beta,\\[3.61371pt]
C|x|^{N-\alpha-\beta}&\mbox{ if }N<\alpha+\beta,\end{cases}

and the conclusion follows.

(iii) Let
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{2\rho}
. We split the convolution integral as follow:

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy=\left\{\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{2\lvert x\rvert}}+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\lvert x\rvert/2,2\lvert x\rvert}}+\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho,\lvert x\rvert/2}}\right\}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy.

(2.2)

To estimate the first integral in the right-hand side of (
2.2
) let
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{2|x|}
. Then
\lvert x-y\rvert\geq|y|-|x|\geq\frac{\lvert y\rvert}{2}
so that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{2|x|}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{|y|^{-\beta}\log^{\tau}(1+|y|)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho}}\frac{|y|^{-\beta}\log^{\tau}(1+|y|)}{(|y|/2)^{\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{2\lvert x\rvert}^{\infty}t^{N-\alpha-\beta-1}\log^{\tau}(1+t)dt

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{N-\alpha-\beta}\log^{\tau}(1+\lvert x\rvert).

For
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\lvert x\rvert/2,2\lvert x\rvert}
we have
\lvert x-y\rvert\leq|x|+|y|\leq 3\lvert x\rvert
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\lvert x\rvert/2,2\lvert x\rvert}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\beta}\log^{\tau}(1+\lvert x\rvert)\int_{\lvert x-y\rvert\leq 3\lvert x\rvert}\frac{dy}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{\alpha}}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{N-\alpha-\beta}\log^{\tau}(1+\lvert x\rvert).

Finally, for
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho,\lvert x\rvert/2}
we have
\frac{\lvert x\rvert}{2}\leq\lvert x-y\rvert\leq\frac{3\lvert x\rvert}{2}
and

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho,\lvert x\rvert/2}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy&\leq C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho,\lvert x\rvert/2}}\lvert y\rvert^{-\beta}\log^{\tau}(1+\lvert y\rvert)dy\\[3.61371pt]
&=C\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}\int_{\rho}^{\lvert x\rvert/2}t^{N-\beta-1}\log^{\tau}(1+t)dt.\end{split}

It follows that

\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega}^{\rho,\lvert x\rvert/2}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}dy\leq C\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lvert x\rvert^{N-\alpha-\beta}\log^{\tau}(1+\lvert x\rvert)&&\text{ if }\beta<N,\\
&\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}\log^{1+\tau}(1+\lvert x\rvert)&&\text{ if }\beta=N,\\
&\lvert x\rvert^{-\alpha}\log^{\tau}(1+\lvert x\rvert)&&\text{ if }\beta>N.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.5)

Now, we combine (
2.2
)-(

) to achieve the conclusion.
∎

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}^{\rho}}(|x|^{-\alpha}*u^{p})u^{q-m}\phi_{\varepsilon}\eta_{R}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle+m\int_{\mathcal{C}_{{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}^{R,4R}}u^{-m-1}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\phi_{\varepsilon}\eta_{R}^{\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{C}_{{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}^{R,4R}}u^{-m}\nabla u\nabla(\phi_{\varepsilon}\eta_{R}^{\lambda})-\mu\int_{\mathcal{C}_{{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}^{R,4R}}\frac{u}{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-m}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}^{R,4R}}\nabla u^{1-m}\nabla(\phi_{\varepsilon}\eta_{R}^{\lambda})-\mu\int_{\mathcal{C}_{{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}^{R,4R}}\frac{u}{\lvert x\rvert^{2}}\varphi.

(3.12)

On the center of near-group fusion category of type ℤ 3 + 6 \mathbb{Z}_{3}+6
Zhiqiang Yu

Abstract
Let 𝒜 \mathcal{A} be a near-group fusion category of type ℤ 3 + 6 \mathbb{Z}_{3}+6 . We show that there is a modular tensor equivalence 𝒵 ⁡ ( 𝒜 ) ≅ 𝒞 ⁡ ( ℤ 3 , η ) ⊠ 𝒞 ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 , 9 ) ℤ 3 0 \mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{Z}_{3},\eta)\boxtimes\mathcal{C}(\mathfrak{sl}_{3},9)_{\mathbb{Z}_{3}}^{0} . Moreover, we construct two non-trivial faithful extensions of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} explicitly, whose Drinfeld centers can also be obtained from representation categories quantum groups at root of unity. Keywords: Drinfeld center; near-group fusion category Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 18M20 ⋅ \cdot 81R50

Introduction

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
is a semisimple finite tensor category. Let
G
be a finite group. A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is called pointed if its simple objects are all invertible
[
7
]
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{C}
is tensor equivalent to the category
\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
of finite-dimensional
G
-graded vector spaces over
\mathbb{C}
, where
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,\mathbb{C}^{*})
is a (normalized)
3
-cocycle, which provides the associator of the monoidal structure of
\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}

[
6
,
7
]
.

\mathcal{C}
is called a near-group fusion category if all of the isomorphism classes of its simple objects except one are invertible
[
21
]
. Specifically, let
n
be a non-negative integer and let
G
be the finite group of isomorphism classes of
\mathcal{C}
, and
X
be the unique non-invertible simple object of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the Grothendieck ring
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
is determined by the following fusion relations

\displaystyle g\cdot h=gh,g\cdot X=X\cdot g=X,X\cdot X=\sum_{g\in G}g+nX,\forall g,h\in G.

We will say
\mathcal{C}
is a near-group fusion category of type
G+n
. It turns out that the group
G
and integer
n
have to meet strict restrictions to guarantee that there exists a near-group fusion category, see
[
9
,
11
,
21
]
and the references therein for details. For example, if
\text{FPdim}(X)
is irrational, then
n
is a multiple of the order of
|G|

[
17
, Appendix A]
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, it was proved in
[
13

that if there is a near-group fusion category of type
\mathbb{Z}_{3}+n

. The existences of fusion categories in the first three cases was clear then, and the existence of near-group fusion category of type

was later proved in
[

Preliminaries

In this section, we will recall some most used definitions and properties about fusion categories and modular fusion categories, we refer the readers to
[
4
,
6
,
7
,
15
]
to standard conclusions for fusion categories and braided fusion categories.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a fusion category,
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{C}
. Then there is a unique ring homomorphism
\text{FPdim}(-)
, called the Frobenius-Perron homomorphism, from the Grothendieck ring
\text{Gr}(\mathcal{C})
to
\mathbb{C}
such that
\text{FPdim}(X)
is a positive algebraic integer for all non-zero object
X
of
\mathcal{C}

[
6
,
7
]
. The sum

\displaystyle\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\text{FPdim}(X)^{2}

is called the Frobenius-Perron dimension of fusion category
\mathcal{C}
.

A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is spherical if
\mathcal{C}
admits a spherical structure
j
. Specifically,
j
is a natural isomorphism from identity tensor functor
\text{id}_{\mathcal{C}}
to the double dual tensor functor
(-)^{**}
and
\dim_{j}(X)=\dim_{j}(X^{*})
for all objects
X
of
\mathcal{C}
, where
\dim_{j}(X)
is the quantum dimension of an object
X
is defined by the (categorical) trace of
\text{id}_{X}
, that is,

\displaystyle\dim_{j}(X)=\text{Tr}(\text{id}_{X}):=\text{ev}_{X}\circ(j_{X}\circ\text{id}_{X})\otimes\text{id}_{X^{*}}\circ\text{coev}_{X},

here we suppress the associativity and unit constraints of
\mathcal{C}
. Then the global (or categorical) dimension of the spherical fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is defined as

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{C}):=\sum_{X\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})}\dim_{j}(X)^{2}.

The global dimension
\dim(\mathcal{C})
is independent of the choice of the spherical structure of
\mathcal{C}
. In this following, we will fix a spherical structure
j
on
\mathcal{C}
and denote
\dim_{j}(-)
by
\dim(-)
. A fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is pseudo-unitary if
\text{FPdim}(\mathcal{C})=\dim(\mathcal{C})
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided fusion category with braiding
c
. Then
\mathcal{C}
is called a pre-modular fusion category if
\mathcal{C}
is spherical. Let
\theta
be the corresponding ribbon structure of
\mathcal{C}
. The two matrices
S=(S_{X,Y})
and
T=(\delta_{X,Y}\theta_{X})
are called the
S
-matrix and
T
-matrix of
\mathcal{C}
, respectively, where
S_{X,Y}:=\text{Tr}(c_{Y,X}c_{X,Y})
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{C})
.
A modular fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is a pre-modular fusion category such that the
S
-matrix
S
is non-degenerate
[
4
,
6
]
. Drinfeld centers of spherical fusion categories are always modular
[
15
]
, for example.
It is well-known that a braided pointed fusion category
\text{Vec}_{G}^{\omega}
is modular if and only if
G
is a metric group equipped with a non-degenerate quadratic form
\eta

[
4
,
6
]
. In this paper, we will use
\mathcal{C}(G,\eta)
to denote the modular fusion category determined by metric group
(G,\eta)
.

Recall that a commutative algebra
A
in a braided fusion category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be a connected étale algebra if the category
\mathcal{C}_{A}
of right
A
-modules in
\mathcal{C}
is semisimple and
\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(I,A)=\mathbb{C}

[
3
, Definition 3.1]
. For example, let
\text{Rep}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be a Tannakian fusion subcategory, then the function (or regular) algebra
\text{Fun}(G)\in\text{Rep}(G)
is a connected étale algebra
[
3
,
4
]
.

Let
A
be a connected étale algebra, and let
(M,\mu_{M})\in\mathcal{C}_{A}
, the fusion category of right
A
-modules in
\mathcal{C}
, where
\mu_{M}:M\otimes A\to M
is the right
A
-module morphism of
M
. Then
M
is called a local (or dyslectic) module if
\mu_{M}=\mu_{M}\circ(c_{A,M}c_{M,A})

[
3
,
12
,
18
]
. The category of local modules over
A
is a braided fusion category
[
12
,
18
]
, which will be denoted by
\mathcal{C}_{A}^{0}
below. In addition, if
\mathcal{C}
is modular, so is
\mathcal{C}_{A}^{0}

[
3
,
4
,
12
]
.

Proposition 3.3

For simple objects

Theorem 3.6

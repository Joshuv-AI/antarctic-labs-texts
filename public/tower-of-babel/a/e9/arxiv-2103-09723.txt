Maximally efficient quantum thermal machines fuelled by nonequilibrium steady states
Tiago F. F. Santos, Francesco Tacchino, Dario Gerace, Michele Campisi, Marcelo F. Santos

Abstract
The concept of thermal machines has evolved from the canonical steam engine to the recently proposed nanoscopic quantum systems as working fluids. The latter obey quantum open system dynamics and frequently operate in non-equilibrium conditions. However, the role of this dynamics in the overall performance of quantum heat engines remains an open problem. Here, we analyse and optimize the efficiency and power output of two-stage quantum heat engines fuelled by non-equilibrium steady states. In a charging first stage, the quantum working fluid consisting of a qutrit or two coupled qubits is connected to two reservoirs at different temperatures, which establish a heat current that stores ergotropy in the system; the second stage comprises a coherent driving force that extracts work from the machine in finite a amount of time; finally, the external drive is switched off and the machine enters a new cycle.

II
Theoretical Background

We hereby analyze the design principles and general operating performances of non-equilibrium quantum thermal machines. In our proposed framework, originally introduced within an idealized setup in Ref.
MFS2020
, these devices operate between two reservoirs at different temperatures, establishing a heat current through the working fluid made of a quantum system with well characterized energy spectrum. Such steady energy flow “charges” the system by leading it to an operational steady state
\rho_{OSS}
that is “active” in the sense that it has positive ergotropy (namely one can withdraw energy from it by applying a unitary operation)

Ergotropy
. In the subsequent stroke, accordingly, a coherent drive is applied that withdraws the energy.

In our description of the cycle the staring point of the cycle is the state
\rho_{OSS}
. In the first stage, an external drive is turned on for a finite time
\tau_{d}
, producing work and transforming the active state
\rho_{OSS}
into a passive
PassiveStates
zero-ergotropy state
\rho(\tau_{d})
. Then, in the second stage, the external drive is switched off and the heat current takes a time
\tau_{r}
to restore the machine to
\rho_{OSS}
leaving it ready for a new cycle. In the limit of large
\tau_{r}
the operational steady state coincides with what is commonly referred to as the Non-equilibrium steady state,
\rho_{NESS}
.

We will assume a Markovian interaction between the quantum system and the heat reservoirs, such that the dynamics of both stages is governed by a master equation in the Lindblad form
OpenSystems
(In the following we shall adopt
\hbar=1
and
k_{B}=1
):

\dot{\rho}=-i[H(t),\rho]+\mathcal{L}(\rho).

(1)

The Hamiltonian reads
H(t)=H_{0}+V(t)
, where
H_{0}
is the free Hamiltonian of the system and
V(t)
accounts for the coupling with the external work extraction drive (e.g., an electromagnetic field). The non-unitary part
\mathcal{L}(\rho)
reads

\mathcal{L}(\rho)=\sum_{j}L_{j}(\rho)=\sum_{j}\Gamma_{j}\left[J_{j}\rho J_{j}^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{J_{j}^{\dagger}J_{j},\rho\}\right],

(2)

where
\{A,B\}=AB+BA
.
\Gamma_{j}
are the transition rates and
J_{j}
the respective jumps induced in the system by the heat reservoirs. In the recharging stage the external drive is switched off (
V(t)=0
) and the energy gained during the recharging balances with the energy extracted from the system during the discharging, so that there is no variation in the internal energy of the system in a full cycle, i.e.
\Delta U_{cycle}=0
.

At the end of the cycle, the total heat exchanged with the hot reservoir and the work performed by the machine are given by
Alicki1979

\displaystyle Q^{H}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{H}+Q_{d}^{H}

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}dt\hskip 0.56917pt\operatorname{Tr}[L_{H}(\rho(t))H(t)]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}dt\hskip 0.56917pt\operatorname{Tr}[L_{H}(\rho(t))H_{0}],

W=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}dt\hskip 0.56917pt\operatorname{Tr}[\rho(t)\dot{H}(t)],

(4)

where
L_{H}
is the superoperator that represents the coupling to the hot reservoir,
\rho(t)
is the density matrix of the working fluid, and
\tau=\tau_{r}+\tau_{d}
is the total duration of the cycle. The efficiency of the machine is given by
\eta=-W/Q^{H}
, and its delivered power can be defined as
\mathcal{P}=-W/\tau
.

We remark that the analysis performed here significantly extends the scope and generality of previous works
MFS2020
by relaxing many simplifying assumptions and providing a compelling study of more realistic experimental conditions. In particular, Ref.
MFS2020
focused on the ideal scenario of an adiabatic discharging stage, where
\tau_{d}\ll\tau_{r}
(
Q_{d}^{H}\rightarrow 0
). In such an approximation, the heat exchange only takes place in the charging stage, the period of the cycle becomes
\tau\approx\tau_{r}
, and the total heat exchanged between the system and the hot bath in a cycle approximates to

Q^{H}\approx Q_{r}^{H}=\int_{0}^{\tau}dt\hskip 0.56917pt\operatorname{Tr}[L_{H}(\rho^{(}t))H_{0}].

(5)

Moreover, in the idealized scenario of Ref.
MFS2020
, the unitary evolution due to
V(t)
was replaced by an ideal unitary operation,
U
, extracting the maximum amount of energy from
\rho_{OSS}
and taking it to the completely passive state
\tilde{\rho}_{OSS}=U\rho_{OSS}U^{-1}
. In this case, the work generated in the cycle equals the full ergotropy of state
\rho_{OSS}
given by
Ergotropy

\mathcal{E}=\sum_{k,j}r_{k}E_{j}(|\langle r_{k}|E_{j}\rangle|^{2}-\delta_{kj}),

(6)

where
E_{j}
are the eigenenergies of
H_{0}
ordered in increasing magnitude, i.e
E_{i+1}>E_{i}
, and
r_{k}
are the eigenvalues of
\rho_{OSS}
ordered in decreasing magnitude, i.e.
r_{k+1}<r_{k}
, with respective eigenvectors
\left|{E_{j}}\right\rangle
and
\left|{r_{j}}\right\rangle
. Here,
U
can be represented as
U=\sum_{k}\left|{E_{k}}\right\rangle\left\langle{r_{k}}\right|
. This ideal situation produces a cycle of maximal efficiency
\eta_{M}=\mathcal{E}/Q_{r}^{H}
and delivered power
\mathcal{P}_{M}=\mathcal{E}/\tau_{r}
. These values are the upper bounds and provide benchmarks for the more realistic scenarios that we analyse in this work, where for example the first stage takes finite time and cannot be considered fully adiabatic (
Q_{d}^{H}>0
). Such upper limit is achievable whenever
\tau_{d}\ll\Gamma_{j}^{-1}
, i.e. when the characteristic time of the discharging stage is much smaller than the inverse of the heat exchange rates.

To summarize, at difference with previous studies, the framework introduced so far essentially solely relies on the Markovianity assumption of the system-reservoirs interaction, and remains fully general in all other aspects. In particular, it can be applied to any quantum system used as the working medium. In the following, we will concentrate our studies on the two simplest examples that represent minimal setups for the operation of a non-equilibrium quantum thermal machine between positive temperature reservoirs: a qutrit and two coupled qubits, respectively.

Results

III.1
A qutrit as the working fluid

We consider a single three-level system, a qutrit, as the simplest but nontrivial working fluid, as originally done in the pioneering work of Ref.

Adapting the schemes previously presented

\mathcal{P}_{SC}^{V}=(\omega_{i}-\omega_{e})K_{V}
and
\mathcal{P}_{SC}^{\Lambda}=\omega_{e}K_{\Lambda}
, where

K_{\Lambda}=\frac{\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}-\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}}{2(\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-})+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}}

(21)

Notice that, differently from the efficiencies, the output powers depend not only on the respective spectra, but also on heat current rates
\Gamma_{k}^{j\pm}
, where
k=e,i
for
j=V
and
k=g,e
for
j=\Lambda
. The most powerful configuration will depend on the specific details of the heat flow and the energy levels of the working fluid. An interesting comparison can be done, for example, by considering fixed and equal spectra for both configurations, the same vacuum coupling to each reservoir
\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{e}=\gamma_{g}=\gamma
,
\Gamma\rightarrow 0
and
T\rightarrow 0
. In this case, we obtain

\lim_{T,\Gamma\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{P}_{SC}^{V}}{\mathcal{P}_{SC}^{\Lambda}}=\frac{\omega_{i}}{\omega_{e}}-1

(22)

i.e. the
V
configuration delivers more power if
\omega_{i}/\omega_{e}>2
, while the
\Lambda
configuration maximises the power output if
1<\omega_{i}/\omega_{e}<2
.

III.2
Two coupled qubits as the working fluid

We now turn our attention to a working fluid composed of two coupled qubits
MFS2020
, described by the Hamiltonian

H_{0}=\omega_{0}(\sigma_{+}^{(1)}\sigma_{-}^{(1)}+\sigma_{+}^{(2)}\sigma_{-}^{(2)})+\lambda(\sigma_{+}^{(1)}\sigma_{-}^{(2)}+h.c)

(23)

where
2\sigma_{\pm}^{j}=\sigma_{x}^{j}\pm i\sigma_{y}^{j}
,
j=1,2
, and
\{\sigma_{k}\}
for
k=x,y,z
are the Pauli matrices. The heat current is established by coupling the system to two reservoirs of temperatures
\mathcal{T}_{A}>\mathcal{T}_{S}
that act respectively on its symmetric (S) and anti-symmetric (A) subspaces. The energy spectrum has four levels,
\{|G\rangle,|S\rangle,|A\rangle,|E\rangle\}
, already ordered by increasing energy
\{E_{G},E_{S},E_{A},E_{E}\}
, where
|G\rangle=|gg\rangle
,
|S\rangle=\frac{|ge\rangle+|eg\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}
,
|A\rangle=\frac{|ge\rangle-|eg\rangle}{\sqrt{2}}
and
|E\rangle=|ee\rangle
. In the weak coupling limit (
\lambda\ll\omega_{0}
), levels
|S\rangle
and
|A\rangle
are close to each other and their energy distance to levels
|G\rangle
and
|E\rangle
are approximately the same. These conditions would allow us to assume, as done in Ref.
MFS2020
, that the bare coupling rates
\gamma_{j}
of each non-unitary transition are approximately the same and the problem is restricted to four overall transition rates given by
\Gamma_{A,S}^{+}=\gamma_{0}\bar{n}_{A,S}
and
\Gamma_{A,S}^{-}=\gamma_{0}(\bar{n}_{A,S}+1)
, where
\bar{n}_{A,S}=(e^{\frac{\omega_{0}}{T_{A,S}}}-1)^{-1}
. However, in the more general scenario that we will consider here, each coupling rate may depend on the energy gap of the respective transition. In such case, the heat current is established by combining eight different non-unitary channels, two for each one of the four incoherently coupled transitions.

Level scheme for the two qubits coupled as the working fluid and the transition rates induced by the coupling with the heat baths. Here

.

Similar to the qutrit case, the steady state
\rho_{NESS}
is diagonal in the total spin basis, i.e,
\rho_{NESS}=\sum_{i}r_{i_{NESS}}\left|{i}\right\rangle\left\langle{i}\right|
, where
i=\{G,S,A,E\}
. However, different from the previous working fluid,
\rho_{NESS}
can in general be entangled
tacchino_steady_2018
. Whenever
r_{A_{NESS}}>r_{S_{NESS}}
, the ergotropy,
\mathcal{E}_{NESS}
, stored in
\rho_{NESS}
is given by (see Appendix C)

\mathcal{E}_{NESS}=2\lambda(r_{A_{NESS}}-r_{S_{NESS}})

(24)

Once again, the machine can operate in cycles of arbitrary periods
\tau
. The operational steady states
\rho_{OSS}=\sum_{i}r_{i}\left|{i}\right\rangle\left\langle{i}\right|
will also be diagonal in the
i=\{G,S,A,E\}
basis, and the ergotropy stored in
\rho_{OSS}
will also be given by

\mathcal{E}_{\tau}=2\lambda(r_{A}-r_{S}).

(25)

However, in general,
\mathcal{E}_{\tau}\leq\mathcal{E}_{NESS}
, equality being reached for
\tau\rightarrow\infty
. Similarly to the qutrit case, in the ideal scenario of an adiabatic first stage (discharging), the unitary operation
U_{\mathcal{E}}
that extracts the maximum amount of energy stored in the system is formally a SWAP between the
\left|{A}\right\rangle
and
\left|{S}\right\rangle
populations. If we now consider a realistic external drive producing such unitary,
V(t)=\epsilon\left(\left|{A}\right\rangle\left\langle{S}\right|e^{i2\lambda t}+\left|{S}\right\rangle\left\langle{A}\right|e^{-i2\lambda t}\right)
, where
E_{A}=\omega_{0}+\lambda
and
E_{S}=\omega_{0}-\lambda
, the work done by the system is given by

W=2i\epsilon\lambda\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}[\varrho_{AS}(t)-\varrho_{SA}(t)]dt.

(26)

If
\epsilon>>\Gamma_{A,S}^{k\pm}
,
k=G,E
, we obtain
W=-\mathcal{E}
as expected (see appendix C). The incoming heat from the hot bath
\mathcal{T}_{A}
during the discharging is given by

\displaystyle Q_{d}^{H}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}4\omega_{0}[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)]dt

\displaystyle+i\epsilon(\omega_{0}+\lambda)\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}[\varrho_{SA}(t)-\varrho_{AS}(t)]

\displaystyle-(\omega_{0}+\lambda)[\varrho_{A_{OSS}}-\varrho_{A}(\tau_{d})]

(27)

During recharging, there is no work performed by or on the system and the heat exchanged between the system and the hot bath is given by

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{H}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}4\omega_{0}[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)]dt

\displaystyle+(\omega_{0}+\lambda)[\varrho_{A_{OSS}}-\varrho_{A}(\tau_{d})],

(28)

This results in an efficiency of the machine given by

\eta=\frac{-W}{Q_{d}^{H}+Q_{r}^{H}}=\frac{1-\frac{E_{S}}{E_{A}}}{1+\alpha+\beta}

(29)

where,

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}4\omega_{0}[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)]dt}{i\epsilon(\omega_{0}+\lambda)\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}dt[\varrho_{SA}(t)-\varrho_{AS}(t)]}

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}4\omega_{0}[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)]dt}{i\epsilon(\omega_{0}+\lambda)\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}dt[\varrho_{SA}(t)-\varrho_{AS}(t)]}.

(30)

Notice that, contrary to the qutrit case, the existence of two (and not one) energy levels (
|G\rangle
and
|E\rangle
) incoherently coupled to the work producing subspace
\{|A\rangle,|S\rangle\}
results, in general, in a larger amount of incoming heat, lowering the efficiency of the machine. Here, the best efficiencies are reached when
\alpha+\beta
is minimized. Since the explicit dependence of
\alpha
and
\beta
on the parameters
\lambda
,
\epsilon
and

involves a rather technical calculation, we will only present below some numerical results for a particular example that already encompass all the relevant features of the general case. Before going there, however, we can still gain some useful insight by an analytical treatment of the short cycle (SC) regime, where the expressions simplify significantly. Gladly, as demonstrated in Ref.
MFS2020

In SC conditions, the ergotropy stored in
\rho_{OSS}

\mathcal{E}_{SC}=4\lambda\kappa\tau,

where
\Gamma_{k}^{\pm}=\Gamma_{A}^{k\pm}+\Gamma_{S}^{k\pm}
and

The heat absorbed from the hot bath
\mathcal{T}_{A}
is given by

\displaystyle Q^{H}_{SC}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{\Omega}[(\omega_{0}-\lambda)\Gamma_{G}^{+}(\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\Gamma_{S}^{E-}-\Gamma_{S}^{E+}\Gamma_{A}^{E-})

(32)

\displaystyle+(\omega_{0}+\lambda)\Gamma_{E}^{-}(\Gamma_{A}^{G+}\Gamma_{S}^{G-}-\Gamma_{S}^{G+}\Gamma_{A}^{G-})]\tau,

where

\Omega=\Gamma_{E}^{-}(\Gamma_{G}^{+}+\Gamma_{G}^{-})+\Gamma_{G}^{+}(\Gamma_{E}^{+}+\Gamma_{E}^{-}).

The efficiency of the machine operating in the ideal short cycle,
\eta_{SC}
, is given by

\eta_{SC}=\left(1-\frac{E_{S}}{E_{A}}\right)\frac{1-f}{1+\frac{E_{S}}{E_{A}}f}

where

f=\frac{\Gamma_{G}^{+}(\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\Gamma_{S}^{E-}-\Gamma_{S}^{E+}\Gamma_{A}^{E-})}{\Gamma_{E}^{-}(\Gamma_{A}^{G+}\Gamma_{S}^{G-}-\Gamma_{S}^{G+}\Gamma_{A}^{G-})}.

For a fixed
\lambda/\omega_{0}

\Gamma_{j}^{k-}>>\Gamma_{j}^{k+}
or if
\Gamma_{S}^{k-}>>\Gamma_{A}^{k-}

which is essentially the same obtained for the qutrit, except that, for coupled qubits, it is only achievable in the short cycle. Note that this is also the efficiency of the short cycle of the weak coupling regime, although here there is no pre-determined restriction over the ratio

From the perspective of the generated power, in the first case,

IV
Summary

In this work we have analysed the thermodynamics of a two-stroke quantum heat engine introducing a single qutrit as a working fluid, and comparing its performances to the case of a pair of coupled qubits. In both cases we have studied the machine operating both in the ideal short cycle limit as well as in a cycle with arbitrary duration
\tau
.

For the qutrit, we have seen that the efficiency of the machine does not depend on the duration of cycle but only on the energy spectrum of the working fluid. This happens because work is generated by driving a two-level subspace of the system that can be effectively considered to be in contact with a single negative temperature reservoir. The power output, however, is affected by the duration of the cycle and maximized in the ideal short cycle limit.

For the two coupled qubits as the working fluid we have seen that the efficiency depends on the spectrum of the working fluid, as well as the incoherent transition rates
\Gamma_{A,S}^{k\pm}
, where
k=G,E
, in the ideal short cycle and for a cycle with arbitrary duration
\tau
depends also on the coupling strength with the external coherent source
\epsilon
. Furthermore, we also saw that the efficiency for cycles with arbitrary duration are less or equal than the efficiency in the ideal short cycle, the latter occurs for long cycles when the temperature gradient is sufficiently small. We also observed that efficiency increases when the temperature difference decreases, a property that was also observed in
Thiago2020
. This was explained based on the fact that at low thermal gradient, the system remains closer to equilibrium, hence it operates closer to the ideal Carnot efficiency, and even though the latter diminishes with decreasing thermal bias, still, the engine efficiency grows.

\displaystyle\textrm{Tr}\left[\rho\left(e^{i(\omega_{i}-\omega_{e})t}\left|{e}\right\rangle\left\langle{i}\right|-e^{-i(\omega_{i}-\omega_{e})t}\left|{i}\right\rangle\left\langle{e}\right|\right)\right]dt,

\displaystyle W^{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}i\epsilon(\omega_{i}-\omega_{e})[\varrho_{ie}(t)-\varrho_{ei}(t)]dt,

where
\varrho_{jk}=\langle j|e^{iH_{0}t}\rho e^{-iH_{0}t}|k\rangle
and
H(t)=H_{0}+V^{V}(t)
.

The incoming heat from the hot bath in the discharging stage is given by

\displaystyle Q_{d}^{HV}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left[L_{i}^{V+}(\rho(t))+L_{i}^{V-}(\rho(t))\right]H(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\omega_{i}\left[\Gamma_{i}^{V+}\varrho_{gg}(t)-\Gamma_{i}^{V-}\varrho_{ii}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle-\frac{\epsilon\Gamma_{i}^{V-}}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\left[\varrho_{ie}(t)+\varrho_{ei}(t)\right]dt.

(49)

Note that
\varrho_{kk}=\rho_{kk}
. From Eq. (
1
), in the interaction picture, we have

\dot{\varrho}_{ii}=i\epsilon(\varrho_{ie}(t)-\varrho_{ei})+\Gamma_{i}^{V+}\varrho_{gg}-\Gamma_{i}^{V-}\varrho_{ii}

(50)

\dot{\varrho}_{ei}=-i\epsilon(\varrho_{ii}-\varrho_{ee})-\frac{\Gamma_{e}^{V-}}{2}\varrho_{ei}-\frac{\Gamma_{i}^{V-}}{2}\varrho_{ei}

(51)

In the discharging processes
\rho(0)=\rho_{OSS}
. Therefore, from Eq. (
51
) (and its c.c.) and remembering that
\varrho_{ie}(0)=\varrho_{ei}(0)=0
, it follows that
\varrho_{ei}(t)+\varrho_{ie}(t)=0
. Using this and Eq. (
50
) in Eq. (
VI.1
), we obtain

Q_{d}^{HV}=\omega_{i}\left[\varrho_{ii}(\tau_{d})-\varrho_{ii_{OSS}}\right]-\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}i\epsilon\omega_{i}\left[\varrho_{ie}(t)-\varrho_{ei}(t)\right]dt.

(52)

In the second stage (recharging), there is only heat exchange between the system and the reservoirs (
V(t)=0)
and we consider that we are not restricted to the short cycle limit
\sum_{j}\Gamma_{k}^{V\pm}\tau\ll 1
. In this situation, the heat exchanged with the hot reservoir is given by

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{HV}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left[L_{i}^{V+}(\rho(t))+L_{i}^{V-}(\rho(t))\right]H_{0}\right]dt

(53)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}\omega_{i}\left[\Gamma_{i}^{V+}\varrho_{gg}(t)-\Gamma_{i}^{V-}\varrho_{ii}(t)\right]dt.

In this stage, we have
\rho(\tau)=\rho_{OSS}
. From Eq. (
1
), in the interaction picture, for the recharging stage, we have
\dot{\varrho}_{ii}=\Gamma_{i}^{V+}\varrho_{gg}-\Gamma_{i}^{V-}\varrho_{ii}
, so

Q_{r}^{HV}=\omega_{i}\left[\varrho_{ii_{OSS}}-\varrho_{ii}(\tau_{d})\right]

(54)

Finally, the efficiency of the machine is given by

\eta_{V}=\frac{-W^{V}}{Q_{d}^{HV}+Q_{r}^{HV}}=1-\frac{\omega_{e}}{\omega_{i}},

(55)

which is the same as obtained for the short cycle, i.e. for the qutrit in V, the efficiency only depends on its energy levels. Again, maximum efficiency, tending to one, is obtained for
\omega_{e}\ll\omega_{i}
.

VI.2
Calculations for the Qutrit in
\Lambda

The system in
\Lambda
is very similar to the system in
V
, as expected. Once again, the operational steady state in the ideal short cycle is defined by the equation
\rho_{OSS}=\tilde{\rho}_{OSS}+\tau\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\rho}_{OSS})
, where
\tilde{\rho}_{OSS}=U\rho_{OSS}U^{-1}
but, now,
U=-i(\left|{g}\right\rangle\left\langle{e}\right|+\left|{e}\right\rangle\left\langle{g}\right|)+\left|{i}\right\rangle\left\langle{i}\right|
. The operational steady state is also diagonal in the energy basis:
\rho_{OSS}=\sum_{j}p_{j}\left|{E_{j}}\right\rangle\left\langle{E_{j}}\right|
,
j=g,e,i
.

The coupling of the system with the reservoirs is given by
\mathcal{L}(\rho)=\sum_{k}[L_{k}^{\Lambda+}+L_{k}^{\Lambda-}](\rho)
,
k=g,e
, where

L_{k}^{\Lambda+}(\rho)=\Gamma_{k}^{\Lambda+}\left[\sigma_{i,k}\rho\sigma_{k,i}-\frac{1}{2}\{\sigma_{k,k},\rho\}\right]

(56)

and

L_{k}^{\Lambda-}(\rho)=\Gamma_{k}^{\Lambda-}\left[\sigma_{k,i}\rho\sigma_{i,k}-\frac{1}{2}\{\sigma_{i,i},\rho\}\right]

(57)

From Eqs. (
56
) and (
57
), the populations of
\rho_{OSS}
are defined by the following equations:

\displaystyle p_{g}-p_{e}

\displaystyle=\tau(\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}p_{i}-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}p_{e})

(58)

\displaystyle p_{e}-p_{g}

\displaystyle=\tau(\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}p_{i}-\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}p_{g})

(59)

\displaystyle p_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}p_{e}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}p_{g}}{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}}.

(60)

The equations above allow us to calculate
\rho_{OSS}
in terms of transitions rates
\Gamma_{k}^{\Lambda\pm}
and the cycle duration
\tau
.

After some algebraic manipulation, we obtain

\displaystyle p_{e}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}-\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\tau}{\mu}

(61)

\displaystyle p_{g}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{e}\left[1-(\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+})\tau\right]+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\tau}{1+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\tau}

(62)

where,

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=2(\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-})+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}

\displaystyle-[\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}(\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-})+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}]\tau

(63)

Using the relations between
p_{e}
and
p_{g}
and keeping terms up to first order in
\tau

\left(\Gamma_{j}^{\Lambda\pm}\tau\ll 1\right)
, the ergotropy, given by
\mathcal{E}_{SC}^{\Lambda}=\omega_{e}(p_{e}-p_{g})
, becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{SC}^{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\omega_{e}\tau[(2\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+})p_{e}-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-})]

\displaystyle=\omega_{e}\tau\frac{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}}{2(\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-})+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}}.

(64)

Note that, the ergotropy is positive when
\frac{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}}{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}}>\frac{\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}}{\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}}
. It means that for the ergotropy to be positive the effective temperature
T_{g}^{\Lambda}=\frac{E_{i}}{\log{\left[\frac{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}}{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}}\right]}}
has to be larger than the effective temperature
T_{e}^{\Lambda}=\frac{E_{i}-E_{e}}{\log{\left[\frac{\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}}{\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}}\right]}}
. Using the same approximations made in (
VI.2
) and the relations between
p_{e}
and
p_{g}
, the heat exchange with the hot reservoir is given by

\displaystyle Q_{SC}^{H\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Tr}\left[H_{0}\left[L_{g}^{\Lambda+}(\tilde{\rho}_{OSS})+L_{g}^{\Lambda-}(\tilde{\rho}_{OSS})\right]\tau\right]

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}(\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}p_{e}-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}p_{i})\tau

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}\tau\left(\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}p_{e}-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\frac{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}p_{e}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}p_{g}}{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}}\right)

\displaystyle=\omega_{i}\tau\frac{(\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+})}{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}}p_{e}=\frac{\omega_{i}}{\omega_{e}}\mathcal{E}_{SC}^{\Lambda}

(65)

From Eqs. (
VI.2
) and (
VI.2
), the efficiency of the machine, in the short cycle limit (
SC
), is given by

\eta_{SC}^{\Lambda}=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{SC}^{\Lambda}}{Q_{SC}^{H\Lambda}}=\frac{\omega_{e}}{\omega_{i}}.

(66)

Note that, as in the V-type configuration, the efficiency does not depend on the transition rates
\Gamma_{k}^{\Lambda\pm}
, however the maximum efficiency, tending to one, is obtained when
\omega_{e}\approx\omega_{i}
.

The power of the machine at this limit of operation, given by
\mathcal{P}_{SC}^{\Lambda}=\frac{\mathcal{E}_{SC}^{\Lambda}}{\tau}
, reduces to

\mathcal{P}_{SC}^{\Lambda}=\omega_{e}\frac{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-}-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}}{2(\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda-})+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}+\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}}

(67)

Note that in the limit of maximum efficiency we have
\mathcal{P}_{SC}^{\Lambda}\approx\frac{Q_{SC}^{\Lambda}}{\tau}
.

Similar to the
V
-type configuration, a calculation out of the short cycle limit gives

\displaystyle W^{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\rho(t)\dot{H}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}i\epsilon\omega_{e}\left[\varrho_{eg}(t)-\varrho_{ge}(t)\right]dt

(68)

for the work extracted from the qutrit, where,
H(t)=H_{0}+V^{\Lambda}(t)
, and

\displaystyle Q_{d}^{H\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left[L_{g}^{\Lambda+}(\rho(t))+L_{g}^{\Lambda-}(\rho(t))\right]H(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\omega_{i}\left[\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}\varrho_{gg}(t)-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\varrho_{ii}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle-\frac{\epsilon\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\left[\varrho_{ge}(t)+\varrho_{eg}(t)\right]dt.

(69)

as the incoming heat from the hot source in the discharging stage.

From Eq. (
1
), in the interaction picture, we obtain the following equations of motion:

\dot{\varrho}_{gg}=-i\epsilon(\varrho_{eg}-\varrho_{ge})-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}\varrho_{gg}+\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\varrho_{ii}

\dot{\varrho}_{eg}=-i\epsilon(\varrho_{gg}-\varrho_{ee})-\frac{\Gamma_{e}^{\Lambda+}}{2}\varrho_{eg}-\frac{\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}}{2}\varrho_{eg}.

And, given that
\rho(0)=\rho_{OSS}
and that
\varrho_{ge}(0)=\varrho_{eg}(0)=0

\varrho_{ge}(t)+\varrho_{eg}(t)=0

\displaystyle Q_{d}^{H\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}i\epsilon\omega_{i}\left[\varrho_{ge}(t)-\varrho_{eg}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle-\omega_{i}\left[\varrho_{gg}(\tau_{d})-\varrho_{gg_{OSS}}\right]

(72)

In the second stage (recharging), the heat exchanged with the hot reservoir is given by

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{H\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\left[L_{g}^{\Lambda+}(\rho(t))+L_{g}^{\Lambda-}(\rho(t))\right]H_{0}\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}\omega_{i}\left[\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda+}\varrho_{gg}(t)-\Gamma_{g}^{\Lambda-}\varrho_{ii}(t)\right]dt.

In this stage we have
\rho(\tau)=\rho_{OSS}

Since

we obtain

Q_{r}^{H\Lambda}=\omega_{i}\left[\varrho_{gg}(\tau_{d})-\varrho_{gg_{OSS}}\right]

Finally, the efficiency of the machine is given by

\eta_{\Lambda}=\frac{-W^{\Lambda}}{Q_{d}^{H\Lambda}+Q_{r}^{H\Lambda}}=\frac{\omega_{i}}{\omega_{e}}

(75)

Note that the efficiency depends only on
\omega_{e}
and
\omega_{i}
as in the short cycle limit and the maximum efficiency, tending to one, is obtained for

.

VI.3
Two qubits calculations

To obtain the operational steady state
\rho_{OSS}
of the two qubits coupled operating in short cycles we have to solve the equation

\displaystyle Q_{d}^{H}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}4\omega_{0}\left[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle+i\epsilon(\omega_{0}+\lambda)\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\left[\varrho_{SA}(t)-\varrho_{AS}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle-(\lambda+\omega_{0})[\varrho_{A_{OSS}}-\varrho_{A}(\tau_{d})].

(103)

In
t=0
the system is in the operational steady state
\rho_{OSS}
that is diagonal in the energy basis, so by the equations of motions for the coherence we see that
\varrho_{AS}(t)+\varrho_{SA}(t)=0
. Under the condition where the swap between the populations is approximately instantaneous (
\tau_{d}\rightarrow 0
,
\epsilon>>\Gamma_{A,S}^{E\pm},\Gamma_{A,S}^{E\pm}
) we see that
Q_{d}^{H}\rightarrow 0
. It means that the process is adiabatic in the thermodynamic sense in this limit, as expected.

In the recharging process there is no external field (
\epsilon=0
), so
W=0
. Now we consider that we are not restricted to the short cycle limit, so the heat exchanged with the hot bath is given by

\displaystyle Q_{r}^{H}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\operatorname{Tr}\left[\mathcal{L}_{A}(\rho(t))H_{0}\right]dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}\{4\omega_{0}\left[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)\right]

\displaystyle+(\omega_{0}+\lambda)[2\Gamma_{A}^{G+}\varrho_{G}+2\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)

\displaystyle-2(\Gamma_{A}^{G-}+\Gamma_{A}^{E+})\varrho_{A}(t)]\}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}4\omega_{0}\left[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle+(\omega_{0}+\lambda)[\varrho_{A}(\tau)-\varrho_{A}(\tau_{d})]

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}4\omega_{0}\left[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)\right]dt

\displaystyle+(\omega+\lambda)[\varrho_{A_{OSS}}-\varrho_{A}(\tau_{d})].

(104)

In this stage the system back to the initial state
\rho_{OSS}
, so
\rho_{A}(\tau)=\rho_{A_{OSS}}
.

Finally, the efficiency of the machine is given by

\eta=\frac{-W}{Q_{d}^{H}+Q_{r}^{H}}=\frac{1-\frac{E_{S}}{E_{A}}}{1+\alpha+\beta},

(105)

where,

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}4\omega_{0}\left[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)\right]dt}{i\epsilon(\omega_{0}+\lambda)\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\left[\varrho_{SA}(t)-\varrho_{AS}(t)\right]dt}

(106)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{\int_{\tau_{d}}^{\tau}4\omega_{0}\left[\Gamma_{A}^{E+}\varrho_{A}(t)-\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\varrho_{E}(t)\right]dt}{i\epsilon(\omega_{0}+\lambda)\int_{0}^{\tau_{d}}\left[\varrho_{SA}(t)-\varrho_{AS}(t)\right]dt}.

(107)

Different from the qutrit case, here we see that the efficiency of the machine depends on
\epsilon
.

For a large enough
\tau
the operational steady state,
\rho_{OSS}
, converges to the
bona fide
steady state,
\rho_{NESS}
, defined by
\dot{\rho}=0
. In the steady state
\rho_{NESS}
the populations are defined by the following equations:

r_{E_{NESS}}=\frac{\Gamma_{S}^{E+}r_{S_{NESS}}+\Gamma_{A}^{E+}r_{A_{NESS}}}{\Gamma_{S}^{E-}+\Gamma_{A}^{E-}}

(108)

r_{G_{NESS}}=\frac{\Gamma_{S}^{G-}r_{S_{NESS}}+\Gamma_{A}^{G-}r_{A_{NESS}}}{\Gamma_{S}^{G+}+\Gamma_{A}^{G+}}

(109)

r_{A_{NESS}}=\frac{\Gamma_{A}^{G+}r_{G_{NESS}}+\Gamma_{A}^{E-}r_{E_{NESS}}}{\Gamma_{A}^{E+}+\Gamma_{A}^{G-}}

(110)

r_{S_{NESS}}=\frac{\Gamma_{S}^{g+}r_{G_{NESS}}+\Gamma_{g}^{E-}r_{e_{NESS}}}{\Gamma_{S}^{E+}+\Gamma_{S}^{G-}}.

(111)

Adding equations (
108
) e (

\displaystyle r_{G_{NESS}}+r_{E_{NESS}}

\displaystyle=1-(r_{A_{NESS}}+r_{S_{NESS}})

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma_{S}^{G-}r_{S_{NESS}}+\Gamma_{A}^{G-}r_{A_{NESS}}}{\Gamma_{G}^{+}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma_{S}^{E+}r_{S_{NESS}}+\Gamma_{A}^{E+}r_{A_{NESS}}}{\Gamma_{E}^{-}}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=r_{A_{NESS}}\left[1+\frac{\Gamma_{A}^{G-}}{\Gamma_{G}^{+}}+\frac{\Gamma_{A}^{E+}}{\Gamma_{E}^{-}}\right]

\displaystyle+r_{S_{NESS}}\left[1+\frac{\Gamma_{S}^{G-}}{\Gamma_{G}^{+}}+\frac{\Gamma_{S}^{E+}}{\Gamma_{E}^{-}}\right]

\displaystyle=r_{A_{NESS}}\nu+r_{S_{NESS}}\mu

(112)

where,

(113)

(114)

By equation (

\displaystyle(\Gamma_{A}^{E+}+\Gamma_{A}^{G-})r_{A_{NESS}}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{A}^{G+}\frac{\Gamma_{S}^{G-}r_{S_{NESS}}+\Gamma_{A}^{G-}r_{A_{NESS}}}{\Gamma_{G}^{+}}

\displaystyle+\Gamma_{A}^{E-}\frac{\Gamma_{S}^{E+}r_{S_{NESS}}+\Gamma_{A}^{E+}r_{A_{NESS}}}{\Gamma_{E}^{-}}.

Multiplying both sides by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{E}^{-}\Gamma_{G}^{+}(\Gamma_{A}^{E+}+\Gamma_{A}^{G-})r_{A_{NESS}}

After some algebraic manipulation, we obtain

The local Floer cohomology of indicator functions
Yoel Groman

Abstract
. For a compact set K K with contact type boundary in a symplectic manifold M M we construct a spectral sequence from the local Floer homology of the Reeb orbits, as studied by [ 21 ] , to the relative symplectic cohomology of K K in M M over the Novikov ring. The spectral sequence is functorial with respect to inclusions which are not required to be exact. This functoriality is key to the closed string reconstruction problem near the singularity of an SYZ fibration. We illustrate this in the case of dimension 2 ​ n = 4 2n=4 for symplectic cluster manifolds. In higher dimension, an additional ingredient, the locality spectral sequence, is required, and is the subject of a forthcoming work in progress.

1.3.
Local and infinitesimal Floer homology of indicator functions

The invariant

over the Novikov ring. Moreover, given an isolated homotopy, this allows one to understand the leading term of the continuation map. Namely, an isotopy from

We would like to imitate this type of reasoning to the case of the function

It turns out that it is advantageous to first do this for non-smooth but continuous Hamiltonians

1.3.1.
Removing the exactness assumption?

It is clear from the above outline that the contact type assumption plays a crucial role in the proof. To be more precise, the argument extending Hein’s lemma to Hamiltonians such as
H_{K,I}
is completely general and is applicable to stable Hamiltonian hyper-surfaces. However, the argument for localization to a symplectization neighborhood in a uniform window uses the action filtration in an essential way. In forthcoming work we produce a homological perturbation type algorithm which constructs a chan level model for
CF^{*}(H_{K})
whose underlying module is a direct sum of one Morse-Bott complex for each Reeb component. For this algorithm the exactness assumption is not crucial, and we expect it can be used to establish a version of Theorem
3
whenever
\partial K
is stable Hamiltonian. This concern is not just about mindless generality. Even if we limit ourselves to the setting of SYZ mirror symmetry, imposing a contact type assumption on the boundary is too restrictive. For example, in a forthcoming work we show that for symplectic cluster manifolds a smoothing of the singularities of
mSpec(SH)
is achieved by deforming the symplectic form so that we no longer have exactness near the boundary.

We part with a fundamental question raised by the above discussion. Let
M
be a geometrically bounded symplectic manifold and let
H:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper Hamiltonian which is bounded from below. For concreteness, consider
M
the twisted cotangent bundle of a smooth compact manifold and
H
a mechanical Hamiltonian. We don’t necessarily know that
H
satisfies
C^{0}
estimates for the direct definition of Hamiltonian Floer cohomology. However, it is shown in
[
12
]
that one can define it using a sequence of Lipschitz Hamiltonians which converge to
H
on compact sets. Moreover, the definition is independent of the choice of approximating sequence.

Problem 1.14
.

Does the analogue of Theorem
1
hold? Namely, suppose
H
has isolated periodic components, is
HF^{*}_{0^{+}}(H)
the direct sum of components associated with the periodic components of
H
.

Note in the case of the magnetic cotangent bundle, the approximating scheme involves drastically slowing down the Hamiltonian which introduces many periodic orbit not coming from
H
. The problem is thus akin to the problem mentioned in Remark
1.1
.

Let

The quantity on the right hand side is referred to as the

Denote by

2.1.
The Floer complex for general Hamiltonians

The naive definition of the Floer complex requires considering smooth non-degenerate Hamiltonians, and a choice of
J
(and, in the fully general case, some scheme for virtual counts). The definition extends to more general Hamiltonians

by considering a monotone sequence
(H_{i},J_{i})
of generic Floer data such that

converges pointwise to

Here

we recall the construction for indicator functions. The widehat denotes completion with respect to the adic norm. The complex

The Hamiltonian Floer cohomology

is defined as the homology of

. This is a vector space over the Novikov field. Note that

consisting of elements of norm

. For a half interval

the truncated Floer cohomology

to be the homology of the sub-quotient

]

that for any proper Hamiltonian

we have

where the colimit is taken over all dissipative non-degenerate Hamiltonians.

The energy distance inequality

The following proposition is slight refinement of

Proposition A.4
.

Let
V
be a possibly time dependent vector field whose flow is complete on a Riemannian manifold
M

t

\psi_{t}

be a constant such that
\|d\psi_{t}v\|>\lambda^{-1}|v|
for all tangent vectors

be a smooth path. Then

(76)

d(\gamma(0),\psi_{T}^{-1}(\gamma(T)))<\lambda\int_{0}^{T}\|\gamma^{\prime}(t)-V_{t}\circ\gamma(t)\|dt.

Proof.

Let
\eta(t):=\psi_{t}^{-1}(\gamma(t))
. Then
\dot{\gamma}(t)=d\psi_{t}\dot{\eta}(t)+V_{t}\circ\gamma(t)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|\gamma^{\prime}(t)-V_{t}\circ\gamma(t)\|dt

\displaystyle>\lambda^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}\|\dot{\eta}(t)\|dt

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{-1}d(\eta(0),\eta(T))

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}d(\gamma(0),\psi_{T}^{-1}(\gamma(T))).

∎

As a corollary we have

.

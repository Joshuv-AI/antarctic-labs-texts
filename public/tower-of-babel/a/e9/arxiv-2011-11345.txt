Weak cuspidality and the Howe correspondence
Jesua Epequin

Abstract
. We study the effect of the Howe correspondence on Harish-Chandra series for type I dual pairs over finite fields with odd characteristic. We define a bijection obtained from this correspondence, and enjoying the property of “having minimal unipotent support”. Finally, we examine the interaction between the Howe correspondence and weak cuspidality.



Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q
elements and odd characteristic. A pair of reductive subgroups of
\Sp_{2n}(q)
, where each one is the centralizer of the other, is called
reductive dual pair
. We focus our attention on
irreducible
dual pairs (cf.
[
15
]
). One such pair can be
linear

(\GL_{m}(q),\GL_{m^{\prime}}(q))
,
unitary

(\U_{m}(q),\U_{m^{\prime}}(q))
or
symplectic-orthogonal

(\Sp_{2m}(q),\Or_{m^{\prime}}(q))
, with
n=mm^{\prime}
in all cases. The last two are also called
type I
dual pairs, and the groups belonging to them are called
type I groups
.

For a reductive dual pair
(G_{m},G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}})
, Roger Howe defined the a correspondence
\Theta_{m,m^{\prime}}:\mathscr{R}(G_{m})\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}})
between their categories of complex representations. Known as
Howe correspondance
, it arises from the restriction to
G_{m}\times G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}
of the
Weil representation

\omega
of the symplectic group
\Sp_{2n}(q)
(cf.
[
13
]
).

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive group defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
\mathbf{G}^{*}
its dual group. Denote by
G
and
G^{*}
their groups of rational points. In
[
17
]
Lusztig defined a partition of the set
\mathscr{E}(G)
of irreducible representations of
G
into
Lusztig series

\mathscr{E}(G,(s))
. These series are parametrized by rational conjugacy classes of semisimple elements
s
of
G^{*}
. The elements of
\mathscr{E}(G,(1))
are called
unipotent representations
of
G
.

In general, the Howe correspondence is not compatible with unipotent representations. Therefore, we make use of a similar correspondance
\Theta^{\flat}_{m,m^{\prime}}:\mathscr{R}(G_{m})\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}})
arising from a Weil representation
\omega^{\flat}
introduced by Gérardin in
[
10
]
. This correspondence preserves unipotent representations (cf. Proposition 2.3 in
[
2
]
). Since in this paper we only work with this modified Howe correspondence we will drop the superscript and denote
\Theta^{\flat}_{m,m^{\prime}}
, and
\omega^{\flat}
by
\Theta_{m,m^{\prime}}
, and
\omega
respectively.

In
[
16
]
Lusztig found that, for every irreducible representation
\pi
of a connected reductive group
\mathbf{G}
, there is a unique rational unipotent class
\mathscr{O}_{\pi}
in
\mathbf{G}
which has the property that
\sum_{x\in\mathscr{O}_{\pi}(q)}\pi(x)
is non trivial, and that has maximal dimension among classes with this property. This class is called the
unipotent support
of
\pi
. Such classes are ordered by the relation given by
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\preceq\mathcal{O}
, if and only if
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\subset\overline{\mathcal{O}}
, referred to as the
closure order
.

In
[
4
]
we defined a bijective mapping
\theta_{G,G^{\prime}}:\mathscr{E}(G,1)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(G^{\prime},1)
between the unipotent series of the members of a type I dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
; in such a way that, for a unipotent representation
\pi
of
G

– The representation
\theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\pi)
occurs in
\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\pi)
.

– If
\pi^{\prime}
belongs to
\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\pi)
, then
\mathcal{O}_{\theta(\pi)}\preceq\mathcal{O}_{\pi^{\prime}}

The last item above asserts that
\theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\pi)
has the smallest unipotent support among irreducible representations in
\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\pi)
, it is in this sense minimal.

We want to extend this definition to the whole set of irreducible representations. Naturally, any attempt to do this must make use of the
Lusztig bijection
. This bijection, known as well as the
Lusztig correspondence
is a one-to-one map between the series
\mathscr{E}(G,(s))
and the series
\mathscr{E}(C_{G^{*}}(s),(1))
, of unipotent representations of the centralizer
C_{G^{*}}(s)
. For classical groups, this centralizer can be expressed as a product of smaller reductive groups.

For instance, when
G
is a unitary group

C_{G^{*}}(s)\simeq G_{\#}\times G_{(1)}

where
G_{\#}
is a product of linear or unitary groups, and
G_{(1)}
is a unitary group. We obtain in this way a modified Lusztig correspondence
\Xi_{s}
sending
\pi\in\mathscr{E}(G,(s))
to
\pi_{\#}\otimes\pi_{(1)}\in\mathscr{E}(G_{\#}\times G_{(1)},1)
. For a unitary dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
this bijection fits in the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{E}(G,(s))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{E}(G_{\#}\times G_{(1)},1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{R}(G^{\prime},(s^{\prime}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{R}(G_{\#}\times G^{\prime}_{(1)},1).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Xi_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota\hskip 1.42271pt\otimes\hskip 1.42271pt\Theta_{G_{(1)},G^{\prime}_{(1)}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Xi_{s^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

This motivates us to define
\theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\pi)
, for
\pi
in
\mathscr{E}(G)
, to be the unique irreducible representation of
G^{\prime}
such that

\Xi_{s^{\prime}}(\theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\pi))=\tilde{\pi}_{\#}\times\theta_{G_{(1)},G^{\prime}_{(1)}}(\pi_{(1)}).

We also define such a mapping
\theta_{G,G^{\prime}}:\mathscr{E}(G)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(G^{\prime})
, for symplectic-orthogonal pairs. As we could expect, this bijection also selects representations with smallest unipotent supports. This is our first result (Theorem
4
).

Theorem
.

Let
\pi
,
\tau
belong to
\mathscr{E}(G,(s))
, and
\pi_{u}
,
\tau_{u}
denote the corresponding unipotent representations of
C_{G^{*}}(s)
. If
\mathcal{O}_{\pi_{u}}\preceq\mathcal{O}_{\tau_{u}}
then
\mathcal{O}_{\pi}\preceq\mathcal{O}_{\tau}
. In particular, for
\pi
in
\mathscr{E}(G)
, the subrepresentation
\theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\pi)
of
\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\pi)
has the smallest unipotent support.

In a recent paper
[
12
]
, Howe and Gurevich presented the notion of rank for representations of finite symplectic groups (see Section
4
). This conduced to the introduction of the
eta
correspondence. Consider a dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
formed by one orthogonal and one symplectic group; and suppose the pair is in the stable range. Howe and Gurevich show that for
\rho
in
\mathscr{E}(G)
, there is a unique irreducible representation in
\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\rho)
of maximal rank, it is denoted by
\eta(\rho)
.

The correspondences
\theta
and
\eta
are defined in different ways. The former chooses a subrepresentation of
\Theta
with smallest unipotent support, whereas the latter selects one with greatest rank. In
[
20
]
, Shu-Yen Pan shows that these two agree on their common domain of definition (the stable range), i.e. among the irreducible constituents of
\Theta(\pi)
, the representation with the smallest unipotent support is the one having the greatest rank. This points out to an inverse relation between these two features. We discuss this in Section
4
.

In
[
11
]
, Gerber, Hiss and jacon introduced the notion of
weak cuspidality
for modular representations in non-defining (including zero) characteristic. The spirit of the definition is the same as for cuspidal representations: the vanishing of parabolic restriction functors. For weakly cuspidal representations we nonetheless restrict our attention to certain kind of parabolic subgroups, called
pure
by the authors. This yields a weak Harish-Chandra theory that refines the usual one. Moreover, for representations of unitary groups in prime characteristic, this new definition provides a natural partition of the set of unipotent representations (see Proposition
5
), in the same way usual series do for ordinary unipotent representations.

A natural question arises: how does the Howe correspondence behaves with respect to this weak Harish-Chandra theory? We provide an answer to this question for ordinary representations. We first prove that the Howe correspondence respects weak cuspidality in case of first occurrence. This is our second result.

Theorem
.

Let
(G_{m},G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}})
be a type I dual pair, and
\pi
be an irreducible weakly cuspidal representation of
G_{m}
, and let
m^{\prime}(\pi)
be its first occurrence index.

1. If
m^{\prime}<m^{\prime}(\pi)
, then
\Theta_{m,m^{\prime}}(\pi)
is empty

2. The representation
\Theta_{m,m^{\prime}(\pi)}(\pi)
is irreducible and weakly cuspidal

3. If
m^{\prime}>m^{\prime}(\pi)
, then none of the constituents of
\Theta_{m,m^{\prime}}(\pi)
is weakly cuspidal

The proof of this result can divided in two independent parts: existence and uniqueness, each making use of different tools. The former relies on the computation of coinvariants of the Weil representation presented in Section
1
. The latter, on the study of orbits and stabilizers for the action of a block diagonal subgroup of a type I group on the set of maximal isotropic subspaces of the underlying module of this group. We need to mention that the proof is inspired by the equivalent result for cuspidal representations, but that it is more refined. Indeed, for cuspidal representations, the uniqueness proof used the fact that the stabilizers above are contained in a direct product of parabolic subgroups. For weakly cuspidal representations this is not enough, we had to calculate this stabilizers explicitly. In order to do so we had first to find explicit representatives for the orbits (which is also not needed in the cuspidal setting).

From the last theorem above, the work required to establish the agreement between the Howe correspondence and weak cuspidal support is very much the same required in the cuspidal setting.

1.
Howe correspondance and cuspidal support

Theorem 3.7 of
[
2
]
states that the Howe correspondence is compatible with unipotent Harish-Chandra series. In this section, we generalize this theorem to arbitrary Harish-Chandra series. The proof follows that of Theorem 2.5 in
[
15
]
.

We fix two Witt towers,
\mathscr{T}
and
\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
, such that
(G_{m},G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}})
is a type I dual pair for any
G_{m}\in\mathscr{T}
and
G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}\in\mathscr{T}^{\prime}
.

Let
D
be a field equal to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
when the dual pair is symplectic-orthogonal, and equal to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
when the pair is unitary. Let
W_{m}
be the underlying
D
-vector space of
G_{m}
. Let
P_{k}
be the stabilizer in
G_{m}
of the totally isotropic subspace of
W_{m}
, spanned by the
k

k\leq m
. Denote by
N_{k}
its unipotent radical,
\GL_{k}=\GL(D)
and
M_{k}=\GL_{k}\times G_{m-k}
the standard Levi subgroup of
P_{k}
. Denote by
\GL^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}
,
P^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}
,
N^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}
, and
M^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}
the analogous groups for
G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}
.
Finally, denote by
\mathbf{R}_{G}
, the natural representation of the group
G\times G
on the space
\mathscr{S}(G)
. This representation is isomorphic to the one obtained by inducing the trivial representation of
G
to
G\times G
(diagonal inclusion). It decomposes as

\mathbf{R}_{G}=\sum_{\pi\in\mathscr{E}(G)}\pi\otimes\tilde{\pi},

where
\tilde{\pi}
denotes the contragredient representation of
\pi
.

.

[
5
, Corollary 1]

Let
\prescript{*}{}{R}_{k}\otimes 1
and

to

and

a)
The representation
(\prescript{*}{}{R}_{k}\otimes 1)(\omega_{m,m^{\prime}})
decomposes as :

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\min\{k,m^{\prime}\}}R_{\GL_{k-i}\times\GL_{i}\times G_{m-k}\times M^{\prime}_{i}}^{M_{k}\times G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}}1_{\GL_{k-i}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{\GL_{i}}\otimes\omega_{m-k,m^{\prime}-i}.

Likewise, the representation

\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\min\{k^{\prime},m\}}R_{M_{i}\times\GL_{k^{\prime}-i}\times\GL_{i}\times G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}}^{G_{m}\times M^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}}1_{\GL_{k^{\prime}-i}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{\GL_{i}}\otimes\omega_{m-i,m^{\prime}-k^{\prime}}.

. The set of standard Levi subgroups of

, where

(resp.

The image of

2.
Howe correspondence and Lusztig correspondence

The purpose of this section is to see the effect of the Lusztig correspondence on the Howe correspondence for type I dual pairs.

2.1.
Centralizers of rational semisimple elements

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a reductive group defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and
C_{\textbf{G}}(x)
be the centralizer of a rational element
x
in
G
. Denote by
G
and
C_{G}(x)
their groups of rational elements.

Assume that
\mathbf{G}
is also connected. In Proposition 5.1 of
[
18
]
, Lusztig found a bijection

(1)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{s}:\mathscr{E}(G,(s))\simeq\mathscr{E}(C_{G^{*}}(s),(1)).

where
s
is a rational semisimple element of
\textbf{G}^{*}
. Aubert, Michel and Rouquier extended this bijection to even orthogonal groups (Proposition 1.7 of
[
2
]
). It is known as the
Lusztig bijection
or
Lusztig correspondence
. Taking
s=1
yields a bijection between the series of unipotent representations of
G
with that of its dual :

(2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}_{1}:\mathscr{E}(G,1)\simeq\mathscr{E}(G^{*},1),

We can also extend (
1
) by linearity in order to obtain an isometry between the categories
\mathscr{R}(G,(s))
and
\mathscr{R}(C_{G^{*}}(s),(1))
spanned by the Lusztig series
\mathscr{E}(G,(s))
and
\mathscr{E}(C_{G^{*}}(s),(1))
respectively.

Following Section 1.B in
[
2
]
, let
\mathbf{G}
be a classical group of rank
n
, and
\mathbf{T}_{n}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Let
s
be a rational semisimple element of its Langlands dual
\mathbf{G^{*}}
. By definition, a rational semisimple element is conjugate to an element
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
in
\mathbf{T}_{n}
. Let
\nu_{\lambda}(s)
the number of times
\lambda
appears in this list. There is a decomposition

C_{\mathbf{G^{*}}}(s)=\prod\mathbf{G}_{[\lambda]}(s),

where
[\lambda]
is the orbit of
\lambda
by the action of the Frobenius endomorphism, intersected with
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
. Each
\mathbf{G}_{[\lambda]}(s)
is a reductive quasi-simple group of rank
|[\lambda]|\nu_{\lambda}(s)
. Moreover, if
\lambda\neq\pm 1
, then
\mathbf{G}_{[\lambda]}(s)
is a unitary or general linear group (possibly over some finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
). Additionally :

(1) If
\mathbf{G}=\boldsymbol{\GL}_{n}
is unitary, then
\mathbf{G}_{[\pm 1]}(s)
is a unitary group.

(2) If
\mathbf{G}=\boldsymbol{\SO}_{2n+1}
, then
\mathbf{G}_{[-1]}(s)=\boldsymbol{\Or}_{2\nu_{-1}(s)}
, and
\mathbf{G}_{[1]}(s)=\boldsymbol{\SO}_{2\nu_{1}(s)+1}
.

(3) If
\mathbf{G}=\boldsymbol{\Or}_{2n}
, then
\mathbf{G}_{[\pm 1]}(s)=\boldsymbol{\Or}_{2\nu_{\pm 1}(s)}
.

In all cases we see that
\mathbf{G}_{[1]}(s)
is a group of the same kind as
\mathbf{G}
, but of smaller rank.

2.2.
Weil representation and Lusztig correspondence

Consider a type I dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})
. Let
m
(resp.
m^{\prime}
) be the Witt index of
G
(resp.
G^{\prime}
). According to Proposition 2.3 in
[
2
]
, if
s
is a rational semisimple element in
\mathbf{G}^{*}
, then there is a rational semisimple element
s^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{G^{\prime}}^{*}
, such that the Howe correspondence relates
\mathscr{E}(G,(s))
and
\mathscr{E}(G^{\prime},(s^{\prime}))
. Moreover, in this case,
s^{\prime}=(s,1)
if
m\leq m^{\prime}
, and

otherwise. In particular, there is some

, and
t
in
\mathbf{T}_{l}
with eigenvalues different from
1
, such that
s=(t,1)
, and
s^{\prime}=(t,1)
. Let
\omega_{G,G^{\prime},t}
denote the projection of the Weil representation
\omega_{G,G^{\prime}}
onto
\mathscr{R}(G,(s))\otimes\mathscr{R}(G^{\prime},(s^{\prime}))
, and
\mathbf{T}_{l,0}
the subset of
\mathbf{T}_{l}
whose elements have all their eigenvalues different from
1
. Proposition 2.4 in
[
2
]
asserts that

\omega_{G,G^{\prime}}=\bigoplus_{l=0}^{\min(m,m^{\prime})}\bigoplus_{t\in\mathbf{T}_{l,0}}\omega_{G,G^{\prime},t}.

We now endeavour to study the effect of the Lusztig correspondence on the Weil representation
\omega_{G,G^{\prime},t}
. We treat unitary and symplectic-orthogonal groups independently.

A.
Unitary pairs

Suppose that
\mathbf{G}
is a unitary group. Let
s
be a rational semisimple element in
\mathbf{G}^{*}

\mathbf{G}_{\#}
denote the product of
\mathbf{G}_{[\lambda]}(s)

, and
\mathbf{G}_{(1)}
be the Langlands dual of

. The groups of rational elements of

(3)

\displaystyle\Xi_{s}:\mathscr{E}(G,(s))\simeq\mathscr{E}(G_{\#},1)\times\mathscr{E}(G_{(1)},1).

For

and

The groups

B.
Symplectic-orthogonal pairs

\mathbf{G}

We now introduce a partial order on the set of unipotent conjugacy classes. It is crucial for results below.

Definition 1

Before extending this to arbitrary representations, we recall the definition for unipotent representations. We work with symplectic orthogonal and unitary pairs independently.

3.3.

4.
Unipotent support and rank

In a recent paper
[
12
]
, Howe and Gurevich presented the notion of rank for representations of finite symplectic groups. This conduced to the introduction of the
eta
correspondence, defined by the property of “having maximal rank”; as explained below.

Let
\mathscr{L}_{n}(q)
the group of symmetric matrices of size
n
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; or equivalently, of symmetric bilinear forms over a
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of dimension
n
. Consider the
Siegel parabolic

P
of
\Sp_{2n}(q)
, with Levi decomposition
P=LN
, where
L\simeq\GL_{n}(q)
and

N=\left\{\left(\begin{matrix}1&A\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\mbox{ : }A\mbox{ belongs to }\mathscr{L}_{n}(q)\right\}.

We identify the group
N
to the group
\mathscr{L}_{n}(q)
. Being abelian, all its irreducible representations are one-dimensional. Moreover, fixing a non-trivial additive character
\psi
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we can define a bijection between
N
and
\mathscr{E}(N)
relating a symmetric
A
to
\psi_{A}
; the latter being defined by
\psi_{A}(B)=\psi(\tr(AB))
, for
B
symmetric.

Let
\rho
be a representation of
\Sp_{2n}(q)
. The restriction of
\rho
to
N
decomposes as a weighted sum of representations
\psi_{A}
, for
A
symmetric. The orbits of the action of
L
on
N
can be identified with the orbits of
\GL_{n}(q)
on
\mathscr{L}_{n}(q)
, i.e. with equivalence classes of symmetric matrices. The coefficients on the sum above are therefore constant on these classes. The first major invariant of a symmetric bilinear form is its rank. It is well known that, over
finite fields of odd characteristic, there are just two isomorphism classes of symmetric bilinear forms of a given rank
r
. We denote by
\mathscr{O}_{r}^{+}
and
\mathscr{O}_{r}^{-}
, the two equivalence classes of symmetric matrices of rank
r
. The restriction of
\rho
to
N
decomposes as :

\rho\rvert_{N}=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\sum_{\pm}m_{r\pm}\sum_{A\in\mathscr{O}_{r}^{\pm}}\psi_{A}

Definition 2
.

– The
rank
of the character
\psi_{A}
is defined as the rank of
A
.

– The
rank
of
\rho
, denoted by
\rk(\rho)
, is defined as the greatest
k
such that the restriction to
N
contains characters of rank
k
, but of no higher rank.

Consider the dual pair
(G,G^{\prime})=(O^{\pm}_{2k},Sp_{2n^{\prime}})
where
2k\leq n^{\prime}
, i.e., the dual pair is in stable range. Howe and Gurevich show that for
\rho
in
\mathscr{E}(O^{\pm}_{2k}(q))
, there is a unique irreducible representation in
\Theta_{G,G^{\prime}}(\rho)
of rank
2k
; all other constituents having smaller rank. This gives rise to a mapping called the
eta
correspondence :

\eta:\mathscr{E}(O_{2k}^{\pm}(q))\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(Sp_{2n^{\prime}}(q)).

We now have two one-to-one correspondences
\theta
and
\eta
between the set of irreducible representations
\mathscr{E}(G)
of
G
, and the corresponding set
\mathscr{E}(G^{\prime})
of
G^{\prime}
. They are defined in different ways. The former chooses a subrepresentation of
\Theta
with smallest unipotent support, whereas the latter selects one with greatest rank. In
[
20
]
, Shu-Yen Pan shows that these two “theta relations” agree on their common domain of definition (the stable range). This amounts to say that, among the irreducible constituents of
\Theta(\pi)
, the representation with the smallest unipotent support is the one having the greatest rank. This points out to an inverse relation between these two features. We could ask if this also holds for all representations. This is indeed the case for unipotent representations, the proof is based on results yet to be published by Shu Yen Pan (he has managed to calculate the rank of a unipotent representation in terms of its associated symbol). The general case still needs to be settled.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
\pi
and
\tau
be two representations of
G
. If
\mathcal{O}_{\pi}\preceq\mathcal{O}_{\tau}
then
\rk(\tau)\leq\rk(\pi)
.

The statement in the previous theorem is the best we can hope for. That is to say, we cannot ask the reverse implication to hold as well. The unipotent support is a geometrical object attached to a representation, whereas its rank is just a number. Since the closure order is a partial order, the reverse implication would tell us that the rank of the latter determines the former.

5.1.
Weak Harish-Chandra theory

Let
G=G_{n}
a type I group. For an integer

Levi subgroup

of

where the linear group appears
r

G
is called

By the very definition, the set of pure Levi subgroups is stable by

that if

and

are pure Levi then the intersection
\prescript{x}{}{M}\cap M^{\prime}
is pure as well. This property is crucial in showing that Harish-Chandra philosophy holds when we focus on set of pure Levi subgroups.

Let
\pi
be a representation of
G
, we say that
\pi
is

if the parabolic restriction of
\pi
to a proper pure Levi subgroup is trivial, i.e.
\prescript{*}{}{R}_{M}^{G}(\pi)=0
for every pure Levi subgroup

. A pair
(M,\pi)
, where

is a pure Levi subgroup and
\pi
is a weakly cuspidal representation of

, is called a

. As in the usual cuspidal setting, these pairs provide a partition of the set of irreducible representations of
G
. Indeed, defining the

corresponding by

, as the set of irreducible subrepresentations of the parabolic induced

, we have.

Proposition 4
.

[
11
, Proposition 2.3]

a)

The weak Harish-Chandra series partition the set of (isomorphism classes of) irreducible
G
-representations.

b)

Every weak Harish-Chandra series is contained in some usual Harish-Chandra series.

Item (b) implies that every usual Harish-Chandra series is partitioned into weak Harish-Chandra series, and hence shows that weak series refine the usual theory. These definitions are made for characteristic zero representations. However, the same applies to non-defining prime characteristic.

In the non-zero characteristic case, weak Harish-Chandra prove better suited for studying unipotent representations. Let
l
be a prime different from
p
, and
G_{n}
denote the unitary group
U_{2n}(q)
or

. From the work of Geck
[
7
]
we know that irreducible unipotent representations in characteristic

of

are (just as in trivial characteristic) labelled by partitions of
n
. Calling
\pi_{\mu}
the unipotent representation corresponding to the partition
\mu
we get a result analogous to
[
6
, Appendice, proposition p. 224]
.

Proposition 5
.

If the unipotent representations
\pi_{\mu}
and
\pi_{\nu}
of
G_{n}
lie in the same weak Harish-Chandra series, then
\mu
and
\nu
have the same 2-core.

This result was originally part of a series of conjectures stated in
[

]
, the main of which (Conjecture 5.7) is now a theorem
[
3
]
.

i=1,\ldots,\min\{n_{1},n_{2}\}

T_{i}&1\end{matrix}\right]\mbox{, where }T_{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c|c|c}&\begin{matrix}&K_{i}\\
0_{n_{2}-i}&\end{matrix}&\\
\hline\cr&&\begin{matrix}&K_{i}\\

\hline\cr 0&&\end{array}\right]

V_{i}P_{n}

\Sp_{2n_{1}}\times\Sp_{2n_{2}}

The coset

\displaystyle U_{1}=V_{i}X_{n}\cap W_{1}\mbox{, and }U_{2}=V_{i}X_{n}\cap W_{2}.

An easy calculation shows that

\displaystyle U_{1}^{\perp}/U_{1}=\langle e_{n_{1}-i+1},\ldots,e_{n_{1}},e^{\prime}_{n_{1}},\ldots,e^{\prime}_{n_{1}-i+1}\rangle

\displaystyle U_{2}^{\perp}/U_{2}=\langle f_{n_{2}-i+1},\ldots,f_{n_{2}},f^{\prime}_{n_{2}},\ldots,f^{\prime}_{n_{2}-i+1}\rangle.

Using coordinates in these basis, we define the isomorphism

\phi:U_{1}^{\perp}/U_{1}\rightarrow U_{2}^{\perp}/U_{2}\mbox{, }\phi(z)=Kz,

where

. Using the description (

(8)

\displaystyle x_{1}U_{1}=U_{1},\mbox{ }x_{2}U_{2}=U_{2}\mbox{, }x_{2}\phi=\phi x_{1}.

As before, let

x_{1}\in P_{n_{1}-i}\subset\Sp_{2n_{1}},\mbox{ and }x_{2}\in P_{n_{2}-i}\subset\Sp_{2n_{2}}.

Elements

5.3.
First occurrence of weakly cuspidal representations

In this section we endeavour to study the effect the Howe correspondence has on weakly cuspidal representations in the case of first occurrence. We also comment on the relation between the correspondence and weak series.

Let
G_{m}
and
P_{m}
be as in Proposition
6
.

Lemma 1
.

[
15
, Corollary 3.3]

If
(\omega,S)
is a model for the Weil representation, then space of
G_{m}
–coinvariants
S_{G_{m}}
is isomorphic to a submodule of
\Ind_{P_{m}}^{G_{m}}(1)
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(G_{m},G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}})
be a type I dual pair, and
\pi
be an irreducible weakly cuspidal representation of
G_{m}
, and let
m^{\prime}(\pi)
be its first occurrence index.

1. If
m^{\prime}<m^{\prime}(\pi)
, then
\Theta_{m,m^{\prime}}(\pi)
is empty

2. The representation
\Theta_{m,m^{\prime}(\pi)}(\pi)
is irreducible and weakly cuspidal

3. If
m^{\prime}>m^{\prime}(\pi)
, then none of the constituents of
\Theta_{m,m^{\prime}}(\pi)
is weakly cuspidal

Proof.

To avoid the excessive use or apostrophes we swap the groups in the dual pair and consider the pair
(G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}},G_{m})
instead.

Consider a weakly cuspidal representation
\pi^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}
. The proof of the item 1 in the statement is the definition of the first occurrence index. The proof of the other two items can be divided in two independent parts : existence and uniqueness. The methods used in each are different.

A.
Existence

We prove that if
\pi^{\prime}\otimes\pi
appears in the oscillator representation
\omega_{m^{\prime},m(\pi^{\prime})}
, then
\pi
is weakly cuspidal.

Assume that
\pi
is not weakly cuspidal. In this case we can find an irreducible representation
\varphi_{1}
of
G_{m(\pi^{\prime})-1}
such that
\pi|R_{M_{1}}^{G_{m}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{1})
. Using Frobenius reciprocity we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\neq\langle\omega_{m^{\prime},m},\pi^{\prime}\otimes\pi\rangle\leq\langle\omega_{m^{\prime},m},\pi^{\prime}\otimes R_{M_{1}}^{G_{m}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle 1\otimes\prescript{*}{}{R}_{M_{1}}^{G_{m}}(\omega_{m^{\prime},m}),\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{1}\rangle.

Proposition
1
implies that the last term above is bounded by

\langle\omega_{m^{\prime},m-1}\otimes 1_{\GL_{1}},\pi^{\prime}\otimes\chi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{1}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{R}_{\GL_{1}}\otimes\omega_{m^{\prime}-1,m-1},\prescript{*}{}{R}_{M^{\prime}_{1}}^{G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}}(\pi^{\prime})\otimes\chi_{1}\otimes\varphi_{1}\rangle.

Since
\pi^{\prime}
is weakly cuspidal the second term in this sum must be trivial. The first term yields
\varphi_{1}|\Theta_{m^{\prime},m(\pi^{\prime})-1}(\pi^{\prime})
, which contradicts the minimality of
m(\pi^{\prime})
.

B.
Uniqueness

In order to establish uniqueness we prove that at most one weakly cuspidal irreducible representation can appear in the union of
\Theta_{m^{\prime},m}(\pi^{\prime})
for all non negative
m
.

Let
\pi_{1}
, and
\pi_{2}
belong to
\Theta_{m^{\prime},m_{1}}(\pi^{\prime})
, and
\Theta_{m^{\prime},m_{2}}(\pi^{\prime})
respectively. Following the arguments in Section 3 of
[
15
]
, it can be shown that the representation
\pi_{1}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{2}
is a constituent of the space of
G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}
–coinvariants
S_{G^{\prime}_{m^{\prime}}}
of the Weil representation. Therefore, from Lemma
1
the representation
\pi_{1}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{2}
is a constituent of

\Ind_{P_{m}}^{G_{m}}(1)|_{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}}\Ind_{(G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}})^{V_{i}P_{m}}}^{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}}(1),

where the
V_{i}P_{m}
, for
i=1,\ldots,\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}
, are the representatives of the orbits of
G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}
in
G_{m}/P_{m}
, described above. By transitivity

\displaystyle\Ind_{(G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}})^{V_{i}P_{m}}}^{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}}(1)=\Ind_{P_{m_{1}-i}\times P_{m_{2}-i}}^{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}}\circ\Ind_{(G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}})^{V_{i}P_{m}}}^{P_{m_{1}-i}\times P_{m_{2}-i}}(1).

Moreover, from the explicit description of stabilizers already established,

\displaystyle\Ind_{(G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}})^{V_{i}P_{m}}}^{P_{m_{1}-i}\times P_{m_{2}-i}}(1)=1_{\GL_{m_{1}-i}}\otimes 1_{N_{m_{1}-i}}\otimes 1_{\GL_{m_{2}-i}}\otimes 1_{N_{m_{2}-i}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{G_{i}},

whence we deduce

\displaystyle\Ind_{(G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}})^{V_{i}P_{m}}}^{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}}(1)=R_{M_{m_{1}-i}\times M_{m_{2}-i}}^{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}}(1_{\GL_{m_{1}-i}}\otimes 1_{\GL_{m_{2}-i}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{G_{i}}).

Since
1_{\GL_{k}}
is a subrepresentation of
R_{\GL_{1}^{k}}^{\GL_{k}}(1)
, the character on the righthand side of the last equality is a constituent of

\displaystyle R_{M_{m_{1}-i}\times M_{m_{2}-i}}^{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}}(R_{\GL_{1}^{m_{1}-i}}^{\GL_{m_{1}-i}}(1)\otimes R_{\GL_{1}^{m_{2}-i}}^{\GL_{m_{2}-i}}(1)\otimes\mathbf{R}_{G_{i}})

\displaystyle=R_{M_{m_{1}-i}\times M_{m_{2}-i}}^{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}}\circ R_{M_{m_{1}-i,i}\times M_{m_{2}-i,i}}^{M_{m_{1}-i}\times M_{m_{2}-i}}(1_{\GL_{1}^{m_{1}-i}}\otimes 1_{\GL_{1}^{m_{2}-i}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{G_{i}})

\displaystyle=R_{M_{m_{1}-i,i}\times M_{m_{2}-i,i}}^{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}}(1_{\GL_{1}^{m_{1}-i}}\otimes 1_{\GL_{1}^{m_{2}-i}}\otimes\mathbf{R}_{G_{i}})

We must now distinguish the following two cases.

(a) If
m_{1}\neq m_{2}
then, for all
i=1\ldots,\min\{m_{1},m_{2}\}
, one of the Levi subgroups
M_{m_{1}-i,i}
or
M_{m_{2}-i,i}
is going to be proper in
G_{m_{1}}
or
G_{m_{2}}
respectively. In this case

\langle\Ind_{P_{m}}^{G_{m}}(1)|_{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}},\pi_{1}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{2}\rangle=0

since
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are both weakly cuspidal.

(b) If
m_{1}=m_{2}=k
, then for all
i=1\ldots,k
,

\langle\Ind_{P_{m}}^{G_{m}}(1)|_{G_{m_{1}}\times G_{m_{2}}},\pi_{1}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{2}\rangle\leq 1.

Indeed,

– In case
0\leq i<k
, the Levi subgroups
M_{k-i,i}
and
M_{k-i,i}
are proper, and again

\langle\Ind_{(G_{k}\times G_{k})^{V_{i}P_{m}}}^{G_{k}\times G_{k}}(1),\pi_{1}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{2}\rangle=0

– In case
i=k
, we get

\langle\Ind_{(G_{k}\times G_{k})^{V_{i}P_{m^{\prime}}}}^{G_{k}\times G_{k}}(1),\pi_{1}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{2}\rangle\leq\langle\mathbf{R}_{G_{k}},\pi_{1}\otimes\tilde{\pi}_{2}\rangle=\langle\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\rangle.

Uniqueness follows from Items (a) and (b).
∎

Now that we have proven that the Howe correspondence preserves weak cuspidality on the first occurrence, we can ask whether it also behaves nicely with respect to weak Harish-Chandra series. It turns out that this is indeed the case. The statement and proof of this result is very much the same as Theorem
1
and it will be therefore ommited.

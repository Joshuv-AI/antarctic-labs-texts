Oscillons of Axion-Like Particle: Mass distribution and power spectrum
and Eisuke Sonomoto

Abstract
In string theory, the simultaneous existence of many Axion-Like Particles (ALPs) are suggested over a vast mass range, and a variety of potentials have been developed in the context of inflation. In such potentials shallower than quadratic, the prominent instability can produce localized dense objects, oscillons. Because of the approximate conservation of their adiabatic invariant, oscillons generally survive quite long, maybe up to the current age of the universe in the case of ultra-light ALPs with m ∼ 10 − 22 ​ eV m\sim 10^{-22}\ {\rm eV} . Such oscillons can have significant effects on the evolution of the recent universe. In this paper, we investigate the oscillons of the pure-natural type potential by classical lattice simulation to explore the key quantities necessary for phenomenological application: the number density of oscillons, the oscillon mass distribution, the energy ratio of oscillons to the ALP field, and the power spectrum. Then, we evolve these values in consideration of the analytic decay rate.

2
Oscillons of Axion-Like Particle

Various kinds of ALP potentials are derived
when the instanton effect breaks the shift symmetry of ALP.
In this paper,
we use the pure natural type potential arising from the coupling
between the ALP and the pure Yang-Mills gauge fields
[
12
]
,

\displaystyle V(\phi)=\frac{m^{2}F^{2}}{2p}\left[1-\left(1+\frac{\phi^{2}}{F^{2}}\right)^{-p}\right].

(2.1)

This potential is flatter than quadratic for
p>-1
,
which causes the instability of the field
and produces localized objects, oscillons
[
24
,
23
]
.

We focus on the ALP which starts to oscillate during the radiation dominated era.
In this potential,
the relation between the Hubble parameter at the onset of the oscillation
and the initial field value
\phi_{i}
is represented by
[
34
]

\displaystyle\left(\frac{H}{m}\right)^{2}\simeq\frac{1}{m^{2}\phi}\frac{\partial V(\phi)}{\partial\phi}~\bigg|_{\phi=\phi_{i}}=\left(1+\frac{\phi_{i}^{2}}{F^{2}}\right)^{-(p+1)}.

(2.2)

Thus, the large
|\phi_{i}|
and large
p
delay the start of the oscillation,
and the ALP mass must be

\displaystyle m\gtrsim 10^{-28}\ {\rm eV}\ \left(1+\frac{\phi_{i}^{2}}{F^{2}}\right)^{\frac{p+1}{2}}\left(\frac{H_{\rm eq}}{10^{-28}\ {\rm eV}}\right).

(2.3)

where
H_{\rm eq}
is the Hubble parameter at the matter radiation equality.

2.1
Instability Analysis

In this subsection,
we will show that the coherent oscillation in this potential
causes two kinds of instabilities
[
15
]
,
which affect oscillon formation as we will see in Sec.
4.2
.

We perform the linear analysis by decomposing the field into the background field
\phi_{0}(t)
and its perturbation
\delta\phi(x)
as

\displaystyle\phi(t,\bm{x})=\phi_{0}(t)+\delta\phi(t,\bm{x}).

(2.4)

We assume that the background field coherently oscillates as

\phi_{0}(t)=\phi_{i}(a_{i}/a)^{3/2}\cos mt

where the lower index
i
represents the beginning of the oscillation,
and define the Fourier components as
\phi_{k}

where
k
is the comoving wavenumber.

The first kind of instability is the parametric resonance
which causes oscillon formation.
When
\phi\ll F
,
we can approximate the potential as
V_{,\phi\phi}/m^{2}\simeq 1+\lambda(\phi/F)^{2}/2

with
\lambda=-6(1+p)
.
In this case, the time evolution of
\phi_{k}
is described by the Mathieu equation,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{d(mt)^{2}}\phi_{k}

\displaystyle+[A_{k}-2q\cos(2mt)]\phi_{k}=0,

(2.5)

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda}{8}\left(\frac{\phi_{i}}{F}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{a_{i}}{a}\right)^{3},

(2.6)

\displaystyle A_{k}

\displaystyle=1+\left(\frac{k}{am}\right)^{2}-2q.

(2.7)

Neglecting the cosmic expansion (
a=a_{i}
),
the most efficient resonance band is

\displaystyle\frac{k}{a_{i}m}\sim\sqrt{2q}=\frac{\sqrt{|\lambda|}|\phi_{i}|}{2F}.

(2.8)

The oscillon formation becomes effective
when the perturbations with the wavelength of the oscillon size
are amplified by the parametric resonance.
If these instabilities amplify the perturbations up to
\mathcal{O}(1)
,
the localized field configuration, the oscillon, can be formed,

The second kind of instability is tachyonic instability,
which hinders the coherent oscillation needed for the oscillon formation.
The tachyonic instability is caused by the negative effective mass,

\displaystyle V,_{\phi\phi}<0\hskip 10.00002pt\Leftrightarrow\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{\phi}{F}>\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2p}}\equiv\frac{\phi_{c}}{F}.

(2.9)

Thus, the potential has the inflection point only when
p>-1/2
.
The tachyonic instability occurs in the small
k
mode with

\displaystyle\frac{k}{am}<\left|\frac{V_{,\phi\phi}}{m^{2}}\right|^{1/2}.

(2.10)

2.2
Oscillon Profile

The oscillon configuration is quasi-stable
because of the approximate conservation of the adiabatic invariant
[
35
,
36
]
,

\displaystyle I\equiv\frac{1}{\omega}\int\text{d}^{3}\bm{x}\overline{\dot{\phi}^{2}},

(2.11)

where
\omega
is the frequency of the periodic motion
and the overline represents the time average over one period.
When we assume that
I
is invariant and set
\phi(t,\bm{x})=\Phi(r)\cos(\omega t)
,
the lowest energy configuration of the scalar field
\Phi(r)
is determined by the following differential equation:

\displaystyle\frac{\text{d}^{2}\Phi}{\text{d}r^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{\text{d}\Phi}{\text{d}r}+\omega^{2}\Phi-2\,\overline{\frac{\text{d}V}{\text{d}\Phi}(\Phi\cos(\omega t))\cos(\omega t)}=0,

(2.12)

with boundary conditions
\Phi|_{r\to\infty}=0
and
\text{d}\Phi/\text{d}r|_{r\to 0}=0
.
The profile of produced oscillons depends on the parameter
\omega
.
When we set
\omega
smaller, the initial condition of
\Phi(r=0)
becomes larger, i.e., we obtain a larger oscillon.
The oscillon is produced under the condition that the Eq. (
2.12
) has the solution,
and the potential Eq. (
2.1
) satisfies this condition only when
p>-1
as we mentioned earlier.

The range of
\omega
is constrained by the stability condition

\displaystyle\frac{dI}{d\omega}<0.

(2.13)

This charge
I
relates to the classical particle number conservation
[
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
.

2.3
Oscillon Decay

Because the adiabatic invariance is only approximate,
oscillons gradually decay by emitting the self-radiation.
In this subsection,
we calculate the classical decay rate of the oscillon by perturbative method following refs.
[
29
,
30
]
.

The classical decay rate is defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma(M_{\text{osc}})\equiv\frac{|\overline{\dot{M}_{\text{osc}}}|}{M_{\text{osc}}},

(2.14)

where
M_{\text{osc}}
is the energy of the oscillon configuration.
We take the radiation term
\xi(t,r)
as

\displaystyle\phi(t,{\bf x})=\Phi(r)\cos(\omega t)+\xi(t,r).

(2.15)

The linear approximated equation of motion for
\xi(t,r)
is

\displaystyle\left[\,\square+V^{\prime\prime}(\Phi\cos(\omega t))\right]\xi=-V^{\prime}(\Phi\cos(\omega t))+2\,\overline{V^{\prime}(\Phi\cos(\omega t))\cos(\omega t)}\cos(\omega t),

(2.16)

where prime denotes the derivative of
\phi
.

It is very convenient to use the Fourier expansions in time for the calculation of the decay rate.
We expand
\xi(t,r)=\sum_{j=3}\xi_{j}(r)\cos(j\omega t)
and the potential terms.
The equation of motion for
\xi_{j}
is written as

\displaystyle\left[\nabla^{2}+\kappa_{j}^{2}\right]\xi_{j}=-\mathcal{S}_{j},

(2.17)

where
\kappa_{j}=\sqrt{j^{2}\omega^{2}-m^{2}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{j}(r)
is the source term which includes the combination of
\xi_{k}
’s.
The solution of
\xi_{j}
’s is derived under the same boundary condition as for the oscillon profile calculation,
but it includes not only inhomogeneous solution but also homogeneous solution, i.e. free field solution.
We compare the derived solutions with the formal outgoing solutions at the origin to remove the homogeneous solution,

\displaystyle\xi_{j}(0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dr^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{j}(r^{\prime})\,r^{\prime}\cos(\kappa_{j}r^{\prime}).

(2.18)

The radiative solution of
\xi
for
r\to\infty
is obtained as

\displaystyle\xi_{j}^{\mathrm{rad}}(t,r)\approx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi r}\sum_{j=3}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j}(\kappa_{j})\cos(\kappa_{j}r-j\omega t),

(2.19)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j}(k)=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\bf r}^{\prime}\mathcal{S}_{j}(r^{\prime})\,e^{-i{\bf k\cdot r}^{\prime}}.

(2.20)

The time averaged classical energy loss rate is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\dot{M}_{\text{osc}}}=4\pi r^{2}\,\overline{\partial_{t}\xi^{\mathrm{rad}}(t,r)\partial_{r}\xi^{\mathrm{rad}}(t,r)}=-\frac{1}{8\pi}\sum_{j=3}j\omega\kappa_{j}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}_{j}(\kappa_{j})^{2}.

(2.21)

Therefore, we can write
\Gamma=\sum_{j=3}\Gamma_{j}
and find
\Gamma_{j}\geq 0
.
In the following, we just calculate
\Gamma_{3}
and assume
\Gamma\simeq\Gamma_{3}
because there is relation of
|\xi_{j}|\gg|\xi_{j+1}|
(hence
\Gamma_{j}\gg\Gamma_{j+1}
) confirmed in
\omega\gtrsim 0.8m
except for
poles of
\Gamma_{3}
according to the previous study
[
30
]
.

The decay rates are shown in Fig.
1
as a function of
\omega
.
The upper limit of
\omega
is determined by the relation
dI/d\omega=0
.
In Fig.
1
,
there are poles that the decay rate is extremely small.
Generally, the position of poles of the decay rate
\Gamma_{j}
does not correspond to that of
\Gamma_{j+1}
and therefore,
the relation
\Gamma_{j+1}\gg\Gamma_{j}
could hold at the poles of
\Gamma_{j}
.
This means that the lifetime at poles calculated here is the maximal lifetime,
i.e., the lifetime could be overestimated by the pole effect.

) with
j=3
.

\omega
.

The time dependence of oscillon mass is formally given by

\displaystyle M_{\text{osc}}(t)=M_{\text{osc}}(0)\exp\left(-\int^{t}_{0}\Gamma(M_{\text{osc}}(t^{\prime}))\text{d}t^{\prime}\right).

(2.22)

We will discuss the time evolution of oscillons
based on Eq. (
2.22
) in Sec.

2.4
Extended Poisson Distribution

The dark matter oscillons produce large isocurvature fluctuations.
The simple approximation assuming the random distribution of point particles with a single mass
leads to Poisson distribution.
In this section,
extending it with consideration of broad mass of oscillons
and energy conservation inside the horizon scale,
we analytically derive the power spectrum of ALP oscillons.
In the following, we use the comoving coordinate
\bm{x}
and momentum
\bm{k}
.

Assuming that
the spatial profile of oscillons is approximated by the Gaussian function
and their velocity is almost zero due to the Hubble friction,
the energy density of a single oscillon
\rho_{\rm osc}
with the total energy
M
is represented by

\displaystyle\rho_{\text{osc}}(t,\bm{x};\bm{x}_{0},M)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{[\omega(M)\psi_{0}(M)]^{2}}{2}\exp\left(-\frac{a(t)^{2}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}_{0})^{2}}{R(M)^{2}}\right)

(2.23)

where
\bm{x}_{0}
,
R(M)
,
\omega(M)
, and
\psi_{0}(M)
are
the comoving position, the physical radius, the oscillation frequency, and the central field value of the oscillon.
Then, the total energy of the oscillon is written as

\displaystyle M=a(t)^{3}\int\text{d}^{3}\bm{x}\rho_{\text{osc}}(t,\bm{x};\bm{x}_{0},M)=\frac{[\omega(M)\psi_{0}(M)]^{2}}{2}(\pi R(M)^{2})^{3/2}.

(2.24)

The density perturbation
\delta_{\text{tot}}
is given by the contribution of
N
oscillons over the average energy density of ALP
{\rho}_{\phi}
including oscillons, i.e,

\displaystyle\delta_{\rm tot}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i}^{N}\frac{\rho_{\rm osc}(t,\bm{x};\bm{x}_{i},M_{i})}{\rho_{\phi}(t)},

(2.25)

where and

\bm{x}_{i}
and
M_{i}
is the comoving coordinate and mass of the
i
-th oscillon.
Then, the Fourier component of
\delta_{\rm tot}
is

\displaystyle\delta_{\bm{k}}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv\int\text{d}\bm{x}^{3}\delta_{\rm tot}(\bm{x})e^{-i\bm{x}\cdot\bm{k}},

(2.26)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a(t)^{3}\rho_{\phi}(t)}\sum_{i}^{N}e^{-i\bm{x}_{i}\cdot\bm{k}}M_{i}\exp\left(-\frac{R(M_{i})^{2}k^{2}}{4a(t)^{2}}\right),

(2.27)

To calculate the sum
\sum_{i}^{N}
,
let us consider the comoving volume
V
including
N
oscillons.
When the volume
V
is smaller than the horizon size at oscillon formation,
we expect that the oscillons are randomly distributed.
The power spectrum is given by the ensemble average of the oscillon coordinates,

1
1

1

Here, the power spectrum is defined as

\displaystyle\braket{\delta_{\bm{k}}\delta_{\bm{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\bm{k}-\bm{k}^{\prime})P_{\text{osc},k}\hskip 10.00002pt,\hskip 10.00002pt\delta_{\bm{k}}\equiv\int\text{d}x^{3}\delta_{\rm tot}e^{-i\bm{x}\cdot\bm{k}}.

(2.28)

\displaystyle P_{\text{osc},k}(t)

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\prod_{i}\int_{V}\frac{\text{d}\bm{x}_{i}^{3}}{V}\right)\frac{|\delta_{\bm{k}}(t)|^{2}}{V},

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{a(t)^{3}\rho_{\phi}(t)}\right)^{2}\left(\prod_{i}\int_{V}\frac{\text{d}\bm{x}_{i}^{3}}{V}\right)\frac{1}{V}\sum_{l,n}^{N}e^{i(\bm{x}_{l}-\bm{x}_{n})\cdot\bm{k}}M_{l}M_{n}\exp\left(-\frac{(R(M_{l})^{2}+R(M_{n})^{2})k^{2}}{4a(t)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\frac{1}{a(t)^{3}\rho_{\phi}(t)}\right)^{2}\frac{1}{V}\sum_{i}^{N}M_{i}^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{R(M_{i})^{2}k^{2}}{2a(t)^{2}}\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{\text{osc}}^{2}}{n_{\text{osc}}(t)}\frac{\Braket{M^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{R(M)^{2}k^{2}}{2a(t)^{2}}\right)}}{\braket{M}^{2}}.

(2.29)

where we define
\braket{}
as the average over mass
M
, and
n_{\rm osc}
and
r_{\rm osc}
are number density and energy ratio of oscillons to the ALP, respectively:

2
2

2

We have used physical energy density and comoving number density.

\displaystyle n_{\rm osc}\equiv\frac{N}{V}\hskip 10.00002pt,\hskip 10.00002ptr_{\text{osc}}=\frac{n_{\text{osc}}(t)\braket{M}}{a(t)^{3}\rho_{\phi}(t)}\hskip 10.00002pt.

(2.30)

On a scale larger than the horizon size at the oscillon formation,
the position of oscillons is not purely randomized
because of the energy conservation inside the horizon.
More precisely, energy is conserved in a volume inside which the energy transfer of the scalar field is not effective.
Thus, the power spectrum damps over the scale of energy transfer at the oscillon formation
k_{c}
,
which is smaller than the horizon size.
Considering the energy conservation,
the average of oscillon coordinates in Eq. (
2.29
) is modified, and we introduce the following damping factor (see Appendix
A
for the derivation)

\displaystyle P_{\text{osc},k}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{r_{\text{osc}}^{2}}{n_{\text{osc}}(t)}\frac{\Braket{M^{2}\exp\left(-\frac{R(M)^{2}k^{2}}{2a(t)^{2}}\right)}}{\braket{M}^{2}}K(k/k_{c}),

(2.31)

\displaystyle K(x)

\displaystyle\equiv\left[1-\left(\frac{2}{x}\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)\right].

(2.32)

where
k_{c}
is the cutoff scale of perturbations
and should be determined by simulation.

Note that the above formula reproduces the Poisson distribution when we neglect the size of oscillons (
R(M)\to 0
), use the monochromatic mass (
\braket{M^{2}}=\braket{M}^{2}
) and ignore the energy conservation (
k_{c}/k\ll 1
).

We call the above formula as extended Poisson distribution in this paper.
In the following section,
we will confirm that the above estimation is in good agreement with the power spectrum derived from the numerical simulation.

3.2
Oscillon Identification

The numerical simulation of localized object are investigated in the early works for Q-ball
[
42
]
and oscillon
[

,

,

]
.

To estimate the contribution from the tail of the oscillon profile correctly,
we regard the connected regions with the energy density
larger than the threshold value
\tilde{\rho}_{\text{th}}

and one more grid outside the surface of them as oscillons.
The threshold value
\tilde{\rho}_{\rm th}
is chosen so that

\tilde{\rho}_{\rm th}=\alpha\tilde{\rho}_{\phi}(\tilde{\tau}_{\rm e})

where
\alpha
is
\mathcal{O}(0.1)
parameter
and the time
\tilde{\tau}_{\rm e}
is the end of oscillon formation.

The mass of the oscillons is estimated
by summing over the energy density
\tilde{\rho}
inside the identified region
[
42
]
as

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{\rm osc}=\sum_{\bm{x}\in V_{\text{osc}}}\tilde{\rho}(\bm{x})\left(\frac{\tilde{a}L}{N}\right)^{d},

(3.5)

where
d
is the spatial dimension
and the sum is over the grid
\bm{x}
inside the oscillon volume
V_{\text{osc}}
.
The number of oscillon inside the box
N_{\rm osc}
is given
by the number of these disconnected regions.

We define the energy ratio of oscillons to the total scalar field as

\displaystyle r_{\rm osc}(t)=\frac{\sum_{i}^{N_{\rm osc}}\tilde{M}_{{\rm osc},i}}{\tilde{\rho}(t)(\tilde{a}(t)L)^{d}},

(3.6)

and the normalized comoving number density of the oscillons
\tilde{n}_{\rm osc}

and the average mass of the oscillon

6
Conclusion

In this paper,
we have performed a comprehensive analysis of oscillons
from its formation to the decay
and have established the basics of oscillon phenomenology.

We firstly performed classical lattice simulation and calculated various quantities:
the dependence of energy ratio of oscillons to the ALP field on the initial field value in Fig.
3
,
the oscillon mass distribution in Fig.
4
,
and the power spectrum of oscillons in Fig.
5
.
We found that the oscillon formation becomes efficient
when the tachyonic instability which hinders the instability mode necessary for oscillon formation is weak.
Comparing the resultant power spectrum and the analytical formula derived in Sec.
2.4
,
we confirmed that the extended Poisson distribution is well approximation of the oscillon power spectrum.

We analyzed the time evolution of oscillons in Sec.
5

including the effect of their decay.
With the simulation results,
we calculated the current number density and typical mass of the oscillons.
Combining them with the oscillon decay and the gravitational growth,
we have estimated the power spectrum of oscillons
after oscillons partially decay shown in Fig.
5
.

Finally, let us comment on the gravitational amplification of oscillon fluctuations.
Although we simply treated them as the matter fluctuations in Sec.
5
,
the evolution of the fluctuations produced by oscillons in the matter-dominated universe is still unclear.
Thus,
given our result of the matter power spectrum as the initial condition,
we need a more precise study including the gravitational effect in future work.

Appendix A
Suppression of Power Spectrum by Energy Conservation

We estimate the effect of energy conservation, which suppresses the power spectrum on the large scale as Eq. (
2.32
).
Here, we simply assume that all the energy of ALP is confined inside oscillons and oscillons have the same energy
M_{o}
.
In this case, Eq.(
2.27
) contains the following sum of phases:

\displaystyle\psi=\sum_{i}^{N}\exp(-i\bm{q}_{i}\cdot\bm{k}),

(A.1)

where index
i
refers to
i
-th oscillon and
N
is the total oscillon number.

At first, we revisit the calculation when the volume
V
is smaller than the horizon size at oscillon formation, that is, we expect that the oscillons are randomly distributed.
Then, each phase factor
\bm{x}_{i}\cdot\bm{k}
is a random variable,
and the sum is estimated in the large number limit (
N\to\infty
) as

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i}^{N}\exp(-i\bm{x}_{i}\cdot\bm{k})\right|^{2}=N.

(A.2)

Next, we include the effect of energy conservation.
Let us take
R
as the scale for energy conservation where the energy transfer occurs only for scales smaller that
R

and oscillon distribution is almost similar on the scales larger than
R
.
The oscillons are randomly distributed inside boxes with the volume
R^{3}
,
that is, the position of a oscillon
\bm{q}=(q_{x},q_{y},q_{z})
has the uniform distribution
q_{w}\in[j_{w}R,(j_{w}+1)R]
with
w=x
,
y
and
z
.
The index
\bm{j}=(j_{x},j_{y},j_{z})
refers to the different box in which the energy of oscillons is conserved independently.
The energy conservation requires that the number of oscillons in each box is universal constant
N_{o}
.
We take the total space with volume
V=L^{3}
and calculate the power spectrum
\mathcal{P}_{k}
with
k=2\pi/L
, where
L
is larger than the single box size,
J\equiv L/R\gg 1
.
Note that the total number of oscillons is now
N=N_{o}J^{3}
.

The average over the position of oscillons is given by

\displaystyle\braket{\cdots}_{o}=\prod_{\bm{j},n_{j}}\left(\int_{j_{x}R}^{(j_{x}+1)R}\frac{\text{d}q_{x,n_{j}}}{R}\int_{j_{y}R}^{(j_{y}+1)R}\frac{\text{d}q_{y,n_{j}}}{R}\int_{j_{z}R}^{(j_{z}+1)R}\frac{\text{d}q_{z,n_{j}}}{R}\right)(\cdots),

(A.3)

where index
n_{j}
refers to oscillons inside the
\bm{j}
-th box and
n_{j}
runs from
1
to
N_{o}
.
We take
\bm{k}=(2\pi/L,0,0)
without loss of generality assuming the isotropic oscillon distribution.
The average of
\psi
is calculated as

\displaystyle\braket{\psi}_{o}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j_{x}^{\prime},n_{j}^{\prime}}\left(\int_{j_{x}^{\prime}R}^{(j_{x}^{\prime}+1)R}\frac{\text{d}q_{n_{j}^{\prime}}}{R}\right)\sum_{j_{x},n_{j}}^{J,N_{o}}\exp(-2\pi iq_{n_{j}}/L),

(A.4)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j_{x},n_{j}}^{J,N_{o}}\frac{1}{-ikR}\left[\exp(-2\pi i(j_{x}+1)/J)-\exp(-2\pi ij_{x}/J)\right],

(A.5)

\displaystyle=N_{o}\frac{\exp(-2\pi i/J)-1}{-ikR}\sum_{j_{x}}^{J}\exp(-2\pi ij_{x}/J)=0,

(A.6)

where we use the orthogonal condition
\sum_{j=1}^{J}\exp(-i2\pi j/J)=0
.
The variance is calculated as

\displaystyle\braket{|\psi|{}^{2}}_{o}

\displaystyle=\prod_{j_{x}^{\prime\prime},n_{j}^{\prime\prime}}\left(\int_{j_{x}^{\prime\prime}R}^{(j_{x}^{\prime\prime}+1)R}\frac{\text{d}q_{n_{j}^{\prime\prime}}}{R}\right)\left|\sum_{j_{x},n_{j}}^{J,N_{o}}\exp(-2\pi iq_{n_{j}}/L)\right|^{2},

(A.7)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j^{\prime\prime},n_{j}^{\prime\prime}}\left(\int_{j^{\prime\prime}R}^{(j^{\prime\prime}+1)R}\frac{\text{d}q_{n_{j}^{\prime\prime}}}{R}\right)\left[\sum_{j,j^{\prime}=1}^{J}\sum_{n_{j},n_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}}^{N_{o}}e^{-2\pi i(q_{n_{j}}-q_{n_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}})/L}\right],

(A.8)

where we omit the subscript ”
x
” of
j
.
The average over
\text{d}q_{n_{j}}
is 1 for the terms with
j=j^{\prime}
and
n_{j}=n_{j^{\prime}}^{\prime}
while the other combination can be integrated separately for
q_{n_{j}}
and
q_{n^{\prime}_{j^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\int_{j_{x}R}^{(j_{x}+1)R}\frac{\text{d}q_{n_{j}}}{R}e^{-2\pi iq_{n_{j}}/L}=\frac{e^{-2\pi i/J}-1}{-ikR}e^{-2\pi ij_{x}/J}=\epsilon e^{-2\pi ij_{x}/J},

(A.9)

where we define the suppression factor:

\displaystyle\epsilon=\frac{J}{-i2\pi}(e^{-2\pi i/J}-1).

(A.10)

The integration is performed as

\displaystyle\braket{|\psi|{}^{2}}_{o}

\displaystyle=N_{o}J+N_{o}(N_{o}-1)|\epsilon|^{2}J+N_{o}^{2}|\epsilon|^{2}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}(\neq j)}^{J}e^{-2\pi i(j-j^{\prime})/J},

(A.11)

\displaystyle=N_{o}J+N_{o}(N_{o}-1)|\epsilon|^{2}J+N_{o}^{2}|\epsilon|^{2}\left(\left|\sum_{j=1}^{J}e^{-2\pi ij/J}\right|^{2}-J\right),

(A.12)

\displaystyle=N_{o}J(1-|\epsilon|^{2})=N\left[1-\left(\frac{2}{Rk}\right)^{2}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{Rk}{2}\right)\right].

(A.13)

For the modes smaller than the box size

\braket{|\psi|{}^{2}}_{o}\to N
.
On the other hand, for the modes larger than the box size
Rk\ll 1

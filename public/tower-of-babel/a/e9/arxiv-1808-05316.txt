1
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq 2)
,
W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega)
be the
completion of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
under the Sobolev norm

\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{N}dx\right)^{1/N},

where
\nabla
denotes the gradient operator. Write
\alpha_{N}=N\omega_{N-1}^{1/(N-1)}
, where
\omega_{N-1}
stands for
the area of the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Then the classical Trudinger-Moser inequality
[
39
,
29
,
28
,
33
,
24
]
says

\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega),\,\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx<\infty,\quad\forall\alpha\leq\alpha_{N}.

(1)

This inequality is sharp in the sense that if
\alpha>\alpha_{N}
, all integrals in (
1
) are still finite, but the supremum is infinite.
While the existence of extremal functions for it was solved by Carleson-Chang
[
5
]
, Flucher
[
12
]
and Lin
[
21
]
.

Using a symmetrization argument and a change of variables, Adimurthi-Sandeep
[
1
]
generalized (
1
) to the following
singular version:

\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega),\,\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{e^{\alpha_{N}\gamma|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}}{|x|^{N\beta}}dx<\infty,\quad\forall\,\,0\leq\beta<1,\,\,\,0<\gamma\leq 1-\beta.

(2)

Also, it is sharp in the sense that if
\gamma>1-\beta
, integrals are still finite, but the above supremum is infinite.
The inequality (
2
) was extended to the whole Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by Adimurthi-Yang
[
2
]
.
The existence of extremal functions for (
2
) in the case
N=2
was due to Casto-Roy
[
6
]
, Yang-Zhu
[
38
]
and
Iula-Mancini
[
13
]
. An interesting question is whether or not (
2
) still holds for
\beta<0
. Generally, the answer is negative.
To see this, we choose
x_{0}\not=0
,
r_{0}>0
such that
B_{2r_{0}}(x_{0})\subset\Omega\setminus\{0\}
. For any
0<\epsilon<r_{0}
, we write the
Moser function

m_{\epsilon}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{1}{\omega_{N-1}^{1/N}}\left(\log\frac{r_{0}}{\epsilon}\right)^{\frac{N-1}{N}},&{\rm when}&|x-x_{0}|\leq\epsilon\\[6.45831pt]
\frac{1}{\omega_{N-1}^{1/N}}\frac{\log\frac{r_{0}}{|x-x_{0}|}}{\left(\log\frac{r_{0}}{\epsilon}\right)^{1/N}},&{\rm when}&\epsilon<|x-x_{0}|\leq r_{0}\\[6.45831pt]
0,&{\rm when}&|x-x_{0}|>r_{0}.\end{array}\right.

An easy computation shows
\|m_{\epsilon}\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\Omega)}=1
. Since
\beta<0
, we have

\int_{\Omega}e^{\alpha_{N}(1-\beta)m_{\epsilon}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}|x|^{-N\beta}dx\geq\int_{B_{\epsilon}(x_{0})}e^{\alpha_{N}(1-\beta)m_{\epsilon}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}|x|^{-N\beta}dx\rightarrow\infty\quad{\rm as}\quad\epsilon\rightarrow 0.

Even worse, the above estimate still holds if
\alpha_{N}(1-\beta)
is replaced by any
\alpha>\alpha_{N}
.
In conclusion, the singular Trudinger-Moser inequality (
2
) does not hold for
\beta<0
.

Let us consider the unit ball
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, which is centered at the origin. Let

\mathscr{S}
be a set of all radially symmetric functions. With a slight abuse of notations, we say that
u
is radially symmetric
if
u(x)=u(|x|)
for almost every
x\in\mathbb{B}
. It was proved by Ni
[
27
]
that
W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})\cap\mathscr{S}
can be imbedded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{B},|x|^{\alpha})
with
\alpha>0
and
p=2(N+\alpha)/(N-2)
greater than
2^{\ast}=2N/(N-2)
.
Motivated by results of Bonheure-Serra-Tarallo
[
3
]
, Calanchi-Terraneo
[
4
]

and de Figueiredo-dos Santos-Miyagaki
[
7
]
, de Figueiredo-do Ó-dos Santos
[
8
]
observed that
in the case
N=2
,

\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B})\cap\mathscr{S},\,\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,2}(\mathbb{B})}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\alpha u^{2}}|x|^{2\gamma}dx<\infty,\quad\forall\,\alpha\leq 4\pi(1+\gamma),\,\,\gamma\geq 0;

(3)

moreover, extremal function for the above supremum exists. Of course they discussed more general weight
h(|x|)

and fast growth
F(u)
instead of
|x|^{\alpha}
and
e^{4\pi(1+\alpha)u^{2}}
respectively.

Our aim is to generalize (
3
) to higher dimensional case and to stronger versions. We first have the following:

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbb{B}
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(N\geq 2)
,
W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})
and
\mathscr{S}
be as above. Then there holds
for any
\beta\geq 0
,

\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})\cap\mathscr{S},\,\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}|x|^{N\beta}dx<\infty,\quad\forall\,\gamma\leq\alpha_{N}(1+\beta).

(4)

Here
\alpha_{N}(1+\beta)
is the best constant in the sense that if
\gamma>\alpha_{N}(1+\beta)
, all integrals are finite but
the supremum is infinity. Moreover, for any
\beta\geq 0
and any
\gamma\leq\alpha_{N}(1+\beta)
,
the supremum in (
4
) can be attained.

By a rearrangement argument, for any
\gamma\leq\alpha_{N}
, there holds

\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B}),\,\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx=\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})\cap\mathscr{S},\,\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx.

(5)

Therefore, when
\Omega=\mathbb{B}
, Theorem
1
includes the classical Trudinger-Moser inequality (
1
) as a special case and complements
Adimurthi-Sandeep’s inequality (
2
).

Motivated by
[
34
,
41
,
40
]
, we would generalize Theorem
1
to a version involving eigenvalue of the
N
-Laplace. For
p>1
,
define

\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})=\inf_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B}),\,u\not\equiv 0}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\nabla u|^{N}dx}{(\int_{\mathbb{B}}|u|^{p}dx)^{N/p}}.

(6)

For
\alpha<\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})
, we write for simplicity

\|u\|_{1,\alpha}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\nabla u|^{N}dx-\alpha(\int_{\mathbb{B}}|u|^{p}dx)^{N/p}\right)^{1/N}.

(7)

Theorem 2
.

Given
p>1
.
In addition to the assumptions of Theorem
1
, let
\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})
and
\|\cdot\|_{1,\alpha}
be defined as in (
6
) and (
7
)
respectively. Then if
\alpha<\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})
, there holds

\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B}),\,\|u\|_{1,\alpha}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx<\infty,\quad\forall\,\gamma\leq\alpha_{N}.

Moreover, the above supremum can be attained.

When
p=N
, Theorem
2
was proved by Nguyen
[
26
]
for a smooth bounded domain. As a consequence of Theorem
2
, we improve Theorem
1
as follows:

Theorem 3
.

Given
p>1
.
Under the same assumptions of Theorem
2
, for any
\beta\geq 0
and any
\alpha<(1+\frac{p}{N}\beta)^{N-1+N/p}\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})
,
there holds

\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})\cap\mathscr{S},\,\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\nabla u|^{N}dx-\alpha(\int_{\mathbb{B}}|u|^{p}|x|^{p\beta}dx)^{N/p}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}|x|^{p\beta}dx<\infty,\quad\forall\,\gamma\leq\alpha_{N}(1+\frac{p}{N}\beta),

(8)

where
\alpha_{N}(1+\frac{p}{N}\beta)
is the best constant in the same sense as in Theorem
1
. Furthermore, the supremum in (
8
) can be attained.

We now explain the geometric meaning of the term
|x|^{p\beta}dx
.
Let
g_{0}
be the standard Euclidean metric, namely
g_{0}(x)=d{x_{1}}^{2}+\cdots+d{x_{N}}^{2}
. Define a metric
g(x)=|x|^{\frac{2p}{N}\beta}g_{0}(x)
for

x\in\mathbb{B}
. Then
(\mathbb{B},g)
is a conical manifold with the volume element
dv_{g}=|x|^{p\beta}dx
. Moreover,
|\nabla u|^{N}dx=|\nabla_{g}u|^{N}dv_{g}
.

The proof of Theorems
1
and
3
is based on a change of variables. While the proof of Theorem
2
is based on
blow-up analysis.
In the remaining part of this note, we shall prove Theorems
1
-
3
respectively.

2
Proof of Theorem
1

Let
\beta\geq 0
and
\gamma\leq\alpha_{N}(1+\beta)
. Write for simplicity
u(x)=u(r)
with
r=|x|
. Following
Smets-Willem-Su
[
31
]
and Adimurthi-Sandeep
[
1
]
,
we make a change of variables. Define a function

v(r)=(1+\beta)^{1-1/N}u(r^{1/(1+\beta)}).

A straightforward calculation shows

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\nabla v|^{N}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{1}|v^{\prime}(r)|^{N}r^{N-1}dr

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{1+\beta}\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(r^{1/(1+\beta)})|^{N}r^{{(N-1-\beta)/(1+\beta)}}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime}(t)|^{N}t^{N-1}dt=\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\nabla u|^{N}dx

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}|x|^{N\beta}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{\gamma|u(r)|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}r^{N-1+N\beta}dr

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\beta}|v(r^{1+\beta})|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}r^{N-1+N\beta}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{1+\beta}\int_{0}^{1}e^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\beta}|v(t)|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}t^{N-1}dt=\frac{1}{1+\beta}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\beta}|v|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx.

Then it follows from (
9
), (
10
) and (
5
) that

\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})\cap\mathscr{S},\,\|u\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}|x|^{N\beta}dx=\frac{1}{1+\beta}\sup_{v\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B}),\,\|v\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\frac{\gamma}{1+\beta}|v|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx.

(11)

According to Carleson-Chang
[
5
]
, the supremum on the right-hand side of (
11
) can be attained, so does the supremum
on the left-hand side. This concludes Theorem
1
.
\hfill\Box

3.1
The existence of maximizers for subcritical functionals

Let
\alpha<\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})
. Denote

\Lambda_{\gamma,\alpha}=\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B}),\,\|u\|_{1,\alpha}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx.

Lemma 4
.

For any positive integer
k
, there exists a decreasing radially symmetric function
u_{k}\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})\cap C^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{B}})
with
\|u_{k}\|_{1,\alpha}=1

such that

\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma_{k}|u_{k}|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx=\Lambda_{\gamma_{k},\alpha}
,
where
\gamma_{k}=\alpha_{N}-1/k
. Moreover,
u_{k}
satisfies the Euler-Lagrange equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta_{N}u_{k}-\alpha\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}u_{k}^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{N}{p}-1}u_{k}^{p-1}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}u_{k}^{\frac{1}{N-1}}e^{\gamma_{k}u_{k}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}\\[5.16663pt]
u_{k}\geq 0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{B}\\[5.16663pt]
u_{k}=0\quad{\rm on}\quad\partial\mathbb{B}\\[5.16663pt]
\lambda_{k}=\int_{\mathbb{B}}u_{k}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}e^{\gamma_{k}u_{k}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx,\end{array}\right.

(12)

where
\Delta_{N}u_{k}={\rm div}(|\nabla u_{k}|^{N-2}\nabla u_{k})
.

Proof.

Let
k
be a positive integer. By a rearrangement argument, there exists a sequence of decreasing radially symmetric functions
u_{j}\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})

with
\|u_{j}\|_{1,\alpha}\leq 1
and
\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma_{k}|u_{j}|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx\rightarrow\Lambda_{\gamma_{k},\alpha}
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Since

\alpha<\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})
,
u_{j}
is bounded in
W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})
. Without loss of generality, we assume

u_{j}
converges to some function
u_{k}
weakly in
W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})
, strongly in
L^{s}(\mathbb{B})
for any
s>1
and almost everywhere
in
\mathbb{B}
. If
u_{k}\equiv 0
, then
\|u_{j}\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}\leq 1+o_{j}(1)
. Thus
e^{\gamma_{k}u_{j}^{{N}/{(N-1)}}}
is bounded in

L^{q}(\mathbb{B})
for some
q>1
. It follows that
e^{\gamma_{k}u_{j}^{{N}/{(N-1)}}}
converges to
1
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{B})
.
This implies that
\Lambda_{\gamma_{k},\alpha}=|\mathbb{B}|
, the volume of
\mathbb{B}
, which is impossible. Therefore
u_{k}\not\equiv 0
. Clearly
u_{k}
is also decreasingly radially symmetric and
\|u_{k}\|_{1,\alpha}\leq 1
. Define a function sequence

v_{j}=\frac{u_{j}}{\left(1+\alpha(\int_{\mathbb{B}}u_{j}^{p}dx)^{N/p}\right)^{1/N}}.

It follows that
\|v_{j}\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}\leq 1
,
v_{j}
converges to
v_{k}=u_{k}/(1+\alpha(\int_{\mathbb{B}}u_{k}^{p}dx)^{N/p})^{1/N}

weakly in
W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})
. By a result of Lions (
[
22
]
, Theorem I.6), for any
q<1/(1-\|v_{k}\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}^{N})^{1/(N-1)}
, there holds

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{q\alpha_{N}v_{j}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx<\infty.

(13)

One can easily check that

\left(1+\alpha(\int_{\mathbb{B}}u_{k}^{p}dx)^{N/p}\right)\left(1-\|v_{k}\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}^{N}\right)=1-\|u_{k}\|_{1,\alpha}^{N}<1.

(14)

It follows from (
13
) and (
14
) that
e^{\gamma_{k}u_{j}^{N/(N-1)}}
is bounded in
L^{r}(\mathbb{B})
for some
r>1
,
and thus
e^{\gamma_{k}u_{j}^{N/(N-1)}}\rightarrow e^{\gamma_{k}u_{k}^{N/(N-1)}}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{B})
as
j\rightarrow\infty
. Hence

\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma_{k}u_{k}^{N/(N-1)}}dx=\Lambda_{\gamma_{k},\alpha}
and
u_{k}
is the desired extremal function.
Clearly
\|u_{k}\|_{1,\alpha}=1
. Moreover, the Euler-Lagrange equation of
u_{k}
is (
12
). According to the regularity theory for degenerate elliptic equations, see Serrin
[
30
]
, Tolksdorf
[
32
]
and Lieberman
[
20
]
, we have

u_{k}\in C^{1}(\overline{\mathbb{B}})
.
\hfill\Box

It is indicated by Lemma
4
that for any
\gamma<\alpha_{N}
and
\alpha<\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})
, the supremum
\Lambda_{\gamma,\alpha}
can be attained.
In particular, for any
\gamma_{k}=\alpha_{N}-1/k
, there exists a maximizer
u_{k}\geq 0
satisfies (
12
). It is not difficult to see that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\gamma_{k}u_{k}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx=\Lambda_{\alpha_{N},\alpha}=\sup_{u\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B}),\,\|u\|_{1,\alpha}\leq 1}\int_{\mathbb{B}}e^{\alpha_{N}|u|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx.

(15)

Since
\|u_{k}\|_{1,\alpha}=1
, without loss of generality, we can assume that
u_{k}
converges to
u_{0}

weakly in
W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})
, strongly in
L^{s}(\mathbb{B})
for any
s>1
, and almost everywhere in
\mathbb{B}
.
Let
c_{k}=u_{k}(0)=\max_{\mathbb{B}}u_{k}
. If
c_{k}
is bounded, then applying the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem to
(
15
), we know that
u_{0}
is the desired extremal function for the supremum
\Lambda_{\alpha_{N},\alpha}
.
Hereafter we assume

c_{k}\rightarrow\infty\quad{\rm as}\quad k\rightarrow\infty.

(16)

Lemma 5
.

Let
u_{0}
be the limit of
u_{k}
as above. Then

u_{0}\equiv 0
and
|\nabla u_{k}|^{N}dx\rightharpoonup\delta_{0}
weakly in the sense of measure, where
\delta_{0}
stands for the Dirac measure
centered at the origin.

Proof.

We first prove
u_{0}\equiv 0
. Suppose not. It follows from Lions’ lemma that

e^{\gamma_{k}u_{k}^{N/(N-1)}}
is bounded in
L^{q}(\mathbb{B})
for some
q>1
. Then applying elliptic estimates to (
12
),
we conclude
u_{k}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{B}
, which contradicts our assumption (
16
). Therefore
u_{0}\equiv 0
.

Next we prove
|\nabla u_{k}|^{N}dx\rightharpoonup\delta_{0}
. Suppose not. Since
\|u_{k}\|_{1,\alpha}=1
and
u_{0}\equiv 0
, there would hold

\|u_{k}\|_{W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})}=1+o_{k}(1)
. Thus there exists some
0<r_{0}<1
such that

\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{|x|\leq r_{0}}|\nabla u_{k}|^{N}dx<1.

It follows from the classical Trudinger-Moser inequality (
1
) that
e^{\gamma_{k}(u_{k}-u_{k}(r_{0}))^{N/(N-1)}}
is bounded in
L^{q}({B}_{r_{0}})

for some
q>1
. Since
u_{k}
is decreasing radially symmetric and
\|u_{k}\|_{1,\alpha}=1
with
\alpha<\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})
, we have

u_{k}^{N}(r_{0})\leq\left(\frac{1}{|B_{r_{0}}|}\int_{|x|\leq r_{0}}u_{k}^{p}dx\right)^{N/p}\leq\frac{1}{(\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{B})-\alpha)|B_{r_{0}}|^{N/p}}.

Hence
e^{\gamma_{k}u_{k}^{N/(N-1)}}
is also bounded in
L^{q_{1}}({B}_{r_{0}})

for some
q_{1}>1
. Then applying elliptic estimates to (
12
), we conclude that
u_{k}
is uniformly bounded in
\mathbb{B}
. This contradicts
(
16
) and ends the proof of the lemma.
\hfill\Box

3.2
Blow-up analysis

Let

r_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{N}}c_{k}^{-\frac{1}{N-1}}e^{-\frac{\gamma_{k}}{N}c_{k}^{{N}/{(N-1)}}}
.
Using the same argument as in the proof of (
[
34
]
, Lemma 4.3), one has by Lemma
5
and the classical
Trudinger-Moser inequality (
1
) that

\displaystyle r_{k}e^{au_{k}^{N/(N-1)}}\rightarrow 0~~~~\mbox{as}~~k\rightarrow\infty,~~\forall a<\alpha_{N}/N.

(17)

For

3.3
Test function computation

In this subsection, we construct a sequence of functions to show that

\Lambda_{\alpha_{N},\alpha}>|\mathbb{B}|+\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N}e^{\alpha_{N}A_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{j}}.

(30)

The contradiction between (
30
) and (
29
) indicates that
c_{k}
is a bounded sequence, and whence the desired extremal
function exists. This completes the proof of Theorem
2
.

For any positive integer
k
, we set

\phi_{k}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}c+\frac{1}{c^{1/(N-1)}}\left(-\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\log(1+c_{N}|kx|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})+b\right),&|x|<\frac{\log k}{k}\\[5.16663pt]
\frac{G}{c^{1/(N-1)}},&\frac{\log k}{k}\leq|x|\leq 1,\end{array}\right.

(31)

where
c
and
b
are constants, depending only on
k
, to be determined later. To ensure
\phi_{k}\in W_{0}^{1,N}(\mathbb{B})
,
we need

c+\frac{1}{c^{1/(N-1)}}\left(-\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\log(1+c_{N}|\log k|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})+b\right)=\frac{G(\frac{\log k}{k})}{c^{1/(N-1)}},

which implies that

c^{\frac{N}{N-1}}=G(\frac{\log k}{k})+\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\log(1+c_{N}(\log k)^{\frac{N}{N-1}})-b.

(32)

We now calculate the energy of
\phi_{k}
. In view of (
31
), a straightforward calculation gives

\int_{|x|<\frac{\log k}{k}}|\nabla\phi_{k}|^{N}dx=\frac{1}{c^{{N}/{(N-1)}}}\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\left\{-\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{j}+\log(1+c_{N}(\log k)^{\frac{N}{N-1}})+O((\log k)^{-\frac{N}{N-1}})\right\}.

By (
28
) and the divergence theorem

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\log k}{k}\leq|x|\leq 1}|\nabla G|^{N}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\log k}{k}\leq|x|\leq 1}G(-\Delta_{N}G)dx+\int_{|x|=\frac{\log k}{k}}G|\nabla G|^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha(\int_{\frac{\log k}{k}\leq|x|\leq 1}G^{p}dx)^{N/p}+G(\frac{\log k}{k})\int_{|x|=\frac{\log k}{k}}|\nabla G|^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G(\frac{\log k}{k})+\alpha(\int_{\mathbb{B}}G^{p}dx)^{N/p}+O\left(\frac{(\log k)^{N+p-1}}{k^{N}}\right).

As a consequence

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{B}}|\nabla\phi_{k}(x)|^{N}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c^{N/(N-1)}}\left\{-\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{j}+\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\log(1+c_{N}(\log k)^{\frac{N}{N-1}})\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left.+G(\frac{\log k}{k})+\alpha(\int_{\mathbb{B}}G^{p}dx)^{N/p}+O((\log k)^{-\frac{N}{N-1}})\right\}.

Noting also that

\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}\phi_{k}^{p}(x)dx\right)^{N/p}=\frac{1}{c^{N/(N-1)}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{B}}G^{p}dx+O(\frac{(\log k)^{2N}}{k^{N}})\right)^{N/p},

we obtain

\|\phi_{k}\|_{1,\alpha}^{N}=\frac{1}{c^{N/(N-1)}}\left(-\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{j}+\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\log(1+c_{N}(\log k)^{\frac{N}{N-1}})+G(\frac{\log k}{k})+O((\log k)^{-\frac{N}{N-1}})\right).

Set
\|\phi_{k}\|_{1,\alpha}=1
. It then follows that

c^{\frac{N}{N-1}}=\frac{N}{\alpha_{N}}\log k-\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{j}+\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\log c_{N}+A_{0}+O((\log k)^{-\frac{N}{N-1}}).

(33)

This together with (
32
) leads to

b=\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{j}+O((\log k)^{-\frac{N}{N-1}}).

(34)

When
|x|<\frac{\log k}{k}
, we calculate

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}\phi_{k}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}c^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\left(1+\frac{1}{c^{N/(N-1)}}\left(-\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\log(1+c_{N}|kx|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})+b\right)\right)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}

(35)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}c^{\frac{N}{N-1}}\left(1+\frac{N}{N-1}\frac{1}{c^{N/(N-1)}}\left(-\frac{N-1}{\alpha_{N}}\log(1+c_{N}|kx|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})+b\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{N}c^{\frac{N}{N-1}}+\frac{N\alpha_{N}b}{N-1}-{N}\log(1+c_{N}|kx|^{\frac{N}{N-1}}).

In view of (
33
) and (
34
),

\alpha_{N}c^{\frac{N}{N-1}}+\frac{N\alpha_{N}b}{N-1}=N\log k+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{1}{j}+(N-1)\log c_{N}+\alpha_{N}A_{0}+O((\log k)^{-\frac{N}{N-1}}).

(36)

Moreover, integration by parts leads to

\displaystyle\int_{|x|<\frac{\log k}{k}}e^{-{N}\log(1+c_{N}|kx|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{-N}\int_{|y|<\log k}\frac{dy}{(1+c_{N}|y|^{\frac{N}{N-1}})^{N}}

(37)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{-N}\int_{0}^{(\log k)^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}\frac{N-1}{N}\frac{t^{N-2}dt}{(1+c_{N}t)^{N}}

), (

(38)

Using an inequality

\int_{\frac{\log k}{k}\leq|x|\leq 1}e^{\alpha_{N}\phi_{k}^{\frac{N}{N-1}}(x)}dx\geq|\mathbb{B}|+\frac{\alpha_{N}\int_{\mathbb{B}}{G^{\frac{N}{N-1}}}dx}{c^{{N}/{(N-1)^{2}}}}+O((\log k)^{-\frac{N}{N-1}}).

(39)

38
) and (
39

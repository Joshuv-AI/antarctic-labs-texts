Lemma 3.5
.

\soc({A({\Delta})})\leq\omega(G({\Delta}))
.

Proof.

Suppose
x_{F}=x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}\cdots x_{i_{j}}
is a top socle of
A({\Delta})
. By part
(i)
of
Proposition

2.2
,
F
is a face of
{\Delta}
of dimension
(j-1)
. For every quadric
x_{i_{k}}x_{i_{t}}
that divides
x_{F}
, there is a corresponding edge in
G({\Delta})
. Thus,
\{x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\ldots,x_{i_{j}}\}
is a clique of
G({\Delta})
and hence,
\soc({A({\Delta})})=\deg(x_{F})\leq\omega(G({\Delta}))
.

∎

Corollary 3.6
.

When
A({\Delta})
has WLP in degree
1
, if
\dim_{\Bbbk}{A({\Delta})}_{2}\leq\dim_{\Bbbk}{A({\Delta})}_{1}
, then
\soc(A({\Delta}))
is at most
3
.

Proof.

When WLP holds in degree
1
, if
\dim_{\Bbbk}{A({\Delta})}_{2}\leq\dim_{\Bbbk}{A({\Delta})}_{1}
, from part
(ii)
of
Theorem

3.3
, we see that
G({\Delta})
may have bipartite components, in which case the maximum possible clique size is
2
, or every connected nonbipartite component has the property that the number of edges in the component is equal to the number of vertices in the component, allowing clique size at most
3
since for any clique of size greater than
3
, number of edges is greater than the number of vertices. Hence, the clique number of
G({\Delta})
and by
Lemma

3.5
,
\soc(A({\Delta}))
is at most
3
.
∎

4.
Weak Lefschetz Property in degree
d
for Pseudomanifolds

In this section, we look at the Weak Lefschetz Property in degree
d
of
{A({\Delta})}
corresponding to the
d
-dimensional pseudomanifold
{\Delta}
.

Definition 4.1
.

A combinatorial
d
-dimensional
pseudomanifold
is a simplicial complex such that :

(i)
each facet is a
d
-simplex.

(ii)
every
(d-1)
-simplex is a face of at least one and at most two
d
-simplices for
d>1
.

(iii)
given any two
d
-simplices
F_{1},F_{k}
, there exists a chain of
d
-simplices
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},\ldots,F_{k}
such that
F_{i}\cap F_{i+1}
is a
(d-1)
-simplex for
1\leq i\leq k-1
.

We refer to a
d
-dimensional pseudomanifold to be one with boundary if there exists at least one
boundary
(d-1)
-simplex
, i.e., a
(d-1)
-simplex that is a face of exactly one
d
-simplex. We refer to a
d
-simplex having a boundary
(d-1)
-simplex as a
boundary
d
-simplex
. A
d
-dimensional pseudomanifold having no boundary
d
-simplex is called a
pseudomanifold without boundary
.

Given a pure
d
-dimensional complex
\Delta
, any face of dimension
(d-1)
is called a
ridge
. The
dual graph

G^{*}({\Delta})
of a pure complex
\Delta
is the graph in which every facet of
\Delta
becomes a vertex and two vertices in
G^{*}({\Delta})
are adjacent (i.e., are connected by an edge) if and only if the corresponding facets share a ridge.

Remark 4.1
.

For
{A({\Delta})}
corresponding to a
d
-dimensional pseudomanifold
{\Delta}
,

\dim_{\Bbbk}{A({\Delta})}_{d}=
number of ridges and
\dim_{\Bbbk}{A({\Delta})}_{d+1}=
number of
d
-simplices. Further,
{A({\Delta})}
is a level algebra.

4.1.
Pseudomanifold without boundary

Theorem 4.2
.

For
d\geq 1
,
{A({\Delta})}
corresponding to a
d
-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary has WLP in degree
d
if and only if the dual graph is not bipartite.

Proof.

For
d\geq 1
, let
\Delta
be a
d
-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary, i.e., every ridge is the face of exactly two
d
-simplices and every
d
-simplex contains
(d+1)
many ridges. Then,

(4.1)

\dim_{\Bbbk}A({\Delta})_{d}=\left(\frac{d+1}{2}\right)\cdot\dim_{\Bbbk}A({\Delta})_{d+1}

Hence,
\dim_{\Bbbk}A({\Delta})_{d}\geq\dim_{\Bbbk}A({\Delta})_{d+1}
.

Now, consider the dual graph
G^{*}({\Delta})
of
{\Delta}
. The number of vertices in
G^{*}({\Delta})
,
|V_{G^{*}({\Delta})}|
, equals the number of
d
-simplices of
{\Delta}
, and the number of edges in
G^{*}({\Delta})
,
|E_{G^{*}({\Delta})}|
, equals the number of ridges of
{\Delta}
. The map
\mu_{d}:A({\Delta})_{d}\to A({\Delta})_{d+1}
then gives the transpose of the map
\mu_{1}:V_{G^{*}({\Delta})}\to E_{G^{*}({\Delta})}
and hence, by
Lemma

3.2
, the corresponding matrix is the incidence matrix
I(G^{*}({\Delta}))
. So,
\mu_{d}
has full rank if and only if
I(G^{*}({\Delta}))
has full rank. Note that here, by
Eq.

4.1
,

|E_{G^{*}({\Delta})}|\geq|V_{G^{*}({\Delta})}|

Hence, by part
(i)
of
Theorem

3.3
, we see that
\mu_{d}
has full rank if and only if the connected graph
G^{*}({\Delta})
is not bipartite.
∎

Example 4.3
.

Let
{\Delta}
be the octahedron (
2
-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary). For the corresponding
{A({\Delta})}
, WLP fails in degree
2
.

\displaystyle\Delta
is the octahedron
\displaystyle G^{*}(\Delta)
is the edge graph of a cube

This can be observed from the fact that the dual graph of the octahedron is the
1
-skeleton (edge graph) of a cube, and is bipartite.

4.2.
Pseudomanifold with boundary

For
d\geq 1
, let
\Delta
be a
d
-dimensional pseudomanifold with boundary, i.e., there exists at least one boundary
(d-1)
-simplex.

Theorem 4.4
.

WLP in degree
d
always holds for
{A({\Delta})}
corresponding to a
d
-dimensional pseudomanifold
{\Delta}
with boundary.

Proof.

We use the definition of WLP to prove this theorem by showing that
\mu_{d}:A({\Delta})_{d}\to A({\Delta})_{d+1}
is surjective. We use the notation
x_{F}
for the monomial in
{A({\Delta})}
corresponding to the face
F\in{\Delta}
.

For a boundary
d
-simplex
F^{\prime}
, any boundary ridge
B^{\prime}
such that
B^{\prime}\subseteq F^{\prime}
gives
\mu_{d}(x_{B^{\prime}})=x_{F^{\prime}}
. Now we consider a
d
-simplex
F
which is not a boundary facet. By part
(iii)
in
Definition

4.1
, we get some boundary
d
-simplex
F_{k}
, and a chain of
d
-simplices
F=F_{1},F_{2},F_{3},\ldots,F_{k}
such that
F_{i}\cap F_{i+1}=B_{i}
is a ridge for
1\leq i\leq k-1
. Let
B_{k}
be a boundary ridge of
F_{k}
.

We have
\mu_{d}(x_{B_{k}})=x_{F_{k}}
and
\mu_{d}(x_{B_{i}})=x_{F_{i}}+x_{F_{i+1}}
for
1\leq i\leq k-1
.

Now, define

\displaystyle x_{M_{1}}

\displaystyle:=x_{B_{k}}

\displaystyle x_{M_{j}}

\displaystyle:=x_{B_{k-j+1}}-x_{M_{j-1}}\text{ for }2\leq j\leq k

Then,

\mu_{d}(x_{M_{1}})=x_{F_{k}}
,

\mu_{d}(x_{M_{2}})=x_{F_{k-1}}
and continuing this way,

\mu_{d}(x_{M_{k}})=x_{F_{1}}=x_{F}
.
Hence,
\mu_{d}
is surjective.
∎

We now look at some interesting corollaries of the above results.

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
{\Delta}
be a
2
-dimensional pseudomanifold.
{A({\Delta})}
has WLP if and only if
{\Delta}
has boundary or if
{\Delta}
has no boundary, the dual graph
G^{*}({\Delta})
is not bipartite.

Proof.

Let
{\Delta}
be a
2
-dimensional pseudomanifold. Note that here, we only need to check for WLP in degrees
1
and
2
. Firstly, for degree
1
, the
1
-skeleton
G({\Delta})
has only one connected component. Further, each vertex is incident with at least two edges and each edge is incident with two vertices. Thus,

\left(\frac{k}{2}\right)\cdot\dim_{\Bbbk}A({\Delta})_{1}=\dim_{\Bbbk}A({\Delta})_{2}

where
k\geq 2
. Hence,
\dim_{\Bbbk}A({\Delta})_{2}\geq\dim_{\Bbbk}A({\Delta})_{1}
. Since a
2
-dimensional pseudomanifold has triangular facets making
G({\Delta})
nonbipartite, by part
(i)
of
Theorem

3.3
, WLP holds in degree
1
for
{A({\Delta})}
.

Next, in degree
2
, we see that WLP holds for
{A({\Delta})}
either if
{\Delta}
has boundary (
Theorem

4.4
) or, if
{\Delta}
has no boundary, the dual graph
G^{*}({\Delta})
is not bipartite (
Theorem

4.2
).
∎

Definition 4.2
.

A simplicial complex
{\Delta}
is a
triangulation
if there exists a homeomorphism between the geometric realization of
{\Delta}
and a topological manifold.
In the specific case when
{\Delta}
is a
2
-dimensional pseudomanifold, the facets of
{\Delta}
are triangles (
2
-simplices) and the triangulation is said to be
face
2
-colorable
if it is possible to color all the triangles using
2
-colors such that no two adjacent triangles have the same color.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
{\Delta}
be a
2
-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary that is a triangulation. Then, WLP holds in degree
2
for the corresponding
A({\Delta})
, if and only if the triangulation is not face
2
-colorable.

Proof.

When
{\Delta}
is a
2
-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary, by
Theorem

4.2
,
{A({\Delta})}
has WLP in degree
2
if and only if the dual graph has no bipartite components. Since the vertices in the dual graph correspond to triangles in the triangulation, this implies that WLP holds in degree
2
if and only if the triangulation is not face
2
-colorable.
∎

Example 4.7
.

A tetrahedron is a triangulation without boundary of the sphere
S^{2}
. It is clearly not
2
-colorable, hence, the corresponding
{A({\Delta})}
satisfies WLP in degree
2
.

Remark 4.8
.

Face
2
-colorability has some interesting connections with the concept of Grünbaum coloring
[
14
]
. In particular, from
[
14
, Theorem 1]
, we see that if the given triangulation of a closed manifold is not Grünbaum colorable, then, it is not face

-colorable and hence, WLP holds in degree

.

Definition 4.3
.

A graph is said to be
Eulerian
if and only if it has no vertex of odd degree. A
planar triangulation
is a triangulation that can be embedded in the plane.

By
[

, Theorem 1.4(b)]
,
[

, Proposition 2]
, a planar triangulation of a graph

is face

-colorable if and only if the graph

is Eulerian. This gives the following corollary.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
{\Delta}
be a
2
-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary that is a planar triangulation. Then, WLP holds in degree
2
for the corresponding
A({\Delta})
, if and only if the
1
-skeleton
G({\Delta})
is not Eulerian.

Definition 4.4
.

The first barycentric subdivision
of a triangulation is obtained by introducing
4
new vertices and
6
new edges in every triangle as follows - the midpoints of the edges and the centroid of the triangle are the new vertices, and the new edges are created by joining the centroid with the midpoints of the three edges and the vertices of the triangle.

Corollary 4.10
.

Let
{\Delta}
be a

-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary that is the first barycentric subdivision of a triangulation. Then, WLP in degree

fails for
{A({\Delta})}
.

Proof.

When
{\Delta}
is a
2
-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary that is the first barycentric subdivision of a triangulation, by

, Corollary 2]
, we see that the complex is now face

-colorable and hence WLP fails in degree

.
∎

Example 4.11
.

For the first barycentric subdivision of a tetrahedron, WLP fails in degree

for
{A({\Delta})}
.

We note that

5.
Examples of Artinian Gorenstein algebras that fail WLP

In this section we construct Artinian Gorenstein algebras that fail WLP by combining our results and the standard technique of Nagata idealization. We briefly recall the basic set-up that’s relevant for our purpose. Let
{\Bbbk}
be a field of characteristic
0
. Let
R
be a standard graded level Artinian
{\Bbbk}
-algebra with socle degree
d
, that is, the socle of
R
is
R_{d}
(see
Definition

2.1
). Let
\omega
be the graded canonical module of
R
, and let
\tilde{R}=R\ltimes\omega(-d-1)
be the Nagata idealization of
\omega
. Then
\tilde{R}
is a standard graded Artinian Gorenstein algebra with socle degree
d+1
, and
\dim_{{\Bbbk}}\tilde{R}_{i}=\dim_{{\Bbbk}}R_{i}+\dim_{{\Bbbk}}R_{d+1-i}
. For more details on this construction we refer to
[
16
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume the set-up above with
d\geq 2
. Further assume that
\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{2}+\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{d-1}\geq\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{1}+\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{d}
. If
R
fails surjectivity at degree
d-1
, then
\tilde{R}
fails surjectivity, and hence fails WLP in degree
d-1
.

Proof.

Let
l\in\tilde{R}_{1}
be a general linear form. If
l
induces a surjective map
\tilde{R}_{d-1}\to\tilde{R}_{d}
, then the restriction of
l
to
R_{1}
must induce a surjective map from
R_{d-1}\to R_{d}
. But indeed no linear form can give surjective map
R_{d-1}\to R_{d}
(as general linear forms have maximal possible rank). Finally, to show that failing surjectivity implies failing WLP, we need to show that
\dim_{\Bbbk}\tilde{R}_{d-1}\geq\dim_{\Bbbk}\tilde{R}_{d}
, which follows from the assumption on
R
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\Delta
be a
(d-1)
-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary such that the dual graph of
\Delta
is bipartite. Let
R=A(\Delta)
. The idealization
\tilde{R}
of
R
is an Artinian Gorenstein ring that fails WLP in degree
d-1
.

Proof.

R
is a level algebra (
Remark

4.1
) that fails surjectivity at degree
d-1
by
Theorem

4.2
. Thus, by applying
Proposition

5.1
, we just need to show that
\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{2}+\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{d-1}\geq\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{1}+\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{d}
. Note that
\dim_{\Bbbk}R_{i}=f_{i-1}(\Delta)
. Clearly
f_{1}({\Delta})\geq f_{0}({\Delta})
: the
1
-skeleton of
\Delta
is connected and hence
f_{1}({\Delta})\geq f_{0}({\Delta})-1
with equality if and only if it is a tree, which is impossible as
\Delta
is a pseudo-manifold. Equally clearly,
f_{d-2}({\Delta})=(d/2)f_{d-1}({\Delta})
: each
(d-2)
-face is in exactly
2
facets, and each facet has exactly
d
many
(d-2)
-faces. Thus the desired inequality follows.
∎

Example 5.3
.

Let
{\Delta}=\{12,23,34,14\}
, a
4
-cycle which is certainly a
1
-dimensional pseudomanifold without boundary,
R={A({\Delta})}={\Bbbk}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]/J_{{\Delta}}
where
J_{{\Delta}}=\langle x_{1}^{2},x_{2}^{2},x_{3}^{2},x_{4}^{2},x_{1}x_{3},x_{2}x_{4}\rangle
, is a graded Artinian level
{\Bbbk}
-algebra with
\soc({A({\Delta})})=2
. By
Theorem

3.3
(i), since
G({\Delta})
is bipartite, WLP fails in degree
1
(this can also be observed from
Theorem

4.2
, since the dual graph of
{\Delta}
is also a
4
-cycle which is bipartite).

We now refer to
[
16
, Lemma 3.3]
to compute
\tilde{R}
, which is a quotient of the polynomial ring
T={\Bbbk}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}]
, where the variables
y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4}
correspond to the generators of
\omega_{R}
. From the presentation of
\omega_{R}
, we get the ideal defining the relations involving generators of
\omega_{R}
,

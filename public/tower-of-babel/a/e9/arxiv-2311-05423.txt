Constraints on Non-Gaussian primordial curvature perturbation from the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA third observing run
Shuichiro Yokoyama

Abstract
The SIGW , arising from large amplitude primordial density fluctuations, provide a unique observational test for directly probing the epoch of inflation. In this work, we provide constraints on the SIGW background by taking into account the non-Gaussianity in the primordial density fluctuations, using the first three observing runs (O1-O3) data of the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA collaboration. We find that the non-Gaussianity gives a non-negligible effect on the GW energy density spectrum and starts to affect the analysis of the O1-O3 data when the non-Gaussianity parameter is F NL > 3.55 F_{\mathrm{NL}}>3.55 . Furthermore, the constraints exhibit asymptotic behavior given by F NL ​ A g = const . F_{\mathrm{NL}}A_{g}=\rm{const.} at large F NL F_{\mathrm{NL}} limit, where A g A_{g} denotes the amplitude of the curvature perturbations. In the limit of large F NL F_{\mathrm{NL}} , we placed a 95% credible level upper limit F NL ​ A g ≤ 0.115 , 0.106 , 0.112 F_{\mathrm{NL}}A_{g}\leq 0.115,0.106,0.112 at fixed scales of 10 16 , 10 16.5 , 10 17 ​ Mpc − 1 10^{16},10^{16.5},10^{17}~\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1} , respectively.

5
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the constraint on the non-Gaussian primordial curvature perturbations, using the most recent data from the LVK O1-O3. By considering the second-order cosmological perturbations, the primordial curvature perturbations can induce the
GW
called
SIGW
, and such
SIGW
are a unique probe for studying inflation physics, as the scales probed by GW interferometer experiments are much smaller than those of the
CMB
.

Our main result is in Sec.
4
where we performed the parameter estimation for the combined model of
SIGW
and
CBC
. We put an upper limit on the amplitude of the primordial curvature power spectrum
A_{g}
and the strength of non-Gaussianity
F_{\mathrm{NL}}
. From the marginalized constraint, shown in Fig.
2
, the most stringent limit on the amplitude,
A_{g}<0.015
at the
95
%
CL
, was obtained at the peak scale
k_{*}/\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}\sim 2.04\times 10^{16}
. Additionally, we observed that the influence of non-Gaussianities on the
GW
power spectrum becomes non-negligible when
F_{\mathrm{NL}}\simeq 3.55
. Since marginalized constraints inevitably depend on the range of priors, we have also presented the analysis by fixing either the
F_{\mathrm{NL}}
parameter or the peak scale
k_{*}
(Figs.
3
and
4
). Although the current constraints appear to be weaker than those imposed by the overproduction of primordial black holes (PBHs), it is anticipated that these constraints will improve with the third-generation
GW
detectors such as the Einstein Telescope (ET) and Cosmic Explorer (CE).

While we have simply assumed the quadratic order local type non-Gaussianity (the simple
F_{\mathrm{NL}}
parametrization) to impose the form of non-Gaussianity, it is well known that non-Gaussian tails can vary significantly depending on the mechanisms generating large curvature perturbations. One of the primary theoretical challenges lies in developing a calculation method for the
SIGW
spectrum capable of handling arbitrary shapes of non-Gaussianity.
Once we develop a robust framework for calculating the
SIGW
spectrum for all types of non-Gaussianity, we would be able to provide more valuable observational constraints for primordial curvature perturbations. This task is becoming increasingly important given the anticipated increase in observational opportunities with GWs.

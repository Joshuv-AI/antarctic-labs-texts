Abstract
. We consider positive singular solutions (i.e. with a non-removable singularity) of a system of PDEs driven by p p -Laplacian operators and with the additional presence of a nonlinear first order term. By a careful use of a rather new version of the moving plane method, we prove the symmetry of the solutions. The result is already new in the scalar case.

1.
Introduction

The study of symmetry properties of solutions of PDEs is a long–standing topic which dates back to the
foundational works of Alexandrov
[
1
]
, Serrin
[
24
]
, Gidas, Ni and Nirenberg
[
17
]
and
Berestycki and Nirenberg
[
2
]
. The common point among such papers is the use of the celebrated
moving plane method
in the case of semilinear elliptic equations. This powerful technique relies heavily
on the validity of (weak and strong) maximum or comparison, principles. The relevance of the ideas which
are at the core of the aforementioned papers is clearly witnessed by the huge number of
contributions which are now available in the literature, whose aim has been to extend the results above mentioned to several different cases in the local framework: quasilinear equations
(see, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
) and
cooperative elliptic systems (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
27
]
).

A slightly different line of research concerns the study of symmetry properties of
singular
solutions.
In this case, we refer, e.g., to
[
5
,
25
]
for the case of point-singularity.
More recently, Sciunzi
[
22
]
developed a new method which allows to study symmetry properties of
solutions which are singular on sets of
small capacity
.

The aim of this paper is to focus on systems of PDEs
driven by the
p
-Laplacian with the additional presence of a nonlinear gradient term. To be more precise,
we will consider solutions
u_{i}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma)
(
i=1,\ldots,m
) to the
following system of PDEs

(
\mathcal{S}
)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\Delta_{p_{i}}u_{i}+a_{i}(u_{i})|\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}=f_{i}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})&\textrm{in }\Omega\setminus\Gamma\\
u_{i}>0&\textrm{in }\Omega\setminus\Gamma\\
u_{i}=0&\textrm{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Here,
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a smooth and bounded domain and for suitable smooth functions

-\Delta_{p}v:=\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla v|^{p-2}\nabla v\right)

denotes the
p
-Laplacian. Generally, solutions to equations involving the

p
-Laplace operator are of class
C^{1,\alpha}
. This assumption
is natural according to classical
regularity results
[
14
,
19
,
26
]
. We point out that, in this paper, we deal with singular solutions that are
C^{1,\alpha}
far from the critical set
\Gamma
. We consider solutions with a non-removable singularity: we mean that solutions possibly do not admit a smooth extension in
\Omega
, i.e. it is not possible to find
\tilde{u}_{i}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u_{i}\equiv\tilde{u}_{i}
in
\Omega\setminus\Gamma
. Indeed, without any a priori assumption, the gradient of the solutions possibly blows up near the
critical set and hence each equations of (

S

) may exhibit both a degenerate and a
singular
nature at the same time.

Before stating our main result,
we first introduce
the main
structural assumptions

we require on problem (

S

); these assumptions
will be tacitly understood in the sequel.

Assumptions.
Throughout what follows, we assume that

({h}_{\Omega}):

\Omega
is a bounded domain in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
(with
N\geq 2
), which is

convex
in the
x_{1}
-direction and symmetric with respect to the hyperplane

\Pi_{0}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}:\,x_{1}=0\};

({h}_{\Gamma}):

\Gamma\subseteq\Omega\cap\Pi_{0}
is a compact set
satisfying

\mathrm{Cap}_{p}(\Gamma)=0
, where

p:=\max_{1\leq k\leq m}p_{k};

({h}_{p,q}):

for every
i=1,\ldots,m
, we have

(1.1)

\begin{split}\frac{2N+2}{N+2}<p_{i}\leq N\qquad&\text{and}\qquad\max\{p_{i}-1,1\}\leq q_{i}<p_{i}\\
&\,\,\text{or}\\
2\leq p_{i}\leq N\qquad&\text{and}\qquad q_{i}=p_{i}.\end{split}

({h}_{a}):

the functions
a_{i}(\cdot)
are
locally Lipschitz-continuous

on
\mathcal{I}:=[0,+\infty)
, that is,
for every
M>0
there exists a constant

L=L_{M}>0
such that

|a_{i}(t)-a_{i}(s)|\leq L|t-s|\qquad\text{for every $t,s\in[0,M]$};

(h_{f}):

the functions
f_{i}(\cdot)
are
of class
C^{1}

on
\mathcal{I}^{m}
; moreover, we assume that

(a)

f_{i}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})>0
for every
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}>0
;

(b)

\partial_{t_{k}}f_{i}(\cdot)\geq 0
for every
1\leq i,k\leq m
with
i\neq k
.

We remark that, without loss of generality, we are considering the
x_{1}
-direction being the operator invariant with respect to translations and rotations.

Then, we properly define what we mean by a
weak solution

to problem (

S

).

Definition 1.1
.

We say that a vector-valued function

\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})

is a
weak solution
to (

S

)
if it satisfies the properties listed below:

(1)

for every
i=1,\ldots,m
and every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
, one has

(1.2)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\varphi\rangle\,dx+\int_{\Omega}a_{i}(u_{i})|\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f_{i}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})\varphi\,dx;

(2)

u_{i}>0
pointwise in
\Omega\setminus\Gamma

(
for every
i=1,\ldots,m
)
;

(3)

u_{i}=0
on
\partial\Omega

(
for every
i=1,\ldots,m
)
.

Our main results is the following

Theorem 1.2
.

Let assumptions
(h_{\Omega})
-to-
(h_{f})
be in force, and let

\mathbf{u}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma;\mathbb{R}^{m})

be a weak solution to problem (

S

). Then, the following facts hold:

(i)

\mathbf{u}
is symmetric with respect to the hyperplane
\Pi_{0}
, namely

\mathbf{u}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})=\mathbf{u}(-x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{N})\quad\text{in }\Omega.

(ii)

\mathbf{u}
is non-decreasing in the
x_{1}
-direction in the set
\Omega_{0}=\{x_{1}<0\}
and moreover

(1.3)

\partial_{x_{1}}u_{i}>0\quad\text{in }\Omega_{0},

for every
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
.

We remark that, although the technique that we will develop to prove Theorem
1.2
works for any
p_{i}>N
, the result is stated in the range (
1.1
) since there are no sets (different from the empty-set) of zero
p_{i}
-capacity when
p_{i}>N
.

We now want to spend a few comments about
Theorem
1.2
.
Firstly, we notice that Theorem
1.2
extends the results in
[
15
]
to the case of
singular (non regular) solutions.
The main novelty of our result concerns the singular case
p_{i}<2
(for some
i=1,\ldots,m
).
In order to
explain this,
let us consider the case of a scalar equation with
a(u)\equiv 0
: our problem boils then down to the case
considered in
[
16
]
(which is therefore our benchmark case), that is,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)&\textrm{in }\Omega\setminus\Gamma\\
u>0&\textrm{in }\Omega\setminus\Gamma\\
u=0&\textrm{on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

In
[
16
]
the Authors, already in the scalar case, require some a priori assumptions on the nonlinearity
f
involved in the problem, while here we are able to remove the growth a priori assumption made in
[
16
]
on the nonlinearities
f_{i}
. This aspect
is strictly related with a technical issue that has to be faced when using the
integral version
of
the moving plane method. In particular, one needs to apply a weighted Sobolev inequality due to Trudinger

[
28
]
, whose validity depends on proper summability conditions of the weight, which happens to be of
the form
|\nabla u|^{p-2}
. In
[
16
]
this condition is proved to be satisfied after a nice study of
the behavior of the gradient of the solution near the singular set
\Gamma
,
which is based on a subtle
growth estimate proved in
[
21
]
, thanks to a priori assumption on the nonlinearity
f
involved in the problem. Since we are lacking an analogous estimate for our solutions (even in
the simpler case
a_{i}\equiv 0
for every
i=1,\ldots,m
), we are not even entitled to profit of the
weighted Sobolev inequality of Trudinger. Nevertheless, we
can avoid such a priori assumptions by exploiting some a priori regularity results that we prove in this paper, see Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
2.4
below.
We stress that this is a considerable simplification
of the proof in
[
16
]
and it is
crucially based on the new Lemma
2.4
.

Now, by scrutinizing the proof of Theorem
1.2
, one can easily
recognize that a key ingredient for our argument is the fact that,
under assumption
({h}_{p,q})
, we have

p_{i}^{*}=\frac{Np_{i}}{N-p_{i}}\geq 2\qquad(\text{for all $i=1,\ldots,m$)}.

However, since the above inequality is equivalent to require that

p_{i}\geq 2N/(N+2)
,
it is natural to wonder why
we
require

p_{i}
to satisfy the worse lower bound

p_{i}>\frac{2N+2}{N+2}=:\beta_{N}.

The main reason behind this choice is that, when
p\leq\beta_{N}
, we

do not have

at our disposal a Strong Comparison Principle
for the operator
-\Delta_{p}u+a(u)|\nabla u|^{q}
which
allows us to do not take care of the
critical set

\mathcal{Z}=\{\nabla u=0\}
that is, in general, a crucial point when one deals with the
p
-Laplace operator.

In view of this fact, in order
to establish Theorem
1.2
when

\frac{2N}{N+2}\leq p_{i}\leq\frac{2N+2}{N+2}

we would need to recover a technical lemma analogous to
[
16
, Lemma 3.2]
, which also requires
suitable estimates for the second-order derivatives of the solution
of (

S

). Even if these estimates are available in our setting (see

[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
), we prefer
to avoid this technicality here: indeed, we believe that the main novelties of our work
are both to consider systems involving general first order terms and the possibility of considering the case
p_{i}<2
without the need of prescribing precise
(a priori) growth estimates for the solution of (

S

). To the best of our knowledge, our result is new also in the case of a single scalar equation.

The paper is organized as follows: we prove some technical results in Section
2
that we will exploit in
Section
3
to prove Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Notation and preliminary results

In this first section we collect all the relevant notation
which shall be used throughout the paper; moreover, we review some
results
already known
in the literature which shall be fundamental for the
proof of our Theorem
1.2
. We will adopt the symbol
|A|
to denote the
N
-dimensional
Lebesgue measure of a measurable set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

2.1.
Capacity.

Due to the important role played by assumption
(h_{\Gamma})
in our arguments, we briefly
recall a few notions and results concerning the
Sobolev capacity
.

Let
K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a
compact set
,
and let
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be an open set such that
K\subseteq\mathcal{O}
.
Given any
1<r\leq N
, the
r
-capacity
of the
condenser

\mathcal{C}:=(K,\mathcal{O})

is defined as

\mathrm{Cap}_{r}(K,\mathcal{O}):=\inf\bigg\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\phi|^{r}\,\mathrm{d}x\,:\,\text{$\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{O})$ and $\phi\geq 1$ on $K$}\bigg\}.

We then say that
K
has
vanishing
r
-capacity
, and we write

\mathrm{Cap}_{r}(K)=0
, if

\mathrm{Cap}_{r}(K,\mathcal{O})=0\quad\text{\emph{for every} open set $\mathcal{O}\supset K$}.

As is reasonable to expect, compact sets with vanishing
r
-capacity
have to be
very small
; the next theorem shows that
this is actually true in the sense of Hausdorff measure.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a compact set. Then, the following assertions hold.

(1)

If
\mathrm{Cap}_{r}(K)=0
, then
\mathcal{H}^{s}(K)=0
for every
s>N-r
.

(2)

If
r<N
and
\mathcal{H}^{N-r}(K)<\infty
, then
\mathrm{Cap}_{r}(K)=0
.

For a proof of Theorem
2.1
,
we refer the Reader to
[
18
, Sec. 2.24]
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
1<r_{1}<r_{2}\leq N
and let
K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be compact.
Then,

\mathrm{Cap}_{r_{2}}(K)=0\,\,\Longrightarrow\,\,\mathrm{Cap}_{r_{1}}(K)=0.

Proof.

Since, by assumption,
\mathrm{Cap}_{r_{2}}(K)=0
, by Theorem
2.1
-(1)
we have
\mathcal{H}^{s}(K)=0
for every
s>N-r_{2}
;
in particular, since
r_{1}<r_{2}
, we derive that

r_{1}<N\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{H}^{N-r_{1}}(K)=0.

Using this fact and Theorem
2.1
-(2), we then
conclude that
\mathrm{Cap}_{r_{1}}(K)=0
.
∎

On account of Corollary
2.2
, if
\Gamma\subseteq\Omega\cap\Pi_{0}

is as in Theorem
1.2
, we have

(2.1)

\mathrm{Cap}_{p_{i}}(\Gamma)=0\qquad\text{for every $i=1,\ldots,m$}.

Let now
K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be a fixed compact set
with vanishing
r
-capacity
, and let

\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
be an open set containing
K
. We aim to show
that it is possible to construct a family
of functions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, say
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
,
which satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\psi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Lip}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
0\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
pointwise in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
;

(2)

there exists
an open neighborhood
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
of
K
such that

\text{$\psi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0$ on $\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}$};

(3)

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
;

(4)

there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
, only depending on
r
, such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{r}\,dx\leq C_{0}\varepsilon.

In fact, let
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(K,\mathcal{O})>0
be such that

\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}:=\big\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}:\,\mathrm{dist}(x,K)<\varepsilon\big\}\Subset\mathcal{O}\qquad\text{for every $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}$}.

Since
\mathrm{Cap}_{r}(K)=0
and
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is an open neighborhood
of
K
, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})

there exists a smooth function
\phi_{\varepsilon}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon})
such that

(2.2)

\text{$\phi_{\varepsilon}\geq 1$ on $K$}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}|^{r}\,dx<\varepsilon.

We then consider the Lipschitz functions

(i)

T(s):=\max\{0;\min\{s;1\}\}
(for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
),

(ii)

g(t):=\max\{0;1-2t\}
(for
t\geq 0
)

and we define, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
,

(2.3)

\psi_{\varepsilon}:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}},\qquad\psi_{\varepsilon}(x):=g\big(T(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x))\big).

Clearly,
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Lip}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
0\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
; moreover,
by (
2.2
) we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{r}\,dx\leq 2^{r}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\phi_{\varepsilon}|^{r}\,dx\leq 2^{r}\varepsilon.

Hence, the family
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies
properties (1) and (4) (with
C_{0}=2^{r}
).
As for the validity of property (2) we observe that,
by the explicit definitions of
T
and
g
, we have

\psi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0\quad\text{on $\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}:=\big\{\phi_{\varepsilon}>1/2\big\}$};

as a consequence, since
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}
is an open neighborhood of
K
and
since
\mathcal{V}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
(remind that

\phi_{\varepsilon}\geq 1
on
K
and
\mathrm{supp}(\phi_{\varepsilon})\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
),
we immediately conclude that also property (2) is satisfied.
Finally, the validity of property (3) follows from the fact that

(2.4)

\psi_{\varepsilon}\equiv g(0)=1\quad\text{on ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}$}.

Indeed, since the family
\{\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
shrinks to
K
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, by
(
2.4
) we get

\text{$\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\to 1$ as $\varepsilon\to 0^{+}$ \quad\emph{for every $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus K$}};

from this, since
|K|=\mathcal{H}^{N}(K)=0
(remind that
\mathrm{Cap}_{p}(K)=0
and
see Theorem
2.1
), we immediately conclude
that the family
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies also property (3), as claimed.

Throughout what follows, we will repeatedly use the family
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}

with different choices of
K
and
\mathcal{O}
; hence, to simplify the notation,
we shall refer to this family as a
cut-off family for the compact set

K
, related with the open set
\mathcal{O}
.

2.2.
Notations for the moving plane method.

Let

\mathbf{u}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma;{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
be a weak solution
to problem (

S

).
For every fixed
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we denote by
R_{\lambda}

the reflection trough the hyperplane
\Pi_{\lambda}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}:\,x_{1}=\lambda\}
,
that is,

(2.5)

R_{\lambda}(x)=x_{\lambda}:=(2\lambda-x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N});

accordingly, we introduce the vector-valued function

(2.6)

\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}(x)=(u_{1,\lambda}(x),\ldots,u_{m,\lambda}(x)):=\mathbf{u}(x_{\lambda}),\qquad\text{for $x\in R_{\lambda}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma)$}.

We point out that, since
\mathbf{u}
solves (

S

), it is easy to see that

(1)

\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}\in C^{1,\alpha}(R_{\lambda}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma);{\mathbb{R}}^{m})
;

(2)

for every
i=1,\ldots,m
and
every
\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(R_{\lambda}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma))
,
one has

(2.7)

\int_{R_{\lambda}(\Omega)}|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i,\lambda},\nabla\varphi\rangle\,dx+\int_{R_{\lambda}(\Omega)}a_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\varphi\,dx=\int_{R_{\lambda}(\Omega)}f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{\lambda})\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x;

(3)

u_{i,\lambda}>0
pointwise
R_{\lambda}(\Omega\setminus\Gamma)
(for every
i=1,\ldots,m
);

(4)

u_{i,\lambda}\equiv 0
on
R_{\lambda}(\partial\Omega)

(for every
i=1,\ldots,m
).

To proceed further, we let

(2.8)

\varrho=\varrho_{\Omega}:=\inf_{x\in\Omega}x_{1}

and we observe that, since
\Omega
is
bounded and symmetric with respect to

\Pi_{0}
,
we certainly have
-\infty<\varrho<0
.
As a consequence, for every
\lambda\in(\varrho,0)
we can set

(2.9)

\Omega_{\lambda}:=\{x\in\Omega:\,x_{1}<\lambda\}.

We explicitly point out that, since

\Omega
is convex in the
x_{1}
-direction, we have

(2.10)

\Omega_{\lambda}\subseteq R_{\lambda}(\Omega)\cap\Omega.

Finally, for every
\lambda\in(\varrho,0)
we define the function

\mathbf{w}_{\lambda}(x)=(w_{1,\lambda}(x),\ldots,w_{m,\lambda}(x)):=(\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}_{\lambda})(x),\qquad\text{for
$x\in(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma)\cap R_{\lambda}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Gamma)$}.

On account of (
2.10
),

\mathbf{w}_{\lambda}
is surely well-posed on

\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}\setminus R_{\lambda}(\Gamma)
.

2.3.
Preliminary results.

After these preliminaries, we
devote the remaining part of this section
to collect some auxiliary results which shall
be useful for the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
In what follows, we tacitly inherit all the notation
introduced so far.

To begin with, we recall some
identities between vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
which
are useful in dealing
with quasilinear operators:

for every
s>1

there exist constants
C_{1},\ldots,C_{4}>0
, only depending on
s
, such that,
for every
\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, one has

(2.11)

\begin{split}&\langle|\eta|^{s-2}\eta-|\eta^{\prime}|^{s-2}\eta^{\prime},\eta-\eta^{\prime}\rangle\geq C_{1}(|\eta|+|\eta^{\prime}|)^{s-2}|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|^{2},\\[4.26773pt]
&\big||\eta|^{s-2}\eta-|\eta^{\prime}|^{s-2}\eta^{\prime}|\leq C_{2}(|\eta|+|\eta^{\prime}|)^{s-2}|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|,\\[4.26773pt]
&\langle|\eta|^{s-2}\eta-|\eta^{\prime}|^{s-2}\eta^{\prime},\eta-\eta^{\prime}\rangle\geq C_{3}|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|^{s}\qquad(\text{if $s\geq 2$}),\\[4.26773pt]
&\big||\eta|^{s-2}\eta-|\eta^{\prime}|^{s-2}\eta^{\prime}|\leq C_{4}|\eta-\eta^{\prime}|^{s-1}\qquad(\text{if $1<s<2$}).\end{split}

We refer, e.g., to
[
7
]
for a proof of
(
2.11
).

We then establish the following fundamental lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let assumptions
(h_{\Omega})
-to-
(h_{f})
be in force.
Let

i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
be fixed, and let
\lambda\in(\varrho,0)
be such that

R_{\lambda}(\Gamma)\cap\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}\neq\varnothing.

Then, there exists a constant
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}_{i}>0
such that

(2.12)

\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\left(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\right)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx\leq\mathbf{c}_{i}.

Proof.

We first notice that, since
\lambda<0
, by assumption
(h_{\Gamma})

we have
\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}\cap\Gamma=\varnothing
.
Thus, the function
\mathbf{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
is of class
C^{1,\alpha}

on
\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}
, and we can set

M=M_{\mathbf{u}}:=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\big(\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda})}+\|\nabla u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda})}\big)<+\infty.

Moreover, since

u_{i}
and
u_{i,\lambda}
are non-negative, we have

(2.13)

0\leq w_{i,\lambda}^{+}=(u_{i}-u_{i,\lambda})^{+}\leq u_{i}\leq M\qquad\text{pointwise
in $\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}\setminus R_{\lambda}(\Gamma)$}.

Now, to prove (
2.12
) we distinguish two cases.

(i)

\max\{1,p_{i}-1\}\leq q_{i}<p_{i}
;

(ii)

q_{i}=p_{i}
.

Case (i)
. First of all we observe that,
since
R_{\lambda}
is a bijective
linear map and since
\Gamma
has vanishing
p_{i}
-capacity
(see (
2.1
)), the compact set
\Gamma_{\lambda}:=R_{\lambda}(\Gamma)
satisfies

\mathrm{Cap}_{p_{i}}(\Gamma_{\lambda})=0.

As a consequence, if
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is a fixed open neighborhood
of
\Gamma_{\lambda}
, we can choose a
cut-off family

\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}

for
\Gamma_{\lambda}
, related with the open set
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
.
This means, precisely, that

(1)

\psi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Lip}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
0\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
pointwise in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
;

(2)

there exists
an open neighborhood
\mathcal{V}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
of
\Gamma_{\lambda}

such that

\text{$\psi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0$ on $\mathcal{V}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}$};

(3)

\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
;

(4)

there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\leq C_{0}\varepsilon.

We now define, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
,
the map

\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}(x):=\begin{cases}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}(x)\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}(x)=(u_{i}-u_{i,\lambda})^{+}(x)\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}(x),&\text{if $x\in\Omega_{\lambda}$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We then claim that the following assertion hold:

(i)

\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Lip}({\mathbb{R}}^{N});

(ii)

\operatorname{supp}(\varphi_{i,\varepsilon})\subseteq\Omega_{\lambda}
and
\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}\equiv 0
near
\Gamma_{\lambda}
.

In fact, since
u_{i}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda})
and

u_{i,\lambda}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}\setminus\Gamma_{\lambda})
, we readily see that

\text{$w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\in\mathrm{Lip}(\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}\setminus V)$
\emph{for every o\-pen set $V\supseteq\Gamma_{\lambda}$}};

from this, since

\psi_{\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Lip}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})

and
\psi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
on
\mathcal{V}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}\supseteq\Gamma_{\lambda}
,
we get

\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Lip}(\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda})
.
On the other hand, since

\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega_{\lambda}
, we easily conclude that

\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Lip}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
as claimed. As for assertion (ii), it follows from
the definition of
\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}
, jointly with the fact that

\text{$\psi_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0$ on $\mathcal{V}^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}\supseteq\Gamma_{\lambda}$}.

On account of properties (i)-(ii) of
\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}
,
a standard density argument allows us to use

\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}
as a test function
both in (
1.2
) and
(
2.7
)
, obtaining

(2.14)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\langle|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda},\nabla\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}\rangle\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(a_{i}(u_{i})|\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}-a_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\big)\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}\,dx\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(f_{i}(\mathbf{u})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}))\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}\,dx.\end{split}

By unraveling the very definition of
\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}
, we then obtain

(2.15)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\langle|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda},\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\rangle\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+p_{i}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\langle|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda},\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\rangle w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(a_{i}(u_{i})|\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}-a_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\big)w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(f_{i}(\mathbf{u})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}))w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.\end{split}

We now observe that the integral in the
right-hand side of (
2.15
) is actually
performed on the set

A_{i,\lambda}:=\{x\in\Omega_{\lambda}:\,u_{i}\geq u_{i,\lambda}\}\setminus\Gamma_{\lambda}
;
moreover, we have

(2.16)

0\leq u_{i,\lambda}(x)\leq u_{i}(x)\leq M\quad\text{for every $x\in A_{i,\lambda}$}.

The right hand side of (
2.15
) can be arranged as follows

(2.17)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(f_{i}(\mathbf{u})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}))\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}[f_{i}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},\ldots,u_{m,\lambda})]\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big[f_{i}(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})\\
&\quad\qquad\quad+f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,u_{m,\lambda})\big]\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big[f_{i}(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})\\
&\quad\qquad\quad+f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda}\ldots,u_{m})\\
&\quad\qquad\quad\,\,\vdots\\
&\quad\qquad\quad+f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,u_{i},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda}\ldots,u_{i,\lambda},\ldots,u_{m})\\
&\quad\qquad\quad\,\,\vdots\\
&\quad\qquad\quad+f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},u_{3,\lambda}\ldots,{u}_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},u_{3,\lambda}\ldots,u_{m,\lambda})\big]\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.\end{split}

By (
2.17
) we have

(2.18)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}&(f_{i}(\mathbf{u})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}))\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}[f_{i}(u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},...,u_{m,\lambda})]\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\frac{f_{i}(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})}{u_{1}-u_{1,\lambda}}(u_{1}-u_{1,\lambda})w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\frac{f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,u_{m})}{u_{2}-u_{2,\lambda}}(u_{2}-u_{2,\lambda})w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad\,\,\vdots\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\frac{f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{i},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,u_{i,\lambda},\ldots,u_{m})}{u_{i}-u_{i,\lambda}}(u_{i}-u_{i,\lambda})w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad\,\,\vdots\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\frac{f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,{u}_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,u_{m,\lambda})}{u_{m}-u_{m,\lambda}}(u_{m}-u_{m,\lambda})w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\leq\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\frac{f_{i}(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})}{u_{1}-u_{1,\lambda}}(u_{1}-u_{1,\lambda})^{+}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\frac{f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,u_{m})}{u_{2}-u_{2,\lambda}}(u_{2}-u_{2,\lambda})^{+}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad\,\,\vdots\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\frac{f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2},\ldots,u_{i},\ldots,u_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,u_{i,\lambda},\ldots,u_{m})}{u_{i}-u_{i,\lambda}}(u_{i}-u_{i,\lambda})^{+}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad\,\,\vdots\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\frac{f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,{u}_{m})-f_{i}(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda},\ldots,u_{m,\lambda})}{u_{m}-u_{m,\lambda}}(u_{m}-u_{m,\lambda})^{+}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}L_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}w_{j,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx,\end{split}

where in the last inequality we thought

u_{j}-u_{j,\lambda}:=w_{j,\lambda}=w_{j,\lambda}^{+}-w_{j,\lambda}^{-}

and we used the cooperativity
assumption of each

for

, together with
the fact that
f_{j}
is of class

(see assumption (

)). Here,

is the Lipschitz
constant of

on

. Hence, resuming the computations above, we have

(2.19)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(f_{i}(\mathbf{u})-f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}))\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}L_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}w_{j,\lambda}^{+}\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.\end{split}

On the other hand, by using the estimates in (
2.11
), we get

(2.20)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\langle|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda},\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\rangle\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\geq C_{1}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.\end{split}

Gathering
together (
2.19
) and (
2.20
),
from (
2.15
) we then obtain

(2.21)

\begin{split}&C_{1}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\langle|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda},\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\rangle\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq p_{i}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big||\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda}\big|\,|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big|a_{i}(u_{i})|\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}-a_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\big|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{m}L_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}w_{j,\lambda}^{+}\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq p_{i}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big||\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda}\big|\,|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big|a_{i}(u_{i})-a_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})\big|\,|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|a_{i}(u_{i})\big||\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\big|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j=1}^{m}L_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}w_{j,\lambda}^{+}\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.\end{split}

To proceed further, we now turn to provide ad-hoc estimates for the integrals in

). To this end, we first introduce the notation

\displaystyle F_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}L_{j}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}w_{j,\lambda}^{+}\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.

Moreover, we split the set
\Omega_{\lambda}
as

\Omega_{\lambda}=\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}\cup\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda},

where

\displaystyle\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}=\{x\in\Omega_{\lambda}\setminus\Gamma_{\lambda}:\,|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}(x)|<2|\nabla u_{i}|\}\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\qquad\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}=\{x\in\Omega_{\lambda}\setminus\Gamma_{\lambda}:\,|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}(x)|\geq 2|\nabla u_{i}|\}.

Then it follows that

-

By the definition of
\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}
, one has

(2.22)

|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|<3|\nabla u_{i}|.

-

By the definition of the set

and standard triangular

(2.23)

\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\leq|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|-|\nabla u_{i}|\leq|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}|\leq|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|+|\nabla u_{i}|\leq\frac{3}{2}|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|.

Accordingly, we define

(2.24)

\begin{split}&P_{i,1}:=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{(1)}}\big||\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda}\big|\,|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx\\
&P_{i,2}:=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{(2)}}\big||\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda}\big|\,|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx\\

&I_{i,2}:=\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}\big|a_{i}(u_{i})-a_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})\big|\,|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&J_{i,1}:=\int_{\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}}|a_{i}(u_{i})|\big||\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\big|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&J_{i,2}:=\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}|a_{i}(u_{i})|\big||\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\big|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.\end{split}

\varepsilon
(but possibly depending on

-  Estimate of

If

), (
2.22
) and by Hölder’s inequality we obtain

\begin{split}P_{i,1}&\leq C_{4}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{(1)}}|\nabla w^{+}_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-1}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,dx\\

\begin{split}&P_{i,1}+P_{i,2}\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad+\frac{C}{\delta}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p_{i}-2}{p_{i}}}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{i}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p_{i}}}\\[2.84544pt]
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\qquad(\text{for every $\delta>0$}),\end{split}

where
C>0
is a suitable constant depending on
p_{i},\lambda,\,\Omega

and
M
.

In the same way, if
1<p_{i}<2
, we deduce that

\begin{split}&P_{i,1}+P_{i,2}\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w^{+}_{i,\lambda}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad+C\left(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\qquad(\text{for every $\delta>0$}),\end{split}

where
C>0
is a suitable constant depending on
p_{i},\lambda,\,\Omega

and
M
.

In both cases, taking into account
(
2.13
) and the properties of
\psi_{\varepsilon}
, we derive the estimate

(2.29)

\begin{split}&P_{i,1}+P_{i,2}\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+C_{\delta}\varepsilon^{1/p_{i}},\end{split}

holding true for every choice of
\delta>0
.

-  Estimate of
I_{i,1}
.
Since the integral
I_{i,1}

is actually
performed on the set

A_{i,\lambda}=\{u_{i}\geq u_{i,\lambda}\}\setminus\Gamma_{\lambda},

from (
2.16
), assumption
(h_{a})
and the definition
of
\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}
we immediately obtain

(2.30)

\begin{split}&I_{i,1}\leq L\int_{\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}}|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\,|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\leq 2^{q_{i}}L\int_{\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}}|\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}\,|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad(\text{since $u_{i}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda})$
and $0\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1$})\\
&\qquad\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

-  Estimate of
I_{i,2}
.
First of all, since also the integral

I_{i,2}
is actually performed
on
A_{i,\lambda}
, we can use again estimate
(
2.16
) and assumption
(h_{a})
,
obtaining

I_{i,2}\leq L\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\,|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\leq L\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{q_{i}}\,|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx=:(\star).

From this, since
q_{i}<p_{i}
, using (
2.23
) and by the
Young’s inequality we obtain

(2.31)

\begin{split}&(\star)=L\,\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}\big[(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{q_{i}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{q_{i}}\big]\cdot\big[|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-q_{i}}\big]\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{\frac{2p_{i}}{p_{i}-q_{i}}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\[2.84544pt]
&\qquad(\text{since
$0\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1$ and $p_{i}/(p_{i}-q_{i})>1$})\\
&\qquad\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx\qquad(\text{for every $\delta>0$}).\end{split}

-  Estimate of
J_{i,1}
.
We first observe that, since
0\leq u_{i}\leq M

pointwise in
\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}
, by exploiting assumption
(h_{a})

and the Mean Value theorem, we obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle J_{i,1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}}\big||\nabla u_{i}|^{q_{i}}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{q_{i}}\big|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\,\int_{\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{q_{i}-1}\,|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{\frac{2q_{i}-p_{i}}{2}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{\frac{p_{i}-2}{2}}\,|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{(1)}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{2q_{i}-p_{i}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx.

Finally, using (
2.22
), since

q_{i}\geq\max\{p_{i}-1,1\}
and therefore
q_{i}\geq p_{i}/2
, we conclude that

(2.32)

J_{i,1}\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}\,|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx,

and this estimate holds for every choice of
\delta>0
.

-  Estimate of
J_{i,2}
.
Using once again the fact that

0\leq u_{i}\leq M
in
\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}
, and taking into
account assumption
(h_{a})
, we get

\displaystyle J_{i,2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{q_{i}}\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx=:(\bullet).

By Young’s inequality and
estimate (
2.23
) we obtain

(2.33)

\begin{split}&(\bullet)=C\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}\big[\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{q_{i}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{q_{i}}\big]\cdot\big[w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-q_{i}}\big]\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-q_{i}}}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{(2)}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx\\
&\qquad\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}\,|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx,\end{split}

where we used the fact
{p_{i}}/{(p_{i}-q_{i})}\geq 2
(because
q_{i}\geq p_{i}/2
) and
0\leq u_{i}\leq M
in
\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}
; this estimate holds for every
\delta>0
.

-  Estimate of
F_{i}
.

Since

0\leq\psi_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
, by Young’s inequality we immediately get

(2.34)

\begin{split}F_{i}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}L_{j}\bigg(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{j,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx\bigg)\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{j,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

Thanks to all the above estimates, we can finally complete
the proof of (
2.12
): in fact, by gathering together
(
2.29
)-to-(
2.34
),
from (
2.21
) we infer that

(2.35)

\begin{split}C_{1}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx&\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad+C_{\delta}\varepsilon^{1/p_{i}}+\frac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx\\
&\quad+C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{j,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx,\end{split}

and this estimates holds for every
\delta>0
.
As a consequence, if we choose
\delta
sufficiently small
and if we let
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
with the aid of Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

(2.36)

\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{j,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx,

where
C>0
is a suitable constant depending on
p_{i},q_{i},a_{i},f_{i},\lambda,\Omega,M
.
This, together with the estimate in
(
2.13
), immediately implies
the desired (
2.12
).

Case (ii)
. Let us define the function

A_{i}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}a_{i}(s)\,ds.

Using the family of
cut-off
function
\{\psi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}
defined in the previous case, we now define in a very similar way, for every
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
,
the maps

\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(1)}(x):=\begin{cases}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i}(x))}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}(x)\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}(x),&\text{if $x\in\Omega_{\lambda}$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

and

\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(2)}(x):=\begin{cases}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda}(x))}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}(x)\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}(x),&\text{if $x\in\Omega_{\lambda}$},\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is possible to prove that, see e.g.
[
15
]
,

(i)

\varphi^{(1)}_{i,\varepsilon},\varphi^{(2)}_{i,\varepsilon}\in\mathrm{Lip}({\mathbb{R}}^{N});

(ii)

\operatorname{supp}(\varphi^{(1)}_{i,\varepsilon})\subseteq\Omega_{\lambda}
,
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi^{(2)}_{i,\varepsilon})\subseteq\Omega_{\lambda}
and
\varphi^{(1)}_{i,\varepsilon}\equiv\varphi^{(2)}_{i,\varepsilon}\equiv 0
near
\Gamma_{\lambda}
.

Hence, taking into account properties (i)-(ii) of
\varphi^{(1)}_{i,\varepsilon}
and
\varphi^{(2)}_{i,\varepsilon}
,
a standard density argument allows us to use

\varphi^{(1)}_{i,\varepsilon}
and
\varphi^{(2)}_{i,\varepsilon}
as a test functions respectively in (
1.2
) and (
2.7
). We then subtract the latter to the former getting

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\langle|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i},\nabla\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(1)}\rangle\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\langle|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda},\nabla\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(2)}\rangle\,dx\\
&\qquad\qquad+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}a_{i}(u_{i})|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}}\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(1)}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}a_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}}\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(2)}\,dx\\
&\quad=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}f_{i}(\mathbf{u})\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(1)}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{\lambda})\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(2)}\,dx.\end{split}

Now, we use the explicit expression of both
\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(1)}
and
\varphi_{i,\varepsilon}^{(2)}
to get

\begin{split}&-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}a_{i}(u_{i})e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\rangle\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad+p_{i}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\rangle w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx\\
&+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}a_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i,\lambda},\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\rangle\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\quad-p_{i}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i,\lambda},\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\rangle w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx\\
&+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}a_{i}(u_{i})|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}a_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
=&\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}f_{i}(\mathbf{u})e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx-\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}f_{i}(\mathbf{u}_{\lambda})e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.\end{split}

After a simplification, we add on both sides the term

\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\rangle\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx,

and, in the left hand side, we add and subtract the term

p_{i}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\rangle w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx.

Rearranging the terms, we find

(2.37)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}\langle|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}-|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i},\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\rangle\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\left(e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}-e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}\right)|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\rangle\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle+p_{i}\,\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\left(e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}-e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}\right)|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\langle\nabla u_{i},\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\rangle w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx

\displaystyle+p_{i}\,\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}\langle|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda}-|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i},\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\rangle w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\left(e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}f_{i}(\mathbf{u})-e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}f_{i}(\mathbf{u_{\lambda}})\right)w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.

Arguing similarly to
Case (i)
, see (
2.20
), the left hand side can be estimated from below with

(2.38)

C\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\left(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\right)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.

Indeed, in the set
\Omega_{\lambda}\cap\mathrm{supp}(w_{i,\lambda}^{+})
,
one has
e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}\geq C>0
.

We now focus on the right hand side which we firstly bound from above passing to the absolute values, getting

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\left|e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}-e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}\right||\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-1}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle+p_{i}\,\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\left|e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}-e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}\right||\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-1}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx

\displaystyle+p_{i}\,\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}\left||\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i,\lambda}-|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-2}\nabla u_{i}\right||\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}-1}\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\left(e^{-A_{i}(u_{i})}f_{i}(\mathbf{u})-e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}f_{i}(\mathbf{u_{\lambda}})\right)w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx=:\mathcal{J}_{i,1}+\mathcal{J}_{i,2}+\mathcal{J}_{i,3}+\mathcal{J}_{i,4}.

For the reader’s convenience, we recall that, in this case, we are assuming
2\leq p_{i}\leq N
. We have the following

-  Estimate of
\mathcal{J}_{i,1}
.
Using the lipschitzianity of
t\mapsto e^{-A_{i}(t)}
, we get that there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{i,1}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}-1}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p_{i}}{2}}w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\frac{p_{i}}{2}}\,dx.

Now, by exploiting the weighted Young’s inequality, we obtain that for every
\delta>0
it holds the following

(2.39)

\begin{split}\mathcal{J}_{i,1}&\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\dfrac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(w_{i,\lambda}^{+})^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\dfrac{C}{\delta}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(w_{i,\lambda}^{+})^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\end{split}

where in the last step
C>0
depends also on
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\lambda})}
.

-  Estimate of
\mathcal{J}_{i,2}
.
Using once again the
lipschitzianity of
t\mapsto e^{-A_{i}(t)}
together with the boundedness of
\nabla u
in
\Omega_{\lambda}
, and then exploiting the Hölder’s inequality with exponents
(p_{i},p_{i}/(p_{i}-1))
, we get

(2.40)

\mathcal{J}_{i,2}\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{p_{i}}(w_{i,\lambda}^{+})^{p_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{i}}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}}.

-  Estimate of
\mathcal{J}_{i,3}
.
We notice first that

e^{-A_{i}(u_{i,\lambda})}\leq 1
. Arguing similarly as we did in the computations that led to (
2.25
) and subsequently to
(
2.29
), we deduce:

\mathcal{J}_{i,3}\leq C\delta\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}\big(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|\big)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+C_{\delta}\varepsilon^{1/p_{i}}\quad(\text{for every $\delta>0$}).

-  Estimate of
\mathcal{J}_{i,4}
.
We first consider the function

g_{i}(t):=e^{-A_{i}(t_{i})}f_{i}(t),\quad t\in\mathcal{I}^{m},

which is still a
C^{1}
function satisfying the cooperativity condition

\partial_{t_{k}}g_{i}\geq 0
on
\mathcal{I}^{m}
for every
k\neq i
.
Hence, we can repeat the computations made in (
2.17
) up to (
2.19
) to get

(2.41)

\mathcal{J}_{i,4}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}L_{g_{j}}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}w_{j,\lambda}^{+}\,w_{i,\lambda}^{+}\,\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|w_{j,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx.

Putting everything together, we can conclude as in the previous
Case (i)
. Hence, we obtain the desired (
2.12
) for every
i=1,\ldots,m
.

We conclude this section by proving the following useful lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let assumptions
(h_{\Omega})
-to-
(h_{f})
be in force. Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
be fixed,
1<p_{i}<2
, and let
\lambda\in(\varrho,0)
.
Then, there exists a constant
\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{c}_{i}>0
such that

(2.42)

\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{p_{i}}\,dx\leq\mathbf{c}\ \left(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{i}}{2}}.

Proof.

Let us define the set
\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}:=\Omega_{\lambda}\cap\,\text{supp}(w^{+}_{i,\lambda})
and let
\psi_{\varepsilon}
be the cut-off function defined in (
2.3
).
Using Hölder’s inequality with conjugate exponents
({2}/{(2-p_{i})},{2}/{p_{i}})
,
we obtain

(2.43)

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}}&|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{p_{i}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\ \,dx\\
&=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{\frac{p_{i}(2-p_{i})}{2}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{\frac{p_{i}(p_{i}-2)}{2}}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{p_{i}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\
&\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{2-p_{i}}{p_{i}}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{i}}{2}}.\end{split}

Now we are going to give an estimate to the first term in the right hand side of (
2.43
), i.e. the term

\mathcal{I}:=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx.

In the same spirit of the previous lemma, we split the set
\Omega_{\lambda}
as

\Omega_{\lambda}=\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}\cup\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda},

where

\displaystyle\Omega^{(1)}_{\lambda}=\{x\in\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}\setminus\Gamma_{\lambda}:\,|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}(x)|<2|\nabla u_{i}|\}\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle\qquad\Omega^{(2)}_{\lambda}=\{x\in\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}\setminus\Gamma_{\lambda}:\,|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}(x)|\geq 2|\nabla u_{i}|\}.

Hence

\mathcal{I}=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{(1)}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{(2)}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx=:\mathcal{I}_{1}+\mathcal{I}_{2}.

- Estimate of
\mathcal{I}_{1}
.
Since
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\leq 1
, using
(
2.22
) we immediately get

(2.44)

\mathcal{I}_{1}\leq C\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{(1)}}|\nabla u_{i}|^{p_{i}}\,dx,

- Estimate of
\mathcal{I}_{2}
.
Using (
2.23
), we obtain

(2.45)

\begin{split}\mathcal{I}_{2}&=\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{(2)}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}-2}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\,dx\\

Collecting (

Now, since each

where

\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{p_{i}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{p_{i}}\ \,dx\leq C\left(\int_{\Omega_{\lambda}^{+}}(|\nabla u_{i}|+|\nabla u_{i,\lambda}|)^{p_{i}-2}|\nabla w_{i,\lambda}^{+}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{p_{i}}{2}},

Finally by Fatou’s lemma from (

3.
Proof of Theorem

Thanks to all the results established so far, we are ready to give the

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Our approach relies on a suitable adaptation of the integral version
of the moving plane method.
We consider the set

\Lambda:=\big\{\eta\in(\varrho,0):\,\text{$u_{i}\leq u_{i,\lambda}$
on $\Omega_{\lambda}\setminus R_{\lambda}(\Gamma)$ for all $\lambda\in(\varrho,\eta]$ and for all
$i=1,\ldots,m$}\big\},

and we claim that the following facts hold:

(a)

\Lambda\neq\varnothing
;

(b)

setting
\lambda_{0}:=\sup(\Lambda)

\lambda_{0}=0
.

Proof of (a).
First of all we observe that, since
\Gamma
is compact
and contained in
\Omega\cap\Pi_{0}
,
it is possible to find a small
\tau_{0}>0
such that

\varrho+\tau_{0}<0

R_{\lambda}(\Gamma)\cap\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda}=\varnothing\qquad\text{for every $\lambda\in I_{0}:=(\varrho,\varrho+\tau_{0}]$}.

In particular, for every
\lambda\in I_{0}
we have

\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{\lambda})
.
On the other hand, since both
u_{i}
and
u_{i,\lambda}
are non-negative
(for every

), it is immediate to recognize that

\text{$u_{i}\leq u_{i,\lambda}$ on $\partial\Omega_{\lambda}$}\qquad\text{for every $i=1,\ldots,m$}.

Now, it is possible to start the moving plane procedure using
[

reader’s convenience we state such a result adapted to
our context. The proof is exactly the same and
therefore we skip it.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let assumptions
(h_{a})
and
(h_{f})
be in force, and suppose that

p_{i}>1\quad\text{and}\quad q_{i}\geq\max\{p_{i}-1,1\}\qquad\text{
for every $i=1,\ldots,m$}.

In addition, suppose that

\mathbf{u},\,\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}_{\varrho+\tau_{0}})
. Then,
there exists a number
\delta>0
, depending on

m,p_{i},q_{i},a_{i},f_{i},\|u_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varrho+\tau_{0}})},\|\nabla u_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varrho+\tau_{0}})}
and
\|\nabla\tilde{u}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{\varrho+\tau_{0}})}

with the following property: if
\Omega_{\lambda}\subseteq\Omega_{\varrho+\tau_{0}}

is such that

\text{$u_{i}\leq{u}_{i,\lambda}$ on $\partial\Omega_{\lambda}$}\quad\text{for every $i=1,\ldots,m$},

\Omega_{\lambda}
for every

Therefore, by possibly shrinking
\tau_{0}

u_{i}\leq u_{i,\lambda}
in
\Omega_{\lambda}

for every

Proof of (b).
On account of (a),
\lambda_{0}
is well-defined
and
\lambda_{0}\leq 0
. Arguing by contradiction, we then suppose that

\lambda_{0}<0
, and we prove that there exists some
\tau_{0}>0
such that

(3.1)

\text{$u_{i}\leq u_{i,\lambda}$ on $\Omega_{\lambda}\setminus R_{\lambda}(\Gamma)$}\quad\text{for all $i=1,\ldots,m$ and $\lambda\in(\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}+\tau_{0}]$}.

Since

\lambda_{0}=0.

In order to establish the needed (
3.1

first of all,
since both
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\lambda}

are continuous on
\Omega_{\lambda}\setminus R_{\lambda}(\Gamma)
,
we observe that

(3.2)

\text{$u_{i}\leq u_{i,\lambda_{0}}$ on $\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus R_{\lambda_{0}}(\Gamma)$}\qquad\text{for every $i=1,\ldots,m$}.

We then claim that, as a consequence of

(3.3)

\text{$u_{i}<u_{i,\lambda_{0}}$ on $\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus R_{\lambda_{0}}(\Gamma)$}\qquad\text{for every $i=1,\ldots,m$}.

To prove (
3.3

arbitrarily fix

We first point out that, since

As a consequence, since

in

(3.4)

\text{either $u_{i}\equiv u_{i,\lambda_{0}}$ or $u_{i}<u_{i,\lambda_{0}}$ \quad in $\Omega_{\lambda_{0}}\setminus\Gamma_{\lambda_{0}}$}.

Now we have fully established

To begin with, we arbitrarily fix a compact set

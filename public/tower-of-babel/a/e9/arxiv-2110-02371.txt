1.
Introduction

Given an elliptic curve
E
defined over a number field
K
, the Mordell–Weil group
E(K)
is a finitely generated abelian group. The Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves was initiated by Mazur in
[
13
]
, who studied the growth of the Mordell–Weil ranks of an elliptic curve in certain infinite towers of number fields. Given a prime
p
, let
K_{n}^{(p)}
denote the Galois extension of
K
with
[K_{n}^{(p)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]=p^{n}
which is contained in the infinite cyclotomic extension
K(\mu_{p^{\infty}})
. It was shown by Kato
[
10
]
and Rohlrich
[
21
]
that if
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
K
is any abelian number field, then,
\operatorname{rank}E(K_{n}^{(p)})
is bounded as
n\rightarrow\infty
. The study of such questions led to the development of the Iwasawa theory of elliptic curves, and it was in this context that Mazur studied
control theorems
for Selmer groups. With the development of non-abelian Iwasawa theory, such results have subsequently been extended to more general
p
-adic Lie-extensions, in which the asymptotic growth of Mordell–Weil ranks is studied, see for instance,
[
7
,
12
,
5
,
8
,
18
]
. The study of growth questions in towers is an active area of research and is intimately related to Iwasawa theoretic properties of Selmer groups.

Given the interest in studying questions about the growth of ranks of elliptic curves, it is natural to ask similar questions for curves of higher genus. A curve
X_{/K}
is said to be
nice
if it is smooth, projective and geometrically irreducible. In this context, the celebrated theorem of Faltings
[
6
]
states that if
X_{/K}
is an nice curve of genus
g>1
, then the set of rational points
X(K_{n}^{(p)})
is finite for all
n
. A natural question is to characterize the growth of
\#X(K_{n}^{(p)})
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. It follows from results of Mazur
[
13
]
and Imai
[
9
]
that
\#X(K_{n}^{(p)})
is bounded provided some additional conditions are satisfied (see Theorem
3.4
). This requires that
p\geq 5
, that the Jacobian of
X
has good ordinary reduction at all the primes of
K
that lie above
p
, and the
p
-primary Selmer group of the Jacobian (over
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}
) is cotorsion over the Iwasawa algebra. The last condition is conjectured by Mazur in
loc. cit.
, and is known in the case when the Mordell–Weil rank of the Jacobian over
K
is zero, and its Tate–Shafarevich group over
K
is finite (see Remark
2.5
and Theorem
2.4
). This latter is verified in an explicit example, see Example
4.2
.

We prove more explicit results regarding the growth of points in the cyclotomic tower. We prove an upper bound for the minimum value
m_{0}=m_{0}(p)
such that
X(K_{n}^{(p)})=X(K_{m_{0}}^{(p)})
for all
n>m_{0}
. Using this bound, we prove results about the variation of
m_{0}(p)
as
p\rightarrow\infty
. This relies on explicitly understanding the torsion in the Jacobian of
X
over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. We then consider the case when
\operatorname{Jac}(X)
has Mordell–Weil rank zero and establish an explicit criterion for
X(K_{n}^{(p)})
to be equal to
X(K)
for all
n
, see Theorem
4.3
. This criterion indicates that for
100\%
of primes
p
above which the Jacobian of
X
has good ordinary reduction,
X(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})=X(K)
, in the rank zero setting.

We also obtain a generalization of Imai’s theorem for all pro-
p
extensions, see Theorem
3.13
. The result states that if
A_{/K}
is an abelian variety for which the associated adelic Galois representation has big image, and the
p
-torsion subgroup of
A(K)
is trivial, then the torsion subgroup of
A(K_{\infty})
is finite, for all infinite pro-
p
extensions
K_{\infty}/K
. This result makes no assumption on the reduction-type of
A
at the primes above
p
. According to a theorem of Serre and Pink (see Theorem
2.3
), the big image assumption is satisfied for a large number of abelian varieties. In addition to this, we prove a boundedness result for the torsion subgroup of
A(K_{\infty})
for all pro-
p
extensions
K_{\infty}/K
, as
p\rightarrow\infty
, see Theorem
3.14
. We also have an explicit example to illustrate this, see Example
4.2
. Specializing to the case when
A
is the Jacobian of a curve
X
, the result implies that if the rank of
A
is bounded in the number fields contained in
K_{\infty}
, then
X(K_{\infty})
is finite.

Organization:
Including the introduction, the paper consists of four sections. In section
2

3
, we prove results about the growth of rational points in cyclotomic towers, namely Theorems
3.4
,
3.11
,
3.13
and
3.14
. In section
4
, we establish a criterion for
X(K)=X(K_{n}^{(p)})
for all
n
in the setting when
\operatorname{Jac}(X)
has rank zero over
K
, and the adelic Galois representation has big image, see Theorem
4.3
.

Preliminaries

Throughout,
p
will be a prime number such that
p\geq 5
and
K
a number field. Let
X
be a curve over
K
of genus
g>1
. Assume that
X
is
nice
i.e., smooth, projective and irreducible over
\bar{K}
. Let
A
denote the Jacobian of

and assume that

2.1.
Galois representations and monodromy groups

We introduce the
adelic Galois representation

\widehat{\rho}
attached to
A
. For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we let
A[m]
be the
m
-torsion subgroup of
A(\bar{K})
. At a prime
\ell
, the
\ell
-adic Tate-module is the inverse limit with respect to multiplication by
\ell
maps

\operatorname{T}_{\ell}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}A[\ell^{n}].

On the other hand, the
big Tate-module
is the inverse limit

\operatorname{T}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{m}A[m]=\prod_{\ell}\operatorname{T}_{\ell}(A).

The Galois action on
A[m]
coincides with a representation

\rho_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}).

Let
\widehat{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
be the Galois representation on
\operatorname{T}(A)
, and the image of
\widehat{\rho}
is denoted
G\subset\operatorname{GSp}_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. On the other hand the image of the
\ell
-adic Galois representation
\rho_{\ell^{\infty}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is denoted
G_{\ell}
. The group
G
(resp.
G_{\ell}
) is referred to as the
big
(resp.
\ell
-adic
)
monodromy group
. Note that
\rho_{\ell}
is the mod-
\ell
reduction of
\rho_{\ell^{\infty}}
, denote its image by
\bar{G}_{\ell}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The representation
\widehat{\rho}
is said to have
big image
if
G\cap\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
is a finite index subgroup of
\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Remark 2.2
.

We do
not
impose the big image condition in the main result, however, the hypothesis is used in proving a refined result in the special case when the Jacobian has rank zero, see Theorem
4.3
.

The following result is due to Serre
[
23
,
24
]
and Pink
[
17
]
.

Theorem 2.3

(Serre, Pink)
.

Let
A_{/\mathbb{Q}}
be an abelian variety and assume that
\operatorname{End}(A_{/\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})=\mathbb{Z}
. Then the image of
\widehat{\rho}
contains a finite index subgroup of
\operatorname{GSp}_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
provided
g=1,2
, or
g\geq 3
is not in the set

\left\{\frac{1}{2}(2n)^{k}\mid n>0,k\geq 3\text{ is odd}\right\}\cup\left\{\frac{1}{2}{{2n}\choose{n}}\mid n\geq 3\text{ is odd}\right\}=\{4,10,16,32,\dots\}.

Note that
G
is closed, and if
\widehat{\rho}
has big image, then
G^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=G\cap\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
is an open subgroup of
\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. As a result,
G_{\ell}
contains
\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
for all but finitely many primes
\ell
.

2.2.
Selmer groups and number field towers

The results proven in this subsection shall apply to all abelian varieties
A
with good ordinary reduction at the primes of
K
that lie above
p
, however the case of interest is when
A
is the Jacobian of the curve
X
from the previous subsection. We introduce the main object of interest in Iwasawa theory, namely, the
p
-primary Selmer group over the cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extension. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
set
\mathbb{Q}_{n}^{(p)}
to be the unique extension of
\mathbb{Q}
contained in
\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p^{n+1}})
such that
[\mathbb{Q}_{n}^{(p)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{Q}]=p^{n}
. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}
be the infinite extension obtained by taking the union and set
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=K\cdot\mathbb{Q}_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}
. It is easy to show that
\Gamma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}/K)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
K_{n}^{(p)}
be the extension of
K
contained in
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}
such that
[K_{n}^{(p)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K]=p^{n}
. The tower of number fields

K=K_{0}^{(p)}\subseteq K_{1}^{(p)}\subseteq K_{2}^{(p)}\subseteq\dots\subseteq K_{n}^{(p)}\subseteq\dots

is called the cyclotomic tower.

Throughout, we pick a topological generator
\gamma
of
\Gamma
. The Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda
is defined to be the inverse limit of finite group rings

\Lambda\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varprojlim_{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\operatorname{Gal}(K_{n}^{(p)}/K)].

There is an isomorphism of
\Lambda
with the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
which takes
\gamma
to
x+1
. From here on in, we choose such an isomorphism and identify
\Lambda
with the formal power series ring
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
.

Given a number field
F/K
, let
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F)
be the
p
-primary Selmer group of
A
over
F
. We recall the definition here. Let
S
be the set of primes
v
of
F
such that either
v|p
, or,
A
has bad reduction at
v
. Let
F_{S}
be the maximal algebraic extension of
F
in which all primes
v\nmid S
are unramified. Denote by
F_{v}
the completion of
F
at
v
. The Kummer sequence

0\rightarrow A[p^{n}]\rightarrow A\rightarrow A\rightarrow 0

induces a map in cohomology

H^{1}(F_{v},A[p^{n}])\rightarrow H^{1}(F_{v},A)[p^{n}].

Passing to the direct limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have the following map

H^{1}(F_{v},A[p^{\infty}])\rightarrow H^{1}(F_{v},A)[p^{\infty}].

The Selmer group is defined as follows

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left\{H^{1}(F_{S}/F,A[p^{\infty}])\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}H^{1}(F_{v},A)[p^{\infty}]\right\},

where the above restriction map factors through
\bigoplus_{v\in S}H^{1}(F_{v},A[p^{\infty}])
.

Let
\Sha(A/F)
be the
Tate-Shafarevich group
, defined as follows

\Sha(A/F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{H^{1}(\bar{F}/F,A(\bar{F}))\rightarrow\prod_{l}H^{1}(\bar{F}_{v}/F_{v},A(\bar{F}_{v}))\right\}.

The Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F)
fits into a short exact sequence

(2.1)

0\rightarrow A(F)\otimes\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/F)\rightarrow\Sha(E/F)[p^{\infty}]\rightarrow 0.

The Selmer group over
K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}
is taken as the direct limit over restriction maps

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\varinjlim_{n}\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{n}^{(p)}).

It is well known that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
is a cofinitely generated module over the Iwasawa-algebra
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
, i.e., its Pontryagin dual

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Hom}\left(\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}),\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right)

is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K)
is finite, i.e.,
\operatorname{rank}A(K)=0
and
\Sha(E/K)[p^{\infty}]
is finite. Then, the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
is cotorsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
.

Proof.

The statement follows from results in
[
3
]
and the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 2.8]
. In fact, the argument in the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 2.8]
generalizes verbatim to abelian varieties with good ordinary reduction at primes above
p
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

More generally, it was conjectured by Mazur in
[
13
]
that the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
is always cotorsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
.

2.3.

We now introduce Iwasawa-invariants associated to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
-modules. Let
M
be a cofinitely generated cotorsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
-module, i.e., the Pontryagin-dual

M^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p})

is a finitely generated and torsion
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
-module. Recall that a polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
is said to be
distinguished
if it is a monic polynomial whose non-leading coefficients are all divisible by
p
. Note that all height
1
prime ideals of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
are principal ideals
(a)
, where
a=p
or
a=f(x)
, where
f(x)
is an irreducible distinguished polynomial.
According to the structure theorem for
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
-modules (see
[
25
, Theorem 13.12]
),
M^{\vee}
is pseudo-isomorphic to a finite direct sum of cyclic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
-modules, i.e., there is a map

M^{\vee}\longrightarrow\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket/(p^{\mu_{i}})\right)\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{t}\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket/(f_{j}(x))\right)

with finite kernel and cokernel.
Here,
\mu_{i}>0
and
f_{j}(x)
is a distinguished polynomial.
The
characteristic ideal
of
M^{\vee}
is generated by the element

(2.2)

f_{M}^{(p)}(x)=f_{M}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=p^{\sum_{i}\mu_{i}}\prod_{j}f_{j}(x),

which is referred to as the
characteristic element
associated to
M
.
The
\mu
-invariant of
M
is defined as the power of
p
in
f_{M}(x)
.
More precisely,

\mu(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{s}\mu_{i}&\textrm{ if }s>0\\
0&\textrm{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

The
\lambda
-invariant of
M
is the degree of the characteristic element, i.e.,

\lambda(M)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{s}\deg f_{i}&\textrm{ if }s>0\\
0&\textrm{ if }s=0.\end{cases}

Assuming that
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
is cotorsion as a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
-module, set
\mu(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
and
\lambda(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
to be the
\mu
and
\lambda
-invariants of the Selmer group
\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
respectively.

3.
Bounding the growth of rational points in the cyclotomic tower

Recall that
X
is a nice curve over
K
and
A
is the Jacobian of
A
. Let
p
be an odd prime. We assume throughout this section that

•

A
has good ordinary reduction at the primes above
p
,

•

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
is cotorsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
(see Theorem
2.4
and Remark
2.5
).

3.1.
Mordell’s conjecture over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions

First, we recall the well–known result of Mazur, see
[
13
]
.

Theorem 3.1

(Mazur)
.

There exists
n_{0}
such that
\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n}^{(p)})=\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n_{0}}^{(p)})
for all
n>n_{0}
. Furthermore,
\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n}^{(p)})
is bounded above by the
\lambda
-invariant
\lambda(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
.

In fact, Mazur showed that the entire Mordell–Weil group
A(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
is finitely generated when
A
is either an elliptic curve or an abelian variety with complex–multiplication. The above result crucially requires that the assumptions on
A
made at the start of this section.

Definition 3.2
.

Denote by
n_{0}=n_{0}(p)
the minimum value such that

\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n}^{(p)})=\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n_{0}}^{(p)})

for all
n>n_{0}
.

Note that according to Mazur’s result, if
\operatorname{rank}A(K)=\lambda(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
, then,
n_{0}(p)=0
, i.e.,

\operatorname{rank}A(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})=\operatorname{rank}A(K).

We also recall the result of Imai, see the main theorem and last few lines of
[
9
]
.

Theorem 3.3

(Imai)
.

Let
A
be as above, then the torsion subgroup of
A(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
is finite.

For ease of notation, set
\alpha(p)
to denote the order of the torsion subgroup of
A(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
X
be a nice curve of genus
g>1
defined over a number field
K
and
A
let be the Jacobian of
X
. Assume that the following conditions hold:

(1)

A
has good ordinary reduction at the primes above
p
,

(2)

\operatorname{Sel}_{p^{\infty}}(A/K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
is cotorsion over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
.

Then,
X\left(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}\right)
is finite. Furthermore, assume that
X(K)\neq\emptyset
, and let
m_{0}
be the minimum integer such that
X(K_{n}^{(p)})=X(K_{m_{0}}^{(p)})
for all
n>m_{0}
. Then, we have that
m_{0}\leq n_{0}+\lfloor\operatorname{log}_{p}\alpha(p)\rfloor
, where
n_{0}
is defined above (see Definition
3.2
).

Proof.

Recall that
n_{0}
is minimal such that

\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n}^{(p)})=\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n_{0}}^{(p)})

for all
n>n_{0}
. Assume without loss of generality that for some
n>n_{0}
,

A(K_{n}^{(p)})\neq A(K_{n_{0}}^{(p)}),

and let
Q\in A(K_{n}^{(p)})
be such that
Q\notin A(K_{n_{0}}^{(p)})
. Let
n^{\prime}
be such that
Q\in A(K_{n^{\prime}}^{(p)})
and
Q\notin A(K_{n^{\prime}-1})
, we have that
n_{0}<n^{\prime}\leq n
. Since

\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n^{\prime}}^{(p)})=\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n_{0}}^{(p)})

it follows that there is an integer
N>0
such that
NQ\in A(K_{n_{0}}^{(p)})
.

For
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K_{n^{\prime}}^{(p)}/K_{n_{0}}^{(p)})
, the point
P_{\sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=Q-\sigma(Q)
is an
N
-torsion point in
A(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})
. Moreover, as
\sigma
ranges over
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{n^{\prime}}^{(p)}/K_{n_{0}}^{(p)})
the points
\sigma(Q)
are all distinct, since it is assumed that
Q\notin A(K_{n^{\prime}-1})
. Hence, the points
P_{\sigma}
are all distinct points in
A(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})_{\operatorname{tors}}
. According to Imai’s theorem,
A(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})_{\operatorname{tors}}
is finite. The number of points
P_{\sigma}
produced is
[K_{n^{\prime}}^{(p)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K_{n_{0}}^{(p)}]=p^{n^{\prime}-n_{0}}
.

Hence, we find that
p^{n^{\prime}-n_{0}}\leq\alpha(p)
, i.e.,

n^{\prime}\leq n_{0}+\lfloor\log_{p}\alpha(p)\rfloor.

Therefore, this shows that
A(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})=A(K_{m_{0}}^{(p)})
, where
m_{0}\leq n_{0}+\lfloor\log_{p}\alpha(p)\rfloor
.

Assume first that
X(K)\neq\emptyset
. Given a point
P_{0}\in X(K)
, consider the standard embedding
X\hookrightarrow A
mapping a point
P
to
[P]\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=P_{0}-P
. Suppose that
P\in X(K_{n}^{(p)})
, then, we know that
[P]=P_{0}-P\in A(K_{m_{0}}^{(p)})
. Hence,
P
is defined over
K_{m_{0}}^{(p)}
, this shows that
X(K_{n}^{(p)})\subseteq X(K_{m_{0}}^{(p)})
, and hence,

X(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)})=X(K_{m_{0}}^{(p)}).

It remains to show that
X\left(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}\right)
is finite when
X(K)=\emptyset
. Without loss of generality,
X\left(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}\right)\neq\emptyset
, and thus,
X(K_{N}^{(p)})\neq\emptyset
for some value of
N
. Replace
K
with
K_{N}^{(p)}
and the above argument shows that
X\left(K_{\operatorname{cyc}}^{(p)}\right)
is finite.
∎

Thus the Mordell conjecture over cyclotomic
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions follows from results of Mazur and Imai. However, the above result is more explicit, as it gives more precise information about growth. We note here that
A
is an abelian variety of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
-type with supersingular reduction at the primes above
p
, it is shown by Lei and Ponsinet (see
[

]

3.2.
Torsion over pro-
p
extensions

One would like to better understand the quantity
m_{0}
from the statement of Theorem
3.4
. We shall prove in section
4
that
m_{0}=0
under some additional conditions. The result of Imai shows that the torsion subgroup of

is finite. In this section, we shall obtain more precise information about this group. As a result, we show that if
\widehat{\rho}
has big image, then,
\alpha(p)
is bounded as
p\rightarrow\infty
. Recall that
\bar{G}_{\ell}
is the image of the mod-
\ell
residual representation on
A[\ell]
.

Throughout, consider a Galois extension
K_{\infty}
of
K
and assume that the Galois group
G=\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\infty}/K)
is pro-
p
. The lower central
p
-series of

is recursively defined as follows:

G_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=G\text{ and }G_{n+1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=G_{n}^{p}[G_{n},G].

Set
K_{n}
to be
K^{G_{n}}
, observe that
\operatorname{Gal}(K_{n+1}/K_{n})\simeq\left(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\right)^{d_{n}}
for some integer

.

The following result shows that if
\bar{G}_{\ell}
is suitably large, then

.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\ell
be a prime such that

(1)

\bar{H}_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bar{G}_{\ell}\cap\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
does not have any (non-trivial) abelian
p
-quotients,

(2)

A[\ell]^{\bar{H}_{\ell}}=0
,

then,
A(K_{\infty})[\ell^{\infty}]=0
.

Proof.

Let
\rho_{\ell}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)\rightarrow\bar{G}_{\ell}
denote the mod-
\ell
residual representation and set

U\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho_{\ell}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K_{\infty})\right)\subseteq\bar{G}_{\ell}.

Since
\bar{H}_{\ell}
has no
p
-abelian quotients,
\bar{H}_{\ell}\cap U=\bar{H}_{\ell}
. Thus,
U
contains
\bar{H}_{\ell}
, and hence,

A(K_{\infty})[\ell]=A[\ell]^{U}=0.

Since
A(K_{\infty})[\ell]=0
, it follows that
A(K_{\infty})[\ell^{\infty}]=0
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\ell
be a prime such that
\bar{G}_{\ell}
contains
\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
. Assume that either
g>2
or
\ell>2
, then,
A(K_{\infty})[\ell^{\infty}]=0
.

Proof.

If either
g>2
or
\ell>2
, then,
\operatorname{PSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is simple (see
[
16
, Theorem 3.4.1]
). Since
p
is odd, the result follows from Lemma
3.5
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

Suppose that
\widehat{\rho}
has big image, then,
\bar{G}_{\ell}
contains
\operatorname{Sp}_{2g}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
for all but a finite exceptional set of primes
\Sigma
. The above result implies that
A(K_{\infty})[\ell^{\infty}]=0
outside the exceptional set.

The next result applies to all primes
\ell\neq p
and shows that
\#A(K_{\infty})[\ell^{\infty}]

is bounded independent of
p
and the extension

.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\ell
be any prime such that
\ell\neq p
and
L
be the field
K(A[\ell])
. Then,
A(K_{\infty})[\ell^{\infty}]\subseteq A(L)[\ell^{\infty}]

Proof.

Let
L(m)
be the field

, which is the field fixed by the kernel of the Galois representation

\varrho_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\rho_{\ell^{m}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/K)\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbb{Z}).

Given an extension
\mathcal{K}/K
, set
H_{m,\mathcal{K}}
to denote
\varrho_{m}\left(\operatorname{Gal}(\bar{K}/\mathcal{K})\right)
. Note that by construction,

(3.1)

A(\mathcal{K})[\ell^{m}]=A[\ell^{m}]^{H_{m,\mathcal{K}}}.

Also note that since
L\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=K(A[\ell])
,

H_{m,L}=\left(\operatorname{image}\varrho_{m}\right)\cap\mathcal{U}_{m},

where

\mathcal{U}_{m}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{ker}\left(\operatorname{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{m}\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\operatorname{GSp}_{2g}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})\right).

Observe that
\mathcal{U}_{m}
is pro-
\ell
, and hence so is

. On the other, by construction

induces an isomorphism

, hence,

is a pro-
\ell
extension. On the other hand,
L_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=L\cdot K_{\infty}
is a pro-
p
extension of

, and since

, we have that
L_{\infty}\cap L(m)=L
. Therefore, we have that

H_{m,L}=\operatorname{Gal}(L(m)/L)=\operatorname{Gal}(L(m)/L_{\infty}\cap L(m))=H_{m,L_{\infty}},

and as a consequence of (

A(L)[\ell^{m}]=A(L_{\infty})[\ell^{m}],

and thus, taking the union of all

-torsion,

A(L)[\ell^{\infty}]=A(L_{\infty})[\ell^{\infty}].

This proves the result.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose that

, then, we have that
A(K_{\infty})[p^{\infty}]=0

Proof.

For any value of

and

is a finite

Let

4.
The special case when the Jacobian has rank zero

In this section, we assume that

for all

4.1.

to equal

Note that when

, the Selmer group

isomorphic to the
p
-primary part of the Tate-Shafarevich group

2.4
that the Selmer group

\mathbb{Z}_{p}\llbracket x\rrbracket
. Assume further that
A

According to Mazur’s result,

, then,
\operatorname{rank}A(K_{n}^{(p)})=0
for all

. Let

be the characteristic element of

be the absolute value, normalized by

the residue field at

-points of the reduced abelian variety

(at

1
Introduction

Neutrino oscillations are transitions in-flight between the flavor neutrinos, caused by non-zero neutrino masses and neutrino mixing.
In the 3-flavor paradigm, the flavor eigenstates (
\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau}
) are linear combinations of three mass eigenstates (
\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3}
),

\displaystyle|\nu_{\alpha}\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{3}U^{*}_{\alpha i}|\nu_{i}\rangle,\qquad\qquad\alpha=e,\mu,\tau,

(1)

where
U
is the unitary mixing matrix.
The probabilities of flavor transitions will depend on: the elements of the mixing matrix, parameterized by three angles (
\theta_{12},\theta_{13},\theta_{23}
) and one phase (
\delta_{CP}
); the squared differences between mass eigenvalues (
\Delta m^{2}_{21},\Delta m^{2}_{32}
); the energy of the neutrino (
E
), and the distance it traveled (
L
).

Evidence of neutrino oscillations has been onserved in solar, atmospheric, reactor and accelerator experiments. The different types of neutrino sources and experimental setups have characteristic values of
L
and
E_{\nu}
, which set the ranges of
|\Delta m^{2}|
and the kind of oscillations to which they are sensitive
[
1
]
.
In particular, long-baseline accelerator neutrino experiments have
L/E\sim\text{500 GeV/km}
, and are sensitive to the atmospheric-sector oscillations with a
\Delta m^{2}\sim{2.5\times 10^{-3}}{\text{ eV}^{2}}
.
With a primordially muon neutrino (or antineutrino) beam, the combined measurements of long-baseline disappearance (
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{\mu}
and
\mathrel{\mathop{\nu}\limits^{\vbox to-0.60275pt{\kern-2.5pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\textbf{--}$}\vss}}}_{\mu}\to\mathrel{\mathop{\nu}\limits^{\vbox to-0.60275pt{\kern-2.5pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\textbf{--}$}\vss}}}_{\mu}
) and appearance (
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e}
and
\mathrel{\mathop{\nu}\limits^{\vbox to-0.60275pt{\kern-2.5pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\textbf{--}$}\vss}}}_{\mu}\to\mathrel{\mathop{\nu}\limits^{\vbox to-0.60275pt{\kern-2.5pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\textbf{--}$}\vss}}}_{e}
) allow the estimation of
\theta_{23}
and
\Delta m^{2}_{32}
, and the determination of the neutrino mass ordering (
\text{sign}(\Delta m^{2}_{32})
), the octant of
\theta_{23}
, and charge-parity (CP) violation in the neutrino sector.

2
The NOvA experiment

NOvA consists of two finely-segmented liquid-scintillator detectors operating 14.6 mrad off-axis from Fermilab’s NuMI muon neutrino (or antineutrino) beam
[
2
]
. The detectors comprise PVC cells filled with a liquid scintillator, arranged in planes that alternate between horizontal and vertical directions to allow 3D reconstruction, as shown in Fig.
1
.
The near detector (ND) has 214 of such planes, and samples the
beam 1 km from the source. The far detector (FD) has 896 planes, and observes
the oscillated beam 810 km downstream, near Ash River, MN.

Figure 1:
Schematic of the NOvA detectors showing the alternating
plane orientations. The combination of top and side views
offers 3D reconstruction of the
particle trajectory
[
5
]
.

The data is collected in the detectors in the form of individual hits, which get clustered based on proximity in time and space to construct event candidates.
These clusters are then processed to estimate the location of the interaction vertex, its direction, and other reconstructed quantities. Further, a convolutional neural network is used to classify the neutrino event candidates into
\nu_{e}
CC,
\nu_{\mu}

CC
, NC, or cosmogenic backgrounds. Figure
2
shows characteristic topologies of CC and NC events in the detectors.

Figure 2:
Simulated neutrino interactions in the NOvA detectors:
\nu_{\mu}

CC
(top),
\nu_{e}
CC (middle), and NC (bottom)
[
4
]
.

The NOvA detectors are tracking calorimeters, and both characteristics are used to estimate the energy of the neutrino candidates. For
\nu_{\mu}

CC
events, the energy of the muon is estimated using the track length (
\sim
4% resolution), and the energy of the hadronic system is estimated using calorimetry (
\sim
30% resolution). The
\nu_{e}
CC energy is a function of the electromagnetic energy (
\sim
10% resolution)
and the hadronic energy, where both use calorimetry.

3
The NOvA oscillation analysis

Four channels are relevant to the estimation of neutrino oscillation parameters with NOvA:
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{\mu}
,
\mathrel{\mathop{\nu}\limits^{\vbox to-0.60275pt{\kern-2.5pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\textbf{--}$}\vss}}}_{\mu}\to\mathrel{\mathop{\nu}\limits^{\vbox to-0.60275pt{\kern-2.5pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\textbf{--}$}\vss}}}_{\mu}
,
\nu_{\mu}\to\nu_{e}
, and
\mathrel{\mathop{\nu}\limits^{\vbox to-0.60275pt{\kern-2.5pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\textbf{--}$}\vss}}}_{\mu}\to\mathrel{\mathop{\nu}\limits^{\vbox to-0.60275pt{\kern-2.5pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\textbf{--}$}\vss}}}_{e}

\nu_{\mu}

CC
ND samples, for neutrino or antineutrino beam mode separately. The
\nu_{e}
CC ND samples are used to correct the background predictions for the appearance channel.

The best-fit parameters are obtained by minimizing a Poisson negative log-likelihood ratio between oscillated FD predictions and the observed spectra
[
3
]
.
The fit includes three parameters that are allowed to vary freely (
\Delta m^{2}_{32},\sin^{2}\theta_{23},\delta_{CP}
); 67 systematic uncertainties; and constraints from solar and reactor experiments (
\Delta m^{2}_{21}=7.53\times 10^{-5}\penalty\ {\rm eV}^{2}
,
\sin^{2}\theta_{12}=0.307
, and
\sin^{2}\theta_{13}=0.0210\pm 0.0011
). The resulting best-fit oscillation parameters (and their
1\sigma
allowed ranges) are:

\displaystyle\Delta m^{2}_{32}

\displaystyle=(+2.41\pm 0.07)\times 10^{-3}\text{eV}^{2},

(2)

\displaystyle\sin^{2}\theta_{23}

\displaystyle=0.57^{+0.03}_{-0.04},

\displaystyle\delta_{CP}

\displaystyle=(0.82^{+0.27}_{-0.87})\pi.

The data disfavor combinations of parameters that lead to a strong asymmetry in the rate of
\nu_{e}
vs
\mathrel{\mathop{\nu}\limits^{\vbox to-0.60275pt{\kern-2.5pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle\textbf{--}$}\vss}}}_{e}
appearance:
\delta_{\rm{CP}}=\pi/2
is excluded at more than 3
\sigma
in the inverted mass ordering, and
\delta_{\rm{CP}}=3\pi/2
in the normal ordering is disfavored at 2
\sigma
confidence. Considering all permutations of the mass ordering and the octant, degeneracies are such that all values of
\delta_{\rm{CP}}
are compatible with the data, with no preference of CP conservation over CP violation
[
3
]
.

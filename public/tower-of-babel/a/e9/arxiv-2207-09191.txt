\mathcal{N}=4
SYM
Drukker:2000rr

one can easily compute the

W_{SO(N)}

\displaystyle W_{SO(N)}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2\sqrt{2}N}{\sqrt{\lambda^{\prime}}}I_{1}(\sqrt{\lambda^{\prime}/2})-\frac{1}{2}I_{0}(\sqrt{\lambda^{\prime}/2})+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}I_{2}(\sqrt{\lambda^{\prime}/2})}{96N}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime 3/2}I_{3}(\sqrt{\lambda^{\prime}/2})}{384\sqrt{2}N^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-3}).

Plugging this relation into (

\displaystyle W_{SO(N)}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2g_{s}}\left[\widehat{I}_{1}+\frac{\widehat{I}_{2}}{6}g_{s}^{2}\right]-\frac{g_{s}}{4}\widehat{I}_{1}+\mathcal{O}(g_{s}^{3}).

4
Conclusions and outlook

In this paper, we have studied the
1/N_{5}
expansion of the
volume of the gauge group
G

and the
1/2
BPS Wilson loops in the fundamental representation of
G
in

\mathcal{N}=4
SYM with
G=SO(N)
or
G=Sp(N)
.
Due to the shift of
N
coming from the RR charge of O3-plane (
2
),
the
1/N_{5}
expansion with fixed ’t Hooft parameter
\lambda=8g_{s}N_{5}

is different from the ordinary
1/N
expansion. We found that the
1/N_{5}
expansion
looks more “closed string like”
than the ordinary
1/N
expansion.
For instance, we found that the
1/N_{5}
expansion
of the volume of
G
contains only the even powers of
1/N_{5}
, except
for the first term
\mp N_{5}\log 2
in (
8
).
This is different from the
1/N_{\text{top}}
expansion
of
\text{vol}(G)
in topological string

Ooguri:2002gx
.
It would be interesting to find a mathematical meaning, if any,
of the coefficient
of
N_{5}^{2-2g}
in (
10
)
as a certain quantity on the moduli space of Riemann surfaces of genus
g
.

We have also studied the
1/N_{5}
expansion of the
1/2
BPS Wilson loop

W_{G}

in the fundamental representation of
G=SO(N)
or
G=Sp(N)
.
We found that
W_{G}
is decomposed as (
36
).
Except for the constant term
\pm 1/2
in (
36
),

W_{U(2N_{5})}
and
W_{T}
are both expanded
in
1/N_{5}
with only odd powers of
1/N_{5}
.
It is tempting to speculate that
W_{U(2N_{5})}
and
W_{T}

correspond to the untwisted and the twisted sector of bulk type IIB string theory
on
AdS_{5}\times\mathbb{RP}^{5}
.
It would be interesting to understand the bulk gravitational interpretation of the
decomposition (
36
) more clearly.

It would be interesting to extend our analysis to more general
observables in
\mathcal{N}=4
SYM with the gauge group
SO(N)
or
Sp(N)
,
such as an integrated four-point correlator
Dorigoni:2022zcr
and
the
1/2
BPS Wilson loop in the spinor representation of
SO(N)

Fiol:2014fla
;
Giombi:2020kvo
, to name a few.
We leave this as an interesting future problem.

Appendix A
Proof of (
17
)

In this appendix, we present a proof of the relation
(
17
). For definiteness we consider
W_{SO(2n)}
.
To this end, we can use the fact that
the Laguerre polynomial is written as a matrix element of
the harmonic oscillator (see e.g.
Okuyama:2018yep
)

\displaystyle\langle i|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|j\rangle=\langle j|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|i\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{i!}{j!}}g_{s}^{\frac{j-i}{2}}L_{i}^{(j-i)}(-g_{s}),

(43)

where

\displaystyle{[}a,a^{\dagger}{]}=1,\quad a|0\rangle=0,\quad|k\rangle=\frac{(a^{\dagger})^{k}}{\sqrt{k!}}|0\rangle.

(44)

Then
W_{SO(2n)}
in (
15
) is written as

\displaystyle W_{SO(2n)}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\langle 2i|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|2i\rangle

(45)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\bigl[1+(-1)^{k}\bigr]\langle k|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|k\rangle.

Thus
W_{SO(2n)}
is naturally decomposed as

\displaystyle W_{SO(2n)}=W_{SO(2n)}^{+}+W_{SO(2n)}^{-},

(46)

where

\displaystyle W_{SO(2n)}^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\langle k|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|k\rangle,

(47)

\displaystyle W_{SO(2n)}^{-}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}(-1)^{k}\langle k|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|k\rangle.

Note that
W_{SO(2n)}^{+}
is equal to the Wilson loop of

U(2n)

\mathcal{N}=4
SYM
Drukker:2000rr
.
The sum over
k
in
W_{SO(2n)}^{+}

can be simplified as

\displaystyle\sqrt{g_{s}}W_{SO(2n)}^{+}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\langle k|\bigl[a,e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}\bigl]|k\rangle

(48)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\Bigl[\sqrt{k+1}\langle k+1|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|k\rangle-\sqrt{k}\langle k|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|k-1\rangle\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2n}\langle 2n|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|2n-1\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{g_{s}}e^{\frac{1}{2}g_{s}}L_{2n-1}^{(1)}(-g_{s}).

In the last step we used (
43
).
Thus we find

\displaystyle W_{SO(2n)}^{+}=W_{U(2n)}=e^{\frac{1}{2}g_{s}}L_{2n-1}^{(1)}(-g_{s}),

(49)

which agrees with the known result of
W_{U(2n)}
in

Drukker:2000rr
.

Next, let us consider the
g_{s}
-derivative of
W_{SO(2n)}^{-}

\displaystyle\partial_{g_{s}}W_{SO(2n)}^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{g_{s}}}\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}(-1)^{k}\langle k|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}(a+a^{\dagger})|k\rangle

(50)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{g}}\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}(-1)^{k}\Bigl[\sqrt{k}\langle k|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|k-1\rangle+\sqrt{k+1}\langle k|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|k+1\rangle\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{g_{s}}}\sum_{k=0}^{2n-1}\Bigl[(-1)^{k}\sqrt{k}\langle k|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|k-1\rangle-(-1)^{k+1}\sqrt{k+1}\langle k+1|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|k\rangle\Bigr]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{g_{s}}}\sqrt{2n}\langle 2n|e^{\sqrt{g_{s}}(a+a^{\dagger})}|2n-1\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{1}{2}g_{s}}L_{2n-1}^{(1)}(-g_{s}).

Finally, we find

\displaystyle\partial_{g_{s}}W_{SO(2n)}

\displaystyle=\partial_{g_{s}}W_{SO(2n)}^{+}+\partial_{g_{s}}W_{SO(2n)}^{-}

(51)

\displaystyle=\partial_{g_{s}}\Bigl[e^{\frac{1}{2}g_{s}}L_{2n-1}^{(1)}(-g_{s})\Bigr]-\frac{1}{2}e^{\frac{1}{2}g_{s}}L_{2n-1}^{(1)}(-g_{s})

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{1}{2}g_{s}}L_{2n-2}^{(2)}(-g_{s}).

This proves (
17
) for the
SO(2n)
case.

SO(2n+1)
and
Sp(N)
cases can be proved in a similar manner.

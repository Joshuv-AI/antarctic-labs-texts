Abstract
. Leclerc [ Lec16 ] constructed a conjectural cluster structure on Richardson varieties in simply laced types using cluster categories. We show that in type A, his conjectural cluster structure is in fact a cluster structure. We do this by comparing Leclerc’s construction with another cluster structure on type A Richardson varieties due to Ingermanson [ Ing19 ] . Ingermanson’s construction uses the combinatorics of wiring diagrams and the Deodhar stratification. Though the two cluster structures are defined very differently, we show that the quivers coincide and clusters are related by the twist map for Richardson varieties, recently defined by Galashin–Lam [ GL22 ] .



1.
Introduction

In this article, we consider cluster structures on open Richardson varieties
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
in the complete flag variety
\mathcal{F}\ell_{n}
. For
v\leq w\in S_{n}
, the open Richardson variety
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
is the intersection of the Schubert cell
C_{w}
with the opposite Schubert cell
C^{v}
, and is smooth, affine, and irreducible. Open Richardson varieties are related to the geometric interpretation of Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials
[
Deo85
,
KL79
,
KL80
]
; open Richardson varieties also arise in total positivity for
\mathcal{F}\ell_{n}

[
Lus94
,
Rie99
]
. Special cases include
(open) positroid varieties

[
KLS13
,
Pos06
]
, which are Richardson varieties
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
where
w
has a single descent.

A cluster structure on
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
is an identification of the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
with a cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma)
. Cluster algebras were introduced by Fomin–Zelevinsky
[
FZ02
]
to provide an algebraic and combinatorial framework for Lusztig’s dual canonical bases and total positivity
[
Lus90
,
Lus94
]
. Cluster algebras have appeared in a wide range of fields, including Teichmüller theory
[
FG06
]
, mirror symmetry
[
GHKK18
]
, Poisson geometry
[
GSV10
]
, symplectic geometry
[
STWZ19
]
, knot theory
[
FPST22
]
, and scattering amplitudes in high energy physics
[
GGS+14
]
. One major direction of research is to understand when varieties naturally ocurring in representation theory have a cluster structure; examples of varieties with cluster structures include Grassmannians
[
Sco06
]
, double Bruhat cells in semisimple Lie groups
[
BFZ05
]
, and unipotent cells in Kac-Moody groups
[
GLS11
]
.

Cluster algebras are commutative rings with a distinguished set of generators called
cluster variables
, which are grouped together into
clusters
. A cluster can be
mutated
into another cluster, and any two clusters are related by a sequence of mutations. The information of all mutations of a cluster is encoded in a
quiver
, i.e. a directed graph. A cluster and its quiver together form a
seed
.

Leclerc
[
Lec16
]
used categorification to construct a conjectural cluster structure

\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
. In particular, he defined a cluster category inside the module category of a preprojective algebra and identified certain cluster tilting objects in this category. Each cluster tiliting object naturally gives rise to a seed
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}=(\mathbf{B}_{v,\mathbf{w}},Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec})
, where the cluster
\mathbf{B}_{v,\mathbf{w}}
is obtained via a cluster character map and the quiver
Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}

Leclerc showed that the cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec})
is a subring of
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
.

Leclerc conjectured that
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec})
is equal to
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
, and showed that equality holds in some special cases. One of the obstacles in proving Leclerc’s conjecture is the difficulty in computing the quiver
Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}
in general. In
[
SSBW19
]
, the authors together with L. Williams showed that for certain positroid varieties, the quiver

[
GL19

extended this result to all positroid varieties, and used this to show Leclerc’s conjecture in the positroid variety case.

Our main result is a proof of Leclerc’s conjecture in type A.

Theorem A
.

Let
v\leq w
and let
\mathbf{w}
be a reduced expression for
w
. Then

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]=\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}).

Moreover, the cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec})
does not depend on the choice of
\mathbf{w}
.

We prove
A
by comparing
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec})
with another cluster structure on
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
, defined by Ingermanson
[
Ing19
]
. Ingermanson constructed a seed
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing}=(\mathbf{A}_{v,\mathbf{w}},Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})
using the wiring diagram of a
unipeak
expression for
w
. The cluster variables
\mathbf{A}_{v,\mathbf{w}}
are particular factors of the
chamber minors
of Marsh–Rietsch
[
MR04
]
, and the quiver
Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing}
can be read off from the wiring diagram. In her construction, determining the factorization of chamber minors into cluster variables is quite involved, but once this has been done, it is easy to write down the quiver. Ingermanson showed that the upper cluster algebra
\mathcal{U}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})
is equal to
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
; recent results of
[
GLSBS22

imply that Ingermanson’s quiver is locally acyclic, and so
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})=\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]

We show the following relationship between Ingermanson’s seed and Leclerc’s.

Theorem B
.

Let
v\leq w

\mathbf{w}
be a unipeak expression for
w

\tau_{v,w}

\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}

(\mathbf{A}_{v,\mathbf{w}},Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})=(\mathbf{B}_{v,\mathbf{w}}\circ\tau_{v,w},Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}).

We separately show that all of the seeds Leclerc defines are related by mutations. As a corollary of these result, we show that the positive part of
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
defined by Leclerc’s cluster structure agrees with the totally positive part
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Lus}
defined by Lusztig
[
Lus94
]
.

Theorem C
.

Let
v\leq w
. The subset

\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Lec}:=\{F\in\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}:\text{ all cluster variables in }\mathcal{A}{(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec})}\text{ are positive on }F\}

coincides with
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Lus}
.

B
has the effect of simplifying the definitions of both Leclerc’s and Ingermanson’s seeds. We obtain a much more straightforward method to factor chamber minors into Ingermanson’s cluster variables, and an elementary method to draw Leclerc’s quiver from a wiring diagram. This alternate description of Leclerc’s quiver is in the same vein as the descriptions in the positroid variety case given by
[
SSBW19
,
GL19
]
. As such, we hope our results make cluster structures on Richardson varieties more accesible.

Our results show that Leclerc’s and Ingermanson’s cluster structure on
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
are related by the twist map for Richardsons, which generalizes the twist map for positroid varieties
[
MS17
]
. In the positroid variety case, the twist map is conjectured to be a
quasi-cluster transformation

[
Fra16
]
; i.e. the twist map is conjectured to be a sequence of mutations followed by rescaling by Laurent monomials in frozens. We make the same conjecture in the Richardson variety case.

Conjecture 1.1
.

The twist map
\tau_{v,w}:\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}\to\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
is a quasi-cluster transformation. As a result, any cluster of
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})
is related to any cluster of
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec})
by a sequence of mutations and rescaling by Laurent monomials in frozens.

We briefly discuss related work on Richardson variety cluster structures, which has been a very active topic of late. In types ADE, Ménard
[
M2́2
]
constructed another cluster tilting object for each reduced word
\mathbf{w}
of
w
; his construction has the advantage that the quiver is constructed algorithmically. Cao–Keller
[
CK22
]
recently showed that, if
\Sigma^{M}_{v,\mathbf{w}}
is the seed obtained from Ménard’s cluster tilting object via the cluster character map, then
\mathcal{U}(\Sigma^{M}_{v,\mathbf{w}})=\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
(again, in types ADE). The relation between
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{M}
and
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}
is as yet unknown; the quivers are conjectured to be mutation-equivalent. In a separate direction, Casals–Gorsky–Gorsky–Le–Shen–Simental
[
CGG+22
]
and Galashin–Lam–SB–Speyer
[
GLSBS22
,
GLSBS
]
have independently given cluster structures on
braid varieties
in arbitrary type, which generalize Richardson varieties; it is not known how these two cluster structures are related. Ingermanson’s construction is a special case of the construction in
[
GLSBS22
]
; Ménard’s seeds are special cases of those in
[
CGG+22
]
.

Background

We use the following standard combinatorial notation:
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
,
\binom{[n]}{h}
is the set of cardinality
h

[n]
,
w_{0}
is the longest permtuation of
S_{n}
,
s_{i}\in S_{n}
is the transposition exchanging
i
and
i+1
, for

2.1.
Background on Richardson varieties

Let
G=SL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
and let
B,B_{-}\subset G
denote the Borel subgroups of upper and lower triangular matrices, respectively. Let
N,N_{-}
denote the corresponding unipotent subgroups of upper and lower unitriangular matrices, respectively. For
g\in G
, let
g_{i}
denote the
i
th column of
g
. We denote the minor of
g
on rows
R
and columns
C
by
\Delta_{R,C}(g)
.

For
w\in S_{n}
, we choose a distinguished lift
\dot{w}
of
w
to
G
. The lift satisfies

\dot{w}_{ij}=\begin{cases}\pm 1&\text{ if }i=w(j)\\
0&\text{ else }\end{cases}

and the signs of entries are determined by the condition that
\Delta_{w[j],[j]}(\dot{w})=1
for all
j\in[n]
. If the particular lift of
w
to
G
does not matter, we also write
w
for the lift (e.g. we write
BwB
rather than
B\dot{w}B
).

We identify the flag variety
\mathcal{F}\ell_{n}
with the quotient
G/B
. Concretely, a matrix
g\in G
represents the flag
V_{\bullet}=(V_{1}\subset V_{2}\subset\cdots\subset V_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{n})
where
V_{i}
is the span of
g_{1},\dots,g_{i}
.
The flag variety has two well-known decompositions into cells, the Schubert decomposition

G/B=\bigsqcup_{w\in S_{n}}BwB/B=\bigsqcup_{w\in S_{n}}C_{w}

and the opposite Schubert decomposition

G/B=\bigsqcup_{w\in S_{n}}B_{-}wB/B=\bigsqcup_{w\in S_{n}}C^{w}.

The stratum
C_{w}
is a
Schubert cell
and is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{\ell(w)}
. The stratum
C^{w}
is an
opposite Schubert cell
and is isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{\ell(w_{0})-\ell(w)}
. For a fixed lift
w
, it is well-known that the projection map
G\to G/B
restricts to isomorphisms

(1)

Nw\cap wN_{-}\xrightarrow{\sim}C_{w}\qquad\qquad N_{-}w\cap wN_{-}\xrightarrow{\sim}C^{w}.

Or, more concretely, each coset in
C_{w}
(resp.
C^{w}
) has a unique representative matrix which differs from
w
only in entries that lie both above and to the left (resp. both below and to the left) of a nonzero entry of
w
(see e.g.
[
Ful97
]
).

We are concerned with the intersection of an opposite Schubert cell and a Schubert cell

\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}:=C^{v}\cap C_{w}

which is called an
(open) Richardson variety
. We usually drop the adjective “open.” The Richardson variety
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
is nonempty if and only if
v\leq w
, in which case it is a smooth irreducible affine variety of dimension
\ell(w)-\ell(v)

[
Deo85
]
.

Open Richardson varieties were studied in the context of Kazdhan-Lusztig polynomials
[
KL79
]
; the number of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
points of
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
is exactly the
R
-polynomial indexed by
(v,w)

[
Deo85
]
, which can be used to recursively compute Kazhdan-Lusztig polynomials. The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-point counts and more generally the cohomology of
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
are also related to knot homology
[
GL20
]
. Real points of
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
, and in particular
positive points
, feature in work of Lusztig and Rietsch
[
Lus94
,
Rie99
]
on total positivity. Special cases of Richardson varieties include the
(open) positroid varieties
of
[
KLS13
]
, which are Richardson varieties
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
where
w
has a single descent. Richardson varieties themselves are special cases of
braid varieties
(see e.g.
[
CGGS20
]
).

We identify
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
with two different subsets of
G
, one for Ingermanson’s construction and one for Leclerc’s. We will later use these identifications to define functions on
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
. Below, we use the involutive automorphism
g\mapsto g^{\theta}
of
G
from
[
FZ99
, (1.11)]
; the
(i,j)
entry of
g^{\theta}
is the minor of
g
obtained by deleting the
i
th row and
j
th column. It is not hard to check that
B^{\theta}=B_{-}
,
N^{\theta}=N_{-}
, and
\dot{v}^{\theta}
is another lift of
v
to
G
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
v\leq w
, let

N_{v,w}:=N\cap\dot{w}N_{-}\dot{w}^{-1}\cap B_{-}vB\dot{w}^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad N^{\prime}_{v,w}:=N\cap\dot{v}^{-1}N\dot{v}\cap\dot{v}^{-1}B_{-}wB_{-}.

Also, let

We have isomorphisms

\displaystyle\alpha:D(N_{v,w})

\displaystyle\to\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}

\displaystyle\quad\beta:N^{\prime}_{v,w}

\displaystyle\to\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}

\displaystyle gd_{g}

\displaystyle\mapsto gd_{g}\dot{w}B

\displaystyle\quad g

\displaystyle\mapsto(\dot{v}g)^{\theta}B.

If

The map

\displaystyle D(N_{v,w})

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{D^{-1}}

\displaystyle N_{v,w}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\alpha^{\prime}}\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}

\displaystyle gd_{g}

\displaystyle\longmapsto

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\longmapsto g\dot{w}B

since

Now, it follows easily from (

Remark 2.2
.

Leclerc identifies the flag variety with
B_{-}\backslash G
rather than
G/B
, and so considers the variety

{}^{-}\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}:=B_{-}\backslash{(B_{-}vB\cap B_{-}wB_{-})}

which is different from, though isomorphic to,
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}

\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}\xrightarrow{\Theta}{(BvB_{-}\cap B_{-}wB_{-})}/B_{-}\xrightarrow{\delta_{1}}\dot{v}N^{\prime}_{v,w}\xrightarrow{\delta_{2}}{{}^{-}\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}}.

The map

is induced by the involution
g\mapsto g^{\theta}
on

; from
[
FZ99
, Section 2]
, one can see that

and that
\dot{w}^{\theta}
is another lift of

2.2.
Background on wiring diagrams and chamber minors

Before describing Ingermanson’s and Leclerc’s seeds, we need some combinatorial background.

Given
w\in S_{n}
, a
reduced expression
for
w
is an expression
\mathbf{w}=s_{h_{1}}\dots s_{h_{\ell}}
where
\ell
is as small as possible. The number
\ell
is the
length
of
w
, denoted
\ell(w)
. We use the notation

w_{(i)}:=s_{h_{1}}\dots s_{h_{i-1}}\quad\text{ and }\quad w^{(i)}:=s_{h_{\ell}}\dots s_{h_{i}}=w^{-1}w_{(i)}

for prefixes of
\mathbf{w}
and prefixes of
\mathbf{w}^{-1}
, setting
w_{(1)}=e
.

As a shorthand, we write
v\leq\mathbf{w}
to indicate a pair of permutations
v\leq w
and a choice of reduced expression
\mathbf{w}
for
w
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
v\leq\mathbf{w}=s_{h_{1}}\dots s_{h_{\ell}}
. A
subexpression
for
v
in
\mathbf{w}
is an expression for
v
of the form
v=s_{h_{1}}^{v}\dots s_{h_{\ell}}^{v}
where
s_{h_{i}}^{v}\in\{e,s_{h_{i}}\}
.
As for
\mathbf{w}
, we define

v_{(i)}:=s_{h_{1}}^{v}\dots s_{h_{i-1}}^{v}\quad\text{ and }\quad v^{(i)}:=s_{h_{\ell}}^{v}\dots s_{h_{i}}^{v}.

The indices
i
where
s_{h_{i}}^{v}\neq e
is the
support
of the subexpression. The subexpression is
reduced
if the support has size
\ell(v)
. The
positive distinguished subexpression
(PDS) for
v
in
\mathbf{w}
is the reduced subexpression whose support is lexicographically largest. If
\mathbf{w}
is fixed, we denote the PDS for
v
by
\mathbf{v}
.

We denote the support of the PDS by
J^{+}_{\mathbf{v}}
, and call these the
hollow crossings
of
\mathbf{w}
. The complement of the support is
J^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{v}}
; we call these the
solid crossings
of
\mathbf{w}
. Note that
|J^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{v}}|=\ell(w)-\ell(v)=\dim\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{w}=s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}s_{3}s_{2}s_{1}
and let
v=3214
. Reduced subexpressions for
v
in
\mathbf{w}
include

ees_{1}es_{2}s_{1}\qquad es_{2}s_{1}es_{2}e\qquad s_{1}s_{2}eees_{1}\qquad s_{1}s_{2}s_{1}eee.

The first subexpression has support
\{3,5,6\}
and is the PDS for
v
in
\mathbf{w}
. So the hollow crossings are
J^{+}_{\mathbf{v}}=\{3,5,6\}
and the solid crossings are
J^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{v}}=\{1,2,4\}
.

Remark 2.5
.

Alternatively, the PDS for
v
can be defined using a greedy procedure, moving from right to left. Set
v_{(\ell+1)}=v
. If
v_{(i+1)}
is already determined, then
v_{(i)}
is equal to either
v_{(i+1)}
or

.

Remark 2.6
.

The notion of positive distinguished subexpressions (and more generally, distinguished subexpressions) is due to Deodhar
[
Deo85
]
. Our notation for the support and complement of the support is inspired by
[
MR04
]
, as is the terminology “solid” and “hollow” crossing. The
+
in the superscript of
J^{+}_{\mathbf{v}}
is to indicate that
J^{+}_{\mathbf{v}}
records where the length of

increases. The
\bullet

is to remind the reader that these are the solid crossings.

For
v\leq\mathbf{w}
, we will draw both the reduced expression
\mathbf{w}
and the PDS
\mathbf{v}
in the plane as wiring diagrams. Since
\mathbf{w}
is itself a positive distinguished subexpression of
\mathbf{w}
, we make all definitions for the PDS
\mathbf{v}
.

Definition 2.7
.

The
wiring diagram

W_{\mathbf{v}}
is obtained by replacing each simple transposition
s_{i}
in
\mathbf{v}
with the configuration of strands on the left, and each
e
in
\mathbf{v}
with the configuration of strands on the right.

We label the crossings of
W_{\mathbf{v}}
with
J^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{v}}
in the natural way. We also label the endpoints of the strands from
1
to
n
, going from bottom to top. Each crossing
c
has a
rising strand
, whose height immediately to the right of
c
is higher than immediately to the left of
c
, and a
falling strand
.

If a strand
\gamma
in
W_{\mathbf{v}}
has right endpoint
h
, it has left endpoint
v(h)
. Since
\mathbf{v}
is reduced, then no two strands cross more than once.

A
chamber
of a wiring diagram is a connected component of the complement of the strands. We denote chambers by
\chi
; the chamber to the left of crossing
c
is
\chi_{c}
. We can label each chamber with a subset of
[n]
.

Definition 2.8

(Right and left labeling of chambers)
.

Let
\chi_{c}
be a chamber of
W_{\mathbf{v}}
. The
left label
of
\chi_{c}
is

v_{(c)}[h_{c}]=\{i\in[n]:i\text{ is the left endpoint of a strand }\gamma\text{ below }\chi_{c}\}

3.
Ingermanson’s and Leclerc’s cluster algebras

We will define Ingermanson’s cluster algebra and Leclerc’s in parallel, using similar symbols for objects in each construction. “Ingermanson” is before “Leclerc” in alphabetical order, so the symbols will follow the same rule (e.g., Ingermanson’s cluster variables will be

3.1.
Cluster algebras

In this section, we set conventions and notation for cluster algebras and related concepts. We refer the reader to e.g.
[
FWZ16
]
for most definitions.

An

is a directed graph with no loops or 2-cycles, each vertex of which is either
mutable
or
frozen
. A

\Sigma=(\mathbf{A},Q)
in a field
\mathcal{F}
consists of an ice quiver

together with a tuple
\mathbf{A}
of elements of
\mathcal{F}

, which are indexed by the vertices of the quiver. Cluster variables indexed by mutable vertices are
mutable
; the others are
frozen
. The tuple
\mathbf{A}
is the
cluster
of
\Sigma
. For each mutable cluster variable
A_{i}
, we have the corresponding
exchange ratio

\hat{y}_{i}:=\prod_{j\in Q}A_{j}^{\#\text{ arrows }A_{j}\to A_{i}\text{ in }Q}.

By convention, if there are
b
arrows from
A_{i}
to
A_{j}
in
Q
, then there are

arrows from
A_{j}
to
A_{i}
.

There is an involutive operation called

which can be performed at any mutable vertex of

; this produces a new seed
\Sigma^{\prime}=(\mathbf{A}^{\prime},Q^{\prime})
. The collection of all seeds which can be obtained from
\Sigma
by a sequence of mutations is the

of
\Sigma
. The

is the

-algebra generated by all mutable variables in the seed pattern of

Let

be an affine variety. We say

Fra16
]
for additional details).
We emphasize that a variety

]

Throughout this section we fix

5.1.

Proposition 5.1

Let
\chi,\chi^{\prime}
be two chambers adjacent to the crossing

denote the right endpoints of the falling and rising

-strands at the crossing

be the right endpoints of the

(a)

If

and

is obtained from

by adding a strip to the top between steps

. In particular, there exists an injective morphism

.

(b)

If
\chi=\chi_{i^{\leftarrow}}
and
\chi^{\prime}=\chi_{i^{\uparrow}}
then

is obtained from
U_{\chi}
by removing a strip from the top starting at
a
and adding a strip to the bottom starting at

. In particular, there exists a surjective morphism

.

(c)

If
\chi=\chi_{i^{\leftarrow}}
and
\chi^{\prime}=\chi_{i^{\downarrow}}
then

is obtained from

by adding a strip to the top starting at

and removing a strop from the bottom starting at

. In particular, there exists a morphism

whose image is

without the strip starting at

.

By construction it is easy to see that the maps defined above have the largest possible images. If

is one of

, then there may be other morphisms

. However by construction

as

would lie inside

. Since we are interested in describing irreducible maps between indecomposable summands in Leclerc’s seed, it suffices to consider only the maps

between the appropriate summands.

5.2.
Arrows in Leclerc’s quiver

The goal of this section is to show that arrows in Leclerc’s quiver come from morphisms between the modules appearing in the neighboring chambers. We begin with some preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{w}=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{\ell}}
and
v\leq\mathbf{w}
. Let
w^{\prime}=w_{(\ell-1)}
and
v^{\prime}=v_{(\ell-1)}
. The simple module
S(i_{\ell})
is not a summand of the top of
U^{\prime}_{j}
for any
j\in[\ell-1]
.

Proof.

Let
i_{\ell}=k
. If the simple
S(k)
is a summand of the top of
U^{\prime}_{j}
then
k+1\in(w^{\prime})^{(j)}[i_{j}]=s_{i_{l-1}}\dots s_{i_{j+1}}s_{i_{j}}[i_{j}]
and
k\not\in(w^{\prime})^{(j)}[i_{j}]
. Then
k\in w^{(j)}[i_{j}]
and
k+1\not\in w^{(j)}[i_{j}]
, since
w
is obtained by multiplying
w^{\prime}
on the right by
s_{k}
. However, this contradicts the assumption that
(w^{\prime})^{(j)}<w^{(j)}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathbf{w}=s_{i_{1}}\dots s_{i_{\ell}}
and
v\leq w
. Let
w^{\prime}=w_{(\ell-1)}
and
v^{\prime}=v_{(\ell-1)}
. For every
i,j<\ell
there is a bijection between irreducible morphisms
f:M_{i}\to M_{j}
in
\textup{add}\,U_{v,\mathbf{w}}
and irreducible morphisms
f^{\prime}:M_{i}^{\prime}\to M_{j}^{\prime}
in
\textup{add}\,U_{v^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
such that
\textup{im}\,f
and
\textup{im}\,f^{\prime}
differ possibly at vertex
k:=i_{\ell}
. In particular, there is an exact sequence

S(k)\to\textup{im}\,f^{\prime}\to\textup{im}\,f\to S(k).

Proof.

First, suppose that
\ell\in J^{+}_{\mathbf{v}}
. Suppose that there exists a nonzero morphism
f^{\prime}:M^{\prime}_{i}\to M^{\prime}_{j}
such that
\textup{im}\,f^{\prime}
is indecomposable. Now consider
\textup{im}\,f^{\prime}
inside
M_{i}^{\prime}
. If the top contour of the image, and hence also the top contour of
M^{\prime}_{i}
, has vertical step
k
and horizontal step

then let
\textup{im}\,f
be obtained from
\textup{im}\,f^{\prime}
by adding
S(k)
to the top. Moreover, if
\textup{im}\,f^{\prime}
contains
S(k)
in the socle then in addition let
\textup{im}\,f
be obtained from
\textup{im}\,f^{\prime}
by removing
S(k)
. Otherwise, let
\textup{im}\,f=\textup{im}\,f^{\prime}
. Recall that
M_{i},M_{j}
are obtained from
M_{i}^{\prime},M_{j}^{\prime}
respectively by adding
S(k)
to the top when possible and then also removing
S(k)
from the socle when possible. It is easy to see that
f^{\prime}
induces the corresponding nonzero map
f:M_{i}\to M_{j}
with image
\textup{im}\,f
as described above. Note that
\textup{im}\,f^{\prime}\not\cong S(k)
by Lemma
5.2
, which means that the induced map

is indeed nonzero.

Conversely, we can also see that every nonzero

induces a corresponding nonzero

by the same reasoning. Here note that
\textup{im}\,f\not\cong S(k)
as
M_{j}
cannot have
S(k)
in the socle since
\ell\in J^{+}_{\mathbf{v}}
, so indeed
f^{\prime}
is nonzero. This shows that there is a bijection between nonzero morphisms

in the seed
\textup{add}\,U_{v,\mathbf{w}}
and nonzero morphisms

in
\textup{add}\,U_{v^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
such that the images of

and

differ at most by

in the top and socle. Since every such morphism is a composition of irreducible ones, we obtain the desired bijection between irreducible morphisms as in the statement of the lemma. This completes the proof in the case

.

Now, suppose that
\ell\in J^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{v}}
. Then a similar argument as above implies that there is a bijection between nonzero morphisms

and nonzero morphisms

such that

. Note that if
\textup{im}\,f\cong S(k)
then by construction the corresponding morphism

would be zero. However, in

the module

since

. Hence, for

no morphism

with image

can be irreducible in

, as it would factor through

.
Therefore, we obtain the desired bijection between irreducible morphisms

in
\textup{add}\,U_{v^{\prime},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
and irreducible morphisms

in
\textup{add}\,U_{v,\mathbf{w}}
as claimed.
∎

.

Definition 6.1

6.2.
Equality of quivers

We have shown that each arrow of Leclerc’s quiver
Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}
is an arrow of Ingermanson’s quiver
Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing}
. Moreover, we know that Leclerc’s cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec})
is a subalgebra of
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
and Ingermanson’s cluster algebra
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})
is equal to
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
. Finally, we have an automorphism
\tau_{v,w}^{*}
of
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
which takes Ingermanson’s cluster
\mathbf{A}
to Leclerc’s cluster
\mathbf{B}
. We will now use these facts to show that in fact, Ingermanson’s quiver cannot have any additional arrows.

Lemma 6.7
.

Consider two seeds
(\mathbf{x},Q)
and
(\mathbf{x},Q^{\prime})
in the field of rational functions
\mathbb{C}(\mathbf{x})
with the same cluster but different quivers. Let
\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{x},Q)
be the cluster algebra of the first seed and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{A}(\mathbf{x},Q^{\prime})
be the cluster algebra of the second.

Suppose that
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{A}
and that
Q^{\prime}
is a subquiver of
Q
(identifying a vertex of
Q^{\prime}
with the vertex of
Q
labeled by the same cluster variable). Then in fact
Q=Q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Consider a mutable vertex
k
of
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
. We will argue that for all
j
,
\#(\text{arrows }k\to j)
is the same in
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
.

In
(\mathbf{x},Q^{\prime})
, mutating at
k
gives the cluster variable

\tilde{x}_{k}^{\prime}=\frac{M_{+}+M_{-}}{x_{k}}

where
M_{+},M_{-}
are monomials in
\mathbf{x}
. Since
Q^{\prime}
is a subquiver of
Q
, mutating at
k
in
(\mathbf{x},Q)
gives the cluster variable

\tilde{x}_{k}=\frac{M_{+}N_{+}+M_{-}N_{-}}{x_{k}}.

By assumption,
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{A}
, so in particular
\tilde{x}^{\prime}_{k}
is a Laurent polynomial in
\mu_{k}(\mathbf{x},Q)
, say

\frac{M_{+}+M_{-}}{x_{k}}=\sum_{\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}c_{\textbf{a}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots\tilde{x}_{k}^{a_{k}}\dots x_{n}^{a_{n}}.

Clearing the denominator on the left and writing
\tilde{x}_{k}
in terms of the cluster
\mathbf{x}
, we have

M_{+}+M_{-}=x_{k}\cdot\sum_{\textbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}c_{\textbf{a}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots(M_{+}N_{+}+M_{-}N_{-})^{a_{k}}x_{k}^{-a_{k}}\dots x_{n}^{a_{n}}.

Note that the left hand side is a polynomial with degree
0
in
x_{k}
, so the same must be true of the right hand side. Because the binomial
M_{+}N_{+}+M_{-}N_{-}
is also degree 0 in
x_{k}
, this implies that
a_{k}=1
for all nonzero
c_{\textbf{a}}
. So we have

M_{+}+M_{-}=(M_{+}N_{+}+M_{-}N_{-})\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{a}\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0})^{n}\\
a_{k}=1\end{subarray}}c_{\textbf{a}}x_{1}^{a_{1}}\dots x_{k-1}^{a_{k-1}}x_{k+1}^{a_{k+1}}\dots x_{n}^{a_{n}}.

Comparing degrees, we see that
a_{1}=\dots=a_{n}=0
and
N_{+}=N_{-}=1
. That is,
\tilde{x}^{\prime}_{k}=\tilde{x}_{k}
, which implies
\#(\text{arrows }k\to j)
is the same in
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
for all
j
.
∎

Corollary 6.8
.

Choose
v\leq\mathbf{w}
with
\mathbf{w}
unipeak. Label the vertices of both Ingermanson’s quiver
Q_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing}
and Leclerc’s quiver

by the set of solid crossings

Proof.

This follows directly from

, with

and

. Indeed,

and

3.12
together imply that

is a subset of

. Because

is an automorphism of
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})
, we also have that

s_{i}s_{j}\leftrightarrow s_{j}s_{i}
where
|i-j|>1
and braid moves
s_{i}s_{i+1}s_{i}\leftrightarrow s_{i+1}s_{i}s_{i+1}
. So it suffices to consider the case where
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
are related by a single commutation move or a single braid move.

If
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
are related by a commutation move, it’s easy to see that the right chamber minors of
W_{v,\mathbf{w}}
are the same as the right chamber minors of
W_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
, up to an indexing change. This means that the indecomposable summands of the cluster tilting objects
U_{v,\mathbf{w}}
and
U_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
are the same (though the summands may be indexed differently), and thus the seeds
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}
and
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{\Lec}
are the same.

If
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
are related by a braid move involving at least one hollow crossing, then the seeds
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}
and
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{\Lec}
are also related by reindexing. Let
(\Delta^{\rho}_{d})^{\prime}
denote the right chamber minors for
W_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
, and also denote the cluster variables of
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{\Lec}
with primes. Say the braid move occurs at indices
c-1,c,c+1
. By inspection,
\Delta^{\rho}_{d}=(\Delta^{\rho}_{d})^{\prime}
for all crossings
d\notin\{c-1,c,c+1\}
, so in particular
B_{d}=B_{d}^{\prime}
for
d\in J^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{v}}\setminus\{c-1,c,c+1\}
. It is not hard to check that
\Delta^{\rho}_{c-1}=(\Delta^{\rho}_{c})^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\rho}_{c}=(\Delta^{\rho}_{c-1})^{\prime}
by comparing the
v
and
w
-strands of
W_{v,\mathbf{w}}
and
W_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
; the key insight is that the same number of crossings in
\{c-1,c,c+1\}
will be hollow in each wiring diagram. Now, by considering the various cases of which of
\{c-1,c,c+1\}
can be hollow in
W_{v,\mathbf{w}}
, one can see that if
d\in\{c-1,c,c+1\}
is solid, then
B_{d}
is an irreducible factor of either
\Delta^{\rho}_{c}
or
\Delta^{\rho}_{c-1}
. Since each of those chamber minors is also a chamber minor for
W_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
, this means that
B_{d}
is also a cluster variable in
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{\Lec}
. Thus the indecomposable summands of
U_{v,\mathbf{w}}
and
U_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
are the same (though summands are indexed differently), and the corresponding seeds are the same.

If
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
are related by a braid move involving three solid crossings, say at indices
c-2,c-1,c
, then
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}
and
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{\Lec}
are the same or are related by mutation in direction
c
. As above, use primes to indicate the right chamber minors and cluster variables for
v\leq\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
. Again, by inspection,
\Delta^{\rho}_{d}=(\Delta^{\rho}_{d})^{\prime}
for
d\notin\{c-2,c-1,c\}
,
\Delta^{\rho}_{c-1}=(\Delta^{\rho}_{c-2})^{\prime}
and
\Delta^{\rho}_{c-2}=(\Delta^{\rho}_{c-1})^{\prime}
. So the irreducible factors of

P=\prod_{c\neq d}\Delta^{\rho}_{d}

coincide with the irreducible factors of

Q=\prod_{c\neq d}(\Delta^{\rho}_{d})^{\prime}.

Now, if
\Delta^{\rho}_{c}P
and
(\Delta^{\rho}_{c})^{\prime}Q
have the same set of irreducible factors, then
U_{v,\mathbf{w}}
and
U_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
have the same set of indecomposable summands and the corresponding seeds are equal. If they do not have the same set of factors, then in fact their factors differ only by
B_{c}\neq B_{c}^{\prime}
. This implies that

(T_{v,\mathbf{w}}/M_{c})\oplus M_{c}^{\prime}=T_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}

where

T_{v,\mathbf{w}}:=\bigoplus_{d\in J^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{v}}}M_{d}\qquad T_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}:=\bigoplus_{d\in J^{\bullet}_{\mathbf{v}}}M^{\prime}_{d}

are the basic cluster tilting modules obtained from
U_{v,\mathbf{w}}
and
U_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
and
M_{d},M_{d}^{\prime}
are the indecomposable modules which map to
B_{d},B_{d}^{\prime}
under the cluster character map
\varphi
. The definition of mutation of basic cluster tilting objects (see e.g.
[
Lec16
, Definition 3.9 c)]
) implies that
T_{v,\mathbf{w}}
and
T_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}
are related by categorical mutation. This in turn implies
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}
and
\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}^{\Lec}
are related by mutation in direction
c
.

∎

Proof of
A
.

That
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec})
does not depend on the choice of reduced expression
\mathbf{w}
is exactly
7.1
.

When
\mathbf{w}
is unipeak,
B
implies that
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})=\tau_{v,w}^{*}(\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}))
. From
3.9
, we have
\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})=\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
. By
2.14
,
\tau_{v,w}^{*}
is an automorphism of
\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]
, so

\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}]=(\tau_{v,w}^{*})^{-1}(\mathbb{C}[\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}])=(\tau_{v,w}^{*})^{-1}(\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing}))=\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Lec}).

∎

Proof of
C
.

The
totally positive part

\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Lus}
of
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
defined by Lusztig is the subset of
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}
where all the Marsh-Rietsch chamber minors are positive
[
MR04
, Proposition 12.1]
. The left chamber minors
\Delta^{\lambda}_{c}
are monomially related to the Marsh-Riestch chamber minors, and the cluster variables
\mathbf{A}_{v,\mathbf{w}}
are in turn monomially related to the left chamber minors. Thus,
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Lus}
coincides with

\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Ing}:=\{F\in\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}:\text{all cluster variables in }\mathcal{A}(\Sigma_{v,\mathbf{w}}^{\Ing})\text{ are positive on }F\}.

On the other hand, using
B
,
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Lec}
is equal to
\tau_{v,w}(\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Ing})=\tau_{v,w}(\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Lus})
. By
[
GL22
, Theorem 2.6, Remark 3.1]
, the map
\tau_{v,w}
preserves
\mathring{\mathcal{R}}_{v,w}^{>0,\Lus}
, so we are done.
∎

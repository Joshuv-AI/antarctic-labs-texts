Contagious McKean–Vlasov problems with common noise: from smooth to singular feedback through hitting times
Ben Hambly, Aldaïr Petronilia, Christoph Reisinger, Stefan Rigger, Andreas Søjmark

Abstract
We consider a family of McKean–Vlasov equations arising as the large particle limit of a system of interacting particles on the positive half-line with common noise and feedback. Such systems are motivated by structural models for systemic risk with contagion. This contagious interaction is such that when a particle hits zero, the impact is to move all the others toward the origin through a kernel which smooths the impact over time. We study a rescaling of the impact kernel under which it converges to the Dirac delta function so that the interaction happens instantaneously and the limiting singular McKean–Vlasov equation can exhibit jumps. Our approach provides a novel method to construct solutions to such singular problems that allows for more general drift and diffusion coefficients and we establish weak convergence to relaxed solutions in this setting. With more restrictions on the coefficients we can establish an almost sure version showing convergence to strong solutions. Under some regularity conditions on the contagion, we also show a rate of convergence up to the time the regularity of the contagion breaks down. Lastly, we perform some numerical experiments to investigate the sharpness of our bounds for the rate of convergence.

\Delta L_{t}=\inf\{x>0\,:\,\bm{\nu}_{t-}([0,\alpha x])<x\},

Returning to (

The main contributions and structure of this paper are as follows:

•

Firstly, in Section

•

Secondly, in Section

•

Lastly, in Section

We also give numerical tests of the convergence order in scenarios of different regularity, with and without common noise.

2.1
Limit points of the smoothed system

In order to show the existence of a limit point of

, we must first choose a suitable topology to establish convergence. By Theorem 2.4 in
[

]
,

is continuous for every
\varepsilon>0
, but the loss of the limiting process may in fact jump. Skorokhod’s

-topology is sufficiently rich to facilitate the convergence of continuous functions to those with jumps.

Remark 2.7
.

The equation (
1.1
) has been posed in slightly more generality (with
\alpha(t,X_{t}^{\varepsilon},\bm{\nu}_{t}^{\varepsilon})
) in
[
18
]
. As the convergence is strictly in the

-topology, not in the

-topology, we only consider
\alpha
to be of the form
\alpha(t)
. If we considered it to also be a function of
X_{t}
and/or
\bm{\nu}_{t}
, then we cannot expect to obtain an equation of the form (
1.2
) in the limit as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, due to
X
,
\bm{\nu}
, and

having jumps at the same time.

The theory in
[
33
]
requires our càdlàg processes to be uniformly right-continuous at the initial time point and left-continuous at the terminal time point, when working with functions on compact time domains. As we are starting from an arbitrary initial condition
X_{0-}
which is positive almost surely, the limiting process may exhibit a jump immediately at time
0
given sufficient mass near the boundary. For this reason, we shall embed the process from
D([0,T],\mathbb{R})
into
D([-1,\bar{T}],\mathbb{R})
, where
\bar{T}=T+1
, using the extension defined in (
2.3
). Unless stated otherwise, for notational convenience we shall denote the latter space,
D([-1,\bar{T}],\mathbb{R})
, by
D_{\mathbb{R}}
. Recall,
\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}
is defined to be the law of
\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}
conditional on
W^{0}
. That is
\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}\mid W^{0})
.

To show tightness and convergence of the collection of random measures
\{\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
, we shall follow the ideas in
[
10
]
and
[
25
]
. To begin, we first derive a Grönwall-type estimate of the smoothed system uniformly in
\varepsilon
. These estimates are necessary to show the tightness of
\{\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
and the existence of a limiting random measure. In the following Proposition and its sequels,
C
will denote a constant that may change from line to line, and we will denote the dependencies of the value of
C
in its subscript. To further simplify notation, we use
Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}
,
Y_{t}^{0,\varepsilon}
, and
\mathcal{Y}_{t}^{\varepsilon}
to denote

\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u,X_{u}^{\varepsilon})\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}(u,\bm{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{u})}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W_{u},\qquad\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(u,X_{u}^{\varepsilon})\rho(u,\bm{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{u})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}W^{0}_{u},\quad\text{and}\quad Y_{t}^{\varepsilon}+Y_{t}^{0,\varepsilon}

respectively. We shall use

,

Proposition 2.8

(Grönwall upper bound)
.

For any
p\geq 1
and
t\leq T
, there exists a constant
C_{\alpha,b,p,T,\sigma}>0
independent of
\varepsilon>0
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\underset{s\leq T}{\sup}{\left|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right|}^{p}\right]\leq C_{\alpha,b,p,T,\sigma}.

(2.8)

Proof.

By the linear growth condition on
b
and the triangle inequality, we have

\left|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right|\leq\left|X_{0-}\right|+C_{b}\int_{0}^{t}1+\underset{u\leq s}{\sup}\left|X_{u}^{\varepsilon}\right|+\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left|X^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}\right|\right|W^{0}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\underset{s\leq T}{\sup}\left|\mathcal{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\right|+\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{\infty}.

By
[
18
, Lemma A.3]
,
\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left|X^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}\right|^{p}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s<\infty
for any
p\geq 1
. Therefore, a simple application of Grönwall’s inequality shows that

\underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\left|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right|\leq C_{T,b,\alpha}\left(\left|X_{0-}\right|+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left|X^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}\right|\right|W^{0}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\underset{s\leq T}{\sup}\left|\mathcal{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\right|+1\right).

By (
vi
) in Assumption
2.2
,
X_{0-}
has finite
L^{p}
-moments for every
p>0
. Furthermore, by employing the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality to control
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\leq T}|\mathcal{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s}|]
, we may deduce that
\mathbb{E}[\sup_{s\leq t}\left|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right|^{p}]<\infty
for all
t\geq 0
and
p\geq 1
. Now, observing that
\left|X^{\varepsilon}_{t\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}\right|\leq\sup_{s\leq t}\left|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right|
, we have by the monotonicity of expectation and Jensen’s inequality that

\displaystyle\underset{s\leq t}{\sup}\left|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,b,\alpha}^{p}\left(\left|X_{0-}\right|+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\left|X^{\varepsilon}_{s\wedge\tau^{\varepsilon}}\right|\right|W^{0}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\underset{s\leq T}{\sup}\left|\mathcal{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\right|+1\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C_{T,b,\alpha,p}\left(\left|X_{0-}\right|^{p}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\underset{u\leq s}{\sup}\left|X_{u}^{\varepsilon}\right|^{p}\right|W^{0}\right]\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\underset{s\leq T}{\sup}\left|\mathcal{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\right|^{p}+1\right).

Taking expectations and applying Fubini’s theorem followed by Grönwall’s inequality, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\left[\underset{s\leq T}{\sup}\left|X_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right|^{p}\right]\leq C_{T,b,\alpha,p}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|X_{0-}\right|^{p}+\underset{s\leq T}{\sup}\left|\mathcal{Y}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\right|^{p}+1\right].

(2.9)

Lastly, by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and (
ii
) from Assumption
2.2
, we may bound (
2.9
) independently of
\varepsilon
. This completes the proof.
∎

The collection
\{\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
consists of
\mathcal{P}(D_{\mathbb{R}})
-valued random measures. To show that this collection is tight, we need to find, for any
\gamma>0
, a compact set
K_{\gamma}
in
\mathcal{P}(D_{\mathbb{R}})
so that
\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon})(K_{\gamma})=\mathbb{P}(\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}\in K_{\gamma})\leq\gamma
for all
\varepsilon>0
. In other words, we need the probability measures
\{\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon>0}
to be tight in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(D_{\mathbb{R}}))
. Rather than tackling this directly, we follow an indirect approach of first showing that the measures
\{\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon>0}
are tight in
\mathcal{P}(D_{\mathbb{R}})
and then we construct
K_{\gamma}
from there. Due to how the processes are defined, the latter follows easily from properties of the
M_{1}
-topology and
[
1
, Theorem 1]
.

Proposition 2.9

(Tightness of smoothed random measures)
.

Let
\mathfrak{T}_{M_{1}}^{\text{wk}}
denote the topology of weak convergence on
\mathcal{P}(D_{\mathbb{R}})
induced by the
M_{1}
-topology on
D_{\mathbb{R}}
. Then, the collection
\{\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}=\{\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}\mid W^{0})\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is tight on
(\mathcal{P}(D_{\mathbb{R}}),\mathfrak{T}_{M_{1}}^{\text{wk}})
under Assumption
2.2
.

Proof.

Define
\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon})
. By
[
33
, Theorem 12.12.3]
, we need to verify two conditions to show the tightness of the measures on
D_{\mathbb{R}}
endowed with the
M_{1}
-topology:

(i)

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{x\in D_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;\left\lVert x\right\rVert_{\infty}>\lambda\right\}\right)=0
.

(ii)

For any
\eta>0
, we have
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{x\in D_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;w_{M_{1}}(x,\delta)\geq\eta\right\}\right)=0
, where
w_{M_{1}}
is the oscillatory function of the
M_{1}
-topology, defined as in
[
33
, Section 12, Equation 12.2]
.

To show the first condition, we observe that by the definition of the extension of our process, we have

\sup_{t\leq\bar{T}}|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|\leq\sup_{t\leq T}|X_{t}^{\varepsilon}|+\sup_{t\leq 1}|W_{T+t}-W_{T}|+\left\lVert\alpha\right\rVert_{\infty}.

Then, it is clear by Markov’s inequality and Proposition
2.8
that for any
\lambda>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\leq\bar{T}}|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}|>\lambda\right]=O(\lambda^{-1})

uniformly in
\varepsilon
. Therefore, by taking the supremum over
\varepsilon
and then the
\limsup
over
\lambda
, the first condition holds. We shall not show the second condition directly. By
[
1
, Theorem 1]
, the second condition is equivalent to showing:

(I)

There exists some
C>0
, uniformly in
\varepsilon
, such that
\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde{X}_{t_{1}}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}_{t_{2}}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}_{t_{3}}^{\varepsilon})\geq\eta\right]\leq C\eta^{-4}|t_{3}-t_{1}|^{2}
for all
\eta>0
and
-1\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq t_{3}\leq\bar{T}
where
\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=\inf_{\lambda\in[0,1]}|x_{2}-(1-\lambda)x_{1}-\lambda x_{3}|
.

(II)

\lim_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in(-1,-1+\delta)}|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{X}_{-1}|+\sup_{t\in(\bar{T}-\delta,\bar{T})}|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{X}_{\bar{T}}|\geq\eta\right]=0
for all
\eta>0
.

Note that by Assumption
2.2
, we have
\alpha
is non-decreasing and non-negative. Therefore, by the properties of Lebesgue-Stielitjes integration,
t\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}
is non-decreasing. As monotone functions are immaterial to the
M_{1}
modulus of continuity, we have

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde{X}_{t_{1}}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}_{t_{2}}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}_{t_{3}}^{\varepsilon})\leq\left|Z_{t_{1}}-Z_{t_{2}}\right|+|Z_{t_{2}}-Z_{t_{3}}|,

where
Z
is given by

Z_{t}=X_{0-}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge T}b(u,X_{u}^{\varepsilon},\bm{\nu}_{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u+\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}

for
t\geq 0
and
Z_{t}=X_{0-}
for
t<0
. Hence, to show (I), it is sufficient to bound the increments of
Z
. Note that when
s<t<-1
,
Z
is constant. Therefore, trivially we have
\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Z_{t}-Z_{s}\right|^{4}\right]\leq C(t-s)^{2}
for any
C>0
. When
0\leq s<t,
by the formula above for
Z
we have that

Z_{t}-Z_{s}=\int_{s\wedge T}^{t\wedge T}b(u,X_{u}^{\varepsilon},\bm{\nu}_{u}^{\varepsilon})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u+\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}-\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{s}.

(2.10)

Employing the linear growth condition on
b
and Proposition
2.8
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\int_{s\wedge T}^{t\wedge T}b(u,X_{u}^{\varepsilon},\bm{\nu}^{\varepsilon}_{u})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\right|^{4}\right]\leq C(t-s)^{4}\left(1+\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{u\leq T}|X_{u}^{\varepsilon}|^{4}\right]\right)=O((t-s)^{2})

(2.11)

uniformly in
\varepsilon
. By the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy inequality and the upper bound on
\sigma
, it is clear that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{t}-\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{s}\right|^{4}\right]=O((t-s)^{2})

(2.12)

uniformly in
\varepsilon
. Therefore, by Markov’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde{X}_{t_{1}}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}_{t_{2}}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}_{t_{3}}^{\varepsilon})\geq\eta\right]

\displaystyle\leq\eta^{-4}\mathbb{E}\left[\mathcal{H}_{\mathbb{R}}(\tilde{X}_{t_{1}}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}_{t_{2}}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{X}_{t_{3}}^{\varepsilon})^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{-4}\mathbb{E}\left[\left|Z_{t_{1}}-Z_{t_{2}}\right|^{4}+|Z_{t_{2}}-Z_{t_{3}}|^{4}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{-4}\left((t_{2}-t_{1})^{2}+(t_{3}-t_{2})^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\eta^{-4}(t_{3}-t_{1})^{2},

where all the constants hold uniformly in
\varepsilon
. To verify the second condition, we observe that for any
\eta>0
and
\delta<1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in(-1,-1+\delta)}|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}_{-1}|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in(-1,-1+\delta)}|X_{0-}-X_{0-}|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}\right]=0,\textnormal{ and}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in(\bar{T}-\delta,\bar{T})}|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{X}_{\bar{T}}|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in(\bar{T}-\delta,\bar{T})}|W_{t}-W_{\bar{T}}|\geq\frac{\eta}{2}\right]=O(\delta^{2}),

uniformly in
\varepsilon
. Hence, we have shown that

\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{t\in(-1,-1+\delta)}|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}_{-1}|+\sup_{t\in(\bar{T}-\delta,\bar{T})}|\tilde{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon}_{\bar{T}}|\geq\eta\right]=O(\delta^{2})\quad\text{for all}\quad\eta>0,\,\delta<1.

Therefore, together, conditions (I) and (II) show

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon}\left(\left\{x\in D_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;w_{M_{1}}(x,\delta)\geq\eta\right\}\right)=O(\delta^{2}),

for all
\delta<1
uniformly in
\varepsilon
. This shows condition (ii).

Based on the above, we can now employ Markov’s inequality and Prokhorov’s theorem to deduce that
\{\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon})\}_{\varepsilon>0}
is tight in
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{P}(D_{\mathbb{R}}))
. To begin, fix a
\gamma>0
. For any
l,k\in\mathbb{N}
, we may find a
\lambda_{l},\,\delta_{k,l}>0
such that

\displaystyle\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon}(A^{\complement}_{k,l})

\displaystyle<\gamma 2^{-(k+2l+1)}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\qquad\text{uniformly in $\varepsilon$,}

\displaystyle\text{where}\qquad\qquad\qquad A_{0,l}

\displaystyle=\{x\in D_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;\left\lVert x\right\rVert\leq\lambda_{l}\},

\displaystyle A_{k,l}

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in D_{\mathbb{R}}\;:\;w_{M_{1}}(x,\delta_{k,l})<\frac{1}{k+2l}\right\}.

We define
A_{l}=\cap_{k\geq 0}A_{k,l}
. By
[
33
, Theorem 12.12.2]
,
A_{l}
has compact closure in the
M_{1}
-topology. The closure of
A_{l}
is denoted by
\bar{A}_{l}
. Furthermore, by construction
\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon}(A_{l}^{\complement})\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon}(A_{k,l}^{\complement})\leq\gamma 4^{-l}
. By the subadditivity of measures and Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcup_{l=1}^{\infty}\left\{\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}(A^{\complement}_{l})>2^{-l}\right\}\right]\leq\sum_{l\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\left[\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}(A^{\complement}_{l})>2^{-l}\right]\leq\sum_{l\geq 1}2^{l}\mathbb{E}\left[\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon}(A^{\complement}_{l})\right]\leq\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{\gamma 2^{l}}{4^{l}}=\gamma.

(2.13)

Finally, let
K\coloneqq\{\mu\in\mathcal{P}(D_{\mathbb{R}})\,:\,\mu(\bar{A}_{l}^{\complement})\leq 2^{-l}\,\forall\,l\in\mathbb{N}\}
. As
D_{\mathbb{R}}
endowed with the
M_{1}
-topology is a Polish space, Prokhorov’s theorem may be applied, and it suffices to show that the set of measures
K
is tight; hence,
K

and

\tau_{0}(\eta)\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq-1\,:\,\eta_{t}\leq 0\}

(2.14)

with the convention that
\inf\{\emptyset\}=\bar{T}
. Our first result is that for
\operatorname{Law}(\mathbf{P}^{*})
-almost every measure
\mu
,
\mu
-almost every path
\eta\in D_{\mathbb{R}}
is constant on the interval

Lemma 2.10
.

For

Proof.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbf{P}^{*}\left(\inf_{s\leq t}\{\eta_{s}-\eta_{-1}\}<-\gamma\right)\right]

Corollary 2.12

(Hitting time continuity)
.

For
\operatorname{Law}(\mathbf{P}^{*})
-almost every measure
\mu
, we have that the hitting time map

-topology for
\mu
-almost every
\eta\in D_{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.11
, for
\operatorname{Law}(\mathbf{P}^{*})
-almost every measure
\mu
,
\mu
-almost every path
\eta\in D_{\mathbb{R}}
will have only downward jumps and one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\tau_{0}(\eta)<\bar{T}
and
\eta
takes a negative value on any neighbourhood of
\tau_{0}(\eta)
,

(ii)

\tau_{0}(\eta)=\bar{T}
and
\inf_{s\leq\bar{T}}\eta_{s}>0
,

(iii)

\tau_{0}(\eta)=\bar{T}
and
\eta_{\bar{T}}=0
.

If
(i)
holds, then by Lemma
A.3
,
\tau_{0}
is
M_{1}
-continuous at
\eta
. If
(ii)
holds–
\tau_{0}(\eta)=\bar{T}
and

–then for any approximating sequence
(\eta^{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset D_{\mathbb{R}}
in the
M_{1}
-topology, we must have

eventually as the parametric representations get arbitrarily close in the uniform topology. Therefore, as

eventually. Therefore,
\tau_{0}(\eta)

M_{1}
-continuous at
\eta

\tau_{0}(\eta)=\bar{T}
and
\eta_{\bar{T}}=0

\gamma>0
, with
\bar{T}-\gamma
being a continuity point, we must have

because
\eta
only jumps downwards. So for any approximating sequence
(\eta^{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset D_{\mathbb{R}}
in the

-topology, eventually

. Therefore,

is
M_{1}
-continuous at
\eta
.
∎

-continuous map, weak convergence of the loss function follows immediately.

Lemma 2.15

(Independence from idiosyncratic noise)

Let
\mathbf{P}^{*}
,
W^{0}
, and

Proof.

As

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathbb{P}^{*}_{\mu,\omega^{0},\omega},\,f\otimes g\otimes h\right\rangle

(\tilde{\mathbf{P}}^{\varepsilon},\,W^{0})
from

3
Stronger mode of convergence

One of the limitations of the method in Section
2
is that it fails to yield a strong solution. That is,
\mathbf{P}
is not equal to
\operatorname{Law}(X\mid W^{0})
. This is due to the mode of convergence employed being weak. To the best of our knowledge, there are no results in the existing literature relating to the existence of strong physical solutions in the setting with common noise. By Remark 2.5 from
[
28
]
, the existence of strong solutions in the setting when
b
,
\sigma
and
\rho
are functions of time only is shown; however, it remains unclear whether these solutions are physical or not.

The work introduced in
[
9
]
provided an alternative framework to construct solutions to systems with simplified dynamics and without common noise. This is done by a fixed-point approach. Notably, the constructed solutions possess a minimality property, meaning that any alternative solution to the system will dominate the solution obtained in
[
9
]
. By utilising the mean-field limit of a perturbed finite particle system approximation, the authors deduce that minimal solutions are in fact physical.

This section extends this work to the case with common noise. Provided more restrictive assumptions on the coefficients than those introduced in Assumption
2.2
, we provide an algorithm to construct minimal
W^{0}
-measurable solutions to the singular and smoothed system. Furthermore, we get almost sure convergence of the smoothed minimal system towards the singular minimal system. As a consequence, we are able to conclude that the minimal
W^{0}
-measurable solution is, in fact, physical. This provides an alternative method to show minimal solutions are physical in the setting of
[
9
]
.

We fix a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\,\mathcal{F},\,(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\,\mathbb{P})
that satisfies the usual conditions and supports two independent Brownian motions. This differs from Section
2
as the filtered probability space may change as we change
\varepsilon
. The mode of convergence was weak in Section
2
, therefore the smoothed systems needed not be defined on the same probability space. In this section, to be able to show a stronger mode of convergence, we require that our probability space and our Brownian motions are fixed because our methods employ a comparison principle approach.

We would like the loss process to be adapted and measurable with respect to the common noise. Hence, for measurability reasons, we define
\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}}
as the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
W^{0}
and augmented to contain all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets. We define
\mathcal{F}_{t}^{W^{0}}
to be the right continuous filtration generated by
W^{0}
that contains all the information up to time
t
and augmented to contain all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets. To be precise, that is

\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}}_{t}=\left(\bigcap_{s>t}\sigma(\{W_{u}^{0}\,:\,u\leq s\})\right)\vee\sigma(\{N\in\mathcal{F}\,:\,\mathbb{P}(N)=0\}).

As Brownian motion is continuous and has independent increments,
W^{0}
is still a standard Brownian motion under the filtration
(\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

We now propose our alternative method of solution construction. We will be considering the equation

\begin{cases}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}X_{t}=b(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t)\sqrt{1-\rho(t)^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}W_{t}+\sigma(t)\rho(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}W_{t}^{0}-\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}L_{t},\\
\hskip 12.03563pt\tau=\inf\{t>0\;:\;X_{t}\leq 0\},\\
\hskip 9.6456pt\mathbf{P}=\mathbb{P}\left[\left.X\in\,\cdot\,\right|\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}}\right],\quad\bm{\nu}_{t}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{P}\left[\left.X_{t}\in\cdot,\tau>t\right|\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}}_{t}\right],\\
\hskip 7.11317ptL_{t}=\mathbb{P}\left[\left.\tau\leq t\right|\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}}_{t}\right],\end{cases}

where

is a constant. The coefficients

,
\sigma
, and

are a measurable maps from

into

satisfying Assumption

. The system starts at time

with initial condition

which is almost surely positive. We require no further assumptions on the initial condition.

Given any solution

-measurable. Therefore we define

\underaccent{\bar}{L}_{t}\vcentcolon=\underset{s\downarrow t,\,s\in\mathbbm{D}}{\lim}\ell_{s}\qquad\forall\;t\geq 0.

By construction,
\underaccent{\bar}{L}_{t}
is a càdlàg
W^{0}
-measurable process with paths in

. A similar proof as that used in the end of Proposition
3.1
, shows that
\Gamma^{n}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\infty\}}]\xrightarrow{}\underaccent{\bar}{L}
almost surely in
M
. Hence by Proposition
3.1
,
\Gamma^{n+1}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\infty\}}]\xrightarrow{}\Gamma[{\underaccent{\bar}{L}}]
. As
\Gamma[{\underaccent{\bar}{L}}]
and
\underaccent{\bar}{L}
are càdlàg
W^{0}
-measurable processes that are limits of

\Gamma[{\underaccent{\bar}{L}}]=\underaccent{\bar}{L}
almost surely. Lastly, if

is any càdlàg

-measurable process that solves (

), then by Lemma

we have
\Gamma^{n}[\mathbbm{1}_{\{\infty\}}]\leq L

almost surely. Taking limit, we deduce
\underaccent{\bar}{L}\leq L
almost surely.
∎

We now turn our attention to the smoothed version of (

). We will work on the same filtered probability space
(\Omega,\,\mathcal{F},\,(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\,\mathbb{P})
as in (

) that satisfies the usual conditions and supports two independent Brownian motions. For an

\begin{cases}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}X_{t}^{\varepsilon}=b(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(t)\sqrt{1-\rho(t)^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}W_{t}+\sigma(t)\rho(t)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}W_{t}^{0}-\alpha\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{L}^{\varepsilon}_{t},\\
\hskip 9.33258pt\tau^{\varepsilon}=\inf\{t>0\;:\;X_{t}^{\varepsilon}\leq 0\},\\
\hskip 7.02765pt\mathbf{P}^{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{P}\left[\left.X^{\varepsilon}\in\,\cdot\,\right|\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}}\right],\quad\bm{\nu}_{t}^{\varepsilon}\vcentcolon=\mathbb{P}\left[\left.X_{t}^{\varepsilon}\in\cdot,\tau^{\varepsilon}>t\right|\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}}_{t}\right],\\
\hskip 8.2511ptL_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{P}^{\varepsilon}\left[\left.\tau^{\varepsilon}\leq t\right|\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}}_{t}\right],\quad\mathfrak{L}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)L_{s}^{\varepsilon}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\end{cases}

(3.4)

where
\alpha>0
is a constant. The coefficients

,
\sigma
,
\rho
and
\kappa
are a measurable maps from
\mathbb{R}
into
\mathbb{R}
satisfying Assumption

. The system starts at time

with the same initial condition,

, as in (

). As the assumptions on

is more general than those imposed in Section

to guarantee existence of solutions to (

). So, we propose an alternative proof to show existence of solutions. The proof follows in the same faith as Proposition
3.3
. We define the operator

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}[\ell]\vcentcolon=\Gamma[(\kappa^{\varepsilon}\ast\ell)],\quad\textnormal{where}\quad(\kappa^{\varepsilon}\ast\ell)\vcentcolon=\int_{0}^{\cdot}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(\cdot-s)\ell_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, solutions to (

\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
. A simple consequence of Proposition
3.1
, is that
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
is also continuous.

Corollary 3.4

(Continuity of
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
)
.

Let
\ell^{n}
and
\ell
be a sequence of adapted
W^{0}
-measurable processes that take values in
M
such that
\ell^{n}\xrightarrow{}\ell
almost surely in
M
. Then
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}[\ell^{n}]\xrightarrow{}\Gamma_{\varepsilon}[\ell]
almost surely in
M
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
, it is sufficient to show that the map
\tilde{\ell}\mapsto\kappa^{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{\ell}
is continuous on
M
. It is clear that if we implicitly define the value of
\kappa^{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{\ell}
to be
1
at
\infty
, then it is an element of
M
. Let
\tilde{\ell}^{n}
and
\tilde{\ell}
be deterministic functions in
M
such that
\tilde{\ell}^{n}\xrightarrow{}\tilde{\ell}
in
M
. That is, we have pointwise convergence on the continuity points of
\tilde{\ell}
. As
\kappa\in\mathcal{W}^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, it has a continuous representative. So without loss of generality, we take
\kappa
to be this representative. Hence
\kappa
is bounded on compacts, so an easy application of the Dominated Convergence Theorem gives

\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}(\kappa^{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{\ell}^{n})_{t}=\lim_{n\xrightarrow{}\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\tilde{\ell}^{n}_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\tilde{\ell}_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=(\kappa^{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{\ell})_{t}

∎

As convolution with non-negative functions preserves monotonicity, we further deduce that
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
is also monotonic by Lemma
3.2
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\ell^{1}
and
\ell^{2}
be
W^{0}
-measurable processes with paths in
M
such that
\ell^{1}\leq\ell^{2}
almost surely, then
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}[\ell^{1}]\leq\Gamma_{\varepsilon}[\ell^{2}]
almost surely.

With monotonicity and continuity of the operator

in hand, we have all the necessary results to deduce the existence of solutions to (
3.4
).

Proposition 3.6
.

There exists a càdlàg
W^{0}
-measurable process
\underaccent{\bar}{L}^{\varepsilon}
which solves (
3.4
) and for any other càdlàg
W^{0}
-measurable process

which satisfies (
3.4
), we have
\underaccent{\bar}{L}^{\varepsilon}\leq L^{\varepsilon}
almost surely.

Proof.

By employing Corollary
3.4
and Corollary
3.5
, this proof is verbatim to that of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

The purpose of
\kappa^{\varepsilon}
in (
3.4
) is two-fold. Firstly, it smoothens the effect of the feedback component on the system, hence preventing the system from jumping and making it continuous. Secondly, it delays the effect of
L_{t}^{\varepsilon}
of the system. Intuitively, one would expect that the system with instantaneous feedback, i.e. (
3.1
), will be dominated by that with delayed feedback. Furthermore, intuitively as we decrease
\varepsilon
, then the system with the smaller value of
\varepsilon
should be dominated by one with a larger value. This is because as
\varepsilon
decreases, the rate at which the feedback is felt by the system increases.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
\varepsilon,\,\tilde{\varepsilon}>0
such that
\tilde{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon
, it holds that

\underaccent{\bar}{L}^{\varepsilon}\leq\underaccent{\bar}{L}.\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\underaccent{\bar}{L}^{\tilde{\varepsilon}}\geq\underaccent{\bar}{L}^{\varepsilon}

almost surely.

Proof.

For any deterministic functions
\tilde{\ell}^{1},\,\tilde{\ell}^{2}\in M
such that
\tilde{\ell}^{1}\leq\tilde{\ell}^{2}
, a straightforward computation shows that
(\kappa^{\varepsilon}\ast\tilde{\ell}^{1})\leq\tilde{\ell}^{2}
. Furthermore, for any
t>0
, we have:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(s)\tilde{\ell}_{t-s}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\varepsilon^{-1}\kappa(s\varepsilon^{-1})\tilde{\ell}_{t-s}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\frac{t\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}}\tilde{\varepsilon}\kappa(\tilde{s}\tilde{\varepsilon}^{-1})\tilde{\ell}_{t-\frac{\tilde{s}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{s}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(\tilde{s})\tilde{\ell}_{t-\tilde{s}}^{1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{s}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(\tilde{s})\tilde{\ell}_{t-\tilde{s}}^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{s}.

The second equality follows from employing the substitution
\tilde{s}\tilde{\varepsilon}^{-1}={s}{\varepsilon}^{-1}
, and the third inequality follows from the fact that
\tilde{\varepsilon}\varepsilon^{-1}<1
, hence
\frac{t\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}<t
and
\tilde{\ell}_{t-\frac{\tilde{s}\tilde{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}}^{1}\leq\tilde{\ell}_{t-\tilde{s}}^{1}
. The claim now follows from the monotonicity from Proposition
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
.
∎

3.2
Convergence of minimal solutions

From now on, we will fix a sequence of positive real numbers
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 1}
that converge to zero. As we have established that
\underaccent{\bar}{L}^{\varepsilon}
is a decreasing process in
\varepsilon
by Lemma

, we shall exploit this structure to construct a solution to (
3.1
). This will be a
W^{0}
-measurable solution that will be dominated by every other

-measurable solution. Therefore, we may conclude that this solution must coincide with
\underaccent{\bar}{L}

Theorem 3.8

(Almost sure convergence)
.

Let
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive real numbers that converges to zero. Let
(\underaccent{\bar}{X}^{\varepsilon},\underaccent{\bar}{L}^{\varepsilon})
denote the

-measurable solution to (
3.4
) constructed in Proposition
3.6
, and
(\underaccent{\bar}{X},\underaccent{\bar}{L})
denote the
W^{0}
-measurable solution to (
3.1
) constructed in Proposition
3.3
. Then by considering the extended system

\tilde{\underaccent{\bar}{X}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}X_{0-}\hskip 19.56126ptt\in[-1,0),\\
\underaccent{\bar}{X}_{t}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\qquad\,\,t\geq 0,\end{cases}\qquad\tilde{\underaccent{\bar}{X}}\vcentcolon=\begin{cases}X_{0-}\hskip 19.56126ptt\in[-1,0),\\
\underaccent{\bar}{X}_{t}\qquad\,\,t\geq 0,\end{cases}

\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{\underaccent{\bar}{X}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\mid\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}})\xrightarrow{}\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{\underaccent{\bar}{X}}\mid\mathcal{F}^{W^{0}})
almost surely in

\underaccent{\bar}{L}
almost surely in

and

Proof.

As

. For simplicity, we will denote

4
Rates of convergence

One of the limitations of the previous arguments is that they fail to yield a rate at which the convergence will occur. For simple systems, that is in the case of no drift, no common noise and a volatility parameter set to
1
, we employ a coupling argument to derive the speed of convergence, which depends on the regularity of
L
.

The regularity of the loss process,
L
, has been established in the literature,
[
11
,
17
]
, for a suitable class of initial conditions. In this setting, we not only have almost-sure convergence of the stochastic process along a subsequence, but we will have uniform convergence on any time domain before the time that the regularity of
L
decays. These results are in some sense parallel to those presented in
[
21
]
, with the crucial difference that we are looking at the rate of convergence of systems with smoothed loss to the limiting system, as opposed to the convergence of timestepping schemes that approximate the limiting system.
Note also that
[
22
,
8
]
provide convergence orders for timestepping schemes in the case of Hölder continuous
L
, but convergence without order in the case jump case.

To be precise, we will be considering the following system of equations

\begin{cases}X_{t}^{\varepsilon}=X_{0-}+W_{t}-\alpha\mathfrak{L}_{t}^{\varepsilon},\\
\tau^{\varepsilon}=\inf{\{t\geq 0\,:\,X_{t}^{\varepsilon}\leq 0\}},\\
L_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau^{\varepsilon}\leq t\right),\\
\mathfrak{L}_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)L_{s}^{\varepsilon}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\end{cases}\begin{cases}X_{t}=X_{0-}+W_{t}-\alpha{L}_{t},\\
\tau=\inf{\{t\geq 0\,:\,X_{t}\leq 0\}},\\
L_{t}=\mathbb{P}\left(\tau\leq t\right),\end{cases}

(4.1)

where
t\geq 0
,
W
is a standard Brownian motion,
\kappa
is a function from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
satisfying Assumption
2.2
and
\operatorname{supp}(\kappa)\subset[0,\,1]
.

4.1
Theoretical estimates on rates of convergence

The main result of this section is the following:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(X,\,L)_{t\geq 0}
be a physical solution to (
4.1
) with initial condition
X_{0-}
. Suppose further that
X_{0-}
admits a bounded initial density
V_{0-}
s.t.

V_{0-}(x)\leq Cx^{\beta}\mathbbm{1}_{\{x\leq x_{*}\}}+D\mathbbm{1}_{\{x>x_{*}\}}\quad\forall\,x>0,

where
C,\,D,\,x_{*},\,\beta>0
are constants with
\beta<1
. Then, for any
t_{0}\in(0,\,t_{\mathrm{explode}})
there exists a constant
\tilde{K}=\tilde{K}(t_{0})
s.t.

\sup\limits_{s\in[0,\,t_{0}]}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\leq\tilde{K}\varepsilon^{\beta/2},

where

t_{\mathrm{explode}}\vcentcolon=\sup\{t>0\;:\;\left\lVert L\right\rVert_{H^{1}(0,\,t)}<+\infty\}\in(0,\,+\infty].

(4.2)

Proof.

By assumption, we are in the setting of
[
17
, Theorem 1.8]
. Hence, we have a unique solution,
L
, to (
4.1
) up to the time
t_{\mathrm{explode}}
defined as in (
4.2
). Also, for all
t_{0}\in(0,\,t_{\mathrm{explode}})
there exists
K=K(t_{0})
s.t.
L\in\mathcal{S}(\frac{1-\beta}{2},\,K,\,t_{0})
where

\mathcal{S}\left(\frac{1-\beta}{2},\,K,\,t_{0}\right)\vcentcolon=\{l\in H^{1}(0,\,t_{0})\;:\;{l}_{t}^{\prime}\leq Kt^{-\frac{1-\beta}{2}}\text{ for almost all t }\in[0,\,t_{0}]\}

Step 1: Regularity of
L
.

Choose
t_{0}\in(0,\,t_{\mathrm{explode}})
. As
L\in H^{1}(0,\,t_{0})
, for Lebesgue a.e.
t,\,s\in(0,\,t_{0})
we may write

L_{t}-L_{s}=\int_{s}^{t}{L}_{s}^{\prime}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}\leq K(1-\gamma)^{-1}(t^{1-\gamma}-s^{1-\gamma}),

where the last inequality is from
L\in\mathcal{S}(\gamma,\,K,\,t_{0})
with
\gamma=(1-\beta)/2
. This implies

\frac{|L_{t}-L_{s}|}{|t-s|^{1-\gamma}}\leq\frac{K(t^{1-\gamma}-s^{1-\gamma})}{(1-\gamma)|t-s|^{1-\gamma}}\leq\frac{K}{1-\gamma}.

The last inequality is due to the subadditivity of concave functions. Therefore,
L_{t}
is almost everywhere
\frac{\beta+1}{2}
- Hölder continuous.

Step 2: Decomposition of
L
into an integral form.

We may write
L
as

L_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)L_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}+\left[1-\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}\right]L_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)(L_{t}-L_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}.

Observe

\left[1-\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}\right]L_{t}\leq\frac{2K\varepsilon^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)(L_{t}-L_{s})\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}\leq\frac{K\varepsilon^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}.

Therefore

L_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(t-s)L_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{s}+\Psi^{\varepsilon}(t)\qquad\text{ where }|\Psi^{\varepsilon}(t)|\leq\frac{3K\varepsilon^{1-\gamma}}{1-\gamma}\,\forall\,t\in[0,\,t_{0}].

(4.3)

Step 3: Comparison between the delayed loss and instantaneous loss.

By Lemma
3.7
, we have that
L\geq L^{\varepsilon}
, in the same spirit as
[
17
, Proposition 3.1]
,

\displaystyle 0\leq L_{t}-L_{t}^{\varepsilon}\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{L_{u}-\mathfrak{L}_{u}^{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{t-u}}L^{\prime}_{u}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u\leq c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{u}\kappa^{\varepsilon}(u-s)\frac{L_{s}-L_{s}^{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{t-u}}L^{\prime}_{u}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u+c_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\Psi^{\varepsilon}(s)L^{\prime}_{s}}{\sqrt{t-s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

where
c_{1}=\alpha\sqrt{2/\pi}
and the second inequality follows by (
4.3
). As
L\in\mathcal{S}(\gamma,\,K,\,t_{0})
,

0\leq\left|L_{t}-L_{t}^{\varepsilon}\right|\leq Kc_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{u}\frac{\kappa^{\varepsilon}(u-s)\left|L_{s}-L_{s}^{\varepsilon}\right|}{u^{\gamma}\sqrt{t-u}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u+Kc_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left|\Psi^{\varepsilon}(s)\right|}{s^{\gamma}\sqrt{t-s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

By (
4.3
), we may find a constant
C_{K,t_{0},\alpha}
such that the second term above is bounded by
C_{K,t_{0},\alpha}\varepsilon^{1-\gamma}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle 0\leq|L_{t}-{L}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|\leq Kc_{1}\int_{0}^{t}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+C_{K,t_{0},\alpha}\varepsilon^{1-\gamma},

(4.4)

where

\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,s)=\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\kappa^{\varepsilon}(u-s)}{u^{\gamma}\sqrt{t-u}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u.

Step 4: Bounds on
\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,s)

As
\rho^{\varepsilon}
depends on
t
and
s
, we may not immediately apply Grönwall’s lemma or any of its generalisations. To this end, we first construct upper bounds to relax the dependence of
\rho^{\varepsilon}
on
t
and
s
via the function
\kappa
. In the case when
0\leq t-s\leq\varepsilon

\displaystyle\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\kappa^{\varepsilon}(u-s)}{u^{\gamma}\sqrt{t-u}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u=\int_{0}^{\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}}\frac{\kappa(\tilde{u})}{(\varepsilon\tilde{u}+s)^{\gamma}\sqrt{t-s-\varepsilon\tilde{u}}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{u}\leq\frac{\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{s^{\gamma}\varepsilon^{1/2}}\int_{0}^{\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\tilde{u}}{\sqrt{\frac{t-s}{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}(t-s)^{1/2}}{s^{\gamma}\varepsilon}\leq\frac{2\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{s^{\gamma}(t-s)^{1/2}},

where we used the substitution
\tilde{u}=(u-s)\varepsilon^{-1}
. In the case when
{t-s}>\varepsilon
, as the support of
\kappa^{\varepsilon}
is in
[0,\,\varepsilon]

\displaystyle\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,s)

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\kappa^{\varepsilon}(u-s)}{u^{\gamma}\sqrt{t-u}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u=\int^{s+\varepsilon}_{s}\frac{\kappa^{\varepsilon}(u-s)}{u^{\gamma}\sqrt{t-u}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{s^{\gamma}\varepsilon}\int^{s+\varepsilon}_{s}\frac{\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}u}{\sqrt{t-u}}=\frac{2\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}}{s^{\gamma}}\left[\frac{(t-s)^{1/2}-(t-s-\varepsilon)^{1/2}}{\varepsilon}\right].

Step 5: Grönwall type argument

4.2
Numerical simulations

Lastly, we investigate the convergence rate of the smoothed loss function towards the singular loss function through numerical simulations. The estimates from the previous section, for the case without common noise, give upper bounds on the rate at which the smoothed system will approach the singular system, prior to the decline in regularity of the singular loss function. The proofs employed in the analysis utilised relatively crude upper bounds, prompting the question of whether the obtained rates are optimal.

To the best of our knowledge, there is no existing literature on the regularity of the loss process in the presence of common noise. Consequently, the theoretical methods employed earlier may not be applicable in this scenario. Nevertheless, we can still explore the convergence rate numerically in this context as well. We consider the simplest setting with common noise,

Lemma A.1
.

Suppose that
\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon_{n}}=\operatorname{Law}(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon_{n}},\,\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}})
converges weakly in
\mathcal{P}(D_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{R}})
to
\tilde{P}^{*}
, where
\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
are the extensions of
{X}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
and
{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
, respectively. Let
X^{*}
and
Y^{*}
be the canonical processes on
D_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{R}}
such that for
(\eta,\,\omega)\in D_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{R}}
,
X^{*}(\eta,\,\omega)=\eta
and
Y^{*}(\eta,\,\omega)=\omega
. Then, under
\tilde{P}^{*}
,
Y^{*}
is a martingale with respect to the filtration generated by
(X^{*},\,Y^{*})
with quadratic variation

\left\langle Y^{*}\right\rangle_{t}=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &0&\qquad&t\in[-1,\,0),\\
&\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\,X^{*}_{s})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&\qquad&t\in[0,\,T],\\
&\int_{0}^{T}\sigma(s,\,X_{s}^{*})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+(t-T)&\qquad&t\in(T,\,\bar{T}].\end{aligned}\end{cases}

Proof.

Set
\tilde{P}^{*}
to be the limit point of
(\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon_{n}})_{n\geq 0}
and

\mathbb{T}^{\tilde{P}^{*}}\coloneqq\left\{t\in[-1,\,\bar{T}]\;:\;\tilde{P}^{*}(\eta_{t}=\eta_{t-})=1\right\}.

Now for any
s_{0},\,t_{0}\in\mathbb{T}^{\tilde{P}^{*}}
with
s_{0}<t_{0}
and
\{s_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}\subset[-1,\,s_{0}]\cap\mathbb{T}^{\tilde{P}^{*}}
, we define the function

F\,:\,D_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R},\quad(\eta,\omega)\mapsto(\omega_{t_{0}}-\omega_{s_{0}})\prod_{i=1}^{k}f_{i}(\eta_{s_{i}},\omega_{s_{i}}),

for arbitrary
f_{i}\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(D_{\mathbb{R}}\times\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{R}})
. To show that
Y^{*}
is a martingale, it is sufficient to show that
\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}^{*}}[F(X^{*},\,Y^{*})]=0
.

As
\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\implies\tilde{P}^{*}
, then by Skorokhod’s Representation Theorem, see
[
2
, Theorem 7.6]
, there exist
((x^{n},\,y^{n}))_{n\geq 1}
and
(x,\,y)
defined on the same background space such that
(x^{n},\,y^{n})
converges to
(x,\,y)
almost surely in
(D_{\mathbb{R}},\,M_{1})\times(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{R}},\,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\infty})
with
\operatorname{Law}(x^{n},\,y^{n})=\tilde{P}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
and
\operatorname{Law}(x,y)=\tilde{P}^{*}
. Now for any
p>1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|F(x^{n},\,y^{n})\right|^{p}\right]\leq C\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq\bar{T}}|\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}_{s}^{\varepsilon_{n}}|^{p}\right]\leq C,

where
C
is a constant that changes from line to line and depends only on
p,\,\sigma,\,T
and the
f_{i}
’s but is independent of
\varepsilon
. Therefore,
F(x^{n},\,y^{n})
is
L^{p}
-bounded uniformly in
n
. For
t\in\{t_{0},\,s_{0},\,s_{1},\,\ldots,\,s_{k}\}
,
t
is an almost sure continuity point of
x
. Therefore, by the properties of
M_{1}
-convergence,
(x^{n}_{t},\,y^{n}_{t})
converges to
(x_{t},\,y_{t})
almost surely. Hence, we have almost sure convergence of

to

. Vitali’s Convergence Theorem states that almost sure convergence and uniform integrability imply convergence of means, hence

\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}^{*}}[F(X^{*},\,Y^{*})]=\mathbb{E}[F(x,\,y)]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F(x^{n},\,y^{n})]=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[F(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon_{n}},\,\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}})]=0,

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\mathbb{E}[F(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon_{n}},\,\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}})]=0
for all
n
as
\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
is a martingale. Therefore, by a monotone class theorem argument,
Y^{*}
is a continuous local martingale.

Recall
x^{n}\to x
almost surely in
(D_{\mathbb{R}},\,M_{1})

, see
[
33

. As
\sigma

\mathcal{C}^{1,\,2}

2.2

\sigma(s,x^{n}_{s})\to\sigma(s,\,x_{s})
for a set of

[0,\,T]
. Furthermore, as
\sigma
is bounded, by the Bounded Convergence Theorem

\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\,x^{n}_{s})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\to\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\,x_{s})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

(A.1)

almost surely for any
t\in[0,\,T]
. Set

\left\langle Y\right\rangle_{t}=\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &0&\qquad&t\in[-1,\,0),\\
&\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(s,\,X_{s})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s&\qquad&t\in[0,\,T],\\
&\int_{0}^{T}\sigma(s,\,X_{s})^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+(t-T)&\qquad&t\in(T,\,\bar{T}],\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where

is any one of

\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}
and

\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{\tilde{P}^{*}}\left[F(X^{*},\,(Y^{*})^{2}-\langle Y^{*}\rangle)\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[F(x^{n},\,(y^{n})^{2}-\langle y^{n}\rangle)\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left[F(\tilde{X}^{\varepsilon_{n}},\,(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}})^{2}-\langle\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\rangle)\right]=0,

where the last equality follows from the fact that
(\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}})^{2}-\langle\tilde{\mathcal{Y}}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\rangle

. This completes the proof.

Lemma A.2
.

Consider the process
Z_{t}=M_{t}+tX
for
t\in[-1,\,\bar{T}]
, where
M_{t}
is a continuous local martingale with
c_{M}(t-s)\leq\langle M\rangle_{t}-\langle M\rangle_{s}\leq C_{M}(t-s)
for any
0\leq s<t
almost surely, and
X
is a non-negative random variable such that
\mathbb{E}[X]<\infty
. Then, for any stopping time
\tau
where
\tau\geq 0
almost surely, then

\mathbb{P}\left[\inf_{s\in(\tau,(\tau+h)\wedge\bar{T})}\{Z_{s}-Z_{\tau}\}\geq 0,\,\tau<\bar{T}\right]=0,

for any
h>0
.

Proof.

In the case when
M
is simply a Brownian motion, the result readily follows from the Strong Markov Property and the standard properties of Brownian motion. As
M
is a continuous local martingale, we may view it as a (random) time-changed Brownian motion. We exploit this fact to show the claim. To begin, fix a
\Delta\in(0,h)
,
\lambda>0
and set
\bar{\tau}\coloneqq\tau\wedge(\bar{T}-\Delta)
. Then, conditioning on the event
E\coloneqq\{\tau\leq\bar{T}-\Delta\}\cap\{X\leq\lambda\}
and its complement, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left[\underset{s\in(\tau,(\tau+h)\wedge\bar{T})}{\inf}\{Z_{s}-Z_{\tau}\}>-\Delta,\,\tau<\bar{T}\right]&\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\underset{s\in(\bar{\tau},\bar{\tau}+\Delta)}{\inf}\{M_{s}-M_{\bar{\tau}}+(s-\bar{\tau})\lambda\}>-\Delta\right]\\
&\qquad+\mathbb{P}\left[E^{\complement},\,\tau<\bar{T}\right].\end{split}

(A.2)

Focussing on the first term, we observe

\mathbb{P}\left[\underset{s\in(\bar{\tau},\bar{\tau}+\delta)}{\inf}\{M_{s}-M_{\bar{\tau}}+(s-\bar{\tau})\lambda\}>-\Delta\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\underset{s\in(\bar{\tau},\bar{\tau}+\delta)}{\inf}\{M_{s}-M_{\bar{\tau}}\}>-\Delta(1+\lambda)\right]

By the Dubins-Schwarz Theorem,
M
is a time-changed Brownian motion. Therefore there exists a Brownian Motion
B
such that

\mathbb{P}\left[\underset{s\in(\bar{\tau},\bar{\tau}+\delta)}{\inf}\{M_{s}-M_{\bar{\tau}}\}>-\Delta(1+\lambda)\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\underset{s\in(\bar{\tau},\bar{\tau}+\delta)}{\inf}\{B_{\langle M\rangle_{s}-\langle M\rangle_{\bar{\tau}}}\}>-\Delta(1+\lambda)\right]

Now as
\bar{\tau}=\tau\wedge(\bar{T}-\Delta)>0
almost surely,
\langle M\rangle_{s}-\langle M\rangle_{\bar{\tau}}\geq c_{M}(s-\bar{\tau})
for any
s>\bar{\tau}
almost surely. So

\mathbb{P}\left[\underset{s\in(\bar{\tau},\bar{\tau}+\delta)}{\inf}\{B_{\langle M\rangle_{s}-\langle M\rangle_{\bar{\tau}}}\}>-\Delta(1+\lambda)\right]\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\underset{s\in(0,\Delta)}{\inf}\{B_{c_{M}s}\}>-\Delta(1+\lambda)\right]

By the reflection principle of Brownian motion, we have

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{P}\left[B_{\gamma c_{M}}\leq-\Delta(1+\lambda)\right]

\displaystyle=2(2\pi)^{-1/2}\int_{-\infty}^{-\Delta^{1/2}c_{M}^{-1/2}(1+\lambda)}e^{\frac{-y^{2}}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}y\geq 1-2\Delta^{1/2}(1+\lambda)(2\pi c_{M})^{-1/2}.

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\underset{s\in(0,\Delta)}{\inf}\{B_{c_{M}s}\}>-\Delta(1+\lambda)\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2\Delta^{1/2}(1+\lambda)(2\pi c_{M})^{-1/2}

Setting

) converges to 0 as we send

∎

Lemma A.3

(Convergence of Stopping Times)

Step 2:

\displaystyle\liminf_{k\to\infty}z^{n_{k}}_{\tau_{0}(z^{n_{k}})}

Lemma A.4

Proposition A.10

(Grönwall Type Inequality I)
.

Suppose
a,\,\tilde{\alpha},\,\tilde{\beta}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that
a\geq 0,\,0<\tilde{\beta}<1,\,\tilde{\alpha}>0
. Suppose
g(t)
is a nonnegative, nondecreasing continuous function defined on
0\leq t<T
,
g(t)\leq M
(constant), and suppose
u(t)
is nonnegative and bounded on
0\leq t<T
with

u(t)\leq a+g(t)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\tilde{\beta}-1}s^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}u(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}s

on this interval. Then

u(t)\leq a\left[1+\sum_{n\geq 1}g_{t}^{n}C_{n}t^{n(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}\right],\quad 0\leq t<T,

where

C_{0}\coloneqq 1,C_{n+1}\coloneqq B\left((n+1)\tilde{\alpha}+n\tilde{\beta}-n,\tilde{\beta}\right)C_{n},

with
B(\tilde{\alpha},\,\tilde{\beta})\coloneqq\int_{0}^{1}(1-\tilde{s})^{\tilde{\beta}-1}\tilde{s}^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}\tilde{s}.

Proof.

Let
B\phi_{t}=g_{t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\tilde{\beta}-1}s^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}\phi_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}s
,
t\geq 0
, for localling integrable functions
\phi
. Then

implies

u_{t}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}B^{k}a+B^{n}u_{t}.

Let us prove that

(A.15)

and
B^{n}u_{t}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
for each t in
0\leq t<T
.

Step 1:
B^{n}(1)_{t}\leq C_{n}t^{n(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}g_{t}^{n}
.

For
n=1
,

\displaystyle B(1)_{t}

\displaystyle=g_{t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\tilde{\beta}-1}s^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}s,\quad\text{set }\tilde{s}=s/t

\displaystyle=g_{t}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\tilde{\beta}-1}(1-\tilde{s})^{\tilde{\beta}-1}t^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}\tilde{s}^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}\tilde{s}

\displaystyle=g_{t}t^{\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1}B(\tilde{\alpha},\,\tilde{\beta}).

Now, suppose the claim is true for
n=k
, then for
n=k+1

\displaystyle B^{k+1}(1)_{t}

\displaystyle=g_{t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\tilde{\beta}-1}s^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}B^{k}(1)_{s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}s,

\displaystyle\leq g_{t}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\tilde{\beta}-1}s^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}s^{k(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}g_{s}^{k}C_{k}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}s,\quad\text{by above}

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}g_{t}^{k+1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\tilde{\beta}-1}s^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}s^{k(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}s,\quad\text{set }\tilde{s}=s/t

\displaystyle=C_{k}g_{t}^{k+1}\int_{0}^{1}t^{\tilde{\beta}-1}(1-\tilde{s})^{\tilde{\beta}-1}t^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}\tilde{s}^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}t^{k(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}\tilde{s}^{k(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}t\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}\tilde{s},

\displaystyle=C_{k+1}g^{k+1}_{t}t^{(k+1)(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}.

Hence the claim is true by the principle of induction.

Step 2:

We observe that
B
is monotone, that is if
\phi_{1}\leq\phi_{2}\,\forall t\in[0,\,T]
, then by the nonnegativity of
g
we have
B(\phi_{1})\leq B(\phi_{2})
. Also by the linearity of integration, we see also that
B
is a linear operator. Therefore,

B(u)_{t}=g(t)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\tilde{\beta}-1}s^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}u(s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}s\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}g(t)\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\tilde{\beta}-1}s^{\tilde{\alpha}-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}{}s=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}B(1)_{t}

Therefore, by linearity, monotonicity and step 1

B^{n}(u)_{t}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}B^{n}(1)_{t}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}C_{n}g_{t}^{n}t^{n(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}

.

By Gautschi’s inequality,
[
16
]
, we have that for all
x>0
and

x^{1-s}<\frac{\Gamma(x+1)}{\Gamma(x+s)}<(x+1)^{1-s}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{C_{n+1}}{C_{n}}

\displaystyle=B\left((n+1)\tilde{\alpha}+n\tilde{\beta}-n,\,\tilde{\beta}\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma\left((n+1)\tilde{\alpha}+n\tilde{\beta}-n\right)\Gamma(\tilde{\beta})}{\Gamma\left((n+1)\tilde{\alpha}+(n+1)\tilde{\beta}-n\right)},

\displaystyle=\Gamma(\tilde{\beta})\left[\frac{\Gamma\left((n+1)\tilde{\alpha}+(n+1)\tilde{\beta}-(n+1)+1-\tilde{\beta}\right)}{\Gamma\left((n+1)\tilde{\alpha}+(n+1)\tilde{\beta}-(n+1)+1\right)}\right],

and

is summable.

Step 4: Summability of

By step

\frac{C_{n+1}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}t^{(n+1)(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}g_{t}^{n+1}}{C_{n}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}t^{n(\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1)}g_{t}^{n}}=\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}t^{\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1}g_{t}\frac{C_{n+1}}{C_{n}}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow+\infty}0

Step 5:

As

\displaystyle|L_{t}-{L}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|\leq Kc_{1}\int_{0}^{t}2\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}(t-s)^{-1/2}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+C_{K,t_{0}}\varepsilon.

By step 4 case 2 and (
A.16
), we have for
t>\varepsilon

\displaystyle|L_{t}-{L}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq Kc_{1}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+Kc_{1}\int_{t-\varepsilon}^{t}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\rho^{\varepsilon}(t,s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+C_{K,t_{0}}\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq 2Kc_{1}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}\left[\frac{(t-s)^{1/2}-(t-s-\varepsilon)^{1/2}}{\varepsilon}\right]|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}+2Kc_{1}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{t-\varepsilon}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+C_{K,t_{0}}\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq 2Kc_{1}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{j\geq 2}\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}+2Kc_{1}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+C_{K,t_{0}}\varepsilon,

where the second term in the last line captures the higher order terms from Taylor’s Theorem. The Monotone Convergence Theorem allows us to swap integrals and sums. We now consider

\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

We shall proceed in two cases.

Case 1:

\varepsilon<t\leq 2\varepsilon

\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s={\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}

where the first inequality follows from the fact that
(-2j+1)/2<0
as
j\geq 2
and
t-s\in[\varepsilon,\,t]
for
s\in[0,\,t-\varepsilon]
.

Case 2:

t>2\varepsilon

We observe that

\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s={\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}

and

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\int_{\varepsilon}^{t-\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\frac{2}{2j-3}\left.(t-s)^{\frac{-2j+3}{2}}\right|_{s=\varepsilon}^{t-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}}{2j-3}\left[\varepsilon^{\frac{-2j+3}{2}}-(t-\varepsilon)^{\frac{-2j+3}{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{-2j+3}{2}}}{2j-3}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{1/2}}{2j-3}\leq{2\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{1/2}},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
j\geq 2
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{j-1}\int_{\varepsilon}^{t-\varepsilon}(t-s)^{\frac{-2j+1}{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq{3\tilde{C}_{j}\varepsilon^{1/2}},

for all
t>\varepsilon
. As
L
and
{L}^{\varepsilon}
are bounded by
1
, so independent of
t
being greater or less than
\varepsilon
we have

\displaystyle|L_{t}-{L}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq 2Kc_{1}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+{12Kc_{1}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\varepsilon^{1/2}\sum_{j\geq 2}\tilde{C}_{j}}+C_{K,t_{0}}\varepsilon

\displaystyle=2Kc_{1}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-1/2}s^{-\gamma}|L_{s}-{L}_{s}^{\varepsilon}|\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s+C_{K,t_{0}}\varepsilon^{1/2},

for any
t\in[0,\,t_{0}]
. Lastly by Proposition
A.10
using
\tilde{\beta}=1/2
and
\tilde{\alpha}=1
, then
\tilde{\alpha}+\tilde{\beta}-1>0
as
\gamma<1/2
and

\displaystyle|L_{t}-{L}^{\varepsilon}_{t}|

\displaystyle=C_{K,t_{0}}\varepsilon^{1/2}\sum_{n\geq 0}(2Kc_{1}t_{0}^{1/2}\left\lVert\kappa\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}})^{n}C_{n}.

This completes the proof.

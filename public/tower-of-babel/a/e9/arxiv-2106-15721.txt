Extraction of autonomous quantum coherences
Artur Slobodeniuk, Tomáš Novotný, Radim Filip

Abstract
Quantum coherence is an essential resource to gain advantage over classical physics and technology. Recently, it has been proposed that a low-temperature environment can induce quantum coherence of a spin without an external coherent pump. We address a critical question if such coherence is extractable by a weak coupling to an output system dynamically affecting back the spin-environment coupling. Describing the entire mechanism, we prove that such extraction is generically possible for output spins (also oscillators or fields) and, as well, in a fermionic analogue of such a process. We compare the internal spin coherence and output coherence over temperature and characteristic frequencies. The proposed optimal coherence extraction opens paths for the upcoming experimental tests with atomic and solid-state systems.

Is such autonomous coherence extractable, i.e. can an output system continuously and even weakly connected to the spin extract the steady-state coherence? Would such weak extraction not disturb the coherence generation? These essential questions are already critical for a fundamental understanding and detectability of quantum coherence as a resource. Simultaneously, to answer them will be necessary for more autonomous quantum devices and their applications. Apparently, our current picture about an extraction of coherence caused by an external drive is not sufficient. It assumes that strong coherent external drive is largely immune to coupling the output system. As the autonomous coherences appear by an interaction with environment, their extraction is still an open issue.

The existing experimental proposal
[

The extraction assumed a weak linear coupling of the cavity field to the spin in a simple static approximation without any back action on the spin and coupling with the environment. For the cavity-field frequency much below the spin frequency and mean spin population almost unchanged (see equation (70) in Supplementary Notes
[

[

Here, we build upon that analysis and propose a minimal extraction mechanism suitable for fields, oscillators, spins, and fermions. We compare that static approximation to our full dynamical description with an essential back action of extraction on spin-environment interaction. In this way, we thoroughly answer the question about autonomous coherence extractability. To provide an in-depth analysis, we adopt the spin as the output system, as illustrated in
Fig.
1

For the sake of completeness, we present an adequate extension to fermionic systems. Importantly, we confirm that the autonomous coherence is generically extractable to all these output systems. It paves a road to the first experimental tests. In the full dynamical description, extracted coherence nontrivially depends on the environment temperature and characteristic system frequencies. For the spin system, we reach coherence maximum asymptotically for a low temperature. Interestingly, the coherence maximum appears at finite temperature for a fermionic analogue. It is stimulating for further proposals to obtain autonomous quantum coherences.

to remove the constant terms, which do not affect the quantum dynamics significantly.

\displaystyle H_{F}=

4
Summary

We have hereby confirmed the extractibility of autonomous quantum coherence for all basic quantum systems coupled to a generic bosonic environment. The results justified a qualitative prediction from static approximation

[
40
]
. They proved that the back action of a weak extraction is fortunately not critical for the coherence generation and extraction in the low-temperature limit. Moreover, we obtained the required frequency and temperature dependences that are not reliably predictable from the static approximation. We optimized the extraction for spins
(oscillators and fields) and fermions and found different roles of the environment bandwidth to obtain extractable coherence. Importantly, all those effects are numerically confirmed beyond the asymptotic limit for a finite strength of the extraction (detailed analysis is presented in Appendix). Extractability principally opens a way to experimental verifications of proposals with quantum dots
[
40
]
and also to others under development in the superconducting circuits
[
45
]
, electro-mechanical systems
[
46
]
,
and trapped ions
[
47
]
.

We can extend the systems used to generate and extract quantum coherence. The fermionic system generating a maximum of coherence at a finite temperature
k_{B}T\leq\hbar\omega_{1}
inspires further extensions. We can investigate three-level and four-level models to optimize the proposed methods. It is a viable investigation area as such systems appear for both atomic and solid-state systems. A possible maximum of quantum coherence at the finite temperature in the region

k_{B}T>\hbar\omega_{1}
remains an open problem. However, further engineering of the systems and interaction may overcome such a limit. Another open problem is an accumulation of coherence in an output system from many such parallel interactions. Recently, it has been demonstrated in a collision model that multiple environments can enhance quantum coherence substantially
[
39
]
.
Further research can be focused on coherence distillation and use a possible extension of the protocols to more spins or fermions giving an even more considerable amount of the extractable quantum coherence.
Thermodynamical analysis of autonomous coherence has been already initiated too

[
37
]
. These directions for immediate exploration illustrate the novelty of this subject in theory and stimulate current experiments. It is very timely as quantum coherence from a low-temperature environment will directly transform quantum sensors and quantum engines to be more autonomous.

Appendix G
Qualitative analysis of the spin-boson system coherences

In order to understand the proportionality between the coherences of the first and second spin

\langle\sigma_{2}^{x}\rangle=-\frac{t}{\omega_{2}}\tanh\left(\frac{\beta\omega_{2}}{2}\right)\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle

(105)

and physical meaning of such proportionality we provide the following qualitative analysis.
Let us consider the bath with the Hamiltonian

H_{B}=\sum_{k}\Omega_{k}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k},

(106)

and system of two weakly interacting spins

H_{12}=\frac{\omega_{1}}{2}\sigma_{1}^{z}+\frac{\omega_{2}}{2}\sigma_{2}^{z}+t(\sigma_{1}^{+}\sigma_{2}^{-}+\sigma_{2}^{+}\sigma_{1}^{-}),

(107)

at temperature
T=1/\beta
.
When two systems are far from each other, both of them have corresponding thermal distributions.
Taking into account that the interaction between the spins are week (
t\ll\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
), we can
estimate their averages of
\langle\sigma_{j}^{x}\rangle=\langle\sigma_{j}^{y}\rangle=0
and

\langle\sigma_{j}^{z}\rangle=-\tanh(\beta\omega_{j}/2)
, for
j=1,2
.
When system of two spins is close to the bath, the interaction term start to play an important role

H_{B1}=(f_{1}\sigma_{1}^{z}+f_{2}\sigma_{1}^{x})\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}).

(108)

Due to this term the first spin start to “polarize” the bath system. This process can be observed from
the Hamiltonian

H_{BS_{1}}=\sum_{k}\Omega_{k}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}+f_{1}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger})+\frac{\omega_{1}}{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle,

(109)

where we replace the operator
\sigma_{1}^{z}
by its average value
\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle
.
Due to the interaction of the first spin with the bath the boson operators get the non-zero average values

\langle b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle\neq 0
. This values can be calculated by diagonalization of the
boson part of the obtained Hamiltonian. It can be done by substitution

b_{k}=\beta_{k}-f_{1}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\lambda_{k}/\Omega_{k}
, where
\beta_{k}
is the set of
boson annihilation operators with the same commutation rules as
b_{k}
operators, and zero average values.
Hence, we have

\langle b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle=\langle\beta_{k}+\beta_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle-2f_{1}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\lambda_{k}/\Omega_{k}=-2f_{1}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\lambda_{k}/\Omega_{k}.

(110)

The non-zero
\langle b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle
affects the term

f_{2}\sigma_{1}^{x}\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger})\rightarrow-2f_{1}f_{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\sigma_{1}^{x}\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}/\Omega_{k}=-2f_{1}f_{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\Omega\sigma_{1}^{x}.

(111)

Therefore, the back-action of the bath modifies the Hamiltonian of the first spin

H_{S_{1}}^{mod}=\frac{\omega_{1}}{2}\sigma_{1}^{z}-2f_{1}f_{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\Omega\sigma_{1}^{x}.

(112)

Due to non-zero
\sigma_{1}^{x}
term, the first spin gets non-zero coherence

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=Tr[e^{-\beta H_{S_{1}}^{mod}}\sigma_{1}^{x}]=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\tanh\Big(\beta\sqrt{(2f_{1}f_{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\Omega)^{2}+(\omega_{1}/2)^{2}}\Big)\Big[\frac{-2f_{1}f_{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\Omega}{\sqrt{(2f_{1}f_{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\Omega)^{2}+(\omega_{1}/2)^{2}}}\Big]\approx-\frac{4f_{1}f_{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle^{2}\Omega}{\omega_{1}}.

(113)

The final approximate result is obtained for the case
4f_{1}f_{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\Omega\ll\omega_{1}
.
This means that the interaction with the bath gives negligibly small correction to the energy and coherence
of the first spin. In other words the latter result corresponds to weak interaction limit.

The non-zero
\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle
induces the effective
\sigma_{2}^{x}

term in the second spin system

t(\sigma_{1}^{+}\sigma_{2}^{-}+\sigma_{2}^{+}\sigma_{1}^{-})\rightarrow t(\langle\sigma_{1}^{+}\rangle\sigma_{2}^{-}+\sigma_{2}^{+}\langle\sigma_{1}^{-}\rangle)=t\frac{\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle}{2}\sigma_{2}^{x}.

(114)

Therefore the Hamiltonian of the second spin is modified

H_{S_{2}}^{mod}=\frac{\omega_{2}}{2}\sigma_{2}^{z}+t\frac{\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle}{2}\sigma_{2}^{x},

(115)

which leads to the non-zero coherence

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{2}^{x}\rangle=

\displaystyle Tr[e^{-\beta H_{S_{2}}^{mod}}\sigma_{2}^{x}]=

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\tanh\Big(\beta\sqrt{(t\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle/2)^{2}+(\omega_{2}/2)^{2}}\Big)\frac{t\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle}{2\sqrt{(t\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle/2)^{2}+(\omega_{2}/2)^{2}}}\approx-\frac{t}{\omega_{2}}\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\omega_{2}}{2}\Big)\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle.

(116)

The latter approximate expression is valid only for the case
t\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle\ll\omega_{2}
.
Therefore the linear dependence between the coherences of the second and first spins appears if two
additional restrictions are satisfied a) the weak coupling regime
4f_{1}f_{2}\langle\sigma_{1}^{z}\rangle\Omega\ll\omega_{1}

and b) the weak coupling between spins
t\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle\ll\omega_{2}
. Note that both inequalities are natural
for the derivations based on the thermodynamical perturbation approach presented before. The first inequality allows
to take only the leading term in perturbation series to evaluate the result. If this inequality is not satisfied one needs to use the next terms of perturbation series to get the correct answer. The second condition defines the domain of the parameters where the linear dependence between both coherences exists.

Let us consider the Hamiltonian of two spins interacting with the bosons

t\rightarrow-t

parameters for simplicity in further calculations.
It was shown before that the perturbation theory calculations with this Hamiltonian are bulky due to non-commutativity of spin operators and absence of the Wick theorem for them. Therefore, it looks
interesting to rewrite the spin Hamiltonian in terms of bosons or fermions where the Wick theorem is applicable.
This allows us to use the Green’s function technique and, potentially, make the final result more clear for understanding. As an example we fermionize the Hamiltonian with the help of the Jordan-Wigner transformation
[
44
]
.
In order to do it we introduce the additional (“zeroth”) spin subsystem, which is described by the operators
\{\sigma_{0}^{x},\sigma_{0}^{y},\sigma_{0}^{z}\}
and has zero energy. Then we replace the spin operators by the fermion ones

\displaystyle\sigma_{0}^{-}

\displaystyle=a_{0},\quad\sigma_{0}^{+}=a_{0}^{\dagger},\quad\sigma_{0}^{z}=2a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}-1,

(144)

\displaystyle\quad\sigma_{1}^{-}

\displaystyle=(2a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}-1)a_{1},\quad\sigma_{1}^{+}=(2a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}-1)a_{1}^{\dagger},\quad\sigma_{1}^{z}=2a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}-1,

(145)

\displaystyle\sigma_{2}^{-}

\displaystyle=(2a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}-1)(2a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}-1)a_{2},\quad\sigma_{2}^{+}=(2a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}-1)(2a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}-1)a_{2}^{\dagger},\quad\sigma_{2}^{z}=2a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{2}-1,

(146)

and make the additional substitution

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{1}}{2}\sigma_{1}^{z}=

\displaystyle\omega_{1}(a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}-\frac{1}{2})\rightarrow\omega_{1}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1},\quad\frac{\omega_{2}}{2}\sigma_{2}^{z}=\omega_{2}(a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{2}-\frac{1}{2})\rightarrow\omega_{2}a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{2},

(147)

\displaystyle\sigma_{1}^{x}=

\displaystyle(\sigma_{1}^{+}+\sigma_{1}^{-})\rightarrow(\sigma_{1}^{+}\sigma_{0}^{-}+\sigma_{0}^{+}\sigma_{1}^{-})=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}-1)a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}(2a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}-1)a_{1}=-(a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}).

(148)

Then the Hamiltonian takes the following form

H\rightarrow\omega_{2}a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{2}+\omega_{1}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+t(a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{1}+a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{2})+\sum_{k}\Omega_{k}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}+[2f_{1}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+f_{2}(a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1})]\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}^{\dagger}+b_{k}).

(149)

Note that the fermionization procedure changes the sign of the spin exchange terms.
The coherence of the first spin can be written in terms of
\mathrm{T}_{\tau}
-ordered fermion Green’s function

G_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)=-\langle\mathrm{T}_{\tau}a_{\alpha}(\tau)a_{\beta}^{\dagger}(0)\rangle
as

\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle|_{t,f_{2}}\rightarrow-\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle|_{-t,-f_{2}}=-[G_{01}(0_{-})+G_{10}(0_{-})]|_{-t,-f_{2}}.

(150)

Here subscripts define the correspondence rule between spin and fermion models —
the coherence in the spin system with parameters
t,f_{2}
corresponds to the coherence
in the fermion system with parameters
-t,-f_{2}
and vice versa.
We introduce the second spin coherence by analogy

\langle\sigma_{2}^{x}\rangle|_{t,f_{2}}\rightarrow-\langle a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{2}\rangle|_{-t,-f_{2}}=-[G_{02}(0_{-})+G_{20}(0_{-})]|_{-t,-f_{2}}.

(151)

Therefore we need to calculate the fermion Green’s function for the latter (fermionized) Hamiltonian.

Appendix K
Green’s function method

It is convenient to present the Hamiltonian

H=\omega_{1}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{2}+t(a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{2}+a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{1})+\sum_{k}\Omega_{k}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}+[2f_{1}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+f_{2}(a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1})]\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}^{\dagger}+b_{k})

(152)

as a sum of three parts: fermion Hamiltonian

H_{F}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{2}^{\dagger}&a_{1}^{\dagger}&a_{0}^{\dagger}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\omega_{2}&t&0\\
t&\omega_{1}&0\\
0&0&0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{2}\\
a_{1}\\
a_{0}\\
\end{array}\right]=a_{\mu}^{\dagger}[\mathcal{H}_{F}]_{\mu\nu}a_{\nu},

(153)

where we introduce
3\times 3
matrix
\mathcal{H}_{F}
and a summation over repeated (greek) indices;
boson Hamiltonian

H_{B}=\sum_{k}\Omega_{k}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k};

(154)

and fermion-boson interaction

H_{BF}=[2f_{1}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+f_{2}(a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1})]\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}^{\dagger}+b_{k}).

(155)

The latter term is considered as a perturbation. It gives the contribution to the full
fermion Green’s function
G_{\alpha\beta}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})=-\langle\mathrm{T}_{\tau}a_{\alpha}(\tau)a_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle
, where the averaging is defined as

\langle\star\rangle=\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta H}\star]/\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta H}]
. We evaluate the contribution to the full Green’s function in terms of the non-interaction fermion
G^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
and boson

D^{(0)}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})
Greens’ functions.

Let us consider the fermion subsystem first. The non-interacting Green’s function reads

G^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})=-\langle\mathrm{T}_{\tau}a_{\alpha}(\tau)a_{\beta}^{\dagger}(\tau^{\prime})\rangle_{0},

(156)

where the averaging is defined as
\langle\star\rangle_{0}=\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta H_{F}}\star]/\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta H_{F}}]
. This Green’s function has a compact form in the Matsubara representation

G^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(ip_{n})=\Big[\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\mathcal{H}_{F}}\Big]_{\alpha\beta}=U_{\alpha\mu}\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu}}[U^{\dagger}]_{\nu\beta}=U_{\alpha\mu}\widetilde{G}^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(ip_{n})[U^{\dagger}]_{\nu\beta}.

(157)

Here
p_{n}=(2n+1)\pi/\beta
with integer
n
,
\epsilon_{2}=(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})/2+\sqrt{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}/4+t^{2}}
,
\epsilon_{1}=(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})/2-\sqrt{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}/4+t^{2}}
,
\epsilon_{0}=0
,
\sin\theta=2t/\sqrt{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}+4t^{2}}
and
\cos\theta=(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})/\sqrt{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}+4t^{2}}
.
The
3\times 3
matrix
U
diagonalizes the fermion Hamiltonian matrix
\mathcal{H}_{F}

\mathcal{H}_{F}=U\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\epsilon_{2}&0&0\\
0&\epsilon_{1}&0\\
0&0&\epsilon_{0}\end{array}\right]U^{\dagger},\quad U=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}\cos(\theta/2)&-\sin(\theta/2)&0\\
\sin(\theta/2)&\cos(\theta/2)&0\\
0&0&1\end{array}\right].

(158)

The non-interacting boson Green’s function is

D^{(0)}(\tau-\tau^{\prime})=-\langle\mathrm{T}_{\tau}\phi(\tau)\phi(\tau^{\prime})\rangle_{0},

(159)

where we introduced the boson field
\phi=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}^{\dagger}+b_{k})
and statistical averaging
\langle\star\rangle_{0}=\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta H_{B}}\star]/\mathrm{Tr}[e^{-\beta H_{B}}]
.
Then

\displaystyle D^{(0)}(\tau)=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k,l}\lambda_{k}\lambda_{l}\langle\mathrm{T}_{\tau}(b_{k}e^{-\tau\Omega_{k}}+b_{k}^{\dagger}e^{\tau\Omega_{k}})(b_{l}+b_{l}^{\dagger})\rangle_{0}=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}D^{(0)}(k,\tau)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\theta(\tau)\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}\Big[N_{k}e^{\tau\Omega_{k}}+(N_{k}+1)e^{-\tau\Omega_{k}}\Big]-\theta(-\tau)\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}\Big[N_{k}e^{-\tau\Omega_{k}}+(N_{k}+1)e^{\tau\Omega_{k}}\Big],

(160)

where
N_{k}=N(\Omega_{k})=(e^{\beta\Omega_{k}}-1)^{-1}
. The corresponding Matsubara’s Green’s function with
\omega_{m}=2\pi m/\beta
and integer
m
reads

D^{(0)}(k,i\omega_{m})=\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau e^{i\omega_{m}\tau}D^{(0)}(k,\tau)=-\Big[\frac{1}{i\omega_{m}+\Omega_{k}}-\frac{1}{i\omega_{m}-\Omega_{k}}\Big]=-\frac{2\Omega_{k}}{\omega_{m}^{2}+\Omega_{k}^{2}}.

(161)

The leading non-zero correction to the fermion Green’s function is

G_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)\approx G^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)+\Delta G^{F}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)+\Delta G^{H}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau).

(162)

It consists of Fock

\Delta G^{F}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)=-\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{2}D^{(0)}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})G^{(0)}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau_{1})M_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})M_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}\beta}(\tau_{2}).

(163)

and Hartree terms

\Delta G_{\alpha\beta}^{H}(\tau)=M_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(0)\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}G^{(0)}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau_{1})M_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime}\beta}(\tau_{1})\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{2}D^{(0)}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}).

(164)

Note that there is a summation over the repeated indices. The matrix
M_{\alpha\beta}
is

M=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&2f_{1}&f_{2}\\
0&f_{2}&0\end{array}\right].

(165)

Appendix L
Fock contribution

We transform the correction into the Matsubara representation using the substitutions

\displaystyle G^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{p_{n}}e^{-ip_{n}\tau}G^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(ip_{n}),\quad D^{(0)}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}\sum_{\omega_{m}}e^{-i\omega_{m}\tau}D^{(0)}(k,i\omega_{m}),

(166)

where
G^{(0)}_{\alpha\beta}(ip_{n})
and
D^{(0)}(k,i\omega_{m})
have been derived before.
For the Fock term we have

\Delta G_{\alpha\beta}^{F}(ip_{n})=G^{(0)}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(ip_{n})M_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}\Big[-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\omega_{m}}D^{(0)}(k,i\omega_{m})G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(ip_{n}-i\omega_{m})\Big]M_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}\beta}(ip_{n}).

(167)

We evaluate the sum in the square brackets

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\omega_{m}}D^{(0)}(k,i\omega_{m})

\displaystyle G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(ip_{n}-i\omega_{m})=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{\beta^{\prime}\mu}\Big\{-\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\omega_{m}}D^{(0)}(k,i\omega_{m})\widetilde{G}^{(0)}_{\mu\nu}(ip_{n}-i\omega_{m})\Big\}[U^{\dagger}]_{\nu\alpha^{\prime\prime}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{\beta^{\prime}\mu}\Big\{\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{\beta}\sum_{\omega_{m}}\frac{2\Omega_{k}}{\omega_{m}^{2}+\Omega_{k}^{2}}\frac{1}{ip_{n}-i\omega_{m}-\epsilon_{\mu}}\Big\}[U^{\dagger}]_{\nu\alpha^{\prime\prime}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{\beta^{\prime}\mu}\delta_{\mu\nu}\Big[\frac{N_{k}+n(\epsilon_{\mu})}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu}+\Omega_{k}}+\frac{N_{k}+1-n(\epsilon_{\mu})}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu}-\Omega_{k}}\Big][U^{\dagger}]_{\nu\alpha^{\prime\prime}}.

(168)

and introduce the function

\displaystyle F(\epsilon_{\mu})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}\Big[\frac{N_{k}+n(\epsilon_{\mu})}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu}+\Omega_{k}}+\frac{N_{k}+1-n(\epsilon_{\mu})}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu}-\Omega_{k}}\Big]=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\,\mathcal{I}(\xi)\Big[\frac{N(\xi)+n(\epsilon_{\mu})}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu}+\xi}+\frac{N(\xi)+1-n(\epsilon_{\mu})}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu}-\xi}\Big],

(169)

with
n(\epsilon_{\mu})=(e^{\beta\epsilon_{\mu}}+1)^{-1}
and
\mathcal{I}(\xi)=\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}\delta(\xi-\Omega_{k})
. Then the Fock correction takes the form

\Delta G^{F}_{\alpha\beta}(ip_{n})=U_{\alpha\mu^{\prime}}\widetilde{G}^{(0)}_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}(ip_{n})[U^{\dagger}MU]_{\nu^{\prime}\mu}\delta_{\mu\nu}F(\epsilon_{\mu})[U^{\dagger}MU]_{\nu\mu^{\prime\prime}}\widetilde{G}^{(0)}_{\mu^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}(ip_{n})[U^{\dagger}]_{\nu^{\prime\prime}\beta}.

(170)

Then we evaluate the matrix sum

[\widetilde{M}]_{\alpha\beta}=[U^{\dagger}MU]_{\alpha\beta}=[U^{\dagger}]_{\alpha\mu}M_{\mu\nu}U_{\nu\beta}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}2f_{1}\sin^{2}(\theta/2)&f_{1}\sin\theta&f_{2}\sin(\theta/2)\\
f_{1}\sin\theta&2f_{1}\cos^{2}(\theta/2)&f_{2}\cos(\theta/2)\\
f_{2}\sin(\theta/2)&f_{2}\cos(\theta/2)&0\end{array}\right],

(171)

and calculate the matrix elements

F_{\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime\prime}}=[\widetilde{M}]_{\nu^{\prime}\mu}\delta_{\mu\nu}F(\epsilon_{\mu})[\widetilde{M}]_{\nu\mu^{\prime\prime}}.

(172)

The matrix
F_{\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime\prime}}
is real valued
\text{Im}[F_{\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime\prime}}]=0
and symmetric
F_{\nu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime\prime}}=F_{\mu^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime}}
. Therefore we have

\displaystyle F_{22}=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{2}\Big[4F(\epsilon_{2})\sin^{4}(\theta/2)+F(\epsilon_{1})\sin^{2}(\theta)\Big]+f_{2}^{2}F(\epsilon_{0})\sin^{2}(\theta/2),

(173)

\displaystyle F_{11}=

\displaystyle f_{1}^{2}\Big[F(\epsilon_{2})\sin^{2}(\theta)+4F(\epsilon_{1})\cos^{4}(\theta/2)\Big]+f_{2}^{2}F(\epsilon_{0})\cos^{2}(\theta/2),

(174)

\displaystyle F_{00}=

\displaystyle f_{2}^{2}\Big[F(\epsilon_{2})\sin^{2}(\theta/2)+F(\epsilon_{1})\cos^{2}(\theta/2)\Big],

(175)

\displaystyle F_{21}=

\displaystyle 2f_{1}^{2}\sin\theta\Big[F(\epsilon_{2})\sin^{2}(\theta/2)+F(\epsilon_{1})\cos^{2}(\theta/2)\Big]+f_{2}^{2}F(\epsilon_{0})\sin(\theta)/2,

(176)

\displaystyle F_{20}=

\displaystyle 2f_{1}f_{2}\sin(\theta/2)\Big[F(\epsilon_{2})\sin^{2}(\theta/2)+F(\epsilon_{1})\cos^{2}(\theta/2)\Big],

(177)

\displaystyle F_{10}=

\displaystyle 2f_{1}f_{2}\cos(\theta/2)\Big[F(\epsilon_{2})\sin^{2}(\theta/2)+F(\epsilon_{1})\cos^{2}(\theta/2)\Big],

(178)

Finally the Fock correction takes the simple form

\Delta G^{F}_{\alpha\beta}(ip_{n})=U_{\alpha\mu^{\prime}}\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu^{\prime}}}F_{\mu^{\prime}\mu^{\prime\prime}}\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu^{\prime\prime}}}[U^{\dagger}]_{\mu^{\prime\prime}\beta}.

(179)

We are interested in the matrix elements
\Delta G^{F}_{01}(\tau=0_{-})
and
\Delta G^{F}_{02}(\tau=0_{-})
which correspond to Fock contribution in
\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}\rangle
and
\langle a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{0}\rangle
respectively. To do it we evaluate the sum

\Delta G^{F}_{\alpha\beta}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{p_{n}}e^{-ip_{n}\tau}\Delta G^{F}_{\alpha\beta}(ip_{n}),

(180)

and take the limit
\tau\rightarrow 0_{-}
in the final result. We have

\displaystyle\Delta G^{F}_{01}(ip_{n})=2f_{1}f_{2}\frac{F(\epsilon_{2})\sin^{2}(\theta/2)+F(\epsilon_{1})\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0}}\Big[\frac{\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2})}+\frac{\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1})}\Big],

(181)

\displaystyle\Delta G^{F}_{02}(ip_{n})=f_{1}f_{2}\sin\theta\frac{F(\epsilon_{2})\sin^{2}(\theta/2)+F(\epsilon_{1})\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0}}\Big[\frac{1}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2})}-\frac{1}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1})}\Big].

(182)

These expressions are simplified the limit
t\rightarrow 0
, which equals to the case
\sin\theta\rightarrow 0
and
\cos\theta\rightarrow 1
. Here and further in the text we consider the case
\omega_{2}>\omega_{1}
for brevity.
Then in the leading order by small parameter
\sin\theta
we have

\displaystyle\Delta G^{F}_{01}(ip_{n})\approx\frac{2f_{1}f_{2}F(\epsilon_{1})}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0})(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1})},\quad\Delta G^{F}_{02}(ip_{n})\approx\sin\theta\frac{f_{1}f_{2}F(\epsilon_{1})}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0})}\Big[\frac{1}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2})}-\frac{1}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1})}\Big].

(183)

One can note the correspondence between the obtained corrections

\Delta G^{F}_{02}(ip_{n})=\frac{t}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}}\Delta G^{F}_{01}(ip_{n}).

(184)

Let us evaluate now
\Delta G^{F}_{01}(0_{-})
and
\Delta G^{F}_{02}(0_{-})
in the limit
t\rightarrow 0

\displaystyle\Delta G^{F}_{01}(0_{-})=2f_{1}f_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\,\mathcal{I}(\xi)\Big[

\displaystyle-\frac{(2N(\xi)+1)\epsilon_{1}+(2n(\epsilon_{1})-1)\xi}{2\epsilon_{1}(\xi^{2}-\epsilon_{1}^{2})}+\frac{(2n(\epsilon_{1})-1)n(\epsilon_{1})}{\epsilon_{1}\xi}+

\displaystyle+\frac{[N(\xi)+1]n(\epsilon_{1})}{\xi(\xi-\epsilon_{1})}+\frac{N(\xi)n(\epsilon_{1})}{\xi(\xi+\epsilon_{1})}\Big],

(185)

\displaystyle\Delta G^{F}_{02}(0_{-})=\sin\theta f_{1}f_{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\,\mathcal{I}(\xi)\Big[W(\epsilon_{2})-W(\epsilon_{1})\Big],

(186)

where we introduced the function

\displaystyle W(w)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2w}\Big[\frac{N(\xi)+1-n(\epsilon_{1})}{\epsilon_{1}+\xi}-\frac{N(\xi)+n(\epsilon_{1})}{\xi-\epsilon_{1}}\Big]+\frac{N(\xi)+1-n(\epsilon_{1})}{(\epsilon_{1}+\xi)(\epsilon_{1}+\xi-w)}n(\epsilon_{1}+\xi)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{n(w)}{w}\Big[\frac{N(\xi)+n(\epsilon_{1})}{w-\epsilon_{1}+\xi}+\frac{N(\xi)+1-n(\epsilon_{1})}{w-\epsilon_{1}-\xi}\Big]+\frac{N(\xi)+n(\epsilon_{1})}{(\epsilon_{1}-\xi)(\epsilon_{1}-\xi-w)}n(\epsilon_{1}-\xi).

(187)

Appendix M
Hartree contribution

Let us consider the correction which appears due to the Hartree term

\Delta G_{\alpha\beta}^{H}(\tau)=M_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(0)\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}G^{(0)}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau_{1})M_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime}\beta}(\tau_{1})\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{2}D^{(0)}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}).

(188)

The first multiplier can be evaluated in the following way. We calculate this term in Matsubara representation

M_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(ip_{n})=M_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}}U_{\beta^{\prime\prime}\mu}\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu}}[U^{\dagger}]_{\nu\alpha^{\prime\prime}}=\sum_{\mu=0}^{2}\frac{[U^{\dagger}MU]_{\mu\mu}}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu}}

(189)

We evaluated the matrix
\widetilde{M}=U^{\dagger}MU
before. Its diagonal matrix elements are

\widetilde{M}_{22}=2f_{1}\sin^{2}(\theta/2),\quad\widetilde{M}_{11}=2f_{1}\cos^{2}(\theta/2),\quad\widetilde{M}_{00}=0.

(190)

It results in the following

\displaystyle M_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(0)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{p_{n}}e^{-ip_{n}\tau}M_{\alpha^{\prime\prime}\beta^{\prime\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime\prime}\alpha^{\prime\prime}}(ip_{n})\Big|_{\tau=0_{-}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2f_{1}\sin^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{2})+2f_{1}\cos^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{1}).

(191)

Then we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{1}G^{(0)}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}(\tau-\tau_{1})M_{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}G^{(0)}_{\beta^{\prime}\beta}(\tau_{1})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{p_{n}}e^{-ip_{n}\tau}U_{\alpha\mu}\frac{\delta_{\mu\nu}}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\nu}}\widetilde{M}_{\nu\mu^{\prime}}\frac{\delta_{\mu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{\mu^{\prime}}}[U^{\dagger}]_{\nu^{\prime}\beta}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{p_{n}}e^{-ip_{n}\tau}R_{\alpha\beta}.

(192)

We are interested in
\Delta G_{01}^{H}(\tau)
and
\Delta G_{02}^{H}(\tau)
.
Therefore we calculate the corresponding matrix elements

R_{01}=\frac{f_{2}}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0})}\Big[\frac{\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}}+\frac{\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1}}\Big],\quad R_{02}=\frac{f_{2}\sin\theta}{2(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0})}\Big[\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}}-\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1}}\Big].

(193)

The latter term reads

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{2}D^{(0)}(\tau_{1}-\tau_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}^{2}\sum_{\omega_{m}}D^{(0)}(k,i\omega_{m})e^{-i\omega_{m}\tau_{1}}\int_{0}^{\beta}d\tau_{2}e^{i\omega_{m}\tau_{2}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\frac{\mathcal{I}(\xi)}{\xi}=-2\Omega.

(194)

As a result we have

\Delta G^{H}_{01}(ip_{n})=-\frac{4f_{1}f_{2}\Omega}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0})}\Big[\sin^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{2})+\cos^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{1})\Big]\Big[\frac{\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}}+\frac{\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1}}\Big].

(195)

\Delta G^{H}_{02}(ip_{n})=-\frac{2f_{1}f_{2}\Omega\sin\theta}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0}}\Big[\sin^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{2})+\cos^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{1})\Big]\Big[\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}}-\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1}}\Big].

(196)

Considering
\epsilon_{0}=0
and taking the limit
\sin\theta\rightarrow 0,\cos\theta\rightarrow 1
we get

\Delta G^{H}_{01}(0_{-})=-\frac{4f_{1}f_{2}\Omega n(\epsilon_{1})}{\beta}\sum_{p_{n}}\frac{e^{-ip_{n}\tau}}{ip_{n}(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1})}\Big|_{\tau=0_{-}}\!\!\!=\frac{2f_{1}f_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\Omega n(\epsilon_{1})\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{1}}{2}\Big),

(197)

\displaystyle\Delta G^{H}_{02}(0_{-})=

\displaystyle-\frac{2f_{1}f_{2}\sin\theta\Omega n(\epsilon_{1})}{\beta}\sum_{p_{n}}\frac{e^{-ip_{n}\tau}}{ip_{n}}\Big[\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}}-\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1}}\Big]\Big|_{\tau=0_{-}}\!\!\!=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{1}f_{2}\sin\theta n(\epsilon_{1})\Omega\Big[\frac{\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{2}}{2}\Big)}{\epsilon_{2}}-\frac{\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{1}}{2}\Big)}{\epsilon_{1}}\Big],

(198)

where we took into account the sum

\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{p_{n}}\frac{e^{-ip_{n}\tau}}{ip_{n}(ip_{n}-w)}=-\frac{1}{2w}+\frac{e^{-\tau w}}{w}n(w).

(199)

Appendix N
Analysis of the coherences

In this section we analyze the coherence of the first
\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle_{F}
and second
\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle_{F}
spins. Here the subscript
F
indicates that we derived the coherences from the fermionized Hamiltonian. In accordance with the aforementioned fermion-boson correspondence we have

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{1}^{x}\rangle_{F}=-\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle|_{-t,-f_{2}}=

\displaystyle-[G_{01}(0_{-})+G_{10}(0_{-})]|_{-t,-f_{2}}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2[\Delta G^{F}_{01}(0_{-})+\Delta G^{H}_{01}(0_{-})]|_{-t,-f_{2}},

(200)

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{2}^{x}\rangle_{F}=-\langle a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{2}\rangle|_{-t,-f_{2}}=

\displaystyle-[G_{02}(0_{-})+G_{20}(0_{-})]|_{-t,-f_{2}}=

\displaystyle=

Appendix O
Fermion coherences for
\omega_{2}<\omega_{1}
case

Here we derive the expression for the coherence of the first and the second spins in the fermionized spin-boson model,
for the case
\omega_{1}>\omega_{2}
and
t/(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\ll 1
.

First we consider the general expression for the Hartree contribution

\Delta G^{H}_{01}(ip_{n})=-\frac{4f_{1}f_{2}\Omega}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0}}\Big[\sin^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{2})+\cos^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{1})\Big]\Big[\frac{\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}}+\frac{\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1}}\Big].

(214)

\Delta G^{H}_{02}(ip_{n})=-\frac{2f_{1}f_{2}\Omega\sin\theta}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0}}\Big[\sin^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{2})+\cos^{2}(\theta/2)n(\epsilon_{1})\Big]\Big[\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}}-\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1}}\Big].

(215)

Here
\sin\theta=2t/\sqrt{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}+4t^{2}}
,
\cos\theta=(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})/\sqrt{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}+4t^{2}}
.
In considering case these expressions are
\sin\theta\approx 2t/|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}|
,

\cos\theta\approx\textrm{sign}(\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})=-1
. Hence, the angle

\theta\approx\pi-2t/|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}|
and
\sin(\theta/2)\approx 1
,

\cos(\theta/2)\approx t/|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}|

\Delta G^{H}_{01}(ip_{n})\approx-\frac{4f_{1}f_{2}}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\frac{\mathcal{I}(\xi)}{\xi}n(\epsilon_{2})\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}},

(216)

\Delta G^{H}_{02}(ip_{n})\approx-\frac{2f_{1}f_{2}\sin\theta}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\xi\frac{\mathcal{I}(\xi)}{\xi}n(\epsilon_{2})\Big[\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2}}-\frac{1}{ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1}}\Big].

(217)

The general expressions for the Fock terms are

\displaystyle\Delta G^{F}_{01}(ip_{n})=2f_{1}f_{2}\frac{F(\epsilon_{2})\sin^{2}(\theta/2)+F(\epsilon_{1})\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{0})}\Big[\frac{\sin^{2}(\theta/2)}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{2})}+\frac{\cos^{2}(\theta/2)}{(ip_{n}-\epsilon_{1})}\Big],

In the considering case they read

(220)

(221)

\epsilon_{1}

Appendix P
Qualitative analysis of the fermionized spin-boson system coherences

Here we provide qualitative static response calculation in order to compare it with the solution obtained before.
We consider the bosonic bath with the Hamiltonian

H_{B}=\sum_{k}\Omega_{k}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k},

(227)

and system of three weakly interacting fermions

H_{F}=\omega_{1}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+\omega_{2}a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{2}+t(a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{2}+a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{1})+0*a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0},

(228)

at temperature
T=1/\beta
.
When two systems are far from each other, both of them have corresponding thermal distributions.
Taking into account that the interaction between the fermions are week (
t\ll\omega_{1},\omega_{2}
), we can
estimate their occupation numbers of
\langle a^{\dagger}_{j}a_{j}\rangle\approx n(\omega_{j})

for
j=1,2
, where
n(\omega)=[\exp(\beta\omega)+1]^{-1}
is Fermi occupation number
When system of two fermions is close to the bath, the interaction term start to play an important role

H_{BF}=(2f_{1}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+f_{2}[a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}])\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}).

(229)

Due to this term, the non-zero occupation number
\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle
“polarizes” the bath system.
This process can be observed from the Hamiltonian

H_{BF_{1}}=\sum_{k}\Omega_{k}b_{k}^{\dagger}b_{k}+2f_{1}\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger})+\omega_{1}\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle,

(230)

where we replace the operator
a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}
by its average value
\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle
.
The interaction of the first spin with the bath induces the non-zero average values

\langle b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle\neq 0
. This values can be calculated by diagonalization of the
boson part of the obtained Hamiltonian. It can be done by substitution

b_{k}=\beta_{k}-2f_{1}\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle\lambda_{k}/\Omega_{k}
, where
\beta_{k}
is the set of
boson annihilation operators with the same commutation rules as
b_{k}
operators, and zero average values.
Hence, we have

\langle b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle=\langle\beta_{k}+\beta_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle-4f_{1}\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle\lambda_{k}/\Omega_{k}=-4f_{1}\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle\lambda_{k}/\Omega_{k}.

(231)

The non-zero
\langle b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger}\rangle
affects the term

f_{2}[a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}]\sum_{k}\lambda_{k}(b_{k}+b_{k}^{\dagger})\rightarrow-4f_{1}f_{2}\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle\Omega[a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}]=q[a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}],

(232)

where we introduce the short notation
q=-4f_{1}f_{2}\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle\Omega
.
Here
\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle
should be considered as
\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle=n(\omega_{1})
.
The back action of the bath modifies the Hamiltonian of the fermions which
we write as a quadratic form

H_{F}^{mod}=[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{0}^{\dagger}&a_{1}^{\dagger}&a_{2}^{\dagger}\end{array}]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&q&0\\
q&\omega_{1}&t\\
0&t&\omega_{2}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}a_{0}\\
a_{1}\\
a_{2}\end{array}\right]=\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}\mathbf{a}.

(233)

When we introduce the unitary transformation
\mathbf{A}=U\mathbf{a}
which diagonalizes the Hamiltonian

\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}\mathcal{H}\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{a}^{\dagger}U^{\dagger}U\mathcal{H}U^{\dagger}U\mathbf{a}=\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}\Lambda\mathbf{A}.

(234)

The matrix
\Lambda=U\mathcal{H}U^{\dagger}
is a diagonal matrix
[\Lambda]_{ij}=\delta_{ij}\lambda_{j}
,
where
\lambda_{j},\,j=0,1,2
are the eigenenergies of the system.
The general expression for the matrix
U
is quite complex. We find it approximately up to qubic order
by small parameters
q
and
t
. Namely, we consider the unitary matrix
U
in the form
U=\exp(-S)
,
and find the anti-unitary matrix
S^{\dagger}=-S

S=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}&-\frac{qt}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\\
-\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}&0&0\\
\frac{qt}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}&0&0\\
\end{array}\right].

(235)

Then we calculate
U_{app}
approximately
\exp(-S)\approx\mathcal{I}-S+S^{2}/2
,
where
\mathcal{I}
is a unit
3\times 3
matrix

U_{app}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&0&0\\
0&1&0\\
1&0&1\\
\end{array}\right]-\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}&-\frac{qt}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\\
-\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}&0&0\\
\frac{qt}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}&0&0\\
\end{array}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}-\frac{q^{2}t^{2}}{\omega_{1}^{2}\omega_{2}^{2}}-\frac{q^{2}}{\omega_{1}^{2}}&0&0\\
0&-\frac{q^{2}}{\omega_{1}^{2}}&\frac{q^{2}t}{\omega_{1}^{2}\omega_{2}}\\
0&\frac{q^{2}t}{\omega_{1}^{2}\omega_{2}}&-\frac{q^{2}t^{2}}{\omega_{1}^{2}\omega_{2}^{2}}\\
\end{array}\right].

(236)

One can check that
U_{app}U^{\dagger}_{app}=\mathcal{I}+O(q^{4},q^{4}t)
.
Applying the transformation
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=U_{app}\mathcal{H}U_{app}^{\dagger}
we get

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}-\frac{q^{2}}{\omega_{1}}&0&0\\
0&\frac{q^{2}}{\omega_{1}}+\omega_{1}&t\\
0&t&\omega_{2}\\
\end{array}\right]+O(q^{2}t,qt^{2}).

(237)

It doesn’t fully diagonalize
\mathcal{H}
, but reduced it to the block-diagonal form.
Let us introduce the second transformation
W=\exp(-T)
with the matrix

T=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&0\\
0&0&-\frac{t}{q^{2}/\omega_{1}+\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}\\
0&\frac{t}{q^{2}/\omega_{1}+\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}&0\\
\end{array}\right].

(238)

Then calculate
W_{app}=\mathcal{I}-T+T^{2}/2
and evaluate
\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}=W_{app}\mathcal{H}^{\prime}W_{app}^{\dagger}

\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}-\frac{q^{2}}{\omega_{1}}&0&0\\
0&\omega_{1}+\frac{q^{2}}{\omega_{1}}+\frac{t^{2}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}&0\\
0&0&\omega_{2}-\frac{t^{2}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}\\
\end{array}\right]+O(t^{3},q^{2}t^{2}).

(239)

We got fully diagonalized matrix with the eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}=-\frac{q^{2}}{\omega_{1}}\approx 0,\quad\lambda_{2}=\omega_{1}+\frac{q^{2}}{\omega_{1}}+\frac{t^{2}}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}\approx\omega_{1},\quad\lambda_{3}=\omega_{2}+\frac{t^{2}}{\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}}\approx\omega_{2}.

(240)

These eigenvalues have the correct form up to quadratic terms by
q,t
. They can be alternatively
found in “Mathematica”. Therefore the composition of
U_{app}
and
W_{app}
gives the full unitary
transformation
U

\mathcal{H}^{\prime\prime}=U\mathcal{H}U^{\dagger}=W_{app}U_{app}\mathcal{H}U_{app}^{\dagger}W_{app}^{\dagger}

(241)

which diagonalizes the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
. Then we have

U=W_{app}U_{app}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1-\frac{q^{2}}{2\omega_{1}^{2}}&-\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}&\frac{qt}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\\
\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}&1-\frac{q^{2}}{2\omega_{1}^{2}}-\frac{t^{2}}{2\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\right)^{2}}&\frac{t}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}\\
-\frac{qt}{\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\right)\omega_{2}}&\frac{t}{\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}}&1-\frac{t^{2}}{2\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\right)^{2}}\\
\end{array}\right]+O(q^{2}t,qt^{2}).

(242)

The Hermitian conjugated matrix is

U^{\dagger}=U_{app}^{\dagger}W_{app}^{\dagger}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1-\frac{q^{2}}{2\omega_{1}^{2}}&\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}&-\frac{qt}{\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\right)\omega_{2}}\\
-\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}&1-\frac{q^{2}}{2\omega_{1}^{2}}-\frac{t^{2}}{2\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\right)^{2}}&\frac{t}{\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}}\\
\frac{qt}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}&\frac{t}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}&1-\frac{t^{2}}{2\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\right)^{2}}\\
\end{array}\right]+O(q^{2}t,qt^{2}).

(243)

Then, taking into account the definition
\mathbf{a}=U^{\dagger}\mathbf{A}
we get the following relations

\displaystyle a_{0}=

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{q^{2}}{2\omega_{1}^{2}}\Big)A_{0}+\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}A_{1}-\frac{qt}{\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\right)\omega_{2}}A_{2},

(244)

\displaystyle a_{1}=

\displaystyle-\frac{q}{\omega_{1}}A_{0}+\Big(1-\frac{q^{2}}{2\omega_{1}^{2}}-\frac{t^{2}}{2\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\right)^{2}}\Big)A_{1}+\frac{t}{\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}}A_{2},

(245)

\displaystyle a_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{qt}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}A_{0}+\frac{t}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}A_{1}+\Big(1-\frac{t^{2}}{2\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}\right)^{2}}\Big)A_{2}.

(246)

It give us the expression for the coherence

\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle=\frac{2q}{\omega_{1}}[\langle A_{1}^{\dagger}A_{1}\rangle-\langle A_{0}^{\dagger}A_{0}\rangle]\approx-\frac{8f_{1}f_{2}\Omega}{\omega_{1}}n(\omega_{1})\Big[n(\omega_{1})-n(-q^{2}/\omega_{1})\Big].

(247)

The expression for another coherence is

\displaystyle\langle a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{2}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{2qt}{\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\langle A_{0}^{\dagger}A_{0}\rangle+\frac{2qt}{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})}\Big[\frac{\langle A_{1}^{\dagger}A_{1}\rangle}{\omega_{1}}-\frac{\langle A_{2}^{\dagger}A_{2}\rangle}{\omega_{2}}\Big]=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2qt}{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\omega_{1}}\Big[\langle A_{1}^{\dagger}A_{1}\rangle-\langle A_{0}^{\dagger}A_{0}\rangle\Big]-\frac{2qt}{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\omega_{2}}\Big[\langle A_{2}^{\dagger}A_{2}\rangle-\langle A_{0}^{\dagger}A_{0}\rangle\Big].

(248)

If
\omega_{2}\gg\omega_{1}
then this expression can be simplified

\langle a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{2}\rangle\approx-\frac{t}{\omega_{2}}\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle

(249)

In order to compare the obtained result with the one, derived with Green’s function technique we need to
make the substitution
\langle A_{0}^{\dagger}A_{0}\rangle=n(-q^{2}/\omega_{1})\rightarrow n(0)=1/2
, since we consider
the linear in
q
contributions only and
\propto q^{2}
terms are beyond of this approximation.

Then we get the following expressions

\langle a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{1}\rangle\rightarrow-\frac{8f_{1}f_{2}\Omega}{\omega_{1}}n(\omega_{1})[n(\omega_{1})-1/2]=\frac{4f_{1}f_{2}\Omega}{\omega_{1}}n(\omega_{1})\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\omega_{1}}{2}\Big),

(250)

\langle a_{2}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{2}\rangle=-\frac{4f_{1}f_{2}\Omega t}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}n(\omega_{1})\Big[\frac{\tanh(\beta\omega_{2})}{\omega_{2}}-\frac{\tanh(\beta\omega_{1})}{\omega_{1}}\Big].

(251)

Note that these expressions coincide with the Hartree contributions obtained before, see Appendix
M
.
Indeed, in this chapter we used the same boson-fermion Hamiltonian (see Eq. (
152
)) and
define the averages as

\langle a_{j}^{\dagger}a_{0}+a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{j}\rangle=[G_{0j}(0_{-})+G_{0j}(0_{-})]\approx[G^{(0)}_{0j}(0_{-})+\Delta G^{F}_{0j}(0_{-})+\Delta G^{H}_{0j}(0_{-})+\{j\leftrightarrow 0\}],

(252)

with
j=1,2
,
G_{0j}(\tau)=-\langle\mathrm{T}_{\tau}a_{0}(\tau)a_{j}(0)^{\dagger}\rangle
full thermal Green’s function, that
is presented as the sum of non-interacting Green’s function
G^{(0)}_{0j}(\tau)
and two corrections –
Fock and Hartree, respectively.
The Hartree corrections are

\Delta G^{H}_{01}(0_{-})=\frac{2f_{1}f_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}}\Omega n(\epsilon_{1})\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{1}}{2}\Big),

(253)

\Delta G^{H}_{02}(0_{-})=f_{1}f_{2}\sin\theta n(\epsilon_{1})\Omega\Big[\frac{\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{2}}{2}\Big)}{\epsilon_{2}}-\frac{\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\epsilon_{1}}{2}\Big)}{\epsilon_{1}}\Big].

(254)

Here
\epsilon_{2}=(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})/2+\sqrt{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}/4+t^{2}}
and

\epsilon_{1}=(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})/2-\sqrt{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}/4+t^{2}}
are the largest and middle energies of fermions in the system,
\sin\theta=2t/\sqrt{(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})^{2}+4t^{2}}
. Then, for example, in the limit
t\rightarrow 0
and
\omega_{2}>\omega_{1}
these expressions are simplified

\Delta G^{H}_{01}(0_{-})=\frac{2f_{1}f_{2}\Omega}{\omega_{1}}n(\omega_{1})\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\omega_{1}}{2}\Big),

(255)

\Delta G^{H}_{02}(0_{-})=\frac{2f_{1}f_{2}\Omega t}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}}n(\omega_{1})\Big[\frac{\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\omega_{1}}{2}\Big)}{\omega_{1}}-\frac{\tanh\Big(\frac{\beta\omega_{2}}{2}\Big)}{\omega_{2}}\Big].

(256)

Both expressions are real, so the Hartree contributions to the corresponding coherences just twice larger than the latter expressions. They coincide with the coherences obtained in this section.

Anti-C*algebras
Robert Pluta, Bernard Russo

Abstract
. We introduce a class of Banach algebras that we call anti-C*-algebras. We show that the normed standard embedding of a C*-ternary ring is the direct sum of a C*-algebra and an anti-C*-algebra. We prove that C*-ternary rings and anti-C*-algebras are semisimple. We give two new characterizations of C*-ternary rings which are isomorphic to a TRO (ternary ring of operators), providing answers to a query raised by Zettl in 1983, and we propose some problems for further study.

Theorem 2.7

The normed standard embedding

4.
Ideals

There is a one to one correspondence between closed ideals in a TRO
M
and closed ideals in the C*-algebra
R(M)
. This was proved directly (that is, not using
[
15
]
) in
[
2
, Prop. 3.8]
. This result was extended to C*-ternary rings in

[
1
, Prop. 4.2]
, by using the TRO
M_{+}\oplus M_{-}^{op}
and appealing to
[
15
, Theorem 3.22]
. It can also be proved by using the TRO
M_{+}\oplus M_{-}^{op}
and appealing to

[
2
, Prop. 3.8]
(in general, if (
M,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
is a C*-ternary ring, then
M^{op}
denotes the C*-ternary ring
(M,-[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
).
.

We now consider the corresponding property in the context of C*-ternary rings. We begin by modifying some earlier notation. Recall that if
M
is a C*-ternary ring,
\ell(f,g)
, for
f,g\in M
, is the element of
B(M\oplus M)
defined by
\ell(f,g)(x,y)=([fgx],[gfy])
. To emphasize the dependence on
M
we denote
\ell(f,g)
by
\ell_{M}(f,g)
.
Thus

L(M):=\overline{sp}\{\ell_{M}(f,g):f,g\in M\}\subset B(M\oplus M).

Let
I
be a closed ideal in the C*-ternary ring
M
.
Since
I
is also a C*-ternary ring,

L(I):=\overline{sp}\{\ell_{I}(f,g):f,g\in I\}\subset B(I\oplus I).

We now define

\widehat{L}(I)=\overline{sp}\{\ell_{M}(f,g):f,g\in I\}\subset L(M)\subset B(M\oplus M).

Note that

\ell_{I}(f,g)=\ell_{M}(f,g)|_{I\oplus I}.

Similarly, recall that
r(f,g)
, for
f,g\in M
, is the element of
B(M\oplus M)
defined by
r(f,g)(x,y)=([xfg],[ygf)
. To emphasize the dependence on
M
we denote
r(f,g)
by
r_{M}(f,g)
.
Thus

R(M):=\overline{sp}\{r_{M}(f,g):f,g\in M\}\subset B(M\oplus M).

For a close ideal
I
in
M
, we have

R(I):=\overline{sp}\{r_{I}(f,g):f,g\in I\}\subset B(I\oplus I),

and we define

\widehat{R}(I)=\overline{sp}\{r_{M}(f,g):f,g\in I\}\subset R(M)\subset B(M\oplus M).

Note that

r_{I}(f,g)=r_{M}(f,g)|_{I\oplus I}.

The proof of the following proposition is a straightforward application of the definitions.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
I
be a closed ideal in the C*-ternary ring
M
.

(a):

\widehat{L}(I)
is a closed two-sided ideal in the C*-algebra
L(M)
, and the map
\phi
defined as
\phi(\ell_{M}(f,g))=\ell_{I}(f,g)
, for
f,g\in I
, extends to a contractive *-homomorphism of
\widehat{L}(I)
onto
L(I)
.

(b):

\widehat{R}(I)
is a closed two-sided ideal in the C*-algebra
L(M)
, and the map
\psi
defined as
\psi(r_{M}(f,g))=r_{I}(f,g)
, for
f,g\in I
, extends to a contractive *-homomorphism of
\widehat{R}(I)
onto
R(I)
.

We next define

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(I)=\left[\begin{matrix}\widehat{L}(I)&I\\
\overline{I}&\widehat{R}(I)\end{matrix}\right]\subset{\mathcal{A}}(M)=\left[\begin{matrix}L(M)&M\\
\overline{M}&R(M)\end{matrix}\right].

The proof of the following proposition is also a straightforward application of the definitions.
It is included for the convenience of the reader and to motivate one of the questions which follows it.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
I
be a closed ideal in a C*-ternary ring
M
. Then
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(I)
is a closed self-adjoint ideal in
{\mathcal{A}}(M)
. The map
I\mapsto\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(I)
is injective from closed ideals of
M
to closed self-adjoint two-sided ideals of
{\mathcal{A}}(M)
.

Proof.

We show for example that
{\mathcal{A}}(I)
is a right ideal in
{\mathcal{A}}(M)
.
For

\left[\begin{matrix}A&f\\
\overline{g}&B\end{matrix}\right]\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}(I)\hbox{ and }\left[\begin{matrix}A^{\prime}&f^{\prime}\\
\overline{g^{\prime}}&B^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]\in{\mathcal{A}}(M)

we have, by (
2.1
),

\left[\begin{matrix}A&f\\
\overline{g}&B\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}A^{\prime}&f^{\prime}\\
\overline{g^{\prime}}&B^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}AA^{\prime}+\ell(f,g^{\prime})&A\cdot f^{\prime}+f\cdot B^{\prime}\\
\overline{g}\cdot A^{\prime}+B\cdot\overline{g^{\prime}}&r(g,f^{\prime})+B\circ B^{\prime}\end{matrix}\right].

and it is required to show that

(4.1)

AA^{\prime}+\ell(f,g^{\prime})\in\widehat{L}(I),

(4.2)

A\cdot f^{\prime}+f\cdot B^{\prime}\in I

(4.3)

\overline{g}\cdot A^{\prime}+B\cdot\overline{g^{\prime}}\in\overline{I}

(4.4)

r(g,f^{\prime})+B\circ B^{\prime}\in\widehat{R}(I).

To prove (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we may assume that
A=\ell_{M}(g,h)
for
g,h\in I
,
A^{\prime}=\ell_{M}(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})
for
g^{\prime},h^{\prime}\in M
, and
B^{\prime}=r_{M}(g^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime})
for
g^{\prime\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in M
. By
[
14
, Lemma 1.1(ii)]
,

AA^{\prime}=\ell_{M}(g,h)\ell_{M}(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})=\ell_{M}(g,[h^{\prime}g^{\prime}h])\in\widehat{L}(I).

Write
f=[ggg]
for some
g\in I
(see footnote 3). Then
\ell_{M}(f,g^{\prime})=\ell_{M}(g,[g^{\prime}gg])\in\widehat{L}(I)
, proving (
4.1
).
Since
A\cdot f^{\prime}=[ghf^{\prime}]\in I
and
f\cdot B^{\prime}=[fg^{\prime\prime}h^{\prime\prime}]\in I
, this proves (
4.2
). (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) are proved similarly.
∎

We close this section by using the above notation to prove the last statement of Theorem
2.7
.

Proposition 4.3
.

An anti-C*-algebra is linearly homeomorphic to a C*-algebra.

Proof.

We consider the anti-C*-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(M_{-})
corresponding to an arbitrary C*-ternary ring with Zettl decomposition
M=M_{+}\oplus M_{-}
.
The map

{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(M_{-})\ni\left[\begin{matrix}\sum_{i}\ell_{M_{-}}(x_{i},y_{i})&z\\
\overline{w}&\sum_{j}r_{M_{-}}(u_{j},v_{j})\end{matrix}\right]\mapsto\left[\begin{matrix}\sum_{i}\ell_{M_{-}^{op}}(x_{i},y_{i})&z\\
\overline{w}&\sum_{j}r_{M_{-}}^{op}(u_{j},v_{j})\end{matrix}\right]\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}(M_{-}^{op})

is bijective and bounded, so extends to a bicontinuous bijection between
{\mathcal{A}}(M_{-})
and the C*-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}(M_{-}^{op})
.
∎

5.2.

Remark 5.10

(Completion of the proof of

In the proof of

In the proof of

Proposition 5.11

A C*-ternary ring

satisfies Pelczynski’s property V.

According to the theorem of Zettl
[

, there is a bounded operator

on

such that

is isomorphic to a TRO. Therefore

satisfies Pelczynski’s property V. Since
(M,[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
and
(M,T\circ[\cdot,\cdot,\cdot])
are identical as Banach spaces, the proposition is proved.
∎

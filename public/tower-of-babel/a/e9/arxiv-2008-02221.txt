On automorphisms of flexible varieties
S. Kaliman and D. Udumyan

Abstract
. Let X X be a flexible variety and φ : C 1 → C 2 \varphi:C_{1}\to C_{2} be an isomorphism of closed one-dimensional subschemes of X X . We develop several criteria which guarantee that φ \varphi extends to an automorphism of X X .



Introduction

Every algebraic variety
X
in this paper is considered over an algebraically closed field
{\rm\bf k}
of characteristic 0.
We study the following question:

when can an isomorphism

f:Y_{1}\to Y_{2}
of subschemes of
X
be extended to an automorphism of
X
?

For
X\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
and
Y_{i}\simeq{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
the answer to this question is known to be always positive in all cases
but
n=3
(see
[
AMo
]
,
[
Su
]
,
[
Cr
]
,
[
Je
]
). For
n=3
the answer is unknown but if
{\rm\bf k}=\mathbb{C}
, then

f
can be extended to a holomorphic automorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{3}

[
Ka92
]
. Furthermore, very general sufficient conditions
for extension of isomorphisms of closed subvarieties of
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
to automorphisms of
{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
where found in
[
Ka91
]
and
[
Sr
]

(in a weaker form they were already present in
[
Je
]
).
For
X\neq{\mathbb{A}}^{n}
the first break-through is due
to van Santen (formerly Stampfli) who proved that every isomorphism of smooth polynomial curves in
X\simeq\mathop{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),\,n\geq 3

is extendable to an automorphism of
X

[
St
]
. Later jointly with Feller he showed that the same is true when
Y_{i}\simeq\mathbb{C}

and
X
is any complex linear algebraic group different from
\mathbb{C}^{3}
,
\mathop{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
or
{\rm PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

[
FS
]
.
Some extra cases with a positive answer to the extension problem were found in
[
Ka20
]
. Say, the answer is positive when

X
is a nonzero fiber of a non-degenerate quadric form in
{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
(we call such
X
an
(m-1)
-sphere over
{\rm\bf k}
)
and
Y_{i}
is a smooth closed subvariety with
\dim Y_{i}\leq\frac{m}{3}-1
.

Starting from dimension 2 affine spaces, linear algebraic groups without nontrivial characters and spheres over
{\rm\bf k}
are examples
of so-called flexible varieties (recall that a normal quasi-affine variety
X
is flexible if
\mathop{\rm SAut}(X)
acts transitively on the smooth part

X_{\rm reg}
of
X
where
\mathop{\rm SAut}(X)
is
the subgroup of the group
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
of algebraic automorphisms of
X
generated by one-parameter unipotent subgroups).
For a smooth flexible variety
X
every isomorphism of zero-dimensional subvarieties is extendable to an automorphism
[
AFKKZ
]
. Furthermore,
recall that for a closed subvariety
Y
of
X
with defining ideal
I
in the ring
A={\rm\bf k}[X]
of regular functions on
X

its
k
th infinitesimal neighborhood is the subscheme of
X
with the defining ideal
I^{k}
(in particular, an automorphism of such neighborhood
is essentially an automorphism of the algebra
\frac{A}{I^{k}}
).
It follows from
[
AFKKZ
, Theorem 4.14 and Remark 4.16-4.17]
that any automorphism
g:Z\to Z
of an
k
th infinitesimal neighborhood
Z

of a zero dimensional subvariety
Y
of a smooth flexible variety
X
is extendable to an automorphism of
X

provided that
g|_{Y}={\rm id}_{Y}
and
g
preserves local volume forms modulo
I^{k-1}
(we say that such maps of infinitesimal neighborhoods
have Jacobian 1).
The second author gave a proof in
[
Ud
]
of the similar result
in the case when
Y
is a smooth polynomial curve in a smooth flexible variety
X
of
\dim X\geq 4
.

In this paper we give a much stronger version of this result (Theorem
6.6
) and develop some new important technical tools for the extension problem.
In particular, instead of polynomial curves we consider smooth curves in
X
with trivial normal bundles.
(The class of such curves is sufficiently big. It contains, for instance, curves which have only rational irreducible components.
Furthermore, consider
X
as a closed subvariety of an affine space.
Then by the Bertini theorem the intersection of
X
with
n-1
general hyperplanes where
\dim X=n

is a smooth curve. Every irreducible component of this curve has a trivial normal bundle.)
As an application we get the following generalization of the Feller-van Santen theorem (Corollary
8.6
).

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
X
be isomorphic to a complex linear algebraic group without nontrivial characters
and let
\dim X\geq 4
.
Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be smooth polynomial curves in
X
.
Then every isomorphism of
k
th infinitesimal neighborhoods of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
with Jacobian 1 extends to an automorphism of
X
.

The absence of nontrivial characters is essential - without this assumption this theorem does not hold (see Remark
8.7
).
If
C_{1}=C_{2}
, then we can make a stronger statement in the holomorphic category without assuming that
C_{i}
is
a polynomial curve or even that
C_{i}
is irreducible. Namely, the holomorphic category has the advantage of
the Ivarsson-Kutzschebauch theorem
[
IK
]
which leads, in particular, to
the following (Corollary
9.11
).

Theorem 0.2
.

Let
X
be isomorphic to a complex linear algebraic group without nontrivial characters
and
\dim X\geq 4
. Suppose that
C
is a smooth closed curve in
X
with a trivial normal bundle
and defining ideal
{\hat{I}}
in the algebra
\operatorname{{\rm Hol}}(X)
of holomorphic functions on
X
.
Let
{\hat{A}}=\frac{\operatorname{{\rm Hol}}(X)}{{\hat{I}}}
be the algebra of holomorphic functions on
C
.
Then every
{\hat{A}}
-automorphism
\theta:\frac{\operatorname{{\rm Hol}}(X)}{{\hat{I}}^{k}}\to\frac{\operatorname{{\rm Hol}}(X)}{{\hat{I}}^{k}}

with Jacobian 1 extends to a holomorphic automorphism of
X
.

In the case of
X\simeq\mathop{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
we can say even more (Corollary
9.12
).

Theorem 0.3
.

Let
X
be isomorphic to
\mathop{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
where
n\geq 3
. Suppose that
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are isomorphic smooth closed curves in
X

such that their normal bundles in
X
are trivial.
Then every isomorphism of
k
th infinitesimal neighborhoods of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
with Jacobian 1 extends to a
holomorphic automorphism of
X
.

In the case of sphere over
{\rm\bf k}
the similar result holds in the algebraic category (Corollary
8.11
).

Theorem 0.4
.

Let
X
be an
(m-1)
-sphere over
{\rm\bf k}
where
m\geq 6
.
Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be isomorphic smooth closed curves in
X
such that their irreducible components are rational.
Then every isomorphism of
k
th infinitesimal neighborhoods of
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
with Jacobian 1 extends
to an automorphism of
X
.

Let us briefly discuss the crucial step behind these theorems and the idea of its proof. As we mentioned, we
consider a more general situation when
X
is a flexible variety of
n:=\dim X\geq 4
,
C
is a smooth closed
curve in
X
with a trivial normal bundle
N_{X}C
and defining ideal
I\subset{\rm\bf k}[X]
. The consideration can be reduced to the
case of an irreducible
C
. Then
A={\rm\bf k}[C]\simeq\frac{{\rm\bf k}[X]}{I}
is a Dedekind domain and
N_{X}C

can be viewed as a free
A
-module with a basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}
.
In particular, any
A
-automorphism of
N_{X}C
can be presented by an element of
\operatorname{{\rm GL}}_{n-1}(A)
where the latter group contains
a subgroup
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A)
generated by elementary matrices of the form
{\rm id}+ae_{ij}
(where
{\rm id}
is the identity matrix,
a\in A
,
1\leq i\neq j\leq n-1
and
e_{ij}

is the matrix with entry 1 in position
(i,j)
and zeros everywhere else).
The crucial step is that under certain mild assumptions one has the following claim.

Every
A
-automorphism of
N_{X}C
that is presented by an element of
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A)
is induced by an automorphism of
X
.

Assume for simplicity that
X
is smooth affine and that the natural action of a one-parameter unipotent subgroup of
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
on
X

(which is also called a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action) has a finitely generated ring
A^{\mathbb{G}_{a}}
of invariants.
That is, there exists the algebraic quotient morphism
\varrho:X\to Q=\operatorname{{\rm Spec}}A^{\mathbb{G}_{a}}
of this action sending
X
to the normal affine variety
Q
.
Recall that such a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action can be viewed as the flow of a so-called locally nilpotent vector field
\delta
on
X
.
Let
C^{\prime}
be the closure of
\varrho(C)
in
Q
and
f
be a regular function on
Q
which vanishes on
C^{\prime}
.
Then
\varrho^{*}(f)\delta
is again a locally nilpotent vector field whose flow
\exp(t\varrho^{*}(f)\delta)
leaves every point of
C
fixed and, in particular,
it induces
A
-automorphisms of
N_{X}C
. The plan is to show that every
A
-automorphism of
N_{X}C
given by an
element of
\mathop{\rm E}(A)
is induced by a composition of automorphisms of
X
of the form
\exp(t\varrho^{*}(f)\delta)
with
\delta
running over the set of locally
nilpotent vector fields on
X
.
To follow through this plan we use the fact (
[
Ka20
, Theorem 6.1]
) that
\delta
can be chosen so that for a given finite subset
K
of
C

there is an open subset
V^{\prime}
of
C^{\prime}
for which
V=\varrho^{-1}(C^{\prime})\cap C
contains
K
and
\varrho|_{V}:V\to V^{\prime}
is an isomorphism.
Let
C\setminus V
be the zero locus of an ideal
{\mathfrak{q}}\subset A
in
C
,

\mathop{\rm F}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}})
be the subgroup of
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A)
generated by elementary matrices of the form
{\rm id}+ae_{ij}
where
a\in{\mathfrak{q}}

and
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}})
be the normal closure of
\mathop{\rm F}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}})
in
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A)
.
Consider sections
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n-1}
of
N_{X}C
such that they generate the free
{\rm\bf k}[V]
-module
N_{X}V
and let
M
be the
free
A
-submodule of
N_{X}C

generated by these sections. Then abundance of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on
X
enables us to prove that shrinking
V
one can suppose that
for an appropriate choice of
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n-1}
and sufficiently large
k>0
every
A
-automorphism of
M
presented by a matrix from
\mathop{\rm F}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}}^{k})

with respect to the basis
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n-1}
is
induced by a composition of automorphisms of the form
\exp(t\varrho^{*}(f)\delta)
(see, Corollary
6.14
).
By
[
Ni
, Theorem 3]

\mathop{\rm F}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}}^{k})\supset\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}}^{2k})
.
Now one needs to switch from the basis
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n-1}
in
M
to the basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}
in
N_{X}C

which requires some results about matrices over
Dedekind domains from
[
BMS
]
and
[
Ba
]
(see, Theorem
5.2
).
Under this change of bases the group
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}}^{2k})
is transformed to a group containing

\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}}^{2mk})
for some
m>0
. Hence,
every
A
-automorphism of
N_{X}C
presented by a matrix from
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}}^{2mk})

with respect to the basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}
is
induced by a composition of automorphisms of the form
\exp(t\varrho^{*}(f)\delta)
(see, Corollary
6.14
).
Then, replacing
K
by
C\setminus V
in the argument above, we show that for some ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}
in
A
such that
{\mathfrak{q}}+{\mathfrak{p}}=A

every
A
-automorphism of
N_{X}C
presented by a matrix from
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{p}}^{2mk})
is again induced by an automorphism of
X
.
Observing, that
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{q}}^{2mk})
and
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A,{\mathfrak{p}}^{2mk})
generate
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A)
we get the desired claim.

With this claim at hand we note that for an
A
-automorphism
\theta:\frac{{\rm\bf k}[X]}{I^{2}}\to\frac{{\rm\bf k}[X]}{I^{2}}

of the second infinitesimal neighborhood of
C
the assumption that the Jacobian is 1 is
equivalent to the fact that the induced automorphism of
N_{X}C
is contained in
\mathop{\rm SL}_{n-1}(A)
.
This implies that if the claim before is valid, then
\theta
is induced by an automorphism of
X
provided
\mathop{\rm SL}_{n-1}(A)=\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}(A)
. Say, rational curves satisfy this property
(which is the reason why such curves appear in Theorem
0.4
),
whereas in the holomorphic category one has always
\mathop{\rm SL}_{n-1}({\hat{A}})=\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}({\hat{A}})
by virtue of the Ivarsson-Kutzschebauch theorem
(see, Proposition
9.3
). To extend this result to automorphisms
\tilde{\theta}:\frac{{\rm\bf k}[X]}{I^{k}}\to\frac{{\rm\bf k}[X]}{I^{k}}
of the
k
th infinitesimal neighborhood of
C

we use induction on
k
with the case
k=2
as the first step of induction. For implication
k-1\implies k

it suffices to show that
\tilde{\theta}
is induced by an automorphism of
X
if its restriction to the
(k-1)
st infinitesimal neighborhood of
C
is trivial. The proof of the latter statement essentially copies the similar step in the proof of

[
AFKKZ
, Theorem 4.14]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 1 we collect technical tools developed in
[
Ka20
]

that are important for this paper.
Section 2 contains some criterion which enables us to deal with
curves that are not once-punctured (recall that a curve
C
is once-punctured
if for any completion
{\bar{C}}
of
C
the set
{\bar{C}}\setminus C
is a singleton).
The advantage of a once-punctured curve (like
\mathbb{C}
)
is that every morphism of this curve
to a quasi-affine algebraic variety is automatically proper. Meanwhile, the assumptions under which we can prove the crucial step described before
include the properness of the morphisms
\varrho|_{C}:C\to Q
.
The criterion we developed guarantees that for any closed curve
Z
in a flexible variety
X

and a morphism
\tau:X\to P
to an affine variety
P
there is an automorphism
\alpha
of
X
such that

\tau\circ\alpha|_{Z}:Z\to Q
is proper.
In Section 3 we fix some notations for the rest of the paper and spell out a convention under which our criterion of properness works.
In Section 4 using this convention we describe the structure of normal bundles of smooth curves in a large class of flexible varieties.
Actually, we show later (Corollary
7.4
) that if a normal bundle of a smooth closed curve in such variety is trivial, then every nonvanishing
section of this bundle is induced by a locally nilpotent vector field (cf.,
[
AFKKZ
, Corollary 4.3]
).
Section 5 contains some facts about special linear groups and their subgroups generated by elementary transformations over commutative rings.
In Sections 6 we prove the main theorem (Theorem
6.6
) that states that
under the assumption on Jacobians and some other mild assumptions automorphisms of
k
th infinitesimal neighborhoods of smooth closed irreducible curves in a flexible
variety
X
whose normal bundles are trivial extend to automorphisms of
X
. In Section 7 we remove the assumption of irreducibility of these curves in
the main theorem.
As applications we prove Theorems
0.1
and
0.4
in Section 8. Section 9 contains Theorems
0.2
and
0.3
.

1.
Preliminaries

In this section we present some technical tools developed in
[
Ka20
]
which require the following
definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

(1) Given an irreducible algebraic variety
{\mathcal{A}}
and
a map
\varphi:{\mathcal{A}}\to\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
(where
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
is the group of algebraic automorphisms of
X
) we say that
({\mathcal{A}},\varphi)

is an
algebraic family of automorphisms of
X
if the induced map

{\mathcal{A}}\times X\to X
,
(\alpha,x)\mapsto\varphi(\alpha).x
is a morphism (see
[
Ra
]
).

(2) If we want to emphasize additionally that
\varphi({\mathcal{A}})
is contained in a subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
, then we say that

{\mathcal{A}}
is an
algebraic
G
-family
of automorphisms of
X
.

(3) In the case when
{\mathcal{A}}
is a connected algebraic group and the induced map

{\mathcal{A}}\times X\to X
is not only a morphism but also an action of
{\mathcal{A}}
on
X
we call this family a
connected algebraic subgroup
of
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
.

Definition 1.2
.

We call a subgroup
G
of
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)

algebraically generated
if it is generated as an abstract group by a family

{\mathcal{G}}
of connected algebraic subgroups of
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
.

Definition 1.3
.

(1) A nonzero derivation
\delta
on the ring
A
of regular functions on an affine algebraic variety
X
is called
locally nilpotent

if for every
0\neq a\in A
there exists a natural
n
for which
\delta^{n}(a)=0
.
This derivation can be viewed as a vector field on
X
which
we also call
locally nilpotent
. The set of all locally nilpotent vector fields on
X
will be denoted by
\operatorname{{\rm LND}}(X)
.
The flow of
\delta\in\operatorname{{\rm LND}}(X)

is an algebraic
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on
X
, i.e., the action of the group
({\rm\bf k},+)

which can be viewed as a one-parameter unipotent group
U
in the group
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
of all algebraic automorphisms of
X
.
In fact, every
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action is a flow of a locally nilpotent vector field (e.g, see
[
Fr
, Proposition 1.28]
).

(2) Note that the kernel
\operatorname{{\rm Ker}}\delta
of
\delta
coincides with the ring
{\rm\bf k}[X]^{\mathbb{G}_{a}}
of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-invariants in
{\rm\bf k}[X]

where
{\rm\bf k}[X]^{\mathbb{G}_{a}}
can be always treated as the ring of regular functions on a quasi-affine variety
V

[
Wi1
, Theorem 1]
).
The morphism
\tau:X\to V
induced by the natural embedding

{\rm\bf k}[X]^{\mathbb{G}_{a}}\hookrightarrow{\rm\bf k}[X]
is called the
categorical quotient morphism
of the
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action.
When
V
is an affine variety (i.e., when
{\rm\bf k}[X]^{\mathbb{G}_{a}}
is finitely generated)
we call
\tau
an
algebraic quotient morphism
.
Since
V
is not always affine by Nagata’s example, we use sometimes instead of
\tau
partial quotient morphisms.
Namely, a morphism
\varrho:X\to Q
into a normal affine variety
Q
is called a
partial quotient morphism associated with
\delta
if
the ring
\varrho^{*}({\rm\bf k}[Q])
is contained in
\operatorname{{\rm Ker}}\delta
and general fibers of
\varrho
are isomorphic
to
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
. If
X
is normal, then partial quotient
morphisms always exist by virtue of the Rosenlicht Theorem (e.g., see
[
PV
, Theorem 2.3]
and
[
FKZ
, Definition 2.16]
).

(3) If
X
is a quasi-affine variety, then an algebraic vector field
\delta
on
X
is called
locally nilpotent
if
\delta
extends
to a locally nilpotent vector field
\tilde{\delta}
on some affine algebraic variety
Y
containing
X
as an open subset such
that
\tilde{\delta}
vanishes on
Y\setminus X
where
{\rm codim}_{Y}(Y\setminus X)\geq 2
. Note that under this assumption

\delta
generates a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on
X
and we use again the notation
\operatorname{{\rm LND}}(X)
for the set of all locally nilpotent vector
fields on
X
. As a partial quotient morphism
\varrho:X\to Q
of
\delta
we consider the restriction of a partial quotient morphism of
\tilde{\delta}
to
X
.

Remark 1.4
.

Definition
1.3
(2)-(3) implies that
for a dense open smooth subvariety

Q_{0}
of
Q
and
X_{0}=\varrho^{-1}(Q_{0})
the morphism
\varrho|_{X_{0}}:X_{0}\to Q_{0}
is smooth and surjectve
(in fact, one can assume that
X_{0}
is naturally isomorphic to
Q_{0}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
by
[
KaMi
, Theorem 2]
)
and
\delta
does not vanish on
X_{0}
.

Definition 1.5
.

(1) For every locally nilpotent vector fields
\delta
and each function
f\in\operatorname{{\rm Ker}}\delta
from its kernel the field

f\delta
is called a replica of
\delta
. Recall that such replica is automatically locally nilpotent.

(2) Let
{\mathcal{N}}
be a set of locally nilpotent vector fields on
X
and
G_{\mathcal{N}}\subset\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
denotes the group generated
by all flows of elements of
{\mathcal{N}}
. We say that
G_{\mathcal{N}}

is generated by
{\mathcal{N}}
.

(3) A collection of locally nilpotent vector fields
{\mathcal{N}}
is called saturated if
{\mathcal{N}}
is closed under conjugation by elements in
G_{\mathcal{N}}

and for every
\delta\in{\mathcal{N}}
each replica of
\delta
is
also contained in
{\mathcal{N}}
.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
X
be a normal quasi-affine algebraic variety of dimension at least 2,

{\mathcal{N}}
be a saturated set of locally nilpotent vector fields on
X
and
G=G_{\mathcal{N}}
be the group
generated by
{\mathcal{N}}
.
Then
X
is called
G
-flexible if for any point
x
in the smooth part
X_{\rm reg}
of
X
the vector space
T_{x}X
is generated
by the values of locally nilpotent vector fields from
{\mathcal{N}}
at
x

(which is equivalent to the fact that
G
acts transitively on
X_{\rm reg}

[
FKZ
, Theorem 2.12]
). In the case of
G=\mathop{\rm SAut}(X)
we call
X
flexible
without referring to
\mathop{\rm SAut}(X)
(recall that
\mathop{\rm SAut}(X)
is the subgroup of
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}X
generated by all one-parameter unipotent subgroups).

The following Collective Transversality Theorem is one of the main technical tools in
[
Ka20
]
.

Theorem 1.7
.

(
[
Ka20
, Theorem 1.4]
, cf.
[
AFKKZ
, Theorem 1.15 ]
)
Let
X
and
P
be smooth irreducible
algebraic varieties and
\kappa:X\to P
be a smooth morphism.
Let
G
be a subgroup of the group
\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X/P)
of automorphisms of
X
over
P
such that
G
is
algebraically generated by a system
{\mathcal{G}}
of connected algebraic
subgroups closed under conjugation in
G
.
Suppose that the restriction of the
G
-action to
\kappa^{-1}(p)
is transitive for every
p\in P
.

Then there exist subgroups
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}\in{\mathcal{G}}
such that for
any locally closed reduced subschemes
Y
and
Z
in
X
one can
find a dense open subset
U=U(Y,Z)\subseteq H_{1}\times\ldots\times H_{m}
so that every element
(h_{1},\ldots,h_{m})\in U
satisfies the following:

(i)

\dim(Y\cap(h_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot h_{m}).Z)\leq\dim(Y\times_{P}Z)+\dim P-\dim X
.

In particular, when
\dim Y\times_{P}Z\leq\dim Y+\dim Z-\dim P

-suitable.

Proof.

Fixing some basis
\bar{u}=(u_{1},u_{2})
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with the natural action of
\mathop{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
we get
a unipotent subgroup
H_{1}
(resp.
H_{2}
) of upper (resp. lower) triangular matrices in
\mathop{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\simeq F
.
The
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action induced by
H_{1}
(resp.
H_{2}
) on
X
is
the flow of a locally nilpotent vector field
\delta_{1}
(resp.
\delta_{2}
).
By
[
Had
, Theorem 3.1]
there exists an algebraic quotient morphism
\varrho:X\to Q
associated with
\delta_{1}
.
The central fact we use now is that

\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
are semi-compatible and even compatible in the terminology of
[
DDK
, Theorem 12]
.
However,
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
(resp.
\delta_{1}
and
\delta_{2}
) depend on the choice of
\bar{u}
.
In particular, for every
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
the groups
\alpha H_{1}\alpha^{-1}
and
\alpha H_{2}\alpha^{-1}

present upper and lower triangular matrices in an appropriate basis (note that this conjugation sends
\delta_{i}
to
\alpha_{*}(\delta_{i})
).
Consider
{\mathcal{B}}=H_{2}
and let us show that for general
\alpha\neq\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}
the locally nilpotent vector fields

\alpha_{*}(\delta_{1})
and
\beta_{*}(\delta_{1})
are semi-compatible, or, equivalently,
in an appropriate basis
\alpha H_{1}\alpha^{-1}
and
\beta H_{1}\beta^{-1}
correspond to upper and lower unipotent matrices respectively.
The latter is equivalent to the similar claim about the groups
\beta^{-1}\alpha H_{1}\alpha^{-1}\beta
and
H_{1}
.
We can suppose that
\alpha:(u_{1},u_{2})\mapsto(u_{1},u_{2}+au_{1})
and
\beta:(u_{1},u_{2})\mapsto(u_{1},u_{2}+bu_{1})
.
Then a direct computation shows that
\beta^{-1}\alpha H_{1}\alpha^{-1}\beta
consists of matrices of the form

\left[{\begin{array}[]{cc}1-(a-b)t&t\\
-(a-b)^{2}t&1+(a-b)t\end{array}}\right].

Another direct computation shows that for the automorphism
\gamma:(u_{1},u_{2})\mapsto(u_{1}+(a-b)^{-1}u_{2},u_{2})

one has
\gamma H_{2}\gamma^{-1}=\beta^{-1}\alpha H_{1}\alpha^{-1}\beta
. Hence,
\beta^{-1}\alpha H_{1}\alpha^{-1}\beta
and
H_{1}

correspond to upper and lower unipotent matrices in an appropriate basis since
\gamma H_{2}\gamma^{-1}
and
H_{1}=\gamma H_{1}\gamma^{-1}
do.
Thus,

\alpha_{*}(\delta_{1})
and
\beta_{*}(\delta_{1})
are suitable for general
\alpha\neq\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}
which yields the desired conclusion.
∎

Example 2.8
.

Let
H
be a complex semi-simple Lie group with Lie algebra different from
{\mathfrak{s}}{\mathfrak{l}}_{2}
.
Let
R
be a proper closed reductive subgroup of
H
. Then the homogeneous space
H/R
is
parametrically suitable. Indeed, by
[
DDK
, Theorem 24 and the proof of Corollary 25]

H/R
admits a non-degenerate fixed point free
\mathop{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-action.
Hence, we are done by Theorem
2.7
.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
X
be a smooth affine algebraic variety equipped with a fixed point free
non-degenerate
\mathop{\rm SL}_{2}({\rm\bf k})
-action. Let
\varrho:X\to Q
and
{\mathcal{B}}
be the same as in the proof of Theorem
2.7
(but with
\mathbb{C}
replaced by
{\rm\bf k}
).
Then for every closed curve
C
in
X
and a general
\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}
the morphism
\varrho_{\beta}|_{C}=\varrho\circ\beta^{-1}|_{C}:C\to Q
is proper.

Proof.

If
{\rm\bf k}
is a universal domain (i.e., it is an algebraically closed field of characteristic 0 with infinite transcendence degree over
\mathbb{Q}
),
then one can just refer to
[
Ek
]
. To include a finite transcendence degree in the proof one can argue like this.
Let
{\tilde{C}}
be an open subset in a completion of
C
consisting of
C
and one extra point
z
.
Consider
\tau_{\beta}:{\tilde{C}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\dashrightarrow Q
that sends each
(c,\beta)\in C\times{\mathcal{B}}
to
\varrho_{\beta}(c)
.
Assume that
\varrho_{\beta}:C\to Q
is not proper for
a general
\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Then for some
{\tilde{C}}
as above
\tau_{\beta}
is a morphism.
By the “finite extension principle" there exist an algebraically closed subfield
{\rm\bf k}^{0}
of
{\rm\bf k}
which is of a finite transcendence degree over of
\mathbb{Q}
,
algebraic varieties
X^{0},Q^{0},{\tilde{C}}^{0}=C^{0}\cup z^{0},{\mathcal{B}}^{0}
over
{\rm\bf k}^{0}
,
morphisms
\varrho_{\beta}^{0}:C^{0}\to Q^{0}
,
\tau_{\beta}^{0}:{\tilde{C}}^{0}\times{\mathcal{B}}^{0}\rightarrow Q^{0}
and an
\mathop{\rm SL}_{2}({\rm\bf k}_{0})
-action on
X^{0}
such that

\varrho_{\beta}:C\to Q
,
\tau_{\beta}:{\tilde{C}}\times{\mathcal{B}}\to Q
and the
\mathop{\rm SL}_{2}({\rm\bf k})
-action on
X
are obtained by the base extension
\otimes_{\operatorname{{\rm Spec}}{\rm\bf k}_{0}}\operatorname{{\rm Spec}}{\rm\bf k}
.
By the Lefschetz principle we can view
{\rm\bf k}^{0}
as a subfield of
\mathbb{C}
. The extension
\otimes_{\operatorname{{\rm Spec}}{\rm\bf k}_{0}}\operatorname{{\rm Spec}}\mathbb{C}

leads to morphisms
\varrho_{\beta}^{\mathbb{C}}:C^{\mathbb{C}}\to Q^{\mathbb{C}}
,
\tau_{\beta}^{\mathbb{C}}:{\tilde{C}}^{\mathbb{C}}\times{\mathcal{B}}^{\mathbb{C}}\rightarrow Q^{\mathbb{C}}
of complex varieties and a non-degenerate
fixed point free
\mathop{\rm SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-action on
X^{\mathbb{C}}
. However, Theorems
2.4
and
2.7
imply that
\varrho_{\beta}^{\mathbb{C}}:C^{\mathbb{C}}\to Q^{\mathbb{C}}

is proper for general
\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}
which contradicts the existence of the morphism
\tau_{\beta}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Hence, we have the desired conclusion.
∎

3.
Notations and Convention

Let us list some notations which will remain unchanged through the rest of the paper.

Setting 3.1
.

We suppose further that

-
X
is a normal quasi-affine variety of dimension
n
;

-
\delta
is a locally nilpotent nonzero vector field on
X
;

-
\varrho:X\to Q
is a partial quotient morphism associated with
\delta
;

-
Q_{0}
is a smooth open dense subset of
Q
such that
\varrho|_{\varrho^{-1}(Q_{0})}:\varrho^{-1}(Q_{0})\to Q_{0}
is a smooth morphism
and
\delta
does not vanish on
\varrho^{-1}(Q_{0})
(i.e.,
Q_{0}
is as in Remark
1.4
);

- for any
\alpha\in\operatorname{{\rm Aut}}(X)
we let
\delta_{\alpha}=\alpha_{*}(\delta)
and
\varrho_{\alpha}=\varrho\circ\alpha^{-1}:X\to Q

(i.e.,
\varrho_{\alpha}
is a partial quotient morphism associated with
\delta_{\alpha}
);

-
C
is a smooth (but not necessarily irreducible) curve in
X
such that
C\subset X_{\rm reg}
and
C
is closed
in an affine variety containing
X
as a dense open subset;

-
I
is the defining ideal of
C
in
{\rm\bf k}[X]
(except for Section 5 where
I
is an identity matrix);

-
\mathop{\rm pr}:TX|_{C}\to N_{X}C
is the natural projection onto the normal bundle
N_{X}C
of
C
in
X
.

In the case when
C
contains an irreducible component which is not a once-punctured curve we shall often use
the following important convention.

Convention 3.2
.

Suppose additionally that
X
is a
G
-flexible variety
with
\dim X=n\geq 4
where the group
G\subset\mathop{\rm SAut}(X)
is generated
by a saturated set
{\mathcal{N}}\subset\operatorname{{\rm LND}}(X)
such that
0\neq\delta\in{\mathcal{N}}
and the next two
conditions are true:

(
\#
) the variety
\varrho^{-1}(Q\setminus Q_{0})
is of codimension at least
2
in
X

(in particular, the zero locus of
\delta
is at least of codimension 2);

(
\#\#
)
X
is affine and parametrically
G
-suitable with respect to
\delta
.

Remark 3.3
.

The condition (
\#
) cannot be acheived for some flexible Gizatullin surfaces but in
higher dimensions the authors do no know examples of smooth affine flexible varieties when (
\#
) does not hold for an appropriate
\delta
.
For instance, if
X
is a linear algebraic group without nontrivial characters, then every nonzero nilpotent element of its Lie algebra
yields a locally nilpotent vector field on
X
for which condition (
\#
) is true.
Another example is a complex sphere given by
z_{1}^{2}+\ldots+z_{m}^{2}=1
where
\bar{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})
is a coordinate system on
{\mathbb{A}}^{m}
.
Then
\sigma=z_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{1}}-z_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{2}}
is a locally nilpotent vector field on this sphere whose zero locus
has codimension 2. The kernel of
\sigma
is generated by
z_{3},\ldots,z_{m}
and, therefore, the quotient space is
smooth and (
\#
) holds.

4.

Before describing

from Setting

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

be
G
-flexible

.

{\mathcal{A}}\to\prod_{x\in K}T_{x}X,\,\alpha\mapsto(\delta_{\alpha}(x))_{x\in K}

Statement (i) follows from Theorem
1.15
and Remark
1.16
(2) applied to

and the fact that

).

of

It turns out that under Convention
3.2
we can get a much stronger version of Proposition

.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let Convention
3.2
hold.
Then one can choose a perfect

-family

so

\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}

(i)

1.7

If

Recall that by Remark

6.
Main Theorem

In this section we present a strengthened version of the main result in
[

]
. Let us start with terminology.

Definition 6.1
.

(1) Let

and

and

(2) Let

.

(3) If in the previous definition

and

(resp.

) is an

-automorphism
of

\frac{{\rm\bf k}[X]}{I_{1}^{k}}

In the case of a flexible variety the Jacobian of an automorphism must be a constant function because of the following.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-affine algebraic variety equipped with a volume form
\omega
such that
every two points of

can be joined by a chain of polynomial curves (which is the case when

is flexible). Let
\alpha:X\to X

Then the Jacobian of

is constant. Furthermore, if
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm SAut}(X)

Proof.

The action of
\alpha

transforms

into a volume form
\omega_{\alpha}
. The Jacobian
f_{\alpha}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{\omega}
of this automorphism is an invertible function on

and

where

is the identity automorphism. Since the restriction of

to every polynomial curve is constant,
we see that
f_{\alpha}
is constant as
{\rm\bf k}
is an algebraically closed field.
Thus, for
\alpha\in\mathop{\rm SAut}(X)
the Jacobian

can be viewed
as a value of an invertible function

on
\mathop{\rm SAut}(X)
. By the definition any two elements

can be joined by
a chain of polynomial curves (corresponding to one-parameter unipotent groups). Hence,

is constant and, consequently,

which is the desired conclusion.
∎

To see how the above condition on Jacobians can be reformulated for automorphisms of infinitesimal neighborhoods of closed subvarieties of

Lemma 9.6
.

Let
v_{i}^{j}=\kappa_{j}(v_{i})
where the map
\kappa_{j}:N_{X}C\to N_{Q}C_{j}

is induced by
\varrho_{\alpha_{j}}:X\to Q
.
Then for every
j=1,\ldots,n-2
the vector bundle
N_{Q}C_{j}
is generated as
{\rm\bf k}[C_{j}]
-module by

v_{0}^{j},v_{1}^{j}
,
\ldots,v_{j-1}^{j},v_{j+1}^{j},\ldots,v_{n-2}^{j}
. Furthermore,
v_{1}^{n-1},\ldots,v_{n-2}^{n-1}

generate
N_{Q}C_{n-1}^{*}
where
C_{n-1}^{*}=\varrho_{\alpha_{n-1}}(C\setminus S)
and
S
is the zero locus of
q
.

Proof.

Let
x\in C
and
x_{j}=\varrho_{\alpha_{j}}(x)\in C_{j}
. For proving the first claim, it suffices to show that
the vectors
v_{0}^{j}(x_{j}),v_{1}^{j}(x_{j})
,
\ldots,v_{j-1}^{j}(x_{j}),v_{j+1}^{j}(x_{j})
,
\ldots,v_{n-2}^{j}(x_{j})
are linearly independent.
Note that
\kappa_{j}\circ\mathop{\rm pr}=\mathop{\rm pr}_{j}\circ\varrho_{\alpha_{j}*}
where
\mathop{\rm pr}_{j}:TQ|_{C_{j}}\to N_{Q}C_{j}

is the natural projection. Since the kernel of
\varrho_{\alpha_{j}*}
is generated by
\delta_{\alpha_{j}}
,
we see that the kernel of
\kappa_{j}
is generated by
v_{j}
.
Hence, dependence of the vectors above implies
dependence of
v_{0}(x),v_{1}(x),\ldots,v_{j}(x),\ldots,v_{n-2}(x)
contrary to the fact
that
v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-2}
generate
N_{X}C
. Hence, we have the first claim.
Since
v_{n-1}=qv_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}a_{i}v_{i}
, we see that
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}
form a basis of
N_{X}(C\setminus S)
.
Hence, the argument before implies that
v_{1}^{n-1},\ldots,v_{n-2}^{n-1}
form a basis of
N_{Q}C_{n-1}^{*}

and we are done.
∎

Lemma 9.7
.

Let

. Suppose that the rows and columns of every matrix in

by indices

Then

where

is contained in

(ii)

contains also all matrices of the form

where
1\leq k\neq l\leq n-2
,
b\in{\mathfrak{q}}
and
{\mathfrak{q}}\subset A
is an ideal whose zero locus is

.

Proof.

By Lemmas
9.4
and
9.6
for every

1\leq j\leq n-2
we can find
regular functions
f_{0},f_{1}
,
\ldots,f_{j-1},f_{j+1},\ldots,f_{n-2}\in\mathbb{C}[Q]
vanishing on

such that
v_{i}^{j}(f_{k})=\delta_{ik}
where
\delta_{ik}
is the Kronecker symbol and
k\neq i\in\{0,\ldots,n-2\}\setminus\{j\}

Recall that by Corollary
7.4
for every

there exists a locally nilpotent vector field
\sigma_{i}\in\operatorname{{\rm LND}}(X)

such that
v_{i}=\mathop{\rm pr}(\sigma_{i})
(e.g., for
1\leq i\leq n-1
one can put
\sigma_{i}=\delta_{\alpha_{i}}

We also have the equality
\kappa_{j}\circ\mathop{\rm pr}=\mathop{\rm pr}_{j}\circ\varrho_{\alpha_{j}*}
where
\mathop{\rm pr}_{j}:TQ|_{C_{j}}\to N_{Q}C_{j}
and
\kappa_{j}:N_{X}C\to N_{Q}C_{j}

are the natural projections. Thus,
v_{i}^{j}=\mathop{\rm pr}_{j}\circ\varrho_{\alpha_{j}*}(\sigma_{i})
.
Since the differential
{\rm d}f_{k}
vanishes on
TC_{j}
, one has

(5)

\delta_{ik}=v_{i}^{j}(f_{k})=\varrho_{\alpha_{j}*}(\sigma_{i})(f_{k})|_{C_{j}}=\sigma_{i}(\varrho_{\alpha_{j}}^{*}(f_{k}))|_{C}.

Suppose that

is any holomorphic function on

.
By Formula (

) in Lemma
1.17
and Formula (

-automorphisms of

such that

Similarly, Lemmas

Formulas (

) and (

which is of the form

Lemma 9.8
.

Let the assumptions of Lemma
9.7
hold. Then
\mathop{\rm E}_{n-1}({\hat{A}})
is
contained in
{\rm Ind}(M)
.

Proof.

Recall that
v_{i}=\mathop{\rm pr}(\delta_{\alpha_{i}})
where
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}
are general elements of some perfect
G
-family
{\mathcal{A}}
.
While keeping
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n-2}
intact, choose another general element
\alpha_{n-1}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}
and let
v_{n-1}^{\prime}=\mathop{\rm pr}(\delta_{\alpha_{n-1}^{\prime}})
.
By Corollary
4.6
we have
v_{n-1}^{\prime}=q^{\prime}v_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-2}a_{i}^{\prime}v_{i}
. By Lemma
9.7
this yields holomorphically induced automorphisms
of the form

\theta^{\prime}=I+a_{l}^{\prime}b^{\prime}e_{0k}-a_{k}^{\prime}b^{\prime}e_{0l}
where
1\leq k\neq l\leq n-2
and
b^{\prime}\in{\hat{A}}
is divisible by
(q^{\prime})^{m}
for some large
m>0
.
By Proposition
4.2
(4) we can suppose that
at some point
x\in C
the vector
\delta_{\alpha_{n-1}^{\prime}}(x)
is such that the function
a_{l}a_{k}^{\prime}-a_{l}^{\prime}a_{k}
does not vanish at
x

and, therefore,
a_{l}a_{k}^{\prime}-a_{l}^{\prime}a_{k}
is a nonzero function. Let
b=ca_{k}^{\prime}(qq^{\prime})^{m}
and
b^{\prime}=-ca_{k}(qq^{\prime})^{m}
where
c\in{\hat{A}}
. Then the holomorphically induced
automorphism
\theta\theta^{\prime}
is given by
I+c(a_{l}a_{k}^{\prime}-a_{l}^{\prime}a_{k})(qq^{\prime})^{m}e_{0k}
.
Thus,
{\rm Ind}(M)
contains all automorphisms given by
I+de_{0k}
where
d
belongs to a nonzero ideal
{\mathfrak{p}}\subset{\hat{A}}
.
Choose now new general elements
\alpha_{n-1}
and
\alpha_{n-1}^{\prime}
in
{\mathcal{A}}
. By Proposition
4.2
(4)
we can suppose that for every
x
in the zero locus
K
of
{\mathfrak{p}}
the vectors
\delta_{\alpha_{n-1}}(x)
and
\delta_{\alpha_{n-1}^{\prime}}(x)

are such that the new function

. Hence,
{\rm Ind}(M)

contains every automorphism of the form

where

is an ideal whose zero locus in

is disjoint from

. By the weak Nullstellensatz
[

]

{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{q}}={\hat{A}}
and, hence, every elementary transformation
of the form

. In combination with Lemma

∎

Corollary 9.11
.

Let
X
be isomorphic to a connected complex linear algebraic group without nontrivial characters
and
\dim X\geq 4
. Suppose that
C
is a smooth closed curve in
X
with a trivial normal bundle
and defining ideal
{\hat{I}}
in the algebra
\operatorname{{\rm Hol}}(X)
of holomorphic functions on
X
.
Let
{\hat{A}}=\frac{\operatorname{{\rm Hol}}(X)}{{\hat{I}}}
be the algebra of holomorphic functions on

.
Then every
{\hat{A}}
-automorphism
\frac{\operatorname{{\rm Hol}}(X)}{{\hat{I}}^{k}}\to\frac{\operatorname{{\rm Hol}}(X)}{{\hat{I}}^{k}}

with Jacobian 1 extends to a holomorphic automorphism of
X
.

Proof.

By Theorem
9.2
it suffices to check the validity of Convention
3.2
, i.e., conditions (
\#
) and (
\#\#
) of that convention.
Condition (
\#
) is obvious since multiplication produces free
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-actions on
X
.
Condition (
\#\#
) follows from the followng fact: every connected linear algebraic group
Y
of dimension
n\geq 2
without nontrivial
characters is parametrically suitable. Indeed, by Mostow’s theorem
[
Mo
]

is isomorphic (as a variety) to the product

where
U

is unipotent and

is reductive. The absence of nontrivial characters implies that

is semi-simple. If

is not trivial, then we
have parametric suitability by Theorem
2.4
.
Otherwise,

∎

Corollary 9.12
.

Let
X
be isomorphic to
\mathop{\rm SL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
where

. Suppose that

and

are isomorphic smooth closed curves in

Then every isomorphism

By

every isomorphism

. Hence, we are done.

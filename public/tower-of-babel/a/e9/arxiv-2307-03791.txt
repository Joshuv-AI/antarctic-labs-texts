Tameness conditions and the Milnor fibrations for composite singularities
R. N. Araújo dos Santos, D. Dreibelbis, M. F. Ribeiro, I. D. Santamaría Guarín

Abstract
. In this paper, we introduce a new regularity condition that characterizes the tameness of a composite singularity H = G ∘ F H=G\circ F in a sharp way. Our approach provides a natural tool that links the topology of the Milnor tube fibrations through the Milnor fibers of the respective components of the map germs F F , G G and H = G ∘ F H=G\circ F . We also study the invariance of tameness by ℒ \mathcal{L} -equivalence, ℛ \mathcal{R} -equivalence, and hence by 𝒜 \mathcal{A} -equivalence, and we give conditions for when two component map germs of the composite singularity H = G ∘ F H=G\circ F being tame implies the third one is tame. As an application, we show how to relate the Euler characteristics of the Milnor fibers of F , G F,G and H H to each other.

Introduction

Let

In

In this paper we develop a study without imposing any condition under the dimension of the singular set of

, showing how to relate the singular sets and the Milnor sets of

and

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we recall the main results about the existence of a local tube fibration. In Section 3, we relate the singular sets and the discriminants of

,
G

-equivalence, and it is invariant by

-equivalence if we consider in the source space the pullback of the metric

and

3.
The Milnor sets of the composites singularities

Let
F:(\mathbb{R}^{M},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{N},0)
and
G:(\mathbb{R}^{N},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{K},0)
,
M\geq N\geq K\geq 2

H:(\mathbb{R}^{M},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{K},0)
be the composition map germ
H=G\circ F
.

3.1.
The Singular set of a composition

It follows that
V_{H}:=H^{-1}(0)=F^{-1}(V_{G})
. By the chain rule, the Jacobian matrix
{\rm{d}}H(x)={\rm{d}}G(F(x))\cdot{\rm{d}}F(x)
, hence

(3)

{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H\subseteq{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}F\cup F^{-1}({\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G).

It follows that if both
{\rm{Disc\hskip 2.0pt}}F=\{0\}
and
{\rm{Disc\hskip 2.0pt}}G=\{0\}
then
{\rm{Disc\hskip 2.0pt}}H=\{0\}.

In general, the inclusion (
3

{\rm{Disc\hskip 2.0pt}}F=\{0\},
as can be seen in the next example.

Example 3.1
.

Consider
F(x,y,z,w)=(x,y,z(x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}+w^{2}))

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
F:(\mathbb{R}^{M},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{N},0)
and
G:(\mathbb{R}^{N},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{K},0)

{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H=F^{-1}({\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G)

Proof.

If

3.2.
The Milnor set of the composite maps

Considering both

M(H)\subseteq M(F)

In this subsection we will state and prove some important results concerning the Milnor set of the composite singularity. Once more we point out that all results in this section holds for an arbitrary
\rho:U\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F:(\mathbb{R}^{M},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{N},0)
with
{\rm{Disc\hskip 2.0pt}}F=\{0\},

G:(\mathbb{R}^{N},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{K},0)
,
M\geq N\geq K\geq 2
, be analytic map germs. Then

{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H\subset M(H)\subseteq M(F)\cup{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H.

In particular, if
\{0\}\subseteq{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G
then
M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H\subseteq M(F)\setminus V_{F}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H
and consider the matrix

A(x)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\rm{d}}H(x)\\
{\rm{d}}\rho(x)\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\rm{d}}G(F(x))\cdot{\rm{d}}F(x)\\
{\rm{d}}\rho(x)\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{\rm{d}}G_{1}(F(x))\cdot{\rm{d}}F(x)\\
\vdots\\
{\rm{d}}G_{k}(F(x))\cdot{\rm{d}}F(x)\\
{\rm{d}}\rho(x)\end{array}\right].

Since
x\in M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H
,
\textrm{rank}(A(x))
is not maximal. However, the
\textrm{rank}~{\rm{d}}H(x)
is maximal and for
j=1,\ldots,k
each line
{\rm{d}}G_{j}(F(x))\cdot{\rm{d}}F(x)
is a linear combination of the gradients
{\rm{d}}F_{i}(x),i=1,\ldots,n
with the coefficients of the partial derivatives of
G_{j}.
Hence
x\in M(F)
.

Moreover, if
\{0\}\subseteq{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G
then
V_{F}:=F^{-1}(0)\subset F^{-1}({\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G)={\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H
and hence
M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H\subseteq M(F)\setminus V_{F}.

∎

Proposition 3.5

Let

(ii)

Proof.

The item

For the item

M(G_{2})={\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}(G_{2},\rho_{2})=F^{-1}({\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}(G_{1},\rho_{1}))=F^{-1}(M(G_{1}))

\rho_{2}

is calculated with respect to
\rho_{1}

\overline{M(G_{2})\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G_{2}}\cap{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G_{2}=F^{-1}(\overline{M(G_{1})\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G_{1}}\cap{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G_{1})

and so the tameness of

∎

To recap: Tameness with respect to a particular proper function

Proof.

4.1.
Main results and its consequences

Before we prove the main Theorem of this section let us recall some basic facts on the properness of a map and also fix some notations.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
F,G
and
H=G\circ F
be analytic map germs as before. For all
\epsilon>0
small enough, denote by
M(H):=M(H)\cap\bar{B}_{\epsilon}
, similarly,
M(F):=M(F)\cap\bar{B}_{\epsilon}
, where all sets are considered as a germ of sets once they are well defined. An easy exercise shows that

(4)

F(\overline{M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H})=\overline{F(M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H)}=\overline{F(M(H))\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G}.

We now state our main theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

For
M\geq N\geq K\geq 2,

F:(\mathbb{R}^{M},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{N},0)
tame with
{\rm{Disc\hskip 2.0pt}}F=\{0\}
and
G:(\mathbb{R}^{N},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{K},0)
with
\{0\}\subseteq{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G,
then the composition
H=G\circ F
is tame if and only if the following condition holds true:

(5)

\overline{F(M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H)}\cap{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G\subseteq\{0\}.

Proof.

To prove the “if” implication, take a sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subset\overline{F(M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H)}\cap{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G
such that
y_{n}\to 0.

From Remark
4.3
, the first equality of (
4
), it follows that there exists a sequence
\{x_{n}\}\subset\overline{M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H}
such that
y_{n}=F(x_{n}).

Since
{y_{n}}\in{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G
also, one has
x_{n}\in\overline{M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H}\cap{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H.
By the tameness of
H
there must be a natural number
n_{0}>0
great enough such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
one has
x_{n}=0.
Hence,
y_{n}=F(0)=0
for all
n
great enough and the “if” condition proved.

For the converse implication, let us now take a sequence
\{x_{n}\}\subset\overline{M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H}\cap{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H
, such that
x_{n}\to 0
.

Thus
y_{n}=F(x_{n})\in F(\overline{M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H})\cap{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}G.
Applying Condition (
5
) there must be a great enough
n_{0}>0
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
y_{n}=0
and thus
x_{n}\in F^{-1}(0)
.

Hence we conclude that
x_{n}\in\overline{M(H)\setminus{\rm{Sing\hskip 2.0pt}}H}\cap V_{F}\subseteq\overline{M(F)\setminus V_{F}}\cap V_{F}\subseteq\{0\},
where the first inclusion follows by Lemma
3.4
and the second inclusion follows by the tameness of
F.
Therefore, for all
n
great enough the sequence
x_{n}=0
and the proof is finished.
∎

Plasmon dispersion and Landau damping in the nonlinear quantum regime
F. Haas, J. T. Mendonça, H. Terças

Abstract
We study the dispersion properties of electron plasma waves, or plasmons, which can be excited in quantum plasmas in the nonlinear regime. In order to describe nonlinear electron response to finite amplitude plasmons, we apply the Volkov approach to non-relativistic electrons. For that purpose, we use the Schrödinger equation and describe the electron population of a quantum plasma as a mixture of quantum states. Within the kinetic framework that we are able to derive from the Volkov solutions, we discuss the role of the wave amplitude on the nonlinear plasma response. Finally, we focus on the quantum properties of nonlinear Landau damping and study the contributions of multi-plasmon absorption and emission processes.



I
Introduction

Landau damping of electron plasma waves was introduced in 1946, in the frame of classical plasma physics, and plays a central role in plasma theory
Landau 1946
;
Dawson 1961
;
Nicholson 1983
;
Weiland 1981
;
Ryutov 1999
. Although quite well understood in its linear formulation, the nonlinear regime of Landau damping remains a problem of major mathematical and physical importance
Villani 2002
;
Zhou et al. 2001
;
Xu et al. 2021
, not only in plasmas but also in self-gravitating systems
Wiechen et al. 1988
;
Moretti et al. 2020
. Its quantum version was introduced in the early 60’s of the last century
Klimontovich and Silin 1960
;
Pines and Schrieffer 1962
, and seems to play a more modest role in quantum plasma theory
Manfredi and Haas 2001
;
Haas 2011
. Despite some progress in more recent years
Daligault 2014
;
Brodin et al. 2015
;
Chatterjee and Misra 2016
, the nonlinear effects associated with quantum Landau damping, such as those associated with particle trapping, wave satellites and field harmonics, are still not well understood. While the basic properties of quantum Landau damping can be found in books
Haas 2011
and review articles
Misra and Brodin 2022
, a variety of effects associated with the quantum nature of the plasmon excitations remain to be tackled. For instance, it has been recently reported numerically that quantum Landau damping consists of a
multi-plasmon damping
, and it can be understood as a balance between emission and absorption of plasmons by the plasma electrons
Brodin et al. 2017
.

In this paper, we propose an innovative approach to quantum Landau damping in the nonlinear regime. Our method is based on the used of Volkov (also known as Wolkow
not
) solutions to describe the single particle (test electron) states in the presence of an electrostatic wave. A remarkable feature of our approach is the fact of being, by construction, nonperturbative in the wave amplitude. Volkov solutions to the Dirac equation were originally obtained for relativistic quantum particles in vacuum
Wolkow 1935
;
Itzykson and Zuber 2006
. In recent years, they have been extended to plasmas, where approximate solutions can also be found
Mendonça and Serbeto 2011
;
Raicher and Eliezer 2013
;
Varró 2013
. These solutions can be used to describe multi-photon and multi-plasmon effects associated with inverse bremsstrahlung
Mendonça et al. 2013
and Compton scattering
Brown and Kibble 1964
;
Mendonça and Haas 2023
. In the following, we consider the non-relativistic quantum plasma case, where the Dirac equation is replaced by the Schrödinger equation.

Previous studies of Volkov solutions of the Schrödinger equation have focused on single particle states in vacuum
Reiss and Krainov 1994
;
Bauer 2002
;
Li and Thumm 2020
. Here, instead, we consider an electron plasma wave with a finite amplitude. We use the single-particle Volkov solutions in the presence of this wave and establish the electron plasma population as a mixture of quantum states. This allows us to describe the nonlinear plasma response to the electron plasma wave. Such a description allows us to derive a new dispersion relation where multi-plasmon effects are included. We then focus on the quantum properties of nonlinear Landau damping, and study the contributions of multi-plasmon absorption and emission processes. Our analytical results confirm and extend previous work, mainly based on numerical solutions
Brodin et al. 2015
;
Brodin et al. 2017
, and propose a new conceptual approach to nonlinear plasma. Our results may certainly contribute to enlarge the discussion around wave-particle effects in nonlinear waves in quantum systems, namely in the context of graphene devices
Hwang and Das Sarma 2007
;
Badikova and Glazov 2017
;
Cosme and Terças 2020
;
Cosme and Terças 2021
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section II, we introduce exact single-electron solutions driven by the plasma wave with the help of the Volkov formalism. Then, in Section III, we obtain a nonlinear dispersion relation for a generic equilibrium based on the quantum (Wigner) kinetic equation. Finally, in Sec. IV some conclusions are stated and applications in topical physical problems are motivated.

II
Volkov solutions for non-relativistic electrons

We start from Schrödinger’s equation describing single-electron states in the presence of an electrostatic potential, as

i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi=\left[-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+eV\right]\psi\,,

(1)

where
\displaystyle\psi\equiv\psi({\bf r},t)
is the electron wavefunction and
\displaystyle V\equiv V({\bf r},t)
is the electrostatic potential associated with an electron plasma wave with frequency
\displaystyle\omega
and wavevector
\displaystyle{\bf k}
that propagates in the plasma. We now look for quantities varying in terms of the
proper time

\displaystyle\tau=t-{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}/\omega
, such that
\displaystyle V({\bf r},t)=V_{0}f(\tau)
, where
\displaystyle V_{0}
is the wave amplitude, and the function
\displaystyle f(\tau)
describes the wave shape to be specified. Following the Volkov procedure, we assume a solution of the form

\psi({\bf r},t)=\Phi(\tau)\exp(i\theta)\,,\quad\theta={\bf k}_{e}\cdot{\bf r}-\omega_{e}t.

(2)

Here,
\displaystyle{\bf p}_{e}=\hbar{\bf k}_{e}
is the electron momentum and
\displaystyle\hbar\omega_{e}=p_{e}^{2}/2m
the corresponding kinetic energy. This allows us to use

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=\left(\Phi^{\prime}-i\omega_{e}\Phi\right)\exp(i\theta)\,,

(3)

with
\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}\equiv\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\tau}
. Similarly, for the space derivatives, we have

\nabla\psi=\left(i{\bf k}_{e}\Phi-\frac{{\bf k}}{\omega}\Phi^{\prime}\right)\exp(i\theta)\,,

(4)

and

\nabla^{2}\psi=\left(\frac{k^{2}}{\omega^{2}}\Phi^{\prime\prime}-2i\frac{({\bf k}_{e}\cdot{\bf k})}{\omega}\Phi^{\prime}-k_{e}^{2}\Phi\right)\exp(i\theta)\,.

(5)

Replacing this in Eq. (
1
), we get an evolution equation for
\displaystyle\Phi
, of the form

\alpha^{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}+ig\Phi^{\prime}-G(\tau)\Phi=0\,,

(6)

with the following quantities

\alpha^{2}=\frac{\hbar^{2}k^{2}}{2m\omega^{2}}\,,\quad g=\hbar\left(1-\frac{{\bf p}_{e}\cdot{\bf k}}{m\omega}\right)\,,\quad G(\tau)=eV_{0}f(\tau)\,.

(7)

These auxiliary quantities have a very clear physical meaning:
\displaystyle\alpha^{2}
represents the kinetic energy of a particle with the wave momentum
\displaystyle\hbar{\bf k}
(the factor
\displaystyle\omega^{2}
appear as to weight the second time derivative); the factor
\displaystyle g
represents the deviation of the particle velocity with respect to the wave phase velocity; and the function
\displaystyle G(\tau)
is simply the potential energy. Eq. (
6
) can now be converted into a first order equation using

\Phi(\tau)=\Phi_{0}\exp\left[-i\int_{0}^{\tau}\Omega(\tau^{\prime})d\tau^{\prime}\right],

(8)

where
\displaystyle\Phi_{0}\equiv\Phi(\tau=0)
is a constant. We get the Riccati equation

-\alpha^{2}\left(i\Omega^{\prime}+\Omega^{2}\right)+g\Omega-G(\tau)=0.

(9)

We now assume a sinusoidal electrostatic wave in the medium, such that

\displaystyle G(\tau)=eV_{0}\exp(-i\omega\tau)
. To the lowest order in the wave amplitude
\displaystyle V_{0}
, we can neglect the nonlinear term, we can derive the following particular solution for the eikonal phase

\Omega(\tau)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{n}e^{-in\omega\tau},

(10)

with the first coefficients being given as

\Omega_{1}=\frac{eV_{0}}{g-\omega\alpha^{2}},\quad\Omega_{2}=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{g-2\omega\alpha^{2}}\left(\frac{eV_{0}}{g-\omega\alpha^{2}}\right)^{2},\quad\Omega_{3}=\frac{2\alpha^{4}}{(g-2\omega\alpha^{2})(g-3\omega\alpha^{2})}\left(\frac{eV_{0}}{g-\omega\alpha^{2}}\right)^{4},~\ldots.

(11)

The formal particular solution in Eq. (
10
) is not very useful for the collective treatment we will perform below. As such, in order to cast the nonlinear effects at first order the in the wave amplitude, we neglect terms of the order
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(V_{0}^{2})
. In order to avoid unbounded (exponentially growing) solutions, we take the real part of the phase in Eq. (
10
). Consequently, the formal solution to Eq. (
8
) can be given in terms of the Jacobi-Anger expansion, yielding the following bounded functions

\Phi(\tau)\simeq\Phi_{0}\sum_{\ell}i^{\ell}J_{\ell}(\xi)e^{-i\ell\omega\tau},

(12)

where
\displaystyle\xi=\Omega_{1}/\omega
plays the role of a dimensionless electrostatic energy. This shows that the single electron states include multi-plasmon transitions (emission and absorption), that are weighted by the wave amplitude
\displaystyle V_{0}
, with probability
\displaystyle\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}
. Such effects will be explored below. This is justified as we are interested in investigating stable solutions only. We notice, nevertheless, that an alternative and more generic treatment of the problem would require the solutions of the Mathieu

\alpha^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}+\left(\frac{g^{2}}{4\alpha^{2}}-G(\tau)\right)\varphi=0,

(13)

obtained from Eq. (
6
) upon the transformation
\displaystyle\varphi(\tau)=\exp[ig\tau/(2\alpha^{2})]\phi(\tau)
. However, this would lead to formal difficulties preventing our analysis, since it does explicitly show the multi-plasmon characteristic of the nonlinear solution of Eq. (
12
), as it will become apparent below. In the remainder of this work, we are interested in recasting the effect of the finite amplitude in the dispersion of the waves, and not in investigating the solutions of single electrons.

III
Nonlinear plasmons in a quantum plasma

According to Eqs. (
2
) and (
12
), we can characterize a given electron state with velocity
\displaystyle{\bf v}={\bf p}_{e}/m
, in the presence of a finite amplitude plasma wave, by a wavefunction of the form

\psi_{\bf v}({\bf r},t)=\Phi_{0}\exp(i\theta_{\bf v})\sum_{\ell}i^{\ell}J_{\ell}(\xi)\exp(-i\ell\omega\tau)\,.

(14)

and describe the quantum fluid as a mixture of states
Haas 2011
with quasi-probability

W({\bf r},{\bf v},t)=\int d{\bf v^{\prime}}W_{0}({\bf r},{\bf v^{\prime}}-{\bf v})W_{\bf v^{\prime}}({\bf r},t),

(15)

where
\displaystyle\theta_{\mathbf{v}}=m{\bf v}\cdot{\bf r}/\hbar-\omega_{e}t
is the test electron phase (the subscript
\displaystyle{\bf v}
is simply introduced to render the dependence on the electron velocity
\displaystyle{\bf v}
more explicit),
\displaystyle W_{0}({\bf r},{\bf v})
is the equilibrium distribution at a given electron temperature
\displaystyle T_{e}
(typically, the Fermi-Dirac distribution), and the functions

\displaystyle W_{\bf v}({\bf r},t)
are Wigner functions defined by

W_{\bf v}({\bf r},t)=\int\psi_{\bf v}({\bf r}-{\bf s}/2,t)\psi_{\bf v}^{*}({\bf r}+{\bf s}/2,t)e^{im{\bf v}\cdot{\bf s}/\hbar}\,d{\bf s}.

(16)

Let us now study the plasma dispersion using this nonlinear function of the wave amplitude as the undisturbed distribution.
For this purpose, we use the wave-kinetic equation for a quantum plasma
Haas 2011

\displaystyle\displaystyle i\hbar\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+{\bf v}\cdot\bm{\nabla}\right)W=e\int d{\bf q}V({\bf q})\left[W^{-}-W^{+}\right]e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\,,

(17)

where
\displaystyle V({\bf q})
is the Fourier transform of the electrostatic potential created by the plasma,
\displaystyle W^{\pm}=W({\bf{r}},{\bf v}\pm{\bf v_{q}},t)
, and
\displaystyle{\bf v_{q}}=\hbar{\bf q}/2m
. Here, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int d{\bf q}V({\bf q})W({\bf r},{\bf v}\pm{\bf v_{q}},t)e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\int d{\bf q}V({\bf q})W_{0}({\bf r},{\bf v}\pm{\bf v_{q}})e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\int d{\bf s}\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}e^{im(\ell+1){\bf v}\cdot({\bf r}\pm{\bf s})/\hbar}

(18)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\int d{\bf q}V({\bf q})W_{0}\left[{\bf r},{\bf v}\pm(\ell+1){\bf v_{q}}\right]e^{i{\bf q}\cdot{\bf r}}\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}.

(19)

Equation (
17
) must be consistent with the Poisson equation

\nabla^{2}V({\bf r},t)=\frac{e}{\epsilon_{0}}\left(|\psi({\bf r},t)|^{2}-n_{0}(\bf r)\right)=\frac{e}{\epsilon_{0}}\int\left[W({\bf r},{\bf v},t)-W_{0}({\bf r},{\bf v})\right]d{\bf v},

(20)

with
\displaystyle n_{0}(\bf r)
being the background ionic density forcing the plasma quasi-neutrality. In order to obtain the kinetic dispersion relation, we perturb the Wigner-Poisson system as
\displaystyle W=W_{0}+\tilde{W}
and
\displaystyle V=\tilde{V}
, evolving as
\displaystyle(\tilde{W},\tilde{V})\sim\exp(i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf r}-i\omega t)
. Using the usual properties of the delta function in Eq. (
19
), and replacing the dummy variables for the wavevector
\displaystyle\bf{q}\to\bf{k}
, we obtain, for the case of a homogenous equilibrium
\displaystyle W_{0}({\bf r},{\bf v})=n_{0}G_{0}({\bf v})
,

-i\left(\omega-{\bf k}\cdot{\bf v}\right)\tilde{W}=n_{0}\sum_{\ell}G_{0}\left[{\bf v}-(\ell+1){\bf v_{k}}\right]\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}\tilde{V}\,,

(21)

and

-k^{2}\tilde{V}=\frac{e}{\epsilon_{0}}\int\tilde{W}d{\bf v}.

(22)

Finally, combining the latter, we get the nonlinear dispersion relation,

1-\frac{m\omega_{p}^{2}}{\hbar k^{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\int\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}\frac{G_{0}\left[{\bf v}-(\ell+1){\bf v_{k}}\right]-G_{0}\left[{\bf v}+(\ell+1){\bf v_{k}}\right]}{(\omega-{\bf k}\cdot{\bf v})}d{\bf v}=0\,.

(23)

Integrating over the perpendicular velocity
\displaystyle{\bf v}_{\perp}
, and defining the parallel Wigner function

G_{0}(u)=\int G_{0}(u,{\bf v}_{\perp})d{\bf v}_{\perp}\,,

(24)

where
\displaystyle u
is the particle velocity component along the direction of wave propagation, we get

1-\omega_{p}^{2}\sum_{\ell}(\ell+1)\int\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}\frac{G_{0}(u)du}{(\omega-ku)^{2}-(\ell+1)^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}}=0,

(25)

where
\displaystyle\nu_{k}=\hbar k^{2}/2m
is the recoil frequency. This is a generalization of the usual quantum dispersion, when the Volkov solutions are taken into account. In the limit of infinitesimal wave amplitudes,
\displaystyle\xi\to 0
, we have

J_{0}(\xi)\rightarrow 1\,,\quad J_{\ell\neq 0}(\xi)\rightarrow 0\,,

(26)

and we are reduced to the usual linear dispersion relation of quantum plasmas, as obtained within the random phase approximation (RPA) formalism
Moldabekov et al. 2018
;
Haas 2011

1-\omega_{p}^{2}\int\frac{G_{0}(u)du}{(\omega-ku)^{2}-\nu_{k}^{2}}=0.

(27)

In the cold plasma approximation, where we can use
\displaystyle G_{0}(u)=n_{0}\delta(u)
, the leading order correction to the plasmon dispersion expression is implicitly given by

\omega^{2}=\omega_{p}^{2}\sum_{\ell}(\ell+1)J_{\ell}^{2}\left(\xi_{0}\right)\frac{\omega^{2}-\nu_{k}^{2}}{\omega^{2}-(\ell+1)^{2}\nu_{k}^{2}}+\nu_{k}^{2},

(28)

where
\displaystyle\xi_{0}\equiv\xi|_{{\bf p}_{e}=0}=eV_{0}/\hbar\omega_{p}
. In the linear regime,
\displaystyle\xi_{0}\to 0
, we simply get
\displaystyle\omega\simeq\sqrt{\omega_{p}^{2}+\nu_{k}^{2}}

Tsintsadze et al. 2009
;
Haas 2011
, and at order
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\xi_{0}^{2})
, we get two modes,
\displaystyle\omega\simeq\omega_{\pm}
, which, in the long wavelength limit, read

\omega_{+}\simeq\omega_{p}\left(1+\frac{|\xi_{0}|^{2}}{2}\right)^{1/2},\quad\omega_{-}\simeq\nu_{k}\left(1-\frac{3}{8}|\xi_{0}|^{2}\right).

(29)

This results expresses a remarkable feature, as depicted in Fig.
1
, which is intrinsic to the nonlinear nature of the oscillations in the presence of a finite amplitude wave: the hybridization between the collective (gapped,
\displaystyle\omega\sim\omega_{p}
) mode and the single-particle (gapless,
\displaystyle\omega\sim k^{2}
) mode. At the crossing point,
\displaystyle k_{*}=(4/3)^{1/4}k_{p}
, with
\displaystyle k_{p}=\sqrt{\omega_{p}/\hbar^{2}m}
defining the spatial scale of the oscillations in a quantum plasma, the two modes repeal by the amount of
\displaystyle 2\Omega
, where
\displaystyle\Omega=\sqrt{3}\omega_{p}|\xi_{0}|/4
plays the role of a
Rabi frequency
, in similarity to what happens in quantum optics
Merlin 2021
. Indeed, such avoided crossing has been recently identified in magnetized plasmas interacting with axions
Terças et al. 2018
. We attribute this effect to intricate wave-particle interaction that takes place in the nonlinear regime: while part of the electrons participate in the collective mode, oscillating alongside with the wave at frequency
\displaystyle\omega=\omega_{+}\simeq\omega_{p}
(corrected by the Volkov mode
\displaystyle\ell=0
), others remain trapped inside the wave, featuring essentially single particle motion
\displaystyle\omega=\omega_{-}\simeq 2\nu_{k}
. A factor of two correcting the value of the frequency, however, appears here as this oscillation involves, at leading order, the exchange of two plasmons,
\displaystyle\ell=\pm 1
. Of course, multiple plasmon exchange is also expected, but such effect is less important for weakly nonlinear waves, for which the
\displaystyle\ell=\pm 1
mixture is enough to explain the physical picture of the wave-particle interaction. The interplay of multiple plasmons in wave-particle problem has been observed in numerical simulations
Brodin et al. 2017
. We also observe that the frequency shift to the linear plasma frequency
\displaystyle\omega_{p}
in Eq. (
29
) proportional to
\displaystyle|\xi_{0}|\propto V_{0}
. This strongly differs from the classical result, associated to electron trapping at late stages of the wave-particle interaction, in which trapping is proportional to
\displaystyle\sqrt{V_{0}}
, and not to
\displaystyle V_{0}
as we obtain here.

A more interesting situation concerns the isentropic decay of plasmons in thermal plasmas,
\displaystyle T_{e}\neq 0
, known as
Landau damping
. Coming back to Eqs. (
23
) and (
24
), we make use of the Landau prescription, in which the integral in
\displaystyle u
is performed by forcing the contour
\displaystyle\mathcal{L}
to pass
below
the pole
\displaystyle u=\omega/k

Manfredi and Haas 2001
;
Zhu et al. 2009
, and assuming that the frequency acquires a small imaginary part,
\displaystyle\omega\to\omega+i\gamma
, such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\epsilon(k,\omega)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=1-\frac{m\omega_{p}^{2}}{\hbar k^{2}}\sum_{\ell}(\ell+1)\int_{\mathcal{L}}\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}\frac{G_{0}\left[u-(\ell+1)v_{k}\right]-G_{0}\left[u+(\ell+1)v_{k}\right]}{\omega+i\gamma-ku}du

(30)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\simeq 1-\frac{m\omega_{p}^{2}}{\hbar k^{2}}\sum_{\ell}(\ell+1){\wp}\int\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}\frac{G_{0}\left[u-(\ell+1)v_{k}\right]-G_{0}\left[u+(\ell+1)v_{k}\right]}{\omega-ku}du

(31)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+i\pi\frac{m\omega_{p}^{2}}{\hbar k^{2}}\sum_{\ell}(\ell+1)\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}\left[G_{0}\left(\frac{\omega}{k}-(\ell+1)v_{k}\right)-G_{0}\left(\frac{\omega}{k}+(\ell+1)v_{k}\right)\right].

(32)

Here,
\displaystyle\wp
stands for the principal value of the integral, and the last line of Eq. (
32

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}(x\pm i\epsilon)^{-1}=\wp x^{-1}\mp i\pi\delta(x)
. Finally, by expanding Eq. (

) at first order in
\displaystyle\gamma
, we obtain

\gamma=\frac{\pi\omega_{p}^{3}}{4k^{2}}\sum_{\ell}\frac{\ell+1}{v_{k}}\left|J_{\ell}(\xi)\right|^{2}\left[G_{0}\left(\frac{\omega}{k}+(\ell+1)v_{k}\right)-G_{0}\left(\frac{\omega}{k}-(\ell+1)v_{k}\right)\right]\,.

(33)

G_{0}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{z^{-1}e^{\zeta}+1},

IV
Conclusions

In this paper, we have developed a theoretical framework capturing the main aspects of the nonlinear Landau damping of electron plasma (Langmuir) waves in the quantum regime. By making use of a Volkov formulation of the problem, we are able to derive a formal solution to the test particle driven by a finite amplitude electrostatic wave of the plasma. The subsequent statistical, self-consistent treatment of the plasma electrons is obtained for Wigner function describing a mixture state, which evolves according to a quantum kinetic equation of the Wigner-Moyal type. The crucial difference with respect to standard applications of the Wigner-Moyal equation in the study of plasma waves stems in the possibility to consider finite amplitude waves, which now appears as a parameter in the dispersion relation. We show that, in the presence of finite amplitude waves, the nonlinear kinetic dispersion relation provides different modes, unlike to the case of Langmuir waves. Such modes are hybridizations between the (linear) Langmuir mode (now corrected by a quantity proportional to the amplitude of the wave) and the single-particle modes composed by the electrons that are not trapped by the wave. The latter corresponds to a mode quasi-particle which mass is controlled by the number of plasmons exchanged between the electrons. Moreover, we have shown that Landau damping in the nonlinear regime is strongly suppressed. This is, again, a consequence of particle trapping, which prevents particles to participate in the process of extracting energy from the wave. The calculations performed here are in agreement with the numerical results performed on the numerical simulation of Wigner-Moyal equation
Brodin et al. 2017
, while keeping the simplicity of the formulation. This is a consequence of the versatility and power of the Volkov approach.

Given the versatility of the present approach, we believe that our findings will stimulate further discussions around nonlinear wave-particle interactions, in general, and around Landau damping, in particular, in a plethora of systems featuring long range interactions. First, there is a vast set of condensed-matter platforms in which this formalism can be applied and tested, namely two-dimensional plasmonics in graphene and related materials
Gardner 1994
;
Mustafa and Hashim 2010
;
Moradi 2017
;
Figueiredo et al. 2022
;
Cosme et al. 2022
;
Cosme and Terças 2023
, including bilayer graphene, black phosphorus and transition metal dichalgogenides (TMDCs)
Khan et al. 2020
, one-dimensional plasmas in carbon nanotubes and metallic nanowires
Chang et al. 2006
;
Zhang et al. 2011
;
Huang et al. 2023
, and three-dimensional Dirac materials such as Weyl semimetals
Zhang et al. 2021
. Eventual differences in regard to this work come from the dispersive nature of the carries in the band theories, which must be taken into account in the derivation of both single-particle and kinetic (Wigner) equations. The strategy, however, remains the same. Second, in self-gravitating systems, numerous quantum gravity studies are performed with the help of Schrödinger-Poisson system, given the quantum mechanical nature of the fields composing dark matter. For instance, in Refs.
Chavanis 2011
;
Gomes and Ourabah 2023
dark matter is treated as a Bose-Einstein condensate, while a quantum kinetic formulation of the problem has been put forward in Refs.
Mendonça 2019
;
Mendonça 2021
. Moreover, classical self-gravitating phenomena, such as Jeans instability and the formation of structures, are usually described by a kinetic equation of the Vlasov type
Farias et al. 2018
;
Moretti et al. 2020
;
Ourabah 2020a
;
Ourabah 2020b
. Indeed, at a first glance, no quantum mechanics takes place here. The important point lies, however, in the fact that the Vlasov equation easily follows from the classical limit of the Wigner equation, which can be formally obtained by setting
\displaystyle\hbar\to 0
in Eq. (
17
). Thus, multiple plasmon effects, such as those patent in Eq. (25) and subsequent equations are, in principle, possible in the context of self-gravitating phenomenology. More precisely, the Jeans length
\displaystyle\lambda_{J}
establishes the typical length at which structures form; if nonlinear Landau damping occurs as we here predict for plasmas, then important changes may take in the correct definition of
\displaystyle\lambda_{J}
, which would now be determined through the competition between the Jeans instability (positive growth) and the damping (negative growth) of normal modes.

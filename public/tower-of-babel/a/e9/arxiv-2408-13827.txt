Weak Almost Contact Structures: a Survey
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
Weak almost contact structures, i.e., the linear complex structure on the contact distribution is replaced by a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor, defined by the author and R. Wolak, allowed us to take a new look at the theory of contact manifolds. The paper surveys recent results (concerning geodesic and Killing fields, rigidity and splitting theorems, Ricci-type solitons and Einstein-type metrics, warped products, etc.) in this new field of Riemannian geometry. Keywords : almost contact structure, K-contact structure, (nearly) Sasakian and cosymplectic structures, β \beta -Kenmotsu manifold, Ricci-type soliton, Einstein-type metric Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) Primary 53C12; Secondary 53C21

Introduction

Contact Riemannian geometry is of growing interest due to its important role in mechanics,
[
1
,
20
]
.
Sasakian manifolds (normal contact metric manifolds) and, more generally,
K
-contact manifolds
have become an important and active subject, especially after the appearance of the fundamental treatise
[
4
]
of C. Boyer and K. Galicki.
On a
{K}
-contact manifold
M(f,\xi,\eta,g)
the vector field
\xi
is Killing (the Lie derivative
\pounds_{\xi}\,g=0
),
and geodesic (
\nabla_{\xi}\,\xi=0
).
The restriction of
f
to
f(TM)
determines a complex structure.
If a plane in
TM
contains
\xi
, then its sectional curvature is called
\xi
-
sectional curvature
.
The
\xi
-sectional curvature of a
K
-contact manifold is equal to one.
Two important subclasses of
{K}
-contact manifolds are

(\nabla_{X}f)Y=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}g(X,Y)\,\xi-\eta(Y)X\,,&{\rm Sasakian,}\\
0\,,&{\rm cosymplectic.}\end{array}\right.

(1)

Any cosymplectic manifold is locally the product of a Kähler manifold and
\mathbb{R}
.
A Riemannian manifold
(M^{2n+1},g)
with a contact 1-form
\eta
(i.e.,
\eta\wedge(d\eta)^{n}\neq 0
)
is Sasakian, if its
cone
M\times\mathbb{R}^{>0}
with the
metric
t^{2}g+dt^{2}
is a Kähler manifold.
We get
\nabla_{X}\,\xi=-\frac{1}{2}\,fX
on Sasakian manifolds, and
\nabla_{X}\,\xi=0
on cosymplectic manifolds.

Nearly Kähler manifolds
[
13
]
are defined by the condition that the symmetric part of
\nabla J
vanishes,
in contrast to the Kähler case where
\nabla J=0
.
Nearly Sasakian/cosymplectic manifolds are defined similarly
– by a constraint on the symmetric part of the structural tensor
f

– starting from Sasakian/cosymplectic manifolds:

(\nabla_{X}{f})Y+(\nabla_{Y}{f})X=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\hskip-2.84526pt2\,g(X,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)X-\eta(X)Y\,,&{\rm nearly\ Sasakian,}\\
0\,,&\hskip-17.07164pt{\rm nearly\ cosymplectic.}\end{array}\right.

(2)

In dimensions greater than 5, every nearly Sasakian manifold is Sasakian,
and a nearly cosymplectic manifold
M^{2n+1}
splits into
{\mathbb{R}}\times F^{2n}
or
B^{5}\times F^{2n-4}
,
where
F
is a nearly Kähler manifold and
B
is a 5-dimensional nearly cosymplectic manifold.
These structures
appeared in the study of harmonic almost contact manifolds.
For nearly Sasakian/cosymplectic manifolds we get
g(R_{\,\xi,Z}\,{f}X,{f}Y)=0
, see
[
8
,
17
]
,
where
R_{{X},{Y}}Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z
is the Riemann tensor;
thus,

R_{X,Y}Z\perp\xi\quad(X,Y,Z\perp\xi)-\mbox{the distribution $\ker\eta$ is {curvature invariant}}.

(3)

For example,
the distribution
\ker\eta
of any 1-form
\eta

\mathbb{R}^{m}

satisfies (

).

The Ricci tensor is given by
{\rm Ric}({X},{Y})=\operatorname{Tr}(Z\to R_{Z,X}Y)=\!\sum_{i}g(R_{e_{i},{X}}{Y},e_{i})
,
where
(e_{i})
is any local orthonormal basis of
TM
.
The Ricci operator
\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}

associated with the Ricci tensor
is given by

{\rm Ric}({X},{Y})=g(\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}{X},{Y})
.
The scalar curvature of
(M,g)
is given by
r={\rm trace}_{g}\operatorname{Ric}
.
On some compact manifolds there are no Einstein metrics, which motivates the study of generalizations of such metrics.
The
generalized Ricci soliton

is given for some smooth vector field
{V}
and real
c_{1},c_{2}
and
\lambda

(1/2)\,\pounds_{{V}}\,g=-c_{1}{V}^{\flat}\otimes{V}^{\flat}+c_{2}\operatorname{Ric}+\lambda\,g.

(4)

If
{V}=\nabla f
in (

f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, then by

{\rm Hess}_{f}({X},{Y})=\frac{1}{2}\,(\pounds_{\nabla f}\,g)({X},{Y})
,
we get the
generalized gradient Ricci soliton
equation, see
[
5
]
:

{\rm Hess}_{f_{1}}=-c_{1}df_{2}\otimes df_{2}+c_{2}\operatorname{Ric}+\lambda\,g

(5)

for some

f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{\infty}(M)
and real
c_{1},c_{2}
and
\lambda
.
For different values of
c_{1},\,c_{2},\lambda
, equation (
4

Einstein metric,
\operatorname{Ric}+\lambda\,g=0
(
c_{1}=0,\,c_{2}=-1,\,V=0
),
Killing equation (
c_{1}=c_{2}=\lambda=0
),
Ricci soliton equation
(
c_{1}=0,\,c_{2}=-1
), etc.,
see
[
12
]
.
In
[
6
]
, Cho-Kimura
defined
\eta
-
Ricci soliton
on a
contact metric manifold

by the following equation:

({1}/{2})\,\pounds_{V}\,g+{\rm Ric}+\lambda\,g+\mu\,\eta\otimes\eta=0,

(6)

where

For

, then (

A contact metric manifold
is called
\eta
-
Einstein

{\rm Ric}=a\,g+b\,\eta\otimes\eta

is true,
where

Note that

Many articles study the questions: How interesting Ricci-type solitons are for contact metric manifolds?
When an almost contact metric manifold
equipped with a Ricci-type soliton
carries Einstein-type metrics?

In
[
21
,
23
]
, we introduced metric structures on a smooth manifold that generalize the almost contact, Sasakian, cosymplectic, etc. metric structures.
Such so-called “weak” structures
(the complex structure on the contact distribution
f(TM)
is replaced by a nonsingular skew-symmetric tensor)
allow us to take a fresh look at the theory of classical structures and find new applications.
A
weak almost contact structure
on a smooth manifold
M^{2n+1}
is defined by a
(1,1)
-tensor field
f
of rank
2n
,
a vector field
\xi
, a 1-form
\eta
and a nonsingular
(1,1)
-tensor field
Q
satisfying, see
[
21
]
,

f^{2}=-Q+\eta\otimes\xi,\qquad\eta(\xi)=1,\qquad Q\,\xi=\xi.

(7)

A “small” (1,1)-tensor
\widetilde{Q}=Q-{\rm id}
measures the difference between a weak almost contact structure and an almost contact one.
We assume that a
2n
-dimensional distribution
\ker\eta
is
{f}
-invariant (as in the classical theory
[
1
]
, where
Q={\rm id}
).

If there exists a Riemannian metric
g
such that

g({f}X,{f}Y)=g(X,Q\,Y)-\eta(X)\,\eta(Y),\quad X,Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{M},

(8)

then
({f},Q,\xi,\eta,g)
is called a
weak almost contact metric structure
,
and
g
is a
compatible metric
.
Putting
Y=\xi
in (
8
) and using
Q\,\xi=\xi
, we get

g(X,\xi)=\eta(X)
;
moreover,
the tensor
{f}
is skew-symmetric and the tensor
Q
is self-adjoint.

Remark 1

(i) The concept of an almost para-contact structure
is closely related to an almost contact structure and an almost product structure, see
[
9
]
.
Similarly to (
7
), we define a
weak almost para-contact structure
by

f^{2}=Q-\eta\otimes\xi,\ Q\,\xi=\xi
, see
[
21
]
.

(ii) A weak almost contact structure admits a compatible Riemannian metric if
{f}

admits a skew-symmetric representation, i.e., for any
x\in M
there exist a neighborhood
U_{x}\subset M
and a frame
\{e_{k}\}
on
U_{x}
,
for which
{f}
has a skew-symmetric matrix.

A
weak contact metric structure
is a weak almost contact metric structure satisfying
d\eta=\Phi
,
where the
2
-form
\Phi
is defined by
\Phi({X},{Y})=g({X},{f}{Y}),\ {X},{Y}\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
.
For a weak contact metric structure
({f},Q,\xi,\eta,g)
, the 1-form
\eta
is contact.
A weak almost contact structure is
normal
if the tensor
{\cal N}^{\,(1)}=[{f},{f}]+2\,d\eta\otimes\,\xi
vanishes.

A weak almost contact metric structure
is called a
weak
{K}
-structure
if it is normal and

d\Phi=0
.
We define two subclasses of weak
{K}
-manifolds as follows:

weak cosymplectic manifolds
if
d\eta=0
, and
weak Sasakian manifolds
if

d\eta=\Phi
.
Omitting the normality condition, we get the following:
a weak almost contact metric structure
is called
a
weak almost cosymplectic structure
if
d\eta=\Phi
is valid;
and
a
weak almost Sasakian structure
if
\Phi
and
\eta
are closed forms.

In
[
23
]
, we proved rigidity results: a weak Sasakian structure is the Sasakian structure
and a weak almost contact metric structure satisfying
\nabla f=0
is a weak cosymplectic structure.
For this, we calculated the derivative
\nabla f
, using a new tensor
{\cal N}^{(5)}
, see (
12
),
in addition to classical tensors
{\cal N}^{(i)}\ (i=1,2,3,4)
.

Example 1

Let
M^{2n+1}(\varphi,Q,\xi,\eta)
be a weak almost contact manifold.
Consider the product manifold
\bar{M}=M^{2n+1}\times\mathbb{R}
,
and define tensor fields
\bar{f}
and
\bar{Q}
on
\bar{M}
putting

\bar{f}(X,\,a\partial_{1})=(fX-a\,\xi,\,\eta(X)\partial_{1}),\quad\bar{Q}(X,\,a\,\partial_{1})=(QX,\,a\partial_{1}),\quad a\in C^{\infty}(M).

Hence,
\bar{f}(X,0)=(fX,0)
,
\bar{Q}(X,0)=(QX,0)
for
X\in\ker f
,

\bar{f}(\xi,0)=(0,\partial_{1})
,
\bar{Q}(\xi,0)=(\xi,0)

and

\bar{f}(0,\partial_{1})=(-\xi,0)
,
\bar{Q}(0,\partial_{1})=(0,\partial_{1})
.
Then it is easy to verify that
\bar{f}^{\,2}=-\bar{Q}
. The tensors
{\cal N}^{\,(i)}\ (i=1,2,3,4)
appear when we use
the integrability condition
[\bar{f},\bar{f}]=0
of
\bar{f}
to express the normality condition
{\cal N}^{\,(1)}=0
of a weak almost contact structure.

The relationships between some classes of weak structures is summarized in the following diagram (known for classical structures):

\left|\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-5.69054pt\textrm{weak\ almost}\\
\textrm{contact\ metric}\end{array}\right|\overset{\Phi=d\eta}{\longrightarrow}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-2.84526pt\textrm{weak\ contact}\\
\textrm{metric}\end{array}\right|\overset{\xi\,\textrm{-Killing}}{\longrightarrow}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-5.69054pt\textrm{weak}\\
\textrm{K-contact}\end{array}\right|\overset{{\cal N}^{\,(1)}=0}{\longrightarrow}\left|\begin{array}[]{c}\hskip-5.69054pt\textrm{weak}\\
\textrm{Sasakian}\end{array}\right|.

In
[
21
]
, we prove that in the case of a weak structure, the partial Ricci flow,

\partial g/\partial t=-2\,(\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}(g)-\Phi\,{\rm id}^{\bot})

where
\Phi:M\to\mathbb{R}

reduces to ODE’s,

(d/dt)\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}=4\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}(\operatorname{Ric}^{\bot}-\Phi\,{\rm id}^{\bot})

and that weak structures with positive partial Ricci curvature retract to the classical structures with positive constant partial Ricci curvature.

We define a weak almost Hermitian structure on a Riemannian manifold of even dimension,
(M^{2n},g)
, equipped with a skew-symmetric (1,1)-tensor

.
Such

is a weak Kählerian structure, if
\nabla f=0
, where
\nabla

Weak nearly Sasakian/cosymplectic manifolds are defined by a similar condition (

in the same spirit starting from Sasakian and cosymplectic manifolds, see

A distribution

\nabla_{X}Y+\nabla_{Y}X\in{\cal D}\ (X,Y\in{\cal D})

this is the case when any geodesic of

[

Any such integrable distribution defines a totally geodesic foliation.
A foliation, whose orthogonal distribution is totally geodesic, is said to be a Riemannian foliation.

The notion of a warped product is popular in differential geometry as well as in general relativity.
Some solutions of Einstein field equations are warped products and some spacetime models

and extend some results on Ricci-type solitons and Einstein-type metrics from Kenmotsu

\beta

2
Preliminaries

Here, we discuss the basic properties of weak structures.

By
(
7
),
\ker\eta
is
Q
-invariant
and the following equalities are true:

\displaystyle{f}\,\xi=0,\quad\eta\circ{f}=0,\quad\eta\circ Q=\eta,\quad[Q,\,{f}]:={Q}\circ{f}-{f}\circ{Q}=0.

Recall the following formulas with the Lie derivative
\pounds
in the
Z
-direction:

\displaystyle(\pounds_{Z}{f})X=[Z,{f}X]-{f}[Z,X],\quad(\pounds_{Z}\,\eta)X=Z(\eta(X))-\eta([Z,X]),

\displaystyle(\pounds_{Z}\,g)(X,Y)=g(\nabla_{X}\,Z,Y)+g(\nabla_{Y}\,Z,X).

The Nijenhuis torsion
of
{f}
and the exterior derivative
of
\eta
and
\Phi
are given by

\displaystyle[{f},{f}](X,Y)={f}^{2}[X,Y]+[{f}X,{f}Y]-{f}[{f}X,Y]-{f}[X,{f}Y],

\displaystyle d\eta(X,Y)=(1/2)\,\{X(\eta(Y))-Y(\eta(X))-\eta([X,Y])\},

\displaystyle d\Phi(X,Y,Z)=({1}/{3})\,\big\{X\,\Phi(Y,Z)+Y\,\Phi(Z,X)+Z\,\Phi(X,Y)

\displaystyle\qquad-\,\Phi([X,Y],Z)-\Phi([Z,X],Y)-\Phi([Y,Z],X)\big\}.

The following tensors on almost contact manifolds are well known, see
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(2)}(X,Y)=(\pounds_{{f}X}\,\eta)Y-(\pounds_{{f}Y}\,\eta)X=2\,d\eta({f}X,Y)-2\,d\eta({f}Y,X),

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(3)}(X)=(\pounds_{\xi}{f})X=[\xi,{f}X]-{f}[\xi,X],

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(4)}(X)=(\pounds_{\xi}\,\eta)X=\xi(\eta(X))-\eta([\xi,X])=2\,d\eta(\xi,X).

The following statement is based on
[
1
, Theorem 6.1]
, i.e.,
Q={\rm id}_{\,TM}
.

Proposition 1

Let a weak almost contact structure be normal. Then
{\cal N}^{\,(3)}={\cal N}^{\,(4)}=0
and

{\cal N}^{\,(2)}(X,Y)=\eta([\widetilde{Q}X,\,{f}Y])
;
moreover, the vector field
\xi
is geodesic.

Proposition 2

For a metric weak almost contact structure
we get

\displaystyle 2\,g((\nabla_{X}{f})Y,Z)=3\,d\Phi(X,{f}Y,{f}Z)-3\,d\Phi(X,Y,Z)+g({\cal N}^{\,(1)}(Y,Z),{f}X)

\displaystyle\quad+\,{\cal N}^{\,(2)}(Y,Z)\,\eta(X)+2\,d\eta({f}Y,X)\,\eta(Z)-2\,d\eta({f}Z,X)\,\eta(Y)+{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(X,Y,Z),

where a skew-symmetric with respect to
Y
and
Z
(0,3)-tensor
{\cal N}^{\,(5)}
is defined by

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(X,Y,Z)=({f}Z)\,(g(X,\widetilde{Q}Y))-({f}Y)\,(g(X,\widetilde{Q}Z))+g([X,{f}Z],\widetilde{Q}Y)

\displaystyle\qquad-\,g([X,{f}Y],\widetilde{Q}Z)+g([Y,{f}Z]-[Z,{f}Y]-{f}[Y,Z],\ \widetilde{Q}X).

(12)

For particular values of the tensor
{\cal N}^{\,(5)}
we get

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(X,\xi,Z)=-{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(X,Z,\xi)=g({\cal N}^{\,(3)}(Z),\,\widetilde{Q}X),

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(\xi,Y,Z)=g([\xi,{f}Z],\widetilde{Q}Y)-g([\xi,{f}Y],\widetilde{Q}Z),

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(\xi,\xi,Y)={\cal N}^{\,(5)}(\xi,Y,\xi)=0.

Theorem 1

(i)
On a weak
{K}
-contact manifold the vector field
\xi
is Killing and geodesic, and

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{\,(1)}(\xi,\,\cdot)={\cal N}^{\,(5)}(\xi,\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot)={\cal N}^{\,(5)}(\,\cdot\,,\xi,\,\cdot)={\cal N}^{\,(5)}(\cdot\,,\xi,\,\cdot)=0,

\displaystyle\pounds_{\,\xi}{Q}=\nabla_{\xi}{Q}=0,\ \nabla_{\xi}{f}=0,\quad\nabla_{X}\,\xi=-fX,

\displaystyle g((\nabla_{X}{f})Y,Z)=d\eta({f}Y,X)\,\eta(Z)-d\eta({f}Z,X)\,\eta(Y)+\frac{1}{2}\,\eta([\widetilde{Q}Y,\,{f}Z])\,\eta(X)+\frac{1}{2}\,{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(X,Y,Z).

(ii)
For a weak almost Sasakian structure,
\xi
is a geodesic vector field and the tensors
{\cal N}^{\,(2)}
and
{\cal N}^{\,(4)}
vanish;
moreover,
{\cal N}^{\,(3)}
vanishes if and only if
\,\xi

\displaystyle g((\nabla_{X}{f})Y,Z)=\frac{1}{2}\,g({\cal N}^{\,(1)}(Y,Z),{f}X)+g(fX,fY)\eta(Z)-g(fX,fZ)\eta(Y){+}\frac{1}{2}\,{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(X,Y,Z).

(13)

In particular,
g((\nabla_{\xi}{f})Y,Z)=\frac{1}{2}\,{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(\xi,Y,Z)
.

(iii)
For a weak almost cosymplectic manifold, we get

,
{\cal N}^{\,(1)}=[{f},{f}]
, and

\xi

{\cal N}^{\,(3)}=0
if and only if

3
The rigidity of Sasakian structure

and characteristic of a cosymplectic structure

Here, we present rigidity results for weak Sasakian manifolds
and characterize weak cosymplectic manifolds in the class of weak almost contact metric manifolds.

Proposition 3

For a weak Sasakian structure, (
13
) reduces to

\displaystyle g((\nabla_{X}{f})Y,Z)=g(QX,Y)\,\eta(Z)-g(QX,Z)\,\eta(Y)

\displaystyle+\,\eta(X)\big(\eta(Y)\,\eta(Z)-\eta(Y)\,\eta(Z)\big)+\frac{1}{2}\,{\cal N}^{\,(5)}(X,Y,Z).

The equality
{\cal N}^{(3)}=0
is valid for weak Sasakian manifolds, since it is true for Sasakian manifolds, see Theorem
2

We get the rigidity of the Sasakian structure.

Theorem 2

A weak almost contact metric structure is a weak Sasakian structure if and only if it is a Sasakian structure.

Proposition 4

Let
({f},Q,\xi,\eta,g)
be a weak
cosymplectic structure. Then

\displaystyle 2\,g((\nabla_{X}{f})Y,Z)={\cal N}^{\,(5)}(X,Y,Z).

In particular, using (

Theorem 3

A weak almost contact metric structure with

Let

To construct a weak cosymplectic structure on

Minimal and Cellular Free Resolutions over Polynomial OI-algebras Thanks: The second author was partially supported by Simons Foundation grants #317096 and #636513.
Nathan Fieldsteel, Uwe Nagel

Abstract
. Minimal free resolutions of graded modules over a noetherian polynomial ring have been attractive objects of interest for more than a hundred years. We introduce and study two natural extensions in the setting of graded modules over a polynomial OI \mathrm{OI} -algebra, namely minimal and width-wise minimal free resolutions. A minimal free resolution of an OI \mathrm{OI} -module can be characterized by the fact that the free module in every fixed homological degree, say i i , has minimal rank among all free resolutions of the module. We show that any finitely generated graded module over a noetherian polynomial OI \mathrm{OI} -algebra admits a graded minimal free resolution and that it is unique. A width-wise minimal free resolution is a free resolution that provides a minimal free resolution of a module in every width. Such a resolution is necessarily minimal. Its existence is not guaranteed. However, we show that certain monomial OI \mathrm{OI} -ideals do admit width-wise minimal free or, more generally, width-wise minimal flat resolutions. These ideals include families of well-known monomial ideals such as Ferrers ideals and squarefree strongly stable ideals. The arguments rely on the theory of cellular resolutions.



1.
Introduction

\mathrm{FI}
-modules were introduced in
[
4
]
. Since then their
theory has been further developed in many directions, see, e.g.,

[
3
,
5
,
6
,
10
,
22
]
. Here

\mathrm{FI}
denotes the category of finite sets and injective functions,
and an
\mathrm{FI}
-module is a functor from
\mathrm{FI}
to
R
-mod where

R
is a commutative ring. Central to the theory of
\mathrm{FI}
-modules
is the notion of noetherianity, which encodes the observed
stability. Some of the proofs in the theory of
\mathrm{FI}
-modules are
predicated on the introduction of
\mathrm{OI}
-modules, where
\mathrm{OI}
is
the category of totally-ordered finite sets with order-preserving
injective functions. Every
\mathrm{FI}
-module can be thought of as an

\mathrm{OI}
-module by composition with the forgetful functor

\mathrm{OI}\rightarrow\mathrm{FI}
.

A separate but related area of interest involves the study of a
non-noetherian ring
R
equipped with an action of a group or monoid

G
, the prime example being a polynomial ring

k[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n},\ldots]
in infinitely many
variables, equipped with an action of the infinite symmetric group.
For suitable

it is shown that every
G
-invariant ideal is
generated by finitely many
G
-orbits (see
[
7
,
1
,
14
]
).

These two subjects were unified in
[
19
]
with the
introduction of
\mathrm{FI}
-and
\mathrm{OI}
-algebras and modules over these
algebras, together with a theory of Gröbner bases and free
resolutions. Hilbert functions of
\mathrm{OI}
-modules were studied in

[
18
,
13
,
20
]
.

In this paper, we add to the theory of ideals and modules over
OI-algebras by first introducing notions of
minimal
free
resolutions of modules over polynomial OI-algebras. We prove that if

\mathbf{A}
is a noetherian graded polynomial OI-algebra and
\mathbf{M}

is a finitely generated
\mathbf{A}
-module, then a minimal graded
free resolution of
\mathbf{M}
exists and is unique up to isomorphism, and
any finitely generated free resolution of
\mathbf{M}
contains a minimal
free resolution as a direct summand (see
3.10
). This is directly analogous to the theory of minimal free
resolutions for graded modules over noetherian polynomial rings.

In addition, we provide a related notion of a
width-wise
minimal
free resolution, corresponding to the situation where a
free resolution of an
\mathbf{A}
-module
\mathbf{M}
in fact parametrizes a
family of minimal free resolutions of modules over different
polynomial rings. This is a much stricter condition, and it is not as
well behaved as ordinary minimality for free resolutions.

We also consider flat resolutions. If an
\mathrm{OI}
-module
\mathbf{M}
admits
a resolution by
\mathrm{OI}
-modules that gives a minimal free resolution
in each width
\mathbf{M}(w)
of
\mathbf{M}
with

, then such a
resolution is a resolution by flat
\mathrm{OI}
-modules (see

3.17
). There are modules that do not
admit a width-wise minimal free resolution, but do have a width-wise
minimal flat resolution.
In particular we prove that if
\mathbf{I}
is an
\mathrm{OI}
-ideal
parametrizing a family of Ferrers ideals or of certain squarefree
strongly stable ideals, then the complex-of-boxes resolutions
introduced in
[
17
]
can be assembled into a resolution of

\mathbf{I}
by flat
\mathbf{A}
-modules, providing an example of a width-wise
minimal flat resolution (see
4.10
). In some cases, this
resolution is even a width-wise minimal free resolution (see

4.11
).

The methods in this note allow one also to establish that any finitely
generated
\mathrm{FI}
-module over a noetherian polynomial
\mathrm{FI}
-algebra
admits a minimal resolution of free
\mathrm{FI}
-modules. However, we are
not aware of any non-free
\mathrm{FI}
-module that admits a width-wise
minimal resolution by flat
\mathrm{FI}
-modules. Thus, we focus on
resolutions of
\mathrm{OI}
-modules.

In
Section

2
, we introduce and define our objects of study and
establish some basic properties. In
Section

3
, we define minimal free
resolutions and width-wise minimal free resolutions as well as
width-wise minimal flat resolutions, and we connect their properties

\mathrm{OI}
-algebra (see
3.13
).

Using cellular resolutions, we give explicit constructions of

\mathrm{OI}
-algebras in

Section

4
.

2.

We recall needed concepts, fix notation and present illustrating

Definition 2.1
.

We use
\mathrm{OI}
to denote the category whose objects are
totally-ordered finite sets and whose morphisms are the
order-preserving injective functions.

For any natural number

[n]=\left\{i\in\mathbb{Z}~|~1\leqslant i\leqslant n\right\}

to
refer to this object in
\mathrm{OI}

The category
\mathrm{OI}
is equivalent to its skeleton, the category with
just one object
n

and morphisms being
order-preserving injective maps
\varepsilon\colon m\to n

morphism will be denoted with the string of natural numbers

\varepsilon(1)\varepsilon(2)\cdots\varepsilon(m)

\varepsilon:3\rightarrow 8
defined by its values

,

2\mapsto 5
and
3\mapsto 7
will be written as the string

357
. While this can lead to distinct morphisms in OI being
represented by the same string of digits, we will only use this
notation in contexts where no confusion or ambiguity can arise.

Definition 2.2

For a commutative unital ring

-module over

3.
Minimality for OI Resolutions

We begin to develop a theory of minimal free resolutions for

\mathbf{A}
-modules analogous to the theory of minimal free resolutions for
graded modules over a polynomial ring.

Recall that such minimal free resolutions can be characterized by the
minimality of the ranks of the free modules or, equivalently, by the
minimality of the maps appearing in a resolutions. One of our results
shows that both concepts of a minimality can be extended and lead to
equivalent descriptions of graded minimal free resolutions over a
polynomial
\mathrm{OI}
-algebra. We also consider width-wise minimal and
flat resolutions.

Throughout this section we assume that
\mathbf{A}
is a noetherian
commutative OI-algebra over a field
k
. If the category of

\mathbf{A}
-modules is noetherian, then any finitely generated

\mathbf{A}
-module
\mathbf{M}
admits a resolution by finitely generated free

\mathbf{A}
-modules (see
[
19
, Theorem 7.1]
). In fact, since

\mathbf{M}
is finitely generated, there exists a surjective map from a
finitely generated free
\mathbf{A}
-module
\mathbf{F}_{0}
onto
\mathbf{M}
. By
assumption, the kernel of this map is finitely generated . Thus, one
can iterate the construction and obtains a free resolution of
\mathbf{M}
,

\cdots\xrightarrow{\varphi_{3}}\mathbf{F}_{2}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{2}}\mathbf{F}_{1}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{1}}\mathbf{F}_{0}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{0}}\mathbf{M}\rightarrow 0

where each module
\mathbf{F}_{d}
is free and finitely
generated. Moreover, if
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{M}
are graded, there is a graded
free resolution of
\mathbf{M}
, that is, all maps
\varphi_{d}
are
degree-preserving.

In this section, we prove a family of results analogous to classical
results on minimal free resolutions for finitely generated graded
modules over polynomial rings. Recall that in the classical setting,
a homogeneous map
\varphi:F\rightarrow G
between graded free
modules over a polynomial ring
R
is called
minimal
if it
can be represented as a matrix having no unit entries. We adapt this
definition of minimality to maps between free
\mathbf{A}
-modules.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a graded polynomial
\mathrm{OI}
-algebra, and let
\mathbf{F}

and
\mathbf{G}
be finitely generated graded free
\mathbf{A}
-modules
with bases
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{s}\}
and

\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}\}
, where each
f_{i}
has width

u_{i}
and each
g_{j}
has width
v_{j}
. A (graded) map

\varphi:\mathbf{F}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}
is determined by its value
on the elements of the basis of
\mathbf{F}
, i.e., it is determined by

s
expressions of the form

\varphi(f_{i})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{t}\left(\sum\limits_{\varepsilon_{i,j}\in\operatorname{Hom}(v_{j},u_{i})}a_{\varepsilon_{i,j}}\cdot\varepsilon_{i,j}(g_{j})\right),

where each
a_{\varepsilon_{i,j}}
is a (homogenous) element of

\mathbf{A}(u_{i})
. We say that
\varphi
is
minimal
if
whenever any map
\varepsilon_{i,j}
is an identity morphism in

\mathrm{OI}
, the coefficient
a_{\varepsilon_{i,j}}
is not a unit. A
graded complex of finitely generated free
\mathbf{A}
-modules is
called
minimal
if all of the maps in the complex are
minimal.

We will prove in
3.10
that this definition of
minimality is equivalent to the definition one might expect, i.e.,
that a graded free resolution

\cdots\xrightarrow{\varphi_{3}}\mathbf{F}_{2}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{2}}\mathbf{F}_{1}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{1}}\mathbf{F}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbf{M}\rightarrow 0

of an
\mathbf{A}
-module
\mathbf{M}
is minimal if and only if for any other
graded free resolution
\mathbf{G}_{\bullet}
of
\mathbf{M}
and for
any homological degree
d
, the rank of
\mathbf{F}_{d}
is less than
or equal to the rank of
\mathbf{G}_{d}
. Furthermore, in analogy
with the classical setting, any finitely generated graded

\mathbf{A}
-module admits a minimal free resolution, and any two minimal
free resolutions of
\mathbf{M}
are isomorphic as chain complexes.

Observe that any graded free resolution
\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}
of

\mathbf{M}
,

\cdots\xrightarrow{\varphi_{3}}\mathbf{F}_{2}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{2}}\mathbf{F}_{1}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{1}}\mathbf{F}_{0}\xrightarrow{}\mathbf{M}\rightarrow 0

gives, for each width
w
, a graded free resolution

\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(w)
of
\mathbf{M}(w)
over
\mathbf{A}(w)
,

\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{F}_{2}(w)\rightarrow\mathbf{F}_{1}(w)\rightarrow\mathbf{F}_{0}(w)\rightarrow\mathbf{M}(w)\rightarrow 0.

If
\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}
is minimal one may wish that each resolution

\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}(w)
is also minimal, but in general this is not
the case. Requiring this property is a stronger, but slightly less well behaved
condition on free resolutions, which we define here.

Definition 3.2
.

With the same setup as in
3.1
, a map

\varphi:\mathbf{F}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}
is called
width-wise
minimal
if none of the coefficients
a_{\varepsilon_{i,j}}
is a
unit, and a complex
\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}
of free
\mathbf{A}
-modules is
called width-wise minimal if all of the maps in the complex are
width-wise minimal.

It is clear from the definition that every width-wise minimal free
resolution is also a minimal free resolution. However, the converse is
not true in general. We illustrate this fact and the above concepts
with an example that is closely related to the Koszul complex defined
in
[
19
]
.

Example 3.3
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be the polynomial OI-algebra

\operatorname{Sym}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{OI},1})
, and let

be the submodule which is

in width

and, for

that is generated by

For our study of minimal resolutions, we first prove a relationship
between minimality and trivial complexes which is a direct analog of
the corresponding result for graded modules over a polynomial ring,
namely that the only way a complex can fail to be minimal is if it has
a trivial complex as a direct summand.

Definition 3.4
.

A complex of free
\mathbf{A}
-modules is called
trivial
if it is
isomorphic to a direct sum of complexes of the form

\cdots\rightarrow 0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\mathbf{F}\xrightarrow{\text{id}}\mathbf{F}\rightarrow 0\rightarrow 0\rightarrow\cdots,

where
\mathbf{F}
is a finitely generated free
\mathbf{A}
-module.

Lemma 3.5
.

A graded complex
\mathbf{F}_{\bullet}
of finitely generated free

\mathbf{A}
-modules is minimal if and only if it does not have a trivial
complex as a direct summand.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the result for a two-term complex

\varphi\colon\mathbf{F}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}
. With notation as in

3.1
, suppose that
\varphi
is determined by

\varphi(f_{i})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{t}\left(\sum\limits_{\varepsilon_{i,j}\in\operatorname{Hom}(v_{j},u_{i})}a_{\varepsilon_{i,j}}\cdot\varepsilon_{i,j}(g_{j})\right),

and assume without loss of generality that

u_{1}=v_{1}
and that
a=a_{\varepsilon_{1,1}}
is a unit in

\mathbf{A}(u_{1})
. Since the identity map is the only element of
\operatorname{Hom}(v_{1},u_{1})
we get
\varepsilon_{1,1}=\text{id}
, and thus

\varphi(f_{1})=ag_{1}+\sum\limits_{j=2}^{t}\left(\sum\limits_{\varepsilon_{i,j}\in\operatorname{Hom}(v_{j},u_{i})}a_{\varepsilon_{i,j}}\cdot\varepsilon_{i,j}(g_{j})\right).

Hence the set
\{g_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,g_{t}^{\prime}\}
defined by

is also a basis of
\mathbf{G}
. Similarly, we get another basis

\{f_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,f_{s}^{\prime}\}
of
\mathbf{F}
by setting

f_{1}^{\prime}=f_{1}\quad\text{ and }\quad f_{i}^{\prime}=f_{i}-a^{-1}\cdot\left(\sum\limits_{\varepsilon_{i,1}\in\operatorname{Hom}(v_{1},u_{i})}a_{\varepsilon_{i,1}}\cdot\varepsilon_{i,1}(f_{1})\right)\;\text{ if }2\leqslant i\leqslant s.

Since
\varphi
is a natural transformation, we have that

\varphi(\varepsilon_{i,1}(f_{1}))=\varepsilon_{i,1}(\varphi(f_{1}))
. If follows
for

that
\varphi(f_{i}^{\prime})
has no summands
involving

for any
\mathrm{OI}
-morphism
\varepsilon
. Since

\varphi(f_{1}^{\prime})=g_{1}^{\prime}
, this shows that
\varphi
is a direct sum
of two maps, at least one of which is trivial.

Conversely, it is clear by definition that if
\varphi
has a
trivial summand, then it is not minimal.
∎

Next, we prove a version of Nakayama’s lemma for modules over
polynomial
\mathrm{OI}
-algebras, which states that for a finitely
generated
\mathbf{A}
-module, there is a well-behaved notion of a minimal
generating set.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathbf{M}\not=0
be a finitely generated graded
\mathbf{A}
-module. A
subset

of homogeneous elements is called a

minimal generating set
of
\mathbf{M}
if

is a generating set
for
\mathbf{M}
and no proper subset of

is a generating set for

\mathbf{M}
. Let

and

be minimal generating sets for

\mathbf{M}
. Then

and (up to a permutation),
g_{i}
and

h_{i}
have the same width and same degree for each
i
with

1\leqslant i\leqslant n
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, let
w
be the
width of
g_{1}
. Then
\mathbf{M}(w)
is minimally generated as
an
\mathbf{A}(w)
-module by

, and
\mathbf{M}
is
generated by
\mathbf{M}(w)
as an
\mathbf{A}
-module. Since

w
is the smallest width for which
\mathbf{M}(w)
is non-zero,
there must be at least one

of width
w
. Let

be the generators of width
w
and let

H^{\prime\prime}\subseteq H
be the complement of
H^{\prime}
. Now
H^{\prime}
is a
minimal generating set for
\mathbf{M}(w)
as an

\mathbf{A}(w)
-module, and it follows that
H^{\prime}
is a singleton
set

, and that the degree of

is the
degree of
g_{1}
. Since
\mathbf{M}(w)
generates

\mathbf{M}
as an
\mathbf{A}
-module and
H
is a minimal
generating set, it follows that
H^{\prime\prime}
is empty.

Next we consider the case where
n>1
. Let
w
be the smallest
natural number for which
\mathbf{M}(w)
is nonzero. Then let

G^{\prime}\subseteq G
and

be the (necessarily

\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}

\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}
and

\overline{H^{\prime\prime}}

and

∎

The implication (ii)

Lemma 3.9
.

A free
\mathbf{A}
-module
\mathbf{F}
is determined up to isomorphism by the

\{\rank\mathbf{F}(w)~|~w\in\mathbb{N}\}
, where
\mathbf{F}(w)

as an
\mathbf{A}(w)
-module.

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{F}
and
\mathbf{G}
are free, we may assume

{\displaystyle\mathbf{F}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{OI},m_{i}}}
and

{\displaystyle\mathbf{G}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{A}}^{\mathrm{OI},n_{j}}}

∎

We collect the preceding results into the following theorem, which
draws an analogy between minimal free resolutions for modules over
noetherian polynomial rings and minimal free resolutions for modules
over
\mathrm{OI}
-algebras.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a polynomial
\mathrm{OI}
-algebra, and let
\mathbf{M}
be a
finitely generated graded
\mathbf{A}
-module. Then one has:

(i)

Any graded resolution of
\mathbf{M}
by finitely generated
free
\mathbf{A}
-modules contains a minimal free resolution as a direct
summand.

(ii)

Any two graded minimal free resolutions of
\mathbf{M}
are
isomorphic.

(iii)

If
\mathbf{A}
is noetherian, then a graded minimal free
resolution of
\mathbf{M}
exists.

Proof.

Statement (i) is a consequence of
3.8
. For statement
(ii),
3.8
implies that for two minimal free resolutions
of
\mathbf{M}
, each contains the other as a direct summand. Together
with
3.9
this means those resolutions are
isomorphic. For statement (iii), noetherianity implies the existence
of a resolution of
\mathbf{M}
by finitely generated free
\mathbf{A}
-modules
(see
[
19
, Theorem 6.15]
), and applying statement (i)
gives that any such resolution has a minimal free resolution as a
direct summand.
∎

For explicit examples of graded minimal free resolutions we refer to

4.11
in the next section.

Although any graded module
\mathbf{M}
over a noetherian polynomial

\mathrm{OI}
-algebra
\mathbf{A}
admits a graded minimal free resolution, such a
resolution is not necessarily width-wise minimal (see

Example

3.3
). However, as the following example shows,
\mathbf{M}
may
admit a graded complex of not necessarily free modules that in each
width
w
gives a minimal free resolution of
\mathbf{M}(w)
over

\mathbf{A}(w)
.

Example 3.11
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be the polynomial OI-algebra

\operatorname{Sym}_{\bullet}(\mathbf{F}^{\mathrm{OI},1})
, and let
\mathbf{I}
be
the ideal generated by
x_{1}\in\mathbf{A}(2)
.

As in
Example

3.3
, one can show that
\mathbf{I}
cannot have a
width-wise minimal free resolution. However, as a special case of

4.10
,
\mathbf{I}
is resolved by graded exact chain complex

\mathbf{B}_{\bullet}
where in each width
w
,

\mathbf{B}_{\bullet}(w)
is a minimal free resolution of

\mathbf{I}(w)
. We sketch its construction here. In each width
w
,

\mathbf{I}(w)
is the ideal in
\mathbf{A}(w)=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{w}]

generated by the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{w-1}
, and so

\mathbf{I}(w)
is minimally resolved by a standard Koszul complex on
these variables. Consider this Koszul complex as the graded
components of the exterior algebra on a free
\mathbf{A}(w)
-module with
basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{w-1}\}
and with the standard
Koszul differentials. So
\mathbf{B}_{i}(w)
is the degree
i

component of this exterior algebra. These width-wise Koszul
resolutions form a resolution
\mathbf{B}_{\bullet}
of
\mathbf{I}
by

\mathbf{A}
-modules, where an
\mathrm{OI}
-morphism
\varepsilon:w\rightarrow w^{\prime}

induces a map of exterior algebras

\mathbf{B}_{i}(w)\rightarrow\mathbf{B}_{i}(w^{\prime})
by acting on
indices
e_{i}\mapsto e_{\varepsilon(i)}
.

The
\mathbf{A}
-module
\mathbf{B}_{1}
in this resolution is
generated by the element
e_{1}
in width
2
. But the rank of
this module in any width
w>1
is
w-1
. So it is not a free

\mathbf{A}
-module.

In order to extend the observation in the above example and to guide
the search for complexes that provide width-wise minimal free
resolutions we introduce flat
\mathrm{OI}
-modules.

Definition 3.12
.

An
\mathbf{A}
-module
\mathbf{Q}
is called
\mathbf{A}
-flat
(or
simply
flat
when there is no ambiguity) if the functor

{-}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{Q}
is an exact functor from the category
of
\mathbf{A}
-modules to itself.

We now explain how this concept relates to width-wise minimal free
resolutions.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
\mathbf{Q}
be a finitely generated graded module over a
polynomial
\mathrm{OI}
-algebra
\mathbf{A}
. Then
\mathbf{Q}
is

\mathbf{A}
-flat if and only if, for every width
w
,
\mathbf{Q}(w)

is a free
\mathbf{A}(w)
-module.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathbf{Q}
is
\mathbf{A}
-flat and consider

\mathbf{Q}(w)
. Any sequence of
\mathbf{A}(w)
-modules

0\rightarrow N^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow N\rightarrow N^{\prime}\rightarrow 0

can be lifted to an exact sequence of
\mathbf{A}
-modules, which is this
sequence in width
w
and is the zero sequence in all other
widths. Since
{-}_{\mathbf{A}}\otimes\mathbf{Q}
is exact, it follows
that
{-}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}(w)}\mathbf{Q}(w)
is exact. Hence,

\mathbf{Q}(w)
is a finitely generated flat module over a
polynomial ring
\mathbf{A}(w)
, and so it is a projective

\mathbf{A}(w)
-module (see
[
2
]
). By the Quillen-Suslin
Theorem
[
21
,
23
]
, it follows that
\mathbf{Q}(w)
is a
free
\mathbf{A}(w)
-module.

Conversely, if
\mathbf{Q}(w)
is a free
\mathbf{A}(w)
-module for each
width
w
, then the functor
{-}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{Q}
is exact
in each width, and so it is exact. Hence
\mathbf{Q}
is

\mathbf{A}
-flat.
∎

While free
\mathbf{A}
-modules are also
\mathbf{A}
-flat, the converse is of
course not true. Indeed, by the preceding result, the module
\mathbf{M}

considered in
Example

3.3
is flat, but not free.

We now extend the notion of width-wise minimal resolutions to include
resolutions by flat modules.

Definition 3.14
.

For an
\mathbf{A}
-module
\mathbf{M}
, a
width-wise minimal flat
resolution of
\mathbf{M}
is an exact sequence

\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\colon\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{3}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbf{M}\rightarrow 0,

where each
\mathbf{Q}_{i}
is a flat
\mathbf{A}
-module and,
in each width
w
, the complex
\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(w)
is a
minimal free resolution of
\mathbf{M}(w)
as an

\mathbf{A}(w)
-module.

Example 3.15
.

In
Example

3.3
, we exhibited an
\mathbf{A}
-module
\mathbf{M}
, which does
not admit a width-wise minimal free resolution. However, we observed
above that
\mathbf{M}
is actually a flat
\mathbf{A}
-module. Hence, it has a
(trivial) width-wise minimal flat resolution of the form

0\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbf{M}\rightarrow 0,

where
\mathbf{Q}_{0}=\mathbf{M}
and the map above is an
isomorphism. We present non-trivial examples of width-wise minimal
flat resolutions in the next section.

As in the classical situation, width-wise minimality admits an alternate characterization.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\mathbf{M}
be a finitely generated graded
\mathbf{A}
-module over a field

k
, and let
\mathbf{k}
be the constant
\mathrm{OI}
-algebra
determined by
k
. Consider a resolution of
\mathbf{M}
by finitely
generated flat graded
\mathbf{A}
-modules

\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\colon\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{2}\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}}\mathbf{Q}_{1}\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}}\mathbf{Q}_{0}.

The resolution
\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}
is width-wise minimal if and
only if in the complex

\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{k}
, the maps

\partial_{i}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{k}
are all zero.

Proof.

This follows immediately from our definition of width-wise
minimal. Indeed, the complex
\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}
is width-wise
minimal if and only if, for any width
w
, the maps in the complex

\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{2}(w)\otimes_{\mathbf{A}(w)}k\xrightarrow{\partial_{2}(w)\otimes_{\mathbf{A}(w)}k}\mathbf{Q}_{1}(w)\otimes_{\mathbf{A}(w)}k\xrightarrow{\partial_{1}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}(w)}k}\mathbf{Q}_{0}(w)\otimes_{\mathbf{A}(w)}k

are all zero (see, e.g.,
[
11
, Lemma 19.4]
). These are precisely the width-wise maps
in the complex

\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbf{A}}\mathbf{k}.

∎

The following result summarizes the above discussion and motives the search for flat resolutions.

Proposition 3.17
.

Consider a graded exact sequence of finitely generated modules over a polynomial
\mathrm{OI}
-algebra
\mathbf{A}
,

\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}\colon\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{3}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{2}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{0}\rightarrow\mathbf{M}\rightarrow 0.

Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

For every width
w
, the restricted complex

\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}(w)\colon\cdots\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{3}(w)\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{2}(w)\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{1}(w)\rightarrow\mathbf{Q}_{0}(w)\rightarrow\mathbf{M}(w)\rightarrow 0,

is a minimal free resolution of the
\mathbf{A}(w)
-module
\mathbf{M}(w)
.

(ii)

\mathbf{Q}_{\bullet}
is a width-wise minimal flat resolution of
\mathbf{M}
.

Proof.

3.13
shows that (i) implies (ii). The converse is clear.
∎

noetherian polynomial
\mathrm{OI}
-algebra. To this end we use the theory

Definition 4.3
.

Let
P
be a partially ordered set. For any
a,b
in
P

interval
from
a
to
b

[a,b]

in

. (Some authors

in

Often we will identify a monomial with its exponent vector and make
use of the following partially ordered sets.

Definition 4.4

Fix positive integers

with

, and

m_{1}\leqslant m_{2}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant m_{d}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\left\{(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{d})~|~1\leqslant i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{d}\leqslant n\right\}\subset[n]^{d},

\displaystyle=\left\{(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{d})~|~1\leqslant i_{1}\leqslant i_{2}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant i_{d}\leqslant n\right\}\subset[n]^{d},

\displaystyle=\left\{(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{d})~|~i_{1}<i_{2}<\ldots<i_{d}~\text{and}~1\leqslant i_{j}\leqslant m_{j}~\text{for each}~j\right\}\subset[m_{1}]\times\cdots\times[m_{d}]

.

\mathcal{Q}
and the set

d
in
R
. Using these

following
[

Let

)

Consider a squarefree strongly stable ideal

Let

Remark 4.14

It is worth noting that in

monomial ideals mentioned in

complex-of-boxes resolutions: squarefree strongly stable monomial
ideals, strongly stable monomial ideals, and Ferrers

-paremetrized families of strongly stable ideals and Ferrers
ideals, the complex-of-boxes resolutions can be given an

stable monomial ideals because in this case the analog of

This ideal is not strongly stable since it is missing

.

Introduction

Let
X
be a separable Banach space, and let
(X,\mu,H)
be an abstract Wiener space, i.e.,
\mu
is a centred non-degenerate Gaussian measure on
X
and
H
is the Cameron-Martin space associated to
\mu
. Further, let
\nu:=Ke^{-U}\mu
, where
U:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a suitable function (see Hypothesis
2.6
) and
K=\|e^{-U}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(X,\mu)}^{-1}
is the normalizing constant which gives
\nu(X)=1
, and let
V
be a separable Hilbert space.
The aim of this paper is to generalize to the spaces
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
and
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)
some important results which are already known in the
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
setting.

Abstract Wiener spaces have been introduced by Gross in
[
23
]
to study the properties of Gaussian measures on infinite-dimensional spaces. The fundamental idea in the theory of Gaussian measures is that every centred Gaussian measure is the realization of the same ”canonical” Gaussian measure: the countable product of the standard normal Gaussian distributions on the line. This fact, and the fact that in infinite dimension does not exist an analogous of Lebesgue measure, have increased the interest around Gaussian measures in infinite dimension, both from an analytic and a probabilistic point of view (see e.g.
[
11
,
25
,
28
,
30
]
). In an abstract Wiener space, the Gaussian measure
\mu
factors according to an orthogonal decomposition of
H
, and this implies that many results can be obtained arguing in finite dimension and then letting the dimension to infinity. The situation completely change when
\mu
is replaced by the measure
\nu
; in this case the finite dimensional approximations do not always work, and even when it happens computations are delicate and much more complicated.

The interest in weighted Gaussian measures
\nu
in infinite dimension increases in last years, since in general they represent a class of infinite dimensional measures which are not decomposable along directions of
H
. Further, by means of the Malliavin derivative
D_{H}
, on
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
it is possible to define a strongly continuous semigroup
(T_{p}(t))_{t\geq 0}
whose infinitesimal generator
L_{p}
is a perturbation of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck operator. Features of
(T_{p}(t))_{t\geq 0}
and of
L_{p}
, such as maximal Sobolev regularity for solution to the elliptic problem
\lambda u-L_{p}u=f
, with
\lambda>0
and
f\in\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
, smoothness properties of the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
and some functional inequalities are investigated both from an analytic and a probabilistic point of view also in more general setting, see
[
2
,
1
,
5
,
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
16
,
17
]
. We also refer to
[
20
]
for an in-depth analysis of the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\nu)
.

The central point of our investigation is a characterization of the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\nu)
with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(1,\infty)
(see below for the definition of
W^{k,p}(X,\nu)
), obtained by means of a family of vector-valued Poincaré inequalities and the Wiener chaos decomposition. To be more precise, we show that, for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
, there exists a positive constant
k_{p}
such that for every
F\in W^{1,p}(X,\nu;V)
it holds that

\displaystyle\|F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}\leq k_{p}\|\overline{D_{H}}F\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H\otimes V)},

(1.1)

where

\displaystyle\nu(F):=\int_{X}Fd\nu\in V.

We recall that a Poincaré inequality for weighted Gaussian measures has been already proved in
[
21
, Theorem 6.1]
, for smooth cylindrical real valued functions with
p=2
, in
[
21
, Theorem 6.2]
dropping the differentiability assumptions on
U
, and in
[
5
]
on convex domains in Hilbert spaces. However, we deal with the closure of
D_{H}
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
and therefore the belonging of
U
to some Sobolev space is necessary in order to define the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(X,\nu)
.

In the case of Gaussian measures, i.e., when
U=0
, these inequalities for vector-valued functions have been obtained in
[
11
,
30
]
when
p\in[1,+\infty)
by exploiting properties of Gaussian measures when
V
is a separable Hilbert space,
and in
[
26
, Proposition 3.1]
for
p\in(1,+\infty)
in the setting of Malliavin Calculus and when
V
is a UMD space. In the last paper, the authors use a vector-valued version of Meyer’s Multiplier Theorem, which does not work for
p=1
and which cannot be adapted to our situation since it strongly relies on the decomposition of the Gaussian measure
\mu
, which is no longer available for
\nu
. We also mention that in
[
31
]
the author provides a Poincaré inequality in Gaussian setting with respect to generalized directional gradients.

In this specific case when
V=\mathcal{H}_{k}(H)
,
f\in W^{k+1,p}(X,\nu)
and
F:=D_{H}^{k}f
, inequality (
1.1
) reads as

\displaystyle\|D_{H}^{k}f-\nu(D_{H}^{k}f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H))}\leq k_{p}\|D_{H}^{k+1}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(H))}.

(1.2)

Formula (
1.2
) allows us to prove the characterization of the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\nu)
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
, in terms the graph norm of
D_{H}^{k}
. The Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\nu)
have been introduced in
[
20
]
and they are defined as the domain of the closure of the operator

\displaystyle(D_{H},\ldots,D_{H}^{k}):\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)\times\ldots\times\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H)),

in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
, endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{k,p}:=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|D_{H}^{j}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{j}(H))}^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad f\in W^{k,p}(X,\nu).

We prove that the question if
\|\cdot\|_{k,p}
is equivalent to the graph norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p,D_{H}^{k}}:=\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}+\|D_{H}^{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H))},\quad f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V),

has positive answer, and this extends the analogous result for the Gaussian measure
\mu
. The case
U=0
and
p=1
has been partially solved in
[
3
]
, but it still remains an open problem.

As a byproduct, we get the closability of the operator
D_{H}^{k}:\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{k}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H))
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
when
U\in W^{k-1,q}(X,\mu)
and a sufficient condition for the belonging of a function
f
to
W^{k,p}(X,\nu)
. Finally, we provide an exponential decay for
(T^{V}(t))_{t\geq 0}
.

The paper is organized as follows.

In Section
2
we collect the background which we need in the sequel of the paper. In particular, we present the structure of abstract Wiener spaces, listing the main features of the Cameron-Martin space, of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup and of the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\mu)
. Then, we give the definition and the principal properties of the Wiener chaos decomposition which plays a crucial role in the proof of Theorems
5.1
and
5.5
. Later, we provide the assumptions on the function
U
, we introduce the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\nu)
and
W^{1,p}(X,\nu;V)
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in[1,+\infty)
, where
V
is a separable Hilbert space. Besides the results in
[
20
]
, we show other properties of the elements of the functions belonging to these Sobolev spaces which will be used in the following. We also introduce the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
associated with the symmetric bilinear form

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u,v):=\int_{X}[D_{H}u,D_{H}v]_{H}d\nu,\quad u,v\in W^{1,2}(X,\nu),

and its vector-valued extension
(T^{V}(t))_{t\geq 0}
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)
,
p\in[1,+\infty)
.

Section
3
is devoted to the study of the asymptotic behaviour of
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
and to provide pointwise estimates for
|D_{H}T(t)f|_{H}^{p}
by means both of
T(t)|D_{H}f|_{H}^{p}
and of
T(t)|f|^{p}
. We also generalize these results to the vector-valued semigroup
(T^{V}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, of which we investigate both the asymptotic behaviour in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)
and pointwise estimates for
|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F|_{H\otimes V}^{p}
when
p\in[1,+\infty)
. The gradient estimates follow from computations similar to those in the proofs of
[
5
, Theorems 3.1 & 3.3]
and are inspired by an idea of Bakry and Émery (see
[
6
]
), while the asymptotic behaviour of
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is obtained with different techniques with respect to those in the quoted paper: here, we take advantage of the asymptotic estimate (
3.2
) for
\|D_{H}T(t)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu;H)}
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, which allows us to characterize the limit of
T(t)f
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. The approximation results which we exploit to obtain the gradient estimates are postponed in Appendix
A
.

Sections
4
and
5
are the core of this paper. In the former we provide the vector-valued Poincaré inequalities (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), in the latter we give the equivalence of the norms
\|\cdot\|_{k,p}
and
\|\cdot\|_{p,D_{H}^{k}}
. We remark that the proof of (
1.1
), inspired by that in
[
3
]
, relies on duality arguments: when
p\geq 2
we use the fact that, for every
F\in W^{1,p}(X,\nu)
, the function
F^{*}:=|F|^{p-2}F\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)
and an explicit formula for
D_{H}F^{*}
is available (see Lemma
2.18
), while when
p\in[1,2)
we employ the duality between
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H\otimes V)
and
\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu;H\otimes V)
. We stress that the constant
k_{p}
which we obtain equals that in
[
3
]
when
U=0
.

Regarding the equivalence of
\|\cdot\|_{k,p}
and
\|\cdot\|_{p,D_{H}^{k}}
, we sketch the idea when
k=2
. In this case, it is enough to estimate
\|D_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)}
by means of
\|f\|_{p,D_{H}^{2}}
. From (
1.2
) we have

\displaystyle\|D_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)}\leq

\displaystyle\|D_{H}f-\nu(D_{H}f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)}+\|\nu(D_{H}f)\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k_{p}\|D_{H}^{2}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))}+\|\nu(D_{H}f)\|_{H}.

To conclude, it remains to prove that there exists a positive constant
c
such that
\|\nu(D_{H}f)\|_{H}\leq c\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}
. Similar same arguments allows us to extend the equivalence for
k\geq 3
, provided further assumptions on
U
. The last result of this section is an improvement of the asymptotic behaviour of
(T^{V}(t))_{t\geq 0}
, for which we show an exponential decay in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
.

Finally, in Appendix
A
we discuss the finite dimensional approximations which we use to prove the gradient estimates in Appendix
B
. Many of the computations follow the lines of those in
[
5
]
, but some slight modification is necessary since we weaken the assumptions on the weighted function
U
. Hence, we provide some details for reader’s convenience.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
The abstract Wiener space

Let
X
be a real separable Banach space and let
\mu
be a centered non-degenerate Gaussian measure on
X
. We follow
[
11
, Chapter 2]
to construct the Cameron–Martin space
H
associated to
\mu
. This construction will give us the abstract Wiener space
(X,\mu,H)
which will be the primary setting of our studies.
From the Fernique’s theorem
[
11
, Theorem 2.8.5]
, it follows that
X^{*}\subseteq L^{2}(X,\mu)
, and we denote by
j:X^{*}\rightarrow L^{2}(X,\mu)
the injection of
X^{*}
in
L^{2}(X,\mu)
, namely for every
x^{*}\in X^{*}
we have

\displaystyle(j(x^{*}))(x):={\left\langle x,x^{*}\right\rangle},\quad x\in X.

We remark that by
[
11
, Theorem 2.2.4]
there exists a nonnegative symmetric linear bounded operator
Q:X^{*}\rightarrow X
such that for every
x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2}\in X^{*}
we have

\displaystyle\langle Qx^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2}\rangle=\int_{X}j(x^{*}_{1})j(x^{*}_{2})d\mu.

(2.1)

We denote by
X_{\mu}^{*}
the closure of
j(X^{*})
in
L^{2}(X,\mu)
and we define
R:X^{*}_{\mu}\rightarrow(X^{*})^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle R(f)(x^{*}):=\int_{X}fj(x^{*})d\mu,\quad f\in X_{\mu}^{*},\ x^{*}\in X^{*}.

(2.2)

It is well-known that
R(X^{*}_{\mu})\subseteq X
. This means that for every
f\in X^{*}_{\mu}
there exists
\mathcal{R}(f)\in X
such that for every
x^{*}\in X^{*}

{\left\langle\mathcal{R}(f),x^{*}\right\rangle}=\int_{X}fj(x^{*})d\mu.

In particular the operator
\mathcal{R}:R(X^{*}_{\mu})\rightarrow X
is the adjoint of
j
.

We are now able to define the Cameron–Martin space
H
(see
[
11
, Section 2.2]
).

Definition 2.1
.

The Cameron–Martin space associated to
\mu
is
H:=\left\{h\in X:|h|_{H}<+\infty\right\}
,
where

|h|_{H}:=\sup\left\{\langle h,x^{*}\rangle\,\middle|\,x^{*}\in X^{*}\text{ such that }\|j(x^{*})\|_{L^{2}(X,\mu)}\leq 1\right\}.

From
[
11
, Lemma 2.4.1]
it follows that
h\in H
if and only if there exists
\widehat{h}\in X_{\mu}^{*}
such that
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{h})=h
. Furthermore
H
is a Hilbert space if endowed with the inner product

\displaystyle[h,k]_{H}=\int_{X}\widehat{h}\widehat{k}d\mu,\quad h,k\in H.

(2.3)

We stress that for every
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, from (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) we have
Qx^{*}\in H
and that
\mathcal{R}(j(x^{*}))=Qx^{*}
, i.e.,
\widehat{Qx^{*}}=j(x^{*})
.
Further, from (
2.3
) we deduce that

\displaystyle\langle Qx^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2}\rangle=[Qx^{*}_{1},Qx^{*}_{2}]_{H},\quad x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2}\in X^{*}.

(2.4)

We provide the following characterization of
H
.

Lemma 2.2
.

H
is the RKHS associated to
Q
in
X
, i.e.,
H=\overline{QX^{*}}^{|\cdot|_{H}}
, where
[Qx^{*},Qy^{*}]_{H}:=\langle Qx^{*},y^{*}\rangle
.

Proof.

The proof is quite simple but we provide it for the convenience of the reader. Let
h\in H
. Then, there exists
\widehat{h}\in X_{\mu}^{*}
such that
\mathcal{R}(\widehat{h})=h
. In particular, there exists
(x^{*}_{n})\subseteq X^{*}
such that the sequence
(j(x^{*}_{n}))
converges to
\widehat{h}
in
L^{2}(X,\mu)
. We claim that the sequence
(Qx^{*}_{n})
converges to
h
in
H
. Indeed, by (
2.3
) and recalling that
\widehat{Qx^{*}_{n}}=j(x^{*}_{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and (
2.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}|Qx^{*}_{n}-h|_{H}^{2}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}|j(x^{*}_{n})-\widehat{h}|^{2}d\mu=0.

This means that
\displaystyle H\subseteq\overline{QX^{*}}^{|\cdot|_{H}}
. The converse inclusion follows by analogous arguments.
∎

Let us denote by
i
the injection
i:H\rightarrow X
, and let
i^{*}:X^{*}\rightarrow H
be the adjoint operator of
i
(here we have identified
H^{*}
with
H
by means of the Riesz representation theorem), then
Q=i\circ i^{*}
. In particular, for every
x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2}\in X^{*}
, by (
2.1
) and (
2.3
) we have

\displaystyle[i^{*}x^{*}_{1},i^{*}x^{*}_{2}]_{H}=\langle(i\circ i^{*})x^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2}\rangle=\langle Qx^{*}_{1},x^{*}_{2}\rangle=\int_{X}j(x^{*}_{1})j(x^{*}_{2})d\mu.

(2.5)

Finally we introduce the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
(P(t))_{t\geq 0}
: for every
f\in C_{b}(X)
we set

\displaystyle P(t)f(x)=\int_{X}f(e^{-t}x+\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}y)\mu(dy),\quad x\in X,\ t\geq 0.

(P(t))_{t\geq 0}
extends to a strongly continuous semigroup of contractions on
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
(see
[
11
, Theorem 2.9.1]
). We suggest
[
11
, Section 2.9]
for an in-depth study of
((P(t))_{t\geq 0}
.

2.2
The
H
-gradient and the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\mu)

In this subsection we define the Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\mu)
. For an extensive treatment of these spaces we refer to
[
11
, Chapter 5]
.
We consider the operator
D_{H}
defined on
C^{1}(X)
by

\displaystyle D_{H}F(x):=i^{*}DF(x),\ F\in C^{1}(X),\ x\in X.

On the space
\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}^{1}(X)
the operator
D_{H}
acts as follows:

\displaystyle D_{H}f(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial\xi_{j}}(\langle x,x_{1}^{*}\rangle,\ldots,\langle x,x_{n}^{*}\rangle)i^{*}x^{*}_{j},\quad x\in X,

(2.6)

where
f(x)=\varphi(\langle x,x_{1}^{*}\rangle,\ldots,\langle x,x_{n}^{*}\rangle)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{n}^{*}\in X^{*}
. We say that a function
f
is
H
-differentiable at
x\in X
if there exists
k\in H
such that

\displaystyle f(x+h)=f(x)+[k,h]_{H}+o(|h|_{H}),\quad|h|_{H}\rightarrow 0.

(2.7)

In this case we set
D_{H}f(x):=k
. It is not hard to see that if
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
then the definitions of
D_{H}
given in (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) coincide.

We define the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(X,\mu)
as the domain of the closure of the operator
D_{H}:\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu;H)
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
, for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
, and we denote by
D_{H}^{\mu}
its closure. These spaces are Banach spaces if endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{1,p}(X,\mu)}:=\left(\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)}^{p}+\|D^{\mu}_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu;H)}^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad f\in W^{1,p}(X,\mu),

and the space
W^{1,2}(X,\mu)
is a Hilbert space if endowed with the scalar product

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{W^{1,2}(X,\mu)}:=\langle f,g\rangle_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}+\langle D^{\mu}_{H}f,D_{H}^{\mu}g\rangle_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu;H)},\quad f,g\in W^{1,2}(X,\mu).

For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we define the operator
D_{H}^{k}
which acts on elements of
\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}^{k}(X)
as follows:

\displaystyle D_{H}^{k}f(x)=\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{k}=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{k}\varphi}{\partial\xi_{j_{1}}\cdots\partial\xi_{j_{k}}}(\langle x,x_{1}^{*}\rangle,\ldots,\langle x,x_{n}^{*}\rangle)i^{*}x^{*}_{j_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes i^{*}x^{*}_{j_{k}},\quad x\in X,

(2.8)

where
f(x)=\varphi(\langle x,x_{1}^{*}\rangle,\ldots,\langle x,x_{n}^{*}\rangle)
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\varphi\in C^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
x_{1}^{*},\ldots,x_{n}^{*}\in X^{*}
.
The Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\mu)
are the domain of the closure of the operator
(D_{H},\ldots,D_{H}^{k}):\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}\rightarrow\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu;H)\times\cdots\times\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H))
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
, for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
, and we denote by
D_{H}^{\mu,k}
its closure. These spaces are Banach spaces if endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{k,p}(X,\mu)}:=\left(\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)}^{p}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|D^{\mu,j}_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu;\mathcal{H}_{j}(H))}^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad f\in W^{k,p}(X,\mu),

and
W^{k,2}(X,\mu)
is a Hilbert space if endowed with the scalar product

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{W^{k,2}(X,\mu)}:=\langle f,g\rangle_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\langle D^{\mu,j}_{H}f,D_{H}^{\mu,j}g\rangle_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu;\mathcal{H}_{j}(H))},\quad f,g\in W^{k,2}(X,\mu).

2.3
Wiener chaos decomposition

In this subsection we briefly present the Wiener chaos decomposition. For a systematic study of the Wiener chaos decomposition in the setting of Malliavin calculus we refer to
[
25
]
, while for the main results of the Wiener chaos decomposition in our setting we refer to
[
11
, Section 2.9]
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
H_{n}
be the
n
-th Hermite polynomial, which is defined by

\displaystyle H_{n}(\xi):=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}e^{\xi^{2}/2}\frac{d^{n}}{d\xi^{n}}\left(e^{-\xi^{2}/2}\right),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

We notice that
H_{1}(\xi)=\xi
and
H_{2}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2}(\xi^{2}-1)
. We denote by
\Lambda
the set of multiindices
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\ldots)
such that all the terms, except a finite number of them, vanish. For every
\alpha\in\Lambda
we set

\displaystyle\alpha!:=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i},\quad|\alpha|:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{i}.

For every multiindex
\alpha
we define the generalized Hermite polynomial
H_{\alpha}
by

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}(\xi):=\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}H_{\alpha_{i}}(\xi_{i}),\quad\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\ n=|\alpha|.

Let us fix an orthonormal basis
\{h_{n}=i^{*}(x^{*}_{n}):\ x^{*}_{n}\in X^{*},\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
H
(this basis exists since
j(X^{*})
is dense in
X^{*}_{\mu}
), and for every
\alpha\in\Lambda
let us define

\displaystyle\Phi_{\alpha}(x):=\sqrt{\alpha!}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}H_{\alpha_{i}}(\langle x,x^{*}_{i}\rangle),\quad x\in X.

It is possible to prove (see
[
25
, Proposition 1.1.1]
) that the set
\{\Phi_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Lambda\}
is a complete orthonormal system in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
. Further, if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
we the set

\displaystyle E_{n}:={\rm span}\{\Phi_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Lambda,\ |\alpha|=n\},

then
E_{0}=\mathbb{R}
,
E_{1}=X_{\mu}^{*}
,
E_{n}
and
E_{m}
are orthogonal subspaces of
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
when
n\neq m
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}E_{n}.

Let us denote by
I_{n}
the projection on
E_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Then, for every
f\in\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
we have

\displaystyle f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}I_{n}f,\quad I_{n}f=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda,|\alpha|=n}\left(\int_{X}f\Phi_{\alpha}d\mu\right)\Phi_{\alpha}.

Remark 2.3
.

The properties of
\Phi_{\alpha}
and
E_{n}
are independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
\{h_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
H
, i.e., if
\{k_{n}:k_{n}=i^{*}(y_{n}^{*}),\ y^{*}_{n}\in X^{*},\ n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is another orthonormal basis of
H
and we set

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}_{\alpha}(x):=\sqrt{\alpha!}\prod_{i=1}^{\infty}H_{\alpha_{i}}(\langle x,y^{*}_{i}\rangle),\quad x\in X,\ \alpha\in\Lambda,\quad E^{\prime}_{n}:={\rm span}\{\Phi^{\prime}_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Lambda,\ |\alpha|=n\},

then
E^{\prime}_{n}
and
E^{\prime}_{m}
are orthogonal subspaces of
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
when
n\neq m
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}E^{\prime}_{n},

and for every
f\in\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
we have

\displaystyle f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}I^{\prime}_{n}f,\quad I^{\prime}_{n}f=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda,|\alpha|=n}\left(\int_{X}f\Phi^{\prime}_{\alpha}d\mu\right)\Phi^{\prime}_{\alpha}.

Remark 2.4
.

For every multiindex
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=1
it follows that
\Phi_{\alpha}(x)=\widehat{h_{n}}(x)
for every
x\in X
, where
\alpha_{n}=1
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is the unique component of
\alpha
different from
0
. Further, for every multiindex
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=2
, we have
\Phi_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}((\widehat{h_{n}}(x))^{2}-1)
for every
x\in X
, if
\alpha_{n}=2
, with
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is the unique component of
\alpha
different from
0
, while
\Phi_{\alpha}(x)=\widehat{h_{n}}(x)\widehat{h_{m}}(x)
for every
x\in X
, if
\alpha_{n}=\alpha_{m}=1
, with
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\neq m
, are the unique components of
\alpha
which do not vanish.

The Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
(P(t))_{t\geq 0}
behaves good on
E_{n}
: indeed, (see
[
11
, Theorem 2.9.2]
) for every
t\geq 0
and every
f\in\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
we have

\displaystyle P(t)f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-nt}I_{n}f.

In particular, for every
f\in E_{n}
it follows that
T(t)f=e^{-nt}f
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and every
t\geq 0
.

We prove the following useful lemma which is a consequence of Nelson’s hypercontractivity theorem for the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck semigroup
(P(t))_{t\geq 0}
(see
[
11
, Theorem 5.5.3]
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
. The projection
I_{k}
is a bounded linear operator from
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
onto
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
, and

\displaystyle\|I_{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k}}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)},\quad f\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu),\quad p\in(1,2),

\displaystyle\|I_{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}\leq

\displaystyle(p-1)^{k/2}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)},\quad f\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu),\quad p\in[2,+\infty),

Proof.

The case
p=2
follows from the fact that
I_{k}
is a projection on
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
. If
p\in(2,+\infty)
the statement is easy to prove. Indeed, let
p\in(2,+\infty)
and let
f\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
. Then,
f\in\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
and from
[
30
, Chapter IV, Theorem 1]
we have

\displaystyle\|I_{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)}\leq(p-1)^{k/2}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)}.

Further,

\displaystyle\|I_{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}\leq\|I_{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)},

and combining the above inequalities we have the thesis.

Let
p\in(1,2)
, let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
f\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
. We consider a sequence
(f_{n})\subseteq C_{b}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
which converges to
f
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. We recall that for every element
g\in E_{k}
we have
P(t)g=e^{-kt}g
. Then,

\displaystyle\|I_{k}f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}=e^{kt}\|P(t)I_{k}f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Further, the hypercontractivity property of
(P(t))_{t\geq 0}
(see
[
11
, Theorem 5.5.3]
) gives

\displaystyle\|P(t)I_{k}f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}\leq\|I_{k}f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X;\mu)},\quad n\in\mathbb{N},

with
t=-\ln(p-1)^{1/2}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\|I_{k}f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}\leq e^{kt}\|I_{k}f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X;\mu)}=\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k/2}}\|I_{k}f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X;\mu)},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

[
30
, Chapter IV, Theorem 1]
implies that

\displaystyle\|I_{k}f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)}\leq\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k/2}}\|f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)}.

Therefore,
(I_{k}f_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
. If we denote by
I_{k}f
the limit of
(I_{k}f_{n})
in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
, it follows that
I_{k}f
is well defined, it belongs to
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
and

\displaystyle\|I_{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}\leq\frac{1}{(p-1)^{k}}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)}.

∎

2.4
The Sobolev spaces
W^{k,p}(X,\nu)
and the
H
-divergence operator

Let us provide the assumptions on the function
U
, which will be used to define the weighted Gaussian measure
\nu
.

Hypothesis 2.6
.

U
is a
H
-convex
|\cdot|_{X}
-lower semicontinuous function which belongs to
W^{1,p}(X,\mu)
for every
p\in(1,+\infty)
.

Remark 2.7
.

1.

From
[
21
, Theorem 4.3]
it follows that
U
admits a modification
U^{\prime}
which is a Borel measurable convex function on
X
. Since
U=U^{\prime}
for
\mu
-a.e., in the following we simply denote by
U
this modification, and therefore we can assume that
U
is a convex function.

2.

It is possible to weaken the assumptions on
U
requiring that it belongs to
W^{1,p}(X,\mu)
for some
p\in(1,+\infty)
. This implies that the results of this paper hold for exponents
q
smaller than a fixed exponent
\overline{p}\in(1,+\infty)
which depends on
p
.

Hereafter, we always assume Hypothesis
2.6
. The convexity of
U
implies that
U
is bounded from below by a linear functional. From Fernique’s theorem (see
[
11
, Theorem 2.8.5]
) we infer that the function
e^{-U}
belongs to
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
. We introduce the probability measure

\displaystyle\nu:=Ke^{-U}\mu,\quad K=\|e^{-U}\|^{-1}_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(X,\mu)},

(2.9)

where
K
is the normalizing factor. Hypothesis
2.6
implies that
|D^{\mu}_{H}U|_{H}\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Indeed, for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}|D_{H}^{\mu}U|^{p}_{H}d\nu=

\displaystyle\int_{X}|D_{H}^{\mu}U|^{p}_{H}e^{-U}d\mu\leq\|D_{H}^{\mu}U\|_{\operatorname{L}^{pq}(X,\mu)}\|e^{-U}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{q^{\prime}}(X,\mu)}<+\infty,

for every
q\in(1,+\infty)
. Analogously, we can prove that
\widehat{h}\in\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\nu)
for every
q\in[1,+\infty)
.

We define the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(X,\nu)
as in
[
20
]
. The integration by parts formula (see
[
20
, Lemma 3.1]
)

\displaystyle\int_{X}[D_{H}f,h]_{H}d\nu=\int_{X}f(\widehat{h}+[D_{H}^{\mu}U,h]_{H})d\nu,

(2.10)

implies that the operator
D_{H}:\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)
is closable for
p\in[1,+\infty)
.

Proposition 2.8
.

The operator
D_{H}:\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)\rightarrow\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)
is closable in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
. We denote by
D_{H}
its closure and by
W^{1,p}(X,\nu)
the domain of its closure.
W^{1,p}(X,\nu)
is a Banach space if endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{1,p}:=\left(\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{p}+\|D_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)}^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad f\in W^{1,p}(X,\nu),

W^{1,2}(X,\nu)
is a Hilbert space if endowed with inner product

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{W^{1,2}(X,\nu)}=\langle f,g\rangle_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)}+\langle D_{H}f,D_{H}g\rangle_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu;H)},\quad f,g\in W^{1,2}(X,\nu).

Remark 2.9
.

We have introduced the notation
D_{H}^{\mu}
to underline the difference between the closure of
D_{H}
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)
and in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
; the importance of this different notation will arise in the proof of Proposition
2.12
, where we use a property of
D_{H}^{\mu}
. This means that the notation
D_{H}f
represents both the action of the closure of
D_{H}
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
for
f\in W^{1,p}(X,\nu)
, and the action of the operator
D_{H}
defined in (
2.6
) on smooth functions
f\in C^{1}(X)
.

Proof.

If
p>1
the statement has been already proved in
[
20
, Proposition 3.2]
. Let us consider
p=1
and let
\{h_{m}:m\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an orthonormal basis of
H
. In this case we cannot directly use the method applied in the proof of
[
20
, Proposition 3.2]
, since
\widehat{h}_{n}-[D_{H}^{\mu}U,h_{n}]_{H}
does not belong to
\operatorname{L}^{\infty}(X,\nu)
. We introduce the function
\theta\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\theta(0)=0
and
\theta^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
. Let
(f_{n})\subseteq\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
be such that
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
\operatorname{L}^{1}(X,\nu)
and
D_{H}f_{n}\rightarrow G
in
\operatorname{L}^{1}(X,\nu;H)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Then, for every
\varphi\in C_{b}^{1}(X)
and every
m\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}[D_{H}(\theta\circ f_{n}),h_{m}]_{H}\varphi d\nu=\int_{X}(\theta^{\prime}\circ f_{n})[D_{H}f_{n},h_{m}]_{H}\varphi d\nu.

(2.11)

Letting
n\rightarrow+\infty
, the right-hand side converges to
\int_{X}\theta^{\prime}(0)[G,h_{m}]_{H}\varphi d\nu
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}(\theta^{\prime}\circ f_{n})[D_{H}f_{n},h_{m}]_{H}\varphi d\nu-\int_{X}\theta^{\prime}(0)[G,h_{m}]_{H}\varphi d\nu\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}(\theta^{\prime}\circ f_{n})[D_{H}f_{n},h_{m}]_{H}\varphi d\nu-\int_{X}(\theta^{\prime}\circ f_{n})[G,h_{m}]_{H}\varphi d\nu\right|

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{X}(\theta^{\prime}\circ f_{n})[G,h_{m}]_{H}\varphi d\nu-\int_{X}\theta^{\prime}(0)[G,h_{m}]_{H}\varphi d\nu\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}|(\theta^{\prime}\circ f_{n})\varphi||[D_{H}f_{n},h_{m}]_{H}-[G,h_{m}]_{H}|d\nu+\int_{X}|(\theta^{\prime}\circ f_{n})-\theta^{\prime}(0)||[G,h_{m}]_{H}\varphi|d\nu.

Since
\theta^{\prime}
and
\varphi
are bounded, the first integral vanishes as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Further, there exists a subsequence
(f_{k_{n}})\subseteq(f_{n})
such that
f_{k_{n}}(x)\rightarrow 0
for
\nu
-a.e.
x\in X
. Moreover,

\displaystyle|(\theta^{\prime}\circ f_{n})-\theta^{\prime}(0)||[G,h_{m}]_{H}\varphi|\leq 2\|\theta^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\|\varphi\|_{\infty}|G|_{H}.

By the dominated convergence theorem we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{X}|(\theta^{\prime}\circ f_{k_{n}})-\theta^{\prime}(0)||[G,h_{m}]_{H}\varphi|d\nu\rightarrow 0,\quad n\rightarrow+\infty.

(2.12)

In particular, we have prove that every subsequence
(f_{k_{n}})\subseteq(f_{n})
admits a subsequence
(f_{k_{n_{s}}})\subseteq(f_{k_{n}})
such that (
2.12
), with
(f_{k_{n}})
replaced by
(f_{k_{n_{s}}})
holds true. Hence,

From (

3.1
Asymptotic behaviour and pointwise gradient estimates to
(T(t))_{\geq 0}

In this subsection we show that for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is ergodic in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
, i.e., for every

we have

in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, where

\displaystyle\nu(f):=\int_{X}fd\nu,\quad f\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu).

(3.1)

To prove this fact we use the following intermediate result, whose proof is inspired by those in
[
15
, Corollary 3.6 & Proposition 3.7]
in finite dimension.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
f\in\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
. Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|D_{H}T(t)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu;H)}^{2}=0.

(3.2)

Proof.

We claim that for every
f\in D(L_{2})
, the function
t\mapsto\chi_{f}(t):=\|D_{H}T(t)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu;H)}^{2}\in L^{1}(0,+\infty)
. To prove the claim, let us fix
f\in D(L_{2})
and
t>0
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{X}|T(t)f|^{2}d\nu=

\displaystyle 2\int_{X}(T(t)f)(L_{2}T(t)f)d\nu=-2\int_{X}|D_{H}T(t)f|^{2}_{H}d\nu.

Integrating between
0
and
t
we get

\displaystyle\|T(t)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)}^{2}-\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)}^{2}=-2\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{H}T(s)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu;H)}^{2}ds,\quad t\geq 0.

This implies that

\displaystyle\|T(t)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|D_{H}T(s)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu;H)}^{2}ds\leq\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)}^{2},\quad t\geq 0,

and the claim is so proved. Let us consider
f\in D(L_{2}^{2})
, where
L_{2}^{2}
is the square power of the operator
L_{2}
. This means that both
\chi_{f}
and
\chi_{Lf}
belong to
\operatorname{L}^{1}(0,+\infty)
. Since

\displaystyle\left|\frac{d}{dt}\chi_{f}(t)\right|=2\left|\int_{X}[D_{H}T(t)L_{2}f,D_{H}T(t)f]_{H}d\nu\right|\leq\chi_{f}(t)+\chi_{Lf}(t),

it follows that both
\chi_{f}
and
\chi_{f}^{\prime}
belong to
\operatorname{L}^{1}(0,+\infty)
. This implies that
\chi_{f}\in W^{1,1}(0,+\infty)
, and therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\chi_{f}(t)=0,\quad f\in D(L_{2}^{2}).

Since
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is an analytic semigroup in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
, it follows that
T(1)f\in D(L_{2}^{n})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|D_{H}T(t)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu;H)}^{2}=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|D_{H}T(t-1)T(1)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu;H)}^{2}=0,\quad f\in\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu).

∎

Proposition 3.2
.

For every
p\in[1,+\infty)
and every

(3.3)

where

Proof.

Let us split the proof into three steps.

STEP
1
. Let

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}T(t_{n})fvd\nu\right|\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{X}vd\nu.

Letting

we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}gvd\nu\right|\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\int_{X}vd\nu.

The arbitrariness of
v
implies that
-\|f\|_{\infty}\leq g\leq\|f\|_{\infty}
for
\nu
-a.e. in
X
.

Let us consider
u\in D(D_{H}^{*})
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{X}gD_{H}^{*}ud\nu=

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}T(t_{n})fD_{H}^{*}ud\nu=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}[D_{H}T(t_{n})f,u]_{H}d\nu=0,

where the last equality follows from Lemma
3.1
. Therefore,
g\in D((D_{H}^{*})^{*})=W^{1,2}(X,\nu)
and
D_{H}g=0
. From Proposition
2.12
it follows that
g
is constant
\nu
-a.e. in
X
. Finally, we have

\displaystyle g=

\displaystyle\int_{X}gd\nu=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}T(t_{n})fd\nu=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}fd\nu=\nu(f),

where the third equality follows from the fact that
\nu
is an invariant measure for
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
. In particular, the above arguments show that for every sequence
(t_{n})\subseteq(0,+\infty)
diverging to
+\infty
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
there exists a subsequence
(t_{k_{n}})\subseteq(t_{n})
such that

weakly in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Hence,
T(t)f\rightarrow\nu(f)
weakly in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

STEP
2
.
From Step
1
we know that
T(t)f\rightarrow\nu(f)
weakly in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
for every

. Then,

\displaystyle\|T(t)f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)}^{2}=\int_{X}(T(t)f)(T(t)f)d\nu=\int_{X}(T(2t)f)fd\nu\rightarrow\int_{X}\nu(f)fd\nu=\|\nu(f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)}^{2},

(3.4)

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Here, we have used the self-adjointness of
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
and the semigroup property of
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
. (
3.4
) implies that
T(t)f\rightarrow\nu(f)
in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
. Therefore, for every sequence
(t_{n})\subseteq(0,+\infty)
diverging to
+\infty
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
there exists a subsequence
(t_{k_{n}})\subseteq(t_{n})
such that
T(t_{k_{n}})f(x)\rightarrow\nu(f)
for
\nu
-a.e.
x\in X
. By the dominated convergence theorem it follows that
T(t_{k_{n}})f\rightarrow\nu(f)
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
as

, for every

. This means that
T(t)f\rightarrow\nu(f)
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
for every
p\in[1,+\infty)
.

STEP
3
. Let
f\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
and let
(f_{n})\subseteq C_{b}(X)
be such that
f_{n}\rightarrow f
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. From Hölder’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\|\nu(f_{n})-\nu(f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}\leq\|f_{n}-f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|T(t)f-\nu(f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}\leq

\displaystyle\|T(t)f-T(t)f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}+\|T(t)f_{n}-\nu(f_{n})\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}

\displaystyle+\|\nu(f_{n})-\nu(f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|f-f_{n}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}+\|T(t)f_{n}-\nu(f_{n})\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)},

(3.5)

where we have used the fact that
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup of contractions in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
. Let us fix
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exists
\overline{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

. From Step

such that

for every

. This means that, by choosing

in (

), for every

there exists
\overline{t}>0
such that
\|T(t)f-\nu(f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}\leq\varepsilon
for every

. This gives the thesis.
∎

(3.6)

(3.7)

4
Vector-valued Poincaré inequality

4.1
Poincaré inequality

Thanks to Proposition
3.5
we are able to prove a vector-valued Poincaré inequality with explicit constant.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
V
be a separable Hilbert space and let
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Then, there exists a positive constant
k_{p}
defined by

\displaystyle k_{p}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{(p-1)},&p\in[2,+\infty),\\
\displaystyle\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}},&p\in[1,2),\end{cases}

(4.1)

such that for every
F\in W^{1,p}(X,\nu;V)
we have

\displaystyle\|F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}\leq k_{p}\|\overline{D_{H}}F\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H\otimes V)},

(4.2)

where
\nu(F)
has been defined in (
3.8
).

Remark 4.2
.

We notice that, for
p\geq 2
, the constant
k_{p}
equals the constant in
[
3
]
when
U=0
.

Proof.

We split the proof into two parts. In the former we prove the thesis for
p\in[2,+\infty)
, in the latter we consider the remaining cases
p\in[1,2)
. As usual, by density it is enough to prove the statement for
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
.

STEP
1
. Let
p\in[2,+\infty)
and let
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)

with

\displaystyle F:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}v_{i},\quad f_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X),\ v_{i}\in V,\ i=1,\ldots,m,

where
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{m}\}
are orthonormal vectors in
V
. Then,

\displaystyle T^{V}(t)F=\sum_{i=1}^{m}T(t)f_{i}v_{i},

belongs to
D(L^{V}_{p})
and
\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}T^{V}(t)F=L_{p}^{V}T^{V}(t)F
for every
t\geq 0
. We define
G:=F-\nu(F)\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
. From Lemma
2.18
we infer that
G^{*}:=|G|_{V}^{p-2}G\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}(X,\nu;V)
and

\displaystyle\overline{D_{H}}G^{*}=(p-2)|G|^{p-4}_{V}[\overline{D_{H}}G,G]_{V}\otimes G+|G|^{p-2}_{V}\overline{D_{H}}G,

(4.3)

where
[\overline{D_{H}}G,G]_{V}
is meant as an element of
H
. Formula (
3.8
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{X}|G|_{V}^{p}d\nu=

\displaystyle\int_{X}[G,G^{*}]_{V}d\nu=\int_{X}[F-\nu(F),G^{*}]_{V}d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}[T^{V}(0)F-T^{V}(t)F,G^{*}]_{V}d\nu.

(4.4)

Let us consider the argument of the limit. We get

\displaystyle\int_{X}[T^{V}(0)F-T^{V}(t)F,G^{*}]_{V}d\nu=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left[\frac{d}{ds}T^{V}(s)F,G^{*}\right]_{V}ds\right)d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{X}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left[L_{p}^{V}T^{V}(s)F,G^{*}\right]_{V}ds\right)d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{X}\left[L_{p}^{V}T^{V}(s)F,G^{*}\right]_{V}d\nu\right)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{X}\left[\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)F,\overline{D_{H}}G^{*}\right]_{H\otimes V}d\nu\right)ds,

(4.5)

where we have used the Fubini’s theorem and Lemma
2.27
(ii)
. From (
4.3
) we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}\left[\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)F,\overline{D_{H}}G^{*}\right]_{H\otimes V}d\nu\right|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)F|_{H\otimes V}|\overline{D_{H}}G^{*}|_{H\otimes V}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(p-1)\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)F|_{H\otimes V}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{H\otimes V}|G|_{V}^{p-2}d\nu,

(4.6)

since
\overline{D_{H}}G=\overline{D_{H}}F
. Let us consider
p>2
. From the generalized Hölder’s inequality for three functions with exponents
p,p
and
\frac{p}{p-2}
and (
3.10
) it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{X}

\displaystyle|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)F|_{H\otimes V}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{H\otimes V}|G|_{V}^{p-2}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)F|_{H\otimes V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{H\otimes V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{X}|G|_{V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{1-2/p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-s}\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{H\otimes V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{2/p}\left(\int_{X}|G|_{V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{1-2/p}.

(4.7)

Putting together (
4.4
)-(
4.7
) we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{X}|G|^{p}d\nu\leq

\displaystyle(p-1)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s}ds\right)\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{2/p}\left(\int_{X}|G|_{V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{1-2/p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p-1)\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{2/p}\left(\int_{X}|G|_{V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{1-2/p}.

Dividing both the sides by
\left(\int_{X}|G|_{V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{1-2/p}
we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|G|^{p}d\nu\right)^{2/p}\leq(p-1)\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{2/p},

which gives the thesis with
k_{p}=\left(p-1\right)^{1/2}
. If
p=2
, then
G^{*}=G
and
\overline{D_{H}}G^{*}=\overline{D_{H}}F
, and from (
3.10
) we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}\left[\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)F,\overline{D_{H}}G^{*}\right]_{H\otimes V}d\nu\right|=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}\left[\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)F,\overline{D_{H}}F\right]_{H\otimes V}d\nu\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)F|_{H\otimes V}^{2}d\nu\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{H\otimes V}^{2}d\nu\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-s}\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{H\otimes V}^{2}d\nu\right),

(4.8)

for every
s>0
. By collecting (
4.4
), (
4.5
) and (
4.8
) we get the thesis with
c_{2}=1=(2-1)^{1/2}
.

STEP
2
.
Let
p\in(1,2)
and let
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}=

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{array}[]{l}G\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V),\\
\|G\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu;V)}\leq 1\end{array}}\int_{X}[F-\nu(F),G]_{V}d\nu.

Let
G\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
with
\|G\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu;V)}\leq 1
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{X}[F-\nu(F),G]_{V}d\nu=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}[T^{V}(0)F-T^{V}(t)F,G]_{V}d\nu=\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{X}[F,T^{V}(0)G-T^{V}(t)G]_{V}d\nu,

(4.11)

where we have applied Lemma
2.27
(i)
and (
3.8
). Arguing as in Step
1
we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{X}[F,T^{V}(0)G-T^{V}(t)G]_{V}d\nu=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{X}[\overline{D_{H}}F,\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)G]_{H\otimes V}d\nu\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{H\otimes V}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)G|_{H\otimes V}d\nu\right)ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}F|_{H\otimes V}^{p}d\nu\right)^{1/p}\!\!\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)G|^{p^{\prime}}_{H\otimes V}d\nu\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\!\!ds,

(4.12)

with
p^{\prime}>2
. By applying (
3.14
) to
\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)G|^{p^{\prime}}_{H\otimes V}d\nu
and recalling that
\|G\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu;V)}\leq 1
we infer that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)G|^{p^{\prime}}_{H\otimes V}d\nu\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\max\{s^{-1/2},1\}}{\sqrt{2}}\min\{1,e^{-s+1}\}\|G\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu;V)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\max\{s^{-1/2},1\}}{\sqrt{2}}\min\{1,e^{-s+1}\},

(4.13)

which gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(s)G|^{p^{\prime}}_{H\otimes V}d\nu\right)^{1/p^{\prime}}\!\!ds\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\int_{0}^{1}s^{-1/2}ds+\int_{1}^{t}e^{-s+1}ds\right)=\frac{3-e^{-t+1}}{\sqrt{2}},

(4.14)

for every
t>1
. Collecting (
4.11
)-(
4.14
) we get

\displaystyle\int_{X}[F-\nu(F),G]_{V}d\nu\leq\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\|\overline{D_{H}}F\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H\otimes V)},

for every
G\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
with
\|G\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu;V)}\leq 1
. From (
4.1
) we get

\displaystyle\|F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}\leq\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}\|\overline{D_{H}}F\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H\otimes V)},

(4.15)

for every
p\in(1,2)
and every
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
. Since the constant
k_{p}=\frac{3}{\sqrt{2}}
does not depend on
p
, letting

we get (

) also for

.
∎

W^{k,p}(X,\nu)

The combination of Wiener chaos decomposition (see Subsection
2.3
) and of vector-valued Poincaré inequality (

) allows us to prove that
D_{H}^{k}:\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)\to\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H))
is closable in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{k,p}
is equivalent to the graph norm of
D_{H}^{k}
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
for every
p\in(1,+\infty)
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where we still denote by
D_{H}^{k}
the closure of
D_{H}^{k}
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
. We split this section into two parts: in the former we consider the case
k=2
, which holds true when
U
only satisfies Hypothesis
2.6
, in the latter we consider the cases
k\geq 3
, when we need additional assumptions on
U
. For every
p\in(1,+\infty)
and every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we introduce the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p,D_{H}^{k}}:=\left(\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{p}+\|D_{H}^{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H))}^{p}\right)^{1/p},\quad f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X).

5.1
The case
k=2

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Then, there exists two positive constants
K_{p}
and
\widetilde{K}_{p}
such that
K_{p}\|f\|_{p,D_{H}^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{2,p}\leq\widetilde{K}_{p}\|f\|_{p,D^{2}_{H}}
for every
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
.

Proof.

From the definition it follows that
\|f\|_{p,D_{H}^{2}}\leq 2^{1-1/p}\|f\|_{2,p}
for every
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
.

Let us prove the second inequality. We show that for every
p\in(1,+\infty)
there exist a positive constant
\widetilde{C}_{p}
, which only depends on
p
and
U
, such that for every
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
we have

\displaystyle\|D_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))}\leq k_{p}\|D_{H}^{2}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))}+\widetilde{C}_{p}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)},

(5.1)

and
k_{p}
is the constant in (
4.16
). Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
and let
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
. Then,

\displaystyle\|D_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)}\leq

\displaystyle\|D_{H}f-\nu(D_{H}f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)}+\|\nu(D_{H}f)\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k_{p}\|D^{2}_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))}+\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}[D_{H}f,e_{n}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where in the last inequality we have taken advantage from (
4.16
) and
\{e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is any orthonormal basis of
H
. Let us estimate the second addend above. Integrating by parts we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}[D_{H}f,e_{n}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}f(\widehat{e_{n}}+[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{n}]_{H})d\nu\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}e^{-U}f\widehat{e_{n}}d\mu\right|^{2}+2\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}f[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{n}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}.

(5.2)

From Remarks
2.3
and
2.4
it follows that
\{\widehat{e}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is an orthonormal basis of the first Wiener chaos
E_{1}
and that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}e^{-U}f\widehat{e_{n}}d\mu\right|^{2}=\|I_{1}(e^{-U}f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}^{2}.

Let
q=\frac{p+1}{2}\in(1,p)
. From Lemma
2.5
there exists a positive constant
a_{p}
, which only depends on
p
, such that

\displaystyle\|I_{1}(e^{-U}f)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}^{2}\leq a_{p}\|e^{-U}f\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\mu)}.

By applying the Hölder’s inequality with
r=\frac{p}{q}
and
r^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-q}
we get

\displaystyle\|e^{-U}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\mu)}^{2}=

\displaystyle\left\|\left(e^{-\frac{q}{p}U}|f|^{q}\right)e^{\left(-q+\frac{q}{p}\right)U}\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{1}(X,\mu)}^{2/q}\leq\|fe^{-\frac{1}{p}U}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)}^{2}\left(\int_{X}e^{-r^{\prime}(q-\frac{q}{p})U}d\mu\right)^{2/(qr^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle C_{p}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{2},

(5.3)

where
C_{p}
is a constant which only depends on
p
and
U
. Further,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}f[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{n}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}f\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{n}]_{H}e_{n}d\nu\right|_{H}^{2}=\left|\int_{X}fD_{H}^{\mu}Ud\nu\right|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}|f||D_{H}^{\mu}U|_{H}d\nu\right)^{2}\leq\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{2}\||D_{H}^{\mu}U|_{H}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)}^{2}.

(5.4)

From Remark
2.10
we infer that
\||D_{H}^{\mu}U|_{H}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)}^{2}<+\infty
.

Collecting together (
5.2
)-(
5.4
) we infer that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}[D_{H}f,e_{n}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\left(C_{p}+\||D_{H}^{\mu}U|_{H}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}_{p}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)},

which gives the thesis.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

For every
p\in(1,+\infty)
the operator
D_{H}^{2}:\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)\subseteq\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)\to\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))
is closable in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
and the domain of its closure, endowed with the graph norm, coincides with
W^{2,p}(X,\nu)
.

Proof.

Let us consider a sequence of functions
(f_{n})\subseteq\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
such that
f_{n}\to 0
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
and
D^{2}_{H}f_{n}\rightarrow\Phi
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. From (
5.1
) it follows that
(D_{H}f_{n})
is a Cauchy sequence in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;H)
and so
f_{n})
converges in
W^{2,p}(X,\nu)
. Since
f_{n}\rightarrow 0
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
it follows that
\Phi=0
, which gives the closability of
D_{H}^{2}
. From Theorem (
5.1
) the graph norm of
D^{2}_{H}
and the
W^{2,p}(X,\nu)
-norm are equivalent, and the thesis follows.
∎

The case

) to

for every

,

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 2
. Then, there exist a positive constant
\widetilde{C}_{p}
, which only depends on
p
and
U
, such that for every
f\in W^{k+1,p}(X,\nu)
we have

\displaystyle\|D_{H}^{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H))}\leq k_{p}\|D_{H}^{k+1}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(H))}+\widetilde{C}_{p}\|D_{H}^{k-1}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k-1}(H))},

(5.5)

where
k_{p}
is the constant in (
4.16
).

Proof.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 2
, and let
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
. From (
4.16
) we have

\displaystyle\|D_{H}^{k}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H))}\leq

\displaystyle\|D_{H}^{k}f-\nu(D_{H}f^{k})\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k}(H))}+\|\nu(D_{H}^{k}f)\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k}(H)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle k_{p}\|D^{k+1}_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k+1}(H))}

\displaystyle+\left(\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}D^{k}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}})d\nu\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(5.6)

where
\{e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is any orthonormal basis of
H
. We notice that

\displaystyle D^{k}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}})=[D^{k}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}}),e_{i_{k}}]_{H},\quad i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\mathbb{N},

where
D^{k}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})
is understood as an element of
H
.
By applying formula (
2.15
) to the last addend in (
5.2
) we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}D^{k}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}})d\nu\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}D^{k-1}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})(\widehat{e_{i_{k}}}+[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{i_{k}}]_{H})d\nu\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}D^{k-1}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})\widehat{e_{i_{k}}}d\nu\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}D^{k-1}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{i_{k}}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}.

(5.7)

By applying Lemma
2.5
for every
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}D^{k-1}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})\widehat{e_{i_{k}}}d\nu\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\|I_{1}(D^{k-1}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})e^{-U})\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a_{p}\|D^{k-1}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})e^{-U}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\mu)},

where
q=\min\left\{\frac{p+1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right\}
and
a_{p}
is a positive constant which only depends on
p
. Summing up
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}
over
\mathbb{N}
we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}D^{k-1}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})\widehat{e_{i_{k}}}d\nu\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle a_{p}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\|D^{k-1}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})e^{-U}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\mu)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{p}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\int_{X}|D_{H}^{k-1}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})e^{-U}|^{q}d\mu\right)^{2/q}.

Let us apply the Minkowski’s integral inequality with
\mu_{1}=\mu
,
\mu_{2}
being the product of
k-1
counting measures on
\mathbb{N}
and
p=2/q>1
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\left(\int_{X}|D_{H}^{k-1}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})e^{-U}|^{q}d\mu\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\Big(\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\int_{X}|D_{H}^{k-1}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})e^{-U}|^{q}d\mu\Big)^{2/q}\Big)^{q/2}\bigg)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{X}\Big(\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}}|D_{H}^{k-1}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})e^{-U}|^{2}\Big)^{q/2}d\mu\bigg)^{2/q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\|D_{H}^{k-1}f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k-1}(H)}e^{-U}\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\mu)}^{2}.

(5.8)

By repeating the computations in (
5.3
) it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\|D^{k-1}_{H}f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k-1}(H)}e^{-U}\right\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\mu)}\leq

\displaystyle C_{p}\|D^{k-1}_{H}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k-1}(H))}^{2},

(5.9)

for some positive constant
C_{p}
which only depends on
p
and
U
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}D^{k-1}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k-1}})[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{i_{k}}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}\|D^{k-1}_{H}f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{k-1}(H)}|D_{H}^{\mu}U|_{H}d\nu\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|D_{H}^{k-1}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k-1}(H))}^{2}\||D_{H}^{\mu}U|_{H}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)}^{2},

(5.10)

for every
p\in(1,+\infty)
, and from Remark
2.10
it follows that
\||D_{H}^{\mu}U|_{H}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)}<+\infty
. Formulae (
5.2
)-(
5.10
) imply that there exists a positive constant
\widetilde{C}_{p}
, which only depends on
p
and
U
, such that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}D^{k}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}})d\nu\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\widetilde{C}_{p}\|D_{H}^{k-1}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{k-1}(H))}.

By density we conclude.
∎

A generalization of Theorem
5.1

)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{X}D^{k}_{H}f(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{k}})d\nu\right|^{2},

. By applying this procedure the derivatives

Remark 5.4
.

for every

for every

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\geq 3
. If
U\in W^{k-1,q}(X,\mu)
for every
q\in[1,+\infty)
, then there exists positive constants
K_{p}
and
\widetilde{K}_{p,k}
such that

K_{p}\|f\|_{p,D_{H}^{k}}\leq\|f\|_{k,p}\leq\widetilde{K}_{p,k}\|f\|_{p,D^{k}_{H}}
for every
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
.

Proof.

From the definition we get
\|f\|_{p,D_{H}^{k}}\leq 2^{1-1/p}\|f\|_{k,p}
for every
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
.

We limit ourselves to prove second inequality of the statement when
k=3
, the other cases following by analogous computations.

Let
k=3
and let
p\in(1,+\infty)
. We notice that from Theorem
5.1
there exists a positive constant
\widetilde{K}_{p}
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2,p}\leq\widetilde{K}_{p}\left(\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}+\|D_{H}^{2}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))}\right),

for every
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
.
This gives

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{3,p}\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|^{p}_{2,p}+\|D_{H}^{3}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{3}(H))}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{p-1}(\widetilde{K}_{p})^{p}\left(\|f\|^{p}_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}+\|D_{H}^{2}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))}^{p}\right)+\|D_{H}^{3}f\|^{p}_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{3}(H))},

for every
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
.
Hence, it is enough to find a positive constant
c
, independent of
f
, such that

\displaystyle\|D_{H}^{2}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))}\leq c\left(\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}+\|D_{H}^{3}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{3}(H))}\right),

for every
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
.
Arguing as in (
5.2
) and taking into account (
4.16
), we get

\displaystyle\|D_{H}^{2}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{2}(H))}\leq k_{p}\|D_{H}^{3}f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;\mathcal{H}_{3}(H))}+\left(\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}[D^{2}_{H}fe_{i},e_{j}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(5.11)

for every
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X)
, where
\{e_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is any orthonormal basis of
H
. Integrating by parts twice in the second addend of the right-hand side of (
5.11
) it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{X}[D^{2}_{H}fe_{i},e_{j}]_{H}d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{X}[D_{H}f,e_{i}]_{H}(\widehat{e_{j}}+[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H})d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{X}f\big((\widehat{e_{i}}+[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{i}]_{H})(\widehat{e_{j}}+[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H})-\delta_{ij}-[D^{\mu,2}_{H}Ue_{i},e_{j}]_{H}\big)d\nu

\displaystyle=\int_{X}f(\widehat{e_{i}}\widehat{e_{j}}-\delta_{ij})d\nu-\int_{X}f[D^{\mu,2}_{H}Ue_{i},e_{j}]_{H}d\nu+\int_{X}f[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{i}]_{H}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}d\nu

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\int_{X}f(\widehat{e_{i}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}+\widehat{e_{j}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{i}]_{H})d\nu

\displaystyle=:J_{1}^{i,j}+J_{2}^{i,j}+J_{3}^{i,j}+J_{4}^{i,j},

(5.12)

for every
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker symbol and
[D_{H}^{\mu}\widehat{e_{i}},e_{j}]_{H}=\delta_{ij}
follows from
[
11
, Lemma 2.10.5]
. Let us separately estimate the four terms in the last line of (
5.12
). From Remarks
2.3
and
2.4
it follows that
J^{i,j}_{1}
is related to the second Wiener chaos
E_{2}
: indeed,

\displaystyle J^{i,j}_{1}=\int_{X}\Phi_{\alpha(i,j)}fe^{-U}d\mu,\quad i,j\in\mathbb{N},\ i\neq j,

where
\alpha(i,j)
is the multiindex which satisfies
|\alpha|=2
and
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}=1
, and

\displaystyle J^{i,i}_{1}=\sqrt{2}\int_{X}\Phi_{\alpha(i)}fe^{-U}d\mu,\quad i\in\mathbb{N},

where
\alpha(i)
is the multiindex which satisfies
|\alpha|=2
and
\alpha_{i}=2
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|J_{1}^{i,j}\right|^{2}\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}fe^{-U}(\widehat{e_{i}}\widehat{e_{j}}-\delta_{ij})d\mu\right|^{2}=2\|I_{2}(fe^{-U})\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)}^{2}\leq\!a_{p}\|fe^{-U}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\mu)}^{2}\leq\!C_{p}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{2},

(5.13)

where the last inequality can be obtained arguing as in (
5.3
) and
C_{p}
is a positive constant which only depends on
p
and
U
.

As far as
J_{2}^{i,j}
is considered, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|J_{2}^{i,j}\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}f[D^{\mu,2}_{H}Ue_{i},e_{j}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}=\left\|\int_{X}D^{\mu,2}_{H}Ufd\nu\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}(H)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{X}\|D^{\mu,2}_{H}U\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}(H)}|f|d\nu\right)^{2}\leq\left\|\|D^{\mu,2}_{H}U\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}(H)}\right\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)}\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{2},

(5.14)

where in the last inequality we have applied the Hölder’s inequality with exponents
p
and
p^{\prime}
. From Remark
5.4
we infer that
\left\|\|D^{\mu,2}_{H}U\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}(H)}\right\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)}<+\infty
.

J_{3}^{i,j}
can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|J_{3}^{i,j}\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}f[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{i}]_{H}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}\leq\left(\int_{X}|f||D^{\mu}_{H}U|_{H}^{2}d\nu\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{2}\||D^{\mu}_{H}U|_{H}^{2}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)}^{2},

(5.15)

and the last inequality follows from the Hölder’s inequality with exponents
p
and
p^{\prime}
.
Again, Remark
5.4
gives

\||D^{\mu}_{H}U|_{H}^{2}\|^{2}_{\operatorname{L}^{p^{\prime}}(X,\nu)}<+\infty
.

Finally, we take
J^{i,j}_{4}
into account. We get

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|J_{4}^{i,j}\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}f(\widehat{e_{i}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}+\widehat{e_{j}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{i}]_{H})d\nu\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}f\widehat{e_{i}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}+2\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}\widehat{e_{j}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{i}]_{H}d\nu\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}f\widehat{e_{i}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}e^{-U}d\mu\right|^{2}.

(5.16)

We recall that for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
the element

\displaystyle\int_{X}f\widehat{e_{i}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}e^{-U}d\mu,

is the projection of
f[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}e^{-U}
on the subspace of
E_{1}
generated by
\widehat{e_{i}}
, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, from Lemma
2.5
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\int_{X}f\widehat{e_{i}}[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}e^{-U}d\mu\right|^{2}=

\displaystyle\|I_{1}(f[D_{H}^{\mu}U,e_{j}]_{H}e^{-U})\|_{\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)}^{2}\leq a_{p}\|f[D^{\mu}_{H}U,e_{j}]_{H}e^{-U}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\mu)}^{2},

(5.17)

for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
, where
q=\min\left\{\frac{1+p}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right\}
and
a_{p}
is a positive constant which only depends on
p
. By applying the Minkowski integral inequality, as in (
5.8
), with indeces
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|f[D^{\mu}_{H}U,e_{j}]_{H}e^{-U}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\mu)}^{2}\leq\|f|D^{\mu}_{H}U|_{H}e^{-U}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{q}(X,\mu)}^{2}.

(5.18)

We apply the Hölder’s inequality with
r=\frac{p}{q}
and

Appendix A
Smooth approximations

This section is devoted to the approximation procedure which have been exploited to obtain gradient estimates for the semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
. The results are quite technical and follows the lines of the proofs of similar statements in
[
5
]
, but we deal with Banach spaces instead of Hilbert spaces and we weaken the assumptions on
U
. Hence, we provide the details for reader’s convenience.

We recall the definition of
H
-Lipschitz function: let
Y
be a separable Banach space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{Y}
, we say that

is a
H
-Lipschitz continuous function if there exists a positive constant

such that

\displaystyle\|F(x+h)-F(x)\|_{Y}\leq M|h|_{H},\quad\forall h\in H,\ \mu\textup{-a.e. }x\in X.

(A.1)

We denote by
[F]_{H-{\rm Lip}}
the smaller constant
M
which realizes (
A.1
).

Remark A.1
.

Hereafter, we fix an orthonormal basis
\Phi:\{e_{m}=i^{*}(e_{m}^{*}):m\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
H
(the existence of this basis follows from the density of
j(X^{*})
in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
and the isometry between
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\mu)
and

), and for every

we set

is dense in
\operatorname{L}^{P}(X,\nu)
for every

. Further, for every

the closure of the operator

For every

.

STEP
2
.
Let
f\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b,\Phi}^{\infty}(X)
and let
p\in(1,2]
. Let us multiply both the sides of (
B.18
) by a positive, bounded and continuous function
g
and let us integrate on
X
with respect to
\nu
. We get

\displaystyle\int_{X}|D_{H}T_{\varepsilon}(t)f|^{p}_{H}gd\nu\leq c_{p}t^{-p/2}\int_{X}T_{\varepsilon}(t)|f|^{p}gd\nu,\quad t>0.

Since
D_{H}T_{\varepsilon}(t)f\rightarrow D_{H}T(t)f
weakly in
\operatorname{L}^{2}(X,\nu)
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
(see Proposition
A.9
(ii)
), arguing as in Step
2
in the proof of Proposition
3.3
it is possible to prove that
g^{1/p}D_{H}T_{\varepsilon}(t)f
weakly converges to
g^{1/p}D_{H}T(t)f
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\int_{X}|D_{H}T(t)f|^{p}_{H}gd\nu\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{X}|D_{H}T_{\varepsilon}(t)f|^{p}_{H}gd\nu.

From Proposition
A.9
(ii)
we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{X}T_{\varepsilon}(t)|f|^{p}gd\nu\rightarrow\int_{X}T(t)|f|^{p}gd\nu,\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{X}|D_{H}T(t)f|^{p}_{H}gd\nu\leq c_{p}t^{-p/2}\int_{X}T(t)|f|^{p}gd\nu,\quad t>0.

The arbitrariness of
g
implies that

\displaystyle|D_{H}T(t)f(x)|^{p}_{H}\leq c_{p}t^{-p/2}T(t)|f|^{p}(x),\quad\nu\textup{-a.e.}\ x\in X.

(B.27)

If
p>2
, we apply (
B.18
) with
p=2
and (
2.27
) with
p=1
and
q=p/2
, and we get

\displaystyle|D_{H}T(t)f(x)|^{p}_{H}=\left(|D_{H}T(t)f(x)|^{2}_{H}\right)^{p/2}\leq\left(\frac{1}{2}t^{-1}T(t)|f|^{2}(x)\right)^{p/2}\!\!\!\!\leq c_{p}t^{-p/2}T(t)|f|^{p}(x),\ \nu\textup{-a.e. }x\in X,

with
c_{p}=2^{-p/2}
.

STEP
3
.
Let
f\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
and let
(g_{n})\subseteq\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b,\Phi}^{\infty}(X)
converge to
f
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Replacing
f
with
g_{n}-g_{m}
in (
B.27
) and integrating on
X
with respect to
\nu
we get

\displaystyle\int_{X}|D_{H}T(t)(g_{n}-g_{m})|^{p}_{H}d\nu\leq c_{p}t^{-p/2}\int_{X}T(t)|g_{n}-g_{m}|^{p}d\nu=c_{p}t^{-p/2}\int_{X}|g_{n}-g_{m}|^{p}d\nu,

(B.28)

where in the last part we have used the fact that
\nu
is an invariant measure for
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
.
Conclusion follows by repeating the same computations as in Step
3
in the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.5

Proof.

\boldsymbol{(i)}

Let
p\in[1,+\infty)
. Let us prove the result for
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
. Let

\displaystyle T^{V}(t)F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}T(t)f_{i}v_{i},\quad F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}v_{i},\quad f_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X),\ v_{i}\in V,\ i=1,\ldots,n,

Without loss of generality we can assume that
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
are orthonormal vectors in
V
. We have

\displaystyle\nu(F)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\int_{X}f_{i}d\nu\right)v_{i}.

If
p>2
, by applying the Hölder inequality with
q=p/(p-2)
and
q^{\prime}=p/2
then

\displaystyle\|T^{V}(t)F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}^{p}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(\left|T^{V}(t)F-\int_{X}Fd\nu\right|^{2}_{V}\right)^{p/2}d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|T(t)f_{i}-\int_{X}f_{i}d\nu\right|^{2}\right)^{p/2}d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|T(t)f_{i}-\int_{X}f_{i}d\nu\right|^{2}\right)^{p/2-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|T(t)f_{j}-\int_{X}f_{j}d\nu\right|^{2}d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{X}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|T(t)f_{i}-\int_{X}f_{i}d\nu\right|^{2}\right)^{p/2-1}\left|T(t)f_{j}-\int_{X}f_{j}d\nu\right|^{2}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T^{V}(t)F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}^{p-2}\|T(t)f_{j}-\nu(f_{j})\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|T^{V}(t)F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}^{p-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T(t)f_{j}-\nu(f_{j})\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{2},

\displaystyle\|T^{V}(t)F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|T(t)f_{j}-\nu(f_{j})\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{2},

(B.29)

for every
t\geq 0
. If
p=2
estimate (
B.29
) immediately follows, while if
p\in[1,2)
then we get

\displaystyle\|T^{V}(t)F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}^{p}=

\displaystyle\int_{X}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big|T(t)f_{i}-\int_{X}f_{i}d\nu\Big|^{2}\Big)^{p/2}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{X}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|T(t)f_{i}-\nu(f_{i})|^{p}d\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|T(t)f_{i}-\nu(f_{i})\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)}^{p}.

(B.30)

From Proposition
3.2
, (
B.29
) and (
B.30
) we conclude that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\|T^{V}(t)F-\nu(F)\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}=0.

(B.31)

Let us consider a function
F\in\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu)
and let
(F_{m})\subseteq\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
be a sequence converging to
F
in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. From the properties of Bochner integrals (see
[
19
, Chapter II, Thereom 4(ii)]
) and Hölder’s inequality we infer that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{X}F_{m}d\nu-\int_{X}Fd\nu\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}\leq\|F-F_{m}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}.

Since
(T^{V}(t))_{t\geq 0}
is a semigroup of contractions in
\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)
it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|T^{V}(t)F-\int_{X}Fd\nu\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|T^{V}(t)F-T^{V}(t)F_{m}\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}

\displaystyle+\left\|T^{V}(t)F_{m}-\int_{X}F_{m}d\nu\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}

\displaystyle+\left\|\int_{X}F_{m}d\nu-\int_{X}Fd\nu\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\|F-F_{m}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}+\left\|T^{V}(t)F_{m}-\int_{X}F_{m}d\nu\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}.

(B.32)

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exists
\overline{m}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|F-F_{\overline{m}}\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}\leq\varepsilon/4
. Further, from (
B.31
) there exists
\overline{t}
such that for every
t>\overline{t}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|T^{V}(t)F_{\overline{m}}-\int_{X}F_{\overline{m}}d\nu\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}\leq\varepsilon/2.

Therefore, from (
B.32
) with
m
replaced by
\overline{m}
, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\overline{t}>0
such that for every
t>\overline{t}
we have

\displaystyle\left\|T^{V}(t)F-\int_{X}Fd\nu\right\|_{\operatorname{L}^{p}(X,\nu;V)}\leq\varepsilon.

This gives the thesis.

\boldsymbol{(ii)}
.
The further part follows by integrating (
3.9
) on
X
with respect to
\nu
and by recalling that
\nu
is an invariant measure for
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
. Hence, let us prove the first part. By density we can limit ourselves to prove the result for
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
.

Let
p\in[1,+\infty)
, let
t>0
and let
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
of the form

\displaystyle F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}v_{i},\quad f_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X),\ v_{i}\in V,\ i=1,\ldots,n.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
v_{i}
are orthonormal vectors in
V
. From (
3.6
) with
p=1
, for every
x\in X
we get

\displaystyle|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F(x)|_{H\otimes V}=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|D_{H}T(t)f_{i}(x)|_{H}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq e^{-t}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(T(t)|D_{H}f_{i}|_{H}(x))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Now we apply formula (
2.30
) with
\gamma
being the counting measure on
\mathbb{N}
and with
q=2
. We get

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(T(t)|D_{H}f_{i}|_{H}(x))^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq T(t)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|D_{H}f_{i}|^{2}_{H}\right)^{1/2}\!\!\!(x),\quad x\in X.

(B.33)

If
p=1
then we conclude. If
p\in(1,+\infty)
then from (
B.33
) it follows that

\displaystyle|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F(x)|_{H\otimes V}^{p}\leq

\displaystyle e^{-pt}\left(T(t)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|D_{H}f_{i}|^{2}_{H}\right)^{1/2}\!\!\!(x)\right)^{p}\leq e^{-pt}T(t)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|D_{H}f_{i}|^{2}_{H}\right)^{p/2}\!\!\!(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-pt}T(t)|\overline{D_{H}}F(x)|_{H\otimes V}^{p},

for every
x\in X
, where the last inequality follows from (
2.27
) with
p=1
and
q=p/2
.

\boldsymbol{(iii)}
. The further part follows by integrating (
3.11
) on
X
with respect to
\nu
and by recalling that
\nu
is an invariant measure for
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
. Hence, let us prove the first part. By density we can limit ourselves to prove the result for
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
. We split two cases: in the former we prove the statement for
p\in[2,+\infty)
, in the latter we consider the remaining values of
p
.

Let
p\geq 2
, let
t>0
and let
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
of the form

\displaystyle F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}v_{i},\quad f_{i}\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X),\ v_{i}\in V,\ i=1,\ldots,n.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
v_{i}
are orthonormal vectors in
V
. From (
3.7
) with
p=2
we get

\displaystyle|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F|_{H\otimes V}^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|D_{H}T(t)f_{i}|_{H}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}T(t)|f_{i}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2t}T(t)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f_{i}|^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2t}T(t)\left(|F|_{V}^{2}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F|_{H\otimes V}^{p}=

\displaystyle\left(|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F|_{H\otimes V}^{2}\right)^{p/2}\leq\frac{1}{(2t)^{p/2}}\left(T(t)\left(|F|_{V}^{2}\right)\right)^{p/2}\leq\frac{1}{(2t)^{p/2}}T(t)\left(|F|_{V}^{p}\right),

where the last inequality follows from (
2.27
) with
p=1
and
q=p/2
. This gives the thesis since for
p\in[2,+\infty)
we have
c_{p}=2^{-p/2}
.

Let
p\in(1,2)
, and let
t
and
F
as above. By applying (
3.7
) we have

\displaystyle|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F(x)|_{H\otimes V}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|D_{H}T(t)f_{i}(x)|_{H}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq c_{p}^{1/p}t^{-1/2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(T(t)|f_{i}|^{p}(x))^{2/p}\right)^{1/2},\quad x\in X.

Now we apply the Minkowski’s integral inequality (
2.30
) with
\gamma
being the counting measure on
\mathbb{N}
and with
q=2/p
. It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(T(t)|f_{i}(x)|^{p})^{2/p}\leq\left(T(t)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|f_{i}|^{2}(x)\right)^{p/2}\right)^{2/p}=(T(t)|F|_{V}^{p}(x))^{2/p},\quad x\in X.

Therefore, we conclude that

\displaystyle|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F(x)|_{H\otimes V}^{p}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{p}}{t^{p/2}}T(t)|F|_{V}^{p}(x),\quad x\in X.

\boldsymbol{(iv)}
. By density, it is enough to show that (
3.13
) holds for
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
. Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
, let
F\in\operatorname{\mathscr{F}C}_{b}^{\infty}(X;V)
and let
t\in(0,1]
. By applying Proposition
3.5
(iii)
we get

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F|_{H\otimes V}^{p}d\nu\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c_{p}}{t^{p/2}}\int_{X}|F|^{p}_{V}d\nu,\quad t\in(0,1].

(B.34)

If
t\geq 1
, then from the semigroup property of
T^{V}(t)
and by applying Proposition
3.5
(ii)
with
t-1
and (
3.12
) with
t=1
we have

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t)F|_{H\otimes V}^{p}d\nu=

\displaystyle\int_{X}|\overline{D_{H}}T^{V}(t-1)T^{V}(1)F|_{H\otimes V}^{p}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{-p(t-1)}\int_{X}|D_{H}T^{V}(1)F|^{p}_{H\otimes V}d\nu

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{p}e^{-p(t-1)}\int_{X}|F|^{p}_{V}d\nu,\quad t>1.

(B.35)

By combining (
B.34
) and (
B.35
) we get the thesis.

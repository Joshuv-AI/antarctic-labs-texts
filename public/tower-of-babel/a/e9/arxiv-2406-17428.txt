Space-time symmetry breaking on non-geodesic leaves and a new form of matter
João Magueijo

Abstract
We examine the permanent damage caused by the historical breakdown of full diffeomorphism invariance induced by a foliation. We focus on the case where the foliation is allowed to be non-geodesic after the interactions with the foliation switch off. Gravity and other forms of matter recover full diffeomorphism invariance only at the expense of introducing a new matter-like component, carrying the non-vanishing Hamiltonian (and momentum, as it turns out) left over from the violating past interactions. This matter form must be stress-free in the preferred frame; this is the only way a matter action can mimic the evolution of the leftover Hamiltonian (and momentum) driven by the Dirac hypersurface deformation algebra. Hence, if the preferred frame is non-geodesic, the equivalent matter component must have energy and a momentum current in this frame, but still no spatial stresses: an unusual form of “matter”. It is equivalent to a fluid with anisotropic stress in some regimes, reducing to dust in others, or even displaying completely new features in extreme situations. Its stress energy tensor is conserved. We provide two examples based on accelerated frames: Rindler space-time and the canonical Schwarzchild frame.

\displaystyle n_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}T_{m}^{\mu\nu}=0

\displaystyle\iff\dot{\cal H}_{m}=\partial_{i}(N^{i}{\cal H}_{m})+\partial_{i}({\cal H}_{m}^{i}N)+{\cal H}_{m}^{i}\partial_{i}N,

(48)

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\mu}\nabla_{\nu}T_{m}^{\mu\nu}=0

\displaystyle\iff\dot{\cal H}^{m}_{i}={\cal H}_{m}\,\partial_{i}N+\partial_{j}(N^{j}{\cal H}^{m}_{i})+{\cal H}^{m}_{j}\partial_{i}N^{j},

(49)

supplying an interesting physical interpretation of the Dirac algebra and the evolution of the constraints. They are the Hamiltonian expression of stress-energy conservation (and ultimately of the generalized Bianchi identities, as we will discuss in
CDMLIV
).

To prove this we will need the decomposition (
IV
) with (
25
), always bearing in mind that
n_{\mu}\Pi^{\mu}=0
,
h^{\mu}_{\alpha}\Pi^{\alpha}=\Pi^{\mu}
. In addition to (
25
), the components

\Pi_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}(N^{k}{\cal H}^{m}_{k},{\cal H}^{m}_{i})

(50)

will also be useful, as well as the contraction:

h_{\mu}^{\;\beta}T^{m}_{\beta\nu}=-\Pi_{\mu}n_{\nu}.

(51)

The other possible single contraction of the stress-energy tensor is:

P_{\mu}=T^{m}_{\mu\nu}n^{\nu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}(N{\cal H}_{m}+N^{k}{\cal H}^{m}_{k},{\cal H}^{m}_{i})

or

P^{\mu}=\frac{1}{{\sqrt{-g}}}(-{\cal H}_{m},N^{i}{\cal H}_{m}+N{\cal H}_{m}^{i})

(52)

so that
n^{\mu}P_{\mu}=\rho
and
h_{\mu}^{\;\nu}P_{\nu}=\Pi_{\mu}
.
We can think of
\rho
as the energy density (the scalarized version of the Hamiltonian),
\Pi^{\mu}
as the momentum density (a covariantized version of the Momentum constraint), and
P^{\mu}
as the energy-momentum 4-vector, conflating the previous two.

V.1
Proof of the first equivalence

We first prove equivalence (
48
).
We start by writing:

\displaystyle n_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}T_{m}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}(n_{\nu}T_{m}^{\mu\nu})-T_{m}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}P^{\mu}-T_{m}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu},

and since
K_{\mu\nu}=-(\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}+n_{\mu}a_{\nu})
,
h^{\mu}_{\alpha}h^{\nu}_{\beta}T^{m}_{\mu\nu}=0
and
h^{\mu}_{\alpha}a_{\mu}=a_{\alpha}
, we can write the last term as:

\displaystyle-T_{m}^{\mu\nu}\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}=T_{m}^{\mu\nu}(n_{\mu}a_{\nu}+K_{\mu\nu})=T_{m}^{\mu\nu}n_{\mu}a_{\nu}=\Pi^{\mu}a_{\mu}

resulting in the tensorial identity:

\displaystyle n_{\nu}\nabla_{\mu}T_{m}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\mu}P^{\mu}+\Pi^{\mu}a_{\mu}.

(53)

Introducing components (as in (
52
)) converts the first term in:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}P^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{\mu}({\sqrt{-g}}P^{\mu})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}(-\dot{\cal H}_{m}+\partial_{i}(N^{i}{\cal H}_{m})+\partial_{i}(h^{ij}{\cal H}^{m}_{j}N)),

and (
1
) and (
25
) convert the second in:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mu}a_{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}{\cal H}_{m}^{i}\partial_{i}N.

Substituting in (
53
) therefore proves the equivalence (
48
).

V.2
Proof of the second equivalence

We next prove equivalence (
49
). First we rewrite:

\displaystyle h_{\alpha\mu}\nabla_{\nu}T_{m}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\nu}(h_{\alpha\mu}T_{m}^{\mu\nu})-(\nabla_{\nu}h_{\alpha\mu})T_{m}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{\mu}(n^{\mu}\Pi_{\alpha})+\rho a_{\alpha}+\Pi^{\nu}K_{\nu\alpha}.

The projector is idempotent, so we will have achieved our task by proving that:

\displaystyle h_{\alpha}^{\beta}\nabla_{\mu}(n^{\mu}\Pi_{\beta})=\rho a_{\alpha}+\Pi^{\nu}K_{\nu\alpha}

(54)

becomes proportional to:

\displaystyle\dot{\cal H}^{m}_{i}-\partial_{j}(N^{j}{\cal H}^{m}_{i})={\cal H}_{m}\,\partial_{i}N+{\cal H}^{m}_{j}\partial_{i}N^{j}

(55)

term by term except for connection terms that cancel out.

In order to do this,
first we note that (
54
) is a transverse identity, so its full content can be found by setting
\alpha=i
. Then we write:

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}(n^{\mu}\Pi_{i})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}({\sqrt{-g}}n^{\mu}\Pi_{i})_{,\mu}-\Gamma^{\beta}_{\mu i}n^{\mu}\Pi_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}(\dot{\cal H}^{m}_{i}-\partial_{j}(N^{j}{\cal H}^{m}_{i}))-\Gamma^{\beta}_{\mu i}n^{\mu}\Pi_{\beta},

(56)

and

\displaystyle\rho a_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}{\cal H}_{m}\partial_{i}N.

(57)

The last term in (
54
) can be manipulated as:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\nu}K_{\nu i}

\displaystyle=-\Pi^{\nu}h^{\alpha}_{\nu}\nabla_{i}n_{\alpha}=-\Pi^{\alpha}\nabla_{i}n_{\alpha}=-\Pi_{\nu}\nabla_{i}n^{\nu}

since
K_{\mu\nu}
is symmetric and can be obtained from a single projector applied to
\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}
, instead of the two that appear in its definition. This can then be expanded as:

\displaystyle-\Pi_{\nu}\nabla_{i}n^{\nu}

\displaystyle=-\Pi_{\nu}(\partial_{i}n^{\nu}+\Gamma_{i\mu}^{\nu}n^{\mu})

\displaystyle=-\frac{N^{k}{\cal H}^{m}_{k}}{\sqrt{h}}\partial_{i}\frac{1}{N}+\frac{{\cal H}^{m}_{k}}{\sqrt{h}}\partial_{i}\frac{N^{k}}{N}-\Gamma_{\mu i}^{\beta}n^{\mu}\Pi_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\cal H}^{m}_{k}}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_{i}N^{k}-\Gamma_{\mu i}^{\beta}n^{\mu}\Pi_{\beta}.

(58)

Collecting all the terms we find the promised cancellation of the cumbersome connection terms, leaving us with a term by term correspondence between (
54
) and (
55
) (up to a common factor).

VI
Examples

We close by providing examples of how the general results derived in this paper would be realized in concrete examples of non-geodesic foliations. We focus on the simple case where the effective matter acts as a test body, generalizing the exercise performed in
geoCDM
for geodesic frames.

VI.1
Rindler space-time

If we take for local preferred frame Minkowski space (the background to the linearizing exercise in Section
IV.2
) then the evolution is trivial:
\dot{\cal H}=\dot{\cal H}_{i}=0
, so that any non-vanishing initial conditions are simply preserved. But what if the preferred frame were accelerated with respect to this frame, or indeed the asymptotic cosmological frame?

As a model for such an eventuality we take the Rindler frame:

ds^{2}=-(\alpha x)^{2}dt^{2}+dx^{2}+dy^{2}+dz^{2}

(59)

(say, the
x>|t|
wedge),
so that
N=\alpha x
,
N^{i}=0
,
h_{ij}={\rm diag}(1,1,1)
. The evolution equations (
16
) and (
17
) then become:

\displaystyle\dot{\cal H}

\displaystyle=\alpha x\partial_{x}{\cal H}_{x}+2\alpha{\cal H}_{x}

(60)

\displaystyle\dot{\cal H}_{x}

\displaystyle=\alpha{\cal H}

(61)

\displaystyle\dot{\cal H}_{y}

\displaystyle=0

(62)

\displaystyle\dot{\cal H}_{z}

\displaystyle=0.

(63)

We assume
{\cal H}_{i}=0
at the initial time
t=0
when free evolution started; then only
{\cal H}_{x}
can become non-vanishing at
t>0
. We can derive a second order equation for
{\cal H}
:

\ddot{\cal H}=\alpha^{2}x\partial_{x}{\cal H}+2\alpha^{2}{\cal H}

(64)

showing that there is an instability. For example, an initially homogeneous
{\cal H}(t_{0},x)={\cal H}_{0}
evolves as:

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle={\cal H}_{0}\exp{\left(\sqrt{2}\alpha t\right)}.

(65)

We can map these results into the fluid analogy of Section
IV
. Eq. (
23
) leads to stress energy tensor:

\displaystyle T_{00}

\displaystyle=(\alpha x)^{2}{\cal H}

\displaystyle T_{0x}

\displaystyle=\alpha x{\cal H}_{x}.

(66)

It can be checked that this is conserved (as it should be; cf. Section
V
). Comparing with (
IV
) and (
25
) we find:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle={\cal H}={\cal H}_{0}\exp{\left(\sqrt{2}\alpha t\right)}

(67)

\displaystyle\Pi_{x}

\displaystyle={\cal H}_{x}=\frac{{\cal H}_{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\exp{\left(\sqrt{2}\alpha t\right)}-1\right).

(68)

Using (
45
), we see that such fluid would tend to a uniform velocity in the Rindler frame:

V_{x}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(1-\exp{\left(-\sqrt{2}\alpha t\right)}\right)\rightarrow-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}

(69)

that is
\gamma\rightarrow\sqrt{2}
. The analogy with an anisotropic fluid therefore works, with “rest” energy density

\rho_{d}=2\rho=2{\cal H}=2{\cal H}_{0}\exp{\left(\sqrt{2}\alpha t\right)}

(70)

velocity 4-vector:

u^{\mu}=\left(\frac{2}{\alpha x},1,0,0\right)

(71)

and anisotropic pressure along the
x
direction:

p_{x}=-2\rho=-\rho_{d}

(72)

with
p_{y}=p_{z}=0
.

VI.2
The Schwarzchild frame

As another example, we study the passive gravity of the effective fluid around the outside of the horizon of a black hole foliated with Schwarzchild leaves, the counterpart of the example given in
geoCDM
(with the geodesic foliation provided by the Lemaitre-Tolman-Bondi frame).
Then:

\displaystyle N^{2}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2m}{r}

\displaystyle N^{i}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle h_{ij}

\displaystyle={\rm diag}(1/N^{2},r^{2},r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta)

(73)

so that (
9
) and (
10
) reduce to:

\displaystyle\dot{\cal H}(t,r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\partial_{r}(N^{4}{\cal H}_{r})

\displaystyle\dot{\cal H}_{r}(t,r)

\displaystyle={\cal H}\,\partial_{r}N

(74)

(where we have dropped the label
m
). These can be combined into:

\displaystyle\ddot{\cal H}=\frac{1}{N}\partial_{r}\left(N^{4}{\cal H}\partial_{r}N\right).

(75)

It can be checked that the associated stress-energy tensor:

\displaystyle T_{00}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{3}}{r^{2}\sin\theta}{\cal H}

\displaystyle T_{0r}

\displaystyle=\frac{N^{2}}{r^{2}\sin\theta}{\cal H}_{r}

(76)

is again conserved (as proved in general).

In the Newtonian limit this is just dust, so there are no novelties. The differences arise if the “velocity”
V_{r}
becomes comparable or larger than 1. The most extreme location for this to happen in near the horizon,
r\approx 2m
, and regime we now explore. Then, (
75
) reduces to:

\ddot{\cal H}\approx\frac{3}{16m^{2}}{\cal H}+\frac{N^{2}}{4m}\partial_{r}{\cal H}

(77)

if we allow any
\epsilon=r-2m
dependence in
\cal H
. For a Black hole formed around
t=0
with a uniform leftover Hamiltonian as initial condition, we have for a possible growing mode:

\displaystyle{\cal H}

\displaystyle={\cal H}_{0}\exp{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4m}t\right)}.

(78)

Thus the momentum constraint diverges at the horizon, since
\dot{\cal H}_{r}(t,r)={\cal H}\,\partial_{r}N\approx{\cal H}/(4mN)
, but this leads to

V_{r}=-\frac{{\cal H}_{r}}{\cal H}=-\frac{1}{N\sqrt{3}}\implies V^{2}=\frac{1}{3}

(79)

or
\gamma^{2}=3/2
. An anisotropic fluid analogy is therefore possible. We can read off:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle=\frac{N{\cal H}_{0}}{16m^{2}\sin\theta}\exp{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4m}t\right)}

(80)

\displaystyle\Pi_{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\frac{N{\cal H}_{0}}{16m^{2}\sin\theta}\exp{\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}}{4m}t\right)}

(81)

mapping into:

\displaystyle\rho_{d}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}\rho

(82)

\displaystyle u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=\left(-\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}N,-\frac{N}{\sqrt{2}}\right).

(83)

On each leave (with constant
t
) this solution shows a smooth transition to
\rho=0
at the boundary of the foliation
r\rightarrow 2m
. Any observer, however, would see all the leaves up to
t\rightarrow\infty
as
r=2m
is approached. The exponential in (
80
) would therefore win over the power law coming from the determinant, and a soft singularity would be experienced.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper we continued the work of
geoCDM
to investigate the effects of past 4D symmetry breaking followed by restoration, with the significant difference that the foliation breaking the symmetry is allowed to be non-geodesic. In such a case, the regained Dirac algebra implies that the local momentum constraint must be violated along with the Hamiltonian constraint, after the sources of symmetry violation are withdrawn (see Section
II
). It is possible to transfer these violations to an effective matter fluid, so that the theory recovers full symmetry, but this requires that the fluid must not have spatial stresses or pressure. Only then does the fluid evolution mimic the evolution predicted by the Dirac algebra, as discussed in Section
III
. This no-stress condition implies a dust fluid in the geodesic case studied in
geoCDM
(for which the momentum constraint is enforceable, so rendering
\Sigma_{t}
the dust rest frame, for which there are no stresses). The implications for a non-geodesic frame are more dramatic, and point towards a new type of (dark) matter.

As shown in Section
IV
, on a non-geodesic
\Sigma_{t}
the no-stress condition implies a type of effective matter which in one frame (the preferred
\Sigma_{t}
) would be observed as possessing energy density and Eulerian energy current (or momentum density) but no Eulerian stresses. This property is rather peculiar: energy and momentum, but no spatial stresses in one frame. It cannot be associated with dust as seen away from its rest frame, for which stresses always arise together with momentum density. Indeed it precludes any type of perfect fluid. It can be interpreted as a fluid with anisotropic stresses in some regimes, but the analogy breaks down in others. If the momentum is much larger than the Hamiltonian the “effective velocity” would be superluminal. This is an effective fluid and not a real matter component, so just as one can argue that negative energy is not a serious impediment
shinji
, faster than light speeds do not need to have signal communication implications. Alternatively one might simply view this extreme regime and a break down of the anisotropic fluid interpretation. In spite of these oddities, we can also define a “Newtonian” regime where this form of matter would be indistinguishable from dust. Indeed this is how our results reduce to those in
geoCDM
in some appropriate limit, but this is far from generic.

An important consistency check is conservation of the stress-energy tensor of this effective fluid. This is because it will be coupled to gravitational structures contained in
S_{0}
which do satisfy 4D diffeomorphism invariance, and so Bianchi-like identities, implying stress-energy conservation of matter. We showed that this is indeed the case in Section
V
. In fact, we showed that it could not be otherwise, since the evolution of the Hamiltonian and Momentum dictated by the Dirac algebra, Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
) (inherited by
matter as (
16
) and (
17
)) are nothing but the Hamiltonian expression of stress-energy conservation. That is the content of the remarkable Eqs. (
48
) and (
49
), the impact of which will only be fully appreciated in the final installment to the series of papers started in
geoCDM
, where violations of the Dirac algebra will be examined. As shown in
CDMLIV
, violations of energy conservation are then inevitable.

We finally included a couple of examples in Section
VI
, notably identifying
\Sigma_{t}
with the Schwarzchild frame. It is tempting to derive phenomenology from the associated accretion phenomena around black holes, but we leave a full treatment to future work.

Introduction

A topological dynamical system
is a pair
(X,T)
, where
X
is a compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
is a continuous map. For the rest of the paper we endow
X
with a fixed compatible metric

We study properties of the
mean orbital pseudo-metric

\bar{E}
on

(1)

\bar{E}(x,y)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\min_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}(x),T^{\sigma(k)}(y)),

\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}

Hence,

2.
Preliminaries

Let
X
be a compact metric space with a metric
d
.
Given a point
x\in X
and a non-empty subset
A
of
X
we define

d(x,A)=\inf\{d(x,y)\colon y\in A\}.

Let
\mathcal{M}(X)
be the set of all Borel probability measures on
X
endowed with the weak
∗
topology.
We equip
\mathcal{M}(X)
with the
Prokhorov metric
denoted by
\rho
, that is
for any
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
, we have

\rho(\mu,\nu)=\inf\{\varepsilon>0\colon\mu(B)\leq\nu(B^{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\text{ for every Borel set }B\subset X\}.

Here (and elsewhere)
B^{\varepsilon}
denotes the
\varepsilon
-hull of
B
, that is,

B^{\varepsilon}=\{x\in X\colon d(x,B)<\varepsilon\}.

Let
2^{X}
be the
hyperspace
of
X
, that is

2^{X}=\{A\subset X\colon A
is a non-empty closed subset of
X\}
.
The
Hausdorff metric
on
2^{X}
is defined as follows:
for every
A,B\in 2^{X}
we set

d_{H}(A,B)=\max\bigl\{\inf\{\varepsilon>0\colon B\subset A^{\varepsilon}\},\inf\{\varepsilon>0\colon A\subset B^{\varepsilon}\}\bigr\}.

Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous, not necessarily invertible map.
The pair
(X,T)
forms a topological dynamical system. We write
\orb(x,T)
for the
orbit
of
x\in X
with respect to
T
, that is,
\orb(x,T)=\{T^{n}(x):n\geq 0\}
.
The set of all probability Borel
T
-invariant measures is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
.
An invariant measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
is called
ergodic
if for every Borel subset
B
in
X
,
T^{-1}B=B
implies that
\mu(B)=0
or
1
. The set of all ergodic invariant measures is denoted by
\mathcal{M}_{T}^{e}(X)
.
It is well known that
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
is a non-empty, closed, convex subset of
\mathcal{M}(X)
and
\mathcal{M}_{T}^{e}(X)
is the set of extreme points of
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
.
We say that
(X,T)
is
uniquely ergodic
if
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
consists of only one measure.
For
x\in X
, let
\hat{\delta}(x)\in\mathcal{M}(X)
be the Dirac measure supported on
\{x\}
.
Given
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define an
empirical measure

m_{T}(x,n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\hat{\delta}(T^{i}x).

A measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
is a
distribution measure
for

x\in X
if
\mu

is a limit of some subsequence of
\{m_{T}(x,n)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. In this situation, we also say that
x

quasi-generates

\mu
(synonymously we say that
x
is a
quasi-generic point
for
\mu
). If a measure is a distribution measure, then it is necessarily invariant.
The set of all distribution measures for
x\in X
(all measures quasi-generated by
x
) is denoted by
\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
.
We say that
x\in X
is a
generic
point for
\mu\in M(X,T)
if
\mu
is the unique member of
\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
. Every ergodic invariant measure has a generic point, but generic points need not exist for non-ergodic invariant measures. By compactness, for every
x\in X
the set
\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
is a non-empty closed connected subset of
\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
. For a generic point
x\in X
the unique measure in
\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
is denoted by
\mu(x)
.
Let
2^{\mathcal{M}(X)}
be the hyperspace (the space of all non-empty closed subsets of
\mathcal{M}(X)
) and let
\rho_{H}
be the Hausdorff metric on
2^{\mathcal{M}(X)}
induced by the Prokhorov metric
\rho
. The assignment
x\mapsto\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
determines a map from
X
to
2^{\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)}\subset 2^{\mathcal{M}(X)}
.

3.
The mean orbital pseudo-metric and invariant measures

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system.
Fix
x,y\in X
.
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\sigma\in S_{n}
we define

\displaystyle\Delta_{\sigma}(x,y,\delta)=\bigl|\{0\leq j\leq n-1\colon d(T^{j}(x),T^{\sigma(j)}(y))>\delta\}\bigr|,

where
|\,\cdot\,|
stands for the cardinality of a set,
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}(x,y)=\inf\Bigl\{\varepsilon>0\colon\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\min_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\Delta_{\sigma}(x,y,\varepsilon)<\varepsilon\Bigr\}.

The next fact follows from standard techniques (for example, see
[
1
]
,
[
15
]
or
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
).
Here we provide a proof for completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

For a topological dynamical system
(X,T)
,
the pseudo-metrics
\bar{E}
and
\widetilde{E}

are uniformly equivalent on
X
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality
we assume that the diameter of
X
is
1
.
Fix
x,y\in X
.
For every
\delta>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}

and
\sigma\in S_{n}
, we have

(2)

\delta\cdot\Delta_{\sigma}(x,y,\delta)\leq\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}d(T^{j}(x),T^{\sigma{(j)}}(y))\leq\Delta_{\sigma}(x,y,\delta)+\delta\cdot(n-\Delta_{\sigma}(x,y,\delta)).

Put
\Delta_{n}(x,y,\delta)=\min_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\Delta_{\sigma}(x,y,\delta)
and

\bar{d}(\Delta(x,y,\delta))=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\Delta_{n}(x,y,\delta).

It follows from (
2
) that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

(3)

\delta\cdot\Delta_{n}(x,y,\delta)\leq\min_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}d(T^{j}(x),T^{\sigma{(j)}}(y))\leq\Delta_{n}(x,y,\delta)+\delta\cdot(n-\Delta_{n}(x,y,\delta)).

Dividing (
3
) by
n
and passing with
n
to infinity we obtain

(4)

\delta\cdot\bar{d}(\Delta(x,y,\delta))\leq\bar{E}(x,y)\leq(1-\delta)\bar{d}(\Delta(x,y,\delta))+\delta.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and
assume that
\delta>0
satisfies
\delta<\varepsilon^{2}
. If
\bar{E}(x,y)<\delta
, then using (
4
) we see that

\varepsilon\cdot\bar{d}(\Delta(x,y,\varepsilon))\leq\bar{E}(x,y)<\varepsilon^{2}.

Then
\bar{d}(\Delta(x,y,\varepsilon))<\varepsilon
, and by the definition one has

\widetilde{E}(x,y)<\varepsilon
.
This implies that the identity map
\text{id}\colon(X,\bar{E})\to(X,\widetilde{E})
is uniformly continuous.

It remains to show that
\text{id}\colon(X,\widetilde{E})\to(X,\bar{E})

is uniformly continuous.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
and take
\delta>0
such that
\delta<\varepsilon/2
. Assume
\widetilde{E}(x,y)<\delta
,
that is
\bar{d}(\Delta(x,y,\delta))<\delta
.
We want to show that
\bar{E}(x,y)<\varepsilon
.
By
(
4
)
and
\delta<\varepsilon/2
we see that
\bar{E}(x,y)<\varepsilon
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

By Lemma
3.1
it is easy to see that the pseudo-metric
\bar{E}
does not depend on the metric
d
on
X
,
that is if
d^{\prime}
is another compatible metric on
X
, then
the pseudo-metric
\bar{E}
and
\bar{E}^{\prime}

induced by
d
and
d^{\prime}
are uniformly equivalent. This result can be also deduced from Lemma
3.3
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define

\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)=\min_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}d(T^{k}x,T^{\sigma(k)}y).

It is easy to see that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the function
\bar{E}_{n}
is a continuous metric on
X
.
With this notation, we can rewrite (
1
) as

(5)

\bar{E}(x,y)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\bar{E}_{n}(x,y).

Now, a crucial observation is that
\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)
equals the Wasserstein
2
2

2

The Wasserstein distance is also known as Kantorovich-Rubinstein distance, which probably is a more historically accurate name, see
[
22
]
. Note also that Leonid Nisonovich Vaserstein after whom the Wasserstein distance is named prefers different spelling of his surname.
We have resisted the temptation to rename things to set the record straight and for the sake of consistency with the existing literature we have kept the traditional name of
W_{1}
and its spelling.
distance
W_{1}
between the probability measures
m_{T}(x,n)
and
m_{T}(y,n)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Recall that a
coupling
of
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X)
is a measure

\pi\in\mathcal{M}(X\times X)
such that

\pi(A\times X)=\mu(A)
and
\pi(X\times B)=\nu(B)
for all Borel sets
A,B\subseteq X
. Write
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
for the convex set of all couplings of
\mu
and
\nu
. The Wasserstein distance
W_{1}
between
\mu,\nu
is the cost of optimal transportation (cf. Chapter 21 in
[
11
]
) between
\mu
and
\nu
for the cost function provided by the metric
d
, that is,

W_{1}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)\,\textrm{d}\pi(x,y).

For a compact metric space
X
the Wasserstein distance
W_{1}
is compatible with the weak
∗
topology on
\mathcal{M}(X)
(to see this one should combine Thm. 18.6.1, Thm. 18.5.5, Cor. 18.5.6, and Cor. 21.2.4 in
[
11
]
). Now, the formula

(6)

\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)=W_{1}(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))

can be seen as an easy consequence of the fact that if
\mu
and
\nu
are two discrete probability measures and each of them is uniformly distributed over a set of
n
points in
X
, then the couplings of
\mu
and
\nu
are in a one-to-one correspondence with bistochastic
n\times n
matrices. The latter fact is certainly known by aficionados (cf. the example given in
[
11
, pp. 303–304]
) and then (
6
) follows from the Birkhoff-von Neumann theorem (see
[
11
, Thm. 12.2.11]
) stating that the extreme points of the convex set of bistochastic
n\times n
matrices are permutation
n\times n
matrices. We decided to provide a proof of (
6
) for completeness.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
(X,T)
is a topological dynamical system, then for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x,y\in X
the equation (
6
) holds.

Proof.

First, note that we can identify a permutation
\sigma\in S_{n}
with a discrete measure

\bar{\sigma}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\hat{\delta}_{(j,\sigma(j))}

supported on the set
\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}\times\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}
. The measure
\frac{1}{n}\bar{\sigma}
can be also considered as a coupling of

m_{T}(x,n)
and
m_{T}(y,n)
if we interpret
\hat{\delta}_{(j,\sigma(j))}
as a Dirac measure concentrated on the pair
(x_{j},T^{\sigma(j)}(y))\in X\times X
.
Furthermore, we have

\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)=\min_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}d(x_{j},T^{\sigma(j)}(y))=\min_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)\,\textrm{d}\bar{\sigma}(x,y)\\
\geq\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))}\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)\,\textrm{d}\pi(x,y)=W_{1}(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n)).

For the reverse inequality, we note that every coupling
\pi\in\Pi(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))
corresponds to a bistochastic matrix
\Xi
whose
(i+1,j+1)
entry is given by
n\pi(\{(T^{i}(x),T^{j}(y)\})
, where
0\leq i,j\leq n-1
. By the Birkhoff-von Neumann theorem (see
[
11
, Thm. 12.2.11]
)
\Xi
must be a convex combination of permutation matrices, which in turn correspond to measures
\bar{\sigma}
with
\sigma\in S_{n}
. Therefore we can write

\pi=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\lambda_{\sigma}\frac{1}{n}\bar{\sigma},

where
\lambda_{\sigma}
(
\sigma\in S_{n}
) are nonnegative real coefficients summing up to
1
. It follows that

\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)\,\textrm{d}\pi(x,y)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\lambda_{\sigma}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)\,\textrm{d}\bar{\sigma}(x,y).

We conclude that for every
\pi\in\Pi(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))
it holds

\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)\,\textrm{d}\pi(x,y)\geq\min_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\frac{1}{n}\int_{X\times X}d(x,y)\,\textrm{d}\bar{\sigma}(x,y)=\bar{E}_{n}(x,y).

Thus,
W_{1}(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))\geq\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system.

(1)

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
\rho(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))<\delta
we have
\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon
.

(2)

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that for every
x,y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)<\delta
we have
\rho(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Since
W_{1}
and
\rho
are compatible with the weak
∗
topology on

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system.
For every
\varepsilon>0

such that for every

.

Theorem 3.6
.

For a topological dynamical system
(X,T)
,
the map
(X,\bar{E})\to(2^{\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)},\rho_{H})

is uniformly continuous,
that is for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0

for every
x,y\in X

\bar{E}(x,y)<\delta

\rho_{H}(\hat{\omega}_{T}(x),\hat{\omega}_{T}(y))<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Applying Lemma
3.5
for
\varepsilon/6
in place of
\varepsilon
, we get a desired constant
\delta>0
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\delta<\varepsilon/6
. Choose
x,y\in X
such that
\bar{E}(x,y)<\delta
.
Pick
\mu\in\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
.
Find a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
such that
m_{T}(x,n_{k})\to\mu
as
k\to\infty
. Let
N_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that for all
k
with
n_{k}\geq N_{1}
it holds that
\rho(\mu,m_{T}(x,n_{k}))<\varepsilon/6
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
m_{T}(y,n_{k})\to\nu
as
k\to\infty
. Then
\nu\in\hat{\omega}_{T}(y)
and we can find
N_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
be such that for all

\rho(\nu,m_{T}(y,n_{k}))<\varepsilon/6

By Lemma
3.5
, there exists

such that for all

with

k
with

\rho(\mu,\nu)\leq\rho(\mu,m_{T}(x,n_{k}))+\rho(m_{T}(x,n_{k}),m_{T}(y,n_{k}))+\rho(m_{T}(y,n_{k}),\nu)\leq\varepsilon/2.

Therefore,

\rho(\mu,\hat{\omega}_{T}(y))=\min\{\rho(\mu,\eta)\colon\eta\in\hat{\omega}_{T}(y)\}\leq\varepsilon/2.

Hence

and

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system.
For every

\bar{E}(x,y)=0

\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)=\hat{\omega}_{T}(y)

We will show in subsection

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system. If
x\in X
is a generic point, then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a

such that for every

with

one has

\bar{E}(x,y)\leq\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Assume
\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)=\{\mu\}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
. By Corollary
3.4
,
there exists a
\delta>0
such that for every
y\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
we have

\rho_{H}(\hat{\omega}_{T}(x),\hat{\omega}_{T}(y))<\delta

Note that there exists a strictly increasing sequence

3.1.
An Example

In this subsection we will provide an example showing that
\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)=\hat{\omega}_{T}(y)
need not imply
\bar{E}(x,y)=0
. This shows that the assumption that
x
is a generic point can not be omitted neither in Corollary
3.8
nor in Corollary
3.9
.

We endow the space of all infinite sequences of symbols from

\{0,1\}
indexed by the non-negative integers with the product topology, and denote it by
\{0,1\}^{\infty}
.
A compatible metric
\{0,1\}^{\infty}
is given by
for any
x=(x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty},y=(y_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}\in\{0,1\}^{\infty}

d(x,y)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }x=y;\\
2^{-k},&k=\min\{i\geq 0\colon x_{i}\neq y_{i}\}.\end{cases}

The shift map
S
on
\{0,1\}^{\infty}
is defined by
S(x_{0}x_{1}x_{2}\ldots)=x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}\ldots
.
Note that given two points
x=(x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
y=(y_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
in the full shift, some
N\geq 1
and a permutation
\sigma\in S_{N}
the sum

\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}d(S^{k}(x),S^{\sigma(k)}(y))=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}d(x_{k}x_{k+1}\ldots,y_{\sigma(k)}y_{\sigma(k)+1}\ldots)

is bounded below by the number of
0\leq k<N
such that
x_{k}\neq y_{\sigma(k)}
. This is because
d(x_{k}x_{k+1}\ldots,y_{\sigma(k)}y_{\sigma(k)+1}\ldots)=1
provided
x_{k}\neq y_{\sigma(k)}
. Therefore,

(8)

\min_{\sigma\in S_{N}}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}d(S^{k}(x),S^{\sigma(k)}(y))\geq\left|\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}x_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}y_{k}\right|.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of positive integers such that

(9)

\frac{a_{1}+\dotsb+a_{n}}{a_{n+1}}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

We also define an auxiliary sequence

(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}
by

b_{n}=a_{1}+\dotsb+a_{n}\quad\text{for }n=1,2,3\ldots

Define two sequences of finite words over
\{0,1\}
, denoted

(U_{n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(V_{n})_{n\geq 1}
by

U_{n}=\begin{cases}0^{a_{n}}&\text{if $n$ is odd,}\\
1^{a_{n}}&\text{if $n$ is even,}\end{cases}\quad V_{n}=\begin{cases}1^{a_{n}}&\text{if $n$ is odd,}\\
0^{a_{n}}&\text{if $n$ is even.}\end{cases}

Define

x=(x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}=U_{1}U_{2}U_{3}\ldots\quad\text{and}\quad y=(y_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}=V_{1}V_{2}V_{3}\ldots

We claim that
\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)=\hat{\omega}_{T}(y)
, but
\bar{E}(x,y)=1
. We first prove the latter equality.
It follows from (
8
) that for every
n\geq 1
we have

(10)

\frac{1}{b_{n}}\min_{\sigma\in S_{b_{n}}}\sum_{k=0}^{b_{n}-1}d(S^{k}(x),S^{\sigma(k)}(y))\geq\frac{1}{b_{n}}(a_{n}-(a_{1}+\ldots+a_{n-1})).

As a consequence of (
9
) we see that the right-hand side of (
10
) tends to
1
as
n\to\infty
. This proves that
\bar{E}(x,y)=1
.

Let

K=\{\alpha\delta_{\bar{0}}+(1-\alpha)\delta_{\bar{1}}:\alpha\in[0,1]\},

where
\delta_{\bar{0}}
, respectively
\delta_{\bar{1}}
is the Dirac measure concentrated on the fixed point
\bar{0}=000\ldots
, respectively
\bar{1}=111\ldots
.
It is easy to see that

\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)=\hat{\omega}_{T}(y)=K
.

4.
\bar{E}
-continuity and mean equicontinuity

In this section, we provide an extended characterisation of
\bar{E}
-continuity (Theorem
4.4
). The
\bar{E}
-continuity was first introduced in
[
24
]
.
Recall that a topological dynamical system
(X,T)
is called
\bar{E}
-continuous
if for every
\varepsilon>0

there exists a
\delta>0
such that for every
x,y\in X

with
d(x,y)<\delta
one has
\bar{E}(x,y)<\varepsilon
.
We first note a corollary to Lemma
3.5
, which is crucial for our approach in this section.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
(X,T)
is a topological dynamical system such that the map
\hat{\omega}_{T}\colon(X,d)\to(2^{\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)},\rho_{H})
,
x\mapsto\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
is continuous, then for every
x\in X
the topological dynamical system
(\overline{\orb(x,T)},T)
is uniquely ergodic.

Proof.

It is clear that the map
\hat{\omega}_{T}
is constant on
\orb(x,T)
. By the continuity,
\hat{\omega}_{T}
must be also constant on
\overline{\orb(x,T)}
.
But there exists an ergodic measure
\nu
supported on
\overline{\orb(X,T)}
and
\nu
has a generic point
y\in\overline{\orb(X,T)}
, so
\hat{\omega}_{T}(z)=\hat{\omega}_{T}(y)=\{\nu\}
for every
z\in\overline{\orb(X,T)}
.
∎

The following is essentially Corollary 5.2 of
[
24
]
.
Here we give a proof for completeness.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system.
If
(X,T)
is
\bar{E}
-continuous and has a dense orbit
then
(X,T)
is uniquely ergodic.

Proof.

Pick a point
x\in X
such that
\orb(x,T)
is dense in
X
.
Then
\bar{E}(a,b)=0
for every
a,b\in\orb(x,T)
.
As
(X,T)
is
\bar{E}
-continuous and
X=\overline{\orb(x,T)}
,
we have
\bar{E}(a,b)=0
for all
a,b\in X
.
Then
(X,T)
is uniquely ergodic by Corollary
3.10
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Note that the Hausdorff metric
\rho_{H}
agrees with
\rho
on the space of all singletons in
\mathcal{M}(X)
, that is, we have

\rho_{H}(\{\mu\},\{\nu\})=\rho(\mu,\nu)\quad\text{for every }\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}(X).

Therefore if
(X,T)
is a topological dynamical system such that every
x\in X
is generic for some measure, then the map
x\mapsto\mu(x)
is well defined and its continuity as a map from
(X,d)
to
(\mathcal{M}_{T}(X),\rho)
is equivalent to continuity of the map
x\mapsto\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
treated as a map from
(X,d)
to
(2^{\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)},\rho_{H})
.

We have the following characterisation of
\bar{E}
-continuity.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(1)

(X,T)
is
\bar{E}
-continuous;

(2)

the map
\hat{\omega}_{T}\colon(X,d)\to(2^{\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)},\rho_{H})
is continuous;

(3)

the family of empirical measure maps

(m_{T}(\cdot,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
is uniformly equicontinuous on
X
, where
m_{T}(\cdot,n)\colon X\to\mathcal{M}(X)
is given by

x\mapsto m_{T}(x,n)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\hat{\delta}(T^{j}(x))\qquad\text{for $x\in X$ and $n\in\mathbb{N}$};

(4)

for every
x\in X
the topological dynamical system
(\overline{\orb(x,T)},T)
is uniquely ergodic and the map
X\ni x\mapsto\mu(x)\in\mathcal{M}_{T}^{e}(X)
is continuous;

(5)

every
x\in X
is generic for some ergodic invariant measure and the map
X\ni x\mapsto\mu(x)\in\mathcal{M}_{T}^{e}(X)
is continuous;

(6)

every
x\in X
is generic for some invariant measure and the map
X\ni x\mapsto\mu(x)\in\mathcal{M}_{T}(X)
is continuous;

(7)

for every continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
,
the sequence of continuous functions
\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f\circ T^{k}\}
is pointwise convergent to a continuous function
f^{*}
;

(8)

for every continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a continuous function
f^{*}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
such that for every
x\in X

we have

\lim_{n-m\to\infty}\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}f(T^{k}(x))=f^{*}(x);

(9)

for every continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
,
the sequence of continuous functions
\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f\circ T^{k}\}
converges uniformly to a continuous function
f^{*}
.

If any of the conditions
(1)
–
(9)
holds, then the limit continuous function
f^{*}
mentioned in
(7)
–
(9)
is the function given for
x\in X
by

f^{*}(x)=\int_{X}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu(x).

Proof.

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-1}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-1B}}
: By definition of
\bar{E}
-continuity the identity map mapping
(X,d)
to
(X,\bar{E})
is continuous. With this observation, the conclusion follows from Theorem
3.6
.

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-1B}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-1A}}
: By Lemma
4.1
every
x\in X
is a generic point so the map
x\mapsto\mu(x)
is well-defined and continuous (see Remark
4.3
).

First, we claim that for every
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
there exist
N=N(x)\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta=\delta(x)>0
such that if
y\in X
and
d(x,y)<\delta
, then
\rho(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))<\varepsilon
for every
n\geq N
.

Before proving the claim, we will show that it is all we need to finish the proof. To this end, we fix
\varepsilon>0
and use the claim for every
x\in X
to find
\delta(x)>0
and
N(x)\in\mathbb{N}
, so that for every
y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\delta(x)
we have

(11)

\rho(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))<\varepsilon/2\quad\text{for all $n\geq N(x)$}.

Next, we cover the space
X
by open balls
B(x,\delta(x))
and choose a finite subcover
\mathcal{U}
. Denote the centres of the balls in
\mathcal{U}
by
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
. Let
N=\max\{N(x_{1}),\ldots,N(x_{k})\}
. Let
\delta_{0}>0
be the Lebesgue number for
\mathcal{U}
. We use continuity of the maps
m_{T}(\cdot,1),\ldots,m_{T}(\cdot,N)
to find
\delta_{1}>0
so that if
x,y\in X
satisfy

(12)

\rho(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))<\varepsilon\quad\text{for }1\leq n\leq N.

Let
\delta=\min\{\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\}
. To see that our choice of
\delta
is right, we pick
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\delta
. Note that this choice of
\delta
implies that (
12
) holds. Since
\{x,y\}
has the diameter smaller than the Lebesgue number for the cover
\mathcal{U}
there is
1\leq j\leq k
such that
d(x,x_{j})<\delta(x_{j})
and
d(y,x_{j})<\delta(x_{j})
. Using that
N(x_{j})\leq N
and the triangle inequality it now easily follows from (

) that

(13)

\rho(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))<\varepsilon\quad\text{for }N\leq n.

Combining (
12
) and (

) we see that the condition
(3)
of Theorem
4.4
holds as required.

It remains to prove the claim holds. Aiming for a contradiction, suppose that there are
x\in X
and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
k,N\in\mathbb{N}
we can find
y_{k}\in X
and
n_{k}\geq N
satisfying

and

(14)

\rho(m_{T}(x,n_{k}),m_{T}(y_{k},n_{k}))\geq\varepsilon_{0}.

Since given
k\in\mathbb{N}
our choice of
N\in\mathbb{N}
is arbitrary, we can use the fact that
x
is generic for
\mu(x)
and pick
N=N(k)
such that

(15)

\rho(m_{T}(x,n),\mu(x))\leq 1/k\quad\text{for all }n\geq N=N(k).

In particular, (
15
) holds with

substituted for

.
Note that the way we have chosen
y_{k}
implies that
y_{k}\to x
with

. In addition,
passing to a subsequence, if necessary, we assume that the sequence

(m_{T}(y_{k},n_{k}))_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges to
\xi
when

. We necessarily have that
\xi
is an invariant measure, but it is not necessarily true that
\xi
is ergodic. By the ergodic decomposition theorem, there is a probability measure
\lambda
supported on the set
\mathcal{M}_{T}^{e}(X)
of ergodic

-invariant measures such that

\xi=\int_{\mathcal{M}_{T}^{e}(X)}\nu\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\lambda(\nu).

Let
\text{supp}(\xi)
be the support of
\xi
, that is, the smallest closed set with the full measure with respect to
\xi
. By (

) we get that
\nu(\text{supp}(\xi))=1
for
\lambda
-almost every
\nu\in\mathcal{M}_{T}^{e}(X)
. By (

), we also have
\rho(\nu,\mu(x))>0
for
\nu
in a subset of
\mathcal{M}_{T}^{e}(X)
with positive measure with respect to
\lambda
.

We conclude that there must be an ergodic measure
\nu
such that
\text{supp}(\nu)\subset\text{supp}(\xi)
and
\rho(\nu,\mu(x))>0
.
Since points generic for
\nu
lie densely in

\text{supp}(\nu)
and

is assumed to be continuous, any point

satisfies
\mu(z)=\nu
and
\xi(U)>0
for every open neighbourhood
U
of
z
. By the portmanteau theorem, for each open neighbourhood
U
of
z
we have

\liminf_{k\to\infty}m_{T}(y_{k},m_{k})(U)=\frac{1}{n_{k}}|\{0\leq j<n_{k}:T^{j}(y_{k})\in U\}|\geq\xi(U)>0.

It follows that for some sequence
(j_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
we have
T^{j_{k}}(y_{k})\to z
when
k\to\infty
.
Continuity of the map
\mu(\cdot)
implies that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mu(T^{j_{k}}(y_{k}))=\mu(\lim_{k\to\infty}T^{j_{k}}(y_{k}))=\mu(z)=\nu.

Now, since for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\mu(T^{j_{k}}(y_{k}))=\mu(y_{k})
, we conclude that
\mu(x)=\mu(z)=\nu
contradicting
\rho(\nu,\mu(x))>0
.

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-1A}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-2}}
: Since
\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
is the set of limit points of the sequence
(m_{T}(x,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
the continuity of the map
x\mapsto\hat{\omega}_{T}(x)
follows immediately from
(3)
. To finish the proof it is enough to apply Lemma
4.1
.

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-2}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-3}}
: By Remark
4.3
, continuity of the map
x\mapsto\mu(x)
follows from
(4)
. The rest of the proof is clear.

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-3}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-4}}
: This is clear.

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-4}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-1}}
:
This follows from Corollary
3.8
.

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-1A}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-7}}
:
Note that
for every
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following holds

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f\circ T^{k}(x)=\int_{X}f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}m_{T}(x,n).

Therefore uniform equicontinuity of the family of maps
(m(\cdot,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
implies that for every continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
,
the family of continuous functions

\left\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f\circ T^{k}:n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

is also uniformly equicontinuous. To end the proof it suffices to follow the proof of (2)
\Rightarrow
(3) in
[
17
, Lemma 3.3]
almost verbatim (the same reasoning is implicit in
[
19
]
).

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-7}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-6}}
: Fix a continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
.
Assume that the sequence
\{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f\circ T^{k}\}
converges uniformly to
f^{*}\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
. Note that
f^{*}=f^{*}\circ T^{m}
for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and use uniform convergence to find
L\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
\ell\geq L
and
x\in X
we have

\left|\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}f\circ T^{k}(x)-f^{*}(x)\right|<\varepsilon.

It follows that if
n-m\geq L
and
x\in X
, then

\left|\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}f\circ T^{k}(x)-f^{*}(x)\right|=\left|\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{k=0}^{n-m-1}f\circ T^{k}(T^{m}(x))-f^{*}(T^{m}(x))\right|<\varepsilon

and
(8)
is proved.

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-6}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-5}}
: This is clear.

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-5}}\implies\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-4}}
: It is a consequence of the fact that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
as
n\to\infty
in
\mathcal{M}(X)
if and only if

\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\to\int f\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\mu
as
n\to\infty
for every continuous function
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

One may wonder if the condition saying that the map
x\mapsto\mu(x)
is continuous may be omitted from Theorem
4.4
(5)
. Dowker and Lederer
[
2
]
seems to be the first to investigate the question of what happens when all points of a topological dynamical system are generic for some invariant measure. It turns out
that this property together with minimality implies unique ergodicity
[
2
]
.
Katznelson and Weiss
[
13
]
proved that
if all points are generic and there is only one minimal set, but more than one ergodic measure, then there must be uncountably
many ergodic measures, and showed that this
possibility can actually arise. In the Katznelson–Weiss example there exists exactly one fully supported ergodic invariant measure and every point is generic for some ergodic invariant measure, which means that the map
x\mapsto\mu(x)
cannot be continuous. It follows that weakening of Theorem
4.4
(5)
is not possible. Another example of this type is constructed in
[
7
]
.

Remark 4.7
.

Note that Theorem
4.4
provides an almost purely topological proof that a group rotation is uniquely ergodic if and only if it has a dense orbit. This is because if
X
is a compact topological group then there exists a left-invariant metric
d
on
X
, that is a metric such that for every
g\in X
the rotation by
g
, that is the map
x\mapsto gx
denoted
R_{g}
is an isometry (
d(x,y)=d(R_{g}(x),R_{g}(y))
for all
x,y\in X
). It is now easy to see that every isometry must be
\bar{E}
-continuous, hence it is uniquely ergodic if and only if it has a dense orbit. We also see that for an isometry every point is a generic point for an ergodic measure and has a uniquely ergodic orbit closure, which must be a minimal subset, and all isometries must have a closed set of ergodic measures.

The concept of mean equicontinuity was introduced in
[
17
]
. Mean equicontinuity is equivalent to the mean-L-stable property introduced in
[
6
]
.
It is clear that if
(X,T)
is mean equicontinuous
then it is also
\bar{E}
-continuous.

We have the following characterisation of mean equicontinuity. Note that according to Theorem
4.4
we can replace the condition
(2)
of Theorem
4.8
by any condition listed in Theorem
4.4
. For the reasons explained in Remark
4.9
below we mention only one such possibility.

Theorem 4.8
.

For a topological dynamical system
(X,T)
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

(X,T)
is mean equicontinuous;

(2)

(X\times X,T\times T)
is
\bar{E}
-continuous;

(3)

for every
(x,y)\in X\times X
the system
\overline{\orb((x,y),T\times T)}
is uniquely ergodic and the map
(x,y)\mapsto\mu(x,y)
is continuous;

(4)

(X,T)
is Weyl mean equicontinuous: for every
\varepsilon>0

there exists a
\delta>0
such that for every
x,y\in X

with
d(x,y)<\delta
one has

\limsup_{n-m\to\infty}\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}d(T^{k}(x),T^{k}(y))<\varepsilon.

Proof.

\text{\ref{mean-1}}\implies\text{\ref{mean-2}}
:
As
(X\times X,T\times T)
is also mean equicontinuous,

(X\times X,T\times T)
is
\bar{E}
-continuous.

\text{\ref{mean-2}}\Longleftrightarrow\text{\ref{mean-2A}}
: See Theorem
4.4

\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-1}}\Longleftrightarrow\text{\ref{Fbar-equi-2}}
and Remark
4.3
.

\text{\ref{mean-2}}\implies\text{\ref{mean-3}}
: As the metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)

is a continuous function on
X\times X
, by
Theorem
4.4
(8)
the function

(x,y)\mapsto\lim_{n-m\to\infty}\frac{1}{n-m}\sum_{k=m}^{n-1}d(T^{k}(x),T^{k}(y))

is continuous.
Note that the limit in the above formula exists.

\text{\ref{mean-3}}\implies\text{\ref{mean-1}}
:
This is clear.
∎

Remark 4.10
.

The property in Theorem
4.8
(4)

was called Banach mean equicontinuity in
[
17
]
. The authors of

[
17

also asked whether Banach mean equicontinuity is equivalent to mean equicontinuity.
It is shown in
[
3
]
that the answer is positive for minimal systems and in
[
20
]
for general systems.
Our Theorem
4.8
provides a new proof of this fact for topological dynamical systems, in particular for

We wrap up with yet another definition motivated by
[
12
]
. The authors of
[
12
]
introduced
equicontinuity in the mean
and showed that it is equivalent to mean equicontinuity for minimal systems and the authors of
[
20
]
showed the equivalence for all topological dynamical systems. In analogy to these results, we introduce
\{\bar{E}_{n}\}
-equicontinuity and show that it is actually equivalent to
\bar{E}
-continuity.

Definition 4.11
.

Let
(X,T)
be a topological dynamical system. We say that
(X,T)
is
\{\bar{E}_{n}\}
-equicontinuous if for any
\epsilon>0
there exists a
\delta>0
, such that for every
x,y\in X
with

d(x,y)<\delta
we have
\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

It is easy to see that
\{\bar{E}_{n}\}
-equicontinuity implies
\bar{E}
-continuity. We will show that the converse also holds.

Theorem 4.12
.

For a topological dynamical system
(X,T)
the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

(X,T)
is
\bar{E}
-continuous;

(2)

(X,T)
is
\{\bar{E}_{n}\}
-equicontinuous.

Proof.

\text{\ref{last-1}}\implies\text{\ref{last-2}}
:
Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Use Corollary
3.4
to find
\eta>0
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x,y\in X
with
\rho(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))<\eta
we have
\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon
. By Theorem
4.4
(3)
there exists
\delta>0
, such that for every
x,y\in X
with
d(x,y)<\delta
we have
\rho(m_{T}(x,n),m_{T}(y,n))<\eta
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It is now clear that for every
x,y\in X
, the inequality
d(x,y)<\delta
implies
\bar{E}_{n}(x,y)<\varepsilon
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

\text{\ref{last-2}}\implies\text{\ref{last-1}}
: It is clear.
∎

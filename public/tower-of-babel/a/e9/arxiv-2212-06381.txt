Core shells and double bubbles in a weighted nonlocal isoperimetric problem
Stanley Alama, Lia Bronsard, Xinyang Lu, Chong Wang

Abstract
We consider a sharp-interface model of A ​ B ​ C ABC triblock copolymers, for which the surface tension σ i ​ j \sigma_{ij} across the interface separating phase i i from phase j j may depend on the components. We study global minimizers of the associated ternary local isoperimetric problem in ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} , and show how the geometry of minimizers changes with the surface tensions σ i ​ j \sigma_{ij} , varying from symmetric double-bubbles for equal surface tensions, through asymmetric double bubbles, to core shells as the values of σ i ​ j \sigma_{ij} become more disparate. Then we consider the effect of nonlocal interactions in a droplet scaling regime, in which vanishingly small particles of two phases are distributed in a sea of the third phase. We are particularly interested in a degenerate case of σ i ​ j \sigma_{ij} in which minimizers exhibit core shell geometry, as this phase configuration is expected on physical grounds in nonlocal ternary systems.

Introduction

In this paper we continue our study of ternary systems, in which three constituents or phases interact through both short range attractive and long range repulsive forces. A prominent example of such ternary systems are the
ABC
triblock copolymers, linear chains of molecules consisting of three subchains, joined covalently to each other. A subchain of type
A
monomer is connected to one of type
B
, which in turn is connected to another subchain of type
C
monomer. Because of the repulsive forces between different types of monomers, different types of subchain tend to segregate. However, since subchains are chemically bonded in molecules, segregation can lead to a phase separation only at microscopic level,
where
A,B
and
C
-rich micro-domains emerge, forming morphological phases, many of which have been observed experimentally: see Figure
1
.

A triblock copolymer can be described as a stable critical point of an energy derived with Nakazawa and Ohta’s density functional theory for triblock copolymers
[
17
,
22
]
. This is a diffuse interface model, a nonlocal version of the vector-valued Cahn-Hilliard energy. In this paper we consider periodic configurations in two dimensions, so we pose our problem in the flat unit torus
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}=\left[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right]^{2}
.
The system is determined via a vector-valued density function
u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{0})\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{2}},\mathbb{R}^{3})
, in which each scalar function
u_{i},i=0,1,2
, gives the density of one constituent of the mixture. The free energy of the system is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\epsilon}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}\left[\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}+W(u)\right]dx+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\epsilon\gamma_{ij}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}G(x-y)\;u_{i}(x)\;u_{j}(y)dxdy.

(1.1)

W
is a triple-well potential which achieves minimum value
0
at exactly three points:
\alpha_{1}=(1,0,0),\alpha_{2}=(0,1,0)
and
\alpha_{0}=(0,0,1)
, and
G
is the Laplace Green’s function on
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
, of mean zero.
The functional
\mathcal{E}^{\epsilon}
is defined on

\displaystyle\left\{u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{0})\,:\,u_{i}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{T}^{2}};\mathbb{R}),\ i=0,1,2;\ \sum_{i=0}^{2}u_{i}=1;\ \frac{1}{|{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}u_{i}(x)dx=M_{i},i=1,2\right\}.

(1.2)

Using
\Gamma
-convergence
[
9
,
21
]
, the variational structure of
\mathcal{E}^{\epsilon}
is connected to minimization of an associated sharp-interface model,

\mathcal{E}(u):=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq 2}\sigma_{ij}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\partial\Omega_{j})+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\gamma_{ij}}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}G(x-y)\;u_{i}(x)\;u_{j}(y)dxdy,

(1.3)

where the densities
u_{i}
are replaced by phase domains described by characteristic functions
u_{i}=\chi_{\Omega_{i}}
of sets
\Omega_{i}
,
i=0,1,2
, of finite perimeter.

The constants
\sigma_{ij}
,
i\neq j
, represent
surface tension
along the interfaces separating the phase domains, and they are positive, material-dependent constants. Their values are determined from the
\Gamma
-limit of the vector-valued Cahn-Hilliard part of
\mathcal{E}^{\epsilon}

[
6
,
26
]
, as a geodesic distance in a degenerate Riemannian metric induced on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
by
\sqrt{W(u)}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}=\inf\left\{\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{W(\zeta(t))}|\zeta^{\prime}(t)|dt:\zeta\in C^{1}([0,1];\mathbb{R}^{3}),\zeta(0)=\alpha_{i},\zeta(1)=\alpha_{j}\right\}.

(1.4)

Conversely, given positive constants
\sigma_{ij}=\sigma_{ji}
,
i\neq j
, one might want to engineer a potential
W
for which
\sigma_{ij}
give the surface tensions along each interface in
\mathcal{E}(u)
. This may or may not be possible, as the definition (
1.4
) imposes a necessary condition for
\sigma_{ij}
to arise as coefficients of the perimeter in
\mathcal{E}(u)
in any
\Gamma
-limit of
\mathcal{E}^{\epsilon}
, in the form of a set of
triangle inequalities,

\sigma_{ij}\leq\sigma_{ik}+\sigma_{kj},\quad 0\leq i,j,k\leq 2.

(1.5)

Our previous paper
[
1
]
discusses the special case
\sigma_{01}=\sigma_{02}=\sigma_{12}=1
, which arises (for instance) when
W
is symmetric with respect to permutations of the
u_{i}
-axes. In this paper we consider the case of unequal surface tensions
\sigma_{ij}
, in which the geometry of minimizers can be quite different. Although it is not motivated by
\Gamma
-convergence, we also consider a case where the triangle inequalities (
1.5
) are violated,
\sigma_{02}>\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12}
.

When the surface tensions
\sigma_{ij}
obey the triangle inequalities (
1.5
), the variational problem (
1.3
) may conveniently be posed with characteristic functions lying in BV spaces,

\displaystyle\left\{u=(u_{1},u_{2},u_{0}):u_{i}\in BV({\mathbb{T}^{2}};\{0,1\}),i=0,1,2;\sum_{i=0}^{2}u_{i}=1;\frac{1}{|{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}u_{i}(x)dx=M_{i},i=1,2\right\}.

(1.6)

Here
BV({\mathbb{T}^{2}};\{0,1\})
is the space of functions of bounded variation that only take two values:
0
and
1
. Thus, each
u_{i}=\chi_{\Omega_{i}}
where
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{0}
is a partition of
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
. The weighted perimeter is then re-expressed in terms of the total variations of the components
u_{i}
,

P_{\sigma}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{0})=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq 2}\sigma_{ij}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\partial\Omega_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\beta_{i}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u_{i}|,

(1.7)

with weights

\beta_{1}=\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12}-\sigma_{02},\qquad\beta_{2}=\sigma_{02}+\sigma_{12}-\sigma_{01},\qquad\beta_{0}=\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{02}-\sigma_{12}.

(1.8)

When (
1.5
) holds, each
\beta_{i}\geq 0
. Moreover, at most one of
\beta_{i}=0
, since we assume each
\sigma_{ij}>0
. We note that since
u_{0}=1-u_{1}-u_{2}
, the state of the system is completely determined by the pair
(u_{1},u_{2})
, or indeed any pair of the triple
(u_{1},u_{2},u_{0})
.
In particular, the weighted perimeter
P_{\sigma}
is equivalent to the standard BV norm on the cluster
(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2},\Omega_{0})
, and thus is coercive and lower semicontinuous with respect to
L^{1}
convergence in this case.
The energy may then be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\beta_{i}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u_{i}|+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\gamma_{ij}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(x-y)\;u_{i}(x)\;u_{j}(y)dxdy.

Our goal in this paper is to characterize global minimizers of this functional, both in the absence of the nonlocal interaction (i.e.,
\gamma_{ij}=0
) and in an appropriate “droplet scale” limit, in which minimizers form a very dilute lattice of particles of two minority phases in a sea of the third phase
[
8
,
2
,
1
]
.

In section 2 we consider minimizers of the local isoperimetric problem, minimizing the perimeter
among clusters
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
with given areas
M_{1},M_{2}
, in the absence of the nonlocal term.
We pose the problem in all of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
both because it is a natural setting, and because it plays an important role in the global minimization of the nonlocal energy in the droplet regime, considered in section 4.
The qualitative nature of minimizers depends strongly on the choice of
\sigma_{ij}
, and in particular on whether the triangle inequalities (
1.5
) hold
strictly
or not. In case of strict triangle inequalities, isoperimetric sets with nonzero prescribed mass
M_{1},M_{2}>0
are
double bubbles

[

,

and Figure

. This is consistent with our result in our first paper,
[

]
, in the unweighted case
\sigma_{ij}=1
,
i\neq j
, in which the isoperimetric sets are standard double bubbles with equal angles
{2\pi\over 3}
at the triple junction points. In the weighted case, the angles will be uniquely determined by the surface tensions via Young’s law
[
33
,
25
,
32
,
29
]
.

The case of equality in one of (
1.5
) is also interesting, and yields a completely different geometry for minimizers. If
\beta_{1}=0
, then we show that the optimal geometry is that of a
core shell,

\mathcal{C}^{M_{1}}_{M_{2}}
, with an inner disk of area
M_{2}
surrounded by an annular region of area
M_{1}
; see (e,f) in Figure
2
and Figure
3
. While this is a degenerate case, physically it is highly relevant as core shell constructions are often observed in nature. Indeed, for an ABC triblock copolymer it seems more natural that minimizers form core shells than double bubbles, because of the linear structure of the blocks. Since the bonding of the chains can only occur in linear order, one might expect that between regions of phase A and phase C there must be a phase B region, and that adjacency of phase A and C states should be energetically unfavorable.

This is indeed a degenerate setting, and the geometry of core shell minimizers is not uniquely determined by the perimeter alone, since the position of the interior disk in a core shell is arbitrary, from the point of view of the weighted perimeter alone. The addition of the nonlocal term will resolve this degeneracy, as we will see in Proposition
4.4
.

For completeness, in numerical simulations, we also include the case
\beta_{1}<0
. In this case, the optimal geometry is also a core shell; see (f) in Figure
2
and Figure
3
.

Finally, one may also consider the case
\beta_{0}=0
, in which case minimization prefers the separation of the cluster into disjoint single bubbles. Again, this is a degenerate case, and after adding the nonlocal term, the relative position of the bubbles is not determined by the perimeter alone. Indeed, we expect that the effect of the nonlocal repulsive term will be to push the two constituents far apart, as in the binary case.

Nonlocal effects

It is well-known that the Green’s function term in
\mathcal{E}(u)
competes directly with the local isoperimetric term, in the sense that it is maximized by disks. In order to analyze its role in global minimizers we adopt the
droplet regime
scaling, introduced by Choksi-Peletier
[
8
]
, a critical scaling in which both the isoperimetric and nonlocal terms in the energy act at the same energy scale. This choice of material parameters (discussed in some detail in section 4) represents a dilute limit as the masses of phases
A
and
B
both tend to zero, but with correspondingly large interaction coefficients on the Green’s function so that a rescaled characteristic function of each’s phase domain behaves as Dirac delta measures in the limit.
Introducing a small parameter
\eta>0
, which gives the length scale of a “droplet” of phase
A
or
B
, we consider the rescaled energy

E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})=\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\beta_{i}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla v_{i,\eta}|+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\ \frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2|\log\eta|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(x-y)v_{i,\eta}(x)v_{j,\eta}(y)dxdy,

(1.9)

for
v_{\eta}=(v_{1,\eta},v_{2,\eta})=\eta^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\eta}}\in[BV({\mathbb{T}^{2}};\{0,\eta^{-2}\})]^{2}
with prescribed masses,
\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}v_{i,\eta}=M_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Here
\Gamma_{ij}=|\log\eta|\eta^{3}\gamma_{ij}
; see Section
4
for more details.

\displaystyle E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(A)\ |\ A=(A_{1},A_{2})\text{ 2-cluster, with $|A_{i}|=m_{i}$, $i=1,2$}\right\},\quad\text{and}

Core shell assemblies

Ren & Wang have constructed critical points of
\mathcal{E}(u)
representing dilute lattices of core shells
[
19
]
and single bubbles
[
20
]
, and stationary solutions with double-bubble lattices were constructed by Ren & Wei
[
23
]
. Numerical studies of periodic minimizers suggest
that these assemblies do appear as minimizers of
\mathcal{E}(u)

in many parameter regimes
[
28
]
, and it is an interesting and challenging problem to verify this rigorously. At least in the droplet regime limit described above, a first step is to study the limiting energy
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}
, with given total mass
M=(M_{1},M_{2})
and weights
\sigma
. In our first paper
[
1
]
we proved many properties of the minimizers of
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}
in the case of equal weights. We showed that the number of droplets is finite, and the size of the constituent components is bounded in terms of the coefficients and masses. We showed that, while a minimizer can exhibit both double-bubbles and single-bubbles, there can only be one species of single-bubble if there is coexistence of single- and double-bubbles.

In section 3 we take up the same questions in case
\beta_{1}=0
, and the local isoperimetric problem favors core shell minimizers. In Lemma 3.1 we show that there is a lower bound
m^{-}
on the mass of any droplet constituent, whether single-bubble or component of a core shell, which depends only on the weights
\sigma_{ij}
, the coefficients
\Gamma_{ij}
, and the total masses
M_{1},M_{2}
. From this we may conclude (Corollary
3.2
) that minimizing configurations of
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}
can have only finitely many nontrivial indecomposable components. We note that the finiteness of components in the binary case studied by Choksi & Peletier
[
8
]
was proven using the concavity of the perimeter; as in our study of double-bubbles
[
1
]
, the perimeter in the ternary case is not globally concave, and so more delicate arguments are required.

As was the case with double-bubbles, many open questions remain. Numerical studies show regimes in which minimizers appear to have
only
core shells, but we have no theorem which shows that this must be the case. On the other hand, simulations also show that coexistence of core shells and single bubbles can occur when one chooses values of
M_{i},\Gamma_{ij}
which are very different from each other, so any result in this direction would have to take the ranges of values of the parameters into account.

On clusters

In this paper we use the framework of
clusters
of finite perimeter sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, as set out in
[
16
, Part IV]
. A 2-cluster in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is a disjoint pairing
A=(A_{1},A_{2})
of finite perimeter sets,
|A_{1}\cap A_{2}|=0
, each of finite Lebesgue measure. We write the mass as
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
,
m_{i}=|A_{i}|
,
i=1,2
. It will be convenient for us to permit one of the chambers to be empty, so the case where one of
m_{i}=0
is allowed. The exterior domain
A_{0}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\overline{(A_{1}\cup A_{2})}
has infinite measure, but its perimeter is included in the total weighted perimeter of the cluster. As
\widetilde{A}_{0}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus A_{0}=\overline{(A_{1}\cup A_{2})}
has the same perimeter, it will often be convenient to replace
A_{0}
by
\widetilde{A}_{0}
in calculating the perimeter, that is:

P_{\sigma}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\beta_{1}P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(A_{1})+\beta_{2}P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(A_{2})+\beta_{0}P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(\overline{(A_{1}\cup A_{2})})\right].

The chambers of a cluster
A=(A_{1},A_{2})
do not need to be connected, and indeed in studying the nonlocal problem we expect that they will split into disjoint components. The proper measure theoretic definition is that of
indecomposability
: a set
E
is indecomposable if whenever
E=E_{1}\cup E_{2}
with
|E_{1}\cap E_{2}|=0
and
P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(E)=P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(E_{1})+P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(E_{2})
, then one of
|E_{1}|,|E_{2}|=0
. (See
[
5
]
.)

To minimize the free energy (
1.1

L^{2}
gradient flow dynamics. Periodic boundary conditions are used here. To fulfill the mass constraints, we adopt a modified augmented Lagrange multiplier approach. The coupled nonlocal Allen-Cahn equations with mass constraints are first reformulated via a linear splitting scheme and then be solved efficiently by using the semi-implicit scheme to discretize the time variable and the spectral method to discretize the space variables.
The numerical simulations start from random initial configurations satisfying the mass constraints.

The local isoperimetric problem

In this section, we discuss among all the partitions of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the minimizers of the local part
P_{\sigma}
;
that is, minimizing

P_{\sigma}(A_{1},A_{2},A_{0}):=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq 2}\sigma_{ij}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{i}\cap\partial A_{j}),

(2.1)

among

\{(A_{1},A_{2},A_{0}):|A_{1}\cap A_{2}|=0;|A_{i}|=m_{i},i=1,2;A_{0}={\mathbb{R}}^{2}\setminus\overline{(A_{1}\cup A_{2})}\}.

(2.2)

As noted above, since
A_{0}
and
\widetilde{A}_{0}=\overline{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}
have the same boundary, the perimeter above may be expressed in terms of
A_{1},A_{2}
alone, and when convenient we may replace the exterior domain with the union
\overline{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}
. In particular, we may re-express the total perimeter as

\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(A)=P_{\sigma}(A_{1},A_{2},\overline{A_{1}\cup A_{2}}).

(2.3)

In case the
\sigma_{ij}
satisfy the triangle inequalites (
1.5
) we have the equivalent formulation of this isoperimetric problem in terms of BV characteristic functions, given in (
1.7
). We are also interested in the case of
\sigma_{02}>\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12}
, in which the representation (
1.7
) is not coercive in the BV norm, and in this case a more careful treatment of the minimization problem is required.

The geometry of minimizers depends strongly on the surface tension values
\sigma_{ij}
, and we consider each case separately.

2.1
Pattern 1: One Double bubble

When we assume that the triangle inequalities (
1.5
) hold with
strict
inequalities in each, minimizers of (

) with
m_{1}\neq 0\neq m_{2}
are
double bubbles
. This has been proven (in any dimension) by Lawlor
[

. This geometrical problem was already studied (in the context of grain boundaries) by Mullins & Smith
[

. Minimizers consist of smooth circular arcs which meet at triple junctions. The angle formed at a triple junction must satisfy Young’s Law (also known as a Herring Condition): at each triple junction point, the normal vectors
n_{ij}
to the arc separating phases
i
and
j
must satisfy the balancing condition,

\sum_{i\neq j}\sigma_{ij}\,n_{ij}=0.

This is equivalent to

{\sin\theta_{1}\over\sigma_{02}}={\sin\theta_{2}\over\sigma_{01}}={\sin\theta_{0}\over\sigma_{12}};

(2.4)

see Figure

.

.

The special case of identical weights

. In this case,

which is a two component isoperimetric problem. The standard double bubble satisfying the

2.2
Pattern 2: One Core Shell

Core shell geometries can be expected in the degenerate case of the triangle inequality (
1.5
) where

\sigma_{02}\geq\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12},

(2.6)

and that the other two inequalities in (
1.5
) hold strictly.
With this hypothesis, the cost
\sigma_{02}
of a transition from phase 2 to the background zero-phase is at least as large as a “composite interface” passing through phase 1, so intuitively we expect a shell structure to minimize perimeter.

Definition 2.1
.

Given masses
m_{1},m_{2}>0
, we define the class of generalized core shells, denoted
\mathcal{C}_{m_{2}}^{m_{1}}
, to consist of pairs
(A_{1},A_{2})
,
|A_{i}|=m_{i}
,
i=1,2
,
with an inner disk
A_{2}
of mass
m_{2}
, and an outer annulus
A_{1}
of mass
m_{1}
.

That is,
A_{2}=B_{r_{2}}(p_{2})
and
A_{1}=B_{r_{1}}(p_{1})\setminus B_{r_{2}}(p_{2})
, with
r_{2}=\sqrt{M_{2}/\pi}
,
r_{1}=\sqrt{(M_{1}+M_{2})/\pi}
, and centers
p_{1},p_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
chosen such that
A_{2}=B_{r_{2}}(p_{2})\subset B_{r_{1}}(p_{1})
.
Note that this definition does not require the two circles (the boundary of the disk and the outer boundary of the annulus) to be concentric; see (f) in Figure
2
and
3
.
In addition, this definition permits a special case in which the two circles are tangent to each other; see (e) in Figure
2
and
3
. Indeed, the position of the inner circle
A_{2}
does not affect the total perimeter of the configuration, and so the local isoperimetric problem cannot distinguish between these generalized core shells.

We expect the same geometry of minimizers in the more extreme case where
\sigma_{02}>\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12}
. In this situation we cannot rely on the equivalent formulation (
1.3
) in BV in order to ensure existence of a minimizer or lower semicontinuity,
but we may still assert that core shell configurations
\mathcal{C}_{m_{2}}^{m_{1}}
must have smaller perimeter than any other competitor. Hence, we state our geometry result for both cases at once:

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\sigma_{ij}
be given, such that

\sigma_{02}\geq\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12},

and let
m_{1},m_{2}>0
be given. Then the minimizer of
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
is a core shell, ie, of the class
\mathcal{C}_{m_{2}}^{m_{1}}
.

In the case of equality in (
1.5
),

\sigma_{02}=\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12},

(2.7)

the proof is straightforward. So we start with the proof of this case.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
, case of equality in (
2.6
).

Applying (
2.7
) to (
2.1
), for any admissible cluster
A_{1},A_{2},A_{0}
,

\displaystyle P_{\sigma}(A_{1},A_{2},A_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{01}\left[\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{0}\cap\partial A_{1})+\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{0}\cap\partial A_{2})\right]+\sigma_{12}\left[\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{1}\cap\partial A_{2})+\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{0}\cap\partial A_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{01}P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(A_{1}\cup A_{2})+\sigma_{12}P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(A_{2}).

Since the area of
A_{1}\cup A_{2}
is fixed, that is,
|A_{1}\cup A_{2}|=m_{1}+m_{2}
, by the isoperimetric inequality,
P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(A_{1}\cup A_{2})
is minimized when
\partial(A_{1}\cup A_{2})
is a circle. Similarly, the area of
A_{2}
is fixed,
P_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}(A_{2})
is minimized when
\partial A_{2}
is a circle. Since
A_{2}\subset A_{1}\cup A_{2}
, we obtain a core shell, of the form
\mathcal{C}_{m_{2}}^{m_{1}}
. That is, given any admissible cluster
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{0})
with masses
m_{1},m_{2}
, its weighted total perimeter is bounded below by that of a core shell in
\mathcal{C}_{m_{2}}^{m_{1}}
;
a posteriori
a minimizer exists, which is a core shell. Moreover, the inequality will be strict in case either
A_{1}\cup A_{2}
or
A_{2}
are not disks, and so the class of minimizers is exactly
\mathcal{C}_{m_{2}}^{m_{1}}
.
∎

When the inequality (
2.6
) is strict the situation is more delicate, as the weighted perimeter functional is no longer lower semicontinuous.
We need some preliminary results.

Lemma 2.3
.

Given
\sigma_{ij}
, with
\sigma_{02}>\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12}
,
then we can
construct explicit sequences

, converging to some

in the strong

topology, such that

\lim_{n\to+\infty}P_{\sigma}(A_{1,n},A_{2,n},A_{0,n})<P_{\sigma}(A_{1},A_{2},A_{0}).

Proof.

Consider a sequence of configurations like in Figure
7
, and we denote by
T_{n}
(resp.
B_{n}
) the thin layer
(resp. small ball) of type I material wrapping around the lobe of type II
constituent (resp. hollowed out from the lobe of type I material).
Then by construction, we can choose
|B_{n}|\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
,
and the thickness of
T_{n}
also goes to zero.
For any
n
, the perimeter of

A_{2,n}
is completely insulated from
A_{0,n}
, and
the contribution of the perimeter between type II and
T_{n}

is

+\sigma_{12}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{2,n}\cap\partial T_{n}),

while that between
T_{n}
and
A_{0,n}
is

+\sigma_{01}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{0,n}\cap\partial T_{n}).

As the thickness of
T_{n}
goes to zero, both

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{0,n}\cap\partial T_{n}),\qquad\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{2,n}\cap\partial T_{n})

converge to
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{2}\cap\partial A_{0})
. Thus

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\sigma_{12}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{2,n}\cap\partial T_{n})+\sigma_{01}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{0,n}\cap\partial T_{n})=(\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12})\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{2}\cap\partial A_{0}).

(2.8)

But in the limit case,
the boundary of the lobe of type II constituent will
have no layer of type I constituent insulating it anymore, so it
will contribute

+\sigma_{02}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{2}\cap\partial A_{0})

to the perimeter term. This is greater than the sum
(
2.8
). As the other terms are continuous
when passing to the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}

\displaystyle\sigma_{1j}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial(A_{1,n}\setminus B_{n})\cap\partial A_{j,n})=\sigma_{1j}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{1}\cap\partial A_{j}),\qquad j=0,2

\displaystyle\sigma_{01}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial(A_{1,n}\setminus B_{n})\cap\partial B_{n})=0,

we infer

\lim_{n\to+\infty}P_{\sigma}(A_{1,n},A_{2,n},A_{0,n})<P_{\sigma}(A_{1},A_{2},A_{0}),

.

As a consequence of Lemma

, the full nonlocal energy

is also not lower semicontinuous with respect to
the strong

topology.

Proof.

It suffices to notice that the interaction term is continuous with respect

.

As a consequence of Lemma
2.3
, the existence of optimal configurations is significantly more
challenging when
\sigma_{02}>\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12}
, as it
prevents us from using most classical
arguments relying on
minimizing sequences.
Thus, we have to rely on an ad hoc construction.

We require a geometrical lemma which proves an inner-cone condition for minimizers of
\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}
.
We recall that, since we are working with sets
A_{1},A_{2},A_{0}
of finite perimeter, both

\partial A_{i}\cap\partial A_{0}
,
i=1,2
, are
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-rectifiable and hence Lipschitz regular. The unit tangent vectors thus exist
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-a.e, but there may be corners on any of the components of the interfaces, and the following lemma restricts the sharpness of these angles. Define the angle
\alpha_{0}
as the the solution in
(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
of

1-\sin\alpha-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\sin(2\alpha)}=0.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
m_{1},m_{2}>0
, and

(A_{1},A_{2},A_{0})
in (
2.2
) be given.
If there exists
a point
x_{0}\in\Sigma:=\partial A_{i}\cap\partial A_{j}
such that
the interior (to
A_{i}
) angle between the right and left tangent lines to
\Sigma
at
x_{0}

has amplitude
\alpha<\alpha_{0}
, then
there exists a perturbation of
(A_{1},A_{2},A_{0})
with
the same respective masses but
lower (weighted) perimeter.

Proof.

Assume there exists
a point
x_{0}\in\Sigma:=\partial A_{i}\cap\partial A_{j}
such that
the angle between the right and left tangent lines to
\Sigma
at
x_{0}

has amplitude
2\alpha>0
. Then we perturb the entire
configuration in the following way:

1.

first, we choose points
p_{\varepsilon},q_{\varepsilon}\in\Sigma
such
that the path distance on
\Sigma
between
p_{\varepsilon},q_{\varepsilon}
and

x_{0}
is
\varepsilon
. Due to the Lipschitz regularity,
\Sigma

is approximated in first order, near
x_{0}
, by its tangent line,
therefore we have also

\delta_{\varepsilon}:=|x_{0}-p_{\varepsilon}|=|x_{0}-q_{\varepsilon}|=\varepsilon+o(\varepsilon).

2.

Second, we connect
p_{\varepsilon}
and
q_{\varepsilon}
with a straight
line segment
\llbracket p_{\varepsilon},q_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket
, and define

\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon}:=\Sigma\setminus\{\text{portion of }\Sigma\text{ between }p_{\varepsilon}\text{ and }q_{\varepsilon}\}\cup\llbracket p_{\varepsilon},q_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket.

Geometrically, it is clear that

|p_{\varepsilon}-q_{\varepsilon}|=2\delta_{\varepsilon}\sin\alpha+o(\varepsilon),

thus

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\widetilde{\Sigma}_{\varepsilon})=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Sigma)-2\delta_{\varepsilon}(1-\sin\alpha)+o(\varepsilon).

3.

The construction in the previous step, however,
alters the total masses of each type of constituent. Indeed, it is clear that the mass
of type
i
constituent
inside
the region delimited by

\{\text{portion of }\Sigma\text{ between }p_{\varepsilon}\text{ and }q_{\varepsilon}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\llbracket p_{\varepsilon},q_{\varepsilon}\rrbracket,

whose area is

A_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{2}\sin(2\alpha)+o(\varepsilon^{2}),

has been simply removed, and been replaced with type
j
constituent. To balance this issue, we remove a ball of area
A_{\varepsilon}
from
type
j
constituent, and replace it with
type
i
constituent. Our final competitor is the set
obtained in this way.

By construction, such a competitor from the previous
three steps will have the exact same total masses for each type
of constituent as the original
configuration. The perimeter between types
i
and
j
constituents
has been decreased by
2\delta_{\varepsilon}(1-\sin\alpha)+o(\varepsilon)
due to the construction
in Step 2, and increased by
2\sqrt{\pi A_{\varepsilon}}
in Step 3.
Since

2\delta_{\varepsilon}(1-\sin\alpha)-2\sqrt{\pi A_{\varepsilon}}=2\varepsilon[1-\sin\alpha-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\sin(2\alpha)}]+o(\varepsilon),

which,
since we assumed
\alpha<\alpha_{0}
, where
\alpha_{0}
is the solution of

1-\sin\alpha-\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\sin(2\alpha)}=0,

becomes positive for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
,
we get that such a construction produces a competitor with less
total
(weighted) perimeter.
The proof is thus complete.
∎

Let
\sigma_{ij}

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{0}\cap\partial A_{2})=0

Proof.

Case I:
\sigma_{02}>\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12}

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{0}\cap\partial A_{2})>0

a competitor with lower perimeter in the following way:

first, remove a ball
B_{\varepsilon}
of area
\varepsilon
from

A_{0}
. This step

\partial B_{\varepsilon}

+2\sigma_{01}\sqrt{\pi\varepsilon}.

2.

Next, we claim that we can add a thin layer around

of thickness

\varepsilon/\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial A_{2}\cap\partial A_{0})

and the background

However, this step also completely erases the former boundary

Pattern 3: Two single bubbles

Let

be given, such that

Let

A_{2}

Proof.

We can follow the same steps as in the proof of Lemma

We recall (

First we show that having too small a mass is not energetically advantageous.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a lower bound
m^{-}

depending on

M_{i},\;\sigma_{01},\text{ and }\Gamma_{ij},\qquad i,j=1,2,

such that no single bubble
or core shell in a minimizing configuration can have total mass less than

m^{-}:=\frac{32\pi^{3}\sigma_{01}^{2}}{(1+\sqrt{2})^{2}(\Gamma_{11}+2\Gamma_{12}+\Gamma_{22})^{2}(M_{1}+M_{2})^{2}}.

Proof.

Consider an arbitrary minimizing configuration. If there is only one bubble (single bubble or core shell)
then the thesis is trivial.
Pick two bubbles (single bubble or core shell) whose
masses are
m_{i}^{k}
,
i,k=1,2
, where the index
i
denotes the constituent type, and

m_{1}^{k}
(resp.
m_{2}^{k}
) is the “outer” (resp. “inner”) shell.
We allow for

m_{i}^{k}=0
corresponding to the case of a single bubble.
Their
energy
contribution is thus

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2}2\sqrt{\pi}\Big[\sigma_{01}\sqrt{m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{k}}+\sigma_{12}\sqrt{m_{2}^{k}}\Big]+\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{4\pi}m_{i}^{k}m_{j}^{k}.

(3.6)

Combining them into a bubble, whose inner disk
(resp. outer shell)
has mass
m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}
(resp.
m_{1}^{1}+m_{1}^{2}
), then the energy contribution
becomes

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\pi}\Bigg[\sigma_{01}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{2}(m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{k})}+\sigma_{12}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{2}m_{2}^{k}}\Bigg]+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{4\pi}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{2}m_{i}^{k}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{2}m_{j}^{k}\Big).

(3.7)

Denote
m^{k}:=m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{k}
,
k=1,2
. By
subtracting
(
3.6
) from
(
3.7
) we get

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\pi}\Bigg[\sigma_{01}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{2}(m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{k})}+\sigma_{12}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{2}m_{2}^{k}}\Bigg]+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{4\pi}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{2}m_{i}^{k}\Big)\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{2}m_{j}^{k}\Big)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{2}2\sqrt{\pi}\Big[\sigma_{01}\sqrt{m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{k}}+\sigma_{12}\sqrt{m_{2}^{k}}\Big]-\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{4\pi}m_{i}^{k}m_{j}^{k}

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{01}[\sqrt{m^{1}+m^{2}}-\sqrt{m^{1}}-\sqrt{m^{2}}]+2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{12}[\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}}-\sqrt{m_{2}^{2}}]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[\Gamma_{11}m_{1}^{1}m_{1}^{2}+\Gamma_{12}(m_{1}^{1}m_{2}^{2}+m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{1})+\Gamma_{22}m_{2}^{1}m_{2}^{2}\Big].

By the optimality of our initial configuration, we need the above term to be nonnegative, i.e.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{01}[\sqrt{m^{1}+m^{2}}-\sqrt{m^{1}}-\sqrt{m^{2}}]+2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{12}[\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}}-\sqrt{m_{2}^{2}}]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[\Gamma_{11}m_{1}^{1}m_{1}^{2}+\Gamma_{12}(m_{1}^{1}m_{2}^{2}+m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{1})+\Gamma_{22}m_{2}^{1}m_{2}^{2}\Big].

(3.8)

Thus,

0\leq 2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{01}[\sqrt{m^{1}+m^{2}}-\sqrt{m^{1}}-\sqrt{m^{2}}]+\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[\Gamma_{11}m_{1}^{1}m_{1}^{2}+\Gamma_{12}(m_{1}^{1}m_{2}^{2}+m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{1})+\Gamma_{22}m_{2}^{1}m_{2}^{2}\Big]

Assume without loss of generality that

Note that

\displaystyle\sqrt{m^{1}}+\sqrt{m^{2}}-\sqrt{m^{1}+m^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{m^{1}m^{2}}}{\sqrt{m^{1}}+\sqrt{m^{2}}+\sqrt{m^{1}+m^{2}}}\geq\frac{2}{2+\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{m^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\Gamma_{11}m_{1}^{1}m_{1}^{2}+\Gamma_{12}(m_{1}^{1}m_{2}^{2}+m_{1}^{2}m_{2}^{1})+\Gamma_{22}m_{2}^{1}m_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Gamma_{11}m^{1}m^{2}+\Gamma_{12}(m^{1}m^{2}+m^{2}m^{1})+\Gamma_{22}m^{1}m^{2}

\displaystyle=(\Gamma_{11}+2\Gamma_{12}+\Gamma_{22})m^{1}m^{2}

Thus (

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{01}}{2+\sqrt{2}}\sqrt{m^{2}}+\frac{1}{2\pi}(\Gamma_{11}+2\Gamma_{12}+\Gamma_{22})(M_{1}+M_{2})m^{2}

\displaystyle\Longrightarrow m^{2}\geq\frac{32\pi^{3}\sigma_{01}^{2}}{(1+\sqrt{2})^{2}(\Gamma_{11}+2\Gamma_{12}+\Gamma_{22})^{2}(M_{1}+M_{2})^{2}},

concluding the proof since

Corollary 3.2
.

(Finiteness)
For any

Proof.

. Since the total combined mass of types I and II constituents

Lemma 3.3
.

There exist computable lower bounds

,

If type

The proof is slightly different between the cases where there are at least two core shells, and where there is only a single core shell with
the rest being single bubbles.

Case 1: at least two core shells.

Denote by

m_{i}^{k}
,

the masses of their lobes.
By Lemma
3.1
we have

, and assume that

is the smallest (for the other scenarios, the proof is similar).
We need to show that

cannot be both too small. The energy contribution

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{2}2\sqrt{\pi}\Big[\sigma_{01}\sqrt{m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{k}}+\sigma_{12}\sqrt{m_{2}^{k}}\Big]+\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{4\pi}m_{i}^{k}m_{j}^{k}.

(3.9)

Now we do the following construction: move the entire bubble of type II constituent, with mass
m_{2}^{2}
, from the second bubble to the first,
Then take the equivalent amount
m_{2}^{2}
of type I constituent from the first bubble and move it to the second.
Therefore, this construction a core shell with inner disk (resp. outer

m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}
(resp.
m_{1}^{1}-m_{2}^{2}
), and
a single bubble of type I constituent of mass

. Their energy contribution is thus

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\pi}\Big(\sigma_{01}\sqrt{m_{1}^{1}+m_{2}^{1}}+\sigma_{12}\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{\Gamma_{11}(m_{1}^{1}-m_{2}^{2})^{2}+2\Gamma_{12}(m_{1}^{1}-m_{2}^{2})(m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2})+\Gamma_{22}(m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2})^{2}}{4\pi}

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{01}\sqrt{m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2}}+\frac{\Gamma_{11}(m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2})^{2}}{4\pi}.

(3.10)

Subtracting (
3.10

3.9
)
gives

\displaystyle 2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{12}

\displaystyle\Big[\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}}+\sqrt{m_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigg[\Gamma_{11}({m_{1}^{2}-m_{1}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}})+\Gamma_{12}(m_{1}^{1}-m_{2}^{1}-m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2})+\Gamma_{22}m_{2}^{1}\bigg]m_{2}^{2},

and due to the optimality of our initial configuration, such a difference must be negative. Thus
we need

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq 2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{12}\Big[\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}}+\sqrt{m_{2}^{2}}-\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}}\Big]-\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigg[\Gamma_{11}(m_{1}^{2}-m_{1}^{1}+m_{2}^{2})+\Gamma_{12}(m_{1}^{1}-m_{2}^{1}-m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2})+\Gamma_{22}m_{2}^{1}\bigg]m_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{12}\frac{2\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}m_{2}^{2}}}{\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}}+\sqrt{m_{2}^{2}}+\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}}}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[\Gamma_{11}(m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2})+\Gamma_{12}m_{1}^{1}+\Gamma_{22}m_{2}^{1}\Big]m_{2}^{2},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq 2\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{12}\frac{2\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}m_{2}^{2}}}{\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}}+\sqrt{m_{2}^{2}}+\sqrt{m_{2}^{1}+m_{2}^{2}}}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[\Gamma_{11}(m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2})+\Gamma_{12}m_{1}^{1}+\Gamma_{22}m_{2}^{1}\Big]m_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}\sigma_{12}}{(2+\sqrt{2})}\sqrt{m_{2}^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\pi}\Big[\Gamma_{11}(M_{1}+M_{2})+\Gamma_{12}M_{1}+\Gamma_{22}M_{2}\Big]m_{2}^{2}.

Case 2: only one core shell.

Using the same arguments from
[
8
]
, we may conclude that
all single bubbles of the same type constituent have the same mass.
Several cases are possible.

1.

If there are two

from being too small.
Then, by noting that

If there is only one other single bubble,
then the entire configuration is made of a core shell and a single bubble.

We first show that, in the core-shell, the type I constituent is
forming the outer annulus
contacting the background, while the type II constituent is always concentrated in a ball.

(a)

\displaystyle 0=f_{1}^{\prime}(\eta)

M_{i}
,
\sigma_{ij}
,
\Gamma_{ij}
, such that
there
cannot be any stable configurations (and thus no optimal ones) if

\eta<\eta_{0}
or
\eta>M_{2}-\eta_{0}
. Consequently, this means that
neither the single bubble, nor
any lobes of the core-shell, can have too small a mass.

Combining all the above cases concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
M=(M_{1},M_{2}),M_{1},M_{2}>0
, when
\sigma_{02}=\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12}
and
\Gamma_{12}=0
, a minimizing configuration for
\overline{e_{0}}(M)
can not have two different type of single bubbles.

Proof.

Assume in a minimizing configuration for
\overline{e_{0}}(M)
, there are two single bubbles
(m_{1}^{k},0)
and
(0,m_{2}^{l})
. Then replacing these two single bubbles by a core shell
(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{l})
will reduce the energy
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e_{0}(m^{k})
since

\displaystyle e_{0}(m_{1}^{k},0)+e_{0}(0,m_{2}^{l})-e_{0}(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{l})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sigma_{01}\sqrt{\pi m_{1}^{k}}+\frac{\Gamma_{11}(m_{1}^{k})^{2}}{4\pi}+2\sigma_{02}\sqrt{\pi m_{2}^{l}}+\frac{\Gamma_{22}(m_{2}^{l})^{2}}{4\pi}

\displaystyle-\left[2\sigma_{01}\sqrt{\pi(m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{l})}+2\sigma_{12}\sqrt{\pi m_{2}^{l}}+\frac{\Gamma_{11}(m_{1}^{k})^{2}}{4\pi}+\frac{\Gamma_{22}(m_{2}^{l})^{2}}{4\pi}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sigma_{01}\sqrt{\pi}\left[\sqrt{m_{1}^{k}}+\sqrt{m_{2}^{l}}-\sqrt{(m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{l})}\right]>0.

This contradicts with the minimality.
∎

Existence of Core Shells
: Lemma
3.4
implies that for any
M=(M_{1},M_{2}),M_{1},M_{2}>0
, when
\sigma_{02}=\sigma_{01}+\sigma_{12}
and
\Gamma_{12}=0
, any minimizing configuration for
\overline{e_{0}}(M)
must contain at least one generalized core shell.

Remark 3.1
.

In a binary system
[
8
]
have shown that all bubbles are of equal size, by using the concavity of
e_{0}(m)
with respect to the single mass parameter
m
.
In simulations it appears that the same phenomenon should hold for double bubbles and core shells, that all components of a minimizer of the same type are congruent. However, unlike the binary case, in ternary systems (
3.3
) a direct calculation of the Hessian shows it to be indefinite, and so the observed congruence of forms remains an open question.

4
The nonlocal problem in the droplet regime

To see the effects of the nonlocal interaction we consider the Droplet rescaling, as in Choksi-Peletier (
[
8
]
; see also
[
1
,
3
]
.)

4.1
The Droplet Regime

We take a periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
In the remainder of the paper, we assume that
\sigma_{ij}
satisfies (
1.5
), and so the perimeter term may be expressed in terms of total variation norms.
Let
u_{i}=\chi_{\Omega_{i}}
,
i=1,2
. As remarked upon earlier, while we must keep track of the perimeter of the exterior domain
\Omega_{0}=(\overline{\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}})^{c}
, the perimeter term is the same for its complement,
\widetilde{\Omega}_{0}=\overline{\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}}.
Thus, it will be convenient to define

u_{0}=\chi_{\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{2}},

as it yields the same total variation,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u_{0}|=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla(1-u_{1}-u_{2})|=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla\chi_{\Omega_{0}}|.

Thus, the local part of the functional may be expressed in terms of the two “small” sets
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
and the constants
\beta_{i}\geq 0
,
i=0,1,2
as:

\displaystyle P_{\sigma}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})=\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq 2}\sigma_{ij}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega_{i}\cap\partial\Omega_{j})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\beta_{i}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u_{i}|,

with
\beta_{i}
determined by (
1.8
), and so the energy becomes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\beta_{i}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla u_{i}|+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\gamma_{ij}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G(x-y)\;u_{i}(x)\;u_{j}(y)dxdy.

Remark 4.1
.

We note throughout that as long as the triangle inequalities (
1.5
) hold (even with
\beta_{i}=0
in the Core Shell or Single Bubble cases), the weighted perimeter
P_{\sigma}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
is equivalent to the standard norm for the cluster in
BV({\mathbb{T}^{2}};\{0,1\})
. That is, there exists a constant
C=C(\sigma_{ij})
with

{1\over C}P_{\sigma}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})\leq\sum_{i=0}^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}|\nabla\chi_{\Omega_{i}}|\leq CP_{\sigma}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}).

In particular, the compactness and convergence proofs done for the unweighted case
\beta_{i}=1
,
\forall i=0,1,2
, in
[
1
]
may be carried through to the weighted case with straightforward modifications.

As in
[
8
,
1
]
,
we introduce a new parameter
\eta
which is to represent the characteristic length scale of the droplet components. Thus, areas scale as
\eta^{2}
, and so we choose mass constraints on
u=(u_{1},u_{2})
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u_{i}=\eta^{2}M_{i}

for some fixed
M_{i}
,
i=1,2
. We then rescale
u_{i}
as

\displaystyle v_{i,\eta}=\frac{u_{i}}{\eta^{2}},\quad i=0,1,2,\quad\text{with}\quad{\int}_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}v_{i,\eta}=M_{i},\quad i=1,2,

(4.1)

and thus the appropriate space for droplet configurations is:

X_{\eta}:=\left\{v_{\eta}=(v_{1,\eta},v_{2,\eta}):\ \eta^{2}v_{i,\eta}\in BV({\mathbb{T}^{2}};\{0,1\}),i=1,2;\ v_{1,\eta}v_{2,\eta}=0\ a.e.,\ \int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}v_{i,\eta}=M_{i},\;i=1,2\right\},

(4.2)

We recall that we represent the exterior domain equivalently as
v_{0,\eta}=v_{1,\eta}+v_{2,\eta}
.

In terms of
v_{\eta}\in X_{\eta}
, the energy takes the form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\beta_{i}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla v_{i,\eta}|+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\gamma_{ij}\eta^{3}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}v_{i,\eta}(x)v_{j,\eta}(y)dxdy\right)

(4.3)

Finally, we scale the interaction matrix
\gamma=[\gamma_{ij}]
in such a way that both terms in (
4.3
) contribute at the same order in
\eta
. This is accomplished by choosing

\displaystyle\gamma_{ij}=\frac{1}{|\log\eta|\eta^{3}}\Gamma_{ij},

with fixed constants
\Gamma_{ij}\geq 0
.
Dividing by
\eta
, we thus obtain an
O(1)
valued energy for
v_{\eta}\in X_{\eta}
, defined by:

\displaystyle E_{\eta}(v_{\eta}):=\frac{1}{\eta}\mathcal{E}(u)=\frac{\eta}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\beta_{i}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla v_{i,\eta}|+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\ \frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2|\log\eta|}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}G_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(x-y)v_{i,\eta}(x)v_{j,\eta}(y)dxdy,

(4.4)

and
E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})=+\infty
otherwise. This is the droplet scaling of
[
8
]
, which we adopt in the remainder of the paper.

4.2
Expanding the energy in droplets

The advantage of the droplet regime for nonlocal isoperimetric problems is that it separates length scales in the energy, so that both the local isoperimetric effects and the nonlocal repulsive interaction are both observed but at different scales in
\eta
. As a result of this balancing of the strengths of the competing terms, any finte energy configuration
v_{\eta}
decomposes into an at most countable number of well-separated droplets, each of diameter
O(\eta)
.

In the following we assume that
(v_{\eta})_{\eta>0}
are a family in
X_{\eta}
with bounded energy,
\exists C>0
with
E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\leq C
, for all
\eta>0
. Such configurations may be decomposed into indecomposable clusters (that is, connected in a measure-theoretic sense) of diameter of
O(\eta)
, for which the BV norm separates exactly.
As in
[
1
]
we may prove:

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume (
1.5
) holds, and
let
v_{\eta}=\eta^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\eta}}\in X_{\eta}
with
\eta\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}|\nabla v_{\eta}|\leq C
. Then, there exists an at most countable collection
v_{\eta}^{k}=\eta^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\eta}^{k}}\in X_{\eta}
, with clusters
\Omega_{\eta}^{k}=(\Omega_{1,\eta}^{k},\Omega_{2,\eta}^{k})
, such that

(a)

\Omega_{i,\eta}^{k}\cap\Omega_{j,\eta}^{\ell}=\emptyset
, for
k\neq\ell
and
i,j=1,2
.

(b)

v_{\eta}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}v_{\eta}^{k}
in
X_{\eta}
; in particular,

\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}|\nabla v_{i,\eta}|=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}|\nabla v_{i,\eta}^{k}|,\quad i=0,1,2.

(c)

There exists
C>0
with
\diam(\Omega_{\eta}^{k})\leq C\eta
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

Lemma
4.1
was proven in the case of equal weights in
[
1
]
, and the same proof may be employed here. However, it is also a consequence of the more general Decomposition Theorem
[
5
, Theorem 1]
, for finite perimeter sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
; we sketch the argument here for completeness. Indeed, applying the Decomposition Theorem in
[
5
]
to

\Omega_{0,\eta}=\bar{\Omega}_{1,\eta}\cup\bar{\Omega}_{2,\eta}

for each
\eta>0
we obtain an at most countable disjoint collection of finite perimeter sets,

\Omega_{0,\eta}=\bigcup_{k}\Omega_{0,\eta}^{k},

for which each
\Omega_{0,\eta}^{k}
is indecomposable. In two dimensions, the diameter of each indecomposable component is controlled by its perimeter, and so (c) holds for each
\Omega^{k}_{0,\eta}
. Using the
\{\Omega^{k}_{0,\eta}\}
to separate
\Omega_{i,\eta}
into essentially disjoint components
\Omega_{i,\eta}^{k}=\Omega_{i,\eta}\cap\Omega^{k}_{0,\eta}\,
,
i=1,2
, the decomposition of the perimeters (b) then follows. ∎

In order to prove Gamma-convergence results and convergence of minimizers we need to expand the energy in terms of the indecomposable clusters
\Omega_{\eta}^{k}=(\Omega_{1,\eta}^{k},\Omega_{2,\eta}^{k})
. Since the diameter of the support
\Omega_{\eta}^{k}
is small compared to
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
, we may think of each
v_{i,\eta}^{k}
as a function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. This enables us to blow up each component at scale
\eta
to determine the fine structure of the configuration
v_{\eta}
. For each fixed
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
choose any point
\xi_{\eta}^{k}\in\Omega_{0,\eta}^{k}\subset{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
. Then for
i=1,2,
we define a pair

z_{i}^{k}(x):\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
,
i=1,2
, by

\displaystyle z_{i}^{k}(x):=\eta^{2}v_{i,\eta}^{k}(\xi_{\eta}^{k}+\eta x)=\chi_{A_{i,\eta}^{k}}(x),\quad i=1,2,

(4.5)

where
A_{i,\eta}^{k}=\eta^{-1}\left(\Omega_{i,\eta}^{k}-\xi_{\eta}^{k}\right)\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
i=1,2
, with
A^{k}_{\eta}=(A^{k}_{1,\eta},A^{k}_{2,\eta})
a finite perimeter 2-cluster in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}z_{i}^{k}=\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}v_{i,\eta}^{k},\text{ and }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla z_{i}^{k}|=\eta\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}|\nabla v_{i,\eta}^{k}|.

(4.6)

As above, we set
A^{k}_{0,\eta}=\bar{A}^{k}_{1,\eta}\cup\bar{A}^{k}_{2,\eta}\,
, which has the same perimeter as the exterior domain.

For a 2-cluster
A=(A_{1},A_{2})
,
A_{i}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
,
i=1,2
of finite perimeter, and
A_{0}=A_{1}\cup A_{2}
, we define

\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(A):=P_{\sigma}(A)+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{4\pi}|A_{i}|\,|A_{j}|,

and for
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
,

\displaystyle e_{0}(m)

\displaystyle:=\min\left\{\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(A)\ |\ A=(A_{1},A_{2})\text{ 2-cluster, with $|A_{i}|=m_{i}$, $i=1,2$}\right\}

By the results of Section 2, the minimum in
e_{0}(m)
is attained for all
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
nonzero, with the geometry of minimizers determined by the choice of weights
\sigma_{ij}
, a double bubble, core shell, or single bubble (which in particular occurs if either
m_{i}=0
,
i=1
or
2
.)
We expect that the effect of the nonlocal interaction will be to enforce splitting of the mass into several droplets, but that energy minimization will determine the distribution of the droplet masses. To this end, we define

\displaystyle\overline{e_{0}}(M):=\inf\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e_{0}(m^{k}):m^{k}=(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}),\ m_{i}^{k}\geq 0,\ \sum_{k=1}^{\infty}m_{i}^{k}=M_{i},i=1,2\right\},

(4.7)

where
M=(M_{1},M_{2})
.

Expanding
v_{\eta}
into component clusters, and separating the on-diagonal terms in the double sum,

\displaystyle E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\frac{\eta}{2}\beta_{i}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}|\nabla v_{i,\eta}^{k}|+{\Gamma_{ij}\over 2|\log\eta|}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}v_{i,\eta}^{k}(x)\,G_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(x-y)\,v_{j,\eta}^{\ell}(y)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{2}\frac{\beta_{i}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla z_{i}^{k}|+{\Gamma_{ij}\over 2|\log\eta|}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\int_{A_{i}^{k}}\int_{A_{j}^{\ell}}G_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\xi^{k}_{\eta}+\eta\tilde{x}-\xi^{\ell}_{\eta}-\eta\tilde{y})\,d\tilde{x}\,d\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[P_{\sigma}(A^{k}_{\eta})+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}{\Gamma_{ij}\over 4\pi}|A^{k}_{i,\eta}|\,|A^{k}_{j,\eta}|\right]+|\log\eta|^{-1}\Phi_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(A^{k}_{\eta})+|\log\eta|^{-1}\Phi_{\eta},

(4.8)

where the remainder terms include a first piece depending on only the geometry of the indecomposable clusters, and a second piece containing off-diagonal terms which encodes the interactions between them,

\displaystyle\Phi_{\eta}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i,j=1,2}{\Gamma_{ij}\over 2}\int_{A^{k}_{i,\eta}}\int_{A^{k}_{j,\eta}}\left({1\over 2\pi}\log{1\over|x-y|}+R_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}(\eta(x-y))\right)dx\,dy

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k\neq\ell=1}^{\infty}\sum_{i,j=1,2}{\Gamma_{ij}\over 2}\int_{A_{i,\eta}^{k}}\int_{A_{j,\eta}^{\ell}}G_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\xi_{\eta}^{k}+\eta\tilde{x}-\xi_{\eta}^{\ell}-\eta\tilde{y})\,d\tilde{x}\,d\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=:\Phi^{1}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}})+\Phi^{2}_{\eta}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}},\{\xi^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}})

(4.9)

4.3
The first order limit

The decomposition (
4.8
) induced by Lemma
4.1
suggests a first order Gamma limit for the functional
E_{\eta}
, in which the mass splits via
\overline{e}_{0}(M)
, encoding the geometric information from the weighted isoperimetric energy
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}
.

We define a class of measures with countable support on
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
,

Y:=\left\{v_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k})\,\delta_{x^{k}}\ |\ m_{i}^{k}\geq 0,\ x^{k}\in{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\ \text{distinct points}\right\},

and a functional on
Y
,

\displaystyle E_{0}(v_{0}):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\overline{e_{0}}(m^{k}),&&\text{ if }v_{0}\in Y,\\
\infty,&&\text{ otherwise. }\end{array}\right.

As in
[
1
]
, we have a first Gamma-convergence result:

Theorem 4.2

(First
\Gamma
-Limit)
.

We have

\displaystyle E_{\eta}\overset{\Gamma}{\longrightarrow}E_{0}\ \text{ as }\ \eta\rightarrow 0.

That is,

(a)

Let
v_{\eta}\in X_{\eta}
be a sequence with
\sup_{\eta>0}E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})<\infty
. Then there exists a subsequence
\eta\to 0
and
v_{0}\in Y
such that

v_{\eta}\rightharpoonup v_{0}
(in the weak topology of the space of measures), and

\displaystyle\underset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{\lim\inf}E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\geq E_{0}(v_{0}).

(b)

Let
v_{0}\in Y
with
E_{0}(v_{0})<\infty
. Then there exists a sequence
v_{\eta}\rightharpoonup v_{0}
weakly as measures, such that

\displaystyle\underset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{\lim\sup}E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\leq E_{0}(v_{0}).

As the proof is very similar to that of
[
1
, Theorem 3.2]
, we provide only a sketch here.
For the lower semicontinuity result (a), we take any sequence
(v_{\eta})_{\eta>0}
with uniformly bounded energy
E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\leq C
, and
apply
[
1
, Lemma 3.5]
. Note that the proof of Lemma 3.5 of
[
1
]
, and of all the convergence results in that paper, were given for the case of equal weights
\beta_{i}=1
,
i=0,1,2
, but the proofs are identical for the weighted case, as long as the triangle inequalities (
1.5
) hold. It states that, up to a subsequence
\eta\to 0
(not relabelled,) there exist at most countably many 2-clusters
\{A^{k}\}
in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}
and corresponding points
\{x_{\eta}^{k}\}
in
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
, such that the domain

\Omega_{\eta}=\bigcup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\Omega_{\eta}^{k}
(with indecomposable clusters
\Omega_{\eta}^{k}
,) satisfies:

\left|A^{k}\ \triangle\,\left(\eta^{-1}\left[\Omega_{\eta}^{k}-x_{\eta}^{k}\right]\right)\right|=\left|A^{k}\ \triangle\,A^{k}_{\eta}\right|\xrightarrow{\eta\rightarrow 0}0,\qquad\forall k.

(4.13)

Moreover, the masses of the clusters are preserved in the limit,

M_{i}=\lim_{\eta\to 0}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\eta^{-2}|\Omega_{i,\eta}^{k}|=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|A_{i}^{k}|,\quad i=1,2,

(4.14)

and the total energy is bounded below by
\overline{e_{0}}(M)
.

\liminf_{\eta\to 0}E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\geq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}(A^{k})\geq\overline{e_{0}}(M).

(4.15)

At this point, note that (
4.15
) is the lower bound of the first Gamma convergence result, Theorem
4.2
. The remainder of the Gamma convergence then follows the proof of Theorem 3.2 in
[
1
]
.

The geometrical structure can be made more precise for minimizers: the limiting clusters after blow-up must minimize the weighted perimeter functional
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}
with the given mass distribution imposed by
\overline{e_{0}}(M)
. Indeed, it is enough to assume that
E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\to\overline{e_{0}}(M)
to obtain this conclusion:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
v_{\eta}^{\ast}=\eta^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\eta}}\in X_{\eta}
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{\eta\to 0}E_{\eta}=\overline{e_{0}}(M)
. Then, there exists a subsequence
\eta\to 0
(still denoted by
\eta
) such that:

(i)

there exist connected clusters
(A^{k})_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}
and points
x_{\eta}^{k}\in{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, for which

\eta^{-2}\left|\Omega_{\eta}\ \triangle\ \bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\eta A^{k}+x_{\eta}^{k}\right)\right|\xrightarrow{\eta\rightarrow 0}0,

(4.16)

and

M_{i}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|A_{i}^{k}|;

(ii)

each
A^{k}
is a minimizer of
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}
:

\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(A^{k})=e_{0}(m^{k}),\qquad m^{k}=(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k})=|A^{k}|;

(4.17)

Moreover,

\overline{e_{0}}(M)=\lim_{\eta\to 0}E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(A^{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}e_{0}(m^{k}).

(4.18)

(iii)

If
k\neq\ell
,
d(x^{k}_{\eta},x^{\ell}_{\eta})\gg\eta
; more precisely,

\lim_{\eta\to 0}{|\log d(x^{k}_{\eta},x^{\ell}_{\eta})|\over|\log\eta|}=0,\quad\forall k\neq\ell.

Remark 4.2
.

In Corollary
3.2
we prove that in the core shell case
\beta_{1}=0
, minimizers of
\overline{e_{0}}(M)
may only have finitely many nontrivial clusters,
A^{1},\dots,A^{K}
, for some
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
. This was also the case in the unweighted case, and in the cases of single bubbles (
\beta_{0}=0
) as proven in
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
.

The proof of Proposition
4.3
follows exactly those of Lemmas 3.5 and 3.6 in
[
1
]
(recalling from Remark
4.1
that the weighted perimeter controls the relevant BV norms in the convergence steps.)

4.4
Second order limit

To obtain the Second Gamma Limit, Theorem
4.5
, and the more detailed description of limiting configurations, we must analyze the remainder term in (
4.9
). As the analysis in the case of strict triangle inequalities (
1.5
) (leading to minimizers with double bubbles) or in case
\beta_{0}=0
(yielding only single bubbles) is essentially identical to the unweighted case of our previous paper
[
1
]
, we will concentrate on the case
\beta_{1}=0
, in which core shells are favored.

We subtract the first
\Gamma
-limit from
E_{\eta}
, which (from the decomposition (
4.8
), (
4.9
)) we expect to have scale
|\log\eta|^{-1}
.
For
v_{\eta}\in X_{\eta}
, let

\displaystyle F_{\eta}(v_{\eta}):=|\log\eta|\left[E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})-\overline{e_{0}}(M)\right].

(4.19)

For the second
\Gamma
-limit we consider
v_{\eta}\in X_{\eta}
for which
F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})
is bounded. For these
v_{\eta}
, Proposition
4.3
applies, and we may thus assume that (along subsequences) the detailed concentration structure described by (
4.13
), (
4.14
), (
4.15
) is observed. Given Lemma
2.6
, each limiting cluster
A^{k}=(A^{k}_{1},A^{k}_{2})
is either a core shell
\mathcal{C}^{m^{k}_{1}}_{m^{k}_{2}}
(if
m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}>0
,) or a single bubble (in case one of
m_{i}^{k}=0
.) We recall that minimization of
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}
does not determine the centers of core shells; this will be addressed below.

Next we define the second order limit function, starting with its domain, which is suggested from the first order limit Theorem
4.2
and the structure described in Proposition
4.3
.
For
K\in\mathbb{N}
,
m_{1}^{k}\geq 0
,
m_{2}^{k}\geq 0
and
m_{1}^{k}+m_{2}^{k}>0
, the sequence
K\otimes(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k})
is defined by

\displaystyle(K\otimes(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}))^{k}:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}),&1\leq k\leq K,\\
&(0,0),&K+1\leq k<\infty.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
{\cal{M}}_{M}
be the set of optimal sequences made of all clusters for the problem (
4.7
):

\displaystyle{\cal{M}}_{M}:=\Bigg\{K\otimes(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k}):K\otimes(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k})\text{ minimizes \eqref{mine0} for }M_{i},i=1,2,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\ \overline{e_{0}}(m^{k})=e_{0}(m^{k}),\ m^{k}=(m_{1}^{k},m_{2}^{k})\Bigg\}.

Let
Y_{M}
denote the space of all measures
v_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}m^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}
with
\{x^{1},\dots,x^{K}\}
distinct points in
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
and
K\otimes m^{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{M}
. That is,
v_{0}\in Y_{M}
represents the limit (in the sense of distributions) of a sequence of energy minimizers
v_{\eta}
of mass
M
, which obey the conclusions of Proposition
4.3
. We recall that the finiteness of these components is a consequence of Corollary
3.2
.

We now describe the terms appearing in the second
\Gamma
-limit, which arise from passage to the limit in
\Phi_{\eta}
, defined in (
4.9
). First, the “self-interaction” terms making up
\Phi^{1}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}})
. For
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
,
m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0
and not both zero, define

f_{0}(m)=\inf\biggl\{\sum_{i,j=1,2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy+m_{i}\,m_{j}\,R_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}(0)\right]\ :\ \\
\ \ A=(A_{1},A_{2})\ \text{minimizes $\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}$ with $|A_{\ell}|=m_{\ell}$, $\ell=1,2$}\biggr\}

(4.20)

The minimization here is only pertinent in case
m_{1},m_{2}>0
, that is, where
A=\mathcal{C}^{m_{1}}_{m_{2}}
is a core shell; for single bubbles the minimizers of
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}
with one of
m_{i}=0
are unique up to rigid motion, and the set above is a singleton. It is in this term that energy minimization of the nonlocal energy resolves the degeneracy in the local isoperimetric problem, in favor of
centered
core shells:

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\beta_{1}=0
, and
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
with
m_{1},m_{2}>0
.

(a)

If
\Gamma_{11}>\Gamma_{12}
, then the minimum in
f_{0}(m)
is attained by a concentric core shell
A=\mathcal{C}^{m_{1}}_{m_{2}}
.

(b)

If
\Gamma_{11}<\Gamma_{12}
, then the minimum in
f_{0}(m)
is attained by a core shell
A=\mathcal{C}^{m_{1}}_{m_{2}}
whose inner boundary circle is tangent to the outside circle.

Proof.

Given
m_{1},m_{2}>0
, from Lemma
2.6
we know that all minimizers of
\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}
with
m_{1},m_{2}>0
are core shells, that is they are composed of two (round) balls
B_{1}=B_{r_{1}}(0)
,
B_{2}=B_{r_{2}}(p)
, with
r_{1}>r_{2}
, and the outer domain
A_{1}=B_{1}\setminus B_{2}
, with inner disk
A_{2}=B_{2}
. The distance between
p
(the center of
B_{2}
) and
0
must be small enough that
A_{2}\subset A_{1}
. We must choose
p
to minimize the quantity

\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}\left[I_{ij}+m_{i}m_{j}R_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}(0)\right],\quad\text{where}\quad I_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy.

Notice that
I_{22}
is independent of the location of
p
, as are of course the terms
m_{i}m_{j}R_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}(0)
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{2}
, by the Mean Value Theorem for harmonic functions,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dy=|B_{2}|\log{1\over|x-p|}.

Thus,

\displaystyle I_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{1}\setminus B_{2}}\log{1\over|x-p|}dx=\frac{m_{2}}{2\pi}\left[\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-p|}dx-\int_{B_{2}}\log{1\over|x-p|}dx\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{2\pi}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-p|}dx-I_{22}.

We claim that
I_{12}=I_{12}(p)
is maximized when
p=0
and minimized when the circles are tangent. By symmetry we may assume
p=(t,0)
, with
t\geq 0
lying in an interval
t\in[0,\alpha]
for which
A_{2}\subset A_{1}
. Differentiating,

{d\over dt}I_{12}(t,0)={1\over 2\pi}\int_{B_{1}}{x_{1}-t\over|x-(t,0)|^{2}}dx.

(4.21)

Let
\omega_{t}=\{x\in B_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}1}}\ |\ x_{1}\geq t\}
, and
\tilde{\omega}_{t}
its reflection in the line
\{x_{1}=t\}
. As the integrand in (
4.21
) is odd around the line
\{x_{1}=t\}
, and

B_{1}\setminus(\omega_{t}\cup\tilde{\omega}_{t})\subset\{x_{1}<t\}
, we have
\forall t\in(0,\alpha]
,

{d\over dt}I_{12}(t,0)={1\over 2\pi}\int_{B_{1}\setminus(\omega_{t}\cup\tilde{\omega}_{t})}{x_{1}-t\over|x-(t,0)|^{2}}dx<0.

Thus,
I_{12}(t,0)
is even and strictly monotone decreasing in
t\in[0,\alpha]
, so it attains its maximum value at
t=0
and minimum at the extreme value
t=\alpha
, where the circles are tangent. This concludes the proof of the claim.

To complete the argument, we note that,

\displaystyle I_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{1}}\int_{A_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy-I_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-I_{12}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\left[\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy-\int_{B_{2}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2I_{12}+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy-I_{22}.

As
I_{22}
and
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy
are independent of the location of
p
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}I_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{11}}{2}(-2I_{12}(p))+\Gamma_{12}I_{12}(p)+\text{ terms independent of }p

(4.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\Gamma_{11}-\Gamma_{12})I_{12}(p)+\text{ terms independent of }p.

The stated conclusions then follow, depending on the sign of the coefficient
(\Gamma_{11}-\Gamma_{12})
.
∎

We note that in the case
\Gamma_{11}<\Gamma_{12}
it may be difficult to observe tangential core shells, since that choice of strong repulsion between the two phases may result in core shells being split into single bubbles.

With this satisfactory resolution of the question of the specific geometry of optimal core shells, we may now define the second
\Gamma
-limit functional, by including the formal limits of the two remainder terms from (
4.9
).
For
v_{0}\in Y_{M}
, we define

F_{0}(v_{0})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}f_{0}(m^{k})+\sum_{k,\ell=1\atop k\neq\ell}^{K}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}m_{i}^{k}m_{j}^{\ell}G_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(x_{i}^{k}-x_{j}^{\ell}),

(4.23)

and
F_{0}(v_{0})=+\infty
otherwise. The first term in (
4.23
) is thus already completely determined by the first
\Gamma
-limit and the fine structure of core shells as determined by Proposition
4.4
, and only the spatial distribution of droplets is at play in
F_{0}
.

Theorem 4.5

(Second
\Gamma
-limit)
.

We have

\displaystyle F_{\eta}\overset{\Gamma}{\longrightarrow}F_{0}\ \text{ as }\ \eta\rightarrow 0.

That is,

(a)

Let
v_{\eta}\in X_{\eta}
be a sequence with
\sup_{\eta>0}F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})<\infty
. Then there exists a subsequence
\eta\to 0
and
v_{0}\in Y_{M}
such that

v_{\eta}\rightharpoonup v_{0}
(in the weak topology of the space of measures), and

\displaystyle\underset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{\lim\inf}F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\geq F_{0}(v_{0}).

(b)

Let
v_{0}\in Y_{M}
with
F_{0}(v_{0})<\infty
. Then there exists a sequence
v_{\eta}\rightharpoonup v_{0}
weakly as measures, such that

\displaystyle\underset{\eta\rightarrow 0}{\lim\sup}F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\leq F_{0}(v_{0}).

The tools required for the proof of the second limit are essentially included in our paper
[
1
]
, in the unweighted case. We provide a slightly more detailed sketch here for the case
\beta_{1}=0
, as the paper
[
1
]
concentrated on the first
\Gamma
-limit and the structure of minimizers and did not give details for the second
\Gamma
-limit.

Sketch of the proof of Theorem
4.5
.

We begin with compactness and the lower limit. Take a sequence
\eta_{n}\to 0
and assume
v_{\eta_{n}}\in X_{\eta}
is a sequence with bounded
F_{\eta_{n}}(v_{\eta_{n}})
. (Recall
F_{\eta}
is defined in (
4.19
).) For convenience, we abuse notation and denote this sequence (and all forthcoming subsequences) simply as
v_{\eta}
, and
\eta\to 0
along this sequence (and eventually, certain subsequences.) By the boundedness of
F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})
we immediately conclude that

\lim_{\eta\to 0}E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})=\overline{e_{0}}(M),

and hence Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
4.3
(Lemma 3.5 in
[
1
]
) apply to the sequence
v_{\eta}
. As a consequence,
v_{\eta}=\eta^{-2}\chi_{\Omega_{\eta}}
splits into at most countably many indecomposable clusters,
\Omega_{\eta}=\bigcup_{k}\Omega_{\eta}^{k}
, whose energy
E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})
breaks down via (
4.8
). Moreover, by Corollary
3.2
in the core shell case
\beta_{1}=0
minimizers of
\overline{e_{0}}(M)
can only have a finite number
K\in{\mathbb{N}}
of nontrivial components. Hence,

\displaystyle E_{\eta}(v_{\eta})-\overline{e_{0}}(M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{G}_{\sigma}(A^{k}_{\eta})-\overline{e_{0}}(M)+|\log\eta|^{-1}\Phi_{\eta}(A_{\eta}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}k}},\xi^{k}_{\eta})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{K}e_{0}(|A^{k}_{\eta}|)-\overline{e_{0}}(M)+|\log\eta|^{-1}\Phi_{\eta}(A^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{k}_{\eta})

\displaystyle\geq|\log\eta|^{-1}\Phi_{\eta}(A^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{k}_{\eta}),

since
\overline{e_{0}}(M)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}e_{0}(m^{k})
whenever
\sum_{k=1}^{K}m^{k}=M
. That is,
\Phi_{\eta}(A^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{k}_{\eta})\leq F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\leq C
are uniformly bounded as
\eta\to 0
.

Next, we pass to the limit in
\Phi^{1}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}})
in (
4.9
). By (
4.16
), for each
k
, the clusters
|A^{k}_{\eta}\triangle A^{k}|\to 0
. In addition, by Lemma
4.1
(c), the diameters diam
\,{A^{k}_{\eta}}\leq C
are uniformly bounded in
k,\eta
. As
\log{1\over|x-y|}
is locally integrable, we may then pass to the limit using dominated convergence,

\lim_{\eta\to 0}\Phi^{1}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}})=\sum_{i,j=1,2}\frac{\Gamma_{ij}}{2}\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{A_{i}}\int_{A_{j}}\log{1\over|x-y|}dx\,dy+m_{i}\,m_{j}\,R_{{\mathbb{T}^{2}}}(0)\right]\geq\sum_{k=1}^{K}f_{0}(m^{k}).

(4.24)

Since
\Phi^{1}
converges, we conclude that the second interaction term
\Phi^{2}_{\eta}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}},\{\xi^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}})
is also bounded above. To pass to the limit in this term, we first note that if for some pair
k\neq\ell
,
d(\xi^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{\ell}_{\eta})\geq\delta_{k,\ell}>0
for all
\eta>0
, then by a similar argument as above we may pass to the limit in this
k,\ell
term and obtain the corresponding term in
F_{0}
(see (
4.23
).) We claim that this must be the case for all pairs
k\neq\ell
. Indeed, we assume for some
k\neq\ell
(and a subsequence
\eta\to 0
that
r_{\eta}^{k,\ell}:=d(\xi^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{\ell}_{\eta})\to 0
, then recall that by Proposition
4.3
(iii) we must nevertheless have
for
i,j=1,2
,

\displaystyle\int_{A_{i,\eta}^{k}}\int_{A_{j,\eta}^{\ell}}G_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\xi_{\eta}^{k}+\eta\tilde{x}-\xi_{\eta}^{\ell}-\eta\tilde{y})\,d\tilde{x}\,d\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{i,\eta}^{k}}\int_{A_{j,\eta}^{\ell}}\left[{1\over 2\pi}\log{1\over|\xi^{k}_{\eta}-\xi^{\ell}_{\eta}|}+R_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(\xi_{\eta}^{k}+\eta\tilde{x}-\xi_{\eta}^{\ell}-\eta\tilde{y})\right]d\tilde{x}\,d\tilde{y}

\displaystyle=|A_{i,\eta}^{k}||A_{j,\eta}^{\ell}|\left({1\over 2\pi}|\log r^{k,\ell}_{\eta}|+R_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}(0)+o(1)\right).

Since
|A^{k}|>0
for
k=1,\dots,K
, there exists
i,j\in\{1,2\}
for which
|A^{k}_{i}||A^{\ell}_{j}|>0
, and that term will be unbounded above. This contradicts the upper bound on
\Phi^{2}_{\eta}(\{A^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}},\{\xi^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}})
, and hence we conclude that the points
\{\xi^{k}_{\eta}\}_{k=1,\dots,K}
must remain distinct as
\eta\to 0
. Passing to a further subsequence if necessary, we may assume
\xi^{k}_{\eta}\to x^{k}\in{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
, with distinct
x^{k}\neq x^{\ell}
,
k\neq\ell
, and

\liminf_{\eta\to 0}F_{\eta}(v_{\eta})\geq\liminf_{\eta\to 0}\Phi_{\eta}(A^{k}_{\eta},\xi^{k}_{\eta})\geq F_{0}(v_{0}),

where
v_{0}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}m^{k}\delta_{x^{k}}\in Y_{M}
.
Note that (repeating an argument from above) by (
4.16
) and the uniform boundedness of the diameters of
A^{k}_{\eta}
, we may also conclude that
v_{\eta}\rightharpoonup v_{0}
in the weak topology on the space of measures on
{\mathbb{T}^{2}}
.
This concludes the proof of the compactness and lower limit of the second
\Gamma
-limit.

The construction of a recovery sequence for part 2 is straightforward: given points
x^{k}
and masses
m^{k}=(m^{k}_{1},m^{k}_{2})
, it suffices to assemble a superposition of
\eta
-rescaled minimizers of
e_{0}(m^{k})
at each
x^{k}
. When the minimizing cluster
A^{k}
is a core shell, the central disk is positioned
centered when
\Gamma_{12}
is small according to Proposition
4.4
.∎

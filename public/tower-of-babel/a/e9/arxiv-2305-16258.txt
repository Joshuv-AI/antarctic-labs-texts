Tree independence number I. (Even hole, diamond, pyramid)-free graphs
Tara Abrishami ∗†, Bogdan Alecu ∗∗¶, Maria Chudnovsky ∗∗∗∐, Sepehr Hajebi §, Sophie Spirkl §∥, Kristina Vušković ∗∗‡

Abstract
. The tree-independence number tree − α \ta , first defined and studied by Dallard, Milanič and Štorgel, is a variant of treewidth tailored to solving the maximum independent set problem. Over a series of papers, Abrishami et al. developed the so-called central bag method to study induced obstructions to bounded treewidth. Among others, they showed that, in a certain superclass 𝒞 \mathcal{C} of (even hole, diamond, pyramid)-free graphs, treewidth is bounded by a function of the clique number. In this paper, we relax the bounded clique number assumption, and show that 𝒞 \mathcal{C} has bounded tree − α \ta . Via existing results, this yields a polynomial-time algorithm for the Maximum Weight Independent Set problem in this class. Our result also corroborates, for this class of graphs, a conjecture of Dallard, Milanič and Štorgel that in a hereditary graph class, tree − α \ta is bounded if and only if the treewidth is bounded by a function of the clique number.

The tree independence number was defined and studied by Dallard, Milanič and Štorgel in

(

A class

Further context

First, observe that the class
\mathcal{C}
was already known to be
(\tw,\omega)
-bounded
[
7
]
, so Theorem
1.2
corroborates Conjecture
1.1
for this class. Although many of the ingredients of our proof are class-specific, it is worth investigating whether any of our methods can be used for tackling the conjecture in general.

The second consideration is an algorithmic one. Even-hole-free graphs have been studied extensively since the 1990s, and yet despite their structural similarity to perfect graphs, the complexity of some fundamental
optimization problems that are solvable in polynomial time for perfect graphs is not known for even-hole-free graphs. Indeed, while we can find maximum cliques in even-hole-free graphs efficiently by a result shown by Farber
[
23
]
and later Alekseev
[
9
]
stating that
C_{4}
-free graphs have quadratically many maximal cliques, we still do not have efficient algorithms for the MWIS, minimum vertex coloring, and minimum clique cover problems.

In this direction, a number of subclasses of even-hole-free graphs have been studied: for instance, in
[
26
]
it was shown that (even hole, diamond)-free graphs can be colored in polynomial time. A sketch of that proof is as follows. First, a decomposition theorem is obtained for (even hole, diamond)-free graphs: these graphs are decomposed by star cutsets that partition into two cliques, and by 2-joins, into line graphs of a tree plus at most two vertices. This decomposition theorem is used to show that (even hole, diamond)-free graphs always have a vertex that is either simplicial or of degree 2. This then implies that the class is “
\beta
-perfect,” which in turn yields a polynomial time coloring algorithm which proceeds by coloring greedily on a particular, easily constructible
ordering of the vertices.

Our current paper continues this study by investigating the MWIS problem, in the hope of extending the methods presented here to (even hole, diamond)-free graphs (see Conjecture
1.6
).
We now survey some subclasses of even-hole-free graphs in relation to the MWIS
problem. Recall that even-hole-free graphs are (
C_{4}
, theta, prism, even wheel)-free.

A superclass of (even hole, pyramid)-free graphs

In
[
5
]
, a superclass of (even hole, pyramid)-free graphs is studied, namely the class of (
C_{4}
, theta, prism, pyramid, turtle)-free graphs
(where a
turtle
is a graph derived from a wheel that cannot be present in even-hole-free graphs, or in fact, in even-wheel-free graphs). This yields, in particular, a polynomial-time algorithm for MWIS in our class
\mathcal{C}
. However, their method (which we outline below) does not generalize beyond the pyramid-free setting. In contrast, we believe the method we present in this paper extends to the larger class of (even hole, diamond)-free graphs.

To understand why the results from
[
5
]
do not generalize, we outline their procedure for solving MWIS here. It is shown in
[
5
]
that the class under study has polynomially many minimal separators, and that the set of all minimal separators can be constructed in polynomial time.

The number of minimal separators of a graph is related to the number of potential maximal cliques (PMCs) introduced by Bouchitté and Todinca in

[
12
,
13
]
.
For a graph
G
, denote by

the number of vertices,

the number of edges,

the number of minimal separators, and

the number of PMCs in

In
[
13
]
, it is proved that

\mathcal{O}(ns^{2}+ns+1)
, and that given the list of minimal separators, the PMCs of

can be listed in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{2}ms^{2})
.
In
[
29
]
, based on
[
24
]
, it is proved that given the list of PMCs, the MWIS problem can be solved in

\mathcal{O}(n^{5}mp)
.
So, summing everything up, the ability to list all minimal separators in polynomial time implies a polynomial-time algorithm for the MWIS problem.

On the other hand, if pyramids are allowed in even-hole-free graphs, the polynomial minimal separator property is lost, as witnessed for example by a
k
-pyramid
(a graph
consisting of a clique
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\}
, a vertex

and paths

, for

, such that any 3 of them induce a pyramid).

A subclass of (even hole, pyramid)-free graphs

The class of (even hole,
K_{3}
)-free graphs is a proper subclass of (even hole, pyramid)-free graphs.
In

it is shown that (even hole,

On the other hand, in

The question remains whether the following is true (it can be shown that the outcome holds when forbidding, in addition, another family of graphs, called “generalized

Conjecture 1.7

The class of (even hole,

2.
Tools

In this section, we describe the tools and preliminary results needed to prove that graphs in
\mathcal{C}
have bounded tree-

2.1.
\chi
-boundedness of the complements and the tree-clique cover number

Many of the results from
[
7
]
that we use provide bounds on the size of certain sets in terms of
\omega
, but in actuality the proofs work by exhibiting clique covers of those sets. Correspondingly, our arguments revolve around clique covers rather than stable sets. As such, it is natural to define the
tree-clique cover number

\tc(G)
of a graph
G
analogously to
\ta
. More explicitly, we define the clique cover number of a tree decomposition
(T,\varphi)
as the maximum value of
\overline{\chi}(G[\varphi(t)])
over nodes
t\in V(T)
, and we let
\tc(G)
be the minimum value of the clique cover number over all tree decompositions of
G
.

We note that, for
C_{4}
-free graphs, statements about
\tc
have
\ta
analogues, and vice-versa. Indeed, in one direction,
\alpha(G)\leq\overline{\chi}(G)
for any graph

. In the other direction, by a result of Wagon
[
34
]
, the class of

-free graphs is

-bounded, meaning that there exists a non-decreasing function
f:\mathbb{N}^{+}\to\mathbb{N}^{+}
such that, for any

-free graph

,
\chi(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
. In fact, from
[

]
, one may take

to be

. We record this in a lemma, noting that the complement of a

-free graph

is

-free:

Lemma 2.1

(
[
34
]
)
.

Let
G
be a
C_{4}
-free graph. Then
\overline{\chi}(G)\leq{\alpha(G)+1\choose 2}
.

The above discussion immediately yields the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a
C_{4}
-free graph. Then
\ta(G)\leq\tc(G)\leq{\ta(G)+1\choose 2}
.

We remark that

-freeness (or at least,
\chi
-boundedness in the complement) is necessary to obtain a relationship between
\tc
and
\ta
:

Remark 2.3
.

For any

, there exists a graph with
\ta\leq 2
and
\tc\geq C
.

Proof.

It is pointed out in
[
21
]
that, in any tree decomposition
(T,\varphi)
of a graph

and a node

with

; in particular, if we start with a graph

and construct

from two copies of

by adding all possible edges between them, for any tree decomposition of

. We can use this to construct an example as claimed. Start with any triangle-free graph

with chromatic number greater than

(such graphs are well-known to exist); take its complement

, and construct the graph

as above. The resulting graph will have

∎

Let

be a
C_{4}
-free graph, let
c\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, and let
k
be a positive integer. Suppose that for every weight function
w:V(G)\to[0,1]
with
w(G)=1
,
G
has a
(w,c)
-balanced separator with clique cover number at most
k
. Then there exists a number
g(k,c)
such that
\tc(G)\leq g(k,c)
.

Proof.

We adapt the proof from
[
30
]
.
Put
h(k,c):=\lceil 2k/(1-c)\rceil
, and
g(k,c):={h(k,c)+1\choose 2}+k
. We claim that
g(k,c)
satisfies the statement of the lemma. In fact, we will recursively show the following slightly stronger statement:

(*)

Let
(G,W)
be a pair where
G
is as in the lemma, and

has
\overline{\chi}(W)\leq{h(k,c)+1\choose 2}
. Then there exists a tree decomposition
(T,\varphi)
of
G
of clique cover number at most
g(k,c)
, and such that

for some

. In particular,
\tc(G)\leq g(k,c)
.

The statement (*) is clear if
\alpha(G)\leq h(k,c)
. Indeed, in that case, Lemma
2.1
yields that a tree decomposition consisting of a single bag does the job.

If
\alpha(G)>h(k,c)
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\alpha(W)\geq h(k,c)
(otherwise, we simply add vertices to it arbitrarily until
\alpha(W)=h(k,c)
, and by Lemma

, we do not lose the property that
\overline{\chi}(W)\leq{h(k,c)+1\choose 2}
in doing so). We then select a stable subset

of
W
with
|W^{\prime}|=h(k,c)
, and define a weight function
w:V(G)\to[0,1]
by putting
w(x):=\frac{1}{h(k,c)}
for each
x\in W^{\prime}
, and
w(x):=0
for all other

. By assumption,
G
has a
(w,c)
-balanced separator

with
\overline{\chi}(X)\leq k
.

Now for any component

of

, we have
w(D)\leq c
, and so there are at least
(1-c)h(k,c)\geq 2k
vertices of
W^{\prime}
outside of

. In particular, since
\alpha(X)\leq\overline{\chi}(X)\leq k
, there are
k
vertices of
W^{\prime}
, say

, which do not belong to
D\cup X
.

Let us now pick a clique cover of

with at most
{h(k,c)+1\choose 2}
cliques, and consider the set

. By the above, the (distinct) cliques containing

do not intersect

, so
\overline{\chi}(Y)\leq{h(k,c)+1\choose 2}-k
. This shows
\overline{\chi}(Y\cup X)\leq\overline{\chi}(Y)+\overline{\chi}(X)\leq{h(k,c)+1\choose 2}
, and we may recursively find a tree decomposition of

by applying (*) to the pair
(D\cup X,Y\cup X)
(noting that

is a smaller graph).

We do the above for each component

of

, defining

analogously to

; we obtain, for each

, a tree decomposition
(T_{i},\varphi_{i})
of

with a node
t_{i}\in T_{i}
such that
Y_{i}\cup X\subseteq\varphi_{i}(t_{i})
. We then assemble those tree decompositions into a decomposition
(T,\varphi)
of
G
with the desired property by starting with the union of the
T_{i}
’s and adding single vertex

adjacent to all

’s, and with
\varphi(t)=W\cup X
(and with
\varphi
restricting to each
\varphi_{i}
on the corresponding

). Note that
\overline{\chi}(G[\varphi(t)])=\overline{\chi}(W\cup X)\leq\overline{\chi}(W)+\overline{\chi}(X)\leq g(k,c)
. Inductively, this is also true of every other bag. This yields a tree decomposition of
G
(see Fact 2.8 from page 111 of
[
30
]
).

∎

A

, and

if there exists

. Let

We note the following result from

Lemma 2.13

(

]
)

2.5.
Central bags

Let
G
be a graph and let
w:V(G)\to[0,1]
be a weight function on
G
with
w(G)=1
. We call a collection
\mathcal{S}
of separations of

smooth
if the following hold:

(i)

S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are nearly non-crossing for all distinct
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathcal{S}
;

(ii)

There is a set of unbalanced vertices

such that there is a bijection
f
from
v(\mathcal{S})
to
\mathcal{S}
with
v\in C(f(v))\subseteq N[v]
and
A(f(v))\subseteq A_{v}
for each
v\in v(\mathcal{S})
;

(iii)

v(\mathcal{S})\cap A(S)=\emptyset
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a smooth collection of separations of
G
. Then, the
central bag for
\mathcal{S}
, denoted
\beta_{\mathcal{S}}
, is defined as follows:

\beta_{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcap_{S\in\mathcal{S}}(B(S)\cup C(S)).

By property (
iii
) of smooth collections of separations, it holds that
v(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\beta_{\mathcal{S}}
. Also note that
G\setminus\beta_{\mathcal{S}}=\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}A(S)
. Moreover, by property (
i
), every component
D
of
G\setminus\beta_{\mathcal{S}}
is contained in
A(f(v_{i}))
for some
i
(to see this, we consider a maximal subset of
D
belonging to a single
A(f(v_{i}))
, and use (
i
) to extend the subset if it does not equal the whole of

, contradicting maximality).

We next define an
inherited weight function

w_{\mathcal{S}}
on
\beta_{\mathcal{S}}
by recording the weight of each component of
G\setminus\beta_{\mathcal{S}}
into a unique vertex
v_{i}\in\beta_{\mathcal{S}}
. To this end, we start by fixing an ordering
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
of
v(\mathcal{S})
. For every
f(v_{i})\in\mathcal{S}
, let
A^{*}(f(v_{i}))
be the union of all connected components

of
\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq k}A(f(v_{j}))
such that
D\subseteq A(f(v_{i}))
, and
D\not\subseteq A(f(v_{j}))
for every
j<i
. In particular,
(A^{*}(f(v_{1})),\ldots,A^{*}(f(v_{k})))
is a partition of
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}A(S)
. For a component
D
of
\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}}A(S)
, we call the unique
v_{i}
with

the
anchor
of
D
. In other words, the anchor of
D
is the first
v_{i}
in our ordering with
D\subseteq A(f(v_{i}))
. We define
w_{\mathcal{S}}:V(\beta_{\mathcal{S}})\to[0,1]
by
w_{\mathcal{S}}(v_{i})=w(v_{i})+w(A^{*}(f(v_{i})))
for all

, and
w_{\mathcal{S}}(v)=w(v)
for all
v\not\in v(\mathcal{S})
.

(

such that

is complete to

(

Let

2.7.
Trisimplicial elimination orderings

A vertex
v
of a graph
G
is called
simplicial
if its neighborhood is a clique,
bisimplicial
if there exist cliques
K_{1},K_{2}
of
G
such that
N(v)=K_{1}\cup K_{2}
, and
trisimplicial
if there exist cliques
K_{1},K_{2},K_{3}
of
G
such that
N(v)=K_{1}\cup K_{2}\cup K_{3}
.

A central ingredient in our argument is the fact that the graphs we consider admit elimination orderings
v_{1},\dots,v_{|V(G)|}
of their vertex sets such that for each
i
,
N(v_{i})\cap\{v_{i},v_{i+1},\dots,v_{|V(G)|}\}
is the union of a bounded number of cliques (in particular, bisimplicial or trisimplicial elimination orderings qualify).

It is worth pointing out that every even-hole-free graph has a bisimplicial vertex
[
15
]
, and as such admits a bisimplicial elimination ordering. This gives us hope that it might be possible to generalize the results of this paper in order to obtain a polynomial algorithm for MWIS in the whole class of even-hole-free graphs. However, for the purposes of this paper, we would like to derive and use an analogous result for the class
\mathcal{C}
. It is not clear how to adapt the proof from
[
15
]
to this setting; nonetheless, there is another result from
[
26
]
that one may use as a basis for what we need here:

Theorem 2.17
.

(
[
26
]
)
Every (even hole, diamond)-free graph has a vertex which is either simplicial or of degree 2.

We note that this result is in a sense orthogonal to the one from
[
15
]
: it requires the additional condition of diamond-freeness, and yields a stronger outcome than just a bisimplicial vertex. While the proof of this result does generalize to the class
\mathcal{C}
(and in fact to the even more general class of (
C_{4}
, diamond, theta, prism, even wheel)-free graphs) in a relatively straightforward way, it is a bit long. Instead, we include here a shorter proof of a weaker result which is still strong enough for our purposes.

Lemma 2.18
.

Every graph in
\mathcal{C}
has a trisimplicial vertex.

Proof.

We first prove that graphs in
\mathcal{C}
with no wheel satisfy a stronger statement:

(1)
If a graph
G\in\mathcal{C}
contains no wheel, then it has a bisimplicial vertex.

Note that
G
is (3PC, wheel)-free, so by Lemma
2.7
, it either has a clique cutset, or is a complete graph or a hole. In the latter two cases,
G
satisfies the statement, so assume
G
has a clique cutset. In that case, it is well-known (see e.g. Lemma 3.3 of
[
33
]
) that
G
has an extreme clique cutset
C
, that is, a clique cutset
C
such that, for some component
D
of
G\setminus C
,
D\cup C
has no clique cutset. As above,
D\cup C
is a complete graph or a hole; in particular, any vertex in
D
is bisimplicial in
G
, proving (
2.7
).

We are ready to show that general graphs in
\mathcal{C}
have trisimplicial vertices. We do so using an approach conceptually similar to the proof of (
2.7
), by finding an “extreme” star cutset in
G
.

If
G
is wheel-free, then we are done by (
2.7
), so assume
G
contains a wheel
(H,w)
. Since
G
is pyramid-free,
(H,w)
is not a bug, and since it is diamond-free, it is not a twin or universal wheel, and hence Lemma
2.16
implies that
G
has a star cutset. Let us pick a star cutset
C
with center
v
such that some component
D
of
G\setminus C
is minimal, in the sense that it does not properly contain a component of
G\setminus C^{\prime}
for any other choice of star cutset
C^{\prime}
.

Case 1:

v
is complete to
D
. In this case,
D
is a singleton (since otherwise we may add all but one vertex of
D
to the cutset, and removing the new cutset leaves a singleton component properly contained in
D
, contradicting the minimality of
D
). But in this case, the unique vertex in
D
is simplicial in
G
by diamond-freeness, since its neighborhood is contained in
N[v]
, and we are done.

Case 2:

v
is not complete to
D
. In this case, once more by the minimality of
D
,
v
must be anticomplete to
D
(since otherwise we may add all neighbors of
v
in
D
to the cutset and obtain once more a smaller component). We now claim:

(2)

G[D]
is wheel-free.

Indeed, suppose not, and let
(H,u)
be a wheel in
G[D]
. Put
C^{\prime}:=N[u]
, and note that, by Lemma
2.16
,
C^{\prime}
is a star cutset in
G
with center
u
, and
H\nsubseteq Q\cup C^{\prime}
for any component
Q
of
G\setminus C^{\prime}
(in actuality, the statement of the lemma yields
H\nsubseteq Q\cup N(Q)\cup\{u\}
, but by diamond-freeness, we cannot have
H\setminus(Q\cup N(Q)\cup\{u\})\subseteq C^{\prime}\setminus N(Q)
, since if that were the case, any vertex in
H\setminus(Q\cup N(Q)\cup\{u\})
would be the center of a
P_{3}
in
H\cap N(u)
). Let
B^{\prime}
be the component of
G\setminus C^{\prime}
containing

. By the above, there exists
x\in D\setminus(B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime})
. Let
D^{\prime}
be the component of
G\setminus C^{\prime}
containing
x
. Since every path from
x
to

intersects

, and since
C\subseteq B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}
, it follows that
D^{\prime}\subseteq D
, and this containment is proper (as

), contradicting the choice of
C
and

.

Now by (
2.7
),

has a bisimplicial vertex

. But

has at most one neighbor in

, since

, and
x
and

are non-adjacent (and so by (

, diamond)-freeness, they have at most one common neighbor). Thus

is trisimplicial in

, completing the proof.

∎

S\subseteq D_{1}\cup\dots\cup D_{r}\cup Q_{1}\cup\dots\cup Q_{s}
. Let
S_{H}:=H[\{a_{i}:S\cap D_{i}\neq\emptyset\}\cup\{b_{j}:S\cap Q_{j}\neq\emptyset\}]
, and note that
S_{H}
is a connected induced subgraph of
H
. Finally, we look at two cases:

•

S_{H}\cap Z^{\prime}=\emptyset
. In this case,
S_{H}
is contained in a connected component of
H\setminus Z^{\prime}
, and
S\subseteq(S_{H})^{G}
(where as above,
(S_{H})^{G}
is the union of the connected components corresponding to vertices of
S_{H}
), so
w(S)\leq\frac{1}{2}
by (
4
).

•

S_{H}\cap Z^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. In this case, we first note that
|S_{H}\cap Z^{\prime}|=1
. Indeed, let
b_{j}\in S_{H}\cap Z^{\prime}
, and note that, by construction of
Y
, the only
D_{i}
with a neighbor in
Q_{j}\setminus Y
are
D_{i}
with
v(i)\in Q_{j}
. These
D_{i}
have no neighbors in any other
Q_{j}
(since
N(D_{i})\subseteq N[v(i)]
). In particular,
S_{H}
is a star centered at
b_{j}
, and
v(i)\in Q_{j}
for all
a_{i}\in S_{H}
. Now

w(S)\leq w((S_{H})^{G})\leq w_{M}(Q_{j})\leq\frac{1}{2},

as required.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Finally, we restate and prove Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

There exists an integer
\Gamma
such that
\ta(G)\leq\Gamma
for every graph
G\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
, we may assume that
G
has no clique cutset, so
G\in\mathcal{C}_{\text{nc}}
. Let
w:V(G)\to[0,1]
be a weight function on
G
with
w(G)=1
, and let
(\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\},m,M,\tilde{S}_{M})
be the hub division of
G
. Let
\beta_{M}
be the central bag for
\tilde{S}_{M}
and let
w_{M}
be the inherited weight function on
\beta_{M}
. By Theorem
3.8
,
\beta_{M}
has a
(w_{M},\frac{1}{2})
-balanced separator of clique cover number at most
9
. Now, by Theorem
4.3
,
G
has a
(w,\frac{1}{2})
-balanced separator of clique cover number at most
\Gamma^{\prime}(9)
. Finally, by Lemma
2.10
,
\tc(G)\leq\Gamma:=g(\Gamma^{\prime}(9),\frac{1}{2})
, where
g
is as defined in the lemma, and by Lemma
2.2
,
\ta(G)\leq\tc(G)
.
∎

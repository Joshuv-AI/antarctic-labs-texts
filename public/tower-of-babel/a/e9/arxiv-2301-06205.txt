Remarks on the Oscillation Energy of Legendrian Isotopies
Dylan Cant

Abstract
. We construct non-compact contact manifolds containing compact Legendrians which can be displaced from their Reeb flow with arbitrarily small oscillation energy. We use this to show the Shelukhin-Chekanov-Hofer pseudo-metric considered by [ RZ20 ] is degenerate on the isotopy class of the constructed Legendrians. Other aspects related to oscillation energy of Legendrian isotopies are explored.

1.1.
LDR contact manifolds

The oscillation energy is related to the problem of finding Legendrian isotopies
\Lambda_{t}
so that there are no Reeb chords between
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
(henceforth we refer to this as
disjoining
the Legendrian). For a certain class of contact manifolds, there is a minimum oscillation energy needed to disjoin a Legendrian. This can be formalized with the following definition:

Definition 1
.

A contact manifold
(Y,\alpha)
is
Legendrian disjoinment rigid
(LDR) if for all isotopies
\Lambda_{t}
:

(3)

\text{energy}^{\mathrm{osc}}_{\alpha}(\Lambda_{t})<C(\alpha,\Lambda_{0})\implies\text{there is a Reeb chord between $\Lambda_{0}$ and $\Lambda_{1}$,}

where
C(\alpha,\Lambda_{0})
is some positive constant depending on
\alpha
and
\Lambda_{0}
. In words, in an LDR contact manifold, isotopies require a minimum amount of oscillation energy to disjoin a given Legendrian. It is important to note that the LDR notion depends on the choice of form, see §
1.2.1
for further discussion.

In an analogous fashion,
(Y,\alpha)
is
strict LDR
if it satisfies (
3
) for all strict contact isotopies.

Let us say that a contact manifold
Y
is
quasi-fillable
if
SY
can be symplectically embedded into a tame symplectic manifold
X
. We say that
Y
is
aspherically quasi-fillable
if we can take
X
with
\pi_{2}(X,SY)=0
, as in
[
Moh01
]
. Our first result is:

Theorem 1
.

Quasi-fillable contact manifolds
Y
are strict LDR for any choice of contact form
\alpha
. If
Y
is aspherically quasi-fillable and the contact form has a complete Reeb flow, then we can take
C(\Lambda,\alpha)
to be the minimal positive action of a Reeb chord of
\Lambda
.

Results such as this, guaranteeing the existence of chords subject to a bound on the oscillation energy, can be considered as a contact version of Chekanov’s result
[
Che98
]
on the persistence of Lagrangian intersections. See
[
DRS20a
, Theorem 1.2]
,
[
DRS20b
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[

, Theorem 1.13]
,
[
DRS21
]
for results in this vein.

The persistence of Reeb chords appears earlier in the literature with
[
EHS95
]
,
[
Ono96
]
, where the authors prove that, for certain data
(Y,\alpha,\Lambda_{0})
, and arbitrary isotopies
\Lambda_{t}
, there are Reeb chords between
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
. The authors do not use the language of Reeb chords, but rather of intersection points of
\Lambda_{t}
with a
pre-Lagrangian

L
containing
\Lambda_{0}
. In fact,
L
is foliated by Legendrians, and
\Lambda_{0}
is a particular leaf. In the cases considered one can pick a contact form whose Reeb flow preserves
L
and induces a free
S^{1}
-action so that
\Lambda_{0}
is a section of the action. The authors prove that intersections between
\Lambda_{0}
and
L
persist under arbitrary contact isotopies of
\Lambda_{0}
. The proofs are based on two Floer homology theories counting intersection points between
S\Lambda_{0}
and
L
in

[

]

[

Note that
[

1.1.1.
On strict LDR versus general LDR and short chords

The strictness of the isotopy
\Lambda_{t}
is a technical requirement in our geometric argument for Theorem
1
. We require a lower bound on the length of Reeb chords of
\Lambda_{t}
depending only on
\Lambda_{0}
. For general isotopies no such lower bound exists, as chords of arbitrarily short length can be born. The appearance of short chords also poses a problem when considering holomorphic curves in symplectizations.

As an example, consider counts of holomorphic strips in
SY
with moving boundary conditions (i.e., boundary values on the Lagrangians
\left\{S\Lambda_{t}:t\in[0,1]\right\}
in a prescribed way, e.g.,
u(s,0)\in S\Lambda_{0}
,
u(s,1)\in S\Lambda_{f(s)}
, for some
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,1]
). To obtain compactness of such moduli spaces, one needs to handle bubbling (in the sense of
[
BEH+03
]
) along chords of
any
of the intermediate Legendrians
\Lambda_{t}
. Thus, the shortest chords which appear during the isotopy are relevant to the study of such moduli spaces. The work of
[
Oh21
]
establishes a priori estimates for the
\mathrm{d}\alpha
-energy of such holomorphic curves in terms of the oscillation energy of isotopies.

In another direction, one can consider
Lagrangian cobordisms

L
in
SY
interpolating between
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
. The papers
[
ST17
,
EHK16
]
explain how to use an isotopy
\Lambda_{t}
to construct such a cobordism. The length of the shortest Reeb chord of
\Lambda_{t}
determines the ‘‘length’’ of the constructed cobordism.
3
3

3

Recall that, using the contact form
\alpha
to provide a vertical coordinate
s_{\alpha}:SY\to\mathbb{R}
, the
length
of a Lagrangian
L
is the length of the minimal interval
I
so that
L
is cylindrical (tangent to the Liouville field) in the region where
s_{\alpha}\not\in I
.
The length of the cobordism then appears in a priori estimates for holomorphic curves with boundary on
L
. This is the approach taken in
[
DRS20a
]
.

1.2.
Non-LDR contact manifolds

There are contact manifolds which are not strict LDR (and thus are also not LDR). The construction is a familiar one: given two contact manifolds
Y_{0},Y_{1}
and a choice of form
\alpha_{0}
for
Y_{0}
we define the (generalized)
contactization
to be the product
Q=Y_{0}\times SY_{1}
with the contact form
A=\alpha_{0}-\lambda_{1}
(often this is considered with
Y_{0}=\mathbb{R}
or
S^{1}
).

Theorem 2
.

For certain exotic choices of
Y_{1}
, and arbitrary
Y_{0}
, the contactizations
(Q,A)
contain compact Legendrians
\Lambda
which can be disjoined by strict contact isotopies with arbitrarily small oscillation energy. Moreover, the Shelukhin-Chekanov-Hofer pseudo-metric for the contact form
A
is degenerate on the space of Legendrians isotopic to
\Lambda
.

The dichotomy proved in
[
RZ20
]
immediately implies:

Corollary
.

With
Q,A,\Lambda
as in Theorem
2
, the Shelukhin-Chekanov-Hofer pseudo-metric is identically zero on the space of Legendrians isotopic to
\Lambda
.

Our proof of Theorem
2
is based on Sikorav’s example, as conveyed in
[
Che00
, §4]
. Indeed, our “exotic” contact manifolds are those
Y_{1}
for which the symplectization
SY_{1}
admits a
compact embedded exact Lagrangian
. The results of
[
Che00
]
imply that such symplectizations cannot be embedded into any tame symplectic manifold (and hence we deem these
Y_{1}
exotic). Sikorav’s argument uses an exotic contact
\mathbb{R}^{5}
whose symplectization admits a compact Lagrangian sphere due to
[
Mul90
]
.

Using the
h
-principle for Lagrangian caps established in
[
EM13
]
, Murphy explains in
[
Mur13
]
that there are many compact
Y_{1}
, with
\dim(Y_{1})\geq 5
, whose symplectizations contain compact exact Lagrangians; the requirement is the existence of a certain
plastikstufe
in
Y_{1}
. The papers
[
BEM15
,
CMP19
]
relate the existence of a plastikstufe to the
overtwistedness
of
Y_{1}
. This suggests the following questions:

Question 1
.

Is there a three-dimensional compact contact manifold whose symplectization contains a compact exact Lagrangian? Note: Any such Lagrangian is displaceable and hence is necessarily a 2-torus.

Question 2
.

Are there tight contact manifolds whose symplectizations contain compact exact Lagrangians?

Dependence of the LDR property on the contact form

The argument proving Theorem
2
implies that
\mathbb{R}^{7}\simeq\mathbb{R}\times S\mathbb{R}^{5}

1.3.
Disjoinment rigidity for Legendrians admitting generating functions

The next result is that certain Legendrians in
1
-jet spaces are disjoinment rigid. The relevant class of Legendrians are those which admit
linear-at-infinity generating functions
, as in
[
CP05
,
JT06
,
ST10
,
FR11
,
ST13
,
BST15
]
. A function
F:B\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
, is called
linear-at-infinity
if
F(x,\eta)=f(x,\eta)+\ell(\eta)
where
\ell\neq 0
is a linear function and
f
is compactly supported. Let us say that
F
is a
generating function
provided the fiber derivative
\partial_{\eta}F:B\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
has
0
as a regular value. In this case,
\partial_{\eta}F=0
defines a smooth fiberwise singular set
\Sigma
, and the map
\Sigma\to J^{1}(B)
(returning the critical value and horizontal derivative) defines an immersed Legendrian. A Legendrian which can be parametrized by such a map
\Sigma\to J^{1}(B)
is said to admit a generating function. As detailed in
[
FR11
, §3]
, for Legendrian knots in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
the existence of a linear-at-infinity generating function is equivalent to the existence of a graded normal ruling of the front projection. The paper
[
BST15
]
gives many examples of Legendrians which admit linear-at-infinity generating functions. The main result of this section is:

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
\Lambda_{0}\subset J^{1}(B)
is an embedded Legendrian which admits a linear-at-infinity generating function. Then any isotopy
\Lambda_{t}
disjoining
\Lambda_{0}
has oscillation energy at least the minimal positive action of a Reeb chord of
\Lambda_{0}
.

The crux of the argument is to construct a
barcode
4
4

4

[
Ghr08
]

\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{t}
. The lengths of the bars are Lipshitz continuous as a function of

\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}

Our argument is inspired by the papers

1.4.
Oscillation energy for regular homotopies of Legendrians

The notions of length and oscillation energy can be generalized to families of immersions, i.e.,
regular homotopies
of Legendrians, as in
[
Lau09
]
. Given a family of Legendrian immersions
i_{t}:\Lambda\to(Y,\alpha)
, we define the contact Hamiltonian
h_{t}(x)=\alpha_{i_{t}(x)}(i_{t}^{\prime}(x))
, length, and oscillation energy exactly as in (
2
). The difference when compared with embeddings is that regular homotopies cannot generally be extended to ambient contact isotopies.

Our first result about Legendrian immersions is a construction of a Legendrian which can be disjoined by a regular homotopy with arbitrarily small oscillation energy. Introduce the following local model:
B(a)\subset\mathbb{C}
is the disk of area
a
, and
Q(a)
is the contactization:

Q(a)=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\times B(a)\text{ with contact form }\alpha=\mathrm{}t-\lambda,

where
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}(x\mathrm{d}y-y\mathrm{d}x)
is the standard Liouville form.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a contact three-manifold which contains an embedded copy of
Q(1+\epsilon)
for some
\epsilon>0
. Let
\alpha
be a contact form which agrees with the
\mathrm{d}t-\lambda
form on
Q(1+\epsilon^{\prime})
for some
0<\epsilon^{\prime}<\epsilon
. Then
(Y,\alpha)
contains an embedded Legendrian
\Lambda_{0}
so that there are regular homotopies
\Lambda_{t}
of arbitrarily small oscillation energy, with no Reeb chords between
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
.

Note that
[
EKP06
, Corollary 1.25]
states that every contact three-manifold contains an embedded copy of
Q(a)
for
every

a>0
. Thus our theorem applies to all contact three-manifolds.

1.5.
Dichotomy for an induced Finsler metric

Let
(Y^{2n+1},\alpha)
be a contact manifold, let
\Lambda^{n}
be a compact smooth manifold, and let
{I}={I}(\Lambda)
be the space of Legendrian immersions
i:\Lambda\to Y
modulo reparametrization by diffeomorphisms. The notion of length of regular homotopies equips
{I}
with a Finsler pseudo-metric. More precisely, given two equivalence classes of immersions
[j_{0}]
,
[j_{1}]
, the pseudo-distance between them is:

\mathrm{dist}_{{I},\alpha}([j_{0}],[j_{1}])=\mathrm{inf}\left\{\mathrm{length}_{\alpha}(i_{t}):i_{0}=j_{0}\circ\sigma\text{ and }i_{1}=j_{1}\circ\sigma^{\prime}\right\}

where
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
are diffeomorphisms of
\Lambda
. We should note that if
\sigma_{t}
is an isotopy of diffeomorphisms then
\mathrm{length}_{\alpha}(i\circ\sigma_{t})=0
, and hence, if we want any hope of a non-degenerate metric, we need to at least mod out by diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity. However, in order to prove our next result, we require modding out by all diffeomorphisms.

Let
{E}\subset{I}
be the subset of embeddings, and let
{E}={E}_{1}\cup{E}_{2}\cup\dots
be the decomposition into isotopy classes of embeddings. We have the following dichotomy:

Theorem 6
.

For each isotopy class
{E}_{k}
, the restriction of
\mathrm{dist}_{{I},\alpha}
to
{E}_{k}
is either identically zero or is non-degenerate.

Indeed, observe that for any ambient compactly-supported contactomorphism
\phi
, with
\phi^{*}\alpha=e^{g}\alpha
,

(4)

\mathrm{dist}_{{I},\alpha}(\phi(\Lambda_{0}),\phi(\Lambda_{1}))\leq e^{\max g}\mathrm{dist}_{{I},\alpha}(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}),

and then Theorem
6
follows from:

Lemma 7
.

Let
{E}_{k}
be an isotopy class of compact Legendrians in
(Y,\xi)
, and suppose that
\delta:{E}_{k}\times{E}_{k}\to[0,\infty)
is a pseudo-metric so that for each compactly supported contactomorphism
\phi
there is
C(\phi)
so that
\delta(\phi(\Lambda_{0}),\phi(\Lambda_{1}))\leq C(\phi)\delta(\Lambda_{0},\Lambda_{1}).
Then either
\delta=0
or
\delta
is non-degenerate.

The proof of Lemma
7
is exactly the argument used in
[
Che00
, Theorem 2]
. See also
[
RZ20
]
. This dichotomy suggests the following question:

Question 3
.

Is
\mathrm{dist}_{\alpha,{I}}
non-degenerate on any isotopy class
{E}_{k}
?

1.6.
The oscillation norm and translated points

Shelukhin relates the oscillation norm
\left|\varphi\right|^{\mathrm{osc}}_{\alpha}
to the existence of translated points of the contactomorphism
\varphi

Y
to compactly supported Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms of
SY

[
She14

uses earlier results of

(5)

\left|\varphi\right|^{\mathrm{osc}}_{\alpha}<2(\text{spectral gap of $\alpha$})\implies\begin{minipage}[1.4cm][c][172.5pt]$\varphi$ has at least one translated point, with at least $\dim H_{*}(Y,\mathbb{Z}/2)$ in the case all the translated points are non-degenerate.
\end{minipage}

Here the
spectral gap
is the minimal positive action of a Reeb orbit of
(Y,\alpha)

[
AF10
]

Conjecture 4

(Conjecture 31 in
[

If

\mathrm{d}\lambda_{0}-\mathrm{d}\lambda_{1}

\mathbb{R}

Q(Y)=(SY\times SY)/\mathbb{R}
is a well-defined contact manifold (as the Liouville flow is free and proper) and so the Lagrangian graph of an equivariant lift of a contactomorphism
\varphi

\Lambda(\varphi)\subset Q(Y)
. This procedure of relating contactomorphisms to Legendrians was previously considered in
[
Lyč77
, §5]

[
Che96
]
,
[
Bhu01
]
,
[

]
,
[
San12
]
,
[
CS15
]

[

]
.

It is not hard to see that a choice of contact form

]
. For our purposes, we require only the following statement. Let

(with

(12)

\liminf_{t\to t_{0}-}L(t)=\liminf_{t\to t_{0}+}L(t).

While the barcode can be defined for any family

, we prefer to perturb

\epsilon

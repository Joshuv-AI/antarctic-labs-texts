Large deviation principles for renewal-reward processes
Marco Zamparo

Abstract
We establish a sharp large deviation principle for renewal-reward processes, supposing that each renewal involves a broad-sense reward taking values in a real separable Banach space. In fact, we demonstrate a weak large deviation principle without assuming any exponential moment condition on the law of waiting times and rewards by resorting to a sharp version of Cramér’s theorem. We also exhibit sufficient conditions for exponential tightness of renewal-reward processes, which leads to a full large deviation principle. Keywords: Large deviations; Cramér’s theorem; Renewal processes; Renewal-reward processes; Banach space valued random variables Mathematics Subject Classification 2020: 60F10; 60K05; 60K35



1
Main results

Renewal models
are widespread tools of probability that find
application in Queueing Theory
[
1
]
, Insurance

[
2
]
, Finance
[
3
]
, and Statistical Physics

[
4
]
among others. A renewal model describes some
event that occurs at the
renewal times

T_{1},T_{2},\ldots

involving the
rewards

X_{1},X_{2},\ldots
respectively. If

S_{1},S_{2},\ldots
denote the
waiting times
for a new occurrence
of the event, then the renewal time
T_{i}
can be expressed for each

i\geq 1
in terms of the waiting times as

T_{i}=S_{1}+\cdots+S_{i}
. Through this paper we assume that the waiting
time and reward pairs
(S_{1},X_{1}),(S_{2},X_{2}),\ldots
form an independent
and identically distributed sequence of random vectors on a
probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, the waiting times
taking positive real values and the rewards taking values in a real
separable Banach space
(\mathcal{X},\|{\cdot}\|)
equipped with the Borel

\sigma
-field
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
. Any dependence between
S_{i}
and
X_{i}
is
allowed and we can suppose without restriction that

\lim_{i\uparrow\infty}T_{i}(\omega)=+\infty
for all
\omega\in\Omega
.
The
cumulative reward
by the time
t\geq 0
is the random
variable
W_{t}:=\sum_{i\geq 1}X_{i}\mathds{1}_{\{T_{i}\leq t\}}
, which is
measurable because
\mathcal{X}
is separable
[
5
]
. The
stochastic process
t\mapsto W_{t}
is the so-called
renewal-reward
process
or
compound renewal process
, which plays an important
role in applications

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. The strong law
of large numbers holds for a renewal-reward process under the optimal
hypotheses
\mathbb{E}[S_{1}]<+\infty
and
\mathbb{E}[\|X_{1}\|]<+\infty
,
\mathbb{E}
being
expectation with respect to the law
\mathbb{P}
, and can be proved by
combining standard arguments of renewal theory
[
1
]

with the classical strong law of large numbers of Kolmogorov in
separable Banach spaces
[
5
]
. This paper aims to
characterize the fluctuations of the cumulative reward
W_{t}
as
t

goes to infinity by means of large deviation bounds.

1.1
Large deviation bounds

The
Cramér’s rate function
of waiting time
and reward pairs is the function
J
that maps each

(s,w)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}
in the extended real number

J(s,w):=\sup_{(\zeta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}^{\star}}\Big\{s\zeta+\varphi(w)-\ln\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}\big]\Big\}.

Hereafter
\mathcal{X}^{\star}
denotes the topological dual of
\mathcal{X}
, which
is understood as a Banach space with the norm induced by

\|{\cdot}\|
. In this paper a special role is played by the function

\inf_{\gamma>0}\{\gamma J(\cdot/\gamma,\cdot/\gamma)\}
, whose
lower-semicontinuous regularization
\Upsilon
associates every

(\beta,w)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}
with

\Upsilon(\beta,w):=\adjustlimits{\lim}_{\delta\downarrow 0}{\inf}_{s\in(\beta-\delta,\beta+\delta)}\inf_{v\in B_{w,\delta}}\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{\gamma J(s/\gamma,v/\gamma)\big\},

B_{w,\delta}:=\{v\in\mathcal{X}:\|v-w\|<\delta\}
being the open ball of
center
w
and radius
\delta
. Setting

\ell_{\mathrm{i}}:=-\liminf_{s\uparrow+\infty}(1/s)\ln\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>s]
and

\ell_{\mathrm{s}}:=-\limsup_{s\uparrow+\infty}(1/s)\ln\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>s]
and observing
that
0\leq\ell_{\mathrm{s}}\leq\ell_{\mathrm{i}}\leq+\infty
, we make use of
\Upsilon
to build
two rate functions
I_{\mathrm{i}}
and
I_{\mathrm{s}}
on
\mathcal{X}
according to the formulas

I_{\mathrm{i}}:=\begin{cases}\inf_{\beta\in[0,1]}\{\Upsilon(\beta,\cdot\,)+(1-\beta)\ell_{\mathrm{i}}\}&\mbox{if }\ell_{\mathrm{i}}<+\infty,\\
\Upsilon(1,\cdot\,)&\mbox{if }\ell_{\mathrm{i}}=+\infty\end{cases}

and

I_{\mathrm{s}}:=\begin{cases}\inf_{\beta\in[0,1]}\{\Upsilon(\beta,\cdot\,)+(1-\beta)\ell_{\mathrm{s}}\}&\mbox{if }\ell_{\mathrm{s}}<+\infty,\\
\Upsilon(1,\cdot\,)&\mbox{if }\ell_{\mathrm{s}}=+\infty.\end{cases}

The rate functions
I_{\mathrm{i}}
and
I_{\mathrm{s}}
enter into a lower large deviation
bound and an upper large deviation bound, respectively, as stated by
the following theorem which collects the main results of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

The following conclusions hold:

(a)

the rate functions
I_{\mathrm{i}}
and
I_{\mathrm{s}}
are lower
semicontinuous and convex;

(b)

if
G\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is open, then

\liminf_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]\geq-\inf_{w\in G}\big\{I_{\mathrm{i}}(w)\big\};

(c)

if
F\subseteq\mathcal{X}
is compact, then

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in F\bigg]\leq-\inf_{w\in F}\big\{I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)\big\};

(d)

if
F\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
is open convex, closed convex, or just convex
when
\mathcal{X}
is finite-dimensional, then the bound of part
(c)
is valid whenever
\ell_{\mathrm{s}}<+\infty
or
I_{\mathrm{s}}(0)<+\infty
;

(e)

if
\mathcal{X}
is finite-dimensional and
\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for some numbers
\zeta\leq 0
and

\sigma>0
, then
I_{\mathrm{i}}
and
I_{\mathrm{s}}
have compact level sets and the
bound of part
(c)
is valid for any closed set
F
;

(f)

if
\mathcal{X}
is infinite-dimensional and
\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for all
\sigma>0
, then

I_{\mathrm{i}}=I_{\mathrm{s}}=\Upsilon(1,\cdot\,)
,
\Upsilon(1,\cdot\,)
has compact
level sets, and the bound of part
(c)
is valid for any
closed set
F
.

Theorem
1.1
is proved in section
2
. When

I_{\mathrm{i}}=I_{\mathrm{s}}
the theorem establishes, through the lower large deviation
bound for open sets of part (b) and the upper large deviation bound
for compact sets of part (c), a
weak large deviation principle

with
rate function

I_{\mathrm{i}}=I_{\mathrm{s}}
for the renewal-reward process

t\mapsto W_{t}
. We refer to
[
6
]
for the language of large
deviation theory. Part (d) states that the upper large deviation
bound also holds for open and closed convex sets provided that

\ell_{\mathrm{s}}<+\infty
or
I_{\mathrm{s}}(0)<+\infty
. It fails in general when

\ell_{\mathrm{s}}=+\infty
and
I_{\mathrm{s}}(0)=+\infty
, as we shall show in section

2
by means of two examples. We stress that no
assumption on the law of waiting time and reward pairs is made to
deduce parts (a), (b), (c), and (d) of theorem
1.1
. Some
assumption is instead necessary for exponential tightness of the
distribution of the scaled cumulative reward
W_{t}/t
, which leads to a
full large deviation principle where the large deviation upper bound
is valid for all closed sets, and not only for those that are compact.
If
I_{\mathrm{i}}=I_{\mathrm{s}}
and
\mathcal{X}
is finite-dimensional, then part (e)
establishes a
full large deviation principle
with
good rate
function

I_{\mathrm{i}}=I_{\mathrm{s}}
under the exponential moment condition

\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for some numbers
\zeta\leq 0
and
\sigma>0
. We recall that a rate function is “good” when it
has compact level sets. Part (f) states that the same is true when

\mathcal{X}
is infinite-dimensional and
\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for all
\sigma>0
. Obviously, we
have
I_{\mathrm{i}}=I_{\mathrm{s}}
if
\ell_{\mathrm{i}}=\ell_{\mathrm{s}}
as expected in most applications. We
have
I_{\mathrm{i}}=I_{\mathrm{s}}
even if
\ell_{\mathrm{i}}>\ell_{\mathrm{s}}
but rewards are dominated by
waiting times according to the following proposition, whose proof is
reported in appendix
A
.

Proposition 1.1
.

Assume that there exists a positive real function
f
on
[0,+\infty)

such that
\lim_{s\uparrow+\infty}f(s)/s=0
and
\|X_{1}\|\leq f(S_{1})

with full probability. Then
I_{\mathrm{i}}=I_{\mathrm{s}}=\Upsilon(1,\cdot\,)
.

1.2
Discussion

Large deviation principles (LDPs) for renewal-reward processes have
been investigated by many authors over the past decades. Their
attention has been focused mostly on rewards taking real values and an
almost omnipresent hypothesis of previous works is the Cramér
condition on the law of waiting time and reward pairs:
\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for some number
\sigma>0
.

The simplest example of renewal-reward process has unit rewards and
corresponds to the counting renewal process
t\mapsto N_{t}:=\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathds{1}_{\{T_{i}\leq t\}}
. Glynn and Whitt
[
7
]

investigated the connection between LDPs of the inverse processes

t\mapsto N_{t}
and
i\mapsto T_{i}
, providing a full LDP for
N_{t}

under the Cramér condition. This condition was later relaxed by
Duffield and Whitt
[
11
]
. Jiang
[
13
]
studied
the large deviations of the extended counting renewal process

t\mapsto\sum_{i\geq 1}\mathds{1}_{\{T_{i}\leq i^{\alpha}t\}}
with

\alpha\in[0,1)
under the Cramér condition. Glynn and Whitt

[
7
]
and Duffield and Whitt
[
11
]
,
together with Puhalskii and Whitt
[
10
]
, also
investigated the connection between sample-path LDPs of the
processes
t\mapsto N_{t}
and
i\mapsto T_{i}
under the Cramér
condition.

Starting from sample-path LDPs of inverse and compound processes,
Duffy and Rodgers-Lee
[
12
]
sketched a full LDP for
renewal-reward processes with real rewards by means of the contraction
principle under the stringent exponential moment condition

\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for all
\sigma>0
. Some
full LDPs for real renewal-reward processes were later proposed by
Macci
[
8
,
9
]
under existence and essentially
smoothness of the scaled cumulant generating function, which allow for
an application of the Gärtner-Ellis theorem
[
6
]
.
Essentially smoothness of the scaled cumulant generating function has
been recently relaxed by Borovkov and Mogulskii

[
14
,
15
]
, which used the Cramér’s theorem

[
6
]
to establish a full LDP under the Cramér
condition. Under this condition, they

[
16
,
17
,
18
]
have also obtained
sample-path LDPs for real renewal-reward processes.

A different approach based on empirical measures has been considered
by Lefevere, Mariani, and Zambotti
[
19
]
, which have
investigated large deviations for the empirical measures of forward
and backward recurrence times associated with a renewal process, and
have then derived by contraction a full LDP for renewal-rewards
processes with rewards determined by the waiting times:
X_{i}:=f(S_{i})

for each
i
with a bounded and continuous real function
f
. Later,
Mariani and Zambotti
[
20
]
have developed a renewal
version of Sanov’s theorem by studying the empirical law of rewards
that take values in a generic Polish space under the hypothesis

\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma S_{1}}]<+\infty
for all
\sigma>0
. By appealing to the
contraction principle, this result could give a full LDP for a
renewal-reward process with rewards valued in a separable
Banach space provided that the exponential moment condition

\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
is satisfied for all
\sigma>0
as
discussed by Schied
[
21
]
.

These works leave open the question of whether some LDPs free from
exponential moment conditions can be established for renewal-reward
processes, in the wake of the sharp version of Cramér’s theorem
demonstrated by Bahadur and Zabell
[
22
]
. In a recent paper,
the author
[
23
]
has dropped the Cramér condition in the
discrete-time framework, whereby waiting times have a lattice
distribution, by establishing a weak LDP for cumulative rewards free
from hypotheses. The discrete-time framework is special because
allows a super-multiplicativity property of the probability that

W_{t}/t
belongs to a convex set to emerge by conditioning on the event
that the time
t
is a renewal time. This super-multiplicativity
property was the key to get at sharp LDPs in

[
23
]
. Unfortunately, the same strategy does not extend to
waiting times with non-lattice distribution since conditioning on the
event that a certain time is a renewal time is not a meaningful
procedure in this case. The present paper overcomes the difficulty to
deal with general waiting times by making a better use of Cramér’s
theorem than Borovkov and Mogulskii

[
14
,
15
]
. In fact, starting from the
Cramér’s theory for waiting time and reward pairs, here we establish
a weak LDP for the renewal-reward process
t\mapsto W_{t}
with no
restriction on waiting times and without assuming that the Cramér
condition is satisfied. Moreover, when finite-dimensional rewards are
considered, we provide a full LDP under the exponential moment
condition
\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for some numbers

\zeta\leq 0
and
\sigma>0
, which is weaker than the Cramér
condition
\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for some

\sigma>0
. For instance, rewards that define macroscopic observables
in applications to Statistical Physics
[
24
]
are of the order
of magnitude of waiting times and always satisfy our weak exponential
moment condition, whereas in general they do not fulfill the Cramér
condition. But after all is said and done, a super-multiplicativity
argument is still the key, as it underlies the sharp version of
Cramér’s theorem we have exploited to reach our results.

To conclude, we point out that, at variance with Borovkov and
Mogulskii, we propose optimal lower and upper large deviation bounds
with possibly different rate functions in order to even address
situations where the tail of the waiting time distribution is very
oscillating. For instance, a physical renewal model giving rise to two
possibly different rate functions has been found by Lefevere, Mariani,
and Zambotti
[
25
,
26
]
in the description of
a free particle interacting with a heat bath.

2.1
Rate functions

The function
\Upsilon
satisfies the following properties, which will
be used in the sequel.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following conclusions hold:

(i)

\Upsilon
is lower semicontinuous and convex;

(ii)

\Upsilon(\beta,w)\geq 0
and

\Upsilon(a\beta,aw)=a\Upsilon(\beta,w)
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

w\in\mathcal{X}
, and
a>0
;

(iii)

\Upsilon(0,0)=0
and
\Upsilon(\beta,w)=+\infty
for all
\beta<0
and
w\in\mathcal{X}
;

(iv)

for every
\beta>0
and
w\in\mathcal{X}

\Upsilon(\beta,w)=\adjustlimits{\lim}_{\delta\downarrow 0}{\inf}_{v\in B_{w,\delta}}\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{\gamma J(\beta/\gamma,v/\gamma)\big\}.

Proof.

As
\Upsilon
is lower semicontinuous by construction, in order to
prove part
(i)
it suffices to verify convexity. We show that, for
every given integer
k\geq 1
, the function

\inf_{\gamma\in[1/k,k]}\{\gamma J(\cdot/\gamma,\cdot/\gamma)\}
over

\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}
is the convex conjugate of a certain function
F_{k}
on

\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}^{\star}
. This way,
\inf_{\gamma>0}\{\gamma J(\cdot/\gamma,\cdot/\gamma)\}=\lim_{k\uparrow\infty}\inf_{\gamma\in[1/k,k]}\{\gamma J(\cdot/\gamma,\cdot/\gamma)\}
is convex, and so is
\Upsilon
. Pick

s\in\mathbb{R}
and
w\in\mathcal{X}
and denote by
\mathcal{D}
the set

\{(\zeta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}^{\star}:\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(w)}]<+\infty\}
. The function that associates

(\zeta,\varphi)\in\mathcal{D}
with
\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}]
is lower semicontinuous by Fatou’s lemma and
convex, so that the real function that maps

(\gamma,\zeta,\varphi)\in[1/k,k]\times\mathcal{D}
in

\varphi(w)-s\zeta-\gamma\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}]
is
concave and upper semicontinuous with respect to
(\zeta,\varphi)
for
each fixed
\gamma\in[1/k,k]
and convex and continuous with respect
to
\gamma
for each fixed pair
(\zeta,\varphi)\in\mathcal{D}
. Then,
the compactness of the interval
[1/k,k]
allows an application of
Sion’s minimax theorem to get

\displaystyle\inf_{\gamma\in[1/k,k]}\big\{\gamma J(s/\gamma,w/\gamma)\big\}

\displaystyle=\adjustlimits{\inf}_{\gamma\in[1/k,k]}{\sup}_{(\zeta,\varphi)\in\mathcal{D}}\Big\{s\zeta+\varphi(w)-\gamma\ln\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}\big]\Big\}

\displaystyle=\adjustlimits{\sup}_{(\zeta,\varphi)\in\mathcal{D}}{\inf}_{\gamma\in[1/k,k]}\Big\{s\zeta+\varphi(w)-\gamma\ln\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}\big]\Big\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\zeta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}^{\star}}\Big\{s\zeta+\varphi(w)-F_{k}(\zeta,\varphi)\Big\}

with

F_{k}(\zeta,\varphi):=\max\Big\{(1/k)\ln\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}\big],k\ln\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}\big]\Big\}

for all
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
.

Let us move to part
(ii)
. We have
J(s,w)\geq 0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}

and
w\in\mathcal{X}
because
s\zeta+\varphi(w)-\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}]=0
when
\zeta=0
and
\varphi=0
. Then,

\Upsilon(\beta,w)\geq 0
for all
\beta
and
w
. The property

\Upsilon(a\beta,aw)=a\Upsilon(\beta,w)
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

w\in\mathcal{X}
, and
a>0
is immediate.

Regarding part
(iii)
, let us observe that

\lim_{\zeta\downarrow-\infty}\{s\zeta+\varphi(w)-\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}]\}=+\infty
for each
s<0
and

\varphi\in\mathcal{X}^{\star}
. This way,
J(s,w)=+\infty
for each
s<0
and

w\in\mathcal{X}
, which yields
\Upsilon(\beta,w)=+\infty
for every

\beta<0
and
w\in\mathcal{X}
. As far as the equality
\Upsilon(0,0)=0
is
concerned, in the light of part
(ii)
it remains to demonstrate that

\Upsilon(0,0)\leq 0
. The function that maps

(\zeta,\varphi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}^{\star}
with
\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}]
is proper convex, so that there exist

s_{o}\in\mathbb{R}
,
w_{o}\in\mathcal{X}
, and a constant
c
such that

\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\varphi(X_{1})}]\geq s_{o}\zeta+\varphi(w_{o})-c
for
all
\zeta
and
\varphi
(see
[
27
]
, theorem 2.2.6). It
follows that
J(s_{o},w_{o})\leq c<+\infty
. Let
a
be a small positive
number such that
as_{o}\in(-1,1)
and
aw_{o}\in B_{0,1}
. Then, for
all
\delta>0
we find the bound

\adjustlimits{\inf}_{s\in(-\delta,\delta)}{\inf}_{v\in B_{0,\delta}}\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{\gamma J(s/\gamma,v/\gamma)\big\}\leq\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{\gamma J(a\delta s_{o}/\gamma,a\delta w_{o}/\gamma)\big\}\leq a\delta J(s_{o},w_{o}),

which gives
\Upsilon(0,0)\leq 0
once
\delta
is sent to 0.

To conclude, let us prove part
(iv)
. Fix
\beta>0
and

w\in\mathcal{X}
. It is clear that

\Upsilon(\beta,w)\leq\adjustlimits{\lim}_{\delta\downarrow 0}{\inf}_{v\in B_{w,\delta}}\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{\gamma J(\beta/\gamma,v/\gamma)\big\}.

Let us demonstrate the opposite bound. For all
\delta\in(0,\beta)

and
s\in(\beta-\delta,\beta+\delta)
we have
s>0
and
B_{\beta w/s,\beta\delta/s}\subseteq B_{w,(\beta+\|w\|)\delta/(\beta-\delta)}
. The latter is due to the
fact that if
s\in(\beta-\delta,\beta+\delta)
and
v\in B_{\beta w/s,\beta\delta/s}
, then

\displaystyle\|v-w\|

\displaystyle\leq\|v-\beta w/s\|+\|\beta w/s-w\|<\beta\delta/s+|\beta/s-1|\|w\|

\displaystyle<\beta\delta/(\beta-\delta)+\delta\|w\|/(\beta-\delta)=(\beta+\|w\|)\delta/(\beta-\delta).

Thus, recalling that
J
is non-negative, for every

\delta\in(0,\beta)
we can write

\displaystyle\adjustlimits{\inf}_{s\in(\beta-\delta,\beta+\delta)}{\inf}_{v\in B_{w,\delta}}\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{\gamma J(s/\gamma,v/\gamma)\big\}

\displaystyle=\adjustlimits{\inf}_{s\in(\beta-\delta,\beta+\delta)}{\inf}_{v\in B_{\beta w/s,\beta\delta/s}}\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{(\gamma J(s/\gamma,sv/\gamma\beta)\big\}

\displaystyle=\adjustlimits{\inf}_{s\in(\beta-\delta,\beta+\delta)}{\inf}_{v\in B_{\beta w/s,\beta\delta/s}}\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{(s\gamma/\beta)J(\beta/\gamma,v/\gamma)\big\}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\delta/\beta)\inf_{v\in B_{w,(\beta+\|w\|)\delta/(\beta-\delta)}}\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{\gamma J(\beta/\gamma,v/\gamma)\big\}.

This inequality shows that

\Upsilon(\beta,w)\geq\adjustlimits{\lim}_{\delta\downarrow 0}{\inf}_{v\in B_{w,\delta}}\inf_{\gamma>0}\big\{\gamma J(\beta/\gamma,v/\gamma)\big\}.\qed

We are now in the position to prove part (a) of theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The rate functions
I_{\mathrm{i}}
and
I_{\mathrm{s}}
are lower semicontinuous and convex.

Proof.

We address the rate function
I_{\mathrm{i}}
. The same arguments apply to
I_{\mathrm{s}}
.
If
\ell_{\mathrm{i}}=+\infty
, then
I_{\mathrm{i}}=\Upsilon(1,\cdot\,)
and lower
semicontinuity and convexity of
I_{\mathrm{i}}
immediately follow from part

(i)
of lemma
2.1
. Assume that
\ell_{\mathrm{i}}<+\infty
.
In order to demonstrate lower semicontinuity of
I_{\mathrm{i}}
, let us show
that the set
F:=\{w\in\mathcal{X}:I_{\mathrm{i}}(w)\leq\lambda\}
is closed for any
given real number
\lambda
. Let
\{w_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence in

F
converging to a point
w
. We claim that
w\in F
. In fact, by the
lower semicontinuity of
\Upsilon
and the compactness of
[0,1]
,
for each
k\geq 1
there exists
\beta_{k}\in[0,1]
such that

I_{\mathrm{i}}(w_{k})=\Upsilon(\beta_{k},w_{k})+(1-\beta_{k})\ell_{\mathrm{i}}
. The compactness of

[0,1]
also entails that there exists a subsequence

\{\beta_{k_{j}}\}_{j\geq 1}
that converges to some number

\beta_{o}\in[0,1]
. We have
\lambda\geq I_{\mathrm{i}}(w_{k_{j}})=\Upsilon(\beta_{k_{j}},w_{k_{j}})+(1-\beta_{k_{j}})\ell_{\mathrm{i}}
for all
j\geq 1
,
which gives
\lambda\geq\Upsilon(\beta_{o},w)+(1-\beta_{o})\ell_{\mathrm{i}}\geq I_{\mathrm{i}}(w)
once
j
is sent to infinity. Thus,
w\in F
.

As far as convexity of
I_{\mathrm{i}}
is concerned, given
w_{1}\in\mathcal{X}
and

w_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
, let
\beta_{1}\in[0,1]
and
\beta_{2}\in[0,1]
be such that

I_{\mathrm{i}}(w_{1})=\Upsilon(\beta_{1},w_{1})+(1-\beta_{1})\ell_{\mathrm{i}}
and

I_{\mathrm{i}}(w_{2})=\Upsilon(\beta_{2},w_{2})+(1-\beta_{2})\ell_{\mathrm{i}}
. We recall that the
existence of
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
is guaranteed by the lower
semicontinuity of
\Upsilon
. This way, if
a_{1}\geq 0
and
a_{2}\geq 0

are two numbers such that
a_{1}+a_{2}=1
, then convexity of
\Upsilon

shows that

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{i}}(a_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}w_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\Upsilon(a_{1}\beta_{1}+a_{2}\beta_{2},a_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}w_{2})+(1-a_{1}\beta_{1}-a_{2}\beta_{2})\ell_{\mathrm{i}}

\displaystyle\leq a_{1}\big[\Upsilon(\beta_{1},w_{1})+(1-\beta_{1})\ell_{\mathrm{i}}\big]+a_{2}\big[\Upsilon(\beta_{2},w_{2})+(1-\beta_{2})\ell_{\mathrm{i}}\big]

\displaystyle=a_{1}I_{\mathrm{i}}(w_{1})+a_{2}I_{\mathrm{i}}(w_{2}).\qed

2.2
The lower large deviation bound for open sets

The proof of part (b) of theorem
1.1
relies on the following
lower bound: for each set
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
and integers
1\leq p<q

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in A\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in A,\,T_{p}\leq t<T_{q}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=p}^{q-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in A,\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg].

(2.1)

This lower bound gives the forthcoming lemma, which applies for both

\ell_{\mathrm{i}}<+\infty
and
\ell_{\mathrm{i}}=+\infty
and demonstrates part (b) of
theorem
1.1
directly when
\ell_{\mathrm{i}}=+\infty
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For every
G\subseteq\mathcal{X}
open and
w\in G

\liminf_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]\geq-\Upsilon(1,w).

Proof.

Pick an open set
G
in
\mathcal{X}
. We shall prove that for each point

w\in G
and real number
\gamma>0

\liminf_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]\geq-\gamma J(1/\gamma,w/\gamma).

(2.2)

This bound yields the lemma by optimizing over
\gamma
and by
invoking part
(iv)
of lemma
2.1
.

Fix an arbitrary point
w\in G
and an arbitrary real number

\gamma>0
. As
G
is open, there exists
\delta>0
such that

B_{w,2\delta}\subseteq G
. Since
\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>0]=1
there exist a
small number
m>0
and a large number
M>0
with the property

\mathbb{P}[S_{1}\geq m,\,\|X_{1}\|\leq M]\geq 1/2
. Let
\epsilon\in(0,1)
be
such that
(1+2M/m+\|w\|)\epsilon<\delta
and let
t_{o}>0
be such that

\gamma(1-\epsilon)t_{o}\geq 1
,
m\leq 2\epsilon t_{o}
,

\|w\|<\delta\gamma t_{o}
, and
\epsilon^{2}t_{o}\geq m+1/\gamma
. Set

p_{t}:=\lfloor\gamma(1-\epsilon)t\rfloor
and
q_{t}:=p_{t}+\lfloor 2\epsilon t/m\rfloor
. For
t>t_{o}
we have
p_{t}\geq 1
as

\gamma(1-\epsilon)t>\gamma(1-\epsilon)t_{o}\geq 1
and
p_{t}<q_{t}
as

2\epsilon t/m>2\epsilon t_{o}/m\geq 1
. For brevity, we denote by

\mathbb{M}_{t}
the probability measure that maps a set
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
in

\mathbb{M}_{t}[A]:=\mathbb{P}\bigg[A\,\bigg|\min_{p_{t}<i\leq q_{t}}\big\{S_{i}\big\}\geq m,\,\max_{p_{t}<i\leq q_{t}}\big\{\|X_{i}\|\big\}\leq M\bigg],

and we observe that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[A]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{M}_{t}[A]\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigg[\min_{p_{t}<i\leq q_{t}}\big\{S_{i}\big\}\geq m,\,\max_{p_{t}<i\leq q_{t}}\big\{\|X_{i}\|\big\}\leq M\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{M}_{t}[A]\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigg[S_{1}>m,\,\|X_{1}\|\leq M\bigg]^{2\epsilon t/m}\geq\frac{1}{4^{\epsilon t/m}}\,\mathbb{M}_{t}[A].

Bound (
2.1
) gives for any
t>t_{o}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{n=p_{t}}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in B_{w,2\delta},\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4^{\epsilon t/m}}\sum_{n=p_{t}}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{M}_{t}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in B_{w,2\delta},\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg].

We now notice that the condition
(1/p_{t})\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w/\gamma,\epsilon/\gamma}
implies
(1/t)\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in B_{w,2\delta}
for each
n\in[p_{t},q_{t})
when
t>t_{o}
and

\max_{p_{t}<i\leq q_{t}}\{\|X_{i}\|\}\leq M
. In fact, recalling that

(1+2M/m+\|w\|)\epsilon<\delta
and that
\|w\|<\delta\gamma t_{o}
,
for
t>t_{o}
we find

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}-w\Bigg\|

\displaystyle=\Bigg\|\frac{p_{t}}{t}\bigg(\frac{1}{p_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}-\frac{w}{\gamma}\bigg)+\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=p_{t}+1}^{n}X_{i}+\bigg(\frac{p_{t}}{\gamma t}-1\bigg)w\Bigg\|

\displaystyle<\frac{p_{t}\epsilon}{\gamma t}+\frac{M(n-p_{t})}{t}+\bigg|\frac{p_{t}}{\gamma t}-1\bigg|\|w\|\leq(1+2M/m+\|w\|)\epsilon+\frac{\|w\|}{\gamma t_{o}}<2\delta.

This argument yields for
t>t_{o}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4^{\epsilon t/m}}\sum_{n=p_{t}}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{M}_{t}\Bigg[\frac{1}{p_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w/\gamma,\epsilon/\gamma},\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{\epsilon t/m}}\,\mathbb{M}_{t}\Bigg[\frac{1}{p_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w/\gamma,\epsilon/\gamma},\,T_{p_{t}}\leq t<T_{q_{t}}\Bigg].

The condition
T_{p_{t}}<(1/\gamma+\epsilon/\gamma)p_{t}
implies

T_{p_{t}}\leq t
. Moreover, under the constraints
t>t_{o}
and

\min_{p_{t}<i\leq q_{t}}\{S_{i}\}\geq m
, the condition

T_{p_{t}}>(1/\gamma-\epsilon/\gamma)p_{t}
entails
t<T_{q_{t}}
. Indeed,
since
\epsilon^{2}t_{o}\geq m+1/\gamma
by construction we have

\displaystyle T_{q_{t}}

\displaystyle=T_{p_{t}}+\sum_{i=p_{t}+1}^{q_{t}}S_{i}>(1/\gamma-\epsilon/\gamma)p_{t}+(q_{t}-p_{t})m

\displaystyle>(1/\gamma-\epsilon/\gamma)[\gamma(1-\epsilon)t-1]+(2\epsilon t/m-1)m

\displaystyle=t+\epsilon^{2}t-m-(1-\epsilon)/\gamma>t+\epsilon^{2}t_{o}-m-1/\gamma\geq t.

It follows that for
t>t_{o}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4^{\epsilon t/m}}\,\mathbb{M}_{t}\Bigg[\frac{1}{p_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w/\gamma,\epsilon/\gamma},\,(1/\gamma-\epsilon/\gamma)p_{t}<T_{p_{t}}<(1/\gamma+\epsilon/\gamma)p_{t}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{\epsilon t/m}}\,\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{p_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}(S_{i},X_{i})\in(1/\gamma-\epsilon/\gamma,1/\gamma+\epsilon/\gamma)\times B_{w/\gamma,\epsilon/\gamma}\Bigg],

where the last equality is due to the fact that

\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}(S_{i},X_{i})
is independent of
\min_{p_{t}<i\leq q_{t}}\{S_{i}\}
and
\max_{p_{t}<i\leq q_{t}}\{\|X_{i}\|\}
. At this point,
part
(ii)
of proposition
B.1
allows us to conclude that

\liminf_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]\geq-\gamma(1-\epsilon)J(1/\gamma,w/\gamma)-\frac{\epsilon}{m}\ln 4.

We get (
2.2
) from here since
\epsilon\in(0,1)
is
any number smaller than
\delta/(1+2M/m+\|w\|)
.
∎

The next lemma proves part (b) of theorem
1.1
when

\ell_{\mathrm{i}}<+\infty
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\ell_{\mathrm{i}}<+\infty
. For each
G\subseteq\mathcal{X}
open,
w\in G
, and
\beta\in[0,1]

\liminf_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]\geq-\Upsilon(\beta,w)-(1-\beta)\ell_{\mathrm{i}}.

Proof.

The instance
\beta=1
is solved by lemma
2.2
, so
that we must tackle the case
\beta<1
. Given an open set
G
in

\mathcal{X}
, we prove that for each
w\in G
and real numbers
\gamma>0

and
s<1

\liminf_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]\geq-\gamma J(s/\gamma,w/\gamma)-(1-s)\ell_{\mathrm{i}}.

(2.3)

The lemma follows from here by recalling the definition of
\Upsilon

and by optimizing over
\gamma
,
s
, and
w
.

Fix
w\in G
,
\gamma>0
, and
s<1
. If
s<0
, then there is nothing
to prove because
J(s/\gamma,w/\gamma)=+\infty
as we have seen in the
proof of part
(iii)
of lemma
2.1
. Assume that

s\in[0,1)
and pick a small number
\epsilon>0
such that

B_{w,\epsilon}\subseteq G
and
s+\gamma\epsilon\leq 1
. Let

\delta>0
and
t_{o}>0
be two real numbers satisfying

\gamma\delta+\|w\|/(\gamma t_{o})\leq\epsilon
and
\lfloor\gamma t_{o}\rfloor\geq 1
. Set
p_{t}:=\lfloor\gamma t\rfloor
. For
t>t_{o}

we have
1\leq p_{t}\leq\gamma t
and (
2.1
) gives

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w,\epsilon},\,T_{p_{t}}\leq t<T_{p_{t}+1}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w,\epsilon},\,\gamma\frac{T_{p_{t}}}{p_{t}}\leq 1,\,S_{p_{t}+1}>t-T_{p_{t}}\Bigg].

Since
s+\gamma\epsilon\leq 1
, we can write down the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w,\epsilon},\,\gamma\frac{T_{p_{t}}}{p_{t}}\in(s-\gamma\epsilon,s+\gamma\epsilon),\,S_{p_{t}+1}>t-T_{p_{t}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w,\epsilon},\,\frac{T_{p_{t}}}{p_{t}}\in(s/\gamma-\epsilon,s/\gamma+\epsilon),\,S_{p_{t}+1}>t-(s/\gamma-\epsilon)p_{t}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w,\epsilon},\,\frac{T_{p_{t}}}{p_{t}}\in(s/\gamma-\epsilon,s/\gamma+\epsilon)\Bigg]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[S_{1}>t-(s/\gamma-\epsilon)p_{t}\big].

We now observe that the condition
(1/p_{t})\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w/\gamma,\delta}
implies
(1/t)\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w,\epsilon}
for
t>t_{o}
. Indeed, recalling that

\gamma\delta+\|w\|/(\gamma t_{o})\leq\epsilon
, for
t>t_{o}
we find

\Bigg\|\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}-w\Bigg\|=\frac{p_{t}}{t}\Bigg\|\frac{1}{p_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}-w/\gamma+w/\gamma-\frac{t}{p_{t}}w\Bigg\|<\gamma\delta+\|w\|/(\gamma t)\leq\epsilon.

Then, for
t>t_{o}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{p_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}X_{i}\in B_{w/\gamma,\delta},\,\frac{T_{p_{t}}}{p_{t}}\in(s/\gamma-\epsilon,s/\gamma+\epsilon)\Bigg]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[S_{1}>t-(s/\gamma-\epsilon)p_{t}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{p_{t}}\sum_{i=1}^{p_{t}}(S_{i},X_{i})\in(s/\gamma-\epsilon,s/\gamma+\epsilon)\times B_{w/\gamma,\delta}\Bigg]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[S_{1}>t-(s/\gamma-\epsilon)p_{t}\big],

so that part
(ii)
of proposition
B.1
and

-\liminf_{\sigma\uparrow+\infty}(1/\sigma)\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>\sigma]=:\ell_{\mathrm{i}}
yield

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in G\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq-\gamma J(s/\gamma,w/\gamma)-(1-s)\ell_{\mathrm{i}}-\gamma\epsilon\ell_{\mathrm{i}}.

Since
\epsilon
is any positive number small enough, this bound
demonstrates (
2.3
).
∎

2.3
The upper large deviation bound for convex sets

The starting point to prove some upper large deviation bounds is the
following inequality, which holds for every set
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
and
integer
q>1
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in A\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in A,\,T_{q}>t\bigg]+\mathbb{P}\big[T_{q}\leq t\big]

\displaystyle\leq\mathds{1}_{\{0\in A\}}\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>t]+\sum_{n=1}^{q-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in A,\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg]+\mathbb{P}\big[T_{q}\leq t\big].

(2.4)

We complement this inequality with two lemmas, the first of which
controls the small values of the waiting times.

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists a real number
\kappa>0
such that for all sufficiently
large numbers
\gamma
and
t

\mathbb{P}\big[T_{\lfloor\gamma t\rfloor}\leq t\big]\leq e^{-\kappa\gamma t}.

Proof.

Since
\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>0]=1
, there exists a number
\eta>0
such that

\xi:=\mathbb{P}[S_{1}\geq\eta]>0
. Pick three real numbers
\gamma\geq 3/\eta\xi
,
t\geq\eta\xi
, and
\lambda\geq 0
and set
i:=\lfloor\gamma t\rfloor
for brevity. Chernoff bound and the equality

e^{-z}\leq 1-z+z^{2}/2
valid for all
z\geq 0
allows us to write down
the bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big[T_{i}\leq t\big]

\displaystyle\leq e^{\lambda t}\,\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda T_{i}}]\leq e^{\lambda t}\,\mathbb{E}[e^{-\lambda S_{1}\wedge\eta}]^{i}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\lambda t}\Big\{1-\lambda\mathbb{E}\big[S_{1}\wedge\eta\big]+\lambda^{2}\mathbb{E}\big[(S_{1}\wedge\eta)^{2}\big]/2\Big\}^{i}\leq e^{\lambda t}\Big\{1-\lambda\eta\xi+\lambda^{2}\eta^{2}/2\Big\}^{i}.

At this point, the inequality
1+z\leq e^{z}
valid for all
z\in\mathbb{R}

gives

\mathbb{P}\big[T_{i}\leq t\big]\leq e^{\lambda t-\lambda\eta\xi i+\lambda^{2}\eta^{2}i/2}.

(2.5)

Since
\gamma\geq 3/\eta\xi
and
t\geq\eta\xi
we have

\displaystyle\eta\xi i-t\geq\eta\xi(\gamma t-1)-t=\gamma t[\eta\xi-1/\gamma(1+\eta\xi/t)]\geq\gamma t[\eta\xi-2/\gamma]\geq\gamma t\eta\xi/3>0.

This way, we can set
\lambda:=(\eta\xi i-t)/(\eta^{2}i)
in
(
2.5
) to get

\mathbb{P}\big[T_{i}\leq t\big]\leq e^{-\frac{(\eta\xi i-t)^{2}}{2\eta^{2}i}}\leq e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}t^{2}\eta^{2}\xi^{2}}{18\eta^{2}\gamma t}}=e^{-\frac{\gamma\xi^{2}}{18}t}.\qed

The second lemma is more technical and is needed to estimate
probabilities involving convex sets.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\alpha<\beta
be two real numbers and let
C\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be
open convex, closed convex, or any convex set in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
when
\mathcal{X}

is finite-dimensional. Then, for all
n\geq 1
and
t>0

\ln\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(S_{i},X_{i})\in[\alpha,\beta]\times C\Bigg]\leq-t\inf_{(s,w)\in[\alpha,\beta]\times C}\big\{\Upsilon(s,w)\big\}.

Proof.

Fix an integer
n\geq 1
and a real number
t>0
. Set

\alpha_{o}:=t\alpha/n
and
\beta_{o}:=t\beta/n
and denote by
C_{o}
the
convex set
\{tw/n:w\in C\}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
. Then,
C_{o}
is open or closed
if
C
is open or closed and lemma
B.3
shows that

\displaystyle\ln\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(S_{i},X_{i})\in[\alpha,\beta]\times C\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\ln\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(S_{i},X_{i})\in[\alpha_{o},\beta_{o}]\times C_{o}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq-n\inf_{(s,w)\in[\alpha_{o},\beta_{o}]\times C_{o}}\big\{J(s,w)\big\}

\displaystyle=-t\inf_{(s,w)\in[\alpha,\beta]\times C}\big\{(n/t)J(ts/n,tw/n)\big\}.

On the other hand, we have
(n/t)J(ts/n,tw/n)\geq\Upsilon(s,w)
for
all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
w\in\mathcal{X}
by definition.
∎

We are now in the position to demonstrate part (d) of theorem

1.1
. An upper large deviation bound for convex sets comes
from the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\ell\leq\ell_{\mathrm{s}}
be a real number and let
C\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be
open convex, closed convex, or any convex set in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
when
\mathcal{X}

is finite-dimensional. Then

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C\bigg]\leq-\inf_{w\in C}\inf_{\beta\in[0,1]}\big\{\Upsilon(\beta,w)+(1-\beta)\ell\big\}.

If
\ell_{\mathrm{s}}<+\infty
, then part (d) of theorem
1.1
follows from
this lemma by making the choice
\ell=\ell_{\mathrm{s}}
. If
\ell_{\mathrm{s}}=+\infty
and

I_{\mathrm{s}}(0)<+\infty
, then part (d) of theorem
1.1
is obtained by
taking
\ell=I_{\mathrm{s}}(0)=\Upsilon(1,0)
. While the former is manifest, the
latter is due to parts
(i)
and
(ii)
of lemma

2.1
, which show that for all
w\in\mathcal{X}
and

\beta\in[0,1]

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{s}}(w):=\Upsilon(1,w)=\Upsilon\bigg(\frac{2\beta+2-2\beta}{2},\frac{2w+0}{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Upsilon(2\beta,2w)+\frac{1}{2}\Upsilon(2-2\beta,0)

\displaystyle=\Upsilon(\beta,w)+(1-\beta)\Upsilon(1,0).

Proof.

Pick a real number
\ell\leq\ell_{\mathrm{s}}
and notice that
\inf_{w\in C}\inf_{\beta\in[0,1]}\{\Upsilon(\beta,w)+(1-\beta)\ell\}>-\infty

as
\Upsilon
is non-negative by lemma
2.1
. Fix
real numbers
\lambda<\inf_{w\in C}\inf_{\beta\in[0,1]}\{\Upsilon(\beta,w)+(1-\beta)\ell\}
and

\epsilon>0
. By lemma
2.4
there exists a large number

\gamma
such that
\mathbb{P}\big[T_{\lfloor\gamma t\rfloor}\leq t\big]\leq e^{-\lambda t}
for all sufficiently large
t
. Set

q_{t}:=\lfloor\gamma t\rfloor
. Then, (
2.4
) gives
for all sufficiently large
t

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C\bigg]\leq\mathds{1}_{\{0\in C\}}\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>t]+\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in C,\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg]+e^{-\lambda t}.

Since
-\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}(1/t)\ln\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>t]=:\ell_{\mathrm{s}}\geq\ell
,
there exists a constant
M>0
such that
\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>t]\leq Me^{(\epsilon-\ell)t}
for all
t>0
. Moreover, by definition we have

\lambda<\Upsilon(\beta,w)+(1-\beta)\ell
for all
\beta\in[0,1]
and

w\in C
. Recall that
\Upsilon(0,0)=0
by lemma

2.1
. Thus, if
0\in C
, then

\lambda<\Upsilon(0,0)+\ell=\ell
, which shows that

\mathds{1}_{\{0\in C\}}\leq e^{(\ell-\lambda)t}
for any
t>0
. It
follows that for all sufficiently large
t

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C\bigg]\leq e^{-\lambda t}+Me^{(\epsilon-\lambda)t}+\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in C,\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg].

To address the third term in the r.h.s., pick real numbers

0:=\beta_{0}<\beta_{1}<\cdots<\beta_{K}:=1
such that

\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1}\leq\epsilon
for each
k
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in C,\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in C,\,\frac{T_{n}}{t}\in[\beta_{k-1},\beta_{k}],\,S_{n+1}>t-T_{n}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in C,\,\frac{T_{n}}{t}\in[\beta_{k-1},\beta_{k}]\Bigg]\cdot\mathbb{P}\big[S_{1}>(1-\beta_{k})t\big]

\displaystyle\leq M\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(S_{i},X_{i})\in[\beta_{k-1},\beta_{k}]\times C\Bigg]e^{(\epsilon-\ell)(1-\beta_{k})t}.

On the other hand, lemma
2.5
, together with the fact that

\lambda<\Upsilon(s,w)+(1-s)\ell
for all
s\in[0,1]
and
w\in C
,
show that for every
t>0
,
n\geq 1
, and
k\leq K

\displaystyle\ln\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(S_{i},X_{i})\in[\beta_{k-1},\beta_{k}]\times C\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq-t\inf_{(s,w)\in[\beta_{k-1},\beta_{k}]\times C}\big\{\Upsilon(s,w)\big\}

\displaystyle\leq-t\inf_{s\in[\beta_{k-1},\beta_{k}]}\big\{\lambda-(1-s)\ell\big\}

\displaystyle\leq t\big[(1-\beta_{k})\ell+\epsilon|\ell|-\lambda\big].

It follows that for each
t>0

\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in C,\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg]\leq MKq_{t}\,e^{(\epsilon+\epsilon|\ell|-\lambda)t}\leq\gamma MKt\,e^{(\epsilon+\epsilon|\ell|-\lambda)t}.

In conclusion, for all sufficiently large
t
we get

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C\bigg]\leq e^{-\lambda t}+Me^{(\epsilon-\lambda)t}+\gamma MKt\,e^{(\epsilon+\epsilon|\ell|-\lambda)t},

which shows that

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C\bigg]\leq\epsilon+\epsilon|\ell|-\lambda.

The lemma is proved by sending
\lambda
to
\inf_{w\in C}\inf_{\beta\in[0,1]}\{\Upsilon(\beta,w)+(1-\beta)\ell\}
and

\epsilon
to zero.
∎

We conclude the section by showing that the hypotheses
\ell_{\mathrm{s}}<+\infty

and
I_{\mathrm{s}}(0)<+\infty
in part (d) of theorem
1.1
can not be
relaxed at the same time. Assume that the waiting times satisfy

\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>s]=e^{-s^{2}}
for all positive
s
, so that
\ell_{\mathrm{s}}=+\infty
.
Set
\mathcal{X}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and for each
i
consider the reward

X_{i}:=(S_{i},Z_{i})
with
Z_{i}
independent of
S_{i}
and distributed
according to the standard Cauchy law:
\mathbb{P}[Z_{i}\leq z]=1/2+(1/\pi)\arctan(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{R}
. It is a simple exercise
of calculus to show that
I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)=0
if
w_{1}=1
and
I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)=+\infty
if

w_{1}\neq 1
for all
w:=(w_{1},w_{2})\in\mathcal{X}
, so that in particular we
have
I_{\mathrm{s}}(0)=+\infty
. The upper large deviation bound fails for the
open convex set
C:=\{w\in\mathcal{X}:w_{1}<1\}
. In fact,
\inf_{w\in C}\{I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)\}=+\infty
and for all
t>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C\bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big[S_{1}>t\big]+\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\big[T_{n}<t<T_{n+1}\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big[T_{1}>t\big]+\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\big[T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\big]=1.

It also fails for the closed convex set
C:=\{w\in\mathcal{X}:w_{1}<1\mbox{
and }(1-w_{1})w_{2}\geq 1\}
. Indeed,
\inf_{w\in C}\{I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)\}=+\infty

and

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C\bigg]=0

(2.6)

as we now demonstrate. Pick a real number
\epsilon\in(0,1)
and an
integer
N\geq 1
. Let
\mu
and
\sigma^{2}
be the mean and the
variance of
S_{1}
, respectively. Since
(1/N)\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}
is
distributed as
Z_{1}
by the stability property of the Cauchy law, for
every
t>0
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Bigg[T_{N}<t<T_{N+1},\,\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}\geq\frac{1}{1-T_{N}/t}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Bigg[t-\sqrt{\epsilon\sigma^{2}N}<T_{N}\leq t-\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}\sigma^{2}N},\,S_{N+1}>t-T_{N},\,\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{t-T_{N}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{P}\Big[-\sqrt{\epsilon\sigma^{2}N}<T_{N}-t\leq-\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}\sigma^{2}N}\Big]\cdot\mathbb{P}\Big[S_{1}>\sqrt{\epsilon\sigma^{2}N}\Big]\cdot\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}\sigma^{2}N}}\Bigg]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\Big[-\sqrt{\epsilon\sigma^{2}N}<T_{N}-t\leq-\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}\sigma^{2}N}\Big]\cdot e^{-\epsilon\sigma^{2}N}\cdot\mathbb{P}\bigg[\epsilon\sigma Z_{1}\geq\frac{t^{2}}{\sqrt{N^{3}}}\bigg].

At this point, by taking
t=\mu N
and by sending
N
to infinity we
obtain

\limsup_{t\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in C\bigg]\geq-\frac{\epsilon\sigma^{2}}{\mu}

(2.7)

because
\lim_{N\uparrow\infty}\mathbb{P}[-\sqrt{\epsilon\sigma^{2}N}<T_{N}-\mu N\leq-\sqrt{\epsilon^{2}\sigma^{2}N}]=(1/\sqrt{2\pi})\int_{-\sqrt{\epsilon}}^{-\epsilon}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\zeta^{2}}d\zeta>0
by the central limit theorem and

\lim_{N\uparrow\infty}\mu^{2}\sqrt{N}\,\mathbb{P}[\epsilon\sigma Z_{1}\geq\mu^{2}\sqrt{N}]=\epsilon\sigma/\pi
. The arbitrariness of

\epsilon
in (
2.7
) gives (
2.6
).

2.4
The upper large deviation bound for compact sets

The upper large deviation bound for compact sets is basically due to
the following lemma.

Lemma 2.7
.

For each
w\in\mathcal{X}
and real number
\lambda<I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)
there exists

\delta>0
such that

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta}\bigg]\leq-\lambda.

Proof.

Pick
w\in\mathcal{X}
and a real number
\lambda<I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)
. If

\ell_{\mathrm{s}}<+\infty
, then the lemma is an immediate application of part
(d) of theorem
1.1
. In fact, by the lower semicontinuity of

I_{\mathrm{s}}
there exists
\delta>0
such that
I_{\mathrm{s}}(v)\geq\lambda
for all

v\in B_{w,\delta}
, and the open ball
B_{w,\delta}
is
convex. Assume
\ell_{\mathrm{s}}=+\infty
. In such case we have

\lambda<I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)=\Upsilon(1,w)
and by the lower semicontinuity of

\Upsilon
there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that
\Upsilon(s,v)\geq\lambda

for every
s\in[1-\delta,1]
and
v\in B_{w,\delta}
. It follows by lemma

2.5
that for all
n\geq 1
and
t>0

\ln\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(S_{i},X_{i})\in[1-\delta,1]\times B_{w,\delta}\Bigg]\leq-t\inf_{(s,w)\in[1-\delta,1]\times B_{w,\delta}}\big\{\Upsilon(s,v)\big\}\leq-\lambda t.

Moreover, lemma
2.4
gives that
\mathbb{P}[T_{\lfloor\gamma t\rfloor}\leq t]\leq e^{-\lambda t}
for all sufficiently large
t

and some real number
\gamma>0
. Setting
q_{t}:=\lfloor\gamma t\rfloor
, bound (
2.4
) yields for all sufficiently
large
t

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>t]+\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in B_{w,\delta},\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg]+e^{-\lambda t}

\displaystyle\leq q_{t}\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>\delta t]+\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in B_{w,\delta},\,t-S_{n+1}<T_{n}\leq t,\,S_{n+1}\leq\delta t\Bigg]+e^{-\lambda t}

\displaystyle\leq q_{t}\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>\delta t]+\sum_{n=1}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in B_{w,\delta},\,\frac{T_{n}}{t}\in[1-\delta,1]\Bigg]+e^{-\lambda t}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma t\,\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>\delta t]+\gamma te^{-\lambda t}

This inequality proves the lemma since

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}(1/t)\ln\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>\delta t]=-\infty
as

\ell_{\mathrm{s}}=+\infty
.
∎

Let us verify part (c) of theorem
1.1
. Pick a compact set
F

in
\mathcal{X}
and a real number
\lambda<\inf_{w\in F}\{I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)\}
. Lemma

2.7
guarantees that for each
w\in F
there exists

\delta_{w}>0
such that

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{w,\delta_{w}}\bigg]\leq-\lambda.

Since
F
is compact, we can find a finite number of points

w_{1},\ldots,w_{K}
in
F
such that
F\subset\cup_{k=1}^{K}B_{w_{k},\delta_{w_{k}}}
. It follows that

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in F\bigg]\leq\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in B_{w_{k},\delta_{w_{k}}}\bigg]\leq-\lambda,

which yields

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\in F\bigg]\leq-\inf_{w\in F}\big\{I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)\big\}

once
\lambda
is sent to
\inf_{w\in F}\{I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)\}
.

2.5
The upper large deviation bound for closed sets

The upper large deviation bound can be extended from compact sets to
close sets if the probability distribution of
W_{t}/t
is exponential
tight (see
[
6
]
, lemma 1.2.18), namely if for each real
number
\lambda>0
there exists a compact set
K
in
\mathcal{X}
such that

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\notin K\bigg]\leq-\lambda.

The following lemma establishes exponential tightness of the scaled
cumulant reward when
\mathcal{X}
has finite dimension and proves part (e)
of theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Assume that
\mathcal{X}
has finite dimension and that there exist numbers

\zeta\leq 0
and
\sigma>0
such that
\mathbb{E}[e^{\zeta S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
. Then, the probability distribution
of
W_{t}/t
is exponential tight. Moreover,
I_{\mathrm{i}}
and
I_{\mathrm{s}}
have
compact level sets.

Proof.

To begin with, let us observe that under the hypotheses of the lemma
we have
\lim_{\eta\downarrow-\infty}\mathbb{E}[e^{\eta S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]=0
by the dominated convergence theorem, so
that there exists
\eta\leq 0
that satisfies
\mathbb{E}[e^{\eta S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]\leq 1/2
. Let
d
be the dimension of
\mathcal{X}
,
let
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}\}
be a basis of
\mathcal{X}
, and let

\{\vartheta_{1},\ldots,\vartheta_{d}\}\subset\mathcal{X}^{\star}
be the dual
basis:
\vartheta_{k}(v_{l})
equals 1 if
k=l
and 0 otherwise for all

k
and
l
. For
k
ranging from
1
to
d
set

\varphi_{k}:=\vartheta_{k}/\|\vartheta_{k}\|
and

\varphi_{d+k}:=-\varphi_{k}
. We have
\mathbb{E}[e^{\eta S_{1}+\sigma\varphi_{k}(X_{1})}]\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{\eta S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]\leq 1/2
for every
k
. Fix a real number
\lambda>0
and introduce the
compact set
K:=\cap_{k=1}^{2d}\{w\in\mathcal{X}:\varphi_{k}(w)\leq\rho\}
,
where we have set
\rho:=(\lambda-\eta)/\sigma>0
for brevity. Since

0
does not belong to the complement
K^{c}
of
K
, for all
t>0

\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\notin K\bigg]=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in K^{c},\,T_{n}\leq t<T_{n+1}\Bigg].

As
K^{c}=\cup_{k=1}^{2d}\{w\in\mathcal{X}:\varphi_{k}(w)>\rho\}
, by making use
of the Chernoff bound twice and by recalling that
\mathbb{E}[e^{\eta S_{1}+\sigma\varphi_{k}(X_{1})}]\leq 1/2
for any
k
, we obtain for
every
t

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\notin K\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{k=1}^{2d}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varphi_{k}(X_{i})>\rho t,\,T_{n}\leq t\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\eta t-\sigma\rho t}\sum_{k=1}^{2d}\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\Big[e^{\eta S_{1}+\sigma\varphi_{k}(X_{1})}\Big]^{n}\leq 2d\,e^{-\lambda t}.

This inequality proves exponential tightness of the distribution of
W_{t}/t
.

To conclude, let us show that the level sets of
I_{\mathrm{i}}
and
I_{\mathrm{s}}
are
compact. Regarding
I_{\mathrm{i}}
, compactness of level sets follows by
combining the lower large deviation bound of part (b) of theorem

1.1
and the exponential tightness (see
[
6
]
,
lemma 1.2.18). Let us move to
I_{\mathrm{s}}
. To begin with, we observe that
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
w\in\mathcal{X}
, and
k\leq 2d

J(s,w)\geq s\eta+\sigma\varphi_{k}(w)-\ln\mathbb{E}\big[e^{\eta S_{1}+\sigma\varphi_{k}(X_{1})}\big]\geq s\eta+\sigma\varphi_{k}(w)

by definition. It follow that
\Upsilon(\beta,w)\geq\beta\eta+\sigma\varphi_{k}(w)
for each
\beta\in[0,1]
,
w\in\mathcal{X}
,
and
k\leq 2d
, which yields
I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)\geq\eta+\sigma\varphi_{k}(w)
for
every
w
and
k
. Thus, if
w
is such that
I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)\leq\lambda
for a
given real number
\lambda\geq 0
, then
\varphi_{k}(w)\leq(\lambda-\eta)/\sigma
for all
k
. This demonstrates that level set

\{w\in\mathcal{X}:I_{\mathrm{s}}(w)\leq\lambda\}
is bounded. It is closed by the lower
semicontinuity of
I_{\mathrm{s}}
.
∎

The case in which
\mathcal{X}
has infinite dimension is solved by the next
lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume that
\mathcal{X}
has infinite dimension and that
\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma S_{1}+\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for all
\sigma>0
. Then, the
probability distribution of
W_{t}/t
is exponential tight. Moreover,

\ell_{\mathrm{s}}=+\infty
and
\Upsilon(1,\cdot\,)
has compact level sets.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda>0
and set
\epsilon:=\lambda/\ln\mathbb{E}[e^{2\lambda S_{1}}]
, which is positive since
\mathbb{E}[e^{2\lambda S_{1}}]<+\infty

by hypothesis. As
\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma\|X_{1}\|}]<+\infty
for all

\sigma>0
, the theory of Cramér in separable Banach spaces tells
us that the distribution of
(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
is exponential
tight (see
[
6
]
, exercise 6.2.21). Thus, there exists a
compact set
K_{o}
in
\mathcal{X}
such that for all sufficiently large
n

\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\notin K_{o}\Bigg]\leq e^{-\lambda n/\epsilon}.

(2.8)

Let
C
be the closure of the convex hull of
\{0\}\cup K_{o}
, which is
compact (see
[
28
]
, theorem 3.20). Let
\gamma>\epsilon
be a
real number such that
\mathbb{P}[T_{\lfloor\gamma t\rfloor}\leq t]\leq e^{-\lambda t}
for all
t
large enough, which exists by lemma

2.4
, and set
K:=\{w\in\mathcal{X}:\gamma w\in C\}
, which clearly
is a compact set. Set
p_{t}:=\lfloor\epsilon t\rfloor
and

q_{t}:=\lfloor\gamma t\rfloor
. For all sufficiently large
t
we have

1\leq p_{t}<q_{t}
and
\mathbb{P}[T_{p_{t}}>t]\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{2\lambda S_{1}}]^{p_{t}}e^{-2\lambda t}\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{2\lambda S_{1}}]^{\epsilon t}e^{-2\lambda t}=e^{-\lambda t}
by the Chernoff bound. Then, for
all sufficiently large
t
we can write

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\notin K\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\big[T_{p_{t}}>t\big]+\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\notin K,\,T_{p_{t}}\leq t<T_{q_{t}}\bigg]+\mathbb{P}\big[T_{q_{t}}\leq t\big]

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-\lambda t}+\sum_{n=p_{t}}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\notin K\Bigg].

On the other hand, the condition
(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in C
implies

(n/\gamma t)(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in C
, namely

(1/t)\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\in K
, for
n<q_{t}
as
C
is convex and contains
the origin. This shows that if
(1/t)\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\notin K
for

n<q_{t}
, then
(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\notin C
. Thus, by recalling that

K_{0}\subseteq C
and by invoking (
2.8
), for all
sufficiently large
t
we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\notin K\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-\lambda t}+\sum_{n=p_{t}}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\notin C\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{-\lambda t}+\sum_{n=p_{t}}^{q_{t}-1}\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\notin K_{o}\Bigg]\leq 2e^{-\lambda t}+\gamma t\,e^{-\lambda p_{t}/\epsilon}.

This bound yields

\limsup_{t\uparrow+\infty}\frac{1}{t}\ln\mathbb{P}\bigg[\frac{W_{t}}{t}\notin K\bigg]\leq-\lambda.

As before, the lower large deviation bound of part (b) of theorem

1.1
and the exponential tightness imply that
I_{\mathrm{i}}
has
compact level sets. On the other hand, since
e^{\sigma s}\,\mathbb{P}[S_{1}>s]\leq\mathbb{E}[e^{\sigma S_{1}}]<+\infty
for all
\sigma>0

and
s>0
, we have
\ell_{\mathrm{s}}=\ell_{\mathrm{i}}=+\infty
. Thus,

\Upsilon(1,\cdot\,)=I_{\mathrm{i}}
.
∎

Appendix B
Cramér’s theory for waiting times and rewards

This appendix introduces the basics of Cramér’s theory that are used
to prove theorem
1.1
. Let the space
\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}
be
endowed with the product topology and the Borel
\sigma
-field

\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X})
and consider the measure
\mu_{n}
over
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X})
defined for
each integer
n\geq 1
by

\mu_{n}:=\mathbb{P}\Bigg[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(S_{i},X_{i})\in\cdot\,\Bigg].

Of fundamental importance is the following super-multiplicativity
property.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X})
be convex and let
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 1
be two
integers. Then,
\mu_{m+n}(C)\geq\mu_{m}(C)\cdot\mu_{n}(C)
.

Proof.

See lemma 6.1.12 of
[
6
]
.
∎

Super-multiplicativity, which becomes super-additivity once logarithms
are taken, makes it possible to describe in general terms the
exponential decay with
n
of the measure
\mu_{n}
. To this purpose, we
denote by
\mathcal{L}
the extended real function over
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X})

defined by the formula

\mathcal{L}:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\bigg\{\frac{1}{n}\ln\mu_{n}\bigg\}.

If
C\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X})
is convex, then
\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}(1/n)\ln\mu_{n}(C)=\mathcal{L}(C)
due the super-additivity of
\ln\mu_{n}(C)
. The following lemma improves this result when
C
is open
as well as convex.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}
be open and convex. Then,

\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}(1/n)\ln\mu_{n}(C)
exists as an extended real
number and is equal to
\mathcal{L}(C)
.

Proof.

See lemma 1.1.14 of
[

]
.
∎

Lemma
B.2
prompts one to consider the rate function
J

that maps any
(s,w)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}
in the extended real number

J(w)
defined by

J(s,w):=-\inf\Big\{\mathcal{L}(C):C\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}\mbox{ is open convex and contains }(s,w)\Big\}.

In fact, the following weak large deviation principle is verified.

Proposition B.1
.

The following conclusions hold:

(i)

the function
J
is lower semicontinuous and convex;

(ii)

\displaystyle{\liminf_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln\mu_{n}(G)\geq-\inf_{(s,w)\in G}\{J(s,w)\}}
for each
G\subseteq\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{X}
open;

(iii)

\displaystyle{\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\ln\mu_{n}(K)\leq-\inf_{(s,w)\in K}\{J(s,w)\}}
for each

compact.

Proof.

See lemma 6.1.7 of

We conclude the appendix with a result about certain convex sets that are
met in the proof of theorem
1.1
.

Lemma B.3
.

Let
\alpha<\beta
be two real numbers and let
C\subseteq\mathcal{X}
be
open convex, closed convex, or any convex set in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
when
\mathcal{X}

is finite-dimensional. Then, for all
n\geq 1

\frac{1}{n}\ln\mu_{n}\big([\alpha,\beta]\times C\big)\leq-\inf_{(s,w)\in[\alpha,\beta]\times C}\big\{J(s,w)\big\}.

Proof.

Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{L}
, we show that

\mathcal{L}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)\leq-\inf_{(s,w)\in[\alpha,\beta]\times C}\{J(s,w)\}.

Assume
\mathcal{L}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)>-\infty
, otherwise there
is nothing to prove, and pick
\epsilon>0
. By definition, there
exists an integer
N\geq 1
such that
\mathcal{L}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)\leq(1/N)\ln\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)+\epsilon
. Notice that we
must have
\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)>0
. Completeness and
separability of
\mathcal{X}
entail that the measure that associates any

A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{X})
with
\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times A)
is tight (see

[
29
]
, theorem 7.1.7). Consequently, a compact set

K_{o}\subseteq C
can be found so that
\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)\leq\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times K_{o})+[1-\exp(-\epsilon N)]\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)
. Thus,

\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)\leq\exp(\epsilon N)\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times K_{o})
and

\mathcal{L}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)\leq(1/N)\ln\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times K_{o})+2\epsilon
follows. We
shall show in a moment that there exists a compact convex set
K
with
the property that
K_{o}\subseteq K\subseteq C
. Then, using the fact
that
K_{o}\subseteq K
we reach the further bound

\mathcal{L}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)\leq(1/N)\ln\mu_{N}([\alpha,\beta]\times K)+2\epsilon\leq\mathcal{L}([\alpha,\beta]\times K)+2\epsilon
. At
this point, we observe that on the one hand

\mathcal{L}([\alpha,\beta]\times K)=\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}(1/n)\ln\mu_{n}([\alpha,\beta]\times K)
by
super-additivity as
K
is convex, and on the other hand

\limsup_{n\uparrow\infty}(1/n)\ln\mu_{n}([\alpha,\beta]\times K)\leq-\inf_{(s,w)\in[\alpha,\beta]\times K}\{J(s,w)\}
by part
(ii)

of proposition
B.1
as
K
is compact. Thus,

\mathcal{L}([\alpha,\beta]\times C)\leq-\inf_{(s,w)\in[\alpha,\beta]\times K}\{J(s,w)\}+2\epsilon\leq-\inf_{(s,w)\in[\alpha,\beta]\times C}\{J(s,w)\}+2\epsilon
because
K\subseteq C
and the lemma follows
from the arbitrariness of
\epsilon
.

Let us prove at last that there exists a compact convex set
K
with
the property that
K_{o}\subseteq K\subseteq C
. The hypothesis that
the convex set
C
is either open or closed when
\mathcal{X}
if
infinite-dimensional comes into play here. Let
C_{o}
be the convex
hull of the compact set
K_{o}\subseteq C
and denote the closure of a
set
A
by
\mbox{cl}\,A
. The set
C_{o}
is convex and compact when
\mathcal{X}
is
finite-dimensional, whereas
\mbox{cl}\,C_{o}
is convex and compact even when

\mathcal{X}
is infinite-dimensional (see
[
28
]
, theorem
3.20). Clearly,
K_{o}\subseteq C_{o}\subseteq C
. If
\mathcal{X}
is
finite-dimensional, then the problem to find
K
is solved by

K=C_{o}
. If
\mathcal{X}
is infinite-dimensional and
C
is closed, then the
problem is solved by
K=\mbox{cl}\,C_{o}
. Some more effort is needed when

\mathcal{X}
is infinite-dimensional and
C
is open. Assume that
C
is
open and for each
w\in C
let
\delta_{w}>0
be such that
\mbox{cl}\,B_{w,\delta_{w}}\subseteq C
. As
K_{o}
is compact, there exist finitely
many points
w_{1},\ldots,w_{n}
in
K_{o}
so that

K_{o}\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{n}B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}}
. Let
K
be the convex
hull of
\cup_{i=1}^{n}(\mbox{cl}\,B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}}\cap\mbox{cl}\,C_{o})
, which
contains
K_{o}
. The set
K
is convex and compact since it is the
convex hull of the union of the compact convex sets
\mbox{cl}\,B_{w_{1},\delta_{w_{1}}}\cap\mbox{cl}\,C_{o},\ldots,\mbox{cl}\,B_{w_{n},\delta_{w_{n}}}\cap\mbox{cl}\,C_{o}
(see
[
28
]
, theorem 3.20). On the other hand, we have

K\subseteq C
because
\cup_{i=1}^{n}(\mbox{cl}\,B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}}\cap\mbox{cl}\,C_{o})\subseteq\cup_{i=1}^{n}\mbox{cl}\,B_{w_{i},\delta_{w_{i}}}\subseteq C
.
∎

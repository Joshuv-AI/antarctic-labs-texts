Stochastic integration with respect to canonical α \alpha -stable cylindrical Lévy processes
Gergely Bodó, Markus Riedle

Abstract
In this work, we introduce a theory of stochastic integration with respect to symmetric α \alpha -stable cylindrical Lévy processes. Since α \alpha -stable cylindrical Lévy processes do not enjoy a semi-martingale decomposition, our approach is based on a decoupling inequality for the tangent sequence of the Radonified increments. This approach enables us to characterise the largest space of predictable Hilbert-Schmidt operator-valued processes which are integrable with respect to an α \alpha -stable cylindrical Lévy process as the collection of all predictable processes with paths in the Bochner space L α L^{\alpha} . We demonstrate the power and robustness of the developed theory by establishing a dominated convergence result allowing the interchange of the stochastic integral and limit.

60H05, 60G20, 60G52, 28C20

Keywords and phrases:

cylindrical Lévy process, stochastic integration, stable processes, decoupled
tangent sequence

Introduction

Symmetric
\alpha
-stable distributions are popular for modelling random perturbations in the Euclidean space; see e.g. Samorodnitsky and Taqqu
[
32
]
. This
is because they are natural, discontinuous and non-Gaussian generalisations of Brownian motions meeting various empirical requests, such as heavy tails, self-similarity and infinite variance. As perturbations of infinite dimensional systems, such as partial differential equations, models with stable distributions can usually only be found realised as random fields but not as stochastic processes in a Hilbert or Banach space. This fact is not surprising since a random noise with a symmetric
\alpha
-stable distribution does not exist as an ordinary Hilbert space-valued process but only in the generalised sense of Gel’fand and Vilenkin
[
8
]
or Segal
[
34
]
as cylindrical processes. This is analogous to the standard Brownian motion in an infinite dimensional Hilbert space which only exists as a cylindrical process.

The purpose of this work is to provide a comprehensive and robust theory of stochastic integration for predictable processes with respect to symmetric
\alpha
-stable cylindrical processes, which will lay the foundation for the application of these processes as a model of random perturbations of infinite dimensional systems. We do not only introduce the stochastic integral but also characterise the largest set of predictable Hilbert-Schmidt-valued processes which are integrable with respect to a symmetric
\alpha
-stable cylindrical process.
Including predictable integrands in the theory of stochastic integration is important, as solutions of stochastic partial differential equation driven by cylindrical Lévy processes do not necessarily have càdlàg trajectories; see e.g. Brzeźniak et al.
[
3
]
.
We demonstrate the power of the developed integration theory by establishing a dominated convergence result allowing the interchange of the stochastic integral and limit.

The classical approach to stochastic integration in finite-dimensional spaces is based on the semi-martingale decomposition; see Dellacherie and Meyer
[
6
]
. The reverse approach, starting with good integrators, is introduced in Protter
[
28
]
or in Kurtz and Protter
[
15
]
for an infinite dimensional setting; however, extending the space from adapted, càglàd to arbitrary predictable integrands in Protter
[
28
]
also depends on the semi-martingale decomposition. A completely different approach is introduced in Bichteler
[
2
]
, where the construction of the stochastic integral for real-valued integrands and integrators mimics that of the Daniell integral in calculus. Another approach defines the stochastic integral as a vector-valued random measure;
see e.g. Métivier and Pellaumail
[
22
]
or Rao
[
29
]
. A decoupling inequality for tangent sequences is the foundation for stochastic integration developed by Kwapień and Woyczyński in
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
; their approach also allows for the characterisation of the largest
space of predictable integrands as a randomised Musielak-Orlicz space. This approach is extended to Hilbert space-valued semi-martingales in Nowak
[
25
]
.

Turning to stochastic integration with respect to cylindrical processes, an extensive theory has been developed
for stochastic integrals with respect to cylindrical Brownian motions, which has recently been extended even to UMD Banach spaces; see van Neerven
[
37
]
. Surprisingly, stochastic integration with respect to other cylindrical processes than cylindrical Brownian motion is much less considered. In fact, only with respect to cylindrical martingales a stochastic integration theory is developed either by following a Doléans measure approach by Métivier and Pellaumail in
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
, or by constructing a family of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces in Mikulevičius and Rozovskiǐ in
[
23
]
and
[
24
]
.
For the special case of a cylindrical Lévy process with finite weak second moments, an Itô approach to stochastic integration is developed in Riedle
[
30
]
. The reason for the restriction to cylindrical martingales is due to the fact that cylindrical semi-martingales do not enjoy a semi-martingale decomposition, and thus the classical approach to stochastic integration, mentioned above, is not applicable. This obstacle is overcome in Jakubowski and Riedle
[
12
]
, in which a stochastic integral for adapted, càglàd integrands and arbitrary cylindrical Lévy processes is introduced. The approach is based on a decoupling inequality for tangent sequences, but seems neither to allow an extension to include predictable integrands nor to establish some powerful limit theorems.

The approach in the current work also relies on a decoupling inequality for tangent sequences, but we characterise convergence of the stochastic integral in the semi-martingale topology to be equivalent to convergence in probability in the space of random variables with values in a Bochner space. This approach originates from Kwapień and Woyczyński
[
19
]
, in which the equivalent topology is defined on the space of random variables with values in a certain Musielak-Orlicz space. This equivalent description of a topology for the convergence of the stochastic integral allows the characterisation of the largest space of predictable integrands.

The paper is organised as follows: we summarise some preliminaries on genuine and cylindrical Lévy processes in Section 2. The theory of stochastic integration for deterministic integrands is introduced in Section 3. The following Section 4 is devoted to providing an approximation result for the predictable integrands under consideration. In Section 5, we explicitly construct the tangent sequence of the Radonified increments, which is
fundamental for our approach. In the final Section 6, we state our main result on the equivalent description of the largest space of predictable integrands as a Bochner space
L^{\alpha}
, and provide its proof. We finish the section with a result on dominated convergence.

2
Preliminaries

Let
G
and
H
be separable Hilbert spaces with inner products
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and corresponding norms
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert
. Let
(a_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(b_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be orthonormal bases of
G
and
H
, respectively. We identify the dual of a Hilbert space by the space itself. The Borel
\sigma
-algebra of
H
is denoted by
\Borel(H)
and the open unit ball by
B_{H}:=\{h\in H:\left\lVert h\right\rVert<1\}
.

The Banach space of bounded linear operators from
G
to
H
will be denoted by
L(G,H)
with the operator norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{G\rightarrow H}
. Its subspace
L_{2}(G,H)
of Hilbert-Schmidt operators is endowed with the norm

\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{\rm HS}^{2}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\lVert Fa_{k}\right\rVert^{2}
for
F\in L_{2}(G,H)
.
We denote by
L_{\rm Leb}^{\alpha}([0,T],L_{2}(G,H))
the space of Borel measurable functions
f:[0,T]\rightarrow L_{2}(G,H)
that satisfy the condition
\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert f(t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}t<\infty
. For
\alpha\geq 1
, this is a Banach space
if equipped with the usual norm
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}^{\alpha}:=\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert f(t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}t
, and for
0<\alpha<1
it is a metric space under the translation invariant metric
d(f,g)=\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert f(t)-g(t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}t
. For ease of notation, we also use the notation
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}
to denote the metric
d(0,f)
for
0<\alpha<1
.

Let
(\Omega,\Sigma,P)
be a complete probability space. We will denote by
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega,H)
the space of equivalence classes of measurable functions
X\colon\Omega\rightarrow H
, equipped with the topology of convergence in probability.

Let
S
be a subset of
G
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, elements
g_{1},...,g_{n}\in S
and Borel set
A\in\Borel(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define

C(g_{1},...,g_{n};A):=\{g\in G:(\langle g,g_{1}\rangle,...,\langle g,g_{n}\rangle)\in A\}.

Such sets are called cylindrical sets with respect to
A
. The set of all these cylindrical sets is denoted by
\mathcal{Z}(G,S)
, and it is a
\sigma
-algebra if
S
is finite and otherwise an algebra. We write

\mathcal{Z}(G)
for
\mathcal{Z}(G,G)

A set function
\mu:\mathcal{Z}(G)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is called a cylindrical measure on
\mathcal{Z}(G)
if for each finite dimensional subset
S\subseteq G
, the restriction of
\mu
to the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{Z}(G,S)
is a
\sigma
-additive measure. A cylindrical measure is said to be a cylindrical probability measure if
\mu(G)=1
.

A cylindrical random variable
X
in
G
is a linear and continuous mapping
X\colon G\rightarrow L_{P}^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
. It defines a cylindrical probability measure
\mu_{X}
by

\displaystyle\mu_{X}\colon\mathcal{Z}(G)\to[0,1],\qquad\mu_{X}(Z)=P\big((Xg_{1},\dots,Xg_{n})\in A\big)

for cylindrical sets
Z=C(g_{1},...,g_{n};A)
. The cylindrical probability measure
\mu_{X}
is called the
cylindrical distribution
of
X
.
We define the characteristic function of the cylindrical random variable
X
by

\varphi_{X}\colon G\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\qquad\varphi_{X}(g)=E\big[e^{iXg}\big].

Let
T\colon G\to H
be a linear and continuous operator. By defining

\displaystyle TX\colon H\to L_{P}^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}),\qquad(TX)h=X(T^{\ast}h)

we obtain a cylindrical random variable on
H
. In the special case when
T
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator and hence
0
-Radonifying by
[
36
, Th. VI.5.2]
, it follows from
[
36
, Pr. VI.5.3]
that the cylindrical random variable
TX
is induced by a genuine random variable
Y\colon\Omega\to H
, that is

(TX)h=\langle Y,h\rangle
for all
h\in H
. The following result shows that the inducing random variable
Y
continuously depends on the Hilbert-Schmidt operator.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a cylindrical random variable and
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
a sequence in
L_{2}(G,H)
converging to
F
in
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\rm HS}
. Then
(F_{n}X)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
FX
in
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega,H)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu_{X}
denote the cylindrical distribution of
X
.
As the sequence
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is compact in
L_{2}(G,H)
, the collection of measures
\{\mu_{X}\circ F_{n}^{-1}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is relatively compact in the space of probability measures on
\Borel(H)
; see
[
12
, Pr. 5.3]
. Continuity of
X
implies for all
h\in H
that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\langle F_{n}X,h\rangle=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}X(F_{n}^{*}h)=X(F^{*}h)=\langle FX,h\rangle\qquad\text{in }L^{0}_{P}(\Omega,\R).

Together with relative compactness, this implies that
(F_{n}X)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
FX
in
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega,H)
; see e.g.
[
10
, Le. 2.4]
.
∎

A family
(L(t):t\geq 0)
of cylindrical random variables
L(t)\colon G\rightarrow L_{P}^{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
is called a cylindrical Lévy process if for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g_{1},...,g_{n}\in G
, the stochastic process

\big(\big(L(t)g_{1},...,L(t)g_{n}\big):t\geq 0\big)

is a Lévy process in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The filtration generated by
(L(t):t\geq 0)
is defined by

\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(\{L(s)g\colon g\in G,s\in[0,t]\})\quad\text{for all}\;t\geq 0.

Denote by
\mathcal{Z}_{*}(G)
the collection

\big\{\{g\in G:(\langle g,g_{1}\rangle,...,\langle g,g_{n}\rangle)\in B\}:n\in\mathbb{N},g_{1},...,g_{n}\in G,B\in\Borel{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})\big\}

of cylindrical sets, which forms an algebra of subsets of
G
. For fixed
g_{1},...,g_{n}\in G
, let
\lambda_{g_{1},\dots,g_{n}}
be the Lévy measure of
\big((L(t)g_{1},...,L(t)g_{n}):t\geq 0\big)
. Define a function

\lambda\colon\mathcal{Z}_{*}(G)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
by

\displaystyle\lambda(C):=\lambda_{g_{1},\dots,g_{n}}(B)\quad\text{for }C=\{g\in G:\,(\langle g,g_{1}\rangle,...,\langle g,g_{n}\rangle)\in B\},

for
B\in\Borel(\R^{n})
. It is shown in
[
1
]
that
\lambda
is well defined. The set function
\lambda
is called the cylindrical Lévy measure of
L
.

In this paper, we restrict our attention to canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy processes. These are
cylindrical Lévy processes with characteristic function
\varphi_{L(t)}(g)=\exp(-t\left\lVert g\right\rVert^{\alpha})
for each
t\geq 0
and
g\in G
.
By
[
31
, Le. 2.4]
, the cylindrical Lévy measure
\lambda
of the canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy processes satisfies the spectral
representation

\lambda\circ\pi_{a_{1},...,a_{n}}^{-1}(B)=\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\nu_{n}({\rm d}x)\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}_{B}(rx)\frac{1}{r^{1+\alpha}}\,{\rm d}r\quad\text{for}\;B\in\Borel({\mathbb{R}^{n}}),

(2.1)

where
S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}:=\{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\beta|=1\}
,
c_{\alpha}>0
is a constant dependent only on
\alpha
and
\nu_{n}
denotes a uniform distribution on the sphere
S_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
.

Infinitely divisible measures on a Hilbert space
H
can be defined as in the Euclidean space; see
[
26
]
.
As in finite dimensions, infinitely divisible distributions are completely characterised by a triplet
(a,\rho,\lambda)
, where
a\in H
, the mapping
\rho\colon H\to H
is nuclear and non-negative, and the Lévy measure
\lambda
is a
\sigma
-finite measure on
\Borel(H)
satisfying

\int_{H}\big(\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\wedge 1\big)\,\lambda({\rm d}h)<\infty
. Given any
\delta>0
and Lévy measure
\lambda
on
\Borel(H)
, we say that
\delta\in C(\lambda)
if
\lambda(\{h\in H:\left\lVert h\right\rVert=\delta\})=0
. A sequence of infinitely divisible measures
\mu_{n}=(a_{n},\rho_{n},\lambda_{n})
with associated sequence
(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
S
-operators
T_{n}\colon H\to H
, which are defined by

\displaystyle\langle T_{n}h_{1},h_{2}\rangle=\langle\rho_{n}h_{1},h_{2}\rangle+\int_{\left\lVert h_{1}\right\rVert\leq 1}\langle h_{1},u\rangle\langle h_{2},u\rangle\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}u)\qquad\text{for all }h_{1},h_{2}\in H,

converges weakly to an infinitely divisible measure
\mu=(a,\rho,\lambda)
if and only if the following conditions hold:

\displaystyle(1)\;\displaystyle a=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta\downarrow 1\\
\delta\in C(\lambda)\end{subarray}}\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(a_{n}+\int_{1<\left\lVert h\right\rVert\leq\delta}h\;\lambda_{n}(dh)\bigg);

(2.2)

\displaystyle(2)\;\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\left\lVert h\right\rVert\leq\delta}\langle h,u\rangle^{2}\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}u)+(\rho_{n}h,h)=(\rho h,h)\text{ for all }h\in H;

(2.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle(3)\;\lambda_{n}\to\lambda\text{ weakly outside of every closed neighbourhood of the origin;}

(2.4)

\displaystyle(4)\;\text{$(T_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}$ is compact in the space of nuclear operators.}

(2.5)

The necessity of these conditions can be found in
[
20
, Pr. 5.7.4]
and their sufficiency is an adaption of
[
26
, Th. 5.5]
to the case of a discontinuous truncation function.

It is well known, even in finite dimensions, that the small jumps of the converging sequence
\mu_{n}
may contribute to the Gaussian part of the limit distribution
\mu
; see (
2.3
). However, if we rule out this situation, we obtain the following result.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mu_{n}=(0,0,\lambda_{n})
be a sequence of infinitely divisible measures on
\Borel(H)
converging weakly to
\mu=(0,0,\lambda)
. Then, it holds that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{H}\Big(\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\wedge 1\Big)\;\lambda_{n}\,({\rm d}h)=\int_{H}\Big(\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\wedge 1\Big)\;\lambda\,({\rm d}h).

Proof.

It is enough to prove the statement under the assumption that
\lambda_{n}
is symmetric for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, since the general case can be reduced back to this setting by a symmetrisation argument. Let
\delta\in C(\lambda)
be such that
\delta\in(0,1)
. Then, it follows directly from Condition (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\left\lVert h\right\rVert>\delta}\Big(\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\wedge 1\Big)\;\lambda_{n}\,({\rm d}h)=\int_{\left\lVert h\right\rVert>\delta}\Big(\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\wedge 1\Big)\;\lambda\,({\rm d}h).

To establish the result for the integrals over the closed ball of radius
\delta
, note that the infinitely divisible measures
\tilde{\mu}_{n}=(0,0,\lambda_{n}|_{\bar{B}_{H}(\delta)})
, where
\lambda_{n}|_{\bar{B}_{H}(\delta)}=\lambda_{n}(\bar{B}_{H}(\delta)\cap\,\cdot\,)
, converge weakly to the infinitely divisible measure
\tilde{\mu}=(0,0,\lambda|_{\bar{B}_{H}(\delta)})
. Therefore, the set of measures
\{\tilde{\mu}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is weakly compact and it follows from
[
26
, Le. VI.5.3]
that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{H}\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{4}\,\tilde{\mu}_{n}({\rm d}h)<\infty.

By the Skorokhod representation theorem and the Vallée-Poussin theorem
[
5
, Le. Th. II.22]
, it follows as
n\to\infty
that

\int_{\bar{B}_{H}(\delta)}\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\,\lambda_{n}({\rm d}h)=\int_{H}\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\,\tilde{\mu}_{n}({\rm d}h)\to\int_{H}\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\,\tilde{\mu}({\rm d}h)=\int_{\bar{B}_{H}(\delta)}\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\,\lambda({\rm d}h),

which completes the proof.
∎

3
Deterministic integrands

The definition of the stochastic integral for deterministic integrands with respect to a canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process
L
depends on two classes of step functions. We give in the following a precise definition of what is meant by a step function.

Definition 3.1
.

(1)

An
L_{2}(G,H)
-valued step function is of the form

\displaystyle\psi\colon[0,T]\rightarrow L_{2}(G,H),\qquad\psi(t)=F_{0}\mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}F_{i}\mathbb{1}_{(t_{i},t_{i+1}]}(t),

(3.1)

where
0=t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=T
,
F_{i}\in L_{2}(G,H)
for each
i\in\{0,...,n-1\}
. The space of
L_{2}(G,H)
-valued step functions is denoted by
\mathcal{S}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}:=\mathcal{S}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}(G,H)
.

(2)

An
L(H,H)
-valued step function is of the form

\displaystyle\gamma\colon[0,T]\rightarrow L(H,H),\qquad\gamma(t)=F_{0}\mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}F_{i}\mathbb{1}_{(t_{i},t_{i+1}]}(t),

(3.2)

where
0=t_{1}<\cdots<t_{n}=T
and
F_{i}\in L(H,H)
for each
i\in\{0,...,n-1\}
. The space of
L(H,H)
-valued step functions with
\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\gamma(t)\right\rVert_{H\rightarrow H}\leq 1
is denoted by
\mathcal{S}^{\rm 1,op}_{\rm det}:=\mathcal{S}^{\rm 1,op}_{\rm det}(H,H)
.

Consider an increment
L(t_{i+1})-L(t_{i})
of the cylindrical Lévy process
L
and let
F_{i}\in L_{2}(G,H)
for each
i\in\{1,...,n-1\}
. Since Hilbert-Schmidt operators are
0
-Radonifying by
[
36
, Th. VI.5.2]
, it follows from
[
36
, Pr. VI.5.3]
that there exist a genuine random variables
F_{i}\big(L(t_{i+1})-L(t_{i})\big)\colon\Omega\to H
for each
i\in\{1,...,n-1\}
satisfying

(L(t_{i+1})-L(t_{i}))(F_{i}^{*}h)=\langle F_{i}(L(t_{i+1})-L(t_{i})),h\rangle\quad\text{$P$-a.s.\ for all }h\in H.

We call the random variables
F_{i}\big(L(t_{i+1})-L(t_{i})\big)
for each
i\in\{1,...,n-1\}
Radonified increments.
The stochastic integral is defined for any
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}
with representation (
3.1
) as the sum of the Radonified increments

I(\psi):=\int_{0}^{T}\psi\,{\mathrm{d}}L=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}F_{i}(L(t_{i+1})-L(t_{i})).

Thus, the integral
I(\psi):\Omega\rightarrow H
is a genuine
H
-valued random variable.

The following definition of the stochastic integral originates from the theory of vector measures, and was adapted to the probabilistic setting in
[
35
]
by Urbanik and Woyczyński.

Definition 3.2
.

A function
\psi\colon[0,T]\rightarrow L_{2}(G,H)
is integrable if there exists a sequence
(\psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of elements of
\mathcal{S}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}
satisfying

(1)

(\psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
\psi
Lebesgue a.e.;

(2)

\displaystyle\lim_{m,n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{S}^{\rm 1,op}_{\rm det}}E\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\gamma(\psi_{m}-\psi_{n})\,{\mathrm{d}}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0.

In this case, the stochastic integral of the deterministic function
\psi
is defined by

I(\psi):=\int_{0}^{T}\psi\,{\mathrm{d}}L:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\psi_{n}\,{\mathrm{d}}L\quad\text{in}\;L_{P}^{0}(\Omega,H).

The class of all deterministic integrable Hilbert-Schmidt operator-valued functions is denoted by
\mathcal{I}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}:=\mathcal{I}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}(G,H)
.

Remark 3.3
.

If Conditions (
1
) and (
2
) in Definition
3.2
are satisfied, then completeness of
L_{P}^{0}(\Omega,H)
implies the existence of the limit. Furthermore, it follows that the integral process
(\int_{0}^{t}\psi\,{\mathrm{d}}L)_{t\geq 0}
, defined by
\int_{0}^{t}\psi\,{\mathrm{d}}L:=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t]}\psi\,{\mathrm{d}}L
has cádlág paths. To see this, note that for each
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
the process
(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\psi_{m}-\psi_{n}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}L)_{t\geq 0}
has cádlág paths. By an extension of
[
18
, Pr. 8.2.1]
to
H
-valued processes and Condition (
2
) above, we obtain

\lim_{m,n\rightarrow\infty}P\Bigg(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\left(\psi_{m}-\psi_{n}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}L\right\rVert>\epsilon\Bigg)\\
\leq 3\lim_{m,n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}P\Bigg(\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\left(\psi_{m}-\psi_{n}\right)\,{\mathrm{d}}L\right\rVert>\frac{\epsilon}{3}\Bigg)=0.

By passing on to a suitable subsequence if necessary, we obtain that there exists a subsequence
(\int_{0}^{t}\psi_{n_{k}}\,{\mathrm{d}}L)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
that converges uniformly almost surely, which guarantees that the limiting process has cádlág paths.

The following is the main result of this section, which characterises the space
\mathcal{I}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}
of deterministic integrands,
i.e. the set of functions
\psi\colon[0,T]\rightarrow L_{2}(G,H)
that satisfy Definition
3.2

for the canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process
L
.

Theorem 3.4
.

The space
\mathcal{I}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}
of deterministic functions integrable with respect to the canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process in
G
for
\alpha\in(0,2)
coincides with
L_{\rm Leb}^{\alpha}\big([0,T],L_{2}(G,H)\big)
.

The rest of this chapter is devoted to proving the above theorem which is divided into a few lemmas in the following.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
L
be the canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process in
G
with cylindrical Lévy measure
\lambda
. Then there exists a constant
c_{\alpha}>0
such that

\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\psi(t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}t\leq c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{H}\big(\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\wedge 1\big)\,(\lambda\circ\psi(t)^{-1})({\rm d}h)\,{\rm d}t

for all measurable functions
\psi:[0,T]\rightarrow L_{2}(G,H)
.

Proof.

Let
F
be an operator in
L_{2}(G,H)
.
The spectral theorem for compact operators, see e.g.
[
7
, Th. 4.1]
,
guarantees that
F
has a decomposition of the form

\displaystyle F=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\gamma_{j}\langle a_{j},\cdot\rangle b_{j},

(3.3)

where
(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(b_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
are orthonormal bases of
G
and
H
, respectively, and
(\gamma_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence in
\mathbb{R}
. Let
\pi_{n}\colon H\rightarrow H
be the projection onto
\text{Span}\{b_{1},...,b_{n}\}
.
We conclude from the spectral representation (
2.1
) of the stable measure
\lambda\circ\pi_{a_{1},\dots,a_{n}}^{-1}
for each

that

\displaystyle(\lambda\circ F^{-1}\circ\pi^{-1}_{n})(\bar{B}_{H}^{c})

\displaystyle=\lambda\Big(\Big\{g\in G:\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{2}\langle a_{j},g\rangle^{2}>1\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle=\lambda\circ\pi_{a_{1},...,a_{n}}^{-1}\Big(\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{2}x_{j}^{2}>1\Big\}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{1}_{\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{2}y_{j}^{2}>1\}}(rx)\frac{1}{r^{1+\alpha}}\;{\mathrm{d}}r\;\nu_{n}({\mathrm{d}}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{2}x_{j}^{2}\Big)^{\alpha/2}\nu_{n}\,({\rm d}x),

where
\nu_{n}
is a uniform distribution on the sphere
S_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
not necessarily of unit mass.
By defining
c_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{2}
and applying Jensen’s inequality to the concave function
\beta\mapsto\beta^{\alpha/2}
with respect to the discrete probability measure
\{c_{n}^{-1}\gamma_{1}^{2},...,c_{n}^{-1}\gamma_{n}^{2}\}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{2}x_{j}^{2}\Big)^{\alpha/2}\,\nu_{n}({\rm d}x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{c_{n}^{\alpha/2}}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\gamma_{j}^{2}}{c_{n}}|x_{j}|^{\alpha}\,\nu_{n}({\rm d}x).

Letting
\nu_{n}^{1}=\frac{1}{\nu_{n}(S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})}\nu_{n}
, Lemma 2.4 and A2 in
[
31
]
imply

\displaystyle\frac{c_{n}^{\alpha/2}}{c_{\alpha}}\int_{S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\gamma_{j}^{2}}{c_{n}}|x_{j}|^{\alpha}\;\nu_{n}({\rm d}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{n}^{\alpha/2}}{c_{\alpha}}\nu_{n}(S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\gamma_{j}^{2}}{c_{n}}\int_{S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}}|x_{j}|^{\alpha}\nu_{n}^{1}({\rm d}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{n}^{\alpha/2}}{c_{\alpha}}\nu_{n}(S_{\mathbb{R}^{n}})\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(\frac{1+\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n+\alpha}{2})}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\gamma_{j}^{2}}{c_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\alpha}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\gamma_{j}^{2}\Big)^{\alpha/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c_{\alpha}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert Fa_{j}\right\rVert^{2}\Big)^{\alpha/2},

where the last step follows from the spectral representation (
3.3
). Thus, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c_{\alpha}}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\lVert Fa_{j}\right\rVert^{2}\Big)^{\alpha/2}\leq(\lambda\circ F^{-1}\circ\pi_{n}^{-1})(\bar{B}_{H}^{c}).

(3.4)

Since
\pi_{n}F\rightarrow F
in
L_{2}(G,H)
, Lemma
2.1
implies that
\big((\pi_{n}\circ F)(L(1))\big)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in probability to the random variable
F(L(1))
. Condition (
2.4
) yields

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(\lambda\circ F^{-1}\circ\pi^{-1}_{n})(\bar{B}_{H}^{c})=(\lambda\circ F^{-1})(\bar{B}_{H}^{c}).

By taking limits as
n\rightarrow\infty
on both sides in (

), we obtain

\frac{1}{c_{\alpha}}\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{\rm HS}^{\alpha}\leq(\lambda\circ F^{-1})(\bar{B}_{H}^{c}).

It follows for any measurable function
\psi:[0,T]\rightarrow L_{2}(G,H)
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left\lVert\psi(t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}^{\alpha}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\bar{B}_{H}^{c}}(\lambda\circ\psi(t)^{-1})\,({\rm d}h)\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq c_{\alpha}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{H}\big(\left\lVert h\right\rVert^{2}\wedge 1\big)(\lambda\circ\psi(t)^{-1})\,({\rm d}h)\,{\rm d}t,

which completes the proof.
∎

(3.5)

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
L
be the canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process in
G

and
(\psi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
a sequence in
\mathcal{S}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}
. Then the following are
equivalent:

(a)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert\psi_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}=0
;

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{S}^{\rm 1,op}_{\rm det}}E\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\gamma\psi_{n}\,dL\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0
.

Proof.

Let
(\psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of elements of
\mathcal{S}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}
with
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\psi_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}=0

[
14
]

\displaystyle\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{S}^{\rm 1,op}_{\rm det}}P\Bigg(\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\gamma\psi_{n}\;{\mathrm{d}}L\right\rVert>\epsilon\Bigg)

As in the case of deterministic integrands, we begin by introducing two classes of functions on which our definition of the stochastic integral depend.

Definition 4.1
.

(1)

An

-valued adapted step process

\displaystyle\Psi(\omega,t)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N(0)}F_{0,k}\mathbb{1}_{A_{0,k}}(\omega)\right)\mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N(i)}F_{i,k}\mathbb{1}_{A_{i,k}}(\omega)\right)\mathbb{1}_{(t_{i},t_{i+1}]}(t),

where

and

for all

and

. The space of all
L_{2}(G,H)
-valued adapted step processes is denoted by

\displaystyle\Gamma(\omega,t)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N(0)}F_{0,k}\mathbb{1}_{A_{0,k}}(\omega)\right)\mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}(t)+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N(i)}F_{i,k}\mathbb{1}_{A_{i,k}}(\omega)\right)\mathbb{1}_{(t_{i},t_{i+1}]}(t),

where

and

Lemma 4.3

Let

-a.e.

\Psi

\Psi\in L_{P_{T}}^{\infty}\big(\Omega\times[0,T],L_{2}(G,H)\big)

N_{\omega}:=\Big\{t\in[0,T]\colon\big(\Psi_{k}(\omega,t)-\Psi(\omega,t)\big)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\;\text{does not converge to 0}\Big\}.

The above implies that
{\rm Leb}|_{[0,T]}(N_{\omega})=0
for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
, that is, there exists an
\Omega_{0}\subseteq\Omega
with
P(\Omega_{0})=1
such that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
we have

{\rm Leb}|_{[0,T]}\Big(t\in[0,T]\colon\big(\Psi_{k}(\omega,t)-\Psi(\omega,t)\big)_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\;\text{does not converge to 0}\Big)=0.

Because
(\Psi_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly bounded and
\Psi
is bounded, we can conclude from Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem that
\left\lVert\Psi_{k}(\omega,\cdot)-\Psi(\omega,\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
for each
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
. Another application of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem yields

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi-\Psi_{k}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\left\lVert\Psi-\Psi_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}\wedge 1\Big)\,{\rm d}P=0,

which shows the claim if
\Psi
is bounded. In the case of a general
\Psi
, we define

\Psi_{n}:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow L_{2}(G,H),\qquad\Psi_{n}(\omega,t)=\begin{cases}\Psi(\omega,t)&\text{if }\left\lVert\Psi(\omega,t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}\leq n,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Clearly,
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi-\Psi_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}=0
. The first part of the proof shows that
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a sequence
(\Psi_{n,k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{\rm HS}
converging to
\Psi_{n}
as
k\to\infty
in
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}
and
P_{T}
-a.e. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
choose
k_{n}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\Psi_{n}-\Psi_{n,k_{n}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}<\frac{1}{n}
.
It follows that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\Psi-\Psi_{n,k_{n}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}\leq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg({\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\Psi-\Psi_{n})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}+{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|(\Psi_{n}-\Psi_{n,k_{n}})\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}\bigg)=0,

which completes the proof.
∎

5
Construction of the decoupled tangent sequence

The technique of constructing decoupled tangent sequences is a powerful tool to obtain strong results on a sequence of possibly dependent random variables. In this section, we briefly recall the fundamental definition, see e.g. Kwapień and Woyczyński
[
18
]
or de la Peña and Giné
[
27
]
, and construct the decoupled tangent sequence in our setting which will enable us to identify the largest space of predictable integrands in the next section.

Remark 5.1
.

We repeatedly use the fact in the following that given a random variable
X
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P)
and another probability space
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F^{\prime}},P^{\prime})
, the random variable
X
can always be considered as a random variable on the product space
(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P\otimes P^{\prime})
by defining

X(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=X(\omega)\quad\text{for all}\;(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\in\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}.

In this case, if
X
is real-valued and
P
-integrable we have
E_{P}[X]=E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}[X]
.

In the next definition, we follow closely Chapter 4.3 of
[
18
]
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\big(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,(\mathcal{F}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big)
be a filtered probability space and
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
an
(\mathcal{F}_{n})
-adapted sequence of
H
-valued random variables. If
\big(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F^{\prime}},P^{\prime},(\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big)
is another filtered probability space, then a sequence
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
H
-valued random variables defined on
\big(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}},P\otimes P^{\prime},(\mathcal{F}_{n}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big)
is said to be a decoupled tangent sequence to
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
if

(1)

for each
\omega\in\Omega
, we have that
(Y_{n}(\omega,\cdot))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of independent random variables on
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
;

(2)

the sequences
(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(Y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\mathcal{L}(X_{n}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{n-1})=\mathcal{L}(Y_{n}|\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{n-1})\quad P\otimes P^{\prime}-\text{a.s.}

The main tool for establishing the stochastic integral in the next section is a cylindrical Lévy process
\widetilde{L}
on an enlarged probability space, whose Radonified increments are decoupled to the Radonified increments of the original canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process. This cylindrical Lévy process
\widetilde{L}
is explicitly constructed in the following result.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
L
be a cylindrical Lévy process on
G
,
0=t_{0}\leq...\leq t_{N}=T
be a partition of
[0,T]
and for each
n=1,...,N
we define
\Theta_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{M(n)}F_{n,k}\mathbb{1}_{A_{n,k}}
, where
F_{n,k}\in L_{2}(G,H)
,
A_{n,k}\in\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}
for all
k=1,...,M(n)
.
By defining cylindrical random variables

\widetilde{L}(t)\colon G\rightarrow L^{0}_{P\otimes P}(\Omega\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}),\qquad\Big(\widetilde{L}(t)g\Big)(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=\Big(L(t)g\Big)(\omega^{\prime}),

it follows that
(\widetilde{L}(t):t\geq 0)
is a cylindrical Lévy process on
G
and the sequence of its Radonified increments

\left(\Theta_{n}\big(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1})\big)\right)_{n\in\{1,...,N\}}

defined on
\big(\Omega\times\Omega,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F},P\otimes P,(\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}})_{n\in\{0,...,N\}}\big)
is a decoupled tangent sequence to the sequence of Radonified increments

\Big(\Theta_{n}\big(L(t_{n})-L(t_{n-1})\big)\Big)_{n\in\{1,...,N\}}

defined on
\big(\Omega,\mathcal{F},P,(\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}})_{n\in\{0,...,N\}}\big).

Proof.

In order to make it easier to follow this proof, we define
\Omega^{\prime}=\Omega
,
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}=\mathcal{F}
,
P^{\prime}=P
and
\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n}}=\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}
for all
n\in\{0,...,N\}
and instead of denoting the filtered product space by

\Big(\Omega\times\Omega,\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F},P\otimes P,(\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}})_{n\in\{0,...,N\}}\Big),

we write

\Big(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}},P\otimes P^{\prime},(\mathcal{F}_{t_{n}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n}})_{n\in\{0,...,N\}}\Big).

The fact that for each
t\geq 0
the mapping
\widetilde{L}(t)\colon G\rightarrow L_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}^{0}(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime},\mathbb{R})
is continuous follows directly from the definition of
\widetilde{L}
and Remark
5.1
. Thus
\widetilde{L}
is a cylindrical stochastic process. To prove that it is in fact a cylindrical Lévy process, let us fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in G
and consider the
n
-dimensional processes

Y
and
Z
defined by
Y(t)=(\widetilde{L}(t)g_{1},\ldots,\widetilde{L}(t)g_{n})
and
Z(t)=(L(t)g_{1},\ldots,L(t)g_{n})
.
It is enough to show that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and times
0\leq t_{0}<\cdots<t_{m}\leq T
the random variables
Y(t_{m})-Y(t_{m-1}),\ldots,Y(t_{1})-Y(t_{0})
and
Z(t_{m})-Z(t_{m-1}),\dots,Z(t_{1})-Z(t_{0})

have the same distribution. Here we only prove that for any
0\leq s<t\leq T
the random variables
Y(t)-Y(s)
and
Z(t)-Z(s)
have the same distribution. The general case follows analogously. To see this, let
A\in\Borel(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be arbitrary. The very definition of
\widetilde{L}
shows

\displaystyle(P\otimes P^{\prime})\left(Y(t)-Y(s)\in A\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=(P\otimes P^{\prime})\left((\widetilde{L}(t)g_{1}-\widetilde{L}(s)g_{1},...,\widetilde{L}(t)g_{n}-\widetilde{L}(s)g_{n})\in A\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=(P\otimes P^{\prime})\left(\Omega\times\left\{(L(t)g_{1}-L(s)g_{1},...,L(t)g_{n}-L(s)g_{n})\in A\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=P^{\prime}\left((L(t)g_{1}-L(s)g_{1},...,L(t)g_{n}-L(s)g_{n})\in A\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=P\left(Z(t)-Z(s)\in A\right).

To show that the Radonified increments of
\tilde{L}
satisfy Condition (1) of Definition
5.2
, fix some
\omega\in\Omega
. Then
\Theta_{n}(\omega)
is a (deterministic) Hilbert-Schmidt operator and
(\widetilde{L}(t)(\omega,\cdot):t\geq 0)
is a cylindrical Lévy process in
G
. Thus, for a fixed
\omega\in\Omega
and
n\in\{1,...,N\}
, the Radonified increment
\Theta_{n}(\omega)(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})(\omega,\cdot)-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1})(\omega,\cdot))
is an
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{t_{n}}
-measurable
H
-valued random variable on
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
independent of
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}_{t_{n-1}}
. It follows for each
\omega\in\Omega
that

\left(\Theta_{n}(\omega)(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})(\omega,\cdot)-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1})(\omega,\cdot))\right)_{n\in\{1,...,N\}}

is a sequence of independent random variables.

For establishing Condition (2) of Definition
5.2
, we define for each
n\in\{1,...,N\}

the
H
-valued random variables

\displaystyle X_{n}:=\Theta_{n}\big(L(t_{n})-L(t_{n-1})\big):=\sum_{k=1}^{M(n)}\mathbb{1}_{A_{n,k}}F_{n,k}\big(L(t_{n})-L(t_{n-1})\big),

\displaystyle Y_{n}:=\Theta_{n}\big(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1})\big):=\sum_{k=1}^{M(n)}\mathbb{1}_{A_{n,k}}F_{n,k}\big(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1})\big),

where
F_{n,k}\big(L(t_{n})-L(t_{n-1})\big)
and
F_{n,k}\big(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1})\big)
refer to the Radonified increments. Choose regular versions of the conditional distributions

\displaystyle(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{X_{n}}\colon\Borel(H)\times(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime})\rightarrow[0,1],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{X_{n}}\big(B,(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\big)=(P\otimes P^{\prime})(X_{n}\in B|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n-1}})(\omega,\omega^{\prime}),

\displaystyle(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{Y_{n}}\colon\Borel(H)\times(\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime})\rightarrow[0,1],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{Y_{n}}\big(B,(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\big)=(P\otimes P^{\prime})(Y_{n}\in B|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n-1}})(\omega,\omega^{\prime}).

Since
\widetilde{L}(t)
is a cylindrical Lévy process, we obtain for all
h\in H
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\displaystyle E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\Big[e^{i\langle Y_{n},h\rangle}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n-1}}\Big]

\displaystyle=E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\left[e^{i\left\langle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M(n)}\mathbb{1}_{A_{n,k}\times\Omega^{\prime}}F_{n,k}\right)(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1})),h\right\rangle}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n-1}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M(n)}E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Big[\mathbb{1}_{{A_{n,k}}\times\Omega^{\prime}}\,e^{{i\langle F_{n,k}(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1}))},h\rangle}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n-1}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M(n)}\mathbb{1}_{{A_{n,k}}\times\Omega^{\prime}}\,E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Big[e^{{i\langle F_{n,k}(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1}))},h\rangle}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n-1}}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M(n)}\mathbb{1}_{{A_{n,k}}\times\Omega^{\prime}}\,E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Big[e^{{i\langle F_{n,k}(\widetilde{L}(t_{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{n-1}))},h\rangle}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M(n)}\mathbb{1}_{{A_{n,k}}\times\Omega^{\prime}}\,E_{P^{\prime}}\Big[e^{{i\langle F_{n,k}({L}(t_{n})-{L}(t_{n-1}))},h\rangle}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M(n)}\mathbb{1}_{{A_{n,k}}\times\Omega^{\prime}}\,e^{{-(t_{n}-t_{n-1})\left\lVert F_{n,k}^{*}h\right\rVert^{\alpha}}}

\displaystyle=e^{{-(t_{n}-t_{n-1})\left\lVert\Theta_{n}^{*}h\right\rVert^{\alpha}}}.

(5.1)

In the same way we obtain

\displaystyle E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\Big[e^{i\langle X_{n},h\rangle}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n-1}}\Big]=e^{{-(t_{n}-t_{n-1})\left\lVert\Theta_{n}^{*}h\right\rVert^{\alpha}}}.

(5.2)

It follows from (
5
) and (
5.2
) by calculating the conditional expectation from the conditional probability, see e.g.
[
13
, Th. 6.4]
, that for
P\otimes P^{\prime}
a.a.
(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\in\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}
and for all
u\in H
we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{X_{n}}(\cdot,(\omega,\omega^{\prime}))}(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}e^{i\langle h,u\rangle}\,(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{X_{n}}\big({\rm d}h,(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\big)

\displaystyle=E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Big[e^{i\langle X_{n},u\rangle}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n-1}}\Big](\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Big[e^{i\langle Y_{n},u\rangle}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{t_{n-1}}\otimes\mathcal{F^{\prime}}_{t_{n-1}}\Big](\omega,\omega^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{H}e^{i\langle h,u\rangle}\,(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{X_{n}}\big({\rm d}h,(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\big)=\varphi_{(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{Y_{n}}(\cdot,(\omega,\omega^{\prime}))}(u).

Since characteristic functions uniquely determine distributions on
\Borel(H)
, we obtain

(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{X_{n}}(\cdot,(\omega,\omega^{\prime}))=(P\otimes P^{\prime})_{Y_{n}}(\cdot,(\omega,\omega^{\prime}))\quad P\otimes P^{\prime}-\text{a.s.},

establishing Condition (2) of Definition
5.2
.
∎

6
Characterisation of random integrable processes

The following is the main result of our work characterising the largest space of predictable integrands which are stochastically integrable with respect to a canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process.

Theorem 6.1
.

The space
\mathcal{I}_{\rm prd}^{\rm HS}
of predicable Hilbert-Schmidt operator-valued processes integrable
with respect to a canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process in
G
for
\alpha\in(0,2)

coincides with predictable processes in
L_{P}^{0}\big(\Omega,L_{{\rm Leb}}^{\alpha}\big([0,T],L_{2}(G,H)\big)\big)
.

As in the case of deterministic integrands, the above characterisation of the space of integrable predictable processes
strongly relies on the equivalent notion of convergences in the two spaces.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
L
be the canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process in
G

and
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
a sequence in
\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{\rm HS}
. Then the following are
equivalent:

(a)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}=0
;

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}E\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0
.

Proof.

To prove (a)
\Rightarrow
(b), let
\epsilon>0
be fixed. Lemma
3.7
enables us to choose

\delta>0
such that for every
\psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm det}^{\rm HS}
we have
the implication:

\displaystyle\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}\leq\delta\;\Rightarrow\;\sup_{\gamma\in\mathcal{S}^{1,{\rm op}}_{\rm det}}E\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\gamma\psi\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]\leq\epsilon.

(6.1)

Since
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}=0
, there exists
n_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that the set
A_{n}:=\{\left\lVert\Psi_{n}\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}\leq\delta\}
satisfies
P(A_{n})\geq 1-\epsilon
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. Implication
(
6.1
) implies for all
\omega\in A_{n}
and
n\geq n_{0}
that

\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}P^{\prime}\left(\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}\colon\left\lVert\left(\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma(\omega)\Psi_{n}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}(\omega,\cdot)\right)(\omega^{\prime})\right\rVert>\epsilon\right)\leq\epsilon.

Since
P(A_{n})\geq 1-\epsilon
for all
n\geq n_{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}P\left(\omega\in\Omega\colon P^{\prime}\left(\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}\colon\left\lVert\left(\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma(\omega)\Psi_{n}(\omega)\,\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}(\omega,\cdot)\right)(\omega^{\prime})\right\rVert>\epsilon\right)\leq\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geq P(A_{n})\geq 1-\epsilon.

Fubini’s theorem implies for all
n\geq n_{0}
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}
that

\displaystyle(P\otimes P^{\prime})\left((\omega,\omega^{\prime})\in\Omega\times\Omega^{\prime}\colon\left\lVert\left(\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}\right)(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\right\rVert>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}P^{\prime}\left(\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}:\left\lVert\left(\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma(\omega)\Psi_{n}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}(\omega,\cdot)\right)(\omega^{\prime})\right\rVert>\epsilon\right)\,P({\rm d}\omega)\leq\epsilon+\epsilon(1-\epsilon).

As
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary, we obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0.

(6.2)

By the ideal property of
L_{2}(G,H)
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the integrand
\Gamma\Psi_{n}
lies in
\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{\rm HS}
and has a representation of the from

\displaystyle\Gamma\Psi_{n}=\Gamma_{0}^{n}F_{0}^{n}\mathbb{1}_{\{0\}}+\sum_{i=1}^{N(n)-1}\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}\mathbb{1}_{(t_{i}^{n},t_{i+1}^{n}]},

(6.3)

where
0=t_{1}^{n}\leq\cdots<t_{N(n)}^{n}=T
, and
\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{t_{i}^{n}}
-measurable
L_{2}(G,H)
-valued random variable taking only finitely many values for each
i=0,...,N(n)-1
,.
Proposition
5.3
guarantees for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
that the sequence of Radonified increments

\Big(\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}(L(t_{i+1}^{n})-L(t_{i}^{n}))\Big)_{i=1,...,N_{n}-1}

has the decoupled tangent sequence

\Big(\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}(\widetilde{L}(t_{i+1}^{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{i}^{n}))\Big)_{i=1,...,N_{n}-1}.

We conclude from the decoupling inequality
[
18
, Pr. 5.7.1.(ii)]
that there exists a constant
c>0
such that, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}
, we have

\displaystyle E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{N(n)-1}\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}(L(t_{i+1}^{n})-L(t_{i}^{n}))\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq cE_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{N(n)-1}\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}(\widetilde{L}(t_{i+1}^{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{i}^{n}))\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=cE_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg].

(6.4)

We conclude from Remark
5.1
and (
6.2
) that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}E_{P}\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0,

which shows (b).

For establishing (b)
\Rightarrow
(a), we assume the representation (
6.3
) of
\Gamma\Psi_{n}
. We conclude from
[
18
, Pr. 5.7.2]
that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\displaystyle E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{N(n)-1}\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}(\widetilde{L}(t_{i+1}^{n})-\widetilde{L}(t_{i}^{n}))\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c\max_{\epsilon_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}}E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{N(n)-1}\epsilon_{i}\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}(L(t_{i+1}^{n})-L(t_{i}^{n}))\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=c\max_{\epsilon_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}}E_{P}\Bigg[\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{N(n)-1}\epsilon_{i}\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}(L(t_{i+1}^{n})-L(t_{i}^{n}))\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq c\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}E_{P}\Bigg[\left\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{N(n)-1}\Gamma_{i}^{n}F_{i}^{n}(L(t_{i+1}^{n})-L(t_{i}^{n}))\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]

\displaystyle=c\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}E_{P}\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg].

(6.5)

By choosing
\Gamma=\text{Id}\mathbb{1}_{\Omega\times(0,T]}
, the hypothesis on
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
implies

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{P\otimes P^{\prime}}\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0.

It follows that there exists a subsequence
(\Psi_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and a set

with
(P\otimes P^{\prime})(N)=0
satisfying

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\Psi_{n_{k}}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}\Bigg)(\omega,\omega^{\prime})=0\quad\text{for each}\;(\omega,\omega^{\prime})\in N^{c}.

Define the section of the set
N
for each
\omega\in\Omega
by

N_{\omega}=\Bigg\{\omega^{\prime}\in\Omega^{\prime}\colon\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\Psi_{n_{k}}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}(\omega,\cdot)\right)(\omega^{\prime})\neq 0\Bigg\},

where we note that since
\Psi_{n_{k}}
are adapted step processes it holds that

\Bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\Psi_{n_{k}}\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}\Bigg)(\omega,\cdot)=\int_{0}^{T}\Psi_{n_{k}}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}(\omega,\cdot)\qquad\text{for all }\omega\in\Omega.

Fubini’s theorem implies
0=(P\otimes P^{\prime})(N)=\int_{\Omega}P^{\prime}(N_{\omega}){\rm d}P(\omega)
, from which it follows that there exists
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega
with
P(\Omega_{1})=1
such that
P^{\prime}(N_{\omega})=0
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
. In other words, for each fixed
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
, the sequence of random variables

\Bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\Psi_{n_{k}}(\omega)\;{\rm d}\widetilde{L}(\omega,\cdot)\Bigg)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

converges
P^{\prime}
-a.s. to
0
as
H
-valued random variables on
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},P^{\prime})
. Lemma
3.7
implies
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\Psi_{n_{k}}(\omega)\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}=0
for each fixed
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
. As
P(\Omega_{1})=1
, Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem implies

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi_{n_{k}}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\left\lVert\Psi_{n_{k}}(\omega)\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}\wedge 1\Big)\,P({\rm d}\omega)=0,

which completes the proof.
∎

.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
L
be the canonical
\alpha
-stable cylindrical Lévy process in
G

and
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
a sequence in
\mathcal{I}_{\rm prd}^{\rm HS}
. Then the following are

(a)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}=0

(b)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}E\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0
.

Proof.

To establish the implication (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) let
\epsilon>0
be fixed. Lemma
6.2
implies that there exists a
\delta(\epsilon)>0
such that we have for all
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{\rm HS}

{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}<\delta(\epsilon)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}E\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]<\epsilon.

(6.6)

Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}=0
, there exists an
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi_{n}\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}<\tfrac{\delta(\epsilon)}{2}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. As
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq L_{P}^{0}\big(\Omega,L_{{\rm Leb}}^{\alpha}\big([0,T],L_{2}(G,H)\big)\big)
, Lemma
4.3
guarantees for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the existence of a sequence
(\Psi_{n}^{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{\rm HS}
converging to
\Psi_{n}
in
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\cdot\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}

-a.e. Consequently, we can find
m_{0}(n,\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
for each

) that

Theorem 6.4
.

If
\Psi\in\mathcal{I}_{\rm prd}^{\rm HS}
, then the integral process
\left(I(\Psi)(t):t\in[0,T]\right)
defined by

\displaystyle I(\Psi)(t):=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbbm{1}_{[0,t]}(s)\Psi(s)\,L({\rm d}s)\qquad\text{for }t\in[0,T],

is a semi-martingale.

Proof.

By
[
11
, Th. 2.1]
, it suffices to show that the set

\displaystyle\left\{\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\,{\rm d}I(\Psi):\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{1,\rm{op}}\right\}

is bounded in probability. Suppose, aiming for a contradiction, that it is not the case. Then there exists an
\epsilon>0
and a sequence
(\Gamma_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{1,\rm{op}}
satisfying for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle P\left(\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{n}\,{\rm d}I(\Psi)\right\rVert>n\right)\geq\epsilon.

(6.12)

For each
\Psi\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{\rm HS}
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{1,\rm{op}}
, the very definitions of stochastic integrals show

\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\,{\rm d}I(\Psi)=\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma\Psi\,{\rm d}L.

This equality can be generalised to arbitrary
\Psi\in\mathcal{I}_{\rm prd}^{\rm HS}
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{\rm adp}^{1,\rm{op}}
by a standard approximation argument. Using this to rewrite Equation (
6.12
), we obtain for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\epsilon\leq P\left(\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma_{n}\,{\rm d}I(\Psi)\right\rVert>n\right)=P\left(\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{n}\Gamma_{n}\Psi\,{\rm d}L\right\rVert>1\right).

(6.13)

On the other hand, since
{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\frac{1}{n}\Gamma_{n}\Psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
, Corollary
6.3
implies

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E\left[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{n}\Gamma_{n}\Psi\,{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\right]=0,

which contradicts (

) because of the equivalent characterisation of the topology in
L^{0}_{P}(\Omega,H)
.
∎

We finish this section with a stochastic dominated convergence theorem.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of processes in
\mathcal{I}_{\rm prd}^{\rm HS}
such that

(1)

(\Psi_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges
P_{T}
-a.e. to an
L_{2}(G,H)
-valued predictable process
\Psi
;

(2)

there exists a process
\Upsilon\in\mathcal{I}_{\rm prd}^{\rm HS}
satisfying for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
that

\left\lVert\Psi_{n}(\omega,t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}\leq\left\lVert\Upsilon(\omega,t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}\quad\text{for $P_{T}$-a.a.\ $(\omega,t)\in\Omega\times[0,T]$.}

Then it follows that
\Psi\in\mathcal{I}_{\rm prd}^{\rm HS}
and

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P\Bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}L-\int_{0}^{t}\Psi\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert>\epsilon\Bigg)=0\qquad\text{for all }\epsilon>0.

Proof.

By assumption, there exists a set
N\subseteq\Omega\times[0,T]
with
P_{T}(N)=0
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{n}(\omega,t)=\Psi(\omega,t)
and
\left\lVert\Psi_{n}(\omega,t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}\leq\left\lVert\Upsilon(\omega,t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}
for all
(\omega,t)\in N^{c}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Fubini’s theorem yields that

0=P_{T}(N)=\int_{\Omega}{\rm Leb}|_{[0,T]}(N_{\omega})\,P({\rm d}\omega),

where

N_{\omega}:=\left\{t\in[0,T]:\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{n}(\omega,t)\neq\Psi(\omega,t)\}\,\text{or}\,\left\lVert\Psi_{n}(\omega,t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}>\left\lVert\Upsilon(\omega,t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}\right\}.

It follows that there exists an
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega
with
P(\Omega_{1})=1
such that
{\rm Leb}|_{[0,T]}(N_{\omega})=0
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
. Consequently, for each
\omega\in\Omega_{1}
we have
\left\lVert\Psi_{n}(\omega,t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}\leq\left\lVert\Upsilon(\omega,t)\right\rVert_{\rm HS}
and
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Psi_{n}(\omega,t)=\Psi(\omega,t)
for Lebesgue almost every
t\in[0,T]
. Theorem
6.1
guarantees
that there exists
\Omega_{2}\subseteq\Omega
with
P(\Omega_{2})=1
such that
\left\lVert\Upsilon(\omega,\cdot)\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}<\infty
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{2}
. The classical Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem implies that
(\Psi_{n}(\omega))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{\alpha}}
and its limit
\Psi(\omega,\cdot)
is in
L^{\alpha}
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}
. Since
P(\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2})=1
, Theorem
6.1
shows
\Psi\in\mathcal{I}_{\rm prd}^{\rm HS}
.

Another application of Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem establishes that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\kern-1.07639pt\left|\Psi_{n}-\Psi\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|\kern-1.07639pt\right|}_{L^{\alpha}}=0
. Corollary
6.3
implies

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\displaystyle\sup_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{S}_{{\rm adp}}^{1,{\rm op}}}E\Bigg[\left\lVert\int_{0}^{T}\Gamma(\Psi_{n}-\Psi)\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert\wedge 1\Bigg]=0,

(6.14)

which means that the sequence
\left(I(\Psi_{n})\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of processes converges in the semi-martingale topology to the process
I(\Psi)
. Since convergence in the semi-martingale topology implies convergence in probability on compact time intervals, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P\Bigg(\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}\Psi_{n}\;{\rm d}L-\int_{0}^{t}\Psi\;{\rm d}L\right\rVert>\epsilon\Bigg)=0\qquad\text{for all }\epsilon>0,

Linear extensions of Baire-one and Borel functions
Waldemar Sieg

Abstract
Let X X and Y Y be the Hausdorff topological spaces and let A A be both an F σ F_{\sigma} - and G δ G_{\delta} - subset of X X . Let also f : A → Y f\colon A\to Y be a function for which the inverse image of every open subset U ⊂ Y U\subset Y is F σ F_{\sigma} in X X . We show that f f can be linearly extended to a function with the same property defined on X X . A similar result is proved for Baire-one function defined on an analogous subset of ℝ {\mathbb{R}} . We give also an answer when the extension map is (with a supremum norm) an isometry.



1
Introduction

Let
X
,
Y
,
A
and
f
have the same meaning as in the abstract.
The mapping
r\colon X\to A
is an
algebraic retraction
if
r(x)=x
for every
x\in A
.
Recall that
{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(X,Y)
is the space of pointwise limits of sequences
of continuous functions
X\to Y
. We say that a function
f\colon X\to Y
is

1.

piecewise continuous
, if there is an increasing sequence
\left(X_{n}\right)\subset X
of nonempty closed sets such
that
X=\bigcup^{\infty}_{n=0}X_{n}
and the restriction
f_{\mathord{\restriction}X_{n}}
is continuous for each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
;

2.

of the first Borel class
, if the
f
-inverse image of every open subset of
Y
is
F_{\sigma}
in
X
(see
[
2
, p. 1]
);

3.

of the first level Borel class
, if the
f
-inverse image of every
F_{\sigma}
subset of
Y
is
F_{\sigma}
in
X
(see
[
2
, p. 1]
).

Note that in the last definition, the words ”
F_{\sigma}
subset of
Y
” may be equivalently replaced by ”closed subset of
Y
”.
The symbols
{\mathscr{P}}(X,Y)
,
\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,Y)
and
\mathfrak{flB}(X,Y)
stand for the families of piecewise continuous, first Borel class and first level Borel class of functions

X\to Y
, respectively. The inclusion
\mathfrak{flB}(X,Y)\subset\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,Y)

holds whenever any open set in
Y
is
F_{\sigma}
(e.g. if
Y
is perfectly normal). A function witnessing the fact that

\mathfrak{flB}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})\neq\mathfrak{fcBor}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})
is the classical Riemann function (i.e.
f(x)=0
for
x
irrational and
f(x)=\frac{1}{q}
if
x=\frac{p}{q}
where

p
,
q
are mutually prime), which is of the first Borel class but not of the first level Borel class.
If
f\in\mathfrak{fcBor}^{b}(A,Y)
and
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}^{b}(A,Y)
are bounded elements of
\mathfrak{fcBor}(A,Y)
and
{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A,Y)
, respectively, the
supremum norm

\|\cdot\|_{A}
of
f
on
A
is defined as
\|f\|_{A}=\sup_{x\in A}|f(x)|
.

This paper deals with the general problem in the theory of real functions, which is inspired by the Tietze extension theorem:

(P)
Let
A
be a nonempty subset of a topological space
X
and let
f_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{A}
be a function with a certain
property
(W)
. Can
f_{0}
be extended to a function
f\in{\mathbb{R}}^{X}
with the same property
(W)
?

Classical Tietze’s theorem says that every continuous function defined on a closed subset of a normal topological space
X
can be extended
to a function that is continuous on whole
X
. Moreover, in 1982 Jayne and Rogers
[
2
]
proved the following

Theorem 1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be metric,
Y
complete. Let
A\subset X
and
f\in{\mathscr{P}}(A,Y)
. There exists a set
A_{1}\subset X
which is
an intersection of
G_{\delta}
- and
F_{\sigma}
- subsets of
X
and a function
\overline{f}\in{\mathscr{P}}(A_{1},Y)
extending
f
.

Finally, in 2008 Karlova
[
4
, Theorem 3.2]
showed that if
X
is perfectly paracompact and
A
is its complete metrizable subspace
then every continuous function
f\colon A\to Y
has an extension
\overline{f}\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,Y)
.
In this paper we prove an analogous results for Baire-one mappings and the first Borel class of functions.
In (key) Lemma
3
we construct an algebraic retraction
{\varphi}
. Its properties allow us to formulate and prove these results.

2
Useful lemmas

The first lemma is a well-known fact (see
[
6
, Theorem 2, Section VI of
\lx@sectionsign
31]
).

Lemma 1
.

Let
X
be the Hausdorff topological space. If
f,g\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
then
f+g\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
.

The next lemma follows from the fact that
|f|
is a composition of
f
and an absolute value, which is continuous.

Lemma 2
.

Let
X
be the Hausdorff topological space. If
f\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
then
|f|\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
.

From Lemmas
1
and
2
we get the following

Corollary 1
.

If
X
is the Hausdorff topological space then
\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
is a linear lattice.

The next lemma is crucial for our future considerations.

Lemma 3
.

Let
X
be the Hausdorff topological space and let
A
be both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of
X
. Let also
g\colon X\setminus A\to A

be a continuous map. The algebraic retraction
\varphi\colon X\to A\subset X
given by the formula

\varphi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}x&\textrm{:}&x\in A\\
g(x)&\textrm{:}&x\in X\setminus A,\end{array}\right.

(1)

belongs to the class
{\mathscr{P}}(X,X)\cap\mathfrak{flB}(X,X)
. Furthermore, if
X
is perfectly normal then
{\varphi}\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,X)
. Finally, if
X={\mathbb{R}}
then

{\varphi}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(X,X)
.

Proof.

We show first that
{\varphi}\in{\mathscr{P}}(X,X)
. Let
i_{A}\colon A\to A
be an identity on
A
.
Since
A
is both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of
X
, there are increasing sequences
(F_{n})
and
(G_{n})
of closed sets such that

\bigcup_{n}F_{n}=A
and
\bigcup_{n}G_{n}=X\setminus A
. Obviously
F_{j}\cap G_{k}=\emptyset
for every
j,k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Since
g
is continuous,
the restrictions
i_{A_{\mathord{\restriction}F_{n}}}
and
g_{\mathord{\restriction}G_{n}}
are continuous for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Set
H_{n}=F_{n}\cup G_{n}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Obviously

\bigcup_{n}{H_{n}}=X
. Furthermore, the continuity of restrictions

i_{A_{\mathord{\restriction}F_{n}}}
and
g_{\mathord{\restriction}G_{n}}
implies continuity of
{\varphi}
on every
H_{n}
. That completes this part of the proof.

To show that
{\varphi}\in\mathfrak{flB}(X,X)
we assume
F
to be a closed subset of
X
. We have
{\varphi}^{-1}(F)=(A\cap F)\cup g^{-1}(F)
.
Since
A
is
F_{\sigma}
and
F
is closed,
A\cap F
is
F_{\sigma}
. Furthermore,
g^{-1}(F)
is a relatively closed subset of
X\setminus A

(which is
F_{\sigma}
), hence it is again
F_{\sigma}
. Thus
{\varphi}^{-1}(F)
is
F_{\sigma}
.

If
X
is perfectly normal then any open subset of
X
is
F_{\sigma}
. Hence, from the above proven facts, it follows that in this case
the
{\varphi}
-inverse image of any open set is
F_{\sigma}
. Thus
{\varphi}\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,X)
.

To see that
{\varphi}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})
it is enough to use the well-known fact that
\mathfrak{fcBor}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})={\mathscr{B}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})
(cf. the Lebesgue-Hausdorff theorem,

[
6
,
\lx@sectionsign
31, Section IX]
).
∎

3
The main results

Our first main result is about extending functions from the first Borel class.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X
be a perfectly normal topological space and let
A
be its both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset. Moreover, let
f\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(A,{\mathbb{R}})

and
g\colon X\setminus A\to A
be a continuous map. There exists a non-negative, preserving unity and linear extension operator

{\varphi}^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{fcBor}(A,{\mathbb{R}})\to\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
such that its restriction to

\mathfrak{fcBor}^{b}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
is an isometry.

Proof.

Set
{\varphi}^{\star}(f)=f\circ{\varphi}
, where
{\varphi}
is an algebraic retraction given by (
1
).
Positivity of
{\varphi}^{\star}
is due to the fact that the class
\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
equipped with the order by the coordinates
is a linear lattice (see Corollary
1
).
We shall show that
{\varphi}^{\star}(f)\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
for every
f\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
. Let
U
be an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
. There is

\left({\varphi}^{\star}(f)\right)^{-1}(U)=(f\circ{\varphi})^{-1}(U)={\varphi}^{-1}\left(f^{-1}(U)\right).

Since
f\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(A)
,
f^{-1}(U)
is
F_{\sigma}
in
A
. Hence
f^{-1}(U)=\bigcup_{n}K_{n}
with every
K_{n}
closed in
A
. Thus

f^{-1}(U)=\bigcup_{n}K_{n}=\bigcup_{n}(A\cap G_{n})=A\cap\bigcup_{n}G_{n}

with every
G_{n}
closed in
X
. Therefore

\left({\varphi}^{\star}(f)\right)^{-1}(U)={\varphi}^{-1}\left(A\cap\bigcup_{n}G_{n}\right)={\varphi}^{-1}(A)\cap{\varphi}^{-1}\left(\bigcup_{n}G_{n}\right)=

=X\cap\bigcup_{n}{\varphi}^{-1}(G_{n})=\bigcup_{n}{\varphi}^{-1}(G_{n}).

According to Lemma
3
, every set
{\varphi}^{-1}(G_{n})
is
F_{\sigma}
in
X
. Thence a set

\left({\varphi}^{\star}(f)\right)^{-1}(U)=\bigcup_{n}{\varphi}^{-1}(G_{n})
is
F_{\sigma}
in
X
as well.
Thus
{\varphi}^{\star}(f)\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X)
.

{\varphi}^{\star}
is of course an extension of
f
. Its linearity and preserving of the unity are obvious.
Moreover, if
f
is a bounded element of
\mathfrak{fcBor}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
, we have

\|{\varphi}^{\star}(f)\|_{X}=\sup_{x\in X}|f\left({\varphi}(x)\right)|=\sup_{a\in A}|f(a)|=\|f\|_{A},

which means that
{\varphi}^{\star}
is an isometry.
∎

Note that in
[
7
, Theorem 2]
we have shown that if
X
is normal and
A
is both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of
X
then
f\in{\mathscr{P}}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
has
a linear extension
{\varphi}^{\star}(f)=f\circ{\varphi}
. In 2005 Kalenda and Spurný showed
[
3
, Example 10]
that there is a bounded Baire-one function

f\colon{\mathbb{Q}}\cap[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
which cannot be extended to a Baire-one function on
[0,1]
. From these considerations and by Theorem
1
we obtain the following

Remark 1
.

Let
A
be a nonempty subset of a metric space
X
. Let also
f\in{\mathscr{P}}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
. If
\overline{f}\in{\mathscr{P}}(A_{1},{\mathbb{R}})
extends
f

then in Theorem
1
we cannot require
A_{1}
to be simultanoeusly
F_{\sigma}
and
G_{\delta}
.

Proof.

Let
A=[0,1]\cap{\mathbb{Q}}
and
X={\mathbb{R}}
.
A
is of course an
F_{\sigma}
-subset of
X
. Moreover,
{\mathscr{P}}(A,{\mathbb{R}})={\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A,{\mathbb{R}})={\mathbb{R}}^{A}
. Let
f\colon A\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Suppose that there is
an extension
\overline{f}\in{\mathscr{P}}(A_{1},{\mathbb{R}})
such that
A_{1}\supset A
is both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
and
\overline{f}_{\mathord{\restriction}A}=f
. Then, by

[
7
, Theorem 2]
, the mapping
{\varphi}^{\star}(\overline{f})=\overline{f}\circ{\varphi}
is a piecewise continuous (and thus Baire-one) extension of
\overline{f}
.
Those arguments would imply that every function
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
has an extension
\overline{f}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})
, a contradiction.
∎

The second main theorem deals with extensions of Baire-one functions.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A
be both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of
{\mathbb{R}}
and let
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
.
There exists a non-negative, preserving unity and linear extension operator
{\varphi}^{\star}\colon{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A,{\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathscr{B}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})

such that its restriction to
{\mathscr{B}}_{1}^{b}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
is an isometry.

Proof.

Set
{\varphi}^{\star}(f)=f\circ{\varphi}
, where
{\varphi}
is an algebraic retraction (
1
).
Since
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
, there is a sequence
\left(f_{n}\right)
of continuous mappings on
A
such that
f(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}(a)
, for every

a\in A
. Since (by Lemma
3
)
{\varphi}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
, we have
{\varphi}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\phi_{n}
with every
\phi_{n}
continuous on
A
.
Obviously every composition
f_{n}\circ\phi_{n}
is continuous on
A
. Moreover, by Lemma
3
, for every
x\in X
there is
n_{x}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\phi_{n}(x)={\varphi}(x)
for every
n>n_{x}
. Therefore

\left(f_{n}\circ\phi_{n}\right)(x)=f_{n}\left(\phi_{n}(x)\right)=f_{n}\left({\varphi}(x)\right)\to f\left({\varphi}(x)\right)

and
f_{n}\circ\phi_{n}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}f\circ{\varphi}={\varphi}^{\star}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{R}})
.
Linearity and preserving of the unity are obvious for
{\varphi}^{\star}
.
Moreover, if
f
is a bounded element of
{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
, we have

\|{\varphi}^{\star}(f)\|_{X}=\sup_{x\in X}|f\left({\varphi}(x)\right)|=\sup_{a\in A}|f(a)|=\|f\|_{A},

which means that
{\varphi}^{\star}
is an isometry.
∎

To conclude the paper, let us give another method of extending functions of the first Borel class.
It has a weaker assumption about
X
and is a generalization of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
X
be the Hausdorff topological space and let
A
be both an
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
- subset of
X
. Let also
f\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
. There exists
a linear extension operator
T\colon\mathfrak{fcBor}(A,{\mathbb{R}})\to\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
such that its restriction to
\mathfrak{fcBor}^{b}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
is an isometry.

Proof.

Fix any
x_{0}\in A
. For any
f\colon A\to{\mathbb{R}}
define
\overline{f}\colon X\to{\mathbb{R}}
by

\overline{f}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(x),&x\in A,\\
f(x_{0}),&x\in X\setminus A.\end{array}\right.

It is obvious that
T(f)=\overline{f}
is linear and
\|f\|_{A}=\|\overline{f}\|_{X}
whenever
f
is bounded. Moreover, if
f\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(A,{\mathbb{R}})
and
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}

is open then

\overline{f}^{-1}(U)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f^{-1}(U),&f(x_{0})\notin U,\\
f^{-1}(U)\cup(X\setminus A),&f(x_{0})\in U.\end{array}\right.

Since
f^{-1}(U)
is a relatively
F_{\sigma}
-subset of an
F_{\sigma}
-set
A
, it is
F_{\sigma}
too. Moreover, since
X\setminus A
is
F_{\sigma}
,

\overline{f}^{-1}(U)
is in both cases
F_{\sigma}
. Hence
\overline{f}\in\mathfrak{fcBor}(X,{\mathbb{R}})
.
∎

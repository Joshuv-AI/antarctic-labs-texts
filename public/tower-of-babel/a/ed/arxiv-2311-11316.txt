Word Measures on Wreath Products II
Yotam Shomroni

Abstract
Every word w w in F r F_{r} , the free group of rank r r , induces a probability measure (the w w -measure) on every finite group G G , by substitution of random G G -elements in the letters. This measure is determined by its Fourier coefficients: the w w -expectations 𝔼 w ⁡ [ χ ] \EX_{w}[\chi] of the irreducible characters of G G . For every finite group G G , every stable character χ \chi of G ≀ S n G\wr S_{n} (trace of a finitely generated FI G \textbf{FI}_{G} -module), and every word w ∈ F r w\in F_{r} , we approximate 𝔼 w ⁡ [ χ ] \EX_{w}[\chi] up to an error term of O ⁡ ( n − π ⁡ ( w ) ) O\left(n^{-\pi(w)}\right) , where π ⁡ ( w ) \pi(w) is the primitivity rank of w w . This generalizes previous works by Puder, Hanany, Magee and the author. As an application we show that random Schreier graphs of representation-stable actions of G ≀ S n G\wr S_{n} are close-to-optimal expanders. The paper reveals a surprising relation between stable representation theory of wreath products and not-necessarily connected Stallings core graphs.

Introduction

In this paper we study word measures on wreath products of finite groups with the symmetric group
S_{n}
.
Explicitly, we bound the

We start by explaining the notions of wreath product, word measures and stable characters.
The
wreath product
of a group

G\wr S_{n}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}G\wr_{[n]}S_{n}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}G^{n}\rtimes S_{n}

The elements are
\left\{(v,\sigma):v\in G^{n},\,\sigma\in S_{n}\right\}

\left(v_{1},\sigma_{1}\right)\cdot\left(v_{2},\sigma_{2}\right)=\left(v_{1}\cdot(\sigma_{1}.v_{2}),\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{2}\right)

An element can also be thought of as a monomial matrix (every row and column has a unique non-zero entry) with
G

\begin{pmatrix}0&i&0\\
0&0&1\\
-i&0&0\end{pmatrix}\in\{\pm 1,\pm i\}\wr S_{3},\quad\begin{pmatrix}g_{1}&0&0\\
0&0&g_{2}\\

Throughout the paper,

We also denote the set of all characters by

What are stable characters?

Informally, given a sequence
(G_{n})_{n\in\N}

To study stable characters on

Application: expansion of random Schreier graphs

We call an action which induces a stable permutation representation
representation-stable
, or just
rep-stable
.
In this paper we handle only finite groups and actions on finite sets.

Every group action
G\curvearrowright X
together with
r
group elements
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}\in G
give rise to a
2r
-regular multi-graph, called a

, with vertex set
X
and edge multi-set
\left\{\left(x,g_{i}(x)\right):\,\,\,x\in X,\,i\in[r]\right\}
.
We regard edges
\left(v,u\right)
coming from different
i\in[r]
as different.
Note that a self-loop contributes
2
to the degree of a vertex.

Definition 1.8
.

Given a finite group
G
and an action
\rho\colon G\rightarrow\textup{Sym}(X)
, we denote by
\textup{Sch}_{r}(\rho)
the

-regular random Schreier graph obtained by
\rho
and
r
random elements

.

For example, for
n,r\in\N
,
\textup{Sch}_{r}\left(S_{n}\curvearrowright[n]\right)
is just a random covering of degree
n
of the bouquet with

2r
-regular multi-graph on
n
vertices.
Given a

-regular graph
\Gamma

n

, and denote
\mu_{\Gamma}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\max(\lambda_{2},-\lambda_{n})
.
In
[

]

-regular graph on

\lambda_{2}\geq 2\sqrt{d-1}-o(1)_{n\rightarrow\infty}
.
Hence a sequence of

-regular graphs satisfying

[

]
, Alon also conjectured that for every

, a random

-regular graph on

Theorem 1.9

\mu_{\Gamma_{n}}\leq 2\sqrt{2r-1}\cdot\exp\left(\frac{2k^{2}}{e^{2}(2r-1)}\right)\quad\quad\textup{with probability }1-o(1)\textup{ as }n\rightarrow\infty.

In particular for every fixed

The notion of (
B
-labeled) core graphs, introduced in
[
Stallings1983TopologyOF
]
, refers to finite, connected graphs with every vertex having degree at least two (so no leaves and no isolated vertices), that comes with a graph morphism to
\Omega_{B}
which is an immersion, namely, locally injective.
In other words, this is a finite connected graph with at least one edge and no leaves, with edges that are directed and labeled by the elements of
B
, such that for every vertex
v
and every
b\in B
, there is at most one incoming
b
-edge and at most one outgoing
b
-edge at
v
. We stress that multiple edges between two vertices and loops at vertices are allowed.

There is a natural one-to-one correspondence between finite
B
-labeled core graphs and conjugacy classes of non-trivial finitely generated subgroups of
F_{r}
.
Indeed, given a core graph
\Gamma
as above, pick an arbitrary vertex
v
and consider the “labeled fundamental group”
\pi_{1}^{\textup{lab}}(\Gamma,v)
: closed paths in a graph with oriented and
B
-labeled edges correspond to words over the alphabet
B
.
In other words, if

is the immersion, then
\pi_{1}^{\textup{lab}}(\Gamma,v)
is the subgroup
p_{*}(\pi_{1}(\Gamma,v))
of
\pi_{1}(\Omega_{r},\otimes)=F_{r}
.
The conjugacy class of
\pi_{1}^{\textup{lab}}(\Gamma,v)
is independent of the choice of

and it corresponds to
\Gamma
.
We denote it by
\pi_{1}^{\textup{lab}}(\Gamma)
. Conversely, if

is a non-trivial finitely generated subgroup, the conjugacy class

corresponds to a finite core graph, denoted
\Gamma_{B}(H^{F_{r}})
, which can be obtained in different manners - see

]
and the references therein.

Definition 3.6

(Multi-word, Cyclic Object, Cyclic Graph)
.

A
cyclic object
of
\mathcal{MOCC}\left(F_{r}\right)
is a multiset consisting only of cyclic subgroups.
Equivalently, a
cyclic graph
is a multi core graph with Euler characteristic 0.
Note that if there exists a morphism
\vec{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow\vec{\mathcal{J}}
and
\vec{\mathcal{J}}
is cyclic, then
\vec{\mathcal{H}}
is cyclic as well (as every non-trivial subgroup of
\Z
is isomorphic to
\Z
).
The category of cyclic objects is a full sub-category of
\mathcal{MOCC}\left(F_{r}\right)
.
Note also that a morphism of cyclic graphs is a covering map onto its image.

A
multi-word
is a multi-set
\vec{w}
of conjugacy classes of words
w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in F_{r}
.
Equivalently, a multi-word is the data of a cyclic object, together with the generators
w_{i}
of the groups
\langle w_{i}\rangle
, thus making
w,w^{-1}
inequivalent (even though they generate the same cyclic group).
Given a
\mathcal{MOCC}\left(F_{r}\right)
-object
\vec{\mathcal{H}}
, a morphism
\eta\colon\vec{w}\rightarrow\vec{\mathcal{H}}
is just an ordinary
\mathcal{MOCC}\left(F_{r}\right)
-morphism
\eta\colon\{\langle w_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle w_{k}\rangle\}\rightarrow\vec{\mathcal{H}}
, i.e. it conjugates the words
w_{i}
into the subgroups in
\vec{\mathcal{H}}
, but the image
\eta(\vec{w})
is a multi-word.
In the language of graphs,
\vec{w}
“remembers” the orientation of every cycle
\Gamma_{B}(w_{i})
(clockwise or anti-clockwise).
Given a multi core graph
\Delta
, a morphism
\eta\colon\Gamma_{B}(\vec{w})\rightarrow\Delta
is just an ordinary morphism of multi core graphs, but
\eta(\vec{w})
is a multi-set of directed paths
\eta(w_{i})
.

We abbreviate notation and denote a multi word by
\left\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\right\}
instead of
\left\{w_{1}^{F_{r}},\ldots,w_{k}^{F_{r}}\right\}
.

Definition 3.7

(
\EX_{\eta}\left[\zeta\right]
)
.

Let
\vec{w}=\left\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\right\}
be a multi-word,
\vec{\mathcal{J}}\in\mathcal{MOCC}\left(F_{r}\right)
and let
\eta\in\textup{Hom}(\vec{w},\vec{\mathcal{J}})
be a choice of representatives
\left\{J_{1},\ldots,J_{\ell}\right\}
of
\vec{\mathcal{J}}
and a function
f\colon[k]\rightarrow[\ell]
.
Also let
G
be a finite group, and
\zeta\colon\vec{w}\rightarrow\C^{\textup{conj}(G)}
a function assigning each word

a class function

. Define

\EX_{\eta}[\zeta]\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\EX_{\alpha_{i}\sim U(\textup{Hom}(J_{i},G))}\left[\prod_{j\in f^{-1}(i)}\zeta_{j}(\alpha_{i}(w_{j}))\right].

It is well defined, i.e. does not depend on the representatives of the conjugacy classes, since
\zeta_{j}
are class functions.
Note that

is multi-linear in

(that is, for every

This quantity also has a geometric interpretation, which generalizes

Proposition 3.8
.

In the notation above, denote

Definition 4.1

(Free morphism)
.

If

are subgroups of the free group

of

(so

) and some subgroup

Now let

be a morphism of multi core graphs with

of

so that

of multi core graphs is free if
\eta_{\eta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})}\colon\eta^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}
is free for every connected component
\Delta^{\prime}
of

5
The Induction-Convolution Lemma

The induction-convolution lemma provides a method to compute the word measure of an induced character, under certain independence assumptions on the group and subgroup used in the induction process which are satisfied in the case of wreath products.
Recall Definition
3.7
(expectation of class functions via a morphism) and Definition
2.29
(induction of a multi partition).
Let
G
be a finite group, let
\vec{w}=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}
be a multi-word, and
\zeta\colon\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}\rightarrow\C^{\textup{conj}(G)}
a class function on every cycle.
We denote by
\textup{Ind}\zeta
the coordinate-wise induction of the corresponding multi partition: for every
n\in\N
,

\textup{Ind}\zeta\colon\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}\rightarrow\C^{\textup{conj}\left(G\wr S_{n}\right)}
is defined by
(\textup{Ind}\zeta)(w_{i})\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\textup{Ind}(\zeta(w_{i}))
.
Our objective is to express

\EX_{\eta}[\textup{Ind}\zeta]
in terms of formulas that do not depend on the wreath product
G_{n}=G\wr S_{n}
, but instead only involve either
G
or
S_{n}
separately.
The induction-convolution lemma gives such a disentanglement, in the form of convolution in the lattice of
B
-surjective morphisms.

Recall that
\textup{Ind}\textbf{1}=\#\textup{fixed points}
is the permutation character of
S_{n}\curvearrowright[n]
.
Thus it is natural to denote for every morphism
\eta\colon\vec{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow\vec{\mathcal{J}}
:

\EX_{\eta}[\textup{Ind}\textbf{1}]\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\EX_{\eta}[S_{n}\curvearrowright[n]].

This notation is less intuitive, but it emphasizes the idea of the induction-convolution lemma:

Lemma 5.1
.

“The Induction-Convolution Lemma”

Let
B\subseteq F_{r}
be a basis, and
\vec{w}=\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\}
a multi-word.
Then

\EX[\textup{Ind}\zeta]=\EX[\zeta]\underset{B}{*}\mu_{B}\underset{B}{*}\EX[\textup{Ind}\textbf{1}]

where
\EX[\zeta],\EX[\textup{Ind}\zeta]
are elements of
\C^{\mathcal{Q}_{B}(\vec{w})}
, i.e. map morphisms
\eta
to their
\zeta
-expectations

\EX_{\eta}[\zeta],\EX_{\eta}[\textup{Ind}\zeta]
.

Note that the convolution is well defined even though
\EX[\zeta],\EX[\textup{Ind}\zeta]
are defined only for morphisms
\eta\in\C^{\mathcal{Q}_{B}(\vec{w})}
.
In contrast,
\mu_{B}
and
\EX[\textup{Ind}\textbf{1}]
are defined on every morphism in
\textup{sur}_{B}
, and this is crucial because the convolution operator involves many morphisms that appear in
\mathcal{D}\textup{ecomp}_{B}(\eta)
. An alternative statement
is that for every morphism
\eta\in\mathcal{Q}_{B}(\vec{w})
,

\EX_{\eta}[\textup{Ind}\zeta]=\sum_{(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in\mathcal{D}\textup{ecomp}_{B}(\eta)}\EX_{\eta_{1}}[\zeta]\cdot L_{\eta_{2}}^{B}(n).

(5.1)

Actually, the induction-convolution lemma is applicable to every wreath product
G\wr_{[n]}S
where
G,S
are compact groups and

is transitive, but we do not need this in this paper.

Weaker versions of the induction-convolution lemma were proven before.
The simplest case

for every

The inner product formula and Frobenius reciprocity

To the end of this section, we denote
G_{n}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}G\wr S_{n}
.

Definition 6.9
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n
be a partition.
Let
\sigma\in S_{n}
be a permutation with cycle-structure
\lambda
.
We denote
z_{\lambda}\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\left|\textup{Cntr}_{S_{n}}(\sigma)\right|\overset{\mathrm{def}}{=\joinrel=}\left|\left\{\tau\in S_{n}:\,\,\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma\right\}\right|
.
Note that

z_{\lambda}=\prod_{r=1}^{\infty}r^{\#_{r}(\lambda)}\#_{r}(\lambda)!,

as we can permute cycles of the same length and then rotate each of them separately.

Lemma
5.1
gives us a tool for computing inner products of stable functions:

Corollary 6.10
.

Let
\vec{\lambda}\in\mathscr{P}_{n}(\textup{char}(G))
.
Then for every non-power word
w\in F_{r}
,

\left\langle\mathfrak{s}\textup{Ind}\left(\vec{\lambda}\right),\textbf{1}\right\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{\tau\vdash k}\frac{1}{z_{\tau}}\sum_{\eta\in Q_{\{w\}}\left(w^{\vec{\lambda}}\right):\,\,\,\textup{Im}(\eta)=w^{\tau}}\EX_{\eta}\left[\vec{\lambda}\right].

Proof.

The following computation takes place in the ambient group
\left\langle w\right\rangle
.
We have

\left\langle\mathfrak{s}\textup{Ind}\left(\vec{\lambda}\right),\textbf{1}\right\rangle=\EX_{w\rightarrow w}[\mathfrak{s}\textup{Ind}\left(\vec{\lambda}\right)]=\EX_{w^{\vec{\lambda}}\twoheadrightarrow w}[\textup{Ind}\vec{\lambda}]=\EX_{\eta}[\textup{Ind}\vec{\lambda}]

where
\eta\colon w^{\vec{\lambda}}\twoheadrightarrow w
is the unique covering map.
Now the induction-convolution lemma tells us

\EX_{\eta}[\textup{Ind}\vec{\lambda}]=\sum_{(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in\textup{Decomp}_{\{w\}}(\eta)}\EX_{\eta_{1}}\left[\vec{\lambda}\right]\cdot L_{\eta_{2}}^{\{w\}}(n),

but
L_{\eta_{2}}^{\{w\}}(n)=1
for every morphism
\eta_{2}
since there is only one letter in the basis
\{w\}
.
Moreover, every quotient
\textup{Im}(\eta_{1})
is cyclic so it is of the form
w^{\tau}
for some
\tau\vdash k,\,\,k\leq n
.
The division by
z_{\tau}
comes from the fact that generally,
\mathcal{D}\textup{ecomp}_{B}(\eta)
consist of equivalence classes:
each graph
w^{\tau}
has exactly
z_{\tau}
automorphisms. Indeed,
z_{\tau}
is the number of permutations that commute with
\tau
, which correspond to automorphism of the cycle graph of
\tau
.
∎

Corollary 6.11

(Stabilization of Characters)
.

For
n\geq\left|\left|\vec{\lambda}\right|\right|
, the inner product

\left\langle\mathfrak{s}\textup{Ind}_{n}\left(\vec{\lambda}\right),\textbf{1}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathfrak{s}\textup{Ind}\left(\vec{\lambda}\right),\textbf{1}\right\rangle

stabilizes (i.e. does not depend on
n
anymore).

Proof.

In Corollary
6.10
, we sum over all
k\leq n
and all partitions
\tau\vdash k
the expectations of (surjective) quotient maps.
Every quotient is of size (i.e. number of vertices = number of edges) at most
\left|\left|\vec{\lambda}\right|\right|
, so when
n
is larger than that we do not get any new possible quotients.
Note that we must count every equivalence class in
\textup{sur}_{B}
only once.
∎

A useful tool for our computation is Frobenius reciprocity, both the classical one, that shows adjunction between induction and restriction, and a version for
\mathfrak{s}\textup{Ind}\left(\vec{\lambda}\right)

We start with an example of a simple usage of Frobenius reciprocity.
Recall Corollary
1.7
:
Let

be a non-power. Then for every

Proof of Corollary

For a uniformly random

\EX[a_{t,c}]=\frac{|c|}{t|G|}.

Indeed, for every

\begin{split}\EX[a_{t,c}]&=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}\in[n]\textrm{ distinct}}\PR_{\sigma}(\sigma(i_{1})=i_{2},\ldots,\sigma(i_{t})=i_{1})\cdot\PR_{v}(v(i_{1})\cdots v(i_{t})\in c)\\

(6.2)

Polyharmonic helices
S. Montaldo, A. Ratto

Abstract
. The main aim of this paper is to investigate the existence of Frenet helices which are polyharmonic of order r r , shortly, r r -harmonic. We shall obtain existence, non-existence and classification results. More specifically, we obtain a complete classification of proper r r -harmonic helices into the 3 3 -dimensional solvable Lie group Sol 3 . Next, we investigate the existence of proper r r -harmonic helices into Bianchi-Cartan-Vranceanu spaces and, in this context, we find new examples. Finally, we shall establish some non-existence results both for Frenet curves and Frenet helices of order n ≥ 4 n\geq 4 when the ambient space is the Euclidean sphere 𝕊 m {\mathbb{S}}^{m} .

1.
Introduction

Harmonic maps
are the critical points of the
energy functional

(1.1)

E(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{1}}\,|d\varphi|^{2}\,dV_{g_{1}}\,,

where
\varphi:M_{1}\to M_{2}
is a smooth map between two Riemannian
manifolds
(M_{1},g_{1})
and
(M_{2},g_{2})
. We refer to
[
12
,
13
]
for an introduction to this important topic.

The study of
polyharmonic maps of order

r
, shortly
r
-harmonic maps
, was first proposed by Eells and Lemaire in
[
12
]
. These maps are defined as the critical points of the functionals

E_{r}(\varphi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M_{1}}\,|(d+d^{*})^{r}\varphi|^{2}\,dV_{g_{1}}

which represent a version of order
r\geq 2
of the classical energy (
1.1
). We refer to
[
5
]
for a detailed discussion on the definition of the functionals
E_{r}(\varphi)
.

In this context, the most widely studied instance is
r=2
. This is the case of the so-called
bienergy
functional and its critical points are known as
biharmonic maps
. At present, a very ample literature on biharmonic maps is available (see
[
26
]
and references therein).

More recently, several authors worked intensively on the case
r\geq 3
. For instance, we refer to
[
5
,
9
,
10
,
14
,
17
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
24
,
25
]
for background and results.

In this paper we shall focus on the study of
r
-harmonic curves
in the Euclidean sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{m}
, in the
3
-dimensional solvable Lie group Sol
3
and into the so-called Bianchi-Cartan-Vranceanu spaces. Thus, in order to simplify the exposition, from now on we shall assume that the ambient space
M
is orientable and that an orientation on
M
has been fixed.

In order to avoid trivialities arising from re-parametrization it is convenient to restrict the attention to curves parametrized by the arc length and we shall adopt this assumption throughout the whole paper.

Now, let
\gamma:I\subset{\mathbb{R}}\to M
denote a smooth curve parametrized by the arc length
s
and let
T
denote its unit tangent vector. Then
\gamma
is
r
-harmonic if and only if its
r
-tension field
\tau_{r}(\gamma)
vanishes, i.e.,(see
[
4
,
17
,
22
]
)

(1.2)

\tau_{r}(\gamma)=\nabla^{2r-1}_{T}T+\sum_{\ell=0}^{r-2}(-1)^{\ell}R\left(\nabla^{2r-3-\ell}_{T}T,\nabla^{\ell}_{T}T\right)T=0\,,

where
\nabla^{0}_{T}T=T,\,\nabla^{k}_{T}T=\nabla_{T}\left(\nabla^{k-1}_{T}T\right)
and
R
is the Riemannian curvature operator of
M
.

We point out that any geodesic is trivially
r
-harmonic for all
r\geq 1
. Therefore, we say that
\gamma
is a
proper

r
-harmonic curve if it is
r
-harmonic and
not
a geodesic.

A general study of (
1.2
) is a difficult task. Therefore, in this paper we restrict our attention to a geometrically rich and significant family of curves. Indeed,

Definition 1.1

(
n
-Frenet curves
)
.

Let
\gamma:I\to M
be a smooth curve parametrized by arc length and assume
2\leq n\leq m=\dim M
. Then we say that
\gamma
is a
Frenet curve of order

n
, shortly, an
n
-Frenet curve
, if it admits a Frenet
n
-field
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}\}
along
\gamma
such that

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}F_{1}&=&T\\
\nabla_{T}F_{1}&=&k_{1}\,F_{2}\\
\nabla_{T}F_{i}&=&-k_{i-1}F_{i-1}+k_{i}F_{i+1}\,,\quad i=2,\ldots,n-1\,,\\
\nabla_{T}F_{n}&=&-k_{n-1}F_{n-1}\end{array}\right.

where
k_{1}(s),\ldots,k_{n-1}(s)
are positive functions, called the
curvatures
of the curve. In the special case that
n=m\geq 2
we choose
F_{n}
in such a way that
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{n}\}
is a positively oriented orthonormal base of
T_{\gamma(s)}M
. In this case we allow that
k_{n-1}(s)
can assume any real value. Finally, we say that an
n
-Frenet curve
\gamma
is
full
if
k_{n-1}(s)
does not vanish at any point.

Definition 1.2

(
n
-Frenet helices
)
.

Let
\gamma
be a Frenet curve of order
n
. We say that
\gamma
is
a Frenet helix of order
n
, shortly, an
n
-Frenet helix
, if its curvatures
k_{1}(s),\ldots,k_{n-1}(s)
are constant functions which will be denoted
\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n-1}
.

Remark 1.3
.

In order to make easier the comparison of our results with those which are available in the literature, in the case of
2
-Frenet helices we shall write
\{T,N\}
instead of
\{F_{1},F_{2}\}
and
\kappa
for
\kappa_{1}
.

As for
3
-Frenet helices, we write
\{T,N,B\}
for
\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
, and
\kappa,\tau
for
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}
.

Moreover, we point out that we adopt the following notation and sign convention for the Riemannian curvature tensor field
R
of the ambient space
M
:

{R}(X,Y)Z={\nabla}_{X}{\nabla}_{Y}Z-{\nabla}_{Y}{\nabla}_{X}Z-{\nabla}_{[X,Y]}Z\,,\quad{R}(X,Y,Z,W)=\langle{R}(X,Y)W,Z\rangle\,.

The Chen-Maeta conjecture states that any
r
-harmonic submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is minimal. As for curves, Maeta (see
[
17
]
) proved that the conjecture is true.

The study of triharmonic helices in space forms was initiated by Maeta (see
[
18
]
) and, more recently, Branding carried out a complete study of
r
-harmonic Frenet helices of order
3
into
3
-dimensional space forms (see
[
4
]
). More precisely, he described explicit examples in
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}
and proved non-existence when the curvature of the ambient space form is non-positive.

This last result supports the idea that, in general, it is difficult to find examples of proper
r
-harmonic submanifolds in an ambient space whose Riemannian curvature tensor field is non-positive. Despite this, the non-existence of proper biharmonic submanifolds into a non-positively curved ambient space, known as generalized Chen’s conjecture, was first proved to be false in
[
27
]
. Moreover, other examples were also constructed in a conformally flat, non-positively curved ambient space (see
[
16
]
).

An investigation of triharmonic
2
-Frenet helices in surfaces and triharmonic
3
-Frenet helices in
3
-dimensional homogeneous spaces with a
4
-dimensional group of isometries was carried out in
[
22
]
. More recently, triharmonic
3
-Frenet helices in the
3
-dimensional solvable Lie group Sol
3
were fully classified in
[
23
]
.

In this paper we shall make some progress concerning
r
-harmonic
n
-Frenet helices,
n=2,3
, into the
3
-dimensional solvable Lie group Sol
3
and into Bianchi-Cartan-Vranceanu spaces. Our results in this context will be described and proved in Sections
3
and
4
respectively.

As for general results, our first contribution is

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\gamma:I\to M
be a Frenet helix of order
2
and
r\geq 2
. Then

\tau_{r}(\gamma)=-\kappa^{2r-3}\Big[\kappa^{2}\,N+(r-1)R(T,N)T\Big]\,.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
\gamma:I\to M
be a Frenet helix of order
2
and
r\geq 2
. If

\langle R(T,N)T,W\rangle=0\quad\forall\,W\in\big({\rm Span}\{T,N\}\big)^{\perp}\,,

then
\gamma
is proper
r
-harmonic if and only if

(1.4)

\kappa^{2}=(r-1)R(N,T,N,T)\,.

Next, we examine the case of
3
-Frenet helices. To this purpose, let us introduce the following quantities:

(1.5)

A_{1}=\kappa^{2}(r-1)+\tau^{2}\,,\quad A_{2}=-\kappa\,\tau\,(r-2)\,,\quad A_{3}=-\left(\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{2}\,.

Then we can state our result:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\gamma:I\to M
be a Frenet helix of order
3
and
r\geq 2
. Then

\tau_{r}(\gamma)=c\,\Big\{A_{3}N+A_{1}R(N,T)T+A_{2}R(N,B)T\Big\}\,,

where
c=(-1)^{r}\kappa\left(\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{r-3}
and
A_{i},\,i=1,2,3,
are defined in (
1.5
).

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
\gamma:I\to M
be a Frenet helix of order
3
and
r\geq 2
. Assume that

\langle R(N,T)T,W\rangle=0\,,\,\langle R(N,B)T,W\rangle=0\quad\forall\,W\in\big({\rm Span}\{T,N,B\}\big)^{\perp}\,.

Then
\gamma
is proper
r
-harmonic if and only if the following two equations hold:

(1.6)

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm(i)}&A_{1}R(N,T,N,T)+A_{2}R(N,B,N,T)+A_{3}=0\\
&\\
{\rm(ii)}&A_{1}R(N,T,B,T)+A_{2}R(N,B,B,T)=0\,.\end{array}

Remark 1.8
.

(i)

Putting together Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.5
we conclude that condition (
1.4
) is a necessary condition for a
2
-Frenet helix to be
r
-harmonic. This condition is also sufficient if one proves that
\tau_{r}(\gamma)
belongs to
{\rm Span}\{T,N\}
. For instance, this surely happens if the ambient space is a surface or any
m
-dimensional space form,
m\geq 2
.
Similarly, we deduce from Theorem
1.6
and Corollary
1.7
that the two equations in (
1.6
) represent a necessary condition for the
r
-harmonicity of a
3
-Frenet helix, and this condition is also sufficient if one proves that
\tau_{r}(\gamma)
belongs to
{\rm Span}\{T,N,B\}
. For instance, this is automatic if the ambient space has dimension
3
or it is an
m
-dimensional space form,
m\geq 3
.

(ii)

Theorems
1.4
and
1.6
imply that the component of
\tau_{r}(\gamma)
along
T
vanishes. Therefore, these helices are always
r
-conservative.

(iii)

In the special case that the ambient space
M
is a
3
-dimensional space form of curvature
K
it is easy to check that equation (
1.6
)(ii) always holds, while (
1.6
)(i) is equivalent to

\left(\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{2}=K\left((r-1)\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)\,.

Then we have recovered Theorem 1.1 of
[
4
]
as a special case of our Theorem
1.6
–Corollary
1.7
.

Our next result suggests that helices and, more generally, curves of high order cannot be
r
-harmonic for small values of
r
. More precisely, we have:

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\gamma
be a full
n
-Frenet curve in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m}
,
m\geq n\geq 2
. If
n\geq 2r
, then
\gamma
cannot be
r
-harmonic.

In the final section of this paper we shall prove that a stronger non-existence statement can be obtained if we assume that the curve is a helix (see Section
5
).

Remark 1.10
.

For the sake of completeness, here we point out that the choice of the most suitable definition of a general helix in a Riemannian geometric context is a delicate matter and may depend on the specific situation. Definition
1.2
above seemed to us the most appropriate for our approach to the study of
r
-harmonic curves. By contrast, the following geometrically interesting definition has been used in a significant paper of Barrios (see
[
1
]
) along the lines of previous work by Langer and Singer (see
[
15
]
).

Definition 1.11
.

[
1
]
A curve
\gamma(s)
into a
3
-dimensional space form will be called a
general helix
if there exists a Killing vector field
\mathcal{V}(s)
with constant length along
\gamma
and such that the angle between
\mathcal{V}
and the unit tangent
T
is a non-zero constant along
\gamma
. We will say that
\mathcal{V}
is an axis of the general helix.

In particular, this definition does not imply that the curvatures of the helix are constant, and this would boost the analytical difficulties in our context. The interested reader can find some further comments in this direction in the last section of our recent paper
[
23
]
.

Our paper is organized as follows.

The results stated in this introduction will be proved in Section
2
. The classification and existence results concerning
r
-harmonic
n
-Frenet helices,
n=2,3
, in the
3
-dimensional solvable Lie group Sol
3
and in BCV-spaces will be stated and proved in Sections
3
and
4
respectively.

Next, in Section
5
we shall study full,
r
-harmonic
n
-Frenet helices in

Finally, at the end of the paper we have added a related appendix where we discuss some conditions which imply that a proper triharmonic curve has constant geodesic curvature.

Proof of the results stated in the introduction

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

First, an elementary argument using

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccl}\nabla_{T}T&=&\kappa\,N\\
\nabla_{T}N&=&-\kappa\,T\end{array}\right.

shows that, for all
\ell\geq 0
,

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\nabla_{T}^{2\ell}T&=&(-1)^{\ell}\kappa^{2\ell}\,T\\
&&\\
\nabla_{T}^{2\ell+1}T&=&(-1)^{\ell}\kappa^{2\ell+1}\,N\,.\end{array}\right.

Next, let us assume that
r=2s
. Then, in order to be able to use (
2.1
), it is convenient to rewrite the
r
tension field
\tau_{r}(\gamma)
given in (
1.2
) splitting odd and even terms as follows:

(2.2)

\tau_{2s}(\gamma)=\nabla_{T}^{4s-1}T+\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}R\left(\nabla_{T}^{2(2s-2-j)+1}T,\nabla_{T}^{2j}T\right)T-\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}R\left(\nabla_{T}^{2(2s-2-j)}T,\nabla_{T}^{2j+1}T\right)T\,.

Next, inserting (
2.1
) into this expression and simplifying we deduce that

\tau_{2s}(\gamma)=-\kappa^{2r-1}N-\Big[\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\kappa^{2r-3}+\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}\kappa^{2r-3}\Big]R(T,N)T=-\kappa^{2r-3}\Big[\kappa^{2}\,N+(r-1)R(T,N)T\Big]

and so the proof is completed in this case. When
r=2s+1
the proof is analogous and so we omit the details.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

It suffices to observe that
\langle\tau_{r}(\gamma),T\rangle=0
and compute
\langle\tau_{r}(\gamma),N\rangle
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

First, we recall a lemma for
3
-Frenet helices which was proved by Branding (see Lemma 2.1 of
[
4
]
):

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

\begin{array}[]{lll}h(\ell)=(-1)^{\ell}\kappa^{2}\big(\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\big)^{\ell-1}&{\rm if}&\ell\geq 1\,,h(0)=1;\\
g(\ell)=(-1)^{\ell-1}\kappa\,\tau\big(\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\big)^{\ell-1}&{\rm if}&\ell\geq 1\,,g(0)=0;\\
f(\ell)=(-1)^{\ell}\kappa\big(\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\big)^{\ell}&{\rm if}&\ell\geq 0\,.\end{array}

Then, for all
\ell\geq 0
, we have:

(2.3)

\begin{cases}\nabla_{T}^{2\ell}T=h(\ell)\,T+g(\ell)\,B\\
\nabla_{T}^{2\ell+1}T=f(\ell)\,N\,.\end{cases}

Next, let
r=2s
. We use again the split (
2.2
). More precisely, inserting (
2.3
) we obtain:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}R\left(\nabla_{T}^{2(2s-2-j)+1}T,\nabla_{T}^{2j}T\right)T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}f(2s-2-j)h(j)\Big)R(N,T)T

\displaystyle+\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}f(2s-2-j)g(j)\Big)R(N,B)T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(f(2s-2)+\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}f(2s-2-j)h(j)\Big)R(N,T)T

\displaystyle+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}f(2s-2-j)g(j)\Big)R(N,B)T\,.

Similarly,

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=0}^{s-2}R\left(\nabla_{T}^{2(2s-2-j)}T,\nabla_{T}^{2j+1}T\right)T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(-\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}f(j-1)h(2s-1-j)\Big)R(T,N)T

\displaystyle-\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}f(j-1)g(2s-1-j)\Big)R(B,N)T\,.

Next, using these last two computations and simplifying we obtain:

\displaystyle\tau_{r}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(2s-1)N

\displaystyle+\Big(f(2s-2)+\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\big[f(2s-2-j)h(j)+f(j-1)h(2s-1-j)\big]\Big)R(N,T)T

\displaystyle+\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{s-1}\big[f(2s-2-j)g(j)+f(j-1)g(2s-1-j)\big]\Big)R(N,B)T\,.

Next, a simple, explicit computation of the sums in
\tau_{r}(\gamma)
gives

\tau_{r}(\gamma)=c\,\Big\{A_{3}N+A_{1}R(N,T)T+A_{2}R(N,B)T\Big\}\,,

where
c=(-1)^{r}\kappa\left(\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{r-3}
and
A_{i},\,i=1,2,3
are defined in (
1.5
). So the proof is completed in the case that
r=2s
. When
r=2s+1
the proof is analogous and so we omit the details.
∎

Proof of Corollary
1.7
.

It suffices to observe that
\langle\tau_{r}(\gamma),T\rangle=0
and compute
\langle\tau_{r}(\gamma),N\rangle
and
\langle\tau_{r}(\gamma),B\rangle
. Then we easily obtain equations (
1.6
)(i) and (
1.6
)(ii) respectively. More precisely, under the assumptions of this corollary, we have

\displaystyle\tau_{r}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\Big\{\Big(A_{1}R(N,T,N,T)+A_{2}R(N,B,N,T)+A_{3}\Big)N

\displaystyle\,\,\quad+\Big(A_{1}R(N,T,B,T)+A_{2}R(N,B,B,T)\Big)B\Big\}

as required to end the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

First, we recall that the curvature tensor field
R
on
{\mathbb{S}}^{m}
is given by:

(2.4)

R(X,Y)Z=-\langle X,Z\rangle Y+\langle Y,Z\rangle X\,.

Next, using the structural equations (
1.3
) of the Frenet field it is elementary to show by induction that, for any
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
, we can write

(2.5)

\nabla^{\ell}_{T}T=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}(s)F_{j}+\Pi_{j=1}^{\ell}k_{j}(s)F_{\ell+1}\,,

where the explicit expression of the coefficients
a_{j}(s)
is not relevant for our purposes.

Now, taking into account (
1.2
), (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), it is immediate to deduce that, whenever
2r\leq n
, we can write

\tau_{r}(\gamma)=\sum_{j=1}^{2r-1}b_{j}(s)F_{j}+\Pi_{j=1}^{2r-1}k_{j}(s)F_{2r}

where, again, the explicit expression of the coefficients
b_{j}(s)
is not relevant for our purposes. Now, inspection of the component of
\tau_{r}(\gamma)
along
F_{2r}
readily ends the proof.
∎

3.
r
-harmonic Frenet helices in the
3
-dimensional solvable Lie group
\sol_{3}

In this section we shall obtain a complete classification of
r
-harmonic Frenet helices in the
3
-dimensional solvable Lie group
\sol_{3}
.

Let
\sol_{3}

=(\mathbb{R}^{3},g_{\sol})
denote the
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold given by
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
endowed with the metric tensor
g_{\sol}=e^{2z}{\rm d}x^{2}+e^{-2z}{\rm d}y^{2}+{\rm d}z^{2}
with respect to the standard Cartesian coordinates

(x,y,z)
.

The space
\sol_{3}
is a
3
-dimensional solvable Lie group and the metric
g_{\sol}
is left-invariant with respect to the group operation

(x,y,z).(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\left(e^{z}x^{\prime}+x,e^{-z}y^{\prime}+y,z^{\prime}+z\right)\,.

A global orthonormal frame field on
\sol_{3}
is

E_{1}=e^{-z}\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\;E_{2}=e^{z}\frac{\partial}{\partial y},\;E_{3}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}.

With respect to this orthonormal frame field, the Lie brackets and the
Levi-Civita connection can be easily computed. We have:

[E_{1},E_{2}]=0,\;[E_{2},E_{3}]=-E_{2},\;[E_{1},E_{3}]=E_{1},

(3.1)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{1}=-E_{3},&\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{2}=0,&\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{3}=E_{1}\\
\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{1}=0,&\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{2}=E_{3},&\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{3}=-E_{2}\\
\nabla_{E_{3}}E_{1}=0,&\nabla_{E_{3}}E_{2}=0,&\nabla_{E_{3}}E_{3}=0.\\
\end{array}

A further computation gives

(3.2)

\begin{array}[]{lll}R(E_{1},E_{2})E_{1}=-E_{2},&R(E_{1},E_{3})E_{1}=E_{3},&R(E_{1},E_{2})E_{2}=E_{1},\\
R(E_{2},E_{3})E_{2}=E_{3},&R(E_{1},E_{3})E_{3}=-E_{1},&R(E_{2},E_{3})E_{3}=-E_{2}\end{array}

and from this we deduce

(3.3)

\begin{array}[]{lll}R_{1212}=1,\\
R_{1313}=-1,\\
R_{2323}=-1.\end{array}

Let
\gamma(s)=(x(s),y(s),z(s))
be a
3
-Frenet curve in
\sol_{3}
parametrized by the arc length
s
. Then

(3.4)

T={x^{\prime}}e^{z}E_{1}+{y^{\prime}}e^{-z}E_{2}+{z^{\prime}}E_{3}=\left(T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\right)\,.

Now, let
V=V_{1}(s)E_{1}+V_{2}(s)E_{2}+V_{3}(s)E_{3}=\left(V_{1}(s),V_{2}(s),V_{3}(s)\right)
be any vector field along
\gamma
. Then a computation using (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) shows that

(3.5)

\nabla_{T}V=\left({V_{1}}^{\prime}+T_{1}V_{3},{V_{2}}^{\prime}-T_{2}V_{3},{V_{3}}^{\prime}-T_{1}V_{1}+T_{2}V_{2}\right)\,.

Our main result in this context is:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
r\geq 3
. Then there exists a proper
r
-harmonic
3
-Frenet helix
\gamma
in
\sol_{3}
if and only if

\kappa=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{3r^{2}-10r+7}{(r-2)^{2}}}\quad{\rm and}\quad\tau=\pm\frac{r-1}{4(r-2)}\,.

Moreover, up to isometries of
\sol_{3}
and orientation of
\gamma
, the parametrization of such a proper
r
-harmonic helix is

(3.6)

Since the ambient space is

.

Remark 3.3

\begin{array}[]{lll}R(T,N,T,N)&=&-1+2B_{3}^{2}\\

Next, we need the following technical Lemma:

Since we work up to isometries and orientation of

4.
r
-harmonic Frenet helices in BCV-spaces

It is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
) that
3
-dimensional homogeneous spaces with group of isometries of dimension
4
admit, as a
local
canonical model, the so called Bianchi-Cartan-Vranceanu spaces (shortly, BCV-spaces)

(4.1)

M^{3}_{m,\ell}=\left(\bar{M}\times{\mathbb{R}},g=\frac{dx^{2}+dy^{2}}{[1+m(x^{2}+y^{2})]^{2}}+\Big[dz+\frac{\ell}{2}\,\frac{ydx-xdy}{1+m(x^{2}+y^{2})}\Big]^{2}\right)\,,

where
\bar{M}=\{(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\colon 1+m(x^{2}+y^{2})>0\}
.

The space
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
is the total space of the following Riemannian submersion over a simply connected surface
M^{2}(4m)
of constant curvature
4m
, see
[
11
]
:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\pi:M^{3}_{m,\ell}\longrightarrow M^{2}(4m)=\left(\bar{M},h=\frac{dx^{2}+dy^{2}}{[1+m(x^{2}+y^{2})]^{2}}\right),\;\;\;\pi(x,y,z)=(x,y).

We point out that while in
{\mathbb{S}}^{n}(\rho)
the letter
\rho
indicates the radius, in
M^{2}(4m),\,{\mathbb{H}}^{2}(4m)
the real number within the brackets represents the sectional curvature.

These BCV-spaces are also a local model for the Thurston eight
3
-dimensional geometries with the exception of the
hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{3}(m)
and
\sol_{3}
. More precisely, we have the following cases:

(1)

when
\ell=m=0
, then
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
is
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
;

(2)

when
\ell^{2}=4m>0
, then
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
is a local model of
{\mathbb{S}}^{3}(1/\sqrt{m})
;

(3)

when
\ell=0
and
m<0
, then
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
is the product space
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}(4m)\times{\mathbb{R}}
;

(4)

when
\ell=0
and
m>0
, then
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
is the product space
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{m}})\setminus\{\infty\}\times{\mathbb{R}}
;

(5)

when
\ell\neq 0
and
m=0
, then
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
is the Heisenberg space
{\rm Nil_{3}}
;

(6)

when
\ell\neq 0
and
m<0
, then
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
is
\widetilde{\rm SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
;

(7)

when
\ell\neq 0,\,m>0
and
\ell^{2}\neq 4m
, then
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
is a local model of
{\rm SU}(2)
with a Berger metric.

The aim of this section is to investigate the existence of
r
-harmonic
3
-Frenet helices in BCV-spaces.

We shall mainly focus on the case
r\geq 3
because the biharmonic case was thoroughly investigated in
[
6
,
7
]
.

We begin by introducing some basic preliminary facts. First, it is easy to check that the vector fields

E_{1}=F\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-\frac{\ell y}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z},\quad E_{2}=F\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+\frac{\ell x}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z},\quad E_{3}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z},

where
F=1+m(x^{2}+y^{2})
, form a global orthonormal
frame field. Moreover,

(4.3)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{1}=2myE_{2}\,,&\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{2}=2mxE_{1}\,,\\
\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{2}=-2myE_{1}+\dfrac{\ell}{2}E_{3}\,,&\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{1}=-2mxE_{2}-\dfrac{\ell}{2}E_{3}\,,\\
\nabla_{E_{3}}E_{1}=\nabla_{E_{1}}E_{3}=-\dfrac{\ell}{2}E_{2}\,,&\nabla_{E_{3}}E_{2}=\nabla_{E_{2}}E_{3}=\dfrac{\ell}{2}E_{1}\,,\\
{\rm all\;\;others\;\;}\nabla_{E_{i}}E_{j}=0,\;i,j=1,2,3.&\end{array}

The Riemannian curvature operator
R
of
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
can be described as follows (see
[
11
]
, where the opposite sign convention for the curvature tensor is adopted):

(4.4)

\displaystyle R(X,Y)Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(4m-\frac{3\ell^{2}}{4}\right)\big(-\langle X,Z\rangle Y+\langle Y,Z\rangle X\big)-(4m-\ell^{2})

\displaystyle\big(\langle Y,E_{3}\rangle\langle Z,E_{3}\rangle X+\langle Y,Z\rangle\langle X,E_{3}\rangle E_{3}-\langle X,E_{3}\rangle\langle Z,E_{3}\rangle Y-\langle X,Z\rangle\langle Y,E_{3}\rangle E_{3}\big)\,.

Then a simple computation gives the possible non-zero values of the sectional curvatures:

(4.5)

\begin{split}R_{1212}&=\langle R(E_{1},E_{2})E_{2},E_{1}\rangle=4m-\frac{3\ell^{2}}{4},\\
R_{1313}&=\langle R(E_{1},E_{3})E_{3},E_{1}\rangle=\frac{\ell^{2}}{4},\\
R_{2323}&=\langle R(E_{2},E_{3})E_{3},E_{2}\rangle=\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}\,.\end{split}

Now, let
\gamma:I\to M_{m,\ell}^{3}
be a curve parametrized by the arc length
s
in a BCV-space. Then, with respect to the orthonormal frame field
\left\{E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}\right\}
,

T=\left(T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\right)=\left(\frac{x^{\prime}}{F},\,\frac{y^{\prime}}{F},\,\frac{2z^{\prime}\,F+\ell(yx^{\prime}-xy^{\prime})}{2\,F}\right)\,.

Moreover, a computation based on (
4.3
) shows that, if
V=\left(V_{1},V_{2},V_{3}\right)
, we have:

\displaystyle\nabla_{T}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(V^{\prime}_{1},V^{\prime}_{2},V^{\prime}_{3}\right)+\Big(-2myT_{1}V_{2}+2mxT_{2}V_{2}+\frac{\ell}{2}\left(T_{2}V_{3}+T_{3}V_{2}\right),

\displaystyle 2myT_{1}V_{1}-2mxT_{2}V_{1}-\frac{\ell}{2}\left(T_{1}V_{3}+T_{3}V_{1}\right),\frac{\ell}{2}\left(T_{1}V_{2}-T_{2}V_{1}\right)\Big)\,.

As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we can assume
B=T\wedge N
. Now, using (
4.5
), we compute:

(4.6)

\begin{array}[]{lll}R(T,N,T,N)&=&\frac{\ell^{2}}{4}+(4m-\ell^{2})B_{3}^{2}\\
R(T,N,T,B)&=&-B_{3}N_{3}\left(4m-\ell^{2}\right)\\
R(B,N,N,T)&=&T_{3}B_{3}\left(4m-\ell^{2}\right)\\
R(B,N,B,T)&=&-N_{3}T_{3}\left(4m-\ell^{2}\right)\,.\end{array}

Next, a straightforward computation using Lemma
2.1
, Corollary
1.7
and (
4.6
) leads us to our first general result:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a
3
-Frenet helix in a BCV-space
M^{3}_{m,\ell}
. Then

(4.7)

\tau_{r}(\gamma)=\tau_{r,N}N+\tau_{r,B}B\,,

where

(4.8)

\tau_{r,N}=\frac{1}{4}\left(4B_{3}^{2}\left(4m-\ell^{2}\right)+\ell^{2}\right)\left((r-1)\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)+(r-2)T_{3}B_{3}\kappa\tau\left(4m-\ell^{2}\right)-\left(\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{2}

and

(4.9)

\tau_{r,B}=-N_{3}\left(4m-\ell^{2}\right)\left(B_{3}\left(\kappa^{2}(r-1)+\tau^{2}\right)+(r-2)T_{3}\kappa\tau\right)\,.

Now, the main objective of this section becomes the description of the best applications of Proposition
4.1
.

Since the case of space forms has been thoroughly discussed in
[
4
]
, from now on we shall restrict our attention to the case that
4m\neq\ell^{2}
. First, we establish the following important technical lemma:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\gamma
be a proper
r
-harmonic
3
-Frenet helix in a BCV-space
M_{m,\ell}^{3}
,
r\geq 2
,
4m\neq\ell^{2}
.
Then

(i)

N_{3}\equiv 0
along
\gamma
.

(ii)

Both
T_{3}
and
B_{3}
are constant along
\gamma
and
B_{3}\neq 0
.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

Although the statement is similar to Lemma
3.4
, the proof is substantially different and so we include it.

(i) First, we observe that

(4.10)

\displaystyle B_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{T}B_{3}=\nabla_{T}\langle B,E_{3}\rangle=\langle\nabla_{T}B,E_{3}\rangle+\langle B,\nabla_{T}E_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tau\,N_{3}+\langle B,\frac{\ell}{2}\,T_{2}E_{1}-\frac{\ell}{2}\,T_{1}E_{2}\rangle=-\tau\,N_{3}+\frac{\ell}{2}\,N_{3}

\displaystyle T_{3}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{T}T_{3}=\nabla_{T}\langle T,E_{3}\rangle=\langle\nabla_{T}T,E_{3}\rangle+\langle T,\nabla_{T}E_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa\,N_{3}+\langle T,\frac{\ell}{2}\,T_{2}E_{1}-\frac{\ell}{2}\,T_{1}E_{2}\rangle=\kappa\,N_{3}\,.

We argue by contradiction and assume that
N_{3}\neq 0
on some open interval. Since
\tau_{r,B}=0
, we deduce that

(4.11)

B_{3}=-\frac{\kappa(r-2)\tau T_{3}}{\left(\kappa^{2}(r-1)+\tau^{2}\right)}\,.

Differentiating with respect to
s
and using (
4.10
) we deduce that

\left(-\tau+\frac{\ell}{2}\right)N_{3}=-\frac{\kappa^{2}(r-2)\tau}{\left(\kappa^{2}(r-1)+\tau^{2}\right)}\,N_{3}\,.

Now, since
N_{3}\neq 0
, we easily deduce that

(4.12)

\ell=\frac{2\tau\left(\kappa^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)}{\kappa^{2}(r-1)+\tau^{2}}\,.

Finally, inserting (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) into (
4.8
) and simplifying we find

\tau_{r,N}=-\frac{\kappa^{2}(r-1)\left(k^{2}+\tau^{2}\right)^{2}}{\kappa^{2}(r-1)+\tau^{2}}\,.

Therefore, since this quantity cannot vanish, we conclude that there are no solutions with
N_{3}\neq 0
, as required to end part (i).

(ii) From (
4.10
) we have
T_{3}^{\prime}=\kappa\,N_{3}
and so the conclusion follows from part (i).
Finally, if
N_{3}=B_{3}=0
then
T=E_{3}
and
\gamma
would be a geodesic.∎

Next, we prepare the ground to give explicit parametrizations of
r
-harmonic
3
-Frenet helices.

Let us assume that
\gamma(s)=(x(s),y(s),z(s))
is a non-geodesic arc-length parametrized curve immersed in a BCV-space
M_{m,\ell}^{3}
with
4m\neq\ell^{2}
and satisfying
N_{3}=0
. Then, following the computations of
[
6
, §5.2]
, we have that there exists a constant
\alpha_{o}\in(0,\pi)
and a unique (up to an additive constant
2k\pi
) smooth function
\beta
such that we can assume that the Frenet frame field along
\gamma
is given by

(4.13)

\begin{cases}T(s)=\sin\alpha_{o}\cos\beta(s)E_{1}+\sin\alpha_{o}\sin\beta(s)E_{2}+\cos\alpha_{o}E_{3}\,,\\
N(s)=-\sin\beta(s)E_{1}+\cos\beta(s)E_{2}\,,\\
B(s)=T(s)\wedge N(s)=-\cos\alpha_{o}\cos\beta(s)E_{1}-\cos\alpha_{o}\sin\beta(s)E_{2}+\sin\alpha_{o}E_{3}\,.\end{cases}

Moreover, the curvature and the torsion of
\gamma(s)
are given by

(4.14)

\displaystyle\kappa(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta\sin\alpha_{o}\,,

(4.15)

\displaystyle\tau(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\zeta\cos\alpha_{o}+\frac{\ell}{2}\,,

where

(4.16)

\zeta=|\beta^{\prime}(s)+2m\sin\alpha_{o}\left[y\cos\beta(s)-x\sin\beta(s)\right]-\ell\cos\alpha_{o}|>0\,.

We point out that in
[
6
]
the sign convention for
\tau
is different because in
[
6
]
the authors assumed

\nabla_{T}B=\tau\,N\,.

If we also require that
\gamma(s)
is a
3
-Frenet helix, then
\zeta
is constant. Furthermore, substituting the expressions (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) in (
4.8
) we conclude that any such
a

3
-Frenet helix is proper
r
-harmonic if and only if the constant
\zeta
is a positive root of the following polynomial of degree four:

(4.17)

\displaystyle P_{4}(\zeta)=8\zeta^{4}+16\ell\cos\alpha_{o}\zeta^{3}

\displaystyle+\left[\cos(2\alpha_{0})\left(16m(r-1)-3\ell^{2}(r-2)\right)-16m(r-1)+\ell^{2}(3r+4)\right]\zeta^{2}

\displaystyle+2\ell\cos\alpha_{o}\left[\ell^{2}-2r(4m-\ell^{2})\sin^{2}\alpha_{o}\right]\zeta-2\ell^{2}\left(4m-\ell^{2}\right)\sin^{2}\alpha_{o}\,.

4.1.
The case
m=0

When
m=0
and
\ell\neq 0
the BCV-space
M_{0,\ell}^{3}
represents the Heisenberg space
{\rm Nil_{3}}
. More precisely, following the terminology of
[
11
]
,
{\rm Nil_{3}}
is
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
endowed with the metric (
4.1
) with
m=0
. We point out that the standard metric on
{\rm Nil_{3}}
corresponds to the choice
\ell=1
.
In this case, the polynomial (
4.17
) becomes

(4.18)

\displaystyle P_{4}(\zeta)\lvert_{m=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8\zeta^{4}+16\ell\cos\alpha_{o}\zeta^{3}-\ell^{2}\left[(3(r-2)\cos(2\alpha_{o})-3r-4)\right]\zeta^{2}

\displaystyle+2\ell^{3}\cos\alpha_{o}\left[1+2r\sin^{2}\alpha_{o}\right]\zeta+2\ell^{4}\sin^{2}\alpha_{o}

while (
4.16
) reduces to

(4.19)

\zeta=\left|\beta^{\prime}(s)-\ell\cos\alpha_{o}\right|\,.

If
\beta(s)=\beta_{o}
is constant, then
\cos\alpha_{o}=\pm\zeta/\ell
. Replacing
\cos\alpha_{o}=-\zeta/\ell
in (
4.18
) gives

-2\,\left(\zeta^{2}-\ell^{2}\right)\left(\ell^{2}+\zeta^{2}(r-2)\right)\,.

In this case, the only positive root is
\zeta=|\ell|
which is not acceptable as we would have
\cos\alpha_{o}=\pm 1
. When we use
\cos\alpha_{o}=\zeta/\ell
in (
4.18
) we obtain the equation

2\ell^{4}\,\left(\frac{\zeta^{4}}{\ell^{4}}(18-5r)+\frac{\zeta^{2}}{\ell^{2}}(5r-1)+1\right)=0\,.

Then a routine analysis shows that, for any
r\geq 2
, there exists no acceptable solution.

The other possible case occurs when
\beta(s)
is a non constant solution of (
4.19
), i.e.,

(4.20)

\beta(s)=(\pm\zeta+\ell\cos\alpha_{o})s+c

where
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
is an integration constant. In this case, we already know that proper biharmonic
3
-Frenet helices do exist (see
[
6
,
7
]
). Here we extend this result to the case
r\geq 3
. More precisely, we prove the following existence result for proper
r
-harmonic helices into the Heisenberg space:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\ell\neq 0,r\geq 2
. Then there exists a proper
r
-harmonic
3
-Frenet helix
\gamma:{\mathbb{R}}\to M_{0,\ell}^{3}
. The Frenet field of
\gamma
is as in (
4.13
), where
\beta(s)
is a non constant function of the type (
4.20
).

Proof.

We assume
\ell>0
(the case
\ell<0
is analogous). We set
y=\zeta/\ell
and rewrite the polynomial
P_{4}(\zeta)\lvert_{m=0}
in (
4.18
) as follows:

\ell^{4}\,P(y)\,,

where

\displaystyle P(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8y^{4}+16\cos\alpha_{o}y^{3}-\left[(3(r-2)\cos(2\alpha_{o})-3r-4)\right]y^{2}

\displaystyle+2\cos\alpha_{o}\left[1+2r\sin^{2}\alpha_{o}\right]y+2\sin^{2}\alpha_{o}\,.

Then, in order to complete the proof, it is sufficient to show that for all
r\geq 2
there exists
0<\alpha_{0}<\pi
such that
P(y)
admits a positive root.
To this purpose we observe that
\lim_{y\to+\infty}P(y)=+\infty
and
P(0)>0
. Then it is enough to show that there exists
0<\alpha_{0}<\pi
such that
P(1/2)<0
for all
r\geq 2
. Now

P(1/2)=\cos^{2}\left(\frac{\alpha_{0}}{2}\right)\big(-2r\cos(2\alpha_{0})+(r+2)\cos(\alpha_{0})+r+4\big)\,.

A direct inspection shows that, for any fixed
r\geq 2
, the equation

-2r\cos(2\alpha_{0})+(r+2)\cos(\alpha_{0})+r+4=0

has a unique solution
\alpha^{*}\in(0,\pi)
given by

\cos(\alpha^{*})=-\frac{\sqrt{49r^{2}+68r+4}-r-2}{8r}\in\left(-1,-\frac{3}{4}\right)\,.

Finally, a routine analysis now shows that, if

\alpha^{*}<\alpha_{0}<\pi
, then we have
P(1/2)<0
as required to end the proof.

∎

Remark 4.4
.

The explicit parametrization of a curve

4.2.
The case

.

[

with

where

5.
r
-harmonic full

In Theorem

,

In this section we shall show that the hypothesis

can be weakened if we assume that the curve is a helix. In this case, the proof of non-existence becomes equivalent to the fact that a system of polynomial equations admits no acceptable solution. To achieve this, in some cases we shall use the notion of a Groebner basis.

In order to describe in detail the general situation, let us first recall that the cases
n=3,\,r\geq 2
have been fully understood in Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.4 of
[
4
]
. Also the case
n=2
is completely understood. Indeed, the full,
r
-harmonic
2
-Frenet helices in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m}
are determined by the condition
\kappa^{2}=r-1
and are the well-known circles of radius

(see
[
24
]
and references therein).

By way of summary, the open cases are
n\geq 4,\,r\geq 3,\,n<2r
.

Our first result in this context is non-existence for the triharmonic case. Indeed,

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\gamma
be a full
n
-Frenet helix in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m}
,
m\geq n\geq 4
. Then
\gamma
cannot be triharmonic.

Proof.

First, we observe that according to the previous discussion the open cases are
n=4
and
n=5
.

Now, a simple computation shows that, for all
\ell\geq 0
, we can write

(5.1)

\nabla_{T}^{\ell}T=\sum_{j=1}^{n}b[\ell,j]F_{j}

where the coefficients
b[\ell,j]
are defined recursively as follows:

b[0,j]=\delta_{1j}\,;\quad b[\ell,j]=\kappa_{j-1}\,b[\ell-1,j-1]-\kappa_{j}\,b[\ell-1,j+1]

with boundary conditions
\kappa_{0}=\kappa_{-1}=\kappa_{n}=0
. Using this expression of
\nabla_{T}^{\ell}T
and (
2.4
) we can easily compute the tension field
\tau_{r}(\gamma)
as prescribed by (
1.2
).

We study the two cases separately.

Case
n=4
:

The components of
\tau_{3}(\gamma)
with respect to the base
\{F_{1},\ldots,F_{4}\}
are

\left(0,\kappa_{1}\Big[\kappa_{1}^{4}+2\kappa_{1}^{2}(-1+\kappa_{2}^{2})+\kappa_{2}^{2}(-1+\kappa_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{3}^{2})\Big],0,-\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}\kappa_{3}(-1+\kappa_{1}^{2}+\kappa_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{3}^{2})\right)\,.

Thus the condition of triharmonicity is equivalent to the following polynomial system:

(5.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm(i)}&\kappa_{1}^{4}+2\kappa_{1}^{2}(-1+\kappa_{2}^{2})+\kappa_{2}^{2}(-1+\kappa_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{3}^{2})=0\\
&\\
{\rm(ii)}&\kappa_{1}^{2}+\kappa_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{3}^{2}=1\,.\end{array}\right.

Now, using (
5.2
)(ii) into (
5.2
)(i) and simplifying we obtain:

\left(1+\kappa_{3}^{2}\right)\left(-1+\kappa_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{3}^{2}\right)=0\,.

Since
\kappa_{1}>0
, the last equation is not compatible with (
5.2
)(ii) and so this case is ended.

Case
n=5
:

In this case the condition of triharmonicity is equivalent to the following polynomial system:

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm(i)}&\kappa_{1}^{4}+2\kappa_{1}^{2}(-1+\kappa_{2}^{2})+\kappa_{2}^{2}(-1+\kappa_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{3}^{2})=0\\
&\\
{\rm(ii)}&\kappa_{1}^{2}+\kappa_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{3}^{2}+\kappa_{4}^{2}=1\,.\end{array}\right.

Next, to simplify the notation, we write

x=\kappa_{1}^{2}\,,\quad y=\kappa_{2}^{2}\,,\quad z=\kappa_{3}^{2}\,,\quad t=\kappa_{4}^{2}\,.

Then our system (
5.3
) becomes:

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm(i)}&x^{2}+2x(-1+y)+y(-1+y+z)=0\\
&\\
{\rm(ii)}&x+y+z+t=1\,.\end{array}\right.

Now, from (
5.4
)(i) we deduce

(5.5)

z=\frac{-x^{2}-2xy+2x-y^{2}+y}{y}\,.

Using this condition in (
5.4
)(ii) we find

-ty+x^{2}+x(y-2)=0\,.

Now, if
t=x
, we have an immediate contradiction. If
t\neq x
, then

(5.6)

y=\frac{x^{2}-2x}{t-x}

which, from (
5.5
), gives

(5.7)

z=\frac{-t^{2}+t+x}{t-x}\,.

Finally, we show that (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) lead us to a contradiction. Indeed,
y>0
implies
t<x
from (
5.6
). But
t<x
and (
5.7
) easily imply
z<0
, a contradiction and so the proof is completed.
∎

Using the explicit expression of

which can always be computed using (

) and (

More precisely, we obtain the following non-existence result:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\gamma
be a full

{\mathbb{S}}^{m}

\gamma

We examine the two cases separately.

Case

\tau_{4}(\gamma)

. The associated algebraic variety can be described by means of a Groebner basis of the ideal

. It follows that the solutions of the polynomial system

P_{1}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3})=0,\quad P_{2}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3})=0,\quad P_{3}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3})=0

\mathcal{I}
. Next, the Mathematica

gives a convenient, simplified Groebner basis

of

. The outcome is:

\mathcal{G}\mathcal{B}=\left\{(c-1)c\left((c-2)\kappa_{3}^{2}+c\right),2\left(\kappa_{2}^{2}+\kappa_{3}^{2}\right)-c((c-3)c+4),(c-2)(c-1)c+2\kappa_{1}^{2}\right\}\,.

admits no solution with

\displaystyle P_{1}(\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},\kappa_{3})=

Fractional non-homogeneous counting process
Nick Laskin

Abstract
A new fractional non-homogeneous counting process has been introduced and developed using the Kilbas and Saigo three-parameter generalization of the Mittag-Leffler function. The probability distribution function of this process reproduces for certain set of the fractality parameters the famous Poisson and fractional Poisson probability distributions as well as the probability distribution function of a counting process displaying stretched exponential interarrival times distribution. Applications of the developed fractional non-homogeneous counting process cover fractional compound process, the generalization of combinatorial polynomials and numbers, statistics of the fractional non-homogeneous counting process and a new representation of the Kilbas and Saigo function. PACS numbers: 05.10.Gg; 05.45.Df; 42.50.-p. Keywords : Fractional non-homogeneous counting process, fractional Poisson process, generating functions, combinatorial polynomials and numbers, Mittag-Leffler function, Kilbas and Saigo function

Recently, a discrete probability distribution function

P_{\sigma}(n,x)=\frac{x^{\sigma n}}{n!}\exp(-x^{\sigma}),\quad x\geq 0,\quad 0<\sigma\leq 1,\quad n=0,1,2,...,

Interarrival times distribution

An important characteristic of any counting process is the time between
successive counts/arrivals - the interarrival time. Interarrival time is a
random variable having the probability distribution function
\psi_{\mu,\beta}(\tau),

\psi_{\mu,\beta}(\tau)=-\frac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\beta}(\tau),

(69)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\beta}(\tau)
is the probability that a given
interarrival time is greater or equal to
\tau

[
4
]

\mathcal{P}_{\mu,\beta}(\tau)=1-\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{\mu,\beta}(n,\tau)=P_{\mu,\beta}(0,\tau),

(70)

here
P_{\mu,\beta}(n,\tau)
is defined by Eq.(
53
).

It follows from Eqs.(
69
) and (
70
) that the probability
distribution function of the interarrival times has the form

\psi_{\mu,\beta}(\tau)=\frac{\mu+\beta}{\tau}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}(l+1)(\lambda_{\mu+\beta}\tau^{\mu+\beta})^{l+1}\prod\limits_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\Gamma[\mu(k(1+\frac{\beta}{\mu})+\frac{\beta}{\mu})+1]}{\Gamma[\mu(k(1+\frac{\beta}{\mu})+\frac{\beta}{\mu}+1)+1]}.

(71)

The Laplace transform
\varphi_{\mu,\beta}(u)
of the interarrival
probability distribution function is defined as

\varphi_{\mu,\beta}(u)=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}d\tau e^{-u\tau}\psi_{\mu,\beta}(\tau),

(72)

and has the form

\varphi_{\mu,\beta}(u)=(\mu+\beta)\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}(l+1)(\lambda_{\mu+\beta})^{l+1}\frac{\Gamma[(\mu+\beta)(l+1)]}{u^{(\mu+\beta)(l+1)}}\times

(73)

\prod\limits_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\Gamma[\mu(k(1+\frac{\beta}{\mu})+\frac{\beta}{\mu})+1]}{\Gamma[\mu(k(1+\frac{\beta}{\mu})+\frac{\beta}{\mu}+1)+1]}.

It easy to see that when
\mu=1
and
\beta=0
, Eq.(
71
)
transforms into the exponential distribution defined by Eq.(
13
). From
Eq.(
73
) we obtain the Laplace transform of the exponential
distribution

\varphi_{1,0}(u)=\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{1}+u}.

When
0<\mu\leq 1
and
\beta=0
, Eq.(
71
) becomes the
probability distribution of interarrival times of the fractional Poisson
process

\psi_{\mu,0}(\tau)=\lambda_{\mu}\tau^{\mu-1}E_{\mu,\mu}(-\lambda_{\mu}\tau^{\mu}),

(74)

where
E_{\mu,\mu}(z)
is the two parameter Mittag-Leffler function
defined by the series
[
16

E_{\mu,\mu}(z)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{\Gamma(\mu n+\mu)}.

(75)

From and Eq.(
73
) it follows that the Laplace transform has the
form
[
4
]

\varphi_{\mu,0}(u)=\frac{\lambda_{\mu}}{\lambda_{\mu}+u^{\mu}}.

When
\mu=1
and

exponential distribution defined by Eq.(
6
) if we take into account
the notation introduced by Eq.(

) we
obtain the Laplace transform of the stretched exponential distribution

\varphi_{\sigma}(u)=\sigma\overline{\lambda}_{\sigma}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-\overline{\lambda}_{\sigma})^{l}}{l!}\frac{\Gamma(\sigma(l+1))}{u^{\sigma(l+1)}},

(76)

here
\sigma
and
\overline{\lambda}_{\sigma}
are defined by Eq.(
43

With
\mu+\beta=1
Eq.(
71
) goes to

\psi_{\mu,1-\mu}(\tau)=\frac{1}{\tau}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}(l+1)(\lambda\tau)^{l+1}\prod\limits_{k=0}^{l}\frac{\Gamma(k+2-\mu)}{\Gamma(k+2)},

(77)

and Eq.(

6.1
Fractional compound process

We call stochastic process
\{X_{\mu,\beta}(t)
,
t\geq 0\}
a fractional
compound process if it is represented by

X_{\mu,\beta}(t)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{\mu,\beta}(t)}Y_{i},

(79)

where
\{N_{\mu,\beta}(t)
,
t\geq 0\}
is a fractional counting process
whose probability distribution function is introduced by Eq.(
48
)
or Eq.(
53
), and
\{Y_{i}
,
i=1,2,...\}
are independent and
identically distributed random variables with probability distribution
function
p(Y_{i})
for each
Y_{i}
. It is assumed that the process
\{N_{\mu,\beta}(t)
,
t\geq 0\}
and the sequence of random variables
\{Y_{i}
,
i=1,2,...\}
are independent.

The moment generating function
J_{\mu,\beta}(s,t)
of fractional
generalized compound process is defined as follows

J_{\mu,\beta}(s,t)=<\exp\{sX_{\mu,\beta}(t)\}>_{Y,N_{\mu,\beta}(t)},

(80)

where
<...>_{Y_{i},N_{\mu,\beta}(t)}
stands for average which includes
two statistically independent averaging procedures:

1. Averaging
<...>_{Y}
over independent random variables
Y_{i}
, defined
by

<...>_{Y}=\int dY_{1}...dY_{n}p(Y_{1})...p(Y_{n})...=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\int dY_{i}p(Y_{i})....

(81)

2. Averaging over random number of counts defined by

<...>_{N_{\mu,\beta}(t)}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{\mu,\beta}(n,t)...,

(82)

where
P_{\mu,\beta}(n,t)
is given by Eq.(
48
).

One can see from Eq.(
80
) that
k^{\mathrm{th}}
order moment of
fractional compound process
X_{\mu,\beta}(t)
is obtained by
differentiating
J_{\mu,\beta}(s,t)

k
times with respect to
s
and
then setting
s=0
, that is

<(X_{\mu,\beta}(t))^{k}>_{Y,N_{\mu,\beta}(t)}=\frac{\partial^{k}}{\partial s^{k}}J_{\mu,\beta}(s,t)_{|s=0}.

(83)

To obtain equation for the moment generating function
J_{\mu,\beta}(s,t)

we apply Eqs.(
81
) and (
82
) to Eq.(
80
)

J_{\mu,\beta}(s,t)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}<\exp\{sX_{\mu,\beta}(t)|N_{\mu,\beta}(t)=n\}>_{Y}P_{\mu,\beta}(n,t)=

(84)

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}<\exp\{s(Y_{1}+...+Y_{n})\}>_{Y}\times\frac{(-z)^{n}}{n!}E_{\mu,1+\frac{\beta}{\mu},\frac{\beta}{\mu}}^{(n)}(z)\left|{}_{z=-\lambda_{\mu+\beta}t^{\mu+\beta}}\right.,

where we used the independence of
\{Y_{1},Y_{2},...\}
and
\{N_{\mu,\beta}(t)
,
t\geq 0\}
and the independence of the
Y_{i}
’s between themselves.
Hence, setting

g(s)=\int dY_{i}p(Y_{i})\exp\{sY_{i}\},

(85)

for the moment generating function of random variables
Y_{i}
, we obtain
from Eq.(
84
) the moment generating function
J_{\mu,\beta}(s,t)

of the fractional compound process

J_{\mu,\beta}(s,t)=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g^{n}(s)\times\frac{(-z)^{n}}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{dz^{n}}\left(E_{\mu,1+\frac{\beta}{\mu},\frac{\beta}{\mu}}(z)\right)|_{z=-\lambda_{\mu+\beta}t^{\mu+\beta}}=

(86)

E_{\mu,1+\frac{\beta}{\mu},\frac{\beta}{\mu}}(\lambda_{\mu+\beta}t^{\mu+\beta}(g(s)-1)).

By differentiating the above with respect to
s
and setting
s=0
, it is
easy to obtain the mean of the fractional compound process,

<X_{\mu,\beta}(t)>_{Y_{i},N_{\mu,\beta}(t)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial s}J_{\mu,\beta}(s,t)_{|s=0}=<Y>_{Y}\lambda_{\mu+\beta}t^{\mu+\beta}\frac{\Gamma(\beta+1)}{\Gamma(\mu+\beta+1)},

(87)

which is a manifestation of independency between fractional counting process

\{N_{\mu,\beta}(t)
,
t\geq 0\}
and random variables
Y_{i}.

7
Conclusions

A new fractional non-homogeneous counting process has been introduced and
developed using the Kilbas and Saigo three-parameter generalization of the
Mittag-Leffler function. The probability distribution function of this
process reproduces for certain set of the fractality parameters the famous
Poisson and fractional Poisson probability distributions as well as the
probability distribution function of a counting process displaying stretched
exponential interarrival times distribution.

Applications of the developed fractional non-homogeneous counting process
cover fractional compound process, the generalization of combinatorial
polynomials and numbers and their generating functions, variance, skewness
and kurtosis excess of the fractional non-homogeneous counting process
expressed through four central moments and a new representation of the
Kilbas and Saigo function.

The presented results will stimulate further development of the fundamentals
and various applications in a new field of probability theory and stochastic
processes, which can be called fractional stochastic processes and their
applications.

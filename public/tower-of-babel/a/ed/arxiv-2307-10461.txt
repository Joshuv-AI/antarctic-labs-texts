Algebraic Hyperbolicity of Very General Hypersurfaces in Homogeneous Varieties
Lucas Mioranci

Abstract
. We generalize techniques by Coskun, Riedl, and Yeong, and obtain an almost optimal bound on the degree for the algebraic hyperbolicity of very general hypersurfaces in homogeneous varieties. As examples, we work out the cases of very general hypersurfaces in products of Grassmannians, orthogonal and symplectic Grassmannians, and flag varieties.

Primary: 32Q45, 14M17 ; Secondary: 14J70, 14M15

1.
Introduction

A complex projective variety
X
is
algebraically hyperbolic
if for some ample divisor
H
there exists a real number
\epsilon>0
such that for any integral curve
C\subset X
the inequality

2g(C)-2\geq\epsilon\deg_{H}(C)

is satisfied, where
g(C)
is the geometric genus of
C
. The goal of this paper is to prove the algebraic hyperbolicity of very general hypersurfaces in homogeneous varieties by generalizing techniques developed by Clemens
[
Cle86
,
Cle03
]
, Ein
[
Ein88
,
Ein91
]
, Voisin
[
Voi96
,
Voi98
]
, Pacienza
[
Pac02
,
Pac03
]
, Clemens and Ran
[
CR04
]
, Coskun and Riedl
[
CR19
,
CR23
]
and Yeong
[
Yeo22
]
.

A complex manifold
X
is called
(Brody) hyperbolic
if every entire map
f:\mathbb{C}\to X
is constant, and it is
Kobayashi hyperbolic
if the Kobayashi pseudometric is nondegenerate. In general, Kobayashi hyperbolicity implies Brody hyperbolicity, and when
X
is a compact complex manifold, Brody proved that the converse holds
[
Bro78
]
. Hyperbolicity is conjectured to have deep connections to the geometry and arithmetic of varieties:

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Lang’s Conjectures
[
Lan86
]
)

•

A projective algebraic variety
X
is hyperbolic if and only if every subvariety of
X
is of general type.

•

A projective variety is hyperbolic if and only if it contains only finitely many rational points over any finite field extension.

Conjecture 1.2
.

(Green-Griffiths-Lang Conjecture
[
GG80
]
)
A smooth projective variety
X
of general type contains a proper subvariety
S\subsetneq X
containing all entire curves of
X
.

See Demailly’s survey paper
[
Dem20
]
for an exposition on hyperbolicity.

It can be, however, challenging to prove the hyperbolicity of a given variety. Demailly introduced algebraic hyperbolicity as an algebraic analogue for hyperbolicity, and proved that, for smooth projective varieties, hyperbolicity implies algebraic hyperbolicity and conjectured the converse
[
Dem97
]
.

A lot of progress has been made in the study of the algebraic hyperbolicity of very general hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. For
n=3
, Xu
[
Xu94
]
proved that very general hypersurfaces
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
of degree
d\geq 6
are algebraically hyperbolic. This was improved by Coskun and Riedl
[
CR19
]
, who showed that also a very general quintic surface is algebraically hyperbolic. Since surfaces of degree at most four contain rational curves and cannot be algebraically hyperbolic, the classification for very general hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is complete. For
n\geq 4
, Clemens and Ein
[
Cle86
,
Ein88
]
proved that
X
is algebraically hyperbolic when
d\geq 2n
. This was improved to
d\geq 2n-1
by Voisin
[
Voi96
,
Voi98
]
. Pacienza
[
Pac03
]
and Clemens and Ran
[
CR04
]
proved it for
d\geq 2n-2
and
n\geq 6
. Yeong
[
Yeo22
]
did the octic 4-fold case and improved the result to
d\geq 2n-2
and
n\geq 5
. If
d\leq 2n-3
,
X
contains lines, thus it is not algebraically hyperbolic. Hence, the only remaining case in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is for sextic threefolds.

Haase and Ilten
[
HI21
]
started to classify algebraically hyperbolic surfaces in toric threefolds, leaving a list of open cases at the end of their paper. Coskun and Riedl resolved almost all of their open cases in
[
CR23
]
. Robins
[
Rob23
]
uses Haase and Ilten’s method to determine many algebraically hyperbolic surfaces in toric varieties with Picard rank 2 and 3. Yeong
[
Yeo22
]
applies Coskun and Riedl’s techniques to almost completely classify algebraically hyperbolic hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
, except for some degrees in
\mathbb{P}^{3}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

In this paper, we generalize the techniques of Coskun, Riedl, and Yeong to the context of more general homogeneous varieties. As an application, we obtain an almost sharp bound for the degree for which very general hypersurfaces of (products of) Grassmannians, orthogonal Grassmannians, symplectic Grassmannians, and flag varieties are algebraically hyperbolic.

Let us start by defining the general setting we will refer to throughout the paper.

Main Setting 1.3
.

Let
A
be a smooth complex variety such that:

•

A
is homogeneous with a transitive action by an algebraic group
G
,

•

A
has a projectively normal embedding into a product of projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
,

•

The divisors
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
of
A
corresponding to the pull-backs of the hyperplane classes
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}}(1)
generate the Picard group of
A
.

Write the class of the canonical divisor of
A
as
K_{A}=a_{1}H_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}H_{m}
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the line bundle
d_{1}H_{1}+\cdots+d_{m}H_{m}
for
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}>0
, invariant under
G
, and assume that
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
is a collection of section-dominating line bundles for
\mathcal{E}
(see section 2). A degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
hypersurface
X
of
A
is a section of
\mathcal{E}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A
be a homogeneous variety as in the Main Setting
1.3
.

•

If
\dim A\geq 4
and
d_{i}\geq\dim A-a_{i}-2
for all
1\leq i\leq m
, then a very general hypersurface
X
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots d_{m})
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d_{i}\leq\dim A-a_{i}-4
for some
1\leq i\leq m
, then a general hypersurface
X
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots d_{m})
contains lines. In particular,
X
is not algebraically hyperbolic.

The case
d_{i}\leq\dim A-a_{i}-3
for some
1\leq i\leq m
is left open. When
\dim A\leq 4
, there are examples when it fails to imply algebraic hyperbolicity (see Examples
4.4
and
4.5
.) For
A=\mathbb{P}^{4}
, the case of sextic threefolds remains open. However, when
\dim A\geq 5
, it does imply algebraic hyperbolicity for
A=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
A=\mathbb{P}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}

[
Yeo22
]
. We conjecture
X
is algebraically hyperbolic when
\dim A\geq 5
(see Conjecture
4.6
.)

We then apply the theorem to concrete examples of homogeneous varieties. We note that the theorem can also be applied to products of these varieties.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
A
be a:

(1)

Grassmannian
A=G(k,n)

•

If
\dim A=k(n-k)\geq 4
and
d\geq k(n-k)+n-2
, then a very general degree
d
hypersurface of
G(k,n)
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d\leq k(n-k)+n-4
, then a general degree
d
hypersurface of
G(k,n)
contains a line. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

(2)

Product of Grassmannians
A=\prod_{i=1}^{m}G(k_{i},n_{i})

•

If
\dim A=\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}(n_{i}-k_{i})\geq 4
and
d_{i}\geq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}(n_{j}-k_{j})\right)+n_{i}-2
, for all
1\leq i\leq m
, then a very general hypersurface of
\prod_{i=1}^{m}G(k_{i},n_{i})
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d_{i}\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}(n_{j}-k_{j})\right)+n_{i}-4
for some
i
, then a general hypersurface of degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
of
\prod_{i=1}^{m}G(k_{i},n_{i})
contains a line. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

(3)

Orthogonal Grassmannian
A=OG(k,n)

•

If
\dim OG(k,n)=\frac{k(2n-3k-1)}{2}\geq 4
and
d\geq\frac{k(2n-3k-1)}{2}+n-3k-1
, then a very general hypersurface of
OG(k,n)
of degree
d
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d\leq\frac{k(2n-3k-1)}{2}+n-3k-3
, then a general hypersurface of degree
d
of
OG(k,n)
contains a line. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

(4)

Symplectic Grassmannian
A=SG(k,n)

•

If
\dim A=\frac{k(2n-3k+1)}{2}\geq 4
and
d\geq\frac{k(2n-3k+1)}{2}+n+3k
, then a very general degree
d
hypersurface of
SG(k,n)
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d\leq\frac{k(2n-3k+1)}{2}+n+3k-2
, then a general degree
d
hypersurface of
SG(k,n)
contains a line. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

(5)

Flag Variety
A=F(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m};n)

•

If
\dim A=\sum_{i=0}^{m}k_{i+1}(k_{i+1}-k_{i})\geq 4
and
d_{i}\geq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}k_{j+1}(k_{j+1}-k_{j})\right)+k_{i+1}-k_{i-1}-2
for all
1\leq i\leq m
, then a very general hypersurface of
F(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m};n)
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d_{i}\leq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}k_{j+1}(k_{j+1}-k_{j})\right)+k_{i+1}-k_{i-1}-4
for some
i
, then a general hypersurface of
F(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m};n)
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
contains a line. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

Organization of the paper.
In section 2, we describe the general setup we work on and obtain a first, weaker bound on algebraic hyperbolicity. Section 3 is dedicated to working out the scroll argument to the general case of homogeneous varieties and proving the bound in Theorem
1.4
. In Section 4 we show when the hypersurface contains lines, thus obtaining the lower bound to algebraic hyperbolicity. In Section 5, we apply the bounds obtained in the previous sections to explicit examples of homogeneous varieties.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Setup

We first describe the general setup for the techniques developed by Clemens and Ein
[
Ein88
,
Ein91
]
, Voisin
[
Voi96
,
Voi98
]
, Pacienza
[
Pac02
,
Pac03
]
, Coskun and Riedl
[
CR19
,
CR23
]
and Yeong
[
Yeo22
]
.

Let
A
be a smooth, complex projective variety of dimension
D
, and assume
A
admits a transitive group action by an algebraic group
G
. Let
\mathcal{E}
a globally generated vector bundle on
A
, invariant under
G
, of rank
r<D-1
, and let
V=H^{0}(A,\mathcal{E})
. We will be particularly interested when
\mathcal{E}
is a very ample line bundle on
A
.

Let
X
be the zero locus of a very general section of
\mathcal{E}
, and suppose
X
contains a curve
Y
of degree
e
and geometric genus
g
. Then, if
\mathcal{X}_{1}
is the universal hypersurface over
V
, we get the relative Hilbert scheme
\mathcal{H}\to V
with universal curve
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
, where the general fiber of
\mathcal{Y}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}
is a curve of geometric genus
g
and degree
e
. We can find a
G
-invariant subvariety
U\subset\mathcal{H}
such that the map
U\to V
is étale. Restricting
\mathcal{Y}_{1}
to
U
and taking a resolution of the general fiber, we get a smooth family
\mathcal{Y}\to U
whose fibers are smooth curves of genus
g
. We pullback
\mathcal{X}_{1}
to a family
\mathcal{X}
over
U
, with projection maps
\pi_{1}:\mathcal{X}\to U
and
\pi_{2}:\mathcal{X}\to A
. There is a natural generically injective map
h:\mathcal{Y}\to\mathcal{X}
.

We define the
vertical tangent sheaf

T_{\mathcal{X}/A}
by the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{X}/A}\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{X}}\longrightarrow\pi_{2}^{*}T_{A}\longrightarrow 0.

Since we constructed
\mathcal{Y}
to be stable under the
G
-action, we have that
\pi_{2}\circ h
dominates
A
and that the map
T_{\mathcal{Y}}\to h^{*}\pi_{2}^{*}T_{A}
is surjective. Similarly, we define the vertical tangent sheaf
T_{\mathcal{Y}/A}
as the kernel in the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{Y}/A}\longrightarrow T_{\mathcal{Y}}\longrightarrow h^{*}\pi_{2}^{*}T_{A}\longrightarrow 0.

Let
M_{\mathcal{E}}
denote the
Lazarsfeld-Mukai bundle
associated to
\mathcal{E}
, defined by the sequence

0\longrightarrow M_{\mathcal{E}}\longrightarrow H^{0}(A,\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow 0.

For a general element
t
of
U
, let
Y_{t}
be the fiber of
\mathcal{Y}
over
t
and
X_{t}
be the fiber of
\mathcal{X}
over
t
. Denote by
h_{t}:Y_{t}\to X_{t}
the restriction of
h
to
Y_{t}
. The following result translates the relation between the vertical tangent sheaves and the Lazarsfeld-Mukai bundle.

Proposition 2.1
.

(cf
[
CR23
, Proposition 2.1]
)

(1)

N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}\cong N_{h/\mathcal{X}}|_{Y_{t}}
.

(2)

T_{\mathcal{X}/A}\cong\pi_{2}^{*}M_{\mathcal{E}}
.

(3)

N_{h/\mathcal{X}}
is the cokernel of the map of vertical tangent sheaves
T_{\mathcal{Y}/A}\to T_{\mathcal{X}/A}
.

2.2.
The degree of the normal bundle and section-dominating line bundles

There is a close relation between the degree of the normal bundle
N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}
and the genus of the curve
Y_{t}
described by the following lemma. We can use it to bound the genus of
Y_{t}
by finding a bound for the degree of the normal bundle
N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(cf
[
CR23
, Lemma 2.2]
) The following holds:

\deg(N_{h_{t}/X_{t}})=2g(Y_{t})-2-K_{X_{t}}\cdot h_{t}(Y_{t}).

By Proposition
2.1
, we have a surjection
M_{\mathcal{E}}|_{Y_{t}}\to N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}
. We can get a surjection to
N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}
from a simpler bundle by using the notion of
section-dominating
line bundles.

Definition 2.3
.

(cf
[
CR23
, Definition 2.3]
) Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
A
. A collection of non-trivial globally generated line bundles
L_{1},\ldots,L_{m}
is a
section-dominating collection
of line bundles for
\mathcal{E}
if
\mathcal{E}\otimes L_{i}^{\vee}
is globally generated for every
1\leq i\leq m
and the map

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}\left(H^{0}(L_{i}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})\otimes H^{0}(\mathcal{E}\otimes L_{i}^{\vee})\right)\to H^{0}(\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})

is surjective for every point
p\in A
.

Example 2.4
.

Suppose
A=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and let
H
be the hyperplane class. Then, for any
\mathcal{E}=dH
with
d>0
,
H
is a section-dominating line bundle for
\mathcal{E}
. To prove this, we need to show that

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})\otimes H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d-1))\to H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})

is surjective. Choose coordinates
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}
for
\mathbb{P}^{n}
so that
p=(1:0:\cdots:0)
. Then

H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})
is the set of degree
d
forms without
x_{0}^{d}
term, and these can be written as a combination of products of linear forms without
x_{0}
term in
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(1)\otimes\mathcal{I}_{p})
and degree
d-1
forms in
H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d-1))
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
A=G(k,n)
be the Grassmannians of
k
-planes in the affine
n
-space, and let
H
correspond to the hyperplane class, that is, the pullback
p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)
via the Plücker embedding
p:G(k,n)\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Then, for any
\mathcal{E}=dH
,
d>0
,
H
is a section-dominating line bundle for
\mathcal{E}
. This follows directly from the previous example and the projective normality of the Grassmannian under the Plücker embedding (cf
[
RR85
]
.) By
[
RR85
]
, a similar result is true for flag varieties and Schubert varieties.

Example 2.6
.

This property can be extended to products: let
A=\prod_{i=1}^{m}A_{i}
be a product of varieties
A_{i}
such that for each
i
,
H_{i}
is a section-dominating line bundle for
\mathcal{E}_{i}=d_{i}H_{i}
for some
d_{i}>0
. Then
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
is a section-dominating collection of line bundles for
\mathcal{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}H_{i}
.

Given a section of
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}\otimes L_{i}^{\vee})
, the natural multiplication map defines maps
L_{i}\to\mathcal{E}
and
H^{0}(L_{i})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{E})
, and induces a map
M_{L_{i}}\to M_{\mathcal{E}}
. By choosing bases for
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}\otimes L_{i}^{\vee})
, we can get a surjection to
M_{\mathcal{E}}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

(cf
[
CR23
, Proposition 2.7]
)
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a globally generated vector bundle and
M_{\mathcal{E}}
the Lazarsfeld-Mukai bundle associated to
\mathcal{E}
. Let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{m}
be a section-dominating collection of line bundles for
\mathcal{E}
. Then, there is a surjection

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}M_{L_{i}}^{\oplus s_{i}}\to M_{\mathcal{E}}

for some integers
s_{i}
.

From Proposition
2.7
and the surjection
M_{\mathcal{E}}|_{Y_{t}}\to N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}
, we obtain a surjection to the normal bundle

\beta:\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}M_{L_{i}}^{\oplus s_{i}}|_{Y_{t}}\to N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}.

By choosing generic polynomials of
H^{0}(\mathcal{E}\otimes L_{i}^{\vee})
for the multiplication in Proposition
2.7
, the rank of the image of
\beta
is a strictly increasing function of the
s_{i}
until surjectivity is reached (cf
[
Cle03
]
.) In particular, we can choose a surjection
\beta
so that
s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}\leq\rk N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}
.

This surjection can be used to get a bound for the degree of
N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}
in terms of the integers
s_{i}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\beta:\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}M_{L_{i}}^{\oplus s_{i}}|_{Y_{t}}\to N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}
be a surjective map as above. Then

\deg N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}\geq-\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\deg L_{i}|_{Y_{t}}.

Proof.

Let
K
be the kernel of
\beta
:

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}M_{L_{i}}^{\oplus s_{i}}|_{Y_{t}}\overset{\beta}{\longrightarrow}N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}\longrightarrow 0.

Then by the definition of the
M_{L_{i}}
, we get an injection
K\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}H^{0}(A,L_{i})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{A}|_{Y_{t}}
. Since it is a direct sum of trivial bundles,
\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}H^{0}(A,L_{i})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{A}|_{Y_{t}}
is a semi-stable vector bundle. It follows that

\frac{\deg K}{\rk K}=\mu(K)\leq 0,

thus

\deg(K)\leq 0,

hence, from the sequence above and the sequence defining
M_{L_{i}}
, we have

\deg N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}=\deg\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}M_{L_{i}}^{\oplus s_{i}}|_{Y_{t}}\right)-\deg K\geq\deg\left(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}M_{L_{i}}^{\oplus s_{i}}|_{Y_{t}}\right)=-\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\deg L_{i}|_{Y_{t}}.

∎

By combining Lemmas
2.2
and
2.8
, we obtain a bound for the genus:

(2.1)

2g(Y_{t})-2\geq K_{X_{t}}\cdot Y_{t}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\deg L_{i}|_{Y_{t}}.

Therefore, if we can control the integers
s_{i}
, we can prove the algebraic hyperbolicity of
X_{t}
. This leads us to our first criteria for hyperbolicity. This result is similar to
[
CR23
, Cor.2.9]
and
[
HI21
, Thm 3.6]
.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
A
be a homogeneous variety of dimension
D
. Let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{m}
be a collection of section-dominating line bundles for
\mathcal{E}
on
A
. If, for some
\epsilon>0
,

K_{X_{t}}\cdot Y_{t}\geq(D-2+\epsilon)\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg L_{i}|_{Y_{t}},

then
X_{t}
is algebraically hyperbolic.

Proof.

As before, we can choose the
s_{i}
so that
s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}\leq\rk N_{h_{t}/X_{t}}=D-2
. In particular, we can assume
s_{i}\leq D-2
for every
1\leq i\leq m
. Thus, by inequality (
2.1
), we get

2g(Y_{t})-2\geq K_{X_{t}}\cdot Y_{t}-(D-2)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg L_{i}|_{Y_{t}}.

Therefore, if
K_{X_{t}}\cdot Y_{t}\geq(D-2+\epsilon)\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg L_{i}|_{Y_{t}}
, we get

2g(Y_{t})-2\geq\epsilon\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{m}\deg L_{i}|_{Y_{t}}.

Hence,
X_{t}
is algebraically hyperbolic.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

This initial setup can also be defined when
A
is not homogeneous but contains a Zariski-open homogeneous set
A_{0}
. Results equivalent to Proposition
2.9
can be obtained in this case, see for example,
[
CR23
, Theorem 1.2]
. This allowed Coskun and Riedl
[
CR23
]
to classify algebraically hyperbolic surfaces
X
in the cases when
A=
a Hirzebruch surface
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(e))
,
A=\mathbb{P}^{3}
blown up at a single point, and
A=
a weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(1,1,1,n)
. We believe the generalizations made in this paper can also be worked out when
A
contains a Zariski-open homogeneous set
A_{0}
, allowing future studies of higher dimensional toric varieties, such as blowups of projective spaces and weighted projective spaces.

3.
Scroll method for homogeneous varieties

In this section, we work out the scroll method introduced by Coskun and Riedl in
[
CR23
]
to a more general setting. This technique builds a surface scroll on the curve
Y_{t}
whose degree can be used to improve the bound for algebraic hyperbolicity obtained in Proposition
2.9
.

Here we will work on homogeneous varieties satisfying the Main Setting
1.3
which we recall here:

Let
A
be a smooth complex variety of dimension
D
such that:

•

A
is homogeneous with a transitive action by an algebraic group
G
,

•

A
has a projectively normal embedding into a product of projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
,

•

The divisors
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
of
A
corresponding to the pull-backs of the hyperplane classes
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}}(1)
generate the Picard group of
A
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the line bundle
d_{1}H_{1}+\cdots+d_{m}H_{m}
for
d_{1},\ldots,d_{m}>0
, invariant under
G
, and assume that
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
is a collection of section-dominating line bundles for
\mathcal{E}
. To simplify the notation, we write
X
for the zero locus of a very general section of
\mathcal{E}
, and
C
for the curve in
X
, instead of
X_{t}
and
Y_{t}
.

Write the class of the canonical divisor of
A
as
K_{A}=a_{1}H_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}H_{m}
. By the adjunction formula,
K_{X}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}+d_{i})H_{i}|_{X}
. In this case, Proposition
2.9
can be rephrased as follows.

Corollary 3.1
.

If there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
d_{i}\geq(D-a_{i}-2)+\epsilon
for all
1\leq i\leq m
, then
X
is algebraically hyperbolic.

Proof.

Since

K_{X}\cdot C=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}+d_{i})H_{i}\cdot C\geq(D-2+\epsilon)\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{m}H_{i}\cdot C,

the result follows from Proposition
2.9
.
∎

The main theorem in this section shows that we do not need the
\epsilon
in the bound above.

Definition 3.2
.

Given a product of projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
, the fibers of the projection

\pi_{1}^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}\to\mathbb{P}^{N_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}

are
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
. We define a
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-line
as a line contained in one of these fibers. Equivalently, a
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-line is a curve of numerical class
H_{1}^{N_{1}-1}H_{2}^{N_{2}}\cdots H_{m}^{N_{m}}
. We define
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-lines
similarly for
i=1,\ldots,m
. We call a surface scroll a
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-scroll
if there is a
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-line through every point of it.

We can see the space of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-lines as the product

G(2,N_{1}+1)\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
and the Fano scheme of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-lines contained in
A
as a subscheme
F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)\subset G(2,N_{1}+1)\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
. Similarly for
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-lines. We will need the dimension of the space of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-lines in
A
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
F_{1}^{N_{i}}(A)
be the Fano scheme of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-lines in
A
. Then

\dim F_{1}^{N_{i}}(A)=\dim A-a_{i}-3.

Proof.

Let
L
be a
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-line in
A
. Since
A
is a homogeneous variety, its tangent bundle
T_{A}
is globally generated, thus
T_{A}|_{L}
is globally generated. A classic deformation argument then shows that
\dim F_{1}^{N_{i}}(A)=h^{0}(L,N_{L/A})=\dim A-K_{A}\cdot L-3=\dim A-a_{i}-3
(cf
[
Deb01
, Chapter 2]
.)
∎

We use the following lemma to construct a scroll.

Lemma 3.4
.

(cf
[
CR23
, Lemma 2.13]
)
A rank one quotient
Q
of
M_{H_{i}}|_{C}
induces a
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-scroll
\Sigma
over
C
of
H_{i}
-degree equal to
\deg Q+(H_{i}\cdot C)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
A
be a homogeneous variety as in the Main Setting
1.3
. If
\dim A\geq 4
and
d_{i}\geq\dim A-a_{i}-2
for all
1\leq i\leq m
, then
X
is algebraically hyperbolic.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.7
, there is a surjection

\beta:\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}M_{H_{i}}^{\oplus s_{i}}|_{C}\to N_{C/X}.

Without loss of generality, assume
s_{1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{m}
. As before, we can assume
s_{1}+\cdots+s_{m}\leq\rk N_{C/X}=D-2
. Assume also that
s_{1}
is the minimum possible for which the map
\beta
is surjective. We divide the proof into the cases
s_{1}\leq D-3
, and the case
s_{1}=D-2
.

Case 1.
Suppose first that
s_{1}\leq D-3
. So,
s_{i}\leq D-3
for all
i
. Then, the inequality

2g(C)-2\geq K_{X}\cdot C-\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}\deg H_{i}|_{C}

from (
2.1
) implies that

2g(C)-2\geq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}+d_{i})H_{i}\right)\cdot C-(D-3)\sum_{i=1}^{m}(H_{i}\cdot C)

or equivalently,

2g(C)-2\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}+d_{i}-D+3)(H_{i}\cdot C).

Since, by hypothesis,
d_{i}\geq D-a_{i}-2
for
i=1,\ldots,m
, we get

2g(C)-2\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}(H_{i}\cdot C).

Case 2.
Suppose now that
s_{1}=D-2
, so we have
s_{2}=\cdots=s_{m}=0
. In this case, we have a surjection

\beta:M_{H_{1}}^{\oplus D-2}|_{C}\to N_{C/X}.

Since we are assuming this is the minimum value for
s_{1}
for which
\beta
is surjective, this means that the induced map

(3.1)

M_{H_{1}}|_{C}\to N_{C/X}/\beta\left(M_{H_{1}}^{\oplus D-3}|_{C}\right)

has a rank-one image
Q
(cf
[
Cle03
]
.) By Lemma
3.4
, this induces a
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-scroll
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
containing
C
of
H_{1}
-degree
\Sigma\cdot H_{1}^{2}=\deg Q+(H_{1}\cdot C)
. And since
\Sigma
is a
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-scroll, we have
H_{1}H_{j}\cdot\Sigma=H_{j}\cdot C
for
j\neq 1
. Thus,
\Sigma
has numerical class

(H_{1}\cdot C+\deg Q)H_{1}^{N_{1}-2}H_{2}^{N_{2}}\cdots H_{m}^{N_{m}}+(H_{2}\cdot C)H_{1}^{N_{1}-1}H_{2}^{N_{2}-1}\cdots H_{m}^{N_{m}}+\cdots+(H_{m}\cdot C)H_{1}^{N_{1}-1}H_{2}^{N_{2}}\cdots H_{m}^{N_{m}-1}.

Since
A
is projectively normal, there exists a degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
hypersurface
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
such that
X=A\cap Z
. We divide into the cases
\Sigma\subset Z
and
\Sigma\not\subset Z
.

Suppose first that
\Sigma\subset Z
. We will prove in Theorem
4.2
that
X
does not contain
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-lines. Thus, we must have
\Sigma\not\subset A
. As homogeneous varieties in projective space are cut out by quadrics (cf
[
Ram87
]
), by the Segre embedding we can express
A
as cut out by varieties of degree
(2,\ldots,2)
in
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
.

Then, there exists a hypersurface
F
of class
2H_{1}+\cdots+2H_{m}
such that
\Sigma\not\subset F
. Intersecting the classes of
\Sigma
and
F
, we have

([\Sigma]\cdot[F])\cdot H_{1}=2(H_{1}\cdot C+\deg Q)+2(H_{2}\cdot C)+\cdots+2(H_{m}\cdot C).

Since
C\subset\Sigma\cap F
, it follows that
([\Sigma]\cdot[F])\cdot H_{1}\geq H_{1}\cdot C
, so

2(H_{1}\cdot C+\deg Q)+2(H_{2}\cdot C)+\cdots+2(H_{m}\cdot C)\geq H_{1}\cdot C

hence

(3.2)

\deg Q\geq-\frac{1}{2}(H_{1}\cdot C)-(H_{2}\cdot C)-\cdots-(H_{m}\cdot C).

Now, a proof similar to Lemma
2.8
for the surjection
3.1
yields the inequality

\deg N_{C/X}\geq\deg Q+\deg\beta(M_{H_{1}}^{\oplus D-3})\geq\deg Q-(D-3)(H_{1}\cdot C).

Thus,

\deg N_{C/X}=2g(C)-2-(K_{X}\cdot C)\geq\deg Q-(D-3)(H_{1}\cdot C),

hence,

\displaystyle 2g(C)-2\geq

\displaystyle(K_{X}\cdot C)-(D-3)(H_{1}\cdot C)+\deg Q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}+d_{i})(H_{i}\cdot C)-(D-3)(H_{1}\cdot C)+\deg Q.

and by the inequality
3.2
, we have

2g(C)-2\geq\left(a_{1}+d_{1}-D+2+\frac{1}{2}\right)(H_{1}\cdot C)+\sum_{i=2}^{m}(a_{i}+d_{i}-1)(H_{i}\cdot C),

and since, by hypothesis,
d_{i}\geq D-a_{i}-2
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and
D\geq 4
, we get

2g(C)-2\geq\frac{1}{2}(H_{1}\cdot C)+\sum_{i=2}^{m}(H_{i}\cdot C).

Suppose now that
\Sigma\not\subset Z
. We now intersect
\Sigma
and
Z
to get

([\Sigma]\cdot[Z])\cdot H_{1}=d_{1}(H_{1}\cdot C+\deg Q)+d_{2}(H_{2}\cdot C)+\cdots+d_{m}(H_{m}\cdot C).

As
C\subset\Sigma\cap Z
, we have
([\Sigma]\cdot[Z])\cdot H_{1}\geq H_{1}\cdot C
, thus

d_{1}(H_{1}\cdot C+\deg Q)+d_{2}(H_{2}\cdot C)+\cdots+d_{m}(H_{m}\cdot C)\geq H_{1}\cdot C,

so

(3.3)

\deg Q\geq-\left(\frac{1}{d_{1}}-1\right)(H_{1}\cdot C)-\frac{d_{2}}{d_{1}}(H_{2}\cdot C)-\cdots-\frac{d_{m}}{d_{1}}(H_{m}\cdot C).

And as before, we have

2g(C)-2\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}(a_{i}+d_{i})(H_{i}\cdot C)-(D-3)(H_{1}\cdot C)+\deg Q,

so by the inequality
3.3
, we get

2g(C)-2\geq\left(a_{1}+d_{1}-D+2+\frac{1}{d_{1}}\right)(H_{1}\cdot C)+\sum_{i=2}^{m}\left(a_{i}+d_{i}-\frac{d_{i}}{d_{1}}\right)(H_{i}\cdot C).

Therefore, it suffices to have
a_{1}+d_{1}\geq D-2
and
a_{i}+d_{i}-\frac{d_{i}}{d_{1}}>\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>0
and
2\leq i\leq m
. The first inequality is true by hypothesis. We now check the second inequality.

First, we observe that
a_{i}\leq-2
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Indeed, if
a_{i}\geq-1
, by Lemma
3.3
, the dimension of the space of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-lines in
A
would be
\dim A-a_{i}-3\leq\dim A-2
, but then the lines could not cover
A
, which is not the case for a homogeneous variety. And since
d_{i}\geq D-a_{i}-2
, this implies that
d_{i}\geq 4
for all
1\leq i\leq m
.

On the other hand, an
n
-dimensional projective manifold
Y
with an ample divisor
H
satisfies
K_{Y}\geq-(n+1)H
, with equality when
X
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
(cf
[
KO73
]
.) Thus, looking at each projection
\pi_{i}:A\to\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
, we obtain that
a_{i}\geq-(D+1)
for each
1\leq i\leq m
.

Now, rearrange the inequality we want to prove,
a_{i}+d_{i}-\frac{d_{i}}{d_{1}}>\epsilon
, as

d_{i}>(\epsilon-a_{i})\frac{d_{1}}{d_{1}-1}.

Since
d_{1}\geq 4
, we have
\frac{d_{1}}{d_{1}-1}\leq\frac{4}{3}
, and as
d_{i}\geq D-a_{i}-2
, it suffices to show that

D-2-a_{i}>(\epsilon-a_{i})\frac{4}{3},

or equivalently,

D-2\geq-\frac{a_{i}}{3}+\epsilon^{\prime},

for
\epsilon^{\prime}=4\epsilon/3
.

Now, as
a_{i}\geq-(D+1)
, we have
-\frac{a_{i}}{3}+\epsilon^{\prime}\leq\frac{D+1}{3}+\epsilon^{\prime}
, so we only need to show that

D-2\geq\frac{D+1}{3}+\epsilon^{\prime}.

Rearranging terms, this is equivalent to

D\geq\frac{7}{2}+\frac{3\epsilon^{\prime}}{2},

which is true since
D\geq 4
.

Therefore, in all cases we get
2g(C)-2\geq\epsilon\sum_{i=1}^{m}(H_{i}\cdot C)
for some
\epsilon>0
, hence
X
is algebraically hyperbolic.
∎

4.
Lines in hypersurfaces of homogeneous varieties

In this section, we obtain the degrees for which the general hypersurface in
A
contains lines. In particular, we get a bound for when
X
is not algebraically hyperbolic. First, we collect a lemma characterizing certain Schubert classes in the Grassmannian of lines, based on Liu’s work
[
Liu22
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

(cf
[
Liu22
, Proposition 3.1]
)
Let
d\geq 2
,
m>0
, and let
X\subset G(2,N)
be a (possibly reducible) variety with class
[X]=m\cdot\sigma_{N-2,N-2-(d+1)}
in the Chow ring of the Grassmannian of lines
G(2,N)
. Then, the lines parametrized by
X
pass through a finite set of fixed points in
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
.

Proof.

Consider the dual Grassmannian
G(N-2,N)
. The class of the dual variety to
[X]=m\cdot\sigma_{N-2,N-2-(d+1)}
is
[X^{*}]=m\cdot\sigma_{2^{N-2-(d+1)},1^{d+1}}
.

By Pieri’s formula
[
EH16
, Proposition 4.9]
,
[X^{*}]\cdot\sigma_{2}=0
. Since
\sigma_{2}
parametrizes
\mathbb{P}^{N-3}
s through a point in
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
, it follows that the general point of
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
is not contained in any of the
\mathbb{P}^{N-3}
s parametrized by
X^{*}
. Hence, the
\mathbb{P}^{N-3}
s parametrized by each component of
X^{*}
sweep out a subvariety
T
of dimension
N-2
of
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
. We claim that
T
is a hyperplane,
T\cong\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
. To see this, consider the universal family

\mathcal{U}=\{(x,p)\ |\ x\in X^{*},\ p\in\mathbb{P}^{N-3}\text{ parametrized by }x\}\subset X^{*}\times T.

The fibers of the projection
\mathcal{U}\to X^{*}
are
\mathbb{P}^{N-3}
, so
\dim\mathcal{U}=N-3+\dim X^{*}
. Thus, by the projection
\mathcal{U}\to T
, there is a
(\dim X^{*}-1)
-parameter family of
\mathbb{P}^{N-3}
s through each point
p
of
T
. Since
d\geq 2
, we have
\dim X^{*}-1\geq 2
, so we get a two-parameter family of
\mathbb{P}^{N-3}
s through each point
p
of
T
. Each of these
\mathbb{P}^{N-3}
s is in the tangent space
T_{p}(T)\cong\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
, thus by dimension,
T_{p}(T)=\mathbb{P}^{N-2}=T
.

Therefore, there exists one
\mathbb{P}^{N-2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
such that every
\mathbb{P}^{N-3}
parametrized by a component of
X^{*}
is contained in
\mathbb{P}^{N-2}
. Taking back duals, every line parametrized by
X
passes through a fixed point in
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
. Taking the union for all components of
X
, there is a finite set of fixed points in
\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
such that every line parametrized by
X
passes through one of these points.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A
be a homogeneous variety as in the Main Setting
1.3
. Let
X
be a general hypersurface of degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
in
A
.

•

If
d_{i}\leq\dim A-a_{i}-4
for some
1\leq i\leq m
, then
X
contains
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-lines. In particular,
X
is not algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d_{i}>\dim A-a_{i}-4
for some
1\leq i\leq m
, then
X
does not contains
\mathbb{P}^{N_{i}}
-lines.

Proof.

Set
D=\dim A
. To simplify the notation, let us assume that
d_{1}\leq D-a_{1}-4
. Then, we want to show that
X
contains
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-lines.

Since
A
is projectively normal, there is a degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
hypersurface
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
such that
X=A\cap Z
. Let
F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)\subset G(2,N_{1}+1)\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
be the Fano scheme of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-lines in
Z
, and let
F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)
be the Fano scheme of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-lines in
A
. Denote by
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)]
and
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)]
their classes in the Chow ring of
G(2,N_{1}+1)\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
. To show that
X
contains lines, we are going to prove that the intersection class
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)]\cdot[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)]
is not zero.

By the degree definition, a general
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
section of
Z
is a general degree
d_{1}
hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
. By
[
EH16
, Theorem 6.34]
, the scheme of lines in a general degree
d_{1}
hypersurface has codimension
d_{1}+1
in
G(2,N_{1}+1)
. Thus,
F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)
has codimension
d_{1}+1
in
G(2,N_{1}+1)\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
. On the other hand, by Lemma
3.3
,
\dim F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)=D-a_{1}-3
. Therefore, it suffices to prove the theorem for
D-a_{1}-3=d_{1}+1
, or equivalently,
d_{1}=D-a_{1}-4
.

The intersection of
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)]
with
H_{2}^{N_{2}}\cdots H_{m}^{N_{m}}
is the class of lines in a general
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
section of
Z
, which is a general degree
d_{1}
hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
. So, by
[
EH16
, Proposition 6.4]
, we can compute the class
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)]
:

[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)]=c_{d_{1}+1}(Sym^{d_{1}}S^{*}),

where
S
is the universal subbundle of
G(2,N_{1}+1)
.

And since
F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)
is a space of
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-lines, its class
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)]
is also generated by classes of
G(2,N_{1})
.

By the Whitney formula and the splitting principle
[
EH16
, Chapter 5]
, we can write

1+\sigma_{1}+\sigma_{1,1}=c(S^{*})=(1+\alpha)(1+\beta),

for
\alpha+\beta=\sigma_{1}
and
\alpha\beta=\sigma_{1,1}
. Thus,

c(Sym^{d_{1}}S^{*})=(1+d_{1}\alpha)[1+(d_{1}-1)\alpha+\beta]\cdots[1+\alpha+(d_{1}-1)\beta](1+d_{1}\beta).

We are interested in the top Chern class

c_{d_{1}+1}(Sym^{d_{1}}S^{*})=(d_{1}\alpha)[(d_{1}-1)\alpha+\beta]\cdots[\alpha+(d_{1}-1)\beta](d_{1}\beta).

Suppose that
d_{1}
is even (the case when
d_{1}
is odd is similar), and rearrange the expression above as

\displaystyle[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)]=c_{d_{1}+1}(Sym^{d_{1}}S^{*})

\displaystyle=(d_{1}\alpha)(d_{1}\beta)[(d_{1}-1)\alpha+\beta][\alpha+(d_{1}-1)\beta]\cdots[i\alpha+(d_{1}-i)\beta][(d_{1}-i)\alpha+i\beta]\cdots\left(\frac{d_{1}}{2}\alpha+\frac{d_{1}}{2}\beta\right)

\displaystyle=d_{1}^{2}(\alpha\beta)[(d_{1}-1)(\alpha+\beta)^{2}+(d_{1}-2)^{2}(\alpha\beta)]\cdots[i(d_{1}-i)(\alpha+\beta)^{2}+(d_{1}-2i)^{2}(\alpha\beta)]\cdots\frac{d_{1}}{2}(\alpha+\beta)

\displaystyle=d_{1}^{2}\sigma_{1,1}[(d_{1}-1)\sigma_{1}^{2}+(d_{1}-2)^{2}\sigma_{1,1}]\cdots[i(d_{1}-i)\sigma_{1}^{2}+(d_{1}-2i)^{2}\sigma_{1,1}]\cdots\frac{d_{1}}{2}\sigma_{1}.

Notice that, after expanding terms, all coefficients are non-negative and the term
\sigma_{1,1}\sigma_{1}^{d_{1}-1}
appears. Recall that these Schubert classes multiply by the rule:
\sigma_{1}\cdot\sigma_{k,l}=\sigma_{k+1,l}+\sigma_{k,l+1}
and
\sigma_{1,1}\cdot\sigma_{k,l}=\sigma_{k+1,l+1}
. Thus, we can see that in the expansion of
\sigma_{1}^{d_{1}-1}
all Schubert classes
\sigma_{i,j}
with
i+j=d_{1}-1
appear with positive coefficients. It follows that in the expansion of
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)]
all classes
\sigma_{i,j}
with
i+j=d_{1}+1
and
i,j\geq 1
appear with positive coefficients, that is, the only degree
d_{1}+1
class not in the expansion of
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)]
is
\sigma_{d_{1}+1,0}
. Since the complementary dimension class of
\sigma_{d_{1}+1,0}
in
G(2,N+1)
is
\sigma_{N-1,N-1-(d_{1}+1)}
, it suffices to show that
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)]
is not a multiple of
\sigma_{N-1,N-1-(d_{1}+1)}
. But, by Lemma
4.1
, if
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)]
is a multiple of
\sigma_{N-1,N-1-(d_{1}+1)}
, then the lines of a general
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
section of
A
pass through a finite set of points, what cannot happen since
A
is homogeneous. Therefore, the product
[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)]\cdot[F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)]
is not zero, hence there are
\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}
-lines contained in
X
.

On the other hand, if
d_{i}>D-a_{i}-4
for some
1\leq i\leq m
, then the dimension of
F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)
is less than the codimension of
F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)
in
G(2,N_{i}+1)
. Since
X
is general,
F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)
is general and therefore the intersection
F_{1}^{N_{1}}(A)\cap F_{1}^{N_{1}}(Z)
is empty.
∎

By Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
4.2
, we have our main result.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
A
be a homogeneous variety as in the Main Setting
1.3
.

•

If
\dim A\geq 4
and
d_{i}\geq\dim A-a_{i}-2
for all
1\leq i\leq m
, then a very general hypersurface
X
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots d_{m})
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d_{i}\leq\dim A-a_{i}-4
for some
1\leq i\leq m
, then a general hypersurface
X
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots d_{m})
contains lines. In particular,
X
is not algebraically hyperbolic.

This leaves open the case
d_{i}=\dim A-a_{i}-3
for some
1\leq i\leq m
. When
\dim A\leq 4
, there are examples when this bound fails to imply algebraic hyperbolicity:

Example 4.4
.

(cf
[
Yeo22
, Lemma 4.2]
)
A very general surface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree
(d_{1},d_{2})
with
d_{1}=4
or
d_{2}=4
contains an elliptic curve. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

The same proof also works for
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
:

Example 4.5
.

A very general surface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
(d_{1},d_{2},d_{3})
with
d_{1}=4
contains an elliptic curve. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

The bound
d_{i}\geq\dim A-a_{i}-3
for all
i
also fails to imply algebraic hyperbolicity in the cases
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
as proved in
[
CR23
]
. For
A=\mathbb{P}^{4}
, the case of sextic threefolds remains open.

However, when
\dim A\geq 5
, it does imply algebraic hyperbolicity in the cases
A=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
A=\mathbb{P}^{m}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
as proved in
[
Yeo22
]
. We conjecture the same holds for homogenous varieties.

Conjecture 4.6
.

If
\dim A\geq 5
and
d_{i}\geq\dim A-a_{i}-3
for all
i
, then is
X
algebraically hyperbolic.

5.
Examples

In this section, we apply Theorem
4.3
to explicit examples of homogeneous varieties. For more details on these varieties, we refer to
[
Cos18
]
. The projective normality hypothesis follows from
[
RR85
]
. The section-dominating collections of line bundles follow from Examples
2.5
and
2.6
. We remark that we can also apply the theorem to products of these varieties.

5.1.
Grassmannians

Let
A=G(k,n)=G
be the Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional subspaces in an
n
-dimensional vector space. Consider
G
embedded in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
via the Plücker embedding
p:G\to\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Let
H
be the hyperplane section in
G
corresponding to
\mathcal{O}_{G}(1)=p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(1)
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the line bundle
dH
for some degree
d>0
. The dimension of
G
is
\dim G=k(n-k)
. The canonical bundle of
G
is
\omega_{G}=\mathcal{O}_{G}(-n)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

(Grassmannians)

•

If
\dim G=k(n-k)\geq 4
and
d\geq k(n-k)+n-2
, then a very general degree
d
hypersurface
X\subset G(k,n)
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d\leq k(n-k)+n-4
, then a general degree
d
hypersurface
X
contains a line. In particular,
X
is not algebraically hyperbolic.

5.2.
Products of Grassmannians

Let
A
be the product of Grassmannians
A=\prod_{i=1}^{m}G(k_{i},n_{i})
. We can see
A\subset\mathbb{P}^{N_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{P}^{N_{m}}
via the Plücker embedding for each factor. Let
H_{i}
be the pullback of the hyperplane section via each projection
A\to G(k_{i},n_{i})
. For
d_{i}\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{E}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}d_{i}H_{i}
. The dimension of
A
is
\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}(n_{i}-k_{i})
. The canonical divisor of
A
is
K_{A}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(-n_{i})H_{i}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

(Products of Grassmannians)

•

If
\dim A=\sum_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}(n_{i}-k_{i})\geq 4
and
d_{i}\geq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}(n_{j}-k_{j})\right)+n_{i}-2
, for all
1\leq i\leq m
, then a very general hypersurface of
\prod_{i=1}^{m}G(k_{i},n_{i})
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d_{i}\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{m}k_{j}(n_{j}-k_{j})\right)+n_{i}-4
for some
1\leq i\leq m
, then a general hypersurface of degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
of
\prod_{i=1}^{m}G(k_{i},n_{i})
contains a line. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

5.3.
Orthogonal Grassmannians

Let
A
be the orthogonal Grassmannian
OG(k,n)
. Similarly, let
H
be the hyperplane section, and
\mathcal{E}=dH
for
d>0
. We have
\dim OG(k,n)=\frac{k(2n-3k-1)}{2}
and
K_{OG}=(-n+3k-1)H
.

Theorem 5.3
.

(Orthogonal Grassmannians)

•

If
\dim OG(k,n)=\frac{k(2n-3k-1)}{2}\geq 4
and
d\geq\frac{k(2n-3k-1)}{2}+n-3k-1
, then a very general hypersurface of
OG(k,n)
of degree
d
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d\leq\frac{k(2n-3k-1)}{2}+n-3k-3
, then a general hypersurface of degree
d
of
OG(k,n)
contains a line. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

5.4.
Symplectic Grassmannians

Let
A=SG(k,n)
be a symplectic Grassmannian. Let
H
by the hyperplane section, and
\mathcal{E}=dH
for
d>0
. In this case,
\dim SG(k,n)=\frac{k(2n-3k+1)}{2}
and
K_{SG}=(-n+3k-2)H
.

Theorem 5.4
.

(Symplectic Grassmannians)

•

If
\dim A=\frac{k(2n-3k+1)}{2}\geq 4
and
d\geq\frac{k(2n-3k+1)}{2}+n+3k
, then a very general degree
d
hypersurface of
SG(k,n)
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d\leq\frac{k(2n-3k+1)}{2}+n+3k-2
, then a general degree
d
hypersurface of
SG(k,n)
contains a line. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

5.5.
Flag varieties

Let
A=F(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m};n)
be a flag variety. The projections

F(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m};n)\to G(k_{1},n)\times\cdots\times G(k_{m},n)

give, via pull-back, the hyperplane classes
H_{1},\ldots,H_{m}
that generate the Picard group of
A
. The dimension of
A
is
D=\sum_{i=0}^{m}k_{i+1}(k_{i+1}-k_{i})
. The canonical divisor of
A
is
K_{A}=-\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(k_{i+2}-k_{i})H_{i+1}=-k_{2}H_{1}-(k_{3}-k_{1})H_{2}-\cdots-(n-k_{m-1})H_{m}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

(Flag varieties)

•

If
\dim A=\sum_{i=0}^{m}k_{i+1}(k_{i+1}-k_{i})\geq 4
and
d_{i}\geq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}k_{j+1}(k_{j+1}-k_{j})\right)+k_{i+1}-k_{i-1}-2
for all
1\leq i\leq m
, then a very general hypersurface of
F(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m};n)
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
is algebraically hyperbolic.

•

If
d_{i}\leq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}k_{j+1}(k_{j+1}-k_{j})\right)+k_{i+1}-k_{i-1}-4
for some
i
, then a general hypersurface of
F(k_{1},\ldots,k_{m};n)
of degree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{m})
contains a line. In particular, it is not algebraically hyperbolic.

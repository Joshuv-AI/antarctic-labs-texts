On the discrete analogues of Appell function F 1 F_{1}
Ravi Dwivedi, Vivek Sahai

Abstract
In the present paper, two discrete forms of Appell function F 1 F_{1} are introduced and studied. We examine the first discrete form in detail and give the results directly for the second. We study their regions of convergence, differential properties, integral representations, recursion relations, finite and infinite sums. The results and identities obtained in the paper are believed to be new and may find applications in various branches of science. AMS Subject Classification: 33C65. Keywords: Appell functions, discrete hypergeometric functions.

Basic definitions and preliminaries

Throughout the paper,
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{C}
will denote the set of natural numbers and complex numbers respectively. For a complex number
w
, the gamma function is defined in terms of Euler integral as
[
17
]

\displaystyle\Gamma(w)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-u}\,u^{w-1}\,du,\quad\Re(w)>0,

(2.1)

and for
v,w\in\mathbb{C}
, the beta function is defined by

\displaystyle\beta(v,w)=\int_{0}^{1}u^{v-1}\,(1-u)^{w-1}\,du,\quad\Re(v),\Re(w)>0.

(2.2)

These two basic special functions are related by the fundamental relation

\displaystyle\beta(v,w)=\frac{\Gamma(v)\,\Gamma(w)}{\Gamma(v+w)}.

(2.3)

The next milestone in the theory of special functions was the discovery of Gauss hypergeometric function. For
u,v,w\in\mathbb{C}
and
w\neq 0,-1,-2,\dots
, the Gauss function is defined by

\displaystyle{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u,v\\
w\end{array};z\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(u)_{k}\,(v)_{k}}{(w)_{k}}\,\frac{z^{k}}{k!},\quad|z|<1,

where
(u)_{k}
is the Pochhammer symbol given by

(u)_{k}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }k=0,\\
u\,(u+1)\cdots(u+k-1),&\text{ if }k\geq 1.\end{cases}

(2.6)

There are several important identities involving Pochhammer symbol and we list some of them here, which are needed in rest of the paper.

\displaystyle(u)_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(u+k)}{\Gamma(u)};

(2.7)

\displaystyle(u)_{k+l}

\displaystyle=(u)_{k}\,(u+k)_{l}=(u)_{l}\,(u+l)_{k};

(2.8)

\displaystyle(u)_{k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k}\,(1-u-k)_{k};

(2.9)

\displaystyle(-k)_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{l}\,k!}{(k-l)!};

(2.10)

\displaystyle(u)_{k-l}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{l}\,(u)_{k}}{(1-u-k)_{l}},\quad 0\leq l\leq k.

(2.11)

The Gauss hypergeometric function (
2
) has the following natural generalization called generalized hypergeometric function, defined by

\displaystyle{}_{p}F_{q}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1},\dots,u_{p}\\
v_{1},\dots,v_{q}\end{array};z\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(u_{1})_{k}\cdots(u_{p})_{k}}{(v_{1})_{k}\cdots(v_{q})_{k}}\,\frac{z^{k}}{k!},\quad|z|<1.

Also, it has two variable generalizations, known as Appell functions, given by

\displaystyle F_{1}(u,v,v^{\prime};w;x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(u)_{k+l}\,(v)_{k}\,(v^{\prime})_{l}}{(w)_{k+l}}\,\frac{x^{k}\,y^{l}}{k!\,l!},\quad|x|,\,|y|<1;

(2.14)

\displaystyle F_{2}(u,v,v^{\prime};w,w^{\prime};x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(u)_{k+l}\,(v)_{k}\,(v^{\prime})_{l}}{(w)_{k}\,(w^{\prime})_{l}}\,\frac{x^{k}\,y^{l}}{k!\,l!},\quad|x|+|y|<1;

(2.15)

\displaystyle F_{3}(u,u^{\prime},v,v^{\prime};w;x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(u)_{k}\,(u^{\prime})_{l}\,(v)_{k}\,(v^{\prime})_{l}}{(w)_{k+l}}\,\frac{x^{k}\,y^{l}}{k!\,l!},\quad|x|,\,|y|<1;

(2.16)

\displaystyle F_{4}(u,v;w,w^{\prime};x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(u)_{k+l}\,(v)_{k+l}}{(w)_{k}\,{(w^{\prime})}_{l}}\,\frac{x^{k}\,y^{l}}{k!\,l!},\quad\sqrt{|x|}+\sqrt{|y|}<1.

(2.17)

Furthermore, the following generalized form of two variable function is known as the Kampé de Fériet function:

\displaystyle F_{{k_{2}}:{l_{2}};{l^{\prime}_{2}}}^{{k_{1}}:{l_{1}};{l^{\prime}_{1}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}A:&B;&C\\
D:&E;&F\end{array};x,\ y\right)=\displaystyle\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\ \frac{\prod_{i=1}^{k_{1}}(a_{i})_{k+l}\,\prod_{i=1}^{l_{1}}(b_{i})_{k}\,\prod_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}_{1}}(c_{i})_{l}}{\prod_{i=1}^{k_{2}}(d_{i})_{k+l}\,\prod_{i=1}^{l_{2}}(e_{i})_{k}\,\prod_{i=1}^{l^{\prime}_{2}}(f_{i})_{l}}\ \frac{x^{k}\,y^{l}}{k!\,l!},

where
A
denotes the sequence of complex numbers
a_{1}
, …,
a_{k_{1}}
, etc. and
d_{i},e_{i},f_{i}\neq 0,-1,\dots
. The Kampé de Fériet function converges
[
18
]
, for

\displaystyle(i)\

\displaystyle k_{1}+l_{1}<k_{2}+l_{2}+1,\,k_{1}+l_{1}^{\prime}<k_{2}+l_{2}^{\prime}+1,\quad|x|<\infty,\,|y|<\infty,\text{ or }

\displaystyle(ii)\

\displaystyle k_{1}+l_{1}=k_{2}+l_{2}+1,\,k_{1}+l_{1}^{\prime}=k_{2}+l_{2}^{\prime}+1,\text{ and }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|x|^{\frac{1}{k_{1}-k_{2}}}+|y|^{\frac{1}{k_{1}-k_{2}}}<1,\text{ if }k_{1}>k_{2},\\
|x|<1,|y|<1,\text{ if }k_{1}\leq k_{2}.\end{cases}

(2.20)

Recently, the discrete analogue of special functions is being studied and is receiving much attention. The generalized discrete hypergeometric function is defined by

3
Discrete Appell function
\mathcal{F}_{1}^{(1)}

In this section, we introduce two distinct discrete forms of Appell function
F_{1}
. We elaborate one of the forms in detail and obtain its regions of convergence, special cases, difference equations, integral representations and its limiting cases.

Let
a
,
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
,
c
,
t
,
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
be complex numbers such that
\Re(c)\neq 0,-1,\dots
and let
k,k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
. We define two discrete versions of Appell hypergeometric function
{F}_{1}
, denoted by
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{1}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n};

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t,k,x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{(m+n)k}\,(-t)_{(m+n)k}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n};

(3.2)

Here, and in subsequent sections, we explore the discrete Appell function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
and later in the last section, we list results for the discrete Appell function
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{1}
.

For different values of
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, the discrete Appell function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
reduces into classical functions. In particular, for
k_{1}=0=k_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},0,0,x,y)

\displaystyle=F_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;x,y).

(3.3)

For
k_{1}=1,k_{2}=0
, it gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},1,0,x,y)

\displaystyle=F_{{1}:{0};{0}}^{{1}:{2};{1}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a:&b_{1},-t_{1};&b_{2}\\
c:&-;&-\end{array};-x,\ y\right).

For
k_{1}=0,k_{2}=1
, the discrete function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
reduces into

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},0,1,x,y)

\displaystyle=F_{{1}:{0};{0}}^{{1}:1;{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a:&b_{1};&b_{2},-t_{2}\\
c:&-;&-\end{array};x,\ -y\right).

Similarly, for
k_{1}=k_{2}=1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},1,1,x,y)=F_{{1}:{0};{0}}^{{1}:{2};{2}}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}a:&b_{1},-t_{1};&b_{2},-t_{2}\\
c:&-;&-\end{array};-x,\ -y\right).

First, we examine the convergence of discrete Appell function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
. Let
\mathcal{A}_{m,n}x^{m}\,y^{n}
be the general term of the discrete function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{m,n}x^{m}\,y^{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}\right|

\displaystyle<\left|\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\,\Gamma(b_{1})\,\Gamma(b_{2})\,\Gamma(-t_{1})\,\Gamma(-t_{2})}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\times\left|\frac{\Gamma(a+m+n)\,\Gamma(b_{1}+m)\,\Gamma(b_{2}+n)\,\Gamma(-t_{1}+mk_{1})\,\Gamma(-t_{2}+nk_{2})}{\Gamma(c+m+n)\,\Gamma(m+1)\,\Gamma(n+1)}\right|\,|x|^{m}\,|y|^{n}.

(3.10)

Using the Stirling formula

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\Gamma(\lambda+n)=\sqrt{(}2\pi)\,n^{\lambda+n-\frac{1}{2}}\,e^{-n},

(3.11)

for large values of
m
and
n
, we write (
3.10
) as

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{m,n}x^{m}\,y^{n}\right|

\displaystyle<\left|\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\,\Gamma(b_{1})\,\Gamma(b_{2})\,\Gamma(-t_{1})\,\Gamma(-t_{2})}\right|

\displaystyle\quad\times\left|2\pi\,(m+n)^{a-c}\,m^{b_{1}-1}\,n^{b_{2}-1}\,(mk_{1})^{mk_{1}-t_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}\,(nk_{2})^{nk_{2}-t_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-(mk_{1}+nk_{2})}\right|\,|x|^{m}\,|y|^{n}.

(3.12)

Let
N>\left|\frac{2\pi\,\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\,\Gamma(b_{1})\,\Gamma(b_{2})\,\Gamma(-t_{1})\,\Gamma(-t_{2})}\right|
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{A}_{m,n}x^{m}\,y^{n}\right|<\frac{N\,(mk_{1})^{mk_{1}-t_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}\,(nk_{2})^{nk_{2}-t_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}}{(m+n)^{c-a}\,m^{1-b_{1}}\,n^{1-b_{2}}\,e^{(mk_{1}+nk_{2})}}\,|x|^{m}\,|y|^{n}.

(3.13)

For
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N},t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and
|x|<1,|y|<1
,
\left|\mathcal{A}_{m,n}x^{m}\,y^{n}\right|\to 0
as
m,n\to\infty
. Hence the discrete function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
converges absolutely.

Next, we discuss the difference equation obeyed by the discrete function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
. Let
\Delta_{t}
be the forwarding operator such that
\Delta_{t}f(t)=f(t+1)-f(t)
and let
\rho_{t}
be the shifting operator defined as
\rho_{t}\,f(t)=f(t-1)
. Then, the discrete analogue of
\theta=t\,\frac{d}{dt}
is denoted by
\Theta_{t}:=t\,\rho_{t}\,\Delta_{t}
. Using the difference operator
\Theta_{t}
, following result can be easily obtained

\displaystyle\Theta_{t}\,((-1)^{nk}\,(-t)_{nk})

\displaystyle=n\,k\,(-1)^{nk}\,(-t)_{nk}.

(3.14)

Now

\displaystyle\Theta_{t_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+c-1\right)\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}\,mk_{1}\,(c+m+n-1)

\displaystyle=k_{1}\,\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{(a)_{m+n+1}\,(b_{1})_{m+1}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{(m+1)k_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{(m+1)k_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m+1}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle=k_{1}\,\sum_{m,n\geq 0}(a+m+n)\,(b_{1}+m)\,(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,x\,\rho_{t_{1}}^{k_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle=k_{1}\,(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,x\,\rho_{t_{1}}^{k_{1}}\,\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+a\right)\,\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+b_{1}\right)\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}.

(3.15)

Thus, we arrive at

\displaystyle\left[\Theta_{t_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+c-1\right)\right]\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left[k_{1}\,(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,x\,\rho_{t_{1}}^{k_{1}}\,\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+a\right)\,\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+b_{1}\right)\right]\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}.

(3.16)

This is the first difference equation satisfied by
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
. Similarly, one can get the second difference equation as

\displaystyle\left[\Theta_{t_{2}}\,\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+c-1\right)\right]\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left[k_{2}\,(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,y\,\rho_{t_{2}}^{k_{2}}\,\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+a\right)\,\left(\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+b_{2}\right)\right]\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}.

(3.17)

From Equation (
3.16
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+a\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+c-1\right)}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}=\frac{\Theta_{t_{1}}}{\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+b_{1}\right)\,k_{1}\,(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,x\,\rho_{t_{1}}^{k_{1}}}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}.

(3.18)

Similarly, Equation (
3.17
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+a\right)}{\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+c-1\right)}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}=\frac{\Theta_{t_{2}}}{\left(\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+b_{2}\right)\,k_{2}\,(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,y\,\rho_{t_{2}}^{k_{2}}}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}.

(3.19)

Equations (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) together give

\displaystyle\frac{\Theta_{t_{1}}}{\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+b_{1}\right)\,k_{1}\,(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,x\,\rho_{t_{1}}^{k_{1}}}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}=\frac{\Theta_{t_{2}}}{\left(\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+b_{2}\right)\,k_{2}\,(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,y\,\rho_{t_{2}}^{k_{2}}}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}

(3.20)

or

\displaystyle\left[k_{2}\,(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,y\,\rho_{t_{2}}^{k_{2}}\,\Theta_{t_{1}}\left(\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Theta_{t_{2}}+b_{2}\right)-k_{1}\,(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,x\,\rho_{t_{1}}^{k_{1}}\,\Theta_{t_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Theta_{t_{1}}+b_{1}\right)\right]\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}=0.

(3.21)

This is the third difference equation obeyed by the discrete function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
. Note that these difference equations generalize the set of differential equations for the classical Appell function
F_{1}
as in the paper
[

]

\displaystyle=\Gamma\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c\\
a,c-a\end{array}\right)\int_{0}^{1}u^{a-1}(1-u)^{c-a-1}{}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{0}(b_{1};-;t_{1},k_{1},ux)\,{}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{0}(b_{2};-;t_{2},k_{2},uy)du.

4
Differential and difference formulae

Let
\Delta_{t}=f(t+1)-f(t)
be the difference operator and let
\theta=x\frac{\partial}{\partial x},\phi=y\frac{\partial}{\partial y}
be the differential operators. Then, we have the following difference and differential formulae satisfied by discrete Appell function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
:

\displaystyle(\Delta_{t_{1}})^{r}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},1,k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(a)_{r}\,(b_{1})_{r}\,x^{r}}{(c)_{r}}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1}+r,b_{2};c+r;t_{1},t_{2},1,k_{2},x,y);

(4.1)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{t_{2}})^{r}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},1,x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(a)_{r}\,(b_{2})_{r}\,y^{r}}{(c)_{r}}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1},b_{2}+r;c+r;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},1,x,y);

(4.2)

\displaystyle(\theta)^{r}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{rk_{1}}\,(a)_{r}\,(b_{1})_{r}\,(-t_{1})_{rk_{1}}\,x^{r}}{(c)_{r}}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1}+r,b_{2};c+r;t_{1}-rk_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y);

(4.3)

\displaystyle(\phi)^{r}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{rk_{2}}\,(a)_{r}\,(b_{2})_{r}\,(-t_{2})_{rk_{2}}\,y^{r}}{(c)_{r}}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1},b_{2}+r;c+r;t_{1},t_{2}-rk_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y).

(4.4)

The proofs of these differential formulae are straightforward. We give here the proof of (
4.1
) only and to accomplish this we use the induction method. Using the identity
\Delta_{t_{1}}[(-1)^{m}\,(-t_{1})_{m}]=m\,(-1)^{m-1}\,(-t_{1})_{m-1}
, we get

\displaystyle(\Delta_{t_{1}})\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},1,k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{m-1}\,m\,(-t_{1})_{m-1}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{a\,b_{1}\,x}{c}\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{(a+1)_{m+n}\,(b_{1}+1)_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{m}\,(-t_{1})_{m}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c+1)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

(4.5)

Thus the formula is true for
r=1
. Assuming the formula to be valid for
r=p
,
we have

\displaystyle(\Delta_{t_{1}})^{p+1}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},1,k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{t_{1}}[(\Delta_{t_{1}})^{p}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},1,k_{2},x,y)]

\displaystyle=\frac{(a)_{p}\,(b_{1})_{p}\,x^{p}}{(c)_{p}}\Delta_{t_{1}}[\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+p,b_{1}+p,b_{2};c+p;t_{1},t_{2},1,k_{2},x,y)]

(4.6)

5

In this section, we establish some finite and infinite summation formulas in terms of discrete Appell function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
. We list them in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

The following summation formulas hold:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1}+r,b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{r}{r\choose s}\frac{(a)_{s}\,(-1)^{sk_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{sk_{1}}}{(c)_{s}}\,x^{s}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+s,b_{1}+s,b_{2};c+s;t_{1}-sk_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y);

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2}+r;c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{r}{r\choose s}\frac{(a)_{s}\,(-1)^{sk_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{sk_{2}}}{(c)_{s}}\,y^{s}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+s,b_{1},b_{2}+s;c+s;t_{1},t_{2}-sk_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y);

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{r}}{r!}\,z^{r}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{-a}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}\left(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},\frac{x}{1-z},\frac{y}{1-z}\right);

(5.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(b_{1})_{r}}{r!}\,z^{r}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1}+r,b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{-b_{1}}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}\left(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},\frac{x}{1-z},y\right);

(5.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(b_{2})_{r}}{r!}\,z^{r}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2}+r;c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{-b_{2}}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}\left(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,\frac{y}{1-z}\right).

(5.5)

Proof.

The Leibnitz rule, for differentiation of product of functions, allows us to write

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{r}\left[x^{b_{1}+r-1}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{r}{r\choose s}\left[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{r-s}\,x^{b_{1}+r-1}\right]\,\left[\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)^{s}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{r}{r\choose s}(-1)^{r-s}\,(1-b_{1}+s)_{r-s}\,x^{b_{1}+s-1}\,\frac{(a)_{s}\,(b_{1})_{s}\,(-1)^{sk_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{sk_{1}}}{(c)_{s}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+s,b_{1}+s,b_{2};c+s;t_{1}-sk_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{r}{r\choose s}\,(-1)^{r-s}\frac{(-1)^{s}\,(1-b_{1}-r)_{r}}{(b_{1})_{s}}\,x^{b_{1}+s-1}\,\frac{(a)_{s}\,(b_{1})_{s}\,(-1)^{sk_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{sk_{1}}}{(c)_{s}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+s,b_{1}+s,b_{2};c+s;t_{1}-sk_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=(b_{1})_{r}\,\sum_{s=0}^{r}{r\choose s}\,x^{b_{1}+s-1}\,\frac{(a)_{s}\,(-1)^{sk_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{sk_{1}}}{(c)_{s}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+s,b_{1}+s,b_{2};c+s;t_{1}-sk_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y).

(5.6)

Taking into account Equation (
4.7
), we have

\displaystyle x^{b_{1}-1}\,(b_{1})_{r}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1}+r,b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=(b_{1})_{r}\,\sum_{s=0}^{r}{r\choose s}\,x^{b_{1}+s-1}\,\frac{(a)_{s}\,(-1)^{sk_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{sk_{1}}}{(c)_{s}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+s,b_{1}+s,b_{2};c+s;t_{1}-sk_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y).

(5.7)

This implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1}+r,b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{r}{r\choose s}\frac{(a)_{s}\,(-1)^{sk_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{sk_{1}}}{(c)_{s}}\,x^{s}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+s,b_{1}+s,b_{2};c+s;t_{1}-sk_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y).

(5.8)

This completes the proof of (
5.1

5.2

To prove (
5.3

\displaystyle(1-z)^{-a}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}\left(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},\frac{x}{1-z},\frac{y}{1-z}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}(1-z)^{-(a+m+n)}\,\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}.

(5.9)

Using the binomial theorem
(1-z)^{-(a+m+n)}=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a+m+n)_{r}}{r!}\,z^{r}
and the identity
(a)_{m+n+r}=(a)_{m+n}\,(a+m+n)_{r}=(a)_{r}\,(a+r)_{m+n}
, we have

\displaystyle(1-z)^{-a}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}\left(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},\frac{x}{1-z},\frac{y}{1-z}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\,\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{r}}{r!}\,z^{r}\frac{(a+r)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{r}}{r!}\,z^{r}\,\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y).

(5.10)

Hence, we arrived at the equation (
5.3
). Remaining results can be proved in a similar manner.

6
Recursion Formulae

Theorem 6.1
.

The discrete Appell function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
satisfies the following recursion formulas:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+s,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,b_{1}\,x}{c}\sum_{r=1}^{s}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1}+1,b_{2};c+1;t_{1}-k_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,b_{2}\,y}{c}\sum_{r=1}^{s}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1},b_{2}+1;c+1;t_{1},t_{2}-k_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y);

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a-s,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,b_{1}\,x}{c}\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a-r,b_{1}+1,b_{2};c+1;t_{1}-k_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,b_{2}\,y}{c}\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a-r,b_{1},b_{2}+1;c+1;t_{1},t_{2}-k_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y);

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1}+s,b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,a\,x}{c}\sum_{r=1}^{s}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+1,b_{1}+r,b_{2};c+1;t_{1}-k_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y);

(6.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1}-s,b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,a\,x}{c}\sum_{r=0}^{s-1}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+1,b_{1}-r,b_{2};c+1;t_{1}-k_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y);

(6.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c-s;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,a\,b_{1}\,x\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+1,b_{1}+1,b_{2};c+2-r;t_{1}-k_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)}{(c-r)\,(c-r+1)}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,a\,b_{2}\,y\sum_{r=1}^{s}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+1,b_{1},b_{2}+1;c+2-r;t_{1},t_{2}-k_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)}{(c-r)\,(c-r+1)}.

(6.5)

Proof.

We first prove the formula (
6.1
) for
s=1
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,b_{1}\,x}{c}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+1,b_{1}+1,b_{2};c+1;t_{1}-k_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,b_{2}\,y}{c}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+1,b_{1},b_{2}+1;c+1;t_{1},t_{2}-k_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\,\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,b_{1}\,x}{c}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\,\frac{(a+1)_{m+n}\,(b_{1}+1)_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{1}+k_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c+1)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,b_{2}\,y}{c}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\,\frac{(a+1)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2}+1)_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{2}+k_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c+1)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\,\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{m}{a}\,\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{n}{a}\,\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{a+m+n}{a}\,\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t_{1})_{mk_{1}}\,(-t_{2})_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+1,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y).

(6.6)

So the formula is true for
s=1
. Assuming the validity of (
6.1
) for
s=p
, we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,b_{1}\,x}{c}\sum_{r=1}^{p+1}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1}+1,b_{2};c+1;t_{1}-k_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,b_{2}\,y}{c}\sum_{r=1}^{p+1}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+r,b_{1},b_{2}+1;c+1;t_{1},t_{2}-k_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+p,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{1}}\,(-t_{1})_{k_{1}}\,b_{1}\,x}{c}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+p+1,b_{1}+1,b_{2};c+1;t_{1}-k_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(-1)^{k_{2}}\,(-t_{2})_{k_{2}}\,b_{2}\,y}{c}\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+p+1,b_{1},b_{2}+1;c+1;t_{1},t_{2}-k_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a+p+1,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k_{1},k_{2},x,y).

(6.7)

Hence, (
6.1
) is true for

and therefore by induction is valid for all

6.4

(7.75)

(7.76)

(7.82)

8
Conclusion

We have discussed two distinct discrete forms of Appell function
F_{1}

viz.

\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{1}
in a detailed manner. Two particular cases are of special interest. These are the first discrete form
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
with

k_{1}=k_{2}=k
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t_{1},t_{2},k,x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{(m+n)k}\,(-t_{1})_{mk}\,(-t_{2})_{nk}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}

(8.1)

and with
t_{1}=t_{2}=t
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c;t,k_{1},k_{2},x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n\geq 0}\frac{(a)_{m+n}\,(b_{1})_{m}\,(b_{2})_{n}\,(-1)^{mk_{1}}\,(-1)^{nk_{2}}\,(-t)_{mk_{1}}\,(-t)_{nk_{2}}}{(c)_{m+n}\,m!\,n!}\ x^{m}\,y^{n}.

(8.2)

These two functions can also be considered as the discrete analogues of Appell function
F_{1}
. Most of the results related to these discrete functions can be deduced from the discrete function
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
. However, there is a scope for studying these functions separately. Furthermore, we have presented the discrete analogues of Humbert function
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
and
\phi_{3}
as the limiting cases of
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{1}
. So, the analytical properties of these discrete Humbert functions can be deduced by taking the limit in the respective properties of discrete Appell functions. Besides, study of the results and properties of these discrete Humbert function in comparative way will be an interesting problem.

As discussed in the paper, the particular cases of the functions
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{1}
reduce to the Appell function
F_{1}
and the Kampé de Fériet functions. Therefore the particular cases of results obtained for
\mathcal{F}^{(1)}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}^{(2)}_{1}
will lead to the results for
F_{1}
and Kampé de Fériet functions. Most of the results obtained for
F_{1}
in this way are already available in the literature but the differential recursion formulas are the new ones. Similarly, the results that can be determined for Kampé de Fériet functions are also believed to be new.

Appell has given four two variable functions
F_{1}
,
F_{2}
,
F_{3}
and
F_{4}
. It was not possible to list all the findings for discrete analogues of these four functions in a single paper and hence we decided to collect the findings of each discrete Appell function separately. In this article, we have recorded the analysis of discrete analogues of Appell function
F_{1}
and for discrete analogues of remaining three Appell functions, we have written articles separately.

Dissipative Superfluidity in a Molecular Bose-Einstein Condensate
Hongchao Li, Xie-Hang Yu, Masaya Nakagawa, Masahito Ueda

Abstract
Motivated by recent experimental realization of a Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) of dipolar molecules, we develop superfluid transport theory for a dissipative BEC to show that a weak uniform two-body loss can induce phase rigidity, leading to superfluid transport of bosons even without repulsive interparticle interactions. A generalized f f -sum rule is shown to hold for a dissipative superfluid as a consequence of weak U(1) symmetry. We also demonstrate that dissipation enhances the stability of a molecular BEC with dipolar interactions. Possible experimental signature of dissipative superfluidity are discussed.

Here we point out that the phase rigidity and superfluidity can be induced by dissipation even without interactions. For a closed system composed of free bosons, the amplitude mode is gapless, leading to the breakdown of the effective action (
7
) and the system does not show superfluidity. Therefore, repulsive interactions are necessary for superfluidity since they suppress density fluctuations and ensure a finite compressibility
[
52

. By using effective field theory, we can show that compressibility of a dissipative BEC becomes finite due to two-body loss even in the absence of interactions
[
49
]
, indicating the stability against external perturbations. The dipolar interaction does not essentially modify the above mechanism but only partially compensates the repulsive interaction, i.e.,
U_{R}\to U_{R}(1-\varepsilon_{dd})
. In the dissipative superfluidity considered here, the two-body loss suppresses density fluctuations by eliminating particles predominantly from where the density is higher than the average. This suppression of density fluctuations leads to the phase rigidity and hence superfluidity. Similar mechanisms by which dissipation induces correlations akin to effective repulsive interactions have been discussed in the context of the quantum Zeno effect
[
53
,
54
,
55
,
56
]
. We note that not all kinds of dissipation can lead to the phase rigidity. Only the dissipation such as two-body and three-body losses leads to correlations between bosons and can generate phase rigidity.

Quantum depletion.—
The above discussion only considers the quadratic terms in the effective action and is only valid for the condensate part. To further vindicate the superfluidity in a dissipative BEC, we now consider the quantum depletion and perform the Bogoliubov analysis by employing the mean-field approximation with
a_{0+}=a_{0-}=a_{0-}^{\dagger}=a_{0+}^{\dagger}\approx\sqrt{N}

a_{0}

f-sum rule.—
The
f
-sum rule plays a fundamental role in the superfluid transport theory
[
6
]
. It is a consequence of the particle-number conservation and not expected to hold in dissipative superfluids. Here we show that weak U(1) symmetry leads to a generalized
f
-sum rule in the present case. We begin with the commutation relation:

[\rho_{-\bm{k}},\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})]=2i\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}\hat{N},

(14)

where
\rho_{-\bm{k}}:=\sum_{\bm{p}}a_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}-\bm{k}}
is the density operator,
\hat{N}
is the total particle-number operator,
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}=|\bm{k}|^{2}/2m
is the kinetic energy and
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}
represents the adjoint Lindbladian
[
44
]
. Note that the right-hand side of Eq. (
14
) is independent of the strength
\gamma
of dissipation. This implies that a generalized
f
-sum rule in open quantum systems holds as in closed systems. Physically, this is because the two-body loss only leads to uniform decrease in the particle-number density and hence does not generate a current inside the system. We use this identity to show that

\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi\omega}\gamma^{L}(\bm{k},\omega,t_{0})=N(t_{0}),

(15)

where
N(t_{0})
represents the total particle number at an arbitrary time
t_{0}
, and
\gamma^{L}(\bm{k},\omega,t_{0})=\sum_{i,j}\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{|\bm{k}|^{2}}\gamma^{i,j}(\bm{k},\omega,t_{0})
is the longitudinal component of the current-current correlation function
\gamma^{i,j}(\bm{k},\omega,t_{0}):=m\langle\int dte^{i\omega t}[{j}_{\mathrm{t}}^{i}(\bm{k},t_{0}+t),{j}_{\mathrm{t}}^{j}(-\bm{k},t_{0})]\rangle

with

{\bm{j}}_{\mathrm{t}}=\frac{i}{2m}\left(\nabla a_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{r}}-a_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}\nabla a_{\bm{r}}\right)+{\bm{j}}_{d}(\bm{r})

(16)

being the total current of the system
[
49
,
50
]
. Here
{\bm{j}}_{d}(\bm{r})=\gamma\int\frac{d\bm{r}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\frac{\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}a^{\dagger}_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}a^{\dagger}_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}a_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}a_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}
represents a dissipative current. Equation (
15
) can be interpreted as a generalized

f
-sum rule in open quantum systems. The normal fluid density corresponds to the transverse part of the current-current
correlation function. The difference between
longitudinal and transverse parts of the current-current correlation function
defines the superfluid
component
[
60
]
.

The generalized
f
-sum rule (
15
) implies that the longitudinal response is determined by the total particle number at a given time and is a direct consequence of the weak U(1) symmetry of the Lindbladian (
2
) which holds even in the absence of particle-number conservation

. The weak U(1) symmetry preserves the block diagonal form of the density matrix in the particle-number basis, and hence the
f
-sum rule holds in each particle-number sector, leading to the generalized
f
-sum rule which is general and exact.

Excitation spectrum and stability of a molecular BEC.—
We now investigate the excitation spectrum of a dissipative BEC. With the mean-field and quasi-steady-state approximations, the action (
3
) is rewritten as

S=\sum_{\bm{k},\omega}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{k},\omega}^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}h_{+}^{2\times 2}&B\\
B^{\dagger}&h_{-}^{2\times 2}\end{pmatrix}\Psi_{\boldsymbol{k},\omega}

(17)

where
\Psi_{\boldsymbol{k},\omega}=(a_{\boldsymbol{k},\omega,+},a_{\boldsymbol{-k},\omega,+}^{\dagger},a_{\boldsymbol{k},\omega,-},a_{\boldsymbol{-k},\omega,-}^{\dagger})^{T}

h_{\pm}^{2\times 2}=\mp\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon_{\bm{k},U}^{\mp}-\omega&\mp(\tilde{U}_{R}-i\gamma)n\\
\mp(\tilde{U}_{R}+i\gamma)n&\varepsilon_{\bm{k},U}^{\mp}+\omega\end{pmatrix},

(18)

and
B=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&-2i\gamma n\end{pmatrix}

with
\varepsilon_{\bm{k},U}^{\mp}:=\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\tilde{U}_{R}n\mp 2i\gamma n
,
\tilde{U}_{R}(\bm{k}):=U_{R}[1-\varepsilon_{dd}(1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{\bm{k}})]
, and
\cos\theta_{\bm{k}}=\bm{k}\cdot\hat{z}/|\bm{k}|
. The Green’s functions of bosonic fields
[
64
]
are given by the inverse
of the Gaussian matrix in Eq. (
17
) (see Supplemental Material for details
[
49
]
).

The excitation spectrum can be obtained from the poles of the Green’s function determined by
\mathrm{det}\begin{pmatrix}h_{+}^{2\times 2}&B\\
B^{\dagger}&h_{-}^{2\times 2}\end{pmatrix}=0
:

\displaystyle\omega_{1,2}

\displaystyle=-2i\gamma n\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}+2\tilde{U}_{R}(\bm{k})n)-\gamma^{2}n^{2}},

(19)

\displaystyle\omega_{3,4}

\displaystyle=2i\gamma n\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\varepsilon_{\boldsymbol{k}}+2\tilde{U}_{R}(\bm{k})n)-\gamma^{2}n^{2}}.

The spectra
\omega_{1,2}
originate from the poles of the retarded Green’s function, which are also eigenfrequencies of the non-Hermitian Gross-Pitaevskii equation
[
41
]
. Similarly,
\omega_{3,4}
are complex conjugate to
\omega_{1,2}
, representing the poles of the advanced Green’s function
2
2

2

Due to the nonunitary Bogoliubov transformation, the amplitude modes have mixed with the phase modes, leading to the difference between the spectrums of the NG mode and that of the bosonic operators.
. The complex spectrum is expected to be measured from the spectral function, which is defined as

A(\bm{k},\omega)=\frac{i}{2\pi}(G^{>}(\bm{k},\omega)+G^{<}(\bm{k},\omega)),

(20)

where
G^{<}
and

represent the lesser and greater Green’s function, respectively. Concrete analytic expressions of the spectral function and other plots under different limits and directions are given in the Supplemental Material

We investigate the dynamical stability of a dissipative BEC via the complex spectra in Eq. (
19
). The system is dynamically stable if and only if both of the two retarded (advanced) spectra
\omega_{1,2\,(3,4)}
have negative (positive) imaginary parts
[
51
]
. We find that the system becomes stable when
\varepsilon_{dd}<1+\sqrt{3}\gamma/U_{R}
, and that the two-body loss enlarges the stable region. Physically, the enhanced stability due to dissipation can be understood as a consequence of the correlation induced by dissipation which plays a role similar to the repulsive interaction. A similar result for a dissipative attractive BEC has been obtained in Ref.
[
51
]
. This stability analysis sets an upper bound on the dipolar interaction strength in order for the molecular BEC to be stable.

One of the important experimental evidences of superfluidity is the formation of quantized vortices by stirring the BEC with a high angular velocity

. Here we simulate the dynamics of a non-interacting trapped BEC with two-body loss. We first prepare the ground state of free bosons in a pancake-shaped harmonic trap with trap frequencies
\omega_{x}=\omega_{y}=\omega_{z}/100
. Then we switch on an anisotropic trap, stirring, and two-body loss at time
t=0
. By simulating the dissipative Gross-Pitaevskii equation
[
41
,
67
]
, we obtain the dynamics of the density and phase profiles of the order parameter
\psi
of the dissipative BEC as shown in Fig.
1
. From the numerical results, we can see that quantized vortices are formed and enter the system in the dynamics in Fig.
1
(b) compared with the non-dissipative case in Fig.
1
(a), indicating that the superfluidity is induced solely by two-body loss which verifies our theoretical prediction. The superfluid density
\rho_{\mathrm{s}}
can be also measured from the total angular momentum
\langle L\rangle
of a quantum gas under a synthetic gauge field
[
69
,
70
]
:

\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{s}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{t}}}=1-\lim_{\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}\to 0}\frac{\langle L\rangle}{I_{\mathrm{cl}}\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}},

(21)

where
\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}
is an effective angular momentum induced by two Laguerre-Gauss beams
[
69
]
,
\rho_{\mathrm{t}}
is the total boson density and
I_{\mathrm{cl}}
represents the classical moment of inertia of the gas.

Conclusion.—
In this Letter, we have investigated the
transport property of a dissipative molecular BEC with two-body loss and
shown that superfluidity can be induced by dissipation without recourse to interparticle interaction both numerically and analytically. We have demonstrated that the normal fluid density in the dissipative BEC vanishes, i.e., all bosons contribute to superfluid transport in the presence of dissipation if we start from the absolute zero in the remote past. We also find that the quantum depletion is induced by dissipation, indicating that dissipation plays a role similar to repulsive interaction. We have further investigated the spectrum and the stability of a dissipative molecular BEC, which can be experimentally tested through the angle-resolved measurement of the spectral function
[
71
,
72
]
.

\varphi_{+}

\varphi_{-}

where

(S.2)

[

(S.3)

[

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[i\varphi_{R}^{\ast}\partial_{t}\varphi_{A}+i\varphi_{A}^{\ast}\partial_{t}\varphi_{R}-\frac{1}{2m}(\nabla\varphi_{R}^{\ast})\cdot(\nabla\varphi_{A})-\frac{1}{2m}(\nabla\varphi_{A}^{\ast})\cdot(\nabla\varphi_{R})\right]

(S.4)

\displaystyle+\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[\mu|\varphi_{+}|^{2}-\mu|\varphi_{-}|^{2}-\frac{U}{2}|\varphi_{+}|^{4}+\frac{U}{2}|\varphi_{-}|^{4}\right].

Let us decompose the bosonic fields

\varphi_{+},\varphi_{-}
around a U(1)-symmetry-broken ground state

[

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(1+\phi_{+})e^{i\theta_{+}},

\displaystyle\varphi_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(1+\phi_{-})e^{i\theta_{-}},

where

The stationary solution to this equation is

\displaystyle\varphi_{R}

\displaystyle\sqrt{n_{0}},

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{0}U,

\displaystyle\varphi_{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(S.9)

By substituting the retarded and advanced fields in Eq. (
S.3

S.8

S.9

obtain

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-\varphi_{0}^{2}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}-\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{0}^{2}(\nabla\theta_{R})(\nabla\theta_{A})-2\varphi_{0}^{2}\phi_{R}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}-2\varphi_{0}^{2}\phi_{A}\partial_{t}\theta_{R}-\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{0}^{2}(\nabla\phi_{R})(\nabla\phi_{A})\right]

\displaystyle+\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}[-4U\varphi_{0}^{4}\phi_{R}\phi_{A}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-\frac{1}{m}\varphi_{0}^{2}(\nabla\theta_{R})(\nabla\theta_{A})-2\varphi_{0}^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{R}&\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}\\
\partial_{t}\theta_{R}\end{array}\right)\right]

\displaystyle+\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{R}&\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-\frac{\varphi_{0}^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+2U\varphi_{0}^{4}\\

\displaystyle+\varphi_{0}^{4}\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}&\partial_{t}\theta_{R}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\left(-\frac{\varphi_{0}^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+2U\varphi_{0}^{4}\right)^{-1}\\

This positive semidefinite term expresses the phase rigidity. We note that the last term in Eq. (

Effective Action in Open Quantum Systems without a Dipolar Interaction

S=\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}[i\varphi_{+}^{\ast}(\bm{r},t)\partial_{t}\varphi_{+}(\bm{r},t)-H_{+}-i\varphi_{-}^{\ast}(\bm{r},t)\partial_{t}\varphi_{-}(\bm{r},t)+H_{-}-i\gamma\varphi_{-}^{\ast}(\bm{r},t)^{2}\varphi_{+}(\bm{r},t)^{2}],

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle S^{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-\frac{1}{m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\theta_{R})(\nabla\theta_{A})+16\gamma^{2}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}^{2}\frac{|\varphi_{0}|^{4}(-i\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4})}{-4\gamma^{2}|\varphi_{0}|^{8}-\left(-\frac{|\varphi_{0}|^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+2U_{R}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\right)^{2}}+2\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}\right]

where we ignore the contribution from

includes the second-order terms in

\displaystyle S^{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[\frac{1}{m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\theta_{R})(\nabla\theta_{A})-2\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}\right]

(S.52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[\frac{1}{2m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\theta_{+})(\nabla\theta_{+})-\frac{1}{2m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\theta_{-})(\nabla\theta_{-})-2\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}(\theta_{+}-\theta_{-})\right]

\displaystyle-\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\left[\frac{1}{2m}(\nabla\theta_{+}+\bm{\psi}(\bm{r}))(\nabla\theta_{+}+\bm{\psi}(\bm{r}))-\frac{1}{2m}(\nabla\theta_{-}+\bm{\psi}(\bm{r}))(\nabla\theta_{-}+\bm{\psi}(\bm{r}))\right],

with
\nabla\cdot\bm{\psi}=2m\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{2}
. Here the vector field
\bm{\psi}(\bm{r})
gives the dissipative current, which describes the loss of particles into the environment
[
50
]
.
Since the twist of the phase of a condensate can be related to the superfluid velocity as
\bm{v}_{s\alpha}=\alpha\nabla\theta_{\alpha}/m

[
2
]
,
the current density is determined as

\displaystyle\langle\bm{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle\bm{j}_{+}+\bm{j}_{-}\rangle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{m\delta\bm{v}_{s+}}+\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{m\delta\bm{v}_{s-}}\right)|_{\bm{v}_{s+}=-\bm{v}_{s-}=\bm{v}_{s}}

(S.53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{|\varphi_{0}|^{2}}{m}(m\bm{v}_{s}+\bm{\psi}),

where
\bm{j}_{\alpha}:=-\delta S^{\text{eff}}/\delta(m\bm{v}_{\alpha})
. Equation (
S.53
) gives
\rho_{s}=|\varphi_{0}|^{2}=n_{0}(t)
by the definition of the superfluid density
\rho_{s}=\partial\langle\bm{j}\rangle/\partial\bm{v}_{s}
. We therefore find that the system shows superfluid transport if the dissipative current is subtracted from the total current.

Let us now introduce the current-density operator as
\hat{\bm{j}_{c}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\bm{k}}\bm{k}(a_{\bm{k}+}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k}+}+a_{\bm{k}-}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k}-})

and we calculate the expectation value of the current as

\bm{j}_{c}=\langle\hat{\bm{j}_{c}}\rangle=\frac{1}{Z}\int D[\varphi_{+}]D[\varphi_{-}]\bm{j}_{c}e^{iS}.

(S.54)

This is the “classical” current in the open quantum systems defined in Ref.
[
45
]
. However, here we have a decay of the particle-number density, which can be seen from the continuous equation as

\frac{dn}{dt}=-\nabla\cdot(\bm{j}_{c}+\bm{j}_{d}),

(S.55)

where
\bm{j}=\bm{j}_{c}+\bm{j}_{d}
is the total current,
\bm{j}_{c}

is the nondissipative current density and
\bm{j}_{d}
is the dissipative current density due to the loss of particles. This dissipative current density obeys the following equation of continuity:

\nabla\cdot\bm{j}_{d}=-\frac{dn_{0}(t)}{dt}=\frac{2\gamma n_{0}(0)^{2}}{(1+2\gamma n_{0}(0)t)^{2}}.

(S.56)

One immediately recognizes that
\bm{j}_{c}=\bm{j}_{s}=|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\bm{v}_{s}
and
\bm{j}_{d}=|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\bm{\psi}/m
.
Nevertheless, the current
\bm{j}_{d}
will not influence the
superfluid density since
\partial j_{\alpha}/\partial v_{\beta}=\rho_{s}\delta_{\alpha\beta}
.
This is also the case in the above field-theoretic calculations.

To investigate the stability of the bosonic gas against density fluctuations, we consider the compressibility of the gas subject to dissipation. In the closed quantum systems, the compressibility is defined as

\kappa=-\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial p}\right)_{T},

(S.57)

which is equivalent to

\kappa=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\frac{\partial n}{\partial\mu}.

(S.58)

However, since the thermodynamic relations do not hold in nonequilibrium open systems, we define the compressibility from the response
\delta n(x)
of the density distribution
n(x)
to the applied external potential
\delta\nu(x)

\delta n(x)=\int dx^{\prime}\kappa(x,x^{\prime})\delta\nu(x^{\prime}),

(S.59)

where
x:=(\bm{r},t)
. The response function
\kappa(x,x^{\prime})
can be calculated with the Schwinger-Keldysh
field theory as

\kappa(x,x^{\prime})=i(\langle n_{+}(x)n_{+}(x^{\prime})\rangle-\langle n_{-}(x)n_{-}(x^{\prime})\rangle).

(S.60)

Since the Schwinger-Keldysh action can be rewritten as

S=\int d^{4}x[n_{+}(x)\partial_{t}\theta_{+}(x)-n_{-}(x)\partial_{t}\theta_{-}(x)]+S_{\text{int}}[n,\theta,\nabla\theta],

(S.61)

where
S_{\mathrm{int}}[n,\theta,\nabla\theta]
does not include
\partial_{t}\theta_{\alpha}
, we can rewrite the response function as

\kappa(x,x^{\prime})=i\left(\left\langle\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\partial_{t}\theta_{+}(x)}\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\partial_{t}\theta_{+}(x^{\prime})}\right\rangle-\left\langle\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\partial_{t}\theta_{-}(x)}\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\partial_{t}\theta_{-}(x^{\prime})}\right\rangle\right).

(S.62)

Performing the Fourier transformation, we have

\delta n(k)=\kappa(k)\delta\nu(k),

(S.63)

where

\kappa(k)=\frac{i}{\omega^{2}}\left(\left\langle\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\theta_{+}(k)}\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\theta_{+}(-k)}\right\rangle-\left\langle\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\theta_{-}(k)}\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\theta_{-}(-k)}\right\rangle\right)=\frac{i}{\omega^{2}}\left\langle\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\theta_{R}(k)}\frac{\delta S^{\text{eff}}}{\delta\theta_{A}(-k)}\right\rangle,

(S.64)

and
k:=(\bm{k},\omega)
. Therefore, the compressibility in Eq. (
S.58
) is given by

\kappa=\frac{1}{n^{2}}\lim_{k\rightarrow 0}\kappa(k).

(S.65)

From Eq. (
S1.2
), we obtain the compressibility as

\kappa=\frac{U_{R}}{(\gamma^{2}+U_{R}^{2})n^{2}}.

(S.66)

In the closed quantum systems without loss, the compressibility diverges as
U_{R}\to 0
, indicating the instability of free bosonic systems against the external perturbation. However, in the open quantum system, the compressibility vanishes and thus remains finite if
U_{R}\to 0
and
\gamma>0
. This result indicates that the density fluctuations are suppressed due to dissipation, ensuring the stability of the bosonic gas. We note that the vanishing compressibility does not indicate the infinite speed of sound since

\kappa\neq-\frac{1}{V}\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial p}\right)_{T},

(S.67)

i.e., the compressibility which is defined from the response function of the nonequilibrium system does not obey the thermodynamic relation. Therefore, the compressibility in the case of
\gamma\neq 0
should be distinguished from the conventional one in a closed quantum system and the two limits
U_{R}\to 0
and
\gamma\to 0
cannot be exchanged.

S1.3
Effective Action in Open Quantum Systems with a Dipolar Interaction

We now move on to the molecular BEC system and consider an effective field theory of a dipolar Bose
gas. Here we take into account the dipole-dipole interaction, which takes the form of

V(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})=c_{dd}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}a_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}a_{\bm{r}},

(S.68)

where we assume that the dipole moments are polarized in the
z
direction and
\cos\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}=(\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime})\cdot\hat{\bm{z}}/|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|
.
By adding this interaction, the
action becomes

S=\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}[i\varphi_{+}^{\ast}(\bm{r},t)\partial_{t}\varphi_{+}(\bm{r},t)-H_{+}-i\varphi_{-}^{\ast}(\bm{r},t)\partial_{t}\varphi_{-}(\bm{r},t)+H_{-}-i\gamma\varphi_{-}^{\ast}(\bm{r},t)^{2}\varphi_{+}(\bm{r},t)^{2}],

(S.69)

where

H_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2m}(\nabla\varphi_{\pm}^{\ast})\cdot(\nabla\varphi_{\pm})+\frac{U_{\pm}}{2}|\varphi_{\pm}|^{4}+c_{dd}\int d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}|\varphi_{\pm}(\bm{r},t)|^{2}|\varphi_{\pm}(\bm{r}^{\prime},t)|^{2}.

(S.70)

We consider the uniform mean-field solution by
\delta S/\delta\varphi_{A}^{\ast}=0

which gives

\left(i\partial_{t}-(U_{R}-i\gamma)|\varphi_{R}|^{2}(\bm{r},t)-c_{dd}\int d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}|\varphi_{R}(\bm{r}^{\prime},t)|^{2}\right)\varphi_{R}(\bm{r},t)=0.

(S.71)

Here we assume
\varphi_{A}=0
as before since in the saddle-point solution the field is the same on the forward and backward contours. This equation can be solved as

\displaystyle\varphi_{R}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varphi_{R}(0)}{\sqrt{1+2\gamma\varphi_{R}(0)^{2}t}}\exp\left(-i\int_{0}^{t}\mu(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{n_{0}(0)}{1+2\gamma n_{0}(0)t}}\exp\left(-i\int_{0}^{t}\mu(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\right),

\displaystyle\mu(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\varphi_{R}(t)|^{2}\left(U_{R}+2c_{dd}\int d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}\right)

(S.72)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n_{0}(t)\left(U_{R}-\frac{8\pi}{3}c_{dd}\right).

Here we regularize the integral over
\bm{r}^{\prime}
as
[
42
]

\displaystyle\int d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}

(S.73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\bm{k}\rightarrow 0}\int d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lim_{\bm{k}\rightarrow 0}4\pi\int\frac{dr^{\prime}}{r^{\prime}}j_{2}(kr^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\pi}{3},

where
j_{2}(x)
is the Bessel function of the second kind. We can see that the effect of the dipolar interaction is just to partially cancel the repulsive interaction. By defining

\varepsilon_{dd}:=\frac{8\pi c_{dd}}{3U_{R}},

(S.74)

we have
\mu=n_{0}(t)(1-\varepsilon_{dd})U_{R}
. By adding the dipolar interaction to the action (
S.35
) on each contour, we obtain

S_{\alpha}\rightarrow S_{\alpha}+\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[\frac{8\pi}{3}c_{dd}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(1+\phi_{\alpha}(\bm{r},t))^{2}+c_{dd}\int d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}(1+\phi_{\alpha}(\bm{r},t))^{2}(1+\phi_{\alpha}(\bm{r}^{\prime},t))^{2}\right].

(S.75)

After simplification, the modified action becomes

S_{\alpha}\rightarrow S_{\alpha}-4c_{dd}\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\phi_{+}(\bm{r},t)\phi_{-}(\bm{r}^{\prime},t).

(S.76)

By substituting the modified action on each contour into the total
action (
S.34
), we have

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}-\frac{1}{m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\theta_{R})(\nabla\theta_{A})-2|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\phi_{R}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}-2|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\phi_{A}\partial_{t}\theta_{R}-\frac{1}{m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\phi_{R})(\nabla\phi_{A})\right]

\displaystyle+\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-4U_{R}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\phi_{R}\phi_{A}+2i\gamma\left(2\phi_{R}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\phi_{A}^{2}\right)+2\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}+8\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\phi_{R}\theta_{A}\right]

\displaystyle-4c_{dd}\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}(\phi_{R}(\bm{r},t)\phi_{A}(\bm{r}^{\prime},t)+\phi_{A}(\bm{r},t)\phi_{R}(\bm{r^{\prime}},t))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}-\frac{1}{m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\theta_{R})(\nabla\theta_{A})-2|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{R}&\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}-4\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\theta_{A}\\
\partial_{t}\theta_{R}\end{array}\right)\right]

\displaystyle-\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{R}&\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-4i\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}&-\frac{|\varphi_{0}|^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+2U_{R}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\\
-\frac{|\varphi_{0}|^{2}}{2m}\nabla^{2}+2U_{R}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}&-i\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{R}\\
\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)-2\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}\right]

\displaystyle-\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}d^{3}\bm{r}^{\prime}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{R}&\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)(\bm{r},t)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&4c_{dd}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\\
4c_{dd}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r-r^{\prime}}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{R}\\
\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)(\bm{r^{\prime}},t).

Then we integrate out the Higgs mode to obtain the effective field
theory for the NG mode. By the Fourier transformation, the action becomes

\displaystyle S^{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}-\frac{1}{m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\theta_{R})(\nabla\theta_{A})-2|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{R}&\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\theta_{A}-4\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{2}\theta_{A}\\
\partial_{t}\theta_{R}\end{array}\right)+2\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}\right]

\displaystyle+\int dtd^{3}\bm{k}\left[-\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi_{R}&\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)(\bm{k},t)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-4i\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}&\frac{|\varphi_{0}|^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+2(U_{R}+V_{dd}(\bm{k}))|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\\
\frac{|\varphi_{0}|^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+2(U_{R}+V_{dd}(\bm{k}))|\varphi_{0}|^{4}&-i\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{R}\\
\phi_{A}\end{array}\right)(-\bm{k},t)\right],

where we define

V_{dd}(\bm{k}):=2c_{dd}\int d^{3}\bm{r}\frac{1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{r}}{r^{3}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}=-\frac{8\pi}{3}c_{dd}(1-3\cos^{2}\theta_{\bm{k}})

(S.95)

with
\theta_{\bm{k}}
being the angle between the momentum

\bm{k}
and the
z
-axis. We integrate out the Higgs mode in the
momentum space and set
\partial_{t}\theta_{A}=\partial_{t}\theta_{R}=0
,
obtaining the effective action as

\displaystyle S^{\text{eff}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-\frac{1}{m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\theta_{R})(\nabla\theta_{A})+2\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}\right]+\int dtd^{3}\bm{k}\left[16\gamma^{2}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}^{2}\frac{|\varphi_{0}|^{4}(-i\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4})}{-4\gamma^{2}|\varphi_{0}|^{8}-\left(\frac{|\varphi_{0}|^{2}k^{2}}{2m}+2(U_{R}+V_{dd}(\bm{k}))|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\right)^{2}}\right]

(S.96)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\int dtd^{3}\bm{r}\left[-\frac{1}{m}|\varphi_{0}|^{2}(\nabla\theta_{R})(\nabla\theta_{A})+2\gamma|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}\right]+\int dtd^{3}\bm{k}\left[4i\gamma^{2}|\varphi_{0}|^{4}\theta_{A}^{2}\frac{\gamma}{\gamma^{2}+(U_{R}+V_{dd}(\bm{k}))^{2}}\right].

In the second equality we also take the long-wavelength limit. Since the last term in Eq. (
S.96
) is of the second order in
\theta_{A}
, we can ignore it and arrive at the same
conclusion as before. Here, the dipolar interaction only plays
a role in modifying the strength of repulsive interaction, and therefore we
can understand the physics behind superfluidity in the same
way as the system only with contact interaction unless
\varepsilon_{dd}
is so large that the system becomes unstable. We discuss the critical point of
\varepsilon_{dd}
in Sec.
S4
(see Eq. (
S.304
)).

Appendix S2
f
-sum Rule

In this section, we generalize the
f
-sum rule to an open quantum system where the number of particles is not conserved, and discuss its relation to the weak U
(1)
symmetry. We first consider the following quantity

\langle[\rho_{-\bm{k}},\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})]\rangle=\text{Tr}[\rho(t)[\rho_{-\bm{k}},\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})]],

(S.97)

where
\rho(t)
is the density matrix of the system at time
t
which obeys the Lindblad master equation and

\displaystyle\rho_{\bm{k}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{p}}a_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}+\bm{k}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(O)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-i[O,H]-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int d^{3}\bm{r}\{O,L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}L_{\bm{r}}\}+\gamma\int d^{3}\bm{r}L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}OL_{\bm{r}}.

(S.98)

Since the Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}}\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}a_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k}}+\frac{U_{R}}{2}\int d^{3}\bm{r}(a_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger})^{2}(a_{\bm{r}})^{2}

(S.99)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}}\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}a_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k}}+\frac{U_{R}}{2V}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q},\bm{p}}a_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}-\bm{q}}a_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}},

with
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}=\bm{k}^{2}/2m
being the kinetic energy, a straightforward calculation
[
6
]
gives

i\left[{\rho_{\bm{k}}},H\right]=i\sum_{\bm{p}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{p}+\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{\bm{p}})a_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}+\bm{k}},

(S.100)

where the right-hand side originates from the kinetic-energy term in the Hamiltonian. The interaction terms do not contribute to the commutator since they only involve the density-density interaction. Then we calculate the following terms in
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})
.
We can see

-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int d^{3}\bm{r}\{O,L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}L_{\bm{r}}\}+\gamma\int d^{3}\bm{r}L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}OL_{\bm{r}}=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int d^{3}\bm{r}[O,L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}]L_{\bm{r}}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\int d^{3}\bm{r}L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}[O,L_{\bm{r}}].

(S.101)

Substituting
L_{\bm{r}}=a_{\bm{r}}^{2}
and performing the Fourier transformation,
we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{l}}[\rho_{\bm{k}},a_{\bm{p}+\bm{l}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{q}-\bm{l}}^{\dagger}]a_{\bm{q}}a_{\bm{p}}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{l}}a_{\bm{p}+\bm{l}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{q}-\bm{l}}^{\dagger}[\rho_{\bm{k}},a_{\bm{q}}a_{\bm{p}}]

(S.102)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma\sum_{\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{l}}(a_{\bm{l}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{l}+\bm{k}-\bm{q}}a_{\bm{p}+\bm{q}}+a_{\bm{l}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}-\bm{q}}{a_{\bm{l}+\bm{k}+\bm{q}}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\gamma\left(\sum_{\bm{q}}\rho_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}}\rho_{\bm{q}}-\sum_{\bm{q}}\rho_{\bm{k}}\right).

By utilizing the fact that
[\rho_{\bm{k}},\rho_{\bm{p}}]=0
for
arbitrary
\bm{k}
and
\bm{p}
, we have

\displaystyle[\rho_{-\bm{k}},\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\rho_{-\bm{k}},-i\sum_{\bm{p}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{p}+\bm{k}}-\varepsilon_{\bm{p}})a_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}+\bm{k}}-2\gamma\left(\sum_{\bm{q}}\rho_{\bm{k}-\bm{q}}\rho_{\bm{q}}-\sum_{\bm{q}}\rho_{\bm{k}}\right)\right]

(S.103)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\sum_{\bm{p}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{p}+\bm{k}}+\varepsilon_{\bm{p}-\bm{k}}-2\varepsilon_{\bm{p}})a_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2i\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}\hat{N},

where
\hat{N}
is the total particle-number operator. Therefore,

\langle[\rho_{-\bm{k}},\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})]\rangle=2i\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}N,

(S.104)

where
N
is the number of particles corresponding to the current state. We can see that dissipation does not change the expression of
[\rho_{-\bm{k}},\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})]
since the two-body loss only causes a uniform decrease in the density and hence does not generate current inside the system. Actually, this commutation relation originates from the weak U
(1)
symmetry of the Lindblad equation. One can verify that other systems obeying weak U
(1)
symmetry, like systems with
n
-body loss, also satisfy Eq. (
S.104
). Below, we show how to use the eigensystem of the Lindbladian to represent
\langle[\rho_{-\bm{k}},\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})]\rangle
. To begin with, we define the right eigenoperators
\hat{r}_{\alpha}

and the left eigenoperators
\hat{l}_{\alpha}
of
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}

as
[
73
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\hat{r}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}\hat{r}_{\alpha},

(S.105)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\hat{l}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}^{\ast}\hat{l}_{\alpha}.

(S.106)

The right and left eigenvectors satisfy the biorthogonal relation and the completeness relation:

\text{Tr}(\hat{l}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\hat{r}_{\beta})=\delta_{\alpha\beta},\sum_{\alpha}\hat{r}_{\alpha}\hat{l}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}=\mathbb{I}.

(S.107)

In terms of the left and right eigenvectors, we can expand
\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})
as

\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\hat{r}_{\alpha}\text{Tr}(\hat{l}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rho_{\bm{k}}).

(S.108)

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle[\rho_{-\bm{k}},\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}})]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}(\text{Tr}[\rho(t)\rho_{-\bm{k}}\hat{r}_{\alpha}]-\text{Tr}[\rho_{-\bm{k}}\rho(t)\hat{r}_{\alpha}])\text{Tr}(\hat{l}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rho_{\bm{k}})

(S.109)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\text{Tr}[[\rho(t),\rho_{-\bm{k}}]\hat{r}_{\alpha}]\text{Tr}(\hat{l}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rho_{\bm{k}}).

We further define a retarded Green’s function as

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{R}(\bm{k},t_{0},t):=-i\theta(t)\text{Tr}[\rho_{\bm{k}}e^{\mathcal{L}t}(e^{\mathcal{L}t_{0}}(\rho)\rho_{-\bm{k}})-\rho_{\bm{k}}e^{\mathcal{L}t}(\rho_{-\bm{k}}e^{\mathcal{L}t_{0}}(\rho))],

(S.110)

where
t_{0}
is an arbitrary time. We can expand the Green’s function as

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{R}(\bm{k},t_{0},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\theta(t)\sum_{\alpha}e^{\lambda_{\alpha}t}\text{Tr}\left\{[\rho(t_{0}),\rho_{-\bm{k}}]\hat{r}_{\alpha}\right\}\text{Tr}(\hat{l}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rho_{\bm{k}}).

(S.111)

Performing the Fourier transformation with respect to
t
, we obtain

\tilde{G}^{R}(\bm{k},t_{0},\omega)=\int dte^{i\omega t}\tilde{G}^{R}(\bm{k},t_{0},t)=\sum_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\omega-i\lambda_{\alpha}+i0^{+}}\text{Tr}[[\rho(t_{0}),\rho_{-\bm{k}}]\hat{r}_{\alpha}]\text{Tr}(\hat{l}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rho_{\bm{k}}).

(S.112)

Integrating
\tilde{G}^{R}
along a closed contour
C
that contains all the poles
i\lambda_{\alpha}-i0^{+}
, we obtain

\oint_{C}\frac{\omega d\omega}{2\pi}\tilde{G}^{R}(\bm{k},t_{0},\omega)=\sum_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\text{Tr}[[\rho(t_{0}),\rho_{-\bm{k}}]\hat{r}_{\alpha}]\text{Tr}(\hat{l}_{\alpha}^{\dagger}\rho_{\bm{k}})=2i\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}N(t_{0}).

(S.113)

This is the
f
-sum rule in the open quantum system. We note that this
f
-sum rule holds even though the particle number is not conserved during the dynamics. This
f
-sum rule can be used to derive the relation
between the current-current correlation function and the number operator. To show this, we start from the dynamics of the local boson number
\langle\rho_{\bm{r}}(t)\rangle:=\text{Tr}[a_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{r}}\rho(t)]
given by

\frac{\partial\langle\rho_{\bm{r}}(t)\rangle}{\partial t}=\text{Tr}[\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}\rho_{\bm{r}}\rho(t)]=i\langle[H,\rho_{\bm{r}}]\rangle+\frac{\gamma}{2}\int d^{3}\bm{r}\langle[2L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}\rho_{\bm{r}}L_{\bm{r}}-L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}L_{\bm{r}}\rho_{\bm{r}}-\rho_{\bm{r}}L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}L_{\bm{r}}]\rangle.

(S.114)

After simplification, this dynamics can be expressed as

\frac{\partial\langle\rho_{\bm{r}}(t)\rangle}{\partial t}=-\nabla\cdot(\bm{j}_{t}):=-\nabla\cdot(\bm{j}_{c}+\bm{j}_{d}),

(S.115)

where
\bm{j}_{t}:=\bm{j}_{c}+\bm{j}_{d}
is the total current with

\bm{j}_{c}
being the current flow in the system and
\bm{j}_{d}
being the current from the system to an environment induced by the two-body loss. They are given by

\displaystyle\bm{j}_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2m}\langle[\nabla a^{\dagger}(\bm{r})a(\bm{r})-a^{\dagger}(\bm{r})\nabla a(\bm{r})]\rangle,

(S.116)

and
\nabla\cdot\bm{j}_{d}=2\gamma a^{\dagger}_{\bm{r}}a^{\dagger}_{\bm{r}}a_{\bm{r}}a_{\bm{r}}
, which is solved as

\bm{j}_{d}(\bm{r})=2\gamma\int\frac{d\bm{r}^{\prime}}{4\pi}\frac{\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}}{|\bm{r}-\bm{r}^{\prime}|^{3}}\langle a^{\dagger}_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}a^{\dagger}_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}a_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}a_{\bm{r}^{\prime}}\rangle.

(S.117)

Fourier transforming Eq. (
S.114
), we obtain

\langle\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{\bm{k}}(\omega))\rangle=-i\omega\langle\rho_{\bm{k}}(\omega)\rangle=-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{j}_{t}(\bm{k},\omega).

(S.118)

It follows from Eq. (
S.103
) that

m\langle[\rho_{\bm{k}}(t),\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{-\bm{k}}(t))]\rangle=ik^{2}N(t),

(S.119)

where the correlation function is defined on the basis of the Schwinger-Keldysh field theory as

\langle[A(t),B(t))]\rangle:=\int D[a]D[a^{\dagger}](A_{+}(t)B_{+}(t)-A_{-}(t)B_{-}(t))e^{iS}=\text{Tr}[[A,B]e^{\mathcal{L}t}\rho].

(S.120)

For convenience of the notations, we will use
\langle[A(t),B(t))]\rangle
in the sense of Eq. (
S.120
) in the following calculation. From the Fourier transformation of Eq. (
S.119
), we have

m\int dte^{i(\omega-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t}\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega_{2}}{2\pi}\langle[\rho_{\bm{k}}(\omega_{1}),\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{-\bm{k}}(\omega_{2}))]\rangle=ik^{2}N(\omega).

(S.121)

The left-hand side of Eq. (
S.121
) can be simplified as

\displaystyle m\int dt\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega_{2}}{2\pi}e^{i(\omega-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t}\langle[\rho_{\bm{k}}(\omega_{1}),\mathcal{L}^{\dagger}(\rho_{-\bm{k}}(\omega_{2}))]\rangle

(S.122)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2\pi}\int d\omega_{1}d\omega_{2}\delta(\omega-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\left\langle\left[\frac{-\bm{k}\cdot\bm{j}_{t}(\bm{k},\omega_{1})}{\omega_{1}},-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{j}_{t}(-\bm{k},\omega_{2})\right]\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ik_{i}k_{j}m\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi\omega_{1}}\langle[j_{t}^{i}(\bm{k},\omega_{1}),j_{t}^{j}(-\bm{k},\omega-\omega_{1})]\rangle,

and hence Eq. (
S.119
) can be rewritten as

N(\omega)=\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}m\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi\omega_{1}}\langle[j_{t}^{i}(\bm{k},\omega_{1}),j_{t}^{j}(-\bm{k},\omega-\omega_{1})]\rangle.

(S.123)

Furthermore, we define the total current-current correlation function as

\displaystyle\gamma^{i,j}(\bm{k},\omega,t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\int dte^{i\omega t}\langle[j_{t}^{i}(\bm{k},t+t_{0}),j_{t}^{j}(-\bm{k},t_{0})]\rangle

(S.124)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\int dte^{i\omega(t+t_{0})}\langle[j_{t}^{i}(\bm{k},t+t_{0}),j_{t}^{j}(-\bm{k},t_{0})]\rangle e^{-i\omega t_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\langle[j_{t}^{i}(\bm{k},\omega),j_{t}^{j}(-\bm{k},t_{0})]\rangle e^{-i\omega t_{0}}.

By substituting this into (
S.123
), we have

\displaystyle N(t_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}m\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi\omega_{1}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega t_{0}}\langle[j_{t}^{i}(\bm{k},\omega_{1}),j_{t}^{j}(-\bm{k},\omega-\omega_{1})]\rangle

(S.125)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}m\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi\omega_{1}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{-i\omega_{1}t_{0}}\langle[j_{t}^{i}(\bm{k},\omega_{1}),j_{t}^{j}(-\bm{k},\omega-\omega_{1})]\rangle e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{1})t_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi\omega_{1}}e^{-i\omega_{1}t_{0}}\langle[j_{t}^{i}(\bm{k},\omega_{1}),j_{t}^{j}(-\bm{k},t_{0})]\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi\omega_{1}}\gamma^{i,j}(\bm{k},\omega_{1},t_{0}).

Since the longitudinal component of the correlation function is given
by
\gamma^{L}(\bm{k},\omega)=\frac{k_{i}k_{j}}{k^{2}}\gamma^{i,j}(\bm{k},\omega)
,
we can also express the
f
-sum rule as

\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi\omega}\gamma^{L}(\bm{k},\omega,t_{0})=N(t_{0}).

(S.126)

In the following discussion of this section, we omit the subscript
c
for the current operator
\bm{j}_{c}
for simplicity.
Let us examine the normal fluid density, which is determined by the current response under an external perturbation
H\to H-\bm{u}\cdot\bm{j}
, where
\bm{u}
is an external velocity field that represents the effect of a wall moving with velocity
\bm{u}

[

(S.127)

where we define the averaged current density as

(S.128)

Since the two-body loss is uniform in space, we assume that the dissipative current does not influence the averaged current density. The only contribution originates from the closed current
\bm{j}

S.114

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\langle J_{i}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V}\int d^{3}\bm{r}\frac{d}{dt}\langle j_{i}(\bm{r},t)\rangle

(S.129)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{V}\int d^{3}\bm{r}\left\langle-i[j_{i},H]-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int d^{3}{\bm{r^{\prime}}}\{j_{i},L_{\bm{r^{\prime}}}^{\dagger}L_{\bm{r^{\prime}}}\}+\gamma\int d^{3}\bm{r^{\prime}}L_{\bm{r^{\prime}}}^{\dagger}j_{i}L_{\bm{r^{\prime}}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\frac{i}{V}m\int d^{3}\bm{r}\int d^{3}\bm{r^{\prime}}\langle[j_{i}(\bm{r},t),j_{j}(\bm{r}^{\prime},t)]\rangle u_{j}.

The averaged current density is thus given by

\displaystyle\langle J_{i}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{V}m\int d^{3}\bm{r}\int d^{3}\bm{r^{\prime}}\int^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle[j_{i}(\bm{r},t),j_{j}(\bm{r}^{\prime},t^{\prime})]\rangle u_{j}

(S.130)

\displaystyle\frac{i}{V}m\int\frac{d\omega_{2}}{2\pi}\int d^{3}\bm{r}\int d^{3}\bm{r^{\prime}}\int^{t}dt^{\prime}e^{-i\omega_{2}t^{\prime}}\langle[j_{i}(\bm{r},t),j_{j}(\bm{r}^{\prime},\omega_{2})]\rangle u_{j}

where we cancel the constant term with the initial condition. Then the normal fluid density tensor is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{n}^{i,j}(t)

\displaystyle\frac{m}{V^{2}}\int\frac{d\omega_{2}}{2\pi\omega_{2}}\int d^{3}\bm{r}\int d^{3}\bm{r^{\prime}}e^{-i\omega_{2}t}\langle[j_{i}(\bm{r},t),j_{j}(\bm{r}^{\prime},\omega_{2})]\rangle

\displaystyle\lim_{\bm{k}\rightarrow 0}m\int\frac{d\omega_{2}}{2\pi\omega_{2}}e^{-i\omega_{2}t}\langle[j_{i}(\bm{k},t),j_{j}(\bm{-k},\omega_{2})]\rangle

\tilde{\gamma}
for the current operator
\bm{j}_{c}

S.124

we first note that the dissipative current does not contribute to the correlation function since it is isotropic, i.e.,

\tilde{\gamma}
with the total current-current correlator
\gamma

\rho_{n}^{i,j}(t)=\lim_{\bm{k}\rightarrow 0}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi\omega}\gamma^{i,j}(\bm{k},\omega,t).

(S.132)

According to an analysis similar to the one applicable to a closed quantum system, we find that the normal fluid density corresponds to the transverse component of the
total current-current correlation function
[

S.126
),
we can define the superfluid density as the difference between the
transverse and the longitudinal parts of the correlation function
tensor. We note that the derivation of the
f

f

symmetry of the system since under the weak

symmetry, the density matrix remains diagonal in the particle-number basis during time evolution. Therefore, the right-hand side of Eq. (

The Case without a Dipolar Interaction

In this section, we investigate the density of the quantum depletion, which is the density of the noncondensate part at absolute zero, of a dissipative molecular BEC. We begin by constructing the mean-field Lindbladian action. The Hamiltonian of a weakly interacting bosonic system is given by

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}}\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}a_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k}}+\frac{U_{R}}{2}\int d^{3}\bm{r}(a_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger})^{2}(a_{\bm{r}})^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}}\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}a_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k}}+\frac{U_{R}}{2V}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q},\bm{p}}a_{\bm{k}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}-\bm{q}}a_{\bm{k}+\bm{q}},

where
V
is the volume of the system. For simplicity of discussions, we consider only a contact interaction here, and we will consider the dipolar-dipole interaction later.
In the presence of dissipation, the dynamics of the system is described by the Lindblad equation:

\frac{d\rho}{dt}=\mathcal{L}\rho=-i[H,\rho]-\frac{\gamma}{2}\int d^{3}\bm{r}(\{L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}L_{\bm{r}},\rho\}-2L_{\bm{r}}\rho L_{\bm{r}}^{\dagger}).

(S.134)

Here we take the Lindblad operator for two-body loss as

[
45
]

(S.135)

where

(S.136)

By applying Fourier transformation to the dissipation part, the Schwinger-Keldysh action is given by

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dt\Bigg[\sum_{\bm{k}}(a_{\bm{k}+}^{\dagger}(i\partial_{t}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}})a_{\bm{k}+}-a_{\bm{k}-}^{\dagger}(i\partial_{t}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}})a_{\bm{k}-})-\frac{U}{2V}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{q},\bm{p}}a_{\bm{k}+}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}+}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{p}-\bm{q},+}a_{\bm{k}+\bm{q},+}

where
U=U_{R}-i\gamma
. We employ the mean-field approximation to separate

(S.138)

Here

From Eqs. (
S.52

and
\theta_{-}

The Matsubara Green’s function can be written as

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}_{ab}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{V}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial v_{a}\partial v_{b}}|_{v=0}

S3.2
The Case with a Dipolar Interaction

In the following, we also take the dipolar interaction into account to discuss application of our theory to a dissipative BEC of dipolar molecules. Following the
same procedure as above, we apply the Bogoliubov approximation and find the
Green’s function as

G(\bm{k},\omega)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{1}&b&0&0\\
b^{\ast}&a_{2}&0&c\\
c&0&-a_{1}^{\ast}&-b\\
0&0&-b^{\ast}&-a_{2}^{\ast}\end{array}\right)^{-1},

(S.225)

where
a_{1}=-(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}-m\bm{v}}+(U_{R}+V_{dd}(\bm{k}))n-2i\gamma n-\omega),b=-Un,a_{2}=-(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}+m\bm{v}}+(U_{R}+V_{dd}(\bm{k}))n-2i\gamma n+\omega)
, and
c=-4i\gamma n
. We can see that the effect of
dipolar interaction is just to replace the interaction strength
U_{R}
with

U_{R}+V_{dd}(\bm{k})=U_{R}(1-\varepsilon_{dd}+3\varepsilon_{dd}\cos^{2}\theta_{\bm{k}})
, which is anisotropic in the momentum space.
For convenience, we introduce an effective interaction strength
\tilde{U}_{R}(\bm{k}):=U_{R}+V_{dd}(\bm{k})
. Therefore, the phase
stiffness is given by

\displaystyle Q_{ab}=

\displaystyle i4m^{2}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d^{3}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{k_{a}k_{b}}{m^{2}}\frac{-|b|^{2}}{2|G^{-1}|^{2}}\big[(a_{1}^{\ast}a_{2}^{\ast}-|b|^{2})(|b|^{2}+c^{2}-a_{1}^{\ast}a_{2}^{\ast})

\displaystyle+\left(a_{1}a_{2}-|b|^{2}\right)(|b|^{2}+c^{2}-a_{1}a_{2})-c^{2}(|a_{2}|^{2}+|a_{1}|^{2})\big],

(S.226)

where
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
include the effect of dipole-dipole interaction as defined above. We note that here the phase stiffness takes different values depending on the direction due to the anisotropic dipolar interaction. To proceed further, we average the phase stiffness over the direction of the applied perturbation. From Eq. (
S.226
), we find that
the nontrivial contribution only comes from the diagonal terms with
a=b
.
For simplicity, we define the direction of the subscript
a
in Eq. (
S.226
) as
(\cos\varphi\sin\zeta,\sin\varphi\sin\zeta,\cos\zeta)
and rewrite the phase stiffness as

Q_{aa}=i4m^{2}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{d^{3}\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{q(k,\theta)k^{2}}{m^{2}}(\cos\theta\cos\zeta+\sin\theta\sin\zeta\cos(\phi-\varphi))^{2},

(S.227)

where

q:=\frac{-|b|^{2}}{2|G^{-1}|^{2}}\left[(a_{1}^{\ast}a_{2}^{\ast}-|b|^{2})(|b|^{2}+c^{2}-a_{1}^{\ast}a_{2}^{\ast})+\left(a_{1}a_{2}-|b|^{2}\right)(|b|^{2}+c^{2}-a_{1}a_{2})-c^{2}(|a_{2}|^{2}+|a_{1}|^{2})\right],

(S.228)

and the direction of
\bm{k}
is defined as
(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\cos\theta)
. Then by integrating Eq. (
S.227
) over
\phi
, we have

Q_{aa}=i4m^{2}\int\frac{d\omega}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int k^{2}dk\sin\theta d\theta\frac{q(k,\theta)k^{2}}{m^{2}}\left[(\cos\theta\cos\zeta)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\sin\theta\sin\zeta)^{2}\right],

(S.229)

which depends only on the angle
\zeta
in the direction of
a
. We then take the average over
\zeta
to determine the averaged quantum depletion given by

\displaystyle\bar{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\frac{4m^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\int k^{2}dk\sin\theta d\theta\frac{q(k,\theta)k^{2}}{m^{2}}\frac{2}{3}[(\cos\theta)^{2}+(\sin\theta)^{2}]

(S.230)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\frac{8m^{2}}{3(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\int k^{2}dk\sin\theta d\theta\frac{q(k,\theta)k^{2}}{m^{2}}.

After the integration over
k
, we are left with the expression of
\bar{Q}
with
an average over
\theta
. We estimate the value of
\bar{Q}
by
substituting
U_{R}
with
U_{R}+V_{dd}
and take the average over the angle

\theta

U_{R},c_{dd}\gg\gamma
and the weak-interaction limit
U_{R},c_{dd}\ll\gamma
.
In the first case, the phase stiffness can be expressed as

\displaystyle\bar{Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{m^{5/2}}{3\pi^{2}}\int\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta d\theta(\tilde{U}_{R}n)^{3/2}\left[1+\frac{(\gamma n)^{2}}{2\sqrt{2}(\tilde{U}_{R}n)^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dx\frac{(3+4x+2x^{2})\sqrt{x(2+x)}}{x(2+x)^{3}}+O\left[\left(\frac{\gamma}{U_{R}}\right)^{3}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=:\frac{m^{5/2}}{3\pi^{2}}(U_{R}n)^{3/2}\left(h_{1}+h_{2}\frac{\eta(\gamma n)^{2}}{2\sqrt{U_{R}n}}+O\left(\frac{\gamma}{U_{R}}\right)^{3}\right),

(S.231)

where

\displaystyle h_{1}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{8}\sqrt{1+2\varepsilon_{dd}}\left[5+\varepsilon_{dd}+\frac{3(1-\varepsilon_{dd})^{2}\text{arcsinh}\sqrt{3\varepsilon_{dd}/(1-\varepsilon_{dd})}}{\sqrt{3\varepsilon_{dd}(1+2\varepsilon_{dd})}}\right],

(S.232)

\displaystyle h_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{3\varepsilon_{dd}}}\log\left(1+\frac{2\left(3\varepsilon_{dd}+\sqrt{3\varepsilon_{dd}(1+2\varepsilon_{dd})}\right)}{1-\varepsilon_{dd}}\right).

(S.233)

We can prove that the two coeffients are enlarged by the ratio

\varepsilon_{dd}
between the dipolar and the contact interactions, which indicates that the dipolar interaction enhances the
repulsive interaction on average and hence increases the quantum depletion. Substituting the experimental data
\varepsilon_{dd}=0.833

[

h_{1}=1.204,h_{2}=1.305

In the weak-interaction limit where
U_{R},c_{dd}\ll\gamma

S.230

\bar{Q}=\int\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta d\theta\frac{m^{5/2}(\gamma n)^{3/2}}{24\pi^{5/2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2}\left[1+6\frac{\tilde{U}_{R}}{\gamma}\frac{\Gamma(3/4)^{2}}{\Gamma(1/4)^{2}}+O\left(\frac{U_{R}}{\gamma}\right)^{2}\right].

(S.234)

After taking the average over the angle
\theta
, we find that this is
equivalent to the result with no dipolar interaction. This is because here the quantum depletion is mainly produced by dissipation and the effect of the dipolar interaction vanishes up to the first order in
\tilde{U}_{R}
.

Furthermore, we consider the normal quantum depletion density. We perturb the Hamiltonians in the Schwinger-Keldysh action by
H_{+}\to H_{+}-\bm{v}\cdot\bm{j}_{+}
and
H_{-}\to H_{-}+\bm{v}\cdot\bm{j}_{-}
. The normal fluid density is determined from
\tilde{Q}_{ab}=\frac{-1}{2V}\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial v_{a}\partial v_{a}}|_{v=0}=mn_{nD}\delta_{ab}
. Here
F
is a functional in the presence of the source term. The normal fluid density tensor is equivalent to the second term in Eq. (
S.164

\tilde{Q}_{ab}=i\frac{m^{2}}{2V}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}\neq 0}\nabla_{a}\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}\nabla_{b}\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}\text{Tr}[(\sigma_{z}\otimes\sigma_{z})G(\sigma_{z}\otimes\sigma_{z})G],

(S.235)

where the diagonal term is the normal fluid density. Using the explicit form of
G
, we obtain

\displaystyle\text{Tr}[(\sigma_{z}\otimes\sigma_{z})G(\sigma_{z}\otimes\sigma_{z})G]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4\left\{\gamma^{2}n^{2}\left[\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}^{2}(2\tilde{U}_{R}n+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$})^{2}-38\omega^{2}\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}(2\tilde{U}_{R}n+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$})+5\omega^{4}\right]+\left(2\tilde{U}_{R}n\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}+\omega^{2}+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}^{2}\right)\left(2\tilde{U}_{R}n\epsilon_{\bm{k}}-\omega^{2}+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}^{2}\right)^{2}\right\}}{\left(3\gamma^{2}n^{2}+4i\gamma n\omega+2\tilde{U}_{R}n\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}-\omega^{2}+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}^{2}\right)^{2}\left[(\gamma n-i\omega)(3\gamma n-i\omega)+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}(2\tilde{U}_{R}n+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$})\right]^{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{4\left[-45\gamma^{6}n^{6}-\gamma^{4}n^{4}\left(21\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}(2\tilde{U}_{R}n+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$})-23\omega^{2}\right)\right]}{\left(3\gamma^{2}n^{2}+4i\gamma n\omega+2\tilde{U}_{R}n\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}-\omega^{2}+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}^{2}\right)^{2}\left[(\gamma n-i\omega)(3\gamma n-i\omega)+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$}(2\tilde{U}_{R}n+\text{$\epsilon_{\bm{k}}$})\right]^{2}}.

(S.236)

The above expression vanishes after intergration over
\omega
from
-\infty
to
\infty
since the residues of all poles of the integrand in the upper-half complex plane of
\omega
vanish. Thus, we can see the superfluid quantum depletion is just equal to the total quantum depletion and the normal fluid density is always zero, i.e., all the bosons belonging to the quantum depletion contribute to the superfluid transport.

Appendix S4
Derivation of The Spectral Function and the Excitation Spectrum

Here we derive an expression of the Green’s function of the dissipative superfluid. Within the mean-field approximation, the correlation-function matrix in the momentum-energy space takes the form of

\displaystyle G(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-i\left\langle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}a_{\bm{k}\bm{,+}}\\
a_{-\bm{k}\bm{,+}}^{\dagger}\\
a_{\bm{k},-}\\
a_{-\bm{k},-}^{\dagger}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{\bm{k},+}^{\dagger}&a_{-\bm{k},+}&a_{\bm{k},-}^{\dagger}&a_{-\bm{k},-}\end{array}\right)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-\frac{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n-2i\gamma n-\omega}{2}&-\frac{Un}{2}\\
-\frac{U^{\ast}n}{2}&-\frac{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n-2i\gamma n+\omega}{2}&&-2i\gamma n\\
-2i\gamma n&&\frac{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n+2i\gamma n-\omega}{2}&\frac{Un}{2}\\
&&\frac{U^{\ast}n}{2}&\frac{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n+2i\gamma n+\omega}{2}\end{array}\right)^{-1}

(see Eq. (
S.159
)). By directly calculating the inverse of the matrix, we have the following Green’s
functions
[
64
]
:

\displaystyle G^{T}(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-i\langle a_{\bm{k},+}a_{\bm{k},+}^{\dagger}\rangle

(S.248)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(\omega+U_{R}n-2i\gamma n+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}})\left[\omega^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}-2\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(U_{R}n+2i\gamma n)+\gamma n(5\gamma n-4iU_{R}n)\right]}{\omega^{4}+2\left[5\gamma^{2}n^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)\right]\omega^{2}+[\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)+3\gamma^{2}n^{2}]^{2}},

\displaystyle G^{<}(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-i\langle a_{\bm{k},+}a_{\bm{k},-}^{\dagger}\rangle

(S.249)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-8i\gamma n(U_{R}^{2}n^{2}+\gamma^{2}n^{2})}{\omega^{4}+2\left[5\gamma^{2}n^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)\right]\omega^{2}+[\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)+3\gamma^{2}n^{2}]^{2}},

\displaystyle G^{>}(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-i\langle a_{\bm{k},-}a_{\bm{k},+}^{\dagger}\rangle

(S.250)

\displaystyle\frac{-8i\gamma n\left[(\omega+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n)^{2}+4\gamma^{2}n^{2}\right]}{\omega^{4}+2\left[5\gamma^{2}n^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)\right]\omega^{2}+[\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)+3\gamma^{2}n^{2}]^{2}},

\displaystyle G^{\tilde{T}}(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-i\langle a_{\bm{k},-}a_{\bm{k},-}^{\dagger}\rangle

(S.251)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(\omega+U_{R}n+2i\gamma n+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}})\left[-\omega^{2}+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(U_{R}n-2i\gamma n)-\gamma n(5\gamma n+4iU_{R}n)\right]}{\omega^{4}+2\left[5\gamma^{2}n^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)\right]\omega^{2}+[\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)+3\gamma^{2}n^{2}]^{2}}.

To obtain the spectral function, we first consider the retarded Green’s function
G^{R}
, which is given by the relation

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}G^{K}&G^{R}\\
G^{A}&0\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&-1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}G^{T}&G^{<}\\
G^{>}&G^{\tilde{T}}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&-1\end{array}\right).

Therefore, the Keldysh, retarded, and advanced Green’s functions are
given by

\displaystyle G^{K}(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-8i\gamma n\left[(\omega+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n)^{2}+U_{R}^{2}n^{2}+5\gamma^{2}n^{2}\right]}{\omega^{4}+2(5\gamma^{2}n^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n))\omega^{2}+[\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)+3\gamma^{2}n^{2}]^{2}},

(S.261)

\displaystyle G^{R}(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(\omega+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n+2i\gamma n)}{\omega^{2}+4i\gamma n\omega-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}-2U_{R}n\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}-3\gamma^{2}n^{2}},

(S.262)

\displaystyle G^{A}(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(\omega+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n-2i\gamma n)}{\omega^{2}-4i\gamma n\omega-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}-2U_{R}n\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}-3\gamma^{2}n^{2}}=(G^{R})^{\ast}.

(S.263)

From the Green’s functions, we obtain the spectral function
as
[
2
]

\displaystyle A(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2\pi}(G^{<}+G^{>})

(S.264)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{4\gamma n\left[(\omega+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n)^{2}+U_{R}^{2}n^{2}+5\gamma^{2}n^{2}\right]}{\omega^{4}+2(5\gamma^{2}n^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n))\omega^{2}+[\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)+3\gamma^{2}n^{2}]^{2}}.

We note that when
U_{R}=0
, the spectral function becomes

\displaystyle A(\bm{k},\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{4\gamma n\left[(\omega+\varepsilon_{\bm{k}})^{2}+5\gamma^{2}n^{2}\right]}{\omega^{4}+2(5\gamma^{2}n^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2})\omega^{2}+[\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}+3\gamma^{2}n^{2}]^{2}}.

(S.265)

In the other limit
\gamma=0
, one can prove that Eq. (
S.264

\delta(\omega^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n))
, which reproduces the spectral function of a closed quantum system.

The excitation spectrum of the dissipative superfluid is given by the poles
of the spectral function as

\omega^{4}+2(5\gamma^{2}n^{2}-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n))\omega^{2}+[\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)+3\gamma^{2}n^{2}]^{2}=0.

(S.266)

This equation can be factorized as

(\omega^{2}+4i\gamma n\omega-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}-2U_{R}n\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}-3\gamma^{2}n^{2})(\omega^{2}-4i\gamma n\omega-\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}-2U_{R}n\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}-3\gamma^{2}n^{2})=0.

(S.267)

The solutions to Eq. (
S.267
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\omega_{1,2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2i\gamma n\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)-\gamma^{2}n^{2}},

(S.268)

\displaystyle\omega_{3,4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2i\gamma n\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)-\gamma^{2}n^{2}}.

(S.269)

For those momenta that satisfy
\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)<\gamma^{2}n^{2}
,
the real parts of all the spectra vanish. For those momenta that satisfy

\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2U_{R}n)>\gamma^{2}n^{2}

the real parts of
\omega_{1(2)}
are equal to those of
\omega_{3(4)}
since
\omega_{1}=\omega_{3}^{\ast}
and
\omega_{2}=\omega_{4}^{\ast}
.
Hence, we reach the conclusion that

\text{Re}[\omega_{1}]=\text{Re}[\omega_{3}]=-\text{Re}[\omega_{2}]=-\text{Re}[\omega_{4}].

(S.270)

There is only one nontrivial real part in the four spectra. The poles of the retarded Green’s
function give the spectra
\omega_{1,2}
. These two spectra indeed coincide
with the spectra of the observables
A_{\bm{k}}
in Ref.
[
51
]
. We can understand the roles of the spectra from another framework of the Schwinger-Keldysh action. We first transform the action into another basis. By defining the retarded or advanced operators
a_{\bm{k},R}=\frac{1}{2}(a_{\bm{k},+}+a_{\bm{k},-})

a_{\bm{k},A}=a_{\bm{k},+}-a_{\bm{k},-}

(S.271)

\displaystyle-H_{+}+H_{-}-4i\gamma n\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}\neq 0}a_{\bm{k}-}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k}+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k},\bm{k}\neq 0}-(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n-2i\gamma n)a_{\bm{k},A}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k},R}-(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+U_{R}n+2i\gamma n)a_{\bm{k},R}^{\dagger}a_{\bm{k},A}

\displaystyle-\frac{U^{\ast}n}{2}(a_{-\bm{k},R}a_{\bm{k},A}+a_{-\bm{k},A}a_{\bm{k},R})-\frac{Un}{2}(a_{\bm{k},R}^{\dagger}a_{-\bm{k},A}^{\dagger}+a_{\bm{k},A}^{\dagger}a_{-\bm{k},R}^{\dagger})

a_{\bm{k},A}\\

where

\displaystyle 0,

(S.277)

We examine the stability of the dissipative BEC with dipolar interactions. A BEC is unstable when one of the imaginary part of the spectra
\omega_{1,2}
is positive. For the spectra (
S.301
), they most likely become unstable for the angle
\theta_{\bm{k}}=\pi/2
, where the spectra can be rewritten as

\omega_{1,2}=-2i\gamma n\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2(U_{R}-V_{dd})n)-\gamma^{2}n^{2}},

(S.303)

where
V_{dd}:=8\pi c_{dd}/3
. The system becomes unstable if the imaginary part of
\omega_{1}
is positive. From Eq. (
S.303
), we can see that the system becomes unstable when

V_{dd}>\sqrt{3}\gamma+U_{R}

(S.304)

for
U_{R}>0
. Thus, the dissipation helps stablize the condensate since the dissipation leads to an effective repulsive interaction.

We note that in the long-wavelength limit, the spectrum
\omega_{1}
can be rewritten as

\omega_{1}\simeq-i\gamma n-iD(\bm{k})k^{2},

(S.305)

where
D(\bm{k}):=\tilde{U}_{R}/(2\gamma m)=(U_{R}-V_{dd}(\bm{k}))/(2\gamma m)
is the diffusion coefficient. The dispersion relation
-iD(\bm{k})k^{2}
represents that the propagation of excitation is diffusive. Hence, in contrast to BECs in closed systems, the speed of sound is ill-defined in the dissipative condensate.

In a two-dimensional dipolar BEC, there exist roton-like excitations if the dipoles are perpendicular to the plane
[
75
]
. Here we place the dipoles on the
x
-
y
plane. The Fourier transformation of the dipole-dipole interaction is given by

V_{dd}(\bm{k})=\int d^{3}\bm{r}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}V_{dd}(\bm{r}).

(S.306)

When the dipoles are polarized along the
z
-axis, the interaction in the momentum space is given by

V_{dd}(\bm{k})=\int d^{3}\bm{r}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{r}}\frac{c_{dd}}{r^{3}}=-2\pi c_{dd}k,

(S.307)

which leads to the roton-like excitations in the system. In the closed quantum system, the condition for an extremum of the excitation energy is given by

\frac{d}{dk}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2\tilde{U}_{R}n)}=0\Rightarrow k=\frac{1}{2}\left(3mnV\pm\sqrt{9(mnV)^{2}-8U_{R}nm}\right),

(S.308)

where
V:=2\pi c_{dd}
is the strength of the dipole-dipole
interaction and
\tilde{U}_{R}:=U_{R}-Vk
is the total interaction
[
75
]
. Thus, the roton-like excitation happens at

k_{c}=\left(3mnV+\sqrt{9(mnV)^{2}-8U_{R}nm}\right)/2.

(S.309)

Now we consider the open quantum system. The Liouvillian spectrum in the two-dimensional system is given by

\omega_{1}=-2i\gamma n\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2(U_{R}-Vk)n)-\gamma^{2}n^{2}},

(S.310)

where the real part of the excitation spectrum is
\text{Re}[\omega_{1}]=0
if

\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2\tilde{U}_{R}n)\leqslant\gamma^{2}n^{2}

\text{Re}[\omega_{1}]=\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2\tilde{U}_{R}n)-\gamma^{2}n^{2}}
if

\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}+2\tilde{U}_{R}n)>\gamma^{2}n^{2}
. By taking the derivative of the real part of the excitation spectrum with respect to
k

\varepsilon_{\bm{k}_{c}}(\varepsilon_{\bm{k}_{c}}+2\tilde{U}_{R}n)>\gamma^{2}n^{2}

\gamma^{2}n^{2}<\frac{1}{32}\left(3\sqrt{m}Vn+\sqrt{9m(nV)^{2}-8U_{R}n}\right)^{2}\left(4U_{R}n-3m(nV)^{2}-Vn\sqrt{9(mnV)^{2}-8U_{R}nm}\right).

(S.311)

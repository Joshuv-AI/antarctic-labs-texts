Rogue waves arising on the standing periodic waves in the Ablowitz-Ladik equation
Jinbing Chen, Dmitry E. Pelinovsky

Abstract
. We study the standing periodic waves in the semi-discrete integrable system modelled by the Ablowitz–Ladik equation. We have related the stability spectrum to the Lax spectrum by separating the variables and by finding the characteristic polynomial for the standing periodic waves. We have also obtained rogue waves on the background of the modulationally unstable standing periodic waves by using the end points of spectral bands and the corresponding eigenfunctions. The magnification factors for the rogue waves have been computed analytically and compared with their continuous counterparts. The main novelty of this work is that we explore a non-standard linear Lax system, which is different from the standard Lax representation of the Ablowitz–Ladik equation.



This article is dedicated to Athanassios S. Fokas on the occasion of his 70th anniversary for his many contributions to studies of integrable nonlinear PDEs and boundary-value problems.

1.
Introduction

The nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation models wave dynamics in many physical problems related to fluids, plasmas, and optics
[
21
,
32
]
. Complicated wave patterns can be expressed analytically by using exact solutions of the NLS equation for periodic and double-periodic standing waves (see review in
[
30
]
). The standing periodic waves are known to be modulationally unstable
[
17
,
18
]
and rogue waves (localized perturbations in space and time) have been observed on their backgrounds in numerical experiments
[
3
,
27
]
. Rogue waves are generated due to modulational instability of the wave background
[
16
,
19
]
. The exact solutions for rogue waves arising on the periodic
stading waves were obtained analytically
[
8
,
13
,
20
]

and confirmed experimentally for fluids and optics
[
36
]
.

It is natural to ask if the modulational instability and rogue waves persist on the standing periodic waves in the integrable discretizations of the integrable NLS equation.
This question has received much less attention
in the literature. The main purpose of our work is to answer this question
for the Ablowitz-Ladik (AL) equation
[
1
]
written
in the normalized form:

i\dot{u}_{n}+(1+|u_{n}|^{2})(u_{n+1}+u_{n-1})=0,

(1.1)

where the dot represents the derivative of
\{u_{n}(t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}

with respect to the time variable
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and
i=\sqrt{-1}
.
The continuum limit of the AL equation (
1.1
) is obtained for slowly varying wave packets of small amplitude, the leading order of which can be represented as

u_{n}(t)=\varepsilon\mathfrak{u}(\varepsilon n,\varepsilon^{2}t)e^{2it},

(1.2)

where
\varepsilon>0
is a formal small parameter. Substituting (
1.2
) into (
1.1
) and expanding

\mathfrak{u}(X\pm\varepsilon,T)
with
X=\varepsilon n
and
T=\varepsilon^{2}t
in the Taylor series in
\varepsilon
yield
at the formal order of
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})
the continuous NLS equation
in the form:

i\mathfrak{u}_{T}+\mathfrak{u}_{XX}+2|\mathfrak{u}|^{2}\mathfrak{u}=0.

(1.3)

Rogue waves on the constant-amplitude wave background have been obtained
for the AL equation (
1.1
) and related discrete equations in
[
4
,
5
,
29
,
38
]
.
They have been observed in numerical experiments
[
2
]
. Higher-order rogue waves have been studied by using the inverse scattering
method
[
14
]
. Further generalization of these rogue waves to fully discrete integrable NLS equation was recently given in
[
28
]
.
What we will develop in this work is the construction of rogue waves on the background
of discrete standing periodic waves.
The discrete periodic and double-periodic waves were obtained previously for the AL equation in
[
15
,
23
,
24
]
.

In a series of recent works, we have constructed rogue waves on the background of standing periodic waves in the continuous integrable models including the NLS equation
[
8
,
13
]
, the derivative NLS equation
[
10
,
12
]
, the modified KdV equation
[
7
,
9
]
, and the sine–Gordon equation
[
31
]
. The latest work in this series was the first construction of such rogue waves in the discrete modified KdV equation
[
11
]
. In all works, we used the nonlinearization method
[
6
,
34
]
which allowed us to characterize the standing periodic waves as the
restriction of solutions of nonlinear models as squared solutions
of the linear Lax equations.

However, we have failed to characterize the discrete standing periodic waves
by using constraints of the nonlinearization method for the AL equation
(developed in
[
22
,
35
]
) because the constraints generate difference equations which are not satisfied by the standing periodic waves. As a result, we had to develop new ideas based on separation of variables for the standing periodic waves
in the linear Lax equations. This separation of variables is similar
to the approach in
[
17
]
used to characterize the modulational stability
of standing periodic waves in the continuous NLS equation. With this approach,
we can obtain analytically the end points of spectral bands of the Lax spectrum and the corresponding eigenfunctions, which are then used to obtain
the rogue wave solutions on the background of discrete standing periodic waves.

Integrability of the AL equation can be expressed by using
two different Lax formulations. The standard formulation explored previously in the context of rogue waves on the constant-amplitude wave background
[
4
,
14
,
29
]
is irrelevant for the modulational stability and rogue waves on the standing periodic waves due to several issues: (i) the location of the Lax spectrum is different, (ii) there exists no relation between squared eigenfunctions and the linearized AL equation, and consequently, (iii) the rogue waves are not properly defined. On the other hand, we show here that the alternative formulation related to symplectic
integrable map of the nonlinearization method in
[
22
]
allows us to fix issues (i), (ii), and (iii)
and to describe properties of the rogue waves on the
standing periodic wave background.
The main novelty of our work is that our results are derived from non-standard Lax formulation of the AL equation.

Among possible applications of our main results, we can mention recent studies
of breathers and rogue waves in non-integrable discrete settings
[
33
]
.
Many numerical results are based on the homotopy continuation of breathers
and rogue waves from their integrable limits expressed by the AL equation.
With the precise construction of the standing periodic waves and their rogue waves, we can then utilize these solutions in the homotopy continuation towards
the nonintegrable versions of the discrete NLS equations.

The article is organized as follows. Section
2
presents the main results of our computations. Section
3
establishes the relation between squared eigenfunctions of the linear Lax equations and solutions of the linearized AL equation. Section
4
presents analytical and numerical approximations of the Lax and stability spectra. Section
5
gives construction of nonperiodic solutions of the linear Lax equations in terms of periodic eigenfunctions. Section
6
explains how the Darboux transformation is used to
obtain the rogue wave solutions from the nonperiodic solutions of the linear Lax equations and to study magnification factors of the rogue waves.

2.
Lax pair, standing periodic waves, and rogue waves

The AL equation (
1.1
) can be represented as the compatibility condition for a Lax pair of linear equations:

\varphi_{n+1}=U(u_{n},\lambda)\varphi_{n},\qquad\dot{\varphi}_{n}=V(u_{n},\lambda)\varphi_{n},

(2.1)

defined by the matrices

\displaystyle U(u_{n},\lambda)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|u_{n}|^{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&u_{n}\\
-\bar{u}_{n}&\lambda^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right)

and

\displaystyle\qquad V(u_{n},\lambda)=i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{-2}+u_{n}\bar{u}_{n-1}+\bar{u}_{n}u_{n-1}\right)&\lambda u_{n}-\lambda^{-1}u_{n-1}\\
-\lambda\bar{u}_{n-1}+\lambda^{-1}\bar{u}_{n}&-\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{-2}+u_{n}\bar{u}_{n-1}+\bar{u}_{n}u_{n-1}\right)\\
\end{array}\right),

where
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\mathbb{Z}}
depends on time
t
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
is a spectral parameter which is constant in
n
and
t
, and
\bar{u}_{n}(t)
denotes the complex-conjugate of
u_{n}(t)
.

Remark 1
.

There exists a simpler Lax pair,

\varphi_{n+1}=U(u_{n},\lambda)\varphi_{n},\quad U(u_{n},\lambda)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&u_{n}\\
-\bar{u}_{n}&\lambda^{-1}\\
\end{array}\right),

(2.2)

and

\dot{\varphi}_{n}=V(u_{n},\lambda)\varphi_{n},\ V(u_{n},\lambda)=i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{-2}\right)+u_{n}\bar{u}_{n-1}&\lambda u_{n}-\lambda^{-1}u_{n-1}\\
-\lambda\bar{u}_{n-1}+\lambda^{-1}\bar{u}_{n}&-\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{-2}\right)-\bar{u}_{n}u_{n-1}\\
\end{array}\right),

(2.3)

commutativity of which also yields the AL equation (
1.1
). We have found that the stability spectrum of the standing periodic waves can be characterized from the Lax spectrum associated with the linear system (
2.1
) due to the squared eigenfunction relation, however, we did not find the squared eigenfunction relation for eigenfunctions of the linear system (
2.2
)–(
2.3
).

We consider the standing wave solution of the AL equation (
1.1
) in the form:

u_{n}(t)=U_{n}e^{2i\omega t},

(2.4)

where
\{U_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
is a frequency parameter. The AL equation (
1.1
) with the standing wave reduction (
2.4
) becomes the second-order difference equation

(1+|U_{n}|^{2})(U_{n+1}+U_{n-1})=2\omega U_{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.5)

It can be integrated with two conserved quantities as in the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\{U_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be a solution of the difference equation (
2.5
). Then, the following real-valued quantities

F_{0}:=i(U_{n}\bar{U}_{n-1}-\bar{U}_{n}U_{n-1})

(2.6)

and

F_{1}:=\omega(U_{n}\bar{U}_{n-1}+\bar{U}_{n}U_{n-1})-|U_{n}|^{2}-|U_{n-1}|^{2}-|U_{n}|^{2}|U_{n-1}|^{2}

(2.7)

are constant in
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By multiplying (
2.5
) by
\bar{U}_{n}
and subtracting the complex conjugate, we obtain

(1+|U_{n}|^{2})[(U_{n+1}\bar{U}_{n}-\bar{U}_{n+1}U_{n})-(U_{n}\bar{U}_{n-1}-\bar{U}_{n}U_{n-1})]=0,

from which conservation of
F_{0}
follows.

Similarly, multiplying (
2.5
) by
\bar{U}_{n-1}
and by
\bar{U}_{n+1}
and adding the complex conjugate yield

(1+|U_{n}|^{2})|U_{n+1}|^{2}-\omega(U_{n}\bar{U}_{n+1}+\bar{U}_{n}U_{n+1})=(1+|U_{n}|^{2})|U_{n-1}|^{2}-\omega(U_{n}\bar{U}_{n-1}+\bar{U}_{n}U_{n-1}),

from which conservation of
F_{1}
follows.
∎

Periodic waves with trivial phase are given by real-valued periodic solutions of the difference equation (
2.5
) with
F_{0}=0
in (
2.6
). Two families were obtained in
[
11
]
(see also
[
23
,
24
]
), which we refer to as the
dnoidal
and
cnoidal
waves.
The dnoidal waves are given in the form:

U_{n}=\frac{{\rm sn}(\alpha;k)}{{\rm cn}(\alpha;k)}{\rm dn}(\alpha n;k),\qquad\omega=\frac{{\rm dn}(\alpha;k)}{{\rm cn}^{2}(\alpha;k)},

(2.8)

with two parameters of
\alpha\in(0,K(k))
and
k\in(0,1)
, where
k
is the elliptic modulus.

•

As
k\to 0
, the dnoidal wave degenerates to the constant-amplitude wave

U_{n}=\tan(\alpha),\qquad\omega={\rm sec}^{2}(\alpha),\qquad\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{\pi}{2}\right),

(2.9)

•

As
k\to 1
, the dnoidal wave degenerates into the solitary wave

U_{n}=\sinh(\alpha){\rm sech}(\alpha n),\qquad\omega=\cosh(\alpha),\qquad\alpha\in(0,\infty).

(2.10)

The cnoidal waves are given in the form:

U_{n}=\frac{k{\rm sn}(\alpha;k)}{{\rm dn}(\alpha;k)}{\rm cn}(\alpha n;k),\qquad\omega=\frac{{\rm cn}(\alpha;k)}{{\rm dn}^{2}(\alpha;k)},

(2.11)

with two parameters of
\alpha\in(0,2K(k))
and
k\in(0,1)
. The limit
k\to 0
gives the trivial solution, whereas the limit
k\to 1
gives the same solitary wave (
2.10
).

The standing waves in the limit
\alpha\to 0
recover the asymptotic approximation (
1.2
) with
\varepsilon=\alpha
.
Since

\displaystyle{\rm sn}(\alpha;k)=\alpha+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{3}),\quad{\rm cn}(\alpha;k)=1-\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4}),\quad{\rm dn}(\alpha;k)=1-\frac{1}{2}k^{2}\alpha^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{4}),

the dnoidal and cnoidal waves of the AL equation (
1.1
) yield solutions

\mathfrak{u}(X,T)=e^{i(2-k^{2})T}{\rm dn}(X;k),\qquad\mathfrak{u}(X,T)=e^{i(2k^{2}-1)T}k{\rm cn}(X;k),

which are the dnoidal and cnoidal waves of the continuous NLS equation (
1.3
).

Remark 2
.

Since the continuous NLS equation (
1.3
) also has a family of the standing periodic waves with nontrivial phase
[
13
,
17
]
, we conjecture that there exist complex-valued periodic solutions of the difference equation (
2.5
) with
F_{0}\neq 0
in (
2.6
). However, such solutions are not considered in our work.

3.
Spectral stability of standing periodic waves

Here we will set up the spectral stability problem for the standing periodic waves and relate its eigenfunctions with squared eigenfunctions of the linear
system (
2.1
).

The spectral stability of the standing waves of the form (
2.4
) in the time evolution of the AL equation (
1.1
) can be studied by adding a perturbation of the form

u_{n}(t)=e^{2i\omega t}[U_{n}+v_{n}(t)].

(3.1)

Substituting (
3.1
) into the AL equation (
1.1
) and truncating at the linear terms in
v_{n}
gives rise to the linearized AL equation

i\dot{v}_{n}-2\omega v_{n}+(1+|U_{n}|^{2})(v_{n+1}+v_{n-1})+(U_{n+1}+U_{n-1})(U_{n}\bar{v}_{n}+\bar{U}_{n}v_{n})=0

(3.2)

and its complex conjugate equation

-i\dot{\bar{v}}_{n}-2\omega\bar{v}_{n}+(1+|U_{n}|^{2})(\bar{v}_{n+1}+\bar{v}_{n-1})+(\bar{U}_{n+1}+\bar{U}_{n-1})(\bar{U}_{n}v_{n}+U_{n}\bar{v}_{n})=0.

Separation of variables with
v_{n}(t)=V_{n}e^{\Lambda t}

and
\bar{v}_{n}(t)=\tilde{V}_{n}e^{\Lambda t}
, where
\tilde{V}_{n}
is no longer a complex conjugate of
V_{n}
if
\Lambda\notin\mathbb{R}
,
gives the spectral stability problem in the form:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\Lambda V_{n}-2\omega V_{n}+(1+|U_{n}|^{2})(V_{n+1}+V_{n-1})+(U_{n+1}+U_{n-1})(U_{n}\tilde{V}_{n}+\bar{U}_{n}V_{n})=0,\\
-i\Lambda\tilde{V}_{n}-2\omega\tilde{V}_{n}+(1+|U_{n}|^{2})(\tilde{V}_{n+1}+\tilde{V}_{n-1})+(\bar{U}_{n+1}+\bar{U}_{n-1})(\bar{U}_{n}V_{n}+U_{n}\tilde{V}_{n})=0.\end{array}\right.

(3.3)

Solutions of the spectral stability problem (
3.3
) are computed by using the squared eigenfunctions of the linear system (
2.1
).

Let us separate the variables for solutions
\varphi_{n}=(p_{n},q_{n})^{\mathrm{T}}
of the linear system (
2.1
) by using

p_{n}(t)=P_{n}(t)e^{i\omega t},\qquad q_{n}(t)=Q_{n}(t)e^{-i\omega t}.

(3.4)

Substituting (
2.4
) and (
3.4
) into (
2.1
) yields the following linear system:

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}P_{n+1}\\
Q_{n+1}\\
\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|U_{n}|^{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&U_{n}\\
-\bar{U}_{n}&\lambda^{-1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}P_{n}\\
Q_{n}\\
\end{array}\right),

(3.5)

and

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{P}_{n}\\
{Q}_{n}\\
\end{array}\right)=i\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}W_{n}-\omega&\lambda U_{n}-\lambda^{-1}U_{n-1}\\
-\lambda\bar{U}_{n-1}+\lambda^{-1}\bar{U}_{n}&\omega-W_{n}\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}P_{n}\\
Q_{n}\\
\end{array}\right),

(3.6)

where
W_{n}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{-2}+U_{n}\bar{U}_{n-1}+\bar{U}_{n}U_{n-1}\right)
.

The following lemma presents the squared eigenfunction relation between
solutions of the linearized AL equation (
3.2
)
and the squared eigenfunctions of the linear system (
3.5
)–(
3.6
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
\{(P_{n}(t),Q_{n}(t))^{\mathrm{T}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a classical solution of the linear system (
3.5
)–(
3.6
) with an arbitrary
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and a solution
\{U_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
of the difference equation (
2.5
). Then,
\{v_{n}(t)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
given by

v_{n}=\lambda P_{n}^{2}-\bar{\lambda}^{-1}\bar{Q}_{n}^{2}+U_{n}(P_{n}Q_{n}+\bar{P}_{n}\bar{Q}_{n}),\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}

(3.7)

is a classical solution of the linearized AL equation (
3.2
).

Proof.

It follows from (
3.5
) that

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}P_{n-1}\\
Q_{n-1}\\
\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|U_{n-1}|^{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda^{-1}&-U_{n-1}\\
\bar{U}_{n-1}&\lambda\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}P_{n}\\
Q_{n}\\
\end{array}\right).

(3.8)

By using (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle i\dot{v}_{n}

\displaystyle=2\omega v_{n}-P_{n}^{2}(\lambda^{3}+\lambda^{-1}+\lambda\bar{U}_{n}U_{n-1}+\lambda^{-1}|U_{n}|^{2})-Q_{n}^{2}(\lambda U_{n}^{2}-\lambda^{-1}U_{n}U_{n-1})

\displaystyle\quad+P_{n}Q_{n}\left[-2\lambda^{2}U_{n}+U_{n-1}-U_{n+1}-|U_{n}|^{2}(U_{n+1}+U_{n-1})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\bar{P}_{n}^{2}(\bar{\lambda}^{-1}U_{n}^{2}-\bar{\lambda}U_{n}U_{n-1})+\bar{Q}_{n}^{2}\left(\bar{\lambda}+\bar{\lambda}^{-3}+\bar{\lambda}^{-1}\bar{U}_{n}U_{n-1}+\bar{\lambda}|U_{n}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\bar{P}_{n}\bar{Q}_{n}\left[-2\bar{\lambda}^{-2}U_{n}+U_{n-1}-U_{n+1}-|U_{n}|^{2}(U_{n+1}+U_{n-1})\right].

By using (
3.5
) and (
3.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle(1+|U_{n}|^{2})v_{n+1}

\displaystyle=P_{n}^{2}(\lambda^{3}-\lambda U_{n+1}\bar{U}_{n})+P_{n}Q_{n}\left[2\lambda^{2}U_{n}+U_{n+1}(1-|U_{n}|^{2})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+Q_{n}^{2}(\lambda U_{n}^{2}+\lambda^{-1}U_{n}U_{n+1})-\bar{P}_{n}^{2}(\bar{\lambda}^{-1}U_{n}^{2}+\bar{\lambda}U_{n}U_{n+1})

\displaystyle\quad+\bar{P}_{n}\bar{Q}_{n}\left[2\bar{\lambda}^{-2}U_{n}+U_{n+1}(1-|U_{n}|^{2})\right]+\bar{Q}_{n}^{2}(\bar{\lambda}^{-1}U_{n+1}\bar{U}_{n}-\bar{\lambda}^{-3})

and

\displaystyle v_{n-1}=\lambda^{-1}P_{n}^{2}-\bar{\lambda}\bar{Q}_{n}^{2}-U_{n-1}(P_{n}Q_{n}+\bar{P}_{n}\bar{Q}_{n}).

By using (
3.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle U_{n}\bar{v}_{n}+\bar{U}_{n}v_{n}=\lambda\bar{U}_{n}P_{n}^{2}-\lambda^{-1}U_{n}Q_{n}^{2}+\bar{\lambda}U_{n}\bar{P}_{n}^{2}-\bar{\lambda}^{-1}\bar{U}_{n}\bar{Q}_{n}^{2}+2|U_{n}|^{2}(P_{n}Q_{n}+\bar{P}_{n}\bar{Q}_{n}).

When these expressions are substituted into the linearized AL equation (
3.2
), all terms cancel out after direct computations.
∎

Here we construct the Lax spectrum and the stability spectrum for the constant-amplitude wave (

(

4.1.
Constant-amplitude wave

We set
U_{n}=A
with
A=\tan(\alpha)
and
\omega={\rm sec}^{2}(\alpha)

for
\alpha\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
as follows from (
2.9
). The following lemma gives the exact location of the Lax spectrum and the stability spectrum for the constant-amplitude wave.

Lemma 4
.

The Lax spectrum
\lambda
of the spectral problem (
3.5
) with
U_{n}=\tan(\alpha)
and
\omega={\rm sec}^{2}(\alpha)
consists of the unit circle in
\mathbb{C}
and two segments on the real axis:

[-\sqrt{\omega}-\sqrt{\omega-1},-\sqrt{\omega}+\sqrt{\omega-1}]\cup[\sqrt{\omega}-\sqrt{\omega-1},\sqrt{\omega}+\sqrt{\omega-1}].

The stability spectrum
\Lambda
of the spectral problem (
3.3
) consists of the segment
[-2(\omega-1),2(\omega-1)]
on the real axis and the segment
[-4i\sqrt{\omega},4i\sqrt{\omega}]
on the purely imaginary axis.

Proof.

We solve the spectral problem (
3.5
) with constant
U_{n}=A
by using the band-limited Fourier transform

P_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{P}(\theta)e^{i\theta n}d\theta,\quad Q_{n}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\hat{Q}(\theta)e^{i\theta n}d\theta.

A nontrivial solution for
(\hat{P}(\theta),\hat{Q}(\theta))^{\mathrm{T}}
exists if and only if the following characteristic equation is satisfied:

\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}\sqrt{1+A^{2}}e^{i\theta}-\lambda&-A\\
A&\sqrt{1+A^{2}}e^{i\theta}-\lambda^{-1}\end{array}\right|=0.

This yields

\sqrt{1+A^{2}}e^{i\theta}\left[2\sqrt{1+A^{2}}\cos(\theta)-\lambda-\lambda^{-1}\right]=0,

which is equivalent to
z:=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}=2\sqrt{1+A^{2}}\cos(\theta)=2\sqrt{\omega}\cos(\theta)
. When
\theta=0
, two roots of the quadratic equation
\lambda+\lambda^{-1}=2\sqrt{\omega}
are given by
\lambda=\sqrt{\omega}+\sqrt{\omega-1}
and
\lambda=\sqrt{\omega}-\sqrt{\omega-1}
. When
\theta
changes in the interval
[0,2\pi]
, the two roots of the quadratic equation cover the two segments on the real axis and the unit circle in
\mathbb{C}
.

The stability spectrum (
3.3
) with constant
U_{n}=A
is written in the form:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}i\Lambda V_{n}-2\omega V_{n}+(1+A^{2})(V_{n+1}+V_{n-1})+2A^{2}(V_{n}+\tilde{V}_{n})=0,\\
-i\Lambda\tilde{V}_{n}-2\omega\tilde{V}_{n}+(1+A^{2})(\tilde{V}_{n+1}+\tilde{V}_{n-1})+2A^{2}(V_{n}+\tilde{V}_{n})=0.\end{array}\right.

Using the band-limited Fourier transform with parameter
2\theta
instead of
\theta
, we obtain a non-trivial solution for
(\hat{V}(\theta),\hat{\tilde{V}}(\theta))^{\mathrm{T}}
if and only if the following characterstic equation is satisfied:

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{cc}2(1+A^{2})\cos(2\theta)-2\omega+2A^{2}+i\Lambda&2A^{2}\\
2A^{2}&2(1+A^{2})\cos(2\theta)-2\omega+2A^{2}-i\Lambda\end{array}\right|=0,

which implies for
\Lambda=2\Omega
that

\displaystyle\Omega=\pm i\sqrt{((1+A^{2})\cos(2\theta)+A^{2}-\omega)^{2}-A^{4}}.

(4.1)

Since
\omega=1+A^{2}
, we obtain that

\Omega=\pm 2i\sqrt{\omega}\sin(\theta)\sqrt{1-\omega\cos^{2}(\theta)}.

When
\theta\in[0,\arccos(\omega^{-1/2})]
, the values of
\Omega
cover the segment
[-(\omega-1),(\omega-1)]
on the real axis, where the maximal value is found from the extremal value of
\theta\mapsto\sin(\theta)\sqrt{\omega\cos^{2}(\theta)-1}
located at
\theta_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\arccos(\omega^{-1})
, for which

\sin\theta_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{\omega-1}}{\sqrt{2\omega}},\qquad\cos\theta_{0}=\frac{\sqrt{\omega+1}}{\sqrt{2\omega}}.

When
\theta\in[\arccos(\omega^{-1/2}),\frac{\pi}{2}]
, the values of
\Omega
cover the segment
[-2i\sqrt{\omega},2i\sqrt{\omega}]
on the purely imaginary axis, where the maximal value is attained at
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
. This yields the result for
\Lambda
since
\Lambda=2\Omega
.
∎

Remark 8
.

The explicit expression (
4.1
) for
\Omega
is consistent with the representation (
3.12
) for
F_{1}=A^{4}=(1-\omega)^{2}
and

since

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{i}{2}\sqrt{\lambda^{4}-4\omega\lambda^{2}+2(1+2\omega^{2}-2F_{1})-4\omega\lambda^{-2}+\lambda^{-4}}

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{i}{2}\sqrt{z^{4}-4(1+\omega)z^{2}+4(1+2\omega+\omega^{2}-F_{1})}

\displaystyle=\pm 2i\sqrt{\omega}\sqrt{\omega\cos^{4}(\theta)-(1+\omega)\cos^{2}(\theta)+1}

\displaystyle=\pm 2i\sqrt{\omega}\sin(\theta)\sqrt{1-\omega\cos^{2}(\theta)},

4.1

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{i}{2}(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{1}^{-2}+\bar{\lambda}_{1}^{2}+\bar{\lambda}_{1}^{-2}+2(u_{n}\bar{u}_{n-1}+\bar{u}_{n}u_{n-1}))p_{n}\bar{q}_{n}).

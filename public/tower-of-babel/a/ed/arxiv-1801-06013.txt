Inverse of Infinite Hankel Moment Matrices This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Orthogonal Polynomials, Special Functions and Applications (OPSFA14). The full collection is available at https://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/OPSFA2017.html
Christian BERG † and Ryszard SZWARC ‡

Abstract
Let ( s n ) n ≥ 0 (s_{n})_{n\geq 0} denote an indeterminate Hamburger moment sequence and let ℋ = { s m + n } \mathcal{H}=\{s_{m+n}\} be the corresponding positive definite Hankel matrix. We consider the question if there exists an infinite symmetric matrix 𝒜 = { a j , k } \mathcal{A}=\{a_{j,k}\} , which is an inverse of ℋ \mathcal{H} in the sense that the matrix product 𝒜 ​ ℋ \mathcal{A}\mathcal{H} is defined by absolutely convergent series and 𝒜 ​ ℋ \mathcal{A}\mathcal{H} equals the identity matrix ℐ \mathcal{I} , a property called (aci). A candidate for 𝒜 \mathcal{A} is the coefficient matrix of the reproducing kernel of the moment problem, considered as an entire function of two complex variables. We say that the moment problem has property (aci), if (aci) holds for this matrix 𝒜 \mathcal{A} . We show that this is true for many classical indeterminate moment problems but not for the symmetrized version of a cubic birth-and-death process studied by Valent and co-authors. We consider mainly symmetric indeterminate moment problems and give a number of sufficient conditions for (aci) to hold in terms of the recurrence coefficients for the orthonormal polynomials. A sufficient condition is a rapid increase of the recurrence coefficients in the sense that the quotient between consecutive terms is uniformly bounded by a constant strictly smaller than one. We also give a simple example, where (aci) holds, but an inverse matrix of ℋ \mathcal{H} is highly non-unique.

Introduction and summary of results

Consider a Hamburger moment sequence
(s_{n})
given as

\displaystyle s_{n}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x^{n}{\rm d}\mu(x),\qquad n\geq 0,

where
\mu
is a probability measure with infinite support and moments of any order. The corresponding orthonormal polynomials
P_{n}
, obtained from the sequence of monomials
x^{n}
by the Gram–Schmidt procedure, satisfy
P_{0}=1
and

\displaystyle xP_{n}(x)=b_{n}P_{n+1}(x)+a_{n}P_{n}(x)+b_{n-1}P_{n-1}(x),\qquad n\geq 0,

(1.1)

where
b_{n}>0,

a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
with the convention
b_{-1}=1
,
P_{-1}=0
. For
n\geq 0
we have
\deg P_{n}=n
and the leading coefficient of
P_{n}
is equal to

\displaystyle{1\over b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}}.

(1.2)

We are interested in the so-called indeterminate case, when the moments
s_{n}
do not determine the measure
\mu
uniquely. Such measures must have unbounded support. In particular the numbers
s_{2n}
must grow faster than any exponential.

The infinite Hankel matrix
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{m,n}\}

\displaystyle h_{m,n}=s_{m+n}=\big\langle x^{m},x^{n}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)},\qquad m,n\geq 0

is positive definite in the sense that

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}v,v)=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}s_{m+n}v_{n}\overline{v}_{m}>0,\qquad v\in\mathcal{F}_{c}(\mathbb{N}_{0}),\qquad v\neq 0,

(1.3)

where
\mathcal{F}_{c}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
denotes the set of complex sequences
v=(v_{n})_{n\geq 0}
, with only finitely many non-zero entries.

Berg, Chen and Ismail
[
3
]
showed that the quadratic form associated with the matrix
\mathcal{H}
is bounded below, i.e., for a suitable constant
C>0

\displaystyle(\mathcal{H}v,v)\geq C\|v\|^{2},\qquad v\in\mathcal{F}_{c}(\mathbb{N}_{0}),

if and only if the moment problem associated with
(s_{n})
is indeterminate.

Hamburger obtained a related characterization of indeterminacy in terms of the quadratic forms associated with the matrices
\{s_{j+k}\}
and
\{s_{j+k+2}\}
, see
[
23
, p. 70]
. For a comparison between Hamburger’s result and the result of Berg–Chen–Ismail, see
[
3
]
.

Yafaev
[
25
]
contains a characterization of closability of the Hankel form (
1.3
) leading to the determinate case.

The smallest eigenvalue
\lambda_{N}
of the truncated matrix
\mathcal{H}_{N}=\{s_{m+n}\}_{0\leq m,n\leq N}
has been studied in a number of papers starting with Szegő and Widom–Wilf, see
[
7
]
and references therein.

The inverse of
\mathcal{H}_{N}
can simply be described as the matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(N)}=\{a_{j,k}(N)\}_{0\leq j,k\leq N},

where

\displaystyle K_{N}(z,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}P_{n}(z)P_{n}(w)=\sum_{j,k=0}^{N}a_{j,k}(N)z^{j}w^{k}.

(1.4)

A proof and historical details can be found in
[
7
, Theorem 1.1]
.

In the indeterminate case it is possible to let
N\to\infty
in (
1.4
) and obtain the infinite matrix
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{j,k}\}
with
a_{j,k}=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}a_{j,k}(N)
as coefficient matrix of the entire function of two complex variables

\displaystyle K(z,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(z)P_{n}(w)=\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}a_{j,k}z^{j}w^{k},

called the reproducing kernel of the indeterminate moment problem.

The coefficients of
\mathcal{H}
can grow fast, but on the other hand the coefficients of
\mathcal{A}
decay fast, so it is natural to examine if it is possible to let
N
tend to infinity in the equations

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_{i,k}(N)s_{k+j}=\delta_{i,j},\qquad 0\leq i,j\leq N,

and to obtain the infinite matrix equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{I},

(1.5)

i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a_{i,k}s_{k+j}=\delta_{i,j},\qquad i,j\geq 0,

(1.6)

meaning that all the above series are convergent with the given sum.
Notice that since both matrices
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{A}
are symmetric, it is enough to prove
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{I}
in (
1.5
).

Since absolute convergence is often more easy to establish than convergence, we investigate the question if the series in (
1.6
) are absolutely convergent for all
i
,
j
.

For the Stieltjes–Wigert moment sequence (sometimes called log-normal moments)

\displaystyle s_{n}=q^{-\tfrac{1}{2}(n+1)^{2}},\qquad 0<q<1,

it was shown that (
1.5
) holds and all the series in (
1.6
) are absolutely convergent, see
[
7
, Section 5]
. After this was done the same results were established but not published for several other known indeterminate moment problems, and we began to search for general theorems about (
1.5
). In this paper we present the general results obtained so far. At present we have no applications of equation (
1.5
).

If
\mathcal{H}
is replaced by an infinite positive definite Toeplitz matrix
M
, the question of existence of a classical inverse of
M
in the sense of (
1.5
) has been studied in
[
15
]
.

The sequence
c_{k}:=\sqrt{a_{k,k}}
plays a crucial role in our investigations. It decays quickly to zero in the sense that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}k\root k \of{c_{k}}=0
, see (
2.13
).

Concerning the matrix
\mathcal{A}
, it was shown in
[
7
, Section 4]
that it is of trace class, and that

\displaystyle\tr\mathcal{A}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\big|P_{k}\big({\rm e}^{{\rm i}t}\big)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t.

In the indeterminate case the infinite Hankel matrix
\mathcal{H}
does not correspond to an operator on
\ell^{2}
defined on the subspace spanned by the standard orthonormal basis
(\delta_{n})_{n\geq 0}
in
\ell^{2}
, where
\delta_{n}=(\delta_{n,m})_{m\geq 0}
.

In fact, by a theorem of Carleman we necessarily have
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}s_{2n}^{-1/(2n)}<\infty
, hence
s_{2n}\geq 1
for
n
sufficiently large, and therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}s_{n+m}^{2}=\infty\qquad\mbox{for all}\ n.

In connection with the study of (
1.5
) we introduce the following:

Definition 1.1
.

An indeterminate moment problem has property

(c)

if all the series in (
1.6
) are convergent,

(ac)

if all the series in (
1.6
) are absolutely convergent,

(aci)

if it has property (ac) and (
1.5
) holds,

(cs)

if
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{2n}s_{2n}<\infty
,

(cs*)

if
\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}c_{l}|s_{l+m}|<\infty
for all
m\geq 0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For an indeterminate moment problem the following holds:

(i)

(cs*)

\implies

(aci)

\implies

(ac)

\implies

(c)
,

(ii)

(cs*)

\implies

(cs)
.

Concerning (i), the claim (cs*)
\implies
(aci) is proved in Lemma
2.1
, the rest is obvious. To see (ii) we put
m=0
in (cs*) and get

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{2n}s_{2n}\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}c_{l}|s_{l}|<\infty.

We shall mainly focus on the case of symmetric moment problems, where the central recurrence coefficients
a_{n}=0
for all
n
, and the moment problem is characterized by the sequence
(b_{n})
.

Remark 1.3
.

We are not able to decide if some of these implications can be reversed. Note however, that for Stieltjes problems and for symmetric moment problems the properties (ac) and (aci) are equivalent, cf. Propositions
2.4
and
2.5
.

We next define some classes of sequences
(b_{n})
which will be considered in connection with the properties of Definition
1.1
.

Definition 1.4
.

A sequence
(b_{n})
of positive numbers is called eventually log-convex if there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle b_{n}^{2}\leq b_{n-1}b_{n+1},\qquad n\geq n_{0},

and it is called eventually log-concave if the reverse inequality holds. For
n_{0}=1
we drop “eventually”.

The following lemma is given in
[
8
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
(b_{n})
be an unbounded positive sequence which is either eventually log-convex or eventually log-concave. Then
(b_{n})
is eventually strictly increasing to infinity.

The following concept is crucial for several results.

Definition 1.6
.

A sequence
(b_{n})
of positive numbers is called eventually
q
-increasing, if there exist a number
0<q<1
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}\leq q,\qquad n\geq n_{0}.

(1.7)

For
n_{0}=1
we drop “eventually”.

Note that if
(b_{n})
is unbounded and eventually log-convex, then it is eventually
q
-increasing with
q=b_{n_{0}-1}/b_{n_{0}}<1
, where
n_{0}
is so large that
(b_{n})
is log-convex and strictly increasing for
n\geq n_{0}
.

We recall that an indeterminate moment problem has an order
\rho
satisfying
0\leq\rho\leq 1
. This is the common order of the four functions of the Nevanlinna matrix, see
[
5
]
.

Proposition 1.7
.

(i)

The symmetric moment problem for an eventually
q
-increasing sequence
(b_{n})
is indeterminate and of order
0
.

(ii)

The symmetric moment problem for a positive sequence
(b_{n})
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}\leq{\rm e}^{-\beta/n},\qquad n\geq n_{0},\qquad\beta>1,

(1.8)

is indeterminate of order
\leq 1/\beta
.

Proof.

Let
(Q_{n})
denote the sequence of polynomials of the second kind. By
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
, it suffices to prove that
\big(P_{n}^{2}(0)\big),\big(Q_{n}^{2}(0)\big)\in\ell^{\alpha}
for all
0<\alpha\leq 1
in the first case and for all
\alpha>1/\beta
in the second case.

By the symmetry assumption we know that
P_{2n+1}(0)=Q_{2n}(0)=0
and by
[
8
, Remark 4.5]
we have

\displaystyle P_{2n}(0)=(-1)^{n}\frac{b_{0}b_{2}\cdots b_{2n-2}}{b_{1}b_{3}\cdots b_{2n-1}},\qquad Q_{2n+1}(0)=(-1)^{n}\frac{b_{1}b_{3}\cdots b_{2n-1}}{b_{0}b_{2}\cdots b_{2n}}.

(1.9)

For
n>n_{0}
such that (
1.7
) holds, we have

\displaystyle P_{2n}^{2}(0)\leq\left(\frac{b_{0}b_{2}\cdots b_{2n_{0}-2}}{b_{1}b_{3}\cdots b_{2n_{0}-1}}\right)^{2}q^{2(n-n_{0})},\qquad Q_{2n+1}^{2}(0)\leq\left(\frac{b_{1}b_{3}\cdots b_{2n_{0}-1}}{b_{0}b_{2}\cdots b_{2n_{0}}}\right)^{2}q^{2(n-n_{0})},

and the first assertion follows.

For
n>n_{0}
such that (
1.8
) holds, we have

\displaystyle P_{2n}^{2}(0)\leq\left(\frac{b_{0}b_{2}\cdots b_{2n_{0}-2}}{b_{1}b_{3}\cdots b_{2n_{0}-1}}\right)^{2}\exp\left(-2\beta\sum_{j=n_{0}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2j+1}\right),

and a similar inequality for
Q_{2n+1}^{2}(0)
. Using the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{j=n_{0}}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2j+1}\geq\int_{n_{0}}^{n}\frac{{\rm d}x}{2x+1}=\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{2n+1}{2n_{0}+1},

we get

\displaystyle P_{2n}^{2}(0)\leq\frac{C}{(2n+1)^{\beta}}

for a suitable constant
C
, and the second assertion follows.
∎

The following examples have
q
-increasing coefficients
b_{n}
.

Example 1.8
.

The discrete
q
-Hermite polynomials of type II are given by

\displaystyle x\tilde{h}_{n}(x;q)=\tilde{h}_{n+1}(x;q)+q^{-2n+1}\big(1-q^{n}\big)\tilde{h}_{n-1}(x;q),

cf.
[
20
]
, where
0<q<1
. This is the monic version and the corresponding coefficients for the orthonormal version are

\displaystyle b_{n}=q^{-n-1/2}\sqrt{1-q^{n+1}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}=q\sqrt{\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q^{n+1}}}<q,

so
(b_{n})
is
q
-increasing and log-concave.

It is known that
s_{2n}=\big(q;q^{2}\big)_{n}q^{-n^{2}}
, where the shifted factorials are defined in (
3.10
).

In
[
14
]
Christiansen and Ismail studied symmetric Al-Salam–Chihara polynomials of type II given by the recursion

\displaystyle 2xp^{n}Q_{n}(x;\beta)=\big(1-p^{n+1}\big)Q_{n+1}(x;\beta)+\big(\beta+p^{n-1}\big)Q_{n-1}(x;\beta),

where
0<p<1
,
\beta\geq 0
. The recurrence coefficients for the orthonormal version are

\displaystyle b_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}p^{-n-1/2}\big[\big(1-p^{n+1}\big)\big(\beta+p^{n}\big)\big]^{1/2}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}=p\left[\frac{1-p^{n}}{1-p^{n+1}}\frac{\beta+p^{n-1}}{\beta+p^{n}}\right]^{1/2}\leq p\left[\frac{\beta+1}{\beta+p}\right]^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{p},

so
(b_{n})
is
\sqrt{p}
-increasing.

One can prove that
(b_{n})
is eventually log-convex when
0<\beta<1/p
and eventually log-concave when
\beta\geq 1/p
or
\beta=0
. The case
\beta=0
yields the
p^{-1}
-Hermite polynomials studied by Ismail and Masson in
[
19
]
.

and

from (

,

\displaystyle k(\alpha):=4^{-\alpha}\exp(2u(\alpha)+v(\alpha)),\qquad\alpha>1,

which are motivated by Theorems

Theorem 1.9
.

Consider a symmetric moment problem given in terms of an eventually

-increasing sequence

, cf. Definition

. We then have

(i)

(U_{n})
,

\big(c_{n}\sqrt{s_{2n}}\big)

(ii)

.

We next relax the condition of
(b_{n})
being eventually

-increasing.

Theorem 1.10
.

Consider a symmetric moment problem given in terms of
(b_{n})
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}\leq{\rm e}^{-f(n)},\qquad n\geq n_{0},

where
f(n)>0
for

and
\alpha:=\liminf\limits_{n\to\infty}nf(n)\in[0,\infty]
.

We then have

(i)

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\root n \of{U_{n}}\leq{\rm e}^{2u(\alpha)}
,

,

(ii)

\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\root n \of{V_{n}}\leq{\rm e}^{v(\alpha)/2}

, which in turn holds for

,

, and

.

2
Preliminaries

In this section we shall summarize some notation and results from our papers
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
, which will be needed in the following. We use the following notation for the orthonormal polynomials

\displaystyle P_{n}(x)=b_{n,n}x^{n}+b_{n-1,n}x^{n-1}+\cdots+b_{1,n}x+b_{0,n},

\displaystyle x^{n}=c_{n,n}P_{n}(x)+c_{n-1,n}P_{n-1}(x)+\cdots+c_{1,n}P_{1}(x)+c_{0,n}P_{0}(x).

(2.1)

By (
1.2
) we get

\displaystyle b_{n,n}={1\over b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}},\qquad c_{n,n}=b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}.

(2.2)

The matrices
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{i,j}\}
and
\mathcal{C}=\{c_{i,j}\}
with the assumption

\displaystyle b_{i,j}=c_{i,j}=0\qquad\text{for}\quad i>j

are upper-triangular. Since
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are transition matrices between two sequences of linearly independent systems of functions, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{I}.

Observe that (for say
m\leq n
)

\displaystyle s_{m+n}=h_{m,n}=\big\langle x^{m},x^{n}\big\rangle_{L^{2}(\mu)}=c_{0,m}c_{0,n}+c_{1,m}c_{1,n}+\cdots+c_{m,m}c_{m,n}=\big(\mathcal{C}^{t}\mathcal{C}\big)_{m,n},

so
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{C}^{t}\mathcal{C}
.

Hence we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{B}\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{B}\big(\mathcal{B}^{t}\mathcal{C}^{t}\mathcal{C}\big)=\mathcal{B}\big(\mathcal{B}^{t}\mathcal{H}\big).

(2.3)

The multiplication is well defined as
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{C}
are upper-triangular matrices and
\mathcal{B}^{t}
,
\mathcal{C}^{t}
are lower-triangular, so we are always dealing with finite sums in the matrix products.

Let us now assume that the moment problem is indeterminate. We recall from
[
7
, Proposition 4.2]
that
\mathcal{B}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt matrix and that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{B}\mathcal{B}^{t},

(2.4)

This means

\displaystyle a_{k,l}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=\max(k,l)}b_{k,n}b_{l,n},

(2.5)

hence

\displaystyle a_{k,l}^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}b_{k,n}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{n=l}^{\infty}b_{l,n}^{2}\right).

Let us recall some of the arguments leading to these results. Because of indeterminacy it
is known that the series

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|P_{n}(z)|^{2}

is convergent uniformly for
z
in bounded subsets of
\mathbb{C}
. Moreover, the sum has subexponential growth by a result of M. Riesz, cf.
[
1
, p. 56]
, i.e., it is dominated by a multiple of
{\rm e}^{\varepsilon|z|}
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Fixing
r>0
, we have

\displaystyle P_{n}\big(r{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t}\big)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k,n}r^{k}{\rm e}^{{\rm i}tk}.

By Parseval’s identity we obtain

\displaystyle{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\big|P_{n}\big(r{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t}\big)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k,n}^{2}r^{2k}.

Next

\displaystyle{1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big|P_{n}\big(r{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t}\big)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{k,n}^{2}r^{2k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}r^{2k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}b_{k,n}^{2}.

Therefore, denoting

\displaystyle c_{k}=\left(\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}b_{k,n}^{2}\right)^{1/2}=\sqrt{a_{k,k}},

(2.6)

we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_{k}^{2}r^{2k}={1\over 2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\big|P_{n}\big(r{\rm e}^{{\rm i}t}\big)\big|^{2}{\rm d}t.

(For
r=1
this proves that
\mathcal{B}
is Hilbert–Schmidt, and in particular the series in (
2.5
) is absolutely convergent.)

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider an indeterminate moment problem. If

\displaystyle\sum_{n,l=0}^{\infty}|b_{k,n}b_{l,n}s_{l+m}|<\infty\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad k,m\geq 0,

(2.7)

then property
(aci)
from Definition
1.1
holds.

For (
2.7
) to hold it suffices that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}c_{l}|s_{l+m}|<\infty\qquad\mbox{for all}\quad m\geq 0.

(2.8)

In particular
(cs*)
implies
(aci)
.

Proof.

We first notice that (
2.7
) implies (ac) because of (
2.5
). We next note that (
2.7
) implies

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\big(\mathcal{B}^{t}\mathcal{H}\big)=\big(\mathcal{B}\mathcal{B}^{t}\big)\mathcal{H},

so by (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{B}\big(\mathcal{B}^{t}\mathcal{H}\big)=\big(\mathcal{B}\mathcal{B}^{t}\big)\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{A}\mathcal{H}.

Suppose next that (
2.8
) holds. By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{n,l=0}^{\infty}|b_{k,n}b_{l,n}s_{l+m}|\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}c_{k}c_{l}|s_{l+m}|<\infty,

where we have used (
2.6
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

It is tempting to use
|s_{l+m}|\leq\sqrt{s_{2l}}\sqrt{s_{2m}}
to assert that the conditions of Lemma
2.1
are satisfied if
\sum c_{l}\sqrt{s_{2l}}<\infty
. This is not possible because
c_{l}\sqrt{s_{2l}}\geq 1
by Lemma
2.6
.

We shall mainly be concerned about (
1.5
) in the symmetric case, characterized by
a_{n}\equiv 0
, but since there is a one-to-one correspondence between Stieltjes moment problems and symmetric moment problems, we shall first compare (
1.5
) in the two cases.

Suppose

\displaystyle t_{n}=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}{\rm d}\nu(t),\qquad n\geq 0

is a Stieltjes moment sequence of a probability measure
\nu
on
[0,\infty[
with infinite support. The corresponding orthonormal polynomials
(P_{n})
satisfy (
1.1
), where now
a_{n}>0
. Let
(R_{n})
denote the orthonormal polynomials with respect to the measure
t{\rm d}\nu(t)
. Then

\displaystyle S_{2n}(x):=P_{n}\big(x^{2}\big),\qquad S_{2n+1}(x):=xR_{n}\big(x^{2}\big)

are the orthonormal polynomials with respect to the unique symmetric probability measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
such that for all non-negative Borel functions
f\colon[0,\infty[\to\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f\big(x^{2}\big){\rm d}\mu(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t){\rm d}\nu(t).

The moments
s_{n}
of
\mu
are
s_{2n}=t_{n}
,
s_{2n+1}=0
.

The three terms recurrence relation for
(S_{n})
has the form

\displaystyle xS_{n}(x)={}^{s}b_{n}S_{n+1}(x)+{}^{s}b_{n-1}S_{n-1}(x)

(2.9)

with

\displaystyle a_{0}=({}^{s}b_{0})^{2},\qquad b_{n}=({}^{s}b_{2n})({}^{s}b_{2n+1}),\qquad n\geq 0,\qquad a_{n}=({}^{s}b_{2n})^{2}+({}^{s}b_{2n-1})^{2},\qquad n\geq 1.

It is known that
\mu
is indeterminate if and only if
\nu
is Stieltjes indeterminate, i.e., there are measures on
[0,\infty[
different from
\nu
with the same moments as
\nu
, cf.
[
13
, p. 333]
. In the affirmative case we denote the reproducing kernels

\displaystyle K(z,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}(z)P_{n}(w)=\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}a_{j,k}z^{j}w^{k},

\displaystyle K^{\prime}(z,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{n}(z)R_{n}(w)=\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}a^{\prime}_{j,k}z^{j}w^{k}.

(Notice that also
t{\rm d}\nu(t)
is Stieltjes indeterminate.)

The reproducing kernel for the symmetric measure
\mu
is given as

\displaystyle K_{s}(z,w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}S_{n}(z)S_{n}(w)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}P_{n}\big(z^{2}\big)P_{n}\big(w^{2}\big)+zw\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}R_{n}\big(z^{2}\big)R_{n}\big(w^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{K_s(z,w)}{}=\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}a_{j,k}z^{2j}w^{2k}+\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}a^{\prime}_{j,k}z^{2j+1}w^{2k+1}=\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}a^{(s)}_{j,k}z^{j}w^{k},

(2.10)

where

\displaystyle a_{j,k}^{(s)}=\begin{cases}a_{j/2,k/2},&j,\ k\ \text{even},\\
0,&j+k\ \text{odd},\\
a^{\prime}_{(j-1)/2,(k-1)/2},&j,\ k\ \text{odd}.\end{cases}

We then easily get the following about property (aci) from Definition
1.1
:

Proposition 2.3
.

The symmetric moment problem with moments
(s_{n})
has property
(aci)
if and only if the two Stieltjes problems with moments

(t_{n})
and
(t_{n+1})
have property
(aci)
.

For a Stieltjes problem we notice that the coefficients
b_{k,n}
of the orthonormal polynomials

have the sign pattern

b_{k,n}=(-1)^{n-k}|b_{k,n}|
because the zeros of

are all positive. This implies that

\displaystyle a_{k,l}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{k,n}b_{l,n}=(-1)^{k+l}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|b_{k,n}||b_{l,n}|,

hence

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}|a_{k,l}s_{l+m}|=\sum_{l,n=0}^{\infty}|b_{k,n}||b_{l,n}|s_{l+m}.

By Lemma
2.1
this shows

Proposition 2.4
.

For an indeterminate Stieltjes problem the properties
(ac)
and
(aci)
from Definition
1.1
are equivalent.

Combining this with Proposition
2.3
we immediately get:

Proposition 2.5
.

For a symmetric indeterminate moment problem the properties
(ac)
and
(aci)
from Definition
1.1
are equivalent.

In order to verify convergence of the series in (

This will be done in the next two sections.

Lemma 2.6
.

(i)

For any moment problem we have

.

(ii)

3
Estimates of the even moments via
\boldsymbol{U_{n}}

In all of this section we assume that the moment problem is symmetric or equivalently that
a_{n}\equiv 0
, so the recurrence relation (
1.1
) takes the simplified form

\displaystyle xP_{n}=b_{n}P_{n+1}+b_{n-1}P_{n-1}.

(3.1)

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(b_{n})
and
\big(\tilde{b}_{n}\big)
denote two sequences of positive numbers satisfying
b_{n}\leq\tilde{b}_{n}
,
n\geq 0
. Then the corresponding even moments satisfy
s_{2n}\leq\tilde{s}_{2n}
,
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Let
J
denote the Jacobi matrix associated to the moment problem, i.e.,

\displaystyle J=\begin{pmatrix}0&b_{0}&0&0&\cdots\\
b_{0}&0&b_{1}&0&\cdots\\
0&b_{1}&0&b_{2}&\cdots\\
0&0&b_{2}&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}.

(3.2)

It is well-known that
s_{n}=(J^{n}\delta_{0},\delta_{0})
, and the conclusion follows.
∎

In the symmetric case
P_{k}
involves only monomials with the same parity as
k
. Thus we have

\displaystyle{x^{n}\over b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}}=\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}u_{n,k}P_{n-2k}=u_{n,0}P_{n}+u_{n,1}P_{n-2}+\cdots,

(3.3)

hence

\displaystyle U_{n}:={s_{2n}\over b_{0}^{2}b_{1}^{2}\cdots b_{n-1}^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}u_{n,k}^{2}.

(3.4)

(Compared with (
2.1
) we have
u_{n,k}=c_{n-2k,n}/(b_{0}\cdots b_{n-1})
, while
c_{n-2k-1,n}=0
.) By (
1.2
) we have
u_{n,0}=1
.

By the recurrence relation (
3.1
) the coefficients
u_{n,k}
satisfy

\displaystyle u_{n+1,k}={b_{n-2k}\over b_{n}}u_{n,k}+{b_{n+1-2k}\over b_{n}}u_{n,k-1},\qquad 1\leq k\leq[n/2].

(3.5)

In fact, multiplying (
3.3
) by
x/b_{n}
and using the recurrence relation (
3.1
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{x^{n+1}}{b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{n}}=\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}\frac{u_{n,k}}{b_{n}}\left(b_{n-2k}P_{n-2k+1}+b_{n-2k-1}P_{n-2k-1}\right),

and (
3.5
) follows. We may extend the definition by setting
u_{n,k}=0
for
k>[n/2]
or
k<0
and
b_{n}=0
for
n<0
. In this way it is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle u_{n+1,k}={b_{n-2k}\over b_{n}}u_{n,k}+{b_{n+1-2k}\over b_{n}}u_{n,k-1},\qquad n,k\geq 0,

(3.6)

and it follows by induction that all coefficients
u_{n,k}\geq 0
.

By (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) this immediately implies the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume
(b_{n})
and
\big(\tilde{b}_{n}\big)
are positive sequences satisfying

\displaystyle{b_{n-1}\over b_{n}}\leq{\tilde{b}_{n-1}\over\tilde{b}_{n}},\qquad n\geq 1,

and let
s_{2n}
and
\tilde{s}_{2n}
denote the corresponding even moments. Then

\displaystyle U_{n}:={s_{2n}\over b_{0}^{2}b_{1}^{2}\cdots b_{n-1}^{2}}\leq\tilde{U}_{n}:={\tilde{s}_{2n}\over\tilde{b}_{0}^{2}\tilde{b}_{1}^{2}\cdots\tilde{b}_{n-1}^{2}}.

For
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
the shifted recurrence sequence
b_{n}^{(n_{0})}:=b_{n+n_{0}}
,
n\geq 0
leads to moments
s_{n}^{(n_{0})}
and

\displaystyle U_{n}^{(n_{0})}:=\frac{s_{2n}^{(n_{0})}}{\big(b_{0}^{(n_{0})}\cdots b_{n-1}^{(n_{0})}\big)^{2}}=\frac{s_{2n}^{(n_{0})}}{b_{n_{0}}^{2}\cdots b_{n_{0}+n-1}^{2}}.

Proposition 3.3
.

The expressions above satisfy

\displaystyle s_{2n+2}\geq b_{0}^{2}s_{2n}^{(1)},\qquad U_{n+1}\geq U_{n}^{(1)}.

(3.7)

If
b_{n}\leq b_{n}^{(n_{0})}
for
n\geq 0
and
\lim b_{n}^{1/n}=1
, then

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}^{1/n}=\limsup_{n\to\infty}\big(U_{n}^{(n_{0})}\big)^{1/n}.

(3.8)

The latter holds for
b_{n}=\lambda(n+1)^{c}
,
\lambda,c>0
.

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle J_{(1)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0&\cdots\\
0&0&b_{1}&0&\cdots\\
0&b_{1}&0&b_{2}&\cdots\\
0&0&b_{2}&0&\cdots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&J^{(1)}\end{pmatrix},

where
J^{(1)}
is the Jacobi matrix (
3.2
) corresponding to
b_{n}^{(1)}
and the matrix to the right is a block matrix. The upper left
0
is a scalar and the upper right
0
is an infinite row vector of zeros. In block matrix notation we then have

\displaystyle\big(J_{(1)}\big)^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\big(J^{(1)}\big)^{n}\end{pmatrix},

hence

\displaystyle s_{2n}^{(1)}=\big((J_{(1)})^{2n}\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\big).

This gives

\displaystyle s_{2n+2}=\big(J^{2n+2}\delta_{0},\delta_{0}\big)=\big(J^{2n}J\delta_{0},J\delta_{0}\big)=b_{0}^{2}\big(J^{2n}\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\big)\geq b_{0}^{2}\big((J_{(1)})^{2n}\delta_{1},\delta_{1}\big)=b_{0}^{2}s_{2n}^{(1)},

hence
U_{n+1}\geq U_{n}^{(1)}
.

If
b_{n}\leq b_{n}^{(n_{0})}
we have
s_{2n}\leq s_{2n}^{(n_{0})}
by Proposition
3.1
, and therefore for
n>n_{0}

\displaystyle U_{n}\leq\frac{b_{n_{0}}^{2}b_{n_{0}+1}^{2}\cdots b_{n_{0}+n-1}^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}b_{1}^{2}\cdots b_{n-1}^{2}}U_{n}^{(n_{0})}\leq\frac{b_{n}^{2}b_{n+1}^{2}\cdots b_{n+n_{0}-1}^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}b_{1}^{2}\cdots b_{n_{0}-1}^{2}}U_{n+n_{0}}.

If
\lim b_{n}^{1/n}=1
we get (
3.8
).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume
(b_{n})
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{i,j\geq 0}\frac{b_{i}}{b_{i+j}}=M<\infty.

Then

\displaystyle U_{n+1}\leq 4M^{2}U_{n},\qquad s_{2n+2}\leq 4M^{2}b_{n}^{2}s_{2n}.

(3.9)

Proof.

By the recurrence equation (
3.5
) we get

\displaystyle u_{n+1,k}\leq M(u_{n,k}+u_{n,k-1}),

hence

\displaystyle u_{n+1,k}^{2}\leq 2M^{2}\big(u_{n,k}^{2}+u_{n,k-1}^{2}\big).

This gives

\displaystyle U_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{[(n+1)/2]}u_{n+1,k}^{2}\leq 4M^{2}U_{n},

hence
s_{2n+2}\leq 4M^{2}b_{n}^{2}s_{2n}
.
∎

In the following we shall use the notation from
[
16
]
about
q
-shifted factorials. For
z,q\in\mathbb{C}
we write

\displaystyle(z;q)_{n}=\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\big(1-zq^{j}\big),\qquad n=1,2,\ldots,\qquad(z;q)_{0}=1.

(3.10)

When
|q|<1
we define the absolutely convergent infinite product

\displaystyle(z;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}\big(1-zq^{j}\big).

(3.11)

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
(b_{n})
is log-concave and strictly increasing. Define
q_{n}:=b_{n-1}/b_{n}
,
n\geq 1
. Then
0<q_{n}\leq q_{n+1}<1
and

\displaystyle u_{n,k}\leq q_{n}^{k^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1-q_{n}^{2j}\big)^{-1}=\frac{q_{n}^{k^{2}}}{\big(q_{n}^{2};q_{n}^{2}\big)_{k}},\qquad n\geq 1,\qquad k\geq 0.

(3.12)

Proof.

The inequality trivially holds when
k=0
independent of
n
, because both sides are 1. Since
u_{1,k}=0
for
k\geq 1
the inequality holds in particular for
n=1
and all
k
.
Notice that by assumption

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-j}}{b_{n}}=q_{n-j+1}q_{n-j+2}\cdots q_{n}\leq q_{n}^{j}.

Let us define

\displaystyle\tau_{n,k}:=\frac{q_{n}^{k^{2}}}{\big(q_{n}^{2};q_{n}^{2}\big)_{k}}.

Since
q_{n}\leq q_{n+1}
we have
\tau_{n,k}\leq\tau_{n+1,k}
. Suppose now that (
3.12
) holds for some
n\geq 1
and all
k
. For
k\geq 1
we get by (
3.5
) and the induction hypothesis

\displaystyle u_{n+1,k}\leq q_{n}^{2k}u_{n,k}+q_{n}^{2k-1}u_{n,k-1}\leq q_{n}^{2k}\tau_{n,k}+q_{n}^{2k-1}\tau_{n,k-1}

\displaystyle\hphantom{u_{n+1,k}}{}\leq q_{n+1}^{2k}\tau_{n+1,k}+q_{n+1}^{2k-1}\tau_{n+1,k-1}=\tau_{n+1,k}.

∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume
(b_{n})
is
q
-increasing, cf. Definition
1.6
, and let

be given by (

\displaystyle U_{n}\leq\big(q^{2};q^{2}\big)_{\infty}^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{2k^{2}},\qquad n\geq 0.

(b_{n})
is log-convex and strictly increasing with

For

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n}}{b_{n+l}}=\frac{b_{n}}{b_{n+1}}\frac{b_{n+1}}{b_{n+2}}\cdots\frac{b_{n+l-1}}{b_{n+l}}\leq q^{l},

(3.13)

so from equation (

) we get

\displaystyle u_{n+1,k}\leq q^{2k}u_{n,k}+q^{2k-1}u_{n,k-1}.

(3.14)

We claim that

\displaystyle u_{n,k}\leq q^{k^{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\big(1-q^{2j}\big)^{-1}=\frac{q^{k^{2}}}{\big(q^{2};q^{2}\big)_{k}},

which is certainly true for

Assume that (

\displaystyle u_{n+1,k}\leq\frac{q^{k^{2}+2k}}{\big(q^{2};q^{2}\big)_{k}}+\frac{q^{(k-1)^{2}+2k-1}}{\big(q^{2};q^{2}\big)_{k-1}}=\frac{q^{k^{2}}}{\big(q^{2};q^{2}\big)_{k}},

which finishes the proof of (

\displaystyle u_{n,k}\leq\frac{q^{k^{2}}}{\big(q^{2};q^{2}\big)_{\infty}},

\displaystyle U_{n}\leq\big(q^{2};q^{2}\big)_{\infty}^{-2}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}q^{2k^{2}}<\infty.

∎

<b_{n_{0}+k}

By increasing

\displaystyle b_{n_{0}}>\max\{b_{n}\colon 0\leq n<n_{0}\}.

Defining

\displaystyle q_{1}=\max_{0\leq n<n_{0}}\left(\frac{b_{n}}{b_{n_{0}}}\right)^{1/(n_{0}-n)},\qquad p=\max(q,q_{1}),

Let

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{n}:=\begin{cases}b_{n_{0}}p^{n_{0}-n},&0\leq n<n_{0},\\
b_{n},&n_{0}\leq n.\end{cases}

Then
\big(\tilde{b}_{n}\big)

we find

\displaystyle U_{n}=\frac{s_{2n}}{b_{0}^{2}b_{1}^{2}\cdots b_{n-1}^{2}}\leq\frac{\tilde{b}_{0}^{2}\cdots\tilde{b}_{n_{0}-1}^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}\cdots b_{n_{0}-1}^{2}}\tilde{U}_{n}.

This shows the boundedness of
(U_{n})
since
(\tilde{U}_{n})
is bounded by Theorem
3.6
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Assume
(b_{n})
is strictly increasing and log-concave. Then
u_{n,k}
from (
3.3
) satisfy

\displaystyle u_{n,0}=1,\qquad u_{n,k}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}{b_{n-2k+j-1}\over b_{n-j}-b_{n-2k+j-2}},\qquad 1\leq k\leq{n\over 2}.

(3.16)

Proof.

We will use induction with respect to
n
. Equation (
3.16
) is certainly true for
n=1
. Assume the conclusion is satisfied for
n\geq 1
. For
1\leq j\leq k
,
k\geq 2
we use log-concavity to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-2k+j-1}}{b_{n-j}-b_{n-2k+j-2}}\leq\frac{b_{n-2k+j}}{b_{n+1-j}-b_{n-2k+j-1}},

hence by the induction hypothesis

\displaystyle u_{n+1,k}={b_{n-2k}\over b_{n}}u_{n,k}+{b_{n-2k+1}\over b_{n}}u_{n,k-1}

\displaystyle\hphantom{u_{n+1,k}}{}\leq{b_{n-2k}\over b_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}{b_{n-2k+j-1}\over b_{n-j}-b_{n-2k+j-2}}+{b_{n-2k+1}\over b_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}{b_{n-2k+j+1}\over b_{n-j}-b_{n-2k+j}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{u_{n+1,k}}{}\leq{b_{n-2k}\over b_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}{b_{n-2k+j}\over b_{n+1-j}-b_{n-2k+j-1}}+{b_{n-2k+1}\over b_{n}}\prod_{j=2}^{k}{b_{n-2k+j}\over b_{n+1-j}-b_{n-2k+j-1}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{u_{n+1,k}}{}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}{b_{n-2k+j}\over b_{n+1-j}-b_{n-2k+j-1}}\left[{b_{n-2k}\over b_{n}}+{b_{n-2k+1}\over b_{n}}{b_{n}-b_{n-2k}\over b_{n-2k+1}}\right]

\displaystyle\hphantom{u_{n+1,k}}{}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}{b_{n-2k+j}\over b_{n+1-j}-b_{n-2k+j-1}}.

For
k=1
we similarly have

\displaystyle u_{n+1,1}={b_{n-2}\over b_{n}}u_{n,1}+{b_{n-1}\over b_{n}}\leq{b_{n-2}\over b_{n}}{b_{n-2}\over b_{n-1}-b_{n-3}}+{b_{n-1}\over b_{n}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{u_{n+1,1}}{}\leq{b_{n-2}\over b_{n}}{b_{n-1}\over b_{n}-b_{n-2}}+{b_{n-1}\over b_{n}}={b_{n-1}\over b_{n}-b_{n-2}}.

∎

Theorem 3.9
.

Assume
(b_{n})
satisfies

\displaystyle{b_{n-1}\over b_{n}}\leq{\rm e}^{-\beta/n},\qquad n\geq 1,\qquad\beta>0.

(3.17)

The quantity
U_{n}
from (
2.11
) satisfies

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}U_{n}^{1/n}\leq{\rm e}^{2u(\beta)},

(3.18)

where

\displaystyle u(\beta):=\max_{2^{-1/\beta}\leq x\leq 1}(x+1)\int_{x}^{1}\log\left(\frac{y^{\beta}}{1-y^{\beta}}\right)\frac{{\rm d}y}{(1+y)^{2}}>0.

(3.19)

The function
u(\beta)
is decreasing with
\lim\limits_{\beta\to 0}u(\beta)=\infty
,
\lim\limits_{\beta\to\infty}u(\beta)=0
.

Proof.

By (
3.17
) we get

\displaystyle b_{n}\geq b_{0}{\rm e}^{\beta(1+1/2+\dots+1/n)},

showing that
(b_{n})
is strictly increasing to infinity.

Defining

\displaystyle\tilde{b}_{0}:=b_{0},\qquad\tilde{b}_{n}:=b_{0}{\rm e}^{\beta(1+1/2+\dots+1/n)},\qquad n\geq 1,

then
\big(\tilde{b}_{n}\big)
is strictly increasing and log-concave.

For
\widehat{b}_{n}=(n+1)^{\beta}
we then have

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}\leq\frac{\tilde{b}_{n-1}}{\tilde{b}_{n}}\leq\frac{\widehat{b}_{n-1}}{\widehat{b}_{n}},

(3.20)

so by Proposition
3.2

\displaystyle U_{n}\leq\tilde{U}_{n}\leq\widehat{U}_{n}.

We claim that

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\tilde{b}_{n-2k+j-1}\over\tilde{b}_{n-j}-\tilde{b}_{n-2k+j-2}}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{k}{\widehat{b}_{n-2k+j-1}\over\widehat{b}_{n-j}-\widehat{b}_{n-2k+j-2}}=:\sigma_{n,k},

(3.21)

which follows from the second inequality in (
3.20
) because of

\displaystyle{\tilde{b}_{n-2k+j-1}\over\tilde{b}_{n-j}-\tilde{b}_{n-2k+j-2}}=\frac{\tilde{b}_{n-2k+j-1}/\tilde{b}_{n-j}}{1-\tilde{b}_{n-2k+j-2}/\tilde{b}_{n-j}}

and a similar expression for
\widehat{b}_{n}
. Let
k_{n}
be the smallest index so that

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,k_{n}}=\max\{\sigma_{n,k}\colon 1\leq k\leq n/2\}.

We have
n=k_{n}(\gamma_{n}+2),
where
\gamma_{n}\geq 0
.
By Proposition
3.8
and the inequality (
3.21
) we have
\tilde{u}_{n,k}\leq\sigma_{n,k}
, hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{n}U_{n}^{1/n}\leq\limsup_{n}\tilde{U}_{n}^{1/n}\leq\limsup_{n}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{[n/2]}\sigma_{n,k}^{2}\right)^{1/n}\leq\limsup_{n}\big(1+[n/2]\sigma_{n,k_{n}}^{2}\big)^{1/n}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\limsup_n U_n^{1/n}}{}\leq\limsup_{n}(1+[n/2])^{1/n}\sigma_{n,k_{n}}^{2/n}\leq\limsup_{n}\sigma_{n,k_{n}}^{2/n},

(3.22)

where we have used that
\sigma_{n,0}=1
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,k_{n}}\geq\sigma_{n,1}=\frac{(n-1)^{\beta}}{n^{\beta}-(n-2)^{\beta}}\to\infty

for
n\to\infty
. By the right-hand side of (
3.21
) we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{n}\sigma_{n,k_{n}}^{1/n}=\limsup_{n}\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k_{n}-1}{\widehat{b}_{n-2k_{n}+j}\over\widehat{b}_{n-j-1}-\widehat{b}_{n-2k_{n}+j-1}}\right)^{1/n}.

Furthermore

\displaystyle\log\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k_{n}-1}{\widehat{b}_{n-2k_{n}+j}\over\widehat{b}_{n-j-1}-\widehat{b}_{n-2k_{n}+j-1}}\right)^{1/n}=\frac{1}{(\gamma_{n}+2)k_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{n}-1}E_{n}(\beta,j),

(3.23)

where

\displaystyle E_{n}(\beta,j)=\beta\log(\gamma_{n}+{j/k_{n}}+1/k_{n})-\log\big[(\gamma_{n}+2-{j/k_{n}})^{\beta}-(\gamma_{n}+{j/k_{n}})^{\beta}\big].

(3.24)

Let
\gamma
be an accumulation point of
(\gamma_{n})
.

We consider first the case, where
\gamma=\infty
is the only accumulation point, so
\gamma_{n}\to\infty
. We have

\displaystyle\log(\gamma_{n}+{j/k_{n}}+1/k_{n})\leq\log(\gamma_{n}+1).

(3.25)

The inequality
(0<y<x)

\displaystyle{x^{\beta}-y^{\beta}\over x-y}\geq\begin{cases}\beta y^{\beta-1},&\beta\geq 1,\\
\beta x^{\beta-1},&0<\beta<1,\end{cases}

implies

\displaystyle(\gamma_{n}+2-{j/k_{n}})^{\beta}-(\gamma_{n}+{j/k_{n}})^{\beta}\geq\begin{cases}2\beta(1-j/k_{n})(\gamma_{n}+{j/k_{n}})^{\beta-1},&\beta\geq 1,\\
2\beta(1-j/k_{n})(\gamma_{n}+2-{j/k_{n}})^{\beta-1},&0<\beta<1\end{cases}

\displaystyle\hphantom{(\gamma_n+2-{j/ k_n} )^\beta- (\gamma_n+{j/ k_n})^\beta}{}\geq\begin{cases}2\beta(1-j/k_{n})\gamma_{n}^{\beta-1},&\beta\geq 1,\\
2\beta(1-j/k_{n})(\gamma_{n}+2)^{\beta-1},&0<\beta<1.\end{cases}

(3.26)

We have

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k_{n}}\sum_{j=0}^{k_{n}-1}\log(1-j/k_{n})\leq-\int_{0}^{1}\log x{\rm d}x=1.

(3.27)

By (
3.25
), (
3.26
) and (
3.27
) we get that the right-hand side of (
3.23
) is bounded above by
C\log(\gamma_{n}+2)/(\gamma_{n}+2)
for suitable
C>0
, hence tends to 0. In this case (
3.22
) gives

\displaystyle\limsup_{n}U_{n}^{1/n}\leq 1,

and (
3.18
) holds.

Assume now that
\gamma<\infty
. Then, in view of (
3.23
) and (
3.24
) the quantity

\displaystyle I={1\over\gamma+2}\int_{0}^{1}\big\{\beta\log(\gamma+x)-\log\big[(\gamma+2-x)^{\beta}-(\gamma+x)^{\beta}\big]\big\}{\rm d}x

is an accumulation point of (

). The substitution

\displaystyle y={\gamma+x\over\gamma+2-x}

leads to

\displaystyle I={2\gamma+2\over\gamma+2}\int_{\gamma\over\gamma+2}^{1}\log\left({y^{\beta}\over 1-y^{\beta}}\right){{\rm d}y\over(1+y)^{2}}.

Set
\sigma=\gamma/(\gamma+2)
. Then

\displaystyle I=(\sigma+1)\int_{\sigma}^{1}\log\left({y^{\beta}\over 1-y^{\beta}}\right){{\rm d}y\over(1+y)^{2}},

hence

\displaystyle I\leq\max_{0\leq x\leq 1}\left\{(x+1)\int_{x}^{1}\log\left({y^{\beta}\over 1-y^{\beta}}\right){{\rm d}y\over(1+y)^{2}}\right\}.

Clearly the maximum is attained for

, as in this case the integrated function is nonnegative. Now (
3.18
) follows.

The last assertion is easy to prove.
∎

Theorem 3.10
.

Assume
(b_{n})
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{b_{n-1}}{b_{n}}\leq{\rm e}^{-f(n)},\qquad n\geq n_{0}\geq 1,

) and

.

Proof.

Let

be arbitrary. There exists

\displaystyle nf(n)\geq\beta,\qquad n\geq N.

(3.30)

Combining (
3.28
) and (

), we see that
(b_{n})
is eventually strictly increasing to infinity. By replacing

4

\boldsymbol{c_{n}}
via

In all of this section we consider symmetric moment problems given by a positive sequence
(b_{n})
, and it is tacitly assumed that the moment problem is indeterminate in order to be able to define
c_{n}
from (
2.6
), hence
\sum 1/b_{n}<\infty
by a classical result due to Carleman, cf.
[
1
, p. 24]
.

For
k,l\geq 0
define

\displaystyle v_{k,l}:=(-1)^{l}b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{k-1}b_{k,k+2l},

(4.1)

where
b_{k,k+2l}
is the coefficient to
x^{k}
in
P_{k+2l}
, so
(-1)^{l}b_{k,k+2l}>0
and hence
v_{k,l}>0
.

Observe that
v_{k,0}=1
and since
b_{k,j}=0
if
j<k
it makes sense to define
v_{k,l}=0
if
l<0
.

From (
1.9
) we know

\displaystyle P_{2l}(0)=b_{0,2l}=(-1)^{l}v_{0,l}=(-1)^{l}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\frac{b_{2j-2}}{b_{2j-1}}.

(4.2)

Notice that by (
2.6
) and (
2.12
)

\displaystyle V_{n}=b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}c_{n}=\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}v_{n,l}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(4.3)

Lemma 4.1
.

The coefficients
v_{k,l}
satisfy

\displaystyle v_{k+1,l}={b_{k}\over b_{k+2l}}v_{k,l}+{b_{k+2l-1}\over b_{k+2l}}v_{k+1,l-1},\qquad k,l\geq 0.

(4.4)

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle xP_{k+2l}=b_{k+2l}P_{k+2l+1}+b_{k+2l-1}P_{k+2l-1}.

By equating the coefficients of
x^{k+1}
we obtain

\displaystyle b_{k,k+2l}=b_{k+2l}b_{k+1,k+2l+1}+b_{k+2l-1}b_{k+1,k+2l-1}.

Multiplying the last identity by
(-1)^{l}b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{k-1}
leads to

\displaystyle v_{k,l}={b_{k+2l}\over b_{k}}v_{k+1,l}-{b_{k+2l-1}\over b_{k}}v_{k+1,l-1}.

This gives (
4.4
).
∎

The following result follows easily from (
4.2
) and (
4.4
).

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume
(b_{n})
and
\big(\tilde{b}_{n}\big)
are positive sequences satisfying

\displaystyle{b_{n-1}\over b_{n}}\leq{\tilde{b}_{n-1}\over\tilde{b}_{n}},\qquad n\geq 1.

Then
v_{k,l}\leq\tilde{v}_{k,l}
and
V_{k}\leq\tilde{V}_{k}
.

For

the shifted sequence

also leads to an indeterminate moment problem, cf.
[
1
, p. 28]
, and we shall consider the associated quantities

,
V_{k}^{(n_{0})}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

The expressions above satisfy

\displaystyle v_{k,l}^{(1)}\leq v_{k+1,l},\qquad V_{k}^{(1)}\leq V_{k+1},\qquad k,l\geq 0,

(4.5)

\displaystyle v_{k+1,l}\leq Lv_{k,l}^{(1)},\qquad V_{k+1}\leq LV_{k}^{(1)},\qquad k,l\geq 0,

(4.6)

where

. For

we similarly have

\displaystyle v_{k,l}^{(n_{0})}\leq v_{k+n_{0},l}\leq L(n_{0})v_{k,l}^{(n_{0})},\qquad V_{k}^{(n_{0})}\leq V_{k+n_{0}}\leq L(n_{0})V_{k}^{(n_{0})},

for a suitable constant

. Furthermore,

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\to\infty}V_{k}^{1/k}=\limsup_{k\to\infty}\big(V_{k}^{(n_{0})}\big)^{1/k}.

(4.8)

Proof.

From (
4.4
) we get

\displaystyle v_{k+1,l}^{(1)}=\frac{b_{k+1}}{b_{k+2l+1}}v_{k,l}^{(1)}+\frac{b_{k+2l}}{b_{k+2l+1}}v_{k+1,l-1}^{(1)},\qquad k,l\geq 0,

(4.9)

\displaystyle v_{k+2,l}=\frac{b_{k+1}}{b_{k+2l+1}}v_{k+1,l}+\frac{b_{k+2l}}{b_{k+2l+1}}v_{k+2,l-1},\qquad k,l\geq 0.

(4.10)

On the other hand

\displaystyle v_{1,l}=\frac{b_{0}}{b_{2l}}v_{0,l}+\frac{b_{2l-1}}{b_{2l}}v_{1,l-1}\geq\frac{b_{2l-1}}{b_{2l}}v_{1,l-1}.

(4.11)

Iterating this we get using
v_{1,0}=1

\displaystyle v_{1,l}\geq\prod_{j=1}^{l}\frac{b_{2j-1}}{b_{2j}}=v_{0,l}^{(1)},

(4.12)

where the last equality follows from (
4.2
). This proves the left inequality in (

) for

and all
l
, and it is clear for
l=0
and all
k
. In particular it is true for
k+l\leq 1
. Assume that it holds for
k+l\leq n
for some

. To prove it for

, it suffices to prove

when

, but this follows immediately by (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) and the induction hypothesis. The right inequality in (
4.5
) is an immediate consequence of the left inequality.

Defining
t_{l}=v_{1,l}/v_{0,l}^{(1)}
,
l\geq 0
we get by (
4.12

\displaystyle t_{l}=v_{1,l}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\frac{b_{2j}}{b_{2j-1}},\qquad l\geq 1,

so by (

\displaystyle t_{l}=\left(\frac{b_{0}}{b_{2l}}v_{0,l}+\frac{b_{2l-1}}{b_{2l}}v_{1,l-1}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{l}\frac{b_{2j}}{b_{2j-1}}=\frac{b_{0}}{b_{2l}}\prod_{j=1}^{l}\frac{b_{2j-2}b_{2j}}{b_{2j-1}^{2}}+t_{l-1}.

Theorem 4.4

Proposition 4.7

Assume

is log-concave and strictly increasing. Then

\displaystyle V_{k}\leq V_{k+1},\qquad c_{k}\leq b_{k}c_{k+1},\qquad k\geq 0.

Proof.

We start by showing that

\displaystyle v_{0,1}<v_{1,1}.

\displaystyle v_{0,1}={b_{0}\over b_{1}},\qquad v_{1,1}={b_{0}^{2}+b_{1}^{2}\over b_{1}b_{2}},

We are going to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-n_{1}}[\log(n+k)-\log(n_{1}+k)]\geq\int_{1}^{n-n_{1}+1}[\log(n+x)-\log(n_{1}+x)]{\rm d}x

Summarizing we obtain

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left({b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}\over b_{n}b_{n+1}\cdots b_{2n-j-1}}\right)^{1/n}\leq 4^{-\beta}.

Since
1<\beta<\alpha
is arbitrary we get

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left({b_{0}b_{1}\cdots b_{n-1}\over b_{n}b_{n+1}\cdots b_{2n-j-1}}\right)^{1/n}\leq 4^{-\alpha}.

(5.4)

Finally, by juxtaposing (
5.2

5.4
), (
3.29
) and (
4.27
), we get
the conclusion.
∎

6
The symmetrized cubic case

In a series of papers Valent and coauthors have studied birth-and-death processes with polynomial rates leading to indeterminate Stieltjes moment problems, if the polynomial degree is at least three, see
[
24
]
and references therein. In
[
24

Valent formulated conjectures about order and type of these Stieltjes problems. The conjecture about order was settled in Romanov
[

together with expressions for the type using multi-zeta values. These formulas have lead to a proof of the Valent conjecture about type in Bochkov

Valent’s conjectures were based on concrete calculation of order and type in a quartic case
[

]
and a cubic case
[

(6.1)

\displaystyle a_{n}=\lambda_{n}+\mu_{n},\qquad b_{n}=\sqrt{\lambda_{n}\mu_{n+1}},\qquad n\geq 0.

The purpose of this section is to estimate the moments in this cubic case. Using these estimates it was possible to prove that property (cs) does not hold for the corresponding symmetric moment problem. This was a surprise to us because (cs) turned out to be true in all examples, we had been able to calculate so far.

In order to distinguish the quantities associated with this special case from the general quantities, we shall equip them with a star
*
.

The recurrence coefficients from (
3.1
) are

\displaystyle b_{2n}^{*}=\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}=\sqrt{3n+1}(3n+2),\qquad b_{2n-1}^{*}=\sqrt{\mu_{n}}=3n\sqrt{3n+1},

(6.2)

cf.
[
9
, p. 343]

Theorem 6.1
.

For the symmetric moment problem given by
(b_{n}^{*})
and corresponding to the Stieltjes problem with rates (

) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}c_{2n}^{*}s_{2n}^{*}=\infty,

so property

does not hold. The series

In accordance with

Let

We shall find explicit formulas for

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
f

be entire functions with power series

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n},\qquad g(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\beta_{n}z^{n}.

Then the entire function of two variables

\displaystyle L(z,w)=\frac{f(z)g(w)-f(w)g(z)}{z-w}

has the power series

\displaystyle L(z,w)=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k,l}z^{k}w^{l},

where

\displaystyle\gamma_{k,l}=\sum_{j=0}^{l}(\alpha_{j+k+1}\beta_{l-j}-\beta_{j+k+1}\alpha_{l-j}).

(6.11)

Remark 6.4
.

By construction
L(z,w)=L(w,z)
and therefore
\gamma_{k,l}=\gamma_{l,k}
, but this is not obvious from (
6.11
).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle L(z,w)=\frac{f(z)-f(w)}{z-w}g(w)-f(w)\frac{g(z)-g(w)}{z-w}

and

\displaystyle\frac{f(z)-f(w)}{z-w}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\frac{z^{n}-w^{n}}{z-w}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}z^{n-1-j}w^{j}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{f(z)-f(w)}{z-w}}{}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}w^{j}\sum_{n=j+1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}z^{n-1-j}=\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{j+k+1}z^{k}w^{j}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{f(z)-f(w)}{z-w}g(w)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\beta_{m}w^{m}\sum_{j,k=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{j+k+1}z^{k}w^{j}=\sum_{k,l=0}^{\infty}z^{k}w^{l}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{l}\alpha_{j+k+1}\beta_{l-j}\right),

and by symmetry we get (
6.11
).
∎

Write

7
The symmetric moment problem with
\boldsymbol{b_{n}=c^{c}(n+1)^{c}}

\boldsymbol{c>0}

The sequence
b_{n}=b_{n}(c)=c^{c}(n+1)^{c}
is log-concave for
c>0
. By the Carleman condition the moment problem is determinate for
0<c\leq 1
, and for

it is indeterminate of order
\rho=1/c
, cf.
[
8
, Theorem 4.11]
. The factor

does not influence the order of the moment problem, but it is convenient when

for comparison with the symmetrized cubic case.

The corresponding orthonormal polynomials
P_{n}(c;x)
do not seem to be known except in the cases
c=1/2
and
c=1
, where they are scaled versions of the Hermite polynomials and Meixner–Pollaczek polynomials. In a precise manner

\displaystyle P_{n}(1/2;x)=(2^{n}n!)^{-1/2}H_{n}\big(x/\sqrt{2}\big),\qquad P_{n}(1;x)=P_{n}^{(1/2)}(x/2;\pi/2),

where
H_{n}
are the Hermite polynomials and
P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\varphi)
the Meixner–Pollaczek polynomials in the notation of
[
20

Proposition A.1

Proof.

,

(A.2)

On the other hand we have

Therefore by (

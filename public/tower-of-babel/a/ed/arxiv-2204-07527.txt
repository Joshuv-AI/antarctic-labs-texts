Local well-posedness of a three-dimensional phase-field model for thrombus and blood flow Thanks: *Corresponding author. Email address: ktawri@iu.edu
Woojeong Kim 1 , Krutika Tawri 1,∗ and Roger Temam 1

Abstract
. In this article we consider a fluid-structure interactions model on a three dimensional bounded domain, that describes the mechanical interaction between blood flow and a thrombus with Hookean elasticity. The interface between the two phases is given by a smooth transition layer, diffuse with a finite thickness. We derive various a priori estimates and prove local well-posedness results using the Faedo-Galerkin method.

Introduction

Historically, phase-field methods have been commonly used to model two-phase flows of macroscopically immiscible fluids. However recently there have been some efforts in using phase-field methods to model fluid-structure
interactions (FSI). In
[
19
]
and
[
17
]
, the authors give a fully Eulerian description of the velocity field in the fluid and the
elastic domain by coupling Oldroyd-B type equations with volume preserving Allen-Cahn and Cahn-Hilliard type equations respectively.

In this article we study an FSI problem proposed in
[
24
]
(see also
[
23
]
) to model the mechanical interaction between blood flow and a thrombus with Hookean elasticity, using a phase-field method to describe a two-phase system, with the interface between two different phases given by a thin smooth transition layer.

Let
x(t,\cdot)
be a time-dependent family of orientation-preserving
diffeomorphisms. Let
X(x,t)
be the
corresponding reference map i.e.
X(\cdot,t)
is the inverse of
x(\cdot,t)
. The velocity field

u(x,t)
is defined as:

u(x,t)=\frac{\mathrm{d}x(t,X)}{\mathrm{d}t}|_{X=X(x,t)}.

The deformation gradient
F
is given by

F(x,t)=\frac{\partial x(t,X)}{\partial X}|_{X=X(x,t)}.

Using the chain differentiation rule it can be seen that
F
satisfies

F_{t}+u\cdot\nabla F=\nabla uF,

which is to be understood component-wise as
F^{ij}_{t}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}u^{k}\partial_{k}F^{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\partial_{k}u^{i}F^{kj}
for
1\leq i,j\leq d
. Here we use the notation
F^{ij}=\frac{\partial x^{i}}{\partial X^{j}}
and
[\nabla u]_{ij}:=\frac{\partial u^{i}}{\partial x^{j}}
.
The incompressibility is expressed in the Lagrangian coordinates by the equation:

\det F\equiv 1,

and it is expressed in the Eulerian coordinates by the equation div
u=0
which appears below in (
1.1
)
2
. See the explanations in
[
22
]
.
For a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
T>0
, we consider the following governing equations on
\Omega\times(0,T)

(1.1)

\begin{split}\begin{cases}\rho(\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+u\cdot\nabla u)+\nabla p-\nabla\cdot(\eta(\phi)\nabla u)=-\lambda\nabla\cdot(\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi)\\
\hskip 158.99377pt+\nabla\cdot(\lambda_{e}(1-\phi)(FF^{T}-I))-\eta(\phi)\frac{(1-\phi)u}{\kappa(\phi)},\\
\nabla\cdot u=0,\\
\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}+u\cdot\nabla F=\nabla uF,\\
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+u\cdot\nabla\phi=\tau\Delta\mu,\\
\mu=-\lambda\Delta\phi+\lambda\gamma f^{\prime}(\phi)-\frac{\lambda_{e}}{2}\text{tr}(FF^{T}-I),\end{cases}\end{split}

where
u,p,\rho,\eta,\kappa
are the velocity, pressure, mass density, dynamic viscosity and
permeability, respectively, and
\phi\in[0,1],\mu
are the phase-field variable and the so-called chemical potential respectively. The phase-field variable
\phi=1
represents
the blood,
0<\phi<1
represents a mixture of blood and thrombus, and
\phi=0
represents the
thrombus. The parameters

\gamma,\tau,\lambda
and
\lambda_{e}
stand for the interfacial mobility, relaxation parameter,
mixing energy density, and visco-elastic modulus, respectively and are assumed to be positive constants. In this article, we shall take the function
f
to be the double-well potential
f(\phi)=\frac{(\phi-1)^{2}\phi^{2}}{4h^{2}},
where
h
is the interfacial thickness. The above equations (
1.1
) are subjected to the following initial and boundary conditions

(1.2)

\begin{split}u=0,\partial_{\bf n}\mu=\partial_{\bf n}\phi&=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},\,\phi(\cdot,0)&=\phi_{0},\,F(\cdot,0)=F_{0}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\end{split}

where
{\bf n}
is the unit vector normal to the boundary
\partial\Omega
, pointing outward.

Since the works
[
13
]
,
[
16
]
there have been several results concerning the viscoelastic systems. In
[
9
]
, the authors studied the case of (
1.1
) with
\phi=0
and an additional linear damping term in the
F
-equation, and proved the global existence of a small smooth solution. When the contribution of the symmetric part of
\nabla u
in the constitutive equation is neglected, the global existence of weak solutions with general initial data was proven in
[
15
]
. In case of bounded domain, the global existence of strong solutions for small initial data was proven in
[
12
]
.

The Cahn-Hilliard-Navier-Stokes system, to be precise the model H (see e.g.
[
3
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
10
]
) obtained by setting
F=0
, has been studied extensively. The mathematical analysis of the system is well established for classical boundary conditions, specially in the case of regular nonlinear terms
f
. In three dimensions, the local existence and uniqueness of strong solutions is known whereas in two dimensions the existence of a global strong solution is known. We refer the reader to
[
18
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
8
]
.

For the purpose of our analysis, we will assume that
\eta,\kappa\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and that for some
\alpha,\beta>0
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\eta(x),\eta^{\prime}(x),\kappa(x)\leq\beta\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

Next we will introduce the functional framework required for our analysis.

Consider the space of solenoidal vector fields
\mathcal{V}=\{u\in{C^{\infty}_{0}}({\Omega})^{d},\text{div }u=0\}
. The closures of
\mathcal{V}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
and
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)^{d}
are called
H
and
V
respectively, and they are characterized as follows (see
[
21
]
):

H=\{u\in L^{2}({\Omega})^{d},\text{ div }u=0,u\cdot{\bf n}=0\text{ on }\partial{\Omega}\}

and

V=\{u\in H^{1}_{0}({\Omega})^{d},\text{ div }u=0\}.

We denote by
D(A)\subset V
the domain of the Stokes operator
A:=-\mathbb{P}\Delta
, where
\mathbb{P}
is the Helmholtz-Leray orthogonal projection from
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
onto
H
; we know that
D(A)=H^{2}(\Omega)^{d}\cap V
.

We will equip the space
L^{2}(\Omega)
with norm
|\cdot|
and inner-product
(\cdot,\cdot)
; same notation for the spaces
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d}
and
L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
. For
\Xi=[\xi_{ij}]\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
where
1\leq i,j\leq d
the norm is
|\Xi|=\left(\sum_{ij}\int_{\Omega}\xi_{ij}^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
which is also equal to
\left(\int_{\Omega}\text{tr}(\Xi\Xi^{T})\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. The associated inner product is given by
(\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2})=\int_{\Omega}\text{tr}(\Xi_{1}\Xi_{2}^{T})\mathrm{d}x
for any
\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
.

Our main result concerning the local well-posedness of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is stated in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
d=2,3
, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set with a sufficiently smooth boundary. We are given
u_{0}\in D(A),\phi_{0}\in H^{3}(\Omega)
such that
\partial_{\bf n}\phi_{0}=0
and
F_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}
. Then there exists
0<T_{0}\leq T
such that (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has a unique solution
(u,F,\phi,p)
on
[0,T_{0}]
such that

(1.4)

\begin{split}&u\in C([0,T_{0}];D(A))\cap L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{3}(\Omega)^{d}),\quad\partial_{t}u\in C([0,T_{0}];H)\cap L^{2}(0,T_{0};V),\\
&F\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{1}(\Omega)^{d\times d})\cap L^{\infty}(0,T_{0};H^{2}(\Omega)^{d\times d}),\\
&\phi\in C([0,T_{0}];H^{3}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{4}(\Omega))\cap H^{1}(0,T_{0};H^{3}(\Omega)),\\
&\nabla p\in L^{2}(0,T_{0};H^{1}(\Omega)).\end{split}

2.
A priori estimates

In this section we perform standard energy estimates on the system (
1.1
) in order to apply the Faedo-Galerkin scheme in Section
3
. We will only consider the case
d=3
; however the same results can be obtained more easily for the case when
d=2
. Hereupon,
C
will represent a generic positive constant depending on
\Omega
,
\alpha
and
\beta
, unless specified otherwise. We will use the short hand
|.|_{s}
to denote
|.|_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
or
|.|_{H^{s}(\Omega)^{d}}
or
|.|_{H^{s}(\Omega)^{d\times d}}
depending on the argument, where
H^{s}(\Omega)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the Sobolev space of order
s
; see e.g.
[
14
]
. We will also use boldface letters to denote spaces containing vector-valued functions.

Observe that, unlike the Cahn-Hilliard equations or the usual model-H, for our model we do not have that
\partial_{\bf n}\Delta\phi=0
on the boundary
\partial\Omega
due to the form of (
1.1
)
5
and (
1.2
). Instead we have
\partial_{\bf n}\Delta\phi=-\frac{\lambda_{e}}{2}\partial_{\bf n}\text{tr}(FF^{T})
; see below. Hence we will frequently use the following consequence of the regularity results of the Agmon-Douglis-Nirenberg type
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set with a sufficiently smooth boundary. Assume that for
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
g\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
,
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
are solutions, in a weak sense, of the following biharmonic inhomogeneous Neumann boundary value problem,

(2.1)

\begin{split}\Delta^{2}\phi&=f\quad\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\partial_{\bf n}\phi&=0,\,\,\partial_{\bf n}\Delta\phi=g\quad\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{split}

where
f
and
g
satisfy the following compatibility condition:

\int_{\Omega}f\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial\Omega}g\mathrm{d}\Gamma.

Then
\phi\in H^{4}(\Omega)
and there exists some constant
\tilde{C}>0
independent of
\phi,f,g
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\phi|^{2}_{H^{4}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}}\leq\tilde{C}\left(|f|^{2}+|g|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}\right),\text{ or}

(2.3)

\displaystyle|\phi|^{2}_{H^{4}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{C}\left(|f|^{2}+|g|^{2}_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}+|\int_{\Omega}\phi\mathrm{d}x|^{2}\right).

Proof.

The Lax-Milgram theorem shows that
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, a weak solution of (
2.1
), exists and is unique up to an additive constant. Then
\phi\in H^{4}(\Omega)
follows from the results of Agmon-Douglis-Nirenberg
[
2
]
and the first inequality (
2.2
) holds. The additive constant is accounted for by the term
|\int_{\Omega}\phi\mathrm{d}x|
.
∎

Similar to (
2.2
) we have that if
\phi\in H^{2}(\Omega)
is such that
\partial_{\bf n}\phi=0
on
\partial\Omega
, then, for some constant
C>0
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\phi|_{2}\leq C\left(|\Delta\phi|+|\int_{\Omega}\phi\mathrm{d}x|\right).

For brevity, we will use the following notation for the mean value of a function
\phi
,

\langle\phi\rangle_{\Omega}:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}\phi\mathrm{d}x.

Observe that by integrating equation (
1.1
)
4
over
\Omega
and using the divergence free property of
u
along with the boundary conditions (
1.2
) we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{t}\rangle_{\Omega}=\langle\Delta\phi\rangle_{\Omega}=\langle\Delta\phi_{t}\rangle_{\Omega}=0.

This further implies that for any time
t>0
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle|\langle\phi(t)\rangle_{\Omega}|=|\langle\phi_{0}\rangle_{\Omega}|=:K_{0}.

We will also use the following lemma to obtain estimates on
u
in the space norm
\text{{\bf H}}^{3}(\Omega)
later in this section. See
[
6
]
for a similar result in the context of the Navier-Stokes equations with density-dependent viscosity.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an open bounded set with a sufficiently smooth boundary. Assume that
(u,p)\in V\times L^{2}(\Omega)
is the weak solution of the following problem

(2.7)

\begin{split}-\text{ div }(\eta(\phi)\nabla u)+\nabla p&=f,\quad{\text{in }}\Omega,\\
\text{ div }u&=0\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
\int_{\Omega}\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\mathrm{d}x&=0,\end{split}

where
\eta\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies the assumption (
1.3
) and
\phi\in H^{3}(\Omega)
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.8)

\displaystyle|u|_{3}+\left|\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{1}\leq C|f|_{1}\left[1+\left(1+|\phi|_{2}^{2}\right)\left(|\phi|^{2}_{2}+|\phi|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\phi|_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right].

Proof of Lemma
2.2
.

Firstly, we know that the divergence operator maps the space
\text{{\bf H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
onto the space
{{L}^{2}(\Omega)/\mathbb{R}}:=\{g\in L^{2}(\Omega);\int_{\Omega}g\mathrm{d}x=0\}
(see Lemma 2.4 in
[
21
]
). Hence we have the existence of
\xi\in\text{{\bf H}}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that,

\text{div }\xi=\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)},

where
\xi
, which is not unique, can be chosen such that

(2.9)

\displaystyle|\xi|_{1}\leq C\left|\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|.

For a proof of the above result see e.g.
[
7
]
(III.3.2-III.3.3).

Testing (
2.7
) with
\xi
and using the standard estimate
|u|_{1}\leq\alpha^{-1}|f|
we obtain,

(2.10)

\begin{split}\left|\frac{p}{\sqrt{\eta(\phi)}}\right|^{2}=(\eta(\phi)\nabla u,\nabla\xi)-(f,\xi)&\lesssim\beta|u|_{1}|\xi|_{1}+|f||\xi|_{1}\\
&\lesssim(1+\beta\alpha^{-1})|f||\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}|.\end{split}

Additionally observe that,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\alpha\left|\frac{p}{{\eta(\phi)}}\right|^{2}\leq\left|\frac{p}{\sqrt{\eta(\phi)}}\right|^{2}.

Combining (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) we obtain,

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{p}{{\eta(\phi)}}\right|\leq C(\alpha,\beta)|f|.

Now observe that (
2.7
)
1
can be re-written as,

(2.13)

\begin{split}-\Delta u+\nabla(\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)})=\frac{f}{\eta(\phi)}+\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta(\phi)}{\eta(\phi)}-\frac{p\nabla\eta(\phi)}{\eta(\phi)^{2}}.\end{split}

Applying the classical result on the regularity of the Stokes problem (see
[
5
]
) we have,

\displaystyle|u|_{2}+\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\frac{f}{\eta(\phi)}\right|+\left|\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta(\phi)}{\eta(\phi)}\right|+\left|\frac{p\nabla\eta(\phi)}{\eta(\phi)^{2}}\right|.

We treat each term on the right hand side of the above inequality using the Hölder inequality and the Sobolev embeddings
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)\subset L^{3}(\Omega)
and
H^{1}(\Omega)\subset L^{6}(\Omega)
in the following way:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta(\phi)}{\eta(\phi)}\right|=\left|\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{\eta(\phi)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-1}|\eta^{\prime}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla u|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{3}(\Omega)}|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-1}\beta|u|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}|u|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}

and,

\displaystyle\left|\frac{p\nabla\eta(\phi)}{\eta(\phi)^{2}}\right|=\left|\frac{p\,\eta^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla\phi}{\eta(\phi)^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-1}|\eta^{\prime}|_{L^{\infty}}\left|\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{L^{3}}|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-1}\beta\left|\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}.

Hence using the above bounds and the Schwarz inequality we obtain, for some
C>0
depending on
\alpha,\beta
, that

\displaystyle|u|_{2}+\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-1}|f|+\alpha^{-1}\beta|u|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}|u|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}+\alpha^{-1}\beta\left|\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|u|_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|+\alpha^{-1}|f|+C|u|_{1}|\nabla\phi|^{2}_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}+C\left|\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right||\nabla\phi|^{2}_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}.

We now use (
2.12
) to obtain,

(2.14)

\begin{split}|u|_{2}+\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|&\leq C(\alpha,\beta)\left(1+|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)|f|.\end{split}

Similarly using the classical regularity results for the Stokes problem (see
[
5
]
) at the next order, we write

\displaystyle|u|_{3}+\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|\frac{f}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{1}+\left|\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\phi)\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{1}+\left|\frac{\eta^{\prime}(\phi)p\nabla\phi}{\eta(\phi)^{2}}\right|_{1}

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-1}|f|_{1}+\alpha^{-1}\beta\sum_{|s|\leq 1}\left(|\partial^{s}\nabla u|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{3}(\Omega)}|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}+|\nabla u|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}|\partial^{s}\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{3}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\alpha^{-1}\beta\sum_{|s|\leq 1}\left(\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{3}(\Omega)}|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}+\left|\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}|\partial^{s}\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{3}(\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\alpha^{-1}|f|_{1}+\alpha^{-1}\beta\left(|u|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|u|_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\phi|_{2}+|u|_{2}|\phi|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\phi|_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\alpha^{-1}\beta\left(\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\phi|_{2}+\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right||\phi|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}|\phi|_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Using the Schwarz inequality we can find further upper bounds as follows,

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}|u|_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{1}+\alpha^{-1}|f|_{1}+C|u|_{2}|\phi|^{2}_{2}+|u|_{2}|\phi|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\phi|_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\left(\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right||\phi|^{2}_{2}+\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right||\phi|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{2}|\phi|_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

That is we have,

(2.15)

\begin{split}|u|_{3}+\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{1}\leq C\left(|f|_{1}+\left(|u|_{2}+\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|\right)(|\phi|^{2}_{2}+|\phi|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\phi|_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}})\right).\end{split}

Substituting (
2.14
) in (
2.15
) we obtain the desired inequality,

(2.16)

\begin{split}|u|_{3}+\left|\nabla\frac{p}{\eta(\phi)}\right|_{1}&\leq C|f|_{1}\left[1+\left(1+|\nabla\phi|_{\text{{\bf L}}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\left(|\phi|^{2}_{2}+|\phi|_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\phi|_{3}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right].\end{split}

With these estimates we conclude the proof of Lemma
2.2
.
∎

1.1
) by first testing equation (
1.1
)
1
with

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\eta(\phi)}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=(\lambda\nabla\cdot(\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi),u)

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda_{e}(\text{div}((1-\phi)(FF^{T}-I)),u)-(\frac{\eta(\phi)}{\kappa(\phi)}(1-\phi)u,u).

The first term on the right hand side of (

)
1
can be reformulated as follows,

\displaystyle-\lambda\nabla\cdot(\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi)

\displaystyle=-\lambda\Delta\phi\nabla\phi-\lambda\nabla(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2});

\displaystyle=\mu\nabla\phi+\frac{\lambda_{e}}{2}\text{tr}(FF^{T}-I)\nabla\phi-\lambda\gamma\nabla f(\phi)-\lambda\nabla(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla\phi|^{2}).

Also observe that, for any matrix

Applying this property we see that,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lambda_{e}\text{div}((1-\phi)(FF^{T}-I))u\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{e}\int_{\Omega}\text{tr}(\nabla u(FF^{T}-I)(1-\phi))\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{e}\int_{\Omega}\text{tr}(\nabla uFF^{T})(1-\phi)\mathrm{d}x+\lambda_{e}\int_{\Omega}\text{tr}(\nabla u)(1-\phi)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\lambda_{e}\int_{\Omega}\text{tr}(\nabla uFF^{T})(1-\phi)\mathrm{d}x.

Thus we obtain,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\eta(\phi)}|\nabla u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\mu\nabla\phi\cdot u\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\lambda_{e}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\text{tr}(FF^{T})(u\cdot\nabla\phi)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda_{e}\int_{\Omega}\text{tr}(\nabla uFF^{T})(1-\phi)\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\eta(\phi)}{\kappa(\phi)}(1-\phi)u^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Next we test (
1.1
)
4
by

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\mathrm{d}t},\mu\right)+(u\cdot\nabla\phi,\mu)+\tau|\nabla\mu|^{2}=0.

We use the expression (
1.1
)
5
for

to expand the first term of equation (

) in the following way,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\mathrm{d}t},\mu\right)

Introduction and summary of main results

Practical realisations of quantum computers are constricted by noise and decoherence that affect
large-scale quantum systems. Although quantum error correction codes can overcome those effects they require usage of thousands of physical qubits to implement a single logical noiseless fault-tolerant qubit

1

2
Short review of relevant matrix concentration inequalities

In this section we review known upper bounds on the probability that
\mathbb{P}(F(X)\geq\delta)
, for classes of random matrices
X
and real valued functions
F
that are relevant in our setting. An interested reader is referred to
[
31
,
36
]
for more details. The first class of inequalities concerns a situation when
X=\sum X_{k}
, where
X_{k}\in\mathrm{Mat}(d,\mathbb{C})
are independent, random, Hermitian matrices and the function
F
is the operator norm of
X
,
F(X)=\|X\|
. Thus we are looking for an upper bound for
\mathbb{P}\left(\|X\|\geq\delta\right)
. The line of reasoning is as follows. Let
\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(X)
denote the largest and the smallest eigenvalues of
X
respectively. In the first step one uses the exponential Markov inequality, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(Y\geq t\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(e^{\theta Y}\geq e^{\theta t}\right)\leq e^{-\theta t}\mathbb{E}e^{\theta Y},

for any
\theta>0
. Taking
Y=\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)
and using the fact that
e^{\theta\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)}\leq\mathrm{tr}e^{\theta X}
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)\geq\delta\right)\leq\mathrm{inf}_{\theta>0}e^{-\theta\delta}\mathbb{E}\mathrm{tr}e^{\theta X}.

(8)

Next we note that
\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(X)\leq\delta\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(-\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(X)\geq-\delta\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(-X)\geq-\delta\right)
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(X)\leq\delta\right)\leq\mathrm{inf}_{\theta>0}e^{\theta\delta}\mathbb{E}\mathrm{tr}e^{-\theta X}.

(9)

Next, using the Lieb theorem
[
39
]
(one can alternatively use the Golden-Thomson inequality
[
40
,
41
]
but the resulting bound is in general weaker
[
31
]
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathrm{tr}e^{\theta\sum_{k}X_{k}}\leq\mathrm{tr}\exp\left(\sum_{k}\log\mathbb{E}e^{\theta X_{k}}\right),

(10)

for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Combining (
8
) and (
9
) with (
10
) we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)\geq\delta\right)\leq\inf_{\theta>0}e^{-\theta\delta}\mathrm{tr}\exp\left(\sum_{k}\log\mathbb{E}e^{\theta X_{k}}\right),

(11)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(X)\leq\delta\right)\leq\inf_{\theta>0}e^{\theta\delta}\mathrm{tr}\exp\left(\sum_{k}\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\theta X_{k}}\right).

(12)

Finally,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|X\|\geq\delta\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{max}\{\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X),-\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(X)\}\geq\delta\right)\leq

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(X)\geq\delta\right)+\mathbb{P}\left(\lambda_{\mathrm{min}}(X)\leq-\delta\right).

Fact 1

(Master bound)
.

Let
X=\sum X_{k}
, where
X_{k}\in\mathrm{Mat}(d,\mathbb{C})
are independent, random, Hermitian matrices. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|X\|\geq\delta\right)\leq\inf_{\theta>0}e^{-\theta\delta}\mathrm{tr}\exp\left(\sum_{k}\log\mathbb{E}e^{\theta X_{k}}\right)+\inf_{\theta>0}e^{-\theta\delta}\mathrm{tr}\exp\left(\sum_{k}\log\mathbb{E}e^{-\theta X_{k}}\right).

A master bound can be also derived for the sum of non-Hermitian random matrices. To this end, following
[
31
]
, we consider the Hermitian dilation map

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}:\mathrm{Mat}(d,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\mathbb{H}(2d),

where
\mathbb{H}(2d)
is the space of
2d\times 2d
Hermitian matrices given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}0&X\\
X^{\dagger}&0\end{pmatrix}.

This map is clearly a real linear map. One can also show (cf.
[
31
]
) that
\|X\|=\|\mathcal{H}(X)\|=\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}\left(\mathcal{H}(X)\right)
. Making use of (
11
) we get:

Fact 2
.

Let
X=\sum X_{k}
, where
X_{k}\in\mathrm{Mat}(d,\mathbb{C})
are independent random matrices. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\|X\|\geq\delta\right)\leq\inf_{\theta>0}e^{-\theta\delta}\mathrm{tr}\exp\left(\sum_{k}\log\mathbb{E}e^{\theta\mathcal{H}(X_{k})}\right).

The upper bounds in Facts
1
and
2
can be further simplified by finding a majorization of
\log\mathbb{E}e^{\theta X_{k}}
in terms of moments
\mathbb{E}X_{k}^{n}
that allow analytic optimization over
\theta
(see chapter 6 of
[
31
]
for more details). Usage of moments up to order two leads to the matrix Bernstein inequality.

Fact 3

(Matrix Bernstein inequality)
.

Let
X=\sum X_{k}
, where
X_{k}\in\mathrm{Mat}(d,\mathbb{C})
are independent, random matrices such that:

\forall_{k}\quad\mathbb{E}X_{k}=0\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\|X_{k}\|\leq L.

Let
v
be the matrix variance statistic of the sum:

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\|\mathbb{E}(XX^{\dagger})\|,\,\|\mathbb{E}(X^{\dagger}X)\|\right\},

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\|\sum_{k}\mathbb{E}(X_{k}X_{k}^{\dagger})\|,\,\|\sum_{k}\mathbb{E}(X_{k}^{\dagger}X_{k})\|\right\}.

Then for all
\delta\geq 0

\mathbb{P}\left(\|X\|\geq\delta\right)\leq 2d\exp\left(\frac{-\delta^{2}/2}{v+L\delta/3}\right).

integers such that
d\geq n+l(\zeta)
then they can be combined into a sequence:

\tilde{\lambda}=(\eta_{1},...,\eta_{n},\underbrace{0,...,0}_{d-n-l(\zeta)},-\zeta_{l(\zeta)},...,-\zeta_{1}),

that is clearly an element of
\Lambda_{t}
and
\|\tilde{\lambda}\|_{1}=2k
. Hence there is a one to one correspondence between sequences like
\lambda
and pairs of partitions like
(\eta,\zeta)
. Thus to prove the formula (
8
) the only thing left to do is to note that the equations
\Sigma(\lambda)=0
and
\lambda\neq 0
imply inequalities
n\geq 1
and
n\leq d-l(\lambda_{-})\leq d-1
.

The formula (
28
) results easily from the fact that for
n>k
we have
p_{n}(k)=0
and
\tilde{p}_{n}(k)=p(k)
for
n\geq k
.
∎

Lemma 9
.

Let
U,V\in U(d)
. Assume
\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\lambda}(U)-\pi_{\lambda}(V)\|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\|\lambda\|_{1}\|U-V\|.

Proof.

As the operator norm is unitarily invariant it is enough to show that for a diagonal unitary matrix
U=\mathrm{diag}\left(e^{i\phi_{1}},\ldots,e^{i\phi_{d}}\right)\in U(d)
,
\phi_{k}\in(-\pi,\pi]
we have:

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\lambda}(U)-\pi_{\lambda}(\text{1})\|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\|\lambda\|_{1}\|U-\text{1}\|.

One easily sees that

\displaystyle\|U-\text{1}\|=2\sup_{1\leq k\leq d}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\phi_{k}}{2}\right)\right|,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\pi}\sup_{1\leq k\leq d}|\phi_{k}|\leq\|U-\text{1}\|\leq\sup_{1\leq k\leq d}|\phi_{k}|.

The eigenvalues of
\pi_{\lambda}(U)
are given by

\displaystyle e^{i\psi_{k}},\,\,\psi_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i,k}\phi_{i},

where
\lambda_{k}=(\lambda_{1,k},\ldots,\lambda_{d,k})
satisfies
\|\lambda_{k}\|_{1}\leq\|\lambda\|_{1}
(see Lemma
7
). Thus

\displaystyle\|\pi_{\lambda}(U)-\text{1}\|=2\sup_{1\leq k\leq d_{\lambda}}\left|\sin\left(\frac{\psi_{k}}{2}\right)\right|\leq\sup_{1\leq k\leq d_{\lambda}}|\psi_{k}|\leq\|\lambda\|_{1}\sup_{1\leq k\leq d}|\phi_{k}|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\|\lambda\|_{1}\|U-\text{1}\|.

∎

4.1
Moment operators for quantum circuits

We can generalize the notion of moment operators to random quantum circuit in a natural way. Recall that a quantum circuit of depth
m
is a product of
m
quantum gates. Thus we can identify quantum circuits with elements of
U(d)^{\times m}
.

Consider an ensemble
\{\mathcal{R},\nu_{\mathcal{R}}\}
where
\mathcal{R}\subset U(d)^{\times m}
is a set of circuits of length
m
and
\nu_{\mathcal{R}}
is a probability measure on
\mathcal{R}
. For
\mathbf{U}=(U_{1},...,U_{m})\in U(d)^{\times m}
let us define

\mathbf{U}^{t,t}:=U_{1}^{t,t}\otimes...\otimes U_{m}^{t,t}.

(29)

Note that the entries of
\mathbf{U}^{t,t}
form a basis of the space of all
(t,t)-
polynomials in entries of
U_{1},...,U_{m}
which we will call
\mathcal{H}_{t,m}
. The average of
\mathbf{U}^{t,t}
over all circuits with the same probability is

\displaystyle T_{\mu^{\times m},t}:=\int_{U(d)^{m}}d\mu^{\times m}(\mathbf{U})\mathbf{U}^{t,t}=\int_{U(d)}d\mu(U_{1})...\int_{U(d)}d\mu(U_{m})\mathbf{U}^{t,t}=

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{U(d)}d\mu(U_{1})U_{1}^{t,t}\right)\otimes...\otimes\left(\int_{U(d)}d\mu(U_{m})U_{m}^{t,t}\right)=T_{\mu,t}^{\otimes m}.

where
\times
is a product of measures. Using Fact
9
we get that:

\mathbf{U}^{t,t}\simeq\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\left(\text{1}^{\oplus m_{0}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}}\pi_{\lambda}(U_{i})^{\oplus m_{\lambda}}\right)\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m}\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\pi_{\lambda_{i}}(U_{i})^{\oplus m_{\lambda_{i}}},

where
\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}:=\Lambda_{t}\cup\{0\}
. To simplify notation let us define:

\displaystyle\bm{\lambda}:=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m})\in\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}^{\times m},

\displaystyle\Lambda_{t,m}:=\tilde{\Lambda}^{\times m}_{t}\setminus\{(0,...,0)\},

\displaystyle\pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\mathbf{U}):=\pi_{\lambda_{1}}(U_{1})\otimes...\otimes\pi_{\lambda_{m}}(U_{m}),

\displaystyle T_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}},t}:=\int_{U(d)^{m}}d\nu_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbf{U})\mathbf{U}^{t,t},

\displaystyle T_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}},\bm{\lambda}}:=\int_{U(d)^{m}}d\nu_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbf{U})\pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\mathbf{U}),

\displaystyle\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{R}},\bm{\lambda}):=\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}},\bm{\lambda}}\|.

It is easy to see that:

\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{R}},t):=\|T_{\mu,t}^{\otimes m}-T_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}},t}\|=\sup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda_{t,m}}\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}},\bm{\lambda}}\|=\sup_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda_{t,m}}\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{R}},\bm{\lambda}).

5
Frobenius-Schur indicators

In Section
2
we explained that to obtain bounds on the norm of the random matrix
X=\sum_{k}X_{k}
one needs to compute all moments
\mathbb{E}\left(X_{k}^{n}\right)
. In this article we will be interested in estimating
\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},t_{1})
for
\nu_{\mathcal{S}}
uniform and
\mathcal{S}
finite with each element of
\mathcal{S}
chosen from an exact
t_{2}
-design
\{\mathcal{D},\nu_{\mathcal{D}}\}
. Thus in our scenario the role of
X
is played by

T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda}=\int_{U(d)}d\nu_{\mathcal{S}}(U)\pi_{\lambda}(U)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{U\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{\lambda}(U),

X_{k}
’s are matrices proportional to
\pi_{\lambda}(U)
and the average is taken over
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}

\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n}):=\int_{U(d)}d\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(U)\pi_{\lambda}(U)^{n},

for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{t_{1}}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. In particular in Lemma
11
we show that this integral is proportional to
\text{1}_{d_{\lambda}}
and the proportionality constant is the Frobenius-Schur indicator
\int_{U(d)}d\mu(U)\chi_{\lambda}(U^{n})
divided by
d_{\lambda}
.

Lemma 10
.

Consider an irreducible finite-dimensional representation
\pi_{\lambda}
of
U(d)
with the highest weight
\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}
, an integer
n
and an exact
(|n|\cdot t)
-design
\{\mathcal{D},\nu_{\mathcal{D}}\}
. Then the average of
\pi_{\lambda}(U)^{n}
taken over
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mu
is the same, that is

\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n})=\int_{U(d)}d\nu_{\mathcal{D}}(U)\pi_{\lambda}(U)^{n}=\int_{U(d)}d\mu(U)\pi_{\lambda}(U)^{n}=\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n})

Proof.

If
n<0
we can use unitarity of
\pi_{\lambda}
to obtain that for any measure
\nu

\int_{U(d)}d\nu(U)\pi_{\lambda}(U)^{n}=\int_{U(d)}d\nu(U)\pi_{\lambda}(U)^{-|n|}=\left(\int_{U(d)}d\nu(U)\pi_{\lambda}(U)^{|n|}\right)^{\dagger}.

Therefore it is enough to prove the Lemma for
n\geq 0
. In such case note that

U^{\otimes nt}\otimes\bar{U}^{\otimes nt}\simeq\left[U^{\otimes t}\otimes\bar{U}^{\otimes t}\right]^{\otimes n}\simeq\left[\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}}\pi_{\lambda}(U)^{\oplus m^{\lambda}}\right]^{\otimes n}.

Entries of the right-hand side operator above are

\pi_{\lambda_{1}}(U)_{i_{1}j_{1}}\cdot\pi_{\lambda_{2}}(U)_{i_{2}j_{2}}\cdot...\cdot\pi_{\lambda_{n}}(U)_{i_{n}j_{n}},

(30)

for all possible
\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n}\in\Lambda_{t}
and
1\leq i_{k},j_{k}\leq d_{\lambda_{k}}
. Since
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}
is a
(n\cdot t)
-design integrating the expression (
30
) over
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}
and
\mu
gives the same result. Thus the Lemma follows from the fact that the entries of
\pi_{\lambda}(U)^{n}
are linear combinations of expressions (
30
) with
\lambda_{1}=...=\lambda_{n}=\lambda
and
j_{k}=i_{k+1}
.
∎

Lemma 11
.

For any irreducible representation
\pi_{\lambda}
of a compact Lie group
G
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{G}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n})d\mu(U)=\delta_{\lambda}(n)\text{1}_{d_{\lambda}},

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}(n):=\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}}\int_{G}\chi_{\lambda}(U^{n})d\mu(U).

Proof.

Assume that
n\geq 0
. For any
g\in G
we have

\displaystyle\pi_{\lambda}(g)\int_{G}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n})d\mu(U)=\int_{G}\pi_{\lambda}(gU^{n})d\mu(U)=\int_{G}\pi_{\lambda}(gVgV\ldots gVg)d\mu(V),

where in the last equality we performed a change of variables
U=Vg
and used the invariance of the Haar measure. Similarly

\displaystyle\left(\int_{G}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n})d\mu(U)\right)\pi_{\lambda}(g)=\int_{G}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n}g)d\mu(U)=\int_{G}\pi_{\lambda}(gVgV\ldots gVg)d\mu(V),

where in the last equality we performed a change of variables
U=gV
and used the invariance of the Haar measure. In case
n<0
the argument is analogous but with the change of variables
U=g^{-1}V
in the first equation and
U=Vg^{-1}
in the second.
Thus for any
g\in G
, the matrix
\pi^{\lambda}(g)
commutes with the integral in (
11
). By Schur lemma this integral must be proportional to identity. The proportionality constant,
\delta_{\lambda}(n)
, can be establish by taking trace of both sides of equation (
11
).
∎

Corollary 12
.

For any irreducible finite-dimensional representation
\pi_{\lambda}
of
U(d)
with the highest weight
\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}
, an integer
n
and an exact
(|n|\cdot t)
-design
\{\mathcal{D},\nu_{\mathcal{D}}\}
we have

\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n})=\delta_{\lambda}(n)\text{1}_{d_{\lambda}}.

Proof.

From Lemma
10
we know that

\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n})=\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n}),

and from Lemma
11
that

\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}\pi_{\lambda}(U^{n})=\delta_{\lambda}(n)\text{1}_{d_{\lambda}}.

∎

From the orthogonality of characters we know that for
\lambda\neq 0

\int_{G}d\mu(U)\chi_{\lambda}(U)=\int_{G}d\mu(U)\,1\cdot\chi_{\lambda}(U)=\int_{G}d\mu(U)\overline{\chi_{0}(U)}\chi_{\lambda}(U)=0,

thus
\delta_{\lambda}(\pm 1)=0
. In case of
\delta_{\lambda}(\pm 2)
we use a well known fact that

\int_{G}\chi_{\lambda}\left(U^{\pm 2}\right)d\mu(U)

is equal to
1
,
0
or
-1
for
\pi_{\lambda}
real, complex or quaternionic respectively. Thus

\delta_{\lambda}(\pm 2)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}}&\mathrm{if}\,\pi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{real}\\
0&\mathrm{if}\,\pi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{complex}\\
-\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}}&\mathrm{if}\,\pi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{quaternionic}\\
\end{cases}.

To calculate
\delta_{\lambda}(n)
for
|n|>2
we will use the following result from
[
32
]
.

Fact 10
.

Let
G
be a finite dimensional semisimple Lie group. Let
\pi_{\lambda^{s}}
be an irreducible representation of
G
with highest weight
\lambda^{s}
,
\mathcal{W}
the Weyl group of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\rho
the half sum of positive roots. Then for
n\neq 0

\delta_{\lambda^{s}}(n)=\frac{1}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{W}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\;m_{\lambda^{s}}\left(\frac{\rho-\sigma\cdot\rho}{n}\right).

Immediate conclusion from Fact
10
is that there is
n_{0}
such that for
n\geq n_{0}
elements
\frac{\rho-\sigma\cdot\rho}{n}
are integral elements if and only if
\sigma\cdot\rho=\rho
. In the next lemma we calculate
n_{0}
for the group
SU(d)
.

Lemma 13
.

For the group
SU(d)
the constant
n_{0}
is equal to
d+1
and for
n\geq n_{0}
the formula (
10
) simplifies to:

\delta_{\lambda^{s}}(n)=\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}(0)}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}.

(31)

Proof.

First, let us calculate
\rho
:

\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\alpha_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}L_{i}-L_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(\frac{d-1}{2}-i+1\right)L_{i}.

Thus for
i\neq j
we have
\rho_{i}\neq\rho_{j}
and the only
\sigma\in S_{d}
such that
\sigma\cdot\rho=\rho
is the identity, which proves the simplified formula (
31
). In order to find minimal
n_{0}
note first that

(\rho-\sigma\cdot\rho)_{i}=\rho_{i}-\rho_{\sigma(i)}=\sigma(i)-i,

and that for
\frac{\rho-\sigma\cdot\rho}{n}
to be an integral element it is required that for all
1\leq i\leq d-1

\braket{\rho-\sigma\cdot\rho|\alpha_{i,i+1}}=(\rho-\sigma\cdot\rho)_{i}-(\rho-\sigma\cdot\rho)_{i+1}=\sigma(i)-\sigma(i+1)+1,

is divisible by
n
or equivalently

\forall_{1\leq i\leq d-1}\exists_{C_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}}\quad\sigma(i+1)-\sigma(i)=C_{i}n+1.

(32)

If
n=d
then
\sigma(i)=i-1\;\mathrm{mod}\;d
satisfies this condition thus
n_{0}>d
. On the other hand, for
n>d
the condition
|\sigma(i+1)-\sigma(i)|\leq d-1
implies that the constant
C_{i}
in (
32
) has to be zero for all
i
. Let us choose
j
such that
j\neq d
and
\sigma(j)=d
then

1=C_{j}n+1=\sigma(j+1)-\sigma(j)=\sigma(j+1)-d\quad\Rightarrow\quad\sigma(j+1)=d+1,

what is an obvious contradiction and
\sigma^{-1}(d)
has to be
d
but this implies
\sigma(d-1)=\sigma(d)-1=d-1
and analogously for all
1\leq i\leq d
we have
\sigma(i)=i
. Therefore for
n>d
the only
\sigma
satisfying (
32
) is the trivial one.
∎

In the next lemma we show when the representation
\pi_{\lambda^{s}}
has the weight
0
. In particular it implies that for every
\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}
the representation
\pi_{\lambda^{s}}
has the weight
0
.

Lemma 14
.

Suppose
\pi_{\lambda^{s}}
is a representation of
SU(d)
with the highest weight
\lambda^{s}
. Then the following are equivalent:

1.

\pi_{\lambda^{s}}
has weight
0
,

2.

\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}j\lambda^{s}_{j}
is an integer,

3.

there exists an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of
U(d)
with highest weight
\lambda
-
\pi_{\lambda}
such that
\Sigma(\lambda)=0
and
\pi_{\lambda}|_{SU(d)}=\pi_{\lambda^{s}}
.

Proof.

We will start with 1)
\Rightarrow
2).

From Fact
7
there exist
c_{1},...,c_{d-1}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\lambda^{s}=\lambda^{s}-0=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}c_{i}\alpha_{i,i+1},

thus

\lambda^{s}_{j}=\braket{\lambda^{s}|\alpha_{j,j+1}}=\braket{\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}c_{i}\alpha_{i,i+1}|\alpha_{j,j+1}}=\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}c_{i}\braket{\alpha_{i,i+1}|\alpha_{j,j+1}}=-c_{j-1}+2c_{j}-c_{j+1},

where we assume
c_{0}=0=c_{d}
. It follows

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}j\lambda^{s}_{j}=\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}j(-c_{j-1}+2c_{j}-c_{j+1})=

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d}\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{d-2}\left[-(j-1)+2j-(j+1)\right]c_{j}+\left[-(d-2)+2(d-1)\right]c_{d-1}\right\}=

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{d}c_{d-1}=c_{d-1}\in\mathbb{Z}.

2)
\Rightarrow
3)

For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}

\lambda=(m+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\lambda^{s}_{i},\;m+\sum_{i=2}^{d-1}\lambda^{s}_{i},\;...\;,\;m+\lambda^{s}_{d-1},\;m),

satisfies
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
and
i<j\,\Rightarrow\,\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{j}
. Therefore there exists an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of
U(d)
with highest weight
\lambda
-
\pi_{\lambda}
. From Lemma
5
clearly
\pi_{\lambda}|_{SU(d)}=\pi_{\lambda^{s}}
and for
m=-\frac{1}{d}\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}j\lambda^{s}_{j}
we have
\Sigma(\lambda)=0
.

3)
\Rightarrow
1)

Let us choose
\forall_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\;t_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{d!}
then
\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}t_{\sigma}=1
and

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}t_{\sigma}\sigma\cdot\lambda=\frac{1}{d!}(\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\lambda_{\sigma(1)},\;...\;,\;\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\lambda_{\sigma(d-1)})=\frac{1}{d}(\Sigma(\lambda),\;...\;,\;\Sigma(\lambda))=0.

What is more for
c_{1}=\lambda_{1}
and
\forall_{2\leq i\leq d-1}\;c_{i}=\lambda_{i-1}+\lambda_{i}
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}c_{i}\alpha_{i,i+1}=\lambda=\lambda-0.

Hence by Fact
7
weight
0
is in
\pi_{\lambda}
what implies that weight
0
is in
\pi_{\lambda^{s}}
.

∎

As an example we next explicitly calculate the value of
\delta_{\lambda}(n)
for
G=SU(2)
.

Lemma 15
.

The constant in Lemma
11
for
SU(2)
and non-trivial
\pi_{\lambda}
is given by

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}(n)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $n=0$,}\\
0&\text{if $n=\pm 1$,}\\
\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}}&\text{if $n\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{-1,0,1\}$ and $\lambda^{s}$-even, }\\
-\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}}&\text{if $n=\pm 2$ and $\lambda^{s}$-odd}\\
0&\text{if $n\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{-2,0,2\}$ and $\lambda^{s}$-odd}\end{cases}

Proof.

The result for
n=0
and
n=1
follows from definition of
\delta_{\lambda}
and irreducibility of
\pi_{\lambda}
. For remaining
n
’s we will use Fact
10
.
First, let us notify that for
SU(2)
:

\Delta=\{\alpha_{12}\},\qquad\alpha_{12}=2,\qquad\rho=1,\qquad[1\;2]\cdot\mu=-\mu,

then from Kostant formula
8
:

m_{\lambda^{s}}(\mu)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,p(\sigma\cdot(\lambda^{s}+\rho)-(\mu+\rho))=p(\lambda^{s}-\mu)-p(-\lambda^{s}-\mu-2).

(33)

Recall that
p(m)
is the number of ways
m
can be expressed as a linear combinations of positive simple roots with non-negative integer coefficients so in our case

p(m)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $m$-even and non-negative,}\\
0&\text{if $m$-odd or negative.}\end{cases}

This combined with the fact that
-\lambda^{s}-\mu-2>0
implies
\|\mu\|_{1}>\|\lambda\|_{1}
and Lemma
7
simplifies (
33
) to

m_{\lambda^{s}}(\mu)=p(\lambda^{s}-\mu)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $(\lambda^{s}-\mu)$-even and non-negative,}\\
0&\text{if $(\lambda^{s}-\mu)$-odd or negative.}\end{cases}

Now, we are ready to use formula (
10
) which for
SU(2)
has a form

\delta_{\lambda^{s}}(n)=\frac{1}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}\left(m_{\lambda^{s}}(0)-m_{\lambda^{s}}\left(\frac{2}{n}\right)\right).

Hence

\delta_{\lambda^{s}}(\pm 2)=\frac{1}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}\left(m_{\lambda^{s}}(0)-m_{\lambda^{s}}(\pm 1)\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}&\text{if $\lambda^{s}$-even,}\\
-\frac{1}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}&\text{if $\lambda^{s}$-odd.}\end{cases},

and for
|n|\geq 3

\delta_{\lambda^{s}}(n)=\frac{1}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}m_{\lambda^{s}}(0)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}&\text{if $\lambda^{s}$-even,}\\
0&\text{if $\lambda^{s}$-odd.}\end{cases}

∎

6
Main results

In this section we will consider two types of gate-sets:

1.

\mathcal{S}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\}
, where
U_{k}
’s are independent random unitaries from an exact
t
-design
\mathcal{D}
chosen according to the measure
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}
. Such
\mathcal{S}
will be called
t
-random gate-set
.

2.

\mathcal{S}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\}\cup\{U_{1}^{-1},\ldots,U_{n}^{-1}\}
, where
U_{k}
’s are independent random unitaries from an exact
t
-design
\mathcal{D}
chosen according to the measure
\nu_{\mathcal{D}}
. Such
\mathcal{S}
will be called
symmetric
t
-random gate-set
.

Note that in the symmetric case we have
U_{k}\in\mathcal{D}
but not necessarily
U_{k}^{-1}\in\mathcal{D}
so to obtain a symmetric
t
-random gate-set of size
2n
we draw
n
gates from
\{\mathcal{D},\nu_{\mathcal{D}}\}
and then we add to the set their inverses. We also want to point out that since
\{U(d),\mu\}
is an exact
t
-design for any
t
all theorems we present below apply to gate-sets where gates are Haar random or Haar random with inverses (we could call them
\infty
-random gate-sets or symmetric
\infty
-random gate-sets).

In order to simplify the notation we often denote the cardinality of
\mathcal{S}
by
\mathcal{S}
(instead of
|\mathcal{S}|
). The moment operators associated with the above two types of random sets of gates are random matrices. When
\mathcal{S}
is symmetric they are actually random Hermitian matrices. Using inequalities listed in Section
2
we derive upper bounds on
\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq\delta\right)
and
\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},t)\geq\delta\right)
. Before we proceed with concrete inequalities we note that

Lemma 16
.

Assume that
\mathcal{S}\subset U(d)
is a random set of quantum gates and that for every
\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}
we have
\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq\delta\right)\leq F(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S})
. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},t)\geq\delta\right)\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}}F(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S}).

Proof.

By (
24
) and using the union bound for probabilities we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},t)\geq\delta\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathrm{sup}_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}}\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq\delta\right)\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}}\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq\delta\right)\leq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}}F(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S}).

∎

Thus, to find a bound on
\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},t)\geq\delta\right)
it is enough to find bounds on
\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq\delta\right)
,
\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}
, which we do in next sections.

6.1
Bernstein type bounds

In this section we derive bounds on
\mathbb{P}\left(\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda}\|\geq\delta\right)
using Bernstein inequality (see Section
2
). It is worth to mention that computationally this is the simplest derivation presented in this paper as it requires only the knowledge of the second moments.

Theorem 17
.

Let
\lambda
be an element of
\Lambda_{t}
and
\mathcal{S}
be a
t
-random gate-set or a symmetric
(2t)
-random gate-set and
\nu_{\mathcal{S}}
a uniform measure. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq\delta\right)\leq\begin{cases}B_{1}(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S}),&\,\mathrm{when}\,\mathcal{S}-\textrm{symmetric {\color[rgb]{0,0,0}$(2t)$-}random gate-set}\\
B_{2}(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S}),&\,\mathrm{when}\,\mathcal{S}-\textrm{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}$t$-}random gate-set}\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle B_{1}(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S})=2d_{\lambda}\exp\left({\frac{-3\mathcal{S}\delta^{2}}{6\left(1+\delta_{\lambda}(2)\right)+4\delta}}\right),

\displaystyle B_{2}(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S})=2d_{\lambda}\exp\left({\frac{-3\mathcal{S}\delta^{2}}{6+2\delta}}\right),

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}(2)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}}&\mathrm{if}\,\pi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{real}\\
0&\mathrm{if}\,\pi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{complex}\\
-\frac{1}{d_{\lambda}}&\mathrm{if}\,\pi_{\lambda}\,\mathrm{quaternionic}\\
\end{cases}.

Proof.

In this proof we will use Fact
3
where we take
X=\sum_{k}X_{k}
to be equal
T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\sum_{U\in\mathcal{S}}\pi_{\lambda}(U)
.

Let us start with the Haar random case. In this case all elements of
\mathcal{S}
are independent. Thus we will put
X_{k}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\pi_{\lambda}(U_{k})
for
k=1,...,\mathcal{S}
. Then from Corollary
12

\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}X_{k}=\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\pi_{\lambda}(U)\right)=\frac{\delta_{\lambda}(1)}{\mathcal{S}}\text{1}_{d_{\lambda}}=0.

Since
\pi_{\lambda}
is unitary we have

\displaystyle\|X_{k}\|=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\|\pi_{\lambda}(U_{k})\|=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}},

\displaystyle X_{k}X_{k}^{\dagger}=X_{k}^{\dagger}X_{k}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}^{2}}\text{1}_{\delta_{\lambda}}.

Hence
L=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}
and
\nu=\|\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{S}}\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}(X_{k}X_{k}^{\dagger})\|=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}
satisfy conditions from Fact
3
and the result follows.

When
\mathcal{S}
is symmetric we put
X_{k}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\left(\pi_{\lambda}\left(U\right)+\pi_{\lambda}\left(U^{-1}\right)\right)
. From Corollary
12
we have
\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}X_{k}=0
. Then using triangle inequality we get

\|X_{k}\|=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}}\|\pi_{\lambda}(U)+\pi_{\lambda}(U^{-1})\|\leq\frac{2}{\mathcal{S}}\|\pi_{\lambda}(U)\|=\frac{2}{\mathcal{S}},

and from hermiticity of
X_{k}

X_{k}X_{k}^{\dagger}=X_{k}^{\dagger}X_{k}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{S}^{2}}\left(\pi_{\lambda}\left(U^{2}\right)+2\text{1}_{d_{\lambda}}+\pi_{\lambda}\left(U^{-2}\right)\right).

Then using Corollary
12
once more we obtain

\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}\left(X_{k}X_{k}^{\dagger}\right)=\frac{2}{\mathcal{S}^{2}}\left(\delta_{\lambda}(2)+1\right)\text{1}_{d_{\lambda}}.

Hence for
L=\frac{2}{\mathcal{S}}
and

\nu=\|\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{S}/2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}}(X_{k}X_{k}^{\dagger})\|=\frac{\delta_{\lambda}(2)+1}{\mathcal{S}},

the result follows from Fact
3
.

∎

6.2
Master bounds

In this section we derive Theorems
1
and
2
from Introduction using the formula for the master bound (see Section
2
). This derivation requires knowledge of all moments and is much more accurate than the Bernstein bound given in the previous section.

6.2.1
Haar random gate-sets

For
t
-random gate-sets the derivation of the bound for
\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq\delta\right)
turns out to be relatively simple.

Theorem 18
.

Let
\lambda
be an element of
\Lambda_{t}
and
\mathcal{S}
be a
t
-random gate-set and
\nu_{\mathcal{S}}
a uniform measure. Then for any
\delta<1

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq\delta\right)\leq F(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S}),

where

\displaystyle F(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S})=\frac{2d_{\lambda}}{\left(1+\delta\right)^{\frac{\mathcal{S}}{2}\left(1+\delta\right)}\left(1-\delta\right)^{\frac{\mathcal{S}}{2}\left(1-\delta\right)}}=\frac{2d_{\lambda}}{(1-\delta^{2})^{\frac{S}{2}}}e^{-\delta\mathcal{S}\,\mathrm{arctanh}(\delta)}.

Proof.

To compute the master bound for non-Hermitian matrix (Fact
2
) we need to compute
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}}\exp\left(\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{H}(\pi_{\lambda}(U))\right)
. First, let us note that for any unitary matrix
U\in U(d_{\lambda})
:

\mathcal{H}(U)^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&U\\
U^{\dagger}&0\end{pmatrix}^{2}=\begin{pmatrix}UU^{\dagger}&0\\
0&U^{\dagger}U\end{pmatrix}=\text{1}_{2d_{\lambda}}.

Since
\pi_{\lambda}
is unitary we have

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}}\exp\left(\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{H}(\pi_{\lambda}(U))\right)=\cosh{\left(\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)}\text{1}_{2d_{\lambda}}+\sinh{\left(\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}}\mathcal{H}(\pi_{\lambda}(U)).

From Corollary
12
and the orthogonality of characters:

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}}\mathcal{H}(\pi_{\lambda}(U))=\mathcal{H}\left({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}}\pi_{\lambda}(U)\right)=0.

Hence the right hand side of the master bound is:

\inf_{\theta>0}e^{-\theta\delta}\mathrm{tr}\exp\left(\mathcal{S}\log{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}}e^{\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{H}(X_{k})}\right)=\inf_{\theta>0}2d_{\lambda}\;e^{-\theta\delta}\cosh^{\mathcal{S}}\left(\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right).

By calculating derivative we easily get that the infimum is obtained for
\theta=\mathcal{S}\,\mathrm{arctanh}(\delta)
and the bound is:

F(\delta,\lambda,\mathcal{S})=\frac{2d_{\lambda}}{\left(1+\delta\right)^{\frac{\mathcal{S}}{2}\left(1+\delta\right)}\left(1-\delta\right)^{\frac{\mathcal{S}}{2}\left(1-\delta\right)}}=\frac{2d_{\lambda}}{(1-\delta^{2})^{\frac{S}{2}}}e^{-\delta\mathcal{S}\,\mathrm{arctanh}(\delta)}.

∎

6.2.2
Symmetric Haar random gate-sets

In the following we develop new bounds which are based on the fact that we can calculate explicitly the expected values that appear in the master bound (see Section
2
). In order to perform this calculation we need to know all moments
\delta_{\lambda}(n)
. To do this for
n\leq d
we use an explicit formula from Fact
10
and for
n>d
we use it simplified form from Lemma
13
. We derive the bound in Theorem
19
which, using Fact
11
, we next optimize to obtain the main result of this section, that is, Theorem
20
.

Theorem 19
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a symmetric Haar random gate-set from
SU(d)
and
\nu_{\mathcal{S}}
a uniform measure. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda^{s})\|\geq\delta\right)\leq d_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\mathrm{inf}_{\theta>0}\;e^{-\theta\delta}F(\theta,\lambda^{s},\mathcal{S})+\mathrm{inf}_{\theta>0}\;e^{-\theta\delta}F(-\theta,\lambda^{s},\mathcal{S})\right],

(34)

where

\displaystyle F(\theta,\lambda^{s},\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}e^{\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}}+\sum_{k=-d}^{d}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(k)I_{|k|}\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)\right]^{\frac{\mathcal{S}}{2}},

(35)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(k)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1-\frac{m^{0}_{\lambda^{s}}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}&\text{if $k=0$},\\
\frac{1}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in S_{d}\\
\sigma\neq id\end{subarray}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)m_{\lambda^{s}}\left(\frac{\rho-\sigma\cdot\rho}{k}\right)&\text{if $k\neq 0$},\end{cases}

and
I_{n}(x)
is
n
-th modified Bessel function of the first kind.

Proof.

Clearly, the bound from Fact
1
is of the form

\mathrm{inf}_{\theta>0}\;e^{-\theta\delta}\tilde{F}(\theta,\lambda^{s},\mathcal{S})+\mathrm{inf}_{\theta>0}\;e^{-\theta\delta}\tilde{F}(-\theta,\lambda^{s},\mathcal{S}),

where

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\theta,\lambda^{s},\mathcal{S})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}\exp\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\mathcal{S}/2}\log{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}e^{\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}X_{U_{k},\lambda^{s}}}\right),

\displaystyle X_{U,\lambda^{s}}

\displaystyle=\pi_{\lambda^{s}}(U)+\pi_{\lambda^{s}}(U^{-1}).

Thus our main objective is to compute the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}e^{\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}X_{U,\lambda^{s}}}
. Using the binomial formula one easily gets:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}X_{U,\lambda^{s}}^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}\pi_{\lambda^{s}}(U^{n-2k})=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\delta_{\lambda^{s}}(n-2k)\right].

(36)

From Lemma
13
we know that for
|n-2k|>d
the value of
\delta_{\lambda^{s}}
is
\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}
. Thus Eq. (
36
) becomes

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}X_{U,\lambda^{s}}^{n}=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\choose k}\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=\lceil\frac{n-d}{2}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{n+d}{2}\rfloor}{n\choose k}\left(\delta_{\lambda^{s}}(n-2k)-\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}\right)\right]=

\displaystyle=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\,2^{n}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=\lceil\frac{n-d}{2}\rceil}^{\lfloor\frac{n+d}{2}\rfloor}{n\choose k}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(n-2k)\right],

where we assume
{n\choose k}=0
for
n<k
or
k<0
. We will consider separately cases for different parities of
d
and
n
:

\bullet\quad d=2p
and
n=2l
:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}X_{U,\lambda^{s}}^{2l}=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\,2^{2l}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=l-p}^{l+p}{2l\choose k}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2l-2k)\right]=

\displaystyle=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\,2^{2l}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=-p}^{p}{2l\choose l+k}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2k)\right],

\bullet\quad d=2p
and
n=2l+1
:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}X_{U,\lambda^{s}}^{2l+1}=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\,2^{2l+1}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=l+1-p}^{l+p}{2l+1\choose k}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2l+1-2k)\right]=

\displaystyle=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\,2^{2l+1}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=-p+1}^{p}{2l+1\choose l+k}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2k-1)\right].

Then we have:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}e^{\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}X_{U,\lambda^{s}}}=\text{1}\left[\frac{m^{0}_{\lambda^{s}}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}e^{\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}}+\sum_{k=-p}^{p}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2k)I_{|2k|}\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)+\sum_{k=-p+1}^{p}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2k-1)I_{|2k-1|}\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)\right]=

\displaystyle=\text{1}\left[\frac{m^{0}_{\lambda^{s}}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}e^{\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}}+\sum_{k=-d}^{d}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(k)I_{|k|}\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)\right].

(37)

\bullet\quad d=2p+1
and
n=2l
:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}X_{U,\lambda^{s}}^{2l}=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\,2^{2l}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=l-p}^{l+p}{2l\choose k}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2l-2k)\right]=

\displaystyle=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\,2^{2l}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=-p}^{p}{2l\choose l+k}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2k)\right],

\bullet\quad d=2p+1
and
n=2l+1
:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}X_{U,\lambda^{s}}^{2l+1}=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\,2^{2l+1}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=l-p}^{l+p+1}{2l+1\choose k}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2l+1-2k)\right]=

\displaystyle=\text{1}_{\lambda^{s}}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\,2^{2l+1}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}+\sum_{k=-p}^{p+1}{2l+1\choose l+k}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2k-1)\right].

Then we have:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}e^{\frac{\theta}{\mathcal{S}}X_{U,\lambda^{s}}}=\text{1}\left[\frac{m^{0}_{\lambda^{s}}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}e^{\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}}+\sum_{k=-p}^{p}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2k)I_{|2k|}\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)+\sum_{k=-p}^{p+1}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(2k-1)I_{|2k-1|}\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)\right]=

\displaystyle=\text{1}\left[\frac{m^{0}_{\lambda^{s}}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}e^{\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}}+\sum_{k=-d}^{d}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(k)I_{|k|}\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)\right].

(38)

Combining Eq.
37
and Eq.
38
with Fact
1
we get the desired result.

∎

Note that from Kostant formula (
8
) the multiplicities
m^{\mu}_{\lambda^{s}}
are polynomials in coefficients of
\lambda^{s}
with degree up to
\frac{(d-2)(d-1)}{2}
while from Weyl dimension formula (
20
) the dimension
d_{\lambda^{s}}
is a polynomial of degree
\frac{(d-1)d}{2}
. Thus for
\lambda^{s}
with large coefficients the expression in bracket in Eq. (
34
) is approximately equal to
\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(0)I_{0}(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}})
. In order to proceed we need the following property of the modified Bessel functions.

Fact 11
.

[
45
]
For any
n\geq 0
we have the following upper and lower bounds on the ratio of the modified Bessel functions

\displaystyle\frac{x}{n-\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+x^{2}}}<\frac{I_{n}(x)}{I_{n-1}(x)}<\frac{x}{n-1+\sqrt{\left(n+1\right)^{2}+x^{2}}}.

Combining Fact
11
with Theorem
19
we arrive at

Theorem 20
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a symmetric Haar random gate-set from
SU(d)
and
\nu_{\mathcal{S}}
a uniform measure. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq\delta\right)\leq d_{\lambda^{s}}\,e^{-\frac{\mathcal{S}\delta^{2}}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}}\left[F\left(\frac{\mathcal{S}\delta}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}},\lambda,\mathcal{S}\right)+F\left(-\frac{\mathcal{S}\delta}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}},\lambda,\mathcal{S}\right)\right],

where
F(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
is given by (
35
).

Proof.

In order to find the best bound we need to determine
\theta
that realizes

\displaystyle\mathrm{inf}_{\theta>0}\;e^{-\theta\delta}\left[\frac{m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}}{d_{\lambda^{s}}}e^{\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}}+\sum_{k=-d}^{d}\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(k)I_{|k|}\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)\right]^{\frac{\mathcal{S}}{2}}.

As finding minimum of the above functions is analytically intractable, in both cases we look for
\theta
that minimizes
e^{-\theta\delta}I_{0}\left(\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}\right)^{\frac{\mathcal{S}}{2}}
. Taking the derivative with respect to
\theta
we get

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{I_{1}\left(x\right)}{I_{0}\left(x\right)},

where
x=\frac{2\theta}{\mathcal{S}}
. Using the upper bound for the ration of modified Bessel functions from Fact
11
we get

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{x}{\sqrt{4+x^{2}}}\,\implies\,x=\frac{2\delta}{\sqrt{1-\delta^{2}}}.

The result follows.
∎

6.3
Bernstein and master bounds for random circuits

Let us consider a scenario where we draw
m
independent gates from the exact
t_{0}
-design
\{\mathcal{D},\nu_{\mathcal{D}}\}
and combine them into a circuit
\mathbf{U}=(U_{1},...,U_{m})
. We repeat this procedure some number of times and in this way we construct a set of random circuits
\mathcal{R}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\times m}
. Then for
f\in\mathcal{H}_{t,m}
we ask how much averaging of
f
over
\mathcal{R}
differs from averaging over all circuits
\mathcal{D}^{\times m}
. More precisely we want to bound the tail probability

\mathbb{P}(\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{D}}^{\times m},t}-T_{\nu_{\mathcal{R}},t}\|\geq\delta),

for
\nu_{\mathcal{R}}
uniform,
0<\delta<1
and
t\leq t_{0}
. Note that since
\mathcal{D}
is an exact
t_{0}
-design for any
t\leq t_{0}
we have:

T_{\nu_{\mathcal{D}}^{\times m},t}=\underset{U_{1}\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}...\underset{U_{m}\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}U_{i}^{t,t}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\left(\underset{U_{i}\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}U_{i}^{t,t}\right)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}T_{\mu,t}=T_{\mu,t}^{\otimes m}.

Thus

\mathbb{P}(\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{D}}^{\times m},t}-T_{\nu_{\mathcal{C}},t}\|\geq\delta)=\mathbb{P}(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{C}},t)\geq\delta)\leq\sum_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda_{t,m}}\mathbb{P}(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{C}},\bm{\lambda})\geq\delta).

With this we can prove Bernstein and master bounds for random circuits in a very similar way to the case with random gates. For example, consider calculation of moments of
\pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\mathbf{U})
for
n\cdot t\leq t_{0}
:

\underset{U_{1}\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}...\underset{U_{m}\sim\nu_{\mathcal{D}}}{\mathbb{E}}\pi_{\bm{\lambda}}(\mathbf{U})^{n}=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{m}\left(\underset{U_{i}\sim\nu_{D}}{\mathbb{E}}\pi_{\lambda_{i}}(U_{i})^{n}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}(n)\text{1}.

Therefore Bernstein, symmetric Bernstein and master bounds for random circuits are almost exactly the same as for random gates with only small changes summarized in the below diagram:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathcal{C},

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\sum_{\bm{\lambda}\in\Lambda_{t,m}},

and then for
\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{m})\in\Lambda_{t,m}
:

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda}(n)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\delta_{\bm{\lambda}}(n):=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\delta_{\lambda_{i}}(n),

\displaystyle d_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow d_{\bm{\lambda}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{m}d_{\lambda_{i}}.

In case of symmetric master bound we have to additionally substitute

m_{\lambda^{s}}^{0}\longrightarrow m^{0}_{\bm{\lambda}^{s}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{m}m_{\lambda_{i}^{s}}^{0},

and redefine
\gamma_{\lambda^{s}}(n)
to

\gamma_{\bm{\lambda}^{s}}(k)=\begin{cases}1-\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{m^{0}_{\lambda^{s}_{i}}}{d_{\lambda^{s}_{i}}}&\text{if $k=0$},\\
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left[\frac{1}{d_{\lambda^{s}_{i}}}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{d}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)m_{\lambda^{s}_{i}}\left(\frac{\rho-\sigma\cdot\rho}{k}\right)\right]-\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{m^{0}_{\lambda^{s}_{i}}}{d_{\lambda^{s}_{i}}}&\text{if $k\neq 0$}.\end{cases}

6.4
Concentration of
\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},t)
and
\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)
around their expected values

In this section we derive Theorems
3
and
4
from Introduction about the concentration properties of
\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},t)
and
\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)
using Fact
4
.

Theorem 21
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset SU(d)
be a Haar random gate-set. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},t)\geq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},t)+\alpha\right)\leq\exp\left({\frac{-d\mathcal{S}\alpha^{2}}{32t^{2}}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset SU(d)
be any set of cardinality
n>1
. For the convenience we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{2}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}\}
two exemplary sets
\mathcal{S}
. Let

\displaystyle F(U_{1},\ldots,U_{n})=\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{1}},t}-T_{\mu,t}\|.

We need to show that there is a constant
L
such that for any
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
we have

\displaystyle|F(U_{1},\ldots,U_{n})-F(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})|\leq L\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}-V_{i}\|^{2}_{F}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In order to determine the value of
L
we go through the following chain of inequalities

\displaystyle\left|\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{1}},t}-T_{\mu,t}\|-\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{2}},t}-T_{\mu,t}\|\right|\leq\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{1}},t}-T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{2}},t}\|\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}^{t,t}-V_{i}^{t,t}\|\leq

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2t}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}-V_{i}\|\leq\frac{2t}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}-V_{i}\|_{F}\leq\frac{2t}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}-V_{i}\|^{2}_{F}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus the Lipschitz constant is
L=\frac{2t}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}}}
. Knowing the value of
L
we use Fact
4
and obtain the result.
∎

Theorem 22
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset SU(d)
be a Haar random gate-set. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)\geq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}},\lambda)+\alpha\right)\leq\exp\left({\frac{-d\mathcal{S}\alpha^{2}}{2\pi^{2}\|\lambda\|_{1}^{2}}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset SU(d)
be any set of cardinality
n>1
. For the convenience we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{2}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}\}
two exemplary sets
\mathcal{S}
. Let

\displaystyle F(U_{1},\ldots,U_{n})=\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{1}},\lambda}\|.

We need to show that there is a constant
L
such that for any
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
we have

\displaystyle|F(U_{1},\ldots,U_{n})-F(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})|\leq L\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}-V_{i}\|^{2}_{F}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In order to determine the value of
L
we go through the following chain of inequalities

\displaystyle\left|\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{1}},\lambda}\|-\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{2}},\lambda}\|\right|\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\pi_{\lambda}({U_{i}})-\pi_{\lambda}({V_{i}})\|\leq

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi\|\lambda\|_{1}}{2n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}-V_{i}\|\leq\frac{\pi\|\lambda\|_{1}}{2\sqrt{n}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|U_{i}-V_{i}\|^{2}_{F}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where in the second inequality we used Lemma
9
. Thus the Lipschitz constant is
L=\frac{\pi\|\lambda\|_{1}}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{S}}}
. Knowing the value of
L
we use Fact
4
and obtain the result.
∎

6.4.1
d
-mode beamsplitters built from random
2
-mode beamsplitters

In this section we consider the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{H}_{d}
, where
\mathcal{H}_{k}\simeq\mathbb{C}
,
d>2
. We will call spaces
\mathcal{H}_{k}
modes. For a matrix
B\in SU(2)
, which we call a
2
-mode beamsplitter, we define matrices
B^{ij}
,
i\neq j
, to be the matrices that act on a
2
-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{H}_{i}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{j}\subset\mathcal{H}
as
B
and on the other components of
\mathcal{H}
as the identity. This way a matrix
B\in SU(2)
gives
d(d-1)
matrices in
SU(d)
. Applying this procedure to a Haar random gate-set set
S\subset SU(2)
we obtain random gate-set
S^{d}
(see
[
37
,
38
]
). We are interested in efficiency of
\mathcal{S}^{d}
.

Theorem 23
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subset SU(2)
be a Haar random gate-set and
\mathcal{S}^{d}\subset SU(d)
the corresponding
d
-mode gate-set. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}^{d}},t)\geq{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}^{d}},t)+\alpha\right)\leq\exp\left({\frac{-\mathcal{S}\alpha^{2}}{16t^{2}}}\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}\subset SU(2)
be two gate-sets of the same size
n
, i.e.
\mathcal{S}_{1}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}\}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{2}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}\}
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{d}
, and
\mathcal{S}_{2}^{d}
the corresponding
d
-mode gate-sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}^{d}=\{U_{k}^{i,j}|k\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\,i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,i\,\neq j\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}^{d}=\{V_{k}^{i,j}|k\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\,i,j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}\,,\,i\neq j\}.

Similarly as in the proofs of Theorems
21
and
22
we calculate,

\displaystyle\left|\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{1}^{d}},t}-T_{\mu,t}\|-\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{2}^{d}},t}-T_{\mu,t}\|\right|\leq\|T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{1}^{d}},t}-T_{\nu_{\mathcal{S}_{2}^{d}},t}\|\leq

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq d}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|(U_{k}^{i,j})^{t,t}-(V_{k}^{i,j})^{t,t}\|\leq\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq d}\frac{2t}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|U_{k}-V_{k}\|_{F}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d(d-1)}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq d}\frac{2t}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|U_{k}-V_{k}\|^{2}_{F}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{2t}{\sqrt{n}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|U_{k}-V_{k}\|^{2}_{F}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus the Lipschitz constant is
L=\frac{2t}{\sqrt{\mathcal{S}}}
. Knowing the value of
L
we use Fact
4
and obtain the result.
∎

As a conclusion we see that a Haar random gate-set
\mathcal{S}\subset SU(2)
gives the gate-set
S^{d}\subset SU(d)
for which
\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}^{d}},t)
has the same concentration rate around the mean as a Haar random gate-set
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subset SU(d)
of size:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\frac{2\mathcal{S}}{d}.

We note, however, that using this approach one cannot say anything about the relationship between
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}^{d}},t)
and
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\underset{U\sim\mu}{\mathbb{E}}}\delta(\nu_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}},t)
.

The twisted conjugacy growth of virtually abelian groups
Karel Dekimpe, Maarten Lathouwers

Abstract
In this paper, we study the asymptotics of several growth functions related to twisted conjugacy on virtually abelian groups. First, we study the twisted conjugacy growth function, which counts the number of twisted conjugacy classes intersecting the ball of radius r r around the identity element. Thereafter we study the function that measures the size of the intersection of a given twisted conjugacy class with the balls around the identity element. Finally, we study the number of induced twisted conjugacy classes in certain finite quotients of the given virtually abelian group. In each of these cases we obtain a polynomial asymptotic behaviour of these growth functions.



Introduction

For any group
G
that is finitely generated by
S
, we will consider the nested sets
B_{G}^{S}(r)
(with
r\in{\wiskunde N}_{>0}
) of
G
defined by

B_{G}^{S}(r):=\{s_{1}\dots s_{k}\>|\>k\in\{0,1,\dots,r\}\text{ and }s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in S\cup S^{-1}\}

where
S^{-1}:=\{s^{-1}\>|\>s\in S\}
. The
conjugacy growth function
counts the number of conjugacy classes of
G
that intersect these sets
B_{G}^{S}(r)
. In 1988, Babenko was the first to introduce this function (
[
Bab88
]
). Over the years, many researchers studied the conjugacy growth function of a wide range of groups, for example solvable groups (
[
BdC10
]
), linear groups (
[
BCLM13
]
), graph products (
[
CHM23
]
),
\dots
. We refer to
[
GS10
]
for a more extensive and detailed overview.

Instead of considering regular conjugacy, we consider
twisted conjugacy
. More precisely, if
\varphi\in\End(G)
we say that
x,y\in G
are
\varphi
-conjugate if there exists some
z\in G
such that
x=zy\varphi(z)^{-1}
. The equivalence classes with respect to this relation are called the
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy classes
of
G
and are denoted as
[x]_{\varphi}
. Similarly as above, we define the
twisted conjugacy growth function

f_{R}^{S}(r)
to be the number of
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy classes that intersect the set
B_{G}^{S}(r)
. In this paper, we start in Section
2
by determining the twisted conjugacy growth function of finitely generated free abelian groups.

Theorem A
.

Let
S
be any finite generating set of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
and
\varphi\in\End({\wiskunde Z}^{n})
, then

f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-\rank(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}-\varphi)})\text{ as }r\to\infty.

Next, in Section
3
we use Theorem
A
to describe the twisted conjugacy growth function with respect to an endomorphism of any finitely generated virtually abelian group. Ciobanu, Hermiller, Holt and Rees studied in 2016 (
[
CHHR16
, section 5]
) the conjugacy growth of these groups.

Theorem B
.

Let
S
be any finite generating set of a finitely generated virtually abelian group
G
and
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
a torsion-free abelian subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
, then

f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta\left(r^{\rank(N)-\min\limits_{gN\in G/N}\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{N})}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\#B_{G}^{S}(r)=\Theta\left(r^{\rank(N)}\right)\text{ as }r\to\infty

where
\tau_{g}:G\to G:h\mapsto ghg^{-1}
denotes conjugation by
g\in G
.

To the authors’ knowledge, Theorem
B
gives the first general explicit description of the twisted conjugacy growth function for a family of groups. However, it was defined earlier and has for example been used by Evetts (
[
Eve23
]
) to determine the conjugacy growth of higher Heisenberg groups. Moreover, Evetts proved earlier that the
conjugacy growth series
of any finitely generated virtually abelian group is rational (
[
Eve19
]
). Benson showed this in 1983 for the
standard growth series
(
[
Ben83
]
) of these groups.

Next, in Section
4
we focus on the
twisted conjugacy class growth

\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}
of one fixed
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class
[g_{0}]_{\varphi}
. This function counts the number of elements in
[g_{0}]_{\varphi}
that belong to the sets
B_{G}^{S}(r)
. For any subset
U\subset G
, the function
\beta_{U\subset G}^{S}
is also known as the
subset growth
of
U
in
G
.

Theorem C
.

Let
S
be any finite generating set of a finitely generated virtually abelian group
G
and
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
a torsion-free abelian subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
and
[g_{0}]_{\varphi}
a fixed
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class of
G
, then

\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{k})\text{ as }r\to\infty\quad\text{where}

k=\max\{\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{N})\>|\>gN\in G/N\text{ and }[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\cap p^{-1}(gN)\neq\emptyset\}

where
p:G\to G/N
denotes the projection of
G
onto
G/N
.

Theorem
C
is based on a recent paper by Dermenjian and Evetts (
[
DE23
]
) in which they argue that the
conjugacy class growth
of a finitely generated virtually abelian group is polynomial.

In Section
5
, we study the number of induced twisted conjugacy classes in certain finite quotients of
G
. More precisely, if
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\subset_{\text{fin}}G
, then we consider the finite quotients
G/(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
. Any endomorphism
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi({\wiskunde Z}^{n})\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
induces morphisms
\overline{\varphi}_{k}\in\End(G/(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n})
. The
quotient growth

f_{Q}
is defined as the number of
\overline{\varphi}_{k}
-twisted conjugacy classes of
G/(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
. It turns out that for finitely generated virtually abelian groups, the quotient growth is asymptotically the same as the twisted conjugacy growth.

Theorem D
.

Let
S
be any finite generating set of a finitely generated virtually abelian group
G
and
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
a torsion-free abelian subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
, then

f_{Q}(k)=\Theta(f_{R}^{S}(k))=\Theta\left(k^{\rank(N)-\min\limits_{gN\in G/N}\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{N})}\right)\text{ as }k\to\infty.

We conclude with Section
6
, in which we give some concrete examples and final remarks. Moreover, we describe a family of groups for which the degree in Theorem
C
becomes cleaner.

Theorem E
.

Let
S
be any finite generating set of a finitely generated virtually abelian group
G
and
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
a torsion-free abelian subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
. If
G/N
is abelian and the induced morphism
\overline{\varphi}\in\End(G/N)
is the identity on
G/N
, then for any
g_{0}\in G
it holds that

\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{g_{0}}\circ\varphi|_{N})})\text{ as }r\to\infty.

1
Twisted conjugacy growth

1.1
Definitions

For a group endomorphism
\varphi\in\End(G)
we say that two group elements
g,h\in G
are
\varphi
-twisted conjugate
if there exists some
z\in G
such that
g=zh\varphi(z)^{-1}
and we denote it with
g\sim_{\varphi}h
. The relation
\sim_{\varphi}
is an equivalence relation on
G
of which the equivalence classes are called the
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy
classes of
G
. We denote the
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class of
G
containing
g\in G
with
[g]_{\varphi}
. The number of
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy classes of
G
is called the
Reidemeister number
R(\varphi)
of
\varphi
. The
Reidemeister spectrum
\Spec(G)
of
G
contains all Reidemeister numbers of automorphisms of
G
.

We want to study the spread of the twisted conjugacy classes in the
Cayley graph
of
G
. For completeness, we include the relevant definitions with their notation.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
G
be finitely generated by
S
.

•

The
Cayley graph
\Cay(S,G)
is the graph with vertex set
G
and edge set

\{\{g,gs\}\>|\>g\in G,\>s\in(S\cup S^{-1})\setminus\{1_{G}\}\}

where
1_{G}
denotes the identity element of
G
.

•

The
word metric
d_{S}
on
G
with respect to
S
is defined for all
g,h\in G
by

d_{S}(g,h):=\min\{k\in{\wiskunde N}\>|\>\exists s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in S\cup S^{-1}:\>g^{-1}h=s_{1}\dots s_{k}\}.

•

The
word length
|g|_{S}
of
g\in G
with respect to
S
equals
d_{S}(g,e)
.

•

The
closed ball
B_{G}^{S}(r)
of radius
r\in{\wiskunde N}
is defined by

B_{G}^{S}(r):=\{g\in G\>|\>|g|_{S}\leq r\}=\{s_{1}\dots s_{k}\>|\>k\in\{0,1,\dots,r\}\text{ and }s_{1},\dots,s_{k}\in S\cup S^{-1}\}.

•

The
word growth
\beta_{G}^{S}(r)
denotes the number of elements in
B_{G}^{S}(r)
, i.e.
\beta_{G}^{S}(r):=\#B_{G}^{S}(r)
.

The
twisted conjugacy growth function

f_{R}^{S}(r)
counts the number of different twisted conjugacy classes that intersect the closed ball
B_{G}^{S}(r)
. Formally we define it in the following way.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
G
be finitely generated by
S
and
\varphi\in\End(G)
.

•

The
twisted conjugacy growth function
f_{R}^{S}
of
\varphi
with respect to
S
is defined by

f_{R}^{S}:{\wiskunde N}\to{\wiskunde N}:r\mapsto\#\{[g]_{\varphi}\>|\>g\in B_{G}^{S}(r)\}.

•

The
twisted conjugacy growth
of
\varphi
with respect to
S
is the following limit (if it exists)

\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log(f_{R}^{S}(r))}{\log(\beta_{G}^{S}(r))}.

Remark 1.3
.

We extend the functions
f_{R}^{S}
and
B_{G}^{S}
to
{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
by taking the floor of any positive real number, so
f_{R}^{S}(r)=f_{R}^{S}(\lfloor r\rfloor)
and
B_{G}^{S}(r)=B_{G}^{S}(\lfloor r\rfloor)
for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
.

1.2
Comparing asymptotic growth

In this section, we describe two different ways to compare functions asymptotically. We provide some relations between them and argue that they are interchangeable when considering polynomial growth. Next, we apply these results to prove that
B_{G}^{S}
and
f_{R}^{S}
grow independent of the generating set. We start by giving the definitions.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
f,g:{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}\to{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
be two increasing functions. We say that
g

quasi-dominates

f
, denoted by
f\prec g
, if there exists
b,c\in{\wiskunde R}_{>0}
such that for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
it holds that

f(r)\leq cg(cr+b)+b.

The functions
f
and
g
are called
quasi-equivalent
, denoted by
f\sim g
, if both
f\prec g
and
g\prec f
.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
f,g:{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}\to{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
be two increasing functions. Then
f(r)=\Theta(g(r))
as
r\to\infty
, if there are
c_{1},c_{2}>0
and
n_{0}\in{\wiskunde N}_{>0}
such that for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq n_{0}}
it holds that

c_{1}g(r)\leq f(r)\leq c_{2}g(r).

It turns out that if two functions can be compared using
\Theta
, then they are quasi-equivalent. The converse also holds if the functions grow polynomially.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
f,g:{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}\to{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
be two increasing functions. If
f(r)=\Theta(g(r))
as
r\to\infty
, then
f\sim g
.

Moreover, if there is some
k\in{\wiskunde N}
such that
f\sim(r\mapsto r^{k})
, then
f(r)=\Theta(r^{k})
if
r\to\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f(r)=\Theta(g(r))
as
r\to\infty
. Thus there exist
c_{1},c_{2}\in{\wiskunde R}_{>0}
and
n_{0}\in{\wiskunde N}_{>0}
such that for all
r\geq n_{0}
we have that

c_{1}g(r)\leq f(r)\leq c_{2}g(r).

Define
c:=\max\{1/c_{1},c_{2},1\}
and take any
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
arbitrary. Thus we obtain that

\displaystyle f(r)

\displaystyle\leq f(r+n_{0})\leq c_{2}g(r+n_{0})\leq cg(cr+n_{0})+n_{0}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle g(r)

\displaystyle\leq g(r+n_{0})\leq 1/c_{1}f(r+n_{0})\leq cf(cr+n_{0})+n_{0}

and thus
f\sim g
.

Suppose
f\sim(r\mapsto r^{k})
(with
k\in{\wiskunde N}
). Thus there exist
b,c,b^{\prime},c^{\prime}\in{\wiskunde R}_{>0}
such that for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
it holds that

f(r)\leq c(cr+b)^{k}+b\quad\text{and}\quad r^{k}\leq c^{\prime}f(c^{\prime}r+b^{\prime})+b^{\prime}.

Thus we obtain that for all
r\geq b^{\prime}
it holds that

\frac{\left(\frac{r-b^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}}\right)^{k}-b^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}}\leq f(r)\leq c(cr+b)^{k}+b.

Note that
\frac{\left(\frac{r-b^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}}\right)^{k}-b^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}}=\Theta(r^{k})
and
c(cr+b)^{k}+b=\Theta(r^{k})
as
r\to\infty
. Thus there exist
n_{1},n_{2}\in{\wiskunde N}_{>0}
and
c_{1},c_{2}\in{\wiskunde R}_{>0}
such that for all
r\geq\max\{n_{1},n_{2}\}
it holds that

c_{1}r^{k}\leq\frac{\left(\frac{r-b^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}}\right)^{k}-b^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad c(cr+b)^{k}+b\leq c_{2}r^{k}.

Hence, define
n_{0}:=\lceil\max\{b^{\prime},n_{1},n_{2}\}\rceil\in{\wiskunde N}_{>0}
. Then for any
r\geq n_{0}
it holds that

c_{1}r^{k}\leq\frac{\left(\frac{r-b^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}}\right)^{k}-b^{\prime}}{c^{\prime}}\leq f(r)\leq c(cr+b)^{k}+b\leq c_{2}r^{k}.

So we argued that
f(r)=\Theta(r^{k})
as
r\to\infty
.
∎

Remark 1.7
.

One can prove that
(r\mapsto 2^{r})\sim(r\mapsto 3^{r})
, but that
2^{r}\neq\Theta(3^{r})
as
r\to\infty
. In particular, quasi-equivalence is more rigid than
\Theta
.

The following lemma will be used frequently to argue that the generating set has no influence on the growth of some particular functions.

Lemma 1.8
.

Let
G
be finitely generated by
S
and let
R\subset G
be a subset of
G
. If
L:R\to\mathcal{P}(G)
is some function assigning to each group element
x\in R
a subset
L(x)
of
G
, then

(r\mapsto\#\{x\in R\>|\>L(x)\cap B_{G}^{S}(r)\neq\emptyset\})\sim(r\mapsto\#\{x\in R\>|\>L(x)\cap B_{G}^{T}(r)\neq\emptyset\})

for any other finite generating set
T
of
G
.

In particular, suppose that there is some
k\in{\wiskunde N}
such that

\#\{x\in R\>|\>L(x)\cap B_{G}^{S}(r)\neq\emptyset\}=\Theta(r^{k})\text{ as }r\to\infty,

then this holds for all finite generating sets of
G
.

Proof.

Fix any finite generating set
T
of
G
. By for example Proposition 5.2.5 in
[
Loh17
]
there exists some
d\in{\wiskunde R}_{>0}
such that for all
x\in G
it holds that

\frac{1}{d}|x|_{S}\leq|x|_{T}\leq d|x|_{S}.

Denote
V^{S}(r):=\{x\in R\>|\>L(x)\cap B_{G}^{S}(r)\neq\emptyset\}
(and similar for
T
). Note that now for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
it holds that

V^{S}(r/d)\subset V^{T}(r)\subset V^{S}(dr).

Define
c:=\max\{d,1\}
and note that for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
we obtain that

\displaystyle\#V^{T}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\#V^{S}(dr)\leq c\#V^{S}(cr)\leq c\#V^{S}(cr+1)+1\text{ and}

\displaystyle\#V^{S}(r)

\displaystyle\leq\#V^{T}(dr)\leq c\#V^{T}(cr)\leq c\#V^{T}(cr+1)+1

and thus it follows that
(r\mapsto\#V^{T}(r))\sim(r\mapsto\#V^{S}(r))
.

Moreover, if
\#V^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{k})
as
r\to\infty
(for some
k\in{\wiskunde N}
), then by Lemma
1.6
and the above, we have that
\#V^{T}(r)=\Theta(r^{k})
as
r\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma
1.8
can be used to argue that the growth of
B_{G}^{S}
and
f_{R}^{S}
is independent of the generating set when considering quasi-equivalence. Moreover, if they grow polynomially, then together with Lemma
1.6
we obtain the same result when considering
\Theta
.

Corollary 1.9
.

Let
S
and
T
be two finite generating sets for
G
. Then

(r\mapsto\beta_{G}^{S}(r))\sim(r\mapsto\beta_{G}^{T}(r))\quad\text{and}\quad(r\mapsto f_{R}^{S}(r))\sim(r\mapsto f_{R}^{T}(r)).

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
G
be finitely generated by
S
. If there exists
k,l\in{\wiskunde N}
with
l>0
such that
f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{k})
and
\beta_{G}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{l})
as
r\to\infty
, then this is independent of the generating set. Moreover, the twisted conjugacy growth is independent of the generating set and equals
k/l
.

2
Twisted conjugacy growth of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}

In this section, we determine the twisted conjugacy growth of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
. The main argument is given in the following lemma. The twisted conjugacy growth of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
will then be a direct corollary.

Lemma 2.1
.

Define for any finite generating set
S
of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
and
B\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n\times n}
the set

V^{S}(r):=\left\{x+\Image(B)\>\middle|\>(x+\Image(B))\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)\neq\emptyset\right\}\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}/\Image(B).

Then
\#V^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-\rank(B)})
as
r\to\infty
for all finite generating sets
S
of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
.

Proof.

Note that by Lemma
1.8
the statement holds for any finite generating set if we are able to prove it for one specific finite generating set
S
of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
. We will use this approach in the proof and construct a specific set
S
.

Denote with
\Lambda=\diag(d_{1},\dots,d_{l},0,\dots,0)
the Smith normal form of
B
where
d_{1}|d_{2}|\dots|d_{l}
with
d_{i}\in{\wiskunde N}_{>0}
and
P,Q\in\Gl_{n}({\wiskunde Z})
invertible matrices such that
P^{-1}BQ=\Lambda
. Note that
l=\rank(B)
. Denote with
S=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
the columns of
P
and with
T=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{n}\}
the columns of
Q
. Hence, it holds for all
i=1,\dots,n
that

Bw_{i}=\begin{cases}d_{i}v_{i}&\text{if }i\leq l\\
0&\text{if }i>l\end{cases}.

Since
S
and
T
are both
{\wiskunde Z}
-bases of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
, it follows that
\Image(B)=\{\sum_{i=1}^{l}z_{i}d_{i}v_{i}\>|\>z_{i}\in{\wiskunde Z}\}
. Note that the size of
V^{S}(r)
does not change if we assume that
S=\varepsilon
(the standard generating set of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
) and that
B=\Lambda
. Hence, we assume this and thus

B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)=\left\{(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\>\middle|\>\sum_{i=1}^{n}|z_{i}|\leq r\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\Image(B)=\{(z_{1}d_{1},\dots,z_{l}d_{l},0,\dots,0)\>|\>z_{i}\in{\wiskunde Z}\}.

Define the sets
V\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
and
\tilde{V}(r)\subset B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)
(for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
) by

V:=\left\{(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\>\middle|\>\forall i\leq l:\>\left\lfloor-\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq z_{i}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{V}(r):=V\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r).

We claim that
\tilde{V}(r)
contains precisely one representative of each coset of
\Image(B)
in
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
that intersect
B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)
. In particular, it consists of so-called
minimal representative elements
of each such coset. For this, define the map
\tilde{\theta}
(for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
) by setting

\tilde{\theta}:\tilde{V}(r)\to V^{S}(r):x\mapsto x+\Image(B).

We argue that
\tilde{\theta}
is a bijection and thus
\#V^{S}(r)=\#\tilde{V}(r)
for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
. For the injectivity, take
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in\tilde{V}(r)
with
x+\Image(B)=y+\Image(B)
. Hence, there exist
z_{i}\in{\wiskunde Z}
such that
(x_{1}-y_{1},\dots,x_{n}-y_{n})=(z_{1}d_{1},\dots,z_{l}d_{l},0,\dots,0)
. Note that for all
i\leq l
it holds that
|x_{i}-y_{i}|\leq d_{i}-1
and thus (since
d_{i}|(x_{i}-y_{i})
) it follows that
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})
. So
\tilde{\theta}
is injective.

For the surjectivity, fix an arbitrary
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})+\Image(B)\in V^{S}(r)
with representative
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)
. Write for all
i=1,\dots,l

x_{i}=q_{i}d_{i}+a_{i}

where
q_{i},a_{i}\in{\wiskunde Z}
and
\left\lfloor-\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq a_{i}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor
. Define
y:=(a_{1},\dots,a_{l},x_{l+1},\dots,x_{n})\in V
and note that
x-y=(q_{1}d_{1},\dots,q_{l}d_{l},0,\dots,0)\in\Image(B)
. So it suffices to argue that
y\in B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)
. We claim that
|a_{i}|\leq|x_{i}|
for all
i\leq l
. Indeed, if
q_{i}=0
then
x_{i}=a_{i}
and thus clearly
|a_{i}|\leq|x_{i}|
. Assume that
q_{i}\neq 0
. Hence, we have that

|x_{i}-a_{i}|=|q_{i}|d_{i}\geq d_{i}.

By the choice of
a_{i}
, we have that
|a_{i}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor
. Also
\left\lfloor\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor\leq|x_{i}|
, since otherwise we would have

|x_{i}-a_{i}|\leq|x_{i}|+|a_{i}|<\left\lfloor\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor\leq d_{i}

which contradicts that
|x_{i}-a_{i}|\geq d_{i}
. Hence, it follows that
|a_{i}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor\leq|x_{i}|
for all
i\leq l
. Since
x\in B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)
we now find

|y|_{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}|a_{i}|+\sum_{i=l+1}^{n}|x_{i}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|\leq r

and thus
y\in V\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)=\tilde{V}(r)
. Concluding, we obtain that
\tilde{\theta}(y)=y+\Image(B)=x+\Image(B)
and thus
\tilde{\theta}
is surjective.

Since
\tilde{\theta}
is bijective, we find that
\#V^{S}(r)=\#\tilde{V}(r)
. Denote with
V^{\prime}\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{l}
the set

V^{\prime}:=\left\{(z_{1},\dots,z_{l})\in{\wiskunde Z}^{l}\>\middle|\>\forall i\leq l:\>\left\lfloor-\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor+1\leq z_{i}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d_{i}}{2}\right\rfloor\right\}

and note that for all
r\geq\sum_{i\leq l}d_{i}
it holds that

\displaystyle\#\tilde{V}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(z_{1},\dots,z_{l})\in V^{\prime}}\#\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\>\middle|\>x_{i}=z_{i},\>\forall i\leq l\text{ and }|x|_{S}\leq r\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(z_{1},\dots,z_{l})\in V^{\prime}}\#\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\>\middle|\>x_{i}=z_{i},\>\forall i\leq l\text{ and }\sum_{i>l}|x_{i}|\leq r-\sum_{i\leq l}|z_{i}|\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(z_{1},\dots,z_{l})\in V^{\prime}}\beta_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n-l}}^{\varepsilon}\left(r-\sum_{i\leq l}|z_{i}|\right)

Since
B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n-l}}^{\varepsilon}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-l})
as
r\to\infty
(see for example
[
Loh17
, Example 6.1.2]
) and
\#V^{\prime}<\infty
, it follows that

\#V^{S}(r)=\#\tilde{V}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-\rank(B)})\text{ as }r\to\infty.

By Lemma
1.8
this holds for all finite generating sets
S
of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
.
∎

Lemma
2.1
allows us to determine the twisted conjugacy growth of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
S
be any finite generating set of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
and
\varphi\in\End({\wiskunde Z}^{n})
, then

f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-\rank(\mathds{1}_{n}-\varphi)})\text{ as }r\to\infty.

In particular, the twisted conjugacy growth of
\varphi
equals

1-\frac{\rank(\mathds{1}_{n}-\varphi)}{n}.

Proof.

Fix any finite generating set
S
of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
and any
\varphi\in\End({\wiskunde Z}^{n})
. Note that for all
x\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
it holds that

[x]_{\varphi}=x+\Image(\mathds{1}_{n}-\varphi)

and thus for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
it holds that

\{[x]_{\varphi}\>|\>x\in B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)\}=\left\{x+\Image(\mathds{1}_{n}-\varphi)\>\middle|\>(x+\Image(\mathds{1}_{n}-\varphi))\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)\neq\emptyset\right\}.

Hence, by Lemma
2.1
it follows that
f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-\rank(\mathds{1}_{n}-\varphi)})
as
r\to\infty
. Since
B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{n})
as
r\to\infty
(which is contained in example
[
Loh17
, Example 6.1.2]
), the result follows by Corollary
1.10
.
∎

3
Twisted conjugacy growth of virtually abelian groups

In this section, we determine the twisted conjugacy growth of all finitely generated virtually abelian groups. For this, we need a generalisation of Lemma
2.1
. In the proof, we describe an upper and lower bound of the considered function and use Lemma
2.1
to obtain the asymptotics of these bounds.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
B\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n\times n}
and
M_{1}=\mathds{1}_{n},M_{2},\dots,M_{k}\in\Gl_{n}({\wiskunde Z})
(with
k,n\in{\wiskunde N}_{>0}
) such that
B
commutes with all matrices
M_{i}
. Let
c_{1}=(0,\dots,0),c_{2},\dots,c_{k}\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
. Define for any finite generating set
S
of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
the set

V^{S}(r):=\left\{x+\Image(B)\>\middle|\>\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}(M_{i}x+c_{i}+\Image(B))\right)\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)\neq\emptyset\right\}.

Then
\#V^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-\rank(B)})
as
r\to\infty
for any
S
.

Proof.

We let
S
be the standard generating set of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
and thus
|(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})|_{S}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|
. Note that for
i=1,\dots,k
the condition
\left(M_{i}x+c_{i}+\Image(B)\right)\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)\neq\emptyset
is independent of the representative
x
of the coset
x+\Image(B)
since
B
and
M_{i}
commute. Hence, we can fix a set
R\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
of representatives of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}/\Image(B)
and redefine

V^{S}(r):=\left\{x\in R\>\middle|\>\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}(M_{i}x+c_{i}+\Image(B))\right)\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)\neq\emptyset\right\}\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}.

Define for any
i=1,\dots,k
the set

V_{i}^{S}(r):=\left\{x\in R\>\middle|\>\left(M_{i}x+c_{i}+\Image(B)\right)\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)\neq\emptyset\right\}.

Note that
V_{1}^{S}(r)\subset V^{S}(r)
for any
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
and thus

\#V_{1}^{S}(r)\leq\#V^{S}(r).

Define for any matrix
A\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n\times n}
the norm
|A|:=\sum_{i,j}|A_{ij}|
. Note that since
S
is the standard generating set of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
, for any
x\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
it holds that
|Ax|_{S}\leq|A|\>|x|_{S}
. Fix any
i=1,\dots,k
. We argue that for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
it holds that

V_{i}^{S}(r)\subset V_{1}^{S}(|M_{i}^{-1}|(r+|c_{i}|_{S})).

For this, take some
x\in V_{i}^{S}(r)
arbitrary. So there is some
y\in\left(M_{i}x+c_{i}+\Image(B)\right)\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(r)
. Note that since
B
and
M_{i}
commute, it follows that
M_{i}^{-1}(y-c_{i})\in x+\Image(B)
. Moreover, it holds that

|M_{i}^{-1}(y-c_{i})|_{S}\leq|M_{i}^{-1}|\>|y-c_{i}|_{S}\leq|M_{i}^{-1}|(r+|c_{i}|_{S}).

Hence, we obtain that
M_{i}^{-1}(y-c_{i})\in(x+\Image(B))\cap B_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{S}(|M_{i}^{-1}|(r+|c_{i}|_{S}))
and thus indeed
x\in V_{1}^{S}(|M_{i}^{-1}|(r+|c_{i}|_{S}))
.

Note that now

V^{S}(r)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}^{S}(r)\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V_{1}^{S}(|M_{i}^{-1}|(r+|c_{i}|_{S}))

and thus in particular we have that

\#V_{1}^{S}(r)\leq\#V^{S}(r)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\#V_{1}^{S}(|M_{i}^{-1}|(r+|c_{i}|_{S})).

By Lemma
2.1
we know that
\#V_{1}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-\rank(B)})
as
r\to\infty
and thus the above implies that
\#V^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-\rank(B)})
as
r\to\infty
. By applying Lemma
1.8
we now obtain that
\#V^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{n-\rank(B)})
as
r\to\infty
for all finite generating sets
S
of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
.
∎

If
G
is a finitely generated virtually abelian group, then
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\subset_{\text{fin}}G
. The next lemma will allow us to use the subset
V^{S}(r)
of cosets of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
from Lemma
3.1
to describe the twisted conjugacy growth of
G
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated group and
H\subset_{\text{fin}}G
a finite index subgroup. Then for any
g\in G
the map
i^{g}:H\to G:h\mapsto hg
is a quasi-isometry (with respect to any word metrics on H and G coming from finite generating sets).

In particular, for all finite generating sets
S
of
G
and
T
of
H
and all
g\in G
there exist
r_{0}\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
,
c_{1},c_{2}\in{\wiskunde R}_{>0}
such that for all
r\geq r_{0}
it holds that

i^{g}(B_{H}^{T}(c_{1}r))\subset B_{G}^{S}(r)\cap i^{g}(H)\subset i^{g}(B_{H}^{T}(c_{2}r)).

Proof.

The first statement is inspired by Corollary 5.4.5 in
[
Loh17
]
and follows directly from its proof which is based on the Švarc-Milnor lemma (see for example
[
Loh17
, Proposition 5.4.1]
).

The second statement only uses that
i^{g}
is a quasi-isometric embedding, i.e. there exists some
c\in{\wiskunde R}_{>0}
such that for all
h,h^{\prime}\in H
we have that

\frac{1}{c}d_{T}(h,h^{\prime})-c\leq d_{S}(i^{g}(h),i^{g}(h^{\prime}))\leq cd_{T}(h,h^{\prime})+c.

Define

c_{1}:=\frac{1}{2c},\>c_{2}:=c+1\text{ and }r_{0}=\max\{2(c+|g|_{S}),c(c+|g|_{S})\}.

Fix any
r\geq r_{0}
and any
h\in B_{H}^{T}(c_{1}r)
. Thus we find that

|i^{g}(h)|_{S}\leq d_{S}(i^{g}(h),i^{g}(1_{G}))+d_{S}(g,1_{G})\leq c|h|_{T}+c+|g|_{S}\leq cc_{1}r+c+|g|_{S}\leq\frac{r}{2}+\frac{r}{2}=r

and thus indeed
i^{g}(h)\in B_{G}^{S}(r)\cap i^{g}(H)
.

Take any
i^{g}(h)\in B_{G}^{S}(r)\cap i^{g}(H)
and note that

|h|_{T}\leq cd_{S}(i^{g}(h),i^{g}(1_{G}))+c^{2}\leq c|i^{g}(h)|_{S}+c|g|_{S}+c^{2}\leq cr+r=c_{2}r

and thus indeed
i^{g}(h)\in B_{H}^{T}(c_{2}r)
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group. So
G
contains a finitely generated normal abelian subgroup
N_{1}
which is of finite index in
G
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

N_{1}
is torsion-free (if
l
is the order of the torsion subgroup of
N_{1}
, we may replace

N_{1}
with its finite index characteristic subgroup which is generated by all
n^{l}
with
n\in N_{1}
).
Now let
k=[G:N_{1}]
and let
N
be the subgroup of
G
which is generated by all elements of the form

g^{k}
. Then
N
is a fully characteristic subgroup of
G
, which is contained in
N_{1}
and so is also free abelian. Note that
N
is of finite index in
N_{1}
(because it contains
\{n_{1}^{k}\mid n_{1}\in N_{1}\}
which is clearly a finite index subgroup of
N_{1}
) hence
N
is also of finite index in
G
.

Thus any finitely generated group
G
contains some
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
which is a torsion-free abelian fully characteristic subgroup of
G
. In the rest of the paper, we do not require
N
to be fully characteristic, but we only need that
N
is invariant under the fixed morphism
\varphi\in\End(G)
.

Notation 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group and
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
a torsion-free abelian subgroup of
G
. We fix a finite set
A\subset G
, containing
1_{G}
, of representatives of
G/N
. Denote for any
a\in A
with
\tau_{a}\in\Inn(G)
conjugation with
a
in
G
and with
p:G\to G/N
the projection of
G
onto
G/N
. If
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
, then the intersection of any twisted conjugacy class with a coset
aN\in G/N
(with
a\in A
) is a finite union of cosets of
\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})
. Before formulating this result in more detail, let us define for any
a\in A
the finite set
E^{a}\subset A
and the map
\varphi^{a}:A\to G
by setting

\begin{split}E^{a}:&=p^{-1}(\Stab_{\overline{\varphi}}(p(a)))\cap A\\
&=\{b\in A\>|\>p(a)=p(ba\varphi(b)^{-1})\}\end{split}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{split}\varphi^{a}:A\to G:b\mapsto\varphi^{a}(b)&:=([a,b^{-1}]^{\varphi})^{-1}\\
&:=\varphi(b)a^{-1}b^{-1}a\end{split}

(3.1)

where
\overline{\varphi}\in\Aut(G/N)
is the induced automorphism on
G/N
,
\Stab_{\overline{\varphi}}(p(a))
is the
\varphi
-stabiliser
of
p(a)
(i.e. the stabiliser of
p(a)
under the
\overline{\varphi}
-conjugacy action on
G/N
) and
[x,y]^{\varphi}:=x^{-1}y^{-1}x\varphi(y)
is the
\varphi
-twisted commutator
of any
x,y\in G
. Remark that
\varphi^{a}(E^{a})\subset N
and
1_{G}\in E^{a}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

With the set-up from Notation
3.4
, it holds for any
a\in A
and for any
x,y\in N
that

i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{a}(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})).

Proof.

Fix some
a\in A
and recall the definition of
i^{a}
in Lemma
3.2
and of
E^{a}
and
\varphi^{a}
in Equation (
3.1
). Note that
\varphi^{a}(E^{a})\subset N
by definition. It holds for all
x,y\in N
that

\displaystyle i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{a}(y)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in N,\>c\in A:\>i^{a}(x)=i^{c}(z)i^{a}(y)\varphi(i^{c}(z))^{-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in N,\>c\in A:\>i^{a}(x)=i^{c}(z)i^{a}(y)\varphi(c)^{-1}\varphi(z)^{-1}

Since
\varphi(N)\subset N
, we find by applying the morphism
p:G\to G/N
to the last equality that
p(a)=p(ca\varphi(c)^{-1})
or thus
c\in E^{a}
. Hence, we obtain that

\displaystyle i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{a}(y)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in N,\>c\in E^{a}:\>i^{a}(x)=i^{c}(z)i^{a}(y)(\varphi^{a}(c)a^{-1}ca)^{-1}\varphi(z)^{-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in N,\>c\in E^{a}:\>x=z\tau_{c}(y)(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)^{-1}(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi)(z)^{-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in N,\>c\in E^{a}:\>x=\tau_{c}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})(z)

where in the last step we used that
\varphi^{a}(E^{a})\subset N
, that
N
is abelian and we switched to additive notation for convenience. Hence, we obtain for all
x,y\in N
that

i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{a}(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})).

∎

Remark 3.6
.

By Lemma
3.5
it especially follows for all
a\in A
and
x\in N
that

x\in\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(x)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})).

Moreover, since
1_{G}\in E^{a}
and
(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(1_{G})=1_{G}\in N
, we also obtain that
x+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})
is always contained in the above finite union.

Lemmas
3.1
,
3.2
and
3.5
give us the necessary tools to determine the twisted conjugacy growth of any finitely generated virtually abelian group.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
S
be any finite generating set of a finitely generated virtually abelian group
G
and
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
a torsion-free abelian subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
, then

f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta\left(r^{\rank(N)-\min\limits_{gN\in G/N}\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{N})}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\#B_{G}^{S}(r)=\Theta\left(r^{\rank(N)}\right)\text{ as }r\to\infty

where
\tau_{g}:G\to G:h\mapsto ghg^{-1}
denotes conjugation by
g\in G
.

In particular, the twisted conjugacy growth of
\varphi
equals

1-\frac{\min\limits_{gN\in G/N}\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{N})}{\rank(N)}.

Proof.

Fix a finite set
A\subset G
of different representatives of the cosets of
N
in
G
. We can assume without loss of generality that
1_{G}\in A
. Recall the definition of
i^{a}
in Lemma
3.2
and note that
i^{a}(N)=p^{-1}(aN)
, where
p:G\to G/N
is the projection onto
G/N
. Define for any
a\in A
(and any
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
) the sets

F(r):=\{[g]_{\varphi}\>|\>g\in B_{G}^{S}(r)\}\quad\text{and}\quad F_{G}^{a}(r):=\{[i^{a}(x)]_{\varphi}\>|\>x\in N,\>i^{a}(x)\in B_{G}^{S}(r)\}.

Thus the set
F_{G}^{a}(r)
precisely contains the twisted conjugacy classes of
\varphi
that intersect the ball
B_{G}^{S}(r)
in an element
g\in G
with
p(g)=aN
. It easily follows that

F(r)=\bigcup_{a\in A}F_{G}^{a}(r)

and thus

\max_{a\in A}\#F_{G}^{a}(r)\leq\#F(r)=f_{R}^{S}(r)\leq\sum_{a\in A}\#F_{G}^{a}(r).

(3.2)

We now argue that
\#F_{G}^{a}(r)=\Theta(r^{\rank(N)-\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})})
as
r\to\infty
for all
a\in A
. Recall from Lemma
3.5
that for all
x,y\in N
it holds that

i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{a}(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}))

where
E^{a}
and
\varphi^{a}
are defined in Equation (
3.1
). Since for all
c\in E^{a}
it holds that
\tau_{c}\in\Aut(N)
, the automorphism corresponds with multiplication on the left with a unique matrix
M_{c}\in\Gl_{\rank(N)}({\wiskunde Z})
.
We check the conditions of Lemma
3.1
with
B:=\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}\in\End(N)
. Note that
1_{G}\in E^{a}
, that
M_{1_{G}}=\mathds{1}_{\rank(N)}
and that
(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(1_{G})=1_{G}\in N
. Moreover, note that for any
c\in E^{a}
the map
\tau_{c}|_{N}
commutes with
\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}
. Indeed, fix any
x\in N
. Since
\varphi(c)=\varphi^{a}(c)a^{-1}ca
with
\varphi^{a}(c)\in N
it holds that

\displaystyle(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}\circ\tau_{c})(x)

\displaystyle=a\varphi(cxc^{-1})a^{-1}=a\varphi^{a}(c)a^{-1}ca\varphi(x)a^{-1}c^{-1}a\varphi^{a}(c)^{-1}a^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)(\tau_{c}\circ\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})(x)(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)^{-1}=(\tau_{c}\circ\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})(x)

where in the last step we used that the three elements belong to
N
, which is abelian. Hence, for any
c\in E^{a}
the map
\tau_{c}|_{N}
commutes with
\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}
or equivalently the corresponding matrices commute. Fix a set
R\subset N
of representatives of different cosets of
\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}
in
N
and fix any finite generating set
T
of
N\cong{\wiskunde Z}^{\rank(N)}
. Lemma
3.1
shows that if we define

\displaystyle V^{a}(r):

\displaystyle=\left\{x\in R\>\middle|\>\left(\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(x)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}))\right)\cap B_{N}^{T}(r)\neq\emptyset\right\}\text{ then }

\displaystyle\#V^{a}(r)

\displaystyle=\Theta(r^{\rank(N)-\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})})\text{ as }r\to\infty.

Define the set

F_{N}^{a}(r):=\{[i^{a}(x)]_{\varphi}\>|\>x\in B_{N}^{T}(r)\}.

By Lemma
3.2
we know that there exists some
r_{0}^{a}\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
and
c_{1}^{a},c_{2}^{a}\in{\wiskunde R}_{>0}
such that for all
r\geq r_{0}^{a}
it holds that

i^{a}(B_{N}^{T}(c_{1}^{a}r))\subset B_{G}^{S}(r)\cap i^{a}(N)\subset i^{a}(B_{N}^{T}(c_{2}^{a}r)).

In particular, we have for all
r\geq r_{0}^{a}
that

F_{N}^{a}(c_{1}^{a}r)\subset F_{G}^{a}(r)\subset F_{N}^{a}(c_{2}^{a}r).

Now we argue that
\#V^{a}(r)/\#E^{a}\leq\#F_{N}^{a}(r)\leq\#V^{a}(r)
(for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
) and thus for all
r\geq r_{0}^{a}
we have that

\frac{\#V^{a}(c_{1}^{a}r)}{\#E^{a}}\leq\#F_{N}^{a}(c_{1}^{a}r)\leq\#F_{G}^{a}(r)\leq\#F_{N}^{a}(c_{2}^{a}r)\leq\#V^{a}(c_{2}^{a}r).

(3.3)

For this, fix any
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
and representatives
\tilde{R}\subset i^{a}(B_{N}^{T}(r))
of different twisted conjugacy classes from
F_{N}^{a}(r)
. Define maps

\pi_{1}:E^{a}\times\tilde{R}\to V^{a}(r)\text{ with }

\pi_{1}(c,i^{a}(x)):=\text{representative in }R\text{ of }\tau_{c}(x)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})

and

\pi_{2}:V^{a}(r)\to\tilde{R}\text{ with }\pi_{2}(x):=\text{representative in }\tilde{R}\text{ of }[i^{a}(x)]_{\varphi}.

Note that
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are well-defined. Indeed, take
(c,i^{a}(x))\in E^{a}\times\tilde{R}
and denote with
y\in R
the unique representative of
\tau_{c}(x)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})
. Hence, by Lemma
3.5
we obtain that
i^{a}(y)\sim_{\varphi}i^{a}(x)
and thus
x\in\left(\bigcup_{d\in E^{a}}(\tau_{d}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(d)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}))\right)\cap B_{N}^{T}(r)
. In particular, we have that
y\in V^{a}(r)
. For
\pi_{2}
, take
x\in V^{a}(r)
and take some
y\in\left(\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(x)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}))\right)\cap B_{N}^{T}(r)
. By Lemma
3.5
we get that
i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{a}(y)
with
y\in B_{N}^{T}(r)
and thus
[i^{a}(x)]_{\varphi}\in F_{N}^{a}(r)
. So we can indeed consider the unique representative of
[i^{a}(x)]_{\varphi}
in
\tilde{R}
.

We argue that
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are surjective. For this, take an arbitrary
x\in V^{a}(r)
and fix some
y\in\left(\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(x)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}))\right)\cap B_{N}^{T}(r)
. By Lemma
3.5
we get that
i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{a}(y)
with
y\in B_{N}^{T}(r)
and thus
[i^{a}(x)]_{\varphi}\in F_{N}^{a}(r)
. Take
i^{a}(z)\in\tilde{R}
the unique representative of
[i^{a}(x)]_{\varphi}
in
\tilde{R}
. Thus by Lemma
3.5
there exists some
c\in E^{a}
such that
x\in\tau_{c}(z)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})
. Since
x\in V^{a}(r)\subset R
, it holds that
x\in R
is the unique representative of
\tau_{c}(z)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})
in
R
. Hence, we find that
\pi_{1}(c,i^{a}(z))=x
and thus
\pi_{1}
is surjective. For
\pi_{2}
, take any
i^{a}(x)\in\tilde{R}
arbitrary and denote with
y\in R
the unique representative of
x+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})
. By Remark
3.6
we obtain that
x\in\left(\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}))\right)\cap B_{N}^{T}(r)
and thus
y\in V^{a}(r)
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.5
we get that
i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{a}(y)
and thus
i^{a}(x)\in\tilde{R}
is the unique representative of
[i^{a}(y)]_{\varphi}
in
\tilde{R}
. Thus, it follows that
\pi_{2}(y)=i^{a}(x)
. Hence, also
\pi_{2}
is surjective.

Since
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are surjective (for any
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
), we obtain that
\#V^{a}(r)/\#E^{a}\leq\#F_{N}^{a}(r)\leq\#V^{a}(r)
for all
r\in{\wiskunde R}_{\geq 0}
. In particular, we can conclude the inequalities in Equation (
3.3
) for all
r\geq r_{0}^{a}
. Using that
\#V^{a}(r)=\Theta(r^{\rank(N)-\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})})
as
r\to\infty
, it follows that

\#F_{G}^{a}(r)=\Theta\left(r^{\rank(N)-\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})}\right)\text{ as }r\to\infty.

Since
a\in A
was taken arbitrary, this holds for all
a\in A
. Using Equation (
3.2
) and the definition of
\Theta
(see Definition
1.5
) it now easily follows that

f_{R}^{S}(r)=\#F(r)=\Theta\left(r^{\rank(N)-\min\limits_{a\in A}\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})}\right)\text{ as }r\to\infty.

Since
N\subset_{\text{fin}}G
and
\beta_{N}^{T}(r)=\Theta(r^{\rank(N)})
as
r\to\infty
(see for example
[
Loh17
, Example 6.1.2]
), it follows (by for example
[
Loh17
, Corollary 5.4.5, Proposition 6.2.4]
) that
\beta_{G}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{\rank(N)})
as
r\to\infty
. In particular, by Corollary
1.10
it follows that

\beta_{G}^{S}(r)=\Theta\left(r^{\rank(N)}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta\left(r^{\rank(N)-\min\limits_{a\in A}\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})}\right)\text{ as }r\to\infty

for all finite generating sets
S
of
G
and the twisted conjugacy growth of
G
equals

1-\frac{\min\limits_{a\in A}\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})}{\rank(N)}

for all finite generating sets
S
of
G
.
∎

4
Twisted conjugacy class growth

Instead of studying the number of twisted conjugacy classes intersecting the balls
B_{G}^{S}(r)
, one could also be interested in the growth of one particular twisted conjugacy class
[g]_{\varphi}
. More precisely, for any subset
U\subset G
we can define the
subset growth
of
U
in
G
(with respect to
S
) as

\beta_{U\subset G}^{S}(r):=\#B_{U\subset G}^{S}(r):=\#\{u\in U\>|\>|u|_{S}\leq r\}.

For any
\varphi\in\End(G)
and any
g\in G
, the
twisted conjugacy class growth
of the twisted conjugacy class
[g]_{\varphi}
equals
\beta_{[g]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}
.

Dermenjian and Evetts proved (see
[
DE23
]
) that any conjugacy class of a finitely generated virtually abelian group grows polynomially. Using Lemma
3.5
, we can prove the analogous result for all twisted conjugacy classes and determine the degree of this polynomial.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S
be any finite generating set of a finitely generated virtually abelian group
G
and
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
a torsion-free abelian subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
, then any twisted conjugacy class grows polynomially. More precisely, for any
g_{0}\in G
it holds that

\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{k})\text{ as }r\to\infty\quad\text{where}

k=\max\{\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{N})\>|\>gN\in G/N\text{ and }[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\cap p^{-1}(gN)\neq\emptyset\}.

Proof.

As before, fix a finite set
A\subset G
, including
1_{G}
, of representatives of
G/N
. Fix some
g_{0}\in G
and define
A_{0}\subset A
by

A_{0}:=\{a\in A\>|\>[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\cap p^{-1}(aN)\neq\emptyset\}

which contains the representatives of all cosets that intersect the twisted conjugacy class of
g_{0}
. For any
a\in A_{0}
we choose some
x_{a}\in N
such that
x_{a}a\sim_{\varphi}g_{0}
. Hence, it holds that

\displaystyle[g_{0}]_{\varphi}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{a\in A_{0}}[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\cap p^{-1}(aN)

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{a\in A_{0}}i^{a}\left(\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(x_{a})-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}))\right)

where in the last step we used Lemma
3.5
. Denote for simplicity (for any
a\in A_{0}
)

V^{a}:=\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(x_{a})-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})).

Since
A_{0}
is finite, it follows by Lemma 2.5 in
[
DE23
]
that
\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}\sim\max\limits_{a\in A_{0}}\beta_{i^{a}(V^{a})\subset G}^{S}
. Recall that
i^{a}:N\to G:x\mapsto xa
is a quasi-isometry (see Lemma
3.2
). Hence, Proposition 2.6 in
[
DE23
]
yields for any
a\in A
that
\beta_{i^{a}(V^{a})\subset G}^{S}\sim\beta_{V^{a}\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{T}
(where
T
is any finite generating set of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
). Using Lemma 2.4 in
[
DE23
]
we obtain for any
a\in A_{0}
and
c\in E^{a}
that

\displaystyle\beta_{\tau_{c}(x_{a})-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{T}

\displaystyle\sim\beta_{\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}^{T}

\displaystyle\sim(r\mapsto r^{\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})})

where we used that
\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})\cong{\wiskunde Z}^{\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})}
grows polynomially of degree its rank. Applying Lemma 2.5 from
[
DE23
]
again and combining all the rest, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}(r)

\displaystyle\sim\max\limits_{a\in A_{0}}(r\mapsto r^{\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})})

\displaystyle\sim(r\mapsto r^{\max\limits_{a\in A_{0}}\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N})}).

Lemma
1.6
now gives us the desired result.
∎

5
Twisted conjugacy quotient growth

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group with
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
. Note that in this section we use
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
instead of
N
. Fix an endomorphism
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi({\wiskunde Z}^{n})\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
and denote for any
k\in{\wiskunde N}_{0}
with
\overline{\varphi}_{k}
the induced morphism on
G/(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
. Since
G/(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
is finite, it follows that
R(\overline{\varphi}_{k})<\infty
. We define the
twisted conjugacy quotient growth function
f_{Q}
as

f_{Q}:{\wiskunde N}_{0}\to{\wiskunde N}_{0}:k\mapsto R(\overline{\varphi}_{k}).

We prove a slight modification of Lemma
3.5
where we use the set-up from Notation
3.4
.

Lemma 5.1
.

It holds for any
a\in A
and for any
x,y\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
that

i^{a}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\sim_{\overline{\varphi}_{k}}i^{a}(y)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+(\Image(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}})+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n})).

Proof.

Fix any
a\in A
and
x,y\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
. Hence, it follows that

\displaystyle i^{a}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\sim_{\overline{\varphi}_{k}}i^{a}(y)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n},\>c\in A:\>i^{a}(x)\in i^{c}(z)i^{a}(y)\varphi(i^{c}(z))^{-1}(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n},\>c\in A:\>i^{a}(x)\in i^{c}(z)i^{a}(y)\varphi(c)^{-1}\varphi(z)^{-1}(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}.

Since
\varphi({\wiskunde Z}^{n})\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
, by projecting onto
G/{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
we obtain that
a{\wiskunde Z}^{n}=ca\varphi(c)^{-1}{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
and thus
c\in E^{a}
. Using the definition of
\varphi^{a}
(see Equation (
3.1
)) we get that

\displaystyle i^{a}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\sim_{\overline{\varphi}_{k}}i^{a}(y)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n},\>c\in E^{a}:\>i^{a}(x)\in i^{c}(z)i^{a}(y)(\varphi^{a}(c)a^{-1}ca)^{-1}\varphi(z)^{-1}(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n},\>c\in E^{a}:\>x\in z\tau_{c}(y)(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)^{-1}(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi)(z)^{-1}(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\exists z\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n},\>c\in E^{a}:\>x\in\tau_{c}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}})(z)+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}

where in the last step we used that
\varphi^{a}(E^{a})\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
, that
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
is abelian and we switched to additive notation for convenience. Thus it follows that for all
x,y\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}

i^{a}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\sim_{\overline{\varphi}_{k}}i^{a}(y)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad x\in\bigcup_{c\in E^{a}}(\tau_{c}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+(\Image(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}})+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n})).

∎

In order to determine the Reidemeister number of
\overline{\varphi}_{k}
, we need the number of cosets of
\Image(B)+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
in
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
for any morphism
B\in\End({\wiskunde Z}^{n})
. For any subgroup
H\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
let us denote with
\sqrt{H}:=\{z\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\>|\>\exists m\in{\wiskunde N}_{0}:\>mz\in H\}
the isolator subgroup of
H
. In particular, it holds that
\sqrt{H}/H
is finite. The next lemma is needed to determine
[{\wiskunde Z}^{n}:H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
0\neq H\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
and
k,l\in{\wiskunde N}_{0}
. Denote
r:=\rank(H)
and fix any
{\wiskunde Z}
-basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
of
H
. If
lv_{r}\in\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
(or
lv_{1}\in(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
if
r=1
), then
k\leq\left|\frac{\sqrt{H}}{H}\right|l
.

Proof.

For
r=1
the proof is completely analogous and thus we assume that
r\geq 2
.
Denote

lv_{r}=\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\lambda_{i}v_{i}+kz

where
z,\lambda_{i}\in{\wiskunde Z}
. In particular, we obtain that
kz\in H
and thus
z\in\sqrt{H}
. Denote with
d
the order of
z+H
in
\sqrt{H}/H
. Thus
dz\in H
and hence there are unique
\mu_{i}\in{\wiskunde Z}
such that

dz=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mu_{i}v_{i}.

Since
kz\in H
, it follows that
d\mid k
. Hence, we obtain that

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\frac{k}{d}\mu_{i}v_{i}=\frac{k}{d}dz=kz=lv_{r}-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\lambda_{i}v_{i}.

Using that
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
is a
{\wiskunde Z}
-basis of
H
we get that
\frac{k}{d}\mu_{r}=l
and thus
k\leq dl
. Since
d\leq\left|\frac{\sqrt{H}}{H}\right|
the result follows.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
H\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
, then

[{\wiskunde Z}^{n}:H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]=\Theta(k^{n-\rank(H)})\text{ as }k\to\infty

Proof.

Note that

[{\wiskunde Z}^{n}:H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]=\frac{[{\wiskunde Z}^{n}:(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]}{[H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}:(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]}=\frac{k^{n}}{[H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}:(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]}

and thus it suffices to argue that
[H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}:(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]=\Theta(k^{\rank(H)})
as
k\to\infty
. We argue this by using induction on the rank of
H
. If
\rank(H)=0
, then the result holds trivially. Let us assume that
\rank(H)\in{\wiskunde N}_{0}
and the result holds for any subgroup of rank less than
\rank(H)
. Denote
r:=\rank(H)
and fix any
{\wiskunde Z}
-basis
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}
of
H
. To simplify notation,
\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle:=0
if
r=1
. Since the rank of the subgroup
\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle
equals
r-1
, it follows by the induction hypothesis that

[\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}:(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]=\Theta(k^{r-1})\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Fix any
k>\left|\frac{\sqrt{H}}{H}\right|
. Thus by Lemma
5.2
it holds that
v_{r}\not\in\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
. Hence,
v_{r}+\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
is a generator of the finite cyclic group
(H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n})/(\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n})
and the size of this group thus equals the order of
v_{r}+\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
. Using Lemma
5.2
we obtain that

\frac{k}{\left|\frac{\sqrt{H}}{H}\right|}\leq[H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}:\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]\leq k.

Since this holds for all
k>\left|\frac{\sqrt{H}}{H}\right|
, it follows that

[H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}:\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]=\Theta(k)\text{ as }k\to\infty

and thus

\displaystyle[H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}:(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]

\displaystyle=[H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}:\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]\cdot[\langle v_{1},\dots,v_{r-1}\rangle+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}:(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]

\displaystyle=\Theta(k^{\rank(H)})\text{ as }k\to\infty

which concludes the proof by induction.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

If
G
is a finitely generated virtually abelian group with
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
and
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi({\wiskunde Z}^{n})\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
, then

f_{Q}(k)=\Theta\left(k^{n-\min\limits_{g{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\in G/{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}\rank(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}})}\right)\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Proof.

Recall the set-up from Notation
3.4
. For any
a\in A
we define the sets

\overline{F}(k):=\{[g(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]_{\overline{\varphi}_{k}}\>|\>g\in G\}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{F^{a}}(k):=\{[i^{a}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]_{\overline{\varphi}_{k}}\>|\>x\in{\wiskunde Z}^{n}\}.

Similarly as in the proof of Theorem
3.7
it follows that

\overline{F}(k)=\bigcup_{a\in A}\overline{F^{a}}(k)

and thus

\max_{a\in A}\#\overline{F^{a}}(k)\leq\#\overline{F}(k)=R(\overline{\varphi}_{k})=f_{Q}(k)\leq\sum_{a\in A}\#\overline{F^{a}}(k).

(5.1)

Thus again it suffices to argue for all
a\in A
that
\#\overline{F^{a}}(k)=\Theta\left(k^{n-\rank(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}})}\right)
as
k\to\infty
. So fix any
a\in A
and denote
H:=\Image(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}})
. We claim that

{}\frac{[{\wiskunde Z}^{n}:H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]}{\#E^{a}}\leq\#\overline{F^{a}}(k)\leq[{\wiskunde Z}^{n}:H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}].

(5.2)

To prove this claim, we fix a set
R\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
of representatives of
{\wiskunde Z}^{n}/(H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n})
and a set
\tilde{R}\subset i^{a}({\wiskunde Z}^{n})
such that
\{i^{a}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\>|\>i^{a}(x)\in\tilde{R}\}
contains representatives of the
\overline{\varphi}_{k}
-conjugacy classes in
\overline{F^{a}}(k)
. In particular, we have that
\#R=[{\wiskunde Z}^{n}:H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}]
and
\#\tilde{R}=\#\overline{F^{a}}(k)
. Define the maps
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
by setting

\pi_{1}:E^{a}\times\tilde{R}\to R\text{ with }

\pi_{1}(c,i^{a}(x)):=\text{representative in }R\text{ of }\tau_{c}(x)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+(H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n})

and

\pi_{2}:R\to\tilde{R}\text{ with }\pi_{2}(x)\in\tilde{R}\text{ such that }\pi_{2}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\sim_{\overline{\varphi}_{k}}i^{a}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}.

The maps
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are clearly well-defined. We argue that they are both surjective. First, we fix some
r\in R\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
. Take
i^{a}(x)\in\tilde{R}
such that
i^{a}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\sim_{\overline{\varphi}_{k}}i^{a}(r)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
. Hence, by Lemma
5.1
we can choose some
c\in E^{a}
such that
r\in\tau_{c}(x)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+(H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n})
. Hence, it follows that
\pi_{1}(c,i^{a}(x))=r
and thus
\pi_{1}
is surjective. Next, we take any
i^{a}(y)\in\tilde{R}
. Fix a representative
x\in R\subset{\wiskunde Z}^{n}
of
y+(H+(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n})
. Since
1_{G}\in E^{a}
and
(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(1_{G})=1_{G}
, it follows by Lemma
5.1
that
i^{a}(x)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}\sim_{\overline{\varphi}_{k}}i^{a}(y)(k{\wiskunde Z})^{n}
and thus
\pi_{2}(x)=i^{a}(y)
. Hence,
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
are both surjective and thus Equation (
5.2
) follows.

Combining Theorem
5.3
and Equation (
5.2
) yields that

\#\overline{F^{a}}(k)=\Theta\left(k^{n-\rank(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}})}\right)\text{ as }k\to\infty.

Since this holds for all
a\in A
, we can conclude by using Equation (
5.1
) that

f_{Q}(k)=\Theta\left(k^{n-\min\limits_{a\in A}\rank(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{n}})}\right)\text{ as }k\to\infty.

∎

Corollary 5.5
.

If
G
is a virtually abelian group that is finitely generated by
S
and
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
is a torsion-free abelian subgroup of
G
. If
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
, then the twisted conjugacy growth
f_{R}^{S}
and the quotient growth
f_{Q}
of
G
are quasi-equivalent, i.e.
f_{R}^{S}\sim f_{Q}
.

Moreover,

f_{R}^{S}(k)=\Theta(f_{Q}(k))=\Theta\left(k^{\rank(N)-\min\limits_{gN\in G/N}\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{N})}\right)\text{ as }k\to\infty.

6
Examples

In this section, we work out some concrete examples regarding the different growth functions. For this, we describe a specific family of virtually abelian groups for which Lemma
3.5
can be strengthened.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated virtually abelian group with
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
a torsion-free abelian subgroup and
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
. If under the set-up from Notation
3.4
, also
G/N
is abelian and the induced morphism
\overline{\varphi}\in\End(G/N)
is the identity on
G/N
, then for any
a,b\in A
and for any
x,y\in N
it holds that

i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{b}(y)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\begin{cases}a=b\\
x\in\bigcup\limits_{c\in A}(\tau_{c}(y)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}))\end{cases}.

Proof.

Note that
i^{a}(x)\sim_{\varphi}i^{b}(y)
if and only if there exists some
c\in A
and
z\in N
such that

i^{a}(x)=i^{c}(z)i^{b}(y)\varphi(i^{c}(z))^{-1}.

Hence, by projecting this equation onto
G/N
, we obtain that
aN=cb\varphi(c)^{-1}N
. Since
G/N
is abelian and
\varphi(c)N=cN
, we obtain that
aN=bN
and thus
a=b
by definition of
A
. Moreover, it holds that
E^{a}=\{c\in A\>|\>aN=ca\varphi(c)^{-1}N\}=A
. Using Lemma
3.5
, we can now conclude the result.
∎

From now on, we assume that
G/N
is abelian and that
\overline{\varphi}=\Id_{G/N}
. In particular, for an arbitrary element
i^{a}(x)\in G
(with
a\in A
and
x\in N
) the
\varphi
-twisted conjugacy class of
i^{a}(x)
will be contained in
i^{a}({\wiskunde Z}^{n})
. More precisely, it holds that

[i^{a}(x)]_{\varphi}=i^{a}\left(\bigcup\limits_{c\in A}(\tau_{c}(x)-(\tau_{a}\circ\varphi^{a})(c)+\Image(\Id_{N}-\tau_{a}\circ\varphi|_{N}))\right).

In other words, the projection of any twisted conjugacy class onto
G/N
is a singleton. Hence, by Theorem
4.1
we obtain the following result.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
S
be any finite generating set of a finitely generated virtually abelian group
G
and
N\lhd_{\text{fin}}G
a torsion-free abelian subgroup of
G
. Let
\varphi\in\End(G)
with
\varphi(N)\subset N
and
\overline{\varphi}=\Id_{G/N}
. If
G/N
is abelian, then for any
g_{0}\in G
it holds that

\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{\rank(\Id_{N}-\tau_{g_{0}}\circ\varphi|_{N})})\text{ as }r\to\infty.

Proof.

By Lemma
6.1
it follows that

\{gN\in G/N\>|\>[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\cap p^{-1}(gN)\neq\emptyset\}=\{g_{0}N\}.

Hence, we can conclude the result by applying Theorem
4.1
.
∎

In the next example, we illustrate our results for a concrete group and some morphisms.

Example 6.3
.

Consider the semi-direct product

G:={\wiskunde Z}^{2}{\wiskunde o}{\wiskunde Z}/2{\wiskunde Z}

where the non-trivial element
t
of
{\wiskunde Z}/2{\wiskunde Z}
acts as
-\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{2}}
on
{\wiskunde Z}^{2}
. Hence, the set
A:=\{1_{G},t\}
is a set of representatives of the cosets in
G/{\wiskunde Z}^{2}\cong{\wiskunde Z}/2{\wiskunde Z}
. Fix any finite generating set
S
of
G
and denote with
\varphi\in\End(G)
the morphism for which
\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{2}}=-\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{2}}
and
\varphi(t)=t
. Note that
\tau_{t}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{2}}=\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{2}}
and thus

\rank(\Id_{{\wiskunde Z}^{2}}-\tau_{g}\circ\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{2}})=\begin{cases}2&\text{if }g\in{\wiskunde Z}^{2}\\
0&\text{if }g\in t{\wiskunde Z}^{2}\end{cases}.

Thus by Theorem
3.7
, Theorem
5.4
and Theorem
6.2
it follows that as
r\to\infty

\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}(r)=\begin{cases}\Theta(r^{2})&\text{if }g_{0}\in{\wiskunde Z}^{2}\\
\Theta(1)&\text{if }g_{0}\in t{\wiskunde Z}^{2}\end{cases}\text{, }\quad f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta(r^{2})\quad\text{ and }\quad f_{Q}(r)=\Theta(r^{2}).

Note that the asymptotics of
\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}
only depends on the coset of
{\wiskunde Z}^{2}
that contains
g_{0}
and thus is the same for any two elements belonging to the same coset. Moreover, the degree of both
f_{R}^{S}
and
f_{Q}
equals two (being the rank of
{\wiskunde Z}^{2}
) minus the minimum of the degrees occurring in the description of
\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}
. These observations hold always if
G/N
is abelian and the induced morphism
\overline{\varphi}=\Id_{G/N}
.

Next, one can check that if
\varphi=\Id_{G}
, then still
f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta(f_{Q}(r))=\Theta(r^{2})
as
r\to\infty
, but

\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}(r)=\begin{cases}\Theta(1)&\text{if }g_{0}\in{\wiskunde Z}^{2}\\
\Theta(r^{2})&\text{if }g_{0}\in t{\wiskunde Z}^{2}\end{cases}\text{ as }r\to\infty.

In particular, the twisted conjugacy class of the identity element does not necessarily grow faster or slower than any other class. It is also possible that the twisted conjugacy class growth is (asymptotically) independent of the considered twisted conjugacy class. One can check that for example for the morphism
\varphi\in\End(G)
which is defined by

\varphi|_{{\wiskunde Z}^{2}}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(t)=t

it holds for any
g_{0}\in G
that

f_{R}^{S}(r)=\Theta(\beta_{[g_{0}]_{\varphi}\subset G}^{S}(r))=\Theta(f_{Q}(r))=\Theta(r)\text{ as }r\to\infty.

Dispersive estimates for two-particles Schrödinger and wave equations in the Aharonov-Bohm field
Xiaofen Gao, Junyong Zhang, Jiqiang Zheng

Abstract
. We study the dispersive behaviors of two-particles Schrödinger and wave equations in the Aharonov-Bohm field. In particular, we prove the Strichartz estimates for Schrödinger and wave equations in this setting. The key point is to construct spectral measure of Schrödinger operator with an Aharonov-Bohm type potential in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} . As applications, we finally prove a scattering theory for the nonlinear defocusing subcritical two-particles Schrödinger equation with Aharonov-Bohm potential.



Key Words: Aharonov-Bohm potential, Decay estimates, Strichartz estimate, Scattering theory.

AMS Classification:

42B37, 35Q40, 35Q41.

1.
Intorduction

In this paper, we continue our program
[
6
,
15
,
18
]
studying the dispersive equations in Aharonov-Bohm field.
The Aharonov-Bohm magnetic potential reads

A_{B}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{(0,0)\}\to\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad A_{B}(x)=\alpha\left(-\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\right),\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2})

(1.1)

so that the Hamiltonian becomes

H_{A}=\left(-i\nabla+\alpha\left(-\frac{x_{2}}{|x|^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{|x|^{2}}\right)\right)^{2}.

(1.2)

The vector potential
A_{B}(x)
generates a
\delta
-type magnetic field

{B}=\nabla\times A_{B}(x)=2\pi\alpha\delta,

which was initially studied by Aharonov and Bohm in
[
1
]
to show the significance of electromagnetic vector potentials in quantum mechanics.
The Aharonov-Bohm effect predicted in
[
1
]
is one of the most interesting and intriguing phenomena of quantum physics.
This effect occurs when electrons propagate in a domain with a zero magnetic field but with a nonzero vector potential
A_{B}
, see
[
26
]
and the references therein.
In another typical cosmic-string scenarios observed by Alford and Wilczek
[
2
]
, the fermionic charges can be non-integer multiples of the Higgs charges.
As the flux is quantized with respect to the Higgs charge, this will lead to a non-trivial Aharonov-Bohm scattering of these fermions.
From the mathematical points,
we refer to
[
11
,
12
]
and references therein for an overview of the spectral theory of this Hamiltonian in Aharonov-Bohm magnetic field. This Hamiltonian model is doubly critical, because of the scaling invariance of the model and the singularities of the potentials, which are not locally integrable.
In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
a generalization of
H_{A}
was considered in
[
11
,
12
]
in which
the dispersive estimates were proved for Schrödinger equation. Recently, in
[
15
,
18
]
, the authors have
proved the Strichartz estimates
for wave and Klein-Gordon equations in the Aharonov-Bohm magnetic fields. It worths mentioning that the wave and Klein-Gordon equations are lack of pseudoconformal invariance which plays an important role in
[
11
]
for Schrödinger equation, hence we have to use a new method in
[
15
,
18
]
.

In the present paper, we consider the Hamiltonian for magnetic many-particles Schrödinger operator arising in the Hall effect
[
22
,
25
]
. In particular,
we recall the Hamiltonian for magnetic multi-particle
Dirichlet forms with Aharonov-Bohm type in
[
20
]
.
Let
{\vec{x}}_{j}=(x_{j1},x_{j2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
j=1,2,\ldots,N
and let

{\bf F}_{j}=\alpha\Big(-\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{{x}_{j2}-{x}_{k2}}{r_{jk}^{2}},\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{{x}_{j1}-{x}_{k1}}{r_{jk}^{2}}\Big),\quad r_{jk}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{2}(x_{ji}-x_{ki})^{2},

(1.3)

then the Hamiltonian is given by

H_{A,N}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}(-i\nabla_{j}+{\bf F}_{j})^{2},

(1.4)

which is singular in the set

S=\cup_{j\neq k}S_{j,k},

where for all
j,k\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\},j\neq k
,

S_{j,k}=\{x=(\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2}\ldots,\vec{x}_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:\vec{x}_{j}=\vec{x}_{k}\}.

(1.5)

We aim to study the dispersive behaviors of the Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+H_{A,N}u=0,\qquad&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus S,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0},\qquad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2N},\end{cases}

(1.6)

and the wave equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}u+H_{A,N}u=0,\qquad&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2N}\setminus S,\\
u(0,x)=f,\quad\partial_{t}u(0,x)=g,\qquad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{2N}.\end{cases}

(1.7)

The research on the dispersive and Strichartz estimates of the wave and Schrödinger propagators with one single potential has a long history, we refer to
[
28
]
and
the survey
[
29
]
and the references therein.
From the physical perspective, it is natural to ask whether similar estimates hold in the presence of interaction potentials.
In the
N
-particles interaction case, there is much fewer result than single case. It is known that the local-in-time dispersive and Strichartz estimates
follow from the kernel in
[
16
]
with a large class of potential interactions. Recently, Hong
[
19
]
has obtained the global-in-time
Strichartz estimates but with “small” interaction potentials. Chong, Grillakis, Machedon and Zhao
[
10
]
proved the global-in-time Strichartz estimates
for the equation
\big(i\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}-\Delta_{y}+\frac{1}{N}V_{N}(x-y)\big)u(t,x,y)=F
in mixed coordinates norm
L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}_{x}L^{2}_{y}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.
To the best of our knowledge, in the
N
-particles case, there is no result about the decay and Strichartz estimates for (
1.6
) and (
1.7
).
The two main obstacles to prove the decay estimates arise from the scaling critical Aharonov-Bohm potential and interactions between the many particles.
Fortunately, in the special two-particles case
N=2
, which is the simplest case, the Hamiltonian
H_{A,2}
can be reduced to a one-particle Hamiltonian
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
in (
2.4
) below but with more singular
Aharonov-Bohm type potential in higher dimensions. Because of this, we can obtain the decay estimates and Strichartz estimates in
the two-particles case.

Our main results about the Strichartz estimates are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N=2
and let

u(t,x)
be a solution of Schrödinger equation (
1.6
), then there exists a constant
C
such that

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

(1.8)

where

(q,r)\in\Lambda^{S}_{0}:=\Big\{(q,r)\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty):\tfrac{2}{q}=4\big(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{r}\big)\Big\}.

(1.9)

Remark 1.2
.

By using dual argument, the endpoint Strichartz estimates (
1.8
) with
(q,r)=(2,4)
gives
the inhomogeneous Strichartz estimates

\Big\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)H_{A,2}}F(s)ds\Big\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{4}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}.

Then by using the argument in
[
21
, Remark 8.8]
, we can obtain the uniform resolvent estimate

\|(H_{A,2}-z)^{-1}\|_{L^{\frac{4}{3}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\to L^{4}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq C,

(1.10)

where the constant
C
is independent of
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Remark 1.3
.

It would be interesting to generalize the result to
N
-particles with
N\geq 3
, but the method of this paper is not available.
Compared with the result of
[
19
]
, here we consider the scaling critical magnetic interaction potentials and the interaction between potentials may be larger.
In constrast to the Strichartz estimates of
[
10
]
, the dispersive and Strichartz estimates (
1.8
) are available for the norm of
L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))
but not for the mixed norm of
L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{2})L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N=2
and let

u(t,x)
be a solution of wave equation (
1.7
), then there exists a constant
C
such that

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}+\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\Big)

(1.11)

where
s\geq 0
and

(q,r)\in\Lambda^{w}_{s}:=\Big\{(q,r)\in[2,\infty]\times[2,\infty):\tfrac{2}{q}\leq 3\big(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{r}\big),s=4\big(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{r}\big)-\tfrac{1}{q}\Big\}.

(1.12)

By the change of variables

{\vec{y}}_{1}=\frac{\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad{\vec{y}}_{2}=\frac{\vec{x}_{1}+\vec{x}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},

(1.13)

and denoting
y=(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
and

v(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2})=u\big(\tfrac{\vec{y}_{1}+\vec{y}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},-\tfrac{\vec{y}_{1}-\vec{y}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)\quad\text{or}\quad u(\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2})=v(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2}),

then, we derive

\displaystyle H_{A,2}u(\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}v(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2}),

(1.14)

where

\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}=\Big(-i\nabla_{y}+{\bf A}(y)\Big)^{2},\quad{\bf A}(y)=\alpha\Big(\frac{-y_{12}}{|y_{11}|^{2}+|y_{12}|^{2}},\frac{y_{11}}{|y_{11}|^{2}+|y_{12}|^{2}},0,0\Big).

Hence,

\displaystyle\big\|e^{itH_{A,2}}u_{0}(x)\big\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\simeq

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}v_{0}(y)\big\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L_{y}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}.

(1.15)

Thus, we can reduce to consider one-particle operator
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
.

As applications, we further
study the nonlinear subcritical Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+H_{A,2}u=|u|^{p-1}u,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus S),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}\in H_{A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.\end{cases}

(1.16)

By changing variables (
1.13
), it is equivalent to consider the nonlinear Schrödinger equation
with Aharonov-Bohm potential in 4D

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}u=|u|^{p-1}u,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0,0,x_{3},x_{4}\}),\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}\in H^{1}_{\bf A}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\end{cases}

(1.17)

where
u:\;\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C},\;2<p<3,
and
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}=\big(-i\nabla_{x}+{\bf A}(x)\big)^{2}.

We aim to establish the scattering theory for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation (
1.17
). In particular, when
{\bf A}=0
, there are rich results about scattering theory for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation. For the defocusing and subcritical nonlinear Schrödinger cases, Ginibre-Velo
[
17
]
proved the scattering theory in energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
n\geq 3
by using the classical Morawetz estimate. Later,
in
[
8
]
, the authors developed a powerful interaction Morawetz estimate and studied the scattering theory for the cubic defocusing Schrödinger equation in a space with regularity less than energy space. The interaction Morawetz estimate reads

\big\||\nabla|^{-\frac{d-3}{4}}u\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{4}}^{4}\leq C\|u(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}}\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L_{t}^{\infty}\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}},

(1.18)

if
u
solves a defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation without potentials. The interaction Morawetz estimate (
1.18
) is so powerful
that the authors of
[
9
]
proved the scattering theory for energy-critical defocusing Schrödinger and the authors of
[
30
]
presented a different proof of the scattering for energy subcritical cases which simplified the argument of
[
17
]
.

In the proof of the scattering theory of nonlinear Schrödinger equation, the Strichartz estimates and interaction Morawetz estimates are two fundamental tools. For our purpose of studying the long time behavior of solution to (
1.17
), in spirit of the papers mentioned above, it is natural to establish the Strichartz and the interaction Morawetz estimates for the linear and nonlinear Schrödinger equations (
1.17
) respectively.
However, the Aharonov-Bohm magnetic potential is scaling-critical and the perturbation is non-trivial, so
the situation becomes more complicated. In
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, the magnetic field is
B=\text{curl}{\bf A}
and the trapping component
B_{\tau}=\frac{x}{|x|}\wedge B
is an obstruction to the dispersion which was observed by
[
3
,
14
]
. Fortunately, the trapping component
B_{\tau}=0
which is generated by the special potential
{\bf A}
in (
2.5
). Hence we can obtain the classical Morawetz estimates from the virial identities
derived by
[
14
]
. While for interaction Morawetz estimates, one needs a positivity of the quantity like
B(x)\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}

which seems impossible for any
y
.
Colliander et.al in
[
7
]
proved the interaction Morawetz estimates for the defocusing nonlinear Schrödinger with magnetic potentials. A key step is to use the method of
[
14
]
to bound the quantity involving
B(x)\frac{x-y}{|x-y|}
when the decay of the magnetic potential
{\bf A}

is fast enough. However, for
{\bf A}
considered here, the decay is not enough to satisfy their assumption

[
7
,
14
, (1.38)]
, therefore the method breaks down. The authors in
[
34
]
obtained the interaction Morawetz estimate for Schrödinger with Aharonov-Bohm potential in 2D,
but assumed that the solution is radial. In 2D,
{\bf A}\cdot\nabla f=0
if
f
is a radial function.
Hence, at present, we have to study the scattering theory via the classical Morawetz estimate by following the method in Ginibre-Velo
[
17
]
.

Now we state our main theorems.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\dot{H}^{1}_{{\bf A}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
be the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\})
with respect to the norm

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}_{{\bf A}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|\nabla_{{\bf A}}f(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(1.19)

where
\nabla_{\bf A}:=-i\nabla+{\bf A}(x)
. Then the inhomogenous space is

H^{1}_{{\bf A}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})=\dot{H}^{1}_{{\bf A}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4}).

(1.20)

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}_{{\bf A}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
. Then, there exists a unique global solution
u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{{\bf A}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))

to (
1.17
). Moreover, the solution

u
scatters in the sense that there exists
u_{\pm}\in H^{1}_{{\bf A}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
such that

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\big\|u(t,\cdot)-e^{it\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}u_{\pm}\big\|_{H^{1}_{{\bf A}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=0.

(1.21)

As mentioned above, in order to establish a scattering theory, we have to prove Strichartz estimates for our model. Fanelli-Vega
[
14
]
obtained the Strichartz estimates for the wave equation with a non-trapping electromagnetic potential with almost Coulomb decay by establishing a family of virial-type identities for
n\geq 3
. However, the method is not applicable to magnetic Schrödinger equation because they only proved the weak dispersive estimate hold in
\dot{\mathcal{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}
instead of
L^{2}
.
Later in
[
3
]
, they proved the Strichartz estimates for Schrödinger when the potentials are almost critical which do not
include the potential (
2.5
). Their method can prove the endpoint Strichartz estimates with derivatives,
but can not cover the usual endpoint Strichartz estimates without derivatives, see
[
3
, (1.13), (1.14)]
.

To achieve the purpose of scattering theory in Theorem
1.6
, we need the chain rules associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
for
differential operators of non-integer order, hence we can derive the desired nonlinear estimates. It is well known that
the traditional Littlewood-Paley theory gives the proof of Leibniz (=product) and chain rules for
differential operators of non-integer order. For example, if
1<p<\infty
and
s>0
, then

\|fg\|_{H^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s,p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{H^{s,p_{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

whenever
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{3}}+\frac{1}{p_{4}}
. For a textbook presentation of these theorems and original references, see
[
32
]
. Our strategy is to prove a boundedness of generalized Riesz transform in
L^{p}

\big\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}^{-\frac{s}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}

(1.22)

and reversed Riesz transform

\big\|\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}^{\frac{s}{2}}(-\Delta)^{-\frac{s}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(1.23)

Hence we obtain

\big\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\simeq\big\|\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}^{\frac{s}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(1.24)

To prove these, we need a boundedness of heat kernel.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}
be in (
2.4
). Then there exists a constant
C
such that

\big|e^{-t\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}(x,y)\big|\leq Ct^{-2}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}},\quad t>0.

(1.25)

From the heat kernel estimates and our previous work
[
23
]
with Killip, Visan and Miao, one can obtain the equivalence of Sobolev norms

\big\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\simeq\big\|\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}^{\frac{s}{2}}f\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},\quad 1<p<\frac{4}{s}

(1.26)

therefore, there holds chain rules

\|fg\|_{H^{s,p}_{\bf A}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s,p_{1}}_{\bf A}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\|g\|_{H^{s,p_{4}}_{\bf A}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},

(1.27)

for
s>0
,
1<p,p_{j}<\tfrac{4}{s}(j=1,2,3,4)

and

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{3}}+\frac{1}{p_{4}}
.

Our paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the representation of the spectral measure associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
. Section 3 is devoted to the proofs of the Strichartz estimates. In Section 4, we establish a scattering theory for the defocusing nonlinear subcritical Schrödinger equation (
1.17
).

2.
The spectral measure

In this section, we first reduce the operator
H_{A,2}

\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}

) below by making change of

2.1.
The operator

From (
1.3
) and (
1.4
) with
N=2
,
we see

H_{A,2}=\sum_{j=1}^{2}(-i\nabla_{j}+{\bf F}_{j})^{2},

(2.1)

where
\vec{x}_{1}=(x_{11},x_{12}),\vec{x}_{2}=(x_{21},x_{22})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and

\displaystyle\nabla_{\vec{x}_{1}}=(\partial_{x_{11}},\partial_{x_{12}}),\quad{\bf F}_{1}=\alpha\Big(-\frac{x_{12}-x_{22}}{|\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2}|},\frac{x_{11}-x_{21}}{|\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2}|}\Big),

\displaystyle\nabla_{\vec{x}_{2}}=(\partial_{x_{21}},\partial_{x_{22}}),\quad{\bf F}_{2}=\alpha\Big(-\frac{x_{22}-x_{12}}{|\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2}|},\frac{x_{21}-x_{11}}{|\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2}|}\Big).

Let

{\vec{y}}_{1}=\frac{\vec{x}_{1}-\vec{x}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad{\vec{y}}_{2}=\frac{\vec{x}_{1}+\vec{x}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},

then

\nabla_{{\vec{x}}_{1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\nabla_{{\vec{y}}_{1}}+\nabla_{{\vec{y}}_{2}}),\quad\nabla_{\vec{x}_{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\nabla_{{\vec{y}}_{2}}-\nabla_{{\vec{y}}_{1}}),

and

\displaystyle H_{A,2}=

\displaystyle\Big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}+\alpha\big(-\frac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\frac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)\Big)^{2}-\Delta_{\vec{y}_{2}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(-i\big(\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}},\nabla_{\vec{y}_{2}}\big)+\alpha\big(-\frac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\frac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},0,0\big)\Big)^{2}.

In fact

\displaystyle H_{A,2}=

\displaystyle(-i\nabla_{\vec{x}_{1}}+{\bf F}_{1})^{2}+(-i\nabla_{\vec{x}_{2}}+{\bf F}_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{2}}+\alpha\big(-\tfrac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\tfrac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)\big)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\big(i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{2}}-\alpha\big(-\tfrac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\tfrac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}+\alpha\big(-\tfrac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\tfrac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{2}}\big)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}+\alpha\big(-\tfrac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\tfrac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)+i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{2}}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}+\alpha\big(-\tfrac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\tfrac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)\big)^{2}-\big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}+\alpha\big(-\tfrac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\tfrac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)\big)\cdot i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{2}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\vec{y}_{2}}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}\big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}+\alpha\big(-\tfrac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\tfrac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)\big)^{2}+\big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}+\alpha\big(-\tfrac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\tfrac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)\big)\cdot i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{2}}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta_{\vec{y}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}+\alpha\big(-\frac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\frac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big)\Big)^{2}-\Delta_{\vec{y}_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}}+\alpha\big(-\frac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\frac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}}\big),-i\nabla_{\vec{y}_{2}}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(-i\big(\nabla_{\vec{y}_{1}},\nabla_{\vec{y}_{2}}\big)+\alpha\big(-\frac{y_{12}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},\frac{y_{11}}{|\vec{y}_{1}|^{2}},0,0\big)\Big)^{2},

which implies (
2.2
). Hence, if we denote
y=(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
and

v(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2})=u\big(\tfrac{\vec{y}_{1}+\vec{y}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},-\tfrac{\vec{y}_{1}-\vec{y}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}}\big)\quad\text{or}\quad u(\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2})=v(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2}),

then,

\displaystyle H_{A,2}u(\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}v(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2}),

(2.3)

with

\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}=\Big(-i\nabla_{y}+{\bf A}(y)\Big)^{2},\quad{\bf A}(y)=\alpha\Big(\frac{-y_{12}}{|y_{11}|^{2}+|y_{12}|^{2}},\frac{y_{11}}{|y_{11}|^{2}+|y_{12}|^{2}},0,0\Big)

which is analogue of (
1.1
). Both of them are effected in the first two variables by the magnetic potential.
Without confusing, from now on, we briefly write
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
to consist with (
1.1
) as follows:

\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}=\Big(-i\nabla_{x}+{\bf A}(x)\Big)^{2},\quad x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0,0,x_{3},x_{4}\},

(2.4)

with

{\bf A}(x)=\alpha\left(-\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}},0,0\right),\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.5)

This operator can be regraded as a one-particle model but with more singular
Aharonov-Bohm type potential in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

We remark that the operator (
2.4
) with magnetic potential (
2.5
) was studied in
[

]
from the Hardy inequality viewpoint and in
[
14
]
from the virial identities, see the special model in
[

and

[

.
However, the Strichartz estimates for dispersive equations with potential (

) have not been proved yet even though the Strichartz estimates obtained in
[

]
for a bit faster decaying
{\bf A}
.
It is known that the magnetic field

Heuristic, since the dispersion obstruction

(the trapping component of magnetic field arising from

18

2.2.
The Schrödinger kernel

In this subsection, we modify the argument of
[

]
, in which we construct the spectral measure for a similar model in

, to construct the kernel of Schrödinger operator

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
x=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,x_{3},x_{4})
,
y=(\bar{r}\cos\bar{\theta},\bar{r}\sin\bar{\theta},y_{3},y_{4})
, and let
K(t;r,\theta,x_{3},x_{4};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y_{3},y_{4})
be the kernel of
e^{it\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}
with
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}
being as in (
2.4
), then there holds

\displaystyle K(t;r,\theta,x_{3},x_{4};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y_{3},y_{4})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{(it)^{2}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4it}}A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})+\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{1}{(it)^{2}}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\times e^{-\frac{r^{2}+\bar{r}^{2}+|x_{3}-y_{3}|^{2}+|x_{4}-y_{4}|^{2}}{4it}}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-\frac{r\bar{r}\cosh s}{2it}}B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle G(t;r,\theta,x_{3},x_{4};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y_{3},y_{4})+D(t;r,\theta,x_{3};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y_{3},y_{4}),

where
A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})
and
B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})
are respectively

\displaystyle A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})

\displaystyle=e^{i\alpha(\theta-\bar{\theta})}\big[1_{[0,\pi]}(|\theta-\bar{\theta}|)+e^{-2\pi i\alpha}1_{[\pi,2\pi]}(|\theta-\bar{\theta}|)\big],

(2.7)

and

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})=

\displaystyle-\Big[\sin(|\alpha|\pi)e^{-|\alpha|s}+\sin(\alpha\pi)

\displaystyle\times\frac{(e^{-s}-\cos(\theta-\bar{\theta}+\pi))\sinh\alpha s-i\sin(\theta-\bar{\theta}+\pi)\cosh\alpha s}{\cosh s-\cos(\theta-\bar{\theta}+\pi)}\Big].

(2.8)

Proof.

We prove (
2.6
) by following the idea in
[
18
]
. First, we obtain the fundamental solution of Schrödinger operator
e^{it\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}
.
In the cylindrical coordinates

x_{1}=r\cos\theta,\quad x_{2}=r\sin\theta,\quad x_{3}=x_{3},\quad x_{4}=x_{4},

(2.9)

from (
2.5
), we write

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}&=-\Delta+2i{\bf A}(x)\cdot\nabla+|{\bf A}(x)|^{2}\\
&=-\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{(i\partial_{\theta}+\alpha)^{2}}{r^{2}}-\partial_{x_{3}}^{2}-\partial_{x_{4}}^{2}.\end{split}

(2.10)

For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
Y_{k}(\theta):=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-ik\theta}
satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(i\partial_{\theta}+\alpha)^{2}Y_{k}(\theta)=(k+\alpha)^{2}Y_{k}(\theta),\quad on\ \ \mathbb{S}^{1},\\
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Y_{k}(\theta)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\theta=1,\end{cases}

(2.11)

hence, the eigenfunctions
\{Y_{k}(\theta)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
construct a complete orthonormal basis in
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Moreover, let
x^{\prime}=(x_{3},x_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we can write
f(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
in the form of

f(x)=f(r,\theta,x^{\prime})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}(r,x^{\prime})Y_{k}(\theta),

(2.12)

where

a_{k}(r,x^{\prime})=\int^{2\pi}_{0}f(r,\theta,x^{\prime})Y_{k}(\theta).

Therefore, from (
2.10
), (
2.11
) and (
2.12
), we know

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}f&=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(A_{k,\alpha}a_{k}(r,x^{\prime})-\partial_{x_{3}}^{2}a_{k}(r,x^{\prime})-\partial_{x_{4}}^{2}a_{k}(r,x^{\prime})\Big)Y_{k}(\theta)\end{split}

(2.13)

where

\begin{split}A_{k,\alpha}=-\partial_{r}^{2}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{(k+\alpha)^{2}}{r^{2}}.\end{split}

(2.14)

Let
\nu=\nu(k)=|k+\alpha|
, and recall Hankel transform (e.g. see
[
4
,
31
]
) of
\nu
order defined by

(H_{\nu}f)(\rho,\theta,x^{\prime})=\int^{\infty}_{0}J_{\nu}(r\rho)f(r,\theta,x^{\prime})r\mathrm{d}r,\quad\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),

(2.15)

where the Bessel function

J_{\nu}(r)=\frac{(r/2)^{\nu}}{\Gamma(\nu+\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(1/2)}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{isr}(1-s^{2})^{(2\nu-1)/2}\mathrm{d}s,\quad\nu>-\frac{1}{2},r>0.

(2.16)

For our purpose, we recall some properties of the Hankel transform, see
[
4
,
31
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(Hankel transform)
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
be the Hankel transform in (
2.15
) and
A_{\nu}:=-\partial^{2}_{r}-\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}+\frac{\nu^{2}}{r^{2}}
. Then

\mathrm{(1)}

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}=\mathcal{H}^{-1}_{\nu},

\mathrm{(2)}

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
is self-adjoint, i.e.
\quad\mathcal{H}_{\nu}=\mathcal{H}^{*}_{\nu}
,

\mathrm{(3)}

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}
is an
L^{2}
isometry, i.e.
\|\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},

\mathrm{(4)}

\mathcal{H}_{\nu}(A_{\nu}f)(\rho,\theta)=\rho^{2}(\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f)(\rho,\theta),
for
f\in L^{2}.

In the cylindrical coordinates (
2.9
), if the initial data

u_{0}(x)=f(r,\theta,x^{\prime})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}a_{k}(r,x^{\prime})Y_{k}(\theta),

then
u(t,x)=e^{it\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}f
satisfies

u(t,x)=u(t;r,\theta,x^{\prime})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}u_{k}(t;r,x^{\prime})Y_{k}(\theta),

where
u_{k}(t;r,x^{\prime})
solves

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u_{k}+A_{k,\alpha}u_{k}-\partial_{x_{3}}^{2}u_{k}-\partial_{x_{4}}^{2}u_{k}=0,\quad(t,r,x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
u_{k}(0,r,x^{\prime})=a_{k}(r,x^{\prime}).\end{cases}

(2.17)

By taking Fourier transform in variables
x^{\prime}
and Hankel transform in variable
r
and using Lemma
2.2
, and let
\xi=(\xi_{3},\xi_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we obtain

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}\tilde{u}_{k}(t;\rho,\xi)+\rho^{2}\tilde{u}_{k}(t;\rho,\xi)+|\xi|^{2}\tilde{u}_{k}(t;\rho,\xi)=0,\\
\tilde{u}_{k}(0;\rho,\xi)=\tilde{a}_{k}(\rho,\xi).\end{cases}

(2.18)

where

\tilde{u}_{k}(t;\rho,\xi)=[H_{\nu(k)}\hat{u}_{k}](t;\rho,\xi),\qquad\tilde{a}_{k}(\rho,\xi)=[H_{\nu(k)}\hat{a}_{k}](\rho,\xi)

and
\hat{a}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-ix^{\prime}\cdot\xi}a(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}
.
The solution for ordinary differential equation (
2.18
) is

\tilde{u}_{k}(t;\rho,\xi)=\tilde{a}_{k}(\rho,\xi)e^{it(\rho^{2}+|\xi|^{2})}.

By using Lemma
2.2
again, we obtain

u(t;r,\theta,x^{\prime})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{2\pi}K(t;r,\theta,x^{\prime};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y^{\prime})f(\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y^{\prime})\,\bar{r}\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}\bar{\theta}dy^{\prime},

where

\begin{split}&K(t;r,\theta,x^{\prime};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y^{\prime})=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}Y_{k}(\theta)\overline{Y_{k}(\bar{\theta})}\\
&\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})\cdot\xi}e^{it(\rho^{2}+|\xi|^{2})}J_{\nu(k)}(r\rho)J_{\nu(k)}(\bar{r}\rho)\rho\mathrm{d}\rho\mathrm{d}\xi.\end{split}

(2.19)

Noting that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}e^{-it|\xi|^{2}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi=\frac{\pi}{it}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4it}}

and
Y_{k}(\theta)=(2\pi)^{-1/2}e^{-ik\theta}
, we rewrite
K(t;r,\theta,x^{\prime};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y^{\prime})
as

\displaystyle K(t;r,\theta,x^{\prime};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2it}e^{-\frac{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{4it}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-ik(\theta-\bar{\theta})}\int_{0}^{\infty}J_{\nu(k)}(r\rho)J_{\nu(k)}(\bar{r}\rho)e^{it\rho^{2}}\rho\mathrm{d}\rho,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2it}e^{-\frac{|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{4it}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-ik(\theta-\bar{\theta})}\times\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\frac{e^{-\frac{r^{2}+\bar{r}^{2}}{4(\varepsilon+it)}}}{2(\varepsilon+it)}I_{\nu(k)}\left(\frac{r\bar{r}}{2(\varepsilon+it)}\right),

(2.20)

where the Weber identity
[
31
]
is used in the last equality and the term
I_{\nu(k)}(z)
(
[
33
]
) is given by

I_{\nu(k)}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int^{\pi}_{0}e^{z\cos s}\cos\nu s\mathrm{d}s-\frac{\sin\nu\pi}{\pi}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-z\cosh s}e^{-s\nu}\mathrm{d}s.

Recall
\nu=|k+\alpha|
, similarly as in
[
15
,
18
]
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-ik(\theta-\bar{\theta})}\int^{\pi}_{0}e^{z\cos s}\cos\nu s\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\times\begin{cases}e^{z\cos(\theta-\bar{\theta})}e^{i\alpha(\theta-\bar{\theta})},\quad|\theta-\bar{\theta}|<\pi,\\
e^{z\cos(\theta-\bar{\theta})}e^{i\alpha(\theta-\bar{\theta}-2\pi)},\quad\pi<|\theta-\bar{\theta}|<2\pi,\end{cases}

(2.21)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-ik(\theta-\bar{\theta})}\sin\nu\pi\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-z\cosh s}e^{-s\nu}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-z\cosh s}\Big[\sin(|\alpha|\pi)e^{-|\alpha|s}+\sin(\alpha\pi)

\displaystyle\times\frac{(e^{-s}-\cos(\theta-\bar{\theta}+\pi))\sinh\alpha s-i\sin(\theta-\bar{\theta}+\pi)\cosh\alpha s}{\cosh s-\cos(\theta-\bar{\theta}+\pi)}\Big]\mathrm{d}s.

(2.22)

By noticing that

(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+(x_{2}-y_{2})^{2}=r^{2}+\bar{r}^{2}-2r\bar{r}\cos(\theta-\bar{\theta})

and collecting (
2.2
)-(
2.2
) together, we finally prove Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Decay estimates follow from the representation of fundamental solution (

).

Proof.

Indeed, from

|A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})|+\int^{\infty}_{0}|B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})|\leq C,

2.3.
The representation of spectral measure

To prove the dispersive estimates for wave equation, we need the representation of the spectral measure associated with
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
x=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta,x^{\prime})
,
y=(\bar{r}\cos\bar{\theta},\bar{r}\sin\bar{\theta},y^{\prime})
where
x^{\prime}=(x_{3},x_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
y^{\prime}=(y_{3},y_{4})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)
be the spectral measure kernel associated with Schrödinger operator
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
given by (
2.4
). Then

\displaystyle dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{3}}{\pi^{2}}\sum_{\pm}a_{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)e^{\pm i\lambda|x-y|}\times A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda^{3}}{\pi^{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\sum_{\pm}a_{\pm}(\lambda|\vec{n}|)e^{\pm i\lambda|\vec{n}|}\times B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})\mathrm{d}s,

(2.25)

where
a_{\pm}(r)
satisfies

|\partial_{r}^{k}a_{\pm}(r)|\leq C_{k}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}-k},\quad\forall k\geq 0,

(2.26)

and
A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})
,
B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})
are as in (
2.7
) and (
2.1
) respectively, and

\vec{n}=\vec{n}_{s}=(r+\bar{r},\sqrt{2r\bar{r}(\cosh s-1)},x_{3}-y_{3},x_{4}-y_{4}).

(2.27)

Proof.

Note that for each
z\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\Im z>0
, there holds

\frac{1}{s-z}=i\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-ist}e^{izt}\mathrm{d}t,\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.28)

This together with (
2.6
) shows

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}-(\lambda^{2}+i0))^{-1}=

\displaystyle i\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{-it\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}e^{it(\lambda^{2}+i\varepsilon)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int^{\infty}_{0}G(t;r,\theta,x_{3},x_{4};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y_{3},y_{4})e^{it(\lambda^{2}+i\varepsilon)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+i\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int^{\infty}_{0}D(t;r,\theta,x_{3},x_{4};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y_{3},y_{4})e^{it(\lambda^{2}+i\varepsilon)}\mathrm{d}t.

(2.29)

As did in
[
18
]
, noting that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}e^{-it|\xi|^{2}}\;d\xi=\frac{\pi}{(it)^{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4it}},

we get for
z=\lambda^{2}+i\epsilon
with
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4it}}}{(it)^{2}}e^{itz}\;dt=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{-i(x-y)\cdot\xi}e^{-it|\xi|^{2}}\;d\xi\,e^{itz}\;dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{-i(x-y)\cdot\xi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-it(|\xi|^{2}-z)}\;dt\;d\xi=\frac{1}{i\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{e^{-i(x-y)\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-z}\;d\xi,

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-\frac{r_{1}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}+|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2}}{4it}}}{(it)^{2}}e^{-\frac{r_{1}r_{2}}{2it}\cosh s}e^{itz}\;dt=\frac{1}{i\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{e^{-i{\vec{n}}\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-z}\;d\xi,

(2.30)

where
{\vec{n}}=(r_{1}+r_{2},\sqrt{2r_{1}r_{2}(\cosh s-1)},x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})
.
Then from (
2.6
), we have

i\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int^{\infty}_{0}G(t;r,\theta,x^{\prime};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y^{\prime})e^{it(\lambda^{2}+i\varepsilon)}\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{e^{-i(x-y)\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+i0)}\mathrm{d}\xi\times A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta}),

(2.31)

and

\displaystyle i\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\int^{\infty}_{0}D(t;r,\theta,x^{\prime};\bar{r},\bar{\theta},y^{\prime})e^{it(\lambda^{2}+i\varepsilon)}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{e^{-i(r+\bar{r},\sqrt{2r\bar{r}(\cosh s-1)},x_{3}-y_{3},x_{4}-y_{4})\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+i0)}\mathrm{d}\xi\times B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})\mathrm{d}s.

(2.32)

Therefore, collecting (
2.3
)-(
2.3
) together and recalling (
2.27
), we get

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}-(\lambda^{2}+i0))^{-1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{e^{-i(x-y)\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+i0)}\mathrm{d}\xi\times A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{e^{-i\vec{n}\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+i0)}\mathrm{d}\xi\times B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})\mathrm{d}s.

(2.33)

Observing that
\overline{A_{-\alpha}}=A_{\alpha}
and
\overline{B_{-\alpha}}=B_{\alpha}
, then we obtain

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}-(\lambda^{2}-i0))^{-1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{e^{-i(x-y)\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+i0)}\mathrm{d}\xi\times A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{e^{-i\vec{n}\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}-i0)}\mathrm{d}\xi\times B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})\mathrm{d}s.

(2.34)

By using the Stone’s formula, we deduce that

\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)=\frac{\lambda}{i\pi}\big[(\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}-(\lambda^{2}+i0))^{-1}-(\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}-(\lambda^{2}-i0))^{-1}\big]\mathrm{d}\lambda.

(2.35)

Combining (
2.3
)-(
2.35
), we further write

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}E_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}\frac{\lambda}{4\pi^{3}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{-i(x-y)\cdot\xi}(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}-i0)}-\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+i0)})\mathrm{d}\xi\times A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{i}\frac{\lambda}{4\pi^{3}}\int^{\infty}_{0}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{-i\vec{n}\cdot\xi}(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}-i0)}-\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+i0)})\mathrm{d}\xi\right]\times B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})\mathrm{d}s.

Similarly to
[
18
]
again, we observe
the fact that
the Poisson kernel is an approximation to the identity which implies that, for any reasonable function
m(x)

\begin{split}m(x)&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\rm Im}\Big(\frac{1}{x-(y+i\epsilon)}\Big)m(y)dy\\
&=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\epsilon}{(x-y)^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}m(y)dy,\end{split}

(2.36)

then we have

\begin{split}&\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\lambda}{i\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\Big(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+i\epsilon)}-\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}-i\epsilon)}\Big)d\xi\\
=&\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\lambda}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\Im\Big(\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+i\epsilon)}\Big)d\xi\\
=&\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\lambda}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\epsilon}{(\rho^{2}-\lambda^{2})^{2}+\epsilon^{2}}\int_{|\omega|=1}e^{-i\rho x\cdot\omega}d\sigma_{\omega}\,\rho^{3}d\rho\\
=&\lambda^{3}\int_{|\omega|=1}e^{-i\lambda x\cdot\omega}d\sigma_{\omega}.\end{split}

(2.37)

On the other hand, from
[
27
, Theorem 1.2.1]
, we also note that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}e^{-ix\cdot\omega}d\sigma(\omega)=\sum_{\pm}a_{\pm}(|x|)e^{\pm i|x|},\end{split}

(2.38)

where

\begin{split}|\partial_{r}^{k}a_{\pm}(r)|\leq C_{k}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}-k},\quad k\geq 0.\end{split}

(2.39)

Therefore we finally obtain representation (
2.4
).

∎

3.1.
The proof of Theorem

From the dispersive estimates in Proposition

-estimate (obtained from the mass conservation law for Schrödinger equation or the unitary property of

directly shows

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}
be in (

u(t,x)
be a solution of Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}u=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0,0,x_{3},x_{4}\}\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.\end{cases}

(3.1)

Then there exists a constant
C
such that

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},

(3.2)

where

(q,r)\in\Lambda_{0}^{S}
defined in (
1.9
).

Remark 3.2
.

The Strichartz estimates (

) can be extended to the solution of

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A},a}u=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\},\\

(3.3)

where the electromagnetic operator

and

and

\min_{\hat{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{3}}a(\hat{x})>-1
. Indeed, this is a consequence of (

) and the local smoothing estimates

\||x|^{-1}u(t,x)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},

which is implied by

\sup_{\sigma\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)}\big\||x|^{-1}(\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A},a}-\sigma)^{-1}|x|^{-1}\big\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\leq C.

(3.5)

Remark 3.3
.

The method also works for dimension three in which

{\bf A}(x)=\alpha\left(-\frac{x_{2}}{|x_{1}|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{|x_{1}|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2}},0\right),\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.

In this case, the endpoint Strichartz estimate (
3.2
) with
(q,r)=(2,6)
is a generalization of
[
3
, (1.14)]
.

Now we prove Theorem
1.1
by using (
3.2
). Let
u(t,\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2})
solve (
1.6
), from (
2.3
), then

v(t;\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2})=u\Big(t;\frac{\vec{y}_{1}+\vec{y}_{2}}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{\vec{y}_{2}-\vec{y}_{1}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big)

(3.6)

solves (
3.1
).
Therefore, by (
1.15
) and (

), we obtain for

defined in (

)

\begin{split}\|u(t,\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2})\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}&\leq C\|v(t;\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2})\|_{L^{q}_{t}(\mathbb{R};L^{r}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\\
&\leq C\|v_{0}(\vec{y}_{1},\vec{y}_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq C\|u_{0}(\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.\end{split}

(3.7)

Thus, we complete the proof of Theorem

3.2.
The proof of Theorem
1.4

By the same argument of (

), to prove Theorem

, it suffices to establish the Strichartz estimates
for the wave equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{tt}u+\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}u=0,\qquad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0,0,x_{3},x_{4}\}\\
u(0,x)=f(x),\qquad\partial_{t}u(0,x)=g(x)\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{4}.\end{cases}

(3.8)

To this goal, we first prove the localized dispersive estimates for wave (
3.8
).

Proposition 3.4

(Dispersive estimate for wave)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
be given in (
2.4
) and
let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([1/2,2])
take value in
[0,1]
such that
1=\sum\limits_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)
. Assume
f=\phi(2^{-k}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}})f
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, then there exists a constant
C
independent of
t
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\begin{split}\|e^{it\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq C2^{\frac{5}{2}k}(2^{-k}+|t|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.\end{split}

(3.9)

Proof.

With Proposition
2.4
in hand, we use stationary phase argument to prove Proposition
3.4
.
We write

e^{it\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda}\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)f(y)dy.

Now we aim to show kernel estimate

\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda}\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|\leq C2^{\frac{5}{2}k}(2^{-k}+|t|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

(3.10)

For this purpose, from Proposition
2.4
, it suffices to show

\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda}\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{3}a_{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)e^{\pm i\lambda|x-y|}d\lambda A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})\Big|\leq C2^{\frac{5}{2}k}(2^{-k}+|t|)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

(3.11)

and

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda}\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{\pm}(\lambda|\vec{n}_{s}|)e^{\pm i\lambda|\vec{n}_{s}|}&B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})ds\,d\lambda\Big|\\
&\leq C2^{\frac{5}{2}k}(2^{-k}+|t|)^{-\frac{3}{2}},\end{split}

(3.12)

where
a_{\pm}
satisfies (
2.26
),

\vec{n}_{s}
is in (
2.27
), and
A_{\alpha}(\theta,\bar{\theta})
,
B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})
are in (
2.7
) and (
2.1
).
Since
a_{\pm}
satisfies (
2.26
), let
r=|x-y|
or
|\vec{n}_{s}|
, hence

\big|\partial_{\lambda}^{N}[a_{\pm}(\lambda r)]\big|\leq C_{N}\lambda^{-N}(1+\lambda r)^{-\frac{3}{2}},\quad N\geq 0.

(3.13)

We first prove (
3.11
). If
2^{k}|t\pm|x-y||\leq 1
, by (
3.13
), we directly estimate

\begin{split}&\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda}\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{3}a_{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)e^{\pm i\lambda|x-y|}d\lambda\Big|\\
\leq&C\int_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k+1}}\lambda^{3}(1+\lambda|x-y|)^{-3/2}d\lambda\\
\leq&C2^{4k}(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{-3/2}.\end{split}

If
2^{k}|t\pm|x-y||\geq 1
, we use (
3.13
) again and perform
N
-times integration by parts to obtain

\begin{split}&\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda}\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{3}a_{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)e^{\pm i\lambda|x-y|}d\lambda\Big|\\
\leq&\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{i(t\pm|x-y|)}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\right)^{N}\big(e^{i(t\pm|x-y|)\lambda}\big)\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{3}a_{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)d\lambda\Big|\\
\leq&C_{N}|t\pm|x-y||^{-N}\int_{2^{k-1}}^{2^{k+1}}\lambda^{3-N}(1+\lambda|x-y|)^{-3/2}d\lambda\\
\leq&C_{N}2^{k(4-N)}|t\pm|x-y||^{-N}(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{-3/2}.\end{split}

It follows that

\begin{split}&\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda}\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{3}a_{\pm}(\lambda|x-y|)e^{\pm i\lambda|x-y|}d\lambda\Big|\\
\leq&C_{N}2^{4k}\big(1+2^{k}|t\pm|x-y||\big)^{-N}(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{-3/2}.\end{split}

(3.14)

If
|t|\sim|x-y|
, we see (
3.11
).
Otherwise, we have
|t\pm|x-y||\geq c|t|
for some small constant

c
, choose
N=1
and
N=0
, and then use geometric mean argument to obtain (
3.11
).

We next prove (
3.12
). We follow the same lines to obtain

\begin{split}\Big|\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda}\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)\lambda^{3}&\int_{0}^{\infty}a_{\pm}(\lambda|\vec{n}_{s}|)e^{\pm i\lambda|\vec{n}_{s}|}B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})ds\,d\lambda\Big|\\
&\leq C2^{\frac{5}{2}k}(2^{-k}+|t|)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})|ds.\end{split}

(3.15)

From (
2.24
), we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}|B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})|ds\leq C,

(3.16)

which implies (
3.12
). Therefore we prove (
3.10
), hence (
3.9
) holds.

∎

We also need the following proposition to achieve our goal.

Proposition 3.5

(A kernel estimate)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
be given in (
2.4
) and
let
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([1/2,2])
and take value in

[0,1]
. For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and any
K\geq 0
, there exists a constant
C
independent of
k\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\begin{split}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(2^{-k}\lambda)dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|\lesssim\frac{2^{4k}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{K}}.\end{split}

(3.17)

Proof.

On one hand, by using scaling, we see

\begin{split}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(2^{-k}\lambda)dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|=2^{4k}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(\lambda)dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;2^{k}x,2^{k}y)\Big|.\end{split}

(3.18)

On the other hand, by using Proposition
2.4
and integration by parts K times, we have

\begin{split}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(\lambda)dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{K}}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+|\vec{n}_{s}|)^{K}}|B_{\alpha}(s,\theta,\bar{\theta})|ds.\end{split}

From (
2.27
), we observe that
|\vec{n}_{s}|^{2}\geq(r_{1}+r_{2})^{2}+|x_{3}-y_{3}|^{2}+|x_{4}-y_{4}|^{2}\geq|x-y|^{2}
.
Thus it follows from (
3.16
) that

\begin{split}\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\psi(\lambda)dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{K}}.\end{split}

This together with (
3.18
) gives (
3.17
).

∎

Next we need the Littlewood-Paley square function inequality associated with the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
.
This can be done by following the proof of
[
15
, Proposition 2.2]
, once we show the heat kernel estimate in Theorem

Even

is slightly different from the operator

. We omit the detail of the proof of Theorem

.

Therefore, by the standard argument deriving Littlewood-Paley theory from the Gaussian boundedness of heat kernel (e.g. see
[
15
]
), we have

Proposition 3.6

(LP square function inequality)
.

Let
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be in Proposition
3.4
and let
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
be given in (
2.4
).
Then for
1<p<\infty
,
there exist constants
c_{p}
and
C_{p}
depending on

such that

c_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq\Big\|\Big(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\phi_{k}(\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}})f|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(3.19)

For each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\phi_{k}(\lambda)=\phi(2^{-k}\lambda)
, we define

\displaystyle U_{k}(t)=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{it\lambda}\phi_{k}(\lambda)dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y),

(3.20)

where
dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)
is the spectral measure in Proposition
2.4
.
Hence by spectral property (e.g.
[
21
, Lemma 5.3]
), we see

U_{k}(t)U_{k}^{\ast}(s)=\int^{\infty}_{0}e^{i(t-s)\lambda}\big(\phi_{k}(\lambda)\big)^{2}dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y).

On one hand, by Proposition
3.4
, we have

\|U_{k}(t)U_{k}^{\ast}(s)\|_{L^{1}\to L^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{5k}(2^{-k}+|t-s|)^{-3}.

(3.21)

On the other hand, by duality argument, we have

\|U(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}=\|U_{k}(t)U_{k}^{\ast}(t)\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\lesssim 1.

(3.22)

Indeed, this is implied by

\Big|\int^{\infty}_{0}\big(\phi_{k}(\lambda)\big)^{2}dE_{\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}(\lambda;x,y)\Big|\lesssim\frac{2^{4k}}{(1+2^{k}|x-y|)^{N}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),

which follows from Proposition (
3.5

Then using Keel-Tao’s argument (also see
[

), for

(3.23)

Let
f_{j}(x)=\phi(2^{-j}\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}})f

(3.24)

By using (
3.19
) and Minkowski’s inequality, we show that

\displaystyle\|e^{it\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}f\|_{L_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R}L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big\|\big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{k}(t)f_{j}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R}L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{k}(t)f_{j}\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R}L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

We observe that
\phi_{k}(\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}})f_{j}
vanishes when

, thus

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\big\|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{k}(t)f_{j}\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R}L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{|j-k|\leq 10}\|U_{k}(t)f_{j}\|_{L_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R}L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

u(t,x)=\frac{e^{it\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}+e^{-it\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}}{2}f(x)+\frac{e^{it\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}-e^{-it\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}}{2i\sqrt{\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}}g(x),

(3.27)

we finally prove the Strichartz estimates

\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{t}^{q}(\mathbb{R}L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}_{\bf A}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}+\|g\|_{\dot{H}_{\bf A}^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(3.28)

By the equivalence of Sobolev norms (
1.26

1.11

1.4
is now complete.

4.1.
Global well-posedness

By interpolating (

) and mass conservation, we obtain the following dispersive estimates.

Proposition 4.1

(Dispersive Estimate)
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
2\leq p\leq+\infty
. Then we have the following estimate

\|e^{it\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq C|t|^{-4(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|f\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

(4.1)

for some constant
C=C(\alpha,p)>0
which does not depend on
t
and
f
.

Then Strichartz estimates follow from a direct application of Keel-Tao’s argument
[
24
]
.

Proposition 4.2

(Strichartz estimate including inhomogeneous term)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{{\bf A}}
be in (
2.4
),

u(t,x)
be a solution of nonlinear Schrödinger equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}u=F(t,x),\qquad(t,x)\in I\times\mathbb{R}^{4},\\
u(t_{0},x)=u_{0},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\end{cases}

(4.2)

then for admissible pairs
(q,r),(\bar{q},\bar{r})\in\Lambda_{0}^{S}

there exists a constant
C
such that

\|u(t,x)\|_{L^{q}_{t}(I\times L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}+\big\|F\big\|_{L_{t}^{\bar{q}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\bar{r}^{\prime}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}.

(4.3)

Remark 4.3
.

From (
4.3
) and (
1.26
), we obtain

\big\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}_{\bf A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}+\big\|\nabla_{\bf A}F\big\|_{L_{t}^{\bar{q}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\bar{r}^{\prime}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})},

(4.4)

and

\big\|\nabla u\big\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}+\big\|\nabla F\big\|_{L_{t}^{\bar{q}^{\prime}}L_{x}^{\bar{r}^{\prime}}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})},\quad r<4.

(4.5)

With dispersive estimates (
4.1
), Strichartz estimates (
4.3
)-(
4.5
) and chain rules of non-integer order for operator
\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}
(
1.27

Proposition 4.4

(Local well-posedness theory)
.

Let
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{\bf A}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
. Then there exists

T=T(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}})>0
such that the equation
(
1.17
) with initial data
u_{0}
has a unique solution
u
with

u,~\nabla_{\bf A}u\in C(I;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))\cap L_{t}^{q}(I;L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})),\quad I=[0,T),

(4.6)

with
(q,r)\in\Lambda_{0}^{S}
.

Proof.

We argue by the standard Banach fixed point argument. To this end, let

X(I)=C(I;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))\cap L_{t}^{q_{0}}(I;L^{r_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})),\quad(q_{0},r_{0})=\big(\tfrac{p+1}{p-1},p+1\big),

we consider the map

\Phi:u\mapsto e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}|u(s)|^{p-1}u(s)\mathrm{d}s

on the complete metric space

\displaystyle B:=\Bigl\{u,~\nabla_{A}u\in C(I;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))\cap L_{t}^{q_{0}}(I;L^{r_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{4})):\,\|u\|_{X(I)},\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\|_{X(I)}\leq 2C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}}\Bigr\}

and the metric

d(u,v):=\|u-v\|_{L_{t}^{q_{0}}(I;L^{r_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}.

The constant
C
depends only on the dimension and
\alpha
, and it
reflects implicit constants in the Strichartz and Sobolev embedding
inequalities. We need to prove that the operator
\Phi
is
well-defined on
B
and is a contraction map under the metric
d

for
I
.

Throughout the proof, all spacetime norms will be on
I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
.
To see that
\Phi
maps the ball
B
to itself, we use the
Strichartz inequality in Theorem
4.2
and Sobolev embedding theorem and equivalent norms (
1.26
) to arrive at

\displaystyle\|\Phi(u)\|_{X(I)}\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+C\big\||u|^{p-1}u\big\|_{L_{t}^{q^{\prime}_{0}}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,L^{r_{0}})}^{p-1}\|u\|_{L^{q_{0}}(I,L^{r_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\||\nabla|u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,L^{2})}^{p-1}\|u\|_{L^{q_{0}}(I,L^{r_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,L^{2})}^{p-1}\|u\|_{L^{q_{0}}(I,L^{r_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\|_{X(I)}^{p-1}\|u\|_{X(I)}.

Noting that
\|u\|_{X(I)},~\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\|_{X(I)}\leq 2C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}}
, we see that for
u\in B
,

\|\Phi(u)\|_{X(I)}\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\big(2C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}}\big)^{p}.

Since
1<p<3
, then

1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}=1-\frac{2(p-1)}{p+1}>0.

(4.7)

Therefore, we take
T
sufficiently small such that

T^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\big(2C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}}\big)^{p}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}},

we have
\|\Phi(u)\|_{X(I)}\leq 2C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}}
for

u\in B
. Similarly, by Strichartz estimate (
4.4
), chain rules (
1.27
), equivalent norms (
1.26
) and inequality (
4.7
), we get

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla_{\bf A}\Phi(u)\big\|_{X(I)}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla_{\bf A}u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+C\big\|\nabla_{\bf A}(|u|^{p-1}u)\big\|_{L^{q^{\prime}_{0}}(I,L^{r^{\prime}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla_{\bf A}u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,L^{r_{0}})}^{p-1}\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\|_{L^{q_{0}}(I,L^{r_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{H_{\bf A}^{1}}}+CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\||\nabla|u\|_{L^{\infty}(I,L^{2})}^{p-1}\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\|_{L^{q_{0}}(I,L^{r_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\|_{X(I)}^{p}.

This shows that
\Phi
maps
the ball
B
to itself.

Finally, to prove that
\Phi
is a
contraction, for
u,v\in B
, we argue as above

\displaystyle d\big(\Phi(u),\Phi(v)\big)\leq

\displaystyle C\big\||u|^{p-1}u-|v|^{p-1}v\big\|_{L_{t}^{q^{\prime}_{0}}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\|u-v\|_{L_{t}^{q_{0}}L_{x}^{r_{0}}}\big(\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{p-1}+\|\nabla_{\bf A}v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{p-1}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2CT^{1-\frac{2}{q_{0}}}\big(2C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}}\big)^{p-1}d(u,v)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}d(u,v)

by taking
T
sufficiently small such that

2CT^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(2C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}}\big)^{p-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

The standard fixed point argument gives a unique solution
u
of
(
1.17
) on
I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}
. By Strichartz estimate, we also get
the bound

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}+\|\nabla_{\bf A}u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}\leq 2C\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}(\mathbb{R}^{4})},\quad\forall~(q,r)\in\Lambda_{0}^{S},\;r<4

∎

On the other hand, conservation laws for equation (
1.17
) hold.

Lemma 4.5

(Conservation laws)
.

If the solution
u
for equation (
1.17
) has sufficient decay at infinity and smoothness, it conserves the mass

M(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=M(u_{0})

(4.8)

and energy

E(u(t))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|\nabla_{\bf A}u(t)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u(t)|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x=E(u_{0}).

(4.9)

Proof.

From equation (
1.17
) and using the integration by parts, we see

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle M(u(t))=2\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}u_{t}\bar{u}\mathrm{d}x=2\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}i(\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}u-|u|^{p-1}u)\bar{u}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}(\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}u-|u|^{p-1}u)\bar{u}\mathrm{d}x=2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|\nabla_{\bf A}u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+2\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u|^{p+1}\mathrm{d}x=0,

which implies (
4.8
).

A similar computation yields

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(u(t))=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\nabla_{\bf A}u_{t}\overline{\nabla_{\bf A}u}+|u|^{p-1}\bar{u}u_{t}\mathrm{d}x=0,

which implies (
4.9
).
∎

Using the mass and energy conservation laws, we obtain

\|u(t)\|_{H_{\bf A}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}^{2}\leq M(u_{0})+2E(u_{0}).

(4.10)

Therefore, the global well-posedness follows from the local well-posedness theory and mass and energy conservation.

4.2.
Morawetz estimate

In this subsection, we derive the classical Morawetz estimate from the virial identity in Theorem
4.7
.

Proposition 4.6

(Morawetz estimate)
.

Assume that
u:\;I\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
solves the equation (
1.17
), then there holds

\int_{I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{p+1}}{|x|}\;dx\;dt\leq C\big(M(u_{0}),E(u_{0})\big).

(4.11)

Proposition
4.6
follows from Theorem
4.7
with
a(x)=|x|
below.

Theorem 4.7

(Virial identity)
.

Let
a:\mathbb{R}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a radial, real-valued multiplier,
a(x)=a(|x|)
and let

\Phi_{a}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}a(x)|u|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Then for any solution
u
of magnetic Schrödinger equation (
1.17
) and initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{2}
,
\nabla_{\bf A}u_{0}\in L^{2}
, the following virial-type identity holds:

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\Phi_{a}(t)=4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\nabla_{\bf A}uD^{2}a\overline{\nabla_{\bf A}u}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u|^{2}\Delta^{2}a\mathrm{d}x+\frac{2(p-1)}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u|^{p+1}\Delta a\mathrm{d}x,

(4.12)

where

(D^{2}a)_{j,k}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}a,\quad\Delta^{2}a=\Delta(\Delta a),

for
j,k=1,\cdots,4
are respectively the Hessian matrix and the bi-Laplaican of
a
.

Proof.

From equation (
1.17
), we see that

u_{t}=i(\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}+|u|^{p-1})u.

Then a simple calculation yields

\frac{\;d\Phi_{a}(t)}{\;dt}=i\langle u,[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},a]u\rangle,

and

\frac{\;d^{2}\Phi_{a}(t)}{\;dt^{2}}=-\langle u,[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},a]]u\rangle-(\langle u,[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},a]|u|^{p-1}u\rangle+\langle|u|^{p-1}u,[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},a]u\rangle).

(4.13)

Consider the first term in (
4.13
), using the virial identity in
[
14
, Theorem1.2]
, we arrive at

-\langle u,[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},a]]u\rangle=4\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\nabla_{\bf A}uD^{2}a\overline{\nabla_{\bf A}u}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u|^{2}\Delta^{2}a\mathrm{d}x.

(4.14)

Recalling that the commutator

[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},a]=2\nabla a\cdot\nabla_{\bf A}+\Delta a

(see
[
14
, (2.7)]
), we obtain for the reminder term by using integration by parts

\displaystyle-\langle u,[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},a]|u|^{p-1}u\rangle+\langle|u|^{p-1}u,[\mathcal{L}_{\bf A},a]u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\langle u,\nabla a\cdot\nabla_{\bf A}(|u|^{p-1}u)\rangle+2\langle|u|^{p-1}u,\nabla a\cdot\nabla_{\bf A}u\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(p-1)}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u|^{p+1}\Delta a\mathrm{d}x.

(4.15)

Collecting (
4.13
)-(
4.2
) together, we finally conclude (
4.12
).
∎

4.3.
Scattering Theory

In this subsection, we aim to establish a scattering theory by following the idea in
[
17
]
.

4.3.1.
Decay of potential energy

We will utilize the classical Morawetz estimate in Proposition
4.6
and dispersive estimates (
4.1
) and Strichartz estimates (
4.3
)-(
4.5
) that we obtained in the last two subsections to show the decay of potential energy.

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
u:\;\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
be the global solution to (
1.17
). Then, there holds

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=0.

(4.16)

By interpolating with mass and energy, we obtain

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=0,\quad\forall\;2<r<p+1.

(4.17)

Proof.

We adapt Ginibre-Velo’s method in
[
17
]
to show this proposition. For completeness, we give the detailed proof. We only need to show the positive time direction.

We have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\;dx=\int_{|x|\leq t\log t}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\;dx+\int_{|x|>t\log t}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\;dx.

(4.18)

Step 1:
In this step, we will show

\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{|x|>t\log t}|u(t,x)|^{p+1}\;dx=0.

(4.19)

To do this, for given
M>0
, we define the smoothing function

\theta_{M}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{|x|}{M}\quad\text{if}\quad|x|\leq M\\
1\quad\text{if}\quad|x|\leq 2M.\end{cases}

(4.20)

Then, it is easy to check that

\theta_{M}\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{4}),\quad\|\nabla\theta_{M}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\frac{1}{M},\quad\theta_{M}u\in C(\mathbb{R},H^{1}_{\bf A}(\mathbb{R}^{4})).

By (
1.17
) and a simple computation, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\theta_{M}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\;dx=

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\theta_{M}u_{t}\bar{u}\;dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\theta_{M}\bar{u}\big[i\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}u+i|u|^{p-1}u\big]\;dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\rm Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\theta_{M}\bar{u}\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}u\;dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}(-i\nabla+{\bf A})\theta_{M}\cdot(i\nabla+{\bf A})u\bar{u}\;dx.

Hence

\Big|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\theta_{M}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\;dx\Big|\lesssim\big\|(-i\nabla+{\bf A})\theta_{M}\big\|_{\infty}\cdot\|u\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\cdot\big\|(i\nabla+{\bf A})u\big\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq\frac{C}{M}.

Integrating in time, we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\theta_{M}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}\;dx\leq\frac{C}{M}t+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\theta_{M}(x)|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\;dx,\quad\forall\;t>0.

(4.21)

Taking
M=t\log t
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|x|>t\log t}|u(t,x)|^{2}\;dx\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log t}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\theta_{M}(x)|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log t}+\int_{|x|\leq t\log t}\frac{|x|}{t\log t}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\;dx+\int_{|x|>t\log t}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\log t}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{t\log t}}\int_{|x|\leq\sqrt{t\log t}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\;dx+\int_{|x|>\sqrt{t\log t}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}\;dx

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle 0\qquad\text{as}\quad t\to\infty.

Since
3<p+1<4
, so (
4.19
) follows by interpolating the above inequality with
\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{4}}\lesssim\|u(t,x)\|_{H_{\bf A}^{1}}\lesssim 1
.

Step 2:
By Morawetz estimate (
4.11
), we derive that for any
\epsilon>0,\;t>1
and
\tau>0
, there exists
t_{0}>\max\{t,2\tau\}
such that

\int_{t_{0}-2\tau}^{t_{0}}\int_{|x|\leq s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\;ds<\epsilon.

(4.22)

Actually, by Morawetz estimate (
4.11
), we see that

\displaystyle\infty>

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{4}}\frac{|u(s,x)|^{p+1}}{|x|}\;dx\;ds>\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{s\log s}\int_{|x|\leq s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\;ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{t+2k\tau}^{t+2(k+1)\tau}\frac{1}{s\log s}\int_{|x|\leq s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\;ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(t+2(k+1)\tau)\log(t+2(k+1)\tau)}\int_{t+2k\tau}^{t+2(k+1)\tau}\int_{|x|\leq s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\;ds.

We note that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(t+2(k+1)\tau)\log(t+2(k+1)\tau)}=+\infty,

then there exists
k>0
such that

\int_{t+2k\tau}^{t+2(k+1)\tau}\int_{|x|\leq s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\;ds\leq\epsilon,

therefore, (
4.22
) holds by taking
t_{0}=t+2(k+1)\tau
.

Step 3:
We claim that for any
\epsilon>0
and
b>0
, there exists
t_{0}>b
such that

\sup_{s\in[t_{0}-b,t_{0}]}\|u(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}<\epsilon.

(4.23)

Taking
t>\tau>0
, we have by Duhamel’s formula

\displaystyle u(t)=

\displaystyle e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t-\tau}e^{i(t-s)\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}(|u|^{p-1}u)(s)\;ds-i\int_{t-\tau}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}(|u|^{p-1}u)(s)\;ds

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle v(t)+w(t,\tau)+z(t,\tau).

First, by density
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{4})\hookrightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{4})
and the dispersive estimate, we obtain

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|v(t)\|_{L^{p+1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{4})}=0.

(4.24)

Estimate of
w(t,\tau)
:
Using dispersive estimate and
\|u\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim 1
, we get

\|w(t,\tau)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t-\tau}(t-s)^{-2}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{p+1}\;ds\lesssim\tau^{-1}.

(4.25)

On the other hand, we have

w(t,\tau)=e^{i\tau\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}u(t-\tau)-e^{it\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}u_{0}.

This implies

\|w(t,\tau)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim 1.

Interpolating this with (
4.25
) yields that

\|w(t,\tau)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}\leq K\tau^{-\frac{p-1}{p+1}}

(4.26)

for some
K>0
.

Estimate of
z(t,\tau)
:

Using Minkowski’s inequality, the dispersive estimate and Hölder’s inequality and the boundedness of potential energy, one has

\displaystyle\|z(t,\tau)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{t-\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{p+1}}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{p}\;ds

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{t-\tau}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{3p-1}{2(p+1)}}\;ds\Big)^{\frac{4(p-1)}{3p-1}}\Big(\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{\frac{3p-1}{3-p}p}\;ds\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\tau^{\frac{2(p-1)(3-p)}{(p+1)(3p-1)}}\Big(\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{p+1}\;ds\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{p+1}}^{\frac{4p^{2}-3p-3}{3(p-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\tau^{\frac{2(p-1)(3-p)}{(p+1)(3p-1)}+\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}\Big(\sup_{s>t-\tau}\int_{|x|>s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}

\displaystyle+L\tau^{\frac{2(p-1)(3-p)}{(p+1)(3p-1)}}\Big(\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{|x|\leq s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\;ds\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}},

for some
L>0
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}\leq

\displaystyle\|v(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}+K\tau^{-\frac{p-1}{p+1}}

\displaystyle+L\tau^{\frac{2(p-1)(3-p)}{(p+1)(3p-1)}+\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}\Big(\sup_{s>t-\tau}\int_{|x|>s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}

\displaystyle+L\tau^{\frac{2(p-1)(3-p)}{(p+1)(3p-1)}}\Big(\int_{t-\tau}^{t}\int_{|x|\leq s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\;ds\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}.

(4.27)

For any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\tau_{\epsilon}>b
such that for any
\tau>\tau_{\epsilon}
s.t.

K\tau^{-\frac{p-1}{p+1}}\leq K\tau_{\epsilon}^{-\frac{p-1}{p+1}}=\frac{\epsilon}{4}.

(4.28)

On the other hand, by Step 1 and (
4.24
), we deduce that there exists
t_{1}>\tau_{\epsilon}
such that for any
t\geq t_{1}

\|v(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}+L\tau_{\epsilon}^{\frac{2(p-1)(3-p)}{(p+1)(3p-1)}+\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}\Big(\sup_{s>t-\tau_{\epsilon}}\int_{|x|>s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}<\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

(4.29)

Furthermore, by Step 2, we derive that there exists
t_{0}>\max\{t_{1}+b,2\tau_{\epsilon}\}
such that

L\tau_{\epsilon}^{\frac{2(p-1)(3-p)}{(p+1)(3p-1)}}\Big(\int_{t_{0}-2\tau_{\epsilon}}^{t_{0}}\int_{|x|\leq s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\;ds\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}<\frac{\epsilon}{4}.

(4.30)

Noting that for
t\in[t_{0}-b,t_{0}]
and
\tau_{\epsilon}>b,
we have

[t-\tau_{\epsilon},t]\subset[t-b,t]\subset[t_{0}-2\tau_{\epsilon},t_{0}].

Thus,

L\tau_{\epsilon}^{\frac{2(p-1)(3-p)}{(p+1)(3p-1)}}\Big(\int_{t-\tau_{\epsilon}}^{t}\int_{|x|\leq s\log s}|u(s,x)|^{p+1}\;dx\;ds\Big)^{\frac{3-p}{3(p-1)}}<\frac{\epsilon}{4}.

(4.31)

Plugging (
4.28
), (
4.29
) and (
4.31
) into (
4.27
), we obtain the claim (
4.23
).

Step 4:
Now we turn to show (
4.16
). It is equivalent to show that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
T_{\epsilon}>0
such that for any
t>T_{\epsilon}

\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}<\epsilon.

(4.32)

From Step 3, we know that for any
t>\tau>0

\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}\leq\|v(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}+K\tau^{-\frac{p-1}{p+1}}+\|z(t,\tau)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}.

(4.33)

For any
\epsilon>0
, we take
\tau_{\epsilon}>0
such that

K\tau_{\epsilon}^{-\frac{p-1}{p+1}}=\frac{\epsilon}{4}.

(4.34)

On the other hand, by (
4.24
), we can choose
t_{1}>\tau_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\|v(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}<\frac{\epsilon}{4},\quad\forall\;t>t_{1}.

(4.35)

Thus,

\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\|z(t,\tau_{\epsilon})\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}},\quad\forall\;t>t_{1}.

(4.36)

Using the dispersive estimate, we get

\displaystyle\|z(t,\tau_{\epsilon})\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{t-\tau_{\epsilon}}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{2(p-1)}{p+1}}\|u(s)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{p}\;ds

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M\tau_{\epsilon}^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}\sup_{s\in[t-\tau_{\epsilon},t]}\|u(s)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{p}

(4.37)

for some
M>0
.
Hence,

\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}+M\tau_{\epsilon}^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}\sup_{s\in[t-\tau_{\epsilon},t]}\|u(s)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}^{p},\quad\forall\;t>t_{1}.

(4.38)

On the other hand, applying (
4.23
) with
b=\tau_{\epsilon}
, we derive that there exists
t_{0}\geq\tau_{\epsilon}
such that

\sup_{t\in[t_{0}-\tau_{\epsilon},t_{0}]}\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}<\epsilon.

(4.39)

Now, we utilize the bootstrap argument to prove (
4.32
). To do this, we define

t_{\epsilon}:=\sup\{t\geq t_{0}:\;\|u(s)\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}<\epsilon,\quad\forall\;s\in[t_{0}-\tau_{\epsilon},t]\}.

(4.40)

Then, it is equivalent to show that
t_{\epsilon}=+\infty.
By contradiction, we assume that
t_{\epsilon}<+\infty
. Then,

\|u(t_{\epsilon})\|_{L_{x}^{p+1}}=\epsilon.

(4.41)

Using (
4.36
) with
t=t_{\epsilon}
, we obtain

\epsilon\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}+M\tau_{\epsilon}^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}\epsilon^{p}\Longrightarrow\;\tau_{\epsilon}^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}\epsilon^{p-1}\geq\frac{1}{2M}.

This together with (
4.34
) implies that

\tau_{\epsilon}^{\frac{3-p}{p+1}}(4K\tau_{\epsilon}^{-\frac{p-1}{p+1}})^{p-1}\geq\frac{1}{2M}\Longrightarrow\;\tau_{\epsilon}^{\frac{p^{2}-p-2}{p+1}}\leq 2M(4K)^{p-1}

which contradicts with the fact that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\tau_{\epsilon}=+\infty.

Therefore, we conclude the proof of Proposition
4.8
.

∎

4.4.
Global space-time bound

In this subsection, we utilize the decay of potential energy in Proposition
4.8
and the continuous argument to show the global space-time bound for the global solution.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
u:\;\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{C}
be the global solution to (
1.17
). Then, there holds

\|u(t,x)\|_{L_{t}^{q}H^{1,r}_{\bf A}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{4})}<+\infty,

(4.42)

for any
(q,r)\in\Lambda_{0}^{S}
. As a consequence, the solution
u(t,x)
scatters.

Proof.

We first utilize Proposition
4.8
and continuous argument to prove

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}([0,\infty),H_{\bf A}^{1,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}<+\infty,\;(\gamma,\rho)=\big(\tfrac{p+1}{p-1},p+1\big).

(4.43)

Using local well-posed theory, we get for any
T>0
,

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}([0,T],H_{\bf A}^{1,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\leq C\big(T,M(u_{0}),E(u_{0})\big).

Thus, we only need to show for some
T>0

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}([T,\infty),H_{\bf A}^{1,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}<+\infty.

(4.44)

For
t>T>0
, we have by Duhamel’s formula

u(t)=e^{i(t-T)\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}u(T)-i\int_{T}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\mathcal{L}_{\bf A}}(|u|^{p-1}u)(s)\;ds.

By Strichartz’s estimate and Sobolev embedding, we get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma}([T,\infty),H_{\bf A}^{1,\rho}(\mathbb{R}^{4}))}\leq

\displaystyle C\|u\|_{H^{1}_{\bf A}}+C\big\||u|^{p-1}u\big\|_{L_{t}^{\gamma^{\prime}}([T,\infty),H_{\bf A}^{1,\rho^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\leq

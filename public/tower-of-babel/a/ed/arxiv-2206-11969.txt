Periodic fractional Ambrosetti-Prodi for one-dimensional problem with drift
B. Barrios, L. Carrero, A. Quaas

Abstract
We establish Ambrosetti–Prodi type results for periodic solutions of one-dimensional nonlinear problems with drift term and drift-less whose principal operator is the fractional Laplacian of order s ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) s\in(0,1) . We establish conditions for the existence and nonexistence of solutions. The proofs of the existence results are based on the sub-supersolution method combined with topological degree type arguments. We also establish a priori bounds in order to get multiplicity results. We also prove that the solutions are C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} under some regularity assumptions in the nonlinearities, that is, the solutions of equations are classical. We finish the work obtaining existence results for problems with the fractional Laplacian with singular nonlinearity. In particular, we establish an Ambrosetti-Prodi type problem with singular nonlinearities.

\blacksquare

3.1
The Fractional Laplacian problem with attractive-repulsive singular nonlinearities

In this subsection we provide some extension of the bounds found in the previous subsection, and we consider an equation with two singularities of the form,

(\Delta)^{s}u(x)+cu^{\prime}(x)+e(x)-\frac{\gamma(x)}{u^{\mu}(x)}+\frac{\beta(x)}{u^{\rho}(x)}=0,\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},

(3.9)

where
s\in(0,1)
,
c>0
,
\gamma
,
\beta
and
e
are Lipschitz continuous and
2\pi
-periodic,
\mu
and
\rho
are positive constants with
\mu\geq\rho
and
\mu\geq 1
. We take advantage to prove the existence of positive solutions of
(\ref{12})
using degree theory, inspired by some recent works when
s=1
, (for example
[
21
]
). Notice that
\gamma
and
\beta
can change signs which makes the proofs more interesting.

From now on, we will use denotation

\omega_{+}(x)=\max\{\omega(x),0\},\qquad\omega_{-}(x)=-\min\{\omega(x),0\}.

Clearly,

\omega(x)=\omega_{+}(x)-\omega_{-}(x)\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\qquad\overline{\omega}=\overline{\omega_{+}}-\overline{\omega_{-}}.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\bar{\gamma}>0
,
\bar{e}>0
and that following condition holds

\lim\limits_{x\to+0}\frac{cx}{2}-\frac{2\pi\overline{\gamma_{-}}}{x^{\mu}}-\frac{2\pi\overline{\beta_{+}}}{x^{\rho}}=+\infty,

(3.10)

where
c>0
is the that appears in (
\ref{12})
. Then, equation (
3.9
) has at least one
2\pi
-periodic positive classical solution.

To prove this Theorem we will use degree theory with some similar ideas to those of
[
21
]
adapted to the nonlocal case. Consider now the family of equations

(\Delta)^{s}u(x)+cu^{\prime}(x)+\lambda e(x)-\lambda\frac{\gamma(x)}{u^{\mu}(x)}+\lambda\frac{\beta(x)}{u^{\rho}(x)}=0\quad\lambda\in(0,1].

(3.11)

We need to find a priori estimates, for this, we will use the following Lemma that proves upper and lowers bound at some point.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume
\bar{\gamma}>0
and
\bar{e}>0
, then for each possible
2\pi
-periodic solution
u
of (
3.11
) there exist
x_{0},x_{1}\in[0,2\pi]
such that

u(x_{0})\leq\max\left\{1,\left(\dfrac{\overline{\gamma_{+}}+\overline{\beta_{-}}}{\bar{e}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}\right\}=A_{0}

(3.12)

and

u(x_{1})\geq A_{1},

(3.13)

where

A_{1}=\begin{cases}\min\left\{\left(\dfrac{\overline{\gamma}}{2\overline{e_{+}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu}},\left(\dfrac{\overline{\gamma}}{2\overline{\beta_{+}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu-\rho}}\right\},&\text{si $\overline{\beta_{+}}>0$},\\
\left(\dfrac{\bar{\gamma}}{\overline{e_{+}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\mu}},&\text{si $\overline{\beta_{+}}=0$}.\end{cases}

Proof:

Let
u
is a
2\pi
-periodic solution of (
3.11
). Then integrating the equation (
3.11
) in
[0,2\pi]
, we obtain

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(\Delta)^{s}u(x)+c\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}u^{\prime}(x)+\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e(x)-\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\gamma(x)}{u^{\mu}(x)}+\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\beta(x)}{u^{\rho}(x)}=0

By
[
9
, Lemma 2.2]
, together with the
2\pi
-periodicity of
u
, we get

\displaystyle 0<2\pi\bar{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\gamma(x)}{u^{\mu}(x)}-\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\beta(x)}{u^{\rho}(x)}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\gamma_{+}(x)}{u^{\mu}(x)}+\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\beta_{-}(x)}{u^{\rho}(x)}.

From the above, we can conclude that there is a point
x_{0}\in[0,2\pi]
such that
(\ref{A0})
is true. Indeed, if (
3.12
) does not hold, then

u(x)>1\quad\text{for all}\;x\in[0,2\pi],

(3.15)

and

u(x)>\left(\dfrac{\overline{\gamma_{+}}+\overline{\beta_{-}}}{\bar{e}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\rho}}\quad\text{for all}\;x\in[0,2\pi].

(3.16)

Since
\mu\geq\rho
and by (
3.15
), using the Mean Value Theorem of integrals in (
3.14
), we have that there exists a point
x_{0}\in[0,2\pi]
such that

2\pi\bar{e}\leq\frac{2\pi(\overline{\gamma_{+}}+\overline{\beta_{-}})}{u^{\rho}(x_{0})}

which implies that

u(x_{0})\leq\left(\dfrac{\overline{\gamma_{+}}+\overline{\beta_{-}}}{\bar{e}}\right)^{\frac{1}{\rho}},

this contradicts (
3.16
), so (
3.12
) holds.

Now, we shall proof that (
3.13
) is true. Indeed, multiplying (
3.11
) for
u^{\mu}
and integrating the equation (
3.11
) in
[0,2\pi]
we get

\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(\Delta)^{s}u(x)u^{\mu}(x)dx+c\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}u^{\prime}(x)u^{\mu}(x)dx+\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e(x)u^{\mu}(x)dx-\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\gamma(x)dx+\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(x)u^{\mu-\rho}(x)dx=0,

that is,

\lambda 2\pi\bar{\gamma}+\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)u^{\mu}(x)dx-c\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}u^{\prime}(x)u^{\mu}(x)dx=\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(x)u^{\mu-\rho}(x)dx+\lambda\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e(x)u^{\mu}(x)dx.

(3.17)

Since,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)u^{\mu}(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)u(x)u^{\mu-1}(x)dx

(3.18)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle u_{min}^{\mu-1}[u]_{H^{s}(0,2\pi)}^{2}\geq 0.

Using the fact that

-c\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}u^{\prime}(x)u^{\mu}(x)=0,

we have

\displaystyle 2\pi\bar{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\beta(x)u^{\mu-\rho}(x)+\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e(t)u^{\mu}(x)

(3.19)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}\beta_{+}(x)u^{\mu-\rho}(x)+\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}e_{+}(x)u^{\mu}(x)

Thus, reasoning as before we can conclude that there is a point
x_{1}\in[0,2\pi]
such that

u(x_{1})\geq A_{1}.

\blacksquare

Introduction

The logarithmic integral, denoted by
\operatorname{li}(x)
, is a classical special function in mathematics with applications in physics and number theory. In particular, it plays a central role as an approximation to the prime-counting function
\pi(x)
, which counts the number of primes less than or equal to a given real number
x

Narkiewicz (2000)
. The logarithmic integral is defined for all positive real numbers except
x=1
, where the integrand exhibits a singularity
1
1

1

\ln(x)
denotes the natural logarithm.
, by

\operatorname{li}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}\frac{dt}{\ln(t)}.

(1.1)

As
x\to\infty
, the function
\operatorname{li}(x)
admits a classical asymptotic expansion in inverse powers of
\ln(x)
, which is divergent and therefore must be truncated after a finite number of terms in practical computations. Repeated integration by parts yields the
n
-term truncated expansion
Masina (2019)

\operatorname{li}(x)=\operatorname{li}_{n}(x)+\int_{0}^{x}\frac{n!}{\ln^{\,n+1}(t)}\,dt,\qquad\operatorname{li}_{n}(x)=\frac{x}{\ln(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{k!}{\ln^{k}(x)}.

(1.2)

If
\operatorname{li}_{n}(x)
is used as an approximation of
\operatorname{li}(x)
, the resulting absolute error is given by

\left|\varepsilon_{n}(x)\right|=\left|\operatorname{li}_{n}(x)-\operatorname{li}(x)\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{x}\frac{n!}{\ln^{\,n+1}(t)}\,dt\right|.

(1.3)

Stieltjes
Stieltjes (1886)
observed that the truncation index minimizing
|\varepsilon_{n}(x)|
is asymptotically given by

n^{*}=\lfloor\ln(x)\rfloor.

Building on this observation, van Boven
et al.

van Boven et al. (2012)
introduced a refined approximation incorporating a fractional remainder term and defined, for all
x\geq e
, the
Stieltjes approximation
of
\operatorname{li}(x)
as

\operatorname{li}_{*}(x)=\frac{(\ln(x)-n^{*})\,x\,n^{*}!}{\ln^{\,n^{*}+1}(x)}+\frac{x}{\ln(x)}\sum_{k=0}^{n^{*}-1}\frac{k!}{\ln^{\,k}(x)},\qquad n^{*}=\lfloor\ln(x)\rfloor.

(1.4)

We denote by

\varepsilon(x)=\operatorname{li}_{*}(x)-\operatorname{li}(x)

the approximation error associated with the Stieltjes formula. Regarding its magnitude, van Boven
et al.
remark that Stieltjes suggested an “order of approximation” proportional to
\sqrt{2\pi/\ln(x)}
, while also noting that this estimate may significantly overstate the true error and that the notion of approximation order was not rigorously defined at the time.

Motivated by these observations, we formulate the following conjecture, which refines and makes precise Stieltjes’ heuristic estimate.

Conjecture 1

(Asymptotic behaviour of the Stieltjes approximation error)
.

For all real numbers
x\geq e
,

\varepsilon(x)=\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{\ln(x)}}+o\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln(x)}}\right),\qquad x\to\infty.

Equivalently, for the discrete sequence
\varepsilon_{k}=\varepsilon(e^{k})
,

\varepsilon_{k}=\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{k}}+o\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\right),\qquad k\to\infty.

Rather than attempting a proof of Conjecture
1
, the present paper develops unconditional upper and lower bounds, analytic estimates, and extensive numerical evidence

4
Stieltjes Partial Approximation Error (
\Delta(x)
)

The Stieltjes partial approximation error is defined as

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\Delta(x)=\int_{e^{n}}^{x}\frac{n!}{\ln^{\,n+1}(t)}\,dt\;-\;\frac{(\ln(x)-n)\,x\,n!}{\ln^{\,n+1}(x)},\\[4.30554pt]
\text{with }x>e,\quad n=\lceil\ln(x)\rceil-1,\quad\alpha=\ln(x)-n.\end{array}

(4.1)

Differentiating the second term yields

\displaystyle d\!\left(\frac{(\ln(x)-n)\,x\,n!}{\ln^{n+1}(x)}\right)=\frac{n!}{\ln^{n+1}(x)}\left(1+(\ln(x)-n)-\frac{(n+1)(\ln(x)-n)}{\ln(x)}\right)\,dx.

Incorporating this expression into the integral representation of
\Delta(x)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta(x)=\int_{e^{n}}^{x}-\frac{n!}{\ln^{n+1}(t)}\left((\ln(t)-n)-\frac{(n+1)(\ln(t)-n)}{\ln(t)}\right)\,dt.

Simplifying and reorganizing terms leads to

\displaystyle\Delta(x)=\int_{e^{n}}^{x}\frac{n!}{\ln^{n+2}(t)}(\ln(t)-n)(\ln(t)-n-1)\,dt.

Introducing the change of variables
u=\ln(t)-n
gives the compact form

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\Delta(x)=\int_{0}^{\ln(x)-n}f_{n}(u)\,du,\\[4.30554pt]
\displaystyle f_{n}(u)=\frac{u(1-u)\,e^{n+u}\,n!}{(n+u)^{\,n+2}},\quad n=\lceil\ln(x)\rceil-1.\end{array}

(4.2)

Since
f_{n}(u)>0
for all
u\in(0,1)
and
f_{n}(0)=f_{n}(1)=0
, the integral is strictly positive for every
e^{n}<x\leq e^{n+1}
. Moreover,
f_{n}(u)
is increasing on
(0,1)
, which implies the monotonicity of
\Delta(x)
. Consequently,

0<\Delta(x)<\Delta(y)\leq\Delta_{n+1},\quad\text{for }e^{n}<x<y\leq e^{n+1},\;n\geq 1,

(4.3)

where
\Delta_{k}=\Delta(e^{k})
.

Proofs of the Main Results

6.1
Structural decomposition of
\varepsilon(x)

Proposition 2
.

For all
x>e
,

\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon_{s}-\sum_{k=s+1}^{n}\Delta_{k}-\Delta(x),\quad\quad n:=\lceil\ln(x)\rceil-1\geq s\geq 1,\\

Proof.

Let
n=\lceil\ln(x)\rceil-1
. By definition of the logarithmic integral (Equation
1.1
) and elementary properties of integrals,

\operatorname{li}(x)=\operatorname{li}(e^{n})+\int_{e^{n}}^{x}\frac{1}{\log t}\,dt.

Integrating by parts
n
times yields

\operatorname{li}(x)=\operatorname{li}(e^{n})+\frac{x}{\log x}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{k!}{\log^{k}x}-\frac{e^{n}}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{k!}{n^{k}}+\int_{e^{n}}^{x}\frac{n!}{\log^{n+1}t}\,dt.

Adding and subtracting the quantity
\displaystyle\frac{(\log x-n)xn!}{\log^{n+1}x}
, and completing
\operatorname{li}_{*}(x)
, we obtain

\operatorname{li}(x)=\operatorname{li}(e^{n})+\operatorname{li}_{*}(x)-\operatorname{li}_{*}(e^{n})-\frac{(\log x-n)xn!}{\log^{n+1}x}+\int_{e^{n}}^{x}\frac{n!}{\log^{n+1}t}\,dt.

Rearranging terms gives

\operatorname{li}_{*}(x)-\operatorname{li}(x)=\operatorname{li}_{*}(e^{n})-\operatorname{li}(e^{n})-\int_{e^{n}}^{x}\frac{n!}{\log^{n+1}t}\,dt+\frac{(\log x-n)xn!}{\log^{n+1}x}.

By definition of
\varepsilon(x)
,
\varepsilon_{k}=\varepsilon(e^{k})
, and
\Delta(x)
(Equation
4.1
), this identity can be written as

\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon_{n}-\left(\int_{e^{n}}^{x}\frac{n!}{\log^{n+1}t}\,dt-\frac{(\log x-n)xn!}{\log^{n+1}x}\right)=\varepsilon_{n}-\Delta(x).

Applying recursively the relation
\varepsilon_{k}=\varepsilon_{k-1}-\Delta_{k}
, we finally obtain

\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon_{s}-\sum_{k=s+1}^{n}\Delta_{k}-\Delta(x),\quad\quad n:=\lceil\ln(x)\rceil-1\geq s\geq 1.

∎

\varepsilon(x)
is strictly decreasing

Proposition 3
.

\varepsilon(x)
is strictly decreasing on
[e,\infty)
, i.e.,

6.10
Riemann–sum estimates for unimodal functions

We establish upper and lower Riemann–sum bounds for integrals of functions admitting a unique maximizer. These bounds are repeatedly used in the numerical and asymptotic estimates for
\Delta_{k}

Lemma 12

(Riemann–sum bounds for unimodal functions)
.

Let
f:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
be differentiable on
(a,b)
and assume that there exists a unique maximizer
x^{*}\in(a,b)
such that

f^{\prime}(x)>0\quad\text{for }x<x^{*},\qquad f^{\prime}(x)<0\quad\text{for }x>x^{*}.

Let
\Delta=\frac{b-a}{n}
and define a uniform partition
x_{i}=a+i\Delta
,
i=0,\dots,n
. Then

\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f(x_{i})-f(x^{*})\right)\Delta\;\leq\;\int_{a}^{b}f(x)\,dx\;\leq\;\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}f(x_{i})+f(x^{*})\right)\Delta.

(6.14)

Proof.

Since
f
is strictly increasing on
[a,x^{*}]
and strictly decreasing on
[x^{*},b]
, there exists an index
k\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}
such that

x_{k}\leq x^{*}<x_{k+1}.

Step 1: Interval

[a,x^{*}]
. For each subinterval
[x_{i},x_{i+1}]\subset[a,x^{*}]
, monotonicity implies

f(x_{i})\Delta\leq\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}f(x)\,dx\leq f(x_{i+1})\Delta.

Summing over
i=0,\dots,k-1
yields

\Delta\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}f(x_{i})\;\leq\;\int_{a}^{x_{k}}f(x)\,dx\;\leq\;\Delta\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(x_{i}).

(6.15)

Step 2: Interval

[x^{*},b]
. For each subinterval
[x_{i},x_{i+1}]\subset[x^{*},b]
, monotonicity implies

f(x_{i+1})\Delta\leq\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}f(x)\,dx\leq f(x_{i})\Delta.

Summing over
i=k+1,\dots,n-1
yields

\Delta\sum_{i=k+2}^{n}f(x_{i})\;\leq\;\int_{x_{k+1}}^{b}f(x)\,dx\;\leq\;\Delta\sum_{i=k+1}^{n-1}f(x_{i}).

(6.16)

Step 3: Central interval

Since
f(x^{*})
is the unique maximum,

\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}f(x)\,dx\leq f(x^{*})\Delta.

Moreover, by monotonicity,

(f(x_{k})+f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{*}))\Delta\;\leq\;\int_{x_{k}}^{x_{k+1}}f(x)\,dx.

Step 4: Combination

Adding inequalities (
6.15
), (
6.16
), and the bounds on

Appendix A
Relation between
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor}}
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}
, and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor+1}}

Lemma 14
.

For all
x\geq e
, the following asymptotic relations hold:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}+o\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}\right),\qquad\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}+o\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}\right),

as
x\to\infty
.

Proof.

Let
x\geq e
and write

\ln x=\lfloor\ln x\rfloor+\theta(x),\qquad 0\leq\theta(x)<1.

We first estimate the difference

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}=\frac{\sqrt{\ln x}-\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor}}{\sqrt{\ln x}\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor}}.

Using the identity

\sqrt{a}-\sqrt{b}=\frac{a-b}{\sqrt{a}+\sqrt{b}},

we obtain

\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}=\frac{\theta(x)}{\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor}\sqrt{\ln x}\left(\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor}+\sqrt{\ln x}\right)}.

Since
0\leq\theta(x)<1
and
\lfloor\ln x\rfloor\sim\ln x
as
x\to\infty
, it follows that

\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{2(\ln x)^{3/2}}=o\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}\right).

This proves the first asymptotic relation.

For the second relation, note that

\lfloor\ln x\rfloor+1=\ln x+(1-\theta(x)),\qquad 0<1-\theta(x)\leq 1,

and an identical argument yields

\left|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lfloor\ln x\rfloor+1}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}\right|\leq\frac{C}{(\ln x)^{3/2}}=o\!\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ln x}}\right),

for some absolute constant
C>0
.

The proof is complete.
∎

e^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}.

(B.2)

Splitting the Taylor series at the
m
-th term, we write

e^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}.

If
0\leq x\leq 1
, then
x^{k}\geq 0
for all
k\geq 0
, and therefore the remainder term is nonnegative. This yields the inequality

\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\leq e^{x},\quad 0\leq x\leq 1,\ \ m\geq 0.

(B.3)

We now split the Taylor series at the
(2m-1)
-th term:

e^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{k=2m}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}.

(B.4)

Rewriting the remainder series in (
B.4
), we obtain

e^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2(m+k)}}{(2(m+k))!}\left(1+\frac{x}{2(m+k)+1}\right).

If
-1<x\leq 0
, then
x^{2(m+k)}\geq 0
and

1+\frac{x}{2(m+k)+1}\geq 0\quad\text{for all }k\geq 0,

and hence

\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\leq e^{x},\quad-1<x\leq 0,\ \ m\geq 0.

(B.5)

Alternatively, rewriting the remainder term in (
B.4

e^{x}=\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+x^{2m}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{(2m+k)!},

(2m+k)!\geq(2m)!

e^{x}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+\frac{x^{2m}}{(2m)!}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}.

Using the geometric series expansion, this yields

e^{x}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}+\frac{x^{2m}}{(1-x)(2m)!},\quad-1<x<1,\ \ m\geq 0.

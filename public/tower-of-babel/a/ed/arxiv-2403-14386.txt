Exploiting Over-The-Air Consensus for Collision Avoidance and Formation Control in Multi-Agent Systems
Michael Epp, Fabio Molinari, Jörg Raisch

Abstract
This paper introduces a distributed control method for multi-agent robotic systems employing Over the Air Consensus (OtA-Consensus). Designed for agents with decoupled single-integrator dynamics, this approach aims at efficient formation achievement and collision avoidance. As a distinctive feature, it leverages OtA’s ability to exploit interference in wireless channels, a property traditionally considered a drawback, thus enhancing communication efficiency among robots. An analytical proof of asymptotic convergence is established for systems with time-varying communication topologies represented by sequences of strongly connected directed graphs. Comparative evaluations demonstrate significant efficiency improvements over current state-of-the-art methods, especially in scenarios with a large number of agents.

III
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{N}=\{1,\dots,n\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, be a set of autonomous agents moving on a two dimensional plane with a decoupled single integrator dynamics, i.e.,

\forall i\in\mathcal{N},\quad\dot{p}_{i}(t)=u_{i}(t),

The agents exchange information at times

\exists\underline{\Delta},\bar{\Delta}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}:\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},~\underline{\Delta}\leq t_{k+1}-t_{k}\leq\bar{\Delta}.

(3)

The control scheme will be designed to let the system reach a stationary formation. This happens when,

\forall i\in\mathcal{N},\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}p_{i}(t)=\bar{p}^{*}+d_{i},

(4)

where

Each agent is equipped with an underlying object detection system.
We will use this to ensure a minimum distance
\delta_{s}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}

We will achieve this by a mechanism (described later) that actively steers agents away from each agent closer than

Let

be the distance between agents
i

j
.
We assume the desired formation to be well-posed, i.e.,
\forall i,j\in\mathcal{N}
,

\left\lVert d_{i}-d_{j}\right\rVert>\delta_{c}.

Let’s also define the set of agents that are not in danger of colliding at time

as

\mathcal{D}_{c}(t):=\left\{i\in\mathcal{N}\mid\forall j\in\mathcal{N}\setminus\{i\},~\left\lVert l_{ij}(t)\right\rVert>\delta_{c}\right\}.

(5)

We generalize this notion to time intervals
[t_{1},t_{2}]
by

\mathcal{D}_{c}(t_{1},t_{2}):=\left\{i\in\mathcal{N}\mid\forall t\in[t_{1},t_{2}],~i\in\mathcal{D}_{c}(t)\right\}.

(6)

IV
Communication Model

As in
[
12
]
, this study adopts the so-called

Wireless Multiple Access Channel (WMAC)
to model
the superposition property (interference) of the wireless medium, i.e.,
the effect of multiple signals being simultaneously
transmitted in the same frequency band.
Interference has traditionally been avoided by using more wireless resources, e.g., by creating orthogonal transmission (each agent is assigned its own time or frequency slot).

Definition 1

(Wireless Multiple Access Channel (WMAC))
.

Agents in set
\mathcal{N}
simultaneously broadcast
\bar{\mu}_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
. The obtained superimposed value at the receiver is

\bar{\nu}_{i}=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\xi_{ij}\bar{\mu}_{j},

(7)

where
\xi_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
are unknown time-varying (fading) channel coefficients; they are assumed to be a realization of a stochastic process, as in
[
12
]
, where they are also shown to be positive
as resulting from power modulation techniques.

To deal with unknown channel coefficients,
[
11
]
proposed to additionally broadcast a known value, e.g.,
1
via an orthogonal channel (i.e., in a different time slot or frequency range).
In our multi-agent scenario, each agent orthogonally broadcasts the entries of a two-dimensional vector and the known value
1
.
If we use TDMA (Time Division Multiple Access), we assume that the delays between the three broadcasts are so small that they can be considered to occur at the same time
t_{k}
.

According to the WMAC model, all agents
i\in\mathcal{N}
then receive

\displaystyle\nu_{i}(t_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\xi_{ij}(k)\mu_{j}(t_{k})\quad\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

(8)

\displaystyle\nu_{i}^{\prime}(t_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\xi_{ij}(k)(t_{k}).

(9)

In the following, we will assume that,
\forall i\in\mathcal{N},\forall k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},i\in\mathcal{N}_{i}
, i.e.,
\xi_{ii}>0
.
Therefore
\nu_{i}^{\prime}(t_{k})
is positive and

\zeta_{i}(t_{k})\coloneqq\frac{\nu_{i}(t_{k})}{\nu_{i}^{\prime}(t_{k})}

(10)

is well defined. Clearly,

\zeta_{i}(t_{k})=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}}h_{ij}(k)\mu_{j}(t_{k}),

(11)

where

h_{ij}(k)=\begin{cases}\frac{\xi_{ij}(k)}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}\xi_{ij}(k)}&\text{if }(j,i)\in\mathcal{A}(k)\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(12)

are the normalized channel coefficients.

By construction,
h_{ij}(k)\in[0,1]
, and

\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}}h_{ij}(k)=1.

(13)

Hence,
\zeta_{i}(t_{k})
is in the convex hull of the
\mu_{j}(t_{k})
,
j\in\mathcal{N}
.
We refer to
\zeta_{i}(t_{k})
as the over-the-air variable of agent
i
.
It encapsulates aggregated (superimposed) information from neighboring agents.

V
Control Strategy

With the definitions from Section
III

V-A

Flow Dynamics

Note that,
\forall t\in[t_{k},t_{k+1}],\mathcal{D}_{c}(t_{k},t)\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{c}(t)\subseteq\mathcal{N}
.
To discuss the flow dynamics, we distinguish three cases:

•

i\in\mathcal{D}_{c}(t_{k},t)
, i.e., agent
i
has not been in danger of collision since the last communication update

•

i\in\mathcal{D}_{c}(t)\setminus\mathcal{D}_{c}(t_{k},t)
, i.e., agent
i
is currently not in danger of collision, but was so after the last communication update

•

i\in\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{c}(t)
, i.e., agent
i
is currently in danger of collision.

Consider the following flow dynamics,
\forall t\in(t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{i}(t)=u_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-a(p_{i}(t)-\vartheta_{i}(t))&\text{if }i\in\mathcal{D}_{c}(t_{k},t)\\
-\cfrac{p_{i}(\tau_{t}^{i})-\vartheta_{i}(\tau_{t}^{i})}{t_{k+1}-\tau_{t}^{i}}&\text{if }i\in\mathcal{D}_{c}(t)\setminus\mathcal{D}_{c}(t_{k},t)\\
r_{i}(t)-a(p_{i}(t)-\vartheta_{i}(t))&\text{if }i\in\mathcal{N}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{c}(t)\end{cases}

(14)

\displaystyle\dot{\vartheta}_{i}(t)=0,

(15)

where
a\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and,
\forall i\in\mathcal{D}_{c}(t)\setminus\mathcal{D}_{c}(t_{k},t)
,

\tau_{t}^{i}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tilde{t}<t\\
i\notin\mathcal{D}_{c}(\tilde{t})\end{subarray}}\tilde{t}

(16)

is the most recent time where agent
i
was in danger of colliding, and
r_{i}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a collision avoidance term.
We define the latter as

\displaystyle r_{i}(t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}\rho_{ij}(t)

(17)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{i}}\rho(t),

(18)

where

\displaystyle\rho_{ij}(t)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{\delta_{c}\left(\delta_{c}-\delta_{s}\right)^{2}}{\left\lVert l_{ij}(t)\right\rVert-\delta_{s}}+\frac{\left\lVert l_{ij}(t)\right\rVert^{2}}{2}&\text{if }\delta_{s}<\left\lVert l_{ij}(t)\right\rVert\leq\delta_{c}\\
\quad-\frac{3\delta_{c}^{2}}{2}+\delta_{c}\delta_{s}&\\
\infty&\text{if }\left\lVert l_{ij}(t)\right\rVert\leq\delta_{s}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(19)

can be interpreted as a potential field variable.
Clearly, (
19
) is decreasing over
\left\lVert l_{ij}\right\rVert\in(\delta_{s},\delta_{c}]
.

VIII
Conclusion

In this paper, we have introduced a consensus-based control strategy, tailored for multi-agent systems with single-integrator dynamics. Central to our approach is the use of an OtA broadcast protocol. This protocol exploits the superposition property of the wireless channel, leading to a drastic reduction of transmissions compared to approaches that avoid interference by using multiplexing.

We have advanced the results of
[
3
]
by integrating a collision avoidance mechanism using artificial potential fields. This enhancement not only guarantees the safety of the agents but also demonstrates the adaptability and practicality of our control strategy in real-world scenarios.

A key finding of our research is the superior efficiency of our proposed controller compared to existing state-of-the-art methods. Notably, this efficiency becomes more pronounced as the complexity of the network increases in terms of the number of agents involved. This scalability is a crucial advantage, particularly in applications involving large-scale systems
[
3
]
.

However, it is important to acknowledge that while our controller assures convergence, in certain “pathological” cases, the system may settle into a local minimum where agents cease movement without achieving the desired formation. Addressing this limitation by identifying and avoiding such cases forms a pivotal part of our future work. We also aim to extend our results to a broader class of systems, including agents with more complex dynamics, and to undertake experimental validation of our proposed controller using a group of real-world mobile robots.

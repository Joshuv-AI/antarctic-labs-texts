1.
Introduction

Let
S_{k}(N)
denote the space of cusp forms of even integer weight
k
with respect to
\Gamma_{0}(N)
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)
be an orthonormal basis of
S_{k}(N)
consisting of joint eigenfunctions of the Hecke operators
T_{n}
with
(n,N)=1
. For
f\in S_{k}(N)
,
the Fourier expansion of
f
at the cusp
\infty
is given by

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{f}(n)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz}.

We denote
\kappa_{f}(n)
to be the
n
th normalised Hecke eigenvalue of
f
. Thus, we have

a_{f}(n)=a_{f}(1)\kappa_{f}(n),\,(n,N)=1.

Let
p
be a fixed prime number with
\gcd(p,N)=1.
By the Ramanujan-Deligne bound, we know that
\kappa_{f}(p)\in[-2,2]
.

Let

\mu_{p}(x):=\frac{p+1}{\pi}\frac{\big(1-\frac{x^{2}}{4}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{p^{-1}})^{2}-x^{2}}

and

\mu_{k,N}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\delta_{\kappa_{f}(p)}

where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at
x.

Serre
[
Ser97
]
proved that for a fixed prime
p
,
\mu_{k,N}
converges weakly to
\mu_{p}
as
k+N\rightarrow\infty
with
k
even and
\gcd(p,N)=1.
Thus, for any interval
I\subset[-2,2]
,

(1)

\lim_{k+N\rightarrow\infty\atop{(p,N)=1\atop{k\text{ even}}}}\mu_{k,N}(I)=\int_{I}\mu_{p}(x)dx,

where

\mu_{k,N}(I):=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\delta_{\kappa_{f}(p)}(I).

Equivalently, for any continuous function
g:\,[-2,2]\to\mathbb{C}
,

\lim_{k+N\rightarrow\infty\atop{(p,N)=1\atop{k\text{ even}}}}\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}g\left(\kappa_{f}(p)\right)=\int_{-2}^{2}g(x)\mu_{p}(x)dx.

The study of the “vertical” distribution of Hecke eigenvalues
\kappa_{f}(p)
where
p
is a fixed prime and
f
varies over suitable cusp forms goes back to the work of Sarnak
[
Sar87
]
, who derived the above law in the context of Maass cusp forms. The asymptotic in equation (
1
) for the case
N=1
was also proved by Conrey, Duke and Farmer
[
CDF97
]
.

Let
S_{k}(N)^{*}
be the subspace of primitive cusp forms in
S_{k}(N)
. Let
T^{*}_{n}
be the restriction of Hecke operator
T_{n}
on the subspace
S_{k}(N)^{*}
of
S_{k}(N)
. Let

\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}
be the orthonormal basis of
S_{k}(N)^{*}
consisting of joint eigenfunctions of the Hecke operators
T_{n}^{*}
,
(n,N)=1
such that
a_{f}(1)=1
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}
.
We denote
\mu_{k,N}^{*}
to be the corresponding measure associated to
T^{*}_{p}
, that is,

\mu_{k,N}^{*}:=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{k}(N)}\delta_{\kappa_{f}(p)}.

Using (
1
), it is not too difficult to deduce that for a fixed prime
p
,
\mu_{k,N}^{*}
converges weakly to
\mu_{p}
as
k+N\rightarrow\infty
with
\gcd(p,N)=1.
That is,
for any interval
I\subset[-2,2]
,

(2)

\lim_{k+N\rightarrow\infty\atop{(p,N)=1\atop{k\text{ even}}}}\mu_{k,N}^{*}(I)=\int_{I}\mu_{p}(x)dx,

where

\mu_{k,N}^{*}(I):=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}|}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{*}(N)}\delta_{\kappa_{f}(p)}(I).

Denote

\mu_{p}(I):=\int_{I}\mu_{p}(x)dx.

The discrepancies

D(\mu_{k,N},\mu_{p}):=\sup\{|\mu_{k,N}(I)-\mu_{p}(I)|\,:\,I=[a,b]\subset[-2,2]\}

and

D(\mu_{k,N}^{*},\mu_{p}):=\sup\{|\mu_{k,N}^{*}(I)-\mu_{p}(I)|\,:\,I=[a,b]\subset[-2,2]\}

have been well investigated, along with applications (see
[
GJS99
]
,
[
Roy00
]
,
[
Gol04
]
,
[
MS09
]
,
[
MS10
]
,
[
SZ22
]
,
[
JS20
]
).

In
[
GJS99
]
, Gamburd, Jakobson and Sarnak obtain the bound

D(\mu_{k,2},\mu_{p})=\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{1}{\operatorname{log}k}\right)

by deriving equivalent results related to the spectrum of the elements in the group ring of
SU(2)
.

For
N=1
, Golubeva
[
Gol04
]
obtains the bound

D(\mu_{k,1},\mu_{p})=\operatorname{O}_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{(\operatorname{log}k)^{1-\epsilon}}\right)

for any
\epsilon>0
.
The above bound was sharpened and generalized in
[
MS09
]
for any positive integer
N
with
(p,N)=1
as follows:

(3)

D(\mu_{k,N},\mu_{p})=\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{1}{\operatorname{log}kN}\right).

A similar upper bound
[
MS10
]
can also be obtained for
D(\mu_{k,N}^{*},\mu_{p})
.

(4)

D(\mu_{k,N}^{*},\mu_{p})=\operatorname{O}\Big(\frac{1}{\operatorname{log}kN}\Big).

We note here that the implied constants in (
3
) and (
4
) are absolute and effectively computable.

Lau and Wang
[
LW11
]
obtain a “higher-dimensional” analogue of the above bounds by considering a discrepancy bound for the joint distribution of Hecke eigenvalues, that is, families of the form

\{(a_{f}(p_{1}),a_{f}(p_{2}),\dots,a_{f}(p_{r})):\,f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)\}

where
p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{r}
are distinct primes not dividing
N
.

The fundamental idea in
[
Gol04
]
,
[
MS09
]
and
[
LW11
]
is to use an Erdös-Turan type of inequality. For any interval
I\subset[-2,2]
, and any positive integer
M\geq 1
, we have

(5)

|\mu_{k,N}(I)-\mu_{p}(I)|\leq\frac{b_{1}\operatorname{dim}(S_{k}(N))}{M+1}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}{b_{m,I}G(m)},

where

•

b_{1}
is an absolute, positive constant,

•

b_{m,I}
is a positive quantity that depends on
m
and the interval
I
, and

•

G(m)
is a function that depends on the sum
\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\kappa_{f}(p^{m})
, which is equal to the trace of the normalized Hecke operator
\frac{T_{p^{m}}}{{p^{m}}^{(k-1)/2}}
.

The constants
b_{1}
and
b_{m,I}
come from certain test functions with suitable analytic properties, which approximate the characteristic function of the interval
I
. One then uses the Eichler-Selberg trace formula to obtain estimates for
\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\kappa_{f}(p^{m})
and
G(m)
. Finally, we choose a value of
M
to obtain an optimal bound for the right hand side of
5
. This technique works for all positive integers
N
, and gives us the estimates in (
3
) and (
4
). In recent work, Sarnak and Zubrilina
[
SZ22
]
give improved uniform estimates for
D(\mu_{2,N}^{*},\mu_{p})
with the help of a new technique which involves the use of the Petersson trace formula.

So far, we have been discussing upper bounds for the discrepancies
D(\mu_{k,N}^{*},\mu_{p})
and
D(\mu_{k,N},\mu_{p})
. A natural question to ask in this context is if one can find
lower bounds
or
\Omega
-type estimates for these discrepancies.

Question
.

Can we find a function
E(k,N)
for positive integers
(k,N)
such that

D(\mu_{k,N}^{*},\mu_{p})=\Omega(E(k,N))\text{ as }k+N\to\infty?

That is, can we find a sequence
(k_{\lambda},N_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
k_{\lambda}
is even,
(p,N_{\lambda})=1
and

D(\mu_{k_{\lambda},N_{\lambda}}^{*},\mu_{p})\gg E\left(k_{\lambda},N_{\lambda}\right)\text{ as }k_{\lambda}+N_{\lambda}\to\infty?

The above question was addressed for
N=2
by Gamburd, Jakobson and Sarnak
[
GJS99
]
, and for all squarefree levels
N
by Jung and Sardari
[
JS20
]
. In
[
GJS99
]
, it is shown that there exists a sequence of even integers
k_{n}\to\infty
such that

(6)

D(\mu_{k_{n},2},\mu_{p})\gg\frac{1}{k_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\operatorname{log}^{2}k_{n}}.

Jung and Sardari
[
JS20
]
generalize the above result to any fixed squarefree level
N
with an improved exponent for
k_{n}
. That is, given a fixed squarefree level
N
, they obtain a sequence of weights
k_{n}
with
k_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that

(7)

D(\mu_{k_{n},N}^{*},\mu_{p})\gg\frac{1}{k_{n}^{\frac{1}{3}}\operatorname{log}^{2}k_{n}}.

Remark 1
.

The limitation of the Eichler-Selberg trace formula in obtaining discrepancy bounds arises from the difficulty in obtaining healthy estimates for the traces of Hecke operators
T_{p^{m}}^{*}
when
p^{m}\gg k
. Therefore, further enquiry into discrepancy estimates necessitates the use of other tools, such as the Petersson trace formula.

The strategy of Jung and Sardari
[
JS20
]
to prove equation (
7
) is as follows.

•

They consider weighted variants of (
1
) and (
2
).
For
f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)
(resp.
\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}
), define

\omega_{f}:=\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}|a_{f}(1)|^{2},

\mathcal{H}_{k}(N):=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\omega_{f},

and

\mathcal{H}_{k}(N)^{*}:=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}}\omega_{f}.

Further, for any interval
I=[a,b]\subset[-2,2]
, define

\nu_{k,N}(I):=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}_{k}(N)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\omega_{f}\,\delta_{\kappa_{f}(p)}(I),

\nu_{k,N}^{*}(I):=\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}_{k}(N)^{*}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}^{*}_{k}(N)}\omega_{f}\,\delta_{\kappa_{f}(p)}(I),

and

\mu_{\infty}(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{1-\frac{x^{2}}{4}}.

Instead of the Eichler-Selberg trace formula, the Petersson trace formula can be used to show that

(8)

\lim_{k+N\rightarrow\infty\atop{(p,N)=1\atop{k\text{ even}}}}\nu_{k,N}(I)=\mu_{\infty}(I),

and

(9)

\lim_{k+N\rightarrow\infty\atop{(p,N)=1\atop{k\text{ even}}}}\nu_{k,N}^{*}(I)=\mu_{\infty}(I),

where

\mu_{\infty}(I)=\int_{I}\mu_{\infty}(x)dx.

Equivalently, for any continuous function
g:[-2,2]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,

\lim_{k+N\rightarrow\infty\atop{(p,N)=1\atop{k\text{ even}}}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}_{k}(N)}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\omega_{f}\,g(\kappa_{f}(p))=\int_{-2}^{2}g(x)\,d\mu_{\infty}(x),

and

\lim_{k+N\rightarrow\infty\atop{(p,N)=1\atop{k\text{ even}}}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}_{k}(N)^{*}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}}\omega_{f}\,g(\kappa_{f}(p))=\int_{-2}^{2}g(x)\,d\mu_{\infty}(x).

We refer the interested reader to
[
Li04
]
and
[
KL13
]
for a discussion of the above weighted distribution theorems, other variants as well as analogues for Maass cusp forms.

•

Jung and Sardari extend the above result and obtain, for a fixed squarefree level
N
, a sequence of weights
k_{n}
with
k_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that the lower bound

(10)

D(\nu_{k_{n},N}^{*},\mu_{\infty})\gg\frac{1}{k_{n}^{\frac{1}{3}}\operatorname{log}^{2}k_{n}}

holds.

•

The transition from (
10
) to (
7
) is made with the help of an explicit asymptotic version of the Petersson trace formula, which is one of the most important ingredients in
[
JS20
]
.

Another natural question that arises is the extension of the above bounds to Hilbert modular forms. In this direction, we recall the following asymptotic result of Knightly and Li
[
KL08
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem

(Knightly, Li,
[
KL08
]
)
.

Let
F
be a totally real number field, and let
m
be a totally positive element of the inverse different
\mathfrak{d}^{-1}\subset F.
For a cusp form
\phi
on
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(F)\backslash\mathrm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{F})
with trivial central character, let
W_{m}^{\phi}
denote its
m
-th Fourier coefficient (see (
16
)). The weight associated to the cusp form
\phi
is defined as

w_{\phi}:=\frac{|W_{m}^{\phi}(1)|^{2}}{\|\phi\|^{2}},

where
\|\phi\|
is the Petersson norm of
\phi
.

For an integral ideal
\mathfrak{N}
of
\mathcal{O}
, let
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},1)
denote the space of holomorphic Hilbert cusp forms of weight
k=(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})
(each
k_{i}>2
and even) with respect to the Hecke congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(\mathfrak{N})
.

For integral ideals
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{N}
such that
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{N})=1
, let
T_{\mathfrak{n}}
denote the
\mathfrak{n}
-th Hecke operator acting on
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},1)
. Let
\{\phi\}
be a Hecke eigenbasis of
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},1)
, that is, a basis of
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},1)
consisting of simultaneous eigenfunctions of the Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{n}},\,(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{N})=1
.

For each
\phi
, let
\lambda^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{n}}
be an eigenvalue for the Hecke operator
T_{\mathfrak{n}}
with eigenvector
\phi
. Let
\kappa^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{n}}:=\frac{\lambda^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{n}}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{n})}}.

Consider a fixed prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
not dividing
m\mathfrak{d}
.

Then for any continuous function
f:[-2,2]\rightarrow\mathbb{C},

\lim_{\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{N})\rightarrow\infty\atop{(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{N})=1}}\frac{\sum_{\phi}f(\kappa_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\phi})w_{\phi}}{\sum_{\phi}w_{\phi}}=\int_{-2}^{2}f(x)\,d\mu_{\infty}(x).

This leads to questions about effective versions of the above theorem. For example, the discrepancy bound in (
3
) was extended to Hilbert modular forms in
[
LLW14
]
. The strategy of proof in
[
LLW14
]
is to use a higher-dimensional variant of the Erdös-Turan type of inequality indicated in (
5
). The analogue of the term
G(m)
in the case of Hilbert modular forms is then estimated with the help of Arthur’s trace formula (see
[
LLW14
, Section 3]
).

In the current article, our main goal is to generalize the lower bound in equation (
10
) to the context of Hilbert cusp forms. The proof of (
10
) follows from an asymptotic formula of Petersson trace formula for the Hecke operators
T_{n}
acting on the space
S_{k}(N)^{*}
for a square-free level
N
, and under a certain relationship between
n
and
k
. Thus, a generalization of (
10
) to Hilbert modular forms necessitates the use of a version of the Petersson trace formula for Hilbert cusp forms. This is presented below.

•

Let
\|x\|
denote the standard Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

•

Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
be the embeddings of
F
into
\mathbb{R}
and let
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}):F\to\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

•

Let
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{N}
be ideals in
\mathcal{O}
such that
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{N})=1
.

•

Consider the equation
1=[\mathfrak{b}]^{2}[\mathfrak{n}]
in terms of ideal class groups, and let

[\mathfrak{b}_{1}],\,[\mathfrak{b}_{2}],\,\dots,\,[\mathfrak{b}_{t}]

be solutions of the above equation. We choose
\eta_{i}\in F
such that
\eta_{i}
generates the principal ideal
\mathfrak{b}_{i}^{2}\mathfrak{n}
.

•

Let us consider

(11)

\displaystyle\delta_{i}=\inf\{\|\sigma(s)\|\,:\,s\in\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N}/\pm,s\neq 0\},

where each
\mathfrak{b_{i}}
is as defined above.

•

We take
\tilde{\delta}_{i}=\frac{\delta_{i}}{2\sqrt{r}}
and
let

(12)

\displaystyle A_{i}=\cap_{j=1}^{r}\{s\in\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N}/\pm\,:\,|\sigma_{j}(s)|\leq 2\delta_{i},s\neq 0\}.

For a fixed
i,

A_{i}
is a discrete bounded set in
\mathbb{R}^{r}.
This makes
A_{i}
finite for each
i
.

•

Let
F^{+}
denote the set of totally positive elements of
F
.

•

Let
\mathcal{O}^{\times}
denote the unit group of
F
, and let
U
be a fixed set of representatives for
\mathcal{O}^{\times}/{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}^{2}
.
U
is a finite set, and
|U|=2^{r}
.

•

We define

\gamma_{j}=\max\left\{\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(\eta_{i}u)}\,|\,i=1,\,...\,,t,u\in U,\eta_{i}u\in F^{+}\right\}.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathfrak{N}
and
\mathfrak{n}
be fixed integral ideals in
F
such that
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{N})=1
. Let
\omega:\,F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a unitary Hecke character. Let the conductor of
\omega
divide
\mathfrak{N}
and
\omega_{\infty_{j}}(x)=\mathrm{sgn}(x)^{k_{j}}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
. For
k=(k_{1},k_{2},\dots,k_{r})
with all
k_{j}>2
, let
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
denote the space of Hilbert cusp forms of weight
k
and character
\omega
with respect to
K_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
(see equation (
15
) for the definition of
K_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
) and let
\mathcal{F}
be an orthogonal basis for
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
consisting of eigenfunctions of the Hecke operator
T_{\mathfrak{n}}
. Let
A_{i}
’s and
\tilde{\delta}_{i}
be as defined above. Let
k_{0}=\min\{k_{j}\,|\,j\leq r\}
, and suppose
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathfrak{d}^{-1}_{+}
satisfy

\frac{2\pi\gamma_{j}\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})}}{\tilde{\delta}_{i}}\in\Big((k_{j}-1)-(k_{j}-1)^{\frac{1}{3}},(k_{j}-1)\Big)\text{ for all }j\leq r.

Then, as
k_{0}\rightarrow\infty
,

\frac{e^{2\pi tr_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m_{1}+m_{2})}}{{\psi(\mathfrak{N})}}\Bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(k_{j}-2)!}{(4\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})})^{k_{j}-1}}\Bigg]\sum_{{\phi}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\phi}W_{m_{1}}^{\phi}(1)\overline{W_{m_{2}}^{\phi}(1)}}{\|\phi\|^{2}}

=\,\hat{T}(m_{1},m_{2},\mathfrak{n})\frac{\sqrt{d_{F}\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}(m_{1}/s)\omega_{\mathrm{f}}(s)}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{u\in U,\eta_{i}u\in F^{+}}\sum_{s\in A_{i}}\Bigg\{\omega_{\mathrm{f}}(s\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-1})S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}}(m_{1},m_{2};\eta_{i}u\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-2};s\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-1})

\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Nm}(\eta_{i}u)}}{\mathrm{Nm}(s)}\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{2\pi}{(\sqrt{-1})^{k_{j}}}J_{k_{j}-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(\eta_{i}um_{1}m_{2})}}{|\sigma_{j}(s)|}\Big)\Bigg\}+\operatorname{o}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\big(k_{j}-1\big)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\right).

A detailed description of all the terms above is provided in §
2
.

Theorem
1
does not require any additional assumption on the field
F
, ideal
\mathfrak{N}
, or the central character
\omega
. The triple sum in Theorem
1
is a finite sum. The main idea of the proof is to use the Petersson trace formula in the Hilbert modular setting. This formula was first stated in
[
KL08
]
, and is recalled in this article as Theorem
5
. We also apply Lemma
8
, which consists of various estimates for the
J
-Bessel function of the first kind.

Remark 2
.

We note that Theorem
1
is an analogue of Theorem
[
JS20
, Theorem 1.7]
for the space
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},1)
. This can be seen in the following manner. The term

\hat{T}(m_{1},m_{2},\mathfrak{n})\frac{\sqrt{d_{F}\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}(m_{1}/s)\omega_{\mathrm{f}}(s)}
in the above theorem takes the role of

. The term

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{u\in U,\eta_{i}u\in F^{+}}\sum_{s\in A_{i}}\Bigg\{\omega_{\mathrm{f}}(s\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-1})S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}}(m_{1},m_{2};\eta_{i}u\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-2};s\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-1})

\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Nm}(\eta_{i}u)}}{\mathrm{Nm}(s)}\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{2\pi}{(\sqrt{-1})^{k_{j}}}J_{k_{j}-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(\eta_{i}um_{1}m_{2})}}{|\sigma_{j}(s)|}\Big)\Bigg\}

is an analogue of
2\pi i^{-k}\frac{\mu(N)}{N}\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2})J_{k-1}(4\pi\sqrt{mn})
in Theorem
[
JS20
, Theorem 1.7]
.

In our next result, we find a lower bound for the main term under additional assumptions that
F
has odd narrow class number and the level
\mathfrak{N}=\tilde{s}\mathcal{O}
, for
\tilde{s}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
For details about notations in the next theorem, see §

.

Theorem 2
.

Let
F
have odd narrow class number. Further let
\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{N}=\tilde{s}\mathcal{O}
with
\tilde{s}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
, the following statements are true.

(a)

The main term in Theorem
1
is equal to

\hat{T}(m_{1},m_{2},\mathfrak{n})\frac{\sqrt{d_{F}\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}(m_{1}/s)\omega_{\mathrm{f}}(s)}+\Bigg\{\omega_{\mathrm{f}}(s\mathtt{b}_{1}^{-1})S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}}(m_{1},m_{2};\eta_{1}\mathtt{b}_{1}^{-2};s\mathtt{b}_{1}^{-1})\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Nm}(\eta_{1})}}{\mathrm{Nm}(s)}\\
\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{2\pi}{(\sqrt{-1})^{k_{j}}}J_{k_{j}-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(\eta_{1}m_{1}m_{2})}}{|\sigma_{j}(s)|}\Big)\Bigg\}

where

(b)

Assume that
S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}}(m_{1},m_{2};\eta_{1}\mathtt{b}_{1}^{-2};s\mathtt{b}_{1}^{-1})\neq 0
for some
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
. Then, as

\displaystyle\Bigg|\frac{e^{2\pi tr_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m_{1}+m_{2})}}{{\psi(\mathfrak{N})}}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(k_{j}-2)!}{(4\pi\sqrt{|\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})|})^{k_{j}-1}}

\displaystyle\sum_{{\phi}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{\lambda_{\mathfrak{n}}^{\phi}W_{m_{1}}^{\phi}(1)\overline{W_{m_{2}}^{\phi}(1)}}{\|\phi\|^{2}}-\hat{T}(m_{1},m_{2},\mathfrak{n})\frac{\sqrt{d_{F}\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}(m_{1}/s)\omega_{\mathrm{f}}(s)}\Bigg|

\displaystyle\gg\prod_{j=1}^{r}(k_{j}-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

A detailed description of all the terms above is provided in §

.

Remark 3
.

For squarefree

We obtain the following variant of (

, there exists an infinite sequence of weights

2.
Petersson trace formula

In this section, we recall some basic facts about Hilbert modular forms and the Petersson trace formula for the space
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
.

Let
F
be a totally real number field and
r=[F:\mathbb{Q}].
Let
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
be the distinct embeddings of
F\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}.
Let
\sigma:F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{r}
be given by
\sigma(s)=(\sigma_{1}(s),\,...\,,\sigma_{r}(s))
. Let
\infty_{1},\dots,\infty_{r}
denote the corresponding Archimedean valuations. Let
\mathcal{O}
be the ring of integers of
F
. Let
N^{\prime}:F\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}
denote the norm map. For a nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}
, let
\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{a})=|\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{a}|.
For
\alpha\in F^{\times},
we define

\mathrm{Nm}(\alpha):=\mathrm{Nm}(\alpha\mathcal{O})=|N^{\prime}(\alpha)|.

Let
d_{F}
denote the discriminant of
F
.

Let
\nu=\nu_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the discrete valuation corresponding to a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
F_{\nu}
be the completion of
F
with respect to the valuation
\nu
. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
be the ring of integers of the local field
F_{\nu}.

Let
\mathbb{A}
denote the adèle ring of
F
with finite adèles
\mathbb{A}_{f}
, so that
\mathbb{A}=F_{\infty}\times\mathbb{A}_{f}
where
F_{\infty}=F\otimes\mathbb{R}\cong\mathbb{R}^{r}
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{O}}=\prod_{\nu<\infty}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}.
Let
F^{+}
denote the set of totally positive elements of
F
, i.e., all
x\in F
such that
\sigma_{i}(x)>0
for all
i=1,\dots,r
. We let
F^{+}_{\infty}
denote the subset of
F_{\infty}
of vectors whose entries are all positive. Let
\mathfrak{d}^{-1}=\{x\in F\,:\,\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(x\mathcal{O})\subset\mathbb{Z}\}

denote the inverse different. We also let
\mathfrak{d}_{+}^{-1}=\mathfrak{d}^{-1}\cap F^{+}
.

Let
\mathfrak{N}
be an integral ideal of
\mathcal{O}
. Let
k=(k_{1},\,...\,,k_{r})
be an
r
-tuple of integers with
k_{j}\geq 2
. Let
\omega:F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be a unitary Hecke character. We can decompose
\omega
as a product of local characters,
\omega=\prod_{\nu}\omega_{\nu}
where
\omega_{\nu}:F_{\nu}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
are the local characters. We further assume that

(1)

the conductor of
\omega
divides
\mathfrak{N}
,

(2)

\omega_{\infty_{j}}(x)=\mathrm{sgn}(x)^{k_{j}}
for all
j=1,\dots,r
.

The first condition means that
\omega_{\nu}
is trivial on
1+\mathfrak{N}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
for all
\nu|\mathfrak{N},
and unramified for all
\nu\nmid\mathfrak{N}.

Let
\theta:\mathbb{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
be the standard character of
\mathbb{A}
. Concretely,
\theta(x)=\theta_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\cdot\prod_{\nu<\infty}\theta_{\nu}(x_{\nu})
, where

(1)

\theta_{\infty}:F_{\infty}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is defined by
\theta_{\infty}(x_{\infty})=e^{-2\pi i(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{r})}
for
x_{\infty}=(x_{1},...,x_{r})
, and

(2)

for
\nu<\infty
,
\theta_{\nu}:F_{\nu}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is given by
\theta_{\nu}(x_{\nu})=e^{2\pi i\{\mathrm{Tr}_{\nu}(x_{\nu})\}}
.
Here
\{\mathrm{Tr}_{\nu}(x_{\nu})\}
is obtained by composing the following maps:
\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{F_{\nu}}:F_{\nu}\to\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, going modulo
p
-adic integers:
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and identifying
\mathbb{Q}_{p}/\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
. This map is well-defined since
e^{2\pi i\mathbb{Z}}=1
. Moreover, the kernel of
\theta_{\nu}
is the local inverse different
\mathfrak{d}_{\nu}^{-1}=\{x\in F_{\nu}:\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}^{F_{\nu}}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\}
. Note that
\theta(x)=1
for
x\in F
, thus
\theta
is a character on
F\backslash\mathbb{A}
.

We now define Kloosterman sums, first locally and then globally. For any finite valuation
\nu
of
F
, let
\mathtt{n}_{\nu}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu},

\mathtt{n}_{\nu}\neq 0,
and
\mathtt{m}_{1\nu},\mathtt{m}_{2\nu}\in\mathfrak{d}_{\nu}^{-1}
. For
\mathtt{c}_{\nu}\in\mathfrak{N}_{\nu}
,
\mathtt{c}_{\nu}\neq 0
, we define the local Kloosterman sum

(13)

S_{\omega_{\nu}}(\mathtt{m}_{1\nu},\mathtt{m}_{2\nu};\mathtt{n}_{\nu};\mathtt{c}_{\nu})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}/\mathtt{c}_{\nu}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}\\
{s_{1}s_{2}\equiv\mathtt{n}_{\nu}\ \mathrm{mod}\ c_{\nu}\mathcal{O}_{\nu}}\end{subarray}}\theta_{\nu}\Big(\frac{\mathtt{m}_{1\nu}s_{1}+\mathtt{m}_{2\nu}s_{2}}{\mathtt{c}_{\nu}}\Big)\omega_{\nu}(s_{2})^{-1}.

The value of the sum is
1
if
\mathtt{c}_{\nu}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{\times}
.

For a fractional ideal
\mathfrak{a},
let
\hat{\mathfrak{a}}=\prod_{\nu<\infty}\mathfrak{a}_{\nu}
where
\mathfrak{a}_{\nu}
denote its localisation.
In the global setting, for
\mathtt{n}\in\hat{\mathcal{O}}\cap\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{f}
,
\mathtt{c}\in\hat{\mathfrak{N}}\cap\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{f}
, and
\mathtt{m_{1}},\mathtt{m_{2}}\in\hat{\mathfrak{d}}^{-1},
we define the global Kloosterman sum

(14)

S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}}(\mathtt{m}_{1},\mathtt{m}_{2};\mathtt{n};\mathtt{c})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s_{1},s_{2}\in\hat{\mathcal{O}}/\mathtt{c}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\\
{s_{1}s_{2}\equiv\mathtt{n}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathtt{c}\hat{\mathcal{O}}}\end{subarray}}\theta_{f}\Big(\frac{\mathtt{m}_{1}s_{1}+\mathtt{m}_{2}s_{2}}{\mathtt{c}}\Big)\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}(s_{2})^{-1},

where
\theta_{f}(x)=\prod_{\nu<\infty}\theta_{\nu}(x_{\nu})
,
\omega_{\mathfrak{N},\nu}=\omega_{\nu}
if
\nu|\mathfrak{N}
, and
1

if
\nu\nmid\mathfrak{N}
. Also, let

\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}=\prod\omega_{\mathfrak{N},\nu}=\prod_{\nu|\mathfrak{N}}\omega_{\nu}.

We have the following relation between the global and local Kloosterman sums,

S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}}(\mathtt{m}_{1},\mathtt{m}_{2};\mathtt{n};\mathtt{c})=\prod_{\nu<\infty}S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N},\nu}}(\mathtt{m}_{1\nu},\mathtt{m}_{2\nu};\mathtt{n}_{\nu};\mathtt{c}_{\nu}).

Note that the product on the RHS above is well-defined because
\mathtt{c}_{\nu}\in\mathcal{O}_{\nu}^{*}
except for finitely many
\nu
.

Let
K_{f}=\prod_{\nu<\infty}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\nu})
be the standard maximal compact subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{f}).

Let

(15)

K_{1}(\mathfrak{N})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K_{f}:c\in\mathfrak{N}\hat{\mathcal{O}},d\in 1+\mathfrak{N}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\right\},

and let
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
be the space of Hilbert cusp forms with respect to
K_{1}(\mathfrak{N})
, of weight
k
and central character
\omega
. We also define
K_{0}(\mathfrak{N})=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in K_{f}:c\in\mathfrak{N}\hat{\mathcal{O}}\right\}
.

For
\phi\in A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
and
m\in\mathfrak{d}^{-1}_{+}
, we now recall the definition of the
m
th Fourier coefficient of
\phi
, denoted by
W_{m}^{\phi}
, as in
[
KL08
, §3.4]
.
Consider the unipotent subgroup
\tilde{N}=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}1&*\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\bigg\}
of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
. For any
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A})
, the map
n\mapsto\phi(ng)
is a continuous function on
\tilde{N}(F)\backslash\tilde{N}(\mathbb{A}),
with a Fourier expansion

\phi\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{pmatrix}g\bigg)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{F}}}\sum_{m\in F}W_{m}^{\phi}(g)\theta_{m}(x).

The coefficients are Whittaker functions defined by

(16)

W_{m}^{\phi}(g)=\int_{F\backslash\mathbb{A}}\phi\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}1&x\\
&1\end{pmatrix}g\bigg)\theta(mx)\,dx

where
dx
denotes the Lebesgue measure.
For
y\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}
, we also denote

W^{\phi}_{m}(y)=W^{\phi}_{m}\bigg(\begin{pmatrix}y&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\bigg),

ignoring the abuse of notation. We identify
\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\times}
with
\{1_{\infty}\}\times\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\times}\subset\mathbb{A}^{\times},
so that
y\in\mathbb{A}_{f}^{\times}
can be identified as
\{1_{\infty}\}\times y
in
\mathbb{A}^{\times}
.
We refer the reader to
[
KL08
, Section 4.3]
for the definition of the Hecke operators
T_{\mathfrak{n}}
.
We now recall the following result which we use later.

Lemma 4

(
[
KL08
, Cor. 4.8]
)
.

Let
\tilde{d}\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{f}
such that
\tilde{d}\hat{\mathcal{O}}=\hat{\mathfrak{d}}
and
m\in\mathfrak{d}^{-1}_{+}
. If
(m\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{N})=1,
then for any
T_{m\mathfrak{d}}
-eigenfunction
\phi\in A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
with
W_{1}^{\phi}(1/\tilde{d})=1
and
T_{m\mathfrak{d}}\phi=\lambda^{\phi}_{m\mathfrak{d}},
we have

W_{m}^{\phi}(1)=\frac{e^{2\pi r}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sigma_{j}(m)^{(k_{j}/2)-1}}{d_{F}e^{2\pi\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m)}}\lambda^{\phi}_{m\mathfrak{d}}.

We now recall the statement of Petersson’s trace formula in the Hilbert modular setting.

Theorem 5

(
[
KL08
, Thm. 5.11]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
and
\mathfrak{N}
be integral ideals with
(\mathfrak{n},\mathfrak{N})=1.
Let
k=(k_{1},...,k_{r})
with all
k_{j}>2.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be an orthogonal basis for
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
consisting of eigenfunctions for the Hecke operator
T_{\mathfrak{n}}.
Then for any
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathfrak{d}_{+}^{-1}
, we have

(17)

\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m_{1}+m_{2})}}{{\psi(\mathfrak{N})}}\Bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(k_{j}-2)!}{(4\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})})^{k_{j}-1}}\Bigg]\sum_{{\phi}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{\lambda^{\phi}_{\mathfrak{n}}W_{m_{1}}^{\phi}(1)\overline{W_{m_{2}}^{\phi}(1)}}{\|\phi\|^{2}}=\hat{T}(m_{1},m_{2},\mathfrak{n})\frac{\sqrt{d_{F}\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{n})}}{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}(m_{1}/s)\omega_{f}(s)}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in U\\
\eta_{i}u\in F^{+}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N}/\pm\\
s\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Bigg\{\omega_{f}(sb_{i}^{-1})S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}}(m_{1},m_{2};\eta_{i}u\mathrm{b}_{i}^{-2};s\mathrm{b}_{i}^{-1})\\
\times\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Nm}(\eta_{i}u)}}{\mathrm{Nm}(s)}\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{2\pi}{(\sqrt{-1})^{k_{j}}}J_{k_{j}-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(\eta_{i}um_{1}m_{2})}}{|\sigma_{j}(s)|}\right)\Bigg\}.

•

where
\hat{T}(m_{1},m_{2},\mathfrak{n})\in\{0,1\}
is non zero if and only if there exists
s\in\hat{\mathfrak{d}}^{-1}
such that
m_{1}m_{2}\in s\hat{\mathcal{O}}
and
m_{1}m_{2}\hat{\mathcal{O}}=s^{2}\hat{\mathfrak{n}}
,

•

U
is a set of representative for
\mathcal{O}^{\times}/{\mathcal{O}^{\times}}^{2}
,

•

\mathrm{b_{i}}\hat{\mathcal{O}}=\hat{\mathfrak{b}}_{i}
for integral ideal
\mathfrak{b}_{i}
and
i=1,\,\dots\,,t
, where
\mathfrak{b_{i}}
s are a set of representatives for the equation
[\mathfrak{b}]^{2}[\mathfrak{n}]=1
in the ideal class group,

•

\eta_{i}\in F
generates the principal ideal
\mathfrak{b}_{i}^{2}\mathfrak{n}
,

•

\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}=\prod_{v|\mathfrak{N}}\omega_{v}\prod_{v\nmid\mathfrak{N}}1
,

•

and
\psi(\mathfrak{N})=[K_{f}:K_{0}(\mathfrak{N})]=\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{N})\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{N}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{\mathrm{Nm}(\mathfrak{p})}\Big)
.

Corollary 6
.

Let
\tilde{d}\in\mathbb{A}^{\times}_{f}
such that
\tilde{d}\hat{\mathcal{O}}=\hat{\mathfrak{d}}
and
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathfrak{d}_{+}^{-1}
such that
(m_{1}\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{N})=(m_{2}\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{N})=1.
Let
W_{1}^{\phi}(1/\tilde{d})=1
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{F},
where

\mathcal{F}
is an orthogonal basis for
A_{k}(\mathfrak{N},\omega)
.
Then

\frac{e^{4\pi r}}{\psi(\mathfrak{N})d_{F}^{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{Nm}(m_{1}m_{2})}}\Bigg[\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{(k_{j}-2)!}{(4\pi)^{k_{j}-1}}\Bigg]\sum_{{\phi}\in\mathcal{F}}\frac{\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{1}\mathfrak{d}}\overline{\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{2}\mathfrak{d}}}}{\|\phi\|^{2}}=\hat{T}(m_{1},m_{2},\mathcal{O})\frac{\sqrt{d_{F}}}{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}(m_{1}/s)\omega_{f}(s)}\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in U\\
\eta_{i}u\in F^{+}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\in\mathfrak{b_{i}}\mathfrak{N}/\pm\\
s\neq 0\end{subarray}}\Bigg\{\omega_{f}(s\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-1})S_{\omega_{\mathfrak{N}}}(m_{1},m_{2};\eta_{i}u\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-2};s\mathtt{b}_{i}^{-1})\frac{\sqrt{\mathrm{Nm}(\eta_{i}u)}}{\mathrm{Nm}(s)}\\
\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{2\pi}{(\sqrt{-1})^{k_{j}}}J_{k_{j}-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{\sigma_{j}(\eta_{i}um_{1}m_{2})}}{|\sigma_{j}(s)|}\right)\Bigg\}.

Proof.

This corollary is proved by substituting the expressions for
W_{m_{1}}^{\phi}(1)
and
W_{m_{2}}^{\phi}(1)
obtained from Lemma
4
and taking
\mathfrak{n}=\mathcal{O}
in Theorem
5
. Using Lemma
4
we have

W_{m_{1}}^{\phi}(1)=\frac{e^{2\pi r}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sigma_{j}(m_{1})^{(k_{j}/2)-1}}{d_{F}e^{2\pi\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m_{2})}}\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{1}\mathfrak{d}}

and

W_{m_{2}}^{\phi}(1)=\frac{e^{2\pi r}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sigma_{j}(m_{2})^{(k_{j}/2)-1}}{d_{F}e^{2\pi\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m_{2})}}\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{2}\mathfrak{d}}.

Multiplying the first equation with the conjugate of the second one, we get

\displaystyle W_{m_{1}}^{\phi}(1)\overline{W_{m_{2}}^{\phi}(1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{4\pi r}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})^{(k_{j}/2)-1}}{d_{F}^{2}e^{2\pi\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m_{1}+m_{2})}}\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{1}\mathfrak{d}}\overline{\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{2}\mathfrak{d}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{4\pi r}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})^{((k_{j}-1)/2)}}{d_{F}^{2}\sqrt{\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})}e^{2\pi\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m_{1}+m_{2})}}\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{1}\mathfrak{d}}\overline{\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{2}\mathfrak{d}}}.

Thus

\frac{e^{2\pi\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{Q}}^{F}(m_{1}+m_{2})}}{\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sigma_{j}(m_{1}m_{2})^{((k_{j}-1)/2)}}W_{m_{1}}^{\phi}(1)\overline{W_{m_{2}}^{\phi}(1)}=\frac{e^{4\pi r}}{d_{F}^{2}\sqrt{\mathrm{Nm}(m_{1}m_{2})}}\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{1}\mathfrak{d}}\overline{\lambda^{\phi}_{m_{2}\mathfrak{d}}}.

In Theorem
5
, taking
\mathfrak{n}=\mathcal{O}
implies
\lambda^{\phi}_{\mathcal{O}}=1

and the proof is complete.
∎

3.
Estimating the error term in trace formula

In this section, we primarily prove Theorem
1
. First, we try to estimate the triple sum appearing on the right-hand side of the trace formula given by Theorem
5
. One of the main objects appearing in the sum is the J-Bessel function. Hence
in the next lemma, we recall bounds for the J-Bessel function of the first kind that will be useful.

Lemma 8
.

We have the following estimates of the
J
-Bessel function.

(i) If
a\geq 0
and
0<x\leq 1
, we have

1\leq\frac{J_{a}(ax)}{x^{a}J_{a}(a)}\leq e^{a(1-x)}

(ii)

0<J_{a}(a)\ll\frac{1}{a^{\frac{1}{3}}}
as
a\rightarrow\infty

(iii) If
|d|<1
, then

\frac{1}{a^{\frac{1}{3}}}\ll J_{a}(a+da^{\frac{1}{3}})\ll\frac{1}{a^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

(iv) For
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
a>0
,
|J_{a}(x)|\leq\min(ba^{-\frac{1}{3}},c|x|^{-\frac{1}{3}})
where
b=0.674885...\,
and
c=0.7857468704...\,
.

(v) For

\sqrt{(1^{2}+...+1^{2})(\sigma_{1}^{2}(s)+...+\sigma_{r}^{2}(s))}\geq\sigma_{1}(s)+...+\sigma_{r}(s).

Using AM-GM inequality,

\frac{\sigma_{1}(s)+...+\sigma_{r}(s)}{r}\geq(\sigma_{1}(s)\times...\times\sigma_{r}(s))^{\frac{1}{r}}=\mathrm{Nm}(s)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

The equality holds when
\sigma_{1}(s)=...=\sigma_{r}(s).
In such case
\sigma_{1}^{2}(s)+...+\sigma_{r}^{2}(s)=r\sigma_{1}^{2}(s)=r(\mathrm{Nm}(s))^{\frac{2}{r}}.
As
\mathrm{Nm}(s)\in\mathbb{N},
the minimum value of
r(\mathrm{Nm}(s))^{\frac{2}{r}}=r,
which happens when
\mathrm{Nm}(s)=1
and
\sigma_{1}^{2}(s)=1
implying
\sigma_{1}(s)=1.
Thus
\delta_{1}=\sqrt{r}
and on applying Lemma
10
we have
A_{1}=\{\sigma(1)\}.

∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{N}
be an ideal in
\mathcal{O}
such that
\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{N}=\tilde{s}\mathcal{O}
with
\tilde{s}\in\mathbb{Z}.
Then,
\delta_{1}=|\tilde{s}|\sqrt{r}
and
A_{1}=\{\sigma(|\tilde{s}|)\}.

Proof.

We wish to minimize
\inf\{\|\sigma(s)\|\,:\,s\in\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{N}/\pm,s\neq 0\}.
Without loss of generality this is equivalent to minimize
\|\sigma(s)\|^{2}=\sigma_{1}^{2}(s)+...+\sigma_{r}^{2}(s)
on the set

B=\{s\,:\,s\in\mathfrak{b}_{1}\mathfrak{N}/\pm,s\neq 0,\sigma_{j}(s)\geq 0
for
j=1,..,r

\}.
Proceeding similar to the argument in Lemma
11
, possible minimum value of
\|\sigma(s)\|^{2}
will be
r(\mathrm{Nm}(s))^{\frac{2}{r}}.

For
s\in B,

s=\tilde{s}s^{\prime}
for some
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{O}
implying
\mathrm{Nm}(s)=\mathrm{Nm}(\tilde{s})\mathrm{Nm}(s^{\prime}).
Hence
\mathrm{Nm}(\tilde{s})
divides
\mathrm{Nm}(s)
and the minimum value of
\mathrm{Nm}(s)
is
\mathrm{Nm}(\tilde{s}).
Therefore
r(\mathrm{Nm}(s))^{\frac{2}{r}}
has minimum value
r(\mathrm{Nm}(\tilde{s}))^{\frac{2}{r}}.
This shows
\|\sigma(s)\|^{2}
has minimum value
r(\mathrm{Nm}(\tilde{s}))^{\frac{2}{r}}
that happens for
\sigma(s)=(|\tilde{s}|,\,...\,,|\tilde{s}|).
By Lemma
10
, we get
A_{1}=\{\sigma(|\tilde{s}|)\}.

∎

Lemma 13

, and consider

\displaystyle\Bigg[e\Bigg(\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\frac{m_{1\nu_{l}}s_{1}+m_{2\nu_{l}}s_{2}}{\varpi_{\nu_{l}}}\Big)\Bigg)\Bigg]^{p_{l}}=e\Bigg(p_{l}\cdot\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\frac{m_{1\nu_{l}}s_{1}+m_{2\nu_{l}}s_{2}}{\varpi_{\nu_{l}}}\Big)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=e\Bigg(\mathrm{Tr}\Big(p_{l}\cdot\frac{m_{1\nu_{l}}s_{1}+m_{2\nu_{l}}s_{2}}{\varpi_{\nu_{l}}}\Big)\Bigg)=e\Bigg(\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\frac{m_{1\nu_{l}}p_{l}s_{1}+m_{2\nu_{l}}p_{l}s_{2}}{\varpi_{\nu_{l}}}\Big)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=e\Bigg(\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\frac{m_{1\nu_{l}}p_{l}s_{1}}{\varpi_{\nu_{l}}}\Big)\Bigg)\cdot e\Bigg(\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\frac{m_{2\nu_{l}}p_{l}s_{1}}{\varpi_{\nu_{l}}}\Big)\Bigg).

\Bigg[e\Bigg(\mathrm{Tr}\Big(\frac{m_{1\nu_{l}}s_{1}+m_{2\nu_{l}}s_{2}}{\varpi_{\nu_{l}}}\Big)\Bigg)\Bigg]^{p_{l}}=1.

should be
0.

and hence
\operatorname{log}k_{l_{j}}\gg l
.
Therefore

D(\tilde{\nu}_{k_{l},\mathfrak{N}},\mu_{\infty})\gg\frac{1}{\big(\operatorname{log}k_{l_{j_{0}}}\big)^{2}\times\prod_{j=1}^{r}(k_{l_{j}}-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

for any
j_{0}\in\{1,...\,,r\}.

∎

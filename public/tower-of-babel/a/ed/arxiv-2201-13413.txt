An Iterative Energy Estimate for Degenerate Einstein model of Brownian motion
Isanka Garli Hevage 1 , Akif Ibraguimov 2 , Zeev Sobol 3

Abstract
. We consider the degenerate Einstein’s Brownian motion model for the case when the time interval ( τ \tau ) of particle Jumps before collision (free jumps) reciprocal to the number of particles per unit volume u ⁡ ( x , t ) > 0 u(x,t)>0 at the point of observation x x at time t t . The parameter 0 < τ ≤ C < ∞ 0<\tau\leq C<\infty , controls characteristic of the fluid "almost decreases" to 0 0 when u → ∞ u\rightarrow\infty . This degeneration leads to the localisation of the spread of particle propagation in the media. In our report we will present a structural condition of the time interval of free jumps - τ \tau and the frequency of these free jumps ϕ \phi as functions of u u which guarantees the finite speed of propagation of u u .

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

The key parameters

\varphi
in general can depend on the concentration of the particles and also space coordinate and time itself.

Axiom 1

Whole universe axiom:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{P}(\tau)}\varphi(\vec{\Delta})d\vec{\Delta}=1.

Assumption 2
.

Symmetry of free jumps

(2.2)

\varphi(\vec{\Delta})=\varphi(-\vec{\Delta}).

Existence of second moments

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{P(\tau)}}|\vec{\Delta}|^{2}\varphi(\vec{\Delta})d\vec{\Delta}<\infty

Note that it follows from the preceding assumption that the expectation of

of

Definition 1
.

Standard Co-variance matrix of free jump

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}^{2}\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{P}(\tau)}{\Delta_{i}}{\Delta_{j}}\varphi(\vec{\Delta})d\vec{\Delta}\quad\text{ where }i,j=1,2,\dots,N.

Evidently

depend on space
x

. We postulate generalized Einsteins Axiom for the number of particles found at time

in the control volume

by :

Axiom of Mass conservation
.

(2.5)

u(x,t+\tau)\cdot dv=\int_{\mathbb{P}(\tau)}u(x+\vec{\Delta},t)\varphi(\vec{\Delta})d\vec{\Delta}\cdot dv+\tau\cdot\int_{\mathbb{P}(\tau)}M(x+\vec{\Delta},t)\varphi(\vec{\Delta})d\vec{\Delta}\cdot dv.

Mass conservation law (

density" of particles which "produced" and "

For comparison see

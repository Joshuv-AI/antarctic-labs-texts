Motivation and results

We work on the field

1.1.
Rational curves and its moduli spaces

Two ways are well-known to compactify rational curves space.
Let
X
be a smooth projective variety with a fixed embedding
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
.

•

(
[
FP97
]
) Let

be the moduli space of
stable
maps
[f:C\rightarrow X]
in

\deg(f)=d
and genus

•

(
[
Sim94
]
) Let
\mathbf{M}_{d}(X)
be the moduli space of
stable
sheaves
F
in
X
with Hilbert polynomial
\chi(F(m))=dm+1
.

The first (resp. second) one
\mathcal{M}_{d}(X)
(resp.
\mathbf{M}_{d}(X)
) is often called by the Konstevich space (resp. Simpson space). The first moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{d}(X)
for a convex variety
X
has at worst a finite group quotient singularity (i.e., orbifold singularity) (
[
FP97
, Theorem 2]
). Both spaces play a crucial role in counting of the number of curves, as they are known as Gromov-Witten (GW) and Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants (and also descendant invariants) respectively. Besides this aspect, it has played an important role in birational geometry (specially, log minimal model program or Fano/hyperkähler geometry), and the first-named author studied the birational relationship between these spaces
\mathcal{M}_{d}(X)
and
\mathbf{M}_{d}(X)
,
d\leq 3
in a various setting (
[
CK11
,
CHL18
,
Chu22
]
). To analyze the geometric structure of the Simpson space
\mathbf{M}_{d}(X)
in higher dimensional variety
X
(and degree
d
), it is necessary to study the most basic case, namely,
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
. For example, as shown in
[
FT04
,
CCM16
]
, the moduli space
\mathbf{M}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{3})
,
d\leq 4
consists of (several) irreducible components such that the intersection part is generically a fibration structure over the Grassimannian
\mathrm{Gr}(3,4)
of planes in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
In the viewpoint of enumerative geometry, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
provides a solution of a long standing problem called by
Konstevich’s formula
(
[
FP97
]
). In detail, let

be the number of
degree
d
rational curves passing through
3d-1
given points in general position in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. In 1995, through the work of Kontsevich (
[

]
), it has been found a recursive formula to compute

N_{d}=\sum_{d_{1}+d_{2}=d}N_{d_{1}}N_{d_{2}}d_{1}^{2}d_{2}(d_{2}{3d-4\choose 3d_{1}-2}-d_{1}{3d-4\choose 3d_{1}-1}).

Clearly
N_{1}=N_{2}=1
. Even the case of lower degrees has a very long history. In the mid-1800s, Steiner (resp. Zeuthen) showed that
N_{3}=12
(resp.
N_{4}=620
) (
[
Ste54
,
Zeu73
]
). Also

was computed in middle of 1980.

On the other hand, prior to Kontsevich’s work, research on the geometry of plane curves was focused on the study of Severi varieties
V_{d,\delta}
(so called, the space of curves with prescribed degree
d
and the number
\delta
of nodes), and J. Harris made significant contributions and provided direction in this area. One of his notable achievements was proving the irreducibility of the Severi variety
V_{d,\delta}
(
[
Har86
]
). Related with this, in
[
DH88
]
, the authors mentioned the need for a smooth space with a moduli meaningful points. As one may expect, the boundary points of plane curves space may have bad singularity as possible and thus it is very hard to construct a smooth moduli space globally.

Although the management of orbifold singularities of the Kontsevich space has led to the development of a rich theory (so called,
perfect obstruction
theory
[
BF97
]
) in enumerative geometry, the fundamental question about smooth compactification of

has been a central concern by following the monumental work of Hironaka (
[
Hir64
]
). In this stream, we propose a smooth compactification of the space of degree
4
rational plane curves, which is the first non-trivial case in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

2.3.
Symmetric powers and its basics of
\mathrm{SL}_{3}
-representation

Let
\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}
be the tensor power of a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on a smooth projective variety

k
-th symmetric power of
\mathcal{F}
, denoted by
\operatorname{Sym}^{k}\mathcal{F}
, is defined as the quotient of the tensor power
\mathcal{F}^{\otimes k}
by the ideal sheaf generated by all elements of the form
s_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes s_{k}-\sigma(s_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\sigma(s_{k})
, where
s_{1},\dots,s_{k}
are local sections of
\mathcal{F}
and
\sigma
is any element of the symmetric group
S_{n}
of
n
-letters. Let us denote the equivalence class by
s_{1}\cdot s_{2}\cdots\cdot s_{k}:=[s_{1}\otimes s_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes s_{k}]\in\operatorname{Sym}^{k}\mathcal{F}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
,
\mathcal{F}
, and
\mathcal{H}
be coherent sheaves on
X
. Consider an exact sequence
{\mathcal{E}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\delta}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\epsilon}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathcal{H}}\longrightarrow 0
. Then, for all
k\geq 1
, we obtain an exact sequence

\mathcal{E}\otimes\mathrm{Sym}^{k-1}\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s(\delta)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{sym}^{k}(\epsilon)}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathrm{Sym}^{k}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow 0

where
s(\delta)
is defined by

e\otimes(f_{1}\cdot f_{2}\cdots f_{k-1})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{k}(f_{1}\cdot f_{2}\cdots\delta(e)\cdots f_{k-1}).

For our purpose, we need a standard
\mathrm{SL}_{3}
-representation theory related with symmetric power spaces. We summarize the contents of Section 13.3 in
[
FH91
]
. Let
V
be a vector space with dimension
\dim V=3
. Then the standard representation of
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V)
is isomorphic to the space

\begin{split}\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V)&\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{4}V\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V^{*}\\
&\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{4}V\oplus\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\wedge^{2}V)\end{split}

where the kernel of the multiplication map
m:\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V)\twoheadrightarrow\mathrm{Sym}^{4}V
is identified with
\text{ker}(m)=\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\wedge^{2}V)\cong\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V^{*}
under a fixed isomorphism
\wedge^{2}V\cong V^{*}\otimes\wedge^{3}V
. Here the embedding is given by

\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\wedge^{2}V)\subset\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathrm{Sym}^{2}V),\;(u\wedge v)\cdot(w\wedge z)\mapsto(u\cdot w)\cdot(v\cdot z)-(u\cdot z)\cdot(v\cdot w).

Example 2.10
.

Let
(I_{Z})_{2}=\langle x^{2},xy,y^{2}\rangle
be the degree

. As a

-vector space,

for

. One may choose a basis of

as

\left\{x^{2}\cdot x^{2},\;x^{2}\cdot(xy),\;x^{2}\cdot y^{2},\;(xy)\cdot(xy),\;(xy)\cdot y^{2},\;y^{2}\cdot y^{2}\right\},

which is a subvector space of

Elliptic curves and spin
Peter Koymans, Peter Vang Uttenthal

Abstract
In the early 2000s, Ramakrishna asked the question: For the elliptic curve E : y 2 = x 3 − x , E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x, what is the density of primes p p for which the Fourier coefficient a p ​ ( E ) a_{p}(E) is a cube modulo p p ? As a generalization of this question, Weston–Zaurova [ 14 ] formulated conjectures concerning the distribution of power residues of degree m m of the Fourier coefficients of elliptic curves E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} with complex multiplication. In this paper, we prove the conjecture of Weston–Zaurova for cubic residues using the analytic theory of spin. Our proof works for all elliptic curves E E with complex multiplication.

Introduction

For the elliptic curve

E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x,

with complex multiplication by
\mathbb{Q}(i)
and error terms

a_{p}(E)=p+1-|E(\mathbb{F}_{p})|,

are there infinitely many primes
p\equiv 1\bmod 12
such that
a_{p}(E)
is a cubic residue modulo
p
? This question was formulated by Ramakrishna in the early 2000s. At that time, the question was perceived as interesting yet inaccessible. Note that it is natural to restrict to primes
p\equiv 1\bmod 12
. Indeed, for
p\equiv 3\bmod 4
, we have
a_{p}(E)=p+1
if
p\geq 5
, so
a_{p}(E)
is a cube modulo
p
. Furthermore, all elements of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
are automatically cubes if
p\equiv 2\bmod 3
, so in particular
a_{p}(E)
is once more a cube modulo
p
.

In 2005, Weston
[
13
]
formulated a family of conjectures inspired by the question, but only for elliptic curves without complex multiplication. In a subsequent paper, Weston and Zaurova considered the analogous conjectures for elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with complex multiplication. Write
w_{K}
for the number of roots of unity contained in a number field
K
.

Conjecture 1

(Weston–Zaurova
[
14
, Conjecture 3.1]
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic field
K
. Let
m
be an integer coprime with
w_{K}
. Then the density of primes
p\equiv 1\bmod m
with
a_{p}(E)\neq 0
for which
a_{p}(E)
is an
m^{th}
power residue modulo
p
is
1/m
. Furthermore, the density is independent of any Chebotarev class
\mathcal{P}
contained in the set of primes
p\equiv 1\bmod m
with
a_{p}(E)\neq 0
in the sense that if the primes are restricted to
\mathcal{P}
, the relative density remains
1/m
.

Note that, for all but finitely many primes,
p\equiv 1\bmod m
and the reduction of
E
at
p
being ordinary is equivalent to
p
splitting completely in
K(\zeta_{m})
.

In
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
, Weston and Zaurova prove the special cases of the conjecture to which classical techniques are applicable. In particular, they settle the case when
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
and
m=3
by using that
K
contains the third roots of unity so that the classical cubic reciprocity law is available. For this reason, we exclude the field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
in the statements of this paper.

In contrast, the classical techniques do not apply to the case
K=\mathbb{Q}(i)
and
m=3
, and therefore the original question for the curve
E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x
remained open. In this paper, we answer Ramakrishna’s question in the affirmative, and prove that among the primes
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
for which
E:y^{2}=x^{3}-x
has ordinary reduction (equivalently,
p\equiv 1\bmod 4
for
p\geq 5
), the density of
a_{p}(E)
being a cubic residue modulo
p
is
1/3
. In addition, we prove Conjecture
1
for all elliptic curves with complex multiplication (CM) by an imaginary quadratic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
where
d\neq 3
in the case of cubic power residues (i.e.
m=3
). We do not need the curves to be defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, nor do we need them to have CM by a maximal order; it suffices that they have CM by some order
\mathcal{O}
in
K
and are defined over the ring class field
L
of
\mathcal{O}
.

To prove the main theorems of this paper, we extend the analytic theory of spin to cubic symbols over totally imaginary biquadratic fields. Our results are
unconditional
, as we do not need to assume any standard conjectures on short character sums. This is atypical for results on spin symbols, which are most often conditional on such conjectures.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field satisfying
\gcd(3,w_{K})=1
, or equivalently
K\neq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
. For any prime
p
that splits completely in
K
as
(\pi\overline{\pi})
, where
\overline{\pi}
is the conjugate of
\pi
, and where
\pi
lies below a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
K(\zeta_{3})
, there is a well-defined cubic spin symbol of the form

[\mathfrak{p}]=\left(\frac{\overline{\pi}}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)_{K(\zeta_{3}),3}.

Furthermore, there is a constant
C>0
such that for all
X\geqslant 100

\left|\sum_{N_{K(\zeta_{3})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})\leqslant X}[\mathfrak{p}]\right|\leqslant CX^{1-\frac{1}{3200}}.

One readily checks that the symbol does not depend on the choice of generator
\pi
, see Proposition
2.2
. The last part of Theorem
1.1
lies much deeper and its proof occupies the majority of this paper.

As a corollary, for any elliptic curve
E
with complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic field
K
, we prove asymptotics with a power saving error term for the primes
p
that split completely in
K(\zeta_{3})
such that
a_{p}(E)
is a cubic residue modulo
p
. Let us now explain what we mean by
a_{p}(E)
in this context.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve with CM by an order
\mathcal{O}
in an imaginary quadratic field
K
and let
L
be the ring class field of
\mathcal{O}
. Assume that

is defined over

. If we take a prime
\mathfrak{p}
above
p
where

has good reduction, we may introduce the quantity

\displaystyle a_{\mathfrak{p}}(E)=p+1-|E(O_{L}/\mathfrak{p})|.

(1.1)

If
\mathcal{O}^{\times}=\{\pm 1\}
and if
\mathfrak{q}
is another prime of

above
p
, it is a classical result of Deuring that
a_{\mathfrak{p}}(E)^{2}=a_{\mathfrak{q}}(E)^{2}
, i.e.

is determined up to sign (for a reference, see our Lemma

). Therefore

being a cube modulo

does not depend on the choice of prime

. A somewhat more involved argument gives the same result if

, see Proposition

.

Since it is possible to impose arbitrary congruence conditions when handling spin symbols, we are able to get density results even when imposing arbitrary
abelian splitting conditions
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let

be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by an order

in an imaginary quadratic field

different from
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
. Suppose

\frac{\#\{p\leqslant X,p\equiv 1\bmod 3,p\textup{ splits in }L:a_{\mathfrak{p}}(E)\textup{ is a cube modulo }p\}}{\#\{p\leqslant X,p\equiv 1\bmod 3,p\textup{ splits in }L\}}=\frac{1}{3}+O\left(\frac{1}{X^{1/3200}}\right).

is defined as in equation (

) with

an arbitrary prime of

above

.

If
\mathcal{P}
is a Chebotarev class of rational primes defined by a splitting condition in an abelian extension of

containing

and projecting trivially in

, then the result remains valid when the set of primes is restricted to
p
with

.

For an imaginary quadratic field
K
and a prime
p
, we let

be the maximal abelian extension of
K
killed by
m
and unramified away from
p
. We prove the following density theorem on the splitting of primes
p
in the number fields

that depend on
p
. In the statement of Theorem

, the splitting condition on

is imposed to control the size of the maximal abelian extension of

unramified away from
p
.

Let
m
be a prime and let
\zeta_{m}
be a primitive
m^{\operatorname{th}}
root of unity. Let
F
be a number field, and define

V_{\emptyset}=\{x\in F^{\times}:(x)=J^{m}\text{ for some fractional ideal }J\},

where
(x)
is the fractional ideal generated by
x\in F^{\times}
. We define the
m
-governing field of

to be the field
F(\zeta_{m},\sqrt[m]{V_{\emptyset}}).

Theorem 1.3

(Splitting in number fields that depend on the prime)
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field and let
m\geq 3
be a prime such that
\gcd(m,w_{K})=1
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
, and let
p
be a prime that splits completely in the ring class field of
\mathcal{O}
and the
m
-governing field of

. Let

be the maximal
m
-elementary abelian extension of

if and only if

In particular, for

, among the primes

\mathcal{O}
and the

In this theorem, we are using implicitly that the density results on spin symbols remain valid when the class of primes considered is restricted to Chebotarev classes defined by Chebotarev conditions in abelian extensions of

. We expect that it is possible to prove the Weston–Zaurova conjecture for all power residues

by applying techniques from the joint distribution of spins from Koymans–Milovic

]

Below, we state a corollary of Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\ell
be a prime, and let

\rho:\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\longrightarrow\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})

be a Galois representation arising from the Galois action on
\ell^{\infty}
-torsion points of an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with complex multiplication by an imaginary quadratic field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-d})
where

. Let
S
be the smallest set of places containing
\ell
and the archimedean place of
\mathbb{Q}
such that
\rho
is unramified outside
S
. For primes
p\not\in S
, let
\operatorname{Frob}_{p}
be any lift of Frobenius to
\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p})
. Among the primes

splitting completely in the ring class field of
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(E)
,

\operatorname{trace}(\rho(\operatorname{Frob}_{p}))\in\mathbb{Z}

is a cube modulo
p
one third of the time.

Our method of proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a version of Vinogradov’s sieve extended to totally imaginary biquadratic fields. In
[
2
]
, Friedlander–Iwaniec–Mazur–Rubin introduced quadratic spin symbols of primes in totally real, cyclic number fields (satisfying some additional technical conditions) and proved equidistribution of such symbols by extending Vinogradov’s sieve to totally real, cyclic fields. In general, their results are conditional on standard conjectures on short character sums (denoted
C_{n}
in their paper where

is the degree of the totally real cyclic number field), and their results are only unconditional in the case
n=3
. In this paper, we extend the methods in
[
2
]
to
cubic
spin symbols over biquadratic, totally imaginary fields of degree 4 over
\mathbb{Q}
. It is natural to compare this to work of Merikoski
[
6
]
, although we should warn the reader that his spin symbol is not the same as ours.

Our main analytic accomplishment is to make our equidistribution results unconditional in this setting. To make our results unconditional, we follow a similar strategy strategy as employed previously by Koymans–Milovic
[
3
]
and Piccolo
[
8
]
. However, unlike the works
[
3
,
8
]
, we do not assume that our biquadratic field has class number
1
and we deal with cubic symbols instead of quartic symbols.

f(p,K^{(m)}(p)/\mathbb{Q})=1\iff f(\mathfrak{q},K^{(m)}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})/K)=1.

Proof.

Note that the compositum
K^{(m)}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})H_{K}^{(m)}
does not depend on the choice of
K^{(m)}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})/K
as it is the maximal
m
-elementary abelian extension of
K
unramified away from
\mathfrak{q}
. Since
p
is assumed to split completely in
H_{K}^{(m)}
, we conclude that
f(\mathfrak{q},K^{(m)}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})/K)
is independent of this choice.

Since
f(p,K/\mathbb{Q})=1
, we have
f(p,K^{(m)}(p)/\mathbb{Q})=f(\mathfrak{q},K^{(m)}(p)/K)
. Because
K^{(m)}(p)=K^{(m)}(\mathfrak{q})K^{(m)}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})H_{K}^{(m)}
and because
p
splits completely in
H_{K}^{(m)}
, we conclude that

\displaystyle f(p,K^{(m)}(p)/\mathbb{Q})=1

\displaystyle\iff f(\mathfrak{q},K^{(m)}(p)/K)=1

\displaystyle\iff f(\mathfrak{q},K^{(m)}(\mathfrak{q})/K)=1\text{ and }f(\mathfrak{q},K^{(m)}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})/K)=1.

(2.1)

Since
e(\mathfrak{q},K^{(m)}(\mathfrak{q})/K)=m
, the condition
f(\mathfrak{q},K^{(m)}(\mathfrak{q})/K)=1
is automatically satisfied. Removing it from the equivalence (
2.1
) gives the lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field and let
m\geq 3
be any odd integer such that
\gcd(m,w_{K})=1
. Set
M:=K(\zeta_{m})
. Let
\mathcal{O}
be an order in
K
. Let
p
be a prime that splits completely in the ring class field of
\mathcal{O}
and the

-governing field of

, and let

with

. Let
\mathfrak{q}=(\kappa)
,
\mathfrak{p}
a prime in
M
above
\mathfrak{q}
and

. Then we have for any choice of

f(\mathfrak{q},K^{(m)}(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})/K)=1\iff[\mathfrak{p}]=1.

Proof.

Let

be the full ray class group of modulus
\overline{\mathfrak{q}}
above
K
and let

be the full ray class field of modulus
\overline{\mathfrak{q}}
above
K
. If
\psi_{K(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})/K}
is the Artin reciprocity map, there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Gal}(K(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})/K)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})/G(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})^{m}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{K(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})/K}}

where the horizontal arrows are isomorphisms. Let
\operatorname{Cl}_{K}
be the class group of

. By Theorem 1.7, Chapter V, in

]
, there is a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{O}/\overline{\mathfrak{q}})^{\times}/\mathcal{O}^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G(\overline{\mathfrak{q}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Cl}_{K}}

Field lowering

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
K

3
. Assume that
L
is a quadratic extension of
K
such that
L
contains
\zeta_{3}
and
\mathfrak{p}
splits in

. Write
\sigma
for the non-trivial element of
\mathrm{Gal}(L/K)
. Then we have for all
\alpha\in O_{K}

\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{p}O_{L}}\right)_{L,3}=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{p}O_{K}}\right)_{K,3}^{2}&\textup{if }\sigma\textup{ fixes }\zeta_{3}\\
\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\alpha}&\textup{if }\sigma\textup{ does not fix }\zeta_{3}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{p}
splits in
L
, we may write
\mathfrak{p}O_{L}=\mathfrak{q}\sigma(\mathfrak{q})
for some prime
\mathfrak{q}
of

. This gives

\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{p}O_{L}}\right)_{L,3}=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{L,3}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sigma(\mathfrak{q})}\right)_{L,3}=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{L,3}\left(\frac{\sigma(\alpha)}{\sigma(\mathfrak{q})}\right)_{L,3}=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{L,3}\sigma\left(\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{L,3}\right),

where we used that
\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha
thanks to the assumption
\alpha\in O_{K}
. If
\sigma
fixes
\zeta_{3}
, then the above becomes

\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{L,3}\sigma\left(\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{L,3}\right)=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{L,3}^{2}=\left(\frac{\alpha}{\mathfrak{p}O_{K}}\right)_{K,3}^{2}.

If
\sigma
does not fix
\zeta_{3}
, then we get
\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\alpha}
by checking all possibilities for the cubic residue symbol

.

Lemma 3.3
.

3.4

Given an integral ideal

of

Using our fixed integral basis

of

Lemma 3.5

There exists a subset

(1)

\alpha\in O_{M}
, there exists a unique unit
v\in V
with
v\alpha\in\mathcal{D}
. Moreover, we have
\{u\in O_{M}^{\ast}:u\alpha\in\mathcal{D}\}=\{vt:t\in T\}
;

(2)

the set
\mathcal{D}(1)
has a
3
-Lipschitz parametrizable boundary. Furthermore, there exists a constant
C>0
such that the boundary of
\mathcal{D}(1)
intersects every line in at most
C
points;

(3)

there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
\alpha=a_{1}\eta_{1}+a_{2}\eta_{2}+a_{3}\eta_{3}+a_{4}\eta_{4}\in\mathcal{D}
(with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
), we have
|a_{i}|\leq C|N_{M/\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha)|^{1/4}
.

3.5
Vinogradov’s sieve

We will now state the main sieve that we will use. This sieve was originally developed by Vinogradov in the context of representing odd integers as sums of three primes. Recall that
M:=K(\zeta_{3})
. From now on we shall abbreviate
N_{M/\mathbb{Q}}
simply as
N
and our ideals will be ideals of
O_{M}
unless otherwise indicated.

In order to prove our later results, we will handle a slightly more general symbol than
[\mathfrak{p}]
. To this end, fix an ideal
\mathfrak{M}
of
M
and an invertible element
\mu\in O_{M}/\mathfrak{M}O_{M}
. We introduce the function
r_{i}(\mathfrak{a},\mu,\mathfrak{M})
for
i=1,\dots,h
, which is the indicator function of
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
having a generator
\alpha
satisfying
\alpha\equiv\mu\bmod\mathfrak{M}
; recall that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is one of our fixed representatives for
\mathrm{Cl}(M)
. We shall treat
\mu
and
\mathfrak{M}
as fixed throughout the paper, and we shall abbreviate
r_{i}(\mathfrak{a}):=r_{i}(\mathfrak{a},\mu,\mathfrak{M})
.

Proposition 3.6
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{a})\leq X\\
\mathfrak{m}\mid\mathfrak{a}\end{subarray}}r_{i}(\mathfrak{a})[\mathfrak{a}]\right|\leq CX^{1-\frac{1}{24}+\epsilon}

uniformly for all non-zero integral ideals
\mathfrak{m}
of
O_{M}
and all
X\geq 2
.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is postponed until Section
4
.
∎

Proposition 3.7
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\sum_{N(\mathfrak{m})\leq M}\sum_{N(\mathfrak{n})\leq N}\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}\beta_{\mathfrak{n}}r_{i}(\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n})[\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}]\right|\leq C(M+N)^{\frac{1}{24}}(MN)^{1-\frac{1}{24}+\epsilon}

uniformly for all
M,N\geq 2
and all sequences of complex numbers
|\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}|\leq 1
and
|\beta_{\mathfrak{n}}|\leq 1
.

Proof.

The proof of this proposition is postponed until Section
5
.
∎

Once Propositions
3.6
and
3.7
are proven, Vinogradov’s sieve for number fields gives the following result.

Theorem 3.8
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left|\sum_{N(\mathfrak{p})\leq X}r_{i}(\mathfrak{p})[\mathfrak{p}]\right|\leq CX^{1-\frac{1}{3136}+\epsilon}

for all
X\geq 2
.

Proof.

Propositions
3.6
and
3.7
together with Vinogradov’s sieve for number fields as presented in
[
2
, Proposition 5.2]
yield

\left|\sum_{N(\mathfrak{a})\leq X}r_{i}(\mathfrak{a})[\mathfrak{a}]\Lambda(\mathfrak{a})\right|\leq CX^{1-\frac{1}{3136}+\epsilon},

where
\Lambda
is the natural generalization of the von Mangoldt function to number fields. The theorem is then a direct consequence of partial summation.
∎

4
Sums of type I

This section is entirely devoted to the proof of Proposition
3.6
. Our proof follows among the same lines as
[
3
, Section 4]
and
[
2
, Section 6]
. From now on we fix a non-zero integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
O_{M}
. Let
\mathfrak{M}
be an ideal of
O_{M}
and let
\mu
be an element of
O_{M}
. Recall that we introduced the function
r_{i}(\mathfrak{a})
for
i=1,\dots,h
, which is the indicator function of
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{p}_{i}
having a generator
\alpha
satisfying
\alpha\equiv\mu\bmod\mathfrak{M}
(recall that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
is one of our fixed representatives for
\mathrm{Cl}(M)
). We set

F=2^{4}\cdot 3^{4}\cdot f\cdot N(\mathfrak{M})\cdot\Delta(M/\mathbb{Q}),

where
f
is defined in equation (
3.1
) and where
\Delta(M/\mathbb{Q})
is the absolute discriminant. Each non-zero ideal has exactly
6
or
12
generators in
\mathcal{D}
, which is exactly the size of the torsion subgroup
T
. Further splitting the sum into progressions modulo
F
, we unwrap the sum as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}N(\mathfrak{a})\leq X\\
\mathfrak{m}\mid\mathfrak{a}\end{subarray}}r_{i}(\mathfrak{a})[\mathfrak{a}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|T|}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{D}(XN(\mathfrak{p}_{i}))\\
\alpha\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{p}_{i}\end{subarray}}r_{i}((\alpha)/\mathfrak{p}_{i})[(\alpha)/\mathfrak{p}_{i}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|T|}\sum_{\rho\bmod F}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{D}(XN(\mathfrak{p}_{i}))\\
\alpha\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{p}_{i}\\
\alpha\equiv\rho\bmod F\end{subarray}}r_{i}((\alpha)/\mathfrak{p}_{i})[(\alpha)/\mathfrak{p}_{i}],

where our first identity is justified due to the change of variables
\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{p}_{i}=(\alpha)
. At this point we insert the identity

\displaystyle[(\alpha)/\mathfrak{p}_{i}]=[(\alpha)]\cdot\gamma_{\rho}

(4.1)

for all
\alpha\equiv\rho\bmod F
, where
\gamma_{\rho}
is some fixed number in
\{1,\zeta_{3},\zeta_{3}^{2},0\}
depending only on
\rho
and
i
. We also note that
r_{i}((\alpha)/\mathfrak{p}_{i})
depends only on
\alpha\bmod F
. Prompted by these calculations, we define for each
\rho\bmod F
and each ideal
\mathfrak{m}

A(X,\rho)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{D}(X)\\
\alpha\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{m}\\
\alpha\equiv\rho\bmod F\end{subarray}}[(\alpha)].

By the above manipulations it suffices to estimate each
A(X,\rho)
individually. Furthermore, we may assume that
\gcd(3,\rho)=(1)
as otherwise the symbol
[(\alpha)]
is constantly zero.

Recalling that
\eta_{1}=1
by convention and writing
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{Z}\eta_{2}+\mathbb{Z}\eta_{3}+\mathbb{Z}\eta_{4}
allows us to decompose
O_{M}=\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{M}
. Therefore every
\alpha\in O_{M}
can uniquely be written as

\alpha=a+\beta,\quad\quad a\in\mathbb{Z},\beta\in\mathbb{M}.

This transforms the sum
A(X,\rho)
as

A(X,\rho)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+\beta\in\mathcal{D}(X)\\
a+\beta\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{m}\\
a+\beta\equiv\rho\bmod F\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{\sigma(a+\beta)}{a+\beta}\right)_{M,3}\left(\frac{\sigma\tau(a+\beta)}{a+\beta}\right)_{M,3}.

Our aim is now to rewrite the cubic residue symbols. We have

\left(\frac{\sigma(a+\beta)}{a+\beta}\right)_{M,3}=\left(\frac{a+\sigma(\beta)}{a+\beta}\right)_{M,3}=\left(\frac{\sigma(\beta)-\beta}{a+\beta}\right)_{M,3},

and similarly for the other cubic residue symbol. If
\sigma(\beta)-\beta=0
or if
\sigma\tau(\beta)-\beta=0
, then our cubic residue symbols are constantly
0
and we may safely remove such
\beta
from consideration. In all other cases, we are thus incentivized to factor
\sigma(\beta)-\beta
and
\sigma\tau(\beta)-\beta
. Define
\mathfrak{c}
to be the largest divisor of
\sigma(\beta)-\beta
coprime to
F
and define
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
to be the largest divisor of
\sigma\tau(\beta)-\beta
coprime to
F
. After applying cubic reciprocity as presented in Proposition
3.1
and following the argument on
[
2
, p. 726]
, we obtain

\left(\frac{\sigma(\beta)-\beta}{a+\beta}\right)_{M,3}=\mu_{1}\cdot\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{c}}\right)_{M,3},\quad\quad\left(\frac{\sigma\tau(\beta)-\beta}{a+\beta}\right)_{M,3}=\mu_{2}\cdot\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}}\right)_{M,3}

for some numbers
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\{1,\zeta_{3},\zeta_{3}^{2},0\}
depending on
\rho
and
\beta
but not on
a
. We record our progress by applying the triangle inequality to deduce that

|A(X,\rho)|\leq\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{M}}|T(X,\rho,\beta)|,

where

T(X,\rho,\beta):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}\\
a+\beta\in\mathcal{D}(X)\\
a+\beta\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{m}\\
a+\beta\equiv\rho\bmod F\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{c}}\right)_{M,3}\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}}\right)_{M,3}.

We will treat
\beta
as fixed and work towards estimating each
T(X,\rho,\beta)
individually. It is at this stage that we bring the material from Subsection
3.3
into play. To this end, we remark that
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
is in fact the extension of an ideal of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3d})
. Here we use that
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
is coprime to
F
and thus the discriminant of
M
and that
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
divides an element of the shape
\sigma\tau(\beta)-\beta
. From now on we shall view
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
as an ideal of
O_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3d})}
. We factor it as

\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}O_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3d})}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathfrak{p}_{i}^{e_{i}}

for prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
of
O_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3d})}
, so that

\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}O_{M}}\right)_{M,3}=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{p}_{i}O_{M}}\right)_{M,3}^{e_{i}}.

Observe that the ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
are coprime to
F
by construction and hence unramified in the extension
M/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3d})
. We now claim that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{p}_{i}O_{M}}\right)_{M,3}=\mathbf{1}_{\gcd(a+\beta,\mathfrak{p}_{i})=(1)}.

(4.2)

Since
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
divides
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
and since
\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}
divides
\sigma\tau(\beta)-\beta
, it follows that
\sigma\tau(a+\beta)\equiv a+\beta\bmod\mathfrak{p}_{i}O_{M}
. Therefore Lemma
3.4
allows us to replace
a+\beta
by some
\beta^{\prime}\in O_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3d})}
. Then Lemma
3.2
gives the claim in case
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
splits in
M
. Instead suppose that
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
stays inert in
M
. If we write
p_{i}
for the unique prime of
\mathbb{Z}
below
\mathfrak{p}_{i}
, we certainly find that
p\equiv 2\bmod 3
. In this case the claim is a consequence of Lemma
3.3
.

Having proved our claim, we perform a similar operation on the other cubic residue symbol. This residue symbol shall not disappear, but we will be able to “lower it” to
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
. This is the crucial step in making our results unconditional compared to
[
2
]
despite working in a field of degree
4
.

Arguing as before, we may view
\mathfrak{c}
as the extension of an ideal of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
, and by abuse of notation we shall view
\mathfrak{c}
as an ideal of
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
from now on. In this case we factor
\mathfrak{c}\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]=\mathfrak{g}\mathfrak{q}
, where
\mathfrak{q}
has squarefree norm,
\mathfrak{g}
has squarefull norm and
\gcd(N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{g}),N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{q}))=1
. Furthermore, observe that
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{g}
are both coprime to
F
, so all prime divisors of
\mathfrak{q}
and
\mathfrak{g}
stay unramified in
M/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
. So far we have shown that

\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{c}O_{M}}\right)_{M,3}=\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{g}O_{M}}\right)_{M,3}\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{q}O_{M}}\right)_{M,3}.

Lemma
3.4
and the Chinese remainder theorem allow us to replace
\beta
by some
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
. Combining Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
yields

\displaystyle\left(\frac{a+\beta^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{q}O_{M}}\right)_{M,3}=\left(\frac{a+\beta^{\prime}}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}],3}^{2},

(4.3)

as any prime
\mathfrak{p}
in
\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}]
that stays inert in
M
satisfies
p\equiv 1\bmod 3
(writing
p:=\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathbb{Z}
). Finally, as
q:=N_{\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{q})
is squarefree, the Chinese remainder theorem guarantees the existence of some rational integer
b
with
\beta^{\prime}\equiv b\bmod\mathfrak{q}
.

Thanks to equations (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we have thus arrived at

T(X,\rho,\beta)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}\\
a+\beta\in\mathcal{D}(X)\\
a+\beta\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{m}\\
a+\beta\equiv\rho\bmod F\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{a+\beta}{\mathfrak{g}O_{M}}\right)_{M,3}\cdot\left(\frac{a+b}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}],3}^{2}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\gcd(a+\beta,\mathfrak{c}^{\prime})=(1)}.

Following the proof on
[
3
]
, we fix
a
modulo the radical
g_{0}
of
g
and detect the condition
\mathbf{1}_{\gcd(a+\beta,\mathfrak{c}^{\prime})=(1)}
using the Mobius function. Continuing in the footsteps of
[
3
]
, we apply the triangle inequality

|T(X,\rho,\beta)|\leq\sum_{a_{0}\bmod g_{0}}\sum_{\mathfrak{d}\mid\mathfrak{c}^{\prime}O_{M}}|T(X,\rho,\beta,a_{0},\mathfrak{d})|,

where

T(X,\rho,\beta,a_{0},\mathfrak{d}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathbb{Z}\\
a+\beta\in\mathcal{D}(X)\\
a+\beta\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{m}\\
a+\beta\equiv\rho\bmod F\\
a\equiv a_{0}\bmod g_{0}\\
a+\beta\equiv 0\bmod\mathfrak{d}\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{a+b}{\mathfrak{q}}\right)_{\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{3}],3}^{2}.

Since squaring a cubic residue symbol is the same as conjugating it, we may now remove the square factor without changing the absolute value of
T(X,\rho,\beta,a_{0},\mathfrak{d})
. After doing so, the sum
T(X,\rho,\beta,a_{0},\mathfrak{d})
has the same shape as
[
3
, Eq. (4.2)]
. Having successfully applied our field lowering technique, the remainder of the proof is identical to
[
3
, p. 7422-7424]
.

Sums of type II

This section is entirely devoted to the proof of Proposition
3.7
. This will be relatively straightforward, as the required tools have already been developed in the literature. This was started in
[
3
, Proposition 3.6]
, which is unfortunately not applicable here as the symbol is required to take values in
\{\pm 1,0\}
. Smith
[
11
]
developed a very general large sieve that is completely explicit in its dependence on the underlying number field. Another interesting recent result in this direction can be found in recent work of Santens
[
9
]
. Here we shall appeal to
[
5
, Proposition 4.3]
.

In the notation of
[
5
]
, we shall use
[
5
, Proposition 4.3]
with the integer
M
on
[
5
, p. 11]
equal to
F
, with the number field
K
equal to our
M=K(\zeta_{3})
, with
n
equal to
4
, with
\ell=3
, with
t_{1}=t_{2}=1
, with the function
\gamma
equal to

\gamma(w,z):=\left(\frac{\sigma(w)}{z}\right)_{M,3}\left(\frac{\sigma(z)}{w}\right)_{M,3}\left(\frac{\sigma\tau(w)}{z}\right)_{M,3}\left(\frac{\sigma\tau(z)}{w}\right)_{M,3}

and with
A_{\text{bad}}
the set of squarefull integers. Then we can take
C_{1}
to be an absolute constant (
C_{1}=100
suffices) and
C_{2}=1/2
. We now check that this data satisfies the conditions (P1)-(P4) on
[
5
, p. 11-12]
.

Condition (P1) is a consequence of Proposition
3.1
. Condition (P2) follows from the definition of the cubic residue symbol. Condition (P4) is readily verified by counting squarefull integers in the usual way. Finally, (P3) follows from the Chinese remainder theorem and orthogonality of characters. Applying
[
5
, Proposition 4.3]
, we exhibit, for any given
\epsilon>0
, the existence of a constant
C(\epsilon)>0
satisfying the inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathcal{D}(X)\\
w\equiv\delta_{1}\bmod F\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}z\in\mathcal{D}(Y)\\
z\equiv\delta_{2}\bmod F\end{subarray}}\alpha_{w}\beta_{z}\gamma(w,z)\leq C(\epsilon)(X+Y)^{\frac{1}{24}}(XY)^{1-\frac{1}{24}+\epsilon}

(5.1)

for all
\alpha_{w},\beta_{z}
of magnitude bounded by
1
.

With this calculated, recall that our aim is to demonstrate the bound

\left|\sum_{N(\mathfrak{m})\leq M}\sum_{N(\mathfrak{n})\leq N}\alpha_{\mathfrak{m}}\beta_{\mathfrak{n}}r_{i}(\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n})[\mathfrak{m}\mathfrak{n}]\right|\leq C(M+N)^{\frac{1}{24}}(MN)^{1-\frac{1}{24}+\epsilon}.

Let us first deal with the case that
\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{n}
are both principal. Observe that every principal ideal has exactly
|T|
generators in
\mathcal{D}
by Lemma
3.5
, so our sum becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|T|^{2}}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{D}(M)}\sum_{z\in\mathcal{D}(N)}\alpha_{w}\beta_{z}r_{i}(wz)[wz].

(5.2)

We note that
r_{i}(wz)
depends only on
wz\bmod F
and that

[(wz)]=\gamma(w,z)\left(\frac{\sigma(w)}{w}\right)_{M,3}\left(\frac{\sigma(z)}{z}\right)_{M,3}\left(\frac{\sigma\tau(w)}{w}\right)_{M,3}\left(\frac{\sigma\tau(z)}{z}\right)_{M,3}.

Absorbing the last four cubic residue symbols in the coefficients
\alpha_{w},\beta_{z}
and splitting the sum over congruence classes modulo

shows that we can bound the expression (
5.2
) by at most
F^{8}
sums appearing in the inequality (
5.1
).

This finishes the proof of Proposition
3.7
when the ideals
\mathfrak{m}
,
\mathfrak{n}
of that proposition are restricted to principal ideals. The general case is handled by appealing to (
4.1
) and reducing to the case of principal ideals.

Proof of Corollary

For any elliptic curve

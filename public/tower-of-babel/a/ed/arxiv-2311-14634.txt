New Bounds on the Local and Global Edge-length Ratio of Planar Graphs
Emilio Di Giacomo, Walter Didimo, Giuseppe Liotta, Henk Meijer, Fabrizio Montecchiani, Stephen Wismath

Abstract
The local edge-length ratio of a planar straight-line drawing Γ \Gamma is the largest ratio between the lengths of any pair of edges of Γ \Gamma that share a common vertex. The global edge-length ratio of Γ \Gamma is the largest ratio between the lengths of any pair of edges of Γ \Gamma . The local (global) edge-length ratio of a planar graph is the infimum over all local (global) edge-length ratios of its planar straight-line drawings. We show that there exist planar graphs with n n vertices whose local edge-length ratio is Ω ⁡ ( n ) \Omega(\sqrt{n}) . We then show a technique to establish upper bounds on the global (and hence local) edge-length ratio of planar graphs and apply it to Halin graphs and to other families of graphs having outerplanarity two.

Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph and let

. We denote by

\displaystyle\rho_{g}(\Gamma)=\max_{(u,v),(z,w)\in E}\frac{|(u,v)|_{\Gamma}}{|(z,w)|_{\Gamma}},\quad\quad\rho_{\ell}(\Gamma)=\max_{(u,v),(v,w)\in E}\frac{|(u,v)|_{\Gamma}}{|(v,w)|_{\Gamma}}.

The
global edge-length ratio of

\rho_{g}(G)
, is the infimum of
\rho_{g}(\Gamma)
over all planar straight-line drawings

of
G

\rho_{g}(G)=\inf_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{D}(G)}\rho_{g}(\Gamma)
, where

is the set of all planar straight-line drawing of

, is the infimum of
\rho_{\ell}(\Gamma)
over all planar straight-line drawings

of
G
, i.e.,
\rho_{\ell}(G)=\inf_{\Gamma\in\mathcal{D}(G)}\rho_{\ell}(\Gamma)
.

Eades and Wormald
[

prove that deciding when biconnected planar graphs have edge-length ratio one is NP-hard, and Cabello et al.
[

extend this result to triconnected instances.
These results have motivated further studies on the existence of graph families for which the local or the global edge length ratio does not depend on the size of the graph. Concerning lower bounds, Borrazzo and Frati

[

, while

[
4
]
prove that the local edge-length ratio of planar 2-trees is at most

.

Note that the lower bound on the global edge-length ratio of the

Preliminaries

Given a graph
G
a

of
G
maps every vertex of
G
to a distinct point in the plane, and every edge

of
G
to a Jordan arc connecting the two points representing the end-vertices of

and not passing through any other vertex. A drawing is

if its edges are straight-line segments, and it is

if no two edges intersect, except possibly at a common endpoint. A
planar graph
is a graph that admits a planar drawing. A planar drawing subdivides the plane into topologically connected regions, called
faces
. The infinite region is called the

; every other face is called an
inner face
. A
planar embedding
of a planar graph is an equivalence class of topologically equivalent planar drawings of

. A planar embedding of a connected planar graph can be described by the circular order of the edges around each vertex together with the choice of the outer face. A planar graph with a given planar embedding is a
plane graph
. The cycle delimiting the outer face of a plane graph is called the
outer boundary
of the graph.

A planar drawing is
outerplanar
if all its vertices belong to the outer boundary,
and
2
-outerplanar
if removing the vertices of the outer boundary we obtain an outerplanar graph. A graph is
2
-outerplanar
(
outerplanar
) if it admits a
2
-outerplanar drawing (outerplanar drawing).

A
k
-span weakly level planar drawing
(
k-SWLP drawing
, for short)
\Gamma
is a planar drawing whose vertices are drawn on a set of horizontal equispaced lines, called
levels
and whose edges are drawn as straight-line segments each intersecting at most
k+1
levels. To fix the ideas in the following descriptions we assume that the levels are numbered from top to bottom. An edge that intersects
k+1
levels has
span

k
. If no edge of
\Gamma
has span
0
,
\Gamma
is a
k
-span level planar drawing (k-SLP drawing, for short)
. A graph is
k
-SWPL
if it admits a
k
-SWLP drawing and it is
k
-SPL
if it admits a
k
-SLP drawing.

Given a degree-2 vertex
v
of a graph
G
with incident edges
(u,v)
and
(v,w)
, to
smooth
v
means to remove
v
and its incident edges from
G
and replace them with the edge
(u,w)
.

Lemma 2

Let
\Gamma
be an embedding preserving planar straight-line drawing of the plane

Proof

We start by proving that

Notice that, if

Upper Bounds

In this section we prove various upper bounds on the global edge-length ratio using a common approach based on level planar drawings.

Lemma 3

If
G
is a
k
-SLP graph for some
k\geq 1
, then
\rho_{g}(G)\leq k
.

Proof

Let
\Gamma
be a
k
-SLP drawing of

such that the distance between two consecutive levels is one. Assume that
\Gamma
is a straight-line drawing. If not, it is possible to achieve this condition
[
10

Arbitrarily chosen a value of
\varepsilon>0

\Gamma
so that its width is
\varepsilon
. After this squeezing, for every edge

we have
1\leq|e|_{\Gamma}\leq k+\varepsilon
. It follows that
\rho_{g}(\Gamma)\leq k+\varepsilon
and
\rho_{g}(G)\leq k
.

∎

The next lemma follows from the fact that a

Lemma 4

If

Proof

It is easy to see that a

5
Concluding Remarks and Open Problems

Our lower bound holds for planar graphs with treewidth three. On the other hand, the upper bounds are for families with treewidth larger than two. Since partial 2-trees have local edge-length ratio at most four
[
4
]
, we find it interesting to further study families of planar graphs with constant treewidth larger than two and constant local edge-length ratio.

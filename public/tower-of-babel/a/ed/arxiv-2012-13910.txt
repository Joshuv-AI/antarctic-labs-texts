Correlation and fluctuation in chain of active particles
Prashant Singh, Anupam Kundu

Abstract
We study motion of tagged particles in a harmonic chain of active particles. We consider three models of active particle dynamics - run and tumble particle, active Ornstein-Uhlenbeck particle and active Brownian particle. We investigate the variance, auto correlation, covariance and unequal time cross correlation of two tagged particles. For all three models, we observe that the mean squared displacement undergoes a crossover from the super-diffusive ∼ t μ \sim t^{\mu} scaling for t ≪ τ A t\ll\tau_{A} ( τ A \tau_{A} being the time scale arising due to the activity) to the sub-diffusive ∼ t \sim\sqrt{t} scaling for t ≫ τ A t\gg\tau_{A} , where μ = 3 2 \mu=\frac{3}{2} for RTP, μ = 5 2 \mu=\frac{5}{2} for AOUP. For the x x and y y -coordinates of ABPs we get μ = 7 2 \mu=\frac{7}{2} and μ = 5 2 \mu=\frac{5}{2} respectively. We show that these crossover behaviours in each case can be described by appropriate crossover function that connects these two scaling regimes. We compute these crossover functions explicitly. In addition, we also find that the equal and unequal time auto and cross correlations obey interesting scaling forms in the appropriate limits of the observation time t t . The associated scaling functions are rigorously derived in all cases. All our analytical results are well supported by the numerical simulations.

Our aim in this paper is two-fold. Firstly, we aim to study the statistical features of the dynamics of the tagged particle for active systems and compare and contrast them with that of the passive systems. For this purpose, we will focus on the MSD, covariance and the equal and unequal time correlations.
The second question that we address is - How do these quantities vary for different active particle models? It is known that at times larger than the “activity” time scale, all models converge to the Brownian motion with an effective diffusion constant. Question is - What happens when the time is comparable to (or smaller than) the “activity” time scale? We answer this question in the context of the above mentioned three models, RTP, AOUP and ABP.

Models and summary of the results

We consider a harmonic ring of
N
active particles with spring constant
K
. Denoting the position of the
\alpha
-th particle at time
t
by
\vec{r}_{\alpha}(t)=(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})
where
\alpha=0,1,2,....N-1
. We consider over-damped motion of the particles described by the following evolution equations

\displaystyle\frac{d\vec{r}_{\alpha}}{dt}=-K\left(2\vec{r}_{\alpha}-\vec{r}_{\alpha+1}-\vec{r}_{\alpha-1}\right)+\vec{F}_{\alpha}^{A}(t),~~~~~\text{ for }\alpha=1,...,N,

(1)

with the periodic boundary condition
\vec{r}_{\alpha+N}(t)=\vec{r}_{\alpha}(t)
.
For simplicity, we have taken the friction constant to be unity. In this paper, we consider RTP and AOUP in one dimension and ABP in two dimension. Therefore, the position is denoted by
\vec{r}_{\alpha}(t)=(x_{\alpha}(t),0)
for RTP and AOUP and by
\vec{r}_{\alpha}(t)=\left(x_{\alpha}(t),y_{\alpha}(t)\right)
for ABP. Note that the first term in the R.H.S. of Eqs. (
1
) arises due to the harmonic interactions between successive particles with spring constant
K
. The second term
\vec{F}_{\alpha}^{A}(t)
represents the noises from some non-equilibrium source which gives rise to the active nature of the particles. The form and the properties of the noises are different for different models of active particle dynamics. For RTP/AOUP, we denote the active noises by
\vec{F}_{\alpha}^{A}(t)=(F_{\alpha}^{RTP/AOUP}(t)~,~0)
and for ABP by
\vec{F}_{\alpha}^{A}(t)=\vec{F}_{\alpha}^{ABP}(t)=\left(\xi_{\alpha}(t),\psi_{\alpha}(t)\right)
.

For AOUP, the properties of the noise
F_{\alpha}^{AOUP}(t)
are described by

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}^{AOUP}(t)=\lambda_{\alpha}(t),~~~\forall\alpha~~~~\text{ with},

\displaystyle\frac{d\lambda_{\alpha}}{dt}=-\gamma\lambda_{\alpha}+\sqrt{2D}\eta_{\alpha}(t),

(2)

where
\eta_{\alpha}(t)
is the Gaussian white noise of mean
\langle\eta_{\alpha}(t)\rangle_{c}=0
and variance
\langle\eta_{\alpha}(t)\eta_{\beta}(t^{\prime})\rangle_{c}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}~\delta(t-t^{\prime})
. Here the parameters
\gamma>0
and
D>0
in Eq. (
2
). For the RTP model, the noise
F_{\alpha}^{A}(t)
(written as
F_{\alpha}^{RTP}(t)
) takes the form

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}^{RTP}(t)

\displaystyle=v_{0}~\sigma_{\alpha}(t),~~~~\text{ for }\alpha=0,1,..,N-1,

(3)

where
v_{0}~(>0)
is the speed of the particle and
\sigma_{\alpha}(t)
is the dichotomous noise that alternates between
\pm 1
at a constant rate
\zeta
. The noise has mean
\langle\sigma_{\alpha}(t)\rangle_{c}=0
and unequal time correlation given by

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{\alpha}(t)\sigma_{\beta}(t^{\prime})\rangle_{c}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}~\text{exp}\left(-2\zeta|t-t^{\prime}|\right).

(4)

Finally for ABP, the form of noise term
\vec{F}_{\alpha}^{A}(t)
written as
\vec{F}^{ABP}_{\alpha}(t)=\left(\xi_{\alpha}(t),\psi_{\alpha}(t)\right)
is

\displaystyle\xi_{\alpha}(t)=v_{A}\cos\phi_{\alpha}(t),~~~\psi_{\alpha}(t)=v_{A}\sin\phi_{\alpha}(t),

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{d\phi_{\alpha}}{dt}=\sqrt{2D_{rot}}~\eta_{\alpha}(t).

(6)

Here
v_{A}~(>0)
is the speed of the particle and
D_{rot}~(>0)
is the rotational diffusion constant which also sets the timescale for the actvity. Also, here
\eta_{\alpha}(t)
is the Gaussian white noise with
\langle\eta_{\alpha}(t)\rangle=0
and
\langle\eta_{\alpha}(t)\eta_{\beta}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}~\delta(t-t^{\prime})
. To proceed further, we consider that for ABP, all particles are initially oriented along
x
-axis, i.e.
\phi_{\alpha}(0)=0
for all
\alpha
. For this choice of the initial orientation, it was shown in
[
51
]
that the mean and correlations of
\xi_{\alpha}(t)
and
\psi_{\alpha}(t)
are given by

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{\alpha}(t)\rangle=v_{A}e^{-D_{rot}t},~~~\langle\psi_{\alpha}(t)\rangle=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{\alpha}(t)\xi_{\beta}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{v_{A}^{2}}{2}\left[e^{-D_{rot}|t-t^{\prime}|}+e^{-D_{rot}\{t_{1}+t_{2}+2\text{min}(t_{1},t_{2})\}}\right],

(8)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\alpha}(t)\psi_{\beta}(t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\frac{v_{A}^{2}}{2}\left[e^{-D_{rot}|t-t^{\prime}|}-e^{-D_{rot}\{t_{1}+t_{2}+2\text{min}(t_{1},t_{2})\}}\right],

(9)

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{\alpha}(t)\psi_{\beta}(t^{\prime})\rangle=0.

(10)

In this paper, we study the position statistics of two tagged particles for the three models mentioned earlier. More specifically, we will study the equal and unequal time correlation functions between these two particles and their mean squared displacement and covariance.

\displaystyle\begin{split}&C^{1}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})=\langle x_{\alpha_{1}}(t_{1})x_{\alpha_{2}}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}=\langle x_{\alpha_{1}}(t_{1})x_{\alpha_{2}}(t_{2})\rangle-\langle x_{\alpha_{1}}(t_{1})\rangle\langle x_{\alpha_{2}}(t_{2})\rangle,\\

(11)

Once again, we emphasise that
\vec{r}_{\alpha}(t)=(x_{\alpha}(t),0)
for RTP and AOUP, and
\vec{r}_{\alpha}(t)=\left(x_{\alpha}(t),y_{\alpha}(t)\right)
for ABP. Consequently for RTP and AOUP, one has only the correlation
C^{1}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})

C^{1/2}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})

\xi_{\alpha}(t)
and
\psi_{\beta}(t)
are mutually uncorrelated [see Eq. (
10
)], the cross correlations among the
x
and
y
-coordinates for the particles in the ABP chain are zero

\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})y_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}=0
. We study
C^{1/2}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})
in different asymptotic regimes of time for three models of active particle chains of length
N
. In all models the particles are connected to each other by harmonic springs of strength
K
and each particle is driven by independent active noises
\vec{F}^{A}(t)
which have different statistical properties. There are three time scales in this problem, namely, (i) the interaction time scale
\tau_{K}=\frac{1}{K}
(ii) the activity time scale
\tau_{A}
and (iii) the relaxation time scale
\tau_{N}
. The interaction time scale
\tau_{K}
is coming from interaction among the particles. The activity time scale
\tau_{A}
arises due to the activity present in the system. The activity time scale
\tau_{A}=\frac{1}{\zeta}
for RTP,
\tau_{A}=\frac{1}{\gamma}
for AOUP and
\tau_{A}=\frac{1}{D_{rot}}
for ABP particles. Finally,
\tau_{N}=\frac{N^{2}}{K}
represents the relaxation time scale on a ring of size
N
. Throughout this work, we will consider
\tau_{K}\ll\tau_{A}\ll\tau_{N}
(see Fig.
2
). As illustrated in this work, with this order of time scales we find various interesting scaling structures and scaling functions for different position correlations. The other case of
\tau_{A}\ll\tau_{K}\ll\tau_{N}
does not give rise to these scaling structures and only reproduces the results of the passive particles.

More precisely, in this paper we study the two point correlation
C^{1/2}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})
in the following three regimes (i)
t_{1},t_{2}\ll\tau_{K}

(ii)
\tau_{K}\ll t_{1},t_{2}\ll\tau_{N}
with
N\to\infty
(equivalently
\tau_{N}\to\infty
) (iii)
t_{1},t_{2}\sim\tau_{N}
for finite but large
N
. The MSD of a tagged particle, say
\alpha
th, is obtained from

\sigma^{2}_{1/2}(t)=C^{1/2}_{\alpha,\alpha}(t,t)
. Due to homogeneity and periodic boundary of the chain, the MSD of a tagged particle is independent of the tag of the particle. Covariance between the positions of two particles can similarly be obtained from
C_{0,\beta}^{1}(t)=\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}
and
C_{0,\beta}^{2}(t)=\langle y_{0}(t)y_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}
which are again same as
\langle x_{\alpha}(t)x_{\alpha+\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}
and
\langle y_{\alpha}(t)y_{\alpha+\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}
respectively due to translational symmetry of the problem. We also study position auto correlation
C_{\alpha,\alpha}^{1/2}(t_{1},t_{2})
and finally un-equal time correlation
C_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}^{1/2}(t_{1},t_{2})
. We consider two cases: (a)
N\to\infty
in which
\tau_{N}\to\infty
and (b) finite but large
N
in which
\tau_{N}
is finite. At times much smaller than
\tau_{K}
, the statistical properties of one particle are independent of other particles as in such short time they do not realise the presence of other particle. In this time regime, the correlation behaves as
C_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}^{1/2}(t_{1},t_{2})\sim C_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{1}}^{1/2}(t_{1},t_{2})\delta_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}
. On the other hand, in case (a) we observe that the above four quantities exhibit interesting scaling behaviours for times much larger than
\tau_{K}
. For RTP and AOUP dynamics we get

\displaystyle C_{\alpha,\alpha}^{1}(t,t)

\displaystyle\propto t^{3/2}~\mathcal{T}^{RT/OU}\left(\frac{t}{\tau_{A}}\right),~~~~~~~~~~~~\tau_{K}\ll t\ll\tau_{N}

Writing the Langevin Eqs. (

\displaystyle\frac{d\bar{x}_{s}}{dt}=-a_{s}\bar{x}_{s}+\bar{F}_{s}^{A}(t)~,

(23)

one now gets rid of the cross terms. Also here

\displaystyle\bar{x}_{s}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{N-1}e^{-\frac{2\pi is}{N}\alpha}~x_{\alpha}(t),

(24)

\displaystyle\bar{F}^{A}_{s}(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\alpha=0}^{N-1}e^{-\frac{2\pi is}{N}\alpha}~F_{\alpha}^{A}(t),

(25)

\displaystyle a_{s}=4K\sin^{2}\left(\frac{\pi s}{N}\right).

(26)

It is now straightforward to solve Eq. (
23
) for
\bar{x}_{s}(t)
to get

\displaystyle\bar{x}_{s}(t)=e^{-a_{s}t}~\bar{x}_{s}(0)+e^{-a_{s}t}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau~e^{a_{s}\tau}~\bar{F}_{s}^{A}(\tau),

(27)

using which in Eq. (
21
) we get the displacement
x_{\alpha}(t)
of the
\alpha^{\text{th}}
particle.

Remember we are interested to compute the two point correlation defined in Eq. (
11
) which after some manipulations can be written as

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha_{1}}(t_{1})x_{\alpha_{2}}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}=\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}e^{\frac{2\pi is}{N}\alpha_{1}}e^{\frac{2\pi is^{\prime}}{N}\alpha_{2}}\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t)\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t)\rangle_{c}

(28)

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t)\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t)\rangle_{c}&={\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t)\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t)\rangle-\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t)\rangle\langle\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t)\rangle}\\
&=e^{-(a_{s}+a_{s^{\prime}})t}\int_{0}^{t}d\tau_{1}~d\tau_{2}~e^{a_{s}\tau_{1}+a_{s^{\prime}}\tau_{2}}~\langle\bar{F}_{s}^{A}(\tau_{1})\bar{F}_{s^{\prime}}^{A*}(\tau_{2})\rangle_{c},\end{split}

(29)

where
\bar{x}_{s}^{*}(t)
is the complex conjugate of
\bar{x}_{s}(t)
and
\langle...\rangle
denotes average over noise
\bar{\sigma}_{s}(t)
for RTP. We substitute
F^{A}_{\alpha}(t)
from Eq. (
3
) for RTP in Eq. (
25
) to get
\bar{F}^{A}_{s}(t)
. Inserting this expression in Eq. (
29
) and performing the integration over
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
gives
\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t)\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t)\rangle_{c}
. For continuity of the presentation, we have relegated the details of this calculation to
A
and present only the final results here. The final expressions for the correlations read

\displaystyle\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t)\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}

\displaystyle=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{N}~\mathcal{G}(2\zeta,a_{s},t),

(30)

where
\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}
is the Kronecker delta which takes value
1
if
s=s^{\prime}
and
0
otherwise. Also here,
\mathcal{G}(a,b,t)
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(a,b,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{b\left[1+e^{-2bt}-2e^{-(a+b)t}\right]-a(1-e^{-2bt})}{b(b^{2}-a^{2})}.

(31)

3.1
MSD for RTP chain

To compute the MSD, we use Eq. (
24
) to write
x_{\alpha}(t)
in terms of
\bar{x}_{s}(t)
and then substitute the correlation in Eq. (
30
) to get

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}

\displaystyle=\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle^{RTP}-\left(\langle x_{\alpha}(t)\rangle^{RTP}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\langle|\bar{x}_{s}(t)|^{2}\rangle_{c}^{RTP},

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{N}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{G}(2\zeta,a_{s},t),

(32)

with
a_{s}
and
\mathcal{G}(2\zeta,a_{s},t)
given respectively in Eqs. (
26
) and (
31
). Note that, due to the translational symmetry in
\alpha
, the R.H.S. of
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
is independent of
\alpha
. To gain more understanding of
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
, we first analyze the expression in the limit
N\to\infty
. In this limit, the summation in the R.H.S. of Eq. (
32
) can be changed to an integral as
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{s}\to\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq
where
q=\frac{2\pi s}{N}
. Changing the summation to integration in Eq. (
32
), we get

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq~\mathcal{G}\left(2\zeta,b_{q},t\right),

(33)

\displaystyle\text{where}~~b_{q}

\displaystyle=4K\sin^{2}(q/2).

(34)

As mentioned before (see discussion below Eq. (
11
)), we are interested in
\tau_{K}\ll\tau_{A}
in this work with
\tau_{N}\to\infty
as
N\to\infty
. Remember in this case
\tau_{A}=\frac{1}{\zeta}
. We first look at the mean squared displacement for
t\ll\tau_{K}
followed by a discussion for
t\gg\tau_{K}
.

Since
\tau_{K}
is the smallest time scale in the model, the observation time
t
is smaller than all the time scales (see Fig.
2
). We can, then, consider
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}
in Eq. (
33
) upto leading order in
t
. Expanding
\mathcal{G}(2\zeta,b_{q},t)
in Eq. (
31
) in small
t
, we find that
\mathcal{G}(2\zeta,b_{q},t)\simeq t^{2}
. Inserting this in the expression of
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}
and performing the integration over
q
gives

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq v_{0}^{2}t^{2},~~~~~~~\text{for }t\ll\tau_{K}.

(35)

For small
t
, the particles do not feel the presence of the neighbouring particles and move ballistically with speed
v_{0}
. This observation essentially asserts the form of
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
in Eq. (
35
).

We now look at the other limit when
t\gg\tau_{K}
. Substituing the expression of
\mathcal{G}\left(2\zeta,b_{q},t\right)
and
b_{q}
from Eqs. (
31
) and (
34
) respectively in
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
in Eq. (
33
), we find integrals of the form
\sim\int dq~h(q)~\text{exp}\left[-Kt\sin^{2}(q/2)\right]
where
h(q)
is some function of
q
. For
Kt\to\infty
, such integrals will be dominated by the small values of
q
. Therefore, we approximate
b_{q}\simeq Kq^{2}
in Eq. (
33
). We have explicitly computed
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
for
t\gg\tau_{K}
in
B
. The final approximate expression obeys the scaling form

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2}{K}}~~\mathcal{T}^{RT}\left(\zeta t\right),

(36)

where the scaling function
\mathcal{T}^{RT}(y)
is given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{RT}(y)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dw~\mathcal{G}\left(2y,\frac{w^{2}}{2},1\right),~~~~~~~~\text{for }t\gg\tau_{K}

(37)

with
\mathcal{G}\left(2y,\frac{w^{2}}{2},1\right)
given in Eq. (
31
). In Fig.
3
(a), we have plotted the scaling function
\mathcal{T}^{RT}(y)
and compared it with the numerical simulation. We observe an excellent match between them. Expanding
\mathcal{G}\left(2y,\frac{w^{2}}{2},1\right)
in various limits of
y
, it is easy to see that
\mathcal{T}^{RT}(y)
has the following asymptotic forms

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{RT}(y)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}{3}(2-\sqrt{2})-{\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}}{15}\left(8-5\sqrt{2}\right)y},~~~~\text{as }y\to 0,

(38)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2y}+{O\left(\frac{1}{y^{2}}\right)},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to\infty.

(39)

Using these asymptotic forms in the expression of
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
in Eq. (
36
), we observe that it crosses from
t^{3/2}
for
t\ll\frac{1}{\zeta}
to
\sqrt{t}
for
t\gg\frac{1}{\zeta}

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{4(\sqrt{2}-1)}{3\sqrt{\pi K}}v_{0}^{2}t^{\frac{3}{2}}+{O\left(t^{5/2}\right)},~~~~~~~~\text{for }t\ll\frac{1}{\zeta},

(40)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\zeta}\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\pi K}}+{O\left(t^{-1/2}\right)},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }t\gg\frac{1}{\zeta}.

(41)

For
t\gg\frac{1}{\zeta}
, the RTPs behave like Brownian particles with an effective diffusion constant
D_{R}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\zeta}
. For
N\to\infty
, the motion of the tagged particle is effectively like single file diffusion which gives rise to the
\sim\sqrt{t}
scaling for the MSD. This behaviour of the MSD for persistent random walkers in one dimensional lattice with hardcore interactions was obtained in
[
72
,
75
]
. On the other hand, for
\tau_{K}\ll t\ll\frac{1}{\zeta}
, we obtain that the MSD for RTPs scales super-diffusively as
\sim t^{3/2}
in contrast to the
t^{2}
scaling for non-interacting RTPs. We emphasise that the
t^{3/2}
scaling of the MSD arises due to the interplay of the activity of the particles and interactions between them. Due to the interaction with other particles, the motion of the tagged particle is hemmed and we consequently get
t^{3/2}
scaling for the MSD. The
t^{3/2}
behaviour for the MSD was also obtained in
[
76
]
even though the crossover function was not obtained.

In Fig.
3
(b), we have plotted these asymptotic forms and compared with the same obtained from the numerical simulations. We clearly observe two scaling regimes of
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
with respect to
t
and an excellent match of the analytic results in Eqs. (
40
) and (
41
) with the numerical simulations. Interestingly, the scaling form of the MSD in Eq. (
36
) was also conjectured for RTPs with hardcore interactions in
[
75
]
however the form of the scaling function was not provided. We have derived this form explicitly in our simple setting of harmonic interaction.

(a) Comparision of the scaling function
\Omega^{RT}(y)
in Eq. (
47
) for run and tumble particles when
t\ll\frac{1}{\zeta}
. Solid orange line is the result of theory in Eq. (
47
) while the symbols correspond to simulation at different times. We have chosen

and
v_{0}=1
. (b) Plot of the scaling function

in Eq. (

) (shown by red solid line) obtained for RTPs when
t\gg\frac{1}{\zeta}
and comparision with the numerical simulations for three different values of

(shown by symbols). We have chosen
K=2,~\zeta=2
and
v_{0}=1
. For both (a) and (b), simulation is done with
N=200
particles on a ring.

3.2
Covariance for RTP chain

We next look at the covariance for the chain of RTPs which is obtained by putting
t_{1}=t_{2}
in
C^{1}_{\alpha,\beta}(t_{1},t_{2})
in Eq. (
11
). In the previous section, we saw that the MSD possesses interesting scaling behaviours with repect to
t
depending on where the observation time
t
lies. Question is - How does
C^{1}_{\alpha,\beta}(t,t)=\langle x_{\alpha}(t)x_{\alpha+\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}
behave for different regimes of
t
? Before answering this question, we note that due to the translational symmetry with respect to
\alpha
,
\langle x_{\alpha}(t)x_{\alpha+\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}
will be independent of
\alpha
. Hence without loss of generality we take
\alpha=0
.

Proceeding further, we use the definition of Fourier transform in Eq. (
21
) to write
x_{\alpha}(t)
in terms of
\bar{x}_{s}(t)
which finally gives the correlation function as

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}e^{\frac{2\pi is\beta}{N}}\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t)\bar{x}^{*}_{s^{\prime}}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}.

(42)

Inserting
\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t)\bar{x}^{*}_{s^{\prime}}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
from Eq. (
30
) in Eq. (
42
), we find that the imaginary part of
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}
vanishes and we get

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{N}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi s\beta}{N}\right)\mathcal{G}(2\zeta,a_{s},t),

(43)

where
a_{s}
and
\mathcal{G}(2\zeta,a_{s},t)
are given by Eqs. (
26
) and (
31
) respectively. We first analyse Eq. (
43
) in the limit
N\to\infty
. The finite
N
case will be discussed in sec.
3.5
. For large
N
, we can change the summation to integration on the R.H.S. of Eq. (
43
). Changing
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{s}\to\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq
where
q=\frac{2\pi s}{N}
, the expression of
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
now reads

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq~\cos\left(q\beta\right)~\mathcal{G}(2\zeta,b_{q},t),

(44)

where
b_{q}=4K\sin^{2}\left(q/2\right)
.

As done for the MSD, we study correlations for different limits of
t
. For
t\ll\tau_{K}
, as explained before, the motion of the two particles will be uncorrelated, hence
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq v_{0}^{2}t^{2}~\delta_{0,\beta}
.
In the opposite limit
t\gg\tau_{K}
they get nontrivially correlated. As in the case of the MSD, here also, we get terms like
\sim\int dq~h(q)~\text{exp}\left[-Kt\sin^{2}(q/2)\right]
when we insert
\mathcal{G}(2\zeta,b_{q},t)
from Eq. (
31
) in Eq. (
44
). In the limit
Kt\to\infty
, such integrals will be dominated by the small values of
q
where one can approximate
b_{q}\simeq Kq^{2}
. The details of the calculation of
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
for
t\gg\tau_{K}
is relegated to
C
. The final expression reads

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{4\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2t^{3}}{K}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dw~\cos\left(\frac{w\beta}{\sqrt{2Kt}}\right)~\mathcal{G}\left(2\zeta t,\frac{w^{2}}{2},1\right),

(45)

To gain more insights, we consider the expression in various limits of
\zeta t
. Let us first consider
\zeta t\to 0
limit for which we use Eq. (
31
) to approximate
\mathcal{G}\left(2\zeta t\to 0,\frac{w^{2}}{2},1\right)\simeq 4\left(\frac{1-e^{-\frac{w^{2}}{2}}}{w^{2}}\right)^{2}
. Substituting this form of
\mathcal{G}\left(2\zeta t,\frac{w^{2}}{2},1\right)
in the R.H.S. of Eq. (
45
) and performing the integration over
w
, we find that
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
obeys the scaling form

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{\pi}\sqrt{\frac{2t^{3}}{K}}~\Omega^{RT}\left(\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2Kt}}\right),~~~~~~~\text{for }\tau_{K}\ll t\ll\frac{1}{\zeta},

(46)

where the scaling function
\Omega^{RT}(y)
is given as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Omega^{RT}(y)=&\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{6}\left[2e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4}}\left(4+y^{2}\right)-2\sqrt{2}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}\left(2+y^{2}\right)\right.\\
&\left.-\sqrt{\pi}y(6+y^{2})~\text{Erfc}\left(\frac{y}{2}\right)+2\sqrt{\pi}y(3+y^{2})~\text{Erfc}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right].\end{split}

(47)

In Figure
4
(a) we have plotted
\Omega^{RT}(y)
and compared with the results of numerical simulations for three different times. For all values of
t
in range
\tau_{K}\ll t\ll\frac{1}{\zeta}
, the simulation data collapse over our theoretical result in Eq. (
47
). The scaling function
\Omega^{RT}(y)
has the following asymptotic forms:

\displaystyle\Omega^{RT}(y)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}{3}(2-\sqrt{2}){-\sqrt{\pi}\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)y^{2}+O(y^{3})},~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to 0,

(48)

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\frac{2\sqrt{\pi}}{y^{4}}+O\left(y^{-6}\right)\right)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4}},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to\infty.

(49)

Inserting the asymptotic form of
\Omega^{RT}(y)
for large
y
, one finds that
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
in Eq. (
46
) has faster than exponential decay for large
\beta
(
\gg\sqrt{Kt}
) with a decay length
l_{d}=2\sqrt{2Kt}
. On the other hand, for
\beta\to 0
,
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
correctly reduces to the MSD obtained in Eq. (
40
).

Let us now consider the opposite limit
\zeta t\to\infty
for
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
in Eq. (
45
). In this limit, from Eq. (
31
), we get
\mathcal{G}\left(2\zeta t\to\infty,\frac{w^{2}}{2},1\right)\simeq\frac{1-e^{-w^{2}}}{\zeta tw^{2}}
which can be used in Eq. (
45
) to obtain the scaling form

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq D_{R}\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\pi K}}~\mathcal{C}\left(\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2Kt}}\right),~~~~~~~\text{for }t\gg\frac{1}{\zeta},

(50)

where
D_{R}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\zeta}
and the scaling function
\mathcal{C}(y)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(y)=e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4}}-\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}y~\text{Erfc}\left(\frac{y}{2}\right).

(51)

Same scaling form for the correlation function has been observed in the context of single file problems in random average process
[
20
,
21
,
18
]
and in symmetric exclusion process
[
22
,
23
]
.
In Figure
4
(b), we have plotted and compared
\mathcal{C}(y)
with the numerical simulations for three different values of
t
for
\zeta=2
. We observe an excellent agreement of the theoretical result with the simulation results for all
t
. The scaling function
\mathcal{C}(y)
has the following asymptotic forms;

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(y)

\displaystyle\simeq 1-{\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}y},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to 0,

(52)

\displaystyle\simeq{\left(\frac{2}{y^{2}}+O\left(y^{-4}\right)\right)e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{4}},}~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to\infty.

(53)

Again, for
\beta\to 0
, the scaling form in Eq. (
50
) correctly reduces to the MSD in Eq. (
41
) for
t\gg\frac{1}{\zeta}
. Also,
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
has faster than exponential decay for large
\beta
(
\gg\sqrt{Kt}
) with a decay length
l_{d}=2\sqrt{2Kt}
.

) and (

) for

3.3
Position auto correlation for RTP chain

We next study the position auto correlation
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
between the positions of a tagged particle (say
\alpha
th) at times
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
. The translational symmetry in
\alpha
makes
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
independent of
\alpha
. Also without loss of generality, we assume
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
throughout our analysis. To proceed further, we insert the unequal time correlations of
\bar{F}^{RTP}_{s}(t)
derived in Eqs. (
167
) in
\bar{x}_{s}(t)
in Eq. (
27
) to yield

\displaystyle\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t_{1})\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}~=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}~\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{N}\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta,a_{s},t_{1},t_{2}\right),

(54)

where
a_{s}
is given in Eq. (
26
) and the function
\mathcal{H}(a,b,t_{1},t_{2})
is

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}(a,b,t_{1},t_{2})=&-\frac{a}{b}\frac{e^{-b|t_{1}-t_{2}|}-e^{-b(t_{1}+t_{2})}}{(b^{2}-a^{2})}+\frac{e^{-a|t_{1}-t_{2}|}-e^{-(bt_{1}+at_{2})}}{(b^{2}-a^{2})}\\
&~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-\frac{e^{-(at_{1}+bt_{2})}-e^{-b(t_{1}+t_{2})}}{(b^{2}-a^{2})}\end{split}

(55)

The correlation function
\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t_{1})\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
can be easily translated to
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
by writing
x_{\alpha}(t)
in terms of
\bar{x}_{s}(t)
from Eq. (
21
). Finally, we get

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime},s=0}^{N-1}\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t_{1})\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP},

(56)

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{N}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta,a_{s},t_{1},t_{2}\right),

(57)

As we show later, for this case also, one can obtain various interesting scaling forms and scaling functions in the limit
N\to\infty
. Changing the summation in the R.H.S. of Eq. (
57
) to the integration as
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{s}\to\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq
in Eq. (
57
) with
q=\frac{2\pi s}{N}
, we get

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq~\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta,b_{q},t_{1},t_{2}\right).

(58)

Here
b_{q}=4K\sin^{2}(q/2)
. We now look at this expression in various limits of
t_{1}
with respect to
\tau_{K}
keeping the ratio
\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}
fixed. We again emphasise that we consider
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
.

We begin with the case when
t_{1}\ll\tau_{K}
and
t_{2}\ll\tau_{K}
while keeping the ratio
\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}
is fixed. Recall that
\tau_{K}
is the smallest time scale in the model (see Fig.
2
) which means that
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
are smaller than all the time scales present in the model in this case. At this small time scale, the particle moves ballistically and independently. As a result, we expect
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
which can be easily shown by putting
\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta,b_{q},t_{1},t_{2}\right)\simeq t_{1}t_{2}
in Eq. (
58
) and then performing the integration over
q
.

with the scaling function

\displaystyle\simeq D_{R}\sqrt{\frac{2t_{1}}{\pi K}}+{O\left(\sqrt{t_{2}-t_{1}}\right)},~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }t_{2}\to t_{1},

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{D_{R}t_{1}}{\sqrt{\pi Kt_{2}}}+{O\left(\frac{t_{1}^{3}}{t_{2}^{5/2}}\right)},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }t_{2}\gg t_{1}.

\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
for fixed

\sim t_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
for

.

. This is illustrated in Fig.

(\beta=0)

, attains a maximum value and then starts decreasing again. To understand this behaviour, it is instructive to look at the asymptotic forms of
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
when

increases they realise the presence of other particles through harmonic springs and as a result their motion start getting correlated. However at very very large time the particles behaves effectively like Brownian particles and because of the finiteness of the ring they eventually reach an equilibrium state with an effective temperature where their motion again become less correlated. Hence for some intermediate

,
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
will exhibit a maximum value.

3.4
Unequal Time Position Correlations For RTP chain

This section deals with the unequal time correlations
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha+\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
for the chain of RTPs. We again consider
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
. Also, due to the translational symmetry in
\alpha
,
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha+\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
will be independent of
\alpha
because of which we set
\alpha=0
without loss of generality. Starting from the Fourier transfrom in Eq. (
21
), one gets the correlation in terms of
\bar{x}_{s}(t_{1})
as

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s^{\prime},s=0}^{N-1}e^{-\frac{2\pi is\beta}{N}}\langle\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}(t_{1})\bar{x}_{s}^{*}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP},

(72)

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{N}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi s\beta}{N}\right)\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta,a_{s},t_{1},t_{2}\right).

(73)

While going from first line to the second line, we have substituted
\langle\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}(t_{1})\bar{x}_{s}^{*}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}

from Eq. (
54
) with
\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta,a_{s},t_{1},t_{2}\right)
given in Eq. (
55
). Once again, we analyse this expression for
N\to\infty
first and then the finite
N
case. Changing the summation in the R.H.S. to an integration for
N\to\infty
gives

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq~\cos(q\beta)~\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta,b_{q},t_{1},t_{2}\right),

(74)

where
b_{q}=4K\sin(q/2)
. Here also, one can compute correlations for
t_{1}\ll\tau_{K}
and
t_{1}\gg\tau_{K}
limits of
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
keeping the ratio
\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}
fixed. It is easy to realise that for
t_{1}\ll\tau_{K}
the correlation would be given by

\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq v_{0}^{2}t_{1}t_{2}\delta_{\beta,0}
. This can be easily proved in a similar way as done for the previous cases.

In the opposite limit
t_{1}\gg\tau_{K}
, as observed earlier, it will be convenient to approximate
b_{q}\simeq Kq^{2}
. Inserting
\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta,b_{q},t_{1},t_{2}\right)
from Eq. (
55
) in Eq. (
74
) along with
b_{q}\simeq Kq^{2}
and finally taking
Kt_{1}\to\infty
keeping
\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}
fixed gives (see discussion above Eq. (
59
))

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{t_{1}^{3}}{K}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dw~\cos\left(\frac{w\beta}{\sqrt{Kt_{1}}}\right)~\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta t_{1},w^{2},1,\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right),~~~\text{for }t_{1}\gg\tau_{K}.

(75)

Next we analyse this expression in various limits of
\zeta t_{1}
. In the limit
\zeta t_{1}\to 0
, we get
\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta t_{1},w^{2},1,\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right)\simeq e^{-w^{2}(1+t_{r})}\frac{(e^{t_{r}w^{2}}-1)(e^{w^{2}}-1)}{w^{4}}
where
t_{r}=t_{2}/t_{1}
. Putting this expression in Eq. (
75
) and performing the integration over
w
results in the following scaling form:

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{t_{1}^{3}}{K}}~\mathcal{P}^{RT}\left(\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2Kt_{1}}},\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right),~~~~\text{for }t_{1}<t_{2}\ll\frac{1}{\zeta},

(76)

where the scaling function
\mathcal{P}^{RT}(y,t_{r})
is given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{RT}(y,t_{r})=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{3}\left[2\sqrt{1+t_{r}}(2+2t_{r}+y^{2})e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+t_{r})}}-2(2+y^{2})e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2}}-2\sqrt{t_{r}}(2t_{r}+y^{2})e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2t_{r}}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.~~~~~~~~~~+\sqrt{2\pi}(3+y^{2})y~\text{Erfc}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+\sqrt{2\pi}(3t_{r}+y^{2})y~\text{Erfc}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{2t_{r}}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\left.-\sqrt{2\pi}(3+3t_{r}+y^{2})y~\text{Erfc}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{2(1+t_{r})}}\right)\right].

(77)

For
t_{r}\to 1~{i.e.}~\left(t_{1}\to t_{2}\right)
, it is easy to see
P^{RT}(y,t_{r}\to 1)=2\sqrt{2}\Omega^{RT}(y)
and correctly recovers the scaling relation for
\langle x_{0}(t_{2})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
in Eq. (
46
). Also for
y\to 0
, we find
\mathcal{P}^{RT}(y\to 0,t_{r})\simeq\mathcal{L}^{RT}(t_{r})
(illustrated later) and we recover the scaling form of Eq. (
60
) for the mean squared auto fluctuations. In Fig.
7
(a), we have plotted the scaling function
\mathcal{P}^{RT}(y,t_{r})
and compared against the same obtained from the numerical simulation for three different values of
t_{r}=\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}
. We observe an excellent match between them. To see the decay of
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
with respect to
\beta
, we look at the asymptotic forms of the scaling function
\mathcal{P}^{RT}(y,t_{r})
in various limits of
y
. The asymptotic forms read

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{RT}(y,t_{r})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}}{3}\left[(1+t_{r})^{3/2}-1-t_{r}^{3/2}\right]+{2\sqrt{\pi}\left(\sqrt{1+t_{r}}-1-\sqrt{t_{r}}\right)y^{2}},~~\text{as }y\to 0,

\displaystyle\simeq\left(\frac{4\sqrt{\pi}\left(1+t_{r}\right)^{7/2}}{y^{4}}+{O\left(y^{-6}\right)}\right)\text{exp}\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+t_{r})}\right),~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to\infty.

(78)

Using these expressions, it is easy to show that
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
for large
\beta
has a faster than exponential decay with a decay length
l_{d}=2\sqrt{K(t_{1}+t_{2})}
.

Let us now look at
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
of Eq. (
75
) in the other limit
\zeta t_{1}\to\infty
. In this limit, we obtain
\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta t_{1},w^{2},1,\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right)\simeq\frac{e^{-w^{2}(t_{r}-1)}-e^{-w^{2}(t_{r}+1)}}{w^{2}}
which we insert in Eq. (
75
) to obtain that
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
obeys the scaling form

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq D_{R}\sqrt{\frac{2t_{1}}{\pi K}}~\mathcal{Q}\left(\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2Kt_{1}}},\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}\right),~~~~~~\text{for }t_{1}\gg\frac{1}{\zeta},

(79)

where
D_{R}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\gamma}
and the scaling function
\mathcal{Q}(y,t_{r})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(y,t_{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+t_{r})}}\left[\sqrt{t_{r}+1}-\sqrt{t_{r}-1}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{(t_{r}^{2}-1)}}\right]

\displaystyle~~~~+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}y\left[\text{Erf}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{2(t_{r}+1)}}\right)\right.\left.-\text{Erf}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{2(t_{r}-1)}}\right)\right].

(80)

For
t_{r}\to 1~{i.e.}~\left(t_{1}\to t_{2}\right)
,
\mathcal{Q}(y,t_{r}\to 1)=\mathcal{C}(y)
in Eq. (
51
) and we correctly recover the scaling form in Eq. (
50
). In Fig.
7
, we have presented a comparision of
\mathcal{Q}(y,t_{r})
with the same obtained from numerical simulation for different values of
t_{1}
and
t_{2}

\mathcal{Q}(y,t_{r})
in various limits of
y

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}(y,t_{r})

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\sqrt{t_{r}+1}-\sqrt{t_{r}-1}\right]-{\frac{y^{2}}{2\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{r}-1}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{t_{r}+1}}\right)},~~~\text{as }y\to 0,

(81)

\displaystyle\simeq\left[\frac{(1+t_{r})^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\sqrt{2}y^{2}}+O\left(y^{-4}\right)\right]\text{exp}\left(-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1+t_{r})}\right),~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to\infty.

(82)

For
\beta=0
,
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
reduces to the expression of the auto fluctuation in Eq. (
66
). Also for large

(

decays spatially over the length scale

Interestingly, for a given

3.5
Two point correlation in RTP chain for large but finite

:

In this section we compute two point correlation between to tagged particle positions for large but finite
N
such that
\tau_{N}
is not infinite.
We start with the exact expression of
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
in Eq. (

).
Substituting
\mathcal{H}\left(2\zeta,a_{s},t_{1},t_{2}\right)
from Eq. (

) in Eq. (

) and performing some manipulation, we finally obtain the following expression upto leading order in

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{2D_{R}t_{1}}{N}\\

From this expression, we notice that
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
possesses the scaling form

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{2D_{R}N}{K}~\mathcal{W}\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}},\frac{\beta}{N},\frac{Kt_{1}}{N^{2}}\right)

(84)

where the scaling function
\mathcal{W}(t_{r},z,y)
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(t_{r},z,y)=y+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\cos\left(2\pi sz\right)\frac{\sinh\left(4\pi^{2}s^{2}y\right)}{s^{2}}e^{-4\pi^{2}s^{2}t_{r}y}

(85)

For large
y
, we approximate
\mathcal{W}(t_{r},z,y)\simeq y
which yields

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{2D_{R}t_{1}}{N},~~~~~~~~\text{for }Kt_{1}\gg N^{2}.

(86)

On the other hand for
Kt_{1}\ll N^{2}
,
Kt_{2}\ll N^{2}
keeping
t_{2}/t_{1}
fixed, the summation in Eq. (
85
) can be converted to an integral and performing the integration we recover the scaling result obtained in Eq. (

) for
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
. Taking various limits of the arguments in the expression of the two-point correlation in Eqs. (

) and (
85
) one can the MSD, covariance and auto correlations.

3.5.1
MSD:

To get MSD, we put
\beta=0
and
t_{1}=t_{2}=t
in Eqs. (
84
-
85
) and get

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{2D_{R}N}{K}~\Phi\left(\frac{Kt}{N^{2}}\right),

(87)

where
D_{R}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\zeta}
and the scaling function
\Phi(y)
is given by

\displaystyle\Phi(y)=y+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}~\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-e^{-8\pi^{2}s^{2}y}}{s^{2}}.

(88)

In Figure
8
(a), we have compared the theoretical result for
\Phi(y)
with the results of the numerical simulations of RTP for three different values of
N
. We observe an excellent agreement between them for all values of
N
. To obtain the asymptotic forms of the MSD, we look at the various limits of
\Phi(y)
. For
y\to\infty
, the first term in Eq. (
88
) dominates which gives
\Phi(y\to\infty)\simeq y
. On the other hand, for
y\to 0
, the second term dominates and gives
\Phi(y\to 0)\sim\sqrt{y}
. The approximate expressions read

\displaystyle\Phi(y)

\displaystyle\simeq\sqrt{\frac{y}{2\pi}}~,~~~~~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to 0,

(89)

\displaystyle\simeq y+{\frac{1}{24}}~,~~~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to\infty.

(90)

Using these asymptotic expressions in Eq. (
87
) for
\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
, it is easy to show that there is a crossover from
\sim\sqrt{t}
behaviour to the
\sim t
behaviour at
t\sim\mathcal{O}\left(N^{2}\right)
for the MSD.

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}

\displaystyle\simeq D_{R}\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\pi K}},~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }t\ll\frac{N^{2}}{K},

(91)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2D_{R}}{N}t+O\left(t^{0}\right)~,~~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }t\gg\frac{N^{2}}{K}.

(92)

Recall that for
t
greater than the activity time scale
\tau_{A}
, the dynamics of the
RTP
is effectively described by that of the Brownian particle with an effective diffusion constant
D_{R}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\zeta}
. Therefore, the scaling form obtained in Eq. (
87
) will remain valid for the harmonic chain of Brownian particles in an one dimensional ring. We close this section by remarking that a similar scaling form as in Eqs. (
87
,
88
) was obtained for the mean position of the driven tracer in the random average process
[
20
]
.

3.5.2
Covariance:

To get the covariance, we put
t_{1}=t_{2}=t
in Eqs. (
84
-
85
). We find

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{2D_{R}N}{K}~\Psi\left(\frac{\beta}{N},\frac{Kt}{N^{2}}\right),

(93)

with
D_{R}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\zeta}
and the scaling function
\Psi(z,y)
defined as

\displaystyle\Psi(z,y)=y+\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\cos\left(2\pi sz\right)\frac{1-e^{-8\pi^{2}s^{2}y}}{s^{2}}.

(94)

For
y>>1
, we approximate
\Psi(z,y)\simeq y
using which in Eq. (
93
) gives

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{2D_{R}t}{N},~~~~~~\text{for }Kt\gg N^{2}.

(95)

For
Kt\gg N^{2}
, the tagged particle has interacted with all the
N-1
particles. This fact essentially asserts the
\beta
independence of Eq. (
95
). Let us now look at the opposite limit
Kt\ll N^{2}
for which the second term in the R.H.S. of Eq. (
94
) dominates and one recovers the scaling results for
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}^{RTP}
in Eq. (
50
) for
t\gg\frac{1}{\zeta}
. This can also be verified by changing the summation in Eq. (
94
) to integration and explicitly performing the integration. We end this section by re-emphasising that for
t\gg\frac{1}{\zeta}
, the run and tumble particles behave effectively like the Brownian particles with effective diffusion constant
D_{R}=\frac{v_{0}^{2}}{2\zeta}
. Therefore, the analytic results obtained for the correlation function in Eqs. (
50
) and (
93
) will remain valid also for the harmonic chain of the Brownian particles.

3.5.3
Auto-correlation:

Putting
\beta=0
in Eqs. (
84
-
85
) one finds that the auto-correlation satisfies the following scaling form

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{2D_{R}N}{K}~\mathcal{R}\left(\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}},\frac{Kt_{1}}{N^{2}}\right),

(96)

where the scaling function
\mathcal{R}(t_{r},z)
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(t_{r},z)=z+\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sinh\left(4\pi^{2}s^{2}z\right)}{s^{2}}e^{-4\pi^{2}s^{2}t_{r}z}.

(97)

For
t_{2}\to t_{1}
, the scaling form in Eq. (
96
) correctly reduces to that of the MSD in Eq. (
87
). Also for
z>>1
but fixed
t_{r}
,
\mathcal{R}(t_{r},z)\simeq z
which when inserted in Eq. (
96
) gives

\displaystyle\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{RTP}\simeq\frac{2D_{R}t_{1}}{N},~~~~~~\text{for }Kt_{1}\gg N^{2}.

(98)

On the other hand, in the opposite limit
Kt_{1}\ll N^{2}
and
t_{2}/t_{1}
fixed, the second term in the R.H.S. of Eq. (
97
) dominates as observed before. Analysing this sum by converting to an integral and carrying it out one recovers the scaling result obtained in Eq. (
66
) and (
67
).

4
MSD, COVARIANCE AND TWO POINT CORRELATION FOR AOUP CHAIN

In this section, we investigate the correlations in the positions of the tagged particles for the AOUP chain. To this end, we use the correlation
\langle\bar{F}_{s}^{AOUP}(\tau_{1})\bar{F}_{s}^{AOUP*}(\tau_{2})\rangle_{c}
in Eq. (
170
) to write the unequal time correlation for the Fourier variables
\bar{x}_{s}(t)
as

\displaystyle\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t_{1})\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t_{2})\rangle^{AOUP}_{c}=\delta_{s,s^{\prime}}\frac{D}{\gamma N}~\mathcal{H}_{1}\left(\gamma,a_{s},t_{1},t_{2}\right),

(99)

where
a_{s}
is in Eq. (
26
) and
\mathcal{H}_{1}(a,b,t_{1},t_{2})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}(a,b,t_{1},t_{2})=\mathcal{H}(a,b,t_{1},t_{2})-\frac{\left(e^{-bt_{1}}-e^{-at_{1}}\right)\left(e^{-bt_{2}}-e^{-at_{2}}\right)}{(b-a)^{2}}

(100)

with
\mathcal{H}(a,b,t_{1},t_{2})
given in Eq. (
55
). Here also, we take
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
without loss of generality. Proceeding further, we use
\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t_{1})\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}^{*}(t_{2})\rangle^{AOUP}_{c}
in Eq. (
99
) to write the unequal time correlation as

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{AOUP}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s,s^{\prime}=0}^{N-1}e^{-\frac{2i\pi\beta s}{N}}\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t_{1})\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{AOUP},

(101)

\displaystyle=\frac{D}{\gamma N}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\cos\left(\frac{2\pi s\beta}{N}\right)\mathcal{H}_{1}\left(\gamma,a_{s},t_{1},t_{2}\right).

(102)

In going to the second line, we substitute
\langle\bar{x}_{s}(t_{1})\bar{x}_{s^{\prime}}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{AOUP}
from Eq. (
99
). Note that for
\beta=0
and
t_{1}=t_{2}
, Eq. (
102
) reduces to the MSD of the tagged particle. On the other hand, for
\beta\neq 0
and
t_{1}=t_{2}
, it reduces to the covariance of the positions of the tagged particles. Similarly, for
\beta=0
and
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
, Eq. (
102
) becomes the position auto correlation. In what follows, we look at
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{AOUP}
and use it to study the MSD, covariance and position auto correlation by suitably changing
\beta,~t_{1}
and
t_{2}
.

As seen for RTP, the correlation function
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{AOUP}
possesses various interesting scaling behaviours depending on where the observation time
t
lies. To decipher these scaling forms, we analyse Eq. (
102
) in the limit
N\to\infty
for which we change the summation in the R.H.S. to the integration and rewrite

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{AOUP}\simeq\frac{D}{2\pi\gamma}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}dq\cos\left(q\beta\right)~\mathcal{H}_{1}\left(\gamma,b_{q},t_{1},t_{2}\right),

(103)

where
b_{q}=4K\sin^{2}\left(q/2\right)
. In conjunction with the previous case of RTP, we analyse this expression for (i)
t_{1}\ll\tau_{k},~t_{2}\ll\tau_{K}
and (ii)
t_{1}\gg\tau_{K},~t_{2}\gg\tau_{K}
keeping
\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}
fixed.

4.2
Case II:

MSD:

We first look at the MSD
\langle x_{0}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{ABP}

\beta=0
and

in Eq. (
105

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{AOUP}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{Dt^{\frac{3}{2}}}{2\pi\gamma\sqrt{K}}~\mathcal{T}^{OU}\left(\gamma t\right),~~~~~~~~\text{for }t\gg\tau_{K},

(106)

where the scaling function
\mathcal{T}^{OU}(y)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{OU}(y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}~dw~\mathcal{H}_{1}\left(y,w^{2},1,1\right).

(107)

In Figure

\mathcal{T}^{OU}(y)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{OU}(y)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{32\sqrt{\pi}}{15}\left(\sqrt{2}-1\right)y-{\frac{32\sqrt{\pi}}{105}\left(8\sqrt{2}-9\right)y^{2}},~~~~~\text{as }y\to 0,

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2\sqrt{2\pi}}{y}+O(y^{-2}),~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{as }y\to\infty.

(108)

Using these asymptotic forms, we find that there is crossover from
t^{\frac{5}{2}}
to
\sqrt{t}
at

as

\displaystyle\langle x_{0}^{2}(t)\rangle_{c}^{AOUP}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{16(\sqrt{2}-1)D}{15\sqrt{\pi K}}t^{\frac{5}{2}}+O\left(t^{\frac{7}{2}}\right),~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }t\ll\frac{1}{\gamma},

(109)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{D}{\gamma^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{2t}{\pi K}}+O\left(t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right),~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\text{for }t\gg\frac{1}{\gamma}.

(110)

for AOUP which is different than
\sim t^{\frac{3}{2}}

40
). Again, this scaling arises due to the interplay of the activity and interactions between the particles. The caging effect due to the interactions causes the scaling of the MSD to change from
t^{3}
to
t^{\frac{5}{2}}
. On the other hand, for
t\gg\tau_{A}
, both models give
\sim\sqrt{t}
scaling for the MSD. In Figure

(b), we have illustrated this cross-over behaviour from
t^{5/2}
to
t^{1/2}
by comparing them against the same obtained from numerical simulation. As seen in Figure
9
(b), the asymptotic results of Eqs. (
109
) and (
110
) are consistent with the numerical simulation. Finally, we allude that a similar scaling form as in Eq. (
107
) was conjectured in
[
75
]
for the AOUPs with hadrcore interaction even though exact form of the scaling function was not obtained. Our consideration of the simple model of harmonic chain has given both the scaling form and the scaling function.

Coming back to
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}^{AOUP}

6
Conclusions

To summarize, we have studied a chain of
N
active particles with nearest neighbour quadratic interaction. We considered three models of active particles, namely, run and tumble particle, active Ornstein-Uhlenbeck particle and active Brownian particle. While the first two models are considered in one dimension, the ABP has been studied in two dimension. For all models, there exists three time scales - (i)
\tau_{K}=\frac{1}{K}
due to the interaction among particles, (ii)
\tau_{A}=\frac{1}{\zeta}~/\frac{1}{\gamma}~/\frac{1}{D_{rot}}
due to the activity of RTP / AOUP / ABP. and (iii)
\tau_{N}=\frac{N^{2}}{K}
due to the finiteness of the ring (note that
\tau_{K}\ll\tau_{A}\ll\tau_{N}
as shown in Fig.
2
). We investigated various statistical quantities for the position of the tagged particles which were shown to exhibit interesting scaling structures depending on where the observation time
t
lies. We emphasise that using our simple and analytically tractable model, we were able to re-derive many features which are seen in particles with non-trivial interactions.

We first looked at the MSD of the tagged particle’s position. For all models, there is a crossover of the MSD from super-diffusive scaling
(\sim t^{\mu})
for
t\ll\tau_{A}
to the sub-diffusive scaling
(\sim\sqrt{t})
for
t\gg\tau_{A}
with
\mu=\frac{3}{2}
for RTP,
\mu=\frac{5}{2}
for AOUP,
\mu=\frac{7}{2}~\left(=\frac{5}{2}\right)
for
x~(y)
-coordinate of ABP. We analytically computed the crossover function that connects these two distinct scaling regimes in Eqs. (
37
) and (
107
) for RTP and AOUP respectively and in Eqs. (
139
) and (
140
) for ABP. Note that
t^{\mu}
scaling of the MSD arises due to the interplay of the activity and caging effect due to the surrounding particles. Furthermore, we also studied the MSD for
t\sim\tau_{N}
and showed that it exhibits crossover from
\sqrt{t}
scaling for
t\ll\tau_{N}
to the diffusive scaling for
t\gg\tau_{N}
for all models. Here also, we explicitly computed the corresponding crossover function in Eq. (
88
). Such crossover behaviour for the MSD of active particles with hardcore repulsive interactions was predicted in
[
75
]
which we have rigorously derived in the simple setting of harmonic chain.

Next we studied the equal time two-point correlation function
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}
for the two models. It was shown that
\langle x_{0}(t)x_{\beta}(t)\rangle_{c}
exhibits scaling forms with respect to
y=\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2Kt}}
with distinct scaling functions
\Omega^{RT}(y)
,
\Omega^{OU}(y)
and
\Omega^{AB}_{1/2}(y)
which were derived in Eqs. (
47
), (
116
), (
159
) and (
160
) respectively. On the other hand, all three models give rise to the same scaling form (as expected) for
t\gg\tau_{A}
with the corresponding scaling function
\mathcal{C}(y)
given in Eq. (
51
). In addition to these extreme limits, we also computed the correlation function when
t\sim\tau_{A}
.

We have also investigated the mean squared auto fluctuations
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
with
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
. Keeping the ratio
t_{r}=\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}
fixed, we analysed the auto fluctuation for two different limits for
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
- (A)
t_{1}\ll\tau_{A}
,
t_{2}\ll\tau_{A}
and (B)
t_{1}\gg\tau_{A}
,
t_{2}\gg\tau_{A}
. For case (A), the three models exhibit distinct scaling functions
\mathcal{L}^{RT}(t_{r})
,
\mathcal{L}^{OU}(t_{r})
and
\mathcal{L}^{AB}_{1/2}(t_{r})
which we have computed in Eqs. (
61
), (
113
), (
155
) and (
156
) respectively. However for case (B), all models converge to the same scaling function
\mathcal{M}(t_{r})
in Eq. (
67
) for the auto correlation. We also showed that the auto correlation has a non-monotonic dependence on
t_{2}
in which
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
first increases with
t_{2}
, reaches a maximum value and then starts decreasing again. This non-monotonic nature of
\langle x_{\alpha}(t_{1})x_{\alpha}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
is again the signature of the activity of the particles.

Finally, we studied the unequal time correlation function
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
(
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
) which also shows various scaling structures depending on where
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
lie. For case (A),
\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
displays scaling form with respect to
t_{r}=\frac{t_{2}}{t_{1}}
and

\mathcal{P}^{RT}(y,t_{r})
,
\mathcal{P}^{OU}(y,t_{r})

\mathcal{P}^{AB}_{1/2}(y,t_{r})

112

150

152

\mathcal{Q}(y,t_{r})

80

\langle x_{0}(t_{1})x_{\beta}(t_{2})\rangle_{c}
also exhibits non-monotonic behaviour with respect to
t_{2}
in which it increases with
t_{2}

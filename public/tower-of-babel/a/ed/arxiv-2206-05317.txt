Intrinsic dimensionality and generalization properties of the ℛ \mathcal{R} -norm inductive bias
Navid Ardeshir, Daniel Hsu, Clayton Sanford

Abstract
We study the structural and statistical properties of ℛ \mathcal{R} -norm minimizing interpolants of datasets labeled by specific target functions. The ℛ \mathcal{R} -norm is the basis of an inductive bias for two-layer neural networks, recently introduced to capture the functional effect of controlling the size of network weights, independently of the network width. We find that these interpolants are intrinsically multivariate functions, even when there are ridge functions that fit the data, and also that the ℛ \mathcal{R} -norm inductive bias is not sufficient for achieving statistically optimal generalization for certain learning problems. Altogether, these results shed new light on an inductive bias that is connected to practical neural network training.

Introduction

The study of inductive biases in neural network learning is important for theoretical understanding and for developing practical guidance in network training.
Recent theories of generalization rely on inductive biases of training algorithms to explain how neural nets that (nearly) interpolate training data can be accurate out-of-sample
(
Neyshabur et al. 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2021
)
.
When inductive biases are made explicit and their effects are elucidated, they can be incorporated into training procedures when deemed appropriate for a problem at hand.

In this paper, we study the inductive bias for two-layer neural networks implied by a variational norm called the
\mathcal{R}
-norm

We focus on the

It is well-known that the size of the weights can play a critical role in generalization properties of neural networks

(

1.2
Related work

Variational norms and inductive biases of optimization methods.

Many variational norms (such as
\mathcal{R}
-norm) from functional analysis
can be regarded as representational costs that induce topologies on the space of infinitely-wide neural networks with certain activation functions.
Prior works have analyzed these norms for homogeneous activation functions like ReLU
(
Kurková and Sanguineti 2001
;
Mhaskar 2004
;
Bach 2017
;
Savarese et al. 2019
;
Ongie et al. 2019
, e.g.,)
; see
Siegel and Xu 2021
and references therein for a comparison.
In particular, the work of
Ongie et al. 2019

provided analytical descriptions of
\mathcal{R}
-norm in terms of the Radon transform of the function itself.
This work was extended to higher powers of ReLU by
Parhi and Nowak 2021a
.

The variational norms are also connected to the implicit biases of optimization methods for training neural networks.
In the context of univariate functions, the dynamics of gradient descent was shown to be biased towards (adaptive) linear or cubic spline depending on the optimization regime
(
Williams et al. 2019
;
Shevchenko et al. 2021
;
Maennel et al. 2018
)
, and these results have been partially extended to the multivariate case
(
Jin and Montúfar 2020
)
.
For classification problems, the implicit bias of gradient descent was connected to a variational problem related to
\mathcal{R}
-norm with margin constraints on the data
(
Bach and Chizat 2021
)
.

Solutions to the variational problem.

Debarre et al. 2022
and
Hanin 2021
fully characterized the form of all solutions of (
VP
) for one-dimensional datasets (as discussed above).
However, pinning down even a single solution for general multidimensional datasets appears to be difficult;
Ergen and Pilanci 2021
was able to do so for rank-one datasets, where all the feature vectors lie on a line.
The datasets we study do not satisfy the rank-one condition of
Ergen and Pilanci 2021
, and thus we require different techniques to analyze multi-directional functions.

Adaptivity.

In the context of non-parametric regression, it is well-known that (deep) neural networks achieve minimax-optimal rates in the presence of low-dimensional structure in the target function
(
Schmidt-Hieber 2020
;
Bauer and Kohler 2019
;
Kohler and Krzyżak 2005
;
Györfi et al. 2002
, e.g.,)
.
The convergence rates in these works depend only on the intrinsic dimension of the target function (and not the ambient dimension) and are achieved by optimally trading off accuracy and regularization in certain deep neural network architectures.
Recent works
(
Klusowski and Barron 2016
;
Parhi and Nowak 2021b
;
Bach 2017
)
consider two-layer neural networks with variational norm (similar to
\mathcal{R}
-norm) regularization, which also allows for adaptivity to low-dimensional structure.
That is, a function
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
depending only on a
k
-dimensional projection of its input
x
, i.e.,
g(x)=\phi(Ux)
for some
U\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
(with orthonormal rows) and
\phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
has variational norm no greater than that of the corresponding low-dimensional function
\phi

(
Bach 2017
)
.
In particular,
Bach 2017
and
Klusowski and Barron 2016
studied minimax rates under ridge target functions where
k=1
. Our results on generalization are of a different flavor:
rather than striking a careful balance between fitting and regularization to achieve minimax rates, we study the behavior of
\mathcal{R}
-norm-minimizing interpolation.

Regularization based on weight decay (equivalent to
\mathcal{R}
-norm for shallow networks) has also been used to obtain minimax rates for learning smooth target functions.

Parhi and Nowak 2021b
do so by drawing analogies to spline theory, while
Wang and Lin 2021
consider a connection to the Group Lasso.

Zhang and Wang 2022
exploits depth to promote stronger sparsity regularizes.
This is distinct from the low-dimensional structures studied in this work and mentioned above.

Learning ridge functions and parity functions with neural nets.

Target functions that depend on low-dimensional projections of the input (of which ridge functions are the simplest case) have been long studied in statistics
(
Li 2018
, see, e.g.,)
, and learning such functions
is one of the simplest problems where neural network training demonstrates adaptivity.
Such demonstrations typically require going beyond the neural tangent kernel regime and have been used to explain the “feature learning” ability of neural networks
(
Frei et al. 2022
;
Damian et al. 2022
;
Mousavi-Hosseini et al. 2022
;
Bietti et al. 2022
)
.
Several recent works have considered the prospects of learning (sparse) parity functions by training neural nets with gradient-based algorithms
(
Abbe and Sandon 2020
;
Daniely and Malach 2020
;
Malach et al. 2021
;
Barak et al. 2022
;
Telgarsky 2022
)
.
The positive results express parities as low-weight linear combinations of (random) ReLUs, which motivates our focus on the variational norm of approximating neural nets.
Our sample complexity lower bound shows that, even if computational and optimization considerations are set aside, the inductive bias imposed by the
\mathcal{R}
-norm may lead to suboptimal statistical performance.

Averaging and ensembling.

Neural networks have been interpreted as forms of averaging or ensemble methods to explain their statistical properties
(
Bartlett 1996
;
Baldi and Sadowski 2013
;
Gal and Ghahramani 2016
;
Olson et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
.
Our use of averaging is different in that it serves as an approximation-theoretic mechanism for achieving smaller
\mathcal{R}
-norm.

Weight lower bounds for other explicit functions.

Representation costs for two-layer neural networks to approximate other explicit functions have been explored in several prior works
(
Martens et al. 2013
;
Daniely 2017
;
Safran and Shamir 2017
;
Safran et al. 2019
)
.
These works establish exponential lower-bounds on the width of two-layer networks needed to approximate functions that are represented more compactly by three-layer networks.
These results also imply lower-bounds on the size of second-layer weights in a two-layer network after fixing the width of the network.
In contrast, our results hold for networks of unbounded width and for a target function that can be exactly represented by a two-layer networks of
\poly(d)
width.

We now recall the definition of the

Indeed,

The appearance of the affine component

3.1
Every ridge parity interpolant has
\mathcal{R}
-norm

We first show that any

This lower-bound is established even for

Theorem 3
.

For
d\geq 2
, let
\operatorname{Ridge}_{d}
be the set of functions

such that

for some

and Lipschitz continuous
\phi\colon[-\sqrt{d},\sqrt{d}]\to\mathbb{R}

Then

\inf\{\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}:g\in\operatorname{Ridge}_{d},\,\left\|g-\chi\right\|_{L^{\infty}{(\nu)}}\leq 1/2\}\geq d^{3/2}/(2\sqrt{2}).

The proof
constructs a labeled dataset of
d+1
points, and shows that any ridge function
g(x)=\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x)
that approximates that dataset must have many high-magnitude oscillations.
These oscillations imply a lower bound on
\left\|\phi^{\prime}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}}
, which proves the claim by way of Theorem
2
.

Proof.

Take any
g\in\operatorname{Ridge}_{d}
of the form
g(x)=\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x)
for some function
\phi
and vector
w
satisfying the approximation constraint
\left\|g-\chi\right\|_{L^{\infty}{(\nu)}}\leq 1/2
.
Suppose for sake of contradiction that
w_{i}=0
for some
i\in[d]
.
Then, there exists a pair of points
x,x^{\prime}\in\{-1,1\}^{d}
that are identical except in the
i
-th positions,
x_{i}
and
x^{\prime}_{i}
.
Thus,
\chi(x)=-\chi(x^{\prime})
, but
w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x=w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{\prime}
and hence
g(x)=g(x^{\prime})
; this contradicts the approximation constraint.
So, we may henceforth assume that
w_{i}\neq 0
for all
i\in[d]
.

For each
i\in\{0,1,\dotsc,d\}
, define

x^{(i)}:=(\operatorname{sign}(w_{1}),\dots,\operatorname{sign}(w_{i}),-\operatorname{sign}(w_{i+1}),\dots,-\operatorname{sign}(w_{d})).

Because the parity of
x^{(i)}
alternates with
i
, i.e.,
\chi(x^{(i)})\neq\chi(x^{(i+1)})
,
\operatorname{sign}(g(x^{(i)}))
also alternates because
g
satisfies the approximation constraint.
Furthermore, again due to the approximation constraint, we have
|g(x^{(i)})-g(x^{(i+1)})|\geq 1
.
We claim that, because
\phi
interpolates
d+1
well-separated data points
(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)},\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)}))
that satisfy
w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)}<w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i+1)}
for all
i\in\{0,1,\dotsc,d-1\}
,
there must be a large cost for representing
\phi
using a neural network.
By Theorem
2
, it suffices to obtain a lower bound on
\left\|\phi^{\prime}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}}
, since this will imply a lower bound on
\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}
.

By Lemma
22
(in Appendix
D.1
; essentially the mean value theorem), for every
i\in\{0,1,\dotsc,d-1\}
, there exists
A_{i}\subseteq[w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)},w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i+1)}]
with Lebesgue measure
\Leb(A_{i})>0
such that, for every

, we have

|\phi^{\prime}(z^{(i)})|\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{|\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i+1)})-\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)})|}{w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i+1)}-w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)}},

and
\smash{\operatorname{sign}\left(\phi^{\prime}(z^{(i)})\right)=\operatorname{sign}\left(\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i+1)})-\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)})\right)}
.
The fact that the signs of
\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)})
alternate with
i
implies that the signs of
\phi^{\prime}(z^{(i)})
also alternate with
i
.
We now lower-bound the total variation of
\phi^{\prime}
using the fact that
\prod_{i=1}^{d}\Leb(A_{i})>0
and taking advantage of the alternating signs:

\displaystyle 2\left\|\phi^{\prime}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}}

\displaystyle=2\esssup_{-\sqrt{d}\leq t_{0}<t_{1}<\dotsb<t_{r}\leq\sqrt{d};\,r\in\mathbb{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left|\phi^{\prime}(t_{i})-\phi^{\prime}(t_{i-1})\right|

\displaystyle\geq 2\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}|\phi^{\prime}(z^{(i)})-\phi^{\prime}(z^{(i-1)})|=2\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(|\phi^{\prime}(z^{(i)})|+|\phi^{\prime}(z^{(i-1)})|)\geq 2\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}|\phi^{\prime}(z^{(i)})|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{|\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i+1)})-\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)})|}{w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i+1)}-w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)}}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}\frac{1}{w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i+1)}-w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d^{2}}{\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i+1)}-w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(i)}}=\frac{d^{2}}{w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(d)}-w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x^{(0)}}\geq\frac{d^{2}}{\|w\|_{2}\|x^{(d)}-x^{(0)}\|_{2}}=\frac{d^{3/2}}{\sqrt{2}}.

The second-to-last inequality is a consequence of Cauchy-Schwarz: for any
a_{1},\dots,a_{d}>0
,
d^{2}=\smash{(\sum_{i}\sqrt{a_{i}}/\sqrt{a_{i}})^{2}\leq(\sum_{i}a_{i})(\sum_{i}1/a_{i})}
.
Therefore,
\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}=\left\|\phi^{\prime}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}}\geq d^{3/2}/(2\sqrt{2})
.
∎

.

3.2
Existence of a multi-directional parity interpolant with
\mathcal{R}
-norm

We now show that the

For any even

Conclusion and future work

In this work, we shed light on the

This paper considers the approximation and generalization implications of bounding the complexity of shallow neural networks with the

-norm.

\left|c\right|

•

Let

\displaystyle\left|g(x_{0})-g(0)\right|

Lemma 22
.

Suppose
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous on the interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]
with
\phi(t_{1})\neq\phi(t_{2})
.
Define

A:=\biggl\{t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]:\text{$\phi^{\prime}(t)$ exists},\\
\left|\phi^{\prime}(t)\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\left|\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})\right|}{t_{2}-t_{1}},\ \operatorname{sign}\left(\phi^{\prime}(t)\right)=\operatorname{sign}\left(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})\right)\biggr\}.

(The factor
1/2
in the definition of
A
can be replaced by any constant in
(0,1)
.)
Then,
\Leb(A)>0
, where
\Leb
is the Lebesgue measure.

Proof.

Recall that
\phi^{\prime}
denotes the right continuous derivative of
g
(or the right-hand Dini derivative) which is guaranteed to exist except on a null set by Rademacher’s theorem.
Let
s:=\operatorname{sign}\left(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})\right)
.
By the assumption
\phi(t_{1})\neq\phi(t_{2})
and the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, we have

0<|\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})|=s\left(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1})\right)=\int_{t_{2}}^{t_{1}}s\phi^{\prime}(z)\dd z\leq(t_{2}-t_{1})\esssup_{z\in[t_{1},t_{2}]}s\phi^{\prime}(z).

Recall that, by definition,

\esssup_{z\in[t_{1},t_{2}]}s\phi^{\prime}(z)=\inf\left\{a:\Leb(\{z\in[t_{1},t_{2}]:\text{$\phi^{\prime}(z)$ exists},\ s\phi^{\prime}(z)>a\})=0\right\},

and thus

B:=\left\{z\in[t_{1},t_{2}]:\text{$\phi^{\prime}(z)$ exists},\ s(\phi(t_{2})-\phi(t_{1}))\leq 2\cdot(t_{2}-t_{1})s\phi^{\prime}(z)\right\}

satisfies
\Leb(B)>0
.
Observe that
B=A
, so
\Leb(A)>0
, concluding the proof.
∎

)
.

\displaystyle q

Fact 1

\left|\left\langle\bar{r}_{w,b},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\right|\leq\left|\left\langle\bar{r}_{w,b},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\nu)}}\right|+C_{1}\sqrt{\frac{d(\ln{n}+\ln(3/\delta))}{n}}\leq\frac{8}{d}+2C_{1}\sqrt{\frac{d\ln{n}}{n}}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{d},

where
C_{1}
is the constant from Lemma
28
and
n>C(d^{3}(\log{d}+\log(1/\delta)))
by assumption.

We now show that the linear components cannot be substantially correlated with the parity function and bound
\left\langle v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x+c,\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}
.
Because no linear term correlates with the full parity dataset, Lemma
27
provides an upper bound on the inner product between the linear perturbation and sampled parity dataset and implies the following bound with probability at least
1-\delta/3
:

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x+c,\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 8\max\{\left|\mu\right|,1\}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left|c\right|\leq 1\\
\left\|v\right\|_{2}\leq 1\end{subarray}}{\left|\left\langle v^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}x+c,\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\right|}

\displaystyle=8\max\{\left|\mu\right|,1\}\left(\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\mathbf{y}}_{i}}\right|+\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\mathbf{y}}_{i}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}\right\|_{2}\right).

By Lemma
26
and our assumptions on
n
, we bound the two data-dependent terms with probability at least
1-\frac{\delta}{3}
for some absolute constant
C_{2}
:

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x+c,\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 8\max\{\left|\mu\right|,1\}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2\ln(12/\delta)}{n}}+2\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{d}\max\{\left|\mu\right|,1\}.

Combining both bounds, we have with probability at least
1-\delta

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\left\langle\bar{g}_{\mu}(x)+v^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}x+c,\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{d}\left(\left|\mu\right|+\max\{\left|\mu\right|,1\}\right)\leq\frac{2C_{2}}{d}\max\{\left|\mu\right|,1\}.

Therefore, we conclude

Lemma 26
.

Fix any
\delta\in(0,1)

(ii)

Claim
(i)

12

(iv)

1-\frac{\delta}{2}

17

\sigma_{1}({\mathbf{X}})\leq\sqrt{n}+C\left(\sqrt{d}+\sqrt{\ln\frac{2}{\delta}}\right)\leq 2\sqrt{n}

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}\right\|_{2}

Lemma 27
.

Fix any

\delta\in(0,1)
.
Assume
n\geq O(\log(1/\delta))
and

n=\omega(d)
.
With probability at least
1-\delta
over the random measure
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}
, if
\mu\in\mathcal{M}
satisfies

\left\|g_{\mu}({\mathbf{x}})+c+v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}{\mathbf{x}}-\chi({\mathbf{x}})\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\leq 1
,
then
\max\{\left|c+g_{\mu}(0)\right|,\left\|v\right\|_{2}\}\leq 8\max\{\left|\mu\right|,1\}.

Proof.

We draw inspiration from the fact that the full parity dataset is orthogonal to any linear term and can never be well-approximated with large linear components.
In other words, the square loss on approximating the full parity dataset with a linear function is minimized by the constant-zero function and strictly worsens as the linear terms increase.
That is, orthogonality ensures that
\|c+v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x-\chi(x)\|_{L^{2}{(\nu)}}^{2}=1+|c|^{2}+\|v\|_{2}^{2}
.
Thus, having an upper bound on the squared error imposes similar upper bounds on the norms of the linear terms.
We make a similar argument for the
sampled
parity dataset, where we replace
\nu
with
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n}
.

Without loss of generality, we incorporate
g_{\mu}(0)
into
c
and define
\bar{g}_{\mu}(x)=g_{\mu}(x)-g_{\mu}(0)
which can be also represented as
\bar{g}_{\mu}(x)=\int{\bar{r}_{w,b}(x)\mu(dw,db)}
where
\bar{r}_{w,b}=\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x+b)-\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(b)
.
Let
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
be the collection of samples
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
and let
{\mathbf{y}}_{i}=\chi({\mathbf{x}}_{i})
.
We bound the squared loss of the linear component
v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x+c
, ignoring the neural network
\bar{g}_{\mu}
:

\displaystyle\left\|c+v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x-\chi(x)\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}^{2}

\displaystyle=1+c^{2}+v^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}}\right)v-\frac{2}{n}v^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{({\mathbf{y}}_{i}-c){\mathbf{x}}_{i}}\right)-\frac{2c}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbf{y}}_{i}

\displaystyle\geq 1+c^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\left\|v\right\|_{2}^{2}\sigma_{d}({\mathbf{X}})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-2\left\|v\right\|_{2}\left(\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\mathbf{y}}_{i}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}\right\|_{2}+\left|c\right|\left\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}}\right\|_{2}\right)-2\left|c\right|\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{{\mathbf{y}}_{i}}\right|.

With probability
1-\delta
, all events of Lemma
26
hold, and we use them to lower-bound the squared loss.

\displaystyle\left\|c+v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x-\chi(x)\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 1+c^{2}+\frac{9}{16}\left\|v\right\|_{2}^{2}-4\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}}(1+\left|c\right|)\left\|v\right\|_{2}-\frac{2\sqrt{2\ln(8/\delta)}}{\sqrt{n}}\left|c\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\max\{\left|c\right|,\left\|v\right\|_{2}\}^{2}.

where we have used the assumptions on
n
and the AM/GM inequality.
We now provide upper bounds on the square loss based on measure
\mu
using the triangle inequality:

\left\|c+v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x-\chi(x)\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\leq\left\|\bar{g}_{\mu}\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}+\left\|\bar{g}_{\mu}(x)+c+v^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}x-\chi(x)\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\leq\left\|\bar{g}_{\mu}\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}+1.

We may now connect
L^{2}(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})
norm of
\bar{g}_{\mu}
to its variational norm.
We bound the output of
\bar{g}_{\mu}
on a single input
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
by employing Cauchy-Schwarz:

\displaystyle\bar{g}_{\mu}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int{|\bar{r}_{w,b}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})|\left|\mu\right|(\dd w,\dd b)}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mu\right|\int{\bar{r}_{w,b}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})^{2}\left|\mu\right|(\dd w,\dd b)}.

We sum over all
i
to bound the norm of
\bar{g}_{\mu}
:

\displaystyle\left\|\bar{g}_{\mu}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mu\right|\int{\left\|\bar{r}_{w,b}(x)\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}^{2}}\left|\mu\right|(\dd w,\dd b)\leq\left|\mu\right|^{2}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},|b|\leq\sqrt{d}\end{subarray}}{\left\|\bar{r}_{w,b}\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mu\right|^{2}\sup_{w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{|w^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}|^{2}}}=\left|\mu\right|^{2}\frac{\sigma_{1}({\mathbf{X}})^{2}}{n}\leq 4\left|\mu\right|^{2}.

The second inequality relies on the Lipschitzness of
\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}
. Combining all the above,

\frac{1}{2}\max\{\left|c\right|,\left\|v\right\|_{2}\}\leq\left\|c+v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x-\chi(x)\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\leq 1+\left\|g_{\mu}\right\|_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}<2+2\left|\mu\right|.\qed

Lemma 28
.

For
\bar{r}_{w,b}(x)=\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(w^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}x+b)-\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(b)
and
n\geq d
, there exists an absolute constant
C
such that for any
\delta\in(0,1)
with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\left|\left\langle\bar{r}_{w,b},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\right|\leq\left|\left\langle\bar{r}_{w,b},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\nu)}}\right|+C\sqrt{\frac{d(\ln{n}+\ln(1/\delta))}{n}},

for all
w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},b\in[-\sqrt{d},\sqrt{d}]
.

Proof.

Observe that the inner product over the sampled parity dataset is an unbiased estimate of the inner product over the full parity dataset,

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\left\langle\bar{r}_{w,b},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\right]=\left\langle\bar{r}_{w,b},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\nu)}}.

Let
{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}
denote the deviation from the mean, i.e.

{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}=\left\langle\bar{r}_{w,b},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}-\left\langle\bar{r}_{w,b},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\nu)}}.

We use standard concentration of measure techniques for the following steps:

1.

{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}
is Lipschitz in terms of its parameterization
(w,b)
in the sense that
\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w_{1},b_{1}}-{\mathbf{Z}}_{w_{2},b_{2}}\right|\leq 4\sqrt{d}\gamma((w_{1},b_{1}),(w_{2},b_{2}))
, where
\gamma
is a distance defined later on.

2.

{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}
is
O(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})
-subgaussian for fixed
w,b
.

3.

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},b\in[-\sqrt{d},\sqrt{d}]}{\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}\right|}\right]=O(\sqrt{\frac{d}{n}})
using a covering argument.

4.

The maximum of
\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}\right|
is close to its expectation due to the bounded difference inequality.

(Step
1
)

Using the fact that
\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}
is 1-Lipschitz and triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w_{1},b_{1}}-{\mathbf{Z}}_{w_{2},b_{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\langle\bar{r}_{w_{1},b_{1}},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}-\left\langle\bar{r}_{w_{2},b_{2}},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\right|+\left|\left\langle\bar{r}_{w_{1},b_{1}},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\nu)}}-\left\langle\bar{r}_{w_{2},b_{2}},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\nu)}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|\bar{r}_{w_{1},b_{1}}-\bar{r}_{w_{2},b_{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}{(\nu)}}

\displaystyle\leq 2(\left\|\bar{r}_{w_{1},b_{1}}-\bar{r}_{w_{2},b_{1}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}{(\nu)}}+\left\|\bar{r}_{w_{2},b_{1}}-\bar{r}_{w_{2},b_{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}{(\nu)}})

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\max_{x\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}}(w_{1}-w_{2})^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}x+2\left|b_{1}-b_{2}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{d}\left(\left\|w_{1}-w_{2}\right\|_{2}+\frac{|b_{1}-b_{2}|}{\sqrt{d}}\right)\eqqcolon 4\sqrt{d}\gamma((w_{1},b_{1}),(w_{2},b_{2})).

Thus
{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}
is
4\sqrt{d}
-Lipschitz with respect to
\gamma
.

(Step
2
)

We bound the subgaussianity of
{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}
.

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}\right\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left\|\left\langle\bar{r}_{w,b},\chi\right\rangle_{L^{2}{(\boldsymbol{\nu}_{n})}}\right\|_{\psi_{2}}=C_{1}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbf{y}}_{i}(\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(w^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}+b)-\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(b))\right\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|{\mathbf{y}}_{1}(\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(w^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{1}+b)-\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(b))\right\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(w^{{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{1}+b)-\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(b)\right\|_{\psi_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{1}\right\|_{\psi_{2}}\leq\frac{2C_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}

The first, second, and fourth inequalities rely on the centering, averaging, and Lipschitzness properties of subgaussian random variables in Lemma
16
.
The third inequality follows from
\left|{\mathbf{y}}_{1}\right|=1
, and the final is due to the 2-subgaussianity of a vector with i.i.d. Rademacher components.

(Step
3
)

Let
\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}
be an
\epsilon
-covering of
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-\sqrt{d},\sqrt{d}]
with respect to
\gamma
.
We bound its size using the standard
\epsilon
-net result in Lemma
19
for
\epsilon\leq 2
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\left(\epsilon,\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-\sqrt{d},\sqrt{d}],\gamma\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2},\mathbb{S}^{d-1},\|\cdot\|_{2}\right)\times\mathcal{N}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{2},[-1,1],|\cdot|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{6}{\epsilon}\right)^{d}\cdot\frac{4}{\epsilon}\leq\left(\frac{6}{\epsilon}\right)^{d+1}.

We bound the expected maximum deviation over all
w
and
b
by employing a bound on the expected maximum of subgaussian random variables (Lemma
16
), applying the covering numbers argument, letting
\pi(w,b)=\argmin_{(w^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}\gamma((w,b),(w^{\prime},b^{\prime}))
, and setting
\epsilon\coloneqq 1/\sqrt{n}
.

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1},b\in[-\sqrt{d},\sqrt{d}]\end{subarray}}{\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}\right|}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{w,b}{\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}-{\mathbf{Z}}_{\pi(w,b)}\right|}\right]+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{(w,b)\in\mathcal{N}_{\epsilon}}{\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}\right|}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{d}\epsilon+\frac{2C_{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\ln\mathcal{N}\left(\epsilon,\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\times[-\sqrt{d},\sqrt{d}],\gamma\right)}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{d}\epsilon+2C_{2}\sqrt{\frac{d+1}{n}\ln\frac{6}{\epsilon}}\leq C_{3}\sqrt{\frac{d\ln n}{n}}.

(Step
4
)

We conclude by showing that
\sup_{w,b}\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}\right|
is close to its expectation with high probability due to the McDiarmid’s inequality (Lemma
15
).
Consider a perturbation where
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
is replaced by some
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\prime}\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}
with
{\mathbf{y}}_{i}^{\prime}=\chi({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\prime})
, and let
{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}^{i}
denote the resulting deviation term.

\displaystyle\left|\sup_{w,b}\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}\right|-\sup_{w,b}\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}^{i}\right|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{w,b}\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}-{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}^{i}\right|=\frac{1}{n}\sup_{w,b}\left|{\mathbf{y}}_{i}\bar{r}_{w,b}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})-{\mathbf{y}}_{i}^{\prime}\bar{r}_{w,b}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\sup_{w,b}\left[\left|\bar{r}_{w,b}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})-\bar{r}_{w,b}({\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\prime})\right|+\left|({\mathbf{y}}_{i}-{\mathbf{y}}_{i}^{\prime})\bar{r}_{w,b}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n}\left[\left\|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}+2\left\|{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\right\|_{2}\right]\leq\frac{4\sqrt{d}}{n}

Hence, with probability at least
1-\delta
:

\sup_{w,b}\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}\right|\leq\sqrt{\frac{8d\ln 1/\delta}{n}}+\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\sup_{w,b}\left|{\mathbf{Z}}_{w,b}\right|\right]\leq C_{4}\sqrt{\frac{d(\ln n+\ln 1/\delta)}{n}}.

The bound in the lemma statement immediately follows.
∎

{\mathbf{g}}\colon\varOmega\to\mathbb{R}
.
Let
{\mathbf{A}}_{j}
denote the random
n_{j}\times d
matrix whose rows are the
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}
for
i\in G_{j}
, and define the random vector
{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}:={\mathbf{A}}_{j}^{{\dagger}}(z^{(j)}\vec{1})
.
Observe that
{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}
is a least squares solution to the system of linear equations
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}w={\mathbf{y}}_{i}:i\in G_{j}\}
, since
{\mathbf{y}}_{i}=z^{(j)}
for all
i\in G_{j}
.
We define
{\mathbf{g}}
as follows:

{\mathbf{g}}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}z^{(j)}\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(2z^{(j)}{\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}x-1).

To analyze our construction, we consider the realizations of
{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\dotsc,{\mathbf{x}}_{n}
, and establish some basic properties that hold with sufficiently high probability (conditional on the “good event”).
Note that within a group
G_{j}
, the
\{{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\}_{i\in G_{j}}
are (conditionally) iid, and the realizations across groups are also (conditionally) independent.

We claim that with probability at least
5/6
(conditional on the “good event”),

•

(P1)
{\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}={\mathbf{y}}_{i}
for all
j\in[m]
and
i\in G_{j}

•

(P2)
\|{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}\|_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{n_{j}/d}
for all
j\in[m]
.

To establish this claim, we lower-bound the
n_{j}
-th largest singular value
\sigma_{n_{j}}({\mathbf{A}}_{j})
.
Note that
\sigma_{n_{j}}({\mathbf{A}}_{j})
is at least the corresponding singular value of the
n_{j}\times(d-1)
submatrix
{\mathbf{B}}_{j}
obtained from
{\mathbf{A}}_{j}
by removing the
t
-th column of
{\mathbf{A}}_{j}
for some
t\in S
.
(If
S
is empty, we may remove any column.)
Since the rows of
{\mathbf{A}}_{j}
are independent, and since the entries of
{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
after removing the
t
-th one are iid
\operatorname{Unif}(\{\pm 1\})
random variables, it follows that the
n_{j}\times(d-1)
entries of
{\mathbf{B}}_{j}
are iid
\operatorname{Unif}(\{\pm 1\})
random variables.
Hence, the rows of
{\mathbf{B}}_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}
are independent, mean-zero, isotropic, and
O(1)
-subgaussian.
By Lemma
17
and a union bound, with probability at least
1-2m\exp(-\min_{j\in[m]}\{n_{j}\})

\sigma_{n_{j}}({\mathbf{A}}_{j})\geq\sigma_{n_{j}}({\mathbf{B}}_{j}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}})\geq\sqrt{d-1}-C_{2}\sqrt{n_{j}}\geq\left(\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{d}}-C_{2}\sqrt{\frac{c_{1}}{\ln d}}\right)\sqrt{d}\quad\text{for all $j\in[m]$},

where
C_{2}>0
is twice the absolute constant from Lemma
17
, and the final inequality uses the upper-bound on
n_{j}
.
The fact
d\geq C
and the upper-bounds on
m
and
n
altogether imply that the probability of the above event is at least
5/6
, and also that
\sqrt{1-1/d}-C_{2}\sqrt{c_{1}/\ln d}\geq 1/2
.
So in this event, for each
j\in[m]
, the column space of
{\mathbf{A}}_{j}
has rank
n_{j}
, so the system of linear equations defining
{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}
is feasible, and

\|{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}\|_{2}=\|{\mathbf{A}}_{j}^{\dagger}(z^{(j)}\vec{1})\|_{2}\leq\sigma_{1}({\mathbf{A}}_{j}^{\dagger})\|\vec{1}\|_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{n_{j}}}{\sigma_{n_{j}}({\mathbf{A}}_{j})}\leq 2\sqrt{\frac{n_{j}}{d}}.

This establishes P1 and P2 in the event as claimed.

We further claim that with probability at least
5/6
(conditional on the “good event”),

•

(P3)
|{\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}|\leq 4\|{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}\|_{2}\sqrt{\ln d}
for all
j\in[m]
and
i\in[n]\setminus G_{j}
.

{\mathbf{x}}_{i}
and
{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}
are independent for

.
Moreover, by Lemma
29

{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}
(with

is a mean-zero random variable satisfying

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\left[\exp({\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}})\mid\chi_{S}({\mathbf{x}}),{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}\right]\leq\exp(\|{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}\|_{2}^{2}).

So, by Markov’s inequality and a union bound, we have with probability at least
5/6
,

|{\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}|\leq(\sqrt{2}\|{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}\|_{2})\sqrt{2\ln(12mn)}\quad\text{for all $j\in[m]$ and $i\in[n]\setminus G_{j}$}.

Using
d\geq C
and the upper-bounds on
m
and
n
, we obtain
\sqrt{\ln(12mn)}\leq 2\sqrt{\ln d}
, and hence we deduce P3 from the above inequality.

So, by a union bound, with probability at least
2/3
(conditional on the “good event”), the properties P1, P2, and P3 all hold simultaneously.
We can now establish the desired properties of
{\mathbf{g}}
.
Using
d\geq C
, P2, and the upper-bounds on
m
and
n
, we obtain

\left\|{\mathbf{g}}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}\|_{2}\leq 4\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sqrt{\frac{n_{j}}{d}}\leq 4\sqrt{m\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{n_{j}}{d}}=4\sqrt{\frac{mn}{d}}\leq\frac{4n\sqrt{\ln d}}{d}.

Furthermore, by P1, we have for any
j\in[m]
and
i\in G_{j}

2z^{(j)}{\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-1=2z^{(j)}{\mathbf{y}}_{i}-1=1.

And by P2, P3, and the upper-bound on
n_{j}
, we have for any
j\in[m]
and
i\in[n]\setminus G_{j}
,

2z^{(j)}{\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-1\leq 2|{\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}|-1\leq 16\sqrt{\frac{n_{j}\ln d}{d}}-1\leq 16\sqrt{c_{1}}-1=0,

and hence
\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(2z^{(j)}{\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-1)=0
.
Therefore, for any
i\in[n]
, if
i\in G_{j}
,

{\mathbf{g}}({\mathbf{x}}_{i})=z^{(j)}\varphi_{\operatorname{r}}(2z^{(j)}{\mathbf{w}}^{(j){\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}{\mathbf{x}}_{i}-1)=z^{(j)}={\mathbf{y}}_{i}.\qed

Theorem 30

(Detailed version of Theorem
10
)
.

\phi\colon[-\sqrt{d},\sqrt{d}]\to[-1,1]

-Lipschitz and
\rho

.
Let
{\mathbf{w}}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{-(1/\rho)v\}

Let
{\mathbf{w}}^{(1)},\dotsc,{\mathbf{w}}^{(k)}

{\mathbf{w}}

{\mathbf{h}}^{(j)}:=h_{{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}}

j

-Lipschitz for

-Lipschitzness of
\phi

v

\epsilon

By Lemma
31
(with

and
\delta:=\epsilon/3
), there exist

such that:

•

(H1)
\tilde{\mathbf{h}}^{(j)}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

•

(H2)
\|\tilde{\mathbf{h}}^{(j)}\|_{\mathcal{R}}=O(\tau L^{2}\|v+\rho{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}\|_{2}/\epsilon)

•

Our approximating neural network

By construction and using properties H1 and H2 (above), the following properties of

are immediate:

•

(G1)
{\mathbf{g}}

•

(G2)
\max\{\|{\mathbf{g}}\|_{\mathcal{R}},\|{\mathbf{g}}\|_{\mathscr{V}_{2}}\}=O(\tau L^{2}\max_{j\in[k]}\|v+\rho{\mathbf{w}}^{(j)}\|_{2}/\epsilon)

So, in the remainder of the proof, we choose a particular distribution for

{\mathbf{w}}

We let

{\mathbf{w}}_{1},\dotsc,{\mathbf{w}}_{d}

(2)

Proof.

We prove the claim by a reduction to Theorem

.
That is, we show that an interpolant with better
\mathcal{R}
-norm than the bound stipulates can be used to construct a neural network that contradicts Theorem
3
.

To do so, we consider a lower dimension
d^{\prime}=4\lfloor d/4q\rfloor-4
and create a mapping from points
z\in\{\pm 1\}^{d^{\prime}}
to
x_{z}\in\{\pm 1\}^{d}
.
We define
a\in[0,4q-1]
such that
2a\equiv d\pmod{4q}
.
For any
z
, we define
x_{z}
as follows:

x_{z}=(\underbrace{z_{1},\dots,z_{1}}_{q},\dots,\underbrace{z_{d^{\prime}},\dots,z_{d^{\prime}}}_{q},\underbrace{1,\dots,1}_{a},\underbrace{-1,\dots,-1}_{d-d^{\prime}q-a}).

Observe that

\vec{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x_{z}=q\vec{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}z+2a-d+d^{\prime}q\equiv q\vec{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}z\pmod{4q}.

Due to the periodicity of cosine and the fact that
d^{\prime}
is a multiple of 4,

\cos(\frac{2\pi}{\rho}v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x_{z})=\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}\vec{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}z)=\chi(z).

Consider some
g(x)=\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x)
with
\|g-\cos(\frac{2\pi}{\rho}v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
Define
w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{\prime}}
such that
w^{\prime}_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{q}w_{(i-1)q+j}
. Observe that
\left\|w^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{q}
and that
w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x_{z}=w^{\prime{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}z+c_{w}
, where
c_{w}
depends only on the remaining elements of
w
.
Define
\tilde{g}(z)=\phi(w^{\prime{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}}z+c_{w})
.
Then,

|\tilde{g}(z)-\chi(z)|=|\phi(w^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x_{z})-\cos(\frac{2\pi}{\rho}v^{\scriptscriptstyle{\mathsf{T}}}x_{z})|\leq\frac{1}{2}

for all
z\in\{\pm 1\}^{d^{\prime}}
.
Since translation can only decrease the
\mathcal{R}
-norm (by exhausting some neurons to effectively behave linearly in the domain) namely,
\left\|\tilde{g}\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}\leq\left\|w^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\left\|\phi^{\prime}\right\|_{\operatorname{TV}}=\left\|w^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}
, Theorem
3

\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{R}}=\Omega({d^{\prime 3/2}}/{\sqrt{q}})

Probabilistic learning constrained by realizations using a weak formulation of Fourier transform of probability measures Journal: arXiv
Christian Soize

Abstract
This paper deals with the taking into account a given set of realizations as constraints in the Kullback-Leibler minimum principle, which is used as a probabilistic learning algorithm. This permits the effective integration of data into predictive models. We consider the probabilistic learning of a random vector that is made up of either a quantity of interest (unsupervised case) or the couple of the quantity of interest and a control parameter (supervised case). A training set of independent realizations of this random vector is assumed to be given and to be generated with a prior probability measure that is unknown. A target set of realizations of the QoI is available for the two considered cases. The framework is the one of non-Gaussian problems in high dimension. A functional approach is developed on the basis of a weak formulation of the Fourier transform of probability measures (characteristic functions). The construction makes it possible to take into account the target set of realizations of the QoI in the Kullback-Leibler minimum principle. The proposed approach allows for estimating the posterior probability measure of the QoI (unsupervised case) or of the posterior joint probability measure of the QoI with the control parameter (supervised case). The existence and the uniqueness of the posterior probability measure is analyzed for the two cases. The numerical aspects are detailed in order to facilitate the implementation of the proposed method. The presented application in high dimension demonstrates the efficiency and the robustness of the proposed algorithm.

1.2
Novelty of the paper

In this paper, we propose to use the Kullback-Leibler minimum principle to estimate the closest probability measure to a prior measure, which is indirectly defined by giving a training dataset, under the constraint defined by a set of realizations for which the statistical moments cannot be estimated and therefore are assumed to be unknown. As the considered problem is in high dimension and as the target corresponds to given realizations from which statistics such as high-order statistical moments cannot be estimated, this problem is not trivial at all and requires the development of an appropriate approach. We therefore propose a novel functional method, which allows the target set of the realizations to be integrated as a constraint imposed in the form of a mathematical expectation. The functional approach consists in constructing and analyzing a weak formulation of the Fourier transform of the probability measure and to derive from it a finite representation of the functional constraint.

{\bm{X}}^{(\nu)}
is mean-square convergent to
{\bm{X}}
when
\nu
goes to
N_{d}-1
, and if
\nu=N_{d}-1=n_{x}-1
, then Eq. (
2.1
) is not an approximation and corresponds to a change of basis.
In general, for the high-dimension problems,
n_{x}
is very large and
N_{d}\ll n_{x}
. Therefore, Eq. (
2.1
) corresponds to a reduced representation, which is an approximation whose accuracy depends on
\nu
and
N_{d}
and which is classically controlled as follows.
For
N_{d}
fixed and for
\nu<N_{d}-1
, let
\kappa_{1}\geq\ldots\geq\kappa_{\nu}>0
be the
\nu
largest positive eigenvalues of

[\widehat{C}_{\bm{X}}]
. Let
\nu
be chosen such that

{\hbox{{\pcarac err}}}_{\hbox{{\pppppcarac PCA}}}(\nu\,;N_{d})=\frac{E\{\|\,{\bm{X}}-{\bm{X}}^{(\nu)}\,\|^{2}\}}{E\{\|\,{\bm{X}}\,\|^{2}\}}\simeq 1-\frac{\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\kappa_{\alpha}}{{\hbox{{{t}r}}}\{[\widehat{C}_{\bm{X}}]\}}\,\leq\varepsilon_{\hbox{{\pppppcarac PCA}}}\quad,\quad\nu<N_{d}-1\,,

(2.3)

in which
\varepsilon_{\hbox{{\pppppcarac PCA}}}
is a given positive real number sufficiently small. The trace
{\hbox{{{t}r}}}\{[\widehat{C}_{\bm{X}}]\}
of
[\widehat{C}_{\bm{X}}]
is calculated by estimating the diagonal entries of
[\widehat{C}_{\bm{X}}]
using the training set.
Note that
\nu\mapsto{\hbox{{\pcarac err}}}_{\hbox{{\pppppcarac PCA}}}(\nu\,;N_{d})
defined by Eq. (
2.3
) gives the relative error as a function of
\nu<N_{d}-1
for a fixed value of
N_{d}
.

Throughout the rest of the paper, in order to simplify the notations, the superscript "
(\nu)
" will be omitted and the random variable

{\bm{X}}^{(\nu)}=({\bm{Q}}^{(\nu)},{\bm{W}}^{(\nu)})
will simply be denoted by
{\bm{X}}=({\bm{Q}},{\bm{W}})
.
From Eq. (
2.1
), it can be deduced that

{\bm{Q}}=\underline{{\bm{q}}}+[\Phi_{q}]\,[\kappa]^{1/2}\,{\bm{H}}\quad,\quad{\bm{W}}=\underline{{\bm{w}}}+[\Phi_{w}]\,[\kappa]^{1/2}\,{\bm{H}}\,,

(2.4)

in which
\underline{{\bm{x}}}=(\underline{{\bm{q}}},\underline{{\bm{w}}})\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{x}}}}={{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{q}}}}\times{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{w}}}}
, and where

[\Phi_{q}]\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n_{q},\nu}
and
[\Phi_{w}]\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n_{w},\nu}
are the corresponding block extraction with respect to
{\bm{Q}}
and
{\bm{W}}
.
The training set related to
{\bm{H}}
is

{\mathcal{D}}_{d}=\{{\bm{\eta}}_{d}^{1},\ldots,{\bm{\eta}}_{d}^{N_{d}}\}\,\quad,\quad{\bm{\eta}}_{d}^{j}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}\quad,\quad j=1,\ldots,N_{d}\,,

(2.5)

in which
{\bm{\eta}}_{d}^{j}
is given by Eq. (
2.2
).
Using
{\mathcal{D}}_{d}
, the estimates
\underline{{\bm{\eta}}}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
and
[\widehat{C}_{\bm{H}}]\in{\mathbb{M}}_{\nu}^{+}
of the mean value and the covariance matrix of
{\bm{H}}
are such that

\underline{{\bm{\eta}}}={\hbox{\bf 0}}_{\nu}\quad,\quad[\widehat{C}_{\bm{H}}]=[I_{\nu}]\,.

(2.6)

The first Eq. (
2.4
) allows for defining the
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{q}}}}
-valued random variable
\widetilde{{\bm{Q}}}
such that

\widetilde{{\bm{Q}}}={\bm{Q}}-\underline{{\bm{q}}}\quad,\quad\widetilde{\bm{Q}}=[\Phi_{q}]\,[\kappa]^{1/2}\,{\bm{H}}\,.

(2.7)

Note that we have
[\Phi_{q}]^{T}[\Phi_{q}]\not=[I_{\nu}]
. We also introduce the
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{q}}}}
-valued random variable

\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}={\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}-\underline{{\bm{q}}}\,,

(2.8)

whose
N_{r}
realizations are

\tilde{\bm{q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r}={\bm{q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r}-\underline{{\bm{q}}}\quad,\quad r\in\{1,\ldots,N_{r}\}\,.

(2.9)

Note that
\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
is generally not centered because
\underline{{\bm{q}}}
is not the mean value of
{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
, but
{\bm{H}}
is a centered one (see Eq. (
2.6
)).

3
Definition of the functional constraint for estimating the posterior probability measure

The objective is to construct the posterior probability measure
P_{{\bm{Q}}}^{\hbox{{\ppppcarac post}}}(d{\bm{q}})=P_{{\bm{Q}},{\bm{W}}}^{\hbox{{\ppppcarac post}}}(d{\bm{q}},{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{w}}}})
that is closest to
P_{\bm{Q}}^{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}(d{\bm{q}})
, which is equivalent (see Eqs. (
2.7
) and (
2.8
)) to construct the posterior probability measure
P_{\widetilde{\bm{Q}}}^{\hbox{{\ppppcarac post}}}(d{\bm{q}})
that is closest to the probability measure

P_{\widetilde{\bm{Q}}}^{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}(d{\bm{q}})
of
\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
for which
N_{r}
independent realizations
\{\tilde{\bm{q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r},r=1,\ldots,N_{r}\}
are given.
For using the Kullback-Leibler minimum principle, we need to express the constraint as a mathematical expectation of a random variable.
We propose to use the equality of the Fourier transforms of the probability measures (characteristic functions) instead of the probability measures for the reason given in Remark
1
-(ii).

Definition 1
(Constraint defined by the equality of the Fourier transform of the probability measures)

The constraint is defined as follows,

\forall{\bm{y}}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{q}}}}\quad,\quad{\mathbb{f}}({\bm{y}})={\mathbb{f}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}({\bm{y}})\,,

(3.1)

in which the complex-valued functions
{\mathbb{f}}
and
{\mathbb{f}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
are the characteristic functions defined on
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{q}}}}
of the
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{q}}}}
-valued random variables
\widetilde{\bm{Q}}
and
\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
,

{\mathbb{f}}({\bm{y}})=E\{e^{i\,\langle\,{\bm{y}},\,\widetilde{\bm{Q}}\,\rangle}\}\quad,\quad{\mathbb{f}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}({\bm{y}})=E\{e^{i\,\langle\,{\bm{y}},\,\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}\,\rangle}\}\,.

(3.2)

The constraint defined by Eq. (
3.1
) is in high dimension because
{\bm{y}}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{q}}}}
. We then propose to reduce the dimension using the representation of
\widetilde{\bm{Q}}
given by Eq. (
2.7
).

Lemma 1
(Functional constraint using the representation of
\widetilde{\bm{Q}}
)

It is chosen to project the target on the prior model. Therefore, let
{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
be the
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
-valued random variable defined by

{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}=[V]^{T}\,\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}\,,

(3.3)

in which the matrix
[V]\in{\mathbb{M}}_{n_{q},\nu}
is written as

[V]=[\Phi_{q}]\,([\Phi_{q}]^{T}\,[\Phi_{q}])^{-1}\,[\kappa]^{-1/2}
.
Using the representation of
\widetilde{\bm{Q}}
defined by Eq. (
2.7
), the functional constraint associated with Eq. (
3.1
) is written as

\forall{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}\quad,\quad f({\hbox{\bf{v}}})=f_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}({\hbox{\bf{v}}})\,,

(3.4)

in which the complex-valued functions
f
and
f_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
are the characteristic functions, defined on
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
, of the
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
-valued random variables
\widetilde{\bm{H}}
and
\widetilde{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
,

f({\hbox{\bf{v}}})=E\{e^{i\,\langle\,{\hbox{\pbf{v}}},\,{\bm{H}}\,\rangle}\}\quad,\quad f_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}({\hbox{\bf{v}}})=E\{e^{i\,\langle\,{\hbox{\pbf{v}}},\,{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}\,\rangle}\}\,.

(3.5)

Proof 1

(Lemma
1
).
Using Eq. (
2.7
), for all
{\bm{y}}
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{q}}}}
, we have

\langle\,{\bm{y}}\,,\widetilde{\bm{Q}}\,\rangle=\langle\,{\bm{y}}\,,[\Phi_{q}]\,[\kappa]^{1/2}{\bm{H}}\,\rangle=\langle\,[\kappa]^{1/2}\,[\Phi_{q}]^{T}{\bm{y}}\,,{\bm{H}}\,\rangle=\langle\,{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\,,{\bm{H}}\,\rangle
in which

{\hbox{\bf{v}}}=[\kappa]^{1/2}[\Phi_{q}]^{T}\,{\bm{y}}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
.
In the other hand, we perform the projection of
\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
on the prior model. Since the matrix

[\Phi_{q}]^{T}[\Phi_{q}]\in{\mathbb{M}}_{\nu}^{+}
is invertible and since the diagonal matrix
[\kappa]\in{\mathbb{M}}_{\nu}^{+}
is also invertible, we introduce the
pseudo-inverse
[V]
of
[\kappa]^{1/2}[\Phi_{q}]^{T})
(projection) such that
([\kappa]^{1/2}[\Phi_{q}]^{T})\,[V]=[I_{\nu}]
.
Therefore, taking
{\bm{y}}=[V]\,{\hbox{\bf{v}}}
for all
v
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
, we have

\langle\,{\bm{y}}\,,\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}\,\rangle=\langle\,[V]\,{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\,,\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}\,\rangle=\langle\,{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\,,[V]^{T}\,\widetilde{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}\,\rangle=\langle\,{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\,,{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}\,\rangle
.
Eqs. (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) are then deduced from Eqs. (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).

Remark 1

There are two difficulties.

(i) The constraint does not concern all the variables, that is to say
{\bm{X}}=({\bm{Q}},{\bm{W}})
, but only the quantity of interest
{\bm{Q}}
. We are therefore in an under-observed case with respect to the applied constraint. This choice of the developments framework imposes to project the target
{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
on the prior model in order to obtain a representation of the target
{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
that only depends on
{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
and not on
{\bm{Q}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
and
{\bm{W}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
because
{\bm{W}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
is not given as a constraint.

(ii) The explicitness of the constraint defined by Eq. (
3.4
) requires to sample
v
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
, what is not easy, not efficient, and not accurate in high dimension (
\nu\gg 1
). If such a sampling method was used, then the number of constraints that should be considered in the Kullback-Liebler minimum principle would be huge or even unrealistic. We thus propose to construct a weak formulation of the functional equation defined by Eq. (
3.4
) using the fundamental properties of the Fourier transform of the probability measures (see for instance
[
54
]
).

4
Weak formulation of the functional constraint

Notation 1
(
Defining
{\mathscr{C}}_{b},{\mathscr{C}}_{0},{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
complex vector spaces
).
(i) Let
C^{0}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})
(resp.
L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})
) be the complex vector space of continuous (resp. bounded) functions on
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
with values in
{\mathbb{C}}
. The norm
\|\,g\,\|_{L^{\infty}}
in
L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})
of a function

g\in C^{0}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})\cap L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})

\|\,g\,\|_{L^{\infty}}\,={\rm{ess}}.\,{\rm{sup}}_{{\hbox{\bf{v}}}}\,|\,g({\hbox{\bf{v}}})\,|\,={\rm{sup}}_{{\hbox{\bf{v}}}}\,|\,g({\hbox{\bf{v}}})\,|\,.

(4.1)

The norm
\|\,\varphi\,\|_{L^{q}}
in
L^{q}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})
of the complex-valued functions on
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
is

\|\,\varphi\,\|_{L^{q}}=\left(\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}}|\,\varphi({\hbox{\bf{v}}})\,|^{q}\,d{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\right)^{1/q}\quad,\quad 1\leq q<+\infty\,.

(4.2)

(ii) We define the vector spaces of complex-valued functions,
{\mathscr{C}}_{b},{\mathscr{C}}_{0},{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
and
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
, such that

{\mathscr{C}}_{b}=C^{0}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})\cap L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})\,,

(4.3)

{\mathscr{C}}_{0}=\left\{g\in C^{0}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})\,\,,\,\,|\,g({\hbox{\bf{v}}})\,|\rightarrow 0\,\,{\rm{as}}\,\,\|\,{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\,\|\rightarrow+\infty\right\}\subset{\mathscr{C}}_{b}\subset L^{\infty}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})\,,

(4.4)

{\mathcal{H}}_{0}={\mathscr{C}}_{0}\cap L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})\,,

(4.5)

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})\cap L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})\,.

(4.6)

Hypothesis 1
(Existence, regularity, and integrability of the density of
P_{\bm{H}}
(resp.
P_{{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}}
))

It is assumed that the probability measure
P_{\bm{H}}
(resp.
P_{{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}}
) on
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
of the
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
-valued random variable
{\bm{H}}
(resp.
{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
) is defined by a density
{\bm{\eta}}\mapsto p_{\bm{H}}({\bm{\eta}})
(resp.
{\bm{\eta}}\mapsto p_{{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}}({\bm{\eta}})
) with
respect to the Lebesgue measure
d{\bm{\eta}}
, such that

p_{\bm{H}}\,\,(\hbox{resp.}\,p_{{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}})\,\,\in\,C^{0}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{R}})\,.

(4.7)

Remark 2

(i) A probability density function on
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
is always in
L^{1}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{R}})
. The unusual hypothesis is the belonging to
C^{0}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{R}})\cap L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{R}})
. As part of the method we propose, the prior probability density
p_{\bm{H}}
of
{\bm{H}}
will be estimated using the KDE method of the nonparametric statistics with the training set (see Section
6.1
). In this situation, the hypothesis will be verified.
This hypothesis will allow us an efficient finite representation of the weak formulation to be constructed.

(ii) The prior pdf
p_{\bm{H}}
will effectively be used to construct the posterior pdf
p_{\bm{H}}^{\hbox{{\ppppcarac post}}}
by using the Kullback-Leibler minimum principle under the constraint defined by the target set
D_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
as we have previously explained. The pdf
p_{{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}}
is not used in the methodology proposed and moreover, if it were to be used, there would be a difficulty because it is assumed that
\nu
is large enough and that
N_{r}
is not sufficiently large for obtaining a converged estimate of
p_{{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}}
using the KDE method with the target set
D_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
. The hypothesis defined by Eq. (
4.7
) for
p_{{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}}
will strongly be used and is coherent with the one introduced for
p_{\bm{H}}
.

Lemma 2
(Fourier transform of the probability measures
P_{\bm{H}}
and
P_{{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}}
)

The Fourier transform of the probability measures
P_{\bm{H}}
and
P_{{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}}
(characteristic functions)
{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\mapsto f({\hbox{\bf{v}}})=E\{e^{i\,\langle\,{\hbox{\pbf{v}}},\,{\bm{H}}\,\rangle}\}

and
{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\mapsto f_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}({\hbox{\bf{v}}})=E\{e^{i\,\langle\,{\hbox{\pbf{v}}},\,{\bm{H}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}\,\rangle}\}
from
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
into
{\mathbb{C}}
are such that

f\,\in\,{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\quad,\quad f_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}\,\in\,{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\,.

(4.8)

Proof 2

(Lemma
2
).
Since
\forall\,{\hbox{\bf{v}}}\in{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
,
f({\hbox{\bf{v}}})=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}}e^{\,i\,\langle\,{\hbox{\pbf{v}}},\,{\bm{\eta}}\,\rangle}\,p_{\bm{H}}({\bm{\eta}})\,d{\bm{\eta}}
and taking into account Eq. (
4.7
), it is deduced that

because

, and on the other hand, since

p_{\bm{H}}\in L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})
then
f\in L^{2}({{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}},{\mathbb{C}})
. Consequently,
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
and the proof is similar for
f_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
.

Remark 3

Since
f
is the Fourier transform of probability measure
P_{\bm{H}}(d{\bm{\eta}})=p_{\bm{H}}({\bm{\eta}})\,d{\bm{\eta}}
(positive bounded measure), then it is known (Bochner’s theorem) that

(same properties for
f_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
) is a positive-type function, that is to say, for all integer

, for all complex numbers

, and for all vectors
{\hbox{\bf{v}}}^{1},\ldots,{\hbox{\bf{v}}}^{m}
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
, we have

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{m}f({\hbox{\bf{v}}}^{k}-{\hbox{\bf{v}}}^{k^{\prime}})\,z_{k^{\prime}}\,\overline{z}_{k}\,\geq\,0\,.

(4.9)

Note that Eq. (
4.9
) can simply be deduced without evoking the Bochner theorem because

\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}f({\hbox{\bf{v}}}^{k}-{\hbox{\bf{v}}}^{k^{\prime}})\,z_{k^{\prime}}\,\overline{z}_{k}=\sum_{k,k^{\prime}}E\{e^{\,i\,\langle\,{\hbox{\pbf{v}}}^{k}-{\hbox{\pbf{v}}}^{k^{\prime}}\!\!,\,{\bm{H}}\,\rangle}\}\,z_{k^{\prime}}\,\overline{z}_{k}=

.
We have a similar property to Eq. (
4.9
) for
f_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}
, that is to say,

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{m}f_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}({\hbox{\bf{v}}}^{k}-{\hbox{\bf{v}}}^{k^{\prime}})\,z_{k^{\prime}}\,\overline{z}_{k}\,\geq\,0\,.

(4.10)

(Lemma

{\bm{w}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{N_{r}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{r}}

\langle\,E\{{\bm{h}}^{c}({\bm{H}})\otimes{\bm{h}}^{c}({\bm{H}})\}\,{\bm{w}}\,,{\bm{w}}\,\rangle=E\{\langle\,{\bm{h}}^{c}({\bm{H}})\,,{\bm{w}}\,\rangle^{2}\}\geq 0
.
We then have to prove that, for all
{\bm{w}}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N_{r}}
with

or equivalently, that

E\{\langle\,{\bm{h}}^{c}({\bm{H}})\,,{\bm{w}}\,\rangle^{2}\}=0\Leftrightarrow{\bm{w}}=0
.
We have

E\{\langle\,{\bm{h}}^{c}({\bm{H}})\,,{\bm{w}}\,\rangle^{2}\}=\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}}\langle\,{\bm{h}}^{c}({\bm{\eta}})\,,{\bm{w}}\,\rangle^{2}P_{{\bm{H}}}(d{\bm{\eta}})=0

\Longleftrightarrow\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}}\langle\,{\bm{h}}^{c}({\bm{\eta}})\,,{\bm{w}}\,\rangle^{2}\,d{\bm{\eta}}=0

\Longleftrightarrow\sum_{r=1}^{N_{r}}\sum_{r^{\prime}=1}^{N_{r}}w_{r}w_{r^{\prime}}\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}}\exp\big(-(\nu s^{2})^{-1}\gamma_{rr^{\prime}}({\bm{\eta}})\big)\,d{\bm{\eta}}=0

in which for all
{\bm{\eta}}
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}
,

\gamma_{rr^{\prime}}({\bm{\eta}})=\|\,{\bm{\eta}}-{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r}\|^{2}+\|\,{\bm{\eta}}-{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r^{\prime}}\|^{2}\,=

2\,\|\,{\bm{\eta}}-\frac{1}{2}({\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r}+{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r^{\prime}})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\,\|\,{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r}-{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r^{\prime}}\|^{2}
.
Consequently,

E\{\langle\,{\bm{h}}^{c}({\bm{H}})\,,{\bm{w}}\,\rangle^{2}\}=0

\Longleftrightarrow\sum_{r=1}^{N_{r}}\sum_{r^{\prime}=1}^{N_{r}}w_{r}w_{r^{\prime}}\bigg(\exp\big(-(2\nu s^{2})^{-1}\|\,{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r}-{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r^{\prime}}\|^{2}\big)\bigg)\,

\int_{{{{\mathbb{R}}^{\nu}}}}\exp\big(-2(\nu s^{2})^{-1}\|\,{\bm{\eta}}-\frac{1}{2}({\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r}+{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r^{\prime}})\|^{2}\big)\,d{\bm{\eta}}=0

\Longleftrightarrow((s/2)\sqrt{2\pi\nu})^{\nu}\,\sum_{r=1}^{N_{r}}\sum_{r^{\prime}=1}^{N_{r}}w_{r}w_{r^{\prime}}\exp\big(-(2\nu s^{2})^{-1}\|\,{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r}-{\bm{\eta}}_{\hbox{{\ppppcarac targ}}}^{r^{\prime}}\|^{2}\big)=0

\Longleftrightarrow{\bm{w}}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{N_{r}})={\hbox{\bf 0}}_{N_{r}}
.

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we have presented a novel functional approach that makes it possible to take into account a target set of realizations
in the Kullback-Leibler minimum principle for constructing a posterior probability measure from a prior probability measure defined by a given training set of realizations. This approach thus allows for integrating a target set of realizations in a supervised model, which is defined only by a small number of points in a training set.
It consists in constructing and analyzing a weak formulation of the Fourier transform of the probability measure (characteristic function) of the observed quantities of interest and to derive from it a finite representation of the functional constraint. On the basis of the positive Hermitian form associated with the Fourier transform of the probability measure, we have constructed and analyzed the properties of a functional family of functions, which only depends on the target set of the given realizations. These properties have allowed us to show the existence and the uniqueness of the posterior probability measure constructed by using the Kullback-Leibler minimum principle. The numerical aspects have been detailed in order to facilitate the implementation of the algorithms. The presented numerical illustration that is in high dimension demonstrates the efficiency and the robustness of the proposed method.

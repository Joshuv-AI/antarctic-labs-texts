Local well-posedness for the Zakharov system in dimension d ⩽ 3 d\leqslant 3
Akansha Sanwal

Abstract
. The Zakharov system in dimension d ⩽ 3 d\leqslant 3 is shown to be locally well-posed in Sobolev spaces H s × H l H^{s}\times H^{l} , extending the previously known result. We construct new solution spaces by modifying the X s , b X^{s,b} spaces, specifically by introducing temporal weights. We use contraction mapping principle to prove local well-posedness in the same. The result obtained is sharp up to endpoints.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem for the Zakharov system

(1.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u

\displaystyle=nu

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}n-\Delta n

\displaystyle=\Delta|u|^{2}

with initial data

(1.2)

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~~~n(x,0)=n_{0}(x),~~~\partial_{t}{n}(x,0)=n_{1}(x),

where
(u_{0},n_{0},n_{1})\in H^{s}\times H^{l}\times H^{l-1}
.

Here
u:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and
n:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denote the slowly changing amplitude of a high frequency electric field and the deviation of ion density from its equilibrium, respectively. This system was derived by Zakharov
[
23
]
to model the propagation of Langmuir waves in a plasma.

1.1.
Known results

Local well-posedness in
H^{2}\times H^{1}\times L^{2}
was shown in
[
18
]

along with some smoothing for the Schrödinger part for
d\leqslant 3
. The result was improved by Bourgain and Colliander
[
7
]

who proved well-posedness of the two and three dimensional system in the energy space (
H^{1}\times L^{2}
) using Fourier restriction norms and contraction mapping. Their result also includes small data global well-posedness if the
H^{1}
norm of the Schrödinger data is sufficiently small. In
[
11
]
, local well-posedness for dimension
d\geqslant 1
was shown for a range of indices
(s,l)
depending on
d
by refining the contraction mapping argument in Fourier restriction spaces. In particular, for the two and three dimensional system, local well-posedness is proved in
H^{s}\times H^{l}
for
(s,l)
satisfying
l\leqslant s\leqslant l+1
,
l\geqslant 0
and
2s-l\geqslant 1
. For the 1D system, the result covers the range
-\frac{1}{2}\leqslant s-l\leqslant 1,2s-l\geqslant\frac{1}{2}
. The best available result for local well-posedness of the system in three dimensions is proved in
[
2
]

which covers the range
l>-\frac{1}{2}
,
l\leqslant s\leqslant l+1
and
2s-l>\frac{1}{2}
(see Figure 1) while that in two space dimensions is proved in
[
3
]

for
(s,l)=(0,-\frac{1}{2})
. Both the aforementioned results exploit the transversality of the characteristic hypersurfaces in the resonant interactions to obtain sharp multilinear estimates. These estimates, referred to as generalisations of the Loomis Whitney inequality
[
16
,
4
,
5
]
, shall play a vital role in proving the non-linear estimates in sections 3 and 4.
For the 1D system, in
[
19
]
, global well-posedness without any smallness assumption on the initial data is proved in
H^{2}\times L^{2}
for
\frac{9}{10}<s<1
. In
[
20
]
, local well-posedness is shown in spaces
\widehat{H^{s,p}}
for
1<p<2
. Here, the regularity of the initial data can be lowered so that
s<0
and
l<-\frac{1}{2}
by using modified
X^{s,b}
spaces. Global well-posedness using mass conservation is proved in
[
9
]
for
(s,l)=(0,-\frac{1}{2})
which is the largest space where the system is locally well-posed. In
[
1
]
, global well-posedness and scattering results are proved.

We throw some light on ill-posedness results now. In
[
14
]
, for the 1D system, it is shown that the constraint
2s-l\geqslant\frac{1}{2}
is optimal in the sense that norm inflation occurs otherwise. For
l<-\frac{3}{2}
and
s=0
, it is shown that the data to solution map fails to be uniformly continuous from
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{l}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{l-1}(\mathbb{R})
to
C([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))\times C([0,T];H^{l}(\mathbb{R}))\times C([0,T];H^{l-1}(\mathbb{R}))
. This nearly matches the 1D result of
[
6
]
where the authors prove ill-posedness for
s<0
and
l\leqslant-\frac{3}{2}
. The failure of
C^{2}
differentiability of the data to solution map is proved for
l<-\frac{1}{2}
. In
[
8
, section 9]
, the authors prove that for a general dimension
d\geqslant 1
, the flow map is not
C^{2}
for
s,l
and
d
satisfying

l<\frac{d}{2}-2,~s-l>2,~2s-l<\frac{d-2}{2}
or
s-l<-1
. For
d=3
, this is the complement of the region described by
(1.3)
up to the boundaries. For
d=2
, stronger counterexamples in
[
3

prove that the flow map is not
C^{2}
for
2s-l<\frac{1}{2}
or
l<-\frac{1}{2}
. In
[
11

, using the close relation of the system
(1.1)-(1.2)
to the cubic non-linear Schrödinger equation, the authors prove that the data to solution map for the system fails to be Lipschitz continuous at the point
(s,l)=(\frac{1}{2},-\frac{1}{2})
. Using power series expansion for the solutions, as done in
[
15
]
for the Schrödinger equation, norm inflation results have been proved for the Zakharov system in dimension
d\geqslant 1
in
[
12
]
. In particular, for
d=3
it is proved that all the boundaries but

[

in dimension

In this article, we provide a unified local well-posedness result for the system

in

Theorem 1.1
.

The system
(1.1)
with initial conditions
(1.2)

d\leqslant 3
provided

(1.3)

l>-\frac{1}{2},\quad\max\Big\{l-1,\frac{l}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\Big\}<s<l+2.

1.1.1.
Outline

In section 2, we introduce the notation and previously known estimates. We also define our function spaces and state the required properties of
X^{s,b}
spaces therein. Known multilinear estimates are also enlisted. Sections 3 and 4 are devoted to the proof of the crucial multilinear estimates for the Schrödinger and wave non-linearity, respectively. Finally, in section 5, we give a short proof of theorem 1.1.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

2.1.
Reduced system

For
|\nabla|
:=
\sqrt{-\Delta}
, using the transformation
v=n-i|\nabla|^{-1}\partial_{t}n
, the system
(1.1)
formally reduces to the following first order system:

(2.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u

\displaystyle=uRe(v)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}v+|\nabla|v

\displaystyle=-|\nabla||u|^{2}.

We observe that
(u,v)
solves
(2.1)
iff
(u,Re(v))
solves
(1.1)
.
Henceforth, we shall consider the system
(2.1)
for our analysis.

2.2.
Fourier multipliers

Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be non-negative such that
\chi(r)=1
for
|r|\leqslant 1
,
\chi(r)=0
for
|r|\geqslant 2
. Set
\phi(r):=\chi(r)-\chi(2r)
and
\phi_{\lambda}(r):=\phi(r/\lambda)
. Then

\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}\\
,\lambda\geqslant 1}\phi_{\lambda}(r)=1,~~~\phi_{1}=\chi.

For
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}},\lambda>1
, we define the Fourier multipliers

P_{\lambda}=\phi_{\lambda}(|\nabla|)
,

P_{\lambda}^{(t)}=\phi_{\lambda}(|\partial_{t}|)
,
C_{\lambda}=\phi_{\lambda}(|i\partial_{t}+\Delta|)
,
Q_{\lambda}=\phi_{\lambda}(|i\partial_{t}\pm|\nabla||)
,

and for
\lambda=1
, we define

P_{1}=\chi(|\nabla|)
,

P_{1}^{(t)}=\chi(|\partial_{t}|)
,
C_{1}=\chi(|i\partial_{t}+\Delta|)
,
Q_{1}=\chi(|i\partial_{t}\pm|\nabla||)
.

Thus,
P_{\lambda}
is a Fourier multiplier which localises the spatial frequencies to the set
\{\frac{\lambda}{2}\leqslant|\xi|\leqslant 2\lambda\}
while
P_{\lambda}^{(t)}
localises the temporal frequencies to the set
\{\frac{\lambda}{2}\leqslant|\tau|\leqslant 2\lambda\}
. In a similar spirit,
C_{\lambda}
and
Q_{\lambda}
localise the space-time Fourier support to distances
\sim\lambda
from the paraboloid and the cone, respectively.
More precisely, for
L\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
the space-time Fourier supports of
C_{L},C_{1},Q_{L}
and
Q_{1}
can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{L}=\Big\{(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{L}{2}\leqslant|\tau+|\xi|^{2}|\leqslant 2L\Big\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}=\Big\{(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\tau+|\xi|^{2}|\leqslant 2\Big\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{L}^{\pm}=\Big\{(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{L}{2}\leqslant|\tau\pm|\xi||\leqslant 2L\Big\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\pm}=\Big\{(\tau,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\tau\pm|\xi||\leqslant 2\Big\}.

To restrict the Fourier support to larger sets, we use the notation

\displaystyle P_{\leqslant\lambda}=\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu\leqslant\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}(|\nabla|),\quad P_{\leqslant\lambda}^{(t)}=\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu\leqslant\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}(|\partial_{t}|),

\displaystyle C_{\leqslant\lambda}=\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu\leqslant\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}(|i\partial_{t}+\Delta|),\quad Q_{\leqslant\lambda}=\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{N}},\mu\leqslant\lambda}\phi_{\lambda}(|i\partial_{t}\pm|\nabla||).

For
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
, we use the shorthand
f_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}f
.

We use the notation
A\lesssim B
to indicate
A\leqslant cB
, for a constant
c>0
and
A\sim B
when both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
hold. We use
A\ll B
to denote
A\leqslant cB
for a constant
c
much smaller than 1.

2.3.
Function spaces

The space-time Fourier transform of a function
f
is denoted by
\mathcal{F}(f)
, where
(t,x)
are the physical space variables and
(\tau,\xi)
the corresponding Fourier variables. We use angled brackets to denote
\langle x\rangle:=(1+x^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
x+
denotes a number slightly bigger than
x
in the following.

We set
\theta:=\frac{1}{2}+
. Given
s,l\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the parameters
0\leqslant a,b<2-2\theta,\theta^{\prime},s^{\prime}
and
\beta
as follows:

\displaystyle a:=\begin{cases}s-l-3+4\theta,&s-l\geqslant 1\\
0,&s-l<1\end{cases}\text{,}\quad b:=\begin{cases}0,&s-l>0\\
(l-s)+2\theta-1,&s-l\leqslant 0\end{cases}\text{, }\quad\theta^{\prime}:=\begin{cases}\theta~,s-l\geqslant 0\\
1~,s-l<0\end{cases}\text{,}\quad

(2.2)

\displaystyle s^{\prime}:=\begin{cases}s,&s-l\geqslant 0\\
s+2\theta-2,&s-l<0\end{cases}\quad\text{ and}\quad\beta:=\begin{cases}l+a+2\theta-1,&s-l\geqslant 1\\
l,&-\frac{1}{2}\leqslant s-l<1\\
s-\theta+1,&s-l<-\frac{1}{2}\end{cases}.

For
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
,
s,a,b,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we control the frequency localised Schrödinger component of the Zakharov evolution by the following

\|u_{\lambda}\|_{S_{\lambda}^{s,l,\theta}}=\lambda^{s}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\lambda^{s}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta}\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda^{2}}u_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}+\lambda^{s^{\prime}-2a+b}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}u_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}.

To control the frequency localised Schrödinger non-linearity, we define

\displaystyle\|F_{\lambda}\|_{N_{\lambda}^{s,l,\theta-1}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{s+2\theta-3}\|P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\lambda^{s}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta-1}\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda^{2}}F_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda^{s^{\prime}-2a+b}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}F_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}.

For
l,\beta,a,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, the evolution of the frequency localised wave component is controlled by the following norm

\|v_{\lambda}\|_{W_{\lambda}^{l,s,\theta}}=\lambda^{l}\|v_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\lambda^{l-a}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle^{\theta}\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}Q_{\ll\lambda^{2}}v_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}+\lambda^{\beta-1}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle\mathcal{F}(Q_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}v_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}},

and the RHS of the wave equation is controlled by

\|G_{\lambda}\|_{R_{\lambda}^{l,s,\theta-1}}=\lambda^{l+2\theta-3}\|G_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\lambda^{l-a}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle^{\theta-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}Q_{\ll\lambda^{2}}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}+\lambda^{\beta-1}\|\mathcal{F}(Q_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}.

The parameters
a,b,s^{\prime},\beta
and
\theta^{\prime}
are required to prove the bilinear estimates in the full region
(1.3)
.

a
measures the loss of regularity for the Schrödinger component in the low (
\ll\lambda^{2}
) temporal frequency region as can be seen from the weight
m_{S}(\tau):=\big(\frac{\lambda+|\tau|}{\lambda^{2}}\big)^{a},0\leqslant a<1
. Note that

m_{S}(\tau)\sim\begin{cases}\lambda^{-a},~~~|\tau|\lesssim\lambda,\\
1,~~~~~|\tau|\sim\lambda^{2},\\
\end{cases}\\
\text{and}~~m_{S}(\tau)\gg 1,~~~|\tau|\gg\lambda^{2},

while

m_{W}(\tau):=\Big(\frac{\lambda+|\tau|}{\lambda}\Big)^{a}\gtrsim 1.

The parameter
b
gives a gain in regularity to the Schrödinger evolution in the high modulation regime and helps to achieve bilinear estimates for the wave non-linearity in the region
(1.3)
.
a
and
b
are not non-zero simultaneously and hence their sum is always less than
1
. It is this restriction on the upper bounds for the parameters
a
and
b
that does not allow us to achieve the boundaries (
s-l=2,-1
) of the region described by
(1.3)
.

In the region
l+1\leqslant s<l+2
, where the Schrödinger component is more regular, we require
a>0
. In the
“balanced”
region
l<s<l+1
, we choose
a=0=b
. In the final regime i.e.
l-1<s\leqslant l
, where the wave component is more regular, we choose
a=0,b>0
. Similarly, for the high modulation wave regularity
\beta
, we have
\beta\sim s-1
when
1\leqslant s-l<2
, which is greater than or equal to the
“ideal”
regularity
l
. In the balanced regime, it is chosen to be
l
, while in the thin strip
-1<s-l\leqslant-\frac{1}{2}
, where the wave is more regular, we choose
\beta
to be
\sim s+\frac{1}{2}
which is less than
l
. For the regularity
s^{\prime}
of the high modulation Schrödinger norm, we see that the change in the exponent of the modulation weight viz
\theta^{\prime}
, compensates for the loss. Note that in the low modulation, the
S_{\lambda}
and
W_{\lambda}
norms are exactly the
X^{s,\theta}
norms with
\theta>\frac{1}{2}
.

We also observe that the choice of the parameter
\theta^{\prime}(>\frac{1}{2})
enables us to use various properties of the standard
X^{s,b}
spaces and the bilinear estimates from
[
3
,
2
]
(see Section
2.6
) appropriately.

Remark 2.1
.

Equation
(2.2)
provides us with one possible choice for the parameters. We reckon other choices might be plausible too.

The evolution of the full Schrödinger solution and non-linearity is controlled by the following norms

\|u\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}=\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\|u_{\lambda}\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}_{\lambda}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},~~~\|F\|_{N^{s,l,\theta-1}}=\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\|F_{\lambda}\|_{N^{s,l,\theta-1}_{\lambda}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and that of the wave solution and wave non-linearity is controlled by

\|v\|_{W^{l,s,\theta}}=\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\|v_{\lambda}\|_{W^{l,s,\theta}_{\lambda}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},~~~\|G\|_{R^{l,s,\theta-1}}=\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\|G_{\lambda}\|_{R^{l,s,\theta-1}_{\lambda}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For
0<T\leqslant 1
, we localise the norms in time by defining

\displaystyle\|u\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}(T)}=\inf\{\|\tilde{u}\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}:\tilde{u}\in S^{s,l,\theta},\tilde{u}\big|_{(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}=u\}

The norms
N^{s,l,\theta-1}(T),W^{l,s,\theta}(T)
and
R^{l,s,\theta-1}(T)
are defined similarly.

2.4.
Properties of
X^{s,b}
spaces

Since our function spaces are a variant of the standard
X^{s,b}
spaces, we note some properties of the same in the following. The proofs can be found in
[
11
, Lemma 2.1, 2.2]
,
[
22
, section 2.6]
.

(i)
Let
u
be a solution to the problem
\partial_{t}u=L(u)+F
with
L=ih(\nabla/i)
for some real valued polynomial
h

with
u(0)=f
. For a smooth time cutoff
\chi
such that
\chi(t)=1,|t|\leqslant 1,\chi(t)=0,|t|>2
and
\chi_{T}(t):=\chi\big(\frac{t}{T}\big)
,
0<T\leqslant 1
and
s,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

(2.3)

\|\chi(t)e^{tL}f\|_{X^{s,b}_{\tau=h(\xi)}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}}.

(ii)
If
v
is a solution to the problem
\partial_{t}v=L(v)+F
, with
L=ih(\nabla/i)~v(0)=0
, we have for
-\frac{1}{2}<b^{\prime}\leqslant 0\leqslant b\leqslant b^{\prime}+1
,

(2.4)

\|\chi_{T}(t)v\|_{X^{s,b}_{\tau=h(\xi)}}\lesssim T^{1+b^{\prime}-b}\|F\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}_{\tau=h(\xi)}}.

(iii)
For
\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{d}{2},2\leqslant r<\infty
, the following Strichartz estimate holds

(2.5)

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}+}_{\Delta}},

where the
\Delta
in the subscript denotes that the space in consideration is for the Schrödinger equation.
For the wave part, we only use

(2.6)

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{0,\frac{1}{2}+}_{|\nabla|}}.

(iv)
For
b>\frac{1}{2}
,
X^{s,b}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow C(\mathbb{R};H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),~X^{s,b}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\hookrightarrow C((0,T);H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

(v)
For
2\leqslant p<\infty,b\geqslant\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}
, we have

(2.7)

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{p}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{X^{0,b}}.

(vi)
For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
-\frac{1}{2}<b^{\prime}<b<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

(2.8)

\|u\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}(T)}\lesssim T^{b-b^{\prime}}\|u\|_{X^{s,b}(T)}.

(vii)
Duality and complex conjugation:

(X^{s,b}_{\tau=h(\xi)})^{\prime}=X^{-s,-b}_{\tau=-h(-\xi)},\quad\|\overline{u}\|_{X^{s,b}_{\tau=-h(-\xi)}}=\|u\|_{X^{s,b}_{\tau=h(\xi)}}.

2.5.
Duhamel formulae and energy inequalities

Let
\mathcal{I}_{S}
and
\mathcal{I}_{W}
be the solution operators for the inhomogeneous Schrödinger equation and the half wave equation, respectively, i.e.

\mathcal{I}_{S}[F](t)=-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)\Delta}F(s)ds,\quad\mathcal{I}_{W}[G](t)=-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-s)|\nabla|}G(s)ds.

Then we have the following estimates.

Lemma 2.2
.

(Energy inequality for the Schrödinger equation)
Let
a,b,s^{\prime},\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
be as defined in
(2.2)
. For any
\lambda\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
and a smooth time cutoff
\chi
, we have:

\displaystyle\|\chi(t)e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}_{\lambda}}\lesssim\lambda^{s}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

\displaystyle\|\chi(t)\mathcal{I}_{S}[F_{\lambda}]\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}_{\lambda}}\lesssim\|F_{\lambda}\|_{N^{s,l,\theta-1}_{\lambda}}.

Proof.

We first note the following property of the linear group wrt temporal frequency and modulation localisation, which we shall use repeatedly

(2.9)

C_{\star}f=e^{tL}P_{\star}^{(t)}(e^{-tL}f).

The norm for the free solution is given by

\displaystyle\|\chi(t)e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{s}\|\chi(t)e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}+\lambda^{s}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta}\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda^{2}}\chi(t)e^{it\Delta}u_{0})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\lambda^{s^{\prime}-2a+b}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}\chi(t)e^{it\Delta}u_{0})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}.

Using
(2.5)
and
(2.3)
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{s}\|\chi(t)e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{s}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta}\mathcal{F}(\chi(t)e^{it\Delta}u_{0})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{s}\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}},

and for the second term using
(2.9)
, we have

(iii)~b>0,a=0:

Lemma 2.3
.

|m_{W}|\gtrsim 1
,

\displaystyle\text{(II)}\lesssim\lambda^{\beta-1}\Big\|\chi(t)\int_{0}^{t}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(s)}e^{-is|\nabla|}G_{\lambda}(s)ds\Big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\beta-1}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle^{-1}\mathcal{F}(Q_{\ll\lambda^{2}}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{l-a}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle^{\theta-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}Q_{\ll\lambda^{2}}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}.

(III)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\beta-1}\Big\|P_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}(\chi^{\prime}(t)\int_{0}^{t}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(s)}e^{-is|\nabla|}G_{\lambda}(s)ds)\Big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}+\lambda^{\beta-1}\|P_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}(\chi(t)P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}e^{-it|\nabla|}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

The first term of (III) is treated like (II) and the second decomposed as follows

\displaystyle\lambda^{\beta-1}\|(P_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}(P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}\chi(t)P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}e^{-it|\nabla|}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}+\lambda^{\beta-1}\|(P_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}(P_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}\chi(t)P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}e^{-it|\nabla|}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

The first term above does not contribute and for the second, we have

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\beta-1}\|(P_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}\chi(t)-\chi(t)P_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}^{(t)})P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}e^{-it|\nabla|}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}+\lambda^{\beta-1}\|\chi(t)P_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}e^{-it|\nabla|}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{\beta-3}\|P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}e^{-it|\nabla|}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{l-a}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle^{\theta-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}Q_{\ll\lambda^{2}}G_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}},

where the choice of the parameter
\beta
and
|m_{W}|\gtrsim 1
ensure that the last inequality holds.
∎

3.
Multilinear estimates for Schrödinger non-linearity

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
d\leqslant 3
and
s,l
in the range
(1.3)
. There exist
a,b,s,l,\beta,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
such that the estimate

(3.1)

\|uRe(v)\|_{N^{s,l,\theta-1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}\|v\|_{W^{l,s,\theta}}

holds.

Proof.

We choose the parameters
a,b,s^{\prime},l,\beta,\theta^{\prime}
as in
(2.2)
and begin by noting the following characterisation of the
N^{s,l,\theta-1}
norm for the non-linearity in the case
0\leqslant a<\frac{1}{2}
:

(3.2)

\|F_{\lambda}\|_{N_{\lambda}^{s,l,\theta-1}}\approx\lambda^{s}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta-1}\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda^{2}}F_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}+\lambda^{s^{\prime}-2a+b}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}F_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}.

For
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2}-a
, using Bernstein’s inequality and Sobolev embedding, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lambda^{s+2\theta-3}\|P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{s+2\theta-3+\frac{2}{r}}\|P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{r}L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{s+2\theta-2-a}\|P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{s+2\theta-2-2a}\|(\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{s+2\theta-2-2a+b}\|\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}C_{\approx\lambda^{2}}F_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{s^{\prime}-2a+b}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda^{2}}F_{\lambda})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}},

where we obtain the second last inequality by noting that
b\geqslant 0
.

So, for
0\leqslant a<\frac{1}{2}
, it suffices to show:

(3.4)

\Big(\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{0}^{2s}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta-1}\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}P_{\lambda_{0}}(uv))\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim\|u\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}\|v\|_{W^{l,s,\theta}}

(3.5)

\Big(\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{0}^{2(s^{\prime}-2a+b)}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda_{0}+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda_{0}^{2}}P_{\lambda_{0}}(uv))\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\lesssim\|u\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}\|v\|_{W^{l,s,\theta}}.

In the case
\frac{1}{2}\leqslant a<1
, we additionally need to prove the following:

(3.6)

\Big(\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\lambda_{0}^{2(s+2\theta-3)}\|P_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}^{(t)}P_{\lambda_{0}}(uv)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{s}}\|v\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{l}}.

We now proceed to prove
(3.4)
and
(3.5)
.
We decompose the non-linearity
uv
as

uv=\sum_{\lambda_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}P_{\lambda_{0}}(uv)

Further, we distinguish the high-low, low-high and the balanced interactions as follows:

P_{\lambda_{0}}(uv)=\sum_{\lambda_{1}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}P_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{\lambda_{1}}v)=\sum_{\lambda_{1}\ll\lambda_{0}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}P_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{\lambda_{1}}v)+\sum_{\lambda_{0}\ll\lambda_{1}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}P_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{\lambda_{1}}v)+\sum_{\lambda_{0}\sim\lambda_{1}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}P_{\lambda_{0}}(u_{\lambda_{1}}v).

We state that high (low) modulation for the spatially localised Schrödinger solution
u_{|\xi|}
means
\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle\gtrsim(\ll)|\xi|^{2}
while for the wave
v_{|\xi|}
, it means
\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle\gtrsim(\ll)|\xi|^{2}
. We abbreviate high modulation by
H
and low modulation by
L
.
The subscripts
1
and
2
are appended with
\tau
and
\xi
to distinguish the temporal frequencies and the spatial frequencies of the Schrödinger and wave solutions.
The output temporal frequencies are given by
\tau_{0}=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}
. For the Schrödinger solution, low modulation occurs when the temporal frequencies are of size
\sim|\xi|^{2}(\tau=-|\xi|^{2})
.
For the wave solution, temporal frequencies of size
\sim|\xi|(\tau=|\xi|)
lead to a free wave solution. Other than that, the sizes of the modulation and the temporal frequencies go hand in hand for a frequency localised wave solution.

All the possible interactions are treated individually. In cases with low output modulation, the required bilinear estimates are reduced to trilinear estimates by duality using the property (vii) from section 2.4. To distinguish the dual term, we use
-s
and
-l
instead of
s
and
l
, respectively, in the subscripts of the norms for the Schrödinger and wave components.
Each subcase can be summed up depending on the size of the interacting frequencies. The constraints required to obtain the estimate for the full norms are listed at the end of each subcase, and we do not exclude points with logarithmic losses since our result does not cover the boundaries.

Remark 3.2
.

In the following, we treat the case of high spatial (and temporal) frequencies (
|\xi_{i}|,|\tau_{i}|\gg 1,i=0,1,2
). In most of the cases, the estimates for the low frequencies (all the frequencies are of size
\sim 1
or the size of the lowest frequency in the interaction is
\sim 1
) follow using the same arguments without having to decompose the space-time Fourier supports of the interacting solutions. Hence, we do not mention it explicitly for each case. However, in some cases the arguments need to be modified for the low frequency cases. We shall do it wherever required.

Case I. Low to high interaction (
\lambda_{1}\ll\lambda_{0}
)

We decompose
u_{\lambda_{1}}
and
v_{\lambda_{0}}
as follows:

u_{\lambda_{1}}=C_{\ll\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}+C_{\gtrsim\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}},\quad v_{\lambda_{0}}=Q_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}+Q_{\gtrsim\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}.

The following interactions can be distinguished on the basis of the size of the modulation:

1.
H\times H\rightarrow H

We require to prove
(3.5)
. Using the size of the modulation, the product estimate and Bernstein’s inequality respectively, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}^{s^{\prime}-2a+b}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda_{0}+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda_{0}^{2}}(C_{\gtrsim\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}Q_{\gtrsim\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}))\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{s^{\prime}-3a+b+2\theta^{\prime}-2}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{d}{2}}\|(\lambda_{0}+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|(\lambda_{0}+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}Q_{\gtrsim\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{s^{\prime}-3a+b+2\theta^{\prime}-\beta-1}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{d}{2}-s+2a}\sup_{|\tau_{1}|}\Big(\frac{\lambda_{0}+|\tau_{1}|}{\lambda_{1}+|\tau_{1}|}\Big)^{a}\sup_{|\tau_{2}|\gtrsim\lambda_{0}^{2}}\frac{(\lambda_{0}+|\tau_{2}|)^{a}}{\langle\tau_{2}-\lambda_{0}\rangle}

\displaystyle\quad\times\lambda_{1}^{s^{\prime}-2a}\|(\lambda_{1}+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}~\lambda_{0}^{\beta-1}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle\mathcal{F}(Q_{\gtrsim\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{s^{\prime}+b+2\theta^{\prime}-\beta-3}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{d}{2}-s+a}\|u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{S_{\lambda_{1}}^{s,l,\theta}}\|v_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{W_{\lambda_{0}}^{l,s,\theta}}.

The last inequality above follows from
(2.5)
. Since
s^{\prime}+2\theta^{\prime}=s+2\theta
, we can sum up the subcase in
\lambda_{1}\ll\lambda_{0}
to obtain
(3.5)
provided
s-\beta\leqslant 3-2\theta-b
and
\beta\geqslant-3+\frac{d}{2}+2\theta+a+b
.

2.
H\times H\rightarrow L

We consider two cases for the temporal frequencies:

a.

|\tau_{1}|\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{2}
,
|\tau_{2}|\sim\lambda_{0}^{2}

We prove
(3.4)
using duality. We consider the expression
I
defined in
(2.10)
and use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality as follows:

(3.7)

\displaystyle|I(\mathcal{F}(Q_{\sim\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\lambda_{1}^{2}\lesssim\cdot\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}))|\lesssim\|Q_{\sim\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|C_{\lambda_{1}^{2}\lesssim\cdot\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}\overline{C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

On decomposing the space-time Fourier supports of
C_{\lambda_{1}^{2}\lesssim\cdot\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}
,
\overline{C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}}}
into pieces
L_{1}
and
L_{0}
, respectively and applying the bilinear estimate (2.12), we get

\displaystyle(3.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\sim\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L_{0}\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}\\
L_{1}\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{2}\end{subarray}}(L_{0}L_{1})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\lambda_{1}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{\lambda_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|C_{L_{1}}u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|C_{L_{0}}w_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{-\beta-\frac{5}{2}+s+2\theta}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{d-1}{2}-s+a}\lambda_{1}^{s^{\prime}-2a}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}((\lambda_{1}+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\lambda_{1}^{2}\lesssim\cdot\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\lambda_{0}^{\beta-1}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle\mathcal{F}(Q_{\sim\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}\lambda_{0}^{-s}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{1-\theta}\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

In case
\lambda_{1}\sim\lambda_{0}\sim 1

\displaystyle|I(\mathcal{F}(Q_{\sim\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\lambda_{1}^{2}\lesssim\cdot\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}))|

b.

|\tau_{1}|,|\tau_{2}|\gg\lambda_{0}^{2}

\displaystyle|I(\mathcal{F}(Q_{\gg\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\gg\lambda_{0}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}))|

H\times L\rightarrow H

(3.8)

\displaystyle|I(\mathcal{F}(Q_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\sim\lambda_{0}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}))|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|C_{\sim\lambda_{0}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|P_{\lambda_{1}}(\overline{Q_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}}C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}})\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

The spatial frequency support of

is localised to frequencies of size
\sim\lambda_{1}
. Using orthogonality, we can reduce the estimate to the case when the spatial supports of
Q_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}
and

are also localised to frequencies of size
\sim\lambda_{1}

\overline{Q_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{0}}}
and
C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}}
into pieces
L_{2}
and
L_{0}
respectively and applying the bilinear estimate
(2.13)

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{\frac{d-3}{2}+s+2\theta}\lambda_{1}^{-\beta-s-\frac{3}{2}}\|u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{S_{\lambda_{1}}^{s,l,\theta}}\|v_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{W_{\lambda_{1}}^{l,s,\theta}}\|w_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{S^{-s,l,1-\theta}_{\lambda_{0}}}.

One can sum the above estimate to obtain
(3.4)
provided
s+\beta\geqslant-\frac{3}{2}
and
\beta\geqslant-3+\frac{d}{2}+2\theta
.

For

\displaystyle|I(\mathcal{F}(Q_{\sim\lambda_{1}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{1}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}}),\mathcal{F}(C_{\ll\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}))|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|Q_{\sim\lambda_{1}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}\|C_{\ll\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\|C_{\ll\lambda_{0}^{2}}w_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{W^{l,s,\theta}_{\lambda_{1}}}\|u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}_{\lambda_{1}}}\|w_{\lambda_{0}}\|_{S^{-s,l,1-\theta}_{\lambda_{0}}}.

6.
L\times L\rightarrow H

From the constraint
\tau_{0}=\tau_{1}+\tau_{2}
, we note that the output temporal frequencies are of size
\sim\lambda_{1}^{2}
. This implies that the output has a modulation of size
\sim\lambda_{1}^{2}
. We use the size of the modulation and the temporal frequencies and employ the bilinear estimate for wave-Schrödinger interaction. By orthogonality arguments, we reduce the estimates to the case when the spatial Fourier supports of
C_{\ll\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}
and

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}^{s^{\prime}-2a+b}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda_{0}+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda_{0}^{2}}(C_{\ll\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}Q_{\ll\lambda_{1}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{1}}))\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{s^{\prime}-2a+b}\lambda_{1}^{2\theta^{\prime}-2+2a}\|C_{\ll\lambda_{1}^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}Q_{\ll\lambda_{1}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

To sum this estimate, we require
s+l\geqslant-\frac{5}{2}+2\theta^{\prime}+2a
and

7.
L\times H\rightarrow H

With Bernstein’s inequality and the product estimate, we get

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}^{s^{\prime}-2a+b}\|\langle\tau+|\xi|^{2}\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{F}((\lambda_{0}+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}C_{\gtrsim\lambda_{0}^{2}}(C_{\ll\lambda^{2}}u_{\lambda_{1}}Q_{\gtrsim\lambda_{1}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{1}}))\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\lambda_{1}^{\beta-1}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle\mathcal{F}(Q_{\gtrsim\lambda_{1}^{2}}v_{\lambda_{1}})\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda_{0}^{s^{\prime}-2a+b+2\theta^{\prime}-2+\frac{d}{2}}\lambda_{1}^{-s-\beta+3a-1}\|u_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{S_{\lambda_{1}}^{s,l,\theta}}\|v_{\lambda_{1}}\|_{W_{\lambda_{1}}^{l,s,\theta}}.

If
s+\beta\geqslant-1+3a
and
\beta\geqslant-3+\frac{d}{2}+2\theta+a+b
, we can obtain
(3.5)
by summing up the above estimate.

Hence, for
\lambda_{0}\ll\lambda_{1}
, we conclude that the following constraints are required for summability in dimension
d\leqslant 3
:

(3.17)

\bullet~l\geqslant-\frac{3}{2}+2\theta+b\quad\bullet~\beta\geqslant-\frac{3}{2}+2\theta+a+b\quad\bullet~s+l\geqslant\max\Big\{-2\theta+2a,-\frac{5}{2}+2\theta^{\prime}+2a\Big\}\quad\bullet~s+\beta\geqslant-1+3a.

For
\frac{1}{2}\leqslant a<1
, the estimate
(3.6)
holds provided
l\geqslant-\frac{3}{2}+2\theta,s+l\geqslant 0
and
s-l\leqslant 3-2\theta
. It can be proved by imitating the proof of the product estimate
\|fg\|_{H^{s-3+2\theta}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|g\|_{H^{l}}
for each case above.

From
(3.10),(3.14)
and
(3.17)

\displaystyle\bullet~l>-\frac{3}{2}+2\theta+b\quad\bullet~\beta>-\frac{3}{2}+2\theta+a+b\quad\bullet~s-l\leqslant\min\{2-2\theta+a-b,3-2\theta\}

\displaystyle\bullet~s-\beta\leqslant\min\Big\{3-2\theta-b,\frac{5}{2}-2\theta\Big\}\quad\bullet~s+l\geqslant\max\Big\{-\frac{5}{2}+2\theta^{\prime}+2a,-2\theta+2a\Big\}\quad\bullet s+\beta\geqslant-1+3a

are the requirements for the estimate
(3.1)
to hold.
∎

4.
Multilinear estimates for wave non-linearity

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
d\leqslant 3
and
s,l
satisfy
(1.3)
. There exist

such that the estimate

(4.1)

\||\nabla|(\overline{\varphi}\psi)\|_{R^{l,s,\theta-1}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}\|\psi\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}

holds.

Proof.

We choose the parameters as in
(2.2)
. Using the definition of the norms, it suffices to show the following:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\mu^{2(l-a+1)}\|\langle\tau-|\xi|\rangle^{\theta^{\prime}-1}\mathcal{F}((\mu+|\partial_{t}|)^{a}Q_{\ll\mu^{2}}P_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi}\psi))\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}\|\psi\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\mu^{2\beta}\|\mathcal{F}(Q_{\gtrsim\mu^{2}}P_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi}\psi))\|_{L_{\tau,\xi}^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}\|\psi\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{\mu\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}}\mu^{2(l+2\theta-2)}\|P_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi}\psi)\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}\|\psi\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}}

The definition of high and low modulation remains the same as in section 3, so do the abbreviations
H
and
L
. We now append
1
or
2
as subscripts with
\tau
to denote the temporal frequencies of
\varphi
and
\psi
. The same is done for the spatial frequencies.

Employing the frequency trichotomy, we decompose the non-linearity
P_{\mu}(\bar{\varphi}\psi)
into high-low, balanced (high-high) and low-high interactions as follows:

(4.5)

P_{\mu}(\overline{\varphi}\psi)=P_{\mu}(\overline{\varphi}\psi_{\ll\mu})+\sum_{\mu\lesssim\lambda_{1}\sim\lambda_{2}}P_{\mu}(\overline{\varphi_{\lambda_{1}}}\psi_{\lambda_{2}})+P_{\mu}(\overline{\varphi_{\ll\mu}}\psi)=\sum_{\lambda\ll\mu}P_{\mu}(\overline{\varphi_{\mu}}\psi_{\lambda})+\sum_{\mu\lesssim\lambda_{1}\sim\lambda_{2}}P_{\mu}(\overline{\varphi_{\lambda_{1}}}\psi_{\lambda_{2}})+\sum_{\lambda\ll\mu}P_{\mu}(\overline{\varphi_{\lambda}}\psi_{\mu})

It suffices to consider the first two interactions above.

Remark 4.2

As in section 3, we consider the cases where
|\xi_{i}|,|\tau_{i}|\gg 1,i=1,2
. For the very low frequency cases (
|\xi_{i}|\sim 1,i=1,2

5.
Proof of Theorem 1.1

Given the non-linear estimates proved in sections 3 and 4, we can achieve a small data local well posedness result, see
[
8
, section 5]
for a simplified small data local well-posedness argument. To achieve a large data result, we need to extract a small power of
T
on the RHS of the non-linear estimates.

We start with proving a slightly weaker form of the energy inequalities but with a small power of
T
on the RHS. Note that for the
X^{s,\theta}
type part of the norms, the required factor comes from property
(2.8)
. It remains to extract the factor for the
L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}
part of the norm.

To that end, we note for
0<T<1
and a smooth time cutoff
\chi_{T}

\displaystyle\lambda^{s}\|\chi_{T}\mathcal{I}_{S}[C_{\sim\lambda^{2}}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}]\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{s}\|\chi_{4T}C_{\sim\lambda^{2}}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{s}T\|C_{\sim\lambda^{2}}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}.

Interpolating this with
\lambda^{s}\|\chi_{T}\mathcal{I}_{S}[C_{\sim\lambda^{2}}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}]\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{s-2}\|P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}
, we obtain for some
0<\delta_{1}\leqslant-\frac{1}{2}+\theta\ll 1
,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lambda^{s}\|\chi_{T}\mathcal{I}_{S}[C_{\sim\lambda^{2}}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}]\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{s-2+2\delta_{1}}T^{\delta_{1}}\|C_{\sim\lambda^{2}}P_{\ll\lambda^{2}}^{(t)}F_{\lambda}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim T^{\delta_{1}}\|F_{\lambda}\|_{N^{s,l,\theta-1}}

Note that we considered only the low (
\ll\lambda^{2}
) temporal frequency localised non-linearity because for the other case, we can again use property
(2.8)
since
m_{S}(\tau)\gtrsim 1
.

As stated, from property
(2.8)
in section 2.4, for
\delta_{2}>0,~-\frac{1}{2}<\theta-1<\theta-1+\delta_{2}<\frac{1}{2}
, we have
only
for the
X^{s,\theta}
part of the
N
norm

(5.2)

\displaystyle\|F\|_{N^{s,l,\theta-1}(T)}\lesssim T^{\delta_{2}}\|F\|_{N^{s,l,\theta-1+\delta_{2}}(T)}.

From
(5.1)
and
(5.2)
we conclude

(5.3)

\|\chi_{T}\mathcal{I}_{S}[F]\|_{S^{s,l,\theta}(T)}\lesssim T^{\delta}\|F\|_{N^{s,l,\tilde{\theta}}(T)}

where
\delta=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}>0
and
\tilde{\theta}=\theta-1+\delta
.

Using the same arguments for the wave solution and wave non-linearity, we have

(5.4)

\|\chi_{T}\mathcal{I}_{W}[G]\|_{W^{l,s,\theta}(T)}\lesssim T^{\delta}\|G\|_{R^{l,s,\tilde{\theta}}(T)}.

We now head to prove theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

We call
(u,v)\in S^{s,l,\theta}(T)\times W^{l,s,\theta}(T)
a solution to the system
(2.1)-(2.2)
with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s}\times H^{l}
if

(5.5)

\displaystyle u(t)

\displaystyle=\chi(e^{it\Delta}u_{0}+\chi_{T}\mathcal{I}_{S}[uRe(v)])(t)

\displaystyle v(t)

\displaystyle=\chi(e^{it|\nabla|}v_{0}+\chi_{T}\mathcal{I}_{W}[|\nabla||u|^{2}])(t),

for all
0<T<1
.

To apply a contraction mapping argument, we write
(5.5)
as
\Gamma(u,v)(t)=(u,v)(t)
. We prove that
\Gamma
is a contraction on the space
S^{s,l,\theta}(T)\times W^{l,s,\theta}(T)
for a suitably chosen
T
. Define
R:=\|u_{0}\|_{H_{x}^{s}}+\|v_{0}\|_{H_{x}^{l}}
. We shall drop the superscripts on the norms for notational convenience. Then, using lemma 2.2, lemma 2.3,
(5.3),(5.4),(3.1),(4.1)
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(u,v)\|_{S\times W(T)}

\displaystyle=\|\chi(t)e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{S(T)}+\|\chi_{T}(t)\mathcal{I}_{S}[uRe(v)]\|_{S(T)}+\|\chi(t)e^{it|\nabla|}v_{0}\|_{W(T)}+\|\chi_{T}(t)\mathcal{I}_{W}[|\nabla||u|^{2}]\|_{W(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}+T^{\delta}\|u\|_{S(T)}\|v\|_{W(T)}+\|v_{0}\|_{H^{l}_{x}}+T^{\delta}\|u\|_{S(T)}^{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant C(\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}_{x}}+\|v_{0}\|_{H^{l}_{x}}+T^{\delta}(\|(u,v)\|_{S\times W(T)}+\|(u,v)\|_{S\times W(T)}^{2})).

Hence,
\Gamma
is well-defined on a ball of radius
2CR
in the space
S^{s,l,\theta}(T)\times W^{l,s,\theta}(T)
if
T
is chosen such that
C_{1}T^{\delta}R<1
for some constant
C_{1}
. In order to show that
\Gamma
is a contraction, we consider

\displaystyle\|

\displaystyle\Gamma(u_{1},v_{1})-\Gamma(u_{2},v_{2})\|_{S\times W(T)}

\displaystyle=\|\chi_{T}(t)\mathcal{I}_{S}[u_{1}Re(v_{1})-u_{2}Re(v_{2})]\|_{S(T)}+\|\chi_{T}(t)\mathcal{I}_{W}[|\nabla|(|u_{1}|^{2}-|u_{2}|^{2})]\|_{W(T)}

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\delta}(\|u_{1}Re(v_{1})-u_{2}Re(v_{1})+u_{2}Re(v_{1})-u_{2}Re(v_{2})\|_{N^{s,l,\tilde{\theta}}(T)}+\||u_{1}|^{2}-|u_{2}|^{2}\|_{R^{l,s,\tilde{\theta}(T)}})

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\delta}(\|v_{1}\|_{W(T)}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{S(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{S(T)}\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{W(T)}+\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{S(T)}\|u_{1}+u_{2}\|_{S(T)})

\displaystyle\lesssim T^{\delta}(\|u_{1}\|_{S(T)}+\|v_{1}\|_{W(T)}+\|u_{2}\|_{S(T)}+\|v_{2}\|_{W(T)})(\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{S(T)}+\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{W(T)})

\displaystyle=CT^{\delta}(\|(u_{1},v_{1})\|_{S\times W(T)}+\|(u_{2},v_{2})\|_{S\times W(T)})(\|(u_{1}-u_{2},v_{1}-v_{2})\|_{S\times W(T)}),

Weak and Strong Solutions to Nonlinear SPDEs with Unbounded Noise
Daniel Goodair

Abstract
We introduce an extended variational framework for nonlinear SPDEs with unbounded noise, defining three different solution types of increasing strength along with criteria to establish their existence. The three notions can be understood as probabilistically and analytically weak, probabilistically strong and analytically weak, as well as probabilistically and analytically strong. Our framework facilitates several well-posedness results for the Navier-Stokes Equation with transport noise, equipped with the no-slip and Navier boundary conditions.



Introduction

The theoretical analysis of stochastic partial differential equations, abbreviated to SPDEs, has been recently established as one of mathematics’ most exciting prospects. Attention has long been given to these equations due to their rich applications in areas such as turbulence, population dynamics, finance and neuroscience, of which more can be understood from the monographs [
39
,
40

49

regularity structures

27

variational approach
pioneered by Étienne Pardoux, [
48
], whereby the equation is formatted in a Gelfand Triple for a noise valued in the Hilbert Space.

From the modelling perspective, it is in applications to fluid dynamics and turbulence where the literature has been thriving. The choice of noise to best encapsulate physical properties of fluid equations is in constant study, now yielding a strong argument for transport type stochastic perturbations (where the stochastic integral depends on the gradient of the solution). The paper of Brzeźniak, Capinski and Flandoli [
4
] in 1992 was one of the first to bring attention to the significance of these equations with transport noise, whilst such ideas have recently been cemented through the specific stochastic transport schemes of [
29
] and [
43
]. In these papers Holm and Mémin establish a new class of stochastic equations driven by transport type noise which serve as fluid dynamics models by adding uncertainty in the transport of fluid parcels to reflect the unresolved scales. The physical significance of such equations in modelling, numerical analysis and data assimilation continues to be well documented, see [

The first-order noise operator introduces a singularity into the Hilbert Space of the variational framework, so such equations do not fit into this classical theory. The noise is, however, still well-defined in traditional function spaces, suggesting that the heavy machinery and distribution-tailored approaches of Hairer, Gubinelli et al. are not particularly appropriate. Our approach is to extend techniques of the variational framework in order to treat nonlinear SPDEs with potentially unbounded noise in their optimal spaces. •

Section
3
concerns weak solutions. Assumption Set 1 is expanded upon in order to obtain uniqueness, giving rise to Assumption Set 2 of Subsection
3.1
. The definition of a solution and statement of the existence and uniqueness is given in Subsection
3.2
. The proof is given in Subsection
3.3
, where uniqueness facilitates passage from martingale weak solutions to weak solutions through a classical Yamada-Watanabe result. We draw attention to the fact that martingale weak solutions are only shown to exist for an initial condition belonging to
L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;U\right)
; weak solutions are initially shown to exist only for such an initial condition as well, though this is then relieved to the unbounded case. The method involves chopping up the unbounded initial condition onto intervals on which it is bounded, obtaining the relevant solutions, and then piecing these solutions together to obtain one for the original unbounded initial condition. We are unable to execute this approach for probabilistically weak solutions, as for each interval on which the initial condition is bounded one may obtain a solution on a distinct probability space.

•

Section
4
addresses strong solutions. Our assumptions are again expanded with Assumption Set 3 in Subsection
4.1
. The result boils down to showing that the weak solution has the additional regularity of a strong solution. The first step towards this end is taken in Subsection
4.3
where uniform estimates at the level of the Galerkin System are shown, for the energy norm of strong solutions, up until first hitting times of the processes in the energy norm of weak solutions. Relative compactness arguments ensure that our weak solution gains this regularity up until any stopping time which is less than at least a subsequence of the aforementioned first hitting times. Two questions now present themselves in looking to quantify these times:

1.

Can such a lesser stopping time be chosen to be
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
positive?

2.

If we increase the threshold for the first hitting times, can this lesser stopping time be taken to infinity?

The first question was answered by Glatt-Holtz and Ziane in [
19
], Lemma 5.1. The abstract lemma asserts that, under assumptions of a Cauchy property of the Galerkin System up until their first hitting times and some weak equicontinuity at the
initial
time, then a limiting process and
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
positive stopping time smaller than a subsequence of the first hitting times exist (which are then argued to be a local strong solution, as a limit of the Galerkin Approximation). No characterisation of this limiting stopping time is given though, hence completely separate arguments are required to pass to a
maximal
solution and further still to show that the maximal solution is global. Complete, and heavy, arguments to give rise to a maximal strong solution, and further to characterise the maximal time by the blow-up in the energy norm of strong solutions, are given in [
24
] Subsection 3.6. Further still, global solutions would then be deduced by directly controlling the energy of the solution, which requires analysis in a space in which the evolution equation may not hold; this is certainly true of the example which we consider in Subsection
5.1
.

We clarify the second question with an extension of [
19
] Lemma 5.1, Proposition
6.1
. This proposition greatly simplifies the quoted procedure for maximality and blow-up, and enables global solutions to be deduced through an estimate of the energy norm of
weak
solutions, requiring analysis in a space in which the evolution equation certainly does hold. The extension is such that if instead one imposes that the processes satisfy a weak equicontinuity assumption at
all
times then the limiting stopping time can be taken as a first hitting time of the limiting process for an arbitrarily large hitting parameter. Application of this result
immediately
yields that solutions exist up until blow-up, pertinently as blow-up
in the energy norm of weak solutions
in our application; we emphasise again the advantage that this has, as the previous approaches discussed would only produce a characterisation of the maximal time in the energy norm of the strong solution. Our result demands a higher level estimate only for the Galerkin System, which is clearer as the evolution equation is satisfied in all relevant spaces. We state the result abstractly and in the appendix given its significance in the broader theory of SPDEs. Towards the application of Proposition
6.1
, the Cauchy property is validated in Subsection
4.4
with the equicontinuity following in Subsection
4.5
. This is all tied together in Subsection
4.6
, before a discussion around the potential continuity of the process in Subsection
4.7
.

•

Section
5
sketches the applications of the main results of the paper to the 2D and 3D Navier-Stokes Equations with Stochastic Lie Transport, under both the no-slip and Navier boundary conditions. The equation is properly introduced there, with further details in [
22

•

Section
6

6.3

\mathcal{P}_{n}:f\mapsto\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\langle f,a_{k}\right\rangle_{U}a_{k}.

\left\|\mathcal{P}_{n}f\right\|_{H}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H}.

(2)

\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega\in\Omega

\bm{\Psi}^{n}

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.

2.4
Tightness

We now look to deduce the existence of a process taken as the limit of
\bm{\Psi}^{n}
in some sense, which is done through a tightness argument. We pursue this with Lemma
6.6
in the Appendix, with the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{1}:=H
,
\mathcal{H}_{2}:=U
. Having already demonstrated (
97
) we now justify (
98
), so for any
\varepsilon>0
define the set

A^{\delta,n}:=\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{U}ds>\varepsilon\right\}

where we remove the explicit dependence on
\omega
for brevity, and the required condition (
98
) is that

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(A^{\delta,n}\right)=0.

(32)

We look to show this condition by taking advantage of the control granted to us with the stopping times
\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}
, and the property (
31
). For any
M>1
,

\displaystyle A^{\delta,n}=A^{\delta,n}\cap\left[\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}>T\right\}\cup\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\left[A^{\delta,n}\cap\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}>T\right\}\right]\cup\left[A^{\delta,n}\cap\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\subset\left[A^{\delta,n}\cap\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}>T\right\}\right]\cup\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}.

In particular,

\mathbbm{P}\left(A^{\delta,n}\right)\leq\mathbbm{P}\left(A^{\delta,n}\cap\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}>T\right\}\right)+\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}\right)

(33)

where

A^{\delta,n,M}:=A^{\delta,n}\cap\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}>T\right\}=\left\{\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{U}ds>\varepsilon\right\}

(34)

continuing to use the notation (
23
). From (
33

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(A^{\delta,n}\right)\leq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(A^{\delta,n,M}\right)+\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}\right)

holds for any

\delta
in the final probability, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(A^{\delta,n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(A^{\delta,n,M}\right)+\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}\leq T\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{M\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{P}\left(A^{\delta,n,M}\right).

(35)

Recalling the definition (
34

\mathbbm{P}\left(A^{\delta,n,M}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{U}ds.

Therefore using (
35

\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}\right\|^{2}_{U}ds=0

for any fixed
M>1

Proposition 2.13
.

For any

(36)

Therefore the sequence of the laws of
(\bm{\Psi}^{n})
is tight in the space of probability measures over

Just ahead of proving the result, we note that from the definition of

\sup_{r\in[0,t]}K_{U}(\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}(\omega))\leq c,

(37)

\bm{\Psi}_{0}

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of Proposition
2.12

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}=\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{0}+\int_{0}^{s+\delta}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(r,\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{r})\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}dr+\int_{0}^{s+\delta}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{G}(r,\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{r})\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}d\mathcal{W}_{r}.

\displaystyle\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}=\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(r,\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{r})\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}dr+\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{G}(r,\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{r})\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}d\mathcal{W}_{r}

(38)

which for any fixed

is just an evolution equation in parameter
\delta
, so we can apply the Itô Formula (e.g. Proposition
6.4
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{A}(r,\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}),\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}-\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{U}\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}dr

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{G}_{i}(r,\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r})\right\|^{2}_{U}\mathbbm{1}_{r\leq\tau^{M,T+1}_{n}}dr+2\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{n}\mathcal{G}(r,\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}),\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}-\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{U}d\mathcal{W}_{r}

where we have incorporated the indicator function into the
\bm{\Psi}^{n}
and
\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}

26

25

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq c\int_{s}^{s+\delta}1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{U}^{2}dr-2\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\left\langle\mathcal{A}(r,\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}),\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{U}dr

\displaystyle+2\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\left\langle\mathcal{G}(r,\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}),\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}-\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{U}d\mathcal{W}_{r}

37

27

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}\leq c\delta-2\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\left\langle\mathcal{A}(r,\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}),\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{U}dr+2\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\left\langle\mathcal{G}(r,\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}),\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}-\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\rangle_{U}d\mathcal{W}_{r}

\delta

8

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}\right)\leq c\delta+c\mathbbm{E}\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\left(1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left(1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr

\displaystyle\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}\right\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\mathbb{E}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s+\delta}-\bm{\Psi}^{n,M}_{s}\right\|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0^{+}}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{T-\delta}\left[c\delta+c\mathbbm{E}\int_{s}^{s+\delta}\left(1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left(1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)dr\right]ds

\displaystyle\int_{0\vee(r-\delta)}^{r\wedge T-\delta}\left(1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left(1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)ds

(41)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\left(1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\left[\left(\int_{0\vee(r-\delta)}^{r\wedge T-\delta}1ds\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\int_{0\vee(r-\delta)}^{r\wedge T-\delta}\left(1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}}ds\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq c\delta^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right)\left(\int_{0\vee(r-\delta)}^{r\wedge T-\delta}1+\left\|\check{\bm{\Psi}}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{3}{4}}

We now state the definitions and main result for strong solutions.

\displaystyle\leq c\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\left(\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}1+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{2}ds\right)\left(\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}ds\right)\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}ds\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left[\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[0,\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{r}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}1ds\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c\left[\mathbbm{E}(\delta_{j}^{2})\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}.

Noting that
\delta_{j}
is
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
monotone decreasing (as
j\rightarrow\infty
) and convergent to
0
, the Monotone Convergence Theorem thus justifies that

c\left[\mathbbm{E}(\delta_{j}^{2})\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}=o_{j}

where
o_{j}
represents a constant independent of
n
which goes to zero as
\delta_{j}\rightarrow 0
. We can now employ this bound directly as we take expectation, the supremum over
r\in[0,\delta_{j}]
and then use the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy Inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\right\|_{U}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\right\|_{U}^{2}+\gamma\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{2}ds\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq o_{j}+c\mathbbm{E}\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}1ds+c\mathbbm{E}\left(\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\langle\mathcal{G}_{i}\left(s,\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right),\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\rangle^{2}_{U}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We then use (
7
) and again control the
U
norm by a constant, achieving that

\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\right\|_{U}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\right\|_{U}^{2}+\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{2}ds\right]\leq o_{j}

(72)

having also scaled out the
\gamma
. We now need to relate the expression on the left hand side with what we are interested in, which is
\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{UH,(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{UH,\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}
. We have that

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{UH,(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{UH,\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{s\in[0,(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}-\sup_{s\in[0,\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}+\int_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{2}ds

and claim

\sup_{s\in[0,(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}-\sup_{s\in[0,\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}\leq\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\right\|_{U}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\right\|_{U}^{2}.

(73)

Indeed, we have that

\sup_{s\in[0,(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}\leq\sup_{s\in[0,\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}+\sup_{s\in[\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n},(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\right\|_{U}^{2}

as the left hand side must equal either
\sup_{s\in[0,\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}
or
\sup_{s\in[\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n},(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}
, both of which are greater than the subtracted term
\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\right\|_{U}^{2}
. Appreciating that

\sup_{s\in[\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n},(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{s}\right\|_{U}^{2}=\sup_{r\in[0,\delta_{j}]}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}_{(\theta+r)\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}\right\|_{U}^{2}

then yields the claim (
73
), which in combination with (
72
) grants that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{UH,(\theta+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{UH,\theta\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}\right]\leq o_{j}.

This proves the result.

∎

Proposition 4.12
.

The process

\omega

4.7
Continuity?

We close this section with a somewhat more informal discussion around the pathwise continuity of the strong solution
\bm{\Psi}
in
H
. One may well have expected this to appear in the definition of the strong solution, though it seems challenging to verify and additional assumptions that could facilitate it are perhaps too restrictive. A first result is given now.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let Assumption Sets 1, 2 and 3 hold. Suppose that there exists a continuous bilinear form
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{U\times V}:U\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for every
f\in H
,
\phi\in V
,

\left\langle f,\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}=\left\langle f,\phi\right\rangle_{H}.

Then for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C_{w}\left([0,T];H\right)
.

Proof.

Fixing arbitrary
\phi\in V
,
\bm{\Psi}
satisfies the identity

\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{t},\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}=\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{0},\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}+\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s})ds,\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}+\left\langle\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s})d\mathcal{W}_{s},\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
for all
t\in[0,T]
, and by continuity of the bilinear form,

\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{t},\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}=\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{0},\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{A}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}),\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{G}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}),\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}d\mathcal{W}_{s}.

We want to use that
\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{t},\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}=\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{t},\phi\right\rangle_{H}
, but it is not yet clear that
\bm{\Psi}_{t}\in H

\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
for all
t\in[0,T]
. It can, in fact, be verified by a very similar process to the proof of Proposition
4.12
. To show this let us fix any
t\in[0,T]
. For
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}(\omega)\in L^{\infty}\left([0,T];H\right)
thus for a countable dense subset
(t_{n})
of
[0,T]
,
\bm{\Psi}_{t_{n}}(\omega)\in H
and such that
\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{t_{n}}(\omega)\right\|_{H}\leq c
independent of
n
. We choose now any sequence
(t_{k})
which converges to
t
. The sequence
(\bm{\Psi}_{t_{k}}(\omega))
is uniformly bounded in
H
, so admits a weakly convergent subsequence to a limit
\psi
. However, as
\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];U\right)
then
(\bm{\Psi}_{t_{k}}(\omega))
is strongly hence weakly convergent to
\bm{\Psi}_{t}(\omega)
in
U
, so by uniqueness of limits in the weak topology then
\bm{\Psi}_{t}(\omega)=\psi
and in particular
\bm{\Psi}_{t}(\omega)\in H
. Thus,
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{t},\phi\right\rangle_{H}=\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{0},\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{A}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}),\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{G}(s,\bm{\Psi}_{s}),\phi\right\rangle_{U\times V}d\mathcal{W}_{s}

so that
\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{t},\phi\right\rangle_{H}
is
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
continuous. This is not quite enough for weak continuity, as we instead require that
\left\langle\bm{\Psi}_{t},f\right\rangle_{H}
is
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
continuous for any
f\in H
, not just any
\phi\in V
. From this point, though, we can extend the continuity to
f
by the density of
V
in
H
, identically to the end of the proof of Proposition
2.18
.
∎

Pathwise continuity of
\bm{\Psi}
in
H
would now follow from only pathwise continuity of the norm
\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}\right\|_{H}
, see e.g. [
20
] Lemma 1.5.3. A popular way to show such a property is through Kolmogorov’s Continuity Theorem, by verifying that there exists a constant

such that for all

,

\mathbbm{E}\left(\left|\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{t}\right\|_{H}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right\|_{H}^{2}\right|^{4}\right)\leq C(t-s)^{2}

. There is, however, a simpler case where continuity can be deduced.

Lemma 4.14
.

\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega

This is now a direct application of Proposition
6.4
.

We present applications of our framework for the Navier-Stokes Equation under Stochastic Advection by Lie Transport (SALT) proposed in [

5

5

is defined relative to a collection of functions

N

component mapping. We note this is the sum of a classical transport term and a zeroth-order term. The equation (
74

\mathscr{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and we introduce the notation

to be the subspace of

To study this equation more freely, we commit two manipulations of (

5.2
Navier Boundary Conditions

In two spatial dimensions we can impose different boundary conditions for (
75
), namely the Navier boundary conditions. These are defined on
\partial\mathscr{O}
by

u\cdot\mathbf{n}=0,\qquad 2(Du)\mathbf{n}\cdot\mathbf{\iota}+\alpha u\cdot\mathbf{\iota}=0

(76)

where
\mathbf{n}
is the unit outwards normal vector,
\mathbf{\iota}
the unit tangent vector,
Du
is the rate of strain tensor
(Du)^{k,l}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{k}u^{l}+\partial_{l}u^{k}\right)
and
\alpha\in C^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
represents a friction coefficient which determines the extent to which the fluid slips on the boundary relative to the tangential stress. These conditions were first proposed by Navier in [
44
,
45
], and have been derived in [
42
] from the kinetic theory of gases and in [
41
] as a hydrodynamic limit. Furthermore these conditions have proven viable for modelling rough boundaries as seen in [
2
,
18
,
50
]. To fit the framework of this paper we again have to embed the boundary conditions into useful function spaces. We shall use the space
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
, which was the intersection of
W^{1,2}
with
L^{2}_{\sigma}
, and contains the divergence-free and impermeable boundary condition (that
u\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
). The remaining component of (
76
) has to be included at the
W^{2,2}
level, as we are concerned with the trace of a derivative which needs more than
W^{1,2}
regularity to be understood in the usual sense. Thus, we define

\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}:=\left\{f\in W^{2,2}(\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}:2(Df)\mathbf{n}\cdot\iota+\alpha f\cdot\iota=0\textnormal{ on }\partial\mathscr{O}\right\}.

Of course this space does not appear in the definition of a weak solution, so it is perhaps unclear how the boundary conditions (
76
) inform the weak solution. The answer comes from how to extend the Stokes Operator
-\mathcal{P}\Delta
to
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
. In [
32
] equation (5.1), c.f. [
21
] Lemma 1.4, it is verified that for
\phi\in\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}
,
f\in\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
,

\left\langle\mathcal{P}\Delta\phi,f\right\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\left\langle\phi,f\right\rangle_{1}+\left\langle(\kappa-\alpha)\phi,f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})}

where
\kappa:\partial\mathscr{O}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
represents the curvature of the boundary. Therefore, for each
\alpha
as in (
76
), we extend the Stokes Operator from
\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}
to
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
as a mapping into
\left(\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)^{*}
by the duality pairing for
g,f\in\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
of

\left\langle\mathcal{P}\Delta g,f\right\rangle_{\left(\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)^{*}\times\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}}=-\left\langle g,f\right\rangle_{1}+\left\langle(\kappa-\alpha)g,f\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

The nonlinear term requires no special attention to be understood in the weak sense, similarly to the no-slip boundary condition. We equip
\bar{W}^{2,2}_{\alpha}
with the inner product
\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{2}:=\left\langle\mathcal{P}\Delta f,\mathcal{P}\Delta g\right\rangle_{L^{2}}
which is equivalent to the standard
W^{2,2}
inner product (see [
21
] Lemma 1.2), and
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
with the
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{1}
inner product. Then as a direct application of Theorem
3.5
we obtain the following.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\alpha\in C^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
,
u_{0}:\Omega\rightarrow L^{2}_{\sigma}
be
\mathcal{F}_{0}-
measurable,
(\xi_{i})\in W^{1,2}_{\sigma}\cap W^{2,\infty}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\xi_{i}\right\|_{W^{2,\infty}}^{2}<\infty
. Then there exists a progressively measurable process
u
in
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
such that for
\mathbb{P}-a.e.

\tilde{\omega}
,
u_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];L^{2}_{\sigma}\right)\cap L^{2}\left([0,T];\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)
and (
75
) holds
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
in
\left(\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}\right)^{*}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

This result is given in [
21
] Theorem 1.14, and a strong existence result is proven as Theorem 1.15 in the same paper. For this, we need to make some adjustments; to verify Assumption
4.2
we cannot use the
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{1}
inner product for
H
as this leads to an uncontrollable boundary integral. Instead, we have to manufacture a more reasonable boundary integral into our inner product. Thus, we instead equip
\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
with

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{H}:=\left\langle f,g\right\rangle_{1}+\left\langle(\kappa-\alpha)f,g\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R}^{2})}

which is an inner product equivalent to the usual
W^{1,2}
form when
\alpha\geq\kappa
everywhere on
\partial\mathscr{O}
. This requirement appears in the result, obtainable through Theorem
4.5
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\alpha\in C^{2}(\partial\mathscr{O};\mathbb{R})
be such that
\alpha\geq\kappa
,
u_{0}:\Omega\rightarrow\bar{W}^{1,2}_{\sigma}
be
\mathcal{F}_{0}-

(\xi_{i})\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap W^{3,2}_{0}\cap W^{3,\infty}

\mathbb{P}-a.e.

\tilde{\omega}

\mathbb{P}-a.s.

We note use of Lemma

here, as in this case, the extension

of

Of course this invites the question as to how the Navier boundary conditions solve the issue of

The Cauchy Result

Proposition 6.1
.

Fix

. For

let

denote a Banach Space with norm

such that for all

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X,t}\leq\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X,s}

(\bm{\Psi}^{n})

,
\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,\cdot}

\mathbb{P}-a.s.

\bm{\Psi}^{n}\in L^{2}\left(\Omega;X_{T}\right)
, and such that
\sup_{n}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,0}\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)

\tau^{M,T}_{n}:=T\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,s}^{2}\geq M+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,0}^{2}\right\}.

(77)

Furthermore suppose

\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\geq m}\mathbbm{E}\left[\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}-\bm{\Psi}^{m}\right\|^{2}_{X,\tau_{m}^{M,t}\wedge\tau_{n}^{M,t}}\right]=0

and that for any stopping time
\gamma

(\delta_{j})
which converge to
0

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
,

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,(\gamma+\delta_{j})\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,\gamma\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}\right)=0.

(79)

Then there exists a stopping time
\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}
, a process
\bm{\Psi}:\Omega\mapsto X_{\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}}
whereby
\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|_{X,\cdot\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}}
is adapted and
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
continuous, and a subsequence indexed by
(m_{j})
such that

•

\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}\leq\tau^{M,T}_{m_{j}}

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
,

•

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\bm{\Psi}-\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}}=0

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
.

Moreover for any

we can choose

to be such that the stopping time

\tau^{R,T}:=T\wedge\inf\left\{s\geq 0:\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|_{X,s\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}}^{2}\geq R\right\}

.

Remark 6.2
.

A consequence of the properties that
\sup_{n}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,0}\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)
and

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\left\|\bm{\Psi}-\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}}=0

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
is that
\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|_{X,0}\in L^{\infty}\left(\Omega;\mathbb{R}\right)
. Therefore for

we have that
\tau^{R,T}
is
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
positive, hence so too is
\tau^{M,T}_{\infty}
for appropriately chosen

.

Proof.

Property (
78
) implies that for any given
j\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose an
n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
k\geq n_{j}
,

\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{k}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|^{2}_{X,\tau_{n_{j}}^{M,t}\wedge\tau_{k}^{M,t}}\right)\leq 2^{-4j}.

(81)

We shall make use of delicate manipulations of the subsequence indexed by
(n_{j})
, and for this we introduce a new sequence of stopping times. We now impose that

M>2+\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,0}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}

and define

\tilde{M}^{2}:=\frac{M-\left\|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,0}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}}{2}>1.

The purpose of this is to define

\sigma^{M}_{j}:=T\wedge\inf\left\{s>0:\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,s}\geq(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}\right\}

and ensure that
\sigma^{M}_{j}\leq\tau^{M,T}_{n_{j}}
at every
\omega
. Note the key difference in not squaring the norm, and also that
\tilde{M}>1
so each
\sigma^{M}_{j}
is necessarily positive. To demonstrate the inequality, it is sufficient to show that,
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
,

\left((\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}\right)^{2}\leq M+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}^{2}

(82)

or more easily

\left(\tilde{M}+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}\right)^{2}\leq M+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}^{2}.

This is possible as

\displaystyle\left(\tilde{M}+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\tilde{M}^{2}+2\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}^{2}=M-\left\|\sup_{n}\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,0}^{2}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})}+2\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq M+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}^{2}.

The property (
82

\sigma^{M}_{j}\leq\tau^{M,T}_{n_{j}}
and hence the subsequence
(\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}})
enjoys the same properties up until the corresponding
\sigma^{M}_{j}

\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma_{j+1}^{M}}\right)\leq\left[\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma_{j+1}^{M}}^{2}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq 2^{-2j},

(83)

hence in defining the sets

\Omega_{j}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}(\omega)-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}(\omega)\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}(\omega)\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}(\omega)}<2^{-(j+2)}\right\}

(84)

we have, by Chebyshev’s Inequality and (

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{j}^{C}\right)\leq 2^{j+2}\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j+1}}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{j}\wedge\sigma^{M}_{j+1}}\right)\leq 2^{-j+2}.

We have, therefore, that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\Omega_{j}^{C}\right)<\infty

from which we see

\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{K=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{j=K}^{\infty}\Omega_{j}^{C}\right)=0

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
properties, we can in fact simply show that they hold
everywhere
on
\hat{\Omega}
. More precisely, we also take
\hat{\Omega}
to be such that every

\hat{\Omega}
, which is only a further countable intersection of full measure sets. We proceed by considering the sets

\mathbb{P}-a.s.
continuous, as

\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left|\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|_{X,r\wedge\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,r\wedge\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}\right|\leq\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\left|\left\|\bm{\Psi}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,r\wedge\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}\right|=\left\|\bm{\Psi}-\bm{\Psi}^{n_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}},

which has
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
limit as
j\rightarrow\infty
equal to zero. Thus
\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|_{X,\cdot\wedge\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}
is given,
\mathbb{P}-a.s.
, as the uniform in time limit of adapted and continuous processes, verifying the result. Moving on, it is now that we make use of (
79
) much in the same way as we did for (
78
). This will be done in the context of
\gamma:=\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
and
\delta_{j}:=\sigma^{M}_{j}-\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
. Indeed for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
we can choose an
m_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
(where
m_{j}=n_{l}
some
l
) such that for all
k\geq m_{j}
,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{k}\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{n}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}\wedge\tau^{M,T}_{n}}^{2}\right)\leq 2^{-2j}.

In particular, through a relabelling of
\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}=\sigma^{M}_{l}
,

\mathbbm{E}\left(\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}^{2}\right)\leq 2^{-2j}

by choosing
n
as
m_{j}
and using that
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}\leq\sigma^{M}_{k}\leq\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}\leq\tau^{M,T}_{m_{j}}
. In a familiar way we define

\Omega^{\prime}_{j}:=\left\{\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}^{2}<2^{-(j+2)}\right\}

so that, just as we showed for (
84
),

\check{\Omega}_{K}:=\bigcap_{j=K}^{\infty}\Omega^{\prime}_{j},\qquad\check{\Omega}:=\bigcup_{K=1}^{\infty}\check{\Omega}_{K},\qquad\mathbb{P}\left(\check{\Omega}\right)=1.

For arbitrary given
R>0
, the plan now is to find a constant
M
such that at every
\omega\in\hat{\Omega}\cap\check{\Omega}
, either
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}=T
or
\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|^{2}_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}\geq R
. In both instances it is clear that
\tau^{R,T}\leq\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
, thus proving the proposition. To this end we fix an
\omega\in\hat{\Omega}\cap\check{\Omega}
such that
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}<T
. As
\sigma^{M}_{\infty}
is the decreasing limit of
(\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}})
then for sufficiently large
m_{j}
we must also have that
\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}<T
. Exactly as in (
86
),

\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}}=(\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}.

(90)

From the proven convergence we also have that for sufficiently large
m_{j}
,

\left\|\bm{\Psi}-\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}<1

(91)

which implies that
\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}>\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}-1
, and likewise as
\omega\in\check{\Omega}_{K}
for some
K
,

\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{m_{j}}}^{2}-\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}^{2}<1.

(92)

We fix an
m_{j}
large enough so that (
90
), (
91
) and (
92
) all hold. Substituting (
90
) into (
92
) gives that

\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}^{2}>\left((\tilde{M}-1+2^{-j})+\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,0}\right)^{2}-1>(\tilde{M}-1)^{2}-1.

If
\tilde{M}>2
then the expression on the right is positive and

\left\|\bm{\Psi}^{m_{j}}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}>\left((\tilde{M}-1)^{2}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Furthermore

\left\|\bm{\Psi}\right\|_{X,\sigma^{M}_{\infty}}>\left((\tilde{M}-1)^{2}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1

where the right hand side is of course monotone increasing and unbounded in
\tilde{M}
and hence
M
. By choosing
M
large enough such that

\left[\left((\tilde{M}-1)^{2}-1\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}-1\right]^{2}>R

we complete the proof.
∎

6.2
Useful Results from the Literature

We state some key theorems used throughout the paper. Firstly is a well-posedness result for an SPDE in a finite dimensional Hilbert Space (though driven by a Cylindrical Brownian Motion) in the standard case of Lipschitz and linear growth constraints.

Proposition 6.3
.

Fix a finite-dimensional Hilbert Space
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose the following:

1:

For any
T>0
, the operators
\mathscr{A}:[0,T]\times\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
and
\mathscr{G}:[0,T]\times\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathscr{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{U};\mathcal{H})
are measurable;

2:

There exists a
C_{\cdot}:[0,\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
bounded on
[0,T]
for every
T
, and constants
c_{i}
such that for every
\bm{\phi},\psi\in\mathcal{H}
and
t\in[0,\infty)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\mathscr{A}(t,\bm{\phi})\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{t}\left[1+\left\|\bm{\phi}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\left\|\mathscr{G}_{i}(t,\bm{\phi})\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{t}c_{i}\left[1+\left\|\bm{\phi}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}c_{i}

\displaystyle<\infty

\displaystyle\left\|\mathscr{A}(t,\bm{\phi})-\mathscr{A}(t,\psi)\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left\|\mathscr{G}_{i}(t,\bm{\phi})-\mathscr{G}_{i}(t,\psi)\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C_{t}\left\|\bm{\phi}-\psi\right\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

3:

\bm{\Phi}_{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
.

Then there exists a process
\bm{\Phi}:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
\bm{\Phi}_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}\right)
for every
T>0
,
\bm{\Phi}
is progressively measurable in
\mathcal{H}
and the identity

\bm{\Phi}_{t}=\bm{\Phi}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{A}(s,\bm{\Phi}_{s})ds+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{G}(s,\bm{\Phi}_{s})d\mathcal{W}_{s}

(93)

holds
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in
\mathcal{H}
for every
t\geq 0
.

Moreover, suppose that
\bm{\Psi}:[0,\infty)\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is another process such that for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}(\omega)\in C\left([0,T];\mathcal{H}\right)
for every
T>0
,
\bm{\Psi}
is progressively measurable in
\mathcal{H}
and the identity (
93
) holds
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in
\mathcal{H}
for every
t\geq 0
. Then for every
T\geq 0
,

\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\bm{\Phi}_{t}(\omega)=\bm{\Psi}_{t}(\omega)\quad\forall t\in[0,T]\right\}\right)=1.

Proof.

See [
20
] Theorems 3.1.1 and 3.1.2.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}\subset\mathcal{H}_{2}\subset\mathcal{H}_{3}
be a triplet of embedded Hilbert Spaces where
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is dense in
\mathcal{H}_{2}
, with the property that there exists a continuous nondegenerate bilinear form
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{3}\times\mathcal{H}_{1}}:\mathcal{H}_{3}\times\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for
\phi\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{H}_{1}

\left\langle\phi,\psi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{3}\times\mathcal{H}_{1}}=\left\langle\phi,\psi\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}.

Suppose that for some
T>0
and stopping time
\tau
,

1.

\bm{\Psi}_{0}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}
is
\mathcal{F}_{0}-
measurable;

2.

f:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{3}

\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
f(\omega)\in L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{3})
;

3.

B:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathscr{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{U};\mathcal{H}_{2})
is progressively measurable and such that for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
B(\omega)\in L^{2}\left([0,T];\mathscr{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{U};\mathcal{H}_{2})\right)
;

4.

\bm{\Psi}:\Omega\times[0,T]\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1}
is such that for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,
\bm{\Psi}_{\cdot}(\omega)\mathbbm{1}_{\cdot\leq\tau(\omega)}\in L^{2}([0,T];\mathcal{H}_{1})

\mathcal{H}_{1}
;

5.

The identity

\bm{\Psi}_{t}=\bm{\Psi}_{0}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}f_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}B_{s}d\mathcal{W}_{s}

(94)

holds
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in
\mathcal{H}_{3}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.

The the equality

\displaystyle\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{t}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}=\left\|\bm{\Psi}_{0}\right\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\bigg(2\left\langle f_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{3}\times\mathcal{H}_{1}}+\left\|B_{s}\right\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{U};\mathcal{H}_{2})}\bigg)ds+2\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}\left\langle B_{s},\bm{\Psi}_{s}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}d\mathcal{W}_{s}

(95)

holds for any

,
\mathbbm{P}-a.s.
in

Proof.

Lemma 6.5

(Stochastic Grönwall)
.

Fix
t>0
and suppose that
\bm{\phi},\bm{\psi},\bm{\eta}
are real-valued, non-negative stochastic processes. Assume, moreover, that there exists constants
c^{\prime},\hat{c},\tilde{c}
(allowed to depend on
t
) such that for
\mathbbm{P}-a.e.

\omega
,

\int_{0}^{t}\bm{\eta}_{s}(\omega)ds\leq c^{\prime}

(96)

and for all stopping times
0\leq\theta_{j}<\theta_{k}\leq t
,

\mathbbm{E}\left(\sup_{r\in[\theta_{j},\theta_{k}]}\bm{\phi}_{r}\right)+\mathbbm{E}\int_{\theta_{j}}^{\theta_{k}}\bm{\psi}_{s}ds\leq\hat{c}\mathbbm{E}\left(\left[\bm{\phi}_{\theta_{j}}+\tilde{c}\right]+\int_{\theta_{j}}^{\theta_{k}}\bm{\eta}_{s}\bm{\phi}_{s}ds\right)<\infty.

Then there exists a constant
C
dependent only on
c^{\prime},\hat{c},\tilde{c},t
such that

\mathbbm{E}\sup_{r\in[0,t]}\bm{\phi}_{r}+\mathbbm{E}\int_{0}^{t}\bm{\psi}_{s}ds\leq C\left[\mathbbm{E}(\bm{\phi}_{0})+\tilde{c}\right].

Proof.

See [
19
] Lemma 5.3.

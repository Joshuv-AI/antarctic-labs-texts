Holography in CGHS ^ \widehat{\text{CGHS}} Supergravity
Hamid Afshar a , b {}^{\,a,b} , Narges Aghamir a {}^{\,a}

Abstract
We study holographic aspects of 2D dilaton-supergravity in flat space-time using gauge theoretic BF formulation. The asymptotic symmetries in Bondi gauge and at finite temperature span a supersymmetric extension of the warped Virasoro algebra at level zero. The boundary action is determined such that the bulk variational principle is ensured and turns out to be a super-warped Schwarzian theory at the vanishing level. We also study the thermodynamics of the black hole saddle in this model.

4
Variational principle

The action principle tells that the physical equations of motion must extremize the action. This implies that our action should be differentiable (at least on-shell).
A generic variation of the BF bulk action (
2.7
) when equations of motions hold, equals to a boundary term

\displaystyle\delta I_{0}\approx\kappa\oint\langle B,\delta A_{\tau}\rangle\,,

(4.30)

where
\approx
denotes on-shell equality.
This variation is generically non-zero and thus the action is not differentiable at on-shell configurations and thus the variational principle is ill-defined.
In order to make the theory well-defined we need to define our action functional up to an appropriate boundary term
I_{\partial}=\kappa\oint L
such that the improved action is differentiable,

\displaystyle I[B,A]=I_{0}+\kappa\oint L\,.

(4.31)

In general, the fields
B
and
A
are independent off-shell. However, we can relate them using the field equation
\extdm\!B+[A,B]=0
such that the varied action
\kappa\oint\delta L
cancels the boundary term (
4.30
). Our method for finding the boundary Lagrangian
L
is to use half of the field equations to write the boundary integrand in (
4.30
) as a total integrable quantity
V=-L
up to possible extra variation terms
\delta V_{i}
;

\displaystyle\langle B,\delta A_{\tau}\rangle\approx\delta V+C_{i}\delta V_{i}+\cdots

(4.32)

where
\cdots
are total derivative terms on-shell and thus can be thrown away.
The final variation of the action (
4.31
) is thus

\displaystyle\delta I\approx\kappa\oint C_{i}\delta V_{i}\,.

(4.33)

Now if we use all field equations to show that
C_{i}
’s are indeed constants of motions, they can be drawn out of the integral and we are left with some integrability conditions
\delta\oint V_{i}=0
which should be solved and finally, we have
\delta I\approx 0
. In the following, we apply this method first to the case of JT supergravity as an example and then to the supersymmetric (twisted-)
\widehat{\text{CGHS}}
model (or Cangemi-Jackiw supergravity) which is the focus of our work.

, is the value of the dilaton field
X
at the horizon and
\hat{Y}=-4\pi T\hat{x}_{1}
is the on-shell value of the scalar field
Y
.
This entropy is two times bigger than the entropy for the same black hole solution with zero fermionic gauge component (
\psi=0
). We notice that in this case the value of the zero mode of the auxiliary field,
\hat{Y}
, is also doubled. The thermodynamic properties of the
\psi=0
case have been considered in
Afshar:2020dth
where it was shown that the value of the specific heat
C=T\partial_{T}S
for the case
b\neq 0
(twisted-
\widehat{\text{CGHS}}
) is finite and linear in
T
. Here for
\psi\neq 0
, it will be
C=(4\pi)^{2}\kappa bT{\hat{x}}_{1}
which is 4 times bigger than the case
\psi=0
.

Supersymmetric solution

Is the supersymmetry preserved by our background (
3.15
) as a ‘supersymmetric’ solution? The requirement that the residual susy transformation does not change our background fields corresponds to
\delta_{\chi}\mathcal{P}=\delta_{\chi}\mathcal{T}=\delta_{\chi}\psi=0
. From (
3.18
) the first requirement is automatically satisfied while other conditions lead to

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{2}\psi\chi^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\psi^{\prime}\chi=0\,,\qquad\frac{1}{4}\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{2}\chi-\chi^{\prime\prime}=0\,.

(5.70)

The solution to these equations (assuming
\hat{\mathcal{P}}\neq 0
) are as follows

\displaystyle\chi(u)=c_{1}e^{\frac{\hat{\mathcal{P}}u}{2}}+c_{2}e^{-\frac{\hat{\mathcal{P}}u}{2}}\,,\qquad\psi(u)=c_{3}\chi(u)^{-3}\,.

(5.71)

Thus the first Holonomy-consistent black-hole solution (
\hat{\psi}=0
and
\hat{\mathcal{P}}=-2\pi n/\beta
) is supersymmetric where the spinor
\chi
is given by (
5.71
). In the Euclidean theory,
since we define our boundary theory on a circle
\tau\sim\tau+\beta
where
\tau=iu
and
\hat{\mathcal{P}}\to i\hat{\mathcal{P}}\equiv\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\text{\tiny{E}}}=-2i\pi n/\beta
, these solutions are anti-periodic consistent supersymmetric solutions.

5.2
Stabilizer subalgebra

The stabilizer of the Wick-rotated (
u\to iu\equiv\tau
) background (
5.63
) can be interpreted as the isometry group of the Euclidean version of the flat connection (
3.15
). In order to achieve them we need to solve the stabilizer condition
\delta\mathcal{T}=\delta\mathcal{P}=\delta\psi=0
in the Euclidean case. We thus Wick-rotate the functions and parameters of gauge transformation as mentioned in (
3.20
) such that the form of the variations (
3.18
) is preserved.

For the zero-mode Euclidean solution consistent with the Holonomy condition (
\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\text{\tiny{E}}}=-\frac{2\pi in}{\beta},\psi=0,\mathcal{T}=\hat{\mathcal{T}}
) we are led to a pair of non-trivial differential equations for (
\varepsilon,\sigma,\chi
)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\Big(-\frac{2\pi in}{\beta}\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}\Big)^{\prime}=0\,,

(5.72a)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon^{\prime}\hat{\mathcal{T}}+\frac{2\pi in}{\beta}\sigma^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime}=0\,,

(5.72b)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Big(-\frac{2\pi in}{\beta}\Big)^{2}\chi-\chi^{\prime\prime}=0\,,

(5.72c)

which are solved in terms of some integration constants;

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{{}_{0}}+\varepsilon_{{}_{{}_{n}}}e^{\frac{2\pi}{\beta}in\tau}\,,

(5.73a)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\sigma_{{}_{0}}+\sigma_{{}_{-n}}{e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}in\tau}}-\frac{\hat{\mathcal{T}}\beta}{2\pi in}\,\varepsilon_{{}_{n}}{e^{\frac{2\pi}{\beta}in\tau}}\,,

(5.73b)

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{{}_{n/2}}}e^{\frac{\pi}{\beta}in\tau}+\chi_{{}_{{}_{-n/2}}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{\beta}in\tau}\,.

(5.73c)

In this case, the functions
(\varepsilon,\sigma)
are periodic while the spinor function
\chi
is anti-periodic. We can thus identify it with the NS sector. In this case, we see that our infinite-dimensional super warped-Virasoro symmetry algebra (
3.24
) breaks to the
\mathcal{N}=1
graded extension of Maxwell algebra as was anticipated in (
3
) (for
n=1
) as the wedge subalgebra.

Alternatively, if we consider the second Holonomy-consistent zero-mode solution
(\hat{\mathcal{P}}_{\text{\tiny{E}}}=-\frac{4\pi in}{\beta},\psi=\hat{\psi}\neq 0,\mathcal{T}=\hat{\mathcal{T}})
we have

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\Big(-\frac{4\pi in}{\beta}\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\prime}\Big)^{\prime}=0\,,

(5.74a)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon^{\prime}\hat{\mathcal{T}}+\frac{4\pi in}{\beta}\sigma^{\prime}-\frac{3}{2}\hat{\psi}\chi^{\prime}+\sigma^{\prime\prime}=0\,,

(5.74b)

\displaystyle\delta\mathcal{\psi}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\hat{\psi}\varepsilon^{\prime}+\frac{1}{4}\Big(-\frac{4\pi in}{\beta}\Big)^{2}\chi-\chi^{\prime\prime}=0\,,

(5.74c)

which are solved as

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{{}_{0}}+\varepsilon_{{}_{2n}}{e^{\frac{4\pi}{\beta}in\tau}}\,,

(5.75a)

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\sigma_{{}_{0}}+\sigma_{{}_{-2n}}e^{-\frac{4\pi}{\beta}in\tau}{-}\frac{\beta}{4\pi in}\left[\left(\hat{\mathcal{T}}{-}\frac{3\hat{\psi}^{2}\beta}{8\pi in}\right)\varepsilon_{{}_{2n}}e^{\frac{4\pi}{\beta}in\tau}{-}\hat{\psi}\left(3\chi_{{}_{-n}}e^{-\frac{2\pi i}{\beta}inu}+\chi_{{}_{n}}e^{\frac{2\pi}{\beta}in\tau}\right)\right],\,

(5.75b)

\displaystyle\chi

\displaystyle=\chi_{{}_{n}}e^{\frac{2\pi}{\beta}in\tau}+\chi_{{}_{-n}}e^{-\frac{2\pi}{\beta}in\tau}{+}\frac{\hat{\psi}\beta}{2\pi in}\varepsilon_{{}_{2n}}\,e^{\frac{4\pi}{\beta}in\tau}\,.

(5.75c)

In this case, the functions
(\varepsilon,\sigma,\chi)
are all periodic and we can identify them with the Ramond sector.

5.3
Canonical charges

We can obtain the same value for the entropy in the Lorenzian theory where the Hamiltonian formulation applies and one can define the canonical charges of the model.
Canonical charges associated with a gauge transformation
A\to A+\extdm\!\epsilon
in a BF theory like in any other gauge theory are defined on codimension-2 surfaces, which in two dimensions are points. They are defined as
Grumiller:2017qao

\displaystyle\delta Q[\epsilon]=\kappa\langle\epsilon,\delta B\rangle\,.

(5.76)

Here
\epsilon
is the set of allowed gauge transformations given in (
3.16
). The canonical charge (
5.76
) associated with the Killing vector
\xi=\partial_{u}
which becomes null at the horizon gives the entropy as a conserved quantity from the first law of thermodynamics
T\delta S=\delta Q[\xi]
. A generic gauge transformation in the first order formulation of gravity (the BF-theory in this case) induces a diffeomorphism on-shell with
\epsilon=A_{\mu}\xi^{\mu}
. We thus have

\displaystyle T\delta S=\kappa\langle A_{u},\delta B\rangle\,.

(5.77)

This formula defines the first law of thermodynamics in our BF theory.
Applying it to the zero mode solution (
5.64
) in the presence of fermionic modes and integrating it (with
\delta T=0
) we recover the entropy (
5.1
).

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we considered the gauge theory formulation of the matterless conformally scaled CGHS supergravity dubbed
\widehat{\text{CGHS}}
model or CJ gravity. By imposing suitable boundary conditions we addressed different holographic visions on the would-be boundary quantum theory such as asymptotic symmetries, effective action, and entropy. The asymptotic symmetries span the supersymmetric warped Virasoro algebra (
3.24
), where the anti-commutators of the supersymmetric generators are quadratic twisted Sugawara construction of the null
U(1)
current algebra. The presence of null currents (abelian Heisenberg subalgebra) in the asymptotic symmetry algebra seems to be a generic feature in flat space holography
Barnich:2006av
;
Barnich:2009se
;
Bagchi:2010eg
;
Barnich:2011mi
;
Barnich:2013axa
;
Oblak:2016eij
;
Afshar:2015wjm
;
Afshar:2013vka
;
Safari:2019zmc
;
FarahmandParsa:2018ojt
;
Afshar:2021qvi
. Our analysis confirms that this feature remains valid in the supersymmetric case — for other studies on asymptotic symmetries in flat space-time, supergravity sees e.g.
Barnich:2014cwa
;
Fuentealba:2020zkf
. It would be interesting to investigate the unitary and short representation of the infinite-dimensional superalgebra (
3.24
). The boundary action (
4.2
) ensures a well-defined variational principle. It is the supersymmetric extension of the warped-Schwarzian theory at level zero
Afshar:2019tvp
and thus should be the geometric action of the corresponding supergroup.
It would be interesting to derive this action from group-theoretical arguments as the geometric (group) actions on coadjoint orbits of the super warped-Virasoro group see e.g.
Oblak:2016eij
;
Barnich:2017jgw
. Such extensions of the Schwarzian action are key links between gravity and field theory sides. It is thus curious to obtain this supersymmetric action from the supersymmetric charged SYK model in a double scaling limit in the spirit of
Afshar:2019axx
. Since we have black hole solutions in our theory we computed the entropy by both evaluating the on-shell action and calculating the canonical charges. The essential difference with the non-supersymmetric case is due to the holonomy condition which necessitates the zero mode of
\mathcal{P}
which basically is the Rindler acceleration, to be quantized in
4\pi T
instead of
2\pi T
. This is due to the presence of the fermionic degrees of freedom which double the value of the entropy.

Recently the
\widehat{\text{CGHS}}
theory is conjectured to acquire a dual quantum matrix ensemble description similar to JT gravity
Saad:2019lba
;
Kar:2022sdc
;
Rosso:2022tsv
which can potentially capture non-perturbative effects.
Finally, it would be interesting to embed more dilaton-gravity theories in the 2D holography setup — see recent papers
Grumiller:2021cwg
;
Grumiller:2020elf
;
Ecker:2021guy
and their references.

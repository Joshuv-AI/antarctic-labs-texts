1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and Motivation

In this paper, we study pointwise estimate for the fundamental solution of higher order Schrödinger equation

{\rm i}\partial_{t}u(x,t)=((-\Delta)^{m}+V(x))u(x,t),\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.1)

where
m
is any positive integer, and
V
is a real-valued decaying potential in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In the second order case (
m=1
), it is well known that the fundamental solution of the free Schrödinger equation is given by

e^{{\rm i}t\Delta}(x,y):=(4\pi{\rm i}t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{\frac{{\rm i}|x-y|^{2}}{4t}},\qquad t\neq 0,\,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

from which, we obtain immediately the dispersive estimate

\|e^{{\rm i}t\Delta}\|_{L^{1}-L^{\infty}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

When
V\neq 0
, the dispersive estimate for
e^{-{\rm i}tH}
(
H=-\Delta+V(x)
) has evoked considerable interest in the past three decades, and we refer to
[
25
,
38
,
33
]
for pioneering works on this theme as well as the survey papers
[
34
,
35
]
.

In the higher order case (
m>1
), one of the differences that calls our attention is the spatial decay of the fundamental solution of (
1.1
) with
V=0
(see
[
30
]
):

|e^{-{\rm i}t(-\Delta)^{m}}(x,y)|\leq C|t|^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\left(1+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\right)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},\qquad t\neq 0,\,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.2)

(
1.2
) clearly implies the dispersive estimate, but the converse is not true. We refer to
[
21
,
27
,
30
,
40
]
for fundamental solution estimates of
e^{{\rm i}tP(D)}
with general elliptic
P(D)
.
The study of dispersive estimates for
m>1
in (
1.1
) has attracted a lot of attention in recent years.
Local decay estimates were studied by Feng-Soffer-Yao
[
11
]
and Feng-Soffer-Wu-Yao
[
12
]
.
For the fourth order (
m=2
) Schrödinger operators, Erdoǧan-Green-Toprak
[
6
]
obtained (global) dispersive estimates when
n=3
, and the case of
n=4
was established by Green-Toprak
[
19
]
. Later, Soffer-Wu-Yao
[
36
]
and Li-Soffer-Yao
[
29
]
proved dispersive bounds in 1-D and 2-D respectively.
Very recently, Erdoǧan-Goldberg-Green
[
9
]
proved dispersive estimates for scaling-critical potentials for
2m<n<4m
and
m>1
.
For
n>2m
, Erdoǧan-Green
[
7
,
8
]
obtained dispersive estimates by studying the
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators. We refer to
[
10
,
15
,
31
,
13
]
for related works on wave operators.

Motivated by space-time pointwise estimate (
1.2
), it is natural to ask:

Does the pointwise estimate (
1.2
) hold for general higher order Schrödinger operators
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
?

To our best knowledge, such pointwise estimates have not yet been established.
We aim at establishing estimates for the
fundamental solution of (
1.1
) in all odd dimensions
n\geq 1
and for all integers
m\geq 1
.
However, the main challenge in high dimensions (
n>4m
) lies in the high energy part, while that in low dimensions (
n<4m
) lies in the low energy part, and the technical difficulties of the two are quite distinct. We only address the low dimensional case in this paper, and the results for high dimensions are available in the second paper of this series, see
[
3
]
.

1.2.
Main results

Throughout the paper, we assume, unless stated otherwise,
n
is odd and
1\leq n<4m
.

Recall that zero is an eigenvalue of
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
if there exists some nonzero
\phi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, such that

((-\Delta)^{m}+V)\phi=0,

(1.3)

in distributional sense. In general, there may exist nontrivial resonant solutions of (
1.3
) in weighted
L^{2}
space
L_{s}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\{f;\,(1+\lvert\cdot\rvert)^{s}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}
, which can affect the time decay of
e^{-{\rm i}tH}
. This leads to the definition of resonance. Throughout the paper, we use the notation

W_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\bigcap\limits_{\sigma<s}L_{\sigma}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(1.4)

We also denote

m_{n}=\min\{m,\mbox{$2m-\frac{n-1}{2}$}\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m,\quad\,\qquad\,\mbox{if}\,\,1\leq n\leq 2m-1,\\[8.5359pt]
\mbox{$2m-\frac{n-1}{2}$},\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,2m+1\leq n\leq 4m-1,\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

and

\tilde{m}_{n}=\max\left\{\mbox{$m-\frac{n-1}{2},\,\,0$}\right\}=\begin{cases}m-\frac{n-1}{2},\quad&\mbox{if}\,\,1\leq n\leq 2m-1,\\[8.5359pt]
0,&\mbox{if}\,\,\,2m+1\leq n\leq 4m-1.\end{cases}

(1.6)

Using the above notations, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
V\in L^{\infty}
be real-valued.

(i)
We say zero is a resonance of the
\mathbf{k}
-
th kind
(1\leq\mathbf{k}\leq m_{n})
, if (
1.3
) has a non-trivial solution in
W_{-\frac{1}{2}-m_{n}+\mathbf{k}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, but has no non-trivial solution in
W_{\frac{1}{2}-m_{n}+\mathbf{k}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)
We say zero is a resonance of the
(m_{n}+1)
-
th kind, if zero is an eigenvalue of
H
.

(iii)
We say zero is a resonance of the
0
-
th kind, if
(i)
and
(ii)
are not satisfied. In this case, zero is also called regular.

Our definition coincides with the second and fourth order cases, see e.g. in
[
6
,
22
,
24
,
36
]
.
Given real-valued
V\in L^{\infty}
, by Definition
1.1
, zero must be a
\mathbf{k}
-
th kind resonance for some
0\leq\mathbf{k}\leq m_{n}+1
.
We make the following assumption.

Assumption 1.2
.

Let
V\in L^{\infty}
be real-valued and zero be a
\mathbf{k}
-
th kind
resonance of
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
for some
0\leq\mathbf{k}\leq m_{n}+1
. We also assume that

(i)

H
has no positive embedded eigenvalue.

(ii)

\lvert V(x)\rvert\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-\beta-}
, where
\beta=\max\left\{4m-n,\,n\right\}+4\mathbf{k}+4
.

In the second order case, a classical result due to Kato (see
[
32
, Theorem XIII.58]
) says that
-\Delta+V
has no positive eigenvalues embedded into the absolutely continuous spectrum provided
V
is bounded and decays faster than
|x|^{-1}
at infinity. However, the situation in higher order cases is subtle, for instance, when
m\geq 2
is even,
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
may have positive eigenvalue even if
V\in C^{\infty}_{0}
, see e.g. in
[
12
,
20
]
.

The main result of this paper is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
n
is odd and
n<4m
. Let
H=(-\Delta)^{m}+V
satisfy Assumption
1.2
and
P_{ac}(H)
be the projection onto the absolutely continuous spectrum space of
H
.
Then
e^{-{\rm i}tH}P_{ac}(H)
has integral kernel
K(t,x,y)
satisfying

|K(t,x,y)|\leq C(1+|t|)^{-h(m,n,\mathbf{k})}(1+|t|^{-\frac{n}{2m}})\left(1+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\right)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},\quad t\neq 0,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(1.7)

where

h(m,n,\mathbf{k}):=\begin{cases}\frac{n}{2m},\quad&\mbox{if}\,\,0\leq\mathbf{k}\leq\tilde{m}_{n},\\
\frac{2m_{n}+1-2\mathbf{k}}{2m},&\mbox{if}\,\,\tilde{m}_{n}<\mathbf{k}\leq m_{n},\\
\frac{1}{2m},&\mbox{if}\,\,\mathbf{k}=m_{n}+1.\end{cases}

(1.8)

In particular, when
0\leq\mathbf{k}\leq\tilde{m}_{n}
, the integral kernel has the same estimate as (
1.2
).

Theorem
1.3
implies immediately the following dispersive estimate

\|e^{-{\rm i}tH}P_{ac}(H)\|_{L^{1}-L^{\infty}}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-h(m,n,\mathbf{k})}(1+|t|^{-\frac{n}{2m}}),\qquad\,\,t\neq 0.

(1.9)

To our best knowledge, when
1\leq n\leq 2m-1
and
m>2
,
the dispersive estimate for higher order Schrödinger equations has not been investigated before. The main difficulty is to obtain the asymptotic expansion of the perturbed resolvent near zero energy.
We mention that when
n>4m
, the perturbed resolvent expansion has been proved in
[
12
]
. When
n<4m
, the situation is much more complicated. For instance, when
m=2
and
n=4
, Green-Toprak
[
19
]
first obtained the expansion of the perturbed resolvent by an iteration scheme originating from Jensen-Nenciu
[
24
]
. Employing such techniques, the results for
n=3,2,1
were also established in
[
6
,
29
,
36
]
respectively. It seems that such an iteration scheme will become increasingly complex when
m
becomes larger. To avoid such an iteration scheme,
we
prove the expansion by introducing suitable orthogonal subspaces, and simplify the problem to the study of the inverse of a finite dimensional operator matrix, and in particular, the operator matrix has some specific global structure that allows us to handle in a way unified for all odd
n<4m
.

1.3.
Outline of the proof

We outline the strategy for proving Theorem
1.3
.
Our starting point is the Stone’s formula

\langle e^{-{\rm i}tH}P_{ac}(H)f,\,g\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda}\langle(R^{+}(\lambda)-R^{-}(\lambda))f,g\rangle{\rm d}\lambda,\quad f,g\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.13)

where
R^{\pm}(\lambda):=(H-\lambda\mp{\rm i}0)^{-1}
. We divide the proof into low and high energy parts.
Let
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a cut-off function such that

\chi(\lambda)=\begin{cases}1,\,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,|\lambda|\leq(\frac{\lambda_{0}}{2})^{\frac{1}{2m}}\\
0,\,\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,|\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}^{\frac{1}{2m}},\end{cases}

(1.14)

for some
\lambda_{0}>0
, and denote
\tilde{\chi}(\lambda)=1-\chi(\lambda)
. Using spectral theorem and Stone’s formula, (i) of Assumption
1.2
allows us to split

\displaystyle e^{-{\rm i}tH}P_{ac}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda}\left(R^{+}(\lambda)-R^{-}(\lambda)\right)(\chi(\lambda)+\tilde{\chi}(\lambda))\,{\rm d}\lambda

\displaystyle:=e^{-{\rm i}tH}\chi(H)P_{ac}(H)+e^{-{\rm i}tH}\tilde{\chi}(H)P_{ac}(H),

into low and high energy part. Therefore Theorem
1.3
follows immediately from the two theorems below.

Theorem
1.3
(low energy part)
.

Under Assumption
1.2
, if
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
is given by Theorem
2.7
, then
e^{-{\rm i}tH}\chi(H)P_{ac}(H)
has integral kernel
K_{L}(t,x,y)
satisfying

\left|K_{L}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim(1+|t|)^{-h(m,n,\mathbf{k})}(1+|t|^{-\frac{n}{2m}})\left(1+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\right)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},\quad t\neq 0,\,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
h(m,n,\mathbf{k})
is given by (
1.8
).

Theorem
1.3
(high energy part)
.

Under Assumption
1.2
,
e^{-{\rm i}tH}\tilde{\chi}(H)P_{ac}(H)
(for any fixed
\lambda_{0}>0
) has integral kernel
K_{H}(t,x,y)
satisfying

\left|K_{H}(t,x,y)\right|\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{n}{2m}}\left(1+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2m}}|x-y|\right)^{-\frac{n(m-1)}{2m-1}},\quad t\neq 0,\,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Section
2
and
3
are dedicated to the low energy part of Theorem
1.3
, while Section
4
is devoted to the high energy part of Theorem
1.3
.
We next briefly overview the main points covered in each section.

The goal of Section
2
is to obtain asymptotic expansions of
R^{\pm}(\lambda)
near zero energy, which is a major step towards to the expression for the kernel of
e^{-{\rm i}tH}P_{ac}(H)
in the low energy part,
and such expansions may have independent interests.

In subsection
2.1
, we first prove some expansions for the kernel of the free resolvents.
In subsection
2.2
, we study the perturbed case. By the symmetric resolvent identity, the crux of the problem lies in the asymptotic expansions of
(M^{\pm}(\lambda))^{-1}
for small
\lambda>0
, where

M^{\pm}(\lambda)=U+vR_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})v,\quad v(x)=|V(x)|^{\frac{1}{2}},\,\,U=\sgn V.

The main result of this subsection is Theorem
2.7
, where we shall establish the asymptotic expansions

(M^{\pm}(\lambda))^{-1}=\sum\limits_{i,j\in J_{\mathbf{k}}}\lambda^{2m-n-i-j}Q_{i}\big(M^{\pm}_{i,j}+\Gamma_{i,j}^{\pm}(\lambda)\big)Q_{j},\,\,\,0<\lambda<\lambda_{0}.

We prove Theorem
2.7
in a unified way by studying the operator matrices of
M^{\pm}(\lambda)
according to a direct sum decomposition of
L^{2}
. We first decompose
L^{2}
into the direct sum of an infinite dimensional subspace and a finite dimensional one. In our case, the choice of the infinite dimensional subspace is similar to the one in
[
22
]
, and the inverses of
M^{\pm}(\lambda)
in this part are immediately obtained. However, the situation is much more complicated for the finite dimensional part of
M^{\pm}(\lambda)
, and there are three major steps in the treatment:
firstly, based on the relation between
R^{\pm}(1)
and
R^{\pm}(-1)
(see Lemma
2.3
), we are allowed to convert the finite dimensional part of
M^{\pm}(\lambda)
to operator matrices with the same invertibility; secondly, we prove that the complementary parts in the diagonals of the these matrices are strictly positive or negative by congruent Gram matrices (see Lemma
2.6
); finally, we finish the argument by applying the abstract Feshbach formula (
[
24
, Lemma 2.3]
).

In Section
3
, we prove the low energy part of Theorem
1.3
.
Some lemmas are provided in subsection
3.1
.
In subsection
3.2
, by resolvent identity, we start by writing

e^{-{\rm i}tH}P_{ac}(H)\chi(H)=e^{-{\rm i}tH_{0}}\chi(H_{0})+\Omega^{-,low}_{r}-\Omega^{+,low}_{r}.

Thus the goal is to estimate the kernels
\Omega^{\pm,low}_{r}(t,x,y)
of
\Omega^{\pm,low}_{r}
.
A key ingredient is to use Theorem
2.7
to
write
\Omega^{\pm,low}_{r}(t,x,y)
in the the following form

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-{\rm i}t\lambda^{2m}+{\rm i}\lambda\left(s_{1}^{p}|y|+s_{2}^{q}|x|\right)}T^{\pm}\left(\lambda,x,y,s_{1},s_{2}\right)\chi(\lambda^{2m}){\rm d}\lambda{\rm d}s_{1}{\rm d}s_{2},\end{split}

where
p,q\in\{0,1\}
, and the properties of
T^{\pm}\left(\lambda,x,y,s_{1},s_{2}\right)
rely on the specific type of zero energy resonance.
Another key ingredient to obtain sharp pointwise estimates is to take advantage of the cancellation of
\Omega^{+,low}_{r}-\Omega^{-,low}_{r}
in some cases, which yields better properties in
\lambda
of
T^{+}-T^{-}
, and then better decay of the above oscillatory integral.

In Section
4
, similar to the low energy part, we first decompose the high energy part
e^{-{\rm i}tH}\tilde{\chi}(H)P_{ac}(H)
, by a slightly different resolvent identity of
R^{\pm}(\lambda)
, into

e^{-\mathrm{i}tH}P_{ac}(H)\tilde{\chi}(H)=\sum_{k=0}^{2K-1}\Omega_{k}^{high}+\Omega_{K,r}^{+,high}-\Omega_{K,r}^{-,high}.

We first deal with
\Omega_{k}^{high}(t,x,y)
, which can be written as the oscillatory integral

\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{\mathrm{i}t\lambda}\tilde{\chi}(\lambda)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{kn}}\left(\mbox{$\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k}R_{0}^{+}(\lambda)(x_{i}-x_{i+1})-\prod\limits_{i=0}^{k}R_{0}^{-}(\lambda)(x_{i}-x_{i+1})$}\right)\mbox{$\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}V(x_{i})$}{\rm d}x_{1}\cdots{\rm d}x_{k}\right){\rm d}\lambda.

where

are the kernels of
R_{0}^{\pm}
(
\lambda
).
The estimates for
\Omega_{K,r}^{\pm,high}(t,x,y)
can be proved if

\Omega_{K,r}^{\pm,high}
is an integral of a composition (see (

)) that has

which supply sufficient decay in

In Section
5
, we prove Proposition
1.4
, where the result for smoothing operators follows from a parallel argument as in Section
3
and
4

Definition 1.6
.

For
b\in\mathbb{R}
,
K\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
.

(i)
We say
f\in S_{K}^{b}(\Omega)
if
f(\lambda)\in C^{K}(\Omega)
and

|\partial_{\lambda}^{j}f(\lambda)|\lesssim|\lambda|^{b-j},\quad\lambda\in\Omega,\,\,0\leq j\leq K.

(1.15)

We also denote

S^{b}(\Omega)=\bigcap_{K=0}^{\infty}S_{K}^{b}(\Omega).

(1.16)

(ii)

For functions with parameters in the following form, we say

f\left(\lambda,x,y,s_{1},s_{2}\right)\in S_{K}^{b}(\Omega)
, if
\lambda\mapsto f\left(\lambda,x,y,s_{1},s_{2}\right)
is in
C^{K}(\Omega)
for fixed
x,y,s_{1},s_{2}
, and

|\partial_{\lambda}^{\gamma}f\left(\lambda,x,y,s_{1},s_{2}\right)|\lesssim\lambda^{b-\gamma},\quad\lambda\in\Omega,\,\,\,0\leq j\leq K,

(1.17)

holds uniformly with respect to the parameters
x,y,s_{1},s_{2}
.

(iii)

We say
g(\lambda,s,\cdot,x)\in S_{K}^{b}(\Omega,\,\,\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}})
, if

\lambda\mapsto g(\lambda,s,y,x)
is in
C^{K}(\Omega)
for any fixed
s,x,y
, and

\|\partial_{\lambda}^{j}g(\lambda,s,\cdot,x)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{j}|\lambda|^{b-j},\quad\lambda\in\Omega,\,\,\,0\leq j\leq K

(1.18)

and
C_{j}>0
does not depend on the parameters
x,s
.

(iv)

We say
T(\lambda)\in\mathfrak{S}_{K}^{b}(\Omega)
if
\{T(\lambda)\}_{\lambda\in\Omega}
is a family of bounded operators in
L^{2}
such that

|\partial_{\lambda}^{j}\langle T(\lambda)f,g\rangle|\leq C_{j}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}|\lambda|^{b-j},\quad\lambda\in\Omega,\,\,\,0\leq j\leq K

(1.19)

holds for all
f,g\in L^{2}
, and the constant
C_{j}
is independent of
f,g,\lambda
.

2.
Resolvent expansions around zero energy

2.1.
Notes on the free case

For
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
, we set

R_{0}(z)=((-\Delta)^{m}-z)^{-1}
and
\mathfrak{R}_{0}(z)=(-\Delta-z)^{-1}
. It is known that
R_{0}(z)
has the following expression (see
[
7
]
)

R_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{mz^{1-\frac{1}{m}}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}e^{{\rm i}\frac{2\pi k}{m}}\mathfrak{R}_{0}(e^{{\rm i}\frac{2\pi k}{m}}z^{\frac{1}{m}}),

and therefore,

\displaystyle R_{0}(z)(x)=\frac{1}{mz^{1-\frac{1}{m}}}\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}e^{{\rm i}\frac{2\pi k}{m}}\mathfrak{R}_{0}(e^{{\rm i}\frac{2\pi k}{m}}z^{\frac{1}{m}})(x),

(2.1)

where
R_{0}(z)(x-y)
(resp.
\mathfrak{R}_{0}(z)(x-y)
) is the kernel of
R_{0}(z)
(resp.
\mathfrak{R}_{0}(z)
) and

\mathfrak{R}_{0}(z)(x)=\frac{{\rm i}}{4}\left(\frac{z^{\frac{1}{2}}}{2\pi\lvert x\rvert}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}(z^{\frac{1}{2}}|x|).

Here
\mathrm{Im}\,z^{\frac{1}{2}}\geq 0
and
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}_{\frac{n}{2}-1}
is the first Hankel function.

For
\lambda>0
, the well known limiting absorption principle (
[
1
]
) implies that the weak* limits
R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}):=R_{0}(\lambda^{2m}\pm i0)=w*-\lim_{\epsilon\downarrow 0}R_{0}(\lambda^{2m}\pm{\rm i}\epsilon)
exist as bounded operators between certain weighted
L^{2}
spaces, so a change of variable in (
2.1
) gives

R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(x)=\frac{1}{m\lambda^{2m}}\sum_{k\in I^{\pm}}\lambda_{k}^{2}\mathfrak{R}_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda_{k}^{2})(x),

(2.1’)

where
\lambda_{k}=\lambda e^{{\rm i}\frac{\pi k}{m}}
and

I^{+}=\{0,1,\cdots,m-1\},\quad I^{-}=\{1,\cdots,m\},

(2.2)

When
n\geq 1
is odd, it follows from explicit expression of the Hankel function (see
[
17
]
) that

R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}m\lambda^{2m}}\sum_{k\in I^{\pm}}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{2}e^{{\rm i}\lambda_{k}|x|}}{|x|^{n-2}}\sum^{\frac{n-3}{2}}_{j=\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\}}{c_{j}({\rm i}\lambda_{k}|x|)^{j}},

(2.3)

where

c_{j}=\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{2},\quad&\mbox{if}\,\,j=-1,\\
\frac{(-2)^{j}(n-3-j)!}{j!(\frac{n-3}{2}-j)!},&\mbox{if}\,\,0\leq j\leq\frac{n-3}{2}.\end{cases}

(2.4)

We need the following expansion of
R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(x)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\lambda>0
and
\theta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then

R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(x)=\sum_{0\leq j\leq\frac{\theta-1}{2}}a_{j}^{\pm}\lambda^{n-2m+2j}|x|^{2j}+\sum_{0\leq l<\frac{n-2m+\theta}{2m}}b_{l}\lambda^{2ml}|x|^{2m-n+2ml}+r_{\theta}^{\pm}(\lambda)(x),

(2.5)

a^{\pm}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
and
b_{l}\in\mathbb{R}
are defined in (
2.8
), and

\big|\partial_{\lambda}^{l}r_{\theta}^{\pm}(\lambda)(x)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{n-2m+\theta-l}|x|^{\theta},\quad l=0,\cdots,\mbox{$\theta+\frac{n-1}{2}$}.

(2.6)

Proof.

For each
j\in\{\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\},\cdots,\frac{n-3}{2}\}
in (
2.3
), we apply the Taylor formula for
e^{{\rm i}\lambda_{k}|x|}
(of order
n-j+\theta-3
) to get

({\rm i}\lambda_{k}|x|)^{j}e^{{\rm i}\lambda_{k}|x|}=\sum_{p=j}^{n+\theta-3}\frac{({\rm i}\lambda_{k}|x|)^{p}}{(p-j)!}+\frac{({\rm i}\lambda_{k}|x|)^{n+\theta-2}}{(n-j+\theta-2)!}\int_{0}^{1}e^{{\rm i}s\lambda_{k}|x|}(1-s)^{n-j+\theta-3}{\rm d}s.

Plugging this into (
2.3
), we have

R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda_{k}^{2m})(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}m}\sum_{k\in I^{\pm}}\sum_{l=0}^{n+\theta-3}d_{l}\lambda_{k}^{l+2-2m}|x|^{l-n+2}+r_{\theta}^{\pm}(\lambda)(x),

where
d_{l}:=\sum^{\min\{l,\,\frac{n-3}{2}\}}_{j=\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\}}{\frac{c_{j}}{(l-j)!}},

c_{j}
is given in (
2.4
), and

\displaystyle r_{\theta}^{\pm}(\lambda)(x)=\sum_{k\in I^{\pm}}\sum_{j=\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\}}^{\frac{n-3}{2}}C_{j,\theta}\lambda_{k}^{n-2m+\theta}|x|^{\theta}\int_{0}^{1}e^{{\rm i}s\lambda_{k}|x|}(1-s)^{n-j+\theta-3}{\rm d}s.

(2.7)

Denoted by

a_{j}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}m}\sum_{k\in I^{\pm}}d_{2j+n-2}e^{\frac{k\pi{\rm i}}{m}(2j+n-2m)},\quad\,\,b_{l}=(4\pi)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}d_{2ml+2m-2},

(2.8)

then (
2.5
) follows by using the property

\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}{\lambda_{k}^{2j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}{\lambda_{k}^{2j}}=0,\qquad\text{when}\,\,\,j\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus m\mathbb{Z},

and the fact that
d_{l}=0
when
l
is odd and
1\leq l\leq n-4
(see
[
23
, Lemma 3.3]
).
(
2.6
) results from (
2.7
) by a direct computation.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

For each
\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\}\leq l\leq\frac{n-3}{2}
, if we apply the Taylor formula for
e^{{\rm i}\lambda_{k}|x|}
of order
\frac{n-5}{2}-l

then by the same arguments above we can obtain expansions which have better decay on
x
:

\begin{split}R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(x)=&\sum_{k\in I^{\pm}}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}|x|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\frac{n-5}{2}}C_{l,-\frac{n-1}{2}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{{\rm i}s\lambda_{k}|x|}(1-s)^{\frac{n-5}{2}-l}{\rm d}s\right)\\
&+\sum_{k\in I^{\pm}}D_{\frac{n-1}{2}}\lambda_{k}^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}|x|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}e^{{\rm i}\lambda_{k}|x|}.\end{split}

(2.9)

Here
C_{l,-\frac{n-1}{2}}
and
D_{l}
are absolute constants.

We also need the following expression of
R_{0}(-1)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1\leq n\leq 4m-1
, then we have

R_{0}(-1)(x-y)=\sum\limits_{|\alpha+\beta|\leq 4m-n-1}(-1)^{|\beta|}A_{\alpha,\beta}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}+\sum_{l=0}^{1}b_{l}|x-y|^{2m-n+2ml}+r_{4m-n+1}(1)(x-y),

(2.10)

where the remainder
r_{4m-n+1}
satisfies the same estimate in (
2.6
) with
\theta=4m-n+1
, and

\ A_{\alpha,\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{{\rm i}^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}}{(2\pi)^{n}\alpha!\beta!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\xi^{\alpha+\beta}}{1+|\xi|^{2m}}\,{\rm d}\xi,\qquad\,\qquad\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,0\leq|\alpha|,|\beta|<\tilde{m}_{n},\\[14.22636pt]
\frac{{\rm i}^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}}{(2\pi)^{n}\alpha!\beta!}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{-\xi^{\alpha+\beta}}{(1+|\xi|^{2m})|\xi|^{2m}}\,{\rm d}\xi,\qquad\,\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,\tilde{m}_{n}\leq|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq 2m-\frac{n+1}{2},\end{array}\right.

(2.11)

where
\tilde{m}_{n}
is defined in (
1.6
).

Proof.

By (
2.1
), it follows that for
\lambda>0
,

R_{0}(-\lambda^{2m})(x)=\frac{1}{-m\lambda^{2m}}\sum_{k\in I^{\pm}}(e^{\frac{\pm\pi{\rm i}}{m}}\lambda^{2}_{k})\mathfrak{R}_{0}^{\pm}\left(e^{\frac{\pm\pi{\rm i}}{m}}\lambda_{k}^{2}\right)(x).

Following the proof of Proposition
2.1
and choosing
\theta=4m-n+1
,
\lambda=1
in (
2.5
), we have

R_{0}(-1)(x-y)=\sum_{j=0}^{2m-\frac{n+1}{2}}a_{j}|x-y|^{2j}+\sum_{l=0}^{1}b_{l}|x-y|^{2m-n+2ml}+r_{4m-n+1}(1)(x-y),

where

a_{j}=e^{\frac{\pm\pi{\rm i}}{m}(n-2m+2j)}a_{j}^{\pm},

(2.12)

and the remainder term
r_{4m-n+1}
satisfies (
2.6
). Hence (
2.10
) follows immediately by the following identity

|x-y|^{2j}=\sum_{|\alpha|+|\beta|=2j}C_{\alpha,\beta}(-1)^{|\beta|}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},

and setting

A_{\alpha,\beta}=a_{\frac{|\alpha|+|\beta|}{2}}C_{\alpha,\beta}.

(2.13)

Now we prove (
2.11
).
Note that when
0\leq|\alpha|,|\beta|<\tilde{m}_{n}
, it suffices to consider
1\leq n\leq 2m-1
since

\tilde{m}_{n}=0
if
n\geq 2m+1
.
When
1\leq n\leq 2m-1
, by (
2.10
), we deduce that

\begin{split}(-1)^{|\beta|}A_{\alpha,\beta}\alpha!\beta!&=\lim\limits_{x,y\to 0}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{y}^{\beta}R_{0}(-1,x-y)\\
&=\lim\limits_{x,y\to 0}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{y}^{\beta}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{e^{{\rm i}(x-y)\cdot\xi}}{|\xi|^{2m}+1}\,{\rm d}\xi\right)\\
&=\frac{{\rm i}^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}(-1)^{|\beta|}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\xi^{\alpha+\beta}}{|\xi|^{2m}+1}\,{\rm d}\xi,\end{split}

where the last two equalities follow from the fact that
\xi^{\alpha+\beta}/{(|\xi|^{2m}+1)}\in L^{1}
and the dominated convergence theorem, since
0\leq|\alpha|,|\beta|<\tilde{m}_{n}=m-\frac{n-1}{2}
.

On the other hand, if
\tilde{m}_{n}\leq|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq 2m-\frac{n+1}{2}
, it follows from (
2.10
) that

(-1)^{|\beta|}A_{\alpha,\beta}\alpha!\beta!=\lim\limits_{x,y\to 0}\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{y}^{\beta}\big(R_{0}(-1)(x-y)-b_{0}|x-y|^{2m-n}\big).

(2.14)

Observe that
b_{0}|\cdot|^{2m-n}
is the fundamental solution of
(-\Delta)^{m}
, then

(-\Delta)^{m}\partial^{\alpha+\beta}(b_{0}|\cdot|^{2m-n})=\partial^{\alpha+\beta}(-\Delta)^{m}(b_{0}|\cdot|^{2m-n})=\partial^{\alpha+\beta}\delta_{0}

holds in distributional sense. Taking Fourier transform on both sides of (
2.1
) yields

\mathcal{F}\Big(\partial^{\alpha+\beta}(b_{0}|\cdot|^{2m-n})\Big)(\xi)=\frac{{\rm i}^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\xi^{\alpha+\beta}}{|\xi|^{2m}}.

Note that
|\alpha|,|\beta|\geq\tilde{m}_{n}
, thus
|\alpha|+|\beta|-2m>-n
,
and
\xi^{\alpha+\beta}/|\xi|^{2m}
is a tempered distribution. Using Fourier inversion formula we deduce that

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{y}^{\beta}\big(b_{0}|x-y|^{2m-n}\big)=\frac{{\rm i}^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\xi^{\alpha+\beta}e^{{\rm i}\xi(x-y)-\epsilon|\xi|^{2}}}{|\xi|^{2m}}{\rm d}\xi.

On the other hand,

\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{y}^{\beta}R_{0}(-1)(x-y)=\frac{{\rm i}^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0+}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\xi^{\alpha+\beta}e^{{\rm i}\xi(x-y)-\epsilon|\xi|^{2}}}{|\xi|^{2m}+1}{\rm d}\xi.

Combining (
2.14
) and the above two relations,
we have

\begin{split}(-1)^{|\beta|}A_{\alpha,\beta}\alpha!\beta!&=\lim\limits_{x,y\to 0}\lim\limits_{\epsilon\to 0+}\frac{(-1)^{|\beta|}{\rm i}^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{-\xi^{\alpha+\beta}e^{{\rm i}\xi(x-y)-\epsilon|\xi|^{2}}}{(1+|\xi|^{2m})|\xi|^{2m}}{\rm d}\xi\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{|\beta|}{\rm i}^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{-\xi^{\alpha+\beta}}{(1+|\xi|^{2m})|\xi|^{2m}}{\rm d}\xi,\end{split}

where the last equality follows from the fact that
\frac{\xi^{\alpha+\beta}}{(1+|\xi|^{2m})|\xi|^{2m}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
when
|\alpha|,|\beta|\leq 2m-\frac{n+1}{2}
.
∎

2.2.
The perturbed case

In order to study the spectral measure of
H
near zero, we set

R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}):=(H-\lambda^{2m}\mp{\rm i}0)^{-1},

which is well defined under our assumptions on
V
(see e.g. in
[
1
]
). Denoted by

M^{\pm}(\lambda)=U+vR_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})v,\quad v(x)=|V(x)|^{\frac{1}{2}},\,\,U=\sgn V,

(2.15)

where
\sgn x=1
, if
x\geq 0
and
\sgn x=-1
, if
x<0
.
If
(M^{\pm}(\lambda))^{-1}
exists in
L^{2}
, we obtain from the resolvent identity that

R_{V}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})=R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})-R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})v(M^{\pm}(\lambda))^{-1}vR_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}),

(2.16)

and thus we consider the existence and asymptotic expansion of
(M^{\pm}(\lambda))^{-1}
under various spectral assumptions on the zero energy.

The following notations will be used throughout the section. Denoted by

J_{{\textbf{k}}}:=\begin{cases}\{m-\frac{n}{2}\}\cup\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0};\,i<\tilde{m}_{n}+\mathbf{k}\},\quad&\mbox{if}\,0\leq{\textbf{k}}\leq m_{n},\\[8.5359pt]
J_{m_{n}}\cup\{2m-\frac{n}{2}\},&\mbox{if}\,\,{\textbf{k}}=m_{n}+1,\end{cases}

(2.17)

where
m_{n}
and
\tilde{m}_{n}
are given by (
1.5
) and (
1.6
). We define

G_{2j}f=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x-y|^{2j}f(y)\,{\rm d}y,\quad j\in J_{m_{n}+1},\,f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

and

T_{0}=U+b_{0}vG_{2m-n}v,

(2.18)

where
b_{0}
is given by (
2.8
).

We start by introducing some subspaces of
L^{2}
and related orthogonal projections
S_{j}
.
When
j
is a negative integer, we set
S_{j}L^{2}=L^{2}
; while when
j\in J_{{\textbf{k}}}
, we set

{\small S_{j}L^{2}=\begin{cases}\{x^{\alpha}v;\,\,|\alpha|\leq j\}^{\perp},&\mbox{if}\,0\leq j<m-\frac{n}{2},\\
\mbox{ker}\,(S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}T_{0}S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}})\bigcap S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}L^{2},\,&\mbox{if}\,j=m-\frac{n}{2},\\
\{x^{\alpha}v;\,\,\,|\alpha|\leq j\}^{\perp}\bigcap\mbox{ker}(S_{2m-n-j-1}T_{0}S_{m-\frac{n}{2}})\bigcap S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2},&\mbox{if}\,m-\frac{n}{2}<j\leq\tilde{m}_{n}+{\textbf{k}}-1,\\
\{0\},&\mbox{if}\,j=2m-\frac{n}{2}.\end{cases}}

(2.19)

These subspaces are well-defined by (ii) of Assumption
1.2
. Meanwhile, we define orthogonal projections
Q_{j}
by

\displaystyle Q_{j}:=S_{j^{\prime}}-S_{j},\,\,\,\,\,\,j^{\prime}=\max\{l\in J_{m_{n}+1};\,\,l<j\},\,\,j\in J_{m_{n}+1}.

(2.20)

Obviously, it follows from the definition of
S_{j}L^{2}
(

) that when
1\leq n\leq 2m-1
,

S_{0}L^{2}\supset S_{1}L^{2}\supset\cdots\supset S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}L^{2}\supset S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}\supset S_{m-\frac{n-1}{2}}L^{2}\supset\cdots\supset S_{2m-\frac{n+1}{2}}L^{2},

while when

S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}\supset S_{0}L^{2}\supset S_{1}L^{2}\supset\cdots\supset S_{2m-\frac{n+1}{2}}L^{2}.

We use

to characterize different kinds of zero energy resonances.

Proposition 2.4
.

Assume
(ii)
in Assumption
1.2
holds. Then the following statements are valid:

(i)
zero is regular if and only if
S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}=\{0\}
;

(ii)
zero is a
\mathbf{k}
-th kind resonance with
1\leq\mathbf{k}\leq m_{n}+1
if and only if

S_{k_{0}^{\prime}}L^{2}\neq\{0\}\,\,\,\mbox{ and }\,\,\,S_{k_{0}}L^{2}=\{0\},

where
k_{0}=\max J_{{\textbf{k}}}
and
k_{0}^{\prime}=\max\{l\in J_{{\textbf{k}}};\,l<k_{0}\}
.

Proof.

In view of Definition
1.1
, it suffices to prove that
\psi\in S_{k^{\prime}_{0}}L^{2}
if and only if there exists some
\phi(x)\in W_{-\frac{1}{2}-m_{n}+{\textbf{k}}}
such that
\psi(x)=Uv\phi(x)
and (
1.3
) hold. We only sketch the proof since similar situations have been treated in
[
22
,
24
,
36
]
.

If
\psi\in S_{k^{\prime}_{0}}L^{2}
, then we have
\psi\in S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}
. By the definition of
S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}
, we have

U\psi(x)=-b_{0}vG_{2m-n}v\psi(x)+T_{0}\psi(x).

Note that
\psi\in S_{k^{\prime}_{0}}L^{2}
also implies that
S_{m-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}}T_{0}\psi(x)=0
, thus

\displaystyle U\psi(x)=\begin{cases}-b_{0}vG_{2m-n}v\psi(x)+(I-S_{m-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}})T_{0}\psi(x)\quad&\text{if $1\leq{\textbf{k}}\leq m-\frac{n-1}{2}$},\\
-b_{0}vG_{2m-n}v\psi(x)&\text{if ${\textbf{k}}>m-\frac{n-1}{2}$}.\end{cases}

(2.21)

The definition of
S_{m-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}}
indicates that

(I-S_{m-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}})T_{0}\psi(x)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}}C_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}v,

moreover, by Lemma
3.1
, we have

|G_{2m-n}v\psi(x)|=\left|\int|x-y|^{2m-n}v(y)\psi(y)\right|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{m_{n}-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}},

where we have used the fact
2m-n-k_{0}-2=m_{n}-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}
.
Set

\phi(x)=-b_{0}G_{2m-n}v\psi(x)+\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}}C_{\alpha}x^{\alpha},

we remark that when
m-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}<0
, then the sum
\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m-\frac{n-1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}}C_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}
vanishes.
From the definition of
\phi(x)
, we obtain that
\phi(x)\in W_{-\frac{1}{2}-m_{n}+{\textbf{k}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
((-\Delta)^{m}+V)\phi=0
in the distributional sense.
By (
2.21
), we have
\psi(x)=Uv\phi(x)
.

Conversely, assume that there exists
\phi(x)\in W_{-\frac{1}{2}-m_{n}+{\textbf{k}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\psi(x)=Uv\phi(x)
and (
1.3
) hold.
Choose
\eta(x)\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
\eta(x)=1
for
|x|\leq 1
and
\eta(x)=0
for
|x|>2
. For any
0<\delta<1
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}^{n}
, we obtain from (
1.3
) that when
|\alpha|\leq\tilde{m}_{n}+{\textbf{k}}-2
,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}v(x)\psi(x)\eta(\delta x)dx\right|=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}\eta(\delta x)(-\Delta)^{m}\phi(x)\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{\alpha_{i}\geq\beta_{i}\ \text{for}\ i=1,\cdots,n\atop|\beta|+|\gamma|=2m,\ \beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}^{n}}C_{\beta,\gamma}\delta^{|\gamma|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha-\beta}\phi(x)(\partial^{\gamma}\eta)(\delta x)\,{\rm d}x\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\alpha_{i}\geq\beta_{i}\ \text{for}\ i=1,\cdots,n\atop|\beta|+|\gamma|=2m,\ \beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}^{n}}\delta^{\tilde{m}_{n}+{\textbf{k}}-1-|\alpha|-}\left\|\langle x\rangle^{|\alpha|-|\beta|+m_{n}+\frac{1}{2}-{\textbf{k}}+}\partial^{\gamma}\eta(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\langle x\rangle^{-(m_{n}+\frac{1}{2}-k+)}\phi(x)\right\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow 0,

as
\delta\rightarrow 0
, where the last inequality follows from scaling and the fact that
|\gamma|+|\beta|-|\alpha|-m_{n}-\frac{1}{2}+{\textbf{k}}-\frac{n}{2}=\tilde{m}_{n}+{\textbf{k}}-1-|\alpha|.

In particular, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}v(x)\psi(x)\eta(\delta x)dx=0,\quad\,\,\text{if}\,\,|\alpha|\leq\tilde{m}_{n}+{\textbf{k}}-2,

(2.22)

which yields that
\psi(x)\in S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}L^{2}
. Meanwhile, since

((-\Delta)^{m}+V)\phi(x)=0
in the distributional sense, it follows that

\displaystyle S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}T_{0}S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}\psi

\displaystyle=S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}T_{0}(Uv\phi)

\displaystyle=S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}(U+b_{0}vG_{2m-n}v)Uv\phi

\displaystyle=S_{m-\frac{n+1}{2}}v(I+b_{0}G_{2m-n}V)\phi

\displaystyle=0,

which implies that
\psi(x)\in S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}L^{2}
. Thus the case
{\textbf{k}}=1
follows. If
{\textbf{k}}>1
, repeating the above arguments, we have

S_{2m-n-j-1}T_{0}S_{m-\frac{n}{2}}\psi(x)=0,\quad j\leq k_{0}-1.

Combining this and (
2.22
) yields that
\psi(x)\in S_{k_{0}^{\prime}}L^{2}
. Therefore the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.6

Proof.

is equivalent to that of

on

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\langle D_{00}u_{\alpha},u_{\beta}\rangle=\langle d_{|\alpha||\beta|}u_{\alpha},u_{\beta}\rangle

\displaystyle=(-{\rm i})^{|\alpha|+|\beta|}\sum_{|\alpha^{\prime}|=|\alpha|,|\beta^{\prime}|=|\beta|}A_{\alpha,\beta}\langle u_{\alpha},y^{\alpha^{\prime}}v\rangle\langle x^{\beta^{\prime}}v,u_{\beta}\rangle.

by scaling, and estimates starting from the integral in

4.3.

In this part, we assume

\Omega_{K,r}^{\pm,high}(t,x,y)=\frac{m}{\pi{\rm i}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{-\mathrm{i}t\lambda^{2m}\pm\lambda(|x|+|y|)}\tilde{\chi}(\lambda^{2m})\lambda^{2m-1}T_{\pm}(\lambda,x,y){\rm d}\lambda,

Lemma 4.3
.

For any

, it follows uniformly in

and

that

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{s}R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})(x-y)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}\langle|x-y|^{-(n-2-\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\})}\rangle\langle x-y\rangle^{-(\frac{n-1}{2}-s)},

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{s}(R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})(x-y)e^{\mp\mathrm{i}\lambda|y|})\right|\lesssim\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}-2m}\langle x\rangle^{s}\langle|x-y|^{-(n-2-\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\})}\rangle\langle x-y\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}.

(4.21)

Proof.

We only prove for the case of

, since the proof for

is the same. It follows from (

where

and

. Notice that
\partial_{\lambda}^{s}(h_{0,l}(\lambda,x-y)e^{-\mathrm{i}\lambda|y|})

while

Therefore (

and

Lemma 4.4
.

For
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, it follows that

\|\partial_{\lambda}^{s}(R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}))\|_{L_{s+\frac{1}{2}+}^{2}\rightarrow L_{-(s+\frac{1}{2})-}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-(2m-1)},\quad\lambda\gtrsim 1,

(4.22)

and if

, it also follows that

\|\partial_{\lambda}^{s}(R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}))\|_{L_{s+\frac{1}{2}+}^{2}\rightarrow L_{-(s+\frac{1}{2})-}^{2}}\lesssim\lambda^{-(2m-1)},\quad\lambda\gtrsim 1.

Proof.

It is well known (see
[
28
]
) that

\|\partial_{z}^{s}\mathfrak{R}_{0}(z)\|_{L_{s+\frac{1}{2}+}^{2}\rightarrow L_{-(s+\frac{1}{2})-}^{2}}\lesssim z^{\frac{-1-s}{2}},\quad|z|\gtrsim 1,\,z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus[0,+\infty).

Thus, the first statement follows by the limiting absorption principle and (
2.1
).

Next, by the resolvent identity

R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})=(I+R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})V)^{-1}R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}),

\partial_{\lambda}^{s}(R^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}))
can be written as a linear combination of

\prod_{j=1}^{p}\Big(I+R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})V)^{-1}\partial_{\lambda}^{s_{j}}(R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}))V\Big)(I+R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})V)^{-1}\partial_{\lambda}^{s_{p+1}}(R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})),

where

and
\sum_{j=1}^{p+1}s_{j}=s

is uniformly bounded for
\lambda
in

\|\partial_{\lambda}^{s_{j}}(R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m}))V\|_{L^{2}_{-(s_{j+1}+\frac{1}{2})-}\rightarrow L^{2}_{-(s_{j}+\frac{1}{2})-}}\lesssim\lambda^{-(2m-1)},\quad\lambda\gtrsim 1,

by (

) when

. Then we have the second statement.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

If
K>[\frac{n-4}{4}]+2
,
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
|V(x)|\lesssim\langle x\rangle^{-(\frac{n+1}{2}+s)}

\left|\partial_{\lambda}^{s}T_{\pm}(\lambda,x,y)\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-(2K+1)(2m-1)+([\frac{n-4}{4}]+2)(n-1)}\langle x\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\langle y\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}},\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,\lambda\gtrsim 1.

Proof.

Denoted by
R_{0}^{\pm,(j)}=\partial_{\lambda}^{j}R_{0}(\lambda^{2m})
and
R_{V}^{\pm,(j)}=\partial_{\lambda}^{j}R_{V}(\lambda^{2m})
, we know

is a linear combination of

\begin{split}\langle R^{\pm,(s_{V})}VR_{0}^{\pm,(s_{K-1})}V\cdots R_{0}^{\pm,(s_{1})}V\partial_{\lambda}^{s_{0}}(R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot-y)e^{\mp\mathrm{i}\lambda|y|}),\\
VR_{0}^{\mp,(\tilde{s}_{K-1})}V\cdots R_{0}^{\mp,(\tilde{s}_{1})}V\partial_{\lambda}^{\tilde{s}_{0}}(R^{\mp}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot-x)e^{\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda|y|})\rangle,\end{split}

(4.23)

where
s_{0}+\cdots s_{K-1}+s_{V}+\tilde{s}_{0}+\cdots+\tilde{s}_{K-1}=s
. We first use Lemma
4.3

4.2

\begin{split}&\left|\langle\tilde{z}\rangle^{-s_{1}}\left(R_{0}^{+,(s_{1})}V\partial_{\lambda}^{s_{0}}(R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot-y)e^{\mp\mathrm{i}\lambda|y|})\right)(\tilde{z})\right|\\
\lesssim&\lambda^{2(\frac{n+1}{2}-2m)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\langle|\tilde{z}-z|^{-(n-2-\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\})}\rangle\langle|z-y|^{-(n-2-\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\})}\rangle\langle\tilde{z}\rangle^{-s_{1}}\langle z\rangle^{-(\frac{n+1}{2}+s-s_{0})-}{\rm d}z}{\langle\tilde{z}-z\rangle^{\frac{n-1}{2}-s_{1}}\langle z-y\rangle^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\\
\lesssim&\lambda^{2(\frac{n+1}{2}-2m)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\langle|\tilde{z}-z|^{-(n-2-\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\})}\rangle\langle|z-y|^{-(n-2-\min\{0,\frac{n-3}{2}\})}\rangle\langle z\rangle^{-(\frac{n+1}{2}+s-s_{0}-s_{1})-}{\rm d}z}{\langle\tilde{z}-z\rangle^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\langle z-y\rangle^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\\

\lesssim&\lambda^{2(\frac{n+1}{2}-2m)}\langle|\tilde{z}-z|^{-(n-4)}\rangle\langle\tilde{z}-y\rangle^{-\frac{n-1}{2}},\end{split}

where we have used

. If

, we obtain inductively by such argument that

\begin{split}&\left|\langle\tilde{z}\rangle^{-s_{l}}\left(R_{0}^{+,(s_{l})}V\cdots R_{0}^{+,(s_{1})}V\partial_{\lambda}^{s_{0}}(R_{0}^{\pm}(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot-y)e^{\mp\mathrm{i}\lambda|y|})\right)(\tilde{z})\right|\\

Now by Lemma
4.4

\|V\|_{L_{-(s+\frac{1}{2})-}^{2}\rightarrow L_{s+\frac{1}{2}+}^{2}}\lesssim 1

2(n-2-2l)\leq n-1
, it follows that

\begin{split}&\left\|VR_{0}^{\pm,(s_{K-1})}V\cdots R_{0}^{\pm,(s_{1})}V\partial_{\lambda}^{s_{0}}(R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2m})(\cdot-y)e^{\mp\mathrm{i}\lambda|y|})\right\|_{L_{s+\frac{1}{2}+}^{2}}\\
\lesssim&\|R_{0}^{\pm,(s_{K-1})}\|_{L_{s+\frac{1}{2}+}^{2}\rightarrow L_{-(s+\frac{1}{2})-}^{2}}\cdots\|R_{0}^{\pm,(s_{l+2})}\|_{L_{s+\frac{1}{2}+}^{2}\rightarrow L_{-(s+\frac{1}{2})-}^{2}}\|R_{0}^{\pm,(s_{l+1})}\|_{L_{s_{l+1}+\frac{1}{2}+}^{2}\rightarrow L_{-(s+\frac{1}{2})-}^{2}}\\

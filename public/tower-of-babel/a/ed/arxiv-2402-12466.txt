Legendre-signed partition numbers
Taylor Daniels

Abstract
. Let f : ℕ → { 0 , ± 1 } f:\mathbb{N}\to\{0,\pm 1\} , for n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} let Π ⁡ [ n ] \Pi[n] be the set of partitions of n n , and for all partitions π = ( a 1 , a 2 , … , a k ) ∈ Π ⁡ [ n ] \pi=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})\in\Pi[n] let f ( π ) := f ( a 1 ) f ( a 2 ) ⋯ f ( a k ) . f(\pi):=f(a_{1})f(a_{2})\cdots f(a_{k}). With this we define the f f - signed partition numbers 𝔭 ⁡ ( n , f ) = ∑ π ∈ Π ⁡ [ n ] f ⁡ ( π ) . \mathfrak{p}(n,f)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[n]}f(\pi). In this paper, for odd primes p p we derive asymptotic formulae for 𝔭 ⁡ ( n , χ p ) \mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p}) as n → ∞ n\to\infty , where χ p ​ ( n ) \chi_{p}(n) is the Legendre symbol ( n p ) (\frac{n}{p}) associated to p p . Special attention is paid to the sequence ( 𝔭 ⁡ ( n , χ 5 ) ) ℕ (\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5}))_{\mathbb{N}} , and a formula for 𝔭 ⁡ ( n , χ 5 ) \mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5}) supporting the recent discovery that 𝔭 ⁡ ( 10 ​ j + 2 , χ 5 ) = 0 \mathfrak{p}(10j+2,\chi_{5})=0 for all j ≥ 0 j\geq 0 is discussed. 1.
Introduction

The
partitions
of a given
n\in\mathbb{N}
are the tuples
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
of positive integers such that
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k}=n
and
a_{1}\geq\cdots a_{k}
. For a fixed
f:\mathbb{N}\to\{0,\pm 1\}
and any partition
\pi=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
of any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

f(\pi):=f(a_{1})f(a_{2})\cdots f(a_{k}).

With this we define the
f
-
signed partition numbers

(1.1)

\mathfrak{p}(n,f)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi[n]}f(\pi),

where
\Pi[n]
is the set of (ordinary) partitions of
n
. The integers
\mathfrak{p}(n,f)
generalize several classical partition number quantities: With the constant function
1
the quantities
\mathfrak{p}(n,1)
are the
ordinary partition numbers
, and with
f=\mathbf{1}_{A}
for some
A\subset\mathbb{N}
the quantities
\mathfrak{p}(n,\mathbf{1}_{A})
are the
A
-
restricted partition numbers
. The sequences
(\mathfrak{p}(n,f))_{\mathbb{N}}
using the Möbius-
\mu
and Liouville-
\lambda
functions for
f
are examined in
[
5
,
4
]
.

The term
signed
partition number (in the sense of (
1.1
)) was introduced in
[
5
]
to distinguish the quantities
\mathfrak{p}(n,f)
from
weighted
partition numbers
\mathfrak{p}_{f}(n)
; in short, signed- and weighted- partition numbers have generating functions of the forms

\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-f(n)z^{n})^{-1}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{p}(n,f)z^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-z^{n})^{-f(n)}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{p}_{f}(n)z^{n},

respectively, where
f(n)
is some integer-valued function. In a recent work of Basak, Robles, and Zaharescu
[
1
]
, this distinction between weighted- and signed-partitions is reinforced. In particular, in
[
5
]
an application of the Hardy-Littlewood method yields two asymptotic main terms for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\mu)
, while in
[
1
]
similar methods demonstrate the nonexistence of main terms for
\mathfrak{p}_{\mu}(n)
. Additional recent work on weighted partitions (even if not called as such) may be found in, e.g.,
[
14
,
2
,
7
,
3
]
.

For odd primes
p
let
\chi=\chi_{p}
denote the Legendre symbol
(\frac{n}{p})
for
p
, i.e., let

\chi_{p}(n)=\begin{cases}+1&\text{$n$ is a quadratic residue (mod $p$),}\\
-1&\text{$n$ is a quadratic nonresidue (mod $p$),}\\
0&p\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptn.\end{cases}

In addition let
\chi_{2}(n)
be the Kronecker symbol
(\tfrac{n}{2})
, i.e., let

(1.2)

\chi_{2}(n)=\begin{cases}+1&n\equiv\pm 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8),\\
-1&n\equiv\pm 3\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8),\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We note that in what follows, the letter
p
is reserved for odd primes, and
\chi=\chi_{p}
and
\chi_{2}
always indicate the Legendre and Kronecker symbols, respectively.

To serve as a benchmark for our formulae, we recall from
[
9
]
Hardy and Ramanujan’s formula

\mathfrak{p}(n,1)=(4\sqrt{3})^{-1}n^{-1}\exp(\kappa\sqrt{n})\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-1/5})}}\right],

where, for the remainder of this section, we let

\kappa:=\pi\sqrt{2/3}.

This immediately implies the corollary relation

\log\mathfrak{p}(n,1)\sim\kappa\sqrt{n},

where
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
indicates that
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}/b_{n}=1
. To avoid technicalities due to small values of
n
for which
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})=0
, in our statements below we let

\log^{+}x:=\max\{0,\log x\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
p\neq 5
and
p\not\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
. As
n\to\infty
one has

(1.3)

\log^{+}{\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})}\sim\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{(1-\tfrac{1}{p})n}.

When
p\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
and
n
is even, relation (
1.3
) still holds, but when
n
is odd there may be large cancellations in the asymptotic main terms of
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
. As such, we presently only demonstrate a crude bound for
\mathfrak{p}(2m+1,\chi_{p})
as seen in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
p\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
, then for even
n
, say
n=2m
, one has

\log^{+}{\mathfrak{p}(2m,\chi_{p})}\sim\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{(1-\tfrac{1}{p})(2m)},

and for odd
n
, say
n=2m+1
, one has

\mathfrak{p}(2m+1,\chi_{p})=O\Big(m^{-\frac{19}{20}}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{(1-\tfrac{1}{p})(2m+1)}\,}}\right)\!\Big).

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are corollaries of our main results which we now discuss. Ultimately, the two most salient properties of the prime
p
that determine the asymptotic behavior of
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
are: i) the residue of
p
(mod
4
); and ii) whether or not
2
is a quadratic residue modulo
p
, i.e., the value of
\chi_{p}(2)
. Thus, we separate odd primes by their residues modulo
8
.

In stating our formulae it is convenient to let

L_{1}(\chi_{p})=L(1,\chi_{p})\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{1}^{\prime}(\chi_{p})=L^{\prime}(1,\chi_{p}),

where
L(s,\chi_{p})
is Dirichlet
L
-function for
\chi_{p}
. Many of our formulae involve complicated constants, and hence we wish to convey to the reader that the “structures” of the formulae are more important than the exact values of the constants therein.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p\neq 5
and suppose that
p\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
. As
n\to\infty
one has

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})=\mathfrak{a}_{p}n^{-\frac{3}{4}}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{(1-\tfrac{1}{p})n}\,}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+(-1)^{n}\mathfrak{b}_{p}+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right],

where

\kappa=\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}},\qquad\mathfrak{a}_{p}=(\tfrac{p-1}{384\,p^{2}})^{\frac{1}{4}}\exp(\tfrac{1}{4}\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi_{p}))

and

\mathfrak{b}_{p}=\begin{cases}1&p\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8),\\
\exp(-\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi_{p}))&\text{$p\equiv 5\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)$ and $p\neq 5$.}\end{cases}

Since
L_{1}(\chi_{p})>0
for all primes
p
(and
L_{1}(\chi_{2})>0
as well), see, e.g.
[
12
]
*p. 124, we have the following corollary which provides a “middle ground” of complexity between Theorems
1.1
and
1.3
. We recall that the relation
a_{n}\asymp b_{n}
holds if

a_{n}=O(b_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad b_{n}=O(a_{n})\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

Corollary 1.4
.

Suppose that
p\equiv 5\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
and
p\neq 5
. As
n\to\infty
one has

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})\asymp n^{-\frac{3}{4}}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{(1-\tfrac{1}{p})n}\,}}\right).

Although the asymptotic formulae for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
with
p\equiv 3\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
have the same overall structure as those for
p\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
, in these cases the constants
\mathfrak{a}_{p}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{p}
have significantly more complicated formulae.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
p\equiv 3\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
, let
\kappa=\pi\sqrt{2/3}
, let
\chi=\chi_{p}
, and let

(1.4)

\mathfrak{a}_{p}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{p-1}{384\,p^{2}}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\exp\!\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi)}{2\pi}\bigg(\gamma+\frac{1}{2}\log\!\bigg(\frac{384}{p-1}\bigg)-\frac{L_{1}^{\prime}(\chi)}{L_{1}(\chi)}\bigg)\!\bigg),

where
\gamma
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.
If
p\equiv 3\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
, then as
n\to\infty
one has

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})=\mathfrak{a}_{p}n^{\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi)/4\pi-3/4}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{(1-\tfrac{1}{p})n}\,}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+(-1)^{n}\mathfrak{b}_{p}n^{-\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi)/\pi}+O(n^{-1/5})}}\right],

where

\mathfrak{b}_{p}=\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\frac{\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi)}{\pi}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2\frac{L_{1}^{\prime}(\chi)}{L_{1}(\chi)}+\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{p(p-1)}{192}}}\right)-2\gamma}}\right)}}\right].

If
p\equiv 7\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
, then as
n\to\infty
one has

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})=\mathfrak{a}_{p}n^{\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi)/4\pi-3/4}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{(1-\tfrac{1}{p})n}\,}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+(-1)^{n}\mathfrak{b}_{p}+O(n^{-1/5})}}\right],

where

\mathfrak{b}_{p}=2^{-\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi)/\pi}.

Again because
L_{1}(\chi_{p})>0
for all
p
, we have the following analogue of Corollary
1.4
.

Corollary 1.6
.

If
p\equiv 7\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
, then as
n\to\infty
one has

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})\asymp n^{\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi)/4\pi-3/4}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{(1-\tfrac{1}{p})n}\,}}\right).

If
p\equiv 3\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
, then as
n\to\infty
one has the stronger relation

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})\sim\mathfrak{a}_{p}n^{\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi)/4\pi-3/4}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{(1-\tfrac{1}{p})n}\,}}\right),

where
\mathfrak{a}_{p}
is again given by (
1.4
).

Given the appearance of the term
\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi)/4\pi-3/4
in the exponents of
n
in Theorem
1.5
, we pause to consider the potential size of this term. Siegel’s theorem (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
*Thm. 11.14) states that: For each
\varepsilon>0
there exists a constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
such that: if
\psi
is a quadratic character modulo
q
, then

L_{1}(\psi)>C(\varepsilon)q^{-\varepsilon}.

Although it is well-known that the constant
C(\varepsilon)
above is ineffective, Siegel’s theorem implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
we have

\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi_{p})/4\pi-3/4\asymp p^{1/2-\varepsilon}\qquad(p\to\infty).

As such, the terms
n^{\sqrt{p}L_{1}(\chi_{p})/4\pi-3/4}
in Theorem
1.5
and Corollary
1.6
may have arbitrarily large exponents as the prime
p
increases.

1.1.
The special cases of
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
and
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{2})

Examining Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
, one sees that the formulae for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
therein all contain a factor of the form

(1.5)

1+(-1)^{n}\mathfrak{b}_{p}n^{-\delta}+O(n^{-1/5})\qquad(\delta\geq 0).

In essence, the sequence
(\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5}))_{\mathbb{N}}
behaves so differently than the other
(\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p}))_{\mathbb{N}}
because the factor in the formula for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
corresponding to (
1.5
) includes an extra cosine term. We remark that since
p
is now explicitly set to be
5
, the constants
\mathfrak{a}_{p}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{p}
(now
\mathfrak{a}_{5}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{5}
) of Theorem
1.3
may be explicitly computed.

Theorem 1.7
.

As
n\to\infty
one has

(1.6)

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})=\mathfrak{a}_{5}n^{-\frac{3}{4}}\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{\tfrac{4}{5}n}\,\Big)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+(-1)^{n}\mathfrak{b}_{5}+\mathfrak{d}_{5}\cos\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{2\pi}{5}n-\tfrac{\pi}{10}}}\right)+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right],

where

\kappa=\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}},\quad\mathfrak{a}_{5}=\bigg(\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{960}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}},\quad\mathfrak{b}_{5}=\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{d}_{5}=\sqrt{2(5-\sqrt{5})}.

Ignoring the error term
O(n^{-1/5})
in formula (
1.6
), we see that the behavior of
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
is essentially dictated by two core parts, namely the exponential term

\mathfrak{a}_{5}n^{-\frac{3}{4}}\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{\tfrac{4}{5}n}\,\Big)

and the
10
-periodic “signed” term

\mathfrak{S}(n):=1+(-1)^{n}\bigg(\frac{3-\sqrt{5}}{2}\bigg)+\sqrt{2(5-\sqrt{5})}\,\cos\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2\pi n}{5}-\frac{\pi}{10}}}\right).

Computing the values of
\mathfrak{S}(n)
for
1\leq n\leq 10
, it is surprising to find that

\mathfrak{S}(2)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{S}(n)\neq 0\quad\text{for $1\leq n\leq 10$ with $n\neq 2$}.

Indeed, since

\cos\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{7\pi}{10}}}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{2}},

when
n=2
we have

\mathfrak{S}(2)=\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{(5-\sqrt{5})^{2}}}{2}=0.

Although formula (
1.6
) is not an exact formula for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
, the fact that
\mathfrak{S}(10j+2)=0
for
j\geq 0
provides a heuristic explanation for the following surprising result, established in
[
6
]
using
q
-series methods.

Theorem 1.8

(
[
6
]
*Thm. 1.1)
.

One has
\mathfrak{p}(10j+2,\chi_{5})=0
for all
j\geq 0
.

We now consider
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{2})
, recalling that

\chi_{2}(n)=\begin{cases}1&n\equiv\pm 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8),\\
-1&n\equiv\pm 3\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8),\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Like our formula for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
, our formula for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{2})
is unique among those for other
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
. Specifically, the dominant asymptotic term for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{2})
is a single 8-periodic cosine term, rather than the common terms such as
1+(-1)^{n}\mathfrak{b}_{p}
(or similar quantities).

Theorem 1.9
.

As
n\to\infty
, one has

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{2})=\mathfrak{a}_{2}n^{-\frac{3}{4}}\exp\!\Big(\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{\tfrac{11}{16}n}\,\Big)\mathfrak{S}_{2}(n),

where

(1.7)

\mathfrak{S}_{2}(n)=\cos\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{2\pi n}{8}-\frac{3\pi}{16}}}\right)+\mathfrak{b}_{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+\frac{(-1)^{n}}{1+\sqrt{2}}}}\right]\exp\!\Big(\!\!-\!\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}n}\Big[\sqrt{\tfrac{11}{8}}-1\Big]\!\Big),

and

\kappa=\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}},\quad\mathfrak{a}_{2}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{11}{384}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{b}_{2}=\sqrt{\frac{1+\sqrt{2}}{2\sqrt{11}}}.

Motivated by Theorem
1.8
, we say that a sequence
(a_{n})_{\mathbb{N}}

vanishes on some arithmetic progression
if
a_{mj+r}=0
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, some
0\leq r<m
, and all
j\geq 0
; in such a case we may say that
a_{n}
“vanishes for all
n\equiv r\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,m)
”. Thus
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
vanishes for all
n\equiv 2\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,10)
.

As
n
runs from
1
to
8
, the quantity
\cos(\frac{2\pi}{8}n-\frac{3\pi}{16})
never vanishes; consequently, the signed term
\mathfrak{S}_{2}(n)
in (
1.7
) never vanishes either, and it follows that
(\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{2}))_{\mathbb{N}}
does not vanish on any arithmetic progression. Through this lens of vanishing on arithmetic progressions, the results of Corollaries
1.4
and
1.6
and Theorem
1.9
combine to yield the following interesting result.

Theorem 1.10
.

If
p
is an odd prime such that
p\neq 5
and
p\not\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,8)
, then the sequence
(\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p}))_{\mathbb{N}}
does not vanish on any arithmetic progression. Similarly, the sequence
(\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{2}))_{\mathbb{N}}
does not vanish on any arithmetic progression.

Although outside the scope of our discussions, we mention another example of a signed partition sequence that vanishes on arithmetic progressions.

Theorem 1.11

(
[
6
]
*Thm. 1.1)
.

For all partitions
\pi=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
of
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\chi_{5}^{\dagger}(\pi):=(-1)^{k}\chi_{5}(a_{1})\cdots\chi_{5}(a_{k}).

Then
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5}^{\dagger})=0
vanishes for all
n\equiv 6\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,10)
.

1.2.
A result on exponential sums

For
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
with
|f|\leq 1
, the generating function
\Phi(z)=\Phi(z,f)
for
(\mathfrak{p}(n,f))_{\mathbb{N}}
is defined via

\Phi(z)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{1-f(n)z^{n}}}}\right)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{p}(n,f)z^{n}\qquad(|z|<1).

With this, by Cauchy’s theorem we have

(1.8)

\mathfrak{p}(n,f)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z|=\rho}\Phi(z)z^{-n-1}\,dz=\rho^{-n}\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(\rho e(\alpha))e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha

for all
0<\rho<1
, where
e(\alpha):=\exp(2\pi i\alpha)
. In practice, one often analyzes the integrand in (
1.8
) by examining a logarithm of
\Phi(z)
rather than
\Phi(z)
itself. Explicitly, the majority of our analyses consider the series
\Psi(z)=\Psi(z,f)
defined via

(1.9)

\Psi(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f^{k}(n)}{k}z^{nk}\qquad(|z|<1),

so that

\Phi(z)=\exp(\Psi(z)).

As outlined above, our primary focus is on sequences
(\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p}))_{\mathbb{N}}
and on functions
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha),\chi_{p})
for different primes
p
. However, because it may be of independent interest, in section
3
we establish the following general result using work of Montgomery and Vaughan
[
11
]
.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
X
be a large positive parameter, and let
\alpha
have the property that: if
|\alpha-a/q|\leq 1/(qX^{2/3})
for coprime
q\in\mathbb{N}
and
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
q>X^{1/3}
. Then, writing
\rho=\exp(-1/X)
, as
X\to\infty
one has

\Psi(\rho e(\alpha),f)=O(X/\log{X}),

uniformly among multiplicative
f
with
|f|\leq 1
.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

The letter
p
is reserved for odd primes, the notation
\chi=\chi_{p}
is reserved for the Legendre symbol
(\frac{n}{p})
, and
\chi_{2}
is reserved for the Kronecker symbol
(\frac{n}{2})
. The notation
\chi_{0}
is reserved for principal Dirichlet characters, where the associated modulus is specified in context. To avoid confusion, we emphasize the following convention: Except in statements such as “the Legendre symbol
(\frac{n}{p})
,” fractional expressions
(\frac{a}{b})
are never used to indicate a Legendre or Kronecker symbol; rather, these functions are always indicated by
\chi_{p}
(or simply
\chi
) and
\chi_{2}
, respectively.

Expressions
O(g(x))
with
g(x)>0
denote quantities bounded by some constant multiple of
g(x)
, and
f(x)\ll g(x)
holds if
f(x)=O(g(x))
as
x\to\infty
. The relation
f(x)\asymp g(x)
holds when
f(x)\ll g(x)
and
g(x)\ll f(x)
. Expressions
X_{0}(A)
indicate some constant
X_{0}>0
depending on
A
. Statements involving
\varepsilon
are understood to hold for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
unless noted otherwise, and constants’ dependencies on
\varepsilon
are generally suppressed.

For real
\alpha
let
e(\alpha)=\exp(2\pi i\alpha)
, let
\|\alpha\|=\min_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\alpha-n|
, and let
\{\alpha\}=\alpha-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor
. For
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
let

S_{f}(t,\alpha)=\sum_{n\leq t}f(n)e(n\alpha).

Expressions
n\equiv a
(mod
q
) are often abbreviated as
n\equiv a\,(q)
in subscripts, and sums the form
\sum_{n\equiv a\,(q)}
or
\sum_{2|k}
indicate sums over positive
n
and
k
satisfying the conditions
n\equiv a\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,q)
and
2\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk
, respectively.

Let
1\leq Q\leq X
. For
q\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq a\leq q
with
(a,q)=1
we define

\mathfrak{M}(a/q)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\alpha\in[0,1):|\alpha-a/q|\leq Q/(qX)}}\right\}.

The
major arcs
are those
\mathfrak{M}(a/q)
with
q\leq Q
, and
\mathfrak{M}(X,Q)
denotes the union of all major arcs. The connected components of
[0,1)\setminus\mathfrak{M}(X,Q)
are the
minor arcs
, and their union is denoted
\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
. In many cases, only
\alpha
lying in small subsets of the arcs
\mathfrak{M}(\tfrac{0}{1})
,
\mathfrak{M}(\tfrac{1}{2})
, and
\mathfrak{M}(\tfrac{1}{1})
significantly contribute to the integral (
1.8
), with all other
\alpha
contributing to an error term. Anticipating this, for
X,X_{*}>1
we define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in[0,1):\|\alpha\|\leq 3/(8\pi X)\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{*}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in[0,1):|\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}|\leq 3/(8\pi X_{*})\},

and, following Gafni
[
8
]
, we term the sets
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{*}
the
principal arcs
.

For
\rho,\rho_{*}\in(0,1)
it is convenient to write

X=\frac{1}{\log(1/\rho)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad X_{*}=\frac{1}{\log(1/\rho_{*})},

so that

\rho=e^{-1/X}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho_{*}=e^{-1/X_{*}},

and this convention is maintained throughout this paper.

By comparison with the series
\Psi(z,1)
, for
|f|\leq 1
the series
\Psi(z,f)
in (
1.9
) converges absolutely for
|z|<1
. Thus, we exchange the sums in equation (
1.9
) and define

F_{k}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{k}(n)z^{n}

for
k>0
, so that

(2.1)

\Psi(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{f^{k}(n)}{k}z^{nk}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}F_{k}(z^{k}).

Often we analyze the sum (
2.1
) by considering the sum of terms with
k\leq K
for some parameter
K
and treating the remaining sum over
k>K
as an error term. For this purpose we record the following lemma bounding these tail sums.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
with
|f|\leq 1
. For real
\alpha
and large
X
and
K
, one has

\sum_{k>K}\frac{1}{k}F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))\ll\frac{X}{K}.

Proof.

For fixed
k
we see that

F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{k}(n)\rho^{nk}e(nk\alpha)\ll\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho^{nk}=\frac{\rho^{k}}{1-\rho^{k}}.

Using the relation
\rho=e^{-1/X}
it follows that

\sum_{k>K}\frac{1}{k}F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))\ll\sum_{k>K}\frac{1}{k}\,\frac{e^{-k/X}}{1-e^{-k/X}}=\sum_{k>K}\frac{1/k}{e^{k/X}-1}\ll\sum_{k>K}\frac{X}{k^{2}},

and the result follows.
∎

3.
The minor arcs

The goal of this section is the proof of Theorem
1.12
, which, by the definitions of
\mathfrak{M}(X,Q)
and
\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
, concerns
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))=\Psi(\rho e(\alpha),f)
for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,X^{1/3})
. Although the statement of Theorem
1.12
indicates that ultimately
Q:=X^{1/3}
, we begin with an undetermined
Q
to better illustrate our derivations.

Recalling that
S_{f}(x,\alpha)=\sum_{n\leq x}f(n)e(n\alpha)
and
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k^{-1}F_{k}(\rho e(\alpha))
, our plan is to use summation by parts to write

F_{k}(\rho e(\alpha))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{k}(n)\rho^{nk}e(nk\alpha)=\frac{k}{X}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xk/X}S_{f}(x,k\alpha)\,dx,

and then estimate these
F_{k}
by employing suitable bounds on
S_{f}(x,k\alpha)
. The key to these bounds is the following result due to Montgomery and Vaughan.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
11
]
*Cor. 1)
.

Suppose
\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\alpha-a/q}}\right|\leq q^{-2}
,
(a,q)=1
, and
2\leq R\leq q\leq x/R
. Then

(3.1)

S_{f}(x,\alpha)\ll\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{x\log^{3/2}R}{R^{1/2}}

uniformly for all multiplicative
f
with
|f|\leq 1
. In addition, the implicit constant in inequality (
3.1
) does not depend on
q
or
a
.

Prior to applying Lemma
3.1
, we must address a subtle issue concerning the quantities
S_{f}(x,k\alpha)
. Namely, as
k
runs over
\mathbb{N}
the quantities
k\alpha
may fall outside our minor arcs
\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
, critically weakening our “savings” from the above lemma. To work around this issue, we reuse methods from
[
5
]
*sec. 4.

First, we suppose that there exist quantities
K
,
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
such that
k\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})
for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
and
k\leq K
. Next, we observe that the definitions of the sets
\mathfrak{M}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})
and
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})
imply that if
x\geq\mathcal{X}
, then
\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})\subset\mathfrak{m}(x,\mathcal{Q})
. Using these two ideas together, we deduce that for all
x\geq\mathcal{X}
, one has

(3.2)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)\\
k\leq K\end{subarray}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|S_{f}(x,k\alpha)}}\right|\leq\sup_{\theta\in\mathfrak{m}(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Q})}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|S_{f}(x,\theta)}}\right|\leq\sup_{\theta\in\mathfrak{m}(x,\mathcal{Q})}|S_{f}(x,\theta)|.

We now consider how
K
,
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{Q}
ought to be selected. Loosely speaking, we prioritize decreasing
\mathcal{X}
at the cost of increasing
\mathcal{Q}
, demonstrating the mechanism of this “exchange” in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
\kappa\in(0,1)
, let
1\leq Q\leq X
, let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
, and let
1\leq k\leq Q^{\kappa}
. If
(a_{k},q_{k})=1
and

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|k\alpha-\frac{a_{k}}{q_{k}}}}\right|\leq\frac{Q^{1-\kappa}}{q_{k}X},

then
q_{k}>Q^{1-\kappa}
; in particular
\{k\alpha\}\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q^{1-\kappa})
. Finally, if
0<\nu<1-\kappa
then

\mathfrak{m}(X,Q^{1-\kappa})\subset\mathfrak{m}(XQ^{-\nu},Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}).

Proof.

The proof is an elementary application of Dirichlet approximation and the definitions of
\mathfrak{M}(X,Q)
and
\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
, as shown in
[
5
]
*Lem. 4.6.
∎

We now return to Lemma
3.1
; for convenience we provide a proof of said lemma using the proof outlined in
[
11
]
, in which Lemma
3.1
is demonstrated as a corollary of the following theorem.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
11
]
*Thm. 1)
.

Suppose that
(a,q)=1
and
q\leq x
. One has

S_{f}(x,a/q)\ll\frac{x}{\log 2x}+\frac{x}{\phi(q)^{1/2}}+\frac{x\log^{3/2}(2x/q)}{(x/q)^{1/2}}

uniformly for all multiplicative
f
with
|f|\leq 1
, where
\phi(q)
is the Euler totient function.

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

If
q=2
or
R=2
then (
3.1
) is trivial, so assume that
q\geq 3
, that
R\geq 3
, and that
x\geq 9
. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\leq x
let

\mathcal{R}(x,r):=\frac{x}{\log 2x}+\frac{x}{\phi(r)^{1/2}}+\frac{x\log^{3/2}(2x/r)}{(x/r)^{1/2}}.

Writing
e(\alpha n)=e(\beta n)e((\alpha-\beta)n)
and summing by parts, one sees that

S_{f}(x,\alpha)=S_{f}(x,\beta)e((\alpha-\beta)x)-2\pi i(\alpha-\beta)\int_{1}^{x}S_{f}(u,\beta)e((\alpha-\beta)u)\,du.

Suppose that
\beta=b/r
with
(b,r)=1
and
r\leq x
. Trivially bounding
S_{f}(u,\beta)\ll u
when
u\leq r
and using Lemma
3.3
when
r<u\leq x
, one finds that

2\pi i(\alpha-\beta)\int_{1}^{x}S_{f}(u,\beta)e((\alpha-\beta)u)\,du\ll|\alpha-b/r|(r^{2}+x\mathcal{R}(x,r)),

whereby

S_{f}(x,\alpha)\ll\mathcal{R}(x,r)+|\alpha-b/r|(r^{2}+x\mathcal{R}(x,r)).

If
q>x^{1/2}
, then setting
b=a
and
r=q
it follows that

(3.3)

S_{f}(x,\alpha)\ll\mathcal{R}(x,r)+r^{-2}(r^{2}+x\mathcal{R}(x,r))\ll\mathcal{R}(x,r).

Now suppose that
q\leq x^{1/2}
. By Dirichlet’s approximation theorem there exist coprime
b
and
r
, with
r\leq 2x/q
, such that
|\alpha-b/r|\leq q/(2xr)
. If
r=q
then
|\alpha-b/r|\leq 1/(2x)
and we again recover (
3.3
). If
r\neq q
then

1\leq|ar-bq|=qr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\frac{b}{r}-\frac{a}{q}}}\right|\leq\frac{q^{2}}{2x}+\frac{r}{q}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{r}{q},

whereby
r\geq q/2
and
|\alpha-b/r|\leq 1/x
, and it follows that
S_{f}(x,\alpha)\ll\mathcal{R}(x,r)+x/q^{2}
.

In each of the three scenarios for
x
,
q
, and
r
described, we have
r\geq\tfrac{1}{2}q
and

S_{f}(x,\alpha)\ll\mathcal{R}(x,r)+x/q^{2}.

Since
r\geq\frac{q}{2}\geq\frac{R}{2}\geq\tfrac{3}{2}
(so that
r\geq 2
), we use the bound
\phi(n)\gg\frac{n}{|\log\log{n}|}
for
n\geq 2
(see, e.g.,
[
10
]
*Thm. 328) to deduce that

\phi(r)\gg\frac{r}{|\log\log r|}\gg\frac{R}{\log\log R},

and because
2\leq R\leq q\leq 2x/r
it follows that

\mathcal{R}(x,r)\ll\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{x\log^{1/2}(\log R)}{R^{1/2}}+\frac{x\log^{3/2}(2x/r)}{(2x/r)^{1/2}}\ll\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{x\log^{3/2}R}{R^{1/2}},

establishing Lemma
3.1
.
∎

As mentioned previously, ultimately we set
Q=X^{1/3}
, but for now we let
Q=X^{\delta}
for some
\delta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
for illustrative purposes. We note that the upper bound of
\tfrac{1}{2}
for
\delta
ensures that distinct major arcs
\mathfrak{M}(a/q)
do not overlap once
X
is sufficiently large.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
, let
\nu,\kappa\in(0,1)
satisfy
\nu+\kappa<1
, let
Q=X^{\delta}
, and let
X
be sufficiently large. For all
x\geq XQ^{-\nu}
one has

(3.4)

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)\\
k\leq Q^{\kappa}\end{subarray}}|S_{f}(x,k\alpha)|\ll\frac{x}{\log{x}}+\frac{x\log^{3/2}R}{R^{1/2}},

uniformly among multiplicative
f
with
|f|\leq 1
, where
R=Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}
.

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.2
that for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
and
k\leq Q^{\kappa}
, one has

(3.5)

\{k\alpha\}\in\mathfrak{m}(XQ^{-\nu},Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}).

By Dirichlet’s approximation theorem there exist
q_{k}
and
a_{k}
such that
(a_{k},q_{k})=1
,
1\leq q_{k}\leq XQ^{-\nu}/Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}
, and

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\{k\alpha\}-\frac{a_{k}}{q_{k}}}}\right|\leq\frac{Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}}{q_{k}XQ^{-\nu}}\leq\frac{1}{q_{k}^{2}}.

By (
3.5
) it holds that
q_{k}>Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}
, and by the assumptions that
Q=X^{\delta}
and that
X
is sufficiently large, it additionally holds that

(3.6)

2\leq Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}\leq q_{k}\leq\frac{XQ^{-\nu}}{Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}}.

As (
3.6
) remains valid with
XQ^{-\nu}
replaced by any
x\geq XQ^{-\nu}
, setting
R:=Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}
and applying Lemma
3.1
one deduces that

S_{f}(x,\{k\alpha\})\ll\frac{x}{\log{x}}+\frac{x\log^{3/2}R}{R^{1/2}},

and the result follows immediately since
S_{f}(x,\{k\alpha\})=S_{f}(x,k\alpha)
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

Because the supremum in inequality (
3.4
) is taken over all
k\leq Q^{\kappa}
, it is important that the implicit constant in inequality (
3.1
) does not depend on
q_{k}
and thereby depend on
k
. This is why we emphasize said independence in Lemma
3.1
.

Having established our desired bounds on
S_{f}(x,k\alpha)
, we are ready to bound
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))
for
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
and, after taking
Q=X^{1/3}
, establish Theorem
1.12
. For convenience, we note that Theorem
1.12
is equivalent to the statement that:
With
Q=X^{1/3}
, for all
X>X_{0}
and
\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)
one has

\Psi(\rho e(\alpha),f)\ll X/\log X,

uniformly among multiplicative
f
with
|f|\leq 1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.12
.

Let
\delta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
, let
\kappa,\nu\in(0,1)
be such that
\kappa<\nu
and
\kappa+\nu<1
, let
Q=X^{\delta}
, let
X
be sufficiently large, and let
k\leq Q^{\kappa}
. Recall that
F_{k}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{k}(n)z^{nk}
. Summing by parts, we have

(3.7)

F_{k}(\rho e(\alpha))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f^{k}(n)\rho^{nk}e(nk\alpha)=\frac{k}{X}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-xk/X}S_{f}(x,k\alpha)\,dx.

Let
R=Q^{1-\kappa-\nu}=X^{\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)}
. Bounding
S_{f}(x,k\alpha)\ll x
when
x\leq XQ^{-\nu}
and using inequality (
3.4
) when
x>XQ^{-\nu}
, we find that integral (
3.7
) is

\displaystyle\ll\frac{k}{X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{0}^{XQ^{-\nu}}e^{-xk/X}x\,dx+\int_{XQ^{-\nu}}^{\infty}e^{-xk/X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{x}{\log x}+\frac{x\log^{3/2}R}{R^{1/2}}}}\right)\,dx}}\right]

(3.8)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X}{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\int_{0}^{kQ^{-\nu}}e^{-u}u\,du+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{\log XQ^{-\nu}}+\frac{\log^{3/2}R}{R^{1/2}}}}\right)\int_{kQ^{-\nu}}^{\infty}e^{-u}u\,du}}\right].

Observing that

\frac{1}{\log XQ^{-\nu}}+\frac{\log^{3/2}R}{R^{1/2}}=\frac{1}{\log(X^{1-\delta\nu})}+\frac{\log^{3/2}(X^{\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)})}{(X^{\delta(1-\kappa-\nu)})^{1/2}}\ll\frac{1}{\log X},

we integrate by parts in expression (
3.8
)
to deduce that

(3.9)

F_{k}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll\frac{X}{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1-(1+kQ^{-\nu})e^{-kQ^{-\nu}}+\frac{1}{\log X}(1+kQ^{-\nu})e^{-kQ^{-\nu}}}}\right].

As
kQ^{-\nu}\leq Q^{\kappa-\nu}
and
\kappa-\nu<0
, and since
Q
is sufficiently large (because
X
is), we use the approximation

(1+x)e^{-x}=1-\tfrac{1}{2}x^{2}+O(x^{3})

for small
x
to bound the righthand side of (
3.9
) as

\displaystyle\ll\frac{X}{k}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\tfrac{1}{2}Q^{2\kappa-2\nu}+O(Q^{3\kappa-3\nu})+\frac{1}{\log X}(1+O(Q^{2\kappa-2\nu}))}}\right]\ll\frac{X/k}{\log X}.

From this, we conclude that

F_{k}(\rho e(\alpha))\ll\frac{X/k}{\log X}\qquad\text{for $\alpha\in\mathfrak{m}(X,Q)$ and $k\leq X^{\delta\kappa}$.}

Summing over all
k
and using Lemma
2.1
then, we have

\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\sum_{k\leq X^{\delta\kappa}}+\sum_{k>X^{\delta\kappa}}}}\right]\frac{F_{k}(\rho e(\alpha))}{k}\ll\frac{X}{\log X}\sum_{k\leq X^{\delta\kappa}}\frac{1}{k^{2}}+X^{1-\delta\kappa},

and this latter expression is

\ll\frac{X}{\log X}+\frac{X^{1-\delta\kappa}}{\log{X}}+X^{1-\delta\kappa}\ll\frac{X}{\log X}+X^{1-\delta\kappa}.

Taking
\delta=\tfrac{1}{3}
,
\kappa=\tfrac{1}{3}
, and
\nu=\tfrac{2}{5}
we see that

\kappa<\nu,\quad\kappa+\nu<1,\quad\text{and}\quad X^{1-\delta\kappa}=X^{\frac{8}{9}},

and the result follows.
∎

4.
The major arcs for odd prime
p

We now return to our specific consideration of
\Psi(z)=\Psi(z,\chi_{p})
for odd prime
p
. Specifically, we first derive first-order asymptotics for
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))
, as
\rho\to 1^{-}
, when
\alpha
is near a reduced rational
a/q\in[0,1]
. Using Mellin-transform methods one may compute lower order terms for
\Psi(\rho e(a/q))
, as seen in section
5
and later in section
12
, but these computations become cumbersome as
q
grows. At present we are simply interested in determining which major arcs
\mathfrak{M}(a/q)
are the dominant contributors in our asymptotic formulae, and thus coarser methods suffice for now.

As in the previous section, we begin with an undetermined
Q
such that
1\leq Q\leq X
. Now let
\alpha=a/q+\beta
, with
q\leq Q
,
(a,q)=1
, and
|\beta|\leq Q/(qX)
, or in other words, let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{M}(a/q)
with
q\leq Q
, and recall from (
2.1
) that

\Psi(z)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi^{k}(n)z^{nk}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}F_{k}(z^{k}).

It is convenient to let

\tau=X^{-1}(1-2\pi iX\beta),

so that for
\alpha=\tfrac{a}{q}+\beta
we may write
\rho e(\alpha)=e(\tfrac{a}{q})e^{-\tau}
. Doing this and letting
[p,q]:=\mathrm{lcm}[p,q]
, we have

F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{nka}{q}}}\right)\chi^{k}(n)e^{-nk\tau}=\sum_{m=1}^{[p,q]}e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{mka}{q}}}\right)\chi^{k}(m)\sum_{n\equiv m\,([p,q])}e^{-nk\tau}.

Since
[p,q]\ll q
trivially, and

mk|\tau|\ll[p,q]k|\tau|\ll qk\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\frac{1}{X}-2\pi i\beta}}\right|\ll qk\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1}{X}+\frac{Q}{qX}}}\right)\ll\frac{kQ}{X}

for
1\leq m\leq[p,q]
, we have

\sum_{n\equiv m\,([p,q])}e^{-nk\tau}=\frac{e^{-mk\tau}}{1-e^{-[p,q]k\tau}}=\frac{1}{[p,q]k\tau}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1+O(mk\tau)}{1+O(qk\tau)}}}\right)=\frac{1}{[p,q]k\tau}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1}}\right).

Again since
[p,q]\ll q\ll Q
, we deduce that

F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\frac{1}{[p,q]k\tau}\sum_{m=1}^{[p,q]}e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{mka}{q}}}\right)\chi^{k}(m)+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q}}\right).

We are thus motivated to define, for
0\leq a\leq q
,
(a,q)=1
, and
k\geq 1
, the quantities

(4.1)

U_{k}(q,a)=\frac{1}{[p,q]}\sum_{m=1}^{[p,q]}e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{mka}{q}}}\right)\chi^{k}(m),

where again
[p,q]=\mathrm{lcm}(p,q)
, so that

F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\frac{U_{k}(q,a)}{k\tau}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q}}\right)

and

(4.2)

\sum_{k\leq K}\frac{1}{k}F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\tau^{-1}\sum_{k\leq K}\frac{U_{k}(q,a)}{k^{2}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q\log{K}}}\right).

Because
|U_{k}(q,a)|\leq 1
, we complete the sum in (
4.2
) at the cost of
O(|\tau|^{-1}K^{-1})=O(X/K)
to deduce that

\sum_{k\leq K}\frac{1}{k}F_{k}(\rho^{k}e(k\alpha))=\tau^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{U_{k}(q,a)}{k^{2}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{K}}}\right)+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q\log{K}}}\right).

Recalling from Lemma
2.1
that
\sum_{k>K}k^{-1}F_{k}(z)\ll X/K
as well, we further deduce that

\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))=\tau^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{U_{k}(q,a)}{k^{2}}+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{K}}}\right)+O\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(Q\log{K}}}\right).

Setting
Q=X^{1/3}
and
K=X^{1/9}
(consistent with the choices
\delta=\tfrac{1}{3}
and
\kappa=\tfrac{1}{3}
from section
3
), we conclude that

(4.3)

\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{1-2\pi iX\beta}}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{U_{k}(q,a)}{k^{2}}+O(X^{\frac{8}{9}}).

With formula (
4.3
), “sorting” the major arcs
\mathfrak{M}(a/q)
based on their relative contributions to
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
is now a matter of computing
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}U_{k}(q,a)/k^{2}
for different
q
and

. As we ultimately find that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{U_{k}(1,1)}{k^{2}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{U_{k}(2,1)}{k^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{1}{p}}}\right),

which dictates the behavior of
\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))
on our principal arcs, it is convenient in the remainder of this paper to let

(4.4)

\mathfrak{c}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{1}{p}}}\right)

and normalize our constants using
\mathfrak{c}
. In particular, we define

(4.5)

V_{q}(a)=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{U_{k}(q,a)}{k^{2}}=\frac{1}{\mathfrak{c}[p,q]}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{m=1}^{[p,q]}e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{mka}{q}}}\right)\frac{\chi^{k}(m)}{k^{2}}

and note that
|V_{q}(a)|\leq 1
for all
(a,q)=1
.

The explicit computations of different

are little more than exercises in the elementary properties of Dirichlet characters. Because
\chi^{k}
is either
\chi_{0}\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,p)
or
\chi_{p}
depending on the parity of

, and because the parity of

also affects the formula for
V_{q}(a)
, the derivations of the different
V_{q}(a)
are somewhat tedious. Thus, we skip these derivations presently and include them in the appendix. Granting this, we establish the following proposition.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\chi=\chi_{p}
, let
X>X_{0}
, and let
\alpha=a/q+\beta
with
(a,q)=1
and
|\beta|\leq 1/(qX^{2/3})
. There exists a constant
V_{q}(a)
such that as
X\to\infty
one has

(4.6)

\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))=\frac{V_{q}(a)\mathfrak{c}X}{1-2\pi iX\beta}+O(X^{\frac{8}{9}}),

where

\mathfrak{c}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{1}{p}}}\right)

and
V_{q}(a)
, as defined in (
4.5
), has the following closed forms. First, if
p\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptq
then

(4.7)

V_{q}(a)=\begin{cases}1/q^{2}&2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptq,\\
4/q^{2}&2\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptq.\end{cases}

If
p\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptq
and
2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptq
, then

(4.8)

V_{q}(a)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\chi(a)G(\chi)(1-\tfrac{\chi(2)}{4})L_{2}(\chi)/\mathfrak{c}-1}}\right]p/q^{2},

where
G(\chi)=\sum_{m=1}^{p}\chi(m)e(m/p)
and
L_{2}(\chi)=L(2,\chi)
. Finally, if
p\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptq
and
2\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptq
then

(4.9)

V_{q}(a)=-4p/q^{2}.

Because they feature prominently in our later discussions, we note that

V_{1}(1)=V_{2}(1)=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad V_{4}(1)=V_{4}(3)=\tfrac{1}{4}.

5.
The principal arcs

Because

as mentioned above, comparison of the contributions of the arcs near

requires lower-order asymptotic terms for

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in[0,1):\|\alpha\|\leq 3/(8\pi X)\},

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in[0,1):|\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}|\leq 3/(8\pi X_{*})\}.

.

Remark 5.1
.

Despite the above definitions’ in terms of
X_{*}
, we do not presently distinguish
X
and
X_{*}
because, at present, both

and

are simply large real parameters. Thus, in light of (

) and (

with

for
\alpha
in the principal arcs
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{*}
.

Observing that
\chi^{k}(n)=\chi(n)
for odd

\chi^{k}(n)=\chi_{0}(n)
for even

, where
\chi_{0}
is the principal character (mod

), we split

into two sums

\Psi(z)=\sum_{k\equiv 0\,(2)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{0}(n)}{k}z^{nk}+\sum_{k\equiv 1\,(2)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{k}z^{nk},

which we denote by

Using the well-known formula
e^{-w}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty}\Gamma(s)w^{-s}\,ds

, we express

and

as sums of integrals, interchange the summations and integrations using the series’ absolute convergence, and thereby derive the formulae

(5.4)

\displaystyle\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\beta))=\Psi_{0}(e^{-\tau})

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}2^{-s-1}\zeta(s+1)L(s,\chi_{0})\Gamma(s)\tau^{-s}\,ds,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}(\rho e(\beta))=\Psi_{1}(e^{-\tau})

\displaystyle=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}(1-2^{-s-1})\zeta(s+1)L(s,\chi)\Gamma(s)\tau^{-s}\,ds.

In what follows we again use the conventions that

L_{r}(\chi)=L(r,\chi)\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{r}^{\prime}(\chi)=L^{\prime}(r,\chi)\qquad(r=0,1,2).

Lemma 5.2
.

For all
|\beta|\leq 3/(8\pi X)
, as
X\to\infty
one has

(5.6)

\Psi(\rho e(\beta))=\frac{\mathfrak{c}X}{1-2\pi iX\beta}+2\lambda\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{1-2\pi iX\beta}}}\right)+\Lambda+O(X^{-\frac{1}{2}}),

where

\mathfrak{c}=\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{24}(1-\tfrac{1}{p}),\quad\lambda=\tfrac{1}{4}L_{0}(\chi),\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda=\tfrac{1}{2}\big[L_{0}^{\prime}(\chi)+L_{0}(\chi)\log{2}\big]-\tfrac{1}{4}\log{p}.

Proof.

As
L(s,\chi_{0})=(1-p^{-s})\zeta(s)
, equation (
5.4
) states that

(5.7)

\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\beta))=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}2^{-s-1}(1-p^{-s})\zeta(s+1)\zeta(s)\Gamma(s)\tau^{-s}\,ds,

where again we write
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int
to indicate
\tfrac{1}{2\pi i}\int
.
Letting
T
be a large parameter to be chosen below, we first truncate the integral (
5.7
) at height
T
with error term

(5.8)

\ll\int_{T}^{\infty}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\Gamma(2+it)\tau^{-2-it}}}\right|\,dt.

Having assumed that
|\beta|\leq 3/(8\pi X)
we have

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\arg\tau}}\right|=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\arg(1-2\pi iX\beta)}}\right|\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\arctan(\tfrac{3}{4})}}\right|\leq\tfrac{\pi}{4},

which implies that

(5.9)

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\tau^{2-it}}}\right|=|\tau|^{-2}e^{t\arg\tau}\leq X^{2}e^{\pi|t|/4}.

We now recall two bounds on
\Gamma(s)
and
\zeta(s)
. First, one has
[
13
]
*ineq. (2.1.19)

(5.10)

|\Gamma(s)|\ll|s|^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\pi|t|+\tfrac{1}{6}|s|^{-1}}}\right)\qquad(\sigma\geq 0).

Second, in any halfplane
\sigma\geq\sigma_{0}
, as
|t|\to\infty
one has
[
15
]
*sec. 5.1

(5.11)

|\zeta(s)|\ll|t|^{k}\qquad\text{for some $k=k(\sigma_{0})$.}

Using inequalities (
5.9
), (
5.10
), and (
5.11
), it follows that integral (
5.8
) is

\ll X^{2}\int_{T}^{\infty}t^{c_{1}}e^{-\pi t/4}\,dt\ll X^{2}e^{-\pi T/5},

whereby

\Psi_{0}(e^{-\tau})=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{2-iT}^{2+iT}2^{-s-1}(1-p^{-s})\zeta(s+1)\zeta(s)\Gamma(s)\tau^{-s}\,ds+O(X^{2}e^{-\pi T/5}).

respectively. With these and (
5.10

Selecting

and replacing

with

\Psi_{0}(\rho e(\beta))=\frac{\mathfrak{c}X}{1-2\pi iX\beta}-\tfrac{1}{4}\log{p}+O(X^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

Now for

, equation (

\Psi_{1}(\rho e(\beta))=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{2-i\infty}^{2+i\infty}(1-2^{-s-1})\zeta(s+1)L(s,\chi)\Gamma(s)\tau^{-s}\,ds.

and combining this with (

∎

6.
The relationships between

,

, and

Recalling that

[0,1)
into the sets

\mathscr{A}:=[0,\tfrac{1}{4})\cup[\tfrac{3}{4},1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathscr{A}_{*}:=[\tfrac{1}{4},\tfrac{3}{4}),

and change the path of integration on the left side of (
7.1
) to be the positively oriented path consisting of the two semicircles

\rho e(\mathscr{A}):=\{\rho e(\alpha):\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\rho_{*}e(\mathscr{A}_{*}):=\{\rho_{*}e(\alpha):\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{*}\}

and the two connecting line segments
[i\rho,i\rho_{*}]
and
[-i\rho_{*},-i\rho]
. Using this new contour in (
7.1
), we have

(7.2)

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\rho e(\mathscr{A})}\Phi(z)z^{-n-1}\,dz+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\rho_{*}e(\mathscr{A}_{*})}\Phi(z)z^{-n-1}\,dz+\mathfrak{T}(\rho,\rho_{*}),

where

\mathfrak{T}(\rho,\rho_{*}):=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{i\rho}^{i\rho_{*}}\Phi(z)z^{-n-1}\,dz+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{-i\rho_{*}}^{-i\rho}\Phi(z)z^{-n-1}\,dz.

We now show that the transference term
\mathfrak{T}(\rho,\rho_{*})
is small compared to the main integrals in (
7.2
), both of which grow like
n^{c_{0}}\exp(2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n})
, with
\mathfrak{c}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})
and some constant
c_{0}
, as shown in sections
9
and
10
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{c}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})
, let
\lambda
and
\lambda_{*}
be real constants, and for
n>n_{0}
let

(7.3)

X=\big(\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda^{2}}-\lambda\big)/\mathfrak{c}\qquad\text{and}\qquad X_{*}=\big(\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda_{*}^{2}}-\lambda_{*}\big)/\mathfrak{c}.

As
n\to\infty
, one has

\mathfrak{T}(\rho,\rho_{*})\ll\exp(\tfrac{3}{2}\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}).

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
and let
n>n_{0}
. It is evident that
\mathfrak{T}(\rho,\rho_{*})=-\mathfrak{T}(\rho_{*},\rho)
, so there is no loss of generality in assuming that
X\leq X_{*}
, and therefore that
\rho\leq\rho_{*}
. We first note, by (
7.3
), that

n/X=\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda\qquad\text{and}\qquad n/X_{*}=\mathfrak{c}X_{*}+2\lambda_{*},

and further note that as
n\to\infty
we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda^{2}}+\lambda=\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}+O(n^{-1/2}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{c}X_{*}+2\lambda_{*}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda_{*}^{2}}+\lambda_{*}=\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}+O(n^{-1/2}).

Next, observing that
\rho^{-n}=e^{n/X}
and
\rho_{*}^{-n}=e^{n/X_{*}}
, it follows that

(7.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\rho}^{\rho_{*}}\frac{dt}{t^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{n}(\rho^{-n}-\rho_{*}^{-n})=\frac{1}{n}(e^{\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda}-e^{\mathfrak{c}X_{*}+2\lambda_{*}})\ll e^{\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}}.\end{aligned}

Now let
t=\exp(-1/X_{t})
for
\rho\leq t\leq\rho_{*}
. Recalling that
V_{4}(1)=V_{4}(3)=\frac{1}{4}
from the remark following Proposition
4.1
, we have

(7.5)

\operatorname{Re}\Psi(it)=\tfrac{1}{4}\mathfrak{c}X_{t}+O(X_{t}^{8/9})\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\mathfrak{c}X_{*}+O(X_{*}^{8/9})=\tfrac{1}{4}\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}+O(n^{4/9})

for
\rho\leq t\leq\rho_{*}
, which, for
n>n_{0}
, is less than
(\tfrac{1}{4}+\varepsilon)\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}
. Using inequalities (
7.4
) and (
7.5
), we find that

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{i\rho}^{i\rho_{*}}\Phi(z)z^{-n-1}\,dz}}\right|\ll\exp((\tfrac{1}{4}+\varepsilon)\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n})\int_{\rho}^{\rho_{*}}\frac{dt}{t^{n+1}}\ll\exp((\tfrac{5}{4}+\varepsilon)\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}),

and the result follows by taking
\varepsilon
to be sufficiently small.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

For
n>n_{0}
let
\rho=e^{-1/X}
and
\rho_{*}=e^{-1/X_{*}}
, with
X
and
X_{*}
as defined in Lemma
7.1
. As
n\to\infty
one has

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})

\displaystyle=\rho^{-n}\int_{\mathscr{A}}\Phi(\rho e(\alpha))e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha+\rho_{*}^{-n}\int_{\mathscr{A}_{*}}\Phi(\rho_{*}e(\alpha))e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha+O\big(e^{\frac{3}{2}\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}}\big).

8.
The contributions of the nonprincipal arcs

We now show, for odd primes
p\neq 5
, that the magnitude of
\Phi(\rho e(\alpha))
is largest when
\alpha
is close to
0
,
1
, or
\frac{1}{2}
. As
|\Phi(z)|=\exp(\operatorname{Re}\Psi(z))
, we accomplish this by using the formulae of Proposition
4.1
to compare
\operatorname{Re}\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))
for different
\alpha
. We again recall that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in[0,1):\|\alpha\|\leq 3/(8\pi X)\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{P}_{*}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in[0,1):|\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}|\leq 3/(8\pi X_{*})\}.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
p\neq 5
be an odd prime and let
X
and
X_{*}
be sufficiently large. There exists a constant
0<\delta<1
such that: for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathfrak{P}
one has

(8.1)

\operatorname{Re}\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))\leq\delta\Psi(\rho),

and for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{*}\setminus\mathfrak{P}_{*}
one has

(8.2)

\operatorname{Re}\Psi(\rho_{*}e(\alpha))\leq\delta\Psi(-\rho_{*}).

Proof.

Letting
Q=X^{1/3}
, we suppose that
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}
can be written as
\alpha=a/q+\beta
with
0\leq a\leq q\leq X^{1/3}
,
(a,q)=1
, and
|\beta|\leq 1/(qX^{2/3})
. Defining

\Delta=(1+4\pi^{2}X^{2}\beta^{2})^{-1/2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi=\arctan(2\pi X\beta)

with
\arctan(u)\in(-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2})
, equation (
4.6
) states that

\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))=V_{q}(a)\mathfrak{c}X\Delta e^{i\varphi}+O(X^{8/9}).

Recalling that
V_{1}(1)=1
(which we reconfirmed in Lemma
5.2
) we have

\Psi(\rho)=\mathfrak{c}X+O(X^{8/9}),

and thus to establish inequality (
8.1
) it suffices to show that

(8.3)

\operatorname{Re}(V_{q}(a)\Delta e^{i\varphi})\leq\delta\qquad\text{for some $\delta<1$.}

If
q>2
and
(p,q)=1
, then
V_{q}(a)\leq\frac{1}{4}
by (
4.7
). As
V_{q}(a)<0
when
2p\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptq
, inequality (
8.3
) holds
a fortiori
as
X\to\infty
. It thus remains to consider the case where
p\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptq
but
2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptq
, say
q=(2r-1)p
with
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Here

(8.4)

V_{q}(a)\Delta e^{i\varphi}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\chi(a)G(\chi)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{\chi(2)}{4}}}\right)\frac{L_{2}(\chi)}{\mathfrak{c}}-1}}\right]\frac{\Delta e^{i\varphi}}{(2r-1)^{2}p},

where again
L_{2}(\chi)=L(2,\chi)
. Since we are interested in upper bounds for the real part of (
8.4
), it suffices to let
r=1
.

To handle the cumbersome term in square brackets in (
8.4
), it is convenient to let

W_{p}=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1-\frac{\chi(2)}{4}}}\right)\frac{L_{2}(\chi)}{\mathfrak{c}}=\frac{6(4-\chi(2))L_{2}(\chi)}{\pi^{2}(1-1/p)}\qquad(\text{since $\mathfrak{c}=\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{24}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})$})

and record the following crude bounds on
W_{p}
. Namely, because

L_{2}(\chi)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\text{ prime}\\
\ell\neq p\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{1}{1-\chi(\ell)\ell^{-2}}\Big)\geq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\text{ prime}\\
\ell\neq p\end{subarray}}\Big(\frac{1}{1+\ell^{-2}}\Big)=(1+p^{-2})\frac{\zeta(4)}{\zeta(2)}=(1+p^{-2})\frac{\pi^{2}}{15}

and

L_{2}(\chi)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(k)}{k^{2}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi_{0}(k)}{k^{2}}=(1-p^{-2})\frac{\pi^{2}}{6},

after simplifying we deduce that

(8.5)

1<\tfrac{2}{5}(4-\chi(2))\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{1+p^{-2}}{1-p^{-1}}}}\right)\leq W_{p}\leq(4-\chi(2))\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(1+\frac{1}{p}}}\right).

Next we recall the classical facts that the Gauss sum
G(\chi_{p})=\sqrt{p}
if
p\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
, and
G(\chi_{p})=i\sqrt{p}
if
p\equiv 3\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
.

Now suppose that
p\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
. As
\Delta\leq 1
, it follows from (
8.4
) and (
8.5
) that

\operatorname{Re}(V_{q}(a)\Delta e^{i\varphi})\leq(\sqrt{p}W_{p}-1)/p,

and thus it suffices to show that
(\sqrt{p}W_{p}-1)/p<1
. Numerical estimates show that, among primes
p>5
such that
p\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
, one has

\frac{\sqrt{p}W_{p}-1}{p}\leq W_{13}=\frac{1}{13}\bigg(\frac{65\sqrt{13}}{2\pi^{2}}L_{2}(\chi_{13})-1\bigg)\approx 0.69,

which validates inequality (
8.3
) when
p\equiv 1\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
(and
p\neq 5
) and
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\cap\mathfrak{M}(\tfrac{a}{(2r-1)p})
.

We remark that since
L_{2}(\chi_{5})=\tfrac{4\pi^{2}\sqrt{5}}{125}
(see, e.g., Lemma
A.4
), one has

\tfrac{1}{5}(\sqrt{5}W_{5}-1)=1,

which implies that
V_{5}(1)=V_{5}(4)=1
. Thus
\Psi(\rho e(\frac{1}{5}))
and
\Psi(\rho e(\tfrac{4}{5}))
are both “on par” with
\Psi(\rho)
and
\Psi(-\rho_{*})
as
\rho
and
\rho_{*}
tend to
1
. This is precisely why we specially treat
\Psi(z,\chi_{5})
and
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{5})
in section
12
.

Returning to (
8.4
), suppose now that
p\equiv 3\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
. Now
G(\chi)=i\sqrt{p}
, whereby

V_{q}(a)\Delta e^{i\varphi}=\frac{1}{p}\big(i\chi(a)\sqrt{p}W_{p}-1\big)\Delta e^{i\varphi}.

Since

e^{i\varphi}=\frac{1+i(2\pi X\beta)}{(1+4\pi^{2}X^{2}\beta^{2})^{1/2}}=\Delta(1+i(2\pi X\beta))

and
\Delta^{2}=(1+(2\pi X\beta)^{2})^{-1}
, we have

(8.6)

\operatorname{Re}(V_{q}(a)\Delta e^{i\varphi})=\frac{-1-(2\pi X\beta)\chi(a)\sqrt{p}W_{p}}{(1+(2\pi X\beta)^{2})p}.

As we are interested in bounding (
8.6
) above by some
\delta<1
, it suffices to let
\chi(a)=-1
and
\beta>0
, set
u:=(2\pi X\beta)
, and show that

\frac{-1+u\sqrt{p}W_{p}}{(1+u^{2})p}<1,\qquad\text{i.e., that}\qquad u^{2}-(W_{p}/\sqrt{p})u+(1+1/p)>0,

for
all

u\in\mathbb{R}
. This holds if and only if
(1+1/p)>W_{p}^{2}/(4p)
, or equivalently if and only if
W_{p}^{2}/(4(p+1))<1
. Using the crude bound (
8.5
) we have

(8.7)

\frac{W^{2}_{p}}{4(p+1)}\leq\frac{(4-\chi(2))^{2}(1+1/p)^{2}}{4(p+1)},

and for
p\geq 7
the right side of (
8.7
) is maximized when
p=11
(regardless of the residue of
p
modulo 4), where it has value
\tfrac{300}{484}\approx 0.62
. Numerically computing
W_{3}^{2}/16
we have

\tfrac{1}{16}W_{3}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{16}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{45}{\pi^{2}}L_{2}(\chi_{3})}}\right)^{2}\approx 0.79,

and therefore inequality (
8.3
) holds when
p\equiv 3\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,4)
and
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\cap\mathfrak{M}(\tfrac{a}{(2r-1)p})
.

In total now, we have established that inequality (
8.1
) holds for
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\cap\mathfrak{M}(X,X^{1/3})\setminus(\mathfrak{M}(\frac{0}{1})\cup\mathfrak{M}(\frac{1}{1}))
. Since Theorem
1.12
establishes that

\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))\ll X/\log{X}\qquad\text{for $\alpha\in\mathscr{A}\cap\mathfrak{m}(X,X^{1/3})$},

inequality (
8.1
) holds
a-fortiori
for those
\alpha
as well. Thus, it remains to handle those
\alpha\in(\mathfrak{M}(\frac{0}{1})\cup\mathfrak{M}(\frac{1}{1}))\setminus\mathfrak{P}
. However, if
\alpha=\beta\in\mathfrak{M}(\frac{0}{1})
or
\alpha=1-\beta\in\mathfrak{M}(\frac{1}{1})
with
\beta\geq 3/(8\pi X)
, then inequality (
8.3
) is immediate since

\Delta\leq(1+\tfrac{36}{64})^{-\frac{1}{2}}<1,

completing the proof of inequality (
8.1
). The arguments for (
8.2
) for
\alpha\in\mathscr{A}_{*}\setminus\mathfrak{P}_{*}
and (
8.2
) are identical, mutatis mutandis.
∎

Recalling the result of Corollary
7.2
, we now use Lemma
8.1
to further isolate the main terms in our asymptotic formula for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
, which we recall are ultimately of order
n^{c_{0}}\exp(2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n})
for some
c_{0}
.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let
p\neq 5
be an odd prime, let
\mathfrak{c}=\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{24}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})
, let
\lambda
and
\lambda_{*}
be the real constants from Lemmata
5.2
and
5.3
, respectively, and for
n>n_{0}
let

(8.8)

X=\big(\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda^{2}}-\lambda\big)/\mathfrak{c}\qquad\text{and}\qquad X_{*}=\big(\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda_{*}^{2}}-\lambda_{*}\big)/\mathfrak{c}.

For some constant
\delta>0
, as
n\to\infty
one has

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})=\rho^{-n}\int_{\mathfrak{P}}\Phi(\rho e(\alpha))e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha+\rho_{*}^{-n}\int_{\mathfrak{P}_{*}}\Phi(\rho_{*}e(\alpha))e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha+O(e^{(2-\delta)\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}}).

Proof.

Recalling from Lemma
5.2
that

\Psi(\rho)=\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda\log X+\Lambda+O(X^{-1/2}),

with
\lambda
and
\Lambda
given in said lemma (though the exact values are irrelevant here), Lemma
8.1
implies the existence of some
\delta_{1}<1
such that

(8.9)

\displaystyle\rho^{-n}\int_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathfrak{P}}\Phi(\rho e(\alpha))e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha\ll\rho^{-n}e^{\delta_{1}\Psi(\rho)}\ll\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\tfrac{n}{X}+\delta_{1}(\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda\log X)}}\right).

We now note that the first equation in (
8.8
) implies that
n/X=\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda
. As
\delta_{1}(\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda\log X)<\delta_{2}\mathfrak{c}X
for some
\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}<1
and all
X>X_{0}(\delta_{1})
, again using (
8.8
) we have

\tfrac{n}{X}+\delta_{1}(\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda\log X)<(1+\delta_{2})\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda=(1+\delta_{2})\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda^{2}}+(1-\delta_{2})\lambda.

Since, as
n\to\infty
, one has

(1+\delta_{2})\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda^{2}}+(1-\delta_{2})\lambda\sim(1+\delta_{2})\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}<(2-\delta_{3})\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}

for some
\delta_{3}>0
, it follows that

\exp(\tfrac{n}{X}+\delta_{1}(\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda\log X))\ll\exp((2-\delta_{3})\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}).

From (
8.9
) we conclude that

\rho^{-n}\int_{\mathscr{A}\setminus\mathfrak{P}}\exp(\Psi(\rho e(\alpha))-2\pi in\alpha)\,d\alpha\ll\exp((2-\delta_{3})\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}),

and an analogous argument, using Lemmata
5.3
and
8.1
and equations (
8.8
), provides a similar bound for the integral over
\mathscr{A}_{*}\setminus\mathfrak{P}_{*}
. By assuming, without loss of generality, that
\delta_{3}
is small enough that
\frac{3}{2}<2-\delta_{3}
, we obtain the result by taking
\delta=\delta_{3}
.
∎

9.
The principal integrals

We now apply the formulae of sections
5
and
6
to derive asymptotic formulae for the integrals over
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{*}
. As the arguments for both integrals are so similar, and because we must later consider other “principal” integrals in sections
12
and
13
, we handle these cases simultaneously in the following general proposition.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
0\leq a\leq q
and
(a,q)=1
, and let
\mathfrak{c}
,
\kappa
, and
K
be constants dependent on
p
,
q
and
a
, with
\mathfrak{c}>0
. For
n>n_{0}
let

(9.1)

X=\big(\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\kappa^{2}}-\kappa\big)/\mathfrak{c},

and suppose that, for
n>n_{0}
and
|\beta|\leq 3/(8\pi X)
, one has

(9.2)

\Psi(\rho e(\tfrac{a}{q}+\beta))=\frac{\mathfrak{c}X}{1-2\pi iX\beta}+2\kappa\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{1-2\pi iX\beta}}}\right)+K+O(X^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

Then, as
n\to\infty
, one has

(9.3)

\rho^{-n}\int_{\mathfrak{P}(a/q,X)}\Phi(\rho e(\alpha))e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha=e\Big(\frac{-na}{q}\Big)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{\mathfrak{c}}}}\right)^{\kappa}e^{K}\frac{\mathfrak{c}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{3}{4}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right],

where

\mathfrak{P}(a/q,X)=\{a/q+\beta:\beta\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }|\beta|\leq 3/(8\pi X)\}.

Remark 9.2
.

In the following proof, quantities
c_{0},c_{1},\ldots
denote positive constants.

Proof.

By (
9.1
), as
n\to\infty
we have

(9.4)

n/X=\mathfrak{c}X+2\kappa\qquad\text{and}\qquad X=(n/\mathfrak{c})^{1/2}-\kappa/\mathfrak{c}+O(n^{-1/2}).

We start by writing integral (
9.3
) as

(9.5)

\int_{|\beta|\leq\frac{3}{8\pi X}}\rho^{-n}\exp\!\big[\Psi(\rho e(\tfrac{a}{q}+\beta))-2\pi in(\tfrac{a}{q}+\beta)\big]\,d\beta.

Writing
\rho e(\beta)=\exp(X^{-1}(1-2\pi iX\beta))
, we observe that under the change of variable
t=2\pi X\beta
, formula (
9.2
) states that

(9.6)

\Psi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(e(\tfrac{a}{q})e^{\frac{1}{X}(1-it)}}}\right)=\frac{\mathfrak{c}X}{1-it}+2\kappa\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{1-it}}}\right)+K+O(X^{-\frac{1}{2}}).

Making said change of variable in (
9.5
), the integral therein becomes

\frac{e(-na/q)}{2\pi X}\int_{-\frac{3}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Psi\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(e^{\frac{1}{X}(1-it)}}}\right)+(1-it)\frac{n}{X}}}\right]\,dt.

Now letting

\Theta(t,X):=(1-it)\frac{n}{X}+\frac{\mathfrak{c}X}{1-it}+2\kappa\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{1-it}}}\right),

formulae (
9.4
) and (
9.6
) imply that integral (
9.5
) is

(9.7)

=e\Big(\frac{-na}{q}\Big)\frac{e^{K}}{2\pi X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{4}})}}\right]\int_{-\frac{3}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\exp\Theta(t,X)\,dt.

In light of (
9.7
), we focus on the integral
\int_{-\frac{3}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\exp\Theta(t,X)\,dt
. For
|t|<\tfrac{3}{4}
set

(9.8)

\Delta:=(1+t^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi:=\arctan t=t-\tfrac{1}{3}t^{3}+\tfrac{1}{5}t^{5}-\cdots,

so that

(1-it)^{-1}=\Delta e^{i\varphi}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\log\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{X}{1-it}}}\right)=\log X\Delta+i\varphi.

With these, now

(9.9)

\Theta(t,X)=(1-it)(\mathfrak{c}X+2\kappa)+\mathfrak{c}X\Delta e^{i\varphi}+2\kappa(\log X\Delta+i\varphi).

When
|t|<\tfrac{3}{4}
we have
\tfrac{4}{5}<\Delta\leq 1
and

\Delta^{2}=(1+t^{2})^{-1}\leq 1-t^{2}+t^{4}\leq 1-\tfrac{2}{5}t^{2},

and because
\cos\varphi=\Delta
and
X
is large we find that

(9.10)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Re}\Theta(t,X)

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{c}X+2\kappa+\mathfrak{c}X(1-\tfrac{2}{5}t^{2})+2\kappa\log X+2\kappa\log{\Delta}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathfrak{c}X+2\kappa+2\kappa\log X-\tfrac{2}{5}\mathfrak{c}Xt^{2}+c_{0}.

If
|t|\geq X^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{\frac{3}{2}}
as well, then

(9.11)

-\tfrac{2}{5}\mathfrak{c}Xt^{2}+c_{0}<-\tfrac{2}{5}\mathfrak{c}(\log X)^{3}+c_{0}<-c_{1}(\log X)^{3}.

Since
-c_{1}(\log X)^{3}\sim-c_{2}(\log n)^{3}
by (
9.4
), it follows from (
9.10
) and (
9.11
) that

(9.12)

\displaystyle\int_{X^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\log{X})^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq|t|<\frac{3}{4}}\exp\Theta(t,X)\,dt

\displaystyle\ll X^{2\kappa}e^{2\mathfrak{c}X+2\kappa}\exp(-c_{1}(\log X)^{3})\ll X^{2\kappa}e^{2\mathfrak{c}X+2\kappa}n^{-B}

for any
B>0
and
n>n_{0}(B)
.

In light of (
9.12
), for the remainder of the proof it is convenient to let

\delta:=X^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{\frac{3}{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Y:=2(\mathfrak{c}X+\kappa).

It thus remains to consider the integral in (
9.7
) for
|t|<\delta
.
Returning to equation (
9.9
), we now express
\Delta e^{i\varphi}=(1-it)^{-1}
as a geometric series, and write
\varphi=t-\frac{1}{3}t^{3}+\frac{1}{5}t^{5}-\cdots
as in (
9.8
), so that

\displaystyle\Theta(t,X)

\displaystyle=(1-it)(\mathfrak{c}X+2\kappa)+\mathfrak{c}X(1+it-t^{2}+O(|t|^{3}))

\displaystyle\qquad+2\kappa\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\log X\Delta+it+O(|t|^{3})}}\right)

\displaystyle=Y+2\kappa\log X-\mathfrak{c}Xt^{2}-\kappa\log(1+t^{2})+O(X\delta^{3})

\displaystyle=Y+2\kappa\log X-\tfrac{1}{2}Yt^{2}+O(t^{4})+O(X\delta^{3}).

As
t^{4}\leq\delta^{4}\leq X\delta^{3}
and
X\delta^{3}=X^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\log X)^{\frac{9}{2}}\ll n^{-\frac{1}{5}}
, we deduce that

\Theta(t,X)=Y+2\kappa\log X-\tfrac{1}{2}Yt^{2}+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})\qquad(|t|<\delta),

and thus we have

(9.13)

\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\exp\Theta(t,x)\,dt=X^{2\kappa}e^{Y}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right]\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}e^{-\tfrac{1}{2}Yt^{2}}\,dt.

As
Y\asymp X\asymp\sqrt{n}
and
\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}Yt^{2})\,dt\ll\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}Y\delta^{2})/(Y\delta)
, we have

\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}e^{-\frac{1}{2}Yt^{2}}\,dt-\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{Y}}\ll\frac{\exp(-c_{3}(\log X)^{3})}{X^{1/2}(\log X)^{3/2}}\ll\exp(-c_{4}(\log n)^{3})\ll n^{-B}

for
B>0
and
n>n_{0}(B)
. Since
Y=2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\kappa^{2}}\sim 2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}
and
B
can be arbitrarily large, it follows from equation (
9.13
) that

(9.14)

\displaystyle\int_{-\delta}^{\delta}\exp\Theta(t,X)\,dt

\displaystyle=X^{2\kappa}e^{Y}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right]\bigg[\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{Y}}+O(n^{-B})\bigg]

\displaystyle=X^{2\kappa}e^{Y}\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{Y}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right].

Using (
9.12
) and (
9.14
) in (
9.7
) then, and again using the facts that
Y\sim 2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}
and that
B>0
can be arbitrary, we deduce that

e\Big(\frac{-na}{q}\Big)\frac{e^{K}}{2\pi X}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{4}})}}\right]\int_{-\frac{3}{4}}^{\frac{3}{4}}\exp\Theta(t,X)\,dt=e\Big(\frac{-na}{q}\Big)e^{K}\frac{X^{2\kappa-1}e^{Y}}{\sqrt{2\pi Y}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right].

Finally, using the asymptotic relations

X=\sqrt{n/\mathfrak{c}}-\kappa/\mathfrak{c}+O(n^{-1/2})\qquad\text{and}\qquad Y=2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}+O(n^{-1}),

we find that

e\Big(\frac{-na}{q}\Big)e^{K}\frac{X^{2\kappa-1}e^{Y}}{\sqrt{2\pi Y}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right]=e\Big(\frac{-na}{q}\Big)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{\mathfrak{c}}}}\right)^{\kappa}e^{K}\frac{\mathfrak{c}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{3}{4}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right],

completing the proof of the proposition.
∎

10.
A general asymptotic formula

After briefly recalling the pertinent results and constants from the preceding sections, we now apply our results to derive a general asymptotic formula for
\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi_{p})
, again excluding the cases of

and

) and by the values of

, which is equivalent to considering the residues

We first recall the constants

(10.1)

\kappa=\pi\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{3}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{c}=\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{24}(1-\tfrac{1}{p}),

along with Hardy and Ramanujan’s “benchmark” formula

\mathfrak{p}(n,1)\sim(4\sqrt{3})^{-1}n^{-1}\exp\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\kappa\sqrt{n}}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right].

Again writing

Lemmata

and

establish that

(10.2)

\displaystyle\Psi(\rho)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{c}X+2\lambda\log X+\Lambda+O(X^{-\frac{1}{2}})\qquad(X\to\infty),

(10.3)

\displaystyle\Psi(-\rho_{*})

respectively, where

(10.4)

\displaystyle\lambda\hphantom{{}_{*}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}L_{0}(\chi),\qquad

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[L_{0}^{\prime}(\chi)+L_{0}(\chi)\log{2}}}\right]-\tfrac{1}{4}\log{p},

(10.5)

\displaystyle\lambda_{*}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{4}(2\chi(2)-1)L_{0}(\chi),\qquad

\displaystyle\Lambda_{*}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[(2\chi(2)-1)L_{0}^{\prime}(\chi)-L_{0}(\chi)\log{2}}}\right]-\tfrac{1}{4}\log{p}.

Again defining

(10.6)

X=(\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda^{2}}-\lambda)/\mathfrak{c}\qquad\text{and}\qquad X_{*}=(\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n+\lambda_{*}^{2}}-\lambda_{*})/\mathfrak{c},

Proposition
8.2
establishes that

(10.7)

\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi)=\rho^{-n}\int_{\mathfrak{P}}\Phi(\rho e(\alpha))e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha+\rho_{*}^{-n}\int_{\mathfrak{P}_{*}}\Phi(\rho_{*}e(\alpha))e(-n\alpha)\,d\alpha+O(e^{(2-\delta)\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}})

for some
\delta>0
. Using (

)–(

) then, Proposition

provides asymptotic formulae for the integrals over
\mathfrak{P}
and
\mathfrak{P}_{*}
in (

(10.8)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}(n,\chi)

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{\mathfrak{c}}}}\right)^{\lambda}e^{\Lambda}\frac{\mathfrak{c}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{3}{4}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right]+(-1)^{n}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{n}{\mathfrak{c}}}}\right)^{\lambda_{*}}e^{\Lambda_{*}}\frac{\mathfrak{c}^{\frac{1}{4}}e^{2\sqrt{\mathfrak{c}n}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{1}{2}}n^{\frac{3}{4}}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[1+O(n^{-\frac{1}{5}})}}\right]

Because the first term on the righthand side here is

(10.9)

For the moment, we assume that
L_{0}(\chi_{p})\geq 0
for all

. Then, using (

(10.10)

\lambda_{*}-\lambda=\tfrac{1}{2}(\chi(2)-1)L_{0}(\chi)\leq 0,

The special case of

The sequence

U_{k}(q,a)=0\qquad\text{if $q_{1}\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptk$.}

Now supposing that
k=q_{1}k_{1}
for some
k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
, one has

(A.4)

U_{k}(q,a)=\begin{cases}1-1/p&\text{$2\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk$ and $p\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk_{1}$,}\\
-1/p&\text{$2\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk$ and $p\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptk_{1}$,}\\
G(\chi)\chi(ak_{1})/p&\text{$2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptk$.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Now with
[p,q]=q
, similar to equation (
A.2
) we find at once that

qU_{k}(q,a)=\sum_{t=1}^{q_{1}}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\chi^{k}(tp+s)e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{(tp+s)ka}{q}}}\right)=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\chi^{k}(s)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{ska}{q}}}\right)\sum_{t=1}^{q_{1}}e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{tka}{q_{1}}}}\right),

so that clearly
U_{k}(q,a)=0
if
q_{1}\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptk
. Thus, suppose that
q_{1}\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk
, say
k=q_{1}k_{1}
.
Noting that
k/q=k_{1}/p
, it follows that

(A.5)

U_{k}(q,a)=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\chi^{k}(s)e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{sk_{1}a}{p}}}\right).

First, if
2\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk
then
\chi^{k}=\chi_{0}\,\,(\mathrm{mod}\,\,p)
and the righthand side of (
A.5
) is

\frac{1}{p}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\chi_{0}(s)e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{sk_{1}a}{p}}}\right)\begin{cases}1-1/p&p\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk_{1},\\
-1/p&p\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptk_{1},\end{cases}

which gives the first two cases of (
A.4
). If
2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptk
then
\chi^{k}=\chi
, and the righthand side of (
A.5
) is equal to

\frac{1}{p}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\chi(s)e\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\frac{sk_{1}a}{p}}}\right)=\frac{G(\chi)\bar{\chi}(ak_{1})}{p}=\frac{G(\chi)\chi(ak_{1})}{p},

establishing the final case of (
A.4
).
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Suppose that
p\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptq
and
(a,q)=1
. If
2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptq
, then

V_{q}(a)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\chi(a)G(\chi)(1-\tfrac{\chi(2)}{4})L_{2}(\chi)/\mathfrak{c}-1}}\right]\frac{p}{q^{2}},

where
\mathfrak{c}=\tfrac{\pi^{2}}{24}(1-\tfrac{1}{p})
and
L_{2}(\chi)=L(2,\chi)
.

Proof.

Let
q=pq_{1}
for some
q_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
A.2
,
U_{k}(q,a)
is nonzero only if
q_{1}\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk
, so for such
k
let
k=q_{1}k_{1}
. When
2\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk
, since
2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptq
one has
2\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk
if and only if
2\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptk_{1}
, so that

(A.6)

\sum_{2|k}^{\infty}\frac{U_{k}(q,a)}{k^{2}}=\sum_{2p|k_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(q_{1}k_{1})^{2}}-\sum_{2|k_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{1/p}{(q_{1}k_{1})^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{24q^{2}}(1-p)=-\frac{\mathfrak{c}p}{q^{2}}.

Now considering
\sum_{2\hskip 0.81949pt\nmid\hskip 0.81949ptk}U_{k}(q,a)/k^{2}
, by (
A.4
) and the fact that
2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptk
if and only if
2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptk_{1}
, we have

\frac{U_{k}(q,a)}{k^{2}}=\frac{G(\chi)\chi(ak_{1})}{p(q_{1}k_{1})^{2}}=\frac{\chi(a)G(\chi)p}{q^{2}}\cdot\frac{\chi(k_{1})}{k_{1}^{2}}\qquad(\text{for $2\hskip-1.49994pt\nmid\hskip-1.49994ptk$}),

so that

(A.7)

\sum_{2\hskip 0.81949pt\nmid\hskip 0.81949ptk}^{\infty}\frac{U_{k}(q,a)}{k^{2}}=\chi(a)G(\chi)\frac{p}{q^{2}}\sum_{2\hskip 0.81949pt\nmid\hskip 0.81949ptk_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(k_{1})}{k_{1}^{2}}=\chi(a)G(\chi)(1-\tfrac{\chi(2)}{4})L_{2}(\chi)\cdot\frac{p}{q^{2}}.

The result then follows from adding (
A.6
) and (
A.7
) and dividing by
\mathfrak{c}
.
∎

Finally, using similar arguments to those above, we readily verify that

V_{q}(a)=-4p/q^{2}\qquad\text{if $2p\hskip-1.99997pt\mid\hskip-1.99997ptq$},

which completes the verification of the formulae for
V_{q}(a)
in Proposition
4.1
.

( p , q ) (p,q) -analogues of the generalized Touchard polynomials and Stirling numbers
Lahcen Oussi

Abstract
. In this paper we introduce a ( p , q ) (p,q) -deformed analogues of the generalized Touchard polynomials and Stirling numbers, the post-quantum analogues of the q q -deformed generalized Touchard polynomials and Stirling numbers. The connection between these deformations is established. A recurrence relation for the ( p , q ) (p,q) -deformed generalized Touchard polynomials is expounded, elucidating a ( p , q ) (p,q) -deformation of Spivey’s relation.

1.
Introduction

The Stirling numbers of the second kind, denoted by
S(n,k)
, count the number of ways to partition a set of size
n
into
k
non-empty disjoint subsets. They appear as coefficients in the normal ordering of
\left(X\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{n}
as follows

(1)

\left(X\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)X^{k}\left(\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{k},

where
X
denotes the operator of multiplication with the variable, that is,
Xf(x)=xf(x)
, and
\frac{d}{dx}
is the derivative operator with respect to
x
.
The relation (
1
) has deep connection in the physical literature, which was rediscovered by Katriel
[
20
]
. Namely, it is connected with the creation operator
a
and the annihilation operator
a^{\dagger}
satisfying the commutation relation
aa^{\dagger}-a^{\dagger}a=1
in the Boson Fock space
[
1
,
27
]
.

Moreover, the Stirling numbers of the second kind
S(n,k)
appear also as coefficients in the expansion of

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(x-j),

and they satisfy the recurrence formula

(2)

S(n,k)=S(n-1,k-1)+kS(n-1,k).

The Bell numbers
B_{n}
are known by the following relation

B_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k),

and they satisfy the recursive formula

(3)

B_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}B_{k}.

In 2008, Spivey
[
45
]
generalized the relation (
3
) as follows

(4)

B_{n+m}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}k^{m-j}S(n,k)\binom{m}{j}B_{j}.

The above formula is known in the literature as “Spivey’s Bell number formula”.

Subsequently, in the same year, Katriel
[
21
]
derived a
q
-deformed formula of (
4
):

(5)

B_{n+m}(q)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n}[k]_{q}^{m-j}q^{jk}S_{q}(n,k)\binom{m}{j}B_{j}(q),

where

(6)

S_{q}(n,k)=q^{k-1}S(n-1,k-1)+[k]_{q}S_{q}(n-1,k),

are the
q
-Stirling numbers of the second kind with the initial value
S_{q}(0,0)=1
, and
[k]_{q}:=\frac{1-q^{k}}{1-q}
denotes the
q
-number
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. Consequently, the associated
q
-Bell numbers are given by

B_{n}(q)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{q}(n,k).

Recently, the present author
[
38
]
derived a
(p,q)
-deformed variant of Eq. (
4
). The resulting relation can be expressed as follows

(7)

B_{n+m}(p,q;1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{m}p^{\binom{k}{2}}[k]_{p,q}^{m-j}q^{jk}S_{p,q}(n,k)\binom{m}{j}B_{j}(p,q;p^{n+m-j}),

where

(8)

S_{p,q}(n,k)=p^{n-k}q^{k-1}S(n-1,k-1)+[k]_{p,q}S_{p,q}(n-1,k),

are the
(p,q)
-Stirling numbers of the second kind, with
S_{p,q}(0,0)=1
,
[k]_{p,q}:=\frac{p^{k}-q^{k}}{p-q}
denotes the
(p,q)
-number, which reduces to
[k]_{q}
for
p=1
, and
B_{n}(p,q;1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}p^{\binom{k}{2}}S_{p,q}(n,k)
are the corresponding
(p,q)
-Bell numbers in analogy to the classical and deformed cases.

Obviously, for
p=1
, Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
) reduce to (
5
) and (
6
), respectively. If further
q=1
, they reduce to (
4
) and (
2
), respectively.

It is worthwhile to mention that various generalizations of Stirling and Bell numbers have been extended in several ways by several authors, see for instance
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
11
,
15
,
16
,
25
,
26
,
33
,
34
,
37
,
44
]
. In particular, Mansour et al. considered in
[
33
]
the noncommutative operators
U
and
V
satisfying the commutation relation
UV=VU+hV^{s}
(for
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
h\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
), and introduced a new family of generalized Stirling numbers
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k)
by

(9)

\left(VU\right)^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k)V^{s(n-k)+k}U^{k}.

Moreover, they introduced in
[
34
]
the
q
-version of the above generalized Stirling numbers, which appear in the normal ordering of
(VU)^{n}
of the noncommutative operators
U
and
V
satisfy the
q
-commutation relation
UV=qVU+hV^{s}
. Namely

(10)

\left(VU\right)^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|q)V^{s(n-k)+k}U^{k},

and

(11)

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n+1,k|q)=q^{s(n+1-k)+k-1}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k-1|q)+h[s(n-k)+k]_{q}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|q)

for all
n\geq 0
,
k\geq 1
, with the initial values
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,0|q)=\delta_{n,0}
and
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(0,k|q)=\delta_{0,k}
for all
n,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
, with the corresponding generalized Bell numbers

(12)

\mathfrak{B}_{s;h}(n|q):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|q).

In 2015, Mansour and Schork
[
31
]
derived the following
q
-deformed generalization of Spivey’s relation (
4
)

(13)

\begin{split}\mathfrak{B}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{q}}(n+l|q^{m})&=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\bigg(\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q^{m-1},h_{m,j+(m-1)l}(q)}\\
&\times[j+(m-1)l]_{q}^{n-k}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{q}}(l,j|q^{m})q^{k(j+(m-1)l)}\mathfrak{B}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{q}}(k|q^{m})\bigg),\end{split}

where
h_{m,s}(q):=q^{s}\frac{[m-1]_{q}}{[s]_{q}}
and
\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{(q,h)}:=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}(1+h[i]_{q})
is the
(q,h)
-binomial coefficients
[
2
]
, with
\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{q}:=\frac{[n]_{q}!}{[k]_{q}![n-k]_{q}!}
denotes the
q
-Gauss binomial coefficients.

In the present work (Section
3
), we derive the
(p,q)
-analogues of the generalized Stirling numbers and Spivey’s relation above.

The Touchard polynomials (also known as exponential polynomials or Bell polynomials) have many applications and play important rule in areas of probability and statistics, see e.g.,
[
4
,
7
,
23
,
24
,
32
,
35
]
. In particular, the Touchard polynomial
T_{n}(\lambda)
is the
n
-th moment of a random variable
Y
which has Poisson distribution and expected value
\lambda
.

In an operational framework, the Touchard polynomials can be defined
[
4
,
5
,
9
,
39
,
40
]
by the Rodrigues-like formula:

(14)

T_{n}(x)=e^{-x}\left(X\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{n}e^{x}\quad\text{ for }n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

In 2012, Dattoli et al.
[
9
]
introduced a generalization of the Touchard polynomials (
14
) for higher order
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
as follows:

(15)

T_{n}^{(m)}(x)=e^{-x}\left(X^{m}\frac{d}{dx}\right)^{n}e^{x},\text{ for }n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

which reduce to the classical Touchard polynomials (
14
) for
m=1
. Moreover, they established the following recursive formula:

(16)

T_{n+1}^{(m)}(x)=x^{m}\left(1+\frac{d}{dx}\right)T_{n}^{(m)}(x).

Mansour et al. extended the generalized Touchard polynomials for negative integer order
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
in
[
34
]
, and furthermore, for arbitrary real order
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
in
[
30
]
. On the other hand, Mansour and Schork
[
31
]
introduced a
q
-deformation of the generalized Touchard polynomials for
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
by the following

(17)

T_{n;q}^{(m)}(x)=E_{q}(-x)(X^{m}D_{q})^{n}e_{q}(x),

where
D_{q}
denotes the analogue of the derivative operator
\frac{d}{dx}
(also called Jackson derivative), given by
D_{q}f(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{1-q}
,
E_{q}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{\binom{2}{n}}x^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}
and
e_{q}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}
are the
q
-exponential functions, and
[n]_{q}!=\prod_{j=1}^{n}[j]_{q}
is the
q
-factorial function of the
q
-number
[n]_{q}
.

The
q
-deformed generalized Touchard polynomials satisfy the recursion formula
[
31
]
:

(18)

T_{n;q}^{(m)}(x)=x^{m}\Big(1+E_{q}(-x)e_{q}(qx)D_{q}\Big)T_{n;q}^{(m)}(x),

which reduces to (
16
) for q=1.

Using the normal ordering (
1
)
and the fact that (see
[
31
]
)

(X^{m}D_{q})^{n}=X^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{q}}(n,k|q^{m})X^{k}D_{q}^{k},

one obtains directly from (
14
) and (
17
)
the following relationships between the Stirling numbers
S(n,k)
(resp.
q
-generalized Stirling numbers
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|q)
) and the Touchard polynomials
T_{n}(x)
(resp.
q
-generalized Touchard polynomials
T_{n;q}^{(m)}(x)
) :

(19)

T_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S(n,k)x^{k}=:B_{n}(x),

and

(20)

T_{n;q}^{(m)}(x)=x^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{q}}(n,k|q^{m})x^{k}=x^{n(m-1)}\mathfrak{B}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{q}|n;q^{m}}(x),

where
B_{n}(x)
and
\mathfrak{B}_{s;h|n;q}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|q)x^{k}
are the associated Bell polynomials and
q
-generalized Bell polynomials, respectively.

Noting that for
q=1
, the undeformed generalized Touchard polynomials
T_{n}^{(m)}(x)
satisfy

T_{n}^{(m)}(x)=x^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{m,1}(n,k)x^{k},

where,
S_{m,1}(n,k)=\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};m}(n,k|1)
are another version of the generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind considered for instance by Lang
[
26
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we review some basic tools and terminology about
(p,q)
-calculus which we will use in the subsequent sections. For more details about
(p,q)
-calculus and related aspects, we refer the reader to
[
8
,
13
,
14
,
43
]
.

For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
with
p\neq q
, the
(p,q)
-number (or twin-basic number)
[x]_{p,q}
is defined as

(21)

[x]_{p,q}:=\frac{p^{x}-q^{x}}{p-q}.

If
n
is non-negative integer, then

[n]_{p,q}:=\begin{cases}\frac{p^{n}-q^{n}}{p-q}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}p^{n-k}q^{k-1},&\text{if $n\in{\mathbb{N}}:=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}$;}\\
0,&\text{if $n=0$ .}\end{cases}

The
(p,q)
-factorial is given by

[n]_{p,q}!:=\begin{cases}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{n}[k]_{p,q},&\text{if $n\in{\mathbb{N}}$;}\\
1,&\text{if $n=0$ ,}\end{cases}

and the
(p,q)
-Gaussian binomial coefficient is defined by

\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{p,q}:=\begin{cases}\frac{[n]_{p,q}!}{[n-k]_{p,q}![k]_{p,q}!},&\text{if $0\leq k\leq n$;}\\
0,&\text{if $k>n\geq 0$ or $k<0$ .}\end{cases}

Moreover, we define the following operators:

(1)

The
(p,q)
-derivative operator
D_{p,q}
:

D_{p,q}f(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{f(px)-f(qx)}{x(p-q)},&\text{if $x\neq 0$;}\\
f^{\prime}(0),&\text{if $x=0$,}\end{cases}

for functions
f
which are differentiable at
x=0
.

(2)

The operator of multiplication
X
with the variable:

Xf(x)=xf(x)

(3)

The Fibonacci operator
N_{p}

[
36
]
:

N_{p}f(x)=f(px).

It is clear that
D_{p,q}x^{k}=[k]_{p,q}x^{k-1}
, and the
(p,q)
-Leibniz formula reads

(22)

D_{p,q}^{n}(f(x)g(x))=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
k\end{bmatrix}_{p,q}D_{p,q}^{n-k}(f)(p^{k}x)D_{p,q}^{k}(g)(q^{n-k}x),

where,
D_{p,q}^{n}f(x):=D_{p,q}(D_{p,q}^{n-1}f(x))
for
n=1,2,3,\ldots
, and
D_{p,q}^{0}
denotes the identity operator.

The operators
D_{p,q},X
and
N_{p}
satisfy the following
(p,q)
-commutation relations:

(23)

D_{p,q}X=qXD_{p,q}+N_{p},

(24)

N_{p}X=pXN_{p},

and

(25)

D_{p,q}N_{p}=pN_{p}D_{p,q}.

The
(p,q)
-deformed exponential functions
E_{p,q}(x)
and
e_{p,q}(x)
can be defined, respectively, as follows:

(26)

E_{p,q}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{\binom{n}{2}}}{[n]_{p,q}!}x^{n},

and

(27)

e_{p,q}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{p^{\binom{n}{2}}}{[n]_{p,q}!}x^{n},

which satisfy the basic identity

(28)

e_{p,q}(x)E_{p,q}(-x)=1.

The
n
th-
(p,q)
-derivative of the exponential function
e_{p,q}(x)
satisfies the differential equation
[
42
]
:

(29)

D_{p,q}^{n}e_{p,q}(x)=p^{\binom{n}{2}}e_{p,q}(p^{n}x).

The
q
-settings (which correspond to
p=1
) of the above notions are the ones considered in
[
12
,
17
,
19
,
31
,
38
]
.

We conclude this section by recalling the following proposition from
[
38
]
which will be useful in the subsequent computations.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
38
]
)
.

Let
n
be a nonnegative integer. Then the following relation holds true:

(30)

(XD_{p,q})^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{p,q}(n,k)X^{k}N_{p}^{n-k}D_{p,q}^{k},

where

(31)

S_{p,q}(n,k)=p^{n-k}q^{k-1}S_{p,q}(n-1,k-1)+[k]_{p,q}S_{p,q}(n-1,k),

are the
(p,q)
-Stirling numbers of the second kind, with the initial value
S_{p,q}(0,0)=1
, and consequently the numbers

(32)

B_{n}(p,q)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{p,q}(n,k),

can be considered as the associated
(p,q)
-Bell numbers.

3.
Main result

3.1.
The
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind

In this subsection, we introduce a
(p,q)
-analogue of the generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind which were introduced by Mansour et al. in
[
33
]
.

For
h\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, let
U,V
and
W_{p}
be noncommutative operators satisfy by the following
(p,q)
-commutation relations:

(33)

\displaystyle UV-qVU

\displaystyle=hV^{s}W_{p},

(34)

\displaystyle W_{p}V

\displaystyle=pVW_{p},

(35)

\displaystyle UW_{p}

\displaystyle=pW_{p}U,

(36)

\displaystyle W_{1}

\displaystyle=I\quad(\text{ identity operator}).

The case
p=1
and
s=0
, reduces to the
q
-commutation relation
UV-qVU=h
of the
q
-deformed Heisenberg-Weyl algebra. Furthermore,
q=1
yields the usual Heisenberg-Weyl algebra
([U,V]:=UV-VU=h)
. On the other hand, choosing
p=q=s=1
, one obtains the commutation relation
UV-VU=hV
of the two-dimensional non-Abelian Lie algebra
[
41
,
46
,
47
]
, and
p=q=1
and
s=2
yield the Jordan quantum plane
[
10
,
28
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we have the following identity:

(37)

UV^{k}-q^{k}V^{k}U=h[k]_{p,q}V^{s+k-1}W_{p},

where
U,V
and
W_{p}
are noncommutative operators satisfying Eqs. (
33
)-(
36
).

Proof.

The proof will be by induction on
k
. For
k=1
, Eq. (
37
) holds, since Eq. (
33
). Assume that Eq. (
37
) holds for
k
and let us prove it for
k+1
. Using induction hypothesis and Eqs. (
33
) and (
34
), we obtain

\displaystyle UV^{k+1}

\displaystyle=q^{k}V^{k}UV+h[k]_{p,q}V^{s+k-1}W_{p}V

\displaystyle=q^{k}V^{k}(qVU+hV^{s}W_{p})+h[k]_{p,q}V^{s+k-1}W_{p}V

\displaystyle=q^{k+1}V^{k+1}U+hq^{k}V^{k+s}W_{p}+h[k]_{p,q}V^{s+k-1}W_{p}V

\displaystyle=q^{k+1}V^{k+1}U+hq^{k}V^{k+s}W_{p}+hp[k]_{p,q}V^{s+k}W_{p}

\displaystyle=q^{k+1}V^{k+1}U+h(q^{k}+p[k]_{p,q})V^{s+k}W_{p}

\displaystyle=q^{k+1}V^{k+1}U+h[k+1]_{p,q}V^{s+k}W_{p},

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

One can reformulate Proposition
3.1
in terms of the operators
D_{p,q},X
and
N_{p}
such that
U=D_{p,q},V=X
and
W_{p}=N_{p}
(with
h=1,s=0
), as follows

(38)

D_{p,q}X^{k}=q^{k}X^{k}D_{p,q}+[k]_{p,q}X^{k-1}N_{p}.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
h\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
and
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
. For nonnegative integers
n,k
, we define the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)
, as normal ordering coefficients of
(VU)^{n}
by the following:

(39)

(VU)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)V^{s(n-k)+k}W_{p}^{n-k}U^{k},

with
U,V
and
W_{p}
satisfy Eqs. (
33
)-(
36
).

Moreover, the corresponding
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Bell polynomials and Bell numbers are defined, respectively, by

(40)

\mathfrak{B}_{s;h|n;p,q}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)x^{k},

and

(41)

\mathfrak{B}_{s;h}(n|p,q):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q).

Examples 3.4
.

From Definition
3.3
and using Proposition
3.1
with the commutation relation (
35
) we can determine directly the first few instances of the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Stirling numbers as follows:

n=1
:

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(1,1|p,q)=1
and
\mathfrak{B}_{s;h}(1|p,q)=1
.

n=2
:

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(2,1|p,q)=h,\quad\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(2,2|p,q)=q
, and consequently,
\mathfrak{B}_{s;h}(2|p,q)=h+q
.

n=3
:

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(3,1|p,q)=h^{2}[s+1]_{p,q},\hskip 8.5359pt\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(3,2|p,q)=hq\left([2]_{p,q}+pq^{s}\right),\hskip 8.5359pt\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(3,3|p,q)=q^{3}
,
and consequently, the associated Bell numbers

\mathfrak{B}_{s;h}(3|p,q)=q^{3}+hq([2]_{p,q}+q^{s}p)+h^{2}[s+1]_{p,q}.

Theorem 3.5
.

The
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Stirling numbers
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)
satisfy the following recursive formula:

(42)

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n+1,k|p,q)=p^{n-k+1}q^{s(n-k+1)+k-1}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k-1|p,q)+h[s(n-k)+k]_{p,q}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q),

for all
n\geq 0
and
k\geq 1
, with the initial conditions
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,0|p,q)=\delta_{n,0}
for all
n\geq 0
, and
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(0,k|p,q)=\delta_{0,k}
for all
k\geq 0
.

Proof.

By Definition
3.3
, one has

(43)

(VU)^{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n+1,k|p,q)V^{s(n-k+1)+k}W_{p}^{n-k+1}U^{k}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(VU)^{n+1}

\displaystyle=(VU)(VU)^{n}

\displaystyle=VU\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)V^{s(n-k)+k}W_{p}^{n-k}U^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)V(UV^{s(n-k)+k})W_{p}^{n-k}U^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)V\Big(q^{s(n-k)+k}V^{s(n-k)+k}U+h[s(n-k)+k]_{p,q}

\displaystyle\qquad\times V^{s(n-k)+k+s-1}W_{p}\Big)\times W_{p}^{n-k}U^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)\Big(q^{s(n-k)+k}V^{s(n-k)+k+1}UW_{p}^{n-k}U^{k}+h[s(n-k)+k]_{p,q}

\displaystyle\qquad\times V^{s(n-k)+k+s}W_{p}^{n-k+1}U^{k}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)\Big(p^{n-k}q^{s(n-k)+k}V^{s(n-k)+k+1}W_{p}^{n-k}U^{k+1}+h[s(n-k)+k]_{p,q}

\displaystyle\qquad\times V^{s(n-k+1)+k}W_{p}^{n-k+1}U^{k}\Big),

where in the fourth equality we use Eq. (
37
) and in the last equality we use Eq. (
35
).

Comparing the coefficients with Eq. (
43
) finishes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

The
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Stirling numbers
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)
satisfy the following identity:

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)=h^{n-k}\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(n,k|p,q).

Proof.

The proof will be by induction on
n
. According to Eq. (
39
), we have:

For
n=0
,
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(0,0|p,q)=0=\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(0,0|p,q)
.

For
n=1
,
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(1,0|p.q)=0=h\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(1,0|p,q)
and
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(1,1|p,q)=1=\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(1,1|p,q)
.

Now, using induction hypothesis and Eq. (
42
), one obtains

\displaystyle h^{n-k}\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(n,k|p,q)

\displaystyle=h^{n-k}\bigg(p^{n-k}q^{s(n-k)+k-1}\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(n-1,k-1|p,q)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+[s(n-1-k)+k]_{p,q}\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(n-1,k|p,q)\bigg)

\displaystyle=p^{n-k}q^{s(n-k)+k-1}h^{(n-1)-(k-1)}\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(n-1,k-1|p,q)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+h\left([s(n-1-k)+k]_{p,q}h^{n-1-k}\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(n-1,k|p,q)\right)

\displaystyle=p^{n-k}q^{s(n-k)+k-1}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n-1,k-1|p,q)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+h[s(n-1-k)+k]_{p,q}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n-1,k|p,q)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q).

∎

Remark 3.7
.

For
s=0
and
h=1
, the recurrence formula (
42
) becomes

\mathfrak{S}_{0;1}(n+1,k|p,q)=p^{n-k+1}q^{k-1}\mathfrak{S}_{0;1}(n,k-1|p,q)+[k]_{p,q}\mathfrak{S}_{0;1}(n,k|p,q),

which is the recurrence formula for the
(p,q)
-deformed Stirling numbers of the second kind
S_{p,q}(n,k)
, introduced recently by the present author in
[
38
]
. Therefore,

\mathfrak{S}_{0;1}(n,k|p,q)=S_{p,q}(n,k).

Moreover, for
p=1
we obtain the well known recursive formula for the
q
-Stirling numbers of the second kind (see
[
34
]
). In addition, for
p=q=1
, the recurrence formula (
42
) reduces to the one obtained by Mansour et al. for the generalized Stirling numbers of the second kind
[
29
,
33
]
.

3.2.
The
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Touchard polynomials

In this subsection we establish a
(p,q)
-version of the generalized Touchard polynomials introduced in
[
30
]
, and some related properties.

Definition 3.8
.

For
m\in{\mathbb{R}}

(n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
, we define the
n
th
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Touchard polynomials for order
m
by the formula

(44)

T^{(m)}_{n;p,q}(x)=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{n}e_{p,q}(x).

Remark 3.9
.

Obviously, if
p=1
, Eq. (
44
) reduces to the
q
-generalized Touchard polynomials of order
m
(
17
). If further
q=p=1
, we obtain the classical generalized Touchard polynomials (
14
).

Examples 3.10
.

For
m=0
:

T_{n,p;q}^{(0)}(x)=E_{p,q}(-x)D_{p,q}^{n}e_{p,q}(x)=p^{\binom{n}{2}}\text{ for all }n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Note that as
p\rightarrow 1
, it approaches
q
-deformed case, where
T_{n;q}^{0}(x)=1
, for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

For
m=1
:

\displaystyle T_{n;p,q}^{(1)}(x)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)(XD_{p,q})^{n}e_{p,q}(x)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)\sum_{k=0}^{n}S_{p,q}(n,k)X^{k}N_{p}^{n-k}D_{p,q}^{k}e_{p,q}(x)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)\sum_{k=0}^{n}p^{\binom{k}{2}}S_{p,q}(n,k)X^{k}N_{p}^{n-k}e_{p,q}(p^{k}x)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\tilde{S}_{p,k}(n,k)x^{k}e_{p,q}(p^{n}x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\tilde{S}_{p,q}(n,k)x^{k}

\displaystyle=\tilde{B}_{n}(p,q;x),

where in the second equality we use Proposition
2.1
, and
\tilde{S}_{p,q}(n,k)=p^{\binom{k}{2}}S_{p,q}(n,k)
are the other version of
(p,q)
-deformed Stirling numbers of the second kind introduced in
[
38
]
, and
\tilde{B}_{n}(p,q;x)
are the related
(p,q)
-Bell polynomials.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
. Then,

(45)

D_{p,q}X^{m}=q^{m}X^{m}D_{p,q}+[m]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}.

Moreover,

(46)

(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{n}=X^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n,k|p,q^{m})X^{k}N_{p}^{n-k}D_{p,q}^{k}.

Proof.

To prove Eq. (
45
), we use the
(p,q)
-Leibniz formula (
22
):

\displaystyle D_{p,q}(X^{m}f(x))

\displaystyle=D_{p,q}(x^{m}f(x))

\displaystyle=[m]_{p,q}x^{m-1}f(px)+(qx)^{m}D_{p,q}f(x)

\displaystyle=[m]_{p,q}x^{m-1}N_{p}f(x)+(qx)^{m}D_{p,q}f(x)

\displaystyle=[m]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}f(x)+q^{m}X^{m}D_{p,q}f(x).

On the other hand, by recalling
X^{m-1}=(X^{m})^{\frac{m-1}{m}}
, we can rewrite Eq. (
45
) as follows

D_{p,q}X^{m}=q^{m}X^{m}D_{p,q}+[m]_{p,q}(X^{m})^{\frac{m-1}{m}}N_{p}.

Hence, by considering
D_{p,q}=U,X^{m}=V,N_{p}=W_{p}
, we have the following commutation relation for variables
U,V
and
W_{p}

UV=q^{m}VU+[m]_{p,q}V^{\frac{m-1}{m}}W_{p}.

Choosing
s=\frac{m-1}{m},h=[m]_{p,q}
and using Eq. (
39
), we obtain

(VU)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n,k|p,q^{m})V^{\frac{m-1}{m}(n-k)+k}W_{p}^{n-k}U^{k}.

Therefore,

(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{n}=X^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n,k|p,q^{m})X^{k}N_{p}^{n-k}D_{p,q}^{k}.

∎

Remark 3.12
.

Since
\mathfrak{S}_{0;1}(n,k|p,q)=S_{p,q}(n,k)
, the formula (
46
) reduces to (
30
) for
m=1
.

Theorem 3.13
.

For
m\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
, the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Touchard polynomials of order
m
are given by the following formula:

(47)

T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(x)=x^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n,k|p,q^{m})x^{k}=x^{n(m-1)}\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}|n;p,q^{m}}(x),

where
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)=p^{\binom{k}{2}}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)
is one possible definition of the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Stirling numbers with the associated
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Bell polynomials
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{s;h|n;p,q}(x):=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)x^{k}
.

Proof.

According to Definition
3.8
and using Eqs. (
28
), (
29
) and (
46
), one obtains

\displaystyle T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(x)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{n}e_{p,q}(x)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)X^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n,k|p,q^{m})X^{k}N_{p}^{n-k}D_{p,q}^{k}e_{p,q}(x)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)X^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n,k|p,q^{m})X^{k}N_{p}^{n-k}p^{\binom{k}{2}}e_{p,q}(p^{k}x)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)X^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}p^{\binom{k}{2}}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n,k|p,q^{m})X^{k}e_{p,q}(p^{n}x)

\displaystyle=x^{n(m-1)}\sum_{k=0}^{n}p^{\binom{k}{2}}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n,k|p,q^{m})x^{k},

which shows the assertion.
∎

In the next proposition, we establish a
(p,q)
-analogue of the recurrence relations (
16
) and (
18
) satisfied by the classical Touchard polynomials and by the
q
-deformed generalized Touchard polynomials, respectively.

Proposition 3.14
.

The
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Touchard polynomials
T_{n;pq}^{(m)}(x)
satisfy the recursive formula:

(48)

T_{n+1;p,q}^{(m)}(x)=x^{m}\Big(N_{p}+E_{p,q}(-p^{n+1}x)e_{p,q}(p^{n}qx)D_{p,q}\Big)T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(x).

Proof.

According to Definition
3.8
, we have

T_{n+1;p,q}^{(m)}(x)=E_{p,q}(-p^{n+1}x)(X^{m}D_{p,q})(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{n}e_{p,q}(x).

On the other hand,

(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{n}e_{p,q}(x)=e_{p,q}(p^{n}x)T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(x).

Then,

\displaystyle T_{n+1;p,q}^{(m)}(x)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n+1}x)X^{m}\Big(D_{p,q}\Big(e_{p,q}(p^{n}x)T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(x)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=E_{p,q}(-p^{n+1}x)X^{m}\Big(e_{p,q}(p^{n+1}x)T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(px)+e_{p,q}(p^{n}qx)D_{p,q}T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(x)\Big)

\displaystyle=x^{m}T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(px)+x^{m}E_{p,q}(-p^{n+1}x)e_{p,q}(p^{n}qx)D_{p,q}T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(x)

\displaystyle=x^{m}\Big(N_{p}+E_{p,q}(-p^{n+1}x)e_{p,q}(p^{n}qx)D_{p,q}\Big)T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(x),

where in the second equality, we use the
(p,q)
-Leibniz rule (
22
).
∎

Remark 3.15
.

Clearly, for
p=1
, the recurrence
(
48
)
reduces to (
18
), and for
p=q=1
, it reduces to (
16
).

Now we are in the position to derive a
(p,q)
-analogue of the generalized Spivey’s formula.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, we have the following
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Spivey’s relation:

(49)

\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}|n+l;p,q^{m}}(1)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\Bigg(\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,j+(m-1)l}(p,q))}p^{(m-1)\left((n-k)(1+k)+kl\right)}\\
\times q^{k(j+l(m-1))}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})\Bigg)\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}|k;p,q^{m}}(p^{n+l-k})

Let us first prove the following two lemmas which will be the main ingredients for the proof of Theorem
3.16
.

Lemma 3.17
.

For any positive integer
n,s\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

(50)

\displaystyle(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{n}X^{s}

\displaystyle=X^{s}\left([s]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}+q^{s}(X^{m}D_{p,q})\right)^{n}

(51)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}[s]_{p,q}^{n-k}X^{s}(X^{m-1}N_{p})^{n-k}q^{ks}(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{k},

where
h_{m,s}(p,q):=\frac{q^{s}[m-1]_{p,q}}{[s]_{p,q}p^{m-1}}
.

Proof.

To prove Eq. (
50
), we use induction on
n
. For
n=1
and by virtue of Proposition
3.11
, Eq. (
45
), one obtains

\displaystyle(X^{m}D_{p,q})X^{s}

\displaystyle=X^{m}\left([s]_{p,q}X^{s-1}N_{p}+q^{s}(X^{s}D_{p,q})\right)

\displaystyle=X^{s}\left([s]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}+q^{s}(X^{m}D_{p,q})\right).

Moreover,

\displaystyle(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{n}X^{s}

\displaystyle=(X^{m}D_{p,q})(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{n-1}X^{s}

\displaystyle=(X^{m}D_{p,q})\bigg(X^{s}\Big([s]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}+q^{s}(X^{m}D_{p,q})\Big)^{n-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=X^{s}\big([s]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}+q^{s}(X^{m}D_{p,q})\big)\big([s]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}+q^{s}(X^{m}D_{p,q})\big)^{n-1}

\displaystyle=X^{s}\big([s]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}+q^{s}(X^{m}D_{p,q})\big)^{n}.

For the second Eq. (
51
) we can use the
(q,h)
-binomial formula due to Benaoum
[
2
]
. Let us recall the
(q,h)
-binomial formula from
[
2
]
; for two variables
R
and
S
which satisfy
RS-qSR=hS^{2}
, one has

(R+S)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(q,h)}S^{n-k}R^{k},

where
\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(q,h)}:=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{q}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+h[j]_{q})
is the
(q,h)
-binomial coefficients.

Consider
S=[s]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}
and
R=q^{s}(X^{m}D_{p,q})
, then

\displaystyle(RS)f(x)

\displaystyle=q^{s}[s]_{p,q}X^{m}D_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}f(x)

\displaystyle=q^{s}[s]_{p,q}X^{m}D_{p,q}X^{m-1}f(px)

\displaystyle=q^{s}[s]_{p,q}[m-1]_{p,q}X^{m}X^{m-2}f(p^{2}x)+q^{s}[s]_{p,q}q^{m-1}X^{m}X^{m-1}D_{p,q}f(px)

\displaystyle=q^{s}[s]_{p,q}[m-1]_{p,q}X^{2m-2}N_{p}^{2}f(x)+q^{s}[s]_{p,q}q^{m-1}pX^{m}X^{m-1}N_{p}D_{p,q}f(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{s}[m-1]_{p,q}}{[s]_{p,q}p^{m-1}}S^{2}f(x)+\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{m-1}(SR)f(x).

Therefore,

RS=h_{m,s}(p,q)S^{2}+\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)^{m-1}SR.

Let us introduce the
(p,q,h)
-binomial coefficients
\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p,q,h)}
as follows:

\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p,q,h)}:=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(\frac{q}{p},h)}=p^{k(k-1)}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{p,q}\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(1+hp^{1-n}[j]_{p,q}).

Clearly, for
p=1,\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(1,q,h)}=\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(q,h)}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\Big([s]_{p,q}X^{m-1}N_{p}+q^{s}(X^{m}D_{p,q})\Big)^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}[s]_{p,q}^{n-k}(X^{m-1}N_{p})^{n-k}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times q^{sk}(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{k},

and this proves Eq. (
51
).
∎

Lemma 3.18
.

The
(p,q)
-generalized Touchard polynomials of order
m\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
satisfy the recursive formula

(52)

T_{n+l,p,q}^{(m)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}q^{k(j+(m-1)l)}p^{(m-k)(m-1)}\Bigl[j+l(m-1)\Bigr]_{p,q}^{n-k}\\
\times\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})x^{j+(m-1)(l+n-k)}T_{k,p,q}^{(m)}(p^{n-k+l}x).

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\left(X^{m}D_{p,q}\right)^{n+l}

\displaystyle=\left(X^{m}D_{p,q}\right)^{n}\left(X^{m}D_{p,q}\right)^{l}

\displaystyle=\left(X^{m}D_{p,q}\right)^{n}X^{l(m-1)}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})X^{j}N_{p}^{l-j}D_{p,q}^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}[j+l(m-1)]_{p,q}q^{k(j+l(m-1))}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})X^{j+l(m-1)}(X^{m-1}N_{p})^{n-k}(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{k}N_{p}^{l-j}D_{p,q}^{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}p^{(n-k)(m-1)}[j+l(m-1)]_{p,q}q^{k(j+l(m-1))}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})X^{j+(l+n-k)(m-1)}N_{p}^{n-k}(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{k}N_{p}^{l-j}D_{p,q}^{j},

where in the second equality, we use Proposition
3.11
, in the third equality, we use Lemma
3.17
with
s=j+l(m-1)
, and in the last equality we use (
24
).

Applying the above identity to the
(p,q)
-exponential function
e_{p,q}(x)
, yields

\displaystyle(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{l+n}e_{p,q}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}p^{(n-k)(m-1)+\binom{j}{2}}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})

\displaystyle\quad\times[j+l(m-1)]_{p,q}q^{k(j+l(m-1))}X^{j+(l+n-k)(m-1)}N_{p}^{n-k}(X^{m}D_{p,q})^{k}e_{p,q}(p^{l}x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}p^{(n-k)(m-1)+\binom{j}{2}}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})

\displaystyle\quad\times[j+l(m-1)]_{p,q}q^{k(j+l(m-1))}X^{j+(l+n-k)(m-1)}N_{p}^{n-k}\frac{T_{k,p,q}^{(m)}(p^{l}x)}{E_{p,q}(-p^{k+l}x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}p^{(n-k)(m-1)+\binom{j}{2}}\mathfrak{S}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})

\displaystyle\quad\times[j+l(m-1)]_{p,q}q^{k(j+l(m-1))}x^{j+(l+n-k)(m-1)}\frac{T_{k,p,q}^{(m)}(p^{n-k+l}x)}{E_{p,q}(-p^{n+l}x)}.

Multiplying both sides of the above identity by
E_{p,q}(-p^{n+l}x)
gives

T_{n+l,p,q}^{(m)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}p^{(n-k)(m-1)}[j+l(m-1)]_{p,q}q^{k(j+l(m-1))}\\
\times\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})x^{j+(l+n-k)(m-1)}T_{k,p,q}^{(m)}(p^{n-k+l}x).

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.16
.

By virtue of Lemma
3.18
and Theorem
3.13
, one obtains the recurrence relation for
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Bell polynomials as follows

(53)

\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}|n+l;p,q^{m}}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{j=0}^{l}\begin{bmatrix}n\\
n-k\end{bmatrix}_{(p^{m-1},q^{m-1},h_{m,s}(p,q))}p^{(m-1)((n-k)(1+k)+kl)}\\
\times[j+l(m-1)]_{p,q}q^{k(j+l(m-1))}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(l,j|p,q^{m})x^{j}\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}|k;p,q^{m}}(p^{n+l-k}x).

Plugging
x=1
into this identity gives the assertion.
∎

Remark 3.19
.

If
m=1
, the recurrence relation (
49
) reduces to the
(p,q)
-deformed Spivey’s formula obtained by the present author
[
38
]
, and
if
p=1
, one obtains the recurrence relation for
q
-deformed generalized Spivey’s formula given by Mansour and Schork
[
31
]
.

In the next result we present an analogue of Dobiński’s formula for
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Bell polynomials
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{s;h|n;p,q}(x)
.

Proposition 3.20

(Dobiński’s formula)
.

The
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Bell polynomials
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}|n;p,q^{m}}(x)
satisfy the following relation:

(54)

\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}|n;p,q^{m}}(x)=\frac{1}{e_{p,q}(p^{n}x)}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}[k+j(m-1)]_{p,q}\right)}{[k]_{p,q}!}p^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k},

and consequently, the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Bell numbers
\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n|p,q^{m})
satisfy

(55)

\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}}(n|p,q^{m})=\frac{1}{e_{p,q}(p^{n})}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}[k+j(m-1)]_{p,q}\right)}{[k]_{p,q}!}p^{\binom{k}{2}}.

Proof.

Since
\left(X^{m}D_{p,q}\right)^{n}x^{k}=\left(\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}[k+j(m-1)]_{p,q}\right)x^{k+n(m-1)}
, we have

E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)\left(X^{m}D_{p,q}\right)^{n}e_{p,q}(x)=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}[k+j(m-1)]_{p,q}\right)}{[k]_{p,q}!}p^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k+n(m-1)}.

Then, by Definition
3.8

T_{n,p,q}^{(m)}(x)=E_{p,q}(-p^{n}x)\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}[k+j(m-1)]_{p,q}\right)}{[k]_{p,q}!}p^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k+n(m-1)}.

Hence, Theorem
3.13
yields

\tilde{\mathfrak{B}}_{\frac{m-1}{m};[m]_{p,q}|n;p,q^{m}}(x)=\frac{1}{e_{p,q}(p^{n}x)}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\left(\prod\limits_{j=0}^{n-1}[k+j(m-1)]_{p,q}\right)}{[k]_{p,q}!}p^{\binom{k}{2}}x^{k}.

Setting
x=1
, we arrive at (
55
).
∎

Remark 3.21
.

Note that, for

4.
Concluding remarks

Let us mention that for
m=1
, the
(p,q)
-deformed Touchard polynomials
T_{n;p,q}^{(1)}(x)
considered in this paper are different from those considered by Herscovici and Mansour in
[
15
]
, and by Kim et al. in
[
22
]
.

It would be very interesting to find new connections of the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Touchard polynomials
T_{n;p,q}^{(m)}(x)
to well known combinatorial numbers and polynomials. In particular, it was shown in
[
30
]
that the undeformed generalized Touchard polynomials of order
m=\frac{1}{2}
can be expressed in terms of Hermite polynomials

T_{n}^{(\frac{1}{2})}(x)=\left(\frac{i}{2}\right)^{n}H_{n}(-i\sqrt{x}).

On the other hand, Mansour and Schork
[
31
]
showed that the
q
-deformed generalized Touchard polynomials
T_{n;q}^{(m)}(x)
are connected to
q
-deformed Laguerre polynomials and
q
-deformed Bessel polynomials, respectively, for
m=2
and
m=-1
.

Therefore, it would be interesting to determine whether the above relations hold true for the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Touchard polynomials
T_{(m)}^{n;p,q}(x)
considered in this paper.

However, the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Stirling numbers
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n|p,q)
should be established deeply, as well as several special choices for the parameters
s
and
h
should be exhibited explicitly. These and other questions warrant further study.

Simplex based Steiner tree instances yield large integrality gaps for the bidirected cut relaxation
Robert Vicari

Abstract
The bidirected cut relaxation is the characteristic representative of the bidirected relaxations ( ℬ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℛ \operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}} ) which are a well-known class of equivalent LP \operatorname{LP} -relaxations for the NP \operatorname{NP} -hard Steiner Tree Problem in Graphs ( STP \operatorname{STP} ). Although no general approximation algorithm based on ℬ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℛ \operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}} with an approximation ratio better than 2 2 for STP \operatorname{STP} is known, it is mostly preferred in integer programming as an implementation of STP \operatorname{STP} , since there exists a formulation of compact size, which turns out to be very effective in practice. It is known that the integrality gap of ℬ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℛ \operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}} is at most 2 2 , and a long standing open question is whether the integrality gap is less than 2 2 or not. The best lower bound so far is 36 31 ≈ 1.161 \frac{36}{31}\approx 1.161 proven by Byrka et al. [ BGRS13 ] . Based on the work of Chakrabarty et al. [ CDV11 ] about embedding STP \operatorname{STP} instances into simplices by considering appropriate dual formulations, we improve on this result by constructing a new class of instances and showing that their integrality gaps tend at least to 6 5 = 1.2 \frac{6}{5}=1.2 . More precisely, we consider the class of equivalent LP-relaxations ℬ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℛ + \operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}}^{+} , that can be obtained by strengthening ℬ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℛ \operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}} by already known straightforward Steiner vertex degree constraints, and show that the worst case ratio regarding the optimum value between ℬ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℛ \operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}} and ℬ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℛ + \operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}}^{+} is at least 6 5 \frac{6}{5} . Since ℬ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℛ + \operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}}^{+} is a lower bound for the hypergraphic relaxations ( ℋ ​ 𝒴 ​ 𝒫 \operatorname{\mathcal{HYP}} ), another well-known class of equivalent LP-relaxations on which the current best ( ln ⁡ ( 4 ) + ε ) (\ln(4)+\varepsilon) -approximation algorithm for STP \operatorname{STP} by Byrka et al. [ BGRS13 ] is based, this worst case ratio also holds for ℬ ​ 𝒞 ​ ℛ \operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}} and ℋ ​ 𝒴 ​ 𝒫 \operatorname{\mathcal{HYP}} . Keywords Steiner Tree Problem ⋅ \ \cdot\ Bidirected Cut Relaxation ⋅ \ \cdot\ Integrality Gap Remarks This work is a revised version of the author’s master’s thesis [ Vic18 ] .

\mathcal{P}

\bm{{\operatorname{gap}}_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P}}}:=\sup_{I\in\mathcal{I}}\,\operatorname{gap}_{\mathcal{L},\mathcal{P}}(I)

Preliminaries

We will use the common notation and terms for graph theory and linear programming from
[
KV18
]
.
Further useful notation is as follows.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the index set
[n]:=\{1,\dotsc,n\}
.
Let
f:A\to\mathbb{R}
be a function, then for a subset
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
we define
f(A^{\prime}):=\sum_{a\in A^{\prime}}f(a)
.
Furthermore, we define the so called Iverson bracket
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket:\{\text{boolean expressions}\}\to\{0,1\}
by
\llbracket P\rrbracket=1
if and only
P
is true.

For shorter notation we introduce the convention
f(a,b)=f_{a}(b)
for a function
f:A\times B\to\mathbb{R}
as well as
f_{a}(B^{\prime})=\sum_{b\in B^{\prime}}f_{a}(b)
for
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
.
Furthermore, if we apply a directed edge
(v,w)
to a function
f
we omit the brackets, i.e.
f(v,w)=f((v,w))
.

For a given graph
G
we denote by
V(G)
the vertices and
E(G)
the edges.
For a vertex set
X\subseteq V(G)
of an undirected graph
G
we define
G[X]:=(X,\{e\in E(G):e\subseteq X\})
.
We will mainly be interested in undirected graphs
G=(V,E)
and their bidirected versions, i.e. we replace each undirected edge by the two edges directed in opposite directions.
Therefore, we define
E_{\leftrightarrow}:=\{(v,w),(w,v):\{v,w\}\in E\}
.
Moreover, we refine the notation of incident edges
\delta_{G}(v):=\{e\in E:v\in e\}
by
\delta_{G}^{+}(v):=\{(v,w)\in E_{\leftrightarrow}:\{v,w\}\in E\}
and
\delta_{G}^{-}(v):=\{(w,v)\in E_{\leftrightarrow}:\{v,w\}\in E\}
.
Analogously, we refine for a vertex set
X\subseteq V

\delta_{G}(X):=\{e\in E:|e\cap X|=1\}
by
\delta_{G}^{+}(X):=\{(v,w)\in E_{\leftrightarrow}:v\in X\land w\notin X\}
and
\delta_{G}^{-}(X):=\{(v,w)\in E_{\leftrightarrow}:v\notin X\land w\in X\}
.

Let
T
be a tree and
r
be a leaf of
T
.
We denote the arborescence rooted at
r
as
T^{r\to}
, i.e.
T^{r\to}
is obtained from
T
by directing all edges from
r
to the other leafs.
For
v,w\in V(T)
we denote by
T_{[v,w]}^{r\to}
the directed path from
v
to
w
in
T^{r\to}
given that this path exists.

An important notion, when talking about bidirected relaxations, are balance flows.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a directed graph,
u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
edge-usage bounds and
b:V\to\mathbb{R}
vertex-balances.
We call
f:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
a
balance flow
for
(G,u,b)
if and only if the following two conditions hold:

\displaystyle\forall e

\displaystyle\in E

\displaystyle f(e)

\displaystyle\leq u(e)

\displaystyle\forall v

\displaystyle\in V

\displaystyle f(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))

\displaystyle=b(v)

A trivially necessary condition for the existence of a balance-flow is
b(V)=0
.
This can easily be seen by summing up the edge flow values over all vertices.
However it is not sufficient as Gale’s well-known theorem shows.

Theorem 1.7

(
[
Gal57
]
)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a directed graph,
u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
edge-usage bounds and
b:V\to\mathbb{R}
vertex-balances such that
b(V)=0
.
Then there exists a balance-flow for
(G,u,b)
if and only if
u(\delta_{G}^{+}(X))\geq b(X)
for all
X\subseteq V
.

A proof, as well as further results on balance-flows, can also be found in
[
KV18
]
.

We will mainly be interested in balance-flows on bidirected graphs respecting the edge-usage bounds of the corresponding undirected graph.
Therefore we refine the notion of balance-flows for this special case.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph,
u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
edge-usage bounds and
b:V\to\mathbb{R}
vertex-balances.
We call
f:E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
a
bidirected balance flow
for
(G,u,b)
if and only if the following two conditions hold:

\displaystyle\forall\{v,w\}

\displaystyle\in E

\displaystyle f(v,w)+f(w,v)

\displaystyle\leq u(\{v,w\})

\displaystyle\forall v

\displaystyle\in V

\displaystyle f(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))

\displaystyle=b(v)

Gale’s theorem
can be adapted as follows.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be an undirected graph,
u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be edge-usage bounds and
b:V\to\mathbb{R}
be vertex-balances such that
b(V)=0
.
Then there exists a bidirected balance-flow for
(G,u,b)
if and only if
u(\delta_{G}(X))\geq b(X)
for all
X\subseteq V
.

Proof.

Define
u_{\leftrightarrow}:E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
by
u_{\leftrightarrow}(v,w)=u(\{v,w\})
.
Then every bidirected balance-flow for
(G,u,b)
is a balance flow for
((V,E_{\leftrightarrow}),u_{\leftrightarrow},b)
and therefore for every
X\subseteq V
we have
u(\delta_{G}(X))=u_{\leftrightarrow}(\delta_{G}^{+}(X))\geq b(X)
.

So assume
u(\delta_{G}(X))\geq b(X)
holds for every
X\subseteq V
.
By
Theorem

1.7
we know there exists a balance-flow
f^{\prime}:E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
for
((V,E_{\leftrightarrow}),u_{\leftrightarrow},b)
.
We define
f:E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
as
f(v,w)=\max\{f^{\prime}(v,w)-f^{\prime}(w,v),\ 0\}
.
Then
f
is a bidirected balance-flow for
(G,u,b)
since
f(v,w)+f(w,v)=|f^{\prime}(v,w)-f^{\prime}(w,v)|\leq\max\{f^{\prime}(v,w),f^{\prime}(w,v)\}\leq u(\{v,w\})
and
f(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))=\sum_{(v,w)\in\delta_{G}^{+}(v)}\left(\ f^{\prime}(v,w)-f^{\prime}(w,v)\ \right)=f^{\prime}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f^{\prime}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))=b(v)

∎

2
Bidirected relaxations with and without

Steiner vertex degree constraints

We are interested in two classes of equivalent LP-relaxations for
1
Introduction
.
The
bidirected relaxations
, which we denote by
\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}}
, with the classical representative of the
bidirected cut relaxation
and the
bidirected relaxations with Steiner vertex degree constraints
, which we denote by
\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}^{+}}
, consisting of the bidirected relaxations enhanced by certain degree constraints for each non-required vertex.

The first bidirected relaxation is stated in
[
Edm67
]
for the special case of spanning trees, i.e. all vertices are required, and shown to be exact.
In
[
Won84
]
this approach is generalized for Steiner trees resulting in the bidirected cut relaxation (
2.1
Bidirected Cut Relaxation
).
In
[
KM98
]
additional constraints, which the authors call
flow-balance constraints
, are added to
2.1
Bidirected Cut Relaxation
to strengthen the relaxation in practice resulting in the bidirected cut relaxation with Steiner vertex degree constraints (
\operatorname{BCR^{+}}
).

For this whole section we will fix a Steiner tree instance
(G=(V,E),R,c)
.
The idea of the bidirected relaxations is to consider in addition to the undirected edges of the Steiner tree
T
, in contrast to the
undirected cut relaxation
(
\operatorname{UCR}
) introduced in
[

, also the directed edges of the arborescences
T^{r\to}
rooted in the required vertices
r\in R
.
With this additional information one is able to define LPs stronger than

2.1
Bidirected Cut Relaxation

For this formulation we only interested in one arborescence rooted in a fixed required vertex
r\in R
.
We will later see that
\operatorname{opt}_{\operatorname{BCR}}(G,R,c,r)
is independent of the choice of
r
.
This was first shown in
[
GM93
]
.

\operatorname{BCR}

\min

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}c(e)\cdot u(e)

s.t.

u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,,\ f_{r}:E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\begin{aligned} f_{r}(v,w)+f_{r}(w,v)&\leq u(\{v,w\})&\{v,w\}&\in E\\
f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(X))&\geq\llbracket r\in X\land R\setminus X\neq\emptyset\rrbracket&X&\subseteq V\end{aligned}

It is easy to verify that every solution of
2.1
Bidirected Cut Relaxation
provides a solution of
\operatorname{UCR}
, more precisely
\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{BCR})\subseteq\operatorname{UCR}
.
In
[
GB93
]
it is essentially proven that
\operatorname{gap}_{\operatorname{UCR_{\leq k}},\operatorname{BCR_{\leq k}}}=\operatorname{gap}_{\operatorname{UCR_{\leq k}},\operatorname{STP}}=2\cdot(1-\frac{1}{k})
.
Therefore we have
\operatorname{gap}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}},\operatorname{STP}}\leq 2
and, in addition to the case
R=V
,
2.1
Bidirected Cut Relaxation
is also exact for
2
required vertices.
A Steiner tree
T
with respect to
G
and
R
can be associated with the following integral solution:

\displaystyle u(\{v,w\})

\displaystyle=\llbracket\{v,w\}\in E(T)\rrbracket

\displaystyle f_{r}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\llbracket(v,w)\in E(T^{r\to})\rrbracket

2.2
Multi Commodity Flow Relaxation

Gale’s theorem
suggests the first equivalent LP-relaxation, which is introduced in
[
Won84
]
.
The cut-constraints in
2.1
Bidirected Cut Relaxation
can be seen as balance-flow existence conditions for
|R|-1
different flows, namely a unit-flow for each
s\in R\setminus\{r\}
from
r
to
s
.
We obtain the following LP-relaxation commonly known as the
multi commodity flow relaxation
.

\operatorname{MCFR}

\min

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}c(e)\cdot u(e)

s.t.

u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,,\ f_{r}:E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,,\ g:R\setminus\{r\}\times E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\begin{aligned} f_{r}(v,w)+f_{r}(w,v)&\leq u(\{v,w\})&\{v,w\}\in E\\
g_{s}(e)&\leq f_{r}(e)&\quad\quad e\in E_{\leftrightarrow},s\in R\setminus\{r\}\\
g_{s}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-g_{s}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))&=\llbracket v=r\rrbracket-\llbracket v=s\rrbracket&\qquad v\in V,s\in R\setminus\{r\}\end{aligned}

2.2
Multi Commodity Flow Relaxation
also induces an integer program for
1
Introduction
.
A Steiner tree
T
with respect to
G
and
R
can be associated with the following integral solution:

\displaystyle u(\{v,w\})

\displaystyle=\llbracket\{v,w\}\in E(T)\rrbracket

\displaystyle f_{r}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\llbracket(v,w)\in E(T^{r\to})\rrbracket

\displaystyle g_{s}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\llbracket(v,w)\in E(T_{[r,s]}^{r\to})\rrbracket

Note that we obtained an extended formulation for
\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{BCR})
of compact size, namely the number of variables and constraints is in
\mathcal{O}(|R|\cdot|E|)
.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Won84
]
)
.

\operatorname{proj}_{u,f_{r}}(\operatorname{MCFR})=\operatorname{proj}_{u,f_{r}}(\operatorname{BCR})

Proof.

Let
(u,f_{r},g)
be a solution of
2.2
Multi Commodity Flow Relaxation
.
For
s\in R\setminus\{r\}
define vertex-balances
b_{s}:V\to\mathbb{R}
as
b_{s}(v)=\llbracket v=r\rrbracket-\llbracket v=s\rrbracket
, which satisfy
b_{s}(V)=0
.
Then
g_{s}
is a balance flow for
((V,E_{\leftrightarrow}),f_{r},b_{s})
and therefore by
Theorem

1.7
we have
f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(X))\geq\llbracket r\in X\land s\notin X\rrbracket
.
Hence overall we have
f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(X))\geq\llbracket r\in X\land R\setminus X\neq\emptyset\rrbracket
.
On the other hand
Theorem

1.7
shows the existence of
g
if
(u,f_{r})
is a solution of
2.1
Bidirected Cut Relaxation
.
∎

2.3
Multi Balance Flow Relaxation

Instead of only considering one arborescence rooted at a fixed required vertex
r\in R
, we now consider the arborescences rooted at all required vertices.
By connecting these
|R|
directed trees at each vertex through degree constraints, we obtain another equivalent formulation which does not depend on a previously chosen root.
It is based on a similar one stated in
[
CDV11
]
.
We call it the
multi balance flow relaxation
.

\operatorname{MBFR}

\min

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}c(e)\cdot u(e)

s.t.

u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,,\ b:V\to\mathbb{R}\,,\ f:R\times E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\begin{aligned} f_{r}(v,w)+f_{r}(w,v)&\leq u(\{v,w\})&\{v,w\}\in E,r\in R\\
f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))&=b(v)+2\cdot\llbracket v=r\rrbracket&\qquad v\in V,r\in R\end{aligned}

2.3
Multi Balance Flow Relaxation
also induces an integer program for
1
Introduction
.
A Steiner tree
T
with respect to
G
and
R
can be associated with the following integral solution:

\displaystyle u(\{v,w\})

\displaystyle=\llbracket\{v,w\}\in E(T)\rrbracket

\displaystyle b(v)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}|\delta_{T}(v)|-2&\text{if }v\in V(T)\\
0&\text{if }v\in V(G)\setminus V(T)\end{cases}

\displaystyle f_{r}(v,w)

\displaystyle=\llbracket(v,w)\in E(T^{r\to})\rrbracket

Hence, the
b
values of an integral solution encode the degrees within the Steiner tree
T
.
The variables
f_{r}
encode the corresponding arborescence rooted in
s
.

The new variables can roughly be interpreted as global balance values for each of the bidirected flows
f_{r}
.
The only difference occurs at each required vertex
r\in R
.
More precisely, if we define
b_{r}:V\to\mathbb{R}
by
b_{r}(v)=b(v)+2\cdot\llbracket v=r\rrbracket
, then
f_{r}
is a bidirected balance flow for
(G,u,b_{r})
.
Therefore we have the necessary condition
b(V)=-2
.

As already mentioned,
2.3
Multi Balance Flow Relaxation
is a slightly different version of a formulation in
[
CDV11
]
.
The authors proved equivalence to
2.1
Bidirected Cut Relaxation
directly.
Our proof of
Theorem

2.2
is an adapted version of theirs.

Theorem 2.2
.

\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{MBFR})=\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{MCFR})

Proof.

We will prove
\supseteq
and
\subseteq
separately by translating solutions into each other.
In both directions
u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
will remain the same.
Hence, if we can construct a solution with
u
given from the respective other problem, we have shown the statement.

Claim

\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{MBFR})\supseteq\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{MCFR})
:

Let
(u,f_{r},g)
be a solution of
2.2
Multi Commodity Flow Relaxation
.
We need to define a solution
(u,b^{\prime},f^{\prime})
of
2.3
Multi Balance Flow Relaxation
.

We define
b^{\prime}
and
f^{\prime}
as follows:

\displaystyle b^{\prime}(v)

\displaystyle:=f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))-2\cdot\llbracket v=r\rrbracket

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{r}(v,w)

\displaystyle:=f_{r}(v,w)

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{s}(v,w)

\displaystyle:=f_{r}(v,w)-g_{s}(v,w)+g_{s}(w,v)

\displaystyle s\in R\setminus\{r\}

By definition these values are non-negative and fulfil the edge-usage bounds:

f^{\prime}_{s}(v,w)+f^{\prime}_{s}(w,v)\leq f_{r}(v,w)+f_{r}(w,v)\leq u(\{v,w\})\quad s\in R

Again by definition the balance constraints are fulfilled for
r
.

For
s\in R\setminus\{r\}
we can easily check that they are also fulfilled:

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{s}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f^{\prime}_{s}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))

\displaystyle=f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))-2\cdot(\ g_{s}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-g_{s}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))\ )

\displaystyle=b^{\prime}(v)+2\cdot\llbracket v=r\rrbracket-2\cdot(\ \llbracket v=r\rrbracket-\llbracket v=s\rrbracket\ )

\displaystyle=b^{\prime}(v)+2\cdot\llbracket v=s\rrbracket

Claim

\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{MBFR})\subseteq\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{MCFR})
:

Let
(u,b,f)
be a solution of
2.3
Multi Balance Flow Relaxation
.
We need to define a solution
(u,f^{\prime}_{r},g^{\prime})
of
2.2
Multi Commodity Flow Relaxation
.

We define
f^{\prime}_{r}
and
g^{\prime}
as follows:

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{r}(v,w)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\ f_{r}(v,w)-f_{r}(w,v)+u(\{v,w\})\ \right)

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{s}(v,w)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\max\{f_{r}(v,w)-f_{r}(w,v)+f_{s}(w,v)-f_{s}(v,w),\ 0\}

\displaystyle\quad s\in R\setminus\{r\}

We immediately see that
f^{\prime}_{r}(v,w)+f^{\prime}_{r}(w,v)=u(\{v,w\})
.
The flow bound constraints are fulfilled as well since
|f_{s}(v,w)-f_{s}(w,v)|\leq\max\{f_{s}(v,w),f_{s}(w,v)\}\leq u(\{v,w\})
:

g^{\prime}_{s}(v,w)\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\max\{f_{r}(v,w)-f_{r}(w,v)+u(\{v,w\}),\ 0\}=f^{\prime}_{r}(v,w)

For
s\in R\setminus\{r\}
the flow constraints are also fulfilled:

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{s}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-g^{\prime}_{s}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(v,w)\in\delta_{G}^{+}(v)}(\ g^{\prime}_{s}(v,w)-g^{\prime}_{s}(w,v)\ )

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{(v,w)\in\delta_{G}^{+}(v)}(\ f_{r}(v,w)-f_{r}(w,v)+f_{s}(w,v)-f_{s}(v,w)\ )

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\left(f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))\right)-\left(f_{s}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f_{s}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(\ (\ b(v)+2\cdot\llbracket v=r\rrbracket\ )-(\ b(v)+2\cdot\llbracket v=s\rrbracket\ )\ )

\displaystyle=\llbracket v=r\rrbracket-\llbracket v=s\rrbracket\qed

2.5
Subtour Elimination Relaxation

For the next equivalent LP-relaxation we redefine the meaning of the auxiliary vertex-variables.
In an integral solution of
2.4
Multi Balance Cut Relaxation
associated to a Steiner tree
T
we have for
v\in V(T)
that
b(v)=|\delta_{T}(v)|-2=u(\delta_{G}(v))-2
.
Therefore the value
\frac{1}{2}\cdot(u(\delta_{G}(v))-b(v))\in\{0,1\}
indicates whether the vertex
v
is contained in
T
or not.
Hence by introducing the variables
y:V\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, which enforce these values, we obtain the following LP-relaxation, which we call
subtour elimination relaxation
.

\operatorname{STER}

\min

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}c(e)\cdot u(e)

s.t.

u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\,,\ y:V\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\begin{aligned} u(E)&=y(V)-1\\
u(E(G[X]))&\leq y(X)-\llbracket R\cap X\neq\emptyset\rrbracket&\qquad X\subseteq V\end{aligned}

2.5
Subtour Elimination Relaxation
is a reduced formulation of ‘
P^{\prime}_{xy}
’ in
[
GM93
]
.
The additional constraints are presented in
Section

2.6
.
The constraints
u(E(G[X]))\leq y(X)-\llbracket R\cap X\neq\emptyset\rrbracket
forbid cycles involving required vertices within the graph associated to an integral solution and therefore they eliminate subtours.
A Steiner tree
T
with respect to
G
and
R
can be associated with the following integral solution:

\displaystyle u(\{v,w\})

\displaystyle=\llbracket\{v,w\}\in E(T)\rrbracket

\displaystyle y(v)

\displaystyle=\llbracket v\in V(T)\rrbracket

Theorem 2.5
.

\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{STER})=\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{MBCR})

Proof.

If we assume
b(v)=u(\delta_{G}(v))-2\cdot y(v)
, we have the following two equivalences, which prove the claimed result:

\displaystyle b(V)=-2

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}u(\delta_{G}(v))-2\cdot y(V)=-2

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle 2\cdot u(E)-2\cdot y(V)=-2

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow

\displaystyle u(E)=y(V)-1

\displaystyle u(\delta_{G}(X))\geq b(X)+2\cdot\llbracket R\cap X\neq\emptyset\rrbracket

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle u(\delta_{G}(X))\geq\sum_{v\in X}u(\delta_{G}(v))-2\cdot y(X)+2\cdot\llbracket R\cap X\neq\emptyset\rrbracket

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle u(\delta_{G}(X))\geq u(\delta_{G}(X))+2\cdot u(E(G[X]))-2\cdot(\ y(X)-\llbracket R\cap X\neq\emptyset\rrbracket\ )

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad

\displaystyle u(E(G[X]))\leq y(X)-\llbracket R\cap X\neq\emptyset\rrbracket\qed

2.6
Valid constraints

Using
2.4
Multi Balance Cut Relaxation
it is very easy to verify that
\operatorname{proj}_{u}(\operatorname{BCR})
fulfills the well-known
Steiner partition
condition
[

]
proven in
[
Goe94
]
:
Let
S_{1}\dot{\cup}\dotsc\dot{\cup}S_{q}=V
be a partition of the vertices and define
E_{S}:=\{\{v,w\}\in E:\exists i,j\in[q]\ i\neq j\land v\in S_{i}\land w\in S_{j}\}
, i.e.

contains all edges which connect different partition classes.
Then for a Steiner tree
T
we have the necessary condition that
|E(T)\cap E_{S}|\geq|\{i\in[q]:S_{i}\cap R\neq\emptyset\}|-1
.
For a solution

of

we can compute:

u(E_{S})=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{i\in[q]}u(\delta_{G}(S_{i}))\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{i\in[q]}(\ 2\cdot\llbracket S_{i}\cap R\neq\emptyset\rrbracket+b(S_{i})\ )=|\{i\in[q]:S_{i}\cap R\neq\emptyset\}|-1

As already mentioned in
Section

[

]
the formulations are compared with respect to

The first group of equivalent valid constraints is as follows:

\displaystyle\leq\llbracket v\neq r\rrbracket

\displaystyle v\in V

\displaystyle u(\delta_{G}(v))

\displaystyle\leq b(v)+2

\displaystyle v\in V

\displaystyle y(v)

\displaystyle\leq 1

\displaystyle v\in V

The second group is:

\displaystyle\leq f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{-}(X))

\displaystyle v\in X\subseteq V\setminus R

A group of equivalent constraints which are not valid in general for optimum solutions for

Theorem 2.7
.

Let

Proof.

We have to show

Let

be an optimum solution of
\operatorname{MCFR^{+}}
with respect to

We can assume w.l.o.g. that for any edge

This does not increase the objective value since

As

We define

We see easily:

With that and using

\displaystyle g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))-g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left(\ \llbracket v=s_{i}\rrbracket-\llbracket v=r\rrbracket\ \right)+f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))\geq 0

The idea now is to extract a Steiner tree.
For
3

X_{f}:=\left\{v\in V\setminus\{r\}\ :\ g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))>g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))\right\}

contains possible branching vertices for a Steiner tree.

Assume

.
Then there exists

.
Denote this path by

.
Furthermore,

for
i\in\{1,2\}
.

Assume
X_{f}=\emptyset
, i.e.
g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))=g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))
for
v\in V\setminus\{r\}
, and let
v_{*}=r
.
Then
g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v_{*}))<g_{s_{i}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v_{*}))
for

g_{s_{i}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(s_{i}))=g_{s_{i}}(\delta_{G}^{-}(s_{i}))
.

Hence, in both cases, there exists
e_{s_{i}}\in\delta_{G}^{+}(v_{*})

for

.
So there is a path from
v_{*}
to
s_{i}
in

, as

Denote these directed paths by

We obtain a directed Steiner tree

We define further:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{v_{*}}

\displaystyle:=g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v_{*}))-g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v_{*}))

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{[r,v_{*}]}

\displaystyle:=\min_{e\in E(P_{[r,v_{*}]})}g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(e)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{[v_{*},s_{i}]}

\displaystyle:=\min_{e\in E(P_{[v_{*},s_{i}]})}(\ g_{s_{i}}(e)-g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(e)\ )

\varepsilon:=\begin{cases}\min\left\{\varepsilon_{v_{*}}\ ,\ \varepsilon_{[r,v_{*}]}\ ,\ \min_{i\in[2]}\varepsilon_{[v_{*},s_{i}]}\right\}&\mbox{if }v_{*}\neq r\\
\min_{i\in[2]}\varepsilon_{[v_{*},s_{i}]}&\mbox{if }v_{*}=r\end{cases}

By definition

.
We define another solution

of

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{s_{i}}(e)

\displaystyle:=\frac{g_{s_{i}}(e)-\varepsilon\cdot\llbracket e\in E(T_{[r,s_{i}]}^{r})\rrbracket}{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{r}(e)

\displaystyle:=\frac{f_{r}(e)-\varepsilon\cdot\llbracket e\in E(T^{r})\rrbracket}{1-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle u^{\prime}(\{v,w\})

\displaystyle:=f^{\prime}_{r}(v,w)+f^{\prime}_{r}(w,v)

.
It is easy to see that

\varepsilon_{[v_{*},s_{i}]}

is also non-negative.
Hence, it suffices to prove the following three statements:

\displaystyle\text{a)}

\displaystyle\forall e\in E_{\leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\max_{s\in R\setminus\{r\}}g^{\prime}_{s}(e)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}_{r}(e)

\displaystyle\text{b)}

\displaystyle\forall v\in V

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{s_{i}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-g^{\prime}_{s_{i}}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))

\displaystyle=\llbracket v=r\rrbracket-\llbracket v=s_{i}\rrbracket

\displaystyle\text{c)}

\displaystyle\forall v\in V\setminus R

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))

\displaystyle\geq f^{\prime}_{r}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))

a)
By the definition of
f^{\prime}
it suffices to show

.
We assume w.l.o.g.
i=1

Only the case

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\cdot(f^{\prime}_{r}(e)-g^{\prime}_{s_{1}}(e))

\displaystyle=g_{s_{2}}(e)-g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(e)-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon_{[v_{*},s_{2}]}-\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq 0

b)

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\left(g^{\prime}_{s_{i}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-g^{\prime}_{s_{i}}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))\right)

\displaystyle=g_{s_{i}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-g_{s_{i}}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))-\varepsilon\cdot\llbracket v=r\rrbracket+\varepsilon\cdot\llbracket v=s_{i}\rrbracket

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\cdot\left(f^{\prime}_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f^{\prime}_{r}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))\right)

\displaystyle=f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-f_{r}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))-\varepsilon\cdot\llbracket v=r\rrbracket+\varepsilon\cdot\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}\llbracket v=s_{i}\rrbracket-\varepsilon\cdot\llbracket v=v_{*}\rrbracket

\displaystyle=g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{-}(v))-g_{s_{1},s_{2}}(\delta_{G}^{+}(v))-\varepsilon\cdot\llbracket v=v_{*}\rrbracket

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon_{v_{*}}\cdot\llbracket v=v_{*}\rrbracket-\varepsilon\cdot\llbracket v=v_{*}\rrbracket

3.1
Simplex Embedding of Steiner tree instances

Chakrabarty et al.
[
CDV11
]
propose two lower bounds on the optimal cost of a Steiner tree for a given instance
(G,R,c)
.
The idea is to embed the vertices
V(G)
into a simplex of a certain size while respecting the
\operatorname{L}^{1}
-distance between neighbouring vertices given by the respective edge cost.
The lower bound is then given by spreading the required vertices in their assigned dimensions as far as possible and summing over their respective coordinates.
For a precise definition we need the following notion of simplices.

Definition 3.1
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{K}\in\{\mathbb{R},\mathbb{Z}\}
and
s\in\mathbb{K}_{\geq 0}
we define:

\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{K}}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{K}_{\geq 0}^{d+1}\ :\ \sum_{i=1}^{d+1}x_{i}=s\right\}

We call
\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{K}}
the
\bm{d}
-dimensional continuous/discrete simplex of size
\bm{s}
.

For the rest of
Section

3
we denote by
e_{i}
the
i
-th unit vector in the respective space.

\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{R}}
is the convex hull of the points
\{s\cdot e_{i}\ :\ i\in[d+1]\}
.

For a Steiner tree instance
(G=(V,E),R,c)
let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
|R|=d+1
and assume
R=\{r_{1},\dotsc,r_{d+1}\}
.
In this way each required vertex is made to correspond with a dimension of
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
.
Then we obtain the following two maximization problems from
[
CDV11
]
.
Recall that
\|x\|_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|
denotes the
\operatorname{L}^{1}
-distance for a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

\operatorname{SE}

\max

\displaystyle 2\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}y(r_{i})_{i}-s\right)

s.t.

y:V\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d+1}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\begin{aligned} \|y(v)-y(w)\|_{1}&\leq c(\{v,w\})&\quad\{v,w\}&\in E\\[3.0pt]
y(v)&\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{R}}&\quad v&\in V\end{aligned}

\operatorname{SE^{+}}

\max

\displaystyle 2\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d+1}y(r_{i})_{i}-s\right)

s.t.

y:V\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{d+1}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}

\begin{aligned} \|y(v)-y(w)\|_{1}&\leq c(\{v,w\})&\quad\{v,w\}&\in E\\[3.0pt]
y(r)&\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{R}}&\quad r&\in R\\
y(v)&\in\bigcup_{s\leq s^{\prime}}\triangle^{\mathbb{R}}_{d,s^{\prime}}&\quad v&\in V\setminus R\end{aligned}

Theorem 3.2

(
[
CDV11
]
)
.

Let
(G,R,c)
be a Steiner tree instance, then

\displaystyle\operatorname{opt}_{\operatorname{SE}}(G,R,c)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{opt}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}}}(G,R,c)

\displaystyle\operatorname{opt}_{\operatorname{SE^{+}}}(G,R,c)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{opt}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}^{+}}}(G,R,c)

Initially Chakrabarty et al.
[
CDV11
]
prove combinatorially that
3.1
Simplex Embedding of Steiner tree instances
and
3.1
Simplex Embedding of Steiner tree instances
is a lower bound for
1
Introduction
.
But they also show, that these two maximization problems are actually dual formulations of a slightly different but equivalent version of
2.3
Multi Balance Flow Relaxation
.

The constraints
\|y(v)-y(w)\|_{1}\leq c(\{v,w\})
can be implemented using auxiliary variables
z_{i}(\{v,w\})
for each dimension
i\in[d+1]
as we want to maximize
y(r_{i})_{i}
, namely
|y(v)_{i}-y(w)_{i}|\leq z_{i}(\{v,w\})
and
\sum_{i\in[d+1]}z_{i}(\{v,w\})\leq c(\{v,w\})
.
These dual variables correspond to the primal constraints
f_{r}(v,w)+f_{r}(w,v)\leq u(\{v,w\})
.

In
[
CDV11
]

3.1
Simplex Embedding of Steiner tree instances
is called
simplex embedding
since
y
is actually a function which maps to
\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{R}}
.
For
3.1
Simplex Embedding of Steiner tree instances
we allow Steiner vertices to be mapped to
\bigcup_{s\leq s^{\prime}}\triangle_{d,s^{\prime}}^{\mathbb{R}}
, we adopt the term from
[
CDV11
]
and say the Steiner vertices are mapped
above the simplex

\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{R}}
.
With this geometrical relation the authors construct a
\frac{4}{3}
-approximation algorithm for quasi-bipartite instances and also upper bound the integrality gaps for these instances by the same value.
Note that the current best factor of an approximation algorithm for these instances as well as the upper bound on their integrality gaps is
\frac{73}{60}\approx 1.217

[
GORZ12
]
.

Since our main motivation is to construct instances
(G,R,c)
such that
\operatorname{gap}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}},\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}^{+}}}(G,R,c)
is large, we will investigate these dual formulations further.

The optimization goal in
3.1
Simplex Embedding of Steiner tree instances
and
3.1
Simplex Embedding of Steiner tree instances
is to spread the required vertices in their corresponding dimensions as far as the respective graph structure with the given edge-costs admits.
Therefore, we want to construct instances which for given
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
are easy to embed above
\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{R}}
, but need a much more compact embedding into the simplex itself, i.e. it is much more complicated to spread the
d+1
required vertices in their dimensions.

3.2
Simplex based Steiner tree instances

For given
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
our idea is to create a graph on the vertex set
\bigcup_{s\leq s^{\prime}}\triangle^{\mathbb{R}}_{d,s^{\prime}}
with
\{s\cdot e_{i}\ :\ i\in[d+1]\}
as required vertices and the
\operatorname{L}^{1}
-distances between neighbouring vertices as edge-costs.
One could consider the infinite complete graph on the vertex set
\bigcup_{s\leq s^{\prime}}\triangle^{\mathbb{R}}_{d,s^{\prime}}
, but there might be many vertices and edges which do not contribute to a more compact embedding into
\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

Therefore we start with a discretization of
\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{R}}
, namely
\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, and try to figure out which additional points in
\bigcup_{s\leq s^{\prime}}\triangle^{\mathbb{R}}_{d,s^{\prime}}
with appropriate edges contribute to a more compact embedding.
The following observation suggests a possible construction:

A useful decomposition of
\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is given by the notion of support.
We define for a point
v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}
:

\displaystyle\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)

\displaystyle:=\{i\in[d+1]:v_{i}>0\}

\displaystyle\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)

\displaystyle:=|\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)|-1

\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}
contains a point on level
l
if and only if
l+1\leq s
.
Let
d,s\in\mathbb{N}

k\in[d+1]
.
Let
s,\delta\in\mathbb{N}

2\cdot\delta\leq s
.

Let
R_{i}:=\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s+1}^{\mathbb{Z}}:v_{i}=s-\delta+1\right\}

(

Let

be given by

\displaystyle V_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=V_{d,s,\delta}\cup\bigcup_{i\in[d+1]}R_{i}

\displaystyle E_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=\left\{\{v,w\}:v,w\in V_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}\land\|v-w\|_{1}=1\right\}

Let
u^{\prime}:[d]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be given by

u^{\prime}(l)=\frac{1}{\binom{d+1}{2}\cdot(2\cdot s-3\cdot\delta+1)}\cdot\begin{cases}\hfil(d+1)\cdot s-d\cdot(3\cdot\delta-2)-1&\text{if }l=1\\
\hfil 3&\text{if }l=2\\
\hfil 0&\text{if }l\geq 3\\
\end{cases}

Let
b_{k}:V_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}
be given as follows.
Let
v\in V_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}
and
l:=\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)
.

If
v\in V_{d,s,\delta}
, i.e.
\max_{i\in[d+1]}v_{i}\leq s-\delta
:

b_{k}(v)=\begin{cases}+(l-1)\cdot u^{\prime}(l)+(d-l)\cdot u^{\prime}(l+1)&\text{if }v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}\\
-(l-1)\cdot u^{\prime}(l)&\text{if }v\in\triangle_{d,s+1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\end{cases}

If
v\in R_{i}
:

b_{k}(v)=(\llbracket i=k\rrbracket-\llbracket i\neq k\rrbracket)\cdot u^{\prime}(l)

If
d=2
or
d\geq 3\land s=3\cdot\delta-2
, then there exists for
\operatorname{SG}^{\prime}_{d,s,\delta}
a bidirected balance flow
f_{k}:E_{\leftrightarrow}(\operatorname{SG}^{\prime}_{d,s,\delta})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with respect to
b_{k}
and
u:E_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
defined by
u(e)=u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(e))
.

Proof.

Note that we only have to deal with vertices on level
1
and
2
, as
b_{k}(v)=0
for
v\in V_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)\geq 3
.
Also the edges on levels higher than
2
have value
0
under
f_{k}
induced by
u
.
Moreover, edges that we do not specify
f_{k}
for in the following have value
0
under
f_{k}
as well.

We will first define
f_{k}
for edges incident to vertices which contain
k
in their support.
After this we proceed with the definition of
f_{k}
for edges between vertices without
k
in their support.

So we now consider the edges incident to the edge-level representatives
v\in\triangle_{d,s+1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}
with
k\in\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)
.
For
i\in\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)
with
v-e_{i}\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}
we define:

\displaystyle f_{k}(v-e_{i},v)

\displaystyle:=u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))\cdot\llbracket i\neq k\rrbracket

\displaystyle f_{k}(v,v-e_{i})

\displaystyle:=u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))\cdot\llbracket i=k\rrbracket

For an illustration see
Figure

We easily see that
f_{k}(v-e_{i},v)+f_{k}(v,v-e_{i})\leq u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))=u(\{v,v-e_{i}\})
.

Furthermore, the balance conditions hold:
Let
v\in V_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}
be such that
k\in\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)
.
It is clear for
v\in R_{i}
as
\{v,v-e_{i}\}\in E_{d,s,\delta}^{\prime}
is the only incident edge and
b_{k}(v)=(\llbracket i=k\rrbracket-\llbracket i\neq k\rrbracket)\cdot u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))
.
So assume
v\in V_{d,s,\delta}
, i.e.
\max_{i\in[d+1]}v_{i}\leq s-\delta
:

If
v\in\triangle_{d,s+1}^{\mathbb{Z}}
:

\displaystyle f_{k}(\delta_{\operatorname{SG}^{\prime}_{d,s,\delta}}^{+}(v))-f_{k}(\delta_{\operatorname{SG}^{\prime}_{d,s,\delta}}^{-}(v))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)}(\ f_{k}(v,v-e_{i})-f_{k}(v-e_{i},v)\ )

\displaystyle=u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))-(|\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)|-1)\cdot u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))

\displaystyle=-(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)-1)\cdot u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))=b_{k}(v)

If
v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}
:

\displaystyle f_{k}(\delta_{\operatorname{SG}^{\prime}_{d,s,\delta}}^{+}(v))-f_{k}(\delta_{\operatorname{SG}^{\prime}_{d,s,\delta}}^{-}(v))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[d+1]}(\ f_{k}(v,v+e_{i})-f_{k}(v+e_{i},v)\ )

\displaystyle=-u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v+e_{k}))+\sum_{i\in[d+1]\setminus\{k\}}u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v+e_{i}))

\displaystyle=-u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))+(|\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)|-1)\cdot u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))

\displaystyle+(d+1-|\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)|)\cdot u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)+1)

\displaystyle=(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)-1)\cdot u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))+(d-\operatorname{level}_{d}(v))\cdot u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)+1)=b_{k}(v)

This proves all required properties for vertices which contain
k
in their support.

Now we deal with edges between vertices without
k
in their support.
From the construction of
f_{k}
so far, we only have incoming flow at vertices
v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{d,s,\delta}
with
v_{k}=0
and it is of value

\operatorname{level}_{d}(v+e_{k})=\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)+1

.

Note that we actually only have positive flow on edges on level
1
and
2
and therefore only vertices in
\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{d,s,\delta}
on level
1
have incoming flow and it is of value

.
For an illustration see again

Let

v_{k}=0

As

Further note that for

We will now provide combinatorial formulas, which help for counting certain vertices and edges in our simplified graph, to be able to compute the cost of our solution in
Lemma

3.9
.
An important formula is the number of points in a
d
-dimensional discrete simplex of size
s
, i.e.
|\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}|
, as all following formulas are built on this information.
The numbers
|\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}|
are also known as
simplicial polytopic numbers
, but to the best of our knowledge there is no proof that fits our context for counting the points in
\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
However, it is easy to prove:

Lemma 3.10
.

|\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}|=\binom{d+s}{d}

Proof.

We will prove this equation by constructing a bijection
\varphi:\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}\leftrightarrow\{A\subseteq[d+s]:|A|=d\}
.
Since
|\{A\subseteq[d+s]:|A|=d\}|=\binom{d+s}{d}
this is sufficient.

\varphi
is defined by:

\varphi(x):=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i}+k\ :\ k\in[d]\right\}

The inverse
\varphi^{-1}
can be defined as follows:

\varphi^{-1}(\{1\leq a_{1}<\dotsc<a_{d}\leq d+s\}):=\left(x_{i}=\begin{cases}a_{i}-1&\mbox{if }i=1\\
a_{i}-a_{i-1}-1&\mbox{if }i\in[d]\setminus\{1\}\\
s+d-a_{i-1}&\mbox{if }i=d+1\end{cases}\right)_{i\in[d+1]}

With
\varphi^{-1}
it can be easily verified that
\varphi
is indeed a bijection.
Note that for
x\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}

x_{d+1}
is already determined by
x_{1},\dotsc,x_{d}
.
∎

Often the binomial coefficient
\binom{n}{k}
is only defined for
0\leq k\leq n
.
A convenient convention allows arbitrary values by defining
\binom{n}{k}
to be
0
if
k
does not fulfill the above condition.
We will make use of this.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
d,s,k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
2\cdot k+1\geq s
.

\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\max_{i\in[d+1]}v_{i}\leq k\right\}\right|=\binom{d+s}{d}-(d+1)\cdot\binom{d+s-(k+1)}{d}

Proof.

Let
v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Then
|\{i\in[d+1]:v_{i}\geq k+1\}|\in\{0,1\}
since
2\cdot k+1\geq s
and
\sum_{i\in[d+1]}v_{i}=s
.
With that in mind we can prove the claimed equality:

\displaystyle\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\max_{i\in[d+1]}v_{i}\leq k\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}\right|-\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\exists i\in[d+1]\quad v_{i}\geq k+1\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}\right|-\sum_{i\in[d+1]}\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:v_{i}\geq k+1\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}\right|-\sum_{i\in[d+1]}\left|\triangle_{d,s-(k+1)}^{\mathbb{Z}}\right|

\displaystyle=\binom{d+s}{d}-(d+1)\cdot\binom{d+s-(k+1)}{d}\qed

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
d,s,k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
2\cdot k+1\geq s
and
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\max_{i\in[d+1]}v_{i}\leq k\land\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)=l\right\}\right|=\binom{d+1}{l+1}\cdot\left(\binom{s-1}{l}-(l+1)\cdot\binom{s-1-k}{l}\right)

Proof.

\displaystyle\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\max_{i\in[d+1]}v_{i}\leq k\land\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)=l\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[d+1]\\
|I|=l+1\end{subarray}}\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\max_{i\in[d+1]}v_{i}\leq k\land\operatorname{support}_{d}(v)=I\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq[d+1]\\
|I|=l+1\end{subarray}}\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{l,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\max_{i\in[l+1]}v_{i}\leq k\land\forall i\in[l+1]\ v_{i}>0\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\binom{d+1}{l+1}\cdot\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{l,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\max_{i\in[l+1]}v_{i}\leq k\land\forall i\in[l+1]\ v_{i}>0\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\binom{d+1}{l+1}\cdot\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{l,s-(l+1)}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\max_{i\in[l+1]}v_{i}\leq k-1\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=\binom{d+1}{l+1}\cdot\left(\binom{s-1}{l}-(l+1)\cdot\binom{s-1-k}{l}\right)

Note for the last equality that
2\cdot(k-1)+1\geq s-1\geq s-(l+1)
as
l\geq 0
.
This allows the application of
Lemma

3.11
.
∎

implies

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
s,\delta\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
2\cdot\delta\leq s
.
Let
\operatorname{SI}_{d,s,\delta}=(\operatorname{SG}_{d,s,\delta},R_{d,s},c_{\operatorname{L}^{1}})
be a simplified simplex instance.
If
d=2
or
d\geq 3\land s=3\cdot\delta-2
, then there exists a solution
u:E(\operatorname{SG}_{d,s,\delta})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
of
2.4
Multi Balance Cut Relaxation
for
\operatorname{SI}_{d,s,\delta}
of cost

\sum_{e\in E(\operatorname{SG}_{d,s,\delta})}c_{\operatorname{L}^{1}}(e)\cdot u(e)=\begin{cases}\hfil 3\cdot s+\frac{3}{2}\cdot\frac{(s-\delta)\cdot(s-\delta+1)}{2\cdot s-3\cdot\delta+1}&\mbox{if }d=2\\
\hfil\frac{s}{3}\cdot\left(5\cdot d+1+\frac{d-1}{s}\right)&\mbox{if }d\geq 3\land s=3\cdot\delta-2\end{cases}

Proof.

We will define a solution
(u,b,f)
of
2.3
Multi Balance Flow Relaxation
for
\operatorname{SI_{d,s,\delta}}
with the respective solution cost.

Let
u^{\prime}:[d]\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be given by

u^{\prime}(l)=\frac{1}{\binom{d+1}{2}\cdot(2\cdot s-3\cdot\delta+1)}\cdot\begin{cases}\hfil(d+1)\cdot s-d\cdot(3\cdot\delta-2)-1&\text{if }l=1\\
\hfil 3&\text{if }l=2\\
\hfil 0&\text{if }l\geq 3\\
\end{cases}

and

be given by
u(e)=u^{\prime}(\operatorname{level}_{d}(e))
as in

3.9
.

Let

be given as follows:

For

we have
b(r_{i})=-1
and for

and

:

b(v)=\begin{cases}-(l-1)\cdot u^{\prime}(l)&\mbox{if }v\in\triangle_{d,s+1}^{\mathbb{Z}}\\
+(l-1)\cdot u^{\prime}(l)+(d-l)\cdot u^{\prime}(l+1)&\mbox{if }v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}\end{cases}

Now we need to define for each
k\in[d+1]
a bidirected balance-flow for
u
and
b_{r_{k}}
where
r_{k}\in R_{d,s}
and
b_{r_{k}}(v)=b(v)+2\cdot\llbracket v=r_{k}\rrbracket
.
In particular that means

.

Lemma

3.9
shows the existence of such bidirected balance-flows
f_{r_{k}}
.
This is the case because, as remarked
earlier, we only have to contract the auxiliary vertices
R_{i}
in
\operatorname{SG}^{\prime}_{d,s,\delta}
to the respective required vertices

to obtain the corresponding bidirected balance-flow on
\operatorname{SG}_{d,s,\delta}
.
We have
b_{r_{k}}(r_{i})=b_{k}(R_{i})=f_{r_{k}}(\delta_{\operatorname{SG}^{\prime}_{d,s,\delta}}^{+}(R_{i}))-f_{r_{k}}(\delta_{\operatorname{SG}^{\prime}_{d,s,\delta}}^{-}(R_{i}))=\left(\llbracket i=k\rrbracket-\llbracket i\neq k\rrbracket\right)\cdot u(\delta_{\operatorname{SG}_{d,s,\delta}}(R_{i}))
and we further compute:

\displaystyle u(\delta_{\operatorname{SG}_{d,s,\delta}}(R_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in[d]}\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s+1}^{\mathbb{Z}}:v_{i}=s-\delta+1\land\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)=l\right\}\right|\cdot u^{\prime}(l)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in[d]}\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d-1,\delta}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\operatorname{level}_{d-1}(v)=l-1\right\}\right|\cdot u^{\prime}(l)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l\in[d]}\binom{d}{l}\cdot\binom{\delta-1}{l-1}\cdot u^{\prime}(l)

\displaystyle=d\cdot u^{\prime}(1)+\binom{d}{2}\cdot(\delta-1)\cdot u^{\prime}(2)

\displaystyle=\frac{d\cdot\left((d+1)\cdot s-d\cdot(3\cdot\delta-2)-1\right)+\binom{d}{2}\cdot(\delta-1)\cdot 3}{\binom{d+1}{2}\cdot(2\cdot s-3\cdot\delta+1)}=1

Hence

is a solution of
2.4
Multi Balance Cut Relaxation
for
\operatorname{SI_{d,s,\delta}}
.

With the formulas stated in
Lemma

3.11
and
Lemma

3.12
we can compute the solution cost.
Note that
2\cdot(s-\delta)+1\geq s+1
if
2\cdot\delta\leq s
and therefore we can use
Lemma

3.12
for
k=s-\delta
.
We first compute:

\displaystyle|\{e\in E_{d,s,\delta}:\operatorname{level}_{d}(e)=l\}|

\displaystyle=(l+1)\cdot\left|\left\{v\in V_{d,s,\delta}\cap\triangle_{d,s+1}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)=l\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=(l+1)\cdot\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s+1}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\max_{i\in[d+1]}v_{i}\leq s-\delta\land\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)=l\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=(l+1)\cdot\binom{d+1}{l+1}\cdot\left(\binom{s}{l}-(l+1)\cdot\binom{\delta}{l}\right)

\displaystyle=(d+1)\cdot\binom{d}{l}\cdot\left(\binom{s}{l}-(l+1)\cdot\binom{\delta}{l}\right)

\displaystyle|\{e\in F_{d,s,\delta}:\operatorname{level}_{d}(e)=l\}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in[d+1]}\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d,s}^{\mathbb{Z}}:v_{i}=s-\delta\land\operatorname{level}_{d}(v)=l\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=(d+1)\cdot\left|\left\{v\in\triangle_{d-1,\delta}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\operatorname{level}_{d-1}(v)=l-1\right\}\right|

\displaystyle=(d+1)\cdot\binom{d}{l}\cdot\binom{\delta-1}{l-1}

By definition we have
c_{\operatorname{L}^{1}}(e)=2\cdot\delta
for
e\in F_{d,s,\delta}
and
c_{\operatorname{L}^{1}}(e)=1
for
e\in E_{d,s,\delta}
.

\operatorname{opt}_{\operatorname{BCR}^{+}}(\operatorname{SCI}_{\mathcal{S},p})\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{f_{\mathcal{S}}}\right)\cdot\left(|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{p}-1\right)+1

Proof.

Write
\operatorname{SCI}_{\mathcal{S},p}=((V,E),R,c)
.
We will define
u:E\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
f_{r}:E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
g:R\setminus\{r\}\times E_{\leftrightarrow}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
(u,f_{r},g)
is a solution of
\operatorname{MCFR^{+}}
with the claimed upper bound as cost proving the inequality.
We define
u
,
f_{r}
and
g
as follows:

The root is
r
as already indicated.
All upward edges have value
0
with respect to
u
,
f_{r}
and
g
.
Downward edges between level
0
and level
1
and between
p
and
p+1
each have value
f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}
under
u
and
f_{r}
.
The other downward edges each have value
f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-2}
under
u
and
f_{r}
.

For
(x,e)\in{\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}}^{p-1}\times\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}
we define
g_{(x,e)}
as follows:

\displaystyle g_{(x,e)}(r,(x,S))

\displaystyle=\llbracket e\in S\rrbracket\cdot f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\{r,(x,S)\}

\displaystyle\in E_{0}

\displaystyle g_{(x,e)}((x,S),(x|e,S^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\llbracket e\in S\land e\in S^{\prime}\rrbracket\cdot f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-2}

\displaystyle\{(x,S),(x|e,S^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle\in E_{i}

\displaystyle g_{(x,e)}((x,S),(x,e))

\displaystyle=\llbracket e\in S\rrbracket\cdot f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\{(x,S),(x,e)\}

\displaystyle\in E_{p}

It is easy to see that
g_{(x,e)}
is a balance-flow for
b_{(x,e)}(v):=\llbracket v=r\rrbracket-\llbracket v=(x,e)\rrbracket
respecting the edge-usage bounds
f_{r}
as each element has frequency
f_{\mathcal{S}}
.
Therefore,
(u,f_{r},g)
is a solution of
2.2
Multi Commodity Flow Relaxation
.

For proving that
(u,f_{r},g)
is a solution of
\operatorname{MCFR^{+}}
we need to show the flow-balance constraints at each Steiner vertex:
For each
(x,S)\in V_{i}={\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}}^{i-1}\times\mathcal{S}
with
i\in[p]
we have
f_{r}(\delta_{\operatorname{SCG}_{\mathcal{S},p}}^{-}((x,S)))=f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}
and for the outgoing edges we compute:

f_{r}(\delta_{\operatorname{SCG}_{\mathcal{S},p}}^{+}((x,S)))=\begin{cases}|S|\cdot|\mathcal{S}|\cdot f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-2}&\mbox{if }i\neq p\\
|S|\cdot f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}&\mbox{if }i=p\end{cases}

As
|\mathcal{S}|\geq f_{\mathcal{S}}
and
|S|\geq 1
we obtain
f_{r}(\delta_{\operatorname{SCG}_{\mathcal{S},p}}^{+}((x,S)))\geq f_{r}(\delta_{\operatorname{SCG}_{\mathcal{S},p}}^{-}((x,S)))
.

Hence,
(u,f_{r},g)
is a solution of
\operatorname{MCFR^{+}}
.
For the solution cost we compute:

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E}c(e)\cdot u(e)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{e\in E_{i}}u(e)

\displaystyle=f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\cdot(|E_{0}|+|E_{p}|)+f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-2}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}|E_{i}|

\displaystyle=f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\cdot(|V_{1}|+|V_{p+1}|\cdot f_{\mathcal{S}})+f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-2}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}|V_{i+1}|\cdot f_{\mathcal{S}}

\displaystyle=f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\cdot|\mathcal{S}|+|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{p}+f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\cdot|\mathcal{S}|\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{i}

\displaystyle=f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\cdot|\mathcal{S}|+|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{p}+f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\cdot|\mathcal{S}|\cdot\left(\frac{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{p}-1}{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1}-1\right)

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{p}+f_{\mathcal{S}}^{-1}\cdot|\mathcal{S}|\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{p}-1}{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1}\qed

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\operatorname{SCI}_{\mathcal{S},p}
be a set cover based Steiner tree instance.
Then:

\operatorname{opt}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}^{+}}}(\operatorname{SCI}_{\mathcal{S},p})\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{\min_{e\in\mathcal{U}}f_{\mathcal{S}}(e)}\right)\cdot\left(|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{p}-1\right)+1

Let
I\subseteq\mathcal{S}
an optimum solution for the set cover instance
\mathcal{S}
.
Then:

\operatorname{gap}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}^{+}},\operatorname{STP}}(\operatorname{SCI}_{\mathcal{S},p})\geq\frac{\left(1+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1}\cdot|I|\right)\cdot\left(|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{p}-1\right)+1}{\left(1+\frac{1}{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{\min_{e\in\mathcal{U}}f_{\mathcal{S}}(e)}\right)\cdot\left(|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|^{p}-1\right)+1}

Proof.

For a given set cover instance
\mathcal{S}
we obtain a new set cover instance
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
where all elements have the same frequency by choosing for each element
\min_{e\in\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}}f_{\mathcal{S}}(e)
sets in
\mathcal{S}
which contain that element, removing the element from all other sets which contain it and discarding resulting empty sets.
The set cover graph obtained from this new set cover instance is then subgraph of the original one.
The two bounds then follow with
Lemma

A.2
,
Lemma

A.3
and the fact that
|\mathcal{S}^{\prime}|\leq|\mathcal{S}|
and
\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}=\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}
.
∎

Theorem A.5
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set cover instance and
I\subseteq\mathcal{S}
be an optimum solution for the set cover instance
\mathcal{S}
.
Then

\operatorname{gap}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}^{+}},\operatorname{STP}}\geq\frac{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1+|I|}{|\mathcal{U}_{\mathcal{S}}|-1+\frac{|\mathcal{S}|}{\min_{e\in\mathcal{U}}f_{\mathcal{S}}(e)}}

Proof.

This follows by
Lemma

A.4
with
\operatorname{gap}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}^{+}},\operatorname{STP}}\geq\lim_{p\to\infty}\operatorname{gap}_{\operatorname{\mathcal{BCR}^{+}},\operatorname{STP}}(\operatorname{SCI}_{\mathcal{S},p})
.
∎

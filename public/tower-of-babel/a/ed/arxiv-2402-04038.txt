PAC-Bayesian Adversarially Robust Generalization Bounds for Graph Neural Network Journal: Neural Networks
Tan Sun, Junhong Lin

Abstract
Graph neural networks (GNNs) have gained popularity for various graph-related tasks. However, similar to deep neural networks, GNNs are also vulnerable to adversarial attacks. Empirical studies have shown that adversarially robust generalization has a pivotal role in establishing effective defense algorithms against adversarial attacks. In this paper, we contribute by providing adversarially robust generalization bounds for two kinds of popular GNNs, graph convolutional network (GCN) and message passing graph neural network, using the PAC-Bayesian framework. Our result reveals that spectral norm of the diffusion matrix on the graph and spectral norm of the weights as well as the perturbation factor govern the robust generalization bounds of both models. Our bounds are nontrivial generalizations of the results developed in ( Liao et al. 2020 ) from the standard setting to adversarial setting while avoiding exponential dependence of the maximum node degree. As corollaries, we derive better PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds for GCN in the standard setting, which improve the bounds in ( Liao et al. 2020 ) by avoiding exponential dependence on the maximum node degree.

Introduction

Graph neural network (GNN)
(
Scarselli et al. 2009
)
has developed rapidly in recent years, due to the excellent performance in a variety of applications with the graph-structure data. Generally, GNNs are neural networks that combine local aggregation of node features and non-linear transformations to predict the node-level labels or graph-level labels, in semi-supervised learning
(
Kipf and Welling 2017
)
and supervised learning
(
Pan et al. 2015
;
Pan et al. 2016
;
Zhang et al. 2018
;
Ying et al. 2018
)
, respectively. With the rapid development of GNN, there is an increasing interest in understanding the generalization
(
Scarselli et al. 2018
;
Verma and Zhang 2019
;
Du et al. 2019
;
Garg et al. 2020
;
Liao et al. 2020
;
Esser et al. 2021
;
Ju et al. 2023
)
and other theoretical properties of GNN.

Szegedy et al. 2013
and
Goodfellow et al. 2015
reveal that deep neural network (DNN) can be easily fooled by adversarial input samples and that adversarial training can improve the generalization of DNN.
GNN also has a similar neural network structure and can be vulnerable to adversarial attacks on graph data. Previous work focuses on how to attack on GNN
(
Dai et al. 2018
;
Zügner et al. 2018
;
Bojchevski and Günnemann 2019
;
Zügner et al. 2020
;
Liu et al. 2019
)
and how to defend GNN against adversarial attacks
(
Zhang and Zitnik 2020
;
Wu et al. 2019
;
Entezari et al. 2020
;
Zhu et al. 2019
;
Tang et al. 2020
)
, but the theoretical analysis is relatively limited.

A recent line of work discusses the generalization of GNN in the standard (non-adversarial) setting, and derive certain bounds on complexity notations including Vapnik–Chervonenkis (VC) dimension and Rademacher complexity that are used to characterize the generalization error, i.e., the disparity between the performance on training and test datasets of a model. Specifically,
Scarselli et al. 2018
derive an upper bound of order
\mathcal{O}(p^{4}n^{2})
for the VC dimension of GNNs with sigmoid activations, where
p
is the number of parameters and
n
is the number of nodes.

Verma and Zhang 2019
derive a generalization bound for single-layer graph convolutional neural network (GCN) by analyzing the algorithmic stability of stochastic gradient descent, where the bound depends on the number of algorithm iterations and the largest absolute eigenvalue of the graph convolution filter.

Du et al. 2019
consider an infinitely wide multi-layer GNN trained by gradient descent based on neural tangent kernels
(
Jacot et al. 2018
)
, and derive a generalization bound for this particular model.
These two results are constrained to specific settings and do not apply to other scenarios.

Garg et al. 2020
present the pioneering data-dependent generalization bounds for GNNs employing Rademacher complexity, with a scaling factor of
\mathcal{O}\left(hd^{l}\sqrt{l\log\left(hd\right)}\right)
. Here,
d
is the maximum degree of the graph, while
h
and
l
denote the width and depth of the network, respectively.

In light of the earlier analysis, obtaining generalization bounds for GNNs using complexity notations could be challenging, even in the standard setting. Consequently, some studies have shifted their focus to the PAC-Bayesian framework. Based on the PAC-Bayesian framework,
Liao et al. 2020
provide generalization bounds for GCN and message passing graph neural network (MPGNN), where the bounds scale as
\mathcal{O}\left(ld^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)
and
\mathcal{O}\left(ld^{l-2}\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)
respectively.
Recently,
Ju et al. 2023
derive generalization bounds of order
\mathcal{O}\left(l\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\sqrt{h}\right)
for MPGNN. The key idea of their analysis is to measure the stability of GNN against noise perturbations using Hessians, and requires that the nonlinear activations and the loss functions are twice-differentiable, Lipschitz-continuous and their first-order
and second-order derivatives are both Lipschitz-continuous, which thus does not apply to the commonly used ReLU activation.
Here,
\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}
is the spectral norm of the GNN’s feature diffusion matrix
P_{G}
on the graph
G
, and varies between different models. Particularly,
\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}
is less than 1 for GCN
and can be less than
d
for MPGNN.

In seeking to grasp the constrained robust generalization abilities of GNNs in the adversarial setting,
in this paper, we provide adversarially robust generalization bounds for two kinds of popular GNNs, GCN
(
Kipf and Welling 2017
)
and MPGNN
(
Dai et al. 2016
;
Jin et al. 2019
)
, using PAC-Bayesian framework.
Our result reveals that spectral norm of the diffusion matrix on the graph, spectral and Frobenius norm of the weights, as well as the perturbation factor govern the robust generalization bounds of both models.
Our bounds for GCN complement the findings in the standard setting for DNN
in
(
Neyshabur et al. 2017
)
, as well as the results for GCN in
(
Liao et al. 2020
)
. All the bounds of these studies have the same spectrally-normalized term of the weights and the dependencies on both depth and width:

l\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{F}^{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}}.

To our knowledge, our derived PAC-Bayesian adversarially robust generalization bounds may be the first kind for GNN in the adversarial robustness settings. Even in the standard setting, our bounds improve the bounds in
(
Liao et al. 2020
)
from
\mathcal{O}\left(ld^{\frac{l-1}{2}}\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)
to
\mathcal{O}(l\sqrt{h\log(lh)})

for GCN, and
\mathcal{O}\left(ld^{l-2}\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)
to

\mathcal{O}(l\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-2}\sqrt{h\log(lh)})

for MPGNN, avoiding the exponential dependence on the maximum degree
d
of the graph. Meanwhile, our results remove the extra first-order and second-order Lipschitz-continuous assumptions in
(
Ju et al. 2023
)
for MPGNN,
exhibiting comparable tightness on the dependency on the depth, width and maximum degree.
In the context of adversarial robustness, the derived generalization bounds incorporate an additional factor
\epsilon
that represents the intensity of the perturbation and is in general unavoidable.
The derived bounds in the robust setting indicate that one could achieve roughly the same generalization performance as in the non-adversarial attack setting, provided that the
l_{2}
-norm attack level on the graph node, denoted as
\epsilon
, is less than the norm on the node. Additionally, according to our theory, the generalization error bounds do not have a significant effect on the graph edge attack. Therefore, one could overlook the attack on the graph edge and instead focus on the optimization error caused by the graph edge attack.

To achieve the results, we follow the PAC-Bayesian framework
(
McAllester 2003
;
Neyshabur et al. 2017
)
with necessary modification in the adversarial setting, and relate the robust generalization error on a prior distribution of models to that on a single model by adding random perturbation (that results in a small perturbed robust margin loss) to the single model parameters.
We then proceed with the proof by constructing suitable prior distribution and random perturbations, combining with novel concise techniques, which are related to the Frobenius norm
(instead of the spectral norm or the maximum rows’ vector
2
-norm used in the previous literature) of the node representations and related inequalities, to well estimate the sensitivity of the robust margin loss concerning the model parameters and to meet the challenging of avoiding the exponential dependence of the maximum degree.

[k]
be the set of first
k

,
\left\|x\right\|_{p}
denotes the vector

-norm. For a matrix
A
,
\left\|A\right\|_{p}

p
-norm, and

, we briefly review some other related works. In

. Finally, we discuss the limitations and some open problems in

.

Related Work

Generalization Bounds.

Classical results rely on complexity notions of neural networks to regulate generalization bounds, employing measures such as VC-dimension as seen in
(
Anthony and Bartlett 2009
)
, or Rademacher complexity as discussed in
(
Bartlett and Mendelson 2002
)
.
Norm-margin-based generalization bounds are developed using Rademacher complexity for fully-connected neural network in
(
Neyshabur et al. 2015
;
Bartlett et al. 2017
;
Golowich et al. 2018
)
.

Neyshabur et al. 2015
establish a generalization bound with an exponential dependence
2^{l}
on the network depth
l
using Frobenius norms of parameters.

Bartlett et al. 2017
showed a margin based generalization bound that depends on spectral norm
and
1
-norm of the layers of the networks, reducing the dependence on depth.
Golowich et al. 2018

provide bounds on their Rademacher complexity assuming norm constraints on the parameter matrix of each layer, refining the dependency on depth to
\sqrt{l}
.

Neyshabur et al. 2017
establish a PAC-Bayesian framework to bound the generalization gap for fully-connected neural network, where the bound depends on the product of the spectral norm of the weights in
each layer as well as the Frobenius norm of the weights.
As mentioned above, the generalization bounds on GNN are derived using different tools such as VC-dimension
(
Scarselli et al. 2018
)
, stability analysis
(
Verma and Zhang 2019
)
, Rademacher complexity
(
Garg et al. 2020
)
and PAC-Bayesian analysis
(
Liao et al. 2020
;
Ju et al. 2023
)
.

Adversarial Attack and Defense.

Zügner et al. 2018
focus on the adversarial attacks against GCN and provide an algorithm to generate adversarial perturbations targeting on the node features and the graph structures.
Dai et al. 2018
propose a reinforcement learning based attack method for adversarial attacks against GNN by modifying the structures of the graph.
Zügner et al. 2020
develop a general attack on the training procedure of GCN using meta-gradients.
Bojchevski and Günnemann 2019
propose a principled strategy for adversarial attacks on unsupervised node embeddings. There are also some researches focusing on the defense methods.
Wu et al. 2019
provide a defense method by pre-processing the adjacency matrix of the graph. The methods in
(
Zhu et al. 2019
;
Entezari et al. 2020
)
defend against attacks by absorbing or dropping the noise information.

Tang et al. 2020
use transfer learning to improve the robustness of GNN.
Zhang and Zitnik 2020
develop a general algorithm to defend against a variety of training-time attacks that perturb the graph structure.

Adversarial Generalization in DNN.
A line of work presents the adversarially robust generalization bounds on DNNs using Rademacher complexity

(
Khim and Loh 2018
;
Yin et al. 2019
;
Awasthi et al. 2020
;
Gao and Wang 2021
;
Xiao et al. 2022
)
.

Khim and Loh 2018
derive robust generalization bounds for linear and neural network via Rademacher complexity of a transformed function.

Yin et al. 2019
established generalization bounds for both linear models and one-hidden-layer neural networks based on the
surrogate loss introduced in
(
Raghunathan et al. 2018
)
considering
vector-
\infty
-norm-additive perturbation.

Awasthi et al. 2020
give upper
and lower bounds for Rademacher complexity of linear hypotheses with
vector-
r
-norm-additive perturbation for
r\geq 1
, and also provide upper bounds on Rademacher complexity of one hidden layer neural networks, directly applying to the original network.
Gao and Wang 2021
and
Farnia et al. 2019
show robust generalization bounds for DNNs, considering specific attacks.
Xiao et al. 2022
and
Mustafa et al. 2022
employ two different techniques, respectively, to calculate the covering number that can be used to estimate the Rademacher complexity, for adversarial function classes of DNNs. Further,

Xiao et al. 2023
present a robust generalization bound on DNN using PAC-Bayesian framework.

3
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce the problem setup for multi-class graph classification problem with GCN and MPGNN, and preliminary results on PAC-Bayesian analysis.

3.1
Problem Setup

We consider a multi-class graph classification problem, in which, given an undirected graph
G
, we would like to classify it into one of
K
classes. Specifically, the sample
z=(G,y)\in\mathcal{Z}
consists of a graph
G
and a label
y\in\mathcal{Y}=\{1,\dots,K\}
. Each graph
G=(X,A)
has a feature matrix
X\in\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n\times h_{0}}
and an adjacency matrix
A\in\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, where each row of
X
is the feature vector of a node,
n
is the number of nodes and
h_{0}
is the input feature dimension. The classification model
f
maps the graph
G
to a
K
-dimension vector, and we take the index of the maximum element in
f(G)
as the output
\hat{y}=\arg\max_{i\in[K]}f(G)_{i}
, where
f(G)_{i}
is
i
-th element of
f(G)
. We assume that the training set
S=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}
with
m
samples that are independent and identically distributed from an unknown data distribution
\mathcal{D}
, and that the classification model is from the hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}
.

We focus on two kinds of GNN, GCN and MPGNN, which are the most popular variants of GNN. For simplicity, we define the model parameterized by
w
as
f_{w}\in\mathcal{H}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{1\times K}
, where
w
is the vectorization of all model parameters.

GCN.
An
l
-layer (
l>1
otherwise the model is trivial) GCN can be defined as,

\displaystyle H_{k}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{k}\left(P_{G}H_{k-1}W_{k}\right),\ k\in[l-1],

\displaystyle H_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}W_{l},

(1)

where
H_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times h_{k}},k\in[l-1]
are the node representations in each layer,
H_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times K}
is the readout, and
W_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{h_{k-1}\times h_{k}},k\in[l]
are the parameters in the
k
-th layer. And we let
H_{0}=X
. The matrix
P_{G}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is related to the graph structure and
\sigma_{k}(\cdot),k\in[l-1]
are some element-wise non-linear mappings. Practically, for GCN, we take
P_{G}
as the Laplacian of the graph, defined as
\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\tilde{A}\tilde{D}^{-1/2}
, where
\tilde{A}=A+I
and
\tilde{D}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{A}_{ij},i\in[n]\right)
is the degree matrix of
\tilde{A}
. Throughout of this paper, for GCN,
P_{G}
is taken as the Laplacian
\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\tilde{A}\tilde{D}^{-1/2}
, and the non-linear mappings are chosen as ReLU, that is,
\sigma_{k}(x)=\max(0,x),\forall k\in[l-1]
.

MPGNN.
There are several variants of MPGNN
(
Dai et al. 2016
;
Gilmer et al. 2017
;
Jin et al. 2019
)
and we introduce the models stated in
(
Garg et al. 2020
)
which are popular in the generalization analysis of GNN. The
l
-layer (
l>1
otherwise the model is trivial) MPGNN can be defined as,

\displaystyle H_{k}=\phi_{k}\left(XU_{k}+\rho_{k}\left(P_{G}\psi_{k}\left(H_{k-1}\right)W_{k}\right)\right),\ k\in[l-1],

\displaystyle H_{l}=\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}W_{l},

(2)

where
H_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times h_{k}},k\in[l-1]
are the node representations in each layer,
H_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times K}
is the readout,
W_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{h_{k-1}\times h_{k}},k\in[l]
and
U_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{h_{0}\times h_{k}},k\in[l-1]
are the parameters in the
k
-th layer. Here we let
H_{0}=0
. Several common designs for
P_{G}
would be the adjacency matrix, the normalized adjacency matrix or the Laplacian of the graph.
\phi_{k},\rho_{k},\psi_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n\times h}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\times h},k\in[l-1]
are element-wise non-linear mappings, e.g., ReLU and Tanh. In MPGNN, if we take
U_{1}
as identity matrix,
U_{k},2\leq k\leq l-1
as zero matrices and
\rho_{k},\psi_{k}
as identity mappings, then we could get a GCN model with
l-1
layers.

As defined above, we denote
w=vec\left(\left\{W_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{l}\right)
and
w=vec\left(\left\{W_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{l},\left\{U_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{l-1}\right)
in different settings for GCN and MPGNN, respectively.
Here, for a set of matrices,
vec(\cdot)
is the vectorization of the set, which is the stack of columns for all matrices in the set.

Margin Loss.
Given a GNN
f
, we focus on the generalization gap to evaluate the generalization performance of the model. For the multi-class classification problem, we can use the multi-class margin loss to define the generalization gap
(
Bartlett et al. 2017
)
. First, we define the margin operator of the true label
y\in[K]
given
G\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}
for a GNN model
f_{w}
as

\displaystyle M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),y\right)=f_{w}\left(G\right)_{y}-\max_{j\neq y}f_{w}\left(G\right)_{j}.

And for the convenience of the following analysis, we define the margin operator of a pair of two classes
(i,j)
given
G\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}
for a GNN model
f_{w}
as

\displaystyle M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)=f_{w}\left(G\right)_{i}-f_{w}\left(G\right)_{j},\ i,j\in[K].

The margin operator reflects the gap between the output for the true label and other labels.
With this, the model classifies the graph correctly if and only if the margin operator is larger than zero. We expect the model to classify the graph correctly with a confidence, and hence, for a margin
\gamma>0
, we define the expected margin loss of the model
f_{w}
over the data distribution
\mathcal{D}
as the probability that the margin operator is less than the margin, that is,

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{(G,y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left\{M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),y\right)\leq\gamma\right\}.

The empirical margin loss over the dataset
S
is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{(G,y)\in S}\mathbbm{1}\left\{M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),y\right)\leq\gamma\right\}.

Thus, the generalization error of a model
f_{w}
can be defined as the gap between the expected loss and the empirical loss,
L_{\mathcal{D},0}\left(f_{w}\right)-\hat{L}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)
.

Similarly, for adversarial setting, we can define the robust margin loss. Adversarial examples are usually generated from an attack algorithm. Given
G
and
f_{w}
, let
\delta_{w}(G)
be the output set of an attack algorithm, which contains the adversarial samples of
G
with respect to
f_{w}
. As the margin is expected to be larger, we choose the adversarial sample
G^{*}=\mathop{\arg\inf}\limits_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}M\left(f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right),y\right)\in\delta_{w}(G)
which minimizes the margin operator. With this, we can define the robust margin operator of the true label
y
given
G
for the GNN model
f_{w}
as

\displaystyle RM\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),y\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}M\left(f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right),y\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}\left(f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{y}-\max_{j\neq y}f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{j}\right).

And similarly, we define the robust margin operator of a pair of two classes
(i,j)
given
G\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}
for a model
f_{w}
as

\displaystyle RM\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}\left(f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{i}-f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{j}\right).

For margin
\gamma>0
, the expected robust margin loss of the model
f_{w}
over the data distribution
\mathcal{D}
is defined as

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)=\mathbb{P}_{(G,y)\sim\mathcal{D}}\left\{RM\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),y\right)\leq\gamma\right\},

and the empirical robust margin loss over the dataset
S
is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{(G,y)\in S}\mathbbm{1}\left\{RM\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),y\right)\leq\gamma\right\}.

With this, the robust generalization error of a model
f_{w}
can be defined as the gap between the robust expected loss and the robust empirical loss,
R_{\mathcal{D},0}\left(f_{w}\right)-\hat{R}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)
.

3.2
Background of PAC-Bayesian Analysis

PAC-Bayesian analysis
(
McAllester 2003
)
provides probability approximation correct (PAC) bound for generalization errors. In particular, the purpose of a learning process is to provide a posterior distribution
Q
of a parameterized predictor
f_{w}
over the hypothesis class
\mathcal{H}
, which minimizes the expected margin loss with respect to the data distribution
\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}\left(Q\right)=\mathbb{E}_{w\sim Q}\left[L_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)\right].

Since the true distribution of the data is unknowable, we define the empirical margin loss with respect to the data sample
S
,

\displaystyle\hat{L}_{S,\gamma}\left(Q\right)=\mathbb{E}_{w\sim Q}\left[\hat{L}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)\right].

PAC-Bayesian analysis provides guarantees for the gap between the expected and empirical loss. Following
(
McAllester 2003
)
, we present the subsequent theorem with respect to the robust margin loss, defined as

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}\left(Q\right)=\mathbb{E}_{w\sim Q}\left[R_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)\right],

and

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{S,\gamma}\left(Q\right)=\mathbb{E}_{w\sim Q}\left[\hat{R}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)\right].

Theorem 3.1

(
McAllester 2003
)
.

Let
P
be a prior distribution over
\mathcal{H}
that is independent of the training set. Then for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the choice of the training set
S=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}
independently sampled from
\mathcal{D}
, for all distributions
Q
over
\mathcal{H}
and any
\gamma>0
, we have

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{D},\gamma}\left(Q\right)\leq\hat{R}_{S,\gamma}\left(Q\right)+\sqrt{\frac{KL\left(Q\|P\right)+\log(2m/\delta)}{2(m-1)}}.

Here
KL\left(Q\|P\right)
is the KL-divergence between two distributions. The result in the theorem holds for any given prior distribution
P
(that is independent from the training set) and all posterior distributions
Q
. Therefore, we can construct specific priors and posteriors so that we can work out the bound. Additionally, the result is related to quantities defined on distribution over models, while many learning processes learn a single model. Hence, in order to apply
Theorem

3.1
to a single model
f_{w}
, we construct a posterior distribution
Q
by adding random perturbations
\Delta w
to the learned parameters
w
, where
w
is considered to be fixed and
\Delta w
is drawn from a known distribution. We have the following lemma, which relates the loss on a distribution
Q
with the loss on a single model.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f_{w}\left(G\right):\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{K}
be any model with parameters
w
, and
P
be any distribution on the parameters that is independent of the training data. For any
w
, we construct a posterior
Q(w+\Delta w)
by adding a random perturbation
\Delta w
to
w
, s.t.,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\Delta w}\Bigg\{\max_{i,j\in[K],\atop G\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}}\big|RM\left(f_{w+\Delta w}(G),i,j\right)-RM\left(f_{w}(G),i,j\right)\big|<\frac{\gamma}{2}\Bigg\}\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Then, for any
\gamma,\delta>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the choice of the training set
S=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}
independently sampled from
\mathcal{D}
, for any
w
, we have,

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{D},0}\left(f_{w}\right)\leq\hat{R}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{2KL\left(Q\|P\right)+\log(8m/\delta)}{2(m-1)}}.

Here, the KL is evaluated with that
w
is treated as fixed while
\Delta w
is random, i.e. the distribution of
Q
is the distribution of
\Delta w
shifted by
w
.

Neyshabur et al. 2017
provide the lemma to give a margin-based generalization bound derived from the PAC-Bayesian bound.
Liao et al. 2020
improve the lemma and present the lemma in a two-side form. Both of the above lemmas are proposed in the standard settings. We present
Lemma

3.2
of the two-side form with the robust margin loss in the adversarial settings. We provide proofs of
Theorems

3.1
and

in
Sections

3.3
Assumptions

In this part, we first introduce some necessary assumptions and then present our main results on generalization bounds for GNN.

First, we make the following common assumption for the graph data, which also appears in the literature and is easily validated on real-world data.

Assumption 3.3
.

For any graph
G=(X,A)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}
, the node features are contained in a spectral norm ball with radius
B
, that is,
\left\|X\right\|_{2}\leq B
.

We then introduce the following two assumptions which are related to the models.

Assumption 3.4
.

For both GCN and MPGNN models, the maximum layer width is
h
, that is
h_{k}\leq h,\forall k\in[l]\cup\{0\}
.

Assumption 3.5
.

For any model
f_{w}\in\mathcal{H}
of MPGNN defined in
Equation

2
, the weight matrices in different layers are uniformly bounded, that is
\left\|W_{k}\right\|_{2}\leq M_{2},\forall k\in[l]
and
\left\|U_{k}\right\|_{2}\leq M_{1},\forall k\in[l-1]
. The mappings
\phi_{k},\rho_{k},\psi_{k},k\in[l-1]
are all
L
-Lipschitz and centered at zero, that is
\phi_{k}(0)=\rho_{k}(0)=\psi_{k}(0)=0,\forall k\in[l-1]
.

Motivated by practical designs
(
Gilmer et al. 2017
;
Jin et al. 2018
)
, in the above we consider MPGNN models with
W_{k}
and
U_{k}
sharing the same norm upper bounds for each
k
.

4
Robust Generalization Bounds

In this section, we present our main results: improved generalization bounds in standard settings and adversarially robust generalization bounds for GCN and MPGNN.

4.1
Generalization Bounds for GCN

We first present the results for GCN in standard settings.

Theorem 4.1

(Generalization Bounds for GCN)
.

Under Assumptions
3.3
and
3.4
, for any
\delta,\gamma>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta
, over the choice of a training set
S=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}
independently sampled from
\mathcal{D}
, for any
w
, we have

\displaystyle L_{\mathcal{D},0}\left(f_{w}\right)\leq\hat{L}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}l^{2}h\log(lh)\Phi(f_{w})+\log(ml/\delta)}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right),

where
\Phi(f_{w})=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{F}^{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}}
.

Theorem

4.1
shows that the generalization bound of GCN does not grow with the maximal degree of the graph.

For the robust case, we consider an
\epsilon
-attack (
\epsilon>0
), where given a sample
G=(X,A)
, the attack sample
G^{\prime}=(X^{\prime},A^{\prime})
satisfies that
\left\|X^{\prime}-X\right\|_{2}\leq\epsilon
and
A^{\prime}
can be any adjacency matrix.

Theorem 4.2

(Robust Generalization Bounds for GCN)
.

Under Assumptions
3.3
and
3.4
, consider the
\epsilon
-attack with
\epsilon>0
, for any
\delta,\gamma>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the choice of a training set
S=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}
independently sampled from
\mathcal{D}
, for any
w
, we have

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{D},0}\left(f_{w}\right)\leq\hat{R}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{(B+\epsilon)^{2}l^{2}h\log(lh)\Phi(f_{w})+\log(ml/\delta)}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right),

where
\Phi(f_{w})=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{F}^{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}}
.

The above generalization bound in the adversarial setting is as tight as the bound in Theorem
4.1
for the standard setting up to the
\epsilon
factor of attack, which is in general unavoidable.

For the GCN model, we take
P_{G}
as the Laplacian of the graph. As the spectral norm of the Laplacian is less than one, the bound due to the graph architecture
\|P_{G}\|^{l}
raised in our proof can be well controlled and we thus get a bound that is independent on the maximum degree
d
of the graph.

The main difficulty is to well estimate the change
in the robust margin operator with a perturbation on the
parameters, adopting the spectral norm of
P_{G}
rather than the maximum degree
d
. The estimating can be divided into two steps. The first step is to bound the change in the output
of the model with the perturbation. We present the following lemma which shows that the change can be bounded by the norm of the parameters without dependence on the degree of the graph.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f_{w}\in\mathcal{H}
be a GCN model with
l
layers. For any
w
, any perturbation
\Delta w=vec\left(\left\{\Delta W_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{l}\right)
such that for all
i\in[l]
,
\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}/l
, for any
G=(X,A)\in\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{G}
, the change in the output of GCN is bounded as

\displaystyle\left\|f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right)-f_{w}\left(G\right)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}e\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq eB\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}}.

Proof.

We denote the node representation in
j
-th (
j\leq l
) layer as

\displaystyle f_{w}^{j}(G)=H_{j}=\sigma_{j}\left(P_{G}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right),\ j\in[l-1],

\displaystyle f_{w}^{l}(G)=H_{l}=\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}W_{l}.

Adding perturbation
\Delta w
to the parameter
w
, that is, for the
j
-th (
j\leq l
) layer, the perturbed parameters are
W_{j}+\Delta W_{j}
and denote
H_{j}^{\prime}=f_{w+\Delta w}^{j}(G),j\in[l]
.

Upper Bound on the Node Representation.
For any
j<l
,

\displaystyle\left\|H_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sigma_{j}\left(P_{G}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right)\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{G}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{G}H_{j-1}\right\|_{F}\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}\left\|H_{j-1}\right\|_{F}\left\|W_{j}\right\|_{2},

where the first inequality holds since
\sigma_{j}
is 1-Lipschitz and
\sigma_{j}(0)=0
, and the second and the last ones hold by
Proposition

A.1
. Then, using the relationship iteratively and
H_{0}=X
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|H_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{j}\left\|H_{0}\right\|_{F}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{j}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}.

(3)

Upper Bound on the Change of Node Representation.
For any
j<l
,

\displaystyle\left\|H_{j}^{\prime}-H_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sigma_{j}\left(P_{G}H_{j-1}^{\prime}(W_{j}+\Delta W_{j})\right)-\sigma_{j}\left(P_{G}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right)\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{G}H_{j-1}^{\prime}(W_{j}+\Delta W_{j})-P_{G}H_{j-1}W_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\left\|P_{G}\left(H_{j-1}^{\prime}-H_{j-1}\right)(W_{j}+\Delta W_{j})+P_{G}H_{j-1}\Delta W_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{G}\left(H_{j-1}^{\prime}-H_{j-1}\right)(W_{j}+\Delta W_{j})\right\|_{F}+\left\|P_{G}H_{j-1}\Delta W_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}\left\|H_{j-1}^{\prime}-H_{j-1}\right\|_{F}\left\|W_{j}+\Delta W_{j}\right\|_{2}+\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}\left\|H_{j-1}\right\|_{F}\left\|\Delta W_{j}\right\|_{2},

(4)

where the first inequality holds since
\sigma_{j}
is 1-Lipschitz and
\sigma_{j}(0)=0
, and the last one holds by
Proposition

A.1
. Simplify the notations in
Equation

4
as
\left\|H_{j}^{\prime}-H_{j}\right\|_{F}\leq a_{j-1}\left\|H_{j-1}^{\prime}-H_{j-1}\right\|_{F}

+b_{j-1}
where
a_{j-1}=\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}\left\|W_{j}+\Delta W_{j}\right\|_{2}
and
b_{j-1}=\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}\left\|H_{j-1}\right\|_{F}\left\|\Delta W_{j}\right\|_{2}
. With
H_{0}^{\prime}-H_{0}=X-X=0
, we have,

\displaystyle\left\|H_{j}^{\prime}-H_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}b_{k}\left(\prod_{i=k+1}^{j-1}a_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}\left\|H_{k}\right\|_{F}\left\|\Delta W_{k+1}\right\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=k+1}^{j-1}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}\left\|W_{i+1}+\Delta W_{i+1}\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{j-k}\left\|H_{k}\right\|_{F}\left\|\Delta W_{k+1}\right\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=k+2}^{j}\left\|W_{i}+\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right).

With
Equation

3
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|H_{j}^{\prime}-H_{j}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{j-k}\left(\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{k}\left\|X\right\|_{F}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\left\|\Delta W_{k+1}\right\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=k+2}^{j}\left\|W_{i}+\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|X\right\|_{F}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{j}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\left\|\Delta W_{k+1}\right\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=k+2}^{j}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|X\right\|_{F}\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{j}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{k+1}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{k+1}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{k+1}\right\|_{2}}\left(\prod_{i=k+2}^{j}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{j}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{k+1}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{k+1}\right\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j-k-1}

\displaystyle=\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{j}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{j}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{k}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{k}\right\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{j-k},

(5)

where the last inequality holds since
\left\|W_{i}+\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}+\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\left(1+1/l\right)\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}
by the assumption that
\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}/l
.

Final Bound on the Readout Layer.

\displaystyle\left\|H_{l}^{\prime}-H_{l}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}^{\prime}(W_{l}+\Delta W_{l})-\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}W_{l}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}\left(H_{l-1}^{\prime}-H_{l-1}\right)(W_{l}+\Delta W_{l})+\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}\left(H_{l-1}^{\prime}-H_{l-1}\right)(W_{l}+\Delta W_{l})\right\|_{2}+\left\|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}H_{l-1}\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}\right\|_{2}\left\|\left(H_{l-1}^{\prime}-H_{l-1}\right)\left(W_{l}+\Delta W_{l}\right)\right\|_{2}+\left\|\frac{1}{n}\bm{1}_{n}\right\|_{2}\left\|H_{l-1}\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|H_{l-1}^{\prime}-H_{l-1}\right\|_{F}\left\|W_{l}+\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|H_{l-1}\right\|_{F}\left\|\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}.

Using
Equations

3
and
5
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|H_{l}^{\prime}-H_{l}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|W_{l}+\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{k}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{k}\right\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-k-1}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\left\|X\right\|_{F}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\left[\left\|\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\left\|W_{l}+\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l-1}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{k}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{k}\right\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\left[\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{l}\right\|_{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\frac{\left\|W_{l}+\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{l}\right\|_{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{k}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{k}\right\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-k-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\left[\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{l}\right\|_{2}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{k}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{k}\right\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\left[\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{l}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{l}\right\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-l}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\sum_{k=1}^{l-1}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{k}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{k}\right\|_{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{-k}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e}{\sqrt{n}}\left\|X\right\|_{F}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{k}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{k}\right\|_{2}},

where the last inequality holds since
1\leq\left(1+1/l\right)^{l}\leq e
.

The second step is to bound the change in the robust margin operator using
Lemma

4.3
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Under Assumptions
3.3
and
3.4
, for any
w
and any perturbation
\Delta w=vec\left(\left\{\Delta W_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{l}\right)
such that for all
i\in[l]
,
\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}/l
, the change in the margin operator is bounded as

\displaystyle\left|M\left(f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|\leq 2eB\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}}.

Further, consider the
\epsilon
-attack (
\epsilon>0
), the change in the robust margin operator is bounded as

\displaystyle\left|RM\left(f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-RM\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|\leq 2e(B+\epsilon)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}}.

Proof.

For any
i,j\in[K]
, by the definition of margin operator, we have

\displaystyle\left|M\left(f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right)_{i}-f_{w}\left(G\right)_{i}\right|+\left|f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right)_{j}-f_{w}\left(G\right)_{j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right)-f_{w}\left(G\right)\right\|_{2}.

By
Propositions

A.2
and
4.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left|M\left(f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2e\left\|X\right\|_{2}\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2eB\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}}.

Further, let

\displaystyle G(w)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\arg\inf}_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}\left(f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{i}-f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{j}\right)=\mathop{\arg\inf}_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}M\left(f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right),i,j\right),

and

is the corresponding node features matrix. Denoting

\displaystyle\quad\,\left|RM\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-RM\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\inf_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{\tilde{w}}(G)}M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G^{\prime}\right),i,j\right)-\inf_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}M\left(f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left|M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)\right|,\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.\left|M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2e\max\left\{\left\|X(\tilde{w})\right\|_{2},\left\|X(w)\right\|_{2}\right\}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}}

where the first inequality holds since, by the definition of
G(w)
,

\displaystyle M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)\leq M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right),

\displaystyle M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)\leq M\left(f_{w}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right),

3.3

Finally, the key to prove
Theorem

4.2
using

3.2

, is to construct suitable prior and posterior distributions satisfying the conditions in the lemmas.

Proof of
Theorem

4.2
.

Let
\beta=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right)^{1/l}
. We normalize the weights as
\tilde{W}_{i}=\beta W_{i}/\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}
. Due to the homogeneity of ReLU, we have
f_{w}=f_{\tilde{w}}
. We can also verify that
\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\left\|\tilde{W}_{i}\right\|_{2}
and
\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{F}/\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}=\left\|\tilde{W}_{i}\right\|_{F}/\left\|\tilde{W}_{i}\right\|_{2}
, i.e., the terms appear in the bound stay the same after applying the normalization. Therefore, without loss of generality, we assume that the norm is equal across layers, i.e.,
\forall i,\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}=\beta
.

Consider the prior distribution
P=\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
and the random perturbation
\Delta w\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
. Note that the
\sigma
of the prior and the perturbation distributions are the same and will be set according to
\beta
. More precisely, we will set the
\sigma
based on some approximation
\tilde{\beta}
of
\beta
since the prior distribution
P
cannot depend on any learned weights directly. The approximation
\tilde{\beta}
is chosen from a covering set which covers the meaningful range of
\beta
. For now, let us assume that we have a fixed
\tilde{\beta}
and consider
\beta
which satisfies
|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\beta/l
. Note that

\displaystyle|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l}\beta

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left(1-\frac{1}{l}\right)\beta\leq\tilde{\beta}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)\beta

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left(1-\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1}\beta^{l-1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l-1}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1}\beta^{l-1}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left(1-\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l-1}\beta^{l-1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l-1}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l}\right)^{l}\beta^{l-1}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\frac{1}{e}\beta^{l-1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l-1}\leq e\beta^{l-1},

where the last two inequalities hold since
\left(1-1/l\right)^{l-1}\geq 1/e
and
\left(1+1/l\right)^{l}\leq e
. Indeed, using inequalities
\ln(x)\leq x-1,x>0
and
\ln(x)\geq 1-1/x,x>0
, we can show that
l\ln\left(1+1/l\right)\leq 1
and
(l-1)\ln\left(1-1/l\right)\geq(l-1)\left(1-\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{l}}\right)=-1
.

From
(
Tropp 2012
)

4.2
Generalization Bounds for MPGNN

In this part, we provide the adversarially robust generalization bounds for MPGNN.

Theorem 4.5

(Robust Generalization Bounds for MPGNN)
.

Under Assumptions
3.3
to
3.5
, consider the
\epsilon
-attack (
\epsilon>0
). For any
\delta,\gamma>0
, with probability at least
1-\delta
over the choice of a training set
S=\{z_{1},\dots,z_{m}\}
independently sampled from
\mathcal{D}
, for any
w
, the following results hold:

if
\tau=1
, then

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{D},0}\left(f_{w}\right)\leq\hat{R}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{(l+1)^{4}\Psi_{0}\Psi_{1}(f_{w})+\log(m/\delta)}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right),

if
\tau\neq 1

\displaystyle R_{\mathcal{D},0}\left(f_{w}\right)\leq\hat{R}_{S,\gamma}\left(f_{w}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{l^{2}\Psi_{0}\Psi_{2}(f_{w})+\log(m(l+1)/\delta)}{\gamma^{2}m}}\right).

Here,

,
\xi=LM_{1}M_{2}\frac{\tau^{l-1}-1}{\tau-1},

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}(f_{w})

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\zeta^{-1},\xi^{1/l}\right\}^{2l+2}|w|_{2}^{2}.

In the above results, the bounds vary for two typical settings of

\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-2}l\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)

4.3
Comparison

We compare our results with some existing bounds in the standard settings.
For generalization bounds from both models, we will focus on the
dependency on the depth
l
and width
h
as well as maximum degree
d
of the graph.

For GCN in standard settings,

Liao et al. 2020
derive PAC-Bayesian generalization bounds of order
\mathcal{O}\left(d^{\frac{l-1}{2}}l\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)
, under exactly the same set-up and assumptions in
Theorem

4.1
of this paper. Our derived bounds from
Theorem

4.1
scale as
\mathcal{O}\left(l\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)
. The latter is tighter than the former with a factor of the maximum degree
d
, which is usually much larger than
1
in practice.

For MPGNN in standard settings, the Rademacher complexity bound in
(
Garg et al. 2020
)
scales as
\mathcal{O}\left(h\sqrt{l\log(hd)}\right)
. For PAC-Bayesian framework, the bound in
(
Liao et al. 2020
)
scales as
\mathcal{O}\left(d^{l-2}l\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)
, and the bound in
(
Ju et al. 2023
)
scales as
\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-1}l\sqrt{h}\right)
, while our bound scales as
\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-2}l\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)
. Note that
(
Ju et al. 2023
)
requires that the nonlinear activations and the loss functions are twice-differentiable, Lipschitz-continuous and their first-order
and second-order derivatives are both Lipschitz-continuous, which thus does not apply to the commonly used ReLU activation.
When
P_{G}
is chosen as the adjacency matrix
A
of the graph,
it has been shown that for any undirected graph, the spectral norm of
P_{G}
is less than the maximum degree
d
. See
Section

A.1
for a proof.

We also compare our robust bounds with the bounds on DNN using PAC-Bayesian framework as presented in
(
Xiao et al. 2023
)
. Given that GCNs are essentially a specialized DNNs with a similar structure, our robust bounds align well with those in
(
Xiao et al. 2023
)
.
For MPGNN, the robust bound for
\tau\neq 1
scales as
\mathcal{O}\left(\left\|P_{G}\right\|_{2}^{l-2}l\sqrt{h\log(lh)}\right)
, where
\left\|P_{G}\right\|
is less than or equal to
d
. Compared to DNNs, MPGNNs exhibit a more complex structure, and the graph structure significantly influences their performance. Consequently, our results include the additional term
d^{l}
, which is not present in the bounds for DNNs.

5
Discussion

In this paper, we present adversarially robust generalization bounds for two kinds of GNNs, GCN and MPGNN. Our results in adversarial settings complement the bounds in standard settings. And our results for GCN improve the bounds in
(
Liao et al. 2020
)
by avoiding the dependency on the maximum degree.

Our results are limited to graph classification problem, and cannot be generalized to node classification problem, in which the samples are not independent. We use Gaussian prior and posterior distributions in the analysis, while alternative
distributions could be considered. Additionally, in the analysis, we do not consider the optimization algorithm that has an impact on the leaned parameters.
This paper leads to a few interesting problems for future work: (i) Would the analysis still work for other kinds of GNNs, or other kinds of networks? (ii) How the optimization algorithms like SGD impact the generalization of GNNs?

Proposition A.1
.

For any matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m},B\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times p}
, we have,

\displaystyle\left\|AB\right\|_{F}\leq\left\|A\right\|_{F}\left\|B\right\|_{2}.

Proof.

Proposition A.2
.

For any undirected graph
G=(V,E)
with
n
nodes, let
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be the adjacency matrix,
D=\mathrm{diag}(D_{1},\dots,D_{n})
be the degree matrix, and
d=\max_{i\in[n]}\left\{D_{i}\right\}
be the maximum degree, where
D_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{ij},i\in[n]
. Then we have,

1.

\left\|A\right\|_{2}\leq d
;

2.

\left\|\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\tilde{A}\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\right\|_{2}\leq 1
.

Here
\tilde{A}=A+I
and
\tilde{D}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\tilde{D}_{1},\dots,\tilde{D}_{n}\right)=\mathrm{diag}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{A}_{ij},i\in[n]\right)

Proof.

For (i), by the definition of spectral norm, we have

\displaystyle\left\|A\right\|_{2}=\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1}x^{\top}Ax=\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}x_{i}x_{j}\leq\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1}\sum_{(i,j)\in E}\frac{x_{i}^{2}+x_{j}^{2}}{2}\leq\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1}d\sum_{i\in V}x_{i}^{2}=d.

For (ii), let
\tilde{E}=E\cup\{(i,i)|i\in V\}
be the edge set associated with the adjacency matrix
\tilde{A}
. By the definition of spectral norm, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\tilde{A}\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1}x^{\top}\left(\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\tilde{A}\tilde{D}^{-1/2}\right)x=\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1}\sum_{(i,j)\in\tilde{E}}\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{\sqrt{\tilde{D}_{i}\tilde{D}_{j}}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1}\sum_{(i,j)\in\tilde{E}}\left(\frac{x_{i}^{2}}{2\tilde{D}_{i}}+\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{2\tilde{D}_{j}}\right)\leq\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{2}=1}\sum_{i\in V}x_{i}^{2}=1.\qed

Lemma A.3

Let
X

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2(m-1)X^{2}}\right]\leq 2m.

Proof.

If
m=1

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2(m-1)X^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(e^{2(m-1)X^{2}}\geq u\right)\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(X^{2}\geq\frac{\log u}{2(m-1)}\right)\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(X\geq\sqrt{\frac{\log u}{2(m-1)}}\right)\mathrm{d}u+\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq-\sqrt{\frac{\log u}{2(m-1)}}\right)\mathrm{d}u.

Then,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(X\geq\sqrt{\frac{\log u}{2(m-1)}}\right)\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\mathbb{P}\left(X\geq\sqrt{\frac{\log u}{2(m-1)}}\right)\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(X\geq\sqrt{\frac{\log u}{2(m-1)}}\right)\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq 1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(X\geq\sqrt{\frac{\log u}{2(m-1)}}\right)\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq 1+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-2m\frac{\log u}{2(m-1)}}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle=1+\left(-(m-1)u^{-\frac{1}{m-1}}\Big|_{1}^{\infty}\right)

\displaystyle=m.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(X\leq-\sqrt{\frac{\log u}{2(m-1)}}\right)\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\leq m.

Combing the above analysis, we obtain the desired result.

\displaystyle RM\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G\right),y\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{\tilde{w}}(G)}\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{y}-\max_{j\neq y}f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{j}\right)

\displaystyle RM\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),y\right)

\displaystyle=\inf_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}\left(f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{y}-\max_{j\neq y}f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{j\neq y}RM\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G\right),y,j\right)+\frac{\gamma}{2}

\displaystyle=RM\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G\right),y\right)+\frac{\gamma}{2}.

1-\delta
over the choice of a training set

independently sampled from
\mathcal{D}

Note that

\displaystyle KL\left(Q\|P\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tilde{w}\in S_{w}}Q\log\frac{Q}{P}d\tilde{w}+\int_{\tilde{w}\in S_{w}^{c}}Q\log\frac{Q}{P}d\tilde{w}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z}\left[KL\left(Q\|P\right)-(1-Z)KL\left(\bar{Q}\|P\right)+H(Z)\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2KL\left(Q\|P\right)+2\log(2).

Let

e\eta lLM_{1}M_{2}\dfrac{\tau^{l-1}-1}{\tau-1},&\tau\neq 1.\end{cases}

For any
i,j\in[K]
, by the definition of margin operator, we have

\displaystyle\left|M\left(f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right)_{i}-f_{w}\left(G\right)_{i}\right|+\left|f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right)_{j}-f_{w}\left(G\right)_{j}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right)-f_{w}\left(G\right)\right\|_{2}.

By
Propositions

A.2
and
A.4
, we have

\displaystyle\left|M\left(f_{w+\Delta w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left\|X\right\|_{2}\mathcal{C}\leq 2B\mathcal{C}.

Further, let

\displaystyle G(w)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\arg\inf}_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}\left(f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{i}-f_{w}\left(G^{\prime}\right)_{j}\right)=\mathop{\arg\inf}_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right),

and
X(w)
is the corresponding node features matrix. Denoting

, we have

\displaystyle\quad\,\left|RM\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-RM\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\inf_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{\tilde{w}}(G)}M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-\inf_{G^{\prime}\in\delta_{w}(G)}M\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left|M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)\right|,\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\left.\left|M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq 2\max\left\{\left\|X(\tilde{w})\right\|_{2},\left\|X(w)\right\|_{2}\right\}\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\leq 2(B+\epsilon)\mathcal{C},

where the first inequality holds since, by the definition of
G(w)
,

\displaystyle M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)\leq M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right),

\displaystyle M\left(f_{w}\left(G(w)\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)\leq M\left(f_{w}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right)-M\left(f_{\tilde{w}}\left(G(\tilde{w})\right),i,j\right),

and the last inequality holds from Assumption
3.3
and the definition of
\epsilon
-attack.
∎

We consider the cases of different values of

Case

Let

and

Consider the prior distribution

and the random perturbation distribution

. Note that the
\sigma
of the prior and the perturbation distributions are the same and will be set according to
\beta
. More precisely, we will set the
\sigma
based on some approximation
\tilde{\beta}
of
\beta
since the prior distribution

cannot depend on any learned weights directly. The approximation
\tilde{\beta}
is chosen to be a cover set which covers the meaningful range of
\beta
. For now, let us assume that we have a fix
\tilde{\beta}
and consider
\beta
which satisfies
|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l+2}\beta
. Note that this also implies

\displaystyle|\beta-\tilde{\beta}|\leq\frac{1}{l+2}\beta

\displaystyle\ \Rightarrow\ \left(1-\frac{1}{l+2}\right)\beta\leq\tilde{\beta}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l+2}\right)\beta

\displaystyle\ \Rightarrow\ \left(1-\frac{1}{l+2}\right)^{l+1}\beta^{l+1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l+1}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l+2}\right)^{l+1}\beta^{l+1}

\displaystyle\ \Rightarrow\ \left(1-\frac{1}{l+2}\right)^{l+1}\beta^{l+1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l+1}\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{l+2}\right)^{l+2}\beta^{l+1}

\displaystyle\ \Rightarrow\ \frac{1}{e}\beta^{l+1}\leq\tilde{\beta}^{l+1}\leq e\beta^{l+1}.

Denoting
\Delta W_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{h_{i-1}\times h_{i}},\Delta U_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{h_{0}\times h_{i}}
as
\Delta W
, from
(
Tropp 2012
)
, for
\Delta W\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\left\|\Delta W\right\|_{2}\geq t\right\}\leq 2h\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2h\sigma^{2}}\right).

Taking a union bound, we have

\displaystyle\quad\,\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{l-1}\left\{\left\|\Delta U_{i}\right\|_{2}<t\right\}\cap\bigcap_{i=1}^{l}\left\{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}<t\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=1-\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{l-1}\left\{\left\|\Delta U_{i}\right\|_{2}\geq t\right\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\left\{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\geq t\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1-\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}\mathbb{P}\left\{\left\|\Delta U_{i}\right\|_{2}\geq t\right\}-\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mathbb{P}\left\{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\geq t\right\}

\displaystyle\geq 1-2(2l-1)h\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2h\sigma^{2}}\right).

Setting
2(2l-1)h\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2h\sigma^{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{2}
, we have
t=\sigma\sqrt{2h\log(4(2l-1)h)}
. This implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max\left(\left\{\left\|\Delta U_{i}\right\|_{2}\right\}_{i=1}^{l-1},\left\{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}\right\}_{i=1}^{l}\right)<t\right)\geq\frac{1}{2},

and thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\max\left(\left\{\frac{\left\|\Delta U_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|U_{i}\right\|_{2}}\right\}_{i=1}^{l-1},\left\{\frac{\left\|\Delta W_{i}\right\|_{2}}{\left\|W_{i}\right\|_{2}}\right\}_{i=1}^{l}\right)<\dfrac{t}{\zeta}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}.

Plugging this bound into
Lemma

A.5
, we have that with probability at least
\frac{1}{2}
over
\Delta w
,

\displaystyle\left|R\left(f_{w\Delta w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)-R\left(f_{w}\left(G\right),i,j\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2el(B+\epsilon)LM_{1}M_{2}\dfrac{\tau^{l-1}-1}{\tau-1}\frac{t}{\zeta}

\displaystyle\leq 2el(B+\epsilon)\beta^{l+1}t

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{2}l(B+\epsilon)\tilde{\beta}^{l+1}\sigma\sqrt{2h\log(4(2l-1)h)}\leq\frac{\gamma}{2},

where we set
\sigma=\frac{\gamma}{4e^{2}l(B+\epsilon)\tilde{\beta}^{l+1}\sqrt{2h\log(4(2l-1)h)}}
to get the last inequality. Note that

A.5

(17)

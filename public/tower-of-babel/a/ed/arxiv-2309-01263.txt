Abstract
. We answer an open problem posed by Mossel–Oleszkiewicz–Sen regarding relations between p p -log-Sobolev inequalities for p ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] p\in(0,1] . We show that for any interval I ⊂ ( 0 , 1 ] I\subset(0,1] , there exist q , p ∈ I q,p\in I , q < p q<p , and a measure μ \mu for which the q q -log-Sobolev inequality holds, while the p p -log-Sobolev inequality is violated. As a tool we develop certain necessary and closely related sufficient conditions characterizing those inequalities in the case of birth-death processes on ℕ \mathbb{N} .



1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Background

Functional inequalities are one of the central objects of modern probability theory.
They arise naturally when studying mixing times of Markov chains and are an important tool in proving concentration and hypercontractive estimates.
Arguably, the most prominent examples are the Poincaré and log-Sobolev inequalities since most of the other functional inequalities known in the literature arise either as a modification of one of them (e.g., various modified log-Sobolev inequalities or inequalities with defects) or as a product of some sort of procedure that interpolates between the above two.

An important example of the latter type is a family of Beckner inequalities introduced by Beckner
[
6
]
who showed they hold true in the case of the Gaussian measure.
They were further studied, e.g., by Latała and Oleszkiewicz
[
16
]
who used them to obtain intermediate concentration estimates between subexponential and subgaussian.
Another particular example is a family of
p
-log-Sobolev inequalities introduced by Gross
[
14
]
for
p>1
.
His definition was then extended by Bakry
[
4
]
to any real
p
.
Mossel et al.
[
19
]
studied
p
-log-Sobolev inequalities in the context of reverse hypercontraction.

A vibrant area of research is the study of the relations between various inequalities.
E.g., it is by now classical that the log-Sobolev inequality implies the Poincaré inequality.
Such sort of results can often have far-reaching consequences as demonstrated, e.g., in
[
19
]
, where the authors prove that for
0\leq q<p\leq 2
, the
p
-log-Sobolev inequality is stronger than the
q
-log-Sobolev inequality (cf. Theorem
1.6
below).
They exploit this relation further to prove the reverse hypercontraction for measures satisfying the modified log-Sobolev inequality, which found various applications, cf., e.g.,
[
20
,
15
]
.
A similar situation occurred in
[
1
]
, where the authors proved that the modified log-Sobolev inequality implies a particular family of Beckner inequalities, which allows deriving strong concentration and moment bounds from the modified log-Sobolev inequality.
Both of the mentioned results are examples of positive results (i.e., claiming that one inequality implies the other) and follow from abstract arguments that involve direct comparison of Dirichlet forms.
Similar results can be found in, e.g., the works of Diaconis–Saloff-Coste
[
12
]
or Bobkov–Tetali
[
8
]
.

Another approach needs to be taken in the case of negative results, i.e., when showing that one inequality does not imply another.
E.g., it is classical that the Poincaré inequality does not imply the log-Sobolev inequality.
In that case, one counterexample is the exponential measure which satisfies the Poincaré inequality and does not satisfy the log-Sobolev inequality as the latter implies subgaussian concentration.
To prove such statements, it is often useful to derive some characterization of the functional inequality in question.
These characterizations are often of independent interest, as the conditions they are expressed in are usually much more accessible than a direct proof of the functional inequalities.
Some of the results that provide such characterizations include: the work by Bobkov and Götze
[
7
]
who, by viewing the log-Sobolev inequality in the general framework of Orlicz spaces, characterize the log-Sobolev inequality on
\mathbb{R}
; the work by Miclo
[
18
]
who uses Hardy inequalities on
\mathbb{Z}
to characterize the Poincaré inequality on trees
or the work by Barthe–Roberto
[
5
]
who treat the case of the modified log-Sobolev inequalities on
\mathbb{R}
.

The aim of this note is to answer an open question from
[
19
]
on the relation between
p
-log-Sobolev inequalities for
p\in(0,1]
, cf. Problem
1.7
below.
Our result falls into the category of negative examples described above.
As a byproduct, we develop a sufficient condition and complement it with a closely related necessary condition for
p
-log-Sobolev inequalities, which are of independent interest.
Below we describe our setting and results in more detail.

1.2.
General setup

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{B},\mu)
denote some discrete probability space.
We assume that
\mu
is fully supported on
\Omega

Let

We assume that

P_{t}f(x)=\int_{\Omega}f(y)P(t,x,dy).

It can be then shown that for each

(0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto\frac{1}{2t}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}P(t,x,dy)\mu(dx)

Denote

(1.1)

\calH=\Bigl\{\,f\in L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)\,\colon\,\sup_{t\geq 0}\frac{1}{2t}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}(f(x)-f(y))^{2}P(t,x,dy)\mu(dx)<\infty\,\Bigr\}

to be a domain of the Dirichlet form

associated with this semigroup, given by the formula

(1.2)

\calE(f,g)=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{2t}\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))P(t,x,dy)\mu(dx)

for

.
If

is the infinitesimal generator of the semigroup

with the convergence in the

sense, then for

belonging to the domain
\calH_{L}
of

Definition 1.1

For

2.
Main result

Let
\mu
be a measure on
\mathbb{N}
with full support.
We use the convention that
\mu_{k}=\mu(\{k\})
and
\mu[k,\infty)=\mu([k,\infty))
.
For any
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, denote
Df(k)=f(k+1)-f(k)

Consider the following Dirichlet form

(2.1)

\displaystyle\calE(f,g)=\sum_{k\geq 0}Df(k)Dg(k)\mu_{k}

defined for

f,g\in\{\,h\in L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)\colon\sum_{k\geq 0}(Dh(k))^{2}\mu_{k}<\infty\,\}=:\calH
.
The corresponding birth-death dynamics generator is given by the formula

(2.2)

Lf(k)=Df(k)-{\bf 1}_{\{k>0\}}\frac{\mu_{k-1}}{\mu_{k}}Df(k-1),

so that
\calE(f,g)=-\int fLg\,d\mu
for
f,g\in\calH_{L}\subset\calH
.
A Markov process generated by
L
given by (
2.2
) and Dirichlet form given by (
2.1
) with stationary measure
\mu
being a geometric measure is investigated in
[

]
as an example of a pair
(\mu,\calE)
satisfying the Poincaré inequality (
1.6
) and violating the modified log-Sobolev inequality (
1.4
).

Remark 2.1
.

It follows from the general theory of birth and death processes (see, e.g.,
[

\mathcal{E}
is indeed a Dirichlet form corresponding to a Markov process on

As a consequence, when proving a functional inequality in question it actually suffices to consider simple (i.e., having finitely many jumps) functions.
Therefore, henceforth we will sometimes restrict from specifying particular domains and simply assume that the expressions we introduce are considered for functions for which they are well-defined as one can always restrict the attention to the class of simple functions.

For
x>0

(2.3)

H_{p}(x)=\begin{cases}pp^{\prime}(x^{1/p}-1)(x^{1/p^{\prime}}-1)&\text{if }p\in(0,1),\\

so that

\Ent_{\mu}(f)\leq\frac{C}{4}\sum_{k\geq 0}f(k)H_{p}\bigl(\frac{f(k+1)}{f(k)}\bigr)\mu_{k},

while

-LS(

3.1.
Hardy inequality

In the sequel we put

Definition 3.1
.

We say that a probability measure
\mu

satisfies the Hardy inequality with constant

(3.1)

\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(f(l)-f(0))^{2}\mu_{l}\leq C\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(Df(l))^{2}\mu_{l}=C\calE(f,f)

for all

Define

(3.2)

C_{\mu}=\sup_{k\geq 1}\Bigl\{\mu[k,\infty)\cdot\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{l}}\Bigr\}.

The following result states that
C_{\mu}
characterizes the Hardy inequality (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.2

(Miclo
[
18
]
)
.

The best constant
\hat{C}_{H}
in the Hardy inequality (
3.1
),

(3.3)

\hat{C}_{H}=\sup_{f\in\calH}\Bigl\{\frac{\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(f(l)-f(0))^{2}\mu_{l}}{\calE(f,f)}\colon\calE(f,f)>0\Bigr\},

verifies

C_{\mu}\leq\hat{C}_{H}\leq 4C_{\mu}.

Remark 3.3
.

It is easy to see that for fully supported measures, the Poincaré inequality (
2.6
) is satisfied if and only if the Hardy inequality (
3.1
) is satisfied.
More precisely, the best constant
\hat{C}_{P}
in the Poincaré inequality (
2.6
),

(3.4)

\hat{C}_{P}=2\sup_{f\in\calH}\Bigl\{\frac{\Var_{\mu}(f)}{\calE(f,f)}\colon\calE(f,f)>0\Bigr\},

satisfies

2\mu_{0}\hat{C}_{H}\leq\hat{C}_{P}\leq 2\hat{C}_{H}.

Indeed,
\hat{C}_{P}\leq 2\hat{C}_{H}
follows from the estimate
\Var_{\mu}(f)\leq\int(f-f(0))^{2}\,d\mu
.
To see that
2\mu_{0}\hat{C}_{H}\leq\hat{C}_{P}
, let
f_{\varepsilon}\in\calH
for any
\varepsilon>0
be such that
f_{\varepsilon}(0)=0
,
\calE(f_{\varepsilon},f_{\varepsilon})>0
and
\int f_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\mu\geq(\hat{C}_{H}-\varepsilon)\calE(f_{\varepsilon},f_{\varepsilon})
.
Then, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,
(\int f_{\varepsilon}\,d\mu)^{2}\leq(1-\mu_{0})\int f_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\mu
, whence

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{C}_{P}}{2}\calE(f_{\varepsilon},f_{\varepsilon})\geq\Var_{\mu}(f_{\varepsilon})\geq\mu_{0}\int f_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\mu\geq\mu_{0}(\hat{C}_{H}-\varepsilon)\calE(f_{\varepsilon},f_{\varepsilon}),

and we conclude by taking
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.

The quantity
C_{\mu}
is useful for controlling the tail behavior of
\mu
as demonstrated in the lemmas below.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\mu
is fully supported, then

(3.5)

\sup_{k\geq 0}\frac{\mu[k,\infty)}{\mu_{k}}\leq 1+C_{\mu}

(3.6)

\inf_{k\geq 1}\Bigl\{\frac{\mu[k-1,\infty)}{\mu[k,\infty)}\Bigr\}\geq 1+\frac{1}{C_{\mu}}.

Proof.

The estimate (

) follows from the definition (

) of

.
Using (

), we obtain for any

\displaystyle\mu[k,\infty)

\displaystyle=\mu[k-1,\infty)-\mu_{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq\mu[k-1,\infty)-\frac{\mu[k-1,\infty)}{1+C_{\mu}}=\bigl(1+\frac{1}{C_{\mu}}\bigr)^{-1}\mu[k-1,\infty)

and (

) follows.
∎

Lemma 3.5

If

For the proof of Proposition

Lemma 3.7

For any

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\Bigl[\frac{\log\EE e^{sX_{h}}}{s}\Bigr]&=\frac{\Ent(e^{sX_{h}})}{s^{2}\EE e^{sX_{h}}}\\
&\leq\frac{Cpp^{\prime}}{4s^{2}\EE e^{sX_{h}}}\calE(f_{s,h},g_{s,h})\\
&=\frac{Cpp^{\prime}}{4s^{2}\EE e^{sX_{h}}}\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}\bigl(e^{s(k+1)/p}-e^{sk/p}\bigr)\bigl(e^{s(k+1)/p^{\prime}}-e^{sk/p^{\prime}}\bigr)\mu_{k}\\
&=\frac{Cpp^{\prime}}{4s^{2}}\bigl(e^{s/p}-1\bigr)\bigl(e^{s/p^{\prime}}-1\bigr)\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{h-1}e^{sk/p+sk/p^{\prime}}\mu_{k}}{\EE e^{sX_{h}}}\\
&\leq\frac{Cpp^{\prime}}{4s^{2}}\bigl(e^{s/p}-1\bigr)\bigl(e^{s/p^{\prime}}-1\bigr),\end{split}

where in the last inequality we have also used that
p^{\prime}(e^{s/p^{\prime}}-1)>0
.
By Theorem
1.6
,
\mu
satisfies the Poincaré inequality (
2.6
), whence by Lemma
3.5
,
\EE X<1+C_{\mu}<\infty
, cf. Remark
3.3
.
Consequently, for any
\lambda,h>0
,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\log\EE e^{\lambda X_{h}}}{\lambda}&=\EE X_{h}+\int_{0}^{\lambda}\frac{d}{ds}\bigl[\frac{\log\EE e^{sX_{h}}}{s}\bigr]\,ds\\
&\leq\EE X_{h}+\frac{Cpp^{\prime}}{4}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\frac{\bigl(e^{s/p}-1\bigr)\bigl(e^{s/p^{\prime}}-1\bigr)}{s^{2}}\,ds\\
&\leq\EE X+\frac{Cpp^{\prime}}{4}\int_{0}^{\lambda}\frac{\bigl(e^{s/p}-1\bigr)\bigl(e^{s/p^{\prime}}-1\bigr)}{s^{2}}\,ds<\infty.\end{split}

Taking
h\to+\infty
and using monotone convergence theorem in (
3.10
), we obtain that for any
\lambda>0
,
\EE e^{\lambda X}<\infty
.
Therefore, we can repeat reasoning from (
3.9
) to get that for any
s>0
,

(3.11)

\frac{d}{ds}\Bigl[\frac{\log\EE e^{sX}}{s}\Bigr]\leq\frac{Cpp^{\prime}}{4s^{2}}\bigl(e^{s/p}-1\bigr)\bigl(e^{s/p^{\prime}}-1\bigr).

For any
\lambda>1
, by (
3.11
), estimating
1-e^{s/p^{\prime}}\leq 1
for
s>1
and using Lemma
3.7
,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\lambda}\frac{d}{ds}\bigl[\frac{\log\EE e^{sX}}{s}\bigr]\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cpp^{\prime}}{4}\int_{1}^{\lambda}\frac{(e^{s/p}-1)(e^{s/p^{\prime}}-1)}{s^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cp(-p^{\prime})}{4}\int_{1}^{\lambda}\frac{e^{s/p}-1}{s^{2}}\,ds\leq\frac{Cpp^{\prime 2}}{4\lambda}e^{\lambda/p},

whence, for
t>e^{1/p}-1
, by the Chernoff bound and setting
\lambda=p\log(t+1)>1
,

\displaystyle\log\bigl(\PP(X\geq t)\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq\log\EE\exp(-\lambda t+\lambda X)

\displaystyle=-\lambda t+\lambda\log\EE e^{X}+\lambda\int_{1}^{\lambda}\frac{d}{ds}\bigl[\frac{\log\EE e^{sX}}{s}\bigr]\,ds

\displaystyle\leq-\lambda t+\lambda\log\EE e^{X}+\frac{Cpp^{\prime 2}}{4}e^{\lambda/p}

\displaystyle=-pt\log(t+1)+p(\log\EE e^{X})\log(t+1)+\frac{Cpp^{\prime 2}}{4}(t+1)

\displaystyle=-\Bigl(1-\frac{\log\EE e^{X}}{t}-\frac{Cp^{\prime 2}(t+1)}{4t\log(t+1)}\Bigr)tp\log(t+1).

Therefore, for any
p\in(0,1)
and

(3.12)

\varepsilon_{p}(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\quad t\leq e^{1/p}-1,\\
\min\bigl(1,\frac{\log\EE e^{X}}{t}+\frac{C}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{(1/p-1)^{2}}\cdot\frac{t+1}{t\log(t+1)}\bigr)&\text{if}\quad t>e^{1/p}-1,\end{cases}

the estimate (
3.7
) holds for any
t\geq 0
as desired.

We turn to the case
p=1
.
Recall that by Theorem
1.6
, for any
p\in(0,1)
the 1-log-Sobolev inequality implies the
p
-log-Sobolev inequality with the same constant.
Let
\varepsilon_{p}
be given by (
3.12
) and let
\varphi\colon(0,\infty)\to(0,1)
be such that
\varphi(t)\to 1
and
\varepsilon_{\varphi(t)}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
(one can take, e.g.,
\varphi(t)=0.5
for
t\leq e^{e}
and
\varphi(t)=(1+1/\log\log t)^{-1}
for
t>e^{e}
).
For
t\in(0,\infty)
, set

\varepsilon_{1}(t)=1-\varphi(t)\bigl(1-\varepsilon_{\varphi(t)}(t)\bigr).

Then
\varepsilon_{1}(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
and for any
t>0
, we apply (
3.7
) with
p=\varphi(t)
to obtain the conclusion.
∎

3.3.
Reduction to increasing functions

The proposition below is an adaptation of
[
5
, Proposition 3]
to the discrete case.
The proof goes along similar lines – we present it for completeness.

Proposition 3.8
.

Recall the definition (
3.2
) of
C_{\mu}
and assume
C_{\mu}<\infty
.
For any
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with a finite number of jumps and for any
\rho>1
,

\Ent_{\mu}(f)\leq 16C_{\mu}(1+\sqrt{\rho})^{2}\calE(\sqrt{f},\sqrt{f})+\sum_{k\colon g(k)\geq\rho\EE_{\mu}g}g(k)\log\bigl(\frac{g(k)}{\EE_{\mu}g}\bigr)\mu_{k},

where

g(n)=f(0)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(Df(k))_{+}.

Proof.

We use the assumption that
f
has a finite number of jumps to make sure that every quantity below is well-defined.
Note that
g\geq f
since
(Df(k))_{+}\geq Df(k)
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
x,t>0
, denote
\psi(t,x)=x\log\frac{x}{t}-(x-t)
,
\phi(x)=x\log x
and set

\Theta=\{n\in\mathbb{N}\colon f(n)\geq\rho\EE g\}
.
Convexity of
\phi
implies that
\phi(\EE_{\mu}f)\geq\phi(\EE_{\mu}g)+\phi^{\prime}(\EE_{\mu}g)(\EE_{\mu}f-\EE_{\mu}g)
, so that

(3.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Ent(f)&=\EE_{\mu}\bigl[\phi(f)-\phi(\EE_{\mu}f)\bigr]\\
&\leq\EE_{\mu}\bigl[\phi(f)-\phi(\EE_{\mu}g)+\phi^{\prime}(\EE_{\mu}g)\cdot(\EE_{\mu}g-\EE_{\mu}f)\bigr]=\EE_{\mu}\psi(\EE_{\mu}g,f).\end{split}

Set
U_{\rho}=(1+\sqrt{\rho})^{2}
.
Since for
x\in[0,\rho t]
,

\displaystyle\psi(t,x)\leq x\Bigl(\frac{x}{t}-1\Bigr)-(x-t)

\displaystyle=t\Bigl(\frac{x}{t}-1\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(1+\sqrt{\frac{x}{t}}\Bigr)^{2}(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{t})^{2}\leq U_{\rho}(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{t})^{2},

we obtain

(3.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\EE_{\mu}\psi(\EE_{\mu}g,f){\bf 1}_{\Theta^{c}}&\leq U_{\rho}\EE_{\mu}(\sqrt{f}-\sqrt{\EE_{\mu}g})^{2}\\
&\leq 2U_{\rho}\bigl(\EE_{\mu}(\sqrt{f}-\sqrt{g})^{2}+\EE_{\mu}(\sqrt{g}-\sqrt{\EE_{\mu}g})^{2}\bigr)\\
&\leq 2U_{\rho}\bigl(\EE_{\mu}(\sqrt{f}-\sqrt{g})^{2}+2\Var_{\mu}(\sqrt{g})\bigr).\end{split}

Let
\hat{C}_{H}
and
\hat{C}_{P}
be the best constants in the Hardy inequality (
3.1
) and the Poincaré inequality (
2.6
) respectively, cf. the definitions (
3.3
) and (
3.4
).
By Theorem
3.2
and as indicated in Remark
3.3
,
\hat{C}_{P}\leq 2\hat{C}_{H}\leq 8C_{\mu}
.
Recall that
\sqrt{f(0)}=\sqrt{g(0)}
, whence the RHS of (
3.14
) can be estimated from above by

2U_{\rho}\bigl(4C_{\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(D\sqrt{f}-D\sqrt{g})^{2}(k)\mu_{k}+8C_{\mu}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(D\sqrt{g})^{2}(k)\mu_{k}\bigr)\leq 16C_{\mu}U_{\rho}\calE(\sqrt{f},\sqrt{f}),

where we have used the fact that
4(D\sqrt{f}-D\sqrt{g})^{2}+8(D\sqrt{g})^{2}\leq 8(D\sqrt{f})^{2}
, which follows from the pointwise estimate
0\leq D\sqrt{g}\leq(D\sqrt{f})_{+}
and the fact that for a fixed
y
, the convex mapping
x\mapsto 4(y-x)^{2}+8x^{2}
on a closed interval admits a maximum at an end of this interval.

Turning to the remaining part of the RHS of (
3.13
), we get

\displaystyle\EE_{\mu}\psi(\EE g,f){\bf 1}_{\Theta}

\displaystyle=\EE_{\mu}\bigl[f\log\frac{f}{\EE_{\mu}g}-(f-\EE_{\mu}g)\bigr]{\bf 1}_{\Theta}

\displaystyle\leq\EE_{\mu}\bigl[g\log\frac{g}{\EE_{\mu}g}\bigr]{\bf 1}_{\Theta}\leq\sum_{k\colon g(k)\geq\rho\EE_{\mu}g}g(k)\log\frac{g(k)}{\EE_{\mu}g}\mu_{k},

where in both inequalities we have used the definition of
\Theta
and the facts that
f\leq g
and
\rho>1
.
Combining all the above estimates yields the conclusion.
∎

When proving that some
p
-log-Sobolev inequality is satisfied, Proposition
3.8
allows us to restrict our attention to a special subclass of functions from
\calH_{+}
.
This idea is formalized in the corollary below.

Corollary 3.9
.

If
C_{\mu}<\infty
and

(3.15)

\sum_{k\colon g(k)\geq\rho\EE_{\mu}g}g(k)\log\bigl(\frac{g(k)}{\EE_{\mu}g}\bigr)\mu_{k}\leq C_{\rho}\calE_{p}(g)

for some
\rho>1
,
p\in(0,2]
, some constant
C_{\rho}>0
and any non-decreasing function
g\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with a finite number of jumps,
then
\mu
satisfies the
p
-log-Sobolev inequality (
2.5
) with constant

C=64C_{\mu}(1+\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+4C_{\rho}.

Proof.

By applying Proposition
3.8
together with condition (
3.15
), we get that for any
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with a finite number of jumps

(3.16)

\Ent_{\mu}(f)\leq 16C_{\mu}(1+\sqrt{\rho})^{2}\calE(\sqrt{f},\sqrt{f})+C_{\rho}\calE_{p}(g),

where
g(0)=f(0)
and
g(n)=f(0)+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(Df(k))_{+}
for
n\geq 1
.

Note that
f\leq g
by definition and that for any
x,\Delta>0
, the mapping
x\mapsto xH_{p}(1+\Delta/x)
is non-increasing, which follows from the convexity of
H_{p}
on
[1,\infty)
, cf. Lemma
3.10
,
(i)
below, and the fact that
H_{p}(1)=0
.
Using that
H_{p}\geq 0
and monotonicity of
x\mapsto xH_{p}(1+\Delta/x)
, we obtain

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\calE_{p}(f)&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f(k)H_{p}\Bigl(1+\frac{Df(k)}{f(k)}\Bigr)\mu_{k}\\
&\geq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f(k)H_{p}\Bigl(1+\frac{Df(k)}{f(k)}\Bigr)\mu_{k}{\bf 1}_{\{Df(k)>0\}}\\
&\geq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}g(k)H_{p}\Bigl(1+\frac{Df(k)}{g(k)}\Bigr)\mu_{k}{\bf 1}_{\{Df(k)>0\}}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}g(k)H_{p}\Bigl(1+\frac{Dg(k)}{g(k)}\Bigr)\mu_{k}=\calE_{p}(g).\end{split}

By Proposition
1.4
,
\calE(\sqrt{f},\sqrt{f})\leq\calE_{2}(f)\leq\calE_{p}(f)
for any
p\in(0,2]
.
Therefore, combining (
3.16
) and (
3.17
), we obtain that for any
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with a finite number of jumps,

(3.18)

\Ent_{\mu}(f)\leq\bigl(16C_{\mu}(1+\sqrt{\rho})^{2}+C_{\rho}\bigr)\calE_{p}(f).

We conclude the result for arbitrary
f
by the monotone convergence theorem (applied to the RHS of (
3.18
)) and Fatou’s lemma (applied to the LHS of (
3.18
)).
∎

3.4.
Technical lemmas

Recall the definition (
2.3
) of
H_{p}
and note that for
p\in(0,1)
and
x>0
,
H_{p}(x)=pp^{\prime}(x-x^{1/p}-x^{1/p^{\prime}}+1)
.

Lemma 3.10
.

For any
p\in(0,1]
, the following properties are true:

(i)

H_{p}
is increasing and convex on
[1,\infty)
;

(ii)

H_{p}(x)\geq(\log x)^{2}
for
x\geq 1
.

If additionally
x\geq\lambda
for some
\lambda>1
, then

(iii)

H_{p}(x)\geq(x\log x)\cdot\min\bigl\{\frac{H_{p}(\lambda)}{\lambda\log\lambda},\frac{H^{\prime}_{p}(\lambda)}{1+\log\lambda},1\bigr\}
;

(iv)

H_{p}(x)\geq H_{p}(xc)\cdot\min\bigl\{\frac{H_{p}(\lambda)}{H_{p}(\lambda c)},\frac{H_{p}^{\prime}(\lambda)}{cH_{p}^{\prime}(\lambda c)},c^{-1/p}\bigr\}

for any
c>1
;

(v)

\frac{H_{p}(y)}{y}\leq\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\frac{H_{p}(x)}{x}

for any
\lambda\leq y\leq x
.

Proof.

We start with
(i)
and
(ii)
.

For
p=1
,
H_{1}(x)=(x-1)\log x
,
H_{1}(1)=H_{1}^{\prime}(1)=0
and
H_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{1}{x^{2}}+\frac{1}{x}>0
, yielding
(i)
, while
(ii)
follows immediately as
\log x<x-1
.

For
p\in(0,1)
, denote
h(x)=(\log x)^{2}
.
Then,
H_{p}(1)=H_{p}^{\prime}(1)=h(1)=h^{\prime}(1)=0
and, using AM-GM inequality,

(3.19)

H^{\prime\prime}_{p}(x)=\frac{x^{1/p}+x^{1/p^{\prime}}}{x^{2}}\geq\frac{2x^{1/2p+1/2p^{\prime}}}{x^{2}}\geq\frac{2}{x^{2}}\geq 2\frac{1-\log x}{x^{2}}=h^{\prime\prime}(x),

whence
(ii)
follows.
Moreover, (
3.19
) implies that
H_{p}^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0
, yielding also
(i)
.

To see
(iii)
, denote the function on its RHS by
\tilde{h}(x)
and note that by the definition of
\tilde{h}
,
H_{p}(\lambda)\geq\tilde{h}(\lambda)
and
H_{p}^{\prime}(\lambda)\geq\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\lambda)
.
Since for any
x\geq 1
,

H^{\prime\prime}_{p}(x)=\frac{x^{1/p}+x^{1/p^{\prime}}}{x^{2}}\geq x^{1/p-2}\geq\frac{1}{x}=(x\log x)^{\prime\prime},

then also
H^{\prime\prime}_{p}(x)\geq\tilde{h}^{\prime\prime}(x)
for any
x\geq\lambda
and
(iii)
follows (note that the calculation above also covers the case
p=1
, as then
1/p^{\prime}=0
).

Similarly, if
\tilde{h}(x)
is the RHS of
(iv)
, then
H_{p}(\lambda)\geq\tilde{h}(\lambda)
and
H_{p}^{\prime}(\lambda)\geq\tilde{h}^{\prime}(\lambda)
by the definition of
\tilde{h}
.
As

H^{\prime\prime}_{p}(x)=\frac{x^{1/p}+x^{1/p^{\prime}}}{x^{2}}\geq c^{-1/p}\cdot c^{2}\frac{(cx)^{1/p}+(cx)^{1/p^{\prime}}}{(cx)^{2}}=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\bigl(c^{-1/p}H_{p}(cx)\bigr),

it follows that
H_{p}^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq\tilde{h}^{\prime\prime}(x)
for any
x\geq\lambda
, yielding
(iv)
.

Finally, since
H_{p}(1)=0
and
H_{p}
is convex by
(i)
, we have for any
\lambda\leq y\leq x
,

\frac{H_{p}(y)}{y}\leq\frac{H_{p}(y)}{y-1}\leq\frac{H_{p}(x)}{x-1}\leq\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}\frac{H_{p}(x)}{x}

yielding
(v)
.
∎

For
1<\rho\leq x
and
k\geq 1
, denote

(3.20)

\alpha_{x,\rho}(k)=\inf\Bigl\{\,\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}H_{p}\bigl(\frac{g_{s+1}}{g_{s}}\bigr)\mu_{s}\;:\;g_{0}=1\leq\ldots\leq g_{k-1}<\rho\leq x\leq g_{k}\,\Bigr\}.

This quantity plays a crucial role in providing sufficient condition for the
p
-log-Sobolev inequalities in Theorem
2.3
.
Its definition is partially inspired by an analogous quantity defined in
[
5
]
in the continuous setting.

Lemma 3.11
.

For any
1<\rho\leq x
and
k\geq 1
,

(3.21)

\alpha_{x,\rho}(k)\geq\Bigl[{\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\mu_{s}^{-1}}\Bigr]^{-1}\cdot{(\log x)^{2}}

and

(3.22)

\alpha_{x,\rho}(k)\geq H_{p}(x\rho^{-1})\mu_{k-1}.

Proof.

We start with (
3.21
).
By Lemma
3.10
,
(ii)
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{x,\rho}(k)

\displaystyle\geq\inf\Bigl\{\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\Bigl(\log\frac{g_{s+1}}{g_{s}}\Bigr)^{2}\mu_{s}\;:\;g_{0}=1\leq\ldots\leq g_{k-1}<\rho\leq x\leq g_{k}\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\Bigl\{\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{s}^{2}\mu_{s}\;:\;\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{s}\geq\log x\Bigr\}

\displaystyle\geq\inf\Bigl\{\Bigl[\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{s}\Bigr]^{2}\Bigl[{\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\mu_{s}^{-1}}\Bigr]^{-1}\;:\;\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\lambda_{s}\geq\log x\Bigr\}=\Bigl[{\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}\mu_{s}^{-1}}\Bigr]^{-1}\cdot(\log x)^{2},

where in the last estimate we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

We turn to (
3.22
).
By Lemma
3.10
,
H_{p}
is non-negative and increasing on
[1,\infty)
, whence

\displaystyle\alpha_{x.\rho}(k)

\displaystyle\geq\inf\Bigl\{\,H_{p}\bigl(\frac{g_{k}}{g_{k-1}}\bigr)\mu_{k-1}\;:\;1\leq g_{k-1}<\rho\leq x\leq g_{k}\,\Bigr\}=H_{p}(x\rho^{-1})\mu_{k-1}

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2.3

4.1.
Sufficient condition

Fix
p\in(0,1]
and assume that
\mu
satisfies the Poincaré inequality (
2.6
) with constant
C_{P}<\infty
and that
\hat{C}<\infty
, i.e., the condition (
2.7
) holds.
Recall the definition (
3.2
) of
C_{\mu}
and that for fully supported measures,
C_{\mu}\leq C_{P}/2\mu_{0}<\infty
, cf. Theorem
3.2
and Remark
3.3
.
We show that
\mu
satisfies the
p
-log-Sobolev inequality (
2.5
) with constant
C
bounded from above by a quantity depending on
C_{\mu}
and
\hat{C}
only.

Define
\rho
as

(4.1)

\rho=\min\Bigl(\bigl(\frac{1+C_{\mu}}{C_{\mu}}\bigr)^{1/3},2\Bigr).

By Corollary
3.9
, it suffices to show that for any non-decreasing function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with a finite number of jumps,

(4.2)

\sum_{k\colon f(k)\geq\rho\EE f}f(k)\log\big(\frac{f(k)}{\EE_{\mu}f}\big)\mu_{k}\leq C_{\rho}\calE_{p}(f)=C_{\rho}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f(k)H_{p}\bigl(\frac{f(k+1)}{f(k)}\bigr)\mu_{k}

for some constant
C_{\rho}>0
independent of
f
.
By the homogeneity of (
4.2
), we can assume that
f(0)=1
.
Let us consider such
f
and denote

\displaystyle\tau_{0}=0,\quad\tau_{k}=\inf\{\,l>\tau_{k-1}\colon f(l)\geq\rho f(\tau_{k-1})\,\}

for
k\geq 1
.
Since
f
has a finite number of jumps, then there exists
M\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that
\tau_{M-1}<\tau_{M}=\infty
.
If
M=1
, then the LHS of (
4.2
) equals zero as
\EE f\geq f(0)=1
and (
4.2
) holds with any
C_{\rho}>0
.
Assume therefore from now on that
M>1
.
For
k\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\}
, denote

\gamma_{k}=\rho f(\tau_{k})\log_{+}\big(\frac{\rho f(\tau_{k})}{\EE_{\mu}f}\big)\mu[\tau_{k},\infty),

where
\log_{+}(x):=\max(\log x,0)

and let

\delta_{k}=f(\tau_{k-1})\sum_{l=\tau_{k-1}}^{\tau_{k}-1}H_{p}\bigl(\frac{f(l+1)}{f(l)}\bigr)\mu_{l}.

Since
\EE f\geq f(0)=1
and as for
k\geq 1
and
l\in[\tau_{k},\tau_{k+1})

f(\tau_{k})\leq f(l)<\rho f(\tau_{k})

\displaystyle\sum_{k\colon f(k)\geq\rho\EE_{\mu}f}f(k)\log\big(\frac{f(k)}{\EE_{\mu}f}\big)\mu_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\colon f(k)\geq\rho}f(k)\log_{+}\big(\frac{f(k)}{\EE_{\mu}f}\big)\mu_{k}

and as

and by the monotonicity of
f
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}f(k)H_{p}\bigl(\frac{f(k+1)}{f(k)}\bigr)\mu_{k}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{M-1}\delta_{k}.

Therefore, to prove (
4.2
), it suffices to show that

(4.3)

\sum_{k=1}^{M-1}\gamma_{k}\leq C_{\rho}\sum_{k=1}^{M-1}\delta_{k}.

Recall that
H_{p}(1)=0
,
H_{p}\geq 0
and recall the definition (
3.20
) of
\alpha_{x,\rho}
.
Consider
g_{l}=1
for

and
g_{l}=f(l)/f(\tau_{k-1})
for
l=\tau_{k-1}+1,\ldots,\tau_{k}
.
Then, by the definition of
\delta_{k}
, we have for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,M-1\}
,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{f(\tau_{k})/f(\tau_{k-1}),\rho}(\tau_{k})\leq\delta_{k}/f(\tau_{k-1}).\end{split}

For
k=1
, using the monotonicity of
f
, estimate (
4.4
) and the fact that
f(\tau_{0})=1<\rho\leq f(\tau_{1})
, we get

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\gamma_{1}&\leq\rho f(\tau_{1})\log_{+}\big(\frac{\rho f(\tau_{1})}{\mu[0,\tau_{1})+f(\tau_{1})\mu[\tau_{1},\infty)}\big)\mu[\tau_{1},\infty)\cdot\frac{\delta_{1}}{\delta_{1}}\\

4.2.

\displaystyle\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{M}k\log k

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{M}\tau_{k}\log\tau_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{cp}\sum_{k=k_{0}}^{M}\log\bigl(\frac{1}{\mu[\tau_{k},\infty)}\bigr)\leq\tilde{c}\cdot(M+1)\log(M+1)

for

5.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

As a counterexample we will take the Conway–Maxwell–Poisson distribution with parameter
\nu>0
, defined as
\mu_{\nu}(k)=\frac{1}{Z_{\nu}}(k!)^{-\nu}
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, where

is the normalizing constant, cf.
[
10
]
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, using the estimate

valid for

we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mu_{\nu}(\{n\})\leq\mu_{\nu}[n,\infty)&=\frac{1}{Z_{\nu}}\sum_{k\geq n}\frac{1}{(k!)^{\nu}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{Z_{\nu}(n!)^{\nu}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{(k!)^{\nu}}=\frac{1}{(n!)^{\nu}}=Z_{\nu}\mu_{\nu}(\{n\}).\end{split}

Recall the definition (
3.2
) of
C_{\mu}
.
Using (
5.2
) and (
5.1
), we obtain for

,

\displaystyle\mu_{\nu}[n,\infty)\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{\nu}(\{k\})}\leq Z_{\nu}\mu_{\nu}(\{n\})\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\mu_{\nu}(\{k\})}=Z_{\nu}\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(k!)^{\nu}}{(n!)^{\nu}}\leq Z_{\nu}^{2},

whence

and thus
\mu_{\nu}
satisfies the Poincaré inequality (

) with constant

, cf. Theorem
3.2
and Remark

We first show that for any
\nu\in(0,1]

verifies the

-log-Sobolev inequality (

) for any

.
Fix some

By the first part of Theorem
2.3
, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{n^{\nu}}{Z_{\nu}}=\frac{\mu_{\nu}(\{n-1\})}{\mu_{\nu}(\{n\})}\frac{1}{Z_{\nu}}&\leq\frac{\mu_{\nu}(\{n-1\})}{\mu_{\nu}[n,\infty)}\\

Combining (

Finally, we show that for any

We choose

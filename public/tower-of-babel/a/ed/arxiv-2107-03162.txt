Distance covariance for random fields
Muneya Matsui, Thomas Mikosch, Rasool Roozegar, Laleh Tafakori

Abstract
. We study an independence test based on distance correlation for random fields ( X , Y ) (X,Y) . We consider the situations when ( X , Y ) (X,Y) is observed on a lattice with equidistant grid sizes and when ( X , Y ) (X,Y) is observed at random locations. We provide asymptotic theory for the sample distance correlation in both situations and show bootstrap consistency. The latter fact allows one to build a test for independence of X X and Y Y based on the considered discretizations of these fields. We illustrate the performance of the bootstrap test by simulations, and apply the test to Japanese meteorological data observed over the entire area of Japan. Keywords: Empirical characteristic function, distance covariance, random field, independence test

1.
Introduction to model

1.1.
Distance covariance in Euclidean space and literature review

It is well known that two
q
- and
r
-dimensional random vectors
{\bf X}
and

{\bf Y}
, respectively, are independent if and only if their joint characteristic function factorizes, i.e.,

\displaystyle\varphi_{{\bf X},{\bf Y}}({\bf s},{\bf t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp(i\,{\bf s}^{\top}{\bf X}+i{\bf t}^{\top}{\bf Y})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp(i\,{\bf s}^{\top}{\bf X})\big]\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\exp(i\,{\bf t}^{\top}{\bf Y})\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varphi_{\bf X}({\bf s})\,\varphi_{\bf Y}({\bf t})\,,\qquad{\bf s}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{q}\,,{\bf t}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{r}\,.

However, this identity is difficult to check if one has data
at the disposal; a replacement of the corresponding characteristic functions
by empirical versions does not lead to powerful statistical tools for
detecting independence between
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
.
In the univariate case, Klebanov and Zinger

[
21
]
, Feuerverger
[
14
]
, and later Székely et al.
[
33
,
30
,
31
,
32
]
in the general multivariate case recommended to
use a weighted
L^{2}
-distance between
\varphi_{{\bf X},{\bf Y}}
and

\varphi_{\bf X}\,\varphi_{\bf Y}
: for
\beta\in(0,2)
, the
distance covariance between
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
is given by

\displaystyle T_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf Y})=c_{q}c_{r}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{q+r}}\big|\varphi_{{\bf X},{\bf Y}}({\bf s},{\bf t})-\varphi_{\bf X}({\bf s})\varphi_{\bf Y}({\bf t})\big|^{2}|{\bf s}|^{-(q+\beta)}|{\bf t}|^{-(r+\beta)}\,d{\bf s}\,d{\bf t}\,,

where the constants
c_{d}
for
d\geq 1
are chosen such that

(1.1)

\displaystyle c_{d}\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(1-\cos({\bf s}^{\prime}{\bf x}))\,|{\bf x}|^{-(d+\beta)}d{\bf x}=|{\bf s}|^{\beta}\,.

Here and in what follows, we suppress the dependence of the
Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
on the dimension; it will always be clear
from the context what the dimension is.

The quantity
T_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf Y})
is finite under suitable moment conditions on
{\bf X},{\bf Y}
.
The corresponding
distance correlation
is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle R_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf Y})=\dfrac{T_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf Y})}{\sqrt{T_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf X})}\sqrt{T_{\beta}({\bf Y},{\bf Y})}}\,.

Of course,
{\bf X}
and
{\bf Y}
are independent if and only if
R_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf Y})=T_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf Y})=0
.

Thanks to the choice of the weight function
|{\bf s}|^{-(q+\beta)}|{\bf t}|^{-(r+\beta)}

and (
1.1
),

T_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf Y})
has an explicit form: assuming that
({\bf X}_{i},{\bf Y}_{i})
,
i=1,2,\ldots,
are iid copies of
({\bf X},{\bf Y})
, we have

(1.3)

\displaystyle T_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|{\bf X}_{1}-{\bf X}_{2}|^{\beta}|{\bf Y}_{1}-{\bf Y}_{2}|^{\beta}]+{\mathbb{E}}[|{\bf X}_{1}-{\bf X}_{2}|^{\beta}]{\mathbb{E}}[|{\bf Y}_{1}-{\bf Y}_{2}|^{\beta}]

\displaystyle-2\,{\mathbb{E}}[|{\bf X}_{1}-{\bf X}_{2}|^{\beta}|{\bf Y}_{1}-{\bf Y}_{3}|^{\beta}]\,,

and
R_{\beta}(c{\bf X},c{\bf Y})=R_{\beta}({\bf X},{\bf Y})
for
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e.,
R_{\beta}

is scale-invariant.

The definition already points at some
nice properties of the distance covariance/correlation. It vanishes
if and only if
{\bf X},{\bf Y}
are independent, hence one can capture
arbitrary dependence structures such as non-linear and non-monotone onces.
By a handy choice of the weight function as in (
1.1
)
an explicit interpretation through moments is
available. It translates to the empirical moments (cf. (
1.9
),(
1.10
)) which can be easily calculated. Moreover, the empirical distance covariance
is approximated by a (degenerate)
U
-statistic whose theoretical properties
have been studied in great detail in the literature.
Due to the aforementioned advantages the empirical distance correlation
has found applications in a rather wide area where dependence modeling is crucial: time series
[
36
,
8
,
16
]
,
functional time series
[
17
,
24
]
, continuous-time stochastic processes
[
23
,
9
]
.
Moreover, taking advantage of the arbitrariness of dimensions,
applications in high-dimensional data have been studied intensively in recent years; see

[
31
,
34
,
15
,
35
]
.

There are numerous other applications of distance correlation
in addition to those mentioned above. However, to the best of our knowledge,
applications to continuous-parameter random fields (which have
infinite dimension) have not been considered.
Some work has been conducted on independence tests for continuous-parameter
random fields, for example

[
18
]
, where a high-dimensional
case is tractable, but a dimension reduction approach was used. In this paper we consider an independence test based
on distance correlation for random fields, exploiting
the arbitrariness of dimensions and making use of techniques developed for
tests on stochastic processes.
Our approach will be clarified in the following text.

1.2.
Distance covariance for
random fields on a lattice in
[0,1]^{d}

Székely et al.
[
31
]
showed that distance
correlation fails for high-dimensional vectors
{\bf X},{\bf Y}
if
their components are independent. Therefore Matsui et al.
[
23
]

and Dehling et al.
[
9
]

required some dependence structure on the components. They
extended distance covariance and distance correlation to stochastic
processes. In particular,
[
9
]
studied discretizations of stochastic processes
X,Y
on
[0,1]
.
Instead of using the vectors

(X(t_{i}))_{i=1,\ldots,p}
and
(Y(t_{i}))_{i=1,\ldots,p}
for
partitions
t_{0}=0<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{p}=1
,
\Delta_{i}=(t_{i-1},t_{i}]
such that
p=p_{n}\to\infty
and

\delta_{n}=\max_{i}|\Delta_{i}|=\max_{i}(t_{i}-t_{i-1})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, one introduces the
weighted vectors

(1.4)

\displaystyle{\bf X}^{(p)}=\big(\sqrt{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}\,X(t_{i}))_{i=1,\ldots,p}\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\bf Y}^{(p)}=\big(\sqrt{t_{i}-t_{i-1}}\,Y(t_{i}))_{i=1,\ldots,p}.

Here we introduce a direct analog of this approach to
[0,1]^{d}

for
d>1
.
We assume that any random field

Z=(Z({\bf u}))_{{\bf u}\in[0,1]^{d}}
of interest is observed on a
lattice with constant mesh size. We start by partitioning

[0,1]
into equidistant points

t_{i}=i/q
,
i=0,1,\ldots,q
, for some positive integer
q
. From them we construct a
lattice in
[0,1]^{d}
via the points

\displaystyle{\bf t}_{{\bf i}}=(t_{i_{1}},\ldots,t_{i_{d}}),\qquad{\bf i}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{d})\in\{0,1,\ldots,q\}^{d}\,.

We have
p:=q^{d}
lattice points in
(0,1]^{d}
.
We discretize
Z
on the cells
\Delta_{{\bf i}}=({\bf t}_{{\bf i}-{\mathbf{1}}},{\bf t}_{\bf i}]

with volume

(1.5)

\displaystyle|\Delta|=|\Delta_{{\bf i}}|=p^{-1}\,.

Consider the vector

\displaystyle{\bf Z}_{p}=\big(|\Delta|^{1/2}Z({\bf t}_{{\bf i}})\big)\,,\qquad{\bf i}\in\Pi_{d}=\{1,\ldots,q\}^{d}\,,

and a step-function approximation to
Z
:

(1.6)

\displaystyle Z^{(p)}({\bf t})=\sum_{{\bf i}\in\Pi_{d}}Z({\bf t}_{\bf i}){\mathbf{1}}({\bf t}\in\Delta_{\bf i}),\qquad{\bf t}\in[0,1]^{d}\,.

For a measurable bounded square-integrable field

Z
on
[0,1]^{d}
we have for a.e. sample path

(1.7)

\displaystyle|{\bf Z}_{p}|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{p}\,\sum_{{\bf i}\in\Pi_{d}}Z^{2}({\bf t}_{\bf i})=\sum_{{\bf i}\in\Pi_{d}}Z^{2}({\bf t}_{{\bf i}})\,|\Delta_{{\bf i}}|=\|Z^{(p)}\|^{2}_{2}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{d}}Z^{2}({\bf t})\,d{\bf t}=\|Z\|_{2}^{2}\,,\qquad p\to\infty,

where
\|\cdot\|_{2}
denotes the
L_{2}
-norm on
[0,1]^{d}
.
Motivated by (
1.3
) and this approximation, we define the distance covariance

T_{\beta}(X,Y)
,
\beta\in(0,2)
,
between
two random fields
X,Y
on some bounded Borel set
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

of finite positive Lebesgue measure by

(1.8)

\displaystyle T_{\beta}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}\,\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}\big]+{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}\big]\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}\big]

\displaystyle-2\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}\|Y_{1}-Y_{3}\|_{2}^{\beta}\big]\,,

where
(X_{i},Y_{i})
,
i=1,2,\ldots,
are iid copies of
(X,Y)
,
and the distance correlation
R_{\beta}(X,Y)
is defined correspondingly.
(The
L^{2}
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is to be understood on
B
.)
A further motivation is the following fact:

Lemma 1.1
.

Assume that
\beta\in(0,2)
,
X,Y
are random fields on
B

which are square-integrable, bounded, stochastically continuous and

{\mathbb{E}}[\|X\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|Y\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|X\|_{2}^{\beta}\|Y\|_{2}^{\beta}]<\infty.
Then

T_{\beta}(X,Y)=0
if and only if
X,Y
are independent.

For
\beta\leq 1
the statement follows from Lyons
[
22
]
,
who also addressed the more general problem of distance covariance in
metric spaces, and for
\beta\in(0,2)

a straightforward modification of the proofs in Dehling et al.
[
9
]
for random fields
on general Borel sets
B

yields the result.

Sample versions of
T_{\beta}
and
R_{\beta}
are given by

(1.9)

\displaystyle\qquad T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\|Y_{k}-Y_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\|Y_{k}-Y_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}

\displaystyle\qquad-2\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{n}\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\|Y_{k}-Y_{m}\|_{2}^{\beta}\,,

(1.10)

\displaystyle\qquad R_{n,\beta}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)}{\sqrt{T_{n,\beta}(X,X)T_{n,\beta}(Y,Y)}}\,.

Since
T_{n,\beta}
is a
V
-statistic, under suitable moment conditions

T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)
and
R_{n,\beta}(X,Y)

are consistent estimators of
T_{\beta}(X,Y)
and
R_{\beta}(X,Y)
, respectively,
and if
X,Y
are independent, one also has

\displaystyle n\,R_{n,\beta}(X,Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\,(N_{i}^{2}-1)+{\rm const},

for a square-summable real sequence
(\lambda_{i})
, iid
N(0,1)
random variables
(N_{i})
.
These asymptotic results
in combination with the fact that
T_{\beta}(X,Y)=0
if and only if
X,Y
are independent
encourage one to build a statistical test about independence of
X,Y

on the quantities
T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)
.

Unfortunately, a sample of paths of
(X,Y)
is rarely at our disposal, and so one has to think about discretizations
of
(X,Y)
. Motivated by the Riemann sum approximation (
1.7
)
possible choices are the lattice discretizations

(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})
in (
1.6
) and, for an iid sample

(X_{i},Y_{i})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, the sample version
T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})

as replacement of the test statistic

T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)
.
In agreement with

[
9
]
in the case

d=1
we will show that this idea can be made to work for
d>1
if

p=p_{n}\to\infty
.

1.3.
Distance covariance for random fields
at random locations

In this paper, we follow a second path of research.

•

We introduce distance covariance
T_{\beta}(X,Y)
and
R_{\beta}(X,Y)
for
random fields
X,Y
on some Borel set
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of
positive Lebesgue measure.

•

We define
T_{\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})
and
R_{\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})
for non-lattice
based
discretizations
X^{(p)},Y^{(p)}

of
X,Y
on
B
. In contrast to (
1.4
), we
choose a random number
N_{p}
of random locations
({\bf U}_{i})
where the
processes
X,Y
are observed. Typically, these locations are uniformly
distributed on
B
and
N_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}\infty
as

p
increases with the sample size
n
to infinity.

The second idea has already been advocated in Matsui et al.
[
23
]
. There it was
assumed that
({\bf U}_{i})
are the points of a Poisson process with
constant intensity
p
.
For statistical purposes this means that one would have to observe
a sample of iid copies
(X_{i}^{(p)},Y_{i}^{(p)})
of
(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})

at random locations that change across
i
and their number would
change as well.
The asymptotic analysis of this setting is not very elegant and
does not lead beyond consistency of
T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})
.

To be precise, we consider bounded
stochastically continuous square-integrable random fields

X,Y
on a bounded Borel set
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of positive Lebesgue measure.
We define the
distance covariance

T_{\beta}(X,Y)

between
X,Y
as in (
1.8
)
but the norm is now given by the (standardized)
L^{2}
-norm on
B
:

(1.11)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2}=\Big(|B|^{-1}\int_{B}f^{2}({\bf u})\,d{\bf u}\Big)^{1/2}\,.

For ease of notation, we assume without loss of generality that the Lebesgue
measure
|B|
of
B
is one.
The
distance correlation

R_{\beta}(X,Y)
is defined correspondingly.

Consider the point process

(1.12)

\displaystyle N^{(p)}(\cdot)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\varepsilon_{{\bf U}_{i}}(\cdot)=\#\{i\leq N_{p}:{\bf U}_{i}\in\cdot\}\,,\qquad p>0\,,

on the state space
B

where
({\bf U}_{i})
is iid uniform on
B
independent of the counting number

N_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}\infty
as
p\to\infty
. If
N_{p}
is Poisson distributed and
p
is fixed then
N^{(p)}
constitutes a homogeneous Poisson process on
B

; see p. 132 in Resnick
[
26
]
. In view of the order statistics property of any
homogeneous Poisson process
N
on
B
we also know that the points of
N
,
conditionally on
N(B)
, are iid uniform on
B
. However, the
representation (
1.12
) goes beyond the order statistics
property since we require the point sequence
({\bf U}_{i})
to be the same for
all
p>0
.

Recycling the notation
X^{(p)},Y^{(p)}
, the discretizations of
X,Y
are now
given by

\displaystyle X^{(p)}({\bf u})=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}X({\bf u}){\mathbf{1}}_{\{{{\bf U}_{i}}\}}({\bf u})\quad\text{and}\quad Y^{(p)}({\bf u})=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}Y({\bf u}){\mathbf{1}}_{{\{{\bf U}_{i}\}}}({\bf u})\,,\qquad{\bf u}\in B\,,

where
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
and
X,Y
are independent.
If
N_{p}=0
,
X^{(p)}=Y^{(p)}=0
by convention.
Again recycling the symbol
\|\cdot\|_{2}
, we write

(1.13)

\displaystyle\|X^{(p)}-Y^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}:=\dfrac{1}{N}_{p}\sum_{i:{\bf U}_{i}\in B}(X-Y)^{2}({\bf U}_{i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N}_{p}\,\int_{B}(X-Y)^{2}\,d\,N^{(p)}\,,

with the convention that the right-hand side is zero if
N_{p}=0
.
We notice that for a.e. realization of
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
,

{\bf U}_{1},\ldots,{\bf U}_{N_{p}}
are getting arbitrarily tight in
B

since we assume

N_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}\infty
as
p\to\infty
.
If we assume that
X,Y
are path-wise square-integrable on
B
and we condition
on
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
then for a.e. realization of
X,Y

we have the Riemann sum approximation
\|X^{(p)}-Y^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}\to\|X-Y\|_{2}^{2}
.

In what follows, we define the sample versions
T_{n,\beta}(X,Y),R_{n,\beta}(X,Y)

in the natural way by applying the
L^{2}
-norm (
1.11
) on
B
.
We assume that the iid sequence
((X_{i},Y_{i}))
is independent of

(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
. Then the random locations
({\bf U}_{i})
are the same for
any sample size
n
, and
((X_{i}^{(p)},Y_{i}^{(p)}))
are iid, conditional on

(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
.
Moreover, we define
T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)}),R_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})

accordingly by using the notation in (
1.13
).

The random discretizations of
X,Y

have some advantages over the lattice case:

•

they can be defined
on quite general sets
B
,

•

the random fields can be observed on
irregularly spaced locations,

•

the smoothness of the field
does not play a significant role for the asymptotic theory of
the sample distance covariance.

Of course, one needs preliminary confirmation about the Poisson property
of the counting variable
N_{p}
and the
uniformity of the locations
({\bf U}_{i})
. There exists a large literature
on tests for Poisson point process models
(see e.g.
[
27
,
25
,
11
]
and references therein).
This research topic is still progressing, and tests on arbitrary
multi-dimensional subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or
even on sets of unknown support have been developed e.g. in
[
6
,
7
,
12
]
.

1.4.
Organization of the paper

We provide the necessary asymptotic theory for the
two aforementioned methods:

(i)

in Section
2

for random locations and increasing intensity
p
on a bounded
Borel set
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of positive Lebesgue measure,

(ii)

in Section
3

for the lattice case on
B=[0,1]^{d}
for increasing

p
which is the total (deterministic)
number of grid points.

(iii)

in Section
4
we combine the two methods by averaging
the observations in each cell of the regular lattice on
B=[0,1]^{d}
.

Since the (non-Gaussian) limit distributions of the considered discretized
sample distance covariances are not tractable, in
Section
5
we consider some modifications of
these quantities. Roughly speaking, we find approximating
degenerate
U
-statistics to the sample distance covariances and
apply bootstrap techniques tailored for these
U
-statistics. We show the consistency of the bootstrap.
In the case of random locations,
this part is quite delicate and rather different from the theory developed in Dehling et al.
[
9
]

in the lattice case for
d=1
. Indeed, we show bootstrap consistency for these U-statistics
conditional
on the random locations
and their number.
In Section
6
we illustrate
how the convergence of the sample distance correlation depends on the sample
size and the number of discretization points. We also
show how the bootstrap performs for
the sample distance correlation on selected discretized random
fields, in particular for fractional Brownian and infinite variance stable
Lévy sheets. In Section
7

we apply the sample distance correlation for testing the independence of Japanese meteorological data observed at
stations all over Japan. We interpret their number as random and their location as uniformly distributed.

In the remaining sections we provide the proofs of the main
results.

2.
Random field at random locations

2.1.
Technical conditions

We use the notation and assumptions of Section
1.3
.
In particular,

X,Y
are measurable bounded stochastically continuous
random fields on a bounded Borel set
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
of positive Lebesgue measure with Riemann square-integrable sample paths on
B
. Also recall that
p=p_{n}\to\infty

and
N_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

In what follows,
c
denotes any positive constant whose value is not of
interest.

The following result gives some asymptotic results for the sample
distance covariance. They are the basis for proving Theorem
2.6
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Choose
\beta\in(0,2)
and assume that
X,Y
are independent.
Consider the following conditions:

(1a)

X,Y
have finite second moment and

\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}\big[X^{2}({\bf u})+Y^{2}({\bf u})\big]\,d{\bf u}<\infty
.

(1b)

X,Y
have infinite second moment but

{\mathbb{E}}\big[\sup_{{\bf u}\in B}|X|^{\beta}({\bf u})+\sup_{{\bf u}\in B}|Y|^{\beta}({\bf u})\big]<\infty
.

(2a)

X
has finite second moment,
\big[\beta\in(0,1]
and

\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[X^{2}({\bf u})]\,d{\bf u}<\infty\big]
or

\big[\beta\in(1,2)

and
\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[|X|^{2\beta}({\bf u})]\,d{\bf u}<\infty\big]
.

(2b)

X
has infinite second moment,
\beta\in(0,1)

and
{\mathbb{E}}[\sup_{{\bf u}\in B}|X|^{2\beta}({\bf u})]<\infty
.

If
(1a)
or
(1b)
hold then

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)\big|\;\big|\;N^{(p)}\big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

If
(2a)
or
(2b)
hold then

(2.2)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big(|T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},X^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,X)|>\varepsilon\;\big|\;N^{(p)}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

The proof of this proposition is given in Section
8
.

2.2.
Some examples

We consider some examples of random fields and discuss the fulfillment
of the conditions in Proposition
2.1
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
X({\bf u})=B^{H}({\bf u})
,
{\bf u}\in[0,1]^{d}
,
be a
fractional Brownian sheet
with Hurst parameter
H=(H_{1},\ldots,H_{d})\in(0,1)^{d}
introduced by Kamont
[
19
]
; see also Ayache and Xiao
[
5
]
. It is a centered
Gaussian random field with continuous sample paths and
covariance function

(2.3)

\displaystyle{\rm cov}(B^{H}({\bf s}),B^{H}({\bf t}))=\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{1}{2}(|s_{i}|^{2H_{i}}+|t_{i}|^{2H_{i}}-|s_{i}-t_{i}|^{2H_{i}}),\qquad{\bf s},{\bf t}\in[0,1]^{d}\,.

Moreover, for all
\gamma>0
,
{\mathbb{E}}[|B^{H}({\bf u})|^{\gamma}]
is a continuous
function of
{\bf u}
. Therefore all conditions on
X
are satisfied.

Example 2.3
.

Let
H\in(0,1)
and
X^{H}
be a centered Gaussian
random field on
[0,1]^{d}
with covariance function

\displaystyle{\rm cov}(X^{H}({\bf s}),X^{H}({\bf t}))=\dfrac{1}{2}\big(|{\bf s}|^{2H}+|{\bf t}|^{2H}-|{\bf s}-{\bf t}|^{2H}\big)\,,\qquad{\bf s},{\bf t}\in[0,1]^{d}\,.

This process is called
Lévy fractional Brownian field
; see Samorodnitsky and Taqqu
[
28
]
, p. 393.
It has stationary increments and continuous sample paths and moments
of any order.

Example 2.4
.

Let
X
be a centered
Lévy sheet
on
[0,1]^{d}
with
Lévy-Khintchine triplet
(\mu,\sigma^{2},\nu)
; see Khoshnevisan and Xiao
[

]

for disjoint intervals

are independent and
for each interval

is given by

(2.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[{\rm e}\,^{iuX(A)}]=\exp(-|A|\,\Psi(u))\,,

If

has finite
\gamma
th moment for some

{\bf u}
and

The field
X

has càdlàg sample paths which are bounded and square-integrable on

[0,1]^{d}
.

Example 2.5
.

Consider a symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy sheet
on

2.3.
Main result

The following statement is the main result in the case of random locations.

Theorem 2.6
.

Choose
\beta\in(0,2)
and assume that
X,Y
are independent,

1.

If

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big(\big|R_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})\big|>\varepsilon\mid N^{(p)}\big)\to 0\,.

Proof.

1.
From Proposition

,
in particular from (

), we conclude

3.
Random field at a lattice

In the present and next sections we consider sampling schemes
of
(X,Y)
on a deterministic lattice on
B=[0,1]^{d}
. We will assume conditions on the smoothness of
(X,Y)

from which we can derive convergence rates of
T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})

to
T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)
.
Here we closely follow Dehling et al.
[
9
]
who dealt with the lattice case for
d=1
.
The following conditions and results are adaptations of those in
[
9
]
.

3.1.
Technical conditions

We use the notation and assumptions of Section
1.2
.
In what follows, we introduce moment and smoothness conditions on the fields
X,Y
and require certain rates for
p=p_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
The conditions are separated in two parts depending on whether
(X,Y)

have finite second moments or not.

If
X,Y
have finite second moments we will work under the following
conditions.

Condition (A): finite second moments

(A1)

Smoothness of increments.
There exist

\gamma_{X},\gamma_{Y}>0
and
c>0
such that

\displaystyle{\rm var}\big(X({\bf t})-X({\bf s})\big)\leq c\,|{\bf t}-{\bf s}|^{\gamma_{X}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\rm var}\big(Y({\bf t})-Y({\bf s})\big)\leq c\,|{\bf t}-{\bf s}|^{\gamma_{Y}}\,.

(A2)

Additional moment conditions.

If
\beta\in(1,2)
we have

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf t}\in[0,1]^{d}}{\mathbb{E}}[|X({\bf t})|^{2(2\beta-1)}]+\max_{{\bf t}\in[0,1]^{d}}{\mathbb{E}}[|Y({\bf t})|^{2(2\beta-1)}]<\infty\,.

(A3)

Growth condition on
p=p_{n}\to\infty
.
We have

\displaystyle p^{-1}n^{2/\big(d^{-1}(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})\,(\beta\wedge 1)\big)}\to 0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

If
X,Y
possibly have infinite
second moments we will work under the following conditions:

Condition (B): infinite second moments
Assume
\beta\in(0,2)
.

(B1)

Finite
\beta
th moment.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\max_{{\bf t}\in(0,1]^{d}}|X({\bf t})|^{\beta}\big]<\infty\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;{\mathbb{E}}\big[\max_{{\bf t}\in(0,1]^{d}}|Y({\bf t})|^{\beta}\big]<\infty\,.

(B2)

Smoothness of increments.

There exist
\gamma_{X},\gamma_{Y}>0
and
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf i}\in\Pi_{d}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\max_{{\bf t}\in\Delta_{{\bf i}}}|X({\bf t})-X({\bf t}_{\bf i})|^{\beta}\big]\leq c\,p^{-\gamma_{X}/d}\ \mbox{and}\ \max_{{\bf i}\in\Pi_{d}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\max_{t\in\Delta_{\bf i}}|Y({\bf t})-Y({\bf t}_{\bf i})|^{\beta}\big]\leq c\,p^{-\gamma_{Y}/d}\,.

(B3)

Additional moment and smoothness conditions.

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\max_{{\bf t}\in(0,1]^{d}}|X({\bf t})|^{2\beta}\big]<\infty\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;{\mathbb{E}}\big[\max_{{\bf t}\in(0,1]^{d}}|Y({\bf t})|^{2\beta}\big]<\infty\,.

If
\beta\in(0,1)
there also exist
\gamma_{X}^{\prime},\gamma_{Y}^{\prime}>0
and
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\max_{{\bf i}\in\Pi_{d}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\max_{{\bf t}\in\Delta_{\bf i}}|X({\bf t})-X({\bf t}_{\bf i})|^{2\beta}\big]\leq c\,p^{-\gamma_{X}^{\prime}/d}\mbox{ and }\ \max_{{\bf i}\in\Pi_{d}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[\max_{{\bf t}\in\Delta_{\bf i}}|Y({\bf t})-Y({\bf t}_{\bf i})|^{2\beta}\big]\leq c\,p^{-\gamma_{Y}^{\prime}/d}\,.

(B4)

Growth condition on
p=p_{n}\to\infty
.
We have
\beta/2+(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})/d>1
and

\displaystyle p^{-1}n^{(\beta/(\beta\wedge 1))\big/(\beta/2+(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})/d-1)}\to 0\,,\quad n\to\infty\,.

In the lattice case the following result is the analog of Proposition
2.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume the following conditions.

(1)

X,Y
are independent stochastically continuous bounded processes on
[0,1]^{d}
defined
on the same probability space.

(2)

If
X,Y
have finite expectations, then these are assumed to be equal
to
0
.

(3)

\beta\in(0,2)
.

(4)

p=p_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Then the following statements hold.

1.

If also
(A1)
holds then there is a constant
c
such that for any
n\geq 1
,

(3.1)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)|\big]\leq c\,p^{-d^{-1}(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})\,(\beta\wedge 1)/2}\,.

2.

If also
(B1),(B2)
hold then there is a constant
c
such that for any
n\geq 1
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)|\big]\leq c\,\big(p^{1-(\beta/2+(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})/d)}\big)^{(\beta\wedge 1)/\beta}\,.

3.

If also
(A1), (A2)
hold
in the finite variance case and
\big[\mbox{$\beta\in(0,1)$, {\rm(B3)}}\ and\ 1<\beta+(\gamma_{X}^{\prime}\wedge\gamma_{Y}^{\prime})/d\big]
in the infinite variance

\displaystyle T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},X^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbb{P}}}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

and the analogous result holds for

.

The proof of this result is completely analogous to the case
d=1
given in

[
9
]
and therefore omitted.

A comparison of Propositions

and

shows
that in the former result one does not need conditions on the smoothness
of the sample paths such as (A1), (B2) or (B3). In the latter result, the
smoothness parameters

appear explicitly in the
approximation rates in (

) and (

For example, consider two iid Brownian sheets
X,Y
. According to Example
3.2
below,

\gamma_{X}=\gamma_{Y}=1
since
H_{i}=0.5

)
turn into
c\,p^{-(\beta\wedge 1)/(2d)}
. In Proposition
2.1

the parameter
p
has a distinct meaning. Assume that
(N_{p})_{p>0}
are Poisson
variables, e.g.
N_{p}=M(p\,B)
for a unit rate homogeneous Poisson process

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}

(

on the grid points of a lattice in combination

Example 3.2

Let

\displaystyle{\rm var}(B^{H}({\bf t})-B^{H}({\bf s}))

\displaystyle=\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{d}|t_{i}|^{2H_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{d}0.5(|t_{i}|^{2H_{i}}+|s_{i}|^{2H_{i}}-|t_{i}-s_{i}|^{2H_{i}})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\Big(\prod_{i=1}^{d}|s_{i}|^{2H_{i}}-\prod_{i=1}^{d}0.5(|t_{i}|^{2H_{i}}+|s_{i}|^{2H_{i}}-|t_{i}-s_{i}|^{2H_{i}})\Big)

.

Let

4.
Random field at random locations grouped in lattice cells

In this section we consider a combination of the two previous sampling schemes for

(X,Y)
. For the sake of argument we restrict ourselves to fields on

B=[0,1]^{d}
. We assume that the fields are observed at the locations

({\bf U}_{i})_{i=1,\ldots,p}
which are iid uniformly distributed on
B
and

p=p_{n}\to\infty
is a deterministic integer sequence.
This time we average the randomly scattered
{\bf U}_{i}
in each cell of a regular lattice grid.

Similarly to Section
1.2

we partition
B
into
\tilde{p}=[p/\log p]
disjoint cells
\Delta_{i},i=1,\ldots,\tilde{p}
with
side length
\tilde{p}^{-1/d}
and volume
\tilde{p}^{-1}
.
The main idea of this approach is to average the observations

(X({\bf U}_{j}),Y({\bf U}_{j}))
in a given cell. Since
p
increases slightly
faster than the number of cells
\tilde{p}
the probability that
there is no observation in a given cell decreases at a certain rate.

For any random field
Z
on
B
we consider the discretization (again abusing notation),

(4.1)

\displaystyle Z^{(p)}({\bf u})=\mbox{$\sum^{\prime}$}{\mathbf{1}}_{\Delta_{j}}({\bf u})\overline{Z}_{j},

where

denotes summation over those
j=1,\ldots,\tilde{p}

such that
\#\Delta_{j}=\#\{i\leq p:{\bf U}_{i}\in\Delta_{j}\}\neq 0
and

\displaystyle\overline{Z}_{j}=\sum_{k:{\bf U}_{k}\in\Delta_{j}}\dfrac{Z({\bf U}_{k})}{\#\Delta_{j}}\,.

We also recycle the notation

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|Z^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{[0,1]^{d}}\mbox{$\sum^{\prime}$}{\mathbf{1}}_{\Delta_{j}}({\bf u})\,\overline{Z}_{j}^{2}\,d{\bf u}={\tilde{p}}^{-1}\mbox{$\sum^{\prime}$}\overline{Z}_{j}^{2}\,.

4.1.
Technical conditions

The results in this section parallel those in the lattice case
3
.
The conditions are similar to
(A)
and
(B)
; we also
use the notation from
(A)
and
(B)
in the sequel.

If
X,Y
have finite second moments we will need the following
condition.

(A3’)

Growth condition on
p=p_{n}\to\infty
.

\displaystyle(p^{-1}\vee\tilde{p}^{-(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})/d})n^{2/(\beta\wedge 1)}\to 0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

If
X,Y
possibly have infinite
second moments we will need the following condition.

Assume
\beta\in(0,2)
and

(B4’)

Growth condition on

.

We have

\displaystyle(p^{-1}\vee\tilde{p}^{-(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})/d})p^{1-\beta/2}n^{\beta/(1\wedge\beta)}\to 0\,,\quad n\to\infty\,.

If we remove

in (A3’) and (B4’) and
replace
\tilde{p}
by

recover (A3) and (B4).

The following result is an analog of Proposition 2.1 for the discretizations
X^{(p)},Y^{(p)}
of
X,Y
defined via (
4.1
).
In contrast to the latter case, we have explicit rates of convergence for
{\mathbb{E}}\big[|T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)|\big]\to 0
.
These rates are achieved due to the averaging of values in the cells

\Delta_{j}
. Then one can also exploit the smoothness of the field over
these small cells.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume the following conditions.

(1)

X,Y
are independent stochastically continuous bounded processes on
[0,1]^{d}
defined
on the same probability space.

(2)

If
X,Y
have finite expectations, then these are assumed to be equal
to
0
.

(3)

\beta\in(0,2)
.

Then the following statements hold.

1.

If also
(A1)
holds then there is a constant
c
such that for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)|\big]\leq c\,\big(\tilde{p}^{-(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})/d}+p^{-1}\big)^{(\beta\wedge 1)/2}\,.

2.

If also
(B1),(B2)
hold then there is a constant
c
such that for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)|\big]\leq c\,\big(p^{1-\beta/2}(\tilde{p}^{-(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})/d}+p^{-1})\big)^{(\beta\wedge 1)/\beta}\,.

3.

Under the additional conditions
(A1), (A2)
in the finite
variance case and under
\big[\beta\in(0,1)
,

(B1)-(B3)
and
1<\beta+\gamma_{X}^{\prime}/d

\big]
in the infinite variance
case,

\displaystyle T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},X^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbb{P}}}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
is rather technical and given in
Section
9
.

4.2.
Main result

The following is the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume the following conditions:

1.

X,Y
are independent stochastically continuous bounded processes on
[0,1]^{d}
.

2.

If
X,Y
have finite expectations, then they are centered.

3.

\beta\in(0,2)
and
p_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.

Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If either
(A1)
or
\big[\mbox{{\rm(B1),(B2)} and
$1<\beta/2+(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})/d$}\big]
are
satisfied then

T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbb{P}}}}{{\rightarrow}}0

hold.

(2)

If either
(A1),(A3’)
or
(B1),(B2),(B4’)
hold
then

\displaystyle n\,T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\rightarrow}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}(N_{i}^{2}-1)+c

for an iid sequence of standard normal random variables
(N_{i})
, a constant
c
,
and a square summable
sequence
(\lambda_{i})
.

(3)

If either
(A1),(A2)
or
\big[\mbox{$\beta\in(0,1)$, {\rm(B1)-(B3)}}\ and\ 1<\min(\beta+(\gamma_{X}^{\prime}\wedge\gamma_{Y}^{\prime})/d,\beta/2+(\gamma_{X}\wedge\gamma_{Y})/d)\big]
hold then

\displaystyle R_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbb{P}}}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,.

(4)

If either
(A1),(A2),(A3’)
or
\big[\mbox{$\beta\in(0,1)$, {\rm(B1)-(B3),(B4')}}\ and\ 1<\beta+(d^{-1}(\gamma_{X}^{\prime}\wedge\gamma_{Y}^{\prime}))\wedge 1)\big]
hold then

n\,R_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})
converges to a scaled version of the limit
in (
(2)
).

One can follow the lines of the proof of Theorem
3.6
.

5.
The bootstrap for
T_{n,\beta}

In this section we introduce a bootstrap procedure for
T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)

and
T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})
.

5.1.
T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)
as a degenerate
V
-statistic

We recall some facts from the Appendix in Dehling et al.
[
9
]
(see also Lyons
[
22
]
) and adapt them to the situation of a random field
on
B
.
We assume that
Z_{i}=(X_{i},Y_{i})
,
i=1,2,\ldots,
is an iid sequence with generic element
Z=(X,Y)
whose components are bounded
Riemann square-integrable random fields on
B
, and

{\mathbb{E}}[\|X\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|Y\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|X\|_{2}^{\beta}\|Y\|_{2}^{\beta}]<\infty
for some
\beta\in(0,2)
. Under these assumptions,
T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)

has representation as a
V
-statistic of order 4 with symmetric degenerate kernel
of order 1.

We start with the kernel

\displaystyle f((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}),(x_{3},y_{3}),(x_{4},y_{4}))\ (=:f(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4}))

\displaystyle\quad=\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}\|y_{3}-y_{4}\|_{2}^{\beta}-2\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}\|y_{1}-y_{3}\|_{2}^{\beta}.

From this representation,

T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{n^{4}}\sum_{1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n}f(Z_{i},Z_{j},Z_{k},Z_{l}).

Then one can define the corresponding symmetric kernel via
the usual symmetrization as

(5.1)

h(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4})=\frac{1}{24}\sum_{(l_{1},l_{2},l_{3},l_{4})\mbox{ permutation of }(1,2,3,4)}f(z_{l_{1}},z_{l_{2}},z_{l_{3}},z_{l_{4}}).

The kernel
h
is at least
1
-degenerate: under the null hypothesis of
independence between
X
and
Y
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[f(z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3},Z_{4})]+{\mathbb{E}}[f(Z_{2},z_{1},Z_{3},Z_{4})]+{\mathbb{E}}[f(Z_{2},Z_{3},z_{1},Z_{4})]

\displaystyle\qquad+{\mathbb{E}}[f(Z_{2},Z_{3},Z_{4},z_{1})]=0\,.

Still under the null hypothesis of independence between
X
and
Y
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[h(z_{1},z_{2},(X_{3},Y_{3}),(X_{4},Y_{4}))]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{6}\big(\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}+{\mathbb{E}}[\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}]-{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{1}-X\|_{2}^{\beta}]-{\mathbb{E}}[\|x_{2}-X\|_{2}^{\beta}]\big)

\displaystyle\ \ \times\bigl(\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}+{\mathbb{E}}[\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}]-{\mathbb{E}}[\|y_{1}-Y\|_{2}^{\beta}]-{\mathbb{E}}[\|y_{2}-Y\|_{2}^{\beta}]\big)\,,

and the right-hand side is not constant. Hence, the kernel
h
is
precisely
1
-degenerate. One can follow the lines of the proof in
the Appendix in
[
9
]
to show the following result (the only necessary change is the replacement of
[0,1]
by
B
).

Lemma 5.1
.

If
X,Y
are independent and
{\mathbb{E}}[\|X\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|Y\|_{2}^{\beta}]<\infty
for some
\beta\in(0,2)
then
T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)
has representation as a

V
-statistic of order
4
with symmetric
1
-degenerate kernel
h
. The corresponding
U
-statistic
\widetilde{T}_{n,\beta}(X,Y)

is obtained from
T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)
by restricting the summation to indices

(i_{1},i_{2},i_{3},i_{4})
with mutually distinct components. Then

\displaystyle\qquad n\,\big(T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)-\widetilde{T}_{n,\beta}(X,Y)\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{\mathbb{P}}}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{E}}[\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}]{\mathbb{E}}[\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}^{\beta}]\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

An immediate consequence of this result is that, up to an additive
constant,
(n\,T_{n,\beta}(X,Y))
and
(n\,\widetilde{T}_{n,\beta}(X,Y))

have the same limit distribution which is indicated in (
1.2
).
In what follows, we will focus on
\widetilde{T}_{n,\beta}(X,Y)
.

5.2.
Bootstrapping
T_{n}(X,Y)

For the sake illustration of the method we restrict ourselves to

\beta=1
and suppress
\beta
in the notation. We also assume
that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[X^{2}({\bf u})+Y^{2}({\bf u})]d{\bf u}<\infty\,.

A generic element
Z=(X,Y)
has trajectory
z=(x,y)
on
B\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
5.1
,

\widetilde{T}_{n}(X,Y)
has representation as
U
-statistic of order 4 with a 1-degenerate symmetric
kernel
h(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})
.
Applying the Hoeffding decomposition to
\widetilde{T}_{n}(X,Y)
, the limit distributions
(modulo a change of location/scale)
of
nT_{n}(X,Y)
and the following normalized version of
\widetilde{T}_{n}(X,Y)

coincide:

\displaystyle U_{n}(Z)=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}h_{2}(Z_{i},Z_{j};F_{Z})

where
F_{Z}=F_{X}\times F_{Y}
and
h_{2}
is defined by

\displaystyle h_{2}(z_{1},z_{2};F_{Z})=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[h(z_{1},z_{2},Z_{3},Z_{4})]-{\mathbb{E}}[h(z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3},Z_{4})]

\displaystyle-{\mathbb{E}}[h(Z_{1},z_{2},Z_{3},Z_{4})]+{\mathbb{E}}[h(Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3},Z_{4})]\,.

We write
F_{n}
for the empirical distribution of the sample
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
and

(Z_{n1}^{\ast},\ldots,Z_{nn}^{\ast})
for a bootstrap sample, i.e.,
given
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
it is iid with distribution
F_{n}
.
Arcones and Giné
[
4
]
proved that the correct
bootstrap version of
U_{n}(Z)
is

\displaystyle U_{n}(Z^{\ast})=\dfrac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}h_{2}(Z_{ni}^{\ast},Z_{nj}^{\ast};F_{n})\,.

The fact that the limiting distributions of
U_{n}(Z)

and
U_{n}(Z^{\ast})
coincide follows from Dehling and Mikosch
[
10
]
; see also Corollary 5.2 in
[
9
]
in the case
B=[0,1]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Under the aforementioned conditions, and if also

{\mathbb{E}}[|h(Z_{i_{1}},\ldots,Z_{i_{4}})|^{2}]<\infty
for all indices
1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{4}\leq 4
,
we have

\displaystyle d_{2}\big({\mathcal{L}}\big(U_{n}(Z)\big)\;,{\mathcal{L}}\big(U_{n}(Z^{\ast})\big)\big)\to 0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

for almost all realizations of
(Z_{i})
. Here
d_{2}
denotes the Wasserstein
distance of order
2
.

The additional second moment assumption on
h

is satisfied for our kernel. Note that it suffices to consider the non-symmetric kernel
f
, and to show that

{\mathbb{E}}[(f(Z_{i_{1}},Z_{i_{2}},Z_{i_{3}},Z_{i_{4}}))^{2}]<\infty

for all indices
1\leq i_{1},\ldots,i_{4}\leq 4
. For our specific kernel, this condition reads as

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|X_{i_{1}}-X_{i_{2}}\|\big[\|Y_{i_{1}}-Y_{i_{2}}\|+\|Y_{i_{3}}-Y_{i_{4}}\|-2\|Y_{i_{1}}-Y_{i_{3}}\|\big]\big)^{2}\big]<\infty,

and this holds under the moment conditions in this paper.

The Wasserstein distance
d_{2}
metrizes weak convergence and moment convergence up to the second order. Therefore Proposition
5.2
proves bootstrap consistency
for
U_{n}(Z^{\ast})
. In particular,
U_{n}(Z^{\ast})
and
n\,T_{n}(X,Y)
have the
same limit distribution up to some change of scale/location.
From now, we will work with

U_{n}(Z)
as a surrogate of the normalized sample distance covariance
n\,T_{n}(X,Y)

and with its bootstrap version
U_{n}(Z^{\ast})
.

5.3.
Bootstrapping
U_{n}(Z^{(p)})

Our goal is to show
that we are allowed to replace
Z=(X,Y)
in
U_{n}(Z)

by the corresponding
discretizations
Z^{(p)}=(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})
as well as the
corresponding result for
U_{n}(Z^{\ast})
. We restrict ourselves to the case
of random locations studied in Section
2
. The
corresponding bootstrap consistency results
in the lattice case of Section
3
follow by a straightforward
adaptation of the results in Dehling et al.
[
9
]
who considered the case
d=1
and
B=[0,1]
.

We start by showing that
U_{n}(Z)
and
U_{n}(Z^{(p)})
are close conditionally on
N^{(p)}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume that
X,Y
are independent, (
5.3
) holds, and
N_{p}\to\infty

a.s.
as
p=p_{n}\to\infty
.
Then

(5.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[(U_{n}(Z)-U_{n}(Z^{(p)}))^{2}\mid N^{(p)}]\to 0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

for a.e. realization of
(N^{(p)})
.

Proof.

We consider

(5.5)

\displaystyle U_{n}(Z)-U_{n}(Z^{(p)}\mid N^{(p)}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\big(h_{2}(Z_{i},Z_{j})-h_{2}(Z_{i}^{(p)},Z_{j}^{(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\big)\,,

where

\displaystyle h_{2}(z_{1},z_{2};F_{Z}^{(p)}\mid N^{(p)})

\displaystyle:={\mathbb{E}}[h(z_{1},z_{2},Z_{3}^{(p)},Z_{4}^{(p)})\mid N^{(p)}]-{\mathbb{E}}[h(z_{1},Z_{2}^{(p)},Z_{3}^{(p)},Z_{4}^{(p)})\mid N^{(p)}]

\displaystyle\quad-{\mathbb{E}}[h(Z_{1}^{(p)},z_{2},Z_{3}^{(p)},Z_{4}^{(p)})\mid N^{(p)}]+{\mathbb{E}}[h(Z_{1}^{(p)},Z_{2}^{(p)},Z_{3}^{(p)},Z_{4}^{(p)})\mid N^{(p)}]\,.

By construction, given
N^{(p)}
,

U_{n}(Z)-U_{n}(Z^{(p)}\mid N^{(p)})
only depends on the sample

Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
and is a
U
-statistic of order
2
with symmetric

1
-degenerate kernel.
By Lemma A in Serfling
[
29
, p. 183]
, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(U_{n}(Z)-U_{n}(Z^{(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(h_{2}(Z_{1},Z_{2})-h_{2}(Z_{1}^{(p)},Z_{2}^{(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(h(Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3},Z_{4})-h(Z_{1}^{(p)},Z_{2}^{(p)},Z_{3}^{(p)},Z_{4}^{(p)})\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(f(Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3},Z_{4})-f(Z_{1}^{(p)},Z_{2}^{(p)},Z_{3}^{(p)},Z_{4}^{(p)})\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\ {\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\|Y_{1}^{(p)}-Y_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}\|Y_{3}-Y_{4}\|_{2}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\|Y_{3}^{(p)}-Y_{4}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}\|Y_{1}-Y_{3}\|_{2}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\|Y_{1}^{(p)}-Y_{3}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\,.

Now we can proceed as in the proof of Proposition
2.1

to show that the right-hand side converges to zero as
n\to\infty
along a.e. sample
path of
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
. We illustrate this for the first term on the
right-hand side. We have by independence of
(X_{i})
and
(Y_{i})
,

(5.7)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\|Y_{1}^{(p)}-Y_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\,{\mathbb{E}}[\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}^{2}]

\displaystyle+c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}-\|Y_{1}^{(p)}-Y_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\,.

The expectation
{\mathbb{E}}[\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}^{2}]
is finite by (
5.3
).
For the same reason and by Riemann-sum approximation along a.e. sample
path of
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}\big[(X_{1}-X_{2})^{2}({\bf U}_{i})\mid{\bf U}_{i}\big]

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}\big[(X_{1}-X_{2})^{2}({\bf u})\big]\,d{\bf u}\,.

We have by dominated convergence

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\mid(N^{(q)})_{q>0}\big]

\displaystyle\leq{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|\int_{B}(X_{1}-X_{2})^{2}({\bf u})d{\bf u}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}\big|\mid(N^{(q)})_{q>0}\big]

\displaystyle\to 0\,,\qquad p\to\infty\,,

and the corresponding result holds if we replace
(X_{1},X_{2})
by

(Y_{1},Y_{2})
. Hence the right-hand side in (
5.7
) converges to zero
along a.e. sample path of
(N^{(p)})
.
∎

Our next goal is to show that

U_{n}(Z^{\ast})
and
U_{n}(Z^{(p)\ast}\mid N^{(p)})
are asymptotically close
where the latter quantity is defined as in (
5.5
) if we
replace the distribution of
Z
by
F_{n}
.
We write
{\rm var}^{\ast}
for the variance with respect to bootstrap probability measure.

We will need some stronger assumptions to achieve this goal.

Lemma 5.4
.

We assume that
(N_{p})
are Poisson variables such that
{\mathbb{E}}[N_{p}]=p=p_{n}
and
N_{p}\to\infty
a.s. as
n\to\infty
,
\sum_{n}p_{n}^{-1/2}<\infty
and

\displaystyle\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[X^{4}({\bf u})+Y^{4}({\bf u})]\,d{\bf u}<\infty\,.

Then for a.e. sample paths of
(N^{(p)})
and
(Z_{i})
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big(U_{n}(Z^{\ast})-U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\to 0\,,n\to\infty\mid(Z_{i}),(N^{(p)})\big)=1\,.

Proof.

We observe that given
N^{(p)}
,

\displaystyle U_{n}(Z^{\ast})-U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p)}\mid N^{(p)})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq n}\big(h_{2}(Z_{i}^{\ast},Z_{j}^{\ast})-h_{2}(Z_{i}^{\ast(p)},Z_{j}^{\ast(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\big)

is a
U
-statistic of order
2
with a
1
-degenerate kernel. Again applying
the variance formula in Lemma A of
[
29
]
, p. 183, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm var}^{\ast}(U_{n}(Z^{\ast})-U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\mid N^{(p)})

\displaystyle\leq c\,{\rm var}^{\ast}(h_{2}(Z_{1}^{\ast},Z_{2}^{\ast})-h_{2}(Z_{1}^{\ast(p)},Z_{2}^{\ast(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\mid N^{(p)})

\displaystyle=c\,\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,\ell=1}^{n}\big(h_{2}(Z_{k},Z_{\ell})-h_{2}(Z_{k}^{(p)},Z_{\ell}^{(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\big)^{2}.

Taking expectations on both sides, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[{\rm var}^{\ast}(U_{n}(Z^{\ast})-U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\mid N^{(p)})\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(h_{2}(Z_{1},Z_{2})-h_{2}(Z_{1}^{(p)},Z_{2}^{(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\big)^{2}\big]\,.

Now appeal to (
5.6
) and take expectations
on both sides. Keeping in mind
(
5.7
), we have to bound expressions of the
following type:

(5.8)

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\,{\mathbb{E}}[\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}^{2}]

\displaystyle+c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}\,\big(\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}-\|Y_{1}^{(p)}-Y_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle+c\,\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{4}\big]\big)^{1/2}\,\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{2}-\|Y_{1}^{(p)}-Y_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{4}\big]\big)^{1/2}\,.

We write
A=X_{1}-X_{2}
. Then we have for the first expression on the right-hand side

(5.9)

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|A\|_{2}-\|A^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big[N_{p}^{-1}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\Big(A^{2}({\bf U}_{i})-\int_{B}A^{2}({\bf u})d{\bf u}\Big)\Big|\;\Big|A,N_{p}\Big]\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big({\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(N_{p}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\Big(A^{2}({\bf U}_{i})-\int_{B}A^{2}({\bf u})d{\bf u}\Big)\Big)^{2}\;\Big|A,N_{p}\Big]\Big)^{1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[N_{p}^{-1/2}{\mathbf{1}}(N_{p}>0)\big]\,{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\big({\rm var}\big(A^{2}({\bf U}_{1})\mid A\big)\big)^{1/2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[N_{p}^{-1/2}{\mathbf{1}}(N_{p}>0)]\,\Big(\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[(X_{1}-X_{2})^{4}({\bf u})]d{\bf u}\Big)^{1/2}\,.

The second factor is finite by the moment assumptions on
X
.
Using the Poisson structure of
N_{p}
, we can apply Lemma 4.1 to the first factor and conclude that it has the asymptotic order
O(p^{-1/2})
.
Now we consider the first expectation in (
5.8
):

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|A^{(p)}\|_{2}^{4}\big]={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(N_{p}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}A^{2}({\bf U}_{i})\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\Big[N_{p}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}A^{4}({\bf U}_{i})\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[{\mathbf{1}}(N_{p}>0)]\,\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}\big[(X_{1}-X_{2})^{4}({\bf u})\big]d{\bf u}\,.

The right-hand side is finite in view of the moment conditions on
X
.
Next we turn to the second expression in (
5.8
).
Write
C=Y_{1}-Y_{2}
. It remains to bound

\displaystyle\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|C\|_{2}-\|C^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{4}\big]\big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,\Big({\mathbb{E}}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(N_{p}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\Big(C^{2}({\bf U}_{i})-\int_{B}C^{2}({\bf u})d{\bf u}\Big)\Big)^{2}\;\Big|C,N_{p}\Big]\Big]\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[N_{p}^{-1}{\mathbf{1}}(N_{p}>0)]\big)^{1/2}\,\Big(\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[C^{4}({\bf u})]\,d{\bf u}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle O(p^{-1/2})\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

Here we again used Lemma 4.1. Summarizing the bounds above, we conclude that for
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n}{\mathbb{P}}(|U_{n}(Z^{\ast})-U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p))}\mid N^{(p)})|>\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_{n}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[{\rm var}^{\ast}\big(U_{n}(Z^{\ast})-U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p)})\mid N^{(p)})\mid N^{(p)}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq c\,\sum_{n}p_{n}^{-1/2}<\infty\,.

Therefore the Borel-Cantelli lemma implies that

\displaystyle U_{n}(Z^{\ast})-U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p)}\mid N^{(p)})\to 0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,.

for a.e. sample path of
(Z_{i})
and
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
5.4
,
(n\,T_{n}(X,Y))
and

(U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p)})
(conditional on
(N^{(p)})
) have the same limit distribution in (
3.4
) (up to changes of scale/location).

This result means that the modified bootstrap for

(n\,T_{n}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)}))
is
consistent.

Proof.

Following the discussion in Sections
5.1
and
5.3
,

(nT_{n}(X,Y))
,
(U_{n}(Z))
and
(U_{n}(Z^{\ast}))
(given the data) have the same
limit distribution (up to possible changes of scale/location). In this section
we proved that
(U_{n}(Z))
and
(U_{n}(Z^{(p)}))
(given
(N^{(p)})
) and

(U_{n}(Z^{\ast}))
and
(U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p)}))
(given the data and
(N^{(p)})
) have the
same limit distribution. This finishes the proof.
∎

It remains to show the bootstrap consistency for
(n\,R_{n}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)}))
.
We consider the following modified bootstrap version of the latter sequence:

\displaystyle\dfrac{n\,U_{n}(Z^{\ast(p)})}{\sqrt{T_{n}(X^{(p)},X^{(p)})\,T_{n}(Y^{(p)},Y^{(p)})}}.

Since under our moment conditions,
T_{n}(X,X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}T(X,X)
and
T_{n}(Y,Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}T(Y,Y)
by the strong law of large numbers for
V
-statistics it remains to show that

(5.10)

\displaystyle T_{n}(X^{(p)},X^{(p)})-T_{n}(X,X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,,

and the corresponding result for
(T_{n}(Y^{(p)},Y^{(p)}))
. This is the content
of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.6
.

Under the conditions of Lemma
5.4
and summability of

(p_{n}^{-1/4})
, (
5.10
) and the corresponding
result for
(T_{n}(Y^{(p)},Y^{(p)}))
hold.

Proof.

We have the decomposition

\displaystyle T_{n}(X,X)-T_{n}(X^{(p)},X^{(p)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\big(\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|_{2}^{2}-\|X_{i}^{(p)}-X_{j}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2})

\displaystyle+\Big[\Big(\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|_{2}\Big)^{2}-\Big(\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|X_{i}^{(p)}-X_{j}^{(p)}\|_{2}\Big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle-2\dfrac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{n}\Big[(\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|_{2}-\|X_{i}^{(p)}-X_{j}^{(p)}\|_{2})\,\|X_{i}-X_{k}\|_{2}

\displaystyle+\|X_{i}^{(p)}-X_{j}^{(p)}\|_{2}\,(\|X_{i}-X_{k}\|_{2}-\|X_{i}^{(p)}-X_{k}^{(p)}\|_{2})\Big]

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}-2(I_{31}+I_{32})\,.

Writing
A=X_{1}-X_{2}
, we see that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|I_{1}|]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\big[|\|A\|_{2}^{2}-\|A^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}|\big]={\mathbb{E}}\Big[{\mathbb{E}}\Big[N_{p}^{-1}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}\Big(A^{2}({\bf U}_{i})-\int_{B}A^{2}({\bf u})d{\bf u}\Big)\Big|\;\Big|A,N_{p}\Big]\Big]\,.

Now we can proceed as for (
5.9
) and conclude that the right-hand side is
O(p^{-1/2})
. Since we assume summability of
(p_{n}^{-1/4})
the Borel-Cantelli lemma yields
I_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0
.

For
I_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0
it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle I_{4}:=\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\big(\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|_{2}-\|X_{i}^{(p)}-X_{j}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,,\qquad n\to\infty\,

since, by the strong law of large numbers for
U
-statistics,
n^{-2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|X_{i}-X_{j}\|_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}{\mathbb{E}}[\|X_{1}-X_{2}\|_{2}]
.
We have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|I_{4}|]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|\|A\|_{2}-\|A^{(p)}\|_{2}\big|\big]\leq\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\big|\|A\|_{2}-\|A^{(p)}\|_{2}\big|^{2}\big]\big)^{1/2}.

Following the proof in (
5.9
), the right-hand side is bounded
by
c\,({\mathbb{E}}[N_{p}^{-1/2}{\mathbf{1}}(N_{p}>0)])^{1/2}=O(p^{-1/4})
. The summability of

(p_{n}^{-1/4})
and the Borel-Cantelli lemma prove that
I_{4}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0
.

We focus on showing
I_{32}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0
; the case
I_{31}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0
is analogous.
We have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|I_{32}|]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{2}^{(p)}\|_{2}\,\big|\|X_{1}-X_{3}\|_{2}-\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{3}^{(p)}\|_{2}\big|\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\|A^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}\big]\big)^{1/2}\,\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(\|A\|_{2}-\|A^{(p)}\|_{2}\big)^{2}\big]\big)^{1/2}\,.

The first expected value is bounded uniformly for
p
. Another application
of (
5.9
) shows that the right-hand side is of the order
O(p_{n}^{-1/4})
.
Another application of the Borel-Cantelli lemma proves
I_{32}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.1

Write for any Riemann square-integrable random field
Z
on
B

independent of
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
, and any
\beta\in(0,2]
,

\displaystyle D_{p}^{(\beta)}(Z)=\|Z^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}-\|Z\|_{2}^{\beta}=\Big(N_{p}^{-1}\int_{B}Z^{2}\,dN^{(p)}\Big)^{\beta/2}-\Big(\int_{B}Z^{2}({\bf u})\,d{\bf u}\Big)^{\beta/2}\,.

Then we have

(8.1)

\displaystyle|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(Z)|\leq|D_{p}^{(2)}(Z)|^{\beta/2}\,.

We start by proving (
2.1
). We have

(8.2)

\displaystyle T_{n,\beta}(X^{(p)},Y^{(p)})-T_{n,\beta}(X,Y)=I_{1}+I_{2}-2\,I_{3},

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\Big(\|X_{k}^{(p)}-X_{l}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}\,\|Y_{k}^{(p)}-Y_{l}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}-\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\,\|Y_{k}-Y_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\,\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\|X_{k}^{(p)}-X_{l}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}\,\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\,\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\|Y_{k}^{(p)}-Y_{l}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}-\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\,\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\,\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\|Y_{k}-Y_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta},

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{n}\Big(\|X_{k}^{(p)}-X_{l}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}\,\|Y_{k}^{(p)}-Y_{m}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}-\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\|Y_{k}-Y_{m}\|_{2}^{\beta}\Big).

We have

\displaystyle|I_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(X_{k}-X_{l})|\,\|Y_{k}^{(p)}-Y_{l}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(Y_{k}-Y_{l})|\,\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{11}+I_{12}.

Since the conditions on
X
and
Y
are symmetric
it suffices to consider
I_{11}
. It will be convenient to set
Z=X_{1}-X_{2}
.
Since
((X_{i},Y_{i}))
are iid
and independent of
N^{(p)}
in view of (
8.1
) we get the bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[I_{11}\mid N^{(p)}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(Z)|\mid N^{(p)}\big]{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Y_{1}^{(p)}-Y_{2}^{(p)}\|^{\beta}_{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|D_{p}^{(2)}(Z)|^{\beta/2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Y_{1}^{(p)}-Y_{2}^{(p)}\|^{\beta}_{2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\,.

Assume that
X,Y
have finite variance. Then, by the strong law of large numbers,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|D_{p}^{(2)}(Z)|^{\beta/2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\dfrac{1}{N_{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}{\mathbb{E}}[|Z|^{2}({\bf U}_{i})\mid{\bf U}_{i}]+\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[|Z|^{2}({\bf u})]\,d{\bf u}\Big)^{\beta/2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}

\displaystyle\Big(2\,\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[|Z|^{2}({\bf u})]\,d{\bf u}\Big)^{\beta/2}\,,\hskip 18.49988ptp\to\infty\,.

The right-hand side is finite by assumption (1a).
If
X,Y
have infinite variance and
\beta\in(0,2)
then

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|D_{p}^{(2)}(Z)|^{\beta/2}\mid N_{p}]\leq 2\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\sup_{{\bf u}\in B}|Z|^{\beta}({\bf u})\big].

The right-hand side is finite by assumption (1b).
Conditional on
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}

we have the convergence

\displaystyle|D_{p}^{(2)}(Z)|^{\beta/2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,,\hskip 18.49988ptp\to\infty\,,

due the convergence of the Riemann sums
\|Z^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2}\to\|Z\|_{2}^{2}
for a.e. realization of
Z
. Then an application
of dominated convergence yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|D_{p}^{(2)}(Z)|^{\beta/2}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,.

A similar argument shows that
({\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Y_{1}^{(p)}-Y_{2}^{(p)}\|^{\beta/2}\mid N^{(p)}\big])
is bounded a.s. We conclude that

{\mathbb{E}}\big[|I_{1}|\mid N^{(p)}\big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0\,,\,p\to\infty\,.

We can deal with
I_{2},\,I_{3}
in the same way by
observing that

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(X_{k}-X_{l})|\,\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\|Y_{k}^{(p)}-Y_{l}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(Y_{k}-Y_{l})|,

and

\displaystyle|I_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{n}|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(X_{k}-X_{l})|\,\|Y_{k}^{(p)}-Y_{m}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}+\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{n}|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(Y_{k}-Y_{m})|\,\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}.

We omit further details.

Next we deal with the case
X=Y
.
Again using the decomposition (
8.2
) and writing
Z=X_{1}-X_{2}
,
we observe that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|I_{1}|\,\mid\,N^{(p)}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(Z)|\,(\|Z^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|Z\|_{2}^{\beta})\,\mid\,N^{(p)}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Z\|_{2}^{2\beta}+\|Z^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2\beta}\,\mid\,N^{(p)}\big]\,.

Since we already know that
D_{p}^{(\beta)}(Z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm a.s.}}{{\rightarrow}}0
conditionally on
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
we intend to use dominated convergence to show that the left-hand side converges to zero a.s. Therefore we will bound the right-hand terms. First assume that
X
has finite variance.
We observe that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Z\|_{2}^{2\beta}\mid N^{(p)}\big]={\mathbb{E}}\Big[\Big(\int_{B}Z^{2}({\bf u})\,d{\bf u}\Big)^{\beta}\Big]\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Big(\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[Z^{2}({\bf u})\,d{\bf u}]\Big)^{\beta}&\text{for}\hskip 9.24994pt\beta\leq 1\,,\\
\int_{B}{\mathbb{E}}[|Z|^{2\beta}]({\bf u})\,d{\bf u}&\text{for}\hskip 9.24994pt\beta>1\,.\end{array}\right.

The right-hand side is finite by assumption (2a). If
X
has infinite variance
and
\beta\in(0,1)
we have
{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Z\|_{2}^{2\beta}\big]\leq{\mathbb{E}}\big[\sup_{{\bf u}\in B}|Z|^{2\beta}({\bf u})\big]\,,

and the right-hand side is finite by assumption (2b). The same bounds apply to

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Z\|_{2}^{2\beta}\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle={\mathbb{E}}\big[\big(N_{p}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{p}}Z^{2}({\bf U}_{i})\big)^{\beta}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\,.

Thus for any
\varepsilon>0
,
{\mathbb{P}}(|I_{1}|>\varepsilon\,\mid\,N^{(p)})\to 0

as
n\to\infty
along a.e. sample path of
(N^{(p)})
.

We also have

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(X_{k}-X_{l})|\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}\Big(\|X_{k}^{(p)}-X_{l}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\Big)=:I_{21}\,I_{22}.

We have already proved

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[I_{21}^{(\beta)}\mid N^{(p)}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(Z)|\mid N^{(p)}]\to 0\,.

Moreover,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[I_{22}\mid N^{(p)}]\leq{\mathbb{E}}[\|Z^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}\mid N^{(p)}]+{\mathbb{E}}[\|Z\|_{2}^{\beta}]\,.

We have already shown that the right-hand side is bounded for a.e. sample path
of
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
. Therefore we have for any
\varepsilon>0
and a.e. sample
path of
(N^{(p)})_{p>0}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\big(|I_{2}|>\varepsilon\mid N^{(p)}\big)\to 0\,,\hskip 18.49988ptn\to\infty\,.

We have

\displaystyle|I_{3}|\leq\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{n}|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(X_{k}-X_{l})|\,\big(\|X_{k}^{(p)}-X_{m}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|X_{k}-X_{l}\|_{2}^{\beta}\big),

hence by Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|I_{3}|\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|D_{p}^{(\beta)}(X_{1}-X_{2})|\big(\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{3}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|X_{1}-X_{3}\|_{2}^{\beta}\big)\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\big[|D_{p}^{(2)}(X_{1}-X_{2})|^{\beta/2}\big(\|X_{1}^{(p)}-X_{3}^{(p)}\|_{2}^{\beta}+\|X_{1}-X_{3}\|_{2}^{\beta}\big)\mid N^{(p)}\big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[|D_{p}^{(2)}(Z)|^{\beta}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\big)^{1/2}\Big(\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Z^{(p)}\|_{2}^{2\beta}\mid N^{(p)}\big]\big)^{1/2}+\big({\mathbb{E}}\big[\|Z\|_{2}^{2\beta}\big]\big)^{1/2}\Big)\,.

The right-hand side converges to zero by arguments similar to those above.
We finally conclude that

{\mathbb{P}}\big(|I_{3}|>\varepsilon\mid N^{(p)}\big)\to 0\,,\hskip 18.49988ptn\to\infty\,.

This finishes the proof of (
2.2
).

Appendix A
Negative moments of a Poisson random variable

We consider a family
(N_{p})_{p>0}
of Poisson random variables with
{\mathbb{E}}[N_{p}]=p
and
are interested in the asymptotic behavior of
{\mathbb{E}}[N_{p}^{-\gamma}{\mathbf{1}}(N_{p}>0)]
for
\gamma>0
as
p\to\infty
.

Lemma A.1
.

We consider a family
(N_{p})_{p>0}
of Poisson random variables with
{\mathbb{E}}[N_{p}]=p
. Then for
any
\gamma>0
,
p^{\gamma}{\mathbb{E}}[N_{p}^{-\gamma}{\mathbf{1}}(N_{p}>0)]\to 1
.

Proof.

We observe that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[N_{p}^{-\gamma}{\mathbf{1}}(N_{p}>0)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k\in A_{p}}+\sum_{k\in A_{p}^{c}}\Big)k^{-\gamma}{\rm e}\,^{-p}\dfrac{p^{k}}{k!}\,,

where
A_{p}=\{k\geq 1:|k-p|>\varepsilon_{p}\,p\}
and

A_{p}^{c}=\{k\geq 1:|k-p|\leq\varepsilon_{p}\,p\}
and
\varepsilon_{p}=\sqrt{(C\log p)/p}

for some constant
C=C(\gamma)>0
. We will show that

(A.1)

\displaystyle p^{\gamma}\,{\mathbb{P}}(|N_{p}-p|>\varepsilon_{p}\,p)\to 0\,,\hskip 18.49988ptp\to\infty\,.

Then
{\mathbb{P}}(|N_{p}-p|\leq\varepsilon_{p}\,p)\to 1
and

\displaystyle p^{\gamma}\,\sum_{k\in A_{p}^{c}}k^{-\gamma}{\rm e}\,^{-p}\dfrac{p^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in A_{p}^{c}}(p/k)^{\gamma}{\rm e}\,^{-p}\dfrac{p^{k}}{k!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+o(1))\,{\mathbb{P}}(|N_{p}-p|\leq\varepsilon_{p}\,p)\to 1\,.

Next we turn to the proof of (
A.1
). By Markov’s inequality
and a Taylor expansion we have for
h=\varepsilon_{p}
,

\displaystyle p^{\gamma}\,{\mathbb{P}}(N_{p}>(1+\varepsilon_{p})\,p)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle p^{\gamma}\,{\rm e}\,^{-h\,(1+\varepsilon_{p})\,p}\,{\mathbb{E}}[{\rm e}\,^{h\,N_{p}}]=p^{\gamma}\,\exp(-h\,(1+\varepsilon_{p})\,p-p(1-{\rm e}\,^{h}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\gamma}\,\exp\big(-(\varepsilon_{p}\,p+\varepsilon_{p}^{2}\,p)+(\varepsilon_{p}\,p+0.5\varepsilon_{p}^{2}\,p(1+o(1)))\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\big(-0.5\varepsilon_{p}^{2}\,p(1+o(1))+\gamma\,\log p\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\big(-0.5C\log p(1+o(1))+\gamma\,\log p\big)\to 0\,,\hskip 18.49988ptp\to\infty,

provided
C>2\gamma
. On the other hand, by monotonicity of the Poisson
probabilities for
k\leq p
we have

\displaystyle{\rm e}\,^{-p}\dfrac{p^{k}}{k!}\leq{\rm e}\,^{-p}\dfrac{p^{[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]}}{[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]!}\,,\hskip 18.49988ptk\leq[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]\,.

Hence by Stirling’s formula,
(\sqrt{2\pi n}(n/{\rm e}\,)^{n}\leq n!)
,

\displaystyle p^{\gamma}\,{\mathbb{P}}(p-N_{p}>\varepsilon_{p}\,p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p^{\gamma}\,{\mathbb{P}}(N_{p}<p(1-\varepsilon_{p}))\leq p^{\gamma+1}{\rm e}\,^{-p}\dfrac{p^{[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]}}{[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]!}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\,p^{\gamma+0.5}{\rm e}\,^{-p+[p\,(1-\varepsilon_{p})]}\Big(\dfrac{p}{[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]}\Big)^{[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\exp\Big((\gamma+0.5)\log p-p\,\varepsilon_{p}+[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]\,\log\dfrac{p}{[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]}\Big)=:c\,{\rm e}\,^{v(p)}\,.

We have by a Taylor expansion, for large
p
,

\displaystyle v(p)-(\gamma+0.5)\,\log p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-p\,\varepsilon_{p}+[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]\Big(\dfrac{p}{[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]}-1-\dfrac{1}{2}\Big(\dfrac{p}{[p(1-\varepsilon_{p})]}-1\Big)^{2}(1+o(1))\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-0.25\,p\,\varepsilon_{p}^{2}=-0.25\,C\log p\,.

Choosing
C>4\,(\gamma+0.5)
, we have
{\rm e}\,^{v(p)}\to 0
.
Combining these bounds, we proved the lemma.
∎

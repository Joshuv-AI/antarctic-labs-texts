Radiative Transfer in a Fluid
François Golse, Olivier Pironneau

Abstract
We study the Radiative Transfer equations coupled with the time dependent temperature equation of a fluid: existence, uniqueness, a maximum principle are established. A short numerical section illustrates the pros and cons of the method.

Introduction

In fluid mechanics,
Radiative Transfer
is an important subfield of
Heat Transfer
with many applications to combustion, micro-wave ovens and climate models.

For the physics of radiative transfer for the atmosphere the reader is sent to
Goody and Yung 1961
,
Bohren 2006
, to the numerically oriented
Zdunkowski and Trautmann 2003
and to the two mathematically oriented
Chandrasekhar 1950
and
Fowler 2011
.

When Planck’s theory of black bodies is used the radiations have a continuum of frequencies governed by the temperature of the emitting body.

Even when the interactions with the fluid medium are neglected, the radiative transfer equations have 5 spatial dimensions. Hence the problem is numerically quite difficult.
The stratified approximation is used when the radiation source is far, typically, the Sun. It is a two dimensional model with one spatial and one angular dimension to which the authors have contributed recently: see
Bardos and Pironneau 2021
for stratified radiative transfer alone and
Pironneau 2021
,
Golse and Pironneau 2022
,
Golse and Pironneau 2021
for stratified radiative transfer coupled with the temperature equation in the stationary case.

In this article the complete 5 dimensional radiative transfer model is studied when coupled with the time dependent temperature equation in the fluid.

Existence and uniqueness of a solution is well known when the physically constants do not depend on the frequency of the radiating source, the so called grey model (see
Bardos et al 1988
) and
Golse 1987
). In the non-grey case, some results have been obtained by
Mercier 1987
,
Golse and Perthame 1986
, et al. The present article extends these studies done in the eighties.

The radiative transfer system coupled to the Navier-Stokes equations has been studied by
Pomraning 1973
and
Ghattassi et al 2020
at least. In the later an existence theorem is given when the coefficients depend on the spatial variables but not on the frequencies of the source.

The paper begins with a statement of the radiative transfer equations in Section
1
.
In Section
2
an existence result is given.
In Section
3
, uniqueness is shown. The proof is complex and relies on an argument given by
Mercier 1987
and
Crandall and Tartar 1980
. A maximum principle is also shown.
Finally in Section
4
a numerical example is given.

1
Fundamental equations and approximations

To find the temperature
T
in an incompressible fluid exposed to electromagnetic waves it is necessary to solve the Navier-Stokes equations coupled with the Radiative Transfer equations, as explained in
Pomraning 1973
. It is a complex partial differential system formulated in terms of a time dependent temperature field
T({\bf x},t)
function of the position
{\bf x}
in the physical domain
\Omega
and a light intensity field
{I}_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}},t)

of frequency
\nu
in each direction
{\bm{\omega}}
:

Given
{I}_{\nu},T,{\bf u},\rho
at time zero, find
{I}_{\nu},T,{\bf u},p,\rho
, such that for all

\{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}},t,\nu\}\in\Omega\times{\mathbb{S}}_{2}\times(0,\bar{T})\times{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
,

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}{I}_{\nu}+{\bm{\omega}}\cdot\bm{\nabla}{I}_{\nu}+\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}a_{\nu}\left[{I}_{\nu}-{\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}}p({\bm{\omega}},{\bm{\omega}}^{\prime}){I}_{\nu}({\bm{\omega}}^{\prime}){d}\omega^{\prime}\right]

(1)

\displaystyle=\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})[B_{\nu}(T)-{I}_{\nu}],

\displaystyle\rho c_{V}(\partial_{t}T+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla T)-\nabla\cdot(\rho c_{P}\kappa_{T}\nabla T)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{c}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}{I}_{\nu}d\mu d\nu+\bm{\nabla}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}{\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}}{I}_{\nu}({\bm{\omega}}^{\prime}){\bm{\omega}}{d}\omega{d}\nu=0

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\bf u}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla{\bf u}-\frac{\mu_{F}}{\rho}\Delta{\bf u}+\frac{1}{\rho}\nabla p={\bf g},\quad\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=0,\quad\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho{\bf u})=0,

where
\nabla,\Delta
are with respect to
{\bf x}
,

\displaystyle B_{\nu}(T)=\frac{2\hbar\nu^{3}}{c^{2}[{\rm e}^{\frac{\hbar\nu}{kT}}-1]}
, is the Planck function,

\hbar,c,k
are the Planck constant, the speed of light in the medium and
the Boltzmann constant. The density of the medium is
\rho
, the pressure is
p
;
c_{P},c_{V}
are the compressibility of the fluid at constant pressure or volume; in large area these may be altitude/depth dependent.
The absorption coefficient
\kappa_{\nu}:=\rho\bar{\kappa}_{\nu}
comes from computations in atomic physics, but for our purpose it is seen as the percentage of light absorbed per unit length. The scattering albedo is
a_{\nu}\in(0,1)
and
\frac{1}{4\pi}p({\bm{\omega}},{\bm{\omega}}^{\prime})
is the probability that a ray in direction
{\bm{\omega}}^{\prime}
scatters in direction
{\bm{\omega}}
. The constants
\kappa_{T}
and
\mu_{F}
are the thermal and molecular diffusions;
{\bf g}
is the gravity.

Existence of solution for the fluid part of (
1
) has been established by
Lions 1996
.

1.1
The Mathematical Problem

Denote the angular average radiative intensity by

J_{\nu}(t,{\bf x})=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}{I}_{\nu}({\bm{\omega}}){d}\omega.

If
\frac{1}{c}
is neglected in (
1
), the following holds:

\bm{\nabla}\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}{\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{2}}}{I}_{\nu}({\bm{\omega}}^{\prime}){\bm{\omega}}{d}\omega{d}\nu=4\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})\left(B_{\nu}(T)-J_{\nu}\right){d}\nu\,.

(2)

Consequently we are led to study the well-posedness of the following system for
I_{\nu},J_{\nu},T
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} {}&{\bm{\omega}}\cdot\nabla I_{\nu}+\kappa_{\nu}I_{\nu}=\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T)+\kappa_{\nu}a_{\nu}J_{\nu}\,,\quad J_{\nu}:=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}I_{\nu}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}}{4\pi}\,,\\
&{\partial}_{t}T+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla T-\lambda\Delta T=\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})(J_{\nu}-B_{\nu}(T))\mathrm{d}\nu\,,\\
&I_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\!=\!Q_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\,,\quad{\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n}<0\,,\,\,{\bf x}\in{\partial}\Omega\,,\qquad\qquad\frac{{\partial}T}{{\partial}n}\Big|_{{\partial}\Omega}=0\,,\\
&T\big|_{t=0}=T_{in}\,.\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

Here
\Omega
is assumed to be a bounded open subset of
\mathbf{R}^{3}
with
C^{1}
boundary, and we denote by
{\bf n}
the outward unit normal field on
{\partial}\Omega
. We further assume that
\nu\mapsto\kappa_{\nu}
and
\nu\mapsto a_{\nu}
are measurable functions
satisfying

0\leq\kappa_{m}\leq\kappa_{\nu}\leq\kappa_{M}\,,\qquad 0\leq a_{\nu}\leq a_{M}<1\,,\qquad\nu>0\,,~a.e.\,,

for some positive constants
a_{M}
and
\kappa_{m}<\kappa_{M}
. Finally, we assume that the fluid velocity field
(t,{\bf x})\mapsto{\bf u}(t,{\bf x})
is smooth on
[0,+\infty)\times\overline{\Omega}
and satisfies

\nabla\cdot{\bf u}(t,{\bf x})=0\text{ for }{\bf x}\in\Omega\,,\qquad{\bf u}(t,{\bf x})=0\text{ for }{\bf x}\in{\partial}\Omega\,,\qquad t\geq 0\,.

2
Existence

Given a passive parameter
t
, consider the auxiliary problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} {}&{\bm{\omega}}\cdot\nabla I_{\nu}(t,{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})=\kappa_{\nu}(S_{\nu}(t,{\bf x})-I_{\nu}(t,{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}))\,,&&\qquad{\bf x}\in\Omega\,,\,\,|{\bm{\omega}}|=1\,,\\
&I_{\nu}(t,{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})=Q_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\,,&&\qquad{\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n}<0\,,\end{aligned}\right.

where the source
S_{\nu}
is isotropic, i.e. not a function of
{\bm{\omega}}
. Define the exit time

\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}=\sup\{s>0\,\text{ s.t. }{\bf x}-s{\bm{\omega}}\in\Omega\}\,.

By the method of characteristics

I_{\nu}(t,{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})=\mathbf{1}_{\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}<+\infty}Q_{\nu}({\bf x}-\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}{\bm{\omega}}){\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}+\int_{0}^{\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}s}\kappa_{\nu}S_{\nu}(t,{\bf x}-s{\bm{\omega}})\mathrm{d}s\,.

Averaging in
{\bm{\omega}}
, one finds

\displaystyle J_{\nu}(t,{\bf x})=\mathcal{J}[S_{\nu}](t,{\bf x}):=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\mathbf{1}_{\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}<+\infty}Q_{\nu}({\bf x}-\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}{\bm{\omega}}){\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}

(4)

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}s}\kappa_{\nu}S_{\nu}(t,{\bf x}-s{\bm{\omega}})\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}\,.

Since
\kappa_{\nu}>0
, the functional
\mathcal{J}
satisfies the following monotonicity property:

\displaystyle S_{\nu}(t,{\bf x})\leq S^{\prime}_{\nu}(t,{\bf x})\text{ for a.e. }{\bf x}\in\Omega\text{ and }t>0

\displaystyle\implies\mathcal{J}[S_{\nu}](t,{\bf x})\leq\mathcal{J}[S^{\prime}_{\nu}](t,{\bf x})\text{ for a.e. }{\bf x}\in\Omega\text{ and }t>0

\displaystyle.

In particular,

\displaystyle 0\leq Q_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\,,\,\,S_{\nu}(t,{\bf x})\leq B_{\nu}(T_{M})\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,\,\,|{\bm{\omega}}|=1\,,\,\,\nu,t>0

\displaystyle\implies 0\leq\mathcal{J}[S_{\nu}](t,{\bf x})\leq B_{\nu}(T_{M})\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,\,\,\nu,t>0

\displaystyle.

That
\mathcal{J}[S_{\nu}]\geq 0
is obvious. As for the upper bound, observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}[B_{\nu}(T_{M})]=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4\pi}B_{\nu}(T_{M})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}+\tfrac{1}{4\pi}B_{\nu}(T_{M})\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}s}\kappa_{\nu}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4\pi}B_{\nu}(T_{M})\left[\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}(1-{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}})\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}\right]=B_{\nu}(T_{M})\,,

so that the desired upper bound follows from the monotonicity of
\mathcal{J}
.

In order to solve the system (
3
), we consider the iterative scheme detailed in Algorithm
1
, where we have assumed that

0\leq T_{in}({\bf x})\leq T_{M}\,,\quad 0\leq Q_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\leq B_{\nu}(T_{M})\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,\,\,|{\bm{\omega}}|=1\,,\,\,\nu>0\,.

Algorithm 1

to solve (
3
).

1.

Start from
T^{0}\equiv 0
and
J^{0}_{\nu}=\mathcal{J}[0]
;

2.

\FOR

n=0,1,\dots,N-1

(a)

for all
\nu\in(0,\infty)
and all
\tau\in(0,Z)
, by
knowing
T^{n}\equiv T^{n}(t,{\bf x})
and
J^{n}_{\nu}\equiv J^{n}_{\nu}(t,{\bf x})
, define with (
4
)

J^{n+1}=\mathcal{J}[a_{\nu}J^{n}_{\nu}+(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T^{n})]\,;

(b)

Define
T^{n+1}
to be the solution of the semilinear drift-diffusion equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{}{\partial}_{t}T^{n+1}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla T^{n+1}-\lambda\Delta T^{n+1}+\mathcal{B}(T^{n+1})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})J^{n+1}_{\nu}\mathrm{d}\nu\,,\\
&T^{n+1}\big|_{t=0}=T_{in}\,,\qquad\qquad\frac{{\partial}T^{n+1}}{{\partial}n}\Big|_{{\partial}\Omega}=0\,,\quad{\bf x}\in\Omega\,,\,\,t>0\,,\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(T):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(\min(T_{+},T_{M}))\mathrm{d}\nu\,.

Applying Theorem 10.9 in
Brezis 2011
(see also section 4.7.2 in
Lions and Magenes 1972
) shows that, for each
q\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{-1}(\Omega))
, there exists a unique solution to the convection-diffusion problem

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{}{\partial}_{t}\theta+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\theta-\lambda\Delta\theta=q\,,\quad{\bf x}\in\Omega\,,\,\,t>0\,,\\
&\theta\big|_{t=0}=T_{in}\,,\qquad\qquad\frac{{\partial}\theta}{{\partial}n}\Big|_{{\partial}\Omega}=0\,,\end{aligned}\right.

of the form

\theta(t,\cdot)=\Sigma(t,0)T_{in}+\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma(t,s)q(s,\cdot)\mathrm{d}s\in L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}(\Omega))\cap C(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\,.

With

q:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})J^{n+1}_{\nu}\mathrm{d}\nu-\mathcal{B}(T^{n+1})\,,

we see that
T^{n+1}
is a fixed point of the map
\mathcal{F}
defined by

\mathcal{F}(\theta)(t,\cdot)=\Sigma(t,0)T_{in}+\int_{0}^{t}\Sigma(t,s)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})J^{n+1}_{\nu}(s,\cdot)\mathrm{d}\nu-\mathcal{B}(\theta(s,\cdot))\right)\mathrm{d}s\,.

Observe that
\mathcal{B}
is Lipschitz continuous with

\text{Lip}(\mathcal{B})\leq\kappa_{M}(1-a_{M})\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq T_{M}}\int_{0}^{\infty}B^{\prime}_{\nu}(\theta)d\nu=4\kappa_{M}(1-a_{M})T_{M}^{3}\,,

so that, arguing as in the proof of Theorem 1.2 in chapter 6 of
Pazy 1983
shows that
\mathcal{F}
has a unique fixed point. This defines a unique solution
T^{n+1}\in C([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}_{loc}(0,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))
.

Next, we seek to compare the solutions
T
and
T^{\prime}
of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{}{\partial}_{t}T+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla T-\lambda\Delta T+\mathcal{B}(T)=R\,,\quad{\bf x}\in\Omega\,,\,\,t>0\,,\\
&T\big|_{t=0}=T_{in}\,,\qquad\qquad\frac{{\partial}T}{{\partial}n}\Big|_{{\partial}\Omega}=0\,,\end{aligned}\right.

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{}{\partial}_{t}T^{\prime}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla T^{\prime}-\lambda\Delta T^{\prime}+\mathcal{B}(T^{\prime})=R^{\prime}\,,\quad{\bf x}\in\Omega\,,\,\,t>0\,,\\
&T^{\prime}\big|_{t=0}=T^{\prime}_{in}\,,\qquad\qquad\frac{{\partial}T^{\prime}}{{\partial}n}\Big|_{{\partial}\Omega}=0\,,\end{aligned}\right.

under the assumption that
0\leq R\leq R^{\prime}
on
(0,+\infty)\times\Omega
. Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma 6.2 of
Golse and Pironneau 2021
, we multiply both sides of the equality satisfied by the difference
T-T^{\prime}
by
(T-T^{\prime})_{+}
:

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\tfrac{1}{2}(T-T^{\prime})_{+}^{2}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\tfrac{1}{2}(T-T^{\prime})_{+}^{2}-\lambda\Delta\tfrac{1}{2}(T-T^{\prime})_{+}^{2}+\lambda|\nabla(T-T^{\prime})_{+}|^{2}

\displaystyle+(\mathcal{B}(T)-\mathcal{B}(T^{\prime}))(T-T^{\prime})_{+}=(R-R^{\prime})(T-T^{\prime})_{+}\leq 0

\displaystyle.

Integrating over
\Omega
, and taking into account the boundary conditions satisfied by
{\bf u}
and
(T-T^{\prime})
shows that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{1}{2}(T-T^{\prime})_{+}^{2}(t,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+\lambda\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(T-T^{\prime})_{+}|^{2}(t,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+\int_{\Omega}(\mathcal{B}(T)-\mathcal{B}(T^{\prime}))(T-T^{\prime})_{+}\leq 0\,,

since

\int_{{\partial}\Omega}\left({\bf u}\cdot n(T-T^{\prime})_{+}^{2}-\lambda\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}n}(T-T^{\prime})_{+}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}\sigma=0\,.

Then,
T\mapsto\mathcal{B}(T)
is nondecreasing on
\mathbf{R}
, since
\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})\geq 0
and
T\mapsto B_{\nu}(\min(T_{+},T_{M}))
is nondecreasing on
\mathbf{R}
for each
\nu>0
. Hence

(\mathcal{B}(T)-\mathcal{B}(T^{\prime}))(T-T^{\prime})_{+}\geq 0

so that

\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{1}{2}(T-T^{\prime})_{+}^{2}(t,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\leq\int_{\Omega}\tfrac{1}{2}(T_{in}-T^{\prime}_{in})_{+}^{2}({\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}=0\,.

Therefore

T_{in}\leq T^{\prime}_{in}\text{ on }\Omega\text{ and }R\leq R^{\prime}\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\implies T\leq T^{\prime}\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\,.

This comparison argument shows that

0\leq J^{n+1}_{\nu}(t,{\bf x})\leq B_{\nu}(T_{M})\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\implies 0\leq T^{n+1}\leq T_{M}\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\,.

By the same token,

J^{n}_{\nu}\leq J^{n+1}_{\nu}\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\implies T^{n}\leq T^{n+1}\text{ on }(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\,.

On the other hand, the monotonicity property of the function
\mathcal{J}
shows that

\displaystyle T^{n-1}\leq T^{n}\text{ and }J^{n-1}_{\nu}\leq J^{n}_{\nu}\implies J_{\nu}^{n}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}[a_{\nu}J^{n-1}_{\nu}+(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T^{n-1})]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}[a_{\nu}J^{n}_{\nu}+(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T^{n})]=J^{n+1}_{\nu}

on
(0,+\infty)\times\Omega
.
Summarising, we have essentially proved the following result.

Theorem 1
.

Assume that

0\leq\kappa_{m}\leq\kappa_{\nu}\leq\kappa_{M}\,,\qquad 0\leq a_{\nu}\leq a_{M}<1\,,\qquad\nu>0\,,~ a.e.\,,

and pick boundary and initial data satisfying, for some
T_{M}
,

0\leq T_{in}({\bf x})\leq T_{M}\,,\quad 0\leq Q_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\leq B_{\nu}(T_{M})\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,\,\,|{\bm{\omega}}|=1\,,\,\,\nu>0\,.

Then

(1) the sequences
T^{n}
and
J^{n}_{\nu}
satisfy

0=T^{0}\leq T^{1}\leq\ldots\leq T^{n}\leq T^{n+1}\leq\ldots\leq T_{M}\qquad\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times\Omega\,,

and

0\leq J^{0}_{\nu}\leq J^{1}_{\nu}\leq\ldots\leq J^{n}_{\nu}\leq J^{n+1}_{\nu}\leq\ldots\leq B_{\nu}(T_{M})\quad\text{ on }(0,\infty)\times\Omega,\text{ for all $\nu>0$.}

(2) There exist a temperature field
T\in C([0,+\infty);L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}_{loc}(0,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))
and a radiative intensity
I_{\nu}\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty)\times\Omega\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times(0,+\infty))
satisfying (
3
) in the sense of weak solutions.

Proof
Statement (1) is a consequence of the monotonicity properties established above. It implies statement (2) by the following elementary observations: first, one can pass to the limit by monotone convergence in the expression

J_{\nu}^{n+1}=\mathcal{J}[a_{\nu}J^{n}_{\nu}+(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T^{n})]

to find that

J_{\nu}=\mathcal{J}[[a_{\nu}J_{\nu}+(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T)]\,,

since
B_{\nu}
is an increasing function for each
\nu>0
. By the method of characteristics, the formula

\displaystyle I_{\nu}(t,{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})=\mathbf{1}_{\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}<+\infty}Q_{\nu}({\bf x}-\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}{\bm{\omega}}){\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}s}\kappa_{\nu}(a_{\nu}J_{\nu}(t,{\bf x}-s{\bm{\omega}})+(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T(t,{\bf x}-s{\bm{\omega}})))\mathrm{d}s

defines a solution of the transfer equation in (
3
).

Finally, for each
\phi\in C_{c}([0,\infty);H^{1}(\Omega))
such that
{\partial}_{t}\phi\in L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\Omega)
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\lambda\nabla T^{n}\cdot\nabla\phi-T^{n}({\partial}_{t}\phi+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\phi))\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\Omega}T_{in}({\bf x})\phi(0,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})(J^{n}_{\nu}-B_{\nu}(T^{n}))\phi\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle.

One can pass to the limit by dominated convergence in all the terms except the one involving
\nabla T^{n}
. This last term is mastered by the energy balance for the convection-diffusion equation:

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}T^{n}(t,{\bf x})^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla T^{n}(t,{\bf x})|^{2}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}T_{in}({\bf x})^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})(J^{n}_{\nu}-B_{\nu}(T^{n}))T^{n}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}s\leq t|\Omega|\mathcal{B}(T_{M})

\displaystyle,

which implies that
T^{n}
is bounded in
L^{2}_{loc}(0,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))
. Since we already know that
T^{n}\to T
in
L^{p}((0,\tau)\times\Omega)
for all
p\in[1,\infty)
as
n\to\infty
, we conclude that
T^{n}\to T
weakly in
L^{2}_{loc}(0,\infty;H^{1}(\Omega))
.
With this last piece of information, we pass to the limit in the weak formulation of the convection-diffusion equation and conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}(\lambda\nabla T\cdot\nabla\phi-T({\partial}_{t}\phi+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\phi))\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}t=\int_{\Omega}T_{in}({\bf x})\phi(0,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\Omega}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})(J_{\nu}-B_{\nu}(T))\phi\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle.

In other words,
T
is a weak solution of the second equation in (
3
). This concludes the proof.

\square

3
Uniqueness and maximum principle

For each
{\epsilon}>0
let
s_{\epsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R})
be such that

s_{\epsilon}(z)=0\text{ for }z\leq 0\,,\quad s_{\epsilon}(z)=1\text{ for }z\geq{\epsilon}\,,\quad 0\leq{\epsilon}s^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(z)\leq 2\text{ for }z\in\mathbf{R}\,,

and let

s_{+}(z)=\mathbf{1}_{z>0}\,.

Set

S_{\epsilon}(y)=\int_{0}^{y}s_{\epsilon}(z)dz\,.

Henceforth, we use the notation

\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\phi\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle:=\tfrac{1}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}\phi({\bm{\omega}},\nu)\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}\mathrm{d}\nu

Let
(I_{\nu},T)
and
(I^{\prime}_{\nu},T^{\prime})
be two solutions of the system above; then

\nabla\cdot\big\langle\!\!\big\langle{\bm{\omega}}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle+D_{1}+D_{2}=0\,,

where

D_{1}:=\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})((I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime})))s_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\,,

and

D_{2}:=\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\kappa_{\nu}a_{\nu}((I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-(J_{\nu}-J^{\prime}_{\nu}))s_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\,.

Since

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{2}}}((I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-(J_{\nu}-J^{\prime}_{\nu}))\mathrm{d}{\bm{\omega}}=0,

one has

D_{2}:=\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\kappa_{\nu}a_{\nu}((I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-(J_{\nu}-J^{\prime}_{\nu}))(s_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-s_{+}(J_{\nu}-J^{\prime}_{\nu}))\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\geq 0\,,

since
z\mapsto s_{+}(z)
is nondecreasing, so that

((I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-(J_{\nu}-J^{\prime}_{\nu}))(s_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-s_{+}(J_{\nu}-J^{\prime}_{\nu}))\geq 0\,.

On the other hand

D_{1}=D_{3}^{\epsilon}+s_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})((I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime})))\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle

where

D_{3}^{\epsilon}=\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})((I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime})))(s_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-s_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime}))\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\,,

while

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})-\lambda\Delta(T-T^{\prime})s_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})

\displaystyle=s_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})((J_{\nu}-J^{\prime}_{\nu})-(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime})))\mathrm{d}\nu

\displaystyle=4\pi s_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})((J_{\nu}-J^{\prime}_{\nu})-(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime}))\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\,.

Thus

\displaystyle 4\pi\nabla\cdot\big\langle\!\!\big\langle{\bm{\omega}}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle+{\partial}_{t}S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})-\lambda\Delta(T-T^{\prime})s_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})

\displaystyle+4\pi(D_{3}^{\epsilon}+D_{2})=0

Then we integrate both sides of this equality on
\Omega
:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})d{\bf x}+4\pi\int_{{\partial}\Omega}\big\langle\!\!\big\langle{\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})+\int_{{\partial}\Omega}S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime}){\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(T-T^{\prime})|^{2}s^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}-\lambda\int_{{\partial}\Omega}s_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})\frac{{\partial}(T-T^{\prime})}{{\partial}n}\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})

\displaystyle=-4\pi\int_{\Omega}(D_{3}^{\epsilon}+D_{2})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\,.

Using the boundary conditions, specifically that

I_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\!=\!Q_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\text{ and }I^{\prime}_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\!=\!Q^{\prime}_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\,,\quad{\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n}<0\,,

with

(Q_{\nu}-Q^{\prime}_{\nu})({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\leq 0\,,\quad{\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n}<0\,,

implies

\displaystyle\int_{{\partial}\Omega}\big\langle\!\!\big\langle{\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})=\int_{{\partial}\Omega}\big\langle\!\!\big\langle({\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n})_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})\geq 0,

\displaystyle\int_{{\partial}\Omega}S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime}){\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})=\int_{{\partial}\Omega}s_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})\frac{{\partial}(T-T^{\prime})}{{\partial}n}\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})=0.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})(t,{\bf x})d{\bf x}+4\pi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\partial}\Omega}\big\langle\!\!\big\langle({\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n})_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(T-T^{\prime})(\tau,{\bf x})|^{2}s^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\tau+4\pi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(D_{3}^{\epsilon}+D_{2})(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})(0,{\bf x})d{\bf x}=0

under the assumption that

T\Big|_{t=0}=T_{in}\quad\text{ and }T^{\prime}\Big|_{t=0}=T^{\prime}_{in}\quad\text{ with }T_{in}\leq T^{\prime}_{in}\,.

Assume that

\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})(I_{\nu}+I^{\prime}_{\nu}+B_{\nu}(T)+B_{\nu}(T^{\prime}))\in L^{1}([0,t]\times\Omega\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times(0,+\infty));

by dominated convergence

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{3}^{\epsilon}(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\tau\to\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}D_{3}(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\tau

where

D_{3}=\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})((I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime})))(s_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-s_{+}(T-T^{\prime}))\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\geq 0

since
z\mapsto s_{+}(z)
is nondecreasing and

s_{+}(T-T^{\prime})=s_{+}(B_{\nu}(T)-b_{\nu}(T^{\prime}))

because
B_{\nu}
is increasing for each
\nu>0
, so that

((I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime})))(s_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})-s_{+}(T-T^{\prime}))\geq 0\,.

By Fatou’s lemma

\int_{\Omega}S_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})(t,{\bf x})d{\bf x}\to\int_{\Omega}(T-T^{\prime})_{+}(t,{\bf x})d{\bf x}

for a.e.
t\geq 0
, so that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(T-T^{\prime})_{+}(t,{\bf x})d{\bf x}+4\pi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\partial}\Omega}\big\langle\!\!\big\langle({\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n})_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+4\pi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(D_{3}+D_{2})(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\tau\leq 0\,,

since

\varliminf_{{\epsilon}\to 0}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(T-T^{\prime})(\tau,{\bf x})|^{2}s^{\prime}_{\epsilon}(T-T^{\prime})(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\tau\geq 0\,.

Since all the terms on the left hand side of the previous equality are nonnegative, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(T-T^{\prime})_{+}(t,{\bf x})d{\bf x}=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\partial}\Omega}\big\langle\!\!\big\langle({\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n})_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(D_{3}+D_{2})(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}\tau=0\quad\text{ for a.e. }t>0\,.

Once it is known that
T\leq T^{\prime}
a.e. on
(0,+\infty)\times\Omega
, one has

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\mathrm{d}{\bf x}+\int_{{\partial}\Omega}\big\langle\!\!\big\langle({\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n})_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})_{+}\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle(\tau,{\bf x})\mathrm{d}\sigma({\bf x})+\int_{\Omega}D_{2}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\!\!\big\langle\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})(B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime}))(t,{\bf x})s_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})\big\rangle\!\!\big\rangle\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\leq 0

since
B_{\nu}(T)-B_{\nu}(T^{\prime})\leq 0
while
s_{+}(I_{\nu}-I^{\prime}_{\nu})\geq 0
, so that
I_{\nu}\leq I^{\prime}_{\nu}
a.e. on
(0,+\infty)\times\Omega\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times(0,+\infty)
.

Summarising, we have proved the following

Theorem 2
.

Let
(I_{\nu},T)
and
(I^{\prime}_{\nu},T^{\prime})
be two solutions of (
3
) such that

\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})(I_{\nu}+I^{\prime}_{\nu}+B_{\nu}(T)+B_{\nu}(T^{\prime}))\in L^{1}([0,t]\times\Omega\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times(0,+\infty))

for all
t>0
. Assume that

T\Big|_{t=0}=T_{in}\quad\text{ and }T^{\prime}\Big|_{t=0}=T^{\prime}_{in}\quad\text{ with }T_{in}\leq T^{\prime}_{in}\,,

and that, when
{\bf x}\in\partial\Omega
,

I^{\prime}_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\!=\!Q^{\prime}_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\text{ and }I_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\!=\!Q_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\leq Q^{\prime}_{\nu}({\bf x},{\bm{\omega}})\,,\quad{\bm{\omega}}\cdot{\bf n}<0\,.

Then

I_{\nu}\leq I^{\prime}_{\nu}\text{ and }T\leq T^{\prime}\,.

If

and
Q_{\nu}=Q^{\prime}_{\nu}
, exchanging the roles of
(I_{\nu},T)
and
(I^{\prime}_{\nu},T^{\prime})
in the theorem above leads to the following uniqueness result.

Corollary 1
.

There is at most one solution
(I_{\nu},T)
of (
3
) such that

\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})(I_{\nu}+B_{\nu}(T))\in L^{1}([0,t]\times\Omega\times{\mathbb{S}^{2}}\times(0,+\infty))\quad\text{for all $t>0$}.

In the case where

is a Planck equilibrium, i.e.

with

constant, one obtains Mercier’s maximum principle:

.

If

and

and

(I_{\nu},T)
of (

in

.

4
A Numerical Scheme

We begin with an important observation for the numerical implementation:

Proposition 3
.

Equation (
4
) can be written as

\tilde{J}_{\nu}({\bf x})=Y_{\kappa_{\nu}}({\bf x})\star\tilde{S}_{\nu}({\bf x})+\tilde{S}_{\nu}^{E}({\bf x}),\text{ with }Y_{\kappa_{\nu}}({\bf x})=\kappa_{\nu}\frac{{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}|{\bf x}|}}{\pi|2{\bf x}|^{d-1}},~d=2,3.

(5)

where
\star
denotes a convolution and tildes indicate an extension by zero outside
\Omega
and

S_{\nu}^{E}({\bf x})=\frac{1}{2^{d-1}\pi}\int_{|{\bm{\omega}}|=1}{\bf 1}_{\{\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}<+\infty\}}Q_{\nu}({\bf x}-s{\bm{\omega}}){\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}\tau_{{\bf x},{\bm{\omega}}}}d\omega

(6)

Proof
This is because, by integration in spherical coordinates with
|{\bf x}|=s
,

\int_{|{\bm{\omega}}|=1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}\tilde{S}_{\nu}({\bf x}-s{\bm{\omega}}){\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}s}dsd\omega=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\kappa_{\nu}\tilde{S}_{\nu}({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}){\rm e}^{-\kappa_{\nu}|{\bf x}^{\prime}|}\frac{dx^{\prime}}{|x^{\prime}|^{d-1}}

\square

Notice that the Fourier transform of
Y_{\kappa_{\nu}}
satisfies

{\cal F}Y_{\kappa_{\nu}}(\xi)={\cal F}Y_{1}\left(\frac{\xi}{\kappa_{\nu}}\right)=\frac{|\xi|}{2\pi\kappa_{\nu}}\arctan\frac{|\xi|}{\kappa_{\nu}}\,.

The numerical implementation is detailed in Algorithm
2
.

Algorithm 2

To solve (
5
) with
S_{\nu}=a_{\nu}J_{\nu}+(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T)

for
each
\nu>0
,

1.

Compute
{\bf x}\mapsto\tilde{S}^{E}_{\nu}({\bf x})
by (
6
) and
{\cal F}Y_{\kappa_{\nu}}=\frac{\kappa_{\nu}}{2\pi|\xi|}\arctan(\kappa_{\nu}|\xi|)
.

2.

for
n=0,1,..,N

(a)

Compute the Fourier transforms
{\cal F}\tilde{S}_{\nu}
.

(b)

Compute
Y_{\kappa_{\nu}}\star S_{\nu}={\cal F}^{-1}({\cal F}Y_{\kappa_{\nu}}\cdot{\cal F}\tilde{S}_{\nu})
.

(c)

Set
J^{n+1}_{\nu}({\bf x})=S^{E}_{\nu}({\bf x})+{\cal F}^{-1}({\cal F}Y_{\kappa_{\nu}}\cdot{\cal F}S_{\nu})
.

(d)

Compute
T^{n+1}
solution of

\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})B_{\nu}(T)d\nu=\int_{0}^{\infty}\kappa_{\nu}(1-a_{\nu})J_{\nu}^{n+1}d\nu

.

5
Conclusion

By using a technique developed in
Golse and Pironneau 2021
for the stratified radiative transfer problem, we have proved existence, uniqueness and a maximum principle for a rather general form of the multidimensional Radiative Transfer system coupled with the time dependent temperature equation with drift.

The proofs are constructive and yield a robust and fairly fast numerical numerical algorithm, at least in the grey case, which encapsulate the exact solution between a lower and a higher numerical one obtained by starting from a guessed temperature field below (resp. above) the exact temperature field.

The 5 dimensional PDE is thus replaced by an iteration involving a three dimensional integral and a convolution integral easily computed with an FFT and which constitutes a tremendous gain in computing time over more classical finite element discretization as in
LeHardy et al 2017
.

Most remarkable is that there are essentially no constraint, besides positivity, on the absorption
\kappa_{\nu}
and the scattering
a_{\nu}
. If these depend on
{\bf x}
, a change of variable needs to be applied to return to the case
\kappa_{\nu}
independent of
{\bf x}
. However if
\kappa_{\nu}
depends on
T
the method does not work, except by adding an iteration loop, sending this dependency on the right hand side of the equation of
I_{\nu}
.

Integral Hasse principle for Markoff type cubic surfaces
Hrishabh Mishra (with an appendix by Victor Y. Wang)

Abstract
. We establish new upper bounds on the number of failures of the integral Hasse principle within the family of Markoff type cubic surfaces x 2 + y 2 + z 2 − x ​ y ​ z = a x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-xyz=a with | a | ≤ A \mathinner{\!\left\lvert a\right\rvert}\leq A as A → ∞ A\to\infty . Our bound improves upon existing work of Ghosh and Sarnak. As a result, we demonstrate that the integral Hasse principle holds for a density 1 1 of surfaces in certain sparse sequences.

1.
Introduction

We consider the family of affine Markoff type cubic surfaces

(1.1)

U_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-xyz=a,

where
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. We begin by recalling some definitions. For each
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
\mathcal{U}_{a}
denote the integral model of
U_{a}
over
\mathbb{Z}
defined by the above equation. Set
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{p\leq\infty}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
\mathbb{Z}_{\infty}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{R}
. We recall that
\mathcal{U}_{a}
fails the
integral Hasse principle
if
\mathcal{U}_{a}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})
is non-empty but
\mathcal{U}_{a}(\mathbb{Z})
is empty. For
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, we say that
\mathcal{U}_{a}
fails
weak approximation
if the image of
U_{a}(\mathbb{Q})
in
\prod_{v}U_{a}(\mathbb{Q}_{v})
is not dense. Furthermore, we say that
\mathcal{U}_{a}
fails
strong approximation
if
\mathcal{U}_{a}(\mathbb{Z})
is not dense in
\pi_{0}(U_{a}(\mathbb{R}))\times\prod_{p}\mathcal{U}_{a}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Here,
\pi_{0}
denotes the set of connected components. Failures of all three properties in the family (
1.1
) have been extensively studied in recent years.

In
[
GS22
]
, Ghosh and Sarnak investigated the failures of the integral Hasse principle for the family (
1.1
). From
[
GS22
, Proposition 6.1]
, it follows that
\mathcal{U}_{a}(\textbf{A}_{\mathbb{Z}})\neq\emptyset
if and only if
a\not\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
a\not\equiv\pm 3\pmod{9}
. Values of
a
that satisfy these conditions are called
admissible
. Therefore, the set of admissible integers
a\in\mathbb{Z}
has a natural density of
7/12
. Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the set of integers
a\in\mathbb{Z}
for which
\mathcal{U}_{a}
fails the integral Hasse principle. For
A>0
, we define

\mathcal{E}(A)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{a\in\mathcal{E}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}|a|\leq A\}.

It follows from their work that
\#\mathcal{E}(A)=o(A)
as
A\to\infty
. A lower bound on the number of failures of the integral Hasse principle

\#\mathcal{E}(A)\gg\sqrt{A}(\log A)^{-1/2}.

is given by
[
GS22
, Theorem 1.2 (i)]
. They obtain these counterexamples based on the quadratic-reciprocity law. Ghosh and Sarnak also provide numerical computations in
[
GS22
, §10]
indicating that
\#\mathcal{E}(A)
is at least
A^{\theta}
for some
1/2<\theta<1
.

The problem of determining asymptotics for the failures of the integral Hasse principle for Markoff type surfaces remains largely unresolved. In this paper, we aim to improve the upper bound on the size of the set
\mathcal{E}(A)
and to analyze the failures of the integral Hasse principle in sparse sequences. We will now state our main result and present a straightforward corollary.

Theorem 1.1
.

We have that

\#\mathcal{E}(A)\ll_{\epsilon}\frac{A}{(\log A)^{2-\epsilon}},

for all
A\geq 2
.

We note that
[
GS22
, Theorem 1.2(ii)]
, if fully quantified, would likely yield a saving with a power of
\log\log{A}
rather than a power of
\log{A}
. Let
c
be an integer. We consider the following subfamily of Markoff type surfaces

(1.2)

U_{p+c}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-xyz=p+c,

where
p\in\mathbb{Z}
is a rational prime. We have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

For every integer
c
, the integral Hasse principle almost always holds in the family (
1.2
).

Remark 1.3
.

Similar results can be deduced for several other interesting sequences. For example, we can restrict the primes in the family (
1.2
). Using
[
Iwa74
, Theorem 1]
, we can restrict primes in the family (
1.2
) to values of suitable quadratic polynomials in two variables. For instance, we can restrict to prime values of the polynomial
P(s,t)=s^{2}+t^{2}+1
.

Loughran and Mitankin also studied the family (
1.1
) in
[
LM21
]
, focusing on weak and strong approximation in this family
[
LM21
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
. They prove that weak and strong approximation almost always fails for Markoff type surfaces. Simultaneously, using reduction theory, Colliot-Thélène, Wei, and Xu demonstrated in
[
CTWX20
]
that strong approximation always fails.

Failures of the integral Hasse principle have also been studied in
[
LM21
]
and
[
CTWX20
]
using the integral Brauer-Manin obstruction. From
[
LM21
, Theorem 1.4]
, we know that the number of integers
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert a\right\rvert}\leq A
for which
\mathcal{U}_{a}
has an integral Brauer-Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle is
\asymp\sqrt{A}(\log A)^{-1/2}
. Theorem 1.5 in
[
LM21
]
provides an asymptotic lower bound on the number of failures of the integral Hasse principle not explained by the Brauer-Manin obstruction. Specifically, they show that the number of such failures
a\in\mathbb{Z}
with
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert a\right\rvert}\leq A
is at least
c\sqrt{A}(\log A)^{-1}
for some
c>0
. Colliot-Thélène, Wei, and Xu improve this lower bound to
c\sqrt{A}(\log A)^{-1/2}

[
CTWX20
, Theorem 5.8]
. They also prove that the failures of the integral Hasse principle discussed in
[
GS22
]
are all of Brauer-Manin type. An improvement in the power of
\log A
in the denominator of the lower bound together with the bounds on the number of surfaces with an integral Brauer-Manin obstruction to the integral Hasse principle will demonstrate that almost all failures of the integral Hasse principle in the family (
1.1
) are not explained by the Brauer-Manin obstruction, as suggested by
[
GS22
, Conjecture 10.2]
.

The strategy for proving
[
GS22
, Theorem 1.2(ii)]
involves deriving an upper bound for a suitable approximate variance and combining this with an appropriate small value estimate to establish the desired upper bound on the number of failures of the integral Hasse principle. A similar method is used in
[
Wan24
]
to obtain bounds comparable to our main result for the sum of three cubes, conditionally. Our approach integrates these two methods to prove our main result. In doing so, we employ additive approximants for the singular series, as done in
[
Wan24
]
, unlike the multiplicative approximants used in
[
GS22
]
. However, we note that since the Markoff polynomial is not homogeneous, our analysis of the singular series and the real densities is somewhat more involved.

In
[
GS22
, §9]
, bounding the approximate variance with suitable parameters, after fixing one of the variables in (
1.1
), reduces to bounding both a diagonal sum and a non-diagonal sum. Ghosh and Sarnak address the diagonal sum using the methods from
[
BG06
]
and the non-diagonal sum using the version of the circle method described in
[
HB96
]
. A similar situation arises in our work: while the diagonal sum is relatively straightforward and is estimated using elementary methods, the non-diagonal sum requires uniformity in various aspects that is not explicitly addressed in
[
HB96
]
. The required estimate is established in the appendix at the end of the paper.

We conclude the Introduction with a remark that our main result is compatible with the results in
[
GMR19
]
and Conjecture 1.1 in
[
BW24
]
. Additionally, our proof leads to a better lower bound for the number of orbits
h_{M}(a)
for almost all
a\in\mathbb{Z}
; see
[
GS22
, §1]
for relevant definitions and more details.

Remark 1.4
.

It might be possible to adapt these methods to prove more restricted versions of the Theorem
1.1
, for example, by taking one of the variables to be prime or under similar conditions.

2.1.
The approximate variance

Let
0<\eta\ll\delta\ll 1
. Let
\nu\geq 0
be a smooth bump function of mass
1
supported on
[1,2]\subset\mathbb{R}
. Let
\varsigma\in\{\pm 1\}
. For
B\geq 1
and
(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, let

(2.1)

\nu_{B,\varsigma}(x,y,z)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\int_{B^{\delta+\eta}}^{B^{\delta+2\eta}}\nu{\left(\frac{\varsigma z}{Z}\right)}\int_{B^{1-\delta}}^{B^{1-\delta+\eta}}\nu{\left(\frac{y}{Y}\right)}\nu{\left(\frac{x}{X}\right)}\,\frac{dY}{Y}\frac{dZ}{Z},

where we write
X=\frac{B^{2}}{YZ}
for convenience. Note that
\nu_{B,\varsigma}
is supported on

(2.2)

\begin{split}\varsigma z=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z\right\rvert}\asymp&Z\in[B^{\delta+\eta},B^{\delta+2\eta}],\quad y\asymp Y\in[B^{1-\delta},B^{1-\delta+\eta}],\\
&\quad x\asymp X\in[B^{1-3\eta},B^{1-\eta}].\end{split}

In particular, we have
xyz\asymp B^{2}
and
x^{2},y^{2},z^{2}\ll B^{2-2\eta}
. Moreover, the inequality

B^{\delta-4\eta}Y\leq B^{1-3\eta}\leq X\leq B^{1-\eta}\leq B^{-2\eta}YZ

always holds. Next, we define several quantities analogous to those in
[
Wan24
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
a\in\mathbb{R}
and
B\gg 1
, let

\begin{split}\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma)&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}{(2\epsilon)^{-1}\int_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert M_{a}(x,y,z)\right\rvert}\leq\epsilon\end{subarray}}\nu_{B,\varsigma}(x,y,z)\,dx\,dy\,dz}\\
&=\int_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\nu_{B,\varsigma}(x,y,z)}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 2z-xy\right\rvert}}\Big|_{M(x,y,z)=a}\,dx\,dy.\end{split}

The last equality follows from
[
CLT10
, §5.4, par. 4]
. Note that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 2z-xy\right\rvert}\asymp XY\geq B^{2-\delta-3\eta}
by (
2.2
).
For
a\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
M_{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=M-a
. For
B\geq 1
, we also define

(2.3)

r_{a}(B,\varsigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}\\
M(x,y,z)=a\end{subarray}}\nu_{B,\varsigma}(x,y,z).

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
set

(2.4)

T_{a}(m)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u\leq m\\
\gcd(u,m)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{1\leq x,y,z\leq m}e_{m}(uM_{a}(x,y,z)).

For
K\geq 1
, let

(2.5)

s_{a}(K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{m\leq K}m^{-3}T_{a}(m).

Now, we define the analog of the approximate variance
[
Wan24
, Eq. 2.5, pp. 5]
. For
K\geq 1
and
B\gg 1
, let

(2.6)

\operatorname{Var}(B,\varsigma,K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}(r_{a}(B,\varsigma)-s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma))^{2}.

N(q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=q^{-5}\#\{\textbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/q\mathbb{Z})^{6}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\tilde{M}(\textbf{x})\equiv 0\bmod q\}.

Rewriting

Lemma 2.5

\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}e_{m}(-(u+v)b)=m\cdot\mathds{1}_{m\mid u+v}

\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}}T^{2}_{b}(m)=m\cdot\sum_{u\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}}\sum_{\textbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{6}}e_{m}(u\tilde{M}(\textbf{x})).

This proves (

). Next, again using the definition (
2.4
)

\sum_{\textbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}T_{M(\textbf{x})}(m)=\sum_{\textbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq u\leq m\\
\gcd(u,m)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\textbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}e_{m}(u(M(\textbf{y})-M(\textbf{x}))),

changing the order of summation proves (
2.10
).
∎

The next lemma will imply Proposition
2.7
below.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1
be such that
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
, then

\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{2}\mathbb{Z}}T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})=0.

Proof.

Suppose
n_{1}<n_{2}
. Then, for all pairs
(u,v)\in(\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\times(\mathbb{Z}/n_{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
we have

\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{1}\mathbb{Z}}e_{n_{1}n_{2}}(-(un_{2}+vn_{1})b)=0.

Using the definition in (
2.4
) and the above equality, we conclude the lemma. The sum in the lemma is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in(\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}\mathbb{Z})^{\times},\\
v\in(\mathbb{Z}/n_{2}\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\textbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3},\\
\textbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}\end{subarray}}e_{n_{1}n_{2}}(un_{2}M(\textbf{x})+vn_{1}M(\textbf{y}))\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{2}\mathbb{Z}}e_{n_{1}n_{2}}(-(un_{2}+vn_{1})b).

The innermost sum is always zero, hence the entire sum is zero.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let

\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\leq K}\frac{1}{n_{1}n_{2}}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{2}\mathbb{Z}}\frac{T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})}{(n_{1}n_{2})^{3}}=\sum_{m\leq K}\frac{S_{\textbf{0}}(m)}{m^{6}},

\sum_{n\leq K}\frac{1}{n^{3}}\sum_{\textbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{3}}\frac{T_{M(\textbf{x})}(m)}{n^{3}}=\sum_{m\leq K}\frac{S_{\textbf{0}}(m)}{m^{6}}.

Lemma 2.8
.

We have that

(2.13)

\sigma^{\otimes 2}_{\infty,\nu}(a_{1},a_{2},B,\varsigma)\ll\frac{B^{2}(\log B)^{2}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert a_{1}a_{2}\right\rvert}}.

Proof.

We note that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 2v_{1}-v_{2}a_{1}\right\rvert}\gg v_{2}a_{1}
, therefore

\begin{split}\int_{(v_{2},\textbf{w})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}&\frac{\nu_{B,\varsigma}(\textbf{w},a_{2})\nu_{B,\varsigma}(v_{1},v_{2},a_{1})}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 2v_{1}-v_{2}a_{1}\right\rvert}}\Big|_{G_{a_{1},a_{2}}=0}\,d\textbf{w}\,dv_{2}\\
&\ll\int_{(v_{2},\textbf{w})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\nu_{B,\varsigma}(\textbf{w},a_{2})\nu_{B,\varsigma}(v_{1},v_{2},a_{1})}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v_{2}a_{1}\right\rvert}}\Big|_{G_{a_{1},a_{2}}=0}\,d\textbf{w}\,dv_{2}.\end{split}

Further, we also note the last integral is at most

\left(\int_{\textbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nu_{B,\varsigma}(w_{1},w_{2},a_{2})\,d\textbf{w}\right)\left(\int_{v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sup_{v_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}\nu_{B,\varsigma}(v_{1},v_{2},a_{1})}{|v_{2}a_{1}|}\,dv_{2}\right).

We bound both integrals separately. Interchanging the order of integrals we deduce that

\begin{split}\int_{\textbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nu_{B,\varsigma}(w_{1},w_{2},a_{2})\,d\textbf{w}&=\int_{B^{\delta+\eta}}^{B^{\delta+2\eta}}\int_{B^{1-\delta}}^{B^{1-\delta+\eta}}\int_{\textbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nu{\left(\frac{\varsigma a_{2}}{Z}\right)}\nu{\left(\frac{w_{2}}{Y}\right)}\nu{\left(\frac{w_{1}}{X}\right)}\,d\textbf{w}\,\frac{dY}{Y}\frac{dZ}{Z}\\
&\ll\int_{B^{\delta+\eta}}^{B^{\delta+2\eta}}\int_{B^{1-\delta}}^{B^{1-\delta+\eta}}\mathds{1}_{|a_{2}|/2\leq Z\leq a_{2}}XY\,\frac{dY}{Y}\frac{dZ}{Z}\\
&\ll\frac{B^{2}\log B}{|a_{2}|},\end{split}

the last bound follows from the asymptotics

XY\asymp\frac{B^{2}}{Z}\asymp\frac{B^{2}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert a_{2}\right\rvert}}.

Similarly, for the second integral, we deduce that

\begin{split}\int_{v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sup_{v_{1}\in\mathbb{R}}\nu_{B,\varsigma}(v_{1},v_{2},a_{1})}{|v_{2}a_{1}|}\,dv_{2}&\ll\int_{B^{\delta+\eta}}^{B^{\delta+2\eta}}\int_{B^{1-\delta}}^{B^{1-\delta+\eta}}\int_{v_{2}\in\mathbb{R}}\nu{\left(\frac{\varsigma a_{1}}{Z}\right)}\nu{\left(\frac{v_{2}}{Y}\right)}\,\frac{dv_{2}}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert v_{2}a_{1}\right\rvert}}\,\frac{dY}{Y}\frac{dZ}{Z}\\
&\ll\int_{B^{\delta+\eta}}^{B^{\delta+2\eta}}\int_{B^{1-\delta}}^{B^{1-\delta+\eta}}\mathds{1}_{|a_{1}|/2\leq Z\leq a_{1}}\,\frac{dY}{Y\mathinner{\!\left\lvert a_{1}\right\rvert}}\frac{dZ}{Z}\\
&\ll\frac{(\log B)}{|a_{1}|}.\end{split}

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.
Variance analysis

In this section, we combine the results from Section
2
to derive an upper bound for the approximate variance
\operatorname{Var}(B,\varsigma,K)
as defined in (
2.6
), when
K
is a small power of
B
. This upper bound, together with a small value estimate, leads to the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section
5
. Let us first present the upper bound.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
B,K\geq 3
be integers. There exists some
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for
K\leq B^{\delta_{0}}
we have the bound

\operatorname{Var}(B,\varsigma,K)\ll B^{2}(\log B)^{2}.

To establish our desired upper bound, we first express the variance
\operatorname{Var}(B,\varsigma,K)
, as a sum of three terms. This approach is similar to the methods outlined in
[
GS22
]
and
[
Wan24
]
. By obtaining asymptotic bounds for each of these terms with appropriate cancellation, we prove the upper bound in Theorem
3.1
. We begin by expanding the variance

\operatorname{Var}(B,\varsigma,K)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}(r_{a}(B,\varsigma)-s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma))^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Sigma_{1}-2\Sigma_{2}+\Sigma_{3},

where

\Sigma_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}r_{a}(B,\varsigma)^{2},\,\Sigma_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}r_{a}(B,\varsigma)s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma),

and

\Sigma_{3}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}(s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma))^{2}.

We analyze each sum separately.

3.1.
Estimating the three sums

First, we consider the sum
\Sigma_{3}
. Using the definition for
s_{a}(-)
from (
2.5
) and grouping terms modulo
n_{1},n_{2}
we rewrite it as

\begin{split}\Sigma_{3}&=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}}(s_{a}(K)\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma))^{2}\\
&=\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}\leq K}\sum_{b\in\mathbb{Z}/n_{1}n_{2}\mathbb{Z}}(n_{1}n_{2})^{-3}T_{b}(n_{1})T_{b}(n_{2})\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}a\equiv b\mod n_{1}n_{2}}\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma)^{2}.\end{split}

From (
2.22
) in Proposition
2.11
with
n_{0}=5
and using the trivial bound

Lemma 4.7
.

n\operatorname{squarefree}\end{subarray}}\beta(m,n)T_{a}^{\natural}(m)T_{a}^{\natural}(n).

Let

\sum_{a\in[-A,A]}|P_{a}|^{2j}\ll_{j,\epsilon}A\cdot(K_{1}K_{2})^{j+\epsilon}(\max|\beta|)^{2j}.

Proof.

Let
r=2j
. Using Poisson summation (by replacing the sum with a smoothed sum over

\sum_{a\in[-A,A]}|P_{a}|^{r}\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1},\dots,m_{r}\geq 1,\\

where

\textbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})

We now prove an estimate that will be used to prove Theorem

For

\sum_{a\in[-A,A]-\{0\}}\left(\frac{|a(a-4)|}{\phi(|a(a-4)|)}\right)^{\kappa}\ll_{\kappa}A.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We begin this section with a lower bound on the real density
\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma)
defined in
2.1
. This lower bound, in conjunction with the bound on the approximate variance in Theorem
3.1
and the small value estimate in the Theorem
4.10
proves the main result.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}
with
|a|\in[B^{2}/2,B^{2}]
. Then we have that

\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma)\gg(\log B)^{2},

for all
B\geq B_{0}
.

Proof.

Let us pick
0<\epsilon\ll\eta\ll\delta\ll 1
. We consider the range
x\in\mathfrak{I}_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[B^{1-2\eta},B^{1-\eta-3\epsilon}]
and
y\in\mathfrak{I}_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=[B^{1-\delta+\epsilon},B^{1-\delta+2\epsilon}]
. We claim that for all suitably large integers
B
and
x,y,a
in the above ranges the equation

(5.1)

x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}-xyz=a

has a solution
z
with
B^{\delta+\eta+\epsilon}\ll|z|\ll B^{\delta+2\eta-\epsilon}
. It is clear that for all suitably large
B
the quantity
x^{2}y^{2}+4(a-x^{2}-y^{2})
is positive, hence there exist real solutions. Let
B\geq 1
be a sufficiently large integer. Suppose
a,x
, and
y
are real numbers in the described range. Let
z_{1},z_{2}
be two real solutions for (
5.1
) with given
a,x
, and
y
. Then we have that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z_{1}+z_{2}\right\rvert}=|xy|
, therefore, we can assume that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z_{1}\right\rvert}\geq\mathinner{\!\left\lvert xy\right\rvert}/2
. Further, we also have that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z_{1}z_{2}\right\rvert}=\mathinner{\!\left\lvert a-x^{2}-y^{2}\right\rvert}\ll B^{2}
, we conclude that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert}\ll B^{\delta+2\eta-\epsilon}
. Next, we note that as
z_{2}
satisfies (
5.1
) we have that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert xyz_{2}\right\rvert}\gg B^{2}
. We conclude that
\mathinner{\!\left\lvert z_{2}\right\rvert}\gg B^{\delta+\eta+\epsilon}
. Therefore for all suitably large integers
B
, we deduce that
\nu_{B,\varsigma}(x,y,z)\gg 1
for all
x,y
in the described range and
z
such that (
5.1
) holds. We obtain that for all suitable large

,

\begin{split}\sigma_{\infty,a,\nu}(B,\varsigma)&=\int_{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{\nu_{B,\varsigma}(x,y,z)}{\mathinner{\!\left\lvert 2z-xy\right\rvert}}\Big|_{M(x,y,z)=a}\,dx\,dy\\
&\gg\int_{(x,y)\in\mathfrak{I}_{1}\times\mathfrak{I}_{2}}\frac{1}{xy}\,dx\,dy\\
&\gg(\log B)^{2}.\end{split}

This proves the lemma.
∎

We proceed to prove the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

From the definition (
2.6
) of the approximate variance and Lemma
5.1
we deduce that

\frac{\#\mathcal{E}(A)}{A}\ll\frac{\mathcal{S}(A,K,\eta)}{A}+\frac{\operatorname{Var}(B,\varsigma,K)}{\eta^{2}(\log B)^{4}A}

for

with

to be a small power of

in order to apply Theorem

and Theorem

with

Hence, we conclude that

This completes the proof of Theorem

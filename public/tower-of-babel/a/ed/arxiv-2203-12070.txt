A Friedlander type estimate for Stokes operators
C. Denis and A.F.M. ter Elst

Abstract




Abstract
.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be a bounded open connected set with Lipschitz boundary.
Let
A^{N}
and
A^{D}
be the Neumann Stokes operator and Dirichlet Stokes operator on
\Omega
,
respectively.
Further let
\lambda_{1}^{N}\leq\lambda_{2}^{N}\leq\ldots
and

\lambda_{1}^{D}\leq\lambda_{2}^{D}\leq\ldots
be the eigenvalues of
A^{N}
and
A^{D}

repeated with multiplicity, respectively.
Then

\lambda_{n+1}^{N}<\lambda_{n}^{D}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.

†
†
footnotetext:

Mathematics Subject Classification
. 47A75, 35P15.

Keywords
. Eigenvalues, counting function, Stokes operator.

1
Introduction

If
\lambda_{1}^{D}\leq\lambda_{2}^{D}\leq\ldots
are the eigenvalues of the
Dirichlet Laplacian and
\lambda_{1}^{N}\leq\lambda_{2}^{N}\leq\ldots
the eigenvalues
of the Neumann Laplacian on a domain
\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary, then

\lambda_{n+1}^{N}<\lambda_{n}^{D}

(1)

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
This result has a long history, starting with Payne
[
Pay
]
and Pólya
[
Pól
]
.
In 1991, Friedlander
[
Fri
]
proved that
\lambda_{n+1}^{N}\leq\lambda_{n}^{D}

for a
C^{1}
domain, using the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator.
The method was modified and extended by Filonov
[
Fil
]
to prove a strict
inequality on more general domains.
A different and independent proof was obtained by Arendt–Mazzeo
[
AM2
]
.
On the other hand, Mazzeo
[
Maz
]
gave a counter example for manifolds.
Under certain geometric conditions on three dimensional domains
Hansson
[
Han
]
proved (
1
) for Dirichlet and Neumann eigenvalues
of the Heisenberg Laplacian.
This was extended to general Heisenberg groups and fairly general domains
by Frank–Laptev
[
FL
]
.
Other results are for powers
(-\Delta)^{m}
of the Laplacian by
Provenzano
[
Pro
]
and a comparison between Robin and Dirichlet eigenvalues
by Gesztesy–Mitrea
[
GM
]
or for certain operators with mixed boundary conditions
by Lotoreichik–Rohleder
[
LR
]
.
In
[
BRS
]
Behrndt–Rohleder–Stadler considered external domains and added a
potential to the Laplacian.

The aim of this paper is to prove a similar result for the Stokes operator.
Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be an open bounded connected set.
One can define two versions of the Stokes operator: a Dirichlet version and
a Neumann version.
The Dirichlet Stokes operator
A^{D}
is defined in the closure

L_{2,\sigma}(\Omega)
of solenoidal test functions

\{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d}):\mathop{\rm div}u=0\}

in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
If
u,f\in L_{2,\sigma}(\Omega)
, then
u\in D(A^{D})
and
A^{D}u=f

if and only if
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,
\mathop{\rm div}u=0
and
there exists a
\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)
such that

f=-\Delta u+\nabla\pi
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Then
A^{D}
is a positive self-adjoint operator in
L_{2,\sigma}(\Omega)

with discrete spectrum.
Let
\lambda_{1}^{D}\leq\lambda_{2}^{D}\leq\ldots
be the eigenvalues of
A^{D}

repeated with multiplicity.

The Neumann Stokes operator
A^{N}
is defined in the Hilbert space

\{u\in L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d}):\mathop{\rm div}u=0\}
.
In Proposition
3.5
we shall give a precise definition of the
self-adjoint operator
A^{N}
.
Loosely speaking,
u\in D(A^{N})
and
A^{N}u=f
if

u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,
\mathop{\rm div}u=0
and there exists a

\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)
such that
f=-\Delta u+\nabla\pi
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

and
\partial_{\nu}u=({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\pi)\nu
, where
\partial_{\nu}
is the
outward normal derivative.
Also
A^{N}
is a positive self-adjoint operator with discrete spectrum.
Let
\lambda_{1}^{N}\leq\lambda_{2}^{N}\leq\ldots
be the eigenvalues of
A^{D}

repeated with multiplicity.
The main result of this paper is that

\lambda_{n+1}^{N}<\lambda_{n}^{D}

(2)

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.

Although the Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacians are self-adjoint operators in
the same Hilbert space
L_{2}(\Omega)
, this is not the case for the
Stokes Dirichlet operator
A^{D}
and Stokes Neumann operator
A^{N}
.
The Stokes Dirichlet operator
A^{D}
is a self-adjoint operator
in the orthogonal complement in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
of the space

\{\nabla\pi:\pi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\},

whilst the Stokes Neumann operator
A^{N}
is a self-adjoint operator
in the orthogonal complement in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
of the space

\{\nabla\pi:\pi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\}.

Nevertheless, we relate the eigenvalues in (
2
).

As in Friedlander’s original paper, the main ingredient for
our proof is the Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator,
adapted to the Stokes problem.
We use sesquilinear form methods as in
[
AM1
]
and
[
AM2
]
.
In Section
2
we prove an abstract theorem, Theorem
2.2
,
on eigenvalue comparison of Dirichlet-type and Neumann-type operators
associated with forms, which relies (and expands) on a combination of the papers

[
AM1
]
,
[
AM2
]
,
[
AEKS
]
and the BSc(Honours) thesis
[
Gor
]
.
As an immediate application we obtain in Theorem
2.8

the classical result for the Laplacian on a Lipschitz domain.
Section
3
begins with the description of the Dirichlet and
Neumann versions of the Stokes operator on a Lipschitz domain, as well
as of the related Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator.
We then apply our abstract eigenvalue theorem, Theorem
2.2
, to obtain a
Stokes version of the Friedlander eigenvalue theorem.

2
An abstract Friedlander comparison theorem

We recall the basic notions of form methods as developed in

[
AE
]
and
[
AEKS
]
, which extend the classical form methods
of Kato
[
Kat
]
and Lions
[
Lio
]
.
Since we only use self-adjoint graphs in this paper, we restrict our attention to
symmetric forms.

Let
V
,
K
be Hilbert spaces and
\gothic{a}\colon V\times V\to\mathds{C}

be a sesquilinear form.
The form
\gothic{a}
is called
continuous
if there is an
M>0
such that

|\gothic{a}(u,v)|\leq M\,\|u\|_{V}\,\|v\|_{V}
for all
u,v\in V
.
The form
\gothic{a}
is called
positive
if
\gothic{a}(u,u)\geq 0

for all
u\in V
and it is called
symmetric
if

\gothic{a}(v,u)=\overline{\gothic{a}(u,v)}
for all
u,v\in V
.
Let
j\colon V\to K
be a continuous linear map.
In Lions
[
Lio
]
, the map
j
is an inclusion map.
We say that
\gothic{a}
is
j
-elliptic
if there are

\omega,\mu>0
such that

\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(u,u)+\omega\,\|j(u)\|_{K}^{2}\geq\mu\,\|u\|_{V}^{2}

for all
u\in V
.
The
graph associated with
(\gothic{a},j)
is the subspace

\{(j(u),f)\in K\times K:u\in V\mbox{ and }\gothic{a}(u,v)=(f,j(v))_{K}\mbox{ for all }v\in V\}

in
K\times K
.

There are a number of sufficient conditions to conclude that the
graph associated with
(\gothic{a},j)
is a self-adjoint graph if

\gothic{a}
is continuous and symmetric.
For a summary of the terminology in self-adjoint graphs we refer
to
[
AEKS
]
Section 3 and
[
BE
]
Section 2.
In this paper we use the following two sufficient conditions.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
V
,
K
be Hilbert spaces and
\gothic{a}\colon V\times V\to\mathds{C}

be a continuous symmetric sesquilinear form.
Let
j\colon V\to K
be a continuous linear map.
Suppose at least one of the following conditions is valid.

(a)

The form
\gothic{a}
is
j
-elliptic.

(b)

There exists a Hilbert space
H
and a compact linear map

i\colon V\to H
such that
\gothic{a}
is
i
-elliptic.

Then the graph
A
associated with
(\gothic{a},j)
is a self-adjoint
graph which is lower bounded.

Moreover, if
j
is compact, then
A
has compact resolvent
and in particular a discrete spectrum.

Proof
.

‘
(a)
’.
This follows from
[
AE
]
Theorem 2.1 applied to the Hilbert space
\overline{j(V)}
.

‘
(b)
’.
See
[
AEKS
]
Theorems 4.5 and 4.15.

For the last statement, see
[
AEKS
]
Proposition 4.8.
∎

Now we are able to formulate the abstract eigenvalue theorem.
We denote by
A^{\circ}
the single valued part of a self-adjoint graph
A
.
For an immediate example we refer the reader to the beginning of the
proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
V
,
H
and
K
be Hilbert spaces.
Suppose that
V
is embedded in
H
and that the inclusion map
i\colon V\to H
is compact.
Let
j\colon V\to K
be a compact linear map.
Let
\gothic{a}\colon V\times V\to\mathds{C}
be a positive symmetric continuous
i
-elliptic
sesquilinear form.
Let
V_{D}=\ker j
.
Let
A^{N}
be the self-adjoint operator in
\overline{V}\subset H

associated with
\gothic{a}
and let

A^{D}
be the self-adjoint operator in
\overline{V_{D}}\subset H

associated with
\gothic{a}|_{V_{D}\times V_{D}}
, where the
closures are in
H
.
Further, for all
\lambda\in\mathds{R}
define
\gothic{b}_{\lambda}\colon V\times V\to\mathds{C}

by

\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)=\gothic{a}(u,v)-\lambda\,(u,v)_{H}.

Let
{\cal N}_{\lambda}
be the self-adjoint graph associated with
(\gothic{b}_{\lambda},j)
.
Suppose

(I)

the operator
A^{N}
has no eigenvector in
V_{D}
and

(II)

\displaystyle\dim\mathop{\rm span}\{\varphi\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda}):({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi,\varphi)_{K}=0\}=\infty

for all
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
.

Let
\lambda_{1}^{N}\leq\lambda_{2}^{N}\leq\ldots
and

\lambda_{1}^{D}\leq\lambda_{2}^{D}\leq\ldots
be the eigenvalues of
A^{N}
and
A^{D}

repeated with multiplicity, respectively.
Then

\lambda_{n+1}^{N}<\lambda_{n}^{D}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.

Proof
.

Since
\gothic{a}
is
i
-elliptic there are
\omega,\delta>0
such that

\gothic{a}(u,u)+\omega\,\|u\|_{H}^{2}\geq\delta\,\|u\|_{V}^{2}

(3)

for all
u\in V
.
Let
\widehat{A}^{D}
be the self-adjoint graph in
H
associated with

(\gothic{a}|_{V_{D}\times V_{D}},i|_{V_{D}})
.
Then
A^{D}=(\widehat{A}^{D})^{\circ}
, the singular part of
\widehat{A}^{D}

and
\widehat{A}^{D}
is positive.
Moreover,
\widehat{A}^{D}
has compact resolvent since
i
is compact.

For all
\mu\in\mathds{R}
define the form
\gothic{a}_{\mu}\colon V\times V\to\mathds{C}
by

\gothic{a}_{\mu}(u,v)=\gothic{a}(u,v)-\mu\,(j(u),j(v))_{K}.

Then
\gothic{a}_{\mu}
is
i
-elliptic.
Let
A_{\mu}
be the self-adjoint graph associated with
(\gothic{a}_{\mu},i)
.
Then
A_{\mu}
has compact resolvent by Proposition
2.1
.
Let
\lambda_{1}(\mu)\leq\lambda_{2}(\mu)\leq\ldots
be the eigenvalues
of
A_{\mu}
, repeated with multiplicity.
Then
\lambda_{n}\colon\mathds{R}\to\mathds{R}
is a function for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Since
\mu\mapsto\gothic{a}_{\mu}(u)
is a decreasing function for all
u\in V
,
it follows from the mini-max theorem
that
\lambda_{n}
is a decreasing function for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

\lim_{\mu\to-\infty}(I+A_{\mu})^{-1}=(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}
in
{\cal L}(H)
.

Proof
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\ldots\in(-\infty,0)
and suppose that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}=-\infty
.
We shall prove that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(I+A_{\mu_{n}})^{-1}-(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}\|_{{\cal L}(H)}=0
.
Suppose not.
Then there are
\varepsilon>0
and
f_{1},f_{2},\ldots\in H
such that

\|(I+A_{\mu_{n}})^{-1}f_{n}-(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}f_{n}\|_{H}>\varepsilon\,\|f_{n}\|_{H}

(4)

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
\|f_{n}\|_{H}=1
for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Passing to a subsequence if necessary, there exists an
f\in H

such that
\lim f_{n}=f
weakly in
H
.
Then

\lim(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}f_{n}=(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}f
in
H

since
(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}
is compact.
For all
n\in\mathds{N}
set
u_{n}=(I+A_{\mu_{n}})^{-1}f_{n}
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Then

\gothic{a}(u_{n},v)-\mu_{n}\,(j(u_{n}),j(v))_{K}+(u_{n},v)_{H}=(f_{n},v)_{H}

(5)

for all
v\in V
.
Choosing
v=u_{n}
gives

\gothic{a}(u_{n},u_{n})+|\mu_{n}|\,\|j(u_{n})\|_{K}^{2}+\|u_{n}\|_{H}^{2}=\mathop{\rm Re}(f_{n},u_{n})_{H}\leq\|f_{n}\|_{H}\,\|u_{n}\|_{H}=\|u_{n}\|_{H}.

So
\|u_{n}\|_{H}\leq 1
and then also

\gothic{a}(u_{n},u_{n})\leq 1
and
|\mu_{n}|\,\|j(u_{n})\|_{K}^{2}\leq 1
.
Hence
\lim j(u_{n})=0
in
K
.
Moreover,
\delta\,\|u_{n}\|_{V}^{2}\leq(1+\omega)

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
by (
3
).
Therefore the sequence
(u_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
is bounded in
V
.
Passing to a subsequence if necessary, there exists a
u\in V

such that
\lim u_{n}=u
weakly in
V
.
Then
\lim u_{n}=u
strongly in
H
since the embedding
i
is compact.
Similarly
j(u)=\lim j(u_{n})
in
K
.
But
\lim j(u_{n})=0
in
K
.
So
j(u)=0
and
u\in V_{D}
.

Let
v\in V_{D}
.
Taking the limit
n\to\infty
in (
5
) gives

\gothic{a}(u,v)+(u,v)_{H}=(f,v)_{H}
.
So
u\in D(\widehat{A}^{D})
and
u=(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}f
.
We proved that
\lim u_{n}=u
in
H
.
Hence

\lim_{n\to\infty}(I+A_{\mu_{n}})^{-1}f_{n}-(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}f_{n}=(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}f-(I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}f=0

in
H
.
This contradicts (
4
).
∎

Proposition 2.4
.

If
n\in\mathds{N}
, then
\lim_{\mu\to-\infty}\lambda_{n}(\mu)=\lambda_{n}^{D}
.

Proof
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Let
\varepsilon>0
.
By Lemma
2.3
there exists an
M\in\mathds{N}
such that

((I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}f,f)_{H}-\varepsilon\,\|f\|_{H}^{2}\leq((I+A_{\mu})^{-1}f,f)_{H}\leq((I+\widehat{A}^{D})^{-1}f,f)_{H}+\varepsilon\,\|f\|_{H}^{2}

for all
f\in H
and
\mu\in(-\infty,-M]
.
Note that the max-min theorem gives

(1+\lambda_{n}(\mu))^{-1}=\max_{\scriptstyle W\subset H\atop\scriptstyle\dim W=n}\min_{\scriptstyle f\in W\atop\scriptstyle\|f\|_{H}=1}((I+A_{\mu})^{-1}f,f)_{H}

and a similar expression is valid for
\widehat{A}^{D}
.
So

(1+\lambda_{n}^{D})^{-1}-\varepsilon\leq(1+\lambda_{n}(\mu))^{-1}\leq(1+\lambda_{n}^{D})^{-1}+\varepsilon

for all
\mu\in(-\infty,-M]
and the proposition follows.
∎

The next lemma is a version of the Birman–Schwinger principle.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\mathds{R}
.
Then
\lambda\in\sigma(A_{\mu})
if and only if
\mu\in\sigma({\cal N}_{\lambda})
.

Proof
.

‘
\Rightarrow
’.
Suppose
\lambda\in\sigma(A_{\mu})
.
Then there exists a
u\in D(A_{\mu})
such that
u\neq 0
and

A_{\mu}u=\lambda\,u
.
Then

\gothic{a}(u,v)-\mu\,(j(u),j(v))_{K}=(\lambda\,u,v)_{H}

(6)

for all
v\in V
.
Hence
\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)=\gothic{a}(u,v)-\lambda\,(u,v)_{H}=(\mu\,j(u),j(v))_{K}

for all
v\in V
.
Therefore
j(u)\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda})
and
(j(u),\mu\,j(u))\in{\cal N}_{\lambda}
.
It remains to show that
j(u)\neq 0
.
Suppose that
j(u)=0
.
Then it follows from (
6
) that
\gothic{a}(u,v)=(\lambda\,u,v)_{H}

for all
v\in V
.
So
u\in D(A^{N})
and
A^{N}u=\lambda\,u
.
Hence
u
is an eigenvector for
A^{N}
and also
u\in V_{D}
.
By Assumption
(I)
this is not possible.
So
j(u)\neq 0
and
\mu\in\sigma({\cal N}_{\lambda})
.

‘
\Leftarrow
’.
The proof is similar, even easier.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Then
\lambda_{n}
is strictly decreasing.

Proof
.

We already know that
\lambda_{n}
is decreasing.
Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathds{R}
with
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}
.
Suppose that
\lambda_{n}(\mu_{1})=\lambda_{n}(\mu_{2})
.
Write
\lambda=\lambda_{n}(\mu_{1})
.
Let
\mu\in[\mu_{1},\mu_{2}]
.
Since
\lambda_{n}
is decreasing, it follows that
\lambda_{n}(\mu)=\lambda
.
So
\lambda\in\sigma(A_{\mu})
.
Hence
\mu\in\sigma({\cal N}_{\lambda})
by Lemma
2.5
.
So
[\mu_{1},\mu_{2}]\subset\sigma({\cal N}_{\lambda})
.
This is a contradiction since
{\cal N}_{\lambda}
has a discrete spectrum
by Proposition
2.1
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\lambda>0
.
Then
\sigma({\cal N}_{\lambda})\cap(-\infty,0)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof
.

Let
{\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}
be the single valued part of
{\cal N}_{\lambda}
.
Suppose
\sigma({\cal N}_{\lambda})\cap(-\infty,0)=\emptyset
.
Then
{\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}
is a positive self-adjoint operator.

Let
\varphi\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda})
and suppose that

({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi,\varphi)_{K}=0
.
Let
\psi\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ})
.
Then

|({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi,\psi)_{K}|\leq\|({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ})^{1/2}\varphi\|_{K}\,\|({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ})^{1/2}\psi\|_{K}=({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi,\varphi)_{K}^{1/2}\,\|({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ})^{1/2}\psi\|_{K}=0.

Hence
{\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi=0
and
\varphi\in\ker({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ})
.
By Assumption
(II)

\dim\mathop{\rm span}\{\varphi\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda}):({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi,\varphi)_{K}=0\}=\infty.

So
\dim\ker({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ})=\infty
.
But the operator
{\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}
has compact resolvent by Proposition
2.1
.
This is a contradiction.
∎

Now we complete the proof of Theorem
2.2
.
Let
n\in\mathds{N}
.
Choose
\lambda=\lambda_{n}^{D}
.
Since
\lambda_{n}
is strictly decreasing by Lemma
2.6
, it follows from
Proposition
2.4
that

0\leq\lambda_{n}^{N}=\lambda_{n}(0)<\lambda_{n}^{D}=\lambda.

Let
\mu=\min\sigma({\cal N}_{\lambda})
.
Then
\mu<0
by Lemma
2.7
.
Moreover,
\lambda\in\sigma(A_{\mu})
by Lemma
2.5
.
Hence there exists a
j\in\mathds{N}
such that
\lambda_{j}(\mu)=\lambda
.
Using again Lemma
2.6
and Proposition
2.4

it follows that
\lambda_{n}(\mu)<\lambda_{n}^{D}=\lambda=\lambda_{j}(\mu)
.
Therefore
j\geq n+1
.
Then

\lambda_{n+1}^{N}=\lambda_{n+1}(0)<\lambda_{n+1}(\mu)\leq\lambda_{j}(\mu)=\lambda=\lambda_{n}^{D},

where the strict inequality follows from Lemma
2.6
.
∎

The first main example is the classical version of Friedlander’s theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary
and
d\geq 2
.
Let
\Delta^{N}
and
\Delta^{D}
be the Neumann and Dirichlet Laplacians on
\Omega
,
respectively.
Further let
\lambda_{1}^{N}\leq\lambda_{2}^{N}\leq\ldots
and

\lambda_{1}^{D}\leq\lambda_{2}^{D}\leq\ldots
be the eigenvalues of
-\Delta^{N}
and
-\Delta^{D}

repeated with multiplicity, respectively.
Then

\lambda_{n+1}^{N}<\lambda_{n}^{D}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.

Proof
.

Choose
V=H^{1}(\Omega)
,
H=L_{2}(\Omega)
and
K=L_{2}(\partial\Omega)
.
Let
i\colon V\to H
be the inclusion map and
j={\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\colon V\to K
.
Then
i
and
j
are compact.
Moreover,
\ker j=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Define
\gothic{a}\colon V\times V\to\mathds{C}
by

\gothic{a}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}.

Then
\gothic{a}
is a positive symmetric continuous
i
-elliptic sesquilinear form.
Further,
-\Delta^{N}
is the self-adjoint operator associated with
\gothic{a}

and
-\Delta^{D}
is the self-adjoint operator associated with

\gothic{a}|_{H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=\gothic{a}|_{\ker j\times\ker j}
.

It remains to verify Conditions
(I)
and
(II)

of Theorem
2.2
.

‘
(I)
’.
Let
u\in D(A^{N})\cap V_{D}
and
\lambda\in\mathds{R}
.
Suppose that
A^{N}u=\lambda\,u
.
Then
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Let
\tilde{u}\in H^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
be the extension of
u
by zero.
Then

\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\overline{\nabla v}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla(v|_{\Omega})}=(\lambda\,u,v|_{\Omega})_{L_{2}(\Omega)}=(\lambda\,\tilde{u},v)_{L_{2}(\mathds{R}^{d})}

for all
v\in H^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d})
.
So
-\widetilde{\Delta}^{N}\tilde{u}=\lambda\,\tilde{u}
, where

\widetilde{\Delta}^{N}
is the Laplacian on
\mathds{R}^{d}
.
Since
\tilde{u}
vanishes on a non-empty open set, it follows from the
unique continuation property that
\tilde{u}=0
.
(See for example
[
RS
]
Theorem XIII.57.)
Then also
u=0
.
So
A^{N}
has no eigenvector in
V_{D}
.

‘
(II)
’.
Let
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
.
Let
\omega\in\mathds{R}^{d}
and suppose that
|\omega|^{2}=\lambda
.
Define
u\in H^{2}(\Omega)
by
u(x)=e^{i\omega\cdot x}
.
Then
-\Delta u=\lambda u
as distribution and

\displaystyle\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\,\overline{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}+\int_{\Omega}(\Delta u)\,\overline{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\rm div}(\overline{v}\,\nabla u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu\cdot{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(\overline{v}\,\nabla u)=\int_{\partial\Omega}i(\omega\cdot\nu)\,({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u)\,\overline{{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v}=(i(\omega\cdot\nu)\,{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u,j(v))_{K}

for all
v\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, where we used the divergence theorem
(see
[
Alt
]
Theorem A8.8).
So by density (see for example
[
Maz
]
Theorem 1.1.6/2)
one deduces that

\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)=(i(\omega\cdot\nu)\,{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u,j(v))_{K}

for all
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Hence

{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda})
and
({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u,i(\omega\cdot\nu)\,{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u)\in{\cal N}_{\lambda}
.
Moreover,

({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}j(u),j(u))_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,u)=\int_{\partial\Omega}i(\omega\cdot\nu)\,|{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u|^{2}=i\int_{\partial\Omega}(\omega\cdot\nu)=i\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\rm div}\omega=0.

Therefore

{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u\in\{\varphi\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda}):({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi,\varphi)_{L_{2}(\partial\Omega)}=0\}.

It is then obvious that

\dim\mathop{\rm span}\{\varphi\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda}):({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi,\varphi)_{L_{2}(\partial\Omega)}=0\}=\infty.

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

3
The Stokes operators

In this section we describe the Dirichlet and the Neumann version
of the Stokes operator on a Lipschitz domain with the aid of
sesquilinear forms.
We then apply Theorem
2.2
(abstract eigenvalues comparison theorem) to obtain
a Stokes version of Friedlander’s eigenvalues theorem.
For independent interest we present a description of the
Stokes type Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be a bounded open connected set with Lipschitz boundary.
We assume that
d\geq 2
.
We denote by
\nu
the outward normalised normal.
Let
H=L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,

V=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d}):\mathop{\rm div}u=0\}

and
K=L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Here and below
\mathop{\rm div}
is the divergence operator acting on distributions.
Then the inclusion
i\colon V\to H
is compact.
Let
j={\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\colon V\to K
be the trace operator.
Then also
j
is compact.
Define the form
\gothic{a}\colon V\times V\to\mathds{C}
by

\gothic{a}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}.

Then
\gothic{a}
is continuous and
i
-elliptic.
Let
A^{N}
be the self-adjoint operator in
\overline{V}\subset H

associated with
\gothic{a}
and let

A^{D}
be the self-adjoint operator in
\overline{V_{D}}\subset H

associated with
\gothic{a}|_{V_{D}\times V_{D}}
, where the
closures are in
H
and
V_{D}=\ker j
.
We first characterise
A^{D}
and
A^{N}
.

Define

C^{\infty}_{c,\sigma}(\Omega)=\{u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d}):\mathop{\rm div}u=0\},

the space of
solenoidal test functions
,
and let
L_{2,\sigma}(\Omega)
be the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c,\sigma}(\Omega)

in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(a)

The closure of the space
V_{D}
in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
is
L_{2,\sigma}(\Omega)
.

(b)

V_{D}^{\perp}=L_{2,\sigma}(\Omega)^{\perp}=\{\nabla\pi:\pi\in H^{1}(\Omega)\}
,
where the orthogonal complement is in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.

(c)

Let
u,f\in L_{2,\sigma}(\Omega)
.
Then
u\in D(A^{D})
and
A^{D}u=f
if and only if
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,

\mathop{\rm div}u=0
and there exists a
\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)
such that

f=-\Delta u+\nabla\pi
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.

Proof
.

‘
(a)
’.
This follows from
[
Soh
]
Lemma II.2.2.3.

‘
(b)
’.
The first equality is obvious.
The second one follows from
[
Tem
]
Theorem 1.1.4.

‘
(c)
’.
Clearly
V_{D}=\{w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d}):\mathop{\rm div}w=0\}
.
‘
\Rightarrow
’.
Since
A^{D}
is the operator associated with
\gothic{a}|_{V_{D}\times V_{D}}

it follows that
u\in V_{D}
and
\gothic{a}(u,v)=(f,v)_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}

for all
v\in V_{D}
.
So
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and
\mathop{\rm div}u=0
.
Consider
(f+\Delta u)\in H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
If
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and
\mathop{\rm div}v=0
, then

(f+\Delta u)(v)=(f,v)_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}+\langle\Delta u,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=(f,v)_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}-\gothic{a}(u,v)=0.

Since
\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary, it follows from

[
Soh
]
Lemma II.2.1.1b that there exists a
\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)

such that
f+\Delta u=\nabla\pi
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.

‘
\Leftarrow
’.
Obviously
u\in V_{D}
.
If
v\in V_{D}
, then
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and
\mathop{\rm div}v=0
.
So

\displaystyle\gothic{a}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\Delta u,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=\langle f-\nabla\pi,v\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\times H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(f,v)_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}+(\pi,\mathop{\rm div}v)_{L_{2}(\Omega)}=(f,v)_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}

and the proposition follows.
∎

We next consider the operator
A^{N}
.
In order to obtain a description as in Proposition
3.1
(c)

for
A^{D}
, we first need a kind of normal derivative.
If
\Omega
is smooth,
u\in H^{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,
\pi\in H^{1}(\Omega)

and
f\in L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

with
-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=f
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,
then

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\nu}u-({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\pi)\,\nu,{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\Phi\rangle_{H^{-1/2}\times H^{1/2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla\Phi}+\langle\Delta u,\Phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}}-\langle\nabla\pi,\Phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}}-\int_{\Omega}\pi\,\overline{\mathop{\rm div}\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}\pi\,\overline{\mathop{\rm div}\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\overline{\Phi}

for all
\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
We next define a weak version of this normal derivative.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,
\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)
and
f\in L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Suppose that
-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=f
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Then there exists a unique
F\in H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

such that

\langle F,\varphi\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}\pi\,\overline{\mathop{\rm div}\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\overline{\Phi}

for all
\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and

\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
with
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\Phi=\varphi
.

Proof
.

Let
\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and suppose that
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\Phi=0
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}\pi\,\overline{\mathop{\rm div}\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\overline{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta u,\Phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}}+\langle\nabla\pi,\Phi\rangle_{H^{-1}\times H^{1}}-\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\overline{\Phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle-\Delta u+\nabla\pi-f,\Phi\rangle_{H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\times H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=0.

Hence there exists a unique
F\colon H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\to\mathds{C}

such that

F(\varphi)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}\pi\,\overline{\mathop{\rm div}\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\overline{\Phi}

for all
\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and

\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
with
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\Phi=\varphi
.
Obviously
F
is anti-linear and

|F({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\Phi)|\leq(\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}+\|\pi\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})})\|\Phi\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}

for all
\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Since
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\colon H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\to H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

has a continuous right-inverse, the lemma follows.
∎

We denote the element
F\in H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
in Lemma
3.2

by
\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi)
.
So

\langle\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi),{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\Phi\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}\pi\,\overline{\mathop{\rm div}\Phi}-\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\overline{\Phi}

for all
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,
\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)
,
f\in L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

and
\Phi\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
with

-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=f
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.

Let
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,
\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)
,
f\in L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

and
c\in\mathds{C}
with

-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=f
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Then
-\Delta u+\nabla(\pi+c\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega})=f
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

and

\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi+c\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega})=\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi)-c\,\nu

(7)

by a straightforward calculation.

We also need to characterise the range
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}V
of the form domain under the
trace map.
Note that
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\colon H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\to H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
If
u\in V
, then the divergence theorem gives

0=\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\rm div}u=\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu\cdot{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u.

Define

L_{2,0}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})=\{\varphi\in L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d}):\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi\cdot\nu=0\}.

Then
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(V)\subset H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\cap L_{2,0}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
We next show that one actually has an equality.

Lemma 3.3
.

{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(V)=H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\cap L_{2,0}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.

Proof
.

We only have to show ‘
\supset
’.
Let
\varphi\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\cap L_{2,0}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
There exists a
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
such that

{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u=\varphi
.
Then
\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\rm div}u=\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu\cdot{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u=\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu\cdot\varphi=0
.
Hence by
[
Soh
]
Lemma II.2.1.1a there exists a
w\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

such that
\mathop{\rm div}w=\mathop{\rm div}u
.
Set
v=u-w
.
Then
v\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and
\mathop{\rm div}v=0
.
So
v\in V
.
Moreover,
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v={\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u=\varphi
.
∎

Although we do not need the next density lemma, we state it
for completeness.

Lemma 3.4
.

The range
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(V)
is dense in
L_{2,0}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.

Proof
.

Let
\varphi\in L_{2,0}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Since
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
is dense in
L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,
there is a sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}
in
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

such that

\lim\varphi_{n}=\varphi
in
L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Then

\lim\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi_{n}\cdot\nu=\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi\cdot\nu=0
.
Note that
\nu\in L_{\infty}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\subset L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Hence as above there is a sequence
(\nu_{n})_{n\in\mathds{N}}

in
H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
such that

\lim\nu_{n}=\nu
in
L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Then
\lim\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu_{n}\cdot\nu=\sigma(\partial\Omega)\neq 0
,
so we may assume that
\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu_{n}\cdot\nu\neq 0

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.
For all
n\in\mathds{N}
define

\tilde{\varphi}_{n}=\varphi_{n}-c_{n}\,\nu_{n}
,
where
c_{n}=\Big(\int_{\partial\Omega}\nu_{n}\cdot\nu\Big)^{-1}\int_{\partial\Omega}\varphi_{n}\cdot\nu
.
Then
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}\in H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\cap L_{2,0}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})={\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(V)

by Lemma
3.3
.
Moreover,
\lim\tilde{\varphi}_{n}=\varphi
in
L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
∎

Now we are able to characterise the operator
A^{N}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

(a)

The closure of the space
V
in
L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
is

\{u\in L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d}):\mathop{\rm div}u=0\}
.

(b)

(c)

.

if and only if
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,

in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

and

.

‘
(a)
’.
See
[

]

‘
(b)
’.
By definition of

is the closure of

Obviously the latter is the closure of the space

\{\nabla\pi:\pi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\}
in

.

]

in closed in

.

‘
(c)
’.
‘
\Rightarrow
’.
By definition

and
\gothic{a}(u,v)=(f,v)_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}

for all

.
So
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and
\mathop{\rm div}v=0
.
Consider

\mathop{\rm div}v=0

\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary, it follows from

[
Soh
]

\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)

such that

in

.

If

, then

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi),{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}-\int_{\Omega}\pi\,\overline{\mathop{\rm div}v}-\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\overline{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gothic{a}(u,v)-(f,v)_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=0.

\langle\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi),\varphi\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=0

Let

7

\langle\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi+c\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega}),\varphi\rangle_{H^{-1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\times H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=0

\Rightarrow
’.

‘
\Leftarrow
’.
Let

.

and

.
∎

\lambda\in\mathds{R}

.

, where the closure is in

.
Note that

\overline{D({\cal N}_{\lambda})}\subset\overline{j(V)}=\overline{{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}V}=L_{2,0}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

by Lemma
3.4
.
We next characterise

.

.

Let
\lambda\in\mathds{R}\setminus\sigma(A^{D})
.
Let

.
Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\varphi\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ})
and
{\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi=\psi
.

(ii)

There exist

and
\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)

•

\varphi={\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u
,

•

\mathop{\rm div}u=0
,

•

-\Delta u+\nabla\pi=\lambda\,u
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
, and

•

\psi=\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi)
.

Proof
.

‘
(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
’.
By definition there exists a
u\in V
such that
{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}u=\varphi
and

\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)=(\psi,{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v)_{L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}
for all
v\in V
.
Consider
(-\Delta u-\lambda\,u)\in H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
If
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and
\mathop{\rm div}v=0
, then

(-\Delta u-\lambda\,u)(v)=\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)=(\psi,{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v)_{L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=0.

Since
\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary, it follows from

[
Soh
]
Lemma II.2.1.1b that there exists a
\pi\in L_{2}(\Omega)

such that
-\Delta u-\lambda\,u=-\nabla\pi
in
H^{-1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.

Let
v\in V
.
Then

\displaystyle(\psi,{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v)_{L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\overline{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\overline{v}-\int_{\Omega}\pi\,\overline{\mathop{\rm div}v}=\langle\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi),{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v\rangle_{H^{-1/2}\times H^{1/2}}.

So

\langle\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi)-\psi,\tau\rangle_{H^{-1/2}\times H^{1/2}}=0

for all
\tau\in{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(V)=H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})\cap L_{2,0}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})

by Lemma
3.3
.
Now it follows as at the end of the proof of the implication ‘
\Rightarrow
’ in
the proof of Proposition
3.5
(c)
that there exists a

c\in\mathds{C}
such that
\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi+c\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega})=\psi
.

‘
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
’.
Let
u
and
\pi
be as in
(ii)
.
Note that
u\in V
.
Let
v\in V
.
Then

\displaystyle\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\overline{v}-\int_{\Omega}\pi\,\overline{\mathop{\rm div}v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{\nu}(u,\pi),{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v\rangle_{H^{-1/2}\times H^{1/2}}=\langle\psi,{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v\rangle_{H^{-1/2}\times H^{1/2}}=(\psi,{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v)_{L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}

and
(i)
follows.
∎

Now we are able to state and prove the main theorem of this paper.
For an explicit description of the Neumann Stokes operator and
Dirichlet Stokes operator we refer to Propositions
3.5
(c)

and
3.1
(c)
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be a bounded open connected set with Lipschitz boundary.
Let
A^{N}
and
A^{D}
be the Neumann Stokes operator and Dirichlet Stokes operator on
\Omega
,
respectively.
Further let
\lambda_{1}^{N}\leq\lambda_{2}^{N}\leq\ldots
and

\lambda_{1}^{D}\leq\lambda_{2}^{D}\leq\ldots
be the eigenvalues of
A^{N}
and
A^{D}

repeated with multiplicity, respectively.
Then

\lambda_{n+1}^{N}<\lambda_{n}^{D}

for all
n\in\mathds{N}
.

Proof
.

We have to verify Conditions
(I)
and
(II)

of Theorem
2.2
.

‘
(I)
’.
Let
u\in D(A^{N})\cap\ker j
and suppose that
u
is an
eigenvector for
A^{N}
with eigenvalue
\lambda\in\mathds{R}
.
Then
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and
\mathop{\rm div}u=0
in
\Omega
.
Let
\tilde{u}\in H^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d},\mathds{C}^{d})
be the extension by zero of
u
.
Then
\nabla\tilde{u}=\widetilde{\nabla u}
, the extension by zero
of
\nabla u
.
So
\mathop{\rm div}\tilde{u}=0
in
\mathds{R}^{d}
.
Let
v\in H^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d},\mathds{C}^{d})
and suppose that
\mathop{\rm div}v=0
in
\mathds{R}^{d}
.
Then

\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot\overline{\nabla v}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla(v|_{\Omega})}=\gothic{a}(u,v|_{\Omega})=(\lambda\,u,v|_{\Omega})_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=(\lambda\,\tilde{u},v)_{L_{2}(\mathds{R}^{d},\mathds{C}^{d})}.

So
\tilde{u}\in D(A_{\mathds{R}^{d}})
, the Stokes operator on
\mathds{R}^{d}
,
and
A_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\tilde{u}=\lambda\,\tilde{u}
.
Then
-\Delta\tilde{u}=\lambda\,\tilde{u}
by
[
Soh
]
Lemma III.2.3.2.
But
\tilde{u}
vanishes on a non-empty open set.
So
\tilde{u}=0
by the unique continuation property, see
for example
[
RS
]
Theorem XIII.57.
Hence
u=0
and
u
is not an eigenvector.

‘
(II)
’.
Let
\lambda>0
.
Let
\omega\in\mathds{R}^{d}
and suppose that
|\omega|^{2}=\lambda
.
Let
b\in\mathds{R}^{d}
be such that
|b|=1
and
b\cdot\omega=0
.
Define
\tau\in H^{2}(\Omega)
by
\tau(x)=e^{i\omega\cdot x}
.
Then
-\Delta\tau=|\omega|^{2}\,\tau=\lambda\,\tau
.
Let
u=\tau\,b\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Then
\mathop{\rm div}u=i\tau\,(b\cdot\omega)=0
, so
u\in V
.
Let
v\in V
.
Then

\displaystyle\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\overline{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\tau\cdot\overline{\nabla(b\cdot v)}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\tau\,\overline{(b\cdot v)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(\Delta\tau)\,\overline{(b\cdot v)}+\int_{\partial\Omega}(\partial_{\nu}\tau)\,\overline{{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(b\cdot v)}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\tau\,\overline{(b\cdot v)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}(\partial_{\nu}\tau)\,b\cdot\overline{{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v}=\int_{\partial\Omega}i\,(\omega\cdot\nu)\,({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\tau)\,b\cdot\overline{{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v}.

Therefore
j(u)\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda})
and

(j(u),i\,(\omega\cdot\nu)\,({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\tau)\,b)\in{\cal N}_{\lambda}
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}j(u),j(u))_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int_{\partial\Omega}(\omega\cdot\nu)\,|b|^{2}\,|{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\tau|^{2}=i\,|b|^{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}(\omega\cdot\nu)=i\,|b|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\mathop{\rm div}\omega=0.

Consequently

\dim\mathop{\rm span}\{\varphi\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda}):({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}\varphi,\varphi)_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=0\}=\infty

as required.
∎

We finish with a small extension of the previous theorem.

Example 3.8
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
be a bounded open connected set with Lipschitz boundary.
Let
H=L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
,

V=\{u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d}):\mathop{\rm div}u=0\}

and
K=L_{2}(\partial\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
Then the inclusion
i\colon V\to H
is compact.
Let
j={\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\colon V\to K
be the trace operator.
Then
j
is compact.
Fix
\alpha\in(-1,1]
.
Define the form
\gothic{a}\colon V\times V\to\mathds{C}
by

\gothic{a}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}+\alpha\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u)^{\rm T}\cdot\overline{\nabla v},

where the superscript
{\rm T}
denotes the transpose.
So Theorem
3.7
corresponds to the case
\alpha=0
.
Obviously
\gothic{a}
is continuous and if
|\alpha|<1
, then

\gothic{a}
is
i
-elliptic.
If
\alpha=1
, then

\mathop{\rm Re}\gothic{a}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\rm T}|^{2}

for all
u\in V
.
Since
\Omega
has a Lipschitz boundary, Korn’s second inequality states that
there exists a
C>0

such that

\|u\|_{H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}^{2}\leq C\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{\rm T}|^{2}+\|u\|_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}^{2}\Big)

for all
u\in H^{1}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
.
For an easy proof, see
[
Nit
]
Section 3.
Hence the form
\gothic{a}
is also
i
-elliptic if
\alpha=1
.
The associated operator
A^{N}
is the Neumann Stokes operator studied in
[
MMW
]

and
A^{D}
is the (Dirichlet) Stokes operator.
We next show that the conditions in Theorem
2.2
are valid.

‘
(I)
’.
Let
u\in D(A^{N})\cap\ker j
and suppose that
u
is an
eigenvector for
A^{N}
with eigenvalue
\lambda\in\mathds{R}
.
Then as before
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})
and
\mathop{\rm div}u=0
in
\Omega
.
Let
\tilde{u}\in H^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d},\mathds{C}^{d})
be the extension by zero of
u
.
Again
\nabla\tilde{u}=\widetilde{\nabla u}
, the extension by zero
of
\nabla u
, and
\mathop{\rm div}\tilde{u}=0
in
\mathds{R}^{d}
.
Let
v\in H^{1}(\mathds{R}^{d},\mathds{C}^{d})
and suppose that
\mathop{\rm div}v=0
in
\mathds{R}^{d}
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\Big(\nabla\tilde{u}+\alpha\,(\nabla\tilde{u})^{\rm T}\Big)\cdot\overline{\nabla v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big(\nabla u+\alpha\,(\nabla u)^{\rm T}\Big)\cdot\overline{\nabla(v|_{\Omega})}=\gothic{a}(u,v|_{\Omega})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\lambda\,u,v|_{\Omega})_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=(\lambda\,\tilde{u},v)_{L_{2}(\mathds{R}^{d},\mathds{C}^{d})}.

Since
\mathop{\rm div}\tilde{u}=0
, one deduces that

\int_{\mathds{R}^{d}}(\nabla\tilde{u})^{\rm T}\cdot\overline{\nabla v}=0.

Therefore
\tilde{u}\in D(A_{\mathds{R}^{d}})
, the Stokes operator on
\mathds{R}^{d}
,
and
A_{\mathds{R}^{d}}\tilde{u}=\lambda\,\tilde{u}
.
Now one can argue as in the proof of Theorem
3.7
that
u=0

and hence
u
is not an eigenvector.

‘
(II)
’.
Let
\lambda>0
.
Let
\omega\in\mathds{R}^{d}
and suppose that
|\omega|^{2}=\lambda
.
As before let
b\in\mathds{R}^{d}
and
\tau\in H^{2}(\Omega)

be such that
|b|=1
,
b\cdot\omega=0
and
\tau(x)=e^{i\omega\cdot x}
.
Then
\Delta\tau=-|\omega|^{2}\,\tau=-\lambda\,\tau
.
Let
u=\tau\,b
.
Then
\mathop{\rm div}u=i\tau\,(b\cdot\omega)=0
, so
u\in V
.
Let
v\in V
.
Then

\displaystyle\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}+\alpha\int_{\Omega}(\nabla u)^{\rm T}\cdot\overline{\nabla v}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\cdot\overline{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\tau\cdot\overline{\nabla(b\cdot v)}+\alpha\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}i\,\omega_{k}\,\tau\,b_{l}\,\overline{\partial_{l}v_{k}}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\tau\,\overline{(b\cdot v)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(\Delta\tau)\,\overline{(b\cdot v)}+\int_{\partial\Omega}(\partial_{\nu}\tau)\,\overline{{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}(b\cdot v)}

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt{}+\alpha\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{k}\,\omega_{l}\,\tau\,b_{l}\,\overline{v_{k}}+i\,\alpha\sum_{k,l=1}^{d}\int_{\partial\Omega}\omega_{k}\,({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\tau)\,\nu_{l}\,b_{l}\,\overline{{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v_{k}}-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\tau\,\overline{(b\cdot v)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial\Omega}(\partial_{\nu}\tau)\,b\cdot\overline{{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v}+i\,\alpha\int_{\partial\Omega}({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\tau)\,(\nu\cdot b)\,\omega\cdot\overline{{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}v},

where we used in the last step that
b\cdot\omega=0
.
Consequently
j(u)\in D({\cal N}_{\lambda})
and

(j(u),(\partial_{\nu}\tau)\,b+i\alpha\,({\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\tau)\,(\nu\cdot b)\,\omega)\in{\cal N}_{\lambda}
.
Moreover,

({\cal N}_{\lambda}^{\circ}j(u),j(u))_{L_{2}(\Omega,\mathds{C}^{d})}=\gothic{b}_{\lambda}(u,u)\\
=i\int_{\partial\Omega}(\omega\cdot\nu)\,|b|^{2}\,|{\mathop{\rm Tr\,}}\tau|^{2},

where we used once more that
b\cdot\omega=0
.
Then argue as in the proof of Theorem
3.7
.

Quasiparticle electronic structure and optical response ( G 0 ​ W 0 G_{0}W_{0} +BSE) of anatase TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} starting from modified HSE06 functionals
Sruthil Lal S.B, D Murali, Matthias Posselt, Assa Aravindh Sasikala Devi, Alok Sharan

Abstract
The quasiparticle electronic structure and optical excitation of anatase TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} is determined within the framework of many-body perturbation theory (MBPT) by combining the G 0 ​ W 0 G_{0}W_{0} method and the Bethe-Salpeter Equation (BSE). A modified version of the HSE06 screened hybrid functional, that includes 20% exact Fock exchange (HSE06(20)) as opposed to 25% in the standard HSE06 functional, is used to set up the starting Hamiltonian for G 0 ​ W 0 G_{0}W_{0} +BSE calculations. The HSE06(20) functional accurately predicts the ground state electronic band structure. BSE calculations based on data from G 0 ​ W 0 G_{0}W_{0} +HSE06(20) yield direct optical excitation energies and oscillator strengths in excellent agreement with existing experiments and theoretical calculations characterizing direct excitation. In particular, an exciton binding energy of 229 ± \pm 10 meV is obtained, in close agreement with experiments. The projections of excitonic states onto the quasiparticle band structure in a fatband representation shows that the lowest optical transition of anatase TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} consists of excitons originating from the mixing of direct transitions within band pairs running parallel to the Γ − Z \Gamma-Z direction in the tetragonal Brillouin zone. This implies a strong spatial localization of excitons in the x ​ y xy plane of the lattice. This investigation highlights the importance of a suitable non-interacting Hamiltonian for the MBPT based quasiparticle G 0 ​ W 0 G_{0}W_{0} and subsequent BSE calculations and suggests HSE06(20) as an optimal choice in the case of anatase TiO 2 \text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} .

Theoretical estimate of optical gaps by BSE calculations of pristine anatase

IV
Conclusion and Outlook

To conclude, we systematically investigated the quasiparticle electronic structure and and optical excitation of anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
within the framework of many-body perturbation theory (MBPT) by combining the
G_{0}W_{0}
method and the Bethe-Salpeter Equation (BSE). Non-interacting DFT Hamiltonian with a modified version of the HSE06 screened hybrid functional is employed as the staring point for
G_{0}W_{0}
+ BSE calculations. Fraction of exact Fock exchange in the HSE06 functional is tuned and the one which yields reasonably accurate ground state electronic structure is chosen as starting point for
G_{0}W_{0}
+ BSE simulations. Ground state DFT calculation with HSE06(20) functional yields 3.34 eV as the electronic gap of anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. It reliably predicts the covalent character of Ti-O bonds, electronic bandstructure, fundamental gap and density of states of
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Further, carefully converged
G_{0}W_{0}
+ BSE calculation on HSE06(20) starting wavefunction predicts first direct optical excitation of anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
at 3.911 eV, in agreement with experiments that measures direct optical excitation. This optical excitation creates strongly bound excitons with binding energy of 229
\pm
10 meV, with respect to
G_{0}W_{0}
-HSE06(20) quasiparticle gap. The projections of excitonic states onto the quasiparticle band structure in a fatband representation shows that the optical transition at 3.911 eV consists of excitons are originating from the mixing of single direct transitions within band pairs running parallel to the
\Gamma-Z
direction in the tetragonal Brillouin zone.

The present work highlights the importance of a suitable non-interacting Hamiltonian for the use in quasiparticle MBPT and subsequent BSE calculations. We observe that, irrespective of PBE or HSE06(
\alpha=20,25,30\%)
, the BSE excitonic spectra computed with respective starting points predicts the region of Brillouin zone important for optical gap to be along the
\Gamma-Z
. Nevertheless, as we obtained in the present work,
G_{0}W_{0}
+ BSE on PBE functional starting points underestimates the exciton binding energy(93
\pm
10 meV) of anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. The exciton binding energy as predicted in the present work with HSE06(20) starting wavefunction is more consistent with literature than those with LDA/GGA or DFT+U starting points. This leaves HSE06(20) as an optimal choice for non-interacting Hamiltonian for MBPT calculation in the case of anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
.

Ground state (DFT) and excited state (MBPT) properties computed based on HSE06(20) functional are very encouraging for its continued use and extension into other systems too. This generalization is particularly true in the case of other metal and transition metal oxides where localized
d
and
f
electrons strongly influence the electronic structure and optical response. Our study provides a reference to MBPT calculation of doped
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and with intrinsic defects so that reasonable comparison of physical observable at same level theory can be performed. This MBPT calculation of pristine anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
with HSE06(20) functional staring point will serve as a benchmark for extending calculation into systems where we cannot exclude the significant role of exchange-correlations in starting wavefunction and
d
electron localization problem.

The prediction of strongly bound excitons in anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and its localization enabled by BSE calculation on HSE06(20) can potentially help us throw light on some of the challenging and open questions in the literature related to
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. Characterizing band alignment in anatase and rutile structures with HSE06(20) functionals would deepen our insights on longstanding questions such as why anatase-rutile mixed phases
Scanlon et al. 2013
;
Wei et al. 2020
are more photo-catalytically active than individual phases. Another challenge is related to the reactivity of different facets in anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. The particular spatial localization of excitons in the {001} plane can partly offer possible explanations to the question as to why the thermodynamically less stable {001} facets in
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
is more photo-reactive compared to stable {101} ones
Yang et al. 2008
;
Hu et al. 2014
;
Vittadini et al. 1998
;
Pan et al. 2011
. The advancement in controlled synthesis of anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
having defined crystal facets at desired crystallographic orientations
Selloni 2008
;
Yang et al. 2008
;
Gong and Selloni 2005
, harvesting the exciton delocalization in anatase for devices, and nonlinear optical applications has become close to reality. The demonstration of selective and controlled modulation of oscillator strength of excitons by acoustic phonons
Baldini et al. 2018
and huge exciton shifts by coupling exciton to the optically induced strain pulses
Baldini et al. 2019
in anatase
\text{TiO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
nanoparticles hold huge promises in this direction. For modelling such physical processes and design further experiments and devices, HSE06(20) hybrid functional based MBPT computations will definitely act as reliable benchmarks and test strategy.

On a central limit theorem in renewal theory Thanks: Supported by the Knut and Alice Wallenberg Foundation
Svante Janson

Abstract
. Serfozo (2009, Theorem 2.65) gives a useful central limit theorem for processes with regenerative increments. Unfortunately, there is a gap in the proof. We fill this gap, and at the same time we weaken the assumptions. Furthermore, we give conditions for moment convergence in this setting. We give also further results complementing results in Serfozo (2009) on the law of large numbers and estimates for the mean; in particular, we show that there is a gap between conditions for the weak and strong laws of large numbers.



1.
Introduction

The standard setting in renewal theory is that we have a stochastic process
(in continuous or discrete time) such that some event occurs at
random times
0<T_{1}<T_{2}<\dots
, and the process “starts again” at each
such event.
Formally this means that the times between renewals

\xi_{n}:=T_{n}-T_{n-1}
,
n\geqslant 1
,
(where we define
T_{0}:=0
)
are i.i.d.
(independent and identically distributed) random variables.
We thus have

\displaystyle T_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{i},\qquad n\geqslant 0.

(1.1)

with
(\xi_{i})_{1}^{\infty}
i.i.d.,
where
\xi_{1}
may be any (strictly) positive random variable.
We then define, for
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle N(t)

\displaystyle:=\max\{n:T_{n}\leqslant t\}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\boldsymbol{1}\{T_{n}\leqslant t\},

(1.2)

\displaystyle\tau(t)

\displaystyle:=\min\{n:T_{n}>t\}=N(t)+1.

(1.3)

It is well-known that
T_{n}\to\infty
a.s. as
{n\to\infty}
, and thus
N(t)
and

\tau(t)
are well defined for any
t\geqslant 0
.
Note that by the definitions,

\displaystyle T_{N(t)}\leqslant t<T_{{N(t)+1}}=T_{\tau(t)}.

(1.4)

In applications it is common to study the values of another stochastic
process at the renewal times
T_{n}
. One common version of this is to
let
(\eta_{i})_{1}^{\infty}
be another sequence of random variables such that
the random vectors
(\xi_{i},\eta_{i})
,
i\geqslant 1
, are i.i.d.,
and define their partial sums

\displaystyle V_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i};

(1.5)

we then may
consider
V_{N(t)}
or
V_{\tau(t)}
, which can be interpreted as the values
of a stochastic process at the time
T_{N(t)}
or
T_{\tau(t)}
.
Asymptotic results such as a law of large numbers and a central limit theorem
for
V_{N(t)}
or
V_{\tau(t)}
are well known, see e.g.

[
2
, Section 4.2, including Remark 4.2.10]
.

A version of this is that we are given another
(real-valued) stochastic process
Z(t)

defined for all times
t\geqslant 0
such that
Z(t)
also “starts again” at
each renewal time
T_{n}
. We are interested in asymptotic results for
Z(t)

as
{t\to\infty}
, and by (
1.4
), we may under suitable assumptions approximate

Z(t)
by
V_{N(t)}
or
V_{\tau(t)}
(with
\eta_{n}
given below)
and obtain results for
Z(t)
from
results of the type just mentioned.
To make this formal,
Serfozo 2009

makes the following definition:

Definition 1.1
.

The process
Z(t)
has

regenerative increments over the times
T_{n}
if

Z(0)=0
and
the increments

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}:=\bigl(\xi_{n},\,\{Z(t+T_{n-1})-Z(T_{n-1}):0\leqslant t\leqslant\xi_{n}\}\bigr),\qquad n\geqslant 1,

(1.6)

are i.i.d.

Remark 1.2
.

Note that the second component of
\zeta_{n}
is a stochastic process, defined
over the random interval
[0,\xi_{n}]
; we may for the purpose of this
definition regard it as a process on
[0,\infty)
stopped at
\xi_{n}
,
i.e., equal to
Z(\xi_{n}+T_{n-1})-Z(T_{n-1})
for all
t\geqslant\xi_{n}
.
In general we may regard this process as an
element of the product space
\mathbb{R}^{[0,\infty)}

but we will
also need some regularity property.
We assume, for convenience, that
Z(t)
is càdlàg
(right-continuous with left limits, also written
Z\in D[0,\infty)
);
then the second component of

\zeta_{n}
is also càdlàg.

\triangle

Remark 1.3
.

The definition given in
[
4
, Definition 2.52]
actually differs
slightly from the one above, using only the interval
0\leqslant t<\xi_{n}

instead of
0\leqslant t\leqslant\xi_{n}
in (
1.6
).
This is obviously a typo, and should be interpreted as in (
1.6
), since
otherwise the definition would be trivially satisfied by
the process
Z(t):=X_{N(t)}
for
any
random sequence
(X_{n})_{0}^{\infty}

with
X_{0}=0
;
this evidently cannot imply any limit results.

\triangle

Suppose that
Z(t)
has regenerative increments over
T_{n}
, and define

\displaystyle\eta_{n}:=Z(T_{n})-Z(T_{n-1}),\qquad n\geqslant 1.

(1.7)

It follows from the definition above, taking
t=\xi_{n}
in (
1.6
), that
the sequence of pairs
{(\xi_{n},\eta_{n})}
,
n\geqslant 1
, is i.i.d.
Hence, we may define
V_{n}
by (
1.5
), and note that (
1.7
) yields

\displaystyle V_{n}=Z(T_{n}).

(1.8)

Consequently, using (
1.4
), we have for any
t\geqslant 0

\displaystyle Z(t)=V_{N(t)}+\bigl(Z(t^{\prime}+T_{N(t)})-Z(T_{N(t)})\bigr),

(1.9)

where
t^{\prime}:=t-T_{N(t)}\in[0,\xi_{N(t)+1})
.
We define, following
[
4
]
,

\displaystyle M_{n}:=\sup_{T_{n-1}\leqslant t\leqslant T_{n}}|Z(t)-Z(T_{n-1})|,\qquad n\geqslant 1.

(1.10)

In particular,

\displaystyle M_{1}:=\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T_{1}}|Z(t)|.

(1.11)

Note that it follows from Definition
1.1
that the random variables
M_{n}
are i.i.d.

It is clear from (
1.9
) that with suitable conditions on
M_{n}
,
asymptotic results for
Z(T)
follow from results for
V_{N(t)}
.
In particular,
Serfozo 2009
gives the following central limit theorem,
which is well suited for applications.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
4
, Theorem 2.65]
)
.

Suppose
Z(t)
is a stochastic process with
regenerative increments over
T_{n}
such that
\mu:=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[T_{1}]
,

a:=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[Z(T_{1})]/\mu
,
and
\sigma^{2}:=\operatorname{Var}[Z(T_{1})-aT_{1}]
are finite.
In addition, let

M_{1}
be defined by (
1.11
),
and assume
M_{1}<\infty
a.s.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{Z(t)-at}{\sqrt{t}}\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\longrightarrow}N(0,\sigma^{2}/\mu),\qquad\text{as ${t\to\infty}$}.

(1.12)

We include the case
\sigma^{2}=0
, letting
N(0,0)
denote (the distribution of) 0.

Remark 1.5
.

The theorem stated in
[
4
]
also assumes
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}M_{1}<\infty
, but
the proof below shows that it suffices to assume
M_{1}<\infty
a.s., as done
here.

\triangle

Unfortunately, there is a gap in the proof given in
[
4
]
, see
Remark
2.1
, so we
give a proof filling that gap (under our, weaker, conditions)
in Section
2
.

In Section
3
, we give conditions for moment convergence in
Theorem
1.4
.
Furthermore, we give in Section
4
a weak law of large numbers,
complementing the strong law in
[
4
]
; we show that the weak law
holds under weaker conditions than the strong law.
Finally, Section
5
gives further estimates for the mean under various
moment conditions.

Remark 1.6
.

We let throughout the paper the time parameter
t\in[0,\infty)
be a continuous
variable. Results for a discrete time parameter
t\in\mathbb{N}
follow
immediately, by assuming
that the times
T_{n}
are integer-valued and then considering only
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

\triangle

1.1.
Some notation

We use the notation introduced above throughout the paper.
In particular,
\mu
,
a
, and
\sigma^{2}
have the same meanings as in
Theorem
1.4
.
We also use the
renewal function

\displaystyle U(t):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(T_{n}\leqslant t\bigr)=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}N(t)+1.

(1.13)

We let
\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\longrightarrow}
,
\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}
, and
\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}
denote convergence in distribution,
probability,
and almost surely,
respectively.
Unspecified limits are as
{t\to\infty}
.

For real
x,y
, we let
x\land y:=\min\{x,y\}

and
x\lor y:=\max\{x,y\}
.

“Decreasing” is interpreted in the weak sense.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

We basically follow the proof in
[
4
, pp. 136–137]
.
We define as there

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}(t):=\frac{Z(T_{N(t)})-aT_{N(t)}}{\sqrt{t}}=t^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{{N(t)}}\bigl(\eta_{i}-a\xi_{i}\bigr),

(2.1)

where we used (
1.7
) and (
1.1
),
and note that
X_{i}:=\eta_{i}-a\xi_{i}
are i.i.d. random variables with

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}X_{i}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}X_{1}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[Z(T_{1})]-a\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[T_{1}]=0

and
\operatorname{Var}X_{i}=\sigma^{2}
. Hence it follows from
Anscombe’s theorem
[
4
, Theorem 2.64]

(see also
[
2
, Section 1.3]
;
alternatively, use instead Donsker’s theorem
[
3
, Theorem 7.7.13]
),
together with the (weak) law of large numbers
{N(t)}/t\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}1/\mu
,

[
4
, Corollary 2.11]

that

\displaystyle Z^{\prime}(t)\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\longrightarrow}N\bigl(0,\sigma^{2}/\mu\bigr),\qquad\text{as ${t\to\infty}$}.

(2.2)

(The case
\sigma^{2}=0
is trivial since then
X_{i}=0
and thus
Z^{\prime}(t)=0
a.s.)

Hence, by Cramér–Slutsky’s theorem

[
3
, Theorem 5.11.4]
,
it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\frac{Z(t)-at}{\sqrt{t}}-Z^{\prime}(t)\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}0.

(2.3)

We have, by (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\frac{Z(t)-at}{\sqrt{t}}-Z^{\prime}(t)=\frac{Z(t)-Z_{N(t)}-a(t-T_{N(t)})}{\sqrt{t}}

(2.4)

and thus, recalling (
1.4
), (
1.10
), and (
1.1
),

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{Z(t)-at}{\sqrt{t}}-Z^{\prime}(t)\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{M_{{N(t)}+1}+|a|\xi_{{N(t)}+1}}{\sqrt{t}}=:\frac{Y_{{N(t)}+1}}{\sqrt{t}},

(2.5)

where we let

\displaystyle Y_{n}:=M_{n}+|a|\xi_{n}.

(2.6)

Consequently, to show (
2.3
), and thus Theorem
1.4
, it suffices to show
that

\displaystyle\frac{Y_{N(t)+1}}{t^{1/2}}\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}0,

(2.7)

or, equivalently,

\displaystyle\frac{Y_{N(t)+1}}{{N(t)}^{1/2}}\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}0,

(2.8)

By (
2.6
), (
1.10
) and Definition
1.1
, the random vectors
(\xi_{n},Y_{n})

are i.i.d., and thus (
2.7
) follows from Lemma
2.2
below (with

\delta(t):=t^{-1/2}
), which completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

The gap in the proof in
[
4
]
is the claim

n^{-1/2}Y_{n}\overset{\mathrm{d}}{=}n^{-1/2}Y_{1}\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}0
as
{n\to\infty}
made there;
although
Y_{n}\overset{\mathrm{d}}{=}Y_{1}
and
n^{-1/2}Y_{1}\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}0
as
{n\to\infty}
,
this only shows

Y_{n}/n^{1/2}\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}0
, which in general is not enough to imply (
2.8
).
In fact, it is a well-known consequence
of the Borel–Cantelli lemmas
(see
[
3
, Proposition 6.1.1]
)
that,
for any i.i.d. sequence
Y_{n}
, we have
Y_{n}/n^{1/2}\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}0

as
{n\to\infty}

(which does imply (
2.8
))
if and only if

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}{Y_{1}^{2}}<\infty
, which requires the stronger assumption

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}{M_{1}^{2}}<\infty

(and also
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}{T_{1}^{2}}<\infty
unless
a=0
).

\triangle

We used in the proof the following lemma.
(For related results under moment assumptions, see
[
2
, Theorem
1.8.1]
.)
Recall that a family
(X_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}
of random variables is
tight

(a.k.a.
stochastically bounded
) if for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists

c>0
such that
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}(|X_{\alpha}|>c)<\varepsilon
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
.
Recall also that (the distribution of)
\xi_{1}
is
arithmetic
if there
exists
d>0
such that
\xi_{1}\in d\mathbb{N}=\{d,2d,\dots\}
a.s.; then the
largest such
d
is called the
span
of
\xi_{1}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

With the notations above,
let
T_{n}
be a sequence of renewal times with
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}T_{1}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}\xi_{1}<\infty
,
and let
Y_{n}
,
n\geqslant 1
, be another sequence of random variables such that
the random vectors
(\xi_{n},Y_{n})
are i.i.d.
Then the family of random variables

\{Y_{N(t)+1}:t\geqslant 0\}
is tight.
In particular, if

\delta(t)
is any positive function such that
\delta(t)\to 0

as
{t\to\infty}
, then

\displaystyle\delta(t)Y_{N(t)+1}\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}0\qquad\text{as }{t\to\infty}.

(2.9)

Proof.

By replacing
Y_{n}
with
|Y_{n}|
, we may for convenience assume that
Y_{n}\geqslant 0
.
Moreover, if
\xi_{1}
is arithmetic, with span
d>0
, then it suffices to
consider
t\in d\mathbb{N}
.

Let
c>0
.
Then, since
(\xi_{n+1},Y_{n+1})
is independent of
T_{n}
and further

(\xi_{n+1},Y_{n+1})\overset{\mathrm{d}}{=}(\xi_{1},Y_{1})
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(Y_{N(t)+1}>c\bigr)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\boldsymbol{1}\{T_{n}\leqslant t<T_{n+1},\;Y_{n+1}>c\}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(T_{n}\leqslant t<T_{n+1},\;Y_{n+1}>c\mid T_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(0\leqslant t-T_{n}<\xi_{n+1},\;Y_{n+1}>c\mid T_{n}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{c}(t-T_{n}),

(2.10)

where

\displaystyle h_{c}(s):=\boldsymbol{1}\{s\geqslant 0\}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(\xi_{1}>s,\;Y_{1}>c\bigr)=\boldsymbol{1}\{s\geqslant 0\}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(T_{1}>s,\;Y_{1}>c\bigr).

(2.11)

Using the renewal function
U(t)
defined in (
1.13
),
we can write (
2
) as

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(Y_{N(t)+1}>c\bigr)=U*h_{c}(t):=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(t-u)\,\mathrm{d}U(u).

(2.12)

Note that
h_{c}(s)\geqslant 0
and that
h_{c}(s)
is decreasing
on
[0,\infty)
with

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{c}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}(\xi_{1}>s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}\xi_{1}=\mu<\infty.

(2.13)

Hence,
h_{c}(s)
is directly Riemann integrable, and thus the key renewal
theorem
(see
[
4
, Theorems 2.35–37]
or
[
2
, Theorem 2.4.3]
)
yields (in both the arithmetic and non-arithmetic cases)

\displaystyle\lim_{{t\to\infty}}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(Y_{N(t)+1}>c\bigr)=\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}h_{c}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(\xi_{1}>s,\;Y_{1}>c\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s=:\lambda_{c}.

(2.14)

Since
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(\xi_{1}>s,\;Y_{1}>c\bigr)\leqslant\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(\xi_{1}>s\bigr)
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(\xi_{1}>s\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}\xi_{1}<\infty,

(2.15)

dominated convergence yields

\displaystyle\lim_{{c\to\infty}}\lambda_{c}=\lim_{{c\to\infty}}\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(\xi_{1}>s,\;Y_{1}>c\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{0}^{\infty}\lim_{{c\to\infty}}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(\xi_{1}>s,\;Y_{1}>c\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s=0.

(2.16)

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then (
2.16
) shows that we may choose
c>0
such that

\lambda_{c}<\varepsilon
, and then (
2.14
) shows that for all sufficiently large

t
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(Y_{N(t)+1}>c\bigr)<\varepsilon.

(2.17)

This is enough to imply (
2.9
), since
for large
t
we also have
c\delta(t)<\varepsilon
, and consequently

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(\delta(t)Y_{N(t)+1}>\varepsilon\bigr)\leqslant\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(\delta(t)Y_{N(t)+1}>\delta(t)c\bigr)=\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(Y_{N(t)+1}>c\bigr)<\varepsilon.

(2.18)

Moreover, we have shown that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
c
and

t_{0}
such that (
2.17
) holds for
t\geqslant t_{0}
.
There exists
n_{0}
such that
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}(N(t_{0})+1>n_{0})<\varepsilon/2
,
and we may increase
c
so that
\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}(Y_{n}>c)<\varepsilon/(2n_{0})
for all

n\leqslant n_{0}
.
Then, for every
t\leqslant t_{0}
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}(Y_{N(t)+1}>c)\leqslant\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(N(t)+1>n_{0}\bigr)+\sum_{n=1}^{n_{0}}\operatorname{\mathbb{P}{}}\bigl(Y_{n}>c\bigr)<\varepsilon.

(2.19)

Hence, there exists
c
such that (
2.17
) holds for all
t\geqslant 0
,
which proves that the family
\{Y_{N(t)+1}:t\geqslant 0\}
is tight.
∎

3.
Moment convergence

If we assume further moment conditions, we also have convergence of moments
in Theorem
1.4
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
r\geqslant 2
, and
assume in addition to the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[T_{1}^{r}]<\infty
and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[M_{1}^{r}]<\infty
.
Then
the family of random variables

\displaystyle\left\{\left\lvert\frac{Z(t)-at}{\sqrt{t}}\right\rvert^{r}:t\geqslant 1\right\}

(3.1)

is uniformly integrable, and consequently (
1.12
) holds with convergence
of all moments (absolute and ordinary) of orders
\leqslant r
.

Proof.

It will be convenient to use
\tau(t)=N(t)+1
instead of
N(t)
, since

\tau(t)
is a stopping time.
We thus define, similarly to (
2.1
),

\displaystyle Z^{\prime\prime}(t):=\frac{Z(T_{\tau(t)})-aT_{\tau(t)}}{\sqrt{t}}=t^{-1/2}\sum_{i=1}^{{\tau(t)}}\bigl(\eta_{i}-a\xi_{i}\bigr).

(3.2)

Since
|\eta_{n}|\leqslant M_{n}
by (
1.7
) and (
1.10
),
we have, using also (
2.1
) and (
2.6
),

\displaystyle|Z^{\prime\prime}(t)-Z^{\prime}(t)|=\left\lvert\frac{\eta_{\tau(t)}-a\xi_{\tau(t)}}{\sqrt{t}}\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{M_{{\tau(t)}}+|a|\xi_{{\tau(t)}}}{\sqrt{t}}=\frac{Y_{{\tau(t)}}}{\sqrt{t}},

(3.3)

Thus (
2.5
) yields

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{Z(t)-at}{\sqrt{t}}-Z^{\prime\prime}(t)\right\rvert\leqslant\frac{2Y_{{\tau(t)}}}{\sqrt{t}}

(3.4)

and consequently

\displaystyle\left\lvert\frac{Z(t)-at}{\sqrt{t}}\right\rvert\leqslant\left\lvert Z^{\prime\prime}(t)\right\rvert+\frac{2Y_{{\tau(t)}}}{\sqrt{t}}.

(3.5)

Since
Z(T_{\tau(t)})=V_{\tau(t)}
by (
1.8
), the uniform integrability
of
\bigl\{|Z^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{r}:t\geqslant 1\bigr\}
follows by
[
2
, Theorem 4.2.3(ii)]

applied to
\sum_{i=1}^{\tau(t)}\widehat{\eta}_{i}
with
\widehat{\eta}_{i}:=\eta_{i}-a\xi_{i}
;
note that
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}|\eta_{1}|^{r}\leqslant\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[M_{1}^{r}]<\infty
and thus also

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}|\widehat{\eta}_{1}|^{r}<\infty
.

Furthermore, recalling
\xi_{1}=T_{1}
, the assumptions and (
2.6
)
yield
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}|Y_{1}|^{r}<\infty
. The family

\{\tau(t)/t:t\geqslant 1\}
is uniformly integrable by

[
2
, (2.5.6), see also the more general Theorem 3.7.1]
,
and
\tau(t)
are stopping times, and thus

[
2
, Theorem 1.8.1]
shows that the family

\bigl\{|Y_{\tau(t)}|^{r}/t:t\geqslant 1\bigr\}
is uniformly integrable.
Since
r\geqslant 2
, this implies that also the family

\bigl\{\bigl(|Y_{\tau(t)}|/\sqrt{t}\bigr)^{r}:t\geqslant 1\bigr\}
is uniformly integrable.

The uniform integrability of (
3.1
) now follows by (
3.5
).
As is well known, this implies moment convergence in (
1.12
),
see e.g.
[
3
, Theorem 5.5.9]
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose
Z(t)
is a stochastic process with
regenerative increments over
T_{n}

such that

\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[T_{1}^{2}]

and
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[M_{1}^{2}]
are finite,
and
\operatorname{Var}\,[Z(T_{1})-\frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}{Z(T_{1})}}{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}{T_{1}}}T_{1}]>0
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{Z(t)-\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[Z(t)]}{\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}[Z(t)]}}\overset{\mathrm{d}}{\longrightarrow}N(0,1),\qquad\text{as ${t\to\infty}$}.

(3.6)

Proof.

Note that

\bigl\lvert Z(T_{1})\bigr\rvert\leqslant M_{1}
by (
1.11
), and thus
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[Z(T_{1})^{2}]<\infty
.
It follows that
the assumptions of Theorem
1.4
hold, and so do the assumptions of Theorem
3.1

with
r=2
.

4.
Law of large numbers

As a complement to the results on asymptotic normality,
we give both weak and strong laws of large numbers for processes with
regenerative increments. The strong law is given in
[
4
]
, but
repeated here for completeness.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose
Z(t)
is a stochastic process with
regenerative increments over
T_{n}
such that
\mu:=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[T_{1}]
and

a:=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[Z(T_{1})]/\mu

are finite.
In addition, let

M_{1}
be defined by (
1.11
).

(i)

(Weak LLN.)
If
M_{1}<\infty
a.s., then

Z(t)/t\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}a
as
{t\to\infty}
.

(ii)

(Strong LLN
[
4
, Theorem 2.54]
.)
If
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}M_{1}<\infty
, then

Z(t)/t\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}a
as
{t\to\infty}
.

Proof.

By
[
2
, Theorem 4.2.1]
, we have the strong law of large numbers

\displaystyle\frac{Z(T_{\tau(t)})}{t}=\frac{V_{\tau(t)}}{t}\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}\frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[Z(T_{1})]}{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[T_{1}]}=a.

(4.1)

Moreover,

{\tau(t)}/t\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}1/\mu
by

[
2
, Theorem 2.5.1(i)]
,
and thus

[
2
, Theorem 1.2.3(i)]
(with
r=1
)
shows that

\displaystyle\frac{\eta_{\tau(t)}}{t}\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}0.

(4.2)

Consequently,

\displaystyle\frac{Z(T_{N(t)})}{t}=\frac{Z(T_{\tau(t)})-\eta_{\tau(t)}}{t}\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}a.

(4.3)

Hence, the weak and strong laws in
(i)
and
(ii)
are
equivalent to, respectively,

\displaystyle\frac{Z(t)-Z(T_{N(t)})}{t}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}0,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{Z(t)-Z(T_{N(t)})}{t}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}0.

(4.5)

The proof is completed as follows.

(i)
:
We have, by (
1.10
) and (
1.4
),

\displaystyle M_{{N(t)}+1}=\sup_{T_{N(t)}\leqslant s\leqslant T_{N(t)+1}}\bigl\lvert Z(s)-Z(T_{N(t)})\bigr\rvert\geqslant\bigl\lvert Z(t)-Z(T_{N(t)})\bigr\rvert.

(4.6)

Lemma
2.2
with
Y_{n}:=M_{n}
shows that
M_{{N(t)+1}}/t\overset{\mathrm{p}}{\longrightarrow}0
, and thus (
4.4
)
follows from (
4.6
).

(ii)
:
Since
{N(t)}/t\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}1/\mu>0

[
2
, Theorem 2.5.1(i)]
,
(
4.5
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\frac{Z(t)-Z(T_{N(t)})}{{N(t)}}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}0,\qquad\text{as ${t\to\infty}$,}

(4.7)

and thus to

\displaystyle\sup_{T_{n}\leqslant t<T_{n+1}}\frac{\lvert Z(t)-Z(T_{n})\rvert}{n}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}0,\qquad\text{as ${n\to\infty}$}.

(4.8)

Define

\displaystyle M_{n}^{\prime}:=\sup_{T_{n-1}\leqslant t<T_{n}}|Z(t)-Z(T_{n-1})|,\qquad n\geqslant 1;

(4.9)

cf. (
1.10
) and note that

\displaystyle M_{n}=M_{n}^{\prime}\lor|Z(T_{n})-Z(T_{n-1})|.

(4.10)

We can write (
4.8
) as

\displaystyle\frac{M_{n+1}^{\prime}}{n}

\displaystyle\overset{\mathrm{a.s.}}{\longrightarrow}0,\qquad\text{as ${n\to\infty}$}.

(4.11)

Since the sequence
\{M^{\prime}_{n}\}
is i.i.d., (
4.11
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}M^{\prime}_{1}<\infty,

(4.12)

see
[
3
, Proposition 6.1.1]
.
Furthermore,
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}|Z(T_{1})|<\infty
by assumption, and thus (
4.10
) shows
that (
4.12
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}M_{1}=\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}{}[M^{\prime}_{1}\lor|Z(T_{1})|]<\infty.

(4.13)

The chain of equivalences above shows that (
4.5
) is equivalent to
(
4.13
).
∎

The proof shows that, under the assumption that

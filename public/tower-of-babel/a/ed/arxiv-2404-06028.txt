Correlation decoupling of Casimir interaction in an electrolyte driven by external electric fields
Guangle Du, David S. Dean, Bing Miao, Rudolf Podgornik

Abstract
It is well established that the long range van der Waals or thermal Casimir interaction between two semi-infinite dielectrics separated by a distance H H is screened by an intervening electrolyte. Here we show how this interaction is modified when an electric field of strength E E is applied parallel to the dielectric boundaries, leading to a non-equilibrium steady state with a current. The presence of the field induces a long range thermal repulsive interaction, scaling just like the thermal Casimir interaction between dielectrics without the intervening electrolyte, i.e. as 1 / H 3 1/H^{3} . At small E E the effect is of order E 2 E^{2} while at large fields it saturates to an E E independent value. We explain the results in terms of a decoupling mechanism between the charge density fluctuations of cations and anions at large applied fields.

We now turn to the computation of the force between the two dielectrics. In writing Eq. (

The dipole fluctuations also generate their own thermal component of the Casimir force between two dielectrics, which would be present in the absence of any electrolyte,

Conclusions–
Using non-equilibrium SDFT for an electrolyte confined between two dielectrics in an external driving electric field, we first of all recover the screened thermal Casimir interaction from a purely dynamical approach. For asymptotically large separations the behavior is given by the simple form of Eq.(
1
). At large
E
exponentially screened terms correspond to the standard screened thermal Casimir force with the Debye screening length for vanishing electric field, and to a Debye screening length at half the ion density for large electric fields, this effect being due to decoupled ionic correlations in the presence of strong external driving. Many extensions of this work are possible such as the extension to non-symmetric electrolytes (both charges and ion mobilities) and to the case where the field is applied perpendicular to the surfaces which requires the application of SDFT to inhomogeneous systems.
The non-equilibrium effects predicted here could be tested either in the surface force apparatus
[
32
]
or colloidal probe atomic force microscopy interaction geometry
[
33
]
.

Appendix A
Supplementary Material

This Supplementary Material provides additional technical details of the physical model and of the calculations in the letter.

Section (I)–
First we discuss the model for the confined electrolyte and how the fast relaxation of the dielectric components of the model with respect to the slow ionic degrees of freedom lead to an effective model of the ions where image charges are introduced as well as a thermal van der Waals interaction due to the direct interaction of the dielectric materials in the system.

Section (II)–
We then carry out a Debye-Hückel type of analysis where we expand the density fluctuations to second order about the mean density, deriving an effective quadratic Hamiltonian for the density fluctuations. For completeness sake, within this formalism, we rederive the well known equilibrium results for the screened thermal Casimir interaction between two dielectric slabs sandwiching an electrolyte in the limit where
\epsilon^{\prime}
, the dielectric constant of the surrounding dielectric medium is much smaller than
\epsilon
the dielectric constant of the intervening solvent which contains the electrolyte.

Section (III)–
We derive the stress tensor to compute the forces generated by the ionic degrees of freedom for a general non-equilibrium situation and show how ionic density fluctuations lead to an additional term in the stress tensor with respect to the Maxwell stress tensor.

Section (IV)–
We write down the dynamical equations for the densities of the anions and cations and
show how the linearized form of these equations leads to a model B type conserved dynamics with the quadratic Hamiltonian of Section (2). We solve these equations in the presence of a driving electric field
{\bf E}=E{\bf e}_{x}
parallel to the surfaces, here in the
x
direction, to compute the correlation functions of the density fluctuations in the resulting steady state.

Section (V)–
We use the results of Section (4) to compute the
H
(separation between the two surfaces bounding the dielectric) dependent part of the interaction between the two slabs. We show how in the case where
E=0
we recover the equilibrium screened thermal Casimir force. We derive a full integral expression for the
H
dependent force between the two slabs. We then derive the explicit formula for the long range (non-screened) component of these forces.

Appendix B
Effective Hamiltonian for the ions

Here we discuss what is referred to in Ref.
[
1
]
as the
separation hypothesis
which gives an effective electrostatic Hamiltonian for the ions, including the image charges, as well as introducing an effective unscreened van der Waals interaction between two dielectric slabs, independent of the charge density between the slabs (and is indeed present in the absence of charge density).

The partition function for a fixed charge density in a medium with a local dipole field
{\bf p}({\bf x})
and local polarizability
\chi({\bf x})
can be written as

Z=\int\mathrm{d}[\phi]\mathrm{d}[{\bf p}]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\beta\epsilon_{0}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}[\nabla\phi({\bf x})]^{2}+\mathrm{i}\beta\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\phi({\bf x})[\rho_{\mathrm{ic}}({\bf x})-\nabla\cdot{\bf p}({\bf x})]-\frac{1}{2}\beta\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\frac{{\bf p}^{2}({\bf x})}{\chi({\bf x})}\right)

(S1)

The total charge density in the above is
\rho_{c}({\bf x})=\rho_{\mathrm{ic}}({\bf x})-\nabla\cdot{\bf p}({\bf x})
, the first term being the ionic charge density and the second the charge density due to the dipoles in the dielectrics and solvent. Integrating over the field
\phi
simply introduces the direct pure Coulomb interaction between the total charge densities in the problem and the determinant involved in the integration does not depend on the configuration of the charge densities.

The local quadratic term
\frac{{\bf p}^{2}({\bf x})}{2\chi({\bf x})}
corresponds to a simple harmonic spring model for the local dipole field, but at the level of linear response other models are equivalent. We will see below how
\chi({\bf x})
is related to the local static dielectric constant.
The above model can be used to study the out of equilibrium behavior of the thermal and/or non-zero Matsubara contribution to the van der Waals forces. However, here we assume that the dynamics of the field

{\bf p}({\bf x})
is so rapid that it equilibrates instantaneously with any charge density configuration of the ions.
Integrating over the field
{\bf p}
then yields (up to an overall prefactor)

Z=\int\mathrm{d}[\phi]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\beta\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\epsilon({\bf x})[\nabla\phi({\bf x})]^{2}+\mathrm{i}\beta\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\phi({\bf x})\rho_{\rm ic}({\bf x})\right),

(S2)

where
\epsilon({\bf x})=\epsilon_{0}+\chi({\bf x})
can be simply interpreted as the local dielectric constant.
Strictly speaking the above integration over the dipole field introduces a bulk term in the free energy but this term does not give an
H
dependent contribution to the force, so we will ignore it for the purposes of our analysis. Now carrying out the Gaussian integration over the field
\phi
we find

Z=Z_{\rm vdW}\exp(-\beta H_{\rm ES}[\rho_{\rm ic}]),

(S3)

where
H_{\rm ES}
is the electrostatic Hamiltonian

H_{\rm ES}[\rho_{\rm ic}]=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}^{\prime}\rho_{\rm ic}({\bf x})G({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})\rho_{\rm ic}({\bf x}^{\prime})

(S4)

with
G({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})
the Green’s function

\nabla\cdot\epsilon({\bf x})\nabla G({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})=-\delta({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime}),

(S5)

and

Z_{\rm vdW}=\int\mathrm{d}[\phi]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\beta\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\epsilon({\bf x})[\nabla\phi({\bf x})]^{2}\right)

(S6)

the partition function for the van der Waals interaction in the system in the absence of any ionic charge density. At a risk of laboring the point therefore we see that assuming that the dipole degrees of freedom equilibrate instantaneously for any fixed ionic charge distribution, the effective interaction between the ionic charges is mediated by the Green’s function
G
in Eq. (
S5
) which contains the effects of image charges. There is also a direct van der Waals free energy, corresponding to
Z_{\rm vdW}
, which would be present even without any electrolyte. There are many ways to compute
Z_{\rm vdW}
and the result is well known. However for the sake of completeness we will calculate it out here using an eigenfunction expansion method as this method will be used to obtain results for the driven system.

We can write the field
\phi
as a Fourier/eigenfunction expansion

\phi(x,y,z)=\phi({\bf x}_{\|},z)=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n}\tilde{\phi}_{n}({\bf k})\psi_{n}(z)\exp(\mathrm{i}{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}_{\|}),

(S7)

with boundary condition

\epsilon^{\prime}\left.\frac{\partial\psi_{n}}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0^{-},H^{+}}=\epsilon\left.\frac{\partial\psi_{n}}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0^{+},H^{-}}

(S8)

at
z=0
and
z=H
. This means that when
\epsilon\gg\epsilon^{\prime}

the boundary conditions at
z=0,H
become

\left.\frac{\partial\psi_{n}}{\partial z}\right|_{z=0^{+},H^{-}}=0

(S9)

and so

\psi_{0}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{H}},\ {\rm and}\ \psi_{n}(z)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{H}}\cos(p_{n}z)\ {\rm for}\ n\geq 1,

(S10)

where
p_{n}=n\pi/H
.
Using this gives a product of simple Gaussian integrals with each mode contributing an
H
dependent factor

Z_{\mathrm{vdW},n,{\bf k}}=\left(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(S11)

Computing the van der Waals force per unit area from the resulting free energy we then find

f_{\rm vdW}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{(2\pi)^{2}H}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf k}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n}\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{p_{n}^{2}+k^{2}},

(S12)

where for convenience we have introduced the notation
\sum^{\prime}_{n}
to denote the sum of terms with
n\neq 0
but where the
n=0
term is taken with a weight of
1/2
. A term of the form
\frac{1}{H}\sum^{\prime}_{n}
is a bulk term which is independent of
H
as the number of modes in a region of length
H
is
H/\sum^{\prime}_{n}1=a
, where
a
is a microscopic cut off length scale. The
H
dependent part of
f_{\rm vdW}
can thus be written as

\displaystyle f^{*}_{\rm vdW}(H)=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{(2\pi)^{2}H}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf k}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n}\frac{k^{2}}{p_{n}^{2}+k^{2}}.

(S13)

Now we use the identity

\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n}\frac{k^{2}}{p_{n}^{2}+k^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}kH\coth(kH)

(S14)

to obtain

f^{*}_{\rm vdW}(H)=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{2(2\pi)^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf k}k\coth(kH).

(S15)

This term is divergent as
H\to\infty
but the divergence is independent of
H
, and can therefore be subtracted off to give a
H
dependent force
f_{\rm vdW}(H)=f^{*}_{\rm vdW}(H)-\lim_{H\to\infty}f^{*}_{\rm vdW}(H)
which is given by

f_{\rm vdW}(H)=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{2(2\pi)^{2}}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf k}k[\coth(kH)-1]=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}kk^{2}[\coth(kH)-1].

(S16)

The integral over
k
can then be carried out to find the well known result

f_{\rm vdW}(H)=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T\zeta(3)}{8\pi H^{3}},

(S17)

where
\zeta(z)
is the Riemann zeta function.

Appendix C
Treatment of ions by expanding about mean density

We now consider the statics and dynamics of the ionic density fluctuations. We write the density of cations/anions as

\rho_{\pm}({\bf x})=\overline{\rho}+n_{\pm}({\bf x}).

(S18)

Formally we can write the partition function of the ionic part of the system as

\displaystyle Z_{\rm ion}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}[\rho_{+}]\mathrm{d}[\rho_{-}]\exp\left(-\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\left(\rho_{+}({\bf x})[\ln(\rho_{+}({\bf x}))-1]+\rho_{-}({\bf x})[\ln(\rho_{-}({\bf x}))-1]\right)\right.

(S19)

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\left.-\frac{\beta q^{2}}{2}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}^{\prime}(\rho_{+}({\bf x})-\rho_{-}({\bf x}))G({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})(\rho_{+}({\bf x}^{\prime})-\rho_{-}({\bf x}^{\prime}))\right).

Expanding to quadratic order in the charge fluctuations we find

Z_{\rm ion}=\exp(-2V_{i}~\overline{\rho}[\ln\overline{\rho}-1])\int\mathrm{d}[n_{+}]\mathrm{d}[n_{-}]\exp(-{\beta}H_{\rm ion}[n_{+},n_{-}]),

(S20)

where the first term corresponds to the partition function of two ideal gases of density
\overline{\rho}
in the total volume
V_{i}
between the two slabs corresponding to
z\in[0,H]
. The second quadratic term represents the contribution from the charge fluctuations to the Hamiltonian

H_{\rm ion}[n_{+},n_{-}]=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{2\overline{\rho}}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\ [n_{+}^{2}({\bf x})+n_{-}^{2}({\bf x})]+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}^{\prime}\ (n_{+}({\bf x})-n_{-}({\bf x}))G({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})(n_{+}({\bf x}^{\prime})-n_{-}({\bf x}^{\prime})),

(S21)

and the spatial integration regions above are in the volume
V_{i}
. Defining the total density fluctuation by

n_{\mathrm{t}}=n_{+}+n_{-}
and the difference
n_{\mathrm{d}}=n_{+}-n_{-}
we can write

H_{\rm ion}[n_{\mathrm{t}},n_{\mathrm{d}}]=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{4\overline{\rho}}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\ [n_{\mathrm{t}}^{2}({\bf x})+n_{\mathrm{d}}^{2}({\bf x})]+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}^{\prime}\ n_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x})G({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})n_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x}^{\prime})

(S22)

The Gaussian integrals can be performed using the same eigenfunction expansion as in Section (I) and as we consider again the case where
\epsilon\gg\epsilon^{\prime}
we find that the
H
dependent part of the partition function from each mode is given by

Z_{n,\bf{k}}=\left(1+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(S23)

where
\kappa=\sqrt{2\overline{\rho}q^{2}\beta/\epsilon}
is the inverse Debye screening length. Computing the force per unit surface area from the resulting free energy then yields

f_{\rm ion}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{(2\pi)^{2}H}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf k}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n}\left[\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{p_{n}^{2}+\kappa^{2}+k^{2}}-\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{p_{n}^{2}+k^{2}}\right].

(S24)

Notice that the second term cancels exactly with the attractive van der Waals interaction given in Eq. (
S12
) and that the first term has exactly the same form but with
k^{2}
replaced by
k^{2}+\kappa^{2}
. We thus find that the total
H
dependent force is given by

f_{\rm t}(H)=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{4\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k\,kK[\coth(KH)-1],

(S25)

where
K^{2}=k^{2}+\kappa^{2}
. Changing the integration to
K
then gives the (attractive) screened thermal Casimir force

f_{\rm t}(H)=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{4\pi}\int_{\kappa}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}KK^{2}[\coth(KH)-1]=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{4\pi H^{3}}\int_{H\kappa}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}K\,K^{2}(\coth K-1).

(S26)

This force is attractive and at large
H
decays as
\simeq\frac{k_{B}T\kappa^{2}}{4\pi H}\exp(-2\kappa H)
, and hence justifying the terminology “screened”. We have therefore seen from this calculation that the ionic fluctuations produce a repulsive unscreened Casimir force that exactly cancels that coming from the van der Waals interaction due to the dielectrics, plus an attractive screened interaction that represents the net force.

Appendix D
The stress tensor for the ionic fluctuations

In order to derive the out of equilibrium forces, one has no free energy from which to derive them and instead one must use a stress tensor to calculate forces. In the presence of an applied electric field, the density fluctuations give a local body force which is given by
[
2
]

{\bf f}_{\rm B}({\bf x})=-n_{+}({\bf x})\nabla\frac{\delta H_{\rm ion}}{\delta n_{+}({\bf x})}-n_{-}({\bf x})\nabla\frac{\delta H_{\rm ion}}{\delta n_{-}({\bf x})}+q{\bf E}(n_{+}({\bf x})-n_{-}({\bf x})),

(S27)

that can be written as

{\bf f}_{\rm B}({\bf x})=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\overline{\rho}}n_{+}({\bf x})\nabla n_{+}({\bf x})-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{\overline{\rho}}n_{-}({\bf x})\nabla n_{-}({\bf x})-q(n_{+}({\bf x})-n_{-}({\bf x}))\nabla\phi({\bf x})+q{\bf E}(n_{+}({\bf x})-n_{-}({\bf x})),

(S28)

where
\phi({\bf x})
is the electrostatic field generated by the ionic charges and is given by

\nabla\cdot\epsilon({\bf x})\nabla\phi({\bf x})=-q(n_{+}({\bf x})-n_{-}({\bf x})).

(S29)

From this it is easy to see that
f_{\mathrm{B}i}({\bf x})=\nabla_{j}\sigma_{ij}({\bf x})+qE_{i}(n_{+}({\bf x})-n_{-}({\bf x})),
where
\sigma_{ij}({\bf x})
is a stress tensor with

\sigma_{ij}({\bf x})=-\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{2\overline{\rho}}\delta_{ij}(n^{2}_{+}({\bf x})+n^{2}_{-}({\bf x}))+\epsilon({\bf x})(\nabla_{i}\phi({\bf r})\nabla_{j}\phi({\bf x})-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ij}[\nabla\phi({\bf x})]^{2}).

(S30)

Notice that the second term in
\phi
is simply the Maxwell stress tensor, while the first two terms correspond to van’t Hoff osmotic stresses coming from the density fluctuations.
Noting that the field
{\bf E}
is parallel to the surface and that at the surface
\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}=0
, we find that the
outward
force due to the ions per unit surface acting on the surface at
z=0
in the direction
z
is given by

f_{\rm ion}=-\langle\sigma_{zz}(0,0,z=0^{+})-\sigma_{zz}(0,0,z=0^{-})\rangle,

(S31)

(there is a change of sign as the inner normal points inwards) which gives

f_{\rm ion}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{2\overline{\rho}}\langle n^{2}_{+}({\bf 0})+n^{2}_{-}({\bf 0})\rangle+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\langle[\nabla_{\|}\phi({\bf 0})]^{2}\rangle,

(S32)

with
{\bf 0}=(0,0,z=0^{+})
and where we have again invoked the Neumann boundary condition on the field
\phi
, and
\nabla_{\|}
denotes the gradient in the plane of the surface of the dielectric. Written in terms of the field
n_{\mathrm{t}}
and
n_{\mathrm{d}}
this gives Eq. (11) of the main text

f_{\rm ion}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{4\overline{\rho}}\langle n^{2}_{\rm t}({\bf 0})+n^{2}_{\rm d}({\bf 0})\rangle+\frac{\epsilon}{2}\langle[\nabla_{\|}\phi({\bf 0})]^{2}\rangle.

(S33)

This is the expression that we use in the main text to compute the total force.

Appendix E
Stochastic density functional theory for the driven electrolyte

The formally exact equation for the stochastic density fields

\rho_{\pm}({\bf x},t)=\sum_{i\pm}\delta({\bf x}-{\bf x}_{i}(t))

(S34)

for the cations and anions
i\pm
in Eq. (2) of the main text is
[
3
]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{+}({\bf x})}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot D[\nabla\rho_{+}({\bf x})+\beta q\rho_{+}({\bf x})(\nabla\phi({\bf x})-{\bf E})]+\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{2D\rho_{+}({\bf x})}~\bm{\eta}_{+}({\bf x},t)\right),

(S35)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{-}({\bf x})}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot D[\nabla\rho_{-}({\bf x})-\beta q\rho_{-}({\bf x})(\nabla\phi({\bf x})-{\bf E})]+\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{2D\rho_{-}({\bf x})}~\bm{\eta}_{-}({\bf x},t)\right).

(S36)

where the noise terms are Gaussian white noise vector fields in time and space and independent for each species

\langle\bm{\eta}_{\mu\alpha}({\bf x},t)\bm{\eta}_{\nu\beta}({\bf x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime}),\quad\mu,\,\nu\in\{+,\,-\}.

(S37)

The boundary conditions of the fields at the dielectric interfaces are that no current for either species crosses the interface. This no flux boundary condition must be applied to the deterministic and random part of the currents so that

{\bf e}_{z}\cdot[\nabla\rho_{\pm}({\bf x})\pm\beta q\rho_{\pm}({\bf x})(\nabla\phi({\bf x})-{\bf E})]=0\ {\rm and}\ {\bf e}_{z}\cdot\bm{\eta}_{\pm}=0

(S38)

at the surfaces
z=0
and
z=H
.
These nonlinear stochastic equations are difficult to solve. However if one expands to first order in the fluctuations

n_{\pm}({\bf x})=\rho_{\pm}({\bf x})-\overline{\rho},

(S39)

one obtains the soluble linearized SDFT linear equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial n_{+}({\bf x})}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot D[\nabla n_{+}({\bf x})+\beta q\overline{\rho}\nabla\phi({\bf x})-\beta qn_{+}({\bf x}){\bf E}]+\nabla\cdot\sqrt{2D\overline{\rho}}~\bm{\eta}_{+}({\bf x},t),

(S40)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial n_{-}({\bf x})}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot D[\nabla n_{-}({\bf x})-\beta q\overline{\rho}\nabla\phi({\bf x})+\beta qn_{-}({\bf x}){\bf E}]+\nabla\cdot\sqrt{2D\overline{\rho}}~\bm{\eta}_{-}({\bf x},t).

(S41)

In terms of the fields
n_{\mathrm{t}}({\bf x})
and
n_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x})
these equations become

\displaystyle\frac{\partial n_{\mathrm{t}}({\bf x})}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot D[\nabla n_{\mathrm{t}}({\bf x})-\beta qn_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x}){\bf E}]+\nabla\cdot\sqrt{4D\overline{\rho}}~\bm{\eta}_{\rm t}({\bf x},t),

(S42)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial n_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x})}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot D[\nabla n_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x})+2\beta q\overline{\rho}\nabla\phi({\bf x})-\beta qn_{\mathrm{t}}({\bf x}){\bf E}]+\nabla\cdot\sqrt{4D\overline{\rho}}~\bm{\eta}_{\rm d}({\bf x},t),

(S43)

where
\bm{\eta}_{\rm t}({\bf x},t)
and
\bm{\eta}_{\rm d}({\bf x},t)
are again independent Gaussian white noise vector fields in space and time with correlation functions

\langle\bm{\eta}_{\mu\alpha}({\bf x},t)\bm{\eta}_{\nu\beta}({\bf x}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta({\bf x}-{\bf x}^{\prime})\delta(t-t^{\prime}),\quad\mu,\,\nu\in\{\mathrm{t},\,\mathrm{d}\}.

(S44)

One can use Eq. (
S29
) to simplify Eq. (
S43
) which then becomes

\frac{\partial n_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x})}{\partial t}=D(\nabla^{2}-\kappa^{2})n_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x})-D\beta q{\bf E}\cdot\nabla n_{\mathrm{t}}({\bf x})+\nabla\cdot\sqrt{4D\overline{\rho}}\,\bm{\eta}_{\rm d}({\bf x},t),

(S45)

this being the stochastic version of the Debye-Falkenhagen equation. The analysis of the resulting equations is rendered complicated because the no flux boundary condition on the field
n_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x})

\frac{\partial n_{\mathrm{d}}}{\partial z}+2\beta q\overline{\rho}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial z}=0

(S46)

is non local and the problem of finding the correct eigenfunction expansion is very involved. However if we consider the limit
\epsilon^{\prime}\ll\epsilon
the boundary conditions on the electrostatic field
\phi
is Neumann and consequently the boundary condition on the field
n_{\mathrm{d}}
also becomes Neumann. In addition we see that the boundary condition on the field
n_{\mathrm{t}}
is also Neumann. All of the fields in the problem can thus be written in terms of a Fourier cosine expansion in the
z
coordinate and a Fourier transform in the
(x,y)
plane

\displaystyle n_{\mathrm{t}}({\bf x}_{\|},z,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{n}}}\tilde{n}_{tn}({\bf k},t)\exp(i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}_{\|})\cos(p_{n}z),

\displaystyle n_{\mathrm{d}}({\bf x}_{\|},z,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{n}}}\tilde{n}_{dn}({\bf k},t)\exp(i{\bf k}\cdot{\bf x}_{\|})\cos(p_{n}z),

where
p_{n}=n\pi/H
enforces the Neumann conditions at
z=0
and
z=H
, while
N_{n}
normalizes the corresponding eigenfunction, with
N_{n}=H/2
for
n\geq 1
and
N_{0}=H
. The equations for the Fourier components then read

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{n}_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k})}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-D(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})\tilde{n}_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k})-iD\beta qEk_{x}\tilde{n}_{\mathrm{d}n}({\bf k})+\xi_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k},t),

(S48)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{n}_{\mathrm{d}n}({\bf k})}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-D(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}+\kappa^{2})\tilde{n}_{\mathrm{d}n}({\bf k})-iD\beta qEk_{x}\tilde{n}_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k})+\xi_{\mathrm{d}n}({\bf k},t),

(S49)

where,
k_{x}={\bf e}_{x}\cdot{\bf k}
, the noise terms
\xi_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k},t)
and
\xi_{\mathrm{d}n}({\bf k},t)
are independent with correlation functions

\langle\xi_{\mu n}({\bf k},t)\xi_{\nu n^{\prime}}({\bf k}^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle=(2\pi)^{2}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\delta({\bf k}+{\bf k}^{\prime})\delta_{\mu\nu}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}~4D\overline{\rho}(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}),\quad\mu,\,\nu\in\{\mathrm{t},\,\mathrm{d}\}.

(S50)

The equal time correlation functions of the Fourier components in the steady state are denoted as

\langle\tilde{n}_{\mu n}({\bf k})\tilde{n}_{\nu n}({\bf k}^{\prime})\rangle=(2\pi)^{2}\delta({\bf k}+{\bf k^{\prime}})~\tilde{C}_{\mu\nu n}({\bf k}),\quad\mu,\,\nu\in\{\mathrm{t},\,\mathrm{d}\}

(S51)

and we can define the correlation matrix

C_{n}({\bf k})=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{tt}n}({\bf k})&\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{td}n}({\bf k})\\
\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dt}n}({\bf k})&\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dd}n}({\bf k})\end{pmatrix}.

(S52)

It is now standard to see that in the steady state that
C_{n}({\bf k})
obeys the so called Lyapunov equation

A_{n}({\bf k})C_{n}({\bf k})+C_{n}({\bf k})A_{n}(-{\bf k})=2R_{n}({\bf k}),

(S53)

where

A_{n}({\bf k})=D\begin{pmatrix}k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}&\mathrm{i}k_{x}\beta qE\\
\mathrm{i}k_{x}\beta qE&k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}+\kappa^{2}\end{pmatrix},

(S54)

and

R_{n}({\bf k})=2D\overline{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}&0\\
0&k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(S55)

Solving this linear system then gives

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{tt}n}({\bf k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\overline{\rho}\frac{(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})\left[(K^{2}+p_{n}^{2})(K^{2}+k^{2}+2p_{n}^{2})+2\beta^{2}q^{2}E^{2}k_{x}^{2}\right]}{(K^{2}+k^{2}+2p_{n}^{2})\left[(K^{2}+p_{n}^{2})(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})+\beta^{2}q^{2}E^{2}k_{x}^{2}\right]},

(S56)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{td}n}({\bf k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dt}n}({\bf k})=2\mathrm{i}\overline{\rho}\frac{\beta qEk_{x}\kappa^{2}(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})}{(K^{2}+k^{2}+2p_{n}^{2})\left[(K^{2}+p_{n}^{2})(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})+\beta^{2}q^{2}E^{2}k_{x}^{2}\right]},

(S57)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dd}n}({\bf k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\overline{\rho}\frac{(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})\left[(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})(K^{2}+k^{2}+2p_{n}^{2})+2\beta^{2}q^{2}E^{2}k_{x}^{2}\right]}{(K^{2}+k^{2}+2p_{n}^{2})\left[(K^{2}+p_{n}^{2})(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})+\beta^{2}q^{2}E^{2}k_{x}^{2}\right]}.

(S58)

This is the solution of the Lyapunov equation that we use in the main text.

Appendix F
Computation of the force due to the ions

Using the Fourier representation of the correlation functions and Eq. (
S33
) for the ionic force in terms of the stress tensor we find that

f_{\rm ion}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{H}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n}S_{n}({\bf k}),

(S59)

where

S_{n}({\bf k})=\frac{1}{2\overline{\rho}}(\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{tt}n}({\bf k})+\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dd}n}({\bf k}))+\frac{\beta q^{2}k^{2}\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dd}n}({\bf k})}{\epsilon(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\overline{\rho}}\left[\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{tt}n}({\bf k})+\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dd}n}({\bf k})+\frac{\kappa^{2}k^{2}\tilde{C}_{\mathrm{dd}n}({\bf k})}{(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})^{2}}\right].

(S60)

Inserting the expressions in Eq. (
S56
) and Eq. (
S58
) then gives

S_{n}({\bf k})=2+\frac{2k^{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}}-\frac{2K^{2}+2k^{2}}{K^{2}+k^{2}+2p_{n}^{2}}+\frac{p_{n}^{2}\kappa^{4}}{(K^{2}+k^{2}+2p_{n}^{2})\left((K^{2}+p_{n}^{2})(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})+\beta^{2}\ q^{2}E^{2}k_{x}^{2}\right)}.

(S61)

Before examining the effect of driving we shall consider the case
E=0
which gives

S_{n}({\bf k})=2+\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}}-\frac{k^{2}+\kappa^{2}}{k^{2}+\kappa^{2}+p_{n}^{2}}.

(S62)

Using this it is straightforward to verify that we recover that the resulting
H
dependent force is in perfect agreement with the static result as given in Eq. (
S24
).

As we are interested in the total force
f_{\rm t}=f_{\rm ion}+f_{\rm vdW}
, we will add on the van der Waals contribution to the effective interaction which can be read off from Eq. (
S13
) and write

f_{\rm t}=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{H}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}{\bf k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n}S_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k}),

(S63)

where

S_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k})=S_{n}({\bf k})-\frac{k^{2}}{p_{n}^{2}+k^{2}}.

(S64)

This gives

S_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k})=2+\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}}-2\frac{K^{2}+k^{2}}{K^{2}+k^{2}+2p_{n}^{2}}+\frac{p_{n}^{2}\kappa^{4}}{(K^{2}+k^{2}+2p_{n}^{2})\left((K^{2}+p_{n}^{2})(k^{2}+p_{n}^{2})+\beta^{2}\ q^{2}E^{2}k_{x}^{2}\right)}.

(S65)

The long range component of the force can be deduced from the low wave vector behavior of
S_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k})
which is given by

S_{\mathrm{t}n}({\bf k})\approx 2+\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}+\frac{\beta^{2}\ q^{2}E^{2}k_{x}^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}}+\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}},

(S66)

which has a long range component (the constant term
2
yields a bulk term independent of
H
)

S_{tn}({\bf k})\approx\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}}-\frac{Q^{2}}{p_{n}^{2}+Q^{2}},

(S67)

where

Q=\sqrt{1+\frac{\beta^{2}E^{2}q^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{\kappa^{2}}}k

(S68)

and we have written
k_{x}=k\cos\theta
.
Now computing the
H
dependent part of the interaction in exactly the same way as the similar calculations performed above we find

f_{\rm t}(H)\approx-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}\beta}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf k}[Q(\coth(QH)-1)-k(\coth(kH)-1)].

(S69)

The remaining integrals can be carried out to yield

f_{\rm t}(H)\approx\frac{\zeta(3)}{8\pi\beta H^{3}}\left[1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{\beta^{2}q^{2}E^{2}}{\kappa^{2}}}}\right].

(S70)

We now discuss the case where
H
is finite but the large driving limit
E\to\infty
is taken. Here we can write

S_{tn}({\bf k})=2+\frac{k^{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}}-2\frac{k^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}},

(S71)

from which we find that the
H
dependent force is given by

f_{\rm t}(H,E\to\infty)=\frac{\zeta(3)}{8\pi\beta H^{3}}-\frac{2}{4\pi\beta H^{3}}\int_{\frac{H\kappa}{\sqrt{2}}}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}KK^{2}(\coth K-1).

(S72)

The first term is the repulsive long range Casimir force seen in Eq. (
S70
) and the second term is twice the screened thermal Casimir interaction for an electrolyte with inverse screening length
\kappa^{*}=\kappa/\sqrt{2}
.
This screening length corresponds to
\kappa^{*}=\sqrt{\overline{\rho}q^{2}\beta/\epsilon}
, that is to say the density
\overline{\rho}
is replaced by
\overline{\rho}/2
.
This halving of the effective density and the appearance of a factor of
2
in the screened contribution can be explained by invoking a decoupling mechanism between the cations and anions.
The field drives the cations and anions with velocities
v_{\pm}=\pm\beta DqE
.
Each species moves with corresponding average velocity in its own Galilean reference frame where they equilibrate with each other. However, the strong driving means that the interactions between the different species effectively disappear, the cations and anions thus behave as if they are in equilibrium but are decoupled. The Hamiltonian for the charge fluctuations in this strongly driven limit is thus

H_{\rm decoupled}[n_{+},n_{-}]=\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{2\overline{\rho}}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}[\ n_{+}^{2}({\bf x})+n_{-}^{2}({\bf x})]+\frac{q^{2}}{2}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}^{\prime}\left[\ n_{+}({\bf x})G({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})n_{+}({\bf x}^{\prime})+n_{-}({\bf x})G({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})n_{-}({\bf x}^{\prime})\right],

(S73)

that is to say we have removed the interactions between charges of different species.

The partition function is thus given by

Z_{\rm decoupled}=Z_{1}^{2},

(S74)

where

Z_{1}=\int\mathrm{d}[n]\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2\overline{\rho}}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\ n^{2}({\bf x})-\frac{\beta q^{2}}{2}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf x}\mathrm{d}{\bf x}^{\prime}\ n({\bf x})G({\bf x},{\bf x}^{\prime})n({\bf x}^{\prime})\right)

(S75)

is the partition function for a single species. Computing
Z_{1}
gives a contribution from each mode of

Z_{1,n,\bf{k}}=\left(1+\frac{\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}}{k^{2}+p_{n}^{2}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

(S76)

Comparing with the zero driving equilibrium computation from Eq. (
S23
) then gives the force due to the decoupled ions as

f_{\rm decoupled}=2\times\frac{k_{\mathrm{B}}T}{(2\pi)^{2}H}\int\mathrm{d}{\bf k}\sideset{}{{}^{\prime}}{\sum}_{n}\left[\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{p_{n}^{2}+\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}+k^{2}}-\frac{p_{n}^{2}}{p_{n}^{2}+k^{2}}\right].

(S77)

Computing this and then adding the attractive van der Waals component gives exactly the strong driving nonequilibrium result Eq. (
S72
).

Introduction

Grothendieck
[
15
]
in 1955 characterized a relatively compact set in Banach spaces by means of sequences converging to zero, showing that compactness is also a geometric property. Inspired by Grothendieck’s characterization, Sinha and Karn
[
31
]
defined the concept of a relatively (weakly)
p
-compact set, and, in parallel, a (weakly)
p
-compact linear operator. Kim
[
21
]
introduced the notion of a relatively unconditionally
p
-compact set as a weaker version of a relatively
p
-compact set and a stronger version of a relatively compact set, and, in parallel, the concept of an unconditionally
p
-compact linear operator. The works
[
31
,
21
]
have inspired many papers exploring various aspects of linear operators associated with these sets (see
[
12
,
13
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
32
]
), as well as Lipschitz operators (see
[
1
,
20
]
).

When
E
and
F
are Banach spaces and
U
is an open subset on
E
, linearization results assist to relate a class of holomorphic mappings defined on
U
and valued in
F
to the space of bounded linear operators from a certain Banach space
G(U)
into
F
. This relation effectively connects a holomorphic mapping with a corresponding linear operator via linearization, thus allowing many properties of the linear case to be treated in the holomorphic case.

The study of weighted Banach spaces of holomorphic mappings and operators between them is a topic with a long tradition that continues to attract the attention of researchers today. The interested reader can find complete information on this important function space in the survey
[
5
]
by Bonet and the references therein.

The weighted spaces of holomorphic mappings defined on an open subset of the finite dimensional space
\mathbb{C}^{N}
(
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
) have been investigated in
[
3
,
4
,
8
,
30
]
while the infinite dimensional case has been addressed by
[
14
,
16
,
19
]
.

For this latter case, the research on holomorphic mappings with a relatively compact range was started in
[
26
]
and continued in
[
18
]
. A study on holomorphic mappings with a relatively
p
-compact range was conducted in
[
17
]
. The weighted holomorphic mappings with a relatively compact
v
-range were briefly discussed in
[
16
]
.

In this study, we consider classes of weighted holomorphic mappings associated to various types of
p
-compact sets, inspired by the studies
[
13
,
22
,
24
,
31
]
and
[
17
,
18
]
on different versions of bounded linear operators and holomorphic mappings, respectively.

This work consists of two parts divided in some subsections and is organized as follows. In Section
1
, we provide the necessary notation and preliminary information for the study. In Section
2
, we introduce and study classes of weighted holomorphic mappings with relatively (resp., relatively weakly, relatively unconditionally)
p
-compact
v
-range.

To study the Banach ideal structure of these classes, we release and examine the ideal structure of weighted holomorphic mappings. We obtain important characterizations between the introduced classes of mappings and their linearizations. Using these characterizations, we derive factorization results for such classes and explore various characterizations and results connecting these mapping classes with their transpositions.

We also deal with finite-dimensional
v
-rank and approximable weighted holomorphic mappings and show that a
p
-approximable (resp.,
wp
-approximable,
up
-approximable) weighted holomorphic mapping has a relatively (resp., relatively weakly, relatively unconditionally)
p
-compact
v
-range. Finally, we present the class of right
p
-nuclear weighted holomorphic mappings and discuss the relationship between this class and its linearization. We also show that this class belongs to the class of weighted holomorphic maps with a relatively
p
-compact
v
-range.

1.1.
Notation

As usual,
\mathbb{K}
denotes the field
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
. Let
E
and
F
be complex Banach spaces and let
U
be an open subset of
E
. Let
\mathcal{H}(U,F)
be the space of all holomorphic mappings from
U
into
F
. A
weight

v
on
U
is a (strictly) positive continuous function.

The
space of weighted holomorphic mappings

\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
is the Banach space of all mappings
f\in\mathcal{H}(U,F)
such that

\left\|f\right\|_{v}:=\sup\left\{v(x)\left\|f(x)\right\|\colon x\in U\right\}<\infty,

endowed with the
weighted supremum norm

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v}
. It is usual to write
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v}(U)
instead of
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v}(U,\mathbb{C})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}}(U,F)
and
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{w}}(U,F)
stand for the spaces of mappings
f\in\mathcal{H}(U,F)
such that
vf
has relatively compact range and relatively weakly compact range in
F
, respectively.

We denote by
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
the Banach space of all bounded linear operators from
E
into
F
, equipped with the operator canonical norm, and by
\mathcal{F}(E,F)
,
\mathcal{K}(E,F)
and
\mathcal{K}_{w}(E,F)
the subspaces of
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
consisting of all finite-rank operators, compact operators and weakly compact operators from
E
into
F
, respectively. As usual,
E^{*}
and
B_{E}
represent the topological dual space and the closed unit ball of
E
, respectively. Given a set
A\subseteq E
,
\overline{\mathrm{lin}}(A)
and
\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(A)
stand for the norm closed linear hull and the norm closed absolutely convex hull of
A
in
E
. Given two normed spaces
(X,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X})
and
(Y,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y})
, the inequality
(X,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X})\leq(Y,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y})
will indicate that
X\subseteq Y
and
\left\|x\right\|_{Y}\leq\left\|x\right\|_{X}
for all
x\in X
.

1.2.
Preliminaries on weighted holomorphic mappings

Let
U
be an open set of a complex Banach space
E
and let
v
be a weight on
U
. The key tool in our study is the space
\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
, a Banach predual space of the space
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
.

Following
[
3
,
16
]
,
\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
is the space of all linear functionals on
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
whose restriction to
B_{\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)}
is continuous for the compact-open topology. In fact,
\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
, and the evaluation mapping
J_{v}\colon\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)\to\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
, given by
J_{v}(f)(\phi)=\phi(f)
for
\phi\in\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
, is an isometric isomorphism.

For each
x\in U
, the
evaluation functional

\delta_{x}\colon\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v}(U)\to\mathbb{C}
, defined by
\delta_{x}(f)=f(x)
for
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v}(U)
, is in
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)
, and the map
\Delta_{v}\colon U\to\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
given by
\Delta_{v}(x)=\delta_{x}
is in
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
with
\left\|\Delta_{v}\right\|_{v}\leq 1
.

By
an atom of
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)
we mean an element of
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)
of the form
v(x)\delta_{x}
for
x\in U
. The set of all atoms in
\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)
will be denoted here by
\mathrm{At}_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)}
. It is known that
B_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)}
coincides with
\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\mathrm{At}_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)})\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v}(U)^{*}
and, consequently,
\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
with
\overline{\mathrm{lin}}(\mathrm{At}_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
.

The space
\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
enjoys
an universal property
: for every complex Banach space
F
and every mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
, there exists a unique operator
T_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
such that
T_{f}\circ\Delta_{v}=f
. Furthermore,
\left\|T_{f}\right\|=\left\|f\right\|_{v}
. As a consequence, the correspondence
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
onto
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
(resp., from
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}}(U,F)
onto
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
, from
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{w}}(U,F)
onto
\mathcal{K}_{w}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F))
.

For each
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v}(U,F)
, the mapping
f^{t}\colon F^{*}\to\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v}(U)
, defined by
f^{t}(y^{*})=y^{*}\circ f
for all
y^{*}\in F^{*}
, is in
\mathcal{L}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v}(U))
with
||f^{t}||=\left\|f\right\|_{v}
and
f^{t}=J_{v}^{-1}\circ(T_{f})^{*}
, where
(T_{f})^{*}\colon F^{*}\to\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
T_{f}
.

1.3.
Preliminaries on
p
-compact operators

Given a Banach space
E
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, consider the Banach spaces of
p
-summable sequences, weakly
p
-summable sequences and unconditionally
p
-summable sequences
in
E
defined, respectively, by

\displaystyle\ell_{p}(E)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x_{n})\in E^{\mathbb{N}}\colon\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{p}:=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle\ell^{w}_{p}(E)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x_{n})\in E^{\mathbb{N}}\colon\left\|(x_{n})\right\|^{w}_{p}:=\sup_{x^{*}\in B_{E^{*}}}\left\|(x^{*}(x_{n}))\right\|_{p}<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle\ell^{u}_{p}(E)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x_{n})\in\ell^{w}_{p}(E)\colon\lim_{m\to\infty}\left\|(x_{n})_{n\geq m}\right\|^{w}_{p}=0\right\}.

In the case
E=\mathbb{K}
, we will write
\ell_{p}
,

\ell^{w}_{p}
and
\ell^{u}_{p}
. The following relations are known:

\left(\ell_{p}(E),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p}\right)\leq\left(\ell^{u}_{p}(E),\left\|\cdot\right\|^{w}_{p}\right)\leq\left(\ell^{w}_{p}(E),\left\|\cdot\right\|^{w}_{p}\right).

We also deal with the Banach spaces of
norm null sequences
and
weakly null sequences
in
E
,

\displaystyle c_{0}(E)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x_{n})\in E^{\mathbb{N}}\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|x_{n}\right\|=0\right\},

\displaystyle c^{w}_{0}(E)

\displaystyle=\left\{(x_{n})\in E^{\mathbb{N}}\colon\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|x^{*}(x_{n})\right|=0,\;\forall x^{*}\in E^{*}\right\},

equipped with the
supremum norm

\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{\infty}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|x_{n}\right\|.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, the
p
-convex hull
of a sequence
(x_{n})\in\ell_{p}(E)
is defined by

p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{n}\colon(a_{n})\in B_{\ell_{p^{*}}}\right\},

where
p^{*}
denotes the
conjugate index of
p
. Similarly, we set

\displaystyle 1\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{n}\colon(a_{n})\in B_{c_{0}}\right\},\ \ (x_{n})\in\ell_{1}(E),

\displaystyle\infty\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})

\displaystyle=\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}x_{n}\colon(a_{n})\in B_{\ell_{1}}\right\},\ \ (x_{n})\in c_{0}(E).

Given
p\in[1,\infty]
, a set
K\subseteq E
is said to be
relatively
p
-compact (resp., relatively weakly
p
-compact, relatively unconditionally
p
-compact)
if there exists a sequence
(x_{n})\in\ell_{p}(E)
(resp.,
(x_{n})\in\ell^{w}_{p}(E)
,
(x_{n})\in\ell^{u}_{p}(E)
) for
p\in[1,\infty)
, or
(x_{n})\in c_{0}(E)
(resp.,
(x_{n})\in c_{0}^{w}(E)
,
(x_{n})\in c_{0}(E)
) for
p=\infty
, such that
K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})
.

Given Banach spaces
E,F
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
is said to be
p
-compact (resp., weakly
p
-compact, unconditionally
p
-compact)
if
T(B_{E})
is a relatively
p
-compact (resp., relatively weakly
p
-compact, relatively unconditionally
p
-compact) subset of
F
. The space of all
p
-compact (resp., weakly
p
-compact, unconditionally
p
-compact) operators from
E
into
F
is denoted by
\mathcal{K}_{p}(E,F)
(resp.,
\mathcal{K}_{wp}(E,F)
,
\mathcal{K}_{up}(E,F)
).

The sequence
(x_{n})
in the definition of a relatively
p
-compact set
K\subseteq E
is not unique but, following to Lassalle and Turco
[
25
]
(see also
[
12
]
), a
measure of the size of the
p
-compactness of
K
comes given by

m_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}(K)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\inf\left\{\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{p}\colon(x_{n})\in\ell_{p}(E),K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})\right\}&\text{if}\;1\leq p<\infty,\\
\inf\left\{\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{p}\colon(x_{n})\in c_{0}(E),K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})\right\}&\text{if}\;p=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Similarly, we can define

m_{\mathcal{K}_{wp}}(K)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\inf\left\{\left\|(x_{n})\right\|^{w}_{p}\colon(x_{n})\in\ell^{w}_{p}(E),K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})\right\}&\text{if}\;1\leq p<\infty,\\
\inf\left\{\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{p}\colon(x_{n})\in c^{w}_{0}(E),K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})\right\}&\text{if}\;p=\infty,\end{array}\right.

and

m_{\mathcal{K}_{up}}(K)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\inf\left\{\left\|(x_{n})\right\|^{w}_{p}\colon(x_{n})\in\ell^{u}_{p}(E),K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})\right\}&\text{if}\;1\leq p<\infty,\\
\inf\left\{\left\|(x_{n})\right\|_{p}\colon(x_{n})\in c_{0}(E),K\subseteq p\text{-}\mathrm{conv}(x_{n})\right\}&\text{if}\;p=\infty,\end{array}\right.

whenever
K\subseteq E
is relatively weakly
p
-compact or relatively unconditionally
p
-compact, respectively.

Considering for
i=p,wp,up
and
p\in[1,\infty]
, the norm

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}=m_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}(T(B_{E}))\qquad(T\in\mathcal{K}_{i}(E,F)),

it is known that
[\mathcal{K}_{i},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}]
is a Banach operator ideal (see
[
31
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.2]
,
[
12
, Proposition 3.15]
,
[
24
, Theorem 2.1 and Remark 2.3]
and
[
21
, Theorem 2.1]
), and the following relations hold whenever

:

\displaystyle[\mathcal{K}_{p},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}]

\displaystyle\leq[\mathcal{K}_{up},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{up}}]\leq[\mathcal{K}_{u\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{u\infty}}]=[\mathcal{K},\left\|\cdot\right\|]=[\mathcal{K}_{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}],

\displaystyle[\mathcal{K}_{up},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{up}}]

\displaystyle\leq[\mathcal{K}_{wp},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{wp}}]\leq[\mathcal{K}_{w},\left\|\cdot\right\|].

With these concepts, we are ready to present the classes of weighted holomorphic mappings we will be dealing with.

From now on, unless otherwise stated,

and

will denote complex Banach spaces,

will be an open subset of

,

will be a weight on

and

We will study different classes of weighted holomorphic mappings

whose

satisfies some type of

Definition 2.1
.

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}(U,F)

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)\colon(vf)(U)\text{ is a relatively $p$-compact subset of }F\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}(U,F)

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)\colon(vf)(U)\text{ is a relatively weakly $p$-compact subset of }F\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{up}}^{\infty}(U,F)

\displaystyle=\left\{f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)\colon(vf)(U)\text{ is a relatively unconditionally $p$-compact subset of }F\right\}.

For
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
, we set

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}}=m_{\mathcal{I}}((vf)(U)).

We will say that
(vf)(U)

has the
\mathcal{I}
-property
if
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
.

Our approach in this paper covers some of these spaces because in view of

, p. 20]
and
[

, p. 135]
, both relatively
\infty
-compact sets and relatively unconditionally
\infty
-compact sets in a Banach space are precisely the relatively compact sets, and thus we have the following result.

Proposition 2.2
.

(\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}^{\infty}})=(\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{u\infty}}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{u\infty}}^{\infty}})=(\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}}^{\infty}(U,F),\|\cdot\|_{v})
.
\hfill\Box

Considering the relations of inclusion between the three types of relatively

-compact sets considered, we easily get the following.

Proposition 2.3
.

[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}]\leq[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{\text{u}p}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{up}}^{\infty}}]\leq[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}}]

\hfill\Box

2.1.
Banach ideal property

Our next goal is to study the Banach ideal structure of
[\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{I}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}}]
for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
. Influenced by the concept of Banach operator ideal (see
[
29
]
), the following type of ideals was considered in
[

for
v=1_{U}
(the function constantly
1
on
U
).

Definition 2.4
.

A
normed (Banach) ideal of weighted holomorphic mappings
(or simply, a
normed (Banach) weighted holomorphic ideal
) is an assignment
[\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}}]
which associates with every pair
(U,F)
, where
U
is an open subset of a complex Banach space
E
and
F
is a complex Banach space, a subset
\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}(U,F)
of
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
and a function
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}}\colon\mathcal{I}^{\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}(U,F)\to\mathbb{R}
such that the following conditions are satisfied

(P1)

To construct new ideals of weighted holomorphic mappings, we introduce in this setting the composition method with an operator ideal. This has been a standard method to yield new ideals of function spaces in different contexts as, for example, spaces of Lipschitz functions

Definition 2.5
.

Given a normed operator ideal

U
F

If

as above.

It is easy to show that

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
[\mathcal{I},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{I}}]
be a normed operator ideal. Then
[\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}]
is a normed weighted holomorphic ideal.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
and assume that
f=T\circ g
for some complex Banach space
G
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{I}(G,F)
and a mapping
g\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,G)
.

(P1): Therefore, for all
x\in U
, we have

v(x)\left\|f(x)\right\|=v(x)\left\|T(g(x))\right\|\leq v(x)\left\|T\right\|\left\|g(x)\right\|\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g\right\|_{v},

and so
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
with
\left\|f\right\|_{v}\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g\right\|_{v}
. Taking the infimum over all such factorizations of
f
yields
\left\|f\right\|_{v}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}
. This implies that
f=0
if
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}=0
.

If
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, then
\lambda f=\lambda T\circ g
, hence
\lambda f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
with
\|\lambda f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|\lambda T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g\right\|_{v}=\left|\lambda\right|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g\right\|_{v}
and taking the infimum over all the factorizations of
f
yields
\|\lambda f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}\leq\left|\lambda\right|\|f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}
. Conversely, if
\lambda\neq 0
, this implies that
\|f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}=\|\lambda^{-1}(\lambda f))\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}\leq\left|\lambda\right|^{-1}\|\lambda f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}
and thus
\left|\lambda\right|\|f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}\leq\|\lambda f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}
, while if
\lambda=0
, it is clear that
\|\lambda f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}=0=\left|\lambda\right|\|f\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}
.

If
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
, given
\varepsilon>0
, for each
i=1,2
we can find a complex Banach space
G_{i}
, an operator
T_{i}\in\mathcal{I}(G_{i},F)
and a mapping
g_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,G_{i})
with
\left\|g_{i}\right\|_{v}=1
and
\left\|T_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\|f_{i}\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}+\varepsilon/2
such that
f_{i}=T_{i}\circ g_{i}
. Consider the Banach space
G=G_{1}\oplus_{\infty}G_{2}
and define the mappings
T\colon G\to F
and
g\colon U\to G
by
T(y_{1},y_{2})=T_{1}(y_{1})+T_{2}(y_{2})
for all
(y_{1},y_{2})\in G
and
g(x)=(g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x))
for all
x\in U
, respectively. An easy calculation shows that
T\in\mathcal{I}(G,F)
with
\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\left\|T_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}+\left\|T_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,G)
with
\left\|g\right\|_{v}\leq 1
. Clearly,
T\circ g=f_{1}+f_{2}
, and so
f_{1}+f_{2}\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
with

\|f_{1}+f_{2}\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g\right\|_{v}\leq\left\|T_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}+\left\|T_{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\|f_{1}\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}+\|f_{2}\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}+\varepsilon.

The arbitrariness of
\varepsilon>0
gives
\|f_{1}+f_{2}\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}\leq\|f_{1}\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}+\|f_{2}\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}
. So we have proved that
(\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}})
is a normed space.

(P2): Take
h
and
y
as in (P2) of Definition
2.4
, note that
h\cdot y=M_{y}\circ h
, where
M_{y}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{C},F)\subseteq\mathcal{I}(\mathbb{C},F)
is the operator defined by
M_{y}(\lambda)=\lambda y
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, and so
h\cdot y\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
with
\|h\cdot y\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|M_{y}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|h\right\|_{v}=\left\|M_{y}\right\|\left\|h\right\|_{v}=\left\|y\right\|\left\|h\right\|_{v}
and, conversely,
\left\|h\right\|_{v}\left\|y\right\|=\left\|h\cdot y\right\|_{v}\leq\|h\cdot y\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}
by the inequality in (P1).

(P3): Take
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
, and
h
and
S
as in (P3) of Definition
2.4
. Hence
S\circ f\circ h=(S\circ T)\circ(g\circ h)
, where

by the ideal property of
\mathcal{I}
, and

with

for all

with

. Taking the infimum over all the factorizations of

gives
\left\|S\circ f\circ h\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|S\right\|\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}c_{v}(h)
.
∎

2.2.
Linearization

We now study the linearization of the composition ideal
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
[\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}]
be a normed operator ideal and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f
belongs to
\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(ii)

T_{f}
belongs to
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
.

In this case,
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}=||T_{f}||_{\mathcal{I}}
.
Further, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}})
onto
(\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}})
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: If
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
, one can write
f=T\circ g
for some complex Banach space
G
,
T\in\mathcal{I}(G,F)
and
g\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,G)
. Since
f=(T\circ T_{g})\circ\Delta_{v}
and
T\circ T_{g}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
, it follows that
T_{f}=T\circ T_{g}
, and thus
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
by the ideal property of
\mathcal{I}
. Further,

\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|T\circ T_{g}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|T_{g}\right\|=\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|g\right\|_{v},

and taking the infimum over all factorizations of
f
as above yields
||T_{f}||_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Assume that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
. Since
f=T_{f}\circ\Delta_{v}
and
\Delta_{v}\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v}(U,\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
, it follows that
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
with

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|\Delta_{v}\right\|_{v}\leq\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}.

For the last assertion, it is sufficient to show that the map
f\mapsto T_{f}
in the statement is surjective. For it,
take
T\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
, hence
T=T_{f}
for some
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
, and then
f\in\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
by proved above.
∎

The last assertion in the statement of Theorem
2.7
gives the following result.

Corollary 2.8
.

If
[\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}]
is a Banach operator ideal, then
[\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}]
is a Banach weighted holomorphic ideal.
\hfill\qed

We analyse the relationship of a map
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
with its linearization
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty]
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
. For the Banach operator ideal
[\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}]
with
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
, the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

f
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(ii)

T_{f}
belongs to
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)

is an isometric isomorphism from

Proof.

We know the following relations:

\displaystyle(vf)(U)=T_{f}(\mathrm{At}_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)})

\displaystyle\subseteq T_{f}(B_{\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)})=T_{f}(\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\mathrm{At}_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)}))

\displaystyle\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(T_{f}(\mathrm{At}_{\mathcal{G}^{\infty}_{v}(U)}))=\overline{\mathrm{abco}}((vf)(U)).

Assume
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: If
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
, then
(vf)(U)
has the
\mathcal{I}
-property. It follows that
\overline{\mathrm{abco}}((vf)(U))
enjoys the
\mathcal{I}
-property with

(see

]
). Now, the second inclusion above implies that

has the
\mathcal{I}
-property, that is,

, with

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)

, then

has the
\mathcal{I}
-property. The first inclusion above yields that

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}}=m_{\mathcal{I}}((vf)(U))\leq m_{\mathcal{I}}(T_{f}(B_{\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)}))=\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}.

∎

\varepsilon>0
, there exist a closed subspace
M\subseteq\ell_{p^{*}}
(
c_{0}
instead of
\ell_{p^{*}}
if
p=1
), a separable Banach space
G
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\ell_{p^{*}}/M,G)
, an operator
S\in\mathcal{K}(G,F)
and an operator
R\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),\ell_{p^{*}}/M)
such that
T_{f}=S\circ T\circ R
with
\left\|S\right\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\left\|R\right\|\leq\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}+\varepsilon
. Moreover,
R=T_{g}
with
\left\|g\right\|_{v}=\left\|R\right\|
for some
g\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}}^{\infty}(U,\ell_{p^{*}}/M)
. Thus, we obtain

f=T_{f}\circ\Delta_{v}=S\circ T\circ R\circ\Delta_{v}=S\circ T\circ T_{g}\circ\Delta_{v}=S\circ T\circ g,

with

\left\|S\right\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\left\|g\right\|_{v}=\left\|S\right\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\left\|R\right\|\leq\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}+\varepsilon=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}+\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, we deduce that
\left\|S\right\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\left\|g\right\|_{v}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Assume that
f=S\circ T\circ g
is a factorization as in
(ii)
. Since
S\circ T\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\ell_{p^{*}}/M,F)
by the ideal property of
\mathcal{K}_{p}
, Corollary
2.10
yields that
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
with

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}\leq\left\|S\circ T\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\left\|g\right\|_{v}\leq\left\|S\right\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\left\|g\right\|_{v},

and taking infimum over all such factorizations of
f
, we have
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}\leq\inf\{\left\|S\right\|\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\left\|g\right\|_{v}\}
.
∎

The next result –which can be proven similarly to Corollary
2.12
using
[
22
, Theorem 2.3]
– is a factorization theorem stronger than Corollary
2.11
for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p}
and Corollary
2.12
.

Corollary 2.13
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(ii)

There exist a quotient space
Z
of
\ell_{p^{*}}
(
c_{0}
instead of
\ell_{p^{*}}
if
p=1
), an operator
S
in
\mathcal{K}_{p}(Z,F)
, a mapping
g
in
\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{up}}^{\infty}(U,Z)
such that
f=S\circ g
.

In this case,
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{up}}^{\infty}}=\inf\{\left\|S\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{up}}^{\infty}}\}
, where the infimum is taken over all possible
S^{\prime}
s and
g
’s.
\hfill\qed

The celebrated Davis–Figiel–Johnson–Pełczynski theorem
[
11
]
asserts that any weakly compact linear operator factors through a reflexive Banach space. Since
[\mathcal{K}_{wp},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{wp}}]\leq[\mathcal{K}_{w},\left\|\cdot\right\|]
whenever
p\in(1,\infty)
, the cited theorem and Corollary
2.11
yields immediately the following.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(ii)

There are a quotient space
G
of
\ell_{p^{*}}
, a reflexive Banach space
H
, operators
T\in\mathcal{L}(G,H)
and
Q\in\mathcal{L}(H,F)
and a mapping
g\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}(U,G)
such that
f=Q\circ T\circ g
.

In this case, we have

\inf\{\left\|Q\circ T\right\|\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}}\}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}}\leq\inf\{\left\|Q\right\|\left\|T\right\|\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}}\},

where both infimums are taken over all such factorizations of
f
as in (ii).
\hfill\Box

2.4.
Transposition

The Banach operator ideals
\mathcal{K}_{p}
,
\mathcal{K}_{wp}
and
\mathcal{K}_{up}
are associated by duality with the ideals of quasi
p
-nuclear operators, quasi weakly
p
-nuclear operators and quasi unconditionally
p
-nuclear operators, respectively.

According to
[
28
,
24
,
21
]
, for every
p\in[1,\infty)
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
is said to be
quasi
p
-nuclear (resp., quasi weakly
p
-nuclear, quasi unconditionally
p
-nuclear)
if there is a sequence
(x_{n}^{*})\in\ell_{p}(E^{*})
(resp.,
(x_{n}^{*})\in\ell_{p}^{w}(E^{*})
,
(x_{n}^{*})\in\ell_{p}^{u}(E^{*})
) such that
\left\|T(x)\right\|\leq\left\|(x_{n}^{*}(x))\right\|_{p}
for every
x\in E
.

The space of all quasi
p
-nuclear (resp., quasi weakly
p
-nuclear, quasi unconditionally
p
-nuclear) operators from
E
into
F
is denoted by
\mathcal{QN}_{p}(E,F)
(resp.,
\mathcal{QN}_{wp}(E,F)
,
\mathcal{QN}_{up}(E,F)
).

It is known that
[\mathcal{QN}_{p},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{p}}]
, equipped with the norm

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{p}}=\inf\left\{\|(x_{n}^{*})\|_{p}\colon||T(x)||\leq\left\|(x_{n}^{*}(x))\right\|_{p},\;\forall x\in E\right\},

is a Banach operator ideal. Similarly,
[\mathcal{QN}_{wp},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{wp}}]
and
[\mathcal{QN}_{up},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{wp}}]
, endowed with the norm

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{wp}}=\inf\left\{\|(x_{n}^{*})\|^{w}_{p}\colon||T(x)||\leq\left\|(x_{n}^{*}(x))\right\|_{p},\;\forall x\in E\right\},

are Banach operator ideals.

By
[
12
, Proposition 3.8]
and
[
13
, Corollary 2.7]
for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p}
and
i=p
, and
[
22
, Theorem 5.6]
for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{up}
and
i=up
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{I}(E,F)
if and only if its adjoint
T^{*}\in\mathcal{QN}_{i}(F^{*},E^{*})
, in whose case
\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|T^{*}\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{i}}
.

A weighted holomorphic version of these results can be stated as follows.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
. For the Banach operator ideal
[\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}]
with
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p}
(resp.,
\mathcal{K}_{up}
) and
i=p
(resp.,
up
), the following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(ii)

f^{t}\in\mathcal{QN}_{i}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
.

In this case,
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}}=\left\|f^{t}\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{i}}
.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
2.9
and the results preceding to this corollary, we have

\displaystyle f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{I}}(U,F)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(T_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{QN}_{i}(F^{*},\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f^{t}=J_{v}^{-1}\circ(T_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{QN}_{i}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U)).

Moreover,
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}}=\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|(T_{f})^{*}\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{i}}=\left\|f^{t}\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{i}}
.
∎

In
[
23
, Theorem 3.7 (c)]
, Kim proved that the adjoint of a weakly
p
-compact operator
T
is quasi weakly
p
-nuclear and
\left\|T^{*}\right\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{wp}}\leq\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{wp}}
. Using this fact, we obtain the following with a proof similar to that of Corollary
2.15
.

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. If
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}(U,F)
, then
f^{t}\in\mathcal{QN}_{wp}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
and
\|f^{t}\|_{\mathcal{QN}_{wp}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}}
.
\hfill\Box

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
, let us recall (see
[
29
]
) that an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
is
p-summing
if there exists a constant
C\geq 0
such that

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}||T(x_{i})||^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq C\sup_{x^{*}\in B_{E^{*}}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|x^{*}(x_{i})\right|^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in E
. The infimum of such constants
C
is denoted by
\pi_{p}(T)
and the linear space of all
p
-summing operators from
E
into
F
by
\Pi_{p}(E,F)
. The pair
[\Pi_{p},\pi_{p}]
is a Banach operator ideal.

Based on
[
12
, Proposition 3.13]
, we propose a weighted holomorphic variant of this result.

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
and
g\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}}^{\infty}(U,F^{*})
. Assume that
T_{f}\in\Pi_{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then
f^{t}\circ g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
with
\left\|f^{t}\circ g\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}\leq\pi_{p}(T_{f})\left\|g\right\|_{v}
.

Proof.

Observe that
T_{g}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F^{*})
with
||T_{g}||=\left\|g\right\|_{v}
. Hence
(T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*})
with
\left\|(T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\leq\pi_{p}(T_{f})||T_{g}||
by
[
12
, Proposition 3.13]
. From the equality
f^{t}\circ T_{g}=J_{v}^{-1}\circ(T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g}
, we infer that
f^{t}\circ T_{g}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
with
\left\|f^{t}\circ T_{g}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}=\left\|(T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}
by the ideal property of
\mathcal{K}_{p}
. Applying Theorem
2.9
, there exists
h\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
with
\left\|h\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}=\left\|T_{h}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}
such that
f^{t}\circ T_{g}=T_{h}
. Hence
f^{t}\circ g=h
and thus
f^{t}\circ g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
with
\left\|f^{t}\circ g\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}=\left\|T_{h}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}=\left\|(T_{f})^{*}\circ T_{g}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\leq\pi_{p}(T_{f})\left\|T_{g}\right\|=\pi_{p}(T_{f})\left\|g\right\|_{v}
.
∎

A proof similar to that of Corollary
2.17
but applying
[
31
, Proposition 5.4]
instead of
[
12
, Proposition 3.13]
provides another result closely related.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
and
g\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}(U,F^{*})
. Assume that
(T_{f})^{*}\in\Pi_{p}(F^{*},\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*})
with
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then
f^{t}\circ g\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
with
\left\|f^{t}\circ g\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}\leq\pi_{p}((T_{f})^{*})\left\|g\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{wp}}^{\infty}}
.
\hfill\qed

As we have recalled,
p
-compact operators are characterized as those operators
T
for which
T^{*}
factor through a subspace of
\ell_{p}
. We now obtain a similar factorization for the transpose of a map in
\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(ii)

There exist a closed subspace
M\subseteq\ell_{p}
and
R\in\mathcal{QN}_{p}(F^{*},M)
,
S\in\mathcal{L}(M,\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
such that
f^{t}=S\circ R
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: If
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
, then
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
by Theorem
2.9
. By
[
12
, Proposition 3.10]
, there exist a closed subspace
M\subseteq\ell_{p}
and operators
R\in\mathcal{QN}_{p}(F^{*},M)
and
S_{0}\in\mathcal{L}(M,\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*})
such that
(T_{f})^{*}=S_{0}\circ R
. Taking
S=J_{v}^{-1}\circ S_{0}\in\mathcal{L}(M,\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
, we have
f^{t}=S\circ R
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Assume that
f^{t}=S\circ R
being
S
and
R
as in the statement. Then
(T_{f})^{*}=J_{v}\circ f^{t}=J_{v}\circ S\circ R
, and thus
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
12
, Proposition 3.10]
. Hence
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
by Theorem
2.9

∎

2.5.
Relations with
p
-approximable type weighted holomorphic mappings

In similarity with the linear case, it seems natural to introduce the following classes of weighted holomorphic mappings which are related to the preceding ones. The particular case of bounded holomorphic mappings was dealed by Mujica in
[
26
]
.

Definition 2.20
.

A mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
is said to
have finite-dimensional
v
-rank
if the linear hull of
(vf)(U)
is a finite-dimensional subspace of
F
. In that case, we define the
v-
\mathrm{rank}(f)
of
f
to be the dimension of this subspace. We denote by
\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(U,F)
the space of all weighted holomorphic mappings from
U
to
F
that have finite-dimensional
v
-rank.

A mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
is said to be
approximable
if there exists a sequence
(f_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(U,F)
such that
\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{v}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
, a mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
is said to be
p
-approximable (resp.,
wp
-approximable,
up
-approximable)
if there exists a sequence
(f_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(U,F)
such that
\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{H_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

We denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(U,F)
(resp.,
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\overline{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{I}}}(U,F)
,
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
) the space of all approximable (resp.,
p
-approximable,
wp
-approximable,
up
-approximable) weighted holomorphic mappings from
U
to
F
.

We now study the linearization and transposition of these mappings.

Proposition 2.21
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(ii)

T_{f}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
.

(iii)

f^{t}\in\mathcal{F}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
.

In this case,
v
-
\mathrm{rank}(f)=\mathrm{rank}(T_{f})
. Furthermore, the correspondence
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v})
onto
(\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F),\left\|\cdot\right\|)
.

Proof.

(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)
and
v
-
\mathrm{rank}(f)=\mathrm{rank}(T_{f})
are deduced from the equality

\mathrm{lin}((vf)(U))=\mathrm{lin}(T_{f}(\mathrm{At}_{\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)})=T_{f}(\mathrm{lin}(\mathrm{At}_{\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)}))=T_{f}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)).

(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
follows from

\displaystyle T_{f}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(T_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{F}(F^{*},\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f^{t}=J_{v}^{-1}\circ(T_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{F}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}^{\infty}(U))

since
\mathcal{F}
is a completely symmetric operator ideal
[
29
, 4.4.7]
.
∎

In view of Theorem
2.9
, Propositions
2.6
and
2.21
give the following.

Corollary 2.22
.

[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{F}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v}]=[\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}]
and, in particular,
[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{F}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v}]
is a normed weighted holomorphic ideal.
\hfill\qed

Corollary
2.10
shows that
[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{F}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v}]\leq[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}}]
for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
. Now, we give the following result.

Corollary 2.23
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
. Then
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\overline{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{I}}}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\overline{\mathcal{F}}^{\mathcal{I}}}(U,F)
. Hence there is a sequence
(f_{n})
in
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{F}}(U,F)
such that
\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{I}}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
T_{f_{n}}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
by Proposition
2.21
;
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)\subseteq\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
by
[
31
, Theorem 4.2]
,
[
31
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
21
, Theorem 2.1]
for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K}_{p},\mathcal{K}_{wp},\mathcal{K}_{up}
, respectively; and
\left\|T_{f_{n}}-T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{I}}=\left\|f_{n}-f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{I}}^{\infty}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by Theorem
2.9
, we deduce that
T_{f}\in\overline{\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)}^{\mathcal{I}}
. Hence
T_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
by the same results of
[
31
]
and
[
21
]
cited above, and so
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{I}}(U,F)
by Theorem
2.9
.
∎

We can also easily prove the following result using Proposition
2.21
and the fact that the class of approximable linear operators is also a completely symmetric operator ideal
[
29
, 4.4.7]
.

Proposition 2.24
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{H}_{v\overline{\mathcal{F}}}^{\infty}(U,F)
.

(ii)

T_{f}\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
.

(iii)

f^{t}\in\overline{\mathcal{F}}(F^{*},\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
.

Furthermore, the correspondence
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
(\mathcal{H}_{v\overline{\mathcal{F}}}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v})
onto
(\overline{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F),\left\|\cdot\right\|)
.
\hfill\qed

Now, Theorem
2.9
, Propositions
2.6
and
2.24
provide the following.

Corollary 2.25
.

[\mathcal{H}_{v\overline{\mathcal{F}}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v}]=[\overline{\mathcal{F}}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}]
and, in particular,
[\mathcal{H}_{v\overline{\mathcal{F}}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v}]
is a Banach weighted holomorphic ideal.
\hfill\qed

Let us recall that a Banach space
E
is said to have the
approximation property
if given a compact set
K\subseteq E
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is an operator
T\in\mathcal{F}(E,E)
such that
\left\|T(x)-x\right\|<\varepsilon
for every
x\in K
. On the approximation property in holomorphic function spaces, we refer to Mujica
[
26
]
.

Grothendieck
[
15
]
proved that a dual Banach space
E^{*}
has the approximation property if and only if given a Banach space
F
, an operator
S\in\mathcal{K}(E,F)
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is an operator
T\in\mathcal{F}(E,F)
such that
\left\|T-S\right\|<\varepsilon
. Since
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
is a dual space, combining the Grothendieck’s result with Proposition
2.2
, Theorem
2.9
and Proposition
2.24
, we next give a necessary and sufficient condition for the space
\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
have the approximation property.

Corollary 2.26
.

\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
has the approximation property if and only if
\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}}^{\infty}(U,F)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{v\overline{\mathcal{F}}}^{\infty}(U,F)
for each complex Banach space
F
.
\hfill\qed

2.6.
Relations with right
p
-nuclear weighted holomorphic mappings

Let us recall (see
[
27
]
) that an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E,F)
is said to be
right
p
-nuclear
if there are sequences
(x_{n}^{*})\in\ell_{p^{*}}^{w}(E^{*})
and
(y_{n})\in\ell_{p}(F)
such that

T(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{*}(x)y_{n}\qquad(x\in E)

and the series converges in
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
. The set of such operators, denoted
\mathcal{N}^{p}(E,F)
, is a Banach space with the norm

\left\|T\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}=\inf\left\{\|(x_{n}^{*})\|_{p^{*}}^{w}\|y_{n}\|_{p}\right\},

where the infimum is taken over all representations of
T
as above.

A weighted holomorphic variant of this class of operators can be introduced as follows.

Definition 2.27
.

Given
p\in[1,\infty)
, a mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
is said to be
right
p
-nuclear
if there exist sequences
(g_{n})
in
\ell_{p^{*}}^{w}(\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
and
(y_{n})
in
\ell_{p}(F)
such that
f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\cdot y_{n}
in
\left(\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F),\|\cdot\|_{v}\right)
. We set

\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}_{p}}}=\inf\left\{\|(g_{n})\|_{p^{*}}^{w}\|(y_{n})\|_{p}\right\},

with the infimum taken over all right
p
-nuclear weighted holomorphic representations of
f
as above. Let
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F)
denote the set of all right
p
-nuclear weighted holomorphic mappings from
U
into
F
.

We now linearize right
p
-nuclear weighted holomorphic mappings.

Proposition 2.28
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and
f\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(i)

f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F)
.

(ii)

T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
.

In this case, we have
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}}=\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}
. Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto T_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\left(\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}}\right)
onto
\left(\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}\right)
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Assume that
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F)
and let
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\cdot y_{n}
be a right
p
-nuclear weighted holomorphic representation of
f
. Take the unique operator
T_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
such that
T_{f}\circ\Delta_{v}=f
. Similarly, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, take
T_{g_{n}}\in\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*}
with
||T_{g_{n}}||=\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{v}
such that
T_{g_{n}}\circ\Delta_{v}=g_{n}
. Notice that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{g_{n}}\cdot y_{n}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
since

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|T_{g_{k}}\cdot y_{k}\right\|=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|T_{g_{k}}\right\|\left\|y_{k}\right\|=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\left\|g_{k}\right\|_{v}\left\|y_{k}\right\|\leq\left\|(g_{n})\right\|^{w}_{p^{*}}\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{p},

for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We can write

f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\cdot y_{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(T_{g_{n}}\circ\Delta_{v})\cdot y_{n}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{g_{n}}\cdot y_{n}\right)\circ\Delta_{v},

in
(\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v})
. Hence
T_{f}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}T_{g_{n}}\cdot y_{n}
, where
(T_{g_{n}})\in\ell^{w}_{p^{*}}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U)^{*})
with
\left\|(T_{g_{n}})\right\|^{w}_{p^{*}}\leq\left\|(g_{n})\right\|^{w}_{p^{*}}
. Therefore
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}\leq\left\|(g_{n})\right\|^{w}_{p^{*}}\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{p}
. Taking infimum over all right
p
-nuclear weighted holomorphic representations of
f
, we deduce that
\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}}
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
: Suppose that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
and let
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\phi_{n}\cdot y_{n}
be a right
p
-nuclear representation of
T_{f}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a
g_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U)
such that
J_{v}(g_{n})=\phi_{n}
with
\left\|g_{n}\right\|_{v}=||\phi_{n}||
. We have

\displaystyle v(x)\left\|\left(f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right)(x)\right\|

\displaystyle=v(x)\left\|f(x)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}(x)y_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle=v(x)\left\|T_{f}(\Delta_{v}(x))-\sum_{k=1}^{n}J_{v}(g_{k})(\Delta_{v}(x))y_{k}\right\|

\displaystyle=v(x)\left\|\left(T_{f}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right)(\Delta_{v}(x))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{f}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right\|v(x)\left\|\Delta_{v}(x)\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|T_{f}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right\|

for all
x\in U
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking supremum over all
x\in U
, we obtain

\left\|f-\sum_{k=1}^{n}g_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right\|_{v}\leq\left\|T_{f}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\phi_{k}\cdot y_{k}\right\|,

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}\cdot y_{n}
in
(\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U,F),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{v})
, where
(g_{n})\in\ell^{w}_{p^{*}}(\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}(U))
with
\left\|(g_{n})\right\|^{w}_{p^{*}}\leq\left\|(\phi_{n})\right\|^{w}_{p^{*}}
. So
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F)
with
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}}\leq\left\|(\phi_{n})\right\|^{w}_{p^{*}}\left\|(y_{n})\right\|_{p}
, and therefore
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}}\leq\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}
.

∎

Combining Proposition
2.28
with Theorem
2.7
and Proposition
2.6
, we derive the following.

Corollary 2.29
.

[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}^{\infty}}]=[\mathcal{N}^{p}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}\circ\mathcal{H}_{v}^{\infty}}]
and, in particular,
[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}^{\infty}}]
is a Banach weighted holomorphic ideal for any
p\in[1,\infty)
.
\hfill\qed

We now show that every right
p
-nuclear weighted holomorphic mapping has a relatively
p
-compact
v
-range.

Corollary 2.30
.

[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}^{\infty}}]\leq[\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty},\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}^{\infty}}]
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}(U,F)
. By Proposition
2.28
, we have
T_{f}\in\mathcal{N}^{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}=\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}}
. It follows that
T_{f}\in\mathcal{K}_{p}(\mathcal{G}_{v}^{\infty}(U),F)
with
\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{K}_{p}}\leq\left\|T_{f}\right\|_{\mathcal{N}^{p}}
(see
[
12
, p. 295]
). Hence
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}(U,F)
with
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{K}_{p}}}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{v\mathcal{N}^{p}}}
by Theorem
2.9
.
∎

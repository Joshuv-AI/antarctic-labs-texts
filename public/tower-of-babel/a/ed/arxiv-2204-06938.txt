Abstract
isac ( isac ) is recognized as a promising technology for the next-generation wireless networks, which provides significant performance gains over individual snc ( snc ) systems via the shared use of wireless resources. The characterization of the snc performance tradeoff is at the core of the theoretical foundation of isac . In this paper, we consider a p2p ( p2p ) isac model under vector Gaussian channels, and propose to use the crb ( crb )-rate region as a basic tool for depicting the fundamental snc tradeoff. In particular, we consider the scenario where a unified isac waveform is emitted from a dual-functional isac transmitter (Tx), which simultaneously performs snc tasks with a communication receiver (Rx) and a sensing Rx. In order to perform both snc tasks, the isac waveform is required to be random to convey communication information, with realizations being perfectly known at both the isac Tx and the sensing Rx as a reference sensing signal as in typical radar systems. In this context, we treat the isac waveform as a random but known nuisance parameter in the sensing signal model, and define a Miller-Chang type crb for the analysis of the sensing performance. As the main contribution of this paper, we characterize the snc performance at the two corner points of the crb -rate region, namely, P SC P_{\rm SC} indicating the maximum achievable communication rate constrained by the minimum crb , and P CS P_{\rm CS} indicating the minimum achievable crb constrained by the maximum communication rate. In particular, we derive the high-SNR communication capacity at P SC P_{\rm SC} , and provide lower and upper bounds for the sensing crb at P CS P_{\rm CS} . We show that these two points can be achieved by the conventional Gaussian signalling and a novel strategy relying on the uniform distribution over the set of semi-unitary matrices, i.e., the Stiefel manifold, respectively. Based on the above-mentioned analysis, we provide an outer bound and various inner bounds for the achievable crb -rate regions. Our main results reveal a two-fold tradeoff in isac systems, consisting of the st ( st ) and the drt ( drt ) that depend on the resource allocation and data modulation schemes employed for snc , respectively. Within this framework, we examine the state-of-the-art isac signalling strategies and study a number of illustrative examples, which are validated through numerical simulations.

Discussions

In this section, we discuss the intuitions and implications of the results presented in Section

IV-A

snc
Tradeoff as a Two-fold Tradeoff: ST and DRT

A central implication of the results in Section
III
is that the
snc
tradeoff is two-fold, detailed as follows.

IV-A
1
st
(
st
)

According to the discussions in Section
III-B
and Section
III-C
, at point
P_{\rm SC}
, any sensing-optimal
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
has the following structure

\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}=\bm{U}_{\rm s}\bm{\varLambda}_{\rm s}\bm{U}_{\rm s}^{\rm H},

(39)

where
\bm{U}_{\rm s}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times r}
contains the eigenvectors of the maximum-rank (
=r
) sensing-optimal
\bm{R}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
corresponding to the non-zero eigenvalues, while
\bm{\varLambda}_{\rm s}
is a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix that ensures
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
to be an optimal solution of the problem (
14
). In particular, when the sensing optimal
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
is unique,
\bm{\varLambda}_{\rm s}
is a diagonal matrix containing the non-zero eigenvalues of
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
. By contrast, at point
P_{\rm CS}
, the communication-optimal
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
takes the following form

\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}=\bm{U}_{\rm c}\bm{\varLambda}_{\rm c}\bm{U}_{\rm c}^{\rm H},

(40)

where
\bm{U}_{\rm c}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times{\rm rank}(\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{\rm c})}
contains the left-singular vectors of the communication channel matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{\rm c}
corresponding to the non-zero singular values, while
\bm{\varLambda}_{\rm c}
is a diagonal matrix determined by the water-filling power allocation strategy.

From the discussion above, we may observe that the matrix
\bm{U}_{\rm s}
characterizes the
sensing subspace
, while
\bm{U}_{\rm c}
characterizes the
communication subspace
. Given a specific statistical covariance matrix
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
, when its column space is more closely aligned with the sensing subspace, we may achieve a more favorable sensing performance at the cost of degraded communication performance, and vice versa. This depicts a part of the entire picture of the
snc
tradeoff, which will be referred to as the
st
(
st
).

IV-A
2
drt
(
drt
)

Given a specific
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
, the
snc
tradeoff can be further adjusted by controlling the “degree of randomness” of the signal
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
. To obtain a higher communication rate, one should modulate as much information as possible onto the carrier signal, hence the transmitted waveform should be “as random as possible”. However, sensing systems prefer deterministic signals for achieving a stable sensing performance. This has been an intuitive insight consistent with both the engineers’ experience and
snc
signal processing theory developed during the past few decades
[
29
]
. Indeed, Proposition
3
tells us that to achieve the optimal sensing performance, the transmitted waveform has to be deterministic to a degree, in the sense that its sample covariance matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
should be deterministic. Consequently, following the notations in (
39
), any sensing-optimal signal should take the following form

\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}=\sqrt{T}\bm{U}_{\rm s}\bm{\varLambda}_{\rm s}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\mathsfbr{Q}},

(41)

where
\bm{\mathsfbr{Q}}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times T}
is a semi-unitary matrix satisfying
\bm{\mathsfbr{Q}}\bm{\mathsfbr{Q}}^{\rm H}=\bm{I}
. Among these sensing-optimal signals, those achieving the highest communication rate are given by Corollary
3
. By contrast, at point
P_{\rm CS}
, the communication-optimal signal is given by

\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}=\bm{U}_{\rm c}\bm{\varLambda}_{\rm c}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bm{\mathsfbr{D}},

(42)

where
\bm{\mathsfbr{D}}\in\mathbb{C}^{{\rm rank}(\bm{\mathsfbr{H}}_{\rm c})\times T}
contains
iid
standard circularly symmetric complex Gaussian entries.

Comparing (
41
) with (
42
), it can be observed that the sensing-optimal signal
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
sacrifices some communication
dof
, in the sense that the rows in
\bm{\mathsfbr{Q}}
are forced to have unit norms and to be orthogonal to one another. On the other hand, as indicated by Theorem
2
, the communication-optimal
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
also sacrifices some sensing
dof
. We refer to this part of the
snc
tradeoff as the
drt
(
drt
).
A noteworthy issue is that, when the coherent sensing period
T
is sufficiently long compared to
\mathsfbr{M}_{\rm SC}
, the
drt
becomes less prominent, since even a communication-optimal Gaussian waveform
\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}
would have asymptotically orthogonal rows as
T/\mathsfbr{M}_{\rm SC}\rightarrow\infty

[
30
]
. A related fact is that most existing contributions on the joint design of
isac
systems treat the sample covariance matrix
\bm{\mathsfbr{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}
as a deterministic matrix
[
7
,
31
,
32
,
33
]
. Such designs may now be interpreted as neglecting the
drt
, and hence can be viewed as
infinite-
T
approximations
to the original
isac
design problem.

IV-A
3

and

in terms of

Next, we provide some quantified characterizations of the

and
drt
. Let us first take the point

Regarding the point

\displaystyle\min_{p(\bm{\mathsfbr{X}})}

IV-B

In Section

Remark 1
(Null space completion by semi-unitary signals)

According to (
51
), when
\mathsfbr{M}_{\rm CS}<M
, the Gaussian inner bound–achieving signalling scheme does not send pure Gaussian signals. To elaborate, in this case, since
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}^{\rm CS}
is rank-deficient, the sensing
dof
is zero. In particular, at point
P_{\rm CS}
, the
isac
signal cannot provide any information about the target response matrix in the null space of
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}^{\rm CS}
, and hence we have to rely entirely on the a priori knowledge when estimating this part of the target response matrix. As we move from
P_{\rm CS}
to
P_{\rm SC}
on the outer bound,
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}(\alpha)
becomes full-rank. However, the power allocated to the null space of
\widetilde{\bm{R}}_{\bm{\mathsfbr{X}}}^{\rm CS}
does not contributed to the communication rate. Therefore, we may transmit semi-unitary signals instead of Gaussian signals in the null space, without eroding the communication rate at all.

VII
Conclusions

In this paper, we have proposed a general framework for the analysis of the
snc
performance tradeoff. In particular, we have introduced the
crb
-rate region for characterizing the
snc
tradeoff, and have shown that it has a pentagon inner bound obtained by connecting the communication-optimal point
P_{\rm CS}
and the sensing-optimal point
P_{\rm SC}
. Especially for
P_{\rm SC}
, we have shown that it is achieved when the transmitted waveform has a sample covariance matrix with deterministic trace. In particular, when the
crb
minimization problem has a unique solution, the sample covariance matrix is deterministic, and hence the
isac
waveform should be modulated by symbols generated based on the uniform distribution of the Stiefel manifold. For
P_{\rm CS}
, we have shown that the maximum sensing
dof
loss is determined by the number of sensing parameters
K

Appendix L
Proof of Proposition
6

Proof:

To prove this proposition, it suffices to show that there exists at least one point
(\epsilon,R)
on the Gaussian inner bound or the semi-unitary inner bound that lies above the line segment connecting
P_{\rm SC}
and
P_{\rm CS}
. Once we found such a point
P=(\epsilon,R)
, we see that the time-sharing strategies between
P
,
P_{\rm SC}
and
P_{\rm CS}
readily constitute a tighter inner bound than the pentagon inner bound.

Next we show that there do exist such points, under mild assumptions. To this end, let us consider the slope of the semi-unitary–Gaussian inner bound at a specific value of
\alpha
. For our purpose, it suffices to show that at
P_{\rm SC}
(resp.
P_{\rm CS}
), the slope of the semi-unitary–Gaussian inner bound is larger (resp. smaller) than the slope of the line segment connecting
P_{\rm SC}
and
P_{\rm CS}
. First note that, as long as the optimal objective function value is not identical for all
\alpha\in[0,1]
, the slope of the outer bound (
50
) at a specific value of
\alpha
is given by

\frac{\partial R_{\rm out}(\alpha)}{\partial\epsilon_{\rm out}(\alpha)}=\frac{T}{\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}\cdot\lambda_{\alpha},

(252)

where
\lambda_{\alpha}
is the optimal Lagrange dual variable corresponding to the constraint (
49c
). According to the correspondence between the formulations (
48
) and (
49
) (c.f.
[
57
, Sec. 5.3.3]
),
\lambda_{\alpha}
can be explicitly obtained as

\lambda_{\alpha}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha},

(253)

which tends to infinity as
\alpha\rightarrow 0
. Now, the slope of the semi-unitary inner bound can be expressed as

\frac{\partial R_{\rm in,U}(\alpha)}{\partial\epsilon_{\rm in,U}(\alpha)}=\Big(1-\frac{\mathsfbr{M}_{\rm U}}{2T}\Big)\frac{(1-\alpha)T}{\alpha\sigma_{\rm s}^{2}}+O(\sigma_{\rm c}^{2}),

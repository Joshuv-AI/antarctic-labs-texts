Improved Mixing Rates of Directed Cycles with Additional Sparse Interconnections
Balázs Gerencsér, Julien M. Hendrickx

Abstract
We analyze the absolute spectral gap of Markov chains on graphs obtained from a cycle of n n vertices and perturbed only at approximately n 1 / ρ n^{1/\rho} random locations with an appropriate, possibly sparse, interconnection structure. Together with a strong asymmetry along the cycle, the gap of the resulting chain can be bounded inversely proportionally by the longest arc length (up to logarithmic factors) with high probability, providing a significant mixing speedup compared to the reversible version.



1
Introduction

This work focuses on understanding how the mixing properties of Markov chains, and how they can be improved by making as little change as possible on the initial connection graph.
Some impressive results in that direction are already available. The authors of
[
15
]
show how a rather general class of initial graphs on
n
vertices can be turned into an expander, thereby making the simple random walk rapidly mixing, by adding approximately
\epsilon n
random edges. A more refined analysis resulting in stronger bounds is performed in
[
14
]
and its follow-up
[
1
]
, where a (possibly weighted) random perfect matching is added. These authors are then able to determine a mixing-time with cut-off. However, this line of works always involves adding a number of edges that
grows linearly
with the number of vertices. By contrast, we aim at understanding if significant speed-ups can still be obtained by adding a much smaller number edges that create shortcuts, provided
we adapt the transition probabilities
of the initial chain, specifically without requiring them to remain reversible.

Since determining the mixing time is currently out of reach in the settings we consider, we will aim at approximating the (absolute) spectral gap,
measuring the mixing efficiency of the Markov chain in the asymptotic regime.

Definition 1
.

For any irreducible doubly stochastic matrix
A
let its
spectral gap
be

\lambda(A)=\min\left\{1-|\mu|\penalty\ :\penalty\ \mu\neq 1\text{ is an eigenvalue of }A\right\}.

(1)

We focus on the cycle graph, and show that the combination of transition probabilities adaptation and addition of a small number of edges does indeed significantly outperform any of these two operations taken individually. Indeed, it was shown in
[
10
]
that optimizing the transition probabilities on a cycle graph could not provide any significant speedup even when allowing for non-reversibility. On the other hand, one can verify that in the reversible case, the spectral gap of a cycle on
n
nodes to which one adds edges incident to
k\ll n
nodes will always remain
O(k^{2}/n^{2})
.
By contrast, in our recent work
[
11
]
, we have shown that a significant speed-up to
\Theta(k\log^{-\gamma}k/n)
(for
\gamma>4
) could be obtained if a small number
k\approx n^{1/\rho}
(for
\rho>1
) of randomly selected positions were perturbed and interconnected by a
fully connected graph
, and the walk on the rest of the cycle was made directed and deterministic.
The current paper generalizes these results by showing that the speed-up is present for more general interconnection structures, provided it is sufficiently expanding, see Definition
2
and Theorem
6
for a precise statement of the model and the result.

We note the existence of several alternative ways of improving the spectral gaps of Markov chains. The first one relies on optimizing the individual transition probabilities in an algorithmically accessible way
[
6
,
5
]
, which then can be then exploited and carried further for special cases, e.g. for random geometric graphs
[
7
]
. But these works almost all impose the reversibility of the Markov chain. Moreover, we have seen above that in the case of the cycle graph, optimization of the probabilities alone cannot lead to any significant speed-up, even in the absence of reversibility requirements.
In fact, it can even be shown that appropriate homogeneous reversible random walks are optimal in some sense
[
12
]
.
A different recent technique is based on alternating between stepping with the original Markov chain and moving by a permutation fixed apriori, see
[
8
,
3
,
13
]
for the initial theory, refined cut-off analysis for random permutations, and some specialized case study. But this does clearly involve very significant modification of the Markov chain structure.
Another possibility is to reduce the backtracking of the chain, in
[
9
]
a non-backtracking component is introduced and indeed an improvement of the spectral gap is shown.

Our paper is organized as follows: We introduce our precise models of Markov chains and state our main results in Section
2
. We then show in Section
3
how the spectral gap can be characterized in terms of a lower-dimensional modified eigenvalue problem, and what it entails for the corresponding eigenvectors. Sections
4
and
5
focus then on the analysis of eigenvalues with respectively small and large arguments, and their combinations allows establishing the results of Section
2
. We compare our results with experimental studies in Section
6
and observe that our bounds (without the polylogarithmic factors) appear to predict the actual behavior of the spectral gap. We finish by discussing our result and some possible further works in Section
7
.

Models and Main Results

2.1
Models

We now formally define the random Markov transition matrix model outlined in the introduction.

(reconnected cycle model)
.

Let

and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
be a symmetric doubly stochastic matrix. We define the

H_{n}(A)
as the random Markov transition matrix corresponding to the directed weighted graph obtained by the three steps below, depicted in Figure

1.

Start with a directed cycle on

2.

For each

A_{ij}=A_{ji}

Main Results

We first state our theorem for our proxy reconnected arcs model

.

Assume

\lambda(\bar{A})\geq\frac{k}{n\log^{\gamma}k},

These rates are comparable, up to logarithmic factors, to those obtained in
[

, which was restricted to the fully connected

3
Low-Dimensional Representation for Reconnected Arcs

3.1
Modified Eigenvalue Problem

On the way to find the eigenvalues - eigenvectors of the reconnected arcs
G_{L}(A)
described in Definition
3
, our first step will be to transform
G_{L}(A)y=\mu y
into a condensed equivalent form involving only
A
and one variable per arc.

Proposition 7
.

The eigenvalues
\mu
of reconnected arcs
G_{L}(A)
are exactly the values for which the following equation admits a non-trivial solution
x

CAx=D(\mu)x,

(2)

where
D(\mu)
is the diagonal matrix with
D_{ii}=\mu^{L_{i}}
and
C
is a circular permutation matrix with
C_{i,i-1}=1
for all
i
(with
k+1\equiv 1
).

Proof.

Consider an eigenvalue
\mu
and corresponding eigenvector
y
for
G_{L}(A)
thus satisfying
G_{L}(A)y=\mu y
. Starting from a node
(i,\ell)
for
\ell>1
, since this node only has one incoming edge from
(i,\ell-1)
, the equation
G_{L}(A)y=\mu y
implies
\mu y_{i,\ell}=y_{i,\ell-1}
. The value of all the
y_{i,\ell}
is thus uniquely specified by
y_{i,L_{i}}
. Defining
x_{i}:=y_{i,L_{i}}
, we indeed have
y_{i,\ell}=x_{i}\mu^{L_{i}-\ell}
. In particular,
y_{i,1}=x_{i}\mu^{L_{i}-1}
.

Let us now consider node
y_{i,1}
. This node has incoming edges with weights
A_{i-1,j}
from all nodes
(j,L_{j})
for which
A_{i-1,j}\neq 0
. The eigenvalue equation yields thus

\mu y_{i,1}=\sum_{j}A_{i-1,j}y_{j,L_{j}}.

Taking into account the relation
y_{i,1}=x_{i}\mu^{L_{i}-1}
, we have equivalently

\mu\cdot\mu^{L_{i}-1}x_{i}=\sum_{j}A_{i-1,j}x_{j},\hskip 14.22636pt\forall i=1,\dots,k,

which can be rewritten as

CAx=D(\mu)x.

(3)

So any solution of
G_{L}(A)y=\mu y
yields a solution of (
3
), and by expanding
x
to
y
it is easy to confirm that the converse is also true.
∎

The sequel of the proof focuses thus on this condensed equation (
3
). We will show the impossibility, w.h.p. of obtaining solution
x,\mu
(
\mu\neq 1
) of (
3
) with
\mu\in R_{\gamma}
, defined as

R_{\gamma}:=\{\mu\neq 1|\geq|\mu|\geq 1-\frac{1}{L\log^{\gamma}k}\}.

We will in fact follow different approaches for
\mu
with “small” or “large” arguments, and consider thus respectively in Section
4
and Section
5
the sets

\displaystyle B_{\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\{\mu\in R_{\gamma},|\arg\mu|\leq\frac{\pi}{M_{\varphi}L\log k}\},

(4)

\displaystyle U_{\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\{\mu\in R_{\gamma},|\arg\mu|>\frac{\pi}{M_{\varphi}L\log k}\},

(5)

for a constant
M_{\varphi}
to be chosen later, with obviously
R_{\gamma}=B_{\gamma}\cup U_{\gamma}
.
Our analysis will be valid under the assumption that the arcs are not exceptionally long which is characterized by the event

S(M):=\{\max_{i}L_{i}\leq ML\log k\},

(6)

for a constant
M>1
to be chosen later.

Before going further, we need certain technical lemmas establishing that
S(M)
holds with high probability and deriving approximations related to
\mu^{L_{i}}
that are valid in that case.

3.2
High Probability Approximations

We first re-state a lemma from
[
11
]
, characterizing the probability of having no exceptionally long arc length in the reconnected arcs model.

Lemma 8
.

Consider the event
S(M)
defined in (
6
) with
M>1
.
This event
S(M)
occurs asymptotically almost surely, as its probability satisfies

P(S(M))\geq 1-2k^{1-M}.

Proof.

Assume
k\geq 2
. Remember that
(L_{i})
are i.i.d. variables with law

Geo(1/L)
, thus for any of them we have that

P(L_{i}\geq ML\log k)=P(L_{i}\geq\lceil ML\log k\rceil)=\left(1-\frac{1}{L}\right)^{\lceil ML\log k\rceil-1}\leq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{L}\right)^{ML\log k},

based on
\left(1-\frac{1}{L}\right)^{\lceil ML\log k\rceil}\leq\left(1-\frac{1}{L}\right)^{ML\log k}
and

\left(1-\frac{1}{L}\right)^{-1}<2
.
Knowing
(1-1/L)^{L}<1/e
we get

P(L_{i}\geq ML\log k)\leq 2e^{-M\log k}=2k^{-M}.

To treat all
(L_{i})
together for
1\leq i\leq k
, we use a simple union bound.

P(\max_{i}L_{i}\geq ML\log k)\leq 2k^{1-M}.

∎

We now provide some linear approximations of quantities involving
\mu^{L_{i}}
that are valid in the set
R_{\gamma}
(and specifically
B_{\gamma}
), where we aim to establish the absence of eigenvalues.

Lemma 9
.

If
\mu\in R_{\gamma}
, then for
k
large enough under event
S(M)
defined in (
6
) there holds

\left|\mu^{L_{i}}\right|^{2}\geq 1-4M\log^{-(\gamma-1)}k=:1-\epsilon_{k}.

(7)

Proof.

By the definition of
R_{\gamma}
we have

\left|\mu^{L_{i}}\right|^{2}\geq\left|1-\frac{1}{L\log^{\gamma}k}\right|^{2ML\log k}>\exp(-4M\log^{-(\gamma-1)}k)>1-4M\log^{-(\gamma-1)}k.

Here we simply use the linear approximation of the exponential function near 0, in particular
k
has to be large enough for the middle inequality.
∎

Lemma 10
.

If
\mu\in B_{\gamma}
, with
M_{\varphi}\geq 3\pi M
then for
k
large enough under event
S(M)
there holds

\left|(1-\mu^{L_{i}})-L_{i}(1-\mu)\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}L_{i}|1-\mu|.

(8)

Furthermore this leads to

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i}(1-\mu^{L_{i}})\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}|1-\mu|\left(\sum_{i}L_{i}\right),

(9)

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left|1-\mu^{L_{i}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3|1-\mu|^{2}\left(\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\right).

(10)

Proof.

For the main inequality we rearrange the left hand side to write

|(1-\mu^{L_{i}})-L_{i}(1-\mu)|=\left|\frac{1-\mu^{L_{i}}}{1-\mu}-L_{i}\right|\cdot|1-\mu|=\left|\sum_{j=0}^{L_{i}-1}\mu^{j}-L_{i}\right|\cdot|1-\mu|.

(11)

Note that
|\mu^{j}-1|\leq 1-|\mu^{j}|+|\arg\mu^{j}|
by the triangle inequality. Moreover, it follows from the definition of
B_{\gamma}
that

|\arg\mu^{j}|\leq j\left|\arg\mu\right|\leq j\frac{\pi}{M_{\varphi}L\log k}\leq\frac{M\pi}{M_{\varphi}}\leq\frac{1}{3},

(12)

where we have used
M_{\varphi}\geq 3\pi M
and
j\leq L_{i}\leq ML\log k
under
S(M)
.
On the other hand, using Lemma
9

1-|\mu^{j}|\leq 1-{\mu^{L_{i}}}=1-\sqrt{\left|\mu^{L_{i}}\right|^{2}}\leq 1-\sqrt{1-4M\log^{-(\gamma-1)}k}\leq M\log^{-(\gamma-1)}k\leq\frac{1}{6}

for
k
large enough. Combining this with (
12
), we obtain

|\mu^{j}-1|\leq 1-|\mu^{j}|+|\arg\mu^{j}|\leq\frac{1}{2}

for
k
large enough, which together with (
11
) yields the claim (
8
).

Let us turn to the second inequality and introduce
\nu_{i}=(1-\mu^{L_{i}})-L_{i}(1-\mu)
for the approximation error of this linearization, for which we have the bound above. Then we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i}(1-\mu^{L_{i}})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{i}L_{i}(1-\mu)+\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\sum_{i}L_{i}(1-\mu)\right|-\left|\sum_{i}\nu_{i}\right|

\displaystyle\geq|1-\mu|\sum_{i}L_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}L_{i}|1-\mu|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}|1-\mu|\left(\sum_{i}L_{i}\right).

Considering the third inequality we can write

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left|1-\mu^{L_{i}}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left|L_{i}(1-\mu)+\nu_{i}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\left(|L_{i}(1-\mu)|+|\nu_{i}|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\left(|L_{i}(1-\mu)|+\frac{1}{2}L_{i}|(1-\mu)|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 3|1-\mu|^{2}\left(\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\right).

∎

3.3
Approximate Eigenvector Decomposition

The next lemma shows that any hypothetical solution
x
to the modified eigenvalue equation
(
3

\mu\in R_{\gamma}
would be asymptotically
parallel to the vector
1
. To lighten the notation,
\left|\left|.\right|\right|

Lemma 11
.

Let
x
be a non-zero solution of (

) with
\mu\in R_{\gamma}
.
Let
x^{\parallel}=(\frac{\textbf{1}^{T}x}{k})\textbf{1}
and
x^{\perp}=x-x^{\parallel}
. Then under event

there holds

\left|\left|x^{\perp}\right|\right|^{2}\leq\frac{\epsilon_{k}}{2\Delta_{k}-\Delta_{k}^{2}-\epsilon_{k}}\left|\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\right|^{2}=:\delta_{k}\left|\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\right|^{2},

\lim_{k\to\infty}\delta_{k}=0

Observe that the denominator of the right hand part of (

grows. In the meantime the numerator converges to 0, so that
\delta_{k}
converges to 0 a.a.s. (due to the event

) at a rate asymptotically comparable to that of

.

Proof.

Taking the square norm on both sides of (

) leads to

\left|\left|D(\mu)x\right|\right|^{2}=\left|\left|CAx\right|\right|^{2}=\left|\left|Ax\right|\right|^{2}

(14)

where we have use the fact that permuting entries with
C
leaves the norm invariant.
We begin by analyzing the right hand side. Since
A
is doubly stochastic, observe that
Ax^{\parallel}=x^{\parallel}
is orthogonal to

. Hence there holds

\displaystyle\left|\left|Ax\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|Ax^{\parallel}+Ax^{\perp}\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|x^{\parallel}+Ax^{\perp}\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left|\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\right|^{2}+\left|\left|Ax^{\perp}\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\right|^{2}+(1-\Delta_{k})^{2}\left|\left|x^{\perp}\right|\right|.

(15)

We now move to the left-hand part of (

\displaystyle\left|\left|D(\mu)x\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left|\mu^{L_{i}}\right|^{2}|x_{i}^{2}|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i}(1-\epsilon_{k})|x_{i}^{2}|

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon_{k})\left|\left|x\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1-\epsilon_{k})\left(\left|\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\right|^{2}+\left|\left|x^{\perp}\right|\right|^{2}\right).

(16)

Reintroducing (

(1-\epsilon_{k})\left(\left|\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\right|^{2}+\left|\left|x^{\perp}\right|\right|^{2}\right)\leq\left|\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\right|^{2}+(1-\Delta_{k})^{2}\left|\left|x^{\perp}\right|\right|,

and thus

(1-\epsilon_{k}-(1-\Delta_{k})^{2})\left|\left|x^{\perp}\right|\right|^{2}\leq(1-(1-\epsilon_{k}))\left|\left|x^{\parallel}\right|\right|^{2},

which implies (

). The last part of the result follows from the definitions of

and

.
∎

4

Proposition 12

4.1
A Deterministic Criterion

We first show that solutions to (
3
) with
\mu\in B_{\gamma}
can only exist if a certain condition on the first and second empirical moments of
(L_{i})
is satisfied. Note that this condition is deterministic given the values of
(L_{i})
.

Proposition 13
.

Assume the event
S(M)
holds for the collection
(L_{i})
, then the existence of a solution
x,\mu
to (
3
) with
\mu\in B_{\gamma}
implies
the following inequality:

\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}\right)^{2}\leq 12\delta_{k}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\right).

(18)

Proof.

Suppose there exists a solution
x,\mu
to (
3
), with
\mu\in B_{\gamma}
. Pre-multiplying (
3
) by
\textbf{1}^{T}
shows that there would hold

\textbf{1}^{T}D(\mu)x=\textbf{1}^{T}CAx=\textbf{1}^{T}Ax=\textbf{1}^{T}x,

(19)

where we use that both the circulant
C
and the matrix
A
are column-stochastic and thus

\textbf{1}^{T}D(\mu)(x^{\parallel}+x^{\perp})=\textbf{1}^{T}(x^{\parallel}+x^{\perp}).

Remembering
D(\mu)_{ii}=\mu^{L_{i}}
and our scaling assumption
x^{\parallel}=\textbf{1}
, we have then

\sum_{i}\mu^{L_{i}}(1+x^{\perp}_{i})=\sum_{i}(1+x^{\perp}_{i}),

which implies

|\sum_{i}(1-\mu^{L_{i}})|^{2}=|\sum_{i}(1-\mu^{L_{i}})x^{\perp}_{i}|^{2}.

(20)

Now applying Cauchy-Schwartz together with Lemma

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i}(1-\mu^{L_{i}})x^{\perp}_{i}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{i}|1-\mu^{L_{i}}|^{2}\right)\left|\left|x^{\perp}\right|\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{k}k\left(\sum_{i}|1-\mu^{L_{i}}|^{2}\right).

Reintroducing this last inequality in (

) and dividing by

leads to

\left|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}(1-\mu^{L_{i}})\right|^{2}\leq\delta_{k}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}|1-\mu^{L_{i}}|^{2}\right).

Relying on
S(M)
and

4.2
Impossibility for the Reconnected Arcs Model

We now show a concentration result on the moments of
(L_{i})
, which we will exploit to show that condition (

is large.

Lemma 14
.

For the random arc lengths
(L_{i})
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}\leq\frac{L}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4}{k},

(21)

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\geq 4L^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{5}{k}.

(22)

Proof.

Both inequalities follow from simple applications of Chebyshev’s inequality based on the moments of the Geometric distribution.
For the first inequality, consider

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}\leq\frac{L}{2}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}-L\right|\geq\frac{L}{2}\right)\leq\frac{\frac{1}{k}D^{2}(L_{1})}{L^{2}/4}<\frac{4L^{2}}{kL^{2}}=\frac{4}{k}.

For the second one, we have to work with the i.i.d. series of random variables
L_{1}^{2},L_{2}^{2},\ldots,L_{k}^{2}
, for which
D^{2}(L_{1}^{2})=E(L_{1}^{4})-E(L_{1}^{2})^{2}<20L^{4}
.

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\geq 4L^{2}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}-2L^{2}\right|\geq 2L^{2}\right)\leq\frac{\frac{1}{k}D^{2}(L_{1}^{2})}{4L^{4}}<\frac{20L^{4}}{4kL^{4}}=\frac{5}{k}.

∎

This last lemma already allows deriving a first conclusion for the reconnected arcs model.

Proposition 15
.

Assume the conditions of Theorem
5
.

G_{L}(A)
has no eigenvalue in
B_{\gamma}
a.a.s. as
k,L\to\infty
.

Proof.

Assume that neither of the two events of (
21
) or (

) appearing in Lemma

hold, which is true with probability at least
1-9/k=1-o(1)
. Then Proposition
13
implies

\frac{L^{2}}{4}\leq\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}\right)^{2}\leq 12\delta_{k}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\right)\leq 48\delta_{k}L^{2}.

However, we know by Lemma

that

4.3
Impossibility for the Reconnected Cycle Model

We now turn to the reconnected cycle model. Our approach will be to consider it with respect to the
reconnected arcs model, conditioned to the total number of nodes being exactly
n
, which is justified by the following lemma, similar to Proposition 3 in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 16
.

The probability distribution for the eigenvalues of
H_{n}(A)
is exactly the probability distribution for the eigenvalues of
G_{L}(A)
conditioned to
\sum_{i}L_{i}=n
.

The idea of the proof is that the two (conditional) distributions are uniform and lead to transition matrices that have the same structure, but there is an additional technical element related to the extra degree of freedom of rotation in
H_{n}(A)
.

Proof.

Observe that for a given matrix
A
, the reconnected arcs matrix
G_{L}(A)
is entirely determined by the realization of the
(L_{i})
, wile the reconnected cycle matrix
H_{n}(A)
is entirely determined by the realization of the edges to be removed from the cycles and their label, see Definitions
3
and
2
.
We are going to build a bijection between the realizations of these edges to be removed and realizations of
(L_{i})
summing to
n
concatenated with one number between
1
and
n
, playing no role the transition matrix construction for reconnected arcs but that will be useful for our argument:
For given
(L_{i})
summing to
n
and the additional
j=1,\dots,n
meant to represent the position of the first removed edge, we introduce the mapping
T
on
((L_{i}),j)
by setting
[b_{1},e_{1}]=[j,j+L_{1}-1]
, then recursively
[b_{i},e_{i}]=[e_{i-1}+1,e_{i-1}+L_{i}]
, identifying
n+p
to
p
if the indices exceed
n
. This mapping is clearly a bijection, noting that we distinguish the vertices of
A
. Moreover, it preserves the eigenvalues of the matrix (and indeed, the structure of the graph).

Observe now that the reconnected arcs transition matrix obtain from the
(L_{i})
is isomorphic to the reconnected cycle transition matrix obtained from
T((L_{i}),j)
(and vice-versa), and have in particular the same eigenvalues.

To conclude, we also ensure the probability distributions are being matched.
Observe that the probability of a realization of
G_{L}(A)
when
\sum_{i}L_{i}=n
is
L^{-k}(1-\frac{1}{L})^{n-k}
, consequently it is uniform once conditioned on the event
\{\sum_{i}L_{i}=n\}
.
Applying the induced measure transformation
T^{*}
we get the uniform distribution. Therefore, this matches the distribution of
H_{n}(A)
, which is uniform by construction. Consequently, combined with the isomorphism claim above, this shows the distribution of eigenvalues are equal as well.
∎

We will exploit this equivalence to extend Proposition
15
to the reconnected cycle model by showing that the high probability events discussed for the reconnected arcs model keep a high probability when conditioned to the sum of the arc lengths being
n
, for
L=n/k
. For this purpose, we need to establish two claims.

Lemma 17
.

For
k,L=\frac{n}{k}
large enough, for the reconnected arcs model
we have

P\left(\sum_{i}L_{i}\geq n\right)\geq 0.4.

Proof.

We exploit the Berry-Esseen theorem ensuring that the sum of random variables in question are sufficiently close to a Gaussian, therefore being above their expectation has probability around
1/2
, as needed. Let us center the random variables, defining
L_{i}^{\prime}=L_{i}-L
.
Knowing that
L_{i}
are Geometric random variables, we have

\mathbb{E}L_{i}^{\prime}=0,\quad\mathbb{E}L_{i}^{\prime 2}=L^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{L}\right).

We rewrite the complement of the probability to bound as
\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i}L_{i}<kL=n)=\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i}(L_{i}-L)<0)
. To get a handle on it, we apply the Berry-Esseen theorem at position
0
to get

\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{\sum_{i}L_{i}^{\prime}}{\sqrt{k}DL_{i}^{\prime}}<0\right)-\Phi(0)\right|\leq\frac{C\mathbb{E}|L_{i}^{\prime}|^{3}}{\sqrt{k}D^{3}(L_{i}^{\prime})}

for some global positive constant
C
. We now estimate different quantities involved in this bound. In general, for some
X\sim Geo(p)
it is known that

\mathbb{E}X^{3}=\frac{p^{2}-6p+6}{p^{3}}
. In our case, since
L_{i}\sim Geo(1/L)
, we have thus for growing
L

\mathbb{E}|L_{i}^{\prime}|^{3}=\mathbb{E}|L_{i}-L|^{3}\leq\mathbb{E}L_{i}^{3}+3L\mathbb{E}L_{i}^{2}+3L^{2}\mathbb{E}L_{i}+L^{3}=(16+o(1))L^{3}.

At the same time,
D^{2}(L^{\prime 2})=\mathbb{E}L_{i}^{\prime 2}
, so together with
\Phi(0)=1/2
we obtain

\left|\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i}(L_{i}-L)<0\right)-1/2\right|\leq\frac{C(16+o(1))L^{3}}{\sqrt{k}(1+o(1)L^{2})^{3/2}}=\frac{16C(1+o(1))}{\sqrt{k}}.

which gets arbitrarily small when
k
grows, and in particular smaller than 0.1 if
k
is sufficiently large, implying the desired result.
∎

Lemma 18
.

For any
K,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
there holds

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\geq K\mid\sum_{i}L_{i}=m+1\right)\geq\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\geq K\mid\sum_{i}L_{i}=m\right).

Proof.

Recall that all realizations
(L_{1},\dots,L_{k})
for which
\sum_{i}L_{i}=m
(resp.
m+1
) have the same probability, in other words, the conditional distributions are uniform (see the proof of Lemma
16
).

We are going to jointly construct coupled random variables
J,J^{+}
on
\{(j_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \sum j_{i}=m\}
and
\{(j^{+}_{i})\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{k}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \sum j^{+}_{i}=m+1\}
whose marginals are also uniform on their range:
For a pair
((j_{i}),(j^{+}_{i}))
we assign a positive weight if they agree on all but one coordinate, where we have
j_{t}+1=j^{+}_{t}
. In this case the weight given is
j_{t}
. We get a distribution on the pairs of
k
-tuples simply by normalizing these weights, and sample
(J,J^{+})
from this distribution.

We now confirm that the marginals of this distribution are indeed uniform on their range. Concerning
J
, a realization
(j_{i})
will appear in
k
pairs with positive weights, corresponding to the
k
possibilities of increasing a coordinate,
leading to a total weight of

\sum_{i}j_{i}=m.

This is indeed the same on all realizations, consequently the marginal for
J
is uniform.
Concerning
J^{+}
, each
j^{+}
appears in up to
k
pairs, corresponding to all the ways of decreasing a coordinate larger than 1. This has total weight

\sum_{i}(j_{i}^{+}-1)=m+1-k,

where the coordinates
j^{+}_{i}=1
do not need to be explicitly excluded as their contribution to the sum is zero. In the end, we once again get a constant for all realizations of
J^{+}
, confirming its uniform distribution.

By construction we have
J^{+}\geq J
elementwise for any realization. Together with the fact that that the conditional distribution of
(L_{1},\dots,L_{k})
is exactly the distribution of
J
(resp.
J^{+}
) when conditioned on the sum being
m
(resp.
m+1
), this implies

\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\geq K\mid\sum_{i}L_{i}=m+1)=\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i}J_{i}^{+2}\geq K)\geq\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i}J_{i}^{2}\geq K)=\mathbb{P}(\sum_{i}L_{i}^{2}\geq K\mid\sum_{i}L_{i}=m).

∎

Now that we have built the link between the reconnected arcs and reconnected cycle model and have collected some technical bounds related to the conditional probabilities involved, we are ready to prove the eigenvalue impossibility in
B_{\gamma}
for the reconnected cycle model.

5.1
Polynomial Analysis

The following polynomial, which is a scaled version of the trace of

appearing in our modified eigenvalue equation (

P(\mu)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu^{L_{i}}

Analogously to Section

\mu\in U_{\gamma}
.

Lemma 20
.

Under the event
S(M)
, if there exists a nontrivial solution
(x,\mu)
to the modified eigenvalue problem (

\mu\in R_{\gamma}
, then

\left|1-P(\mu)\right|\leq\sqrt{\delta_{k}},

where
\delta_{k}
is defined in Lemma

Proof.

As before, we assume without loss of generality that
x=x^{\parallel}+x^{\perp}
with
x^{\parallel}=\textbf{1}
, so that
\textbf{1}^{\top}x=k
, which is possible because
x^{\parallel}\neq 0
, see Lemma
11
.
Left-multiplying (
3
) by
\textbf{1}^{\top}
leads then to

\textbf{1}^{\top}D(\mu)x=\textbf{1}^{\top}CAx=\textbf{1}^{\top}x=k,

(24)

because
A
is column stochastic and
C
is a permutation matrix.
The left-hand side can be rewritten as

\textbf{1}^{\top}D(\mu)(x^{\parallel}+x^{\perp})=\sum_{i}\mu^{L_{i}}+\sum_{i}\mu^{L_{i}}x^{\perp}_{i}=kP(\mu)+\sum_{i}\mu^{L_{i}}x^{\perp}_{i},

so that (
24
) becomes

k(1-P(\mu))=\sum_{i}\mu^{L_{i}}x^{\perp}_{i}.

(25)

Since
\mu\in R_{\gamma}
implies
\left|\mu\right|\leq 1
, the vector containing all the
\mu^{L_{i}}
has a 2-norm at most
\sqrt{k}
. Therefore, Cauchy-Schwartz implies

\left|\sum_{i}\mu^{L_{i}}x^{\perp}_{i}\right|\leq\sqrt{k}\left|\left|x^{\perp}\right|\right|_{2}.

Moreover, under
S(M)
, and using
x^{\parallel}=\textbf{1}
, Lemma
11
implies
\left|\left|x^{\perp}\right|\right|_{2}\leq\sqrt{k\delta_{k}}
.
Combining this with (
25
) leads then to the desired inequality:

k\left|1-P(\mu)\right|\leq\left|\sum_{i}\mu^{L_{i}}x^{\perp}_{i}\right|\leq k\sqrt{\delta_{n}}.

∎

To obtain a contradiction, we now show that for
\mu\in U_{\gamma}
,
P(\mu)
is a.a.s. sufficiently far from 1, first for the reconnected arcs model. Intuitively, this is because it can take the value 1 only if all the
\mu^{L_{i}}
are aligned, and their angles will almost surely be sufficiently different from each other. This is formalized in the following result, which is parallel to a development in
[
11
]
involving

due to a different parametrization.

Lemma 21
.

Consider the reconnected arcs model, and fix any constants
\eta>3,\theta>1
.
There is a probability at least
1-e^{-\Omega(k/\log^{\theta}k)}
that for any
\mu\in U_{\gamma}

\Re(P(\mu))\leq 1-\frac{1}{\log^{\eta}k}.

The proof uses the following proposition .

Proposition 22

(Proposition 2,
[
11
]
)
.

Consider the reconnected arcs model together with the assumptions of Theorem
5
.
For any constants
\theta,\beta>1
, let

m_{k}=\lceil\log^{\theta}k\rceil
and
\sigma_{k}=\log^{-\beta}k.

Then for

large enough we have

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\mu\in U_{\gamma}}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k}}\cos^{+}(L_{i}\arg(\mu))<m_{k}-\sigma_{k}^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{3},

where
\cos^{+}(y)=\max(\cos(y),0).

Proof of Lemma
21
.

Since
\eta>3
, wet can chose
\theta,\beta\geq 1
such that

\theta+2\beta<\eta
, possibly decreasing the originally given
\theta
leading to a stronger probability bound.
Taking into account

\Re\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k}}(\mu^{L_{i}}-1)\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k}}|\mu|^{L_{i}}\cos^{+}(L_{i}\arg(\mu))-m_{k}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m_{k}}\cos^{+}(L_{i}\arg(\mu))-m_{k},

which is always non-positive, and by Proposition

has a probability at least

of being uniformly bounded from above by

for all
\mu\in U_{\gamma}
.
Observe now that
kP(\mu)-k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\mu^{L_{i}}-1)
.
We cut this sum into blocks of size

m_{k}

For each block, let

7
Conclusions

We have shown that polynomially rare interconnection points with appropriate, possibly sparse, structure added is sufficient to reveal mixing on the order of the diameter (up to logarithmic factors), provided the transition probabilities are adapted in a strongly non-reversible way, generalizing the results in
[
11
]
which only applied to fully connected added interconnections.

There are several natural open questions following this work.
First, the statement of Theorem
6
is independent of the specific properties of the interconnection structure introduced provided its own spectral gap remains bounded away from zero. The impact of the precise structure of this interconnection, which can be observed in Figures
2
and
3
, remains unexplored. Note that an improvement of the polylogarithmic factors involved in the bound would also be welcome.

Second, our result show that the cycle exploited in a fully asymmetric manner by a deterministic walk, when combined with the addition of long-distance edges, leads to a much faster convergence than for the fully symmetric walk on the cycle with the same added edges. However, this choice prevents any local diffusion along the arcs between meeting the additional edges. Hence a natural question would be whether an even better spectral gap could be obtained for similar Markov chains with non-trivial, but still non-reversible transition probability structures for the bulk of the vertices.

A third natural direction is towards the
refined understanding of the mixing behavior, more specifically getting analogous mixing time bounds to the current spectral gap approximations and determining whether cutoff occurs.
We should note that while a deterministic walk could be handled, this required separation of outgoing and incoming edges at a connection point. Therefore it would be interesting to see the variant with a lazy chain along the cycle, but single connection points.

Finally, the long term goal is to carry further and abstract the results for more setups to be able to exploit the combination of minor perturbation of allowed connections and non-reversible modification of a reference Markov chain to reach substantially improved mixing.

Such progress could have a notable impact on multiple processes building upon Markov kernels, such as consensus, push-sum, or decentralized optimization, many algorithms which can be seen as Markov chains constantly perturbed by gradient-type information at each node.

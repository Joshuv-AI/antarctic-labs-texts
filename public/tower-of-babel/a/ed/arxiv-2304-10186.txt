Algebraic Characterization of the Voronoi Cell Structure of the 𝒜 n \mathcal{A}_{n} Lattice
Minho Kim

Abstract
We characterized the combinatorial structure of the Voronoi cell of the 𝒜 n \mathcal{A}_{n} lattice in arbitrary dimensions. Based on the well-known fact that the Voronoi cell is the disjoint union of ( n + 1 ) ! (n+1)! congruent simplices, we show that it is the disjoint union of ( n + 1 ) (n+1) congruent hyper-rhombi, which are the generalized rhombi or trigonal trapezohedra. The explicit structure of the faces is investigated, including the fact that all the k k -dimensional faces, 2 ≤ k ≤ n − 1 2\leq k\leq n-1 , are hyper-rhombi. We show it to be the vertex-first orthogonal projection of the ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -dimensional unit cube. Hence the Voronoi cell is a zonotope. We prove that in low dimensions ( n ≤ 3 n\leq 3 ) the Voronoi cell can be understood as the section of that of the 𝒟 n + 1 \mathcal{D}_{n+1} lattice with the hyperplane orthogonal to the diagonal direction. We provide all the explicit coordinates and transformation matrices associated with our analysis. Most of our analysis is algebraic and easily accessible to those less familiar with the Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams.

Introduction

Due to their
higher symmetry compared to other lattices,
the root lattices have been
investigated in various fields
including
crystallography,
signal sampling,
data discretization, etc.
In many applications, the Voronoi cells
of root lattices play a crucial
role.

Conway and Sloane 1982

derived the Voronoi cells of root lattices
(and their dual lattices) based on the Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams.
Specifically, they showed that the Voronoi cell

V\left(\boldsymbol{0}\right)

for a root lattice
\mathcal{L}
is the
union of the images of its fundamental simplex
under its Weyl group
W_{\!a}\!\!\left(\mathcal{L}\right)
.

Moody and Patera 1992

determined the number of facets of all dimensions of
the Voronoi cells of the root lattices based on the
the decorated Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams.

Koca et al. 2018

analyzed the structure of the
(n-1)
-dimensional faces of the
Voronoi cells and related polytopes of the
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{D}_{n}

lattices and their duals.
They showed that the
(n-1)
-dimensional faces of the Voronoi cells of the
\mathcal{A}_{n}

lattice are the generalized rhombohedra, which we call
hyper-rhombi
in this work.

9
Conclusion

We characterized the combinatorial structure of the Voronoi cell
of the
\mathcal{A}_{n}
lattice.
Based on the well-known fact that it is composed of
(n+1)!
congruent
simplices, we showed that it is composed of
(n+1)
congruent
hyper-rhombi.
We analyzed the explicit structure of the hyper-rhombic cells
of the Voronoi cell, including the fact that all the
k
-dimensional faces,

2\leq k\leq n-2
, are hyper-rhombic.
We showed that it is the vertex-first projection
of the
(n+1)
-dimensional unit cube and finally
verified that in low dimensions,
n\leq 3
, it is the section
of the Voronoi cell of the
\mathcal{D}_{n+1}
lattice with the diagonal
hyperplane.
All the analyses are done algebraically without any Coxeter-Dynkin diagrams
so they are more accessible to those who are not familiar with them.

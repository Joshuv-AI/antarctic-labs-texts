Waveshape of THz radiation produced by two-color laser-induced air plasmas
A. Stathopulos, S. Skupin, B. Zhou, P. U. Jepsen, L. Bergé

Abstract
The spatial and spectral distributions of terahertz (THz) pulses emitted by two-color air plasmas are theoretically investigated for focused laser pulses and in the filamentation regime. We derive a so-called ”augmented” conical emission model, which, similarly to the one originally proposed by You et al. [Phys. Rev. Lett. 109 , 183902 (2012)], involves phase matching between laser harmonics along the plasma channel, the plasma density and length, and the emitted frequency as key parameters. Our augmented model, however, treats envelope effects and accounts for transverse variations of both plasma- and Kerr-driven potential THz emitters. We highlight the importance of the characteristic spatio-spectral distributions of these two conversion mechanisms in the expression of the angular radiated power. The results of our model are successfully compared with data provided by a comprehensive, fully space and time-resolved unidirectional solver. Importantly, these numerical simulations clear up the effective plasma length along which THz emission develops, compared with the dephasing length along which the laser fundamental and second harmonic become out-of-phase. The impact of common optical aberrations, such as sphericity, astigmatism, and coma, on the THz generation is also investigated. Aberrations are shown to generally decrease the laser-to-THz conversion efficiency and potentially induce spatial asymmetries and narrowing in the THz spectra.

II
Physical mechanism for THz generation and laser propagation model

We study the THz emission by two-color femtosecond (fs) light pulses that trigger air plasmas. The laser field can be either linearly polarized along the
x

(1)

\displaystyle\vec{E}_{\rm CP-S}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\binom{\mathcal{E}_{1}\cos(\omega_{0}t)+\mathcal{E}_{2}\cos(2\omega_{0}t+\phi)}{\mathcal{E}_{1}\sin(\omega_{0}t)+\mathcal{E}_{2}\sin(2\omega_{0}t+\phi)}

(2)

where

\mathcal{E}_{1,2}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp},t)=\sqrt{r_{1,2}}E_{0}\exp\!\left[-\frac{t^{2}}{t_{1,2}^{2}}-\frac{r_{\perp}^{2}}{w_{1,2}^{2}}\right].

(3)

Here

their corresponding wavenumbers. The peak intensity ratio of fundamental over second harmonic is determined by

. A focusing lens with focal length

(4)

Later in Sect.

III
THz emission model

Analytical results from the LC model provide the dependence on the phase angle
\phi
of the THz amplitudes generated by photocurrents and Kerr response
[
56
]
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}_{\rm THz,LP-P}^{\rm J}

\displaystyle\propto\sin\phi\ \mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}_{\rm THz,LP-P}^{\rm K}

\displaystyle\propto\cos\phi\ \mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}},

(14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}_{\rm THz,CP-S}^{\rm J}

\displaystyle\propto\binom{\sin\phi}{\cos\phi},

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}_{\rm THz,CP-S}^{\rm K}

\displaystyle\propto\binom{\cos\phi}{-\sin\phi}.

(15)

Here,
E_{\rm THz}^{\rm J}
and
E_{\rm THz}^{\rm K}
are the THz electric fields produced by the photocurrents (index J) and Kerr response (resp. K) of LP-P and CP-S pump pulses, respectively. In practice, plasma channels extend over millimeter to centimeter lengths in the focused regime and up to several meters in the (collimated or loosely focused) filamentation regime. Over sufficiently long propagation ranges, the optical and plasma dispersion induce an important variation of the phase
\phi
along the plasma channel, i.e., for an axially homogeneous plasma:

\phi(z)=\phi_{0}+\pi\frac{z}{l_{d}},

(16)

where
\phi_{0}
is the phase angle taken at the plasma onset distance. The ”dephasing length”
l_{d}
represents the propagation distance over which
\phi
changes by
\pi
due to dispersion, that is,

l_{d}=\frac{\lambda_{0}}{4\left[n(2\omega_{0})-n(\omega_{0})\right]}

(17)

with
\lambda_{0}=2\pi c/\omega_{0}
. Here,
n(\omega)
is the medium refractive index that accounts for the contributions of Kerr and plasma

n^{2}(\omega)=n^{2}_{\rm opt}(\omega)+2n_{0}n_{2}I_{0}-\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{\omega^{2}(1+i\nu_{c}/\omega)},

(18)

where
I_{0}=c\varepsilon_{0}n_{0}E_{0}^{2}/2
is the incident pulse intensity and
\omega_{p}=\sqrt{e^{2}N_{e}/\varepsilon_{0}m_{e}}
is the electron plasma frequency. Here, we recall that
l_{d}
depends on
N_{e}
through the plasma frequency in Eq. (
18
). Note the difference by a factor of 2 with You et al.’s definition of the dephasing length, which, we believe, proceeds from a misprint in the original Ref.
[
47
]
.

III.1
You et al.’s conical emission model

Before dwelling upon our revised model, we find it instructive to recall the main ingredients of You et al.’s
[
47
,
48
]
theory that describes the THz intensity radiated in the far-field by a homogeneous (
N_{e}=
const.) cylindrical plasma of length
L
and radius
a
. Here, the only mechanism generating THz waves is the photocurrent density excited by an LP-P laser pulse, and thus, according to Eq. (
14
), the local THz yield evolves as
\sin{\phi(z)}
. Derived from the radiated electric field in the far-field approximation, You et al. obtained the angular THz intensity spectrum (see Appendix for a detailed derivation):

|\widehat{E}(\omega_{\rm THz},\Theta)|^{2}\propto|A(\omega_{\rm THz})|^{2}\left(\frac{J_{1}(\beta)}{\beta}\right)^{2}\\
\times L^{2}\left[\kappa_{+}^{2}+\kappa_{-}^{2}-2\kappa_{+}\kappa_{-}\cos(2\phi_{0})\right],

(19)

where
A(\omega)
is the spectral amplitude of the THz source, and
J_{1}
is the Bessel function of first order. The quantities
\kappa_{\pm}
and
\beta
are given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sinc}\ \alpha_{\pm}

(20)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi L}{\lambda_{\rm THz}}\left(1-\cos\Theta\pm\frac{\lambda_{\rm THz}}{2l_{d}}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{\rm THz}}a\sin\Theta,

(22)

with
\mathrm{sinc}(x)=\sin x/x
and
\lambda_{\rm THz}
denotes the wavelength associated with the THz frequency
\omega_{\rm THz}
. The functions
\kappa_{\pm}
, linked to the phase matching condition for THz generation, depend on the extent
(L)
of the axial filament. The quantity
\beta
accounts for the radial dimensions of the plasma (
\beta\propto a
) entering the term
J_{1}^{2}(\beta)/\beta^{2}
that describes the circular diffraction of the THz waves emitted by the plasma cylinder. For common filaments, the radius
a
is limited to a few tens of
\mu
m and
\Theta<10
°, i.e.,
\beta\ll 1
in the main part of the spectrum, so this diffraction term has limited influence. The maximum emission is reached for
\alpha_{-}=0
, thus providing the theoretical emission angle
\Theta_{p}
:

\cos\Theta_{p}=1-\frac{\lambda_{\rm THz}}{2l_{d}}.

(23)

The phase mismatch between the THz waves emitted at different places in the plasma filament causes destructive interference for the on-axis THz component for plasmas of length
L>l_{d}
, and the THz radiation propagates as a cone in space, far from the plasma volume. The emission angles are usually narrow, i.e.,
\Theta
is in the 2° – 10° range
[

,
72
,
51
,
73
]
.

As summarized in Fig.
1
, the angular THz spectrum described by this model depends mainly on three parameters. The first is the length of the plasma

), the on-axis THz amplitude vanishes and the THz beam becomes a cone with angle

In addition to the above well-known properties, additional information can be extracted from the function describing the phase-matching condition in Eq. (
19
), namely,
P(L,\Theta,\lambda_{\rm THz})\equiv L^{2}[\kappa_{+}^{2}+\kappa_{-}^{2}-2\kappa_{+}\kappa_{-}\cos(2\phi_{0})]
. First, on-axis components associated with the interference function
P(L,\Theta=0,\lambda_{\rm THz})=(2l_{d}/\pi)^{2}[1-\cos{(\pi L/l_{d})}][1-\cos{(2\phi_{0})]}
develop as a

-periodic function of the plasma length

\phi_{0}
differs from 0 [
\pi
]. Second, phase-matched off-axis components parabolically increase like

and increase monotonously to higher amplitude levels for longer plasma lengths. The value of
\phi_{0}
does not change this behavior and simply introduces secondary oscillations. Importantly, these properties result from interference in the far-field THz intensity spectrum.

In the following, we revisit this THz emission model by making it as general as possible. This includes (i) treating envelope effects, (ii) allowing for transverse variations in the plasma density, (iii) accounting for the characteristic spectral signatures of
A(\omega)
depending on the nonlinear propagation regime at play, and (iv) passing from LP-P to CP-S driver pulses.

\Phi
), respectively. We are interested in the far-field distribution, so the distance between the plasma and the detector is large compared to the dimensions of the filament, that is,
|\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}|\gg|\mathrm{\mathbf{r^{\prime}}}|
, which leads to the simplification of the Fourier-transformed field (see Appendix
A
):

\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}}(r,\Theta,\Phi,\omega)=\frac{i\omega\mu_{0}}{4\pi r}\mbox{e}^{i\frac{\omega}{c}r}\int_{-L/2}^{+L/2}dz^{\prime}\mbox{e}^{-i\frac{\omega}{c}\cos\Theta z^{\prime}}\\
\times\iint d^{2}\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}_{\perp}\widehat{{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime},\omega)\mbox{e}^{-i{\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}}_{\perp}\cdot{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}_{\perp}},

(25)

with
{\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}}_{\perp}=(\omega/c)(\sin\Theta\cos\Phi,\sin\Theta\sin\Phi)
. The double integral formally applies to the entire transverse plane.
\widehat{{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime},\omega)
is zero for
|\mathrm{\mathbf{r^{\prime}}}|\rightarrow\infty
, in particular when considering a finite plasma cylinder of radius
a
.

The second step consists in taking the Kerr polarization [Eq. (
7
)] into account through the substitution

\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},t)\rightarrow\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{e}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},t)+\partial_{t}\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}_{K}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},t),

(26)

and calculating the free-electron contribution
\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{e}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},t)
using a toy ionization rate satisfying
W(E)\propto E^{2}(t)
. This toy ionization rate was already employed in Refs.
[
75
,
76
]
and allows a semi-analytical treatment that provides the major characteristics of multi-color-driven THz pulses, including their polarization state. Most importantly, it captures the asymmetries in the temporal location of the ionization peaks introduced by the second pump color, which are crucial for the photocurrent mechanism. In this respect, approaches based on Fourier series using more realistic, highly nonlinear ionization rates but involving only a single pump color
[
77
,
78
]
cannot account for these asymmetries.

The third step is to consider a pump field that admits transverse envelopes and different delays for the two pump colors. Introducing the retarded times
t_{j=\{1,2\}}^{\prime}=t-z/v_{pj}
, with
v_{pj}=c/n(j\omega_{0})
being the respective phase velocities, our LP-P two-color laser field takes the general form

{\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}}_{\rm L}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t)={\mathrm{\mathbf{\cal{E}}}}_{1}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t_{1}^{\prime})\cos{[\omega_{0}t_{1}^{\prime}+\phi_{1}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})]}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}\\
+{\mathrm{\mathbf{\cal{E}}}}_{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t_{2}^{\prime})\cos{[2\omega_{0}t_{2}^{\prime}+\phi_{2}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})]}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}},

(27)

where
\phi_{j}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})
is the spatial phase of the
j
th color. We assume that the envelopes
\mathcal{E}_{j}
and their phases
\phi_{j}
vary little along the filament, that is, they do not explicitly depend on
z
, and we can write
\mathcal{E}_{j}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t_{1}^{\prime})\approx\mathcal{E}_{j}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{1}^{\prime})
and
\phi_{j}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}})\approx\phi_{j}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
. We note that this is a strong assumption, in particular for the phases, but is necessary for the upcoming semianalytical treatment. In contrast, our UPPE simulation results comprehensively account for the complete spatiotemporal dynamics of the pump field. Furthermore, we assume that temporal and radial variations of the laser envelopes are separable as

\mathcal{E}_{j}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{j}^{\prime})=\mathcal{E}_{tr,j}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp})\mathcal{E}_{ax,j}(t_{j}^{\prime}),

(28)

where the maximum of
\mathcal{E}_{tr,j=1,2}
is normalized to unity and
\mathcal{E}_{ax,j}
contains the electric field amplitude expressed in V/m.

Straightforward computations detailed in Appendix
A
then enable us to establish the following generic dependencies

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad(\partial_{t}+\nu_{c})\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{e}^{\rm THz}\\
&\propto\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{1}^{\prime})\mathcal{E}_{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{2}^{\prime})\sin{\left[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\right]}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}},\end{split}

(29)

\displaystyle{\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}}_{K}^{\rm THz}

\displaystyle\propto\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{1}^{\prime})\mathcal{E}_{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{2}^{\prime})\cos{\left[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\right]}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}},

(30)

where
\Delta k
is the phase mismatch in wavenumber between the harmonics and
\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
their phase angle:

\displaystyle\Delta k=\frac{2\omega_{0}}{c}\left[n(\omega_{0})-n(2\omega_{0})\right]=\pi/l_{d},

(31)

\displaystyle\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\equiv\phi_{2}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})-2\phi_{1}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}).

(32)

Further computations lead to

\left|\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},\omega)\right|^{2}=\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}r^{2}}\left|\mathrm{\mathbf{C}}_{J}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)+\mathrm{\mathbf{C}}_{K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)\right|^{2},

(33)

where the functions
\mathrm{\mathbf{C}}_{J}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)
and
\mathrm{\mathbf{C}}_{K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)
represent the contributions to the THz yield from the photocurrents (index
J
) and the Kerr effect (index
K
), respectively. These general functions are expressed as

\mathrm{\mathbf{C}}_{J,K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)=A_{J,K}(\omega)\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J,K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega),

(34)

A_{J,K}(\omega)
containing the frequency dependency of the considered source term and
\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J,K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)
being the associated angle-dependent longitudinal phase matching function, namely,

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J}^{\rm LP-P}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{2}\left[I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}-I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}\right]\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}},

(35)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{K}^{\rm LP-P}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=i\frac{L}{2}\left[I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}+I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}\right]\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}.

(36)

Here,
\kappa_{\pm}
are those given in Eq. (
20
).

The quantities
I_{S}^{\pm}
describe the transverse effects of the plasma on the far-field THz radiation,

I_{S}^{\pm}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)=\mathcal{F}_{\perp}\left[J_{\rm tr}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})e^{\pm i\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})}\right](\Theta,\Phi,\omega),

(37)

where notation
\mathcal{F}_{\perp}
refers to the Fourier transform with respect to the transverse variables
\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime}
and

J_{\rm tr}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r_{\perp}}})=\mathcal{E}_{\rm tr,1}^{2}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\mathcal{E}_{\rm tr,2}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})

(38)

represents the transverse envelope of the source terms.
I_{S}
reduces to the Bessel-like circular diffraction function recalled in Eq. (
19
) for a cylindrical homogeneous plasma of radius
a
(see Appendix
B
). We also mention that CP-S pulses can be treated similarly, cf. Appendix
A
, and one finds

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J}^{\rm CP-S}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{2}\binom{I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}-I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}}{I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}+I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}},

(39)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{K}^{\rm CP-S}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=i\frac{L}{2}\binom{I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}+I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}}{-I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}+I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}}.

(40)

By construction, our model does not account for variations in the plasma density along
z
, and plasma absorption and opacity are also neglected for reasons addressed in the appendix.

Integration of our model requires some prior knowledge of key parameters of the emitting filament. First, we need to model the transverse shape of the THz source
J_{\rm tr}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})
to calculate
I_{S}^{\pm}
. To this end, we use our linear laser propagation code up to a point
z=z_{p}
where the width of the laser beam is small enough to reach high intensities corresponding to efficient plasma generation. At this distance, assuming that the envelopes of the two colors remain superimposed in time, we can extract the quantities
\phi({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp})
and the transverse field profiles, allowing us to reconstruct
J_{\rm tr}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r_{\perp}}})
at this distance and thus evaluate
I_{S}^{\pm}
. Second, as addressed in the appendix for illustrative purposes, Gaussian temporal envelopes impose a radiated Gaussian spectrum. This is unrealistic because the temporal pump pulse profiles strongly evolve along propagation due to the Kerr and plasma response and typical THz spectra obtained from experiments or simulations look very different. We therefore replace the profiles
A_{J,K}(\omega=2\pi\nu)
in Eq. (
34
) by typical spectral shapes delivered by the respective source term in the specific propagation regime under consideration. In what follows, we use

\displaystyle A_{J}(\nu)

\displaystyle\propto\left(1-\mbox{e}^{-\nu/f_{\rm max}}\right)\mbox{e}^{-(\nu/f_{0})^{2}}

(41)

\displaystyle A_{K}(\nu)

\displaystyle\propto\left(1-\mbox{e}^{-\nu/f_{\rm max}}\right)\mbox{e}^{-(\nu/f_{0})^{4}}

(42)

yielding the synthetic profiles shown in Fig.
2
for typical THz spectra produced by photocurrents alone (blue curve), or Kerr effect discarding SRRS (red curve) in either short-range focused (solid) and long-range filamentation (dashed curves) regimes. They have been inspired by Figs. 3(c,d) of Ref.

. Three typical interaction regimes will be explored in the following three subsections: purely plasma-driven emission in focused geometry, plasma regime interplaying with a strong Kerr response, and long-range filamentation regime.

IV
Influence of optical aberrations

This section aims to characterize the impact of common optical aberrations (OAs) on the nonlinear propagation of two-color pulses producing THz radiation. OAs modify the propagation of a laser beam through distortion of its transverse phase, thus of its optical wavefront
[
81
]
. They tend to alter the position and shape of the focal spot and, thereby, the geometry of the plasma channel. OAs are usually classified using two integers: the radial degree
n
and the azimuthal degree
m\leq n
entering the Zernike polynomial
Z_{n}^{m}(\rho,\Phi)

[
54
]
:

Z_{n}^{m}(\rho,\Phi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}R_{n}^{m}(\rho)\cos(m\Phi)&\mathrm{if\ }m\geq 0\\
-R_{n}^{-m}(\rho)\sin(m\Phi)&\mathrm{if\ }m<0\end{array}\right.,

(43)

where
\rho\equiv\sqrt{2}r_{\perp}/w_{0}
with
r_{\perp}=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}
and
w_{0}
being the beam width.

R_{n}^{m}(\rho)=\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{n-m}{2}}\frac{(-1)^{k}(n-k)!}{k!\left(\frac{n+m}{2}-k\right)!\left(\frac{n-m}{2}-k\right)!}\rho^{n-2k}

(44)

is the radial distribution for an even number of
n-m
, and
R_{n}^{m}(\rho)\equiv 0
otherwise.

Below we shall concentrate on the basic configuration of focus (
n=2
,
m=0
) - consisting of a simple focusing lens effect - and will add to this elementary lensing three characteristic OAs of interest, namely, vertical astigmatism (
n=m=2
), vertical coma aberration (
n=3
,
m=-1
) and sphericity (
n=4
,
n=0
). For these four configurations, the polynomials
Z_{n}^{m}(\rho,\Phi)
express as:

Focus

\displaystyle Z_{2}^{0}(\rho)=2\rho^{2}-1,

(45)

Astigmatism

\displaystyle Z_{2}^{2}(\rho,\Phi)=\rho^{2}\cos(2\Phi),

(46)

Coma

\displaystyle Z_{3}^{-1}(\rho,\Phi)=(3\rho^{3}-2\rho)\sin(\Phi),

(47)

Sphericity

\displaystyle Z_{4}^{0}(\rho)=6\rho^{4}-6\rho^{2}+1.

(48)

Each OA is associated with an amplitude coefficient,
c_{n}^{m}
, which has the dimension of a distance and determines the impact of the respective OA on the transverse phase of the beam. The total phase distortion is then conveniently expressed as

-\frac{\omega}{c}Z_{\rm OA}(r_{\perp},\Phi)=-\frac{\omega}{c}\sum_{n,m\leq n}c_{n}^{m}Z_{n}^{m}\left(\frac{\sqrt{2}r_{\perp}}{w_{0}},\Phi\right).

(49)

The polynomial
Z_{\rm OA}(r_{\perp},\Phi)
henceforth substitutes the transverse spatial dependency of the phase term
r_{\perp}^{2}/2f
in Eq. (
4
). The sum symbol means that when considering OAs such as sphericity, astigmatism, or coma, we add the latter to the baseline focus configuration.

V
Conclusion

In this paper, we have derived a so-called ”augmented” conical emission model, able to satisfactorily reproduce angular THz spectra provided by 3D UPPE simulations, both in focused propagation and filamentation regimes. This model is called ”augmented” because it still relies on the key parameters found by You et al.
[
47
]
. Yet, it includes complementary information on three other crucial quantities, namely, (i) the spectral distribution of the THz source in the nonlinear propagation regime of interest, (ii) the transverse profile and phase of the beam when it undergoes lensing effect or more complex phase distortions such as, e.g., those induced by deformable mirrors, and (iii) Kerr contribution to the overall THz radiation when self-focusing may become relevant, e.g., in the long-range filamentation regime. Our augmented model also addressed the role of pulse envelopes, which has been shown to be relatively limited, and it could be generalized to circularly polarized two-color pulses.

We carefully and systematically compared the results obtained from our augmented model with comprehensive UPPE simulation data. From this, we find an alternative to running heavy simulations before evaluating the angular spectra of the THz to interpret current experiments. Indeed, it appears sufficient to model a class of spectral profiles appropriately with respect to the targetted nonlinear propagation regime (tightly or loosely focused geometry) and appreciate the weight of the Kerr contribution to get reliable angular radiation patterns. Alternatively, LC-computed spectra could have been used, or, if available, the 3D UPPE spectral profiles could directly be implemented in the spectral amplitude
A(\omega)
. However, we have shown that simple test functions for the spectral distributions are sufficient to obtain valuable radiated intensities.

Importantly, instead of selecting one single radiated spectrum at a given propagation distance, we probed, for the first time to our knowledge, the whole spectral dynamics by examining changes in the radiated patterns for various plasma lengths around the dephasing length
l_{d}
. It appears that a good knowledge of the spectral dynamics along the propagation range covering the THz emitting zone is much more instructive than only analyzing the spectrum at the end of the plasma channel.

In connection with this property, our 3D UPPE simulations displayed evidence that the choice of the actual plasma length
L
must be given by the length over which THz field emission takes place. This length is generally much shorter than the overall plasma extent along the optical axis. By computing the evolution of the relative phase between the two laser harmonics numerically along this effective plasma length (Fig.
3
), we have proven that in focused propagation geometry
L
remains close to
l_{d}
, which justifies why most of the radiated spectra measured in such emission regimes
[
46
,
52
,
85
]
contain both on-axis emissions at small THz frequencies and net conical emission at higher frequencies. In contrast, a collimated or loosely focused propagation promotes plasma lengths
L\gg l_{d}
and leads to conical emission in the entire THz frequency range.

In conclusion, we think that the present study provides the terahertz community with a deeper understanding of the THz spectral dynamics and additional tools to predict angular THz radiation patterns delivered by two-color air plasmas in future experiments.

Appendix A
THz emission model

Here, we establish a general conical emission model that includes laser envelope profiles in time and in the transverse plane. Let
t
be the time variable and
\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}
be the position of the THz wave detector. The frame’s origin is located at the center of the plasma with length
L
assumed to be finite. In what follows,
\Theta
corresponds to the polar angle at which the radiation is emitted with respect to the optical axis;
\Phi
is the azimuthal angle.

Helmholtz’s theorem gives the vector potential
\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},t)
emitted by a plasma source of volume
V
with current density
\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},t)
as

{\mathrm{\mathbf{A}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t)=\frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{V}d^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}\frac{{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}{|{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}-{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}|},

(50)

where
t^{\prime}=t-|{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}-{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}|/c
corresponds to the delayed time at which the detector receives the signal generated in
t
. The associated electric field
\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},t)
, in the absence of a scalar potential, follows from

{\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t)=-\frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{V}d^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}\frac{\partial_{t}{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}{|{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}-{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}|},

(51)

and the magnetic field from

{\mathrm{\mathbf{B}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t)=\frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{V}d^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}\frac{\nabla\times{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime},t^{\prime})}{|{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}-{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}|}.

(52)

For the photocurrent mechanism considered here, it is safe to assume that the relevant current density is transverse and that no charge separation occurs (
\nabla\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}=0
), which justifies the use of Eq. (
51
) to compute the radiated far-field power. We note that for the transition Cherenkov (TC) mechanism
[

\nabla\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}\neq 0
produces a charge density, one would need to account for a scalar potential in Eq. (
51
) or resort to Eq. (
52
).

The temporal Fourier transform of Eq. (
51
) leads to

\widehat{{\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},\omega)=\frac{i\omega\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\int_{V}d^{3}{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}\frac{\mbox{e}^{i|{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}-{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}|\omega/c}}{|{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}-{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime}|}\widehat{{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}^{\prime},\omega)\,.

(53)

We assume that the distance between the plasma and the detector is large compared to the filament dimensions, that is,
|\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}|\gg|\mathrm{\mathbf{r^{\prime}}}|
. Cartesian, cylindrical and spherical coordinates are written (
x
,

,

), (

\varphi
,

) and (

\Theta
,

). The notation

(54)

with

To evaluate the source terms
\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{e}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},t)
and
\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}_{K}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},t)

is calculated using the toy ionization rate and assuming a small ionization yield,

W(E)=CE^{2}(t),\quad N_{e}(t)=CN_{a}\int_{-\infty}^{t}E^{2}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}.

(55)

The square of the LP-P two-color laser field Eq. (

E_{L}^{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t)=\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}\cos^{2}{[\omega_{0}t^{\prime}_{1}+\phi_{1}}]+\mathcal{E}_{2}^{2}\cos^{2}{[2\omega_{0}t^{\prime}_{2}+\phi_{2}]}\\
+2\mathcal{E}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{2}\cos{[2\omega_{0}t^{\prime}_{2}+\phi_{2}]}\cos{[\omega_{0}t^{\prime}_{1}+\phi_{1}]},

(56)

where we omitted the arguments of
\mathcal{E}_{1}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp},t^{\prime}_{1})
,
\mathcal{E}_{2}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp},t^{\prime}_{2})
,
\phi_{1}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp})
, and
\phi_{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp})

For the plasma term, straightforward integration of Eq. (

\frac{CN_{a}}{4\omega_{0}}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}\sin[2\omega_{0}t^{\prime}_{1}+2\phi_{1}]\mathcal{E}_{2}\cos[2\omega_{0}t^{\prime}_{2}+\phi_{2}]\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}

and

\frac{CN_{a}}{\omega_{0}}\mathcal{E}_{1}\mathcal{E}_{2}\sin[\omega_{0}(2t^{\prime}_{2}-t^{\prime}_{1})+\phi_{2}-\phi_{1}]\mathcal{E}_{1}\cos[\omega_{0}t^{\prime}_{1}+\phi_{1}]\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}.

The treatment of the Kerr term proceeds similarly from Eq. (
7
) without integration in time and, therefore, only contains cosine contributions. After retaining only the quasi-stationary terms, the two potential THz sources can then be expressed as

(\partial_{t}+\nu_{c})\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{e,{\rm THz}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t)={J}_{e,0}\ \mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{1}^{\prime})\mathcal{E}_{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{2}^{\prime})\\
\times\sin{\left[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\right]}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}

(57)

using
{J}_{e,0}=3CN_{a}e^{2}/(8m_{e}\omega_{0})
, and

{\mathrm{\mathbf{P}}}_{K,{\rm THz}}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}},t)=P_{K,0}\ \mathcal{E}_{1}^{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{1}^{\prime})\mathcal{E}_{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp},t_{2}^{\prime})\\
\times\cos{\left[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\right]}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}

(58)

with
P_{K,0}=3\varepsilon_{0}\chi^{(3)}/4
,
\Delta k=\pi/l_{d}
, and
l_{d}
denoting the dephasing length given in Eq. (
17
). With the assumption of separable laser envelopes in space and time [Eq. (
28
)] we can simplify the relations (
57
) and (
58
), and the unified source term
\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\omega)
, cf. Eq. (
26
), reads:

\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\omega)=\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{0}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\omega)J_{\rm tr}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})\mathcal{F}_{t}\!\left[{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t_{1}^{\prime}){\cal{E}}_{ax,2}(t_{2}^{\prime})\right]\!(\omega),

(59)

where

J_{\rm tr}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})=\mathcal{E}_{\rm tr,1}^{2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp})\mathcal{E}_{\rm tr,2}({\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp})

[see Eq. (
38
)], and

\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{0}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp},z,\omega)=\frac{J_{e,0}}{\nu_{c}-i\omega}\sin{(\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}))}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}\\
-i\omega P_{K,0}\cos{(\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}))}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}.

(60)

\mathcal{F}_{t}
refers to Fourier transform in time, and using
t_{j=\{1,2\}}^{\prime}=t-zn(j\omega_{0})/c
we can rewrite

\displaystyle\quad{\cal F}_{t}\!\left[{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t_{1}^{\prime}){\cal{E}}_{ax,2}(t_{2}^{\prime})\right]\!(\omega)=\int{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t_{1}^{\prime}){\cal{E}}_{ax,2}(t_{2}^{\prime})e^{i\omega t}dt

\displaystyle=e^{ikn(\omega_{0})z}\int{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t_{1}^{\prime}){\cal{E}}_{ax,2}\!\left(t_{1}^{\prime}-\frac{\pi z}{2\omega_{0}l_{d}}\right)e^{i\omega t_{1}^{\prime}}dt_{1}^{\prime},

with
k=\omega/c
being the THz wavenumber in vacuum. We can use the convolution theorem to rewrite the integral as

\displaystyle\quad\int{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t){\cal{E}}_{ax,2}(t-\gamma)e^{i\omega t}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iiint{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t)e^{i\omega^{\prime}t}{\cal{E}}_{ax,2}(t^{\prime}-\gamma)e^{i(\omega^{\prime}-\omega)t^{\prime}}dtdt^{\prime}d\omega^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\iiint{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t)e^{i\omega^{\prime}t}{\cal{E}}_{ax,2}(\tilde{t})e^{i(\omega^{\prime}-\omega)(\tilde{t}+\gamma)}dtd\tilde{t}d\omega^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-i\omega\gamma}}{2\pi}\int{\cal F}_{t}\!\left[{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t)\right]\!(\omega^{\prime}){\cal F}_{t}\!\left[{\cal{E}}_{ax,2}({t})\right]\!(\omega^{\prime}-\omega)e^{i\omega^{\prime}\gamma}d\omega^{\prime}

where
\gamma=\pi z/(2\omega_{0}l_{d})
. Provided that the spectra of the temporal envelopes are sufficiently narrow (not too short pulses), we can conclude that the integrand is nonzero for
\omega^{\prime}\ll\omega_{0}
only. Then, for plasma lengths of at most a few
l_{d}
we can neglect the
z
-dependent term
\exp(i\omega^{\prime}\gamma)
under the integral. Because we are interested in THz frequencies only (
\omega\ll\omega_{0}
), with the same argument we can neglect the factor
\exp(-i\omega\gamma)
in front of the integral. Thus, we have established that

\quad{\cal F}_{t}\!\left[{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t_{1}^{\prime}){\cal{E}}_{ax,2}(t_{2}^{\prime})\right]\!(\omega)\approx e^{ikn(\omega_{0})z}C_{\rm env}(\omega)

(61)

with

C_{\rm env}(\omega)=\int{\cal{E}}_{ax,1}^{2}(t_{1}^{\prime}){\cal{E}}_{ax,2}(t_{1}^{\prime})e^{i\omega t_{1}^{\prime}}dt_{1}^{\prime}.

(62)

While this expression is valid for arbitrary (slowly varying) pulse envelopes, it is instructive to inspect it for Gaussian envelopes:

{\cal{E}}_{ax,j}(t)\propto\mbox{e}^{-(t-t_{c}^{j})^{2}/t_{j}^{2}}

(63)

with
t_{j}
being the duration of the
j^{th}
color envelope and
t_{c}^{j}
its temporal offset. After straightforward computations, we obtain a Gaussian power spectrum

C_{env}(\omega)\propto\frac{t_{1}t_{2}\sqrt{\pi}}{t_{eq}}e^{-\frac{2\Delta t^{2}}{t_{eq}^{2}}}e^{i\omega(t_{c}^{1}-\Delta t\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{t_{eq}^{2}})}e^{-\frac{t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}}{4t_{eq}^{2}}\omega^{2}},

(64)

where
t_{eq}=\sqrt{2t_{2}^{2}+t_{1}^{2}}
and
\Delta t=t_{c}^{1}-t_{c}^{2}
is the temporal separation of the two envelopes. Most notably, the amplitude strongly decreases with increasing
\Delta t^{2}
. Interestingly, deviations from the above slowly-varying assumptions and
\omega\ll\omega_{0}
would produce additional contributions of orders
\Delta tL/(\omega_{0}t_{eq}^{2}l_{d})
and
\omega\lambda_{\rm THz}/(\omega_{0}l_{d})
, respectively, in the argument of the sinc function of Eq. (
20
). Deviations from the above requirement
\omega\gamma\ll 1
would introduce a
z
-dependent correction
e^{-i\gamma\omega t_{1}^{2}/t_{eq}^{2}}
into
C_{env}
. For a
0.8\,\mu
m fundamental pulse, this contribution remains small for THz frequencies
<60
THz (120 THz) as long as
L\leq 4\,l_{d}
(resp.
L\leq 2\,l_{d}
).

The source term
\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\omega)
hence reads

\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\omega)\approx\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{J}}_{0}}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}},\omega)J_{\rm tr}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})e^{ikzn(\omega_{0})}C_{\rm env}(\omega).

(65)

By injecting relations (
65
) into equation (

), the radiated electric field takes the form:

\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}}(r,\Theta,\Phi,\omega)=\frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi r}\mbox{e}^{ikr}\sum_{j=J,K}C_{j}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega),

(66)

where

C_{J,K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)=A_{J,K}(\omega)\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J,K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega).

(67)

Here,
A_{J,K}(\omega)
are the THz spectral profiles associated with the photocurrents and Kerr response,

\displaystyle A_{J}(\omega)

\displaystyle=i\omega C_{env}(\omega)\frac{J_{e,0}}{\nu_{c}-i\omega},

(68)

\displaystyle A_{K}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}C_{env}(\omega)P_{K,0},

(69)

while
\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J,K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)
describes the interferences between the THz waves generated along the plasma channel:

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\frac{L}{2}}^{\frac{L}{2}}dz^{\prime}\mbox{e}^{ikz^{\prime}[n(\omega_{0})-\cos{\Theta}]}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\mbox{e}^{i\Delta kz^{\prime}}I_{S}^{+}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)-\mbox{e}^{-i\Delta kz^{\prime}}I_{S}^{-}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)}{2i}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\frac{L}{2}}^{\frac{L}{2}}dz^{\prime}\mbox{e}^{ikz^{\prime}[n(\omega_{0})-\cos{\Theta}]}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\mbox{e}^{i\Delta kz^{\prime}}I_{S}^{+}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)+\mbox{e}^{-i\Delta kz^{\prime}}I_{S}^{-}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)}{2}\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}.

The quantities
I_{S}^{\pm}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)
refer to the transverse integral contributions to the THz spectrum:

I_{S}^{\pm}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)=\iint d^{2}\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}^{\prime}_{\perp}J_{\rm tr}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})e^{\pm i\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})}\mbox{e}^{-i{\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}}_{\perp}\cdot{\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}}_{\perp}^{\prime}},

(70)

with
{\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}}_{\perp}=(\omega/c)(\sin\Theta\cos\Phi,\sin\Theta\sin\Phi)
. The functions
I_{S}^{\pm}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)
can be rewritten as

I_{S}^{\pm}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)=\mathcal{F}_{\perp}\left[J_{\rm tr}(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})e^{\pm i\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})}\right](\Theta,\Phi,\omega),

(71)

where
\mathcal{F_{\perp}}
represents the transverse Fourier transform.

It can be checked that the elementary transformations

\displaystyle\sin[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})]\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}

\displaystyle\to\binom{\sin[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})]}{\cos[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})]}

(72)

\displaystyle\cos[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})]\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}}

\displaystyle\to\binom{\cos[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})]}{-\sin[\Delta kz+\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime})]}

(73)

allow one to treat similarly CP-S laser pulses, cf. Eq. (
2
).

Straightforward calculation of the integrals
\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J,K}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)
gives, for a LP-P laser pulse:

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J}^{\rm LP-P}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{2}\left[I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}-I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}\right]\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}},

(74)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{K}^{\rm LP-P}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=i\frac{L}{2}\left[I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}+I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}\right]\mathrm{\mathbf{e_{x}}},

(75)

and for a CP-S pulse:

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{J}^{\rm CP-S}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=\frac{L}{2}\binom{I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}-I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}}{I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}+I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}},

(76)

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}_{K}^{\rm CP-S}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)

\displaystyle=i\frac{L}{2}\binom{I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}+I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}}{-I_{S}^{+}\kappa_{+}+I_{S}^{-}\kappa_{-}}.

(77)

The quantities
\kappa_{\pm}
are defined by:

\displaystyle\kappa_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{sinc}(\alpha_{\pm})

(78)

\displaystyle\alpha_{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}kL\left(n_{0}-\cos\Theta\pm\frac{\lambda_{\rm THz}}{2l_{d}}\right),

(79)

with
\mathrm{sinc}(x)=\sin x/x
and
k=\omega/c=2\pi/\lambda_{\rm THz}
.

Equations (
62
), (
74
) and (
75
) [alternatively (
76
) and (
77
)] can finally be injected into the relation (
66
) to obtain the far-field spectral intensity:

\left|\widehat{\mathrm{\mathbf{E}}}(r,\Theta,\Phi,\omega)\right|^{2}=\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}r^{2}}\left|\sum_{j=J,K}C_{j}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)\right|^{2}.

(80)

We have implicitly assumed that the dephasing length
l_{d}
is constant in space, although the source terms of THz radiation include radial variations. This means that similarly to You et
al.
’s model, we need to select an effective electron density level
N_{e}^{\rm eff}
to estimate
l_{d}
and thus evaluate our model.

Note that similarly to
[
47
]
, we ignore plasma absorption and opacity in the THz frequency range. These assumptions can be justified as follows. By neglecting the Kerr contribution and focusing on plasma dispersion, Eq. (
18
) reads

\begin{split}n^{2}(\omega)&=n^{2}_{\rm opt}(\omega)-\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{\omega^{2}(1+i\nu_{c}/\omega)}\\
&=n^{2}_{\rm opt}(\omega)-\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{\omega^{2}+\nu_{c}^{2}}+i\frac{\nu_{c}}{\omega}\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{\omega^{2}+\nu_{c}^{2}}.\end{split}

(81)

The real and imaginary parts of the medium index are given by

\displaystyle\Re[n(\omega)]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|n^{2}(\omega)|+\Re[n^{2}(\omega)]}{2}\right)^{1/2},

(82)

\displaystyle\Im[n(\omega)]

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{|n^{2}(\omega)|-\Re[n^{2}(\omega)]}{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(83)

For
\Re[n^{2}(\omega)]>0
, attenuation of THz waves through
\Im[n(\omega)]
is due to plasma absorption (
\nu_{c}\approx 3
THz), which recovers Kim et al.’s
[
49
]
approximation
\Im[n(\omega)]\approx\nu_{c}\omega_{p}^{2}/(2\omega(\omega^{2}+\nu_{c}^{2}))
in the limits
\nu_{c}/\omega\ll 1,\,n^{2}_{\rm opt}(\omega)\approx 1
. At density levels
N_{e}\lesssim 10^{17}
cm
-3
, the absorption length
L_{a}=c/(\omega\Im[n(\omega)])
is of the order of
\sim 100
µm at
\omega=2\pi\nu_{c}
. As typical filaments have a radius of
a\approx 20
µm and THz radiation is emitted at angles
\Theta\approx 5
°, the propagation distance of those waves to exit the plasma is
\approx 200
µm
\sim L_{a}
, meaning that plasma absorption should already have a limited effect on the emitted THz field. At higher frequencies,
L_{a}
quickly increases (
\sim 500
µm at 5 THz), and absorption becomes negligible.

In the lower region of the THz spectrum,
\Re[n^{2}(\omega)]=n^{2}_{\rm opt}(\omega)-\omega_{p}^{2}/(\omega^{2}+\nu_{c}^{2})
may become negative. Then, the attenuation of the THz waves through
\Im[n(\omega)]
is due to the plasma opacity. However, this effect barely impacts the forward emitted spectrum, as the THz field is emitted at the front of the plasma, i.e., before the plasma builds up
[
91
,
92
]
, so that the local
N_{e}
and thus
\omega_{p}
take smaller values.

Appendix B
Derivation of You et
al.
’s model

The original You et
al.
’s model [Eq. (
19
)]
[
47
,
48
]
can be easily derived from our augmented emission model. We assume a homogeneous cylindrical plasma of length
L
and radius
a
. Thus, the source term
J_{\rm tr}
contained in
I_{S}^{\pm}(\Theta,\Phi,\omega)
, is constant on the disk of radius
r_{\perp}<a
and zero outside. Moreover, we introduce
\phi_{0}
as the constant value taken by the phase
\phi(\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp})
. Hence, we can write

\displaystyle I_{S}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=e^{\pm i\phi_{0}}\iint d^{2}\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime}e^{-i\mathrm{\mathbf{k}}_{\perp}\cdot\mathrm{\mathbf{r}}_{\perp}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=e^{\pm i\phi_{0}}\int_{0}^{a}dr_{\perp}^{\prime}r_{\perp}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{2\pi}d\varphi^{\prime}e^{-ik_{\perp}r_{\perp}^{\prime}\cos\varphi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=2\pi e^{\pm i\phi_{0}}\int_{0}^{a}dr_{\perp}^{\prime}r_{\perp}^{\prime}J_{0}(k_{\perp}r_{\perp}^{\prime}),

with
J_{0}(x)
being the Bessel function of first kind and rank 0. The simplifications continue by using the identity
\int dxxJ_{0}(x)=xJ_{1}(x)

[
93
]
, where
J_{1}(x)
describes the Bessel function of first kind and rank 1:

I_{S}^{\pm}(\Theta,\omega)=2\pi e^{\pm i\phi_{0}}a^{2}\frac{J_{1}(\beta)}{\beta},

(84)

where we used
k_{\perp}=k\sin\Theta
and
\beta=ak\sin\Theta
.

If we now neglect the contribution of the Kerr response to the THz radiation (
A_{K}(\omega)=0
), assume that the laser pulse is linearly polarized (LP-P) and
n(\omega_{0})=1
, we can evaluate Eq. (
80
) as

\left|\widehat{E}(r,\Theta,\omega)\right|^{2}=\frac{\mu_{0}^{2}}{16r^{2}}a^{4}L^{2}\left|A_{J}(\omega)\right|^{2}\left(\frac{J_{1}(\beta)}{\beta}\right)^{2}\\
\times\left|\mbox{e}^{i\phi_{0}}\kappa_{+}-\mbox{e}^{-i\phi_{0}}\kappa_{-}\right|^{2},

where the term depending on
\alpha_{\pm}
develops as:

\left|\mbox{e}^{i\phi_{0}}\kappa_{+}-\mbox{e}^{-i\phi_{0}}\kappa_{-}\right|^{2}=\kappa_{+}^{2}+\kappa_{-}^{2}-2\kappa_{+}\kappa_{-}\cos(2\phi_{0}).

(85)

The far-field THz intensity radiated by the plasma thus restores You
et al.
’s Eq. (
19
), where
A(\omega)\equiv A_{J}(\omega)
.

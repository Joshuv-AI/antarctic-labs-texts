Descriptive Complexity of the Generalized Spectra of Graphs
Aida Abiad, Anuj Dawar, Octavio Zapata

Abstract
Two graphs are cospectral if their respective adjacency matrices have the same multiset of eigenvalues, and generalized cospectral if they are cospectral and so are their complements. We study generalized cospectrality in relation to logical definability. We show that any pair of graphs that are elementary equivalent with respect to the three-variable counting first-order logic C 3 C^{3} are generalized cospectral, and this is not the case with C 2 C^{2} , nor with any number of variables if we exclude counting quantifiers. Using this result we provide a new characterization of the well-known class of distance-regular graphs using the logic C 3 C^{3} . We also show that, for controllable graphs (it is known that almost all graphs are controllable), the elementary equivalence in C 2 C^{2} coincides with isomorphism.



1
Introduction

Let
\Gamma
be a graph on
n
vertices with adjacency matrix
A
.
The
characteristic polynomial

\varPhi(\Gamma,x)
of
\Gamma
is the characteristic polynomial of
A
:

\varPhi(\Gamma,x):=\det(xI-A).

Two graphs are
cospectral
if they have the same characteristic polynomial.
Since the adjacency matrices of isomorphic graphs are permutation similar, we see that isomorphic graphs are cospectral.
For any real number
y
the
generalized characteristic polynomial

\varPhi_{y}(\Gamma,x)
of the adjacency matrix
A
is defined by

\varPhi_{y}(\Gamma,x):=\det(xI-yJ-A),

where
J
and
I
denote the all-one matrix and the identity matrix, respectively.
Two graphs are
generalized cospectral
(sometimes also called
\mathbb{R}
-
cospectral
) if they have the same polynomial
\varPhi_{y}
for all values of
y
, that is, if the have the same generalized spectrum.
Since
\varPhi_{y}(\Gamma,x)
is the characteristic polynomial of
(A+yJ)
, it follows that isomorphic graphs are generalized cospectral.
Since

\varPhi(\Gamma,x)=\varPhi_{0}(\Gamma,x)

we see that generalized cospectral graphs are cospectral.
If
{\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern-2.0mu\Gamma}}
denotes the complement of
\Gamma
, then

\varPhi({\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern-2.0mu\Gamma}},x)=(-1)^{n}\ \varPhi_{-1}(\Gamma,-x-1)

and hence generalized cospectral graphs have cospectral complements. Johnson and Newman
[
13
]
showed that cospectral graphs with cospectral complements are generalized cospectral.

It is still an open question whether almost all graphs are characterized by their generalized characteristic polynomial.
More precisely, a graph
\Gamma
is
determined by its generalized spectrum
if every graph which is generalized cospectral with
\Gamma
is isomorphic to
\Gamma
.
It has been conjectured that the proportion of graphs on
n
vertices which are determined by their generalized spectra goes to 1 as
n
tends to infinity.
Wang et al.
[
18
,
21
,
14
,
19
]
have a number of results supporting this conjecture. They gave sufficient conditions for a graph to be determined by its generalized spectrum, and the majority of their results are proven for a wide class of graphs, the so-called controllable graphs. In fact, it is known that almost all graphs are controllable
[
15
]
.

In this paper we study these concepts from the perspective of logical definability. We prove that generalized cospectrality is implied by elementary equivalence with respect to the three-variable counting first-order logic
C^{3}
.
We also show that this is not the case with
C^{2}
, nor with any number of variables if we exclude counting quantifiers.
Using this result we prove that generalized cospectrality coincides with
C^{3}
-equivalence for a relevant class of regular graphs (including all distance-regular graphs). Finally we show that, for controllable graphs,
C^{2}
-equivalence coincides with isomorphism. The latter yields a new proof of the classical result of Immerman and Lander
[
12
]
that almost all graphs are definable (up to isomorphism) in the logic
C^{2}
. Our work implies some of the results in the work of Dawar, Severini and Zapata
[
6
]
and presents them in a more general setup.

2
Generalized Cospectrality

If
u
and
v
are vertices from a graph
\Gamma
with adjacency matrix
A
, the number of walks in
\Gamma
from
u
to
v
with length
\ell
is equal to
(A^{\ell})_{u,v}
.
Thus the number of closed walks of length
\ell
in
\Gamma
is
\tr(A^{\ell})
, and so

\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\tr(A^{\ell})x^{\ell}

is the generating function for closed walks in
\Gamma
counted by length.
If
\varPhi^{\ \!\!\prime}(\Gamma,x)
denotes the derivative of
\varPhi(\Gamma,x)
with respect to
x
, we find that

\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\tr(A^{\ell})x^{\ell}=\frac{x^{-1}\ \varPhi^{\ \!\!\prime}(\Gamma,x^{-1})}{\ \varPhi(\Gamma,x^{-1})}.

Therefore, two graphs are cospectral if and only if they have the same number of closed walks of each length.

If
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are non-cospectral graphs, there exists some
\ell\geq 1
such that the number of closed walks of length
\ell
in
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
is not the same.
This fact can be expressed in the language of first-order logic using counting quantifiers and not more than 3 variables.
The language
L^{k}
consists of the fragment of first-order logic in which only
k\geq 1
distinct variables can be used.
We use
C^{k}
to denote extension of
L^{k}
with
counting quantifiers
: for each non-negative integer
r
, we have a quantifier
\exists^{\geq r}
whose semantics is defined so that
\exists^{\geq r}\phi(x)
is true in a graph if there are at least
r
distinct vertices which can be substituted for
x
to make
\phi
true. We use the abbreviation
\exists^{=r}\phi
for the formula
\exists^{\geq r}\phi\land\lnot\exists^{\geq r+1}\phi
that asserts the existence of exactly
r
vertices satisfying
\phi
.

Two graphs
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are
elementary equivalent
with respect to a first-order language
L
(or
L
-equivalent
), just in case
\Gamma\models\phi
if and only if
\Gamma^{\prime}\models\phi
for any
L
-sentence
\phi
.
In other words,
L
-equivalent graphs are precisely those graphs that cannot be distinguished by any property defined by a sentence in the language
L
.
We write
\Gamma\equiv_{L}\Gamma^{\prime}
to denote that
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are elementary equivalent with respect to
L
.

The next result shows that
C^{3}
-equivalence implies generalized cospectrality.

Theorem 2.1
.

C^{3}
-equivalent graphs are generalized cospectral.

Proof
.

For every non-negative integer
r
, we use counting quantifiers and not more than three variables to write a formula
\psi_{\ell}^{r}(x,y)
that asserts the existence of exactly
r
distinct walks of length
\ell\geq 1
between
x
and
y
.
We define
\psi_{\ell}^{r}(x,y)
by induction on
\ell
.
For
\ell=1
, let

\psi_{1}^{0}(x,y):=\lnot E(x,y),\quad\psi_{1}^{1}(x,y):=E(x,y)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\psi_{1}^{r}(x,y):=\bot\ \textnormal{ if }\ r>1.

Assuming that we have defined
\psi_{\ell}^{r}(x,y)
for every
r
, we proceed with the definitions for
\ell+1
:

\psi_{\ell+1}^{0}(x,y):=\forall z(E(x,z)\to\psi_{\ell}^{0}(z,y)).

An
integer partition
of
r>1
is a sequence
(r_{1}^{a_{1}},r_{2}^{a_{2}},\dots,r_{d}^{a_{d}})
of
d\leq r
pairwise distinct positive integers (called the parts of
r
),
such that for any
i
(
1\leq i\leq d
) the part
r_{i}\geq 0
is repeated
a_{i}\geq 1
times and
r=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}r_{i}
.
Let
\Pi_{r}
denote the set of all integer partitions of
r
and note that this is a finite set.
Then

\psi_{\ell+1}^{r}(x,y):=\bigvee_{(r_{1}^{a_{1}},\ldots,r_{d}^{a_{d}})\in\Pi_{r}}[\bigwedge_{i=1}^{d}(\exists^{=a_{i}}x\ \psi_{\ell}^{r_{i}}(z,y))\land\exists^{\geq a}z\ E(x,z)]

where
a=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}
.
Here
a_{i}
denotes the number of neighbors of
x
for which there are exactly
r_{i}
walks of length
\ell
from each of them to
y
.

All of these definitions make use of at most three variables, and therefore each
\psi_{\ell}^{r}
is a formula of the language
C^{3}
.
Thus we can write a
C^{3}
-sentence
\phi_{\ell}^{r}
which is true in a graph when the total number of closed walks of length
\ell
is exactly
r
:

\phi_{\ell}^{r}:=\bigvee_{(r_{1}^{a_{1}},\dots,r_{d}^{a_{d}})\in\Pi_{r}}\bigwedge_{i=1}^{d}\exists^{=a_{i}}x\exists y\ (\psi_{\ell}^{r_{i}}(x,y)\land x=y).

If
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are not generalized cospectral graphs, then either
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are non-cospectral or
{\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern-2.0mu\Gamma}}
and
{\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern-2.0mu\Gamma^{\prime}}}
are non-cospectral.
Without loss of generality we assume that
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
do not have the same number of closed walks of length
\ell
for some
\ell\geq 1
.
Let
r
be the number of closed walks of length
\ell
in
\Gamma
.
Then
\Gamma\models\phi_{\ell}^{r}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\not\models\phi_{\ell}^{r}

and therefore
\Gamma\not\equiv_{C^{3}}\Gamma^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem
2.1
is optimal in the sense that
C^{2}
-equivalence is not sufficient for generalized cospectrality.
To see this, let
\Gamma
be the disjoint union of two triangles and let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a cycle of length 6.
A simple pebble game argument shows that
\Gamma\equiv_{C^{2}}\Gamma^{\prime}
(see e.g.
[
12
, Proposition 4.7.4]
).
However,
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are not generalized cospectral because they have a different number of triangles and the spectrum determines the number of triangles.

The converse of Theorem
2.1
does not hold in general.
Suppose, for instance, that
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are the left and right graphs from Figure
1
, respectively. This is the smallest pair of nonisomorphic generalized cospectral graphs.
Since
\Gamma
has one isolated vertex and
\Gamma^{\prime}
is connected, then
\Gamma\models\exists x\forall y\lnot E(x,y)
and
\Gamma\not\models\exists x\forall y\lnot E(x,y)
.
Hence
\Gamma\not\equiv_{C^{2}}\Gamma^{\prime}
, and therefore
\Gamma\not\equiv_{C^{k}}\Gamma^{\prime}
for
k\geq 2
.

The use of counting is essential for the distinguishability of the generalized spectra in finite-variable logics.

Theorem 2.2
.

There is a pair of nonisomorphic
L^{k}
-equivalent graphs which are not generalized cospectral for each positive integer
k
.

Proof
.

For
k\geq 1
, let
n_{k}
be the smallest integer greater than

2(k\log(4)+\log(k))/\log(13)
and let
q_{k}=13^{n_{k}}
.
Then
q_{k}>k^{2}2^{4k}
.
We write
{\mathbb{F}}_{q_{k}}
to denote the finite field of order
q_{k}
.

Let
\Gamma_{k}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{k}
be a pair of graphs with vertex set
{\mathbb{F}}_{q_{k}}
defined as follows.
Two vertices
i
and
j
are adjacent in
\Gamma_{k}
if there exists a positive integer
x
such that
x^{2}\equiv(i-j)\ \mathopen{(}\textnormal{mod}\ q_{k}\mathclose{)}
.
The vertices
i
and
j
are adjacent in
\Gamma^{\prime}_{k}
if there exists a positive integer
x
such that
x^{3}\equiv(i-j)\ \mathopen{(}\textnormal{mod}\ q_{k}\mathclose{)}
.
Hence,
\Gamma_{k}
is a Paley graph and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{k}
is a cubic Paley graph.

It can be shown that with our choice of parameters
\Gamma_{k}\equiv_{L^{k}}\Gamma^{\prime}_{k}
(see
[
2
]
and also
[
1
]
).
Since Paley graphs and cubic Paley graphs are strongly regular, the parameters (which include the degree) of these classes of graphs determine their characteristic polynomials.
Thus
\Gamma_{k}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{k}
are cospectral if and only if they have the same parameters.
However,
\Gamma_{k}
is regular of degree
(q_{k}-1)/2
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{k}
is regular of degree
(q_{k}-1)/3
.
Therefore
\Gamma_{k}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{k}
are not isomorphic and not generalized cospectral. ∎

Coherent Algebras

A matrix algebra is a real or complex vector space of matrices closed under matrix multiplication.
The
Schur product
of two
n\times n
matrices
A
and
B
is the
n\times n
matrix
A\circ B
given by

(A\circ B)_{i,j}=(A)_{i,j}(B)_{i,j}.

This is a commutative and associative operation with the identity
J
.
A
coherent algebra
is a matrix algebra closed under conjugate-transpose and Schur multiplication which contains
I
and
J
.

If
A
is the adjacency matrix of a graph
\Gamma
, the
adjacency algebra
of
\Gamma
is the matrix algebra
{\mathbb{C}}[A]
of polynomials in
A
with complex coefficients.
It is the unique minimal matrix algebra that contains
A
.
The
coherent closure
of
\Gamma
is the intersection of all matrix algebras that contain
{\mathbb{C}}[A]
which are closed under Schur product and conjugate-transpose.
It is the unique minimal coherent algebra that contains
{\mathbb{C}}[A]
.

A homomorphism of algebras is a ring homomorphism that commutes with scalar multiplication.
An algebra homomorphism between two coherent algebras need not preserve the Schur product.
A
coherent homomorphism
is an algebra homomorphism that commutes with Schur multiplication.

Any vector space of matrices which is closed under Schur product has a unique basis of matrices with entries 0 and 1, see for instance
[
3
, Theorem 2.6.1(i)]
.
An algebra homomorphism between two coherent algebras commutes with Schur product if and only if it maps the 01-basis of one algebra to the 01-basis of the other (see e.g.
[
5
, Proposition 2.3.17]
).

Weisfeiler and Leman
[
22
]
provided an algorithm that computes in polynomial time the 01-basis of the coherent closure of any graph.
Two graphs are called
WL-equivalent
if there is an invertible coherent homomorphism between their coherent closures.
The adjacency matrix of a graph does not necessarily belong to the 01-basis of its coherent closure.
However, we have the following result which implies that WL-equivalent graphs are generalized cospectral.
It follows from
[
4
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Two graphs are WL-equivalent if and only if they are
C^{3}
-equivalent.

Distance-Regular Graphs

Let
\Gamma
be a graph on
n
vertices with diameter
d
.
We say that
\Gamma
is
distance-regular
with intersection array
\{b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{d-1};c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{d}\}
if
\Gamma
is regular of degree
k=b_{0}
, and if for any two vertices
u
and
v
at distance
i
, there are precisely
c_{i}
neighbors of
v
at distance
i-1
from
u
, and
b_{i}
neighbors of
v
at distance
i+1
from
u
.
It is well known that two distance-regular graphs are cospectral if and only if they have the same intersection array (see e.g.
[
3
]
).
Since the only vertex at distance 0 from a given vertex is itself, we have that
c_{1}=1
.
The five Platonic solids are simple examples: the tetrahedron has intersection array
\{3;1\}
, the octahedron
\{4,1;1,4\}
, the cube
\{3,2,1;1,2,3\}
, the icosahedron
\{5,2,1;1,2,5\}
and the dodecahedron
\{3,2,1,1,1;1,1,1,2,3\}
.
Every strongly regular graph with parameters
\{n,k;a,c\}
is a distance-regular graph with diameter at most 2 and intersection array
\{k,k-a-1;1,c\}
.

The
i
-th
distance matrix
of
\Gamma
is the
n\times n
01-matrix
A_{i}
with
uv
-entry equal to 1 if and only if the distance between vertex
u
and vertex
v
in
\Gamma
is
i
.
By convention, we set
A_{0}=I
and
A_{i}=0
if
i
is greater than
d
.
Then
\sum_{i=0}^{d}A_{i}=J
and so
{\mathcal{D}}=\{A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{d}\}
is a linearly independent set of symmetric 01-matrices.
It follows that
\mathop{\mathrm{span}}({\mathcal{D}})
is a Schur-closed vector space of symmetric matrices which contains
I
and
J
.

Since
A_{1}
is the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma
, if we set
a_{i}=k-b_{i}-c_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,d
where
b_{-1}=b_{d}=c_{0}=c_{d+1}=0
, then we have

A_{1}A_{i}=b_{i-1}A_{i-1}+a_{i}A_{i}+c_{i+1}A_{i+1}.

If
i
is less than
d
then
c_{i+1}
is non-zero, and thus
A_{i+1}
is a linear combination of
A_{i-1},A_{i}
and
A_{1}A_{i}
.
We see by induction that
A_{i}
is a polynomial of degree
i
in
A_{1}
for
i=1,\dots,d
.
Again by induction, we find that
A_{i}A_{j}\in\mathop{\mathrm{span}}({\mathcal{D}})
for all
i
and
j
(see
[
10
, Lemma 4.2.2]
).
Thus
\mathop{\mathrm{span}}({\mathcal{D}})
is closed under matrix multiplication and so it is a coherent algebra.

Consider two graphs
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
with adjacency matrices
A
and
A^{\prime}
, respectively.
Since
A
and
A^{\prime}
are real symmetric matrices, they are diagonalizable.
This implies that if
A
and
A^{\prime}
have the same characteristic polynomial, then
Q^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}AQ=A^{\prime}
for some orthogonal matrix
Q
.
It follows that cospectral graphs have similar adjacency matrices.
In particular, if
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are cospectral distance-regular graphs, then all their distance matrices are simultaneously similar. This is because the
i
-th distance matrix of
\Gamma
is a polynomial of degree
i
in the adjacency matrix of
\Gamma
, and the same is true for
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
So if
A_{i}
and
A_{i}^{\prime}
are respectively the
i
-th distance matrices of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
, then there is a polynomial
p_{i}
of degree
i
such that

A^{\prime}_{i}=p_{i}(A_{1}^{\prime})=p_{i}(Q^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}A_{1}Q)=Q^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}p_{i}(A_{1})Q=Q^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}A_{i}Q

for all
i
.

Suppose
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are distance-regular graphs.
Friedland
[
9
]
proved that if
\varPsi
is an invertible coherent homomorphism between two coherent algebras, then there exists a unitary matrix
U
such that
\varPsi(M)=UMU^{\ast}
for all
M
.
The converse is also true.
In particular, there is an invertible coherent homomorphism
\varPsi
between the coherent algebras generated by the distance matrices of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
provided
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are generalized cospectral.
By taking the restriction of
\varPsi
to the coherent closures of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
, we obtain that
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are WL-equivalent. Thus we obtain the following consequence of Lemma
3.1
. A proof of a stronger result will be given in Section
5
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For distance-regular graphs, generalized cospectrality implies
C^{3}
-equivalence.

Now assume
\Gamma
is a regular graph of degree
k
. Then we have that

\varPhi({\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern-2.0mu\Gamma}},x)=(-1)^{n}\ \frac{x-n+k+1}{x+k+1}\ \varPhi(\Gamma,-x-1).

Therefore, two regular graphs are cospectral if and only if they are generalized cospectral.
The unique distance-regular graph with intersection array
\{5,4,1,1;1,1,4,5\}
is called the
Wells graph
, see for instance
[
3
, Theorem 9.2.9]
.
Van Dam and Haemers
[
16
]
showed that there is a regular graph cospectral with the Wells graph which is not distance-regular.
We have the following result which implies that the Wells graph and its cospectral mate are not
C^{3}
-equivalent.

Theorem 4.2
.

For each intersection array
{\mathcal{I}}
there is a
C^{3}
-sentence
\phi_{\mathcal{I}}
such that a graph
\Gamma
satisfies
\phi_{\mathcal{I}}
if and only if
\Gamma
is distance-regular with intersection array
{\mathcal{I}}
.

Proof
.

Let
{\mathcal{I}}=\{b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{d-1};c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{d}\}
.
We write a
C^{3}
-sentence which is true in a graph
\Gamma
if and only if
\Gamma

is distance-regular with intersection array
{\mathcal{I}}
.
We can express the existence of a path from
x
to
y
of length
i\geq 0
in the three-variable counting logic
C^{3}
as follows:

\phi_{0}(x,y):=(x=y),\quad\quad\phi_{1}(x,y):=E(x,y)

and

\phi_{i+1}(x,y):=\exists z[E(x,z)\land\exists x(z=x\land\phi_{i}(x,y))].

Let
\delta_{i}(x,y)
be the
C^{3}
-formula defined by

\delta_{i}(x,y):=\phi_{i}(x,y)\land\bigwedge_{j=0}^{i-1}\lnot\phi_{j}(x,y).

Hence
\Gamma\models\delta_{i}(x,y)
precisely when
x
and
y
are vertices at distance
i
in
\Gamma
.
If
r
is a positive integer, we define the
C^{3}
-sentences

\gamma^{r}_{i}:=\forall x\forall y[\delta_{i}(x,y)\to\exists^{=r}z(E(y,z)\land\delta_{i-1}(x,z))]\ \textnormal{ for }\ i\geq 1.

and

\beta^{r}_{i}:=\forall x\forall y[\delta_{i}(x,y)\to\exists^{=r}z(E(y,z)\land\delta_{i+1}(x,z))]\ \textnormal{ for }\ i\geq 0.

Then
\Gamma\models\gamma^{c_{i}}_{i}\land\beta^{b_{i}}_{i}
if and only if for any two vertices
x
and
y
at distance
i
in
\Gamma
, there are exactly
c_{i}
vertices adjacent to
y
at distance
i-1
from
x
, and
b_{i}
vertices adjacent to
y
at distance
i+1
from
x
.
Therefore

\Gamma\models\bigwedge_{i=1}^{d}(\beta^{b_{i}}_{i-1}\land\gamma^{c_{i}}_{i})

whenever
\Gamma
is distance-regular with intersection array

{\mathcal{I}}
. ∎

One consequence of the previous result is that all graphs which are
C^{3}
-equivalent to the Wells graph are isomorphic to it.
Indeed

\Gamma\models\beta^{5}_{0}\land\beta^{4}_{1}\land\beta^{1}_{2}\land\beta^{1}_{3}\land\gamma^{1}_{1}\land\gamma^{1}_{2}\land\gamma^{4}_{3}\land\gamma^{5}_{4}

if and only if
\Gamma
is distance-regular with intersection array
\{5,4,1,1;1,1,4,5\}
, and the Wells graph is unique up to isomorphism with this intersection array.

Corollary 4.3
.

There is a pair of nonisomorphic generalized cospectral regular graphs which
are not
C^{3}
-equivalent.

Proof
.

Let
\Gamma
be the Wells graph and let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be a nonisomorphic cospectral mate of
\Gamma
.
Then
\Gamma
is distance-regular and so it is regular.
Also
\Gamma^{\prime}
is regular.
Thus
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
cannot be
C^{3}
-equivalent for then we would have that
\Gamma^{\prime}
is distance-regular with the same intersection array as
\Gamma
, and this would imply that
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are isomorphic. ∎

Quotient-Polynomial Graphs

Quotient-polynomial graphs were introduced by Fiol and Penjić
[
8
]
as a generalization of orbit-polynomial and distance-regular graphs.

Let
\Gamma
be a graph on
n
vertices with adjacency matrix
A
.
For each eigenvalue
\theta
of
A
, let
E_{\theta}
be the matrix representing the orthogonal projection onto the eigenspace belonging to
\theta
.
Then
A
has the spectral decomposition

A=\sum_{\theta}\theta E_{\theta}.

For any two vertices
u
and
v
from
\Gamma
, let
\mathbf{w}_{\ell}(u,v)
be the number of walks of length
\ell
between
u
and
v
in
\Gamma
.
Thus

\mathbf{w}_{\ell}(u,v)=(A^{\ell})_{u,v}=\sum_{\theta}\theta^{\ell}(E_{\theta})_{u,v}.

Suppose
A
has
d+1
distinct eigenvalues (and hence the diameter of
\Gamma
is at least
d
).
We say that a partition
\pi=(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
of
V(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)
is
walk-regular
if all the pairs
(u,v)
in cell
C_{i}
have the same vector

\mathbf{w}(u,v)=(\mathbf{w}_{0}(u,v),\dots,\mathbf{w}_{d}(u,v))^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}.

It follows that all pairs of vertices from the same cell are at the same distance.

Given a walk-regular partition
\pi=(C_{1},\dots,C_{m})
of
\Gamma
, let
B_{i}
be the

n\times n
01-matrix with
uv
-entry equal to 1 if and only if
(u,v)\in C_{i}
.
Let
w_{\ell}(i)
be the the number of walks of length
\ell
between any two vertices
u
and
v
such that
(u,v)\in C_{i}
.
Then, for all
\ell=0,\dots,d
, we have

A^{\ell}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}w_{\ell}(i)B_{i}.

Thus
{\mathbb{C}}[A]=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{A^{0},\dots,A^{d}\}\subset\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\}
, and so it is necessary that
m\geq d+1
.
We say that
\Gamma
is
quotient-polynomial
if there are polynomials
p_{i}
with degree at most
d
such that
p_{i}(A)=B_{i}
for all
i
.
It follows that
\Gamma
is quotient-polynomial if and only if
{\mathbb{C}}[A]=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}\{B_{1},\dots,B_{m}\}
(and hence
m=d+1
).
Every distance-regular graph is quotient-polynomial, see for instance
[
7
, Proposition 5.1]
.

Fiol and Penjić
[
8
]
showed that a graph is quotient-polynomial if and only if its adjacency algebra is closed under Schur product.
Since
\pi
is a partition, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{m}B_{i}=J
and so
J\in{\mathbb{C}}[A]
.
It follows that
\Gamma
is quotient-polynomial if and only if
{\mathbb{C}}[A]
is a coherent algebra.
Therefore, a graph is quotient-polynomial if and only if its coherent closure is its adjacency algebra.

Theorem 5.1
.

Two quotient-polynomial graphs are generalized cospectral if and only if they are
C^{3}
-equivalent.

Proof
.

We show that for quotient-polynomial graphs generalized cospectrality implies
C^{3}
-equivalence.
For the converse we already have Theorem
2.1
.
Let
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
be graphs with adjacency matrices
A
and
A^{\prime}
, respectively.
If
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are generalized cospectral, then they are cospectral and so there exists an orthogonal matrix
Q
such that
Q^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}AQ=A^{\prime}
.
Let
B_{i}
and
B_{i}^{\prime}
be the matrices associated with the walk-regular partitions of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
, respectively.
Suppose
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are quotient-polynomial.
Then there is a polynomial
p_{i}
of degree at most
d
such that

B^{\prime}_{i}=p_{i}(A^{\prime})=p_{i}(Q^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}AQ)=Q^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}p_{i}(A)Q=Q^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}B_{i}Q

for all
i
.
Thus there is an invertible coherent homomorphism
\varPsi(M)=Q^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}MQ
between the coherent closures of
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
Therefore
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are WL-equivalent, and hence Lemma
3.1
implies that
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are
C^{3}
-equivalent. ∎

Since quotient-polynomial graphs are a generalization of orbit-polynomial and distance-regular graphs, as a corollary of Theorem
5.1
we obtain Theorem
4.1
.

6
Controllable Graphs

Let
\Gamma
be a graph on
n
vertices with adjacency matrix
A
, and let
{\mathbf{1}}
denote the all-one vector of length
n
.
The number of walks of length
\ell
in
\Gamma
is equal to

\tr(A^{\ell}J)={\mathbf{1}}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}A^{\ell}{\mathbf{1}}.

Since

\sum_{\ell\geq 0}A^{\ell}x^{\ell}=(I-xA)^{-1}

we have that the generating function for all walks in
\Gamma
is

\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\tr(A^{\ell}J)x^{\ell}={\mathbf{1}}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}(I-xA)^{-1}{\mathbf{1}}.

We say that two graphs are
walk-equivalent
if their generating functions for all walks are equal.
Since

xI-(J-A)=(xI+A)(I-(xI+A)^{-1}J)

and

I-(xI+A)^{-1}J=I-((xI+A)^{-1}{\mathbf{1}}){\mathbf{1}}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}

then using the identity
\det(\mathbf{u}\mathbf{v}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}})=1-\mathbf{v}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}\mathbf{u}
, we find that

\frac{\varPhi({\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern-2.0mu\Gamma}},x-1)}{(-1)^{n}\ \varPhi(\Gamma,-x)}=1-{\mathbf{1}}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}(xI+A)^{-1}{\mathbf{1}}

and so

{\mathbf{1}}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}(I-xA)^{-1}{\mathbf{1}}=\frac{1}{x}\left(\frac{\varPhi({\mkern 2.0mu\overline{\mkern-2.0mu\Gamma}},-x^{-1}-1)}{(-1)^{n}\ \varPhi(\Gamma,x^{-1})}-1\right).

Thus, if two graphs are generalized cospectral then they are walk-equivalent.

Theorem 6.1
.

C^{2}
-equivalent graphs are walk-equivalent.

Proof
.

We write a
C^{2}
-formula
\psi^{r}_{\ell}(x)
such that for any graph
\Gamma
and any vertex
u
from
\Gamma
, we have

\Gamma\models\psi^{r}_{\ell}(u)

if and only if there are
r\geq 0
walks of length
\ell\geq 1
in
\Gamma
starting at
u
.
We proceed by induction on
\ell
.
If
\ell=1
then we let

\psi^{0}_{1}(x):=\forall y\lnot E(x,y)\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\psi^{r}_{1}(x):=\exists^{=r}y\ E(x,y)\quad\textnormal{for}\ r>0.

For
\ell>1
, we define

\psi^{0}_{\ell+1}(x):=\forall y(E(x,y)\to\psi^{0}_{\ell}(y))

and if
r>0
then

\psi^{r}_{\ell+1}(x):=\bigvee_{(r_{1}^{a_{1}},\dots,r_{d}^{a_{d}})\in\Pi_{r}}[(\bigwedge_{i=1}^{d}\exists^{=a_{i}}y\ \psi_{\ell}^{r_{i}}(y))\land\exists^{\geq a}y\ E(x,y)]

where
\Pi_{r}
is the set of integer partitions
(r_{1}^{a_{1}},\dots,r_{d}^{a_{d}})
of

r
(so
r=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}r_{i}
) and
a=\sum_{i=1}^{d}a_{i}
.
With this notation we define the
C^{2}
-sentence
\phi_{\ell}^{r}
as follows:

\phi_{\ell}^{r}:=\bigvee_{(r_{1}^{a_{1}},\dots,r_{d}^{a_{d}})\in\Pi_{r}}\ \bigwedge_{i=1}^{d}\exists^{=a_{i}}x\ \psi_{\ell}^{r_{i}}(x).

By definition
\Gamma\models\phi_{\ell}^{r}
if and only if there are
r
walks of length
\ell
in
\Gamma
.

Now suppose that
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are two graphs which are not walk-equivalent.
Then
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
have a different number of walks of length
\ell
for some
\ell
.
Let
r
be the number of walks of length
\ell
in
\Gamma
.
We have
\Gamma\models\phi_{\ell}^{r}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}\not\models\phi_{\ell}^{r}
.
Therefore
\Gamma\not\equiv_{C^{2}}\Gamma^{\prime}
.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Two regular graphs are walk-equivalent if and only if they are
C^{2}
-equivalent.

Proof
.

Let
\Gamma
be a
k
-regular graph on
n
vertices with adjacency matrix
A
.
If
A
has spectral decomposition

A=\sum_{\theta}\theta E_{\theta}

then
A{\mathbf{1}}=k{\mathbf{1}}
and
E_{k}=n^{-1}{\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}
.
Thus
E_{\theta}E_{k}=E_{\theta}{\mathbf{1}}{\mathbf{1}}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}=0
and so
{\mathbf{1}}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}E_{\theta}{\mathbf{1}}=0
for all
\theta\neq k
.
Since

(I-xA)^{-1}=\sum_{\theta}\frac{1}{1-x\theta}E_{\theta}

we find that the generating function for all walks in
\Gamma
is

{\mathbf{1}}^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}(I-xA)^{-1}{\mathbf{1}}=\frac{n}{1-xk}.

Hence, any two
k
-regular graphs on
n
vertices are walk-equivalent.
Therefore if
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are two regular graphs such that
\Gamma\not\equiv_{C^{2}}\Gamma^{\prime}
, then either the number of vertices in
\Gamma
is different than the number of vertices in
\Gamma^{\prime}
or the degree of
\Gamma
is different than the degree of
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
In either case,
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are not walk-equivalent.
The converse follows immediately from Theorem
6.1
.
∎

The
walk matrix
of
\Gamma
is the
n\times n
matrix

W_{\Gamma}=\big({\mathbf{1}}\quad A{\mathbf{1}}\quad\cdots\quad A^{n-1}{\mathbf{1}}\big).

Our next result combined with Theorem
2.1
and the fact that
C^{3}
-equivalent graphs are necessarily
C^{2}
-equivalent implies
[
17
, Lemma 3]
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
be
C^{2}
-equivalent graphs.
Then

W^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}_{\Gamma}W_{\Gamma}=W^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}.

Proof
.

We shall use the
C^{2}
-formulas
\psi^{r}_{\ell}(x)
which are defined in the proof of Theorem
6.1
.
Recall that
\psi^{r}_{\ell}(x)
asserts the existence of exactly
r
walks of length
\ell
starting at
x
.
Since
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are
C^{2}
-equivalent,
there is a vertex
v
from
\Gamma
such that
\Gamma\models\psi^{r}_{\ell}(v)
if and only if there is a vertex
v^{\prime}
from
\Gamma^{\prime}
such that
\Gamma^{\prime}\models\psi^{r}_{\ell}(v^{\prime})
.
Hence the mapping
v\mapsto v^{\prime}
is a bijection between the sets

\{v\in V(\Gamma):\Gamma\models\psi^{r}_{\ell}(v)\}\qquad\textnormal{and}\qquad\{v^{\prime}\in V(\Gamma^{\prime}):\Gamma^{\prime}\models\psi^{r}_{\ell}(v^{\prime})\}

for each
r\geq 0
and
\ell\geq 1
.
Since the rows of the walk matrix of a graph are indexed by the vertices of the graph, it follows that
W_{\Gamma}
and
W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
are equal up to a permutation of the rows induced by the above bijection.
Thus there is a permutation matrix
P
such that
PW_{\Gamma}=W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
.
Then

W^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}_{\Gamma^{\prime}}W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}=(PW_{\Gamma})^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}(PW_{\Gamma})=W_{\Gamma}^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}P^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}PW_{\Gamma}=W^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}_{\Gamma}W_{\Gamma}.\qed

We say that a graph
\Gamma
is
controllable
if its walk matrix
W_{\Gamma}
is invertible.
The theory of controllable graphs was developed in
[
11
]
, where it was conjectured that the proportion of graphs on
n
vertices which are controllable goes to 1 as
n\to\infty
.
It was later confirmed in
[
15
]
that indeed almost all graphs are controllable.
Wang and Xu
[
20
]
proved that if
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are two walk-equivalent controllable graphs with adjacency matrices
A
and
A^{\prime}
, respectively, then
Q=W_{\Gamma}W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{-1}
is an orthogonal matrix such that
Q^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}AQ=A^{\prime}
and
Q{\mathbf{1}}={\mathbf{1}}
.
Thus we have the following consequence of Theorem
6.1
, Lemma
6.3
and the preceding remark.

Corollary 6.4
.

Two controllable graphs are isomorphic if and only if they are
C^{2}
-equivalent.

Proof
.

We prove that
C^{2}
-equivalent controllable graphs are isomorphic. Consider two controllable graphs
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
with adjacency matrices
A
and
A^{\prime}
, respectively.
If
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are
C^{2}
-equivalent, then by Theorem
6.1

\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are walk-equivalent.
Hence the matrix
Q=W_{\Gamma}W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{-1}
satisfies
Q^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}AQ=A^{\prime}
.

Now, in the proof of Lemma
6.3
we saw that there is a permutation matrix
P
such that
W_{\Gamma}=P^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}
.
Then we have

Q=W_{\Gamma}W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{-1}=P^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}W_{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{-1}=P^{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}

and thus
PAP^{{{}^{\!\top\!\!}}}=A^{\prime}
.
Therefore
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are isomorphic.
∎

Remark 6.5
.

For any fragment
L
of the first-order language of graphs, we say that a graph
\Gamma
is
L
-definable if there is an
L
-sentence
\phi
such that every graph satisfying
\phi
is isomorphic to
\Gamma
.
Since almost all graphs are controllable
[
15
]
, Corollary
6.4
implies the classical result of Immerman and Lander
[
12
]
that almost all graphs are
C^{2}
-definable.

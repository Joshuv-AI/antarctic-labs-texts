Wilson lines construction of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} toroidal conformal blocks
Vladimir Belavin, Pietro Oreglia, J. Ramos Cabezas

Abstract
We study 𝒲 3 \mathcal{W}_{3} toroidal conformal blocks for degenerate primary fields in AdS/CFT context. In the large central charge limit 𝒲 3 \mathcal{W}_{3} algebra reduces to 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} algebra and 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} blocks are defined as contributions to 𝒲 3 \mathcal{W}_{3} blocks coming from the generators of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} subalgebra. We consider the construction of 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} toroidal blocks in terms of Wilson lines operators of 3 ​ d 3d Chern-Simons gravity in the thermal AdS 3 space-time. According to the correspondence, degenerate primary fields are associated with Wilson lines operators acting in the corresponding finite-dimensional 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} representations. We verify this dual construction for one-point toroidal block using 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 3 \mathfrak{sl}_{3} tensor technique in the bulk theory and an algorithm based on AGT correspondence in the boundary CFT.

2.1
\mathcal{W}_{3}
Conformal Field Theory

The symmetry of the
\mathcal{W}_{3}
CFT is generated by the energy-momentum tensor
\mathbf{T}(z)
(a spin-
2

\mathbf{W}(z)

\mathbf{T}(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{L}_{n}}{z^{n+2}},\qquad\mathbf{W}(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathbf{W}_{n}}{z^{n+3}}.

(2.1)

The modes
\mathbf{L}_{n}
and
\mathbf{W}_{m}
generate the
\mathcal{W}_{3}

\begin{split}\left[\mathbf{L}_{n},\mathbf{L}_{m}\right]&=(n-m)\mathbf{L}_{n+m}+\displaystyle\frac{c}{12}(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m,0}\;,\\
\left[\mathbf{L}_{n},\mathbf{W}_{m}\right]&=(2n-m)\mathbf{W}_{n+m}\;,\\
\left[\mathbf{W}_{n},\mathbf{W}_{m}\right]&=\frac{c}{3\cdot 5!}(n^{2}-1)(n^{2}-4)n\delta_{n+m,0}+\frac{16}{22+5c}(n-m)\mathbf{\Lambda}_{n+m}+\\
&\qquad+\frac{(n-m)}{30}\left(2m^{2}+2n^{2}-mn-8\right)\mathbf{L}_{n+m}\;,\end{split}

(2.2)

where

\mathbf{\Lambda}_{m}=\sum_{p\leq-2}\mathbf{L}_{p}\mathbf{L}_{m-p}+\sum_{p\geq-1}\mathbf{L}_{m-p}\mathbf{L}_{p}-\frac{3(m+2)(m+3)}{10}\mathbf{L}_{m}\;.

(2.3)

In the limit
c\rightarrow\infty

\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

\{\mathbf{L}_{-1},\mathbf{L}_{0},\mathbf{L}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{-1},\mathbf{W}_{1},\mathbf{W}_{0},\mathbf{W}_{-2},\mathbf{W}_{2}\}\;,

(2.4)

that satisfy

\begin{split}[\mathbf{L}_{n},\mathbf{L}_{m}]&=(n-m)\mathbf{L}_{n+m}\;,\\
[\mathbf{L}_{n},\mathbf{W}_{m}]&=(2n-m)\mathbf{W}_{n+m}\;,\\
[\mathbf{W}_{n},\mathbf{W}_{m}]&=(n-m)\Big(\frac{1}{15}(n+m+2)(n+m+3)-\frac{1}{6}(n+2)(m+2)\Big)\mathbf{L}_{n+m}\;.\end{split}

(2.5)

[

\displaystyle+\frac{(a+1)\big(a^{2}(m_{1}+m_{2})+3a(m_{1}+m_{2})-9(a+3)m_{1}m_{2}\big)}{9m_{1}m_{2}}\,+

\displaystyle+\frac{(a+3)(a-3m_{1})(a+3m_{1}+3)(a-3m_{1}-3m_{2}-3)}{81m_{1}(m_{1}+1)(m_{1}+m_{2}+1)}\,+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{18}(a+6)(a+3)+\frac{a}{3}+1\bigg)q^{2}+\dots\;.

(4.1)

Now, we propose the following formula that relates the

block to the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}

\frac{\mathcal{F}(\alpha,\alpha_{1},q)}{\mathcal{F}(\alpha,\alpha_{1},q)_{\mathfrak{sl}_{3}}}=\frac{\chi^{\mathcal{W}_{3}}}{\chi^{\mathfrak{sl}_{3}}}\;,

where

where the second line is obtained by fixing the proper normalization of (

\mathfrak{sl}_{2}

The relation (

5.1
Computation of the Wilson line operators

We are interested in performing the computation of the Wilson network operator exploiting the tensor products of representations of the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
algebra. This is based on the fact that every state in a given representation of the algebra can be represented as a symmetric traceless tensor. Let us show how this procedure works.

The fundamental representation of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
is characterized by the highest-weight vector
j=w_{1}=(1,0)

\ket{e_{1}}=w_{1},\quad\ket{e_{2}}=w_{2}-w_{1},\quad\ket{e_{3}}=-w_{2}\;.

Similarly, we denote the states of the anti-fundamental representation
(0,1)

(5.10)

For a generic

\begin{split}T^{k_{1}....k_{m_{2}}}_{i_{1}...i_{m_{1}}}&=\frac{1}{m_{1}!m_{2}!}\ket{e_{(i_{1}}}\otimes\ket{e_{i_{2}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ket{e_{i_{m_{1}})}}\,\ket{\bar{e}^{(k_{1}}}\otimes\ket{\bar{e}^{k_{2}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\ket{\bar{e}^{k_{m_{2}})}}=\\

to be equal to

The idea is to represent (

6
Discussion

In this work we studied the semiclassical limit of
\mathcal{W}_{3}
CFT on the torus, with a focus on the

\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
one-point conformal blocks of fully degenerate primary fields for an external field satisfying (
1.8
). We compute the
\mathcal{W}_{3}
block in the light
c\rightarrow\infty
limit using AGT correspondence. In order to obtain the
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
block we propose the relation (
4.2
). The utility of this relation is twofold since it serves as a check of the dual representation for
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
conformal block and provides a computational method to calculate its higher-level contributions.

The dual description of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
one-point toroidal conformal block is given in terms of the Wilson lines in
3d
Chern-Simons gravity. The proposal summarizes in (
5.41
). The dual approach seems to give more manageable computations compared to those based on the original definition, at least in its construction. The simplest test of this proposal is the case when the one-point block reduces to the zero-point block, which we confirmed analytically and numerically in section
5.1.1
. Further, we have performed more general tests for specific cases of representations (
5.24
), obtaining that all these examples confirm (
5.15
). The absence of a general expression of
\mathfrak{sl}_{3}
Clebsch-Gordan coefficients forces us to use the tensor technique in section
5.1
, the general proof of (
5.41
) remains an open problem.

From the Wilson line construction a natural generalization follows, that is the study of the dual description in the cases of higher-point conformal blocks, which show additional features, like the presence of different intermediate channels (usually called
s
and
t
), as shown for the
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
case in
[
43
]
. This generalization is straightforward in the context of the Wilson lines formulation of the conformal blocks, according to the construction of section
5
. Also, it would be worth to extend this approach to the whole
\mathcal{W}_{3}
CFT, which would require to focus also on quantum corrections (for related consideration see
[
29
]
).

Another interesting question is how the Wilson line approach works if multiplicities in the representations under consideration are present. From the dual construction it follows that in such cases we can obtain multiple nonequivalent expressions for the projector (
5.13
). For instance, if the external field is in the
adjoint representation, there is a multiplicity two for the conformal blocks in the
\mathcal{W}_{3}
CFT
[
45
]
, which corresponds to the fact that Clebsch-Gordan coefficients are not uniquely defined
[
53
]
. In this case, the multiplicity is present also in the definition of the projector. It would be interesting to establish a precise correspondence between uncertainty related to the OPE multiplicities for conformal blocks
and multiplicities arising in the definition of the projector in the dual construction.

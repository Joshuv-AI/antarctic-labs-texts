Separability Properties of Nilpotent ℚ ⁡ [ x ] \mathbb{Q}[x] -Powered Groups II
Stephen Majewicz, Marcos Zyman

Abstract
. In this paper, we study nilpotent ℚ ⁡ [ x ] \mathbb{Q}[x] -powered groups that satisfy the following property: For some set of primes ω \omega in ℚ ⁡ [ x ] , \mathbb{Q}[x], every ω ′ \omega^{\prime} -isolated ℚ ⁡ [ x ] \mathbb{Q}[x] -subgroup in some family of its ℚ ⁡ [ x ] \mathbb{Q}[x] -subgroups is finite ω \omega -type separable.

1.
Introduction

This paper is a continuation of our paper entitled “Separability Properties of Nilpotent
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-Powered Groups”
[
12
]
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of groups. A group
G
is
residually
\mathcal{C}
if for every
1\neq g\in G,
there exists
K\in\mathcal{C}
and a homomorphism
\varphi
from
G
onto
K
such that
\varphi(g)\neq 1.
A subgroup
H
of a group
G
is called
\mathcal{C}
-separable
in
G
if, for every element
g\in G\setminus H,
there is a homomorphism
\varphi
of
G
onto
K\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\varphi(g)\notin\varphi(H).
It is immediate that
G
is residually
\mathcal{C}
if and only if
\left\{1\right\}
is
\mathcal{C}
-separable in
G
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a non-empty set of primes and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
the set of all primes not in
\mathcal{P}.
A natural number
n
is called a
\mathcal{P}
-number
if every prime divisor of
n
belongs to
\mathcal{P}.
By convention,
1
is a
\mathcal{P}
-number for any set of primes
\mathcal{P}.
A
\mathcal{P}
-group
is a torsion group such that each of its elements has order a
\mathcal{P}
-number. Denote the class of all finite
\mathcal{P}
-groups by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}},
and let
G
be any group with
H\leq G.
If
H
is
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}}
-separable in
G,
then it can be shown that
H
is a
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-isolated subgroup of
G
(i.e. if
h\in G
and
h^{r}\in H
for some
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-number
r,
then
h\in H
).

The converse, however, fails in general. For instance, in
[
1
]
, Bardakov showed that if
G
is a non-abelian group and
H
is an isolated (finitely generated) subgroup of
G,
then
H
is not separable in the class of nilpotent groups (hence,
H
is not
\mathcal{F}_{\{p\}}
-separable in
G
for all primes
p
).

In
[
13
]
, Sokolov continued the study of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}}
-separability of subgroups in nilpotent groups. A group
G
is said to have
property
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
(for some family of its subgroups) if every
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
-isolated subgroup of
G
belonging to the family is
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{P}}
-separable. Prior to Sokolov’s work, it was shown by Loginova
[
6
]
that for every prime
p,
all finitely generated nilpotent groups have property
\mathcal{G}_{\{p\}}.
This result can be generalized for a set of primes rather than a single prime. Two of Sokolov’s results are:

(1)

An abelian group
A
has property
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
if and only if
A
satisfies the following condition: In every quotient group
B
of
A,
all
p
-primary components of the torsion subgroup of
B
have finite exponent for all
p\in\mathcal{P}.

(2)

Every
\mathcal{P}
-restricted nilpotent group has property
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}.

(An abelian group is
\mathcal{P}
-restricted
if in every quotient
B
of
A,
all
p
-primary components of the torsion subgroup of
B
are finite for all
p\in\mathcal{P}.
A nilpotent group is
\mathcal{P}
-restricted
if it has a finite central series with
\mathcal{P}
-restricted abelian factors.)

(3)

A torsion-free nilpotent group has property
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{P}}
if and only if it is
\mathcal{P}
-restricted.

In this paper, we prove analogues of these results for nilpotent
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-powered groups. The ring
\mathbb{Q}[x]
is an example of a
binomial ring
, an integral domain with unity
R
that has characteristic zero such that for any
r\in R
and positive integer
k,

\binom{r}{k}=\frac{r(r-1)\cdots(r-k+1)}{k!}\in R.

A
nilpotent
R
-powered group
is a nilpotent group
G
that comes equipped with an action by
R
such that
g^{\alpha}
is an element of
G
for all
g\in G
and
\alpha\in R
, and satisfies the following:

(1)

g^{1}=g,\,g^{\alpha}g^{\beta}=g^{\alpha+\beta},
and
(g^{\alpha})^{\beta}=g^{\alpha\beta}
for all
g\in G
and
\alpha,\beta\in R.

(2)

(h^{-1}gh)^{\alpha}=h^{-1}g^{\alpha}h
for all
g,\ h\in G
and for all
\alpha\in R.

(3)

If
\{g_{1},\,\ldots,\,g_{n}\}\subset G
and
\alpha\in R,
then

g_{1}^{\alpha}\cdots g_{n}^{\alpha}=\tau_{1}(\bar{g})^{\alpha}\tau_{2}(\bar{g})^{\binom{\alpha}{2}}\cdots\tau_{k-1}(\bar{g})^{\binom{\alpha}{k-1}}\tau_{k}(\bar{g})^{\binom{\alpha}{k}},

where
k
is the nilpotency class of the group generated by
\{g_{1},\,g_{2},\,\ldots,\,g_{n}\},

\bar{g}=(g_{1},\,\ldots,\,g_{n}),
and
\tau_{i}(\bar{g})
is the
i
th
Hall-Petresco word
.

An
abelian
R
-group
is a nilpotent
R
-powered group  of class
1.
If
G
is an abelian
R
-group and
g,\,h\in G,
then
(gh)^{\alpha}=g^{\alpha}h^{\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in R.
This follows from (3), together with the fact that
\tau_{1}(g,\,h)=gh.
Thus, every abelian
R
-group can be viewed as an
R
-module. Throughout the paper, we use multiplicative and exponential notation for the operations in an abelian
R
-group.

Nilpotent
R
-powered groups naturally arise from taking the
R
-completion of a finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group with respect to a given Mal’cev basis. Notions such as
R
-subgroup,
R
-morphism, direct products,
R
-series, etc. are defined in the obvious way (see
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
, and
[
14
]
).

In this paper, all direct products will be restricted.

All necessary background material will be provided in the preliminary section. Throughout the paper,
R
will always be a binomial ring (possibly with other qualifications). Unless otherwise told, we use “
1
” to denote the unity element in a ring and the identity element in a group.

The definitions of residually
\mathcal{C}
and

-separable carry over to the category of nilpotent

.

-powered groups, and let

•

For a set of primes

•

)

•

An abelian

Our focus will be on the case

Let

The main results in this paper are:

Definition 2.6
.

Let

-powered group  with

.

The element

of

Clearly, any associate of

Hence, the order of

Definition 2.8

.

The element

The bounded exponent

\mathcal{FT_{\pi}}.
Since
\pi\in\omega,
we conclude that
\overline{G}
is residually
\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}.

Case 2
: Suppose that
aH
has non-infinite order that is monic and not a power of a prime, say
\pi^{r}\mu\in\mathbb{Q}[x],
where
\pi\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
is a monic prime,
r\in\mathbb{N},
and
\mu\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
is monic and not divisible by
\pi.
As in Case 1, we have that
\pi\in\omega
; for otherwise,
a^{\pi^{r}\mu}\in H
would imply that
a^{\mu}\in H
since
H
is
\omega^{\prime}
-isolated, and this would be impossible.

Set
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{M}\setminus\{\pi\}.
By Theorem
2.4
the
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-subgroups
\tau_{\beta}\left(\overline{G}\right)
form a direct product for the various primes
\beta\in\mathbb{P}.
Set

N=\prod_{\beta\in\mathbb{P}}\tau_{\beta}\left(\overline{G}\right).

We claim that
N
is
\omega^{\prime}
-isolated in
\overline{G}
. To see this assume that
(gH)^{\alpha}\in N
for some
gH\in\overline{G}
and
\omega^{\prime}
-member
\alpha.
Then
\pi
is relatively prime to both
\alpha
and to the order of
(gH)^{\alpha}
. Hence,
\pi
is relatively prime to the order of
gH
itself. This shows that
gH\in N
, and our claim holds. Note also that
(aH)^{\pi^{r}}\in N
since the order of
(aH)^{\pi^{r}}
is
\mu
, which is relatively prime to
\pi
.
Hence
(aH)N
has prime power order in
\overline{G}/N
.

By Case 1, there exist
K\in\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}
and an onto
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-homomorphism

\psi:\overline{G}/N\rightarrow K

such that
\psi[(aH)N]\neq 1
. If
\Phi
is the natural
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-homomorphism from
\overline{G}
onto
\overline{G}/N
, then
\Phi\circ\psi
is a
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-homomorphism from
\overline{G}
onto
K
that maps
aH
to a non-trivial element in
K
. This completes Case 2.

Case 3
: Let
aH
have infinite order and set
C=gp_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}(aH).
We assert that
\overline{G}/C
satisfies Condition (
B_{\omega}
). Well, if
\overline{K}/C\leq_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}\overline{G}/C,
then

\frac{\overline{G}/C}{\overline{K}/C}\cong_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}\overline{G}/\overline{K}\cong_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}G/K.

Since
G
satisfies Condition (
B_{\omega}
),
\tau_{\pi}(G/K)
is bounded for any
\pi\in\omega.
Hence
\tau_{\pi}\left(\left(\overline{G}/C\right)/\left(\overline{K}/C\right)\right)
is also bounded and, consequently,
\overline{G}/C
satisfies Condition (
B_{\omega}
) as asserted. Thus,
\tau_{\pi}\left(\overline{G}/{C}\right)
is bounded for each
\pi\in\omega.

Choose a specific prime
\alpha\in\omega.
We prove that there exists a natural number
r
such that
aH\notin\overline{G}^{\alpha^{r}}.
Suppose, on the contrary, that
aH\in\overline{G}^{\alpha^{t}}
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}.
Then there exist

g_{1},\,g_{2},\,\ldots\in G
such that

aH=\left(g_{1}H\right)^{\alpha}=\left(g_{2}H\right)^{\alpha^{2}}=\cdots

in
\overline{G}
. So, in
\overline{G}/C
, we have

C=(aH)C=\left(g_{1}H\right)^{\alpha}C=\left(g_{2}H\right)^{\alpha^{2}}C=\cdots.

Consequently,
\left(g_{i}H\right)C\in\tau_{\alpha}\left(\overline{G}/C\right)
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}.
We claim that the order of
(g_{i}H)C
is precisely
\alpha^{i}.
This is obviously the case for
i=1
. Let
i>1
. Assume that the order of
(g_{i}H)C
is
\alpha^{k}
for some
k<i
. Since
(g_{i}H)^{\alpha^{k}}\in C=gp_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}(aH)
, there is a
\mu\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that
(g_{i}H)^{\alpha^{k}}=(aH)^{\mu}
. Then

aH=(g_{i}H)^{\alpha^{i}}=\left((g_{i}H)^{\alpha^{k}}\right)^{\alpha^{i-k}}=(aH)^{\mu\alpha^{i-k}}.

Hence,
(aH)^{\mu\alpha^{i-k}-1}=H
. Since
aH
is of infinite order,
\mu\alpha^{i-k}-1=0
, which is false. Thus, there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that

aH\notin\overline{G}^{\alpha^{r}}
, as claimed.

It is clear that
\overline{G}^{\alpha^{r}}
is
\omega^{\prime}
-isolated in
\overline{G}
, and that
(aH)\overline{G}^{\alpha^{r}}
has order a power of
\alpha
in
\overline{G}/\overline{G}^{\alpha^{r}}
. Proceeding as in Case 2, we can find a group
L
in
\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}
and a
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-homomorphism from
\overline{G}
onto
L
that maps
aH
non-trivially. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\omega
be a set of primes in
\mathbb{Q}[x].

\mathbb{Q}[x]
-subgroup and
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-quotient of an
\mathcal{A}_{\omega}

\mathcal{N}_{\omega}

\mathcal{A}_{\omega}
-group (
\mathcal{N}_{\omega}
-group).

Proof.

(i) Let

Suppose

and choose

\mathbb{Q}[x]

(ii) Now suppose that

\mathbb{Q}[x]

.

Proposition 3.12

Let
\omega

-member, then all

\mu
be an

-member and denote the set of monic prime divisors of

Theorem 3.14

\omega
be a set of primes in
\mathbb{Q}[x]
and

\mathbb{Q}[x]
-torsion element of

-torsion if and only if

is residually
\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}.

Suppose

\mathcal{FT_{\omega}},

\mathbb{Q}[x]

and a
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-homomorphism
\varphi
of

onto

such that

there exists

such that

Hence,

Since

is
\omega
-torsion,

\omega
-member. Therefore,
\tau(G)
is
\omega
-torsion.

is an
\omega

•

If

\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}.

•

Assume that

has class

\omega
-restricted nilpotent

-powered groups of smaller class. Let

We claim that there exists a nilpotent
\mathbb{Q}[x]

\mathbb{Q}[x]

\varphi

\mathbb{Q}[x]

\varphi

is
\omega
-torsion since
\tau(G)
is
\omega
-torsion. By Proposition

of

\mathbb{Q}[x]
-homomorphisms, then
\varphi=\varphi_{2}\circ\varphi_{1}
satisfies the claim.

∎

We are now prepared to prove the following:

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
\omega
be a set of primes in
\mathbb{Q}[x].
Every
\omega
-restricted nilpotent
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-powered group has property
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}
for its family of normal
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-subgroups.

Proof.

Let
G\in\mathcal{N}_{\omega}
and assume that
H
is an
\omega^{\prime}
-isolated normal
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-subgroup of
G.
By Lemma
2.13
,
G/H
is
\omega^{\prime}
-torsion-free. Since
G\in\mathcal{N}_{\omega},
we have
G/H\in\mathcal{N}_{\omega}
by Proposition
3.8
. By Theorem
3.14
,
G/H
is residually
\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}
because
\tau(G/H)
is
\omega
-torsion. And so,
H
is
\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}
-separable in
G
by Lemma
3.1
.
∎

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
\omega
be a set of primes in
\mathbb{Q}[x].
If
G
is a
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-torsion-free nilpotent
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-powered group and has property
\mathcal{G}_{\omega},
then each
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-factor group
\zeta_{i+1}G/\zeta_{i}G
of its upper central
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-series has property
\mathcal{G}_{\omega}.

By Theorem
3.4
,
\zeta_{i+1}G/\zeta_{i}G
also satisfies Condition (
B_{\omega}
).

Proof.

We begin by observing that
\zeta_{i}G
is
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-isolated in
G
because
G
is
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-torsion-free (see
[
10
]
). If
H\unrhd_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}\zeta_{i}G
for some
H\leq_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}G,
then it easily verified that
H
is
\omega^{\prime}
-isolated in
G
whenever
H/\zeta_{i}G
is an
\omega^{\prime}
-isolated
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-subgroup in
\zeta_{i+1}G/\zeta_{i}G.

We claim that
H/\zeta_{i}G
is
\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}
-separable in
\zeta_{i+1}G/\zeta_{i}G.
By hypothesis, we know that
H
is
\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}
-separable in
G.
Thus, for each
g\in\zeta_{i+1}G\setminus H,
there exists a normal
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-subgroup
N
of
G
(which depends on
g
) such that
G/N\in\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}
and
g\notin HN.

It is clear that

\frac{N\zeta_{i}G\cap\zeta_{i+1}G}{\zeta_{i}G}\unlhd_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}\frac{\zeta_{i+1}G}{\zeta_{i}G}\hbox{ \ \ and \ \ }g\zeta_{i}G\notin\frac{H}{\zeta_{i}G}\frac{(N\zeta_{i}G\cap\zeta_{i+1}G)}{\zeta_{i}G}.

Using the
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-isomorphism theorems, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{i+1}G/\zeta_{i}G}{(N\zeta_{i}G\cap\zeta_{i+1}G)/\zeta_{i}G}

\displaystyle\cong_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{i+1}G}{N\zeta_{i}G\cap\zeta_{i+1}G}\cong_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}\frac{\zeta_{i+1}G(N\zeta_{i}G)}{N\zeta_{i}G}

\displaystyle\cong_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}

\displaystyle\frac{N\zeta_{i+1}G/N}{N\zeta_{i}G/N}\leq_{\mathbb{Q}[x]}\frac{G/N}{N\zeta_{i}G/N}.

Thus,
(\zeta_{i+1}G/\zeta_{i}G)/((N\zeta_{i}G\cap\zeta_{i+1}G)/\zeta_{i}G)
is
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-isomorphic to a
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-subgroup of a
\mathbb{Q}[x]
-quotient of
G/N
and, thus, finite
\omega
-type by Corollary
2.3
. Since
g
was chosen arbitrarily,
H/\zeta_{i}G
is
\mathcal{FT_{\omega}}
-separable in
\zeta_{i+1}G/\zeta_{i}G.
This proves the claim and completes the proof.
∎

An Algebraic Treatment of Recursion
Rob van Glabbeek

Abstract
I review the three principal methods to assign meaning to recursion in process algebra: the denotational, the operational and the algebraic approach, and I extend the latter to unguarded recursion.



Jan Bergstra has put his mark on theoretical computer science by a consistent stream of original
ideas, controversial opinions, and novel approaches. He sometimes reorganised the arena, enabling
others to follow. I, for one, might never have entered computer science if it wasn’t for Jan’s
support and encouragement, and will never forget the team spirit in the early days of process

th

birthday and retirement.

1
Process Algebra

In process algebra, processes are often modelled as closed terms of
single-sorted specification languages.

Definition 1.1
.

signatures
Let
{\it Var}
be a set of variables.
A signature is a set of pairs
(f,n)
of a function symbol
f\notin{\it Var}
and an arity
n\in\mathbbm{N}
.
The set
\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)
of terms over a signature
\Sigma
is generated by:

•

{\it Var}\subseteq\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)
,

•

if
(f,n)\in\Sigma
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\in\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)
then

f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)
,

•

If
V_{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq{\it Var}
,
\mathcal{S}:V_{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)
and
X\in V_{\mathcal{S}}
,
then
\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!X|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}$}\in\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)
.

A function
\mathcal{S}
as appears in the last clause is called a recursive specification. A
recursive specification
\mathcal{S}
is often displayed as
\{X\mathbin{=}\mathcal{S}_{\!X\!}\mid X\mathbin{\in}V_{\!\mathcal{S}\!}\}
.
An occurrence of a variable
y
in a term
t
is free if it does not
occur in a subterm of the form
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!X|\mathcal{S}\!\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
with
y\mathbin{\in}V_{\!\mathcal{S}\!\!}
. A term
is closed if it contains no free occurrences of variables.

The
semantics
of such a language is a function
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\_\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}:\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)\rightarrow(\mathbbm{D}^{\it Var}\rightarrow\mathbbm{D})
.
It assigns to every term
t\in\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)
its meaning
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,t\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}\in\mathbbm{D}^{\it Var}\!\rightarrow\mathbbm{D}
.
The meaning of a closed term is a
value
chosen from a class of values
\mathbbm{D}
, called a
domain
.
The meaning of an open term is a
{\it Var}\!
-ary operator
on
\mathbbm{D}
: a function of
type
\mathbbm{D}^{\it Var}\!\rightarrow\mathbbm{D}
. It associates a value
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,t\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)\mathbin{\in}\mathbbm{D}
to
t
that
depends on the choice of a
valuation

\rho\!:{\it Var}\rightarrow\mathbbm{D}
.

Sometimes, only a subset of
\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)
is given a semantics, for instance by restricting to terms
satisfying a syntactic criterion of
guardedness
.

Another approach lacks the recursion construct itself, but declares a single recursive specification
\mathcal{S}:V_{\mathcal{S}}\rightarrow\mathbbm{T}(\Sigma)

for the entire language
[
5
]
. A term
t
in such a language can be seen as a the term
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!t|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
,
obtained from
t
by substituting, for each
Y\in V_{\mathcal{S}}
,
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!Y|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
for each occurrence of
Y
.
Conversely, each term in the general language of Definition
can be converted into the form

\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!t|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
with
t
and
\mathcal{S}
recursion-free.

2
Denotational, Operational and Algebraic Semantics

The standard (denotational) semantics assigns to each function
(f,n)\in\Sigma

an
n
-ary operator
f_{n}{D}:\mathbbm{D}^{n}\!\rightarrow\mathbbm{D}
. The semantics of a recursion-free expression
t

is then given by

•

\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,X\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)=\rho(X)
for
X\in{\it Var}
, and

•

\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,f(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)=f_{n}^{\mathbbm{D}}(\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,t_{1}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho),\dots,\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,t_{n}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho))

for
(f,n)\in\Sigma
.

Three approaches appear in the literature to give semantics to recursion.

The
denotational
approach
[
3
]
recognises
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\mathcal{S}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}
as having type

\mathbbm{D}^{{\it Var}\setminus V_{\mathcal{S}}}\!\rightarrow(\mathbbm{D}^{V_{\mathcal{S}}}\!\rightarrow\mathbbm{D}^{V_{\mathcal{S}}})
and
defines
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!X|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}$}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)
for
\rho\in\mathbbm{D}^{{\it Var}\setminus V_{\mathcal{S}}}

to be the
X
-component of the least fixed point of
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\mathcal{S}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)
.
For this least fixed point to exists, either
\mathbbm{D}
, equipped with a suitable preorder
\sqsubseteq
,
needs to be a complete lattice, with the operators
f{D}
monotonic,
or
(\mathbbm{D},\sqsubseteq)
be a c.p.o., with the
f{D}
continuous,
or
\mathbbm{D}
be a complete metric space, with the
f{D}
contracting (or some variation on this theme).

The
operational
approach
[
5
]
is based on a set of inference rules that derive a collection of
(labelled) transitions between closed terms. The semantic domain is now the collection
\mathbbm{G}

of
process graphs

(S,T,I)
, with
S
a set of states,
T
a set of transitions between
states, and
I\in S
an initial state, possibly subject to some cardinality restrictions.
The operational semantics
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,P\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}
of a closed term
P
takes
S
to be the set of closed terms,

I=P
, and
T
the derivable transitions. The semantics of open terms can be dealt with by encoding
the process graphs
\rho(X)
for
X\in{\it Var}
as constants in an appropriate extension of the process
algebra. This approach covers the meaning of recursion constructs too.

Let
guardedness
be a criterion on recursive specifications, such that if
\mathcal{S}
is guarded then
is has a unique solution, meaning that if
\rho_{i}
for
i=1,2
are valuations with

\rho_{1}(Z)=\rho_{2}(Z)
for all
Z\in{\it Var}\setminus V_{\mathcal{S}}
, and

\rho_{i}(X)=\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\mathcal{S}_{X}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho_{i})
for all
X\in V_{\mathcal{S}}
,
then
\rho_{1}(X)=\rho_{2}(X)
for all
X\in V_{\mathcal{S}}
.
The
algebraic
approach
[
2
]
yields a semantics for terms with guarded recursion only,
where
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!X|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}$}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)
for
\rho\in\mathbbm{D}^{{\it Var}\setminus V_{\mathcal{S}}}

is the
X
-component of the unique solution of
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\mathcal{S}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)
.

3
Extending the Algebraic Approach to Unguarded Recursion

In
[
4
]
I proposed an extension of the algebraic approach to unguarded recursion.
An expression
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!X|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
is seen as a kind of variable, only ranging over the solutions of
\mathcal{S}\!\!
.
Taking for example ACP
[
2
]
, interpreted in a domain of process graphs modulo strong
bisimilarity
[
2
]
,
then
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!X|X=aX\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
is a case of guarded recursion and denotes a specific process, namely an

a
-loop. On the other hand,
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!X|X=X\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
is an unguarded recursion, and seen a variable
ranging over all processes, just like
X
itself. In between,
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!X|X=X+aX\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
is a case of
unguarded recursion, and seen as a variable ranging over all processes of the form
a^{*}P
.

To avoid ambiguity in deciding when two, almost identical, processes
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!X|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
denote the same
variable or different ones, here I formalise this approach only for terms
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!t|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}
where no
further recursion occurs in
t
or
\mathcal{S}
, thus following the second approach of Section
1
.

A valuation
\rho:{\it Var}\rightarrow\mathbbm{D}
is
compatible
with a recursive specification
\mathcal{S}

iff
\rho(Y)=\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\mathcal{S}_{Y}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)
for all
Y\in V_{\mathcal{S}}
.
The meaning
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,t\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}
of a recursion-free term
t
in the context of a global recursive
specification
\mathcal{S}
is now a function into
\mathbbm{D}
from the set of compatible valuations only.
It is obtained from the semantics of
t
from Section
2
by restricting
{\it dom}(\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,t\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$})
to the
compatible valuations.

In particular, an equation
t=u
holds under this semantics iff

\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,t\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)=\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,u\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)
for all valuations
\rho
compatible with
\mathcal{S}
.
Hence it is equivalent to the conditional equation

\left(\bigwedge_{X\in V_{\mathcal{S}}}X=\mathcal{S}_{X}\right)\Rightarrow t=u
.

The laws of process algebra remain valid in this approach, including the
congruence property for recursion: if
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\mathcal{S}_{X}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)=\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{X}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}(\rho)
for
all
valuations

\rho
, and all
X\in V_{\mathcal{S}}=V_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}
then
\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!t|\mathcal{S}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}$}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}=\mbox{$[\hskip-1.6pt[$}\,\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$/$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$\backslash$}}}\!\!t|\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\!\!\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\tiny$\backslash$}}}{{\raisebox{-1.29167pt}[1.29167pt]{\tiny$/$}}}$}\,\mbox{$]\hskip-1.6pt]$}
.

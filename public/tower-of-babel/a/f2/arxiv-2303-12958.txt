On the Deformation Theory of 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -Coalgebras
Florian Riedel

Abstract
. We introduce a notion of formally étale 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -coalgebras and show that they admit essentially unique, functorial lifts along square zero extensions of 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -rings. Using this, we show that for a perfect 𝔽 p \F_{p} -algebra k k , Weil restriction along the augmentation 𝕎 ⁡ ( k ) → k \mathbb{W}(k)\to k induces a fully faithful functor from formally étale, connective 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -coalgebras in k k -modules to connective 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -coalgebras in p p -complete modules over the spherical Witt vectors 𝕎 ⁡ ( k ) \mathbb{W}(k) . Finally, we prove that for any connected space X X , the k k -homology k ⁡ [ X ] k[X] is a formally étale 𝔼 ∞ \mathbb{E}_{\infty} -coalgebra in k k -modules. This shows that 𝕎 ⁡ ( k ) ​ [ X ] p ∧ \mathbb{W}(k)[X]^{\wedge}_{p} can be recovered as the essentially unique lift of k ⁡ [ X ] k[X] to a connective coalgebra in p p -complete 𝕎 ⁡ ( k ) \mathbb{W}(k) -modules.



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we systematically study deformation problems of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebras.
The key insight is that there is a well behaved class of coalgebras for which such deformation
problems are uniquely solvable, which we call
formally étale
coalgebras.
Moreover, we show that there is a Witt vector style functor which is defined for all coalgebras
which produces unique lifts when restricted to formally étale coalgebras.

1.1.
Overview

Let
X
be a space and throughout fix a prime
p
. We want to compare two different invariants
associated to
X
, namely the
\F_{p}
-homology
\F_{p}[X]
and the
p
-completed suspension
spectrum
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}X^{\wedge}_{p}=\mathbb{S}[X]^{\wedge}_{p}
. The
\F_{p}
-chains can be recovered from
the spherical chains via the base change

\mathbb{S}[X]^{\wedge}_{p}\otimes\F_{p}\simeq\F_{p}[X].

However, on the level of spectra there is in general no canonical way to go back.
For example, since any module over a field is free, we have that

\F_{2}[\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}]\simeq\F_{2}\oplus\Sigma^{2}\F_{2}
, but the suspension spectrum

\mathbb{S}[\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}]^{\wedge}_{2}\simeq(\Sigma\mathbb{S}/2)^{\wedge}_{2}
is
not
free as module over

\mathbb{S}^{\wedge}_{2}
. Thus, we have two different lifts of
\F_{2}[\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}]
to
\mathbb{S}_{2}^{\wedge}
,
namely
\mathbb{S}[\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{2}]^{\wedge}_{2}
s and
\mathbb{S}_{2}^{\wedge}\oplus\Sigma^{2}\mathbb{S}_{2}^{\wedge}
.
We can rigidify this situation by considering additional structure on the homology of
X
.
For any
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
R
, the assignment
X\mapsto R[X]
canonically refines to a functor

\mathcal{S}\to\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{R})
where the right hand side is the category of

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebras in
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}
and the coalgebra structure on
R[X]
is induced
by the diagonal map
X\to X\times X
. Our main result is that, if
X
is connected, we can recover
the coalgebra
\mathbb{S}[X]^{\wedge}_{p}
from the coalgebra
\F_{p}[X]
in a functorial way.
To state this in the correct generality, let us fix some notation.
Let
k
be a perfect
\F_{p}
-algebra and denote by
\mathbb{W}(k)
the spherical
Witt vectors of
k
as in
[
Lur18a
]
. For an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
R
denote by
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\wedge}

the category of
p
-complete
R
-modules equipped with the symmetric monoidal structure given by
the
p
-completed tensor product.

Theorem A
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{S}
be either connected or compact. The space of lifts of

k[X]\in\cCAlg(\Mod_{k})
to a coalgebra in
p
-complete, connective
\mathbb{W}(k)
-modules
is contractible and the unique point is given by
\mathbb{W}(k)[X]^{\wedge}_{p}
.
Moreover, for any
A\in\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{\mathbb{W}(k)}^{\wedge,\mathrm{cn}})

the base change along the map
\mathbb{W}(k)\to k
induces an equivalence

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{W}(k)}^{\wedge})}(A,\mathbb{W}(k)[X]^{\wedge}_{p})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{k})}(A\otimes k,k[X]).

Since
\mathbb{W}(k)
is a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring, and thus a limit of square zero extensions of

\pi_{0}\mathbb{W}(k)
, which is in turn a limit of square zero extensions of
\pi_{0}\mathbb{W}(k)/p\simeq k
,
we approach Theorem
A
by first understanding how to lift coalgebras along square zero
extensions of connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings. The deformation theory of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebras
has thus far mainly been investigated by Lurie in
[
Lur18a
]
by embedding the category of

flat
coalgebras fully faithfully into the category of sheaves on
\CAlg(\Sp)^{\cn}
.
This provides no insight towards approaching Theorem
A
,
as the
k
-homology of a space is never flat over
k
, unless the space is discrete.
Our approach is instead the following: Suppose we have any map of connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings

q:R^{\prime}\to R
. Colimits of coalgebras are computed underlying and the base change
q^{\ast}

commutes with colimits and preserves connective modules. Moreover, the category
\cCAlg(\mathcal{C})

is presentable whenever
\mathcal{C}
is a presentably symmetric monoidal category.
Thus, the adjoint functor theorem provides us with an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{q^{\ast}:\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{R^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{cn}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}}):q_{\ast}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

We call
q_{\ast}
the (connective)
Weil restriction
along the map
q:R^{\prime}\to R

1
1

1

Such an adjunction exists without the connectivity assumptions, which we call

non-connective
Weil restriction. See Example
2.12
for a more in-depth discussion.
.
Note that, since restriction of scalars is only
lax
monoidal, an
R
-coalgebra
A

has no underlying
R^{\prime}
-coalgebra, and so the ”restriction” of
A
along
q
via the right
adjoint
q_{\ast}
is something of interest. These right adjoints are hard to describe in
general, but turn out to be essential for the deformation theory of coalgebras.
More precisely, in Definition
1.2
we describe a full subcategory of

\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{R})^{\mathrm{cn}}
on which the unit map
q^{\ast}q_{\ast}\to\id
is an
equivalence for any square zero extension
R^{\prime}\to R
.
We call these coalgebras
formally étale
and denote the subcategory
with the superscript
({-})^{\mathrm{f\acute{e}t}}
. We show that Weil restriction
of formally étale coalgebras along
\mathbb{W}(k)\to k
behaves like a coalgebraic version
of the spherical Witt vector construction.

Theorem B
.

Denote by
q:\mathbb{W}(k)\to k
the natural augmentation.
Weil restriction along
q
induces a fully faithful functor

\mathcal{W}:\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{k}^{\mathrm{cn}})^{\mathrm{\fet}}\to\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{\mathbb{W}(k)}^{\cn,\wedge})^{\fet}.

Moreover,
\mathcal{W}(A)
is, up to contractible choice, the unique coalgebra in connective

p
-complete
\mathbb{W}(k)
-modules with an equivalence
\mathcal{W}(A)\otimes k\simeq A
.

This is proven inductively, by first lifting along the
p
-completion tower from
k
to

\pi_{0}\W(k)
and then along the Postnikov tower to
\W(k)
.
Theorem
A
is immediate from Theorem
B
once we know that
the
k
-homology of a space is formally étale. Let us make precise the notion of formally
étale coalgebra before we proceed.

Construction 1.1
.

Let
R
be a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring,
M
a connective
R
-module and denote by

e:R\to R\oplus M
the 0-section of the split square zero extension. For any

A\in\cCAlg(\Mod_{R}^{\cn})
we define the
universal
M
-deformation coalgebra
of
A

as the Weil restriction

\Omega^{\infty}_{A}M:=e_{\ast}e^{\ast}A\in\cCAlg(\Mod_{R}^{\cn}).

From the adjunction we get a natural unit map
\varepsilon:A\to\Omega^{\infty}_{A}M
. Moreover, the map

R\oplus M\to R
induces a section
\pi:\Omega^{\infty}_{A}M\to A
.

We show that deformation problems involving
A
are equivalent to giving lifts of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Omega^{\infty}_{A}M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

If we ask that these be uniquely solvable for all
M
and
B
, the Yoneda lemma leads us to write
down our main definition.

Definition 1.2
.

A coalgebra
A\in\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{R}^{\cn})
is called
formally étale
if the unit map

\varepsilon:A\to\Omega^{\infty}_{A}M
is an equivalence for all
M\in\Mod_{R}^{\cn}
.
We denote by
\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{R}^{\cn})^{\fet}
the full subcategory
spanned by the formally étale coalgebras.

Note that for an algebra
S\in\CAlg(\Mod_{R}^{\cn})
, trying to impose the analogous
criterion would be asking that the inclusion
S\to S\oplus S\otimes_{R}M
is an equivalence,
which is only true if
S=0
. Hence, this may not seem like a reasonable definition at first glance.
To reassure the reader that we are not only talking about the trivial coalgebra, we prove the
following general claim.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
A\in\cCAlg(\Mod_{R}^{\cn})
be dualizable such that for the
R
-linear dual

A^{\vee}\in\CAlg(\Mod_{R})
the relative cotangent complex
L_{A^{\vee}/R}
vanishes.
Then
A
is formally étale.

This allows us to prove Theorem
A
for compact spaces. To access arbitrary connected
spaces, the crucial step is to show that the homology of Eilenberg-MacLane spaces is formally étale.
To this end, we utilize recent work of Bachmann–Burklund. In
[
BB24
]
they show that for any field

k
of characteristic
p
and any
V\in\Mod_{\F_{p}}^{\heartsuit}
, we have for
n\geq 0
pullback
squares in
\cCAlg(\Mod_{k})
of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{k[\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{n}V]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{k}(\Sigma^{n}k\otimes V)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{C_{k}(\Sigma^{n}k\otimes V)}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F-1}

Here
C_{k}
denote the cofree coalgebra and
F-1
is the Artin –Schreier map.
This is the dual picture to the pushout square on cohomology of Mandell in
[
Man01
]
, which
was utilized by Lurie in
[
Lur11
]
to prove that the
\F_{p}
-cohomology of an arbitrary space
is formally étale. The pullback diagram tells us that homology of Eilenberg –MacLane spaces
is cofree up to one ”co-relation”. Since Weil restriction preserves cofree coalgebras,
we show that the co-relation
F=1
forces the coalgebra
k[\Omega^{\infty}\Sigma^{n}V]
to
be formally étale, which allows us to prove our third theorem.

Theorem C
.

Let
X\in\mathcal{S}
be either connected or compact. Then for any
\F_{p}
-algebra
R
, the
R
-homology

R[X]\in\cCAlg(\Mod_{R}^{\cn})
is formally étale.

We strongly believe, but are unable to prove at this time, that the connectivity assumption
can be dropped for arbitrary spaces, not just compact ones.
Recall that a space is called
p
-complete
if it is local with respect to the functor

\F_{p}[{-}]:\mathcal{S}\to\Mod_{\F_{p}}
and denote by
\mathcal{S}_{p}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
the full
subcategory spanned by the
p
-complete spaces. Moreover, call a space
nilpotent

if is connected and
\pi_{1}
is a nilpotent group which acts nilpotently on the higher homotopy
groups. Write
\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathrm{nilp}}
for the category of
p
-complete nilpotent spaces.
Combining Theorem
A
with
[
BB24
, Theorem 1.2]

we also deduce the following spherical lift of Mandells theorem.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
k
be a perfect, separably closed field of characteristic
p
with spherical
Witt vectors
\W(k)
. The
p
-complete
\W(k)
-homology functor

\mathcal{S}_{p}^{\mathrm{nilp}}\to\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod^{\wedge}_{\mathbb{W}(k)})\quad X\mapsto\mathbb{W}(k)[X]^{\wedge}_{p}

is fully faithful. In particular, for any nilpotent space
X
we have a natural equivalence

X^{\wedge}_{p}\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{cCAlg}_{\mathbb{W}(k)}^{\wedge}}(\mathbb{W}(k),\mathbb{W}(k)[X]^{\wedge}_{p}).

It would be pleasant if we could replace
\W(k)
by
\mathbb{S}_{p}^{\wedge}
in Corollary
1.4
.
On the nose this not possible as we need to keep track of descent data. In upcoming work,
we plan to identify this descent datum with the
coalgebra Frobenius
, a dual notion to the
Tate Frobenius of
[
NS17
]
and construct and embedding of
\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{nilp}}_{p}
into coalgebras

\cCAlg(\Sp^{\wedge}_{p})
equipped with a trivialization of the Frobenius action. This would dualize
work of Yuan in
[
Yua19
]
and allow us to drop more finiteness assumptions on the spaces we input.

Outline

We proceed along the following structure:
In Section 1 we recall the definition and basic properties of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebras
and discuss some relevant coalgebraic right adjoints.
In Section 2 we review the setup of deformation theory developed by Lurie
in
[
Lur12
]
and
[
Lur17
]
. We recall the notions of square zero extensions of

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings and discuss cohesive and nilcomplete functors.
We then define the tangent complex of a cohesive functor and give proofs of some important facts
about its behavior.
In Section 3 we prove that the moduli of coalgebras are cohesive and thus we can analyze them using
the machinery discussed in the previous section. We then introduce formally étale coalgebras
and prove that they can be lifted uniquely and functorially against square zero extensions.
In Section 4 we define spherical and
p
-typical Witt vector style functors and prove that the
homology of any connected space is formally étale.

2.
Coalgebras

In this section, we recall the definition of an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebra
in a symmetric monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
and collect some facts about it.
Colimits of coalgebras are computed underlying and if
\mathcal{C}
is presentable and the tensor
product commutes with colimits in each variable separately, then
\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})
is again
presentable. For a map of connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings
f:R\to S
this lets us introduce the adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathrm{Mod}_{S}^{\mathrm{cn}})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{\ast}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f^{\ast}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

where
f^{\ast}
is the base change and
f_{\ast}
is conjured up by the adjoint functor theorem. Crucially,

f_{\ast}
is
not
induced from the restriction of scalars on the level of module categories.
We then consider the coalgebra structure on the
R
-chains of a space
X
and the algebra structure on
the dual of a coalgebra in a presentably monoidal category.

2.1.
Generalities

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal category. We denote by
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})
the category
of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebras in
\mathcal{C}
. For
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Sp}
we write
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Sp})=\mathrm{CAlg}

and refer to objects of
\mathrm{CAlg}
simply as
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings.

Proposition 2.2

(Lurie)
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\otimes)
be a symmetric monoidal category, then the following statements hold:

(1)

The forgetful functor
U:\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
is conservative and
commutes with limits.

(2)

The coproduct of two algebras

is given by the tensor

.

(3)

If
\mathcal{C}
is presentable and
{-}\otimes{-}
commutes with colimits in both variables
separately, then
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})
is presentable as well.

Proof.

The first claim is a combination of
[
Lur17
, Lemma 3.2.2.6]
and
[

.
The second is shown in
[

[

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal category. The opposite category
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
inherits
a natural symmetric monoidal structure and we define the category of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebras
in
\mathcal{C}
as

\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C}):=\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{op}}.

Remark 2.4
.

In particular, a coalgebra
A\in\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})
comes equipped with the datum of a
“coherently commutative” comultiplication and counit maps

\displaystyle\Delta_{A}:A

\displaystyle\to(A\otimes A)^{h\Sigma_{2}}

\displaystyle\eta:A

\displaystyle\to 1_{\mathcal{C}},

where
1_{\mathcal{C}}
denotes the unit of
\mathcal{C}
. Note that, in general, there is no way
to describe
\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})
as a category of algebras in some suitable category
\mathcal{D}
.
Nonetheless, Proposition
2.2
immediately implies the following:

(1)

The forgetful functor
U:\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
is conservative and commutes with colimits.

(2)

The product of two coalgebras
R,S\in\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})
is given by
R\otimes S
.

However, we cannot deduce presentability this way since the opposite of a presentable category is
almost never presentable.

Proposition 2.5

(Lurie)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal category such that
{-}\otimes{-}
commutes with
colimits in each variable separately and
\mathcal{C}
is presentable. Then, the category

\mathrm{cCAlg}({\mathcal{C}})
is presentable.

Proof.

This is
[
Lur18
, Corollary 3.1.4.]
.
∎

This can be seen as an analogue of the classical theorem by Sweedler that every coalgebra
in the 1-category of vector spaces over a field is a filtered colimit of its finite dimensional
sub-coalgebras, see
[
Swe69
]
. However, unlike in the classical situation, if
\mathcal{C}

is
\kappa
-presentable, we only deduce that
\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})
is
\tau
-presentable for some

\tau\geq\kappa
. This is one of the main defects the category of coalgebras has over that
of algebras.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be as in Proposition
2.5
. Since by Remark
2.4
the
forgetful functor
U:\cCAlg(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
commutes with colimits, it admits
a right adjoint

C:\mathcal{C}\to\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}).

For any

we call

the
cofree coalgebra
on

.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\otimes}
denote the (large) category of symmetric monoidal
categories and monoidal functors. Then the functors

\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}\quad\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}),

\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\otimes}\to\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}\quad\mathcal{C}\mapsto\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})

commute with limits.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
the functor
\mathrm{CAlg}({-})
factors through the (large) category

\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\amalg}
of categories which admit finite coproducts and functors which
preserve finite coproducts. As such it admits a left adjoint which equips
\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\amalg}
with the
cocartesian monoidal structure. Moreover, the inclusion
\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\amalg}\xhookrightarrow{}\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}

admits a left adjoint which takes a category
\mathcal{C}
to the free finite coproduct completion, namely
the full subcategory of
\mathrm{P}(\mathcal{C})
spanned by finite coproducts of representables.
Thus, both functors commute with limits, and so the composition
\CAlg({-})
does as well.

For the second functor, we simply observe that it is given by the composition

\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\otimes}\xrightarrow{({-})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}^{\otimes}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{CAlg({-})}}\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty}\xrightarrow{({-})^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{Cat}_{\infty},

which immediately implies the claim, since taking the opposite category is an involution, i.e. an
equivalence of categories and thus commutes with limits.
∎

2.2.
Coalgebraic right adjoints and duality

Definition 2.8

.

Let

and
\mathcal{D}

of

.

Construction 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathrm{Pr^{L}})
be stable and equipped with a compatible

Then
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}

Since connective coalgebras form a full subcategory, it is true that
\tau_{\geq 0}(A)\simeq A

Moreover, if we have a map

in

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{D})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0})}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\geq 0}^{c}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau^{c}_{\geq 0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\ast}}

Note that, for

it necessarily agrees with

.

Example 2.12

(Weil restriction)
.

Let

be a map of connective

-rings. The base

commutes with colimits and preserves connective objects. Thus the restriction to connective modules

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f^{\ast}:\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{R})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{S}):f_{!}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f^{\ast}:\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{S}^{\mathrm{cn}}):f_{\ast}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

as the

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cCAlg(\Mod_{R})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\cCAlg(\Mod_{S})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Mod_{R}}}

Example 2.13

(Duality)
.

For any
\mathcal{C}\in\CAlg(\mathrm{Pr^{L}})
and
X\in\mathcal{C}
,
the functor

commutes with colimits and thus admits a right adjoint

\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,{-})
.
This assignment defines a functor
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})
, and we denote
its adjoint as

\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{C}}({-},{-}):\mathcal{C}^{{}^{\mathrm{op}}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}.

The target of
({-})^{\vee}:=\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{C}}({-},\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{C}}):\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}

is not presentable, so this is not a map in
\CAlg(\mathrm{Pr^{L}})
. However, it still admits a coalgebraic
right adjoint via the following construction. The functor
\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{C}}({-},{-})
is lax monoidal with

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}
, defined by

(A,B)\otimes_{\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}}(C,D):=(A\otimes C,B\otimes D).

Thus, it refines to a functor

\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{C}}({-},{-}):\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}).

In particular, for each

we get a functor

\displaystyle\mathrm{map}_{\mathcal{C}}({-},R):\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})=\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}})^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})^{\mathrm{op}}.

Since colimits of coalgebras and limits of algebras are computed underlying, the assignment

takes colimits of coalgebras to limits of algebras. By the adjoint functor theorem, we get an adjunction.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{({-})^{\vee}:\cCAlg(\mathcal{C})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}):({-})^{\circ}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

For

and

. We call

the
dual algebra

of
A
and
R^{\circ}
the
pre-dual coalgebra
of
R
.

If the underlying object of

is dualizable, then the dual

also inherits a natural
coalgebra structure. In fact this construction agrees with
R^{\circ}
as we will see in Corollary

.

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal category and denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{dual}}
the full subcategory
spanned by the dualizable objects. The functor

\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{dual}})^{\mathrm{op}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{dual}})\quad A\mapsto A^{\vee}

is an equivalence of categories with inverse taking

to the dual

with the induced coalgebra structure.

This is immediate from
[

, Proposition 3.2.4]
.
∎

For the next lemma we employ the following terminology:

For a functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
we say that
A\in\mathcal{C}
is
F
-local, if the natural
transformation
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(A,{-})\to\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{D}}(F(A),F({-}))

is an equivalence.

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
be symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories and
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a lax symmetric
monoidal functor. Suppose we have
R\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})
such that each tensor power

R^{\otimes n}
considered as an object in
\mathcal{C}
is
F
-local. Then the map

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg(\mathcal{C})}}(R,S)\xrightarrow{F}\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{D})}(F(R),F(S))

is an equivalence.

Proof.

Since
F
is lax symmetric monoidal, it induces a map of
\infty
-operads

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

which takes any
(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})\in\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}_{\langle n\rangle}
to the point

(F(A_{1}),\dots,F(A_{n}))\in\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}_{\langle n\rangle}
. A commutative algebra structure on an object

R\in\mathcal{C}
is precisely given by a a section
s_{R}
of
p
which takes
\langle n\rangle
to

(R,\dots,R)\in\mathcal{C}_{\langle n\rangle}
and maps inert morphisms to inert morphisms. Let

\varphi:\langle n\rangle\to\langle m\rangle
be a morphism in
\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}
and denote by
p_{i}:\langle n\rangle\to\langle 1\rangle
the unique inert
map which sends
i\mapsto 1
. For each
i
we have a factorization

\langle n\rangle\xrightarrow{\psi_{i}}\langle k_{i}\rangle\xrightarrow{\pi_{i}}\langle 1\rangle

of
p_{i}\circ\varphi
into an inert map
\psi_{i}
and an active map
\pi_{i}
.
Then for each
B=(B_{1},\dots B_{m})\in\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
we get equivalences

\displaystyle\mathrm{Map}^{\varphi}_{\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}}(s_{R}(\langle n\rangle),B)

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{i=1,\dots,m}\mathrm{Map}^{p_{i}\circ\varphi}_{\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}}((R,\dots,R),B_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{i=1,\dots,m}\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(R^{\otimes k_{i}},B_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{i=1,\dots,m}\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{D}}(F(R)^{\otimes k_{i}},F(B_{i}))

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{i=1,\dots m}\mathrm{Map}^{p_{i}\circ\varphi}_{\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}}((f\circ s_{R})(\langle n\rangle),B_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathrm{Map}_{\mathcal{D}^{\otimes}}^{\varphi}((f\circ s_{R})(\langle n\rangle),B),

hence each value of
s_{R}:\mathrm{Fin}_{\ast}\to\mathcal{C}^{\otimes}
is
f
-local, and thus
s_{R}

is local with respect to the functor
f_{\ast}:\Gamma(p)\to\Gamma(q)
. Since
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})

and
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{D})
are full subcategories of
\Gamma(p)
and
\Gamma(q)
respectively, the
claim follows.
∎

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a symmetric monoidal category and
A,B\in\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})
with
A
dualizable.
The natural map

\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})}(B,A)\to\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})}(A^{\vee},B^{\vee})

is an equivalence. In particular, we have that
(A^{\vee})^{\circ}\simeq A
.

Proof.

This is immediate by applying Lemma
2.15
to the duality functor

({-})^{\vee}:\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
since dualizable objects are closed under tensor products.
∎

We now review why the homology of a space carries an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebra structure. The following
lemma in particular shows that every

admits a unique coalgebra structure with respect
to the cartesian product induced from the diagonal map

.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category which admits finite products equipped with the cartesian monoidal structure.
Then the forgetful functor
\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

By
[
Lur17
, Corollary 2.4.4.10.]
the map
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}})\to\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
is an equivalence, hence
the claim follows by applying

.
∎

The following generalization of the Eilenberg –Zilber Theorem equips every “generalized suspension”
of a space with the structure of an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-coalgebra.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
a presentably symmetric monoidal category. The functor

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{C}\qquad X\mapsto 1_{\mathcal{C}}[X]

which sends a space

to the colimit over the constant diagram

is symmetric monoidal with respect to the cartesian monoidal structure on
\mathcal{S}
.
In particular, this induces a functor

\mathcal{S}\simeq\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{S})\to\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C})\quad X\mapsto 1_{\mathcal{C}}[X]

Proof.

Since
{-}\otimes{-}
commutes with colimits in both variables
separably by assumption, we have that:

\displaystyle(\colim_{X}1_{\mathcal{C}})\otimes(\colim_{Y}1_{\mathcal{C}})\simeq\colim_{X}\colim_{Y}(\underbrace{1_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{C}}}_{\simeq 1_{\mathcal{C}}})\simeq\colim_{X\times Y}1_{\mathcal{C}}.

The second statement then follows from Lemma
2.17
.
∎

Example 2.19
.

By Proposition

, for each

-ring

, the

Hence, the

-homology of a space

-cohomology of

inherits a natural

3.
Review of deformation theory

In this chapter we review the theory of square zero extensions and the deformation theory of functors

X:\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{cn}}\to\mathcal{S}
, as developed in
[
Lur17
]
and
[
Lur12
]
.
Let
R^{\eta}\to R
be a square zero extensions of connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings with fiber

M\in\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}}
. The goal is to understand the fiber

\mathrm{fib}_{A}(X(R^{\eta})\to X(R))
over some point
A\in X(R)
.
The key insight is that
R^{\eta}\to R
is classified by a map into
the split square zero extension
R\oplus M[1]\in\mathrm{cCAlg}_{/R}
.
For well behaved functors
X:\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{cn}}\to\mathcal{S}

the space
X(R^{\eta})
can then be obtained by pulling back along the induced map

X(R)\to X(R\oplus M[1])
. In this case the fiber
\mathrm{fib}_{A}(X(\widetilde{R})\to X(R))
is given a path space in
X(R\oplus M[1])
. Moreover, for each
A\in X(R)
writing

X_{A}^{R\oplus M[n]}:=\mathrm{fib}_{A}(X(R\oplus M[n])\to X(R))

the sequence
\{X_{A}^{R\oplus M[n]}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defines a spectrum

T^{M}_{X_{A}}
which collects the deformation theoretic data into one spectrum
called the
tangent complex
of
X
at the point
A
.

3.1.
Square zero extensions and deformations

Proposition 3.1
.

For every
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
R
there is an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Sp}(\mathrm{CAlg}_{/R})

such that for each
n\geq 0
the functor

\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{Sp}(\mathrm{CAlg}_{/R})\xrightarrow{\Omega^{\infty-n}}\mathrm{CAlg}_{/R}

sends a module
M
to an augmented
R
-algebra whose underlying
R
-module is given by
the direct sum
R\oplus M[n]
.

Proof.

This is
[
Lur17
, Theorem 7.3.4.13]
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

For a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
R
and a connective
R
-module
M
we call

\Omega^{\infty}M=R\oplus M
with the
R
-algebra structure described above the

split square zero extension
of
R
along
M
. If
R
and
M
are discrete, the
multiplication is explicitly given by

(a+m)(b+n):=ab+an+mb,

i.e. we have that
R\oplus R\simeq R[x]/x^{2}
.

Definition 3.3
.

For a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
R
and a connective
R
-module
M
we say that a map
R^{\eta}\to R
is
a
square zero extension
of
R
along
M
if it fits into
a pullback diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R^{\eta}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R\oplus M[1]}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\id,0)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\id,\eta)}

Moreover, we call the mapping space
\mathrm{Map}_{\mathrm{CAlg}_{/R}}(R,R\oplus M)
the
space of
derivations

\eta:R\to M
.

This definition make sense without any connectivity assumptions. However, since our interest
lies primarily in the case where everything is connective, we have chosen to include them
in the definition to avoid awkward terminology.

Remark 3.4
.

Note that, the split square zero extension is precisely the one classified by the zero derivation

R\xrightarrow{(\id,0)}R\oplus M[1].

Moreover, if
p:R^{\eta}\to R
is any square zero extension classified by a derivation

\eta:R\to M[1]
, then by taking fibers we get a commutative diagram with exact columns

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R^{\eta}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R\oplus M[1]}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\id,\eta)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\id,0)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

In particular, the fiber of
f
inherits a natural
R
-module structure, such that the multiplication
map factors as

M\otimes_{R^{\eta}}M\to M\otimes_{R}M\to M

where the second map is induced from the 0-map
M\to R
by applying
{-}_{R}\otimes M
.
Since any map of the form
f_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes f_{n-1}\otimes 0
admits a
\Sigma_{n}
-equivariant
nullhomotopy, we deduce that all coherent multiplication maps

(M^{\otimes n})_{h\Sigma_{n}}\to M

are nullhomotopic. In this sense, square zero extensions of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings are ”coherently” square
zero and admit no nontrivial power operations on the fiber.

Remark 3.5
.

For any connective
R
-module
M
the augmented
R
-algebra
\Omega^{\infty}M=R\oplus M
inherits the
structure of an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-monoid in
\mathrm{CAlg}_{/R}
with delooping given by
R\oplus M[1]
.
Thus, we can think of
R\oplus M[1]
as the classifying object for square zero extensions with
fiber
M
the “universal” derivation being given by the trivial section
R\to R\oplus M[1]
.
From this perspective, a square zero extension is precisely a
R\oplus M
-torsor in
\mathrm{CAlg}_{/R}
.

To make this definition work for us we require a way to check in practice whether a given map
of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings is a square zero extension. This is provided by the following proposition, which
in particular implies that for discrete rings our notion agrees with the classical definition of a
square zero extension.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
R^{\prime}\to R
be a map of connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings with fiber

such that

M\in\mathrm{Sp}_{\geq n}\cap\mathrm{Sp}_{\leq 2n}
and the multiplication map

is nullhomotopic.
Then

is a square zero extension.

Proof.

This is immediate from
[
Lur17
, Theorem 7.4.1.23.]
.
∎

Example 3.7
.

If
R^{\prime}\to R
is a surjective map of ordinary commutative rings with kernel

,
then

is a square zero extension if and only if

.
In particular, for every

\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}/p^{n-1}
is square zero with kernel

.

Example 3.8
.

If

is any connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring, then the map

is a square zero

.

For a functor

and a square zero extension

we want to study the fibers of the map

, i.e. given

we wish
to understand the space of deformations of

to an object
\widetilde{A}\in X(R^{\eta})
.
Notice that, since

and

are connective, the derivation

is surjective on
\pi_{0}
, indeed an isomorphism. This motivates the following definition for
a class of functors which are “well behaved” with regard to square zero extensions.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a category. A functor

is called:

(1)

if for any pullback diagram of

-rings

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X(S^{\prime})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X(R)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X(S)}}

is a pullback. We refer to such pullbacks of

-rings as

.

(2)

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring

the natural map

Example 3.10
.

Let
R
be a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring, then the functor

Construction 3.11
.

Let

be a cohesive functor,

and

. Then we define a functor

We call

of

.

Remark 3.12
.

If
X
is cohesive and nilcomplete, then for every connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
R

and every

we have an equivalence

3.2.
The tangent complex

Definition 3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D}
be
\infty
-categories, then a functor
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is called:

(1)

Reduced
if it preserves the terminal object.

(2)

Excisive
if it takes pushouts to pullbacks.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
X:\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{cn}}\to\mathcal{S}
be cohesive and
A\in X(R)
be an
R
-valued point.
Then the functor given by the composition

\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}}\xrightarrow{\Omega^{\infty}|_{\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}}}}\mathrm{CAlg}_{/R}^{\mathrm{cn}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{X}^{{-}}_{A}}\mathcal{S}

is reduced and excisive.

Proof.

Clearly, we have
\mathrm{X}^{R}_{A}\simeq\ast
, so the functor is reduced.
Since
\mathrm{X}
is cohesive and taking fibers commutes with limits,
the functor
\mathrm{X}^{{-}}_{A}
takes small pullbacks to pullbacks.
Hence, the claim follows from
[
Lur17
, Proposition 1.4.2.13]
by observing that

\Omega^{\infty}|_{\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}}}
sends

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{M[1]}}

to the small pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R\oplus M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{R\oplus M[1]},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\id,0)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\id,0)}

i.e. we have
\Omega\mathrm{X}^{R\oplus M[1]}_{A}\simeq\mathrm{X}_{A}^{R\oplus M}
for any
M\in\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}}
.
∎

Construction 3.15
.

Let
X:\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{cn}}\to\mathcal{S}
be cohesive and
A\in X(R)
.
Then by
[
Lur17
, Proposition 1.4.2.22]
we obtain an essentially unique factorization

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Sp}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{S}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{A}^{R\oplus{-}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega^{\infty}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{X_{A}}^{{-}}}

where for

the spectrum

is given by
the sequence of spaces

.
For

we call

the

of

.

Warning 3.16
.

The name tangent complex is a historical convention and somewhat misleading.
In general, the spectrum

is not contained in the full subcategory

D(\mathbb{Z})\subseteq\mathrm{Sp}
i.e. cannot be modeled by a chain complex of abelian groups.

Lemma 3.17
.

For a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
R

the full

Proof.

This is

.
∎

Proposition 3.18
.

Let

be a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring,

be cohesive and

inherits a natural

-module structure such that for any perfect connective

-module

a natural equivalence

Proof.

By Lemma

\mathrm{Sp}

∎

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
X:\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{cn}}\to\mathcal{S}
be cohesive,
R\in\mathrm{CAlg}^{\mathrm{cn}}
and
A^{\prime}\in X(R)
. Let

R^{\eta}\to R
be a square zero extension with fiber
M
such that
A^{\prime}
admits a lift
A\in X(R^{\eta})
.
Then for any
N\in\mathrm{Mod}_{R^{\eta}}^{\mathrm{cn}}
if we have that

T^{M\otimes_{R}(R\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N)}_{X_{A^{\prime}}}\simeq T^{N\otimes_{R^{\eta}}R}_{X_{A^{\prime}}}\simeq 0
it follows that

T^{N}_{X_{A}}\simeq 0
.

Proof.

Applying the functor
{-}\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N
to the extension

M\to R^{\eta}\to R

yields a cofiber sequence

M\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N\to N\to N\otimes_{R^{\eta}}R

of connective
R^{\eta}
-modules. Now the functor
T^{{-}}_{X_{A}}:\mathrm{Mod}_{R^{\eta}}^{\mathrm{cn}}\to\mathrm{Sp}

is excisive, hence we get a fiber sequence of spectra

T^{M\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N}_{X_{A}}\to T^{N}_{X_{A}}\to T^{N\otimes_{R^{\eta}}R}_{X_{A}}.

Since the extension
R^{\eta}\to R
is square zero,
the action of
R^{\eta}
on
M
factors through
R
, i.e. we have that

M\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N\simeq(M\otimes_{R}R)\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N\simeq M\otimes_{R}(R\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N).

Applying Proposition
3.20
we see that

T^{M\otimes_{R}(R\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N)}_{X_{A}}\simeq T^{M\otimes_{R}(R\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N)}_{X_{A^{\prime}}}\simeq 0

and similarly

T^{N\otimes_{R^{\eta}}R}_{X_{A}}\simeq T^{N\otimes_{R^{\eta}}R}_{X_{A^{\prime}}}\simeq 0,

which proves the claim.
∎

Remark 3.22
.

Note that in the setting of Proposition
3.21
, if
M
and
N\otimes_{R^{\eta}}R
are perfect
R
-modules,
Proposition
3.18
implies that it suffices to assume that
T_{X_{A^{\prime}}}\simeq 0
.
Moreover, as part of the proof we have seen that every connective
R^{\eta}
-Module
N
sits
in a cofiber sequence

M\otimes_{R}(R\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N)\to N\to R\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N.

If we think of
N
as a lift of
R\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N
along the square zero extension
R^{\eta}\to R
,
this is part of a description of the deformation theory of connective modules. The complete
description may be deduced from Proposition
4.3
.

As an example we now summarize the well-known deformation theory of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings in our language.
Later we will use this description to analyze the deformation theory of dualizable coalgebras.

Example 3.23
.

Let

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring.
The composition

4.
Deformation theory of coalgebras

We now apply the machinery reviewed in the previous section to study deformation theoretic
questions about coalgebras in the category of spectra. We first prove that the functors which
assign to a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring
R
the categories of connective
R
-modules and
p
-complete
connective
R
-modules respectively, are cohesive an nilcomplete and that this implies the same for
coalgebras in those categories.
We then introduce
formally étale coalgebras
in an arbitrary cohesive moduli problem

\mathcal{C}_{-}:\CAlg^{\cn}\to\CAlg(\mathrm{Pr^{L}})

and show that the space of lifts of a formally
étale coalgebra

along any square zero extension

Moreover, we show that the assignment of

refines to a fully faithful

4.2.
p
-complete moduli

Throughout this section fix a prime
p
.
Let
R
be an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring. Recall that a module
M\in\mathrm{Mod}_{R}
is called

p
-
complete
if the limit

\lim\left(\dots\xrightarrow{\cdot p}M\xrightarrow{\cdot p}M\right)

vanishes. We denote the full subcategory spanned by the
p
-complete modules
by
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\wedge}
.
The inclusion
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\wedge}\to\mathrm{Mod_{R}}
admits a left adjoint which
takes a module
M
to its
p
-completion
given by the limit

M^{\wedge}_{p}:=\lim\left(\dots\to M/p^{2}\to M/p\right).

In fact,
M
is
p
-complete if and only if the natural map
M\to\lim M/p^{n}
is an equivalence.
The spectrum
M^{\wedge}_{p}
inherits a natural
R^{\wedge}_{p}
-module structure,
and
p
-completion induces an equivalence of categories

\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\wedge}\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{R^{\wedge}_{p}}^{\wedge}.

The tensor product of
p
-complete modules is in general not
p
-complete. However, the
category
(\mathrm{Mod}_{R})_{p}^{\wedge}
admits a presentably symmetric monoidal structure
given by the formula

M\otimes_{(\mathrm{Mod}_{R})_{p}^{\wedge}}N:=(M\otimes N)^{\wedge}_{p}.

With this monoidal structure the
p
-completion functor
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\to(\mathrm{Mod}_{R})_{p}^{\wedge}

is monoidal, while the inclusion is only lax monoidal.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
R_{{-}}:I\to\CAlg^{\mathrm{cn}}
be a diagram of connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
rings with limit
R
, such
that the natural functor

F:\Mod_{R}\to\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i\in I}\Mod_{R_{i}}

This follows since, for a point

Proposition 4.9

-algebra and denote by

The functor

to the limit

\lim_{n}M_{n}
taken in the category of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules. Since

\mathrm{Tor}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{k},\mathbb{Z}/p^{n})[1]\simeq\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}[1].

The reduction map

is induced by the map of projective resolutions

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{Z}_{p}},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdot p^{k}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdot p}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdot p^{k-1}}

hence, on Tor it induces the multiplication by

Thus, if we have

is an equivalence.

To see that the functor
\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{\wedge}\to\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{n}\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}}

we observe that since cofibers and limits are exact, we have for each

4.3.

Construction 4.12

Let

Construction 4.13

Suppose we are given an adjunction

Let

Definition 4.16
.

Let
R
be a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring and
A\in\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})
.
By Proposition
4.15
, the assignment

canonically refines to a functor

such that
\Omega^{\infty}L_{A}(M)\simeq\Omega^{\infty}_{A}M

if

and denote by
\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})^{\fet}
the full subcategory spanned
by the formally étale coalgebras.

Remark 4.17
.

By construction of the functor
L_{A}
, a coalgebra
A\in\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})

is formally étale if and only if for any
M\in\Mod_{R}^{\cn}
either of the natural maps

A\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}\Omega^{\infty}_{A}M\xrightarrow{\pi}A

is an equivalence. In fact, since the composition
\pi\circ\varepsilon
is always homotopic to the
identity, it suffices to show that there exists some isomorphism
\Omega^{\infty}_{A}M\simeq A
.
This is how we verify the condition in practice.

Remark 4.18
.

We do not know whether the functor
L_{A}
commutes with limits or colimits. Hence, all we may
deduce about the closure properties of formally étale coalgebras is the following

(1)

Since the product of coalgebras is given by the underlying tensor product and base
change is symmetric monoidal, finite products of formally étale coalgebras are formally
étale.

(2)

Let
\kappa
be a regular cardinal such that
\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})
is
\kappa
-presentable.
Then
\kappa
-filtered colimits of formally étale coalgebras are formally étale.

One may contemplate the a priori weaker condition where we only ask that
L_{A}(\Sigma^{n}R)=0
for
all
n\geq 0
. This admits stronger closure properties, for example it is closed under
limits since
R\oplus\Sigma^{n}R
is dualizable as an
R
-module. Moreover, one
can combine Proposition
3.18
with Proposition
4.28
to see that this is
equivalent to
L_{A}(M)
vanishing for all dualizable
M
. With appropriate modification, the results
of this section also apply to this notion of formally étale coalgebras. However, working
with this notion makes lifting along iterated square zero extensions more annoying and we do not
know of an example of a coalgebra which satisfies this condition but not the stronger one of
Definition
4.16
.

.

We can also characterize formally étale coalgebras in this way, as the following proposition shows.

Proposition 4.28
.

Let
R
be a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring and
A\in\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C}_{R})
. We write

\mathcal{X}(R)=\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C}_{R})
. Then
A
is formally étale if and only if for every

B\in\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathcal{C}_{R}),M\in\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{cn}
and every morphism

\varphi:B\to A
, the map

\mathrm{ev}_{0}:T_{(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{1}})_{\varphi}}^{M}\to T_{(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{0}})_{B}}^{M}

induced by the evaluation at the domain is an equivalence.

Proof.

Write

F_{B^{\prime}}^{M}:=\mathrm{fib}_{B^{\prime}}\left((\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{1}})_{\varphi}^{R\oplus M}\to(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{0}})_{B}^{R\oplus M}\right)

for the fiber over some point
B^{\prime}\in(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{0}})_{B}^{R\oplus M}
.
Then by definition of the tangent complex, the map
T_{(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{1}})_{\varphi}}^{M}\to T_{(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{0}})_{B}}^{M}
is an equivalence
if and only if we have
F_{B^{\prime}}^{M}\simeq 0
for all
M\in\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}},B^{\prime}\in(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{0}})_{B}^{R\oplus M}
.
The two pasted pullback squares

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{B^{\prime}}^{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{1}})_{\varphi}^{R\oplus M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Der}_{\id}(A,C_{A}(M[1]))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ast}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Der}_{\id}(B,C_{B}(M[1]))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Der}_{\varphi}(B,C_{A}(M[1]))}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

F_{M}\to\mathrm{Der}_{\id}(A,C_{A}(M[1]))\xrightarrow{{-}\circ\varphi}\mathrm{Der}_{\varphi}(B,C_{A}(M[1])).

Hence, the “only if” direction holds. Moreover, we see that if

for every

denotes the initial coalgebra. Thus, we have that

\mathrm{Der}_{\varphi}(B,C_{A}(M))\simeq\Omega\mathrm{Der}_{\varphi}(B,C_{A}(M[1]))\simeq\ast

as claimed.
∎

.

Let
R
be a connective ring spectrum and

be formally étale.
For a square zero extension

with fiber

denote by

the essentially

to

. Then

is also formally étale.

Let

be any map of

\mathcal{X}({-})=\mathrm{cCAlg}^{\mathrm{cn}}_{{-}}
.
Then for any

we need to show that the induced map

is an equivalence. Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
3.21
, we see that

sits in a cofiber sequence

M\otimes_{R}(R\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N)\to N\to R\otimes_{R^{\eta}}N,

where the

-action on the outer terms factors through

.
Thus, writing

and

and using that the tangent complex functors are excisive, we obtain a commutative diagram

We can neatly organize the results of this section into the following proposition.

Proposition 4.30
.

Let
R
be a connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring and
q:R^{\eta}\to R
be a square zero extension.
Weil-restriction along
q
induces a fully faithful functor

q_{\ast}:\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})^{\mathrm{f\acute{e}t}}\to\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R^{\eta}})^{\mathrm{f\acute{e}t}}

Moreover, for any
A\in\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})^{\mathrm{f\acute{e}t}}
the coalgebra
q_{\ast}A
is, up to contractible
choice, the unique lift of
A
to
\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R^{\eta}})
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.21
, there exists a unique lift
A^{\prime}\in\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R^{\eta}})
which is formally
étale by Corollary
4.29
. Moreover, by Corollary
4.27
, we have a natural equivalence

\Map_{\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R^{\eta}})}(B,A^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Map_{\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})}(p^{\ast}A,A)

and so
A^{\prime}=q_{\ast}A
. Since
A^{\prime}
was a lift of
A
, the counit
q^{\ast}q_{\ast}A\xrightarrow{\sim}A
is an equivalence
which implies that the restriction of
q_{\ast}
is fully faithful as claimed.
∎

We can also iterate lifting along square zero extensions into a limit. This will be crucial in
the next section for going from
\F_{p}
to the
p
-completed sphere
\mathbb{S}_{p}^{\wedge}
.

Proposition 4.31
.

Suppose we are given a diagram

\dots\to R_{2}\xrightarrow{q^{21}}R_{1}\xrightarrow{q^{10}}R_{0}

in
\CAlg^{\cn}
with limit
R=\lim_{n}R
such that each map
R_{n+1}\to R_{n}
is a square
zero extension and the functor
\mathcal{C}_{R}\to\lim_{n}\mathcal{C}_{R_{n}}
is an equivalence. Write

q:R\to R_{0}
for the natural map. Weil restriction along
q
restricts to a fully faithful functor

q_{\ast}:\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R_{0}})^{\fet}\to\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R}),

such that for any
A\in\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R_{0}})^{\fet}
the coalgebra
q_{\ast}A
is, up to contractible choice,
the unique lift of
A
to an object of
\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})
.

Proof.

First observe that by Lemma
2.7
the map

\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})\to\lim\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R_{n}})

is also an equivalence. Denote by
q^{n}
the map
R_{n}\to R
.
Then
q_{\ast}:\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R_{0}})\to\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R})
is given
by the limit of the functors

q^{n}_{\ast}:\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R_{0}})\to\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R_{n}}).

We have that
q^{n}_{\ast}\simeq q^{10}_{\ast}\circ q^{21}_{\ast}\circ\dots\circ q^{n(n-1)}_{\ast}
and for each

k
the restriction

q^{k(k-1)}_{\ast}:\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R_{k-1}})^{\fet}\to\cCAlg(\mathcal{C}_{R_{k}})^{\fet}

is fully faithful by Proposition
4.30
.
Thus,
q^{n}_{\ast}
is fully faithful when restricted to the formally étale coalgebras.
Moreover, since mapping spaces in a limit of categories are given by the limit of mapping spaces, a
limit of fully faithful functors is fully faithful and so
q_{\ast}
is fully faithful when restricted
to formally étale coalgebras. The fact that
q_{\ast}A
is the unique lift of
A
is immediate
by Proposition
4.30
and the fact that fibers commute with limits.
∎

5.
Homology and spherical Weil-restriction

5.1.
Lifting to the spherical Witt vectors

Fix a prime
p
. We now collect the fruits of our labor and apply the machinery of formally
étale coalgebras to the moduli problem

\Mod_{-}^{\wedge}:\CAlg^{\cn}\to\CAlg(\mathrm{Pr^{L}}),

where
\Mod_{R}^{\wedge}
denotes the category of
p
-complete
R
-modules. This is a legal move by

. Throughout this section we fix a perfect
\F_{p}
-algebra

and
denote the spherical Witt vectors by
\mathbb{W}(k)
and the
p
-typical Witt Vectors as

\mathbb{W}_{0}(k)=\pi_{0}\mathbb{W}(k)
.
Before we can state our main theorems, we require one more technical result about
p
-adic lifts
of formally étale coalgebras.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
A\in\cCAlg(\Mod_{\W_{0}(k)}^{\cn,\wedge})

Then
A
is formally étale if

Proof.

Write
\mathcal{X}=(\mathrm{cCAlg}_{{-}}^{\mathrm{cn}})^{\wedge}_{p}

and let

be a point in
\mathcal{X}(\W_{0}(k))^{\Delta^{1}}
.
Denote for each

by
\varphi_{n}:B_{n}\to A_{n}
the base change of

\varphi
to
\W_{0}(k)/p^{n}
. By Proposition
4.28
it suffices to show that for
every
\W_{0}(k)_{p}
-module
M
the natural map

T_{(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{1}})_{\varphi}}^{M}\to T_{(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{0}})_{B}}^{M}

is an equivalence. This map is induced by the maps of spaces

(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{1}})_{\varphi}^{\W_{0}(k)\oplus M[k]}\to(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{0}})_{B}^{\W_{0}(R)\oplus M[k]}

and hence it suffices to show that these are equivalences. Since fibers commute with limits
we have for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
\eta\in\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{i}}(\W_{0}(R)
with base change

\eta_{n}
to
\W_{0}(R)/p^{n}
and each
\W_{0}(R)
-module

an equivalence

(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{i}})_{\eta}^{\W_{0}(R)\oplus N}\simeq\lim_{n}(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{i}})_{\eta_{n}}^{\W_{0}(R)/p^{n}\oplus N/p^{n}}.

As each
A_{n}
is a lift of

to
\W_{0}(R)/p^{n}
and hence by Corollary

formally étale, the map

(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{1}})_{\varphi_{n}}^{\W_{0}(R)/p^{n}\oplus M/p^{n}}\to(\mathcal{X}^{\Delta^{1}})_{B_{n}}^{\W_{0}(R)/p^{n}\oplus M/p^{n}}

is an equivalence for all

and the claim follows.
∎

We now summarize our results in our first main theorem, namely that the right adjoint to base change
defines a
p
-typical Witt vector style functor for formally étale connective coalgebras over
\F_{p}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
q:\W_{0}(k)\to k
be the augmentation. Connective Weil-restriction along
q
induces a
fully faithful functor

\mathcal{W}_{0}:\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{k}^{\cn})^{\fet}\to\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{\W_{0}(k)}^{\cn,\wedge})^{\fet}.

Moreover, for any
A\in\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{k}^{\mathrm{cn}})^{\fet}
the coalgebra
\mathcal{W}_{0}(A)

is, up to contractible choice, the unique lift of
A
to an object
of
\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{\W_{0}(k)}^{\wedge})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.9
we may apply Proposition
4.31
to the functor

\CAlg^{\cn}\to\CAlg(\mathrm{Pr^{L}})\quad R\mapsto\Mod_{R}^{\cn,\wedge}

and the
p
-completion tower

\dots\to\W_{0}(k)/p^{2}\to\W_{0}(k)/p=k

to get fully faithfulness and the uniqueness of the lifts.
Then Proposition
5.1
tells us that the lift is again formally étale and we are done.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
A\in(\mathrm{cCAlg}_{W(k)}^{\mathrm{cn}})^{\wedge}_{p}
, then
A
is formally étale if

A\otimes W_{0}(k)
is.

Proof.

Argue exactly as in Proposition
5.1
.
∎

With this in hand, we obtain a spherical lift of Theorem

.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
q:\W(k)\to k
be the augmentation. Connective Weil-restriction along
q
induces a fully
faithful functor

\mathcal{W}:\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{k}^{\cn})^{\fet}\to\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{\W(k)}^{\cn,\wedge})^{\fet}.

Moreover, for any
A\in\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{k}^{\mathrm{cn}})^{\fet}

the coalgebra
\mathcal{W}(A)
is, up to contractible choice, the unique lift of
A
to an object
of
\mathrm{cCAlg}(\Mod_{\W(k)}^{\wedge})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.2
it suffices to show that Weil restriction along

\W(k)\to\W_{0}(k)=\pi_{0}\W(k)
is fully faithful on formally étale coalgebras. By Lemma
4.8
we can again apply Proposition
4.31
to the
functor

\CAlg^{\cn}\to\CAlg(\mathrm{Pr^{L}})\quad R\mapsto\Mod_{R}^{\cn,\wedge}

and the Postnikov tower

\dots\to\tau_{\leq 2}\W(k)\to\tau_{\leq 1}\W(k)\to\tau_{\leq 0}\W(k)=\W_{0}(k)

and win.
∎

5.2.

As observed in Example

In the following, let

We begin by quickly recalling some facts about pro-objects, see loc.cit. Section 5 for a more

be a category which admits finite limits. Recall that,

\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})
is defined as the full subcategory of

\mathrm{Fun}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{S})^{\mathrm{op}}
spanned by those functors which preserve finite limits.
We think of objects of
\Pro(\mathcal{C})
as formal cofiltered limits of objects of
\mathcal{C}
.
If
\mathcal{C}
admits all small limits, the restricted Yoneda embedding gives an adjunction

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{c:\mathcal{C}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Pro(\mathcal{C}):M}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

Here we think of
c
(aka restricted Yoneda) as taking

to the constant diagram on
X
.
The functor
M
is called materialization and takes a formal limit
\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{\lambda}X_{\lambda}
to
the actual limit computed in
\mathcal{C}
. An object
\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits X_{\lambda}\in\mathrm{Pro}(\Mod_{R})
is called

if each

is
bounded above. The inclusion of pro-truncated objects admits a left adjoint denoted
\tau_{<\infty}
,
which can explicitly described by the formula

\tau_{<\infty}\lim_{\lambda}X_{\lambda}=\lim_{n,\lambda}\tau_{\leq n}X_{\lambda}.

An object

is called

if the natural
map

\tau_{<\infty}cM(E)\to\tau_{<\infty}E

is an equivalence in
\mathrm{Pro}(\Mod_{R})
. If
\mathcal{C}
is symmetric monoidal, then
\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})

inherits a natural symmetric monoidal structure via the pointwise tensor product such that the
inclusion
\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{C})
is monoidal. We denote the partially defined right adjoint
to

by

and refer to it as

. Moreover, we write

\widehat{C}:\Pro(\mathcal{C})\to\cCAlg(\Pro(\mathcal{C}))

for the pro-version of the cofree coalgebra functor. Observe that, for any

the coalgebraic materialization

exists and is given by

. The first key insight is that, although
\Pro(\mathcal{C})
is usually not presentable even if

is,
the category
\cCAlg(\Pro(\mathcal{C}))
is better behaved with respect to limits.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
f:R\to S
be a map of
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings. The base change

f^{\ast}:\mathrm{cCAlg}(\mathrm{Pro}(\Mod_{R}))\to\cCAlg(\mathrm{Pro}(\Mod_{S}))

preserves all limits, cofree coalgebras and constant objects.

Proof.

The base change
\Mod_{R}\to\Mod_{S}
commutes with finite limits and is accessible. Thus,
the induced functor
f^{\ast}:\mathrm{Pro}(\Mod_{R})\to\mathrm{Pro}(\Mod_{S})
commutes with all limits.
In particular for any
M\in\mathrm{Pro}(\Mod_{R})
the cofree coalgebra on
M
is computed
by the formula

\widehat{C}_{R}(M)\simeq\prod_{n}(M^{\otimes n})^{h\Sigma_{n}}\in\cCAlg(\Pro(\Mod_{R})),

and thus, since
f^{\ast}
commutes with tensor products and limits we get

f^{\ast}\widehat{C}_{R}(M)=\widehat{C}_{S}(f^{\ast}M)

as claimed. Finally, the fact that
f^{\ast}
preserves constant objects is clear,
since it is induced by
\mathrm{Pro}({-})
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
f:R\to S
be a map of connective
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-rings and let

be a connective

R
-module. Then the base change
f^{\ast}(\tau_{<\infty}cE)\in\mathrm{Pro}(\Mod_{S})
is

Proof.

We need to show that the natural map

\tau_{<\infty}cM(f^{\ast}(\tau_{<\infty}cE))\to\tau_{<\infty}(f^{\ast}\tau_{<\infty}cE)

\tau_{<\infty}cM(f^{\ast}(\tau_{<\infty}cE))=\tau_{<\infty}c(\lim_{n}((\tau_{\leq n}M)\otimes_{R}S))\simeq\tau_{<\infty}c(E\otimes_{R}S)\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i}c(\tau_{\leq i}(E\otimes_{R}S)),

\tau_{<\infty}(f^{\ast}\tau_{<\infty}cE)\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{k,n}c(\tau_{\leq k}((\tau_{\leq n}E)\otimes_{R}S)).

∎

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\widehat{{-}}):\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{S})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{nilp}}):{\iota}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\dashv}

The left adjoint is given by taking a space

to
\widehat{X}=\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{X_{\alpha}\to X}X_{\alpha}

where the cofiltered limit runs over all maps
X_{\alpha}\to X
where
X_{\alpha}
is nilpotent.
Moreover, the homology
R[{-}]\colon\mathcal{S}\to\Mod_{R}
induces a functor

R[{-}]\colon\mathrm{Pro}(\mathcal{S})\to\mathrm{Pro}(\Mod_{R})\quad\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i}X_{i}\mapsto\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{i}R[X_{i}].

Since the infinite loop space of any spectrum is nilpotent, applying
R[{-}]
to the reflection

X\to\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}
into

induces an equivalence

cR[X]\to\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits_{\alpha}R[X_{\alpha}]

in

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
X
be a connected space and
R
a connective
\F_{p}
-algebra.
Then the
R
-homology
R[X]\in\cCAlg(\Mod_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}})
is formally étale.

Proof.

Since
\Omega^{\infty}N
is
p
-complete for any
N\in\Mod_{\F_{p}}
, the natural map

R[X]\to R[X^{\wedge}_{p}]

is an equivalence, so we may assume that
X
is
p
-complete. Let
M\in\Mod_{R}^{\mathrm{cn}}
and
denote by
e:R\to R\oplus M
be the 0-section.
By Lemma
5.15
we again have that

e^{\ast}R[X]=\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits e^{\ast}R[X_{\alpha}]

where each
X_{\alpha}
is nilpotent. Thus, since Weil-restriction again commutes with limits
we can assume that
X
is nilpotent. Write

X^{n}:=(\Omega^{\infty}\F\{{-}\})^{\circ n}\circ X,

which defines a co-augmented simplicial diagram
X^{\bullet}\to X
, which admits an additional
degeneracy by choosing base-points. Then, since
X
is nilpotent and
p
-complete we have
by
[
BK72
, Proposition VI.6.2]
that
X\simeq\lim X^{n}
. Moreover, the additional degeneracy
tells us that this is a universal limit diagram, and hence

e^{\ast}R[X]\simeq\mathop{\mathchoice{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\textstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}{\vtop{\halign{#\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\operator@font lim$\hfil\cr\kern 1.5pt\cr\leftarrow\textstyle\cr\kern-1.0pt\cr\cr}}}}\displaylimits e^{\ast}R[X^{\bullet}]\in\cCAlg(\Mod_{R}).

So we can assume that
X
is of the form
\Omega^{\infty}\F_{p}\{Y\}
. Now, by Lemma
5.14

we see that
e^{\ast}R[X]\simeq\lim_{i}e^{\ast}R[X_{i}]
where each

is a finite
product of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces. Hence, the claim follows from Proposition
5.12

since
R[{-}]
takes products of spaces to products of coalgebras.
∎

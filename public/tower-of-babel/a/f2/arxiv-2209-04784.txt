Approximating diamond principles on products at an inaccessible cardinal
Omer Ben-Neria and Jing Zhang

Abstract
. We isolate the approximating diamond principles , which are consequences of the diamond principle at an inaccessible cardinal. We use these principles to find new methods for negating the diamond principle at large cardinals. Most notably, we demonstrate, using Gitik’s overlapping extenders forcing, a new method to get the consistency of the failure of the diamond principle at a large cardinal θ \theta without changing cofinalities or adding fast clubs to θ \theta . In addition, we show that the approximating diamond principles necessarily hold at a weakly compact cardinal. This result, combined with the fact that in all known models where the diamond principle fails the approximating diamond principles also fail at an inaccessible cardinal, exhibits essential combinatorial obstacles to make the diamond principle fail at a weakly compact cardinal.

1.
Introduction

Fix a regular uncountable cardinal
\theta
and a stationary set
S\subset\theta
. A sequence
\langle x_{\alpha}\subset\alpha:\alpha\in S\rangle
is a
\diamondsuit(S)
-sequence
if for any
X\subset\theta
, the set
\{\alpha\in S:X\cap\alpha=x_{\alpha}\}
is stationary. This principle is due to Jensen
[
Jen72
]
and was discovered during the course of his fine structural analysis of the constructible universe. Note that
\diamondsuit(\theta)
implies
\theta^{<\theta}=\theta
.

The history of the relation between the diamond and compactness principles, goes back to the work of Kunen and Jensen
[
JK69
]
, who showed that
\diamondsuit(\theta)
must hold at every subtle cardinal.
In fact, they prove that the stronger property
\diamondsuit(Reg^{\theta})
holds at such cardinals, where
Reg^{\theta}
is the stationary collection of regular cardinals below
\theta
.

The consistency of
\neg\diamondsuit(Reg^{\theta})
along with
\theta
being weakly compact,
\Pi^{m}_{n}
-indescribable, strongly unfolddable
was established by Woodin, Hauser
[
Hau92
]
and Džamonja-Hamkins
[
DH06
]
respectively. Each of these consistency results is established from its minimal corresponding large cardinal assumption.

In contrast,
\neg\diamondsuit(\theta)
at Mahlo
\theta
, is known to have a significantly stronger consistency strength.
Jensen
[
Jen91
]
has shown that
\neg\diamondsuit(\theta)

at a Mahlo cardinal
\theta
implies the existence of
0^{\#}
. Zeman
[
Zem00
]
improved
the lower bound to the existence of an inner model with a cardinal
\theta
, in which for every
\gamma<\theta
, the set

\{\alpha<\theta\mid o(\alpha)\geq\gamma\}
is stationary in
\theta
.

There are also tight connections between the diamond principles and cardinal arithmetic. Shelah
[
She10
]
, building on the previous work by Gregory
[
Gre76
]
and after a series of partial results, showed that for any uncountable cardinal
\lambda
,
2^{\lambda}=\lambda^{+}
iff
\diamondsuit(\lambda^{+})
. On the other hand, it was previously known from work of Jensen
[
DJ74
]
that CH does not imply
\diamondsuit(\omega_{1})
. See
[
Rin11
]
for a comprehensive survey regarding the diamond principle at successor cardinals. The situation at inaccessible cardinals is different. In
[
She03
]
, Shelah showed that it is consistent with GCH that
\theta
is inaccessible and
\neg\diamondsuit(S)
for some stationary
S
such that
\theta-S
is fat, from the minimal assumption.
Works of Gitik (see for example
[
Git86
]
) demonstrate the consistency of GCH along with the saturation of
\mathrm{NS}_{\theta}\restriction S
, hence
\neg\diamondsuit(S)
, where
S
is also co-fat. However, in all of these scenarios, the diamond principle only fails partially. In other words,
\diamondsuit(\theta)
holds in all of these models mentioned above.

Two of the main problems in this area are:

(1)

Is it consistent that
\diamondsuit(\theta)
fails at a weakly compact
\theta
?

(2)

Is it consistent with GCH that
\diamondsuit(\theta)
fails?

The only previously known method to violate the diamond principle fully at an inaccessible cardinal is due to Woodin
[
Cum
]
, based on the analysis of Radin forcing extensions. This approach has been further developed in
[
BN19
]
and
[
BNZ
]
to obtain the failure of the full diamond at a stationary reflecting cardinal and even at a cardinal carrying no amenable C-sequences.

Nevertheless, this method is not applicable for solving the two problems above, due to the following. In the following, when we say “Radin forcing at
\theta
”, we mean “Radin forcing defined from a measure sequence on
\theta
”.

(1)

It was shown in
[
BNZ
]
that in any Radin forcing extension at
\theta
, if
\theta
is weakly compact, then
\diamondsuit(\theta)
holds.

(2)

It was shown in
[
Cum13
]
that in the Radin forcing extension at
\theta
over a model of GCH, if
\theta
is regular, then
\diamondsuit(\theta)
holds.

These results motivate the need for new methods to make the diamond principle fail at an inaccessible cardinal.

In order to state the main results of this paper, we need some definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\delta
be a limit ordinal and
f,g:\delta\to\Ord
be functions. We write
f<^{*}g
if
\{\alpha<\delta:f(\alpha)<g(\alpha)\}
contains a closed unbounded set in
\delta
if
cf(\delta)>\omega
and contains co-bounded set if
cf(\delta)=\omega
.

Let
\theta
be an inaccessible cardinal,
\vec{\lambda}=\langle\lambda_{i}<\theta:i<\theta\rangle
be an increasing sequence of cardinals cofinal in
\theta
and
S\subset\theta
be a stationary set. We define what it means to be an
approximating diamond sequence on
\Pi_{i<\theta}\lambda_{i}
with support
S
.

Definition 1.2
.

We say
\langle g_{\gamma}\in\Pi_{i<\gamma}\lambda_{i}:\gamma\in S\rangle
is a
\diamondsuit_{\vec{\lambda}}(S)
-sequence if for any
f\in\Pi_{i<\theta}\lambda_{i}
, the collection
\{\gamma\in S:g_{\gamma}\not<^{*}f\restriction\gamma\}
is stationary.

The principle can be considered as the inaccessible analogue of the
parametrized diamond principle
corresponding to the bounding number
\mathfrak{b}
studied by
Moore, Hrušák and Džamonja
[
MHD04
]
at the level of
\omega_{1}
.

Easy observation reveals that for every sequence
\vec{\lambda}=\langle\lambda_{i}:i<\theta\rangle
,
\diamondsuit_{\vec{\lambda}}(S)
follows from
\diamondsuit(S)
(see Lemma
2.4
), suggesting to force
\neg\diamondsuit_{\vec{\lambda}}(\theta)
as a new route for obtaining
\neg\diamondsuit(\theta)
at a large cardinal
\theta
.
Indeed, we show in Theorem
3.1
that
\diamondsuit_{\vec{\beth}}(\theta)
fails in Woodin’s model
[
Cum
]
, where
\vec{\beth}
denote the sequence
\langle 2^{\alpha}:\alpha<\theta\rangle
.
A similar argument shows that
\diamondsuit_{\vec{\beth}}(\theta)
fails in various Extender-based Radin forcing of Merimovich
[
Mer03
]
.
More surprising to the authors was that one can force the failure of
\diamondsuit_{\vec{\beth}}(\theta)
without changing cofinalities or adding fast clubs. This is fundamentally different from Woodin’s method.
To obtain this result we
utilize a version of the recent forcing discovered by Gitik
[
Git19
]
using overlapping extenders.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
\theta
is an inaccessible limit of
\theta^{++}
-strong cardinals, then there exists a cofinality and cardinal preserving forcing extension
V[G]
of
V
in which:

(1)

\diamondsuit_{\vec{\beth}}(\theta)
fails,

(2)

V,V[G]
agree on cardinals and cofinalities, and

(3)

any closed unbounded subset of
\theta
in
V[G]
contains a closed unbounded subset of
\theta
from
V
.

The third condition
in Theorem
1.3
holds
suggests that
V[G]
is a relatively tame forcing extension of
V
in some sense and will be useful in proving certain compactness properties of
\theta
in
V
remain valid in
V[G]
.
This reasoning is shown to be true to an extent in Section
6
.
However, this approach of forcing
\neg\diamondsuit(\theta)
must not succeed at the level of weak compactness, as evidenced by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

If
\theta
is weakly compact, then
\diamondsuit_{\vec{\lambda}}(Reg^{\theta})
holds for any increasing sequence
\vec{\lambda}
in
\theta
. Moreover,
\Diamond_{\vec{\lambda}}(S)
holds for every
S
in the weak compact filter on
\theta
.

The last theorem prompts the investigation of a possible implication from
\diamondsuit_{\vec{\lambda}}(S)
to
\diamondsuit(\theta)
, for some sequence
\vec{\lambda}
and stationary set
S\subset\theta
.
We do not know of such implication in general but are able to prove an implication under GCH, in the case
S\subset Sing^{\theta}_{>\omega}
, where
Sing^{\theta}_{>\omega}
is the set of singular ordinals of uncountable cofinalities.

Theorem 1.5
.

\diamondsuit_{\vec{\beth}}(Sing^{\theta}_{>\omega})
and
\diamondsuit(Sing^{\theta}_{>\omega})
are equivalent under GCH.

The organization of the paper is:

(1)

Section
2
contains the proofs of Theorem
1.4
and
1.5
as well as some generalizations.

(2)

Section
3
outlines a few results in the Radin forcing extension. In particular, the consistency of
\neg\diamondsuit_{\vec{\lambda}}(\theta)
and the consistency of
\diamondsuit_{\vec{\beth}}(Sing^{\theta}_{>\omega})+\neg\diamondsuit(\theta)
are demonstrated.

(3)

Section
4
gives a short description of Gitik’s forcing with overlapping extenders of inaccessible length
and review some of its key properties.

(4)

Section
5
focuses on the proof of Theorem
1.3
. It also includes a proof showing that the assumption of the extenders being strong past
\theta
is needed.

(5)

Section
6
illustrates the possibilities of preserving certain compactness properties of
\theta
from the ground model, extending some results from
[
BNZ
]
.

(6)

Section
7
concludes with open questions.

2.
ZFC Results

Let
\theta
be a strongly inaccessible cardinal.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
J
be an ideal on
\gamma
and
f,g\in{}^{\gamma}\Ord
, then
f<_{J}g
abbreviates
\{\alpha\in\gamma:f(\alpha)\geq g(\alpha)\}\in J
. Similary we can define
\leq_{J},=_{J}
.

Unless otherwise noted, all the ideals we consider are proper.

Notation 2.2
.

Given an ideal
J
, let

•

J^{+}
denote the collection of positive sets, namely
J^{+}=\{A:A\not\in J\}
and

•

J^{*}
denote its dual filter, namely
J^{*}=\{A:A^{c}\in J\}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Suppose we are given

(1)

\vec{J}=\langle J_{\gamma}\subset P(\gamma):\gamma\leq\theta\rangle
where each
J_{\gamma}
is an ideal on
\gamma
,

(2)

an increasing sequence
\vec{\lambda}=\langle\lambda_{i}:i<\theta\rangle
cofinal in
\theta
, and

(3)

S\in J_{\theta}^{+}
.

Then a
\diamondsuit_{\vec{J},\vec{\lambda}}(S)
-sequence
is
\langle g_{\gamma}\in\Pi_{i<\gamma}\lambda_{i}:\gamma\in S\rangle
satisfying: for any
f\in\Pi_{i<\theta}\lambda_{i}
,
\{\gamma\in S:g_{\gamma}\not<_{J_{\gamma}}f\restriction\gamma\}\in J_{\theta}^{+}
.

We say
\diamondsuit_{\vec{J},\vec{\lambda}}(S)
holds if there exists a
\diamondsuit_{\vec{J},\vec{\lambda}}(S)
-sequence. In this paper, unless noted otherwise, we will assume the index set
S
is a subset of the club set
\{\gamma<\theta:\forall i<\gamma,\lambda_{i}<\gamma\}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

\diamondsuit(S)
implies
\diamondsuit_{\vec{J},\vec{\lambda}}(S)
for any
\vec{J},\lambda,S\in\mathrm{NS}_{\theta}^{+}
satisfying that
J_{\theta}\subseteq\mathrm{NS}_{\theta}
.

Proof.

Let
\langle g^{\prime}_{\gamma}\in{}^{\gamma}\gamma:\gamma\in S\rangle
be a given
\diamondsuit(S)
-sequence. Define a
\diamondsuit_{\vec{J},\vec{\lambda}}(S)
by letting
g_{\gamma}=g^{\prime}_{\gamma}
if
g^{\prime}_{\gamma}\in\Pi_{i<\gamma}\lambda_{i}
, and
g_{\gamma}\equiv 0
otherwise. Given
f\in\Pi_{i<\theta}\lambda_{i}
, there exists a stationary set
S^{\prime}\subset S
such that for all
\gamma\in S^{\prime}
,
f\restriction\gamma=g^{\prime}_{\gamma}=g_{\gamma}
, where the last equality follows from the definition of
g_{\gamma}
. As
J_{\theta}\subseteq\mathrm{NS}_{\theta}
,
S^{\prime}\in J_{\theta}^{+}
.
∎

Definition 2.5
.

A transitive model
M\models\mathrm{ZFC}^{-}
(
\mathrm{ZFC}-
Power Set Axiom) is a
\theta
-model
if it contains
\theta
and
{}^{<\theta}M\subset M
.

A typical
\theta
-model will be the transitive collapse of some
X\prec H(\chi)
where
\chi
is a large enough regular cardinal,
\theta+1\subset X
and
{}^{<\theta}X\subset X
.

Definition 2.6
.

A sequence of ideals
\vec{J}=\langle J_{\gamma}:\gamma\leq\theta\rangle
is

(1)

uniformly definable
if there exist a formula
\varphi(x,y)
and a parameter

, such that for any large enough
\gamma
,
A\in J_{\gamma}
iff
H(\gamma^{+})\models\varphi(A,a\cap\gamma)
.

(2)

\theta
-upward uniformly definable
if it is uniformly definable as witnessed by
\varphi(x,y),a
and for any
\theta
-model
N
containing

,
(J_{\theta})^{N}\subset J_{\theta}
. More precisely, the latter asserts for any

H(\theta^{+})\models\varphi(A,a)

Remark 2.7
.

Usually it suffices to work with
\langle J_{\gamma}:\gamma\in A\cup\{\theta\}\rangle
where
A\in J_{\theta}^{*}
. Hence, for the rest of this article, whenever we define a sequence of ideals on a large subset of

Example 2.8
.

We give two concrete examples of

(1)

\vec{J}_{bdd}=_{\mathrm{def}}\langle P_{bdd}(\gamma):\gamma\in\lim\theta\rangle

\diamondsuit_{\vec{\lambda}}(S)

Theorem 2.9
.

If
\theta
is weakly compact, then
\diamondsuit_{\vec{J},\vec{\lambda}}(S)
holds when

(1)

\vec{\lambda}=\langle\lambda_{i}:i<\theta\rangle
is increasing and cofinal in
\theta
,

(2)

\vec{J}
is
\theta
-upward uniformly definable,

(3)

J_{\theta}
is
\sigma
-complete,

(4)

J_{\theta}\subset\mathrm{WC}_{\theta}
, where
\mathrm{WC}_{\theta}
is the weakly compact ideal on
\theta
,

(5)

S\in\mathrm{WC}_{\theta}^{+}
.

Proof.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction that the conclusion fails. More explicitly, this means for any sequence
\langle g_{\gamma}\in\Pi_{i<\gamma}\lambda_{i}:\gamma\in S\rangle
, there exist
f\in\Pi_{i<\theta}\lambda_{i}
and
A\in J^{*}_{\theta}
such that for all
\gamma\in A\cap S
,
g_{\gamma}<_{J_{\gamma}}f\restriction\gamma
. Note that by the assumption that
J_{\theta}\subset\mathrm{WC}_{\theta}
,
A\in\mathrm{WC}_{\theta}^{*}
. Let
\varphi(x,y)
and

witness the
\theta
-upward uniform definability of
\vec{J}

Recursively define
\langle g_{\gamma}\in\Pi_{i<\gamma}\lambda_{i}:\gamma\in\lim(\theta+1)\rangle
such that for each
\gamma\in\lim\theta+1
, if there exists
g\in\Pi_{i<\gamma}\lambda_{i}
such that:

(1)

for
J_{\gamma}^{*}
-many
\eta\in S\cap\gamma
,
g\restriction\eta>_{J_{\eta}}g_{\eta}
and

(2)

there does not exist
f<_{J_{\gamma}}g
satisfying the property above,

then
g_{\gamma}
is any such
g
. Otherwise,
g_{\gamma}\equiv 0
and we say
g_{\gamma}
is
defined vacuously
.

Claim 2.10
.

If
J_{\gamma}
is
\sigma
-complete and there exists
g
satisfying (1), then there exists
g
satisfying (2).

Proof of the Claim.

Suppose for the sake of contradiction, no
g
satisfies (2). Define
g_{0}>_{J_{\gamma}}g_{1}>_{J_{\gamma}}g_{2}>_{J_{\gamma}}\cdots g_{n}>_{J_{\gamma}}\cdots
with each
g_{n}
satisfying (1). Since
J_{\gamma}
is
\sigma
-complete, there exists
\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma
such that for any
m<n
,
g_{m}(\gamma^{\prime})>g_{n}(\gamma^{\prime})
. Contradiction.
∎

Note that by Claim
2.10
and the initial assumption,
g_{\theta}
is not defined vacuously. Therefore, there exists
A\in J_{\theta}^{*}
, such that for any
\tau\in A\cap S
,
g_{\tau}<_{J_{\tau}}g_{\theta}\restriction\tau
.

As
A\cap S\in\mathrm{WC}^{+}_{\theta}
, we can find

•

a transitive
\theta
-model
M
containing
\vec{g}=\langle g_{\gamma}:\gamma\in S\cup\{\theta\}\rangle
,
\vec{J}\restriction\theta
and
a
,

•

a transitive
\theta
-model
N
and an elementary embedding

with critical point
\theta
,

•

\theta\in j(A\cap S)

Let

. Observe that

is not defined vacuously since

-decreasing sequence, contradicting with Claim

. By the elementarity of

and the fact that

N\models f_{\theta}<_{J_{\theta}}j(g_{\theta})\restriction\theta=g_{\theta}
. Since
\vec{J}
is

-upward uniformly definable, we have

, we have that

Remark 2.13
.

Recall a theorem of Woodin (see

\diamondsuit(\mathrm{Reg}^{\theta})

\diamondsuit_{\vec{\mathrm{NS}},\vec{\lambda}}(\mathrm{Reg}^{\theta})

Proposition 2.14

(GCH)
.

Let
S
be a stationary subset of
S^{\theta}_{>\omega}=\theta\cap\{\gamma:\omega<cf(\gamma)<\gamma\}
. Then
\diamondsuit_{\vec{\mathrm{NS}},\vec{\beth}}(S)
implies
\diamondsuit(S)
.

Proof.

We may assume any
\gamma\in S
is a singular cardinal.
Fix a
\diamondsuit_{\vec{J},\vec{\lambda}}(S)
-sequence
\vec{g}=\langle g_{\gamma}:\gamma\in S\rangle
. For each
\gamma\in S
, let
c_{\gamma}\subset\gamma
be a club of order type
cf(\gamma)
. For each
\gamma\in S
, let
F_{bdd}(\gamma)=\{h\in{}^{c_{\gamma}}\gamma:\mathrm{ran}(h)\subset\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma\}
. By the cardinal arithmetic assumption,
|F_{bdd}(\gamma)|=\gamma
for each strong limit singular cardinal
\gamma
. For each ordinal
\beta
, let
f_{\beta}:|\beta|\to\beta
be some bijection and let
\langle x^{\beta}_{i}:i<\beta^{+}\rangle
be an enumeration of
P(\beta)
.

Define
\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}=\{\bigcup_{\beta\in x}x^{\beta}_{f_{g_{\gamma}(\beta)+1}(h(\beta))}:x\subset c_{\gamma},h\in F_{bdd}(\gamma),\dom(h)=x\}
. Note that
|\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}|=\gamma
. We check that
\langle\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}:\gamma\in S\rangle
is a
\diamondsuit^{-}(S)
-sequence. Let
X\subset\theta
be given. Let
l_{X}\in\Pi_{i<\theta}i^{+}
be such that for each
i
,
l_{X}(i)=j<i^{+}
where
x^{i}_{j}=X\cap i
. By the fact that
\vec{g}
is a
\diamondsuit_{\vec{J},\vec{\lambda}}(S)
-sequence,
S^{\prime}=\{\gamma\in S:\exists A\in\mathrm{NS}^{+}_{\gamma},\forall\beta\in A,l_{X}(\beta)\leq g_{\gamma}(\beta)\}\in\mathrm{NS}_{\theta}^{+}
. It suffices to show that for each
\gamma\in S^{\prime}
,
X\cap\gamma\in\mathcal{C}_{\gamma}
. Fix
\gamma\in S^{\prime}
as witnessed by
A\in\mathrm{NS}^{+}_{\gamma}
. Consider the following regressive function:
\beta\in A\cap c_{\gamma}\mapsto i_{\beta}<\beta
such that
f_{g_{\gamma}(\beta)+1}(i_{\beta})=l_{X}(\beta)
. By the Pressing Down lemma, there exists a stationary
x\subset c_{\gamma}\cap A
and some
\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma
such that for all
\beta\in x
,
i_{\beta}<\gamma^{\prime}
. Consider
h\in F_{bdd}(\gamma)
defined on
x
mapping
\beta\in x
to

4.1.

From the strong Prikry Lemma, it is easy to deduce the Prikry property.

For every sequence of dense open sets

Before turning to the proof of the Lemma, we introduce a useful terminology of totally

-generic conditions.

Definition 4.16
.

Let

be a poset which belongs to

for some regular cardinal

. Namely, the set

Proof.

To simplify the presentation, we assume that
s(p)=\emptyset
.

We recursively define a
\leq^{*}
-increasing
\langle q(\beta):\beta\leq\theta\rangle
such that

(1)

,

(2)

q(\beta)\restriction\alpha=q(\alpha)\restriction\alpha
for any
\alpha<\beta<\theta
.

At limit stages
\delta\leq\theta
, granted the existence of such a sequence, we can define

q(\delta)_{<\delta}=\bigvee_{\alpha<\delta}q(\alpha)_{<\alpha}

and

q(\delta)_{\geq\delta}=\bigvee_{\alpha<\delta}q(\alpha)_{\geq\delta}

where
\bigvee
is the natural limit condition obtained by taking coordinate-wise unions of the Cohen functions and intersecting the appropriate pullbacks of the measure one sets. Note that
q(\delta)
is easily seen to be a condition which satisfies the requirements above.

At stage
\beta+1
, given
q(\beta)
, we take an elementary submodel

containing relevant objects including
\mathbb{P}_{\vec{E}},D=D_{\beta}
, and

with

and
{}^{<\kappa_{\beta}}N\subset N
. By the
\kappa_{\beta}^{+}
-closure of the direct extension ordering of

, we can find a totally
N
-generic condition
f^{*}_{\beta}{}^{\frown}r^{*}_{>\beta}
with

. Let

A_{\beta}^{*}=\{\nu\in OB_{E_{\beta}}(f^{*}_{\beta})\mid\nu\restriction\dom(f^{q(\beta)}_{\beta})\in A^{q(\beta)}_{\beta}\},

and define

q(\beta+1)=q(\beta)\restriction\beta{}^{\frown}\langle f^{*}_{\beta},A^{*}_{\beta}\rangle{}^{\frown}r^{*}_{>\beta}.

For each
\nu\in A^{*}_{\beta}
, the forcing above

breaks into a product

.
For each

•

, or

•

.

D_{w,\geq\beta}
is clearly dense open in

and belongs to
N
.

By the strong Prikry property, there exists
a dense open set

in the direct extension ordering, such that for every

there is a

-fat-tree

.

Since

as well. It follows from our

.

Let

is clearly dense open in

We verify that for every maximal sequence in the associated

Abstract




Abstract.
Let

2.
Torsionless differential forms

Motivated by ?THM?
our strategy was to find a suitable definition of “torsion”, which produces a “torsion free” functor compatible with étale base change, such that the relative de Rham-Witt complex is obtained as a “torsion free version” of the naive differential forms of the associated Witt scheme. The following example shows that the relative de Rham-Witt complex is not reflexive.

Example 2.1
.

For
S=\{1,2\}
we show that
W_{S}\Omega^{\bullet}_{{\mathbb{Z}}[t]/{\mathbb{Z}}}
is not reflexive.
By
[

, Lem. 3.11]
we can identify

with
\Omega^{\bullet}_{W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t])/W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}})}/{\mathbb{Z}}\text{-torsion}
.
An explicit computation shows that the
W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t])
-dual module in degree one is given by

\displaystyle(W_{S}\Omega_{{\mathbb{Z}}[t]/{\mathbb{Z}}}^{1})^{\vee}\cong(\Omega_{W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t])/W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}})}^{1})^{\vee}\cong\text{Der}_{W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}})}(W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t]),W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t]))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}d\colon W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t])\to W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t])\\
W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}})\text{-linear derivation}\end{array}\Big|\begin{array}[]{l}d[t]=(2-V_{2}[1])[f]+V_{2}[gt]\\
dV_{2}[t]=V_{2}[g]\end{array},\ f,g\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]\right\}

Let
\varphi_{f,g}\in(\Omega_{W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t])/W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}})}^{1})^{\vee}
denote the
W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t])
-linear map corresponding to
f,g\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t]
.
We identify
(W_{S}\Omega_{{\mathbb{Z}}[t]/{\mathbb{Z}}}^{1})^{\vee\vee}
with

(\Omega_{W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t])/W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}})}^{1})^{\vee\vee}

and show that the

-linear map
\psi\colon\varphi_{f,g}\mapsto(2-V_{2}[1])[f]+V_{2}[gt],f,g\in{\mathbb{Z}}[t],
does not lie in the image of the evaluation map.

Let us assume the existence of
\omega\in\Omega_{W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}}[t])/W_{S}({\mathbb{Z}})}^{1}
such that
\psi=\mathrm{ev}_{\overline{\omega}}
with
\overline{\omega}=\omega+{\mathbb{Z}}\text{-torsion}
.
There are

such that
\omega=ad[t]+bdV_{2}[t]
.
We set
a_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\gh_{n}(a)
,
b_{n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\gh_{n}(b)
for

and compute
\gh_{S}(\varphi_{1,1}(\overline{\omega}))=2(a_{1},a_{2}t+b_{2})
.
This is equal to
\gh_{S}(\psi(\varphi_{1,1}))=2(1,t)
, hence implies

and

.
By

both

and

lie in

and the degree

is

, which contradicts the assumption.

We introduce the weaker property of being torsionless. This property does not appear very often in literature, but deserves to be better known.

Definition 2.2

(
[
Bas60
, II, sec. 4]
)
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module.
The coimage
T_{R}(M)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=M/\ker\varepsilon_{M}
of the canonical map
\varepsilon_{M}\colon M\to M^{\vee\vee}=\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,R),R)
is called the
torsionless quotient
.
The module

is called

if
\varepsilon_{M}\colon M\to M^{\vee\vee}
is injective or equivalently
T_{R}(M)=M
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Lam12
, Rem. 4.65]
)
.

Let
M
be an
R
-module.

(i)

Torsionless modules are torsion free.

(ii)

M
is torsionless if and only if
M
can be embedded into a direct product
R^{I}
for some (possibly infinite) index set
I
.

(iii)

Any submodule of a free module is torsionless.

(iv)

Any submodule of a torsionless module is torsionless.

(v)

Direct sums and direct products of torsionless modules are torsionless.

(vi)

If
R
is an integral domain and
M
a finitely generated torsion free

-module, then

is torsionless.

Forming the torsionless quotient defines a covariant endofunctor on the category of

-modules compatible with arbitrary direct sums. Moreover, the projections

constitute natural transformations from the identity functors to

. The functor

is neither right exact nor preserves injectivity.

On the Bruhat-Tits stratification of a quaternionic unitary Rapoport-Zink space
Haining Wang

Abstract
. In this article we study the special fiber of the Rapoport-Zink space attached to a quaternionic unitary group. The special fiber is described using the so called Bruhat-Tits stratification and is intimately related to the Bruhat-Tits building of a split symplectic group. As an application we describe the supersingular locus of the related Shimura variety.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivations

In this article we study the basic locus of a specific Shimura variety. Namely the Shimura variety associated to a quaternionic unitary group. This Shimura variety is particular interesting as it is of PEL-type and is intimately related to Siegel and orthogonal type Shimura varieties. This article is inspired by the paper
[
KR00
]
where the basic locus of this Shimura variety is studied at a prime
p
where the quaternion algebra is split. Using this description, special cycles on this Shimura variety are defined and their intersection numbers are related to Fourier coefficients of Eisenstein series. In this case the Shimura variety has good reduction and the quaternionic unitary group at
p
agrees with the symplectic group of degree
4
. Therefore the supersingular locus can be studied essentially in the same way as in the case of a Siegel threefold of hyperspecial level. An explicit description of this supersingular locus is available in
[
KO87
]
. However in
[
KR00
]
the authors are able to relate the description to the Bruhat-Tits building of an inner form of the symplectic group. In this article we treat the case where the quaternion algebra is ramified at
p
and we describe the supersingular locus with its relation to Bruhat-Tits building. Note in this case the Shimura variety has bad reduction at
p
and the local model is discussed in this article. In a subsequent work we will define and study special cycles on this Shimura variety.

In general, to describe the supersingular locus of a PEL-type Shimura variety, one can pass to the associated moduli space of
p
-divisible groups then use the uniformization theorem of Rapoport-Zink
[
RZ96
]
. These moduli spaces of
p
-divisible groups are known as the basic Rapoport-Zink spaces and we are interested in calculating their special fibers. By passing from
p
-divisible groups to their assciated Dieudonnė modules, one obtain lattices in an isocrystal. By comparing the relative position between these lattices and the lattices representing the faces of the base alcove of an inner form of the underlying group of the PEL-problem, once can partite the Rapoport-Zink space into pieces which are related to classical Deligne-Lusztig varieties. This decomposition is known as the
Bruhat-Tits stratification
. The terminology come from the pioneering work of
[
Vol10
]
where the case of the Rapoport-Zink space of
\mathrm{GU}(1,n-1)
at an inert prime is analyzed. The analysis is completed in
[
VW10
]
. This program has been extended to cover other PEL-type problems by many authors. We provide a list of works of this type and acknowledge their influences on this article.

-

For
\mathrm{GU}(1,n-1)
,
p
-inert and hyperspecial level, this is the work of
[
Vol10
]
and
[
VW10
]
. In a recent preprint
[
Cho18
]
, Sungyoon Cho is able to treat many cases of parahoric level structures.

-

For
\mathrm{GU}(1,n-1)
,
p
-ramified and with level structure related to self-dual lattices, this is the work of
[
RTW14
]
. When the level structure is related to a special parahoric and the Rapoport-Zink space admits the so called exotic good reduction, this is the work of
[
Wu16
]
.

-

For
\mathrm{GU}(2,2)
,
p
-split, this is considered by the author in
[
Wang18
]
. For
\mathrm{GU}(2,2)
,
p
-inert, this is done in
[
HP14
]
by transferring the problem to
\mathrm{GSpin}(4,2)
. In the process of preparing of this note, we find the methods in this note can be also used to deal with
\mathrm{GU}(2,2)
,
p
-inert directly and this is documented in
[
Wang18
]
.

-

For
\mathrm{GSpin}(n-2,2)
with hyperspecial level, this is the work of
[
HP17
]
. In this case, the Shimura variety is not of PEL-type in general except for small ranks case where exceptional isomorphism happens. For example,
\mathrm{GU}(2,2)
is essentially
\mathrm{GSpin}(4,2)
and
\mathrm{GSp}(4)
is essentially
\mathrm{GSpin}(3,2)
. The two Rapoport-Zink space studied in this note are both of non-hypersepcial level and therefore is not covered in
[
HP17
]
, however they are all
\mathrm{GSpin}(3,2)
-type Shimura varieties.

A general understanding of the Bruhat-Tits stratification is achieved in the powerful work of
[
GH15
]
and the subsequent work of
[
GHN16
]
. There the problem is studied in the setting of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties and a general group theoretic method is employed. Their work not only classifies which types of Shimura varieties admit Bruhat-Tits stratification but also gives an algorithm to compute the Deligne-Lusztig varieties occurring in the strata. The types of Shimura varieties in the above list as well as the two Shimura varieties in this article are covered by their work. A comparison between our results with
[
GH15
]
is contained in the very last section of this article.

Besides the Bruhat-Tits stratification, there are other methods to study the supersingular locus. We will only mention the works
[
Hel
]
,
[
HTX17
]
and
[
CV18
]
. The method used in
[
Hel
]
,
[
HTX17
]
is the so called isogeny trick and the isogeny is referred to the isogeny between the universal abelian varieties on different Shimura varieties. Notice that the isogeny trick used in Section
4
in this note is of same nature but in a local set up. The work of
[
CV18
]
introduced the so called
J
-stratification for the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties and is closely related to Bruhat-Tits stratification. In fact it can be shown that the
J
-stratification agrees with the Bruhat-Tits stratification for those classified by
[

1.2.
Results on Rapoport-Zink spaces

We now introduce some notations and state the local results proved in this article. Let
p
be an odd prime and let
\mathbb{F}
be an algebraically closed field containing
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
W_{0}=W(\mathbb{F})
be the Witt ring of
\mathbb{F}
and
K_{0}=W(\mathbb{F})_{\mathbb{Q}}
its fraction field. Let
B
be a quaternion division algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
which ramifies at
p
and splits at
\infty
. Denote by
*
a neben involution on
B
, see
[
KR00
, A.4]
. We fix a maximal order
\mathcal{O}_{B}
that is stable under
*
. Let
N
be a height
8
isocrystal of slope
\frac{1}{2}
equipped with a map
\iota:B_{p}\rightarrow\End({N})
and an alternating form
(\cdot,\cdot):N\times N\rightarrow K_{0}
. We are concerned with the following moduli space for
p
-divisible groups with additional structures.
Let
(\mathrm{Nilp})
be the category of
W_{0}
-schemes on which
p
is locally nilpotent. We fix a
p
-divisible group
\mathbb{X}
whose associated isocrystal is
N
and a polarization
\lambda:\mathbb{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}^{\vee}
corresponding to
(\cdot,\cdot)
on
N
. We consider the set valued functor
\mathcal{N}
that sends
S\in(\mathrm{Nilp})
to the isomorphism classes of the collection
(X,\iota_{X},\lambda_{X},\rho_{X})
where

-

X
is a
p
-divisible group of dimension
4
and height
8
over
S
;

-

\lambda_{X}:X\rightarrow X^{\vee}
is a principal polarization;

-

\iota_{X}:\mathcal{O}_{B_{p}}\rightarrow\End_{S}(X)
is an action of
\mathcal{O}_{B_{p}}
on
X
defined over
S
;

-

\rho_{X}:X\times_{S}S_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}\times_{\mathbb{F}}S_{0}
is an
\mathcal{O}_{B_{p}}
-linear quasi-isogeny where
S_{0}
is the special fiber of
S
at
p
.

We require that
\iota_{X}
satisfies the Kottwitz condition

(1.1)

\mathrm{det}(T-\iota(c);\mathrm{Lie}(X))=(T^{2}-\mathrm{Trd}^{0}(c)T+\mathrm{Nrd}^{0}(c))^{2}

for
c\in\mathcal{O}_{B_{p}}
, where
\mathrm{Trd}^{0}(c)
is the reduced trace of
c
and
\mathrm{Nrd}^{0}(c)
is the reduced norm of
c
. For
\rho_{X}:X\times_{S}S_{0}\rightarrow\mathbb{X}\times_{\mathbb{F}}S_{0}
, we require that

(1.2)

\rho_{X}^{-1}\circ\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}\circ\rho_{X}=c(\rho)\lambda_{X}

for a
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
-multiple
c(\rho)
.
This moduli problem is representable by a formal scheme
\mathcal{N}
, locally formally of finite type over
\mathrm{Spf}(W_{0})
. The formal scheme
\mathcal{N}
can be decomposed into open and closed sub formal schemes
\mathcal{N}=\bigsqcup_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{N}(i)
where each
\mathcal{N}(i)
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{N}(0)
. Denote by
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}_{red}(0)
the underlying reduced scheme of
\mathcal{N}(0)
. Then our main result concerns the structure of
\mathcal{M}
.

Theorem 1
.

The scheme
\mathcal{M}
can be decomposed into
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{\{0\}}\cup\mathcal{M}_{\{2\}}
. The irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}_{\{0\}}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\{2\}}
are all isomorphic to the surface defined by the equation

x_{3}^{p}x_{0}-x^{p}_{0}x_{3}+x^{p}_{2}x_{1}-x^{p}_{1}x_{2}=0

where
x_{i}
are the projective coordinates on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. If an irreducible component in
\mathcal{M}_{\{0\}}
intersect with an irreducible component of
\mathcal{M}_{\{2\}}
non-trivially, then the intersection is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. If an irreducible component in
\mathcal{M}_{\{0\}}
intersect with an other irreducible component of
\mathcal{M}_{\{0\}}
non-trivially, then the intersection is a point which is superspecial. If an irreducible component in
\mathcal{M}_{\{2\}}
intersect with another irreducible component of
\mathcal{M}_{\{2\}}
non-trivially, then the intersection is a point which is superspecial.

The decomposition
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{\{0\}}\cup\mathcal{M}_{\{2\}}
in Theorem
1
can be made finer and we have the
Bruhat-Tits stratification
of
\mathcal{M}
.

Theorem 2
.

We have the Bruhat-Tits stratification

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{0\}}\sqcup\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{2\}}\sqcup\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{0,2\}}\sqcup\mathcal{M}_{\{1\}}.

The stratum
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{0\}}
is open an dense in
\mathcal{M}_{\{0\}}
and the complement is precisely
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{0,2\}}\sqcup\mathcal{M}_{\{1\}}
. Similarly the stratum
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{2\}}
is open and dense in
\mathcal{M}_{\{2\}}
and the complement is precisely

\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{0,2\}}\sqcup\mathcal{M}_{\{1\}}.

The irreducible components of
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{0\}}
,
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{2\}}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{0,2\}}
can be identified with classical Deligne-Lusztig varieties.

We explain the connection of this stratification with Bruhat-Tits building. The quaternionic unitary Rapoport-Zink space is acted on by the split symplectic group of
4
-variables. If we label the vertices of a base alcove of the Bruhat-Tits building of
\mathrm{Sp}(4)
by
\{0,1,2\}
, then the strata
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{0\}}
,
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{2\}}
,
\mathcal{M}_{\{1\}}
and
\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{\{0,2\}}
correspond to precisely the vertices
0,2,1
and the face
\{0,2\}
. The intersection behaviour is also determined by the incidence relation among faces of the Bruhat-Tits building. We refer the reader to Section
5.1
for the details.

The quaternionic unitary Rapoport-Zink space in this article is closely related to the Rapoport-Zink space for
\mathrm{GSp}(4)
. Their relation is formally similar to the relation between the Lubin-Tate spaces and the Drinfeld upper-half spaces. But from a group theoretic point view or from the point view of local models, the quaternionic unitary Rapoport-Zink space is closer to the Rapoport-Zink space for
\mathrm{GSp}(4)
with the so called paramodular level structure instead of with the hyperspecial level. Therefore we also include the calculation for the Bruhat-Tits stratification of the Rapoport-Zink space for
\mathrm{GSp}(4)
with the paramodular level. However we should point out the description of the supersingular locus for the corresponding Shimura variety in the paramodular case is already known, we refer the reader to
[
Yu06
]
and
[
Yu11
]
for the statement and proof of this result. The calculations in
[
Yu06
]
and
[
Yu11
]
do not involve Rapoport-Zink space or Bruhat-Tits building and are indirect in the sense that they involve Shimura varieties with different level structures. Our calculation is direct and it is interesting to compare the two cases.

1.3.
Applications to the supersingular locus

We consider the following moduli problem
\mathfrak{S}h_{U^{p}}
in
[
KR00
]
. To a scheme
S
over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
, we classify the set of isomorphism classes of the collection
\{(A,\iota,\lambda,\eta)\}
where:

-

A
is an abelian scheme of relative dimension
4
over
S
;

-

\lambda:A\rightarrow A^{\vee}
is a principal polarization which is
\mathcal{O}_{B}
-linear;

-

\iota:\mathcal{O}_{B}\rightarrow\End_{S}(A)
is a morphism such that
\lambda\circ i(a^{*})=i(a)^{\vee}\circ\lambda
and satisfies the Kottwitz condition

(1.3)

\mathrm{det}(T-\iota(a),\mathrm{Lie}(A))=(T^{2}-\mathrm{Trd}^{0}(a)T+\mathrm{Nrd}^{0}(a))^{2}

for all
a\in\mathcal{O}_{B}
and
\mathrm{Nrd}^{0}
is the reduced norm of
B
and
\mathrm{Trd}^{0}
is the reduced trace of
B
;

-

\eta
is a suitable prime to
p
level structure.

This moduli problem is representable by a quasi-projective scheme. We are interested in the closed subscheme
Sh^{ss}_{U^{p}}
in
\mathfrak{S}h_{U^{p}}\otimes\mathbb{F}
known as the supersingular locus which we view as a reduced scheme. The uniformization theorem of Rapoport-Zink
[
RZ96
, Theorem 6.1]
transfers the problem of describing the supersingular locus to that of describing the Rapoport-Zink space. More precisely there is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}
schemes

Sh^{ss}_{U^{p}}\cong I(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathcal{N}_{red}\times G(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})/U^{p}.

where
I
is an inner form of
G=\mathrm{GU}_{B}(2)
.

Theorem 3
.

The scheme
Sh^{ss}_{U^{p}}
is pure of dimension
2
. For
U^{p}
sufficiently small, the irreducible components are isomorphic to the surface
x_{3}^{p}x_{0}-x^{p}_{0}x_{3}+x^{p}_{2}x_{1}-x^{p}_{1}x_{2}=0
. The intersection of two irreducible components if nonempty is either isomorphic to a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or is a point.

2.1.

Let

8

\mathbb{F}
which is isotypic of slope

\iota:B_{p}\rightarrow\End({N})

. Suppose

\begin{split}&x^{*}=\sigma(x),x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p^{2}}\\
&\Pi^{*}=\Pi.\\

The action of

decomposes

. Restricting the symplectic form

\begin{split}&s_{1}=(12)(34);\\
&s_{2}=(23);\\

]

and
VL^{+}(M)=VV^{-1}(L^{-}(M))=L^{-}(M)
. This implies that
a(L^{+}(M))=2
. By the same argument as above using Lemma
7.6
,
F^{2}(M)+pM=pM^{\perp}\cap F(M)
. Therefore
\tau(M)\subset M^{\perp}
and
L^{+}(M)=M+\tau(M)\subset M^{\perp}\cap\tau(M)^{\perp}=L^{+}(M)^{\perp}
. This shows (
7.4
).

\square

Remark 7.7
.

It is clear by considering the indices in (

) and (

) that if
M
is
\tau
-stable, then

corresponds to a vertex lattice of type

. If

is not

corresponds to a vertex lattice of type

and

. Hence

.

We define similarly as in the quaternionic case

\begin{split}&\mathcal{M}_{\{0,2\}}(\mathbb{F})=\bigcup_{L_{0},L_{2}}\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})\\
&\text{ where $(L_{0},L_{2})$ runs through all the vertex lattices of type $\{0,2\}$}.\\

By the above remark we obtain the following decomposition of the set

.

Lemma 7.9
.

We have
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{F})=\mathcal{M}_{\{0,2\}}(\mathbb{F})\cup\mathcal{M}_{\{1\}}(\mathbb{F})
and
\mathcal{M}_{\{1\}}(\mathbb{F})

Proof.

The first statement is a translation of Proposition

and the second statement is clear as

is superspecial if and only if

-stable.

7.2.
Bruhat-Tits stratification

Our next goal is to relate the set theoretic lattice stratum
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
to a Deligne-Lusztig variety which in this case is simply
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{F})
. Suppose
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
is non-empty, we define a two dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-vector space by
V_{L_{0,2}}=L_{0,W_{0}}/L_{2,W_{0}}
.

Proposition 7.10
.

Let
L_{0}
be a vertex lattice of type
0
and
L_{2}
be a vertex lattice of type
2
. Suppose
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
is non-empty. There is a bijection between
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}(V_{L_{0,2}})(\mathbb{F})
. Moreover the superspecial points on
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
correspond to the
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
-points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(V_{L_{0,2}})(\mathbb{F})
.

Proof.

We define a map
f:\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(V_{L_{0,2}})(\mathbb{F})
by sending
M\in\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
to
M/L_{2,W_{0}}\subset L_{0,W_{0}}/L_{2,W_{0}}
. Conversely let
m\subset V_{L_{0,2}}=L_{0,W_{0}}/L_{2,W_{0}}
be a point in
\mathbb{P}^{1}(V_{L_{0,2}})
and denote by
M\subset L_{0,W_{0}}
the preimage of
m
under the natural reduction map. By the proof of Proposition
7.3
, we have
L_{2,W_{0}}=VL_{0,W_{0}}
. Then we have
VM\subset VL_{0,W_{0}}=L_{2,W_{0}}\subset M
and
pM\subset pL_{0,W_{0}}=V^{2}L_{0,W_{0}}=VL_{2,W_{0}}\subset VM
. Hence
M
is a Dieudonné module. It is clear these two maps are inverse to each other and we have a bijection. It is also clear from the construction that the superspecial points are the
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
-points.
∎

Lemma 7.11
.

Let
L_{1}
be a vertex lattice of type
1
. Then we can find a vertex lattice
L_{0}
of type
0
containing
L_{1}
and a vertex lattice
L_{2}
of type
2
contained in
L_{1}
.

Proof.

This is proved the same way as Lemma
3.27
and we state it again for the reader’s convenience.
∎

The lemma and its proof shows that any superspecial point is contained in a lattice stratum of the form
\mathcal{M}_{(L_{0},L_{2})}
for a vertex lattice
(L_{0},L_{2})
of type
\{0,2\}
. Let
(L_{0},L_{2})
and
(L^{\prime}_{0},L^{\prime}_{2})
be two vertex lattices of type
\{0,2\}
. We study the set theoretic intersection between
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
and
\mathcal{M}_{L^{\prime}_{0},L^{\prime}_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
.

Lemma 7.12
.

Suppose that the intersection
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})\cap\mathcal{M}_{L^{\prime}_{0},L^{\prime}_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
is non-empty. Then
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})\cap\mathcal{M}_{L^{\prime}_{0},L^{\prime}_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
consists of a superspecial point.

Proof.

Given
M\in\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})\cap\mathcal{M}_{L^{\prime}_{0},L^{\prime}_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
, we have
L_{0,W_{0}}\subset M\subset L_{2,W_{0}}
and
L^{\prime}_{0,W_{0}}\subset M\subset L^{\prime}_{2,W_{0}}
. By considering the index,
L_{0,W_{0}}+L^{\prime}_{0,W_{0}}=M
and
L^{\prime}_{2,W_{0}}\cap L^{\prime}_{2,W_{0}}=M
. This forces
M
to be
\tau
-stable and hence superspecial.

∎

7.3.
The isogeny trick

We are now ready to translate the set theoretic results in the previous sections to the scheme theoretic setting. We will again rely on the isogeny trick used in Section
4
. Let
(L_{0},L_{2})
be a vertex lattice of type
\{0,2\}
such that
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
is non-empty.

Lemma 7.13
.

The vertex lattices
L_{0}
and
L_{2}
give rise to
p
-divisible groups
\mathbb{X}_{L_{0}}
and
\mathbb{X}_{L_{2}}
. Moreover there is a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{X}_{L_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{X}^{\vee}_{L_{0}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{X}^{\vee}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{L_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lambda_{\mathbb{X}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{\vee}_{L_{0}}}

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}(\mathbb{F})
is non-empty, we have
VL_{0,W_{0}}=L_{2,W_{0}}
and a chain

(7.14)

pM^{\perp}\subset pL^{\perp}_{2,W_{0}}\subset^{0}L_{2,W_{0}}\subset M\subset L_{0,W_{0}}\subset^{0}L^{\perp}_{0,W_{0}}\subset M^{\perp}\subset L^{\perp}_{2,W_{0}}.

Therefore

pL_{0,W_{0}}=pL^{\perp}_{0,W_{0}}\subset pL^{\perp}_{2,W_{0}}=L_{2,W_{0}}=VL_{0,W_{0}}\subset L_{0,W_{0}}

and
L_{0}
is a Dieudonné module. Since
L_{0}
is
\tau
-stable,
V^{2}L_{0,W_{0}}=pL_{0,W_{0}}
. Then
pL_{2,W_{0}}\subset pL_{0,W_{0}}=V^{2}L_{0,W_{0}}=VL_{2,W_{0}}\subset VL_{0,W_{0}}=L_{2,W_{0}}
. The rest of the diagram follows from the chain (
7.14
).

∎

Given
(X,\lambda_{X},\rho_{X})\in\mathcal{M}(R)
for a
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
R
, we define two quasi-isogenies by the following composites

(7.15)

\rho_{X,L_{0}}:X\xrightarrow{\rho_{X}}\mathbb{X}_{R}\xrightarrow{\rho^{-1}_{L_{0}}}\mathbb{X}_{L_{0},R},

and

(7.16)

\rho_{X,L_{2}}:\mathbb{X}_{L_{2},R}\xrightarrow{\rho_{L_{2}}}\mathbb{X}_{R}\xrightarrow{\rho^{-1}_{X}}X.

We define the subfunctor
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}
of
\mathcal{M}
over
\mathbb{F}
by classifying those
(X,\lambda_{X},\rho_{X})
such that
\rho_{X,L_{0}}
and
\rho_{X,L_{2}}
are both actual isogenies.

Proposition 7.17
.

The functor
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}
is representable by a closed subscheme of
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}
is projective.

Proof.

First of all,
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}
is a closed subscheme by
[
RZ96
, Proposition 2.9]
. Moreover
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}
is bounded in the sense of
[
RZ96
, 2.30]
. This follows from the fact that we have isogenies

\mathbb{X}_{L_{2},R}\xrightarrow{\rho_{X,L_{2}}}X\xrightarrow{\rho_{X,L_{0}}}\mathbb{X}_{L_{0},R}.

Hence
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}
is a closed subscheme of a projective scheme which means it is projective itself by using
[
RZ96
, Corollary 2.29]
. ∎

We define a map
f:\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(V_{L_{0,2}})
by sending
(X,\rho_{X},\lambda_{X})
to

M/L_{2,R}\subset L_{0,R}/L_{2,R}

for any
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
R
and where
M=\mathbb{D}(X)(R)
is the covariant Dieudonnè crystal of
X
evaluated at
R
.

Proposition 7.18
.

The map
f:\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(V_{L_{0,2}})
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Using the theory of windows of display
[
Zin99
]
in place of Dieudonné modules, one can show

is bijective on
k
-points for any field extension
k
over
\mathbb{F}
following the same proof as in Proposition
7.10
. Since
\mathbb{P}^{1}(V_{L_{0},L_{2}})
is normal and
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}
is a projective scheme, we can apply Zariski’s main theorem to show

is an isomorphism.
∎

We set

(7.19)

\mathcal{M}^{\circ}_{L_{0},L_{2}}=\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}-\mathcal{M}_{\{1\}}

and

(7.20)

\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2},\{1\}}=\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}\cap\mathcal{M}_{\{1\}}.

\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2}}

Corollary 7.22
.

The set of superspecial points on

is precisely the stratum
\mathcal{M}_{L_{0},L_{2},\{1\}}
. The isomorphism

respects the Ekedahl-Oort stratification in the sense that

corresponds to the

-points on

and

corresponds to the complement of
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
-points on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

The statements can be checked on

-points and they follow from Proposition

.

7.5.
Application to the supersingular locus

Let
V
be a vector space of dimension
4
over
\mathbb{Q}
. We assume that
V
is equipped with a symplectic form
(\cdot,\cdot)
. Then we define

G(\mathbb{Q})=\{g\in\mathrm{GL}(V);(gx,gy)=c(g)(x,y),c(g)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\}.

That is
G=\mathrm{GSp}(4)
. Let
h:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow G(\mathbb{C})
be the cocharacter sending
z
to
\text{diag}(z,z,1,1)
. Moreover
h
defines a decomposition
V_{\mathbb{C}}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
where
h(z)
acts on
V_{1}
by
z
and on
V_{2}
by
\bar{z}
. We fix an open compact subgroup
U=U_{p}U^{p}
of
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})
and we assume
U^{p}
is sufficiently small. We also assume that there is a lattice
\Lambda
in
V
such that
\Lambda
is paramodular in the sense that
p\Lambda^{\perp}\subset^{2}\Lambda\subset^{2}\Lambda^{\perp}
. We choose
U_{p}
that stabilize
\Lambda\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

To
(V,(\cdot,\cdot),h,\Lambda)
, we consider the following moduli problem
\mathfrak{S}h_{U^{p}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
: for a scheme
S
over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
we associate the set of isomorphism classes of the triple
(A,\lambda,\eta)
where:

-

A
is an abelian scheme of relative dimension
2
over
S
;

-

\lambda:A\rightarrow A^{\vee}
is a polarization of degree
p^{2}
;

-

\eta:V\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}{\mathbb{A}}^{(p)}_{f}\rightarrow V^{(p)}(A)
is a
U^{p}
-orbit of isomorphisms. Here

V^{p}(A)=\prod_{p^{\prime}\neq p}\mathrm{Ta}_{p^{\prime}}(A)\otimes\mathbb{A}_{f}^{(p)}

is the prime to
p
-part of the rational Tate module of
A
.

This moduli problem is representable by a quasi-projective variety
\mathfrak{S}h_{U^{p}}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{(p)}
which is well-known.
We are interested in the supersingular locus
Sh^{ss}_{U^{p}}
in
\mathfrak{S}h_{U^{p},\mathbb{F}}
which is considered as a closed reduced subscheme. The uniformization theorem of Rapoport-Zink
[
RZ96
, Theorem 6.1]
translates this problem to problem of describing the corresponding Rapoport-Zink space.

Theorem 7.24
.

There is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}
-schemes

Sh^{ss}_{U^{p}}\cong I(\mathbb{Q})\backslash\mathcal{N}_{red}\times G(\mathbb{A}^{p}_{f})/U^{p}.

Here
I
is an inner form of
G
such that
I(\mathbb{Q}_{p})=J_{b}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})
defined exactly as we did in the quaternionic unitary case in Theorem
5.3
.

7.6.
Main theorem on the supersingular locus

We apply the results obtained previously to describe the supersingular locus of the Siegel threefold with paramodular level structure.

Theorem 7.25
.

The
\mathbb{F}
-scheme
Sh^{ss}_{U^{p}}
is pure of dimension
1
. For
U^{p}
sufficiently small, the irreducible components are isomorphic to the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The intersection of two irreducible components is a superspecial point.

Proof.

Using the uniformization Theorem
7.24
, the result follows from the main theorem for the Rapoport-Zink space Theorem
7.23
.
∎

8.
Comparison with Affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

In this final section we would like to point out how our results fit in the results proved in Görtz and He
[
GH15
]
in terms of the affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties. In the following we will abbreviate affine Deligne-Lusztig variety as ADLV.

8.1.
Affine Deligne Lusztig variety

Let
F
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and
\breve{F}
be the completion of the maximal unramified extension of
F
. Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
F
and we write
\breve{G}
its base change to
\breve{F}
. Then
\breve{G}
is quasi-split and we choose a maximal split torus
S
and denote by
T
its centralizer. We know
T
is a maximal torus and we denote by
N
its normalizer. The relative Weyl group is defined to be
W_{0}=N(\breve{F})/T(\breve{F})
. Let
\Gamma
be the Galois group of
\breve{F}
and we have the following Kottwitz homomorphism
[
Ko97
]
:

\kappa_{G}:G(\breve{F})\rightarrow X_{*}(\breve{G})_{\Gamma}.

Denote by
\widetilde{W}
the Iwahori Weyl group of
\breve{G}
which is by definition given by
\widetilde{W}=N(\breve{F})/T(\breve{F})_{1}
where
T(\breve{F})_{1}
is the kernel of the Kottwitz homomorphism for
T(\breve{F})
. Let
\breve{\mathfrak{B}}(G)
be the Bruhat-Tits building of
G
over
\breve{F}
. The choice of
S
determines an standard apartment
\breve{\mathfrak{A}}
which
\widetilde{W}
acts on by affine transformations. We fix a
\sigma
-invariant alcove
\mathfrak{a}
and a special vertex of
\mathfrak{a}
. Inside the Iwahori Weyl group
\widetilde{W}
, there is a copy of the affine Weyl group
W_{a}
which can be identified with
N(\breve{F})\cap G(\breve{F})_{1}/T(\breve{F})_{1}
where
G(\breve{F})_{1}
is the kernel of the Kottwitz morphism for
G
. The group
\widetilde{W}
is not quite a Coxeter group while
W_{a}
is generated by the set of simple affine reflections denoted by
\tilde{\mathbb{S}}
and
(\widetilde{W},\tilde{\mathbb{S}})
form a Coxeter system. We in fact have
\widetilde{W}=W_{a}\rtimes\Omega
where
\Omega
is the normalizer of a fixed base alcove and more canonically
\Omega=X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma}/X_{*}(T_{sc})_{\Gamma}
where
T_{sc}
is the preimage of
T\cap G_{der}
in the simply connected cover
G_{sc}
of
G_{der}
.

Let
\mu\in X_{*}(T)
be a minuscule cocharacter of
G
over
\breve{F}
and
\lambda
its image in
X_{*}(T)_{\Gamma}
. We denote by
\tau
the projection of
\lambda
in
\Omega
. The
admissible subset
of
\widetilde{W}
is defined to be

\Adm(\mu)=\{w\in\widetilde{W}:w\leq x(\lambda)\text{ for some }x\in W_{0}\}.

Here
\lambda
is considered as a translation element in
\widetilde{W}
. Let
K\subset\tilde{\mathbb{S}}
and
\breve{K}
its corresponding parahoric subgroup. Let
\widetilde{W}_{K}
be the subgroup defined by
(N(\breve{F})\cap\breve{K})/T(\breve{F})_{1}
. We have the following decomposition
\breve{K}\backslash G(\breve{F})/\breve{K}=\widetilde{W}_{K}\backslash\widetilde{W}/\widetilde{W}_{K}
. Therefore we can define a relative position map by

\mathrm{inv}:G(\breve{F})/\breve{K}\times G(\breve{F})/\breve{K}\rightarrow\widetilde{W}_{K}\backslash\widetilde{W}/\widetilde{W}_{K}.

For
w\in\widetilde{W}_{K}\backslash\widetilde{W}/\widetilde{W}_{K}
and
b\in G(\breve{F})
, we define the
affine Deligne-Lusztig variety

to be the set

X_{w}(b)=\{g\in G(\breve{F})/\breve{K}:\mathrm{inv}(g,b\sigma(g))=w\}.

Thanks to the work of
[
BS17
]
and
[
Zhu17
]
, this set can be viewed as an ind-closed-subscheme in the affine flag variety
\breve{G}/\breve{K}
. In this article, we only consider it as a set. The Rapoport-Zink space is not directly related to the affine Deligne-Lusztig variety but rather to the following union of affine Deligne-Lusztig varieties

X(\mu,b)_{K}=\{g\in G(\breve{F})/\breve{K}:g^{-1}b\sigma(g)\in\breve{K}w\breve{K},w\in\Adm(\mu)\}.

We recall that the group
J_{b}
is defined as the
\sigma
-centralizer of
b
that is

J_{b}(R)=\{g\in G(R\otimes_{F}\breve{F}):g^{-1}b\sigma(g)=b\}

for any
F
-algebra
R
. In the following we will assume that
b
is basic and in this case
J_{b}
is an inner form of
G
.

8.2.
Coxeter type ADLV

We define
\Adm^{K}(\mu)
to be the image of
\Adm(\mu)
in
\widetilde{W}_{K}\backslash\widetilde{W}/\widetilde{W}_{K}
and
{}^{K}\widetilde{W}
to be the set of elements of minimal length in
\widetilde{W}_{K}\backslash\widetilde{W}
. We define the set
\mathrm{EO}^{K}(\mu)=\Adm^{K}(\mu)\cap^{K}\widetilde{W}
. For
w\in W_{a}
, we set

\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w\tau)=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\tau\sigma)^{n}(\mathrm{supp}(w)).

If the length
l(w)
of
w
agrees with the cardinality of the set
\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w\tau)/\langle\tau\sigma\rangle
, we say
w\tau
is a
\sigma
-Coxeter element
. We denote by
\mathrm{EO}^{K}_{\sigma,\mathrm{cox}}(\mu)
the subset of
\mathrm{EO}^{K}(\mu)
such that
w
is a
\sigma
-Coxeter element and
\mathrm{supp}_{\sigma}(w)
is not equal to
\widetilde{\mathbb{S}}
. A
K
-stable piece
is a subset of
G(\breve{F})
of the form
\breve{K}\cdot_{\sigma}IwI
where
\cdot_{\sigma}
means
\sigma
-conjugation,
I

Now we assume that

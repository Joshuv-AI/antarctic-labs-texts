Packing and Covering Triangles in Bilaterally- Complete Tripartite Graphs
Naivedya Amarnani ⋅ \boldsymbol{\cdot} Amaury De Burgos ⋅ \boldsymbol{\cdot} Wayne Broughton

Abstract
We use Menger’s Theorem and König’s Line Colouring Theorem to show that in any tripartite graph with two complete (bipartite) sides the maximum number of pairwise edge-disjoint triangles equals the minimum number of edges that meet all triangles. This generalizes the corresponding result for complete tripartite graphs given by Lakshmanan, et al. Keywords: Maximum Packing, Tripartite Graphs, Minimum T-Transversal, Triangles, Complete Bipartite Graph

For standard graph-theoretic terms or notation, refer to
[
11
]
. All graphs will be finite, simple and undirected unless indicated otherwise. An edge with endpoints
u
and
v
will generally be denoted
uv
. In graph
G=(V,E)
, if
X\subseteq V
or
Y\subseteq E
then
G[X]
denotes the subgraph of
G
induced by
X
and
G[Y]
denotes the subgraph edge-induced by
Y
.

A
triangle
in a graph
G
is a subgraph isomorphic to
K_{3}
, consisting of 3 vertices that are all joined by edges. Most of the following definitions and notation are from
[
9
]
and
[
5
]
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
denote the set of all triangles in
G=(V,E)
. A subset
E^{\prime}\subseteq E
is said to be a
\mathcal{T}
-transversal
if every triangle in
\mathcal{T}
has an edge in
E^{\prime}
.

A subset
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
is said to be a
packing
in
G
if all the triangles in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
are pairwise edge-disjoint.

We denote the cardinality of the smallest
\mathcal{T}
-transversal in
G
by
\tau_{\triangle}(G)
and the cardinality of the largest packing by
\nu_{\triangle}(G)
. It is not difficult to see that
\nu_{\triangle}(G)\leq\tau_{\triangle}(G)\leq 3\nu_{\triangle}(G)
.

In 1981 Zsolt Tuza conjectured (
[
10
]
) that
\tau_{\triangle}(G)\leq 2\nu_{\triangle}(G)
for every graph
G
. The general case is still open, but when
G
is a tripartite graph an even stronger bound was proved by Haxell and Kohayakawa (
[
3
]
). In 2012, Lakshmanan et al. (
[
5
]
) proved that in fact the equality
\tau_{\triangle}(G)=\nu_{\triangle}(G)
holds for certain classes of tripartite graphs. Their results can be viewed as extensions to these tripartite graphs of the König-Egerváry Theorem, equating the minimum size of a vertex cover and the maximum matching size in bipartite graphs. In particular, it is observed in
[
5
]
that
\tau_{\triangle}(G)=\nu_{\triangle}(G)
always holds for complete tripartite graphs. In this paper, we show that this equality also holds for tripartite graphs with only two complete bipartite sides.

If
G=(V,E)
is a tripartite graph whose vertex set is partitioned into
V=A\cup B\cup C
, then we write
G=(A,B,C;E)
and we call the induced bipartite subgraphs
G[A\cup B]
,
G[A\cup C]
, and
G[B\cup C]
the
sides
of
G
. The sets of edges on the sides are written
E_{AB}
,
E_{AC}
, and
E_{BC}
, respectively. More generally, if
M,W\subseteq V
then
E_{MW}\subseteq E
denotes the set of edges with one endpoint in each of
M
and
W
(abusing notation slightly if
M
or
W
is just a single vertex).

Definition 1

(Bilaterally-Complete Tripartite Graph)
:

Let
G=(A,B,C;E)
be a tripartite graph. If (at least) two of the sides of
G
are complete bipartite graphs, then we call
G
a
bilaterally-complete tripartite graph
.

Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1
:

If
G
is a bilaterally-complete tripartite graph, then
\tau_{\triangle}(G)=\nu_{\triangle}(G)
.

Note:
It is possible to derive a proof of this fact from a complicated chain of results in some old papers (particularly
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
), but we give a straightforward proof relying only on classical theorems. The alternative approach will be discussed in the last section of this paper.

Characterization of Minimum
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}
-transversals

Let
G=(A,B,C;E)
be a bilaterally-complete tripartite graph with complete sides
G[A\cup B]\cong K_{p,q}
and
G[A\cup C]\cong K_{p,r}
, where
|A|=p
,
|B|=q
,
|C|=r
. We will describe the structure of a minimum
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
in terms of vertices and edges of
G[B\cup C]
.

For each vertex
a\in A
, the triangles in
G
containing
a
are in one-to-one correspondence with the edges in
E_{BC}
, since
G
is bilaterally-complete. Likewise, edges in
E_{aB}
and
E_{aC}
can be identified with vertices in
B
and
C
, respectively.

Now let
E^{\prime}
be a
\mathcal{T}
-transversal in
G
, and fix
a\in A
. If we let
E^{\prime}_{BC}=E^{\prime}\cap E_{BC}
, then the triangles containing
a
and corresponding to edges in
E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}
must be covered by
E^{\prime}
using edges in
E_{aB}\cup E_{aC}
. This means the corresponding vertices in
B\cup C
must cover all the edges in
E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}
; that is, they form a vertex cover
W_{a}
of
G[E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}]
for each
a\in A
.

Conversely, if we choose a set of edges
E^{\prime}_{BC}\subseteq E_{BC}
and a vertex cover
W_{a}
of
G[E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}]
for each
a\in A
, then

E^{\prime}=E^{\prime}_{BC}\cup\bigcup_{a\in A}E_{aW_{a}}

will be a
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
with size
|E^{\prime}|=|E^{\prime}_{BC}|+\sum_{a\in A}|W_{a}|
.

If
W_{a}=W
is the same vertex cover for all
a\in A
we will call this a
uniform

\mathcal{T}
-transversal.
Without loss of generality, a minimum
\mathcal{T}
-transversal can be assumed to be uniform by choosing
E^{\prime}_{BC}\subseteq E_{BC}
and a minimum vertex cover
W
of
G[E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}]
, such that the value of
|E^{\prime}_{BC}|+p|W|
is minimized.
Hence,

\displaystyle\tau_{\triangle}(G)=\min_{E^{\prime}_{BC}\subseteq E_{BC}}\{|E^{\prime}_{BC}|+p|W|\},

where
W
is a minimum vertex cover of
G[E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}]
.

It is worth pointing out that in bilaterally-complete tripartite graphs, the edges of the smallest side do not always form a minimum
\mathcal{T}
-transversal. In fact, there exist bilaterally-complete tripartite graphs whose minimum
\mathcal{T}
-transversals must have an edge in every side of the graph. Such a graph is pictured in Figure 1
. This makes the bilaterally-complete case more complicated than the complete tripartite case, where the smallest side is always a minimum
\mathcal{T}
-transversal and the maximum packing can be described explicitly as a selection of one triangle on each edge in the smallest side of the graph.

Minimum
\boldsymbol{\mathcal{T}}
-transversal and Maximum Packing Equality

Before proving our main result, we recall two classical theorems that we will use.

Menger’s Theorem for Digraphs

[
6
]

Let
D
be a finite directed graph and
s
and
t
be two vertices of
D
with no arc from
s
to
t
. Then the minimum size of an
(s,t)
-separator (that is, the minimum number of vertices, distinct from
s
and
t
, whose removal breaks all directed paths from
s
to
t
in
D
) is equal to the maximum number of pairwise internally vertex-disjoint directed paths from
s
to
t
in
D
.

König’s Line Colouring Theorem

[
4
]

The minimum number of disjoint matchings that partition the edges of a bipartite graph equals the maximum degree of the graph.

We now prove Theorem
1
:
If
G
is a bilaterally-complete tripartite graph, then
\tau_{\triangle}(G)=\nu_{\triangle}(G)
.

Let
G=(A,B,C;E)
be a bilaterally-complete tripartite graph with
G[A\cup B]\cong K_{p,q}
and
G[A\cup C]\cong K_{p,r}
, where
|A|=p
,
|B|=q
,
|C|=r
. We first show that every uniform
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
corresponds to an
(s,t)
-separator of
H(G)
of the same size, and every
(s,t)
-separator of
H(G)
corresponds to a (not necessarily uniform)
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
of the same size.

Let
E^{\prime}=E^{\prime}_{BC}\cup E_{AB^{\prime}}\cup E_{AC^{\prime}}
be a uniform
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
, where (as described in Section
2
)
E^{\prime}_{BC}\subseteq E_{BC}
,
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
,
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
, and
W=B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}
is a vertex cover of
G[E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}]
. Then we construct an
(s,t)
-separator of size
|E^{\prime}|
in the network graph
H(G)
as follows: Let
V^{\prime}_{BC}\subseteq V_{BC}
be the vertices in
H(G)
corresponding to
E^{\prime}_{BC}
, and for
1\leq i,j\leq p
let
B^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq B_{i}
and
C^{\prime}_{j}\subseteq C_{j}
be the vertices in each copy of
B
and
C
corresponding to
B^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime}
, respectively.

Example:
The minimum
\mathcal{T}
-transversal shown in
Figure 1
corresponds in
Figure 3
to the
(s,t)
-separator
\{2,2^{\prime},38,9,9^{\prime}\}
.

Claim:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=V^{\prime}_{BC}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}B^{\prime}_{i}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}C^{\prime}_{j}
is an
(s,t)
-separator of
H(G)
of size
|E^{\prime}|
.

To see why this is true, note that every directed path from
s
to
t
in
H(G)
contains one vertex in
B_{i}
, one vertex in
V_{BC}
, and one vertex in
C_{j}
, for some
i
and
j
. Since
W=B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}
covers all edges in
E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}
(in
G
), every vertex of
H(G)
in
V_{BC}\setminus V^{\prime}_{BC}
must be adjacent to at least one vertex in
B^{\prime}_{i}\cup C^{\prime}_{j}
, and therefore every path from
s
to
t
contains a vertex in
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
. We also have

|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}|=|V^{\prime}_{BC}|+\sum_{i=1}^{p}|B^{\prime}_{i}|+\sum_{j=1}^{p}|C^{\prime}_{j}|=|E^{\prime}_{BC}|+p|B^{\prime}|+p|C^{\prime}|=|E^{\prime}|.

Conversely, let
\displaystyle\mathcal{V}^{\prime}=V^{\prime}_{BC}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}B^{\prime}_{i}\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{p}C^{\prime}_{j}
be any
(s,t)
-separator in
H(G)
, with
V^{\prime}_{BC}\subseteq V_{BC}
,
B^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq B_{i}
, and
C^{\prime}_{j}\subseteq C_{j}
. Every
s
–
t
path in
\{s\}\cup B_{i}\cup V_{BC}\cup C_{j}\cup\{t\}
must be cut by
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}
, so in particular (when
i=j
), every vertex in
V_{BC}\setminus V^{\prime}_{BC}
must be adjacent to a vertex in
B^{\prime}_{i}\cup C^{\prime}_{i}
for each
i=1,\ldots,p
.

By slightly abusing notation we will write
B^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq B
and
C^{\prime}_{i}\subseteq C
(in
G
) as well. (Note that we are not assuming that all of the
B^{\prime}_{i}
are the same set of vertices in
B
, for example.) Let
E^{\prime}_{BC}
be the edges in
E_{BC}
corresponding to the vertices in
V^{\prime}_{BC}
. Then
W_{i}=B^{\prime}_{i}\cup C^{\prime}_{i}
must cover all the edges in
E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}
. So, labelling the vertices in
A
as
a_{1},\ldots,a_{p}
, we see that
\displaystyle E^{\prime}=E^{\prime}_{BC}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{p}E_{a_{i}W_{i}}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
, and

|E^{\prime}|=|E^{\prime}_{BC}|+\sum_{i=1}^{p}|W_{i}|=|V^{\prime}_{BC}|+\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left(|B^{\prime}_{i}|+|C^{\prime}_{i}|\right)=|\mathcal{V}^{\prime}|.

Since there always exists a minimum
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
that is uniform, the above correspondences show that the smallest size of an
(s,t)
-separator in
H(G)
equals
\tau_{\triangle}(G)
, the minimum
\mathcal{T}
-transversal size of
G
.

Thus
Menger’s Theorem
guarantees that there exists a set
P
of
\tau_{\triangle}(G)
pairwise internally vertex-disjoint
s
–
t
paths in the network graph
H(G)
. Once again, each
s
–
t
path in
H(G)
contains one vertex from each of
B_{i}
,
V_{BC}
, and
C_{j}
(for some
i,j
), which correspond to an edge in
E_{BC}
and its endpoints. Let
F
denote the subgraph of the bipartite graph
G[B\cup C]
consisting of these edges and vertices corresponding to all paths in
P
. Because the paths in
P
are internally vertex-disjoint,
F
contains exactly
\tau_{\triangle}(G)
edges.
F
also has maximum degree at most
p
, because any vertex in
B
or
C
corresponds to only
p
vertices in
H(G)
and none of those vertices in
H(G)
can appear more than once in the paths in
P
.

Therefore by
König’s Line Colouring Theorem
, the edges of
F
can be partitioned into
p
or fewer matchings, say
M_{1},\ldots,M_{k}
(
k\leq p
). We then join the edges in each matching
M_{i}
to the vertex
a_{i}\in A
for
1\leq i\leq k
, and the resulting set of triangles is clearly a packing in
G
. Since the number of edges in the graph
F
was
\tau_{\triangle}(G)
, we have obtained a packing of size
\tau_{\triangle}(G)
in
G
, which proves that
\tau_{\triangle}(G)=\nu_{\triangle}(G)
. ∎

Alternative Derivation

The key step in our proof above is the existence of (up to)
p
pairwise disjoint matchings in
G[B\cup C]
that consist of a total of
\tau_{\triangle}(G)
edges. This could actually be derived easily from Theorem 6 in Mao Cheng’s 1983 paper
[
1
]
(in Chinese), as we show below. But that short paper gives only a brief proof based on Theorem 8 in the 1971 paper
[
2
]
, whose proof in turn relies on (essentially) Theorem 2.2 in the 1968 paper
[
8
]
(itself proved in a more general form from Menger’s Theorem) and an unspecified result in
[
7
]
. It was due to the difficulty of tracing this convoluted logical route that we decided to present our own direct and self-contained proof above.

The statement of Mao Cheng’s Theorem 6 is given below, with notation adjusted to correspond more closely with the present paper. Note that when
X
is a set of vertices in a graph,
\partial(X)
refers to the set of all edges in the graph that are incident on vertices in
X
.

Theorem 6

(
[
1
]
, translated)

Let
p,t\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
and let
G[B\cup C]
be a bipartite graph with parts
B
and
C
. There exist
p
pairwise edge-disjoint matchings in
G[B\cup C]
such that the sum of their cardinalities equals
t
if and only if for all
B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B
and for all
C^{\prime\prime}\subseteq C
,

\displaystyle|\partial(B^{\prime\prime})\cap\partial(C^{\prime\prime})|+p\left(|B|+|C|-|B^{\prime\prime}|-|C^{\prime\prime}|\right)\geq t.

This result implies that
G[B\cup C]
admits
p
pairwise disjoint matchings with a total number of edges equal to the minimum value of

\displaystyle|\partial(B^{\prime\prime})\cap\partial(C^{\prime\prime})|+p\left(|B|+|C|-|B^{\prime\prime}|-|C^{\prime\prime}|\right)

over all
B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B
and
C^{\prime\prime}\subseteq C
. We now show that this minimum value equals
\tau_{\triangle}(G)
in the context of our tripartite graph
G=(A,B,C;E)
, which would allow us to complete the proof of Theorem
1
by constructing a packing as before.

It was shown in Section
2
that a (uniform)
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
corresponds to a set of edges
E^{\prime}_{BC}\subseteq E_{BC}
and subsets
B^{\prime}\subseteq B
,
C^{\prime}\subseteq C
such that
W=B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}
is a vertex cover of
G[E_{BC}\setminus E^{\prime}_{BC}]
. If we let
B^{\prime\prime}=B\setminus B^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime\prime}=C\setminus C^{\prime}
, then
E_{B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}=\partial(B^{\prime\prime})\cap\partial(C^{\prime\prime})\subseteq E^{\prime}_{BC}
is precisely the set of edges in
G[B\cup C]
that are
not
covered by
B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}
, so
E_{B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\cup E_{AW}
is a
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
of size

|E_{B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}|+p|W|\leq|E^{\prime}_{BC}|+p|W|.

Conversely, for any
B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B
and
C^{\prime\prime}\subseteq C
, define
B^{\prime}=B\setminus B^{\prime\prime}
,
C^{\prime}=C\setminus C^{\prime\prime}
, and
W=B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}
. So
W
is a vertex cover of
E_{BC}\setminus E_{B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}
, and
E_{B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\cup E_{AW}
is a uniform
\mathcal{T}
-transversal of
G
.

Therefore the minimum value of

|\partial(B^{\prime\prime})\cap\partial(C^{\prime\prime})|+p\left(|B|+|C|-|B^{\prime\prime}|-|C^{\prime\prime}|\right)=|E_{B^{\prime\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}|+p|B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}|

over all
B^{\prime\prime}\subseteq B
and
C^{\prime\prime}\subseteq C
is the minimum size of a uniform
\mathcal{T}
-transversal, which is
\tau_{\triangle}(G)
.

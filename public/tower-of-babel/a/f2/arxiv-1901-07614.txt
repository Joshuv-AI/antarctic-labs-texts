1.
Introduction

Let
\{p_{0},p_{1},\dots\}
be a sequence of polynomials where each
p_{i}
is of degree
i
. Let
\{\xi_{j}:j\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of i.i.d. complex random variables. Throughout the paper, we always assume that the
\xi_{j}
are non-degenerate (that is, their distribution is not supported on a single point). We consider the sequence of random polynomials

(1)

G_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\xi_{j}p_{j},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

The polynomial
G_{n}
has
D_{n}
zeros
z_{1},\dots z_{D_{n}}
, where
D_{n}=\max\{j\leq n:\xi_{j}\neq 0\}
. Define the
zero measure
of
G_{n}
by

\mu_{G_{n}}=\frac{1}{D_{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{D_{n}}\delta_{z_{i}}.

We are interested in understanding the global asymptotic behaviour of
\mu_{G_{n}}
for various sequences of polynomials
\{p_{i}\}
.
In particular, we are interested in finding limits, both almost surely and in probability, of the random measure
\mu_{G_{n}}
.
Here the underlying topology is the weak topology on probability measures on
\mathds{C}
.

This problem was first considered by Hammersley
[
11
]
in the context of the
Kac ensemble

K_{n}(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\xi_{j}z^{j},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Shortly thereafter, Shparo and Shur
[
22
]
proved the first results about the concentration of the zeros of
G_{n}
near the unit circle.

The global zero distribution of the Kac ensemble has been extensively studied (see, for example,
[
12
,
13
,
22
]
).
In particular, Ibragimov and Zaporozhets
[
13
]
showed that the condition

(2)

\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)<\infty

is both necessary and sufficient for
\mu_{K_{n}}
to converge weakly almost surely
to normalized Lebesgue measure
\frac{1}{2\pi}d\theta
on the unit circle
C=\{z:|z|=1\}
.

We can view the sequence of monomials
\{1,z,z^{2},\dots\}
used to form the Kac ensemble
as an orthonormal basis for the space
L^{2}(\frac{1}{2\pi}d\theta)
,
and the measure
\frac{1}{2\pi}d\theta
as the equilibrium measure of
C=\text{supp}(\frac{1}{2\pi}d\theta)
. Based on this observation, it is natural to replace
\{1,z,z^{2},\dots\}
with another orthonormal polynomial sequence with respect to a compactly supported measure

\tau
to form a sequence as in (
1
).
We can then ask if the zero measure
\mu_{G_{n}}
converges to the equilibrium measure of
\text{supp}(\tau)
. Note that in the Kac ensemble case, it is a coincidence that the equilibrium measure and the measure
\tau
are the same.

This approach was first taken up by Shiffman and Zelditch in
[
21
]
.
They proved almost sure convergence results for particular types of measures
\tau
,
in the case where the coefficients
\xi_{j}
are i.i.d. complex Gaussian random variables.
Other investigations in this direction have been conducted in
[
6
,
9
,
7
,
2
,
1
,
14
,
18
,
5
,
19
]
.
In this paper, we continue this line of research. Our primary concern is to find the weakest possible
conditions on the coefficients
\xi_{j}
so that the sequence
\mu_{G_{n}}
converges weakly almost surely to the equilibrium measure of
K=\text{supp}(\tau)
, which we denote by
\mu_{K}
.

In particular, we generalize the Ibragimov-Zaporozhets necessity and sufficiency theorem about the Kac ensemble to a wide class of random sums of asymptotically minimal polynomials with i.i.d. weights. The asymptotic minimality condition that we use is needed to ensure that the underlying sequence of polynomials is linked in some way to the equilibrium measure of
K
, see the discussion after Definition
1.3
. This class includes random sums of orthonormal polynomials with i.i.d. weights where the background measure
\tau
satisfies a weak density condition (regularity) on its support. We also analyze when the sequence
\mu_{G_{n}}
converges weakly in probability, and prove necessity-sufficiency statements in that case.

The specific problem of finding weak convergence conditions for the zeros of random sums of orthonormal or asymptically minimal polynomials has been tackled previously by various authors. The following papers are those with results most directly related to our work.

Kabluchko and Zaporozhets
[
14
]
proved convergence of
\mu_{G_{n}}
to
\mu_{K}

in probability in the case where the
p_{j}
are of the form

a_{j}z^{j}
for certain sequences
a_{j}\in\mathds{C}
under the Ibragimov-Zaporozhets condition (
2
) and proved almost sure convergence in some special cases. Their results include the case of random sums of orthonormal polynomials when the measure
\tau
is rotationally symmetric. Their results also address more general random analytic functions, including random orthogonal polynomial arrays generated from a circularly symmetric measure and a circularly symmetric weight function.

Bloom and Dauvergne
[
6
]
extended this result by showing that
\mu_{G_{n}}
converges to
\mu_{K}

in probability for any sequence of polynomials
\{p_{j}\}
generated
from a measure
\tau
satisfying the Bernstein-Markov property (we will discuss this property later in the introduction)
under the condition

(3)

\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|>e^{n})=o(n^{-1}).

This condition is slightly weaker than (
2
).
They also established almost sure convergence of
\mu_{G_{n}}
to
\mu_{K}
in the case of a rotationally symmetric measure
\tau
and weights satisfying (
2
).

In
[
19
]
, Pritsker considered random sums of polynomials with asymptotically minimal
L^{\infty}
-norm on a compact set
K
. He established almost sure convergence of
\mu_{G_{n}}
to
\mu_{K}
when the i.i.d. coefficients
\xi_{i}
satisfy the moment conditions

(4)

\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)<\infty\qquad\;\text{and}\;\quad\sup_{z\in\mathds{C}}\mathbb{E}(\log^{-}|\xi_{0}+z|)^{t}<\infty\;\;\;\;\text{for some $t>1$.}

Here
f^{-}=-\min(0,f)
. Pristker’s results also hold when
K
is simply connected with empty interior when the second condition above is replaced by

\mathbb{E}\log^{-}|\xi_{0}|<\infty.

Pritsker and Ramachandran
[
18
]
have studied this problem for sequences
\{p_{j}\}
given by Faber, Bergman, or Szegő polynomials where the compact set
K
is the closure of a Jordan domain with an analytic boundary
D
. In this case they showed that condition (
2
) was both necessary and sufficient for almost sure convergence of the zero measure
\mu_{G_{n}}
. All these classes of polynomials fall into the framework of this paper.

In the papers discussed above, almost sure convergence results are only obtained when either the underlying polynomials have a particular explicit structure which makes them easier to analyze, or else the random variables satisfy particular anticoncentration estimates (i.e. (
4
)). In contrast, our proofs only require estimates that can be verified for broad classes of polynomials and estimates from small ball probability that hold for all non-degenerate random variables. This allows us to prove optimal convergence theorems in great generality. Moreover, our proofs of necessity and sufficiency go through in the same way for both convergence in probability and almost sure convergence. In much of the previous work, different types arguments were needed to establish the two different types of convergence despite the fact that the underlying phenomena are quite similar.

1.1.
Main Results

Let
\tau
be a probability measure with non-polar compact support
K\subset\mathds{C}
(note that if
K
is polar, then the equilibrium measure is not even uniquely defined).
Let
\{p_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n,i}z^{i}\}
be the sequence of orthonormal polynomials formed by applying the Gram-Schmidt
procedure to the sequence
\{1,z,z^{2},\dots\}
. We say that the measure
\tau
is
regular
on its support
K
if

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|a_{n,n}|=-\log\capa(K).

Regularity of a measure is a very weak density condition that is satisfied by almost any natural measure (see the discussion in Section
1.3
). We first state our main convergence theorems for random sums of orthonormal polynomials.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Almost Sure Convergence)

Let
\tau
be a regular measure with non-polar compact support
K\subset\mathds{C}
. Let
\{p_{i}\}
be the sequence of orthonormal polynomials in
L^{2}(\tau)
formed by the Gram-Schmidt procedure, and let
\{\xi_{i}\}

be a sequence of i.i.d. non-degenerate complex random variables.
Define the random orthonormal polynomial

G_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\xi_{i}p_{i},

and let
\mu_{G_{n}}
be the zero measure of
G_{n}
.

The measure
\mu_{G_{n}}
converges weakly almost surely to the equilibrium measure
\mu_{K}
if and only if

(5)

\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)<\infty.

In particular, if (
5
) fails, then the sequence
\mu_{G_{n}}
has no almost sure limit in the space of probability measures on
\mathds{C}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Convergence in Probability)

Let
G_{n}
be as in Theorem
1.1
.
The measure
\mu_{G_{n}}
converges weakly in probability to
\mu_{K}
if and only if

(6)

\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|>e^{n})=o(n^{-1}).

Again, if (
6
) fails, then the sequence
\mu_{G_{n}}
has no limit in probability in the space of probability measures on
\mathds{C}
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are special cases of our main convergence result about random asymptotically minimal polynomials.

Definition 1.3
.

A sequence of degree-
n
polynomials
\{p_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n,i}z^{i}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is
asymptotically minimal
on a compact set
K\subset\mathds{C}
if there exists a regular measure
\tau
with
\text{supp}(\tau)=K
and a
p\in(0,\infty]
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|a_{n,n}|=-\log\capa(K)\qquad\;\text{and}\;\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|p_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\tau)}=0.

Note that in the case when
p=\infty
, we can drop the need for a measure
\tau
.

In the above definition, the term asymptotically minimal comes from the fact that the ratio
\frac{1}{n}\log(\|p_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\tau)}/|a_{n,n}|)
converges to the minimal value
\log\capa(K)
. Either this minimality condition or something similar is essentially necessary for the sequence
\{p_{n}\}
to be linked to the equilibrium measure of
K
. Note that the orthonormal sequences of polynomials
\{p_{n}\}
in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
are truly minimal, rather than just asymptotically minimal: for every
n
, the polynomial
p_{n,n}/a_{n,n}
minimizes the
L^{2}(\tau)
-norm among all degree-
n
monic polynomials.

We require the convergence of both the leading coefficients and the
L^{p}(\tau)
-norms (rather than just the ratio) so that different terms are of comparable size when we take a random sum of asymptotically minimal polynomials. If only the ratio
\frac{1}{n}\log(\|p_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\tau)}/|a_{n,n}|)
converges, then the corresponding random sum of asymptotically minimal polynomials need not have any structure. For example, for any sequence
\{a_{n,n}\}
, the polynomials
\{p_{n}=a_{n,n}z^{n}\}
satisfy convergence of the ratio
\frac{1}{n}\log(\|p_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\tau)}/|a_{n,n}|)
, but we can choose the numbers
\{a_{n,n}\}
so that the zero measure of randomized sum
\sum_{i=0}^{n}\xi_{i}p_{i}
will not converge for any choice of the distribution of
\xi_{0}
.

Since regular measures are defined with respect to orthonormal polynomials, the
L^{2}
norm seems to play a special role in the above definition. However, this is not the case. Let
\mathcal{M}_{n}
be the set of monic, degree-
n
polynomials. By Theorem 3.4.1 from
[
23
]
, if

(7)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log(\inf_{q\in\mathcal{M}_{n}}||q||_{L^{p}(\tau)})=\log\capa K

for one
p\in(0,\infty)
, then it holds for all
p\in(0,\infty)
. As discussed above, the minimizing sequence of polynomials in (
7
) with
p=2
is the renormalized sequence of orthonormal polynomials, so we can take Equation (
7
) to be the definition of regularity of
\tau
when
p=2
. Hence a measure is ‘
2
-regular’ if and only if it is ‘
p
-regular’ (in the sense of Equation (
7
)) for all
p\in(0,\infty)
. Before moving on, we remark that (
7
) implies that for any sequence of degree-
n
polynomials
p_{n}(z)=a_{n,n}z^{n}+\dots,n\in\mathbb{N}
and any regular measure
\tau
,

(8)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}}{|a_{n,n}|}\right)\geq\log\capa(K).

This will useful to keep in mind later on.

We can now state the more general version of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
K
be a non-polar compact subset of
\mathds{C}
. Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
still hold when the sequence
\{p_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is any sequence of asymptotically minimal polynomials on
K
.

As discussed above, the sufficiency of condition (
6
) for Theorem
1.2
was proven as Theorem 5.3 in
[
6
]

for Bernstein-Markov measures
\tau
with regular support
K
. These proofs can be extended to include all cases of Theorem
1.2
with a few modifications. The proof ideas can also be used to show the convergence in probability in Theorem
1.4
when the sequence
\{p_{n}\}
has an additional assumption about root concentration or speed of convergence (i.e. see
[
5
]
).

However, the method of
[
6
]
does not extend as easily to asymptotically minimal polynomials without any condition on root concentration or speed of convergence, and does
not extend at all to the case of almost sure convergence.

Finally, in Theorem
1.4
, the condition of non-degeneracy on the random variables is crucial. The asymptotic structure of the roots can change when the coefficients are degenerate. For example, consider the case when
p_{0}=1
and
p_{n}=z^{n}-z^{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
. This is a sequence of asymptotically minimal polynomials for the unit circle
C
, but the zero measure of the deterministic sum
\sum_{i=0}^{n}p_{i}(z)=z^{n}
converges to a
\delta
-mass at
0
, rather than the equilibrium measure on
C
. For a less contrived example that does quite not fit into our framework here, see
[
14
]
, Figure 2.

1.2.
Examples

In addition to orthonormal polynomials, many other natural classes of polynomials fit into the framework of Theorem
1.4
. We give three examples here.

Example 1.5

(
L^{p}
-minimal polynomials and Chebyshev polynomials)
.

Let
K
be a non-polar compact set. Fix a
p\in(0,\infty]
and a regular measure
\tau
on
K
. Let
r_{n}
be a monic degree-
n
polynomial on
K
with minimal
L^{p}(\tau)
norm. Note that
r_{n}
exists by a compactness argument, but is not necessarily unique when
p<1
(see discussion on pg. 84,
[
23
]
). Then we have

(9)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||r_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}=\log\capa(K).

In particular, the normalized sequence
\{q_{n}=r_{n}/||r_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is asymptotically minimal on
K
.

For
p\in(0,\infty)
, Equation (
9
) follows from the discussion above about regularity. For the Chebyshev polynomial case
p=\infty
, the measure
\tau
plays no role and Equation (
9
) holds for any non-polar compact set
K
(see
[
24
]
, Theorem III.26).

Example 1.6

(Fekete polynomials)
.

Fix a non-polar compact set
K
. Let
z_{n,1},\dots,z_{n,n}
be Fekete points in
K
(i.e. a set of
n
points which maximize the Vandermonde determinant
\Pi_{1\leq i<j\leq n}|z_{n,i}-z_{n,j}|
). Let

p_{n}(z)=\gamma_{n}\Pi_{j=1}^{n}(z-z_{n,j})
, where
\gamma_{n}\in\mathds{R}^{+}
is a normalizing constant chosen so that
p_{n}
has uniform norm equal to
1
on
K
. Then the sequence
\{p_{n}\}
is asymptotically minimal on
K
(see Theorem 5.4.4,
[
20
]
).

Example 1.7

(Faber polynomials)
.

Let
K\subset\mathds{C}
be compact and assume that
(\mathds{C}\setminus K)\cup\{\infty\}
is simply connected in
\mathds{C}\cup\{\infty\}
. We can define the Faber polynomials
f_{n}
on
K
as follows. Let

\Phi:(\mathds{C}\setminus K)\cup\{\infty\}\to(\mathds{C}\setminus\{z:|z|\leq 1\})\cup\{\infty\}

be the Riemann mapping satisfying
\Phi(\infty)=\infty
and
\Phi^{\prime}(\infty)>0
. We can write

\Phi(z)=\frac{z}{\capa(K)}+a_{0}+\frac{a_{1}}{z}+\dots.

Define the
n
th Faber polynomial
f_{n}(z)
as the unique polynomial of the form
\Phi^{n}-g_{n}
, where
g_{n}
contains only negative powers of
z
. After renormalizing
f_{n}
to have uniform norm equal to
1
on
K
, the sequence
f_{n}
is asymptotically minimal (see, for example,
[
15
]
).

1.3.
Remarks about the regularity of
\tau
and related conditions

(i) Regularity of a measure
\tau
on
\text{supp}(\tau)=K
is essentially a statement about how dense the measure
\tau
is on
K
. Almost all naturally arising measures are regular. For example, when
K
is regular (that is, the outer boundary of
K
is regular for the Dirichlet problem), regularity of
\tau
is implied by the following rather weak density condition (see Proposition 3.3,
[
8
]
): there exists an
r_{0}>0
and
t>0
such that for any
z
in the outer boundary of
K
and any
r<r_{0}
, we have that

\tau(B(z,r))\geq r^{t}.

(ii) A measure
\tau
with support
K
has the
Bernstein-Markov property
if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that for any polynomial
p
of degree
n
, we have that

||p||_{K}\leq ce^{\epsilon n}||p||_{L^{2}(K)},

where
||\cdot||_{K}
is the uniform norm on
K
.
Any Bernstein-Markov measure is regular (see discussion on p. 67,
[
23
]
). Also, for a regular compact set
K
, regularity of
\tau
and the Bernstein-Markov property are equivalent (see Theorem 3.2.3,
[
23
]
). Much of the previous work on complex zeros of random sums of orthonormal polynomials has focussed on the case when
\tau
has regular support (i.e.
[
6
,
1
]
), where the distinction between regular and Bernstein-Markov measures is unnecessary.

The multivariable and weighted cases

The global zero distribution of random polynomials has also been studied in other contexts.
In particular, much of the potential theory used in the study of one-variable random polynomials can be adapted to the multivariable setting (see, for example,
[
9
,
1
,
2
,
4
,
7
]
).
However, the properties of asymptotically minimal polynomials and orthonormal polynomials used for the proofs in this paper do not have obvious multivariable equivalents.
It is thus unclear if the methods we employ here can be fully extended to this context.

Another interesting extension is to the case of random sums of orthonormal polynomials in the presence of an external field (i.e. random sums of the first
n
orthonormal polynomials in
L^{2}(w^{2n}\tau)
for a weight function
w:\mathds{C}\to[0,\infty)
and a compactly supported probability measure
\tau
). This case has been analyzed when the underlying polynomials have circular symmetry and hence can be computed somewhat explicitly (i.e. see
[
2
,
6
,
14
]
), but understanding the general case here seems quite difficult.

Organization of the paper and a brief discussion of the proofs

In Section
2
, we introduce the necessary background for the paper. In Section
3
, we prove a deterministic convergence criterion for zero measures (up to one result, which we leave to the appendix). Letting
\tau
be regular measure on a compact set
K
,
p\in(0,\infty]
, and
\{p_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n,i}z^{i}\}
, our deterministic criterion allows us to prove convergence of the zero measures
\mu_{p_{n}}\to\mu_{K}
whenever both the ratio
||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}/|a_{n,n-i_{n}}|
is asymptotically minimal for some sequence
i_{n}=o(n/\log n)
, and the values of
p_{n}
on the interior of the polynomially convex hull of
K
,
\Int(P(K))
, are sufficiently large in modulus.

Similar criteria have appeared before when
p_{n}
is renormalized by the leading coefficient
a_{n,n}
instead of a close-to-leading coefficient
a_{n,n-i_{n}}
(see, for example,
[
4
]
, Theorem 1.2 or
[
3
]
, pp. 309-310). However, the random polynomials
G_{n}
that we consider may have leading coefficients which are extremely small with a non-negligible probability (i.e. if
\mathbb{E}\log^{-}|\xi_{0}|=\infty
) so theorems that only allow renormalization by the leading coefficient cannot be applied to our case.

In Section
4
, we use this deterministic criterion to prove the sufficiency of the conditions (
5
) and (
6
) for Theorem
1.4
. To check the criterion, the key step is using a small ball probability estimate adapted from a result of Nguyen and Vu
[
17
]
to show that the values of
G_{n}
on
\Int(P(K))
are sufficiently large.

This estimate was also used in
[
6
]
to establish convergence of
\mu_{G_{n}}

in the case when
p_{j}(z)=a_{j}z^{j}
. To apply the Nguyen-Vu result it suffices to show that for almost every
z\in\Int(P(K))
,
the sequence
\{p_{0}(z),p_{1}(z),\dots\}
is sufficiently spread out. This is done via Cartan’s estimate and understanding the structure of the polynomials
\{p_{i}\}
.

In Section
5
, we prove the necessity statements in Theorem
1.4
. To do this, we show that if the random variables
\xi_{i}
fail to satisfy the required moment conditions, then one term
\xi_{j}p_{j}
for
j\in[n/4,n/2]
in the sum for
G_{n}
dominates the others with an asymptotically non-negligible probability. By Rouché’s theorem, this forces at least half of the zeros of
G_{n}
to lie outside a disk of arbitrarily large radius. This will imply that the zero measures
\mu_{G_{n}}
have no weak limit.

A similar idea was used to prove sufficiency in
[
13
]
(see also
[
18
]
). However, unlike in those papers, our proof requires very little knowledge of the structure of the polynomials and relies mostly on understanding of the sequence of random variables
\{\xi_{i}\}.

Notation

Throughout the paper, we use the following notation. For a compact set
K
, let
U(K)
be the unbounded component of
K^{c}
, and let
P(K)=U(K)^{c}
be the polynomially convex hull of
K
(i.e.
K
with its holes filled in). We write
\partial P(K)
for the outer boundary of
K
. All sequences of deterministic polynomials we consider will be written as
\{p_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n,i}z^{i}\}
. We will let
\mu_{n}
denote the zero measure of
p_{n}
. The polynomial
G_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\xi_{i}p_{i}
will always refer to a random polynomial where
\{p_{n}\}
is a sequence of asymptotically minimal polynomials, and the sequence
\{\xi_{i}\}
is an i.i.d. sequence of complex non-degenerate random variables. We will also write
G_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\zeta_{n,i}z^{i}
for the decomposition of
G_{n}
into a sum of monomials. The coefficient
\zeta_{n,i}=\sum_{j=i}^{n}\xi_{j}a_{j,i}
.

As discussed at the beginning of the introduction,
G_{n}
is of degree
D_{n}=\sup\{i\leq n:\xi_{i}\neq 0\}
, and the leading coefficient is
\xi_{D_{n}}a_{D_{n},D_{n}}
. However, the non-degeneracy of
\xi_{i}
guarantees that
D_{n}/n\to 1
almost surely, and no parts of the proofs are affected by this discrepancy between
D_{n}
and
n
. Hence we will treat
G_{n}
as if it were always degree
n
in order to avoid carrying the
D_{n}
notation throughout the paper.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Convergence of random measures

For a random polynomial
G_{n}
, we will often write
G_{n}(z,\omega)
, where
\omega
is a point in a background probability space
\Omega
. We do this so we can more easily distinguish between two types of almost sure statements: one for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, and one for (Lebesgue)-almost every
z
in some subset of
\mathds{C}
.

Recall that a sequence of probability measures
\mu_{n}
on
\mathds{C}
converges weakly to
\mu
if for every continuous, bounded function
f:\mathds{C}\to\mathds{R}
, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathds{C}}fd\mu_{n}=\int_{\mathds{C}}fd\mu.

The random measures
\mu_{G_{n}}\to\mu
almost surely if
\mu_{G_{n}}(\omega)
converges weakly to
\mu
for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
. The random measures

\mu_{G_{n}}\to\mu

in probability if for every weakly open set
\mathcal{O}
containing
\mu
, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\mu_{G_{n}}\in\mathcal{O})=1.

Equivalently,
\mu_{G_{n}}\to\mu
in probability if for every subsequence
J\subset\mathbb{N}
, there is a further subsequence
J_{0}\subset J
such that
\{\mu_{n}:n\in J_{0}\}
converges to
\mu
almost surely.

2.2.
Potential Theory

Let
D\subset\mathds{C}\cup\{\infty\}
be an open set. A function
u:D\to[-\infty,\infty)
is
subharmonic
on
D
if
u
is upper semicontinuous, and if
u
satisfies the sub-mean inequality. That is, for every
z\in D
there exists a radius
\rho>0
such that

u(z)\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u(z+re^{it})dt,\qquad\text{for all }r\in(0,\rho].

If
f
is analytic on
D
then
\log|f|
is subharmonic.

Now let
K\subset\mathds{C}
be a compact set and let
\mu
be a probability measure on
K
. Define the
logarithmic potential
of
\mu
by the formula

p_{\mu}(z)=-\int\log|z-x|d\mu(x).

For a probability measure
\mu
, the function
-p_{\mu}
is always subharmonic on
\mathds{C}
, and harmonic outside of
\text{supp}(\mu)
. In particular, if
\mu
is the zero measure of a degree-
n
monic polynomial
q
, then

\frac{1}{n}\log|q(z)|=-p_{\mu}(z).

We note here that a measure is uniquely determined by its logarithmic potential (this can be seen by noting that applying the distributional Laplacian to
-p_{\mu}
gives the measure
2\pi\mu
).

The
(logarithmic) energy
of
\mu
is given by

I(\mu)=\int p_{\mu}(z)d\mu(z)=-\int\int\log|z-x|d\mu(x)d\mu(z).

For a compact set
K
, set

(10)

A_{K}=\inf\{I(\mu):\mu\text{ is a probability measure on $K$}\}.

We then define the
(logarithmic) capacity
of
K
by

\capa(K)=e^{-A_{K}}.

A set
K
is
polar
if
\capa(K)=0
(i.e.
A_{K}=\infty
). Any polar set has Lebesgue planar measure zero. We say that a property holds
quasi-everywhere
on a set
D\subset\mathds{C}
if it holds for all
z\in D
outside of a polar set.

Note that any compact set
K
has finite capacity. When
K
is a non-polar compact set, there is a unique probability measure
\mu_{K}
on
K
that attains the infimum in (
10
). This is known as the
equilibrium measure
. It is always supported on the outer boundary
\partial P(K)
, and hence its potential is harmonic on both
U(K)
(the unbounded complement of
K
) and on
\Int(P(K))
(the interior of the polynomially convex hull of
K
). We will use the following characterization of
\mu_{K}
.

Theorem 2.1

(see Theorem A.1,
[
23
]
)
.

Let
K
be a non-polar compact subset of
\mathds{C}
. Then
\mu_{K}
satisfies

(i)

p_{\mu_{K}}(z)\leq-\log\capa(K)\quad\text{for }z\in\mathds{C}.

(ii)

p_{\mu_{K}}(z)=-\log\capa(K)\quad\text{for every }z\in\Int P(K)\text{ and quasi-every }z\in\partial P(K).

Moreover,
\mu_{K}
is the only measure satisfying these properties.

As the equilibrium measure is an essential object of study for us, we restrict our attention to non-polar compact sets for the remainder of the paper.

3.
A deterministic convergence statement

In this section, we prove the following deterministic result about the convergence of zero measures of polynomials to an equilibrium measure on a compact set, up to one fact which is left to the appendix. We will also prove a few facts about potentials along the way that will be used later in the paper.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\{p_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{n,k}z^{k}\}
be a sequence of degree-
n
polynomials and let
K\subset\mathds{C}
be a non-polar compact set. Suppose that there exists a sequence
i_{n}=o(\frac{n}{\log n})
such that the following conditions hold:

(i)

There exists a regular probability measure
\tau
on
K
and a
p\in(0,\infty]
such that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}}{|a_{n,n-i_{n}}|}\right)\leq\log\capa(K).

(ii)

For almost every
z\in\Int(P(K))
, we have that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{|p_{n}(z)|}{|a_{n,n-i_{n}}|}\right)\geq\log\capa(K).

Then
\mu_{n}
converges weakly to
\mu_{K}
.

Note that the second condition is vacuously true if
P(K)
has empty interior. The proof of Theorem
3.1
consists of two parts. We first show that the theorem holds with
i_{n}=0
for all
n
. This is done in Section
3.1
. We then extend this to all
i_{n}
in Section
3.2
by showing how the zero measures of polynomials in the case of general
i_{n}
can be related to zero measures of polynomials satisfying the conditions of Theorem
3.1
with
i_{n}=0
.

3.1.
The
i_{n}=0
case of Theorem
3.1

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we first show the following.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\{p_{n}\}
be a sequence of degree-
n
polynomials and let
K\subset\mathds{C}
be a non-polar compact set. Suppose that the following conditions hold:

(i)

There exists a regular probability measure
\tau
on
K
and a
p\in(0,\infty]
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}}{|a_{n,n}|}\right)=\log\capa(K).

(ii)

For almost every
z\in\Int(P(K))
, we have that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{|p_{n}(z)|}{|a_{n,n}|}\right)\geq\log\capa(K).

Then the zero measures
\mu_{n}
of
p_{n}
converge weakly to
\mu_{K}
.

Theorem
3.2
is essentially Theorem
3.1
in the special case when
i_{n}=0
. The only difference between the two statements is in the apparent strengthening of condition (i) in Theorem
3.2
. However, this is only a superficial difference; if condition (i) of Theorem
3.1
holds with
i_{n}=0
, then condition (i) of Theorem
3.2
also holds by (
8
).

The first step needed to prove Theorem
3.2
is to show that the sequence of zero measures
\mu_{n}
is tight.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
p_{n}
be a sequence of degree-
n
polynomials, let
K\subset\mathds{C}
be a non-polar compact set, and suppose that assumption (i) of Theorem
3.2
holds.

(i)

The sequence
\mu_{n}
is tight, and any subsequential limit
\mu
of
\mu_{n}
is supported in
P(K)
.

(ii)

Let
V\subset U(K)
be any closed set. Let
x_{n,1},\dots,x_{n,\ell(n)}
be the roots of
p_{n}
in
V
. Then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(n)}|x_{n,i}|\leq 0.

To prove Lemma
3.3
, we use the following result from
[
23
]
.

Lemma 3.4
.

(Lemma 1.3.2 from
[
23
]
)

Let
S
be a compact subset of
\mathds{C}
, and let
U
be the unbounded component of
S^{c}
. Then for any closed set
V\subset U
, there exists
a<1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any points
x_{1},\mathellipsis,x_{k}\in V
, there exist
k
points
y_{1},\mathellipsis y_{k}\in P(S)
for which the rational function

r_{k}(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{z-y_{j}}{z-x_{j}}

satisfies the inequality
||r_{k}||_{S}\leq a
(here
||\cdot||_{S}
is the uniform norm on
S
). In particular, when
V
is outside of the convex hull of
S
, we may take
k=1
.

Note that in
[
23
]
, the above lemma is stated for the case
V
compact. However, the proof goes through for all closed
V
.

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a_{n,n}=1
. Let
p\in(0,\infty]
and
\tau
be given by assumption (i) of Theorem
3.2
, and let
\{q_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be the sequence of minimal monic polynomials in
L^{p}(\tau)
, as in Example
1.5
. Assumption (i) and the regularity of
\tau
implies that

(11)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}}{||q_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}}\right)=0.

Now define
P(K)_{\epsilon}=\{z\in\mathds{C}:d(z,P(K))<\epsilon\}
. To prove both the tightness and subsequential limit claims in (i), it is enough to show that for every
\epsilon>0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}(P(K)^{c}_{\epsilon})=0.

Equivalently, it is enough to show that the number of roots
\ell(n)
of
p_{n}
in
P(K)^{c}_{\epsilon}
satisfies
\ell(n)=o(n)
.
Let
x_{n,1},\dots,x_{n,\ell(n)}
denote the roots of
p_{n}
in
P(K)^{c}_{\epsilon}
. Let
a<1,k\in\mathbb{N}
be as in Lemma
3.4
for the sets
V=P(K)^{c}_{\epsilon}
and
S=K
. For every
i\in\{0,\dots,{\left\lfloor\ell(n)/k\right\rfloor}-1\}
, Lemma
3.4
implies that we can find points
y_{n,ki+1},\dots,y_{n,ki+k}\in P(K)
such that

\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{z-y_{n,ki+j}}{z-x_{n,ki+j}}\right\|_{K}\leq a.

Therefore

(12)

\begin{split}\left\|p_{n}(z)\prod_{i=1}^{k{\left\lfloor\ell(n)/k\right\rfloor}}\frac{z-y_{n,i}}{z-x_{n,i}}\right\|_{L^{p}(\tau)}&\leq\|p_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\tau)}\prod_{i=0}^{{\left\lfloor\ell(n)/k\right\rfloor}-1}\left\|\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{z-y_{n,ki+j}}{z-x_{n,ki+j}}\right\|_{K}\\
&\leq\|p_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\tau)}a^{{\left\lfloor\ell(n)/k\right\rfloor}}.\end{split}

As the polynomial on the left hand side above is monic and degree
n
, the minimality of
q_{n}
implies that the left hand side of (
12
) is bounded below by
||q_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}.
By equation (
11
), this implies that
\ell(n)=o(n)
, completing the proof of (i).

For (ii), observe that since
V
and
P(K)
are disjoint closed sets and
P(K)
is bounded, there exist a constant
c>0
such that

\frac{|z-y|}{|z-x|}\leq\frac{c}{|x|}\qquad\text{ for every }z,y\in P(K)\;\text{and}\;x\in V.

In particular, this holds for
x=x_{n,i}
and
y=y_{n,i}
in (
12
), implying that the left hand side of (
12
) is bounded above by

||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}c^{\ell(n)}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(n)}|x_{n,i}|^{-1}.

Since the left hand side of (
12
) is also bounded below by
||q_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}
, Equation (
11
) implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(c^{-\ell(n)}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(n)}|x_{n,i}|\right)\leq 0.

Using that
\ell(n)=o(n)
(proved as part (i)) to remove the
c^{-\ell(n)}
term proves (ii).
∎

We also need a version of the principle of descent which we will use repeatedly in the paper to bound logarithmic potentials. This version of the principle of descent is specific to zero measures arising from sequences of polynomials satisfying assumption (i) of Theorem
3.2
, but eliminates the need for
\{\mu_{n}\}
to have a common compact support.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\{p_{n}\}
be a sequence of polynomials satisfying assumption (i) of Theorem
3.2
. Assume that the sequence of zero measures
\mu_{n}
converges weakly to a measure
\mu
. Then for any
z\in\mathds{C}
, and any sequence
z_{n}\to z
, we have that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}p_{\mu_{n}}(z_{n})\geq p_{\mu}(z).

Proof.

We can assume that
a_{n,n}=1
for all
n
.
Let
z\in\mathds{C}
, and let
z_{n}\to z
. There exists some
r>0
such that the compact set
K
, the sequence
\{z_{n}\}
and the point
z
all lie in the disk
D_{r}=\{z\in\mathds{C}:|z|\leq r\}
. To prove the convergence statement for this sequence, we first approximate
\mu_{n}
by a sequence of probability measures supported on
D_{2r}
. Let
y_{n,1},\dots,y_{n,k(n)}
be the zeros of
p_{n}
(with multiplicity) in
D_{2r}
and let
y_{n,k(n)+1},\dots,y_{n,n}
be the roots outside of
D_{2r}
. Define
q_{n}
to be the monic degree-
n
polynomial whose roots are
y_{n,1},\dots,y_{n,k(n)}
, with
n-k(n)
roots at
z_{n}+1
. Let
\nu_{n}
be the zero measure of
q_{n}
.

By Lemma
3.3
, the measure
\mu
is supported on
K\subset D_{r}
, so
n-k(n)=o(n)
and hence
\nu_{n}\to\mu
weakly. Hence by the usual principle of descent (see Appendix A.III in
[
23
]
)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}p_{\nu_{n}}(z_{n})\geq p_{\mu}(z).

Therefore it is enough to show that

(13)

0\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}p_{\mu_{n}}(z_{n})-p_{\nu_{n}}(z_{n})=\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n}(z_{n})|+\frac{1}{n}\log|q_{n}(z_{n})|.

By canceling the common roots of
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
, the right hand side of (
13
) is equal to

(14)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\prod_{i=k(n)+1}^{n}\frac{1}{|z_{n}-y_{n,i}|}.

Since
|y_{n,i}|\geq 2r
for all
n,i
and
|z_{n}|\leq r
, there exist
0<c_{1}<c_{2}
such that
|z_{n}-y_{n,i}|/|y_{n,i}|\in[c_{1},c_{2}]
for all
n,i
. Combining this with the fact that
k(n)=o(n)
, we get that the above liminf is equal to the same liminf with
|z_{n}-y_{n,i}|
replaced by
|y_{n,i}|
. Hence by Lemma
3.3
(ii), (
14
) is bounded below by
0
.
∎

To prove Theorem
3.2
, we will also need one more theorem about asymptotic magnitudes of sequences of polynomials. The proof of this theorem is rather long, and is very similar to parts of the proof of Theorem 1.1.4/Theorem 3.1.1 from
[
23
]
. As a result, we leave it to the appendix.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\{p_{n}\}
be a sequence of degree-
n
polynomials, and let
K\subset\mathds{C}
be a non-polar compact set. If assumption (i) of Theorem
3.2
holds, then for any subsequential limit
\mu
of
\mu_{n}
, we have that

p_{\mu}(z)\geq p_{\mu_{K}}(z)

for all
z\in\mathds{C}
, with equality for all
z\in\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU(K)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
.

We can now easily complete the proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Again, we can assume that
a_{n,n}=1
for all
n
. By Lemma
3.3
, the sequence
\mu_{n}
is tight. Let
\mu
be a subsequential limit of
\mu_{n}
along a subsequence
Y\subset\mathbb{N}
. We have that

p_{\mu}(z)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}-\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n}(z)|\leq-\log(\capa(K))=p_{\mu_{K}}(z)

for almost every

. Here the first inequality comes from Lemma

, the second inequality comes from assumption (ii) of Theorem

, and the final equality is by Theorem

. Combining this with Theorem

implies that

, and hence

.
∎

3.2.
The general case of Theorem
3.1

We now use Theorem
3.2
to prove the general case of Theorem
3.1
. We start with a proposition that will allow us to exchange a polynomial sequence
p_{n}
satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
with a sequence that satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
with
i_{n}=0
, at the expense of moving
o(n/\log n)
roots.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\{p_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{n,k}z^{k}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of polynomials satisfying
conditions (i) and (ii) of Theorem
3.1
for a sequence
i_{n}=o(n/\log n)
. Then there exists a sequence of polynomials
\{q_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}b_{n,k}z^{k}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
satisfying hypotheses (i) and (ii) of Theorem
3.2
such that
q_{n}
and
p_{n}
share all but at most
i_{n}
roots.

Proof.

Let
\{z_{n,i}:i\in\{1,\dots,n\}\}
be the roots of
p_{n}
, ordered so that
|z_{n,1}|\geq|z_{n,2}|\geq\dots\geq|z_{n,n}|
. Let
r>0
be large enough so that
K\subset D_{r}=\{z:|z|\leq r\}
, and for each
n
let
t_{n}
be the largest natural number such that
|z_{n,t_{n}}|\geq 2r
. In the case when
|z_{n,1}|<2r
, we set
t_{n}=0
. Set
s_{n}=\min(t_{n},i_{n})
, and define

q_{n}(z)=b_{n,n}(z-2r)^{s_{n}}\prod_{j=s_{n}+1}^{n}(z-z_{n,j}),

where the coefficient
b_{n,n}
is a positive real number, chosen so that
||q_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}=||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}
. Now observe that for any
z\in K
, we have that

(15)

\frac{|q_{n}(z)|}{|p_{n}(z)|}=\frac{|b_{n,n}||z-2r|^{s_{n}}}{|a_{n,n}||z-z_{n,1}|\dots|z-z_{n,s_{n}}|}\in\left[\frac{|b_{n,n}|}{|a_{n,n}||z_{n,1}|\dots|z_{n,s_{n}}|}(r/2)^{s_{n}},\frac{|b_{n,n}|}{|a_{n,n}||z_{n,1}|\dots|z_{n,s_{n}}|}(6r)^{s_{n}}\right].

Therefore the condition that
||q_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}=||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}
and the fact that
s_{n}\leq i_{n}=o(n/\log n)
implies that

(16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{|b_{n,n}|}{|a_{n,n}||z_{n,1}|\dots|z_{n,s_{n}}|}\right)=0.

Moreover, if
s_{n}<i_{n}
, then for each of the terms
z_{n,j}
for
j\in\{s_{n}+1,\dots,i_{n}\}
we have
|z_{n,j}|\leq 2r
. In particular, this implies that the equality in (
16
) gives

(17)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{|b_{n,n}|}{|a_{n,n}||z_{n,1}|\dots|z_{n,i_{n}}|}\right)\geq 0.

Now, since the roots of
p_{n}
are labelled in decreasing order of magnitude, Vieta’s formula implies the bound

|a_{n,n-i_{n}}|\leq{n\choose i_{n}}|a_{n,n}||z_{n,1}||z_{n,2}|\dots|z_{n,i_{n}}|.

Since
i_{n}=o(n/\log n)
, we have that

\log{n\choose i_{n}}\leq i_{n}\log n=o(n).

Therefore combining the above inequality for
|a_{n,n-i_{n}}|
with (
17
) gives that

(18)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{|b_{n,n}|}{|a_{n,n-i_{n}}|}\right)\geq 0.

Since
q_{n}
was chosen to have that same
L^{p}(\tau)
-norm as
p_{n}
, condition (i) of Theorem
3.1
for
p_{n}
then implies that

(19)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{||q_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}}{|b_{n,n}|}\right)\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}}{|a_{n,n-i_{n}}|}\right)\leq\log\capa(K).

The regularity of
\tau
implies that the inequalities above must in fact be equalities. This shows that the sequence
\{q_{n}\}
satisfies condition (i) of Theorem
3.2
.

Now, since each of the inequalities in (
19
) is an equality, the inequality (
18
) must also be an equality, with a limit in place of a liminf. Combining this with the bound (
15
), which also holds for all points in
P(K)
, we have that

(20)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{|p_{n}(z)||b_{n,n}|}{|a_{n,n-i_{n}}||q_{n}(z)|}\right)=0

for all
z\in P(K)
. This implies condition (ii) of Theorem
3.2
for
\{q_{n}\}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Proposition
3.7
, we can relate the sequence
\{p_{n}\}
to a sequence
\{q_{n}\}
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
3.2
such that all but at most
i_{n}
roots of
q_{n}
agree with those of
p_{n}
. The zero measures of
q_{n}
converge weakly to
\mu_{K}
by Theorem
3.2
. Since
i_{n}=o(n)
, the distance in the weak topology between the zero measures of the sequences
q_{n}
and
p_{n}
goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
, so the zero measures of
p_{n}
also converge weakly to
\mu_{K}
.
∎

Note that in the proof of Proposition
3.7
, the second inequality in (
19
) must in fact be an equality. This follows from (
8
). This leads to a bound on non-leading coefficients of sequences of polynomials that is analogous to the known bound (
8
) on the leading coefficients. We state this here as a separate theorem as it may be of independent interest.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
K\subset\mathds{C}
be a compact non-polar set and let
\tau
be a regular measure on
K
. Then for any
p\in(0,\infty]
, any sequence of polynomials
\{p_{n}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}a_{n,k}z^{k}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and any sequence of natural numbers
i_{n}=o(n/\log n)
, we have that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\frac{||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}}{|a_{n,n-i_{n}}|}\right)\geq\log\capa(K).

In a private communication, Vilmos Totik provided a proof of Theorem
3.8
prior to us formulating and proving Proposition
3.7
. Totik also observed that the theorem is no longer true when
i_{n}\neq o(n/\log n)
. For example, consider the usual Chebyshev polynomials
p(x)=2\cos(n\arccos(x/2))
on the compact set
[-2,2]
.

Before moving on, we prove one more necessary deterministic result.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\{p_{n}\}
be a sequence of asymptotically minimal polynomials on a compact set
K\subset\mathds{C}
. Then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n}(z)|\leq 0\qquad\text{uniformly on compact subsets of $\Int(P(K))$}.

Proof.

First, by Lemma
3.3
and the asymptotic minimality of
\{p_{n}\}
, the sequence of zero measures
\{\mu_{n}\}
is tight (note that asymptotic minimality implies assumption (i) of Theorem
3.2
). Letting
\mu
be any subsequential limit of
\{\mu_{n}\}
, Theorem
3.6
implies that

p_{\mu}(z)\geq-\log\capa(K)

for all
z\in\Int P(K)
. Therefore by Lemma
3.5
, we have that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|p_{n}(z)|-\frac{1}{n}\log|a_{n,n}|\leq\log\capa(K)

uniformly on
\Int(P(K))
. The second term on the left hand side above converges to
\log\capa(K)
by asymptotic minimality, proving the lemma.
∎

4.
Sufficiency

In this section we prove the sufficiency statements in Theorem
1.4
by checking that the conditions of Theorem
3.1
hold (either almost surely or in probability). We first check assumption (i). We start with two basic lemmas about sequences of random variables.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{\xi_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of i.i.d. complex non-zero random variables, and let

L_{n}=\max_{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}}\frac{1}{n}\log|\xi_{i}|.

(i)

If
\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)<\infty
, then
L_{n}\to 0
almost surely.

(ii)

If
\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|>e^{n})=o(n^{-1})
, then
L_{n}\to 0
in probability.

Proof.

For (i) for
L_{n}
, the condition on the random variables implies that for every
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(\log(1+|\xi_{n}|)>\epsilon n)<\infty.

By the Borel-Cantelli Lemma, this implies that for every
\epsilon>0
, that

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n}\log|\xi_{n}|>\epsilon\text{ infinitely often}\right)=0.

This immediately implies that
L_{n}\to 0
almost surely. For (ii), the condition on the random variables and a union bound implies that for
\epsilon>0
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\exists i\in\{0,\dots,n\}\text{ such that }\log(1+|\xi_{i}|)>\epsilon n)\to 0.

This implies that
L_{n}\to 0
in probability.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\xi_{i}
be a sequence of complex i.i.d. non-degenerate random variables, and let
\{a_{n,k}:k\leq n\in\{0,1,\dots\}\}
be a deterministic triangular array of complex numbers satisfying

(21)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|a_{n,n}|=c

for some
c\in\mathds{R}
. Define
\zeta_{n,j}=\sum_{k=j}^{n}\xi_{k}a_{k,j}
, and let

n-I_{n}=\argmax_{j\in\{{\left\lfloor n-\log^{2}n\right\rfloor},\dots,n\}}|\zeta_{n,j}|.

If more than one value of
|\zeta_{n,j}|
obtains the maximum above set
n-I_{n}
to be the largest such value of
j
. Then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\zeta_{n,n-I_{n}}|\geq c.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{j,n}
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the random variables
\xi_{j},\dots,\xi_{n}
, and let
I_{j,n}
denote the event where
|\zeta_{n,i}|\leq m
for all
i\in\{j,\dots,n\}
. Also letting
I_{n+1,n}
denote the whole probability space, we have the telescoping product

(22)

\mathbb{P}(|\zeta_{n,n-I_{n}}|\leq m)=\prod_{j={\left\lfloor n-\log^{2}n\right\rfloor}}^{n}\frac{\mathbb{P}\big(|\zeta_{n,j}|\leq m,I_{j+1,n}\big)}{\mathbb{P}I_{j+1,n}}.

Now,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(|\zeta_{n,j}|\leq m,I_{j+1,n}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{P}(|\zeta_{n,j}|\leq m,I_{j+1,n}\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{j+1,n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathbf{1}(I_{j+1,n})\mathbb{P}(|\zeta_{n,j}|\leq m\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{j+1,n})

\displaystyle\leq M_{j}\mathbb{P}I_{j+1,n},

where
M_{j}
is the maximum value of the conditional probability
\mathbb{P}(|\zeta_{n,j}|\leq m\;|\;\mathcal{F}_{j+1,n})
. Here the second equality follows since
I_{j+1,n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{j+1,n}
-measurable. Therefore the right side of (
22
) is bounded above by

\prod_{j={\left\lfloor n-\log^{2}n\right\rfloor}}^{n}M_{j}.

Now,

\displaystyle M_{j}=\max\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\xi_{j}a_{j,j}-Z\right|\leq m\;\big|\;\mathcal{F}_{j+1,n}\right),

for an
\mathcal{F}_{j+1,n}
-measurable random variable
Z\in\mathds{C}
.
Since the
\{\xi_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
are independent,
\xi_{j}
is independent of
\mathcal{F}_{j+1,n}
. Therefore

\displaystyle M_{j}\leq\max_{z\in\mathds{C}}\mathbb{P}\left(|\xi_{j}a_{j,j}-z|\leq m\right)=\max_{z\in\mathds{C}}\mathbb{P}\left(|\xi_{j}-z|\leq m|a_{j,j}|^{-1}\right).

Since the
\xi_{i}
are non-degenerate and i.i.d.  there exists a
\gamma>0
such that whenever
m|a_{j,j}|^{-1}\leq\gamma
, the right hand side above is less than or equal to
1-\delta
for some
\delta>0
. Hence for any
\epsilon>0
, the condition (
21
) on the coefficients
a_{j,j}
implies that for all large enough
n
we have

\mathbb{P}(|\zeta_{n,n-I_{n}}|\leq e^{n(c-\epsilon)})\leq\prod_{j={\left\lfloor n-\log^{2}n\right\rfloor}}^{n}M_{j}\leq\prod_{j={\left\lfloor n-\log^{2}n\right\rfloor}}^{n}\max_{z\in\mathds{C}}\mathbb{P}\left(|\xi_{j}-z|\leq e^{-\epsilon n/2}\right)\leq(1-\delta)^{\log^{2}n}.

The right hand side above is summable, and hence by the Borel-Cantelli lemma, we have that

\mathbb{P}(|\zeta_{n,n-I_{n}}|\leq e^{n(c-\epsilon)}\text{ infinitely often})=0.

Taking logarithms and dividing by
n
then proves the lemma.
∎

We can now combine Lemma
4.1
with Lemma
4.2
to show that condition (i) of Theorem
3.1
holds for the random asymptotically minimal polynomials that we are working with.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\{p_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{n,i}z^{i}\}
be a sequence of asymptotically minimal polynomials on a compact set
K
, and let

G_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\xi_{i}p_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\zeta_{n,i}z^{i}

be the random polynomials formed from the sequence
\{p_{n}\}
for a sequence of i.i.d. non-degenerate complex random variables
\xi_{i}
.

(i)

If
\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)<\infty
, then there exists a random sequence of natural numbers
I_{n}
such that
I_{n}=o(n/\log n)
almost surely, and such that for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
,

(23)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(||G_{n}(\cdot,\omega)||_{L^{p}(\tau)}\right)=0\quad\;\text{and}\;\quad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|\zeta_{n,n-I_{n}}|=-\log\capa(K).

Here
\tau,p
are as in Definition
1.3
for the sequence
\{p_{n}\}
.

(ii)

If
\mathbb{P}\left(|\xi_{0}|>e^{n}\right)=o(n^{-1})
, then there exists a random sequence of natural numbers
I_{n}
such that
I_{n}=o(n/\log n)
almost surely, and such that both equations in (
23
) hold in probability.

Proof.

We will prove (i) and (ii) together. First, by the definition of asymptotically minimal polynomials (Definition
1.3
), the random variables
\xi_{i}
and the coefficient array
\{a_{n,k}\}
satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma
4.2
with
c=-\log\capa K
, and so there exists a sequence
I_{n}=o(n/\log n)
almost surely such that

(24)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\ |\zeta_{n,n-I_{n}}|\geq-\log\capa K\qquad\text{almost surely}.

Next, we restrict our attention to
p\in(0,1]
. Letting
L_{n}
be as in Lemma
4.1
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log||G_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}\leq\frac{1}{pn}\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}|^{p}||p_{i}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\tau)}\right)\leq L_{n}+\frac{1}{pn}\log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}||p_{i}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\tau)}\right).

By Lemma
4.1
, the first term on the right hand side above converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
, either in probability or almost surely, depending on our assumptions on the random variables. The second term on the right hand side converges to zero by the asymptotic minimality of
\{p_{i}\}
, giving that

(25)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log||G_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}\leq 0,

either almost surely or in probability depending on our underlying assumptions.
The same computation works for
p\in(1,\infty]
, except that we do not need to raise the
L^{p}
-norm
||G_{n}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\tau)}
to the
p
th power before applying the triangle inequality.

Combining the bounds in (
24
) and (
25
) with the lower bound in Theorem
3.8
implies that both (
24
) and (
25
) must in fact be equalities (either almost surely or probability) with limits in place of the liminf and limsup.
∎

To establish condition (ii) of Theorem
3.1
on the polynomials
G_{n}
, we use a result from
[
6
]
. This lemma is a corollary of a small ball probability result of Nguyen and Vu
[
17
]
adapted to proving convergence of logarithmic potentials.

Lemma 4.4
.

[Lemma 6.2,
[
6
]
]
Let
\{a_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of complex numbers such that

(26)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}|a_{i}|\right)=a.

Let
||a^{(n)}||
be the Euclidean norm of
(a_{0},\dots,a_{n})
, and let
w_{n,i}=a_{i}/||a^{(n)}||
. Suppose that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists a
\delta>0
such that for all large enough
n
, the set

\mathcal{W}_{n}=\{w_{n,i}:0\leq i\leq n\}

cannot be covered by a union of
n^{2/3+\delta}
balls of radius
e^{-\epsilon n}
. If
\{\xi_{0},\xi_{1},\dots\}
is a sequence of non-degenerate i.i.d. complex random variables, then

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left|\sum_{i=0}^{n}\xi_{i}a_{i}\right|\geq a\quad\text{ almost surely}.

By Lemma
4.4
, if we can show that for almost every
z\in\Int(P(K))
, the values
\{p_{i}(z):i\in\mathbb{N}\}
are sufficiently well spaced out, then we can prove assumption (ii) of Theorem
3.1
. To do this, we require Cartan’s estimate on the measure of the set where a polynomial can take on small values (see
[
16
]
, Lecture 11). Here and throughout the remainder of the paper,
\mathcal{M}
is planar Lebesgue measure on
\mathds{C}
.

Lemma 4.5

(Cartan’s estimate)
.

Let
p
be a degree
n
monic polynomial. Then for any
h>0
,

\mathcal{M}\{z:|p(z)|\leq h^{n}\}\leq 25\pi e^{2}h^{2}.

We can now prove the following preliminary version of assumption (ii) for Theorem
3.1
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\{p_{n}\}
be a sequence of asymptotically minimal polynomials, and let
\{\xi_{i}\}
be a sequence of non-degenerate i.i.d. complex random variables. Set
G_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\xi_{i}p_{i}
. For almost every
z\in\Int(P(K))
, we have that

(27)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|G_{n}(z,\omega)|\geq 0\qquad\text{ for almost every }\omega\in\Omega.

Proof.

We first show that

(28)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}|p_{i}(z)|\right)=0

for every
z\in\Int(P(K))
outside of the finite set
F=\{z\in\Int(P(K)):p_{i}(z)=0\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Indeed, since
\sum_{i=0}^{n}|p_{i}(z)|
is nondecreasing in
n
, the
\liminf
of the right hand side of (
28
) is nonnegative as long as at least one value of
p_{i}(z)
is nonzero. On the other hand, we have

\frac{1}{n}\log\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}|p_{i}(z)|\right)\leq\frac{1}{n}\log\left((n+1)\max_{i\leq n}|p_{i}(z)|\right)=\frac{\log(n+1)}{n}+\frac{1}{n}\max_{i\leq n}\log|p_{i}(z)|.

The first term on the right hand side above converges to
0
, and the
\limsup
of the second term is
0
by Lemma
3.9
, yielding (
28
).

Now let
w_{n}(z)=p_{n}(z)/||p^{(n)}(z)||
, where
||p^{(n)}(z)||
is the Euclidean norm of
(p_{0}(z),p_{1}(z),\mathellipsis,p_{n}(z))
. For
z\in\mathds{C},n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\mathcal{W}^{n}_{z}=\left\{w_{0}(z),\dots,w_{n}(z)\right\}.

Let
V
be a compact subset of
\Int(P(K))
. Define

A_{\alpha,n}=\{z\in V:\mathcal{W}^{n}_{z}\text{ can be covered by a union of ${\left\lfloor n^{3/4}\right\rfloor}$ balls of radius $e^{-\alpha n}$}\}.

We will show that for any
\alpha>0
, the set

\displaystyle B_{\alpha}=\Big\{z\in V:z\in A_{\alpha,n}\quad\text{for infinitely many $n$}\Big\}

has Lebesgue measure
0
. Once we have this, Lemma
4.4
and (
28
) implies that for every

z\in V\setminus\left(\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{N}}B_{1/m}\cup F\right),

the convergence in (
27
) holds. This set has full Lebesgue measure in
V
. Since
V
was chosen arbitrarily, this will imply that (
27
) holds for almost every
z\in\Int(P(K)).

If
z\in\Int(P(K))
is such that
\mathcal{W}^{n}_{z}
can be covered by a union of
{\left\lfloor n^{3/4}\right\rfloor}
balls of radius
e^{-\alpha n}
, then there must be two points
w_{m_{1}}(z),w_{m_{2}}(z)\in\mathcal{W}_{z}^{n}
with

n^{3/4}\leq m_{1}<m_{2}\leq 2n^{3/4}\qquad\;\text{and}\;\qquad|w_{m_{1}}(z)-w_{m_{2}}(z)|<2e^{-\alpha n}.

In other words,
|p_{m_{2}}(z)-p_{m_{1}}(z)|<2||p^{(n)}(z)||e^{-\alpha n}
. Now by Lemma
3.9
, for all large enough
n
,

2||p^{(n)}(z)||e^{-\alpha n}<e^{-\alpha n/2}\qquad\text{ for all }z\in V.

For such
n
, the set
A_{\alpha,n}
is contained in

A_{\alpha,n}^{*}=\{z\in V:\text{ there exists $n^{3/4}\leq m_{1}<m_{2}\leq 2n^{3/4}$ such that $|p_{m_{1}}(z)-p_{m_{2}}(z)|<e^{-\alpha n/2}$ }\}.

By Cartan’s estimate (Lemma
4.5
), for any
n^{3/4}\leq m_{1}<m_{2}\leq 2n^{3/4}
, we have that

(29)

\mathcal{M}\{z:|p_{m_{2}}(z)-p_{m_{1}}(z)|<e^{-\alpha n/2}\}\leq 25\pi\exp\left(2-\frac{\alpha n}{m_{2}}-\frac{2}{m_{2}}\log|a_{m_{2},m_{2}}|\right).

The sequence
\{\frac{1}{j}\log|a_{j,j}|\}
has a limit by the asymptotic minimality of
\{p_{n}\}
, and hence is uniformly bounded for large enough
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore the right hand side of (
29
) is bounded above by
k\exp(-\alpha n^{1/4})

for some constant
k
independent of
n,m_{2}
, and
m_{1}
. Hence for all large enough
n
, a union bound gives that

\mathcal{M}(A_{\alpha,n})\leq\mathcal{M}(A_{\alpha,n}^{*})\leq kn^{3/2}\exp(-\alpha n^{1/4}),

so
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}(A_{\alpha,n})<\infty
. By the Borel-Cantelli Lemma,
B_{\alpha}
has Lebesgue measure
0
.
∎

We can now prove the sufficiency statements in Theorem
1.4
.

Proof.

We first prove almost sure convergence under the condition
\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)<\infty
. By Lemma
4.3
, condition (i) of Theorem
3.1
is satisfied for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
for the (random) sequence
I_{n}
identified in that lemma. Now, let
J(z,\omega)
denote the indicator of event where

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(|G_{n}(z,\omega)|\right)<0.

We think of
J
as a function from the space
\Int(P(K))\times\Omega
equipped with the product of Lebesgue measure on
\Int(P(K))
and the natural probability measure
\mathbb{P}
on
\Omega
. Rephrased in this language, Proposition
4.6
gives that

\int_{\Int(P(K))}\int_{\Omega}J(z,w)d\mathbb{P}(w)d\mathcal{M}(z)=0.

By Fubini’s theorem, this equality is also true with the order of integration reversed and so
\int J(z,w)d\mathcal{M}(z)=0
for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
. Therefore for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, we have
J(z,w)=0
for almost every
z\in\Int(P(K))
, or equivalently,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(|G_{n}(z,\omega)|\right)\geq 0\qquad\text{ for almost every }z\in\Int(P(K)).

The above bound combined with Lemma
4.3
(i) gives that

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\left(|\zeta_{n,n-I_{n}}|^{-1}|G_{n}(z,\omega)|\right)\geq\log\capa(K)\qquad\text{ for almost every }z\in\Int(P(K)).

Hence condition (ii) of Theorem
3.1
is also satisfied for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
with the same sequence
I_{n}
, and so
\mu_{G_{n}}\to\mu_{K}
almost surely.

We now show convergence in probability under the condition
\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|>e^{n})>o(n^{-1})
. Let
I_{n}
be the sequence identified in Lemma
4.3
. By Lemma
4.3
(ii), for any subsequence
Y\subset\mathbb{N}
we can find a further subsequence
Y_{0}\subset Y
such that condition (i) of Theorem
3.1
holds almost surely when the corresponding limit is taken over
Y_{0}
with the above choice of
I_{n}
. Also, by the same reasoning as in the almost sure case, condition (ii) of Theorem
3.1
holds almost surely along
Y_{0}
with the same choice of
I_{n}
. Therefore
\{\mu_{n}:n\in Y_{0}\}
converges to
\mu_{K}
almost surely. Since
Y
was arbitrary,
\mu_{n}\to\mu_{K}
in probability.
∎

5.
Necessity

In this section, we prove the necessity statements in Theorem
1.4
, completing the proof of that theorem. As discussed in Section
1.1
, we will do this by showing that one term
\xi_{i}p_{i}
dominates the other terms in
G_{n}
and applying Rouché’s theorem. To do so, we require a few lemmas about the magnitude of i.i.d. random variables that fail the moment conditions of Theorem
1.4
, and one lemma about the magnitude of polynomials on an annulus.

We start with a lemma that will be used to show the necessity of the condition
\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)<\infty
for almost sure convergence.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
\{\xi_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a sequence of i.i.d. random variables such that

\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)=\infty\qquad\;\text{and}\;\qquad\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|>e^{n})=o(n^{-1}).

Let
A_{n,c}
be the event where

|\xi_{{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor}}|\geq e^{(c+1)n},\;\text{and}\;\;|\xi_{j}|<e^{n}\text{ for all }\;\;j\in[0,n],j\neq{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor}.

For every fixed
c\in\real
, infinitely many of the events
\{A_{n,c}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
occur almost surely.

To prove this we will use the following strengthening of the Borel-Cantelli Lemma, due to Kochen and Stone (see
[
10
]
, Chapter 6.2).

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\{B_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of events such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(B_{i})=\infty\qquad\;\text{and}\;\qquad\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(B_{i})\mathbb{P}(B_{k})}{\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(B_{i}\cap B_{k})}=L.

Then
\mathbb{P}(\text{Infinitely many}\;B_{i}\;\text{occur})\geq L.

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

First, let

\beta_{x}=\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|\geq e^{x}).

For each
n,c
, we have that

\mathbb{P}(A_{n,c})=\beta_{(c+1)n}(1-\beta_{n})^{n}.

By the two assumptions of the lemma, we have that

(30)

\lim_{n\to\infty}(1-\beta_{n})^{n}=1\qquad\;\text{and}\;\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{(c+1)i}=\infty,

and thus

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(A_{n,c})=\infty.

Moreover, for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
{\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor}\neq{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor}
, we have that

\mathbb{P}(A_{n,c}\cap A_{m,c})\leq\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{{\left\lfloor n/2\right\rfloor}}|\geq e^{(c+1)n},\;\;|\xi_{{\left\lfloor m/2\right\rfloor}}|\geq e^{(c+1)m})=\beta_{(c+1)m}\beta_{(c+1)n}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(A_{i,c}\cap A_{k,c})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|i-k|\geq 2}^{n}\mathbb{P}(A_{i,c}\cap A_{k,c})+3\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(A_{i,c})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\beta_{(c+1)i}\beta_{(c+1)k}+3\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{(c+1)i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{(c+1)i}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{(c+1)k}+3\right)

Hence we have that

(31)

\frac{\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(A_{i,c})\mathbb{P}(A_{k,c})}{\sum_{i,k=1}^{n}\mathbb{P}(A_{i,c}\cap A_{k,c})}\geq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{(c+1)i}(1-\beta_{i})^{i}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{(c+1)k}(1-\beta_{k})^{k}\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{(c+1)i}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\beta_{(c+1)k}+3\right).}

By the two facts in (
30
), the right hand side of (
31
) converges to
1
as
n\to\infty
. By Lemma
5.2
, this implies that infinitely many of the events
A_{n,c}
occur almost surely.
∎

For showing the necessity of the condition
\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|>e^{n})=o(n^{-1})
for convergence in probability, we need the following lemma from
[
6
]
.

Lemma 5.3

(Lemma 5.5,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
X
be a non-negative real random variable. Suppose that

(32)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}(X>n)>0.

Then there exists a function
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that

(i)

The quantity

C(f)=\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}(X>f(n))

is positive and finite.

(ii)

For every
x,y\in[0,\infty)
, we have that

f(x)+y\leq f(x+y).

We will use the following corollary of Lemma
5.3
. The proof of this corollary is quite similar to a statement shown in the proof of Theorem 5.6 in
[
6
]
. We nonetheless include it for completeness.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\{\xi_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\}
be a sequence of i.i.d. random variables with

\limsup_{n\to\infty}n\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|>e^{n})>0.

For
c\in\mathds{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

B_{n,c}=\{\exists j\in[n/4,n/2]:|\xi_{j}|\geq e^{cn}|\xi_{i}|\text{ for all }i\in[0,n],i\neq j\}.

Then for every
c\in\real
, we have that

\limsup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{P}(B_{n,c})>0.

Proof.

For a function
g:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

D_{n}(g):=n\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|>g(n)),\quad\text{and}\quad D(g):=\limsup_{n\to\infty}D_{n}(g).

We apply Lemma
5.3
to the random variable
\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)
to obtain a function
f
satisfying properties (i) and (ii) of that lemma. Letting
g=e^{f}
, we then have that

(i)

D(g)\in(0,\infty)
.

(ii)

For every
x,y\in[0,\infty)
, we have that

g(x+y)\geq e^{y}g(x).

For
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
, define
g_{\alpha}(x):=g(\alpha x)
. Observe that
\alpha D(g_{\alpha})=D(g)
.
Now define

E_{n,\alpha}=\#\{i\leq n:|\xi_{i}|>g_{\alpha}(n)\}\qquad\;\text{and}\;\qquad\tilde{E}_{n,\alpha}=\#\{i\in[n/4,n/2]:|\xi_{i}|>g_{\alpha}(n)\}

For each
\alpha
,
E_{n,\alpha}
is a binomial random variable with
n
trials and mean
D_{n}(g_{\alpha})
. The random variable
\tilde{E}_{n,\alpha}
is binomial with
m_{n}:=\left|\mathds{Z}\cap[n/4,n/2]\right|
trials and mean
D_{n}(g_{\alpha})m_{n}/n
. Of course,
m_{n}/n\to 1/4
as
n\to\infty
.

Now fix
c\in[0,\infty)
. For any
\alpha>c
, there exists a subsequence
Y\subset\mathbb{N}
such that

\lim_{n\in Y}\mathbb{E}\tilde{E}_{n,\alpha}=\frac{D(g)}{4\alpha},\quad\text{whereas}\quad\limsup_{n\in Y}\mathbb{E}E_{n,\alpha-c}\leq\frac{D(g)}{\alpha-c}.

Therefore for large enough
\alpha
, property (i) and Poisson convergence for binomial random variables implies that

(33)

\limsup_{n\in Y}\Big(\mathbb{P}(\tilde{E}_{n,\alpha}=1)-\mathbb{P}(E_{n,\alpha-c}\geq 2)\Big)>0.

By property (ii) of the function
g
, we have that

B_{n,c}\subset\{\tilde{E}_{n,\alpha}=1,E_{n,\alpha-c}<2\},

and hence (
33
) implies the lemma.
∎

The next lemma bounds the magnitude of a monic polynomial on an annulus.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
. Then for any monic polynomial
q
of degree
n\geq 1
, we can find a simple closed curve
C
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

C
is contained in the annulus
A_{r_{1},r_{2}}=\{z:r_{1}<|z|<r_{2}\}
.

(ii)

The disk
D_{r_{1}}=\{z:|z|<r_{1}\}
is contained in the interior of
C
.

(iii)

|q(z)|\geq[(r_{2}-r_{1})/5]^{n}
for all
z\in C
.

Proof.

Fix a polynomial
q
of degree
n
, and let
\mu
be uniform measure on the roots of
q
. For
\alpha>0
, let

S_{\alpha}=\left\{z\in\mathds{C}:\frac{1}{n}\log|q(z)|\leq\log\alpha\right\}.

By Theorem 5.2.5 in
[
20
]
,
\capa(S_{\alpha})\leq\alpha
. By a standard estimate on the diameter of a connected set in terms of its capacity (see
[
20
]
, Theorem 5.3.2), the diameter of each of the connected components of
S_{\alpha}
is at most
4\alpha
. Hence there is a simple closed curve
C
contained in the annulus
A_{r_{1},r_{2}}
which avoids the set
S_{(r_{2}-r_{1})/5}
and contains
D_{r_{1}}
in its interior. The curve
C
satisfies the conditions of the lemma.
∎

We are now ready to prove the necessity statements in Theorem
1.4
. For this lemma, we use the notation
A_{n,c}
and
B_{n,c}
for the events in Lemma
5.1
and Corollary
5.4
.

Proof.

The necessity of the condition
\mathbb{P}(|\xi_{0}|>e^{n})=o(n^{-1})
for convergence in probability:

Since
|p_{n}|=|a_{n,n}|e^{-np_{\mu_{n}}}
, we can bound the value of
|p_{n}|
above by bounding
|a_{n,n}|
above and
p_{\mu_{n}}
below. By the asymptotic minimality of
p_{n}
, there exists
c>0
such that
|a_{n,n}|\leq c^{n}
for all
n
. By the asymptotic minimality of
p_{n}
, Lemma
3.5
, and Theorem
3.6
, for any compact set
K
, the logarithmic potentials
\{p_{\mu_{n}}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
are uniformly bounded below on
K
.

Hence for every
r>0
there exists a constant
b_{r}>0
such that

(34)

|p_{n}(z)|\leq{b_{r}^{n}}

for all
z\in D_{r+1}=\{z:|z|<r+1\}
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Also, by the asymptotic minimality of the polynomials
\{p_{n}\}
, we can find a constant
d>1
such that
|a_{n,n}|\geq d^{-n}
for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence by Lemma
5.5
, for all large enough
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we can find a simple closed curve
C_{j}\subset\{z:|z|\in(r,r+1)\}
containing the disk
D_{r}
, such that

(35)

|p_{j}(z)|\geq\left(\frac{1}{5d}\right)^{j}\qquad\text{ for all }z\in C_{j}.

Combining (
34
) and (
35
), we can choose
c>0
such that for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for every
j\in[n/4,n/2]
we have that

(36)

e^{cn}|p_{j}(z)|>\sum_{i\in[0,n]}\left|p_{i}(z)\right|\qquad\text{ for all }z\in C_{j}.

In particular, this implies that for large enough
n
, on the event
B_{n,c}
there exists a (random)
J\in[n/4,n/2]
such that

(37)

|\xi_{J}p_{J}(z)|>\sum_{i\in[0,n],i\neq j}|\xi_{i}||p_{i}(z)|\geq\left|\sum_{i\in[0,n],i\neq j}\xi_{i}p_{i}(z)\right|\qquad\text{ for all }z\in C_{J}.

Hence by Rouché’s Theorem,
\mu_{G_{n}}(D_{r})\leq 1/2
on the event
B_{n,c}
. By Corollary
5.4
, this implies that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\mu_{G_{n}}(D_{r})\leq 1/2)>0.

Since
r>0
was arbitrary, the sequence
\mu_{G_{n}}
cannot have a limit in probability.

The necessity of the condition
\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)<\infty
for almost sure convergence:

To prove the necessity of
\mathbb{E}\log(1+|\xi_{0}|)<\infty
for almost sure convergence, we may assume that the random variables
\xi_{i}
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
5.1
, since otherwise the sequence
\{\mu_{G_{n}}\}
does not even converge in probability.

In this case, for almost every
\omega\in\Omega
, for every
c>0
infinitely many of the events
A_{n,c}
occur almost surely, where the events
A_{n,c}
are as in Lemma
5.1
. Moreover,
A_{n,c}\subset B_{n,c}
for all
n
. Hence by the above argument, the sequence
\mu_{G_{n}}
has no almost sure limit.
∎

6.
Appendix: Proof of Theorem
3.6

For this proof, we introduce the
fine topology
on
\mathds{C}
. This is the coarsest topology on
\mathds{C}
that makes every subharmonic function continuous. In particular, we will use that if
A
is a connected open set in the usual Euclidean topology, then the fine boundary of
A
and the Euclidean boundary of
A
coincide (see
[
23
]
, Appendix II).

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
a_{n,n}=1
. We also let
p\in(0,\infty]
and
\tau
be a regular measure on
K
such that assumption (i) of Theorem
3.2
holds.

The sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}
is tight by Lemma
3.3
with subsequential limits supported on
P(K)
. Let
\mu_{n_{i}}
be a convergent subsequence of
\mu_{n}
with limit
\mu
. For ease of notation, we relabel
\mu_{n_{i}}=\mu_{n}
.
We will show that

(38)

-p_{\mu}(z)\geq\log\capa(K)

for every
z\in\partial P(K)
. Suppose not. Then letting

A_{\delta}:=\{z\in\mathds{C}:-p_{\mu}(z)-\log\capa(K)<-\delta\},

there must exist some
\delta>0
such that
\partial P(K)\cap A_{\delta}\neq\emptyset
. We show that
A_{\delta}\cap\text{supp}(\mu)\neq\emptyset
.

The function
-p_{\mu}
is upper semicontinuous, so each
A_{\delta}
is open. Also, subharmonicity of
-p_{\mu}
implies that each component of
A_{\delta}
is simply connected by the sub-mean value property. We now restrict our attention to one component
A_{\delta}^{*}
of
A_{\delta}
that intersects
\partial P(K)
.

The set
A_{\delta}^{*}
has the same boundary in the fine topology as it does in the usual Euclidean topology (see the discussion preceding the proof). Therefore
-p_{\mu}(z)=\log\capa(K)-\delta
for all
z
in the Euclidean boundary
\partial A_{\delta}^{*}
.

Moreover, for any
\eta>\delta
, we can apply the same argument to
A_{\eta}^{*}=\{z\in A_{\delta}^{*}:-p_{\mu}(z)-\log\capa(K)<-\eta\}
. This gives that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muA_{\eta}^{*}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subset A_{\delta}^{*}
. If
p_{\mu}
were harmonic on
A_{\delta}^{*}
, then it would be continuous on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muA_{\eta}^{*}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, and thus have an interior minimum on
A_{\eta}^{*}
, violating the minimum principle. Therefore
p_{\mu}
is not harmonic on
A_{\delta}^{*}
, and hence
A_{\delta}^{*}\cap\text{supp}(\mu)\neq\emptyset.

We can thus find an open connected set
B
such that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subset A_{\delta}
,
B\cap U(K)\neq\emptyset
, and
B\cap\text{supp}(\mu)\neq 0
. Let
S\subset B
be a compact set such that
\mu(\Int(S))>0
.

By construction,
S
is contained in the unbounded component of the complement of
P(K)\setminus B
. Therefore we can invoke Lemma
3.4
applied to the sets
P(K)\setminus B
and
S
. Let
\{x_{n,i}:i\leq k(n)\}
be the set of roots of
p_{n}
on
S
. Since
\mu(\Int(S))>0
, we have that
\liminf_{n\to\infty}k(n)/n>0
. By Lemma
3.4
, for any function
g:\mathbb{N}\to[1,\infty)
with

(39)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log g(n)=0,

we can find a sequence of integers
\{\ell(n):n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and rational functions

\theta_{n}(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{\ell(n)}\frac{z-y_{n,i}}{z-x_{n,i}}\qquad\text{ such that }\qquad\limsup_{n\to\infty}g(n)||\theta_{n}||_{P(K)\setminus B}<\frac{1}{2}\quad\;\text{and}\;\quad\ell(n)=o(n).

In the above formula, recall that
||\cdot||_{P(K)\setminus B}
is the uniform norm on
P(K)\setminus B
. We will apply this to the function

g(n)=\frac{||p_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}}{||q_{n}||_{L^{p}(\tau)}},

where
q_{n}
is the normalized
L^{p}(\tau)
-minimal polynomial of degree
n
(see Example
1.5
). The function
g
satisfies condition (
39
) by assumption (i) of Theorem
3.2
and the regularity of
\tau
.

Now set

r_{n}=\theta_{n}p_{n},

and let
\nu_{n}
be uniform measure on the roots of
r_{n}
. We will show that the functions
r_{n}
contradict the minimality of the sequence
\{q_{n}\}
, therefore allowing us to conclude the inequality (
38
).

First, since the zeros of
r_{n}
and
p_{n}
are the same up to a set of size
\ell(n)=o(n)
, we have that

\nu_{n}\to\mu

weakly as
n\to\infty
. Also, because
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muB\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is contained in
A_{\delta}
, Lemma
3.5
implies that

||r_{n}||_{B}\leq[\capa(K)]^{n}e^{-\delta n/2}

for large enough
n
.
We now split into two cases, depending on whether
p\in(0,\infty)
, or
p=\infty
. First assume that
p=\infty
. Using the bound on
\theta_{n}
, for all large enough
n
we have that

\displaystyle||r_{n}||_{L^{\infty}(\tau)}=||r_{n}||_{K}

\displaystyle\leq\max(||r_{n}||_{B},||r_{n}||_{P(K)\setminus B})

\displaystyle\leq\max\left([\capa(K)]^{n}e^{-\delta n/2},||\theta_{n}||_{P(K)\setminus B}||p_{n}||_{P(K)\setminus B}\right)

\displaystyle<||q_{n}||_{K}.

In the final line, we have used that
||q_{n}||_{K}\geq[\capa(K)]^{n}
(see Theorem III.15 in
[
24
]
). As
r_{n}
is a monic polynomial of degree
n
, this contradicts the minimality of
q_{n}
.

The case when
p\in(0,\infty)
is similar. For all large enough
n
we have that

\displaystyle||r_{n}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\tau)}

\displaystyle=\int_{B}|r_{n}|^{p}d\mu+\int_{P(K)\setminus B}|r_{n}|^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle<\tau(B)||r_{n}||^{p}_{B}+||\theta_{n}||^{p}_{P(K)\setminus B}\int_{P(K)\setminus B}|r_{n}|^{p}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq[\capa(K)]^{np}e^{-\delta np/2}+\frac{1}{2^{p}}||q_{n}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\tau)}<||q_{n}||^{p}_{L^{p}(\tau)}.

Again, this contradicts the minimality of
q_{n}
, and so we can conclude (
38
).

Now, the inequality (
38
) and the characterization of
p_{\mu_{K}}
in Theorem
2.1
imply that
p_{\mu_{K}}-p_{\mu}\geq 0
for quasi-every
z\in\partial P(K)
. We now think of
p_{\mu_{K}}-p_{\mu}
as a function on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU(K)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\cup\{\infty\}
. This function is harmonic on the interior
U(K)\cup\{\infty\}
. Moreover, it is continuous at the boundary
\partial P(K)
since subharmonic functions are continuous in the fine topology, and the Euclidean and fine boundaries of connected open sets coincide. Therefore by a variant of the minimum principle for harmonic functions, (see Theorem III.28 in
[
24
]
), we have that

(40)

p_{\mu_{K}}-p_{\mu}\geq 0

for all
z\in U(K)
. Also,

\lim_{z\to\infty}[p_{\mu_{K}}(z)-p_{\mu}(z)]=0.

Therefore by (
40
) and the minimum principle again,
p_{\mu_{K}}-p_{\mu}=0
on
U(K)
, and hence also on
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU(K)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
by continuity. Moreover,
\mu_{K}
is supported on
\partial P(K)=\partial U(K)\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muU(K)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
, so the principle of domination from potential theory (see Appendix A.III,
[
23
]
) implies that
p_{\mu}(z)\geq p_{\mu_{K}}(z)
for all
z\in\mathds{C}
.
∎

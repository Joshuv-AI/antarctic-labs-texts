Equivariant Gromov–Witten Invariants of Algebraic GKM Manifolds This paper is a contribution to the Special Issue on Combinatorics of Moduli Spaces: Integrability, Cohomology, Quantisation, and Beyond. The full collection is available at http://www.emis.de/journals/SIGMA/moduli-spaces-2016.html
Chiu-Chu Melissa LIU † and Artan SHESHMANI ‡§

Abstract
An algebraic GKM manifold is a non-singular algebraic variety equipped with an algebraic action of an algebraic torus, with only finitely many torus fixed points and finitely many 1-dimensional orbits. In this expository article, we use virtual localization to express equivariant Gromov–Witten invariants of any algebraic GKM manifold (which is not necessarily compact) in terms of Hodge integrals over moduli stacks of stable curves and the GKM graph of the GKM manifold.

Introduction

Gromov–Witten invariants of a projective manifold
X
are virtual counts of parametrized algebraic curves in
X
, and can be viewed as intersection numbers on moduli spaces of stable maps
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
to
X
. If
X
is equipped with an algebraic action by a torus
T
, then
T
acts on the moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
, and the Gromov–Witten invariants on
X
can, by localization, be reduced to the intersection theory on torus fixed substack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
.

An algebraic GKM manifold, named after Goresky–Kottwitz–MacPherson, is a non-singular algebraic variety equipped with an algebraic action of
T
, such that there are finitely many 0-dimensional and 1-dimensional orbits. Examples of algebraic GKM manifolds include toric manifolds, Grassmanians, flag manifolds, etc. If
X
is an algebraic GKM manifold then each connected component of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}
is, up to some quasi-finite map, a product of moduli stacks of pointed stable curves, and localization computations reduce Gromov–Witten invariants of
X
to Hodge integrals, which are intersection numbers on the moduli stacks of pointed stable curves. This algorithm was first described by Kontsevich for
genus zero Gromov–Witten invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
in 1994
[
21
]
, before the construction of virtual fundamental class and the proof of virtual
localization. The moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
of genus zero stable maps to
\mathbb{P}^{r}
are proper
smooth
DM stacks, so there exists a fundamental class
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)]\in H_{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d);\mathbb{Q})
, and one may apply the classical Atiyah–Bott localization formula
[
1
]
in this case. In
[
10
, Section 4]
, T. Graber and R. Pandharipande used their virtual localization formula to derive an explicit formula for all genus Gromov–Witten invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. (See also K. Behrend
[
3
, Section 4]
.) H. Spielberg derived a formula of genus zero Gromov–Witten invariants of toric manifolds in his thesis
[
28
]
. Localization computations of all genus equivariant Gromov–Witten invariants of toric manifolds can be found in
[
23
]
.

The main purpose of this paper is to provide details of the virtual localization calculations of all genus equivariant Gromov–Witten invariants for general algebraic GKM manifolds described on pp. 20–21 of preprint version of
[
10
]

.
, for readers who are interested in such details. We do not assume the reader is familiar with GKM manifolds and Gromov–Witten theory: in Section
2
, we define algebraic GKM manifolds and their GKM graphs, following
[
9

; in Section

4
: we compute all genus equivariant descendant Gromov–Witten invariants of an arbitrary algebraic GKM manifold by virtual localization, and express the answer in terms of Hodge integrals and the GKM graph of the algebraic GKM manifold. Most of Section
4
is the straightforward generalization of the
\mathbb{P}^{r}

[

]
(genus 0) and

[

,
[

(higher genus); see also

2
Algebraic GKM manifolds

In this section, we review the geometry of algebraic GKM manifolds, following
[
9
]
, and introduce the GKM graph associated to an algebraic GKM manifold, following
[
11
]
. The GKM graph in this paper can be non-compact since we consider algebraic GKM manifolds which are not necessarily compact. In Section
4
, we will see that the GKM graph contains all the information needed for computing the Gromov–Witten invariants and the equivariant Gromov–Witten invariants of the GKM manifold.

2.1
Basic notation

In this paper, we work over
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
X
be a non-singular algebraic variety of dimension
r
. We say that
X
is an algebraic GKM manifold if it is equipped with an algebraic action of a complex algebraic torus
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}
with only finitely many torus fixed points and finitely many 1-dimensional orbits.

Let
N=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{*},T)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{m}
be the lattice of 1-parameter subgroups of
T
, and let
M=\mathrm{Hom}(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})
be the lattice of irreducible characters of
T
. Then
M=\mathrm{Hom}(N,\mathbb{Z})
is the dual lattice of
N
. Let
N_{\mathbb{R}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
and
M_{\mathbb{R}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
, so that they are dual real vector spaces of dimension
m
. Let
N_{\mathbb{Q}}=N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
and let
M_{\mathbb{Q}}=M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. Then
M_{\mathbb{Q}}
can be canonically identified with
H^{2}_{T}({\rm point};\mathbb{Q})
.

Let

\displaystyle R_{T}:=H^{*}_{T}(\{{\rm point}\};\mathbb{Q})=H^{*}(BT;\mathbb{Q})=\mathbb{Q}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}]

be the
T
-equivariant cohomology of a point, where
u_{i}\in H^{2}_{T}(BT;\mathbb{Q})
. Let
Q_{T}=\mathbb{Q}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
be the fractional field of
R_{T}
.

We make the following assumption on
X
.

Assumption 2.1
.

1.

The set
X^{T}
of
T
fixed points in
X
is non-empty.

2.

The closure of a
1
-dimensional orbit is either a complex projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or a complex affine line
\mathbb{C}
.

Note that 1) and 2) hold when
X
is a proper algebraic GKM manifold. Indeed, if
X
is a proper algebraic GKM manifold then the closure of any 1-dimensional orbit is
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Example 2.2
.

If
X
is a non-singular toric variety defined by a finite fan, then
X
is an algebraic GKM manifold.

Example 2.3

(the Grassmannian
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
)
.

Let
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
be the set of
k
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. It is a nonsingular projective variety of dimension
k(m-k)
. Let
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}
act on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
by

\displaystyle(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\cdot(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})=(t_{1}z_{1},\ldots,t_{m}z_{m}).

Given
t\in T
, let
\phi_{t}\colon\mathbb{C}^{m}\to\mathbb{C}^{m}
be defined by
\phi_{t}(z)=t\cdot z
. Let
T
act on
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
by
t\cdot V=\phi_{t}(V)
, where
V
is a
k
-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{m}
. Given
J\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}
, let
J^{c}:=\{1,\ldots,m\}\setminus J
, and define

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{J}:=\big\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}\colon z_{i}=0\text{ if }i\in J^{c}\big\}\cong\mathbb{C}^{|J|}.

Note that
\phi_{t}\big(\mathbb{C}^{J}\big)=\mathbb{C}^{J}
for any
t\in T
,
J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

The torus-fixed points in
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
are

\displaystyle{\rm Gr}(k,m)^{T}=\big\{\mathbb{C}^{J}\colon J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},\,|J|=k\big\}.

So there are
{m\choose k}
torus-fixed points in
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
.

Let
\mathbb{C}^{J}
and
\mathbb{C}^{J^{\prime}}
be distinct
T
-fixed points in
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
. Then
\mathbb{C}^{J}\cap\mathbb{C}^{J^{\prime}}=\mathbb{C}^{J\cap J^{\prime}}
. There is a torus-fixed line connecting
\mathbb{C}^{J}
and
\mathbb{C}^{J^{\prime}}
if and only of
|J\cap J^{\prime}|=k-1
. In this case,
|J\cup J^{\prime}|=k+1
. The
T
-fixed lines in
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
are

\displaystyle\big\{\ell_{I,K}\colon I\subset K\subset\{1,\ldots,m\},\,|I|=k-1,\,|K|=k+1\big\},

where

\displaystyle\ell_{I,K}=\big\{V\in{\rm Gr}(k,m)\colon\mathbb{C}^{I}\subset V\subset\mathbb{C}^{K}\big\}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}.

Suppose that
I\subset K\subset\{1,\ldots,m\}
, and
|I|=k-1
,
|K|=k+1
. Then
K=I\cup\{j_{1},j_{2}\}
, where
j_{1},j_{2}\in I^{c}
. So there are
{m\choose k-1}{m-k+1\choose 2}
torus-fixed lines in
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
.

2.2
GKM graph

Let
X
be an algebraic GKM manifold of dimension
r
, so that
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}
acts algebraically on
X
.

Following
[
11
]
, we define a graph
\Upsilon
as follows. Let
V(\Upsilon)
(resp.
E(\Upsilon)
) denote the set of vertices (resp. edges) in
\Upsilon
.

1.

(Vertices) We assign a vertex
\sigma
to each torus fixed point
p_{\sigma}
in
X
.

2.

(Edges) We assign an edge
\epsilon
to each 1-dimensional
O_{\epsilon}
in
X
. Let
\ell_{e}
be the closure of
O_{\epsilon}
.

3.

(Flags) The set of flags in the graph
\Upsilon
is given by

\displaystyle F(\Upsilon)=\big\{(\epsilon,\sigma)\in E(\Upsilon)\times V(\Upsilon)\colon\sigma\in\epsilon\big\}=\big\{(\epsilon,\sigma)\in E(\Upsilon)\times V(\Upsilon)\colon p_{\sigma}\in\ell_{\epsilon}\big\}.

The Assumption
2.1
can be rephrased in terms of the graph
\Upsilon
.

Assumption 2.4
.

1.

V(\Upsilon)
is non-empty.

2.

Each edge in
E(\Upsilon)
contains at least one vertex.

Let
E(\Upsilon)_{c}=\{\epsilon\in E(\Upsilon)\colon\ell_{\epsilon}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\}
be the set of compact edges in
\Upsilon
. Note that
E(\Upsilon)_{c}=E(\Upsilon)
if
X
is proper.

Given a vertex
\sigma\in V(\Upsilon)
, we denote by
E_{\sigma}
the set of edges containing
\sigma
, i.e.,
E_{\sigma}:=\{\epsilon\in E(\Upsilon)\colon(\epsilon,\sigma)\in F(\Upsilon)\}
. Then
|E_{\sigma}|=r
for all
\sigma\in V(\Upsilon)
, so
\Upsilon
is an
r
-valent graph.

Given a flag
(\epsilon,\sigma)\in F(\Upsilon)
, let
{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon,\sigma)\in M=\mathrm{Hom}(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})
be the weight of
T
-action on
T_{p_{\sigma}}\ell_{\epsilon}
, the tangent line to
\ell_{\epsilon}
at the fixed point
p_{\sigma}
, namely,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon,\sigma):=c_{1}^{T}(T_{p_{\sigma}}\ell_{\epsilon})\in H^{2}_{T}(p_{\sigma};\mathbb{Z})\cong M.

This gives rise to a map
{\mathbf{w}}\colon F(\Upsilon)\to M
satisfying the following
properties.

1.

(GKM hypothesis) Given any
\sigma\in V(\Upsilon)
, and any two distinct edges
\epsilon,\epsilon^{\prime}\in E_{\sigma}
,
{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon,\sigma)
and
{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon^{\prime},\sigma)
are linearly independent in
M_{\mathbb{R}}
.

2.

Any edge
\epsilon\in E_{\sigma}
connecting the vertices
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in V(\Upsilon)
satisfies the property that:

(a)

{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon,\sigma)+{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon,\sigma^{\prime})=0
.

(b)

Let
E_{\sigma}=\{\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{r}\}
, where
\epsilon_{r}=\epsilon
. For any
\epsilon_{i}\in E_{\sigma}
there exists
\epsilon_{i}^{\prime}\in E_{\sigma^{\prime}}
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon_{i}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})={\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon_{i},\sigma)-a_{i}{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon,\sigma).

In particular,
\epsilon^{\prime}_{r}=\epsilon_{r}=\epsilon
and
a_{r}=2
.

Let
\epsilon
be as in item 2 above. The normal bundle of
\ell_{\epsilon}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
X
is given by

\displaystyle N_{\ell_{\epsilon}/X}\cong L_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{r-1},

where
L_{i}
is a degree
a_{i}

T
-equivariant line bundle over
\ell_{\epsilon}
such that the weights of the
T
-action on the fibers
(L_{i})_{p_{\sigma}}
and
(L_{i})_{p_{\sigma^{\prime}}}
are
{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon_{i},\sigma)
and
{\mathbf{w}}(\epsilon_{i}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime})
, respectively. The map
{\mathbf{w}}\colon F(\Upsilon)\to M
is called the
axial function
.

Example 2.5

(
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
)
.

The GKM graph of
{\rm Gr}(k,m)
is a
k(m-k)
-valent graph
\Upsilon
such that

\displaystyle V(\Upsilon)=\big\{\sigma_{J}\colon J\subset\{1,\ldots,n\},|J|=k\big\},

\displaystyle E(\Upsilon)=E(\Upsilon)_{c}=\big\{\epsilon_{I,K}\colon I\subset K\subset\{1,\ldots,m\},|I|=k-1,|K|=k+1\big\},

\displaystyle F(\Upsilon)=\big\{(\epsilon_{I,K},\sigma_{J})\in E(\Upsilon)\times V(\Upsilon)\colon I\subset J\subset K\big\},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{w}}\big(\epsilon_{IK},\sigma_{I\cup\{j_{1}\}}\big)=-{\mathbf{w}}\big(\epsilon_{IK},\sigma_{I\cup\{j_{2}\}}\big)=u_{j_{2}}-u_{j_{1}},\qquad j_{1},j_{2}\in I^{c},\qquad K=I\cup\{j_{1},j_{2}\}.

We define the 1-skeleton of
X
to be the union of 1-dimensional orbit closures:

\displaystyle X^{1}:=\bigcup_{\epsilon\in E(\Upsilon)}\ell_{\epsilon}.

The formal completion
\hat{X}
of
X
along the 1-skeleton
X^{1}
(defined on p. 194 of
[
12
]
), together with the
T
-action, can be reconstructed from the graph
\Upsilon
and
{\mathbf{w}}\colon F(\Upsilon)\to M
. We call
(\Upsilon,{\mathbf{w}})
the GKM graph of
X
with the
T
-action. If
\rho\colon T^{\prime}\to T
is a homomorphism between complex algebraic tori, then
T^{\prime}
acts on
X
by
t^{\prime}\cdot x=\rho(t^{\prime})\cdot x
, where
t^{\prime}\in T^{\prime}
,
\rho(t^{\prime})\in T
,
x\in X
. If the 0-dimensional and 1-dimensional orbits of this
T^{\prime}
-action coincide with those of the
T
-action, then the GKM graph of
X
with this
T^{\prime}
-action is given by
(\Upsilon,\rho^{*}\circ{\mathbf{w}})
, where
\rho^{*}\colon M=\mathrm{Hom}(T,\mathbb{C}^{*})\to\mathrm{Hom}(T^{\prime},\mathbb{C}^{*})
.

3
Gromov–Witten theory

In this section, we give a brief review of the Gromov–Witten theory and the equivariant Gromov–Witten theory.

3.1
Moduli space of stable curves and Hodge integrals

An
n
-pointed, genus
g
prestable curve
is a connected algebraic curve
C
of
arithmetic genus
g
together with

n
ordered marked points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in C
,
where
C
has at most nodal singularities,
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are distinct smooth points.
An
n
-pointed, genus
g
prestable
curve
(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is
stable
if its automorphism
group is finite, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\big(\Omega_{C}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}),\mathcal{O}_{C}\big)=0.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
be the moduli space of
n
-pointed, genus
g
stable curves, where
n,g
are non-negative integers. We assume that
2g-2+n>0
, so that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is nonempty. Then
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a proper smooth Deligne–Mumford stack of dimension
3g-3+n

[
7
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
. The tangent space of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
at a moduli point
[(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\big(\Omega_{C}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}),\mathcal{O}_{C}\big).

Since
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a proper smooth Deligne–Mumford stack, there is a fundamental class

\displaystyle\big[\,\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big]\in A_{3g-3+n}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q}\big),

which allows us to define

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\colon\ A^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q}\big)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q},\qquad\alpha\mapsto\deg\big(\alpha\cap\big[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\big]\big).

We now introduce some classes in
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q})
. There is a forgetful morphism
\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
given by forgetting the
(n+1)
-th marked point (and contracting the unstable irreducible component if there is one):

\displaystyle[(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1})]\mapsto\big[\big(C^{\rm st},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\big)\big],

where
(C^{\rm st},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is the stabilization of the prestable curve
(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
.
\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
can be identified with the universal curve over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
.

•

(
\lambda
classes) Let
\omega_{\pi}
be the relative dualizing sheaf of
\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. The Hodge bundle
\mathbb{E}=\pi_{*}\omega_{\pi}
is a rank
g
vector bundle over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
whose fiber over the moduli point
[(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}

is
H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})
, the space of sections of the dualizing sheaf
\omega_{C}
of the curve
C
. The
\lambda
classes are defined by

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}=c_{j}(\mathbb{E})\in A^{j}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q}\big).

•

(
\psi
classes) The
i
-th marked point
x_{i}
gives rise a section
s_{i}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}
of the universal curve. Let
\mathbb{L}_{i}=s_{i}^{*}\omega_{\pi}
be the line bundle over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
whose fiber over the moduli point
[(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is the
cotangent line
T_{x_{i}}^{*}C
of
C
at
x_{i}
. The
\psi
classes are defined by

\displaystyle\psi_{i}=c_{1}(\mathbb{L}_{i})\in A^{1}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n};\mathbb{Q}\big).

Hodge integrals
are top intersection numbers of
\lambda
classes and
\psi
classes:

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}\lambda_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{g}^{k_{g}}\in\mathbb{Q}.

(3.1)

By definition, (
3.1
) is zero unless

\displaystyle a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}+k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+gk_{g}=3g-3+n.

Using Mumford’s Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch calculations in
[
26
]
, Faber proved, in
[
8
]
, that general Hodge integrals can be uniquely reconstructed from the
\psi
integrals (also known as
descendant integrals
):

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}.

(3.2)

The descendant integrals can be computed recursively by Witten’s conjecture which asserts that the
\psi
integrals (
3.2
) satisfy a system of differential equations known as the KdV equations
[
29
]
. The KdV equations and the string equation determine all the
\psi
integrals (
3.2
) from the initial value
\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}}1=1
. For example, from the initial value
\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}}1=1
and the string equation, one can derive the following formula of genus
0
descendant integrals:

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}}\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}=\frac{(n-3)!}{a_{1}!\cdots a_{n}!},

where
a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}=n-3

[
21
, Section 3.3.2]
.

The Witten’s conjecture was first proved by Kontsevich in
[
20
]
. By now, Witten’s conjecture has been reproved many times (Okounkov–Pandharipande
[
27
]
, Mirzakhani
[
24
]
, Kim–Liu
[
16
]
, Kazarian–Lando
[
15
]
, Chen–Li–Liu
[
6
]
, Kazarian
[
14
]
, Mulase–Zhang
[
25
]
, etc.).

3.2
Moduli of stable maps

Let
X
be a nonsingular projective or quasi-projective variety, and let
\beta\in H_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. An
n
-pointed, genus
g
, degree
\beta
prestable map to
X
is a morphism
f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X
, where
(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is an
n
-pointed, genus
g
prestable curve, and
f_{*}[C]=\beta
. Two prestable maps

\displaystyle f\colon\ (C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X,\qquad f^{\prime}\colon\ (C^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n})\to X

are isomorphic if there exists an isomorphism
\phi\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to(C^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n})
of
n
-pointed prestable curves such that
f=f^{\prime}\circ\phi
. A prestable map
f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X
is
stable
if its automorphism group is finite. The notion of stable maps was introduced by Kontsevich
[
21
]
.

The moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
of
n
-pointed, genus
g
, degree
\beta
stable maps to
X
is a Deligne–Mumford stack which is proper when
X
is projective
[
5
]
.

3.3
Obstruction theory and virtual fundamental classes

The tangent space
T^{1}
and the obstruction space
T^{2}
at a moduli point
[f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
fit in the
tangent-obstruction exact sequence
:

\displaystyle 0\to\mathrm{Ext}^{0}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\big(\Omega_{C}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}),\mathcal{O}_{C}\big)\to H^{0}(C,f^{*}TX)\to T^{1}

\displaystyle\hphantom{0}{}\to\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}\big(\Omega_{C}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}),\mathcal{O}_{C}\big)\to H^{1}(C,f^{*}TX)\to T^{2}\to 0,

where

•

\mathrm{Ext}^{0}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\Omega_{C}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}),\mathcal{O}_{C})
is the space of infinitesimal automorphisms of the domain
(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,

•

\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\Omega_{C}(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}),\mathcal{O}_{C})
is the space of infinitesimal
deformations of the domain
(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
,

•

H^{0}(C,f^{*}TX)
is the space of infinitesimal deformations of the map
f
, and

•

H^{1}(C,f^{*}TX)
is the space of obstructions to deforming the map
f
.

T^{1}
and
T^{2}
form sheaves
\mathcal{T}^{1}
and
\mathcal{T}^{2}
on the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
.

We say
X
is
convex
if
H^{1}(C,f^{*}TX)=0
for all genus
0
stable maps
f
. Projective spaces
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, or more generally, generalized flag varieties
G/P
, are examples of convex varieties. When
X
is convex and
g=0
, the obstruction sheaf
\mathcal{T}^{2}=0
, and the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}(X,\beta)
is a
smooth
Deligne–Mumford stack.

In general,
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is a
singular
Deligne–Mumford stack equipped with a
perfect obstruction theory
: there is a two term complex of locally free sheaves
E\to F
on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
such that

\displaystyle 0\to\mathcal{T}^{1}\to F^{\vee}\to E^{\vee}\to\mathcal{T}^{2}\to 0

is an exact sequence of sheaves (see
[
4
]
for the complete definition of a perfect obstruction theory). The
virtual dimension

d^{\mathrm{vir}}
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is the rank of the virtual tangent bundle
T^{\mathrm{vir}}=F^{\vee}-E^{\vee}
,

\displaystyle d^{\mathrm{vir}}=\int_{\beta}c_{1}(TX)+(\dim X-3)(1-g)+n.

Suppose that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is
proper
. Then there is a
virtual fundamental class

\displaystyle\big[\,\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\big]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in A_{d^{\mathrm{vir}}}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta);\mathbb{Q}\big).

The virtual fundamental class has been constructed
by Li–Tian
[
22
]
, Behrend–Fantechi
[
4
]
, Behrend
[
2
]
in algebraic Gromov–Witten theory. The virtual fundamental class allows us to define

\displaystyle\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\colon\ A^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta);\mathbb{Q}\big)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q},\qquad\alpha\mapsto\deg\big(\alpha\cap\big[\,\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\big]^{\mathrm{vir}}\big).

3.4
Gromov–Witten invariants

Let
X
be a nonsingular projective variety. Gromov–Witten invariants are rational numbers defined by applying

\displaystyle\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\colon\ A^{*}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\big)\to\mathbb{Q}

to certain classes in
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta))
.

Let
\mathrm{ev}_{i}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to X
be the evaluation at the
i
-th marked point:
\mathrm{ev}_{i}
sends
[f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots
,
x_{n})\to X]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
to
f(x_{i})\in X
. Given
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in A^{*}(X)
, define

\displaystyle\langle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\rangle^{X}_{g,\beta}=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\mathrm{ev}_{1}^{*}\gamma_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathrm{ev}_{n}^{*}\gamma_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}.

(3.3)

These are known as the
primary
Gromov–Witten invariants of
X
. More generally, we may also view
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}
as a class in
H_{2d^{\mathrm{vir}}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta))
. Then (
3.3
) is defined for ordinary cohomology classes
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n}\in H^{*}(X)
,
including odd cohomology classes which do not come from
A^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta))
.

Let
\pi\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}(X,\beta)\!\to\!\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
be the universal curve. For
i=1,{\ldots},n
, let
s_{i}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\!

\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}(X,\beta)
, be the section which corresponds to the
i
-th marked point. Let
\omega_{\pi}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n+1}(X,\beta)
be the relative dualizing sheaf of
\pi
, and let
\mathbb{L}_{i}=s_{i}^{*}\omega_{\pi}
be the line bundle over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
whose fiber at the moduli point
[f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is the cotangent line
T^{*}_{x_{i}}C
at the
i
-th marked point
x_{i}
. The
\psi
-classes are defined to be

\displaystyle\psi_{i}:=c_{1}(\mathbb{L}_{i})\in A^{1}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\big),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.

We use the same notation
\psi_{i}
to denote the corresponding classes in the ordinary cohomology group
H^{2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta))
.

Genus
g
, degree
\beta

descendant
Gromov–Witten invariants of
X
are defined by

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{a_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}(\gamma_{n})\rangle_{g,\beta}^{X}:=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\mathrm{ev}_{1}^{*}\gamma_{1}\cup\psi_{1}^{a_{1}}\cup\cdots\cup\mathrm{ev}_{n}^{*}\gamma_{n}\cup\psi_{n}^{a_{n}}\in\mathbb{Q}.

(3.4)

Suppose that
\gamma_{i}\in H^{d_{i}}(X)
. Then (
3.4
) is zero unless

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+2a_{i}-2)=2\bigg(\int_{\beta}c_{1}(TX)+(\dim X-3)(1-g)\bigg).

3.5
Equivariant Gromov–Witten invariants

Let
X
be a non-singular projective or quasi-projective algebraic variety, equipped with an algebraic action of
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}
. Then
T
acts on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
by

\displaystyle t\cdot[f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X]\mapsto[t\cdot f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X],

where
(t\cdot f)(z)=t\cdot f(z)
,
z\in C
. The evaluation maps

\mathrm{ev}_{i}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\to X
are
T
-equivariant and induce
\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}\colon A^{*}_{T}(X;\mathbb{Q})\to A^{*}_{T}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta);\mathbb{Q})
.

3.5.1
Defintion when
\boldsymbol{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)}
is proper

Suppose that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is proper, so that there are virtual fundamental classes

\displaystyle\big[\,\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\big]^{\mathrm{vir}}\in A_{d^{\mathrm{vir}}}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta);\mathbb{Q}\big),\qquad\big[\,\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\big]^{\mathrm{vir}}_{T}\in A_{d^{\mathrm{vir}}}^{T}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta);\mathbb{Q}\big),

where

\displaystyle d^{\mathrm{vir}}=\int_{\beta}c_{1}(TX)+(\dim X-3)(1-g)+n.

Given
\gamma_{i}\in A^{d_{i}}(X;\mathbb{Q})=H^{2d_{i}}(X;\mathbb{Q})
and
a_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, define
\langle\tau_{a_{i}}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}(\gamma_{n})\rangle^{X}_{g,\beta}
as in Section
3.4
:

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{a_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}(\gamma_{n})\rangle^{X}_{g,\beta}=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}\gamma_{i}\cup\psi_{i}^{a_{i}}\big)\in\mathbb{Q}.

(3.5)

By definition, (
3.5
) is zero unless
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+a_{i})=d^{\mathrm{vir}}
. In this case,

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{a_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\cdots\tau_{a_{n}}(\gamma_{n})\rangle^{X}_{g,\beta}=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}_{T}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}\gamma_{i}^{T}\cup\big(\psi_{i}^{T}\big)^{a_{i}}\big),

where
\gamma_{i}^{T}\in A^{d_{i}}_{T}(X)
is any
T
-equivariant lift of
\gamma_{i}\in A^{d_{i}}(X)
, and
\psi_{i}^{T}\in A^{1}_{T}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta))
is any
T
-equivariant lift of
\psi_{i}\in A^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta))
.

In this paper, we fix a choice of
\psi_{i}^{T}
as follows. A stable map
f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X
induces
\mathbb{C}
-linear maps
T_{x_{i}}C\to T_{f(x_{i})}X
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. This gives rise to
\mathbb{L}_{i}^{\vee}\to\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}TX
. The
T
-action on
X
induces a
T
-action on
TX
, so that
TX
is a
T
-equivariant vector bundle over
X
, and
\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}TX
is a
T
-equivariant vector bundle over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
.

We define

\displaystyle\psi_{i}^{T}=c_{1}^{T}(\mathbb{L}_{i})\in A^{1}_{T}\big(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\big),\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.

Then
\psi_{i}^{T}
is a
T
-equivariant lift of
\psi_{i}=c_{1}(\mathbb{L}_{i})\in A^{1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta))
.

Given
\gamma_{i}^{T}\in A_{T}^{d_{i}}(X;\mathbb{Q})
,
we define genus
g
, degree
\beta

T
-equivariant descendant Gromov–Witten invariants

\displaystyle\big\langle\tau_{a_{1}}\big(\gamma_{1}^{T}\big),\dots,\tau_{a_{n}}\big(\gamma_{n}^{T}\big)\big\rangle_{g,\beta}^{X_{T}}:=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}_{T}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}\gamma_{i}^{T}\cup\big(\psi_{i}^{T}\big)^{a_{i}}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\big\langle\tau_{a_1}\big(\gamma_1^T\big),\ldots,\tau_{a_n}\big(\gamma_n^T\big)\big\rangle_{g,\beta}^{X_T}
:=}{}\in\mathbb{Q}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}]\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+a_{i})-d^{\mathrm{vir}}\right),

where
\mathbb{Q}[u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}](k)
is the space of degree
k
homogeneous polynomials in
u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}
with rational coefficients. In particular,

\displaystyle\big\langle\tau_{a_{1}}\big(\gamma_{1}^{T}\big),\dots,\tau_{a_{n}}\big(\gamma_{n}^{T}\big)\big\rangle_{g,\beta}^{X_{T}}=\begin{cases}0,&\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+a_{i})<d^{\mathrm{vir}},\\
\langle\tau_{a_{1}}(\gamma_{1}),\dots,\tau_{a_{n}}(\gamma_{n})\rangle_{g,\beta}^{X}\in\mathbb{Q},&\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(d_{i}+a_{i})=d^{\mathrm{vir}},\end{cases}

where
\gamma_{i}\in A^{d_{i}}(X;\mathbb{Q})
is the image of
\gamma_{i}^{T}
under
A^{d_{i}}_{T}(X;\mathbb{Q})\to A^{d_{i}}(X;\mathbb{Q})
.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
be the substack of
T
-fixed points, and let
i\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
be the inclusion. Let
N^{\mathrm{vir}}
be the virtual normal bundle of substack
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
; in general,
N^{\mathrm{vir}}
has different ranks on different connected components of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}
. By virtual localization,

\displaystyle\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)]^{\mathrm{vir}}_{T}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}\gamma_{i}^{T}\cup\big(\psi_{i}^{T}\big)^{a_{i}}\big)=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}]^{\mathrm{vir}}_{T}}\frac{i^{*}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}\gamma_{i}^{T}\cup\big(\psi_{i}^{T}\big)^{a_{i}}\big)}{e^{T}(N^{\mathrm{vir}})}.

(3.6)

3.5.2
Definition when
\boldsymbol{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)}
is not proper

Suppose that
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is not proper, but
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}
is. Then the left hand side of (
3.6
) is not defined, but the right hand side of (
3.6
) is. In this case, we use the right hand side of (
3.6
) to
define

-equivariant Gromov–Witten invariants:

\displaystyle\big\langle\tau_{a_{1}}\big(\gamma_{1}^{T}\big),\ldots,\tau_{a_{n}}\big(\gamma_{n}^{T}\big)\big\rangle^{X_{T}}_{g,\beta}:=\int_{[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}]^{\mathrm{vir}}_{T}}\frac{i^{*}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{n}\big(\mathrm{ev}_{i}^{*}\gamma_{i}^{T}\cup\big(\psi_{i}^{T}\big)^{a_{i}}\big)}{e^{T}(N^{\mathrm{vir}})}\in Q_{T}.

(3.7)

When
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
is not proper, the right hand side of (

) is a rational function (instead of a polynomial) in

. It can be nonzero when

, and does not have a nonequivariant limit (obtained by setting

) in general.

4
Virtual Localization

In this section, we compute all genus equivariant descendant Gromov–Witten invariants of any algebraic GKM manifold by virtual localization. This generalizes the toric case in
[
23
, Section 5]
.

Let
X
be an algebraic GKM manifold of dimension
r
, with an algebraic action of
T=(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{m}
, and let
\Upsilon
be the corresponding GKM graph.

4.1
Torus fixed points and graph notation

In this subsection, we describe the
T
-fixed points in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
. Following Kontsevich
[
21
]
, given a stable map
f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X
such that

\displaystyle[f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X]\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T},

we will associate a decorated graph
{\vec{\Gamma}}
.

We first give a formal definition.

Definition 4.1
.

A decorated graph
{\vec{\Gamma}}=\big(\Gamma,\vec{f},\vec{d},\vec{g},\vec{s}\big)
for
n
-pointed, genus
g
, degree
\beta
stable maps to
X
consists of the following data.

1.

\Gamma
is a compact, connected 1-dimensional CW complex. We denote the set of vertices (resp. edges) in
\Gamma
by
V(\Gamma)
(resp.
E(\Gamma)
). Let

\displaystyle F(\Gamma)=\{(e,v)\in E(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)\,|\,v\in e\}

be the set of flags in
\Gamma
.

2.

The
label map

\vec{f}\colon V(\Gamma)\cup E(\Gamma)\to V(\Upsilon)\cup E(\Upsilon)_{c}
sends a vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
to a vertex
\sigma_{v}\in V(\Upsilon)
, and
sends an edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
to an edge
\epsilon_{e}\in E(\Upsilon)_{c}
. Moreover,
\vec{f}
defines a map from the graph
\Gamma
to the graph
\Upsilon
: if
(e,v)
is a flag in
\Gamma
then
(\epsilon_{e},\sigma_{v})
is a flag in
\Upsilon
.

3.

The
degree map

\vec{d}\colon E(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
sends an edge
e\in E(\Gamma)
to a positive integer
d_{e}
.

4.

The
genus map

\vec{g}\colon V(\Gamma)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
sends a vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
to a non-negative integer
g_{v}
.

5.

The
marking map

\vec{s}\colon\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\to V(\Gamma)
is defined if
n>0
.

The above maps satisfy the following two constraints:

(i)

(topology of the domain)
\sum\limits_{v\in V(\Gamma)}g_{v}+|E(\Gamma)|-|V(\Gamma)|+1=g
.

(ii)

(topology of the map)
\sum\limits_{e\in E(\Gamma)}d_{e}[\ell_{\epsilon_{e}}]=\beta
.

Let
G_{g,n}(X,\beta)
be the set of all decorated graphs
{\vec{\Gamma}}=\big(\Gamma,\vec{f},\vec{d},\vec{g},\vec{s}\big)
satisfying the above constraints.

We now describe the geometry and combinatorics of a stable map
f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X
which represents a
T
-fixed point in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
.

For any
t\in T
, there exists an automorphism
\phi_{t}
of
(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
such that
t\cdot f(z)=f\circ\phi_{t}(z)
for any
z\in C
. Let
C^{\prime}
be an irreducible component of
C
, and let
f^{\prime}=f|_{C^{\prime}}\colon C^{\prime}\to X
. There are two possibilities:

Case 1:
f^{\prime}
is a constant map, and
f(C^{\prime})=\{p_{\sigma}\}
, where
p_{\sigma}
is a fixed point in
X
associated to some
\sigma\in V(\Upsilon)
.

Case 2:
C^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
f(C^{\prime})=\ell_{\epsilon}
, where
\ell_{\epsilon}
is a
T
-invariant
\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
X
associated to some
\epsilon\in E(\Upsilon)_{c}
.

We define a decorated graph
{\vec{\Gamma}}
associated to
f\colon(C,x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\to X
as follows.

1.

(Vertices) We assign a vertex
v
to each connected component
C_{v}
of
f^{-1}(X^{T})
.

(a)

(label)
f(C_{v})=\{p_{\sigma}\}
for some
\sigma\in V(\Upsilon)
;
we define
\vec{f}(v)=\sigma_{v}=\sigma
.

(b)

(genus)
C_{v}
is a curve or a point. If
C_{v}
is a curve
then we define
\vec{g}(v)=g_{v}
to be the arithmetic
genus of
C_{v}
; if
C_{v}
is a point then we define

\vec{g}(v)=g_{v}=0
.

(c)

(marking) For
i=1,\ldots,n
, define

\vec{s}(i)=v
if
x_{i}\in C_{v}
.

2.

(Edges)
For any
\epsilon\in E(\Upsilon)
, let

O_{\epsilon}\cong\mathbb{C}^{*}
be the 1-dimensional
orbit whose closure is
\ell_{\epsilon}
.
Then

\displaystyle X^{1}\setminus X^{T}=\bigsqcup_{\epsilon\in E(\Upsilon)}O_{\epsilon},

where the right hand side is a disjoint union of connected components. We assign an edge
e
to each connected component
O_{e}\cong\mathbb{C}^{*}
of
f^{-1}\big(X^{1}\setminus X^{T}\big)
.

(a)

(label) Let
C_{e}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
be the closure of
O_{e}
. Then
f(C_{e})=\ell_{\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon\in E(\Upsilon)_{c}
; we define
\vec{f}(e)=\epsilon_{e}=\epsilon
.

(b)

(degree) We define
\vec{d}(e)=d_{e}
to be the degree of the map
f|_{C_{e}}\colon C_{e}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\ell_{\epsilon}\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

3.

(Flags) The set of flags in the graph
\Gamma
is defined by

\displaystyle F(\Gamma)=\{(e,v)\in E(\Gamma)\times V(\Gamma)\,|\,C_{e}\cap C_{v}\neq\varnothing\}.

The above 1), 2), 3) define a decorated graph
{\vec{\Gamma}}=\big(\Gamma,\vec{f},\vec{d},\vec{g},\vec{s}\big)
satisfying the constraints (i) and (ii) in Definition
4.1
.
Therefore
{\vec{\Gamma}}\in G_{g,n}(X,\beta)
. This gives a map from
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}
to the discrete set
G_{g,n}(X,\beta)
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{\vec{\Gamma}}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}
denote the preimage of
{\vec{\Gamma}}
.
Then

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)^{T}=\bigsqcup_{{\vec{\Gamma}}\in G_{g,n}(X,\beta)}\mathcal{F}_{\vec{\Gamma}},

where the right hand side is a disjoint union of connected components. We next describe the fixed locus
\mathcal{F}_{\vec{\Gamma}}
associated to each decorated graph
{\vec{\Gamma}}\in G_{g,n}(X,\beta)
. For later convenience, we introduce some definitions.

Definition 4.2
.

Given a vertex
v\in V(\Gamma)
, we define

\displaystyle E_{v}=\{e\in E(\Gamma)\,|\,(e,v)\in F(\Gamma)\}

the set of edges emanating from
v
, and define
S_{v}=\vec{s}^{-1}(v)\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The valency of
v
is given by
\mathrm{val}(v)=|E_{v}|
. Let
n_{v}=|S_{v}|
be the number of marked points contained in
C_{v}
. We say a vertex is
stable
if
2g_{v}-2+\mathrm{val}(v)+n_{v}>0
. Let
V^{S}(\Gamma)
be the set of stable vertices in
V(\Gamma)
. There are three types of unstable vertices:

\displaystyle V^{1}(\Gamma)=\{v\in V(\Gamma)\,|\,g_{v}=0,\,\mathrm{val}(v)=1,\,n_{v}=0\},

\displaystyle V^{1,1}(\Gamma)=\{v\in V(\Gamma)\,|\,g_{v}=0,\,\mathrm{val}(v)=n_{v}=1\},

\displaystyle V^{2}(\Gamma)=\{v\in V(\Gamma)\,|\,g_{v}=0,\mathrm{val}(v)=2,\,n_{v}=0\}.

Then
V(\Gamma)
is the disjoint union of
V^{1}(\Gamma)
,
V^{1,1}(\Gamma)
,
V^{2}(\Gamma)
, and
V^{S}(\Gamma)
.

The set of stable flags is defined to be

\displaystyle F^{S}(\Gamma)=\big\{(e,v)\in F(\Gamma)\,|\,v\in V^{S}(\Gamma)\big\}.

Given a decorated graph
{\vec{\Gamma}}=\big(\Gamma,\vec{f},\vec{d},\vec{g},\vec{s}\big)
, the curves
C_{e}
and the maps
f|_{C_{e}}\colon C_{e}\to\ell_{\epsilon_{e}}\subset X
are determined by
{\vec{\Gamma}}
. If
v\notin V^{S}(\Gamma)
then
C_{v}
is a point. If
v\in V^{S}(\Gamma)
then
C_{v}
is a curve, and
y(e,v):=C_{e}\cap C_{v}
is a node of
C
for
e\in E_{v}
,

\displaystyle\bigl(C_{v},\{y(e,v)\colon e\in E_{v}\}\cup\{x_{i}\,|\,i\in S_{v}\}\bigr)

is a
(\mathrm{val}(v)+n_{v})
-pointed, genus
g_{v}
curve, which represents a point in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{v},\mathrm{val}(v)+n_{v}}
. We call this moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g_{v},E_{v}\cup S_{v}}

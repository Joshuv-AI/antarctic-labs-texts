Rigidity aspects of Penrose’s singularity theorem
Gregory Galloway, Eric Ling

Abstract
In this paper, we study rigidity aspects of Penrose’s singularity theorem. Specifically, we aim to answer the following question: if a spacetime satisfies the hypotheses of Penrose’s singularity theorem except with weakly trapped surfaces instead of trapped surfaces, then what can be said about the global spacetime structure if the spacetime is null geodesically complete? In this setting, we show that we obtain a foliation of MOTS which generate totally geodesic null hypersurfaces. Depending on our starting assumptions, we obtain either local or global rigidity results. We apply our arguments to cosmological spacetimes (i.e., spacetimes with compact Cauchy surfaces) and scenarios involving topological censorship.



1
Introduction

Penrose’s original 1965 singularity theorem
[
30
]
, which earned him half of the 2020 Nobel Prize, predicts an incomplete null geodesic (i.e., the singularity) emanating from a closed trapped surface
\Sigma
, provided the spacetime admits a noncompact Cauchy surface and satisfies the null energy condition. Closed trapped surfaces are believed to form during immense gravitational collapse where the spacetime curvature is so strong that, in a vague sense, light cannot escape the gravitational field. Mathematically, they occur when the inner null expansion
\theta^{-}
and the outer null expansion
\theta^{+}
are both negative. In a suitably defined black hole spacetime, closed trapped surfaces must lie within the event horizon.

Focusing solely on the outer direction, a closed surface
\Sigma
is outer trapped if
\theta^{+}<0
, weakly outer trapped if
\theta^{+}\leq 0
, and marginally outer trapped if
\theta^{+}=0
; in the last case,
\Sigma
is referred to as a marginally outer trapped surface (MOTS). For stationary black hole spacetimes, MOTS occur as spatial cross sections of the event horizon. In general dynamical spacetimes, MOTS typically lie within the event horizon, where they can form the boundary of the trapped region.

In this paper, we investigate the rigidity aspects of Penrose’s singularity theorem. Ignoring some technicalities, the question we aim to answer is the following:

If a spacetime satisfies the hypotheses of Penrose’s singularity theorem except that
\Sigma
is only weakly outer trapped, then what can be said about the global structure of the spacetime if we assume that it is null geodesically complete?

In section
3
, we establish a local rigidity result: If the spacetime is null geodesically complete, then
\Sigma
is a MOTS and there is a neighborhood of MOTS around
\Sigma
in the Cauchy surface whose outward null normal geodesics generate totally geodesic null hypersurfaces; see Theorem
4
. If
\Sigma
is also weakly inner trapped, then a spacetime neighborhood of
\Sigma
is foliated by totally geodesic codimension 2 spacelike submanifolds, each diffeomorphic to
\Sigma
; see Theorem
5
. The local foliation by MOTS is obtained via an inverse function theorem argument as in, e.g.,
[
16
,
17
,
11
,
20
,
29
]
. The existence of the totally geodesic null hypersurface is proved in
[
12
]
and follows from the null maximum principle
[
15
]
.

Moving to more global considerations, in section
4
, we establish rigidity results in the case where the Cauchy surface contains a compact, connected top-dimensional submanifold with two boundary components,
\Sigma
and
S
, which are weakly outer trapped and weakly outer untrapped, respectively. In this case, null geodesic completeness implies that the region between
\Sigma
and
S
is foliated by MOTS and each generates a totally geodesic null hypersurface; see Theorem
6
. Our proof relies on a continuity argument used in
[
11
]
.

In section
5
, we apply these results to cosmological spacetimes, i.e., spacetimes with compact Cauchy surfaces, when
\Sigma
is a nonseparating surface. In this case, we obtain rigidity information on the whole spacetime.
In section
6
, we obtain rigidity results within the context of topological censorship. When
\text{dim}(M)=4
and
\Sigma
is topologically a two-sphere, our results are decisive, see Corollary
12
.

Our results can be paraphrased in the following way “in the presence of a weakly trapped surface, either the spacetime is null geodesically complete (future and past) or the spacetime is very special in a suitably rigid sense.” Penrose’s original singularity theorem concluded only future null incompleteness, and our results can be interpreted in this spirit; see specifically Proposition
3
in section
2
. Moreover, sections
4
and
6
are consistent with the setting of gravitational collapse developing from a nonsingular past. In this setting, our results imply that either the spacetime is future null geodesically incomplete or “the spacetime is very special in a suitably rigid sense.” A similar type of interpretation holds in the cosmological setting (section
5
) where one naturally assumes future null completeness. In this setting, our results imply that either the spacetime is past null geodesically incomplete or “the spacetime is very special in a suitably rigid sense.”

Preliminaries

Let
(M^{n+1},g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime,
n\geq 2
. An
initial data set

(V^{n},h,K)
for
(M,g)
is a spacelike Cauchy hypersurface
V\subset M
with induced metric
h
and second fundamental form
K
. Let
\Sigma^{n-1}
be a connected and compact two-sided hypersurface in
V
. Let
\nu
be one of the two unit normal vector fields on
\Sigma
pointing into
V
. We call
\nu
the ‘outward’ direction and
-\nu
the ‘inward’ direction. Let
u
be the future directed unit normal on
V
.
\Sigma
admits two future directed null normal vector fields,
\ell^{\pm}=u\pm\nu
. The
null second fundamental forms

\chi^{\pm}
and
null expansion scalars

\theta^{\pm}
are given by

\chi^{\pm}(X,Y)\,=\,\langle\nabla_{X}\ell^{\pm},Y\rangle

for all
X,Y\in T_{p}\Sigma
and
p\in\Sigma
, and

\theta^{\pm}\,=\,\mathrm{tr}\,_{\Sigma}\chi^{\pm}\,=\,\diver_{\Sigma}\ell^{\pm}\,=\,\mathrm{tr}\,_{\Sigma}K\pm H,

where
H=\diver_{\Sigma}\nu
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
within
V
.

\Sigma
is
outer trapped
if
\theta^{+}<0
,
weakly outer trapped
if
\theta^{+}\leq 0
, and
marginally outer trapped
if
\theta^{+}=0
. In the latter case,
\Sigma
is referred to as a
marginally outer trapped surface
(MOTS). Evidently, in the time-symmetric setting (
K=0
), a MOTS is a minimal surface in
V
. Analogous terminology holds for
MITS
,
\theta^{-}=0
.

Let
\Sigma\subset V
be a MOTS, and let
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{|t|<\varepsilon}
be a variation of
\Sigma
determined by
\Sigma_{t}\colon x\mapsto\exp_{x}\big(t\phi(x)\nu(x)\big)
for some
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
. Let
\theta^{+}(t)
denote the outward null expansion of
\Sigma_{t}
. From e.g.
[
3
, Eq. 1]
(see also
[
26
, Prop. 7.32]
), we have that

\frac{\partial\theta^{+}}{\partial t}\bigg|_{t=0}\,=\,L(\phi),

where

L(\phi)\,=\,-\Delta\phi+2\langle X,\nabla\phi\rangle+(Q+\diver X-|X|^{2})\phi.

Here
X
is the tangential part of
\nabla_{\nu}u
on
\Sigma
and

Q\,=\,\frac{1}{2}S-G(u,\ell^{+})-\frac{1}{2}|\chi^{+}|^{2},

where
S
is the scalar curvature on
\Sigma
and
G
is the Einstein tensor for
M
. We remark that, in terms of initial data
(V,h,K)
,
X
is metrically dual to the one-form
K(\nu,\cdot)|_{\Sigma}
.

In the time-symmetric case (
K=0
),
\theta^{+}=H
,
X=0
, and
L
reduces to the classical stability operator of minimal surface theory. In analogy with the minimal surface case, we refer to
L
as the
MOTS stability operator
. Note, however, that
L
is not in general self-adjoint.

Nevertheless, it is shown in
[
3
,
4
]
(see also

[
26
, Thm. A.10]
) that, as a consequence of the Krein-Rutman theorem,
L
admits a
principal eigenvalue

\lambda_{1}
, which is real and simple. Furthermore there exists an associated principal eigenfunction
\phi
which can be chosen to be strictly positive.
Moreover,
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
(respectively,
\lambda_{1}>0
) if and only if there exists a positive
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
such that
L(\psi)\geq 0
(respectively,
L(\psi)>0
).
In analogy with the minimal surface case, we say a MOTS
\Sigma
is
stable
if
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
.

The following lemma has been used in various rigidity results for MOTS; see
[
16
,
17
,
11
,
20
,
29
]
.
As it does not appear as a stand-alone result in the literature, we provide a proof in the appendix for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a stable MOTS in an initial data set
(V,h,K)
.
If
\lambda_{1}=0
, then there is a neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
in
V
such that, up to isometry,

U\,=\,(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times\Sigma\quad\text{ and }\quad h|_{U}\,=\,\phi^{2}dt^{2}+\gamma_{t},

where
\phi=\phi(t,x)
is a smooth function on
U
and
\gamma_{t}
is the induced metric on
\Sigma_{t}=\{t\}\times\Sigma
. Moreover, the outward null expansion of each
\Sigma_{t}
is constant, i.e.,
\theta^{+}(t)
is constant on
\Sigma_{t}
where
\theta^{+}(t)
is computed with respect to
\ell^{+}(t)=u+\nu(t)
, where
\nu(t)=\frac{1}{\phi(t,\cdot)}\partial_{t}
is the outward unit normal to
\Sigma_{t}
.

The next theorem can be thought of as a ‘one-sided version’ of Penrose’s singularity theorem, and it’s proved in a similar way, see e.g.,
[
2
, Thm. 7.1]
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime satisfying the null energy condition with a spacelike Cauchy hypersurface
V
. Let
\Sigma
be a connected, closed hypersurface in
V
which separates
V
. If
\Sigma
is outer trapped (i.e.,
\theta^{+}<0
) with respect to a component of
V\setminus\Sigma
whose closure in
V
is noncompact, then
M
is future null geodesically incomplete.

The following proposition is a rigid version of the one-sided Penrose singularity theorem.

Proposition 3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime satisfying the null energy condition with a spacelike Cauchy hypersurface
V
. Let
\Sigma
be a connected, closed hypersurface in
V
which separates
V
. Suppose
\Sigma
is a weakly trapped surface (i.e.,
\theta^{+}\leq 0
) with respect to the future directed null normal
\ell^{+}
which points toward a component of
V\setminus\Sigma
whose closure is noncompact.
Then either
M
is future null geodesically incomplete or else
\Sigma
is a MOTS (in fact
\chi^{+}=0
) and the future inextendible null normal geodesics in the direction of
\ell^{+}
form an achronal totally geodesic
1
1

1

By a totally geodesic null hypersurface, we mean that it’s null second fundamental form vanishes everywhere and has the usual interpretation that geodesics tangent to it remain within it.
null hypersurface.

Proposition
3
is proved in the appendix of
[
12
]
in the case that
\Sigma
is a MOTS (
\theta^{+}=0)
, but the proof trivially carries over to the case when

3
Local rigidity

In this section, we present two Penrose-type local rigidity results.

Theorem 4
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime satisfying the null energy condition with a spacelike Cauchy hypersurface
V
. Let
\Sigma
be a connected, closed hypersurface in
V
that separates
V
into two components, both having noncompact closures in
V
. Suppose
\Sigma
is weakly outer trapped,
\theta^{+}\leq 0
, with respect to one of the components of
V\setminus\Sigma
. Then either

(a)

M
is null geodesically incomplete, or else,

(b)

there is a neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
in
V
diffeomorphic to
(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times\Sigma
such that for each
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
,
\Sigma_{t}=\{t\}\times\Sigma
is a MOTS (in fact
\chi^{+}_{t}=0)
and the outward null normal geodesics to
\Sigma_{t}

(past and future complete)
form a closed achronal totally geodesic null hypersurface
N_{t}
.

Proof.

Suppose
M
is null geodesically complete. If there is a point on
\Sigma
where
\theta^{+}<0
, then, by a result of Andersson and Metzger
[
5
, Lem. 5.2]
,
\Sigma
can be deformed via null mean curvature flow within
V
to an outer trapped surface
\Sigma^{\prime}
which contradicts null geodesic completeness by Theorem
2
. Thus
\Sigma
is a MOTS.

Let
\lambda_{1}
be the principal eigenvalue of the MOTS stability operator
L
with respect to
\Sigma
. We show
\lambda_{1}=0
. Let
\phi
be the associated positive eigenfunction and consider the variation
\Sigma_{s}\colon x\mapsto\exp_{x}\big(s\phi(x)\nu(x)\big)
. Let
\theta^{+}(s)
denote the corresponding outward null expansions. Then

\frac{\partial\theta^{+}}{\partial s}\bigg|_{s=0}\,=\,L(\phi)\,=\,\lambda_{1}\phi.

If
\lambda_{1}<0
, then
\theta^{+}(s)<0
for small
s>0
, and hence there are outer trapped surfaces in
V
to the outside of
\Sigma
, contradicting future null completeness of
M
. Similarly, if
\lambda_{1}>0
, then
\theta^{+}(s)<0
for small
s<0
, again contradicting future null completeness. Thus
\lambda_{1}=0
.

By Lemma
1
, there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
in
V
, with
U\approx(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times\Sigma
, such that
\Sigma_{t}=\{t\}\times\Sigma
has constant outward null expansion
\theta^{+}(t)
. We must have
\theta^{+}(t)\geq 0
, otherwise we contradict future completeness. Suppose
\theta^{+}(t)>0
. Note that

\theta^{+}(t)\,=\,\diver_{\Sigma_{t}}\big(u+\nu(t)\big)\,=\,-\diver_{\Sigma_{t}}\big(-u-\nu(t)\big).

Therefore
\Sigma_{t}
is inner trapped if we reverse the time orientation. Since the component in the inward direction also has noncompact closure, this contradicts past null completeness. Thus
\theta^{+}(t)=0
for all
t\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, i.e.,
\Sigma_{t}
a MOTS for all
t
. Proposition
3
along with its time-dual produce two totally geodesic achronal null hypersurfaces with boundary given by
\Sigma_{t}
; one is formed to the future, call it
F_{t}
, and the other formed to the past, call it
P_{t}
. We glue
F_{t}
and
P_{t}
together along
\Sigma_{t}
forming a closed, totally geodesic null hypersurface
N_{t}
. Achronality of
N_{t}
follows since
\Sigma_{t}
separates
V
. Indeed, if
\gamma
is a timelike curve starting on
P_{t}
and ending on
F_{t}
, then the integral curves of a timelike vector field project
\gamma
down to a curve in
V
which must intersect
\Sigma_{t}
at some point
x
. By concatenating this integral curve from
x
with a portion of
\gamma
, we obtain a contradiction either with the achronality of
F_{t}
or the achronality of
P_{t}
.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime satisfying the null energy condition with a spacelike Cauchy hypersurface
V
. Let
\Sigma
be a connected, closed hypersurface in
V
that separates
V
into two components, both having noncompact closures in
V
. Suppose
\Sigma
is weakly trapped, i.e.,
\theta^{+}\leq 0
and
\theta^{-}\leq 0
with respect to one of the unit normals. Then either

(a)

M
is null geodesically incomplete, or else,

(b)

\theta^{\pm}=0
and there are neighborhoods
U^{\pm}\approx(-\varepsilon^{\pm},\varepsilon^{\pm})\times\Sigma
of
\Sigma
in
V
such that
U^{+}
and
U^{-}
are foliated by MOTS (in fact
\chi^{+}=0
) and MITS (in fact
\chi^{-}=0
), respectively. Each MOTS,
\Sigma^{+}_{t}
, generates a closed achronal totally geodesic null hypersurface,
N^{+}_{t}
, via the generators of the outward null normal
\ell^{+}_{t}
. Likewise, each MITS,
\Sigma^{-}_{s}
, generates an analogous
N^{-}_{s}
. Moreover, the intersection of the two families,
N^{+}_{t}
and
N^{-}_{s}
, foliate a spacetime neighborhood of
\Sigma
by totally geodesic codimension
2
spacelike submanifolds, each diffeomorphic to
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Suppose
M
is null geodesically complete. We are assuming
\Sigma
is
weakly outer and inner trapped with respect to one of the components of
V\setminus\Sigma
. As in the proof of Theorem
4
,
\Sigma
is both a MOTS and a MITS. There are neighborhoods
U^{+}
and
U^{-}
of
\Sigma
in
V
foliated by MOTS and MITS, respectively. Call these foliations
U^{+}=\{\Sigma_{t}^{+}\}
and
U^{-}=\{\Sigma_{s}^{-}\}
for
t\in(-\varepsilon^{+},\varepsilon^{+})
and
s\in(-\varepsilon^{-},\varepsilon^{-})
, and note that they coincide at
\Sigma=\Sigma_{0}^{+}=\Sigma_{0}^{-}
. Each
\Sigma_{t}^{+}
generates a totally geodesic outward null hypersurface
N^{+}_{t}
, i.e., it has vanishing null second fundamental. Likewise with the inner direction on
N_{s}^{-}
.

Let
N
be the union of the intersections
\{N^{+}_{t}\cap N^{-}_{s}\mid t\in(-\varepsilon^{+},\varepsilon^{+}),\>s\in(-\varepsilon^{-},\varepsilon^{-})\}
. Then
N
is nonempty since
\Sigma\subset N
. Since the null generators of these null hypersurfaces are distinct, it follows that
N^{+}_{t}
and
N^{-}_{s}
must intersect transversely (if they do at all). It follows that
N
is an open set in
M
containing
\Sigma
. Clearly, since
N^{+}_{0}\cap N^{-}_{0}=\Sigma
,
N^{+}_{t}\cap N^{-}_{s}
will intersect in a spacetime neighborhood of
\Sigma
for all
t
and
s
sufficiently small in magnitude.
The intersections within this neighborhood are codimension 2 compact submanifolds which are totally geodesic since it’s the intersection of two transverse totally geodesic null hypersurfaces. The null geodesic generators of
N^{+}_{t}
establish a diffeomorphism from each intersection
N^{+}_{t}\cap N^{-}_{s}
to
\Sigma^{+}_{t}
and hence they are diffeomorphic to
\Sigma
. Indeed, by an achronality argument, the null geodesic generators of
N^{+}_{t}
intersect
N^{-}_{s}
only once. Therefore the exponential map sets up a well-defined embedding of
\Sigma^{+}_{t}
into
N^{+}_{t}\cap N^{-}_{s}
producing the diffeomorphism.
∎

Remark.
For
t<0
and
s>0
, the intersection
N^{+}_{t}\cap N^{-}_{s}
lies in
J^{+}(\Sigma)
. To see this, let
V^{\pm}
denote the connected components of
V\setminus\Sigma
with
\nu|_{\Sigma}
pointing into
V^{+}
. Since
\Sigma
separates, causal-theoretic arguments as in the proof of Theorem
4
show that
J^{+}(\overline{V^{-}})=J^{+}(\Sigma)\sqcup D^{+}(V^{-})
, where
D^{+}
is the future domain of dependence. The corresponding property holds for
V^{+}
as well. Therefore,
J^{+}(\overline{V^{-}})\cap J^{+}(\overline{V^{+}})=J^{+}(\Sigma)
.

4
Compact-with-boundary rigidity

It is often assumed that the process of gravitational collapse and formation of a black hole develops from a nonsingular past. In this section, we will take this point of view.

Theorem 6
.

Let
(M,g)
be a past null geodesically complete globally hyperbolic spacetime satisfying the null energy condition with a spacelike Cauchy hypersurface
V
. Let
\Sigma
be a connected, closed hypersurface in
V
that separates
V
into two components, both having noncompact closures in
V
. Let
W
be a compact, connected top-dimensional submanifold in
V
with boundary
\partial W=\Sigma\sqcup S
. (
S
may have multiple components.) Assume that
\theta^{+}_{S}\geq 0
with respect to the normal pointing out of
W
.

If
\Sigma
is weakly outer trapped,
\theta^{+}_{\Sigma}\leq 0
, with respect to the normal pointing into
W
, then either

(a)

M
is future null geodesically incomplete, or else,

(b)

S
is diffeomorphic to
\Sigma
and
W
is diffeomorphic to
[0,\delta]\times\Sigma
. Moreover, for each
t\in[0,\delta]
,
\Sigma_{t}=\{t\}\times\Sigma
is a MOTS (in fact
\chi^{+}_{t}=0
) and the outward null normal geodesics to
\Sigma_{t}
form a closed achronal totally geodesic null hypersurface
N_{t}
.

Proof.

This follows from a continuity argument as in the proof of Theorem 1.2 in
[
11
]
. Assume
M
is future null geodesically complete. Briefly, one shows that the leaves of the local foliation
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\varepsilon)}
from Theorem
4
have a smooth embedded limit
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
. (This uses the fact that, since
\chi_{t}^{+}=0
, the second fundamental forms of the
\Sigma_{t}
’s within
W
are uniformly bounded in terms of the second fundamental form of
W
in
M
.) Then the strong maximum principle (
[
7
, Prop. 3.1]
,
[
5
, Prop. 2.4]
) shows that
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}=S
if
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap S\neq\emptyset
. If
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\cap S=\emptyset
, then we can continue the foliation. Connectedness of
W
implies
W\approx[0,\delta]\times\Sigma
with
S\approx\Sigma_{\delta}
.
∎

Remark.
Suppose in Theorem
6
, one assumes that
V
is geodesically complete, with bounded geometry, in the sense described in
[
28
, Sec. 1]
(called ‘homogeneous regularity’ there). Suppose further that
K
is uniformly bounded on all of
V
. Now let
W
be the closure of one of the two components separating
V
. (Note that
W
in this case is noncompact.) One can again argue that the leaves of the local foliation

\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\varepsilon)}
from Theorem
4
have a smooth embedded compact limit

\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
provided the union of the leaves has a limit point in
W\setminus\Sigma
. Then, again by a continuation argument, if
M
is future null geodesically complete, the conclusions (b) of Theorem
6
hold, with
[0,\delta]
replaced by
[0,\delta)
,
\delta\in(0,\infty]
. We thank Michael Eichmair for clarifications regarding this.

In the following, we apply the previous theorem to a situation where
\Sigma
is both weakly outer and inner trapped. A priori, the null expansion assumptions on
S
are compatible with an asymptotically flat end.

Theorem 7
.

Let
(M,g)
be a past null geodesically complete globally hyperbolic spacetime satisfying the null energy condition with a spacelike Cauchy hypersurface
V
. Let
\Sigma
be a connected and closed hypersurface in
V
that separates
V
into two components, both having noncompact closures in
V
. Let
W
be a compact, connected top-dimensional submanifold in
V
with boundary
\partial W=\Sigma\sqcup S
. Assume
S
is weakly outer untrapped and weakly inner trapped, i.e.,
\theta^{+}_{S}\geq 0
and
\theta^{-}_{S}\leq 0
, with respect to the normal pointing out of
W
.

If
\Sigma
is weakly trapped,
\theta^{\pm}_{\Sigma}\leq 0
, with respect to the normal pointing into
W
, then either

(a)

M
is future null geodesically incomplete, or else,

(b)

W
is foliated by a family of MOTS,
\Sigma^{+}_{t}
, and a family of MITS,
\Sigma^{-}_{s}
, each generating totally geodesic null hypersurfaces
N^{+}_{t}
and
N^{-}_{s}
as in Theorem
5
. The two foliations of null hypersurfaces together cover
J^{+}(W)\cup J^{-}(W)
. Moreover, the intersection of the two families of null hypersurfaces cover the domain of dependence
D(W)=D^{+}(W)\cup D^{-}(W)
by totally geodesic codimension
2
spacelike submanifolds.

Proof.

Assume
M
is future null geodesically complete. Theorem
5
implies that
\theta^{\pm}_{\Sigma}=0
. Theorem
6
gives the existence of the foliating family of MOTS,
\{\Sigma^{+}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,\delta^{+}]}
, and MITS,
\{\Sigma^{-}_{s}\}_{s\in[0,\delta^{-}]}
with
\Sigma^{+}_{0}=\Sigma^{-}_{0}=\Sigma
. Let
N^{+}_{t}
and
N^{-}_{s}
denote the corresponding closed achronal totally geodesic null hypersurfaces.

Consider the collection of unions
\{N^{+}_{t}\cup N^{-}_{s}\mid t\in[0,\delta^{+}],\>s\in[0,\delta^{-}]\}
. Let
A
denote their union. We want to show
A=J^{+}(W)\cup J^{-}(W)
. Clearly
A
is a subset. It suffices to show
A
is both open and closed in the subspace topology. Fix
p\in A
and suppose without loss of generality that
p\in N^{+}_{t}
for some
t\in[0,\delta^{+}]
. If
t\in(0,\delta^{+})
, then the exponential map produces a neighborhood of points around
p
all coming from
N^{+}_{t^{\prime}}
for
t^{\prime}\in(t-\varepsilon,t+\varepsilon)
. In the case that
t=0
or
t=\delta^{+}
, we can extend the foliation a little further to generate a neighborhood of points arising from the extended foliation and intersect this neighborhood with
J^{+}(W)\cup J^{-}(W)
producing a relative neighborhood in the subspace topology. Thus
A
is open in
J^{+}(W)\cup J^{-}(W)
. To see that
A
is closed, let
q_{n}\in A
be a sequence limiting to
q
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
q_{n}\in N^{+}_{t_{n}}
for
t_{n}\in[0,\delta^{+}]
. Each
q_{n}
arises from some
p_{n}\in\Sigma_{t_{n}}
via
q_{n}=\exp_{p_{n}}(k_{n})
, where
k_{n}
is an outward normal null vector. A subsequence of
p_{n}
converges to
p\in\Sigma_{t}
for some
t
. Let
q^{\prime}\in I^{+}(q)
, then
J^{-}(q^{\prime})\cap I^{+}(V)

Cosmological rigidity

In the cosmological context (i.e., spacetimes with compact Cauchy surfaces), we have the following two theorems.

Theorem 8

Theorem 10

(M,g)

(b)

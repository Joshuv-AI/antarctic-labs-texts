Twist structures and Nelson conuclei
Manuela Busaniche and Nikolaos Galatos and Miguel Marcos

Abstract
. Motivated by Kalman residuated lattices, Nelson residuated lattices and Nelson paraconsistent residuated lattices, we provide a natural common generalization of them. Nelson conucleus algebras unify these examples and further extend them to the non-commutative setting. We study their structure, establish a representation theorem for them in terms of twist structures and conuclei that results in a categorical adjunction, and explore situations where the representation is actually an isomorphism. In the latter case, the adjunction is elevated to a categorical equivalence. By applying this representation to the original motivating special cases we bring to the surface their underlying similarities.



Introduction

Residuated lattices arise in many contexts in general and ordered algebra. Examples of residuated lattices include lattice-ordered groups, the lattice of ideals of a ring and relation algebras. At the same time they serve as algebraic semantics for substructural logics, including linear, relevance and many-valued logics. As a result, the algebraic semantics of these logics form further examples of residuated lattices and include MV, Heyting and Boolean algebras. In this paper we investigate a construction of involutive residuated lattices that has interesting applications to models of paraconsistent logics and use it to provide a unified approach to these models.

Given a lattice
{\bf L}
, the
twist structure over

{\bf L}
is obtained by considering the direct product of
{\bf L}
and its order-dual
{\bf L}^{\partial}.
The resulting lattice has a natural De Morgan involution given by

\sim(x,y)=(y,x)

for all
(x,y)\in L\times L^{\partial}
. This construction was used by Kalman in 1958
[
15
]
, while the modifier "twist" appeared thirty years later in Kracht’s paper
[
16
]
. Although Kalman only worked with the lattice structure, several other authors considered expansions with additional operations on
{\bf L}
which induce new and interesting operations on the twist structure
[
10
,
27
,
9
,
23
,
16
,
26
,
5
,
18
,
19
,
6
,
7
]
.

In particular, Tsinakis and Wille
[
26
]
, inspired by Chu’s work in category theory
[
3
]
and its specialization to quantales
[
22
]
, considered the twist structure over a residuated lattice
{\bf L}
having a greatest element
\top
and endowed it with a residuated lattice structure with unit
(e,\top)
, such that the pair
(\top,e)
is the dualizing element for the natural involution.

In
[
6
]
,
[
5
]
and
[
7
]
it is proved that the logical systems of Nelson constructive logic with strong negation (CNS, see
[
17
]
), and its paraconsistent analogue (PNS, see
[
19
]
) have as algebraic semantics residuated lattices whose lattice reducts are twist structures (see also
[
24
,
25
]
). Furthermore, the monoid and residuum operators coincide with the ones proposed in
[
26
]
. However, the unit of the residuated lattice is not the same in all the cases, so these structures do not fall directly under the framework of
[
26
]
.

Our aim is to present a unified approach that provides a deeper insight into the classes of residuated lattices that have a representation based on twist structures. Our framework encompasses Nelson residuated lattices
[
24
,
5
]
, Nelson paraconsistent residuated lattices
[
6
,
7
]
and Kalman residuated lattices
[
8
,
1
]
. Our results allow the comparison among them and provide some interesting new examples.

To achieve this aim we start by considering a broader class of algebras: residuated lattice-ordered semigroups. Given a residuated lattice
{\bf L}
we define the general twist-product
{\bf Tw(L)}
as an involutive residuated lattice-ordered semigroup with an extra unary operation of involution; the fact that
{\bf L}
may lack a top element results in
{\bf Tw(L)}
potentially lacking an identity element. By localizing to a specific positive idempotent element of
{\bf Tw(L)}
(induced by an arbitrary fixed element
\imath
of
{\bf L}
), we obtain a subalgebra
{\bf Tw(L,\imath)}
of
{\bf Tw(L)}
that is a residuated lattice; this localization is done by the double-division conucleus given in
[
13
]
, which focuses on the local submonoid of the positive idempotent element. This approach allows us to work with twist products of residuated lattices that do not have a top element and subsumes all of our motivating examples. Thus we accomplish our first goal: we put into the same framework different algebras, such as Nelson residuated lattices and Paraconsistent Nelson residuated lattices.

Having established this first theoretical framework, we pursue our second purpose: to describe the class of involutive residuated lattices that have a representation as a twist-product over a residuated lattice; this requires us to focus on a construction in the reverse direction to the twist product. We show that the desired residuated lattice is also obtained by a conucleus on the involutive residuated lattice, which is of a very different nature than the double-division one, and we call it a
Nelson conucleus
. The main representation result in Theorem
18
shows that pairs of the form
({\bf A},{\bf n})
, where
{\bf A}
is a cyclic involutive residuated lattice and
{\bf n}
is a Nelson conucleus, are representable by a twist-product over a residuated lattice defined on
{\bf n}[{\bf A}].
We call these algebras
Nelson conucleus algebras
and denote the variety they form by
\mathcal{NCA}
. As a corollary we provide an adjunction between the algebraic category given by
\mathcal{NCA}
and a category whose objects are pairs of the form
({\bf L},\imath)
where
{\bf L}
is a residuated lattice and
\imath\in L
is a cyclic element. Along the way of proving the representation, we generalize the original construction of Rasiowa
[
20
,
21
]
on Nelson algebras and their representation by twist structures. In particular, our presentation shows that Rasiowa’s homomorphic image construction can be replaced by a conucleus construction, which is more internal to the original algebra, as it provides representatives for the equivalence classes.

Our motivating examples share some extra common features: they are commutative residuated lattices and the Nelson conucleus
{\bf n}
is definable by a term function; therefore they actually form varieties of commutative involutive residuated lattices. First we identify the subvariety of
\mathcal{NCA}
whose elements are term equivalent to Kalman lattices. Secondly we prove that Nelson residuated lattices and Paraconsistent Nelson residuated lattices form classes term equivalent to subvarieties of

\mathcal{NCA}
, thus Theorem
18
applies to them. Furthermore, we show that both of these subvarieties are contained in
\mathcal{NT}
, a subvariety of
\mathcal{NCA}
, up to term equivalence, whose elements we call
Nelson-type algebras
. Following Sendlewski’s representation for Nelson algebras in
[
23
]
and the paraconsistent analogue given by Odintsov in
[
18
]
, we improve the representation of Theorem
18
for
\mathcal{NT}
by providing a Sendlewski-like theorem, i.e., we identify each Nelson-type algebra with a subalgebra of the twist-product on
{\bf n}[{\bf A}].
As a consequence we get a categorical equivalence between the algebraic category of Nelson-type algebras and the category whose objects are triples of the form
({\bf H},i,F)
, where
{\bf H}
is a Brouwerian algebra,
\imath
is a cyclic element in
{\bf H}
and
F
is a Boolean filter of
{\bf H}
. This then restricts to categorical equivalences for the two subvarieties corresponding to Nelson residuated lattices and Paraconsistent Nelson residuated lattices.

In the last section, we complete the circle of ideas by providing some particular conditions under which the representation Theorem
18
can be turned into an isomorphism theorem even when the Nelson conucleus is not given by a term.

1.
Preliminaries

In this section we review some existing definitions and constructions. We also introduce modifications and combinations of these constructions that will be suitable for the paper.

1.1.
Residuated lattices

A
residuated lattice-ordered semigroup
is an algebra
{\bf A}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,{\backslash},{/})
such that
(A,\cdot)
is a semigroup,
(A,\vee,\wedge)
is a lattice and the residuation condition

\displaystyle x\cdot y\leq z\mbox{ iff }y\leq x{\backslash}z\mbox{ iff }x\leq z{/}y

holds for all
x,y
and
z
in
A
, where
\leq
is the order given by the lattice structure.
A
residuated lattice
is an algebra
{\bf A}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,{\backslash},{/},e)
such that
(A,\cdot,e)
is a monoid and
(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,{\backslash},{/})
is a residuated lattice-ordered semigroup; residuated lattice-ordered semigroups and residuated lattices form varieties. If a residuated lattice-ordered semigroup satisfies
x\cdot y=y\cdot x
, it is called a
commutative
. In such case
x{\backslash}y=y{/}x
and we denote the common value by
x\to y
. We say that the residuated lattice
{\bf A}
is
distributive
if the lattice
(A,\vee,\wedge)
is distributive. For each natural number
n
we define
x^{0}=e
and
x^{n}=x^{n-1}\cdot x
for
n>0
; also we often write
xy
for
x\cdot y
. For more on residuated lattices, see
[
12
,
4
,
14
]
.

We will be also working with
integral residuated lattices
, which are residuated lattices satisfying
x\leq e
, and with
Brouwerian algebras
that are term equivalent to residuated lattices satisfying
xy=x\wedge y
(and hence are integral and commutative).
We also consider expansions with an additional constant, which serves as the bottom element, and refer to these algebras as
bounded residuated lattices
, since then they necessarily also have a term-definable top element.

1.2.
Involutive residuated lattices

Residuated lattices can be expanded into (cyclic) involutive residuated lattices in two term equivalent ways. One is by adding to the signature a
negation constant
element
f
that is
cyclic
: for all
x
,
x{\backslash}f=f{/}x
, and
dualizing
:
f{/}(x{\backslash}f)=x=(f{/}x){\backslash}f
. Alternatively, we can add to the signature a unary
(cyclic) involution
operation
{\sim}
satisfying the equations:

\displaystyle{\sim}{\sim}x

\displaystyle=x

(double negation)

\displaystyle x{\backslash}{\sim}y

\displaystyle={\sim}x{/}y

(contraposition)

The two ways produce term equivalent algebras via the definitions
f={\sim}e
and
{\sim}x=x{\backslash}f
(see
[
11
,
26
]
for
details); in particular
{\sim}x=x{\backslash}{\sim}e
. We prefer the
definition in terms of the involution
{\sim}
, as it makes sense also
for residuated lattice-ordered semigroups. In this paper we will not
make use of the notion of not-necessarily cyclic involutive
residuated lattice, and we will always assume cyclicity for
f
and

{\sim}
. Observe that in commutative involutive residuated lattices
the
contraposition
takes the form
x\mathbin{\rightarrow}{\sim}y=y\mathbin{\rightarrow}{\sim}x
and that cyclicity holds automatically.

In an involutive residuated lattice-ordered semigroup the divisions are definable in terms of the multiplication and the involution, and also the lattice operations are interdefinable via De Morgan equations. More precisely:

Lemma 1
.

(see
[
11
, Lemma 5.1]
and
[
12
, Lemma 2.8]
) If
{\bf A}
is an involutive residuated lattice then

(1)

x{\backslash}y={\sim}({\sim}y\cdot x)
and
y{/}x={\sim}(x\cdot{\sim}y)
.

(2)

x\cdot y={\sim}(y{\backslash}{\sim}x)={\sim}({\sim}y{/}x)
.

(3)

{\sim}(x\vee y)={\sim}x\wedge{\sim}y
and
{\sim}(x\wedge y)={\sim}x\vee{\sim}y
.

An involutive residuated lattice is called
odd
if
f=e
, or equivalently
{\sim}e=e
; also, we have
{\sim}x=x{\backslash}e
. Because of the identification of the two constants, odd involutive residuated lattices are term equivalent to residuated lattices that satisfy the equations
x{\backslash}e=e{/}x
and
(x{\backslash}e){\backslash}e=x
. In the commutative case, the defining equation is just
(x\rightarrow e)\rightarrow e=x
.

1.3.
Conuclei constructions

We review the important notion of conucleus on residuated structures.

1.3.1.
Conuclei and weak conuclei

Definition 2
.

A
weak conucleus

\delta
on a residuated lattice-ordered semigroup
\mathbf{A}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,{\backslash},{/})
is a function on
\mathbf{A}
that satisfies:

(C1)

\delta(x)\leq x
,

(C2)

\delta(\delta(x))=\delta(x)
,

(C3)

if
x\leq y
then
\delta(x)\leq\delta(y)
,

(C4)

\delta(x)\cdot\delta(y)\leq\delta(x\cdot y)
.

If
{\bf A}
is a residuated lattice with neutral element
e
and
\delta
additionally satisfies:

(C5)

\delta(e)\cdot\delta(x)=\delta(x)\cdot\delta(e)=\delta(x),

then
\delta
is called a
conucleus
on
{\bf A}.

Summing up, a conucleus is an interior operator
\delta
on a residuated lattice
{\bf A}
that satisfies (C
4
) and (C
5
).
It is immediate that if
\delta
is a weak conucleus then
\delta(x)\vee\delta(y)=\delta(\delta(x)\vee\delta(y))
and
\delta(x)\cdot\delta(y)=\delta(\delta(x)\cdot\delta(y))
.

Given a residuated lattice
{\bf A}
and a conucleus
\delta
on
{\bf A}
, the algebra

\mathbf{A}_{\delta}=\delta[\mathbf{A}]=(\delta[A],\vee,\wedge_{\delta},\cdot,{\backslash}_{\delta},{/}_{\delta},\delta(e))

is a residuated lattice (
[
12
]
), where
x\wedge_{\delta}y=\delta(x\wedge y)
,
x{\backslash}_{\delta}y=\delta(x{\backslash}y)
and
y{/}_{\delta}x=\delta(y{/}x)
for
x,y\in\delta[A]
(by the previous observation,
\delta[A]
is closed under
\vee
and
\cdot
).

Lemma 3
.

If
\delta
is a weak conucleus on
\mathbf{A}
, then

(C6)

\delta(x\wedge y)=\delta(\delta(x)\wedge\delta(y))
,

(C7)

\delta(\delta(x){\backslash}y)=\delta(\delta(x){\backslash}\delta(y))
and

\delta(y{/}\delta(x))=\delta(\delta(y){/}\delta(x))
.

Proof.

For (C
6
), observe that by (C
1
) we have that
\delta(x)\wedge\delta(y)\leq x\wedge y
, so
\delta(\delta(x)\wedge\delta(y))\leq\delta(x\wedge y)
by (C
3
). As
\delta(x\wedge y)\leq\delta(x),\delta(y)
by (C
3
), we have that
\delta(x\wedge y)\leq\delta(\delta(x)\wedge\delta(y))
again by (C
3
) and (C
2
).
For (C
7
), as
\delta(x){\backslash}\delta(y)\leq\delta(x){\backslash}y
we have one inequality. For the other one, using (C
4
) we have
\delta(x)\cdot\delta(\delta(x){\backslash}y)=\delta(\delta(x))\cdot\delta(\delta(x){\backslash}y)\leq\delta(\delta(x)\cdot\delta(x){\backslash}y)\leq\delta(y)
and (C
7
) follows from (C
3
) and (C
2
).
∎

1.3.2.
Double Division conucleus

Let
\mathbf{A}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,{\backslash},{/})
be a residuated lattice-ordered semigroup and
p\in A
an idempotent element (i.e.,
p=p^{2}
) that is also
positive
(i.e.,
p{\backslash}x,x{/}p\leq x\leq px,xp
, for all
x
). This definition of being positive is justified by the fact that if
\mathbf{A}
has an identity element
e
, then
p
is positive iff
e\leq p
. It is shown in
[
13
]
that the map defined by

\delta_{p}(x)=p{\backslash}x{/}p

is a weak conucleus on
\mathbf{A}
, that
\wedge_{p}=\wedge
,
{\backslash}_{p}={\backslash}
,
{/}_{p}={/}
, that
p
is an identity element for
\delta_{p}[A]
and that
\delta_{p}[A]=p{\backslash}A{/}p=\{p{\backslash}a{/}p:a\in A\}
. Therefore the algebra

p{\backslash}\mathbf{A}{/}p=\delta_{p}[{\bf A}]=(p{\backslash}A{/}p,\wedge,\vee,\cdot,{\backslash},{/},p)

is a residuated lattice. The algebra
p{\backslash}\mathbf{A}{/}p
is called the
double-division conucleus image of
\mathbf{A}
by
p
. Moreover, it turns out that
\delta_{p}[A]=\{a\in A:ap=a,pa=a\}
.

It is further shown in
[
13
]
that if the residuated lattice-ordered semigroup
\mathbf{A}
is (cyclic) involutive with involution
{\sim}
, then the residuated lattice
p{\backslash}\mathbf{A}{/}p
is also (cyclic) involutive and actually
p{\backslash}\mathbf{A}{/}p
is a subalgebra of
\mathbf{A}
with respect to the operations
\wedge,\vee,\cdot,{\backslash},{/},{\sim}
; the constants
e
and
{\sim}e
are replaced by
p
and
{\sim}p
.

2.
Twist structures

Given a residuated lattice-ordered semigroup
\mathbf{L}=(L,\wedge,\vee,\cdot,{\backslash},{/})
, we consider the set
Tw(L)=L\times L
and define the operations
\wedge
and
\vee
as in
\mathbf{L}\times\mathbf{L}^{\partial}
. Also, for
a,a^{\prime},b,b^{\prime}\in L
, we define:

(1)

{\sim}(a,b)=(b,a)

(2)

(a,b)\cdot(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a\cdot a^{\prime},b^{\prime}{/}a\wedge a^{\prime}{\backslash}b)

(3)

(a,b){\backslash}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a{\backslash}a^{\prime}\wedge b{/}b^{\prime},b^{\prime}\cdot a)

(4)

(a^{\prime},b^{\prime}){/}(a,b)=(a^{\prime}{/}a\wedge b^{\prime}{\backslash}b,a\cdot b^{\prime})

The resulting structure

(\text{Tw}(L),\wedge,\vee,\cdot,{\backslash},{/},{\sim})
is an involutive residuated lattice-ordered semigroup, which we denote by
\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L})
. We refer to this algebra as the
full twist structure
over
\mathbf{L}
.
In addition, if
\mathbf{L}
is a topped residuated lattice with neutral element
e
and top element
\top
the structure
(\text{Tw}(L),\wedge,\vee,\cdot,{\backslash},{/},{\sim},(e,\top))

is an involutive residuated lattice (see
[
26
]
).
In particular, if
(L,\wedge,\vee,\cdot,{\backslash},{/},e)
is an integral residuated lattice, the algebra
\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L},e)=(\text{Tw}(L),\wedge,\vee,\cdot,{\backslash},{/},{\sim},(e,e))
is an involutive residuated lattice.

2.1.
Double Division conucleus for twist structures

Next we do not assume that
\mathbf{L}
has a top, so
\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L})
may lack an identity element.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathbf{L}
be a residuated lattice and
\imath\in L
.

(1)

The element
(e,\imath)
is a positive idempotent of the residuated lattice-ordered semigroup
\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L})
.

(2)

An element
(a,b)
is fixed by
\delta_{(e,\imath)}
iff
ab\vee ba\leq\imath
. (Recall that
\delta_{p}(x)=p{\backslash}x{/}p
.)

Proof.

For (1) we have

(e,\imath)(e,\imath)=(e\cdot e,\imath{/}e\wedge e{\backslash}\imath)=(e,\imath)

so
(e,\imath)
is a idempotent. To show that it is positive, we have that for all
(a,b)
,

\displaystyle(a,b)(e,\imath)

\displaystyle=(ae,\imath{/}a\wedge e{\backslash}b)=(a,\imath{/}a\wedge b)\geq(a,b)

\displaystyle(e,\imath)(a,b)

\displaystyle=(ea,b{/}e\wedge a{\backslash}\imath)=(a,b\wedge a{\backslash}\imath)\geq(a,b)

\displaystyle(e,\imath){\backslash}(a,b)

\displaystyle=(e{\backslash}a\wedge\imath{/}b,be)=(a\wedge\imath{/}b,b)\leq(a,b)

\displaystyle(a,b){/}(e,\imath)

\displaystyle=(a{/}e\wedge b{\backslash}\imath,be)=(a\wedge b{\backslash}\imath,b)\leq(a,b).

Note that

\displaystyle(e,\imath){\backslash}(a,b){/}(e,\imath)

\displaystyle=(e{\backslash}a\wedge\imath{/}b,be){/}(e,\imath)=(a\wedge\imath{/}b,b){/}(e,\imath)

\displaystyle=((a\wedge\imath{/}b){/}e\wedge b{\backslash}\imath,eb)=(a\wedge\imath{/}b\wedge b{\backslash}\imath,b).

Therefore,
(a,b)
is fixed iff
(e,\imath){\backslash}(a,b){/}(e,\imath)=(a,b)
iff

(a\wedge\imath{/}b\wedge b{\backslash}\imath,b)=(a,b)
iff

a\leq\imath{/}b\wedge b{\backslash}\imath
iff

ab\leq\imath
and
ba\leq\imath
iff

ab\vee ba\leq\imath
.
∎

Given the fact that
(e,\imath)
is a positive idempotent, for a residuated lattice
\mathbf{L}
, we denote by

\displaystyle\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L},\imath)=\delta_{(e,\imath)}[\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L})]=(e,\imath){\backslash}\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L}){/}(e,\imath)

the double-division conucleus image of the residuated lattice-ordered semigroup
\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L})
by
\delta(e,\imath)
. Note that even though
\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L})
may have no identity element,
\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L},\imath)
is still a residuated lattice with identity element
(e,\imath)
. This allows us to consider this construction when
\mathbf{L}
is a residuated lattice that lacks a top element. We call
\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L},\imath)
the
twist structure
over
(\mathbf{L},\imath)

According to the previous results, the universe of the algebra
\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{L},\imath)
is the set
\text{Tw}(L,\imath)=\{(a,b)\in L\times L^{\partial}:ab\vee ba\leq\imath\}
. In summary we obtain the following:

Theorem 5
.

Let
(L,\wedge,\vee,\cdot,{\backslash},{/},e)
be a residuated lattice and
\imath\in L
. Consider the set

\text{Tw}(L,\imath)=\{(a,b)\in L\times L^{\partial}:ab\vee ba\leq\imath\}

equipped with the operations
\wedge
and
\vee
of
\bf L\times L^{\partial}
and the operations defined in equations (
1
), (
2
), (
3
) and (
4
). Then
{\bf Tw(L,\imath)}=\left(\text{Tw}(L,\imath),\wedge,\vee,\cdot,{\backslash},{/},{\sim},(e,\imath)\right)
is an involutive residuated lattice.

We prove a lemma that sheds some light into the construction.

Lemma 6
.

The set

\text{Tw}(L,\imath)=\{(a,b)\in L\times L:ab\vee ba\leq\imath\}

is a downset of the direct product lattice
\mathbf{L}\times\mathbf{L}
. Moreover,
M_{\imath}=\{(a,b):a=\imath{/}b\wedge b{\backslash}\imath\text{ and }b=\imath{/}a\wedge a{\backslash}\imath\}
consists of maximal elements of
\text{Tw}(L,\imath)
and if
\imath
is cyclic then
\text{Tw}(L,\imath)=\mathord{\downarrow}_{\mathbf{L}\times\mathbf{L}}M_{\imath}
.

Proof.

We prove that
\text{Tw}(L,\imath)
is a downset of
{\bf L}\times{\bf L}
in terms of the coordinatewise order, which is different than the order of the residuated lattice
{\bf{Tw}(L,\imath)}.

If
(c,d),(a,b)\in L\times L
, are such that
c\leq a
,
d\leq b
and
ab\vee ba\leq\imath
, then
cd\vee dc\leq ab\vee ba\leq\imath
.
Also, note that

\displaystyle\text{Tw}(L,\imath)

\displaystyle=\{(a,b):a\leq\imath{/}b\wedge b{\backslash}\imath\text{ and }b\leq\imath{/}a\wedge a{\backslash}\imath\}

\displaystyle=\{(a,b):a\leq\imath{/}b\wedge b{\backslash}\imath\}=\{(a,b):b\leq\imath{/}a\wedge a{\backslash}\imath\}.

It then follows directly that
\text{Tw}(L,\imath)
is a downset. To show that the set
M_{\imath}=\{(a,b):a=\imath{/}b\wedge b{\backslash}\imath\text{ and }b=\imath{/}a\wedge a{\backslash}\imath\}
consists of maximal elements of
\text{Tw}(L,\imath)
, let
(a,b)\in M_{\imath}
and
(c,d)\in\text{Tw}(L,\imath)
, be such that
a\leq c
,
b\leq d
. Then
a=\imath{/}b\wedge b{\backslash}\imath
,
b=\imath{/}a\wedge a{\backslash}\imath
,
c\leq\imath{/}d\wedge d{\backslash}\imath
and
d\leq\imath{/}c\wedge c{\backslash}\imath
. So,
c\leq\imath{/}d\wedge d{\backslash}\imath\leq\imath{/}b\wedge b{\backslash}\imath=a\leq c
and

d\leq\imath{/}c\wedge c{\backslash}\imath\leq\imath{/}a\wedge a{\backslash}\imath=b\leq d
, hence
(c,d)=(a,b)
.

Assume now that
\imath
is cyclic to prove that
\text{Tw}(L,\imath)=\mathord{\downarrow}_{\mathbf{L}\times\mathbf{L}}M_{\imath}
. Since
M_{\imath}\subseteq\text{Tw}(L,\imath)
and
\text{Tw}(L,\imath)
is a downset, we get

2.2.
Motivating examples

We consider three different varieties of algebras that serve as the motivation for our study. The goal is to include them under the same theoretical framework.

Example 7
.

A
Kalman residuated lattice
(
[
8
,
1
]
) is a commutative residuated lattice
{\bf A}=(A,\wedge,\vee,\cdot,\rightarrow,e)
satisfying the following equations:

(K1)

(x\rightarrow e)\rightarrow e=x
,

(K2)

(x\cdot y)\wedge e=(x\wedge e)\cdot(y\wedge e)
,

(K3)

((x\wedge e)\rightarrow y)\wedge(x\mathbin{\rightarrow}(y\vee e))=x\rightarrow y
[equivalently,
((x\wedge e)\rightarrow y)\wedge(({\sim}y\wedge e)\rightarrow{\sim}x)=x\rightarrow y
],

(K4)

e\wedge(x\vee y)=(e\wedge x)\vee(e\wedge y)
and

(K5)

x\land(y\lor e)=(x\land y)\lor(x\land e).

(A,\wedge,\vee,\cdot,\rightarrow,{\sim},e)

is a commutative involutive residuated lattice, where
{\sim}x=x\rightarrow e
and
\sim e=e
, i.e.,
\mathbf{A}
is an odd residuated lattice. Note that the expressions
({\sim}y\wedge e)\rightarrow{\sim}x
and
x\mathbin{\rightarrow}(y\vee e)
in the two forms of (K
3
) are equal by contraposition. It will follow from our analysis that (K
5
) is actually redundant.

If
{\bf L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\to,1)
is an integral commutative residuated lattice, then
{\bf Tw}({\bf L},1)
is a Kalman lattice. Moreover, for each Kalman lattice
{\bf A}
there is an integral residuated lattice
{\bf L}
such that
{\bf A}
is isomorphic to a subalgebra of
{\bf Tw}({\bf L},1)

[
8
, Th. 2.5]
.

Example 8
.

A
Nelson residuated lattice
(
[
24
,
25
,
5
,
10
,
23
]
) is a bounded integral commutative residuated lattice
{\bf A}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\to,\bot,e)
, that with
\neg x:=x\to\bot
satisfies:

(NRL1)

\neg\neg x=x
.

(NRL2)

(x^{2}\to y)\wedge((\neg y)^{2}\to\neg x)=x\to y
.

Involutive residuated lattices defined in
[
5
]
are not the same as the ones defined here, since the involution
\neg
is a definable operation if the constant
\bot
is included in the type.
Nelson residuated lattices are term equivalent to Nelson algebras
[
20
,
21
]
, the algebraic counterpart of Nelson constructive logic with strong negation
[
17
]
.

If
{\bf H}=(H,\vee,\wedge,\to,0,1)
is a Heyting algebra then viewing
\mathbf{H}
as a bounded residuated lattice with
\cdot=\wedge
and bottom element
0
,
{\bf Tw}({\bf H},0)
is a Nelson residuated lattice with bottom element
(0,1)
, which is added to the signature of
{\bf Tw}({\bf H},0)
. As in the previous case, there is a representation theorem since every Nelson residuated lattice is embeddable in
{\bf Tw}({\bf H},0)
for a Heyting algebra
{\bf H}
(see
[
5
, Corollary 3.5]
).

Example 9
.

A
Nelson paraconsistent residuated lattice
(NPc-lattice for short, see
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
19
]
and
[
2
]
) is an odd distributive commutative residuated lattice
{\bf A}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\to,e)
satisfying, for
{\sim}x=x\mathbin{\rightarrow}e
:

(NPc1)

\sim\sim x=x

(NPc2)

(x\cdot y)\wedge e=(x\wedge e)\cdot(y\wedge e)

(NPc3)

(x\wedge e)^{2}=x\wedge e

(NPc4)

((x\wedge e)\rightarrow y)\wedge(x\mathbin{\rightarrow}(y\vee e))=x\rightarrow y
[equivalently,
((x\wedge e)\rightarrow y)\wedge(({\sim}y\wedge e)\rightarrow{\sim}x)=x\rightarrow y
]

NPc-lattices are special Kalman residuated lattices satisfying distributivity and (NPc3).
They were introduced in
[
6
]
in order to present the algebraic semantics of Nelson paraconsistent logic
[
19
]
within the framework of residuated lattices.

If
{\bf H}=(H,\vee,\wedge,\to,1)
is a Brouwerian algebra (also called generalized Heyting algebra), then viewing
\mathbf{H}
as a residuated lattice with
\cdot=\wedge
,
{\bf Tw}({\bf H},1)
is a Nelson paraconsistent residuated lattice. As in the previous cases, every NPc-lattice
{\bf A}
can be embedded into a twist structure
{\bf Tw}({\bf H},1)
(see
[
2
]
).

2.3.
Natural conuclei on twist structures

Another conucleus will be important for understanding
involutive residuated lattices represented by twist structures; this time the conucleus will be on
{\bf{Tw}(L,\imath)}
.
Given a residuated lattice

{\bf L}
and an element
\imath\in L
, we define the function

{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)=(a,\imath{/}a\wedge a{\backslash}\imath)

on
{\bf{Tw}(L,\imath)}
.
Recall that
\imath
is
cyclic
if
x{\backslash}\imath=\imath{/}x
, for all
x
. In that case
ab\vee ba\leq\imath
iff
ab\leq\imath
iff
ba\leq\imath
, for all
a,b
. So, if
\imath
is a cyclic (and in particular in the commutative case),
\text{Tw}(L,\imath)=\{(a,b)\in L\times L^{\partial}:ab\leq\imath\}

and also
{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)=(a,a{\backslash}\imath)
.

Note that, under the assumption that
\imath
is cyclic,
{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}
satisfies (C
1
), (C
2
), (C
3
) and (C
5
). Moreover, we can prove:

Lemma 10
.

Assume that the function
{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)=(a,\imath{/}a\wedge a{\backslash}\imath)
is defined on
{\bf{Tw}(L,\imath)}
with
\imath
cyclic. Then for each
a,b,c,d\in L
we have:

(1)

{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}((a,b)\cdot(c,d))={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)\cdot{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(c,d)

(2)

{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}((a,b)\vee(c,d))={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)\vee{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)

(3)

\left({\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)\cdot(c,d)\right)\vee\left((a,b)\cdot{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(c,d)\right)=(a,b)\cdot(c,d).

In particular,
{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)
is a conucleus.

Proof.

Using Lemma 2.6(6) of
[
12
]
, we have that

\displaystyle{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}((a,b)\cdot(c,d))

\displaystyle=(ac,(ac){\backslash}\imath)=(ac,\imath{/}(ac)\wedge(ac){\backslash}\imath)

\displaystyle=(ac,(c{\backslash}\imath){/}a)\wedge(c{\backslash}(a{\backslash}\imath))=(a,a{\backslash}\imath)\cdot(c,c{\backslash}\imath)

\displaystyle={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)\cdot{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(c,d).

Using Lemma 2.6(3) of
[
12
]
, we also have that:

\displaystyle{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}((a,b)\vee(c,d))

\displaystyle={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a\vee c,b\wedge d)=(a\vee c,(a\vee c){\backslash}\imath)

\displaystyle=(a\vee c,(a{\backslash}\imath)\wedge(c{\backslash}\imath))=(a,a{\backslash}\imath)\vee(c,c{\backslash}\imath)

\displaystyle={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)\vee{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b).

Finally we observe that

\displaystyle\left({\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)(c,d)\right)\vee\left((a,b){\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(c,d)\right)

\displaystyle=(a,a{\backslash}\imath)(c,d)\vee(a,b)(c,c{\backslash}\imath)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=(ac,(d{/}a\wedge c{\backslash}(a{\backslash}\imath)))\vee(ac,(c{\backslash}b\wedge(c{\backslash}\imath){/}a))

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=(ac,(d{/}a\wedge c{\backslash}(a{\backslash}\imath))\wedge(c{\backslash}b\wedge(c{\backslash}\imath){/}a))

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=(ac,(d{/}a\wedge ac{\backslash}\imath)\wedge(c{\backslash}b\wedge ac{\backslash}\imath))

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt=(ac,d{/}a\wedge c{\backslash}b)=(a,b)\cdot(c,d),

where we used that
ac(d{/}a\wedge c{\backslash}b)\leq ac(c{\backslash}b)\leq ab\leq\imath
, so
d{/}a\wedge c{\backslash}b\leq ac{\backslash}\imath
.
∎

3.1.
Nelson conuclei and the variety
\mathcal{NCA}

An operator
{\bf n}
on a residuated lattice
{\bf A}
is called a
Nelson conucleus
if
{\bf n}
is a conucleus and it also satisfies:

(T1)

{\bf n}(x\vee y)={\bf n}(x)\vee{\bf n}(y)

(T2)

{\bf n}(xy)={\bf n}(x){\bf n}(y)

(T3)

xy\leq{\bf n}(x)y\vee x{\bf n}(y)

Any conucleus satisfies

xy\geq{\bf n}(x)y\vee x{\bf n}(y)
. Then we have:

Lemma 11
.

Let
\mathbf{A}
be a residuated lattice and
{\bf n}
a conucleus on it satisfying (T
1
) and (T
2
). Then
{\bf n}
is a Nelson conucleus iff

(T4)

xy={\bf n}(x)y\vee x{\bf n}(y).

Observe that a Nelson conucleus
{\bf n}
also satisfies
{\bf n}(e)=e

since
e=ee={\bf n}(e)e\vee e{\bf n}(e)={\bf n}(e)
. Thus given a residuated lattice
{\bf A}
and a Nelson conucleus
{\bf n}
on
{\bf A}
, the conucleus image
{\bf A}_{{\bf n}}=({\bf n}[{\bf A}],\wedge_{\bf n},\vee,\cdot,{\backslash}_{\bf n},{/}_{\bf n},e)
is a residuated lattice.

From the results of Section
2.3

3.2.
Twist-representation and a categorical adjunction

So far we have described the process where from a residuated lattice
\mathbf{L}
and a cyclic element
\imath\in L
, we construct an algebra
({\bf Tw}(L,\imath),{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}})
in
\mathcal{NCA}
.

We consider the category
\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
with objects algebras
(\mathbf{L},\imath)
, where
\mathbf{L}
is a residuated lattice and
\imath
is a cyclic element of
\mathbf{L}
; the morphisms are homomorphisms of these algebras (they preserve the cyclic element).
Also, note that
\mathcal{NCA}
defines a category where the morphisms are the algebraic homomorphisms.

For an object
(\mathbf{L},\imath)\in\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
and a morphism
f
in
\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
, we define

\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{L},\imath)=({\bf Tw}(L,\imath),{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}})\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \ \mathbf{T}(f)(a,b)=(f(a),f(b)).

It can be easily verified that
\mathbf{T}
is a functor from
\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
to
\mathcal{NCA}
.

We also have the reverse process: given an algebra
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{NCA}
, the algebra

\displaystyle{\bf A}_{{\bf n}}=({\bf n}[A],\vee,\wedge_{{\bf n}},\cdot,{\backslash}_{{\bf n}},{/}_{{\bf n}},e)

is a residuated lattice and
{\bf n}({\sim}e)
cyclic element of
{\bf A}_{{\bf n}}
. Therefore, for an object
({\bf A},{\bf n})
and a homomorphism
f:({\bf A},{\bf n})\rightarrow({\bf B},{\bf n}^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{NCA}
we define

\mathbf{R}({\bf A},{\bf n})=({\bf A}_{{\bf n}},{\bf n}({\sim}e))\ \ \mbox{ and }\ \ \mathbf{R}(f)\mbox{ to be the restriction of }f\mbox{ to }\mathbf{A}_{{\bf n}}.

It can be shown that
\mathbf{R}
is a functor from
\mathcal{NCA}
to
\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
.

We will show that the functors
\mathbf{R}
and
\mathbf{T}
form an adjunction. For that consider, for
(\mathbf{L},\imath)\in\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
and
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{NCA}
, the functions

\psi_{(\mathbf{L},\imath)}:(\mathbf{L},\imath)\rightarrow\mathbf{R}\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{L},\imath)\text{ given by }\psi_{(\mathbf{L},\imath)}(a)=(a,a{\backslash}\imath)

and

\phi_{({\bf A},{\bf n})}:({\bf A},{\bf n})\rightarrow\mathbf{T}\mathbf{R}({\bf A},{\bf n})\text{ given by }\phi_{({\bf A},{\bf n})}(x)=({\bf n}(x),{\bf n}({\sim}x)).

Theorem 16
.

Let
(\mathbf{L},\imath)\in\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
. The function
\psi_{(\mathbf{L},\imath)}
is an isomorphism. Therefore the composition
\mathbf{RT}
of functors is naturally isomorphic to the identity functor on
\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
, via
\psi_{(\mathbf{L},\imath)}^{-1}

Proof.

For
(\mathbf{L},\imath)\in\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
, we have
\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{L},\imath)=({\bf Tw}(L,\imath),{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}})
, where
{\bf Tw}(L,\imath)=\{(a,b)\in L^{2}:ab\leq\imath\}
. Applying
\mathbf{R}
to that we obtain
({\bf Tw}(L,\imath)_{{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}},{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}({\sim}(e,\imath)))
, where
{\bf Tw}(L,\imath)_{{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}}=\{(a,a{\backslash}\imath):a\in L\}
and also
{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}({\sim}(e,\imath))={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(\imath,e)=(\imath,\imath{\backslash}\imath)
.
We will write
\psi
for
\psi_{(\mathbf{L},\imath)}
. Clearly,
\psi:(\mathbf{L},\imath)\rightarrow({\bf Tw}(L,\imath)_{{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}},(\imath,\imath{\backslash}\imath))

•

\psi(a)\vee\psi(b)=(a,a{\backslash}\imath)\vee(b,b{\backslash}\imath)=(a\vee b,a{\backslash}\imath\wedge b{\backslash}\imath)=(a\vee b,(a\vee b){\backslash}\imath)=\psi(a\vee b)

•

\psi(a)\psi(b)=(a,a{\backslash}\imath)(b,b{\backslash}\imath)=(a\cdot b,(b{\backslash}\imath){/}a)\wedge(b{\backslash}(a{\backslash}\imath))=(a\cdot b,\imath{/}(a\cdot b)\wedge(a\cdot b){\backslash}\imath)=(ab,ab{\backslash}\imath)=\psi(ab)

•

\psi(a)\wedge_{{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}}\psi(b)=(a,a{\backslash}\imath)\wedge_{{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}}(b,b{\backslash}\imath)={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a\wedge b,a{\backslash}\imath\vee b{\backslash}\imath)=(a\wedge b,(a\wedge b){\backslash}\imath)=\psi(a\vee b)

•

\psi(a){\backslash}_{{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}}\psi(b)={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}((a,a{\backslash}\imath)\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}(b,b{\backslash}\imath))={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a{\backslash}b\wedge(a{\backslash}\imath){/}(b{\backslash}\imath),(b{\backslash}\imath)a)=\\
{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a{\backslash}b,(b{\backslash}\imath)a)=\psi(a{\backslash}b)

•

\psi(b){/}_{{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}}\psi(a)={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}((b,b{\backslash}\imath)\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}(a,a{\backslash}\imath))={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(b{/}a\wedge(b{\backslash}\imath){\backslash}(a{\backslash}\imath),a(b{\backslash}\imath))=\\
{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(b{/}a,a(b{\backslash}\imath))=\psi(b{/}a)

, that

and cyclicity, to show that

and

. Furthermore,
\psi(e)=(e,e{\backslash}\imath)=(e,\imath)
and
\psi(\imath)=(\imath,\imath{\backslash}\imath)

Therefore,

is an isomorphism in
\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
.
∎

We will show that every algebra
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{NCA}
can be embedded in
\mathbf{TR}({\bf A},{\bf n})
. This means that it can be represented by a twist-product, in the following sense.

Definition 17
.

A
twist-product over

(\mathbf{L},\imath)\in\mathcal{RL}_{\text{cy}}
is a subalgebra of
{\bf{Tw}(L,\imath)}
that contains
{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}[\text{Tw}(L,\imath)]
, i.e. all elements of the form

.

From Theorem

we have the algebra of pairs

{\bf Tw(\mathbf{A}_{\bf n},\imath)}=\left\{\left({\bf n}(x),{\bf n}(y)\right)\in A_{\bf n}\times A_{\bf n}^{\partial}:{\bf n}(x)\cdot{\bf n}(y)\leq\imath\right\}

where
\imath={\bf n}({\sim}e)
.

Theorem 18
.

Let
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{NCA}
and let
\imath={\bf n}(\sim e)
. The function
\phi_{({\bf A},{\bf n})}:A\to Tw(\mathbf{A}_{\bf n},\imath)
given by the prescription

is an injective homomorphism from
(\mathbf{A},{\bf n})
to
(\mathbf{Tw}(\mathbf{A}_{\bf n},\imath),{\bf n}_{Tw})

({\bf A},{\bf n})
is isomorphic to a twist-product over
(\mathbf{A}_{\bf n},\imath)
.

Proof.

We will write
\phi
for
\phi_{({\bf A},{\bf n})}
. To prove that the mapping is well-defined, observe that

and therefore

\displaystyle{\bf n}(x)\cdot{\bf n}({\sim}x)={\bf n}(x\cdot{\sim}x)\leq{\bf n}({\sim}e)=\imath.

Recall that the neutral element on
\text{Tw}(\mathbf{A}_{\bf n},\imath)
is the pair
(e,\imath).
Clearly
\phi(e)=({\bf n}(e),{\bf n}({\sim}e))=(e,\imath).

Using the fact that De Morgan laws hold in
{\bf A}
one can easily check that
\wedge
and
\vee
are preserved by the mapping
\phi.
Also the preservation of
{\sim}
is straightforward from the definition of the mapping and the fact that
{\sim}
is an involution in
{\bf A}
. Due to Lemma

we only need to check that
\cdot
is preserved.
Observe that for all
x,y\in A,

\phi(x\cdot y)=({\bf n}(x\cdot y),{\bf n}({\sim}(x\cdot y)))

\phi(x)\cdot\phi(y)=({\bf n}(x)\cdot{\bf n}(y),{\bf n}({\sim}y){/}_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y){\backslash}_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}x)).

As

An application of Equation (
T’
), Lemma
3

1
give:

\displaystyle{\bf n}({\sim}y){/}_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y){\backslash}_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}x)

\displaystyle={\bf n}({\bf n}({\bf n}({\sim}y){/}{\bf n}(x))\wedge{\bf n}({\bf n}(y){\backslash}{\bf n}({\sim}x)))

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt={\bf n}({\bf n}({\sim}y{/}{\bf n}(x))\wedge{\bf n}({\bf n}(y){\backslash}{\sim}x))

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt={\bf n}(({\sim}y{/}{\bf n}(x))\wedge({\bf n}(y){\backslash}{\sim}x))

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt={\bf n}((({\sim}y)\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)\wedge(y\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}({\sim}x)))

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt={\bf n}({\sim}y{/}x)={\bf n}({\sim}(x\cdot y)).

To see that the morphism
\phi

For the last part, observe that

so using the cyclicity of

\phi({\bf n}(x))=({\bf n}({\bf n}(x)),{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(x)))=({\bf n}(x),{\bf n}(x){\backslash}_{\bf n}\imath)={\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(\phi(x))
.
∎

Theorem 19
.

The functor
\mathbf{R}
is left adjoint of the functor
\mathbf{T}
. They form an adjunction between the categories

to
\mathcal{NCA}

Proof.

We need to show that for every

1_{\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{A},{\bf n})}=\psi^{-1}_{\mathbf{R}(\mathbf{A},{\bf n})}\circ\mathbf{R}(\phi_{(\mathbf{A},{\bf n})})\ \mbox{ and }\ \ 1_{\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{L},\imath)}=\mathbf{T}(\psi^{-1}_{(\mathbf{L},\imath)})\circ\phi_{\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{L},\imath)}

For the second identity, recall that

For all

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}(\psi^{-1}_{(\mathbf{L},\imath)})\circ\phi_{\mathbf{T}(\mathbf{L},\imath)}(a,b)

∎

3.3.
Rasiowa-style presentation

The class of algebras we study is motivated by Nelson lattices and
paraconsistent Nelson lattices. The original representation of these
algebras in terms of twist-products is carried out by considering
the algebra
{\bf A}
and defining an equivalence relation on
{\bf A}
which turns out to be a congruence with respect to some of the
original operations on
{\bf A}
(see

[
10
,
23
,
19
,
6
]
). Although our presentation has a
different flavor, it can be compared to the original presentations
of Nelson lattices (as in
[
20
]
,
[
21
]
) and of
paraconsistent Nelson lattices (as in
[
19
]
). We connect
these ideas in this section.

Let
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{NCA}
. Note that the implications
{\backslash}
and
{/}
are not preserved by the Nelson conucleus
{\bf n}.
However, we will prove that the operations

x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y={\bf n}(x){\backslash}y\ \mbox{ and }\ \ y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x=y{/}{\bf n}(x)

are mapped into the quotient properly.

Lemma 20
.

For a residuated lattice
{\bf A}
and a Nelson conucleus
{\bf n}
on
{\bf A}
we have that the map

n:A\rightarrow A_{{\bf n}},\ \ \mbox{ where }\ \ n(x)={\bf n}(x),

is a homomorphism from the algebra

{\bf\bar{A}}=\left(A,\wedge,\vee,\cdot,\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$},\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$},e\right)
to the algebra
{\bf A}_{{\bf n}}=({\bf n}[{\bf A}],\wedge_{\bf n},\vee,\cdot,{\backslash}_{{\bf n}},{/}_{{\bf n}},{\bf n}(e)).

Proof.

All conuclei produce homomorphisms for meet and
e
, as explained in the first section, while (T
1
) and (T
2
) give the homomorphism property for join and multiplication.
From equation (C
7

{\bf n}(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)={\bf n}({\bf n}(x){\backslash}y)={\bf n}({\bf n}(x){\backslash}{\bf n}(y))={\bf n}(x){\backslash}_{{\bf n}}{\bf n}(y).

Analogously we prove

\Swarrow

.
∎

With this result in mind, we consider the relations
\theta
and
\preceq
on
A
defined by:

(5)

\displaystyle x\theta y\

\displaystyle\mbox{ iff }\ {\bf n}(x)={\bf n}(y)

(6)

\displaystyle x\preceq y\

\displaystyle\mbox{ iff }\ {\bf n}(x)\leq{\bf n}(y)\ (\mbox{iff }\ {\bf n}(x)\leq y).

Lemma 21
.

If
{\bf n}
is a Nelson conucleus on

, then the following hold.

(1)

The relation
\preceq
is a preorder compatible with the operations of
\bf\bar{A}
.

(2)

The relation
\theta
is a congruence on
{\bf\bar{A}}
, which is the kernel of the map

{\bf\bar{A}}/\theta
is isomorphic to
{\bf A}_{{\bf n}}

Proof.

The fact that
\preceq
is a preorder is trivial. For the compatibility of
\preceq
with multiplication, we have
x\preceq y
and

implies
{\bf n}(x)\leq{\bf n}(y)
and
{\bf n}(z)\leq{\bf n}(w)
, so
{\bf n}(x){\bf n}(z)\leq{\bf n}(y){\bf n}(w)
, hence
{\bf n}(xz)\leq{\bf n}(yw)
, by (T
2
). Consequently we obtain

. The compatibility with
\vee
and
\wedge
is similar.
To prove that
\preceq
is compatible with

\Searrow

and

\Swarrow

we observe that if
{\bf n}(x)\leq{\bf n}(x^{\prime})
and
{\bf n}(y)\leq{\bf n}(y^{\prime})

(x^{\prime}\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)\preceq(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y^{\prime})
and
(y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)\preceq(y^{\prime}\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)
.
We give the proof for the case of

\Searrow

and
{\bf n}(y)\leq{\bf n}(y^{\prime})

7

and the definition of

\Searrow

{\bf n}(x^{\prime}\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)={\bf n}({\bf n}(x^{\prime}){\backslash}y)={\bf n}({\bf n}(x^{\prime}){\backslash}{\bf n}(y))\leq{\bf n}({\bf n}(x){\backslash}{\bf n}(y^{\prime}))={\bf n}({\bf n}(x){\backslash}y^{\prime})={\bf n}(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y^{\prime}).

The claim about

∎

Lemma 22
.

For a residuated lattice
{\bf A}
and a Nelson conucleus
{\bf n}
on
{\bf A}
we have

x\preceq y\ \mbox{ iff }\ \ (x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)\leq(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)\ \mbox{ iff }\ \ (y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}(y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)\leq(y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x).

Proof.

If
x\preceq y
then
{\bf n}(x)\leq y
, so

and by Equation (C
5
),

\displaystyle(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)

\displaystyle={\bf n}({\bf n}(x){\backslash}y){\backslash}({\bf n}(x){\backslash}y)

\displaystyle\leq{\bf n}(e){\backslash}({\bf n}(x){\backslash}y)=({\bf n}(x)\cdot{\bf n}(e)){\backslash}y={\bf n}(x){\backslash}y

\displaystyle=x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y.

Now assume

We propose the following definition, which is phrased in terms of

\Searrow

,

\Swarrow

\preceq
and

A
Rasiowa-type algebra

{\bf\bar{A}}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$},\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$},\sim,e)
is an algebra with five binary operations
\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$},\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}
, a unary operation
\sim
and a constant

, that satisfies:

(R1)

(A,\vee,\wedge)
is a lattice and
{\sim}
is a De Morgan involution on it, namely
{\sim}{\sim}x=x
and
{\sim}(x\vee y)={\sim}x\wedge{\sim}y
;

(R2)

the relation
\preceq
is a preorder, where
x\preceq y
if and only if
(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)\leq(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)
, and also if and only if
(y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}(y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)\leq(y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)
;

(R3)

the equivalence relation
\theta
induced by
\preceq
is a congruence on the algebra
{\bf\bar{A}}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$},\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$},e)
and the quotient
{\bf\bar{A}}/\theta
is a residuated lattice;

(R4)

{\sim}(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y)\mathrel{\theta}({\sim}y\cdot x)\
,
\ {\sim}(y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)\mathrel{\theta}(x\cdot{\sim}y)\
and
\ {\sim}(x\cdot y)\mathrel{\theta}(y\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}{\sim}x)\wedge({\sim}y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x)
;

(R5)

x\leq y
if and only if

and
{\sim}y\preceq{\sim}x
.

(R6)

for each

,
(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}{\sim}e)=({\sim}e\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x).

Theorem 24
.

Assume
{\bf\bar{A}}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$},\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$},\sim,e)
is a Rasiowa-type algebra. After setting

\displaystyle x{\backslash}y={\sim}({\sim}y\cdot x)\mbox{ and }\ y{/}x={\sim}(x\cdot{\sim}y),

\displaystyle{\bf n}(x)={\sim}(x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}{\sim}e)

Proof.

We define a map

\displaystyle h(x)=([x],[{\sim}x])

\bf A

First we observe that the image of

{Tw}({\bar{A}}/\theta,[\sim e])
. Indeed, we need to see that for each

(7)

[x]\cdot[{\sim}x]\vee[{\sim}x]\cdot[x]\leq[{\sim}e].

To this aim, by (R4) we get that
[{\sim}x]=[{\sim}(e\cdot x)]\leq[x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}{\sim}e]
and therefore, since

\displaystyle[x]\cdot[{\sim}x]\leq[x]\cdot[x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}{\sim}e]=[x]\cdot([x]\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}[{\sim}e])\leq[{\sim}e],

and similarly

The injectivity of

Note that (R1) implies that

and
{\sim}.
For the product, because of (R4) observe that for any

\displaystyle h(x)\cdot h(y)

∎

Lemma 26

4.2.

In this section we will construct a variety of algebras

such that

\mathcal{NCA}

To motivate our definition, we first rewrite what we need from Lemma

Lemma 28
.

If
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{NCA}
satisfies
{\bf n}(x)\leq e
and
{\bf n}(x)^{2}={\bf n}(x)
, then
{\bf n}(x)=(x\wedge e)^{2}
and
{\bf A}

(N1)

xy=(x\wedge e)^{2}y\vee x(y\wedge e)^{2}

(N2)

(xy\wedge e)^{2}=(x\wedge e)^{2}(y\wedge e)^{2}

{\bf A}_{\bf n}
is an integral residuated lattice where
\cdot

\wedge_{\bf n}

Finally,
{\bf A}
is commutative, distributive, and satisfies

Proof.

The first facts follow from Lemma

{\bf n}(x)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y)={\bf n}(x\wedge y)={\bf n}(x\wedge y)^{2}\leq{\bf n}(x){\bf n}(y)\leq{\bf n}(x)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y),

{\bf A}_{\bf n}
is a Brouwerian algebra.
Therefore,
{\bf A}_{\bf n}
is commutative and distributive and by Theorem
18

will also satisfy commutativity and distributivity, as these properties are inherited by the twist-product.
∎

With this in mind, we define the variety

Lemma 29

If

, then

Proof.

Trivially

\displaystyle((x\vee y)\wedge e)^{2}

(T

If

Lemma 31

We have that:

(2)

1.

\displaystyle xy=\neg\neg(xy)=\neg(x\to\neg y)=\neg(x^{2}\to\neg y)\vee\neg(y^{2}\to\neg x)=x^{2}y\vee y^{2}x.

5.
Sendlewski-style representation

We present two cases in which we can improve the categorical adjunction to a categorical equivalence.

5.1.
The case of Nelson-type algebras

Theorem
18
holds for algebras in
\mathcal{NT}
with the Nelson conucleus given by
{\bf n}(x)=(x\wedge e)^{2}
. To improve the result for this class, we follow Sendlewski’s ideas for Nelson algebras (
[

]
) and Odintsov’s ideas for the paraconsistent case (
[
18
]
).
Recall that if
{\bf A}=(A,\vee,\wedge,\cdot,\to,{\sim},e)
is in
\mathcal{NT}
, then
{\bf H}_{\bf A}=({\bf n}[A],\vee,\wedge_{\bf n},\to_{\bf n},e)
is a Brouwerian algebra.

If
{\bf H}
is a Brouwerian algebra and
F\subseteq H
is a lattice filter, then
F
is called a
Boolean filter
if for every
x,y\in H
the element
x\vee(x\rightarrow y)
belongs to
F
; such elements are called
dense
. The reader may note that if
{\bf H}
is lower bounded, i.e., it is the
\bot
-free reduct of a Heyting algebra and
F
is a Boolean filter of
{\bf H}
, then the quotient
{\bf H}/F
is the
\bot
-free reduct of a Boolean algebra.

Lemma 33
.

Let
\bf H
be a Brouwerian algebra,
\imath\in H
and
F
a Boolean filter of
\mathbf{H}
. Then the set

\text{Tw}(H,\imath,F)=\{(a,b)\in H\times H^{\partial}:a\wedge b\leq\imath,a\vee b\in F\}

is the universe of a subalgebra of
{\bf Tw(H,\imath)}
which is a twist-product over
{\bf H}
.

Proof.

We only need to show the closure of the operations
{\sim}
,
\wedge
and
\cdot
, as
\vee
and
\to
can be derived from them. Consider
(a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\text{Tw}(H,\imath,F)
. This means that
a\wedge b\leq\imath
,
a\vee b\in F
,
a^{\prime}\wedge b^{\prime}\leq\imath
and
a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime}\in F
.

({\sim})

this is immediate from the definition.

(\wedge)

(a,b)\wedge(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a\wedge a^{\prime},b\vee b^{\prime})
. We have to show that
(a\wedge a^{\prime})\vee(b\vee b^{\prime})\in F
. Indeed,

(a\wedge a^{\prime})\vee(b\vee b^{\prime})=(a\vee b\vee b^{\prime})\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee b\vee b^{\prime})\geq(a\vee b)\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime})\in F.

(\cdot)

(a,b)\cdot(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a\wedge a^{\prime},a\to b^{\prime}\wedge a^{\prime}\to b)
, then we have to prove that
(a\wedge a^{\prime})\vee(a\to b^{\prime}\wedge a^{\prime}\to b)\in F
. Recalling that
F
contains all the dense elements,

\displaystyle(a\wedge a^{\prime})\vee(a\to b^{\prime}\wedge a^{\prime}\to b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt=(a\vee(a\to b^{\prime}))\wedge(a\vee(a^{\prime}\to b))\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee(a\to b^{\prime}))\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee(a^{\prime}\to b))

\displaystyle\hskip-113.81102pt\geq(a\vee(a\to b^{\prime}))\wedge(a\vee b)\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee b^{\prime})\wedge(a^{\prime}\vee(a^{\prime}\to b))\in F

Finally, for each
a\in H
, the pair
(a,a\to\imath)
is in
\text{Tw}(H,\imath,F)
since
a\cdot(a\to\imath)\leq\imath
by residuation and
a\vee(a\to\imath)
is a dense element contained in
F.
Thus Definition
17
asserts that
\text{Tw}(H,\imath,F)
is a twist-product over
{\bf H}.

∎

To obtain an isomorphism for each
{\bf A}\in\mathcal{NT}
, we need to find a Boolean filter

on
{\bf H}_{\bf A}
such that
{\bf A}\cong{\bf Tw}({\bf H}_{\bf A},\imath,F)
. Recalling that
x\Rightarrow y=x\rotatebox[origin]{-27.5}{$\Searrow$}y=y\rotatebox[origin]{27.5}{$\Swarrow$}x
in the commutative case, we will frequently use the fact that the equation

holds for every pair of elements

in an algebra
{\bf A}\in\mathcal{NT}
(it follows from the definition of
\Rightarrow

1
and the fact that
\wedge_{\bf n}
and
\cdot
coincide in
\bf H_{\bf A}
).

Lemma 34
.

If
{\bf A}\in\mathcal{NT}
and then the subset
F_{A}=\{{\bf n}(x\vee{\sim}x):x\in A\}=\{{\bf n}(w):{\sim}w\leq w\}=\{{\bf n}(z):{\bf n}({\sim}z)\leq{\bf n}(z)\}
of
H_{A}
is a Boolean filter.

Proof.

For all
x\in A
, we have
{\sim}(x\vee{\sim}x)={\sim}x\wedge x\leq x\vee{\sim}x
, and if
{\sim}w\leq w
, then
w\vee{\sim}w=w
. So,
\{{\bf n}(x\vee{\sim}x):x\in A\}=\{{\bf n}(w):{\sim}w\leq w\}
.
We have that if
{\sim}w\leq w
, then
{\bf n}({\sim}w)\leq{\bf n}(w)
, so
\{{\bf n}(w):{\sim}w\leq w\}\subseteq\{{\bf n}(z):{\bf n}({\sim}z)\leq{\bf n}(z)\}
. Also, if
z
satisfies
{\bf n}({\sim}z)\leq{\bf n}(z)
, then
{\bf n}(z\vee{\sim}z)={\bf n}(z)\vee{\bf n}({\sim}z)={\bf n}(z)
; so,
\{{\bf n}(z):{\bf n}({\sim}z)\leq{\bf n}(z)\}\subseteq\{{\bf n}(x\vee{\sim}x):x\in A\}
.

We will prove that
F_{A}
is a (lattice) filter. Indeed, as
{\bf n}(e)
is the top element of
{\bf H}_{\bf A}
, we have
{\bf n}(e)={\bf n}(e\vee{\sim}e)\in F_{A}
.

To prove closure under
\wedge
, take
{\bf n}(w),{\bf n}(z)\in F_{A}
, with

{\sim}w\leq w
and
{\sim}z\leq z
. For

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle={\sim}\left(w\wedge\left(w\Rightarrow{\sim}(w\vee z)\right)\right)\wedge{\sim}\left(z\wedge\left(z\Rightarrow{\sim}(w\vee z)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=({\sim}w\vee{\bf n}(w)(w\vee z))\wedge({\sim}z\vee{\bf n}(z)(w\vee z)),

we have
{\sim}t=\left(w\wedge\left(w\Rightarrow{\sim}(w\vee z)\right)\right)\vee\left(z\wedge\left(z\Rightarrow{\sim}(w\vee z)\right)\right).

Using divisibility and distributivity,

\displaystyle{\bf n}({\sim}t)

\displaystyle=\left({\bf n}(w)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}(w\vee z))\right)\vee\left({\bf n}(z)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}(w\vee z))\right)

\displaystyle={\bf n}(w\vee z)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}(w\vee z))

\displaystyle={\bf n}({\sim}w)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}z).

Recalling Equation (
8
) and that
p\leq{\sim}{\sim}(p\vee q)
for all
p,q
we get

\displaystyle{\bf n}(t)

\displaystyle=\left({\bf n}({\sim}w)\vee\left({\bf n}(w)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}{\sim}(w\vee z))\right)\right)\wedge\left({\bf n}({\sim}z)\vee\left({\bf n}(z)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}{\sim}(w\vee z))\right)\right)

\displaystyle={\bf n}(w\vee{\sim}w)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(z\vee{\sim}z)

\displaystyle={\bf n}(w)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(z),

so
{\bf n}({\sim}t)\leq{\bf n}(t)
, hence
{\bf n}(t)\in F_{A}
. Since also
{\bf n}(w)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(z)={\bf n}(t)
, we get
{\bf n}(w)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(z)\in F_{A}
.

Additionally, if
{\bf n}(w)\geq{\bf n}(y)
, where
{\sim}w\leq w
, we have
{\sim}(w\vee y)={\sim}w\wedge{\sim}y\leq w\leq w\vee y
, hence
{\bf n}(w\vee y)\in F_{A}
. Since we also have
{\bf n}(w\vee y)={\bf n}(w)\vee{\bf n}(y)={\bf n}(y)
we get
{\bf n}(y)\in F_{A}
.

To show that
F_{A}
contains all dense elements, we need to see for all
x,y\in A
, that the element
{\bf n}(x)\vee({\bf n}(x)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y))={\bf n}(x\vee(x\Rightarrow y))
is in
F_{A}
. This is true since using Equation (
8
) we obtain

\displaystyle{\bf n}({\sim}(x\vee(x\Rightarrow y)))

\displaystyle={\bf n}({\sim}x\wedge({\bf n}(x)\cdot{\sim}y))={\bf n}({\sim}x)\wedge{\bf n}(x)\wedge{\bf n}({\sim}y)

\displaystyle\leq{\bf n}(x)\vee({\bf n}(x)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y))={\bf n}(x\vee(x\Rightarrow y)).

∎

Theorem 35
.

Let
{\bf A}\in\mathcal{NT}
. If
{\bf H_{A}}
,
\imath
and
F_{A}
are as before, then
{\bf A}\cong{\bf Tw({\bf H}_{\bf A},\imath,F_{A})}
.

Proof.

From Theorem
18
, it is sufficient to show that
\phi_{\bf A}(A)=\text{Tw}(A_{\bf n},\imath,F_{A})=\text{Tw}(H_{A},\imath,F_{A})
. One inclusion is immediate, for if
x\in A
, then
{\bf n}(x\vee{\sim}x)\in F_{A}
by definition of
F_{A}
.

For the other, consider
({\bf n}(x),{\bf n}(y))\in\text{Tw}(H_{A},\imath,F_{A})
. We have to find
z\in A
such that
{\bf n}(z)={\bf n}(x)
and
{\bf n}({\sim}z)={\bf n}(y)
. As
{\bf n}(x)\vee{\bf n}(y)\in F_{A}
by hypothesis, let
w\in A
be such that
{\bf n}(x)\vee{\bf n}(y)={\bf n}(w\vee{\sim}w)
. Define now

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=[(w\wedge{\sim}w)\vee{\sim}\left(x\Rightarrow{\bf n}(y)\right)\vee{\sim}\left(y\Rightarrow{\bf n}(x)\right)]\wedge\left(y\Rightarrow{\bf n}(x)\right).

Then as
{\bf n}(x)\wedge{\bf n}(y)\leq{\bf n}({\sim}e)
, we have that
{\bf n}(x)\leq{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(y))
and
{\bf n}(y)\leq{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(x))
, so

\displaystyle{\bf n}(z)

\displaystyle=\left({\bf n}(w\wedge{\sim}w)\vee{\bf n}\left({\sim}\left(x\Rightarrow{\bf n}(y)\right)\right)\vee{\bf n}\left({\sim}\left(y\Rightarrow{\bf n}(x)\right)\right)\right)\wedge_{\bf n}\left({\bf n}(y)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=\left({\bf n}(w\wedge{\sim}w)\vee\left({\bf n}(x)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(y))\right)\vee\left({\bf n}(y)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(x))\right)\right)\wedge_{\bf n}\left({\bf n}(y)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=\left({\bf n}(w\wedge{\sim}w)\vee{\bf n}(x)\vee{\bf n}(y)\right)\wedge_{\bf n}\left({\bf n}(y)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=\left({\bf n}(x)\vee{\bf n}(y)\right)\wedge_{\bf n}\left({\bf n}(y)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt={\bf n}(x)

\displaystyle{\bf n}({\sim}z)

\displaystyle=\left({\bf n}(w\vee{\sim}w)\wedge_{\bf n}\left({\bf n}(x)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y)\right)\wedge_{\bf n}\left({\bf n}(y)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\right)\right)\vee\left({\bf n}(y)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(x))\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=\left(({\bf n}(x)\vee{\bf n}(y))\wedge_{\bf n}\left({\bf n}(x)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y)\right)\wedge_{\bf n}\left({\bf n}(y)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\right)\right)\vee{\bf n}(y)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt=({\bf n}(x)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y))\vee{\bf n}(y)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt={\bf n}(y).

∎

Theorem
35
provides a way to improve the result from Theorem
19
. First, we show how morphisms behave between Nelson-type algebras.

Lemma 36
.

Let
{\bf H}_{1},{\bf H}_{2}
be Brouwerian algebras,
\imath_{1}\in H_{1},\imath_{2}\in H_{2}
and let
F_{1}\subseteq H_{1},F_{2}\subseteq H_{2}
be Boolean filters. If
f:{\bf H}_{1}\to{\bf H}_{2}
is a morphism such that
f(\imath_{1})=\imath_{2}
and
f(F_{1})\subseteq F_{2}
, then
\varphi_{f}:{\bf Tw({\bf L}_{1},\imath_{1},F_{1})}\to{\bf Tw({\bf L}_{2},\imath_{2},F_{2})}
given by
\varphi_{f}(a,b)=(f(a),f(b))
is morphism in
\mathcal{NT}
.

Lemma 37
.

Let
{\bf A}_{1},{\bf A}_{2}\in\mathcal{NT}
. If
\varphi:{\bf A}_{1}\to{\bf A}_{2}
is a morphism, then
f_{\varphi}=\varphi|_{{\bf H}_{{\bf A}_{1}}}
is a morphism from
{\bf H}_{{\bf A}_{1}}
into
{\bf H}_{{\bf A}_{2}}
such that
f_{\varphi}(({\sim}e_{1}\wedge e_{1})^{2})=({\sim}e_{2}\wedge e_{2})^{2}
and
f_{\varphi}(F_{A_{1}})\subseteq F_{A_{2}}
.

Now, consider the category
\mathcal{BF}_{\text{cy}}
with objects triples
(\mathbf{H},\imath,F)
, where
\mathbf{H}
is a Brouwerian algebra,
\imath\in H
and
F\subseteq H
a Boolean filter; as morphisms we take Brouwerian algebra homomorphisms
f:{\bf H}_{1}\to{\bf H}_{2}
such that
f(\imath_{1})=\imath_{2}
and
f(F_{1})\subseteq F_{2}
. Note that
\mathcal{NT}
defines a category where the morphisms are the algebraic homomorphisms. We can conclude:

Theorem 38
.

The categories
\mathcal{NT}
and
\mathcal{BF}_{\text{cy}}
are equivalent.

5.2.
Involutive-image
\mathcal{NCA}

In this section we consider a variety in which the categorical
adjunction in Theorem
19
is strengthened. Unlike in
Nelson-type algebras, the Nelson conucleus
{\bf n}
doesn’t need to be
a term function.

5.2.1.
Twist products and dualizing elements

Let
\bf L
be a commutative involutive residuated lattice and let
f={\sim}e
. We
define the operation
a\oplus b={\sim}(({\sim}b)\cdot({\sim}a))
. It is well known
[
12
]
that this operation is associative, commutative and satisfies, among other things:

a\oplus(b\wedge c)=a\oplus b\wedge a\oplus c\ \ \ \mbox{ and }\ \mbox{
if }a\leq b\mbox{ then }a\oplus c\leq b\oplus c.

Lemma 39
.

Let
\bf L
be a commutative involutive residuated lattice,
\imath\in L
and
F
be a lattice filter of
L
that contains the element
e\oplus\imath=f\to\imath
. Then the set

\displaystyle\text{Tw}(L,\imath,F)=\{(a,b)\in L\times L^{\partial}:a\cdot b\leq\imath,a\oplus b\in F\}

is the universe of a subalgebra of
{\bf Tw(L,\imath)}
which is a twist-product over
{\bf L}
.

Proof.

Let
(a,b),(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\in\text{Tw}(L,\imath,F)
, then
(a\to f)\to b,(a^{\prime}\to f)\to b^{\prime}\in F
.

•

\text{Tw}(L,\imath,F)
is clearly closed under
{\sim}
by commutativity of
\oplus
.

•

(a,b)\wedge(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a\wedge a^{\prime},b\vee b^{\prime})
, and

\displaystyle(a\wedge a^{\prime})\oplus(b\vee b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=a\oplus(b\vee b^{\prime})\wedge a^{\prime}\oplus(b\vee b^{\prime})\geq a\oplus b\wedge a^{\prime}\oplus b^{\prime}\in F.

•

(a,b)\cdot(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a\cdot a^{\prime},a\to b^{\prime}\wedge a^{\prime}\to b)
, and

\displaystyle(a\cdot a^{\prime})\oplus(a\to b^{\prime}\wedge a^{\prime}\to b)

\displaystyle=\left((a\cdot a^{\prime})\oplus(a\to b^{\prime})\right)\wedge\left((a\cdot a^{\prime})\oplus(a^{\prime}\to b)\right)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt=((a\cdot a^{\prime})\to f)\to(a\to b^{\prime})\wedge((a\cdot a^{\prime})\to f)\to(a^{\prime}\to b)

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt=((a\cdot(a\to(a^{\prime}\to f))\to b^{\prime}))\wedge((a^{\prime}\cdot(a^{\prime}\to(a\to f))\to b))

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\geq((a^{\prime}\to f)\to b^{\prime})\wedge((a\to f)\to b)\in F.

•

{\bf n}_{\text{Tw}}(a,b)=(a,a\to\imath)
, and

\displaystyle a\oplus(a\to\imath)

\displaystyle=(a\to f)\to(a\to\imath)=(a(a\to f))\to\imath\geq f\to\imath=e\oplus\imath\in F.

∎

Note that
e\oplus\imath\in F
automatically follows from assuming that
e\in F
in the special case where
f=0
is the bottom element; in that case as
e=f\to f
, so
e
will be the top element.
The next lemma provides a hint on how to define filters starting from the twist structure.

Lemma 40
.

Let
\bf L
be an integral commutative involutive residuated lattice. Consider
\imath\in L
and
F
a lattice filter of
L
. Then
c\in F
if and only if there exists
(a,b)\in Tw(L,\imath,F)
such that
c=a\oplus b
.

Proof.

Note that
0:={\sim}e
is the bottom element.
If
(a,b)\in Tw(L,\imath,F)
, then by definition
c=a\oplus b\in F
. Conversely, if
c\in F
then
c\oplus 0=c\in F
and
c\cdot 0=0\leq\imath
, so
(c,0)\in Tw(L,\imath,F)
satisfies what we wanted. ∎

5.2.2.
Involutive-image Nelson Conucleus Algebras

If
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{NCA}
is such that
\bf A_{{\bf n}}
is an involutive residuated lattice, we want to find a lattice filter
F_{A}
on
\bf A_{{\bf n}}
such that
{\bf A}\cong{\bf Tw}(\bf A_{{\bf n}},\imath,F_{A})
. Given the characterization in Lemma
40
,

F_{A}
should contain the elements of the form
a\oplus b
, where
x=(a,b)\in{\bf Tw}(\bf A_{{\bf n}},\imath,F_{A})
. Note that
{\bf n}(x)=(a,a{\backslash}\imath)
and
{\bf n}({\sim}x)=(b,b{\backslash}\imath)
. The identification between
a
and
(a,a{\backslash}\imath)
and between
b
and
(b,b{\backslash}\imath)
provided by
\psi_{(\mathbf{L},\imath)}
guides us in defining

F_{A}=\{{\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}({\sim}x):x\in A\}.

We define the variety
\mathcal{INCA}
of algebras
({\bf A},{\bf n})
such that
\bf A
is an involutive commutative residuated lattice with a bottom element
\bot
,
(\hat{\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{NCA}
where
\hat{\bf A}
is the
\bot
-less reduct of
\bf A
, and such that the following equation holds:

(IT1)

{\bf n}({\bf n}({\bf n}(x)\to\bot)\to\bot)={\bf n}(x)
.

We note that
\mathbf{A}
is also bounded above with top element
\top={\sim}\bot=\bot\to\bot
.

In this case,
\bf L_{A}:=({\bf A}_{{\bf n}},0,1)
will be an integral commutative residuated lattice, with
0={\bf n}(\bot)
as negation constant element and
1={\bf n}(\top)={\bf n}(e)
as unit element. If we define for all
a\in L_{A}

\neg_{{\bf n}}a=a\to_{{\bf n}}0,

since
a={\bf n}(a)
, we have
\neg_{\bf n}a=a\to_{\bf n}0={\bf n}(a\Rightarrow 0)={\bf n}({\bf n}(a)\to{\bf n}(\bot))={\bf n}({\bf n}(a)\to\bot)
. So if
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{INCA}
, the residuated lattice
\bf L_{A}:=({\bf A}_{{\bf n}},0,1)
is bounded, commutative, and (IT1) translates into: for all
a\in A_{\bf n}

\neg_{\bf n}\neg_{\bf n}a=a.

In Lemma
39
, we saw that if
\bf L
is a commutative residuated lattice where the dualizing element is the least element and
\imath\in L
, then
{\bf Tw({\bf L},\imath,F)}
defines a subalgebra of
{{\bf Tw}({\bf L},\imath)}
. Note that the latter is in
\mathcal{INCA}
.

Example 41
.

As a particular case, if
\bf L
is a bounded commutative residuated lattice satisfying
\neg\neg x=x
, we have that
{\bf n}(x)=x
is a Nelson conucleus, so
({\bf L},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{INCA}
when we consider
{\sim}x=\neg x
, and by Theorem
18
we have that
{\bf L}
embeds into
{\bf Tw}({\bf L},0)
by the mapping
x\mapsto(x,\neg x)
.

In the notation involving
\bot
, note that we have

\displaystyle F_{A}

\displaystyle=\{{\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}({\sim}x):x\in A\}=\{(\neg_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x))\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}x):x\in A\}

\displaystyle=\{({\bf n}(x)\to_{\bf n}0)\to_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}x):x\in A\}=\{{\bf n}(({\bf n}(x)\Rightarrow\bot)\Rightarrow{\bf n}({\sim}x)):x\in A\}

\displaystyle=\{{\bf n}({\bf n}({\bf n}(x)\to\bot)\to{\sim}x):x\in A\}.

We will need the following technical result.

Lemma 42
.

If
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{NCA}
has a lower bound
\bot
, then with the previous notation
F_{A}=\{{\bf n}({\sim}z):{\bf n}(z)=0,z\in A\}
.

Proof.

Recall that
F_{A}=\{{\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}({\sim}x):x\in A\}

Clearly, if
{\bf n}(z)=0
, for some
z\in A
, then
{\bf n}({\sim}z)={\bf n}(z)\oplus{\bf n}({\sim}z)\in F_{A}
. Conversely, let
x\in A
and define
z=x\cdot{\bf n}({\bf n}(x)\to\bot)
. We have

{\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}({\sim}x)={\bf n}({\bf n}({\bf n}(x)\to\bot)\to{\sim}x)={\bf n}({\sim}(x\cdot{\bf n}({\bf n}(x)\to\bot))={\bf n}({\sim}z).

Also,

\displaystyle{\bf n}(z)

\displaystyle={\bf n}(x)\cdot{\bf n}({\bf n}(x)\to\bot)={\bf n}({\bf n}(x)\cdot({\bf n}(x)\to\bot))={\bf n}(\bot)=0.

∎

Theorem 43
.

If
({\bf A},{\bf n})\in\mathcal{INCA}
,
1={\bf n}(e)
and
\imath={\bf n}({\sim}e)
, then
F_{A}
is a lattice filter of
\bf L_{A}
and

{\bf A}\cong{{\bf Tw}({\bf L}_{\bf A},\imath,F_{A})}.

Proof.

We will use Lemma
42
throughout the proof.
First note that
1\oplus\imath={\bf n}(e)\oplus{\bf n}({\sim}e)\in F_{A}
.
Now, we show that
F_{A}
is a lattice filter.

•

1\in F_{A}
, because
{\bf n}(\top)\oplus{\bf n}({\sim}\top)={\bf n}(\top)\oplus{\bf n}(\bot)=1\oplus 0=1
.

•

If
a={\bf n}({\sim}x)
, where
{\bf n}(x)=0
and
b={\bf n}({\sim}y)
, where
{\bf n}(y)=0
, for some
x,y\in A
, define
z:=x\vee y
. Then,
a\wedge_{\bf n}b={\bf n}({\sim}x)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}y)={\bf n}({\sim}x\wedge{\sim}y)={\bf n}({\sim}(x\vee y))={\bf n}({\sim}z)
. Also,
{\bf n}(z)={\bf n}(x\vee y)={\bf n}(x)\vee{\bf n}(y)=0\vee 0=0
. So,
a\wedge_{\bf n}b\in F_{A}
.

•

If
a={\bf n}({\sim}x)
, where
{\bf n}(x)=0
and
b\geq a
with
b={\bf n}(y)
for some
x,y\in A
, consider

z={\sim}y\wedge x,

so
{\sim}z=y\vee{\sim}x
. We have
{\bf n}({\sim}z)={\bf n}(y)\vee{\bf n}({\sim}x)=b\vee a=b
, and

{\bf n}(z)={\bf n}({\sim}y\wedge x)={\bf n}({\sim}y)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)={\bf n}({\sim}y)\wedge_{\bf n}0=0,

So
b\in F_{A}
.

Now, using Theorem
18
, we will show that
\phi_{\bf A}(A)=\text{Tw}(L_{A},\imath,F_{A})
. Note that if
({\bf n}(x),{\bf n}({\sim}x))\in\phi_{\bf A}(A)
, then
{\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}({\sim}x)\in F_{A}
, so
\phi_{\bf A}(A)\subseteq\text{Tw}(L_{A},\imath,F_{A})
. For the other inclusion, consider
({\bf n}(x),{\bf n}(y))\in\text{Tw}(L_{A},\imath,F_{A})
. Therefore we have
{\bf n}(x)\cdot{\bf n}(y)\leq\imath={\bf n}({\sim}e)
and
{\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}(y)\in F_{A}
. Thus there exists
w\in A
such that
{\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}(y)={\bf n}({\sim}w)
and
{\bf n}(w)=0
. We will find
z\in A
such that
{\bf n}(z)={\bf n}(x)
and
{\bf n}({\sim}z)={\bf n}(y)
.

Recall that
\oplus
is an operation on
L_{A}
defined by
a\oplus b=\neg_{\bf n}a\mathbin{\rightarrow}_{\bf n}b
, where for
a\in L_{A}
we have
\neg_{\bf n}a=a\mathbin{\rightarrow}_{\bf n}0={\bf n}(a\Rightarrow 0)={\bf n}({\bf n}(a)\mathbin{\rightarrow}{\bf n}(\bot))={\bf n}({\bf n}(a)\mathbin{\rightarrow}\bot)
.
For convenience we extend these operations to
A
by defining for
x,y\in A
,

\neg_{\bf n}x={\bf n}({\bf n}(x)\mathbin{\rightarrow}\bot)
and
x\oplus y=\neg_{\bf n}x\mathbin{\rightarrow}_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y)
. It then follows that
{\bf n}(x\oplus y)={\bf n}(\neg_{\bf n}x\mathbin{\rightarrow}_{\bf n}{\bf n}(y))={\bf n}(\neg_{\bf n}x\Rightarrow y)={\bf n}(\neg_{\bf n}x)\Rightarrow{\bf n}(y)=\neg_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\Rightarrow{\bf n}(y)={\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}(y)
.

Consider
z=x\oplus w\wedge{\sim}{\bf n}(y)
. Then recalling that
{\bf n}(x)\cdot{\bf n}(y)\leq{\bf n}({\sim}e)
implies that
{\bf n}(x)\leq{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(y))

\displaystyle{\bf n}(z)

\displaystyle={\bf n}(x\oplus w\wedge{\sim}{\bf n}(y))={\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}(w)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(y)))

\displaystyle={\bf n}(x)\oplus 0\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(y)))={\bf n}(x)\wedge_{\bf n}{\bf n}({\sim}{\bf n}(y))={\bf n}(x),

and

\displaystyle{\bf n}({\sim}z)

\displaystyle={\bf n}({\sim}(\neg_{\bf n}\mathbin{\rightarrow}w)\vee{\bf n}(y))={\bf n}({\sim}(\neg_{\bf n}x\mathbin{\rightarrow}w))\vee{\bf n}(y)

\displaystyle={\bf n}(\neg_{\bf n}x\cdot{\sim}w))\vee{\bf n}(y)={\bf n}(\neg_{\bf n}x)\cdot{\bf n}({\sim}w))\vee{\bf n}(y)

\displaystyle={\bf n}(\neg_{\bf n}x)\cdot({\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}(y)))\vee{\bf n}(y)=\neg_{\bf n}{\bf n}(x)\cdot({\bf n}(x)\oplus{\bf n}(y)))\vee{\bf n}(y)={\bf n}(y).

∎

New q q -supercongruences from the Bailey transformation
Xiaoxia Wang, Chang Xu ∗

Abstract
. Inspired by the recent work of Guo, we establish some new q q -supercongruences including q q -analogues of some Ramannujan-type supercongruences, by using the Bailey transformation formula and the ‘creative microscoping’ method recently introduced by Guo and Zudilin.

Primary 33D15; Secondary 11A07, 11B65

1.
introduction

It was van Hamme
[
13
]
that investigated partial sums of Ramanujan’s summation formular for
1/\pi

and conjectured
13
Ramanujan-type supercongruences labeled as (A.2)-(M.2), such as:

\begin{split}&\mathrm{(B.2)}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/2}(-1)^{k}(4k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{2})^{3}_{k}}{k!^{3}}\equiv\frac{-p}{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{2})^{2}}\pmod{p^{3}};\\
&\mathrm{(E.2)}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/3}(-1)^{k}(6k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{3})^{3}_{k}}{k!^{3}}\equiv p\pmod{p^{3}}\quad\mathrm{if}\quad p\equiv 1\pmod{6};\\
&\mathrm{(F.2)}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{(p-1)/4}(-1)^{k}(8k+1)\frac{(\frac{1}{4})^{3}_{k}}{k!^{3}}\equiv\frac{-p}{\Gamma_{p}(\frac{1}{4})\Gamma_{p}(\frac{3}{4})}\pmod{p^{3}}.\end{split}

(1.1)

Here and in what follows,
p
is an odd prime,
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)
is the Pochhammer symbol, and
the
p
-adic gamma function is defined as

\Gamma_{p}(x)=\lim_{m\rightarrow x}(-1)^{m}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<k<m\\
(k,p)=1\end{subarray}}k,

where the limit is for
m
tending to
x

p
-adically in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
In 2015, Swisher
[
11
]
proved the following unified form of
van Hamme’s three supercongruences in (
1.1
):

\sum_{k=0}^{a(bp-1)}(-1)^{k}\left(\frac{2k}{a}+1\right)\frac{(a)_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}\equiv(-1)^{a(bp-1)}p\cdot b=\frac{-pb}{\Gamma_{p}(a)\Gamma_{p}(1-a)}\pmod{p^{3}},

(1.2)

where

a\in\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{4}\right\}
,
p\geq 5
when
a=1/4
,

b=1
when
p\equiv 1\pmod{\frac{1}{a}}
and
b=\frac{1}{a}-1
when
p\equiv-1\pmod{\frac{1}{a}}
.

For completeness, we first present the needed definitions. For any complex numbers
a
and
q
, the

q
-shifted factorial
is defined as

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{j})\quad\mathrm{and}\quad(a;q)_{k}=\frac{(a;q)_{\infty}}{(aq^{k};q)_{\infty}}\quad\mathrm{where}\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

For convenience, we adopt the notation

\displaystyle(a_{1},a_{2},...,a_{r};q)_{k}=(a_{1};q)_{k}(a_{2};q)_{k}\cdots(a_{r};q)_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{C}\cup\infty.

Following Gasper and Rahman
[

\begin{split}{}_{r+1}\phi_{r}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r+1}\\
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{r}\end{array};q,\,z\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{r+1};q)_{k}}{(q,b_{1},\ldots,b_{r};q)_{k}}z^{k}.\end{split}

(1.3)

Let
[n]_{q}=[n]=(1-q^{n})/(1-q)
be the

-

the

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}(q)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant k\leqslant n\\

where

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+\alpha q^{2k})(\alpha^{2},q^{2}/a,q^{2}/b;q^{2})_{k}(-q,\alpha q/\lambda;q)_{k}(-\alpha^{2}\lambda ab)^{k}q^{k^{2}-3k}}{(1+\alpha)(q^{2},\alpha^{2}a,\alpha^{2}b;q^{2})_{k}(\alpha,-\lambda;q)_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha^{2}q^{2},\alpha^{2}ab/{q^{2}};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(\alpha^{2}a,\alpha^{2}b;q^{2})_{\infty}}\,{}_{4}\phi_{3}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{c}q^{2}/a,\ q^{2}/b,\ \lambda,\ q\lambda\\

(1.6)

\displaystyle\quad\equiv(-1)^{({n-s})/{3}}\frac{[n]_{q^{2}}}{[s]_{q^{2}}}q^{{(n-s)(n+s-3)}/{3}}\sum_{k=0}^{(n-s)/3}\frac{\left(q^{2s};q^{6}\right)_{k}^{2}\left(q^{3};q^{6}\right)_{k}}{\left(q^{6},q^{3+s},q^{6+s};q^{6}\right)_{k}}q^{2k(3-s)}.

Performing

We first present an auxiliary

Lemma 1

Let

(2.1)

We next present the following one-parametric generalization of Theorem

Lemma 2
.

Let

Proof.

For

or

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n-r)/d}(-1)^{k}\frac{(1+q^{2dk+r})(q^{2r+2n},q^{2r-2n},q^{2r};q^{2d})_{k}}{(1+q^{r})(q^{2d+2n},q^{2d-2n},q^{2d};q^{2d})_{k}}q^{dk^{2}+2k(d-r)}

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{(n-r)/d}(-1)^{k}\frac{(1+q^{2dk+r})(q^{2r+2n},q^{2r-2n};q^{2d})_{k}}{(1+q^{r})(q^{2d+2n},q^{2d-2n};q^{2d})_{k}}q^{dk^{2}+k(d-r)}\\
&\quad=\frac{(q^{2r+2d},q^{2d-2r};q^{2d})_{\infty}}{(q^{2d-2n},q^{2d+2n};q^{2d})_{\infty}}\sum_{k=0}^{(n-r)/d}\frac{(q^{2r+2n},q^{2r-2n},q^{r};q^{2d})_{k}}{(q^{2d},q^{2d+r},q^{2r};q^{2d})_{k}}q^{2k(d-r)}.\end{split}

Using equation (
2.3
), we immediately get, modulo
1-aq^{2n}
or
a-q^{2n}
,

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{(n-r)/d}(-1)^{k}\frac{(1+q^{2dk+r})(aq^{2r},q^{2r}/a;q^{2d})_{k}}{(1+q^{r})(aq^{2d},q^{2d}/a;q^{2d})_{k}}q^{dk^{2}+k(d-r)}\\
&\quad\equiv(-1)^{(n-r)/d}\frac{[n]_{q^{2}}}{[r]_{q^{2}}}q^{{(n-r)(n+r-d)}/d}\sum_{k=0}^{(n-r)/d}\frac{(aq^{2r},q^{2r}/a,q^{r};q^{2d})_{k}}{(q^{2d},q^{2dk+r},q^{2r};q^{2d})_{k}}q^{2k(d-r)}.\end{split}

(3.4)

Since
1-aq^{2n}
and
a-q^{2n}
are relatively
prime polynomials, (
3.4
) still holds modulo
(1-aq^{2n})(a-q^{2n})
.

From
q
-congruence (
2.1
), we deduce that, for
0\leq k\leq(n-r)/d
,
the
((n-r)/d-k)
-th terms of the left-hand side of (
3.4
) modulo

can be written as follows,

\begin{split}&(-1)^{(n-r)/d-k}\frac{(1+q^{2n-2dk-r)})(aq^{2r},q^{2r}/a;q^{2d})_{(n-r)/d-k}}{(1+q^{r})(q^{2d}/a,aq^{2d};q^{2d})_{(n-r)/d-k}}q^{d((n-r)/d-k)^{2}+((n-r)/d-k)(d-r)}\\
&\equiv(-1)^{(n-r)/d-k}\frac{(1+q^{2dk+r})(aq^{2r},q^{2r}/a;q^{2d})_{k}}{(1+q^{r})(aq^{2d},q^{2d}/a;q^{2d})_{k}}q^{3n(n-r)/d+dk^{2}+k(d-r)-n}\\
&\equiv-(-1)^{k}\frac{(1+q^{2dk+r})(aq^{2r},q^{2r}/a;q^{2d})_{k}}{(1+q^{r})(aq^{2d},q^{2d}/a;q^{2d})_{k}}q^{dk^{2}+k(d-r)}\pmod{\Phi_{n}(-q)},\end{split}

which means the left-hand side of (

Since the right-hand side of (

Fast Computation of Optimal Transport via Entropy-Regularized Extragradient Methods
Gen Li, Yanxi Chen, Yu Huang, Yuejie Chi, H. Vincent Poor, Yuxin Chen

Abstract
Efficient computation of the optimal transport distance between two distributions serves as an algorithm subroutine that empowers various applications. This paper develops a scalable first-order optimization-based method that computes optimal transport to within ε \varepsilon additive accuracy with runtime O ~ ​ ( n 2 / ε ) \widetilde{O}(n^{2}/\varepsilon) , where n n denotes the dimension of the probability distributions of interest. Our algorithm achieves the state-of-the-art computational guarantees among all first-order methods, while exhibiting favorable numerical performance compared to classical algorithms like Sinkhorn and Greenkhorn. Underlying our algorithm designs are two key elements: (a) converting the original problem into a bilinear minimax problem over probability distributions; (b) exploiting the extragradient idea — in conjunction with entropy regularization and adaptive learning rates — to accelerate convergence.



Keywords:
optimal transport, extragradient methods, entropy regularization, first-order methods, adaptive learning rates

Introduction

Quantifying the distance between two probability distributions is an algorithm subroutine that permeates and empowers a wealth of modern data science applications.
For instance, how to measure the difference between the model distribution and the real distribution in generative adversarial networks
Arjovsky et al., 2017
,
how to evaluate the intrinsic dissimilarity between two point clouds in computer graphics
Solomon et al., 2015
;
Kim et al., 2013
,
and how to assess the distribution shift in transfer learning
Gayraud et al., 2017
,
are all representative examples built upon probability distances.

This paper is about computing an elementary instance within this arena that gains increasing popularity,
that is, the optimal transport distance between two distributions
Peyré et al., 2019
.
It sometimes goes by the name of the earth mover’s distance
Werman et al., 1985
;
Rubner et al., 2000
;
Pele and Werman, 2009
or the Wasserstein distance
Villani, 2009
.
In light of the celebrated Kantorovich relaxation
Kantorovich, 1942
;
Peyré et al., 2019
, computing the optimal transport between two
n
-dimensional probability distributions can be cast as solving a linear program over probability matrices with fixed marginals:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathop{\text{minimize}}\limits_{\bm{P}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}&\langle\bm{W},\bm{P}\rangle\\
\text{subject to}&\bm{P}\geq\bm{0},\,\bm{P}\bm{1}=\bm{r},\,\bm{P}^{\top}\bm{1}=\bm{c}.\end{array}

Here,
\bm{r}=[r_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq n}
and
\bm{c}=[c_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq n}
are
n

\bm{W}=[w_{i,j}]_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n\times n}
stands for a given non-negative cost matrix (so that the objective function
\langle\bm{W},\bm{P}\rangle

measures the total transportation cost), and
\bm{1}
denotes the all-one vector.
In a nutshell, the optimal transport problem amounts to finding the most cost-efficient reshaping of one distribution into another,
or equivalently, the most economical coupling of the two distributions.

At first glance, the optimal transport problem (
1
) seems to be readily solvable via relatively mature toolboxes in linear programming.
Nevertheless, the unprecedentedly large problem dimensionality in contemporary applications calls for a thorough (re)-examination of existing algorithms,
so as to ensure feasibility of computing optimal transport at scale.
For example, the linear-programming-based algorithm
Lee and Sidford, 2014
requires a runtime
\widetilde{O}(n^{2.5})
,
which takes much longer than the time needed to read the cost matrix
\bm{W}
.
In comparison, another alternative tailored to this problem, called Sinkhorn iteration

;
Cuturi, 2013

exploits the special structure underlying the solution to an entropy-regularized variant of (
1

This classical approach and its variants have been shown to be near linear-time

Main contributions.

This paper contributes to the above growing literature by proposing a scalable algorithm tailored to the optimal transport problem.
Our focus is first-order optimization-based methods — a family of practically appealing algorithms for large-scale optimization.
Our algorithm is built around several key ideas.

1)

We start with an
\ell_{1}
-penalized variant of the original problem, and reformulate it into a bilinear minimax problem over two sets of probability distributions.

2)

In an attempt to solve this minimax problem, we design a variant of entropy-regularized extragradient methods.
On a high level, the algorithm performs two mirror-descent-type updates per iteration, with learning rates chosen adaptively in accordance with the corresponding row or column marginals.

Encouragingly, the proposed entropy-regularized extragradient method is capable of achieving

\bm{w}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

2.1
Approximate transportation solution of a penalized variant

The equivalent formulation (
2
) motivates one to look at a related
\ell_{1}
-penalized problem:

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{minimize}}\limits_{\{\bm{p}_{i}\in\Delta_{n}\}_{i=1}^{n}}\qquad f_{\ell_{1}}(\{\bm{p}_{i}\})\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\langle\bm{w}_{i},\bm{p}_{i}\rangle+\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}\bigg\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\bm{p}_{i}-\bm{c}\bigg\|_{1},

(7)

a trick that has been inspired by ideas in prior optimization literature; see, e.g.,
Jambulapati et al., 2019
.
Evidently, if we are able to compute an
\varepsilon
-optimal solution
\{\widehat{\bm{p}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
to (
7
)
(in the sense that
f_{\ell_{1}}(\{\widehat{\bm{p}}_{i}\})\leq\min_{\{\bm{p}_{i}\in\Delta_{n}\}_{i=1}^{n}}f_{\ell_{1}}(\{\bm{p}_{i}\})
,
then one has

\displaystyle 0.5\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\langle\bm{w}_{i},\widehat{\bm{p}}_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 0.5\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\langle\bm{w}_{i},\widehat{\bm{p}}_{i}\rangle+\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}\Big\|r_{i}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{\bm{p}}_{i}-\bm{c}\Big\|_{1}

\displaystyle=f_{\ell_{1}}(\{\widehat{\bm{p}}_{i}\})\leq\min_{\{\bm{p}_{i}\in\Delta_{n}\}_{i=1}^{n}}f_{\ell_{1}}(\{\bm{p}_{i}\})+\varepsilon\leq f_{\ell_{1}}(\{\bm{p}^{\star}_{i}\})+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq 0.5\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\langle\bm{w}_{i},\bm{p}_{i}^{\star}\rangle+\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}\Big\|r_{i}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\bm{p}^{\star}_{i}-\bm{c}\Big\|_{1}+\varepsilon

\displaystyle=0.5\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\langle\bm{w}_{i},\bm{p}_{i}^{\star}\rangle+\varepsilon.

(8)

In general, however,
the solution to (
7
) does not satisfy the feasibility constraints of the optimal transport problem,
and one still needs to convert it into a feasible transportation plan.
This can be accomplished via the following result
(
Altschuler et al., 2017

.

Lemma 1
.

For any non-negative matrix
\widehat{\bm{P}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{+}
,
there exists a fast algorithm (see
(
Altschuler et al., 2017

)
that is able to find a probability matrix
\widetilde{\bm{P}}\in\Delta_{n\times n}

with

computation complexity such that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{P}}\bm{1}=\bm{r},\quad\widetilde{\bm{P}}^{\top}\bm{1}=\bm{c},\quad\text{and}\quad\|\widehat{\bm{P}}-\widetilde{\bm{P}}\|_{1}\leq 2\big(\|\widehat{\bm{P}}\bm{1}-\bm{r}\|_{1}+\|\widehat{\bm{P}}^{\top}\bm{1}-\bm{c}\|_{1}\big).

(9)

As a consequence, it boils down to finding a near-optimal solution
\{\widehat{\bm{p}}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
to (
7
) in a computationally efficient manner, while ensuring sufficiently small

\|\widehat{\bm{P}}\bm{1}-\bm{r}\|_{1}+\|\widehat{\bm{P}}^{\top}\bm{1}-\bm{c}\|_{1}

with
\widehat{\bm{P}}
defined as

\widehat{\bm{P}}=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}r_{1}\widehat{\bm{p}}_{1}^{\top}\\
\vdots\\
r_{n}\widehat{\bm{p}_{n}}^{\top}\end{array}\right].

Given that
\widehat{\bm{p}}_{i}^{\top}\bm{1}=1

2.2
Entropy-regularized extragradient methods

In this subsection, we propose a method for solving the
\ell_{1}
-penalized problem (
7
).
To streamline the presentation, we assume without loss of generality that
\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}=1
;
this can be implemented by running
\bm{W}\leftarrow\bm{W}/\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}
at the very beginning of the algorithm.

An equivalent minimax problem and entropy regularization.

The first step of our algorithm lies in converting the objective function of (
7
) into a bilinear function,
for which the key lies in handling the
\ell_{1}
penalty term.
Towards this end, we introduce a set of auxiliary 2-dimensional probability vectors
\bm{\mu}_{j}=[\mu_{j,+},\mu_{j,-}]\in\Delta_{2}

(1\leq j\leq n)
.
As can be easily verified,
this allows us to recast the objective of (
7
) as follows:

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\langle\bm{w}_{i},\bm{p}_{i}\rangle+\bigg\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\bm{p}_{i}-\bm{c}\bigg\|_{1}=\max_{\bm{\mu}_{j}\in\Delta_{2},\forall j}f\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big),

(10)

where we define

\displaystyle f\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\langle\bm{w}_{i},\bm{p}_{i}\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(\mu_{j,+}-\mu_{j,-}\big)\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}-c_{j}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\langle\bm{w}_{i},\bm{p}_{i}\rangle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\mu_{j,+}-\mu_{j,-})c_{j}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{i}(\mu_{j,+}-\mu_{j,-})p_{i,j}.

(11)

Armed with this function, one can recast (
7
) as the following minimax problem:

\mathop{\text{minimize}}_{\bm{p}_{i}\in\Delta_{n},\forall i}~\max_{\bm{\mu}_{j}\in\Delta_{2},\forall j}f\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big),

(12)

or equivalently (by virtue of von Neumann’s minimax theorem
von Neumann, 1928
),

\mathop{\text{maximize}}_{\bm{\mu}_{j}\in\Delta_{2},\forall j}\min_{\bm{p}_{i}\in\Delta_{n},\forall i}f\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big).

(13)

Given that the bilinear objective function is convex-concave but not strongly-convex-strongly-concave,
one strategy for accelerating the optimization procedure is to augment the objective function with entropy regularization terms.
This leads to the following entropy-regularized minimax problem:

\displaystyle\mathop{\text{maximize}}_{\bm{\mu}_{j}\in\Delta_{2},\forall j}\min_{\bm{p}_{i}\in\Delta_{n},\forall i}

\displaystyle F\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big)\coloneqq

\displaystyle f\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big)+\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{\mu,j}\mathcal{H}(\bm{\mu}_{j})-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{p,i}\mathcal{H}(\bm{p}_{i}),

(14)

where
\mathcal{H}(\cdot)
denotes the entropy (which is a strongly concave and non-negative function), and
\{\tau_{\mu,j}\}_{1\leq j\leq n}
and
\{\tau_{p,i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
are a set of
positive
regularization parameters that we shall specify momentarily.
The remainder of this subsection is dedicated to solving (
14
) in an efficient fashion.

An extragradient method for solving (
14
).

The family of extragradient methods has proven effective for solving convex-concave minimax problems
Korpelevich, 1976
;
Tseng, 1995
;
Harker and Pang, 1990
;
Mokhtari et al., 2020a
.
Inspired by a recent development

,
we propose to solve (
14

Let us begin by introducing a basic operation.
Suppose the current iterate is
\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}^{\mathsf{current}}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\mathsf{current}}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big)
.
One step of mirror descent (with the KL divergence chosen to monitor the displacement) takes the following form: for
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\mathsf{next}}

\displaystyle=\arg\max_{\bm{\mu}_{j}\in\Delta_{2}}\left\{\Big\langle\nabla_{\bm{\mu}_{j}}F\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}^{\mathsf{grad}}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\mathsf{grad}}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big),\,\bm{\mu}_{j}\Big\rangle-\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{\mu}_{j}\parallel\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\mathsf{current}}\big)\right\},

(15a)

\displaystyle\bm{p}_{i}^{\mathsf{next}}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\bm{p}_{i}\in\Delta_{n}}\left\{\Big\langle\nabla_{\bm{p}_{i}}F\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}^{\mathsf{grad}}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\mathsf{grad}}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big),\,\bm{p}_{i}\Big\rangle+\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}_{i}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{\mathsf{current}}\big)\right\},

(15b)

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\mu_{j,s}^{\mathsf{next}}

\displaystyle\propto\big(\mu_{j,s}^{\mathsf{current}}\big)^{1-\eta}\exp\bigg(\eta_{\mu,j}s\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{\mathsf{grad}}-c_{j}\Big)\bigg),\hskip 18.49988pt

\displaystyle s\in\{+,-\}

(16a)

\displaystyle\propto\big(p_{i,l}^{\mathsf{current}}\big)^{1-\eta}\exp\Big(-\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big(0.5w_{i,l}+\mu_{l,+}^{\mathsf{grad}}-\mu_{l,-}^{\mathsf{grad}}\big)\Big),\hskip 18.49988pt

\displaystyle l=1,\cdots,n

(16b)

for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
where

and

(

1)

Computing the midpoints:
for each

Choice of algorithmic parameters.

Thus far, we have not yet discussed the choices of multiple parameters required to run Algorithm

Let us begin by looking at the regularization parameters
\{\tau_{\mu,j}\}

\{\tau_{p,j}\}

Evidently, if the regularization parameters are chosen such that

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{\mu,j}\log 2+\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{p,i}\log n\leq\varepsilon,

(20)

then it follows from elementary properties of the entropy that: for any
\{\bm{p}_{i}\in\Delta_{n}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\in\Delta_{2}\}_{i=1}^{n}

\Big|F\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\}_{i=1}^{n}\big)-f\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\}_{i=1}^{n}\big)\Big|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{\mu,j}\log 2+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tau_{p,i}\log n\leq\varepsilon.

(21)

Consequently, any
\varepsilon

14
) — namely, a point whose resulting duality gap is no larger than
\varepsilon
— is an
2\varepsilon
-approximate solution to (
7
).
Moverover, the theory developed in
Cen et al., 2021
for matrix games suggests that a feasible learning rate can be chosen to be inversely proportional to the regularization parameter.
As a result, we take, for some quantity
\eta>0
,

\eta_{\mu,j}=\eta/\tau_{\mu,j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{p,i}=\eta/\tau_{p,i},\qquad\forall 1\leq i,j\leq n

(22)

With the above considerations in mind, we recommend the parameter choices:

\displaystyle B=C_{1}\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon},\qquad\eta=\frac{C_{2}^{2}\varepsilon}{\sqrt{B}\log n},\qquad\eta_{\mu,j}=\frac{15C_{2}\sqrt{B}}{c_{j}+C_{3}/n},\qquad\eta_{p,i}=\frac{C_{2}}{\sqrt{B}r_{i}},

(23)

for

some suitable universal constants, which correspond to

\displaystyle\tau_{\mu,j}=\frac{\eta}{\eta_{\mu,j}}=\frac{C_{2}(c_{j}+C_{3}/n)\varepsilon}{15C_{1}(\log n)\big(\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon}\big)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tau_{p,i}=\frac{\eta}{\eta_{p,i}}=\frac{C_{2}r_{i}\varepsilon}{\log n}

(24)

for all

(resp.
\eta_{\mu,j}

2.3
Theoretical guarantees

Our theoretical analysis delivers intriguing news about the convergence properties of the proposed algorithm, as stated below.

Theorem 1
.

Consider any
0<\varepsilon<\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}
. Algorithm
1
with the parameters (
23
) returns a probability matrix
\widetilde{\bm{P}}\in\Delta_{n\times n}
obeying

\displaystyle\widetilde{\bm{P}}\bm{1}=\bm{r},\qquad\widetilde{\bm{P}}^{\top}\bm{1}=\bm{c},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle\bm{W},\widetilde{\bm{P}}\rangle\leq\langle\bm{W},\bm{P}^{\star}\rangle+\varepsilon,

(26)

provided that
C_{1}>0
is large enough,
C_{2}>0
is small enough,
C_{2}\sqrt{C_{1}}
is large enough,
0<C_{3}\leq 1
,
C_{2}^{2}/C_{3}
is small enough, and

t_{\max}\geq C_{4}\frac{\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}}{\eta}\log\Big(\frac{n\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}}{\varepsilon}\Big)

(27)

for some large enough constant
C_{4}>0
, with
\eta
defined in (
23
).

Remark
1
.

For instance, our analysis reveals that this theorem holds under the following particular values of the constants:

C_{1}=124,C_{2}=0.024,C_{3}=1,C_{4}=2
. Caution needs to be exercised, however, that these values are derived primarily for theoretical purposes and could be overly conservative.

Assuming without loss of generality that
\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}=1
, Theorem
1
in conjunction with the choice of
\eta
in (
23
) asserts that the iteration complexity of our algorithm is

\text{(iteration complexity)}\qquad\widetilde{O}\big(1/\varepsilon\big).

(28)

Given that each iteration can be implemented in
O(n^{2})
time,
the total computational complexity of Algorithm
1
is no larger than

\displaystyle\text{(computation complexity)}\qquad\widetilde{O}\big(n^{2}/\varepsilon\big).

(29)

This matches the state-of-the-art theory
Jambulapati et al., 2019

among all first-order methods tailored to the optimal transport problem;
we will demonstrate the practical efficacy of our algorithm shortly.
Regarding the memory complexity, all computation in our algorithm only involves matrices of dimension no larger than
n\times n
.
Compared to
Blanchet et al., 2018
;
Quanrud, 2018
;
van den Brand et al., 2020
,
our algorithm is easy-to-implement and amenable to parallelism, without the need of calling any unimplementable blackbox subroutine that is mainly of theoretical interest.

Finally, while our analysis is inspired by the prior work
Cen et al., 2021
, a direct application of their analysis framework can only lead to highly suboptimal iteration complexity.
Particularly, they focus on the
\ell_{\infty}
type bound, which can ensure
\|\widehat{\bm{P}}^{\top}\bm{1}-\bm{c}\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
within
\widetilde{O}\big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\big)

However, we need
\|\widehat{\bm{P}}^{\top}\bm{1}-\bm{c}\|_{1}\leq\varepsilon

\widetilde{O}\big(\frac{n^{3}}{\varepsilon}\big)
computation complexity if naively adopting the
\ell_{\infty}
type bound in
Cen et al., 2021
.
Novel algorithmic and analysis ideas (e.g., how to exploit the use of adaptive learning rates) tailored to the optimal transport problem play a central role in establishing the desired performance guarantees.

3
Related works

Let us discuss a broader set of related past works.

Entropy regularization.

The advantages of entropy regularization have been exploited in a diverse array of optimization problems over probability distributions,
with prominent examples including equilibrium computation in game theory
Ao et al., 2023
;
McKelvey and Palfrey, 1995
;
Savas et al., 2019
;
Mertikopoulos and Sandholm, 2016
;
Cen et al., 2021
;
Cen et al., 2022a
and policy optimization in reinforcement learning
Geist et al., 2019
;
Neu et al., 2017
;
Mei et al., 2020
;
Cen et al., 2023
;
Cen et al., 2022b
;
Lan, 2022
;
Zhan et al., 2023
.
The idea of employing entropy regularization to speed up convergence in optimal transport
has been studied for multiple decades (e.g.,
Knight, 2008
;
Kalantari et al., 2008
;
Chakrabarty and Khanna, 2021
;
Altschuler, 2022
) and recently popularized by
Cuturi, 2013
.
By adding a reasonably small entropy penalty term (so that it does not bias the objective function by much),
the optimal solution to the entropy-regularized problem exhibits a special form
\bm{D}_{r}\exp(-\eta\bm{W})\bm{D}_{c}
, where
\bm{D}_{r}
and
\bm{D}_{c}
are certain diagonal matrices and the
\exp(\cdot)
operator is applied in an entrywise manner
Sinkhorn, 1967
.
This special structure motivates one to alternate between row and column rescaling until convergence, the key idea behind the Sinkhorn algorithm.
Note that the use of entropy regularization
links optimal transport with another fundamental problem called matrix scaling
Idel, 2016
;
Allen-Zhu et al., 2017
;
see
Altschuler, 2022
for an exposition of such connections.

Extragradient methods.

Dating back to
Korpelevich, 1976
;
Tseng, 1995
, extensive research efforts have been put forth towards understanding extragradient methods for saddle-point optimization, where a clever step of extrapolation is leveraged to accelerate convergence; partial examples include the optimistic gradient descent ascent (OGDA) method
Mertikopoulos et al., 2018b
;
Mertikopoulos et al., 2018a
;
Rakhlin and Sridharan, 2013
;
Wei et al., 2021
, the implicit update method
Liang and Stokes, 2019
, and their stochastic variants
Hsieh et al., 2019
.
Mokhtari et al., 2020b
analyzed the convergence of extragradient methods for unconstrained smooth convex-concave saddle-point problems under the Euclidean metric, with
Wei et al., 2021
focusing on constrained saddle-point problems. While earlier works analyzed primarily average-iterate or ergodic convergence
Nemirovski, 2004
, significant emphasis was put on achieving last-iterate convergence motivated by machine learning applications
Mertikopoulos et al., 2018b
;
Wei et al., 2021
. By using entropy regularization,
Cen et al., 2021
demonstrated fast last-iterate convergence of extragradient methods for matrix games, under weaker assumptions than those needed for solving the unregularized games directly
Daskalakis and Panageas, 2018
.

Prior algorithms for the optimal transport problem.

Earlier effort towards computing the optimal transport include the development of the Hungarian algorithm,
which is not a linear-time algorithm due to its complexity
\widetilde{O}(n^{3})

Kuhn, 1956
;
Munkres, 1957
;
this algorithm has recently been revisited by
Xie et al., 2022b
, which further came up with a variant that runs faster in a special class of problem instances.
In comparison, Sinkhorn iteration and its variants have achieved widespread adoption in practice since
Cuturi, 2013
.

Altschuler et al., 2017
developed the first theory uncovering the linear-time feature of Sinkhorn iteration with computational complexity
\widetilde{O}(n^{2}/\varepsilon^{3})
,
and inspired a recent strand of works (e.g.,
Dvurechensky et al., 2018
;
Feydy et al., 2019
) that strengthened the runtime for Sinkhorn-type algorithms to
\widetilde{O}(n^{2}/\varepsilon^{2})

(including a fast greedy variant called the Greenkhorn algorithm
Altschuler et al., 2017
;
Lin et al., 2022
).
Additionally, Sinkhorn and Greenkhorn iterations have been shown to converge linearly
to the solution of the entropy-regularized optimization transport problem
Carlier, 2022
;
Kostic et al., 2022
,
although their iteration complexity still depend upon the regularization parameter (which needs to be chosen based on
\varepsilon
). Another recent work
Ghosal and Nutz, 2022
derived sublinear convergence rates for Sinkhorn, where the iteration complexity depends polynomially in the regularization parameter.

First-order methods and their stochastic variants have received much recent attention,
including but not limited to accelerated gradient descent
Dvurechensky et al., 2018
, stochastic gradient descent (SGD)
Genevay et al., 2016
,
Nesterov’s smoothing
An et al., 2022
,
and accelerated primal-dual methods
Lin et al., 2022
;
Xie et al., 2022a
;
Chambolle and Contreras, 2022
.
The convergence guarantees of these algorithms remain suboptimal in terms of the dependency on either
n
or
\varepsilon
.
As mentioned previously, the algorithms designed by
Blanchet et al., 2018
;
Quanrud, 2018
, while achieving an appealing
\widetilde{O}(n^{2}/\varepsilon)
runtime,
rely heavily on reduction to blackbox methods developed in theoretical computer science (e.g., positive linear programming
Allen-Zhu and Orecchia, 2015
),
which hinder practical realization and do not yet admit fast parallelization.
Inspired by
Nesterov, 2007
;
Sherman, 2017
,
Jambulapati et al., 2019
leveraged the concept of area convexity when designing and analyzing the dual extrapolation algorithm,
which has become the best-performing (in theory) first-order method in the previous literature.
It is also worth noting that
Jambulapati et al., 2019
also reformulated the problem into a minimax form,
albeit using different constraint sets (for instance, the decision variables therein are not all probability vectors).
Another recent work
Mai et al., 2022
proposed an algorithm based on the Douglas-Rachford splitting,
which enjoys competitive numerical performance compared with Sinkhorn and is amenable to GPU acceleration.
While this algorithm has been shown to enjoy an iteration complexity of
O\big(1/(\rho\varepsilon)\big)
(with
\rho
some suitable penalty parameter)
and per-iteration cost
O(n^{2})
, the penalty parameter
\rho
is taken to be on the order of
1/n
(see
(
Mai et al., 2022
, Section 4)
),
resulting in a total computational complexity far exceeding
n^{2}/\varepsilon
.
Another recent work
Lahn et al., 2019
tackled this problem via combinatorial algorithms, yielding a runtime as fast as
\widetilde{O}(n^{2}/\varepsilon+n/\varepsilon^{2})
.

The dashed lines stand for the theoretical choices of parameters, while solid lines correspond to the practical choices.

As illustrated in Figure

The numerical results also hint at practical choices of algorithmic parameters.

•

For example, in the presence of a small

in addition, the solid lines show that within a reasonable range, reducing regularization tends to result in slower convergence but also a smaller error floor after convergence, just as expected.

•

With regards to our extragradient method, the theoretical choices of parameters also tend to be too conservative, while the fine-tuned parameters work substantially better, with the aggressive choices of
\eta=0

C

Although these fine-tuned parameters were chosen based on early experiments in one or two settings, it turns out that they work uniformly well under various settings; therefore, we will mostly focus on these fine-tuned parameters in the remaining numerical results.
The superior performance under such choices might merit further theoretical studies.

Moreover, the careful reader might remark that the numerical curves of the proposed extragradient methods exhibit non-monotonicity;
such an oscillation behavior is common in extragradient-type methods, as they are, in general, not descent methods.

We make a few more comments.
Our extragradient method converges fast (almost linearly in early iterations) in the “Synthetic” and “MNIST” settings, while at a sublinear rate in the “Point clouds” setting.
This is likely because, in the latter case, the optimal transport plan typically lies on or close to the boundary of the polytope, where the optimization landscape is less well-conditioned and thus results in slower convergence.
Another interesting observation is that the adjustment step (cf. (
18c

This can be seen from Figure

These results showcase that the adjustment step can help avoid getting stuck at undesirable points, thus accelerating convergence.

Analysis

This section presents the proof for our main result: Theorem
1
.

5.1
Preliminary facts and additional notation

Before proceeding, let us collect several elementary facts concerning the “adjusting” step in our algorithm.
Consider any probability vector
\bm{q}=[q_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq d}\in\Delta_{d}
, and transform it into another probability vector
\bm{q}^{\mathsf{adjust}}=[q_{i}^{\mathsf{adjust}}]_{1\leq i\leq d}\in\Delta_{d}
via the following two steps:

\displaystyle q_{i}^{\mathsf{adjust}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\max\big\{q_{i},\,e^{-B}\|\bm{q}\|_{\infty}\big\}}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\max\big\{q_{j},\,e^{-B}\|\bm{q}\|_{\infty}\big\}},\qquad i=1,\cdots,d.

(30)

We first make note of basic property of this transformation:

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq i\leq d}\frac{q_{i}}{q_{i}^{\mathsf{adjust}}}\leq 1+de^{-B}.

(31)

In addition, consider the special two-dimensional case where
\bm{q}=[q_{+},q_{-}]^{\top}\in\Delta_{2}
and let
\bm{q}^{\mathsf{adjust}}=[q_{+}^{\mathsf{adjust}},q_{-}^{\mathsf{adjust}}]^{\top}\in\Delta_{2}
.
If
B>0
, then it holds that

\frac{q_{+}^{\mathsf{adjust}}}{q_{-}^{\mathsf{adjust}}}=\begin{cases}\min\big\{\frac{q_{+}}{q_{-}},e^{B}\big\}\leq e^{B},&\text{if }q_{+}\geq q_{-},\\
\max\big\{\frac{q_{+}}{q_{-}},e^{-B}\big\}\geq e^{-B},&\text{if }q_{+}<q_{-}.\end{cases}

(32)

The proof of (
31
) and (
32
) are straightforward and hence omitted here for brevity.

Moreover, we would also like to introduce several additional convenient notation that is useful for presenting the proof.
Define

\displaystyle\bm{\zeta}^{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg(\Big\{\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\bm{p}_{i}^{t}\Big\}_{i=1}^{n},\Big\{\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t}\Big\}_{j=1}^{n}\bigg),

(33a)

\displaystyle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg(\Big\{\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\overline{\bm{p}}_{i}^{t}\Big\}_{i=1}^{n},\Big\{\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{t}\Big\}_{j=1}^{n}\bigg),

(33b)

\displaystyle\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg(\Big\{\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\bm{p}_{i}^{t}\Big\}_{i=1}^{n},\Big\{\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\Big\}_{j=1}^{n}\bigg),

(33c)

\displaystyle\bm{\zeta}^{\star}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\bigg(\Big\{\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\Big\}_{i=1}^{n},\Big\{\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\Big\}_{j=1}^{n}\bigg),

(33d)

where
\big\{\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\big\}_{i=1}^{n},\big\{\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\big\}_{j=1}^{n}
denotes the optimizer of the entropy-regularized minimax problem (
14
).
Additionally, for any
\bm{\zeta}^{(1)}=\big(\{\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\bm{p}_{i}^{(1)}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\bm{\mu}_{j}^{(1)}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big)

and
\bm{\zeta}^{(2)}=\big(\{\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\bm{p}_{i}^{(2)}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\bm{\mu}_{j}^{(2)}\}_{j=1}^{n}\big)

(with the
\bm{p}_{i}^{(1)}
’s,
\bm{p}_{i}^{(2)}
’s,
\bm{\mu}_{i}^{(1)}
’s and
\bm{\mu}_{i}^{(2)}
’s being probability vectors), we introduce a weighted KL divergence metric:

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{(1)}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{(2)}\big)\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}^{(1)}_{i}\parallel\bm{p}^{(2)}_{i}\big)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{\mu}^{(1)}_{j}\parallel\bm{\mu}^{(2)}_{j}\big).

(34)

5.2
Proof of Theorem
1

We are now positioned to prove Theorem
1
.
To begin with, we make the observation that

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}\mu_{j,s}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\log\frac{\mu_{j,s}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}}{\mu_{j,s}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}\mu_{j,s}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\log\frac{\mu_{j,s}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}}{\mu_{j,s}^{t}}+\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}\mu_{j,s}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\log\frac{\mu_{j,s}^{t}}{\mu_{j,s}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{KL}(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t})+\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}\mu_{j,s}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\log\max\left\{\frac{\mu_{j,+}^{t}}{\mu_{j,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}},\frac{\mu_{j,-}^{t}}{\mu_{j,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{KL}(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t})+\log\big(1+2e^{-B}\big),

where the last inequality arises from (
31
) and the fact that
\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}\mu_{j,s}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}=1
.
As a result, combine the above inequality with the definitions (
33c
) and (
33a
) to reach

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{t}\big)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{t}\big)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t}\big)+\log\big(1+2e^{-B}\big)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 9.24994pt=\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t}\big)+\log\big(1+2e^{-B}\big)\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{c_{j}+C_{3}/n}{15C_{2}\sqrt{B}}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 9.24994pt=\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t}\big)+\frac{1+C_{3}}{15C_{2}\sqrt{C_{1}\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon}}}\log\big(1+2e^{-B}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 9.24994pt\leq\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t}\big)+e^{-B},

(35)

where the third line results from the choice (
23
),
the penultimate line relies on
\sum_{j}c_{j}=1
, and the last line makes use of
\log(1+x)\leq x
for all
x\geq 0
and holds if
C_{2}\sqrt{C_{1}}
is sufficiently large (recall that
C_{3}\leq 1
).
In a nutshell, (
35
) indicates that the KL divergence between the optimal point and the
t
-th iterate is not increased by much when
\bm{\zeta}^{t}
is replaced with
\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}
.

The next step consists of establishing the following result that monitors the change of KL divergence when
\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}
is further replaced with
\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big)\leq(1-\eta)\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)+8ne^{-B},\hskip 18.49988pt\forall t\geq 0.

(36)

Crucially, a contraction factor of
1-\eta
appears in the above claim, revealing the progress made per iteration.
Suppose for the moment that this claim (
36
) is valid. Then taking it together with (
35
) gives

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big)\leq(1-\eta)\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t}\big)+(8n+1)e^{-B},\hskip 18.49988pt\forall t\geq 0.

(37)

Applying this relation recursively further implies that

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t_{\max}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq(1-\eta)^{t_{\max}}\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{0}\big)+(8n+1)e^{-B}\sum_{t=0}^{t_{\max}-1}(1-\eta)^{t}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\eta)^{t_{\max}}\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{0}\big)+\frac{(8n+1)e^{-B}}{\eta}.

(38)

Regarding the first term in (
38
), it is observed from our initialization (cf. line
1
of Algorithm
1
) that

\begin{cases}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{0}\big)&=-\mathcal{H}\big(\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\big)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{i,j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\log\frac{1}{p_{i,j}^{0}}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{i,j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\log n=\log n,\\
\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{\mu}_{j}^{0}\big)&=-\mathcal{H}\big(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\big)+\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}\mu_{j,s}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\log\frac{1}{\mu_{j,s}^{0}}\leq\sum_{s\in\{+,-\}}\mu_{j,s}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\log 2=\log 2,\end{cases}

and consequently,

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{0}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\log n}{\eta_{p,i}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\log 2}{\eta_{\mu,j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{B}\log n}{C_{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}+\frac{\log 2}{15C_{2}\sqrt{B}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(c_{j}+\frac{C_{3}}{n}\Big)\leq\frac{2\sqrt{B}\log n}{C_{2}},

where we recall that
0<C_{3}\leq 1
.
This in turn leads to
(1-\eta)^{t_{\max}}\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{0}\big)\leq\varepsilon^{2}
, provided that
t_{\max}\geq C_{4}\frac{1}{\eta}\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon}
for some large enough constant
C_{4}>0
.
Turning to the second term in (
38
), one utilizes
B=C_{1}\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon}
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(8n+1)e^{-B}}{\eta}

\displaystyle=\frac{(8n+1)e^{-B}\sqrt{B}\log n}{C_{2}^{2}\varepsilon}\leq\frac{(8n+1)e^{-\frac{1}{2}B}\log n}{C_{2}^{2}\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\frac{(8n+1)e^{-\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon}}\log n}{C_{2}^{2}\varepsilon}\leq\varepsilon^{2},

with the proviso that
C_{1}>0
is sufficiently large. Substitution into (
38
) thus yields

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t_{\max}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}=2\varepsilon^{2}.

(39)

As it turns out, it is more convenient to work with the
\ell_{1}
-based error.
To convert the above bound on KL divergence into
\ell_{1}
-based distance, one invokes Pinsker’s inequality
(
Tsybakov, 2009
, Lemma 2.5)
to reach

\displaystyle\big\|\widehat{\bm{P}}-\bm{P}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\big\|_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big\|\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}-\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,t_{\max}}\big\|_{1}\leq\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\sqrt{2\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{t_{\max}}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\{2\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{t_{\max}}\big)\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\{2\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{t_{\max}}\big)\right\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\frac{2C_{2}}{\sqrt{B}}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{t_{\max}}\big)\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\{\frac{2C_{2}}{\sqrt{C_{1}\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon}}}\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t_{\max}}\big)\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{6}.

(40)

Here, the first inequality invokes Pinsker’s inequality, the second inequality results from Cauchy-Schwarz,
the third line holds since
\sum_{i}r_{i}=1
, the fourth line uses the choice (
22
) of
\eta_{p,i}
,
whereas the last line results from the definition (
34
) and the bound (
39
) and is valid as long as
C_{2}
(resp.
C_{1}
) is sufficiently small (resp. large).

Thus far, we have demonstrated fast convergence of our algorithm to the solution to the entropy-regularized problem (
14

To finish up, we still need to show the proximity of the objective values under the regularized solution
\bm{P}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}

For notational convenience, define

\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{\star}\coloneqq\arg\max_{\bm{\mu}_{j}\in\Delta_{2}}f\big(\{\bm{p}_{i}^{\star,\mathsf{reg}}\}_{i=1}^{n},\{\bm{\mu}_{j}\}_{i=1}^{n}\big),\qquad 1\leq j\leq n.

5.3.1
Step 1: decomposing the KL divergence of interest

Elementary calculation together with the definition (

reveals that

\displaystyle(1-\eta)\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)-(1-\eta)\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)-\eta\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\big)-\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\parallel\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\bm{\zeta}^{t+1},\,\log\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big\rangle+\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\bm{\zeta}^{\star},\,\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}-(1-\eta)\log\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}-\eta\log\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)\big\langle\bm{\zeta}^{\star},\,\log\bm{\zeta}^{\star}-\log\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big\rangle-(1-\eta)\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1},\,\log\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\log\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt-\eta\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1},\,\log\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\log\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\big\rangle-\big\langle\bm{\zeta}^{t+1},\,\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}-\log\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}\big\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt+\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\bm{\zeta}^{t+1},\,\log\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big\rangle+\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\bm{\zeta}^{\star},\,\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}-(1-\eta)\log\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}-\eta\log\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\big\langle\bm{\zeta}^{\star},\,\log\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\big\rangle-\big\langle\bm{\zeta}^{\star},\,\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big\rangle=\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big);

(43)

here and throughout, the logarithmic operator in
\log\bm{\zeta}
is applied in an entrywise manner.
In addition, inspired by
(
Cen et al., 2021
, Lemma 1)
, we observe that

\displaystyle\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\bm{\zeta}^{\star},\,\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}-(1-\eta)\log\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}-\eta\log\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\big\rangle=0;

(44)

see Appendix
B
for the proof of this relation.
Substitution into (
43
) leads to

\displaystyle\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big)

\displaystyle=(1-\eta)\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)-(1-\eta)\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)-\eta\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{\star}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt-\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\parallel\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}\big)+\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\bm{\zeta}^{t+1},\,\log\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big\rangle.

(45)

It then boils down to controlling the term

\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\bm{\zeta}^{t+1},\,\log\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big\rangle
.

Towards this end,
we first invoke the update rules (
17
) and (
18
) to yield

\displaystyle\big\langle\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\bm{\zeta}^{t+1},\,\log\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}-\log\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}+\mu_{j,-}^{t+1}\big)\Big\{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(p_{i,j}^{t}-\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)\Big\}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}+\mu_{j,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)\Big\{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}-p_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)\Big\},

(46)

whose proof is also deferred to Appendix
B
.
To bound the two sums on the right-hand side of (
46
),
we find it convenient to first introduce the following index subset:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{t}\coloneqq\bigg\{j:\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}<2e\Big(c_{j}+\frac{1}{n}\Big)\bigg\}.

(47)

We then divide each sum into two parts —
\big\{j:j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}\big\}
and
\big\{j:j\notin\mathcal{J}_{t}\big\}
— and look at them separately.

5.3.2
Step 2: controlling terms with
j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}

We start by looking at those terms with
j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}
.
Regarding the first sum on the right-hand side of (
46
),
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}+\mu_{j,-}^{t+1}\big)\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(p_{i,j}^{t}-\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\frac{1}{2\eta_{\mu,j}}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}+\mu_{j,-}^{t+1}\big)^{2}+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\frac{\eta_{\mu,j}}{2}\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(p_{i,j}^{t}-\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)\bigg\}^{2}.

(48)

Recognizing that
\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{t+1},\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t+1}\in\Delta_{2}
,
one can invoke Pinsker’s inequality
(
Tsybakov, 2009
, Lemma 2.5)
to bound the first term on the right-hand side of (
48
) as follows:

\displaystyle\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}+\mu_{j,-}^{t+1}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\big\|\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{t+1}-\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t+1}\big\|_{1}^{2}\leq 2\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t+1}\parallel\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{t+1}\big).

(49)

Here, the first identity holds since, according to the properties that
\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{t+1},\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t+1}\in\Delta_{2}
,

\displaystyle\Big|\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}+\mu_{j,-}^{t+1}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}+\big(1-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}\big)-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}-\big(1-\mu_{j,-}^{t+1}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|2\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-2\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}\Big|=\Big|\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}\Big|+\Big|1-\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-1+\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}\Big|+\Big|\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,-}^{t+1}\Big|=\big\|\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{t+1}-\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t+1}\big\|_{1}.

Regarding the second term on the right-hand side of (
48
), we see that, for
\eta<1/2
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(1-\eta)\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\overline{\bm{p}}_{i}^{t+1}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{t}\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}}{\eta_{p,i}}\log\frac{\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}}{p_{i,j}^{t}}

(50)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\geq\frac{1}{2\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}\big((1+x_{ij})\log(1+x_{i,j})\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\overset{\mathrm{(i)}}{=}\frac{1}{2\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}\big((1+x_{ij})\log(1+x_{i,j})-x_{i,j}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\overset{\mathrm{(ii)}}{\geq}\frac{3}{4\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}}\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(p_{i,j}^{t}-\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)\bigg\}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\overset{\mathrm{(iii)}}{\geq}\frac{1}{20e\big(\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big)\big(\max_{j}\eta_{\mu,j}(c_{j}+1/n)\big)}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\eta_{\mu,j}\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(p_{i,j}^{t}-\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)\bigg\}^{2},

(51)

where we define
x_{i,j}\coloneqq\frac{\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}}{p_{i,j}^{t}}-1
.
Here, (i) is valid due to
\sum_{j}p_{i,j}^{t}x_{i,j}=\sum_{j}(\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t}-p_{i,j}^{t})=0
;
(ii) arises since

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}\big((1+x_{i,j})\log(1+x_{i,j})-x_{i,j}\big)\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}\frac{x_{i,j}^{2}}{1+x_{i,j}/3}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t})^{2}x_{i,j}^{2}}{r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}(1+x_{i,j}/3)}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}(1+x_{i,j}/3)}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}x_{i,j}\Big)^{2}=\frac{3}{4\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}-p_{i,j}^{t}\big)\Big)^{2},

where the first inequality holds since
(1+z)\log(1+z)-z\geq\frac{z^{2}}{2(1+z/3)}
for all
z\geq-1
,
and the second line follows from Sedrakyan’s inequality
(
Sedrakyan and Sedrakyan, 2018
, Chapter 8, Lemma 1)
;
(iii) comes from the definition (
47
) of
\mathcal{J}_{t}
, as well as the following claim (which will be proved momentarily):

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}<5e\big(c_{j}+1/n\big)\hskip 18.49988pt\text{and}\hskip 18.49988pt\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t+1}<5e\big(c_{j}+1/n\big),\hskip 9.24994pt\forall j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}.

(52)

Substituting (
49
) and (
51
) into (
48
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}+\mu_{j,-}^{t+1}\big)\bigg\{\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(p_{i,j}^{t}-\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)\bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t+1}\parallel\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{t+1}\big)+(1-\eta)40e\Big(\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\Big)\Big(\max_{j}\eta_{\mu,j}(c_{j}+1/n)\Big)\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\overline{\bm{p}}_{i}^{t+1}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{t}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\frac{1}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t+1}\parallel\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{t+1}\big)+(1-\eta)\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\overline{\bm{p}}_{i}^{t+1}\parallel\bm{p}_{i}^{t}\big),

(53)

provided that (using the choice (
23
) and the assumption
0<C_{3}\leq 1
)

\displaystyle\Big(\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\Big)\Big(\max_{j}\eta_{\mu,j}(c_{j}+1/n)\Big)\leq\frac{1}{C_{3}}\Big(\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\Big)\Big(\max_{j}\eta_{\mu,j}(c_{j}+C_{3}/n)\Big)=\frac{15C_{2}^{2}}{C_{3}}\leq\frac{1}{40e}.

(54)

We then move on to the second sum on the right-hand side of (
46
) when restricted to
\mathcal{J}_{t}
.
Repeating the arguments as above, we can guarantee that

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}+\mu_{j,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}-p_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\leq\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\frac{1-\eta}{2\eta_{\mu,j}}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}+\mu_{j,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)^{2}+\frac{1}{2(1-\eta)}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\eta_{\mu,j}\bigg\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}-p_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)\bigg\}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\leq\sum_{j}\frac{1-\eta}{\eta_{\mu,j}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\overline{\bm{\mu}}_{j}^{t+1}\parallel\bm{\mu}_{j}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)+\sum_{i}\frac{1}{\eta_{p,i}}\mathsf{KL}\big(\bm{p}_{i}^{t+1}\parallel\overline{\bm{p}}_{i}^{t+1}\big),

as long as
0<\eta<1/2
(so that
\frac{1}{1-\eta}\leq 2
) and
Condition (
54
) is met; the details are omitted here for brevity.
Combining this result with (
53
), we reach

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t+1}+\mu_{j,-}^{t+1}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(p_{i,j}^{t}-\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)+\sum_{j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}}\big(\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1}-\mu_{j,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}+\mu_{j,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\big(\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}-p_{i,j}^{t+1}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\leq(1-\eta)\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}\parallel\bm{\zeta}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)+\mathsf{KL}_{\mathsf{gen}}\big(\bm{\zeta}^{t+1}\parallel\overline{\bm{\zeta}}^{t+1}\big).

(55)

Proof of Claim (
52
)

The analyses for
\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}
and for
p_{i,j}^{t+1}
are essentially the same;
we shall thus only present how to establish the first inequality in (
52
) for the sake of brevity.
According to the update rule (
17
),

\displaystyle\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(p_{i,j}^{t}\big)^{1-\eta}\exp\Big(-\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big(0.5w_{i,j}+\mu_{j,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}-\mu_{j,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)\Big)}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(p_{i,k}^{t}\big)^{1-\eta}\exp\Big(-\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big(0.5w_{i,k}+\mu_{k,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}-\mu_{k,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)\Big)}.

(56)

Given that
\|\bm{W}\|_{\infty}=1
,
|\mu_{j,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}-\mu_{j,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}|\leq 1
and
0<\eta<1
, we can bound

\displaystyle\big(p_{i,j}^{t}\big)^{1-\eta}\exp\Big(-\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big(0.5w_{i,j}+\mu_{j,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}-\mu_{j,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)\Big)\geq p_{i,j}^{t}\exp\big(-1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big)

(57a)

and

\displaystyle\big(p_{i,j}^{t}\big)^{1-\eta}\exp\Big(-\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big(0.5w_{i,j}+\mu_{j,+}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}-\mu_{j,-}^{t,\mathsf{adjust}}\big)\Big)\leq\big(p_{i,j}^{t}\big)^{1-\eta}\exp\big(1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i})

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\leq\left\{p_{i,j}^{t}e^{\eta B}\ind\{p_{i,j}^{t}>e^{-B}\}+e^{-(1-\eta)B}\ind\{p_{i,j}^{t}\leq e^{-B}\}\right\}\exp\big(1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i})

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\leq p_{i,j}^{t}\exp\big(1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\big)+\exp\big(1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}-(1-\eta)B\big)

(57b)

for every
1\leq i,j\leq n
.
Substituting these two bounds into (
56
) leads to

\displaystyle\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p_{i,j}^{t}\exp\big(1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\big)+\exp\big(-(1-\eta)B+1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big)}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{i,k}^{t}\exp\big(-1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big)}

\displaystyle=\big(p_{i,j}^{t}+e^{-B}\big)\exp\big(3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\big)\leq 2\big(p_{i,j}^{t}+e^{-B}\big)

(58)

for every
1\leq i,j\leq n
, with the proviso that
3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\leq\log 2
(which is satisfied under the choice (
23
) if
C_{2}
is small enough).
Therefore,
for any
j\in\mathcal{J}_{t}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1}\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}e^{-B}<4e\Big(c_{j}+\frac{1}{n}\Big)+2e^{-B}<5e\Big(c_{j}+\frac{1}{n}\Big),

(59)

where we make use of the definition (
47
) of
\mathcal{J}_{t}
as well as the fact
\sum_{i}r_{i}=1
, provided that
C_{1}
is large enough.

5.3.3
Step 3: controlling terms with
j\notin\mathcal{J}_{t}

We now move on to the terms with
j\notin\mathcal{J}_{t}
.
Employing similar analysis as for (
58
), we derive

\displaystyle p_{i,j}^{t+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(p_{i,j}^{t}\big)^{1-\eta}\exp\big(-\eta_{p,i}r_{i}(0.5w_{i,j}+\overline{\mu}_{j,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{j,-}^{t+1})\big)}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big(p_{i,k}^{t}\big)^{1-\eta}\exp\big(-\eta_{p,i}r_{i}(0.5w_{i,k}+\overline{\mu}_{k,+}^{t+1}-\overline{\mu}_{k,-}^{t+1})\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\big(p_{i,j}^{t}+e^{-B}\big)\exp\big(3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\big)

(60)

for any
1\leq i,j\leq n
, which in turn implies that

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}

\displaystyle\geq\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\left\{\big[\exp\big(3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\big)\big]^{-1}p_{i,j}^{t+1}-e^{-B}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}\Big[\exp\Big(\max_{i}3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\Big)\Big]^{-1}p_{i,j}^{t+1}-\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}e^{-B}

\displaystyle=\Big[\exp\Big(\max_{i}3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\Big)\Big]^{-1}\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t+1}-e^{-B}.

Hence, for any
j\notin\mathcal{J}_{t}
and any integer
0<\tau<\frac{1}{3\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B}
, applying the above relation recursively yields

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t-\tau}

\displaystyle\geq\Big[\exp\Big(\max_{i}3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\Big)\Big]^{-\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}-e^{-B}\sum_{k=0}^{\tau-1}\Big[\exp\Big(\max_{i}3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\Big)\Big]^{-k}

\displaystyle>\Big[\exp\Big(\max_{i}3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\Big)\Big]^{-\tau}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}-\frac{e^{-B}}{1-\big[\exp\big(\max_{i}3\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\big)\big]^{-1}}

(61)

\displaystyle>e^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}-\frac{e^{-B}}{1-e^{-\eta B}}=e^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}-\frac{e^{-C_{1}\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon}}}{1-e^{-\frac{C_{2}^{2}\varepsilon\sqrt{C_{1}\log\frac{n}{\varepsilon}}}{\log n}}}

\displaystyle>e^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t}-\frac{0.5}{n}\geq 2c_{j}+1.5/n,

(62)

where the penultimate line relies on the condition
\tau<\frac{1}{3\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B}
,
and the last line uses the definition of
\mathcal{J}_{t}
in (
47
) and holds as long as
C_{1}
is large enough.

This lower bound (
62
) already leads to one important observation.
For any
0\leq t<\frac{1}{3\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B}
, taking
\tau=t
leads to

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{0}=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{t-\tau}>2c_{j}+1.5/n,

(63)

which contradicts our initialization

\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}p_{i,j}^{0}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_{i}=\frac{1}{n}<2c_{j}+\frac{1.5}{n}.

This essentially implies that

\big\{j:j\notin\mathcal{J}_{t}\big\}=\emptyset,\hskip 18.49988pt\text{for all }0\leq t<\frac{1}{3\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B}.

(64)

Thus, it suffices to focus on
t\geq\frac{1}{3\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B}
when analyzing the sum over
j\notin\mathcal{J}_{t}
.

Next, recalling the bounds (
57
), one can derive, for every
1\leq i,j\leq n
,

\displaystyle\overline{p}_{i,j}^{t+1-\tau}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p_{i,j}^{t-\tau}\exp\big(-1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big)}{\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{i,k}^{t-\tau}\exp\big(1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\big)+\exp\big(-(1-\eta)B+1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{i,j}^{t-\tau}\exp\big(-1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big)}{\exp\big(1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\big)+\exp\big(-(1-\eta)B+1.5\eta_{p,i}r_{i}\big)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\big[\exp\big(3\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+\eta B\big)\big]^{-1}}{1+e^{-B}}p_{i,j}^{t-\tau}.

We then further derive: for any
j\notin\mathcal{J}_{t}
and any integer
0<\tau<\frac{1}{6\max_{i}\eta_{p,i}r_{i}+2\eta B}

6
Discussion

In this paper, we have put forward a first-order method for computing the optimal transport at scale,
which has been shown to enjoy both intriguing convergence guarantees and favorable numerical performance.
This is a step we have taken towards closing the theory-practice gap for solving this problem.
Moving forward, there are several natural research directions to explore.
To begin with, while the state-of-the-art theory
van den Brand et al., 2020
demonstrated the feasibility of a runtime
O(n^{2}\log^{2}(1/\varepsilon))
,
the practical value of the algorithm proposed therein remains unrealized; it would be of great importance to design algorithms that are optimal in theory and practice at once.
Next, the current algorithm still involves several hyper-parameters to tune,
and it would be of interest to develop improved versions that are nearly parameter-free.
Also, there is no shortage of applications where the problems exhibit certain low-dimensional structure
(e.g.,
Altschuler et al., 2019
),
which could be potentially leveraged to achieve further computational savings. Another natural problem to explore is whether we can solve a more general family of linear programs — e.g., the ones taking the form

\text{minimize}_{\bm{x}\in\Delta_{n}}f(\bm{x})+\|\bm{A}\bm{x}-\bm{b}\|_{1}
— using the entropy-regularized extragradient method developed herein.
We leave these for future investigation.

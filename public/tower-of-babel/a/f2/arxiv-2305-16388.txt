Bremsstrahlung-induced Gravitational Waves in Monomial Potentials during Reheating
and Óscar Zapata

Abstract
We discuss the production of primordial gravitational waves (GW) from radiative inflaton decay during the period of reheating, assuming perturbative decay of the inflaton either into a pair of bosons or fermions, leading to successful reheating satisfying constraint from Big Bang nucleosynthesis. Assuming that the inflaton ϕ \phi oscillates in a general monomial potential V ⁡ ( ϕ ) ∝ ϕ n V(\phi)\propto\phi^{n} , which results in a time-dependent inflaton decay width, we show that the resulting stochastic GW background can have optimistic detection prospects, especially in detectors that search for a high-frequency GW spectrum, depending on the choice of n n that determines the shape of the potential during reheating. We also discuss how this GW energy density may affect the measurement of Δ ​ N eff \Delta N_{\text{eff}} for bosonic and fermionic reheating scenarios.

2
The Setup

Here, we briefly discuss the underlying interaction Lagrangian that gives rise to the relevant decay widths for the inflaton. The particle physics framework of the present analysis is entirely based on the model in Ref.
[
6
]
. For two-body decay processes, we consider trilinear interactions between the inflaton
\phi
and a pair of complex scalar doublets
\varphi
(e.g. the Higgs boson doublet) or a pair of vector-like Dirac fermions
\Psi
. The corresponding Lagrangian density reads

\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}\supset-\mu\,\phi\,|\varphi|^{2}-y_{\psi}\,\overline{\Psi}\,\Psi\,\phi\,.

(2.1)

The interaction strengths are parameterized in terms of the couplings
\mu
and
y_{\psi}
, respectively. Note that
\mu
is a dimensionful coupling. On the other hand, when expanding the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
around a flat spacetime:
g_{\mu\nu}\simeq\eta_{\mu\nu}+\frac{2}{M_{P}}\,h_{\mu\nu}
, we end up with gravitational bremsstrahlung processes involving massless graviton final states via the interaction of the form
[
34
]

\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}^{(g)}\supset-\frac{2}{M_{P}}\,h_{\mu\nu}\,T^{\mu\nu},

(2.2)

where
h_{\mu\nu}
refers to the graviton field that appears as a quantum fluctuation on the flat background, and
T_{\mu\nu}
represents the energy-momentum tensor that involves matter particles in the theory. Here
M_{P}
is the reduced Planck mass. The interactions in Eqs. (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) give rise to 2- and 3-body decays of the inflaton into pairs of
\varphi
and
\Psi
, along with the radiative emission of a massless graviton. The 3-body decay processes are of present interest, as they are the source of the stochastic GW background. Full expressions of the differential 3-body decay rates are reported in Appendix
A
.

3
Reheating Dynamics

In this section, we closely follow Ref.
[
31
]
and revisit the details of reheating through bosonic and fermionic decays, considering the evolution of the energy densities of the inflaton and radiation. We consider the post-inflationary oscillation of the inflaton
\phi
at the bottom of a monomial potential
V(\phi)
of the form

V(\phi)=\lambda\,\frac{\phi^{n}}{\Lambda^{n-4}}\,,

(3.1)

where
\lambda
is a dimensionless coupling and
\Lambda
an energy scale. Now, the equation of motion for the oscillating inflaton field reads
[
35
]

\ddot{\phi}+(3\,H+\Gamma_{\phi})\,\dot{\phi}+V^{\prime}(\phi)=0\,,

(3.2)

where
H
denotes the Hubble expansion rate,
\Gamma_{\phi}
the inflaton decay rate, dots
(\dot{\phantom{.}})
derivatives with respect to time, and primes (
′
) derivatives with respect to the field.
Defining the energy density and pressure of
\phi
as
\rho_{\phi}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\,\dot{\phi}^{2}+V(\phi)
and
p_{\phi}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\,\dot{\phi}^{2}-V(\phi)
, together with the EoS parameter
w\equiv p_{\phi}/\rho_{\phi}=(n-2)/(n+2)

[
35
]
, one can write the evolution of the inflaton energy density as

\frac{d\rho_{\phi}}{dt}+\frac{6\,n}{2+n}\,H\,\rho_{\phi}=-\frac{2\,n}{2+n}\,\Gamma_{\phi}\,\rho_{\phi}\,.

(3.3)

During reheating
a_{I}\ll a\ll a_{\text{rh}}
, where
a
is the scale factor, the term associated with expansion, that is,
H\,\rho_{\phi}
typically dominates over the interaction term
\Gamma_{\phi}\,\rho_{\phi}
. Then it is possible to solve Eq. (
3.3
) analytically, leading to

\rho_{\phi}(a)\simeq\rho_{\phi}(a_{\text{rh}})\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{6\,n}{2+n}}.

(3.4)

Here,
a_{I}
and
a_{\text{rh}}
correspond to the scale factor at the end of inflation and at the end of reheating, respectively. Since the Hubble rate during reheating is dominated by the inflaton energy density, it follows that

H(a)\simeq H(a_{\text{rh}})\times\begin{cases}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{3\,n}{n+2}}&\text{ for }a\leq a_{\text{rh}}\,,\\
\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{2}&\text{ for }a_{\text{rh}}\leq a\,.\end{cases}

(3.5)

At the end of the reheating (that is, at
a=a_{\text{rh}}
), the energy densities of the inflaton and radiation are equal,
\rho_{R}(a_{\text{rh}})=\rho_{\phi}(a_{\text{rh}})=3\,M_{P}^{2}\,H(a_{\text{rh}})^{2}
.
Note that to avoid affecting the success of BBN, the reheating temperature
T_{\text{rh}}
must satisfy
T_{\text{rh}}>T_{\text{BBN}}\simeq 4
MeV
[
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
,
41
]
.
The evolution of the SM radiation energy density
\rho_{R}
, on the other hand, is governed by the Boltzmann equation of the form
[
27
]

\frac{d\rho_{R}}{dt}+4\,H\,\rho_{R}=+\frac{2\,n}{2+n}\,\Gamma_{\phi}\,\rho_{\phi}\,,

(3.6)

where it is implicitly assumed that during reheating the inflaton energy density is transferred to the radiation energy density. Using Eq. (
3.4
), one can solve Eq. (
3.6
) and further obtain

\rho_{R}(a)\simeq\frac{2\,\sqrt{3}\,n}{2+n}\,\frac{M_{P}}{a^{4}}\int_{a_{I}}^{a}\Gamma_{\phi}(a^{\prime})\,\sqrt{\rho_{\phi}(a^{\prime})}\,a^{\prime 3}\,da^{\prime}\,.

(3.7)

Note that here a general scale factor dependence of
\Gamma_{\phi}
has been assumed, which may arise, for example, from the field-dependent inflaton mass. In the present setup, the effective mass
M(a)
for the inflaton can be obtained from the second derivative of Eq. (
3.1
), which reads

M(a)^{2}\equiv\frac{d^{2}V}{d\phi^{2}}=n\,(n-1)\,\lambda\,\frac{\phi^{n-2}}{\Lambda^{n-4}}\simeq n\,(n-1)\,\lambda^{\frac{2}{n}}\,\Lambda^{\frac{2\,(4-n)}{n}}\rho_{\phi}(a)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\,,

(3.8)

or equivalently,

M(a)=M_{\text{rh}}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{3\,(n-2)}{n+2}},

(3.9)

where

M_{\text{rh}}\equiv M(a_{\text{rh}})\simeq\sqrt{n\,(n-1)}\,\lambda^{\frac{1}{n}}\,\Lambda^{\frac{4-n}{n}}\left[3\,M_{P}^{2}\,H^{2}(T_{\text{rh}})\right]^{\frac{n-2}{2n}}.

(3.10)

It is interesting to note that for
n\neq 2
,
M
has a field dependence that, in turn, would lead to an inflaton decay rate with a scale factor (or time) dependence.

Before moving further, we would like to note that the potential in Eq. (
3.1
) can naturally arise in a number of inflationary scenarios, for example, the
\alpha
-attractor T- or E-models
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
, or the Starobinsky model
[

,

,
24
,

]

\alpha
-attractor T-model
[
20
,
21

V(\phi)=\lambda\,M_{P}^{4}\left[\tanh\left(\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{6\,\alpha}\,M_{P}}\right)\right]^{n}\simeq\lambda\,M_{P}^{4}\times\begin{cases}1&\;\text{for}\;\phi\gg M_{P},\\
\left(\frac{\phi}{\sqrt{6\,\alpha}\,M_{P}}\right)^{n}&\;\text{for}\;\phi\ll M_{P}\,.\end{cases}

(3.11)

The overall scale of the potential parameterized by the coupling
\lambda
can be determined from the amplitude of the scalar perturbation power spectrum
A_{S}\simeq(2.1\pm 0.1)\times 10^{-9}

[

\lambda\simeq\frac{18\,\pi^{2}\,\alpha\,A_{S}}{6^{n/2}\,N_{\star}^{2}}\,,

(3.12)

where

\lambda

2.1

3.1
Fermionic reheating

We first consider the scenario where the inflaton decays into a pair of fermions via the Yukawa interaction in Eq. (
2.1
), with a decay rate

\Gamma_{\phi}(a)=\frac{y_{\text{eff}}^{2}}{8\pi}\,M(a)\,,

(3.13)

where the effective coupling
y_{\text{eff}}\neq y_{\psi}
(for
n\neq 2
) is obtained after averaging over several oscillations
[
42
,
43
,
27
]
. The evolution of the SM energy density (Eq. (
3.7
)) in this case becomes
[
31
]

\rho_{R}(a)\simeq\frac{3\,n}{7-n}\,M_{P}^{2}\,\Gamma_{\phi}(a_{\text{rh}})\,H(a_{\text{rh}})\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{6(n-1)}{2+n}}\left[1-\left(\frac{a_{I}}{a}\right)^{\frac{2(7-n)}{2+n}}\right],

(3.14)

and, therefore, the temperature of the SM bath evolves as

T(a)\simeq T_{\text{rh}}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\alpha},

(3.15)

with

\alpha=\begin{cases}\frac{3}{2}\,\frac{n-1}{n+2}&\text{ for }n<7\,,\\
1&\text{ for }n>7\,.\end{cases}

(3.16)

Trading the scale factor with temperature, the Hubble expansion rate during reheating (cf. Eq. (
3.5
)) can be rewritten as

H(T)\simeq H(T_{\text{rh}})\left(\frac{T}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{3\,n}{2+n}\,\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(3.17)

Note that, for the case with
n=2
where the inflaton oscillates in a quadratic potential with an EoS parameter
w=0
, the standard dependences with the scale factor
\rho_{R}(a)\propto a^{-3/2}
and
T(a)\propto a^{-3/8}
are reproduced.

3.2
Bosonic reheating

Alternatively, if inflatons only decay into a pair of bosons through the trilinear scalar interaction in Eq. (
2.1
), the decay rate is instead

\Gamma_{\phi}(a)=\frac{\mu_{\text{eff}}^{2}}{8\pi\,M(a)}\,,

(3.18)

where again the effective coupling
\mu_{\text{eff}}\neq\mu
(if
n\neq 2
) can be obtained after averaging over oscillations. Using a procedure similar to the previous fermionic case, one sees that the SM energy density scales as
[
31
]

\rho_{R}(a)\simeq\frac{3\,n}{1+2\,n}\,M_{P}^{2}\,\Gamma_{\phi}(a_{\text{rh}})\,H(a_{\text{rh}})\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{6}{2+n}}\left[1-\left(\frac{a_{I}}{a}\right)^{\frac{2\,(1+2n)}{2+n}}\right]\,,

(3.19)

with which the SM temperature and the Hubble rate evolve as Eqs. (
3.15
) and (
3.17
), respectively, with

\alpha=\frac{3}{2}\,\frac{1}{n+2}

(3.20)

during reheating. As before, we reproduce the results of the oscillation in a quadratic potential for
n=2
. Note that for
n>2
the radiation energy density is diluted faster in the case of fermionic reheating than in bosonic reheating. As an example, for
n=4
, the radiation energy density decreases as
\rho_{R}\propto a^{-3}
for fermionic and
\rho_{R}\propto a^{-1}
for bosonic reheating, respectively. It is important to briefly discuss the qualitative behavior of the two reheating scenarios. As we have already noticed, the inflaton decay width into fermionic final states
\propto M(a)
, while for bosonic final states the decay width
\propto 1/M(a)
. Since the inflaton mass
M(a)
is a decreasing function of time, the reheating process becomes more efficient over time for the bosonic final states than for the fermionic final states for
n>2
.

Finally, we would like to mention that for
n\gtrsim 8
(or equivalently
\omega\gtrsim 0.65
), purely gravitational reheating becomes important
[
44
,
45
,
46
,
47
]
and may dominate over perturbative reheating for certain choices of the inflaton-matter couplings, as shown in Refs.
[
44
,
47
]
. Typically, for
\omega\gtrsim 0.65
, gravitational reheating alone can be sufficient to reheat the Universe, without requiring any contribution from perturbative reheating.

Such scenarios, however, are severely constrained from inflationary GW overproduction
[
48
,
49

50
]

Since we are interested in the perturbative reheating scenario, in the subsequent analysis we shall therefore consider
n<8

4
Gravitational Waves from Graviton Bremsstrahlung

After production via the bremsstrahlung process, gravitons propagate and constitute a stochastic GW background. In this section, we compute the contribution to the effective number of neutrinos
N_{\text{eff}}
and the GW spectrum, considering bosonic and fermionic reheating scenarios. To do that, we first write down the Boltzmann equations for the inflaton, SM radiation, and GW energy densities as

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\phi}}{dt}+\frac{6\,n}{2+n}\,H\,\rho_{\phi}=-\frac{2\,n}{2+n}\,\left(\Gamma^{(0)}+\Gamma^{(1)}\right)\rho_{\phi}\,,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{R}}{dt}+4\,H\,\rho_{R}=+\frac{2\,n}{2+n}\,\Gamma^{(0)}\,\rho_{\phi}+\frac{2\,n}{2+n}\,\int\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}}{dE_{\omega}}\,\frac{M-E_{\omega}}{M}\,\rho_{\phi}\,dE_{\omega}\,,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{dt}+4\,H\,\rho_{\text{GW}}=+\frac{2\,n}{2+n}\,\int\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}}{dE_{\omega}}\,\frac{E_{\omega}}{M}\,\rho_{\phi}\,dE_{\omega}\,,

(4.3)

where
E_{\omega}
corresponds to the graviton energy at the moment of production,
\Gamma^{(0)}
to the 2-body inflaton decay width in Eqs. (
3.13
) and (
3.18
), and
\Gamma^{(1)}
to the 3-body decay width in Eqs. (
A.1
) and (
A
).
4
4

4

We note that the 2-to-2 process inflaton +
\psi

\to
graviton +
\psi
(with
\psi
being a decay product of inflaton) has the same topology as the 3-body decay. However, the contribution of the 2-to-2 process is subdominant compared to the 1-to-3 decay in our case because the inflaton mass is always larger than the bath temperature
[
51
]
.

We note that, for
n=2
, these equations reproduce those presented in Ref.
[
6
]
where a quadratic potential was assumed.
The Hubble expansion rate is given by

H^{2}=\frac{1}{3\,M_{P}^{2}}\,\left(\rho_{\phi}+\rho_{R}+\rho_{\text{GW}}\right)\,.

(4.4)

Note that
(M-E_{\omega})/M
and
E_{\omega}/M
correspond to the fractions of inflaton energy injected into SM radiation and GWs, respectively.

4.1
Contribution to
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}

When the production of gravitons terminates after reheating, the GW energy density redshifts as
a^{-4}
, mimicking that of SM radiation. As a result, GW itself acts as an additional source of radiation with the potential to alter the prediction of BBN. Thus, an excess of the GW energy density around
T\sim\text{MeV}
can be restricted by considering the (present and future) limits on
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
from CMB and BBN. From Eqs. (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), it follows that the evolution of the ratio GW to SM radiation energy density is

\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{da}\simeq\frac{2\,n}{2+n}\,\frac{1}{a\,H}\,\frac{\rho_{\phi}}{\rho_{R}}\left[\int\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}}{dE_{\omega}}\,\frac{E_{\omega}}{M}\,dE_{\omega}-\frac{\rho_{\text{GW}}}{\rho_{R}}\,\Gamma^{(0)}\right],

(4.5)

which, taking into account the scaling of
\rho_{\phi}
and
T
given in Eqs. (
3.4
) and (
3.15
), can be further reduced to

\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{da}\simeq\frac{2\,n}{2+n}\,\frac{1}{a\,H(T_{\text{rh}})}\,\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{3\,n}{2+n}-4\,\alpha}\left[\int_{0}^{\frac{M(a)}{2}}\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}}{dE_{\omega}}\,\frac{E_{\omega}}{M}\,dE_{\omega}-\frac{\rho_{\text{GW}}}{\rho_{R}}\,\Gamma^{(0)}\right],

(4.6)

where, at the end of reheating, the equality
\rho_{R}(a_{\text{rh}})=\rho_{\phi}(a_{\text{rh}})
is met.
Considering the coupling of the inflaton to a pair of fermions or a pair of bosons, we obtain

\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{da}\simeq\frac{2n}{2+n}\frac{1}{a\,H(T_{\text{rh}})}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{3n}{2+n}-4\alpha}\times\begin{cases}\frac{3y_{\text{eff}}^{2}}{1024\pi^{3}}\frac{M(a)^{3}}{M_{P}^{2}}-\frac{\rho_{\text{GW}}}{\rho_{R}}\frac{y_{\text{eff}}^{2}}{8\pi}M(a)&\text{fermions,}\\
\frac{1}{768\pi^{3}}\frac{\mu_{\text{eff}}^{2}M(a)}{M_{P}^{2}}-\frac{\rho_{\text{GW}}}{\rho_{R}}\frac{1}{8\pi}\frac{\mu_{\text{eff}}^{2}}{M(a)}&\text{bosons.}\end{cases}

(4.7)

The previous equations can be analytically solved taking into account the scale dependence of the inflaton mass and assuming that at the beginning of reheating both the SM and GW energy densities were vanishingly small. Imposing
H(T_{\text{rh}})=\Gamma^{(0)}(T_{\text{rh}})
and
y_{\text{eff}}^{2}=8\pi\,H(T_{\text{rh}})/M_{\text{rh}}
for the fermionic and
\mu_{\text{eff}}^{2}=8\pi\,M_{\text{rh}}\,H(T_{\text{rh}})
for the bosonic reheating scenarios, respectively, it follows from Eq. (
4.7
) that

\frac{\rho_{\text{GW}}}{\rho_{R}}(a)\simeq\frac{\mathcal{C}}{192\pi^{2}}\frac{n}{n-3}\left(\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{6(n-2)}{2+n}}\left[\left(\frac{a}{a_{\text{max}}}\right)^{\frac{4(n-3)}{2+n}}-1\right]\,,

(4.8)

with
\mathcal{C}=1
for scalars and
\mathcal{C}=9/4
for fermions.

4.11

T_{\Delta N_{\text{eff}}}\lesssim m_{e}

4.2
Spectrum of stochastic gravitational waves

Contrary to the previous case, for the GW spectrum one needs to keep track of the differential GW energy density
d\rho_{\text{GW}}/dE_{\omega}
. Is it therefore convenient to write Eq. (
4.3
) in its differential form

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{dE_{\omega}}+4\,H\,\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{dE_{\omega}}=+\frac{2\,n}{2+n}\,\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}}{dE_{\omega}}\,\frac{E_{\omega}}{M}\,\rho_{\phi}\,.

(4.15)

The evolution of the differential ratio
d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})/dE_{\omega}
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{da}\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2n}{2+n}\frac{1}{a\,H}\frac{\rho_{\phi}}{\rho_{R}}\left[\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}}{dE_{\omega}}\frac{E_{\omega}}{M}-\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}}\Gamma^{(0)}\right]

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{2n}{2+n}\frac{1}{a\,H(T_{\text{rh}})}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{3n}{2+n}-4\alpha}\left[\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}}{dE_{\omega}}\frac{E_{\omega}}{M}-\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}}\Gamma^{(0)}\right].

(4.16)

It is important to emphasize that in the previous expressions,
E_{\omega}
corresponds to the graviton energy at the moment of emission. However, to compute the GW spectrum, one needs to take into account the redshift and sum over different (redshifted) energies. Therefore, in Eq. (
4.2
) the change of variable
E_{\omega}(E_{\omega}^{\prime},\,a)=E_{\omega}^{\prime}\,\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}
, with
E_{\omega}^{\prime}
being the energy at
a=a_{\text{rh}}
, is required, yielding to

\frac{d}{da}\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\frac{2n}{2+n}\frac{1}{a\,H(T_{\text{rh}})}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{3n}{2+n}-4\alpha}\left[\frac{d\Gamma^{(1)}}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\frac{E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M}-\frac{a}{a_{\text{rh}}}\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\Gamma^{(0)}\right],

(4.17)

where the overall factor
a_{\text{rh}}/a
comes from the Jacobian related to the change of variable.

The primordial GW spectrum at present
\Omega_{\text{GW}}(f)
per logarithmic frequency
f
is defined as

\Omega_{\text{GW}}(f)=\frac{1}{\rho_{c}}\,\frac{d\rho_{\text{GW}}}{d\ln f}=\Omega_{\gamma}^{(0)}\,\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{d\ln f}=\Omega_{\gamma}^{(0)}\,\frac{g_{\star}(T_{\text{rh}})}{g_{\star}(T_{0})}\left[\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{0})}{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{rh}})}\right]^{4/3}\,\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}(T_{\text{rh}})/\rho_{R}(T_{\text{rh}}))}{d\ln E_{\omega}^{\prime}}\,,

(4.18)

where
\rho_{c}
is the critical energy density and
\Omega_{\gamma}^{(0)}h^{2}\simeq 2.47\times 10^{-5}
is the observed photon abundance
[
62
]
.
The GW frequency at present can be associated with the graviton energy
E_{\omega}^{\prime}
at the end of reheating via

f=\frac{E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\,\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a_{0}}=\frac{E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{2\pi}\,\frac{T_{0}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\left[\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{0})}{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{rh}})}\right]^{1/3}\,,

(4.19)

considering the redshift of the GW energy between reheating and the present epoch. The frequency is bounded from above because the graviton at production could carry at most half of the inflaton energy, namely
E_{\omega}(a)\leq M(a)/2

[
6
]
, which translates into

f\leq\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{4\pi}\,\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a_{0}}\left(\frac{a_{\text{rh}}}{a}\right)^{\frac{2(n-4)}{n+2}}\leq\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{4\pi}\,\frac{T_{0}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\left[\frac{g_{\star s}(T_{0})}{g_{\star s}(T_{\text{rh}})}\right]^{1/3}\times\begin{cases}1&\text{ for }n\leq 4,\\
\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{2(n-4)}{\alpha(n+2)}}&\text{ for }n>4\,,\end{cases}

(4.20)

or, equivalently,

f\leq 4.1\times 10^{12}~\text{Hz}\,\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}}\,\frac{5.5\times 10^{15}~\text{GeV}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\times\begin{cases}1&\text{ for }n\leq 4,\\
\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{4(n-4)}{3}}&\text{ for bosons }n>4,\\
\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{4}{3}\frac{n-4}{n-1}}&\text{ for fermions }4<n<7,\\
\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{2(n-4)}{n+2}}&\text{ for fermions }n>7\,.\end{cases}

(4.21)

4.2.1
Fermionic reheating

Figure 2
:
Schematic plot for the regime of integration in the plane

For the fermionic case, Eq. (
4.17
) can be analytically solved. Taking into account that there is no energy stored in GWs at the end of inflation, the expression of
d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})/dE_{\omega}^{\prime}
at the end of reheating, that is at
a=a_{\text{rh}}
, for the case
n=2
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{48\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\left[\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\left[12\left(1-\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{3}}\right)+\frac{9}{2}\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\left(\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{16}{3}}-1\right)\right.\right.

\displaystyle\quad+\left.\left.\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{8}\right)\right]+16\ln\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right]\,,

(4.22)

for
0\leq E_{\omega}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,M_{\text{rh}}\,(T_{\text{max}}/T_{\text{rh}})^{-8/3}
.
Here, the integration has been performed between
a_{\text{max}}\leq a\leq a_{\text{rh}}
. Alternatively,
for
\frac{1}{2}\,M_{\text{rh}}\,(T_{\text{max}}/T_{\text{rh}})^{-8/3}\leq E_{\omega}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,M_{\text{rh}}
one has

\displaystyle\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{96\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\Bigg[2\left(\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{3}-1\right)+9\left(1-\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+24\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}-1\right)-12\ln\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\Bigg]\,,

(4.23)

after integrating between
2a_{\text{rh}}\,E_{\omega}^{\prime}/M_{\text{rh}}\leq a\leq a_{\text{rh}}
.
We note that the two cases correspond to two different regimes, as shown in Fig.
2
. In the first case, low-energy GWs are produced during the entire reheating process, while in the second case, high-energy GWs can only be emitted during the last stage of reheating, as shown in Fig.
2
. This can be understood taking into account:
i)
the maximal energy of the graviton at production is
E_{\omega}(a)<M(a)/2
,
ii)
for
n=2
the inflaton mass is constant
M(a)=M_{\text{rh}}
, and
iii)
the redshift of the GW energy.
Alternatively, for
n=4
one has

\displaystyle\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\left[\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{9}}-1\right]

\displaystyle\quad\times\left[\left(1-\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{3}\right)+3\left(\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}-1\right)+4\left(1-\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)\right]\,,

(4.24)

for
0<E_{\omega}^{\prime}\leq\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{2}
. Interestingly, for
n=4
, a single expression is required, such as the graviton energy and the inflaton mass scale in the same way: as the inverse of the scale factor
a
.

For
n=6
, on the other hand, one has

\displaystyle\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{80\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}\Bigg[24\ln\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}+15\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)\left(\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{-\frac{8}{15}}-1\right)

\displaystyle\quad+60\left(\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)\left(1-\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{15}}\right)+15\left(\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\left(\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{16}{15}}-1\right)\Bigg]\,,

(4.25)

for
0\leq E_{\omega}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,M_{\text{rh}}
, while for
\frac{1}{2}\,M_{\text{rh}}\leq E_{\omega}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,M_{\text{rh}}\,(T_{\text{max}}/T_{\text{rh}})^{8/15}
one has

\displaystyle\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{16\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}\Bigg[3\ln\left[\left(\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{15}}\right]+\left(\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{-\frac{8}{15}}-1\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+4\left(1-\left(\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{15}}\right)+\left(\left(\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{16}{15}}-1\right)\Bigg]\,.

(4.26)

In the latter case, the integration of Eq. (
4.17
) has to be done in two steps: first between
a_{\text{max}}\leq a\leq a_{\text{rh}}\,(M_{\text{rh}}/(2E_{\omega}^{\prime}))^{2}

with
the source term, then between
a_{\text{rh}}\,(M_{\text{rh}}/(2E_{\omega}^{\prime}))^{2}\leq a\leq a_{\text{rh}}

without
the source term, as GW emission can only occur for
E_{\omega}(a)\leq M(a)/2
. It is interesting to note that when
n=6
(and in general when
n\geq 6
), graviton energies higher than
\frac{1}{2}M_{\text{rh}}
, up to
E_{\omega}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}M_{\text{rh}}\,(T_{\text{max}}/T_{\text{rh}})^{\frac{2}{\alpha}\frac{n-4}{n+2}}
, are allowed. This can be understood by noticing that the inflaton mass decreases as
M(a)\propto a^{3/2}
, i.e., faster than the energy redshift. Therefore, high-energy GWs with energies
E_{\omega}^{\prime}>\frac{1}{2}M_{\text{rh}}
can be produced at the beginning of reheating when the inflaton was very massive (heavier than its effective mass at the end of reheating).
GWs with energies
E_{\omega}^{\prime}<\frac{1}{2}M_{\text{rh}}
can be produced during the entire reheating process.

4.2.2
Bosonic reheating

For the scenario where the inflaton decays into scalar final states, we follow the same procedure presented in the previous section.
For the case
n=2
,
d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})/dE_{\omega}^{\prime}
at the end of reheating is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\Bigg[

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}\left(\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{16}{3}}-1\right)-4\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)\left(\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{3}}-1\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{16}{3}\ln\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\Bigg]\,,

(4.27)

for

\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\Bigg[\left(1-\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}\right)+4\left(\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)-1\right)-2\ln\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\Bigg]\,.

(4.28)

Again the
n=4
scenario comes with a single expression as before, that reads

\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\left(1-\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}\left(\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{3}}-1\right)\,,

(4.29)

for
0<E_{\omega}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}M_{\text{rh}}
. Finally, for
n=6
one has

\displaystyle\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\Bigg[16\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}\ln\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}

\displaystyle\qquad+3\left(\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{16}{3}}-1\right)+12\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)\left(1-\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{3}}\right)\Bigg]\,,

(4.30)

for
0\leq E_{\omega}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,M_{\text{rh}}
, whereas for
\frac{1}{2}\,M_{\text{rh}}\leq E_{\omega}^{\prime}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,M_{\text{rh}}\,(T_{\text{max}}/T_{\text{rh}})^{8/3}
one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{d(\rho_{\text{GW}}/\rho_{R})}{dE_{\omega}^{\prime}}\simeq

\displaystyle\frac{3}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{M_{P}^{2}}\left(\frac{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}{M_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{2}\Bigg[2\ln\left[\left(\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{3}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+4\left(1-\left(\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{8}{3}}\right)+\left(\left(\frac{M_{\text{rh}}}{2E_{\omega}^{\prime}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{T_{\text{max}}}{T_{\text{rh}}}\right)^{\frac{16}{3}}-1\right)\Bigg]\,.

(4.31)

Before closing, we note that, as a sanity check, the results of Eq. (

) for both fermions and scalars can be recovered by integrating

over the corresponding range of energy.

6
Conclusions

In this study, we investigate the production of stochastic Gravitational Waves (GWs) through graviton bremsstrahlung during the reheating period. To ensure a viable scenario, we assume that the inflaton oscillates in a generic monomial potential of the form
\phi^{n}
during reheating. Unlike oscillations in a quadratic potential, this leads to a time-dependent decay width of the inflaton due to the field-dependent inflaton mass. The reheating process occurs through the inflaton’s perturbative decay into either a pair of bosons or fermions, resulting in a successful reheating that satisfies the BBN measurements. As a result of the unavoidable gravitational interaction resulting from the graviton-matter coupling, the inflaton undergoes 3-body decay with the radiative emission of massless gravitons, which comprise the GW spectrum.

We find that the GW spectrum is affected by the details of the reheating, or, in other words, by
i)
the equation of state parameter of the inflaton during reheating, and
ii)
the type of inflaton-matter coupling. Importantly, for
n>2
, there is a significant increase in the amplitude of GW during bosonic reheating, which is still safe from the constraint of
\Delta N_{\text{eff}}
on the extra radiation energy density around BBN or CMB, in terms of GWs. However, for the fermionic reheating scenario, this boost becomes negligible for
n>4
due to the typical functional dependence of the decay width on the inflaton mass. For a certain choice of parameters, we find, the predicted high-frequency GW signal is within the sensitivities of resonant cavity detectors for both bosonic and fermionic reheating. Our finding thus opens up the possibility that future (high-frequency) GW experiments could potentially shed light on the microscopic dynamics of reheating, for example, on the shape of the inflaton potential and types of inflaton-matter couplings.

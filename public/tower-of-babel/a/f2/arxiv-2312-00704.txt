Moments of the Poisson distribution of order k k Journal: (internal report CC23-12)
S. R. Mane

Abstract
The factorial moments of the standard Poisson distribution are well known and are simple, but the raw moments are considered to be more complicated (Touchard polynomials). The present note presents a recurrence relation and an explicit combinatorial sum for the raw moments of the Poisson distribution of order k k . Unlike the standard Poisson distribution (the case k = 1 k=1 ), for k > 1 k>1 the structure of the raw and factorial moments have many similarities and the raw moments are not more complicated (formally, at least) than the factorial moments. We remark briefly on the central moments (i.e. moments centered on the mean) of the Poisson distribution of order k k .

It is well known that for a Poisson distribution with rate parameter
\lambda
, the mean and variance both equal
\lambda

and the
n^{th}
factorial moment is
M_{(n)}=\lambda^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
.
However, the expression for the raw moment
M_{n}
is more complicated, and is given by a Touchard polynomial,
viz.
M_{0}=1
and for
n\geq 1
it is

M_{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}{n\brace j}\lambda^{j}\,.

(1)

Here
{n\brace j}
is the Stirling number of the second kind.
The first few raw moments are

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda\,,

(2a)

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}+\lambda\,,

(2b)

\displaystyle M_{3}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{3}+3\lambda^{2}+\lambda\,,

(2c)

\displaystyle M_{4}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{4}+6\lambda^{3}+7\lambda^{2}+\lambda\,,

(2d)

\displaystyle M_{5}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{6}+10\lambda^{4}+25\lambda^{3}+15\lambda^{2}+\lambda\,,

(2e)

\displaystyle M_{6}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{6}+15\lambda^{5}+65\lambda^{4}+90\lambda^{3}+31\lambda^{2}+\lambda\,.

(2f)

The Poisson distribution of order
k

[
1
]
is a variant or extension of the standard Poisson distribution.
For
k=1
it is the standard Poisson distribution.
It is an example of a compound Poisson distribution
[
2
]
.
Its probability mass function is
[
1
]

P_{n}(k,\lambda)=e^{-k\lambda}\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}!}\,.

(3)

For
k=1
this simplifies to the known result
P_{n}(\lambda)=e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{n}/n!
.
Less is known about its moments.
Let
M_{n}(k,\lambda)
and
M_{(n)}(k,\lambda)
respectively denote the
n^{th}
raw and factorial moment of the Poisson distribution of order
k
.
Charalambides
[
3
]
published an elegant analysis of statistical distributions of order
k
, including the Poisson distribution of order
k
.
In a recent note,
[
4
]
, the author presented the following combinatorial sum formula for the
n^{th}
factorial moment.
It agrees with the formalism presented in
[
3
]
.

M_{(n)}(k,\lambda)=n!\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}!}\biggl[\binom{k+1}{j+1}\biggr]^{n_{j}}\,.

(4)

In this note, we present a combinatorial sum for the raw moment
M_{n}
of the Poisson distribution of order
k
.
The formula has a similar structure to that for the factorial moments.
It is simplest to see this by employing the moment generating function and factorial moment generating function.
The probability generating function
G(z)
(which is also the factorial moment generating function) and the moment generating function
M(t)=G(e^{t})

of the Poisson distribution of order
k
are (
[
2
]
, see also
[
3
]
)

\displaystyle G(z,k,\lambda)

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda(z+z^{2}+\dots+z^{k})}\,,

(5a)

\displaystyle M(t,k,\lambda)

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda(e^{t}+e^{2t}+\dots+e^{kt})}\,.

(5b)

To simplify the derivations below, we introduce some useful polynomials.
Define the falling factorial sum
F_{j}(k,z)
and power sum
S_{j}(k,t)
as follows, for
j=1,2,\dots

\displaystyle F_{j}(k,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=j}^{k}s_{(j)}z^{s-j}=\sum_{s=j}^{k}s(s-1)\dots(s-j+1)z^{s-j}\,,

(6a)

\displaystyle S_{j}(k,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}s^{j}e^{st}\,.

(6b)

Also
F_{0}(k,z)=\sum_{s=1}^{k}z^{s}
and
S_{0}(k,t)=\sum_{s=1}^{k}e^{st}
.
Note that
F_{j}(k,z)=0
if
j>k
but
S_{j}(k,t)\neq 0
for any value of
j
.
Also denote their values at
t=0
and
z=1
respectively as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{j}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=j}^{k}s(s-1)\dots(s-j+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;j!\binom{k+1}{j+1}\,,

(7a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{j}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=1}^{k}s^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{s=1}^{k}{j\brace s}\frac{(k+1)_{s+1}}{s+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\sum_{s=1}^{k}{j\brace s}s!\binom{k+1}{s+1}\,.

(7b)

Then observe that

G(z,k,\lambda)=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda F_{0}(k,z)}\,,\qquad\qquad M(t,k,\lambda)=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda S_{0}(k,t)}\,.

(8)

Note the derivatives

\frac{dF_{j}(k,z)}{dz}=F_{j+1}(k,z)\,,\qquad\qquad\frac{dS_{j}(k,t)}{dt}=S_{j+1}(k,t)\,.

(9)

We omit explicit mention of the function arguments below.
The first few derivatives are given as follows

\displaystyle\frac{dG}{dz}

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda F_{0}}F_{1}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\frac{dM}{dt}

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda S_{0}}S_{1}\lambda\,,

(10a)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}G}{dz^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda F_{0}}(F_{1}^{2}\lambda^{2}+F_{2}\lambda)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}M}{dt^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda S_{0}}(S_{1}^{2}\lambda^{2}+S_{2}\lambda)\,,

(10b)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}G}{dz^{3}}

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda F_{0}}(F_{1}^{3}\lambda^{3}+3F_{1}F_{2}\lambda^{2}+F_{3}\lambda)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{d^{3}M}{dt^{3}}

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda F_{0}}(S_{1}^{3}\lambda^{3}+3S_{1}S_{2}\lambda^{2}+S_{3}\lambda)\,,

(10c)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{4}G}{dz^{4}}

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda F_{0}}(F_{1}^{4}\lambda^{4}+6F_{1}^{2}F_{2}\lambda^{3}

\displaystyle\frac{d^{4}M}{dt^{4}}

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}e^{\lambda S_{0}}(S_{1}^{4}\lambda^{4}+6S_{1}^{2}S_{2}\lambda^{3}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+(4F_{1}F_{3}+3F_{2}^{2})\lambda^{2}+F_{4}\lambda)\,,

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+(4S_{1}S_{3}+3S_{2}^{2})\lambda^{2}+S_{4}\lambda)\,.

(10d)

Set
z=0
to obtain the factorial moment and
t=1
for the raw moment.
We obtain

\displaystyle M_{(1)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{1}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{1}\lambda\,,

(11a)

\displaystyle M_{(2)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{1}^{2}\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{F}_{2}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{1}^{2}\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{2}\lambda\,,

(11b)

\displaystyle M_{(3)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{1}^{3}\lambda^{3}+3\mathcal{F}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{2}\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{F}_{3}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle M_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{1}^{3}\lambda^{3}+3\mathcal{S}_{1}\mathcal{S}_{2}\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{3}\lambda\,,

(11c)

\displaystyle M_{(4)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{1}^{4}\lambda^{4}+6\mathcal{F}_{1}^{2}\mathcal{F}_{2}\lambda^{3}+(4\mathcal{F}_{1}\mathcal{F}_{3}+3\mathcal{F}_{2}^{2})\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{F}_{4}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle M_{4}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{1}^{4}\lambda^{4}+6\mathcal{S}_{1}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{2}\lambda^{3}+(4\mathcal{S}_{1}\mathcal{S}_{3}+3\mathcal{S}_{2}^{2})\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{4}\lambda\,.

(11d)

The two sets of expressions are the same, just replace
\mathcal{F}_{j}
by
\mathcal{S}_{j}
.
It is easily seen that this substitution applies for all the higher derivatives, i.e. the higher moments.
The recurrence relations for the probability mass function
P_{n}
, factorial moment
M_{(n)}
and raw moment
M_{n}
are respectively

\displaystyle P_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{n,k\}}jP_{n-j}\,,

(12a)

\displaystyle M_{(n)}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{\min\{n,k\}}\binom{n-1}{j-1}\mathcal{F}_{j}M_{(n-j)}\,,

(12b)

\displaystyle M_{n}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n-1}{j-1}\mathcal{S}_{j}M_{n-j}\,.

(12c)

Note the following:

1.

First, eq. (
12a
) was derived in
[
2
]
for compound Poisson distributions in general,
and for the Poisson distribution of order
k
in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
.

2.

Next, eq. (
12b
) was derived by Charalambides (eq. (4.7) in
[
3
]
, with differences of notation)
and eq. (
12c
) is obtained by analogy with eq. (
12b
),
replacing
\mathcal{F}_{j}
by
\mathcal{S}_{j}
.

3.

The upper limit for the sum in eq. (
12a
) cuts off at
\min\{n,k\}
because
P_{j}=0
for
j<0
.

4.

The upper limit for the sum in eq. (
12b
) cuts off at
\min\{n,k\}
because
\mathcal{F}_{j}=0
for
j>k
.

5.

The upper limit for the sum in eq. (
12c
) is
n
because
\mathcal{S}_{j}\neq 0
for all for
j\geq 0
.

The combinatorial sum expressions for
P_{n}
,
M_{(n)}
and
M_{n}
are respectively

\displaystyle P_{n}

\displaystyle=e^{-k\lambda}\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}!}\,,

(13a)

\displaystyle M_{(n)}

\displaystyle=n!\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}!}\biggl(\frac{\mathcal{F}_{j}}{j!}\biggr)^{n_{j}}

\displaystyle=n!\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}!}\biggl[\binom{k+1}{j+1}\biggr]^{n_{j}}\,,

(13b)

\displaystyle M_{n}

\displaystyle=n!\sum_{n_{1}+2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}!}\biggl(\frac{\mathcal{S}_{j}}{j!}\biggr)^{n_{j}}\,.

(13c)

Note the following:

1.

First, eq. (
13a
) is the same as eq. (
3
)
and eq. (
13b
) is the same as eq. (
4
).
They are reproduced here for ease of reference, and to display the similarity of structure with eq. (
13c
).

2.

For the standard Poisson distribution (
k=1
), the expression for
P_{n}
contains only a single term
e^{-\lambda}\lambda^{n}/n!

and the expression for
M_{(n)}
contains only a single term
\lambda^{n}
,
whereas the expression for
M_{n}
is a polynomial with
k
terms (see eq. (
1
)).

3.

However, for
k>1
, the expression for the raw moment
M_{n}
is not more complicated (formally, at least) than for the factorial moment
M_{(n)}
.
Admittedly, the expression for
\mathcal{F}_{j}
is simpler than that for
\mathcal{S}_{j}
.

We remark on the central moments (i.e. moments centered on the mean), denoted by
\tilde{M}_{n}=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}[X])^{n}
.
The mean of the Poisson distribution of order
k
is (
[
2
]
, see also
[
7
]
)

\mu(k,\lambda)=(1+\dots+k)\lambda=\frac{k(k+1)}{2}\lambda=\mathcal{S}_{1}\lambda\,.

(14)

The value of
\tilde{M}_{2}
is the variance (
[
2
]
, see also
[
7
]
)

\sigma^{2}(k,\lambda)=(1^{2}+\dots+k^{2})\lambda=\frac{k(k+1)(2k+1)}{6}\lambda=\mathcal{S}_{2}\lambda\,.

(15)

For the standard Poisson distribution (
k=1
), both the mean and the variance equal
\lambda
.
Then for all
n\geq 2
, the formula for
\tilde{M}_{n}
is simply that for the noncentral raw moment
M_{n}

without
the terms in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
(see eq. (
13c
))

\tilde{M}_{n}=n!\sum_{2n_{2}+\dots+kn_{k}=n}\prod_{j=2}^{k}\frac{\lambda^{n_{j}}}{n_{j}!}\biggl(\frac{\mathcal{S}_{j}}{j!}\biggr)^{n_{j}}\,.

(16)

From eq. (
12c
), the recurrence relation for the centered moments is (with
\tilde{M}_{0}=1
and
\tilde{M}_{1}=0
, by definition)

\tilde{M}_{n}=\lambda\sum_{j=2}^{n}\binom{n-1}{j-1}\mathcal{S}_{j}\tilde{M}_{n-j}\,.

(17)

The first few central moments of the Poisson distribution of order
k
are

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{2}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{S}_{3}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{4}

\displaystyle=3\mathcal{S}_{2}^{2}\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{4}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{5}

\displaystyle=10\mathcal{S}_{2}\mathcal{S}_{3}\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{5}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{6}

\displaystyle=15\mathcal{S}_{2}^{3}\lambda^{3}+(15\mathcal{S}_{2}\mathcal{S}_{4}+10\mathcal{S}_{3}^{2})\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{6}\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\tilde{M}_{7}

\displaystyle=105\mathcal{S}_{2}^{2}\mathcal{S}_{3}\lambda^{3}+(21\mathcal{S}_{2}\mathcal{S}_{5}+35\mathcal{S}_{3}\mathcal{S}_{4})\lambda^{2}+\mathcal{S}_{7}\lambda\,.

(18)

Note
\tilde{M}_{n}
is a polynomial in
\lambda
of degree
\lfloor(n/2)\rfloor

with no constant term and all powers from
\lambda
up to
\lambda^{\lfloor(n/2)\rfloor}
.
The coefficient of
\lambda
is
\mathcal{S}_{n}
.
For the standard Poisson distribution (
k=1
), then
\mathcal{S}_{j}=1
for all
j
and the above expressions simplify to the known results.
For
k=1
, we write
\mu_{n}
in place of
\tilde{M}_{n}
:

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\mu_{3}

\displaystyle=\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\mu_{4}

\displaystyle=3\lambda^{2}+\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\mu_{5}

\displaystyle=10\lambda^{2}+\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\mu_{6}

\displaystyle=15\lambda^{3}+25\lambda^{2}+\lambda\,,

\displaystyle\mu_{7}

\displaystyle=105\lambda^{3}+56\lambda^{2}+\lambda\,.

(19)

This was extended to treat the raw and centered moments.
This note displays explicit combinatorial sums for the above quantities.

There is a consistent pattern in the combinatorial sums for all four of the above quantities, also for their recurrence relations.

𝖹𝖥 \mathsf{ZF} and its interpretations
S. Jockwich Martinez, S. Tarafder, G. Venturi

Abstract
. In this paper, we unify the study of classical and non-classical algebra-valued models of set theory, by studying variations of the interpretation functions for = = and ∈ \in . Although, these variations coincide with the standard interpretation in Boolean-valued constructions, nonetheless they extend the scope of validity of 𝖹𝖥 \mathsf{ZF} to new algebra-valued models. This paper presents, for the first time, non-trivial paraconsistent models of full 𝖹𝖥 \mathsf{ZF} . Moreover, due to the validity of Leibniz’s law in these structures, we will show how to construct proper models of set theory by quotienting these algebra-valued models with respect to equality, modulo the filter of the designated truth-values.

Introduction

Forcing was introduced in 1963 by Paul Cohen. The revolution that this method brought to set theory cannot be understated. The many different models (or universes) of set theory that have been created since the Sixties had the effect of putting into question the central role of set theory as the foundations of mathematics, suggesting a relativity of the truth-value of many set-theoretical sentences.

A reformulation of the forcing method in terms of Boolean-valued constructions was soon after proposed, during the Sixties, by Scott and, independently, Vopěnka, building on the algebraic work of Rasiowa and Sikorski. The two methods yield equivalent independence results, because of the bridge that the Stone duality theorem offers between partially ordered sets and Boolean algebras.

The models of
\mathsf{ZF}
that we obtain by forcing, as it is the case for Boolean-valued models, thus represent many different ways in which the universe of sets could be. The different and heterogeneous structures we obtain by this method constitute an embarrassment of riches that is responsible for a heated debate over which represent(s) the correct view on set theory.

The originality of this result stems from the novelty of the proposed approach. Indeed, the key ingredient of Theorem
3.17
is a syntactic modification of the axioms of
\mathsf{ZF}
and of the interpretation of the basic relations of the language of set theory: equality (
=
) and set-membership (
\in
). These modifications in a whole depend on the following propositional equivalence:
(A\to B)\equiv(A\to B)\land(\neg B\to\neg A)
.

The reason for these changes is twofold. On the one hand, such a simple modification allows to overcome the limits of the previous approaches to algebra-valued models.
Indeed, it is only with the new interpretation function developed in this paper that we can validate all instances of the Separation, Collection, and Foundation axiom schema as given in Figure
1
, together with Leibniz’s Law of indiscernibility of identicals. Moreover, it is this modified interpretation function that will force us to change the Extensionality axiom by supplementing it with its contrapositive (classically equivalent) counterpart. On the other hand, to resort to contrapositive versions of classical elements of Boolean-valued models has a deeper conceptual motivation. As a matter of fact, in this paper we find ourselves operating in a
weak logical context, where classical equivalences may cease to hold. Therefore, information about sets which are classically encoded in the axioms and the interpretation of the basic relations of set theory may get lost in this new logical environment. Since a logic that tolerates contradictions should necessarily give a separate account of assertion and denial, it is then the task of the axiomatizer to describe both set-membership and non-set-membership. If this is done, in the classical case, only by committing to asserting what belongs to what (and then using the duality of logic to extend it to denial), this is not anymore the case within this weaker logical environment.

Besides the technical and conceptual motivations for the justification of the constructions presented in this paper, it is also worth noting that from a purely classical perspective these changes are innocuous. As a matter of fact, the resulting set theory is only a classically equivalent version of
\mathsf{ZF}
, whose equivalence can be already proved within classical propositional logic.

Another interesting point is that in changing the interpretation of equality and set-membership we do not change the underlying mathematical ontology. As a matter of fact, the definition of an algebra-valued universe
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, based on an algebra
\mathbb{A}
, does not depend on the assignment function that we use to give a truth-value to the formulas of the extended language of set theory (as in the standard case, a language equipped with constants for every element of
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
). This means that the change in the assignment function corresponds only to a change of interpretation of set theory and not to which sets there are. But then, the assignment functions described in this paper suggest that we can have different ways to interpret
\mathsf{ZF}
, that coincide in the classical case but diverge in non-classical contexts.

Disregarding the intrinsic value one can attach to non-classical logics, this paper not only presents new ways to build models of
\mathsf{ZF}
, but it also shows that Boolean-valued models represent only a (small) fragment of a much larger class of models of set theory (classical and not).

The paper is organized as follows: In §1, we review some preliminaries and we introduce the main objects of study. In §2, we review the results on the validity of
\mathsf{ZF}
under the standard assignment function as defined, for example, in
[

]
. The core of the paper, §3, is devoted to the proof of Theorem
3.17
and to the study of a new assignment function, called
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

Then, in §4, we study the internal logics of the algebra-valued models constructed using the new and the standard assignment function. In §5, we show how to build quotient models out of the algebra-valued models constructed using the new assignment function. In §6, we compare the results of this paper with other models of paraconsistent set theory. We end the paper with §7, where we present some open questions which might suggest new and exciting research paths.

1.
Algebra-valued models

1.1.
Algebraic structures

We introduce a few algebraic preliminaries to fix the notations.

Definition 1.1
.

An algebraic structure is a triplet
\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{O},\mathbf{C}\rangle
, where
\mathbf{A}
is a non-empty set,
\mathbf{O}
is a collection of operations on
\mathbf{A}
, and
\mathbf{C}
is a collection (possibly empty) of some elements of
\mathbf{A}
, called constants.

Definition 1.2
.

An algebraic structure
\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{O},\mathbf{C}\rangle
is called a lattice if
\mathbf{O}
contains two binary operators
\wedge
and
\vee
satisfying the following laws for any
a,b,c\in\mathbf{A}
:

(i)

commutative:
a\wedge b=b\wedge a
,
a\vee b=b\vee a
,

(ii)

associative:
a\wedge(b\wedge c)=(a\wedge b)\wedge c
,
a\vee(b\vee c)=(a\vee b)\vee c
,

(iii)

idempotent:
a\wedge a=a
,
a\vee a=a
, and

(iv)

absorption:
a\wedge(a\vee b)=a
,
a\vee(a\wedge b)=a
;

\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{O},\mathbf{C}\rangle
is called a distributive lattice if in addition both the following properties hold:

(v)
a\wedge(b\vee c)=(a\wedge b)\vee(a\wedge c)
,
a\vee(b\wedge c)=(a\vee b)\wedge(a\vee c)
.

A
partial order relation

\leq
is defined in a lattice as follows:

a\leq b\mbox{ if and only if }a\wedge b=a\mbox{ (or equivalently }a\vee b=b),\mbox{ for any }a,b\in\mathbf{A}.

An element
a\in\mathbf{A}
is called an upper bound of a subset
S
of
\mathbf{A}
, if
x\leq a
, for all
x\in S
. Similarly, an element
b\in\mathbf{A}
is called a lower bound of a subset
S
of
\mathbf{A}
, if
x\geq b
, for all
x\in S
.

Definition 1.3
.

A lattice
\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{O},\mathbf{C}\rangle
is said to be bounded if
\mathbf{C}
contains two elements, that we denote by,
\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{0}
such that
\mathbf{1}
is the upper bound of
\mathbf{A}
and
\mathbf{0}
is the lower bound of
\mathbf{A}
. The elements
\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{0}
are called the top and bottom elements of the lattice, respectively.

Definition 1.4
.

A bounded lattice
\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{O},\mathbf{C}\rangle
is said to be complete if for any
S\subseteq\mathbf{A}
,

(i)

there is an element
a\in\mathbf{A}
such that
a
is an upper bound of
S
and for any upper bound
p
of
S
,
a\leq p
, in this case
a
is called the supremum of
S
, denoted by
\bigvee S
, and

(ii)

there is an element
b\in\mathbf{A}
such that
b
is a lower bound of
S
and for any lower bound
q
of
S
,
q\leq b
, in this case
b
is called the infimum of
S
, denoted by
\bigwedge S
.

From now on, we shall denote the structure of a bounded lattice as
\langle\mathbf{A},\wedge,\vee,\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\rangle
.

1.2.
Assignment functions and validity of a sentence

In this section, we shall recall the general construction of algebra-valued models of set theory and define what an assignment function is. Let us begin with an algebraic structure
\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{O},\mathbf{C}\rangle
, which contains a complete distributive lattice
\langle\mathbf{A},\wedge,\vee,\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\rangle
as a substructure, and a language
\mathcal{L}_{\in}
for set theory having the same structure of the algebra, together with the two binary predicate symbols
\in
and
=
and the two quantifiers
\exists
and
\forall
.

Let us consider
\mathbf{V}
as the class of all sets. Then, by transfinite recursion, we construct the following:

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}^{(\mathbb{A})}

\displaystyle=\{x\,:\mbox{ $x$ is a function and }\mathrm{ran}(x)\subseteq\mathbf{A}

\displaystyle\hskip 42.67912pt\mbox{ and there is $\xi<\alpha$ with }\mathrm{dom}(x)\subseteq\mathbf{V}_{\xi}^{(\mathbb{A})}\}\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}

\displaystyle=\{x\,:\,\exists\alpha(x\in\mathbf{V}_{\alpha}^{(\mathbb{A})})\}.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
be the extended language of
\mathcal{L}_{\in}
, constructed by adding constant symbols corresponding to every element in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. To increase the readability, the name corresponding to each
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
will be denoted by the symbol
u
itself, in the extended language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
.

Definition 1.5
.

A function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket^{\mathbb{A}}
from the collection of all sentences of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
into
\mathbf{A}
is said to be an assignment function.

Given an assignment function, the validity of the sentences in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
depends on a subset of
\mathbf{A}
called the
designated set
. Its elements represent the possible truth-values of valid sentences.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=\langle\mathbf{A},\wedge,\vee,\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\rangle
be a bounded lattice. A set
D\subseteq\mathbf{A}
is said to be a designated set if

(i)

\mathbf{1}\in D
, but
\mathbf{0}\notin D
,

(ii)

if
x\in D
and
x\leq y
, then
y\in D
, and

(iii)

for any
x,y\in D
,
x\wedge y\in D
.

Thus, a designated set is nothing but a
filter
in
\mathbb{A}
.

Definition 1.7
.

A sentence
\varphi
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
is said to be valid in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
with respect to a given assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket^{\mathbb{A}}
and a designated set
D
, whenever
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket^{\mathbb{A}}\in D
. We denote this fact by
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket^{\mathbb{A}})}\models_{D}\varphi
(or simply
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models\varphi
, when the algebra
\mathbb{A}
, the assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket^{\mathbb{A}}
, and the designated set
D
are clear from the context).

From now on, we use the notation
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket
to refer to the assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket^{\mathbb{A}}
, when the algebra
\mathbb{A}
is clear from the context.

Notice that, the validity of a set theoretic sentence not only depends on the designated set, but also on the assignment function.
Indeed, different assignment functions may have different interpretations for
\in
and
=
. Then, these differences can be extended recursively to all formulas of set theory.

1.3.
Assignment functions with two different interpretations for
\in
and
=

Let
\mathbb{A}=\langle\mathbf{A},\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,^{*},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\rangle

We then recursively define two different assignment functions:

(i)

\displaystyle\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(v(x)\land\llbracket x=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\big),

\displaystyle\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\big)\land\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(v(y)\Rightarrow\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\big);

(ii)

\displaystyle\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(v(x)\land\llbracket x=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big),

\displaystyle\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big((u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\wedge(\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow u(x)^{*})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\wedge\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big((v(y)\Rightarrow\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\wedge(\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow v(y)^{*})\big);

for any two closed well-formed formulas

\displaystyle\llbracket\top\rrbracket_{X}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1},

\displaystyle\llbracket\bot\rrbracket_{X}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0},

\displaystyle\llbracket\varphi\land\psi\rrbracket_{X}

\displaystyle=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{X}\land\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{X},

\displaystyle\llbracket\varphi\lor\psi\rrbracket_{X}

\displaystyle\llbracket\varphi\rightarrow\psi\rrbracket_{X}

\displaystyle=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{X}\Rightarrow\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{X},

\displaystyle\llbracket\neg\varphi\rrbracket_{X}

\displaystyle=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{X}^{*},

\displaystyle\llbracket\forall x\varphi(x)\rrbracket_{X}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}}\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{X}\mbox{, and}

Definition 2.8
.

An algebra

is called a
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra if

(i)

\langle\mathbf{A},\wedge,\vee,\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\rangle
is a complete distributive lattice,

(ii)

if
\bigvee\limits_{i\in I}a_{i}=\mathbf{1}

, where

is an index set and

(iii)

if
\bigwedge\limits_{i\in I}a_{i}=\mathbf{0}
, then there exists
j\in I
such that
a_{j}=\mathbf{0}
, where
I
is an index set and
a_{i}\in A
for all
i\in I

(iv)

the binary operator
\Rightarrow
is defined as follows: for
a,b\in A

\displaystyle a\Rightarrow b

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{0},&\mbox{if }a\neq\mathbf{0}\mbox{ and }b=\mathbf{0};\\
\mathbf{1},&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Notice that a
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra does not contain the unary operator
∗

\lnot
of
\mathcal{L}_{\in}
. We then extend the structure of
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra by introducing a unary operator

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=\langle\mathbf{A},\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,^{*},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\rangle

\mathbb{A}
. The pair

is called a

\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra if

(i)

\langle\mathbf{A},\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\rangle
is a
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra,

(ii)

the operator
∗
is defined as follows: for
a,b\in A

\displaystyle a^{*}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{0},&\mbox{if }a=\mathbf{1};\\
a,&\mbox{if }a\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\};\\
\mathbf{1},&\mbox{if }a\notin D.\end{array}\right.

A designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra
(\mathbb{A},D)
is called an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra, if the corresponding designated set

is an ultrafilter.

The main result of this paper, Theorem
3.17

\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra-valued models.

Fact 2.10
.

Every
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra is a deductive reasonable implication algebra, but the converse is not true in general.

Proof.

The proof follows from the definitions. On the other hand, a counter example for the converse part could be any complete Boolean algebra, having more than two elements.
∎

Fact 2.11
.

Every
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra
\mathbb{A}
has a unique atom (i.e. an element

for which there is no

such that
\mathbf{0}<x<a
) and a unique coatom (i.e. an element

x\in\mathbb{A}
such that

Proof.

Let

, where
\mathbf{A}
is the underlying set of
\mathbb{A}
. Then by the definition of
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra,
\bigvee S\neq\mathbf{1}
. Let

is a coatom. It is unique as well, otherwise the join of two different coatoms would be

\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra.

Similarly, we can prove that if

\bigwedge X

\mathbb{A}

∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. Then,

Suppose otherwise, so there exists a non-zero element

Arbitrarily fix two elements

\forall x\forall y\forall z\big(((z\in x\leftrightarrow z\in y)\wedge(\neg(z\in x)\leftrightarrow\neg(z\in y)))\to x=y\big).

Lemma 2.15
.

For any complete Boolean algebra
\mathbb{B}
and
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{B})}
,

(i)

\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
and

(ii)

\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
.

Proof.

(i) The proof will be done using the meta-induction principle on sets. Let us choose an arbitrary element
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{B})}
and assume that for any
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
,
\llbracket x=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\llbracket x=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
, for all
v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{B})}
. Now, we prove that
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
.

\displaystyle\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big((u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\wedge(\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow u(x)^{*})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\wedge\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big((v(y)\Rightarrow\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\wedge(\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow v(y)^{*})\big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\wedge\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(v(y)\Rightarrow\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big),

(since for any two elements
a
and
b
of
\mathbb{B}
,
a\Rightarrow b=b^{*}\Rightarrow a^{*}
)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big(u(x)\Rightarrow\bigvee_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}(v(y)\wedge\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\wedge\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(v(y)\Rightarrow\bigvee_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}(u(x)\wedge\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\big),

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big(u(x)\Rightarrow\bigvee_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}(v(y)\wedge\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\wedge\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(v(y)\Rightarrow\bigvee_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}(u(x)\wedge\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big),

(by the induction hypothesis)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\big)\wedge\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(v(y)\Rightarrow\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\big),

\displaystyle=\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}

Hence, by induction we can conclude that for any

(ii) Let us take any
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{B})}

\displaystyle\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}(v(y)\wedge\llbracket y=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}(v(y)\wedge\llbracket y=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}),\mbox{ by using (i)}

\displaystyle=\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}

This completes the proof.

(ii)

(iii)

\mathbb{A}
and a subset
\{a_{i}:i\in I\}
of the domain of
\mathbb{A}
,
f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigwedge_{i\in I}a_{i}\big)=\bigwedge_{i\in I}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{i})\big)
and
f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigvee_{i\in I}a_{i}\big)=\bigvee_{i\in I}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{i})\big)
, where
I
is an index set.

Proof.

We first prove
f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigwedge_{i\in I}a_{i}\big)=\bigwedge_{i\in I}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{i})\big)
. If
\bigwedge_{i\in I}a_{i}=\mathbf{1}
, then all
a_{i}=\mathbf{1}
. This implies
f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{i})=\mathbf{1}
, for all
i\in I
. Hence,

f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigwedge_{i\in I}a_{i}\big)=f_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{1})=1=\bigwedge_{i\in I}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{i})\big).

If
\bigwedge_{i\in I}a_{i}\in\mathbf{A}\setminus\{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\}
, then there exists
j\in I
so that
f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{j})\in\mathbf{A}\setminus\{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\}
and
f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{i})\in\{1,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\}
, for all
i\in I
. Hence,

f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigwedge_{i\in I}a_{i}\big)=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}=\bigwedge_{i\in I}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{i})\big).

If
\bigwedge_{i\in I}a_{i}=\mathbf{0}
, then by the definition of
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra, there exists
j\in I
such that
a_{j}=\mathbf{0}
. So
f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{j})=0
. Hence,

f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigwedge_{i\in I}a_{i}\big)=f_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathbf{0})=0=\bigwedge_{i\in I}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{i})\big).

To prove
f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigvee_{i\in I}a_{i}\big)=\bigvee_{i\in I}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(a_{i})\big)
, we can use the similar arguments and the definition of
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

For a given
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra
\mathbb{A}
and an element
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
an element
\bar{u}\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{PS}_{3})}
is defined as follows:

\bar{u}:=\{\langle\bar{x},f_{\mathbb{A}}(a)\rangle:\langle x,a\rangle\in u\}.

Intuitively,
\bar{u}
is formed by replacing all the entries of
\mathbf{1}
and
\mathbf{0}
in the formation of
u
by 1 and 0, respectively;
and similarly by replacing all the occurrences of any element
a\in\mathbf{A}\setminus\{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\}
in
u
by
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
.
Hence, for any
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
there exists a unique
\bar{u}\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{PS}_{3})}
and conversely, for any
\bar{u}\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{PS}_{3})}
there exists at least one
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
(more than one if
\mathbb{A}
contains more than three elements).

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. Then,
f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})=\llbracket\bar{u}=\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
, for all
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
.

Proof.

We shall prove the theorem using the (meta) induction. Let the theorem hold for all elements
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
, i.e.,
f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket x=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})=\llbracket\bar{x}=\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
, for all
v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. It is now sufficient to prove that
f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})=\llbracket\bar{u}=\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
, for all
v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. Using Lemma
3.2
in the necessary steps we have,

\displaystyle f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\land\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}(v(y)\Rightarrow\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(u(x))\Rightarrow f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)\land\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(v(y))\Rightarrow f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(u(x))\Rightarrow\bigvee_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(v(y))\wedge f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)\big)\land

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\bigwedge_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(v(y))\Rightarrow\bigvee_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(u(x))\wedge f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(\bar{u})}\big(\bar{u}(\bar{x})\Rightarrow\bigvee_{\bar{y}\in\mathrm{dom}(\bar{v})}\big(\bar{v}(\bar{y})\wedge(\llbracket\bar{x}=\bar{y}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)\big)\land

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\bigwedge_{\bar{y}\in\mathrm{dom}(\bar{v})}\big(\bar{v}(\bar{y})\Rightarrow\bigvee_{\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(\bar{u})}\big(\bar{u}(\bar{x})\wedge(\llbracket\bar{x}=\bar{y}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(\bar{u})}\big(\bar{u}(\bar{x})\Rightarrow\llbracket\bar{x}\in\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\big)\land\bigwedge_{\bar{y}\in\mathrm{dom}(\bar{v})}\big(\bar{v}(\bar{y})\Rightarrow\llbracket\bar{y}\in\bar{u}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\big)

\displaystyle=\llbracket\bar{u}=\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}.

Hence, applying (meta) induction the theorem is proved.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. Then,
f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})=\llbracket\bar{u}\in\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
, for all
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
.

Proof.

We have the following:

\displaystyle f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigvee_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}(v(y)\wedge\llbracket y=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(v(y))\wedge f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket y=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})\big)

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{\bar{y}\in\mathrm{dom}(\bar{v})}\big(\bar{v}(\bar{y})\wedge\llbracket\bar{y}=\bar{u}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\big),\mbox{ by Theorem \ref{theorem: cobounded equality}}

\displaystyle=\llbracket\bar{u}\in\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}.

∎

In simple words,
Theorems
3.4
and
3.5
states that for any two elements
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
,

(i)

if
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}=\mathbf{1}
, then
\llbracket\bar{u}=\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}=1
,

(ii)

if
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}=\mathbf{0}
, then
\llbracket\bar{u}=\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}=0
,

(iii)

if
\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}=\mathbf{1}
, then
\llbracket\bar{u}\in\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}=1
,

(iv)

if
\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\in\mathbf{A}\setminus\{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\}
, then
\llbracket\bar{u}\in\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
,

(v)

if
\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}=\mathbf{0}
, then
\llbracket\bar{u}\in\bar{v}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}=0
.

This result will be extended to any negation-free sentence of the extended language
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
. To simplify notation we can use the expressions
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}
and
\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{PS}_{3}}
to indicate the value of the sentence
\varphi
under
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
-assignment function in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
and
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{PS}_{3})}
, respectively.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. For any negation-free sentence
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
,
f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}})=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{PS}_{3}}
.

Proof.

The theorem will be proved using mathematical induction on the complexity of the negation-free sentence
\varphi
of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
.

The Base Case is proved in Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
.

Suppose the theorem holds for any sentence having complexity
n
, where
n
is a natural number.

Let
\varphi
be a sentence having complexity
n+1
. Then, the following cases may occur.

(i)

Case I: Let
\varphi:=\psi\wedge\gamma
. Then,

\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}=\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}\wedge\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}=\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{PS}_{3}}\wedge\llbracket\gamma\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{PS}_{3}}\mbox{ (by induction hypothesis) }=\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{PS}_{3}}.

(ii)

Case II: Let
\varphi:=\psi\vee\gamma
. The proof is similar to the proof of Case I.

(iii)

Case III: Let
\varphi:=\psi\Rightarrow\gamma
. This proof is also similar to Case I.

(iv)

Case IV: Let
\varphi:=\forall x\psi(x)
. Then,

\displaystyle f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}})

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigwedge_{u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}}\llbracket\psi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}\big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}}f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\llbracket\psi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}\big),\mbox{ by Lemma \ref{lemma: completeness of the homomorphism}}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{\bar{u}\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{PS}_{3})}}f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\llbracket\psi(\bar{u})\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{PS}_{3}}\big),\mbox{ by Lemma \ref{lemma: completeness of the homomorphism}}

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(\bigwedge_{\bar{u}\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{PS}_{3})}}\llbracket\psi(\bar{u})\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{PS}_{3}}\big)

\displaystyle=f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket\varphi\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{PS}_{3}})

(v)

Case V: Let
\varphi:=\exists x\psi(x)
. The proof is same as the proof of Case IV.

Hence, by using mathematical induction we can conclude that the theorem holds for all negation-free sentences of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra and
D
be any designated set of it. Then,
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},\;\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\models_{D}\mathrm{NFF}
-
\mathsf{ZF}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{Ax}
be an axiom of
\mathrm{NFF}
-
\mathsf{ZF}
. Then, it is of the form
Q_{1}x_{1}\ldots Q_{n}x_{n}(\varphi\to\psi)
, where
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{n}
are quantifiers.
Therefore,
\llbracket\mathsf{Ax}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}
is either
\mathbf{1}
or
\mathbf{0}
.
If
\llbracket\mathsf{Ax}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}=\mathbf{0}
, then
f_{\mathbb{A}}(\llbracket\mathsf{Ax}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}})=0
and hence by Theorem
3.6
,
\llbracket\mathsf{Ax}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{PS}_{3}}=0
.
This leads to a contradiction since
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{PS}_{3},\;\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\models_{D}\mathrm{NFF}
-
\mathsf{ZF}
.
Hence,
\llbracket\mathsf{Ax}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}=\mathbf{1}
and therefore for any designated set
D
,
\llbracket\mathsf{Ax}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}^{\mathbb{A}}\in D
. This completes the proof.
∎

Let us now consider any designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra
(\mathbb{A},D)
, having more than two elements. Then by definition, there is a unary operator
∗
in the structure of
\mathbb{A}
. One may ask whether
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},\;\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}
validates all
\mathsf{ZF}
-axioms instead of only its negation-free fragment:
\mathrm{NFF}
-
\mathsf{ZF}
.

Consider a designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra
(\mathbb{A},D)
,
where
\mathbf{A}
is an ordinal number
\geq 3
. Notice that,
\mathbf{A}
can neither be a limit ordinal (because
\mathbb{A}
needs to have a top element) nor a successor of a limit ordinal (because
\mathbb{A}
needs to haev a coatom). Then, one can be prove that there exists a formula
\varphi(x):=\lnot\exists y(y\in x)
such that
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},\;\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\not\models_{D}\mathsf{Separation}_{\varphi}

[
Tarafder, 2022
, Theorem 59]
. Hence in general, a designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra-valued model cannot validate the full
\mathsf{ZF}
under the
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
-assignment function.

3.3.
Set-theoretic properties of
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra-valued models under
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

The
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}
assignment function considerably affects the way we look at the elements of
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra-valued models. First of all, notice that for any designated
Cobounded
-algebra
(\mathbb{A},D)
and any
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, either
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
or
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{0}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Consider any ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra
(\mathbb{A},D)
and any two elements
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. Then,
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\models_{D}u=v
if and only if the following holds: if
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
is such that
u(x)\in D
then there exists
y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
such that
v(y)\in D
and
\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
and vice-versa; moreover, if
u(x)=\mathbf{1}
, then there exists
y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
such that
v(y)=\mathbf{1}
and
\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
and vice-versa.

Proof.

Since
D
is an ultrafilter of
\mathbb{A}
, by Lemma
2.12
we have that
\mathbf{A}\setminus D=\{\mathbf{0}\}
, where
\mathbf{A}
is the underlying set of
\mathbb{A}
. Let us consider two elements
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
such that
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\models_{D}u=v
. Then, the following two cases, Case I and Case II are sufficient to prove the property of the theorem.

Case I: Suppose there exists an element
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
such that
u(x)=\mathbf{1}
. Then, it is clear that
u(x)^{*}=\mathbf{0}
. If there is no
y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
such that
v(y)=\mathbf{1}
and
\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, we get
\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}<\mathbf{1}
and hence
\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\in D
.
This concludes that,
\big(\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow u(x)^{*}\big)=\mathbf{0}
, which contradicts the assumption.
Similarly, if there exists
y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
such that
v(y)=\mathbf{1}
, then there also exists
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
such that
u(x)=\mathbf{1}
and
\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, otherwise
\big(\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow v(y)^{*}\big)=\mathbf{0}
, and hence our assumption fails.

Case II: Suppose there exists an element
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
such that
u(x)\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}
. Towards the contradiction, suppose there does not exist any
y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
such that
v(y)\in D
and
\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, i.e., for every
y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
either
v(y)=\mathbf{0}
or
\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{0}
. Hence,
\big(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)=\mathbf{0}
, which implies that
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{0}
.

Conversely, let the condition hold.
Consider an arbitrary
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
. If
u(x)=\mathbf{0}
, then immediately we get,

(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\wedge(\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow u(x)^{*})\in D.

Now, suppose
u(x)\in D
. Then, by our assumption
\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. Hence, the first conjunct of the algebraic expression of
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}
,
(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\in D
.
For the second conjunct, we consider two cases:
u(x)\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}
and
u(x)=\mathbf{1}
.
If
u(x)\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}
, then
u(x)^{*}\in D
and
\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
by our assumption. On the other hand, if
u(x)=\mathbf{1}
, then by our assumption,
\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
, so
\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}=\mathbf{0}
. Hence, in both the cases we have

(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\wedge(\llbracket x\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow u(x)^{*})\in D.

A similar argument shows that, for any
y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
,

(v(y)\Rightarrow\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\wedge(\llbracket y\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow v(y)^{*})\in D.

This leads to the fact that,
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\models_{D}u=v
.
∎

We now prove in Observation
3.9
and Observation
3.10
that the assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}
is finer than the assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
in validating the equality and set-membership relation.

Observation 3.9
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra and
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
be any two elements. If
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\models_{D}u=v
, then
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\models_{D}u=v
. But the converse may not be true.

Proof.

It is not hard to check that, for any
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
,
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\in D
if and only if the following hold:
if
u(x)\in D
then there exists
v(y)\in D
such that
\llbracket x=y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\in D
, and vice-versa.
Then, applying Theorem
3.8
we can conclude that, if
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\models_{D}u=v
, then
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\models_{D}u=v
.

The converse does not always hold: fix two elements
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
such that,
u=\{\langle\ w,\mathbf{1}\rangle\}
,
v=\{\langle w,a\rangle\}
, where
w\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
is an arbitrary element and
a\in\mathbb{A}
is such that
\mathbf{0}<a<\mathbf{1}
.
Then,
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\models_{D}u=v
, but Theorem
3.8
ensures that,
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\not\models_{D}u=v
.
∎

Observation 3.10
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra and
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
be any two elements. If
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\models_{D}u\in v
, then
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\models_{D}u\in v
. But the converse may not be true.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\models_{D}u\in v
holds, for two elements
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. Then, there exists
t\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
such that
v(t)\in D
and
\llbracket u=t\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. By Observation
3.9
, it can be concluded that
\llbracket u=t\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\in D
as well. Hence we have
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\models_{D}u\in v
.

The converse is not always true, as the following example shows.
Let us first consider two elements
u=\{\langle w,\mathbf{1}\rangle\}
and
t=\{\langle w,a\rangle\}
, where
w\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
and
a\in\mathbb{A}
is an arbitrary element such that
\mathbf{0}<a<\mathbf{1}
. Then, take the element

v=\{\langle t,\mathbf{1}\rangle\}
in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. As already observed in the proof of Observation
3.9
we have that
\llbracket u=t\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}\in D
, but
\llbracket u=t\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\notin D
. Hence we get,
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\models_{D}u\in v
, however
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\not\models_{D}u\in v
.
∎

The following set-theoretic properties displayed in Lemma
3.11
, hold for both, Boolean and Heyting-valued models, equipped with the assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}}
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. Then,
for any
u,v,w\in\mathbf{V}^{\mathbb{(A)}}
the following hold:

(i)

\llbracket u=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
,

(ii)

for any
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
, if
u(x)\in D
, then
\llbracket x\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
,

(iii)

if
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket v=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, then
\llbracket u=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
,

(iv)

if
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket v\in w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, then
\llbracket u\in w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
,

(v)

if
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, then
\llbracket w\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
.

Proof.

(i)
Consider any
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
such that
u(x)\in D
. Let us consider the following two cases:

In the first case, by Theorem

Thus:

(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\wedge(\llbracket x\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow u(x)^{*})\in D.

In the second case, since

\llbracket x\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

(u(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket x\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\wedge(\llbracket x\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow u(x)^{*})\in D.

We may conclude
\llbracket u=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

.

(ii)
Let
u(x)\in D
, then we have

\llbracket x\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\geq(u(x)\wedge\llbracket x=x\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\in D,

since
\llbracket x=x\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

(i)

(iii)
By induction on the domain of
w
. Assume that for all

we have:

\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket v=z\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D\mbox{ implies }\llbracket u=z\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

Case I: Let
x_{1},...,x_{n}\in\mathrm{dom}(u)

u(x_{i})=\mathbf{1}
,

. Then by Theorem
3.8
, there exist

\llbracket x_{i}=y_{i}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
,

\llbracket y_{i}=z_{i}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
,

\llbracket x_{i}=z_{i}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
,
i=1,...,n
.
Case II: Let there exist
x_{1}^{\prime},...,x_{k}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
such that
u(x_{i}^{\prime})\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}
,
i=1,...,k
. Then by Theorem
3.8
, there exist
y_{1}^{\prime},...,y_{k}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(v)

v(y_{i}^{\prime})\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}

\llbracket x_{i}^{\prime}=y_{i}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
,

. Since,
\llbracket v=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, as well, there exist
z_{1}^{\prime},...,z_{k}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(w)

By induction hypothesis,
\llbracket x_{i}^{\prime}=z_{i}^{\prime}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

(iv)

\displaystyle\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket u\in w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

\displaystyle=\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\bigvee_{z\in\mathrm{dom}(w)}(w(z)\wedge\llbracket z=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{z\in\mathrm{dom}(w)}w(z)\wedge(\llbracket z=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})

Hence,
\llbracket z=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

\llbracket z=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

\bigvee_{z\in\mathrm{dom}(w)}\big(w(z)\wedge\llbracket z=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)=\llbracket v\in w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

(v)
Suppose, both
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

3.8

.

Hence,

∎

Lemma 3.12
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. For any
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
and any formula
\varphi(x)
in

having one free variable
x

\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

(i)

if
\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
then
\llbracket\varphi(v)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
,

(ii)

if
\mathbf{0}<\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}<\mathbf{1}
then
\mathbf{0}<\llbracket\varphi(v)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}<\mathbf{1}
.

Proof.

We divide the base case into the following three cases:
BS I
,
BS II
, and
BS III
.

BS I:

Let
\varphi(x):=w=x
, where
w\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. If
\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
, then by Theorem
3.11
(iii)
we have that
\llbracket\varphi(v)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
, as for any
p,q\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, either
\llbracket p=q\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
or
\llbracket p=q\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{0}
.

Condition
(ii)
is vacuously true in this case.

BS II:

Let
\varphi(x):=w\in x
, where
w\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
.
Suppose
\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
.
Then, there exists
p\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
such that
u(p)=\mathbf{1}
and
\llbracket p=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
. Since
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, by Theorem
3.8
, there exists
q\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
satisfying
v(q)=\mathbf{1}
and
\llbracket p=q\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
.
By Theorem
3.11
(iii)
,
\llbracket q=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, i.e.,
\llbracket q=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
. Hence
\llbracket\varphi(v)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
.

Suppose
\mathbf{0}<\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}<\mathbf{1}
, i.e.,

\mathbf{0}<\bigvee_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)}(u(x)\wedge\llbracket x=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})<\mathbf{1}.

This can only be the case if;

(1)

There exists
x_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
such that
\mathbf{0}<u(x_{1})<\mathbf{1}
and
\llbracket x_{1}=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
.

(2)

For any
x\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
, if
u(x)=\mathbf{1}
then
\llbracket x=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{0}
.

Now,

\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket=\bigvee_{y\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}(v(y)\wedge\llbracket y=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}).

Since
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
and (1) holds, by Theorem
3.8
we can say that there exists a
y_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
such that
v(y_{1})\in D
and
\llbracket y_{1}=x_{1}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
. Thus by using Lemma
3.11
(iii),
\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, i.e.,
\mathbf{0}<\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}
.
We shall now prove that
\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}<\mathbf{1}
. Suppose otherwise, then by Definition
2.9
(iii) there exists
y_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(v)
such that
v(y_{1})=\mathbf{1}=\llbracket y_{1}=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}
.
Since
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
by Theorem
3.8
there exists a
x_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
such that
u(x_{1})=\mathbf{1}=\llbracket x_{1}=y_{1}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}
.
By Lemma
3.11
(iii)
, we have also
\llbracket x_{1}=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
. This contradicts that

. Hence we get,
\mathbf{0}<\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket<\mathbf{1}
.

BS III:

Let
\varphi(x):=x\in w
, where
w\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. Suppose
\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}

, i.e.,
\llbracket v\in w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}

Suppose
\mathbf{0}<\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}<\mathbf{1}
, i.e.,

\mathbf{0}<\bigvee_{z\in\mathrm{dom}(w)}(w(z)\wedge\llbracket u=z\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})<\mathbf{1}.

This can only be the case if;

(1)

There exists
z_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(w)
such that
\mathbf{0}<w(z_{1})<\mathbf{1}
and
\llbracket z_{1}=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
.

(2)

For any
z\in\mathrm{dom}(w)

\llbracket z=u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{0}

By Lemma

Induction hypothesis

Case II:

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. Then, we have
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\models_{D}\overline{\mathsf{ZF}}

Proof.

\overline{\mathsf{Extensionality}}
: Consider any

\llbracket\forall z\big((z\in x\leftrightarrow z\in y)\wedge(\neg(z\in x)\leftrightarrow\neg(z\in y))\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

Then, we have to prove that
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
as well. By our assumption, for any

\llbracket w\in u\leftrightarrow w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}=\llbracket\neg(w\in u)\leftrightarrow\neg(w\in v)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}
.
This can only be the case if for any
w\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
,

(1)

\llbracket w\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D\mbox{ iff }\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
and,

(2)

\llbracket w\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\in D\mbox{ iff }\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\in D
.

From (1) and (2) we have for any
w\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
,

(i)

\llbracket w\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}\mbox{ iff }\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
,

(ii)

\mathbf{0}<\llbracket w\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}<\mathbf{1}\mbox{ iff }\mathbf{0}<\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}<\mathbf{1}
,

(iii)

\llbracket w\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{0}\mbox{ iff }\llbracket w\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{0}
.

Let
x_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
be such that
u(x_{1})=\mathbf{1}
, so
\llbracket x_{1}\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
. By (i), there exists a

such that

and
\llbracket x_{1}=y_{1}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
. Let

, so

.
Now, applying Theorem

\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}

\llbracket\overline{\mathsf{Extensionality}}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}

\displaystyle\llbracket\forall w\big(

\llbracket\forall w\big((w=x\vee w=y)\rightarrow w\in z\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\bigwedge_{w\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}}\big(\llbracket(w=x\vee w=y)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\Rightarrow\llbracket w\in z\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\in D.

\llbracket\forall w\big((w=x\vee w=y)\rightarrow w\in z\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

\llbracket\mathsf{Pairing}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
.

\check{w_{0}}\in\mathrm{dom}(\check{\omega})

in

\mathsf{Union}:
We have to prove, for any
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, there exists an element
v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
such that
\llbracket\forall x\big(x\in v\leftrightarrow\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. Indeed, it is sufficient to show that,

\llbracket\forall x\big(x\in v\rightarrow\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket\forall x\big(\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\to x\in v\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

Take any
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
and define
v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, as follows:

\mathrm{dom}(v)=\bigcup\{\mathrm{dom}(y)\mid y\in\mathrm{dom}(u)\}\mbox{ and }v(x)=\llbracket\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}},\mbox{ for }x\in\mathrm{dom}(v).

We first prove that,

\llbracket\forall x\big(x\in v\rightarrow\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D,

By using
\mathrm{BQ}_{\varphi}
, we have

\displaystyle\llbracket\forall x\big(x\in v\rightarrow\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(v(x)\Rightarrow\llbracket\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{x\in\mathrm{dom}(v)}\big(\llbracket\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\Rightarrow\llbracket\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)

\displaystyle\in D,\mbox{ since $a\Rightarrow a=\mathbf{1}\in D$, for any element $a\in\mathbb{A}$.}

We shall now show that
\llbracket\forall x\big(\exists y(y\in u\wedge x\in y)\to x\in v\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. Let
x_{0}\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
be an element such that

\llbracket\exists y(y\in u\wedge x_{0}\in y)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. The proof will be completed if it can be derived that
\llbracket x_{0}\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, as well.
By definition,
\llbracket\exists y(y\in u\wedge x_{0}\in y)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
implies that, there exists
y_{0}\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
such that
\llbracket y_{0}\in u~\wedge~x_{0}\in y_{0}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. Now,
\llbracket y_{0}\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D_{\mathbb{T}}
guarantees an element
y_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
such that
u(y_{1})\in D
and
\llbracket y_{1}=y_{0}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
.
So, we have,
\llbracket y_{1}=y_{0}~\wedge~x_{0}\in y_{0}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
and by using Lemma
3.11
(v)
it can be concluded that
\llbracket x_{0}\in y_{1}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. Hence, there exists
x_{1}\in\mathrm{dom}(y_{1})
satisfying
y_{1}(x_{1})\in D
and
\llbracket x_{0}=x_{1}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
.
Since, by our assumption,
u(y_{1})\in D
and
y_{1}(x_{1})\in D
, applying Lemma
3.11
(ii)
both
\llbracket y_{1}\in u\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
and
\llbracket x_{1}\in y_{1}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
hold. Hence,
\llbracket y_{1}\in u~\wedge~x_{1}\in y_{1}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, which leads to the fact that
\llbracket\exists y(y\in u~\wedge~x_{1}\in y)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
, i.e.,
v(x_{1})\in D
.
So, we have derived that,
\llbracket x_{0}=x_{1}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}},~v(x_{1})\in D
. Hence,
\llbracket x_{0}\in v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
.

Therefore, we can conclude
\llbracket\mathsf{Union}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
.

\mathsf{Powerset}:
We have to prove that for any
x\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
there exists a
y\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
such that

\big(\llbracket\forall z\big(z\in y\rightarrow\forall w(w\in z\to w\in x)\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket\forall z\big(\forall w(w\in z\rightarrow w\in x)\rightarrow z\in y\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\in D.

and define
y
such that

The first conjunct can be proved by an immediate application of the property
\mathrm{BQ}_{\varphi}
.

We now show that,

\bigwedge_{z\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}}\llbracket\big(\forall w(w\in z\rightarrow w\in x)\rightarrow z\in y\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

Let us fix an arbitrary
z\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. Then,

\displaystyle\llbracket

\displaystyle\forall w(w\in z\rightarrow w\in x)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\Rightarrow\llbracket z\in y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{w\in\mathrm{dom}(z)}\big(z(w)\Rightarrow\llbracket w\in x\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\Rightarrow\bigvee_{q\in\mathrm{dom}(y)}\big(y(q)\wedge\llbracket z=q\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big),\mbox{ using the property $\mathrm{BQ}_{\varphi}$}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{w\in\mathrm{dom}(z)}\big(z(w)\Rightarrow\llbracket w\in x\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\Rightarrow\bigvee_{q\in\mathrm{dom}(y)}\big(\bigwedge_{p\in\mathrm{dom}(q)}\big(q(p)\Rightarrow\llbracket p\in x\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\wedge

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\bigwedge_{p\in\mathrm{dom}(q)}\big(q(p)\Rightarrow\llbracket p\in z\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\wedge\bigwedge_{p\in\mathrm{dom}(q)}\big(\llbracket p\in z\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow q(p)^{*}\big)\big).

Let us assume that,

(
\dagger
)

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{w\in\mathrm{dom}(z)}\big(z(w)\Rightarrow\llbracket w\in x\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\neq\mathbf{0}.

Then it is enough to show that there exists an element
q^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(y)
so that,

(i)

\bigwedge\limits_{p\in\mathrm{dom}(q^{\prime})}\big(q^{\prime}(p)\Rightarrow\llbracket p\in x\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\neq\mathbf{0}
,

(ii)

,

(iii)

,

(iv)

\bigwedge\limits_{p\in\mathrm{dom}(q^{\prime})}\big(q^{\prime}(p)\Rightarrow\llbracket p\in z\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\big)\neq\mathbf{0}
, and

(v)

\bigwedge\limits_{p\in\mathrm{dom}(q^{\prime})}\big(\llbracket p\in z\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}^{*}\Rightarrow q^{\prime}(p)^{*}\big)\neq\mathbf{0}
.

q^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(y)
such that

, for all
p\in\mathrm{dom}(q^{\prime})
. We now prove that all the properties (i) - (v) hold for the element
q^{\prime}\in\mathrm{dom}(y)
.

(i)

Notice that due to
(\dagger)

\llbracket\forall w(w\in z\rightarrow w\in x)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\neq\mathbf{0}

\displaystyle\mbox{i.e.,}\bigwedge_{w\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}}\big(\llbracket w\in z\rrbracket\Rightarrow\llbracket w\in x\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\neq\mathbf{0}

(ii)

In order to show that
\bigwedge\limits_{w\in\mathrm{dom}(z)}\big(z(w)\Rightarrow\llbracket w\in q^{\prime}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\big)\in D
, if
z(w)=\mathbf{0}
, then we are done. So, let
z(w)\neq\mathbf{0}
. Then, due to
(\dagger)
we can conclude that
\llbracket w\in x\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\neq\mathbf{0}
, i.e.,

\bigvee_{p\in\mathrm{dom}(x)}(x(p)\wedge\llbracket p=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\neq\mathbf{0}.

. Hence,
\llbracket p^{\prime}\in z\rrbracket\geq(z(w)\wedge\llbracket p^{\prime}=w\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\neq\mathbf{0}
by our assumptions and by Definition
2.9
(iv). Hence, we get the following,

\displaystyle\llbracket w\in q^{\prime}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

2.9
(iv).

\displaystyle\llbracket w^{\prime}\in q^{\prime}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

.

\mathsf{Separation}:
Let
\varphi(x)
be any formula in
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbb{A}}
, where

x\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}

y\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}

\llbracket\forall z\big(z\in y\leftrightarrow(z\in x\wedge\varphi(z))\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

\llbracket\forall z\big(z\in y\rightarrow(z\in x\wedge\varphi(z))\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\wedge\llbracket\forall z\big((z\in x\wedge\varphi(z))\rightarrow z\in y\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

y\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}

\llbracket\forall z\big(z\in y\rightarrow(z\in x\wedge\varphi(z))\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

\mathrm{BQ}_{\varphi}
, we have

\displaystyle\llbracket\forall z\big(z\in y\rightarrow(z\in x\wedge\varphi(z))\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

,

\displaystyle\bigwedge_{z\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}}\llbracket(z\in x\wedge\varphi(z))\rightarrow z\in y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

z_{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(x)

. Then by construction of

3.11
(ii)
\llbracket z_{0}\in y\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. Therefore, we can conclude that for any

,
\big(x(z_{0})\Rightarrow\llbracket(\varphi(z_{0})\Rightarrow z_{0}\in y)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})\big)\in D
. Hence,

\llbracket\forall z\big((z\in x\wedge\varphi(z))\rightarrow(z\in y)\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

\mathsf{Separation}

D
under the assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}
, we conclude that
\llbracket\mathsf{Separation}\rrbracket\in D
.

\mathsf{Collection}:
Let
\varphi(x,y)
be any formula in the language of set theory with two free variables. We want to proof that for every

we have

\llbracket\forall x\big(x\in u\to\exists y\varphi(x,y)\big)\rightarrow\exists v\forall x\big(x\in u\to\exists y(y\in v\wedge\varphi(x,y))\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

Take any
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}

\llbracket\forall x\big(x\in u\to\exists y\varphi(x,y)\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D

\llbracket\forall x\big(x\in u\rightarrow\exists y(y\in v\wedge\varphi(x,y))\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

\mathbb{A}

\mathbb{A}\in\mathbf{V}
. Thus, we may apply

. By

. Then, it follows by Lemma

. Therefore,
\llbracket y_{0}\in v\wedge\varphi(x_{0},y_{0})\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
and thus,

\llbracket\exists y\big(y\in v\wedge\varphi(x_{0},y)\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

Since the choice of
x_{0}
is arbitrary we have,

\llbracket\forall x\big(x\in u\to\exists y(y\in v\wedge\varphi(x,y))\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

Therefore, we conclude that
\llbracket\mathsf{Collection}\rrbracket\in D
.

\mathsf{Foundation}:
We want to show that

\llbracket\forall x\big(\forall y(y\in x\to\varphi(y))\rightarrow\varphi(x)\big)\rightarrow\forall x\varphi(x)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

Take any
x\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
and consider the following two cases:

(i) Let
\llbracket\varphi(x)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
for every
x\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, which implies
\llbracket\forall x\varphi(x)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
.
Therefore, we get

\llbracket\forall x\big((\forall y(y\in x\to\varphi(y)))\rightarrow\varphi(x)\big)\rightarrow\forall x\varphi(x)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

(ii) Let
\llbracket\varphi(x)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\notin D
for some
x\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
.
Then, take a minimal
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
such that
\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\notin D
and for any
v\in\mathrm{dom}(u)
,
\llbracket\varphi(v)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. For this
u
, we claim that

\llbracket\forall y(y\in u\to\varphi(y))\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D.

Using
\mathrm{BQ}_{\varphi}
and our assumption, it is immediate that
\llbracket\forall y(y\in u\to\varphi(y))\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
. From this, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\llbracket\forall x\big((\forall y(y\in x\to\varphi(y))\to\varphi(x)\big)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

\displaystyle\leq\llbracket\big(\forall y(y\in u\to\varphi(y))\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\Rightarrow\llbracket\varphi(u)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}.

Hence, we can finally conclude that

\llbracket\forall x\big(\forall y(y\in x\to\varphi(y))\rightarrow\varphi(x)\big)\rightarrow\forall x\varphi(x)\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D,

i.e.,
\llbracket\mathsf{Foundation}\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}\in D
.

∎

4.
Logic(s) corresponding to an algebra
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}

Let
\mathsf{Prop}
and
\mathsf{Sent}
be the collection of all propositional formulas and all sentences of the language
\mathcal{L}_{\in}
, respectively.

Definition 4.1
.

Any homomorphism
\tau:\mathsf{Prop}\to\mathsf{Sent}
is said to be a translation function.

Then, following
[
Löwe et al., 2021
]
, we define the logic of the set theory
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
under a given assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket
, where
\mathbb{A}
is a complete distributive lattice.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}=\langle\mathbf{A},\wedge,\vee,\Rightarrow,^{*},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\rangle
be an algebra, where
\langle\mathbf{A},\wedge,\vee,\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\rangle
is a complete distributive lattice.
Corresponding to a given assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket
and a designated set
D
, the logic of the set theory
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, denoted by
\mathbf{L}(\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket,\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})})
, is the collection of all propositional formulas
\alpha
such that
\llbracket\tau(\alpha)\rrbracket\in D
, for all translation functions
\tau
, i.e.,

\mathbf{L}(\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket,\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}):=\{\alpha\in\mathsf{Prop}:\mbox{ for all translation function }\tau,\llbracket\tau(\alpha)\rrbracket\in D\}.

The logic of the algebra
\mathbb{A}
with respect to the designated set
D
, denoted by
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{A},D)
, is defined as follows:

\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{A},D):=\{\alpha\in\mathsf{Prop}:\mbox{ for all valuation }v,v(\alpha)\in D\}.

For any Boolean algebra
\mathbb{B}
,
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{B},\{\mathbf{1}\})
is nothing but the classical propositional logic. Since, the two-valued designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra
(\mathbb{A},D)
is the two-valued Boolean algebra,
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{A},D)
is the classical propositional logic. Note that in this case
D=\{\mathbf{1}\}
.

4.1.
Logic of a designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra

We shall now prove that, for any designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra
(\mathbb{A},D)
having at least two elements in
D
, the logic
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{A},D)
is
paraconsistent
:
for an algebra
\mathbb{A}
and a designated set
D
, the logic
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{A},D)
is said to be paraconsistent if there exist propositional formulas
\alpha
and
\beta
such that
(\alpha\wedge\neg\alpha)\to\beta\notin\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{A},D)
.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
(\mathbb{A},D)
is a designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra, where
D
contains at least two elements, then
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{A},D)
is paraconsistent.

Proof.

By the given condition
D\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}\neq\varnothing
. Let
a\in D\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}
. By the definition of designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra,
a^{*}=a
.
Consider two propositional formulas
\alpha
and
\beta
, and a valuation function
v
such that
v(\alpha)=a
and
v(\beta)=\mathbf{0}
.
Then,

v((\alpha\wedge\neg\alpha)\to\beta)=\mathbf{0}\notin D.

Hence,
(\alpha\wedge\neg\alpha)\to\beta\notin\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{A},D)
, proving that
\mathbf{L}(\mathbb{A},D)
is paraconsistent.
∎

In Section
3.1
, we introduced the algebra
\mathrm{PS}_{3}
having the designated set
\{1,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}\}
. A paraconsistent logic
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{PS}_{3}
was found which is sound and complete with respect to
\mathrm{PS}_{3}

[
Tarafder and Chakraborty, 2016
, Theorem 3.1 & 3.6]
.
We shall now prove that any ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra (which is not a Boolean algebra) is also sound and complete with respect to
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{PS}_{3}
.
Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. We can check that the function
f_{\mathbb{A}}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{PS}_{3}
remains a homomorphism as
f_{\mathbb{A}}(a^{*})=\big(f_{\mathbb{A}}(a)\big)^{*}
, for all
a\in\mathbf{A}
. We shall use this fact to prove the following soundness and completeness theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra having more than two elements. Then,
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{PS}_{3}
is sound and complete with respect to
(\mathbb{A},D)
.

Proof.

As usual, the proof of soundness is straight forward. We shall prove the completeness only.
For this, we first fix that
\mathsf{Prop}
denotes the collection of all propositional formulas.
Let
\varphi\in\mathsf{Prop}
be such that
\models_{\mathbb{A}}\varphi
, where
\models
has its usual meaning. If possible let
\not\models_{\mathrm{PS}_{3}}\varphi
.
Then, there exists a valuation
v_{\mathrm{PS}_{3}}:\mathsf{Prop}\to\mathrm{PS}_{3}
such that
v_{\mathrm{PS}_{3}}(\varphi)=0
.
Let
p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n}
be the propositional variables present in
\varphi
.
Let us consider a valuation
v_{\mathbb{A}}:\mathsf{Prop}\to\mathbb{A}
, which satisfies the following: for each
i=1,\ldots,n

\displaystyle v_{\mathbb{A}}(p_{i})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{1},&\mbox{if }v_{\mathrm{PS}_{3}}(p_{i})=1;\\
a,&\mbox{if }v_{\mathrm{PS}_{3}}(p_{i})=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}};\\
\mathbf{0},&\mbox{if }v_{\mathrm{PS}_{3}}(p_{i})=0,\end{array}\right.

where
a\in\mathbf{A}\setminus\{\mathbf{1},\mathbf{0}\}
is arbitrarily fixed; the existence of such an element is ensured by the fact that
\mathbf{A}
(the domain of
\mathbb{A}
) has more than two elements.
Then, by definition,
f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(v_{\mathbb{A}}(p_{i})\big)=v_{\mathrm{PS}_{3}}(p_{i})
, for all
i=1,\ldots,n
. Since
f_{\mathbb{A}}
is a homomorphism,
f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(v_{\mathbb{A}}(\varphi)\big)=v_{\mathrm{PS}_{3}}(\varphi)
.
Hence, by our assumption
f_{\mathbb{A}}\big(v_{\mathbb{A}}(\varphi)\big)=0
. This implies that
v_{\mathbb{A}}(\varphi)=\mathbf{0}
, which contradicts the fact that
\models_{\mathbb{A}}\varphi
. Therefore, we conclude that
\models_{\mathrm{PS}_{3}}\varphi
. Since
\mathbb{L}\mathrm{PS}_{3}
is complete with respect to
\mathrm{PS}_{3}
, finally we get that
\vdash\varphi
, where
\vdash
has its usual meaning. This completes the proof.
∎

There exists a formula
\varphi\in\mathsf{Sent}

\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{BA}})}\models_{D}\varphi\wedge\neg\varphi

(\mathbb{A},D)
, where

For any two

5.
The quotient space
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim

In order to simplify notation, in this section we drop the subscript from the assignment function
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}
and we will denote it only by
\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket
. Consequently, the validity of a sentence
\varphi
in a designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra-valued model
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
will be denoted by
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models\varphi
instead of
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A},~\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}})}\models\varphi
.

Definition 5.1
.

A class relation
\sim
in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
is defined as follows
u\sim v
iff
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models u=v
, for
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
.

Observation 5.2
.

The relation
``\sim"
is an equivalence relation on
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from the fact that for any
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, either
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{1}
or
\llbracket u=v\rrbracket_{\mathrm{PA}}=\mathbf{0}
and Theorem
3.8
.
∎

Once we prove that in a designated
Cobounded
-algebra-valued model
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
the relation
\sim
is an equivalence relation, then, we can construct the
quotient space

\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
, as in the classical case.
The elements of
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
are of the form
[u]=\{v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}:\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models u=v\}
, where
u\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
. Moreover, given the validity of
\mathsf{LL}_{\varphi}
in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, we can define a notion of satisfaction and validity of a formula in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
as follows:

Definition 5.3
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. Suppose
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
is a formula in
\mathcal{L}_{\in}
having the list (possibly empty) of free variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
,
then for any
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, the sequence
([u_{1}],\ldots,[u_{n}])
of
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
is said to satisfy the formula
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, denoted by
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models\varphi([u_{1}],\ldots,[u_{n}])
,
if
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models\varphi(u_{1},\ldots,u_{n})
holds. A formula is said to be valid in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
if it is satisfied by every sequence of
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
.

Observation 5.4
.

Using Theorem
3.14
, it can be checked that Definition
5.3
does not depend on the representatives.

By the definition of validity in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
, a sentence
\varphi
of
\mathcal{L}_{\in}
is valid in
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
if and only if it is valid in the algebra-valued model
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models\varphi
iff
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models\varphi
. Then, as a corollary of Theorem
3.17
, we get the following theorem.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. Then,
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models\overline{\mathsf{ZF}}
.

In the case of building quotient models out of Boolean-valued models, first the interpretation of the predicate symbols
\in
and
=
is given and only then a definition of the validity of the rest of the formulas is provided.
Contrary to this order, in this paper, we first define the validity of all formulas in Definition
5.3
and only later we provide the full interpretation of
\in
and
=
, which includes also the case of non-validity.

Definition 5.6
.

We define the following binary relations on
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
. For any
u,v\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}
,

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}R_{=}([u],[v])&\mbox{iff }~\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models u=v,\\
R_{\neq}([u],[v])&\mbox{iff }~\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models\neg(u=v),\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}R_{\in}([u],[v])&\mbox{iff }~\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models u\in v,\\
R_{\notin}([u],[v])&\mbox{iff }~\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models\neg(u\in v).\end{array}\right.

Observation 5.7
.

Applying Theorem
3.14
, one can prove that the relations
R_{=}([u],[v])
,
R_{\neq}([u],[v])
,
R_{\in}([u],[v])
, and
R_{\notin}([u],[v])
are well-defined.

As expected, the interpretations of the predicate symbols
=
and
\in
in the quotient space
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
are the binary relations
R_{=}
and
R_{\in}
, respectively.
But, the construction differs from the classical case when non-satisfaction is considered.
In Theorem
5.9
and Theorem
5.10
, we discuss this issue in full detail.

To keep the expressions simple, for any two elements
[u],[v]\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
we shall abbreviate
(x=y)([u],[v])
,
\neg(x=y)([u],[v])
,
(x\in y)([u],[v])
, and
\neg(x=y)([u],[v])
as
[u]=[v]
,
[u]\neq[v]
,
[u]\in[v]
, and
[u]\notin[v]
, respectively. Then, using Definition
5.3
and Definition
5.6
we get the following fact.

Fact 5.8
.

For any
[u],[v]\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
,

\displaystyle(i)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models[u]=[v]&\mbox{iff }R_{=}([u],[v])\\
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models[u]\neq[v]&\mbox{iff }R_{\neq}([u],[v]);\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle(ii)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models[u]\in[v]&\mbox{iff }R_{\in}([u],[v]);\\
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models[u]\notin[v]&\mbox{iff }R_{\notin}([u],[v]);\end{array}\right.

Theorem 5.9
.

For any ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra
(\mathbb{A},D)
and a pair of elements
[u]
and
[v]
of
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
,

\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models[u]\neq[v]\mbox{ iff }~\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\not\models[u]=[v].

Proof.

Consider any two elements
[u],[v]
of
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\not\models[u]=[v]

\displaystyle\mbox{ iff}~([u],[v])\notin R_{=},

\displaystyle\mbox{by Fact}~\ref{fact: satisfactions of atomic formulas}(i)

\displaystyle\mbox{ iff}~\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\not\models u=v,

\displaystyle\mbox{by Definition}~\ref{Definition: Quotient space identity and belongniness}(i)

\displaystyle\mbox{ iff}~\llbracket u=v\rrbracket=\mathbf{0},

\displaystyle\mbox{since }\llbracket u=v\rrbracket\in\{\mathbf{0},\mathbf{1}\}

\displaystyle\mbox{ iff}~\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\models\neg(u=v),

since
\mathbf{0}^{*}=\mathbf{1}\in D

\displaystyle\mbox{ iff}~\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models[u]\neq[v],

by Definition
5.6
(i) and Fact
5.8
(i).

∎

As a corollary we obtain that the two (class) relations
R_{=}
and
R_{\neq}
are disjoint. Moreover, it is easy to see that
R_{=}^{c}
, the complement of
R_{=}
, is the class
R_{\neq}
. On the other hand, the two class relations
R_{\in}
and
R_{\notin}
are exhaustive but not necessarily exclusive. This is the content of the following theorem.

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},D)
be an ultra-designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra. Then,

(i)

for any
[u],[v]\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
, if
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\not\models[u]\in[v]
, then
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models[u]\notin[v]
,

(ii)

if
D\setminus\{\mathbf{1}\}\neq\varnothing
then there exist
[u],[v]\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
such that
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models[u]\in[v]
and
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\models[u]\notin[v]
both hold.

Proof.

(i) Consider
[u],[v]\in\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
so that
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim~\not\models[u]\in[v]
. By Fact
5.8
(ii),
([u],[v])\notin R_{\in}
, which implies that
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}\not\models u\in v
, by Definition
5.6
(ii).
Hence,
\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket\notin D
. Therefore by the definition of designated
\mathsf{Cobounded}
-algebra,
\llbracket\neg(u\in v)\rrbracket=\llbracket u\in v\rrbracket^{*}=\mathbf{1}\in D

6.
Comparison with other models of paraconsistent set theories

In this section, we compare the paraconsistent models we developed in this paper with models of paraconsistent set theories that we find in the literature. In particular, we will address the models produced by Priest’s
model-theoretic
approach given that they are the only paraconsistent models of set theory in which all
\mathsf{ZF}
-axioms are valid. We will argue that our approach overcomes the limits of Priest’s model-theoretic approach to paraconsistent set theory. In short we will argue that in the models presented in this paper
\mathsf{ZF}
holds consistently and not only paraconsistently. By this we mean that in our models
\mathsf{ZF}
are only valid and not, also, not valid. In this sense, those presented here are the first proper (from a classical perspective) paraconsistent models of
\mathsf{ZF}
.

The model-theoretic approach has been put forward in
[
Priest, 2006
, Section 18.4]
and
[
Priest, 2017
, Section 11]
. In order to keep things short, we will assume some familiarity with these papers. The basic idea of the model-theoretic approach of Priest consists in finding an equivalence relation which allows us to collapse a model of
\mathsf{ZF}
into a model of
\mathsf{NLP}_{=}+\mathsf{ZF}
6
6

6

By
\mathsf{NLP}_{=}
we denote the set theory that we obtain by combining
the axioms of naïve set theory, i.e.,
Extensionality
and
Comprehension

with the logical axioms of LP
=
, where LP
=
denotes the first-order version of the Logic of Paradox, LP extended with a binary predicate
=
, where
x=y
receives value
\mathbf{1}
or
\frac{1}{2}
just in case
x
equals
y
(in the meta-theory).
and which, moreover, contains a large fragment of the cumulative hierarchy as an inner model. Priest proposes two equivalence relations: the type-lift and the Hamkins type-lift.

Let us sketch one of these constructions. In the case of the type-lift, we start with a model of
ZF
with two inaccessible cardinals, say,
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
. While everything is preserved below
V_{\kappa_{1}}
, on the other hand, everything is collapsed between
V_{\kappa_{1}}
and
V_{\kappa_{2}}
. The resulting object of such a collapse, call it
a
, is what witnesses the paraconsistency of the model. Instead, the standard hierarchy below
\kappa_{1}
is responsible for the validity of
ZF
in a cumulative hierarchy.
Applying this construction we obtain the following important result.

Theorem 6.1

(
[
Priest, 2006
]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a classical model of
\mathsf{ZF}
containing two inaccessible cardinals
\kappa_{1}
and
\kappa_{2}
. Then there is an LP-model
\mathcal{M}^{\sim}=\langle D^{\sim},I^{\sim}\rangle
such that:

(i)

\mathcal{M}^{\sim}
is a model of
ZF

\mathsf{NLP}_{=}

(ii)

\mathsf{ZF}

For completeness, let us very briefly address the Hamkins type-lift. An important fact about this second construction is that Theorem
6.1
also holds for it. The Hamkins type-lift models constitutes an improvement over the type-lift models given that different sets witness different instances of
\mathsf{Comprehension}
thus providing more discriminating models; which was not the case for the models obtained by means of the type-lift. Although we have an advance on the set theoretical side, however, this new construction manifests a decisive drawback on the logical side. Indeed, the treatment of identity is highly non-standard, since Leibniz’s law of indiscernibility of identicals fails in the models obtained by the Hamkins type-lift construction.
On the other hand, for any
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
, Leibniz’s law of indiscernibility of identicals holds. This provides a classical treatment for the notion of identity, as witnessed by Theorem
3.14
. For this reason, we concentrate on the comparison between our models and the original Priest’s type-lift models.

Before presenting the point of difference between the models presented in this paper and the type-lift ones, it is worth reporting Priest’s view, who was not completely satisfied with the type-lift constructions.

Clearly, the collapsed model just constructed is not a very interesting one.The much over worked
a
is the witness set for every condition. But it suffices to establish that there are interpretations of both ZF and naive set theory. (
[
Priest, 2017
]
, p. 48)

We believe that the models discussed here fares better than Priest’s in providing a paraconsistent version of
\mathsf{ZF}
for the following reasons.

(1)

The paraconsistency of the present models does not come from a single set on top of the hierarchy, but it is widespread in the cumulative hierarchy and witnessed by many different sets along the hierarchy (see Theorem
4.7
). In a slogan, we can say that these algebra-valued models provide a paraconsistent cumulative hierarchy, while Priest’s ones a cumulative hierarchy together with a paraconsistent set (which is somewhat isolated from the rest).

(2)

The models presented in this paper do not validate the axiom of (unrestricted)
Comprehension
. Thus, the corresponding set theory is different from that of Priest’s models. This is an important point from a classical perspective, since these results show how close we can get to a classical cumulative hierarchy, but with a non-classical logic.

(3)

Not only the axioms of
ZF
are all valid in any
\mathbf{V}^{(\mathbb{A})}/\!\sim
, but it is also important to observe that their negation do not hold in these models. In this case, we can say that
ZF
holds
consistently
. This is a trivial observation in the classical context, which, however, is not obvious in a paraconsistent context. For example, in the models obtained by a type-lifting construction the
Separation Schema
holds together with its negation. In this case we can say that
\mathsf{ZF}
holds
paraconsistently
.

This last point has been objects of criticism. Very recently Incurvati wrote the following.

Indeed, as Priest (2017: 98) points out, once inconsistent sets enter the picture, it can be shown that if ZF’s Separation Schema is true, it is both true and false. What this brings to light is that the paraconsistent set theorist needs, after all, to say more about what the universe of sets looks like. It is not enough to simply suppose that it contains some paradigmatic inconsistent sets and has the cumulative hierarchy as an inner model. (
[
Incurvati, 2020
]
, p.126)

We believe that the structures built in this paper address exactly the issue raised by the above quotation. Not only we have paraconsistent models where
\mathsf{ZF}
holds consistently, but the similarity these structures bear with
V
leave no mystery with respect to what they look like: a very familiar cumulative hierarchy.

Since these new structures satisfy both requirements of being paraconsistent and being non-trivial models of
\mathsf{ZF}
, i.e., validating these axioms consistently (as witnessed by the validity of Theorem 3.11 and Theorem 4.5), we can claim that they are the first example of non-trivial paraconsistent models of
\mathsf{ZF}
.

Abstract
. In this short note, quantum subgroups in finite free products of the Pontryagin duals of free unitary quantum groups are classified. They correspond to pairs of a subgroup Γ \Gamma and a subset S S of the free group 𝔽 n \mathbb{F}_{n} such that S S is Γ \Gamma -invariant, containing Γ \Gamma , and connected in the Cayley graph of 𝔽 n \mathbb{F}_{n} .



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 46L67, Secondary 05C25

1.
Introduction

In this note, we classify quantum subgroups in the Pontryagin duals of free unitary quantum groups or, more generally, in their finite free products.
Associated with
Q\in GL_{N}(\mathbb{C})
for
N\geq 2
, van Daele–Wang
[
4
]
defined a compact quantum group called a free unitary quantum group, which shall be denoted by
U^{+}_{Q}
.
It is constructed by forgetting the commutativity relations for the coefficients of a unitary matrix. Variations of this construction
have provided abundant examples of compact quantum groups.

For general conventions and fundamental facts on compact quantum groups and tensor categories, we refer
[
3
]
.
For a compact quantum group
G
, its finite dimensional unitary representations form a rigid semisimple C*-tensor category with a simple unit object, denoted by
\Rep G
. We write
\Irr G
for the set of all isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\Rep G
.
We write
\widehat{G}
for the Pontryagin dual of
G
, which is a discrete quantum group.

The notion of free product for (the C*-algebras of) discrete quantum groups was studied by Wang
[
5
]
.
The main object of this note is the free product
{\ast}_{j=1}^{n}\widehat{U}^{+}_{Q_{j}}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
Q_{j}\in GL(N_{j},\mathbb{C})
,
N_{j}\geq 2
, defined by the Pontryagin dual
\widehat{F}
of the compact quantum group
F
given by
C_{u}(F)=\ast_{j=1}^{n}C_{u}(U_{Q_{j}}^{+})
with a canonical comultiplication.
This quantum group
\widehat{F}
has the following remarkable property analogous to the free group
\mathbb{F}_{n}
with
n
generators: for any compact quantum group
G
, any
n
-tuple of unitary representations
(u_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
of
G
with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}u_{j}=N_{j}
and
Q_{j}^{*-1}u_{j}^{\top}Q_{j}^{*}
being a unitary uniquely induces a unital
*
-homomorphism
C_{u}(F)\to C_{u}(G)
preserving comultiplications.
Quantum subgroups of
\widehat{F}
has been recently classified by Freslon–Weber
[
2
]
when
\widehat{F}=\widehat{U}^{+}_{Q}
, together with rich structural results for such quantum subgroups.
We classify quantum subgroups of
\widehat{F}
in general, based on a simple observation relating
\Irr F
to the path space of the Cayley graph of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
.
Our classification exhibits to what extent more quantum subgroups exist than mere subgroups of free groups and thus could be regarded as a complementary result to
[
2
]
towards the quantum analogue of Kurosh’s theorem.

2.
Classification

Note that the classification of quantum subgroups in
\widehat{F}
is equivalent to the classification of idempotent complete full rigid C*-tensor subcategories in
\Rep F
, as a consequence of Woronowicz’s Tannaka–Krein duality. Since the latter is a purely categorical problem and in fact relies only on the fusion rule of
\Rep F
, we work with
\Rep F
rather than
\widehat{F}
and assume for simplicity that
\Rep F
is skeletal by strictification.

The theory of unitary representations of
U^{+}_{Q}
was investigated by Banica
[
1
]
. We recall its fusion rule.
Consider the free product of additive monoids
M:=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\ast\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
.
When we regard
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
as elements in
M
, we write
[r]
and
[s]
.
Then, we can identify
\Irr U^{+}_{Q}
with
M
so that
the unit object is
[0]
,
\overline{[r]}=[-r]
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\overline{(xy)}=\bar{y}\bar{x}
for
x,y\in M
, and the tensor product is determined recursively by

\displaystyle(x[r])\otimes([s]y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x[r][s]y=x[r+s]y&\text{if $rs=1$,}\\
x[r][s]y\oplus(x\otimes y)&\text{if $rs=-1$}\end{array}\right.\quad(x,y\in M,r,s\in\{\pm 1\}).

Combined with
[
5
]
, we can canonically identify
\Irr F
with the monoid
M^{\ast n}
as a set.
For
1\leq j\leq n
, we put
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}
for the generators of the
j
th component
M
in
M^{\ast n}
, and
a_{j}
for the generator of the
j
th component
\mathbb{Z}
in
\mathbb{F}_{n}
.
We shall write
[a_{j}^{r}]:=\alpha_{j}^{r}
and
[a_{j}^{-r}]:=\beta_{j}^{r}
in
M^{\ast n}
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Consider the Cayley graph
\mathcal{G}
of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
, where the set of its oriented edges is
\{(g,ga_{j}^{\pm 1})\mid g\in\mathbb{F}_{n},1\leq j\leq n\}
. Let
\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{G})
be the set of all finite paths in
\mathcal{G}
starting from
1_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}
, possibly with turning-backs.
We can identify
\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{G})
with
M^{\ast n}
by assigning each path of the form
(g_{0},g_{1},\cdots,g_{k})\in\mathbb{F}_{n}^{k+1}
with
k\geq 0
,
g_{0}=1_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}
,
g_{l}=g_{l-1}a_{j_{l}}^{r_{l}}
,
1\leq j_{l}\leq n
,
r_{l}=\pm 1
for
1\leq l\leq k
to
[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}][a_{j_{2}}^{r_{2}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\in M^{\ast n}
.
Then, extending our convention of
[a_{j}^{r}]
for
r\in\mathbb{Z}
, we define
[g]\in M^{\ast n}
for
g\in\mathbb{F}_{n}

by the shortest path from
1_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}
to
g
via the identification
M^{\ast n}=\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{G})
.
From now, we promise that the symbol
k
,
j
and
r
, possibly accompanied with indices, stand for elements in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
\mathbb{Z}\cap[1,n]
, and
\{\pm 1\}
respectively, unless clarified otherwise.
Note that any element in
\Irr F=M^{\ast n}
can be expressed in the form
[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}][a_{j_{2}}^{r_{2}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]
without redundancy.

Remark
.

We reinterpret the fusion rule of
\Rep F
in terms of
\mathcal{G}
.
Take any
e,e^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{G})
, whose endpoints are denoted by
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{n}
, respectively.
We write
ge^{\prime}
for the path from
g
to
gg^{\prime}
given by the left translation of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
on
\mathcal{G}
and
ee^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{G})
for the concatenation of
e
and
ge^{\prime}
.
Note that
ee^{\prime}
coincides with the product of the monoid
M^{\ast n}
.
We set
e_{0}:=e
,
e^{\prime}_{0}:=e^{\prime}
and define
e_{l+1},e^{\prime}_{l+1}\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{G})=M^{\ast n}
recursively on
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
so that
e_{l+1}[a_{j}^{r}]=e_{l}
and
[a_{j}^{-r}]e^{\prime}_{l+1}=e^{\prime}_{l}
as long as such
j
and
r
exist. If we regard
e,e^{\prime}\in\Irr F
, the tensor product
e\otimes e^{\prime}
is the direct sum of all paths of the form
e_{l}e_{l}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{G})=\Irr F
.
Also, when we write
\overline{e}\in\mathrm{Path}(\mathcal{G})
for the path from
1_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}
to
g^{-1}
given by reversing
e
, the involution
e\mapsto\overline{e}
corresponds to the conjugate operation on
\Irr F
.
If we put
V(e)\subset\mathbb{F}_{n}
for the set of all vertices appearing in
e
, we have
V(e_{l}e^{\prime}_{l})\subset V(ee^{\prime})=V(e)\cup gV(e^{\prime})
for
e_{l}
and
e_{l}^{\prime}
above,
and
V(\overline{e})=g^{-1}V(e)
.

Our main result is as follows.

Main Theorem
.

For any pair
(\Gamma,S)
of a subgroup
\Gamma\subset\mathbb{F}_{n}
and a left
\Gamma
-invariant subset
S\subset\mathbb{F}_{n}
containing
\Gamma
and connected in the Cayley graph
\mathcal{G}
,
there is an idempotent complete full rigid C*-tensor subcategory of
\Rep F
such that the set of its irreducible objects equals

(I)

\displaystyle\bigl\{[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}][a_{j_{2}}^{r_{2}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\in\Irr F\,\big|\,a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}a_{j_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}\in\Gamma,a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}a_{j_{2}}^{r_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{l}}^{r_{l}}\in S\text{ for all }0\leq l\leq k\bigr\}.

Conversely, any idempotent complete full rigid C*-tensor subcategory of
\Rep F
is of this form for a pair
(\Gamma,S)
with the conditions above, which must be necessarily unique.

Proof.

It is not hard to see the first half of the statement with the aid of the remark above.
We show the latter half.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be an idempotent complete full rigid C*-tensor subcategory of
\Rep F
and
I
be the set of irreducible objects in
\mathcal{C}
.
For any
[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\in I\setminus\{[1_{\mathbb{F}_{n}}]\}
,
inductively on
l=1,\cdots,k
, we see

(1)

\displaystyle[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{l}}^{r_{l}}][a_{j_{l+1}}^{r_{l+1}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\leq\bigl([a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{l-1}}^{r_{l-1}}][a_{j_{l-1}}^{-r_{l-1}}\cdots a_{j_{1}}^{-r_{1}}]\bigr)\otimes\bigl([a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{l-1}}^{r_{l-1}}][a_{j_{l}}^{r_{l}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\bigr)\in\mathcal{C},\text{ and}

(2)

\displaystyle[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{l}}^{r_{l}}][a_{j_{l}}^{-r_{l}}\cdots a_{j_{1}}^{-r_{1}}]\leq\bigl([a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{l}}^{r_{l}}][a_{j_{l+1}}^{r_{l+1}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\bigr)\otimes\bigl([a_{j_{k}}^{-r_{k}}]\cdots[a_{j_{l+1}}^{-r_{l+1}}][a_{j_{l}}^{-r_{l}}\cdots a_{j_{1}}^{-r_{1}}]\bigr)\in\mathcal{C}.

Clearly,
S:=\{a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{l}}^{r_{l}}\mid[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\in I,0\leq l\leq k\}\subset\mathbb{F}_{n}
is connected in
\mathcal{G}
, and

\Gamma:=\{a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}\mid[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\in I\}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{n}
contained in
S
.
Then, (
1
) and (
2
) for
l=k
imply
[g]\in I
for all
g\in\Gamma
and
[g][g^{-1}]\in I
for all
g\in S
, respectively.
For all
g\in\Gamma
and
h\in S
, it holds
[g][h][h^{-1}]\in I
and thus
gh\in S
.
By construction, the set defined by (
I
) contains
I
. They coincide because
for any
[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\in\Irr F
satisfying the conditions in (
I
),

\displaystyle[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\leq\bigl([a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}]\cdots[a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}][a_{j_{k}}^{-r_{k}}\cdots a_{j_{1}}^{-r_{1}}]\bigr)\otimes[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]

\displaystyle\leq{}

\displaystyle\bigl([a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}][a_{j_{1}}^{-r_{1}}]\bigr)\otimes\bigl([a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}a_{j_{2}}^{r_{2}}][a_{j_{2}}^{-r_{2}}a_{j_{1}}^{-r_{1}}]\bigr)\otimes\cdots\otimes\bigl([a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}][a_{j_{k}}^{-r_{k}}\cdots a_{j_{1}}^{-r_{1}}]\bigr)\otimes[a_{j_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots a_{j_{k}}^{r_{k}}]\in\mathcal{C}.\qed

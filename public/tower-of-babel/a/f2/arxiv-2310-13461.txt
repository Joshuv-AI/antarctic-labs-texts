Global well-posedness and large-time behavior of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with hyperbolic heat conduction
Fucai Li, Houzhi Tang, Shuxing Zhang

Abstract
The classical Fourier’s law, which states that the heat flux is proportional to the temperature gradient, induces the paradox of infinite propagation speed for heat conduction. To accurately simulate the real physical process, the hyperbolic model of heat conduction named Cattaneo’s law was proposed, which leads to the finite speed of heat propagation. A natural question is that whether the large-time behavior of the heat flux for compressible flow would be different for these two laws. In this paper, we aim to address this question by studying the global well-posedness and optimal time-decay rates of classical solutions to the compressible Navier-Stokes system with Cattaneo’s law. By designing a new method, we obtain the optimal time-decay rates for the highest derivatives of the heat flux, which cannot be derived for the system with Fourier’s law by Matsumura and Nishida [Proc. Japan Acad. Ser. A Math. Sci., 55(9):337-342, 1979]. In this sense, our results first reveal the essential differences between the two laws.

Introduction

The compressible Navier-Stokes equations with heat conduction can be written in the following form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\textrm{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathrm{div}\big(\rho u\otimes u\big)+\nabla P=\text{div}S,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho E)+\mathrm{div}(\rho Eu+Pu)+\text{div}q=\text{div}(uS).\end{array}\right.

The unknown functions
\rho(x,t)
,
u(x,t)=(u_{1}(x,t),u_{2}(x,t),u_{3}(x,t))^{\top}
,
P(x,t)
,
e(x,t)
,
E(x,t)=e+\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}
and
q(x,t)
represent the fluid density, velocity, pressure, internal energy, total energy, and heat flux, respectively. The stress tensor
S
is given by

S=2\tilde{\nu}\mathbb{D}u+\tilde{\eta}(\text{div}u)\mathbb{I}_{3},

where
\mathbb{D}u=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+\nabla u^{\top})
is the so-called deformation tensor,
\mathbb{I}_{3}
denotes the
3\times 3
identity matrix,
\tilde{\nu}
and
\tilde{\eta}
are viscosity coefficients satisfying

\displaystyle\tilde{\nu}>0,\quad\tilde{\eta}+\frac{2}{3}\tilde{\nu}\geq 0.

The heat flux
q
is assumed to satisfy the following Cattaneo’s law

\tau\partial_{t}q+q+\kappa\nabla\theta=0,

(1.1)

which gives rise to heat waves with finite propagation speeds. Here,
\theta
is the absolute temperature and
\kappa>0
denotes the heat conductivity coefficient. The parameter
\tau
is a relaxation time which represents the time is required to establish a steady state of heat conduction in an element suddenly exposed to the heat flux.

In this paper, we consider the ideal gas model, i.e., the pressure
P
and the internal energy
e
satisfy

P=R\rho\theta,~~e=\frac{R}{\gamma-1}\theta,

where
\gamma>1
is the adiabatic constant and
R>0
represents the ideal gas constant.

Therefore, we conclude from above to obtain the following compressible Navier-Stokes-Cattaneo (NSC) system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\textrm{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\mathrm{div}\big(\rho u\otimes u\big)+R\nabla(\rho\theta)=\tilde{\nu}\Delta u+(\tilde{\nu}+\tilde{\eta})\nabla\text{div}u,\\
\displaystyle\frac{R}{\gamma-1}(\rho\partial_{t}\theta+\rho u\cdot\nabla\theta)+R\rho\theta\text{div}u+\text{div}q=2\tilde{\nu}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}+\tilde{\eta}(\text{div}u)^{2},\\
\displaystyle\tau\partial_{t}q+q+\kappa\nabla\theta=0.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

We consider the above system in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and supplement it with the initial data

(\rho,u,\theta,q)(x,0)=(\rho_{0},u_{0},\theta_{0},q_{0})(x),

(1.3)

and the far-field states

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}(\rho,u,\theta,q)(x,t)=(\rho_{*},0,\theta_{*},0),

(1.4)

where
\rho_{*}
and
\theta_{*}
denote the positive constants.

For the limit case
\tau=0
, the system (
1.2
) turns into the classical compressible Navier-Stokes equations, in which the relation between the heat flux and the temperature is governed by the classical Fourier’s law,

q=-\kappa\nabla\theta.

(1.5)

As it’s well known, Fourier’s law is often used to describe the heat conduction phenomenon. Nevertheless, it has a drawback of an inherent infinite propagation speed of signals, which implies that any local temperature disturbance causes an instantaneous perturbation in the temperature field at any point. In some situations, Fourier’s law does not coincide with the experimental observations, especially when high-temperature gradients are considered, for example, see
[
19
,
20
,
25
,
28
]
. To redeem this deficiency, Cattaneo
[
2
]
proposed a damped version of Fourier’s law, i.e., (
1.1
) by
introducing a heat flux relaxation term to describe the finite speed of
heat conduction. Now, Cattaneo’s law (
1.1
) has been widely used in thermoelasticity and fluid models, see
[
29
,
30
,
11
]
and the references therein. It is very interesting to point out that sometimes the results which hold for Fourier’s law may be not true for Cattaneo’s law. For example, Fernández Sare and Racke
[
7
]
showed that Fourier’s law preserves the property of exponential stability while Cattaneo’s law destroys such a property for certain Timoshenko-type thermoelastic system.

Now we review some mathematical results on the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with Fourier’s law and Cattaneo’s law. First, due to its physical importance and mathematical challenge, there is a lot of works on the well-posedness and large-time behavior of solutions to the compressible Navier-Stokes equations combined with Fourier’s law (
1.5
), for example, see
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
31
,
33
,
23
,
27
,
24
,
18
]
. The local existence and uniqueness of smooth solutions were initiated by Serrin
[
31
]
and Nash
[
27
]
for initial density far away from vacuum. Later, Matsumura and Nishida
[
23
]
proved the global existence and uniqueness of the classical solution for small initial data without vacuum. The
L^{2}
-time decay estimates were investigated by Mastumura and Nishida
[
22
]
for the small solution. Later, Li
[
18
]
analyzed the detailed wave structure of the Green function for linearized system. Du and Wu
[
5
]
established the pointwise estimates of the classical solution for nonlinear system.

When the heat conduction is described by Cattaneo’s law, there are also a few contributions to the well-posedness and singular limit of the compressible NSC type system, see
[
26
,
1
,
12
,
32
,
13
]
and the references therein. In
[
12
]
, Hu and Racke considered the pressure
P=P(\rho,\theta)
and the constant equilibrium state
(\rho,u,\theta,q)=(1,0,1,0)
. Under an additional assumption on the constant relaxation time
0<\tau<\frac{2\kappa}{P_{\theta}(1,1)^{2}}
, they proved the global existence and relaxation limit of the small smooth solution. Generally speaking, the relaxation constant
\tau
is fixed for a specific fluid. Therefore, their results cannot be applied to the general compressible flow since the relaxation constant
\tau
in
[
12
]
is bounded by the constant
\frac{2\kappa}{P_{\theta}(1,1)^{2}}
which depends on the heat conduction coefficient
\kappa
. Later, Hu, Racke and Wang
[
13
]
investigated the formation of singularities in one-dimensional hyperbolic compressible Navier-Stokes equations with the nonlinear Cattaneo’s law for
heat conduction as well as through the constitutive Maxwell type relations for the stress tensor.

The classical Fourier’s law, which states that the heat flux is proportional to the temperature gradient, causes a paradoxical effect of infinite propagation speed for heat conduction. In order to accurately simulate the real process of heat conduction, Cattaneo’s law is considered, which takes into account the finite speed of heat propagation. A natural question is that whether the optimal time-decay rates the heat flux for compressible flow would be different for these two laws. Since the large-time behavior of compressible Navier-Stokes equations with Fourier’s law has been studied in
[
22
]
, the motivation of this paper is to address this question by studying the global well-posedness and large-time behavior of the classical solutions to the compressible Navier-Stokes system with the Cattaneo’s law without any additional conditions on the relaxation time
\tau
. Moreover, due to the damping structure in Cattaneo’s law, we obtain the optimal time-decay rates for the highest derivatives of the heat flux, which cannot be derived for the system with Fourier’s law.

More precisely, our first results on the global existence and upper bound time decay rates of classical solutions to the Cauchy problem (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) is given as

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s\geq 3
be an integer. Assume that the initial data
(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0},\theta_{0}-\theta_{*},q_{0})\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfying

\|(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0},\theta_{0}-\theta_{*},q_{0})\|_{H^{s}}\leq\delta_{0},

(1.6)

for some sufficiently small constant
\delta_{0}>0
, then the Cauchy problem (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) admits a unique global classical solution
(\rho,u,\theta,q)
enjoying

\displaystyle\|(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*},q)(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\|\nabla\rho(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(r)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\nabla\theta(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|q(r)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}r\leq C\delta_{0}^{2}.

(1.7)

Moreover, if the initial perturbation
(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0},\theta_{0}-\theta_{*},q_{0})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, then it holds that, for
0\leq k\leq s
,

\|\nabla^{k}(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{k}q(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},

(1.8)

where the positive constant
C
is independent of time.

Basing on the global existence and the upper bound time-decay rates, we can further show that the above rates are optimal.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that the conditions in Theorem
1.1
hold. If the Fourier transform of the initial perturbation
(\hat{n}_{0},\hat{w}_{0},\hat{\phi}_{0},\hat{\psi}_{0})\triangleq\Big(\frac{\hat{\rho}_{0}-\rho_{*}}{\rho_{*}},\frac{\hat{u}_{0}}{\sqrt{R\theta_{*}}},\frac{\hat{\theta}_{0}-\theta_{*}}{\sqrt{\gamma-1}\,\theta_{*}},\sqrt{\frac{\tau}{\kappa\rho_{*}R\theta_{*}^{2}}}\hat{q}_{0}\Big)
satisfies

\displaystyle\inf_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|\hat{n}_{0}(\xi)|\geq\mu_{0},~~\text{and}~~\hat{w}_{0}(\xi)=\hat{\phi}_{0}(\xi)=0~~\text{for}~~|\xi|\leq r_{0},

(1.9)

where
\mu_{0}
is a positive constant and
r_{0}>0
denotes a sufficiently small constant, then the global solution
(\rho,u,\theta,q)
given by Theorem
1.1
satisfies for large time that

\bar{C}_{*}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\leq\|\nabla^{k}(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},

(1.10)

and

\bar{C}_{*}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\leq\|\nabla^{k}q(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},

(1.11)

where
\bar{C}_{*}
and
C
are positive constants independent of time.

Remark 1.1
.

Following the spectral analysis and energy method developed in this paper, the time-decay of results in
[
22
]
:

\|(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*})(t)\|_{H^{3}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

can be generalized as

\|\nabla^{k}(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad\text{for}\;\;0\leq k\leq s.

However, the classical Fourier’s law
q=-\kappa\nabla\theta
implies for
0\leq k\leq s-1
that

\|\nabla^{k}q(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}.

(1.12)

It is obviously observed from (
1.8
) and (
1.12
) that Cattaneo’s law can provide a faster time-decay rate for the highest derivative of the heat flux but Fourier’s law fails for this.

Remark 1.2
.

Due to the damping structure of
q
in Cattaneo’s law, the time decay rate of
\|\nabla^{k}q\|_{L^{2}}
is faster than
\|\nabla^{k}(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*})\|_{L^{2}}
.

Remark 1.3
.

By Sobolev’s inequality and (
1.8
), it follows that, for any
p\in[2,6]
and
0\leq k\leq s-1
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*})(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}(1-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{k}{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{k}q(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(1+t)^{-2(1-\frac{3}{4p})-\frac{k}{2}}.

Furthermore, it holds, for
0\leq k\leq s-2
,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*})(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3+k}{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{k}q(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{4+k}{2}}.

Remark 1.4
.

It should be pointed out that the approach developed in this paper can be extended to other fluid models with Cattaneo’s law instead of Fourier’s law, for instance, the non-isentropic compressible magnetohydrodynamic system. Furthermore, we can study the pointwise estimates of the solution to the NSC system by using Green function method.

Now, we introduce the main difficulties in this paper and the ideas used in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
. Notice that Hu and Racke
[
12
]
verified the Kawashima condition to prove the global existence by the energy method developed in
[
17
]
, and the condition
0<\tau<\frac{2\kappa}{P_{\theta}(1,1)^{2}}
is necessary in their arguments. Since the relaxation constant
\tau
is fixed for a specific fluid, we need develop new ideas and methods to establish the global well-posedness for the general fluid without any restriction on
\tau
. We first take advantage of the ingenious structure of the system to obtain the basic energy estimate (
3.9
) without any restriction on
\tau
. Then, utilizing the formulated system (
3.3
)-(
3.6
), we establish the uniform estimates for high-order derivatives with the help of the damping structure of
q
and (
3.9
). Therefore, combining the uniform estimates and continuous argument yields global existence.

Since the heat flux
q(x,t)
satisfies Cattaneo’s law, it is natural to solve it as

q(x,t)=e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}t}q_{0}-\frac{\kappa}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}(t-r)}\nabla\theta(x,r)\mathrm{d}r.

(1.13)

It is observed from above that the upper bound decay rate of
\|q\|_{L^{2}}
is the same order as
\|\nabla\theta\|_{L^{2}}
. This enlightens us the time-decay rates of the lower order derivatives
\|\nabla^{k}q\|_{L^{2}}(0\leq k\leq s-1)
can be obtained by calculating
\|\nabla^{k+1}\theta\|_{L^{2}}
. Nevertheless, when considering the highest derivative
\|\nabla^{s}q\|_{L^{2}}
, it can not be achieved by the same method as above since the regularities of
q
and
\theta
are same. In order to surround this difficulty, we first rewrite the perturbated equations in the operator form. Then, by the spectral analysis and energy method, it is shown that

\|\nabla^{k}(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*},q)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad 0\leq k\leq s.

(1.14)

In terms of (
1.13
), we enhance the time-decay rate of
q
as

\|\nabla^{k}q(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad 0\leq k\leq s-1.

(1.15)

By introducing the low-high frequency decomposition of the solution defined in (
4.24
), we find that the low-frequency part of the highest order derivatives
\nabla^{s}q^{\ell}
satisfies

\|\nabla^{s}q^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{s}{2}}.

(1.16)

After an important observation, i.e., the decay rates of the high-frequency part of the highest order derivatives
\|\nabla^{s}q^{h}\|_{L^{2}}
are faster than
\|\nabla^{s}q^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}
given in (
1.16
), we arrive at

\|\nabla^{s}q(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\nabla^{s}q^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{s}q^{h}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{s}{2}}.

(1.17)

Thus, combining (
1.14
), (
1.15
) and (
1.17
) up yields the upper bound time-decay rates (
1.8
).

To illustrate the rates in (
1.8
) are optimal, we need to establish the lower bound decay estimates for nonlinear system (
1.2
)-(
1.4
). Under some suitable assumptions on the initial data, we show that the optimal decay rates of solutions for the linearized system by using the spectral analysis. Due to the smallness of the initial data, we can prove that the solution of the nonlinear system satisfies

\underline{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq\|(\rho-\rho_{*},u,\theta-\theta_{*})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\bar{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}},~~\underline{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\leq\|q(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\bar{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}},

where the positive constants
\underline{C}
and
\bar{C}
are independent of time. Then, with the help of (
1.8
) and the Sobolev interpolation inequality (
2.4
), we derive the lower bound of time-decay rates for high-order derivatives, which implies that the rates in (
1.8
) are optimal.

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

In this subsection, we explain the notation and conventions used throughout the paper.

L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
denote the usual Lebesgue and Sobolev space on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, with norms
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{W^{k,p}}
, respectively. When
p=2
, we denote
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
by
H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{k}}
. We denote by
C
a generic positive constant which may vary in different estimates.
f_{1}\lesssim f_{2}
and
f_{1}=O(f_{2})
describes that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
|f_{1}|\leq C|f_{2}|
.
f_{1}\sim f_{2}
represents that
f_{1}\lesssim f_{2}
and
f_{2}\lesssim f_{1}
. For an integer
\alpha
, the symbol
\nabla^{\alpha}
denotes the summation of all terms
\partial_{x_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\partial_{x_{3}}^{\alpha_{3}}
with the multi-index
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3})
satisfying
\alpha=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}
. For a function
f
,
\|f\|_{X}
represents the norm of
f
on
X
,
\|(f,g)\|_{X}
denotes the summation
\|f\|_{X}+\|g\|_{X}
. As mentioned before, the Fourier transform of
f
is denoted by
\hat{f}
or
\mathscr{F}[f]
satisfying

\hat{f}(\xi)=\mathscr{F}[f](\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(x)e^{-i2\pi x\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}x,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Correspondingly, the inverse Fourier transform of
f
is denoted by
\check{f}
or
\mathscr{F}^{-1}[f]
such that

\check{f}(x)=\mathscr{F}^{-1}[f](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f(\xi)e^{i2\pi\xi\cdot x}\mathrm{d}\xi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Let
\Lambda^{\ell}
be the pseudodifferential operator defined by

\Lambda^{\ell}f=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{\ell}\hat{f})\ \ \text{for}\ \ell\in\mathbb{R}.

We define operators
P_{1}
and
P_{\infty}
on
L^{2}
by

P_{j}f=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\Big(\hat{\chi}_{j}\mathscr{F}[f]\Big),\quad j=1~\text{or}~\infty,

where
\hat{\chi}_{j}(j=1~\text{or}~\infty)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
0\leq\hat{\chi}_{j}\leq 1
are cut-off functions defined by

\hat{\chi}_{1}(\xi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}1\quad(|\xi|\leq r_{0}),\\
0\quad(|\xi|\geq R_{0}),\end{array}\right.\quad\hat{\chi}_{\infty}(\xi)=1-\hat{\chi}_{1}(\xi),

for constants
r_{0}
and
R_{0}
satisfying
0<r_{0}<R_{0}
.

2.2
Auxiliary lemmas

In this subsection, we collect some elementary inequalities and important lemmas that are used extensively in this paper. We first recall the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg’s inequality.

Lemma 2.1
.

(​
[
21
]
or
[
8
, Lemma A.1]
)

Let
l,s
and
k
be any real numbers satisfying
0\leq l,s<k
, and let
p,r,q\in[1,\infty]
and
\frac{l}{k}\leq\theta\leq 1
such that

\frac{l}{3}-\frac{1}{p}=\left(\frac{s}{3}-\frac{1}{r}\right)(1-\theta)+\left(\frac{k}{3}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\theta.

Then, for any
u\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),
we have

\|\nabla^{l}u\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\|\nabla^{s}u\|_{L^{r}}^{1-\theta}\|\nabla^{k}u\|_{L^{q}}^{\theta}.

(2.1)

Next, we introduce the following commutator estimates.

Lemma 2.2
.

(​
[
21
, Lemma 3.4]
)

For any integer
k\geq 1
and the pair of functions
f,g\in H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it holds

\|\nabla^{k}(fg)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{k}g\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(2.2)

Moreover, if
\nabla f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we have

\big\|\nabla^{k}(fg)-f\nabla^{k}g\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{k-1}g\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(2.3)

Finally, we introduce a special Sobolev interpolation inequality (see
[
8
, Lemma A.4]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\ell\geq 0
and the integer
k\geq 0
, then we have

\|\nabla^{k}f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla^{k+1}f\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\theta}\|\Lambda^{-\ell}f\|_{L^{2}}^{\theta},

(2.4)

where
\theta=\frac{1}{1+k+\ell}
and
\Lambda^{-\ell}f=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\big(|\xi|^{-\ell}\hat{f}\big)
.

3
Global existence of the classical solution

3.1
Reformulated problem

In this subsection, we linearize the solution
(\rho,u,\theta,q)
of the system (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) around the constant state
(\rho_{*},0,\theta_{*},0)
. For notation convenience, we introduce some positive constants

c=\sqrt{R\theta_{*}},\quad\sigma=\sqrt{(\gamma-1)R\theta_{*}},\quad\nu=\frac{\tilde{\nu}}{\rho_{*}},\quad\eta=\frac{\tilde{\eta}}{\rho_{*}},\quad a=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa\rho_{*}R\theta_{*}^{2}}{\tau}},~b=\sqrt{\frac{\kappa(\gamma-1)}{\tau\rho_{*}R}}.

(3.1)

Define the perturbed variables as

n=\frac{\rho-\rho_{*}}{\rho_{*}},\quad w=\frac{u}{c},\quad\phi=\frac{\theta-\theta_{*}}{\sqrt{\gamma-1}\,\theta_{*}},\quad\psi=\frac{q}{a}.

(3.2)

Then, the problem (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) is reformulated to

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n+c\,{\rm{div}}w=f_{1},\\
&\partial_{t}w+c\nabla n+\sigma\nabla\phi=\nu\Delta w+(\nu+\eta)\nabla{\rm{div}}w+f_{2},\\
&\partial_{t}\phi+\sigma{\rm{div}}w+b\text{div}\psi=f_{3},\\
&\partial_{t}\psi+\frac{1}{\tau}\psi+b\nabla\phi=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(3.3)

subject to the initial data

(n,w,\phi,\psi)(x,0)=(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})=\Big(\frac{\rho_{0}-\rho_{*}}{\rho_{*}},\frac{u_{0}}{c},\frac{\theta_{0}-\theta_{*}}{\sqrt{\gamma-1}\,\theta_{*}},\frac{q_{0}}{a}\Big),

(3.4)

and the far-field states

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}(n,w,\phi,\psi)=(0,0,0,0),

\displaystyle=b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\nabla^{\alpha}\Big(\frac{n\text{div}\psi}{1+n}\Big)-\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}\psi\Big)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\triangleq J_{41}+J_{42}.

It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle|J_{41}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{n}{1+n}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\Big\|\nabla^{\alpha}\Big(\frac{n}{1+n}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}}\|\text{div}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

As for
J_{42}
, in terms of integration by parts, it holds

\displaystyle J_{42}

\displaystyle=-b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\Big(\frac{n}{1+n}\Big)\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x-b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\triangleq J_{421}+J_{422}.

The first term
J_{421}
is bounded by

|J_{421}|\lesssim\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

For the second term
J_{422}
, we utilize
\eqref{nsac}_{4}
to get

\displaystyle J_{422}

\displaystyle=-b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}(\partial_{t}\psi+\frac{1}{\tau}\psi)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\frac{n}{1+n}\Big)_{t}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{\tau(1+n)}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\partial_{t}n}{(1+n)^{2}}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{\tau(1+n)}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{f_{1}-c\,\text{div}w}{(1+n)^{2}}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{\tau(1+n)}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C\delta\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

It then concludes from above to prove

|J_{4}|\leq|J_{41}|+|J_{421}|+|J_{422}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(3.34)

In terms of (
3.31
)-(
3.34
) and (
3.30
), we verify that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}{\rm{div}}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}{\rm{div}}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

(3.35)

Applying the operator
\nabla^{\alpha}
to
\eqref{nsac}_{4}
, multiplying it by
\nabla^{\alpha}\psi
, and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}{\rm{div}}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x=0.

(3.36)

It then follows from (
3.35
) and (
3.36
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{\tau}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}{\rm{div}}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

Since
\delta
is small, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}{\rm{div}}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(3.37)

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.5
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Assume that
(n,w,\phi,\psi)
is the classical solution of the problem (
3.3
)-(
3.6
) satisfying the a priori assumption (
3.7
), then it holds for
0\leq t\leq T
and
1\leq\alpha\leq s
that

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}{\rm{div}}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C\delta(\|\nabla^{2}n\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

(3.38)

where the positive constant
C
is independent of time.

Proof
.
Applying the operator
\nabla^{\alpha-1}
to
\eqref{nsac}_{2}
, multiplying the resulting equation by
\nabla^{\alpha}n
, and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle c\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nu\nabla^{\alpha-1}\Delta w+(\nu+\eta)\nabla^{\alpha}{\rm{div}}w-\sigma\nabla^{\alpha}\phi)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}f_{2}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad-c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla^{\alpha}{\rm{div}}w+\nabla^{\alpha}f_{1})\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\triangleq J_{5}+J_{6}+J_{7}.

(3.39)

By Young’s inequality, the term
J_{5}
can be estimated as

\displaystyle|J_{5}|

\displaystyle\leq(\nu+\eta)(\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}+\sigma\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\frac{c}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(\varepsilon+\frac{c}{4})\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.40)

where
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary. For the term
J_{6}
, recalling the definition of
f_{2}
in (
3.6
), we deal with the first term of
J_{6}
as follows.

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}(w\cdot\nabla w)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[\nabla^{\alpha-1}(w\cdot\nabla w)-w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}w]\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}+C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{1}}\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{1}})\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla w\|_{H^{1}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{1}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}+C\delta\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

where we use the
a priori
assumption (
3.7
) that for
1\leq\alpha\leq s
that
\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq\delta
. The remainders of
J_{6}
can be estimated similarly. Thus, we have

\displaystyle|J_{6}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\Big(-cw\cdot\nabla w+\frac{(cn-\sigma\phi)\nabla n}{1+n}-\frac{\nu n\Delta w+(\nu+\eta)n\nabla{\rm{div}}w}{1+n}\Big)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}n\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}).

(3.41)

Finally, we deal with the term
J_{7}
. We have

\displaystyle|J_{7}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla^{\alpha}w+\nabla^{\alpha}(nw))\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}w\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(3.42)

Combining the above estimates together yields

\displaystyle c\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq(C\delta+\varepsilon+\frac{c}{4})\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C\delta(\|\nabla^{2}n\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}).

Due to the smallness of
\delta
, we can chose
\varepsilon
sufficiently small to get (
3.38
). This completes the proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Assume
(n,w,\phi,\psi)
is the classical solution of the system (
3.3
)-(
3.6
) satisfying the a priori assumption (
3.7
), then it holds for
0\leq t\leq T
and
1\leq\alpha\leq s
that

\displaystyle\frac{3b}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\emph{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+C(\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

(3.43)

where the positive constant

is independent of time.

Proof
.
Applying the operator
\nabla^{\alpha-1}
to
\eqref{nsac}_{4}
, multiplying the resulting equation by
\nabla^{\alpha}\phi
, and integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle b\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}(\sigma\text{div}w+b\,\text{div}\psi)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}f_{3}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\mathrm{d}x

By Young’s inequality, the term

is estimated as follows

\displaystyle|J_{8}|

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+b\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sigma\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sigma\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+b\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.45)

For the term

, we have

\displaystyle|J_{9}|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\Big(-cw\cdot\nabla\phi-c(\gamma-1)\phi{\rm{div}}w+\frac{\sigma(2\nu|\mathbb{D}w|^{2}+\eta({\rm{div}}w)^{2})}{c(1+n)}+\frac{bn\text{div}\psi}{1+n}\Big)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}\Big(-cw\cdot\nabla\phi-c(\gamma-1)\phi{\rm{div}}w+\frac{\sigma(2\nu|\mathbb{D}w|^{2}+\eta({\rm{div}}w)^{2})}{c(1+n)}+\frac{bn\text{div}\psi}{1+n}\Big)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(3.46)

Finally, we deal with the term
J_{10}
as follows:

\displaystyle|J_{10}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\tau}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{4}b\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{b\tau^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.47)

Combining the above estimates and noticing the smallness of
\delta
yield

\displaystyle\frac{3b}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+C(\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

This completes the proof of Lemma
3.7
.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
s\geq 3
be an integer and
\delta
be a sufficiently small positive constant. Under the conditions of Theorem
1.1
, assume further that
(n,w,\phi,\psi)
is the classical solution of the problem (
3.3
)-(
3.6
) for any time

and satisfying the a priori assumption

Then it holds

\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla(n,\phi)(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla w(r)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\psi(r)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}r\leq C\delta_{0}^{2},

(3.48)

where
\delta_{0}
is a small positive constant given by (
1.6
).

Proof
.
We conclude by Lemmas
3.3
-
3.5
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{3\nu}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3(\nu+\eta)}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

Since
\delta
is sufficiently small, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\nu+\eta}{2}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(3.49)

For the above inequalities, summing
\alpha
from
1
to
s
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\|\nabla n\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla w\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla\psi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}\right)+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\nabla\psi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta(\|\nabla n\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla w\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}).

(3.50)

Similarly, by Lemmas
3.6
and
3.7
, taking
\eqref{lem-nx1}\times\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}+\eqref{lem-1nx1}
, and we sum
\alpha
from 1 to
s
to obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{bc^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{3b}{4}\|\nabla\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{H^{s}}^{2})+C\delta\|\nabla n\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+(C\delta+\frac{b}{4})\|\nabla\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}.

Since
\delta
is small enough, we have

\displaystyle\frac{bc^{2}}{16\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{b}{4}\|\nabla\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{s}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{H^{s}}^{2}).

(3.51)

Choosing
\beta_{1}
sufficiently small, then taking

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\nabla n\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla w\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla\psi\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\beta_{1}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Big(\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle+\frac{bc^{2}\beta_{1}}{16\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{b\beta_{1}}{4}\|\nabla\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\nabla\psi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}

Since
\beta_{1}
and
\delta
are sufficiently small, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\nabla n\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla w\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla\psi\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\beta_{1}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Big(\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x\Big)\Big\}

\displaystyle+\frac{bc^{2}\beta_{1}}{32\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{\nu}{4}\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{b\beta_{1}}{8}\|\nabla\phi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau}\|\nabla\psi\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\beta_{1}(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

We integrate the above inequality over
(0,t)
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\nabla(n,w,\phi)(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla\psi(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\beta_{1}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{s}\Big(\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\mathrm{d}x\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{1}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|\nabla n(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}w(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla\psi(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(\|\nabla w(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\mathrm{d}r,

where
C_{1}
is a positive constant. Then, thanks to the smallness of
\beta_{1}
and Lemma
3.2
, we arrive at

\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla(n,\phi)(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla w(r)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\psi(r)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}r\leq C\delta_{0}^{2}.

Thus, we finish the proof of Proposition
3.8
.

4
Large-time behavior of the classical solution

4.1
Time-decay rates for the linearized problem

In this subsection, we consider the linearized case of the system (
3.3
)-(
3.6
) as follows

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n+c\,{\rm{div}}w=0,\\
&\partial_{t}w+c\nabla n+\sigma\nabla\phi=\nu\Delta w+(\nu+\eta)\nabla{\rm{div}}w,\\
&\partial_{t}\phi+\sigma{\rm{div}}w+b\text{div}\psi=0,\\
&\partial_{t}\psi+\frac{1}{\tau}\psi+b\nabla\phi=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

with the initial data

(n,w,\phi,\psi)(x,0)=(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})(x),

(4.2)

and the far-field states

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}(n,w,\phi,\psi)(x,t)=(0,0,0,0).

(4.3)

Let
U=(n,w,\phi,\psi)^{\top},U_{0}=(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})^{\top}
. The problem (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) can be rewritten as

\partial_{t}U+\mathcal{L}U=0,\quad U(x,0)=U_{0}(x),\quad t\geq 0,

(4.4)

where the linear operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined as

\mathcal{L}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&c\,\rm{div}&0&0\\
c\nabla&-\nu\Delta-(\nu+\eta)\nabla{\rm{div}}&\sigma\nabla&0\\
0&\sigma\rm{div}&0&b\text{div}\\
0&0&b\nabla&\frac{1}{\tau}\mathbb{I}_{3}\end{array}\right).

Let
G(x,t)=(G_{ij})_{4\times 4}
be the Green function of (
4.4
). After taking Fourier transform in
x
, we have

\partial_{t}\hat{G}(\xi,t)+B\hat{G}(\xi,t)=0,\quad\hat{G}(\xi,0)=\mathbb{I}_{8},

where
B=\mathscr{F}[\mathcal{L}](\xi)
satisifes

B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&ic\xi^{t}&0&0\\
ic\xi&\nu|\xi|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{3}+(\nu+\eta)\xi\xi^{t}&i\sigma\xi&0\\
0&i\sigma\xi^{t}&0&ib\xi^{t}\\
0&0&ib\xi&\frac{1}{\tau}\mathbb{I}_{3}\end{array}\right).

The eigenvalues of
B
consist of the double eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}=-\nu|\xi|^{2}
and
\lambda_{2}=-\frac{1}{\tau},
and the other four eigenvalues recorded as
\lambda_{k},(k=3,...,6)
satisfying the following algebraic equation

\displaystyle\tau\lambda^{4}+(1+\tau(2\nu+\eta)|\xi|^{2})\lambda^{3}+(\tau(c^{2}+b^{2}+\sigma^{2})+2\nu+\eta)|\xi|^{2}\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle+(c^{2}+\sigma^{2}+\tau b^{2}(2\nu+\eta)|\xi|^{2})|\xi|^{2}\lambda+\tau c^{2}b^{2}|\xi|^{4}=0.

After a tedious but straightforward calculation, we obtain the explicit formula of the Fourier transform of the Green function

\hat{G}(\xi,t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\hat{G}_{11}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{12}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{13}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{14}(\xi,t)\\
\hat{G}_{21}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{22}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{23}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{24}(\xi,t)\\
\hat{G}_{31}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{32}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{33}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{34}(\xi,t)\\
\hat{G}_{41}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{42}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{43}(\xi,t)&\hat{G}_{44}(\xi,t)\\
\end{array}\right),

where
\hat{G}_{ij}(\xi,t)
are written explicitly as

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{11}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{-c^{2}|\xi|^{2}g_{k}e^{\lambda_{k}t}}{\prod_{j\neq k}\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{12}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{21}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{-cg_{k}e^{\lambda_{k}t}i\xi}{\prod_{j\neq k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{13}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{31}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{-c\sigma|\xi|^{2}e^{\lambda_{k}t}(\lambda_{k}+\frac{1}{\tau})}{\prod_{j\neq k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{14}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{41}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{c\sigma b|\xi|^{2}e^{\lambda_{k}t}i\xi}{\prod_{j\neq k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{22}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{g_{k}\lambda_{k}e^{\lambda_{k}t}\xi\xi^{t}}{\prod_{j\neq k}|\xi|^{2}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})}+\Big(\mathbb{I}_{3}-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)e^{\lambda_{1}t},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{23}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{32}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{-\sigma\lambda_{k}e^{\lambda_{k}t}(\lambda_{k}+\frac{1}{\tau})i\xi}{\prod_{j\neq k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{24}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{42}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{-\sigma b\lambda_{k}e^{\lambda_{k}t}\xi\xi^{t}}{\prod_{j\neq k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{33}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{e^{\lambda_{k}t}(\lambda_{k}+\frac{1}{\tau})h_{k}}{\prod_{j\neq k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{34}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{43}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{-bh_{k}e^{\lambda_{k}t}i\xi}{\prod_{j\neq k}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{44}(\xi,t)=\sum_{k=3}^{6}\frac{\lambda_{k}(h_{k}+\sigma^{2}|\xi|^{2})e^{\lambda_{k}t}\xi\xi^{t}}{\prod_{j\neq k}|\xi|^{2}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{j})}+\Big(\mathbb{I}_{3}-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)e^{\lambda_{2}t},

and the functions
g_{k}
and
h_{k}
satisfy

g_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\lambda_{k}+b^{2}|\xi|^{2},\quad h_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{2}+(2\nu+\eta)|\xi|^{2}\lambda_{k}+c^{2}|\xi|^{2},\quad k=3,...,6.

The asymptotic behaviors of the eigenvalues are established in the following lemma, which can be proved by applying the implicit function theorem. Here we omit the proof for brevity.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume the positive constant
r_{0}
is sufficiently small and
R_{0}
is sufficiently large.

•

For low-frequency part
|\xi|\leq r_{0}
, the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}\,(k=3,...,6)
satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{3}=-\frac{1}{\tau}+\tau b^{2}|\xi|^{2}+O(|\xi|^{4}),\\
&\lambda_{4}=-\frac{\tau b^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+c^{2})}|\xi|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(2\nu+\eta)|\xi|^{2}+i\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}}|\xi|+O(|\xi|^{3}),\\
&\lambda_{5}=-\frac{\tau b^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+c^{2})}|\xi|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}(2\nu+\eta)|\xi|^{2}-i\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}}|\xi|+O(|\xi|^{3}),\\
&\lambda_{6}=-\frac{\tau b^{2}c^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}}|\xi|^{2}+O(|\xi|^{4}).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.5)

In addition, there exists a positive constant
\beta
such that

{\rm{Re}}\lambda_{k}\leq-\beta|\xi|^{2},\quad k=3,...,6.

(4.6)

•

For high-frequency part
|\xi|\geq R_{0}
, the eigenvalues satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lambda_{3}=-\frac{c^{2}}{2\nu+\eta}+O(|\xi|^{-2}),\\
&\lambda_{4}=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2(2\nu+\eta)}-\frac{1}{2\tau}+ib|\xi|+O(|\xi|^{-1}),\\
&\lambda_{5}=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2(2\nu+\eta)}-\frac{1}{2\tau}-ib|\xi|+O(|\xi|^{-1}),\\
&\lambda_{6}=-(2\nu+\eta)|\xi|^{2}+\frac{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}{2\nu+\eta}+O(|\xi|^{-1}).\end{aligned}\right.

(4.7)

In addition, there exists a positive constant
R_{1}
such that

{\rm{Re}}\lambda_{k}\leq-R_{1},\quad k=3,...,6.

(4.8)

•

For medium-frequency part
r_{0}\leq|\xi|\leq R_{0}
, the eigenvalues satisfy

{\rm{Re}}\lambda_{k}\leq-R_{2},\quad k=3,...,6,

(4.9)

where
R_{2}
is a positive constant.

Now we are in a position to establish time-decay estimates of the solution
(n,w,\phi,\psi)
to the problem (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) for some special initial data.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that the conditions in Theorem
1.2
hold. Then, the global solution
(n,w,\phi,\psi)
of the linearized problem (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) satisfies, for large time
t
, that

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq\|(n,w,\phi)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}},

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\leq\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}},

(4.10)

where
\bar{c}_{1}
is a positive constant independent of time.

Proof
.
The solution
\hat{U}(\xi,t)=(\hat{n},\hat{w},\hat{\phi},\hat{\psi})^{\top}
for the linear problem (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) can be solved as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\hat{n}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{11}\hat{n}_{0}+\hat{G}_{12}\hat{w}_{0}+\hat{G}_{13}\hat{\phi}_{0}+\hat{G}_{14}\hat{\psi}_{0},\\
&\hat{w}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{21}\hat{n}_{0}+\hat{G}_{22}\hat{w}_{0}+\hat{G}_{23}\hat{\phi}_{0}+\hat{G}_{24}\hat{\psi}_{0},\\
&\hat{\phi}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{31}\hat{n}_{0}+\hat{G}_{32}\hat{w}_{0}+\hat{G}_{33}\hat{\phi}_{0}+\hat{G}_{34}\hat{\psi}_{0},\\
&\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{41}\hat{n}_{0}+\hat{G}_{42}\hat{w}_{0}+\hat{G}_{43}\hat{\phi}_{0}+\hat{G}_{44}\hat{\psi}_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

For the low-frequency part
|\xi|\leq r_{0}
, by a tedious calculation, we can obtain the leading term of
\hat{G}_{ij}
as

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{11}(\xi,t)=-c^{2}b^{4}\tau^{6}|\xi|^{6}e^{\lambda_{3}t}+\frac{c^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{4}t}+\frac{c^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{5}t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{12}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{21}(\xi,t)=cb^{4}\tau^{5}|\xi|^{4}i\xi e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\frac{c\xi}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}e^{\lambda_{4}t}+\frac{c\xi}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}e^{\lambda_{5}t}-\frac{cb^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau}{(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t}i\xi,

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{13}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{31}(\xi,t)=cb^{2}\sigma\tau^{4}|\xi|^{4}e^{\lambda_{3}t}+\frac{c\sigma}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{4}t}+\frac{c\sigma}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{5}t}-\frac{c\sigma}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{14}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{41}(\xi,t)=-cb\sigma\tau^{3}|\xi|^{2}i\xi e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\frac{cb\sigma\tau}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{4}t}i\xi+\frac{cb\sigma\tau}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{5}t}i\xi+\frac{cb\sigma\tau}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t}i\xi,

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{22}(\xi,t)=\Big(\mathbb{I}_{3}-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)e^{\lambda_{1}t}+b^{4}\tau^{4}|\xi|^{2}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{3}t}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}e^{\lambda_{4}t}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}e^{\lambda_{5}t}-\frac{c^{2}b^{4}\sigma^{2}\tau^{2}}{(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{3}}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{6}t},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{23}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{32}(\xi,t)=-b^{2}\sigma\tau^{3}|\xi|^{2}i\xi e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\frac{\sigma\xi}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}e^{\lambda_{4}t}+\frac{\sigma\xi}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}e^{\lambda_{5}t}+\frac{c^{2}b^{2}\sigma\tau}{(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{2}}i\xi e^{\lambda_{6}t},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{24}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{42}(\xi,t)=-b\sigma\tau^{2}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{3}t}+\frac{ib\sigma\tau}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{4}t}-\frac{ib\sigma\tau}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{5}t}+\frac{c^{2}b^{3}\sigma\tau^{2}}{(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{2}}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{6}t},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{33}(\xi,t)=-b^{2}\tau^{2}|\xi|^{2}e^{\lambda_{3}t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{4}t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{5}t}+\frac{c^{2}}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{34}(\xi,t)=\hat{G}_{43}(\xi,t)=b\tau i\xi e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\frac{b\sigma^{2}\tau}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}i\xi e^{\lambda_{4}t}-\frac{b\sigma^{2}\tau}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}i\xi e^{\lambda_{5}t}-\frac{c^{2}b\tau}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}i\xi e^{\lambda_{6}t},

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{44}(\xi,t)=\Big(\mathbb{I}_{3}-\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}\Big)e^{\lambda_{2}t}+\frac{\xi\xi^{t}}{|\xi|^{2}}e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\frac{b^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{4}t}-\frac{b^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{5}t}-\frac{c^{2}b^{2}\tau^{2}}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{6}t}.

For
|\xi|\geq r_{0}
, we follow a similar method developed in
[
18
]
to prove the boundedness of

where

.

Therefore, due to Parseval’s equality and Lemma
4.1
, it is enough to estimate the decay rate of solution

\displaystyle\|n(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\hat{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\hat{G}_{11}\hat{n}_{0}+\hat{G}_{12}\hat{w}_{0}+\hat{G}_{13}\hat{\phi}_{0}+\hat{G}_{14}\hat{\psi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|-c^{2}b^{4}\tau^{6}|\xi|^{6}e^{\lambda_{3}t}+\frac{c^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{4}t}+\frac{c^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{5}t}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t}\big\|_{L^{2}}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle+\big\|cb^{4}\tau^{5}|\xi|^{4}i\xi e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\frac{c\xi}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}e^{\lambda_{4}t}+\frac{c\xi}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}e^{\lambda_{5}t}-\frac{cb^{2}\sigma^{2}\tau}{(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t}i\xi\big\|_{L^{2}}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle+Ce^{-Rt}\|(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(\|U_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}})

The same process can be repeated for
w(x,t)
and

For
\psi(x,t)

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\hat{\psi}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\hat{G}_{41}\hat{n}_{0}+\hat{G}_{42}\hat{w}_{0}+\hat{G}_{43}\hat{\phi}_{0}+\hat{G}_{44}\hat{\psi}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|-cb\sigma\tau^{3}|\xi|^{2}i\xi e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\frac{cb\sigma\tau}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{4}t}i\xi+\frac{cb\sigma\tau}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}e^{\lambda_{5}t}i\xi+\frac{cb\sigma\tau}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t}i\xi\|_{L^{2}}\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle~~+\|-b\sigma\tau^{2}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{3}t}+\frac{ib\sigma\tau}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{4}t}-\frac{ib\sigma\tau}{2\sqrt{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}|\xi|}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{5}t}+\frac{c^{2}b^{3}\sigma\tau^{2}}{(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})^{2}}\xi\xi^{t}e^{\lambda_{6}t}\|_{L^{2}}\|w_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle~~+\|b\tau i\xi e^{\lambda_{3}t}-\frac{b\sigma^{2}\tau}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}i\xi e^{\lambda_{4}t}-\frac{b\sigma^{2}\tau}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}i\xi e^{\lambda_{5}t}-\frac{c^{2}b\tau}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}i\xi e^{\lambda_{6}t}\|_{L^{2}}\|\phi_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}}.

Now we are going to establish the lower bound of the time-decay rates. For
|\xi|\leq r_{0}
, since
\hat{w}_{0}(\xi)=\hat{\phi}_{0}(\xi)=0
, we write
\hat{n}(\xi,t)
as follows

\displaystyle\hat{n}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\left(-c^{2}b^{4}\tau^{6}|\xi|^{6}e^{\lambda_{3}t}+\frac{c^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}(e^{\lambda_{4}t}+e^{\lambda_{5}t})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t}\right)\hat{n}_{0}(\xi)

\displaystyle\quad+\left(-cb\sigma\tau^{3}|\xi|^{2}e^{\lambda_{3}t}+\frac{cb\sigma\tau}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}(e^{\lambda_{5}t}-e^{\lambda_{4}t})+\frac{cb\sigma\tau}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t}\right)i\xi\cdot\hat{\psi}_{0}(\xi)

\displaystyle\triangleq K_{1}+K_{2}.

(4.11)

To deal with the term

K_{1}=-c^{2}b^{4}\tau^{6}|\xi|^{6}e^{\lambda_{3}t}\hat{n}_{0}(\xi)+\left(\frac{c^{2}}{2(c^{2}+\sigma^{2})}(e^{\lambda_{4}t}+e^{\lambda_{5}t})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c^{2}+\sigma^{2}}e^{\lambda_{6}t}\right)\hat{n}_{0}(\xi)\triangleq K_{11}+K_{12}.

(4.12)

First, a direct calculation yields

\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|K_{11}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}\big|c^{2}b^{4}\tau^{6}|\xi|^{6}e^{\lambda_{3}t}\hat{n}_{0}(\xi)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq C\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\big||\xi|^{6}e^{\lambda_{3}t}\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq Ce^{-\frac{1}{2\tau}t}.

(4.13)

Let
\nu_{1}=\frac{\tau b^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2(\sigma^{2}+c^{2})}+\frac{1}{2}(2\nu+\eta)
,
\hat{c}=\sqrt{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}}
,
\nu_{2}=\frac{\tau b^{2}c^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+c^{2}}
. Then, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|K_{12}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\quad-C\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}e^{-2\upsilon_{1}|\xi|^{2}t}\mathcal{O}(|\xi|^{6}t^{2})\mathrm{d}\xi-C\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}e^{-2\nu_{2}|\xi|^{2}t}\mathcal{O}(|\xi|^{8}t^{2})\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq C\inf_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|\hat{n}_{0}(\xi)|^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}e^{-(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})|\xi|^{2}t}(1+\cos(\hat{c}|\xi|t))^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\quad-C\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}(e^{-2\nu_{1}|\xi|^{2}t}\mathcal{O}(|\xi|^{6}t^{2})+e^{-2\nu_{2}|\xi|^{2}t}\mathcal{O}(|\xi|^{8}t^{2}))\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq C\mu_{0}^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}e^{-(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})|\xi|^{2}t}(1+\cos(\hat{c}|\xi|t))^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\quad-C\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}(e^{-2\nu_{1}|\xi|^{2}t}\mathcal{O}(|\xi|^{6}t^{2})+e^{-2\nu_{2}|\xi|^{2}t}\mathcal{O}(|\xi|^{8}t^{2}))\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\triangleq Z_{1}+Z_{2}.

(4.14)

Let
m=\sqrt{t}|\xi|
. For large time
t\geq t_{0}
, we obtain

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=C\mu_{0}^{2}t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\int_{0}^{r_{0}\sqrt{t}}e^{-(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})m^{2}}(1+\text{cos}(\hat{c}m\sqrt{t}))^{2}m^{2}\mathrm{d}m

\displaystyle\geq C\mu_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{[\hat{c}r_{0}t/\pi]-1}\int_{2k\pi/(\hat{c}\sqrt{t})}^{(2k\pi+\frac{\pi}{3})/(\hat{c}\sqrt{t})}e^{-(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})m^{2}}(1+\text{cos}(\hat{c}m\sqrt{t}))^{2}m^{2}\mathrm{d}m

\displaystyle\geq\frac{9}{4}C\mu_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{[\hat{c}r_{0}t/\pi]-1}\int_{2k\pi/(\hat{c}\sqrt{t})}^{(2k\pi+\frac{\pi}{3})/(\hat{c}\sqrt{t})}e^{-(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2})m^{2}}m^{2}\mathrm{d}m

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{g}\mu_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

(4.15)

where
\tilde{g}
is a positive constant. For the term
Z_{2}
, it is easy to verify that

|Z_{2}|\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}.

(4.16)

Substituting (
4.15
) and (
4.16
) into (
4.14
) yields

\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|K_{12}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\geq\tilde{g}\mu_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}-C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}},

which, together with (
4.13
) and (
4.12
), yields

\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|K_{1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\geq\tilde{g}\mu_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}-C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}-Ce^{-\frac{1}{2\tau}t}.

(4.17)

It is easy to show

\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|K_{2}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}.

(4.18)

Thus, for large-time
t\geq t_{0}
, combining (
4.17
) and (
4.18
), we obtain from (
4.11
) that

\displaystyle\|\hat{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|K_{1}+K_{2}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|K_{1}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi-\int_{|\xi|\leq r_{0}}|K_{2}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\tilde{g}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}-C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}-Ce^{-\frac{1}{2\tau}t}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\tilde{g}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\triangleq\tilde{c}_{1}^{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},

(4.19)

with a positive constant
\tilde{c}_{1}>0
. Therefore, Parseval’s equality and (
4.19
) yield

\|n(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|\hat{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\tilde{c}_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

Similarly, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|w(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\tilde{c}_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}},\quad\|\phi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\tilde{c}_{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}},\quad\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\tilde{c}_{4}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}},

where the positive constants
\tilde{c}_{2},\tilde{c}_{3},\tilde{c}_{4}
are independent of time.

Choosing
\bar{c}_{1}=\min\{\tilde{c}_{1},\tilde{c}_{2},\tilde{c}_{3},\tilde{c}_{4}\}
, then we complete the proof of Proposition
4.2
.

4.2
Nonlinear time-decay estimates

To investigate the upper bound time-decay rates of classical solutions to the nonlinear system (
3.3
), we first introduce the energy
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{s}(t)
as

\mathcal{E}_{k}^{s}(t)\triangleq\|\nabla^{k}(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s-k}}^{2},\quad 0\leq k\leq s,

(4.20)

and the time-weighted energy functional

\mathcal{M}(t)\triangleq\sup_{0\leq r\leq t}\left\{(1+r)^{\frac{3}{4}}\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(r)\|_{H^{s}}\right\}.

(4.21)

Then, it yields

\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\mathcal{M}(t).

(4.22)

The next goal is to prove the uniform time-independent bound of
\mathcal{M}(t)
.

Let
U=(n,w,\phi,\psi)^{\top},U_{0}=(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})^{\top},F=(f_{1},f_{2},f_{3},0)^{\top}
. We rewrite the nonlinear system (
3.3
) into the operator form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}U+\mathcal{L}U=F,\\
&U|_{t=0}=U_{0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Making use of Duhamel’s principle yields

U(x,t)=G*U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}G(t-r)*F(r)\mathrm{d}r.

(4.23)

To analyze the large-time behavior of the solution in frequency space, we introduce the low-high frequency decomposition. Decompose the solution into two parts:

U(x,t)=U^{\ell}(x,t)+U^{h}(x,t)=(n^{\ell},w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell},\psi^{\ell})^{\top}+(n^{h},w^{h},\phi^{h},\psi^{h})^{\top}.

(4.24)

Here
U^{\ell}(x,t)=P_{1}U(x,t)
and
U^{h}(x,t)=P_{\infty}U(x,t)
represent the low-frequency part and high-frequency part, respectively. The projection operator
P_{1}
and
P_{\infty}
are introduced in Section
2
. It is easy to verify that for
0\leq k<m
,

\|\nabla^{m}U^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{k}U^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{k}U^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{m}U^{h}\|_{L^{2}},

(4.25)

\|\nabla^{m}U^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{m}U\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{m}U^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{m}U\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.26)

Now, we are in a position to establish the decay estimates for
\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s}}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that the conditions in Theorem
1.1
hold. Let
(n,w,\phi,\psi)
be the classical solution of the problem (
3.3
)-(
3.6
), then it holds

\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}},

(4.27)

where the positive constant
C
is independent of time.

Proof
.
According to (
3.48
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\nabla n(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla w(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\psi(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq 0.

By the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{0}^{s}(t)
, one has

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}^{s}_{0}(t)+\|\nabla n(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s-1}}+\|\nabla w(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\psi(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq 0,

which implies that there exists a positive constant
C_{2}
such that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}^{s}_{0}(t)+C_{2}\mathcal{E}^{s}_{0}(t)\leq C\|(n^{\ell},w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell},\psi^{\ell})(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

(4.28)

where we have used the fact that
\|(n^{h},w^{h},\phi^{h},\psi^{h})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{L^{2}}
.

In terms of Duhamel’s principle, the low-frequency part
U^{\ell}=(n^{\ell},w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell},\psi^{\ell})
can be written as

U^{\ell}(x,t)=G^{\ell}*U_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}G^{\ell}(t-r)*F(r)\mathrm{d}r.

(4.29)

By Parseval’s equality and H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder’s inequality, one has

\displaystyle\|n^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|G^{\ell}_{11}*n_{0}+G^{\ell}_{12}*w_{0}+G^{\ell}_{13}*\phi_{0}+G^{\ell}_{14}*\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\|G^{\ell}_{11}(t-r)*f_{1}(r)+G^{\ell}_{12}(t-r)*f_{2}(r)+G^{\ell}_{13}(t-r)*f_{3}(r)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{L^{1}}+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq CA_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}+C\mathcal{M}(t)\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\quad\times(\|\nabla(n,w,\phi,\psi)(r)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}w(r)\|_{L^{2}})\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq CA_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}+C\mathcal{M}(t)\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{3}{2}}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(\|\nabla(n,w,\phi,\psi)(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}w(r)\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\mathrm{d}r\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\big(A_{0}+\delta_{0}\mathcal{M}(t)\big),

(4.30)

where
A_{0}\triangleq\|(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{L^{1}}
. Similarly, we can prove

\|(w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\big(A_{0}+\delta_{0}\mathcal{M}(t)\big),\quad\|\psi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\big(A_{0}+\delta_{0}\mathcal{M}(t)\big).

(4.31)

Substituting (
4.30
) and (
4.31
) into (
4.28
) yields

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}^{s}_{0}(t)+C_{2}\mathcal{E}^{s}_{0}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(A_{0}+\delta_{0}\mathcal{M}(t)\right)^{2}.

With the help of Grönwall’s inequality, it is easy to show

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{s}_{0}(t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-C_{2}t}\mathcal{E}^{s}_{0}(0)+C\int_{0}^{t}e^{-C_{2}(t-r)}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(A_{0}+\delta_{0}\mathcal{M}(t)\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\left(\delta_{0}^{2}+A_{0}^{2}+\delta_{0}^{2}\mathcal{M}(t)^{2}\right),

which implies that

(1+t)^{\frac{3}{2}}\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq C\left(\delta_{0}^{2}+A_{0}^{2}+\delta_{0}^{2}\mathcal{M}(t)^{2}\right).

(4.32)

It then follows from (
4.32
) that

\mathcal{M}(t)^{2}\leq C(\delta_{0}^{2}+A_{0}^{2}+\delta_{0}^{2}\mathcal{M}(t)^{2}).

Due to the smallness of
\delta_{0}
and the boundedness of
A_{0}
, it gives

\mathcal{M}(t)\leq C(\delta_{0}+A_{0}).

(4.33)

Noting that since
(\rho_{0}-\rho_{*},u_{0},\theta_{0}-\theta_{*},q_{0})\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we can obtain from (
3.4
) that
A_{0}=\|(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{L^{1}}
is bounded. As a result of (
4.33
) and (
4.22
), it holds

\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq C(\delta_{0}+A_{0})(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.

Next, we are going to enhance the time-decay rate of the derivatives
\nabla^{k}(n,w,\phi,\psi)
in
L^{2}
norm for
1\leq k\leq s
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume that the conditions in Theorem

be the classical solution of the problem (

)-(

(4.34)

and

In terms of Lemmas
3.3
-
3.5

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3\nu}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3(\nu+\eta)}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(4.36)

Applying the operator
\nabla^{\alpha-1}P_{\infty}
to
\eqref{nsac}_{2}

\displaystyle\triangleq X_{1}+X_{2}+X_{3}.

(4.37)

The first term

\displaystyle|X_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq(\nu+\eta)(\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+\sigma\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

(4.38)

where

is arbitrary. For the term
X_{2}

in (

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}P_{\infty}(w\cdot\nabla w)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}P_{\infty}(w\cdot\nabla w)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}(w\cdot\nabla w)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}P_{1}(w\cdot\nabla w)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|.

(4.39)

It is easy to prove that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}(w\cdot\nabla w)\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}[\nabla^{\alpha}(w\cdot\nabla w)-w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w]\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\cdot\nabla^{\alpha-1}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{H^{1}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\nabla w\|_{H^{1}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

where we have used the fact that
\|\nabla^{\alpha-1}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}
.

4.39

\displaystyle|X_{2}|

\displaystyle|X_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla^{\alpha}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\delta_{0}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(4.40)

Combining the estimates of
X_{i}

(i=1,2,3)

and

yield

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C(\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(4.41)

Similarly, it also holds for
\alpha\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\frac{3b}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+C(\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(4.42)

Taking

, one has

\displaystyle\frac{bc^{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{b}{2}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}+\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+C(\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(4.43)

Let the positive constant

be sufficiently small. Then, taking

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\beta_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}+\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\Big\}

\displaystyle+\frac{3\nu}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3(\nu+\eta)}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2\tau}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{bc^{2}\beta_{2}}{8\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{b\beta_{2}}{2}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle+C\delta_{0}\beta_{2}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+C\beta_{2}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

Thanks to the smallness of
\beta_{2}
and
\delta_{0}
, we verify for
\alpha\geq 1
that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\beta_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}+\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\nu+\eta}{2}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4\tau}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{bc^{2}\beta_{2}}{16\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{b\beta_{2}}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta_{0}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(4.44)

It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\beta_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\frac{bc}{4\sigma^{2}}\nabla^{\alpha-1}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}+\nabla^{\alpha-1}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\Big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\sim(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

which, together with (
4.44
), (
4.25
) and (
4.26
), yields the following results, for
\alpha\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+C_{3}(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}w^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{\alpha}\psi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

(4.45)

where
C_{3}
is a positive constant independent of time. Summing up (
4.45
) from
k\leq\alpha\leq s
and using the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{k}^{s}(t)
, one has

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{s}(t)+C_{4}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{s}(t)\leq C\|\nabla^{k}(n^{\ell},w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell},\psi^{\ell})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.46)

To investigate the time-decay rates of the norm
\|\nabla(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}
, we follow in a similar method as above. First, we calculate

\displaystyle\|\nabla(n^{\ell},w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell})(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\|(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{L^{1}}+C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\|F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|\nabla F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\mathrm{d}r,

(4.47)

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\|(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{L^{1}}+C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\|F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\|\nabla F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\mathrm{d}r.

(4.48)

Next, we need to estimate the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.47
). By the definition of
F
, (
3.8
), (
3.6
) and (
4.27
), one has

\displaystyle\|F(r)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|n\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}+\|n\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|n\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}+\|n\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

(4.49)

Similarly, we also have

\|\nabla F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

(4.50)

Substituting (
4.49
), (
4.50
) into (
4.47
), (
4.48
) yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla(n^{\ell},w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell})(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}}+C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{5}{4}}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+C\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}},

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla\psi^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{7}{4}}+C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{7}{4}}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+C\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{5}{4}}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}.

By (
4.46
), we have

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{s}(t)+C_{4}\mathcal{E}_{1}^{s}(t)\leq C(\|\nabla(n^{\ell},w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\psi^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}.

It holds

\mathcal{E}_{1}^{s}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}},

which implies that

\|\nabla(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s-1}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}},

(4.51)

for
s\geq 3
. Based on the above facts, we can further show that, for high-order derivatives
(1\leq k\leq s)
, it holds

\|\nabla^{k}(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s-k}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}.

(4.52)

In fact, we can prove the above time-decay rates (
4.52
) by induction method. The case of
k=1
is proved by (
4.51
). Then, we assume it holds for
k-1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k-1}(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s-k+1}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad 2\leq k\leq s.

The next goal is to establish the time-decay rates of
\|\nabla^{k}(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s-k}}
for
2\leq k\leq s
. Noting that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}(n^{\ell},w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell},\psi^{\ell})(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\|(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{L^{1}}+C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\|F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad+C\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{7}{4}}\|\nabla^{k-2}F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\mathrm{d}r.

(4.53)

It should be noted that at this moment, we have

\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}},\quad\|\nabla^{k-1}(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s-k+1}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}.

As a result, we obtain

\displaystyle\|F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+r)^{-2},\quad\|\nabla^{k-2}F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}.

(4.54)

Substituting (
4.54
) into (
4.53
) yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}(n^{\ell},w^{\ell},\phi^{\ell},\psi^{\ell})(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}+C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}(1+r)^{-2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle+C\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{7}{4}}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}.

In view of (
4.46
), it holds

\frac{d}{\mathrm{d}r}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{s}(t)+C_{4}\mathcal{E}_{k}^{s}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}-k}.

(4.55)

Applying Grönwall’s inequality to (
4.55
), we have

\mathcal{E}_{k}^{s}(t)\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}-k},

which implies for
1\leq k\leq s
and
s\geq 3
that

\|\nabla^{k}(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s-k}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}.

(4.56)

This completes the proof of (
4.52
).

To enhance the time-decay rate of
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}
, we need to apply the damping structure of
\psi
. By
\eqref{nsac}_{4}
, the solution
\psi
can be written as

\displaystyle\psi(x,t)=e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}t}\psi_{0}-b\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}(t-r)}\nabla\phi(x,r)\mathrm{d}r.

In terms of (
4.56
), one has

\displaystyle\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}t}\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+b\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\frac{1}{\tau}(t-r)}\|\nabla\phi(r)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{5}{4}}(1+r)^{-\frac{5}{4}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}}.

It then follows the same method for the derivatives
\nabla^{k}\psi
to show that

\|\nabla^{k}\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq k\leq s-1.

(4.57)

The combination of (
4.56
) and (
4.57
) implies for
1\leq k\leq s-1
that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}(n,w,\phi)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{k}\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},

and

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{s}(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{s}{2}}.

Therefore, we complete the proof Lemma
4.4
.

It should be pointed out that at this moment, the time-decay rate of the highest derivatives of
\psi
is not optimal and can be improved. Thus, we aim to establish the decay estimates
\|\nabla^{s}\psi\|_{L^{2}}
under some new observations in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume that the conditions in Theorem
1.1
hold. Let
(n,w,\phi,\psi)
be the classical solution of the system (
3.3
)-(
3.6
). Then it holds

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{s}\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{s}{2}},

where the positive constant
C
is independent of time.

Proof
.
Applying the operator
P_{\infty}
to
(
3.3
)
, the high-frequency part of the solution can be written as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}n^{h}+c\,{\rm{div}}w^{h}=f_{1}^{h},\\
&\partial_{t}w^{h}+c\nabla n^{h}+\sigma\nabla\phi^{h}=\nu\Delta w^{h}+(\nu+\eta)\nabla{\rm{div}}w^{h}+f_{2}^{h},\\
&\partial_{t}\phi^{h}+\sigma{\rm{div}}w^{h}+b\text{div}\psi^{h}=f_{3}^{h},\\
&\partial_{t}\psi^{h}+\frac{1}{\tau}\psi^{h}+b\nabla\phi^{h}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(4.58)

where the nonliner terms
f_{i}^{h}=P_{\infty}f_{i}
and
f_{i}
are defined in (
3.6
).

Applying the operator
\nabla^{s}
to
\eqref{nsac1}_{1}
, multiplying the resulting equation by
\nabla^{s}n^{h}
, and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{1}^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{1}\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{1}^{\ell}\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x.

By Lemma
2.2
, (
3.48
) and H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder’s inequality, one has

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{1}\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq c\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}(w\cdot\nabla n)\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\Big|+c\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}(n\text{div}w)\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\Big|

\displaystyle\leq c\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla^{s}(w\cdot\nabla n)-w\cdot\nabla^{s}\nabla n)\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|+c\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot\nabla\nabla^{s}n\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\quad+c\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla^{s}(n\text{div}w)-n\nabla^{s}\text{div}w)\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|+c\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}n\nabla^{s}\text{div}w\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}w\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\text{div}w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C(\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}\|\text{div}w\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}w\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}+C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}+C\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}w\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}+C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}+C\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+t)^{-3-s},

where we have used (
4.35
) and the fact that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{\alpha}(n,w,\phi,\psi)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\nabla^{\alpha+1}(n,w,\phi,\psi)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha+2}(n,w,\phi,\psi)\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\alpha}{2}},~~~0\leq\alpha\leq s-2.

It also holds

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{1}^{\ell}\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla^{s-1}f_{1}^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla^{s-1}f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla^{s-1}(w\cdot\nabla n)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{s-1}(n\text{div}w)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla^{s-1}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C(\|\nabla^{s-1}n\|_{L^{2}}\|\text{div}w\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}w\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+t)^{-3-s}.

(4.59)

Similarly, applying the operator
\nabla^{s}
to
\eqref{nsac1}_{2}
, multiplying the resulting equation by
\nabla^{s}w^{h}
, and then integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla^{s}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x-\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3\nu}{4}\|\nabla^{s}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3(\nu+\eta)}{4}\|\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}.

(4.60)

Applying
\nabla^{s}
to
\eqref{nsac1}_{3}
, and taking the inner product with
\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}
over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{3}^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{3}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{3}^{\ell}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x.

(4.61)

The first term on the right-hand side of (
4.61
) is estimated below

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{3}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=-c\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}(w\cdot\nabla\phi)\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x-c(\gamma-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}(\phi\text{div}w)\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\sigma}{c}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}\Big(\frac{2\nu|\mathbb{D}w|^{2}+\eta({\rm{div}}w)^{2}}{1+n}\Big)\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}\Big(\frac{n\text{div}\psi}{1+n}\Big)\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\triangleq Y_{1}+Y_{2}+Y_{3}+Y_{4}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}(w\cdot\nabla\phi)\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(\nabla^{s}(w\cdot\nabla\phi)-w\cdot\nabla^{s}\nabla\phi)\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot\nabla^{s}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}w\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle~~+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot\nabla^{s}\nabla\phi^{\ell}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}w\cdot\nabla^{s}\nabla\phi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}w\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{s}\phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle~~+C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s+1}\phi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\text{div}w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}w\|_{L^{2}})\|\nabla^{s}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+C\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s},

which implies that

\displaystyle|Y_{1}|\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}.

(4.62)

In the same way, one has

\displaystyle|Y_{2}|+|Y_{3}|\leq\varepsilon(\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+C(1+t)^{-3-s}.

(4.63)

Noting that
Y_{4}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle Y_{4}

\displaystyle=b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\nabla^{s}\Big(\frac{n\text{div}\psi}{1+n}\Big)-\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{s}\text{div}\psi\Big)\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{s}\text{div}\psi\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\nabla^{s}\Big(\frac{n\text{div}\psi}{1+n}\Big)-\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{s}\text{div}\psi\Big)\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{s}\text{div}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{s}\text{div}\psi^{\ell}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\triangleq Y_{41}+Y_{42}+Y_{43}.

It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle|Y_{41}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\Big\|\nabla\Big(\frac{n}{1+n}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\Big\|\nabla^{s}\Big(\frac{n}{1+n}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}}\|\text{div}\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{s}n\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla^{s}\phi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+t)^{-3-s}.

As for
Y_{42}
, in terms of integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle Y_{42}

\displaystyle=-b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\Big(\frac{n}{1+n}\Big)\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x-b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\nabla\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\triangleq Y_{421}+Y_{422}.

The first term
Y_{421}
is bounded by

\displaystyle|Y_{421}|\leq C\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{s}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{s}\phi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}.

With the help of
\eqref{nsac1}_{4}
and
\eqref{nsac1}_{1}
, the second term
Y_{422}
is calculated below

\displaystyle Y_{422}

\displaystyle=-b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\nabla\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}(\partial_{t}\psi^{h}+\frac{1}{\tau}\psi^{h})\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\frac{n}{1+n}\Big)_{t}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{\tau(1+n)}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n_{t}}{(1+n)^{2}}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{\tau(1+n)}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{f_{1}-c\text{div}w}{(1+n)^{2}}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{\tau(1+n)}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C(1+t)^{-3-s}.

It then concludes from above to get

\displaystyle|Y_{4}|\leq|Y_{41}|+|Y_{421}|+|Y_{422}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x+C(1+t)^{-3-s}.

(4.64)

It is easy to show that the second term on the right-hand side of (
4.61
) satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}f_{3}^{\ell}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}.

(4.65)

In terms of (
4.62
)-(
4.65
) and (
4.61
), we verify that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x+b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}+\varepsilon(\|\nabla^{s}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{n}{1+n}|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

(4.66)

Taking
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{\alpha}\eqref{nsac1}_{4}\cdot\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\mathrm{d}x
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-b\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x=0.

(4.67)

It then follows from (
4.66
) and (
4.67
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\frac{1}{\tau}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sigma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}+\varepsilon(\|\nabla^{s}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(4.68)

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s-1}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}n^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}+C(\|\nabla^{s}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{c}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(4.69)

and

\displaystyle\frac{3b}{4}\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s-1}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}+C(\|\nabla^{s}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

(4.70)

Choosing the suitably small constant
\beta_{3}>0
, taking (
4.59
)+(
4.60
)+(
4.68
)+
\frac{bc\beta_{3}}{4\sigma^{2}}\times
(
4.69
)+
\beta_{3}\times
(
4.70
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{bc\beta_{3}}{4\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sigma\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x+\beta_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s-1}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3\nu}{4}\|\nabla^{s}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{3(\nu+\eta)}{4}\|\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{bc^{2}\beta_{3}}{8\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{b\beta_{3}}{2}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s}+C(\varepsilon+\beta_{3})(\|\nabla^{s}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\text{div}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

Due to the smallness of
\varepsilon
and
\beta_{3}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big\{\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{bc\beta_{3}}{4\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sigma\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x+\beta_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s-1}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\nu}{2}\|\nabla^{s}\nabla w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{(\nu+\eta)}{2}\|\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\tau}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{bc^{2}\beta_{3}}{16\sigma^{2}}\|\nabla^{\alpha}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{b\beta_{3}}{4}\|\nabla^{\alpha}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s},

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{1+n}\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{bc\beta_{3}}{4\sigma^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\sigma\nabla^{s}{\rm{div}}w^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x+\beta_{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla^{s-1}\psi^{h}\cdot\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\sim(\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

As a consequence, it holds

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle+C_{5}\big(\|\nabla^{s}n^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}w^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\phi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-3-s},

where the positive constant
C_{5}
is independent of time. In terms of Grönwall’s inequality, one has

\|\nabla^{s}(n^{h},w^{h},\phi^{h},\psi^{h})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{s}{2}}.

(4.71)

Now we are in a position to establish the time-decay estimates of
\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}
. First, we have

\displaystyle\psi(x,t)=

\displaystyle G_{41}*n_{0}+G_{42}*w_{0}+G_{43}*\phi_{0}+G_{44}*\psi_{0}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\Big\{G_{41}(t-r)*f_{1}(r)+G_{42}(t-r)*f_{2}(r)+G_{43}(t-r)*f_{3}(r)\Big\}\mathrm{d}r.

Due to Parseval’s equality, one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\nabla^{s}G_{41}^{\ell}*n_{0}+\nabla^{s}G_{42}^{\ell}*w_{0}+\nabla^{s}G_{43}^{\ell}*\phi_{0}+\nabla^{s}G_{44}^{\ell}*\psi_{0}\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\nabla^{s}G_{41}^{\ell}(t-r)*f_{1}(r)+\nabla^{s}G_{42}^{\ell}(t-r)*f_{2}(r)+\nabla^{s}G_{43}^{\ell}(t-r)*f_{3}(r)\big\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{s}{2}}\|(n_{0},w_{0},\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\|_{L^{1}}+C\int_{0}^{\frac{t}{2}}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{s}{2}}(1+r)^{-2}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\frac{t}{2}}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{5}{4}}(1+r)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{s}{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{s}{2}},

which, together with (
4.71
), yields

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{s}\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{s}\psi^{h}(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{s}{2}}.

This completes the proof of this lemma.

5
The Proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2

Since the
a priori
estimates and the time-decay rates have been established in Sections
3
and
4
, we are now in a position to complete the proofs of our main results.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Proposition
3.8
, it gives

\|(n,w,\phi,\psi)(t)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\|\nabla n(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\nabla w(r)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\nabla\phi(r)\|_{H^{s-1}}^{2}+\|\psi(r)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}r\leq C\delta_{0}^{2}.

(5.1)

Choosing the initial data
\delta_{0}
sufficiently small such that
C\delta_{0}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\delta^{2}
, we could close the
a priori
assumption (
3.7
). By virtue of (
3.2
) and (
5.1
), we can obtain (
1.7
). Then, the global well-posedness of the solution is obtained based on the continuous argument.
It remains to establish the large-time behavior of the solution
(\rho,u,\theta,q)
. By Lemmas
4.3
,
4.4
and
4.5
, it is easy to verify that, for
0\leq k\leq s
,

\|\nabla^{k}(n,w,\phi)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{k}\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},

(5.2)

which, together with (
3.2
), yields (
1.8
). This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Due to Parseval’s equality and (
4.23
), one has

\displaystyle\|n(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\|G_{11}*n_{0}+G_{12}*w_{0}+G_{13}*\phi_{0}+G_{14}*\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{0}^{t}\|G_{11}(t-r)*f_{1}(r)+G_{12}(t-r)*f_{2}(r)+G_{13}(t-r)*f_{3}(r)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}r.

(5.3)

By the definition of
F
, (
3.8
), (
4.27
) and (
4.33
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|G_{11}(t-r)*f_{1}(r)+G_{12}(t-r)*f_{2}(r)+G_{13}(t-r)*f_{3}(r)\|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\big\{(1+t-r)^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|F(r)\|_{L^{1}}+e^{-R(t-r)}\|F(r)\|_{L^{2}}\big\}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{M}(t)\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(1+r)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(\|\nabla(n,w,\phi,\psi)(r)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}w(r)\|_{L^{2}})\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq C\mathcal{M}(t)\delta_{0}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(\delta_{0}^{2}+\delta_{0}A_{0}).

(5.4)

Since
\delta_{0}
is sufficiently small, substituting (
4.10
) and (
5.4
) into (
5.3
) yields

\|n(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\bar{c}_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}-C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}(\delta_{0}^{2}+A_{0}\delta_{0})\geq\frac{1}{2}\bar{c}_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

(5.5)

If
t
is large enough, it gives rise to

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{-1}n(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Lambda^{-1}n^{\ell}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{-1}n^{h}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+t-r)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|F(r)\|_{L^{1}}\mathrm{d}r+C\|n^{h}(t)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}+C\|n\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{4}}.

(5.6)

Thanks to Lemmas
2.1
and
2.3
, it is easy to verify that

\|U\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{-1}U\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{k}{k+1}}\|\nabla^{k}U\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{k+1}}.

(5.7)

Then, combining (
5.7
) with (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) yields

\|\nabla^{k}n(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\tilde{c}_{4}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},

(5.8)

where
\tilde{c}_{4}
is a positive constant independent of time. By (
3.2
), there exists a constant
\bar{C}_{1}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{k}(\rho-\rho_{*})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\bar{C}_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}.

Similarly, there also exists positive constants
\bar{C}_{2},\bar{C}_{3}
and
\bar{C}_{4}
independent of time such that

\|\nabla^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\bar{C}_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{k}(\theta-\theta_{*})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\bar{C}_{3}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-\frac{k}{2}},\quad\|\nabla^{k}q(t)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\bar{C}_{4}(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{4}-\frac{k}{2}}.

Then choosing
\bar{C}_{*}=\min\{\bar{C}_{1},\bar{C}_{2},\bar{C}_{3},\bar{C}_{4}\}
, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

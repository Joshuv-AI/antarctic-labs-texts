Measuring quantum relative entropy with finite-size effect
Masahito Hayashi

Abstract
We study the estimation of relative entropy D ( ρ ∥ σ ) D(\rho\|\sigma) when σ \sigma is known. We show that the Cramér-Rao type bound equals the relative varentropy. Our estimator attains the Cramér-Rao type bound when the dimension d d is fixed. It also achieves the sample complexity O ⁡ ( d 2 ) O(d^{2}) when the dimension d d increases. This sample complexity is optimal when σ \sigma is the completely mixed state. Also, it has time complexity O ⁡ ( d 6 ​ polylog d ) O(d^{6}\mathop{\rm polylog}\nolimits d) . Our proposed estimator unifiedly works under both settings.

relative entropy; sample complexity; Cramer-Rao type bound; Schur composition

1
Introduction

Distinguishing a given known state
\sigma
from another state
\rho
is a fundamental task in quantum information.
When the state
\rho
is known as well as
\sigma

and
n
copies of the unknown state are prepared,
quantum Stein’s lemma
[
1
,
2
]
guarantees that
the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathrm{Tr}\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)
gives
the limitation of the optimal discrimination power.
The measurement
achieving the optimal performance depends only on the state
\sigma

[
3
]
.
Further, quantum Sanov theorem
[
4
,
5
]
shows that
the construction of the test
achieving the optimal performance depends only on the state
\sigma
and
the value of the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)
.
That is, when we construct the test achieving the optimal performance,
the information of the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)
is needed
in addition to the information of
\sigma
.
Although the papers
[
6
,
7
]
studied the estimation of von Neumann entropy and
Rényi entropy, they did not study the estimation of the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)
with the known state
\sigma
.
Due to the non-commutativity between
\rho
and
\sigma
,
the method in
[
6
,
7
]
cannot be applied to the estimation of
the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)
even when the state
\sigma
is known.
In particular, the simple use of Schur sampling
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]

does not work for the estimation of
the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)

although the paper
[
6
]
employs Schur sampling.
Therefore, this paper studies how to estimate
the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)
with
n
copies of the unknown state
\rho

when
\sigma
is known.

In fact, when we intend to prepare a specific state
\sigma
,
it is crucial to identify the difference between
the known target state
\sigma
and the unknown actual real state

\rho
.
In this situation, we often focus on the fidelity
F(\rho,\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathrm{Tr}|\sqrt{\rho}\sqrt{\sigma}|
for this aim.
Many papers
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
studied this problem
when
\sigma
is a pure state.
However, no paper focuses on this topic
when
\sigma
is a mixed state.
Instead of the fidelity, one can evaluate the difference between

\sigma
and
\rho
by using the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)

because of the bounding inequality
-2\log F(\rho,\sigma)\leq D(\rho\|\sigma)

[
19
, Eq.(3.49)]
.
That is, our problem works for evaluating this difference.

The estimation of
D(\rho\|\sigma)
with the unknown state
\rho

is formulated as a special case of the estimation of a function output with nuisance parameters.
This general problem setting has been studied in
various researches
[
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
.
The reference
[
25
, Section III]
addressed the estimation problem
of
D(\rho\|\sigma)
as a special case, and
showed that the estimation error is lower bounded by
the relative varentropy

V(\rho\|\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathrm{Tr}\rho((\log\rho-\log\sigma)-D(\rho\|\sigma))^{2}
, which appears
in the second-order theory of hypothesis testing
[
26
,
27
]

while it did not show its attainability.
Under a general setting, the reference
[
22
, Section 4.3]
showed that
the optimal precision of the estimation
is given by the Cramér-Rao type bound
C
with nuisance parameters, which equals the inverse of
the projected symmetric logarithmic derivative (SLD) Fisher information
in the one-parameter case.
The meaning of the Cramér-Rao type bound
C
is the following:
when
n
copies of the unknown state
\rho
is prepared
under a fixed model,
the mean square of our best estimator behaves as

\frac{C}{n}+o(\frac{1}{n})
.
When we have no nuisance parameter,
the recent paper
[
28
]
showed the existence of an estimator whose mean square error (MSE) equals

\frac{C}{n}
without additional term under special models.
However, such an estimator does not exist in general.

In this paper, as the first step, using the results for estimation with nuisance parameters in
[
22
, Section 4.3]
,
we show that the Cramér-Rao type bound
C

of this problem equals
the relative varentropy

V(\rho\|\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathrm{Tr}\rho((\log\rho-\log\sigma)-D(\rho\|\sigma))^{2}
, which was obtained as a lower bound in
[
25
]
.
In practice, it is necessary to find an estimator to achieve the
MSE
\frac{C}{n}+o(\frac{1}{n})
.
In addition, it is also desired to clearly evaluate
the difference between its mean square error and the Cramér-Rao type bound
\frac{C}{n}
.
Since our problem is one-parameter estimation,
it is possible to achieve the above bound without the use of
collective measurements across several quantum systems.
However, this method requires an adaptive choice of measurement.
A typical method is the two-step estimator, consisting of a pre-estimation stage and a local optimal estimation stage.
When we have
n
copies of the unknown state
\rho
,
theoretically, in order to achieve the optimal bound,
it is sufficient to allocate
\sqrt{n}
copies to the
pre-estimation stage
[
29
,
21
]
.
Although this method achieves the optimal mean square error
in the order
\frac{1}{n}
, i.e., the first order,
it is not so clear how the mean square error is close to the leading term

\frac{C}{n}
.
From the practical viewpoint, it is necessary to upper bound this difference by using the number
n
of unknown copies.
Unfortunately, the above method has difficulty to evaluate
the difference between its mean square error and

\frac{C}{n}
.
The papers
[
30
,
31
]
also proposed different adaptive methods for parameter estimation, but their methods have the same problem.
In addition, the above adaptive approach does not work when the dimension
d
of the quantum system
increases in addition to the number
n
of copies.

In summary,
to account for finite-size effects in our estimation,
we need to find an estimator of the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)

to satisfy the following conditions.
It asymptotically attains the Cramér-Rao type bound
V(\rho\|\sigma)
in the sense of the order
\frac{1}{n}
when the dimension
d
is fixed.
The difference between its mean square error and the Cramér-Rao type bound is evaluated in terms of
the number
n
of copies.
Furthermore, the estimator performs well
even when the dimension
d
of the quantum system
increases in addition to the number
n
of the prepared copies.

As the second step of this paper, to satisfy the above requirements,
we propose an alternative estimation method by using Schur transform
[
32
]
.
Then, we evaluate its mean square error and
compare it to the Cramér-Rao type bound with a finite number
n
.
In addition, we evaluate the tail probability.

As the third step, similar to the papers
[
6
,
7
]
,
we study the case when the dimension
d
increases when
n
increases.
Unlike the estimation of von Neumann entropy,
the range of the quantum relative entropy or
the relative varentropy is not bounded even with fixed dimension
d
.
Therefore, to study the sample complexity,
we need to restrict the possible range of
\rho
and
\sigma
.
We show that the sample complexity of our method is
O(d^{2})

under a certain natural restriction for this range.
Our method extensively improves the sample complexity over the simple application of the full tomography
[
33
]
.
In particular, the restriction of the possible range of
\sigma

is crucial in this analysis.
We also show that our time complexity is
O(d^{6}\mathop{\rm polylog}\nolimits d)
.

When
\sigma
is fixed to the completely mixed state,
our problem is the same as the estimation of
von Neumann entropy, which was studied in the papers
[
6
,
7
]
.
Our result contains the case studied in the papers
[
6
,
7
]
.
In this special case, the paper
[
6
]
also showed that the lower bound of the sample complexity is
O(d^{2})

for the Empirical Young Diagram (EYD) algorithm, which is given as
the entropy of the diagonal state determined by the Young indices.
Although this case improves the sample complexity over the simple application of the full tomography, the improvement is incremental.
The paper
[
7
]
also showed a lower bound
O(d)

for the sample complexity,
without restricting our algorithm to EYD algorithm.
In this special case, we show that our measurement can be restricted to
Schur sampling.

The remaining part of this paper is organized as follows.
First, Section
2
derives the Cramér-Rao type bound
for the estimation of quantum relative entropy.
Section
3
presents our estimator for quantum relative entropy,
and its performance.
Then, this section shows that this estimator asymptotically attains
the Cramér-Rao type bound when
d
is fixed.
Section
4
studies the sample complexity of our method
when
d
increases.
Section
5
studies the time complexity of our method
when
d
increases.
Appendices are devoted to the proofs of the results presented in Section
3
.

2
Cramér-Rao type bound

To clarify the minimum mean square error
with the first order
\frac{1}{n}
,
we discuss the Cramér-Rao type bound
C

with nuisance parameters, which
is mathematically formulated as a lower bound of MSE under the local unbiasedness condition.
The reference
[
25
, (3.24), (3.52)]
showed that
the varentropy
V(\rho_{*}\|\sigma)
is a lower bound
under the estimation of
D(\rho\|\sigma)
at
\rho=\rho_{*}
.
That is, they showed that

\displaystyle C\geq V(\rho_{*}\|\sigma),

(1)

and did not discuss whether the equality holds.

In the following, we show the equality in (
1
).
For this aim, we review the Cramér-Rao type bound
C
for a general function

f(\rho)
in the full parameter model under a
d
-dimensional system as follows.
We choose a parameterization

\rho_{\theta}
with
\theta=(\theta^{1},\theta^{2},\ldots,\theta^{d^{2}-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d^{2}-1}
such that
the first parameter gives the value of the function to be estimated
and the other parameters are orthogonal to the first parameter at

\rho=\rho_{*}
.
That is, our parameter needs to satisfy the following conditions.

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta_{0}}

\displaystyle=\rho_{*},

(2)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f(\rho_{\theta})}{\partial\theta^{1}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}

\displaystyle=1,

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f(\rho_{\theta})}{\partial\theta^{j}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}

\displaystyle=0

(4)

for
j=2,\ldots,d^{2}-1
, where

\theta_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(\theta^{1}_{0},0,\ldots,0)
,
and

\theta^{1}_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=f(\rho_{*})
.

For the latter discussion,
we introduce the symmetric logarithmic derivative (SLD) inner product.
Given two Hermitian matrices
X,Y
, we define

X\circ Y\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(XY+YX)/2
.
Then, we define the SLD inner product

\langle X,Y\rangle_{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\circ X)Y=\mathrm{Tr}\rho(X\circ Y)
.
We define the SLD norm
\|X\|_{\rho}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sqrt{\langle X,X\rangle_{\rho}}
.
We choose the SLD
L_{1},\ldots,L_{d^{2}-1}
as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta^{j}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}=\rho_{*}\circ L_{j}

(5)

for
j=1,\ldots,d^{2}-1
.
We also assume the following orthogonal condition;

\displaystyle\langle L_{1},L_{j}\rangle_{\rho_{*}}=\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta^{j}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}L_{1}=0

(6)

for
j=2,\ldots,d^{2}-1
.
Then, we can calculate the Cramér-Rao type bound, i.e.,
the minimum mean square error under the local unbiasedness condition
even under the full parameter model.

Proposition 1

(
[
22
, Theorem 5.3]
)
.

Assume the conditions (
2
)–(
6
).
The Cramér-Rao type bound for the estimation of
f(\rho_{\theta})

is
\langle L_{1},L_{1}\rangle_{\rho_{*}}^{-1}
at
\theta=\theta_{0}
.

To calculate the Cramér-Rao type bound for
D(\rho\|\sigma)
at
\rho=\rho_{*}
,
we choose the Hermitian matrix

\displaystyle L^{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=((\log\rho_{*}-\log\sigma)-D(\rho_{*}\|\sigma)I),

(7)

and
d^{2}-1
linearly independent Hermitian traceless matrices

X_{1},X_{2},\ldots X_{d^{2}-1}
such that

\displaystyle X_{1}

\displaystyle=V(\rho_{*}\|\sigma)^{-1}\rho_{*}\circ L^{1}

(8)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}X_{j}L^{1}

\displaystyle=0

(9)

for
j=2,\ldots,d^{2}-1
.
Then, we choose our
parameterization

\rho_{\theta}
as

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\rho_{*}+(\theta^{1}-\theta^{1}_{0})X_{1}+\sum_{j=2}^{d^{2}-1}\theta^{j}X_{j}.

(10)

This parameterization satisfies the condition (
2
).
Since

\frac{\partial\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta^{j}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}=X_{j}
,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial D(\rho_{\theta}\|\sigma)}{\partial\theta^{1}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta^{1}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}(\log\rho_{*}-\log\sigma)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{Tr}\rho_{*}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta^{1}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}X_{1}(\log\rho_{*}-\log\sigma)+\mathrm{Tr}X_{1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}X_{1}L^{1}+\mathrm{Tr}X_{1}D(\rho_{*}\|\sigma)I+\mathrm{Tr}X_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{=}}1.

(11)

Here,
(b)
and
(c)
follow from (
7
) and
the pair of (
8
) and the relation
\langle L^{1},L^{1}\rangle_{\rho_{*}}=V(\rho_{*}\|\sigma)
, respectively.
In addition,

(a)
can be shown as follows.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\rho_{\theta_{0}}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta^{1}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\int_{0}^{1}\rho_{\theta_{0}}^{s}\frac{\partial\log\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta^{1}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}\rho_{\theta_{0}}^{1-s}ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta^{1}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}=\mathrm{Tr}X_{1},

(12)

where
(a)
follows from
[
19
, Eq. (6.32)]
.

Using (
9
) and a similar discussion, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial D(\rho_{\theta}\|\sigma)}{\partial\theta^{j}}\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{0}}=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}X_{j}(\log\rho_{*}-\log\sigma)+\mathrm{Tr}X_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(13)

Then, (
12
) and (
13
)
imply the conditions (
3
) and (
4
).

Due to (
8
) and (
9
), we have

L_{1}=V(\rho_{*}\|\sigma)^{-1}L^{1}
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\frac{\partial\rho_{\theta}}{\partial\theta^{j}}L_{1}=\mathrm{Tr}X_{j}L_{1}=0\hbox{ for }j=2,\ldots,d^{2}-1,

(14)

which implies the condition (
6
).
Therefore,
since all the conditions (
2
) – (
6
) hold,
due to Proposition
1
,
the Cramér-Rao type bound for the estimation of

D(\rho\|\sigma)

is
\langle L_{1},L_{1}\rangle_{\rho_{*}}^{-1}=V(\rho_{*}\|\sigma)
at
\theta=\theta_{0}
.
That is,
the minimum mean square error under the local unbiasedness condition
is
V(\rho_{*}\|\sigma)
.
That is,
the Cramér-Rao type bound for the estimation of

D(\rho\|\sigma)

is the relative varentropy
V(\rho_{*}\|\sigma)
.
Thus, the quality in (
1
) holds.

3
Estimator based on Schur transform

Similar to the papers
[
34
,
3
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
5
,
35
,
36
]
,
we employ Schur duality of
{\cal H}^{\otimes n}
.
For this aim, we prepare several notations.
A sequence of monotone-increasing non-negative integers

\bm{\lambda}=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
is called Young index.
Although many references
[
36
,
37
,
38
]
define Young index as
monotone-decreasing non-negative integers,
we define it in the opposite way for notational convenience.
We denote the set of Young indices
\bm{\lambda}
with the condition

\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}=n
by
Y_{d}^{n}
.
Then, as explained in
[
36
, Section 6.2]
, we have

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{\bm{\lambda}\in Y_{d}^{n}}{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}\otimes{\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}.

(15)

Here,

{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}
expresses the irreducible space of
\mathop{\rm SU}(d)

and
{\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}
expresses the irreducible space of
the representation
\pi
of the permutation group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
.
We define

d_{\bm{\lambda}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\dim{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}
,

d_{\bm{\lambda}}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\dim{\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}
.
Then, for states
\sigma
,
\rho
, we have

\displaystyle\rho^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\lambda}\in Y_{d}^{n}}\rho_{\bm{\lambda}}\otimes\rho_{\bm{\lambda},\mathop{\rm mix}\nolimits},

(16)

\displaystyle\sigma^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{\lambda}\in Y_{d}^{n}}\sigma_{\bm{\lambda}}\otimes\rho_{\bm{\lambda},\mathop{\rm mix}\nolimits},

(17)

where
\rho_{\bm{\lambda},\mathop{\rm mix}\nolimits}
is the completely mixed state on
{\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}
.
In the above equations, we consider that
matrices on the subspace
{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}\otimes{\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}

are matrices on the whole space
{\cal H}^{\otimes n}
.
In the first step, we apply the Schur transform
U_{Schur}
, which realizes
a basis change to block-diagonalize
U^{\otimes n}
for an arbitrary unitary
U
.
It is known that the Schur transform
U_{Schur}
can be
efficiently done
[
32
]
.
We choose a basis

\{|u_{\bm{\lambda},j}\rangle\}_{j=1}^{d_{\bm{\lambda}}}

of
{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}

such that
|u_{\bm{\lambda},j}\rangle
is an eigenvector of
\sigma_{\bm{\lambda}}
.

To estimate the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)
, we employ the POVM
M=\{M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\}_{\bm{\lambda},j}
defined as

\displaystyle M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=|u_{\bm{\lambda},j}\rangle\langle u_{\bm{\lambda},j}|\otimes I({\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}),

(18)

where
I({\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}})
is the identity operator on
{\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}
.
The measurement outcome is given as
(\bm{\lambda},j)
so that
it obeys the probability distribution

\displaystyle P_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n)}(\bm{\lambda},j)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\rho^{\otimes n}.

(19)

To estimate the quantum relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)
,
we employ the estimate
x_{\bm{\lambda},j}
defined as

-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\sigma^{\otimes n}
.
Our estimation protocol is summarized as Protocol
1
.

Protocol 1

Estimation of relative entropy

Step 1.

We apply the Schur transform
U_{Schur}
.

Step 2.

We measure the system by using the measurement
M=\{M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\}_{\bm{\lambda},j}
.

Step 3.

We set our estimate to be
x_{\bm{\lambda},j}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\sigma^{\otimes n}
.

To evaluate the mean square error of this estimator,
we denote the dimension of

\oplus_{\bm{\lambda}\in Y_{d}^{n}}{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}
by
d_{n,d}
.
Since as shown in
[
36
, (6.16) and (6.18)]
, we have

\displaystyle\dim{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}\leq(n+1)^{d(d-1)/2},\quad|Y_{d}^{n}|\leq(n+1)^{d-1},

(20)

the relation

\displaystyle d_{n,d}\leq(n+1)^{(d+2)(d-1)/2}

(21)

holds.

The reason of our choices of
the POVM
M_{\bm{\lambda},j}
and the estimate
x_{\bm{\lambda},j}
is the following.
The reference
[
34
]
essentially showed the relation

\displaystyle D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})-\frac{d(d-1)}{2}\log(n+1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D_{M}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})\leq D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n}),

(22)

where

\displaystyle D_{M}(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{\lambda},j}\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\rho^{\otimes n}(\log\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\rho^{\otimes n}-\log\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\sigma^{\otimes n}).

This relation shows that
the POVM
M_{\bm{\lambda},j}
almost preserves the information with respect to the relative entropy

D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})
.
Further,
since the Shannon entropy

H(P_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n)}(\bm{\lambda},j))\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=-\sum_{\bm{\lambda},j}\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\rho^{\otimes n}\log\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\rho^{\otimes n}
satisfies

\displaystyle 0\leq H(P_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n)}(\bm{\lambda},j))\leq\log d_{n,d},

(23)

the the combination of (
21
) and (
22
)
implies

\displaystyle D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n})-(d+1)(d-1)\log(n+1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\sum_{\bm{\lambda},j}\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\rho^{\otimes n}\log\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\sigma^{\otimes n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle D(\rho^{\otimes n}\|\sigma^{\otimes n}).

(24)

That is, the average of
-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\sigma^{\otimes n}

is close to the value
D(\rho\|\sigma)
to be estimated.
This is the reason why we choose
the estimate
x_{\bm{\lambda},j}
.

The mean square error of this estimator is evaluated as follows.

Theorem 2
.

For any states
\rho
and
\sigma
,
we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm MSE}\nolimits_{n}(\rho\|\sigma)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{(\bm{\lambda},j)}P_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n)}(\bm{\lambda},j)(x_{\bm{\lambda},j}-D(\rho\|\sigma))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{V(\rho\|\sigma)}+\frac{1}{n}\log d_{n,d}\Big)^{2}.

(25)

This theorem is proved in Appendix
A
.
The difference between the upper bound (
25
)
and the leading term
\frac{1}{n}V(\rho\|\sigma)

is
\frac{1}{n^{2}}(\log d_{n,d})^{2}+\frac{2}{n\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{V(\rho\|\sigma)}\log d_{n,d}
.
Using (
21
), this different is upper bounded by

\frac{1}{n^{2}}(\frac{(d+2)(d-1)}{2}\log(n+1))^{2}+\frac{2}{n\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{V(\rho\|\sigma)}\frac{(d+2)(d-1)}{2}\log(n+1)
.

n
times of these terms go to zero.

Next, we focus on the tail probability for our estimator, i.e.,
we study the estimation error probability as

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),+,\epsilon}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=P_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n)}\{(\bm{\lambda},j)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x_{\bm{\lambda},j}-D(\rho\|\sigma)>\epsilon\}

(26)

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),-,\epsilon}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=P_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n)}\{(\bm{\lambda},j)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x_{\bm{\lambda},j}-D(\rho\|\sigma)<-\epsilon\}.

(27)

When
\epsilon
behaves as

c/\sqrt{n}
, as shown in Appendix
B

the probabilities

\delta_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),\pm,c/\sqrt{n}}

converges to a normal distribution as follows.

Theorem 3

When the error term

Theorem 4

\displaystyle(d_{n,d})^{\alpha}e^{-n\alpha(D_{1-\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)-R)},

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),-,R-D(\rho\|\sigma)}\leq

Proof.

Considering the definition of

\displaystyle\sqrt{V(\rho\|\sigma_{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|\log\rho-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\rho)-\log\sigma_{d}+(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\sigma_{d})\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\log\rho-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\rho)\|_{\rho}

\displaystyle+\|\log\sigma_{d}-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\sigma_{d})\|_{\rho}.

(33)

Using the property of the norm

\displaystyle\|\log\rho-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\rho)\|_{\rho}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\rho(\log\rho-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\rho))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Tr}\rho(\log\rho)^{2})-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\rho)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Tr}\rho(\log\rho)^{2})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}(\log d)^{2},

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\|\log\sigma_{d}-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\sigma_{d})\|_{\rho}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}\rho(\log\sigma_{d}-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\sigma_{d}))^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Tr}\rho(\log\sigma_{d})^{2})-(\mathrm{Tr}\rho\log\sigma_{d})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\mathrm{Tr}\rho(\log\sigma_{d})^{2})\leq(td)^{2}.

(35)

Combining (

), and (

\displaystyle\frac{(\sqrt{c_{0}}+4)^{2}}{c\epsilon^{2}}.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

We choose
s\in(0,1)
. Using
Weyl’s dimension formula
[
38
, Theorem 7.1.7]
, we have

\displaystyle\log\dim{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}=\log\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\frac{j-i+\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}{j-i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq d}\!\!\log\Big(1+\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}}{j-i}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{d-l}\log\Big(1+\frac{\lambda_{l+i}-\lambda_{i}}{l}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}(d-l)\sum_{i=1}^{d-l}\frac{1}{d-l}\log\Big(1+\frac{\lambda_{l+i}-\lambda_{i}}{l}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}(d-l)\log\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{d-l}\frac{1}{d-l}\frac{\lambda_{l+i}-\lambda_{i}}{l}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}(d-l)\log\Big(1+\frac{n}{(d-l)l}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{(d-l)}{s}\Big(\frac{n}{(d-l)l}\Big)^{s}=\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{(d-l)^{1-s}n^{s}}{sl^{s}},

(43)

where
(a)
follows from Jensen inequality for
the concave function
x\mapsto\log(1+x)
and
the uniform distribution over
\{1,\ldots,d-l\}
.

(b)
can be shown as follows.

\displaystyle\log\Big(1+\frac{n}{(d-l)l}\Big)=\frac{1}{s}\log\Big(1+\frac{n}{(d-l)l}\Big)^{s}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}\log\Big(1+\Big(\frac{n}{(d-l)l}\Big)^{s}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{s}\Big(\frac{n}{(d-l)l}\Big)^{s},

(44)

where

(c)
follows from the inequality
(x+y)^{s}\leq x^{s}+y^{s}
for
x,y\geq 0
, and

(d)
follows from the inequality
\log(1+x)\leq x
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle\log d_{n,d}\leq\log|Y_{d}^{n}|\max_{\bm{\lambda}}\dim{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log|Y_{d}^{n}|+\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{(d-l)^{1-s}n^{s}}{sl^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(d-1)\log(n+1)+\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{(d-l)^{1-s}n^{s}}{sl^{s}}.

(45)

When
n=cd^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d^{2}}\log\dim{\cal U}_{\bm{\lambda}}\leq\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{(d-l)^{1-s}c^{s}d^{2s}}{sl^{s}}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-x)^{1-s}c^{s}}{sx^{s}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{c^{s}}{sx^{s}}dx=\Big[\frac{c^{s}}{s(1-s)}x^{1-s}\Big]_{0}^{1}=\frac{c^{s}}{s(1-s)}.

(46)

Since the term

\frac{1}{n}(d-1)\log(n+1)
goes to zero,
the term

is evaluated as

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log d_{n,d}\leq\min_{0<s<1}\frac{c^{s-1}}{s(1-s)}.

(47)

The definition of
c_{0}
implies

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\max_{\rho\in{\cal S}_{d}}\sqrt{V(\rho\|\sigma_{d})}=\frac{\sqrt{c_{0}}}{\sqrt{c}}.

(48)

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}\max_{\rho\in{\cal S}_{d}}\mathop{\rm MSE}\nolimits_{n}(\rho\|\sigma_{d})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(e)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{\sqrt{c_{0}}}{\sqrt{c}}+\min_{0<s<1}\frac{c^{s-1}}{s(1-s)}\Big)^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(f)}}{{\leq}}\frac{(\sqrt{c_{0}}+4)^{2}}{c},

(49)

where
(e)
follows from the combination of
(
47
), (
48
), and Theorem
2
,

(f)
follows by choosing

Finally, applying Chebyshev’s inequality,
we obtain the desired inequalities.

5
Analysis on time complexity

To implement our estimator,
we need to apply suitable unitary gates before the measurement based on the computation basis.
For simplicity, we consider the case when

\sigma
is a diagonal matrix.
For a general density
\sigma
, we need to apply a unitary
diagonalizing the density
\sigma
to each system
{\cal H}

before the following procedure.

The references
[
32
,
41
,
42
,
43
]
proposed quantum algorithms for the Schur transform
U_{Schur}
.
We evaluate its precision as follows.
Given a required error
\epsilon>0
and a target unitary
V
,
we say that a unitary
U
is an
\epsilon
-approximate of
V

when any pure state
|\psi\rangle
satisfies

\displaystyle\|(U-V)|\psi\rangle\|\leq\epsilon.

(50)

When the constructed unitary is
an
\epsilon
-approximate of the Schur transform
U_{Schur}
,
the time complexity of the construction
[
42
]
is

O(nd^{4}\mathop{\rm polylog}\nolimits(n,d,1/\epsilon))
.
Since these constructions realize the approximation (
50
),
the estimation error probability increases at most only
\epsilon^{2}
.
We denote the distribution of
(\lambda,j)

when we employ an
\epsilon
-approximate of the Schur transform
U_{Schur}

by

P_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),\epsilon}
.

In the classical part of our estimator, we need to calculate

x_{\bm{\lambda},j}=-\frac{1}{n}\log\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\sigma^{\otimes n}
.
When
\sigma
has diagonal elements
s_{1},\ldots,s_{d}
,
the eigenvalue of
\sigma^{\otimes n}
on the projection

M_{\bm{\lambda},j}
has the form

\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}^{n_{j}}
, where
\sum_{j=1}^{d}n_{j}=n
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}M_{\bm{\lambda},j}\sigma^{\otimes n}=\dim{\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}s_{j}^{n_{j}}.

(51)

For a Young index
\bm{\lambda}
, the dimension
{\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}

is evaluated as

\displaystyle\dim{\cal V}_{\bm{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{\bm{\lambda}!}e(\bm{\lambda})

(52)

\displaystyle e(\bm{\lambda})

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\prod_{j>i}\frac{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}-i+j}{\lambda_{j}+j-i}<1,

(53)

where
\bm{\lambda}!\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\lambda_{1}!\lambda_{2}!\cdots\lambda_{d}!
.
For its detail, see
[
37
, Eq. (2.72)]
.
Using
[
19
, Theorem 2.5]
, we have

\displaystyle e^{nH(\frac{\bm{\lambda}}{n})}(n+1)^{-(d-1)}\leq\frac{n!}{\bm{\lambda}!}\leq e^{nH(\frac{\bm{\lambda}}{n})}.

(54)

The value
e(\bm{\lambda})
is evaluated as

\displaystyle-\log e(\bm{\lambda})=\sum_{j>i}\log\frac{\lambda_{j}+j-i}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}-i+j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j>i}\log(1+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{i}+j-i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j>i}\log(1+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{j-i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}(d-l)\sum_{i=1}^{d-l}\frac{1}{d-l}\log\Big(1+\frac{\lambda_{i}}{l}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}(d-l)\log\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{d-l}\frac{1}{d-l}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{l}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}(d-l)\log\Big(1+\frac{n}{(d-l)l}\Big)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{(d-l)^{1-s}n^{s}}{sl^{s}},

(55)

where
(a)
follows from Jensen inequality for
the concave function
x\mapsto\log(1+x)
, and

(b)
follows from (
44
) in the same way as (
43
).

Instead of
x_{\bm{\lambda},j}
, we use its approximation

x_{\bm{\lambda},j}^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=H(\frac{\bm{\lambda}}{n})-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{n_{j}}{n}\log s_{j}
,
whose time complexity is
O(n)
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle x_{\bm{\lambda},j}^{*}-x_{\bm{\lambda},j}\leq\frac{1}{n}(d\log(n+1)-\log e(\bm{\lambda}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\Big(d\log(n+1)-\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{(d-l)^{1-s}n^{s}}{sl^{s}}\Big).

(56)

When
n=cd^{2}

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}(d\log(n+1)-\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{(d-l)^{1-s}n^{s}}{sl^{s}})\leq\frac{4}{\sqrt{c}}
.

Given an arbitrary number
\epsilon>0
, we choose

the limit of the upper bound in (

\epsilon
.
Then, we define

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),+,\epsilon,\epsilon_{1}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=P_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),\epsilon_{1}}\{(\bm{\lambda},j)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x_{\bm{\lambda},j}^{*}-D(\rho\|\sigma)>\epsilon\}

(57)

\displaystyle\delta_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),-,\epsilon,\epsilon_{1}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=P_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),\epsilon_{1}}\{(\bm{\lambda},j)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}x_{\bm{\lambda},j}^{*}\!-\!D(\rho\|\sigma)\!<\!-\epsilon\}.

(58)

Thus, when

, using (

we have

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}\max_{\rho\in{\cal S}_{d}}\delta_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),+,\epsilon,\epsilon_{1}}+\delta_{\rho,\sigma}^{(n),-,\epsilon,\epsilon_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(\sqrt{c_{0}}+4)^{2}}{c(\epsilon-\frac{4}{\sqrt{c}})^{2}}+\epsilon_{1}^{2}.

Hence, when we choose

the normalized estimator is the normal distribution
in the parameter estimation with nuisance parameters.

6
Discussion

We have studied the estimation of quantum relative entropy

D(\rho\|\sigma)

when the second argument
\sigma
is known.
Our estimator has a good performance in the following two senses.
First, when the dimension
d
is fixed, our estimator attains Cramér-Rao type bound asymptotically.
Second, when the dimension
d
increases,
our estimator has sample complexity
O(d^{2})
under natural assumptions,
which greatly improves the sample complexity by the simple application of full tomography
[
33
]
.
Third, when
d
is large,
although the calculation of our estimator is difficult,
we have an alternative estimator whose
time complexity is
O(d^{6}\mathop{\rm polylog}\nolimits(d))
.
The difference between this alternative estimator and our original estimator approaches zero
asymptotically.
In the above way, our estimator works well unifiedly,
which is a unique feature and did not appear in existing studies
[
6
,
7
,
22
]
.

Since our estimator assumes the knowledge of the form of the second argument
\sigma
, it is needed to develop an estimator that works without this knowledge.
In this case, we need to prepare
n
copies of the unknown state
\sigma
as well.
To attain the Cramér-Rao type bound with fixed
d
,
it is sufficient to estimate the form of the density matrix
\sigma
.
However, such a method has a large time complexity when
d
increases.
It is an interesting open problem to derive an efficient estimator of the quantum relative entropy in the above setting.

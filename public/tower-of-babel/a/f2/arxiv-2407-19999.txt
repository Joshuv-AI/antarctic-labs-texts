Auslander-Reiten annihilators
Özgür Esentepe

Abstract
. The Auslander-Reiten Conjecture for commutative Noetherian rings predicts that a finitely generated module is projective when certain Ext-modules vanish. But what if those Ext-modules do not vanish? We study the annihilators of these Ext-modules and formulate a generalisation of the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture. We prove this general version for high syzygies of modules over several classes of rings including analytically unramified Arf rings, 2-dimensional local normal domains with rational singularities, Gorenstein isolated singularities of Krull dimension at least 2 and more. We also prove results for the special case of the canonical module of a Cohen-Macaulay local ring. These results both generalise and also provide evidence for a version of the Tachikawa Conjecture that was considered by Dao-Kobayashi-Takahashi.

1.
Introduction

Representation theory of Artin algebras is a source of several long standing homological conjectures and it often helps fueling interesting questions in neighbouring areas. The
Auslander-Reiten Conjecture
is one of the most celebrated examples of this. Motivated by a conjecture of Nakayama
[
Nak58
]
regarding the structure of the minimal injective resolution of a finite dimensional algebra, in
[
AR75
]
Auslander and Reiten conjectured that over an Artin algebra, every indecomposable injective module occurs as a direct summand in a minimal injective resolution of the regular module. They proved in the same paper that this
Generalized Nakayama Conjecture
is equivalent to the following: if
\Lambda
is an Artin algebra and
M
is a generator such that
\mathrm{Ext}_{\Lambda}^{i}(M,M)=0
for all
i\geq 1
, then
M
is projective. This conjecture has made its way to the world of commutative algebra after a couple of decades and the following is now commonly referred to as the
Auslander-Reiten Conjecture
by commutative algebraists.

The Auslander-Reiten Conjecture.
Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring. If
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,M\oplus R)=
for all
i\geq 1
, then
M
is projective (equivalently, free).

It was Auslander, Ding and Solberg who first studied this problem in this context and proved that the conjecture holds for complete intersection rings
[
ADS93
]
. In
[
AY98
]
, it was proved that the conjecture holds for all modules with finite complete intersection dimension (over any commutative Noetherian local ring). Huneke and Leuschke proved the conjecture for locally excellent Cohen-Macaulay normal rings containing the rational numbers in
[
HL04
]
. And over the last two decades, there have been major interest and improvements (including but not limited to
[
Ara09
,
NY09
,
CT13
,
GT17
,
NSW17
,
CIST18
,
ST19
,
NT20
,
KOT22
,
Lin22
,
AINSW22
,
DG23
,
GT24
,
Kim24a
]
) while the conjecture remains open even for Gorenstein rings.

During these two decades, several generalizations of the Ext-vanishing condition of the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture were also introduced such as
symmetric Auslander-Reiten condition, generalized Auslander-Reiten condition
or
symmetric Auslander-Reiten condition for modules with constant rank
(see
[
HŞV04
,
Wei10
,
CH12
,
CT13
,
Div14
,
DKS23
]
and references within). Our goal in this paper is to introduce another generalization of the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture. We will do this by looking at two ideals associated to a finitely generated module over a commutative Noetherian local ring and comparing them.

Our ideals are annihilator ideals. More precisely, they are annihilators of (certain) Ext-modules. Annihilators of Ext modules have been of interest to the commutative algebra community for a very long time and recently there has been several papers connecting them to generation of module categories, derived categories or singularity categories and they have been proven to be useful to understand the singular loci of several classes commutative rings. We refer to
[
Wan94
,
IT14
,
IT16
,
IT21
,
DKT21
,
Dey22
,
DT22
,
Liu23
]
among many others. Our goal is to connect the theory of these annihilators to the study of the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture.

1.1
.

Stable annihilator ideals.

For any two
R
-modules
X
and
Y
, consider the submodule
P(X,Y)
of
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(X,Y)
consisting of morphisms that factor through a projective module. The quotient is often denoted by
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{R}(X,Y)
. The category
\underline{\mathrm{mod}}(R)
whose objects are finitely generated
R
-modules and whose morphism sets are these
stable
morphism sets is called the
stable module category
of
R
. We define the
stable annihilator
of
M
as the annihilator of the stable endomorphism ring (i.e. the endomorphism ring in the stable module category) of
M
and we denote it by
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)
. It is immediate from this definition that

(1.1.1)

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=R\iff M\text{ is projective}.

We also have the following equalities:

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,\Omega_{R}M)

\displaystyle=\bigcap_{i>0}\bigcap_{N\in\mathrm{mod}R}\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)

where
\Omega_{R}(-)
is the syzygy operator
[
DKT21
, Lemma 2.2]
. The vanishing locus of this ideal is precisely the non-free locus

\displaystyle\mathsf{NF}(M)\colon\{p\in\mathsf{Spec}R\colon M_{p}\text{ is not free as an }R_{p}\text{-module}\}.

of
M
.

1.2
.

Auslander-Reiten annihilators.
The next ideal of our interest is the
Auslander-Reiten annihilator
of
M
which we introduce in this paper. We consider the ideal

\displaystyle\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M):=\bigcap_{i>0}\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,M\oplus R)

and with this notation, the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture can be restated as

(1.2.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)=R\iff M\text{ is projective}.

Again, as the annihilators and Ext-modules behave nicely with localisation, the Auslander-Reiten conjecture implies that
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)
should also define the non-free locus of
M
.

1.3
.

Our motivating question.

Comparing
1.1.1
and
1.2.1
, we see that whenever
R
satisfies the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture, we have
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=R
if and only if
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)=R
. Moreover, by the ongoing discussion, we know that the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture holds for a commutative Noetherian local ring
R
if and only if for any finitely generated
R
-module
M
, there is an equality
\sqrt{\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)}=\sqrt{\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)}
.

Motivated by this, we are interested in the following question.

Question 1.4
.

For an arbitrary finitely generated module
M
over
R
, when do we have an equality

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)?

Note that by the above discussion, we know that
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)
is contained in
\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N)
for any
i>0
and any
R
-module
N
. Thus, we always have an inclusion
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)
. The equality would follow, for instance, by showing that for some
i>0
, either
\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,M)
or
\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)
is contained in
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)
.

Let us illustrate two main examples.

Example 1.5
.

Hypersurface rings.

Let
R
be a hypersurface singularity and
M
be a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module. Then, a minimal complete resolution of
M
is 2-periodic
[
Eis80
]
and we have isomorphisms

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{End}}_{R}(M)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Ext}}_{R}^{2}(M,M)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{2}(M,M)

where
\underline{\mathrm{Ext}}_{R}^{i}(-,*)
denotes the
stable Ext
or
Tate cohomology
groups
[
Buc21
]
. Thus, we have an equality

\displaystyle\ \underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{2}(M,M)

and consequently, we have
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)
.

Example 1.6
.

Nearly Gorenstein rings.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with a canonical module
\omega
. Assume that
R
is
nearly Gorenstein
but not Gorenstein which means that we have an equality
\tr(\omega)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)=\mathfrak{m}

[
HHS19
]
. It is clear from the defining property that a nearly Gorenstein ring is Gorenstein on the punctured spectrum and therefore generically Gorenstein. Hence, in this case
R
satisfies a particular case of the Auslander-Conjecture, namely the
Tachikawa Conjecture
which states (in our notation) that
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)=R
if and only if
R
is Gorenstein
[
ABŞ05
]
. We assumed that
R
is not Gorenstein and so
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)\neq R
and we know that
\mathfrak{m}=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)\subseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)
. Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle\tr(\omega)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)=\mathfrak{m}.

1.7
.

The category
\mathcal{E}
.
In the next section, we study some basic properties of the full subcategory

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(R)=\{X\in\mathrm{mod}R\colon\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(X)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(X)\}

of the module category. We note that if
\mathcal{E}(R)=\mathrm{mod}(R)
, then
R
satisfies the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture.

By using standard techniques homological algebra, one can verify the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture for the class of modules with finite projective dimension. Indeed, we have
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{m}(M,R)\neq 0
whenever the module
M
has finite projective dimension
m\geq 1
. On the other hand, with the help of the functor
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,-)
, the natural short exact sequence
0\to\Omega M\to F\to M\to 0
(where
F
is a free module and
\Omega M
is a syzygy of
M
) gives us a long exact sequence which yields isomorphisms
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,M)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\Omega M,\Omega M)
for all
i>0
provided that
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,R)=0
for all
i>0
. Hence, if the condition
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,M\oplus R)=0
is satisfied for all
i>0
, then the condition
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\Omega M,\Omega M\oplus R)=0
is also satisfied for all
i>0
. Therefore, by induction, it is enough to consider the conjecture for (higher) syzygies.

Consequently, we have that the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture holds for the ring
R
if we have an inclusion
\Omega_{R}^{n}(\mathrm{mod}R)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(R)
for some
n\geq 0
. Thus, we believe that studying this category (and other related categories) is a useful thing to do. In the next section, we study properties of this category and prove such as being closed under direct sums, direct summands and syzygies. The following can be considered as the main theorem of Section 2 which is a combination of Lemma
2.11
and Corollary
2.17
.

Theorem A
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring.

(1)

The category is
\mathcal{E}(R)
is closed under finite direct sums.

(2)

We have
\operatorname{\mathsf{smd}}(\mathcal{E}(R))=\mathrm{mod}R
where
\operatorname{\mathsf{smd}}(\mathcal{E}(R))
is the smallest full subcategory that contains
\mathcal{E}(R)
and is closed under direct summands.

In Section 2, we also give examples of rings
R
where, for some
n\geq 0
,
\Omega^{n}(\mathrm{mod}R)
is contained in
\mathcal{E}(R)
in Krull dimension 1 (Arf rings), 2 (rational singularities) and 3 (the (2-1)-scroll).

1.8
.

Cohen-Macaulay rings.
In Section 3, we focus our attention to Cohen-Macaulay rings. We start with looking at one-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local rings and see examples where we observe the inclusions

\displaystyle\Omega(\mathrm{mod}R)\subseteq\mathrm{MCM}(R)\subseteq\mathcal{E}(R).

See, for instance, Proposition
3.3
for the case where

is far-flung Gorenstein and nearly Gorenstein. After this, we utilise Auslander-Reiten theory for maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules which are free on the punctured spectrum. The following is the main theorem of Section 3.

Theorem B
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein local ring of Krull dimension at least 2 and assume that
R
is an isolated singularity. Then, for any maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
M
, we have
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)
.

We also use similar methods to provide a more general version of a theorem of Dao-Kobayashi-Takahashi, namely
[

, Theorem 4.4]
. See Proposition
3.8
, Corollary
3.22
and Theorem
3.24
.

Theorem C
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
. Assume that one of the following conditions is satisfied.

(1)

R
is generically Gorenstein and the canonical trace ideal
\tr(\omega)
is radical.

(2)

R
is Gorenstein on the punctured spectrum and Henselian and is of Krull dimension at least 2 such that the endomorphism ring
\mathrm{End}_{R}(R\oplus\omega)
is a Gorenstein
R

(3)

Then, we have an equality

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we are going to recall some background, set up conventions and notation and prove some preliminary results. Throughout this paper, we will always consider commutative Noetherian local rings and finitely generated modules over them. Hence, a module will always refer to a finitely generated module. Also, we will use free and projective interchangeably as we will be dealing with local rings.

2.6

Auslander-Reiten annihilators.
Finally, let us introduce the Auslander-Reiten annihilators. We refer to the discussion in the Introduction for our motivation to consider this ideal.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
M,N
be
R
-modules.

(1)

We put

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{e}(M,N):=\bigcap_{i>0}\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,N).

(2)

We put
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M):=\boldsymbol{e}(M,M\oplus R)
and call it the
Auslander-Reiten annihilator
of

.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
M
an
R
-module.

(1)

We have an inclusion
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)\subseteq\boldsymbol{e}(M,N)
for any
R
-module
N
. In particular, we have
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)
.

(2)

We have an inclusion
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M\oplus N)\subseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)\cap\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(N)
.

(3)

If
M
is a totally-reflexive module, then there are equalities

\displaystyle\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)=\boldsymbol{e}(M,M)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M^{*})=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\Omega M).

(4)

If there exists a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\Omega M\to X\to M\to 0

such that
\boldsymbol{e}(M,X)=R
(for example if
M
is totally reflexive and

), then we have
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\Omega M)
.

The first assertion follows from the first assertion of Lemma

. For the second one, we observe

\displaystyle\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M\oplus N)=\boldsymbol{e}(M,N)\cap\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)\cap\boldsymbol{e}(N,M)\cap\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(N).

The third assertion follows from the definition of a totally reflexive module, Lemma
2.5
(3) and the fact that
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,M)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\Omega_{R}M,\Omega_{R}M)

For the final assertion, we start by noting that the given short exact sequence induces a long exact sequence

\displaystyle\cdots\to\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,X)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(M,M)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i+1}(M,\Omega M)\to\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i+1}(M,X)\to\cdots

The category

We shall now consider the full subcategory

We shall record some basic properties of the category
\mathcal{E}
.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring.

(1)

Then,
\mathcal{E}
is closed under finite direct sums.

(2)

Let
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}
be finitely generated
R
-modules and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
be positive integers. Then, we have

\displaystyle M_{1}^{\oplus a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{n}^{\oplus a_{n}}\in\mathcal{E}\iff M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{n}\in\mathcal{E}.

(3)

Assume that there exists a short exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to\Omega M\to X\to M\to 0

such that
\boldsymbol{e}(M,X)=R
, then
M\in\mathcal{E}
implies
\Omega M\in\mathcal{E}
. In particular,
\mathcal{E}
is closed under syzygies when restricted to totally-reflexive modules.

Proof.

Assuming that
M,N\in\mathcal{E}
, we have
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)\subseteq\boldsymbol{e}(M,N)
and also
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(N)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(N)\subseteq\boldsymbol{e}(N,M)
. Thus, we can conclude by
2.8.1
, that we have an equality
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M\oplus N)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M\oplus N)
.

For the second assertion, we note that there are equalities

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M_{1}^{\oplus a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{n}^{\oplus a_{n}})

\displaystyle=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M_{1}^{\oplus a_{1}})\cap\cdots\cap\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M_{n}^{\oplus a_{n}})

\displaystyle=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M_{1})\cap\cdots\cap\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M_{n})

\displaystyle=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{n})

and also by additivity of Ext we have the equality

\displaystyle\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M_{1}^{\oplus a_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{n}^{\oplus a_{n}})=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{n})

which gives us the desired result.

The third assertion follows from Proposition
2.8
(4) by noting that the existence of such a short exact sequence for a module
M
implies

\displaystyle\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\Omega M)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\Omega M)

when
M\in\mathcal{E}
.
∎

While we showed that

is closed under direct sums, the next lemma is slightly more stronger.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
M,N
be two

-modules such that
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(N)

, then so is

, then so is

.

Proof.

We have
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)\cap\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(N)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M\oplus N)

Remark 2.14
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with an isolated singularity. Then, there exists a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
M_{0}
such that for any other maximal Cohen-Macaulay module

, we have
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M_{0})\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)

[

, Theorem 1.2]
. Therefore, our lemma tells us that

implies

for any

.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
and assume that
\omega\in\mathcal{E}
.

(1)

For any maximal Cohen-Macaulay module
M
, we have
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M\oplus\omega)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)\cap\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)
.

(2)

If
M\oplus\omega\in\mathcal{E}
for some maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
M
, then we have an equality
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)\cap\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)\cap\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)
. If, moreover, there is an inclusion
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)
, then we have
M\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

For the first assertion, assume
M
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module. The canonical module is an injective object in the category of maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules. Therefore, we have
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)\subseteq R=\boldsymbol{e}(M,\omega)
. By assumption, we have
\omega\in\mathcal{E}
and thus, we have
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)\subseteq\boldsymbol{e}(\omega,M)
. Hence, by
2.8.1
, we have the equality
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M\oplus\omega)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M\oplus\omega)
.

For the second assertion, we use the inclusions in
2.9.1
. The first inclusion is an equality by our assumption and the second inclusion is an equality by the first assertion. The result follows.
∎

We continue this section with a discussion on the direct sum of a module and its syzygy. As stated in Lemma
2.5
(1), the stable annihilator of
M
equals the annihilator of
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,\Omega M)
. This module is a direct summand of
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M\oplus\Omega M,M\oplus\Omega M)
and the next proposition relies on this observation.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
M
be any finitely generated
R
-module. Then, we have
M\oplus\Omega M\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

We know that we always have
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M\oplus\Omega M)\subseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M\oplus\Omega M)
. So, we should show that
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M\oplus\Omega)\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M\oplus\Omega M)
. This follows from

\displaystyle\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M\oplus\Omega M)\subseteq\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M\oplus\Omega M,M\oplus\Omega M)\subseteq\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,\Omega M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)

and the fact that
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M\oplus\Omega M)
.
∎

Therefore, when we take the smallest subcategory
\operatorname{\mathsf{smd}}(\mathcal{E})
that contains
\mathcal{E}
and is closed under direct summands, we get the entire module category.

Corollary 2.17
.

For any commutative Noetherian local ring
R
, we have
\operatorname{\mathsf{smd}}(\mathcal{E})=\mathrm{mod}R
.

We can also strengthen Proposition
2.16
for modules which are high enough syzygies.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
R
ba a commutative Noetherian local ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module. Assume that for some
r\gg 0
and an
R
-module
N
, we have
M=\Omega^{r}N
. Then, for any
n>0
, we have
M\oplus\Omega^{n}M\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

We have an ascending chain
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(N)\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\Omega N)\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\Omega^{2}N)\subseteq\cdots
which stabilizes as
R
is Noetherian. Therefore, if we choose
r
large enough, we see that
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\Omega^{r}N)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\Omega^{r+s}N)
for any
s>0
. This gives us
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\Omega^{n-1}M)
. As earlier, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M\oplus\Omega^{n}M)\subseteq\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{n}(M\oplus\Omega^{n}M,M\oplus\Omega^{n}M)\subseteq\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{n}(M,\Omega^{n}M)

and we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{n}(M,\Omega^{n}M)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\Omega^{n-1}M,\Omega^{n}M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\Omega^{n-1}M)

but also we know
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\Omega^{n-1}M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M\oplus\Omega^{n}M)
which finishes the proof.
∎

Here is an immediate consequence.

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
M
be a finitely generated
R
-module with a periodic projective resolution. Then, we have
M\in\mathcal{E}
.

Remark 2.20
.

In fact, we can do better than this in Corollary
2.19
. Assume that
R
satisfies the Krull-Remak-Schmidt theorem (

R
is a Henselian local ring). For a finitely generated

-module

if and only if

Corollary 2.21
.

Let

be a commutative Noetherian local ring in which Krull-Remak-Schmidt theorem holds and assume that for some

, we have that

, we have

\displaystyle\Omega^{m}(\mathrm{mod}R)\subseteq\mathcal{E}.

Proof.

Assume that there are only finitely many indecomposable modules up to isomorphism in

. Then, in particular, we have finitely many basic modules in
\Omega^{n}(\mathrm{mod}R)
. Hence, for a finitely generated

-module

, some basic module appears in the sequence

infinitely often. Then, by Remark

and Corollary

, we conclude that some high syzygy, say

, of

belongs to
\mathcal{E}
. Then, letting

, we finish the proof (note that since
\Omega^{n}{\mathrm{mod}R}
contains finitely many indecomposable modules up to isomorphism, we know that
m
is finite).
∎

3.
Cohen-Macaulay Rings

In this section, we focus our attention on Cohen-Macaulay rings.

3.1

Dimension 1.
In Example
1.6
, we showed that the canonical module
\omega
belongs to

when

is a nearly Gorenstein ring. We did this by arguing that we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\tr(\omega)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)\subseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)\neq R.

By the same argument, if
R
is any commutative Noetherian local ring,
M
is a finitely generated
R
-module and if we know both
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)

\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)
. Now, we are going to see a class of rings where we can do this for any maximal Cohen-Macaulay module.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
Q(R)
be its total quotient ring. Denote by
\overline{R}
the integral closure of
R
inside
Q(R)
. Then, the set

\displaystyle\mathrm{co}(R)=\{r\in\overline{R}\colon r\overline{R}\subseteq R\}

is called the

of

.

It was proved in
[
Wan94
, Corollary 3.1]
that if
R
is a one-dimensional commutative Noetherian complete reduced local ring (in particular,

is Cohen-Macaulay), then for any two maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules

, we have
\mathrm{co}(R)\subseteq\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,N)
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a one-dimensional Noetherian complete reduced local ring such that
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathrm{co}(R)
. Then, any maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module belongs to
\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module. By Wang’s result mentioned before the statement of the proposition, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathrm{co}(R)\subseteq\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,\Omega M)=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M).

On the other hand, any ring with
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathrm{co}(R)
has minimal multiplicity
[
GMP13
, Theorem 5.1]
and such rings satisfy the Auslander-Reiten Conjecture
[
DG23
]
and therefore for any non-free

, we have
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)\neq R
. This finishes the proof.
∎

.

Even without the assumption that

is complete and reduced, we have an inclusion

by

. Hence, the hypothesis in Proposition

Lemma 3.10

Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(A,X)\cdot\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(C,X)\subseteq\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(B,X).

Let

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(C,X)\xrightarrow{\Phi}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(B,X)\xrightarrow{\Psi}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(A,X)

Definition 3.11

Y
, we have

\displaystyle(\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\mathcal{X},Y))^{r}\subseteq\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(M,Y)

where

\displaystyle\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\mathcal{X},Y)=\bigcap_{X\in\mathcal{X}}\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(X,Y).

Proof.

For
r=1
, this follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\mathrm{ann}_{R}(L_{1}\oplus L_{2})=\mathrm{ann}_{R}L_{1}\cap\mathrm{ann}_{R}L_{2}

for any two modules
L_{1},L_{2}
and the fact that
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(-,Y)
distributes over direct sums. For
r\geq 2
, we use Lemma
3.10
inductively.
∎

We know that
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\omega,\Omega\omega)
. By applying the canonical dual, we get the equality
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(D\Omega\omega,R)
. This implies that if
D\Omega\omega\in[\mathcal{X}]_{r}
, then by Lemma
3.12
we get
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)^{r}\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)
where
\mathcal{X}=\{\Omega^{i}\omega\colon i\geq 0\}
. Since we always have
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)\subseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)
, we can conclude that if
D\Omega\omega\in[\mathcal{X}]_{r}
for some
r
, then we have
\sqrt{\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)}=\sqrt{\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)}
.

Let us now investivage when
D\Omega\omega\in[\mathcal{X}]_{r}
for some
r
, then. The canonical module is an injective cogenerator in the category
\mathrm{MCM}(R)
and we can consider an injective coresolution

\displaystyle 0\to D\Omega\omega\to\omega^{s_{0}}\to\cdots\to\omega^{s_{t}}\to U\to 0

of
D\Omega\omega
in
\mathrm{MCM}(R)
. Then, by
[
DT14
, Lemma 5.8]
, we have

\displaystyle D\Omega\omega\in\left|\Omega^{t+1}U\oplus\bigoplus_{i=0}^{t}\Omega^{i}\omega\right|_{t+2}.

In particular, if
U=\Omega^{j}\omega
for some
j\geq 1
, we have
D\Omega\omega\in|\mathcal{X}|_{t+2}
. This condition is equivalent, by applying
D
, to asking that there is an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to D\Omega^{j}\omega\to R^{s_{t}}\to\ldots\to R^{s_{0}}\to\Omega\omega\to 0

and this is equivalent to asking that
D\Omega^{j}\omega\cong\Omega^{t+2}\omega
. We record this discussion as a corollary.

Corollary 3.13
.

If there is a
j\geq 1
such that
D\Omega^{j}\omega
appears as a syzygy of the canonical module
\omega
, then
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)
and
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)
agree up to radicals.

We give an example where the assumption holds.

Example 3.14
.

Let
S=\mathbb{C}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]]
be a formal power series ring and
R
be the second Veronese subring of
S
. Assume
d
is odd and at least
3
. Then,
R
is a
d
-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring and it is not Gorenstein
[
BH93
, Exercise 3.6.21]
. As an
R
-module, we have
S\cong R\oplus\omega
. The ring
R
has an isolated singularity and for every maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
M
there is an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\to M\to C^{0}\to\cdots\to C^{d-2}\to 0

with
C^{i}\in\mathrm{add}_{R}(S)
for all
i

[
Iya07
, 2.2.3]
. Since
D\Omega^{j}\omega
is a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module, this gives us the desired result.

3.15
.

Higher dimension.

We shall now recall some terminology and results from the Auslander-Reiten theory of Cohen-Macaulay local rings.

Definition 3.16
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian local ring and
M
be an
R
-module.

(1)

We say that
M
is
locally free on the punctured spectrum
if
M_{p}
is a free
R_{p}
-module for all non-maximal prime ideals
p
.

(2)

We say that
R
is an
isolated singularity
if
R_{p}
has finite global dimension for every non-maximal ideal
p
.

(3)

Assume
R
is Cohen-Macaulay with canonical module
\omega
. We say that
R
is
Gorenstein on the punctured spectrum
if
\omega
is locally free on the punctured spectrum.

We include the following lemmas to keep the paper self-contained.

Lemma 3.17

(Lemma 2.10 in
[
DKT21
]
)
.

Let
R
be a d-dimensional Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
. Let
M
be an
R
-module of finite length. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ann}_{R}M=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d}(M,\omega_{R}).

Lemma 3.18

(Auslander-Reiten Duality. See, for instance, Lemma 3.10 in
[
Yos90
]
)
.

Let
R
be a d-dimensional complete Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
and
M,N
be two maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules. If
N
is locally free on the punctured spectrum, then we have an isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d}(\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{R}(M,N),\omega)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(N,D\Omega^{d}\mathrm{Tr}M).

Now, combining these two lemmas, we get the following proposition (
c.f.
[
DKT21
, Proposition 2.11.]
)

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
and assume that it is an isolated singularity. Then,

\displaystyle\mathrm{ann}_{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{R}(M,N)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d+1}(\mathrm{Tr}M,DN)

for any two maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules
M,N
.

Proof.

Since
R
is an isolated singularity, every maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module is free on the punctured spectrum and for any two maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-modules, the module
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{R}(M,N)
is of finite length. Therefore, we can apply Lemma
3.17
to get

\displaystyle\mathrm{ann}_{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{R}(M,N)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d}(\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{R}(M,N),\omega)

and we can apply Lemma
3.18
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{ann}_{R}\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{R}(M,N)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(N,D\Omega^{d}\mathrm{Tr}M).

By using the canonical duality, we know

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(N,D\Omega^{d}\mathrm{Tr}M)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{1}(\Omega^{d}\mathrm{Tr}M,DN)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d+1}(\mathrm{Tr}M,DN).

Combining all, we obtain the desired result.
∎

The following is a consequence of this proposition for the
R
-dual of the canonical module.

Corollary 3.20
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
. Assume that the Krull dimension of
R
is at least 2 and that
R
is Gorenstein on the punctured spectrum. Then, we have
\omega^{*}\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.19
, we have

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d+1}(\mathrm{Tr}\omega,R)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d-1}(\omega^{*},R)\supseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega^{*})\supseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega^{*})\supseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega).

Hence, we conclude that
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega^{*})=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega^{*})=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)
.
∎

For the next corollary, let us recall some more terminology.

Definition 3.21
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
and
\Lambda
be a module-finite
R
-algebra.

(1)

We say that
\Lambda
is an
R
-
order
if
\Lambda
is maximal Cohen-Macaulay as an
R
-module.

(2)

We say that a (right)
\Lambda
-module is
maximal Cohen-Macaulay
if it is maximal Cohen-Macaulay as an
R
-module.

(3)

The
canonical module of

\Lambda
is the (bi-)module
\omega_{\Lambda}=\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\Lambda,\omega)
.

(4)

An order
\Lambda
is called a
Gorenstein order
if
\omega_{\Lambda}
is a projective
\Lambda
-module.

The next corollary and the proposition generalize
[
DKT21
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Corollary 3.22
.

Let
R
be Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module
\omega
of dimension at least
2
and assume that it is and Gorenstein on the punctured spectrum and Henselian. If the endomorphism ring
\mathrm{End}_{R}(R\oplus\omega)
is a Gorenstein order, then we have
\omega\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

By
[
Sta17
, Corollary 4.6]
, with our assumptions, we have
\omega^{*}\cong\omega
whenever the endomorphism ring
\mathrm{End}_{R}(R\oplus\omega)
is a Gorenstein order. Therefore, we have equalities
\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega^{*})=\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)
and
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)=\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega^{*})
. Therefore Corollary
3.20
implies that
\omega\in\mathcal{E}
.
∎

Remark 3.23
.

When
R
is a Cohen-Macaulay local domain, the condition
\omega^{*}\cong\omega
is equivalent to saying that the order of
[\omega]
in the divisor class group is
2
. We refer to
[
Wes91
]
for examples and computations regarding the order of
[\omega]
in the divisor class group.

Next, we give a consequence for Cohen-Macaulay local rings of type 2 which are Gorenstein on the punctured spectrum.

Theorem 3.24
.

Let
R
be a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of type
2
and assume that it is Gorenstein on the punctured spectrum. Then, we have
\omega\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

The key point is that if
R
is generically Gorenstein and of type
2
, then we have isomorphisms
\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i}(\omega,R)\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{i+1}(\mathrm{Tr}\omega,R)
for all
i>0
by
[
DKT21
, Lemma 4.1]
. Then, as before, we have

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d+1}(\mathrm{Tr}\omega,R)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d}(\omega,R)\supseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(\omega)\supseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(\omega)

which gives us the desired equality.
∎

Finally, we have the main theorem of this section.

Theorem 3.25
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein local ring with Krull dimension
d
at least 2. Then, for any maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module
M
which is free on the punctured spectrum, we have
M\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a maximal Cohen-Macaulay
R
-module which is free on the punctured spectrum. Then, we have

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d+1}(\mathrm{Tr}M,M^{*})=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d-1}(M^{*},M^{*})

where the first equality is by Proposition
3.19
. We also have

\displaystyle\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d-1}(M^{*},M^{*})=\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d-1}(M,M)\supseteq\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)

where the equality is due to the fact that
(-)^{*}
is an exact duality on maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules. By definition, we have
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)\subseteq\mathrm{ann}_{R}\mathrm{Ext}_{R}^{d-1}(M,M)
and combining we get
\mathrm{ARann}_{R}(M)\subseteq\underline{\mathrm{ann}}_{R}(M)
which proves the equality.
∎

Corollary 3.26
.

Let
R
be a Gorenstein local ring with Krull dimension
d
at least
2
. Assume that
R
has at most an isolated singularity. Then, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{MCM}(R)=\Omega^{d}(\mathrm{mod}R)\subseteq\mathcal{E}.

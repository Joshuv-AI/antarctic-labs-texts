NEW LAPLACE-TYPE INTEGRAL TRANSFORM FOR SOLVING STEADY HEAT-TRANSFER PROBLEM Journal: Thermal Science
Shehu Maitama*, Weidong Zhao

Abstract
The fundamental purpose of this paper is to propose a new Laplace-type integral transform (NL-TIT) for solving steady heat-transfer problems. The proposed integral transform is a generalization of the Sumudu, and the Laplace transforms and its visualization is more comfortable than the Sumudu transform, the natural transform, and the Elzaki transform. The suggested integral transform is used to solve the steady heat-transfer problems, and results are compared with the results of the existing techniques.

Definition and Theorems

Definition 1.1

The new Laplace-type integral transform of the function
v(t)
of exponential order is defined over the set of functions,

A=\left\{v(t):\exists\,C,\,\xi_{1},\,\xi_{2}>0,\,\,\left|v(t)\right|<Ce^{\frac{\left|t\right|}{\xi_{i}}},\,\textnormal{if}\ t\in(-1)^{i}\times\left[0,\infty\right)\right\}
,

by the following integral:

\Theta\left[v(t)\right](s,u)=V(s,u)=u\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\,v(ut)\,dt=\lim_{\beta\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\beta}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}v(t)dt,\,\,s\,>0,\,\,u>0,

(1)

where
\Theta
is the NL-TIT operator. It converges if the limit
of the integral exists, and diverges if not.

The inverse NL-TI transform is given by:

{\Theta^{-1}}\left[V(s,u)\right]=v(t),\,\,for\,\,t\geq 0.

(2)

Equivalently, the complex inversion formula of the NL-TI transform
is given by:

v(t)={\Theta^{-1}}\left[V(s,u)\right]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\beta-i\infty}^{\beta+i\infty}\frac{1}{u}e^{\frac{st}{u}}V(s,u)\,ds,\,\,t>0,

(3)

where
s
and
u
are the NL-TI transform variables, and
\beta

is a real constant and the integral in eq. (
3
) is
computed along
s=\beta
in the complex plane
s=x+iy
.

Theorem 1.1

The sufficient condition for the existence of the new Laplace-type
integral transform. If the function
v(t)
is piecewise continues
on every finite interval
[0,t_{0}]
and satisfies

|v(t)|\leq Ce^{\beta t},

(4)

for all
t\in[t_{0},\infty)
, and a constant
\beta
, then
{\Theta}\left[v(t)\right](s,u)
exists for all
\frac{s}{u}>\beta
.

Proof.

To prove the theorem, we must first show that the improper
integral converges for
\frac{s}{u}>\beta
. Without loss of
generality, we first split the improper integral into two parts
namely:

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{st}{u}}v(t)dt=\int_{0}^{t_{0}}e^{-\frac{st}{u}}v(t)dt+\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{st}{u}}v(t)dt.

(5)

The first integral on the right hand side of eq. (
5
)
exists by the first hypothesis, hence the existence of the
Laplace-type integral transform completely depends on the second
integral. Then by the second hypothesis we deduce:

\left|e^{-\frac{st}{u}}v(t)\right|\leq Ce^{-\frac{st}{u}}e^{\beta t}=Ce^{-\frac{(s-\beta u)t}{u}}.

(6)

Thus

\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}Ce^{-\frac{(s-\beta u)t}{u}}dt=\frac{u}{s-\beta u}Ce^{-\frac{(s-\beta u)t_{0}}{u}}.

(7)

Hence, eq. (
7
) converges for
\beta<\frac{s}{u}
. This
implies by the comparison test for improper integrals theorem,

{\Theta}\left[v(t)\right](s,u)
exists for

\beta<\frac{s}{u}
.This complete the proof.
\Box

In the next theorem, we prove the uniqueness of the NL-TI
transform.

Theorem 1.2

Uniqueness of the new Laplace-type integral transform.

Let
v(t)
and
w(t)
be continuous functions defined for

t\geq 0
and having NL-TI transforms of
V(s,u)
and
W(s,u)

respectively. If
V(s,u)=W(s,u)
, then
v(t)=w(t)
.

Proof.

From the inverse NL-TI transform eq. (
3
), we have

v(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\beta-i\infty}^{\beta+i\infty}\frac{1}{u}e^{\frac{st}{u}}V(s,u)\,ds.

(8)

Since
V(s,u)=W(s,u)
by the second hypothesis, then replacing this in eq. (
8
), we obtain

v(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\beta-i\infty}^{\beta+i\infty}\frac{1}{u}e^{\frac{st}{u}}W(s,u)\,ds.

(9)

This implies

v(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\beta-i\infty}^{\beta+i\infty}\frac{1}{u}e^{\frac{st}{u}}V(s,u)\,ds=w(t).

(10)

Hence, eq. (
10
) proves the uniqueness of the NL-TI
transform.
\Box

Theorem 1.3

Convolution theorem of the NL-TI transform.
Let the functions
v(t)

and
w(t)
be in set A. If
V(s,u)
and
W(s,u)
are the NL-TI
transforms of the functions
v(t)
and
w(t)
respectively, then

{\Theta}\left[(v*w)(t)\right]=V(s,u)W(s,u).

(11)

Where
v*w
is the convolution of two functions
v(t)
and
w(t)

which is defined as:

\int_{0}^{t}v(\tau)w(t-\tau)d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}v(t-\tau)w(\tau)d\tau.\\

(12)

Proof.

Based on eq. (
1
) and eq. (
12
), we
get:

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[\int_{0}^{t}v(\tau)w(t-\tau)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}v(\tau)w(t-\tau)\right)d\tau.

Changing the limit of integration yields:

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[\int_{0}^{t}v(\tau)w(t-\tau)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(v(\tau)\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}w(t-\tau)dt\right)d\tau.

Substituting
\vartheta=t-\tau
in the inner integral, we deduce:

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}w(t-\tau)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{(\vartheta+\tau)}{u}s}w(\vartheta)d\vartheta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{\tau s}{u}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{\vartheta s}{u}}w(\vartheta)d\vartheta=e^{-\frac{\tau s}{u}}W(s,u).

Hence,

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[\int_{0}^{t}v(\tau)w(t-\tau)\right]=\int_{0}^{\infty}v(\tau)e^{\frac{-s\tau}{u}}W(s,u)d\tau

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W(s,u)\int_{0}^{\infty}v(\tau)e^{\frac{-s\tau}{u}}d\tau=V(s,u)W(s,u).

This complete the proof.
\Box

Theorem 1.4

Derivative of the NL-TI transform.
Suppose that
{\Theta}\left[v(t)\right](s,u)
exists and that
v(t)
is differentiable
n-times on the interval
(0,\infty)
with
n^{th}
derivative

v^{(n)}(t)
, then

{\Theta}\left[v^{\prime}(t)\right](s,u)=\frac{s}{u}V(s,u)-v(0),

(14)

{\Theta}\left[v^{\prime\prime}(t)\right](s,u)=\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}V(s,u)-\frac{s}{u}v(0)-v^{\prime}(0),

(15)

{\Theta}\left[v^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)\right](s,u)=\frac{s^{3}}{u^{3}}V(s,u)-\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}v(0)-\frac{s}{u}v^{\prime}(0)-v^{\prime\prime}(0),

(16)

\vdots

{\Theta}\left[v^{(n)}(t)\right](s,u)=\frac{s^{n}}{u^{n}}V(s,u)-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\frac{s}{u}\right)^{n-(k+1)}v^{(k)}(0).

(17)

Proof.

Using Definition
1.1
of the NL-TI transform and
integration by parts, we deduce:

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[v^{\prime}(t)\right](s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}v^{\prime}(t)dt

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v(0)+\frac{s}{u}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}v(t)dt=-v(0)+\frac{s}{u}V(s,u).

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[v^{\prime\prime}(t)\right](s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s}{u}{\Theta}\left[v^{\prime}(t)\right](s,u)-v^{\prime}(0)=\frac{s}{u}\left[-v(0)+\frac{s}{u}V(s,u)\right]-v^{\prime}(0)

(19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v^{\prime}(0)-\frac{s}{u}v(0)+\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}V(s,u).

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[v^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)\right](s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s}{u}{\Theta}\left[v^{\prime\prime}(t)\right](s,u)-v^{\prime\prime}(0)=\frac{s}{u}\left[-v^{\prime}(0)-\frac{s}{u}v(0)+\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}V(s,u)\right]-v^{\prime\prime}(0)

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v^{\prime\prime}(0)-\frac{s}{u}v^{\prime}(0)-\frac{s^{2}}{u^{2}}v(0)+\frac{s^{3}}{u^{3}}V(s,u).

Finally, eq. (
17
) follows using mathematical
induction.
\Box

Some Properties of the NL-TI transform

Property 1

Linearity property of the NL-TI transform. Let
{\Theta}\left[v(t)\right](s,u)=V(s,u)
and
{\Theta}\left[w(t)\right](s,u)=W(s,u)
, then

{\Theta}\left[\alpha v(t)+\beta w(t)\right](s,u)=\alpha{\Theta}\left[v(t)\right](s,u)+\beta{\Theta}\left[w(t)\right](s,u),

(23)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are constants.

Proof.

Linearity property follows directly from Definition

1.1
.
\Box

Property 2

Exponential Shifting Property of the NL-TI transform. Let the function
v(t)\in A
and
\alpha
is an arbitrary constant, then

{\Theta}\left[e^{\alpha t}v(t)\right](s,u)=V(s-\alpha u).\\

(24)

Proof.

Using Definition
1.1
of the NL-TI transform, we get:

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[v(t)\right](s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}v(ut)dt.

(25)

Then

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[e^{\alpha t}v(t)\right](s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(\alpha t)}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}v(t)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\frac{(s-\alpha u)}{u}}v(t)dt

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle u\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(s-\alpha u)t}v(ut)dt={\Theta}\left[v(t)\right](s-\alpha u)=V(s-\alpha u).

In particular,

{\Theta}\left[\sin(3t)e^{-4t}\right](s,u)={\Theta}\left[\sin(3t)\right](s-(-4u))={\Theta}\left[\sin(3t)\right](s+4u).

(27)

Based on Definition
1.1
, the NL-TI transform of

\sin(3t)
is given by:

{\Theta}\left[\sin(3t)\right](s,u)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}\sin(3t)dt=\frac{1}{2i}\left(\frac{u}{s-3iu}-\frac{u}{s+3iu}\right)=\frac{3u^{2}}{s^{2}+9u^{2}}.

(28)

So, replacing the variable
s
with
(s+4u)
in eq. (
28
),
we obtain:

{\Theta}\left[\sin(3t)\right](s+4u)=\frac{3u^{2}}{(s+4u)^{2}+9u^{2}}=\frac{3u^{2}}{s^{2}+8us+25u^{2}}.

(29)

Alternatively,

\int_{0}^{\infty}\sin(3t)e^{-4t}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}dt=\frac{3u^{2}}{s^{2}+8us+25u^{2}}.

(30)

Moreover,

{\Theta}\left[te^{\alpha t}\right](s,u)=\frac{u^{2}}{(s-\alpha u)^{2}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{(s-\alpha)^{2}},\,\,u=1,\,\,\,\textnormal{Laplace
transform}\ [6]&\\
\\
\frac{u^{2}}{(1-\alpha u)^{2}},\,\,s=1,\,\,\,\textnormal{Yang transform}\ [19].&\\
\\
\end{array}\right.

(31)

This complete the proof.
\Box

Property 3

New Laplace-type transform of integral. Let
{\Theta}\left[v(t)\right]=V(s,u)
and
v(t)\in A
, then

{\Theta}\left[\int_{0}^{t}v(\zeta)d\zeta\right]=\frac{u}{s}V(s,u).

(32)

Proof.

Let
w(t)=\int_{0}^{t}v(\zeta)d\zeta
, then
w^{\prime}(t)=v(t)
and

w(0)=0
. Computing the NL-TI transform of both sides, we get:

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[w^{\prime}(t)\right](s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\Theta}\left[v(t)\right](s,u)=\frac{s}{u}{\Theta}\left[w(t)\right](s,u)-w(0)=V(s,u).

(33)

This implies

{\Theta}\left[\int_{0}^{t}v(\zeta)d\zeta\right]=\frac{u}{s}V(s,u).

(34)

This complete the proof.
\Box

Property 4

Multiple Shift Property of the NL-TI transform. Let
{\Theta}\left[v(t)\right](s,u)=V(s,u)
and
v(t)\in A
, then

{\Theta}\left[t^{n}v(t)\right](s,u)=(-u)^{n}\frac{d^{n}}{ds^{n}}V(s,u).

(35)

Proof.

By Definition
1.1
of the NL-TI transform and Leibnizs
rule, we obtain:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}V(s,u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}v(t)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(e^{\frac{-st}{u}}\right)v(t)dt

(36)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{u}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}tv(t)dt={\Theta}\left[tv(t)\right](s,u)=-u\frac{d}{ds}V(s,u).

Thus, eq. (
36
) above proves the theorem for
n=1
. To
generalized the theorem, we apply the induction hypothesis. Let
assume the theorem holds for
n=k
that is

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}t^{k}v(t)dt=(-u)^{k}\frac{d^{k}}{ds^{k}}V(s,u).

(37)

Then

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}t^{k}v(t)dt=(-u)^{k}\frac{d^{k+1}}{ds^{k+1}}V(s,u).

(38)

Alternatively, using Leibnizs rule, we deduce:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}t^{k}v(t)dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\left(e^{\frac{-st}{u}}\right)t^{k}v(t)dt

(39)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{u}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}t^{k+1}v(t)dt=(-u)^{k}\frac{d^{k+1}}{ds^{k+1}}V(s,u).

This implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\frac{-st}{u}}t^{k+1}v(t)dt=(-u)^{k+1}\frac{d^{k+1}}{ds^{k+1}}V(s,u).

(40)

Since, eq. (
40
) holds for
n=k+1
, then by induction
hypothesis the prove is complete.
\Box

Applications

In this section, we illustrate the applicability of the proposed
Laplace-type integral transform on steady heat-transfer problems
to proves its efficiency and high accuracy.

Example 1

Consider the following steady heat-transfer problem:

-hMv(t)=\rho\Lambda_{c_{p}}v^{\prime}(t),

(41)

subject to the initial condition

v(0)=\beta,

(42)

where
h-
is the convection heat transfer coefficient,
M-
is
the surface area of the body,
\rho-
is the density of the body,

\Lambda-
is the volume,
c_{p}-
is the specific heat of the
material, and

v(t)
is the temperature.

Applying the NL-TI transform on both sides of eq. (
41
),
we get:

-hMV(s,u)=\rho\Lambda_{c_{p}}\left(\frac{s}{u}V(s,u)-v(0)\right).

(43)

Substituting the given initial condition and simplifying, we get:

V(s,u)=\frac{\beta u}{s+\frac{hM}{\rho\Lambda_{c_{p}}}u}

(44)

Taking the inverse NL-TI transform of eq. (
44
), we get:

v(t)=\beta e^{-\frac{hM}{\rho\Lambda_{c_{p}}}t}.

(45)

The exact solution is in excellent agreement with the result
obtained in
[
5
,
20
]
.

Example 2

Consider the following steady heat-transfer problem:

v_{t}(x,t)=2v_{xx}(x,t),\,\,\,\,0<x<5,\,\,\,\,t>0.

(46)

Subject to the boundary and initial conditions

v(0,t)=0,\,\,\,v(5,t)=0,\,\,\,\,v(x,0)=10\sin(4\pi x)-5\sin(6\pi x).

(47)

Applying the NL-TI transform on both sides of eq. (
46
),
we deduce:

\frac{s}{u}V(x,s,u)-v(x,0)=\frac{2d^{2}V(x,s,u)}{dx^{2}}.

(48)

Substituting the given initial condition and simplifying, we get:

\frac{2d^{2}V(x,s,u)}{dx^{2}}-\frac{s}{u}V(x,s,u)=-10\sin(4\pi x)+5\sin(6\pi x).

(49)

The general solution of eq. (
49
) can be written as:

V(x,s,u)=V_{h}(x,s,u)+V_{p}(x,s,u),

(50)

where
V_{h}(x,s,u)
is the solution of the homogeneous part which
is given by:

V_{h}(x,s,u)=\alpha_{1}e^{\sqrt{\frac{s}{u}}x}+\alpha_{2}e^{-\sqrt{\frac{s}{u}}x},

(51)

and
V_{p}(x,s,u)
is the solution of the inhomogeneous part which

V_{p}(x,s,u)=\alpha\sin(4\pi x)+\beta\sin(6\pi x).

(52)

Applying the boundary conditions on eq. (
51

\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=0\Rightarrow\alpha_{1}e^{\sqrt{\frac{s}{u}}}+\alpha_{2}e^{-\sqrt{\frac{s}{u}}}=0\Rightarrow V_{h}(x,s,u)=0,

(53)

since,
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=0.

Using the method of undetermined coefficients on the inhomogeneous
part, we get:

V_{p}(x,s,u)=10\sin(4\pi x)\frac{u}{s+32\pi^{2}u}-5\sin(6\pi x)\frac{u}{s+72\pi^{2}u},

(54)

since,
\alpha=10\frac{u}{s+32\pi^{2}u},
and
\beta=-5\frac{u}{s+72\pi^{2}u}
.

Then eq. (
50
) will become:

V(x,s,u)=10\sin(4\pi x)\frac{u}{s+32\pi^{2}u}-5\sin(6\pi x)\frac{u}{s+72\pi^{2}u}.

(55)

Taking the inverse NL-TI transform of eq. (
55
),
we obtain:

v(x,t)=10e^{-32\pi^{2}t}\sin(4\pi x)-5e^{-72\pi^{2}t}\sin(6\pi x).

(56)

The exact solution is the same with the result obtained in

[
9
]
.

Example 3

Consider the following fractional porous medium equation:

D_{t}^{\alpha}v(x,t)=D_{x}(v(x,t)D_{x}v(x,t)),\,\,0<\alpha\leq 1,

(57)

subject to the initial condition

(58)

Applying theorem (
1.6
) on eq. (

\Theta\left[v(x,t)\right]=\frac{u}{s}x+\frac{u^{\alpha}}{s^{\alpha}}\Theta\left[D_{x}(v(x,t)D_{x}v(x,t))\right].

(59)

Computing the inverse NL-TI transform on both sides of

59

v(x,t)=x+\Theta^{-1}\left[\frac{u^{\alpha}}{s^{\alpha}}\Theta\left[D_{x}(v(x,t)D_{x}v(x,t))\right]\right].

(60)

Based on the basic idea of the homotopy analysis method (see

v(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p^{n}v_{n}(x,t).

(61)

60
) will become:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p^{n}v_{n}(x,t)=x+p\left(\Theta^{-1}\left[\frac{u^{\alpha}}{s^{\alpha}}\Theta\left[D_{x}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p^{n}H_{n}\right)\right]\right]\right),

[

On comparing the coefficients of same powers of
p
in

\displaystyle p^{0}\colon v_{0}(x,t)

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced a powerful Laplace-type integral
transform for finding a solution of steady heat-transfer problems.
The proposed Laplace-type integral transform converges to both
Yang transform, and the Laplace transforms just by changing
variables. Many interesting properties of the suggested integral
transform are discussed and successfully applied to steady
heat-transfer problems. Finally, based on the efficiency and
simplicity of the Laplace-type integral transform, we conclude
that it is a powerful mathematical tool for solving many problems
in science and engineering.

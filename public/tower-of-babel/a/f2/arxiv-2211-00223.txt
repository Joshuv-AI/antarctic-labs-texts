Quickest Change Detection with Leave-one-out Density Estimation
Yuchen Liang, Venugopal V. Veeravalli

Abstract
The problem of quickest change detection in a sequence of independent observations is considered. The pre-change distribution is assumed to be known, while the post-change distribution is completely unknown. A window-limited leave-one-out (LOO) CuSum test is developed, which does not assume any knowledge of the post-change distribution, and does not require any post-change training samples. It is shown that, with certain convergence conditions on the density estimator, the LOO-CuSum test is first-order asymptotically optimal, as the false alarm rate goes to zero. The analysis is validated through numerical results, where the LOO-CuSum test is compared with baseline tests that have distributional knowledge.



I
Introduction

The problem of quickest change detection (QCD) is of fundamental importance in mathematical statistics (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
]
for an overview). Given a sequence of observations whose distribution changes at some unknown change-point, the goal is to detect the change in distribution as quickly as possible after it occurs, while controlling the false alarm rate.
In classical formulations of the QCD problem, it is assumed that the pre- and post-change distributions are known, and that the observations are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) in either the pre-change or the post-change regime. However, in many practical situations, while it is reasonable to assume that we can accurately estimate the pre-change distribution, the post-change distribution is rarely completely known.

There have been extensive efforts to address pre- and/or post-change distributional uncertainty in QCD problems. In the case where both distributions are not fully known, one approach is to assume that they are indexed by a (low-dimensional) parameter that comes from a pre-defined parameter set, and employ a generalized likelihood ratio (GLR) approach to detection – this was first introduced in
[
3
]
and later analyzed in more detail in
[
4
]
.
In particular, in
[
4
]
, it is assumed that the pre-change distribution is known and that the post-change distribution comes from a parametric family, with the parameter being finite-dimensional. A window-limited GLR test is proposed, which is shown to be asymptotically optimal under certain smoothness conditions.
This work has recently been extended to non-stationary post-change settings
[
5
]
.

Another approach to dealing with distributional uncertainty in QCD problems is the minimax robust approach
[
6
]
, where it is assumed that the pre- and post-change distributions come from (known) mutually exclusive uncertainty classes, and the goal is to optimize the performance for the worst-case choice of distributions in the uncertainty classes. Under certain conditions, e.g., joint stochastic boundedness (see, e.g.,
[
7
]
for a definition) and weak stochastic boundedness
[
8
]
, robust solutions can be found
[
9
,
8
]
. However, these robust tests can have suboptimal performance for the actual distributions encountered in practice.

In this paper, we will assume complete knowledge of the pre-change distribution, while not making any parametric assumptions about the post-change distribution.
There have also been approaches to deal with non-parametric uncertainty in the distributions in QCD problems. One approach is to replace the log-likelihood ratio by some other statistic and formulate the test in the non-parametric setting. Examples of this approach include the use of kernel M-statistics
[
10
,
11
]
, one-class SVMs
[
12
]
, nearest neighbors
[
13
,
14
]
, and Geometric Entropy Minimization
[
15
,
16
]
. In
[
10
]
, a test is proposed that compares the kernel maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) within a window to a given threshold. A way to set the threshold is also proposed that meets the false alarm rate asymptotically
[
10
]
. Another approach is to estimate the log-likelihood ratio and thus the CuSum test statistic through a pre-collected training set. The include direct kernel estimation
[
17
]
and, more recently, neural network estimation
[
18
]
.
However,
the tests proposed in
[
10
]
–
[
18
]
lack explicit performance guarantees on the detection delay.

The closest work to ours is
[
19
]
, where a binning approach is proposed to solve the QCD problem asymptotically without any pre-collected training set. The asymptotic optimality is established for the case where the pre-change distribution is known, the post-change distribution is distinguishable from the pre-change with binning, and both distributions have discrete support
[
19
]
.

Our contributions are as follows:

1.

We propose a window-limited leave-one-out (LOO) CuSum test, which does not assume any knowledge of the post-change distribution, and does not require any post-change training samples.

2.

We provide a way to set the test threshold that asymptotically meets the false alarm constraint.

3.

We show that the proposed LOO-CuSum test is first-order asymptotically optimum, as the false alarm rate goes to zero.

4.

We validate our analysis through numerical results, in which we compare the LOO-CuSum test with baseline tests that have distributional knowledge.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Section
II
, we describe several properties required of the density estimators for asymptotically optimal detection. In Section
III
, we propose the LOO-CuSum test, and analyze its theoretical performance.
In Section
IV
, we present numerical results that validate the theoretical analysis.
Finally, we conclude the paper in Section
V
.

II
Leave-one-out (LOO) Density Estimator

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be i.i.d. samples drawn from an unknown distribution
p
. Denote by
\text{supp}(p)
the support of
p
. Denote by
\mathrm{E}_{p}
and
\mathrm{V}_{p}
, the expectation and variance operator, respectively, under
p
.
Denote the estimated density as
\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}
, where the subscript
-i
represents that
X_{i}
, with
k\leq i\leq n
, is the sample that is left out.
Note that the density is a function of
X^{n,k}_{-i}:=X_{k},\dots X_{i-1},X_{i+1},\dots,X_{n}
, and thus
\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}
and
X_{i}
are independent. The estimation procedure is assumed to be sample-homogeneous, i.e.,
\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d.}}{{=}}\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-j},\forall k\leq i<j\leq n
.

The Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between distributions
p
and
q
is

D(p||q):=\int_{\text{supp}(p)}\log(p(x)/q(x))dx.

Suppose that, for large enough
n
, there exists constants
0<\beta_{1},C_{1},C_{2}<\infty
and
1<\beta_{2}<2
(that depend only on the distribution
p
and the estimation procedure) such that, for each
1\leq i\leq n+1
, the KL loss
[
20
]
of the leave-one-out (LOO) estimator satisfies

\text{KL-loss}(\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}):=\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[D(p||\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i})\right]\leq\frac{C_{1}}{(n-k)^{\beta_{1}}}

(1)

where the expectation
\mathrm{E}_{p}
is over the randomness of
\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}
.
Also, the total variance of the estimator satisfies

\mathrm{V}_{p}\left(\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{p(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(X_{i})}\right)\leq C_{2}(n-k+1)^{\beta_{2}}.

(2)

One typical loss measure for a density estimator is the mean-integrated squared error (MISE), defined as (see, e.g.,
[
21
, Chap. 2]
)

\displaystyle\text{MISE}(p,\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\int(\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(x_{i})-p(x_{i}))^{2}dx_{i}\right]=\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\left\lVert\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}-p\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\right],\ \forall k\leq i\leq n.

(3)

The following lemma connects the MISE with the bounds in (
1
) and (
2
).

Lemma II.1
.

Suppose that there exist
\overline{\zeta},\underline{\zeta}
such that

0<\underline{\zeta}\leq p(x),\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(x)\leq\overline{\zeta}<\infty,~\forall x\in\text{supp}(p).

(4)

for any
k\leq i\leq n
.
If the estimator achieves

\text{MISE}(p,\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i})\leq\frac{C_{3}}{(n-k)^{\beta_{1}}}

(5)

for some constant
C_{3}<\infty
, then (
1
) and (
2
) are satisfied with

C_{1}=\frac{C_{3}}{\underline{\zeta}},\quad C_{2}=\frac{\overline{\zeta}C_{3}}{\underline{\zeta}^{2}},\quad\beta_{2}=2-\beta_{1}.

Proof.

Write
\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})=\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(X_{i})
. For the first proof, we use the fact that

\log{s}\leq s-1
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\log\frac{p(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\frac{p(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})}-1\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\int\frac{p^{2}(x_{i})-p(x_{i})\widehat{p}_{i}(x_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(x_{i})}dx_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\int\frac{p^{2}(x_{i})-2p(x_{i})\widehat{p}_{i}(x_{i})+\widehat{p}_{i}^{2}(x_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(x_{i})}dx_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\underline{\zeta}}\text{MISE}(p,\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i})

which shows (
1
), where
(*)
follows because both
p
and
\widehat{p}_{i}
are densities given
x_{-i}
.
For the variance, first note that
(\log{s})^{2}\leq r(s-1)^{2}
on
s\geq\underline{\zeta}/\overline{\zeta}
if
r:=(\ln(\underline{\zeta}/\overline{\zeta}))^{2}/((\underline{\zeta}/\overline{\zeta})-1)^{2}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathrm{V}_{p}\left(\log\frac{p(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[r^{2}\left(\frac{p(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})}-1\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=r^{2}\mathrm{E}_{p}\left[\int\frac{\left(p(x_{i})-\widehat{p}_{i}(x_{i})\right)^{2}}{\widehat{p}^{2}_{i}(x_{i})}p(x_{i})dx_{i}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{\zeta}r^{2}}{\underline{\zeta}^{2}}\text{MISE}(p,\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i})

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathrm{V}_{p}\left(\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{p(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq(n-k+1)^{2}\mathrm{V}_{p}\left(\log\frac{p(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\overline{\zeta}r^{2}C_{3}}{\underline{\zeta}^{2}}(n-k+1)^{2-\beta_{1}}

and thus
\beta_{2}=2-\beta_{1}
.
The proof is now complete. ∎

An example of a LOO estimator that satisfies (
1
) and (
2
) (under condition (
4
)) is the LOO
kernel
density estimator (LOO-KDE), defined as

\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(x_{i})=\frac{1}{(n-k)h}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=k\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{n}K\left(\frac{x_{i}-x_{j}}{h}\right)

(6)

where
K(\cdot)\geq 0
is a kernel function and
h>0
is a smoothing parameter.

The KL loss for kernel density estimators is analyzed carefully in
[
20
]
, where it is shown that the rate of convergence in KL loss is slower than that of MISE for most well-behaved densities. Nevertheless, this rate indeed converges to zero with a polynomial decay rate with the use of appropriate kernel functions, and thus (
1
) is satisfied. Furthermore, using (
4
), it can be shown that (
2
) is also satisfied. We note that the actual choices of
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
do not affect the first-order asymptotic optimality result given in Thm
III.3
.

III
QCD with LOO Density Estimation

Let
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{n},\dots\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a sequence of independent random variables (or vectors), and let
\nu
be a change-point. Assume that
X_{1},\dots,X_{\nu-1}
all have density
p_{0}
with respect to some measure
\mu
. Furthermore, assume that
X_{\nu},X_{\nu+1},\dots
have densities
p_{1}
also with respect to
\mu
. Here
p_{0}
is assumed to be completely known. While
p_{1}
is completely unknown, we assume that (
1
) and (
2
) are satisfied for LOO estimators of
p_{1}
.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{\nu}
denote the probability measure on the entire sequence of observations when the change-point is
\nu
, and let
\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\cdot\right]
denote the corresponding expectation.
The change-time
\nu
is assumed to be unknown but deterministic. The problem is to detect the change quickly, while controlling the false alarm rate. Let
\tau
be a stopping time
[
7
]
defined on the observation sequence associated with the detection rule, i.e.
\tau
is the time at which we stop taking observations and declare that the change has occurred.

III-A

Classical Results

When
p_{1}
is known, Lorden
[
3
]
proposed solving the following optimization problem to find the best stopping time
\tau
:

\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{WADD}\left(\tau\right)

(7)

where

\mathrm{WADD}\left(\tau\right):=\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\esssup\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[\left(\tau-\nu+1\right)^{+}|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]

(8)

characterizes the worst-case delay, and
{\cal F}_{n}
denotes the sigma algebra generated by
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
, i.e.,
{\cal F}_{n}=\sigma(X_{1},\dots,X_{n})
. The constraint set in (
7
) is

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}:=\left\{\tau:\mathrm{FAR}\left(\tau\right)\leq\alpha\right\}

(9)

with
\mathrm{FAR}\left(\tau\right):=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\tau\right]}

which guarantees that the false alarm rate of the algorithm does not exceed
\alpha
. Here,
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\cdot\right]
is the expectation operator when the change never happens, and
(\cdot)^{+}:=\max\{0,\cdot\}
.

Lorden also showed that Page’s Cumulative Sum (CuSum) algorithm
[
22
]
whose test statistic is given by:

W(n)=\max_{1\leq k\leq n+1}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\log\frac{p_{1}(X_{i})}{p_{0}(X_{i})}

solves the problem in (
7
) asymptotically as
\alpha\to 0
.
The CuSum stopping rule is given by:

\tau_{\text{Page}}\left(b\right):=\inf\{n:W(n)\geq b\}

(10)

where the threshold is set as
b=\left|\log\alpha\right|
. It was shown by Moustakides
[
23
]
that the CuSum algorithm is exactly optimal for the problem in (
7
).
The asymptotic performance is

\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{WADD}\left(\tau\right)\sim\mathrm{WADD}\left(\tau_{\text{Page}}\left(\left|\log\alpha\right|\right)\right)\sim\frac{\left|\log\alpha\right|}{D(p_{1}||p_{0})}

(11)

as
\alpha\to 0
.
Here

Y_{\alpha}\sim G_{\alpha}
is equivalent to
Y_{\alpha}=G_{\alpha}(1+o(1))
. Also, we use the notation
o(1)
to denote a quantity that goes to
0
, as
\alpha\to 0
or
b\to\infty
.

When the post-change distribution has parametric uncertainties, Lai
[
4
]
generalized this performance guarantee with the following assumptions. Suppose that
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
satisfy

\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\max_{t\leq n}\sum_{i=\nu}^{\nu+t}Z_{i}\geq(1+\delta)nI\right\}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0

(12)

for any
\delta>0
, and

\sup_{t\geq\nu\geq 1}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\sum_{i=t}^{t+n}Z_{i}\leq(1-\delta)nI\right\}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0

(13)

for any
\delta\in(0,1)
, with some constant
I>0
. Also, suppose that the window size
m_{\alpha}
satisfies

\liminf m_{\alpha}/\left|\log\alpha\right|>I^{-1}\quad\text{ and }\log m_{\alpha}=o(\left|\log\alpha\right|).

Then, the window-limited GLR CuSum test:

\tilde{\tau}_{GLR}\left(b\right):=\inf\left\{n:\max_{n-m_{\alpha}\leq k\leq{n+1}}\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}\sum_{i=k}^{n}Z^{\theta}_{i,k}\geq b\right\}

(14)

with test threshold
b_{\alpha}=\left|\log\alpha\right|
solves the problem in (
7
) asymptotically as
\alpha\to 0
. The asymptotic performance is

\inf_{\tau\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}}\mathrm{WADD}_{\theta}\left(\tau\right)\sim\mathrm{WADD}_{\theta}\left(\tilde{\tau}_{\text{Page}}\left(b_{\alpha}\right)\right)\sim\frac{\left|\log\alpha\right|}{I}.

(15)

Note that
I=D(p_{1}||p_{0})
when
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
are independent.

III-B

Leave-one-out CuSum Test

For the case when
p_{1}
is unknown, we define the leave-one-out log-likelihood ratio as

\widehat{Z}^{n,k}_{i}=\log\frac{\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(X_{i})}{p_{0}(X_{i})},\ \forall k\leq i\leq n.

(16)

and the leave-one-out (LOO) CuSum stopping rule as

\widehat{\tau}(b):=\inf\left\{n:\max_{(n-m_{\alpha})^{+}\leq k\leq n-1}\sum_{i=k}^{n}\widehat{Z}^{n,k}_{i}\geq b\right\}.

(17)

Here the window size
m_{\alpha}
is designed to satisfy

\liminf m_{\alpha}/\left|\log\alpha\right|>fI^{-1}~\text{with}~\log m_{\alpha}=o(\left|\log\alpha\right|)

(18)

where
f>1
is a constant.

In Lemma
III.1
, we show that
\widehat{\tau}
with a properly chosen threshold
b_{\alpha}
satisfies the false alarm constraint in (
9
) asymptotically. In Lemma
III.2
, we establish an asymptotic upper bound on
\mathrm{WADD}\left(\widehat{\tau}(b_{\alpha})\right)
. Finally, in Theorem
III.3
, we combine the two lemmas and establish the first-order asymptotic optimality of
\widehat{\tau}(b_{\alpha})
.

Lemma III.1
.

Suppose that
b_{\alpha}
satisfies

b_{\alpha}=\left|\log\alpha\right|+\log(8m_{\alpha}).

(19)

Then,

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\widehat{\tau}(b_{\alpha})\right]\geq\alpha^{-1}.

Remark
.

If
m_{\alpha}
satisfies (
18
), then
b_{\alpha}=\left|\log\alpha\right|(1+o(1))
as
\alpha\to 0
.

Proof.

Fix
\nu\geq 1
. For all threshold
b>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left\{\nu\leq\widehat{\tau}(b)<\nu+m_{\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=\nu-m_{\alpha}}^{\nu+m_{\alpha}-1}\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left\{\sum_{i=k}^{n}\widehat{Z}^{n,k}_{i}\geq b,\ \exists n:k\leq n\leq k+m_{\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{k=\nu-m_{\alpha}}^{\nu+m_{\alpha}-1}\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left\{\tau_{k}(b)\leq k+m_{\alpha}\right\}

(20)

where we define an auxiliary stopping time
\tau_{k}(b)
as

\tau_{k}(b):=\inf\left\{n\geq k:\sum_{i=k}^{n}\widehat{Z}^{n,k}_{i}\geq b\right\}.

Now, for any
b>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left\{\tau_{k}(b)\leq k+m_{\alpha}\right\}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\tau_{k}(b)\leq k+m_{\alpha}\}}d\mathbb{P}_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\int_{\{\tau_{k}(b)\leq k+m_{\alpha}\}}\prod_{i=k}^{\tau_{k}(b)}\frac{\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(x_{i})}{p_{0}(x_{i})}\prod_{i=k}^{\tau_{k}(b)}\frac{p_{0}(x_{i})}{\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(x_{i})}d\mathbb{P}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq e^{-b}\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\sum_{i=k}^{k+m_{\alpha}}e^{\widehat{Z}^{n,k}_{i}}(X_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=e^{-b}m_{\alpha}

(21)

where the second to last line follows by the definition of
\tau_{k}(b)
, and the last equality follows because

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\frac{\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(X_{i})}{p_{0}(X_{i})}\right]\\
=\int\underbrace{\left(\int\frac{\widehat{p}^{n,k}_{-i}(x_{i})}{p_{0}(x_{i})}p_{0}(x_{i})dx_{i}\right)}_{=1}p_{0}(x_{-i})dx_{-i}=1.

Therefore,

\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\mathbb{P}_{\infty}\left\{\nu\leq\widehat{\tau}(b)<\nu+m_{\alpha}\right\}\leq 2m_{\alpha}^{2}e^{-b},

and by
[
24
, Lemma 2.2(ii)]
,

\mathbb{E}_{\infty}\left[\widehat{\tau}(b)\right]\geq\frac{1}{8m_{\alpha}}e^{b}

for all
b>0
. Choosing
b=b_{\alpha}
gives the desired result. ∎

Lemma III.2
.

Let
b_{\alpha}=\left|\log\alpha\right|(1+o(1))
and
m_{\alpha}
satisfy (
18
). Suppose that (
1
) and (
2
) hold.
Further, suppose (
13
) holds. Then,

\mathrm{WADD}\left(\widehat{\tau}(b_{\alpha})\right)\leq\frac{\left|\log{\alpha}\right|}{D(p_{1}||p_{0})}(1+o(1))

as
\alpha\to 0
.

Proof.

Write
I=D(p_{1}||p_{0})
. Let
\delta_{0}:=1-f^{-1}
and
\delta_{b}\in(0,\delta_{0})
be a function of
b
such that
\delta_{b}\searrow 0
as
b\nearrow\infty
. Define

n_{b}:=\left\lfloor\frac{b}{I(1-\delta_{b})}\right\rfloor.

(22)

By definition of the window size
m_{\alpha}
in (
18
), if
b_{\alpha}=\left|\log\alpha\right|(1+o(1))
, then

n_{b}<\frac{b}{I(1-\delta_{0})}=\frac{fb}{I}\leq m_{\alpha}

for all sufficiently small
\alpha
. Suppose for now that we can get a large enough
b
to satisfy

\sup_{t\geq\nu\geq 1}\esssup\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\left.\sum_{i=t}^{t+n_{b}-1}\widehat{Z}^{t+n_{b}-1,t}_{i}<b\right|{\cal F}_{t-1}\right\}<2\delta_{b}^{2}.

(23)

Then,
\forall\nu\geq 1
,
\forall k\geq 1
, and for all large
b
,

\displaystyle\esssup\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\widehat{\tau}(b)-\nu+1>kn_{b}|\widehat{\tau}(b)-\nu+1>(k-1)n_{b},{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(i)}}{{\leq}}\esssup\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\left.\widehat{\tau}(b)-\nu+1>kn_{b}\right|{\cal F}_{\nu+(k-1)n_{b}-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(ii)}}{{\leq}}\esssup\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\left.\sum_{i=\nu+(k-1)n_{b}}^{\nu+kn_{b}-1}\widehat{Z}^{\nu+kn_{b}-1,\nu+(k-1)n_{b}}_{i}<b\right|{\cal F}_{\nu+(k-1)n_{b}-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(iii)}}{{\leq}}2\delta_{b}^{2}

(24)

In the series of inequalities above,
(i)
is by definition of essential supremum,
(ii)
is explained below, and
(iii)
follows from (
23
). For
(ii)
, consider the complement of the two events. Since
n_{b}\leq m_{\alpha}
, we have

\nu+(k-1)n_{b}\geq\nu+kn_{b}-1-m_{\alpha}

which implies that
\nu+(k-1)n_{b}
is still one of the hypothetical change-points. If

\sum_{i=\nu+(k-1)n_{b}}^{\nu+kn_{b}-1}\widehat{Z}^{\nu+kn_{b}-1,\nu+(k-1)n_{b}}_{i}\geq b,

then
\widehat{\tau}(b)-\nu+1=kn_{b}
. Thus, from recursion,

\esssup\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\widehat{\tau}(b)-\nu+1>kn_{b}|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right\}\leq(2\delta_{b}^{2})^{k}.

Therefore, for sufficiently large
b
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\nu\geq 1}\esssup\mathbb{E}_{\nu}\left[n_{b}^{-1}(\widehat{\tau}(b)-\nu+1)^{+}|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{n_{b}^{-1}(\widehat{\tau}(b)-\nu+1)^{+}>k|{\cal F}_{\nu-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2\delta_{b}^{2})^{k}=\frac{1}{1-2\delta_{b}^{2}}

and, as
b\to\infty
,

\mathrm{WADD}\left(\widehat{\tau}(b)\right)\leq\frac{n_{b}}{1-2\delta_{b}^{2}}\leq\frac{b}{I(1-\delta_{b})(1-2\delta_{b}^{2})}=\frac{b}{I}(1+o(1)).

It remains to show (
23
). Note that
{\cal F}_{t-1}
can be dropped by independence between
{\cal F}_{t-1}
and
\widehat{Z}^{t+n_{b}-1,t}_{i}
. For any
t\geq\nu\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\sum_{i=t}^{t+n_{b}-1}\widehat{Z}^{t+n_{b}-1,t}_{i}<b\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\sum_{i=t}^{t+n_{b}-1}\widehat{Z}^{t+n_{b}-1,t}_{i}<b,\sum_{i=t}^{t+n_{b}-1}Z_{i}-\widehat{Z}^{t+n_{b}-1,t}_{i}\leq\epsilon\right\}+

\displaystyle\qquad\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\sum_{i=t}^{t+n_{b}-1}\widehat{Z}^{t+n_{b}-1,t}_{i}<b,\sum_{i=t}^{t+n_{b}-1}Z_{i}-\widehat{Z}^{t+n_{b}-1,t}_{i}\geq\epsilon\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\sum_{i=t}^{t+n_{b}-1}Z_{i}\leq b+\epsilon\right\}+\mathbb{P}_{\nu}\left\{\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{i=t}^{t+n_{b}-1}\left(Z_{i}-\widehat{Z}^{t+n_{b}-1,t}_{i}\right)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{n_{b}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{1}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n_{b}}Z_{i}\leq b+\epsilon\right\}+\mathbb{P}_{1}\left\{\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{b}}\left(Z_{i}-\widehat{Z}^{n_{b},1}_{i}\right)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{n_{b}}\right\}

(25)

Now, the first term grows larger with large
\epsilon
, while the second term grows smaller. It is important to choose a proper
\epsilon=\epsilon_{b}
in order to keep both terms small. The idea in the following is that, we choose
\epsilon_{b}
by controlling the second term, and then verify that it is small enough also for the first for large
b
.

For the second term in (
III-B
), write
\widehat{p}_{i}
and
\widehat{Z}_{i}
as short-hand notations for
\widehat{p}^{n_{b},1}_{-i}
and
\widehat{Z}^{n_{b},1}_{-i}
, respectively. Note that
\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[Z_{i}-\widehat{Z}_{i}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[D(p_{1}||\widehat{p}_{i})\right]
. Under the conditions for the estimator in (
1
) and (
2
), the mean and variance of
n_{b}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{b}}(Z_{i}-\widehat{Z}_{i})
can be bounded as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{b}}\log\frac{p_{1}(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[D(p_{1}||\widehat{p}_{i})\right]\leq\frac{C_{1}}{n_{b}^{\beta_{1}}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{1}\left(\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{b}}\log\frac{p_{1}(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})}\right)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{n_{b}^{2-\beta_{2}}}.

Using Chebyhsev’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{1}\left\{\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{b}}\left(Z_{i}-\widehat{Z}_{i}\right)\geq\frac{\epsilon}{n_{b}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}_{1}\left\{\left|\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{b}}\left(Z_{i}-\widehat{Z}_{i}\right)-\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[D(p_{1}||\widehat{p}_{i})\right]\right|\geq\frac{\epsilon}{n_{b}}-\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[D(p_{1}||\widehat{p}_{i})\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{Var}_{1}\left(\frac{1}{n_{b}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{b}}\log\frac{p_{1}(X_{i})}{\widehat{p}_{i}(X_{i})}\right)\left(\frac{\epsilon}{n_{b}}-\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[D(p_{1}||\widehat{p}_{i})\right]\right)^{-2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{2}}{n_{b}^{2-\beta_{2}}}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{n_{b}}-\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[D(p_{1}||\widehat{p}_{i})\right]\right)^{-2}

(26)

for any
\epsilon>n_{b}\times\mathbb{E}_{1}\left[D(p_{1}||\widehat{p}_{i})\right]
.
Now, (
III-B
) is less than or equal to
\delta_{b}^{2}

(27)

Among the two terms above, the second term
\leq C_{1}n_{b}^{1-\beta_{1}}

V
Conclusion

We studied a window-limited LOO-CuSum test for QCD that does not assume any knowledge of the post-change distribution, and does not require post-change training samples. We established the first-order asymptotic optimality of the test, and validated our analysis through numerical results.

Geometric inequalities for CR-submanifolds
Mirjana Djorić and Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
We study two kinds of curvature invariants of Riemannian manifold equipped with a complex distribution D D (for example, a CR-submanifold of an almost Hermitian manifold) related to sets of pairwise orthogonal subspaces of the distribution. One kind of invariant is based on the mutual curvature of the subspaces and another is similar to Chen’s δ \delta -invariants. We compare the mutual curvature invariants with Chen-type invariants and prove geometric inequalities with intermediate mean curvature squared for CR-submanifolds in almost Hermitian spaces. In the case of a set of complex planes, we introduce and study curvature invariants based on the concept of holomorphic bisectional curvature. As applications, we give consequences of the absence of some D D -minimal CR-submanifolds in almost Hermitian manifolds. Keywords : almost Hermitian manifold; CR-submanifold; distribution; mutual curvature; mean curvature Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) 53C12; 53C15; 53C42

Introduction

In 1978, A. Bejancu introduced the notion of a CR-submanifold as a generalization of holomorphic and totally real submanifolds of almost Hermitian manifolds.
Since then, CR-submanifolds in various ambient spaces have been actively studied, for example,
[
4
,
5
,
9
]
.

The development of the extrinsic geometry of submanifolds led to the following problem
(for example,
[
3
]
):
find a simple optimal connection between the intrinsic and extrinsic invariants of a submanifold
in a Riemannian manifold; in particular, in space forms
.

B.Y. Chen introduced the concept of
\delta
-curvature invariants for Riemannian manifolds in the 1990s
and proved an optimal inequality for a submanifold, including
\delta
-curvature invariants and the square of mean curvature.
Chen invariants are obtained from the scalar curvature by discarding some of the sectional curvatures.
The case of equality led to the notion of “ideal immersions” in Euclidean space, that is, isometric immersions with the smallest possible tension.
Chen’s theory was extended by geometers to Kähler, (para-)contact, Lagrangian and affine submanifolds, warped products and submersions, see
[
3
,
4
]
.

In
[
7
,
8
]
, we introduced invariants of a Riemannian manifold,
which are related to the mutual curvature of noncomplementary pairwise orthogonal subspaces of the tangent bundle,
and proved
geometrical
inequalities for Riemannian submanifolds with applications to foliations.

In the paper, we study two kinds of curvature invariants of a Riemannian manifold equipped with a complex distribution
D

(in particular, a CR-submanifold of an almost Hermitian manifold), related to sets of pairwise orthogonal subspaces of the distribution.
One kind of invariant is based on the mutual curvature of the subspaces and the other is similar to Chen’s
\delta
-invariants.
We compare the mutual curvature invariants with Chen-type invariants
and prove geometric inequalities with intermediate mean curvature squared for CR-submanifolds in almost Hermitian spaces.
In the case of a set of complex planes, we introduce and study curvature invariants based on the holomorphic bisectional curvature of two planes.
As applications, we provide the consequences of the absence of some
{D}
-minimal CR-submanifolds.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we report some basic information about the curvature invariants of a manifold with a distribution.
In Section
3
, we study geometric inequalities for CR-submanifolds in almost Hermitian manifolds.

2
Curvature invariants of a manifold with a distribution

In this section, we recall
two kinds of curvature invariants of a manifold with a distribution (Chen-type invariants and invariants based on the mutual curvature, see
[
7
,
8
]

Let
(M^{d+l},g)\ (d,m>0)

d

Denote by

R_{X,Y}=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{[X,\,Y]}

Definition 1
.

Define Chen’s type curvature invariants
\delta_{D}
and

by

\displaystyle 2\,\delta_{D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})(x)=\tau_{D}(x)-\min\{\tau(V_{1})+\ldots+\tau(V_{k})\},

\displaystyle 2\,\hat{\delta}_{D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})(x)=\tau_{D}(x)-\max\{\tau(V_{1})+\ldots+\tau(V_{k})\},

(1)

where
\tau(V_{i})=\operatorname{trace}_{g}\operatorname{Ric}|_{V_{i}}
,
and
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
run over all
k\geq 0
mutually orthogonal subspaces of
D_{x}
at

such that
\dim V_{i}=n_{i}\ (0\leq i\leq k)
.

For example,
2\,\delta_{D}=\tau_{D}

2\,\delta_{D}(n_{1})(x)=\tau_{D}(x)-\min\tau(V_{1})

.

Remark 1
.

In (
1

[
3
]
) since the set “all mutually orthogonal subspaces

at a point

Let
\{e_{i}\}
be an orthonormal frame of a subspace
V=\bigoplus_{\,i=1}^{\,k}V_{i}
of
T_{x}M
such that

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n_{1}}\}\subset V_{1},\ \ldots,\ \{e_{n_{k-1}+1},\ldots,e_{n_{k}}\}\subset V_{k}.

For

, the
mutual curvature of a set

is defined by

{\rm S}_{\rm m}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}{\rm S}_{\rm m}(V_{i},V_{j}),

(2)

where

{\rm S}_{\rm m}(V_{i},V_{j})=\sum\limits_{\,n_{i-1}<a\,\leq n_{i},\ n_{j-1}<b\leq n_{j}}g(R_{e_{a},{e}_{b}}\,e_{b},{e}_{a})

of
(V_{i},V_{j})

Note that
{\rm S}_{\rm m}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})
does not depend on the choice of frames.
We get

\tau(V)=2\,{\rm S}_{\rm m}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})+\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}\tau(V_{i}),

(3)

where
\tau(V)=\operatorname{trace}_{g}\operatorname{Ric}|_{\,V}
is
the trace of the Ricci tensor on

V=\bigoplus_{\,i=1}^{\,k}V_{i}

For example, if all subspaces

are one-dimensional, then
2\,{\rm S}_{\rm m}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k})=\tau(V)
.

We introduce the curvature invariants based on the concept of mutual curvature.

Definition 2
.

Define the

\displaystyle\delta^{+}_{{\rm m},D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})(x)=\max{\rm S}_{\rm m}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}),

\displaystyle\delta^{-}_{{\rm m},D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})(x)=\min{\rm S}_{\rm m}(V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}),

V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
run over all
k\geq 2
mutually orthogonal subspaces of

\dim V_{i}=n_{i}

(2\leq i\leq k)

For

\delta^{\pm}_{{\rm m}}=\delta^{\pm}_{{\rm m},TM}
.

The invariants in (
1

Proposition 1
.

For

\displaystyle\delta^{+}_{{\rm m},{D}}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\geq\delta_{D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})-\delta_{D}(n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k})\,,

\displaystyle\delta^{-}_{{\rm m},{D}}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\leq\hat{\delta}_{D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})-\hat{\delta}_{D}(n_{1}+\ldots+n_{k})\,,

and if

\hat{\delta}_{D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})=\delta^{-}_{{\rm m},D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\leq\delta^{+}_{{\rm m},D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})=\delta_{D}(n_{1},\ldots,\linebreak n_{k}).

If the sectional curvature
K

\sum\nolimits_{\,i=1}^{k}n_{i}=s\leq d

\displaystyle\frac{c}{2}\,(s^{2}-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}n_{i}^{2})=c\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}n_{i}\,n_{j}\leq\delta^{-}_{{\rm m},D}(n_{1},\ldots n_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\delta^{+}_{{\rm m},D}(n_{1},\ldots n_{k})\leq C\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}n_{i}\,n_{j}=\frac{C}{2}(s^{2}-\sum\nolimits_{\,i}n_{i}^{2})\,.

(5)

Proof.

This is similar to the proof of Proposition 1 in
[
8
]
.
∎

Corollary 1
.

If
(M^{d+l},D,g)
has non-negative sectional curvature of planes tangent to
D
,
then

\hat{\delta}_{D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\leq\delta^{-}_{{\rm m},D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\leq\delta^{+}_{{\rm m},D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\leq\delta_{D}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}),

and if this sectional curvature is nonpositive, then the above inequalities are opposite.

Given two
J
-invariant planes
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
(2-dimensional subspaces) in
T_{x}M

(M,J,g)
, and unit vectors

Y\in\sigma^{\prime}

Goldberg and Kobayashi

defined the

K_{\rm h}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
by

K_{\rm h}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=R(X,JX,Y,JY).

(6)

This
depends on
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
only, and for
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
gives the
holomorphic sectional curvature
.
For a set of
J
-invariant planes in a complex distribution
of real dimension

Definition 3
.

Let
D
be a
d
-dimensional complex distribution of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, i.e., there is a skew-symmetric (1,1)-tensor
J:D\to D
such that

J^{\,2}X=-X,\quad g(JX,JY)=g(X,Y),\quad X,Y\in D.

The
holomorphic mutual curvature invariants

\delta^{\pm}_{{\,\rm h},D}(k)\ (1<k\leq d/2)
are defined by

\delta^{+}_{{\,\rm h},D}(k)(x)=\max{\rm S}_{\,\rm h}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}),\quad\delta^{-}_{{\,\rm h},D}(k)(x)=\min{\rm S}_{\,\rm h}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}),

where
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}
run over all
k
mutually orthogonal
J
-invariant planes of
D_{x}
at a point
x\in M
,
and
{\rm S}_{\,\rm h}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})
is defined using (
6

{\rm S}_{\,\rm h}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i<j}K_{\rm h}(\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j}).

(7)

For
D=TM
, i.e., for an almost Hermitian manifold,
we get the
holomorphic mutual curvature invariants

\delta^{\pm}_{\,\rm h}(k):=\delta^{\pm}_{\,{\,\rm h},TM}(k)

Lemma 1
.

Let
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}\}\ (2\leq k\leq d/2)
be mutually orthogonal
J
-invariant planes of a complex distribution
D_{x}
at a point
x\in M
.
Then

2\,{\rm S}_{\,\rm h}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k})={\rm S}_{\rm m}(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}).

(8)

Proof.

By the Bianchi identity, we get

K_{\rm h}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=R(X,Y,X,Y)+R(X,JY,X,JY).

(9)

Replacing
X
with
JX
,
we get

K_{\rm h}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=R(JX,Y,JX,Y)+R(JX,JY,JX,JY)
.
Thus,

{\rm S}_{\rm m}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})=2\,K_{\rm h}(\sigma,\sigma^{\prime})
.
From this, (
2
) and (
7
), we get (
8
).
∎

Corollary 2
.

The following inequalities are true for
2\leq k\leq d/2

2\,\delta^{+}_{{\,\rm h},D}(k)\leq\delta^{+}_{{\rm m},D}(\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{k}),\quad 2\,\delta^{-}_{{\,\rm h},D}(k)\geq\delta^{-}_{{\rm m},D}(\underbrace{2,\ldots,2}_{k}).

3
CR-submanifolds in almost Hermitian manifolds

In this section, using mutual curvature invariants, Chen-type invariants and holomorphic mutual curvature invariants,
we prove several geometric inequalities for CR-submanifolds in almost Hermitian manifolds.

An even-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
equipped with a skew-symmetric (1,1)-tensor
\bar{J}
such that
\bar{J}^{\,2}X=-X

and
\bar{g}(\bar{J}X,\bar{J}Y)=\bar{g}(X,Y)
for all
X,Y\in T\bar{M}
is called an almost Hermitian manifold.
We will put a top “bar” for objects related to
\bar{M}
.
A submanifold
M^{d+l}\ (d,l>0)
of an almost Hermitian manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{J},\bar{g})
is called a
CR-submanifold

if
D=\bar{J}(TM)\cap TM
is a complex distribution (the maximal
\bar{J}
-invariant subbundle) of constant real dimension
d
, see
[
5
, Definition 7.2]
.
A different definition is given in
[
1
,
9
]
:
a real submanifold
M^{d+l}\ (d,l>0)
of an almost Hermitian manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{J},\bar{g})
is called a CR-submanifold if there exists on
M
a totally real distribution
D^{\bot}

(i.e.,
\bar{J}(D^{\bot})\subset T^{\bot}M
) whose orthogonal complement
D
(i.e.,
TM=D\oplus D^{\bot}
) is a complex distribution (i.e.,
\bar{J}(D)=D
) of constant real dimension
d
.
Both definitions above give the same thing when

\dim D
is maximum, that is,

D^{\perp}
is one-dimensional. The main examples are real hypersurfaces, others examples are explained in

[
5
]
.

Example 2
.

Let
(M^{d+1},g)

(\bar{M},\bar{J},\bar{g})

Then

admits an almost contact metric structure

\varphi{=}\bar{J}|_{D}

Let

(M,g)

(\bar{M},\bar{g})

Recall the Gauss equation,

\bar{g}(\bar{R}_{Y,Z}\,U,X)=g(R_{Y,Z}\,U,X)+g({h}(Y,U),{h}(Z,X))-g({h}(Z,U),{h}(Y,X)),

(10)

where

\bar{R}
and

are the curvature tensors of
(\bar{M},\bar{g})
and

The mean curvature vector field of a subspace

is given by

H_{V}=\sum_{i}h(e_{i},e_{i})
, where
e_{i}
is an orthonormal basis of

In short form, we will write

,

For a CR-submanifold

{\cal H}_{D_{x}}(s)=\max\{\,\|{H}_{V}\,\|:\ V\subset D_{x},\ \dim V=s>0\}.

If

Note that for

A CR-submanifold

Note that

\delta^{+}_{{\rm m},{D}\,}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k})\leq\delta^{+}_{{\rm m}}(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}),

Theorem 1
.

Let
(M^{d+l},g)
be a CR-submanifold of an almost Hermitian manifold
(\bar{M},\bar{J},\bar{g})

D=\bar{J}(TM)\cap TM

For any natural numbers

\|\,{H}_{D}\,\|^{2},&{\rm if}\ s=d,\end{array}\right.

4
Conclusions

We studied the question of finding a simple optimal connection between the intrinsic and extrinsic invariants of
a manifold equipped with a complex distribution.
The main contribution of the paper is the concept of curvature invariants
\delta^{\pm}_{{\rm m},D}
of
CR-submanifolds of almost Hermitian manifolds, based on the mutual curvature of several pairwise orthogonal subspaces of a contact distribution
D
.
We used these curvature invariants and Chen-type curvature invariants
\delta^{\pm}_{D}

to prove new geometric inequalities involving the squared intermediate mean curvature for CR-submanifolds of almost Hermitian manifolds.
In the case of
complex planes, we study curvature invariants
\delta^{\pm}_{\,{\,\rm h},D}
based on the concept of holomorphic bisectional curvature.
Consequences of the absence of some
D
-minimal CR-submanifolds were provided.

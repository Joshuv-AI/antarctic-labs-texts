Abstract
. We give a version of the usual Jacobian characterization of the defining ideal of the singular locus in the equal characteristic case: the new theorem is valid for essentially affine algebras over a complete local algebra over a mixed characteristic discrete valuation ring. The result makes use of the minors of a matrix that includes a row coming from the values of a p p -derivation. To study the analogue of modules of differentials associated with the mixed Jacobian matrices that arise in our context, we introduce and investigate the notion of a perivation , which may be thought of, roughly, as a linearization of the notion of p p -derivation. We also develop a mixed characteristic analogue of the positive characteristic Γ \Gamma -construction, and apply this to give additional nonsingularity criteria.

The Jacobian criterion is a familiar tool for finding the singular locus of a finitely generated algebra over a perfect field: for an equidimensional variety, the singular locus is cut out by minors of the Jacobian matrix whose entries are partial derivatives of the defining equations. , so we have
(x+y)^{p^{2}}\equiv\sum_{i+j=p}\binom{p^{2}}{pi}x^{pi}y^{pj}\ \mathrm{mod}\ p^{2}R.
We have that
\binom{pn}{pm}\equiv\binom{n}{m}\mathrm{mod}\ p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
, (cf.
[
23
, Exercise 1.6(c)]
), so

\displaystyle(x+y)^{p^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{i+j=p}\binom{p}{i}x^{pi}y^{pj}

\displaystyle\equiv x^{p^{2}}+y^{p^{2}}-pC_{p}(x^{p},y^{p})

modulo
p^{2}R.

∎

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
(V,pV,K)
be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer
p
. Let
\{\gamma_{\lambda}\ |\ \lambda\in\Lambda\}
be a set of elements in
V
that maps bijectively to a
p
-base for
K
. Then the map

\psi\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus\Lambda}\\
0\leq\alpha_{\lambda}<p\end{subarray}}a_{\alpha}^{p}\gamma^{\alpha}+pb\right)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus\Lambda}\\
0\leq\alpha_{\lambda}<p\end{subarray}}a_{\alpha}^{p^{2}}\gamma^{p\alpha}+pb^{p}

is a lift of Frobenius modulo
p^{2}
on
V
. Hence, the map
\delta:V\to K
given by
\delta(f)=\frac{\psi(f)-f^{p}}{p}
is a
p
-derivation modulo
p^{2}
on
V
.

Proof.

First, we show that the map
\psi
is well-defined. We have that

V/pV=\bigoplus_{{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Lambda},0\leq\alpha_{\lambda}<p}}(V/pV)^{p}\overline{\gamma^{\alpha}},

so any element in
V
can be written as a finite sum
\sum a_{\alpha}^{p}\gamma^{\alpha}+pb
for some elements
a_{\alpha},b\in V
. If

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus\Lambda}\\
0\leq\alpha_{\lambda}<p\end{subarray}}a_{\alpha}^{p}\gamma^{\alpha}+pb=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus\Lambda}\\
0\leq\alpha_{\lambda}<p\end{subarray}}c_{\alpha}^{p}\gamma^{\alpha}+pd,

then
\sum_{{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus\Lambda},0\leq\alpha_{\lambda}<p}}(a_{\alpha}^{p}-c_{\alpha}^{p})\gamma^{\alpha}\in pV
, so each
a_{\alpha}^{p}-c_{\alpha}^{p}\in pV
by
K^{p}
-linear independence of the images of the elements
\gamma_{\lambda}
, and hence
a_{\alpha}-c_{\alpha}\in pV
by injectivity of the Frobenius on
K
. Substituting back, we have that

p(b-d)\in\sum_{{\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{\oplus\Lambda},0\leq\alpha_{\lambda}<p}}((a_{\alpha}^{p}+pe_{\alpha})^{p}-a_{\alpha}^{p})\gamma^{\alpha}

for some elements
e_{\alpha}\in V
, and hence this is in
p^{2}V
. Since
p
is a regular element on
V
,
b-d\in pV
as well. Well-definedness of the map is then evident.

We check now that
\psi
is additive. We have

\displaystyle\psi\left(\sum a_{\alpha}^{p}\gamma^{\alpha}+pb+\sum c_{\alpha}^{p}\gamma^{\alpha}+pd\right)

\displaystyle=\psi\left(\sum\left((a_{\alpha}+c_{\alpha})^{p}+pC_{p}(a_{\alpha},c_{\alpha})\right)\gamma^{\alpha}+p(b+d)\right)

\displaystyle=\psi\left(\sum(a_{\alpha}+c_{\alpha})^{p}\gamma^{\alpha}+p(b+d+\sum C_{p}(a_{\alpha},c_{\alpha})\gamma^{\alpha})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum(a_{\alpha}+c_{\alpha})^{p^{2}}\gamma^{p\alpha}+p(b+d+\sum C_{p}(a_{\alpha},c_{\alpha})\gamma^{\alpha})^{p}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum(a_{\alpha}^{p^{2}}+c_{\alpha}^{p^{2}})\gamma^{p\alpha}+p(b^{p}+d^{p})

\displaystyle=\psi\left(\sum a_{\alpha}^{p}\gamma^{\alpha}+pb\right)+\psi\left(\sum c_{\alpha}^{p}\gamma^{\alpha}+pd\right),

where the equivalence in the penultimate line is modulo
p^{2}V
, using the previous lemma and the fact that
p(X+Y+Z)^{p}\equiv p(X^{p}+Y^{p}+Z^{p})
modulo
p^{2}
.

The verification of multiplicativity modulo
p^{2}V
is straightforward, and omitted.
∎

Combining this with Example
2.3
, we obtain:

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
V
be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. Any polynomial ring in arbitrarily many variables, power series ring in finitely many variables, or polynomial ring over a power series ring in finitely many variables over
V
admits a
p
-derivation mod
p^{2}
. Explicitly, one may take a
p
-derivation mod
p^{2}
as constructed in Proposition
2.9
and extend by prescribing arbitrary values to the variables.

Indeed, by parts (4) and (5) of Example
2.3
and the previous proposition, there is a
p
-derivation modulo
p^{2}
on
\widehat{V}[[x]][y]
, for a finite set of variables
x
and an arbitrary set of variables
y
. Since we have canonical isomorphisms
\widehat{V}[[x]][y]/p^{i}\widehat{V}[[x]][y]\cong{V}[[x]][y]/p^{i}{V}[[x]][y]
for
i=1,2
, such a map can be identified with a
p
-derivation modulo
p^{2}
on
{V}[[x]][y]
.

2.4.
Perivations

In this subsection, we discuss the notion of
perivation
, which is essentially the same as that of
total
p
-derivations
in the sense of Dupuy, Katz, Rabinoff, and Zureick-Brown
[
6
]
, and the notion of FW-derivation of Saito
[
20
]
.

Definition 2.11

(cf.
[
6
, Definition 2.1.1]
)
.

Let
R
be a ring, and
p
a prime integer. Let
M
be an
R/pR
-module. A map
\alpha:R\to M
is a
perivation
if for all
x,y\in R
, we have

(1)

\alpha(0)=\alpha(1)=0
,

(2)

\alpha(x+y)=\alpha(x)+\alpha(y)+C_{p}(x,y)\alpha(p)
, and

(3)

\alpha(xy)=x^{p}\alpha(y)+y^{p}\alpha(x)
.

We call
\alpha(p)
the
distinguished element
of
\alpha
.
If additionally,
(A,\delta)
is a mod
p^{2}

\delta
-ring, with
A
a subring of
R
, and

(4)

\alpha(a)=\delta(a)\alpha(p)
for all
a\in A
,

then we say
\alpha
is a
perivation over
A
.

Example 2.12
.

If
(R,\delta)
is a mod
p^{2}

\delta
-ring, then the composition
R\xrightarrow{\delta}R\twoheadrightarrow R/pR
is a perivation with distinguished element
1
. More generally, if
M
is an
R/pR
-module, and
t\in M
, then the map
R\xrightarrow{\delta}R\xrightarrow{\cdot t}M
is a perivation with distinguished element
t
; in this setting, we call a perivation of this form
trivial
. Thus, condition (
4
) above says that the restriction of
\alpha
to
A
is trivial.

Example 2.13
.

Given a ring
R
, consider the ring homomorphism
\beta
given as the composition
R\twoheadrightarrow R/pR\xrightarrow{F}R/pR
, where
F
is the Frobenius map. If
R/pR
is considered as an
R
-algebra via
\beta
, then any derivation of
R
into an
R/pR
-module is a perivation with distinguished element zero. Conversely, a perivation to an
(R/pR)
-module with distinguished element zero is a derivation (with respect to
\beta
). We call such a map a
derivation of the Frobenius
for short. We write
\mathrm{Der}^{F}_{R|A}(M)
for the collection of
A
-linear derivations of the Frobenius from
R
into an
(R/pR)
-module
M
.

If
R
has characteristic
p
, then for any perivation, we have
\alpha(p)=\alpha(0)=0
, so a perivation is just a derivation of the Frobenius.

Remark 2.14
.

Any perivation on a ring
R
is a perivation over
\mathbb{Z}
. Indeed, the unique
p
-derivation on
\mathbb{Z}
is given by the rule
\delta(n)=(n-n^{p})/p
, and by a simple induction, we have
\alpha(n)=n\alpha(1)+C_{p}(1,\dots,1)\alpha(p)=(n-n^{p})/p\cdot\alpha(p)
. Thus, we lose no generality by restriction to the relative setting of perivations over a
\delta
-ring; we may take
\mathbb{Z}
as the base ring.

We record a few basic observations on perivations.

Remark 2.15
.

(1)

If
M
is an
R/pR
-module, then the collection of perivations from
R
to
M
admits a natural
R/pR
-module structure by postmultiplication, and likewise for perivations over a mod
p^{2}

\delta
-ring
A
. We denote these modules by
\mathrm{Per}_{R}(M)
and
\mathrm{Per}_{R|(A,\delta)}(M)
, respectively.

(2)

If
\alpha:R\to M
is a perivation, and
\phi:M\to N
is an
R
-module homomorphism, then
\phi\circ\alpha
is a perivation, as is readily verified from the definition.

Discussion 2.16

(
p
-linear maps and the Peskine-Szpiro functor)
.

Recall that if
R
is a ring of prime characteristic
p>0
, a map
\eta
between two
R
-modules

and
N
is
p
-linear
if it is additive and
\eta(rm)=r^{p}\eta(m)
for all

and

. If

is the Frobenius map on

, let
{}^{1}(-):R\mathrm{-mod}\to R\mathrm{-mod}
be the functor of restriction of scalars through
F
, and
F_{R}(-):R\mathrm{-mod}\to R\mathrm{-mod}
be the functor of extension of scalars through

.

A map
\eta:M\to N
is
p
-linear if and only
\eta:M\to{}^{1}N
is
R
-linear. By Hom-tensor adjunction, there is a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,{}^{1}N)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(F_{R}(M),N)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle\mapsto\big((r\otimes m)\mapsto r\eta(m)\big);

thus, we can identify

-linear maps from

to

with
R
-linear maps

. By abuse of notation, we will use the same name for maps we identify in this way.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
(A,\delta)
be a mod
p^{2}

\delta
-ring,
R
be an
A
-algebra, and
I
be an ideal of
R
. Let
\alpha:R\to M
be a perivation over
A
.

(1)

\alpha
descends to a well-defined perivation
\bar{\alpha}:R/(I^{2}+pI)\to M/IM
.

(2)

The restriction of
\bar{\alpha}
to
I/(I^{2}+pI)\to M/IM
is a
p
-linear map over
R/pR
.

(3)

If
\alpha(I)\subseteq IM
, then
\alpha
descends to a perivation
\alpha^{\prime}:R/I\to M/IM
.

(4)

If

, then there is a natural bijection between perivations from
R/I
to
M
and perivations from
R
to
M
that map
I
to
0
.

Proof.

For part (
1
), let

, and
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{t},a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{t}\in I
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha(r+pa_{0}+\sum_{i}a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i})=\alpha(r)+\alpha(pa_{0})+\alpha(\sum_{i}a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i})+C_{p}(r,pa_{0},\sum_{i}a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i})\alpha(p)

\displaystyle=\alpha(r)+\alpha(pa_{0})+\sum_{i}\alpha(a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i})+C_{p}(r,pa_{0},\sum_{i}a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i})\alpha(p)+C_{p}(a_{1}a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a_{t}a^{\prime}_{t})\alpha(p).

Since
C_{p}(r,pa_{0},\sum_{i}a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i})
and
C_{p}(a_{1}a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a_{t}a^{\prime}_{t})
lie in
I+(p)
, we have, modulo

,

\displaystyle\alpha(r+pa_{0}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i})\equiv\alpha(r)+\alpha(pa_{0})+\sum_{i}\alpha(a_{i}a^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle\equiv\alpha(r)+p^{p}\alpha(a_{0})+a_{0}^{p}\alpha(p)+\sum_{i}{a^{\prime}}_{i}^{p}\alpha(a_{i})+\sum_{i}a_{i}^{p}\alpha({a^{\prime}}_{i})\equiv\alpha(r).

For part (
2
), we note that

\alpha(ra_{0})=r^{p}\alpha(a_{0})+a_{0}^{p}\alpha(r)\equiv r^{p}\alpha(a_{0})\quad\text{modulo}\ IM.

Part (
3
) is clear. For part (
4
), we note first that given a perivation from
R/I
to
M
, the map from
R
to

obtained by precomposing with the quotient map is a perivation from
R
to

that maps

to
0
. Conversely, by part (

), a perivation from

to an
R/I
-module that sends

∎

In particular, any perivation

factors through

.

3.
Universal perivation modules

In this section, we study universal objects for perivations.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(A,\delta)
be a mod
p^{2}

\delta
-ring, and
R
be an
A
-algebra.
We say that a perivation
\alpha:R\to M
is a
universal perivation over
A
and
M
is a
universal perivation module over
A
if for any perivation
\beta:R\to N
over
A
there is a unique
R
-module homomorphism
\phi:M\to N
such that
\beta=\phi\circ\alpha
. We will write
\tilde{d}_{R|(A,\delta)}:R\to\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)}
for a universal perivation.

In particular, for a universal perivation module
\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)}
, there are natural isomorphisms
\mathrm{Per}_{R|(A,\delta)}(M)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)},M)
for all
R
-modules
M
.

Universal perivations for
A=\mathbb{Z}
were constructed in
[
6
]
(where universal perivation modules are called
total
p
-differentials
), and were used there to study existence of
p
-derivations on
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
-algebras. In particular they always exist; a construction for these modules appears below. These modules appear in
[
20
]
as modules of FW-differentials; many of the properties established below also appear there. They also appear implicitly in the work of Zdanowicz
[
26
, §4.2]
on existence of lifts of Frobenius /
p
-derivations on
\mathbb{Z}/p^{2}\mathbb{Z}
-algebras, and are related to a construction of Gabber and Romero
[
7
, 9.6.12]
.

In this section, we record some basic properties of universal perivations. Many of these results overlap with
[
6
]
and
[
20
]
.

By uniqueness of representing objects (i.e., Yoneda’s Lemma), universal perivation modules are unique up to natural isomorphism. Moreover, if
\phi:R\to S
is an
A
-algebra homomorphism, and
M
is an
S/pS
-module, we obtain a map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Per}_{S|(A,\delta)}(M)

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Per}_{R|(A,\delta)}(M)

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\alpha\circ\phi

which induces the natural map

\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)}\xrightarrow{\tilde{d}_{\phi}}\widetilde{\Omega}_{S|(A,\delta)}.

Remark 3.2
.

If
R
has characteristic
p>0
, then
\mathrm{Per}_{R|(A,\delta)}(M)=\mathrm{Der}^{F}_{R/pR|A/pA}(M)
, and hence

\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)}\cong F_{R/pR}(\Omega_{R/pR|A/pA})
.

We record some analogues of the fundamental sequences for differentials.

Proposition 3.3

(First fundamental sequences)
.

(1)

Let
(A,\delta)
be a mod
p^{2}

\delta
-ring. Let
R\xrightarrow{\phi}S
be an
A
-algebra homomorphism. Then there is a natural exact sequence

S\otimes_{R}\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)}\to\widetilde{\Omega}_{S|(A,\delta)}\to F_{S/pS}(\Omega_{S/pS|R/pR})\to 0.

(2)

Let
(A,\delta)\to(B,\varepsilon)
be a mod
p^{2}

\delta
-ring homomorphism (i.e., a ring homomorphism
\psi
such that
\psi\circ\delta=\varepsilon\circ\psi
), and
B\to R
be a ring homomorphism. Then there is a natural exact sequence

R\otimes_{B}F_{B/pB}({\Omega}_{B/pB|A/pA})\to\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)}\to\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(B,\varepsilon)}\to 0.

Proof.

(1)

First, we claim that there are natural exact sequences

0\to\mathrm{Der}^{F}_{S/pS|R/pR}(M)\to\mathrm{Per}_{S|(A,\delta)}(M)\to\mathrm{Per}_{R|(A,\delta)}(M)

for
S/pS
-modules
M
. We have the first inclusion, since
\mathrm{Der}^{F}_{S/pS|R/pR}(M)
is naturally isomorphic to
\mathrm{Der}^{F}_{S|R}(M)
, and the latter naturally embeds as a subset in
\mathrm{Per}_{S|(A,\delta)}(M)
. For exactness in the middle, we need to see that a perivation that vanishes on
R
is an
R
-linear derivation of the Frobenius. For such a perivation, the distinguished element is
0
(since
p\in R
), so it is a derivation of Frobenius, and the claim is then clear.

Now, since

\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(S\otimes_{R}\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)},M)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)},\mathrm{Hom}_{S}(S,M))\cong\mathrm{Per}_{R|(A,\delta)}(M)

and
\mathrm{Hom}_{S|(A,\delta)}(F_{S/pS}(\Omega_{S/pS|R/pR}),M)\cong\mathrm{Der}^{F}_{S|R}(M)
for all
S/pS
-modules
M
, by Yoneda’s Lemma, there is a complex as in the statement, which must be exact.

(2)

Along the same lines, it suffices to show that for every
R/pR
-module
M
we have an exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{Per}_{R|(B,\varepsilon)}(M)\to\mathrm{Per}_{R|(A,\delta)}(M)\to\mathrm{Der}^{F}_{B|A}(M).

For the map
\mathrm{Per}_{R|(A,\delta)}(M)\to\mathrm{Der}^{F}_{B|A}(M)
, we send a perivation
\alpha
to the map
\beta=\alpha-\alpha(p)\varepsilon
. One verifies immediately that this is an
A
-linear derivation of the Frobenius on
B
, and that
\beta
is zero if and only if
\alpha
restricted to
B
is a trivial perivation.∎

Remark 3.4
.

As a particular case of the first fundamental sequence, when

, we have

S/pS\to\widetilde{\Omega}_{S|A}\to F_{S/pS}(\Omega_{S/pS|A/pA})\to 0.

This exact sequence was considered in
[

]
and implicitly in
[

]
in connection with existence of lifts of Frobenius /

-derivations modulo

. In particular, this sequence is split exact if and only if

extends to a

-derivation mod

on

\widetilde{\Omega}_{S|(A,\delta)}\to S/pS
.

Proposition 3.5

(Second fundamental sequence)
.

Let
(A,\delta)
be a mod
p^{2}

\delta
-ring. Let
R
be an
A
-algebra, and

. Then there is a natural exact sequence

F_{R/(I+pR)}(I/I^{2}+pI)\to R/I\otimes_{R}\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)}\to\widetilde{\Omega}_{(R/I)|(A,\delta)}\to 0.

Proof.

For the first map above, we consider the perivation given as the composition:

R\xrightarrow{\tilde{d}_{R}}\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)}\twoheadrightarrow R/I\otimes_{R}\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(A,\delta)};

by Lemma

(

Observe that if

Thus, along the same lines as the proof of Proposition
3.3
, it suffices to show that we have natural exact sequences

0\to\mathrm{Per}_{R/I|(A,\delta)}(M)\to\mathrm{Per}_{R|(A,\delta)}(M)\to\mathrm{Hom}_{R/I}(F_{R/(I+pR)}(I/(I^{2}+pI)),M)

for all

. By Lemma

to

.
∎

See also
[

for a version of a second fundamental sequence. We note that by

Our next goal is to give a construction of universal perivation modules.

Construction 3.6

Let
(A,\delta)

\delta

Proposition 3.7

In the setting of Construction

Proof.

is a perivation that sends

To see this, let

\displaystyle={\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}(p,\delta_{p}(f),x,n)}\cong{\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}}(p,n,\delta_{p}(-n))}.

If

(p,n)=\mathbb{Z}

For

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{R}(p,I_{1}(\widetilde{J}(f)))

\displaystyle\cong\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}{(2,\delta_{2}(f),(2x)^{p},x^{2}-n)}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}{(2,\delta_{2}(f),x^{2}-n)}=\mathcal{V}_{\mathbb{Z}[x]}{(2,\delta_{2}(-n)+nx^{2},x^{2}-n)}.

If

mod

is odd, so

is nonempty, corresponding to the singular maximal ideal
(2,\sqrt{n}-1)\subset R
.

If

mod

, then
\delta_{2}(-n)
is odd, so

If

is empty. Of course, this agrees with the basic number theory fact that for a squarefree nonunit

is integrally closed in its fraction field (and equivalently regular, as

is a one-dimensional domain) if and only if

Remark 4.11
.

Theorem
4.9
can be used to give a quick proof that for any finitely generated algebra over any complete local ring of mixed characteristic, the singular locus is closed, given the result in equal characteristic. We can write such a ring as a quotient of a polynomial ring over a power series ring over an unramified discrete valuation ring
(V,pV)
, say
T
. Given a primary decomposition
Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{n}
of the presenting ideal
I
, the localization at any prime
\mathfrak{p}
that contains two
Q_{i}
’s or some
Q_{i}
that is not a minimal prime is not a domain, and hence not regular: this is a closed set, and the remainder of the singular locus is the singular locus of the quotients by the minimal primes, so we can reduce to the case that the ring
T/Q
is a domain. If
p\in Q
, we reduce to the equal characteristic case. Otherwise, the singular locus of
R=T/Q
is described by Theorem
4.9
. A priori, this describes the singular locus as the union of a closed set and a locally closed set, but Nagata’s openness criterion
[
15
, Theorem 24.2]
applies readily to the complement.

4.2.
Elementary proof

We now provide a concrete self-contained proof of Theorem A in the affine case. For simplicity, we assume that the residue field of the base discrete valuation ring is algebraically closed, though the perfect case follows easily.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
(V,pV,K)
be an unramified discrete valuation ring of mixed characteristic. Assume that
K
is algebraically closed. Let
T=V[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
, and
\delta
be a
p
-derivation mod
p^{2}
on
T
extending the
p
-derivation mod
p^{2}
on
V
. Let
I=(f_{1},\dots,f_{a})
be an ideal of pure height

in
T
, and

. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime ideal of
R
with
p\in\mathfrak{p}
.

Then, the mixed Jacobian matrix
\widetilde{J}(\bm{f})
considered in
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
has rank at most
h
, and equality holds if and only if
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is regular.

Proof.

First, we consider the case where
\mathfrak{p}
is a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. Let
\mathfrak{n}
be the preimage of
\mathfrak{m}
in
T
. It follows from the Nullstellensatz that the image of
\mathfrak{n}
in

is of the form
(x_{1}-a_{1},\dots,x_{n}-a_{n})
for some

, so
\mathfrak{n}

is of the form
\mathfrak{n}=(p,x_{1}-v_{1},\dots,x_{n}-v_{n})
for some
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in V
with
v_{i}=a_{i}+pV
, and
f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in pV
for

.

We aim to compute the dimension of
\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
as a
K
-vector space. To this end, there is a short exact sequence

0\to\frac{I}{\mathfrak{n}^{2}\cap I}\to\frac{\mathfrak{n}}{\mathfrak{n}^{2}}\to\frac{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{m}^{2}}\to 0,

so
R_{\mathfrak{m}}
is regular if and only if the
K
-vector space dimension of
{I}/({\mathfrak{n}^{2}}\cap I)
is equal to
{\dim(T_{\mathfrak{n}})-\dim(R_{\mathfrak{m}})}
. Since

is catenary, and each minimal prime of

has the same height, we have that
\dim(T_{\mathfrak{n}})-\dim(R_{\mathfrak{m}})=\mathrm{ht}(IT_{\mathfrak{n}})=h
for

.

Write
\tilde{x}_{i}=x_{i}-v_{i}
for

, so that
\mathfrak{n}=(p,\tilde{x}_{1},\dots,\tilde{x}_{n})
. Using Taylor’s formula, for each

, we get

\displaystyle f_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})}\frac{\partial^{\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}}\,f_{j}}{\partial x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\tilde{x}_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\tilde{x}_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}

\displaystyle\equiv f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\tilde{x}_{i}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{n}^{2}.

By Remark
2.4
(
6
) we then have

\delta(f_{j})\equiv\delta\left(f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\tilde{x}_{i}\right)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{n}.

Since
f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in pV\subseteq\mathfrak{n}
, we have

C_{p}\left(f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n}),\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\tilde{x}_{i}\right)\right)\in\mathfrak{n},

so

\delta(f_{j})\equiv\delta\left(f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\right)+\delta\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\tilde{x}_{i}\right)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{n}.

Likewise, since
\tilde{x}_{i}\in\mathfrak{n}
,
C_{p}(\{\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\tilde{x}_{i}\})\in\mathfrak{n}
, and hence

\delta(f_{j})\equiv\delta(f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n}))+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta\left(\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\tilde{x}_{i}\right)\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{n}.

By the product rule for
p
-derivations, we then have

\delta(f_{j})\equiv\delta(f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n}))+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\right)^{p}\delta(\tilde{x}_{i})\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{n}.

Note that, for
\alpha\in V
, by Remark
2.4
(
7
),
if we write

, we obtain that
\delta(f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n}))\equiv\alpha^{p}
in

.
Thus,

\alpha\equiv{\delta(f_{j})}^{1/p}-\sum_{i}\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n}){\delta(\tilde{x_{i}})}^{1/p}\ \mathrm{mod}\ \mathfrak{n}.

Using this equivalence to substitute in for
f_{j}(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})
in the first expression for
f_{j}
modulo
\mathfrak{n}^{2}
above, we get that the
K
-linear expression for
[f_{j}]\in\mathfrak{n}/\mathfrak{n}^{2}
in terms of the basis

[p],[\tilde{x_{1}}-p{\delta(\tilde{x_{1}})}^{1/p}],\dots,[\tilde{x_{n}}-p{\delta(\tilde{x_{n}})}^{1/p}]

[f_{j}]=\overline{\delta(f_{j})(\underline{v})}^{1/p}[p]+\overline{\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial{x_{1}}}(\underline{v})}[\tilde{x}_{1}-p{\delta(\tilde{x_{1}})}^{1/p}]+\cdots+\overline{\frac{\partial f_{j}}{\partial{x_{n}}}(\underline{v})}[\tilde{x}_{n}-p{\delta(\tilde{x_{n}})}^{1/p}],

where bars denote images modulo
\mathfrak{n}
.
Thus, the dimension of

as a

\begin{bmatrix}\overline{\delta(f_{1})(\underline{v})}^{1/p}&\overline{\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}(\underline{v})}&\dots&\overline{\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}(\underline{v})}\\
\overline{\delta(f_{2})(\underline{v})}^{1/p}&\overline{\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}(\underline{v})}&\dots&\overline{\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial x_{n}}(\underline{v})}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\

This is just the image of

in

, and thus the

considered in

Since the

-vector space dimension of

is less than or equal to the height with equality if and only if

is regular, the theorem is established in the case of a maximal ideal.

Now, the inequality on the rank of

for general prime ideals follows by a basic semicontinuity argument, since maximal ideals are dense in

. Likewise, the characterization of equality in terms of nonsingularity follows, since

is excellent, and so the singular locus is closed.
∎

4.3.
Theorem B

We now give the proof of Theorem B.

Theorem 4.13
.

Let
V
be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer
p
, and F-finite residue field. Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
a local ring with
p\in\mathfrak{m}
that is essentially of finite type over a complete
V
-algebra.

The local ring

is regular if and only if
\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(\mathbb{Z},\delta)}
is free of rank
{\dim(R)+\log_{p}[k:k^{p}]}
.

Proof.

Let
K=V/pV
, and set
a=\log_{p}[K:K^{p}]
and
b=\log_{p}[k:k^{p}]
. Write
R\cong(T/I)_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
T
is a polynomial ring over a power series ring over
V
, and
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal of
T
that contains
I
. We observe that
\widetilde{J}(\bm{f})
is a presentation matrix for
\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(\mathbb{Z},\delta)}
, with
\dim(T)+a
generators.

First, we claim that
\dim(T)+a=0pt(I_{\mathfrak{p}})+\dim(R)+b
. We use the formula
\log_{p}[\kappa(\mathfrak{q}):\kappa(\mathfrak{q})^{p}]-\log_{p}[\kappa(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}):\kappa(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime})^{p}]=0pt(\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{q})
for
\mathfrak{q}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}^{\prime}
in an F-finite ring of positive characteristic; cf.,
[
13
]
,
[
15
, Lemma 42.7]
. Applying this in
T
with the maximal ideal generated by the variables and the zero ideal, and then with
\mathfrak{p}
and the zero ideal, we have that

\dim(T)+a=\log_{p}[\mathrm{frac}(T):\mathrm{frac}(T)^{p}]=0pt(\mathfrak{p})+b.

Since
\dim(R)=0pt(\mathfrak{p})-0pt(I_{\mathfrak{p}})
, the claim holds.

Now, by the characterization of locally free modules via Fitting ideals (cf.
[
14
, Proposition D.8]
),
\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(\mathbb{Z},\delta)}
is locally free of rank
\dim(R)+b
if and only if

F_{\dim(R)+b}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(\mathbb{Z},\delta)})=R\qquad\text{and}\qquad F_{<\dim(R)+b}(\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(\mathbb{Z},\delta)})=0.

Using the equality above and the presentation by the mixed Jacobian matrix,
\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|(\mathbb{Z},\delta)}
is locally free of rank
\dim(R)+b
if and only if

I_{0pt(I_{\mathfrak{p}})}(\widetilde{J}(\bm{f}))=R\qquad\text{and}\qquad I_{>0pt(I_{\mathfrak{p}})}(\widetilde{J}(\bm{f}))=0.

Now, if
I_{0pt(I_{\mathfrak{p}})}(\widetilde{J}(\bm{f}))=R
, then
by Lemma 4.6,
\dim_{k}(\mathrm{im}(I_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}))=0pt(I_{\mathfrak{p}})
, so

can be generated by

elements; in this case we can replace
\bm{f}
by such a generating set and the latter condition above is automatic. Thus,
\widetilde{\Omega}_{R|\mathbb{Z}}
is locally free of rank
\dim(R)+b
if and only if
I_{0pt(I_{\mathfrak{p}})}(\widetilde{J}(\bm{f}))=R
, which happens if and only if
I_{0pt(I_{\mathfrak{p}})}(\widetilde{J}(\bm{f})_{k})=k
which, by Lemma 4.6 again, is equivalent to
\dim_{k}(\mathrm{image}(I_{\mathfrak{p}}\to\mathfrak{p}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}))=0pt(I_{\mathfrak{p}})
. But this just means that the image of
I
in
T_{\mathfrak{p}}
is minimally generated by a regular system of parameters, so this is equivalent to nonsingularity of
R
.
∎

\Gamma
-construction to mixed characteristic

The hypothesis of Theorem B in §

Chromatic number of randomly augmented graphs
Jan Geest, Anand Srivastav

Abstract
An extension of the Erdős-Renyi random graph model 𝒢 n , p \mathcal{G}_{n,p} is the model of perturbed graphs introduced by Bohman, Frieze and Martin (Bohman, Frieze, Martin 2003). This is a special case of the model of randomly augmented graphs studied in this paper. An augmented graph denoted by pert H , p \textnormal{pert}_{H,p} is the union of a deterministic host graph and a random graph 𝒢 n , p \mathcal{G}_{n,p} . Among the first problems in perturbed graphs has been the question how many random edges are needed to ensure Hamiltonicity of the graph. This question was answered in the paper by Bohman, Frieze and Martin. The host graph is often chosen to be a dense graph. In recent years several papers on combinatorial problems in perturbed graphs were published, e.g. on the emergence of powers of Hamiltonian cycles (Dudek, Reiher, Ruciński, Schacht 2020), some positional games played on perturbed graphs (Clemens, Hamann, Mogge, Parczyk, 2020) and the behavior of multiple invariants e.g. fixed clique size (Bohman, Frieze, Krivelevich, Martin, 2004). In this paper we study the chromatic number of randomly augmented graphs. We concentrate on a host graph H H with chromatic number o ⁡ ( n ) o(n) , augmented by a 𝒢 n , p \mathcal{G}_{n,p} with n − 1 3 + δ ≤ p ⁡ ( n ) ≤ 1 − δ n^{-\frac{1}{3}+\delta}\leq p(n)\leq 1-\delta for some δ ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \delta\in(0,1) . Our main result is an upper bound for the chromatic number: we show that asymptotically almost surely χ ⁡ ( pert H , p ) ≤ ( 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) ) ⋅ n ​ log ⁡ ( b ) 2 ​ ( log ⁡ ( n ) − log ⁡ ( χ ⁡ ( H ) ) ) \chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\leq(1+o(1))\cdot\frac{n\log(b)}{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))} where b = ( 1 − p ) − 1 b=(1-p)^{-1} . This result collapses to the famous theorem of Bollobás (1988), when H H is the empty host graph, thus our result can be regarded as a generalization of the latter. Our proof is not constructive. Further, we give a constructive coloring algorithm, when the chromatic number of the host graph is at most n log ⁡ ( n ) α , \frac{n}{\log(n)^{\alpha}}, α > 1 2 . \alpha>\frac{1}{2}.

Introduction

For a graph
G=(V,E)
the chromatic number
\chi\left(G\right)
is the minimum number
of colors needed to color the vertices of
G
such that no two adjacent vertices are of the same color.
Since the number of colors in this chapter is not bounded by a constant, we will use the first natural
numbers as colors, which is a standard procedure
[
Bol98
]
.
So for a
k\in\mathbb{N}
the function
\phi:V\rightarrow[k]
is called a
k
(vertex) coloring of
G.

\phi
is called a proper
k
-coloring of
G
if
\{v,w\}\in E\implies\phi(v)\neq\phi(w).

\displaystyle{C}\chi\left(G\right):=\min\left\{k:\exists\left(\phi:V\rightarrow[k]\right):\;\{v,w\}\in E\implies\phi(v)\neq\phi(w)\right\}.

We will call a proper vertex
k
-coloring
\phi:V\rightarrow[k]

just a coloring of
G,
if clear from the context.
Let
\phi:V\rightarrow[k]
be a proper coloring of
G.

Then, for each
j\in[k]

\phi^{-1}(j)
is called the
j
-color class of
G
with respect to
\phi.

By definition each color class is an independent set in
G.

For any function
p:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[0,1]
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
denote the random graph model
with vertex set
[n]:=\mathbb{N}_{\leq n},
iwhere
\mathbb{P}\left(\{v,w\}\in E(\mathcal{G}_{n,p})\right)=p,
independently
for each
\{v,w\}\in\binom{[n]}{2}
.
We further define
b:=b(n)=\frac{1}{1-p(n)}
.

Previous Work

For the theory of the chromatic number in
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
we refer to the pioneering papers of Erdős and
Rényi
[
ER59
]
, Bollobás
[
Bol88
]
, Shamir and Spencer

SS87
]
,

Łu91
]

[

]
,
as well as the books of Bollobás
[
Bol85
]
and
Frieze and Karoński
[

]
.

The fact that the chromatic number of
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
for large
p
,
i.e.
c\geq p=p(n)\geq n^{-\theta}
for some constants

\theta\in(0,1/3)
and
c\in(0,1),
is equal to

(1+o(1))\frac{\log(b)n}{2\log(n)}

\emph{a.a.s.}

was first proved by Bollobás in
[
Bol88
]
.
Łuczak
[
Łu91
]
proved that the same asymptotic bounds hold
for
\chi\left(\mathcal{G}_{n,p}\right)
, for all
p=p(n)>d/n
with some
d\in\mathbb{R}
.
In
[
SS87
]
Shamir and Spencer showed that the chromatic number of
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
for each
p

is concentrated around its expected value within a large deviation of essentially
\sqrt{n}
.
Recently, even a long open question about the non-concentration of the chromatic number of
\mathcal{G}_{n,1/2}
has
been answered by Heckel and Riordan
[
HR21
]
.

In extension of the random graph model
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
the interaction between random graphs and deterministic graphs
has been studied.
One way to analyze the interactions between a random graph and a deterministic graph is to analyze
the resilience of the random graph with respect to certain invariants,
asking what kind of graph can be added to the
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
without changing the asymptotics of
the invariant under consideration.
Alon and Sudakov
[
AS10
]
studied this question for the chromatic number of deterministic
graphs of bounded maximum degree and edge number.
The concept of the resilience of a graph invariant is not restricted to random graphs,
but can be applied to deterministic graphs as well.

Another way of analyzing the interaction between a deterministic graph and a random graph
is the model of perturbed or augmented graphs.
In this model a deterministic host graph
H
is augmented (or perturbed) by a
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
,
so the graph
H\cup\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
is studied.
Generally whether we speak of a "perturbed" or "augmented" graph depends on the probability
p
used for

\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
, and we may speak about perturbed graphs for smaller
p
and of augmented graphs for larger
p
.
This model was first introduced by Bohman et al. in
[
BFM03
]
considering the question of Hamiltonicity,
and was further studied for example by Dudek et al. in
[
DRRS20
]
and
Das et al. in
[
DMT19
]
.

1.2
Our Results

In this paper we study the chromatic number of a randomly augmented graph
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p},
where
H
is a deterministic
host graph with chromatic number
\textnormal{o}\left(n\right),
augmented by a
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
for

n^{-\frac{1}{3}+\delta}\leq p\leq(1-\delta)
for any fixed
\delta>0.

In
section 2
, we will show that for each
\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{n\log(b)}{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}\quad\emph{a.a.s.}

(N1)

for a constant
p.

This is done by estimating the expected value of the chromatic number of the
random graphs induced by the large color classes of an optimal coloring of
H
and
adding them up.
Additionally we estimate the number of vertices in small color classes to be negligible.
Using this upper bound on the expected value of the chromatic number and combining it
with McDiarmid’s bounded differences inequality we get our main result.

The challenge is to show (
N1
) for non-constant
p.

In fact we prove (
N1
) for
p=p(n)=n^{-\theta}

where
\theta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{3}\right)
arbitrary but fixed.
In this situation the expected chromatic number of
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
is only known to be smaller than

\frac{n\log(b)}{2\log(n)}
for
p(n)\geq n^{-\theta}
with

\theta\in\left(0,\frac{1}{3}\right).

Let
G^{\prime}
be a new subgraph of
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
of size
k<n.

G^{\prime}
is a
\mathcal{G}_{k,p(n)}
random graph.
If
k
is to small against
n,
we cannot apply the Bollowbás result to
G^{\prime},

because
G^{\prime}
is not a
\mathcal{G}_{k,p(k)}
random graph.
This is true in particular if
k^{-1/3}>p(n).

So, for color classes induced by small color classes of an optimal coloring of
H

we cannot use the result of Bollobás.
Thus we use a different approach, namely the well-known greedy color algorithm.
In the proof we will need that the color classes are of size at least

n^{3\theta}
on average.
To ensure this we restrict ourselves to host graphs with
\chi\left(H\right)\leq n^{1-3\theta}.

Again the proof will be finalized by C. McDiarmid’s bounded differences
inequality.

Since in both cases the bounded differences inequality was used the proofs
are non-constructive.
We further give constructive algorithms to construct
a proper coloring with the bound in (
N1
), but with host graphs
H
satisfying

\chi\left(H\right)\leq\frac{n}{\log(n)^{1/2}}
if
p\penalty\ \in\penalty\ (0,1)
is constant.
The construction relies on a slightly improved version of the coloring of
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}

proposed by Bollobás
[
Bol88
]
.
However in the case
p(n)=n^{-\theta}
we already assumed that
\chi\left(H\right)\leq n^{1-3\theta},

which suffices to construct a coloring with an upper bound as in (
N1
).

In
section 4
we discuss the results and pose a few open problems
not tackled in this paper.
For example we leave open the question about a general upper bound for
\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p(n)}\right)

where
p=p(n)\leq n^{-1/3}.

2
Bounding the chromatic number of
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}

We use a basic result for the chromatic number of the union of any two graphs.

Lemma 2.1

(Divide and Color)
.

Let
G:=(V,E_{G})
and
H:=(V,E_{H})
be graphs.
Further let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(V)
be a partition of
V
such that every
S\in\mathcal{C}
is an independent set in
G
.
Then

\displaystyle\chi\left(G\cup H\right)\leq\sum\limits_{S\in\mathcal{C}}\chi\left(H\left[S\right]\right).

If
G
is a complete
k
-partite graph and
\mathcal{C}
is a
k
-partition of the vertices of
G
as above, then

\displaystyle\chi\left(G\cup H\right)=\sum\limits_{S\in\mathcal{C}}\chi\left(H\left[S\right]\right).

The proof is kind of folklore. For readers convenience we state it briefly in
Appendix A
.
We will combine the above lemma with the following fact.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
H
be a deterministic graph and
p:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[0,1]
.
Let
S\subseteq V
be a color class of a proper coloring of
H
.
Then
H
does not contain edges with both endpoints in
S
.
Thus the only edges in
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}[S]
are random edges and
since the edges of
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
are chosen independently at random
we can identify
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}[S]
with
\mathcal{G}_{\left|S\right|,p}.

We further remind the reader of the following well-known facts:

Remark 2.3
.

Let
p=p(n)\in(0,1)
,
b=b(n)=\frac{1}{1-p(n)}
and
\log_{b}(n)=\frac{\log(n)}{\log(b)}
.

\displaystyle\text{If }p=p(n)\rightarrow 0\text{ then }\log(b)\sim p.

(1)

This can be shown by applying L’Hopital’s rule to the term
\frac{x}{\log\left((1-x)^{-1}\right)}
for
x\rightarrow 0.

2.1
The Case
p\in(0,1)
is constant

In this subsection we consider all
p
, where
p\in(0,1)

First, we bound the number of vertices of the host graph in small color classes.
Here the adjective small shall refer to color classes that are significantly smaller than the average color class,
while the adjective large will be used to describe color classes which are not small.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H=([n],E)
be a graph with
\chi\left(H\right)=\frac{n}{\beta(n)}
for some function
\beta:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\beta(n)\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty.

Furthermore let
\mathcal{C}
be the set of all color classes of an optimal coloring of
H
.
For
\varepsilon>0
set
g(n)=\beta(n)^{\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}
and define
\mathcal{L}^{g}=\penalty\ \left\{S\in\mathcal{C}|\left|S\right|\geq g(n)\right\}
and
\mathcal{S}^{g}=\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{g}
.
For sufficiently large
n=n(\varepsilon)
we have

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{g}}S\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\frac{\log(b)n}{2\log(\beta(n))}.

for every
b
where
b=\frac{1}{1-p}
for some constant
p\in(0,1).

Proof.

Since the sets
S\in\mathcal{S}^{g}
are pairwise disjoint and
(1+\varepsilon)^{-\frac{1}{2}}<1
,
we get the following estimates

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{g}}S\right|=

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{g}}\left|S\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\chi\left(H\right)g(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n}{\beta(n)}\cdot\beta(n)^{\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\cdot\beta(n)^{\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1}

(Note
\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1<0
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\frac{\log(b)n}{2\log(\beta(n))}.

The last inequality can be showed as follows.
Let
x:=\beta(n)
,
c:=\frac{\varepsilon\log(b)}{4}
and

a:=\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}-1
.
Then for sufficiently large
x,
we have
x^{a}\leq\frac{c}{\log(x)}
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
H=([n],E)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\right]\leq(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{n\log(b)}{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the set of all color classes of an optimal coloring of
H
and let
\varepsilon>0
.
Additionally we define for
g(n)=\beta(n)^{\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}}
the set of large

\mathcal{L}^{g}=\penalty\ \left\{S\in\mathcal{C}|\left|S\right|\geq g(n)\right\}
and the set of small color

\mathcal{S}^{g}=\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{L}^{g}
as in

Note that a set

is an independent set in
H
, because

is a color class of an optimal (proper) coloring of
H
.
So, the only edges in
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}[S]
are the edges of
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
.
We can thus identify
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}[S]
with
\mathcal{G}_{\left|S\right|,p}
according to
2.2
.
Furthermore, since

is constant the assumptions of

are
fulfilled for sufficiently large

.
Now

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\right]\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}[S]\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{L}^{g}}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}[S]\right)\right]+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{g}}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}[S]\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{L}^{g}}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\mathcal{G}_{\left|S\right|,p}\right)\right]+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{g}}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\mathcal{G}_{\left|S\right|,p}\right)\right]}_{\leq\left|S\right|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{L}^{g}}\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\mathcal{G}_{\left|S\right|,p}\right)\right]+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{g}}\left|S\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{L}^{g}}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\frac{\log(b)\left|S\right|}{2\log(\left|S\right|)}+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{g}}\left|S\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{L}^{g}}\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\frac{\log(b)\left|S\right|}{2\log(\left|S\right|)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\frac{n\log(b)}{2\log(\beta(n))}

(
2.4
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\frac{\log(b)}{2}\cdot\sum_{S\in\mathcal{L}^{g}}\frac{\left|S\right|}{\log(g(n))}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\frac{n\log(b)}{2\log(\beta(n))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\cdot\frac{\log(b)}{2\log(\beta(n))}\underbrace{\sum_{S\in\mathcal{L}^{g}}\left|S\right|}_{\leq n}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\frac{n\log(b)}{2\log(\beta(n))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{n\log(b)}{2\log(\beta(n))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{n\log(b)}{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}.

We invoke the bounded differences inequality of C. McDiarmid
[
McD89
]
:

Theorem 2.7

(McDiarmid’s inequality)
.

Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
be independent random variables with
X_{j}
taking values in some set
A_{j}
.
Suppose that some measurable function
f:\prod_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

satisfies

\displaystyle\left|f(x)-f(x^{\prime})\right|\leq c_{j}

whenever the vectors
x,x^{\prime}\in\prod_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}
differ only in the
j
-th component.
Let
Y
be the (real valued) random variable
Y:=f(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})
.
Then for any
t>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\left|Y-\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]\right|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(\frac{-2t^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}^{2}}\right).

Remark 2.8
.

In order to apply McDiarmid’s inequality to the chromatic number of
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}
, we set

X_{j}:=\{\{i,j\}\in E(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p})|i<j\}
.
Since the
X_{j}
form a partition of the edges of
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}
,
the random variable
\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)
depends solely on the values of the
X_{j}
.
We define

\displaystyle Y=f(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})=\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right).

Note that the
X_{j}
are mutually independent random variables.
Let
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})

Furthermore for each
j
, if
X
and
\hat{X}
only differ in the
j
-th component, we have

\displaystyle\left|f(X)-f(\hat{X})\right|\leq 1,

since one additional color will always be enough to color the vertex

, if necessary.
Thus McDiarmid’s inequality is applicable with
\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}^{2}=n
and we get the following
upper bound for the chromatic number of
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
H=([n],E)
be a deterministic graph with
\chi\left(H\right)=\frac{n}{\beta(n)}
for some function
\beta:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
\beta(n)\rightarrow\infty
for
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Let further be
p\in(0,1)
and

.
Then for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all

we have

\displaystyle\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\frac{n\log(b)}{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}\quad\emph{a.a.s.}

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\lambda=\frac{n\log(b)}{2\left(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right))\right)}
.
By
Theorem 2.6
and McDiarmid’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\geq(1+\varepsilon)\cdot\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)-\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\cdot\lambda\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\lambda\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left[\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\right]\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\cdot\lambda\right)

(
Theorem 2.6
)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\exp\left(-\frac{2}{n}\cdot\left(\frac{\varepsilon\lambda}{2}\right)^{2}\right)

(McDiarmid’s inequality)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\exp\left(-\frac{2}{n}\cdot\left(\frac{\varepsilon n\log(b)}{4(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\exp\left(-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\log(b)^{2}n}{8(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))^{2}}\right)=o(1).

2.3
Tightness of upper bounds for certain graphs

We proved an upper bound for the chromatic number of an augmented graph,
depending only on
p
and the chromatic number of the host graph.
A natural question is, of course, whether our bounds are tight.
Indeed, the next theorem shows, that there exist host graphs for which the asymptotics for
the chromatic number is equal to the asymptotics of the upper bound in
Theorem 2.9
.
We will prove this by considering the independence number of a certain class of host graphs.
These host graphs are characterized by a "low" number of "large" independent sets.
As an example one can consider the complete
\chi\left(H\right)
-partite graphs.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
H=([n],E)
be a deterministic graph with
\chi\left(H\right)=\frac{n}{\beta(n)}
for some function
\beta=\beta(n)
with
\beta(n)\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
Further let
k=\left\lceil\frac{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}{\log(b)}\right\rceil
and

n_{H,k}
the number of independent sets of size
k
in
H
.

If
n_{H,k}\in o\left(\left(\frac{\chi\left(H\right)}{n}\right)^{-k+1}\right)
,
then
\alpha(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p})\leq\frac{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}{\log(b)}

a.a.s.

Proof.

Let
X_{k}
be the random variable that counts the number of independent sets of size
k
in
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}
.
Now

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\alpha(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p})\geq k\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\left(X_{k}\geq 1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[X_{k}\right]

(Markov’s inequality)

\displaystyle=n_{H,k}(1-p)^{\binom{k}{2}}

\displaystyle=n_{H,k}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\log(b)}{2}k(k-1)\right)

(

)

\displaystyle\leq n_{H,k}\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{\log(b)}{2}\frac{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}{\log(b)}(k-1)\right)

\displaystyle=n_{H,k}\cdot\left(\frac{\chi\left(H\right)}{n}\right)^{-k+1}

\displaystyle=o(1),

with our assumption on
n_{H,k}
.
∎

4
Conclusion and Open Questions

In this paper we first proved that for certain combinations of host graphs
H
with bounded chromatic
number, and constant edge probabilities
p
or edge probabilities
p=p(n)\geq n^{-\frac{1}{3}+\delta}

for some
\delta>0
, the chromatic number of the augmented graph
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}

is
\emph{a.a.s.}
bounded from above by

\displaystyle\frac{n\log(b)}{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}

(
Theorem 2.9
and
Theorem 2.13
).
Furthermore it was shown that this upper bound is asymptotically tight for host graphs that
possess only few independent sets of a certain size (
Theorem 2.15
).

In addition the bound is asymptotically tight for host graphs
H
with finite chromatic number,
as in this case for each
\varepsilon>0.

\displaystyle\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\geq\chi\left(\mathcal{G}_{n,p}\right)\geq\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\frac{n\log(b)}{2\log(n)}\geq(1-\varepsilon)\frac{n\log(b)}{2(\log(n)-\log(\chi\left(H\right)))}.

However the upper bound cannot be tight for each host graph.
Consider for example a host graph chosen uniformly at random from all graphs with
n
vertices.
This is equivalent to considering
H
to be a
\mathcal{G}_{n,\frac{1}{2}}
random graph.
Thus
\chi\left(H\right)\penalty\ =\penalty\ (1\penalty\ +\penalty\ o\penalty\ (1))\penalty\ \frac{n\log(2)}{2\log(n)}

\emph{a.a.s.}
and

H
satisfies the conditions of
Theorem 2.9
.
So the upper bound given by
Theorem 2.9
yields
\emph{a.a.s.}

\displaystyle\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n\log(b)}{2\log(\log(n))}.

(11)

In this case
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}
can be considered as a random graph
\mathcal{G}_{n,\hat{p}}
where

\hat{p}=\frac{1}{2}(p+1),
thus with
\hat{b}=\frac{1}{1-\hat{b}}

we have
\emph{a.a.s.}

\displaystyle\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)=\chi\left(\mathcal{G}_{n,\hat{p}}\right)\leq(1+o(1))\frac{n\log\left(\hat{b}\right)}{2\log(n)}.

(12)

The upper bound on
\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)
in
11
is smaller than the upper
bound in
12
by a factor of
c\frac{\log(n)}{\log(\log(n))}.

We ask the following natural question:

Question 1:
Since now an asymptotically tight upper bound for the chromatic number of augmented
graphs is known, it would be interesting whether there is a better lower bound than the trivial bound

\displaystyle\chi\left(\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}\right)\geq\max(\chi\left(H\right),\chi\left(\mathcal{G}_{n,p}\right))=\max\left(\chi\left(H\right),\frac{n\log(b)}{2\log(n)}\right)?

The chromatic number of
\mathcal{G}_{n,p}
is known for a much wider range of
p
.
Furthermore no result was given for the case
\chi\left(H\right)=\Theta(n).

We may ask

Question 2:
Since an upper bound for the chromatic number of
\textnormal{pert}_{H,p}
for the cases

p(n)\geq n^{\frac{1}{3}+\delta}
and
\chi\left(H\right)=o(n)
has been established
in this paper, can it be extended to
include the case
p(n)=o(1)
and host graphs with
\chi\left(H\right)=\Theta(n)
?

The strategy of divide and conquer that was applied in this paper was shown to be useful
in our context.
However it appears to be rather ill suited to study most other classical graph invariants such as
the independence number.
Thus we may ask

Question 3:
Which graph invariants of randomly augmented graphs are well suited to be
studied by a divide and conquer strategy?

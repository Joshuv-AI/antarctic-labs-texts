The Dimensions of the Hulls of Conorm Codes from Algebraic Geometry Codes
Junmin An

Abstract
Chara et al. introduced conorm codes defined over algebraic geometry codes, but the hulls of conorm codes were not determined yet. In this paper, we study the dimension of the hull of conorm codes using the method introduced by Camps et al. For an algebraic geometry code 𝒞 := C ℒ ​ ( D , G ) \mathcal{C}:=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G) , we consider the divisor gcd ⁡ ( G , H ) \gcd(G,H) , where H H is the divisor satisfying C ℒ ​ ( D , G ) ⟂ = C ℒ ​ ( D , H ) . C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)^{\perp}=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,H). Given an extension F ′ / 𝔽 q t F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}} of an algebraic function field F / 𝔽 q F/\mathbb{F}_{q} , we assume that the divisor gcd ⁡ ( G , H ) \gcd(G,H) is non-special. If the degree of gcd ⁡ ( G , H ) \gcd(G,H) is greater than 2 g − 2 + t [ F ′ : F ] deg Diff ( F ′ / F ) 2g-2+{t\over[F^{\prime}:F]}\deg\text{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F) , then we have determined the exact dimension of the hull of the conorm of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . If not, we have determined the lower bound of the dimension of the hull of the conorm of 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . We provide some examples for the dimension of the hull of certain conorm codes of AG codes defined over a rational function field.

Introduction

Algebraic geometric codes were first introduced by Goppa using algebraic curves over finite fields
[
4
]
. As algebraic geometry codes are a generalization of Reed-Solomon codes, AG code inherit the characteristic of enabling the construction of a code whose minimum distance exceeds the designed distance. Furthermore, it has been observed that algebraic geometry codes can surpass the Gilbert-Varshamov bound
[
10
]
, and this aroused lots of interests in the field of algebraic geometry codes.

Assmus and Key
[
1
]
introduced the concept of the hull of a linear code, defined as the intersection of a code and its dual. The hull determines the complexity of the algorithm for checking permutation equivalence of two linear codes
[
5
]
,
[
8
]
or computing the automorphism group of linear codes
[
8
]
. The complexity of these problems plays an important role in cryptography. Notably, Massey
[
6
]
introduced the notion of LCD codes, which are linear codes characterized by an empty hull. S. Mesnager
[
7
]
proposed the method for constructing LCD codes from algebraic curves. Camps et el.
[
2
]
extended Mesnager’s idea to determine the hulls of algebraic geometry codes.

Chara et al.
[
3
]
devised conorm codes from algebraic geometry codes using the conorm map, which is a homomorphism from the divisor group of a function field
F
to the divisor group of a finite algebraic extension of
F
. They studied the parameters, specifically lengths dimensions, and minimum distances of conorm codes.

The hull of a linear code is an important property which characterizing the code, but the hulls of conorm codes have not been studied yet. So, we have studied the hull of conorm codes in our paper. We have used the method introduced by Camps et el.
[
2
]
to determine the dimension of the hull of conorm codes. Given an algebraic geometry code
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)
associated with divisors
D
and
G
of a function field
F
whose dual is
\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{L}}(D,H)
, if
\gcd(G,H)
is non-special, then we show how the dimension of the hull of the conorm of
\mathcal{C}
, defined over
F^{\prime}
, is related to the dimension of the hull of
\mathcal{C}
. Specifically, if the degree of
\gcd(G,H)
is greater than
2g-2+{t\over m}\deg\text{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F)
, where
\text{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F)
is the different divisor of
F^{\prime}
over
F
, then we have obtained the exact hull dimension of the conorm of
\mathcal{C}
. Otherwise, we have obtained the lower bound of the hull dimension of the conorm of
\mathcal{C}
. Since any univariate function field is a finite algebraic extension of a rational function field, we can represent some AG codes as conorm codes of rational AG codes. We present some examples of the hull dimension of conorm codes of rational AG codes for some well known function fields, namely elliptic, hyperelliptic, and hermitian function fields.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review some concepts of algebraic function fields and algebraic geometry codes. In Section 3 and 4, we present our results regarding the dimension of the hull of conorm codes for unramified extensions and separable ramified extensions, respectively. In Section 5 we provide some examples of the dimension of the hull of conorm codes of rational AG codes, and we conclude our paper in Section 6.

Preliminaries

Here we review some concepts of algebraic function fields and algebraic geometry codes from
[
9
]
.

Let
F
be a univariate function field over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The divisor group of
F
is a free abelian group generated by the places of
F
. Elements of the divisor group are represented as
D=\sum\limits_{P}n_{P}\cdot P
. For a given divisor
D
, we define the support of
D
by
\text{supp}D:=\{P|\alpha_{P}\neq 0\}
and the degree of
D
by
\deg D:=\sum\limits_{P}n_{P}\cdot\deg P
.

The
Riemann-Roch space
of a divisor
D
is defined by

\mathscr{L}(D):=\{x\in F|(x)\geq-D\}

where
(x)
is the principal divisor of
x
. As
\mathscr{L}(D)
is the vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the Riemann-Roch Theorem states that the dimension of
\mathscr{L}(D)
as a
\mathbb{F}_{q}-
vector space is given by

\dim\mathscr{L}(D)=\deg D+1-g+\dim\mathscr{L}(W-D)

(1)

where
W
is any canonical divisor of
F
and
g
is the genus of
F
. We denote the dimension of
\mathscr{L}(D)
by
\ell(D)
.

The index of specialty of the divisor
D
is given by
i(D):=\ell(D)-\deg D+g-1
. By the Riemann-Roch Theorem, we can easily determine that
i(A)=0
if and only if
\ell(W-D)=0
for any canonical divisor
W
of
F
. A divisor
D
with
i(D)=0
is called a non-special divisor; otherwise, it is referred to as a special divisor. The Following are some properties of the index of specialty:

(1)

If
\deg D>2g-2
, then
D
is a non-special divisor.

(2)

If
\deg D<g-1
, then
D
is a special divisor.

Let
D
be a given divisor. If
\deg D>2g-2
, then we have

\ell(D)=\deg D+1-g

by the property of the index of specialty and the Riemann-Roch Theorem. On the other hand, if
\deg D<0
, then
\ell(D)=0
. In the case where
0\leq\deg D\leq 2g-2
, the dimension of the Riemann-Roch space of
D
does not depend solely on the degree of
D
, but still, we can make some observations about
\ell(D)
with respect to
\deg D
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
9
]

For a divisor
D
with
0\leq\deg D\leq 2g-2
,

2(\ell(D)-1)\leq\deg D.

By the Clifford’s Theorem, if
\deg D=0
, then
\ell(D)
is either
0
or
1
. Indeed, if
\deg D=0
, then
\ell(D)=0
if and only if
D
is a principal divisor.

Now, we consider the conorm map on the divisor group. Let
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
be an algebraic extension of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For a place
P
of a function field
F
, the conorm of
P
is defined by

\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(P):=\sum\limits_{P^{\prime}|P}e(P^{\prime}|P)\cdot P^{\prime}

where
e(P^{\prime}|P)
is the ramification index of
P^{\prime}
over
P
. This definition of the conorm map naturally extends to a homomorphism from the divisor group of
F
to the divisor group of
F^{\prime}
. For a divisor
D=\sum\limits_{P}n_{P}\cdot P
of
F
, the conorm of
D
is defined by

\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(D):=\sum\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{P^{\prime}|P}n_{P}\cdot e(P^{\prime}|P)\cdot P^{\prime}.

The conorm map on the divisor group preserves the principal divisors. For a nonzero
x\in F
, let
(x)^{F}
be the principal divisor of
x
in
F
and
(x)^{F^{\prime}}
be the principal divisor of
x
in
F^{\prime}
. Then, we have

\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}((x)^{F})=(x)^{F^{\prime}}.

The degree of the conorm divisor is given by the immediate consequence of the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
9
]

Let
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
be a finite algebraic extension of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For a place
P
of
F
and a place
P^{\prime}
of
F^{\prime}
which is lying over
P
, let
e(P^{\prime}|P)
and
f(P^{\prime}|P)
be the ramification index and the relative degree of
P^{\prime}
over
P
respectively. Then

\sum\limits_{P^{\prime}|P}e(P^{\prime}|P)\cdot f(P^{\prime}|P)=[F^{\prime}:F].

By above equation, we can verify that for a divisor
D
of
F
,

\deg\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(D)={[F^{\prime}:F]\over t}\deg D.

For an algebraic extension
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the different divisor of
F^{\prime}
over
F
is defined by

\text{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F):=\sum\limits_{P}\sum\limits_{P^{\prime}|P}d(P^{\prime}|P)\cdot P^{\prime}

where
d(P^{\prime}|P)
is the different exponent of
P^{\prime}
over
P
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
9
]

Let
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
be an algebraic extension of algebraic function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For a place
P^{\prime}
of
F^{\prime}
lying over a place

d(P^{\prime}|P)\geq e(P^{\prime}|P)-1

and the equality holds if and only if

is not divisible by the characteristic

.

The genus of
F^{\prime}
is determined by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
9
]

Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an algebraic function field with genus
g
and
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
be a finite separable extension of

. Let
g^{\prime}
be the genus of
F^{\prime}
. Then, the following holds.

2g^{\prime}-2={[F^{\prime}:F]\over t}(2g-2)+\deg\textup{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F).

In case that

is an unramified separable extension of

, the degree of the different divisor of

over

Let

be rational places of an algebraic function field

. Let

and

be a divisor of

with support disjoint from

C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G):=\{x(P_{1}),\ldots,x(P_{n})|x\in\mathscr{L}(G)\}.

For an algebraic geometry code

, there always exists a divisor

such that
C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,H)
is the dual of

.

The greatest common divisor of two divisors

\gcd(D_{1},D_{2}):=\sum\limits_{P}\min(n_{P},m_{P})\cdot P

By the definition of the hull of a linear code,

The Following proposition states that equality holds under certain conditions.

.

For a given algebraic geometry code

(2)

Now we define the conorm of an AG code. Let

of F, let

Clearly the two divisors

are distinct.
If

3
The hull of conorm codes on unramified extension

In this section, we show how the dimension of the hull, or simply the hull dimension, of conorm codes is determined in the case that the extension
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
over
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is unramified and
t=1
, i.e.,
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. When considering the hulls of linear codes, LCD codes and self-dual codes are two extreme cases. That is, the hull of an LCD code is a zero vector space and the hull of a self-dual code is identical to the code itself. We consider these two cases separately. We use the notation
h(\mathcal{C})
to represent the hull dimension of a linear code
\mathcal{C}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F^{\prime}
be an unramified finite extension of an algebraic function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
m
with
(q,\,m)=1
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an algebraic geometry code defined on
F
. If
\mathcal{C}
is self-dual, then
\textup{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\mathcal{C})
is also self-dual.

Proof.

It immediately follows from (5).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
F^{\prime}
be an unramified finite separable extension of an algebraic function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
m
with
(q,\,m)=1
, and let
\mathcal{C}:=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)
be an algebraic geometry code defined over
F
with
2g-2<\deg G<n
. Assume that
H
is the divisor of
F
such that

C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)^{\perp}=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,H).

Let the divisor
\gcd(G,H)
is non-special. Then the dimension of the hull of
\mathcal{C^{\prime}}:=\textup{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\mathcal{C})
satisfies

h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})\geq m\cdot h(\mathcal{C}),

and if
2g-2<\deg(\gcd(G,H))
, then

h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})=m\cdot h(\mathcal{C}).

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime}
be the conorm of
G
and
H
respectively. Note that

\gcd(G^{\prime},\,H^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{P}\min(\nu_{P}(G),\,\nu_{P}(H))\cdot\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(P)=\gcd(G,\,H)^{\prime}

where
\gcd(G,\,H)^{\prime}
is the conorm of
\gcd(G,\,H)
.

By (1),

\ell(\gcd(G^{\prime},H^{\prime}))=\ell(\gcd(G,H)^{\prime})={m}\cdot\ell(\gcd(G,H))+\ell(W-\gcd(G,H)^{\prime}),

where
W
is any canonical divisor of
F^{\prime}
. So,

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\geq\ell(\gcd(G,H)^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq{m}\cdot\ell(\gcd(G,H))

\displaystyle={m}\cdot h(\mathcal{C}).

If
2g-2<\deg(\gcd(G,H))
, then,

2g^{\prime}-2={m}(2g-2)<{m}\cdot\deg(\gcd(G,H))=\deg(\gcd(G,H)^{\prime}).

where
g^{\prime}
is the genus of
F^{\prime}
. This implies that
\gcd(G,H)^{\prime}
is a non-special divisor, and so,

\ell(\gcd(G,H)^{\prime})={m}\cdot\ell(\gcd(G,H)).

Hence,

h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})={m}\cdot h(\mathcal{C}).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
F^{\prime}
be an unramified finite extension of an elliptic function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
m
with
(q,\,m)=1
, and let
\mathcal{C}:=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)
be an algebraic geometry code defined over
F
with
0<\deg G<n
. Assume that
H
is the divisor of
F
such that

C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)^{\perp}=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,H).

Let
\gcd(G,H)
be a non-special divisor. If
\mathcal{C}
is an LCD code, then
\textup{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\mathcal{C})
is also an LCD code.

Proof.

If
\gcd(G,H)^{\prime}
is non-special, then it is trivial by the previous theorem. So we assume that
\gcd(G,H)^{\prime}
is special. By Theorem 1,

\ell(\gcd(G,H)^{\prime})\leq 1+{1\over 2}\cdot\deg(\gcd(G,H))=1.

Since
\gcd(G,H)^{\prime}
is special,
\ell(\gcd(G,H)^{\prime})
must be
1
. This implies that
\gcd(G,H)^{\prime}
is a principal divisor, as stated in Corollary 1.4.12. of
[
9
]
. By the property of the conorm map that preserves principal divisors,
\gcd(G,H)
is also a principal divisor. Since this is a contradiction,
\gcd(G,H)^{\prime}
must be a non-special divisor.

Remark 3.4
.

For an algebraic function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, let
F^{\prime}=F\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
with
(q,t)=1
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be an algebraic geometry code defined over
F
. Then

h(\textup{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\mathcal{C}))=h(\mathcal{C}).

Indeed, the generator matrix
G^{\prime}
of
\textup{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\mathcal{C}))
is given by

G^{\prime}=G\otimes[\underbrace{1,1,\dotsc,1}_{t}]

Proof.

Since
F^{\prime}
is a constant extension of
F
,
F^{\prime}/F
is an unramified extension such that
\ell(\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(D))=\ell(D)
and
\deg\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(D)=\deg D
for any divisor
D\in\text{Div}(F)
.

Note that any basis of
\mathscr{L}(G)
is contained in some basis of
\mathscr{L}(\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(G))
. This implies that any basis of
\mathscr{L}(G)
is also basis of
\mathscr{L}(\text{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(G))
. Given a place
P
of
F
, let
P_{i}
be any extension of
P
. Then,

v_{P}(x)=v_{P_{i}}(x)

for all
x\in F
since
F^{\prime}/F
is unramified. So we conclude that

G^{\prime}=G\otimes[\underbrace{1,1,\dotsc,1}_{t}].

Then, by
[
Li
]
, the hull dimension of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\dim(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})-\text{rank}(G^{\prime}(G^{\prime})^{T})

\displaystyle=\dim(\mathcal{C})-\text{rank}(t\cdot GG^{T})

\displaystyle=h(\mathcal{C}).

4
The hull of conorm codes on ramified extension

In this section, we assume that the extension
F/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
over an algebraic function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is separable.

We say that the extension
F/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
over a function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is ramified if there is a place
P
of
F
which is ramified in
F^{\prime}/F
. There are two types of a ramified extension: we say that a ramified place
P
is tamely ramified if the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
does not divide the ramification index of any extension of
P
. Otherwise, we say that the place
P
is wildly ramified. If there is no wildly ramified place, then we say that
F^{\prime}
is a tamely ramified extension of
F
.

If
F^{\prime}/F
is finite, separable extension, then the number of ramified places is finite.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
be a finite separable extension of an algebraic function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
m
. For an algebraic geometry code
\mathcal{C}:=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)
defined over
F
with
2g-2<\deg G<n
, assume that
H
is the divisor of
F
such that

C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)^{\perp}=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,H).

Let the divisor
\gcd(G,H)
be non-special. If the extension
F^{\prime}/F
and the code
\mathcal{C}
satisfies (3) and (5), then the hull of
\mathcal{C^{\prime}}:=\textup{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\mathcal{C})
satisfies,

h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})\geq{m\over t}\cdot h(\mathcal{C})-{1\over 2}\deg\textup{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F),

and if

\deg(\gcd(G,H))>2g-2+{t\over m}\deg\textup{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F),

then,

h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})={m\over t}\cdot h(\mathcal{C})-{1\over 2}\deg\textup{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F).

Proof.

By Theorem 4, the genus
g^{\prime}
of
F^{\prime}
is given by

2g^{\prime}-2={m\over t}(2g-2)+\deg\textup{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F).

So, the hull dimension of
\mathcal{C^{\prime}}
satisfies

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\geq\ell(\gcd(G,H)^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\deg(\gcd(G,H)^{\prime})+1-g^{\prime}

\displaystyle={m\over t}\cdot\ell(\gcd(G,H))-{1\over 2}\deg\textup{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F)

\displaystyle={m\over t}\cdot h(\mathcal{C})-{1\over 2}\deg\textup{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F).

If

\deg(\gcd(G,H))>2g-2+{t\over m}\deg\textup{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F),

then,

\displaystyle\deg(\gcd(G,H)^{\prime})

\displaystyle={m\over t}\cdot\deg(\gcd(G,H))

\displaystyle>{m\over t}(2g-2)+\deg\textup{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F)

\displaystyle=2g^{\prime}-2,

which implies that
\gcd(G,H)^{\prime}
is non-special. So, the assertion holds.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
be a finite Galois tame extension of an algebraic function field

of degree
m
. Let
P_{1},\,P_{2},\,\ldots,\,P_{n}
be all the ramified places in

, assume that

is the divisor of

such that

C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)^{\perp}=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,H).

Let the divisor
\gcd(G,H)
be non-special. If the extension
F^{\prime}/F
and the code
\mathcal{C}
satisfies (3) and (5), then the hull of

h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})\geq{m\over t}\cdot h(\mathcal{C})-{1\over 2t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(m-m_{P_{i}}f_{P_{i}})\cdot\deg P_{i},

and if

\deg(\gcd(G,H))>2g-2+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(1-{m_{P_{i}}f_{P_{i}}\over m})\cdot\deg P_{i},

is the relative degree of
P_{i}
.

Proof.

By Theorem 3,

\displaystyle\deg(\text{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F))

\displaystyle=\deg\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{P^{\prime}|P_{i}}(e(P_{i})-1)\cdot\deg P^{\prime}\right)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(\deg(\textup{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(P_{i}))-\sum\limits_{P^{\prime}|P_{i}}\deg P^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left({m\over t}\deg P_{i}-{1\over t}\sum_{P^{\prime}|P_{i}}f(P_{i})\deg P_{i}\right)={1\over t}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(m-m_{P_{i}}f_{P_{i}})\cdot\deg P_{i}.

So, the statements follows immediately from Theorem 4.1.

Corollary 4.3
.

Use the same notations and condition of Corollary 4.2. Let
R
be the divisor defined as
R=P_{1}+\cdots P_{n}
. If all
P_{1},\,P_{2},\,\ldots,\,P_{n}
are totally ramified, then the dimension of the hull of
\mathcal{C^{\prime}}:=\textup{Con}_{F^{\prime}/F}(\mathcal{C})
satisfies,

h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})\geq{m\over t}\cdot h(\mathcal{C})-{(m-1)\over 2t}\cdot\deg R,

and if

\deg(\gcd(G,H))>2g-2+{(m-1)\over m}\cdot\deg R,

then,

h(\mathcal{C^{\prime}})={m\over t}\cdot h(\mathcal{C})-{(m-1)\over 2t}\cdot\deg R.

Proof.

Since all
P_{1},\,P_{2},\,\ldots,\,P_{n}
are totally ramified,
r_{i}=f_{P_{i}}=1
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. So,

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(1-{m_{P_{i}}f_{P_{i}}\over m})\cdot\deg P_{i}=(1-{1\over m})\cdot\deg R.

Then, the assertion follows from Corollary 4.2.

5
Conorm codes of rational AG codes

A rational AG code is an algebraic geometry code defined on a rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
. For any algebraic function field
F
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, as
F
is an algebraic extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
, the conorm code of a rational AG code is regarded as an AG code over
F
. We determine the hull dimensions of the conorm codes of rational AG codes on specific types of function fields.

Given a rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
, let
P_{\alpha}
be the zero of
x-\alpha
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
and
P_{\infty}
be the infinite place of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
.
For an integer
n\geq 2
that divides
q-1
, let
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a primitive
n
-th root of unity. We consider a two-point rational AG code defined as

\mathcal{C}_{ab}=\mathcal{C}_{\mathscr{L}}(D,aP_{0}+bP_{\infty})
, where
D=P_{1}+P_{\zeta}+\cdots+P_{\zeta^{n-1}}
and
a,\,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
0\leq a+b\leq n-2
and
0\leq b-a\leq n
. The dual of
\mathcal{C}_{ab}
is given by

\mathcal{C}_{ab}^{\perp}=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,-(a+1)P_{0}+(n-b-1)P_{\infty})

which is proved in
[
9
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

The dimension of the hull of
\mathcal{C}_{ab}
is given by

h(\mathcal{C}_{ab})=\begin{cases}n-a-b-1,&\mbox{if }a\geq 0\mbox{ and }b\geq{n-1\over 2}\\
b-a,&\mbox{if }a\geq 0\mbox{ and }b<{n-1\over 2}\\
n+a-b,&\mbox{if }a<0\mbox{ and }b\geq{n-1\over 2}\\
a+b+1,&\mbox{if }a<0\mbox{ and }b<{n-1\over 2}.\\
\end{cases}

Proof.

Assume
a\geq 0
and
b\geq{n-1\over 2}
. Let
G=aP_{0}+bP_{\infty}
and
H=-(a+1)P_{0}+(n-b-1)P_{\infty}
. Since
G\geq H
,
A:=\gcd(G,H)=H
. So

\displaystyle\deg(A)=\deg(H)

\displaystyle=n-a-b-2

\displaystyle\geq 0>-2=g_{0}-2,

where
g_{0}=0
is the genus of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
. Since this implies that
A
is non-special,

\displaystyle h(\mathcal{C}_{ab})

\displaystyle=\deg(A)+1-g_{0}

\displaystyle=n-a-b-1.

For the remaining three cases, we can easily check that the divisor
\gcd(G,H)
is non-special. So
h(\mathcal{C}_{ab})
is immediately given by (1).

For the following three examples, we assume that (3) and (5) always holds for the algebraic function field
F
given in examples and the code
\mathcal{C}_{ab}
defined above.

Example 5.2

(Elliptic function field)
.

Let
F
be an elliptic function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. If
1<b-a<n-1
, and
0<a+b<n-2
then, we show

h(\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{4}(x)}(\mathcal{C}_{ab}))=2h(\mathcal{C}_{ab})-2.

By Proposition 6.1.2 of
[
9
]
, there exists an equation of the form
y^{2}=f(x)
or
y^{2}+y=f(x)
where
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
, such that
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)
. So
[F:\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)]=2
. Since

\deg\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(A)=2\deg A,

it is sufficient to show that the degree of
A
is positive to verify that
\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(A)
is non-special.

Suppose that
a\geq 0
and
b\geq{n-1\over 2}
. Since
a+b<n-2
by assumption,
\deg A>0
. For other three cases of
a
and
b
, we can simply check that the degree of
A
is positive. Therefore,

\displaystyle h(\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(\mathcal{C}_{ab}))

\displaystyle=\ell(\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(A))

\displaystyle=\deg\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(A)+1-g

\displaystyle=2\deg A

\displaystyle=2(h(\mathcal{C}_{ab})-1).

Example 5.3

(Hyperelliptic function field)
.

Let
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,y)
be a hyperelliptic function field defined by an equation

y^{2}+h(x)y=f(x),

where
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is a square-free polynomial of degree
m>4
and
\deg h=\left\lceil{m\over 2}\right\rceil+1
. If
a
and
b

\begin{cases}n-\left\lceil{m\over 2}\right\rceil>a+b,&\mbox{if }a\geq 0\mbox{ and }b\geq{n-1\over 2},\\
b-a>\left\lceil{m\over 2}\right\rceil-1,&\mbox{if }a\geq 0\mbox{ and }b<{n-1\over 2},\\
n-\left\lceil{m\over 2}\right\rceil+1>b-a,&\mbox{if }a<0\mbox{ and }b\geq{n-1\over 2},\\
a+b>\left\lceil{m\over 2}\right\rceil-2,&\mbox{if }a<0\mbox{ and }b<{n-1\over 2},\\
\end{cases}

h(\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(\mathcal{C}_{ab}))=2h(\mathcal{C}_{ab})-\left\lceil{m\over 2}\right\rceil.

By the definition of hyperelliptic curve, genus of
F
is given by
m=2g+1
or

g=\lceil{m\over 2}\rceil-1

a
and
b

\deg\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(A)=2\deg A>2\lceil{m\over 2}\rceil-4=2g-2.

For the remaining three conditions, we can check similarly. Since
\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(A)
is non-special,

\displaystyle h(\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(\mathcal{C}_{ab}))=2h(\mathcal{C}_{ab})-\left\lceil{m\over 2}\right\rceil.

(Hermitian function field)

Let

If

h(\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(\mathcal{C}_{ab}))=ph(\mathcal{C}_{ab})-{p(p+1)\over 2}+1.

Since

a
and
b
which satisfying the first condition, we have

\deg\textup{Con}_{F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)}(A)=p\deg A>p(p-1)-2=2g-2.

Obviously, (6) holds for

6
Conclusion

In this paper, we have determined the hull dimensions of conorm codes of algebraic geometry codes when the extension
F^{\prime}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
over
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is separable, either unramified or ramified. Consequently, we have obtained the exact hull dimensions of the conorm codes of algebraic geometry codes
\mathcal{C}=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,H)^{\perp}
in the case where
\deg\gcd(G,H)>2g-2+{t\over[F^{\prime}:F]}\deg\text{Diff}(F^{\prime}/F)
. Considering unramified extensions, we have showed that the conorm of an LCD code
\mathcal{C}=C_{\mathscr{L}}(D,G)
such that
\gcd(G,H)
is non-special is also an LCD code if the code
\mathcal{C}
is defined over an elliptic function field. For an AG code defined over a non-elliptic function field that is LCD, studying the hull of its conorm may be the subject for future work of this paper.

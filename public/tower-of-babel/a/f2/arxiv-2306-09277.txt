Robust universal relations in neutron star asteroseismology
Deepak Kumar, Tuhin Malik, Hiranmaya Mishra, Constança Providência

Abstract
The non-radial oscillations of the neutron stars (NSs) have been suggested as an useful tool to probe the composition of neutron star matter (NSM). With this scope in mind, we consider a large number of equations of states (EOSs) that are consistent with nuclear matter properties and pure neutron matter EOS based on a chiral effective field theory (chEFT) calculation for the low densities and perturbative QCD EOS at very high densities. This ensemble of EOSs is also consistent with astronomical observations, gravitational waves in GW170817, mass and radius measurements from Neutron star Interior Composition ExploreR (NICER). We analyze the robustness of known universal relations (URs) among the quadrupolar f f mode frequencies, masses and radii with such a large number of EOSs and we find a new UR that results from a strong correlation between the f f mode frequencies and the radii of NSs. Such a correlation is very useful in accurately determining the radius from a measurement of f f mode frequencies in the near future. We also show that the quadrupolar f f mode frequencies of NS of masses 2.0 M ⊙ and above lie in the range ∼ \sim 2-3 kHz in this ensemble of physically realistic EOSs. A NS of mass 2M ⊙ with a low f f mode frequency may indicate the existence of non-nucleonic degrees of freedom.



Introduction.

The neutron star observations in the multi-messenger astronomy have piqued a lot of interest in the field of nuclear astrophysics and strong interaction physics. The recent radio, x-rays and gravitational waves observations in the context of neutron stars have provided interesting insights into the properties of matter at high density. The core of such compact objects is believed to contain matter at few times nuclear saturation density,
\rho_{0}
(
\rho_{0}\approx
0.16 fm
-3

;

On the theoretical side, no controlled reliable calculations are there that can be applicable to matter densities relevant for the neutron star cores. The lattice quantum chromodynamics simulations are challenging at these densities due to sign problem in Monte-Carlo simulations. On the other hand, the analytical calculations like chiral effective field theory is valid only at low densities while perturbative quantum chromodynamics is reliable at extremely high densities. In recent approaches, the equation of states between these two limits have been explored by connecting these limiting cases using a piecewise polytropic interpolation, speed of sound interpolation or spectral interpolation

;
Kurkela et al. 2014
;

;

The neutron star properties such as mass, radius and quadrupole deformation of merging neutron stars can constrain the uncertainty in equation of state. The discovery of massive neutron star with masses of the order of
2M_{\odot}
requires the equation of state to be stiff. However, the fact that non-nucleonic degrees of freedom soften the equation of state at high density, puts a constraint on the equation of state at the intermediate densities. The observations of gravitational waves from binary neutron star inspiral by Advanced LIGO and Advanced Virgo gravitational waves observatories have opened a new window in the field of multi-messenger astronomy and nuclear physics. The inspiral phase of neutron star-neutron star merger leads to tidal deformation (
\Lambda
), which is strongly sensitive to the compactness. Since
\Lambda
is related to the equation of state of the neutron star matter, this measurement acts as another constraint on the equation of state. On the other hand, recovering the nuclear matter properties from the equation of state of
\beta

;
Imam et al. 2022
;
Mondal and Gulminelli 2022
;
Essick et al. 2021

In the context of gravitational waves, the non-radial oscillations of neutron star are particularly interesting as they can carry information of the internal composition of the stellar matter. These oscillations in the presence of perturbations (electromagnetic or gravitational) can emit gravitational waves at the characteristic frequencies of its quasi-normal mode. The frequencies of quasi-normal mode depend on the internal structure of neutron star and it may be another probe to get an insight regarding the composition of neutron star matter also known as asteroseismology. Different quasi-normal modes are distinguished by the restoring forces that act on the fluid element when it gets displaced from its equilibrium position. The important fluid modes related to gravitational waves emission include fundamental
(f)
modes, pressure
(p)
modes and gravity
(g)
modes driven by the pressure and buoyancy respectively. The frequency of
p

g

f

f

Pratten et al. 2020

;

;
Sotani and Kumar 2021

In this letter we propose two major points of interest. Firstly we estimate, within the Cowling approximation

;
Yoshida and Lee 2002

We consider here a large number of equation of states and confirm that a known UR is almost insensitive to the equation of states, while a second one depends slightly on the composition of the equation of states, i.e. the presence or not of non-nucleonic degree of freedom, and, finally, we propose a new UR.

Pulsating equations -

To estimate the specific oscillation frequency of neutron stars, let us discuss the non-radial oscillation of a spherically symmetric neutron star characterized by the background space-time metric where the line element is given by

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-e^{2\Phi}dt^{2}+e^{2\Lambda}dr^{2}+r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right).

(2)

\displaystyle\xi^{i}=\left(e^{-\Lambda}W,~-V\partial_{\theta},~-V\sin^{-2}\theta\partial_{\phi}\right)r^{-2}Y_{lm}

(3)

Summary and conclusion -

The quasi-normal modes are related with the viscous properties of matter. In the future, precise measurements of them can put constraints on the equation of state of dense matter. We have studied the
f

Pratten et al. 2020

\beta-

Malik et al. 2022
based on the relativistic mean field theory, constrained by existing observational, theoretical and experimental data through Bayesian analysis. We have also generated an ensemble of equation of states using a nucleonic
\beta-

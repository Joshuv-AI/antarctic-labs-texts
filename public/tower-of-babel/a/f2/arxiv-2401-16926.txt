Coordination Coding with Causal Encoder for Vector-valued Witsenhausen Counterexample
Mengyuan Zhao 1 , Maël Le Treust 2 and Tobias J. Oechtering 1

Abstract
We investigate the Witsenhausen counterexample in a continuous vector-valued context with a causal encoder and noncausal decoder. Our main result is the optimal single-letter condition that characterizes the set of achievable Witsenhausen power costs and estimation costs, leveraging a modified weak typicality approach. In particular, we accommodate our power analysis to the causal encoder constraint, and provide an improved distortion error analysis for the challenging estimation of the interim state. Interestingly, the idea of dual role of control is explicitly captured by the two auxiliary random variables.

II
System Model

Our vector-valued Witsenhausen setup consists of
causal
source states and channel noises that are drawn independently according to the i.i.d. Gaussian distributions
X_{0}^{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,Q\mathbb{I})
and
Z^{n}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,N\mathbb{I})

X_{1}
the memoryless interim state and
Y_{1}
and output of the memoryless additive channel, generated by

\displaystyle X_{1}=X_{0}+U_{1}

\displaystyle\text{with }X_{0}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,Q),

(1)

\displaystyle Y_{1}=X_{1}+Z_{1}=X_{0}+U_{1}+Z_{1}

\displaystyle\text{with }Z_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,N).

(2)

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}}=\mathcal{N}(0,Q)
the generative Gaussian probability distribution of the state, and by
\mathcal{P}_{X_{1}Y_{1}|X_{0}U_{1}}
the channel probability distribution according to (
1
) and (
2
). Work
[
26
]
considered noncausal encoder paired with causal decoder. In this work, we consider a setup with flipped causality:

Definition II.1
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a “control design” with causal encoder and noncausal decoder is a tuple of stochastic functions
c=(\{f_{t}\}_{t=1}^{n},g)
defined by

f_{t}:\mathcal{X}_{0}^{t}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{1},\ t\in\{1,...,n\},\quad g:\mathcal{Y}_{1}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{2}^{n},

(3)

which induces a distribution over sequences of symbols:

\prod_{t=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{X_{0,t}}\prod_{t=1}^{n}f_{U_{1,t}|X_{0}^{t}}\prod_{t=1}^{n}\mathcal{P}_{X_{1,t},Y_{1,t}|X_{0,t},U_{1,t}}g_{U_{2}^{n}|Y_{1}^{n}}.

(4)

We denote by
\mathcal{C}_{e}(n)
the set of control designs with causal encoder and non-causal decoder.

Definition II.2
.

We define the two long-run cost functions
c_{P}(u_{1}^{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}(u_{1,t})^{2}
and
c_{S}(x_{1}^{n},u_{2}^{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{t=1}^{n}(x_{1,t}-u_{2,t})^{2}
.
The pair of costs
(P,S)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is achievable if for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\bar{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq\bar{n}
, there exists a control design
c\in\mathcal{C}_{e}(n)
such that

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big|P-c_{P}(U_{1}^{n})\big|+\big|S-c_{S}(X_{1}^{n},U_{2}^{n})\big|\Big]\leq\varepsilon.

(5)

Remark 1
.

Equation (
5
) is a stronger requirement than

\left|P-\mathbb{E}\left[c_{P}(U_{1}^{n})\right]\right|+\left|S-\mathbb{E}\left[c_{S}(X_{1}^{n},U_{2}^{n})\right]\right|\leq\varepsilon,

(6)

which was considered in
[
26
]
. Here, both notions lead to the same expression for the single-letter solution.

Theorem II.3

(Main result)
.

The pair of Witsenhausen costs
(P,S)
is achievable if and only if there exists a joint distribution over the random variables
(X_{0},W_{1},W_{2},U_{1},X_{1},Y_{1},U_{2})
that decomposes according to

\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}}\mathcal{P}_{W_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{W_{2}|X_{0},W_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{U_{1}|X_{0},W_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{X_{1},Y_{1}|X_{0},U_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{U_{2}|W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1}},

(7)

such that

\displaystyle I(W_{1},W_{2};Y_{1})-I(W_{2};X_{0}|W_{1})>0,

(8)

\displaystyle P=\mathbb{E}\left[U_{1}^{2}\right],\quad\quad S=\mathbb{E}\left[(X_{1}-U_{2})^{2}\right],

(9)

where
\mathcal{P}_{X_{0}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{X_{1},Y_{1}|X_{0},U_{1}}
are given Gaussian distributions, and
W_{1},W_{2}
are two auxiliary random variables.

Remark 2
.

The probability distribution (
7
) satisfies:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &X_{0}\text{ is independent of }W_{1},\\
&U_{1}-\!\!\!\!\minuso\!\!\!\!-(X_{0},W_{1})-\!\!\!\!\minuso\!\!\!\!-W_{2},\\
&(X_{1},Y_{1})-\!\!\!\!\minuso\!\!\!\!-(X_{0},U_{1})-\!\!\!\!\minuso\!\!\!\!-(W_{1},W_{2}),\\
&U_{2}-\!\!\!\!\minuso\!\!\!\!-(W_{1},W_{2},Y_{1})-\!\!\!\!\minuso\!\!\!\!-(X_{0},U_{1},X_{1}).\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

The first two properties are derived as consequences of causal encoding, where the achievability proof requires the Shannon strategy
[
29
]
for generating new codewords to the channel. The third Markov chain is due to the memoryless property of the channel and the processing order. The last Markov chain comes from the non-causal decoding and the symbol-wise reconstruction.

Remark 3
.

The information constraint (
8
) reformulates as

I(W_{1},W_{2};X_{0})<I(W_{1},W_{2};Y_{1}).

The LHS corresponds to the lossy source coding involved in the quantization of
X_{0}
using
(W_{1},W_{2})
. The RHS stands for the channel coding rate of outputting
Y_{1}
given the input pair
(W_{1},W_{2})
. In particular,
W_{1}
assists in enabling reliable communication, while
W_{2}
is used to coordinate with the state.

II.3

From the Markov inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left((X_{0}^{n},W_{1}^{n},W_{2}^{n})\notin\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}^{(n)}\right)\leq\frac{\delta_{n}}{\sqrt{\delta_{n}}}=\sqrt{\delta_{n}}.

∎

Lemma
III.1
elaborates that the certainty of an outcome being jointly weakly typical AND satisfying the distortion cost constraint is guaranteed, as per the AEP, if it is generated i.i.d.

Due to the causal feature, the encoder performs

The decoder first returns

Forward transmission of the decoder:
Upon receiving all sequences for all first
(B-1)

Witsenhausen costs analysis:

15

\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[U_{1,t}^{2}\right]\leq nP,

\mathbb{E}\left[|c_{P}(U_{1,b}^{n})-P|\right]<\varepsilon,\quad b\in[1:B]

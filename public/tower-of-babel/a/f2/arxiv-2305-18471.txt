Convergence of AdaGrad for Non-convex Objectives: Simple Proofs and Relaxed Assumptions Corresponding Author
Bohan Wang, Huishuai Zhang, Zhi-Ming Ma, Wei Chen

Abstract
We provide a simple convergence proof for AdaGrad optimizing non-convex objectives under only affine noise variance and bounded smoothness assumptions. The proof is essentially based on a novel auxiliary function ξ \xi that helps eliminate the complexity of handling the correlation between the numerator and denominator of AdaGrad’s update. Leveraging simple proofs, we are able to obtain tighter results than existing results ( Faw et al. 2022 ) and extend the analysis to several new and important cases. Specifically, for the over-parameterized regime, we show that AdaGrad needs only 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ε 2 ) \mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}) iterations to ensure the gradient norm smaller than ε \varepsilon , which matches the rate of SGD and significantly tighter than existing rates 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ε 4 ) \mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{4}}) for AdaGrad. We then discard the bounded smoothness assumption, and consider a more realistic assumption on smoothness called ( L 0 , L 1 ) (L_{0},L_{1}) -smooth condition, which allows local smoothness to grow with the gradient norm. Again based on the auxiliary function ξ \xi , we prove that AdaGrad succeeds in converging under ( L 0 , L 1 ) (L_{0},L_{1}) -smooth condition as long as the learning rate is lower than a threshold. Interestingly, we further show that the requirement on learning rate under the ( L 0 , L 1 ) (L_{0},L_{1}) -smooth condition is necessary via proof by contradiction, in contrast with the case of uniform smoothness conditions where convergence is guaranteed regardless of learning rate choices. Together, our analyses broaden the understanding of AdaGrad, and demonstrate the power of the new auxiliary function in the investigations of AdaGrad.

Introduction

Adaptive optimizers have been a great success in deep learning. Compared to stochastic gradient descent (SGD), adaptive optimizers use the gradient information of iterations to dynamically adjust the learning rate, which is observed to converge much faster than SGD in a wide range of deep learning tasks
(
Vaswani et al. 2017
;
Dosovitskiy et al. 2020
;
Yun et al. 2019
)
. Such a superiority has attracted numerous researchers to analyze the behavior of adaptive optimizers.

AdaGrad
(
Duchi et al. 2011
)
is among the earliest adaptive optimizers and enjoys favorable convergence rate for online convex optimization. Specifically, the design of AdaGrad is quite simple: it tracks the gradient magnitudes of the past iterations and use its reciprocal to scale the current gradient. The pseudo-codes of the norm version of AdaGrad (i.e., AdaGrad-Norm) and AdaGrad are presented in Algorithm
1
and Algorithm
2
, respectively.

Despite the popularity and the simplicity of AdaGrad, its theoretical analysis is not satisfactory when optimizing non-convex objectives. Specifically, until recently,
Ward et al. 2020
analyze the convergence of AdaGrad-Norm and achieve
\mathcal{O}(\log T/\sqrt{T})
rate. However, Their result is based on the assumption that the stochastic gradient
g_{t}
is uniformly bounded across the iterations, which does not hold even for quadratic functions, let alone deep neural networks. In comparison, the analysis of SGD does not require such an assumption.

A very recent exception
(
Faw et al. 2022
)
relaxes the assumptions and proves that AdaGrad-Norm converges by only assuming uniformly bounded smoothness (c.f. our Assumption
1
) and affine noise variance (c.f. our Assumption
2
), which matches the conditions of SGD. However, the proof in
(
Faw et al. 2022
)
is rather complicated (around 30 pages), which is hard to understand the intuition behind and to extend to the analysis of other cases. Moreover, the convergence rate in
(
Faw et al. 2022
)
does not get better when strong growth condition holds (i.e., our Assumption
2
with
D_{0}=0
) while SGD does. We believe such a gap is vital as strong growth condition holds in over-parameterized models
(
Vaswani et al. 2019
)
, which are widely adopted in deep learning.

We know that the convergence analysis of SGD under the same set of assumptions is quite simple.
What makes the analysis of AdaGrad so complicated?
We can understand the difficulty from the classical descent lemma

\begin{matrix}\mathbb{E}[f(\bm{w}_{t+1})|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq f(\bm{w}_{t})+\\
\quad\end{matrix}\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\bm{w}_{t+1}-\bm{w}_{t}\right\rangle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]}\\
\text{First Order}\end{matrix}\begin{matrix}+\\
\quad\end{matrix}\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\frac{L}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\bm{w}_{t+1}-\bm{w}_{t}\right\|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]}\\
\text{Second Order}\end{matrix},

(1)

where
\mathcal{F}_{t}:=\sigma(g_{1},\cdots,g_{t-1})
denotes the sigma field of the stochastic gradients up to
t-1
.
Then

•

for SGD,
\bm{w}_{t+1}-\bm{w}_{t}=-\eta g_{t}
and hence the ”First Order” term is
-\eta\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}
, which is negative and able to decrease the objective sufficiently,

•

for AdaGrad(-Norm),
\bm{w}_{t+1}-\bm{w}_{t}=-\eta\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}
. As
\bm{\nu}_{t}
correlates with
g_{t}
, the “First Order” term does not admit a clear form.

To deal with the correlation in AdaGrad(-Norm), a common practice is to use a surrogate
\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{t}
of
\bm{\nu}_{t}

(
Ward et al. 2020
;
Défossez et al. 2020
;
Faw et al. 2022
)
, which is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
, to decompose the “First Order” term as follows,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\bm{w}_{t+1}-\bm{w}_{t}\right\rangle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),-\eta\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\rangle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),-\eta\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{t}}}\right\rangle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta g_{t}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{t}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)\right\rangle|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right].

The first term equals
-\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{t}}}
, which is negative and desired. However, the last term is an additional error term, which is very challenging to deal with. Existing results either assume bounded stochastic gradient to work around it
(
Ward et al. 2020
)
, or resolve it through complicated analysis
(
Faw et al. 2022
)
(c.f. Section
3
).

Contributions.
In this paper, we propose a novel auxiliary function
\xi(t)=\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}
for the convergence analysis of AdaGrad(-Norm), and show the error term can be bounded by
\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\xi(t-1)-\xi(t)]
(c.f.
Lemma
4
), which can be reduced by telescoping. As explained in Section
3
, such a auxiliary function is rooted in the non-increasing nature of the adaptive learning rate
\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k}}}
.

With the new and simplified proof, we are able to obtain stronger results for AdaGrad-Norm and extend the analysis to other important scenarios.

•

Under strong growth condition (or the so-called over-parameterized regime), our convergence rate for AdaGrad-Norm is
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{T})
, which matches that of SGD and stronger than existing results
(
Faw et al. 2022
)
. This demonstrates that AdaGrad-Norm converges faster in the over-parameterized regime than in the under-parameterized regime.

•

We extend the analysis to AdaGrad by utilizing a coordinate version
\tilde{\xi}(t)=\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{t})^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t,l}}}
of
\xi(t)
and obtain similar convergence. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first convergence result of AdaGrad without the requirement of bounded gradient norm. We also prove the convergence for randomly-reshuffled AdaGrad, which is the version of AdaGrad used in deep learning practice.

•

We go beyond the uniform smoothness and consider a realistic non-uniformly smooth condition called
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth condition (Assumption
6
). We prove that AdaGrad(-Norm) still converges under
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth condition, but requires the learning rate smaller than a threshold, whose necessity is conversely verified with a counterexample. Together, AdaGrad can converge under the non-uniform smoothness but may not be exactly tuning-free.

We have observed a concurrent work by
(
Faw et al. 2023
)
, which also establishes the convergence of AdaGrad under
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth condition and affine noise variance assumption. Though results are similar and both appear in COLT 2023, there are some notable differences between their findings and ours. First,
Faw et al. 2023
require either
D_{1}<2
or an additional assumption on the objective function, whereas our result holds for all
D_{1}
values without any additional assumption. Second, their result is based on a novel stopping time, while ours relies on a new auxiliary function. Lastly,
Faw et al. 2023
establish a set of negative results for Clipped SGD and Sign SGD (with momentum) when analyzed under the
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth condition, highlighting the advantage of AdaGrad over these optimizers. All in all, both their work and ours complement each other, providing a more comprehensive understanding of AdaGrad.

Preliminary

Notations.
The following notations are used throughout this paper.

•

(Vector operators)
\odot
stands for the Hadamard product between vectors, and
g^{\odot 2}\triangleq g\odot g
.
\langle\bm{w},\bm{v}\rangle
stands for the
L^{2}
inner product between
\bm{w}
and
\bm{v}
, and
\|\bm{w}\|\triangleq\sqrt{\langle\bm{w},\bm{w}\rangle}
.

•

(Stochastic operators)
\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma(g_{t-1},\cdots,g_{1})
stands for the sigma field of historical gradients up to time
t-1
and thus
\{\bm{w}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{\infty}
is an adapted random process with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t=1}^{\infty}
. For brevity, we abbreviate the expectation conditional on
\mathcal{F}_{t}
as
\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}[*]\triangleq\mathbb{E}[*|\mathcal{F}_{t}]
.

Assumptions.
Throughout this paper, we assume that
f
is lower bounded. We also need the following assumptions:

Assumption 1

(
L
-smooth condition)
.

We assume that
f
is differentiable and its gradient satisfies that
\forall\bm{w}_{1},\bm{w}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{1})-\nabla f(\bm{w}_{2})\|\leq L\|\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\|
.

Assumption 2

(Affine noise variance)
.

We assume that there exist positive constants
D_{0}
and
D_{1}
such that
\forall t\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\|g_{t}\|^{2}]\leq D_{0}+D_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}
.

To the best of our knowledge, the above two assumptions are the weakest requirements for the convergence of AdaGrad(-Norm) among the existing literature.

3
Motivation of the auxiliary function

As mentioned in Introduction, the main obstacle in the analysis of AdaGrad(-Norm) is to bound the error term
\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta g_{t}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{t}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}})\rangle
. Most of the existing works assume that
\|g_{t}\|
is uniformly bounded, and choose
\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{t}=\bm{\nu}_{t-1}
. In this case, the error term can be shown to be as small as the ”Second Order” term in Eq. (
1
) and can be further bounded. If the bounded gradient assumption is removed,
Faw et al. 2022
shows that
most of the iterations are ”good”
, in the sense that the error term is smaller than
\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{t}}}
, which hence won’t affect the negativity of the ”First Order” term in Eq. (
1
) after decomposition. However, it is complicated to deal with the ”bad” iterations, which occupies the main space of the proof in
(
Faw et al. 2022
)
.

Instead, to deal with the error term, we propose a simple auxiliary function
\xi(t)
that can be canceled out during telescoping. The choice of
\xi(t)
is motivated as follows. By choosing
\tilde{\bm{\nu}}_{t}=\bm{\nu}_{t-1}
, we find that the error term can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta g_{t}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)\right\rangle\leq

\displaystyle\eta\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|\|g_{t}\|\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left(\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|\|g_{t}\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|\|g_{t}\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)\right],

(2)

where the inequality is due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and
\bm{\nu}_{t}
is non-decreasing. Note that if we have both
\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|\approx\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t-1})\|
and
\|g_{t}\|\approx\|g_{t-1}\|
, the term (
2
) approximately equals to
\eta\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left(\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t-1})\|\|g_{t-1}\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|\|g_{t}\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)\right]
. In this case, we can use
\hat{\xi}(t)=\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|\|g_{t}\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}
as an auxiliary function, and the sum of the expected error term satisfies

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta g_{t}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)\right\rangle\lesssim\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{\xi}(t-1)-\hat{\xi}(t)\right]=\hat{\xi}(0)-\mathbb{E}[\hat{\xi}(T)].

The RHS of the above inequality is bounded regardless of
T
.
This is the motivation to use the auxiliary function. However, we do not have
\|g_{t}\|\approx\|g_{t-1}\|
but only have
\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|\approx\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t-1})\|
(due to bounded smoothness, i.e., Assumption
1
). To resolve this challenge, we convert
\|g_{t}\|
to
\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|
by Assumption
2
in the above inequality, and use
\xi(t)\triangleq\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}
instead of
\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|\|g_{t}\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}
as the auxiliary function. A formal statement of the above methodology can be seen in Lemma
4
.

Remark 1
.

Note that the above methodology is mainly based on that the adaptive learning rate is non-increasing. Therefore, we believe that similar approach can be applied to the analysis of other adaptive optimizers with non-increasing adaptive learning rates, such as AMSGrad.

4
A refined convergence analysis of AdaGrad-Norm

In this section, we present our refined analysis of AdaGrad-Norm based on the auxiliary function
\xi(t)
. The refined convergence rate is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
2
hold. Then, for AdaGrad-Norm with any learning rate
\eta>0
, we have that with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\min_{t\in[T]}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{2\sqrt{2D_{0}}(2C_{2}\ln(2\sqrt{2D_{0}T}+C_{3})+C_{1})}{\sqrt{T}\delta^{2}}+\frac{C_{3}(C_{1}+2C_{2}\ln(2\sqrt{2D_{0}T}+C_{3}))}{T\delta^{2}},

where
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
, and
C_{3}
are constants defined as
C_{1}:=4(f(\bm{w}_{1})-f^{*}+\frac{\eta D_{1}}{2}\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{0})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{0}}}+(2\eta(L\eta D_{1})^{2}+\eta D_{1}(L\eta)^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2}D_{0})\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{0}}}-\frac{L}{2}\eta^{2}\ln\bm{\nu}_{0})/\eta
,
C_{2}:=2L\eta
, and
C_{3}:=4D_{1}C_{1}+48C_{2}D_{1}\ln(4C_{2}D_{1}+e)+2\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{0}}
.

Remark 3
.

Faw et al. 2022
also prove that AdaGrad-Norm converges under Assumptions
1
and
2
. Their rate is
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\frac{\log^{\frac{9}{4}}T}{\sqrt{T}})
. Compared to their result, our result has a tighter dependence over
T
. Moreover, when restricted to the strong growth condition, i.e.,
D_{0}=0
, our result gives a rate
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{T})
, much faster than that in
(
Faw et al. 2022
)
and matching that of SGD. Such an improvement counts as strong growth condition characterizes the landscapes of over-parameterized models
(
Vaswani et al. 2019
)
. Theorem
2
also shows that AdaGrad-Norm enjoys the tuning-free ability under
L
-smooth condition, i.e., it converges without tuning the learning rate.

Proof of Theorem
2

The proof starts with the so-called expected descent lemma:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}f(\bm{w}_{t+1})\leq

\displaystyle f(\bm{w}_{t})+\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\bm{w}_{t+1}-\bm{w}_{t}\rangle+\frac{L}{2}\|\bm{w}_{t+1}-\bm{w}_{t}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(\bm{w}_{t})+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),-\eta\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\rangle}\\
\text{First Order}\end{matrix}+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\frac{L}{2}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left\|\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}\\
\text{Second Order}\end{matrix}.

(3)

As discussed in Section
1
, the “First Order” term does not have a simple form due to the correlation between
g_{t}
and
\bm{\nu}_{t}
. We follow the standard approach in existing literature to approximate
\bm{\nu}_{t}
with the surrogate
\bm{\nu}_{t-1}
, which is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{t}
. The first-order term can then be decomposed into

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),-\eta\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\rangle\right]=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),-\eta\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\right\rangle\right]+\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}})g_{t}\right\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}+\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)g_{t}\right\rangle\right].

(4)

The last term is an error term, coming from the gap between
\bm{\nu}_{t-1}
and
\bm{\nu}_{t}
. Plugging Eq. (
4
) back to Eq. (
3
) and we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}f(\bm{w}_{t+1})\leq f(\bm{w}_{t})+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{-\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}}\\
\text{First Order Main}\end{matrix}+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)g_{t}\right\rangle\right]}\\
\text{Error}\end{matrix}+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\frac{L}{2}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left\|\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}\\
\text{Second Order}\end{matrix}.

The rest of the proof can be divided into two stages: in Stage I, We proceed by bounding the “Error” term through the auxiliary function
\xi(t)\triangleq\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}
, and bound
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}
. In Stage II, we convert the bound of
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}
into the bound of
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}
.

Stage I: Bounding the “Error” term.
The following lemma summarizes the intuition in Section
3
.

Lemma 4
.

Define an auxiliary function
\xi(t)\triangleq\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}
,
t\geq 1
. Then, the “Error” term can be bounded as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)g_{t}\right\rangle\right]\leq\frac{3}{4}\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}D_{0}\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{\eta}{2}D_{1}\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\xi(t-1)-\xi(t)\right]+\left(\eta(L\eta D_{1})^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2}D_{1}(L\eta)^{2}\right)\frac{\|g_{t-1}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}^{3}}.

Proof.

By a simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)g_{t}\right\rangle\right]\right|=\left|\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}})(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}})(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}})}g_{t}\right\rangle\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{3}}{(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}})(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}})(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}})}\right]\leq\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\right],

where the first inequality is due to Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the second inequality is because
\bm{\nu}_{t}\geq\|g_{t}\|^{2}
. By the mean-value inequality (
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
),

\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\right]\leq\frac{1}{2}\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\left(\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\right]\right)^{2}.

(5)

We focus on the last quantity
(\mathbb{E}^{|\mathcal{F}_{t}}[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}])^{2}
. By further applying Hölder’s inequality,

Rearranging the RHS of the above inequality leads to the claim.

\displaystyle=

5
Extending the analysis to AdaGrad

In this section, we extend the convergence analysis of AdaGrad-Norm to AdaGrad. Such a result is attractive since AdaGrad is more commonly used in practice than AdaGrad-Norm.
A natural hope is to prove the convergence of AdaGrad under the same set of assumptions in Theorem
2
. However, challenge arises when we try to derive an AdaGrad version of Lemma
4
. Concretely, the “First-Order Main” term becomes
\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{i}f(\bm{w}_{t})^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1,l}}}
(we use
\bm{\nu}_{t,l}
as the
l
-th coordinate of
\bm{\nu}_{t}
, and similar does
g_{t,l}
), while the bound of the “Error” term includes a term
\frac{\eta}{4}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1,l}}}
and thus can not be controlled. Such a mismatch is due to that
\mathbb{E}[g_{t,i}^{2}]
can only be bounded by the full gradient
\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|
instead of the partial derivative of the corresponding coordinate
|\partial_{i}f(\bm{w}_{t})|
(see Appendix
C.2
for details). Therefore, to derive the convergence of AdaGrad, we strengthen Assumption
2
to let the affine noise variance hold coordinate-wisely.

Assumption 3

(Coordinate-wise affine noise variance assumption)
.

We assume that there exist positive constants
D_{0}
and
D_{1}
such that
\forall t\geq 1
and
\forall i\in[d]
,

\mathbb{E}[|g_{t,i}|^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq D_{0}+D_{1}\partial_{i}f(\bm{w}_{t})^{2}
.

Note that Assumption
3
is
still general than most of the assumptions in existing works
. As an example, the bounded noise variance assumption is its special case. Next, we obtain the convergence result for AdaGrad as follows.

Theorem 6

(AdaGrad)
.

Let Assumptions
1
and
3
hold. Then, for AdaGrad with any learning rate
\eta>0
, we have that with probability at least
1-\delta
,
\min_{t\in[T]}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1+\ln(1+\sqrt{D_{0}T})}{T\delta^{2}})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{\sqrt{D_{0}}(1+\ln(1+\sqrt{D_{0}T}))}{\sqrt{T}\delta^{2}})
.

The proof is a coordinate-wise version of the proof for Theorem
2
with some modifications, where we leverage a coordinate-wise version of
\xi(t)
, i.e.,
\tilde{\xi}(t)=\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{t})^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t,l}}}
(please refer to Appendix
C.1
for details).

We still seek to relax Assumption
3
back to Assumption
2
. This is because Assumption
3
may preclude some basic objectives. We demonstrate this idea through the following example.

Example 7
.

Consider the following linear regression problem:
f(\bm{w})=\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathcal{D}}(\langle\bm{w},\bm{x}\rangle)^{2}=\|\bm{w}\|^{2}
, where
\mathcal{D}
is a standard Gaussian distribution over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the absolute value of each coordinate truncated by
1
. At point
\bm{w}
, define the stochastic gradient
g(\bm{w})
as
2\bm{x}\bm{x}^{\top}\bm{w}
, where
\bm{x}
is sampled according to
\mathcal{D}
. One can easily verify that

is an unbiased estimation of
\nabla f(\bm{w})
. For this example,
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathcal{D}}\|g(\bm{w})\|^{2}=\Theta(\|\bm{w}\|^{2})
and
\|\nabla f(\bm{w})\|^{2}=\Theta(\|\bm{w}\|^{2})
. Therefore, Assumption
2
holds with
L_{0}=0
. However,
\|\partial_{1}f(\bm{w})\|^{2}=4(\bm{w})_{1}^{2}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathcal{D}}(g(\bm{w}))_{1}^{2}=\Theta(\|\bm{w}\|^{2})

\lim_{|(\bm{w})_{2}|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|\partial_{1}f(\bm{w})\|^{2}}{\mathbb{E}_{\bm{x}\sim\mathcal{D}}(g(\bm{w}))_{1}^{2}}=\infty
, which violates Assumption
3
.

as the auxiliary function due to
g_{t}\not\approx g_{t-1}
. Therefore, tightening the assumption on the smoothness may help us to ensure
g_{t}\approx g_{t-1}
, and we no longer need to bound
g_{t}
using Assumption
2
, and as a result we will not encounter the mismatch between
\frac{\partial_{i}f(\bm{w}_{t})^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1,i}}}
and
\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1,i}}}
. Motivated by the above example, we make the assumption that we have the access to an stochastic oracle
g(\bm{w},\zeta)
, and
g_{t}
is generated by
g_{t}=g(\bm{w}_{t},\zeta_{t})
where
\zeta_{t}
is sampled independently from a distribution
\mathcal{D}
. We further assume that
g
is
L
Lipschitz with respect to
\bm{w}
for a fixed
\zeta
. Such an assumption is common in stochastic optimization literature
(
Yun et al. 2021
;
Shi and Li 2021
)
.
Unfortunately, such assumptions are still not adequate to ensure
g_{t}\approx g_{t-1}
since
g_{t}
and
g_{t-1}
may use different noise
\zeta
. A good news is that, for the without-replacement version of AdaGrad (also called randomly-reshuffled AdaGrad. Please refer to Algorithm
3
), every
\zeta
appears once within one epoch and the above methodology can be used. Note that randomly-reshuffled AdaGrad is the version of AdaGrad commonly adopted in deep learning. Thus although we slightly change the analyzed algorithm, the problem we consider is still of significance.

As mentioned above, we require the following assumptions for the convergence of randomly-reshuffled AdaGrad.

Assumption 4

(Assumption
2
, reformulated)
.

Let
\bm{w}_{k,i}
and
g_{k,i}
be the ones in Algorithm
3
. Then, there exist constants
D_{0}
and
D_{1}
, such that,
\forall k,i
,
\mathbb{E}_{j\sim\textbf{Uniform}(n)}\|\nabla f_{j}(\bm{w}_{k,i})\|^{2}\leq D_{0}+D_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,i})\|^{2}
.

Assumption 5

(Stochastic
L
-smooth condition)
.

We assume that
\forall i\in[n]
,
f_{i}
is differentiable and its gradient
satisfies
\forall\bm{w}_{1},\bm{w}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have
\|\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{1})-\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{2})\|\leq L\|\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\|
.

Theorem 8

(Randomly-reshuffled AdaGrad)
.

Let Assumptions
4
and
5
hold. Then, for randomly-reshuffled AdaGrad with any
\eta>0
,
\min_{t\in[T]}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t,0})\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1+\ln(1+\sqrt{D_{0}T})}{T})+\mathcal{O}(\frac{\sqrt{D_{0}}(1+\ln(1+\sqrt{D_{0}T}))}{\sqrt{T}})
.

The proof utilizes a randomly-reshuffling version of
\xi(t)
, i.e.
\bar{\xi}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{t,1})||\partial_{l}f_{\tau_{t,i}}(\bm{w}_{t,i})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t,i,l}}}
, and we defer the details to Appendix
C.3
. Theorem
8
shows that randomly-reshuffled AdaGrad does converge under affine noise variance assumption, and extends our analysis techniques to new setting of AdaGrad.

6
Convergence of AdaGrad over non-uniformly smooth landscapes

So far, the characterizations of AdaGrad(-Norm) has closely matched those of SGD over the uniformly smooth landscape. However, in practice, the objective function is usually non-uniformly smooth. Simple examples include polynomial functions with degree larger than
2
, and deep neural networks. A natural question is that whether AdaGrad still works well over non-smooth landscapes. In this section, we analyze AdaGrad(-Norm) under the
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth condition
Zhang et al. 2019
, which is considered as a preciser characterization of the landscape of neural networks through exhaustive experiments.

Assumption 6

(
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth condition)
.

We assume that
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is differentiable. We further assume that there exists positive constants
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
, such that if
\bm{w}_{1},\bm{w}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfies
\|\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\|\leq\frac{1}{L_{0}}
, then
\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{1})-\nabla f(\bm{w}_{2})\|\leq(L_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{1})\|+L_{0})\|\bm{w}_{1}-\bm{w}_{2}\|
.

Assumption
6
degenerates to Assumption
1
with
L=L_{0}
if
L_{1}=0
. Therefore, Assumption
6
is more general than Assumption
1
. Moreover, Assumption
6
holds for polynomials of any degree and even exponential functions.
Zhang et al. 2019
demonstrate through extensive experiments that over the tasks where adaptive optimizers outperforms SGD, Assumption
6
is obeyed.

Theorem 9
.

Let Assumptions
2
and
6
hold. Then, for AdaGrad-Norm with
\forall\eta<\frac{1}{L_{1}}\min\{\frac{1}{64D_{1}},\frac{1}{8\sqrt{D_{1}}}\}
, we have that with probability at least
1-\delta
,

\min_{t\in[T]}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1+\ln(1+\sqrt{D_{0}T})}{T\delta^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\sqrt{D_{0}}(1+\ln(1+\sqrt{D_{0}T}))}{\sqrt{T}\delta^{2}}\right).

The proof can be found in Appendix
D.1
, the key insight of which is that the additional error terms caused by
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth condition is at the same order of the “Error” term in the proof of Theorem
2
. Theorem
9
shows that AdaGrad-Norm can provably overcome the non-uniform smoothness in the objective function and converges. Similar result can be derived for AdaGrad. Compared to Theorem
2
, we additionally require the learning rate to be lower than a threshold. To see such a requirement is not artificial due to the proof, we provide the following theorem.

Theorem 10
.

For every learning rate
\eta>\frac{9\sqrt{5}}{2L_{1}}
, there exist a lower-bounded objective function
f
obeying Assumption
6
and a corresponding initialization point
\bm{w}_{0}
, such that AdaGrad with learning rate
\eta
and initialized at
\bm{w}_{0}
diverges over
f
.

The proof can be found in Appendix
D.2
. Theorem
10
shows that the learning rate requirement in Theorem
9
is tight w.r.t.
L_{1}
up to constants. The tuning-free ability of AdaGrad under
L
-smooth condition, i.e, AdaGrad converges under any learning rate, has been considered to be superiority of AdaGrad over SGD. However, combining Theorem
9
and Theorem
10
, we demonstrate that such a property is lost when under a more realistic assumption on the smoothness. On the other hand,
Zhang et al. 2019
show that SGD converges arbitrarily slowly under
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth condition. Together with Theorem
9
, we find another superiority of AdaGrad: it can provably overcome non-uniform smoothness but SGD can not.

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we analyze AdaGrad over non-convex landscapes. Specifically, we propose a novel auxiliary function to bound the error term that is brought by the update of AdaGrad. Based on this auxiliary function, we are able to significantly simplify the proof of AdaGrad-Norm and establish a tighter convergence rate in the over-parameterized regime. We further extend the analysis to AdaGrad and non-uniformly smooth landscapes through different variants of the auxiliary function. One future direction is to explore and compare the convergences of AdaGrad and Adam under the
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth condition, given the fact that convergences of AdaGard and SGD are clearly separated under this condition.

Lemma 11
.

Let
\{a_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}
be a series of non-negative real numbers with
a_{0}>0
. Then, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{a_{t}}{\sqrt{(\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s})^{3}}}\leq 2\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}_{0}},~~\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{a_{t}}{\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}}\leq\ln\sum_{t=0}^{T}a_{t}-\ln a_{0},

\displaystyle\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{a_{t}}{\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}}(\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}a_{s}}+\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}})^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}_{0}}.

Proof of Lemma
11
.

The first inequality is because
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{a_{t}}{\sqrt{(\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s})^{3}}}\leq\int_{x=a_{0}}^{\sum_{t=0}^{T}a_{t}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x}^{3}}\mathrm{d}x
. The second inequality is because
\sum_{t=1}^{T}\frac{a_{t}}{\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}}\leq\int_{x=a_{0}}^{\sum_{t=0}^{T}a_{t}}\frac{1}{x}\mathrm{d}x
.
The third inequality is due to

\displaystyle\frac{a_{t}}{\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}}(\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}a_{s}}+\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}})^{2}}\leq\frac{a_{t}}{\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}}\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}a_{s}}(\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}a_{s}}+\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}a_{s}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum_{s=0}^{t}a_{s}}}.

The proof is completed.
∎

then
\forall k\geq 2

|F_{2}|

\displaystyle|F_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k-1,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k-1,\tilde{\tau}_{k-1,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1,l}}}-\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i},l}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}(\eta L\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{L\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\|\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})-\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k-1,\tilde{\tau}_{k-1,i}})\|^{2})

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}(\eta^{2}L\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k-1,\tilde{\tau}_{k-1,i}})\right\|^{2}+\frac{1}{L}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})-\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k-1,1})\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k-1,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k-1,\tilde{\tau}_{k-1,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1,l}}}-\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i},l}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta L\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{2\eta nL}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta^{2}L\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k-1,\tilde{\tau}_{k-1,i}})\right\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\right)nL\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k-1,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k-1,j}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\overset{(\circ)}{\leq}

\displaystyle\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k-1,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k-1,\tilde{\tau}_{k-1,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k-1,\tilde{\tau}_{k-1,i},l}}}-\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i},l}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta L\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{2\eta nL}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\left(\eta^{2}L\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k-1,\tilde{\tau}_{k-1,i}})\right\|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{2\eta}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\right)nL\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k-1,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k-1,j}\|^{2}\right),

.

When

, we directly bound

\displaystyle|F_{3}|\leq

\displaystyle\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1,l}}}|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k,1})||\partial_{l}f_{\tau_{k,i}}(\bm{w}_{k,1})-g_{k,i,l}|

\displaystyle\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1,l}}}|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k,1})||\partial_{l}f_{\tau_{k,i}}(\bm{w}_{k,1})-\partial_{l}f_{\tau_{k,i}}(\bm{w}_{k,i})|

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\frac{1}{4}\eta L\|\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{L\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\|\nabla f_{\tau_{k,i}}(\bm{w}_{k,1})-\nabla f_{\tau_{k,i}}(\bm{w}_{k,i})\|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n}{4}\eta L\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\eta nL}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\eta}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\right)n^{2}L\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k-1,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k-1,j}\|^{2}+\frac{2\eta}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}n^{2}L\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\|^{2}.

\displaystyle\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1}),\bm{w}_{k+1}-\bm{w}_{k,1}\rangle\leq F_{1}+|F_{2}|+|F_{3}|

\displaystyle f(\bm{w}_{T+1,1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(\bm{w}_{1,1})-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{T}n\eta\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{1,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{1,\tilde{\tau}_{1,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,1,l}}}-\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{1,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{1,\tilde{\tau}_{1,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,\tilde{\tau}_{1,i},l}}}\right)

\displaystyle f(\bm{w}_{1,1})-\sum_{k=1}^{T}n\eta\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{1,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{1,\tilde{\tau}_{1,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,1,l}}}-\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{T,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{T,\tilde{\tau}_{T,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{T,\tilde{\tau}_{T,i},l}}}\right)

We respectively bound the last two terms.

The term
\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}L\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k+1,1}}}\odot\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}L\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k+1,1}}}\odot\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})\right\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}\eta^{2}L\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}}}}\odot\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2}L\left\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\right\|^{2}.

(14)

Thus, we only need to bound the last term in order to bound the first two terms. The third term
\sum_{k=1}^{T}\left(1+\frac{3\eta}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\right)nL^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{3\eta}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\right)nL^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\ln\frac{\bm{\nu}_{T,n,l}}{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}.

Combining the above estimations together, we obtain

\displaystyle f(\bm{w}_{T+1,1})\leq

\displaystyle f(\bm{w}_{1,1})-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k=1}^{T}n\eta\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\right\|^{2}+\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{1,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{1,\tilde{\tau}_{1,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,1,l}}}

(15)

\displaystyle C_{0}\triangleq f(\bm{w}_{1,1})-f(\bm{w}_{T+1,1})+\eta\sum_{l=1}^{d}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{|\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{1,1})||\partial_{l}f_{i}(\bm{w}_{1,\tilde{\tau}_{1,i}})|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,1,l}}}+\left(\left(1+\frac{3\eta}{\min_{l}\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}}\right)nL^{2}+\frac{1}{2}L\right)\sum_{l=1}^{d}\ln\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l},

Based on these notations, Eq. (
15
) can be abbreviated as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}n\eta\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\right\|^{2}\leq 4C_{0}+4C_{1}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\ln\bm{\nu}_{T,n,l}.

(16)

Since

\displaystyle\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\right\|^{2}=\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k,1})^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{k,1,l}}}\geq\sum_{l=1}^{d}\frac{\partial_{l}f(\bm{w}_{k,1})^{2}}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{k,1,l^{\prime}}}}=\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{k,1,l^{\prime}}}},

the LHS of Eq. (
16
) can be lower bounded as

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{T}n\eta\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt[4]{\bm{\nu}_{k,1}}}\odot\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\right\|^{2}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{T}n\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{k,1,l^{\prime}}}}\geq n\eta\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{T}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{k,1})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\overset{(\star)}{\geq}

\displaystyle n\eta\frac{\frac{1}{nD_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,1})\|^{2}-\frac{D_{0}}{D_{1}}T}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\eta\frac{\frac{1}{2nD_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})\|^{2}-\frac{1}{nD_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})-\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,1})\|^{2}-\frac{D_{0}}{D_{1}}T}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\eta\frac{\frac{1}{2nD_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})\|^{2}-\frac{L^{2}}{nD_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}}-\bm{w}_{k,1}\|^{2}-\frac{D_{0}}{D_{1}}T}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\eta\frac{\frac{1}{2nD_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})\|^{2}-\frac{L^{2}}{D_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\|^{2}-\frac{D_{0}}{D_{1}}T}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle n\eta\frac{\frac{1}{2nD_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\nabla f_{i}(\bm{w}_{k,\tilde{\tau}_{k,i}})\|^{2}-\frac{nL^{2}}{D_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\|^{2}-\frac{D_{0}}{D_{1}}T}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle n\eta\frac{\frac{1}{2nD_{1}}(\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}-\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l^{\prime}})-\frac{nL^{2}}{D_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\|^{2}-\frac{D_{0}}{D_{1}}T}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}}}.

As a conclusion,

\displaystyle n\eta\frac{\frac{1}{2nD_{1}}(\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,n,l^{\prime}}-\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l^{\prime}})-\frac{nL^{2}}{D_{1}}\sum_{k=1}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|\bm{w}_{k,j+1}-\bm{w}_{k,j}\|^{2}-\frac{D_{0}}{D_{1}}T}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4C_{0}+4C_{1}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\ln\bm{\nu}_{T,n,l}.

Echoing Eq. (
14
), we then obtain

\displaystyle n\eta\frac{\frac{1}{2nD_{1}}(\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,n,l^{\prime}}-\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l^{\prime}})-\frac{nL^{2}}{D_{1}}\eta^{2}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\ln\frac{\bm{\nu}_{T,n,l}}{\bm{\nu}_{1,0,l}}-\frac{D_{0}}{D_{1}}T}{\sqrt{\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,1,l^{\prime}}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4C_{0}+4C_{1}\sum_{l=1}^{d}\ln\bm{\nu}_{T,n,l}.

Solving the above inequality with respect to
\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,n,l^{\prime}}
leads to

\sum_{l^{\prime}=1}^{d}\bm{\nu}_{T,n,l^{\prime}}=\mathcal{O}(1)+\mathcal{O}(D_{0}T).

Applying the above inequality back to Eq. (
16
) completes the proof.
∎

The proof bears great similarity to that of the descent lemma under
L
-smooth condition and we omit it here. Interested readers can refer to
(
Zhang et al. 2020
, Lemma A.3)

Proof of Theorem

.

Since we set
\eta\leq\frac{1}{L_{1}}
and
\|\frac{\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\|\leq 1
, we have

Therefore, Lemma
12
can be applied to
\bm{w}_{t}
and
\bm{w}_{t+1}
, taking expectation to which leads to

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}f(\bm{w}_{t+1})\leq f(\bm{w}_{t})+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),-\eta\frac{\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\rangle}\\
\text{First Order}\end{matrix}+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\frac{L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{2}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left\|\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}\\
\text{Second Order}\end{matrix}.

We then decompose the ”First Order” term into the ”First Order Main” term and the ”Error” term as follows.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}f(\bm{w}_{t+1})\leq

\displaystyle f(\bm{w}_{t})+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{-\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}}\\
\text{First Order Main}\end{matrix}+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}})g_{t}\right\rangle\right]}\\
\text{Error}\end{matrix}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\frac{L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{2}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left\|\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}\\
\text{Second Order}\end{matrix}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle f(\bm{w}_{t})+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{-\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}}\\
\text{First Order Main}\end{matrix}+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}})g_{t}\right\rangle\right]}\\
\text{Error}\end{matrix}

\displaystyle+

\text{Second Order I}\end{matrix}+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\frac{L_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{2}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left\|\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}\\
\text{Second Order II}\end{matrix}.

(17)

Here the second inequality is obtained by directly expanding the ”Second Order” term. The ”Second Order I” term takes the same form of the ”Second Order” term in the proof of Theorem
2
, and it can be handled in the same way as the proof of Theorem
2
. So does the ”First Order Main” term. As for the ”Error” term, following the same routine as the proof of Lemma
4
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}})g_{t}\right\rangle\right]\right|\leq\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\right].

Meanwhile, since
\bm{\nu}_{t}
is non-decreasing with respect to
t
, we have that

\frac{L_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{2}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left\|\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\|^{2}\leq L_{1}\eta^{2}\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\right].

One can easily observe that the RHSs of the above two inequalities have the same form except the coefficients! Thus, the ”Error” term plus the ”Second Order II” can be bounded by

\displaystyle\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\left\langle\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t}),\eta(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}})g_{t}\right\rangle\right]}\\
\text{Error}\end{matrix}+\begin{matrix}\underbrace{\frac{L_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{2}\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left\|\frac{g_{t}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right\|^{2}}\\
\text{Second Order II}\end{matrix}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\eta+L_{1}\eta^{2})\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\right]\leq 2\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\right],

where in the last inequality we use
\eta L_{1}\leq 1
. By the mean-value inequality (
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
),

\displaystyle 2\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\right]\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}+2\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\|g_{t}\|^{2}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\eta\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}+2\frac{\eta}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}(D_{0}+D_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2})\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}})^{2}}\right],

where the second inequality is due to Hölder’s inequality, and in the last inequality we use Assumption
1
. Similar to the proof of Theorem
2
, we focus on the term
2D_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}})^{2}}\right]
, since the rest of the terms can be easily bounded. Such a term can be bounded as

\displaystyle 2\frac{\eta D_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left[\frac{\|g_{t}\|^{2}}{(\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}+\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle 2\eta D_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\eta D_{1}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left(\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t-1})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)+2\eta D_{1}\frac{\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}-\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t-1})\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}.

By Assumption

\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|-\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t-1})\|\leq\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})-\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t-1})\|\leq(L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|)\|\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t-1}\|

\displaystyle 2\eta D_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\eta D_{1}\frac{2(L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|)\|\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t-1}\|\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|+(L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f(\bm{w}_{t})\|)^{2}\|\bm{w}_{t}-\bm{w}_{t-1}\|^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{\nu}_{t-1}}}

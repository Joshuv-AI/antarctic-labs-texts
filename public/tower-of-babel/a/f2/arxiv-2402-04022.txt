Introduction

In this article, we focus on the posterior computation of
a one-dimensional Gaussian process (GP)
y

having the covariance function, or kernel function in some literature,
K
. Specifically, we consider

y\sim\mathcal{N}(0,K)
, a GP defined on
[t_{0},T]
, where
t_{0}
and
T
can be infinite, that has the following forward stochastic differential equation (SDE) representation:

\mathcal{L}[y]:=y^{(m)}(t)+c_{m-1}(t)y^{(m-1)}(t)+\cdots+c_{0}(t)y(t)=W(t),\quad t\in[t_{0},T],

(1)

where
W(t)
is a white noise process. Without loss of generality, we assume that
W
has spectrum 1. The goal is to obtain the predictive distribution of
y(t)
at a general
t
, given observations
\{y(t_{i})\}_{i=1}^{n}
with
n
sorted data points
\mathbf{T}=\{t_{1}<t_{2}<\cdots<t_{n}\}
. The range of kernel associated to SDEs is broad, refer to references such as
Solin et al. 2016
;
Benavoli and Zaffalon 2016
for details.

This problem can be solved under the GP regression framework. In the original form, GP regression requires
\mathcal{O}(n^{3})
training time and
\mathcal{O}(n)
prediction time. For an exact inference, an efficient algorithm can be obtained by state space (SS) models, which reduces the training time to
\mathcal{O}(n)
. However, its prediction time remains as
\mathcal{O}(n)
. In this work, we develop an exact algorithm that requires
\mathcal{O}(n)
training time and
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
or even
\mathcal{O}(1)
time for each prediction point.

The proposed algorithm is based on the idea of kernel packets (KPs) in
Chen et al. 2022
, which showed that certain linear combinations of kernels can possess the compact-support property. In this work, we prove that KPs generally exist for GPs defined by (
1
), and provide a framework to obtain them. Unlike
Chen et al. 2022
, which constructs KPs through methods in harmonic analysis and unrelated to SDE, we derives KPs from the forward and backward Markov properties of SS models.

Training and prediction of one dimensional GPs using KPs are in Appendix
B
. Generalization of KP to multi-dimension for scattered data are in Appendix
C
. Discussion on connection between SDEs and kernels are in Appendix
D
. The primary focus of the main paper is on the connection between SDE (
1
) and compactly supported basis functions.

2
Motivation and Preliminaries

The idea of kernel packets (KPs) was introduced in
Chen et al. 2022
to address the computational challenges of Gaussian process (GP) regression. For simplicity, we only consider noiseless GP regression, and as shown in
Chen et al. 2022
, the KP approach can accommodate Gaussian noise easily. The objective of (noiseless) GP regression is to reconstruct the underlying function
f
given observations
Y=(f(t_{1}),\ldots,f(t_{n}))^{T}
, by imposing the prior that
f
follows a GP. Assuming that the GP has mean zero and covariance function
K
, the posterior distribution of
f(t)
for any untried
t
is a multivariate normal distribution given by

\displaystyle f(t)|Y\sim N(K(t,\mathbf{T})K^{-1}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T})Y,K(t,\mathbf{T})K^{-1}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T})K(\mathbf{T},t)),

(2)

where
\mathbf{T}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
.

Note that (
2
) involves inverting an
n\times n
matrix, which prohibits the scalability of GP regression in its original form. Here we focus on an exact methodology for one-dimensional GP regression problems, stemmed from the state-space (SS) models.

2.1
State-space models

We restrict our one-dimensional GP model to the following discrete-time model

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} z_{n}&=A_{n}z_{n-1}+q_{n}&\text{ (dynamic model)}\\
y_{n}&=H_{n}z_{n}&\text{ (measurement model)}\end{aligned}\quad,

(3)

where
z_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{m},y_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
,
A_{n},H_{n}
are real-valued matrices,
A_{n}
is invertible, and
z_{0},q_{1},q_{2},\ldots
are mutually independent with
z_{0}\sim N(0,Q_{0}),q_{n}\sim N(0,Q_{n})
. As before, we assume that the measurement model is noiseless for simplicity. While
(y_{0},\ldots,y_{n},\ldots)
defines a GP, GP regression for this problem can be done within
\mathcal{O}(n)
time via the Kalman filter and the Raugh-Tung-Striebel smoother
(
Särkkä and Svensson 2013
)
, when
m
is small and regarded as a fixed number. This drastic improvement is due to the Markovian property of the dynamic model in (
3
), which leads to sparse representation of the covariance so that the matrix inversion in (
2
) can be done easily. It can be shown that the inverse covariance matrix of
(z_{0},\ldots,z_{n})
has a sparse representing as
B^{T}R^{-1}B^{-1}
, where
B,R
are banded matrices with bandwidth independent of
n
.

By regarding the derivatives
\{y^{(1)}(t_{i}),\ldots,y^{(m)}(t_{i}):i=1,\ldots,n\}
as the hidden states, the GP driven by the SDE (
1
) can be translated into a continuous-time analogy of the SS model (
3
) and thus can be solved efficiently; see, for example,
Hartikainen and Särkkä 2010
. A prominent GP model discussed in
Hartikainen and Särkkä 2010
is the Matérn kernels with half-integer smoothness.

2.2
Kernel packets

The method of kernel packets looks for a different sparse representation of
K(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T})
in (
2
). For Matérn kernels with smoothness
\nu=p-1/2
with
p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
Chen et al. 2022
proved that
K(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T})=A^{-1}\Phi
, where
A
has bandwidth
p
and
\Phi
has bandwidth
p-1
. To explain this representation, we need the definition of KPs. Denote
a\wedge b:=\min(a,b)
and
a\vee b:=\max(a,b)
. Below is a rephrased version of the original definition in
Chen et al. 2022
.

Definition 1
.

Given
p,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
with
n>2p+1
, positive definite function
K(\cdot,\cdot)
, and input points
t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}\in(t_{0},T)
, a set of functions
\{\phi_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,\phi_{n}(\cdot)\}
is called a kernel packet system with degree
2p+1
if

1.

\phi_{j}=\sum_{k=(j-p)\vee 0}^{(j+p)\wedge n}a_{jk}K(\cdot,t_{k})
for some not-all-zero constants
a_{jk}
’s.

2.

For
j=1,\ldots,p
,
\phi_{j}(t)=0
whenever
t\geq t_{j+p}
. These
\phi_{j}
’s are called the left-sided KPs.

3.

For
j=p+1,\ldots,n-p-1
,
\phi_{j}(t)=0
whenever
t\not\in(t_{j-p},t_{j+p})
. These
\phi_{j}
’s are called the KPs.

4.

For
j=n-p,\ldots,n
,
\phi_{j}(t)=0
whenever
t\leq t_{j-p}
. These
\phi_{j}
’s are called the right-sided KPs.

A kernel packet system is called a kernel packet basis if the functions are linearly independent.

In other words, a KP basis is a linear transform of
\{K(\cdot,t_{1}),\ldots,K(\cdot,t_{n})\}
and is (mostly) compactly supported. This leads to the aforementioned sparse representation and an
O(n)
time GP regression algorithm. See Figure
1
for an illustration of KP bases.

Figure 1:
KP basis functions corresponding to Matérn-
3/2
and Matérn-
5/2
correlations from
Chen et al. 2022
The KPs, left-sided KPs, and the right-sided KPs are plotted in orange, blue, and green lines, respectively.

What KP can do but SS can’t?
While KP and SS approaches both leads to
\mathcal{O}(n)
time algorithms for GP regression model, yet KP surpasses SS in log-likelihood and prediction computational efficiency. In the training step, we parameterize kernel
K_{\boldsymbol{\theta}}
, use log-likelihood to select the optimal
K=K_{\boldsymbol{\theta}^{*}}
, and then compute and restore
u:=K^{-1}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T})Y
. In the prediction step, we compute
K(t,\mathbf{T}))K^{-1}(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T})Y=K(t,\mathbf{T})u
. Unlike SS, which cannot improve the log-likelihood efficiency and requires
\mathcal{O}(n)
time per prediction, KP reduces computation to
\mathcal{O}(n)
for log-likelihood and
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
or
\mathcal{O}(1)
for predictions, thanks to the compact support of KPs. Moreover, SS cannot accelerate computations involving matrix multiplications of the form
K(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T})u
. Using KPs, computational time for
K(\mathbf{T},\mathbf{T})u
can also be reduced to
\mathcal{O}(n)
. Detailed algorithms for computing predictive mean, variance, and log-likelihood are provided in Appendix
B
.

2.3
Basic theory for kernel packet basis

Now we introduce some general theory for kernel packet basis, which gives sufficient conditions for a KP system to become a KP basis. The main idea, originated from
Chen et al. 2022

Definition 2
.

A KP
\phi=\sum_{k=\underline{k}}^{\overline{k}}a_{k}K(\cdot,t_{k})

1.

No function with the form
\psi=\sum_{k=\underline{k}+1}^{\overline{k}}c_{k}K(\cdot,t_{k})

\psi=\sum_{k=\underline{k}}^{\overline{k}-1}c_{k}K(\cdot,t_{k})

2.

There exist

and
t^{\prime}\in[t_{\overline{k}-1},t_{\overline{k}})
such that
\phi(x)\neq 0
and
\phi(t^{\prime})\neq 0
.

Irreducible left- and right-sided KPs are defined analogously. A KP system is called minimal if each function in the system is irreducible.

Theorem 3
.

A minimal KP system forms a KP basis.

4
Kernel Packets for State-space Models

Given the soundness of KP bases, the main objective is to explore KP basis outside the Matérn family. We start from SS models in view of their known sparse representation.

4.1
General existence of kernel packets

First, we introduce some general ideas and terminology to build KPs, the main ingredients for KP bases.
From a Gaussian-Markov process point of view, the existence of KPs is not entirely surprising. Suppose the dynamic model in (
3
) is described by a general zero-mean
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued Gaussian-Markov process
z(t)
, either in discrete or continuous time.
The observations are
y(t)=Hz(t)
, in analogous to the measurement model in (
3
).
Then the covariance function of
y(\cdot)
is

K(t_{1},t_{2}):=\operatorname{Cov}(y(t_{1}),y(t_{2}))=H\mathbb{E}[z(t_{1})z(t_{2})^{T}]H^{T}.

Now fix
t_{1}<\cdots<t_{s}
, and let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{s}
be undetermined coefficients. Then for any
t\leq t_{1}
, the Gaussian-Markov property implies that

\mathbb{E}[z(t)|z(t_{1}),\ldots,z(t_{s})]=\mathbb{E}[z(t)|z(t_{1})]=A(t,t_{1})z(t_{1}),\quad\text{s.t.}\ A(t_{1},t_{1})=\mathbf{I}_{m}

for some deterministic function
A(t,t_{1})
. Then,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}K(t,t_{j})=H\mathbb{E}\left\{\mathbb{E}\left[z(t)\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}z(t_{j})^{T}\Big|z(t_{1}),\ldots,z(t_{s})\right]\right\}H^{T}

\displaystyle=H\mathbb{E}\left\{A(t,t_{1})z(t_{1})\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}z(t_{j})^{T}\right\}H^{T}=HA(t,t_{1})\underbrace{\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}\mathbb{E}[z(t_{1})z(t_{j})^{T}]}_{(*)}H^{T}.

(4)

Note that
(*)
is independent of
t
and
H
, and (
4
) is zero if
(*)
is zero. Let
R(s,t)
denote
\mathbb{E}[z(s)z(t)^{T}]
, then if

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}R(t_{1},t_{j})=0,

(5)

we have
\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}K(t,t_{j})=0
for all
t\leq t_{1}
. We call (
5
) the
right-sided KP equations
. Analogously, we can write the
left-sided KP equations
as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}R(t_{s},t_{j})=0,

(6)

which are derived from the “backward Markov property”:
\mathbb{E}[z(t)|z(t_{1}),\ldots,z(t_{s})]=\mathbb{E}[z(t)|z(t_{s})]

t\geq t_{s}
. We call the simultaneous equations (
5
) and (

. As shown in Theorem

is positive definite.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose
K
is positive definite,
t_{1}<\cdots<t_{s}
, and
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{s})^{T}
is a non-zero vector satisfying (

\phi(\cdot)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}a_{i}K(\cdot,t_{i})

The question revolves around determining the necessary size of

4.2
Forward stochastic differential equation

Under condition
1

Theorem 6

Define

\mathcal{W}_{j+1}[h_{1},\cdots,h_{j+1}](t)=\text{det}\begin{bmatrix}h_{1}(t)&\cdots&h_{j+1}(t)\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[y]=\frac{1}{u_{m+1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{D}_{m}\tilde{D}_{m-1}\cdots\tilde{D}_{3}\tilde{D}_{2}\frac{y}{u_{1}}=W

Lemma 8

Suppose Condition

(

)
.

The backward equation of (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dx(\tau)=\left[F(\tau)+C(\tau)\right]x(\tau)dt-B(\tau)W(T-\tau)\\[2.15277pt]

(12)

Because both the GP

Kernel Packets Theorems

In this section, we prove the Main Theorem

5.1
Proof of the Main Theorem

With the help of Theorem
7
and Theorem
10

to construct the central KP, which are both left-sided and right-sided KP simultaneously.

Theorem 12
.

For any
2m+1

in

\displaystyle h_{i}=P_{i},\quad h_{i+m}=R_{1,i}(\cdot,t_{2m+1}),\quad\text{for}\ i=1,\cdots,m.

where

for a minimal KP system.

Theorem

5.2
KPs for combined kernels

Kernel combinations, such as addition and multiplication, are powerful methods for creating data-adaptive kernels. Not all combined kernels necessarily have an equivalent representation in the form of SDE (
1
), but these kernels are still corresponding to some SS model (
3
). We will show that KPs can still be constructed for these two major types of combined kernels. In Theorem

and Theorem

and

of GPs both having the form (

) and meeting Condition

Theorem 14
.

Let
\psi=[\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{s}]^{T}
where
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s}
is any minimal spanning set of the space

\text{span}\{D^{(j)}K_{1}(\cdot,t_{1}),\ D^{(j)}K_{1}(\cdot,t_{s+1}),\ D^{(j)}K_{2}(\cdot,t_{1}),\ D^{(j)}K_{2}(\cdot,t_{s+1}):\ j=0,\cdots,m-1\}.

Then

is the minimal KP system. By solving it,
we can have the KP of

compactly supported on

\sum_{j=1}^{s+1}a_{j}\left[K_{1}(t,t_{j})+K_{2}(t,t_{j})\right]=0,\quad\forall\ t\not\in(t_{1},t_{s+1}).

(14)

Theorem 15
.

Let
\psi=[\psi_{1},\cdots,\psi_{s_{1}}]^{T}
where
\{\psi_{i}\}_{i=1}^{s_{1}}
is any minimal spanning set of the space

\displaystyle\text{span}\{D_{t}^{(j)}K_{1}(t,t_{1}),j=0,\cdots,m-1\}\bigotimes\text{span}\{D_{t}^{(j)}K_{2}(t,t_{1}),j=0,\cdots,m-1\}

(15)

Similarly, let
\zeta=[\zeta_{1},\cdots,\zeta_{s_{s}}]^{T}

\displaystyle\text{span}\{D_{t}^{(j)}K_{1}(t,t_{s_{2}}),j=0,\cdots,m-1\}\bigotimes\text{span}\{D_{t}^{(j)}K_{2}(t,t_{s_{2}}),j=0,\cdots,m-1\}

The KP system for

. This implies that the methods for developing both left-sided and right-sided KPs for

7
Conclusion

In this article, we present a general theory to pursue kernel packets—a proxy of the compactly supported basis functions in GP or kernel methods—for a wide class of GPs driven by a set of SDEs. With KPs, exact inference for GPs can be done in
\mathcal{O}(n)
training time and
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
or even
\mathcal{O}(1)
prediction time. Although only one-dimensional input space is considered in this article, the approach can be extended to accommodate some multi-dimensional learning problems. For one-dimensional GPs that do not admit an SDE representation, such as the Gaussian kernel, KPs may not exist; see
Chen et al. 2022
. However, approximated SDEs can be found using standard approximation methods like the Taylor expansion
(
Hartikainen and Särkkä 2010
)
. Another potential benefit of the proposed approach is that the whole method is in terms of an algebraic identity, which can be seamlessly plugged into more complicated models such as the deep kernel learning
(
Wilson et al. 2016
)
or the deep Gaussian process models
(
Damianou and Lawrence 2013
)
.

Appendix D
Background Introduction for SDEs and Kernels

D.1
Converting between SDE and Kernel

Many kernels are associated to GPs that can be formulated in SDE. We first introduce converting between SDE and kernel, providing an initial comprehension of the link between SDEs and GPs. For a detailed introduction, examples, and references of further advanced material, please refer to
Särkkä and Solin 2019
;
Solin et al. 2016
;
Benavoli and Zaffalon 2016
.

The SDE representation (
1
) of a GP can be reformulated as the first order Markov process (
9
). Recall that (
9
) is written as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}dz(t)=F(t)z(t)dt+B(t)W(t)\\[2.15277pt]
y(t)=H(t)z(t)\end{array}\right.,\quad t\in(t_{0},T)

(53)

where
W(t)
is a white noise process

\mathbb{E}[W(t)W(t^{\prime})]=\delta(t-t^{\prime})

with
\delta(t-t^{\prime})
being the Dirac delta. Here, the matrix function
F(t)
is a general matrix function not necessary in the form given in (
9
) and
B(t)
is also a general vector function.

Our goal is then to solve the kernel function
K(t,t^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[y(t),y(t^{\prime})])
, which is simply

K(t,t^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[y(t),y(t^{\prime})]=H(t)\mathbb{E}[z(t)z^{T}(t^{\prime})]H^{T}(t^{\prime}).

We can note that (
53
) should be interpreted as

z(t)=\int_{\tau}^{t}\Phi(t,s)B(s)W(s)ds+\Phi(t,\tau)z(\tau)

where matrix function
\Phi(t,s)
is known as the transfer matrix and it satisfies the following ODE

\partial_{t}\Phi(t,s)=F(t)\Phi(t,s),\quad\Phi(s,s)=\boldsymbol{I}.

(54)

We can solve(
54
) directly to get
\Phi(t,s)=\exp(\int_{s}^{t}F(\tau)d\tau)
for any
s\leq t
.
It is then straightforward to calculate the kernel function of
y(t)
as follows:

K(t,t^{\prime})=H(t\vee t^{\prime})\Phi(t\vee t,t\wedge t^{\prime})\Pi(t\wedge t^{\prime})H^{T}(t\wedge t^{\prime})

(55)

where
t\vee t^{\prime}
denotes the max between
t
and
t^{\prime}
,
t\wedge t^{\prime}
denotes the min between
t
and
t^{\prime}
, and
\Pi(t)=R(t,t)
and obeys

\partial_{t}\Pi(t)=F(t)\Pi(t)+\Pi(t)F^{T}(t)+B(t)B^{T}(t).

(56)

Solution to the matrix equation (
56
) is given as:

\Pi(t)=\Phi(t,t_{0})\Pi(t_{0})\Phi^{T}(t,t_{0})+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\Phi(t,\tau)B(\tau)[\Phi(t,\tau)B(\tau)]^{T}d\tau.

The equation (
55
) plays an important role in the conversion between SDE and kernel because it provides an explicit formula for calculating the kernel
K
from the given SDE (
53
).

D.2
Kernels from Kernels

In this last part of the appendix, we employ basic SDE theorems to demonstrate that kernels derived from linear operations on a kernel associated with a specific SDE also have SDE formulations and, as a result, also have KP factorization. This highlights the broad range of kernels that can be associated with SDEs and have KP factorization.

Given a kernel
K
, recall that it is the covariance function of a SDE as follows:

\mathcal{L}[y]:=c_{m}(t)y^{(m)}(t)+c_{m-1}(t)y^{(m-1)}(t)+\cdots+c_{0}(t)y(t)=W(t),\quad t\in[t_{0},T].

(57)

For the sake of notation simplicity in our analysis, we’ve assumed
c_{m}=1
and the spectrum of
W(t)
is 1. However, our theorem is applicable to a general
c_{m}(t)
and spectrum such that the operator
\mathcal{L}
still satisfies Condition
1
. Condition
1
guarantees that, when reformulating the GP (
57
) as a first-order Markov process (
9
), the coefficient matrix
F(t)
remains bounded between non-zero and infinity values, ensuring the invertibility of the kernel matrix for any point set. Also, Condition
1
also ensures that (
57
) can be written in a product form (
7
). Recall the product form is written as:

\mathcal{L}[y]=\frac{1}{u_{m+1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{D}_{m}\tilde{D}_{m-1}\cdots\tilde{D}_{3}\tilde{D}_{2}\frac{y}{u_{1}}=W

(58)

The product form allows for the following theorems

Theorem 19
.

If kernel
K(t,t^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[y(t)y(t^{\prime})]
has a
(2m)\times(2m+1)
KP system with
y
being a GP satisfying Condition
1

also has a
(2m)\times(2m+1)
KP system if
1/f
is
m
-time continuously differentiable.

Proof.

Notice that
f(t)f(t^{\prime})K(t,t^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[\left(f(t)y(t)\right)\left(f(t^{\prime})y(t^{\prime})\right)]
. We define a new process
\tilde{y}(t)=f(t)y(t)
. So the product form (
58
) associated to
\tilde{y}
is simply

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}[\tilde{y}]=\frac{1}{u_{m+1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{D}_{m}\tilde{D}_{m-1}\cdots\tilde{D}_{3}\tilde{D}_{2}\frac{\tilde{y}}{fu_{1}}=\mathcal{L}[y]=W.

Because we have assumed that
1/f
is
m
-time continuously differentiable, all the
m
fundamental solutions to the new operator
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}
remain bounded and independent. As a result, the same proof for its minimal KP system also remain valid.
∎

58

\tilde{y}

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}[\tilde{y}]=\frac{1}{u_{m+1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{D}_{m}\tilde{D}_{m-1}\cdots\tilde{D}_{3}\frac{\tilde{y}}{u_{2}}=W.

Obviously,

Theorem 21
.

If kernel
K(t,t^{\prime})=\mathbb{E}[y(t)y(t^{\prime})]

(2m)\times(2m+1)

y

), then kernel
\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{t_{0}}^{t^{\prime}}K(\tau,s)d\tau ds

(2m+2)\times(2m+3)

Proof.

Notice that
\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}K(\tau,s)d\tau ds=\mathbb{E}[\int_{t_{0}}^{t}y(s)ds\int_{t_{0}}^{t}y(s)ds]

\tilde{\mathcal{L}}[\tilde{y}]=\frac{1}{u_{m+1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\tilde{D}_{m}\tilde{D}_{m-1}\cdots\tilde{D}_{3}\tilde{D}_{2}\frac{1}{u_{1}}\frac{\partial\tilde{y}}{\partial t}=W.

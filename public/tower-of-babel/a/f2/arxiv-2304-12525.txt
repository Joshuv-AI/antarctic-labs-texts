Searching for high-frequency axion in quantum electromagnetodynamics through interface haloscopes
Tong Li, Chang-Jie Dai, Rui-Jia Zhang

Abstract
The so-called Witten effect implies a close relationship between axion and magnetic monopole. A sound quantization in the presence of magnetic monopoles, called quantum electromagnetodynamics (QEMD), was utilized to construct a more generic axion-photon Lagrangian in the low-energy axion effective field theory. This generic axion-photon Lagrangian introduces the interactions between axion and two four-potentials, and leads to new axion-modified Maxwell equations. The interface haloscopes place an interface between two electromagnetic media with different properties and are desirable to search for high-mass axions m a ≳ 𝒪 ⁡ ( 10 ) ​ μ ​ eV m_{a}\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(10)~\mu{\rm eV} . In this work, for the generic axion-photon couplings built under QEMD, we perform comprehensive calculations of the axion-induced propagating waves and energy flux densities in different interface setups. We also obtain the sensitivity to new axion-photon couplings for high-mass axions.

The traditional interface haloscopes place a disc (or multiple discs) between which there are two dielectric media with different permittivity in a background magnetic field. The axion-induced electric field on either side jumps at the interface surface. II.2
The axion modified Maxwell equations and their macroscopic form

According to the above Lagrangian for generic axion-photon interactions, we can derive the classical equations of motion for the photon field. By applying the Euler-Lagrange equation of motion for the two potentials, one obtains

\displaystyle{1\over n^{2}}(n\cdot\partial n\cdot\partial A^{\mu}-n\cdot\partial\partial^{\mu}n\cdot A-n\cdot\partial n^{\mu}\partial\cdot A-n\cdot\partial\epsilon^{\mu}_{\nu\kappa\lambda}n^{\nu}\partial^{\kappa}B^{\lambda})

\displaystyle-g_{aEE}\partial_{\nu}a(\partial\wedge\tilde{A})^{\nu\mu}-g_{aEM}\partial_{\nu}a(\partial\wedge\tilde{B})^{\nu\mu}=j_{e}^{\mu}\;,

(4)

\displaystyle{1\over n^{2}}(n\cdot\partial n\cdot\partial B^{\mu}-n\cdot\partial\partial^{\mu}n\cdot B-n\cdot\partial n^{\mu}\partial\cdot B+n\cdot\partial\epsilon^{\mu}_{\nu\kappa\lambda}n^{\nu}\partial^{\kappa}A^{\lambda})

\displaystyle-g_{aMM}\partial_{\nu}a(\partial\wedge\tilde{B})^{\nu\mu}-g_{aEM}\partial_{\nu}a(\partial\wedge\tilde{A})^{\nu\mu}=j_{m}^{\mu}\;.

(5)

In terms of the field strength tensors
F^{\mu\nu}
and
\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu}-g_{aEE}\partial_{\mu}a\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}+g_{aEM}\partial_{\mu}aF^{\mu\nu}=j_{e}^{\nu}\;,

(6)

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}+g_{aMM}\partial_{\mu}aF^{\mu\nu}-g_{aEM}\partial_{\mu}a\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}=j_{m}^{\nu}\;,

(7)

where the term responsible for Witten effect is omitted.
The new Maxwell equations in terms of electric and magnetic fields are then given by

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{B}-{\partial\vec{E}\over\partial t}=\vec{j}_{e}+g_{aEE}(\vec{E}\times\vec{\nabla}a-{\partial a\over\partial t}\vec{B})+g_{aEM}(\vec{B}\times\vec{\nabla}a+{\partial a\over\partial t}\vec{E})\;,

(8)

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{E}+{\partial\vec{B}\over\partial t}=\vec{j}_{m}-g_{aMM}(\vec{B}\times\vec{\nabla}a+{\partial a\over\partial t}\vec{E})-g_{aEM}(\vec{E}\times\vec{\nabla}a-{\partial a\over\partial t}\vec{B})\;,

(9)

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{B}=\rho_{m}-g_{aMM}\vec{E}\cdot\vec{\nabla}a+g_{aEM}\vec{B}\cdot\vec{\nabla}a\;,

(10)

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{E}=\rho_{e}+g_{aEE}\vec{B}\cdot\vec{\nabla}a-g_{aEM}\vec{E}\cdot\vec{\nabla}a\;,

(11)

where the magnetic charge

Next, we derive the macroscopic form of the above Maxwell equations in order to deal with the propagating wave in media. Let’s first recall the classical electromagnetism. In media, both the electric charge and the current are composed of a free part and a bound part

\displaystyle\rho_{e}=\rho_{e,f}+\rho_{e,b}\;,~~~\vec{j}_{e}=\vec{j}_{e,f}+\vec{j}_{e,b}\;.

(12)

\displaystyle\rho_{e,b}=-\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{P}_{e}\;,~~~\vec{j}_{e,b}=\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{M}_{e}+{\partial\vec{P}_{e}\over\partial t}\;.

(13)

Here
\vec{P}_{e}
and
\vec{M}_{e}
denote the macroscopic polarization and magnetization, respectively. Moreover, the free parts satisfy the continuity equation

\displaystyle{\partial\rho_{e,f}\over\partial t}+\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{j}_{e,f}=0\;.

(14)

The macroscopic electric displacement field
\vec{D}
and the macroscopic magnetic field
\vec{H}

\displaystyle\vec{D}=\vec{E}+\vec{P}_{e}\;,~~~\vec{H}=\vec{B}-\vec{M}_{e}\;.

(15)

8

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{H}-{\partial\vec{D}\over\partial t}=\vec{j}_{e,f}+g_{aEE}(\vec{E}\times\vec{\nabla}a-{\partial a\over\partial t}\vec{B})+g_{aEM}(\vec{B}\times\vec{\nabla}a+{\partial a\over\partial t}\vec{E})\;.

(16)

\vec{E}

\displaystyle\vec{\nabla}\cdot\vec{D}=\rho_{e,f}+g_{aEE}\vec{B}\cdot\vec{\nabla}a-g_{aEM}\vec{E}\cdot\vec{\nabla}a\;.

\displaystyle\vec{k}\times\hat{\vec{E}}_{\gamma a}-\omega\hat{\vec{B}}_{\gamma a}=-g_{aMM}\vec{B}_{0}\times\vec{k}\hat{a}-g_{aEM}\omega\hat{a}\vec{B}_{0}\;,

\displaystyle\vec{k}\cdot\hat{\vec{B}}_{\gamma a}=g_{aEM}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{B}_{0}\hat{a}\;,

\displaystyle\epsilon\vec{k}\cdot\hat{\vec{E}}_{\gamma a}=g_{aEE}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{B}_{0}\hat{a}\;,

(40)

where
\vec{k}
is a general symbol of wave vector which should be specified for particular fields below.

Then, in the limit of
\vec{k}_{a}=0
, we obtain the axion-induced electromagnetic fields

\displaystyle\vec{E}_{a}(t)={1\over\epsilon}g_{aEE}\vec{B}_{0}a(t)\;,~~\vec{B}_{a}(t)=\mu\vec{H}_{a}(t)=g_{aEM}\vec{B}_{0}a(t)\;,

(41)

as well as the propagating wave equations

\displaystyle\vec{k}_{\gamma}\times\vec{H}_{\gamma}+\omega\epsilon\vec{E}_{\gamma}=0\;,

(42)

\displaystyle\vec{k}_{\gamma}\times\vec{E}_{\gamma}-\omega\vec{B}_{\gamma}=\vec{k}_{\gamma}\times\vec{E}_{\gamma}-\omega\mu\vec{H}_{\gamma}=0\;,

(43)

where
\vec{k}_{\gamma}
denotes the wave vector of a propagating electromagnetic field with
k_{\gamma}^{2}=n^{2}\omega^{2}
and
n^{2}=\epsilon\mu
.
Here and below, we work in the approximation of axion with zero velocity
\vec{k}_{a}=0
and frequency
\omega_{a}\approx m_{a}
. The axion DM field can be given by

\displaystyle a(t)=a_{0}e^{-im_{a}t}\;,

(44)

where
a_{0}=\sqrt{2\rho_{\rm DM}}/m_{a}
with
\rho_{\rm DM}=0.4~{\rm GeV}~{\rm cm}^{-3}
being the local DM density.

For the case of different dielectrics with
\epsilon_{1}\neq\epsilon_{2}
, we obtain the axion-induced electromagnetic fields

\displaystyle\vec{H}_{a}^{I}=\vec{H}_{a}^{II}={1\over\mu}g_{aEM}\vec{B}_{0}a_{0}e^{-im_{a}t}\;,~~\vec{E}_{a}^{I(II)}={1\over\epsilon_{1(2)}}g_{aEE}\vec{B}_{0}a_{0}e^{-im_{a}t}\;,

(45)

and the field values from continuity conditions become

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}^{I}+E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{I}=E_{\gamma}^{II}+E_{a}^{II}+E_{0}^{II}\;,

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{I}=H_{\gamma}^{II}\Rightarrow-{\epsilon_{1}\over n_{1}}E_{\gamma}^{I}={\epsilon_{2}\over n_{2}}E_{\gamma}^{II}\;,

(46)

where
H_{\gamma}
is produced in the direction perpendicular to
E_{\gamma}
.
Inserting the obtained axion-induced fields to the above two equations, the solutions of propagating waves in the two regions are

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}^{I}=+\Big[E_{a}^{II}-E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{II}-E_{0}^{I}\Big]{\epsilon_{2}n_{1}\over\epsilon_{1}n_{2}+\epsilon_{2}n_{1}}\;,

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}^{II}=-\Big[E_{a}^{II}-E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{II}-E_{0}^{I}\Big]{\epsilon_{1}n_{2}\over\epsilon_{1}n_{2}+\epsilon_{2}n_{1}}\;,

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{I}=-\Big[E_{a}^{II}-E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{II}-E_{0}^{I}\Big]{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\over\epsilon_{1}n_{2}+\epsilon_{2}n_{1}}\;,

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{II}=-\Big[E_{a}^{II}-E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{II}-E_{0}^{I}\Big]{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\over\epsilon_{1}n_{2}+\epsilon_{2}n_{1}}\;,

(47)

where

\displaystyle\Big[\cdots\Big]=\Big({1\over\epsilon_{2}}-{1\over\epsilon_{1}}\Big)g_{aEE}B_{0}a_{0}\;.

(48)

Thus, in this case, the external static magnetic field
B_{0}
can be set to measure
g_{aEE}
coupling.

In this case, after applying the ordinary equations for static fields, the macroscopic Maxwell equations in a Fourier expansion become

\displaystyle\vec{k}\times\hat{\vec{E}}_{\gamma a}-\omega\hat{\vec{B}}_{\gamma a}=g_{aMM}\omega\vec{E}_{0}\hat{a}-g_{aEM}\vec{E}_{0}\times\vec{k}\hat{a}\;,

\displaystyle\vec{k}\cdot\hat{\vec{B}}_{\gamma a}=-g_{aMM}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{E}_{0}\hat{a}\;,

\displaystyle\vec{k}\cdot(\omega\hat{\vec{D}}+i\hat{\vec{j}}^{\prime}_{e,f})=-g_{aEM}\omega\vec{k}\cdot\vec{E}_{0}\hat{a}\;.

(49)

As
\dot{\vec{E}}_{0}=0
, we can also define the following relation in the media

\displaystyle\hat{\vec{H}}_{\gamma a}={\hat{\vec{B}}_{\gamma a}\over\mu}\;,~~~\omega\hat{\vec{D}}+i\hat{\vec{j}}^{\prime}_{e,f}=\omega\hat{\vec{D}}_{\gamma a}+i\hat{\vec{j}}^{\prime}_{e,f}=\omega\epsilon\vec{E}_{\gamma a}\;.

(50)

Then, we get the linearized macroscopic axion equations

\displaystyle\vec{k}\times{\hat{\vec{B}}_{\gamma a}\over\mu}+\omega\epsilon\hat{\vec{E}}_{\gamma a}=g_{aEE}\vec{E}_{0}\times\vec{k}\hat{a}-g_{aEM}\omega\vec{E}_{0}\hat{a}\;,

\displaystyle\vec{k}\times\hat{\vec{E}}_{\gamma a}-\omega\hat{\vec{B}}_{\gamma a}=g_{aMM}\omega\vec{E}_{0}\hat{a}-g_{aEM}\vec{E}_{0}\times\vec{k}\hat{a}\;,

\displaystyle\vec{k}\cdot\hat{\vec{B}}_{\gamma a}=-g_{aMM}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{E}_{0}\hat{a}\;,

\displaystyle\epsilon\vec{k}\cdot\hat{\vec{E}}_{\gamma a}=-g_{aEM}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{E}_{0}\hat{a}\;.

(51)

In the limit of
\vec{k}_{a}=0
, we obtain the axion-induced electromagnetic fields

\displaystyle\vec{E}_{a}(t)=-{1\over\epsilon}g_{aEM}\vec{E}_{0}a(t)\;,~~\vec{B}_{a}(t)=\mu\vec{H}_{a}(t)=-g_{aMM}\vec{E}_{0}a(t)\;,

(52)

as well as the propagating wave equations also apply here.

For the case of different dielectrics with
\epsilon_{1}\neq\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle\vec{H}_{a}^{I}=\vec{H}_{a}^{II}=-{1\over\mu}g_{aMM}\vec{E}_{0}a_{0}e^{-im_{a}t}\;,~~\vec{E}_{a}^{I(II)}=-{1\over\epsilon_{1(2)}}g_{aEM}\vec{E}_{0}a_{0}e^{-im_{a}t}\;,

(53)

and the field values from continuity conditions become

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}^{I}+E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{I}=E_{\gamma}^{II}+E_{a}^{II}+E_{0}^{II}\;,

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{I}=H_{\gamma}^{II}\Rightarrow-{\epsilon_{1}\over n_{1}}E_{\gamma}^{I}={\epsilon_{2}\over n_{2}}E_{\gamma}^{II}\;.

(54)

Inserting the obtained axion-induced fields to the above two equations, the solutions of propagating waves in the two regions are

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}^{I}=+\Big[E_{a}^{II}-E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{II}-E_{0}^{I}\Big]{\epsilon_{2}n_{1}\over\epsilon_{1}n_{2}+\epsilon_{2}n_{1}}\;,

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}^{II}=-\Big[E_{a}^{II}-E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{II}-E_{0}^{I}\Big]{\epsilon_{1}n_{2}\over\epsilon_{1}n_{2}+\epsilon_{2}n_{1}}\;,

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{I}=-\Big[E_{a}^{II}-E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{II}-E_{0}^{I}\Big]{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\over\epsilon_{1}n_{2}+\epsilon_{2}n_{1}}\;,

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{II}=-\Big[E_{a}^{II}-E_{a}^{I}+E_{0}^{II}-E_{0}^{I}\Big]{\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\over\epsilon_{1}n_{2}+\epsilon_{2}n_{1}}\;,

(55)

where

\displaystyle\Big[\cdots\Big]=-\Big({1\over\epsilon_{2}}-{1\over\epsilon_{1}}\Big)g_{aEM}E_{0}a_{0}\;.

(56)

Thus, in this case, the external static electric field
E_{0}
can be set to measure
g_{aEM}
coupling.

III.2
Poynting’s theorem and the energy flux density

The energy flux density of an EM propagating field is given by the Poynting’s theorem as

\displaystyle\vec{S}_{\gamma}=\vec{E}_{\gamma}\times\vec{H}_{\gamma}\;.

(67)

Next, we give the results of energy flux density for the above viable cases (case 1, 2 and 4).

The propagating waves in Case 1 are

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}^{I}

\displaystyle=g_{aEE}B_{0}a_{0}\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}+\epsilon_{2}\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}}\;,

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}^{II}

\displaystyle=-g_{aEE}B_{0}a_{0}\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}+\epsilon_{2}\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}}\;,

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{I}=H_{\gamma}^{II}

\displaystyle=-g_{aEE}B_{0}a_{0}\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}+\epsilon_{2}\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}}\;,

(68)

where
\epsilon_{1}=n_{1}^{2}
and
\epsilon_{2}=n_{2}^{2}
.
After plugging them into the Poynting’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{S}^{i}_{\gamma}=\mp\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{i}}}\frac{(g_{aEE}B_{0}a_{0})^{2}}{2}\Big(\frac{\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2}}{\epsilon_{1}\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}+\epsilon_{2}\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}}\Big)^{2}=\mp\frac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon_{i}}}\frac{(g_{aEE}B_{0}a_{0})^{2}}{2}\Big({1\over\sqrt{\epsilon_{2}}}-{1\over\sqrt{\epsilon_{1}}}\Big)^{2}\;,

(69)

where
i=I
(upper sign) or
II
(lower sign), and we take into account a factor of
1/2
to obtain the time-averaged value.
For Case 2, one just needs to make a replacement of
g_{aEE}B_{0}\to-g_{aEM}E_{0}
in Case 1’s results.

The propagating waves in Case 4 are

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{I}

\displaystyle=-g_{aMM}E_{0}a_{0}\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}+\mu_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\;,

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}^{II}

\displaystyle=g_{aMM}E_{0}a_{0}\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}+\mu_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{2}}}\;,

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}^{I}=E_{\gamma}^{II}

\displaystyle=g_{aMM}E_{0}a_{0}\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}+\mu_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\;.

(70)

After plugging them into the Poynting’s theorem, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{S}^{i}_{\gamma}=\mp\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{i}}}\frac{(g_{aMM}E_{0}a_{0})^{2}}{2}\Big(\frac{\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}}{\mu_{1}\sqrt{\mu_{2}}+\mu_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\Big)^{2}=\mp\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{i}}}\frac{(g_{aMM}E_{0}a_{0})^{2}}{2}\Big({1\over\sqrt{\mu_{2}}}-{1\over\sqrt{\mu_{1}}}\Big)^{2}\;.

(71)

IV
Sensitivity of interface haloscopes to new axion couplings in QEMD

In this section, based on the above energy flux density and signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), we calculate the sensitivity of interface haloscopes to the new axion couplings in QEMD. Following Ref.
Millar et al. 2017
, we assume an extreme case with medium II being vacuum (
n_{2}=1
) and thus take the interface as a perfect mirror to emit radiation into vacuum region II. Moreover, as pointed out by Ref.
Millar et al. 2017
, the axion-induced electromagnetic wave can be boosted with a series of parallel interfaces between different media.
The outgoing wave then becomes a coherent superposition of the transmission and reflection at each interface. We follow Ref.
Caldwell et al. 2017
;
Millar et al. 2017
to choose an experimental setup of 80 disks and take a boost factor
\beta
to describe this effect. The signal power can be given by

\displaystyle P_{\rm signal}=A\beta^{2}\eta\overline{S}^{II}_{\gamma}\;,

(72)

where
\eta=0.8
is power efficiency, and
A=1~{\rm m}^{2}
is disk area.
To measure this signal, we choose HEMT amplifiers with
T_{\rm sys}=8~{\rm K}
and the estimated noise is

\displaystyle P_{\rm noise}=T_{\rm sys}\sqrt{\frac{\Delta\nu}{\Delta t}}\;,

(73)

where a suitable measurement time is
\Delta t=1
day, and a signal bandwidth is given by the frequency range
\Delta\nu=\Delta\omega_{a}/2\pi\sim 10^{-6}m_{a}/2\pi
.

\beta
is obtained from Area Law:
\beta^{2}\Delta\nu_{\beta}=K
with
K
being a constant associated with experimental setup
Millar et al. 2017
.

The SNR is required to satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{P_{\rm signal}}{P_{\rm noise}}>5\;.

(74)

The total run time is given by
t_{\rm tot}=(\Delta t+t_{R})\frac{\Delta m_{a}}{\Delta\nu_{\beta}}
for a suitable range of axion mass
\Delta m_{a}
starting from
40~\mu{\rm eV}
. We take
t_{R}=1
day as a conservative estimated value of readjust time before each detection and
\Delta\nu_{\beta}=50~{\rm MHz}
with
t_{\rm tot}=5
years as a reasonable choice to balance the total run time and detection range. This choice results in
\beta\simeq\mathcal{O}(100)

Caldwell et al. 2017
;
Millar et al. 2017
. We keep this
\beta
value unchanged in whole experimental process, and then plug the above parameters into SNR formula to obtained the sensitivity bound of new axion couplings. In Fig.
2
, after taking a few experimental setups for illustration, we show the sensitivity bound of couplings
g_{aEE}
,
g_{aEM}
and
g_{aMM}
together with their theoretical predictions. We find that the reasonable setups of interface haloscopes with perfect mirror can probe the theoretical predictions of
g_{aEE}
,
g_{aEM}
and
g_{aMM}
for
\mathcal{O}(10)~\mu{\rm eV}\lesssim m_{a}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(100)~\mu{\rm eV}
.
It turns out that a large background electric field is needed to probe
g_{aEM}
and
g_{aMM}
. The large static electric field could induce
extra currents/charges in the detector volume depending on the design and therefore may influence the sensitivity. We leave the experimental design to future study.

V
Conclusion

The so-called
Witten effect implies the relationship between axion and magnetic monopole. A sound QFT theory called QEMD in the presence of magnetic monopoles was utilized to construct a more generic axion-photon Lagrangian in the low-energy axion effective field theory. This generic axion-photon Lagrangian introduces the interactions between axion and two four-potentials. It leads to new axion-modified Maxwell equations. To search for high-mass axions
m_{a}\gtrsim\mathcal{O}(10)~\mu{\rm eV}
, the interface haloscopes were proposed by placing an interface between two electromagnetic media with different properties.

In this work, for the generic axion-photon couplings built under QEMD, we provide comprehensive expressions for the axion-induced electromagnetic fields and propagating waves in different setups of interface and background fields. We also calculate energy flux densities, the signal-to-noise ratio and obtain the sensitivity to new axion-photon couplings (
g_{aEE}
,
g_{aEM}
and
g_{aMM}
) for high-mass axions. We find

•

The configuration of interface between two dielectric regions and a parallel static magnetic (electric) field can measure
g_{aEE}
(
g_{aEM}
) coupling.

•

The configuration of interface between two regions with magnetic material and a parallel static electric field can measure
g_{aMM}
coupling.

•

A reasonable setup of interface haloscopes with perfect mirror can probe the theoretical predictions of
g_{aEE}
,
g_{aEM}
and
g_{aMM}
for
\mathcal{O}(10)~\mu{\rm eV}\lesssim m_{a}\lesssim\mathcal{O}(100)~\mu{\rm eV}
.

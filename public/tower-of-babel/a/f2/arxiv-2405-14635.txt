The Defective Parking Space and Defective Kreweras Numbers
Rebecca E. Garcia, Pamela E. Harris, Alex Moon, Aaron Ortiz, Lauren J. Quesada, Cynthia Marie Rivera Sánchez, Dwight Anderson Williams II and Alexander N. Wilson

Abstract
A defective ( m , n ) (m,n) -parking function with defect d d is a parking function with m m cars attempting to park on a street with n n parking spots in which exactly d d cars fail to park. We establish a way to compute the defect of a defective ( m , n ) (m,n) -parking function and show that the defect of a parking function is invariant under the action of 𝔖 m \mathfrak{S}_{m} , the symmetric group on [ m ] = { 1 , 2 , … , m } [m]=\{1,2,\ldots,m\} . We introduce the defective parking space 𝖣𝖯𝖺𝗋𝗄 m , n {{\sf DPark}}_{m,n} spanned by defective parking functions and describe its Frobenius characteristic as an 𝔖 m \mathfrak{S}_{m} representation graded by defect via coefficients Krew d , n ​ ( λ ) \mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda) called defective Kreweras numbers. We provide a conjectured formula for Krew d , n ​ ( λ ) \mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda) for sufficiently large n n . We also show that the set of nondecreasing defective ( m , n ) (m,n) -parking functions with defect d d are in bijection with the set of standard Young tableaux of shape ( n + d , m − d ) (n+d,m-d) . This implies that the number of 𝔖 m \mathfrak{S}_{m} -orbits of defective ( m , n ) (m,n) -parking functions with defect d d is given by n − m + 2 ​ d + 1 n + d + 1 ​ ( m + n n + d ) \frac{n-m+2d+1}{n+d+1}\binom{m+n}{n+d} . We also give a multinomial formula for the size of an 𝔖 m \mathfrak{S}_{m} -orbit of a nondecreasing ( m , n ) (m,n) -parking function with defect d d . We conclude by using these results to give a new formula for the number of defective parking functions.

Given

Remark 1.4
.

In the study of parking functions, most authors add the restriction that there not be more cars than spots — indeed if
m>n
then some amount of cars fail to park. In our case, however, we have no such restriction. When there are more cars than spots, we simply observe that defect
d
is positive and no less than
m-n
.

We recall that an important feature of parking functions is that they are invariant under the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
, the symmetric group on
[m]
, see
[
21
, §4 Example 1]
.
Namely, given
\alpha=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathrm{PF}_{m,n}
and any
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}
, the tuple

\pi\cdot\alpha=(a_{\pi^{-1}(1)},a_{\pi^{-1}(2)},\ldots,a_{\pi^{-1}(m)})\in\mathrm{PF}_{m,n}.

It is a well-documented result (see
[
6
]
for a proof) that the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-orbits of
\mathrm{PF}_{n,n}

are enumerated by the Catalan numbers, which are defined by
[
32
,
A000108
]
:

{\mathcal{C}}_{n}=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.

There are multiple ways to consider parking functions with unequal number of cars and spots. Armstrong, Loehr, and Warrington
[
5
]
consider the case where

m
and
n
are coprime and in which case a car takes up a fraction of a spot.
In this paper, we allow for
m
and
n
to be any positive integers and for each car to occupy exactly one spot.
Our first result establishes that the defect of a parking function is invariant under the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
, although this fact is already implicit in
[
9
]
.
This invariance of defect allows us to consider
{{\sf DPark}}_{m,n}

\mathfrak{S}_{m}
acting on defective parking functions graded by defect.

The graded Frobenius characteristic

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}_{t}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n})

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84526pt=\hskip-2.84526pt\sum_{\lambda\vdash m}\hskip-2.84526pt\left(\sum_{i=0}^{m-n}\hskip-2.84526pt\mathrm{Krew}_{i,n}(\lambda)+\hskip-8.53581pt\sum_{d>m-n}\hskip-8.53581pt\mathrm{Krew}_{d-(m-n),n}(\lambda)t^{d}\hskip-2.84526pt\right)\hskip-2.84526pth_{\lambda},

We then establish the following bijection.

The set of nondecreasing defective parking functions of defect

Proof 2.9
.

There are
n-m

\max(m-n,0)

m

The maximum possible defect for any preference list in

Hence
\max(m-n,0)\leq\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x})\leq m

Next we prove that in fact the defect of a nondecreasing parking list
\mathbf{x}
is always the maximum entry in

Lemma 2.10

If
\mathbf{x}

[n+1]^{m}

\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x})=\max(\delta(\mathbf{x}),0).

Proof 2.11
.

Consider first the case of
m
cars parking in an infinite parking lot with a nondecreasing preference list. We call a spot
wasted
if there is no car in that spot and there is a car in some spot to its right. If we write
w_{k}
for the number of wasted spots immediately after car
k

k
satisfies
p_{k}=k+w_{k}
.

Because the preference list is nondecreasing, the number of new wasted spots created when car
k+1
parks is given by the number of spots after
p_{k}
and before
x_{k+1}
. That is,

\displaystyle w_{k+1}-w_{k}

\displaystyle=\max\{x_{k+1}-p_{k}-1,0\}

\displaystyle=\max\{x_{k+1}-(k+1)-w_{k},0\}.

Then, adding
w_{k}
to both sides yields

\displaystyle w_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\max\{x_{k+1}-(k+1),w_{k}\}.

Because
w_{1}=x_{1}-1
and each
w_{k}
is obtained recursively as above, we have that

\displaystyle w_{k}

\displaystyle=\max_{j\in[k]}\{x_{j}-j\}

for
1\leq k\leq m
.

Note that if no car’s preference exceeds
n+1
, then there can be no wasted spaces past spot
n
. In this case, the number of cars that park in a position larger than
n
is computed as
n
less than the parking position of car
m
(or zero if this subtraction is negative). That is,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\max\{p_{m}-n,0\}

\displaystyle=\max\{m+w_{m}-n,0\}

\displaystyle=\max_{j\in[m]}\{x_{j}-j+(m-n),0\}

\displaystyle=\max_{j\in[m]}(\delta(\mathbf{x})_{j},0).

Defective Parking Spaces

In this section, we consider permutation representations of the symmetric group arising from defective parking functions. We assume familiarity with the representation theory of the symmetric group and symmetric function terminology (see
[
33
]
for a reference on these topics).

For a set
S
, let
\mathbb{C}S
denote the vector space of formal linear combinations of elements of
S
. Let
{{\sf Park}}_{m,n}=\mathbb{C}\mathrm{PF}_{m,n}
. The
classical parking space

{{\sf Park}}_{n,n}
appears in formulas for the decomposition of the
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
-representation
D=R/(R_{+}^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}})
where

R=\mathbb{C}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{m},y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{m}]

and
R_{+}^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\subseteq R
is the ideal generated by elements of
R
without a constant term that are invariant under simultaneous permutation of the variables
x_{i}
and
y_{i}
. This space is called the
diagonal coinvariants
(see
[
27
]
).

The
Frobenius characteristic map
is an isomorphism between the ring
R
of characters for symmetric group representations and the ring
\Lambda
of symmetric functions. Identifying a representation
V
of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
with its character function
\chi_{V}:\mathfrak{S}_{m}\to\mathbb{C}
, we write

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}(V)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\chi_{V}(\sigma)p_{c(\sigma)}

where
c(\sigma)
is the integer partition denoting the cycle type of
\sigma
and
p_{\lambda}
are the
power sum symmetric functions
. For our purposes, the basis of
\Lambda
we are most interested in is that of the
complete homogeneous symmetric functions

h_{\lambda}
, which are the image of the induction of the trivial representation
\mathbb{1}
of the Young subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}=\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{1}}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{2}}\times\cdots\times\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda_{k}}
to
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
. That is,

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}=\mathrm{ch}\left(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{S}_{\lambda}}^{\mathfrak{S}_{m}}(\mathbb{1})\right)

for
\lambda\vdash m
.

For
\lambda\vdash m
of length
k
let

(3)

be the

of

Definition 3.1
.

Let
{{\sf DPark}}_{m,n}=\mathbb{C}[n+1]^{m}
. This vector space has a grading by defect. That is, we let

{{\sf DPark}}_{m,n}^{(d)}=\mathbb{C}\{\mathbf{x}:\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x})=d\},

and we call
{{\sf DPark}}_{m,n}
with this grading the
defective parking space
.

By
Theorem

1.2
each subspace
{{\sf DPark}}_{m,n}^{(d)}
is an
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
-representation, so we can compute a graded Frobenius characteristic

\mathrm{ch}_{t}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n})=\sum_{d=\max(m-n,0)}^{m}\mathrm{ch}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n}^{(d)})t^{d}.

Remark 3.2
.

This graded Frobenius characteristic
\mathrm{ch}_{t}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n})
has two interesting specializations:

1.

When
t=0
, we have that
\mathrm{ch}_{0}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n})=\mathrm{ch}({{\sf Park}}_{m,n})
where
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
acts by permuting the order of the preference list.

2.

When

, we have that
\mathrm{ch}_{1}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n})=\mathrm{ch}(\left(\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\right)^{\otimes m})
where
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
acts by permuting tensor factors.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\alpha=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n+1})
a weak composition of
m
, let
V_{\alpha}\subseteq{{\sf DPark}}_{m,n}
be the span of words

where the multiplicity of

in

\alpha_{i}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}(V_{\alpha})=h_{\alpha}.

Proof 3.4
.

The action of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
on
V_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to the permutation representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
acting by left-multiplication on the right cosets of the Young subgroup
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathfrak{S}_{m}
. Hence,
V_{\alpha}
is isomorphic to the induction of the trivial representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{\alpha}
to
\mathfrak{S}_{m}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Given

, define the
defective Kreweras number

of a partition

of length

\mathbf{x}=({a_{1}}^{\lambda_{\sigma(1)}},{a_{2}}^{\lambda_{\sigma(2)}},\ldots,{a_{k}}^{\lambda_{\sigma(k)}})

\lambda_{\sigma(i)}

Remark 3.6

When

Theorem 3.8
.

The graded Frobenius characteristic
\mathrm{ch}_{t}({{\sf DPark}}_{n,n})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}_{t}({{\sf DPark}}_{n,n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\vdash n}\sum_{d\geq 0}\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda)t^{d}h_{\lambda}.

More generally, the graded Frobenius characteristic
\mathrm{ch}_{t}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}_{t}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip-36.135pt\sum_{\lambda\vdash m}\left(\sum_{d=0}^{n-m}\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda)+\sum_{d>m-n}\mathrm{Krew}_{d-(m-n),n}(\lambda)t^{d}\right)h_{\lambda}.

Proof 3.9
.

For
\alpha
a weak composition of
m
, let

\mathbf{x}(\alpha)=(1^{\alpha_{1}},2^{\alpha_{2}},\ldots).

Moreover, let
\alpha\vDash_{j}k
denote a weak composition of
k
into
j
parts. By
Lemma

3.3
, we compute the graded Frobenius characteristic
\mathrm{ch}_{t}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n})
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{ch}_{t}({{\sf DPark}}_{m,n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha\,\vDash_{n+1}\,m}t^{\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x}(\alpha))}h_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda\,\vdash\,m}a_{\lambda,n}(t)h_{\lambda}

where

\displaystyle a_{\lambda,n}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\,\vDash_{n+1}\,m\\
\mathrm{sort}(\alpha)=\lambda\end{subarray}}t^{\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x}(\alpha))}.

Note that
a_{\lambda,n}(t)=\sum_{d\geq 0}c_{d}t^{d}
where for
d\geq 0
,
c_{d}
is the number of nondecreasing tuples
\mathbf{x}\in[n+1]^{m}
with multiplicities of entries given by
\lambda
and
\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x})=d
. As
\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x})=\max_{j\in[m]}(x_{j}-j+(m-n),0)
, each tuple
\mathbf{x}
with
\max_{j\in[m]}(x_{j}-j)\leq m-n
contributes to
c_{0}
, hence

c_{0}=\sum_{d=0}^{m-n}\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda).

For
d>m-n
, we have that
c_{d}=\mathrm{Krew}_{d-(m-n),n}(\lambda)
.

We conclude this section with results on interpreting and computing the defective Kreweras numbers
\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda)
. We recall that an
(n,m)
-lattice path is a path from
(0,0)
to
(n,m)
consisting of
(0,1)
steps (called north steps and denoted by

) and
(1,0)
steps (called east steps and denoted by

). Such a path is typically denoted by a word with
m

N

n

E

\mathbf{w}
, we let
n_{i}
denote the position of the
i

\mathbf{w}
. The preference list
\mathbf{x}
corresponding to
\mathbf{w}
is defined by

(4)

\displaystyle x_{i}=1+\left|\{j\in[m+n]\mid w_{j}=E,j<n_{i}\}\right|.

In other words, for each
i\in[m]
, the entry
x_{i}
is one more than the number of east steps before the
i
th north step. For an
(n,m)
-lattice path
\mathbf{w}
, let
\mathrm{dip}(\mathbf{w})
be the vertical distance from the line

to the lowest point of
\mathbf{w}
on or below the line
y=x
(where
\mathrm{dip}(\mathbf{w})=0
if no such point exists). Let
\mathrm{runs}(\mathbf{w})\vdash m
be the integer partition consisting of the sizes of runs of the north steps of

.

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\lambda\vdash m
. The defective Kreweras number
\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda)
is the number of
(n,m)
-lattice paths with
\mathrm{dip}(\mathbf{w})=d
and
\mathrm{runs}(\mathbf{w})=\lambda
.

Proof 3.12
.

Given an
(n,m)
-lattice path
\mathbf{w}
, let
\mathbf{x}
be its corresponding nondecreasing preference list
\mathbf{x}\in[n+1]^{m}
given by Equation
4
. Note that each north step in a run of north steps has the same number of east steps before it. Hence,

\mathrm{runs}(\mathbf{w})=\mathrm{sort}(\mu_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\mu_{2}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,\mu_{n+1}(\mathbf{x}))

where
\mu_{i}(\mathbf{x})
is the number of times the number
i
appears in the tuple
\mathbf{x}
, hence
\mathbf{x}
is of the form given in
Definition

3.5
.

Note also that just before the
i
th north step, there have been
x_{i}-1
east steps and
i-1
north steps. If the point at the bottom of this north step is below the line

, its horizontal distance (and hence the vertical distance) to the line

is given by
x_{i}-1-(i-1)=x_{i}-i
. Hence,
\mathrm{dip}(\mathbf{w})=\max_{j\in[m]}(x_{j}-j,0)
.

Next, we provide sufficient conditions for
\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda)
to be zero.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
\lambda\vdash m
have length
k
.

1.

For
n<k-1
, we have that
\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda)=0
for all
d\geq 0
.

2.

For
n\geq k-1
, we have that
\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda)=0
for all
d>m-k+1
.

Proof 3.14
.

If
n<k-1
, then there can be no weak composition of length
n+1
with
\mathrm{sort}(\alpha)=\lambda
. So
\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda)=0
for all
d\geq 0
.

Suppose instead that
n\geq k-1
. If
\mathrm{sort}(\alpha)=\lambda
, then
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}(\alpha)
must be of the form

\mathbf{x}=({a_{1}}^{\lambda_{\sigma(1)}},{a_{2}}^{\lambda_{\sigma(2)}},\ldots,{a_{k}}^{\lambda_{\sigma(k)}})

where
a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{k}
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{k}
. In the run
{a_{i}}^{\lambda_{\sigma(i)}}
of identical values, the largest contribution to
\delta
is at the first of these entries. That is, if we set

b_{i}=a_{i}-\left(\sum_{k=1}^{i-1}\lambda_{\sigma(k)}\right)-1+(m-n)

to be the value of
\delta(\mathbf{x})
at the first index of the
i
th run of identical values, we have that
\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x}(\alpha))=\max(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k},0)
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{x}
has the maximum possible defect among tuples of this form. Note that increasing a value

only increases the corresponding
b_{i}
, so we may assume that
a_{1}=n-k+2
,
a_{2}=n-k+3
, …,
a_{k}=n+1
are their highest possible values. Then, we compute

\displaystyle b_{i+1}-b_{i}

\displaystyle=a_{i+1}-a_{i}-\lambda_{\sigma(i)}

\displaystyle<a_{i+1}-a_{i}

\displaystyle=1.

Hence,
b_{i+1}\leq b_{i}
, meaning

\mathrm{dft}(\mathbf{x})=\max(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k},0)=\max(b_{1},0)=\max(m-k+1,0).

Because
\mathbf{x}
was assumed to have the maximum possible defect among tuples of this form,
\mathrm{Krew}_{d,n}(\lambda)=0
for
d>m-k+1
.

Finally, we have a conjectured formula for

(c.f. Equation

Lemma 4.5

Let

\varphi:\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,d}(x_{n}\leq k)\to N_{n,d-1}(x^{\prime}_{n}\leq k-1)

by

\varphi(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},i),

Then

\varphi
is a bijection.
Moreover,

.

Proof 4.6
.

Given
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,d}
, let
i
denote
\max(D(\mathbf{x}))
, the first index such that
\delta(\mathbf{x})_{i}>0
. Such an index existing follows from
Lemma

2.10
and the fact that
\mathbf{x}
has positive defect. Then choose the map
\varphi
with

\varphi:\mathbf{x}\mapsto((x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i}-1,x_{i+1}-1,\ldots,x_{n}-1),i).

Let
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},\dots,x^{\prime}_{n})=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i}-1,x_{i+1}-1,\ldots,x_{n}-1)
, be the first component of
\varphi(\mathbf{x})
.

•

Well-defined: Since
i
is the smallest index such that

\delta(\mathbf{x})_{i}>0
, this implies that
x_{i}-i>0
and
x_{i-1}-(i-1)\leq 0
.
Hence
x_{i}-1>i-1
and
x_{i-1}\leq i-1
. Thus
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
is still nondecreasing. Also, every index
j
with
\delta(\mathbf{x})_{j}>0
has
j\geq i
by definition, so the
j
th entry of
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
satisfies
x^{\prime}_{j}=x_{j}-1
.
Therefore, by
Lemma

2.10
the defect of
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
satisfies

\max_{1\leq j\leq n}(\delta(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})_{j})=\max_{1\leq j\leq n}(\delta(\mathbf{x})_{j})-1=d-1.

This establishes that
\varphi
is well-defined on the first component.
Also, because
\delta(\mathbf{x})_{i}>0
and
x^{\prime}_{i}=x_{i}-1
, we know
\delta(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})_{i}\geq 0
. If there were some
j<i
with
\delta(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})_{j}>0
, then we would also have
\delta(\mathbf{x})_{j}>0
, as
x^{\prime}_{j}=x_{j}
for all

.
Contradicting the definition of
i
. Hence

and
\varphi
is well-defined on the second component.

•

Injective: Suppose
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}),\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\in\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,d}
and assume that
\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\varphi(\mathbf{y})
. Then the first coordinates are equal, i.e.
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}=\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
. This implies that
x^{\prime}_{j}=y^{\prime}_{j}
for all

.
This together with the definition of
\varphi
implies that

–

if
j<i
, then
y_{j}^{\prime}=x^{\prime}_{j}=x_{j}
, and

–

if
j\geq i
, then
y_{j}^{\prime}=x^{\prime}_{j}=x_{j}-1
.

This implies that
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{y}
.

•

Surjective: Suppose
(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},i)\in N_{n,d-1}
. Let

\mathbf{x}=(x^{\prime}_{1},x^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,x^{\prime}_{i-1},x^{\prime}_{i}+1,x^{\prime}_{i+1}+1,\ldots,x^{\prime}_{n}+1).

Then from the definition of
\varphi
,
\varphi(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},i)
. By proposition
Lemma

2.10

d-1=\max_{j\in[m]}(\delta(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})_{j})
. Let
j
denote an index where
\delta(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})_{j}=d-1
.
From the definition of
D(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
,
i\leq j
. Then
\delta(\mathbf{x})_{j}=d
and this is maximal, so
\mathbf{x}
has defect

.
Finally, because

,
x_{n}\leq k
, so
\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,d}(x_{n}\leq k)
as desired.

Therefore,
\varphi
is a bijection.

To conclude we prove that

by establishing two set containments:

•

: Suppose

,

. Hence, for all

–

–

If

•

Theorem 4.15
.

For fixed
k\in[n+1]
, there is a bijection

\rho:\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,d}(x_{n}\leq k)\to\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n+d,n+d}(x_{n+d}\leq k-d)

such that
\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,d}(x_{n}\leq k)
satisfies

.

Proof 4.16
.

We prove that there exist bijections

\rho_{r}:\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,r}(x_{n}\leq k-d+r)\to\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n+r,n+r}(x_{n+r}\leq k-d)

for
r\geq 0
such that
D(\mathbf{x})=F(\rho_{r}(\mathbf{x}))
, and the desired bijection is obtained as the case when
r=d
.

We proceed by induction on
r\geq 0
. For the base case
r=0
, note that
\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,0}=\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n}
. The elements of the decrement set of a parking function
\mathbf{x}
with no defect are precisely the elements of the fixed point set of
\mathbf{x}
, so we take
\rho_{0}
to be the identity map.

Now for the purpose of induction, suppose there is a bijection

\rho_{r}:\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,r}(x_{n}\leq k-d+r)\to\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n+r,n+r}(x_{n+r}\leq k-d)

such that
D(\mathbf{x})=F(\rho_{r}(\mathbf{x}))
. Because
D(\mathbf{x})=F(\rho_{r}(\mathbf{x}))
, the bijection
\rho_{r}
determines a bijection

\overline{\rho}_{r}:N_{n,r}(x_{n}\leq k-d+r)\to M_{n+r}(x_{n+r}\leq k-d)

where
\overline{\rho}_{r}(\mathbf{x},i)=(\rho_{r}(\mathbf{x}),i)
. From Lemmas
4.5
and
4.12
, we have bijections

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle:\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,r+1}(x_{n}\leq k-d+r+1)\to N_{n,r}(x_{n}\leq k-d+r),\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle:\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n+r+1,n+r+1}(x_{n+r+1}\leq k-d)\to M_{n+r}(x_{n+r}\leq k-d)

that map

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle:\mathbf{x}\mapsto(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},i),\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle:\mathbf{y}\mapsto(\mathbf{y}^{\prime},i)

with

\displaystyle D(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\{j\in D(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\mid j\leq i\},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\{j\in F(\mathbf{y}^{\prime})\mid j\leq i\}.

We then have a bijection

\rho_{r+1}:\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n,r+1}(x_{n}\leq k-d+r+1)\to\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n+r+1,n+r+1}(x_{n+r+1}\leq k-d)

where
\rho_{r+1}=\psi^{-1}\circ\overline{\rho}_{r}\circ\varphi
. It remains only to show that
D(\mathbf{x})=F(\rho_{r+1}(\mathbf{x}))
. Indeed,

\displaystyle F(\rho_{r+1}(\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle=F(\psi^{-1}\circ\overline{\rho}_{r}\circ\varphi(\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle=F(\varphi^{-1}\circ\overline{\rho}_{r}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},i))

\displaystyle=F(\psi^{-1}(\rho_{r}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),i))

\displaystyle=F(\mathbf{y})

where
\psi(\mathbf{y})=(\rho_{r}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),i)
. So,

\displaystyle F(\rho_{r+1}(\mathbf{x}))

\displaystyle=F(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=\{j\in F(\rho_{r}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))\mid j\leq i\}

\displaystyle=\{j\in D(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\mid j\leq i\}

\displaystyle=D(\mathbf{x}).

\sigma:\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n}(x_{n}\leq k)\to\mathrm{SYT}(n,k-1).

Proof 4.30
.

Let
\mathbf{z}
be an element of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
. There is a bijection
\sigma_{1}:\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n}\to\mathcal{D}_{n}
such that if
\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n}
, the
i
th North step in
\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{x})
is preceded by exactly
x_{i}-1
East steps. East steps are appended to the end of the Dyck path to ensure proper length. This is well defined because
x_{i}\leq i
, so the
i
th East step always comes after the
i
th North step. It’s injective because two parking functions that map to the same Dyck path must be equal in every index, and it’s surjective because reversing the process yields a valid parking function.

A bijection
\sigma_{2}:\mathcal{D}_{n}\to\mathrm{SYT}(n,n)
is given in
[
24
, Page 1]
where
for a Dyck path
\mathbf{z}
of semilength
n
, they define the tableau
\sigma_{2}(\mathbf{z})
by putting
i
in the first row if
z_{i}=N
and in the second row if
z_{i}=E
.

Restricting
\sigma_{1}
to
\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n,n}(x_{n}\leq k)
yields a bijection to the set of Dyck paths of semilength
n
that end with
n-k+1
East steps. Restricting
\sigma_{2}
yields a bijection to the set of Young tableaux of shape
(n,n)
such that the numbers
\{n+k+1,\ldots,2n\}
all lie in the second row. Then because we know that the last
n-k+1
entries of the second row are always the same, we can remove those entries to obtain a bijection.

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.6
, which we recall states that the set of nondecreasing defective parking functions of defect
d
, denoted
\mathrm{DPF}_{m,n,d}^{\uparrow},
and the set of standard Young tableaux of shape
(n+d,m-d)
are in bijection.

Proof 4.31

(Proof of Theorem
1.6
)
.

The result follows from the composition of the bijections in Corollary
4.27
and Proposition
4.29
.

5
Enumerative Results

We are now ready to establish a count for the number of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-orbits of defective parking functions. Given the bijection in Theorem
1.6
, it suffices to use the hook length formula to establish the count of Corollary
1.7
.

Proof 5.1

(Proof of Corollary
1.7
)
.

From Corollary
4.27
, there is a bijection

\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{m,n,d}\longleftrightarrow\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n+d,n+d}(x_{n+d}\leq m+1-d).

By Proposition
4.29
,
\mathrm{PF}^{\uparrow}_{n+d,n+d}(x_{n+d}\leq m+1-d)
is in bijection with standard Young tableaux of shape
(n+d,m-d)
. The hook length formula implies

|\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{m,n,d}|=\frac{n-m+2d+1}{n+d+1}\binom{m+n}{n+d}.

We conclude by providing a formula for the size of each orbit.

Theorem 5.2
.

Given a nondecreasing defective parking function
\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{m,n,d}
, let
\ell_{i}
denote the number of elements of
\mathbf{x}
equal to
i
. Then the size of the orbit of defective parking functions with representative
\mathbf{x}
is

\binom{m}{\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ldots,\ell_{n}}=\frac{m!}{\ell_{1}!\ell_{2}!\cdots\ell_{n}!}.

Proof 5.3
.

The size of the orbit is equal to the number of multiset permutations of
\mathbf{x}
, which is given by the multinomial coefficient.

Using our previous results, we establish Theorem
1.8
, thereby giving a new formula for the number of defective parking functions.

Proof 5.4

(Proof of Theorem
1.8
)
.

The formula in equation (
2
) follows directly from summing Theorem
5.2
over
\mathrm{DPF}^{\uparrow}_{m,n,d}
.

6
Future Work

We conclude with the following directions for future research.

1.

We welcome a proof of the conjectured formula for the defective Kreweras numbers as stated in Conjecture
3.15
. If a counterexample is found, we then ask for a correct formula for these numbers.

2.

Our proof of Theorem
1.6
relied on a chain of bijective arguments. We wonder if a more direct bijection can be established.

3.

We studied one expansion of the Frobenius characteristic by introducing the defective Kreweras numbers,
but one could try to expand this symmetric function in other classical bases.
Of particular interest is the expansion in the power-sum basis, which records the number of defective parking functions fixed by a permutation of a particular cycle type.
The first step in this direction is likely a simpler formula than Theorem
1.8
, which solves this question for the case of the identity permutation.

4.

There have been numerous works generalizing parking functions and studying discrete statistics on these sets. We pose the following problems.

(a)

Consider cars of varying lengths (such as parking assortments and sequences
[
11
,
15
]
). Give counts for the number of defective parking assortments and sequences.

(b)

Consider the case where cars have an interval of parking preferences, as in
[
3
]
). Give counts for the number of defective
\ell
-interval parking functions.

(c)

Enumerate defective parking functions based on the number of lucky cars, those cars which park in their preference. When the defect is
d=0
, and
m=n
, there are known product formulas for their enumeration
[
23
]
.

In this paper, we adapt the theory of
\pi
-points for finite group schemes to a theory of

in Theorem

5.
\Pi(\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}))
for
\mathbb{G}
admitting a quasilogarithm

One missing aspect of our understanding of
\Psi_{\tilde{D}}
in (
4.6.1
) is whether or not
it is a homeomorphism. By Proposition
4.6
, this is equivalent to whether or not
\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{D}}
is
surjective. With the added hypothesis that
\mathbb{G}
admits a quasilogarithm and that
p^{r+1}>2dim(\mathbb{G})
,
we prove this surjectivity of
\mathbb{P}_{\tilde{D}}
in Theorem
5.4
.
This enables us to conclude in Theorem
5.6
that
M\mapsto\Pi(\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})_{M}
detects
projectivity: a finite dimensional
\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
-module
M
is projective if and only if

\Pi(\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}))_{M}
is empty.

We first recall the definition of a quasilogarithm for
\mathbb{G}
.

Definition 5.1
.

[
14
]
,
[
5
, Defn 6.2]

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a linear algebraic group over
k
.
A quasilogarithm for
\mathbb{G}
is a
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant map of
k
-schemes

(5.1.1)

L:\mathbb{G}\ \to\ \operatorname{Spec}\nolimits S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\equiv\underline{\mathfrak{g}}

such that
L(e_{\mathbb{G}})=0
and such that the differential
d_{e_{\mathbb{G}}}L:T_{e_{\mathbb{G}}}\mathbb{G}\to T_{0}\underline{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}

is the identity.

We denote by
I_{r}\ \subset\ S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})
the
\mathbb{G}
-stable ideal generated by
\{f^{p^{r}},\ f\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}\}
.

Example 5.2
.

[
2
, Lem C3]
,
[
5
, 6.4]

If
\mathbb{G}
is a simple algebraic group for which
p
is very good, then
\mathbb{G}
admits a quasilogarithm.
Furthermore any Borel subgroup of
\mathbb{G}
also admits a quasilogarithm. If
\mathbb{U}
is a unipotent
subgroup of a semi-simple algebraic group
\mathbb{G}
which is normalized by a maximal torus and if

p
is greater than the nilpotent class of
\mathbb{U}
, then
\mathbb{U}
admits a quasilogarithm.

For us, a critical consequence of the condition that
\mathbb{G}
admits a quasilogarithm

L:\mathbb{G}\to\underline{\mathfrak{g}}
is the following result, a summary of the discussion prior to and
including Lemma 6.6 of
[
5
]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Assume that
\mathbb{G}
is a linear algebraic group smooth over
k
which admits a quasilogarithm.
The
\mathbb{G}
-equivariant map on coordinate algebras associated to the composition

L\circ i:\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\subset\mathbb{G}\to\underline{\mathfrak{g}}

has the form

S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})\ \to\ S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})/I_{r}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\ k[\mathbb{G}_{(r)}].

Moreover, the isomorphism
S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})/I_{r}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\ k[\mathbb{G}_{(r)}]

induces an isomorphism of
k
-algebras

S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})/I_{r}\#k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\ k[\mathbb{G}_{(r)}]\#k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\ \simeq D(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}).

Similarly,
L
determines the isomorphism of
k
-algebras

S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})/I_{r+1}\#k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\ k[\mathbb{G}_{(r+1)}]\#k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}\ \simeq\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}).

These smash products algebras
S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})/I_{r}\#k\mathbb{G}_{(r)},\ S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})/I_{r+1}\#k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
are associated to the
coadjoint action of
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. They both admit a
\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
-equivariant grading, with
\mathfrak{g}^{*}

homogenous of degree 1 and
k\mathbb{G}_{(r)}
homogeneous of degree 0.

Using the structure of a quasilogarithm on
\mathbb{G}
, we obtain the following strengthening of Corollary
4.3
.
The proof of this theorem is essentially that of
[
5
, Thm 6.9]
which gives the analogous

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume that
\mathbb{G}
is a linear algebraic group smooth over
k
which admits a quasilogarithm.
Further assume that
p^{r+1}>2dim(\mathbb{G})
.
Then the map

\tilde{\theta}_{r}:H^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},k)\otimes S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{(r+1)}[2])\ \to H^{*}(\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}),k)

4.2

-module complement
consisting entirely of nilpotent elements.

Similarly, the map

i_{O}^{*}:H^{*}(\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}),k)\ \to\ H^{*}(O(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}),k)
has nilpotent kernel in
H^{*}(\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}),k)
; and
the image in
H^{*}(O(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}),k)

H^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)},k)\otimes S^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{(r+1)}[2])
-module complement
consisting entirely of nilpotent elements.

\mathbb{P}_{O}:\mathbb{P}(H^{*}(O(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}),k))\to\mathbb{P}(H^{*}(\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}),k))

\Psi_{\tilde{D}}:\Pi(\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}))\ \to\ \mathbb{P}(H^{\bullet}(\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)}),k))
is also a homeomorphism

Proof.

The statement that
\tilde{\theta}_{r}
is a split injection is that of Theorem

(1).

The gradings on

(and thus also on

O(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
) determine internal gradings on the spectral sequences of Theorem

these gradings as

-gradings.
As in
[
5
, Lem 6.7]
, the internal grading on

,
hence
\ \equiv 0\ mod\ p^{r+1}
and
the internal grading of

We conclude that the map
\tilde{\theta}_{r}
of Theorem

is an
isomorphism onto the degree
\equiv 0
portion of the cohomoloy of
\tilde{D}(\mathbb{G}_{(r)})
. As argued in the proof of

[

, the hypothesis that

implies that this image has

We may repeat this argument with the spectral sequence (

mapping to the

Thus, the composition

is also a

∎

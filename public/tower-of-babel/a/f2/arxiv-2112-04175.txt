Abstract




1.
Introduction

The classification of finite subgroups in the Cremona groups
\operatorname{Cr}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is an old-standing problem that goes back to works of classics of Italian algebraic geometry. In the last two decades the interest to this problem was reactivated; see for example
[
DI09
]
,
[
Pro21b
]
, and references therein.
In particular, now there is a basically complete classification of finite subgroups in the plane Cremona group
\operatorname{Cr}_{2}(\mathbb{C})

[
DI09
]
.

In this paper we are interested in embeddings of symmetric groups
\mathfrak{S}_{N}

to Cremona group
\operatorname{Cr}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
and, more generally, to groups of birational self-maps of three-dimensional rationally connected varieties.
This problem is interesting not only in its own sake but also in relation
with computation of essential dimension of
\mathfrak{S}_{N}
(cf.
[
Dun10
]
).

.

Let
X

(i)

Note that embeddings to

The following assertion is well known (see e.g.

3.1 Proposition
.

Let

3.2 Proposition

(cf.
[
Pro12

)
.

Let

-Fano threefold.

Proof.

Assume that

. Then

is either

-del Pezzo fibration and

, § 10]
).
The map

Assume that

. Then the generic fiber

of

Thus we may assume that

Again by Lemma

They generate a two-dimensional linear system
(net)
\mathscr{Q}
.
The induced action of
G_{0}=\mathfrak{A}_{7}
on
\mathscr{Q}
is trivial by Lemma
2.5
.
Therefore, all the quadrics in the net
\mathscr{Q}
are
G_{0}
-invariant.
The faithful
G_{0}
-representation
H^{0}(X,-K_{X})^{\vee}

is reducible:

H^{0}(X,-K_{X})^{\vee}=W_{1}\oplus W_{6},

where
\dim(W_{1})=1
and
\dim(W_{6})=6
.
Here
W_{6}
is again a (unique) standard irreducible representation
having exactly one invariant quadric, say
Q^{\prime}
.
On the other hand,
the surface
\mathbb{P}(W_{6})\cap X
is a complete intersection of
(invariant) quadrics
Q_{i}\cap\mathbb{P}(W_{6})
.
Again this gives a contradiction.

From now on we assume that
g\geq 6
.
Let
G_{0}:=\mathfrak{A}_{7}\subset G
. If
\operatorname{r}(X)^{G_{0}}=1
, then by

[
Pro12
, Theorem 1.5]
we have
X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}
. In this case
the action of
\mathfrak{S}_{7}
on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
lifts to a faithful action of a central extension
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{7}

of
\mathfrak{S}_{7}
on
H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{3},\,\mathscr{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(1))
.
Since the Schur multiplier of
\mathfrak{S}_{7}
has order
2
(see e.g.
[
CCN+85
]
),
we may assume that

\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{7}
is either
\mathfrak{S}_{7}
itself or a double cover
of
\mathfrak{S}_{7}
. However such
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{7}
has no faithful four-dimensional representations, a contradiction.

Thus we assume that
\operatorname{r}(X)^{G_{0}}>1
.
We claim that
X
contains no planes.
Indeed, in the case
\uprho(X)=1
this is the statement of
[
Pro15
, Theorem 1.1]
.
For general case, we just note that the proof of
[
Pro15
, Theorem
1.1]
works without changes. It uses only that
X
is a
G
-Fano
threefold with
g\geq 6
such that
-K_{X}
is very ample and the image
of the anticanonical map is an intersection of quadrics
(Lemma
5.1
). Thus
X
contains no planes.

Let

X_{1}\to X
be a small
G_{0}\mathbb{Q}
-factorialization
(we take
X_{1}=X
if
X
is
G_{0}\mathbb{Q}
-factorial). Then
\uprho(X_{1})^{G_{0}}>1
.
Run
G_{0}
-equivariant MMP on
X_{1}
:

\textstyle{X_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{1}}
\textstyle{X_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{2}}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{N-2}}
\textstyle{X_{N-1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{N-1}}
\textstyle{X_{N}}

Since
X
contains no planes, the variety
X_{1}
contains no surfaces
S_{1}

such that
(-K_{X_{1}})^{2}\cdot S_{1}=1
.
Then by the classification of
G
-extremal contractions (see e.g.

[
Pro21a
, Theorems 7.1.1 and 8.2.4]
) the variety
X_{2}

has at worst terminal Gorenstein singularities whose
anticanonical class
-K_{X_{2}}
is nef, big, and does not contract divisors.
Moreover,
\dim|-K_{X_{2}}|\geq\dim|-K_{X_{1}}|
and
X_{2}
contains no

G_{0}
-invariant effective divisors
S_{2}
such that

(-K_{X_{2}})^{2}\cdot S_{2}=1
. Continuing the process we end up with
a
G_{0}
-Mori fiber space
X_{N}/Z
with

\dim|-K_{X_{N}}|\geq\dim|-K_{X}|=g+1\geq 7.

Then by
[
Pro12
, Theorem 1.5]
we have
X_{N}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
Denote
Y:=X_{N-1}
and consider the last contraction

\varphi=\varphi_{N-1}:Y=X_{N-1}\longrightarrow X_{N}=\mathbb{P}^{3}.

Let
E\subset Y
be its exceptional divisor.
Assume that
\dim\varphi(E)=0
.
Again by the classification of extremal contractions

[
Pro21a
, Theorems 7.1.1 and 8.2.4]

\varphi
is the blowup of a
G_{0}
-invariant set of distinct points
P_{1},\dots,P_{r}
.
By Lemma
2.7
the stabilizer of
any point on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is not isomorphic to
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
. Hence,
r\geq 8

and so

(-K_{Y})^{3}=(-K_{\mathbb{P}^{3}})^{3}-8r\leq 0

a contradiction.
Therefore,
C:=\varphi(E)
is a curve. Clearly,
C
is not contained in a plane.
Assume that
C
is irreducible.
Let
C^{\prime}\to C
be the normalization.
Since
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
contains no subgroups isomorphic to
\mathfrak{A}_{7}
,
C^{\prime}
is not
rational. Also, one can see that
C^{\prime}
is not
an elliptic curve. We have

(-K_{X_{N}})^{3}-(-K_{X_{N-1}})^{3}=2(-K_{X_{N-1}})^{2}\cdot E+2\operatorname{p}_{\mathrm{a}}(C)-2.

(see e.g.
[
Pro15
, Proposition 5.1]
).

Thus
2\operatorname{p}_{\mathrm{a}}(C)-2<64-10
and
\operatorname{p}_{\mathrm{a}}(C)\leq 27
.
On the other hand, by the Hurwitz bound
the the order of the automorphism group of
C^{\prime}
is at most
84(\operatorname{g}(C^{\prime})-1)\leq 2184
.
Since
2184<|\mathfrak{A}_{7}|
, the group
G_{0}=\mathfrak{A}_{7}
cannot act effectively on
C
, a contradiction.

Thus, the curve
C
is reducible.
Let
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
be its irreducible components
and let
G_{1}
be the stabilizer of
C_{1}
in
G_{0}=\mathfrak{A}_{7}
.
By
[
Pro12
, Lemma 5.3]
the linear system
|-K_{X_{N-1}}|

is base point free.
Let
S_{1},S_{2}\in|-K_{X_{N-1}}|
be two general members. Then
C\subset\varphi(S_{1})\cap\varphi(S_{2})
, where
\varphi(S_{i})\in|-K_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}|
. Hence

r\leq\deg C\leq 16
.
Since the group
G_{0}
permutes
C_{1},\dots,C_{r}
transitively, by
Proposition
2.3
(ii)
we have only two
possibilities:

(i)

G_{1}\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{7})
,
r=15
;

(ii)

G_{1}\simeq\mathfrak{A}_{6}
,
r=7
.

In both cases
\deg(C_{1})\leq 2
. Hence
C_{1}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
On the other hand, the groups
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{7})
and
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
cannot act on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
effectively, a contradiction.

6.
Examples

In this section we collect examples of
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
-Fano threefolds.
Note that a faithful action of a group
G
on an algebraic variety
X

induces an embedding
G\subset\operatorname{Bir}(X)
and an embedding
G\subset\operatorname{Cr}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

if
X
is rational.

6.1 Example
.

Let
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{6}
be the pull-back of
\mathfrak{S}_{6}\subset\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{R})
under the double cover

\operatorname{SU}_{4}(\mathbb{C})\to\operatorname{SO}_{6}(\mathbb{R}).

Then
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{6}
is a non-trivial central extension of
\mathfrak{S}_{6}

by
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
. This defines an embedding

\mathfrak{S}_{6}\subset\operatorname{PGL}_{4}(\mathbb{C})
, so
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
acts on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

In all examples below the group
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
is supposed to act on
x_{1},\dots,x_{6}

by permutations.

6.2 Example
.

Let
X
be a smooth quadric threefold given in
\mathbb{P}^{5}
by

\sum_{i=1}^{6}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{6}x_{i}^{2}=0.

Then
X
admits a
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
-action by
permutations of coordinates.

6.3 Example
.

The
Segre cubic

X_{3}^{\mathrm{s}}
is a subvariety in
\mathbb{P}^{5}

given by the equations

\sum_{i=1}^{6}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{6}x_{i}^{3}=0.

The singular locus of this cubic consists of 10 nodes
and
\operatorname{Aut}X_{3}^{\mathrm{s}}\simeq\mathfrak{S}_{6}
.
Moreover, the quotient
X_{3}^{\mathrm{s}}/\mathfrak{S}_{6}\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}/\mathfrak{S}_{6}
is
isomorphic to the weighted projective space

\mathbb{P}(2,4,5,6)\subset\mathbb{P}(1,2,3,4,5,6)
. Therefore,
\operatorname{r}(X_{3}^{\mathrm{s}})^{\mathfrak{S}_{6}}=1
and so

X_{3}^{\mathrm{s}}
is a
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
-Fano threefold.
Obviously,
X_{3}^{\mathrm{s}}
is rational.

6.4 Example

(
[
vdG82
, §4]
)
.

Let
X=X_{4}(\lambda)\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}\subset\mathbb{P}^{5}
is a quartic given by the equations

\sum_{i=1}^{6}x_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{6}x_{i}^{4}+\lambda\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{6}x_{i}^{2}\Big)^{2}=0,

where
\lambda
is a constant
\neq-1/4
.
The hypersurface
X_{4}(\lambda)
is singular at the
30
points of
the
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
-orbit of
(1:\omega:\omega^{2}:1:\omega:\omega^{2})

with
\omega:=e^{2\pi i/3}
.
Additional isolated singularities occur only in the following cases

•

\lambda=-1/2
,
|\operatorname{Sing}(X)|=45
,

•

\lambda=-7/10
,
|\operatorname{Sing}(X)|=36
,

•

\lambda=-1/6
,
|\operatorname{Sing}(X)|=40
.

In the case
\lambda=-1/4
, the singularities are not isolated and

is so-called
Igusa quartic
.

As in Example
6.3
one can see that

\operatorname{r}(X_{4}(\lambda))^{\mathfrak{S}_{6}}=1
.
For
\lambda\notin\{-1/2,\,-7/10,\,-1/6\}
the variety
X_{4}(\lambda)
is not rational
[
Bea13
]
.
For
\lambda=-1/2
the variety
X_{4}(\lambda)
is called
Burkhardt quartic
. It is rational.
For
\lambda=-7/10
and
-1/6
,
X_{4}(\lambda)
is also rational
[
CS16
]
.

6.5 Example

(double quadric)
.

Let

6.6 Example

(cubic complex)
.

Consider the following complete intersections of

\displaystyle X_{6}(\lambda):

7.
Del Pezzo threefolds

Recall that a
del Pezzo variety
is a Fano variety
X
with at worst
canonical Gorenstein singularities such that the canonical class
K_{X}

is divisible by
\dim(X)-1
in the group
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
.
In this section, we consider only del Pezzo threefolds
with at worst terminal Gorenstein singularities.
In this case,
-K_{X}=2H
, where
H
is am ample Cartier divisor.
Usually,
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is not considered as a del Pezzo threefold.
The self-intersection number
d:=H^{3}=-K_{X}^{3}/8
is called the
degree
of
X
.

7.1 Example
.

Let
G=\mathfrak{A}_{6}\subset\operatorname{PGL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
be the Valentiner group (see
Lemma
2.6
).
Then
G
acts faithfully on the variety
X_{6}
of complete flags on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
This
X_{6}
is a smooth del Pezzo variety of degree
6

[
IP99
,
Pro13
]
.
Note however that
\uprho(X_{6})=2
and the induced action of
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
on

\operatorname{Pic}(X_{6})
is trivial. Therefore,
X_{6}
is not an
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
-Fano threefold.

7.2 Proposition
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
faithfully acts on a del Pezzo threefold
X

(
here we do not assume that
X
is a
G
-Fano
)
.
Then
X
is equivariantly isomorphic to one of the following varieties:

(i)

X=X_{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is the Segre cubic
(
see Example
6.3
)
;

(ii)

X
is the variety of complete flags on
\mathbb{P}^{2}

(
see Example
7.1
)
.

Proof.

Let
d
be the degree of
X
.
It is easy to see by Riemann-Roch that

\dim H^{0}\big(X,-\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}K_{X}\big)=d+2.

The action of the group
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
on
X
lifts to an action of
its central extension (double cover)
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}
by
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
on
H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})
.
Recall that there exists an exceptional isomorphism

\mathfrak{A}_{6}\simeq\operatorname{PSL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{9})

(see e.g.
[
Wil09
, § 3.3.5]
). Thus a unique central extension
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}
of
\mathfrak{A}_{6}

by
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{2}
can be identified with
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{9})
.

7.3 Lemma
.

Let
X
be a del Pezzo threefold that admit a faithful action of
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
.
Then the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
contains no invariant members.

Proof.

Suppose that
S\in|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
is an invariant divisor.
Then
-(K_{X}+S)
is ample, we can apply quite standard
connectedness arguments of Shokurov
(see, e.g.,
[
Pro14
, Lemma B.5]
):
for a suitable
G
-invariant boundary
D
, the pair

(X,D)
is lc, the divisor
-(K_{X}+D)
is ample,
and the minimal locus
V
of log canonical singularities is also
G
-invariant.
Moreover,
V
is either a point or a smooth rational curve.
By Lemma
2.7
the group
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
has no fixed points.
Hence,
\mathfrak{A}_{6}\subset\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
, a contradiction.
∎

The dimensions of irreducible representations of
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{9})
are as follows:
1
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
10
.
By Lemma
7.3

H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})
has no one-dimensional subrepresentations. Note that

1\leq d\leq 7
(see e.g.
[
Pro13
]
). Therefore,
d\in\{2,\,3,\,6,\,7\}
.
Consider these possibilities one by one.

Case
d=2
.

In this case the half-canonical map
is a double cover
\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{3}=\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})^{\vee})
whose branch divisor

B\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
is a quartic.

But it is easy to compute that the group
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbf{F}_{9})
has no invariants
0\neq\phi\in\operatorname{S}^{4}H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})^{\vee}
.

Case
d=3
.

In this case
X=X_{3}\subset\mathbb{P}^{4}
is a cubic. The action of
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{6}
on

H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})
is not faithful; it
is induced from the standard representation of
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
on
\mathbb{C}^{5}
.
Then there is exactly one invariant hypersurface of degree
3

(see
7.2
(i)
).

Case
d=6
.

By
[
Pro13
]

X
has at most one singular point.
Then by Lemma
2.7

X

is smooth. Assume that
X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Then the induced representation of
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
on
\operatorname{Pic}(X)
is trivial.
Hence the group
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
effectively acts on the factors
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Clearly, this is impossible.
Therefore,
X\not\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.
Then by the classification
[
IP99
]

X
is unique up to isomorphism,
and it can be realized as the variety of complete flags on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. We get the case
7.2
(ii)
.

Case
d=7
.

In this case the variety
X
is smooth and isomorphic to the blowup
of a point on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
(see e.g.
[
Pro13
]
). Thus the action of
\mathfrak{A}_{6}
descends to
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
On the other hand, this action has no fixed points by Lemma
2.7
, a contradiction.
∎

7.4 Corollary
.

Assume that the group
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
faithfully acts on a del Pezzo threefold
X
of
degree
d
.
Then
X
is equivariantly isomorphic to the Segre cubic

(
Example
6.3
)
.

Proof.

The action of the subgroup
\mathfrak{A}_{6}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{6}
is described in Proposition
7.2
.
It is sufficient to show that the case
7.2
(ii)
does not occur.
Assume that
X
is isomorphic to the variety of complete flags on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
In this case the linear system
|-\frac{1}{2}K_{X}|
defines an embedding

X=X_{6}\subset\mathbb{P}^{7}
, where the space
\mathbb{P}^{7}
can be identified with the projectivization of

H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})^{\vee}
. The action of the group
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
on
X=X_{6}\subset\mathbb{P}^{7}
lifts to an action of
a double cover
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{6}
on
H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})^{\vee}
.
By Lemma
7.3
the representation of
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{6}
on
H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})^{\vee}
has no
one-dimensional subrepresentations. Then
H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})^{\vee}=W^{\prime}\oplus W^{\prime\prime}
, where
W^{\prime}
and
W^{\prime\prime}
are four-dimensional irreducible faithful
representations. Thus
\mathbb{P}(H^{0}(X,-\frac{1}{2}K_{X})^{\vee})
contains two disjoint

\mathfrak{S}_{6}
-invariant three-dimensional subspaces
\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})
and
\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime\prime})
.
Recall that
X=X_{6}\subset\mathbb{P}^{7}
is an intersection of quadrics.
Assume that
\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})\cap X
is not empty.
If
\dim(\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})\cap X)=2
, then
\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})\cap X
is a
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
-invariant quadric in

\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})=\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Clearly, this is impossible.
If
\dim(\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})\cap X)=1
, then
\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})\cap X
contains a
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
-invariant
curve of degree
\leq 4
and the genus of this curve is at most
1
.
Again this is impossible.
Let
\dim(\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})\cap X)=0
. Then
\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})\cap X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{k}\}
, where

k\leq 8
. By Corollary
2.4
and Lemma
2.7

the stabilizer
G_{1}\subset\mathfrak{S}_{6}
of
P_{1}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
.
Note that the representation of
G_{1}
in the
tangent space
T_{P_{1},X}
is faithful.
On the other hand, the group
\mathfrak{S}_{5}
has no faithful three-dimensional representations,
a contradiction.

Therefore,
\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})\cap X=\emptyset
.
Then the projection
p:X\to\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime\prime})
from
\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime})
must be a
\mathfrak{S}_{6}
-equivariant finite morphism.
By the Hurwitz formula

K_{X}=p^{*}K_{\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime\prime})}-R

where
R
is the ramification divisor. Since
p^{*}K_{\mathbb{P}(W^{\prime\prime})}\sim-4H\sim 2K_{X}
,
where
H
is a hyperplane section of
X
, the divisor
R
cannot be effective, a contradiction.
∎

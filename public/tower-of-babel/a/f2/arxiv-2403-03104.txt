Low-rank approximated Kalman–Bucy filters using Oja’s principal component flow for linear time-invariant systems
Daiki Tsuzuki, Kentaro Ohki

Abstract
The Kalman–Bucy filter is extensively utilized across various applications. However, its computational complexity increases significantly in large-scale systems. To mitigate this challenge, a low-rank approximated Kalman–Bucy filter was proposed, comprising Oja’s principal component flow and a low-dimensional Riccati differential equation. Previously, the estimation error was confirmed solely for linear time-invariant systems with a symmetric system matrix. This study extends the application by eliminating the constraint on the symmetricity of the system matrix and describes the equilibrium points of the Oja flow along with their stability for general matrices. In addition, the domain of attraction for a set of stable equilibrium points is estimated. Based on these findings, we demonstrate that the low-rank approximated Kalman–Bucy filter with a suitable rank maintains a bounded estimation error covariance matrix if the system is controllable and observable.

II-B

Low-Rank approximated Kalman–Bucy filter

Implementing the Kalman–Bucy filter requires real-time numerical computation or offline calculation of the steady-state solution of the algebraic Riccati equation; both approaches are challenging in case of large system dimensions. To address this issue, we explore a modified LRKB filter
[
15
]

[
14
]

\tilde{P}(t)=U(t)\tilde{R}(t)U(t)^{\top}{\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}

\tilde{R}(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}

\tilde{R}(t)>0

\displaystyle\varepsilon\frac{dU(t)}{dt}=

\displaystyle(I_{n}-U(t){U}(t)^{\top})AU(t),\ U(0)^{\top}U(0)=I_{r},

(5)

\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{R}(t)}{dt}=

\displaystyle A_{U(t)}\tilde{R}(t)+\tilde{R}(t)A_{U(t)}^{\top}+G_{U(t)}G_{U(t)}^{\top}

\displaystyle-\tilde{R}(t)C_{U(t)}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C_{U(t)}\tilde{R}(t),\ \tilde{R}(0)>0,

(6)

where
A_{U}\coloneqq U^{\top}AU{\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}}

C_{U}\coloneqq CU{\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}}

\displaystyle\frac{d\tilde{x}(t)}{dt}=

\displaystyle(A-\tilde{P}(t){C}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C)\tilde{x}(t)

\displaystyle+{\tilde{P}}(t){C}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}y(t).

Bonnabel et al.

III-A

Equilibrium points of the Oja flow

We present several lemmas to examine the structure of the equilibrium points of the Oja flow (
5
).
The following lemma holds true:

Lemma 1
.

Let
K\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times r}
be
K=\Psi^{-1}U
, where
U\in\mathrm{St}(r,n)
. Thereafter,
K
presumes the form

\displaystyle K=P\begin{bmatrix}K_{r}\\
O_{n-r,r}\end{bmatrix},

(9)

where
P\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
represents a unitary matrix, and
K_{r}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
denotes a regular matrix.

Proof.

As
r
denotes the rank of
U\in\mathrm{St}(r,n)
, the rank of
K
is
\mathrm{rank}(K)=\mathrm{rank}({\Psi}^{-1}U)=r
. According to singular value decomposition, this statement holds.
∎

For a unitary matrix
P\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, let
\Sigma^{P}:=\begin{bmatrix}\Sigma_{11}^{P}&\Sigma_{12}^{P}\\
\Sigma_{21}^{P}&\Sigma_{22}^{P}\end{bmatrix}:={P}^{\dagger}{\Psi}^{\dagger}\Psi P

\Sigma_{11}^{P}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
,
\Sigma_{12}^{P}=(\Sigma_{21}^{P})^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times(n-r)}
, and
\Sigma_{22}^{P}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n-r)\times(n-r)}
. If
P=I_{n}
, the superscript is omitted.
Then, the following lemma holds for
K_{r}
of (
9
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
K_{r}{\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}}
be of Lemma
1
. Then,
K_{r}
is represented by
\Sigma_{11}^{P}
and a unitary matrix
W_{{P}}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
such that
K_{r}=(\Sigma_{11}^{P})^{-1/2}W_{{P}}
.
For any orthogonal matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
, the following equation also satisfies (
9

K_{r}=(\Sigma_{11}^{P})^{-1/2}W_{{P}}W
.

Proof.

From polar decomposition, a unitary matrix

exists such that

.
Because

\begin{bmatrix}K_{r}^{\dagger}&&O_{r,n-r}\end{bmatrix}{P}^{\dagger}{\Psi}^{\dagger}\Psi P\begin{bmatrix}{K_{r}}\\

K_{r}^{\dagger}\Sigma_{11}^{P}K_{r}=I_{r}
and
K_{r}=(\Sigma_{11}^{P})^{-1/2}W_{{P}}

Because

,
K_{r}=(\Sigma_{11}^{P})^{-1/2}W_{{P}}W
for any orthogonal matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
always satisfies

.
Therefore, the statement holds.
∎

.

In

Any

Proposition 3

Let

{{\Lambda}_{P,r}}:=\begin{bmatrix}I_{r}&&O_{r,n-r}\end{bmatrix}P^{\top}\Lambda P\begin{bmatrix}I_{r}\\

III-B

Convergence and domain of attraction of the Oja flow

In this section, we analyze the local stability of each
\mathcal{U}_{P}
defined in Prop.
2
and the domain of attraction of the Oja flow.
Accordingly, we set
K(t)=\begin{bmatrix}K_{r}(t)\\
K_{\perp}(t)\end{bmatrix}=\Psi^{-1}U(t),

where
K_{r}(t)\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r},K_{\perp}(t)\in\mathbb{C}^{(n-r)\times r}
.

Theorem 1
.

The set
\mathcal{U}
is locally asymptotically stable. Furthermore, any equilibrium set
\mathcal{U}_{P}\neq\mathcal{U}
is unstable.

Proof.

We consider the perturbations from the equilibrium point
\Psi P\begin{bmatrix}K_{r}\\
O_{n-r,r}\end{bmatrix}
given by
\Psi P\begin{bmatrix}\delta K_{r}(t)\\
\delta K_{\perp}(t)\end{bmatrix}
.
We define
\mathrm{block}\mbox{-}\mathrm{diag}[{\Lambda_{P,r},\Lambda_{P,\perp}}]:=P^{\top}\Lambda P
.
As
\lim_{t\to\infty}\delta K_{\perp}(t)=O_{n-r,r}
is sufficient for this statement, we focus only on
\frac{d}{dt}\delta K_{\perp}(t)
.
Neglecting higher-order terms, the differential equation for
\delta K_{\perp}(t)
is stated as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{d\delta K_{\perp}(t)}{dt}=

\displaystyle{\Lambda_{P,\perp}}\delta K_{\perp}(t)-\delta K_{\perp}(t)K_{r}^{-1}{\Lambda_{P,r}}K_{r}.

The vectorization of the matrices yields

\displaystyle\frac{d\mathrm{vec}(\delta K_{\perp}(t))}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I_{r}\otimes{\Lambda_{P,\perp}}-(K_{r}^{\top}{\Lambda_{P,r}^{\top}}K_{r}^{-\top})\otimes I_{n-r})\mathrm{vec}(\delta K_{\perp}(t)),

where
\otimes
denotes the Kronecker product.
If
P=I_{n}
,
\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{1}(I_{r}\otimes\Lambda_{\perp}-(K_{r}^{\top}\Lambda_{r}^{\top}K_{r}^{-\top})\otimes I_{n-r}))=\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{r+1}(A))-\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{r}(A))<0
; therefore,

\delta K_{\perp}(t)
converges to
O_{n-r,r}
, and consequently,
K_{r}+\delta K_{r}(t)
converges to
K_{r}W
, where
W\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}
denotes an orthogonal matrix.
Therefore,
\mathcal{U}
is asymptotically stable.
In contrast, any element in
\mathcal{U}_{P}\neq\mathcal{U}
leaves
\mathcal{U}_{P}
when it is perturbed. Therefore, any set
\mathcal{U}_{P}\neq\mathcal{U}
is unstable.
∎

Theorem
1
suggests that if there is no other stable invariant set such as limit cycle, any solution
U(t)
of Eq. (
5

\mathcal{U}
; therefore, investigating
\mathcal{U}

As Theorem

\mathcal{U}
is expected.
If we consider
(n,r)=(2,1)

Proposition 4

(n,r)=(2,1)

\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{1}(A))>\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{2}(A))

\Sigma={\Psi^{\top}\Psi}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}

Here,

Based on
K(t)^{\top}\Sigma K(t)=\Sigma_{11}K_{r}(t)^{2}+\Sigma_{22}K_{\perp}(t)^{2}+2\Sigma_{12}K_{r}(t)K_{\perp}(t)=1

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}K(t)=

\displaystyle{(I_{2}-K(t)K(t)^{\top}\Sigma)\Lambda K(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta\lambda}{2}\left\{\begin{bmatrix}0\\
-2K_{\perp}(t)\end{bmatrix}+\gamma(K_{r}(t),K_{\perp}(t))\begin{bmatrix}K_{r}(t)\\
K_{\perp}(t)\end{bmatrix}\right\},

(10)

where
\delta\lambda:=\lambda_{1}(A)-\lambda_{2}(A)
and
\gamma(K_{r},K_{\perp}):=(1+\Sigma_{22}K_{\perp}^{2}-\Sigma_{11}K_{r}^{2})
. Equation (
10
) contains four equilibrium points:
(\bar{K}_{r},\bar{K}_{\perp})=(\pm\Sigma^{-1/2}_{11},0)
and
(0,\pm\Sigma^{-1/2}_{22})
. Herein, we consider the following four cases:
(i) If
\gamma(K_{r}(t),K_{\perp}(t))>2
, then
|K_{r}(t)|
and
|K_{\perp}(t)|
increase.
(ii) If
\gamma(K_{r}(t),K_{\perp}(t))\in(0,2]
, then
|K_{r}(t)|
increases and
|K_{\perp}(t)|
decreases.
(iii) If
\gamma(K_{r}(t),K_{\perp}(t))=0
, then
K_{r}(t)
does not change and
|K_{\perp}(t)|
decreases or does not change if
K_{\perp}(t)=0
.
(iv) If
\gamma(K_{r}(t),K_{\perp}(t))<0
, then
|K_{r}(t)|
and
|K_{\perp}(t)|
decrease.
In (i), the rate of increase of
|K_{r}(t)|
is larger than that of
|K_{\perp}(t)|
. Then, Equation (
10
) enters case (ii) and becomes case (iii). After case (iii), Equation (
10
) does not return to case (ii); thus,
|K_{\perp}(t)|
converges to
0
.
Based on the constraint
K(t)^{\top}\Sigma K(t)=1
, this phenomenon implies that
K_{r}(t)
converges to
\pm\Sigma_{11}^{-1/2}
.
∎

Unlike Proposition
4
, estimating the exact domain of attraction for a general
(n,r)
is challenging. We provide sufficient conditions for the domain of attraction.
Before proceeding, we introduce the following lemmas:

Lemma 3
.

For any matrix
X,Y\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
and any real number
\alpha>0
, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\pm(X^{\dagger}Y+Y^{\dagger}X)\leq\alpha X^{\dagger}X+\frac{1}{\alpha}Y^{\dagger}Y.

Proof.

For any
X,Y\in\mathbb{C}^{m\times n}
, we obtain

\begin{bmatrix}X&Y\end{bmatrix}^{\dagger}\begin{bmatrix}X&Y\end{bmatrix}\geq 0
.
By multiplying both sides of the inequality by
[\sqrt{\alpha}I_{n},\ \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}I_{n}]
and its transposition from the left and right sides, we obtain Lemma
3
.
∎

Lemma 4
.

For any
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, a unitary matrix
S\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
and upper triangle matrix
Q\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
exist such that
S^{\dagger}AS
becomes an upper triangle and satisfies
S=\Psi Q
.

Proof.

Based on the QR decomposition of
\Psi
,
\Psi=ST
, where
T
denotes the upper triangle and regular matrix. As
Q=T^{-1}
is also an upper triangle, this statement holds.
∎

If the matrix
A
is decomposed using the unitary matrix
S
satisfying Lemma
4
, we can consider
S^{\dagger}AS=\begin{bmatrix}L_{11}&&L_{12}\\
O_{n-r,r}&&L_{22}\end{bmatrix}
, where
L_{11}{\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}}
denotes an upper triangular matrix with eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{r}(A)
,
L_{22}{\in\mathbb{C}^{(n-r)\times(n-r)}}
represents an upper triangular matrix with eigenvalues
\lambda_{r+1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{n}(A)
, and
L_{12}{\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times(n-r)}}
.
Note that
\mathrm{St}(r,n)=\{S\begin{bmatrix}F_{1}\\
F_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times r}\mid F_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r},F_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n-r)\times r},\ F_{1}^{\dagger}F_{1}+F_{2}^{\dagger}F_{2}=I_{r}\}
.

Theorem 2
.

If
{\lambda}^{l_{1}}_{r}\coloneqq\lambda_{r}(L_{11}+L_{11}^{\dagger})>{\lambda}^{l_{2}}_{1}\coloneqq\lambda_{1}(L_{22}+L_{22}^{\dagger})
, and
U(0)\in\mathcal{V}_{\beta}:=\{S\begin{bmatrix}F_{1}\\
F_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{St}(r,n)\mid F_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r},F_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n-r)\times r},\ 0\leq F_{2}^{\dagger}F_{2}<\beta I_{r}\}
, then the solution to Equation (
5
) converges to an element of
\mathcal{U}
. Here,
\beta=(1+4\ell_{\max}/({\lambda}^{l_{1}}_{r}-{\lambda}^{l_{2}}_{1})^{2})^{-1}
and
\ell_{\max}
is the maximum eigenvalue of
L_{12}^{\dagger}L_{12}
.

Proof.

By using Lemma
4
,
U(t)
can be expressed as
U(t)=\Psi Q\begin{bmatrix}F_{1}(t)^{\top}&F_{2}(t)^{\top}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
.
We demonstrate the initial domain is such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}F_{2}(t)=O_{n-r,r}
, implying
U(t)
converges to
\mathcal{U}
.
The differential equation of
\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)]
is

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}(L_{22}+L_{22}^{\dagger})F_{2}(t)\{I_{r}-{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)\}]

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)F_{1}(t)^{\dagger}(L_{11}+L_{11}^{\dagger})F_{1}(t)]

\displaystyle-\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)\{{F_{1}(t)}^{\dagger}L_{12}{F_{2}(t)}+{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}L_{12}^{\dagger}{F_{1}(t)}\}].

By applying Lemma
3
with
\alpha\in(0,\delta\lambda)
, where
\delta\lambda:={\lambda}^{l_{1}}_{r}-{\lambda}^{l_{2}}_{1}
, and
{F_{1}(t)}^{\dagger}{F_{1}(t)}=I_{r}-{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)
to the aforementioned equation, the following inequality holds:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\delta\lambda\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)\{I_{r}-{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)\}]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\alpha}\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t){F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}L_{12}^{\dagger}L_{12}{F_{2}(t)}]

\displaystyle+\alpha\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)\{I_{r}-{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)\}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-(\delta\lambda-\alpha)\mathrm{Tr}[{F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)]

\displaystyle+\left(\delta\lambda-\alpha+\frac{\ell_{\max}}{\alpha}\right)\mathrm{Tr}[({F_{2}(t)}^{\dagger}F_{2}(t))^{2}].

We consider the following Riccati differential equation:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Z(t)=

\displaystyle-(\delta\lambda-\alpha)Z(t)+\left(\delta\lambda-\alpha+\frac{\ell_{\max}}{\alpha}\right)Z(t)^{2},

(11)

where
Z(t)=Z(t)^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
and
Z(0)=F_{2}(0)^{\dagger}F_{2}(0)
. The solution of Equation (
11
) converges to
O_{r,r}
if
Z(0)<\frac{\alpha(\delta\lambda-\alpha)}{\alpha(\delta\lambda-\alpha)+\ell_{\max}}I_{r}
.
Since
\mathrm{Tr}[F_{2}(t)^{\dagger}F_{2}(t)]\leq\mathrm{Tr}[Z(t)]
for
t\geq 0
, if
F_{2}(0)^{\dagger}F_{2}(0)<\frac{\alpha(\delta\lambda-\alpha)}{\alpha(\delta\lambda-\alpha)+\ell_{\max}}I_{r}
, then
F_{2}(t)
converges to
O_{n-r,r}
, implying that
U(t)
converges to
\mathcal{U}
.

Subsequently, we estimate the maximum domain of attraction. Let

\displaystyle f(\alpha):=\frac{\alpha(\delta\lambda-\alpha)}{\alpha(\delta\lambda-\alpha)+\ell_{\max}},\ \alpha\in(0,\delta\lambda).

Thereafter,
f
is a concave, positive function over
(0,\delta\lambda)
and is
maximum when
\alpha_{\max}=\delta\lambda/2
.
Thus,
f(\alpha_{\max})=\frac{\delta\lambda^{2}}{\delta\lambda^{2}+4\ell_{\max}}=\beta
, thereby completes the proof.
∎

By Bendixson’s inequality,
{\lambda}^{l_{1}}_{r}>{\lambda}^{l_{2}}_{1}
implies
\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{r}(A))>\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{r+1}(A))
.
A prior numerical study
[
17
]
suggests that solution
U(t)
to Eq. (
5
) converges to
\mathcal{U}
for almost initial matrices if
\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{r}(A))>\mathrm{Re}(\lambda_{r+1}(A))
is satisfied, implying that the conditions in Theorem
2
can be relaxed.
Theorem
2
is similar to Proposition
4
if
\ell_{\max}=0
, satisfied when
A
is a normal matrix:

Corollary 1
.

Let
A
denote a normal matrix. If
U(0)\in\mathcal{V}_{1}
, then the solution to Eq. (
5
) converges to an element of
\mathcal{U}
.

Proof.

For a normal matrix
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
S
diagonalizes
A
. Therefore,
\ell_{max}=0
. This statement follows from Thm.

.

∎

The volume of
\mathrm{St}(r,n)
can be precisely calculated
[
26

. The same calculation reveals that
\mathcal{V}_{1}
has the same volume as
\mathrm{St}(r,n)
, implying that almost all elements of

are in

is generated from, e.g., the uniform distribution on

Demonstrating the equality of two volumes exceeds the scope of this paper, but an illustrative example is provided instead. For
A=A^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
and

,

consists of the North and South poles of a unit sphere by choosing a suitable coordinate system.

\mathcal{V}_{1}
is then equivalent to the surface area of the sphere excluding the equator line; hence,

possesses the same volume as
\mathrm{St}(1,3)

However, for a general

.

IV
Boundedness of estimation error

To ensure a bounded estimation error for the LRKB filter, we define the conditions for the steady-state solution of Eq. (
8
) as in
[
15
]
that addressed symmetric
A
.
As the Oja flow can be computed offline for LTI systems, we assume
U(0)=\bar{U}\in\mathcal{U}
throughout this section.
Initially, we started the analysis by examining the controllability and observability using the following equality verified in Prop.
2
:

\displaystyle{\bar{U}\bar{U}^{\top}A\bar{U}=A\bar{U}\quad\forall\bar{U}\in\mathcal{U}.}

(12)

Proposition 5
.

If
(C,A){\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}
is observable, then
(C_{\bar{U}},A_{\bar{U}}){\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}}
is observable as well.

Proof.

If
(C_{\bar{U}},A_{\bar{U}})
is unobservable, then a nonzero vector
\bm{v}\in{\mathbb{C}^{r}}
and
\lambda_{i}
exist such that
\bar{U}^{\top}A\bar{U}\bm{v}=\lambda_{i}\bm{v}
,
C\bar{U}\bm{v}=0
.
Based on Prop.
3
,
\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{k}(A)
,
k=1,\dots,r
.
From Eq. (
12
), we can state

\displaystyle\bar{U}\bar{U}^{\top}A\bar{U}\bm{v}=A\bar{U}\bm{v}=\lambda_{i}\bar{U}\bm{v},\quad C\bar{U}\bm{v}=0.

Since the column vectors of
\bar{U}
are linearly independent,
\bar{U}\bm{v}\neq 0
. This result contradicts the observability of
(C,A)
. Therefore, the statement holds.
∎

Proposition 6
.

If
(A,G){\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}
is controllable, then
(A_{\bar{U}},G_{\bar{U}}){\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r\times n}}
is also controllable.

Proof.

If
(A,G)
is controllable, we can obtain
\mathrm{rank}\begin{bmatrix}G&A-\lambda_{i}I_{n}\end{bmatrix}=n
for any
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{i}(A)
,
i=1,\dots,n
.
Subsequently, we consider the rank of the following matrix:

\displaystyle\bar{U}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}G&A-\lambda_{i}I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}I_{n}&O_{n,r}\\
O_{n,n}&\bar{U}\end{bmatrix}.

As
\bar{U}
is full rank, the following equation holds:

\displaystyle\mathrm{rank}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{U}^{\top}G&\bar{U}^{\top}A\bar{U}-\lambda_{i}I_{r}\end{bmatrix}=r\quad\forall\lambda_{i},\ i=1,\dots,n.

This signifies that
(A_{\bar{U}},G_{\bar{U}})
is controllable. Therefore, if
(A,G)
is controllable, then
(A_{\bar{U}},G_{\bar{U}})
is also controllable.
∎

Remark 2
.

If
(A,G,C)
is stabilizable and detectable, then
(A_{\bar{U}},G_{\bar{U}},C_{\bar{U}})
are also stabilizable and detectable. The proof adheres to the procedures outlined in Propositions
5
and
6
.

If Propositions
5
and
6
are applicable, then Prop.
1
can be applied to Eq. (
6
). Consequently,
\tilde{R}(t){\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}}
converges to
\tilde{R}_{\bar{U}}>0
and
A_{\bar{U}}-\tilde{R}_{\bar{U}}C_{\bar{U}}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C_{\bar{U}}{\in\mathbb{R}^{r\times r}}
is stable.
However,
\tilde{R}(t)
can converge to different solutions depending on the choice of
\bar{U}
. Even in such cases, the following proposition ensures the uniqueness of
\tilde{P}_{r}:=\lim_{t\to\infty}\tilde{P}(t){\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}
.

Proposition 7
.

Suppose
(A,G,C)
is controllable and observable.
Let
U_{1},U_{2}\in\mathcal{U}
. Subsequently,
U_{1}\tilde{R}_{U_{1}}U_{1}^{\top}=U_{2}\tilde{R}_{U_{2}}U_{2}^{\top}=\tilde{P}_{r}
holds.

The proof follows the proof of Prop. 4 in
[
15
]
.

Subsequently, we present a proposition concerning the stability of the estimation error system and provide the conditions for the boundedness of the estimation error.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\sigma_{i}=\lambda_{i}(A_{\bar{U}}-\tilde{R}_{\bar{U}}C_{\bar{U}}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C_{\bar{U}})
for
i=1,\dots,r
.

Then the eigenvalues of
A-\tilde{P}_{r}{C}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C{\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}}
are
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
,
\lambda_{r+1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{n}(A)
.

Proof.

From Lemma
4
, there exists a unitary
S\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
such that
S^{\dagger}AS=\begin{bmatrix}L_{11}&L_{12}\\
O_{n-r,r}&L_{22}\end{bmatrix}
, where
L_{11}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
with the eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{r}(A)
,
L_{22}\in\mathbb{C}^{(n-r)\times(n-r)}
with the eigenvalues
\lambda_{r+1}(A),\dots,\lambda_{n}(A)
, and
L_{12}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times(n-r)}
. Note that
\bar{U}\in\mathcal{U}=\{S\begin{bmatrix}F_{1}\\
O_{n-r,r}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{St}(r,n)\ |\ F_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r},\ F_{1}^{\dagger}F_{1}=I_{r}\}
. Using the change of the coordinate system by
S
,

\displaystyle S^{\dagger}(A-\tilde{P}_{r}C^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C)S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{\dagger}AS-S^{\dagger}\bar{U}\tilde{R}_{\bar{U}}C_{\bar{U}}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C_{\bar{U}}\bar{U}^{\top}S

\displaystyle-S^{\dagger}\bar{U}\tilde{R}_{\bar{U}}C_{\bar{U}}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C(I_{n}-\bar{U}\bar{U}^{\top})S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}X&Y\\
O_{r,n-r}&L_{22}\end{bmatrix}

holds, where
X:=L_{11}-F_{1}\tilde{R}_{\bar{U}}C_{\bar{U}}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C_{\bar{U}}F_{1}^{\dagger}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
,
Y\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times(n-r)}
, and
F_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
is a unitary matrix. Since
A_{\bar{U}}=\bar{U}^{\top}A\bar{U}=F_{1}^{\dagger}L_{11}F_{1}
,
X=F_{1}(A_{\bar{U}}-\tilde{R}_{\bar{U}}C_{\bar{U}}^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C_{\bar{U}})F_{1}^{\dagger}
. Because the unitary transformation preserves the eigenvalues,
X
has the eigenvalues
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
, implying
A-\tilde{P}_{r}C^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C
has the eigenvalues
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
and
\lambda_{r+1}(A),\dots\lambda_{n}(A)
.

∎

From Proposition
8
, we derive the condition for the necessary and sufficient rank
r
for the bounded estimation error using the LRKB filter.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that system
(A,G,C)
is controllable and observable, and
A
has
r^{\prime}
unstable eigenvalues. Then,
A-\tilde{P}_{r}C^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C
is stable iff
r\geq r^{\prime}
. Furthermore, the error covariance matrix
\tilde{V}(t)
is bounded and converges to a steady-state solution if
A-\tilde{P}_{r}C^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C
is stable.

Proof.

Based on Propositions
1
,
5
, and
6
,

\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
are stable eigenvalues. Thus, from Prop.
8
, if we select
r\geq r^{\prime}
, then
A-\tilde{P}_{r}C^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C
is stable. Conversely, if
r<r^{\prime}
, then
A-\tilde{P}_{r}C^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C
is unstable.

According to the standard argument, the solution
\tilde{R}(t)
of Eq. (
6
) is bounded, which directly implies the boundedness of
\tilde{P}(t)=\bar{U}\tilde{R}(t)\bar{U}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.
Since
\tilde{V}(t)
indicates the solution of Eq. (
8
) with time-varying, bounded coefficient matrices,
\tilde{V}(t)
is a continuous solution.
Furthermore, as
\lim_{t\to\infty}P(t)=P_{r}
,
A-\tilde{P}(t)C^{\top}{(HH^{\top})}^{-1}C
becomes stable, which signifies that
\tilde{V}(t)
converges to a positive semidefinite matrix. As observed in the continuity of
\tilde{V}(t)
in time
t\geq 0
,
\sup_{t\geq 0}\tilde{V}(t)
is bounded.
∎

From Theorems
1
and
3
, we conclude that selecting
r
larger than the number of unstable eigenvalues of
A
is crucial for achieving bounded estimation. The number of unstable eigenvalues can be checked, for example, using the Routh table. However, finding a suitable rank for linear time-varying or nonlinear systems remains a challenge.

V
Conclusion

In this study, we analyzed the properties of the Oja flow and a modified LRKB filter with a general matrix. We removed the restriction on symmetric matrices and investigated the Oja flow for general matrices. Based on the results, we revealed the rank
r
condition for stable estimation.

This paper focused on continuous-time LTI systems. In the future, the applicability of the modified LRKB filter with bounded estimation errors should be analyzed for discrete-time LTI, linear time-varying, and nonlinear systems.

An extension operator for Sobolev spaces with mixed weights
Markus Hansen, Cornelia Schneider, Flóra O. Szemenyei

Abstract
We provide an extension operator for weighted Sobolev spaces on bounded polyhedral cones K K involving a mixture of weights, which measure the distance to the vertex and the edges of the cone, respectively. Our results are based on Stein’s extension operator [ 17 ] for Sobolev spaces and generalize [ 8 ] .

1
Introduction

In this article we construct an extension operator for weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
on polyhedral cones
K
involving mixed weights, which measure the distance to the vertex and the edges of the cone as displayed by the parameters
\beta
and
\delta
, respectively.
Our construction is based on the original extension operator from Stein
[
17
]
for classical Sobolev spaces on sufficiently smooth domains, which was generalized in
[
8
]
by Hansen to the setting of weighted Sobolev spaces involving one weight function only.

Weighted Sobolev spaces are particularly important when it comes to regularity theory for solutions of partial differential equations (PDEs) on non-smooth domains: in this case singularities of the solutions at the boundary may occur which diminish the Sobolev regularity, cf.
[
9
]
, but can be compensated with suitable weight functions. In particular, spaces with mixed weights are needed when studying stochastic PDEs as is demonstrated in
[
3
,
2
]
.
The weighted Sobolev spaces we are interested in can be seen as generalizations of the so-called Kondratiev spaces which appeared in the 60s in
[
10
,
11
]
and were studied in detail in
[
6
]
.
Later on more general spaces were considered by
Kufner, Sändig
[
13
]
, Babuska, Guo
[
1
]
, Nistor, Mazzucato
[
15
]
, and Costabel, Dauge, Nicaise
[
4
]
, to mention at least a few contributions in this context.

The weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
with parameters
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
\beta\in\real
, and
\delta=(\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{n})\in\real^{n}
, were introduced and studied in detail by Maz’ya, Rossmann in
[
14
]
and contain all measurable functions
u
such that the norm

\|u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\|:=\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{K}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}\rho_{0}(x)^{p(\beta-l+|\alpha|)}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\dfrac{r_{k}(x)}{\rho_{0}(x)}\bigg)^{p(\delta_{k}-l+|\alpha|)}|\partial^{\alpha}u(x)|^{p}\>dx\bigg)^{1/p}

is finite. Here
\rho_{0}
denotes the regularized distance of a point
x
to the vertex of the cone
K
and
r_{k}(x)
the (regularized) distances to the respective edges
M_{k}
of the cone. It is precisely this mixture of the different weights involved and their interplay, which allows for an even finer description when investigating the singularities of the solutions of PDEs on polyhedral cones or even general domains of polyhedral type.

Our construction of the extension operator in the weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}
will be done in three steps:
First we show that Stein’s definition of an extension operator for special Lipschitz domains remains bounded in the framework of weighted Sobolev spaces denoted by
V_{\delta}^{l,p}
where the weight involved (displayed by the parameter
\delta\in\real
) measures the distance to the
x_{1}
-axis. In particular, the result holds for unbounded wedges (which are special Lipschitz domains) where the weight in the respective Sobolev spaces now measures the distance to the edge of the wedge. In the next step we establish an extension operator for a fixed layer
K^{\prime}
(bounded away from the vertex) of the cone
K
in the spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}
with mixed weights. The construction uses the first result, since the layer can be covered by finitely many diffeomorphic images of bounded wedges.
Finally, in a third step we decompose the cone into dyadic layers
K_{j}
, where
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, and construct a family of extension operators for each layer, which is uniformly bounded in the norms of
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}
with respect to
j
. Afterwards we glue together these operators properly
and use the localization of the norm of
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}
, which yields an extension operator for the whole cone.

Our motivation for these kinds of studies is as follows: in a forthcoming paper we will use regularity estimates for solutions of elliptic and parabolic PDEs on polyhedral cones in the spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
in order to study the smoothness
\alpha>0
of these solutions in the specific scale

B^{\alpha}_{\tau,\tau}(K),\qquad\frac{1}{\tau}=\frac{\alpha}{d}+\frac{1}{p},

(1)

of Besov spaces.
It is well known that the regularity
\alpha
in these spaces determines the approximation order that can be achieved by adaptive approximation schemes, see
[
5
]
for further details on this subject.
In order to justify the use of adaptive algorithms (compared to non-adaptive ones) for the PDEs under investigation, one needs to know that the Besov regularity
\alpha
of the solutions is sufficiently high.
Our main tool here will be an embedding for the weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}
into the scale of Besov spaces (
1
). In this context the extension operator we construct below will help to improve the upper bound of the smoothness
\alpha
considerably, leading to a far better approximation rate of the adaptive algorithms compared to
[
7
]
. However, the results for the extension operator established in this paper are of interest on their own, which is why we decided to publish them separately.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we introduce the weighted Sobolev spaces on polyhedral cones and study some of their relevant properties. Afterwards, in Section 3, we construct an extension operator for these spaces in three steps as explained above.

d

|x|

\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})

\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

. Furthermore,

A\leq c\,B

A\lesssim B

\Omega

C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
.

L_{p}(\Omega)
,

, be the Lebesgue spaces on
\Omega
as usual.

{C}(\Omega)

f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

{C}^{k}(\Omega)

f\in{C}(\Omega)

\partial^{\alpha}f\in{C}(\Omega)

with
|\alpha|\leq k
,
endowed with the norm
\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\sup_{x\in\Omega}|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|

Let

. Then

Polyhedral cone

We mainly consider function spaces defined on polyhedral cones in the sequel. Let

K:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\>0<|x|<\infty,\>x/|x|\in\Omega\}

be an infinite cone in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with vertex at the origin. Suppose that the boundary
\partial K
consists of the vertex
x=0
, the edges (half lines)
M_{1},...,M_{n}
, and smooth faces
\Gamma_{1},...,\Gamma_{n}
. Hence,
\Omega\cap S^{2}
is a domain of polygonal type on the unit sphere
S^{2}
with sides
\Gamma_{k}\cap S^{2}
.
Moreover, we consider the bounded polyhedral cone
\tilde{K}
obtained via truncation

\tilde{K}:=K\cap B(0,r).

The singular points of the polyhedral cone
K
are those
x\in\partial K
for which for any
\varepsilon>0
the set
\partial K\cap B(x,\varepsilon)
is not smooth, i.e., the vertex
0

.

, which in this case is not the entire singularity set.

2.1
Weighted Sobolev spaces and their properties

Definition 2.1
.

Let
K
be a (bounded or unbounded) polyhedral cone in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
S=\{0\}\cup M_{1}\cup...\cup M_{n}
. Then the space
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K,S)
is defined as the closure of the set

C_{*}^{\infty}(K,S):=\{u|_{K}:\>u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus S)\}

with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K,S)\|:=\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{K}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}\rho_{0}(x)^{p(\beta-l+|\alpha|)}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\dfrac{r_{k}(x)}{\rho_{0}(x)}\bigg)^{p(\delta_{k}-l+|\alpha|)}|\partial^{\alpha}u(x)|^{p}\>dx\bigg)^{1/p},

(2)

where
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
\rho_{0}(x)=\mathrm{dist}(0,x)
denotes the distance to the vertex, and
r_{j}(x):=\mathrm{dist}(x,M_{j})
the distance to the edge
M_{j}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We collect some remarks and properties concerning the weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
.

•

In the sequel if there is no confusion we omit
S
from the notation and write shortly
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
.

•

The space
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
is a Banach space for
1\leq p<\infty
. The proof can be derived from a generalized result, see
[
12
, p. 18, Theorem 3.6]
.

•

V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\subset L_{p}(K)
for
l\geq\beta
and
l\geq\delta_{k},\>k=1,...,n
.

•

We have the following embeddings which one obtains easily from the definition of the spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
:

V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\subset V_{\beta-1,\delta-1}^{l-1,p}(K)\subset...\subset V_{\beta-l,\delta-l}^{0,p}(K).

•

There exist regularized versions of the distance functions
\rho_{0}
and
r_{1}
,…,
r_{n}
. We denote them by
\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{r}_{1},...,\widetilde{r}_{n}
. According to
[
17
, Theorem VI.2.2, p.171]
, these functions defined on
\overline{K}
have nonnegative function values and are infinitely often differentiable. Moreover, there exist positive constants
A_{0},B_{0},C_{\alpha,0}
such that

\displaystyle A_{0}\rho_{0}(x)\leq\widetilde{\rho}_{0}(x)\leq B\rho_{0}(x),\quad x\in K,

and for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
,

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}\widetilde{\rho}_{0}(x)|\leq C_{\alpha,0}\rho_{0}^{1-|\alpha|}(x),\quad x\in K.

One has similar results for
\widetilde{r_{i}},\>i=1,...,n
.

•

Replacing
\rho_{0},r_{1},...,r_{n}
by
\widetilde{\rho}_{0},\widetilde{r}_{1},...,\widetilde{r}_{n}
in the norm of
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
, one can prove that the operator

\displaystyle T_{\beta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\longrightarrow V_{\beta-\beta^{\prime},\delta-\delta^{\prime}}^{l,p}(K),\qquad u\mapsto\rho_{0}^{\beta^{\prime}}(x)\prod_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{r_{k}(x)}{\rho_{0}(x)}\right)^{\delta_{k}^{\prime}}u,

(3)

is an isomorphism, where
\delta-\delta^{\prime}=(\delta_{1}-\delta_{1}^{\prime},...,\delta_{n}-\delta_{n}^{\prime})
. The inverse operator is given by

(T_{\beta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime}})^{-1}=T_{-\beta^{\prime},-\delta^{\prime}}.

•

A function
\varphi\in C^{l}(K)
is a pointwise multiplier in
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
, i.e., for

u\in V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\|\leq c\|u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\|.

(4)

•

The weighted Sobolev spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
are refinements of the Kondratiev spaces
\mathcal{K}_{a,p}^{m}(K)
, which for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
1\leq p<\infty
, and
a\in\mathbb{R}
are defined as the collection of all measurable functions such that

\displaystyle\|u|\mathcal{K}_{a,p}^{m}(K)\|:=\left(\displaystyle\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\int_{K}|\rho(x)^{|\alpha|-a}\partial^{\alpha}u(x)|^{p}\>dx\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

with weight function
\rho(x):=\min(1,\mathrm{dist}(x,S))
for
x\in K
.
In particular, the scales coincide if

m=l,\>\delta=(\delta_{1},...,\delta_{n})=(l-a,...,l-a)\quad\text{and}\quad\beta=l-a.

To show this, one has to prove that the power of the weight function of
\mathcal{K}_{a,p}^{m}(K)
is equivalent to the power of the weight function of
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
with these parameter assumptions, i.e.,

\rho_{0}(x)^{\beta-l+|\alpha|}\prod_{k=1}^{n}\bigg(\dfrac{r_{k}(x)}{\rho_{0}(x)}\bigg)^{\delta_{k}-l+|\alpha|}\sim\rho(x)^{|\alpha|-a}.

This follows from
[
14
, p. 90, Subsection 3.1.1, (3.1.2)]
.

•

As an alternative approach to Definition
2.1
one could define spaces
\tilde{V}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
, which contain all measurable functions such that the norm (
2
) is finite, and investigate under which conditions the set
C^{\infty}_{\ast}(K,S)
is dense in
\tilde{V}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
, i.e., when these spaces coincide with the spaces
{V}_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
. This interesting question is postponed to a forthcoming paper.

Next we provide an equivalent norm in
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
. For this let

\varphi_{j}
be infinitely differentiable functions depending only on
\rho_{0}(x)=|x|
such that

\displaystyle\text{supp }\varphi_{j}\subset\{2^{-j-1}<|x|<2^{-j+1}\},\quad|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi_{j}(\cdot)|\leq c_{\alpha}2^{|\alpha|j},\quad\displaystyle\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}=1,

(5)

where
c_{\alpha}
are constants independent of
j
.

Remark 2.3
.

If
K
is a bounded polyhedral cone, then w.l.o.g. we assume
j_{0}=0
. If it is unbounded, then
j_{0}=-\infty
is required.

The following lemma is a direct consequence of the localization result in
[
14
, p. 91, Lemma 3.1.2]
and has a more convenient form for our setting in order to prove the existence of the extension operator.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K
be a polyhedral cone,
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
1\leq p<\infty
.
Then for all
u\in V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
,

\displaystyle\|u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\|^{p}\sim\displaystyle\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\|\varphi_{j}u|V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)\|^{p},

where
(\varphi_{j})_{j\geq j_{0}}
is resolution of unity satisfying (
5
) as above.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that Lemma
2.4
actually holds for every family
(\varphi_{j})_{j\geq j_{0}}
as above, which satisfies the weaker assumption
\displaystyle\sum_{j=j_{0}}^{\infty}\varphi_{j}\sim 1
.

3
An extension operator for the spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)

In this section we construct an extension operator for the space
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
. Our main result reads as follows:

Theorem 3.1
.

(Extension operator)

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a polyhedral cone and let
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then there exists a bounded linear extension operator

\mathfrak{E}:V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K,S)\rightarrow V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},S),

where
S
is the singularity set of
K
.

Remark 3.2
.

The norm of the space
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},S)
is defined similarly as the norm of
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K,S)
replacing the integral domain
K
by
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

3.1
Extension operator for special Lipschitz domains

In order to prove Theorem
3.1
we need some preparations. We follow the proof of the extension theorem of Stein, who originally proved the theorem for classical Sobolev spaces. This was generalized by Hansen in
[
8
]
to the setting of Kondratiev spaces. Since we now deal with mixed weights in the context of the spaces
V_{\beta,\delta}^{l,p}(K)
, we need to make some careful modifications. We start with an extension theorem for spaces defined on special Lipschitz domains, which is a counterpart of the corresponding theorem for Kondratiev spaces
[
8
, p. 582, Appendix]
. For this we need to deal with the spaces
V_{\delta}^{l,p}(D,\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\ast})
from
[
14
, p. 24]
, where

\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\>x_{2}=x_{3}=0\}

denotes the
x_{1}
-axis.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\>\delta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
1\leq p<\infty
. Furthermore, let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a special Lipschitz domain, i.e.,

\displaystyle D=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\>x=(x^{\prime},x_{3}),\>x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\>x_{3}>\omega(x^{\prime})\},

(6)

for some Lipschitz-continuous function
\omega:\>\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.
Assume
\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2}\subset\partial D
and let
r(x)
denote the distance of
x
to
\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2}
. Then the space
V_{\delta}^{l,p}(D,\mathbb{R}^{2}_{\ast})
is defined as the closure of

C_{\ast}^{\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2})=\{u\big|_{D}:\ u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\real^{3}\setminus\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2})\}

with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|u|V_{\delta}^{l,p}(D,\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2})\|=\left(\displaystyle\int_{D}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq l}r^{p(\delta-l+|\alpha|)}|\partial^{\alpha}u(x)|^{p}\>dx\right)^{1/p}.

Remark 3.4
.

More general, let
D
be a domain of polyhedral type where for the edge
M
we have
M\subset\partial D
. Then the space
V_{\delta}^{l,p}(D,M)
can be defined in a similar manner. In this case the function
r
denotes the distance to the edge
M
. Furthermore, the norm of the space
V_{\delta}^{l,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2})
is the counterpart of the norm of
V_{\delta}^{l,p}(D,\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2})
replacing the integral domain
D
by
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Now we can state and proof an extension theorem for the above spaces defined on special Lipschitz domain.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\>\delta\in\real
, and
1\leq p<\infty
. Moreover, let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a special Lipschitz domain and assume
\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2}\subset\partial D
. Then there exists a universal linear and bounded extension operator

\mathfrak{E}:\ V_{\delta}^{l,p}(D,\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2})\rightarrow V_{\delta}^{l,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2}).

Remark 3.6
.

In particular, note that Theorem
3.5
holds for wedges. In this case we choose
\omega
as the Lipschitz function

\omega(x_{1},x_{2})=|x_{2}|,

and describe the wedge as a special Lipschitz domain according to (
6
), i.e.,

W=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\>x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}),\>x_{3}>\omega(x_{1},x_{2})\}.

Thus, the weight in the
V_{\delta}^{l,p}(W,\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2})
-norm measures the distance of a point to the
x_{1}
-axis, which represents the edge (and therefore the singularity set) of the wedge.

Proof.

For the proof we rely on calculations of Pieperbeck
[
16
]
and Hansen
[
8
]
.
Since by definition the set
C_{\ast}^{\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2})
is dense in
V_{\delta}^{l,p}(D,\mathbb{R}_{*}^{2})
, it suffices to prove the theorem for this dense subset.

Step 1 (Preparations):
We set
\delta(\xi)=\mathrm{dist}(\xi,\partial D)
as the distance of the point
\xi\in\overline{D}^{c}
to the boundary of
D
. Its regularized version is denoted by
\Delta(\xi)
, cf. Stein
[
17
, p. 171, Theorem 2]
. In particular, it holds

\displaystyle C_{1}\delta(\xi)\leq\Delta(\xi)\leq C_{2}\delta(\xi),\quad\text{and}\qquad\left|\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}\Delta(\xi)\right|\leq B_{\alpha}(\delta(\xi))^{1-|\alpha|},\quad\xi\in\overline{D}^{c},

(7)

for constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
and
B_{\alpha}
independent of
D
.
We consider the point
\xi^{0}=(x^{0},\omega(x^{0}))\in\partial D
and denote by

\Gamma_{\xi^{0}}=\{\xi=(x^{\prime},x_{3}):\ x_{3}<\omega(x^{0}),\ |x_{3}-\omega(x^{0})|>M|x^{\prime}-x^{0}|\}

the lower cone with vertex at the point
\xi^{0}
, where
M
is the Lipschitz constant of
\omega
and
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\>x_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we clearly have
\Gamma_{\xi^{0}}\cap\overline{D}=\{\xi^{0}\}
. Moreover, the point
p=(x^{0},y)
,
y<\omega(x^{0})
, lies on the axis of the cone.
We make some elementary geometric calculations using the notation from the figure below. One can check easily that
\delta(p)\geq h
, and we see that

q=\left(\frac{\omega(x^{0})-y+M|x^{0}|}{M},y\right)\in\partial\Gamma_{\xi^{0}}
according to the definition of
\Gamma_{\xi^{0}}
.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\omega(x^{0})-y,

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega(x^{0})-y}{M},

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\frac{\omega(x^{0})-y}{M}\right)^{2}+(\omega(x^{0})-y)^{2}\right)^{1/2},

and hence,

h=\frac{ab}{c}=\frac{\omega(x^{0})-y}{(M^{2}+1)^{1/2}}.

From these observations we obtain

\delta(x^{\prime},x_{3})\geq\frac{\omega(x^{\prime})-x_{3}}{(1+M^{2})^{1/2}},

for arbitrary
(x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\overline{D}^{c}
, which implies

\omega(x^{\prime})-x_{3}\leq C_{3}\Delta(\xi).

Now we put
\delta^{*}(\xi)=2C_{3}\Delta(\xi)
and obtain the estimate

\delta^{*}(\xi)\geq 2(\omega(x^{\prime})-x_{3}).

From the definition of
\delta
and since
D
is a special Lipschitz domain we obtain

\displaystyle\delta(x^{\prime},x_{3})\leq d\left((x^{\prime},x_{3}),(x^{\prime},\omega(x^{\prime}))\right)=|x_{3}-\omega(x^{\prime})|=\omega(x^{\prime})-x_{3}

for all points
(x^{\prime},x_{3})\in\overline{D}^{c}
.
It further follows for all
\lambda>1
that

\displaystyle x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{\prime},x_{3})\geq x_{3}+\delta^{*}(x^{\prime},x_{3})\geq x_{3}+2(\omega(x^{\prime})-x_{3})=2\omega(x^{\prime})-x_{3}.

(8)

Finally, we also have

\displaystyle\delta^{*}(x^{\prime},x_{3})=2C_{3}\Delta(x^{\prime},x_{3})\leq 2C_{3}C_{2}\delta(x^{\prime},x_{3})\leq 2C_{3}C_{2}(\omega(x^{\prime})-x_{3}).

(9)

Step 2 (Stein’s extension operator):

Stein defined the operator
\mathfrak{E}
for a special Lipschitz domain

by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{E}u(x^{\prime},x_{3})=\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}u(x^{\prime},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{\prime},x_{3}))\psi(\lambda)\>d\lambda,\quad x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\>x_{3}\in\mathbb{R},\>x_{3}<\omega(x^{\prime}),

(10)

where

is a rapidly decaying smooth function with
\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\>d\lambda=1

.
Now we fix a point

for some constant

. Using this and the previous estimates for

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{1}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))|\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=A\int_{x_{3}+\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},s)|\frac{\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})^{-1}\>ds}{\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})^{-2}(s-x_{3})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq A\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})\int_{x_{3}+\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},s)|(s-x_{3})^{-2}\>ds

\displaystyle\leq A\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})\int_{2\omega(x^{0})-x_{3}}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},s)|(s-x_{3})^{-2}\>ds

\displaystyle\lesssim(\omega(x^{0})-x_{3})\int_{2\omega(x^{0})-x_{3}}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},s)|(s-x_{3})^{-2}\>ds,

(11)

where we used the integral substitution

as well as formulas (

) and (

) in the last two lines.

This pointwise estimate is now the basis for proving the relevant estimates in weighted Sobolev spaces.

Step 3 (Hardy’s inequality and an estimate for

):
The weight function of the space

\displaystyle\rho(x):=r(x)^{\delta-l+|\alpha|},

where

(i.e. the

Now we multiply both sides of (

) with

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\omega(x^{0})}\rho(x^{0},x_{3})^{p}|\mathfrak{E}u(x^{0},x_{3})|^{p}\>dx_{3}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{-\infty}^{\omega(x^{0})}(\omega(x^{0})-x_{3})^{p}\rho(x^{0},x_{3})^{p}\left(\int_{2\omega(x^{0})-x_{3}}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},s)|(s-x_{3})^{-2}\>ds\right)^{p}\>dx_{3}.

We make an integral substitution

with

and

with

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\omega(x^{0})}(\omega(x^{0})-x_{3})^{p}\rho(x^{0},x_{3})^{p}\left(\int_{2\omega(x^{0})-x_{3}}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},s)|(s-x_{3})^{-2}\>ds\right)^{p}\>dx_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{x_{3}}^{p}\rho(x^{0},\omega(x^{0})-\widetilde{x_{3}})^{p}\left(\int_{\omega(x^{0})+\widetilde{x_{3}}}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},s)|(s-\omega(x^{0})+\widetilde{x_{3}})^{-2}\>ds\right)^{p}\>d\widetilde{x_{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{x_{3}}^{p}\rho(x^{0},\omega(x^{0})-\widetilde{x_{3}})^{p}\left(\int_{\widetilde{x_{3}}}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},\widetilde{s}+\omega(x^{0}))|\widetilde{s}^{-2}\>d\widetilde{s}\right)^{p}\>d\widetilde{x_{3}}.

(13)

We now intend to apply the following version of Hardy’s inequality from

if it holds that

\displaystyle B(v,w):=\displaystyle\sup_{a>0}\left(\int_{0}^{a}|v(x)|^{p}\>dx\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{a}^{\infty}|w(x)|^{-q}\>dx\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

We thus need to check the condition (

), where we choose the functions

and

\displaystyle v(x)

\displaystyle=xr(x^{0},\omega(x^{0})-x)^{\gamma},

\displaystyle w(x)

\displaystyle=x^{2}r(x^{0},x+\omega(x^{0}))^{\gamma},

for some parameter

The assumption

\displaystyle|\omega(x^{0})|=|\omega(x_{1}^{0},x_{2}^{0})-\omega(x_{1}^{0},0)|\leq M|(x_{1}^{0},x_{2}^{0})-(x_{1}^{0},0)|=M|x_{2}^{0}|,

(16)

Furthermore, it holds

\displaystyle|x_{2}^{0}|^{2}\leq r(x^{0},\omega(x^{0})\pm x)^{2}.

(17)

Using (

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a}|v(x)|^{p}\>dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{a}x^{p}r(x^{0},\omega(x^{0})-x)^{\gamma p}\>dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\max(|x_{2}^{0}|,a)^{\gamma p}\int_{0}^{a}x^{p}\>dx\lesssim\max(|x_{2}^{0}|,a)^{\gamma p}a^{p+1}.

(18)

Now we treat the second integral in (

For

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}|w(x)|^{-q}\>dx

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{\infty}x^{-2q}r(x^{0},x+\omega(x^{0}))^{-\gamma q}\>dx\lesssim|x_{2}^{0}|^{-\gamma q}a^{-2q+1}.

Case 2:
For

\ x\geq a\geq 2M|x^{0}_{2}|\geq 2|\omega(x^{0})|,\

from which we conclude

\displaystyle r(x^{0},\omega(x^{0})+x)\geq|\omega(x^{0})+x|\geq|x-|\omega(x^{0})||\geq\frac{x}{2},

and hence,

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}|w(x)|^{-q}\>dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{a}^{\infty}x^{-2q}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{-\gamma q}\>dx\lesssim\int_{a}^{\infty}x^{-(2+\gamma)q}\>dx\lesssim a^{-(2+\gamma)q+1}.

From the two cases above we now see that

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{\infty}|w(x)|^{-q}\>dx\lesssim a^{-2q+1}\max(a,|x_{2}^{0}|)^{-\gamma q}.

(19)

Now we estimate

using (

\displaystyle B(v,w)

\displaystyle\lesssim\displaystyle\sup_{a>0}\left(\max(|x_{2}^{0}|,a)^{\gamma}a^{\frac{p+1}{p}}\max(|x_{2}^{0}|,a)^{-\gamma}a^{\frac{-2q+1}{q}}\right)=\sup_{a>0}1=1<\infty,

independent of

, and therefore, condition (

and

. Applying now Hardy’s inequality (

) with

and
w
for

to (

) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\widetilde{x_{3}}^{p}\rho(x^{0},\omega(x^{0})-\widetilde{x_{3}})^{p}\left(\int_{\widetilde{x_{3}}}^{\infty}|u(x^{0},\widetilde{s}+\omega(x^{0}))|\widetilde{s}^{-2}\>d\widetilde{s}\right)^{p}\>d\widetilde{x_{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(x^{0},\widetilde{x_{3}}+\omega(x^{0}))^{p}|u(x^{0},\widetilde{x_{3}}+\omega(x^{0}))|^{p}\>d\widetilde{x_{3}}=\int_{\omega(x^{0})}^{\infty}\rho(x^{0},x_{3})^{p}|u(x^{0},x_{3})|^{p}\>dx_{3}.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\omega(x^{0})}\rho(x^{0},x_{3})^{p}|\mathfrak{E}u(x^{0},x_{3})|^{p}\>dx_{3}\lesssim\int_{\omega(x^{0})}^{\infty}\rho(x^{0},x_{3})^{p}|u(x^{0},x_{3})|^{p}\>dx_{3}.

Since

(21)

Summing (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x^{0},x_{3})|\mathfrak{E}u(x^{0},x_{3})|^{p}\>dx_{3}\>dx^{0}\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\omega(x^{0})}^{\infty}\rho(x^{0},x_{3})|u(x^{0},x_{3})|^{p}\>dx_{3}\>dx^{0}.

Taking the

\displaystyle\|\rho\,\mathfrak{E}u|L_{p}(\real^{3})\|\lesssim\|\rho u|L_{p}(\real^{3})\|,

which takes care of the term with

Step 4 (The case
\alpha\neq 0
):

Next we need to investigate the partial derivatives of
\mathfrak{E}u
, i.e. we consider now
\partial^{\alpha}(\mathfrak{E}u)
, where

Since

is a rapidly decaying function (i.e. decays faster than every polynomial), applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem we obtain that all of the derivatives of

are bounded, and hence we can change the order of integration and derivation as follows:

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}(\mathfrak{E}u)(x^{0},x_{3})

\displaystyle=\partial^{\alpha}\left(\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}u(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\psi(\lambda)\>d\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\partial^{\alpha}\bigl[u(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\bigr]\>d\lambda.

We consider the first-order derivatives of
\mathfrak{E}u
using the chain rule. This yields

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(\mathfrak{E}u)}{\partial x_{i}}(x^{0},x_{3})

\displaystyle\quad=\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\partial_{x_{i}}\bigl[u(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\bigr]\>d\lambda

\displaystyle\quad=\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\Bigl(\lambda\delta_{x_{i}}^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})u_{x_{3}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))+u_{x_{i}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\Bigr)\>d\lambda

(23)

for

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(\mathfrak{E}u)}{\partial x_{3}}(x^{0},x_{3})

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\partial_{x_{3}}\bigl[u(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\bigr]\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\bigl(\lambda\delta_{x_{3}}^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})+1\bigr)u_{x_{3}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\>d\lambda.

(24)

Clearly higher-order derivative can be calculated similar to (
3.1
) and (
3.1
). Exemplary, we find, once again using the chain rule,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}(\mathfrak{E}u)}{\partial x_{3}^{2}}(x^{0},x_{3})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\left(\frac{\partial(\mathfrak{E}u)}{\partial x_{3}}(x^{0},x_{3})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\left(\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)((\lambda\delta_{x_{3}}^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})+1)u_{x_{3}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})))\>d\lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{3}}\bigg((\lambda\delta_{x_{3}}^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})+1)u_{x_{3}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\bigg)\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)((\lambda\delta_{x_{3}}^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})+1)^{2}u_{x_{3}^{2}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda\delta_{x_{3}^{2}}^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})u_{x_{3}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})))\>d\lambda

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\lambda^{2}\delta_{x_{3}}^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})^{2}u_{x_{3}^{2}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\>d\lambda}_{=:I}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{2\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\lambda\delta_{x_{3}}^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})u_{x_{3}^{2}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\>d\lambda}_{=:II}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)u_{x_{3}^{2}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\>d\lambda}_{=:III}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underbrace{\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\lambda\delta_{x_{3}^{2}}^{*}(x^{0},x_{3})u_{x_{3}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))\>d\lambda}_{=:IV}.

(25)

Derivatives of higher order just result in a higher number of terms of similar form. To derive norm-estimates, every term will be treated separately, using the moment conditions for
\psi
together with suitable Taylor expansions. This will be illustrated again by discussing the case

\frac{\partial^{2}(\mathfrak{E}u)}{\partial x_{3}^{2}}
in detail. Thus we need to derive estimates for the integrals I–IV in (
3.1
). We first recall that according to (

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}\delta^{*}\leq c_{\alpha}(\delta^{*})^{1-|\alpha|}

(26)

and

|\psi(\lambda)|\leq A_{k}\lambda^{-k}\quad\text{for any}\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

We consider
I
for

and obtain with a similar argument as in the case

[cf. (

)]

\displaystyle|I|

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}A_{4}\lambda^{-4}\lambda^{2}c_{x_{3}}^{2}|u_{x_{3}^{2}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))|\>d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim A_{4}c_{x_{3}}^{2}(\omega(x^{0})-x_{3})\int_{2\omega(x^{0})-x_{3}}^{\infty}|u_{x_{3}^{2}}(x^{0},s)|(s-x_{3})^{-2}\>ds.

For

we similarly find

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle\leq 2A_{3}\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-3}\lambda c_{x_{3}}|u_{x_{3}^{2}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))|\>d\lambda

Moreover, concerning

we get [cf. (

)],

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle\leq A_{2}\int_{1}^{\infty}\lambda^{-2}|u_{x_{3}^{2}}(x^{0},x_{3}+\lambda\delta^{*}(x^{0},x_{3}))|\>d\lambda

The last term

\displaystyle|IV|

Altogether, the integral estimates for

Step 5 (The case

3.3
Extension operator for the cone

Having established an extension operator for our weighted Sobolev spaces on the fixed layer
K^{\prime}\subset K
of the cone in Lemma
3.8
we are now going to construct an extension operator on the whole cone
K
as follows. We first decompose the cone
K
into several layers
K_{j}
and use extension operators on each layer (which are uniformly bounded w.r.t.
j
). Afterwards we glue these extension operators together in a suitable way to obtain an extension for the whole cone.
For this let

\displaystyle K_{j}=\{x\in K:2^{-j-1}<|x|<2^{-j+1}\}

(36)

be a (dyadic) layer of the cone. The following lemma shows that on these layers the distance of a point
x\in K_{j}
to the restricted set
S_{j}:=\overline{K_{j}}\cap S
is equivalent to its distance to the whole set
S
.

Lemma 3.9
.

For the layer
E_{j}:=\{x\in\real^{3}:2^{-j-1}<|x|<2^{-j+1}\}
and the set
S_{j}
defined above consider the respective distance function on
E_{j}
w.r.t. the edge
M_{k}
,

r_{k,j}(x):=\mathrm{dist}(x,S_{j}\cap M_{k}).

Then we have

r_{k,j}(x)\sim r_{k}(x)\qquad\text{for all }\quad x\in E_{j},

i.e., the distance of a point
x\in E_{j}
to the set
S_{j}\cap M_{k}
is equivalent to the distance of
x
to the whole edge
M_{k}
.

Proof.

We decompose
x
as
x=x_{\parallel}+x_{\perp}
with
x_{\parallel}
being the component parallel to
M_{k}
and consider the difference

\Delta=2^{-j-1}-|x_{\parallel}|.

Case 1:
Let
x\in E_{j}
and
\Delta>0
, i.e.

|x_{\parallel}|<2^{-j-1}.

It is clear that

\displaystyle r_{k}(x)\leq r_{k,j}(x).

(37)

By geometric considerations we see that for
x\in E_{j}
it holds

r_{k}^{2}(x)+|x_{\parallel}|^{2}=|x|^{2}\geq(2^{-j-1})^{2}=(\Delta+|x_{\parallel}|)^{2},

hence

\displaystyle r_{k}^{2}(x)\geq\Delta^{2}+2\Delta|x_{\parallel}|\geq\Delta^{2}.

(38)

Since
r_{k,j}^{2}(x)=r_{k}^{2}(x)+\Delta^{2}
, using (
38
) we obtain

\ r_{k,j}^{2}(x)\leq 2r_{k}^{2}(x),\

i.e.,

\displaystyle r_{k,j}(x)\lesssim r_{k}(x).

(39)

Therefore (
37
) and (
39
) imply that

\displaystyle r_{k,j}(x)\sim r_{k}(x)\qquad\text{for}\quad x\in E_{j},\ \Delta>0.

Case 2:
Let
x\in E_{j}
and
\Delta\leq 0
, then

2^{-j-1}\leq|x_{\parallel}|\leq 2^{-j+1}.

Obviously in this case we have

r_{k}(x)=r_{k,j}(x).

Together Cases 1 and 2 yield

\ r_{k,j}\sim r_{k}\ \text{ on }\ E_{j}.

∎

1. Introduction

We consider the initial value problem for the zero-pressure gas dynamics system

(1)

u_{t}+\left({u^{2}}/{2}\right)_{x}=0,\ \rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0,\ \left(x,t\right)\in\textbf{R}^{1}\times\left(0,\infty\right),

under the assumption that the initial data consists of a linear combination of a characteristic function and a
\delta
-measure, more precisely,

(2)

u|_{t=0}=u_{a}\ \chi_{{}_{\left(-\infty,a\right)}}+u_{b}\ \delta_{x=b},\ \rho|_{t=0}=\rho_{c}\ \chi_{{}_{\left(-\infty,c\right)}}+\rho_{d}\ \delta_{x=d}.

Here
a
,
b
,
c
,
d
are fixed with
a<c<b<d
and
u_{a}
,
u_{b}
,
\rho_{c}
,
\rho_{d}
are real constants.

The system (
1
) is an important system of partial differential equations finding applications in cosmology and having close relations with the Zeldovich approximation (
[
9
]
). (
1
) is used to describe the evolution of matter in the expansion of universe as cold dust moving only under the effect of gravity. Here the objects under study, namely
u
and
\rho
, respectively denote the velocity and the density of the particles,
x\in{\textbf{R}}^{1}
denotes the space variable and
t>0
denotes time.

In (
1
), the first equation involving the velocity component
u
alone is the Burgers equation. In general, we cannot expect to find solutions of this problem in the class of smooth functions even for smooth initial data and so we need the notion of weak solutions satisfying (
1
) in the sense of distributions, namely

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(u\ \phi_{t}+f(u)\ \phi_{x}\right)\ dxdt+\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(x,0)\ \phi(x,0)\ dx=0,\ \phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\textbf{R}^{1}\times[0,\infty)\right).

The initial value problem for the Burgers equation was studied by Hopf
[
5
]
using the method of vanishing viscosity, where the author has considered the problem

\displaystyle u_{t}+\left(\frac{u^{2}}{2}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=\mu u_{xx},

\displaystyle u|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=u_{0}

for
\mu>0
with locally integrable initial data
u_{0}
under the additional assumption that

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\int_{0}^{x}u_{0}\left(\xi\right)\ d\xi}{x^{2}}=0.

This resulted in an explicit formula

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ \frac{x-y}{t}\ e^{-\frac{F(x,y,t)}{2\mu}}}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ e^{-\frac{F(x,y,t)}{2\mu}}},

where
F:(x,y,t)\longmapsto\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{2t}+\int_{0}^{y}\ u_{0}\left(\xi\right)\ d\xi
. The subsequent aymptotic limit as
\mu\rightarrow 0
was discussed in detail.

The work of Hopf
[
5
]
was generalized by Lax
[
7
]
to general conservation laws of the form

u_{t}+\left[f(u)\right]_{x}=0,

under bounded measurable initial data
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}
, under the assumption that the flux function
f\in C^{2}
is strictly convex
\left(f^{\prime\prime}>0\right)
with superlinear growth at infinity
\left(\lim_{y\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(y)}{|y|}=\infty\right)
. It was observed that for each fixed
t>0
, except possibly for countably many
x\in\textbf{R}^{1}
, there exists a unique minimizer
y(x,t)
for

\displaystyle\theta\left(x,y,t\right):=f^{*}\left(\frac{x-y}{t}\right)+\int_{0}^{y}\ u_{0}\left(\xi\right)d\xi,

where
f^{*}:z\longmapsto{\displaystyle{\max_{p\in\textbf{R}^{1}}}}\{pz-f(p)\}
denotes the convex conjugate of
f
.

A weak solution of the associated initial value problem could then be given by

\displaystyle\overline{u}(x,t)=\left(f^{*}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{x-y(x,t)}{t}\right),

which is defined pointwise a.e.

There are many published works For scalar conservation laws with bounded nonnegative measures as initial data (see Bertsch et. al.
[
1

[
4
]
, Liu and Pierre
[
8

with the initial data

at
t=0
being a bounded Borel measure. The existence and uniqueness of solutions will then depend on the following factors:

(1)

The initial data
u_{0}
and its sign

(2)

Whether the flux
\phi
is odd or convex

It is natural to expect the flux function
\phi
will have a smoothing effect on the solution by virtue of its strong nonlinearity.

The vanishing viscosity method has been previously applied by Joseph

6
]
for the system (

\left(u_{{}_{R}},\rho_{{}_{R}}\right),\ &x>0.\end{cases}

The modified adhesion model corresponding to the given problem (

\left(u_{{}_{R}}x,\rho_{{}_{R}}x\right),\ &x>0.\end{cases}

It was observed that the distributional derivatives

The first step involved the linearization of the first equation in (

The new linear problem to be considered was

\displaystyle S_{t}^{\epsilon}

\displaystyle S^{\epsilon}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\rho_{{}_{L}}x\ e^{-\frac{u_{{}_{L}}x}{\epsilon}},\ &x<0,\\

These linear problems were explicitly solved and

\displaystyle\left(u^{\epsilon}\left(x,0\right),\rho^{\epsilon}\left(x,0\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\left(u_{L},\rho_{L}\right),\ &{x<0},\\

2. Computation of the vanishing viscosity limits

Let us now generalize the ideas discussed in the previous section for our problem.

For each
\epsilon>0
, suppose
\left(u^{\epsilon},\rho^{\epsilon}\right)
are approximate solutions for the problem

(3)

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}_{t}+\left(\frac{(u^{\epsilon})^{2}}{2}\right)_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{2}u^{\epsilon}_{xx},\ \rho^{\epsilon}_{t}+(\rho^{\epsilon}u^{\epsilon})_{x}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\rho^{\epsilon}_{xx},

(4)

\displaystyle u^{\epsilon}|_{t=0}

\displaystyle=u_{a}\ \chi_{{}_{\left(-\infty,a\right)}}+u_{b}\ \delta_{x=b},\ \rho^{\epsilon}|_{t=0}=\rho_{c}\ \chi_{{}_{\left(-\infty,c\right)}}+\rho_{d}\ \delta_{x=d}.

First let us observe that if
\left(U^{\epsilon},R^{\epsilon}\right)
is a solution to the system

(5)

U_{t}^{\epsilon}+\frac{\left(U_{x}^{\epsilon}\right)^{2}}{2}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}U_{xx}^{\epsilon},\ R_{t}^{\epsilon}+R_{x}^{\epsilon}U_{x}^{\epsilon}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}R_{xx}^{\epsilon}

under the initial conditions

(6)

\displaystyle U^{\epsilon}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}u_{a}(x-a),\ &x<a,\\
0,\ &a<x<b,\\
u_{b},\ &x>b,\end{cases}

\displaystyle R^{\epsilon}(x,0)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\rho_{c}(x-c),\ &x<c,\\
0,\ &c<x<d,\\
\rho_{d},\ &x>d,\end{cases}

then the distributional derivatives
\overline{u}^{\epsilon}:=U_{x}^{\epsilon}
and
\overline{\rho}^{\epsilon}:=R_{x}^{\epsilon}
in the space variable
x
will solve the problem (
3
) under the initial conditions (
4
).

The following theorem gives the detailed analysis of the asymptotic behavior of

as

in each of the regions

\left(c,b\right)

\left(b,d\right)
and finally
\left(d,\infty\right)
. Within each of these regions, the limit
\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\ \left(u^{\epsilon},\rho^{\epsilon}\right)
is separately evaluated in the subregions

(1)

Let us prove the following properties of the function erfc:

(a)

For every
z\in\textbf{R}^{1}
,
\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)+\textnormal{erfc}\left(-z\right)=\sqrt{\pi}

(b)

\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)=0

(c)

\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)=\left(\frac{1}{2z}-\frac{1}{4z^{3}}+o\left(\frac{1}{z^{3}}\right)\right)\ e^{-z^{2}}\textnormal{ as }z\rightarrow\infty

(d)

\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\ z\ \textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)e^{z^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}

For proving the first property, we fix
z\in\textbf{R}^{1}
and proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\textnormal{erfc}\left(-z\right)=\int_{-z}^{\infty}e^{-y^{2}}\ dy

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-y^{2}}\ dy-\int_{-\infty}^{-z}e^{-y^{2}}\ dy

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}-\int_{z}^{\infty}e^{-\eta^{2}}\ d\eta

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\pi}-\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right).

The second property follows from the definition of erfc:

\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)=\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\int_{z}^{\infty}\ e^{-s^{2}}\ ds=0.

We now verify the third and fourth properties. For any
z>1
, we can integrate by parts to get

\displaystyle\textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{z}^{\infty}\ \left(-\frac{1}{2t}\right)\ \frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-t^{2}}\right)\ dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2z}\ e^{-z^{2}}+\int_{z}^{\infty}\ \frac{1}{4t^{3}}\ \frac{d}{dt}\left(e^{-t^{2}}\right)\ dt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2z}-\frac{1}{4z^{3}}\right)\ e^{-z^{2}}+\int_{z}^{\infty}\ \frac{3}{4t^{4}}\ e^{-t^{2}}\ dt,

so that

\displaystyle\left|z^{3}\ \left[e^{z^{2}}\ \textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)-\left(\frac{1}{2z}-\frac{1}{4z^{3}}\right)\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{3}{8z^{2}}\ \int_{z}^{\infty}\ \frac{d}{dt}\ \left(e^{z^{2}-t^{2}}\right)\ dt\leq\frac{3}{8z^{2}},

and since
\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\frac{3}{8z^{2}}=0
, this proves our claim. This last inequality also implies that

z^{2}\ \left|z\ \textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)\ e^{z^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right|\rightarrow\frac{1}{4}\textnormal{ as }z\rightarrow\infty,

and hence we conclude the proof of the fourth property
\lim_{z\rightarrow\infty}\ z\ \textnormal{erfc}\left(z\right)e^{z^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}
.

Diffusive limits of the steady state radiative heat transfer system: Curvature effects
Mohamed Ghattassi, Xiaokai Huo, Nader Masmoudi

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the diffusive limit of the nonlinear radiative heat transfer system with curved boundary domain ( two dimensional disk ). The solution constructed in [ 5 ] by the leading order interior solution and the boundary layer corrections fails here to approximate the solutions in L ∞ L^{\infty} sense for the diffusive limit. The present paper aims to construct a geometric correction to the boundary layer problem and obtain a valid approximate solution in L ∞ L^{\infty} sense. The main tools to overcome the convergence problem, are to use matched asymptotic expansion techniques, fixed-point theorems, linear and nonlinear stability analysis of the boundary layer problem. In particular, the spectral assumption on the leading order interior solution, which was proposed for the flat case in [ 6 ] , is shown to be still valid which guarantee the stability of the boundary layer expansion with geometric corrections. Moreover, the convergence result established in [ 5 , Lemma 10] remain applicable for the approximate solution with geometric corrections.



1.
Introduction

We consider the following radiative heat transfer system

(1.1)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta T^{\varepsilon}+\langle\psi^{\varepsilon}-(T^{\varepsilon})^{4}\rangle=0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\beta\cdot\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}+\psi^{\varepsilon}-(T^{\varepsilon})^{4}=0,

on the two dimensional disk
\Omega=\{x=(x_{1},x_{2}):|x|\leq 1\}
with radiative velocity
\beta\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
. Here
T^{\varepsilon}=T^{\varepsilon}(x)
is the temperature and
\psi^{\varepsilon}=\psi^{\varepsilon}(x,\beta)
is the radiative intensity and
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes the integration over
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, i.e.
\langle f\rangle:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}f(\beta)d\beta
. We denote by
\varepsilon>0
a small parameter, describing the ratio of a typical photon mean free path to a typical length scale of the problem, for more details we refer to
[
4
]
. The system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is supplemented with the non-homogenous Dirichlet boundary conditions

(1.3)

\displaystyle T^{\varepsilon}(x)=T_{b}(x),\quad\text{for any }x\in\partial\Omega,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\psi^{\varepsilon}(x,\beta)=\psi_{b}(x,\beta),\quad\text{for any }(x,\beta)\in\Gamma_{-},

where
\partial\Omega
is the boundary of
\Omega
and, the inflow boundary
\Gamma_{-}
and out flow boundary
\Gamma_{+}
are defined respectively by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{-}:=\{0(x,\beta)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1}:n(x)\cdot\beta<0\},

\displaystyle\Gamma_{+}:=\{(x,\beta)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1}:n(x)\cdot\beta>0\},

where
n(x)
is the exterior normal vector on the boundary. Similarly, the out-flow boundary and
\Gamma:=\Gamma_{+}\cup\Gamma_{-}\cup\Gamma_{0}
with
\Gamma_{0}:=\{(x,\beta)\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1}:n(x)\cdot\beta=0\}
.

However, when
\psi_{b}=T_{b}^{4}
, there is no boundary layer for (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) and one can prove the solution to (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) converges to the solution of the nonlinear elliptic equation

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Delta T_{0}+\pi\Delta T_{0}^{4}=0,\quad\psi_{0}=T_{0}^{4},

with Dirichlet boundary conditions
T_{0}(x)=T_{b}(x)
for
x\in\partial\Omega
. However, for general boundary data, boundary layer exists and
when
\varepsilon\to 0
,
T^{\varepsilon}
no longer converges to
T_{0}
solution to the above nonlinear elliptic equation (
1.5
). A boundary layer correction
(\bar{T}_{0},\bar{\psi}_{0})
should be added to
(T_{0},\psi_{0})
to get the approximate solution. For the flat boundary, it was proved in
[
5
]
that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|T^{\varepsilon}-T_{0}-\bar{T}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=O(\varepsilon),\quad\|\psi^{\varepsilon}-T_{0}^{4}-\bar{\psi}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=O(\varepsilon).

The boundary layer
\bar{T}_{0}
is defined by a suitable cut-off of the corresponding half-space boundary Milne problem of system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Let
(\tilde{T}_{0},\tilde{\psi}_{0})
be the leading order of the half-space Milne problem.
The above inequalities were proved under the condition that
\tilde{T}_{0}
satisfy a spectral assumption
[
6
,
(A)
]
, which reads as

(
A
)

A function
h\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfies the spectral assumption if there exists a constant
\beta>0
, such that

(1.7)

\displaystyle M\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\beta\eta}(2h^{\frac{3}{2}})^{2}|\partial_{\eta}f|^{2}d\eta\geq 4\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\beta\eta}|\partial_{\eta}(2h^{\frac{3}{2}})|^{2}f^{2}d\eta,

holds for some constant
M<1
and for any measurable function
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
f(0)=0
.

The spectral assumption implies the linear stability of the boundary layer problem which is crucial for establishing the convergence result (
1.6
). However, for the not-flat boundary case, curvature effects brings singularities of the boundary layer problem at the boundary and the inequalities (
1.6
) fail to hold. We address this problem by constructing a correction
(\bar{T}_{0}^{\varepsilon},\bar{\psi}^{\varepsilon})
of the boundary layer to improve the approximations near the boundary and erase the singularities. Moreover, it is expected that the spectral assumption
(A)
still implies the linear stability of the boundary layer problem with geometric correction.

1.2.
Contributions

When
T^{4}=\langle\psi\rangle/2\pi
, system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) reduce to the classical transport equation
\varepsilon\beta\cdot\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}+\psi^{\varepsilon}-\langle\psi^{\varepsilon}\rangle/2\pi=0
. The diffusive limit was first studied in
[
1
]
, where an approximate solution is constructed by adding interior solution and boundary layer corrections. In
[
10
]
, a counter-example shows that such an approximation can fail when the boundary is not flat. By introducing a geometric correction to the boundary layer problem, a valid approximate solution was constructed and can be proved to satisfy
\|\psi^{\varepsilon}-\psi_{0}-\bar{\psi}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1})}=O(\varepsilon)
, where
\psi_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfy
\Delta\psi_{0}=0
and
\bar{\psi}_{0}^{\varepsilon}
is the solution of the geometric corrected boundary correction. The aim of this paper is to find a similar geometric correction to the approximate solution for system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). However, the above discussion is not comprehensive: we refer the reader to the review articles,
[
7
,
8
]
and references therein for a more thorough review of the boundary layer analysis of some singular perturbation problems.

We now state the difference between our work and
[
10
]
. First, our system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) is nonlinear which generates a coupling between the interior solution and the boundary layer corrections. Indeed, to construct the boundary layer expansions, we need to approximate the interior solutions by their Taylor’s expansion. Moreover, the corresponding boundary half-space Milne problems associated to system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) are nonlinear for the leading order and linear for higher orders, different from
[
10
]
where the Milne problems are linear for any order of expansions. The coupling between the elliptic and transport equation brings additional difficulties for proving the existence, uniqueness and stability of the Milne problems. In particular, the spectral assumption
(A)
is needed to show the linear stability of the Milne problems in our problem.
We use techniques from our previous work
[
6
]
but accounting for the geometric corrections. We can show the spectral assumption
(A)
also implies the linear stability of the geometric corrected Milne problem. Furthermore, due to the nonlniearity of our problem it’s not possible to derive the estimates (
1.8
) by using the Maximum principle, since the difference
(T^{\varepsilon}-T_{0}-\bar{T}_{0}^{\varepsilon},\psi^{\varepsilon}-\psi_{0}-\bar{\psi}^{\varepsilon})
satisfies a different system from (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Instead, the Banach fixed point theorem is used. We here show that such a technique used in
[
5
]
for the flat case, can be extended to the curvature boundary case by considering the geometric corrections.

2.
Approximate solutions

In this section, we construct approximation solutions to system (
1.1
)-(
1.2

2.1.
Interior expansions

We take the ansatz

\displaystyle T^{\varepsilon}\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k}T_{k},\quad\psi^{\varepsilon}\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{k}\psi_{k}

into system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
). Collecting terms with the same order gives

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\langle\psi_{0}-T_{0}^{4}\rangle=0,\\
\psi_{0}-T_{0}^{4}=0,\end{matrix}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\langle\psi_{1}-4T_{0}^{3}T_{1}\rangle=0,\\
\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{0}+\psi_{1}-4T_{0}^{3}T_{1}=0,\end{matrix}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\Delta T_{0}+\langle\psi_{2}-4T_{0}^{3}T_{2}-6T_{0}^{2}T_{1}^{2}\rangle=0,\\
\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{1}+\psi_{2}-4T_{0}^{3}T_{2}-6T_{0}^{2}T_{1}^{2}=0,\end{matrix}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\vdots

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{matrix}\Delta T_{k-2}+\langle\psi_{k}-\mathcal{C}(T,k)\rangle=0,\\
\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-1}+\psi_{k}-\mathcal{C}(T,k)=0,\end{matrix}\right.

where
\mathcal{C}(T,k)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(T,k)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+l+m=k\\
i,j,l,m\geq 0\end{subarray}}T_{i}T_{j}T_{l}T_{m}.

Comparing the two equations in (
2.1
) gives

\displaystyle\Delta T_{k-2}=\nabla\cdot\langle\beta\psi_{k-1}\rangle.

Using
\psi_{k-1}=\mathcal{C}(T,k-1)-\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-2}
in the above equation leads to

\displaystyle\Delta T_{k-2}=-\nabla\cdot\langle\beta\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-2}\rangle.

Using
\psi_{k-2}=\mathcal{C}(T,k-2)-\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-3}
gives

\displaystyle\Delta T_{k-2}=-\nabla\cdot\langle\beta\beta\cdot\nabla\mathcal{C}(T,k-2)\rangle+\langle(\beta\cdot\nabla)^{3}\psi_{k-3}\rangle,

which is

\displaystyle\Delta T_{k-2}+\pi\Delta\mathcal{C}(T,k-2)=\langle(\beta\cdot\nabla)^{3}\psi_{k-3}\rangle.

Thus we get
(T_{k},\psi_{k})
satisfy for any
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Delta T_{k}+\pi\Delta\mathcal{C}(T,k)=\langle(\beta\cdot\nabla)^{3}\psi_{k-1}\rangle,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}=\mathcal{C}(T,k)-\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-1}.

Note here
T_{k},\psi_{k}
are zero for any index
k<0
. Considering
\mathcal{C}(T,0)=T_{0}^{4}
and for
k\geq 1
,
\mathcal{C}(T,k)=4T_{0}T_{k}+\mathcal{E}(T,k-1)
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(T,k-1)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j+l+m=k\\
1\leq i,j,l,m\leq k-1\end{subarray}}T_{i}T_{j}T_{l}T_{m},

which only depends on
T_{s}
,
s\geq k-1
, we can rewrite the previous equations as for
k=0
,

\displaystyle\Delta T_{0}+\pi\Delta T_{0}^{4}=0,\quad\psi_{0}=T_{0}^{4},

and for
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\Delta T_{k}+\pi\Delta(4T_{0}^{4}T_{k})=\langle(\beta\cdot\nabla)^{3}\psi_{k-1}\rangle-\pi\Delta\mathcal{E}(T,k-1),\quad\psi_{k}=4T_{0}^{3}T_{k}+\mathcal{E}(T,k-1)-\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-1}.

Therefore, the leading order system is a nonlinear elliptic equation while the higher order system is a linear elliptic equation.

Boundary layer corrections

2.3.
Construction of approximate solutions

Let
\delta>0
be a small constant. Define the cut-off function

\displaystyle\chi_{0}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\text{for }0\leq r\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\delta,\\
0,&\text{for }r\geq\tfrac{3}{4}\delta,\\

and

\displaystyle\chi(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\text{for }0\leq r\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\delta,\\

\in(0,1),&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

We begin by constructing the leading order approximations. The zeroth-order boundary layer correction
(\bar{T}_{0},\bar{\psi}_{0})
is provided by solving

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{T}_{0}(\eta,\theta)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{T}_{0}(\eta,\theta)-\tilde{T}_{0,\infty}(\infty,\theta)),~\bar{\psi}_{0}(\eta,\theta,\xi)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{\psi}_{0}(\eta,\theta,\xi)-\tilde{\psi}_{0,\infty}(\infty,\theta,\xi))\\
\partial_{\eta}^{2}\tilde{T}_{0}+\langle\tilde{\psi}_{0}-\tilde{T}_{0}^{4}\rangle=0,\\
\sin(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}+\tilde{\psi}_{0}-\tilde{T}_{0}^{4}=0,\end{array}\right.

combined to the following boundary condition

\tilde{T}_{0}(0,\theta)=T_{b}(\theta),\quad\tilde{\psi}_{0}(0,\theta,\xi)=\psi_{b}(\theta,\xi),\text{ for }\sin(\theta+\xi)>0.

Assuming sufficient regularity on the boundary data, one can prove as
\eta\to 0
, there exists a constant function
\tilde{T}_{\infty}=\tilde{T}_{0,\infty}(\theta)
independent of
\eta
such that
\lim_{\eta\to\infty}\tilde{T}_{0}(\eta)=\tilde{T}_{0,\infty}
and
\lim_{\eta\to\infty}\tilde{\psi}_{0}(\eta)=\tilde{T}^{4}_{0,\infty}
. This provides the boundary condition for the interior expansions. The leading order of the interior expansion is

(2.26)

\displaystyle\Delta T_{0}+\pi\Delta T_{0}^{4}=0,\quad\text{in }\Omega

\displaystyle\psi=T_{0}^{4}\qquad\text{in }\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1}

\displaystyle T_{0}(x)=\tilde{T}_{0,\infty},\quad\text{for }x\in\partial\Omega.

We next construct the first order approximations. The boundary layer correction
(\bar{T}_{1},\bar{\psi}_{1})
is given by solving

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{T}_{1}(\eta,\theta)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{T}_{1}(\eta,\theta)-\tilde{T}_{1,\infty}(\infty,\theta)),~\bar{\psi}_{1}(\eta,\theta,\xi)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{\psi}_{1}(\eta,\theta,\xi)-\tilde{\psi}_{1,\infty}(\infty,\theta,\xi))\\
\\
\partial_{\eta}^{2}\tilde{T}_{1}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}-4\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}\tilde{T}_{1}=\frac{\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\partial_{\eta}\tilde{T}_{0}\langle 4(\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}-P_{0}^{3})(P_{1}-P_{1}(0))\rangle,\\
\\
\sin(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{1}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}-4\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}\tilde{T}_{1}=\frac{\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\theta}\bar{\psi}_{0}+4(\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}-P_{0}^{3})(P_{1}-P_{1}(0)),\\
\\
\text{B.C.: }\tilde{T}_{1}(0,\theta)=0,\quad\tilde{\psi}_{1}(0,\theta,\xi)=\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{0}(0),\quad\text{for }\sin(\theta+\xi)>0.\end{array}\right.

The interior expansion
(T_{0},\psi_{0})
is given by

\displaystyle\Delta T_{1}+\pi\Delta(4T_{0}^{3}T_{1})=0,\quad\psi_{1}=4T_{0}^{3}T_{1}-\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{0},\quad\text{in }\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1},

\displaystyle T_{1}(x)=\tilde{T}_{1,\infty},\quad\text{for }x\in\partial\Omega.

The boundary layer corrections
(\bar{T}_{k},\bar{\psi}_{k})
,
k\geq 1
are given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{T}_{k}(\eta,\theta)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{T}_{k}(\eta,\theta)-\tilde{T}_{k,\infty}(\infty,\theta)),~\bar{\psi}_{k}(\eta,\theta,\xi)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{\psi}_{k}(\eta,\theta,\xi)-\tilde{\psi}_{k,\infty}(\infty,\theta,\xi))\\
\\
\partial_{\eta}^{2}\tilde{T}_{k}+\tilde{\psi}_{k}-4\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}\tilde{T}_{k}=\frac{\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\partial_{\eta}\tilde{T}_{k-1}+\frac{\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{(1-\varepsilon\eta)^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\tilde{T}_{k-2}+\langle 4(\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}-P_{0}^{3})(P_{k}-P_{k}(0))\rangle\\
\\
\qquad+\langle\mathcal{E}(\bar{T}+P,k-1)-\mathcal{E}(P,k-1),\rangle\\
\\
\sin(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{k}+\tilde{\psi}_{k}-4\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}\tilde{T}_{k}=\frac{\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\theta}\bar{\psi}_{k-1}+4(\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}-P_{0}^{3})(P_{k}-P_{k}(0))\\
\\
\qquad+\mathcal{E}(\bar{T}+P,k-1)-\mathcal{E}(P,k-1),\\
\\
\text{B.C.: }\tilde{T}_{k}(0,\theta)=0,\quad\tilde{\psi}_{k}(0,\theta,\xi)=\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-1}(0)-\mathcal{E}(T(0),k-1),\quad\text{for }\sin(\theta+\xi)>0.\end{array}\right.

The interior expansions
(T_{k},\psi_{k})
,
k\geq 1
are solved by

\displaystyle\Delta T_{k}+\pi\Delta(4T_{0}^{3}T_{k})=-\pi\Delta\mathcal{E}(T,k-1)+\langle(\beta\cdot\nabla)^{3}\psi_{k-1}\rangle,

\displaystyle\psi_{k}=4T_{0}^{3}T_{k}+\mathcal{E}(T,k-1)-\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-1},

\displaystyle\text{B.C.: }T_{k}(x)=\tilde{T}_{k,\infty},\quad\text{for }x\in\partial\Omega.

It is expected that when
\varepsilon
is small, the addition of the boundary layer corrects well describes the boundary layer effects and we should have for example

\displaystyle\|T^{\varepsilon}-T_{0}-\bar{T}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=O(\varepsilon),\quad\|\psi^{\varepsilon}-\psi_{0}-\bar{\psi}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1})}=O(\varepsilon).

For the flat boundary case, this has been proved in
[
5
]
. However, for the unit disk we consider here, a similar problem arises as the case of linear transport equation in
[
10
]
. When solving (
2.3
), the term
\cos(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}
appears on the right of the equation of
\tilde{\psi}_{1}
. In order for
\tilde{\psi}_{1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[-\pi,\pi)\times[-\pi,\pi))
, we need

(2.34)

\displaystyle\frac{\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[-\pi,\pi)\times[-\pi,\pi)).

Considering that the support of
\chi_{0}
depends on
\varepsilon
, we need
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}\in L^{\infty}
. Note that
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}
is a solution to the equations

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}^{2}(\partial_{\theta}\tilde{T}_{0})+\langle\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}-4\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}\partial_{\theta}\tilde{T}_{0}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\sin(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\eta}(\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\psi}_{0})+\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}-4\tilde{T}_{0}^{3}\partial_{\theta}\tilde{T}_{0}=-\cos(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}.

Thus in order for
\partial_{\theta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}\in L^{\infty}
we need
\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}\in L^{\infty}
. However, this fails to be hold, according to the following lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For the Milne problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}&\partial_{\eta}^{2}\tilde{T}_{0}+\langle\tilde{\psi}_{0}-\tilde{T}_{0}^{4}\rangle=0,\\
&\sin(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}+\tilde{\psi}_{0}-\tilde{T}_{0}^{4}=0,\\
&\mbox{B.C.:}~\tilde{T}_{0}(0,\theta)=T_{b}(\theta),\quad\tilde{\psi}_{0}(0,\theta,\xi)=\psi_{b}(\theta,\xi),\quad\text{for }\sin(\theta+\xi)>0,\end{array}\right.

if
T_{b}=0
,
\psi_{b}(\theta,\xi)=\cos(2(\theta+\xi))
, then

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}\not\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times[-\pi,\pi)\times[-\pi,\pi)).

Proof.

For
\eta=0
, according to (
2
)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}(0,\theta,\xi)=-\frac{\tilde{\psi}_{0}(0,\theta,\xi)-\tilde{T}_{0}^{4}(0,\theta)}{\sin(\theta+\xi)}.

If we take
\theta+\xi=0
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{\xi\to-\theta^{+}}\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}(0,\theta,\xi)=-\lim_{\xi\to-\theta^{+}}\frac{\tilde{\psi}_{0}(0,\theta,\xi)-\tilde{T}_{0}^{4}(0,\theta)}{\sin(\theta+\xi)}=-\frac{1-0}{0}=-\infty,

hence
\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}\not\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty)\times[-\pi,\pi)\times[-\pi,\pi))
.
∎

2.4.
Geometric corrections

As can be seen from above analysis, the term (
2.34
) does not lie in
L^{\infty}
. In order to overcome this difficulty, we follow the work
[
10
]
and introduce a geometric correction to the boundary layer corrections. First we introduce the transformation of variables

(\theta,\xi)\to(\theta,\phi)
defined by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta=\theta,\\
\phi=\theta+\xi.\end{array}\right.

With the new coordinate,
\partial_{\theta}f(\theta,\xi)
becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}f(\theta,\xi)=\partial_{\theta}f(\theta,\phi)+\partial_{\phi}f(\theta,\phi),

and
\partial_{\theta}^{2}f
becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}^{2}f=\partial_{\theta}^{2}f(\theta,\phi)+\partial_{\phi}^{2}f(\theta,\phi)+2\partial_{\theta\phi}f(\theta,\phi).

Note that any function
h=h(\theta)
remains the same in the new coordinate.

Equations (
2.4
)-(
2.5
) become

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}^{2}T^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\partial_{\eta}T^{i}+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{(1-\varepsilon\eta)^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}T^{i}+\langle\psi^{i}-(T^{o}+T^{i})^{4}+(T^{o})^{4}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\psi^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos\phi(\partial_{\theta}\psi^{i}+\partial_{\phi}\psi^{i})+\psi^{i}-(T^{o}+T^{i})^{4}+(T^{o})^{4}=0.

Taking the ansatz (
2.6
) into the above equations and collect the terms with the same order while treating the terms
\varepsilon^{2}\partial_{\phi}^{2}\bar{T}_{k}
,
\varepsilon\partial_{\phi}\bar{\psi}_{k}
to be
O(1)
gives

(2.38)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\bar{T}_{0}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\partial_{\eta}\bar{T}_{0}+\bar{\psi}_{0}-(\bar{T}_{0}+P_{0})^{4}+P_{0}^{4}\rangle=0,\\
\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\bar{\psi}_{0}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\bar{\psi}_{0}+\bar{\psi}_{0}-(\bar{T}_{0}+P_{0})^{4}+P_{0}^{4}=0,\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\bar{T}_{1}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\partial_{\eta}\bar{T}_{1}+\langle\bar{\psi}_{1}-4(P_{0}+\bar{T}_{0})^{3}(P_{1}+\bar{T}_{1})+4P_{0}^{3}P_{1}\rangle=0,\\
\\
\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\bar{\psi}_{1}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\bar{\psi}_{1}+\bar{\psi}_{1}-4(P_{0}+\bar{T}_{0})^{3}(P_{1}+\bar{T}_{1})+4P_{0}^{3}P_{1}=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\theta}\bar{\psi}_{0},\\
\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\vdots

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{\eta}^{2}\bar{T}_{k}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\partial_{\eta}\bar{T}_{k}+\langle\bar{\psi}_{k}-4(P_{0}+\bar{T}_{0})^{3}(P_{k}+\bar{T}_{k})+4P_{0}^{3}P_{k}\rangle=-\frac{1}{(1-\varepsilon\eta)^{2}}\partial_{\theta}\bar{T}_{k-2}\\
\\
\qquad+\langle\mathcal{E}(P+\bar{T},k-1)-\mathcal{E}(P,k-1)\rangle,\\
\\
\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\bar{\psi}_{k}-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\bar{\psi}_{k}+\bar{\psi}_{k}-4(P_{0}+\bar{T}_{0})^{3}(P_{k}+\bar{T}_{k})+4P_{0}^{3}P_{k}=\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos(\theta+\xi)\partial_{\theta}\bar{\psi}_{k-1}\\
\\
\qquad+\mathcal{E}(P+\bar{T},k-1)-\mathcal{E}(P,k-1),\end{array}\right.

In the above, the terms

\displaystyle-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\partial_{\eta}\bar{T}_{k},\quad-\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\bar{\psi}_{k}

are included in the boundary layer problem, which is used to overcome the singularity of the normal derivatives of boundary layer solutions. The second term was introduced in
[
10
]
. Here since we need to consider the elliptic equation, the first term should also be introduced.

Following a similar procedure, we can reformulate the equations using
\tilde{T}_{k},\tilde{\psi}_{k}
. Define
F(\varepsilon;\eta)
by

(2.39)

\displaystyle F(\varepsilon;\eta)=-\frac{\varepsilon\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{1-\varepsilon\eta}.

The approximate solutions can be constructed as follows.

The zeroth-order boundary layer correction
\bar{T}_{0}^{\varepsilon},\bar{\psi}_{0}^{\varepsilon}
with geometric correction is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(\eta,\theta)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(\eta,\theta)-\tilde{T}_{0,\infty}(\infty,\theta)),~\bar{\psi}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(\eta,\theta,\xi)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{\psi}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(\eta,\theta,\xi)-\tilde{\psi}^{\varepsilon}_{0,\infty}(\infty,\theta,\xi))\\
\\
\partial_{\eta}^{2}\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0}+\langle\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-(\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{4}\rangle=0,\\
\\
\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\varepsilon}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\varepsilon}-(\tilde{T}_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{4}=0,\\
\\
\text{B.C.: }\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0}(0,\theta)=T_{b}(\theta),\quad\tilde{\psi}_{0}^{\varepsilon}(0,\theta,\phi)=\psi_{b}(\theta,\phi),\text{ for }\sin\phi>0.\end{array}\right.

The first order boundary layer correction
\bar{T}_{1}^{\varepsilon},\bar{\psi}_{1}^{\varepsilon}
with geometric correction is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{T}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{T}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta)-\tilde{T}_{1,\infty}(\infty,\theta)),~\bar{\psi}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta,\xi)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta,\xi)-\tilde{\psi}^{\varepsilon}_{1,\infty}(\infty,\theta,\xi))\\
\\
\partial_{\eta}^{2}\tilde{T}_{1}^{\varepsilon}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}\tilde{T}_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{\varepsilon}-4(\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{3}\tilde{T}_{1}^{\varepsilon}=\langle 4((\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{3}-(P_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3})(P_{1}^{\varepsilon}-P_{1}^{\varepsilon}(0))\rangle,\\
\\
\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{\varepsilon}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{\varepsilon}-4(\tilde{T}_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3}\tilde{T}_{1}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos\phi\partial_{\theta}\bar{\psi}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\\
\\
\qquad+4((\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{3}-(P_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3})(P_{1}^{\varepsilon}-P_{1}^{\varepsilon}(0)),\\
\\
\text{B.C.: }\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{1}(0,\theta)=0,\quad\tilde{\psi}_{1}^{\varepsilon}(0,\theta,\phi)=\beta\cdot\nabla\psi^{\varepsilon}_{0}(0),\quad\text{for }\sin\phi>0.\end{array}\right.

The boundary layer corrections with geometric corrections
(\bar{T}^{\varepsilon}_{k},\bar{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon})
,
k\geq 1
are given by

(2.56)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\bar{T}_{k}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{T}_{k}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta)-\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{k,\infty}(\infty,\theta)),~\bar{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta,\xi)=\chi(\varepsilon\eta)(\tilde{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta,\xi)-\tilde{\psi}^{\varepsilon}_{k,\infty}(\infty,\theta,\xi))\\
\\
\partial_{\eta}^{2}\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{k}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{k}+\tilde{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon}-4(\tilde{T}_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3}\tilde{T}_{k}^{\varepsilon}=+\frac{\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{(1-\varepsilon\eta)^{2}}\partial_{\theta}^{2}\tilde{T}_{k-2}^{\varepsilon}+\langle 4((\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{3}-(P_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3})(P^{\varepsilon}_{k}-P^{\varepsilon}_{k}(0))\rangle\\
\\
\qquad+\langle\mathcal{E}(\bar{T}^{\varepsilon}+P^{\varepsilon},k-1)-\mathcal{E}(P^{\varepsilon},k-1)\rangle\\
\\
\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\tilde{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\tilde{\psi}^{\varepsilon}_{k}+\tilde{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon}-4(\tilde{T}_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3}\tilde{T}_{k}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\chi_{0}(\varepsilon\eta)}{1-\varepsilon\eta}\cos\phi\partial_{\theta}\bar{\psi}_{k-1}\\
\\
\qquad+4((\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{3}-(P_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3})(P^{\varepsilon}_{k}-P^{\varepsilon}_{k}(0))+\mathcal{E}(\bar{T}^{\varepsilon}+P^{\varepsilon},k-1)-\mathcal{E}(P^{\varepsilon},k-1),\\
\\
\text{B.C.: }\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{k}(0,\theta)=0,\quad\tilde{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon}(0,\theta,\phi)=\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-1}(0)-\mathcal{E}(T(0),k-1),\quad\text{for }\sin\phi>0.\end{array}\right.

We will show later that

\lim_{\eta\to\infty}\tilde{T}_{k}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta)=\tilde{T}_{k,\infty}^{\varepsilon},\quad\text{and}\,\,\,\lim_{\eta\to\infty}\tilde{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\theta,\phi)=\tilde{\psi}^{\varepsilon}_{k,\infty}\quad\text{exist.}

The leading order interior expansion
({T}_{0}^{\varepsilon},{\psi}_{0}^{\varepsilon})
is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta T^{\varepsilon}_{0}+\pi\Delta(T^{\varepsilon}_{0})^{4}=0,\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
\psi_{0}^{\varepsilon}=(T_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{4}\quad\text{in }\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1}\\
\text{B.C.: }T_{0}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{0,\infty},\quad\text{for }x\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

The first order interior expansion
(T_{1}^{\varepsilon},\psi_{1}^{\varepsilon})
is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta T_{1}^{\varepsilon}+\pi\Delta(4(T_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3}T_{1}^{\varepsilon})=0,\quad\text{in }\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1},\\
\psi_{1}^{\varepsilon}=4(T_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3}T_{1}^{\varepsilon}-\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{0}^{\varepsilon},\quad\text{in }\Omega\times\mathbb{S}^{1},\\
\text{B.C.: }T_{1}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{1,\infty},\quad\text{for }x\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

The higher order interior expansion
(T_{k}^{\varepsilon},\psi_{k}^{\varepsilon})
,
k\geq 1
is given by

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta T_{k}^{\varepsilon}+\pi\Delta(4(T_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{4}T_{k}^{\varepsilon})=\langle(\beta\cdot\nabla)^{3}\psi_{k-1}\rangle-\pi\Delta\mathcal{E}(T^{\varepsilon},k-1),\\
\psi_{k}^{\varepsilon}=4(T_{0}^{\varepsilon})^{3}T_{k}^{\varepsilon}+\mathcal{E}(T^{\varepsilon},k-1)-\beta\cdot\nabla\psi_{k-1}^{\varepsilon},\\
\text{B.C.: }T_{k}^{\varepsilon}(x)=\tilde{T}^{\varepsilon}_{k,\infty},\quad\text{for }x\in\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Here we call the equations of
(\tilde{T}_{k}^{\varepsilon},\tilde{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon})
the
Milne problem with geometric corrections
and
(\bar{T}^{\varepsilon},\bar{\psi}^{\varepsilon})
the
boundary layer solution with geometric corrections
, and
(T_{k}^{\varepsilon},\psi_{k}^{\varepsilon})
the interior solutions.

2.5.
Approximate solution with geometric corrections

We thus obtain the approximate solution with geometric corrections by adding the interior solution and the boundary layer solution with geometric corrections as

(2.66)

\displaystyle T^{a,\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{k}(T_{k}^{\varepsilon}+\bar{T}_{k}^{\varepsilon}),\quad\psi^{a,\varepsilon}=\sum_{k=0}^{N}\varepsilon^{k}(\psi_{k}^{\varepsilon}+\bar{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon}),

where
T_{k},\psi_{k}
are given by (
2.4
)-(
2.4
) and
\bar{T}_{k}^{\varepsilon},\bar{\psi}_{k}^{\varepsilon}
are given by (
2.4
)-(
2.56
).

One can show that
(T^{a,\varepsilon},\psi^{a,\varepsilon})
satisfies system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) with boundary conditions (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) in the perturbative sense.

Lemma 3
.

Let
(T^{a,\varepsilon},\psi^{a,\varepsilon})
be the pair of functions constructed above. Then formally, it satisfy

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta T^{a,\varepsilon}+\langle\psi^{a,\varepsilon}-(T^{a,\varepsilon})^{4}\rangle=\mathcal{R}_{1}(T^{a,\varepsilon},\psi^{a,\varepsilon}),

\displaystyle\varepsilon\beta\cdot\nabla\psi^{a,\varepsilon}+\psi^{a,\varepsilon}-(T^{a,\varepsilon})^{4}=\mathcal{R}_{2}(T^{a,\varepsilon},\psi^{a,\varepsilon}),

with boundary conditions

\displaystyle T^{a,\varepsilon}(x)=T_{b}(x),\quad\text{for any }x\in\partial\Omega,

\displaystyle\psi^{a,\varepsilon}(x,\beta)=\psi_{b}(x,\beta),\quad\text{for any }(x,\beta)\in\Gamma_{-},

where
\mathcal{R}_{1}(T^{a,\varepsilon},\psi^{a,\varepsilon})=O(\varepsilon^{N+1})
,
\mathcal{R}_{1}(T^{a,\varepsilon},\psi^{a,\varepsilon})=O(\varepsilon^{N+1})
.

The lemma will be proved later in section 5 after we show the property of the Milne problems with geometric corrections, which is needed to obtain the error estimates.

3.
Nonlinear Milne problem with geometric correction

One clearly notes that the leading order of the Milne problem with geometric corrections (
2.4
) is nonlinear and the higher orders problem (
2.4
), (
2.56
) are linear, we will consider these two problems separately.

In this section, we first study the nonlinear Milne problem (
2.4
). We rewrite the system as follows

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{\varepsilon}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}G^{\varepsilon}+\langle\varphi^{\varepsilon}-(G^{\varepsilon})^{4}\rangle=0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\varphi^{\varepsilon}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{\varepsilon}+\varphi^{\varepsilon}-(G^{\varepsilon})^{4}=0,

with boundary conditions

(3.3)

\displaystyle G^{\varepsilon}(0)=G_{b},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\varepsilon}(0,\phi)=\varphi_{b}(\phi),\quad\text{for }\sin\phi>0.

Note that here we drop the dependence of variables on
\theta
since it is irrelevant here. This section is devoted to the proof of the existence for the above nonlinear system (
3.1
)-(
3.4
).

Theorem 4
.

Given
0\leq G_{b}\leq\gamma
,
0\leq\varphi_{b}(\phi)\leq\gamma^{4}
for
\sin\phi>0
. Then there exists a weak solution
(\varphi^{\varepsilon},G^{\varepsilon})\in L^{2}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty))\times L^{2}_{\rm loc}([0,\infty)\times[-\pi,\pi))
to system (
3.1
)-(
3.2
) with boundary conditions (
3.3
)-(
3.4
). Moreover, the solutions satisfy the following estimates

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}4(G^{\varepsilon})^{3}|\partial_{\eta}{G^{\varepsilon}}|^{2}d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi^{\varepsilon}-(G^{\varepsilon})^{4})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi^{\varepsilon}(0,\phi)-G_{b}^{4})^{2}d\phi

(3.5)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}\sin\phi(\varphi_{b}(\phi)-G_{b}^{4})^{2}d\phi,

and for any
0<\alpha<1/4
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{2\alpha\eta}4(G^{\varepsilon})^{3}|\partial_{\eta}G^{\varepsilon}|^{2}d\eta+\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\alpha\eta}(\varphi^{\varepsilon}-(G^{\varepsilon})^{4})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}(0,\phi)-G^{4}_{b})^{2}d\phi

(3.6)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}\sin\phi(\varphi_{b}-G_{b}^{4})^{2}d\phi.

Furthermore, the limit
G^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}=\lim_{\eta\to\infty}G^{\varepsilon}(\eta)
exists and

(3.7)

\displaystyle|G^{\varepsilon}(\eta)-G^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}|\leq Ce^{-\alpha\eta},

where
C>0
is a constant only depending on
G_{b}
and
\varphi_{b}
.

The proof of the above theorem follows the similar procedure as the case for flat boundary without geometric corrections in
[
6
]
. We first establish the existence in a bounded domain and then use uniform weighted estimates to carry out the solutions to half-space. However, due to the additional terms by geometric corrections, we need to use properties of the function
F(\varepsilon;\eta)
defined by (
2.39
) to obtain the existence proofs.

The following property of
F(\varepsilon;\eta)
was proved in
[
10
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 5

(
[
10
]
)
.

There exists a potential function
V(\varepsilon;\eta)
satisfying
V(\varepsilon;0)=0
and
F(\varepsilon;\eta)=-\partial_{\eta}V(\varepsilon;\eta)
. Moreover,
V(\varepsilon;\eta)
is a monotonically increasing function of
\eta
satisfying

\displaystyle 0\leq V(\varepsilon;\eta)\leq-\log(1-\tfrac{3}{4}\delta),

and that

exists and is independent of
\varepsilon
. For any
\sigma>0
,
\eta>0

\displaystyle e^{V(\eta+\sigma)}-e^{V(\eta)}\leq 1+\frac{4}{4-3\delta}\varepsilon\sigma.

For

, the following inequalities hold:

(3.8)

\displaystyle-\log(1-\tfrac{3}{4}\delta)\leq\int_{y}^{z}F(\varepsilon;\eta)d\eta\leq 0,\quad\text{for any }0\leq y\leq z\leq\infty,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\eta}^{\infty}|F(y)^{2}|dyd\eta\leq\frac{3\delta}{4-3\delta}+\log(1-\tfrac{3}{4}\delta),

(3.10)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(\varepsilon;\eta)|^{2}d\eta\leq\frac{3\delta}{4-3\delta}\varepsilon,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\|F(\varepsilon;\eta)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq\frac{4}{4-3\delta}\varepsilon.

3.1.
Existence on the bounded interval

We first consider the nonlinear Milne problem on the bounded domain with
\eta\in[0,B]
,
\phi\in[-\pi,\pi)
.

(3.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}+\langle\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}-(G^{\varepsilon}_{B})^{4}\rangle=0,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}+\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}-(G^{\varepsilon}_{B})^{4}=0,

subject to the boundary conditons

(3.14)

\displaystyle G_{B}^{\varepsilon}(0)=G_{b},\quad\partial_{\eta}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}(B)=0,

(3.15)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}(0,\phi)=\varphi_{b}(\phi),\quad\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}(B,\phi)=\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}(B,-\phi),\quad\text{for }\sin\phi>0.

The existence of solutions to the above problem is guaranteed by the following lemma.

Lemma 6
.

Assume
0\leq G_{b}\leq\gamma
and
0\leq\varphi_{b}\leq\gamma^{4}
for some constant
\gamma>0
. Then there exists a unique solution
(G_{B}^{\varepsilon},\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})\in C^{2}([0,B])\times C^{1}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))
to system (
3.12
)-(
3.13
) with boundary conditions (
3.14
)-(
3.15
), and the solution satisfies

\displaystyle 0\leq G_{B}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)\leq\gamma,\quad 0\leq\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}(\eta,\phi)\leq\gamma^{4},\quad\text{for }\eta\in[0,B],\,\phi\in[-\pi,\pi)

In order to show the Lemma
6
, we follow our previous work on the Milne problem
[
6
]
. The proof for the flat case in
[
6
]
relys on the monotonicity of solutions for the elliptic equation and transport equation. Here we show that the geometric correction does not alter the monotonicity of the solutions. We start by proving the monotonicity of the elliptic equation (
3.12
).

Lemma 7
.

Suppose
0\leq G_{b}\leq\gamma
for some constant
\gamma>0
. Suppose
\zeta=\zeta(x)
is an increasing monotonic function defined on
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
g\in C([0,B])
is a continuous bounded function satisfying
0\leq g\leq\zeta(\gamma)
. Then there exists a unique bounded solution
G^{\varepsilon}_{B}\in C^{2}([0,B])
to the equation

(3.16)

\displaystyle-\partial_{\eta}^{2}G+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}G+\zeta(G)=g(\eta),\quad\text{on }\eta\in[0,B],

with boundary conditions

(3.17)

\displaystyle G(0)=G_{b},\quad\partial_{\eta}G(B)=0,

and the solution satisfies
0\leq G(\eta)\leq\gamma
for any
\eta\in[0,B]
.

Moreover, let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
be two solutions of the above equation corresponding to source terms
g_{1}
,
g_{2}
and boundary data
G_{b1}
,
G_{b2}
, respectively. Suppose
0\leq g_{1}\leq g_{2}\leq\gamma
on
[0,B]
and
0\leq G_{b1}\leq G_{b2}\leq\gamma
, then
0\leq G_{1}\leq G_{2}\leq\gamma
on
[0,B]
.

Proof.

The proof of the lemma is based on the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem and the maximum principle.

Existence.
Let
h\in C([0,B])
be the solution to the problem

\displaystyle-\partial_{\eta}^{2}f-F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}f=h,\text{ in [0,B]},\quad f(0)=0,\quad\partial_{\eta}f(B)=0.

Then the solution can be explicitly solved by

(3.18)

\displaystyle f(\eta)=\int_{0}^{\eta}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}F(\varepsilon;p)dp}\left(\int_{0}^{s}-e^{\int_{0}^{q}F(\varepsilon;p)dp}h(q)dq+\int_{0}^{B}e^{\int_{0}^{q}F(\varepsilon;p)dp}h(q)dq\right)ds.

Due to (
3.8
),
|\int_{0}^{s}F(\varepsilon;p)dp|\leq-\log(1-\tfrac{3}{4}\delta)
is bounded, so
e^{\pm\int_{0}^{s}F(\varepsilon;p)dp}
is also bounded and the above formula is well-defined. Denote the above relation by
f=\mathcal{G}h
with
\mathcal{G}
being a linear operator mapping from
\mathcal{C}([0,B])
to
C^{2}([0,B])
. We next show the nonlinear operator
\mathcal{N}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}G:=\mathcal{G}(g-\zeta(G))+G_{b},

has a fixed point. First, the operator
\mathcal{N}:C([0,B])\times\mathbb{C}^{2}([0,B])
is a compact mapping from the Banach space
(C[0,B])
to
C([0,B])
, due to the compact embedding
C^{2}([0,B])\subset C([0,B])
. Next we show the set

(3.19)

\displaystyle\{G\in C([0,B]):G=\sigma\mathcal{N}G,\quad\text{for some }\sigma\in[0,1]\}

is bounded. To show this, suppose
G
is in this set, then

\displaystyle-\partial_{\eta}^{2}G-F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}G+\sigma\zeta(G)=\sigma g,\quad\text{ in }[0,B]

\displaystyle G(0)=G_{b},\quad\partial_{\eta}G(B)=0.

First we show
G\geq 0
. Otherwise the minimum value of
G(\eta)
on
[0,B]
is small than
0
. Let
\eta_{\min}
be the minimum point, then
G(\eta_{\min})<0
. If
\eta_{\min}=0
then
G(\eta_{\min})=G_{b}\geq 0
. If
\eta_{\min}\in(0,B]
, then
\partial_{\eta}G(\eta_{\min})=0
and

(3.20)

\displaystyle-\partial_{\eta}^{2}G(\eta_{\min})=\sigma g(\eta_{\min})-\sigma\zeta(G(\eta_{\min}))>0,

due to
\zeta
being an increasing function and
G(\eta_{\min})<0
. Hence
G({\eta})
is locally concave around
\eta_{\min}
, which contradicts to the assumption that
\eta_{\min}
is the minimum point. Hence
G(\eta)\geq 0
for any
\eta\in[0,B]
.

We can also show
G\leq\gamma
. Otherwise the maxium value of
G(\eta)
is larger than
\gamma
. Let
\eta_{\max}
be the maximum point, then
G(\eta_{\max})>\gamma
. Since
G_{b}\leq\gamma
,
\eta_{\max}\neq 0
. For
\eta_{\max}\in(0,B]
, we have
\partial_{\eta}G(\eta_{\max})=0
and

\displaystyle-\partial_{\eta}^{2}G(\eta_{\max})=\sigma g(\eta_{\max})-\sigma\zeta(G(\eta_{\max}))\leq\sigma\zeta(\gamma)-\sigma\zeta(G(\eta_{\max}))<0,

due to
\zeta
being an increasing function and
\zeta(G(\eta_{\max}))>\zeta(\gamma)
. This implies that
G(\eta)
is locally strictly convex near
\eta_{\max}
, which contradicts the assumption that
\eta_{\max}
is a maximum point. Hence
G\leq\gamma
holds.

From
0\leq G\leq\gamma
on
[0,B]
, we have
\|G\|_{C([0,B])}\leq\gamma
and the set (
3.19
) is bounded. Consequently, we can apply the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem and conclude that
\mathcal{N}
has a fixed point in
C([0,B])
. Let
G=G(\eta)\in C([0,B])
be a fixed point, then
\mathcal{N}G=G
and by the definition of
\mathcal{N}
,
G\in C^{2}([0,B])
and solves equation (
3.16
) with boundary condition (
3.17
). By the same argument as above we also have
0\leq G\leq\gamma
in
[0,B]
.

Uniqueness.
Note that the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem only guarantees the existence for equation (
3.16
). To show the uniqueness of solutions, we use the maximum principle. Suppose
G_{1}\neq G_{2}
be two solutions to (
3.16
). Let
\ell:=G_{1}-G_{2}
, suppose
\ell(\eta)
attains its maximum at
\eta_{\max}
and its minimum at
\eta_{\min}
. First we show
\ell(\eta_{\max})\leq 0
. Otherwise suppose
\ell(\eta_{\max})>0
, then
\eta_{\max}\neq 0
since
G_{1}(0)=G_{2}(0)=G_{b}.
For
\eta_{\max}\in(0,B]
, we have
\partial_{\eta}\ell(\eta_{\max})=0
, hence

\displaystyle-\partial_{\eta}^{2}\ell(\eta_{\max})=-(\zeta(G_{1}(\eta_{\max}))-\zeta(G_{2}(\eta_{\max})))<0,

due to
G_{1}(\eta_{\max})>G_{2}(\eta_{\max})
and
\zeta
being an increasing function. This implies
\ell
is locally convex near
\eta_{\max}
, which contradicts the assumption that
\eta_{\max}
is a maximum point. Therefore,
\ell(\eta)\leq\ell(\eta_{\max})\leq 0
for any
\eta\in[0,B]
. By a similar argument, one can show
\ell(\eta)\geq\ell(\eta_{\min})\geq 0
and we conclude that
\ell(\eta)=0
for
\eta\in[0,B]
. Therefore,
G_{1}(\eta)=G_{2}(\eta)
on
\eta\in[0,B]
and the solution to (
3.16
) is unique.

Monotonicity.
Let
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
be two solutions as described in Lemma
7
. Let
\ell:=G_{1}-G_{2}
. We first show the maximum value of
\ell
cannot be bigger than zero. Otherwise, suppose
\ell(\eta)
attains its maximum at
\eta_{\max}
with
\ell(\eta_{\max})>0.
Then due to
G_{1}(0)-G_{2}(0)=G_{b1}-G_{b2}\leq 0
,
\eta_{\max}\neq 0
. For
\eta_{\max}\in(0,B]
, we have
\partial_{\eta}\ell(\eta_{\max})=0
and

\displaystyle-\partial_{\eta}^{2}\ell(\eta_{\max})=g_{1}(\eta_{\max})-g_{2}(\eta_{\max})-(\zeta(G_{1}(\eta_{\max}))-\zeta(G_{2}(\eta_{\max})))<0,

due to the assumption
g_{1}\leq g_{2}
and the monotonicity of
\zeta
. Hence
\ell(\eta)
is strictly convex near
\eta_{\max}
, which contradicts the assumption that
\eta_{\max}
is a maximum point. Therefore,
\ell(\eta)\leq\ell(\eta_{\max})\leq 0
for any
\eta\in[0,B]
. That is,
G_{1}(\eta)\leq G_{2}(\eta)
for any
\eta\in[0,B]
. Thus we finish the proof of the lemma.
∎

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}(\varphi^{\varepsilon}-4h^{3}G^{\varepsilon})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi^{\varepsilon})^{2}(0,\cdot)d\phi

(4.6)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}\sin\phi\varphi_{b}^{2}d\phi+\|e^{\beta^{\prime}\eta}S_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,\infty)\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2},

and there exists constants

, such that

\displaystyle|G^{\varepsilon}(\eta)-G^{\varepsilon}_{\infty}|\leq Ce^{-\beta^{\prime}\eta},\quad|G^{\varepsilon}(\eta,\phi)-4h_{\infty}^{3}G_{\infty}^{\varepsilon}|\leq Ce^{-\beta^{\prime}\eta},

for any

, where

is a constant depending on

.

4.2.
Existence on the bounded interval

We first consider the existence for system (
4.1
)-(
4.2
) on the bounded interval
\eta\in[0,B]
:

(4.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}^{2}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}+\langle\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}-4h^{3}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}\rangle=S_{1},

(4.9)

\displaystyle\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}+\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}-4h^{3}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}=S_{2},

with boundary conditions

(4.10)

\displaystyle G^{\varepsilon}_{B}(0)=0,\quad\partial_{\eta}G^{\varepsilon}(B)=0,

(4.11)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}(0,\phi)=\varphi_{b}(\phi),\quad\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}(B,\phi)=\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}(B,-\phi),\quad\text{for }\sin\phi>0.

We will consider the case when
\langle S_{2}\rangle=S_{1}
and prove the following lemma.

Lemma 15
.

Let
h
satisfy the spectral assumption
(A)
. Assume
\varphi_{b}
,
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
satisfy (
4.5
) and
S_{1}(\eta)=\langle S_{2}(\eta,\cdot)\rangle
for any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\varphi_{b}\in C^{\infty}(0,\pi)
. Then there exists a solution
(G_{B}^{\varepsilon},\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})\in L^{2}([0,B])\times L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))
to system (
4.8
)-(
4.9
) with boundary conditions (
4.10
)-(
4.11
), and the solution verifies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon}-4h^{3}G_{B}^{\varepsilon})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})^{2}(0,\phi)d\phi

(4.12)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{b})^{2}d\phi+\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2},

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.

Proof.

The proof is based on Theorem
22
for existence of the linear Milne problem without geometric correction and the Leray-Schauder fixed point theorem.

First we construct a sequence of functions
\{(G^{k},\varphi^{k})\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
by
G^{0}=0,\varphi^{0}=0
and

(4.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{k}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}G^{k-1}+\langle\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k}\rangle=S_{1},

(4.14)

\displaystyle\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\varphi^{k}+F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}+\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k}=S_{2},

with boundary conditions

(4.15)

\displaystyle G^{k}(0)=0,\quad\partial_{\eta}G^{k}(B)=0,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\varphi^{k}(0,\phi)=\varphi_{b}(\phi),\quad\varphi^{k}(B,\phi)=\varphi^{k}(B,-\phi),\quad\text{for }\sin\phi>0.

Given bounded functions
(G^{k-1},\varphi^{k-1})
, according to Theorem
22
in Appendix
B
, there exists unique solution
(G^{k},\varphi^{k})
for the above system.

Next we show the boundness of
(G^{k},\varphi^{k})
in
L^{2}([0,B])\times L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi])
. First, the relation
\partial_{\eta}G^{k}=\langle\sin\phi\varphi^{k}\rangle
on
[0,B]
is preserved during the iterations. Suppose
\partial_{\eta}G^{k-1}=\langle\sin\phi\varphi^{k-1}\rangle
on
[0,B]
, then comparing (
4.13
) to
\langle
(
4.14
)
\rangle
and using
S_{1}=\langle S_{2}\rangle
yields

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}(\partial_{\eta}G^{k}-\langle\sin\phi\varphi^{k}\rangle)

\displaystyle=-F(\varepsilon;\eta)(\partial_{\eta}G^{k-1}-\langle\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\rangle)

\displaystyle=-F(\varepsilon;\eta)(\partial_{\eta}G^{k-1}-\langle\sin\phi\varphi^{k-1}\rangle)=0.

This, together with the boundary condition
\partial_{\eta}G^{k}(B)=\langle\varphi^{k}(B,\cdot)\rangle=0
, implies that
\partial_{\eta}G^{k}(\eta)=\langle\sin\phi\varphi^{k}(\eta,\cdot)\rangle
for any
\eta\in[0,B]
.

We multiply (
4.13
) by
4h^{3}G^{k}
and (
4.14
) by
\varphi^{k}
and integrate to get

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{B}4h^{3}G^{k}\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{k}d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}(\varphi^{k})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k})^{2}d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\cdot\varphi^{k}d\phi d\eta-\int_{0}^{B}F(\varepsilon;\eta)4h^{3}G^{k}\partial_{\eta}G^{k-1}d\eta

(4.17)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}S_{2}(\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k})d\phi d\eta.

The inequality (
4.7
) from the spectral assumption implies

(4.18)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{B}4h^{3}G^{k}\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{k}=\int_{0}^{B}4h^{3}|\partial_{\eta}G^{k}|^{2}d\eta+\int_{0}^{B}\partial_{\eta}(4h^{3})G^{k}\partial_{\eta}G^{k}d\eta\geq 0.

Boundary conditions (
4.16
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}(\varphi^{k})^{2}d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin\phi(\varphi^{k})^{2}d\phi\bigg|_{0}^{B}

(4.19)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{b})^{2}d\phi+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi^{k})^{2}(0,\phi)d\phi.

The relation
\partial_{\eta}G^{k-1}=\langle\sin\phi\varphi^{k-1}\rangle
implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\cdot\varphi^{k}d\phi d\eta-\int_{0}^{B}4h^{3}G^{k}\partial_{\eta}G^{k-1}d\eta

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\cdot\varphi^{k}d\phi d\eta-\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin\phi\varphi^{k-1}4h^{3}G^{k}d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\cdot\varphi^{k}d\phi d\eta-\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\cdot 4h^{3}G^{k}d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\cdot(\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k})d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|F(\varepsilon;\eta)|^{2}|\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}|^{2}d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\|^{2}\|\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2}

(4.20)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{2}{4-3\delta}\varepsilon\|\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2},

where the last inequality is due to (
3.11
).

Using the Young’s inequality, the last term in (
4.2
) can be estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}S_{2}(\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k})d\phi d\eta\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2}.

Taking the above inequality with (
4.18
)-(
4.2
) into (
4.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi^{k})^{2}(0,\phi)d\phi

(4.21)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{b})^{2}d\phi+\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2}+\frac{2}{4-3\delta}\varepsilon\|\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2}.

Hence, assume
\partial_{\phi}\varphi^{k-1}\in L^{2}([0,B]))
, one can get
\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k}\in L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))
. By equation (
4.13
),
\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{k}\in L^{2}([0,B])
and so

(4.22)

\displaystyle G^{k}(\eta)=-\int_{0}^{\eta}\partial_{s}G^{k}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{\eta}\int_{s}^{B}\partial_{\ell}^{2}G^{k}(\ell)d\ell ds\leq\int_{0}^{B}\int_{0}^{B}|\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{k}|d\eta ds\leq B\sqrt{B}\|\partial^{2}_{\eta}G\|_{L^{2}([0,B])},

and thus is bounded for any
\eta\in[0,B]
. Moreover, by the boundness of
h
,
\varphi^{k}=\varphi^{k}-4h^{3}G^{k}+4h^{3}G^{k}\in L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))
. Note here since
\varphi^{k-1}(\eta,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi))
,
\varphi^{k}(\eta,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}([-\pi,\pi))
, since the regularity on
\phi
is preserved by system (
4.13
)-(
4.14
).

Therefore, we have shown that there exists a solution to the problem (
4.13
)-(
4.16
) satisfying
(G^{k},\varphi^{k})\in L^{2}([0,B])\times L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))
. Define the operator
\mathcal{L}:L^{2}([0,B])\times L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))\mapsto L^{2}([0,B])\times L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))
given by (
4.13
)-(
4.16
) with
(G^{k},\varphi^{k})=\mathcal{L}((G^{k-1},\varphi^{k-1}))
. We can also get from the above derivation that
G^{k}\in H^{2}([0,B])
and
\varphi^{k}\in H^{1}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))
. Hence the operator
\mathcal{L}
is continous and compact. In order to show
\mathcal{L}
has a fixed point, we need to show the set

(4.23)

\displaystyle\{(G,\varphi)\in L^{2}([0,B])\times L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi)):(G,\varphi)=\sigma\mathcal{L}((G,\varphi))\text{ for some }0\leq\sigma\leq 1\}

is bounded. Then by the Schauder fixed-point theorem, there exists a fixed point of
\mathcal{L}
. To show the above set is bounded, let
(G^{,}\varphi)=\sigma\mathcal{L}((G,\varphi))
, then
(G,\varphi)
solves

(4.24)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}^{2}G+\sigma F(\varepsilon;\eta)\partial_{\eta}G+\langle\varphi-4h^{3}G\rangle=S_{1},

(4.25)

\displaystyle\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}\varphi+\sigma F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi+\varphi-4h^{3}G=S_{2},

with boundary conditions

\displaystyle G(0)=0,\quad\partial_{\eta}G(B)=0,

\displaystyle\varphi(0,\phi)=\varphi_{b}(\phi),\quad\varphi(B,\phi)=\varphi(B,-\phi),\quad\text{for }\sin\phi>0.

Note that
\partial_{\eta}\varphi=\langle\sin\phi\varphi\rangle
also hold on
[0,B]
. Comparing equation (
4.24
) to (
4.25
) gives

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}(\partial_{\eta}G-\langle\sin\phi\varphi)\rangle)=-\sigma F(\varepsilon;\eta)(\partial_{\eta}G-\langle\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi\rangle)=-\sigma F(\varepsilon;\eta)(\partial_{\eta}G-\langle\sin\phi\varphi)\rangle),

which together with the boundary condition
\partial_{\eta}G(B)-\langle\sin\phi\varphi(B,\cdot)\rangle=0
, implies

(4.26)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}G=\langle\sin\phi\varphi\rangle.

We can estimate the above system similarly as the estimates of system (
4.13
)-(
4.14
) and the result is (see the estimate of (
4.2
))

\displaystyle\frac{3}{4}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi-4h^{3}G)^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|\varphi^{2}(0,\phi)d\phi

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{b})^{2}d\phi+\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2}+\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi(\varphi-4h^{3}G)d\phi d\eta.

Integration by parts in
\phi
on the last term gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}\varphi(\varphi-4h^{3}G)d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\sin\phi\varphi(\varphi-4h^{3}G)d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\sin\phi(\varphi-4h^{3}G)^{2}d\eta

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi-4h^{3}G)^{2}d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{2}{4-3\delta}\varepsilon\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi-4h^{3}G)^{2}d\phi d\eta.

Taking it into the previous inequality leads to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{2}{4-3\delta}\varepsilon\right)\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi-4h^{3}G)^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|\varphi^{2}(0,\phi)d\phi

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{b})^{2}d\phi+\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2}.

For
\varepsilon
small, for example
\varepsilon\leq(4-3\delta)/8
,
\left(\frac{3}{4}-\frac{2}{4-3\delta}\varepsilon\right)\geq\frac{1}{4}
, and the above estimate becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(\varphi-4h^{3}G)^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|\varphi^{2}(0,\phi)d\phi

(4.27)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{b})^{2}d\phi+\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}^{2}.

Moreover, by equation (
4.24
),

\displaystyle\|\partial_{\eta}^{2}G\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\|F\partial_{\eta}G\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}+\|\langle(\varphi-4h^{3}G)\rangle\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}+\|S_{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}

\displaystyle\leq\sigma\|F\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\|\langle\sin\phi(\varphi-4h^{3}G)\rangle\|_{L^{2}[0,B]}+\|\langle(\varphi-4h^{3}G)\rangle\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}+\|S_{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{4\sigma}{4-3\delta}\varepsilon+1\right)\sqrt{2\pi}\|(\varphi-4h^{3}G)\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}+\|S_{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{\sin\phi>0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{b})^{2}d\phi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\|S_{2}\|_{L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))}+\|S_{1}\|_{L^{2}([0,B])}.

Hence
\|G\|_{H^{2}([0,B])}
is bounded. Moreover
\varphi=(\varphi-4h^{3}G)+4h^{3}G\in L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))
is bounded. Therefore, the set (
4.24
) is bounded. By the Schauder fixed point theorem, there exists a fixed point
(G^{\varepsilon}_{B},\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B})\in L^{2}([0,B])\times L^{2}([0,B]\times[-\pi,\pi))
such that
(G^{\varepsilon}_{B},\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B})=\mathcal{L}((G^{\varepsilon}_{B},\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}))
. By the definition of
\mathcal{L}
,
(G^{\varepsilon}_{B},\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B})
solves system (
4.8
)-(
4.9
) with boundary conditions (
4.10
)-(
4.11
).

Taking
\sigma=1
in the derivation of the estimate (
4.2
) implies that
(G^{\varepsilon}_{B},\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})
satisfy the estimate (
15
) and finishes the proof.
∎

Next we derive the uniform weighted estimate on the solutions of system (

)-(

Lemma 16
.

Assuming the hypothesis of Lemma

hold and
(G_{B}^{\varepsilon},\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}(\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon}-4h^{3}G_{B}^{\varepsilon})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})^{2}(0,\cdot)d\phi

and

\displaystyle|G_{B}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)|\leq C

(4.30)

\displaystyle\partial_{\eta}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}(\eta)=\langle\sin\phi\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}(\eta,\cdot)\rangle,

Proof.

First, we have
\partial_{\eta}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}=\langle\sin\phi\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon}\rangle
which is due to the same reason as (
4.26
). We multiply (
4.8
) by

and (
4.9
) by
\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon}
and integrate to obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}4h^{3}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}d\eta+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B})^{2}d\phi d\eta+\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}-4h^{3}G^{\varepsilon}_{B})^{2}d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}F(\varepsilon;\eta)\cos\phi\partial_{\phi}(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B})^{2}d\phi d\eta-\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}F(\varepsilon;\eta)4h^{3}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}\partial_{\eta}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}d\eta

(4.31)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}S_{2}(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B}-4h^{3}G^{\varepsilon}_{B})d\phi d\eta.

Using integration by parts and the inequality (
4.7
) from the spectral assumption
(A)
, we can get

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}4h^{3}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}\partial_{\eta}^{2}G^{\varepsilon}_{B}d\eta

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}4h^{3}|\partial_{\eta}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}|^{2}d\eta+\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}\partial_{\eta}(4h^{3})G_{B}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{\eta}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}d\eta+2\beta^{\prime}\int_{0}^{B}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}4h^{3}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{\eta}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}d\eta

\displaystyle\quad\geq 2\beta^{\prime}\int_{0}^{B}e^{\beta^{\prime}\eta}4h^{3}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}\partial_{\eta}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}d\eta.

Using integration by parts and the boundary conditions (
4.11
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}\sin\phi\partial_{\eta}(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{B})^{2}d\phi d\eta

\displaystyle\quad=-\beta^{\prime}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}\sin\phi(\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})^{2}d\beta d\eta+\frac{1}{2}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin\phi(\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})^{2}(\eta,\cdot)d\phi\bigg|_{0}^{B}

\displaystyle\quad=-\beta^{\prime}\int_{0}^{B}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}e^{2\beta^{\prime}\eta}\sin\phi(\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})^{2}d\beta d\eta-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi>0}\sin\phi\varphi_{b}^{2}d\phi+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\sin\phi<0}|\sin\phi|(\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon})^{2}(0,\cdot)d\phi.

The relation
\partial_{\eta}G_{B}^{\varepsilon}=\langle\sin\phi\varphi_{B}^{\varepsilon}\rangle

4.6.
Uniqueness

In order to show the uniqueness, let

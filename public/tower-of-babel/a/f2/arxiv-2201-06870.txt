RoCK blocks for double covers of symmetric groups and quiver Hecke superalgebras Thanks: The first author was supported by the NSF grant DMS-2101791 and Charles Simonyi Endowment at the Institute for Advanced Study. The second author was supported by the EPSRC (grant no EP/T004606/1).
Alexander Kleshchev, Michael Livesey

Abstract
. We define and study RoCK blocks for double covers of symmetric groups. We prove that RoCK blocks of double covers are Morita equivalent to standard ‘local’ blocks. The analogous result for blocks of symmetric groups, a theorem of Chuang and Kessar, was an important step in Chuang and Rouquier ultimately proving Broué’s abelian defect group conjecture for symmetric groups. Indeed we prove Broué’s conjecture for the RoCK blocks defined in this article. Our methods involve translation into the quiver Hecke superalgebras setting using the Kang-Kashiwara-Tsuchioka isomorphism and categorification methods of Kang-Kashiwara-Oh. As a consequence we construct Morita equivalences between more general objects than blocks of finite groups. In particular, our results extend to certain blocks of level one cyclotomic Hecke-Clifford superalgebras. We also study imaginary cuspidal categories of quiver Hecke superalgebras and classify irreducible representations of quiver Hecke superalgebras in terms of cuspidal systems.

Chapter 1
Introduction

1.1.
Broué’s conjecture for symmetric groups and beyond

A fundamental conjecture in representation theory of finite groups is:

Broué’s abelian defect group conjecture.

For any finite group
G
, a block
B
of
G
with abelian defect group
D
is derived equivalent to its Brauer correspondent
b
.

1.1a.
Broué’s conjecture for symmetric groups

For symmetric groups this conjecture is a theorem. The proof has two major steps. First, Chuang and Kessar
[
CK
]
proved Broué’s conjecture for the remarkable special blocks of symmetric groups—the so-called RoCK blocks (so named after Rouquier, Chuang and Kessar); then Chuang and Rouquier
[
CR
]
extended the result to all blocks by constructing derived equivalences between the blocks of symmetric groups with isomorphic defect groups.

To be more precise, let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of characteristic
p>0
and
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
denote the symmetric group on
n
letters. The blocks of the symmetric group algebra
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
are labeled by pairs
(\rho,d)
, where
\rho
is a
p
-core partition and
d
is a non-negative integer such that
|\rho|+dp=n
(for a partition
\lambda
of
r
, we always write
|\lambda|
for
r
). We denote by
B^{\rho,d}
the block of
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
corresponding to such a pair
(\rho,d)
. The integer
d
is referred to as the
weight
of the block
B^{\rho,d}
.

Let
\rho
be a
p
-core,
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and
n=|\rho|+pd
. The defect group
D^{\rho,d}
of
B^{\rho,d}
is abelian if and only if
d<p
. Moreover, in this case
D^{\rho,d}\cong C_{p}^{\times d}
and

N_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(D^{\rho,d})\cong\mathfrak{S}_{|\rho|}\times\big((C_{p}\rtimes C_{p-1})\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}\big).

The Brauer correspondent
b^{\rho,d}
of
B^{\rho,d}
is then the block of

N_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(D^{\rho,d})

isomorphic to

B^{\rho,0}\otimes\big(\mathbb{F}(C_{p}\rtimes C_{p-1})\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}\big)
, which is Morita equivalent to
\mathbb{F}(C_{p}\rtimes C_{p-1})\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}

since
B^{\rho,0}
is a matrix algebra.

In fact,
\mathbb{F}(C_{p}\rtimes C_{p-1})=b^{\varnothing,1}
, and a special case of Broué’s conjecture is that
b^{\varnothing,1}
is derived equivalent to
B^{\varnothing,1}
,
where
B^{\varnothing,1}
is the principal block of
\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{p}
.
This special case follows from a result of Rickard
[
Rickard_1
, Theorem 4.2]
. Now using a theorem of Marcus
[
Mar
]
, we have that
b^{\rho,d}
derived equivalent to the wreath product
B^{\varnothing,1}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. Thus, Broué’s conjecture for symmetric groups is equivalent to the assertion that, under the assumption
d<p
, the block
B^{\rho,d}
is derived equivalent to
B^{\varnothing,1}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.

For every
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, Rouquier
[
RoTh
]
defined special
p
-cores, referred to as
d
-Rouquier cores
, which are generic in a certain (combinatorial or Lie theoretic) sense, and conjectured that these should play a special role in representation theory of symmetric groups. The blocks
B^{\rho,d}
, with
\rho
being
d
-Rouquier, are called
RoCK blocks
.

Theorem (Chuang-Kessar).

Let
d<p
and the block
B^{\rho,d}
be RoCK. Then
B^{\rho,d}
is Morita  equivalent to
B^{\varnothing,1}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.

In particular, this proves Broué’s conjecture for RoCK blocks (with abelian defect).
Broué’s conjecture for general blocks (with abelian defect) now comes from:

Theorem (Chuang-Rouquier).

Arbitrary blocks
B^{\rho,d}
and
B^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}}
of symmetric groups are derived equivalent if and only if
d=d^{\prime}
.

To summarize, the scheme of the proof of Broué’s conjecture is as follows. Let
d<p
and
B^{\rho^{\prime},d}
be any block of weight
d
. Choose
\rho
to be a
d
-Rouquier core. Then

(1.1.1)

\displaystyle b^{\rho^{\prime},d}\,

\displaystyle\cong\,\,B^{\rho^{\prime},0}\otimes\big(b^{\varnothing,1}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}\big)

(1.1.2)

\displaystyle\sim_{\mathrm{Mor}}\,\,b^{\varnothing,1}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}

\displaystyle(\text{since $B^{\rho^{\prime},0}$ is a matrix algebra})

(1.1.3)

\displaystyle\sim_{\mathop{\rm der}\nolimits}\,\,B^{\varnothing,1}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}

\displaystyle(\text{by Rickard and Marcus})

(1.1.4)

\displaystyle\sim_{\mathrm{Mor}}\,\,B^{\rho,d}

\displaystyle(\text{by Chuang-Kessar})

(1.1.5)

\displaystyle\sim_{\mathop{\rm der}\nolimits}\,\,B^{\rho^{\prime},d}

\displaystyle(\text{by Chuang-Rouquier}).

1.1b.
Beyond abelian defect for blocks of symmetric groups

Let us now drop the assumption
d<p
, in particular we do not suppose anymore that the defect group of a block is abelian.

By Chuang-Rouquier, any block of weight
d
is still derived equivalent to a RoCK block
B^{\rho,d}
. For any RoCK block
B^{\rho,d}
,
Evseev
[
Evseev
, Theorem 1.1]
constructed an explicit idempotent
\mathsf{f}_{\rho,d}
such that

\mathsf{f}_{\rho,d}B^{\rho,d}\mathsf{f}_{\rho,d}\cong B^{\varnothing,1}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}.

Moreover, the basic algebra of
B^{\varnothing,1}
is the Brauer tree algebra
\mathsf{Br}(p)
corresponding to a linear tree with
p
vertices. So the idempotent
\mathsf{f}_{\rho,d}
can be refined to an idempotent
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}_{\rho,d}
such that
\mathsf{f}^{\prime}_{\rho,d}B^{\rho,d}\mathsf{f}^{\prime}_{\rho,d}\cong\mathsf{Br}(p)\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. Thus we get (without assuming
d<p
):

Theorem (Evseev).

Suppose that
B^{\rho,d}
is a RoCK block. Then there exists an idempotent
\mathsf{f}
such that
\mathsf{f}B^{\rho,d}\mathsf{f}\cong\mathsf{Br}(p)\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
.

However, for
d\geq p
, the algebra
B^{\rho,d}
has more irreducible modules than the algebra
\mathsf{Br}(p)\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, so they are certainly neither Morita nor derived equivalent. Thus we need to ‘upgrade’ the local object
\mathsf{Br}(p)\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
to something more subtle.
Such an upgrade was suggested by Turner in
[
Turner
]
.

Turner defined new algebras
T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)

such that
eT^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)e\cong\mathsf{Br}(p)\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}

for some idempotent
e\in T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)
, but
T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)
is Morita equivalent to
\mathsf{Br}(p)\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
if and only if
d<p
. Turner conjectured that
any
RoCK block
B^{\rho,d}
is Morita equivalent to
T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)
, hence any weight
d
block of symmetric groups is derived equivalent to
T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)
, see Turner
[
Turner
, Conjecture 165]
. Thus,
T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)
was conjectured to be the sought after ‘local object’ which should replace wreath products of Brauer tree algebras in the context of ‘Broué’s conjecture’ for blocks of symmetric groups with non-abelian defect groups.

The definition of Turner’s algebra
T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)
is rather subtle. In the first approximation, it can be thought of as Schur’s algebra of
\mathsf{Br}(p)\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. However, a rigorous definition of
T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)
involves working over integers first and choosing some explicit maximal rank
\mathbb{Z}
-subalgebra in the endomorphism algebra of ‘permutation modules’ over
\mathsf{Br}(p)\wr\mathfrak{S}_{d}
. Alternatively, to define
T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d)
one can use Turner’s double construction (although it is rather difficult to work with explicitly), see
[
EK1
]
for more details on this.

Turner’s conjecture was proved in
[
EK2
]
. The main result of
[
EK2
]
can be interpreted as a construction of an explicit idempotent
\gamma_{\rho,d}
with the following properties:

Theorem (Evseev-Kleshchev).

Suppose that
B^{\rho,d}
is a RoCK block. Then there exists an idempotent
\gamma_{\rho,d}\in B^{\rho,d}
such that

B^{\rho,d}\sim_{\mathrm{Mor}}\gamma_{\rho,d}B^{\rho,d}\gamma_{\rho,d}\cong T^{\mathsf{Br}(p)}(d,d).

1.1c.
Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier algebras

The techniques of
[
EK2
]
rely crucially on the theory of
Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier (KLR) algebras
, also known as quiver Hecke algebras, which categorify the basic module over the quantum group
U_{q}({\mathfrak{g}})
for
{\mathfrak{g}}
of affine type
A_{\ell}^{(1)}
. Here
\ell=p-1
, where
p=\operatorname{char}\mathbb{F}
, but in fact we do not need to suppose that
\ell+1
is prime.

To be more precise, by
[
BKyoung
]
and
[
RoKLR
]
, there is an algebra isomorphism

B^{\rho,d}\cong R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta},

where
R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}
is the cyclotomic KLR algebra of Lie type
A_{p-1}^{(1)}
corresponding to the fundamental dominant weight
\Lambda_{0}
and the element
{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta
of the non-negative part of the root lattice
Q_{+}
, where
{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)\in Q_{+}
is the residue content of the core partition
\rho
, and
\delta
is the null-root.

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and
R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}
be the cyclotomic KLR algebra of Lie type
A_{\ell}^{(1)}
. If
\rho
is a
d
-Rouquier
(\ell+1)
-core we say that
R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}
is a RoCK block. Let
R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}
be a RoCK block. The main result of
[
EK2
]
is then a construction of an idempotent
\gamma_{\rho,d}\in R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}
such that

R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}\sim_{\mathrm{Mor}}\gamma_{\rho,d}R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}\gamma_{\rho,d}\cong T^{\mathsf{Br}(\ell+1)}(d,d).

The result of
[
EK2
]
described in the previous paragraph is proved over any field, in fact even over the integers. So it covers not only blocks of symmetric groups but also blocks of Iwahori-Hecke algebras over arbitrary fields with parameter a root of unity. Note also that the KLR algebras
R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}
come with
\mathbb{Z}
-grading, and Turner’s algebra
T^{\mathsf{Br}(\ell+1)}(d,d)
also inherits a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-grading from
\mathsf{Br}(\ell+1)
. The isomorphism
\gamma_{\rho,d}R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}\gamma_{\rho,d}\cong T^{\mathsf{Br}(\ell+1)}(d,d)
and the Morita equivalence
R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}\sim_{\mathrm{Mor}}\gamma_{\rho,d}R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}\gamma_{\rho,d}
established in
[
EK2
]
are actually
graded
. Moreover, gradings play a crucial role in the proofs.

The cyclotomic KLR algebra
R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{\theta}
is a quotient of the (infinite dimensional) KLR algebra
R_{\theta}
. The category of finitely generated graded
R_{\theta}
-modules is stratified with strata built out of the categories of
cuspidal

R_{\theta}
-modules, see
[
KMJAlg
]
. The cuspidal categories correspond to the positive roots of affine Kac-Moody Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. The real cuspidal categories (i.e. the ones corresponding to the real positive roots) are well-understood, while the
imaginary cuspidal categories
(the ones corresponding to the imaginary roots
d\delta
) are rather mysterious, see for example
[
KM
,
MM
]
. However, it is these imaginary cuspidal categories that are directly related to RoCK blocks.

The
imaginary cuspidal algebra
\bar{R}_{d\delta}
is the quotient of
R_{d\delta}
by the ideal generated by the standard idempotents
e(\text{\boldmath$i$})
corresponding to the non-cuspidal words
i
. The imaginary cuspidal category can be described as the category of
R_{d\delta}
-modules factoring through the surjection
R_{d\delta}\,{\twoheadrightarrow}\,\bar{R}_{d\delta}
.

The homomorphism
\Omega\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}R_{d\delta}\to R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}
defined as the composition of natural maps

R_{d\delta}\hookrightarrow R_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)}\otimes R_{d\delta}\hookrightarrow R_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}\,{\twoheadrightarrow}\,R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta},

factors through the surjection
R_{d\delta}\,{\twoheadrightarrow}\,\bar{R}_{d\delta}
if and only if
R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}
is RoCK. Moreover, in that case
\Omega(R_{d\delta})
is Morita equivalent to
R^{\Lambda_{0}}_{{\operatorname{cont}}(\rho)+d\delta}
and can be further truncated, preserving the Morita equivalence class, to get the Turner’s algebra
T^{\mathsf{Br}(\ell+1)}(d,d)
. In this way we get a connection between RoCK blocks, imaginary cuspidal categories for KLR algebras, and Turner’s algebras, which is crucial for understanding all three, see
[
EK2
]
.

1.2.
RoCK blocks for double covers of symmetric groups

Let
\mathbb{F}
be an algebraically closed field of odd characteristic
p>0
.

1.2a.
Spin blocks of symmetric and alternating groups

We denote by
\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
one of the two double covers of the symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and by
\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
the double cover of the alternating group
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
, see §
.

We have a canonical central element
z\in\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
and a central idempotent
e_{z}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(1-z)/2\in\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}.

This yields an ideal decomposition

\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}=\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}e_{z}\oplus\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(1-e_{z}).

So the blocks of
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}
split into blocks of
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}e_{z}
and the blocks of
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(1-e_{z})
. Note that
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}(1-e_{z})\cong\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, so the latter blocks are relatively well understood, see §
1.1
. From now on we concentrate on the blocks of
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}e_{z}
sometimes referred to as the
spin blocks
of the symmetric group. Little is known about these spin blocks. In particular, Broué’s conjecture for them is wide open.

In §
we introduce the algebra
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, a twisted group algebra of the symmetric group with basis
\{\mathpzc{t}_{w}\mid w\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}\}
. In fact,
\mathcal{T}_{n}\cong\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}e_{z}
and it is often important to consider
\mathcal{T}_{n}
as a superalgebra with

(\mathcal{T}_{n})_{\bar{0}}\cong\mathbb{F}\mathfrak{A}_{n}e_{z}

spanned by the
\mathpzc{t}_{w}
for even
w
, and
(\mathcal{T}_{n})_{\bar{1}}
spanned by the
\mathpzc{t}_{w}
for odd
w
. We can also speak of
superblocks
of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
, i.e. indecomposable superideals of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
. It is well-known (see §
) that superblocks are indecomposable as usual ideals (i.e. superblocks are blocks) unless we deal with trivial defect blocks. Trivial defect blocks are simple algebras so they are completely elementary, in particular Broué’s conjecture for them is trivial. So working with superblocks vs. blocks does not create any serious problems.

Superblocks of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
are labeled by pairs
(\rho,d)
, where
\rho
is a
\bar{p}
-core partition and
d
is a non-negative integer such that
|\rho|+dp=n
, see §
. We denote by
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
the block of
\mathcal{T}_{n}
corresponding to such a pair
(\rho,d)
. The integer
d
is referred to as the
weight
of the block
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
. We note that the case
d=0
corresponds to the defect zero situations, so we will assume that
d>0
throughout this section. The even part
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
is then a spin block of the alternating group algebra
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
.

We denote by
D^{\rho,d}
a defect group of
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
; it is known that
D^{\rho,d}
is also a defect group of
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
, see §
. The defect group
D^{\rho,d}
is abelian if and only if
d<p
. Let
\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}
be the Brauer correspondent block of
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
be the Brauer correspondent block of
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
(note that
\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
is indeed the even part of the superalgebra
\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}
), see §
for more details. Thus,
\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}
is a block of
\mathbb{F}N_{\tilde{\mathfrak{S}}_{n}}(D^{\rho,d})
and
\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
is a block of
\mathbb{F}N_{\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}}(D^{\rho,d})
.

Here and everywhere we denote by ‘
\otimes
’ a
tensor product of superalgebras
. For a superalgebra
A
a
twisted wreath superproduct

A\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d}
is defined in §
. Now the analogue of (
1.1.1
) is given by the following theorem, which comes from Proposition
.

Theorem A.

Let
0<d<p
and
\rho
be any
\bar{p}
-core. Then

\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}\cong\mathcal{B}^{\rho,0}\otimes(\mathfrak{b}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d})

and

\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}\cong\big(\mathcal{B}^{\rho,0}\otimes(\mathfrak{b}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d})\big)_{\bar{0}}.

For a partition
\lambda
we denote by
h(\lambda)
the number of non-zero parts of
\lambda
.
The analogue of (
1.1.2
) is given by the following theorem, which comes easily from Theorem A (see (
) and (
)):

Theorem B.

Let
0<d<p
and
\rho
be any
\bar{p}
-core. Then

\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}\sim_{{\mathrm{Mor}}}\begin{cases}\mathfrak{b}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is even,}\\
(\mathfrak{b}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d})_{{\bar{0}}}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is odd,}\end{cases}

and

\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}\sim_{{\mathrm{Mor}}}\begin{cases}(\mathfrak{b}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d})_{{\bar{0}}}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is even,}\\
\mathfrak{b}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

The analogue of the Rickard and Marcus step (
1.1.3
) is now given by the following theorem, which is Proposition

Theorem C.

Let
0<d<p
and
\rho
be any
\bar{p}
-core. Then

\mathfrak{b}^{\rho,d}\sim_{\mathop{\rm der}\nolimits}\begin{cases}\mathcal{B}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is even,}\\
(\mathcal{B}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d})_{{\bar{0}}}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is odd,}\end{cases}

and

While Theorems A and B are established using standard techniques in modular representation theory and can be considered as a folklore, Theorem C requires more work since it involves extension of the results of Marcus from wreath products to twisted wreath products.

1.2b.
RoCK spin blocks

We now discuss the spin analogue of the Chuang-Kessar step (
1.1.4
). In Section
, we define the notion of a
RoCK spin block
. As in the classical case described in §
1.1a
, for every
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define
d
-Rouquier
\bar{p}
-cores
using
\bar{p}
-abaci (see §
). Then, if
\rho
is a
d
-Rouquier
\bar{p}
-core, the spin block
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
is called
RoCK
. The corresponding spin block
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}
of
\mathbb{F}\tilde{\mathfrak{A}}_{n}
is also called
RoCK
.

One of the main goals of this paper is to establish nice properties of RoCK spin blocks analogous to the properties of classical RoCK blocks established in
[
CK
]
and
[
Evseev
]
, and to prove Broué’s conjecture for RoCK spin blocks of symmetric and alternating groups. In particular, we have the following analogue of the Chuang-Kessar step (
1.1.4
):

Theorem D.

Let
0<d<p
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
be a RoCK block.
Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}\sim_{{\mathrm{Mor}}}\begin{cases}\mathcal{B}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is even,}\\
(\mathcal{B}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d})_{{\bar{0}}}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is odd,}\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{\bar{0}}\sim_{{\mathrm{Mor}}}\begin{cases}(\mathcal{B}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d})_{{\bar{0}}}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is even,}\\
\mathcal{B}^{\varnothing,1}\wr_{\mathop{\tt s}\nolimits}\mathcal{T}_{d}&\text{if }|\rho|-h(\rho)\text{ is odd.}\end{cases}

Theorems D and C have the following immediate corollary:

Theorem E.

Broué’s abelian defect group conjecture holds for RoCK spin blocks of symmetric and alternating groups.

Now, to deduce Broué’s conjecture for all spin blocks of symmetric and alternating groups it would be sufficient to prove the following spin analogue of Chuang-Rouquier, the so-called Kessar-Schaps conjecture, cf.
[
AriSch
]
:

Conjecture (Kessar-Schaps).

Arbitrary spin blocks
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}}
of symmetric groups (or the spin blocks
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}_{{\bar{0}}}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}}_{{\bar{0}}}
of alternating groups) are derived equivalent if and only if
d=d^{\prime}
and
|\rho|-h(\rho)\equiv|\rho^{\prime}|-h(\rho^{\prime})\text{ }(\text{\rm mod }2)\,
. Moreover,
\mathcal{B}^{\rho,d}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\rho^{\prime},d^{\prime}}_{{\bar{0}}}
are derived equivalent if and only if

1.2c.
Sergeev and related superalgebras

It is well-known that Clifford superalgebras play an important role in the theory of spin representations of symmetric groups. For a superalgebra

(2)

1.3.
Quiver Hecke superalgebras

1.3a.
Kang-Kashiwara-Tsuchioka isomorphism

To prove Theorems D and F, we use the Kang-Kashiwara-Tsuchioka isomorphism to translate the problem into the language of
quiver Hecke superalgebras
. Quiver Hecke superalgebras are non-trivial superanalogues of Khovanov-Lauda-Rouquier algebras defined in
[

]
and used to categorify quantum Kac-Moody algebras and superalgebras, cf.
[
KKO
,
KKOII
]
and
[
BE2
]

The Kang-Kashiwara-Tsuchioka isomorphism relates spin blocks and certain cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras, which allows us to deduce Theorems D and F from the corresponding results on these cyclotomic quiver Hecke superalgebras. The proofs use categorification techniques of
[
KKO
]
and the theory of imaginary cuspidal representations for quiver Hecke superalgebras, which is a superanalogue of
[
Kcusp
]
.

To be more precise, let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be the Kac-Moody Lie algebra of type

2.1d
for the corresponding standard Lie theoretic notation. In particular, the vertices of the Dynkin diagram of

are labeled by the elements of the set

is the null-root,

To every element

[

called a

We now review the connections between the quiver Hecke superalgebras and blocks of the superalgebras

[

]

First, it is pointed out in

On the other hand, Kang, Kashiwara and Tsuchioka

L_{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}
, and
\{L_{\text{\boldmath$\mu$}}\mid\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in{\mathscr{P}}^{\ell}(m)\}
is a complete irredundant set of irreducible cuspidal
{\mathcal{R}}_{m\delta}
-supermodules up to isomorphism.

Let
\theta\in Q_{+}
. A
root partition of
\theta
is a pair
({\underline{m}},\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
, where
{\underline{m}}=(m_{\beta})_{\beta\in\Psi}
is a tuple of non-negative integers such that
\sum_{\beta\in\Psi}m_{\beta}\beta=\theta
and
\text{\boldmath$\mu$}\in{\mathscr{P}}^{\ell}(m_{\delta})
. Denote by
{\mathscr{P}}(\theta)
the set of all root partitions of
\theta
. Given
({\underline{m}},\text{\boldmath$\mu$})\in{\mathscr{P}}(\theta)
, note that
m_{\beta}=0
for almost all
\beta
, so we can choose a finite subset

\beta_{1}\succ\dots\succ\beta_{s}\succ\delta\succ\beta_{-t}\succ\dots\succ\beta_{-1}

of

such that
m_{\beta}=0
for
\beta

m_{u}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=m_{\beta_{u}}

({\underline{m}},\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
in the form

({\underline{m}},\text{\boldmath$\mu$})=(\beta_{1}^{m_{1}},\dots,\beta_{s}^{m_{s}},\text{\boldmath$\mu$},\beta_{-t}^{m_{-t}},\dots,\beta_{-1}^{m_{-1}}).

Denote also

\langle{\underline{m}}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(m_{1}\beta_{1},\dots,m_{s}\beta_{s},m_{\delta}\delta,m_{-t}\beta_{-t},\dots,m_{-1}\beta_{-1})\in Q_{+}^{s+t+1},

so we have a parabolic subalgebra
{\mathcal{R}}_{\langle{\underline{m}}\rangle}\subseteq{\mathcal{R}}_{\theta}

Theorem I.

Let
\theta\in Q_{+}
. For every
({\underline{m}},\text{\boldmath$\mu$})\in{\mathscr{P}}(\theta)
, the induced module

{\mathrm{Ind}}_{\langle{\underline{m}}\rangle}L_{m_{1}\beta_{1}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes L_{m_{s}\beta_{s}}\boxtimes L(\text{\boldmath$\mu$})\boxtimes L_{m_{-t}\beta_{-t}}\boxtimes\dots\boxtimes L_{m_{-1}\beta_{-1}}

has an irreducible head denoted
L({\underline{m}},\text{\boldmath$\mu$})
. Moreover,

\{L({\underline{m}},\text{\boldmath$\mu$})\mid({\underline{m}},\text{\boldmath$\mu$})\in{\mathscr{P}}(\theta)\}

is a complete irredundant set of irreducible
{\mathcal{R}}_{\theta}
-supermodules up to isomorphism.

Theorems H and I have to be proved together.

The real cuspidal algebra
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{d\beta}
has an easy structure and it is Morita equivalent to the algebra of symmetric polynomials in
d
variables. On the other hand, the imaginary cuspidal algebras
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{d\delta}
is very interesting. In Theorems
and
, we show that it is related to the
affine Brauer tree algebra

H_{d}({\mathsf{A}}_{\ell})
defined in §
2.2f
:

Theorem J.

There exists an idempotent
\gamma\in\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{d\delta}

such that

\gamma\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{d\delta}\gamma\cong H_{d}({\mathsf{A}}_{\ell}).

Moreover,
\bar{\mathcal{R}}_{d\delta}\sim_{\mathop{\rm sMor}\nolimits}H_{d}({\mathsf{A}}_{\ell})
if and only if
d<p
.

Theorem J is closely connected to Theorem G and the two have to be proved together. The proof depends on Theorem H. A crucial role in the proofs is played by the dimension formulas discussed in the next subsection.

1.3d.

2.1a.
Generalities

\bullet

\mathbb{F}
is an arbitrary ground field.

\bullet

is an indeterminate.

\bullet

\ell

\bullet

2.1b.
Partitions, compositions, symmetric group

\bullet

A
composition
of
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is a tuple

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
of non-negative integers summing to
d
.

\bullet

A
partition
of
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is an infinite tuple
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
of non-negative integers such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots
and
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots=d
.

\bullet

The trivial partition
(0,0,\dots)
of
0
is denoted
\varnothing
.

\bullet

The
length
of a partition
\lambda
is defined as

h(\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\max\{k\mid\lambda_{k}>0\}

(by convention,
h(\varnothing)=0
). If
k\geq h(\lambda)
we also write
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k})
.

\bullet

We denote by
{\mathscr{P}}(d)
the set of all partitions of
d
, and set
{\mathscr{P}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigsqcup_{d\geq 0}{\mathscr{P}}(d).
For
\lambda\in{\mathscr{P}}(d)
we write
d=|\lambda|
.

\bullet

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we have the set
{\mathscr{P}}^{m}
of
m
-tuples of partitions, referred to as
m
-multipartitions
. Typically, we use the notation
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}=(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(m)})
for elements of
{\mathscr{P}}^{m}
. For
1\leq k\leq m
, we refer to
\lambda^{(k)}
as the
k
th component
of the multipartition
\lambda
.

\bullet

For
\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}=(\lambda^{(1)},\dots,\lambda^{(m)})\in{\mathscr{P}}^{m}
, we write
|\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}|\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=|\lambda^{(1)}|+\dots+|\lambda^{(m)}|
,

and for
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, denote
{\mathscr{P}}^{m}(d)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\{\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}\in{\mathscr{P}}^{m}\mid|\text{\boldmath$\lambda$}|=d\}
.

\bullet

{\mathfrak{S}}_{d}
is the
symmetric group
on
\{1,\dots,d\}
(by convention,
\mathfrak{S}_{0}
is the trivial group), and
s_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(r,r+1)\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{d}
for
r=1,\dots,d-1
are the
elementary transpositions
.

\bullet

We denote by
\leq
the Bruhat order on
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, i.e. for
u,w\in\mathfrak{S}_{d}
, we have
u\leq w
of and only if
u=s_{r_{a_{1}}}\cdots s_{r_{a_{b}}}
for some
1\leq a_{1}<\cdots<a_{b}\leq l
, where
w=s_{r_{1}}\cdots s_{r_{l}}
is a reduced decomposition for
w
.

\bullet

For a composition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
of
d
, we have the standard
parabolic subgroup

{\mathfrak{S}}_{\lambda}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\mathfrak{S}}_{\lambda_{1}}\times\dots\times{\mathfrak{S}}_{\lambda_{n}}\leq{\mathfrak{S}}_{d}
.

\bullet

We denote by
{\mathscr{D}}^{\lambda}
(resp.
{}^{\lambda}{\mathscr{D}}
) the set of the minimal length coset representatives for
{\mathfrak{S}}_{d}/{\mathfrak{S}}_{\lambda}
(resp.
{\mathfrak{S}}_{\lambda}\backslash{\mathfrak{S}}_{d}
).

2.1c.
Words and shuffles

\bullet

For
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we write elements of
I^{d}
as
\text{\boldmath$i$}=(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})=i_{1}\cdots i_{d}
and refer to them as
words
. The group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
acts on
I^{d}
by place permutations.

\bullet

Given words
\text{\boldmath$i$}\in I^{d}
and
\text{\boldmath$j$}\in I^{e}
, we have the concatenation
\text{\boldmath$i$}\text{\boldmath$j$}\in I^{d+e}
.

\bullet

If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
is a composition of
d
and
\text{\boldmath$i$}^{r}\in I^{\lambda_{r}}
for
r=1,\dots,n
, the words of the form
w\cdot(\text{\boldmath$i$}^{1}\cdots\text{\boldmath$i$}^{n})
with
w\in{\mathscr{D}}^{\lambda}
are called
shuffles
of
\text{\boldmath$i$}^{1},\dots,\text{\boldmath$i$}^{n}
.

Example 2.1.1
.

Let
\text{\boldmath$i$}=(\ell-1)(\ell-2)\cdots 100
,
\text{\boldmath$j$}=(\ell-1)(\ell-2)\cdots 1
and
\text{\boldmath$l$}=(\ell-1)(\ell-2)\cdots 10
. Note that any shuffle
\text{\boldmath$m$}=m_{1}\cdots m_{2\ell}
of
l
with itself is a word
which satisfies the following condition: for any
i=1,\dots,\ell-1
and any
k=1,\dots,2\ell
, the number of
i
’s among
m_{1},\dots,m_{k}
is not smaller than the number of
(i-1)
’s among
m_{1},\dots,m_{k}
.
It follows that
m
is also a shuffle of
i
and
j
; indeed proceed along
m
from left to right and take into
i
the first
\ell-1
, then the first
\ell-2
, etc. until the first
1
, and both
0
’s. The rest will be the word
j
. This manifests the fact that
m
is a shuffle of
i
and
j
.

2.1d.
Lie theory

\bullet

{\mathfrak{g}}
is the Kac-Moody Lie algebra of type
A_{2\ell}^{(2)}
,
see
[
Kac
, Ch.4]
.

\bullet

The Dynkin diagram of
{\mathfrak{g}}
has vertices labeled by
I=\{0,1,\dots,\ell\}

\bullet

P
is the
weight lattice
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

\bullet

\{\alpha_{i}\>|\>i\in I\}\subset P
are the
simple roots
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

\bullet

\{h_{i}\>|\>i\in I\}\subset P^{*}
and the

simple coroots
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

\bullet

The
Cartan matrix

\left(\begin{matrix}2&-2&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
-1&2&-1&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&-1&2&\cdots&0&0&0\\

0&0&0&\dots&2&-1&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&-1&2&-2\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&-1&2\\
\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{if\ \ $\ell\geq 2$,\ \ and}\quad\left(\begin{matrix}2&-4\\

\bullet

\{\Lambda_{i}\>|\>i\in I\}\subset P

.

\bullet

.

\bullet

generated by the simple roots.

\bullet

\bullet

For

, its

is

{\operatorname{ht}}(\theta)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{i\in I}m_{i}
.

\bullet

(.|.)
is a normalized invariant form on

whose Gram matrix with respect to the linearly independent set
\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}
is:

\left(\begin{matrix}2&-2&0&\cdots&0&0&0\\
-2&4&-2&\cdots&0&0&0\\
0&-2&4&\cdots&0&0&0\\
&&&\ddots&&&\\
0&0&0&\dots&4&-2&0\\
0&0&0&\dots&-2&4&-4\\
0&0&0&\dots&0&-4&8\\
\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{if $\ell\geq 2$, and}\quad\left(\begin{matrix}2&-4\\
-4&8\end{matrix}\right)\quad\text{if $\ell=1$.}

In particular, we have

(\alpha_{0}|\alpha_{0})=2
,
(\alpha_{\ell}|\alpha_{\ell})=8
, and
(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{i})=4
for all other

. We also have

\bullet

For all

, we denote
\alpha_{i}^{\vee}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=2\alpha_{i}/(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{i})
. In particular,
(\alpha_{i}^{\vee}|\Lambda_{j})=\delta_{i,j}
.

\bullet

We denote

\delta=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}2\alpha_{i}+\alpha_{\ell}.

Then
\{\Lambda_{0},\dots,\Lambda_{\ell},\delta\}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis of

[
Kac

. The coefficient of
\alpha_{i}

\bullet

We denote
{\mathtt{K}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=h_{0}+2\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}h_{i}

\langle{\mathtt{K}},\Lambda_{0}\rangle=1
and
\langle{\mathtt{K}},\alpha_{i}\rangle=0
for all
i\in I

[
Kac

h_{i}
in
\delta

a_{i}^{\vee}

a_{i}^{\vee}=1
if

a_{i}^{\vee}=2

\bullet

We denote for

and

q_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=q^{(\alpha_{i}|\alpha_{i})/2},\quad[n]_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\frac{q_{i}^{n}-q_{i}^{-n}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},\quad[n]_{i}^{!}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=[1]_{i}[2]_{i}\cdots[n]_{i}.

\bullet

For
\text{\boldmath$i$}=i_{1}\cdots i_{n}\in I^{n}

\|\text{\boldmath$i$}\|\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\alpha_{i_{1}}+\dots+\alpha_{i_{n}}\in Q_{+}

\bullet

For

2.2.
Graded superalgebras and supermodules

Throughout the paper, unless otherwise stated, we work over the ground field

2.2a.
Graded superalgebras

By a
graded superspace
we understand a (vector) space over
\mathbb{F}
with decomposition

V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z},\,{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2}V_{n,{\varepsilon}}.

We refer to the elements of
V_{n,{\varepsilon}}
as the (homogeneous) elements of
bidegree

(n,{\varepsilon})
. We also refer to
n
as
degree
and
{\varepsilon}
as
parity
and write for
v\in V_{n,{\varepsilon}}
:

\operatorname{bideg}(v)=(n,{\varepsilon}),\quad\deg(v)=n,\quad|v|={\varepsilon}.

A (homogeneous)
subsuperspace

W\subseteq V
is a subspace such that

W=\sum_{n,{\varepsilon}}(W\cap V_{n,{\varepsilon}}).

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
we set
V_{n}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=V_{n,{\bar{0}}}\oplus V_{n,{\bar{1}}}
, thus considering
V
as a graded space
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
. We also set
V_{{\bar{0}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n,{\bar{0}}}
and
V_{{\bar{1}}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n,{\bar{1}}},
thus considering
V
as a superspace
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
.
Conversely, a graded vector space is considered as a graded superspace concentrated in bidegrees
(*,{\bar{0}})
, a superspace is considered as a graded superspace concentrated in bidegrees
(0,*)
, and a vector space is considered as a graded superspace concentrated in bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
.
For a graded vector space
V=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
with finite dimensional graded components, its
graded dimension
is

\dim_{q}V\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(\dim V_{n})q^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}((q)).

For graded vector spaces
V,W
with finite dimensional graded components, we write

(2.2.1)

\dim_{q}V\leq\dim_{q}W

to indicate that
\dim V_{n}\leq\dim W_{n}
for all
n
.

Let
V,W
be graded superspaces. We define
V^{*}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z},\,{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2}(V_{n,{\varepsilon}})^{*}
, considered as a graded superspace via
(V^{*})_{n,{\varepsilon}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(V_{-n,{\varepsilon}})^{*}
.
The tensor product
V\otimes W
is considered as a graded superspace via
\operatorname{bideg}(v\otimes w)=(\deg(v)+\deg(w),|v|+|w|)
(here and below in places like this it is assumed that
v
and
w
are homogeneous). For
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}/2
, a bidegree
(m,\delta)
(homogeneous) linear map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}V\to W
is a linear map satisfying
f(V_{n,{\varepsilon}})\subseteq W_{n+m,{\varepsilon}+\delta}
for all
n,{\varepsilon}
.

Let
V
be a superspace and
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.
The symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{d}
acts on
V^{\otimes d}
via

(2.2.2)

{}^{w}(v_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{d})\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(-1)^{[w;v_{1},\dots,v_{d}]}v_{w^{-1}(1)}\otimes\dots\otimes v_{w^{-1}(d)},

where

[w;v_{1},\dots,v_{d}]\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{1\leq a<c\leq d,w(a)>w(c)}|v_{a}||v_{c}|.

A
graded superalgebra
is a graded superspace
A
which is a unital algebra such that
A_{n,{\varepsilon}}A_{m,\delta}\subseteq A_{n+m,{\varepsilon}+\delta}
for all
n,m,{\varepsilon},\delta
. For example, given a finite dimensional graded superspace
V
, we have the graded superalgebra

\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z},{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}/2}\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)_{n,{\varepsilon}},

where
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)_{n,{\varepsilon}}
consists of all homogeneous linear maps of bidegree
(n,{\varepsilon})
. We refer to
\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}}(V)
as a
graded matrix superalgebra
or simply as a
matrix algebra
.

Let
A,B
be graded superalgebras. An
isomorphism

f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A\to B
is an algebra isomorphism which is homogeneous of bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
.
The tensor product
A\otimes B
of graded superspaces is considered as a graded superalgebra via

\displaystyle(a\otimes b)(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime})=(-1)^{|b||a^{\prime}|}aa^{\prime}\otimes bb^{\prime}.

For
1\leq r<s\leq d
, we have maps

\displaystyle\iota^{d}_{r}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A\to A^{\otimes d},\ a\mapsto 1^{\otimes(r-1)}\otimes a\otimes 1^{\otimes(d-r)},

\displaystyle\iota^{d}_{r,s}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A\otimes A\to A^{\otimes d},\ a\otimes b\mapsto 1^{\otimes(r-1)}\otimes a\otimes 1^{\otimes(s-r-1)}\otimes b\otimes 1^{\otimes(d-s)}.

For
x\in A
and
y\in A\otimes A
, we also denote

(2.2.3)

x_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\iota^{d}_{r}(x),\quad y_{r,s}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\iota^{d}_{r,s}(y).

We define
A^{{\mathrm{op}}}
, the
opposite graded superalgebra
of
A
, to be equal to
A
as a graded superspace but with multiplication given by
a.b=(-1)^{|a||b|}ba
, for all
a,b\in A
.

An
antiautomorphism
of a graded superalgebra
A
is a bidegree
(0,{\bar{0}})
linear map such that
\tau(ab)=\tau(b)\tau(a)
(note there is no sign).
We have an algebra involution

(2.2.4)

\sigma=\sigma_{A}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A\to A,\ a\mapsto(-1)^{|a|}a.

Let
A
be a graded superalgebra and
B\subseteq A
be a (unital) graded subsuperalgebra. The
supercentralizer

Z_{A}(B)
of
B
in
A
is the
graded subsuperalgebra of
A
defined as the linear span of all (homogeneous)
a\in A
such that
ba=(-1)^{|b||a|}ab
for all (homogeneous)
b\in B

Lemma 2.2.5
.

Let
A
be a graded superalgebra and

(A,B)

-bisupermodule

Lemma 2.2.20

Let
G
be a finite group. A
G
-graded crossed product
will refer to an algebra
A
with a decomposition
\bigoplus_{g\in G}A_{g}
into subspaces such that
A_{g}A_{h}\subseteq A_{gh}
, for all
g,h\in G
, and such that, for all
g\in G
, we have
A_{g}\cap A^{\times}\neq\varnothing
. If, in addition,
A
is a superalgebra and each
A_{g}
is a subsuperspace then we refer to
A
as a
G
-graded crossed superproduct
.
If
A
is a
G
-graded crossed (super)product, then so is
A^{{\mathrm{op}}}
by defining
A^{{\mathrm{op}}}_{g}=A_{g^{-1}}
, for all
g\in G
.
Note that
A_{1_{G}}
is always a sub(super)algebra of
A
, and
(A_{1_{G}})^{\mathrm{op}}=A^{\mathrm{op}}_{1_{G}}
.

A
G
-graded crossed (super)product
A
is called
symmetric
if it has a symmetrizing form
{\mathrm{tr}}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A\to\mathbb{F}
that turns
A
into a symmetric superalgebra, as in
\lx@sectionsign
2.2a
and restricts to a symmetrizing form on
A_{1_{G}}
.

If
A
and
B
are
G
-graded crossed (super)products, we define

(2.2.21)

(A,B)_{G}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\sum_{g\in G}A_{g}\otimes B_{g^{-1}}=\sum_{g\in G}A_{g}\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}}_{g}\subseteq A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}}.

The definition of
G
-graded crossed (super)product ensures that

A_{1_{G}}\otimes B_{1_{G}}^{\mathrm{op}}\subseteq(A,B)_{G}\subseteq A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}}

are sub(super)algebras.

In part
we make repeated use of the following result which is essentially just a super version of
[
Mar
, Theorem 3.4(a)]
. Recall that we have identified

(A,B)
-bisupermodules and
(A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}})
-supermodules via (
2.2.7
).

Proposition 2.2.22
.

Let
G
be a finite group, and
A,\,B
be symmetric
G
-graded crossed superproducts. Suppose
M
is an
(A_{1_{G}}\otimes B_{1_{G}}^{\mathrm{op}})
-supermodule inducing a Morita superequivalence between
A_{1_{G}}
and
B_{1_{G}}
. If
M
extends to an
(A,B)_{G}
-supermodule, then
{\mathrm{Ind}}_{(A,B)_{G}}^{A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}}}M
induces a Morita superequivalence between
A
and
B
.

Proof.

Note that
|A|
and
|B|
are symmetric
G
-graded crossed products with
|A|_{g}=A_{g}
and
|B|_{g}=B_{g}
for all
g\in G
.

View
M
as an
(A_{1_{G}},B_{1_{G}})
-bisupermodule, then forget the super structures and view
M
as an
(|A_{1_{G}}|,|B_{1_{G}}|)
-bimodule, which is the same as an

(|A_{1_{G}}|\otimes|B_{1_{G}}|^{{\mathrm{op}}})
-module.
Since by assumption the
(A_{1_{G}}\otimes B_{1_{G}}^{\mathrm{op}})
-supermodule structure on
M
extends to
(A,B)_{G}
, the
(|A_{1_{G}}|\otimes|B_{1_{G}}|^{\mathrm{op}})
-module structure on
M
extends to
(|A|,|B|)_{G}
via:

(2.2.23)

(a_{g}\otimes b_{g^{-1}})*m\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(-1)^{|b_{g^{-1}}||m|}(a_{g}\otimes b_{g^{-1}})\cdot m,

for all
g\in G
,
a_{g}\in A_{g}
,
b_{g^{-1}}\in B_{g^{-1}}
and
m\in M
, where ‘
*
’ denotes the new action of
(|A|,|B|)_{G}
on
M
and ‘
\cdot
’ the given action of
(A,B)_{G}
on
M
.

We now have the
A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}}
-supermodule

\text{\boldmath$M$}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\mathrm{Ind}}_{(A,B)_{G}}^{A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}}}M

and the

|A|\otimes|B|^{{\mathrm{op}}}
-module

\text{\boldmath$M$}^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}={\mathrm{Ind}}_{(|A|,|B|)_{G}}^{|A|\otimes|B|^{{\mathrm{op}}}}M.

By
[
Mar
, Theorem 3.4(a)]
, the module
\text{\boldmath$M$}^{\prime}
induces a Morita equivalence between
|A|
and
|B|
. (Note that
[
Mar
]
primarily deals with blocks of finite groups. However,
[
Mar
, Remarks 3.2(e)]
asserts that one can also apply the theorem in this more general, symmetric algebra setting. In particular, there is no condition on the field/ring the algebras are defined over.)

Recall that
\text{\boldmath$M$}^{\prime}
is naturally a superspace with parity given by
|(a\otimes b)\otimes m|=|a|+|b|+|m|
for
a\in A
,
b\in B
and
m\in M
. So we can view
\text{\boldmath$M$}^{\prime}
as an
(A,B)
-bisupermodule or as an

(A\otimes B^{\mathrm{op}})
-supermodule using (
2.2.7
) so that:

\displaystyle(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime}).[(a\otimes b)\otimes m]=(-1)^{|b^{\prime}|(|a|+|b|+|m|)}(a^{\prime}a\otimes bb^{\prime})\otimes m,

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
,
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and
m\in M
. (Note above that
a\otimes b\in|A|\otimes|B|^{{\mathrm{op}}}
whereas
a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime}\in A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}}
.)
We now deduce using Remark
2.2.16
that
\text{\boldmath$M$}^{\prime}
induces a Morita superequivalence between
A
and
B
since
the natural isomorphism

|B|^{{\mathrm{op}}}\cong\operatorname{End}_{|A|}\left(\text{\boldmath$M$}^{\prime}\right)

is certainly an isomorphism of superalgebras.

The action of
A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}}
on
M
is given by

\displaystyle(a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime}).[(a\otimes b)\otimes m]=(-1)^{|b^{\prime}|(|a|+|b|)}(a^{\prime}a\otimes bb^{\prime})\otimes m,

for all
a,a^{\prime}\in A
,
b,b^{\prime}\in B
and
m\in M
. (Here
a\otimes b
and
a^{\prime}\otimes b^{\prime}
both live in
A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}}
.) Therefore, using (
2.2.23
), we obtain an
(A\otimes B^{{\mathrm{op}}})
-supermodule isomorphism

\displaystyle\text{\boldmath$M$}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\text{\boldmath$M$},\ (a\otimes b)\otimes m\mapsto(-1)^{|b||m|}(a\otimes b)\otimes m.

Since
\text{\boldmath$M$}^{\prime}
induces a Morita superequivalence between
A
and
B
by the previous paragraph, we now deduce that so does
M
.

2.3.
Combinatorics

2.3a.
p
-strict partitions

Let
\lambda
be a partition. Collecting equal parts of
\lambda
, we can write it in the form

(2.3.1)

\lambda=(l_{1}^{m_{1}},\dots,l_{k}^{m_{k}})\ \text{with $l_{1}>\dots>l_{k}>0$ and $m_{1},\dots,m_{k}\geq 1$}.

We denote

(2.3.2)

\mathinner{\!\left\lVert\lambda\right\rVert}\,\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\prod_{r\,\,\text{with\,\,$p|l_{r}$}}\,\prod_{s=1}^{m_{r}}(1-(-q^{2})^{s}).

If
m_{r}>1
implies
p\mid l_{r}
for all
1\leq r\leq k
then
\lambda
is called
p
-strict
. Note that
0
-strict means simply
strict
, i.e. all parts are distinct.
If, in addition, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}<p&\hbox{if $p\mid\lambda_{r}$,}\\
\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{r+1}\leq p&\hbox{if $p\nmid\lambda_{r}$.}\end{array}\right.

then
\lambda
is called
p
-restricted
. We denote by
{\mathscr{P}}_{p}(n)
the set of all
p
-strict partitions of
n
, and let
{\mathscr{P}}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}{\mathscr{P}}_{p}(n).

We denote by
{\mathscr{P}}^{\operatorname{res}}_{p}(n)
the set of all
p
-restricted
p
-strict partitions of
n
, and let
{\mathscr{P}}^{\operatorname{res}}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=\bigsqcup_{n\geq 0}{\mathscr{P}}^{\operatorname{res}}_{p}(n).

Let
\lambda
be a
p
-strict partition. As usual, we identify
\lambda
with its
Young diagram

\lambda=\{(r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\mid s\leq\lambda_{r}\}.

We refer to the element
(r,s)\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\times\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
as the
node
in row
r
and column
s
.
We define a preorder ‘
\leq
’ on the nodes via

(r,s)\leq(r^{\prime},s^{\prime})

if and only if
s\leq s^{\prime}
.

We label the nodes with the elements of the set
I=\{0,1,\dots,\ell\}
as follows: the labeling follows the repeating pattern

0,1,\dots,\ell-1,\ell,\ell-1,\dots,1,0,

starting from the first column and going to the right,
see Example
2.3.6
below. If a node
{\tt A}\in\lambda
is labeled with
i
, we say that
{\tt A}
has
residue
i
and write
{\mathrm{Res}}\,{\tt A}=i
.

Following
[
Morris
,
LT
]
, we can associate to every
\lambda\in{\mathscr{P}}_{p}
its
\bar{p}
-core

{\operatorname{core}}(\lambda)\in{\mathscr{P}}_{p}^{\operatorname{res}}
. It is obtained from
\lambda
by first removing all parts of
\lambda
divisible by
p
to get a strict partition
\mu
(after reordering so that there are no zero parts); next, we remove
p
-bars
as usual to get the usual
\bar{p}
-core of
\mu
.
This means that we either remove two rows
i
and
j
such that
\mu_{i}+\mu_{j}=p
or we remove
p
boxes from row
i
providing no part of
\mu
equals
\mu_{i}-p
. After that we reorder the remaining parts so that we have a strict partition again and repeat the process until we arrive to a partition
{\operatorname{core}}(\lambda)
such that no further removals are possible. It follows from
[
MorYas
, Theorem 1]
that
{\operatorname{core}}(\lambda)
is well defined, see also §
2.3b
. It is clear from the definition that the number of nodes removed to go from
\lambda
to
{\operatorname{core}}(\lambda)
is divisible by
p
, we we can define a non-negative integer called the
\bar{p}
-weight
of
\lambda
:

(2.3.3)

{\operatorname{wt}}(\lambda)\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}=(|\lambda|-|{\operatorname{core}}(\lambda)|)/p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

A partition
\rho\in{\mathscr{P}}_{p}
is called a
\bar{p}
-core
if
{\operatorname{core}}(\rho)=\rho
. For such
\rho

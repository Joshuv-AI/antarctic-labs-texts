The Fundamental Group and Extensions of Motives of Jacobians of Curves
Subham Sarkar, Ramesh Sreekantan

Abstract
In this paper we construct extensions of mixed Hodge structure coming from the mixed Hodge structure on the graded quotients of the group ring of the Fundamental group of a smooth projective pointed curve which correspond to the regulators of certain motivic cohomology cycles on the Jacobian of the curve essentially constructed by Bloch and Beilinson. This leads to a new iterated integral expression for the regulator. This is a generalisation of a theorem of Colombo [ Col02 ] where she constructed the extension corresponding to Collino’s cycles in the Jacobian of a hyperelliptic curve. AMS Classification: 19F27, 11G55, 14C30, 14C35

Introduction

A formula, usually called Beilinson’s formula — though independently due to Deligne as well — describes the motivic cohomology group of a smooth projective variety
X
over a number field as the group of extensions in a conjectured category of mixed motives,
{\mathcal{MM}}_{{\mathds{Q}}}
. If
i
and
n
are two integers then
[
Sch93
]
,

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{MM}}_{{\mathds{Q}}}}({\mathds{Q}}(-n),h^{i}(X))=\begin{cases}CH^{n}_{\hom}(X)\otimes{\mathds{Q}}&\text{ if }i+1=2n\\
H^{i+1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Q}}(n))&\text{ if }i+1\neq 2n\end{cases}

Hence, if one had a way of constructing extensions in the category of mixed motives by some other method, it would provide a way of constructing motivic cycles.

One way of doing so is by considering the group ring of the fundamental group of the algebraic variety
{\mathds{Z}}[\pi_{1}(X,P)]
. If
J_{P}
is the augmentation ideal — the kernel of the map from
{\mathds{Z}}[\pi_{1}(X,P)]\to{\mathds{Z}}
— then the graded pieces
J_{P}^{a}/J_{P}^{b}
with
a<b
are expected to have a motivic structure. These give rise to natural extensions of motives — so one could hope that these extensions could be used to construct natural motivic cycles.

Understanding the motivic structure on the fundamental group is complicated. However, the Hodge structure on the fundamental group is well understood
[
Hai87
]
. The regulator of a motivic cohomology cycle can be thought of as the realisation of the corresponding extension of motives as an extension in the category of mixed Hodge structures. So while we may not be able to construct motivic cycles as extensions of
motives
coming from the fundamental group - we can hope to construct their regulators as extensions of
mixed Hodge structures
(MHS) coming from the fundamental group.

The aim of this paper is to describe this construction in the case of the motivic cohomology group of the Jacobian of a curve. The first work in this direction is due to Harris
[
Har83
]
and Pulte
[
Pul88
]
,
[
Hai87
]
. They showed that the Abel-Jacobi image of the modified diagonal cycle on the triple product of a pointed curve
(C,P)
, or alternatively the Ceresa cycle in the Jacobian
J(C)
of the curve, is the same as an extension class coming from
J_{P}/J_{P}^{3}
, where
J_{P}
is the augmentation ideal in the group ring of the fundamental group of
C
based at
P
.

In
[
Col02
]
, Colombo extended this theorem to show that the regulator of a cycle in the motivic cohomology of a Jacobian of a
hyperelliptic
curve, discovered by Collino
[
Col97
]
, can be realised as an extension class coming from
J_{P}/J_{P}^{4}
, where here
J_{P}
is the augmentation ideal of a related curve.

In this paper we extend Colombo’s result to more general curves. If
C
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g
with a function
f
with divisor
\operatorname{div}(f)=NQ-NR
for some points
Q
and
R
and some integer
N
and such that
f(P)=1
for some other point
P
, there is a motivic cohomology cycle
Z_{QR,P}
in
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(J(C),{\mathds{Z}}(g))
discovered by Bloch
[
Blo00
]
. We show that the regulator of this cycle can be expressed in terms of an extensions coming from
J_{P}/J_{P}^{4}
. When
C
is hyperelliptic and
Q
and
R
are ramification points of the canonical map to
{\mathds{P}}^{1}
, this is Colombo’s result.

A crucial step in Colombo’s work is to use the fact that the modified diagonal cycle is
torsion
in the Chow group
CH^{2}_{\hom}(C^{3})
when
C
is a hyperelliptic curve. This means the extension coming from
J_{P}/J_{P}^{3}
splits and hence does not depend on the base point
P
. This allows her to consider the extension for
J_{P}/J_{P}^{4}
. In general, that is
not
true — in fact the known examples of non-torsion modified diagonal cycles come from the curves we consider - namely modular and Fermat curves. Our main contribution is to use an idea of Rabi
[
Rab01
]
to show that Colombo’s arguments can be extended to work in our case as well. As a result we have a more general situation — which has some arithmetical applications.

In Colombo’s paper there were errors in Propositions 3.2 and 3.3 which were pointed out by a referee of an earlier version of this paper. Hence we had to make some revisions. As it turned out the statement of the main result still holds under some restricted conditions and much of the revisions we made were to understand these conditions. Unfortunately this has made the paper a little long.

We have the following theorem (
Theorem (
4.14
)
):

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g_{C}
over
{\mathds{C}}
. Let
P
,
Q
and
R
be three distinct points such that there is a function
f_{QR}
with
\operatorname{div}(f_{QR})=NQ-NR
for some
N
and
f_{QR}(P)=1
. Let
Z_{QR}=Z_{QR,P}
be the element of the motivic cohomology group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(J(C),{\mathds{Z}}(g))
constructed below in Section
2.3
. There exists an extension class
\epsilon^{4}_{QR,P}
in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{MHS}({\mathds{Z}}(-2),\wedge^{2}H^{1}(C))
constructed from the mixed Hodge structures associated to the fundamental groups
\pi_{1}(C\backslash Q,P)
and
\pi_{1}(C\backslash R,P)
such that

\epsilon^{4}_{QR,P}=(2g_{C}+1)N\operatorname{reg}_{{\mathds{Z}}}(Z_{QR})

in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{MHS}({\mathds{Z}}(-2),\wedge^{2}H^{1}(C))
.

In other words our theorem states that the regulator of a natural cycle in the motivic cohomology group of the Jacobian of curves,
being thought of as an extension class, is the same as the extension class of a natural extension of mixed Hodge structures
coming from the fundamental group of the curve.

The outline of the proof is as follows. In the second chapter we introduce background material on iterated integrals, regulators, motivic cycles and extensions. We then describe the cycle
Z_{QR,P}
and give a formula for its regulator. In the next chapter we construct an extension coming from the MHS on the fundamental group that we expect to be related to the regulator of our explicit cycle. Finally, we prove that that is indeed the case. This is the place where there was an error in the paper of Colombo
[
Col02
]
. One has to restrict to a certain subspace of
H_{1}(C\setminus\{Q,R\})
which is isomorphic to
H_{1}(C)
. There is no canonical way in which to choose this in general, but in our case the function
f_{QR}
determines such as space.

As a result of this we have a new iterated integral formula for the regulator which is more amenable to computation. In a subsequent paper we apply this in the case of Fermat curves to get an explicit expression for the regulator in terms of hypergeometric functions — analogous to the works of Otsubo
[
Ots11
]
,
[
Ots12
]
. As the primary requirement is a nice basis for the homology and the cohomology of the curve, we expect this will also work in the case of modular curve, though that case is quite well understood.

Darmon-Rotger-Sols
[
DRS12
]
have used the modified diagonal cycle to construct points on Jacobians of the curves and used the iterated integral approach to find a formula for the Abel-Jacobi image of these points. Starting with Bloch
[
Blo00
]
and later Collino
[
Col97
]
and Colombo
[
Col02
]
it has been known that these null homologous cycles degenerate to higher Chow cycles on related varieties. Recently Iyer and Müller-Stach
[
IM14
]
have shown that the modified diagonal cycle degenerates to the kind of cycles we consider in some special cases. This degeneration can be understood from the point of view of extensions and in fact the iterated integral expression we have for the regulator show that in a rather natural way in terms of the holomorphic forms degenerating to logarithmic forms.

Iterated Integrals, Cycles, Extensions and Regulators

2.1
Iterated Integrals

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
{\mathds{C}}
. Let
\alpha:[0,1]\to X
be a path and
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{n}
be
1
-forms on
X
. Suppose
\alpha^{*}(\omega_{i})=f_{i}(t)dt
. The
iterated integral of length n
of
{\omega}_{1},{\omega}_{2}\dots{\omega}_{n}
is defined to be

\int_{\alpha}\omega_{1}\omega_{2}\dots\omega_{n}:=\int_{0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq\dots\leq t_{n}\leq 1}f_{1}(t_{1})f_{2}(t_{2})\dots f_{n}(t_{n})dt_{1}dt_{2}\dots dt_{n}.

An
iterated integral of length
\leq n
is a linear combination of integrals of the form above with lengths
\leq n
. It is said to be a
homotopy functional
if it only depends on the homotopy class of the path
\alpha
. A homotopy functional gives a functional on the group ring of the fundamental group or path space.

Iterated integrals can be thought of as integrals on simplices and satisfy the following basic properties. Here we have only stated the results for length two iterated integrals, since that is the only type we will encounter in this paper.

Lemma 2.1

(Basic Properties)
.

Let
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
be smooth
1
-forms on
X
and
\alpha
and
\beta
piecewise smooth paths on
X
with
\alpha(1)=\beta(0)
. Then

1.

\displaystyle{\int_{\alpha\cdot\beta}\omega_{1}\omega_{2}=\int_{\alpha}\omega_{1}\omega_{2}+\int_{\beta}\omega_{1}\omega_{2}+\int_{\alpha}\omega_{1}\int_{\beta}\omega_{2}}

2.

\displaystyle{\int_{\alpha}\omega_{1}\omega_{2}+\int_{\alpha}\omega_{2}\omega_{1}=\int_{\alpha}\omega_{1}\int_{\alpha}\omega_{2}}

3.

\displaystyle{\int_{\alpha}df\omega_{1}=\int_{\alpha}f\omega_{1}-f(\alpha(0))\int_{\alpha}\omega_{1}}

4.

\displaystyle{\int_{\alpha}\omega_{1}df=f(\alpha(1))\int_{\alpha}\omega_{1}-\int_{\alpha}f\omega_{1}}

Proof.

This can be found in any article on iterated integrals, for instance Hain’s excellent article
[
Hai87
]
.∎

2.2
Motivic Cohomology Cycles

Let
X
be a smooth projective algebraic variety of dimension
g
defined over
{\mathds{C}}
. The motivic cohomology group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))
has the following presentation: Generators are represented by finite sums

Z=\sum_{i}(C_{i},f_{i})

where
C_{i}
are curves on
X
and
f_{i}:C_{i}\longrightarrow{\mathds{P}}^{1}
are functions on them subject to the co-cycle condition

\sum_{i}\operatorname{div}(f_{i})=0.

Relations in this group are defined as follows. If
Y
is a surface on
X
and
f
and
g
are functions on
Y
, one has the Steinberg element
\{f,g\}
in
K_{2}({\mathds{C}}(Y))
, where
{\mathds{C}}(Y)
is the function field of
Y
. To such an element one can consider the sum, called the tame symbol of
\{f,g\}
,

\tau(\{f,g\})=\sum_{W\in Y^{(1)}}(W,(-1)^{\operatorname{ord}_{W}(f)\operatorname{ord}_{W}(g)}\frac{f^{\operatorname{ord}_{W}(g)}}{g^{\operatorname{ord}_{W}(f)}}).

where
Y^{(1)}
is the collection of curves on
Y
. This is a finite sum and satisfies the co-cycle condition, hence lies in the above group. An element is said to be
0
in
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))
if it lies in the image of the free abelian group generated by the tame symbols of elements of
K_{2}({\mathds{C}}(Y))
for some surface
Y\subset X
. The group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))\otimes{\mathds{Q}}
is the same as the higher Chow group
CH^{g}(X,1)\otimes{\mathds{Q}}
.

In the group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))
there are certain
decomposable
cycles coming from the product

H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))_{dec}=\operatorname{Im}\left(H^{2g-2}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g-1)\otimes H^{1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(1)))\longrightarrow H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))\right).

This is simply
CH^{1}(X)\times{\mathds{C}}^{*}
. The group of
indecomposable
cycles is defined as the quotient

H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))_{ind}=H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))/H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))_{dec}.

In general it is not easy to find non trivial elements in this group.

2.3
The Cycle
Z_{QR,P}
on on
J(C)

In this section we construct a motivic cohomology cycle on
J(C)
, where
C
is a smooth projective curve over
{\mathds{C}}
. This was first constructed by Bloch
[
Blo00
]
in the case when
C
is the modular curve
X_{0}(37)
. The cycle is similar, in fact, generalises, the cycle constructed by Collino
[
Col97
]
. This section generalises the work of Colombo
[
Col02
]
on constructing the extension corresponding to the Collino cycle and hence many of the arguments are adapted from her paper.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve defined over
{\mathds{C}}
. Let
Q
and
R
be two distinct points on
C
such that there is a function
f=f_{QR}
with divisor

\operatorname{div}(f_{QR})=NQ-NR

for some
N\in{\mathds{N}}
. To determine the function precisely, we choose a distinct third point
P
and assume
f_{QR}(P)=1
.

There exist notable examples of curves where such functions can easily be found. For instance,
modular curves
with
Q
and
R
being cusps,
Fermat curves
with the two points being among the ‘trivial’ solutions of Fermat’s Last Theorem, namely the points with one of the coordinates being
0
, and
hyperelliptic curves
with the two points being Weierstrass points.

Let
C^{Q}
denote the image of
C
under the map
C\rightarrow J(C)
given by
x\rightarrow x-Q
. Similarly, let
{}_{R}C
denote the image of
C
under the map
x\rightarrow R-x
and let
f^{Q}
and
{}_{R}f
denote the function
f
being considered as a function on
C^{Q}
and
{}_{R}C
respectively.

Consider the cycle in
J(C)
given by

Z_{QR,P}=(C^{Q},f^{Q})+(_{R}C,_{R}f)

we have

\operatorname{div}_{C^{Q}}(f^{Q})+\operatorname{div}_{{}_{R}C}(_{R}f)=N(0)-N(R-Q)+N(R-Q)-N(0)=0.

Hence the cycle
Z_{QR,P}
gives an element of
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(J(C),{\mathds{Z}}(g))
.

2.4
Regulators

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
g
over
{\mathds{C}}
. The regulator map of Beilinson is a map from the motivic cohomology group to the Deligne cohomology group.

\operatorname{reg}_{{\mathds{Z}}}:H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))\rightarrow H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{D}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))=\frac{(F^{1}H^{2}(X,{\mathds{C}}))^{*}}{H_{2}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(1))}.

where
*
denotes the
{\mathds{C}}
-linear dual and
F^{\bullet}
denotes the Hodge filtration. The group
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{D}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))
is a generalised torus.

The map is defined as follows
[
Col97
]
: Let
Z=\sum_{i}(C_{i},f_{i})
be a cycle in
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(g))
, so
C_{i}
are curves on
X
and
f_{i}
are functions on them satisfying the cocycle condition. Let
[0,\infty]
denote the positive real axis in
{\mathds{P}}^{1}
and
\gamma_{i}=f_{i}^{-1}([0,\infty])
. Then
\sum_{i}\operatorname{div}(f_{i})=0
implies that the
1
-chain
\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}
is closed and in fact torsion. If
H_{1}(X,{\mathds{Z}})
has no torsion – as is in the case of the Jacobian of curves, it is exact. Assuming that, we have

\sum_{i}\gamma_{i}=\partial(D)

for some
2
-chain
D
. For a closed
2
-form
\omega
whose cohomology class lies in
F^{1}H_{DR}^{2}(X,{\mathds{C}})
,

\operatorname{reg}_{{\mathds{Z}}}(Z)(\omega):=\sum_{i}\int_{C_{i}\setminus\gamma_{i}}\log(f_{i})\omega+2\pi i\int_{D}\omega.

(1)

Here
C_{i}\setminus\gamma_{i}
is the Riemann surface with boundary obtained as follows. Let
n_{\epsilon}(\gamma_{i})
be an open tubular neighbourhood of
\gamma_{i}
in
C_{i}
which is homeomorphic to
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\times\gamma_{i}
.
C_{i}\backslash n_{\epsilon}(\gamma_{i})
is a closed subset of
C_{i}
with the structure of a manifold with boundary. The boundary
\partial(C_{i}\setminus n_{\epsilon}(\gamma_{i}))
is made up of two copies of
\gamma_{i}
with opposite orientation as well as
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\times\gamma_{i}(0)

and
(-\epsilon,\epsilon)\times\gamma_{i}(1)
with opposite orientations.
C_{i}\setminus\gamma_{i}
is the manifold obtained by letting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
.
For a decomposable element
(C,a)
, where
a\in{\mathds{C}}^{*}
, the regulator is particularly simple:

\operatorname{reg}_{{\mathds{Z}}}((C,a))(\omega)=\int_{C}\log(a)\omega=\log(a)\int_{C}\omega.

\operatorname{reg}_{{\mathds{Z}}}(Z_{QR,P})(\omega)=\int_{C^{Q}\setminus\gamma^{Q}}\log(f_{Q})\omega+\int_{{}_{R}C\setminus_{R}\gamma}\log(_{R}f)\omega+2\pi i\int_{D}\omega.

3
Extensions

As stated in the introduction, conjecturally, there is a canonical description of the motivic cohomology group as an extension in the category of mixed motives. From now on,
\operatorname{Ext}
will denote
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}
. Further, we will use
H^{*}(X)
to denote the group
H^{*}(X({\mathds{C}}),{\mathds{Z}})
, the singular (Betti) cohomology group with integral coefficients and
H^{*}(X)_{A}
to denote
H^{*}(X)\otimes_{{\mathds{Z}}}A
, where
A
is typically
{\mathds{Q}},{\mathds{R}}
or
{\mathds{C}}
.

In our case, if one has a suitable category of mixed motives over
{\mathds{Q}}
,
{\mathcal{MM}}_{{\mathds{Q}}}
, one expects for a variety
X

[
Sch93
]
and
i,n
non-negative numbers with
i<2n-1
,

H^{i+1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X,{\mathds{Q}}(n))\simeq\operatorname{Ext}_{{\mathcal{MM}}_{{\mathds{Q}}}}({\mathds{Q}}(-n),h^{i}(X)).

(3)

where
{\mathds{Q}}(-n)
denotes the twist of the Tate motive and
h^{i}(X)
denotes the motive whose Hodge realisation is
H^{i}(X)
.

One
knows
that the Deligne cohomology can be considered as an extension in the category of integral mixed Hodge structures,

H^{i+1}_{{\mathcal{D}}}(X,{\mathds{Z}}(n))\simeq\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}({\mathds{Z}}(-n),H^{i}(X))

Assuming (
3
) holds at the level of integer coefficients, the regulator map above then has a canonical description as the map induced by the realisation map from the category of mixed motives to the category of mixed Hodge structures,

\operatorname{Ext}_{{\mathcal{MM}}_{{\mathds{Q}}}}({\mathds{Z}}(-n),h^{i}(X))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{reg}_{{\mathds{Z}}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}({\mathds{Z}}(-n),H^{i}(X)).

3.1
Extensions of Mixed Hodge structures coming from the Fundamental Group

The key point of this paper is that, in some cases, one can also obtain extensions of mixed Hodge structures in other ways. For instance, if
(X,P)
is a pointed algebraic variety, it was shown by Hain
[
Hai87
]
that the graded quotients
J_{P}^{a}/J_{P}^{b}
, with
a\leq b
, where
J_{P}
is the augmentation ideal of the group ring of the fundamental group
{\mathds{Z}}[\pi_{1}(X,P)]
, carry mixed Hodge structures. Hence natural exact sequences involving them lead to extensions of mixed Hodge structures.

Our aim is to first construct some natural motivic cohomology cycles in the case when
X=J(C)
, the Jacobian of a curve of genus
g=g_{C}
. Their regulators will give rise to extensions of mixed Hodge structures. We will show that there are natural extensions of mixed Hodge structures coming from the Hodge structure on the graded pieces of
{\mathds{Z}}[\pi_{1}(C,P)]
for some suitable point
P
which give the
same
extensions. In particular, since the constructions can be carried out in at the level of mixed motives, if we had a good category of mixed motives the cycle
itself
would be an extension in the conjectured category of mixed motives coming from the fundamental group.

3.2
The Mixed Hodge structure on the Fundamental Group

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over
{\mathds{C}}
and
P
,
Q
and
R
be three distinct points on
C
.
Consider the open curve
C_{Q}=C-\{Q\}
. Let
{\mathds{Z}}[\pi_{1}(C_{Q},P)]
be the group ring of the fundamental group of
C_{Q}
based at
P
. Let
J_{Q,P}:=J_{C_{Q},P}
denote the augmentation ideal

J_{Q,P}:=J_{C_{Q},P}=\operatorname{Ker}\{{\mathds{Z}}[\pi_{1}(C_{Q},P)]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\operatorname{deg}}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathds{Z}}\}.

Let
H^{0}({\mathcal{B}}_{r}(C_{Q};P))
denote the
F
-vector space, where
F
is
{\mathds{R}}
or
{\mathds{C}}
, of homotopy invariant iterated integrals of length
\leq r
. Chen
[
Che77
]
showed that

H^{0}({\mathcal{B}}_{r}(C_{Q};P))\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathds{Z}}}({\mathds{Z}}[\pi_{1}(C_{Q},P)]/J_{C_{Q}.P}^{r+1},F)

under the map

I\longrightarrow I(\gamma)=\int_{\gamma}I.

Using this Hain
[
Hai87
]
was able to put a natural mixed Hodge structure on the graded pieces
J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{r}
.

3.3
The Extension
E^{3}_{Q,P}

From this point on, we will use the following notation. For an extension
E
of mixed Hodge structures,

E:0\longrightarrow B\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow A\longrightarrow 0

we use
m
to denote its class in
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{MHS}(A,B)
and
H
to denote the middle term. We will also use the notation
N\cdot E
to denote
N
times the extension with respect to the Baer sum,
use
N\cdot m
to denote its class of this extension in the
\operatorname{Ext}
group and
N\cdot H
to denote its middle term .

For
r\geq 3
, one can consider the extensions of mixed Hodge structures

E^{r}_{Q,P}:0\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{r-1})^{*}\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{r})^{*}\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}^{r-1}/J_{Q,P}^{r})^{*}\longrightarrow 0

where for a module
M
,
M^{*}=\operatorname{Hom}(M,{\mathds{Z}})
.

The simplest non-trivial case is when
r=3
. In this case
(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{2})^{*}\simeq H^{1}(C_{Q})\simeq H^{1}(C)
and
(J_{Q,P}^{2}/J_{Q,P}^{3})^{*}\simeq\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)
and the exact sequence becomes

E^{3}_{Q,P}:0\longrightarrow H^{1}(C)\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{3})^{*}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)\longrightarrow 0.

Hence
E^{3}_{Q,P}
gives an element
m^{3}_{Q,P}
in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))
. A similar construction with
R
in the place of
Q
gives us the extension
E^{3}_{R,P}
, which also lies in the same
\operatorname{Ext}
group.

There is a surjection
\cup:\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)\longrightarrow H^{2}(C)\simeq{\mathds{Z}}(-1)
coming from the cup product. Let
K
be the kernel of this map. The exact sequence of Hodge structures

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cup}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathds{Z}}(-1)\longrightarrow 0

splits over
{\mathds{Q}}
but
not
over
{\mathds{Z}}
. This happens as follows: There is a bilinear form
[
Kae01
]

b:\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)\times\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)\longrightarrow{\mathds{Z}}

defined by

b(x_{1}\otimes x_{2},y_{1}\otimes y_{2})=(x_{1}\cup y_{2})\cdot(x_{2}\cup y_{1}).

Let
S
denote the orthogonal complement of
K
in
\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)
with respect to this bilinear form. Then, under the cup product
S
projects to
2g_{C}{\mathds{Z}}(-1)
where
g_{C}
is the genus of
C
and

\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)_{{\mathds{Q}}}=K_{{\mathds{Q}}}\oplus S_{{\mathds{Q}}}.

Let
\bar{m}^{3}_{Q,P}
denote the class in
\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}(S,H^{1}(C))
corresponding to the extension

0\longrightarrow H^{1}(C)\longrightarrow\bar{E}^{3}_{Q,P}\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow 0

obtained by restricting
E^{3}_{Q,P}
to the extension of
S
by
H^{1}(C)
. From Kaenders
[
Kae01
]
one knows there is a covering map of complex tori,

\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Ext}(K\oplus S,H^{1}(C))=\operatorname{Ext}(K,H^{1}(C))\times\operatorname{Ext}(S,H^{1}(C)).

It is well known that
\operatorname{Ext}(S,H^{1}(C))=\operatorname{Ext}({\mathds{Z}}(-1),H^{1}(C))\simeq\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)
. To understand the other term, from the work of
Hain
[
Hai87
]
, Pulte
[
Pul88
]
, Kaenders
[
Kae01
]
and Rabi
[
Rab01
]
one has the following theorem

Theorem 3.1
.

The image of the class
m^{3}_{Q,P}
of
E^{3}_{Q,P}
in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))
is given by

\phi(m^{3}_{Q,P})=(m^{3}_{P},\bar{m}^{3}_{Q,P})

where
m^{3}_{P}\in\operatorname{Ext}(K,H^{1}(C))
depends only on
P
and
\bar{m}^{3}_{Q,P}
is given by

2g_{C}Q-2P-\kappa_{C}\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)

where
\kappa_{C}
is the canonical divisor of
C
and
g_{C}
is the genus of
C
.

Recall that in the group
\operatorname{Ext}
, addition is given by the Baer sum. We will denote this by
\oplus_{B}
(or
\ominus_{B}
if we are taking differences). Let
m^{3}_{QR,P}
denote the Baer difference
m^{3}_{Q,P}\ominus_{B}m^{3}_{R,P}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Under the hypothesis that there is a function with divisor
\operatorname{div}(f_{QR})=NQ-NR
the extension class
m^{3}_{QR,P}
is torsion in
\operatorname{Ext}(H^{1}C)\otimes H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))
. Precisely,

N\cdot H^{3}_{QR,P}\simeq H^{1}(C)\bigoplus\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)

where by
N\cdot H^{3}_{QR,P}
we mean the middle term of the exact sequence obtained by adding the sequence
E^{3}_{QR,P}
to itself
N
-times using the Baer sum.

Proof.

This follows from
[
Kae01
]
[Theorem 2.5] which states that the map

\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}(H^{1}(C)\otimes H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))

given by

Q-R\longrightarrow m^{3}_{Q,P}-m^{3}_{R,P}

is well defined and injective. Hence, since
N(Q-R)=0
in
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(C)
,
N(m^{3}_{Q,P}-m^{3}_{R,P})=N(m^{3}_{QR,P})=0
in
\operatorname{Ext}(H^{1}(C)\otimes H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))
.
∎

A consequence of this is that there is a morphism of integral mixed Hodge structures

r_{3}:N\cdot H^{3}_{QR,P}\longrightarrow H^{1}(C)

given by the projection.

Remark 3.3
.

This extension represents the class
Q-R
, at least up to a integral multiple, and is hence the first example of the theme of this paper - namely the Abel-Jacobi image of a null-homologous cycle is described in terms of extensions coming from the fundamental group.

∎

3.4
The Extensions
E^{4}_{Q,P}
and
E^{4}_{R,P}

From the work of Hain, Pulte, Harris and others one knows that the class
m^{3}_{P}
in
\operatorname{Ext}(K,H^{1}(C))
corresponds to the extension of mixed Hodge structures determined by the Ceresa cycle in
J(C)
, or alternately, the modified diagonal cycle in
C^{3}
. We would like to construct a similar class corresponding to the motivic cohomology cycle
Z_{QR,P}
. To that end, we now consider, with
C,P,Q
and
R
as before, the extension corresponding to
r=4

E_{Q,P}^{4}:0\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{3})^{*}\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{4})^{*}\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}^{3}/J_{Q,P}^{4})^{*}\longrightarrow 0.

We have that
(J_{Q,P}^{3}/J_{Q,P}^{4})^{*}\simeq\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C)
and this does not depend on
P,Q
or
R
. However, from Theorem
3.1
,
(J_{Q.P}/J_{Q,P}^{3})^{*}
depends on
Q
and
P
. Similarly
(J_{R.P}/J_{R,P}^{3})^{*}
depends on
R
and
P
. Hence we get classes in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C),(J_{Q.P}/J_{Q,P}^{3})^{*})
and
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C),(J_{R.P}/J_{R,P}^{3})^{*})
– which are different groups - hence we cannot take their difference.

When
C
is hyperelliptic the extension classes
m^{3}_{Q,P}
and
m^{3}_{R,P}
are 2-torsion in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))
. Hence one gets two classes

2m^{4}_{Q,P},2m^{4}_{R,P}\in\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C),\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)\oplus H^{1}(C))

and one can project to get two classes
e^{4}_{Q,P}
and
e^{4}_{R,P}
in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))
. Colombo
[
Col02
]
shows that the class

e^{4}_{QR,P}=e^{4}_{Q,P}\ominus_{B}e^{4}_{R,P}\in\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))

corresponds to the extension determined by the cycle
Z_{QR}
— after pulling back and pushing forward with some standard maps.

Unfortunately, in general the extension classes
m^{3}_{Q,P}
and
m^{3}_{R,P}
are
not
torsion in the
\operatorname{Ext}
group. They correspond to the instances where the Ceresa cycle is non-torsion — which is the generic case. In fact, the instances where it is known that the cycles are non-torsion are precisely the cases we have in mind — modular curves and Fermat curves
[
Har83
]
,
[
Blo84
]
. Hence we cannot use this argument immediately. However, since we know from Lemma
3.2
that their difference
m^{3}_{QR,P}
is torsion, we would like to get an extension of the form

0\longrightarrow H^{3}_{QR,P}\otimes{\mathds{Q}}\longrightarrow``H^{4}_{QR,P}"\otimes{\mathds{Q}}\longrightarrow\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C)\otimes{\mathds{Q}}\longrightarrow 0

where
``H^{4}_{QR,P}"
is the middle term of a sort of generalised Baer difference of the two extensions
E^{4}_{Q,P}
and
E^{4}_{R,P}
. We could then push-forward this extension using the splitting to get a class in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C)_{{\mathds{Q}}},H^{1}(C)_{{\mathds{Q}}})
. We cannot simply consider
E^{4}_{QR,P}=E^{4}_{Q,P}\ominus_{B}E^{4}_{R,P}
as the two extensions lie in different
\operatorname{Ext}
groups. So we have to consider a generalisation of Baer sums to not necessarily exact sequences which we came across in a paper of Rabi
[
Rab01
]
.

3.5
The Baer sum

This is well known but we recall it to fix notation in order to describe Rabi’s work. Recall that if we have two exact sequences of modules

\begin{CD}E_{j}:0@>{}>{}>A@>{f_{j}}>{}>B_{j}@>{p_{j}}>{}>C@>{}>{}>0\end{CD}

for
j\in\{1,2\}
, the Baer difference
E_{1}\ominus_{B}E_{2}
is constructed as follows. We have

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>A\oplus A@>{f_{1}\oplus f_{2}}>{}>B_{1}\oplus B_{2}@>{p_{1}\oplus p_{2}}>{}>C\oplus C@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

Let
\psi:B_{1}\oplus B_{2}\longrightarrow C
be the map

\psi(b_{1},b_{2})=p_{1}(b_{1})-p_{2}(b_{2})

and let
H=\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)=\{(b_{1},b_{2})|\;p_{1}(b_{1})=p_{2}(b_{2})\}
. Let
D
be the image of
\tilde{f}:A\longrightarrow A\oplus A\longrightarrow H

\tilde{f}(a)=(f_{1}(a),f_{2}(a))

Let
B=H/D
. The map
f:A\oplus A\longrightarrow B
given by

f(a_{1},a_{2})=(f_{1}(a_{1}),f_{2}(a_{2}))

factors through
(A\oplus A)/A\simeq A
and so one has a map
\bar{f}:A\longrightarrow B
,

a\longrightarrow(f_{1}(a),0)=(0,-f_{2}(a))

and an exact sequence

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>A@>{\bar{f}}>{}>B@>{p_{1}(or\;p_{2})}>{}>C@>{}>{}>0\end{CD}

The class of this exact sequence in
\operatorname{Ext}(C,A)
is the Baer difference
E_{1}\ominus_{B}E_{2}
. The Baer sum
E_{1}\oplus_{B}E_{2}
is the sequence obtained when one of the maps
f_{2}
or
p_{2}
is replaced by its negative. The Baer sum is essentially the push-out over
A
in the category of modules.

3.6
Rabi’s generalisation

Now suppose we have diagrams of the following type:

\begin{CD}&&0\\
&&@V{}V{}V\\
&&A_{1}\\
&&@V{}V{i_{j}}V\\
0@>{}>{}>B_{1}^{j}@>{f_{j}}>{}>B_{2}^{j}@>{p_{j}}>{}>B_{3}@>{}>{}>0\\
&&@V{}V{\pi_{j}}V\\
&&C_{1}\\
&&@V{}V{}V\\
&&0\end{CD}

where the vertical and horizontal sequences are exact for
j\in\{1,2\}
. Let
E_{j}
denote the horizontal exact sequences:

\begin{CD}E_{j}:0@>{}>{}>B_{1}^{j}@>{f_{j}}>{}>B_{2}^{j}@>{p_{j}}>{}>B_{3}@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

We would like to take the Baer difference of the
E_{j}
— but since they do not lie in the same
\operatorname{Ext}
group we cannot quite do that. However, we can still salvage something.

One gets two types of extension classes in
\operatorname{Ext}
groups which do not depend on
j
. The vertical exact sequences give classes in
\operatorname{Ext}(C_{1},A_{1})
. We can form their Baer difference to get an exact sequence

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>A_{1}@>{}>{}>{\mathbf{B}}_{1}@>{}>{}>C_{1}@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

The horizontal exact sequences give extensions in
\operatorname{Ext}(B_{3},B_{1}^{j})
. These depend on
j
but their push forward under
\pi_{j}
give classes
{\mathbf{f}}_{B_{2}^{j}}
in
\operatorname{Ext}(B_{3},C_{1})
.

Define
{\mathbf{B}}_{2}
as follows: Let
H_{2}=\operatorname{Ker}(\psi)
, where
\psi
is the ‘difference’ map

\psi:B_{2}^{1}\oplus B_{2}^{2}\longrightarrow B_{3}

\psi((b_{2}^{1},b_{2}^{2}))=(p_{1}(b_{2}^{1})-p_{2}(b_{2}^{2}))

Let
D_{2}
be the image of the map

A_{1}\longrightarrow B_{1}^{1}\oplus B_{1}^{2}\longrightarrow H_{2}

a\longrightarrow(f_{1}(i_{1}(a)),f_{2}(i_{2}(a)))

Define
{\mathbf{B}}_{2}=H_{2}/D_{2}
. We call this the
generalised
Baer difference of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
and denote it by
\tilde{\ominus}_{B}
. Observe that this is almost the Baer difference of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
in the sense that if
B_{1}=B_{1}^{1}=B_{1}^{2}
, then we could take the difference in
\operatorname{Ext}(B_{3},B_{1})
. Since that is not the case, we do the best we can — we take the difference of the
inexact
sequences

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>A_{1}@>{}>{}>B_{2}^{j}@>{}>{}>B_{3}@>{}>{}>0.\end{CD}

As a result of this one has a complex

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>{\mathbf{B}}_{1}@>{f_{1}\oplus f_{2}}>{}>{\mathbf{B}}_{2}@>{p_{1}(or\;p_{2})}>{}>B_{3}@>{}>{}>0\end{CD}

However, this complex is
not
exact since
\operatorname{Ker}(p_{1})
is larger than
(f_{1}\oplus f_{2})({\mathbf{B}}_{1})
. The next lemma describes this difference.

Lemma 3.4

(Rabi
[
Rab01
]
)
.

Let
{\mathbf{F}}={\mathbf{F}}_{B^{1}_{2}\tilde{\ominus}_{B}B^{2}_{2}}={\mathbf{B}}_{2}/{\mathbf{B}}_{1}
. Then one has the following diagram, in which the horizontal and vertical sequences are exact.

\begin{CD}&&&&&&0\\
&&&&&&@V{}V{}V\\
&&&&&&C_{1}\\
&&&&&&@V{}V{\phi}V\\
0@>{}>{}>{\mathbf{B}}_{1}@>{f}>{}>{\mathbf{B}}_{2}@>{\eta}>{}>{\mathbf{F}}@>{}>{}>0\\
&&&&&&@V{}V{\bar{p}}V\\
&&&&&&B_{3}\\
&&&&&&@V{}V{}V\\
&&&&&&0\end{CD}

Proof.

[
Rab96
]
, Appendix B. We repeat the proof here as that is unpublished. The horizontal sequence is exact by definition. To show the vertical sequence is exact we have to first describe be map
\phi
.
It is defined as follows. One has maps
\pi_{j}:B_{1}^{j}\longrightarrow C_{1}
. Consider the natural map

\begin{CD}\tilde{\phi}:C_{1}\oplus C_{1}@>{}>{}>(B_{1}^{1}\oplus B_{1}^{2})/\Delta_{A_{1}}@>{(f_{1},f_{2})}>{}>{\mathbf{B}}_{2}=H_{2}/D_{2}\end{CD}

\tilde{\phi}(c_{1},c_{2})\rightarrow(\pi_{1}^{-1}(c),\pi_{2}^{-1}(c))\rightarrow(f_{1}(\pi_{1}^{-1}(c_{1})),f_{2}(\pi_{2}^{-1}(c_{2})))

where
\Delta_{A_{1}}=\{(i_{1}(a),i_{2}(a))|a\in A_{1}\}
.
\phi
gives a well defined map

(C_{1}\oplus C_{1})/\Delta_{C_{1}}\longrightarrow{\mathbf{B}}_{2}/\tilde{\phi}(\Delta_{C_{1}})

where
\Delta_{C_{1}}=\{(c,-c)|c\in C_{1}\}
is the anti-diagonal. This is well defined as if
(b_{1},b_{2})
and
(b^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{2})
are in
(\pi_{1}^{-1}(c_{1}),\pi_{2}^{-1}(c_{2}))
we have to show

(f_{1}(b_{1}),f_{2}(b_{2}))\equiv(f_{1}(b^{\prime}_{1}),f_{2}(b^{\prime}_{2}))\operatorname{mod}\tilde{\phi}(\Delta_{C_{1}})

or

(f_{1}(b_{1}-b^{\prime}_{1}),f_{2}(b_{2}-b^{\prime}_{2}))\in\tilde{\phi}(\Delta_{C_{1}}).

From exactness, we have
b_{1}-b^{\prime}_{1}=i_{1}(a_{1})
and
b_{2}-b^{\prime}_{2}=i_{2}(a_{2})
with
a_{i}\in A_{1}
. The image of
\Delta_{C_{1}}
under
(\pi_{1}^{-1},\pi_{2}^{-1})
consists of
(b,b^{\prime})
such that
\pi_{1}(b)=\pi_{2}(b^{\prime})
.
(i_{1}(a_{1}),i_{2}(a_{2}))
lie in this image, hence

(f_{1}(i_{1}(a_{1})),f_{2}(i_{2}(a_{2})))=(f_{1}(b_{1}-b^{\prime}_{1}),f_{2}(b_{2}-b^{\prime}_{2}))\in\tilde{\phi}(\Delta_{C_{1}}).

Note that the pre-image
(\pi_{1}^{-1},\pi_{2}^{-1})(\Delta_{C_{1}})
in
B_{1}^{1}\oplus B_{1}^{2})/\Delta_{A_{1}}
is the Baer difference
{\mathbf{B}}_{1}
. Further,
(C_{1}\oplus C_{1})/\Delta_{C_{1}})\simeq C_{1}
. Hence one has a map
\phi:C_{1}\rightarrow{\mathbf{F}}={\mathbf{B}}_{2}/{\mathbf{B}}_{1}
and we get a exact sequence

0\longrightarrow C_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbf{F}}_{B^{1}_{2}\tilde{\ominus}_{B}B^{2}_{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{p}}}{{\longrightarrow}}B_{3}\longrightarrow 0

This sequence is exact as if
b=(b^{1}_{2},b^{2}_{2})
is in
{\mathbf{F}}_{B^{1}_{2}\tilde{\ominus}_{B}B^{2}_{2}}
and
\bar{p}(b)=0
, then
p_{1}(b^{1}_{2})=p_{2}(b^{2}_{2})=0
. So
b^{1}_{2}
and
b^{2}_{2}
lie in the image of
B_{1}^{1}\oplus B_{1}^{2}
— say
b_{2}^{1}=f_{1}(b_{1}^{1})
and
b_{2}^{2}=f_{2}(b_{1}^{2})
. Let
c_{i}=\pi_{1}(b_{1}^{1})
and
c_{2}=\pi_{2}(b_{1}^{2})
. Then

b=\phi(c_{1},c_{2})

so it lies in the image of
\phi
.

∎

In general, for any
{\mathds{Z}}
-linear combination
m\cdot B^{1}_{2}\tilde{\ominus}_{B}\;n\cdot B^{2}_{2}
of
B_{2}^{1}
and
B_{2}^{2}
we get an extension class
{\mathbf{f}}_{m\cdot B^{1}_{2}\tilde{\ominus}_{B}\;n\cdot B^{2}_{2}}
in
\operatorname{Ext}(B_{3},C_{1})
corresponding to
{\mathbf{F}}_{m\cdot B^{1}_{2}\tilde{\ominus}_{B}\;n\cdot B^{2}_{2}}
. The relation between this and the extension classes constructed above is given as follows:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}_{B_{2}^{j}}
and
{\mathbf{f}}_{m\cdot B_{2}^{1}\tilde{\ominus}_{B}\;n\cdot B^{2}_{2}}
be the extensions in
\operatorname{Ext}(B_{3},C_{1})
described above. Then,

{\mathbf{f}}_{m\cdot B_{2}^{1}\tilde{\ominus}_{B}\;n\cdot B^{2}_{2}}=m\cdot{\mathbf{f}}_{B_{2}^{1}}\ominus_{B}\;n\cdot{\mathbf{f}}_{B_{2}^{2}}.

Proof.

This follows from the construction of the map
\phi
.
∎

In the next section we apply these constructions in our particular case to get the extension class we want.

3.7
The Extension
e^{4}_{QR,P}

In this section we construct an extension
e^{4}_{QR,P}
in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C),H^{1}(C))
which generalises the element
e^{4}_{Q,P}\ominus_{B}e^{4}_{R,P}
constructed by Colombo. Recall that we have an exact sequence

E^{3}_{Q,P}:0\longrightarrow H^{1}(C)\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{3})^{*}\longrightarrow\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)\longrightarrow 0

and a similar one
E^{3}_{R,P}
. Also, we have the exact sequence

E_{Q,P}^{4}:0\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{3})^{*}\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{4})^{*}\longrightarrow(J_{Q,P}^{3}/J_{Q,P}^{4})^{*}\longrightarrow 0

and a similar
E^{4}_{R,P}
. This gives us diagrams as in Lemma
3.4
, with
B_{1}^{1}=(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{3})^{*}
,
B_{1}^{2}=(J_{R,P}/J_{R,P}^{3})^{*}
,
B_{2}^{1}=(J_{Q,P}/J_{Q,P}^{4})^{*}
,
B_{2}^{2}=(J_{R,P}/J_{R,P}^{4})^{*}
and
A_{1}=H^{1}(C)
,
B_{3}=\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)
and finally
C_{1}=\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)
.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}_{Q}
,
{\mathbf{f}}_{R}
and
{\mathbf{f}}_{QR}
denote the classes in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C),\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C))
with middle terms
H^{23}_{Q,P},H^{23}_{R,P}
and
H^{23}_{QR,P}
corresponding to the diagrams for
Q
,
R
and their generalised Baer difference.
{\mathbf{f}}_{Q}
and
{\mathbf{f}}_{R}
are the push-forwards of
m^{4}_{Q,P}
and
m^{4}_{R,P}
respectively. From Corollary
3.5
one has

{\mathbf{f}}_{QR}={\mathbf{f}}_{Q}-{\mathbf{f}}_{R}.

Lemma 3.6
.

{\mathbf{f}}_{QR}
is
N
-torsion in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C),\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C))
. Namely,

N\cdot H^{23}_{QR,P}=\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C)\oplus\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C).

Proof.

From Rabi
[
Rab01
]
, Corollary 3.3, one has that the class
H^{23}_{Q,P}
in
\operatorname{Ext}(\otimes^{3}H^{1}(C),\otimes^{2}H^{1}(C))
is given by

H^{23}_{Q,P}=H^{1}(C)\otimes E^{3}_{QP}\oplus_{B}E^{3}_{QP}\otimes H^{1}(C).

Similarly,

4
Carlson’s Representatives

The proof of the main theorem will follow by showing that both the algebraic cycle constructed earlier and the extension
class constructed above induce the same current. For that we have to understand the how an extension class induces a current. This comes from understanding the Carlson representative. In the section we once again follow Colombo
[
Col02
]
and adapt her arguments to our situation.

If
V
is a MHS all of whose weights are negative, then the
Intermediate Jacobian of
V
is defined to be

J_{0}(V)=\frac{V_{{\mathds{C}}}}{F^{0}V_{{\mathds{C}}}\oplus V_{{\mathds{Z}}}}.

This is a generalised torus - namely a group of the form
{\mathds{C}}^{a}/{\mathds{Z}}^{b}\simeq({\mathds{C}}^{*})^{b}\times({\mathds{C}})^{a-b}
for some
a
and
b
.

An extension of mixed Hodge structures

is called

if the lowest non-zero weight of
B
is greater than the largest non-zero weight of
A
. This implies that
\operatorname{Hom}_{MHS}(B,A)
has negative weights. Carlson
[
Car80
]
showed that

\operatorname{Ext}_{MHS}(B,A)\simeq J_{0}(\operatorname{Hom}(B,A)).

This is defined as follows. As an extension of
Abelian groups
, the extension splits. So one has a map
r_{{\mathds{Z}}}:H\rightarrow A
which is a retraction,
r_{{\mathds{Z}}}\circ\iota=id
. Let
s_{F}
be a section in
\operatorname{Hom}(B_{{\mathds{C}}},H_{{\mathds{C}}})
preserving the Hodge filtration. Then the Carlson representative of an extension is defined to be the class of

We now describe explicitly the Carlson representative of the extension
\epsilon^{4}_{QR,P}
constructed in the previous section. This is done in a few steps, first we describe the representative for

and then for its various pullbacks and push forwards to obtain that for

.

\int_{C_{QR}\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}}\eta_{\alpha^{\prime}}\wedge\Theta=\sum_{j}\int_{\alpha^{\prime j}}\Theta-2\pi i\int_{\gamma}\eta_{\alpha^{\prime}}\psi

We can make a similar argument for
\beta_{Q}
.

Combining the two we get

N\sum_{j}\int_{\alpha^{\prime j}}\Theta-m_{i}\sum_{s}\int_{\beta_{Q}^{s}}\Theta=\int_{C_{QR}\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}}\eta_{\alpha}\wedge\Theta+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}\eta_{\alpha}\psi

Finally, since
\eta_{\alpha}
is compactly supported in
C_{QR}
, we can replace
C_{QR}\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}
with
C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}
to get

N\sum_{j}\int_{\alpha^{\prime j}}\Theta-m_{i}\sum_{s}\int_{\beta_{Q}^{s}}\Theta=\int_{C\setminus\gamma}\eta_{\alpha}\wedge\Theta+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}\eta_{\alpha}\psi

To link this to the integral over
\tilde{\alpha}
we observe the following. The loop
\alpha^{\prime N}
lifts to a path in
\widetilde{\alpha^{\prime N}}
in
\tilde{C}
which is made up of copies of
\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}
. Let
\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}_{k}
denote the lift of the
k^{th}
copy of
\alpha^{\prime}
so
\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}_{k}(1)=\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}_{k+1}(0)
. We can choose the homeomorphisms between
\alpha^{\prime j}
and
\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime j}
such that the
k^{th}
copy of
\alpha^{\prime j}
is homeomorphic to a path
\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime j}_{k}
in
\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}_{k}
. So we have homeomorphisms

\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\bigcup_{j}\alpha^{\prime j}\simeq\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}\bigcup_{j}\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime j}_{k}\simeq\widetilde{\alpha^{\prime N}}-\partial(\tilde{C}\setminus q^{-1}({n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}))

A similar situation holds for
\beta_{Q}
. Via these homeomorphisms

N\sum_{j}\int_{\alpha^{\prime j}}\Theta-m_{i}\sum_{s}\int_{\beta_{Q}^{s}}\Theta=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{j}\int_{\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime j}_{k}}\tilde{\Theta}-\sum_{r=1}^{m_{i}}\sum_{s}\int_{\tilde{\beta}_{Q,r}^{s}}\tilde{\Theta}

which is

\int_{\widetilde{\alpha^{\prime N}\beta_{Q}^{-m_{i}}}-\partial(\tilde{C}\setminus q^{-1}({n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}))}\tilde{\Theta}=\int_{\tilde{\alpha}-\partial(\tilde{C}\setminus q^{-1}({n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}))}\tilde{\Theta}

Finally, as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
the set
\tilde{\alpha}\cap\partial(\tilde{C}\setminus q^{-1}({n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}))
becomes a set of measure
0
so we have

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\tilde{\alpha}-\partial(\tilde{C}\setminus q^{-1}({n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}))}\tilde{\Theta}=\int_{\tilde{\alpha}}\tilde{\Theta}

We have made a choice of a lifting
\tilde{\alpha}
of
\alpha
. A different choice of basepoint
\tilde{P}^{\prime}
would change the value of
\log(q^{*}(f))
by
2\pi iM
for some
M\in{\mathds{Z}}
. This would change the integral by
2\pi iM\int_{\alpha}\psi
. Hence this equality holds only up to
2\pi i\int_{\alpha}\psi{\mathds{Z}}
.

∎

We have the following useful corollary to the above proposition, which says that in fact, we can replace
\eta_{\alpha}
by any form on

which is cohomologous to

.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
f
,
\psi
, and
W(\psi)
be as above and
\phi_{\alpha}
a closed
1
form on
C
which is cohomologous in
H^{1}(C)
to
i_{*}([\eta_{\alpha}])
for some
\alpha
in
V
. Then

\int_{\alpha}\frac{df}{f}\psi+W(\psi)=\int_{C\setminus\gamma}\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\Theta+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}\phi_{\alpha}\psi\,\left(\operatorname{mod}\,2\pi i\int_{\alpha}\psi{\mathds{Z}}\right)

Proof.

Let
\phi_{\alpha}
denote such a form. Both
\phi_{\alpha}
and
\eta_{\alpha}
are Poincaré duals of the same homology class, so
\phi_{\alpha}-\eta_{\alpha}=dg
. Let
{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}
be as before. One has

\int_{C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}}\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\Theta=\int_{C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}}dg\wedge\Theta+\int_{C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}}\eta_{\alpha}\wedge\Theta

So it suffices to compute the two terms separately.

Any closed form
\phi
on
C
is compactly supported on the manifold with boundary
C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}
as
C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}
is a closed subset of
C
and
C
is compact. Further, by choice of
V
it will be cohomologous to
[i_{*}(\eta_{\alpha})]
for some
\alpha\in V
. In particular we know that
[c(i)\frac{dx_{\sigma(i)}}{N}]
is cohomologous to a Poincaré dual of
[\alpha_{i}]
.

Since both
\phi_{\alpha}
and
\eta_{\alpha}
are compactly supported on
C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}
, so is
dg
and hence
g
and
dg\wedge\Theta
are compactly supported as well. From Stokes’ Theorem we get

\int_{C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}}dg\wedge\Theta=\int_{C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}}d(g\Theta)=\int_{\partial(C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)})}g\Theta

If we choose a different function
h
such that
dh=dg
, then
h-g=c
for some constant
c
. Hence, from Lemma
4.7
we see

\int_{\partial(C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)})}g\Theta-\int_{\partial(C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)})}h\Theta=c\int_{\partial(C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)})}\Theta=\int_{C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}}d\Theta=0

so it does not depend on the choice of primitive.

An argument similar to Lemma
4.7
with
g\Theta
in the place of
\Theta
shows that

\int_{\partial(C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)})}g\Theta=-2\pi i\int_{\gamma}g\psi

Lemma
4.7
along with Lemma
2.1
(3) shows further that

\int_{C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)}}dg\wedge\Theta=\int_{\partial(C\setminus{n_{\epsilon}(\gamma)})}g\Theta=-2\pi i\int_{\gamma}g\psi=-2\pi i\int_{\gamma}dg\psi.

Taking the limit as
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
one gets

\int_{C\setminus\gamma}dg\wedge\Theta=-2\pi i\int_{\gamma}dg\psi.

From Proposition
4.6
we have

\int_{\alpha}\frac{df}{f}\psi+W(\psi)=\int_{C\setminus\gamma}\eta_{\alpha}\wedge\Theta+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}\eta_{\alpha}\psi\,\left(\operatorname{mod}\,2\pi i\int_{\alpha}\psi{\mathds{Z}}\right)

Since the integral of
dg\wedge\Theta
cancels the iterated integral term, we have

\int_{C\setminus\gamma}\phi_{\alpha}\wedge\Theta+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}\phi_{\alpha}\psi=\int_{C\setminus\gamma}\left(\eta_{\alpha}\wedge\Theta+dg\wedge\Theta\right)+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}\left(\eta_{\alpha}\psi+dg\psi\right)

=\int_{C\setminus\gamma}\eta_{\alpha}\wedge\Theta+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}\eta_{\alpha}\psi=\int_{\alpha}\frac{df}{f}\psi+W(\psi)\left(\operatorname{mod}\,2\pi i\int_{\alpha}\psi{\mathds{Z}}\right)

∎

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
Z_{QR,P}
be the element of
H^{2g-1}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({J}(C),{\mathds{Z}}(g))
and let
\eta
and
\omega
be two closed
1
forms on
C
with
\omega
holomorphic. Let
\alpha
be loop in
C_{QR}
based at
P
such that
[\alpha]\in V
and a Poincaré dual of
[\alpha]
is homologous to
\eta
in

. Then

\operatorname{reg}_{{\mathds{Z}}}(Z_{QR,P})(\eta\wedge\omega)\equiv 2(2g_{C}+1)N\int_{\alpha}\frac{df}{f}\omega.

Proof.

It suffices to check this for
\eta=dx_{j}
and
\omega=dz_{i}
. From Theorem
4.14
, one has

{\bf F}_{QR,P}(\Omega)(c(\sigma(j))\alpha_{\sigma(j)}\wedge\zeta_{i})\equiv(2g_{C}+1)N\left(2\int_{C\setminus\gamma}\log(f)dx_{j}\wedge dz_{i}+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}(dx_{j}dz_{i}-dz_{i}dx_{j})\right).

From Lemma
2.1
we have

\int_{\gamma}dx_{j}dz_{i}+\int_{\gamma}dz_{i}dx_{j}=\int_{\gamma}dx_{j}\int_{\gamma}dz_{i}

From Lemma
4.7
one has
\int_{\gamma}dz_{i}=0
. Hence

\int_{\gamma}dx_{j}dz_{i}=-\int_{\gamma}dz_{i}dx_{j}

Therefore, we can simplify the regulator expression to get

\operatorname{reg}_{{\mathds{Z}}}(Z_{QR,P})(dx_{j}\wedge dz_{i})=2(2g_{C}+1)N\left(\int_{C\setminus\gamma}\log(f)dx_{j}\wedge dz_{i}+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}dx_{j}dz_{i}\right)

=2(2g_{C}+1)N\left(\int_{C\setminus\gamma}dx_{j}\wedge\log(f)dz_{i}+2\pi i\int_{\gamma}dx_{j}dx_{i}\right)

Using Corollary
4.8

\operatorname{reg}_{{\mathds{Z}}}(Z_{QR,P})(dx_{j}\wedge dz_{i})=2(2g_{C}+1)c(j)N\int_{\alpha}\frac{df}{f}dz_{i}.

Tensor products of Drinfeld modules and convolutions of Goss L L -series
Wei-Cheng Huang

Abstract
. Following the same framework of the special value results of convolutions of Goss and Pellarin L L -series attached to Drinfeld modules that take values in Tate algebras by Papanikolas and the author, we establish special value results of convolutions of two Goss L L -series attached to Drinfeld modules that take values in 𝔽 q ​ ( ( 1 θ ) ) . {\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(\frac{1}{\theta})\!)}. Applying the class module formula of Fang to tensor products of two Drinfeld modules, we provide special value formulas for their L L -functions. By way of the theory of Schur polynomials these identities take the form of specializations of convolutions of Rankin-Selberg type. Finally, we show an explicit computation of the regulators appearing in Fang’s class module formula for tensor products as well as symmetric and alternating squares of Drinfeld modules.

2.1.4.
Twisted polynomials

Let
R
be any commutative
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra, and let
\tau:R\to R
be an injective
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra endomorphism. Let
R^{\tau}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subalgebra of
R
of elements fixed by
\tau
. For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
for which
\tau^{n}
is defined on
R
and a matrix
B=(b_{ij})
with entries in
R
, we let
B^{(n)}
be defined by twisting each entry. That is,
(b_{ij})^{(n)}=(b_{ij}^{(n)})
. For
\ell\geqslant 1
we let
\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\tau]=\Mat_{\ell}(R[\tau])
be the ring of
twisted polynomials
in
\tau
with coefficients in
\Mat_{\ell}(R)
, subject to the relation
\tau B=B^{(1)}\tau
for
B\in\Mat_{\ell}(R)
.
In this way,
R^{\ell}
is a left
\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\tau]
-module, where if
\beta=B_{0}+B_{1}\tau+\dots+B_{m}\tau^{m}\in\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\tau]
and
\mathbf{x}\in R^{\ell}
, then

(2.1.5)

\beta(\mathbf{x})=B_{0}\mathbf{x}+B_{1}\mathbf{x}^{(1)}+\cdots+B_{m}\mathbf{x}^{(m)}.

If furthermore
\tau
is an automorphism of
R
, then we set
\sigma\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\tau^{-1}
and form the twisted polynomial ring
\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\sigma]
, subject to
\sigma B=B^{(-1)}\sigma
for
B\in\Mat_{\ell}(R)
.
Then
R^{\ell}
is a left
\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\sigma]
-module, where for
\gamma=C_{0}+C_{1}\sigma+\dots+C_{m}\sigma^{m}\in\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\sigma]
and
\mathbf{x}\in R^{\ell}
,

(2.1.6)

\gamma(\mathbf{x})=C_{0}\mathbf{x}+C_{1}\mathbf{x}^{(-1)}+\cdots+C_{m}\mathbf{x}^{(-m)}.

For
\beta\in\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\tau]
(or
\gamma\in\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\sigma]
), we write
\partial\beta
(or
\partial\gamma
) for the constant term with respect to
\tau
(or
\sigma
). We have natural inclusions of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras,

\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\tau]\subseteq{\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\![\tau]\!]},\quad\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\sigma]\subseteq{\Mat_{\ell}(R)[\![\sigma]\!]},

into
twisted power series
rings, the latter when
\tau
is an automorphism.

2.1.7.
Ore anti-involution

We assume that
\tau:R\to R
is an automorphism, and recall the anti-isomorphism
*:R[\tau]\to R[\sigma]
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras originally defined by Ore
[
41
]
(see also [
[
31
]
, §1.7;
[
40
]
, §2.3;
[
44
]
]), given by

\biggl(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}b_{i}\tau^{i}\biggr)^{*}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}b_{i}^{(-i)}\sigma^{i}.

One verifies that
(\alpha\beta)^{*}=\beta^{*}\alpha^{*}
for
\alpha
,
\beta\in R[\tau]
. For
B=(\beta_{ij})\in\Mat_{k\times\ell}(R[\tau])
, we set

B^{*}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\bigl(\beta_{ij}^{*}\bigr)^{{\mathsf{T}}}\in\Mat_{\ell\times k}(R[\sigma]),

which then satisfies

(2.1.8)

(BC)^{*}=C^{*}B^{*}\in\Mat_{m\times k}(R[\sigma]),\quad B\in\Mat_{k\times\ell}(R[\tau]),\ C\in\Mat_{\ell\times m}(R[\tau]).

The inverse of
*:\Mat_{k\times\ell}(R[\tau])\to\Mat_{\ell\times k}(R[\sigma])
is also denoted by “
*
.”

2.1.9.
Orders of finite

F[x]
-modules

For
F[x]
a polynomial ring in one variable over a field
F
, we say that an
F[x]
-module is
finite
if it is finitely generated and torsion.
Now fix a finite
F[x]
-module
M
. Then there are monic polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{\ell}\in F[x]
so that

M\cong F[x]/(f_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus F[x]/(f_{\ell}).

We set
{[M]}_{F[x]}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=f_{1}\cdots f_{\ell}\in F[x]
, which is a generator of the Fitting ideal of
M
, and we call
{[M]}_{F[x]}
the
F[x]
-order
of
M
. If
m_{x}:M\to M
is left-multiplication by
x
, then

{[M]}_{F[x]}=\Char(m_{x},M,X)|_{X=x},

where
\Char(m_{x},M,X)\in F[X]
is the characteristic polynomial of
m_{x}
as an
F
-linear map.
For a variable
y
independent from
x
, but
M
still an
F[x]
-module, we will write

{[M]}_{F[y]}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}={[M]}_{F[x]}|_{x=y}=\Char(m_{x},M,y).

This will be of particular use for us when
M
is an
\mathsf{A}
-module (or
\mathbf{A}
-module), where

{[M]}_{A}={[M]}_{\mathsf{A}}|_{t=\theta}=\Char(m_{t},M,\theta)\in A,\ \textup{(or ${[M]}_{\mathbb{A}}={[M]}_{\mathbf{A}}|_{t=\theta}=\Char(m_{t},M,\theta)\in\mathbb{A}$),}

coercing
A
-orders and
\mathbb{A}
-orders to be elements of our scalar fields.

2.2.
Drinfeld modules, Anderson
t
-modules, and their adjoints

Given a field
F\supseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}
and an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra map
\iota:\mathsf{A}\to F
, we call
F
an
\mathsf{A}
-field
. The kernel of
\iota
is the
characteristic
of
F
, and if
\iota
is injective then the characteristic is
generic
. If
F\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
has generic characteristic, then we always assume that
\iota(t)=\theta
. Otherwise,
\iota(t)=\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\theta}\in F
.

2.2.1.
Drinfeld modules and Anderson
t
-modules

A
Drinfeld module
over
F
is defined by an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\phi:\mathsf{A}\to F[\tau]
such that

(2.2.2)

\phi_{t}=\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\theta}+\kappa_{1}\tau+\dots+\kappa_{r}\tau^{r},\quad\kappa_{r}\neq 0.

We say that
\phi
has
rank
r
. We then make
F
into an
\mathsf{A}
-module by setting

t\cdot x\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\phi_{t}(x)=\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\theta}x+\kappa_{1}x^{q}+\cdots+\kappa_{r}x^{q^{r}},\quad x\in F.

Similarly an
Anderson
t
-module of dimension
\ell
over
F
is defined by an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\psi:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{\ell}(F)[\tau]
such that

(2.2.3)

\psi_{t}=\partial\psi_{t}+E_{1}\tau+\dots+E_{w}\tau^{w},\quad E_{i}\in\Mat_{\ell}(F),

where
\partial\psi_{t}-\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\theta}\cdot\mathrm{I}_{\ell}
is nilpotent. A Drinfeld module is then a
t
-module of dimension
1
.
We write
\psi(F)
for
F^{\ell}
with the
\mathsf{A}
-module structure given by
a\cdot\mathbf{x}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\psi_{a}(\mathbf{x})
through (
2.1.5
). Similarly, we write
\Lie(\psi)(F)
for
F^{\ell}
with
F[t]
-module structure defined by
\partial\psi_{a}
for
a\in\mathsf{A}
. For
a\in\mathsf{A}
, the
a
-torsion submodule of
\psi(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5muF})
is denoted

\psi[a]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\smash{\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5muF}}^{\ell}\mid\psi_{a}(\mathbf{x})=0\}.

Given
t
-modules
\phi:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{k}(F)[\tau]
,
\psi:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{\ell}(F)[\tau]
, a morphism
\eta:\phi\to\psi
is a matrix
\eta\in\Mat_{\ell\times k}(F[\tau])
such that

\eta\phi_{a}=\psi_{a}\eta
for all
a\in\mathsf{A}
.
Moreover,
\eta
induces an
\mathsf{A}
-module homomorphism
\eta:\phi(F)\to\psi(F)
, and we have a functor
\psi\mapsto\psi(F)
from the category of
t
-modules to
\mathsf{A}
-modules. We also have an induced map of
F[t]
-modules,
\partial\psi:\Lie(\phi)(F)\to\Lie(\psi)(F)
.

Anderson defined
t
-modules in
[
2
]
, and following his language we sometimes abbreviate “Anderson
t
-module” by “
t
-module.” For more information about Drinfeld modules and
t
-modules see
[
31
,
50
]
.

2.2.4.
Exponential and logarithm series

Suppose now that
F\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
has generic characteristic and that
\psi
is defined over
F
. Then there is a twisted power series
\Exp_{\psi}\in{\Mat_{\ell}(F)[\![\tau]\!]}
, called the
exponential series
of
\psi
, such that

\Exp_{\psi}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}B_{i}\tau^{i},\quad B_{0}=\mathrm{I}_{\ell},\ B_{i}\in\Mat_{\ell}(F),

and for all
a\in\mathsf{A}
,
\Exp_{\psi}{}\cdot\partial\psi_{a}=\psi_{a}\cdot\Exp_{\psi}
.
This functional identity for
a=t
induces a recursive relation that uniquely determines
\Exp_{\psi}
. That the coefficient matrices have entries in
F
is due to Anderson
[
2
, Prop. 2.1.4, Lem. 2.1.6]
.
The exponential series induces an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear and entire function,

\Exp_{\psi}:\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\ell},\quad\Exp_{\psi}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}B_{i}\mathbf{z}^{(i)},\quad\mathbf{z}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{\ell})^{{\mathsf{T}}},

called the
exponential function
of
\psi
. That
\Exp_{\psi}
converges everywhere is equivalent to

\lim_{i\to\infty}{\lvert B_{i}\rvert}_{\infty}^{1/q^{i}}=0\Leftrightarrow\lim_{i\to\infty}\deg(B_{i})/q^{i}=-\infty.

We also identify the exponential function with the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear formal power series
\Exp_{\psi}(\mathbf{z})\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![\mathbf{z}]\!]}^{\ell}
.
The functional equation for
\Exp_{\psi}
induces the identities,

\Exp_{\psi}(\partial\psi_{a}\mathbf{z})=\psi_{a}\bigl(\Exp_{\psi}(\mathbf{z})\bigr),\quad\forall\,a\in\mathsf{A}.

The exponential function of
\psi
is always surjective for Drinfeld modules, but it may not be surjective when
\ell\geqslant 2
. We say that
\psi
is
uniformizable
if
\Exp_{\psi}:\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\ell}
is surjective. The kernel of
\Exp_{\psi}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\ell}
,

\Lambda_{\psi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\ker\Exp_{\psi},

is a finitely generated and discrete
\partial\psi(\mathsf{A})
-submodule of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\ell}
called the
period lattice
of
\psi
.
Thus if
\psi
is uniformizable, then we obtain an exact sequence of
\mathsf{A}
-modules,

0\to\Lambda_{\psi}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\ell}\xrightarrow{\Exp_{\psi}}\psi(\mathbb{C}_{\infty})\to 0.

As an element of
{\Mat_{\ell}(F)[\![\tau]\!]}
the series
\Exp_{\psi}
is invertible, and we let

\Log_{\psi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Exp_{\psi}^{-1}\in{\Mat_{\ell}(F)[\![\tau]\!]}

be the
logarithm series
of
\psi
, satisfying

\Log_{\psi}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}C_{i}\tau^{i},\quad C_{0}=\mathrm{I}_{\ell},\ C_{i}\in\Mat_{\ell}(F).

Together with the
logarithm function
,
\Log_{\psi}(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{i\geqslant 0}C_{i}\mathbf{z}^{(i)}\in{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\![\mathbf{z}]\!]}^{\ell}
, we have

\partial\psi_{a}\cdot\Log_{\psi}=\Log_{\psi}{}\cdot\psi_{a}
and
\partial\psi_{a}(\Log_{\psi}(\mathbf{z}))=\Log_{\psi}(\psi_{a}(\mathbf{z}))
, for all
a\in\mathsf{A}
.
In general
\Log_{\psi}(\mathbf{z})
converges only on an open polydisc in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{\ell}
. For example, if
\phi:\mathsf{A}\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]
is a Drinfeld module as in (
2.2.2
), then
\Log_{\phi}(z)
converges on the open disk of radius
R_{\phi}
, where

(2.2.5)

R_{\phi}={\lvert\theta\rvert}_{\infty}^{-\max\{(\deg\kappa_{i}-q^{i})/(q^{i}-1)\,\mid\,1\leqslant i\leqslant r,\,\kappa_{i}\neq 0\}}

(see [
[
19
]
, Rem. 6.11;
[
37
]
, Cor. 4.5]).

2.2.6.
Adjoints of
t
-modules

Assume now that
F
is a perfect
\mathsf{A}
-field and that
\psi:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{\ell}(F)[\tau]
is an Anderson
t
-module over
F
defined as in (
2.2.3
). The
adjoint
of
\psi
is defined to be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism
\psi^{*}:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{\ell}(F)[\sigma]
defined by

\psi_{a}^{*}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\psi_{a})^{*},\quad\forall\,a\in\mathsf{A}.

Since for
a
,
b\in\mathsf{A}
we have
\psi_{ab}=\psi_{a}\psi_{b}=\psi_{b}\psi_{a}
, (
2.1.8
) implies that
\psi^{*}
respects multiplication, which is the nontrivial part of checking that
\psi^{*}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism. From (
2.2.3
), we have

\psi^{*}_{t}=(\psi_{t})^{*}=(\partial\psi_{t})^{{\mathsf{T}}}+\bigl(E_{1}^{(-1)}\bigr)^{{\mathsf{T}}}\sigma+\cdots+\bigl(E_{w}^{(-w)}\bigr)^{{\mathsf{T}}}\sigma^{w},

and so for any
\mathbf{x}\in F^{\ell}
, we have
\psi^{*}_{t}(\mathbf{x})=(\partial\psi_{t})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\mathbf{x}+(E_{1}^{(-1)})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\mathbf{x}^{(-1)}+\cdots+(E_{w}^{(-w)})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\mathbf{x}^{(-w)}
.
In this way the map
\psi^{*}
induces an
\mathsf{A}
-module structure on
F^{\ell}
, which we denote
\psi^{*}(F)
. Similarly we denote
\Lie(\psi^{*})(F)=F^{\ell}
with an
F[t]
-module structure induced by
\partial\psi_{a}^{{\mathsf{T}}}
for
a\in\mathsf{A}
. For
a\in\mathsf{A}
, the
a
-torsion submodule of
\psi^{*}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5muF})
is denoted

\psi^{*}[a]\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\mathbf{x}\in\smash{\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5muF}}^{\ell}\mid\psi^{*}_{a}(\mathbf{x})=0\}.

If
\eta:\phi\to\psi
is a morphism of
t
-modules as above, then
\eta^{*}\in\Mat_{k\times\ell}(F)[\sigma]
provides a morphism
\eta^{*}:\psi^{*}\to\phi^{*}
such that
\eta^{*}\psi_{a}^{*}=\phi_{a}^{*}\eta^{*}
for all
a\in\mathsf{A}
(and vice versa). Furthermore,
\partial\eta^{*}:\Lie(\psi^{*})(F)\to\Lie(\phi^{*})(F)
is an
F[t]
-module homomorphism. Adjoints of Drinfeld modules were investigated extensively by Goss
[
31
, §4.14]
and Poonen
[
44
]
.

2.3.
t
-motives and dual
t
-motives

For this subsection we fix a perfect
\mathsf{A}
-field
F
and
t
-module
\psi:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{\ell}(F)[\tau]
as in (
2.2.3
). Recall that
\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\theta}=\iota(t)\in F
.

2.3.1.
t
-motive of
\psi

We let
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Mat_{1\times\ell}(F[\tau])
, and make
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
into a left
F[t,\tau]
-module by using the inherent structure as a left
F[\tau]
-module and setting

a\cdot m\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=m\psi_{a},\quad m\in\mathcal{M}_{\psi},\ a\in\mathsf{A}.

Then
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is called the
t
-motive
of
\psi
. We note that for any
m\in\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
,

(t-\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\theta})^{\ell}\cdot m\in\tau\mathcal{M}_{\psi},

since
\partial\psi_{t}-\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\theta}\mathrm{I}_{\ell}
is nilpotent (and
F
is perfect). If we need to emphasize the dependence on the base field
F
, we write

\mathcal{M}_{\psi}(F)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcal{M}_{\psi}=\Mat_{1\times\ell}(F[\tau]).

A morphism
\eta:\phi\to\psi
of
t
-modules over
F
of dimensions
k
and
\ell
, defined as in §
2.2
, induces a morphism of left
F[t,\tau]
-modules
\eta^{\dagger}:\mathcal{M}_{\psi}\to\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
, given by
\eta^{\dagger}(m)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=m\eta
for
m\in\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
. The functor from
t
-modules over
F
to
t
-motives over
F
is fully faithful, and so every left
F[t,\tau]
-module homomorphism
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}\to\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
arises in this way.

By construction
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is free of rank
\ell
as a left
F[\tau]
-module, and we say
\ell
is the
dimension
of
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
. If
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is further free of finite rank over
F[t]
, then
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is said to be
abelian
and
r=\rank_{F[t]}\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is the
rank
of
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
. We will say that
\psi
is abelian or has rank
r
if
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
possesses the corresponding properties. The
t
-motives in Anderson’s original definition in
[
2
]
are abelian, as will be most of the
t
-motives in this paper, but for example, see [
[
11
]
;
[
31
]
, Ch. 5;
[
40
]
, Ch. 2–4;
[
33
]
] for
t
-motives in this wider context.

2.3.2.
Dual
t
-motive of
\psi

We let
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Mat_{1\times\ell}(F[\sigma])
, and similar to the case of
t
-motives, we define a left
F[t,\sigma]
-module structure on
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
by setting

a\cdot n\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=n\psi_{a}^{*},\quad n\in\mathcal{N}_{\psi},\ a\in\mathsf{A}.

The module
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
is the
dual
t
-motive
of
\psi
. As in the case of
t
-motives, for any
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
we have
(t-\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\theta})^{\ell}\cdot n\in\sigma\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
. Also if we need to emphasize the dependence on
F
, we write

\mathcal{N}_{\psi}(F)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcal{N}_{\psi}=\Mat_{1\times\ell}(F[\sigma]).

Again for a morphism
\eta:\phi\to\psi
of
t
-modules of dimensions
k
and
\ell
, we obtain a morphism of left
F[t,\sigma]
-modules,
\eta^{\ddagger}:\mathcal{N}_{\phi}\to\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
, given by
\eta^{\ddagger}(n)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=n\eta^{*}
for
n\in\mathcal{N}_{\phi}
. Also, every morphism of left
F[t,\sigma]
-modules
\mathcal{N}_{\phi}\to\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
arises in this way.

The dual
t
-motive
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
is free of rank
\ell
as a left
F[\sigma]
-module, and
\ell
is the
dimension
of
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
. If
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
is free of finite rank over
F[t]
, then we say
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
is
\mathsf{A}
-finite
, and we call
r=\rank_{F[t]}(\mathcal{N}_{\psi})
the
rank
of
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
. It has been shown by Maurischat
[
39
]
that for a
t
-module
\psi
, the
t
-motive
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is abelian if and only if the dual
t
-motive
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
is
\mathsf{A}
-finite. In this case the rank of
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is the same as the rank of
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
. We will say that
\psi
is
\mathsf{A}
-finite or has rank
r
if
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
has those properties.
Dual
t
-motives were initially introduced in
[
3
]
over fields of generic characteristic. See [
[
11
]
;
[
33
]
;
[
39
]
;
[
40
]
, Ch. 2–4]), for more information.

We call
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\in\Mat_{r\times 1}(\mathcal{M}_{\psi}(F))
a
basis
of
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}(F)
if
m_{1},\dots,m_{r}
form an
F[t]
-basis of
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}(F)
. Likewise
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\dots,n_{r})^{{\mathsf{T}}}\in\Mat_{r\times 1}(\mathcal{N}_{\psi}(F))
is a
basis
of
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}(F)
if
n_{1},\dots,n_{r}
form an
F[t]
-basis of
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}(F)
. We then define
\Gamma
,
\Phi\in\Mat_{r}(F[t])
so that

\tau\mathbf{m}=\Gamma\mathbf{m},\quad\sigma\mathbf{n}=\Phi\mathbf{n}.

It follows that
\det\Gamma=c(t-\theta)^{\ell}
,
\det\Phi=c^{\prime}(t-\theta)^{\ell}
,
where
c
,
c^{\prime}\in F^{\times}
(e.g., see
[
40
, §3.2]
). Then
\Gamma

represents multiplication by
\tau
on
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
and
\Phi

represents multiplication by
\sigma
on
\mathcal{N}_{\psi}
.

Example 2.3.3
.

Carlitz module.
The Carlitz module
\mathsf{C}:\mathsf{A}\to F[\tau]
over
F
is defined by

\mathsf{C}_{t}=\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\theta}+\tau,

and it has dimension
1
and rank
1
. Then
\mathbf{m}=\{1\}
is an
F[t]
-basis for
\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{C}}=F[\tau]
, and
\mathbf{n}=\{1\}
is an
F[t]
-basis for
\mathcal{N}_{\mathsf{C}}=F[\sigma]
. One finds that
\tau\cdot 1=(t-\theta)\cdot 1
in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathsf{C}}
and
\sigma\cdot 1=(t-\theta)\cdot 1
in
\mathcal{N}_{\mathsf{C}}

2.4.
Tate modules and characteristic polynomials for Drinfeld modules

We fix a Drinfeld module
\phi:\mathsf{A}\to A[\tau]
of rank
r
in generic characteristic, given by

\phi_{t}=\theta+\kappa_{1}\tau+\cdots+\kappa_{r}\tau^{r},\quad\kappa_{i}\in A,\ \kappa_{r}\neq 0.

Letting
f\in A_{+}
be irreducible of degree
d
, the reduction of
\phi
modulo
f
is a Drinfeld module
\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi}:\mathsf{A}\to\mathbb{F}_{f}[\tau]
of rank
r_{0}\leqslant r
, where
\mathbb{F}_{f}=A/fA
. Then
\phi
has
good reduction
modulo
f
if
r_{0}=r
or equivalently if
f\nmid\kappa_{r}
.

For
\lambda\in\mathsf{A}_{+}
irreducible, we form the
\lambda
-adic
Tate modules
,

T_{\lambda}(\phi)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\varprojlim\phi[\lambda^{m}],\quad T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\varprojlim\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi}[\lambda^{m}].

As an
\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}
-module,
T_{\lambda}(\phi)\cong\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}^{r}
, and if
\lambda(\theta)\neq f
, then likewise
T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi})\cong\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}^{r_{0}}
. Fixing henceforth that
\lambda(\theta)\neq f
, we set
P_{f}(X)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Char(\tau^{d},T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi}),X)|_{t=\theta}
to be the characteristic polynomial of the
q^{d}
-th power Frobenius acting on

but, for convenience, with coefficients forced into
A
(rather than
\mathsf{A}
). Thus we have

(2.4.1)

P_{f}(X)=X^{r_{0}}+c_{r_{0}-1}X^{r_{0}-1}+\cdots+c_{0}\in A[X].

Takahashi
[
49
, Prop. 3]
showed that the coefficients in
A
and are independent of the choice of
\lambda
(see also Gekeler
[
22
, Cor. 3.4]
).
We note that if
\phi
has good reduction modulo
\lambda
and if

is a Frobenius element, then (e.g., see [
[
29
]
, §3;
[
31
]
, §8.6])

\Char(\tau^{d},T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi}),X)=\Char(\alpha_{f},T_{\lambda}(\phi),X)\in\mathsf{A}[X].

2.4.2.
Properties of

The following results are due to Gekeler
[

•

We have
c_{0}=c_{f}^{-1}f

•

The ideal

•

The roots

Extending these a little further, for

. Additionally,

(2.4.3)

\bigl[\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi}(\mathbb{F}_{f})\bigr]_{A}=c_{f}P_{f}(1)

by

be the characteristic polynomial in

of

acting on the the dual space of

and
P_{f}^{\vee}(X)

2.4.5.
Everywhere good reduction

Hsia and Yu
[
34
]
have determined precise formulas for
c_{f}
in terms of the
(q-1)
-st power residue symbol. Of particular interest presently is the case that
\phi
has everywhere good reduction, i.e., when
\kappa_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
. In this case, Hsia and Yu
[
34
, Thm. 3.2, Eqs. (2) & (8)]
showed that
c_{f}=(-1)^{r+d(r+1)}\kappa_{r}^{d}
.
This prompts the definition of a completely multiplicative function
\chi_{\phi}:A_{+}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
,

(2.4.6)

\chi_{\phi}(a)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\bigl((-1)^{r+1}\kappa_{r}\bigr)^{\deg_{\theta}a},

for which we see that
c_{f}=(-1)^{r}\chi_{\phi}(f)
. Letting
\overline{\chi}_{\phi}:A_{+}\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
be the multiplicative inverse of
\chi_{\phi}
, we see that

(2.4.7)

\displaystyle P_{f}(X)

\displaystyle=X^{r}+c_{r-1}X^{r-1}+\cdots+c_{1}X+(-1)^{r}\overline{\chi}_{\phi}(f)\cdot f,

\displaystyle P_{f}^{\vee}(X)

\displaystyle=X^{r}+\frac{(-1)^{r}\chi_{\phi}(f)c_{1}}{f}X^{r-1}+\cdots+\frac{(-1)^{r}\chi_{\phi}(f)c_{r-1}}{f}X+\frac{(-1)^{r}\chi_{\phi}(f)}{f},

and likewise

(2.4.8)

\displaystyle Q_{f}(X)

\displaystyle=1+c_{r-1}X+\cdots+c_{1}X^{r-1}+(-1)^{r}\overline{\chi}_{\phi}(f)\cdot fX^{r},

\displaystyle Q_{f}^{\vee}(fX)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} 1+(-1)^{r}\chi_{\phi}(f)c_{1}X+\cdots+(-1)^{r}&\chi_{\phi}(f)c_{r-1}f^{r-2}X^{r-1}\\
&{}+(-1)^{r}\chi_{\phi}(f)f^{r-1}X^{r}.\end{aligned}

Moreover,

(2.4.9)

\mu_{\phi}(f)=(-1)^{r+1}\chi_{\phi}(f)c_{1},\quad\nu_{\phi}(f)=-c_{r-1}.

We record the induced recursive relations (cf.
[
14
, Lem. 3.5]
) on
\mu_{\phi}
and
\nu_{\phi}
, where taking
m+r\geqslant 1
and using the convention that
\mu_{\phi}(b)=\nu_{\phi}(b)=0
if
b\in K\setminus A_{+}
,

(2.4.10)

\displaystyle\mu_{\phi}(f^{m+r})

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \mu_{\phi}(f)\mu_{\phi}(f^{m+r-1})-(-1)^{r}\chi_{\phi}(f)\sum_{j=2}^{r-1}&c_{j}f^{j-1}\mu_{\phi}(f^{m+r-j})\\
&{}-(-1)^{r}\chi_{\phi}(f)f^{r-1}\mu_{\phi}(f^{m}),\end{aligned}

(2.4.11)

\displaystyle\nu_{\phi}(f^{m+r})

\displaystyle=\nu_{\phi}(f)\nu_{\phi}(f^{m+r-1})-\sum_{j=2}^{r-1}c_{r-j}\nu_{\phi}(f^{m+r-j})-(-1)^{r}\overline{\chi}_{\phi}(f)f\nu_{\phi}(f^{m}).

2.5.
Schur polynomials

We review properties of symmetric polynomials and especially Schur polynomials. For more details on symmetric polynomials see [
[
1
]
, Ch. 8;
[
35
]
, §2.5;
[
45
]
, Ch. 7].
Letting
\mathbf{x}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
be independent variables, the
elementary symmetric polynomials

\{e_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}=\{e_{n,i}\}_{i=0}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
are defined by

(2.5.1)

\sum_{i=0}^{n}e_{i}(\mathbf{x})T^{i}=(1+x_{1}T)(1+x_{2}T)\cdots(1+x_{n}T).

We adopt the convention that
e_{i}=0
if
i<0
or
i>n
.
The
complete homogeneous symmetric polynomials

\{h_{i}\}_{i\geqslant 0}=\{h_{n,i}\}_{i\geqslant 0}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
are defined by

(2.5.2)

\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}h_{i}(\mathbf{x})T^{i}=\frac{1}{(1-x_{1}T)(1-x_{2}T)\cdots(1-x_{n}T)},

and similarly if
i<0
then we take
h_{i}=0
. Then
h_{i}
consists of the sum of all monomials in
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
of degree
i
. The
Vandermonde determinant
is

(2.5.3)

V(\mathbf{x})=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}(x_{i}-x_{j}).

When nonzero we have
\deg e_{i}=i
and
\deg h_{i}=i
, and
\deg V=\binom{n}{2}
.

Definition 2.5.4
.

For polynomials
P(T)=(T-x_{1})\cdots(T-x_{k})
and
Q(T)=(T-y_{1})\cdots(T-y_{\ell})
, we set

(P\otimes Q)(T)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant i\leqslant k\\
1\leqslant j\leqslant\ell\end{subarray}}(T-x_{i}y_{j}).

We further set

\displaystyle\left(\Sym^{2}P\right)(T)=\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant j\leqslant k}(T-x_{i}x_{j})

\displaystyle\left(\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}P\right)(T)=\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant k}(T-x_{i}x_{j}).

Letting
B_{m}
be the coefficient of
T^{m}
in
(P\otimes Q)(T)
, we find that
B_{m}
is symmetric in both
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
and
y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell}
, its total degree in
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}
is
k\ell-m
, and its total degree in
y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell}
is also
k\ell-m
. As such,

B_{m}\in\mathbb{Z}[e_{k,1}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,e_{k,k\ell-m}(\mathbf{x});e_{\ell,1}(\mathbf{y}),\ldots,e_{\ell,k\ell-m}(\mathbf{y})]
.
The coefficients of
(P\otimes Q)(T)
and its inverse
(P\otimes Q)(T)^{-1}
are expressible in terms of Schur polynomials (see Theorem
2.5.13
and Corollary
2.5.14
for
(P\otimes Q)(T)^{-1}
).

2.5.5.
Schur polynomials

Let
\lambda
denote an integer partition
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n}\geqslant 0
of length
n
, where
\lambda_{i}=0
is allowed. We set

(2.5.6)

s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})=s_{\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{n}}(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=V(\mathbf{x})^{-1}\cdot\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{\lambda_{1}+n-1}&\cdots&x_{n}^{\lambda_{n}+n-1}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
x_{1}^{\lambda_{n-1}+1}&\cdots&x_{n}^{\lambda_{n-1}+1}\\
x_{1}^{\lambda_{n}}&\cdots&x_{n}^{\lambda_{n}}\end{pmatrix}

We have the following properties (see [
[
1
]
, §8.3;
[
45
]
, §7.15]).

•

s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})
is a symmetric polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

•

\deg s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})=\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}
.

•

For
0\leqslant i\leqslant n
we have
s_{\underbrace{\scriptstyle 1\,\,\cdots\,\,1}_{i}\underbrace{\scriptstyle 0\,\,\cdots\,\,0}_{n-i}}(\mathbf{x})=e_{i}(\mathbf{x})
.

•

For
i\geqslant 0
we have
s_{i\underbrace{\scriptstyle 0\,\,\cdots\,\,0}_{n-1}}(\mathbf{x})=h_{i}(\mathbf{x})
.

The polynomial
s_{\lambda}
is called the
Schur polynomial for
\lambda
. Following the exposition of Bump and Goldfeld [
[
12
]
;
[
26
]
], when
n\geqslant 2
(which we now assume), we consider the subset of Schur polynomials where
\lambda_{n}=0
as follows. For integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{n-1}\geqslant 0
, form

\lambda:k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n-1}\geqslant k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n-1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant k_{n-1}\geqslant 0\geqslant 0.

We set
S_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n-1}}(\mathbf{x})
to be the the Schur polynomial
s_{\lambda}
, i.e.,

(2.5.7)

S_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n-1}}(\mathbf{x})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=V(\mathbf{x})^{-1}\cdot\det\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n-1}+n-1}&\cdots&x_{n}^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n-1}+n-1}\\
x_{1}^{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n-1}+n-2}&\cdots&x_{n}^{k_{2}+\cdots+k_{n-1}+n-2}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
x_{1}^{k_{n-1}+1}&\cdots&x_{n}^{k_{n-1}+1}\\
1&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix}.

The degree of
S_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n-1}}(\mathbf{x})
is
k_{1}+2k_{2}+\cdots+(n-1)k_{n-1}
.

Lemma 2.5.8
.

Let
\lambda:\lambda_{1}\geqslant\cdots\geqslant\lambda_{n}\geqslant 0
be an integer partition. Then

s_{\lambda}(\mathbf{x})=(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{\lambda_{n}}\cdot S_{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{3},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}-\lambda_{n}}(\mathbf{x}).

As a result, we see from the properties of
s_{\lambda}
above that

(2.5.9)

\displaystyle S_{\underbrace{\scriptstyle 0,\,\ldots\,,0,1,0,\,\ldots\,,0}_{\textup{$i$-th place}}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=e_{i}(\mathbf{x}),\qquad

\displaystyle 1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1,

(2.5.10)

\displaystyle S_{i,\underbrace{\scriptstyle 0,\,\cdots\,,0}_{n-2}}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=h_{i}(\mathbf{x}),\qquad

\displaystyle i\geqslant 0.

Lemma 2.5.11
.

For
k_{1},\dots,k_{n-1}\geqslant 0
, we have

(x_{1}\cdots x_{n})^{k_{1}+\dots+k_{n-1}}\cdot S_{k_{1},\dots,k_{n-1}}\bigl(x_{1}^{-1},\dots,x_{n}^{-1}\bigr)=S_{k_{n-1},\dots,k_{1}}(\mathbf{x}).

2.6.
The function
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}

We review the function
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}
and its properties explored in
[
35
, §6.1]
by Papanikolas and the author. They in fact defined the function
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}
and its “dual” version
\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\phi}
. For the purpose of the present paper, we only list the properties for the function
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}
.

Let
f\in A_{+}
be irreducible, and let
P_{\phi,f}(X)
and
P_{\phi,f}^{\vee}(X)
be defined as in (
2.4.7
). We let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}\in\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5muK}
be the roots of
P_{\phi,f}^{\vee}(X)
. For
k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}\geqslant 0
, we define

(2.6.1)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}\bigl(f^{k_{1}},\dots,f^{k_{r-1}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=S_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}}(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r})\cdot f^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r-1}},

where
S_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}}
is the Schur polynomial of (
2.5.7
). We note that by (
2.4.8
) and (
2.5.9
),

(2.6.2)

\displaystyle Q_{\phi,f}^{\vee}(fX)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} 1&{}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f,1,\ldots,1)X+\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(1,f,1,\ldots,1)fX^{2}\\
&{}+\cdots+(-1)^{r-1}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(1,\ldots,1,f)f^{r-2}X^{r-1}+(-1)^{r}\chi_{\phi}(f)f^{r-1}X^{r}.\end{aligned}

We then extend
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}
uniquely to functions on
(A_{+})^{r-1}
, by requiring that if
a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1}
,
b_{1},\dots,b_{r-1}\in A_{+}
satisfy
\gcd(a_{1}\cdots a_{r-1},b_{1}\cdots b_{r-1})=1
, then

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(a_{1}b_{1},\dots,a_{r-1}b_{r-1})

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1})\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(b_{1},\dots,b_{r-1}).

Proposition 2.6.3

(
[
35
, Prop. 6.1.5]
)
.

For
a
,
a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1}\in A_{+}
, the following hold.

(a)

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1})\in A
.

(b)

\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(a,1,\dots,1)=\mu_{\phi}(a)
.

(c)

We have

\displaystyle\deg_{\theta}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{r}\bigl((r-1)\deg_{\theta}a_{1}+(r-2)\deg_{\theta}a_{2}+\cdots+\deg_{\theta}a_{r-1}\bigr).

We also list some recursive relations of the function
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}
induced by relations on Schur polynomials (
[
35
, (6.1.7), (6.1.9), (6.1.11)]
, cf.
[
26
, p. 278]
). Fix

irreducible, and for

,
k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}\geqslant 0
,

(2.6.4)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}\bigl(f^{k},

\displaystyle\,1,\dots,1\bigr)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}\bigl(f^{k_{1}},\dots,f^{k_{r-1}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \smash{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\begin{subarray}{c}m_{0}+\cdots+m_{r-1}=k\\
m_{1}\leqslant k_{1},\,\ldots,\,m_{r-1}\leqslant k_{r-1}\end{subarray}\end{subarray}}}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}\bigl(f^{k_{1}+m_{0}-m_{1}},f^{k_{2}+m_{1}-m_{2}},\dots,&\,f^{k_{r-1}+m_{r-2}-m_{r-1}}\bigr)\\
&{}\cdot\chi_{\phi}(f)^{m_{r-1}}f^{k-m_{0}}.\end{aligned}

For
0\leqslant k\leqslant r-1
,

(2.6.5)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}

\displaystyle\bigl(\underbrace{1,\ldots,1,f,1,\ldots,1}_{\textup{$k$-th place}})\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}\bigl(f^{k_{1}},\dots,f^{k_{r-1}}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \smash{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{0}+\cdots+m_{r-1}=k\\
(m_{0},\ldots,m_{r-1})\,\in\,\mathcal{I}_{k_{1},\dots,k_{r-1}}\end{subarray}}}\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}\bigl(f^{k_{1}+m_{0}-m_{1}},f^{k_{2}+m_{1}-m_{2}},\dots,&\,f^{k_{r-1}+m_{r-2}-m_{r-1}}\bigr)\\
&{}\cdot\chi_{\phi}(f)^{m_{r-1}}f^{1-m_{0}}.\end{aligned}

In particular for
k\geqslant 1
(cf.
[
26
, p. 278]
),

(2.6.6)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k},1,\dots,1)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f,1,\ldots,1)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k+1},&\,1,\ldots,1)\\
&{}+\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k-1},f,1,\ldots,1)\cdot f,\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k},1,\dots,1)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(1,f,1,\ldots,1)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k},&\,f,1,\ldots,1)\\
&{}+\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k-1},1,f,1,\ldots,1)\cdot f,\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k},1,\dots,1)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(1,1,f,1,\ldots,1)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k},&\,1,f,1,\ldots,1)\\
&{}+\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k-1},1,1,f,1,\ldots,1)\cdot f,\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k},1,\dots,1)\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(1,\ldots,1,f)

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k},&\,1,\ldots,1,f)\\
&{}+\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\phi}(f^{k-1},1,\ldots,1)\cdot\chi_{\phi}(f)f.\end{aligned}

2.7.
Matrix operations

Fixing a subring
R\subseteq\mathbb{L}_{t}
with
1
, we say
M\in\Mat_{r}(R)

represents an
R
-module homomorphism
f:R^{r}\to R^{r}
with respect to a basis
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{r})^{\mathsf{T}}\in\Mat_{r\times 1}(R^{r})
if

f\cdot\mathbf{v}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{pmatrix}f(v_{1})\\
\vdots\\
f(v_{r})\end{pmatrix}=M\mathbf{v}.

Remark 2.7.1
.

This is slightly different from the usual sense in linear algebra. For example, if we let
M_{f}
,
M_{g}\in\Mat_{r}(R)
represent two
R
-module homomorphisms
f
,
g:R^{r}\to R^{r}
respectively, then
M_{g}M_{f}
represents
f\circ g
. In fact
M_{f}
is the transpose of the matrix representation of
f
in the usual sense.

For
i
,
j=1,\dots,r
, we define, assuming characteristic of
R
is not
2
,

\alpha_{ij}=v_{i}\otimes v_{j},\quad\beta_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}{\left(v_{i}\otimes v_{j}+v_{j}\otimes v_{i}\right)},\quad\gamma_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(v_{i}\otimes v_{j}-v_{j}\otimes v_{i}),

and consider the following basis of
R
-modules in lexicographical order

(2.7.2)

\mathfrak{Y}_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\alpha_{ij}\}_{i,j}\subseteq{\left(R^{r}\right)}^{\otimes 2},\quad\mathfrak{Y}_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\beta_{ij}\}_{i,j}\subseteq\Sym^{2}{\left(R^{r}\right)},\quad\mathfrak{Y}_{3}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{\gamma_{ij}\}_{i,j}\subseteq\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}{\left(R^{r}\right)}.

Definition 2.7.3
.

Let
M\in\Mat_{r}(R)
, and let
\mathfrak{Y_{1}}
,
\mathfrak{Y_{2}}
and
\mathfrak{Y_{3}}
as in (
2.7.2
). Then
M
represents some
R
-module homomorphism
f:R^{r}\to R^{r}
. We define the following matrix operations

M^{\otimes 2}\in\Mat_{r^{2}}(R),\quad\Sym^{2}(M)\in\Mat_{\frac{r(r+1)}{2}}(R),\quad\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}(M)\in\Mat_{\frac{r(r-1)}{2}}(R)

to be matrices representing
f^{\otimes 2}
,
\Sym^{2}(f)
and
\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}(f)
with respect to
\mathfrak{Y_{1}}
,
\mathfrak{Y_{2}}
and
\mathfrak{Y_{3}}
, respectively.

Remark 2.7.4
.

In Definition
2.7.3
, by a direct checking, the matrix
M^{\otimes 2}
is the Kronecker tensor square of
M
.

Example 2.7.5
.

Suppose
r=2
and
M=(M_{ij})_{i,j=1,2}\in\Mat_{2}(R).
Then

(a)

M^{\otimes 2}=\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}M_{11}&M_{11}M_{12}&M_{12}M_{11}&M_{12}M_{12}\\
M_{11}M_{21}&M_{11}M_{22}&M_{12}M_{21}&M_{12}M_{22}\\
M_{21}M_{11}&M_{21}M_{12}&M_{22}M_{11}&M_{22}M_{12}\\
M_{21}M_{21}&M_{21}M_{22}&M_{22}M_{21}&M_{22}M_{22}\end{pmatrix}.

(b)

\Sym^{2}(M)=\begin{pmatrix}M_{11}^{2}&2M_{11}M_{12}&M_{12}^{2}\\
M_{11}M_{21}&M_{11}M_{22}+M_{12}M_{21}&M_{12}M_{22}\\
M_{21}^{2}&2M_{21}M_{22}&M_{22}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(c)

\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}(M)=\det(M).

Lemma 2.7.6
.

For
M\in\Mat_{r}(R)
, we let
\mathcal{T}(M)
denote
M^{\otimes 2}
,
\Sym^{2}(M)
or
\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}(M).
Then for
M_{1}
,
M_{2}\in\Mat_{r}(R)
, we have the following properties.

(a)

\mathcal{T}(M_{1}M_{2})=\mathcal{T}(M_{1})\mathcal{T}(M_{2})
. Futhermore,
\mathcal{T}(M^{-1})=\mathcal{T}(M)^{-1}
if
M\in\GL_{r}(R).

(b)

\mathcal{T}(M^{(n)})=\mathcal{T}(M)^{(n)}
for
n\geqslant 0.

Proof.

The first part follows from Remark
2.7.1
, and the second part follows from the fact that entries of
\mathcal{T}(M)
are polynomials in entries of
M
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
∎

Tensor powers of Drindeld modules were initiated by Anderson

-modules. In this chapter, we recover Hamahata’s models from the aspect of

We fix two Drinfeld modules
\phi
,

with everywhere good reduction as in (
1.2.1
),
and their

-motives

3.1.
Tensor products of Drinfeld modules defined over
A

The tensor product of
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\psi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]}\mathcal{M}_{\psi}

equipped with a left
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t,\tau]
-module structure by using the
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]
-module structure from the tensor product over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]
and setting

\tau\cdot(a_{1}\otimes a_{2})\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\tau a_{1}\otimes\tau a_{2},\quad a_{1}\in\mathcal{M}_{\phi},~a_{2}\in\mathcal{M}_{\psi}.

In the sense of
[
2
]
,
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\psi}
is a
pure

t
-motive, and its

, denoted by

w(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\psi})=\frac{\rank_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\psi}}{\rank_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\psi}}.

Combining this with

\rank_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\psi}=\left(w(\phi)+w(\psi)\right)\cdot\rank_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{\psi}=\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\cdot r\cdot\ell=r+\ell.

More generally,

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}^{\otimes n}
is a pure
t
-motive of weight

(3.1.1)

(Khaochim

The set

for all

3.2.
Symmetric and alternating powers of Drinfeld modules defined over

Let
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
be the
n
-th symmetric group and
\sgn:\mathfrak{S}_{n}\to\{\pm 1\}
be the sign function.
The
n
-th symmetric power of
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is the
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t,\tau]
-submodule

(3.2.1)

\Sym^{n}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Span_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]}\left\{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}a_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{\sigma(n)}\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\phi}^{\otimes n}.

It is also a pure
t
-motive of weight
w(\Sym^{n}\mathcal{M}_{\phi})=w(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}^{\otimes n})
by
[
2
, Prop. 1.10.2]
. Together with (
3.1.1
), the dimension of
\Sym^{n}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is

(3.2.2)

\rank_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]}\Sym^{n}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\frac{n}{r}\cdot\rank_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]}\Sym^{n}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\frac{n}{r}\cdot\binom{r+n-1}{n}=\binom{r+n-1}{n-1}.

The
n
-th alternating power of
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
is the
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t,\tau]
-submodule

\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!n}}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\Span_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]}\left\{\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}\sgn(\sigma)(a_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{\sigma(n)})\right\}\subseteq\mathcal{M}_{\phi}^{\otimes n}.

Similar to symmetric powers, it is a pure
t
-motive of weight
w(\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!n}}\mathcal{M}_{\phi})=w(\mathcal{M}_{\phi}^{\otimes n})
and dimension

(3.2.3)

\rank_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]}\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!n}}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=n\cdot w(\phi)\cdot\rank_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]}\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!n}}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\frac{n}{r}\cdot\binom{r}{n}=\binom{r-1}{n-1}.

Lemma 3.2.4
.

Suppose
p\neq 2
.

(a)

The set

\mathfrak{X}_{1}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left\{1\otimes 1,\frac{1}{2}(1\otimes\tau+\tau\otimes 1),\dots,\frac{1}{2}(1\otimes\tau^{r}+\tau^{r}\otimes 1)\right\}

is a
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]
-basis for
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
.

(b)

The set

\mathfrak{X}_{2}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left\{\frac{1}{2}(1\otimes\tau-\tau\otimes 1),\dots,\frac{1}{2}(1\otimes\tau^{r-1}-\tau^{r-1}\otimes 1)\right\}

is a
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]
-basis for
\mathop{\bigwedge\nolimits^{\!2}}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
.

Proof.

By (
3.2.2
) and (
3.2.3
), it suffices to show that the two sets span the corresponding
t
-motives as
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]
-modules.

For the first part, we observe that the elements in
\Sym^{2}\mathcal{M}_{\phi}
are of the form,
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{\phi}=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau],~f_{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[t]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i}

\displaystyle f_{i}\cdot(a_{i}\otimes b_{i}+b_{i}\otimes a_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}((f_{i}\cdot a_{i})\otimes b_{i}+b_{i}\otimes(f_{i}\cdot a_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\sum_{j_{1}}\sum_{j_{2}}h_{i,j_{1},j_{2}}\tau^{\min(j_{1},j_{2})}(1\otimes\tau^{|j_{1}-j_{2}|}+\tau^{|j_{1}-j_{2}|}\otimes 1),

where
h_{i,j_{1},j_{2}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
. So it suffices to show, for
m\geqslant 0
,

(3.2.5)

\xi_{m}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1\otimes\tau^{m}+\tau^{m}\otimes 1\in\Span_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]}\mathfrak{X}_{1}.

We proceed by induction on
m
. It is clear that (
3.2.5
) holds for
m=0,\dots,r
. For
m>r
, we consider

(3.2.6)

t\cdot\xi_{m-r}=1\otimes(\tau^{m-r}\phi_{t})+(\tau^{m-r}\phi_{t})\otimes 1=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\kappa_{i}^{(m-r)}\xi_{m-r+i}.

On the other hand,

(3.2.7)

\begin{split}t\cdot\xi_{m-r}&=\phi_{t}\otimes\tau^{m-r}+\tau^{m-r}\otimes\phi_{t}\\
&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\kappa_{i}\tau^{i}\xi_{m-r-i}&\text{if $r\leqslant m-r$}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-r}\kappa_{i}\tau^{i}\xi_{m-r-i}+\sum_{i=1}^{2r-m}\kappa_{m-r+i}\tau^{m-r}\xi_{i}&\text{if $r>m-r$}\end{cases}.\end{split}

Combining (
3.2.6
) and (
3.2.7
), and using that
\kappa_{r}\neq 0
, in both cases
\xi_{m}
is a
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]
-linear combination of
\{\xi_{i}\}_{i=0}^{m-1}
, and the result follows by the induction hypothesis.

For the second part, by a similar observation, it suffices to show, for
m\geqslant 1
,

(3.2.8)

\zeta_{m}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=1\otimes\tau^{m}-\tau^{m}\otimes 1\in\Span_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[\tau]}\mathfrak{X}_{2}.

We again proceed by induction on
m
. It is clear that (
3.2.8
) holds for
m=1,\dots,r-1
. Note that
\zeta_{0}=0
, so we can not use the same process for the case
m=r
. In fact, it follows by expressing
t\cdot(1\otimes 1)
in two ways. Note that

t\cdot(1\otimes 1)=\phi_{t}\otimes 1=1\otimes\phi_{t},

which gives

\zeta_{r}=-\kappa_{r}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}\kappa_{i}\zeta_{i}.

For
m>r
, we consider

(3.2.9)

t\cdot\zeta_{m-r}=1\otimes(\tau^{m-r}\phi_{t})-(\tau^{m-r}\phi_{t})\otimes 1=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\kappa_{i}^{(m-r)}\zeta_{m-r+i}.

On the other hand,

(3.2.10)

\begin{split}t\cdot\zeta_{m-r}&=\phi_{t}\otimes\tau^{m-r}-\tau^{m-r}\otimes\phi_{t}\\
&=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{r}\kappa_{i}\tau^{i}\zeta_{m-r-i}&\text{if $r\leqslant m-r$}\\
\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-r}\kappa_{i}\tau^{i}\zeta_{m-r-i}-\sum_{i=1}^{2r-m}\kappa_{m-r+i}\tau^{m-r}\zeta_{i}&\text{if $r>m-r$}\end{cases}.\end{split}

Similarly, combining (
3.2.9
) and (
3.2.10
), the result follows by the induction hypothesis.
∎

4.2.
Fang’s class module formula

In
[
20
]
, Fang proved an extension of Taelman’s class module formula to abelian Anderson
t
-modules defined over the integral closure of
A
in a finite extension of
K
. Later Anglès, Ngo Dac and Tavares Ribeiro
[
6
]
extended the class module formula for admissible Anderson modules for more general ring, which comes from global function field over a finite field. For the purpose of the present paper, it suffices to focus on Fang’s identity, thus we will mainly provide a summary of it.

Let
E:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{\ell}(A[\tau])
be an abelian Anderson
t
-module defined over
A
.
The exponential series
\Exp_{E}\in{K[\![\tau]\!]}
of
E
induces an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear function

(4.2.1)

\Exp_{E,K_{\infty}}\colon\Lie(E)(K_{\infty})\to E(K_{\infty})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\Exp_{E,K_{\infty}}\colon K_{\infty}^{\ell}\to K_{\infty}^{\ell}.

Now
\Lie(E)(K_{\infty})
has a canonical
K_{\infty}
-vector space structure, but Fang
[
20
, p. 303]
pointed out that it has another structure of a vector space over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(t^{-1})\!)}
. Namely we extend
\partial:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{\ell}(K_{\infty})
to an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism,

(4.2.2)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(t^{-1})\!)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Mat_{\ell}(K_{\infty})\;:\;\sum_{j\geqslant j_{0}}c_{j}t^{-j}\longmapsto\sum_{j\geqslant j_{0}}c_{j}\cdot\partial E_{t}^{-j}.

Notably the series on the right converges by
[
20
, Lem. 1.7]
. As Fang continued,
\Lie(E)(K_{\infty})
obtains an
{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(t^{-1})\!)}
-vector space structure via
\partial
.
For any
g\in{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(t^{-q^{\ell}})\!)}
, we have
\partial g=g\cdot\mathrm{I}_{\ell}
, and so
\Lie(E)(K_{\infty})
has dimension
\ell q^{\ell}
as over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(t^{-q^{\ell}})\!)}
, which implies it has dimension
\ell
over
{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(t^{-1})\!)}
.
Since
K_{\infty}={\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(\theta^{-1})\!)}\cong{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(t^{-1})\!)}
, we will abuse notation and use the map
\partial
to define new
K_{\infty}
-vector space and
A
-module structures on
\Lie(E)(K_{\infty})
that are possibly different from scalar multiplication.
With respect to this
K_{\infty}
-structure, Fang showed
[
20
, Thm. 1.10]
that

\Lie(E)(A)\subseteq\Lie(E)(K_{\infty})

and

\Exp_{E,K_{\infty}}^{-1}\bigl(E(A)\bigr)\subseteq\Lie(E)(K_{\infty})

are
A
-lattices in the sense of
[
20
, Def. 1.9]
. In particular they have rank
\ell
as an
A
-modules via
\partial
.

Choose
A
-bases
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{\ell}\}
and
\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\ell}\}
of
\Lie(E)(A)
and
\Exp_{E,K_{\infty}}^{-1}(A^{\ell})
via
\partial
respectively, and let
V\in\GL_{\ell}(K_{\infty})
be chosen so that its columns are the coordinates of
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{\ell}
with respect to
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{\ell}
(via
\partial
). Following Taelman [
[
47
]
;
[
48
]
], Fang defined the regulator of
E
as

(4.2.3)

\mathrm{Reg}_{E}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\gamma\cdot\det(V)\in K_{\infty},\quad\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times},

where
\gamma
is chosen so that
\mathrm{Reg}_{E}
has sign
1
(leading coefficient as an element of
{\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(\theta^{-1})\!)}
is
1
).
This value is independent of the choice of
A
-bases.

Also following Taelman, Fang
[
20
, Thm. 1.10]
defined the class module of
E
as

(4.2.4)

\mathrm{H}(E)\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{E(K_{\infty})}{\Exp_{E,K_{\infty}}(\Lie(E)(K_{\infty}))+E(A)},

and he proved that
\mathrm{H}(E)
is a finitely generated and torsion
A
-module. Fang’s class module formula is the following.

Theorem 4.2.5

(Fang
[
20
, Thm. 1.10]
)
.

Let
E:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{\ell}(A[\tau])
be an abelian Anderson
t
-module. Then

\prod_{f}\frac{{[\Lie(\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muE})(\mathbb{F}_{f})]}_{A}}{{[\mkern 1.0mu\overline{\mkern-1.0muE}(\mathbb{F}_{f})]}_{A}}=\mathrm{Reg}_{E}\cdot{\left[\mathrm{H}(E)\right]}_{A},

where the left-hand side converges in
K_{\infty}
.

Special

Fixing

.

Let

be irreducible. For
\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi\otimes\psi}:\mathsf{A}\to\Mat_{r+\ell}(\mathbb{F}_{f}[\tau])
defined above,

{\left[\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi\otimes\psi}(\mathbb{F}_{f})\right]}_{A}=\chi_{\phi}(f)^{\ell}\,\chi_{\psi}(f)^{r}\cdot\mathbf{P}_{\phi\otimes\psi,f}(1)=\frac{\mathbf{P}_{\phi\otimes\psi,f}(1)}{\mathbf{P}_{\phi\otimes\psi,f}(0)}\cdot f^{r+\ell}.

Proof.

Let
\lambda\in\mathsf{A}_{+}
be irreducible so that
\lambda(\theta)\neq f
. Let
K_{f}^{\mathrm{nr}}
be the maximal unramified and separable extension of
K_{f}
, and let
K^{\mathrm{nr}}_{f}\supseteq O^{\mathrm{nr}}_{f}\supseteq M^{\mathrm{nr}}_{f}
be its subring of
f
-integral elements and its maximal ideal. Because
\phi
and
\psi
have everywhere good reduction, we see from

(see also

\phi[\lambda^{m}],\ \psi[\lambda^{m}]\subseteq O^{\mathrm{nr}}_{f},\quad\forall\,m\geqslant 1.

Moreover, the natural reduction maps

\phi(O^{\mathrm{nr}}_{f})[\lambda^{m}]=\phi[\lambda^{m}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi}[\lambda^{m}],\quad\psi(O^{\mathrm{nr}}_{f})[\lambda^{m}]=\psi[\lambda^{m}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\psi}[\lambda^{m}],\quad\forall\,m\geqslant 1,

are
\mathsf{A}
-module isomorphisms (e.g., see
[
49
, §2]
), which gives isomorphisms on Tate modules commuting with the Galois action

(4.3.6)

T_{\lambda}(\phi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi}),\quad T_{\lambda}(\psi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\psi}).

Together with
[

, Thm. 3.1]
, the following diagram of

-modules commutes:

(4.3.7)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{\lambda}(\phi\otimes\psi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi\otimes\psi})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi}\otimes\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\psi})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{\lambda}(\phi)\otimes_{\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}}T_{\lambda}(\psi)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi})\otimes_{\mathsf{A}_{\lambda}}T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\psi})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

\scriptstyle\sim

\scriptstyle\sim

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

Furthermore, the maps in the diagram above commute with the Galois action, which implies

\mathbf{P}_{\phi\otimes\psi,f}(X)=\Char(\tau^{d},T_{\lambda}(\mkern 2.5mu\overline{\mkern-2.5mu\phi\otimes\psi}),X).

\mathbf{P}_{f}(0)=\overline{\chi}_{\phi}(f)^{\ell}\overline{\chi}_{\psi}(f)^{r}f^{r+\ell}.

By
[

, it remains to show
\chi_{\phi}(f)^{\ell}\,\chi_{\psi}(f)^{r}\cdot\mathbf{P}_{\phi\otimes\psi,f}(1)\in A
is monic.

Indeed, writing
\mathbf{P}_{f}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{r\ell}b_{i}X^{i}
,
b_{i}\in A[X]
and
letting

be given as in (

), Definition

implies that, for

Assigning the weight

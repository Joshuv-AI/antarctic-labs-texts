Hyperbolic Anderson model 2: Strichartz estimates and Stratonovich setting
Xia Chen, Aurélien Deya, Jian Song, Samy Tindel

Abstract
. We study a wave equation in dimension d ∈ { 1 , 2 } d\in\{1,2\} with a multiplicative space-time Gaussian noise. The existence and uniqueness of the Stratonovich solution is obtained under some conditions imposed on the Gaussian noise. The strategy is to develop some Strichartz type estimates for the wave kernel in weighted Besov spaces, by which we can prove the wellposedness of an associated Young-type equation. Those Strichartz bounds are of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

In
[
4
]
we have started a long term project aiming at defining wave equations driven by rough noises. More specifically,
[
4
]
focused on the following Skorohod type equation on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\in\{1,2,3\}
:

(1.1)

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+u\diamond\dot{W}(t,x),

where
\diamond
stands for the Wick product and where
\dot{W}
is a centered Gaussian noise. In
[
4
]
, the covariance function for
\dot{W}
was fractional in time with proper decay in space. That is for
s,t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we had

(1.2)

\mathbb{E}[\dot{W}(s,x)\dot{W}(t,y)]=|s-t|^{-a_{0}}\gamma(x-y),

where
a_{0}\in[0,1)
and
\gamma
is a nonnegative and nonnegative definite (generalized) function admitting a spectral measure
\mu
. Then we obtained that under appropriate conditions on the initial conditions, the following relation is necessary and sufficient in order to solve (
1.1
):

(1.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}\right)^{3-a_{0}}\mu(d\xi)<\infty.

Notice that (
1.3
) quantifies how irregular in space our noise can be according to its regularity in time. In particular, if the function
\gamma
in (
1.2
) satisfies the scaling property

(1.4)

\gamma(cx)=c^{-a}\gamma(x)

for some
a\in(0,d]
, then condition (
1.3
) can be recast as

(1.5)

a_{0}+a<3\,.

In the current paper, we will contrast the neat condition obtained in (
1.3
) with the situation for a pathwise version of the wave equation (interpreted in the Young or Stratonovich sense). Namely, we consider the following Stratonovich type wave equation on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\in\{1,2\}
,

(1.6)

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u(t,x)+u\dot{W}(t,x),

with initial conditions
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(0,x)=u_{1}(x)
. The Gaussian noise we consider in (
1.6
) also has the covariance (
1.2
) with
\gamma
being a nonnegative definite (generalized) function. We prove the following result on the existence and uniqueness of the Stratonovich solution to (
1.6
) (see Proposition
5.6
and Corollary
5.7
for a precise account).

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
d\in\{1,2\}
and the spectral measure
\mu
of
W
verifies

(1.7)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}\right)^{\rho_{d}-a_{0}-\eta}\mu(d\xi)<\infty\,\quad\text{ for some }\eta>0,

where the parameter
\rho_{d}
is such that
\rho_{1}=1
and
\rho_{2}=\frac{1}{2}
.
Then, under some regularity conditions on
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
, there exists a unique Stratonovich solution to (
1.6
) in a proper path space.
If
\gamma
has the scaling property (
1.4
), condition (
1.7
) is equivalent to

(1.8)

\begin{cases}a_{0}+a<1,&\text{ if }d=1,\\
a_{0}+a<\frac{1}{2},&\text{ if }d=2.\end{cases}

It is well known that Stratonovich solutions to stochastic PDEs demand a more regular noisy input
\dot{W}
than in the Skorohod setting. And indeed in our case, it is readily checked that (
1.5
) is less restrictive than (
1.8
). In fact, for
d\in\{1,2\}
, (
1.5
) is automatically fulfilled as soon as
a_{0}\in[0,1)
and the covariance function
\gamma
is nonnegative (which implies
a\in(0,d]
, see
[
4
, Remark 1.4]
). Nevertheless, Theorem
1.1
gives the first condition on
a_{0}
and
\mu(d\xi)
in order to get a unique pathwise solution to (
1.6
). In addition, as the reader will see, our main result also unifies the treatment for
d=1
and
d=2
.

Let us mention some recent progress, obtained by methods which are completely different from ours, towards the definition of the stochastic wave equation in a pathwise sense.

(i) As far as we know, the first pathwise developments for a stochastic wave equation can be found in
[
10
]
, dealing with the one-dimensional case. Based on the specific expression of the wave kernel for
d=1
, the strategy therein relies on a natural preliminary rotation of the model, that turns (
1.6
) into a more tractable equation in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It is then established that when one injects a noise of the form (
1.2
)-(
1.4
) within the new equation (thus corresponding to a
“rotated”
noise for the original equation), the interpretation and wellposedness can be obtained for all
a_{0},a\in(0,1)
. Unfortunately, this preliminary transform of the model - which will not occur in our direct approach - essentially rules out the possibility to compare the interpretation in
[
10
]
with ours, and accordingly to compare the conditions in
[
10
]
with those in (
1.8
).

(ii) The recent publication
[
1
]
focuses on the wave equation with a noise
\dot{W}
independent of time. The condition obtained in
[
1
]
is

(1.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}\,\mu(d\xi)<\infty,

which is equivalent to
a<1
when
\gamma
has the scaling property (
1.4
). A time independent noise being morally equivalent to
a_{0}=0
, in this situation our condition (
1.8
) can be read

a<1\text{ for }d=1,\quad\text{ and }\quad a<\frac{1}{2}\text{ for }d=2.

Our condition on
a
is thus slightly suboptimal for
d=2
if one compares it to (
1.9
), although our method is currently the only one accommodating for a time-dependent noise.
Let us also mention that
[
1
]
relies on chaos expansions for Stratonovich integrals. This strategy is interesting in its own right and very different from the pathwise considerations in the current paper.

(iii) Some attention has been paid recently to models of wave equation with additive noise and polynomial nonlinearities. A prototype for such an equation can be written as

(1.10)

\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta u-\rho u^{2}+\dot{W},

with an additive fractional noise
\dot{W}
and
\rho>0
. In this context, renormalization procedures are implemented in
[
6
,
8
]
. This yields existence results for (
1.10
) in case of a rough noise
\dot{W}
.

As one can see, the study of wave equations in a rough setting is still wide open. Our contribution aims at a better understanding of the Young regime within this landmark.

As mentioned above, instead of the Skorohod setting advocated in
[
4
]
, the stochastic differential in (
1.6
) is interpreted in the
Stratonovich
sense. This forces to a totally different approach which is based on pathwise type considerations. We briefly elaborate our strategy below.

We will solve the wave equation under its so-called mild form, which will be properly introduced in (
4.29
). The main technical issue will thus be to understand the meaning of integrals like

(1.11)

\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{t-r}(u_{r}d\dot{W}_{r}),

where
u
is a candidate solution,
\mathcal{G}_{t}
stands for the wave operator at time
t
, and where
\dot{W}
is our noisy input. Since we wish to interpret (
1.11
) as a Young integral, we will see it as the limit of a sequence of paths
\{\mathcal{J}_{t}^{(n)};t\in[0,T]\}
defined by

(1.12)

\mathcal{J}_{t}^{(n)}:=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{G}_{t-t^{n}_{k}}(u_{t^{n}_{k}}\,\delta\dot{W}_{t^{n}_{k}t^{n}_{k+1}}),\quad\mbox{ for }t\in(t^{n}_{m-1},t^{n}_{m}]\,,

where
t_{m}^{n}=mT/2^{n}
for
0\leq m\leq 2^{n}
and
\delta\dot{W}_{t_{k}^{n}t_{k+1}^{n}}=\dot{W}_{t_{k+1}^{n}}-\dot{W}_{t_{k}^{n}}
. Our most important endeavour is thus to quantify the smoothing effect of the operator
\mathcal{G}_{t-t_{k}^{n}}
in (
1.12
). This is achieved thanks to some Strichartz type estimates for the operator
\mathcal{G}_{s}
in some weighted Besov spaces, which are new and should be considered as one of the main contributions of the current paper.

Our findings concerning Strichartz type estimates are summarized in Proposition
3.4
below. Roughly speaking, if
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
designates a weighted Besov space with regularity
\alpha\leq 0
, we claim that

(1.13)

\displaystyle\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha+\kappa}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{1-\kappa}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}},\quad\text{for all}\quad\kappa\in[0,1],\ \text{ if }d=1,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha+\kappa}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t-s|^{1/2-\kappa}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}},\quad\text{for all}\quad\kappa\in[0,1/2],\ \text{ if }d=2.

These bounds thus differ from the classical Strichartz estimates (see e.g.
[
7
]
) in two aspects. First, they offer a Hölder-type control (in time) on the action of
\mathcal{G}
, a key ingredient toward a successful Young integration procedure. Secondly, they involve Besov spaces with weights, which is crucial in order to deal with a noise like
\dot{W}
defined on the whole space.

Our considerations are based on a weighted version of Littlewood-Paley analysis introduced by Rychkov in
[
9
]
. This approach is convenient when one wishes to handle kernels with polynomial decay in Fourier modes such as
\mathcal{G}_{t}
. This explains in particular why we have decided to stick to
[
9
]
instead of using the more recent method advocated in
[
5
]
.

We close this section by highlighting some possible generalization of our work:

(i) In the current article, we have focused on a noisy term of the form
u\dot{W}
in (
1.6
) for sake of conciseness. We firmly believe that a noise term
\sigma(u)\dot{W}
with a smooth enough
\sigma
could also be covered by our approach, at the price of longer technical considerations.

(ii) The treatment of rougher noises is expected to rely on higher-order expansions of the model, together with renormalization procedures. These challenging developments may require the adaptation of some ideas from regularity structures theory or paracontrolled calculus in the wave setting.

(iii) The Strichartz type estimate alluded to above are based on convolution estimates for functions. In order to reach
d=3
or above, those estimates should be extended to measures or distributions. At the moment, this is still an open problem for us.

2
, we recall some preliminaries on weighted Besov spaces. The smoothing effect of the wave kernel in weighted Besov spaces is obtained in Section
3
. It is then applied to study a Young-type equation associated with (
1.6
) in Section
4
, where we define Young wave integration and prove the wellposedness of the Young-type equations. In Section
5
, we apply the result on the Young-type equation obtained in Section
4
to our wave equation (
1.6
) and obtain the existence and uniqueness of the solution under condition (
1.8
). Finally, in Appendix
A
, we provide details for some results used in the preceding sections.

2.
Weighted Besov spaces

Equation (
1.6
) will be solved in some properly weighted Besov spaces. In fact for a sharp analysis of the wave kernel properties (in Section
3
), we have found convenient to use the general setting introduced by Rychkov in
[
9
]
. This setting has the advantage to cover a large class of weights. The current section is mostly dedicated to recall the main elements of the latter article.

2.1.
Weighted Besov spaces

In this section we construct and give some basic properties of the weighted Besov spaces used for the wave equation.

Let us first recall the definition of the class
A_{p}^{\textnormal{loc}}
of weights which was introduced in
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

We call weight any locally integrable and strictly positive function
w:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Next for every
1<p<\infty
, we define
A^{\textnormal{loc}}_{p}
as the set of weights
w
for which

(2.1)

\|w\|_{p,\textnormal{loc}}:=\sup_{|I|\leq 1}\frac{1}{|I|^{p}}\bigg(\int_{I}w(x)\,dx\bigg)\bigg(\int_{I}w(x)^{-\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\ <\ \infty\,,

where
p^{\prime}
refers to the Hölder conjugate of
p
,
I=[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\dots\times[a_{d},b_{d}]
with
a_{i}<b_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,d
and
|I|=\prod_{i=1}^{d}|a_{i}-b_{i}|
.

As pointed out in
[
9
]
, this general class
A_{p}^{\textnormal{loc}}
of weights consists of the classical
A_{p}
weights (see e.g.
[
12
]
) and the locally regular weights that grow/decay at most exponentially at infinity. In particular, for
1<p<\infty
,
A_{p}^{\textnormal{loc}}
includes the following exponential and polynomial weights that we will use in this paper:

(2.2)

w_{\mu}(x):=e^{-\mu|x|}\quad\text{ for }\mu\in\mathbb{R},\quad\text{ and }\quad P(x):=\left(1+|x|^{d+1}\right)^{-1}\,.

For a generic weight
w
, we introduce the notion of weighted
L^{p}
space which will be at the heart of our analysis.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
w
be a weight function in the space
A_{p}^{\textnormal{loc}}
for
p>1
. For a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, we set

\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}\,w(x)\,dx\right)^{1/p}\,.

Whenever
w_{\mu}(x)=e^{-\mu|x|}
is an exponential weight, we write
L_{w_{\mu}}^{p}:=~L_{\mu}^{p}.

We also label a notation for functions with centered moments in the definition below.

Definition 2.3
.

For every integer
L\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{L}
the set of functions
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
such that for each multi-index
r
with
0\leq|r|\leq L
, one has

(2.3)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,x^{r}\varphi(x)\,dx=0\,.

The compatibility of weights with convolution products is a crucial and basic feature for a proper Besov analysis. We state and prove a lemma in this sense.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix two constants
\mu_{\ast},K>0
. Then for all
0\leq\mu\leq\mu_{\ast}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
such that
\text{Supp}(\varphi)\subset\mathbf{B}_{K}
, one has

(2.4)

\big\|\varphi\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim\big\||\varphi|\ast\big[w_{\mu}^{1/p}|f|\big]\big\|_{L^{p}}\,,

for some proportional constant that only depends on
p
,
K
and
\mu_{\ast}
. In particular, the following compatibility relation holds true:

(2.5)

\big\|\varphi\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}.

Proof.

We start by writing the
L_{\mu}^{p}
-norm of
\varphi\ast f
according to Definition
2.2
,

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,w_{\mu}(x)\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dy\,\varphi(x-y)f(y)\bigg|^{p}.

We now insert the weight
w_{\mu}(y)
in order to get

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dy\,\bigg[\bigg(\frac{w_{\mu}(x)}{w_{\mu}(y)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\varphi(x-y)\bigg]\Big[w_{\mu}(y)^{\frac{1}{p}}f(y)\Big]\bigg|^{p}

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dy\,\bigg|\bigg(\frac{w_{\mu}(x)}{w_{\mu}(y)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\varphi(x-y)\bigg|\Big|w_{\mu}(y)^{\frac{1}{p}}f(y)\Big|\bigg|^{p}\,.

Recall that
\text{Supp}(\varphi)
is a subset of
\mathbf{B}_{K}
. Hence for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have

\bigg|\bigg(\frac{w_{\mu}(x)}{w_{\mu}(y)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\varphi(x-y)\bigg|\leq e^{\mu K/p}|\varphi(x-y)|.

Plugging this inequality into (
2.6
) we immediately get (
2.4
), which ends our proof.
∎

In order to implement the partition of unity necessary for a proper definition of Besov spaces, we state a technical lemma about convolutions of rescaled functions.

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix an integer
L\geq 0
. Let
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
and suppose
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
, where we recall that
\mathcal{D}_{L}
is introduced in Definition
2.3
. Next for every
j\geq 1
, recall from Notation
1.2
that we have set
\varphi_{j}(x):=2^{dj}\varphi(2^{j}x)
,
\psi_{j}(x):=2^{dj}\psi(2^{j}x)
. Then for all
0\leq j\leq\ell
and
1\leq r\leq\infty
, it holds that

(2.7)

\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim 2^{dj\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}2^{-L(\ell-j)}\,.

Proof.

In order to ease our notation, let us agree on the following convention:

For the sake of clarity, we assume from now on that the supports of functions in
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
are all included in the unit ball
\mathbf{B}_{1}
.

We first prove inequality (
2.7
) for the case
1\leq r<\infty
. Writing the definition of the convolution product and performing the change of variables
2^{\ell}y\to y
and
2^{j}x\to x
, one gets

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{r}}^{r}

\displaystyle=\int dx\,\bigg|\int dy\,2^{dj}\varphi(2^{j}(x-y))2^{d\ell}\psi(2^{\ell}y)\bigg|^{r}

\displaystyle=2^{dj(r-1)}\int_{\mathbf{B}_{2}}dx\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbf{B}_{1}}dy\,\psi(y)\varphi(x-2^{-(\ell-j)}y)\bigg|^{r}.

Next invoke the fact that
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
in order to write

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{r}}^{r}

(2.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{dj(r-1)}\int_{\mathbf{B}_{2}}dx\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbf{B}_{1}}dy\,\psi(y)\bigg[\varphi(x-2^{-(\ell-j)}y)-\sum_{0\leq|k|\leq L-1}\frac{D^{k}\varphi(x)}{k!}(-2^{-(\ell-j)}y)^{k}\bigg]\bigg|^{r}\,.

Furthermore, thanks to classical considerations on remainders for Taylor expansions, for
x,y\in\mathbf{B}_{2}
, we have

(2.9)

\bigg|\varphi(x-2^{-(\ell-j)}y)-\sum_{0\leq|k|\leq L-1}\frac{D^{k}\varphi(x)}{k!}(-2^{-(\ell-j)}y)^{k}\bigg|\lesssim\,2^{-L(\ell-j)}.

Plugging (
2.9
) into (
2.1
), this proves (
2.7
) for
1\leq r<\infty
.

For the case
r=\infty
in (
2.7
), we have similarly

\displaystyle\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}(2^{-j}x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dy\,2^{dj}\varphi(2^{j}(2^{-j}x-y))2^{d\ell}\psi(2^{\ell}y)=2^{dj}\int_{\mathbf{B}_{1}}dy\,\psi(y)\varphi(x-2^{-(\ell-j)}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{dj}\int_{\mathbf{B}_{1}}dy\,\psi(y)\bigg[\varphi(x-2^{-(\ell-j)}y)-\sum_{0\leq|k|\leq L-1}\frac{D^{k}\varphi(x)}{k!}(-2^{-(\ell-j)}y)^{k}\bigg]\,,

and (
2.7
) follows from (
2.9
). This finishes the proof.
∎

Pick an arbitrary function
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}

Definition 2.7
.

Consider a function
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
and the corresponding

. For any fixed length

(

(2.10)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s,\mu}_{p,q}(\varphi_{0})}:=\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{jsq}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}^{q}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,.

φ

, all

(2.11)

2.2.
A few properties of the weighted Besov spaces

We now collect a few basic facts about Besov spaces, whose role for our existence and uniqueness result will be crucial.

First most of our weights
w
in the sequel are exponential. However, we also deal occasionally with polynomial weights. The lemma below gives a link between those classes.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\mu_{\ast}>0
be a fixed constant. Consider the polynomial weight
P(x)
and the family
\{w_{\mu},0<\mu\leq\mu_{\ast}\}
of exponential weights given in (
2.2
). Then, for
1<p<\infty,1<q\leq\infty
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s,w_{\mu}}_{p,q}}\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{d+1}{p}}\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s,P}_{p,q}}\,,

where the multiplicative constant on the right-hand side depends on
\mu_{\ast}
only.

Proof.

Invoking the fact that
0<\mu\leq\mu_{\ast}
, it is readily checked that

e^{-\mu|x|}\lesssim P(x)\bigg\{{\bf 1}_{\{|x|<1\}}+\frac{1}{\mu^{d+1}}{\bf 1}_{\{|x|\geq 1\}}\bigg\}\lesssim\frac{P(x)}{\mu^{d+1}}\big\{\mu_{\ast}^{d+1}+1\big\}\,.

Inserting this inequality into the definition (
2.10
) of Besov norms, our claim is achieved in a straightforward way.
∎

Interpolation inequalities are part of the basic toolkit of Besov spaces. In our weighted context, we can state the following result.

Lemma 2.11
.

Consider nine parameters
s,s_{0},s_{1}\in(-\infty,1]
,
p,p_{0},p_{1}\in(1,\infty)
and
q,q_{0},q_{1}\in(1,\infty]
. We suppose that there exists
\theta\in[0,1]
such that

s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}}\,,\qquad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}\,.

Then for every
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, it holds that

(2.12)

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s,\mu}_{p,q}}\leq\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s_{0},\mu}_{p_{0},q_{0}}}^{1-\theta}\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s_{1},\mu}_{p_{1},q_{1}}}^{\theta}\,.

Proof.

The proof is achieved by standard Hölder-type inequalities. It is left to the reader for the sake of conciseness.

∎

We next move to an embedding property which will be an important step in our analysis.

Lemma 2.12
.

We consider six parameters
1<p<p^{\prime}<\infty
,
-\infty<\kappa^{\prime}<\kappa\leq 1
and
0\leq\mu<\mu^{\prime}\leq\mu_{\ast}
. Assume that the following relation holds true,

(2.13)

\kappa=\kappa^{\prime}+d\bigg(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}\bigg)\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\mu^{\prime}=\frac{p^{\prime}}{p}\mu\,.

Then for the Besov spaces introduced in Definition
2.7
, it holds that

(2.14)

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},\infty}}\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu}_{p,\infty}}\,,

for some proportional constant that only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
(as far as weight parameters are concerned).

Proof.

Consider an integer
L\geq 1
, the exact value of which will be determined later on (namely we will see that
L=L(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})
depends on
(\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})
only). Recall that we have defined our Besov spaces based on a function
\varphi_{0}
as in Notation
2.9
. By Theorem
A.1
in the Appendix, we know that there exist
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
such that

(2.15)

\varphi_{j}\ast f=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}(\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell})\ast(\varphi_{\ell}\ast f)\,.

Observe that we have assumed the supports of the
\varphi
and
\psi
functions to be subsets of
\mathbf{B}_{1}
, and so we have
\text{Supp}\,(\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell})\subset\mathbf{B}_{2}
for all
j,\ell\geq 0
. Hence we can use (
2.4
) to assert that

\big\|(\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell})\ast(\varphi_{\ell}\ast f)\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime}}}\lesssim\big\||\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}|\ast|w_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{p}}(\varphi_{\ell}\ast f)|\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\,,

for some proportional constant that only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
. We can then invoke the classical Young inequality for convolution products to derive

(2.16)

\big\|(\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell})\ast(\varphi_{\ell}\ast f)\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime}}}\lesssim\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{r}}\big\|w_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{p}}(\varphi_{\ell}\ast f)\big\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{r}}\big\|\varphi_{\ell}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}

where
r
is defined through the relation
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}=1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
.
Going back to (
2.15
), we deduce that for every
j\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{r}}\big\|\varphi_{\ell}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu}_{p,\infty}}\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{-\kappa\ell}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{r}}\,.

We now split the sum above into
\ell\leq j
and
\ell>j
. We also assume
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
in Definition
2.7
. Then we apply Lemma
2.5
in two different ways according to those two cases (recall that (
2.7
) is valid for
j\leq\ell
). This yields,

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu}_{p,\infty}}\bigg[\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq j}2^{-\kappa\ell}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{r}}+\sum_{\ell>j}2^{-\kappa\ell}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\psi_{\ell}\big\|_{L^{r}}\bigg]\,,

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu}_{p,\infty}}\bigg[\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq j}2^{-\kappa\ell}2^{d\ell(1-\frac{1}{r})}2^{-L(j-\ell)}+2^{dj(1-\frac{1}{r})}\sum_{\ell>j}2^{-\kappa\ell}2^{-L(\ell-j)}\bigg]\,.

Performing the change of variables
j-\ell\to\ell
and
\ell-j\to\ell
in the two sums above, we thus get

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu}_{p,\infty}}\bigg[2^{-j(\kappa-d(1-\frac{1}{r}))}\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq j}2^{-(L-\kappa+d(1-\frac{1}{r}))\ell}+2^{-j(\kappa-d(1-\frac{1}{r}))}\sum_{\ell>0}2^{-(L+\kappa)\ell}\bigg]\,.

Now remember that
1-\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
, so that
\kappa-d(1-\frac{1}{r})=\kappa^{\prime}
according to (
2.13
). We thus have obtained that

(2.17)

2^{\kappa^{\prime}j}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu}_{p,\infty}}\bigg[\sum_{0\leq\ell\leq j}2^{-(L-\kappa^{\prime})\ell}+\sum_{\ell>0}2^{-(L+\kappa)\ell}\bigg]\,.

Let us now pick
L>\max(-\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})
in (
2.17
). We have obtained

2^{\kappa^{\prime}j}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu}_{p,\infty}}\,,

where the propotional constant depends on
(L,\kappa,\kappa^{\prime})
only. To conclude, we simply recall that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime},\infty}}=\sup_{j\geq 0}2^{\kappa^{\prime}j}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mu^{\prime}}}\,,

from which our claim is easily deduced.
∎

In order to handle the wave equation (
1.6
), we will have to multiply the candidate solution
u
with the distribution-valued noise
\dot{W}
. The lemma below ensures a proper definition of this kind of product in the Young sense. Its proof is deferred to the Appendix (Section
A.2
).

Lemma 2.13
.

Fix
\mu_{\ast}>0
. Let
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
,
p,p_{1},p_{2}\in(1,\infty)
and
\mu,\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in[0,\mu_{\ast}]
be such that

(2.18)

\alpha<\beta,\qquad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\mu}{p}=\frac{\mu_{1}}{p_{1}}+\frac{\mu_{2}}{p_{2}}\,.

Consider
f\in\mathcal{B}_{p_{1},\infty}^{-\alpha,\mu_{1}}
and
g\in\mathcal{B}_{p_{2},\infty}^{\beta,\mu_{2}}
. Then it holds that

(2.19)

\big\|f\cdot g\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\mu}_{p,\infty}}\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\mu_{1}}_{p_{1},\infty}}\big\|g\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\beta,\mu_{2}}_{p_{2},\infty}}\,,

for some proportional constant that only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
.

We close this section by rephrasing relation (
2.19
) in a way which turns out to be more convenient for our later computations.

Corollary 2.14
.

Fix
\mu_{\ast}>0
. Consider some parameters
\mu,\nu\in[0,\mu_{\ast}],\alpha,\kappa\in(0,1]
and
p>2
such that

0<\mu-\nu\leq\frac{2\mu_{\ast}}{p}\,,\qquad\nu\leq\mu_{\ast}\Big(1-\frac{2}{p}\Big)\,,\qquad\kappa>\alpha+\frac{d}{p}\,.

Then setting
\varepsilon:=\frac{p}{2}(\mu-\nu)\in(0,\mu_{\ast}]
, we have

(2.20)

\big\|f\cdot g\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\mu}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\nu}_{2,\infty}}\big\|g\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}}\,,

for some proportional constant that only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
.

Proof.

Set
\bar{\kappa}:=\kappa-\frac{d}{p}
, and consider
r>2
such that
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{2}
. We also set
\bar{\nu}:=\frac{r}{2}\nu\leq\mu_{\ast}
. Since
\bar{\kappa}>\alpha
, we can first apply Lemma
2.13
to assert that

\big\|f\cdot g\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\mu}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\bar{\kappa},\bar{\nu}}_{r,\infty}}\big\|g\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\varepsilon}_{p,\infty}}\,.

It now only remains to apply Lemma
2.12
, which yields
\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\bar{\kappa},\bar{\nu}}_{r,\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\nu}_{2,\infty}}
.
∎

3.
Smoothing effect of the wave kernel

The wave kernel plays a fundamental role in the mild formulation of (
1.6
). In this section, we investigate Strichartz type inequalities for this kernel in the weighted Besov spaces defined in Section
2
.

3.1.
Definition of the wave kernel

In the sequel, we will write
\mathcal{G}_{t}
for the wave operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, generated by
\partial^{2}_{tt}-\Delta
. The corresponding kernel will be denoted by
G_{t}
. For
d=1,2
, the kernel
G_{t}
has the following explicit expression:

(3.1)

G_{t}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}{\bf 1}_{[|x|<t]}&\text{ if }d=1,\\
\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}{\bf 1}_{[|x|<t]}&\text{ if }d=2.\end{array}\right.

It is often easier to express
G_{t}
in Fourier modes. Namely for a function
g(t,x)
we set
\mathcal{F}g(t,\xi)
or
\hat{g}(t,\xi)
for the spatial Fourier transform, defined by

\hat{g}(t,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\imath x\cdot\xi}g(t,x)dt\,.

Then the expression for the Fourier transform of
G_{t}
is

(3.2)

\hat{G}_{t}(\xi)=\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\,,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

3.2.
Strichartz type estimates

The aim of this section is to quantify the smoothing effects of the operator introduced in Section
3.1
. We start by two propositions giving some information about the behavior in time. Note that we are restricted to a spatial dimension
d=1,2
for this section. We first state a regularity estimate in dimension 1.

Proposition 3.1
.

Assume
d=1
. Let
\mu_{\ast}>0
be a given constant, and recall that
\mathcal{G}_{t}
designates the wave operator. Consider
\mu\in[0,\mu_{\ast}]
and
0\leq s<t\leq 1
. Then, for all
f\in L_{\mu}^{p}
, we have

(3.3)

\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim|t-s|\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\,,

where the proportional constant only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
and
p
. As a consequence, for all
-\infty<\alpha\leq 1
and
1<p,q\leq\infty
,

(3.4)

\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}\lesssim|t-s|\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}\,,

where the proportional constant only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
,
p
and
q
.

Proof.

As in the proof Lemma
2.4
, we write Definition
2.2
for the
L^{p}
norm and recall that
\mathcal{G}_{t}
admits the kernel
G_{t}
given by (
3.1
). We get

\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\,w_{\mu}(x)\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy\,\{G_{t}-G_{s}\}(x-y)f(y)\bigg|^{p}.

Still like in Lemma
2.4
, we insert the weight
w_{\mu}(y)
and invoke the fact that
\text{Supp}\,(G_{t}-G_{s})\subset[-2,2]
.
This yields

\displaystyle\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy\,\bigg(\frac{w_{\mu}(x)}{w_{\mu}(y)}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\{G_{t}-G_{s}\}(x-y)\big[w_{\mu}(y)^{\frac{1}{p}}f(y)\big]\bigg|^{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx\,\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy\,\big|\{G_{t}-G_{s}\}(x-y)\big|\big|w_{\mu}(y)^{\frac{1}{p}}f(y)\big|\bigg|^{p}\,,

where the proportional constant only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
and
p
.

Taking into account the expression (
3.1
)
for
d=1
, it is readily checked that

(3.5)

\|G_{t}-G_{s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq|t-s|\,.

Therefore, a direct application of Young’s inequality (see also relation (
2.5
)) leads to

(3.6)

\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}^{p}\lesssim\|G_{t}-G_{s}\|_{L^{1}}^{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}^{p}\lesssim|t-s|^{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}^{p}\,,

which proves our claim (
3.3
).

As for (
3.4
), it now suffices to observe that for every
\ell\geq 0
,

2^{\ell\alpha}\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\ell}\ast\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}=2^{\ell\alpha}\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\ell}\ast f)\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim 2^{\ell\alpha}|t-s|\|\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{\ell}\ast f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\,.

Inserting this expression into the definition (
2.10
) of Besov norm, the desired inequality is achieved.
∎

We now turn to the corresponding property in the 2-dimensional case. We start with a lemma which summarizes the distinction between the 1-d and the 2-d cases. To get a quick understanding of this, recall from (
3.5
) that for
d=1

\|G_{t}-G_{s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq|t-s|\,,

while the same result for
d=2
reads

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume
d=2
and consider the kernel
G_{t}
defined by (
3.1
). Then for all
0\leq s<t\leq 1
, we have

(3.7)

\big\|G_{t}-G_{s}\big\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Proof.

According to relation (
3.1
), for
d=2
we have

\displaystyle\big\|G_{t}-G_{s}\big\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}dx\,\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}{\bf 1}_{\{[x|<t\}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}-|x|^{2}}}{\bf 1}_{\{[x|<s\}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s<|x|<t}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}+\int_{|x|<s}dx\,\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}-|x|^{2}}}\bigg|\,.

Next we use a polar change of coordinates and set
r:=r^{2}
in order to get

\displaystyle\big\|G_{t}-G_{s}\big\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s}^{t}\frac{r\,dr}{\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}}+\int_{0}^{s}dr\,r\,\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-r^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}-r^{2}}}\bigg|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s^{2}}^{t^{2}}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{t^{2}-r}}+\int_{0}^{s^{2}}dr\,\bigg|\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-r}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}-r}}\bigg|\,.

Furthermore, since
s<t
we have
(s^{2}-r)^{-1/2}>(t^{2}-r)^{-1/2}
for
r\in[0,s^{2}]
. This yields

\displaystyle\big\|G_{t}-G_{s}\big\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{s^{2}}^{t^{2}}\frac{dr}{\sqrt{t^{2}-r}}+\int_{0}^{s^{2}}dr\,\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}-r}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-r}}\bigg)\,.

We now simply integrate the right-hand side above in order to get (recall that we assume
s,t\in[0,1]
for this lemma)

\|G_{t}-G_{s}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\Big((t+s)^{\frac{1}{2}}-(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,,

which proves our claim (
3.7
).
∎

As a direct consequence, we have the following result parallel to Proposition
3.1
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume
d=2
. Let
\mu_{\ast}>0
be a given constant, and recall that
\mathcal{G}_{t}
designates the wave operator. Consider
\mu\in[0,\mu_{\ast}]
and
0\leq s<t\leq 1
. Then, for all
f\in L_{\mu}^{p}
, we have

(3.8)

\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\,,

where the proportional constant only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
and
p
. As a consequence, for all
-\infty<\alpha\leq 1
and
1<p,q\leq\infty
,

(3.9)

\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}\lesssim|t-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}\,,

where the proportional constant only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
,
p
and
q
.

Proof.

The proof follows exactly that of Proposition
3.1
, except for replacing (
3.5
) by relation (
3.7
).
∎

We now present our main result for this section, quantifying the smoothing effect of
\mathcal{G}_{t}
in our Besov scale.

Proposition 3.4
.

In this proposition the dimension parameter takes the values
1
or
2
. As in Proposition
3.1
, we consider a constant
\mu_{\ast}>0
and the wave operator
\mathcal{G}_{t}
. Let
\alpha,\mu,p,q
be four parameters with

(3.10)

-\infty<\alpha\leq 0,\qquad 0\leq\mu\leq\mu_{\ast},\qquad 1<p<\infty,\qquad 1<q\leq\infty.

Recall that the weighted Besov spaces are introduced in Definition
2.7
. Then for all
t\in[0,1]
, it holds that

(3.11)

\big\|\mathcal{G}_{t}f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha+\rho_{d},\mu}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\mu}}\,,\text{ with }\rho_{d}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ d=1\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if}\ d=2\end{cases}\,,

and where the proportional constant only depends on
p
,
q
and
\mu_{\ast}
.

Before starting the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we label a technical lemma about integrals of the wave kernel.

Lemma 3.5
.

In the setting of Proposition
3.4
, and for all
t\in[0,1]
,
j\geq 0
,
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define the quantity

(3.12)

\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}(y):=2^{j\rho_{d}}\int_{\mathbf{B}_{2}}dz\,\big|G_{t}(y-2^{-j}z)-G_{t}(y)\big|\,.

Then it holds that

(3.13)

\text{Supp}\,\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}\subset\mathbf{B}_{t+2},\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{j\geq 0}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\big\|\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}\big\|_{L^{1}}\,<\infty.

Proof.

See Appendix.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

The presence of a
2^{j\rho_{d}}
term in front of the integral in (
3.12
) is what will guarantee a smoothing effect from
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha}
to a
\mathcal{B}^{\alpha+\rho_{d}}
space.

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

We begin this proof like in Lemma
2.12
. Namely we introduce a parameter
L\geq 0
whose exact value is to be determined later on. Then we assume that the construction of our weighted Besov spaces hinges on a function
\varphi_{0}
such that
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
(where
\varphi
is defined along Notation
2.6
).

Thanks to Theorem
A.1
in the Appendix, we know that there exist
two functions
\psi_{0},\psi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
such that
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
and for every
\ell\geq 1

we have

(3.14)

\varphi_{\ell}\ast\mathcal{G}_{t}f=\varphi_{\ell}\ast G_{t}\ast f=\sum_{j\geq 0}(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})\ast(\varphi_{j}\ast f)\,,

where we recall that
G_{t}
is the wave kernel given by (
3.1
).
In relation (
3.14
), recall that we have assumed the supports of
\varphi_{0}
,
\varphi
,
\psi
and
\psi_{0}
to be all included in the unit ball
\mathbf{B}_{1}
. However, the arguments could be easily extended to any supporting ball
\mathbf{B}_{K}
.
Let us now split the above sum (
3.14
) into

(3.15)

\sum_{j\geq 0}(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})\ast(\varphi_{j}\ast f)=\sum_{0\leq j<\ell}(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})\ast(\varphi_{j}\ast f)+(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{\ell})\ast(\varphi_{\ell}\ast f)\\
+\sum_{j>\ell}(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})\ast(\varphi_{j}\ast f):=I_{\ell}+I\!I_{\ell}+I\!I\!I_{\ell}\,.

Our inequality (
3.11
) is now easily seen to be reduced to prove

(3.16)

\bigg(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})q}\left(\|I_{\ell}\|^{q}_{L_{\mu}^{p}}+\|I\!I_{\ell}\|^{q}_{L_{\mu}^{p}}+\|I\!I\!I_{\ell}\|^{q}_{L_{\mu}^{p}}\right)\bigg)^{1/q}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\mu}}\,.

We will handle the 3 terms on the left-hand side of (
3.16
) separately.

Step 1: Bound on
I_{\ell}
. With (
3.11
) and (
3.16
) in mind, our aim is to prove an inequality of the form

(3.17)

\bigg(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})q}\|I_{\ell}\|_{L_{\mu}^{p}}^{q}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\mu}}\,.

Our first claim is that for
0\leq j<\ell
we can write

(3.18)

\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j}=(\bar{\psi}\ast\varphi_{\ell-j})_{j}\,,\quad\text{where}\quad\bar{\psi}:=\begin{cases}\psi_{0}&\text{if}\ j=0\\
\psi&\text{if}\ j\geq 1\end{cases}\,.

Indeed, relation (
3.18
) is trivial whenever
j=0
(note that we have used the convention
g_{0}=g
for any given
g\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
in the right-hand side of (
3.18
)). For
j\geq 1
, the left-hand side of (
3.18
) can be expressed as

(3.19)

(\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})(x)=2^{\ell+j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(2^{\ell}(x-y))\psi(2^{j}y)dy\,,

while the right-hand side of (
3.18
) has the form

(3.20)

(\varphi_{\ell-j}\ast\psi)_{j}(x)=2^{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(2^{\ell}x-2^{\ell-j}y)\psi(y)dy\,.

Then an elementary change of variable
z=2^{-j}y
shows that (
3.19
) and (
3.20
) are equal.

Invoking (
3.18
), together with the change of variable
2^{j}z\to z
, it is then readily checked that for
0\leq j<\ell
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
the term
G_{t}*\varphi_{\ell}*\psi_{j}
in (
3.15
) can be decomposed as

(3.21)

(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\,(\bar{\psi}\ast\varphi_{\ell-j})_{j}(z)G_{t}(y-z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\,(\bar{\psi}\ast\varphi_{\ell-j})(z)G_{t}(y-2^{-j}z)\,.

Let us take advantage of some cancellations in the right-hand of (
3.21
). Indeed, owing to the fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi_{k}(x)dx=0
for all
k>0
, we also have

G_{t}(y)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\,(\bar{\psi}\ast\varphi_{k})(z)=0.

Subtracting the above quantity to the right-hand side of (
3.21
), we get that for all
\ell>j
,

\displaystyle(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\,(\bar{\psi}\ast\varphi_{\ell-j})(z)\big\{G_{t}(y-2^{-j}z)-G_{t}(y)\big\}.

In addition, observing
\text{Supp}\,(\bar{\psi}\ast\varphi_{\ell-j})\subset\mathbf{B}_{2}
, we have that

(3.22)

\displaystyle(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{B}_{2}}dz\,(\bar{\psi}\ast\varphi_{\ell-j})(z)\big\{G_{t}(y-2^{-j}z)-G_{t}(y)\big\}\,.

With (
3.22
) in hand, the key point now is that due to (
2.3
) one has easily, for all
0\leq j<\ell
,

(3.23)

\|\bar{\psi}\ast\varphi_{\ell-j}\|_{\infty}\lesssim 2^{-L(\ell-j)}\,,

which follows from Lemma
2.5
directly. Therefore, using the notation introduced in (
3.12
), we have obtained that

(3.24)

|(G_{t}*\varphi_{\ell}*\psi_{j})(y)|\leq 2^{-L(\ell-j)}2^{-j\rho_{d}}\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}(y)\,.

Let us insert (
3.24
) in
I_{\ell}
in order to upper bound this term. That is according to the definition (
3.15
) of
I_{\ell}
, it is obvious that

\displaystyle|I_{\ell}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq j<\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dy\,|(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})(y)||(\varphi_{j}\ast f)(x-y)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq j<\ell}2^{-L(\ell-j)}2^{-j\rho_{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dy\,\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}(y)|(\varphi_{j}\ast f)(x-y)|

(3.25)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq j<\ell}2^{-L(\ell-j)}2^{-j\rho_{d}}\big[\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}\ast|\varphi_{j}\ast f|\big](x).

We are now ready to analyze the weighted
L^{p}
norms of the function
I_{\ell}
. To this aim, we multiply (
3.25
) by
2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}
and compute the
L^{p}_{\mu}
-norm given in Definition
2.2
for
\mu\leq\mu_{\ast}
. We get

\displaystyle 2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}\|I_{\ell}\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{0\leq j<\ell}2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}2^{-L(\ell-j)}2^{-j\rho_{d}}2^{-j\alpha}\Big[2^{j\alpha}\big\|\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}\ast|\varphi_{j}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\Big]

(3.26)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{0\leq j<\ell}2^{(\ell-j)(\alpha+\rho_{d}-L)}\Big[2^{j\alpha}\big\|\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}\ast|\varphi_{j}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\Big].

At this point, we can combine the results of Lemma
2.4
, Lemma
3.5
and Young’s inequality to assert that

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}\ast|\varphi_{j}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\||\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}|\ast w_{\mu}^{1/p}|\varphi_{j}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,j}\big\|_{L^{1}}\big\|w_{\mu}^{1/p}|\varphi_{j}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\,,

for some proportional constant that depends neither on
j
nor on
t
. Injecting this uniform bound into (
3.26
), we deduce

(3.28)

2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}\|I_{\ell}\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim\sum_{0\leq j<\ell}2^{(\ell-j)(\alpha+\rho_{d}-L)}\Big[2^{j\alpha}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\Big]\,.

We shall recast the right-hand side of (
3.28
) as a discrete convolution. Namely, if we set

u_{j}:=2^{j(\alpha+\rho_{d}-L)}{\bf 1}_{\{j\geq 1\}}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad v_{j}:=2^{j\alpha}\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}{\bf 1}_{\{j\geq 0\}}\,,

and if
\ast_{\mathbb{Z}}
refers to the discrete convolution in
\mathbb{Z}
, then (
3.28
) can be written as

(3.29)

2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}\|I_{\ell}\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim(u\ast_{\mathbb{Z}}v)_{\ell}\,.

The inequality above enables the application of Young’s inequality, similarly to what we did in (
2.16
). We end up with

(3.30)

\displaystyle\big\|\ell\mapsto 2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}\|I_{\ell}\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\big\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|j\mapsto 2^{j(\alpha+\rho_{d}-L)}\big\|_{\ell^{1}(\mathbb{N})}\,\big\|j\mapsto 2^{j\alpha}\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\big\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})}\,.

Let us finally pick
L>\rho_{d}+\alpha
, so that the above estimate yields

(3.31)

\bigg(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})q}\|I_{\ell}\|^{q}_{L_{\mu}^{p}}\bigg)^{1/q}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}\,,

which is our claim (
3.17
) and the first part of relation (
3.16
).

Step 2: Bound on
I\!I_{\ell}
. Recall from (
3.15
) that
I\!I_{\ell}=(G_{t}*\varphi_{\ell}*\psi_{\ell})*(\varphi_{\ell}*f)
. Our aim for this step is to obtain inequality (
3.31
) for the sequence
\{I\!I_{\ell},\ell\geq 0\}
. Whenever
\ell=0
, we appeal again to Lemma
2.4
in order to write

\displaystyle\|I\!I_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|G_{t}\ast\varphi_{0}\ast\psi_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\|\varphi_{0}\ast f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim\|G_{t}\|_{L^{1}}\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\|\varphi_{0}\ast f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\,.

Furthermore,
\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{1}},\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
above are finite quantities. Since
t\leq 1
, the norm
\|G_{t}\|_{L^{1}}
is also bounded, along the same lines as for (
3.3
). We thus get

(3.32)

\displaystyle\|I\!I_{0}\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim\|\varphi_{0}\ast f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\,,

where the proportional constant only depends on
p
and
\mu_{\ast}
.

For
\ell\geq 1
, observe that similarly to (
3.18
) we have
\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{\ell}=(\varphi\ast\psi)_{\ell}
. We can then use the same arguments as in (
3.21
)-(
3.24
) (replace
\varphi_{\ell-j}
with
\varphi
,
\bar{\psi}
with
\psi
and
j
with
\ell
) to derive that for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\big|(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{\ell})(y)\big|\lesssim\|\varphi\ast\psi\|_{\infty}2^{-\ell\rho_{d}}\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,\ell}(y)\,.

Just as in Step 1 (see (
3.26
)-(
3.2
)), this entails that for
\ell\geq 1
,

2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}\|I\!I_{\ell}\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim 2^{\ell\alpha}\|\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,\ell}\|_{L^{1}}\|\varphi_{\ell}\ast f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim 2^{\ell\alpha}\|\varphi_{\ell}\ast f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}

for some proportional constant that only depends on
p
and
\mu_{\ast}
. Summing the above inequality over
\ell
and applying Definition
2.7
, we have shown the second part of relation (
3.16
), that is

(3.33)

\bigg(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})q}\|I\!I_{\ell}\|^{q}_{L_{\mu}^{p}}\bigg)^{1/q}\lesssim\bigg(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{\ell\alpha q}\|\varphi_{\ell}\ast f\|^{q}_{L_{\mu}^{p}}\bigg)^{1/q}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha,\mu}}\,.

Step 3: Bound on
I\!I\!I_{\ell}
. We now treat the term
I\!I\!I_{\ell}
in (
3.15
). This will be done along the same lines as for the other steps, and we thus skip some details of computation. First, since
I\!I\!I_{\ell}
involves a sum over
j>\ell
, we replace (
3.18
) by

\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j}=(\bar{\varphi}\ast\psi_{j-\ell})_{\ell}\,,\ \text{where}\ \bar{\varphi}:=\begin{cases}\varphi_{0}&\text{if}\ \ell=0\\
\varphi&\text{if}\ \ell\geq 1\end{cases}\,.

Thus, for all
j>\ell
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
, the equivalent of (
3.21
) is

(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\,(\bar{\varphi}\ast\psi_{j-\ell})_{\ell}(z)G_{t}(y-z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dz\,(\bar{\varphi}\ast\psi_{j-\ell})(z)G_{t}(y-2^{-\ell}z)\,.

Just as in Step 1, it holds that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}dz\,(\bar{\varphi}\ast\psi_{j-\ell})(z)=0
and
\text{Supp}\,(\bar{\varphi}\ast\psi_{j-\ell})\subset\mathbf{B}_{2}
for all
j>\ell
. Accordingly, relation (
3.22
) for the case
j>\ell
becomes

\displaystyle(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{B}_{2}}dz\,(\bar{\varphi}\ast\psi_{j-\ell})(z)\big\{G_{t}(y-2^{-\ell}z)-G_{t}(y)\big\}\,.

Next using the notation in (
3.12
), we obtain that for all
j>\ell
and
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\big|(G_{t}\ast\varphi_{\ell}\ast\psi_{j})(y)\big|\lesssim\|\bar{\varphi}\ast\psi_{j-\ell}\|_{\infty}2^{-\ell\rho_{d}}\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,\ell}(y)\,.

In addition for the same reasons as for (
3.23
) and owing to the fact that
\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{L}
, we have

\|\bar{\varphi}\ast\psi_{j-\ell}\|_{\infty}\lesssim 2^{-L(j-\ell)}\,.

Following the arguments in (
3.26
)-(
3.28
), we derive

\displaystyle 2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}\|I\!I\!I_{\ell}\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j>\ell}2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}2^{-L(j-\ell)}2^{-\ell\rho_{d}}2^{-j\alpha}\Big[2^{j\alpha}\big\|\mathcal{K}^{(d)}_{t,\ell}\ast|\varphi_{j}\ast f|\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\Big]

(3.34)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j>\ell}2^{-(L+\alpha)(j-\ell)}\Big[2^{j\alpha}\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\Big]\lesssim\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-(L+\alpha)j}\Big[2^{(j+\ell)\alpha}\big\|\varphi_{j+\ell}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\Big]\,.

At this point, remember that the pair
(L,\varphi_{0})
has been arbitrarily chosen. Therefore, we can a posteriori pick
(L,\varphi_{0})
such that
L>\max(-\alpha,\rho_{d}+\alpha)
(which is also consistent with the conditions exhibited in Step 1). Going back to (
3.34
), this puts us in a position to apply Jensen inequality and assert that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})q}\|I\!I\!I_{\ell}\|^{q}_{L_{\mu}^{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-(L+\alpha)j}\Big[2^{(j+\ell)\alpha}\big\|\varphi_{j+\ell}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\Big]\bigg)^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\sum_{j\geq 1}2^{-(L+\alpha)j}\Big[2^{(j+\ell)\alpha}\big\|\varphi_{j+\ell}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\Big]^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\ell\geq 0}\Big[2^{\ell\alpha}\big\|\varphi_{\ell}\ast f\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\Big]^{q}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}^{q}.

Accordingly, we have obtained

(3.35)

\bigg(\sum_{\ell\geq 0}2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})q}\|I\!I\!I_{\ell}\|^{q}_{L_{\mu}^{p}}\bigg)^{1/q}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}.

Step 4: Conclusion.
By injecting (
3.31
), (
3.33
) and (
3.35
) into (
3.14
)-(
3.15
), we deduce that for some suitable
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}
satisfying Assumption (

A
φ

),

\big\|\ell\mapsto 2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}\|\varphi_{\ell}\ast\mathcal{G}_{t}f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\big\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}\,.

We are therefore in a position to apply the definition (
2.10
) of Besov norm and assert that

\|\mathcal{G}_{t}f\|_{B^{\alpha+\rho_{d},\mu}_{p,q}(\varphi_{0})}=\big\|\ell\mapsto 2^{\ell(\alpha+\rho_{d})}\|\varphi_{\ell}\ast\mathcal{G}_{t}f\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\big\|_{\ell^{q}(\mathbb{N})}\lesssim\|f\|_{B^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}(\varphi_{0})}\,.

The desired inequality (
3.11
) is now a straightforward consequence of the equivalence property stated in Proposition
2.8
.
∎

We close this section by combining the previous results in order to obtain an appropriate regularity result for the operator
\mathcal{G}_{t}
.

Corollary 3.7
.

We work under the same conditions as for Proposition
3.4
. Namely, consider the wave operator
\mathcal{G}_{t}
, a constant
\mu_{\ast}>0
, and set

\rho_{d}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ d=1\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if}\ d=2.\end{cases}

Let
\alpha,\mu,p,q
be parameters such that (
3.10
) is fulfilled. Let
\kappa
be an additional coefficient in
[0,\rho_{d}]
. Then it holds that for all
0\leq s<t\leq 1
,

(3.36)

\|\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha+\kappa,\mu}_{p,q}}\lesssim|t-s|^{\rho_{d}-\kappa}\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}\,,

for some proportional constant that depends only on
\mu_{\ast}
.

Proof.

The proof of this result, which is a standard application of interpolation properties, is included here for the sake of completeness. Indeed, write
\alpha+\kappa=(1-\frac{\kappa}{\rho_{d}})\alpha+\frac{\kappa}{\rho_{d}}(\alpha+\rho_{d})
. Then apply inequality (
2.12
) with
s_{0}=\alpha,s_{1}=\alpha+\rho_{d},\theta=\frac{\kappa}{\rho_{d}},p_{0}=p_{1}=p,q_{0}=q_{1}=q.
We obtain

(3.37)

\|(\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s})f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha+\kappa,\mu}_{p,q}}\leq\|(\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s})f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}^{1-\frac{\kappa}{\rho_{d}}}\|(\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s})f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha+\rho_{d},\mu}_{p,q}}^{\frac{\kappa}{\rho_{d}}}\,.

Next, for the first term on the right-hand side of (
3.37
) we apply (
3.4
) (for
d=1
) or (
3.8
) (for
d=2
) , while for the second term we invoke (
3.11
) to write

\|(\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s})f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha+\rho_{d},\mu}}\leq\|\mathcal{G}_{t}f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha+\rho_{d},\mu}}+\|\mathcal{G}_{s}f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{\alpha+\rho_{d},\mu}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\alpha,\mu}_{p,q}}\,.

Going back to (
3.37
), our conclusion easily follows.
∎

4.
Young wave equation

This section aims to develop the Young-type integration theory that will allow us to interpret and solve (
1.6
) pathwisely in Section
5
. For this goal, we will introduce an appropriate space of processes and define the Young wave integral in Section
4.1
. Then we will prove the wellposedness of the corresponding Young wave equation in Section
4.2
.

4.1.
Young wave integral

This subsection is devoted to introducing a convenient space of processes
u
, allowing the definition of noisy integrals weighted by the wave operator. We start by giving a new piece of notation.

Notation 4.1
.

Throughout the section we fix two parameters
a\geq 0
and
b>0
. We then set
\mu_{t}=a+bt
.

Next we introduce the set of space-time functions which will allow a proper integration theory in our noisy context.

Definition 4.2
.

Fix
T>0
and let
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be a time-dependent weight as in Notation
4.1
. Consider two parameters
\gamma,\kappa\in[0,1]
. Then we define
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)
as the set of functions
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
for which the following norm is finite:

(4.1)

\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}:=\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u_{s}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{s}}_{2,\infty}}+\sup_{0\leq s<t\leq T}\frac{\|u_{t}-u_{s}\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}}{|t-s|^{\gamma}}\,.

For processes
u
in a space of the form
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}
, let us define some Riemann sum approximation of the integral
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x,y)u_{s}(y)\dot{W}(ds,dy).
This definition is given below.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
P(x)=(1+|x|^{2})^{-1}
be the polynomial given in (
2.2
) for
d=1
. We consider two regularity parameters
\alpha,\theta\in(0,1)
and an integrability parameter
p>2.
Let
\dot{W}
be a noisy input which is
\theta
-Hölder continuous in time with values in the weighted space
\mathcal{B}_{p,\infty}^{-\alpha,P}
; that is
\dot{W}\in\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty}).
In addition, let
\Pi^{n}
be the regular dyadic partition of
[0,T]
, whose generic element is
t_{m}^{n}=mT/2^{n}
for all
0\leq m\leq 2^{n}
. For
u
regular enough we define the following Riemann sum based on
\Pi^{n}
,

(4.2)

\mathcal{J}^{(n)}_{t}:=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{G}_{t-t^{n}_{k}}(u_{t^{n}_{k}}\,\delta\dot{W}_{t^{n}_{k}t^{n}_{k+1}}),~\mbox{ for }t\in(t^{n}_{m-1},t^{n}_{m}]\,,

where we recall that
\delta\dot{W}_{t^{n}_{k}t^{n}_{k+1}}
stands for
\dot{W}_{t^{n}_{k+1}}-\dot{W}_{t^{n}_{k}}
.

We now state a proposition giving natural conditions such that the Riemann sums (
4.2
) converge in the Young sense. This result will be the basic brick towards a proper wellposedness of equation (
1.6
). For this statement we set, just as in Corollary
3.7
,

(4.3)

\rho_{d}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ d=1\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if}\ d=2.\end{cases}

Proposition 4.4
.

Fix two times
0<T\leq T_{0}
. Let
\gamma,\theta
be two time regularity parameters and
\kappa,\alpha
be two space regularity parameters. Also consider
p>d+1
. We assume the following conditions.

(i)

The coefficients
\gamma,\theta,\kappa,\alpha
all sit in the interval
[0,1]
, and we have

(4.4)

\kappa+\alpha+\gamma+(1-\rho_{d})<\theta\,,\quad\gamma+\theta>1\,,\quad\kappa>\alpha+\frac{d}{p}\,,\quad\gamma<1-\frac{d+1}{p}\,.

(ii)

The process
u
is an element of
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)
as introduced in Definition
4.2
, and
\dot{W}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})
.

Let now
\{\mathcal{J}^{(n)};n\geq 1\}
be the sequence defined by (
4.2
). Then we have that
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
converges in
\mathcal{E}_{2,\infty}^{\gamma,\kappa}(T)
. We denote

(4.5)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{J}_{t}^{(n)}=:\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{t-r}(u_{r}\,d\dot{W}_{r})\,.

Moreover, the Young integral (
4.5
) verifies

(4.6)

\bigg\|\int_{0}^{.}\mathcal{G}_{.-r}(u_{r}\,d\dot{W}_{r})\bigg\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}\leq\big\|\mathcal{G}_{.}(u_{0}\,(\dot{W}_{T}-\dot{W}_{0}))\big\|_{\mathcal{E}_{2,\infty}^{\gamma,\kappa}(T)}+c_{T_{0}}\|\dot{W}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})}\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}\,,

where
c_{T_{0}}>0
does not depend on
T
,
u
and
\dot{W}
.

Remark 4.5
.

As the reader will see, we will in fact prove a slightly stronger version of relation (
4.6
): there exists a
finite
constant
q=q(\gamma,\kappa,\theta,\alpha,p)\geq 1
such that

(4.7)

\begin{split}&\bigg\|\int_{0}^{.}\mathcal{G}_{.-r}(u_{r}\,d\dot{W}_{r})\bigg\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}\\
&\leq\big\|\mathcal{G}_{.}(u_{0}\,(\dot{W}_{T}-\dot{W}_{0}))\big\|_{\mathcal{E}_{2,\infty}^{\gamma,\kappa}(T)}+c_{T_{0}}\|\dot{W}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(r)}^{q}\,dr\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,\end{split}

where
c_{T_{0}}>0
does not depend on
T
,
u
and
\dot{W}
. Inequality (
4.7
) will be crucial in our fixed point argument for the existence-uniqueness of a solution to (
1.6
).

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

We shall prove the convergence of
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
thanks to an upper bound on the difference
\mathcal{J}^{(n+1)}-\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
. To this aim, let us denote
\delta f_{st}
for the increment
f_{t}-f_{s}
of a function
f
. Furthermore, for the sake of clarity, consider two dyadic points
s=t^{n}_{\ell}
and
t=t^{n}_{m}
for
\ell\leq m
. Then an elementary manipulation on the expression (
4.2
) for
\mathcal{J}^{(n)}
reveals that

(4.8)

\delta\mathcal{J}_{st}^{(n+1)}-\delta\mathcal{J}_{st}^{(n)}=I^{(n)}_{st}+I\!I^{(n)}_{st}+I\!I\!I^{(n)}_{st}+I\!V^{(n)}_{st}\,,

where the terms
I^{(n)}_{st},\dots,I\!V^{(n)}_{st}
are defined by (writing
t_{j}=t_{j}^{n+1}
for notational sake),

\displaystyle I^{(n)}_{st}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=\ell}^{m-1}\mathcal{G}_{t-t_{2k}}\big(\delta u_{t_{2k}t_{2k+1}}\,\delta\dot{W}_{t_{2k+1}t_{2k+2}}\big);

\displaystyle I\!I^{(n)}_{st}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=\ell}^{m-1}\big\{\mathcal{G}_{t-t_{2k+1}}-\mathcal{G}_{t-t_{2k}}\big\}\big(u_{t_{2k+1}}\,\delta\dot{W}_{t_{2k+1}t_{2k+2}}\big);

\displaystyle I\!I\!I_{st}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\big\{\mathcal{G}_{t-t_{2k}}-\mathcal{G}_{s-t_{2k}}\big\}\big(\delta u_{t_{2k}t_{2k+1}}\,\delta\dot{W}_{t_{2k+1}t_{2k+2}}\big);

\displaystyle I\!V_{st}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell-1}\big\{\mathcal{G}_{t-t_{2k+1}}-\mathcal{G}_{t-t_{2k}}-\mathcal{G}_{s-t_{2k+1}}+\mathcal{G}_{s-t_{2k}}\big\}\big(u_{t_{2k+1}}\,\delta\dot{W}_{t_{2k+1}t_{2k+2}}\big).

Step 0: A general bound on products.
Recall that the (fixed) parameters

\mu_{s}\leq\mu_{\ast}\Big(1-\frac{2}{p}\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{p}{2}(\mu_{t}-\mu_{s})\leq\mu_{\ast}.

for some proportional constant that depends only on

To prove (

\big\|f\cdot\delta\dot{W}_{rv}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{s}}_{2,\infty}}\big\|\delta\dot{W}_{rv}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\varepsilon_{st}}_{p,\infty}}\,,

from which (

Step 1: Bound for

For the term

\int_{s}^{t}(t-v)^{\rho_{d}-(\kappa+\alpha)-\frac{d+1}{p}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(v)}\,dv\,\\

With this set of parameters in hand, inequality (

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}(t-v)^{\rho_{d}-(\kappa+\alpha)-\frac{d+1}{p}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(v)}\,dv

\displaystyle|t-s|^{\rho_{d}-(\kappa+\alpha)-\frac{d+1}{p}+\frac{1}{r_{1}}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(v)}^{q_{1}}\,dv\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}

4.18

\big\|I^{(n)}_{st}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim 2^{-n\varepsilon_{1}}|t-s|^{\gamma}\|\dot{W}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})}\,\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(v)}^{q_{1}}\,dv\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\,.

.

Then we invoke the regularity property (

\big\|I\!I^{(n)}_{st}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim 2^{-n(\rho_{d}-(\kappa+\alpha))}\sum_{k=\ell}^{m-1}\big\|u_{t_{2k+1}}\,\delta\dot{W}_{t_{2k+1}t_{2k+2}}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}\,,

4.13

(4.20)

\big\|I\!I^{(n)}_{st}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}\\
\lesssim 2^{-n(\theta+\rho_{d}-1-(\kappa+\alpha))}\|\dot{W}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})}\bigg(2^{-n}\sum_{k=\ell}^{m-1}\frac{1}{|t-t_{2k+1}|^{\frac{d+1}{p}}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(t_{2k+1})}\bigg)\,.

\displaystyle\big\|I\!I^{(n)}_{st}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim 2^{-n(\theta+\rho_{d}-1-(\kappa+\alpha))}\|\dot{W}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})}\int_{s}^{t}(t-v)^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(v)}\,dv

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-n(\theta+\rho_{d}-1-(\kappa+\alpha))}\|\dot{W}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})}|t-s|^{\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{d+1}{p}}\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\|u\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(v)}^{q_{2}}\,dv\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q_{2}}},

Step 3: Bound for
I\!I\!I_{st}^{(n)}

Then we invoke the fact that

\big\|I\!I\!I^{(n)}_{st}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}\\

Step 4: Bound for

For the term

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{V}^{k}_{st}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|\big\{\mathcal{G}_{t-t_{2k+1}}-\mathcal{G}_{t-t_{2k}}\big\}\big(u_{t_{2k+1}}\,\delta\dot{W}_{t_{2k+1}t_{2k+2}}\big)\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}

4.2.
Wellposedness of the Young wave equation for
\mathbf{d=1,2}

Recall that the wave operator
\mathcal{G}_{t}
has been introduced in Section
3.1
. With the preliminary results of Section
4.1
in hand, equation (
1.6
) with initial conditions
u_{0},u_{1}
will now be written in the following mild sense:

(4.29)

u_{t}:=(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{t}u_{0}+\mathcal{G}_{t}u_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{t-r}(u_{r}\,d\dot{W}_{r}),

In (
4.29
) the integral is interpreted in the Young sense, that is via Proposition
4.4
.

The initial conditions in (
4.29
) have to satisfy some standard smoothness conditions. Those conditions will be expressed in the usual Sobolev scale, for which we introduce a new notation.

Notation 4.6
.

For every
s\geq 0
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}^{s}=\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the usual Sobolev space of order
s
, that is the set of functions
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\{1+|\xi|^{2}\}^{s}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,<\,\infty.

We now turn to our main abstract existence and uniqueness result. Let us recall that we have set

\rho_{d}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ d=1\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if}\ d=2.\end{cases}

Theorem 4.7
.

Assume
d\in\{1,2\}
. Fix an arbitrary time
T>0
, as well as parameters
\gamma,\theta,\kappa,\alpha\in[0,1]
and
p>2
satisfying the conditions in (
4.4
). Assume that
\dot{W}
belongs to
\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})
and pick
(u_{0},u_{1})\in\mathcal{H}^{1+\kappa}\times\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}
.
Then equation (
4.29
) admits a unique solution
u
in the space
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)
introduced in Definition
4.2
.

Before proving Theorem
4.7
, we start with a technical lemma giving a control on the terms related to the initial conditions.

Lemma 4.8
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
4.7
and for all
t\in[0,T]
, set

(4.30)

u^{(0)}_{t}:=(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{t}u_{0}+\mathcal{G}_{t}u_{1}\ .

Then
u^{(0)}
is an element of
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)
and we have

(4.31)

\big\|u^{(0)}\big\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}\lesssim\big\|u_{0}\big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1+\kappa}}+\big\|u_{1}\big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
denote the function involved in our definition of the spaces
\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu}_{p,q}
(see Definition
2.7
and Notation
2.9
). As a preliminary step, observe that owing to assumption (

A
φ

) and the fact that
\varphi
has compact support, one has

|\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)|=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,e^{-\imath\xi\cdot x}\varphi(x)\bigg|=\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,\{e^{-\imath\xi\cdot x}-1\}\varphi(x)\bigg|\lesssim|\xi|^{\kappa},

where we have used the fact that
\kappa\in(0,1)
. Hence for every
j\geq 1
,

(4.32)

|\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(\xi)|=|\widehat{\varphi}(2^{-j}\xi)|\lesssim 2^{-j\kappa}|\xi|^{\kappa}.

Now the weight
w=e^{-\mu|x|}
is smaller than 1. Thus one can trivially bound the norm in
L^{2}_{\mu_{t}}
by the usual norm in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Hence invoking (
3.2
) we get

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\{(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{t}-(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{s}\}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{2}_{\mu_{t}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\big\|\big(\varphi_{j}\ast(\{(\partial_{t}G)_{t}-(\partial_{t}G)_{s}\}\ast u_{0}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\big|\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(\xi)\big|^{2}\big|\cos(t|\xi|)-\cos(s|\xi|)\big|^{2}\big|\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)\big|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,.

Combining this inequality with (
4.32
), this yields

(4.33)

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\{(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{t}-(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{s}\}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{2}_{\mu_{t}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-j\kappa}|t-s|\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,|\xi|^{2+2\kappa}\big|\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)\big|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-j\kappa}|t-s|^{\gamma}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1+\kappa}}\,.

Plugging (
4.33
) into the definition (
2.10
) of Besov norm, we end up with

(4.34)

\big\|\{(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{t}-(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{s}\}u_{0}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{t}}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim|t-s|^{\gamma}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1+\kappa}}\,.

In the same way, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{s}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{2}_{\mu_{s}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\big|\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(\xi)\big|^{2}\big|\cos(s|\xi|)\big|^{2}\big|\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)\big|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j\kappa}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,|\xi|^{2\kappa}\big|\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)\big|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j\kappa}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1+\kappa}}.

Therefore, resorting to (
2.10
) again, we get
\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\big\|(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{s}u_{0}\big\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\kappa,\mu_{s}}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1+\kappa}}
. Gathering this inequality with (
4.34
) we have thus shown that

(4.35)

\big\|(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})u_{0}\big\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}\lesssim\big\|u_{0}\big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1+\kappa}}.

Let us now handle the term
\mathcal{G}_{t}u_{1}
in (
4.30
). It is treated similarly to
(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{t}u_{0}
in (
4.33
)-(
4.35
), and we omit the details for sake of conciseness. Let us just mention that (
4.33
) is replaced by

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi_{j}\ast\{\mathcal{G}_{t}-\mathcal{G}_{s}\}u_{1}\big\|_{L^{2}_{\mu_{t}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,\big|\widehat{\varphi_{j}}(\xi)\big|^{2}\frac{|\sin(t|\xi|)-\sin(s|\xi|)|^{2}}{|\xi|^{2}}\big|\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\big|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-j\kappa}|t-s|\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi\,|\xi|^{2\kappa}\big|\widehat{u_{1}}(\xi)\big|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Following the same steps as before, we then get

(4.36)

\big\|\mathcal{G}u_{1}\big\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}\lesssim\big\|u_{1}\big\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\kappa}}\,.

Plugging together (
4.35
) and (
4.36
), we have thus proved our claim (
4.31
).
∎

With Lemma
4.8
in hand, we now turn to the proof of our existence-uniqueness result.

Proof of Theorem
4.7
.

Due to the expression (
4.31
) for the initial condition
u^{(0)}
, we will not use a standard argument based on patching solutions defined on small intervals. Instead of that, our method will be based on Picard iterations. In other words, we consider the sequence of processes
(u^{(\ell)})_{\ell\geq 0}
defined as: for every
t\in[0,T]
,
u^{(0)}_{t}:=(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{t}u_{0}+\mathcal{G}_{t}u_{1}
and

(4.37)

u^{(\ell+1)}_{t}:=(\partial_{t}\mathcal{G})_{t}u_{0}+\mathcal{G}_{t}u_{1}+\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{t-r}(u^{(\ell)}_{r}\,d\dot{W}_{r}).

By combining the results of Proposition
4.4
and Lemma
4.8
, we can immediately guarantee that
u^{(\ell)}
is well defined in the space
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)
, for every
\ell\geq 0
. We now divide our proof into the existence and the uniqueness parts.

Existence.
In order to show the convergence of
u^{(\ell)}
in
\mathcal{E}_{2,\infty}^{\gamma,\mu}(T)
, let us consider the difference
v^{(\ell)}:=u^{(\ell+1)}-u^{(\ell)}
. Owing to (
4.37
), it is clear that for every
\ell\geq 0
we have
v_{0}^{(\ell)}=0
and
v^{(\ell)}
satisfies

(4.38)

v^{(\ell)}_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{t-r}(v^{(\ell-1)}_{r}\,d\dot{W}_{r}).

We are here in a position to apply the estimate (
4.7
) and assert that, for all
t\in[0,T]

\|v^{(\ell)}\|^{q}_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(t)}\leq c_{T}\int_{0}^{t}\|v^{(\ell-1)}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(t_{1})}^{q}\,dt_{1}\,,

where
c_{T}
only depends on
T
and
\|\dot{W}\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})}
, and
q
is the (finite) parameter provided by Proposition
4.4
. Iterating this inequality, we deduce

\|v^{(\ell)}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}^{q}\leq(c_{T})^{\ell}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\ldots\int_{0}^{t_{\ell}}\|v^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(t_{\ell})}^{q}\,dt_{1}\cdots dt_{\ell}\leq\|v^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}^{q}\frac{(c_{T}T)^{\ell}}{\ell!},

which can naturally be recast as

\|u^{(\ell+1)}-u^{(\ell)}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}\leq\|u^{(1)}-u^{(0)}\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}\frac{\left(c_{T}T\right)^{\frac{\ell}{q}}}{(\ell!)^{\frac{1}{q}}}.

This proves that
(u^{(\ell)})
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)
, and accordingly it converges to some limit
u
. Letting
\ell
tend to infinity in (
4.37
), a standard procedure reveals that
u
is the desired solution to equation (
4.29
).

Uniqueness.
The argument is essentially the same as above. Namely, if
u,v
are two solutions, we have for all
\ell\geq 1
,

\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}^{q}\leq(c_{T})^{\ell}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\ldots\int_{0}^{t_{\ell}}\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(t_{\ell})}^{q}\,dt_{1}\cdots dt_{\ell}\leq\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{E}^{\gamma,\kappa}_{2,\infty}(T)}^{q}\frac{(c_{T}T)^{\ell}}{\ell!},

and we get the conclusion by letting
\ell
tend to infinity.
∎

5.
Application to Gaussian noises

In this section, we prove that the Gaussian noise whose covariance is defined by (
1.2
) is an appropriate input for the wave equation. This amounts to prove that
\dot{W}
sits in a space of the form
C^{\theta}([0,T],\mathcal{B}_{p,\infty}^{-\alpha,P})
as in Proposition
4.4
. Before proceeding to the proof, we first characterize the space
\mathcal{B}_{p,\infty}^{-\alpha,P}
thanks to simple properties of the Fourier transform. Let us start by defining a useful Fourier type operator.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
f
be a smooth enough function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and recall that its Fourier transform is denoted by
\mathcal{F}f
. For an arbitrary constant
c>0
and
s\in(-\infty,1]
, we set

\mathcal{J}^{s}_{c}f:=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\{1+c\,|\cdot|^{2}\}^{\frac{s}{2}}\mathcal{F}f\big)\,.

We now upper bound norms in some weighted Besov spaces
\mathcal{B}
thanks to the Fourier operator
\mathcal{J}
. This is summarized in the following Lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Consider
p\geq 2
and
s\in(-\infty,1]
. Recall that the spaces
L_{w}^{p}
are given in Definition
2.2
and the weighted Besov spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{s,\mu}
are introduced in Definition
2.7
. Let us also recall that we are working with the polynomial weight
P(x)=(1+|x|^{1+d})^{-1}
and the related Besov spaces
\mathcal{B}_{p,q}^{s,P}
as in Lemma
2.10
. Then there exists a constant
c_{p}>0
depending on
p
and
d
only such that

(5.1)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s,P}_{p,\infty}}\lesssim\left\|\mathcal{J}^{s}_{c_{p}}f\right\|_{L^{p}_{P}}\,,

where the operator
\mathcal{J}_{c}^{s}
has been introduced in Definition
5.1
.

Proof.

The proof of this Lemma is basically borrowed from
[
9
]
, to which we will refer for further details. First going back to the definition (
2.10
) of Besov norms and invoking the fact that
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{N})}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}
for
1\leq p<\infty
, we easily get that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,\infty}^{s,P}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s,P}}\,.

Next, write equation (
2.10
), which gives

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s,P}}^{p}=\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{sjp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,P(x)|(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\ast f)(x)|^{p}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,P(x)\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{sjp}|(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\ast f)(x)|^{p}\bigg)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,P(x)\left\|j\mapsto 2^{sj}|(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\ast f)(x)|\right\|^{p}_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}\,.

Owing to the fact that
\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})}\leq\|\cdot\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N})}
for
p\geq 2
, we obtain

(5.2)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}_{p,p}^{s,P}}^{p}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dx\,P(x)\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{2sj}|(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\ast f)(x)|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}\,.

Note that the right-hand side of (
5.2
) is exactly
\|f\|^{p}_{F_{p,2}^{s,P}}
(see (2.2) in
[
9
]
for the definition of
\|\cdot\|_{F_{p,q}^{s,P}}
). Hence (
5.2
) can be rephrased as

\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{s,P}_{p,p}}\leq\|f\|_{F^{s,P}_{p,2}}\,.

The estimate (
5.1
) now stems from the combination of
[
9
, Theorem 2.18]
and
[
9
, Theorem 1.10]
, which allows us to assert that for some suitable
c_{p}>0
,

\big\|f\big\|_{F^{s,P}_{p,2}}\lesssim\big\|\mathcal{J}^{s}_{c_{p}}f\big\|_{L^{p}_{P}}\,,

for some proportional constant that only depends on
p
and
d
.
∎

We now turn to a definition of our Gaussian noise allowing some proper couplings for approximations. Namely we will define
\dot{W}
through an harmonizable representation of the form

(5.3)

\dot{W}_{t}(x):=c_{a_{0}}\int_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\,\frac{e^{\imath\lambda t}-1}{|\lambda|^{\frac{3-a_{0}}{2}}}\,e^{\imath\beta\cdot x}\,\widehat{B}(d\lambda,d\beta)\,.

Here we use a parameter
a_{0}\in(0,2)
,
c_{a_{0}}
is some proper positive constant, and
\widehat{B}
is a complex-valued Gaussian random measure on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for any
I_{1},I_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
and
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

(5.4)

\mathbb{E}[\hat{B}(I_{1}\times A_{1})]=0,\quad\mathbb{E}[\hat{B}(I_{1}\times A_{1})\overline{\hat{B}(I_{2}\times A_{2})}]=m(I_{1}\cap J_{1})\mu(A_{1}\cap A_{2}),

where
m
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
and
\mu(d\beta)
is the spectral measure of a nonnegative definite (generalized) function
\gamma(x)
. In order to get a real-valued noise, we also assume that the Gaussian measure
\widehat{B}
is such that

(5.5)

\overline{\hat{B}(I_{1}\times A_{1})}=\hat{B}\left(-\left(I_{1}\times A_{1}\right)\right)\,.

It is readily checked from (
5.4
)-(
5.5
) that for an appropriate value of
c_{a_{0}}
, the covariance of
\dot{W}
is (formally) given by

(5.6)

\mathbb{E}[\dot{W}_{t}(x)\dot{W}_{s}(y)]=R_{a_{0}}(t,s)\gamma(x-y),

with

R_{a_{0}}(t,s):=\frac{1}{2}\big\{|t|^{2-a_{0}}+|s|^{2-a_{0}}-|t-s|^{2-a_{0}}\big\}.

The definition (
5.3
) of
\dot{W}
comes with a natural approximation by smooth functions. Namely for
n\geq 1
we define

(5.7)

\dot{W}^{n}_{t}(x):=c_{a_{0}}\int_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\int_{\beta\in\mathbf{B}_{2^{n}}}\widehat{B}(d\lambda,d\beta)\,\frac{e^{\imath\lambda t}-1}{|\lambda|^{\frac{3-a_{0}}{2}}}\,e^{\imath\beta\cdot x}\,,

where we recall that the balls
\mathbf{B}_{R}
are introduced in Notation
1.2
.
We shall show that
\dot{W}^{n}
converges to
\dot{W}_{t}(x)
given in (
5.3
) in a proper space.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
a_{0}\in(0,2)
and consider
\theta\in(0,1-\frac{a_{0}}{2})
. Let
\{\dot{W}^{n},t\in[0,T],x\in\mathbb{R}\}
be the field defined by (
5.7
). We assume the existence of a threshold parameter
\alpha_{d}>0
such that the measure
\mu
satisfies

(5.8)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\beta|}\right)^{2\alpha_{d}+\varepsilon}\mu(d\beta)<\infty\,,~\text{ for every }~\varepsilon>0\,.

Then for all
\alpha>\alpha_{d}
and
p
large enough,
\{\dot{W}^{n},n\geq 1\}
forms a Cauchy sequence in the space

L^{p}\left(\Omega;\mathcal{C}^{\theta}\left([0,T],\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty}\right)\right),

with the limit denoted by
\dot{W}
. The noisy input
\dot{W}
satisfies the following properties:

(i)

Almost surely we have
\dot{W}\in\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T],\mathcal{B}_{p,\infty}^{-\alpha,P})
.

(ii)

The covariance function of
\dot{W}
is given by (
5.6
).

Proof.

In the sequel we will write
\delta\dot{W}_{st}^{n}=\dot{W}_{t}^{n}-\dot{W}_{s}^{n}
to alleviate notations. We will reduce our proof to moment computations thanks to two standard steps:

(1) Invoking a telescoping sum argument, the convergence of the sequence
\{\dot{W}^{n};n\geq 1\}
is implied by the convergence of the series

(5.9)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|\dot{W}^{n+1}-\dot{W}^{n}\big\|^{p}_{\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T],\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})}\right]\right)^{1/p}\,.

(2) Owing to a standard application of Garsia’s lemma the following holds true: if we assume that for each summand in (
5.9
) and for
p\geq\frac{1}{1-\frac{a_{0}}{2}-\theta}
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

(5.10)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta\dot{W}_{st}^{n+1}-\delta\dot{W}_{st}^{n}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{B}_{p,\infty}^{-\alpha,P}}\right]\lesssim\frac{|t-s|^{(1-\frac{a_{0}}{2})p}}{2^{\varepsilon np}}\,,

then we also have

(5.11)

\mathbb{E}\left[\big\|\dot{W}^{n+1}-\dot{W}^{n}\big\|^{p}_{\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T],\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})}\right]\lesssim\frac{1}{2^{\varepsilon np}}\,.

Putting together (
5.9
) and (
5.11
), we are now reduced to prove (
5.10
).

In order to upper bound the right-hand side of (
5.10
), we invoke (
5.1
) which can be read

(5.12)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta\dot{W}_{st}^{n+1}-\delta\dot{W}_{st}^{n}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{B}_{p,\infty}^{-\alpha,P}}\right]\\
\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\frac{dx}{1+|x|^{1+d}}\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\{1+|\cdot|^{2}\}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathcal{F}\big(\delta\dot{W}_{st}^{n+1}-\delta\dot{W}_{st}^{n}\big)\Big)(x)\Big|^{p}\Big]\,.

In addition,
\dot{W}^{n+1}-\dot{W}^{n}
is a Gaussian process and
\mathcal{F}
is a linear transform. Hence we get

(5.13)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\delta\dot{W}_{st}^{n+1}-\delta\dot{W}_{st}^{n}\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{B}_{p,\infty}^{-\alpha,P}}\right]\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{dx}{1+|x|^{1+d}}\,\big(Q_{st}^{n}(x)\big)^{p/2}\,,

where we have set

Q_{st}^{n}(x):=\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(\{1+|\cdot|^{2}\}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\mathcal{F}\big(\delta\dot{W}^{n+1}_{st}-\delta\dot{W}^{n}_{st}\big)\Big)(x)\Big|^{2}\Big]\,.

Let us focus our attention on the evaluation of
Q^{n}
above, defined for
0\leq s<t\leq T
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. To this aim, by simply writing the definition of Fourier transform we get

(5.14)

Q^{n}_{st}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}d\xi d\tilde{\xi}\,e^{\imath x\cdot(\xi-\tilde{\xi})}\{1+|\xi|^{2}\}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\{1+|\tilde{\xi}|^{2}\}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\\
\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}dyd\tilde{y}\,e^{-\imath\xi\cdot y}e^{\imath\tilde{\xi}\cdot\tilde{y}}\,\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\{\delta\dot{W}^{n+1}_{st}-\delta\dot{W}^{n}_{st}\big\}(y)\overline{\big\{\delta\dot{W}^{n+1}_{st}-\delta\dot{W}^{n}_{st}\big\}(\tilde{y})}\Big]\,.

In addition, invoking relation (
5.7
) and the covariance structure of a complex valued white noise, we obtain

\mathbb{E}\Big[\big\{\delta\dot{W}^{n+1}_{st}-\delta\dot{W}^{n}_{st}\big\}(y)\overline{\big\{\delta\dot{W}^{n+1}_{st}-\delta\dot{W}^{n}_{st}\big\}(\tilde{y})}\Big]=c\,|t-s|^{2-a_{0}}\int_{\mathbf{B}_{2^{n+1}}\backslash\mathbf{B}_{2^{n}}}e^{\imath\beta\cdot(y-\tilde{y})}\mu(d\beta)\,,

for some constant
c
. Plugging this inequality into (
5.14
) and carefully computing the Fourier transforms reveals that

(5.15)

\displaystyle Q^{n}_{st}(x)

\displaystyle=c\,|t-s|^{2-a_{0}}\int_{\mathbf{B}_{2^{n+1}}\backslash\mathbf{B}_{2^{n}}}\frac{\mu(d\beta)}{\{1+|\beta|^{2}\}^{\alpha}}\,.

We now bound the right-hand side of (
5.15
): by setting
\varepsilon:=\alpha-\alpha_{d}>0
, we get

(5.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{B}_{2^{n+1}}\backslash\mathbf{B}_{2^{n}}}\frac{\mu(d\beta)}{\{1+|\beta|^{2}\}^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{B}_{2^{n+1}}\backslash\mathbf{B}_{2^{n}}}\{1+|\beta|^{2}\}^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\frac{\mu(d\beta)}{\{1+|\beta|^{2}\}^{\alpha_{d}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}}\lesssim 2^{-n\varepsilon},

where the inequality follows from the assumption (
5.8
).
Reporting (
5.16
) into (
5.15
), we have thus obtained

Q_{st}^{n}(x)\lesssim 2^{-n\varepsilon}|t-s|^{2-a_{0}}\,.

Plugging this information into (
5.13
), this trivially yields our claim (
5.10
). Since we have argued that the convergence of
\dot{W}^{n}
could be reduced to (
5.10
), we have proved that
\dot{W}^{n}
converges in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T],\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty}))
for
p\geq\frac{1}{1-\frac{a_{0}}{2}-\theta}
.
In particular, this proves item
(i)
in our Theorem.

Finally, item
(ii)
in our Theorem can be proved by a standard procedure on covariance functions from (
5.7
) to (
5.6
).
The proof is concluded.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

In the current paper, we have chosen the harmonic representation (
5.7
) to approximate the Gaussian noise, which is convenient in our setting. We could also have used, similarly to what is done in
[
2
]
, a sequence of mollifications
\dot{W}^{n}=\varphi_{n}*\dot{W}
. This second approach might be closer in spirit to our previous work
[
4
]
, but is slightly more cumbersome.

Let us recall that we have denoted by
\rho_{d}
the regularization parameter exhibited in Proposition
3.4
, with explicit value

\rho_{d}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ d=1\\
\frac{1}{2}&\text{if}\ d=2.\end{cases}

Proposition 5.6
.

Assume that
d\in\{1,2\}
. Under the same setting as for Theorem
5.3
, assume that
a_{0}<\rho_{d}
and that condition (
5.8
) is satisfied for
\alpha_{d}
such that

(5.17)

0<\alpha_{d}<\frac{1}{2}(\rho_{d}-a_{0}).

Then there exist coefficients
0<\kappa,\alpha,\gamma,\theta<1
and
p>d+1
that satisfy (
4.4
) and such that
\dot{W}\in\mathcal{C}^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,P}_{p,\infty})
almost surely. In particular Theorem
4.7
can be applied to the noise
\dot{W}
and equation (
4.29
) admits a unique solution in
\mathcal{E}_{2,\infty}^{\gamma,\kappa}(T)
.

Proof.

Thanks to condition (
5.17
), we can fix
\varepsilon>0
small enough so that
\alpha_{d}<\frac{1}{2}(\rho_{d}-a_{0})-2\varepsilon
,
\theta:=1-\frac{a_{0}}{2}-\varepsilon\in[0,1]
and
\alpha:=\alpha_{d}+\varepsilon\in[0,1]
. By Theorem
5.3
, we know that
\dot{W}\in C^{\theta}([0,T];\mathcal{B}_{p,\infty}^{-\alpha,P})
. Besides, observe that

\theta-\alpha=1-\frac{a_{0}}{2}-\alpha_{d}-2\varepsilon>1-\frac{\rho_{d}}{2}\,.

We can thus pick two parameters
p
large enough and
\delta>0
small enough so that

(5.18)

\theta-\alpha>1-\frac{\rho_{d}}{2}+\frac{d}{2p}+\delta\,.

We now consider
\kappa,\gamma
such that the second and third conditions in (

) are fulfilled, of the form

\kappa=\alpha+\frac{d}{p}+\delta,\mbox{ and }\gamma=1-\theta+\delta\,,

for
\delta>0
small enough and
p\geq 1
large enough. Notice that since we have
\alpha,\theta\in[0,1]
, we also have
\kappa,\gamma\in[0,1]
whenever

is large and
\delta
is small. Moreover, for
\gamma
as in (

), the last condition in (
4.4
) is verified:

It remains to prove that the first condition in (

) holds true. Towards this aim, consider
\kappa,\gamma
as in (

) and compute

(5.20)

\kappa+\alpha+\gamma+(1-\rho_{d})=2\alpha+\frac{d}{p}+1-\theta+2\delta+(1-\rho_{d})=2-\rho_{d}-2(\theta-\alpha)+\frac{d}{p}+2\delta+\theta\,.

In addition, resorting to (
5.18

. Plugging this relation into (

\kappa+\alpha+\gamma+(1-\rho_{d})<\theta\,.

This shows that the set of conditions (
4.4
) is satisfied and that
\dot{W}
is an element of

. Our proof is achieved.
∎

for a proportional constant that only depends on
\mu_{\ast}
. Reporting this estimate into the sum (
A.3
) defining

, we end up with

(A.5)

\big\|I_{j}\big\|_{L^{p}_{\mu}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{B}^{-\alpha,\mu_{1}}_{p_{1},\infty}}\|g\|_{\mathcal{B}^{\beta,\mu_{2}}_{p_{2},\infty}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell,k\geq 0\\

Treatment of
I\!I_{j}
. As in the previous step, we first analyze a single term

\displaystyle\big(\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{j}\ast\big[\delta_{\ell}f\cdot\delta_{k}g\big]\big)(x)

(A.7)

where we have set

z\in\mathbf{B}_{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}^{(3)}_{t,j}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{dy}{\left(t^{2}-|y|^{2}\right)^{1/2}}{\bf 1}_{\{|y|<t\leq|y+2^{-j}z|\}}.

We are here in the same position as in (
A.21
), and so we can use the same estimates as above to derive that uniformly over
z\in\mathbf{B}_{2}
and
t\in[0,1]
,

(A.25)

\mathcal{I}^{(3)}_{t,j}(z)\lesssim 2^{-\frac{j}{2}}.

Plugging (
A.22
) and (
A.25
) into (
A.20
), we see that

\sup_{z\in\mathbf{B}_{2}}\sup_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{I}_{t,j}(z)\lesssim 2^{-\frac{j}{2}},

which, going back to decomposition (
A.19
), easily leads us to the assertion (
3.13
).
∎

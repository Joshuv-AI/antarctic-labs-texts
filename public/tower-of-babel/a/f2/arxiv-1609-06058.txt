Hilbert-Mumford stability on algebraic stacks and applications to 𝒢 \mathcal{G} -bundles on curves
Jochen Heinloth

Abstract
In these notes we reformulate the classical Hilbert-Mumford criterion for GIT stability in terms of algebraic stacks, this was independently done by Halpern-Leinster [ 22 ] . We also give a geometric condition that guarantees the existence of separated coarse moduli spaces for the substack of stable objects. This is then applied to construct coarse moduli spaces for torsors under parahoric group schemes over curves.



Keywords.
Hilbert-Mumford stability; moduli of

-bundles
on curves

Introduction

The aim of these notes is to reformulate the Hilbert-Mumford
criterion from geometric invariant theory (GIT) in terms of
algebraic stacks (Definition
1.B
) and use it to give an
existence result for separated coarse moduli spaces.

Our original motivation was that for various moduli problems one has
been able to guess stability criteria which have then been shown to
coincide with stability conditions imposed by GIT constructions of
the moduli stacks. It seemed strange to me that in these
constructions it is often not too difficult to find a stability
criteria by educated guessing, however, in order to obtain coarse
moduli spaces one then has to prove that the guess agrees with the
Hilbert-Mumford criterion from GIT, which often turns out to be a
difficult and lengthy task.

Many aspects of GIT have of course been reformulated in terms of
stacks by Alper
[
3
]
. Also Iwanari
[
31
]
gave a
clear picture for pre-stable points on stacks and constructed
possibly non-separated coarse moduli spaces. The analog of the
numerical Hilbert-Mumford criterion has been used implicitly in many
places by several authors. Most recently Halpern-Leinster
[
22
]

independently gave a formulation, very close to ours and applied it
to construct analogs of the Harder–Narasimhan stratification for
moduli problems under a condition he calls
\Theta
-reductivity.

Our main aim is to give a criterion that guarantees that the stable
points form a separated substack (Proposition
2.B
). Once
this is available, one can apply general results (e.g., the theorems
of Keel and Mori
[
32
]
and Alper, Hall and Rydh

[
4
]
) to obtain separated coarse moduli spaces
(Proposition
2.C
). As side effect, we hope that our
formulation may serve as an introduction to the beautiful picture
developed
[
22
]
.

The guiding examples which also provide our main applications are
moduli stacks of torsors under parahoric group schemes. Using our
method we find a stability criterion for such torsors on curves and
construct separated coarse moduli spaces of stable torsors (Theorem

3.H
). Previously such moduli spaces had been
constructed for generically trivial group schemes in characteristic
0 by Balaji and Seshadri
[
7
]
, who obtain that in these cases
the spaces are schemes. Also in any characteristic the special case
of moduli of parabolic bundles has been constructed in
[
26
]
,
but it seems that coarse moduli spaces for twisted groups had not
been constructed before.

This problem was the starting point for the current article, because
in
[
26
]
most of the technical problems arose in the
construction of coarse moduli spaces by GIT that were needed to
prove cohomological purity results for the moduli stack. The results
of this article allow to bypass this issue and apply to the larger
class of parahoric groups.

The structure of the article is as follows. In Section 1 we state
the stability criterion depending on a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on an
algebraic stack
\mathcal{M}
. As a consistency check we then show that this
coincides with the Hilbert Mumford criterion for global quotient
stacks (Proposition
1.D
). To illustrate the
method we then consider some classical moduli problems and show how
Ramanathan’s criterion for stability of
G
-bundles on curves can
be derived form our criterion rather easily. The same argument
applies to related moduli spaces, as the moduli of chains or pairs.

In Section 2 we formulate the numerical condition on the pair

(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{L})
implying that the stable points form a separated
substack (Proposition
2.B
) and derive an existence result
for coarse moduli spaces (Proposition
2.C
). Again, as
an illustration we check that this criterion is satisfied in for
GIT-quotient stacks and for
G
-bundles on curves.

Finally in Section 3 we apply the method to the moduli stack of
torsors under a parahoric group scheme on a curve. We construct
coarse moduli spaces for the substack of stable points of these
stacks. For this we also need to prove some of the basic results
concerning stability of parahoric group schemes that could be of
independent interest.

1.
The Hilbert-Mumford criterion in terms of stacks

Throughout we will work over a fixed base field
k
. The letter

\mathcal{M}
will denote an algebraic stack over
k
, which is locally of
finite type over
k
and we will always assume that the diagonal

\Delta\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}
is quasi-affine, as this
implies that our stack is a stack for the fpqc topology
(
[
34
, Corollaire 10.7]
).

We have two guiding examples in mind: First, global quotient stacks

[X/G]
where
X
is a proper scheme and
G
is an affine algebraic
group acting on
X
and second, the stack
\Bun_{G}
of
G
-bundles on
a smooth projective curve
C
for a semi-simple group
G
over
k
.

1.A.
Motivation: The classical Hilbert-Mumford criterion

As the numerical criterion for stability from geometric invariant
theory
[
36
, Theorem 2.1]
serves as a guideline we start by
recalling this briefly. To state it and in order to fix our sign
conventions we need to recall the definition of weights of

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant line bundles:

1.A.a.
Weights of equivariant line bundles

As usual we denote the multiplicative group scheme by
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}:=\Spec k[t,t^{-1}]
and the affine line by
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}:=\Spec k[x]
. The
standard action

\act\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}

is given
by
t.x:=tx
, i.e. on the level of rings
\act^{\#}\colon k[x]\to k[x,t,t^{-1}]
is given by
\act^{\#}(x)=tx
. We write

[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
for the quotient stack defined by this action. This
stack is called
\Theta
in
[
22
]
.

By definition
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant line bundles
\mathcal{L}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
are
the same as line bundles on
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
. For any line bundle

\mathcal{L}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
the global sections are
H^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{1},\mathcal{L})=k[x]\cdot e
for some section
e
which is unique up to a scalar multiple.

Thus an equivariant line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
defines an integer

d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
by
\act^{\#}(e)=t^{d}e
called the weight of
\mathcal{L}
and
we will denote it as

\wt(\mathcal{L}):=d.

In particular we find that:

H^{0}([{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}],\mathcal{L})=H^{0}({\mathbb{A}}^{1},\mathcal{L})^{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k\cdot x^{d}e&\text{if }\wt(\mathcal{L})=d\leq 0\\
0&\text{if }\wt(\mathcal{L})=d>0.\end{array}\right.

To compare the sign conventions in different articles the above
equation is the one to keep in mind, because Mumford’s construction
of quotients uses invariant sections of line bundles.
Similarly, a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\Spec k
is
given by a morphism of underlying modules
\act^{\#}\colon\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}\otimes k[t,t^{-1}]
with
\act^{\#}(e)=t^{d}e
for some
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. The integer
d
is again denoted by:

\wt_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}(\mathcal{L})\coloneq d.

1.A.b.
Mumford’s notion of stability

Mumford considers a projective scheme
X
, equipped with an action
of a reductive group
G
and a
G
-linearized ample bundle
\mathcal{L}
on

X
. For any
x\in X(k)
and any cocharacter
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
Mumford defines
\mu^{\mathcal{L}}(x,\lambda)\in{\mathbb{Z}}
as follows. The
action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
on
x
defines a morphism
\lambda.x\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to X
which extends to an equivariant morphism
f_{x,\lambda}\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to X
because
X
is projective. He defines

\mu^{\mathcal{L}}(x,\lambda)\coloneq-\wt(f_{x,\lambda}^{*}\mathcal{L}).

The criterion
[
36
, Theorem 2.1]
then reads as follows:

A geometric point
x\in X({\overline{k}})
is
stable
if and only if the
stabilizer of
x
in
G
is finite and for all
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
we have

\mu^{L}(x,\lambda)>0,\text{ equivalently }\wt(f_{x,\lambda}^{*}\mathcal{L})<0.

1.B.
\mathcal{L}
-stability on algebraic stacks

It is easy to reformulate the numerical Hilbert–Mumford criterion in terms of stacks, once we fix some notation. The quotient stack
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
has two geometric points
1
and
0
which are the images of the points of the same name in
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
. For any algebraic stack
\mathcal{M}
and
f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\mathcal{M}
we
will write
f(0),f(1)\in\mathcal{M}(k)
for the points given by the images
of
0,1\in{\mathbb{A}}^{1}(k)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an algebraic stack over
k
and
x\in\mathcal{M}(K)
a
geometric point for some algebraically closed field
K/k
.

A
very close degeneration of
x
is a morphism
f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{K}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m,K}]\to\mathcal{M}
with
f(1)\cong x
and
f(0)\not\cong x
.

Very close degenerations have been used under different names, e.g.
in the context of
K
-stability these are often called
test-configurations. Our terminology should only emphasize that

f(0)
is an object that lies in the closure of a
K
point of

\mathcal{M}_{K}
, which only happens for stacks and orbit spaces, but if

X=\mathcal{M}
is a scheme, then there are no very close degenerations.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an algebraic stack over
k
, locally of finite
type with affine diagonal and
\mathcal{L}
a line bundle on
\mathcal{M}
. A
geometric point
x\in\mathcal{M}(K)
is called
\mathcal{L}
-stable
if

(1)

for all very close degenerations
f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{K}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m,K}]\to\mathcal{M}
of
x
we have

\wt(f^{*}\mathcal{L})<0

and

(2)

\dim_{K}(\Aut_{\mathcal{M}}(x))=0
.

(1)

We can also introduce the notion of
\mathcal{L}
-semistable
points, by requiring only
\leq
in (1) and dropping condition (2).

(2)

The notion admits several natural extensions: Since the weight of line bundles extends to a elements of the groups

\Pic(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}\cong{\mathbb{R}},

the above definition can also be applied if
\mathcal{L}\in\Pic(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
. This is often convenient for classical
notions of stability that depend on real parameters.

In
[
22
]
Halpern-Leinster uses a cohomology class
\alpha\in H^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{{\overline{k}}},{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})
instead of a line bundle. For

\alpha=c_{1}(\mathcal{L})
this gives the same condition. As the condition
given above is a numerical one, this is sometimes more convenient
and we will refer to it as
\alpha
-stability.

(3)

As the above condition is numerical and
H^{2d}([{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}],{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell})={\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
for all
d>0

one can generalize the notion further.

(4)

For the above definition to be nonempty it is convenient to assume that the automorphism groups of generic objects are finite, so that (2) can be fulfilled. This appears to rule out the example of vector bundles on curves, but fortunately there is a quite general method available to rigidify the problem, i.e., to divide out generic automorphism groups (
[
1
, Theorem 5.1.5]
,
[
2
, Appendix C]
).

(5)

General representability results of
[
23
, Theorem 1.6]
imply that under our conditions on
\mathcal{M}
the stack of morphisms
\Mor([{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}],\mathcal{M})
is again an algebraic stack, locally of finite type. As weights of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-actions on line bundles are locally constant in families this implies that
\mathcal{L}
-stability is preserved under extension of algebraically closed fields
L/K
. In particular, if we define an
S
-valued point
\mathcal{M}(S)
to be
\mathcal{L}
-stable if the corresponding objects are stable for all geometric points of
S
, we get a notion that is preserved under pull-back and therefore defines an abstract substack
\mathcal{M}^{s}\subset\mathcal{M}
. We will see that in may cases
\mathcal{L}
-stability turns out to be an open condition and then
\mathcal{M}^{s}
is again an algebraic stack, but this is not true for arbitrary
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{L}
.

Notation.

Given a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on
\mathcal{M}
and
x\in\mathcal{M}(K)
we will denote by
\wt_{x}(\mathcal{L})
the homomorphism

\wt_{x}(\mathcal{L})\colon X_{*}(\Aut_{\mathcal{M}}(x))=\Hom({\mathbb{G}}_{m},\Aut_{\mathcal{M}}(x))\to{\mathbb{Z}}

which maps
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\Aut_{\mathcal{M}}(x)
to
\wt_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}((x,\lambda)^{*}\mathcal{L})
, where

(x,\lambda)\colon[\Spec K/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\mathcal{M}
is the morphism defined by
x
and
\lambda
.

A toy example illustrating the criterion is given by the anti-diagonal action
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
defined as
t.(x,y)\coloneq(tx,t^{-1}y)
.
The only fixed point of this action is the origin
0
. The quotient

({\mathbb{A}}^{2}-\{0\})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
is the affine line with a doubled origin, the
first example of a non-separated scheme.

Since the latter space is a scheme none of its points admits very
close degenerations. This changes if we look at the full quotient

[{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
, which contains the additional point

[(0,0)/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
. The inclusions of the coordinate axes

\iota_{x},\iota_{y}\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
define very close
degenerations of the points
(0,1)
and
(1,0)
and it will turn out
(Lemma
1.D
) that these constitute essentially
the only very close degenerations in this stack.

A line bundle on
[{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
is an equivariant line bundle on

{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
. Since line bundles on
{\mathbb{A}}^{2}
are trivial, all equivariant
line bundles are given by a character
()^{d}\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

and we find
\wt_{0}\colon\Pic([{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}])\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
. Moreover
for the corresponding line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{d}
the weights

\wt(\iota_{x}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{d})=d,\wt(\iota_{y}^{*}\mathcal{L}_{d})=-d
so for each
d\neq 0
only one of the points
(1,0)
and
(0,1)
can be
\mathcal{L}_{d}
-stable.

For
d=0
the points
(1,0),(0,0),(0,1)
are all semistable and
these points would be identified in the GIT quotient.

1.C.
Determining very close degenerations

To apply the definition of
\mathcal{L}
-stability, one needs to classify
all very close degenerations. The next lemma shows that these can be
described by deformation theory of objects
x
that admit
non-constant morphisms
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\Aut_{\mathcal{M}}(x)
. Let us fix our
notation for formal discs:

{\mathbb{D}}:=\Spec k[\![t]\!],\mathring{{\mathbb{D}}}:=\Spec k(\!(t)\!).

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an algebraic stack locally of finite type over
k={\overline{k}}
with quasi-affine diagonal.

(1)

For any very close degeneration
f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\mathcal{M}
the induced morphism

\lambda_{f}\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}=\Aut_{[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]}(0)\to\Aut_{\mathcal{M}}(f(0))

is nontrivial.

(2)

The restriction functor
\mathcal{M}([{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}])\to\varprojlim\mathcal{M}([\Spec(k[x]/x^{n})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}])
is an equivalence of categories.

For part (2) elegant proofs have been given independently by Alper,
Hall and Rydh
[
4
, Corollary 3.6]
and Bhatt,
Halpern-Leistner
[
10
, Remark 8.3]
. Both proofs rely on Tannaka
duality, one uses an underived version the other a derived version.
As the statement produces the point
f(1)
out of a formal datum let
us explain briefly why this is possible: The composition

\phi\colon{\mathbb{D}}=\Spec k[\![x]\!]\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]

is faithfully flat, because both morphisms are flat and the map is
surjective, because both points
1,0
of
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
are in the
image.

By our assumptions
\mathcal{M}
is a stack for the fpqc topology
(
[
34
, Corollaire 10.7]
) we therefore see that

\mathcal{M}([{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}])
can be described as objects in
\mathcal{M}(k[\![x]\!])
together with a descent datum with respect to
\phi
.

Moreover, the canonical map
\mathcal{M}({\mathbb{D}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\varprojlim\mathcal{M}(k[x]/(x^{n}))
is an equivalence of categories: This follows for
example, because the statement holds for schemes and choosing a
smooth presentation
X\to\mathcal{M}
one can reduce to this statement,

[
42
, Tag 07X8]
.

In particular this explains already that an element of
\varprojlim\mathcal{M}([\Spec(k[x]/x^{n})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}])
will produce a
k[\![x]\!]
-point of

\mathcal{M}
. The problem now lies in constructing a descent datum for this
morphism, as

{\mathbb{D}}\times_{[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]}{\mathbb{D}}=\Spec(k[\![x]\!]\otimes_{k[x]}k[x,t,t^{-1}]\otimes_{k[y]}k[\![y]\!])

where
the last tensor product is taken via
y=xt
. The ring on the right
hand side is not complete and the formal descent data coming form an
element in
\varprojlim\mathcal{M}([\Spec(k[x]/x^{n})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}])
only seems to
induce a descent datum on the completion of the above ring.

Here the Tannakian argument greatly simplifies the problem, as it
gives a concise way to capture the information that a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
action
induces a grading and therefore allows to pass from power series to
polynomials.

Let us deduce (1). First note that this holds automatically if
\mathcal{M}

is a scheme, because then
f(1)
is a closed point and
f(0)
lies
in the closure of
f(1)
.

In general choose a smooth presentation
p\colon X\to\mathcal{M}
. If

\lambda_{f}
is trivial, we can lift the morphism
f|_{0}\colon[0/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\Spec k\to\mathcal{M}
to
\tilde{f}_{0}\colon[0/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to X
. Since
p
is smooth, we can inductively lift this morphism to
obtain an element in
\varprojlim X([\Spec(k[x]/x^{n})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}])
. Thus
we reduced (1) to the case
\mathcal{M}=X
.

\Box

Using this lemma, we can compare
\mathcal{L}
-stability to classical
notions of stability, simply by first identifying objects for which
the automorphism group contains
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
and then studying their
deformations. The next subsections illustrate this procedure in
examples.

1.D.
The example of GIT-quotients

Let
X
be a projective variety equipped with the action of a
reductive group
G
and a
G
-linearized line bundle
\mathcal{L}
. Again
bundles on the quotient stack
[X/G]
are the same as equivariant
bundles on
X
, so we will alternatively view
\mathcal{L}
as a line
bundle on
[X/G]
.

Let us fix some standard notation: Given
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
we will denote by
P_{\lambda}
the corresponding parabolic
subgroup,
U_{\lambda}
its unipotent radical and
L_{\lambda}
the
corresponding Levi subgroup, i.e.,

\displaystyle P_{\lambda}(R)

\displaystyle=\{g\in G(R)|\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)g\lambda(t^{-1})\text{ exists}\},

\displaystyle U_{\lambda}(R)

\displaystyle=\{g\in G(R)|\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)g\lambda(t^{-1})=1\},

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}(R)

\displaystyle=\{g\in G(R)|\lambda(t)g\lambda(t^{-1})=g\}\text{
i.e., }L_{\lambda}=\Centr_{G}(\lambda).

To compare
\mathcal{L}
-stability on
[X/G]
to GIT-stability on
X
we
first observe that the test objects appearing in the conditions
coincide:

For any cocharacter
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
and any geometric
point
x\in X(K)
that is not a fixed point of
\lambda
the
equivariant map
f_{\lambda,x}\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{K}\to X
defines a very
close degeneration
\overline{f}_{\lambda,x}\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{K}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m,K}]\to[X/G]
. Moreover, any very close
degeneration in the stack
[X/G]
is of the form

\overline{f}_{\lambda,x}
for some
x,\lambda
.

Since
f_{\lambda,x}(0)
is a fixed point of
\lambda
and
x
is
not, we have
\overline{f}(0)\not\cong\overline{f}(1)
, thus

\overline{f}
is a very close degeneration.

Conversely let
f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to[X/G]
be any very close
degeneration. We need to find a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
equivariant morphism

\textstyle{{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{f}}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{[X/G].}

Since
\pi\colon X\to[X/G]
is a
G
-bundle, the pull-back

p\colon X\times_{[X/G]}[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
is a

G
-bundle on
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
. To find
\tilde{f}
is equivalent to
finding a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant section of this bundle. This will
follow from the known classification of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant

G
-bundles on the affine line:

Let
G
be a reductive group and
\mathcal{P}
a
G
-bundle on

[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
. Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{0}
the fiber of
\mathcal{P}
over
0\in{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
.

(1)

If there exists
x_{0}\in\mathcal{P}_{0}(k)
(e.g. this holds if
k={\overline{k}}
). Then there exists a cocharacter
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
, unique up to conjugation and an isomorphism of
G
-bundles

\mathcal{P}\cong[\big({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times G\big)/\big({\mathbb{G}}_{m},(\act,\lambda)\big)].

Moreover,
\mathcal{P}
has a canonical reduction to
P_{\lambda}
.

(2)

Let
G_{0}\coloneq\Aut_{G}(P_{0})
and
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G_{0}
the cocharacter defined by
\mathcal{P}|_{[0/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]}
. Consider the
G_{0}
bundle
\mathcal{P}^{G_{0}}\coloneq\Isom_{G}(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}_{0})
on
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
. Then

\mathcal{P}^{G_{0}}\cong[\big({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times G_{0}\big)/\big({\mathbb{G}}_{m},(\act,\lambda)\big)],

i.e.

\mathcal{P}\cong\Isom_{G_{0}}([\big({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times G_{0}\big)/\big({\mathbb{G}}_{m},(\act,\lambda)\big)],\mathcal{P}_{0})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{P}^{G_{0}}
has a canonical reduction to
P_{0,\lambda}\subset G_{0}
.

For vector bundles this result is
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
where
some history is given. The general case can be deduced from this
using the Tannaka formalism. As we will need a variant of the
statement later, we give a slightly different argument.

The second part follows from the first, as the
G_{0}
-bundle
\mathcal{P}^{G_{0}}_{0}=\Isom_{G}(\mathcal{P}_{0},\mathcal{P}_{0})
has a canonical point
\id
. We added (2), because it gives an intrinsic statement, independent of choices.

To prove (1) note that
x_{0}
defines an isomorphism
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Aut_{G}(P_{0})
and a section
[\Spec k/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\mathcal{P}|_{[0/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]}
. This induces a section
[\Spec k/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\mathcal{P}/P_{\lambda}
. As the map
\pi\colon\mathcal{P}/P_{\lambda}\to[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
is smooth, any section can be lifted infinitesimally to
\Spec k[x]/x^{n}
for all
n\geq 0
. Inductively the obstruction to the existence of a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-equivariant section is an element in
H^{1}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m},T_{(\mathcal{P}/P_{\lambda})/{\mathbb{A}}^{1},0}\otimes(x^{n-1})/(x^{n}))=0
and the choices of such liftings form a torsor under
H^{0}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{m},T_{\mathcal{P}/P_{\lambda}/{\mathbb{A}}^{1},0}\otimes(x^{n-1})/(x^{n}))
. Now by construction
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
acts with negative weight on
T_{\mathcal{P}/P_{\lambda},0}=\Lie(G)/\Lie(P_{\lambda})
and it also acts with negative weight on the cotangent space
(x)/(x^{2})
, so there exists a canonical
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
equivariant reduction
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
of
\mathcal{P}
to
P_{\lambda}
.

Similarly, the vanishing of
H^{1}
implies that we can also find a compatible family of
\lambda
-equivariant sections
[(\Spec k[t]/t^{n})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\mathcal{P}
and by Lemma
1.C
this defines a section over
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
, i.e. a morphism of
G
-bundles
[({\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times G)/{\mathbb{G}}_{m},\lambda]\to\mathcal{P}
.

Here, we could alternatively see this explicitly as follows: Let us consider
\pi\colon\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\to{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
as
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
equivariant
P_{\lambda}
bundle on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
and consider the twisted action

\star\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\to\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}

given by
t\star p\coloneq(t.p)\cdot\lambda(t^{-1})
. Our point
x_{0}
is a fixed point for this action by construction, as we used it to identify
P_{0}
with
G
and
e\in G
is a fixed point for the conjugation
\lambda(t)\cdot\underline{\quad}\cdot\lambda(t^{-1})
. Moreover
\lambda
acts with non-negative weights on
\Lie(P_{\lambda})
and also on
T_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}=((x)/(x^{2}))^{\vee}
. Therefore Białynicki-Birula decomposition
[
30
]
implies that there exists a point
x_{1}\in\pi^{-1}(1)
such that
\lim_{t\to 0}t\star x_{1}=x_{0}
. This defines a
\lambda
-equivariant section
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to\mathcal{P}
.

\Box

This also completes the proof of Lemma
1.D
.

\Box

Let
X,G,\mathcal{L}
be a projective
G
-scheme together with a

G
-linearized line bundle
\mathcal{L}
. A point

is
GIT-stable with respect to
\mathcal{L}
if and only if the induced point

\overline{x}\in[X/G]({\overline{k}})
is
\mathcal{L}
-stable.

As
X
is projective, given
x\in X({\overline{k}})
and a one parameter
subgroup
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
we obtain an equivariant map

f_{\lambda,x}\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to X
and thus a morphism
\overline{f}\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to X
. By Lemma

all
very close degenerations arise in this way. As

\wt(\overline{f}^{*}\mathcal{L})=\wt(f^{*}\mathcal{L})
we therefore find that

-stable.

1.E.
Stability of vector bundles on curves

We want to show how the classical notion of stability for

G
-bundles arises as
\mathcal{L}
-stability. For the sake of clarity we
include the case of vector bundles first. Let
C
be a smooth,
projective, geometrically connected curve over
k
and denote by

\Bun_{n}^{d}
the stack of vector bundles of rank
n
and degree
d
on

C
.

1.E.a.
The line bundle

A natural line bundle on
\Bun_{n}^{d}
is given by the determinant of
cohomology
\mathcal{L}_{\det}
, i.e., for any vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
C
we
have
\mathcal{L}_{\det,\mathcal{E}}:=\det(H^{*}(C,\cEnd(\mathcal{E})))^{-1},
and more
generally for any
f\colon T\to\Bun_{n}^{d}
corresponding to family

\mathcal{E}
on
C\times T
one defines

f^{*}\mathcal{L}_{\det}:=\big(\det{\mathbb{R}}\mathit{pr}_{T,*}\cEnd(\mathcal{E})\big)^{\vee}.

Since any vector bundle admits
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
as central automorphisms, to apply our criterion we need to pass to the rigidified stack
\overline{\Bun}_{n}^{d}:=\Bun_{n}^{d}\!\!\fatslash\,{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
obtained by dividing all automorphism groups by
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
(
[
1
, Theorem 5.1.5]
). To obtain a line bundle on this stack, we need a line bundle on
\Bun_{n}^{d}
on which the central
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-automorphisms act trivially. This is the reason why we use the determinant of
H^{*}(C,\cEnd(\mathcal{E}))
instead of
H^{*}(C,\mathcal{E})
. It is known that up to multiples and bundles pulled back from the Picard variety this is the only such line bundle on
\Bun_{n}^{d}
(see e.g.
[
11
]
which also gives some history on the Picard group of
\Bun_{n}^{d}
).

1.E.b.
Very close degenerations of bundles

To classify maps
f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\Bun_{n}^{d}
we have as in
Lemma
1.D
.

There is an equivalence

\Map([{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}],\Bun_{n}^{d})\cong\langle(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{i})_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}|\mathcal{E}^{i}\subset\mathcal{E}^{i-1}\subset\mathcal{E},\cup\mathcal{E}^{i}=\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}^{i}=0\text{ for }i\gg 0\rangle,

i.e. giving a vector bundle, together with a weighted filtration is
equivalent to giving a morphism
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\Bun_{n}^{d}
.

We give the reformulation to fix the signs: A vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
C
together with a weighted filtration
\mathcal{E}^{i}\subset\mathcal{E}^{i-1}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{E}
the Rees construction
\Rees(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet}):=\oplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathcal{E}^{i}x^{-i}
defines an
\mathcal{O}_{C}[x]
module, i.e. a family on
C\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
which is
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
equivariant for the action defined on the coordinate parameter with
\Rees(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet})|_{C\times 0}\cong\mathrm{gr}(\mathcal{E}^{\bullet})
.

For the converse we argue as in Lemma
1.D
: Any morphism
f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\Bun_{n}^{d}
defines a morphism
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\Aut_{\Bun_{n}}(f(0))
, i.e., a grading on the bundle
f(0)
such that the corresponding filtration lifts canonically to the family.

\Box

1.E.c.
Computing the numerical invariants

Given a very close degeneration
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\Bun_{n}^{d}
we can
easily compute
\wt(\mathcal{L}_{\det}|_{f(0)})
, as follows. We use the
notation of the preceding lemma and write
\mathcal{E}_{i}:=\mathcal{E}^{i}/(\mathcal{E}^{i+1})

so that
f(0)=\oplus\mathcal{E}_{i}
. Note that
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
acts with weight
-i

on
\mathcal{E}_{i}
. Denoting further

\mu(\mathcal{E}_{i}):=\frac{\deg{\mathcal{E}_{i}}}{\rk{\mathcal{E}_{i}}}
we find

\displaystyle\wt(\mathcal{L}_{\det}|_{f(0)})

\displaystyle=-\wt_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}(\det H^{*}(C,\oplus\cHom(\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{j}))),

because
\mathcal{L}_{det}
was defined to be the dual of the determinant of
cohomology. As
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
acts with weight
(i-j)
on

\cHom(\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{j})
, it acts with the same weight on the cohomology
groups, taking determinants we find the weight
(i-j)\chi(\cHom(\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{j}))
on
\det H^{*}(C,\cHom(\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{j}))
. Thus by
Riemann-Roch we find

\displaystyle\wt(\mathcal{L}_{\det}|_{f(0)})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(j-i)\chi(\cHom(\mathcal{E}_{i},\mathcal{E}_{j}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}(j-i)\big(\rk(\mathcal{E}_{i})\deg(\mathcal{E}_{j})-\rk(\mathcal{E}_{j})\deg(\mathcal{E}_{i})+\rk(\mathcal{E}_{i})\rk(\mathcal{E}_{j})(1-g)\big)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i<j}(j-i)(\rk(\mathcal{E}_{i})\deg(\mathcal{E}_{j})-\rk(\mathcal{E}_{j})\deg(\mathcal{E}_{i})).

As it is more common to express the condition in terms of the subbundles
\mathcal{E}^{l}
instead of the subquotients
\mathcal{E}_{i}
let us replace the factors
(j-i)
by a summation over
l
with
i\leq l<j
:

\displaystyle\wt(\mathcal{L}_{\det}|_{f(0)})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{l}(\sum_{i\leq l}\rk(\mathcal{E}_{i}))(\sum_{j>l}\deg(\mathcal{E}_{j}))-(\sum_{i\leq l}\deg(\mathcal{E}_{i}))(\sum_{j>l}\rk(\mathcal{E}_{j}))

\displaystyle=2\sum_{l}\rk(\mathcal{E}^{l})(n-\rk(\mathcal{E}^{l}))(\mu(\mathcal{E}^{l})-\mu(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}^{l})).

This is
<0
unless
\mu(\mathcal{E}^{i})>\mu(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}^{i})
for some
i
.
Conversely, if
\mu(\mathcal{E}^{i})>\mu(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}^{i})
for some
i
, then the
two step filtration
0\subset\mathcal{E}^{i}\subset\mathcal{E}
defines a very
close degeneration of positive weight. Thus we find the classical
condition:

A vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
is
\mathcal{L}_{\det}
-stable if and only if for all
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
we have

\mu(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})<\mu(\mathcal{E}).

1.F.
G-bundles on curves

Let us formulate the analog for
G
-bundles, where
G
is a
semisimple group over
k
and we assume
k={\overline{k}}
to be
algebraically closed. We denote by
\Bun_{G}
the stack of
G
-bundles
on
C
.

1.F.a.
The line bundle

For the stability condition we need a line bundle on
\Bun_{G}
. One
way to construct a positive line bundle is to choose the adjoint
representation
\Ad:G\to\textrm{GL}(\Lie(G))
, which defines for any

G
-bundle
\mathcal{P}
its adjoint bundle
\Ad(\mathcal{P}):=\mathcal{P}\times^{G}\Lie(G)

and set:

\mathcal{L}_{\det}|_{\mathcal{P}}:=\det H^{*}(C,\Ad(\mathcal{P}))^{\vee}.

If
G

is simple and simply connected, it is known that
\Pic(\Bun_{G})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
(e.g.
[
11
]
). In general
\mathcal{L}_{\det}
will not
generate the Picard group, but since our stability condition does
not change if we replace
\mathcal{L}
by a multiple of the bundle this line
bundle will suffice for us.

1.F.b.
Very close degenerations of
G
-bundles

Recall from section
1.D
that for a cocharacter

\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
we denote by

P_{\lambda},U_{\lambda},L_{\lambda}
the corresponding parabolic
subgroup, its unipotent radical and the Levi subgroup.

To understand very close degenerations of bundles will amount to the
observation that Lemma
1.D
has an extension that holds for
families of bundles.

The source of degenerations is the following analog of the Rees
construction. Given
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
we obtain a
homomorphism of group schemes over
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
:

\displaystyle\conj_{\lambda}\colon P_{\lambda}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

\displaystyle\to P_{\lambda}\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

\displaystyle(p,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto(\lambda(t)p\lambda(t)^{-1},t).

By
[
30
, Proposition 4.2]
this homomorphism extends to a
morphism of group schemes over
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda}\colon P_{\lambda}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}

\displaystyle\to P_{\lambda}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}

in such a way that
\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda}(p,0)=\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)p\lambda(t)^{-1}\in L_{\lambda}\times 0
.

Moreover, these morphisms are
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
equivariant with respect to
the action
(\conj_{\lambda},\act)
on
P_{\lambda}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
.

Given a
P_{\lambda}
bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
on a scheme
X
this
morphism defines a
P_{\lambda}
bundle on
X\times[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]

by:

\Rees(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},\lambda):=[\big((\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1})\times_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}^{\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda}}(P_{\lambda}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1})\big)/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}],

where
\times_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}^{\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda}}
denotes the bundle induced via
the morphism
\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda}
, i.e., we take the product over
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}

and divide by the diagonal action of the group scheme

P_{\lambda}\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
, which acts on the right factor via

\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda}
. By construction this bundle satisfies

\Rees(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},\lambda)|_{X\times 1}\cong\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
and

\Rees(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},\lambda)|_{X\times 0}\cong\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}/U_{\lambda}\times^{L_{\lambda}}P_{\lambda}

is the analog
of the associated graded bundle.

If
\lambda^{\prime}\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to P_{\lambda}
is conjugate to
\lambda
in
P_{\lambda}
, say by an element
u\in U_{\lambda}
then
P:=P_{\lambda}=P_{\lambda}^{\prime}
and

\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda^{\prime}}(p,t)=\mathrm{gr}_{\lambda}(upu^{-1},t).

Therefore we also have

\Rees(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},\lambda)\cong\Rees(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},\lambda^{\prime}),

which tells us that the Rees construction only depends on the reduction to
P
and the homomorphism

\overline{\lambda}\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to Z(P/U)\subset P/U.

In the case
G=\textrm{GL}(V)
this datum is the analog of a weighted filtration on
V
, whereas
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to P\subset\textrm{GL}(V)
would define a grading on
V
.

Given a
G
-bundle
\mathcal{E}
, a cocharacter
\lambda
and a reduction

\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
of
\mathcal{E}
to a
P_{\lambda}
bundle the
G
-bundle

\Rees(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},\lambda)\times^{P_{\lambda}}G
defines a morphism

f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\Bun_{G}
with
f(1)=\mathcal{E}
. We claim that all
very close degenerations arise in this way:

Let
G
be a split reductive group over
k
. Given a very close degeneration
f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\Bun_{G}
corresponding to a family
\mathcal{E}
of
G
-bundles on
X\times[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
there exist:

(1)

a cocharacter
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
, canonical up to conjugation,

(2)

a reduction
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
of the bundle
\mathcal{E}
to
P_{\lambda}
,

(3)

an isomorphism
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\cong\Rees(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}|_{X\times 1},\lambda)
.

Given
\mathcal{E}
we will again denote by
\mathcal{E}_{0}:=\mathcal{E}|_{X\times 0}
and
\mathcal{E}_{1}:=\mathcal{E}|_{X\times 1}
.
We define the group scheme
\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}_{0}}:=\Aut_{G}(\mathcal{P}_{0}/X)=\mathcal{E}_{0}\times^{G,\conj}G
. This is a group scheme over
X
that is an inner form of
G\times X
. And the morphism
f|_{[0/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]}
induces a morphism
\lambda_{0}\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times X\to\Aut_{G}(\mathcal{E}_{0}/X)=\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}_{0}}
.

As in Lemma
1.D
(2) it is convenient to replace
\mathcal{E}
by the
\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}_{0}}
-torsor
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}:=\Isom_{G}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{0})
. We know that
\lambda_{0}
defines a parabolic subgroup
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{0}}\subset\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}_{0}}
and the canonical reduction of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0}
to
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{0}}
lifts uniquely to a reduction
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
by the same argument used in Lemma
1.D
. The last step of the proof is then to consder the twisted action
\star\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\times\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\lambda}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
. Note that the fixed points for the action are simply the points in the Levi subgroup
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda_{0}}\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0}=\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}_{0}}
.

The needed analog of the Białynicki-Birula decomposition is a result of Hesselinck
[
30
]
: By the lemma we already know that for all geometric points
x
of
X
and
p\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\lambda}|_{x}
all limit points
\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)\star p
exist. On the other hand by
[
30
, Proposition 4.2]
the functor whose
S
-points are morphisms
S\times{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
such that the restriction to
S\times{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
is given by the action of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
on
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
is represented by a closed subscheme of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
. Thus the functor is represented by
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{\lambda}
.

Thus the twisted action
\star
on
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{\prime}
extends to a morphism
\overline{\star}\colon{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{\prime}
. In particular this induces
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\times\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,1}\to\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
and thus a morphism of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{0}}
-bundles
\Rees(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda,1},\lambda)\to\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
.

This proves that the statement of the Lemma holds if we replace
G
by
\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}_{0}}
.

To compare this with the description given in the lemma note that for any geometric point
x\in X({\overline{k}})
the choice of a trivialization
\mathcal{E}_{0,x}\cong G
defines an isomorphism
G^{\mathcal{P}_{0}}\cong G
and therefore
\lambda_{0}|_{x}
defines a defines a conjugacy class of cocharacters
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G
. This conjugacy class is locally constant (and therefore does not depend on the choice of
x
) because we know from
[
40
, Exposé XI, Corollary 5.2bis]
that the scheme parametrizing conjugation of cocharacters
\Transp_{G}(\lambda_{0},\lambda)
is smooth over
X
. (This is the analog of the statement for vector bundles, that a
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
action on
\mathcal{E}
allows to decompose
\mathcal{E}=\oplus\mathcal{E}_{i}
as bundles, i.e. the dimension of the weight spaces of the fibers is constant over
x
.)
This defines
\lambda
. To conclude we only need to recall that reductions of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{0}}
correspond to reductions of
\mathcal{E}
to
P_{\lambda}
:

Let
\mathcal{G}\to X
be a reductive group scheme,
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m,X}\to\mathcal{G}
a cocharacter and
\mathcal{E}
a
\mathcal{G}
-torsor over
X
. Then a natural bijection between:

(1)

Reductions
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
of
\mathcal{E}
to
P_{\lambda}
,

(2)

Parabolic subgroups
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}=\Aut_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{E}/X)
that are locally conjugate to
P_{\lambda}
,

is given by
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\mapsto\mathcal{P}:=\Aut_{P_{\lambda}}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}/X)\subset\Aut_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{E}/X)
.

A reduction of
\mathcal{E}
is a section
s\colon X\to\mathcal{E}/P_{\lambda}
. Note that
\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}
acts on
\mathcal{E}/P_{\lambda}
and
\Stab_{\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}}(s)\subset\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}
is a parabolic subgroup that is locally of the same type as
P_{\lambda}
, becasuse this holds if
\mathcal{E}
is trivial and
s
lifts to a section of
\mathcal{E}
. Locally in the smooth topology we may assume these conditions.

Similarly given
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}
locally the action of
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathcal{E}/P_{\lambda}
has a unique fixed point and this defines a section.

\Box

Using the lemma we find that both sections of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}_{\lambda_{0}}
and sections of
\mathcal{E}/P_{\lambda}
correspond to parabolic subgroups of
\Aut_{G}(\mathcal{E})=\Aut_{\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}_{0}}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
. This proves the lemma.

\Box

Note that in the above result we assumed that
G
is a split group. In general, we saw that the natural subgroup that contains a cocharacter is
\Aut_{G}(\mathcal{E}_{0})
, which is an inner form of
G
over
X
. In particular it may well happen that
G
does not admit any cocharacter or any parabolic subgroup.

This is apparent for example in the case

and

. For a
G
-torsor
\mathcal{E}
on

we will find a canonical reduction to a parabolic subgroup

but the descent datum will then only be given for
\mathcal{P}\subset\Aut_{G}(\mathcal{E}/X)
.

1.F.c.
The numerical criterion

Finally we have to compute the weight of
\mathcal{L}_{\det}
on very close
degenerations.

The computation is the same as for vector bundles and for the
criterion it is sometimes convenient to reduce it to reductions for
maximal parabolic subgroups. Let us choose
T\subset B\subset G
a
maximal torus and a Borel subgroup and
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to G

a dominant cocharacter, i.e.
\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\geq 0

for all roots such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\in\Lie(B)
. Let us denote by

I
the set of positive simple roots with respect to
(T,B)
and by

I_{P}:=\{\alpha_{i}\in I|\lambda(\alpha_{i})=0\}
the simple roots

\alpha_{i}
for which
-\alpha_{i}
is also a root of
P_{\lambda}
. For

j\in I
let us denote by
\check{\omega}_{j}\in X_{*}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
the
cocharacter defined by
\check{\omega}_{j}(\alpha_{i})=\delta_{ij}
.
And by
P_{j}
the corresponding maximal parabolic subgroup.

Then
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to Z(L_{\lambda})\subset L_{\lambda}\subset P_{\lambda}
. Thus for any very close degeneration
f\colon[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to\Bun_{G}
given by
\Rees(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda},\lambda)
the

\mathcal{L}_{\det}
defines a morphism

\wt_{\mathcal{L}}\colon X_{*}(Z_{\lambda})\subset\Aut_{\Bun_{G}}(f(0))\to{\mathbb{Z}}.

Then
\lambda=\sum_{j\in I-I_{P}}a_{j}\check{\omega}_{j}

\displaystyle\wt(\mathcal{L}_{\det}|_{f(0)})

\displaystyle=\wt_{\mathcal{L}}(\lambda)=\sum_{j\in I-I_{P}}a_{j}\wt_{\mathcal{L}}(\check{\omega}_{j}).

For each

\Lie(G)=\oplus_{i}\Lie(G)_{i}

and also of the Levi subgroups

of

\displaystyle\wt_{\mathcal{L}}(\check{\omega}_{j})

\displaystyle=-\wt_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}(\det H^{*}(C,\oplus\mathcal{E}_{0,\lambda}\times^{L_{\lambda}}\Lie(G)_{i}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}i\cdot\chi(\mathcal{E}_{0,\lambda}\times^{L_{\lambda}}\Lie(G)_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}i(\deg(\mathcal{E}_{0,\lambda}\times^{L_{\lambda}}\Lie(G)_{i})+\dim(\Lie(G)_{i})(1-g))

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i>0}i(\deg(\mathcal{E}_{0,\lambda}\times^{L_{\lambda}}\Lie(G)_{i})

Now

1.F.d.
Parabolic structures

Parabolic

-bundles are
G
-bundles equipped with a reduction of
structure group at a finite set of closed points.

Let us fix notation for these. We keep our reductive group
G
, the
curve
C
and a finite set of rational points
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}\subset C(k)
and parabolic subgroups

\Bun_{G,\underline{P},\underline{x}}(S):=\left\langle(\mathcal{E},s_{1},\dots,s_{n})|\mathcal{E}\in\Bun_{G}(S),s_{i}\colon S\to\mathcal{E}_{x_{i}\times S}/P_{i}\text{ sections }\right\rangle

In particular, very close degenerations of a parabolic

There are more line bundles on

The weight of a this line bundle on a very close degeneration, is

\chi_{i}
with the one parameter subgroup in

We will come back to this in the section on parahoric bundles
(Section

).

1.G.
The example of chains of bundles on curves

We briefly include the example of chains of bundles as an easy

Again we fix a curve

The forgetful map to

\mathcal{L}_{\det}
on the stacks

[

(1)

(2)

Fix any point

and

. Set

To classify maps

Thus we find that a very close degeneration of a chain

\displaystyle\wt(\mathcal{L}_{\det}|_{\mathrm{gr}(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})})

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i}\rk(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{\bullet})(n-\rk(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{\bullet}))(\mu(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet}/\mathcal{E}^{i}_{\bullet})-\mu(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{\bullet}))

\displaystyle=2\sum_{i}\rk(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{\bullet})n(\mu(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})-\mu(\mathcal{E}^{i}_{\bullet})).

\displaystyle\wt(\mathcal{L}_{j}|_{\mathrm{gr}(\mathcal{E}_{\bullet})})

2.
A criterion for separatedness of the stable locus

We now want to give a criterion which guarantees that the set of

\mathcal{L}
-stable points is a separated substack if the stack
\mathcal{M}
and
the line bundle
\mathcal{L}
satisfy suitable local conditions (Proposition

2.B
). The article
[
35
]
by Martens and Thaddeus was
an important help to find the criterion. Again, the proofs turn out
to be quite close to arguments that already appear in Mumford’s
book.

2.A.
Motivation from the valuative criterion

Let us first sketch the basic idea. Let
\mathcal{M}
be an algebraic stack.
For the valuative criterion for separatedness one considers pairs

f,g\colon{\mathbb{D}}=\Spec k[\![t]\!]\to\mathcal{M}
together with an
isomorphism
f|_{\Spec k(\!(t)\!)}\cong g|_{\Spec k(\!(t)\!)}
and
tries to prove that for such pairs
f\cong g
. The basic datum is
therefore a morphism

f\cup g\colon{\mathbb{D}}\cup_{\mathring{{\mathbb{D}}}}{\mathbb{D}}\to\mathcal{M}.

Note that the scheme
{\mathbb{D}}\cup_{\mathring{{\mathbb{D}}}}{\mathbb{D}}
is a completed
neighborhood of the origins in the affine line with doubled origin

{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\cup_{{\mathbb{G}}_{m}}{\mathbb{A}}^{1}\cong[{\mathbb{A}}^{2}-\{0\}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m},(t,t^{-1})]\subset[{\mathbb{A}}^{2}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}].

Thus also the union of two copies of
{\mathbb{D}}
along their generic point
is naturally an open subscheme of a larger stack:

{\mathbb{D}}\cup_{\mathring{{\mathbb{D}}}}{\mathbb{D}}\subset[({\mathbb{A}}^{2}\times_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}{\mathbb{D}})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]=[\Spec(k[x,y]\otimes_{k[\pi],\pi=xy}k[\![\pi]\!])/{\mathbb{G}}_{m},(t,t^{-1})],

where the right hand side inserts a single point
[0/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
.

Further, the coordinate axes
\Spec k[x],\Spec k[y]\subset\Spec k[x,y]={\mathbb{A}}^{2}
define closed embeddings
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]\to[({\mathbb{A}}^{2}\times_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}{\mathbb{D}})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
that intersect in the origin
[0/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
.
As the weights of the
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
action on the two axes are
1
and

-1
we again see that for any line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on this stack the
weights of the restriction to the two copies of
[{\mathbb{A}}^{1}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]

differ by a sign.

In terms of
\mathcal{L}
-stability on
\mathcal{M}
this means that whenever a
morphism
f,g\colon{\mathbb{D}}\cup_{\mathring{{\mathbb{D}}}}{\mathbb{D}}\to\mathcal{M}
extends to

[({\mathbb{A}}^{2}\times_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}{\mathbb{D}})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
, only one of the two origins
can map to an
\mathcal{L}
-stable point.

As the complement of the origin
[0/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
is of codimension
2
in
the above stack one could expect that a morphism
f\cup g\colon{\mathbb{D}}\cup_{\mathring{{\mathbb{D}}}}{\mathbb{D}}\to\mathcal{M}
extends to a morphism of some
blow up of
[({\mathbb{A}}^{2}\times_{{\mathbb{A}}^{1}}{\mathbb{D}})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}]
centered at the
origin. This will be the assumption that we will impose on
\mathcal{M}
.

The basic observation then is that the exceptional fiber of such a
blow up can be chosen to be a chain of equivariant projective lines
and it will turn out that the weight argument indicated above still
works for such chains if the line bundle
\mathcal{L}
satisfies a numerical
positivity condition.

Combining the above computations we propose the following
definitions:

Let

be an algebraic stack, locally of finite type with affine diagonal. We say that

such that

\overline{f}\colon\mathrm{Bl}_{\tilde{I}}(\overline{\ST}_{R})\to\mathcal{M}
.

Given a line bundle

3.
Torsors under parahoric group schemes on curves

In this section we give our main application to moduli of torsors
under Bruhat-Tits group schemes on curves as introduced by Pappas
and Rapoport
[
37
]
. It will turn out that the notion of
stability we find is a variant of the one introduced by Balaji and
Seshadri in the case of generically split group schemes. We will
then apply this to construct coarse moduli spaces for stable torsors

3.A.
The setup

We fix a smooth projective, geometrically connected curve
C/k
and

\mathcal{G}\to C
a parahoric Bruhat-Tits group scheme in the sense of

[
37

\mathcal{G}

[

(see Appendix

is
not a reductive group.

We will denote by
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
the moduli stack of
\mathcal{G}
-torsors on

for a

It may be helpful to keep the following examples in mind:

(1)

(Parabolic structures) Let

(2)

(The unitary group) Suppose

{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
.

(2)

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(S):=\{g\in\mathcal{G}(S)|\lambda(t)g\lambda(t)^{-1}=g\}
the centralizer of
\lambda
.

(3)

\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}(S):=\{g\in\mathcal{G}(S)|\lim_{t\to 0}\lambda(t)g\lambda(t)^{-1}=1\}

These are analogs of parabolic subgroups and Levi subgroups of

\mathcal{G}
. Alternatively one could define such analogs by taking the
closure of parabolics in the generic fiber
\mathcal{G}_{K}
. The following
Lemma shows that this leads to an equivalent notion:

Let
R
be a discrete valuation ring and
\mathcal{G}_{R}
a parahoric
Bruhat–Tits group scheme over
\Spec R
.

(1)

Given a 1-parameter subgroup
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m,R}\to\mathcal{G}
the group
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
is the closure of
\mathcal{P}_{K,\lambda}\subset\mathcal{G}_{K}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is the closure of the Levi subgroup
\mathcal{L}_{K,\lambda}\subset\mathcal{G}_{K}
and
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
is the closure of the unipotent radical of
\mathcal{P}_{K,\lambda}
. The group
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is again a Bruhat–Tits group scheme.

(2)

Let
P_{K}\subset\mathcal{G}_{K}
be a parabolic subgroup and denote by
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{G}
the closure of
P_{K}
in
\mathcal{G}
. Then there exists a 1-parameter subgroup
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m,R}\to\mathcal{G}_{R}
such that
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
.

(3)

Let
P_{K}\subset\mathcal{G}_{K}
be a parabolic subgroup and
\lambda_{K}\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m,K}\to\mathcal{G}_{K}
be a cocharacter such that
P_{K}=P_{\lambda_{K}}
. Then there exist an element
u\in\mathcal{U}(K)
such that
\lambda^{u}_{K}:=u\lambda_{K}u^{-1}
extends to a morphism
\lambda^{u}\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\mathcal{P}
. The class of
u
in
\mathcal{U}(K)/\mathcal{U}(R)
is uniquely determined by
\lambda_{K}
.

Before proving the lemma let us note that part (3) will be useful to
define a Rees construction for
\mathcal{G}
-bundles. In the global setup of
a group scheme
\mathcal{G}/C
on a curve we cannot expect that every
parabolic subgroup
P_{k(C)}\subset\mathcal{G}_{k(C)}
can be defined by a
globally defined cocharacter
{\mathbb{G}}_{m,C}\to\mathcal{G}
, as
\mathcal{G}
may not
admit any non-trivial cocharacters. However, given
\lambda_{k(C)}

part (3) will give us a canonical inner form of
\mathcal{G}
for which

\lambda
extends.

In the setup of the above lemma given two parabolic subrgoups
\mathcal{P}_{K},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{K}\subset\mathcal{G}_{K}
that are conjugate over
\mathcal{G}_{K}
, their closures
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{G}
need not be conjugate. Roughly this is because the closure contains information about the relative position of the generic parabolic and the parahoric structure in the special fiber. More precisely, the Iwasawa decomposition we know that for any Borel subgroup
\mathcal{B}_{K}\subset\mathcal{G}_{K}
we have
\mathcal{G}(K)=\mathcal{B}(K)W\mathcal{G}(R)
(
[
16
, Propositions 4.4.3, 7.3.1]
). This implies that there are only finitely many conjugacy classes
\mathcal{P}
of closures of generic parabolic subgroups.

To show the first part of (1) we have to show that the generic fibers of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda},\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
are dense.
As
\mathcal{G}
is smooth over
R
the fixed point scheme
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
and the concentrator scheme
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
are both regular (
[
30
, Theorem 5.8]
). Let
x\in\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}(R/(\pi))\subset\mathcal{G}(R/(\pi))
be a closed point of the special fiber then
T_{\mathcal{G},x}\to T_{\Spec R,0}
is surjective and equivariant, so there exists an invariant tangent vector lifting the tangent direction in
0
. Thus
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is smooth over
R
and therefore the generic fiber is dense. The morphism
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\to\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is an affine bundle (
[
30
, Theorem 5.8]
), so the generic fiber of
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
must also be dense and
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda}
is even an affine bundle over
\Spec R
.

Let us prove (2) and (3). Any parabolic subgroup
P_{K}
of the reductive group
\mathcal{G}_{K}
is of the form
P_{K}=P_{K}(\lambda)
for some
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m,K}\to\mathcal{G}_{K}

[
41
, Lemma 15.1.2]
. The image of
\lambda
is contained in a maximal split torus of
\mathcal{G}_{K}
and these are all conjugate over
K

[
41
, Theorem 15.2.6]
. Fixing a maximal split torus
\mathcal{T}_{R}\subset\mathcal{G}_{R}
we therefore find
g\in G(K)
such that
g\lambda g^{-1}\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\mathcal{G}_{K}
factors through
\mathcal{T}
. By Iwasawa decomposition we can write
g=kwu
with
k\in\mathcal{G}(R),w\in N(\mathcal{T}),p\in\mathcal{U}(K)
. Thus we can conjugate
\lambda
by an element of
\mathcal{U}(K)
such that it extends to
\mathcal{G}_{R}
. Applying (1) to this subgroup we find (2). It also shows the existence statement in (3). To show uniqueness assume that
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m,R}\to\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
is given and that
u\in\mathcal{U}(K)
is such that
u\lambda u^{-1}
still defines a morphism over
R
. Recall that
\mathcal{U}_{K}
has a canonical filtration
\mathcal{U}_{K,\geq r}\subset\dots\subset\mathcal{U}_{K,\geq 1}=\mathcal{U}_{K}
such that
\mathcal{U}_{K,i}/\mathcal{U}_{K,{i+}}\cong\prod_{\alpha|\alpha\circ\lambda=i}\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
. Write
\mathcal{U}_{i}:=\prod_{\alpha|\alpha\circ\lambda=i}\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}
and decompose
u=u_{1}\cdot\dots\cdot u_{r}
.

We know that
u\lambda(a)u^{-1}\in P(R)
for all
a\in R^{*}
, i.e.
\mathcal{U}(R)\ni u\lambda(a)u^{-1}\lambda^{-1}(a)
. The image of this element in
\mathcal{U}_{\geq 1}/\mathcal{U}_{\geq 2}
is
u_{1}au_{1}^{-1}a^{-1}
which can only be in
\mathcal{U}_{1}(R)
for all
a
if
u_{1}\in\mathcal{U}(R)
, but then we can replace
u
by
u_{2}\dots u_{r}
and conclude by induction.

Finally we need to show that the group scheme
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
in (1) is a Bruhat–Tits group scheme.
By construction it suffices to show this after an étale base change
\Spec R^{\prime}\to\Spec R
, so we may assume that
\mathcal{G}_{R}
is quasi-split, i.e. that
\mathcal{G}_{K}
contains a maximal torus
\mathcal{T}_{K}
and that
\mathcal{G}
contains the connected Néron model
\mathcal{T}
of
\mathcal{T}_{K}
.

As conjugation by elements of
\mathcal{G}(R)
produces isomorphic group schemes we may assume as above that
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m,R}\to\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{G}
. The scheme
\mathcal{G}_{R}
is given by a valued root system and the restriction of this to the roots of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,K}
defines a Bruhat–Tits group scheme with generic fiber
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda,K}
, contained in
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
. Finally, by definition the special fiber of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
is the centralizer of a torus, so it is connected. Thus the smooth scheme
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
has to be equal to this Bruhat–Tits scheme.

\Box

3.E.

Let us fix our group scheme

In this section we want to describe
\mathcal{L}

\mathcal{E}
. We

Fix
\mathcal{E}

\mathcal{G}

For any point

\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}}\colon\mathcal{B}_{x}^{\mathcal{E}}\to\mathcal{G}_{x}^{\mathcal{E}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\chi}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathbb{G}}_{m}.

This morphism factors through
\mathcal{T}_{x}^{\mathcal{E}}

\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}},\lambda\rangle:=\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}},\lambda|_{x}\rangle
.

The Rees construction applied to a generic dominant 1-parameter subgroup gives us a bundle
\mathcal{E}_{0}
, that is induced from the
\mathcal{T}
bundle
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}}/\mathcal{U}=:\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T}}
.

To compute the weights, we can decompose the adjoint bundle of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
into weight spaces:

\ad(\mathcal{E}_{0})=\ad(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{T}})\oplus_{a\in\Phi(\mathcal{G}_{\eta})}\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{E}_{0}}

and the Rees construction induces a filtration of
\ad(\mathcal{E})
such that the associated graded pieces are
\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{E}}\cong\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{E}_{0}}
.

For any 1-parameter subgroup
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\to\mathcal{G}_{\eta}^{\mathcal{E}}
which is dominant with respect to
\mathcal{B}
we then have:

\wt_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}}}(\lambda):=\wt_{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda)=\sum_{a\in\Phi}\chi(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{E}})\langle a,\lambda\rangle+\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}},\lambda\rangle.

As
\rk(\mathfrak{u}_{a})=\rk(\mathfrak{u}_{-a})
we can further compute:

\displaystyle\wt_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\Phi}\chi(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{E}})\langle a,\lambda\rangle+\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}},\lambda_{x}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\Phi}\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{E}})\langle a,\lambda\rangle+\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}},\lambda_{x}\rangle

In the case of unramified, constant group schemes, the degree
\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a})
is a linear function in the root
a
. This does no longer hold for parahoric group schemes, but we still have relations:

At the generic point
\eta
of
C
we get a canonical isomorphism

k_{a}\colon\mathfrak{u}_{-a,\eta}^{\mathcal{E}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}(\mathfrak{u}_{a,\eta}^{\mathcal{E}})^{\vee}

from the Killing form and this extends to an isomorphism at all points
c\in C
where
\mathcal{G}_{c}
is reductive. Therefore, the determinant of
k_{a}
defines a divisor

D_{a}=\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}f_{a,x}^{\mathcal{B}}x.

With this notation we have

\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{-a}^{\mathcal{E}})=-\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{E}})-\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}f_{a,x}^{\mathcal{B}}.

Here we denote the coefficients by the letter
f
because for a global torus
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{G}
with valuated root systems

(see Section
4
) these numbers are
-(f_{a,x}+f_{-a,x})
. As any two tori are conjugate, we always find

\left|\frac{f_{a,x}^{\mathcal{B}}}{\rk(\mathfrak{u}_{a})}\right|\leq 1.

Thus we find

\displaystyle\wt_{\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a\in\Phi}\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{E}})\langle a,\lambda\rangle+\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}},\lambda\rangle

\displaystyle=2\sum_{a\in\Phi^{+}}\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{E}})\langle a,\lambda\rangle+\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}}+\sum_{a\in\Phi^{+}_{\mathcal{B}}}f_{a,x}^{\mathcal{B}}a,\lambda\rangle

(3.1)

3.F.
Canonical reduction for
\mathcal{G}
-torsors

In this section we want to check that the canonical reduction of

G
-bundles introduced by Behrend also exists for
\mathcal{G}
-bundles. We
will then use this to deduce that the stack of stable and semistable

\mathcal{G}
-bundles are open substacks of finite type of
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
.

In
[
22
]
Halpern-Leistner gives general criteria for the
existence and uniqueness of canonical reductions for

\theta
-reductive stacks. Unfortunately,
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
does not
satisfy this condition, so that we have to give a separate argument.
It will turn out that once we formulate the classical approach for

G
-bundles (see
[
24
]
,
[
8
]
) in a suitable way,
most of the arguments generalize to this framework. This was also
explained by Gaitsgory and Lurie in
[
21
, Section
10]
for a notion of stability induced from

G
-bundles. Note that Harder and Stuhler also introduced the
concept of canonical reductions for Bruhat–Tits groups in the
adelic description of the points of the moduli stack

[
25
]
.

Let us fix our group scheme
\mathcal{G}
and a line bundle
\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{L}_{\det,\underline{\chi}}
as in Section
3.B
. In general, to
define canonical destabilizing 1-parameter subgroups one needs to
fix a norm on the set of all such subgroups. We will simply use the
invariant form
(,)
on
X_{*}(T_{\overline{\eta}})
from the previous
section.

As for parabolic bundles, we will need the following assumption on

\underline{\chi}
. We will call
\mathcal{L}

admissible
if
2\langle\chi_{x},\check{a}\rangle\leq\rk{\mathfrak{u}}_{a}\langle a,\check{a}\rangle
for all roots
a
of
\mathcal{G}_{x}
.

As in
[
22
]
a canonical reduction of a
\mathcal{G}
-torsor should be a
1-parameter subgroup
\lambda\in X_{*}(\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta})
such that

\frac{\wt(\lambda)}{||\lambda||}
is maximal. First of all this
number is bounded:

For every
\mathcal{G}
torsor
\mathcal{E}
there exists
c_{\mathcal{E}}>0
such that
\frac{\wt(\lambda)}{||\lambda||}\leq c
for all
\lambda\colon{\mathbb{G}}_{m,\eta}\to\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\eta}
.

As in the classical case for every reduction
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{P}}
of
\mathcal{E}
to a parabolic subgroup we have that
H^{0}(\ad(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{P}}))\subset H^{0}(\ad(\mathcal{E}))
.
By Riemann-Roch this implies that the degree of the unipotent radical
\deg(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{P}}\times^{\mathcal{P}}\mathfrak{u}_{\mathcal{P}})
is uniformly bounded above for all reductions.
In turn this gives for every reduction to a Borel subgroup
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}
an upper bound for the weight
\wt(\omega_{i})
for all dominant coweights
\omega_{i}
. As any dominant
\lambda
is a positive linear combination of these, this gives the required bound.

\Box

Notation.

For any
\mathcal{G}
-torsor
\mathcal{E}
over an algebraically closed field we define

\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{E}):=\sup_{\lambda}\frac{\wt_{\mathcal{E}}(\lambda)}{||\lambda||}.

3.F.a.
Comparing weights of different reductions

To characterize the canonical reduction we need to compare the
weights of different reductions.

Suppose
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}
are two Borel subgroups.
Any two such subgroups share a maximal split torus
S\subset\mathcal{T}_{\eta}\subset\mathcal{B}_{\eta}\cap\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{\eta}

(
[
13
, Proposition 4.4]
).

Let
\{a_{1},\dots a_{n}\}
be the positive simple roots of
\mathcal{B}
.

We say that
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
are neighboring reductions if there
exists
i_{0}
such that
-a_{i_{0}}
is a positive simple root of

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
and
a_{i}
are positive roots of
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
for all

i\neq i_{0}
.

In this case
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
generate a parabolic
\mathcal{P}_{1}
that is
minimal among the parabolic subgroups that are not Borel subgroups.
Denote by
\mathcal{L}_{1}
the corresponding Levi quotient.

Let
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be neighboring parabolic subgroups. Then we have:

(1)

\wt_{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda)=\wt_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in Z(\mathcal{L}_{I})
.

(2)

\wt_{\mathcal{B}}(\check{\alpha}_{i})+\wt_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(-\check{\alpha}_{i})\leq 0

As the weight only depends on the Rees construction for
\mathcal{E}
we may replace
\mathcal{E}
by the Rees construction applied to any
\lambda
that is dominant for
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
. Thus we may assume that
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\times^{\mathcal{L}_{1}}\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}
is induced from a
\mathcal{L}_{1}
torsor.
In this case (1) is immediate from the definition, as the Rees construction for
\lambda\in Z(L_{1})
does not change
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{L}_{1}}
.

Moreover,
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is of semisimple rank
1
and
\mathcal{B}_{1}:=\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{1}\cap\mathcal{L}_{1}
are Borel subgroups that are opposite over the generic point
\eta\in C
. Let us denote by
\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{u}^{\prime}
the Lie algebras of the corresponding unipotent radicals in
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and by
\mathfrak{u}^{-}:=\Lie(\mathcal{L}_{1})/\Lie(\mathcal{B}_{1})
and similarly
\mathfrak{u}^{\prime,-}
. Then we get an injective homomorphism

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\times^{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\mathfrak{u}\to\ad(\mathcal{E}_{L_{1}})\to\ad(\mathcal{E}_{L_{1}})/\ad(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{1}})\cong\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{1}}\times^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{1}}\mathfrak{u}^{-,\prime}.

If
a
is a multipliable root then the homomorphism respects the filtration on
\mathfrak{u},\mathfrak{u}^{\prime,-}
given by the roots
a,2a
.
So we find:

\displaystyle\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle\leq\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}})=-\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{-a}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}})-\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}f_{-a,x}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{B}})-\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}f_{a,x}^{\mathcal{B}}=\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{-a}^{\mathcal{B}})

\displaystyle\geq\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{-a}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}})

an the same hods for
\mathfrak{u}_{2a}
if
2a
is also a root.

Thus:

2(\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{B}})+\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{-a}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}))\leq\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}f_{-a,x}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}+f_{a,x}^{\mathcal{B}}.

Moreover, the map
\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{B}}\to\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}
is an

\mathcal{B}_{x}^{\mathcal{E}}
and

\mathcal{B}_{x}^{\prime,\mathcal{E}}

\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}_{1}},\check{\alpha}_{i_{0}}\rangle=-\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime}},-\check{\alpha}_{i_{0}}\rangle

\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}_{1}},\check{\alpha}_{i_{0}}\rangle=\langle\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime}},-\check{\alpha}_{i_{0}}\rangle

2\langle\chi_{x},\check{a}\rangle\leq\rk{\mathfrak{u}}_{a}\langle a,\check{a}\rangle

2(\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{a}^{\mathcal{B}})+\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{-a}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}))\langle a,\check{a}\rangle\leq\langle\sum_{x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G})}f_{-a,x}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}+f_{a,x}^{\mathcal{B}}+\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}_{1}}-\chi_{x}^{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime}},\check{a}\rangle.

And this means:

\displaystyle\wt_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}(\check{\alpha}_{i_{0}})+\wt_{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime}}(-\check{\alpha}_{i_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq 0

is admissible.

To compute
\wt_{\mathcal{B}}(\check{\alpha}_{i_{0}})+\wt_{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}(-\check{\alpha}_{i_{0}})

\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{P}_{1}}\cong\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\times^{\mathcal{B}_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{1}\cong\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{\prime}}\times^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{1}

\sum_{c=b+na}(\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{c,\eta}^{\mathcal{B}})-\deg(\mathfrak{u}_{c,\eta}^{\mathcal{B}^{\prime}}))\langle c,\check{\alpha}_{i_{0}}\rangle\leq 0

Summing over all

3.G.
Boundedness for stable
\mathcal{G}
-torsors

With the canonical reduction at hand we can now deduce:

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\det,\underline{\chi}}
be an admissible line bundle on
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
.
Then the stacks
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}^{st}\subset\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}^{sst}\subset\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
of
\mathcal{L}_{\det,\underline{\chi}}
-(semi)-stable
\mathcal{G}
-torsors are open substacks of finite type.

By Lemma
3.F.f
instability and strict semistability are stable under specialization. Therefore we only need to show that
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}^{sst}
is constructible and contained in a substack of finite type.
Again we argue as in
[
8
]
. First we show that for any
c\geq 0
the stack of
\mathcal{G}
-torsors of fixed degree satisfying
\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{E})\leq c
is of finite type. To prove this we may suppose that
\chi=0
, as the linear function
\lambda\mapsto\langle\chi,\lambda\rangle
only changes
\frac{\wt(\lambda)}{||\lambda||}
by a finite constant.

By
[
8
]
the claim holds if
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}_{0}
is a reductive group scheme over
C
. Moreover if
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{G}_{0}
is a parahoric group scheme mapping to
\mathcal{G}_{0}
such that
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
is an Iwahori group scheme at all
x\in\Ram(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
then the morphism
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}\to\Bun_{\mathcal{G}_{0}}
is a smooth morphism with fibers isomorphic to a product of flag varieties
\mathcal{G}_{0,x}/\mathcal{B}_{x}
. Moreover the cokernel of
\Lie(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\to\Lie(\mathcal{G}_{0})
is of finite length. Thus there exists a constant
d
such that for any
\mathcal{E}
is a
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
torsor we have that if
\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}\mathcal{G}_{0}
admits a reduction of slope
\mu(\mathcal{E}\times^{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}\mathcal{G}_{0})>c+d
then this reduction induces a reduction of
\mathcal{E}
of slope
\mu(\mathcal{E})>c
.

Therefore the result also holds for
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
. Now any parahoric group scheme contains an Iwahori group scheme so that by the same reasoning the result also holds if
\mathcal{G}
is any parahoric Bruhat–Tits group scheme such that
\mathcal{G}|_{C-\Ram(\mathcal{G})}
admits an unramified extension.

Now choose
\pi\colon\tilde{C}\to C
a finite Galois covering with group
\Gamma
such that
\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}|_{C-\Ram(\mathcal{G})}
is a generically split reductive group scheme on
\tilde{C}-\pi^{-1}(\Ram(\mathcal{G}))
, equipped with a
\Gamma
-action. We can extend this group scheme to a Bruhat–Tits group scheme
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
that is
\Gamma
equivariant and admits an morphism
\pi^{*}(\mathcal{G})\to\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
that is an isomorphism over
\tilde{C}-\pi^{-1}(\Ram(\mathcal{G}))
. We already know the result for
\Bun_{\tilde{\mathcal{G}}}
. Now if
\mathcal{E}
is a
\mathcal{G}
-torsor then
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}:=\pi^{*}{\mathcal{E}}\times^{\pi^{*}\mathcal{G}}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
is a
\Gamma
-equivariant
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}
torsor. Now if
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
admits a canonical reduction to a parabolic subgroup
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
this will define an equivariant reduction of
\pi^{*}\mathcal{E}|_{C-\Ram(\mathcal{G})}
and therefore a reduction of
\mathcal{E}
.

Again we can compare the weights of the reductions because

\pi_{*}\ad(\pi^{*}(\mathcal{E}))=\ad(\mathcal{E})\otimes\pi_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}})
.
Therefore the determinant of the cohomology
\det(H^{*}(\tilde{C},\ad(\pi^{*}\mathcal{E}))=\det H^{*}(C,\ad(\mathcal{E})\otimes\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{C}})
defines a power of
\mathcal{L}_{\det}
on
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
. This implies that the weight of the reduction of
\pi^{*}\mathcal{E}
is a just
\deg(\pi)
-times the weight of the induced reduction of
\mathcal{E}
. So again a very destabilizing canonical reduction of
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}
induces a destabilizing reduction of
\mathcal{E}
.

We are left to show that the stable and semistable loci are constructible. We saw that unstable bundles admit a canonical reduction to some
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{G}
, i.e., they lie in the image of the natural morphism
\Bun_{\mathcal{P}}\to\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
.
From Remark
3.D
we know that it suffices to consider the image of this morphism for finitely many
\mathcal{P}
.

To conclude, we need to see that for every substack of finite type
U\subset\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
only finitely many connected components of
\Bun_{\mathcal{P}}
contain canonical reductions that map to
U
. We just proved that on
U
the slope
\mu_{\max}
is bounded above.

We claim that for the canonical reduction of a
\mathcal{G}
-bundle with
\mu_{\max}\leq c
the degree of the corresponding
\mathcal{P}
-bundle
\underline{\deg}\in(X^{*}(\mathcal{P}))^{\vee}
lies in a finite set. By construction the canonical reduction was obtained from a complementary polyhedron and therefore the
\mathcal{L}
-degree of the reduction that was defined from the weight of the reduction that is bounded below when evaluated at fundamental weights. As we bounded the
\mu_{max}
this will also be bounded above.
Now we saw that the from equation
3.1
that the weight of the reduction can be computed from the degree of the reduction and a local term only depending on the group
\mathcal{P}
. Therefore we find obtain our bound for the degree of the
\mathcal{P}
-bundle.

To conclude we use that the number of connected components of
\Bun_{\mathcal{P}}
of a fixed degree is finite. This is known for
\mathcal{G}
-bundles (see Proposition
5
). From this we can deduce our statement by looking at the Levi quotient
\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{L}
and the morphism
\Bun_{\mathcal{P}}\to\Bun_{\mathcal{L}}
, which is smooth with connected fibers, because the kernel
\mathcal{U}\to\mathcal{P}
has a filtration by additive groups.

For the stack of stable points we can argue in the same way, oserving that strictly semistable bundles admit a reduction to a parabolic with maximal slope equal to
0
.

\Box

Appendix: Basic results on
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}

As in Section
3
we fix a smooth projective
geometrically connected curve
C
over an algebraically closed field

k
and
\mathcal{G}\to C
a parahoric Bruhat-Tits group scheme. In this
appendix we collect some results on the stack of
\mathcal{G}
-bundles

\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
for which we could not find a reference.

The basic tool will be the Beilinson–Drinfeld Grassmannian

\GR_{\mathcal{G}}
i.e., the ind-projective scheme representing the functor
of
\mathcal{G}
-bundles together with a trivialization outside of a finite
divisor on
C
:

\GR_{\mathcal{G}}(T)=\left\{(\mathcal{E},D,\phi)\left|{\mathcal{E}\in\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}(T),D\in C^{(d)}(T)\text{ for some d}\atop\phi\colon\mathcal{E}|_{C\times T-D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{G}\times_{C}(C\times T-D)}\right.\right\}.

It comes equipped with a natural forgetful maps

p\colon\GR_{\mathcal{G}}\to\Bun_{\mathcal{G}},

\supp\colon\GR_{\mathcal{G}}\to\coprod_{d}C^{(d)}.

For Bruhat-Tits groups it will be useful to consider the open
subfunctor, where the divisor
D
does not intesect some fixed
finite subset of
C
, e.g., the ramification points of the group

\mathcal{G}
. Given
S\subset C
finite, we will denote by

\GR_{\mathcal{G},C-S}:=\supp^{-1}\big(\coprod(C-S)^{(d)}\big)
, i.e. the
ind-scheme parametrizing
\mathcal{G}
bundles together with a
trivialization outside of a divisor
D
that is disjoint form
S
.
Similarly we denote by
\Gr_{\mathcal{G},x}:=\supp^{-1}(x)
, the space
parametrizing
\mathcal{G}
-bundles equipped with a trivialization on

C-\{x\}
.

The following is a probably well-known geometric version of a weak
approximation theorem:

For any Bruhat-Tits scheme
\mathcal{G}\to C
and any finite subscheme
S\subset C
the natural forgetful map
p_{C-S}\colon\GR_{\mathcal{G},C-S}\to\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
is surjective, i.e., after possibly passing to a flat extension, any
\mathcal{G}
-bundle admits a trivialization on an open neighborhood of
S
.

For
S=\emptyset
this follows from a theorem of Steinberg and Borel-Springer
[
12
]
, stating that for any algebraically closed field
\overline{K}
any
\mathcal{G}
-bundle on
C_{\overline{K}}
is trivial over the generic point
\overline{K}(C)
. As any such trivialization is defined over an open subset this allows to deduce that
p\colon\GR_{\mathcal{G}}\to\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
is surjective in the fppf-topology.

To deduce the result for
p_{C-S}
we can argue as in
[
27
, Theorem 5]
:
As
\GR_{\mathcal{G},C-S}\subset\GR_{\mathcal{G}}
is a dense open subfunctor, it follows that the generic point of any connected component of
\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
is in the image of
p_{C-S}
.
Let
\mathcal{E}\in\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
be any bundle. For the inner form
\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}:=\Aut_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{E})
of
\mathcal{G}
we can apply the same reasoning and find that the image of the map
\GR_{\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}},C-S}\to\Bun_{\mathcal{G}^{\mathcal{E}}}\cong\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}
also contains an open subset of every connected component. In particular there exist
\mathcal{G}
-bundles
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
in the image that are also in the image of
p_{C-S}
. For such any such bundle
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
there exist divisors
D_{1},D_{2}
on
C-S
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}|_{C-D_{1}}\cong\mathcal{G}|_{C-D_{1}}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}|_{C-D_{2}}\cong\mathcal{E}|_{C-D_{2}}
. Composing these isomorphisms we find that
\mathcal{E}|_{C-(D_{1}\cup D_{2})}\cong\mathcal{G}|_{C-(D_{1}\cup D_{2})}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{E}
is also in the image of
p_{C-S}
. Thus
p_{C-S}
is surjective on geometric points. As it is the restriction of a flat morphism to an open subfunctor this implies thst it is again fppf surjective.

\Box

\Bun_{\mathcal{G}}

C

\overline{\eta}
will
be a geometric generic point. From

We denote by

(1)

(2)

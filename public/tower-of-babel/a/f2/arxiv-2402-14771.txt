Introduction

The classical Lehmer conjecture for number fields says that there is
an absolute constant
>0
such that for all number

of degree

and all

The best known result
[
2

results for the analogous conjecture for the canonical height on an
elliptic curve

and non-torsion point

are as follows:

\displaystyle{\hat{h}}_{E}(P)

\displaystyle\geq(E)\cdot D^{-3}(\log D)^{-2}

in general

8
]
,

\displaystyle{\hat{h}}_{E}(P)

\displaystyle\geq(E)\cdot D^{-2}(\log D)^{-2}

if

is non-integral
[
3
]
,

\displaystyle{\hat{h}}_{E}(P)

\displaystyle\geq(E)\cdot D^{-1}(\log\log D/\log D)^{3}

if

has CM
[
5
]
.

With notation as in (

Theorem 2

Remark 4
.

We remark that the classical Lehmer conjecture for
{\mathbb{K}}^{*}
is trivial
in the function field setting. Philosophically, we view the difficulty
in Lehmer’s conjecture as coming from places of persistant bad reduction. The
group scheme
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
has everywhere good reduction over a function
field, whence the triviality of a Lehmer estimate. However, we also
philosophically view
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, and indeed all abelian group schemes, as
having persistant bad reduction at all archimedean places, which explains why
Lehmer estimates are non-trivial for
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
over number fields. As we
shall see, the challenge in proving a Lehmer-type estimate for an
elliptic curve over a function field comes almost entirely from the
places of multiplicative reduction, i.e., from the places of stable
bad reduction.

3.
A Parallelogram Law Canonical Height Inequality

We use the parallelogram law for the canonical height
[
12
, Theorem VIII.9.3]
to
derive an inequality that will be applied later.

Lemma 9
.

Let
P_{1},\ldots,P_{N}\in{E({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})}
. Then

\max_{0\leq i\leq N}{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{i})\geq\frac{1}{2(N+1)^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=0\\
j\neq k\\
\end{subarray}}^{N}{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{j}-P_{k}).

(6)

Further, the inequality (
6
) is an equality if
and only if

{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{0})=\cdots={\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{N})\quad\text{and}\quad P_{0}+\cdots+P_{N}\in{E({\overline{\mathbb{F}}})_{\textup{tors}}}.

Proof.

We denote the bilinear form associated to the canonical height by

\langle P,Q\rangle_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}:=\frac{1}{2}\Bigl({\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P+Q)-{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P)-{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(Q)\Bigr).

We compute

\displaystyle\smash[b]{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,k=0\\
j\neq k\\
\end{subarray}}^{N}{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{j}-P_{k})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=0}^{N}{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{j}-P_{k})\quad\text{since ${\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(O)=0$,}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=0}^{N}\Bigl({\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{j})-2\bigl\langle P_{j},P_{k}\bigr\rangle_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}+{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{k})\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j,k=0}^{N}\Bigl({\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{j})+{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{k})\Bigr)-2\left\langle\sum_{j=0}^{N}P_{j},\sum_{k=0}^{N}P_{k}\right>_{\!\!E/{\mathbb{F}}}

\displaystyle=2(N+1)\sum_{i=0}^{N}{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{i})-2{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}\biggl(\sum_{i=0}^{N}P_{i}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq 2(N+1)\sum_{i=0}^{N}{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{i})\quad\text{since ${\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}\geq 0$,}

(7)

\displaystyle\leq 2(N+1)^{2}\max_{0\leq i\leq N}{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{i}).

(8)

This proves (
6
). We conclude by noting that
the inequality (
7
) is an equality if and
only if
{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(\sum{P_{i}})=0
, so if and only
if
\sum{P_{i}}\in{E_{\textup{tors}}}
, and the
inequality (
8
) is an equality if and only
if the
{\hat{h}}_{E/{\mathbb{F}}}(P_{i})
are all equal.
∎

The Coherent State Transform and an Application to the Hyperbolic Laplacian
Timon Ruben Weinmann

Abstract
. We review some basic ideas from the theory of overfilling families and use the special case of coherent states to prove Weyl asymptotics for the euclidean Laplacian and the hyperbolic Laplacian on a domain with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Keywords. hyperbolic laplacian, coherent states, Weyl law AMS Classification. 58C40, 35P05



1.
Introduction

Suppose that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
be an open domain of finite measure. Denote by
\lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbf{N}
the eigenvalues of the Laplacian on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
One version of the Weyl law for the Dirichlet Laplacian reads

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}=\lvert\Omega\rvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

There are countless proofs of this fact, see
[
FLW23
]
for instance.
In this paper, we seek to establish a similar asymptotic formula for the hyperbolic Laplacian.
If we consider the operator

H=-\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}-e^{2x_{1}}{\Delta_{\tilde{x}}}

where
\Delta_{\tilde{x}}=\sum_{j=2}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}
, on a bounded domain in
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
and denote by
\lambda_{k}
its Dirichlet eigenvalues, we find

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

The operator
H+\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{4}
on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is unitarily equivalent to the hyperbolic Laplacian on
L^{2}(\mathbf{H}^{d})
. This asymptotic formula thus easily translates to an assertion about the eigenvalues of the hyperbolic Laplacian. In the following we even call
H
the hyperbolic Laplacian for simplicity.
We prove this asymptotic formula using the theory of coherent states. In section 2 we recall some definitions and theorems from the theory of overfilling families of functions as presented in
[
Ber73
]
. This discussion culminates in a proof of the Weyl asymptotic formula for the euclidean Laplacian. This is in fact a special case of the treatment of Weyl asymptotics for fractional Laplacians using coherent states considered in
[
Gei14
]
. Section 3 constitutes the principal part of this paper and is dedicated to proving the Weyl asymptotic formula for the hyperbolic Laplacian
H
using coherent states. Finally, in section 4, we discuss the asymptotic behavior of the error term in the Weyl law for the hyperbolic Laplacian and obtain the more precise formula

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}=\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}(1-{\xi_{1}}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}+O(\lambda^{d/2+2/3}).

Note that this formula also follows from a more general discussion (
[
bruening
]
) of the asymptotic behavior of the spectral functions of elliptic differential operators with smooth coefficients. Our approach, however, exemplifies the utility of the coherent state approach, a theory that has classically been applied to powers of euclidean Laplacians and Schrödinger operators.

2.
Coherent States

2.1.
Overfilling Families

We introduce overfilling families and prove how they can be used to compute traces of operators following
[
Ber73
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space and let
(A,\mu)
be a measure space. A family
e_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{H}
,
\alpha\in A
of elements of
\mathcal{H}
indexed by
A
is said to be overfilling if the map
A\ni\alpha\mapsto e_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{H}
is measurable and if for all
f\in\mathcal{H}
one has

\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}=\int_{A}\lvert\langle e_{\alpha},f\rangle\rvert^{2}d\mu(\alpha).

Given an overfilling family, we obtain an isometric embedding
\mathcal{H}\to L^{2}(A)
by setting

f(\alpha)=\langle e_{\alpha},f\rangle

for
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Note in particular that
e_{\alpha}(\beta)=\langle e_{\beta},e_{\alpha}\rangle=\overline{e_{\beta}(\alpha)}
. We can therefore use the same notation for the inner products in
\mathcal{H}
and in
L^{2}(A)
. Note that

\langle g,f\rangle=\int_{A}\langle g,e_{\alpha}\rangle\langle e_{\alpha},f\rangle d\mu(\alpha)

for any
g,f\in\mathcal{H}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
T
be a non-negative self-adjoint trace-class operator on
\mathcal{H}
. Then

\tr T=\int_{A}\langle e_{\alpha},Te_{\alpha}\rangle d\mu(\alpha)

Proof.

Indeed let
\psi_{k}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
consisting of eigenfunctions of
T
,

\displaystyle\int_{A}\langle e_{\alpha},Te_{\alpha}\rangle d\mu(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}\sum_{k}\langle e_{\alpha},\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k},Te_{\alpha}\rangle d\mu(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}{\int_{A}\langle e_{\alpha},\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k},Te_{\alpha}\rangle d\mu(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}{\int_{A}\langle T^{*}\psi_{k},e_{\alpha}\rangle\langle e_{\alpha},\psi_{k}\rangle d\mu(\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\langle\psi_{k},T\psi_{k}\rangle.

Note that interchanging the sum and the integral is justified since

\displaystyle\sum_{k}{\int_{A}\lvert\langle e_{\alpha},\psi_{k}\rangle\langle\psi_{k},Te_{\alpha}\rangle\rvert d\mu(\alpha)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\left(\int_{A}\lvert\langle e_{\alpha},\psi_{k}\rangle\rvert^{2}d\mu(\alpha)\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{A}\lvert\langle T\psi_{k},e_{\alpha}\rangle\rvert^{2}d\mu(\alpha)\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\left\lVert\psi_{k}\right\rVert\left\lVert T\psi_{k}\right\rVert=\sum_{k}\left\lVert T\psi_{k}\right\rVert<\infty

by virtue of
T
being trace-class and the
\psi_{k}
being eigenfunctions.
∎

2.2.
Examples

Let us discuss two simple examples of overfilling families.

Example 2.3
.

Consider the case when
A=\mathbf{N}
and
(e_{n})_{n\in\mathbf{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. In this case the above procedure yields the well-known isometric isomorphism
\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{2}(\mathbf{N})
and
f(n)
is simply the
n
-th coefficient
c_{n}
in the Fourier expansions
f=\sum_{n}c_{n}e_{n}
. Given a trace-class operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}
we obtain
\tr T=\sum_{k}T_{k,k}
, where
T_{k,l}=\langle e_{k},Te_{l}\rangle
, as expected.

Example 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
be open and bounded. Let
e_{\xi}(x)=e^{i\xi x}
. By extending
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
by zero outside
\Omega
, we may compute its Fourier transform. Plancherel’s theorem yields

\int\lvert f(x)\rvert^{2}dx=\int\lvert\hat{f}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi=\int\lvert\langle e_{\xi},f\rangle\rvert^{2}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

One thus obtains an overfilling family with
A=\mathbf{R}^{d}
and
d\mu(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-d}d\xi
.

2.3.
Definition of Coherent States

The rest of this paper will deal with a class of overfilling families in
L^{2}
-spaces constructed as follows.
Let
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
be real-valued and such that
g(-x)=g(x)
and
\int g(x)^{2}dx=1
.
For
\xi,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
define
e_{\xi,y}(x)=e^{i\xi x}g(x-y)
. Clearly
\left\lVert e_{\xi,y}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=1
. The space
\mathbf{R}^{2d}
indexing the family
(e_{\xi,y})
shall be equipped with the measure
(2\pi)^{-d}d\xi dy
. The functions
e_{\xi,y}\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
are called coherent states.

Proposition 2.5
.

The family
e_{\xi,y}
,
(\xi,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{2d}
is overfilling in
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

We need to show that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\lvert\langle e_{\xi,y},f\rangle\rvert^{2}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{2}dx.

Note that
\langle e_{\xi,y},f\rangle=\int e^{-i\xi x}g(x-y)f(x)dx
is the Fourier transform of
x\mapsto(2\pi)^{d/2}g(x-y)f(x)
. Therefore, Plancherel’s theorem yields

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\langle e_{\xi,y},f\rangle\rvert^{2}d\xi=(2\pi)^{d}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}g(x-y)^{2}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{2}dx

Integration with respect to
y
concludes the proof.
∎

In the following section we will need to make use of the following technical

Lemma 2.6
.

\langle e_{\xi,y},-\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}e_{\xi,y}\rangle=\xi_{j}^{2}+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\partial_{z_{j}}g(z)\rvert^{2}dz

Proof.

Direct computation yields

\partial_{x_{j}}e_{\xi,y}(x)=i\xi_{j}e^{i\xi x}g(x-y)+e^{i\xi x}\partial_{x_{j}}g(x-y)

and

(1)

\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}e_{\xi,y}(x)=-\xi_{j}^{2}e^{i\xi x}g(x-y)+2i\xi_{j}e^{i\xi x}\partial_{x_{j}}g(x-y)+e^{i\xi x}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}g(x-y).

Therefore

\langle e_{\xi,y},-\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}e_{\xi,y}\rangle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\xi_{j}^{2}(g(x-y))^{2}dx+2i\xi_{j}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\partial_{x_{j}}g(x-y)g(x-y)dx-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}g(x-y)g(x-y)dx.

By symmetry of
g
, the second integral on the right vanishes and by integration by parts, the third integral is equal to

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(\partial_{z_{j}}g(z))^{2}dz
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

The Laplacian acts on the functions
e_{\xi,y}
in the following way:

\langle e_{\xi,y},-\Delta e_{\xi,y}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}+\int_{\mathbf{R}}\lvert\nabla g(z)\rvert^{2}dz.

Proof.

Follows immediately from Lemma
2.6
.
∎

Instead of the entire space
\mathbf{R}^{d}
, consider now an open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
of finite measure.
For
\varepsilon>0
let
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\{x\in\Omega\colon\dist(x,\partial\Omega)>\varepsilon\}
. We impose on
g
the additional condition that
\supp g\subseteq\{z\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\colon\lvert z\rvert<1\}
. Note that
g^{\varepsilon}(z)=\varepsilon^{-d/2}g(z/\varepsilon)
still satisfies the symmetry and normalization conditions demanded of
g
. We may therefore define

e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}(x)=e^{i\xi x}g^{\varepsilon}(x-y)
.

Any function
f\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
can be extended by zero to a function in
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Thus

\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},f\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

The map
y\mapsto\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},u\rangle
vanished outside
\Omega_{\varepsilon}+\{z\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\colon\lvert z\rvert<\varepsilon\}\subseteq\Omega
, since
\supp g^{\varepsilon}\subseteq\{z\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\colon\lvert z\rvert<\varepsilon\}
. Therefore

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},f\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}\rvert^{2}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\lvert\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},f\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\rvert^{2}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

Finally,

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\lvert\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},f\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\rvert^{2}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}.

This proves the following

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
be an open set of finite measure. Assume that
g
is supported in the open ball
\{z\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\colon\lvert z\rvert<1\}
. Then
the family
(e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon})
,
y\in\Omega
,
\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
of coherent states constructed using
g^{\varepsilon}
is overfilling in
L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
.

2.4.
Weyl’s Law for the Dirichlet Laplacian

To illustrate the utility of coherent states, we will prove the celebrated Weyl Law for the Dirichlet Laplacian on a domain in euclidean space.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbf{N}
be the eigenvalues of the Dirichlet Laplacian in
\Omega
counted with multiplicity. Then

\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\lvert\Omega\rvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Proof.

Denote by
\lambda_{k}
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{k}
the
k
-th Dirichlet eigenvalue of the Laplacian with Dirichlet conditions in
\Omega
, respectively
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. By domain monotonicity we have
\lambda_{k}\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{k}
and consequently
(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq(\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}_{k})_{+}
. By Theorem
2.2
and Proposition
2.8
therefore

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\sum_{k}(\lambda-\tilde{\lambda}_{k})_{+}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},(\lambda+\Delta)_{+}e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Clearly

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},(\lambda+\Delta)_{+}e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}\geq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega_{2\varepsilon}}\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},(\lambda+\Delta)_{+}e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

For
y\in\Omega_{2\varepsilon}
the function
e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}
is supported in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
and consequently

\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},(\lambda+\Delta)_{+}e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})}=\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},(\lambda+\Delta)_{+}e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}.

Since
\mu\mapsto\mu_{+}
is convex, applying Jensen’s inequality to the spectral measure of
-\Delta
, we obtain

\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},(\lambda+\Delta)_{+}e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}\geq\left(\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},(\lambda+\Delta)e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}\right)_{+}=\left(\lambda-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz\right)_{+}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega_{2\varepsilon}}\left(\lambda-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega_{2\varepsilon}}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

(2)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Finally we obtain

\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}\geq\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}

by dominated convergence. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
concludes the proof, since
1_{\Omega_{2\varepsilon}}(x)\to 1_{\Omega}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
and thus
\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\to\lvert\Omega\rvert
by dominated convergence.
∎

We now seek to prove an upper bound on
\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}
. This result is due to Li and Yau
[
LY83
]
and our proof follows
[
FLW23
]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}\leq\lvert\Omega\rvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\lambda-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}

Proof.

Let
(\psi_{k})_{k}\subseteq L^{2}(\Omega)
be an orthonormal system of eigenfunctions corresponding to
(\lambda_{k})_{k}
. In particular,
\psi_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, since every
\psi_{k}
can be extended by zero to a function
H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, we have

\lambda_{k}=\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla\psi_{k}(x)\rvert^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\lvert\hat{\psi}_{k}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi.

Therefore

(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}=\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(\lambda-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})\lvert\hat{\psi}_{k}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi\right)_{+}\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\lambda-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\lvert\hat{\psi}_{k}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi,

by Jensen’s inequality, since
\lvert\hat{\psi}_{k}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi
is a probability measure.

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\lambda_{k}<\lambda}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\lambda-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\lvert\hat{\psi}_{k}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\lambda-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\sum_{\lambda_{k}<\lambda}\lvert\hat{\psi}_{k}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\lambda-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\sum_{k}\lvert\hat{\psi}_{k}(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi.

Note that

\hat{\psi}_{k}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\xi}\psi_{k}(x)dx=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\Omega}e^{-i\xi x}\psi_{k}(x)dx=\langle(2\pi)^{-d/2}e^{i\xi\cdot},\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since the
\psi_{k}
form an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we obtain

\sum_{k}\lvert\hat{\psi}_{k}(\xi)\rvert^{2}=\sum_{k}\lvert\langle(2\pi)^{-d/2}e^{i\xi\cdot},\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\rvert^{2}=\left\lVert(2\pi)^{-d/2}e^{i\xi\cdot}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=(2\pi)^{-d}\lvert\Omega\rvert.

This concludes the argument.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

For the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
of the Dirichlet Laplacian on a domain
\Omega
of finite measure, the following asymptotic formula holds

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}=\lvert\Omega\rvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

3.
The Hyperbolic Laplacian

3.1.
Preliminaries

On a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the Laplace–Beltrami operator is locally given by

-\Delta_{g}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{g}}\partial_{x^{i}}\left(\sqrt{g}g^{ij}\partial_{x^{j}}\right),

whereas the canonical Riemannian volume is given by

dV_{g}=\sqrt{g}dx^{1}dx^{2}...\ dx^{d}.

Consider now the hyperbolic space in the half-plane model

M=\mathbf{H}^{d}=\{(y,x)\in\mathbf{R}\times\mathbf{R}^{d-1}\colon\ y>0\},\ g=\frac{1}{y^{2}}(dy^{2}+dx^{2}).

The Laplace–Beltrami operator in this case is

-\Delta_{g}=-y^{d}\partial_{y}(y^{2-d}\partial_{y})-y^{2}\Delta_{x},

where
\Delta_{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}
.
Performing the change of coordinates
y=e^{t}
, one obtains a unitary operator
L^{2}(\mathbf{H}^{d},y^{-d}dx\ dy)\to L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d},dx\ dt)
and
-\Delta_{g}
acts on
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d},dx\ dt)
as
-\partial_{t}^{2}-e^{2t}\Delta_{x}+(d-1)^{2}/4
. This unitary equivalence between
-\Delta_{g}
and
-\partial_{t}^{2}-e^{2t}\Delta_{x}+(d-1)^{2}/4
causes their eigenvalues to coincide.
In
[
ILW24
]
this correspondence was used to extend a class of Lieb–Thirring inequalities from
\mathbf{R}^{d}
to
\mathbf{H}^{d}
. In the rest of this section we adopt the notation
\tilde{x}=(x_{2},...,x_{d})
for
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

3.2.
Lower Trace Bound

Consider the operator

H=-\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}-e^{2x_{1}}{\Delta_{\tilde{x}}}

where
\Delta_{\tilde{x}}=\sum_{j=2}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}
on the space
L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. We are interested in its action on a coherent state of the form
e_{\xi,y}(x)=e^{i\xi x}g(x-y)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

\langle e_{\xi,y},He_{\xi,y}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=\xi_{1}^{2}+e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}g(z)^{2}dz+e^{2y_{1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}\lvert{\nabla_{\tilde{z}}}g(z)\rvert^{2}dz+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(\partial_{z_{1}}g(z))^{2}dz

Proof.

The contribution of
-\partial^{2}_{x_{1}}
was already determined in
2.6
.
We compute
\langle e_{\xi,y},-e^{2x_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}e_{\xi,y}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}
for
j>1
. Equation
1
in the proof of Lemma
2.6
implies

\displaystyle\langle e_{\xi,y},-e^{2x_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}e_{\xi,y}\rangle=

\displaystyle\xi_{j}^{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2x_{1}}(g(x-y))^{2}dx+2i\xi_{j}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2x_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}g(x-y)g(x-y)dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2x_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}g(x-y)g(x-y)dx.

The first term on the right equals

e^{2y_{1}}\xi_{j}^{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2(x_{1}-y_{1})}(g(x-y))^{2}dx=e^{2y_{1}}\xi_{j}^{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}(g(z))^{2}dz.

For the second integral we find

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2x_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}g(x-y)g(x-y)dx=\int_{\mathbf{R}}e^{2x_{1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d-1}}\partial_{x_{j}}g(x-y)g(x-y)d\tilde{x}dx_{1},

where the inner integral vanishes due to symmetry of
g
. Finally, for the third integral we find, again by splitting the integral, that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2x_{1}}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}g(x-y)g(x-y)dx

\displaystyle=-e^{2y_{1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}}e^{2(x_{1}-y_{1})}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d-1}}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}g(x-y)g(x-y)d\tilde{x}dx_{1}

\displaystyle=e^{2y_{1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}}e^{2(x_{1}-y_{1})}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d-1}}(\partial_{x_{j}}g(x-y))^{2}d\tilde{x}dx_{1}

\displaystyle=e^{2y_{1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}(\partial_{z_{j}}g(z))^{2}dz

Summing over
j=2,...,d
completes the argument.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set and let
\lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbf{N}
be the eigenvalues of the operator
H
on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Then

\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Proof.

Let us again consider a
g
supported in the unit ball and construct
e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}
accordingly.
By the same argument as for the Dirichlet Laplacian in the proof of Proposition
2.9
, we have

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega_{2\varepsilon}}\left(\langle e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon},(\lambda-H)e_{\xi,y}^{\varepsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Using the expression obtained for the symbol of
H
in
3.1
then gives

(3)

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times{\Omega_{2\varepsilon}}}\left(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}c^{\varepsilon}_{3}-e^{2y_{1}}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}-c^{\varepsilon}_{1}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}},

where

\displaystyle c^{\varepsilon}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(\partial_{z_{1}}g^{\varepsilon}(z))^{2}dz

\displaystyle c^{\varepsilon}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}\lvert{\nabla_{\tilde{z}}}g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz

\displaystyle c^{\varepsilon}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}g^{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}dz.

Note that since
g(z)^{2}
is a mollifier, we have
c_{3}^{\varepsilon}\to 1
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
After a change of coordinates, we obtain

(4)

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega_{2\varepsilon}}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}c^{\varepsilon}_{3}-\frac{1}{\lambda}e^{2y_{1}}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}c^{\varepsilon}_{1}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Dominated convergence yields

\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega_{2\varepsilon}}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}c^{\varepsilon}_{3}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
and using dominated convergence concludes the argument.
∎

3.3.
Upper Trace Bound

Before proving an upper bound on the trace of the hyperbolic Laplacian, we need to consider some further technicalities concerning coherent states.
Consider again the general situation where
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
is real-valued and such that
g(-x)=g(x)
and
\int g(x)^{2}dx=1
.
As before, define
e_{\xi,y}(x)=e^{i\xi x}g(x-y)
.
The operator
\Phi\colon L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d},dx)\to L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d},(2\pi)^{-d}dyd\xi)
given by
(\Phi\psi)(y,\xi)=\langle e_{\xi,y},\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}
is called the coherent state transform. Since
(e_{\xi,y})
is overfilling,
\Phi
is an isometry, i.e.
\Phi^{*}\Phi=I_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}
.

In this section’s theorem we shall make use of the two following lemmas:

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Then

\langle\psi,\Phi^{*}\xi_{1}^{2}\Phi\psi\rangle=\left\lVert\partial_{x_{1}}\psi\right\rVert^{2}+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(\partial_{z_{1}}g(z))^{2}dz\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

Let us introduce some notation. We write
g_{y}(x)=g(x-y)
. The Fourier transform is denoted by
\mathcal{F}\colon L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
with the following convention for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\cap L^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
:

\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)dx.

Finally, we write
X=L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d},dx)
and
Y=L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{2d},(2\pi)^{-d}dyd\xi)
for brevity.

The coherent state transform of
\psi
is given by

\Phi\psi(y,\xi)=\langle e_{\xi,y},\psi\rangle_{X}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\xi}g_{y}(x)f(x)dx=(2\pi)^{d/2}\mathcal{F}(g_{y}f).

Consequently,

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\Phi^{*}\xi_{1}^{2}\Phi\psi\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{1}\Phi\psi,\xi_{1}\Phi\psi\rangle_{Y}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d}\left\lVert\xi_{1}\mathcal{F}(g_{y}\psi)\right\rVert^{2}_{Y}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d}\left\lVert\mathcal{F}(\partial_{x_{1}}(g_{y}\psi))\right\rVert^{2}_{Y}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\lvert\mathcal{F}(\partial_{x_{1}}(g_{y}\psi))(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\partial_{x_{1}}(g_{y}\psi)(x)\rvert^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\partial_{x_{1}}(g_{y}\psi)(x)\rvert^{2}dydx

For the integrand we find

\lvert\partial_{x_{1}}(g(x-y){\psi(x)})\rvert^{2}=\lvert\partial_{x_{1}}g(x-y){\psi(x)}\rvert^{2}+\lvert g(x-y)\partial_{x_{1}}{\psi(x)}\rvert^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}\overline{{\partial_{x_{1}}g(x-y){\psi(x)}}}g(x-y)\partial_{x_{1}}{\psi(x)}.

Since
g(-z)=g(z)
, it is
(\partial_{z_{1}}g)(-z)=-(\partial_{z_{1}}g)(z)
and thus

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}{{\partial_{x_{1}}g(x-y)}}g(x-y)dy=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}{{\partial_{z_{1}}g(z)}}g(z)dz=0.

Finally,

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\Phi^{*}\xi_{1}^{2}\Phi\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}\left(\lvert\partial_{x_{1}}g(x-y){\psi(x)}\rvert^{2}+\lvert g(x-y)\partial_{x_{1}}{\psi(x)}\rvert^{2}\right){dydx}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\partial_{x_{1}}g(x-y)\rvert^{2}dy\right)\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert g(x-y)\rvert^{2}dy\right)\lvert\partial_{x_{1}}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\partial_{z_{1}}g(z)\rvert^{2}dz\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert g(z)\rvert^{2}dz\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\partial_{x_{1}}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(\partial_{z_{1}}g(z))^{2}dz\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\partial_{x_{1}}{\psi}\right\rVert^{2}.\qed

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that the function in the definition of coherent states can be decomposed as
g(z)=g_{1}(z_{1})\tilde{g}(\tilde{z})
where

g_{1}
and
\tilde{g}
are symmetric in the sense that
g_{1}(-x_{1})=g_{1}(x_{1})
and
\tilde{g}(-\tilde{x})=\tilde{g}(\tilde{x})
, compactly supported, and such that

\int_{\mathbf{R}}g_{1}(z_{1})^{2}dz_{1}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d-1}}\tilde{g}(\tilde{z})^{2}d\tilde{z}=1
.
Let
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
have compact support. Then

\langle\psi,\Phi^{*}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\Phi\psi\rangle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}\lvert{\nabla_{\tilde{z}}}g(z)\rvert^{2}dz\left\lVert e^{x_{1}}\psi\right\rVert^{2}+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}g(z)^{2}dz\left\lVert e^{x_{1}}\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\psi\right\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

We proceed in a similar way as in the proof of the lemma above. Adopting the same notation as before, we find for
j=2,...,d
that

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\Phi^{*}e^{2y_{1}}\xi_{j}^{2}\Phi\psi\rangle_{X}

\displaystyle=\langle e^{y_{1}}\xi_{1}\Phi\psi,e^{y_{1}}\xi_{j}\Phi\psi\rangle_{Y}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d}\left\lVert e^{y_{1}}\xi_{j}\mathcal{F}(g_{y}\psi)\right\rVert^{2}_{Y}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{d}\left\lVert e^{y_{1}}\mathcal{F}(\partial_{x_{j}}(g_{y}\psi))\right\rVert^{2}_{Y}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\mathcal{F}(\partial_{x_{j}}(g_{y}\psi))(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2y_{1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\mathcal{F}(\partial_{x_{j}}(g_{y}\psi))(\xi)\rvert^{2}d\xi\ dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2y_{1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert(\partial_{x_{j}}(g_{y}\psi))(\xi)\rvert^{2}dx\ dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\partial_{x_{j}}(g_{y}\psi)(x)\rvert^{2}dydx.

Summing over
j
we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\Phi^{*}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\Phi\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{x}}(g(x-y){\psi(x)})\rvert^{2}{dydx}.

Note that

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{x}}(g(x-y){\psi(x)})\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{x}}g(x-y)\rvert^{2}\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^{2}+\lvert g(x-y)\rvert^{2}\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=2}^{d}2\operatorname{Re}\overline{{\partial_{x_{j}}g(x-y){\psi(x)}}}g(x-y)\partial_{x_{j}}{\psi(x)},

and that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2y_{1}}{{\partial_{x_{j}}g(x-y)}}g(x-y)dy=\int_{\mathbf{R}}e^{2y_{1}}g_{1}(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}dy_{1}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d-1}}{{\partial_{x_{j}}\tilde{g}(\tilde{x}-\tilde{y})}}\tilde{g}(\tilde{x}-\tilde{y})d\tilde{y},

where the second integral on the right vanishes due to the symmetry of
\tilde{g}
. Finally

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{x}}g(x-y)\rvert^{2}\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}e^{2(y_{1}-x)}\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{x}}g(x-y)\rvert^{2}e^{2x_{1}}\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z}\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{z}}g(z)\rvert^{2}dz\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2x_{1}}\lvert\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dx,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert g(x-y)\rvert^{2}\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}e^{2(y_{1}-x_{1})}\lvert g(x-y)\rvert^{2}e^{2x_{1}}\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}\lvert g(z)\rvert^{2}dz\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2x_{1}}\lvert\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\psi(x)\rvert^{2}dx.

This concludes the proof.
∎

We are now ready to state the main theorem of this subsection.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set and let
\lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbf{N}
be the eigenvalues of the operator
H
on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Then

\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

Proof.

Assume that
g
is supported in the unit ball
B_{1}(0)
and that it can be decomposed as
g(z)=g_{1}(z_{1})\tilde{g}(\tilde{z})
, where

g_{1},\tilde{g}\geq 0
are symmetric in the sense that
g_{1}(-x_{1})=g_{1}(x_{1})
and
\tilde{g}(-\tilde{x})=\tilde{g}(\tilde{x})
, compactly supported and satisfy

\int_{\mathbf{R}}g_{1}(z_{1})^{2}dz_{1}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d-1}}\tilde{g}(\tilde{z})^{2}d\tilde{z}=1
. Define
g^{\varepsilon}(z)=\varepsilon^{-d/2}g(z/\varepsilon)
. Denote by
\Omega^{\varepsilon}
the set
\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\colon\dist(x,\Omega)<\varepsilon\}
.
As before, let
\lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbf{N}
be the eigenvalues of
H
. Since
H
is clearly elliptic, we can find according eigenfunctions
\psi_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
(\psi_{k})_{k}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
(see
[
evans
]
). Extending these eigenfunctions by zero outside
\Omega
, we obtain functions in
H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
that are compactly supported. We can therefore apply the preceding two lemmas to the functions
\psi_{k}
.
For the eigenvalues we find

\lambda_{k}=\langle\psi_{k},H\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle\psi_{k},H\psi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=\left\lVert\partial_{x_{1}}\psi_{k}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert e^{x_{1}}\nabla_{\tilde{x}}\psi_{k}\right\rVert^{2}.

For brevity we denote, again,

\displaystyle c^{\varepsilon}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(\partial_{z_{1}}g^{\varepsilon}(z))^{2}dz

\displaystyle c^{\varepsilon}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}\lvert{\nabla_{\tilde{z}}}g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz

\displaystyle c^{\varepsilon}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}g^{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}dz.

Denoting
R=\sup\{y_{1}|\ y\in\Omega\}
, we find

\displaystyle(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda-\langle\psi_{k},\Phi^{*}{\xi_{1}}^{2}\Phi\psi_{k}\rangle+c^{\varepsilon}_{1}-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}\langle\psi_{k},\Phi^{*}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\Phi\psi_{k}\rangle+(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}\left\lVert e^{x_{1}}\psi_{k}\right\rVert^{2}\right)_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\lambda-\langle\psi_{k},\Phi^{*}{\xi_{1}}^{2}\Phi\psi_{k}\rangle+c^{\varepsilon}_{1}-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}\langle\psi_{k},\Phi^{*}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\Phi\psi_{k}\rangle+(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R}\right)_{+}

\displaystyle=\left(\langle\Phi\psi_{k},(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}+c^{\varepsilon}_{1}+(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R})\Phi\psi_{k}\rangle\right)_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\langle\Phi\psi_{k},(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}+c^{\varepsilon}_{1}+(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R})_{+}\Phi\psi_{k}\rangle

where the last inequality is due to Jensen’s inequality. Summing over
k
then yields

\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{2d}}(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}+c^{\varepsilon}_{1}+(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R})_{+}\sum_{k}\lvert\Phi\psi_{k}(\xi,y)\rvert^{2}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Since
(\psi_{k})
is an orthonormal basis, we obtain

\sum_{k}\lvert\Phi\psi_{k}(\xi,y)\rvert^{2}=\sum_{k}\lvert\langle e_{\xi,y},\psi_{k}\rangle\rvert^{2}=\left\lVert e_{\xi,y}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\left\lVert e_{\xi,y}\right\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})}=1

Since the map
y\mapsto\Phi\psi(y,\xi)
is supported in
\supp\psi+\supp g^{\varepsilon}\subseteq\overline{\Omega}+B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subseteq\Omega^{\varepsilon}
for
\psi\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
, we find

\sum_{k}\lvert\Phi\psi_{k}(\xi,y)\rvert^{2}\leq\chi_{\Omega^{\varepsilon}}(y).

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}}(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}+c^{\varepsilon}_{1}+(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}}(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}+c^{\varepsilon}_{1}\lambda^{-1}+(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R}\lambda^{-1})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Consequently

\displaystyle\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}}(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
finishes the proof by dominated convergence and since
c_{3}^{\varepsilon}\to 1
.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set and let
\lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbf{N}
be the eigenvalues of the operator
H
on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Then

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-1-d/2}\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

4.
Asymptotic Formula

Recall that for the euclidean Laplacian with Dirichlet boundary conditions we obtained inequality
2
reading:

\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Denoting
B=\{\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}|\ \lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\leq 1\}
, we can clearly make the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\int_{B}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\int_{B}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}-\lambda^{d/2}\frac{\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\lvert B\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\int_{B}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}-\lambda^{d/2}\frac{\lvert\Omega\rvert\lvert B\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\lvert\Omega\rvert\int_{B}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}-R(\lambda,\varepsilon)

where

R(\lambda,\varepsilon)=\lambda^{1+d/2}\left(\lvert\Omega\rvert-\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\right)\int_{B}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}+\lambda^{d/2}\frac{\lvert\Omega\rvert\lvert B\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz

Note that

\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}\lvert\nabla g(z)\rvert^{2}dz.

Let
\varepsilon=\lambda^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
. Then there are constants
L,C\geq 0
such that

R(\lambda,\varepsilon)=L\lambda^{1+d/2}\left(\lvert\Omega\rvert-\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert\right)+C\lambda^{d/2+2\alpha}.

Suppose that
\lvert\Omega\rvert-\lvert\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\rvert=O(\varepsilon)
. This is in particular the case when the inner Minkowski content of
\Omega
exists which was proven to be the case when the boundary of
\Omega
is Lipschitz-regular and
\Omega
itself is bounded in
[
ACV08
]
.
Then,

R(\lambda,\lambda^{-\alpha})=C\lambda^{d/2+2\alpha}+O(\lambda^{1+d/2-\alpha}).

Minimizing
\max\{d/2+2\alpha,\ 1+d/2-\alpha\}
, we find
\alpha=1/3
. Consequently

\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\lvert\Omega\rvert\int_{B}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}+O(\lambda^{d/2+2/3}).

Combining this with the upper estimate
2.10
we obtain the following

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain whose boundary is Lipschitz-regular. Then the eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
of the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
satisfy

\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}=\lambda^{1+d/2}\lvert\Omega\rvert\int_{B}\left(1-\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{d}}+O(\lambda^{d/2+2/3}).

This is a significantly weaker result than the one obtained in
[
FL20
]
where the following two-term asymptotic formula was proven for bounded Lipschitz domains:

\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}=L_{d}\lvert\Omega\rvert\lambda^{d/2+1}-\frac{L_{d-1}}{4}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial\Omega)\lambda^{(d-1)/2+1}+o(\lambda^{(d-1)/2+1}),

for constants
L_{d},L_{d-1}\geq 0
.

We intend to prove a similar asymptotic formula for the hyperbolic Laplacian
H
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
is open and bounded and that
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz-regular. Then the trace of the hyperbolic Laplacian on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions satisfies

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\left(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}+O(\lambda^{d/2+2/3}).

Proof.

Recall that in
3
we found

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times{\Omega_{2\varepsilon}}}\left(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}c^{\varepsilon}_{3}-e^{2y_{1}}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}-c^{\varepsilon}_{1}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Defining
B_{\varepsilon}=\{(\xi,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega_{2\varepsilon}\colon\xi_{1}^{2}+e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\leq 1\}
, one evidently has

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}\left(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}c^{\varepsilon}_{3}-e^{2y_{1}}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}-c^{\varepsilon}_{1}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}c^{\varepsilon}_{3}-\frac{1}{\lambda}e^{2y_{1}}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}c^{\varepsilon}_{1}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

For the integrand we find

\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}c^{\varepsilon}_{3}-\frac{1}{\lambda}e^{2y_{1}}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}c^{\varepsilon}_{1}\right)_{+}\geq\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}(c^{\varepsilon}_{3}-1)_{+}-\frac{1}{\lambda}e^{2y_{1}}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}-\frac{1}{\lambda}c^{\varepsilon}_{1}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{B}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}-\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{B\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle-\lambda^{1+d/2}(c_{3}^{\varepsilon}-1)_{+}\frac{\lvert B\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}-\lambda^{d/2}c_{2}^{\varepsilon}e^{2R}\frac{\lvert B\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}-\lambda^{d/2}c_{1}^{\varepsilon}\frac{\lvert B\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}},

where
B=\{(\xi,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega\colon\xi_{1}^{2}+e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\leq 1\}
and
R=\sup\{y_{1}\colon\ y\in\Omega\}
. Note that

\int_{B\setminus B_{\varepsilon}}\left(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}\leq\frac{\lvert B\setminus B_{\varepsilon}\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}},

and that

\displaystyle B\setminus B_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\{(\xi,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{2\varepsilon})\colon\xi_{1}^{2}+e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\{(\xi,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{2\varepsilon})\colon\xi_{1}^{2}+e^{2r}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\{\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\colon\xi_{1}^{2}+e^{2r}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\leq 1\}\times(\Omega\setminus\Omega_{2\varepsilon}),

where
r=\inf\{y_{1}\colon\ y\in\Omega\}
.
Clearly, the set
\{\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\colon\xi_{1}^{2}+e^{2r}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\leq 1\}
is bounded and thus of finite measure. Therefore, assuming that
\partial\Omega
is Lipschitz-regular, we can infer that

\frac{\lvert B\setminus B_{\varepsilon}\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}=O(\varepsilon),\ \varepsilon\to 0.

Conside now the term
c_{3}^{\varepsilon}-1
. We know that

c^{\varepsilon}_{3}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}g^{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}dz=\int_{\mathbf{R}}e^{2z_{1}}g_{1}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1})^{2}dz_{1}=\int_{\mathbf{R}}e^{2z_{1}\varepsilon}g_{1}(z_{1})^{2}dz_{1}.

Hence

c_{3}^{\varepsilon}-1=\int_{\mathbf{R}}(e^{2z_{1}\varepsilon}-1)g_{1}(z_{1})^{2}dz_{1}=\int_{\mathbf{R}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2z_{1})^{k}}{k!}\varepsilon^{k}g_{1}(z_{1})^{2}dz_{1}=O(\varepsilon),\ \varepsilon\to 0.

For
c_{2}^{\varepsilon}
we find

c^{\varepsilon}_{2}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}\lvert{\nabla_{\tilde{z}}}g^{\varepsilon}(z)\rvert^{2}dz=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}\varepsilon}g(z_{1})^{2}dz_{1}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}e^{2z_{1}}\lvert{\nabla_{\tilde{z}}}\tilde{g}(\tilde{z})\rvert^{2}d\tilde{z}=O(\varepsilon^{-2}),\ \varepsilon\to 0,

and finally

c^{\varepsilon}_{1}=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}(\partial_{z_{1}}g^{\varepsilon}(z))^{2}dz=\int_{\mathbf{R}}(\partial_{z_{1}}g_{1}^{\varepsilon}(z_{1}))^{2}dz_{1}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d-1}}\tilde{g}(\tilde{z})^{2}d\tilde{z}=\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{\mathbf{R}}(\partial_{z_{1}}g_{1}(z_{1}))^{2}dz_{1}=O(\varepsilon^{-2}).

Linking
\varepsilon
to
\lambda
, again, via
\varepsilon=\lambda^{-\alpha}
we obtain

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\left(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}-R(\lambda),

where
R(\lambda)
is a linear combination of terms that are in
O(\lambda^{d/2+1-\alpha})
and
O(\lambda^{d/2-2\alpha})
respectively. Again, minimizing
\max\{d/2+2\alpha,\ 1+d/2-\alpha\}
yields
\alpha=1/3
and thus

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\geq\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}\left(\lambda-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\right)_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}+O(\lambda^{d/2+2/3}).

∎

In contrast to the case of the euclidean Laplacian, we need to prove an asymptotic upper bound for the hyperbolic Laplacian as well.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let the same conditions as in the preceding lemma be satisfied. Then

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}\leq\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}(1-{\xi_{1}}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}+O(\lambda^{d/2+2/3})

Proof.

Recall that

\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}\leq\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}}(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}+\lambda^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{1}+\lambda^{-1}(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Let
\varepsilon=\lambda^{-\alpha}
. Then
((1-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1})=O(\lambda^{-\alpha})
,
\lambda^{-1}c_{1}^{\varepsilon}=O(\lambda^{-1+2\alpha})
and
\lambda^{-1}c_{2}^{\varepsilon}=O(\lambda^{-1+2\alpha})
. If
\alpha<1/2
, then all these terms converge to
0
. In this case, for sufficiently large
\lambda
, the integrand is supported in

B^{\varepsilon}=\{(\xi,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega^{\varepsilon}\colon\xi_{1}^{2}+e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\leq 2\}
.
Furthermore, the integrand can be estimated from above in the following way

\displaystyle(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-(c_{3}^{\varepsilon})^{-1}e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}+c^{\varepsilon}_{1}\lambda^{-1}+(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R}\lambda^{-1})_{+}

\displaystyle\leq(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2})_{+}+(1-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1})_{+}e^{2R}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{1}+\lambda^{-1}(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R}.

Define
B=\{(\xi,y)\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega\colon\xi_{1}^{2}+e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2}\leq 2\}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})_{+}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{B}(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle+\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{B^{\varepsilon}\setminus B}(1-\xi_{1}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}

\displaystyle+2e^{R}\lambda^{d/2+1}((1-(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1})_{+}\frac{\lvert B^{\varepsilon}\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}+\lambda^{d/2}c^{\varepsilon}_{1}\frac{\lvert B^{\varepsilon}\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}+\lambda^{d/2}(c^{\varepsilon}_{3})^{-1}c^{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2R}\frac{\lvert B^{\varepsilon}\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}.

Note that

\int_{B^{\varepsilon}\setminus B}(1-{\xi_{1}}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}\leq\frac{\lvert B^{\varepsilon}\setminus B\rvert}{(2\pi)^{d}}=O(\varepsilon)=O(\lambda^{-\alpha}).

Therefore, the error term is of the order
O(\lambda^{\max\{d/2+2\alpha,\ 1+d/2-\alpha\}})
. As for the lower bound, the exponent is minimized for
\alpha=1/3
. This concludes the argument.
∎

Combining the two preceding lemmas, we obtain

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary and let
\lambda_{k}
,
k\in\mathbf{N}
be the eigenvalues of the operator
H
on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Then

\sum_{k}(\lambda-{\lambda}_{k})_{+}=\lambda^{1+d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}\times\Omega}(1-{\xi_{1}}^{2}-e^{2y_{1}}\lvert\tilde{\xi}\rvert^{2})_{+}\frac{d\xi dy}{(2\pi)^{d}}+O(\lambda^{d/2+2/3}).

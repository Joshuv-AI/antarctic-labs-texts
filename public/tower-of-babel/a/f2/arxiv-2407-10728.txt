1.
Introduction

In the article, the set of integers (resp. natural numbers
\{1,2,\ldots\}
) is denoted by
\mathbb{Z}
(resp.
\mathbb{N}
). By a
measure-preserving system
(m.p.s. for short), we mean a quadruple
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
, where
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
is a Lebesgue probability space and
T:X\rightarrow X
is an invertible measure preserving transformation.

Let
T,S
be measure preserving transformations acting on a Lebesgue probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
. Then
for
f,g\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
, the existence in
L^{2}(X,\mu)
of the limit

(1.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(T^{n}x)g(S^{n}x)

was established in the commutative case by Conze and Lesigne
[
8
]
, and the extension to
any finite number of transformations spanning a nilpotent group and to polynomial iterates
was established by Walsh
[
16
]
. When
T,S
do not generate a nilpotent group, then (
1.1
) may not exist
[
5
]
.
Moreover, if both
T
and
S
have positive entropy, (
1.1
) may fail to exist
[
9
, Proposition 1.4]
.
Recently, Frantzikinakis and Host
[
9
]
gave the following beautiful multiple convergence theorem without commutativity.

Theorem F-H.

Let
T,S
be measure preserving transformations acting on a probability
space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
such that the system
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
has zero entropy. Let also
p\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
be a
polynomial with
\deg(p)\geq 2
. Then for every
f,g\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
, the limit

(1.2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(T^{n}x)g(S^{p(n)}x)

exists in
L^{2}(X,\mu)
.

It is unknown that whether Theorem F-H holds when one replaces the iterates
n,p(n)
by the pair
n,n
or
n^{2},n^{3}
or
arbitrary polynomials
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
with
p(0)=q(0)=0
(except the case covered by the above theorem), see
[
9
, Problem]
and the sentence below it. In
[
13
]
, we showed that for polynomials
p_{1},p_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
with
\deg p_{1},\deg p_{2}\geq 5
there exist a probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
, two ergodic measure preserving transformations
T,S
acting on
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
with
h_{\mu}(X,T)=h_{\mu}(X,S)=0
, and
f,g\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
such that the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(T^{p_{1}(n)}x)g(S^{p_{2}(n)}x)

does not exist in
L^{2}(X,\mu)
. In this paper, we show the case
p_{1}(n)=p_{2}(n)=n
.

To be precise, the main result of the paper is as follows.

Main Theorem.

there exist a probability space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
, two ergodic measure preserving transformations
T,S
acting on
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
with
h_{\mu}(X,T)=h_{\mu}(X,S)=0
, and
f,g\in L^{\infty}(X,\mu)
such that the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}f(T^{n}x)g(S^{n}x)

L^{2}(X,\mu)
.

2.
Proof of the main result

2.1.
Discrepancy skew product

For a topological space
X
, we use
\mathcal{B}(X)
denote the
\sigma
-algebra generated by all open subsets of
X
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\cong[0,1)
denote the additive circle. Consider the function
\varphi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\{-1,1\}
defined by

\varphi(\theta)=2\cdot 1_{[0,\frac{1}{2})}(\theta)-1=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&\hbox{$\theta\in[0,\frac{1}{2})$;}\\
-1,&\hbox{$\theta\in[\frac{1}{2},1)$.}\end{array}\right.

Let the skew products
T_{\alpha}:\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}
be defined for
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}
by

T_{\alpha}(\theta,v)=(\theta+\alpha,v+\varphi(\theta)).

They are measure preserving transformations of the
\sigma
-finite measure space
(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}),m_{\mathbb{T}}\times\#)
, where
m_{\mathbb{T}}
is
the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
and
\#
is counting measure on
\mathbb{Z}
.

We have that for
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
T^{n}_{\alpha}(\theta,v)=(\theta+n\alpha,v+\varphi_{n}(\theta))
where

\varphi_{n}(\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi(\theta+k\alpha).

We call the function
(n,\theta)\mapsto\varphi_{n}(\theta)
the
discrepancy
cocycle
and
T_{\alpha}
the
discrepancy skew product

[
2
]
(it is called
deterministic random walk
in
[
1
]
).

Ergodicity of the discrepancy skew product
T_{\alpha}
was established in
[
7
]
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
\Psi_{n}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
by

(2.1)

\Psi_{n}(\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}1_{\mathbb{T}\times\{0\}}\Big(T^{k}_{\alpha}(\theta,0)\Big)=\#\{0\leq k\leq n-1:\varphi_{k}(\theta)=0\}.

The ergodicity of
T_{\alpha}
tells us that

(2.2)

\Psi_{n}(\theta)\rightarrow\infty,\ a.e.\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\Psi_{n}(\theta)}{n}\rightarrow 0,\ a.e.,\ n\to\infty.

We call
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}

badly approximable
if
\inf\{q^{2}|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}|:q\in\mathbb{N},p\in\mathbb{Z}\}>0
. For
a,b>0,M>1
,
a=M^{\pm 1}b
means
\frac{1}{M}\leq\frac{a}{b}\leq M
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
2
]

If
\alpha
is badly approximable, then there is some
M>1
such that

(2.3)

\|\Psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T},m_{\mathbb{T}})}\leq M\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Psi_{n}(\theta)dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta),\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Psi_{n}(\theta)dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)=M^{\pm 1}\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log{n}}}.

In the case
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}
is quadratic, the result was established in
[
1
]
.

2.2.
The ratio ergodic theorem

The ratio ergodic theorems, which are generalizations of Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem, were given by Hopf and Stepanov for infinite measure preserving transformations
[
12
,
15
]
, Hurewicz for non-singular transformations
[
14
]
, and Chacon and Ornstein for operators
[
6
]
. Hochman studied the ratio ergodic theorem for group actions in
[
10
,
11
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Hopf-Stepanov ergodic Theorem)
.

[
12
,
15
]

Let
T
be a conservative, measure preserving transformation of a
\sigma
-finite measure space
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)
. If
f
and
g
are functions in
L^{1}
and
g
is nonnegative, then the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}f}{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}g}

exists and is finite almost everywhere on the set
\{x\in X:\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}T^{k}g(x)>0\}
.

When
T
is ergodic and
\int_{X}gd\mu\neq 0
,
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}f}{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}g}=\frac{\int_{X}fd\mu}{\int_{X}gd\mu}
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N},v\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
\Psi_{n}^{(v)}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
by

(2.4)

\Psi_{n}^{(v)}(\theta)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}1_{\mathbb{T}\times\{v\}}\Big(T^{k}_{\alpha}(\theta,0)\Big)=\#\{0\leq k\leq n-1:\varphi_{k}(\theta)=v\}.

Note that
\Psi_{n}^{(0)}=\Psi_{n}
.

In the sequel we fix a badly approximable
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}
.
Since
(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}),m_{\mathbb{T}}\times\#,T_{\alpha})
is ergodic, by Hopf-Stepanov ergodic Theorem for each
v\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

(2.5)

\frac{\Psi^{(v)}_{n}(\theta)}{\Psi_{n}(\theta)}=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}1_{\mathbb{T}\times\{v\}}\Big(T^{k}_{\alpha}(\theta,0)\Big)}{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}1_{\mathbb{T}\times\{0\}}\Big(T^{k}_{\alpha}(\theta,0)\Big)}\longrightarrow\frac{\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}}1_{\mathbb{T}\times\{v\}}dm_{\mathbb{T}}\times\#}{\int_{\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{Z}}1_{\mathbb{T}\times\{0\}}dm_{\mathbb{T}}\times\#}=1,n\to\infty,

for
m_{\mathbb{T}}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
.
By Egoroff’s Theorem, for every
\epsilon_{v}>0
, there exists
B_{v}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T})
such that
m_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}\setminus B_{v})<\epsilon_{v}
and
\frac{\Psi^{(v)}_{n}(\theta)}{\Psi_{n}(\theta)}\to 1
uniformly on
B_{v}
. In particular, there is some
N_{v}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|\Psi^{(v)}_{n}(\theta)/\Psi_{n}(\theta)-1|<1
for all
n\geq N_{v}
and
\theta\in B_{v}
. Thus
\Psi^{(v)}_{n}(\theta)<2\Psi_{n}(\theta)
for all
n\geq N_{v}
and
\theta\in B_{v}
.
By Theorem
2.1
, there is some
M_{0}>1
such that
\Psi_{n}(\theta)\leq M_{0}\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log{n}}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
m_{\mathbb{T}}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
. It follows that
\Psi^{(v)}_{n}(\theta)<2\Psi_{n}(\theta)\leq 2M_{0}\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log{n}}}
for all
n\geq N_{v}
and
m_{\mathbb{T}}
-a.e.
\theta\in B_{v}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\Psi^{(v)}_{n}(\theta)<2M_{0}\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log{n}}}
for all
n\geq N_{v}
and for all
\theta\in B_{v}
.
Choose an
M_{v}>1
such that
\Psi^{(v)}_{n}(\theta)\leq M_{v}\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log{n}}}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for all
\theta\in B_{v}
.

To sum up, we have:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}
be badly approximable and
v\in\mathbb{Z}
. For any
\epsilon_{v}>0
, there is some
B_{v}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T})
with
m_{\mathbb{T}}(\mathbb{T}\setminus B_{v})<\epsilon_{v}
and
M_{v}>1
such that

(2.6)

\Psi^{(v)}_{n}(\theta)\leq M_{v}\frac{n}{\sqrt{\log{n}}},\ \text{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N},\theta\in B_{v}.

2.3.
Construction of the m.p.s.
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)

2.3.1.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
be the circle.
Let
(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T}),m_{\mathbb{T}},R_{\alpha})
be the circle rotation by an irrational number
\alpha
, where
m_{\mathbb{T}}
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
and

R_{\alpha}:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{T},\ \theta\mapsto\theta+\alpha\pmod{1}.

Let
\Sigma=\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
\big(\Sigma,\mathcal{B}(\Sigma),\nu,\sigma\big)
be the
(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
-shift. That is,
\nu=(\frac{1}{2}\delta_{-1}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{1})^{\mathbb{Z}}
is the product measure on
\Sigma=\{-1,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and for each sequence
\omega\in\Sigma
,

(\sigma\omega)(n)=\omega(n+1),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}.

Now let
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu)=(\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma,\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma),m_{\mathbb{T}}\times\nu)
, and define

(2.7)

T:\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma\rightarrow\mathbb{T}\times\Sigma,\quad(\theta,\omega)\mapsto(\theta+\alpha,\sigma^{\varphi(y)}\omega).

Since
\varphi:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is measurable, it is easy to verify that
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
is a m.p.s.
It is easy to verify that for all
n\geq 1
and
(\theta,\omega)\in X
, we have

(2.8)

T^{n}(\theta,\omega)=(R_{\alpha}^{n}(\theta),\sigma^{\varphi_{n}(y)}\omega)=(\theta+n\alpha,\sigma^{\varphi_{n}(\theta)}\omega).

2.3.2.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
with
a\leq b
and
(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{b-a+1})\in\{-1,1\}^{b-a+1}
, let

(2.9)

_{a}[s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{b-a+1}]_{b}:=\{\omega\in\Sigma:\omega(a)=s_{1},\ldots,\omega(b)=s_{b-a+1}\}.

And for
i\in\{-1,1\}
,
j\in\mathbb{Z}
let

[i]_{j}:={{}_{j}[i]_{j}}=\{\omega\in\Sigma:\omega(j)=i\}.

2.3.3.

The fact that
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
is ergodic and has zero entropy should be well known. Since we can not find a reference, we give a proof for completeness.

Proposition 2.4
.

(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
is an ergodic m.p.s. with
h_{\mu}(X,T)=0
.

Proof.

First we show that
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
is ergodic. Let
A_{1},A_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T})
with
m_{\mathbb{T}}(A_{1})m_{\mathbb{T}}(A_{2})>0
and
B_{j}={{}_{-M_{j}}}[s^{j}]_{M_{j}}\in\mathcal{B}(\Sigma)
, where
s^{j}\in\{0,1\}^{2M_{j}+1}

with some
M_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
,
j=1,2
.
By (
2.8
),

\begin{split}&\mu\big((A_{1}\times B_{1})\cap T^{-n}(A_{2}\times B_{2})\big)\\
=&\int_{\Sigma}\int_{\mathbb{T}}1_{R_{\alpha}^{-n}A_{2}\cap A_{1}}(\theta)\cdot 1_{\sigma^{-\varphi_{n}(\theta)}B_{2}\cap B_{1}(\omega)}dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)d\nu(\omega)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{T}}1_{R_{\alpha}^{-n}A_{2}\cap A_{1}}(\theta)\cdot\nu({\sigma^{-\varphi_{n}(\theta)}B_{2}\cap B_{1}})dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta).\end{split}

Note that when
|q|>M_{1}+M_{2}
,
\nu(\sigma^{-q}B_{1}\cap B_{2})=\nu(B_{1})\nu(B_{2})
. Thus

(2.10)

\begin{split}&\mu\big((A_{1}\times B_{1})\cap T^{-n}(A_{2}\times B_{2})\big)\\
\geq&\int_{\mathbb{T}}1_{R_{\alpha}^{-n}A_{2}\cap A_{1}}(\theta)\cdot\nu({\sigma^{-\varphi_{n}(\theta)}B_{2}\cap B_{1}})1_{\{k\in\mathbb{N}:|\varphi_{k}(\theta)|>M_{1}+M_{2}\}}(n)dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{T}}1_{R_{\alpha}^{-n}A_{2}\cap A_{1}}(\theta)\cdot\nu(B_{1})\nu(B_{2})1_{\{k\in\mathbb{N}:|\varphi_{k}(\theta)|>M_{1}+M_{2}\}}(n)dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)\\
=&\int_{\mathbb{T}}1_{R_{\alpha}^{-n}A_{2}\cap A_{1}}(\theta)\cdot\nu(B_{1})\nu(B_{2})\big(1-1_{\{k\in\mathbb{N}:|\varphi_{k}(\theta)|\leq M_{1}+M_{2}\}}(n)\big)dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)\\
\geq&m_{\mathbb{T}}(R_{\alpha}^{-n}A_{2}\cap A_{1})\nu(B_{1})\nu(B_{2})-\int_{m_{\mathbb{T}}}1_{\{k\in\mathbb{N}:|\varphi_{k}(\theta)|\leq M_{1}+M_{2}\}}(n)dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta).\end{split}

Note that

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}1_{\{k\in\mathbb{N}:|\varphi_{k}(\theta)|\leq M_{1}+M_{2}\}}(n)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\sum_{v=-(M_{1}+M_{2})}^{M_{1}+M_{2}}1_{\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\varphi_{k}(\theta)=v\}}(n)\\
=&\sum_{v=-(M_{1}+M_{2})}^{M_{1}+M_{2}}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}1_{\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\varphi_{k}(\theta)=v\}}(n)=\sum_{v=-(M_{1}+M_{2})}^{M_{1}+M_{2}}\frac{\Psi_{N}^{(v)}(\theta)}{N}\end{split}

Thus

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mu\big((A_{1}\times B_{1})\cap T^{-n}(A_{2}\times B_{2})\big)\\
\geq&\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}m_{\mathbb{T}}(R_{\alpha}^{-n}A_{2}\cap A_{1})\nu(B_{1})\nu(B_{2})-\sum_{v=-(M_{1}+M_{2})}^{M_{1}+M_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\Psi_{N}^{(v)}(\theta)}{N}dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta).\end{split}

By (
2.2
) and (
2.5
), we have for each
v\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\Psi_{N}^{(v)}(\theta)}{N}=0
for
m_{\mathbb{T}}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
. By the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we have for each
v\in\mathbb{Z}

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\Psi_{N}^{(v)}(\theta)}{N}dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)=0.

By the ergodicity of
(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T}),m_{\mathbb{T}},R_{\alpha})
, we deduce

\begin{split}&\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mu\big((A_{1}\times B_{1})\cap T^{-n}(A_{2}\times B_{2})\big)\\
\geq&\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}m_{\mathbb{T}}(R_{\alpha}^{-n}A_{2}\cap A_{1})\nu(B_{1})\nu(B_{2})-\sum_{v=-(M_{1}+M_{2})}^{M_{1}+M_{2}}\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\Psi_{N}^{(v)}(\theta)}{N}dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)\\
=&m_{\mathbb{T}}(A_{1})m_{\mathbb{T}}(A_{2})\nu(B_{1})\nu(B_{2})=\mu(A_{1}\times B_{1})\mu(A_{2}\times B_{2}).\end{split}

Then it is standard to prove that for all
D_{1},D_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
, we have that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mu\big(D_{1}\cap T^{-n}D_{2}\big)\geq\mu(D_{1})\mu(D_{2}).

In particular, we have that for any
D_{1},D_{2}\in\mathcal{X}
with
\mu(D_{1})\mu(D_{2})>0
, there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mu\big(D_{1}\cap T^{-n}D_{2}\big)>0
, which means that
(X,\mathcal{X},\mu,T)
is ergodic.

Now we use Abramov-Rokhlin formula to show that
h_{\mu}(T)=0
.
The proof of this part is almost the same to the proof of Proposition 2.2 in
[
13
]
.

For any finite measurable partition
\beta
of
\Sigma
, we have

h_{\mu}(T|R_{\alpha},\beta)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H_{\nu}(\bigvee_{n=0}^{N-1}\sigma^{-\varphi_{n}(\theta)}\beta)dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta),

where
\varphi_{0}(\theta)\equiv 0
.

For
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote
a_{N}(\theta)
the cardinality of the set
\{\varphi_{n}(\theta):0\leq n\leq N-1\}
, i.e.

a_{N}(\theta)=|\{\varphi_{n}(\theta):0\leq n\leq N-1\}|.

Then
a_{N}
is a measurable function from
\mathbb{T}
to
\{1,2,\cdots,N\}
, and the cardinality of
\bigvee_{n=0}^{N-1}\sigma^{-\varphi_{n}(\theta)}\beta
is not greater than
|\beta|^{a_{N}(\theta)}
for any
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
, and hence

\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H_{\nu}(\bigvee_{n=0}^{N-1}\sigma^{-\varphi_{n}(\theta)}\beta)dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)\leq\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log|\beta|^{a_{N}(\theta)}dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{a_{N}(\theta)}{N}\log|\beta|dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta).

We claim that
for
m_{\mathbb{T}}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
,

(2.11)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}(\theta)}{N}=0.

We now show the claim. Since
(\mathbb{T},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{T}),m_{\mathbb{T}},R_{\alpha})
is ergodic and
\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi dm=0
, by Birkhoff ergodic theorem, for
m_{\mathbb{T}}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
,

\frac{\varphi_{n}(\theta)}{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\varphi(R^{k}_{\alpha}\theta)\to\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi dm_{\mathbb{T}}=0,\ n\to\infty.

Thus for
m_{\mathbb{T}}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
, for any
\epsilon>0
there is some
M(\theta,\epsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that when
n\geq M(\theta,\epsilon)
, we have

|\frac{\varphi_{n}(\theta)}{n}|\leq\epsilon
. Thus for
N>M(\theta,\epsilon)
, we have
|\varphi_{n}(\theta)|\leq\epsilon n\leq\epsilon N
for all
M(\theta,\epsilon)\leq n\leq N
. It follows that

a_{N}(\theta)\leq M(\theta,\epsilon)+2\epsilon N+1,

and

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}(\theta)}{N}\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{M(\theta,\epsilon)+2\epsilon N+1}{N}\leq 2\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we have (
2.11
), i.e. for
m_{\mathbb{T}}
-a.e.
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
,

\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}(\theta)}{N}=0.
This ends the proof of the claim.

Thus by the Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(T|R_{\alpha},\beta)

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}}H_{\nu}(\bigvee_{n=0}^{N-1}\sigma^{-\varphi_{n}(\theta)}\beta)dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\log|\beta|\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{a_{N}(\theta)}{N}dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)=\log|\beta|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{a_{N}(\theta)}{N}dm_{\mathbb{T}}(\theta)

\displaystyle=0.

As
\beta
is an arbitrary finite measurable partition of
\Sigma
, we have
h_{\mu}(T|R_{\alpha})=0
. Then by Abramov-Rokhlin formula,

h_{\mu}(T)=h_{m_{\mathbb{T}}}(R_{\alpha})+h_{\mu}(T|R_{\alpha})=0.

The proof is complete.
∎

2.5.
Proof of the Main Theorem

Let

A_{1}=B\times\Sigma,\quad A_{2}=A_{3}=\mathbb{T}\times[1]_{0}.

We will show that the limit

(2.14)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mu(A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\cap S^{-n}A_{3})

does not exist. Then we have our main theorem.

Note that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

T^{n}(\theta,\omega)=(\theta+n\alpha,\sigma^{\varphi_{n}(\theta)}\omega),

and

S^{n}(\theta,\omega)=\pi^{-1}\circ T^{n}\circ\pi(\theta,\omega)=(\theta+n\alpha,\pi_{E}^{-1}\circ\sigma^{\varphi_{n}(\theta)}\circ\pi_{E}(\omega)),

It follows that
(\theta,\omega)\in A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\cap S^{-n}A_{3}
if and only if

\theta\in B,\ \omega(\varphi_{n}(\theta))=1,\ \text{and }\ (\pi_{E}\omega)(\varphi_{n}(\theta))=1.

By the definition of
\pi_{E}
, we have

(\pi_{E}\omega)(\varphi_{n}(\theta))=1\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\omega(\varphi_{n}(\theta))=1,&\hbox{if $\varphi_{n}(\theta)\in E$;}\\
\omega(\varphi_{n}(\theta))=-1,&\hbox{if $\varphi_{n}(\theta)\not\in E$.}\end{array}\right.

Thus

A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\cap S^{-n}A_{3}=\bigcup\{\{\theta\}\times[1]_{\varphi_{n}(\theta)}\in X:\theta\in B,\varphi_{n}(\theta)\in E\}.

And by Fubini’s Theorem

\mu(A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\cap S^{-n}A_{3})=\int_{\theta\in B\atop{\varphi_{n}(\theta)\in E}}\nu([1]_{\varphi_{n}(\theta)})dm_{\mathbb{T}}=\frac{1}{2}m_{\mathbb{T}}(\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}:\varphi_{n}(\theta)\in E\}).

Hence

(2.15)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mu(A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\cap S^{-n}A_{3})\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\Big(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}1_{E}(\varphi_{n}(\theta))\Big)dm_{\mathbb{T}}\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\frac{\#\{0\leq n\leq N-1:\varphi_{n}(\theta)\in E\}}{N}dm_{\mathbb{T}}.\end{split}

2.5.1.
The case when
N=l_{m+1}

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that for
0\leq n\leq l_{m+1}-1
,
|\varphi_{n}(\theta)|\leq l_{m+1}-1
, and
[l_{m}+r_{m}+1,l_{m+1}-1]\cap E=\emptyset
. Thus we have

\begin{split}&\#\{0\leq n<l_{m+1}:\varphi_{n}(\theta)\in E\}\\
\leq&\#\{0\leq n<l_{m+1}:|\varphi_{n}(\theta)|\leq l_{m}+r_{m}\}\\
\leq&\sum_{k=-(l_{m}+r_{m})}^{l_{m}+r_{m}}\Psi_{l_{m+1}}^{(k)}(\theta)\leq(2l_{m}+2r_{m}+1)C_{l_{m}+r_{m}}\frac{l_{m+1}}{\sqrt{\log{l_{m+1}}}}.\end{split}

The last inequality is from (
2.12
) and (
2.13
). By (
2.15
)

(2.16)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{l_{m+1}}\sum_{n=0}^{l_{m+1}-1}\mu(A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\cap S^{-n}A_{3})\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\frac{\#\{0\leq n\leq l_{m+1}-1:\varphi_{n}(\theta)\in E\}}{l_{m+1}}dm_{\mathbb{T}}\\
\leq&\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\frac{(2l_{m}+2r_{m}+1)C_{l_{m}+r_{m}}}{l_{m+1}}\frac{l_{m+1}}{\sqrt{\log{l_{m+1}}}}dm_{\mathbb{T}}\\
\leq&C_{l_{m}+r_{m}}\frac{l_{m}+r_{m}+1}{\sqrt{\log{l_{m+1}}}}\leq\frac{1}{m}.\quad\quad(\text{by the condition}\ (c))\end{split}

2.5.2.
The case when
N=l_{m}+r_{m}+1

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
[l_{m},l_{m}+r_{m}]\subseteq E
, for
0\leq n\leq l_{m}+r_{m}
,
\varphi_{n}(\theta)\not\in E
implies that
\varphi_{n}(\theta)\leq l_{m}-1
.
Hence we have

\begin{split}&\#\{0\leq n\leq l_{m}+r_{m}:\varphi_{n}(\theta)\in E\}\\
\geq&l_{m}+r_{m}+1-\#\{0\leq n\leq l_{m}+r_{m}:\varphi_{n}(\theta)\not\in E\}\\
\geq&l_{m}+r_{m}+1-\#\{0\leq n\leq l_{m}+r_{m}:|\varphi_{n}(\theta)|\leq l_{m}-1\}\\
\geq&l_{m}+r_{m}+1-\sum_{k=-(l_{m}-1)}^{l_{m}-1}\Psi_{l_{m}+r_{m}}^{(k)}(\theta)\\
\geq&l_{m}+r_{m}+1-C_{l_{m}-1}(2l_{m}-1)\frac{l_{m}+r_{m}}{\sqrt{\log{(l_{m}+r_{m})}}}.\end{split}

The last inequality is from (
2.12
) and (
2.13
). By (
2.15
)

(2.17)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{l_{m}+r_{m}+1}\sum_{n=0}^{l_{m}+r_{m}}\mu(A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\cap S^{-n}A_{3})\\
=&\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\frac{\#\{0\leq n\leq l_{m}+r_{m}:\varphi_{n}(\theta)\in E\}}{l_{m}+r_{m}+1}dm_{\mathbb{T}}\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\Big(1-\frac{C_{l_{m}-1}(2l_{m}-1)}{l_{m}+r_{m}+1}\frac{l_{m}+r_{m}}{\sqrt{\log{(l_{m}+r_{m})}}}\Big)dm_{\mathbb{T}}\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-2\frac{C_{l_{m}}l_{m}}{\sqrt{\log{(l_{m}+r_{m})}}}\Big)\quad\quad\quad(\text{since}\ C_{l_{m}-1}\leq C_{l_{m}})\\
\geq&\frac{1}{2}-\frac{C_{l_{m}}l_{m}}{\sqrt{\log{(l_{m}+r_{m})}}}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{m}.\quad\quad(\text{by the condition}\ (b))\end{split}

By (
2.16
) and (
2.17
), we have that the limit

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mu(A_{1}\cap T^{-n}A_{2}\cap S^{-n}A_{3})

does not exist. The proof of Main Theorem is complete.

Chain rules for quantum channels
Mario Berta, Marco Tomamichel

Abstract
Divergence chain rules for channels relate the divergence of a pair of channel inputs to the divergence of the corresponding channel outputs. An important special case of such a rule is the data-processing inequality, which tells us that if the same channel is applied to both inputs then the divergence cannot increase. Based on direct matrix analysis methods, we derive several Rényi divergence chain rules for channels in the quantum setting. Our results simplify and in some cases generalise previous derivations in the literature.

III
Main results

Our main result is the following Rényi divergences based simple meta chain rule for quantum channels.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\rho,\sigma\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H})
,
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}
be positive maps from
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
to
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
, and
\alpha\in(0,\infty]

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho)\big\|\mathcal{F}(\sigma)\big)\leq\widehat{D}_{\alpha}(\rho\|\sigma)+\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}\big(\mathcal{E}\big\|\mathcal{F}\big)\,,

(27)

where
\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}
denotes any Rényi divergence of order
\alpha
.

Proof.

Using Matsumoto’s construction (Lemma
1
), we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\hat{p}_{x}\,\mathcal{E}\big(\underbrace{\Gamma\big(|x\rangle\!\langle x|\big)}_{=:\rho^{x}}\big)\quad\textrm{and}

(28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}\hat{q}_{x}\,\mathcal{F}\big(\underbrace{\Gamma\big(|x\rangle\!\langle x|\big)}_{=\rho^{x}}\big)\,.

(29)

We then use the data-processing inequality to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho)\big\|\mathcal{F}(\sigma)\big)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{x}\hat{p}_{x}|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes\mathcal{E}\left(\rho^{x}\right)\middle\|\sum_{x}\hat{q}_{x}|x\rangle\!\langle x|\otimes\mathcal{F}\left(\rho^{x}\right)\right)

(30)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\log\left(\sum_{x}\hat{p}_{x}^{\alpha}\hat{q}_{x}^{1-\alpha}2^{(\alpha-1)\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{E}\left(\rho^{x}\right)\middle\|\mathcal{F}\left(\rho^{x}\right)\right)}\right)

(31)

\displaystyle\leq D_{\alpha}(\hat{p}\|\hat{q})+\max_{x}\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{E}\left(\rho^{x}\right)\middle\|\mathcal{F}\left(\rho^{x}\right)\right)\,,

(32)

which implies the statement we set out to prove.
∎

as one would have to divide the logarithm on both sides of Eq. (
20
) with the pre-factor
\alpha-1
, which becomes negative in this range.
Notwithstanding, at least for
\alpha=1
it is known that
[
15
, Theorem 3.5]

\displaystyle\widetilde{D}_{1}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho)\big\|\mathcal{F}(\sigma)\big)\leq\widetilde{D}_{1}(\rho\|\sigma)+\widetilde{D}_{1}^{\infty}\big(\mathcal{E}\big\|\mathcal{F}\big)\,.

(43)

However, this does not directly follow from our Rényi divergences chain rules, as the order of taking the limits in
n\to\infty
and
\alpha\to 1
might ultimately matter when regularizing Eq. (
4
). We briefly get back to this question in Section
V
and present some conjectures.

\displaystyle-\mathbb{D}_{\alpha}\big(\rho_{AR}\big\|\sigma_{AR}\big)\Big\}\,,

V
Outlook

We hope that our matrix analysis based techniques will be more broadly useful to derive entropy inequalities for quantum channels. Many fundamental questions remain with respect to the goal of giving for
\alpha>0
the most general Rényi divergence chain rules for quantum channels. For example, our main results from Section
III
immediately raise the question if

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\Lambda^{n}}\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{D}_{1}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\left(\Lambda^{n}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)\right)\big\|\mathcal{F}^{\otimes n}\left(\Lambda^{n}\left(\sigma^{\otimes n}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\widetilde{D}_{1}\big(\mathcal{E}(\rho)\big\|\mathcal{F}(\sigma)\big)\,,

(48)

where the supremum is over all rank-one projective measurement maps
\Lambda^{n}(\cdot)
acting on
n
copies. Choosing the standard pinching based measurement map
\Lambda^{n}(\cdot)=\mathcal{M}_{\sigma^{\otimes n}}(P_{\sigma^{\otimes n}}(\cdot))
in the form of Eq. (
6
) only leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sup_{\Lambda^{n}}\widetilde{D}_{1}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\left(\Lambda^{n}\left(\rho^{\otimes n}\right)\right)\big\|\mathcal{F}^{\otimes n}\left(\Lambda^{n}\left(\sigma^{\otimes n}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n}\widetilde{D}_{1}\left(\mathcal{E}^{\otimes n}\left(\int\mu(\mathrm{d}t)\,\left(\sigma^{it}\rho\sigma^{-it}\right)^{\otimes n}\right)\big\|\left(\mathcal{F}\left(\sigma\right)\right)^{\otimes n}\right)

(49)

\displaystyle\geq\min_{t}\widetilde{D}_{1}\left(\mathcal{E}\left(\sigma^{it}\rho\sigma^{-it}\right)\middle\|\mathcal{F}(\sigma)\right)-\frac{1}{n}\log\text{poly}(n)\,,

(50)

where we used in the last inequality that by Carathéodory’s theorem the integral
\int\mu(\mathrm{d}t)(\cdot)^{\otimes n}
is discretized with at most
\text{poly}(n)
terms
[
6
, Theorem D.5]
and can then by
[
5
, Lemma 2.5]
be taken outside of the divergence at the price of the asymptotically small
\log\text{poly}(n)
fudge term. The appearance of unitary operators like
\sigma^{it}
is reminiscent of previous work on quantum entropy inequalities
[
18
,
8
,
41
,
36
,
24
,
37
,
38
,
5
]
, but here we would need to eliminate them.

As the
\alpha\in(1,\infty]
analogue of Eq. (
V
) holds by Corollary
4
, we might also ask more generally what happens for the whole parameter range
\alpha\in(0,1]
. This is strongly connected with the question if
\widehat{D}_{\alpha}^{\text{stab},\infty}\big(\mathcal{E}\big\|\mathcal{F}\big)
is continuous for
\alpha\to 1

[
17
]
. Any insight on this question would have widespread applications in quantum information theory
[
16
]
— starting from the fundamental statistical task of channel discrimination.

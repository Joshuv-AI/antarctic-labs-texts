Global regularity results for a class of singular/degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equations
Sumiya Baasandorj, Sun-Sig Byun, Ki-Ahm Lee, Se-Chan Lee

Abstract
. We provide the Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci estimate and global C 1 , α \displaystyle C^{1,\alpha} -regularity for a class of singular/degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equations. We also derive the existence of a viscosity solution to the Dirichlet problem with the associated operator.

\displaystyle(\lambda,\Lambda)

3.

Before we develop

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\partial\Omega

\displaystyle L^{n}
-norm of

Definition 3.1
.

For
\displaystyle v:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
\displaystyle R>0
, the
upper contact set
is defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Gamma^{+}(v,\Omega)=\left\{x\in\Omega:\exists p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ such that }u(y)\leq u(x)+\left\langle p,y-x\right\rangle\text{ for all }y\in\Omega\right\},\\
&\Gamma^{+}_{R}(v,\Omega)=\left\{x\in\Omega:\exists p\in\overline{B_{R}(0)}\text{ such that }u(y)\leq u(x)+\left\langle p,y-x\right\rangle\text{ for all }y\in\Omega\right\}.\end{split}

We are now ready to prove the ABP estimate.

Theorem 3.2

(Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci estimate)
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
is a viscosity subsolution (resp. supersolution) of (
1
) in
\displaystyle\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>0\}
(resp.
\displaystyle\{x\in\Omega:u(x)<0\}
) under the assumptions
(A1):
-
(A2):
. Suppose that
\displaystyle f\in L^{n}(\Omega)\cap C(\Omega)
. Then, there exists a constant
\displaystyle c\equiv c(n,\lambda,i(\Phi),s(\Phi),L,\nu_{0})
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup\limits_{\Omega}u\leq\sup\limits_{\partial\Omega}g^{+}+c\diam(\Omega)\left(\max\left\{\left\lVert f^{-}\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{i(\Phi)+1}}_{L^{n}(\Gamma^{+}(u^{+}))},\left\lVert f^{-}\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{s(\Phi)+1}}_{L^{n}(\Gamma^{+}(u^{+}))}\right\}+1\right),

respectively,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\sup\limits_{\Omega}u^{-}\leq\sup\limits_{\partial\Omega}g^{-}+c\diam(\Omega)\left(\max\left\{\left\lVert f^{+}\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{i(\Phi)+1}}_{L^{n}(\Gamma^{+}(u^{-}))},\left\lVert f^{+}\right\rVert^{\frac{1}{s(\Phi)+1}}_{L^{n}(\Gamma^{+}(u^{-}))}\right\}+1\right)\right).

In particular, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}+c\diam(\Omega)\left(\max\left\{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{n}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{i(\Phi)+1}},\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{n}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{s(\Phi)+1}}\right\}+1\right)

for some constant
\displaystyle c\equiv c(n,\lambda,i(\Phi),s(\Phi),L,\nu_{0})>0
.

Proof.

The proof consists of two parts. In the first part, we prove the above theorem for the viscosity subsolution
\displaystyle u\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
. In the second part, we consider
\displaystyle u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
via approximation based on the sup convolutions.

Part 1.
Suppose that the subsolution
\displaystyle u
belongs to
\displaystyle C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C(\overline{\Omega})
. Let us define

\displaystyle\displaystyle R_{0}\equiv R_{0}(u):=\frac{1}{\diam(\Omega)}\left(\sup\limits_{x\in\Omega}u(x)-\sup\limits_{x\in\partial\Omega}u^{+}\right).

The purpose is to obtain a certain estimate on
\displaystyle R_{0}
in terms of
\displaystyle\left\lVert f^{-}\right\rVert_{L^{n}(\Gamma^{+}(u^{+}))}
and
\displaystyle\data
, from which the estimate (
3.1
) follows. Applying
[
22
, Lemma 3.1]
, for all
\displaystyle R<R_{0}
, we find

(3.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{R}(0)}g(z)\,dz\leq\int\limits_{\Gamma_{R}^{+}(u^{+})}g(Du)\left|\det\left(D^{2}u\right)\right|\,dx\quad(\forall g\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,g\geq 0)

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle D^{2}u\leq 0\text{ on }\Gamma_{R}^{+}(u^{+})\subset\{x\in\Omega:u(x)>0\}.

Let us now discuss the behavior of
\displaystyle Du
in the set
\displaystyle\Gamma_{R}^{+}(u^{+})
. Let
\displaystyle x_{0}\in\Gamma_{R}^{+}(u^{+})
be any point. If
\displaystyle Du(x_{0})\neq 0
, then we are able to take
\displaystyle u
as a test function in the definition of viscosity subsolution. In turn, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(x_{0},|Du(x_{0})|)F(D^{2}u(x_{0}))\geq f(x_{0}).

Then we see

\displaystyle\displaystyle-f^{-}(x_{0})\leq f(x_{0})\leq\Phi(x_{0},|Du(x_{0})|)F(D^{2}u(x_{0}))\leq\Phi(x_{0},|Du(x_{0})|)P_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}u(x_{0})).

Recalling
\displaystyle D^{2}u(x_{0})\leq 0
by (
3.5
), we find
\displaystyle P_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(D^{2}u(x_{0}))=\lambda\tr(D^{2}u(x_{0}))
and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\frac{-\tr(D^{2}u(x_{0}))}{n}\right)^{n}\leq\left(\frac{f^{-}(x_{0})}{n\lambda\Phi(x_{0},|Du(x_{0})|)}\right)^{n}.

If
\displaystyle Du(x_{0})=0
and
\displaystyle D^{2}u(x_{0})\neq 0
, then
\displaystyle x_{0}
is a critical point of
\displaystyle u
. On the other hand, recalling again (
3.5
), we have
\displaystyle D^{2}u(x_{0})<0
, which means that
\displaystyle x_{0}
is a non-degenerate critical point of
\displaystyle u
. However, the set of non-degenerate critical points of
\displaystyle u
is countable since
\displaystyle u\in C^{2}(\Omega)
.

Let us recall also the following classical inequality,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\det(A)\det(B)\leq\left(\frac{\tr(AB)}{n}\right)^{n}\text{ for all }A,B\in\mathcal{S}(n)\text{ with }A,B\geq 0.

In turn, the last display together with (
3.5
) and (
3.6

Lemma 4.2

Here note that

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho^{k(1+\alpha)}|\overline{a}|\leq C_{0}\rho^{k(1+\alpha)},

\displaystyle\displaystyle|b_{k+1}-b_{k}|

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\rho^{k\alpha}|\overline{b}|\leq C_{0}\rho^{k\alpha}.

Therefore,
(E1):
and
(E2):
hold for
\displaystyle k+1
.

∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.1
.

We first consider the degenerate case, i.e.,
\displaystyle i(\Phi)\geq 0
. By
Lemma

5.1
, a viscosity solution can be approximated by an affine function with an error of order
\displaystyle r^{1+\alpha}
. By following the argument in
[
8
]
, we can derive the desired global
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate provided that
\displaystyle i(\Phi)\geq 0
.

On the other hand, for the singular case (
\displaystyle i(\Phi)<0
), we employ the idea of
[
4
]
. Indeed, we claim that
Lemma

5.1
still holds for the singular case.
Theorem

4.4
guarantees that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|u\|_{C^{0,1}(B_{3/4}\cap\{y_{n}>\phi(y^{\prime})\})}\leq c,

for a universal constant
\displaystyle c>0
. Then
\displaystyle u
is a viscosity solution of

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{\Phi}(y,|Du|)F(D^{2}u)=\tilde{f}(y)&\text{in $\displaystyle B_{3/4}\cap\{y_{n}>\phi(y^{\prime})\}$},\\
u(y)=g(y)&\text{on $\displaystyle B_{3/4}\cap\{y_{n}=\phi(y^{\prime})\}$},\end{array}\right.

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(y,t):=t^{-i(\Phi)}\Phi(y,t)\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{f}(y):=|Du|^{-i(\Phi)}f(y).

Here
\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}
satisfies the condition
(A2):
with
\displaystyle i(\tilde{\Phi})=0
,
\displaystyle s(\tilde{\Phi})=s(\Phi)-i(\Phi)
, and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq c^{-i(\Phi)}\varepsilon_{0}.

Thus, one can repeat the argument in the proof of
Lemma

5.1
to obtain the global
\displaystyle C^{1,\alpha}
-estimate.
∎

6.
Comparison principle and Perron’s method

The purpose of this section is to study the classical result of comparison principle and as a consequence, to deduce the existence of a viscosity solution to (
1
) by Perron’s method. Nevertheless, the assumptions
(A1):
-
(A4):
are not sufficient to obtain the aforementioned results. Therefore, we require an additional assumption
(A5):
which guarantees the comparison principle for approximated Dirichlet problems. Before we precisely state this new assumption, we summarize known results regarding the comparison principle.

Remark 6.1

(Comparison principle)
.

Let
\displaystyle H:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
proper
map. In other words,
\displaystyle H
satisfies

(6.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle H(x,r,p,X)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq H(x,r,p,Y)\quad\text{whenever $\displaystyle X\leq Y$},

(6.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle H(x,r,p,X)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq H(x,s,p,X)\quad\text{whenever $\displaystyle s\leq r$.}

Then we say that
\displaystyle H
satisfies the
comparison principle
if the following holds:

Let
\displaystyle v\in\mathrm{USC}(\overline{\Omega})
[resp.
\displaystyle w\in\mathrm{LSC}(\overline{\Omega})
] be a subsolution [resp. supersolution] of
\displaystyle H=0
in
\displaystyle\Omega
and
\displaystyle v\leq w
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. Then
\displaystyle v\leq w
in
\displaystyle\overline{\Omega}
.

We refer to
[
5
,
10
,
19
,
23
,
24
]
for several sufficient conditions of then comparison principle. In short,
\displaystyle H
satisfies the comparison principle if
\displaystyle H(x,r,p,X)
is independent of
\displaystyle x
, and one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

\displaystyle H(x,r,p,X)
is strictly decreasing in
\displaystyle r
and
\displaystyle H
is degenerate elliptic (i.e.,
\displaystyle H
satisfies (
6.1
)), or

(ii)

\displaystyle H(x,r,p,X)
is nonincreasing in
\displaystyle r
and
\displaystyle H
is uniformly elliptic.

It is noteworthy that the condition that
\displaystyle H
is independent of
\displaystyle x
can be relaxed to some extra structure conditions on
\displaystyle H
, which display a kind of smoothness on
\displaystyle H
with respect to
\displaystyle x
-variable; see
[
10
,
19
,
23
]
for details.

We now consider a proper map

\displaystyle\displaystyle H(x,u,Du,D^{2}u):=\Phi(x,|Du|)F(D^{2}u)-f(x)

with
(A1):
-
(A4):
.
It is easily checked that
\displaystyle H
is degenerate elliptic, nonincreasing in
\displaystyle r
, and
\displaystyle H
depends on
\displaystyle x
. In view of the previous remark, we cannot expect the comparison principle for
\displaystyle H
and Perron’s method for the associated Dirichlet problem. To overcome this challenge, we will impose an additional structure condition on
\displaystyle H
and approximate the map
\displaystyle H
to ensure the strict monotonicity with respect to
\displaystyle r
-variable:

(i)

(A5):

There exists a continuous function
\displaystyle\omega:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\displaystyle\omega(0)=0
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(x,\alpha|x-y|)F(X)-\Phi(y,\alpha|x-y|)F(-Y)\leq\omega(\alpha|x-y|^{2}+|x-y|),

whenever
\displaystyle\alpha>0
,
\displaystyle x,y\in\Omega
,
\displaystyle X,Y\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
and

(6.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle-3\alpha\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\
0&Y\end{pmatrix}\leq 3\alpha\begin{pmatrix}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{pmatrix}.

Similar conditions with
(A5):
can be found in
[
5
,
10
]
.

(ii)

Let us consider the approximated problem given by

(6.4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}(x,u,Du,D^{2}u):=\Phi(x,|Du|)F(D^{2}u)-f(x)-\varepsilon u=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega

for
\displaystyle\varepsilon>0
. Clearly,
\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}
is strictly decreasing in
\displaystyle r
.

Lemma 6.2

(Comparison principle II)
.

Suppose that the assumptions
(A1):
,
(A2):
,
(A5):
are in force and
\displaystyle f\in C(\overline{\Omega})
. Then
\displaystyle H_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the comparison principle:

Let
\displaystyle v
and
\displaystyle w
be a viscosity subsolution and a supersolution of (
6.4
), respectively. If
\displaystyle v\leq w
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
, then
\displaystyle v\leq w
in
\displaystyle\Omega
.

Proof.

By contradiction, we suppose that

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{0}:=\sup\limits_{x\in\overline{\Omega}}(v(x)-w(x))>0.

For any
\displaystyle\alpha>0
, we define

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{\alpha}:=\sup\limits_{x,y\in\overline{\Omega}}\left[v(x)-w(y)-(\alpha/2)|x-y|^{2}\right]

and clearly
\displaystyle L_{\alpha}\geq L_{0}
. Suppose that the maximum
\displaystyle L_{\alpha}
is attained at a point
\displaystyle(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})\in\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega}
. It implies from
[
10
, Lemma 3.1]
that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\alpha\to\infty}\alpha|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|^{2}=0.

This one and the fact that
\displaystyle v\leq w
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
yield that
\displaystyle x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}\in\Omega
for
\displaystyle\alpha>0
large enough. At this moment, we are able to apply Ishii-Lions lemma,
[
10
, Theorem 3.2]
, to ensure that there exist a limiting super-jet
\displaystyle\left(\alpha(x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}),X_{\alpha}\right)
of
\displaystyle v
at
\displaystyle x_{\alpha}
and a limiting sub-jet
\displaystyle\left(\alpha(x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}),-Y_{\alpha}\right)
of
\displaystyle w
at
\displaystyle y_{\alpha}
so that

\displaystyle\displaystyle-3\alpha\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&I\end{pmatrix}\leq\begin{pmatrix}X_{\alpha}&0\\
0&Y_{\alpha}\end{pmatrix}\leq 3\alpha\begin{pmatrix}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Phi\left(x_{\alpha},\alpha|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|\right)F(X_{\alpha})-\varepsilon v(x_{\alpha})\geq f(x_{\alpha}),\\
\Phi\left(y_{\alpha},\alpha|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|\right)F(-Y_{\alpha})-\varepsilon w(y_{\alpha})\leq f(y_{\alpha}).\end{cases}

By using the relation
\displaystyle L_{0}=\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}[v(x_{\alpha})-w(y_{\alpha})]
and the assumption
(A5):
, we have, for sufficiently large
\displaystyle\alpha>0
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon L_{0}}{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon[v(x_{\alpha})-w(y_{\alpha})]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq f(y_{\alpha})-f(x_{\alpha})+\Phi\left(x_{\alpha},\alpha|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|\right)F(X_{\alpha})-\Phi\left(y_{\alpha},\alpha|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|\right)F(-Y_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq f(y_{\alpha})-f(x_{\alpha})+\omega(\alpha|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|^{2}+|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|).

Since
\displaystyle f\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and
\displaystyle\omega(0+)=0
, we arrive at a contradiction when
\displaystyle\alpha\to\infty
.
∎

We now turn our attention to showing the existence of a viscosity solution to (
6.4
).

Lemma 6.3

(Existence of sub/supersolutions)
.

Suppose the assumptions
(A1):
-
(A4):
are in force. Then for every
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, there exist a viscosity subsolution
\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
and a viscosity supersolution
\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}\in C(\overline{\Omega})
of (
6.4
) with
\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}=w_{\varepsilon}=g
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. Moreover, there exists a positive constant
\displaystyle c\equiv c(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\nu_{0},L,r,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega),\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)})
such that

\displaystyle\displaystyle-c\leq v_{\varepsilon}\leq w_{\varepsilon}\leq c,\quad\text{for any $\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon<1$.}

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle z\in\partial\Omega
be a fixed point. There exists a point
\displaystyle x_{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
such that
\displaystyle\overline{B_{r}(x_{z})}\cap\overline{\Omega}=\{z\}
with
\displaystyle r=|z-x_{z}|
since
\displaystyle\Omega
satisfies a uniform exterior sphere property; see
Lemma

2.2
. We now consider a function
\displaystyle v_{z}:\overline{\Omega}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
defined by

\displaystyle\displaystyle v_{z}(x):=K\left(r^{-\kappa_{0}}-|x-x_{z}|^{-\kappa_{0}}\right)

for positive constants
\displaystyle\kappa_{0}:=\frac{n\Lambda+1}{\lambda}
and
\displaystyle K\geq\min\left\{1,\frac{R^{\kappa_{0}+1}}{\kappa_{0}}\right\}
to be determined later, with
\displaystyle R:=r+\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
. Note that
\displaystyle v_{z}(z)=0
,
\displaystyle v_{z}>0
in
\displaystyle\Omega
, and direct calculations yield that

\displaystyle\displaystyle Dv_{z}(x)=K\kappa_{0}\frac{x-x_{z}}{|x-x_{z}|^{\kappa_{0}+2}}

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle D^{2}v_{z}(x)=K\kappa_{0}\frac{I}{|x-x_{z}|^{\kappa_{0}+2}}-K\kappa_{0}(\kappa_{0}+2)\frac{(x-x_{z})\otimes(x-x_{z})}{|x-x_{z}|^{\kappa_{0}+4}}.

Due to the choice of
\displaystyle\kappa_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle F(D^{2}v_{z}(x))\leq\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{|x-x_{z}|^{\kappa_{0}+2}}\left((n-1)\Lambda-(\kappa_{0}+1)\lambda\right)\leq-\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{|x-x_{z}|^{\kappa_{0}+2}}.

On the other hand, for a fixed
\displaystyle\delta\in(0,1)
, we further define

\displaystyle\displaystyle v_{z,\delta}(x):=g(z)+\delta+M_{\delta}v_{z}(x),

where the constant
\displaystyle M_{\delta}\geq 1
can be chosen so that
\displaystyle v_{z,\delta}\geq g
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. This is possible because
\displaystyle K\geq 1
and
\displaystyle g
is continuous on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. Indeed,
\displaystyle M_{\delta}
depends only on the modulus of continuity of
\displaystyle g
, and is independent of
\displaystyle z
. Then we see that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(x,|Dv_{z,\delta}(x)|)F(D^{2}v_{z,\delta}(x))-\varepsilon v_{z,\delta}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu_{0}}{L}\min\left\{\left(\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{|x-x_{z}|^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\right)^{i(\Phi)},\left(\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{|x-x_{z}|^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\right)^{s(\Phi)}\right\}\cdot\left(-M_{\delta}\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{|x-x_{z}|^{\kappa_{0}+2}}\right)+\varepsilon\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}.

Here if we let
\displaystyle A:=\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{|x-x_{z}|^{\kappa_{0}+1}}
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\min\left\{A^{i(\Phi)},A^{s(\Phi)}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=A^{-1}\min\left\{A^{i(\Phi)+1},A^{s(\Phi)+1}\right\}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{r^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\right)^{-1}\min\left\{\left(\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{R^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\right)^{i(\Phi)+1},\left(\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{R^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\right)^{s(\Phi)+1}\right\}.

Note that we need to select
\displaystyle K
so that
\displaystyle\frac{K\kappa_{0}}{R^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\geq 1
. Since
\displaystyle 0<i(\Phi)+1\leq s(\Phi)+1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\min\left\{A^{i(\Phi)},A^{s(\Phi)}\right\}\geq\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{r^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{R^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\right)^{i(\Phi)+1}K^{i(\Phi)}.

Hence, we deduce that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(x,|Dv_{z,\delta}(x)|)

\displaystyle\displaystyle F(D^{2}v_{z,\delta}(x))-\varepsilon v_{z,\delta}(x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\nu_{0}}{L}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{r^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{R^{\kappa_{0}+1}}\right)^{i(\Phi)+1}\frac{\kappa_{0}}{R^{\kappa_{0}+2}}K^{i(\Phi)+1}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)}.

Therefore, we can choose
\displaystyle K=K(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\nu_{0},L,r,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega),\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)})
large enough so that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(x,|Dv_{z,\delta}(x)|)

\displaystyle\displaystyle F(D^{2}v_{z,\delta}(x))-\varepsilon v_{z,\delta}(x)\leq-\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\text{in $\displaystyle\Omega$},

i.e.,
\displaystyle v_{z,\delta}
is a viscosity supersolution to (
6.4
).

Finally, we define

\displaystyle\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}(x):=\inf\left\{v_{z,\delta}(x)\text{: $\displaystyle z\in\partial\Omega$ and $\displaystyle\delta\in(0,1)$}\right\}.

It is easy to check that
\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}
is a viscosity supersolution to (
6.4
) in
\displaystyle\Omega
and enjoys the boundary condition
\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}=g
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
. Moreover, it immediately follows from the construction of
\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}
that

\displaystyle\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}\leq C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\nu_{0},L,\mathrm{diam}(\Omega),\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial\Omega)})\quad\text{in $\displaystyle\Omega$},

for any
\displaystyle\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. The existence of a viscosity subsolution
\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}
and its lower bound can be shown in a similar argument. Finally, since
\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}=w_{\varepsilon}=g
on
\displaystyle\partial\Omega
,
Lemma

6.2
implies that
\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}\leq w_{\varepsilon}
in
\displaystyle\Omega
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

1.2
.

An application of Perron’s method
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
together with
Lemma

6.2
and
Lemma

6.3
yields the existence of a viscosity solution
\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}
to the approximated equation (
6.4
) with the boundary condition
\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}=g
.

We now understand
\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}
as a viscosity solution of

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rclcc}\Phi(x,|Du_{\varepsilon}|)F(D^{2}u_{\varepsilon})&=&f_{\varepsilon}(x)&\mbox{in }&\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon}(x)&=&g(x)&\mbox{on }&\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(x):=f(x)+\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}(x)
. Here note that
\displaystyle\{f_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
is uniformly bounded in
\displaystyle L^{\infty}(\Omega)
by
Lemma

6.3
. Then by applying
[
4
, Lemma 3.1]
and
Theorem

4.4
(or just by applying the stronger result
Theorem

1.1
), we have that
\displaystyle\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,1)}
is uniformly bounded in
\displaystyle C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\displaystyle\gamma\in(0,1)
. Therefore, we can extract a uniformly converging subsequence such that
\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon_{j}}\to u_{\infty}
when
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}\to 0
, and by the standard stability argument, we conclude that
\displaystyle u_{\infty}
solves (
1
).
∎

Signed graphs and inverses of their incidence matrices
Abdullah Alazemi, Milica Anđelić, Sudipta Mallik

Abstract
. The Laplacian matrix L L of a signed graph G G may or may not be invertible. We present a combinatorial formula of the Moore-Penrose inverse of L L . This is achieved by finding a combinatorial formula for the Moore-Penrose inverse of an incidence matrix of G G . This work generalizes related known results on incidence and Laplacian matrices of an unsigned graph. Several examples are provided to show the usefulness of these combinatorial formulas.

05C50, 15A09

1.
Introduction

A signed graph is a graph in which each of its edges is positive or negative. More formally, a
signed graph

\Gamma
is a pair
\Gamma=(G,\sigma)
where
G=(V,E)
is a graph, called the
underlying graph
, with vertex and edge sets
V=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and
E=\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}\}
respectively and
\sigma:E\rightarrow\{-1,1\}
is the
sign function
or
signature
. An edge
e_{\ell}
is
positive
(resp.
negative
) if
\sigma(e_{\ell})=1
(resp.
\sigma(e_{\ell})=-1
). By
E^{-}(\Gamma)
(resp.
E^{+}(\Gamma)
) we denote the set of negative (resp. positive) edges in
\Gamma
. Throughout this article, we consider only simple signed graphs, i.e., signed graphs that do not have loops and multiple edges.

A cycle
C
in
\Gamma
is
balanced
if it contains an even number of negative edges.
\Gamma
is said to be
balanced
if all its cycles are balanced, otherwise it is
unbalanced
. The adjacency matrix of
\Gamma=(G,\sigma)
is
A(\Gamma)=[a^{\sigma}_{ij}]
with
a^{\sigma}_{ij}=\sigma(ij)a_{ij}
, where
A=[a_{ij}]
is the adjacency matrix of the underlying graph
G
. The corresponding Laplacian matrix
L(\Gamma)=D(G)-A(\Gamma)
, where
D(G)
is the diagonal matrix of vertex degrees. Before we define incidence matrix of
\Gamma
in details, note that
L(\Gamma)=NN^{\intercal}
for any incidence matrix
N
of
\Gamma
.

An incidence matrix
N=[n_{ij}]
of
\Gamma
is an
n\times m
binary matrix with rows indexed by vertices and columns indexed by edges such that each of its columns has exactly two nonzero entries as follows:

(a)

when
e_{\ell}=\{i,j\}
is a negative edge,
n_{i\ell}=n_{j\ell}
and it is either
1
or
-1
, and

(b)

when
e_{\ell}=\{i,j\}
is a positive edge,
n_{i\ell}=-n_{j\ell}
and it is either
1
or
-1
.

Multiple incidence matrices can be associated with
\Gamma
. In particular, if
\Gamma=(G,\sigma)
has all negative edges, then the incidence matrix of the underlying graph
G
is an incidence matrix of
\Gamma
. Similarly, if
\Gamma=(G,\sigma)
has all positive edges, then an oriented incidence matrix of the underlying graph
G
is an incidence matrix of
\Gamma
. It is important to mention that an oriented incidence matrix
N=[n_{ij}]
of
G
gives each of its edges an orientation as follows
[
7
,
4
]
: when
n_{i\ell}=-n_{j\ell}=1
, edge
e_{\ell}=\{i,j\}
is oriented as
(i,j)
which is directed from vertex
i
to vertex
j
.

We get a fixed incidence matrix of
\Gamma=(G,\sigma)
if each of its edges has a bi-direction in which each edge has two arrowheads at the ends. We define
n_{i\ell}=\eta(i,e_{\ell})
where
\eta:V\times E\to\{+1,-1,0\}
is defined as follows:

(i)

\eta(i,e_{\ell})=0
if
i\notin e_{\ell}
,

(ii)

\eta(i,e_{\ell})=-1
(resp.
+1
) if an arrow at
i
goes into (resp. out of)
i
and
e_{\ell}=\{i,j\}
,

(iii)

\eta(i,e_{\ell})\eta(j,e_{\ell})=-\sigma(e_{\ell})
for
e_{\ell}=\{i,j\}
.

Note that the last two conditions imply that two arrows of a positive (resp. negative) edge have the same direction (resp. opposite directions). In the literature of signed graphs the signs in condition (ii) are reversed. We changed it to be consistent with the literature of oriented incidence matrix of an unsigned graph
[
7
,
4
]
. This work is a generalization of work on graphs to signed graphs and we did not want to contradict the existing results on the Moore-Penrose inverse of an oriented matrix of a graph
[
4
]
.

For
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, a matrix
X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
is called the
Moore-Penrose inverse
of
A
if

(1.1)

AXA=A,\,\,XAX=X,\,\,(AX)^{\intercal}=AX,\,\,(XA)^{\intercal}=XA,

and it is denoted by
A^{\dagger}.

In 1965, Ijiri first studied the Moore-Penrose inverse of the oriented incidence matrix of a graph in
[
12
]
. Bapat did the same for the Laplacian and oriented incidence matrices of trees
[
4
]
. Further research studied the same topic for different graphs such as distance regular graphs
[
2
,
3
]
. With the emergence of research on the signless Laplacian of graphs
[
8
,
9
]
, Hessert and Mallik studied the Moore-Penrose inverses of the incidence matrix and signless Laplacian of a tree and an unicyclic graph in
[
10
,
11
]
. Ipsen and Mallik studied wheel graphs in the same context
[
13
]
. Alazemi, Alhalabi, and Anđelić did the same for a larger graph class namely bipartite graphs
[
1
]
. Since a signed graph is a generalization of an unsigned graph, it is a natural open problem to extend the investigation regarding Moore-Penrose inverse to signed graphs. In this article we find combinatorial formula for the entries of the Moore-Penrose inverse of the Laplacian and incidence matrices of a signed graph.

The content of the present paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we deliver several preliminary results. Section
3
covers combinatorial formulas for the Moore-Penrose inverse of incidence matrices of signed trees and unbalanced unicyclic signed graphs. Section
4
extends the results of Section
3
to incidence matrices of connected signed graphs. Results related to Laplacian matrix are subject of Section
5
. Several open problems are reported in Section
6
.

2.
Preliminaries

We recall several results from
[
4
,
6
]
, essential for some initial considerations regarding the rank and minors of incidence matrices of signed graphs.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
6
, Lemma 3.4]
)

Let
N
be the incidence matrix of a connected signed graph
\Gamma
of order
n
. Then

{\rm rank}(N)=\begin{cases}n-1&\text{ if \,$\Gamma$ is balanced,}\\
n&\text{ if \,$\Gamma$ is unbalanced.}\end{cases}

For a matrix
N
,
N(i;\,)
denotes the submatrix of
N
with deleted row
i
.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
6
, Lemma 3.7]
)

Let
N
be the incidence matrix of a signed graph
\Gamma
. If
\Gamma
is a tree, then
\det(N(i;\,))=\pm 1
, and if
\Gamma
is an unbalanced unicyclic graph, then
\det(N(i;\,))=\pm 2.

For an
n\times m
real matrix
A
and a positive integer
r\leq n,m
,
{\rm vol}\,A
denotes the square root of the sum of squares of all
r\times r
minors of
A
and

A(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r}),\;1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq m
denotes the matrix obtained from
A
by replacing all the columns of
A
that are different from columns
i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r}
by zero vectors. Then the Moore-Penrose inverse of
A
can be computed in the following way.

Theorem 2.3
.

(
[
4
,
1
]
)

Let
A
be an
n\times m
real matrix of rank
r>0
. Then

(2.1)

A^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{{\rm vol}^{2}A}\sum_{(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r})\in\mathcal{M}}{\rm vol}^{2}A(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r})A(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r})^{\dagger},

where

\mathcal{M}=\{(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}\;|\;1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{r}\leq m,\;{\rm rank}(A(i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{r}))=r\}.

According to Lemma
2.1
the rank of an incidence matrix of a signed graph
\Gamma
is either
n-1
, if
\Gamma
is balanced or
n
if
\Gamma
is unbalanced. In addition, by Lemma
2.2
, maximal rank is attained through spanning trees, in case
\Gamma
is balanced or through unbalanced unicyclic graphs for
\Gamma
balanced.

A signed
TU
-subgraph
H
of
\Gamma
is a subgraph whose components are trees or unbalanced unicyclic graphs with
c(H)
denoting the number of unbalanced unicyclic components. By Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
, we have the following.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected signed graph on
n\geq 2
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
with incidence matrix
N
. Let
H
be a spanning signed subgraph of
\Gamma
with incidence matrix
N_{H}
.

(a)

If
\Gamma
is balanced and
H
is a spanning tree of
\Gamma
, then
{\rm rank}(N)={\rm rank}(N_{H})=n-1
and for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
,

\det(N_{H}(i;))=\pm 1,

and consequently
{\rm vol}^{2}(N_{H})=n
. If
H
is any other kind of spanning subgraph of
\Gamma
with
n-1
edges, then
\det(N_{H}(i;))=0

for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.

(b)

If
\Gamma
is unbalanced and
H
is a spanning
TU
-subgraph of
\Gamma
with
n
edges consisting of
c(H)
unbalanced unicyclic graphs,
then
{\rm rank}(N)={\rm rank}(N_{H})=n
and

\det(N_{H})=\pm 2^{c(H)},

and consequently
{\rm vol}^{2}(N_{H})=4^{c(H)}
.
If
H
is any other kind of spanning subgraph of
\Gamma
with
n
edges, then
\det(N_{H})=0
.

Proof.

(a) Suppose
\Gamma
is balanced and
H
is a spanning tree of
\Gamma
. By Lemmas
2.1
,
2.2
,
{\rm rank}(N)={\rm rank}(N_{H})=n-1
and for
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,
\det(N_{H}(i;))=\pm 1
. Then

{\rm vol}^{2}(N_{H})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\det(N_{H}(i;)))^{2}=n.

Now suppose
H
is a spanning subgraph of
\Gamma
with
n-1
edges that is not a spanning tree of
\Gamma
. Then
H
has more than one connected (balanced) component and consequently
{\rm rank}(N_{H}(i;))<n-1
. Thus
\det(N_{H}(i;))=0
.

(b) Suppose
\Gamma
is unbalanced and
H
is a spanning
TU
-subgraph of
\Gamma
with
n
edges consisting of
c(H)
unbalanced unicyclic graphs. By Lemma
2.1
,
{\rm rank}(N)={\rm rank}(N_{H})=n
. By Lemma
2.2
the determinant of the submatrix of
N_{H}
corresponding to each unbalanced unicyclic graph is
\pm 2
. Thus
\det(N_{H})=(\pm 2)^{c(H)}
. Then

{\rm vol}^{2}(N_{H})=(\det(N_{H}))^{2}=4^{c(H)}.

Now suppose
H
is a spanning subgraph of
\Gamma
with
n
edges whose connected components are not all unbalanced unicyclic graphs. If
H
has a component
H_{1}
such that
|E(H_{1})|<|V(H_{1})|
, then the columns of
N_{H}
corresponding to
V(H_{1})
are linearly dependent and consequently
\det(N_{H})=0
. Now suppose
H
has
t
unicyclic components
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{t}
. Since not all of
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{t}
are unbalanced, say
H_{1}
is balanced. Then the determinant of the submatrix of
N_{H}
corresponding to
H_{1}
is
0
. Thus

\det(N_{H})=\pm\prod_{i=1}^{n}\det(N_{H_{i}})=0.

∎

In what follows by
\tau(\Gamma)
we denote the number of spanning trees of
\Gamma
, while by
\mathcal{TU}_{n}(\Gamma)
we denote the set of
TU
-subgraphs of
\Gamma
on
n
edges.

Corollary 2.5
.

Let
\Gamma
be a connected signed graph on
n
vertices with an incidence matrix
N
and Laplacian matrix
L
.

(a)

If
\Gamma
is balanced, then
\det(L)=0
and
{\rm vol}^{2}(N)=n\tau(\Gamma)
.

(b)

If
\Gamma
is unbalanced, then

\det(L)={\rm vol}^{2}(N)=\sum_{H\in\mathcal{TU}_{n}(\Gamma)}4^{c(H)}.

Moreover,
\frac{\det(L)}{4}\geq
the number of unbalanced unicyclic graphs in
G
and the equality holds if and only if
G
is balanced or an unbalanced unicyclic graph.

In order to implement (
2.1
) to compute
N^{\dagger}
as main ingredients we need formulas for the Moore-Penrose inverse of the incidence matrix of a tree as well of an unbalanced unicyclic graph.
These are covered in subsequent sections.

3.
Signed trees and unbalanced unicyclic signed graphs

We first introduce necessary notation. For a given signed tree
T
on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
, we introduce the
head
and
tail components
in
T\setminus e
for any edge
e
.

•

If
e_{i}=\{l_{i},m_{i}\}
such that
l_{i}<m_{i}
is a negative edge, then the
head component
of
T\setminus e_{i}
, denoted by
T_{h}(e_{i})
, is the connected component of
T\setminus e_{i}
containing the vertex
m_{i}
. The
tail component
of
T\setminus e_{i}
, denoted by
T_{t}(e_{i})
, is the connected component of
T\setminus e_{i}
containing the vertex
l_{i}
.

•

If
e_{i}=(l_{i},m_{i})
is a positive edge, directed from
l_{i}
to
m_{i}
, then the
head component
of
T\setminus e_{i}
is the component containing
m_{i}
, while the
tail component
contains
l_{i}
.

We recall that
|T_{h}(e_{i})|+|T_{t}(e_{i})|=n
, while according to
[
10
, Lemma 2.4]

\sum_{e_{p}\in E_{h}(i)}|T_{t}(e_{p})|+\sum_{e_{q}\in E_{T}(i)}|T_{h}(e_{q})|=n-1,

where

E_{h}(i)=\{e_{k}\in E(G):e_{k}\text{ is incident at }i\text{ and }i\in T_{h}(e_{k})\},

E_{t}(i)=\{e_{k}\in E(G):e_{k}\text{ is incident at }i\text{ and }i\in T_{t}(e_{k})\}.

The shortest path between a vertex
j
and an edge
e_{i}
, that does not contain
e_{i}
is denoted by
P_{e_{i}-j}
. The product of
\sigma(e_{k})
, for
e_{k}
being on the path
P_{e_{i}-j}
is denoted by
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-j})
.

For a connected balanced graph
\Gamma
on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
, we define a new
n\times n
matrix
S=[s_{ij}]
, we call the
path sign matrix
of
\Gamma
,
s_{ii}=1
for all
i
and
s_{ij}=1
(
-1
) if there is a positive (resp. negative) path between distinct vertices
i
and
j
. The corresponding sign we denote by
{\rm sgn}(P_{i-j})
. The role of
S
in computation of Moore-Penrose inverse of balanced graphs is justified in following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(G,\sigma)
be a connected signed graph on
n\geq 2
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
with an incidence matrix
N
.

(a)

If
\Gamma
is unbalanced, then
NN^{\dagger}=I_{n}
.

(b)

If
\Gamma
is balanced, then

NN^{\dagger}=I_{n}-\frac{1}{n}S,

where
S
is the path sign matrix of
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Since
NN^{\dagger}N=N
,
(I_{n}-NN^{\dagger})N=O_{n,m}
. Then each row of
I_{n}-NN^{\dagger}
is orthogonal to each column of
N
. Suppose
x^{T}=[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
is orthogonal to each column of
N
. Then
x_{i}-x_{j}=0
for any positive edge
\{i,j\}
and
x_{i}+x_{j}=0
for any negative edge
\{i,j\}
in
\Gamma
. Therefore, if there is a path
P_{i-j}
between
i
and
j
, then
x_{i}={\rm sgn}(P_{i-j})x_{j}
.

(a) If
\Gamma
is unbalanced, then by Lemma
2.1
,
{\rm rank}\,N=n
and
N
is invertible, hence
N^{\dagger}=N^{-1}
.

(b) Suppose
\Gamma
is balanced. Then
{\rm rank}\,N=n-1
and all paths between two distinct vertices in
G
have the same sign. For a fixed vertex
i
of
G
,
x_{v}={\rm sgn}(P_{i-v})x_{i}
for any vertex
v
of
G
. Thus

[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]=x_{i}[{\rm sgn}(P_{i-1}),{\rm sgn}(P_{i-2}),\ldots,{\rm sgn}(P_{i-n})].

Consequently any row of
I_{n}-NN^{\dagger}
is a multiple of
[{\rm sgn}(P_{i-1}),{\rm sgn}(P_{i-2}),\ldots,{\rm sgn}(P_{i-n})]
.
Suppose for each
i=1,2,\ldots,n
,
i
th row of
I_{n}-NN^{\dagger}
is

c_{i}[{\rm sgn}(P_{i-1}),{\rm sgn}(P_{i-2}),\ldots,{\rm sgn}(P_{i-n})].

Note that if
c_{1}=c_{2}=\cdots=c_{n}=0
, then
NN^{\dagger}=I_{n}
and consequently
n={\rm rank}(NN^{\dagger})\leq{\rm rank}(N)=n-1
, a contradiction. Thus
c_{i}\neq 0
for some
i
and by symmetry of
NN^{\dagger}
,
c_{i}\neq 0
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,n
. Now observe that
(I_{n}-NN^{\dagger})^{2}=I_{n}-NN^{\dagger}
because
N^{\dagger}NN^{\dagger}=N^{\dagger}
. Then the
(i,i)
-entry of
(I_{n}-NN^{\dagger})^{2}
, that is
c_{i}^{2}n
, is same as the
(i,i)
-entry of
I_{n}-NN^{\dagger}
, which is
c_{i}{\rm sgn}(P_{i-i})=c_{i}
. Thus
c_{i}=\frac{1}{n}
and the
(i,j)
-entry of
I_{n}-NN^{\dagger}
is
\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{i-j})}{n}
.
∎

We next give combinatorial expression for the Moore-Penrose inverse of the incidence matrix of a tree.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\Gamma=(T,\sigma)
be a signed tree on
n\geq 2
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
and
n-1
edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{n-1}
. Let
N
be an incidence matrix of
\Gamma
. Then the Moore-Penrose inverse
N^{\dagger}=[n_{i,j}^{\dagger}]
of
N
is given by

(3.1)

n_{i,j}^{\dagger}=\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-j})w(e_{i})}{n}\begin{cases}|T_{h}(e_{i})|&\text{ if }j\in T_{t}(e_{i})\\
-\sigma(e_{i})|T_{t}(e_{i})|&\text{ if }j\in T_{h}(e_{i}),\end{cases}

where

w(e_{i})=\begin{cases}-1&\text{if $e_{i}$ is negative with both negative ends}\\
\,\,\,\,1&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

We first consider the case, when every negative edge has both positive ends.
Let
H=[h_{i,j}]
be the
(n-1)\times n
matrix given by (
3.1
). Then,

(3.2)

h_{i,j}=\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-j})}{n}\begin{cases}|T_{h}(e_{i})|&\text{ if }j\in T_{t}(e_{i})\\
|T_{t}(e_{i})|&\text{ if }j\in T_{h}(e_{i}),\;e_{i}\in E^{-}(\Gamma)\\
-|T_{t}(e_{i})|&\text{ if }j\in T_{h}(e_{i}),\;e_{i}\in E^{+}(\Gamma).\end{cases}

We show that
H=N^{\dagger}
, by proving
HN=I_{n-1}
.

For
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, suppose
e_{i}=\{l,m\}
where
l<m
and
e_{j}=\{r,s\}
. For
N=[n_{i,j}]
, the
(i,j)
-entry of
HN
is given by

(HN)_{i,j}=\sum^{n}_{k=1}h_{i,k}n_{k,j}=\begin{cases}h_{i,r}+h_{i,s}&\text{ if }e_{j}\in E^{-}(\Gamma)\\
h_{i,r}-h_{i,s}&\text{ if }e_{j}=(r,s)\in E^{+}(\Gamma)\\
-h_{i,r}+h_{i,s}&\text{ if }e_{j}=(s,r)\in E^{+}(\Gamma).\end{cases}

The values of
(HN)_{i,j}
are computed through the following cases and subcases.

Case 1.

i=j
.

First note that
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-l})={\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-m})=1
(being the empty product).

Let
e_{i}\in E^{-}(\Gamma)
. Since
l<m
,
l\in T_{t}(e_{i})
and
m\in T_{h}(e_{i})
. Then

(HN)_{i,i}=\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-l})}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|+\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-m})}{n}|T_{t}(e_{i})|=\frac{1}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|+\frac{1}{n}|T_{t}(e_{i})|=\frac{1}{n}n=1.

Let
e_{i}\in E^{+}(\Gamma)
. Since
e_{j}=(l,m)
, then

l\in T_{t}(e_{i})
,
m\in T_{h}(e_{i})
, and

(HN)_{i,i}=\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-l})}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|-\frac{-{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-m})}{n}|T_{t}(e_{i})|=\frac{1}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|+\frac{1}{n}|T_{t}(e_{i})|=\frac{1}{n}n=1.

If
e_{i}=(m,l)
, then

m\in T_{t}(e_{i})
,
l\in T_{h}(e_{i})
, and

(HN)_{i,i}=-\frac{-{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-l})}{n}|T_{t}(e_{i})|+\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-m})}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|=\frac{1}{n}|T_{t}(e_{i})|+\frac{1}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|=\frac{1}{n}n=1.

Case 2.

i\neq j
.

Since
e_{i}\not=e_{j}=\{r,s\}
and
T
is a tree, either both
r,s\in T_{h}(e_{i})
or both
r,s\in T_{t}(e_{i})
.

Subcase (a)

r,s\in T_{t}(e_{i})
.

Either
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-r})={\rm sgn}(P_{l-r})={\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-s})\sigma(e_{j})
or
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-s})={\rm sgn}(P_{l-s})={\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-r})\sigma(e_{j})
.

If
e_{j}\in E^{-}(\Gamma)
, then
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-r})
and
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-s})
have the opposite signs and

(HN)_{i,j}=\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-r})}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|+\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-s})}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|=0.

If
e_{j}\in E^{+}(\Gamma)
, then
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-r})
and
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-s})
have the same sign and

(HN)_{i,j}=\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-r})}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|-\frac{{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-s})}{n}|T_{h}(e_{i})|=0.

Subcase (b)

r,s\in T_{h}(e_{i})
.

Either
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-r})={\rm sgn}(P_{m-r})={\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-s})\sigma(e_{j})
or
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-s})={\rm sgn}(P_{m-s})={\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-r})\sigma(e_{j})
.

If
e_{j}\in E^{-}(\Gamma)
, then

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Gamma=(G,\sigma)
be a connected balanced unicyclic graph on
n
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
with incidence matrix
N
, the unique cycle
C
with
E(C)=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{|C|}\}
,
T_{k}=G\setminus e_{k}
,
1\leq k\leq|C|
and
w(e_{i})
being
-1
for negative edges with both negative ends and
1
, otherwise. Then the following holds.

(a)

If

e_{i}\notin C
and
j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C]
, then either
j\in T_{h}^{T_{k}}(e_{i})
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,|C|\}
or
j\in T_{t}^{T_{k}}(e_{i})
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,|C|\}
.

(b)

(N^{\dagger})_{ij}=\frac{w(e_{i}){\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-j})}{n|C|}\cdot\begin{cases}|C||G\setminus e_{i}(C)|\,\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,e_{i}\notin E(C),j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C],j\in T_{t}^{T_{k}}(e_{i})\mbox{ for some $T_{k}$,}\\
-{\rm sgn}(e_{i})|C||G\setminus e_{i}[C]|\,\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,e_{i}\notin E(C),j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C],j\in T_{h}^{T_{k}}(e_{i})\mbox{ for some $T_{k}$,}\\
\sum_{k=1\atop e_{i}\in T_{k}}^{|C|}\phi_{T_{k}}(e_{i},j)\,\,\,\mbox{if}\,\,\,e_{i}\in E(C),j\in V(G),\end{cases}

where

\phi_{T}(e_{i},j)=\begin{cases}|T_{h}(e_{i})|\mbox{ if }j\in T_{t}(e_{i})\\
-\sigma(e_{i})|T_{t}(e_{i})|\mbox{ if }j\in T_{h}(e_{i}).\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
\Gamma
is connected balanced unicyclic graph, the number of spanning trees is
\tau(G)=|C|
and the spanning trees are
T_{k},1\leq k\leq|C|
. Let
e_{i}\notin C,
and
j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C]
. Then for all
k
,
T_{k}\setminus e_{i}
contains
j
either in the tail or in the head. In addition,
|T^{T_{k}}_{t}(e_{i})|=|G\setminus e_{i}(C)|,
while
|T^{T_{k}}_{h}(e_{i})|=|G\setminus e_{i}[C)].

By Theorem
4.1
(a)

(N^{\dagger})_{ij}=\frac{1}{|C|}\sum_{k=1}^{|C|}(N^{\dagger}_{T_{k}})_{ij},

where

(N^{\dagger}_{T_{k}})_{ij}=\frac{w(e_{i}){\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-j})}{n}\begin{cases}|G\setminus e_{i}(C)|&\mbox{ if $e_{i}\notin E(C),j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C],j\in T_{t}^{T_{k}}(e_{i})$ }\\
|G\setminus e_{i}[C]|&\mbox{ if $e_{i}\notin E(C),j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C],j\in T_{h}^{T_{k}}(e_{i})$ }\\
|T^{T_{k}}_{h}(e_{i})|&\mbox{ if $e_{i}\in C,j\in T^{T_{k}}_{t}(e_{i})$ }\\
-\sigma(e_{i})|T^{T_{k}}_{t}(e_{i})|&\mbox{ if $e_{i}\in C,j\in T^{T_{k}}_{h}(e_{i})$. }\end{cases}

Hence,

\sum_{k=1}^{|C|}(N^{\dagger}_{T_{k}})_{ij}=\frac{w(e_{i}){\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-j})}{n}\begin{cases}|C||G\setminus e_{i}(C)|&\mbox{ if $e_{i}\notin E(C),j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C],j\in T_{t}^{T_{k}}(e_{i})$ }\\
|C||G\setminus e_{i}[C]|&\mbox{ if $e_{i}\notin E(C),j\in G\setminus e_{i}[C],j\in T_{h}^{T_{k}}(e_{i})$ }\\
\sum_{k=1\atop e_{i}\in T_{k}}^{|C|}\phi_{T_{k}}(e_{i},j)&\mbox{ if}\,\,\,e_{i}\in E(C),j\in V(G).\end{cases}

Finally, devision by
|C|
in the previous equality leads to
(N^{\dagger})_{ij}
, i.e., to the expression given in (b).
∎

5.
Moore-Penrose inverse of the Laplacian matrix of a signed graph

In this final section we consider the Moore-Penrose inverse of the Laplacian matrix of a connected signed graph.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(G,\sigma)
be a connected signed graph on
n\geq 2
vertices
1,2,\ldots,n
and
m
edges
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}
with the Laplacian matrix
L
.

(a)

If
\Gamma
is balanced, then

(5.1)

(L^{\dagger})_{ij}=\frac{1}{n^{2}\tau(\Gamma)^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T_{r},T_{s}\in\mathcal{S}(\Gamma)\\
e_{k}\in T_{r},T_{s}\end{subarray}}{\rm sgn}(P_{T_{r}}(e_{k}-i)){\rm sgn}(P_{T_{s}}(e_{k}-j))\Psi(e_{k},i,j),

where

(5.2)

\Psi(e_{k},i,j)=\begin{cases}|T^{T_{r}}_{h}(e_{k})||T^{T_{s}}_{h}(e_{k})|&\text{ if }i\in T^{T_{r}}_{t}(e_{k})\text{ and }j\in T^{T_{s}}_{t}(e_{k})\\
|T^{T_{r}}_{t}(e_{k})||T^{T_{s}}_{t}(e_{k})|&\text{ if }i\in T^{T_{r}}_{h}(e_{k})\text{ and }j\in T^{T_{s}}_{h}(e_{k})\\
-{\rm sgn}(e_{k})|T^{T_{r}}_{h}(e_{k})||T^{T_{s}}_{t}(e_{k})|&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

(b)

If
\Gamma
is unbalanced, then

(5.3)

(L^{-1})_{ij}=\frac{1}{(\sum_{H\in\mathcal{TU}_{n}(\Gamma)}4^{c(H)})^{2}}\sum_{k=1}^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathcal{TU}_{n}(\Gamma)\\
e_{k}\in H\end{subarray}}4^{c(H)}(N_{H}^{+})_{ki}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}H\in\mathcal{TU}_{n}(\Gamma)\\
e_{k}\in H\end{subarray}}4^{c(H)}(N_{H}^{+})_{kj},

where
\mathcal{TU}_{n}(\Gamma)
is the set of all spanning
TU
-subgraphs
H
of
\Gamma
with
n
edges consisting of
c(H)
unbalanced unicyclic graphs
U^{H}_{1},U^{H}_{2},\ldots,U^{H}_{c(H)}
with cycles
C^{H}_{1},C^{H}_{2},\ldots,C^{H}_{c(H)}
respectively and when
e_{i}\in U^{H}_{r}
for some
r
, then

(5.4)

(N_{H}^{\dagger})_{ij}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}{\rm sgn}\left(P_{e_{i}^{t}-j}\right)&\text{ if }e_{i}\in C^{H}_{r}\\
0&\text{ if }e_{i}\not\in C_{r}\text{ and }j\in U^{H}_{r}\setminus e_{i}[C^{H}_{r}]\\
-{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-j})&\text{ if }e_{i}\in E^{+}(U^{H}_{r})\setminus E(C^{H}_{r})\text{ and }j\not\in U^{H}_{r}\setminus e_{i}[C^{H}_{r}]\text{ and }j\in(U^{H}_{r})_{h}(e_{i})\\
{\rm sgn}(P_{e_{i}-j})&\text{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

where
P_{e_{i}^{t}-j}
denotes the path between the tail of
e_{i}\in C^{H}_{r}
and
j
in the tree
U_{r}^{H}\setminus e_{i}
.

Proof.

The expressions follow from

L=NN^{\intercal}\implies L^{\dagger}=(N^{\dagger})^{\intercal}N^{\dagger}.

(a) For
N^{\dagger}=[n_{ij}]
,

(L^{\dagger})_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}n_{ki}n_{kj}=\frac{1}{n\tau(\Gamma)}\sum_{T\in S(\Gamma)\atop e_{k}\in T}({N_{T}^{\dagger}})_{ki}\frac{1}{n\tau(\Gamma)}\sum_{T\in S(\Gamma)\atop e_{k}\in T}({N_{T}^{\dagger}})_{kj}=\frac{1}{n^{2}\tau(\Gamma)^{2}}\sum_{T_{t},T_{s}\in S(\Gamma)\atop e_{k}\in T_{r},T_{s}}({N_{T}^{\dagger}})_{ki}({N_{T}^{\dagger}})_{kj}

Taking into account the results on
N_{T}^{\dagger}
given in Theorem
3.2
we arrive to

({N_{T}^{\dagger}})_{ki}({N_{T}^{\dagger}})_{kj}={\rm sgn}(P_{T_{r}}(e_{k}-i)){\rm sgn}(P_{T_{s}}(e_{k}-j))\Psi(e_{k},i,j),

as desired.

The proof of (b) follows in a similar way.
∎

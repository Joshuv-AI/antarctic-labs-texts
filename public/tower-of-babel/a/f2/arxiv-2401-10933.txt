On the class of almost subadditive weight functions Thanks: This research was funded in whole by the Austrian Science Fund (FWF) project 10.55776/P33417
Gerhard Schindl

Abstract
. We answer a question from A. V. Abanin and P. T. Tien about so-called almost subadditive weight functions in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor. Using recent knowledge of a growth index for functions, crucially appearing in the ultraholomorphic setting, we are able to show the existence of weights such that there does not exist an equivalent almost subadditive function and hence almost subadditive weights are a proper subclass.

1.
Introduction

In
[
1
]
it has been shown that
almost subadditive weight functions
are sufficient to describe the theory of ultradistributions in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor; see
[
4
]
. Thus the authors are dealing with non-quasianalytic weight functions
\omega
; see Section
2.2
for precise definitions and conditions. All relevant information about growth and regularity properties for weight functions and weight sequences can be found in Section
2
.

In their closing discussion in
[
1
, Rem. 1.5]
the authors have asked the following:

Let
\omega
be a given non-quasianalytic weight function (in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor), does there always exist a weight
\sigma
such that

(*)

\sigma
is
equivalent
to
\omega
which means that
0<\liminf_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{\sigma(t)}\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{\sigma(t)}<+\infty
, and such that

(*)

\sigma
is
almost subadditive:

\forall\;q>1\;\exists\;C_{q}\geq 1\;\forall\;s,t\geq 0:\;\;\;\sigma(s+t)\leq q(\sigma(s)+\sigma(t))+C_{q}.

The notion of
almost subadditivity
is more recent and more general than the classical (standard) assumption
subadditivity
appearing in the Beurling-Björck framework; see e.g.
[
2
]
. On the other hand we mention that in the literature different ”subadditivity-like” conditions for (associated) weight functions have been studied and used; see e.g.
[
17
]
and the citations there.

The aim of this article is to answer this question and to prove that in general there does not exist an equivalent almost subadditive weight. This fact shows that the class of almost subadditive weight functions is a proper subclass and hence the main result
[
1
, Thm. 1.4]
means that this particular subclass of weight functions is sufficient to describe the theory of ultradistributions in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor. Our statements can be found in Section
3
. In this work, since we are mainly focusing on functions and their growth properties, it is not required to introduce the associated ultradifferentiable function spaces explicitly; we refer to
[
4
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
13
]
.

In order to treat this problem, crucially we involve recent knowledge about the growth index
\gamma(\omega)
from
[
6
]
; see Section
2.3
for precise definitions and explanations. This value becomes relevant when proving extension results in the ultraholomorphic setting; see
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
. We establish a connection between the notion of almost subadditivity and
\gamma(\omega)
(see Lemma
3.1
and Remark
3.2
). Involving this information, intrinsic properties of
\gamma(\omega)
, in particular using the fact that this growth index is preserved under equivalence of weights, we show the main result Theorem
3.3
.

Note that using this method we are able to give more information: On the one hand, we are working within a more general setting of weight functions; see Definition
2.1
. Thus our basic assumptions on
\omega
are weaker than being a weight in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor, but also weaker than the standard requirements assumed in
[
2
]
and even slightly weaker than in
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
. This is possible by the general setting studied in
[
6
, Sect. 2.3 & 2.4]
. On the other hand, instead of almost subadditivity, we treat a more general condition (see (
3.1
)) and obtain that the above problem is not depending on the notion of (non-)quasianalyticity. So the main result is valid for quasianalytic weights as well; see Remark
3.5
for more detailed comments.

However, Theorem
3.3
provides abstract information and the aim in Theorem
3.6
and Theorem
3.7
is to construct explicitly weights with the desired property. There we are focusing on so-called
associated weight functions

\omega_{M}
with
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
being a
weight sequence;
see Section
2.4
. The advantage in this case is that the desired growth properties for
\omega_{M}
can be expressed in terms of
M
and so one is able to proceed by constructing an appropriate weight sequence
M
. Such explicit constructions of (counter-)examples can be useful for further applications in different contexts. Here, the growth index
\gamma(M)
originally introduced by V. Thilliez in
[
19
]
is becoming relevant; see again
[
6
]
for more details and its relation to
\gamma(\omega_{M})
.

2.
Weights, growth conditions and the index
\gamma(\omega)

2.1.
General notation

We use the notation
\mathbb{R}_{>0}:=(0,+\infty)
,
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
and
\mathbb{N}_{>0}:=\{1,2,\dots\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is called a
weight function
if

(*)

\omega
is non-decreasing and

(*)

\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
.

These are the basic assumptions resp. the notion for being a weight which is used in
[
6
, Sect. 2.3 & 2.4]
. These requirements are sufficient to introduce and work with the crucial index
\gamma(\omega)
; see Section
2.3
for details.

2.2.
Relevant growth conditions

We list now conditions for weight functions
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
; they are known and standard in the ultradifferentiable setting involving weight functions in the sense of
Braun-Meise-Taylor;
see
[
4
]
. We use the abbreviations appearing in
[
15
]
.

(\omega_{1})

\omega(2t)=O(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(\omega_{2})

\omega(t)=O(t)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(\omega_{3})

\log(t)=o(\omega(t))
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(\omega_{4})

\varphi_{\omega}:t\mapsto\omega(e^{t})
is a convex function (on
\mathbb{R}
).

(\omega_{5})

\omega(t)=o(t)
as
t\rightarrow+\infty
.

(\omega_{\text{nq}})

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{t^{2}}dt<+\infty.

(\omega_{\text{snq}})

\exists\;C>0\;\forall\;y>0:\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\omega(yt)}{t^{2}}dt\leq C\omega(y)+C
.

In the literature, when being defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, usually
\omega
is radially extended, i.e.
\omega(x):=\omega(|x|)
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

We call a weight function
\omega

normalized
if
\omega(t)=0
for all
0\leq t\leq 1
,
non-quasianalytic
if
(\omega_{\operatorname{nq}})
is valid and
quasianalytic
if
(\omega_{\operatorname{nq}})
is violated.

Definition 2.2
.

A function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is called a BMT-weight function (BMT standing for Braun-Meise-Taylor), if
\omega
is continuous, non-decreasing,
\omega(0)=0
, and such that
\omega
satisfies
(\omega_{1})
,
(\omega_{3})
and
(\omega_{4})
.

If
\omega
is in addition normalized, then it is called a normalized BMT-weight function; if in addition
(\omega_{\operatorname{nq}})
is valid, then
\omega
is called a non-quasianalytic BMT-weight function and it is called quasianalytic, if
(\omega_{\operatorname{nq}})
fails.

In
[
1
]
it has been assumed that
\omega
is continuous and non-decreasing. Moreover, the following abbreviations have been used:
(\alpha)
is
(\omega_{1})
,
(\beta)
is
(\omega_{\operatorname{nq}})
,
(\gamma)
denotes
(\omega_{3})
and
(\delta)
is
(\omega_{4})
. Summarizing, in
[
1
]
non-quasianalytic BMT-weight functions (not necessarily satisfying
\omega(0)=0
, but this failure is not effecting the results) have been considered and this set is denoted by
\Omega
there. Note that in the proof of the main technical statement
[
1
, Prop. 1.3]
non-quasianalyticity has been applied crucially.

Let
\sigma,\tau:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
be arbitrary, then we write
\sigma\hypertarget{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau
if

\tau(t)=O(\sigma(t))\;\text{as}\;t\rightarrow+\infty,

and
\sigma
and
\tau
are called
equivalent,
written
\sigma\hypertarget{sim}{\sim}\tau
, if

\sigma\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\tau\;\text{and}\;\tau\hyperlink{ompreceq}{\preceq}\sigma.

This relation is crucial in the ultradifferentiable weight function setting (in the sense of Braun-Meise-Taylor), see
[
13
, Lemma 5.16, Cor. 5.17]
, and it is precisely the same notion of equivalence used in
[
1
]
; see
[
1
, Sect. 1, p. 297 & Rem. 1.5]
. Moreover, it also has been considered in
[
6
]
; see
[
6
, Sect. 2.1 & Rem. 2.6
(5)
]
. It is immediate that all properties listed above, except the convexity condition
(\omega_{4})
, are preserved under equivalence and that normalization can be assumed w.l.o.g. by switching to an equivalent weight.

According to
[
1
, Def. 1.1]
we say that a weight function
\omega:[0,+\infty)\rightarrow[0,+\infty)
is
almost subadditive
if

(2.1)

\forall\;q>1\;\exists\;C_{q}\geq 1\;\forall\;s,t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(s+t)\leq q(\omega(s)+\omega(t))+C_{q}.

This should be compared with the more original condition
subadditivity
in the Beurling-Björck framework (see
[
2
]
):

(\omega_{\text{sub}})

\omega(s+t)\leq\omega(s)+\omega(t)
for all
s,t\geq 0
.

Obviously
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
implies (
2.1
) with the uniform choice
C_{q}=0
for all
q>1
.
Note:
If the inequality in (
2.1
) is valid for some
q_{0}>1
, then for all
q\geq q_{0}
as well when choosing
C_{q}=C_{q_{0}}
. So, in order to verify almost subadditivity, one has to study
q\rightarrow 1
. Note that by definition neither
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
nor (
2.1
) is preserved under equivalence of weight functions in general.

2.3.
The growth index
\gamma(\omega)

We briefly recall the definition of the growth index
\gamma(\omega)
and some consequences; see
[
6
, Sect. 2.3]
and
[
7
, Sect. 4.2]
and the references therein. For given
\gamma>0
we say that a weight function
\omega
has property
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
if

\exists\;K>1:\;\;\;\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(K^{\gamma}t)}{\omega(t)}<K.

If
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
holds for some
K>1
, then
(P_{\omega,\gamma^{\prime}})
is satisfied for all
\gamma^{\prime}\leq\gamma
with the same
K
since
\omega
is non-decreasing. Moreover we can restrict to
\gamma>0
, because for
\gamma\leq 0
condition
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
is satisfied for all
\omega
(again since
\omega
is non-decreasing and
K>1
). Then we put

\gamma(\omega):=\sup\{\gamma>0:(P_{\omega,\gamma})\;\;\text{is satisfied}\},

and if none condition
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
holds then set
\gamma(\omega):=0
. We recall now some connections between
\gamma(\omega)
and subadditivity-like conditions for
\omega
.

Remark 2.3
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function.

(i)

By
[
6
, Cor. 2.14]
(see also
[
7
, Lemma 4.2]
) we get
\gamma(\omega)>0
if and only if
(\omega_{1})
holds. In particular, for each BMT-weight function
\omega
we have
\gamma(\omega)>0
.

(ii)

By
[
6
, Thm. 2.11, Cor. 2.13]
(see also
[
7
, Lemma 4.3]
) we have that
\gamma(\omega)>1
if and only if
\omega
has
(\omega_{\operatorname{snq}})
.

(iii)

Moreover, by (the proof of)
[
6
, Thm. 2.11, Cor. 2.13]
we know that
(\omega_{\operatorname{snq}})
is equivalent to the fact that
\omega\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\kappa_{\omega}
, where

(2.2)

\kappa_{\omega}(y):=\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\omega(yt)}{t^{2}}dt.

If
\omega
is also continuous, then by (the proof of)
[
12
, Prop. 1.3]
we have that

\kappa_{\omega}
is concave, continuous and
\kappa_{\omega}(0)=\omega(0)\geq 0
; i.e.
\kappa_{\omega}(0)=0
if and only if
\omega(0)=0
. Hence it follows that also
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
holds for
\kappa_{\omega}
; see e.g.
[
15
, Lemma 3.8.1
(1)
]
.

(If continuity for
\omega
fails, then the proof of
[
6
, Thm. 2.11
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
]
yields that we get the same properties for
y\mapsto\widetilde{\kappa}_{\omega}(y):=\int_{1}^{+\infty}\frac{\kappa_{\omega}(yt)}{t^{2}}dt
, and so
\omega
in (
2.2
) is replaced by
\kappa_{\omega}
. Consequently, in any case one has that
\omega
is equivalent to a weight satisfying
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
.)

(iv)

By definition of
\gamma(\omega)
we immediately get (see e.g.
[
7
,
(4.3)
]
)

(2.3)

\forall\;s>0:\;\;\;\gamma(\omega^{1/s})=s\gamma(\omega),

with
\omega^{1/s}(t):=\omega(t^{1/s})
,
t\geq 0
and
s>0
. Note that
\omega^{1/s}
is again a weight function; if
\omega
is a (normalized) BMT-weight function, then each
\omega^{1/s}
, too.

(v)

The value of this growth index is preserved under equivalence of weight functions; i.e. if
\sigma,\tau
are weights such that
\sigma\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\tau
, then
\gamma(\sigma)=\gamma(\tau)
: This follows from the characterization
[
6
, Thm. 2.11]
, see also
[
6
, Rem. 2.12]
.

Next we summarize some relations between
\gamma(\omega)
and the notion of (non-)quasianalyticity.

Remark 2.4
.

We have the following:

(i)

Recall that by
[
6
, Lemma 2.10, Rem. 2.15
(i)\Rightarrow(iv)
]
we know that each quasianalytic weight function
\omega
has to satisfy
1\leq\frac{1}{\gamma(\omega)}(=:\alpha(\omega))
; i.e.
\gamma(\omega)\leq 1
.

(ii)

Any non-quasianalytic weight function
\omega
satisfies
(\omega_{5})
(and hence
(\omega_{2})
) since
\int_{a}^{+\infty}\frac{\omega(t)}{t^{2}}dt\geq\omega(a)\int_{a}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{2}}dt=\frac{\omega(a)}{a}
for all
a\geq 1
.

2.4.
Weight sequences and associated weight functions

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
be given and consider the corresponding sequence of quotients
\mu=(\mu_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
by
\mu_{j}:=\frac{M_{j}}{M_{j-1}}
,
j\geq 1
,
\mu_{0}:=1
.
M
is called
normalized
if
1=M_{0}\leq M_{1}
and
M
is called
log-convex
if

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;M_{j}^{2}\leq M_{j-1}M_{j+1},

equivalently if
(\mu_{j})_{j}
is non-decreasing. Consider the following set of sequences

\hypertarget{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}:=\{M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}:\;M\;\text{is normalized, log-convex},\;\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}(M_{j})^{1/j}=+\infty\}.

We see that
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
if and only if
1=\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{1}\leq\dots
,
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{j}=+\infty
(see e.g.
[
13
, p. 104]
) and there is a one-to-one correspondence between
M
and
\mu=(\mu_{j})_{j}
by taking
M_{j}:=\prod_{k=0}^{j}\mu_{k}
.

M
has condition
moderate growth,
denoted by
(\text{mg})
, if

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;j,k\in\mathbb{N}:\;M_{j+k}\leq C^{j+k+1}M_{j}M_{k}.

In
[
9
]
this is denoted by
(M.2)
and also known under the name
stability under ultradifferential operators.

M
is called
non-quasianalytic,
denoted by
(\text{nq})
and by condition
(M.3)^{\prime}
in
[
9
]
, if

\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{\mu_{j}}<+\infty.

We call two sequences
M,N\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}

equivalent
if

0<\inf_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{j}}{N_{j}}\right)^{1/j}\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}\left(\frac{M_{j}}{N_{j}}\right)^{1/j}<+\infty.

Let
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
(with
M_{0}=1
), then the
associated (weight) function

\omega_{M}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is defined by

\omega_{M}(t):=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\log\left(\frac{|t|^{j}}{M_{j}}\right)\;\;\;\text{for}\;t\in\mathbb{R},\;t\neq 0,\hskip 30.0pt\omega_{M}(0):=0.

For an abstract introduction of the associated function we refer to
[
10
, Chapitre I]
, see also
[
9
, Def. 3.1]
and the more recent work
[
18
]
.

3.
Main results

We formulate and prove now the main results of this article. In order to proceed we crucially involve the information about the growth index
\gamma(\omega)
. Section
3.1
deals with an abstract statement and in Sections
3.2
and
3.3
we are investigating in more detail the (non-)quasianalytic weight sequence case.

3.1.
General result for weight functions

The next technical and crucial result illustrates the intimate connection between the notion of almost subadditivity and the value of
\gamma(\omega)
. Indeed, in this context we study a more general growth relation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\omega
be a weight function.

(i)

Assume that
\omega
satisfies

(3.1)

\exists\;q_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}\;\forall\;q>q_{0}\;\exists\;C_{q}\geq 1\;\forall\;s,t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(s+t)\leq q(\omega(s)+\omega(t))+C_{q}.

If
q_{0}>\frac{1}{2}
, then
\gamma(\omega)\geq\frac{\log(2)}{\log(2)+\log(q_{0})}=\frac{1}{1+\log(q_{0})/\log(2)}>0
and if
q_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
, then
\gamma(\omega)=+\infty
.

(ii)

If
\omega
is almost subadditive, i.e. if (
2.1
) holds, then
\gamma(\omega)\geq 1
.

(iii)

Each almost subadditive and quasianalytic weight function
\omega
has to satisfy
\gamma(\omega)=1
.

Concerning
(i)
note that
\frac{1}{1+\log(q_{0})/\log(2)}>0
because
1+\log(q_{0})/\log(2)>0\Leftrightarrow\log(q_{0})>-\log(2)\Leftrightarrow q_{0}>\frac{1}{2}
. Thus, in particular, (
3.1
) yields
\gamma(\omega)>0
and hence
(\omega_{1})
; see
(i)
in Remark
2.3
but also the proof below.

Proof.

(i)
First, (
3.1
) yields

\exists\;q_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}\;\forall\;q>q_{0}\;\exists\;C_{q}\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(2t)\leq 2q\omega(t)+C_{q},

which precisely means

(3.2)

\exists\;q_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}\;\forall\;q>q_{0}:\;\;\;\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(2t)}{\omega(t)}\leq 2q;

in particular
(\omega_{1})
. Assume
q_{0}>\frac{1}{2}
. Let
\epsilon>0
be small and set
\gamma:=\frac{\log(2)}{\log(2q_{0}+\epsilon)}
,
K:=2q_{0}+\epsilon>1
and so
\gamma=\frac{\log(2)}{\log(K)}
, equivalently
2=K^{\gamma}
. Then, when taking
q
with
q_{0}<q<q_{0}+\frac{\epsilon}{2}
in (
3.2
), and so
2q<2q_{0}+\epsilon=K
, this property verifies
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
with the aforementioned choice of
K
. One has
0<\gamma<\frac{\log(2)}{\log(2q_{0})}
and when we let
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
then we get
\gamma\rightarrow\frac{\log(2)}{\log(2q_{0})}
(and
q\rightarrow q_{0}
). Hence
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
holds for all
0<\gamma<\frac{\log(2)}{\log(2)+\log(q_{0})}
and by definition this gives
\gamma(\omega)\geq\frac{1}{1+\log(q_{0})/\log(2)}
.

However, in general it is not clear that (
3.2
) implies
(P_{\omega,\gamma_{0}})
with
\gamma_{0}:=\frac{1}{1+\log(q_{0})/\log(2)}
and also
\gamma(\omega)>\frac{1}{1+\log(q_{0})/\log(2)}
might be violated.

If
q_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
, then take
\gamma:=\frac{\log(2)}{\log(1+\epsilon)}
,
K:=1+\epsilon
and when
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
by the arguments before we have
(P_{\omega,\gamma})
for any
\gamma>0
and so
\gamma(\omega)=+\infty
.

(ii)
(
2.1
) is precisely (
3.1
) with
q_{0}=1
.

(iii)
If
\omega
is quasianalytic, then the previous part
(ii)
and
(i)
in Remark
2.4
yield the conclusion.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

We comment on Lemma
3.1
:

(*)

The value
\frac{1}{2}
for the choice of
q_{0}
in (
3.1
) is critical: If
0<q_{0}<\frac{1}{2}
is allowed, then take
q
with
q_{0}<q<\frac{1}{2}
and we get (see (
3.2
)) that

\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\omega(2t)}{\omega(t)}\leq 2q<1.

But this clearly contradicts the standard assumption that
\omega
is non-decreasing.

(*)

As
q_{0}\rightarrow+\infty
the lower bound
\frac{1}{1+\log(q_{0})/\log(2)}
tends to
0
.

(*)

We give now a partial converse to
(i)
in Lemma
3.1
:

If
\gamma(\omega)>0
, equivalently
(\omega_{1})
(see
(i)
in Remark
2.3
), then since
\omega
is non-decreasing we estimate for all
s,t\geq 0
as follows

(3.3)

\omega(s+t)\leq\omega(2\max\{s,t\})\leq L\omega(\max\{s,t\})+L\leq L(\omega(t)+\omega(s))+L.

Consequently,
\gamma(\omega)>0
yields (
3.1
) for
q_{0}=L
, with
L
being the constant such that
\omega(2t)\leq L\omega(t)+L
for all
t\geq 0
; see
(\omega_{1})
. Note that
L\geq 1
because
L<1
yields a contradiction to the fact that
\omega
is a weight function (so
\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\omega(t)=+\infty
and
\omega
is non-decreasing).

Similarly, it is not clear that (
3.2
) implies (
3.1
): The analogous estimate as in (
3.3
) gives that (
3.2
) for
q_{0}
yields (
3.1
) for
2q_{0}
.

Using this information we are able to show the following main statement.

Theorem 3.3
.

For any weight function
\omega
with
\gamma(\omega)<+\infty
we get:

(*)

There exist uncountable infinite many pair-wise different (i.e. non-equivalent) weight functions
\tau_{a}:=\omega^{1/a}
,
a\in(0,\gamma(\omega)^{-1})=:\mathcal{I}_{\omega}
(with
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=(0,+\infty)
if
\gamma(\omega)=0
), such that each
\tau_{a}
has the following property:

There does not exist a weight function
\sigma
being equivalent to
\tau_{a}
and such that
\sigma
is almost subadditive (see (
2.1
)). In particular, each
\tau_{a}
itself is violating (
2.1
).

(*)

If
\omega
is a (normalized) BMT-weight function, then each
\tau_{a}
, too.

Roughly speaking this result means that “many” BMT-weight functions are not equivalent to an almost subadditive function in the sense of
[
1
]
; to each weight with finite
\gamma(\omega)
we can assign an uncountable infinite family of pair-wise different equivalence classes of weights such that in each of these classes no almost subadditive weight exists.

Proof.

Take any
a>0
with
\gamma(\omega)<\frac{1}{a}
, then set
\tau_{a}:=\omega^{1/a}
and by (
2.3
) we get

(3.4)

\gamma(\tau_{a})=\gamma(\omega^{1/a})=a\gamma(\omega)<1.

Note: If
\gamma(\omega)=0
, then any choice for
a>0
in (
3.4
) is admissible and
\gamma(\tau_{a})=0
for all
a>0
. This verifies the interpretation
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=(0,+\infty)
if
\gamma(\omega)=0
. (On the other hand, in order to choose
a
the assumption
\gamma(\omega)<+\infty
is indispensable. In the case
\gamma(\omega)=+\infty
, which is excluded by assumption, we put
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}:=\emptyset
.)

Consider now for such
a>0
(fixed) the function
\tau_{a}=\omega^{1/a}
. By
(ii)
in Lemma
3.1
and (
3.4
) we have that
\omega_{a}
is violating (
2.1
). Since
\gamma(\cdot)
is preserved under equivalence of weight functions, see
(v)
in Remark
2.3
, we have that each weight
\sigma
with
\sigma\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\tau_{a}
satisfies
\gamma(\sigma)<1
and hence also cannot have (
2.1
).

Recall that if
\omega
is a (normalized) BMT-weight, then each
\tau_{a}=\omega^{1/a}
, too.

Finally, let
a_{1}>a_{2}>0
satisfy
\gamma(\omega)<\frac{1}{a_{1}}<\frac{1}{a_{2}}
and let these choices be arbitrary but from now on fixed. We prove that
\omega^{1/a_{1}}
and
\omega^{1/a_{2}}
are not equivalent:

On the one hand, since
\omega
is assumed to be non-decreasing, we clearly have
\omega^{1/a_{1}}(t)\leq\omega^{1/a_{2}}(t)\Leftrightarrow\omega(t^{1/a_{1}})\leq\omega(t^{1/a_{2}})
for any
0<a_{2}<a_{1}
and all
t\geq 1
. But, on the other hand, if the weights are equivalent, i.e. if
\omega^{1/a_{1}}\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega^{1/a_{2}}
, then

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(t^{1/a_{2}})\leq C\omega(t^{1/a_{1}})+C,

and so

(3.5)

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(t^{a_{1}/a_{2}})\leq C\omega(t)+C.

We show that this is equivalent to

(3.6)

\exists\;C\geq 1\;\forall\;t\geq 0:\;\;\;\omega(t^{2})\leq C\omega(t)+C.

(
3.5
)
\Rightarrow
(
3.6
): If
a_{1}\geq 2a_{2}
, then the claim follows because
\omega
is non-decreasing. If
2a_{2}>a_{1}>a_{2}
, then an
n
-times iteration with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
(minimal) such that
(a_{1}/a_{2})^{n}\geq 2
yields the conclusion.

(
3.6
)
\Rightarrow
(
3.5
): Similarly, if
2a_{2}\geq a_{1}>a_{2}
, then the implication is clear. If
a_{1}>2a_{2}
, then an
n
-times iteration with
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
(minimal) such that
a_{1}/a_{2}\leq 2^{n}
yields the conclusion.

Finally, we verify that (
3.6
) implies
\gamma(\omega)=+\infty
for any weight function: Fix some
K>1
arbitrary and take any
\gamma>0
. (As we are going to see the choice for
K
can be taken uniformly for all
\gamma>0
.) Then we can find
t_{\gamma,K}:=K^{\gamma}>0
such that
\omega(K^{\gamma}t)\leq\omega(t^{2})
for all
t\geq t_{\gamma,K}
since
\omega
is non-decreasing and so
\omega(K^{\gamma}t)\leq C\omega(t)+C
for all
t\geq t_{\gamma,K}
. Hence it suffices to choose
K:=C-\epsilon
with
C
denoting the constant from (
3.6
) and
\epsilon>0
small and fixed.

But this is a contradiction to the basic assumption
\gamma(\omega)<+\infty
. (This step of the proof should be compared with
[
8
,
(A.1)
, p. 2171]
and the proof of
[
8
, Lemma A.1, p. 2173]
; formally there we have dealt with more specific weight functions but the additional assumptions on the weights are superfluous.)
∎

Example 3.4
.

Consider the Gevrey weights
\omega_{s}(t):=t^{1/s}
,
s>0
. Each
\omega_{s}
is a BMT-weight function; if
s>1
then
\omega_{s}
is non-quasianalytic and for all
0<s\leq 1
the weight is quasianalytic. It is straight-forward to see that
\gamma(\omega_{s})=s
. Fix
s>0
and by Theorem
3.3
for each
a\in(0,s^{-1})
, and so
as<1,
the weight
\tau_{a}=\omega_{s}(t^{1/a})=t^{1/(sa)}
has the desired property.

Note that for each
s\geq 1
we have that
(\omega_{\operatorname{sub}})
is valid and for each
s>1
even
(\omega_{\operatorname{snq}})
; see
(iii)
in Remark
2.3
.

Summarizing, for any
s\geq 1
the weight
\omega_{s}
is (almost) subadditive but, if
0<s<1
, then there does not exist a weight function
\tau
such that
\tau
is almost subadditive and
\tau\hyperlink{sim}{\sim}\omega_{s}
. Compare this also with
(iii)
in Lemma
3.1
. However, since for each such small

the weight is quasianalytic, formally this example does not solve the question from
[
1
, Rem. 1.5]
.

\sigma_{s}(t):=\max\{0,(\log(t))^{s}\}
,
s>1
, is another prominent standard example for a (normalized) BMT-weight function. However, here
\gamma(\sigma_{s})=+\infty
for any

and hence Theorem
3.3
cannot be applied.

Remark 3.5
.

The (standard) assumptions on the weight
\omega
in Theorem
3.3
are more general than in
[
1
]
and, in particular, this statement applies to quasianalytic weights as well since by
(i)
in Remark
2.4
in this case

is valid.

Moreover, if instead of almost subadditivity we consider the more general condition (
3.1
), then Theorem
3.3
transfers to this situation with the analogous proof; only the interval

changes. If
q_{0}>\frac{1}{2}
, then we get
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=(0,\frac{1}{1+\log(q_{0})/\log(2)}\gamma(\omega)^{-1})
(again with
\mathcal{I}_{\omega}=(0,+\infty)
provided that

): choose
a>0
with
\left(1+\frac{\log(q_{0})}{\log(2)}\right)\gamma(\omega)<\frac{1}{a}
and so by (

)

The rest follows as before. If

, then we have

and any choice for

is valid.

3.2.
The non-quasianalytic weight sequence case

From now on we focus on the case
\omega=\omega_{M}
for a specific sequence
M\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{\mathbb{N}}
. The proofs are involved and use technical preparations and therefore we split it into several steps. However, the ideas and constructions can be useful for further applications.

Theorem 3.6
.

There exist uncountable infinite many pair-wise different (i.e. non-equivalent) weight sequences
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
and normalized and non-quasianalytic BMT-weight functions
\omega=\omega_{M}
such that each of these functions has the following property:

There does not exist a weight function
\tau
such that
\tau
is equivalent to
\omega_{M}
and
\tau
is almost subadditive.

Note that
\omega_{s}
in Example
3.4
corresponds, up to equivalence, to
\omega_{G^{s}}
with
G^{s}:=(j!^{s})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
. But for
0<s\leq 1
the weights
\omega_{s}
,
\omega_{G^{s}}
(and the sequences
G^{s}
) are quasianalytic and hence not an appropriate choice.

Proof.

Step I
- Comments on sufficient growth and regularity properties.

By Lemma
3.1
in order to conclude it suffices to construct a normalized BMT-weight function
\omega
which is

(*)

non-quasianalytic and

(*)

such that
0<\gamma(\omega)<1
.

In order to achieve this goal the idea is now to consider
\omega\equiv\omega_{M}
with
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
satisfying explicit growth and regularity requirements and to define
M
explicitly.

Step II
- Sufficient growth and regularity properties for the weight sequence.

We want to find
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
such that

(a)

M
satisfies (
2.4
),

(b)

M
satisfies
(\operatorname{nq})
,

(c)

M
satisfies
(\operatorname{mg})
,

(d)

M
satisfies

(3.7)

\exists\;0<\beta<1\;\forall\;\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}:\;\;\;\liminf_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mu_{\ell j}}{\mu_{j}}\leq\ell^{\beta}.

Because for such
M
we get the following properties; for the precise definition of the growth index
\gamma(M)
, its comparison with
\gamma(\omega_{M})
and more details we refer to
[
6
, Sect. 3-5]
:

(*)

By
(a)
and
(b)
the function
\omega_{M}
is a non-quasianalytic (and normalized) BMT-weight (see Remark
2.5
).

(*)

By
(c)
and
[
6
, Cor. 4.6
(iii)
]
we get
\gamma(M)=\gamma(\omega_{M})
. Note: Without having
(\operatorname{mg})
we only know
\gamma(M)\leq\gamma(\omega_{M})
and the difference can be large in general; see
[
6
, Cor. 4.6
(i)
, Sect. 5]
.

(*)

By
(d)
and
[
6
, Thm. 3.11
(v)\Leftrightarrow(viii)
]
we see that
\gamma(M)\leq\beta<1
. Recall that
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
implies
\gamma(M)\geq 0
.

(*)

Note that in
[
6
]
a weight sequence denotes a sequence satisfying all requirements from the class
\mathcal{LC}
except necessarily
M_{0}\leq M_{1}
(see
[
6
, Sect. 3.1]
). Moreover, concerning the quotients an index-shift appears; i.e.
m_{p}
in
[
6
]
is corresponding to
\mu_{p+1}
. However, this does not effect the value of the index and hence the conclusion; see
[
6
, Rem. 3.8]
and the comments after
[
6
, Cor. 3.12]
.

Altogether,
\omega_{M}
is a (normalized) non-quasianalytic BMT-weight function satisfying
\gamma(\omega_{M})=\gamma(M)<1
and by
Step I
this function provides a convenient example. Thus it remains to construct explicitly
M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
such that
(a)-(d)
holds.

Step III
- Definition of the sequence
M
.

We introduce
M
in terms of its quotient sequence
\mu=(\mu_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
(with
\mu_{0}:=1
) and so set
M_{j}:=\prod_{k=0}^{j}\mu_{k}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
. For this, first fix an arbitrary real constant
A>2
. Second, we consider three auxiliary sequences (of positive integers)
(a_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
,
(b_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
and
(d_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
with
a_{j}<b_{j}<a_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and being defined as follows:

(3.8)

a_{1}:=1,\hskip 20.0ptb_{j}:=2^{d_{j}}a_{j},\hskip 20.0pta_{j+1}:=2^{j}b_{j},\hskip 20.0ptj\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}.

For the sequence
(d_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}}
we assume that it is strictly increasing with
d_{1}\geq 1
and satisfying the following growth restriction:

(3.9)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\left(\frac{2}{A}\right)^{d_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\frac{j}{2}+1}}.

This choice for
d_{j}
is possible since
A>2
.

For notational reasons we split each interval
(a_{j},b_{j}]
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, into
d_{j}
-many subintervals
I_{a_{j},i}:=(2^{i}a_{j},2^{i+1}a_{j}]
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq i\leq d_{j}-1
. Similarly, each interval
(b_{j},a_{j+1}]
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, is split into
j
-many subintervals
I_{b_{j},i}:=(2^{i}b_{j},2^{i+1}b_{j}]
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
0\leq i\leq j-1
. To be formally precise, we only consider all integers belonging to these intervals.

We define
\mu
on
a_{1}
and on the intervals
(a_{j},b_{j}]
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, as follows:

(3.10)

\mu_{a_{1}}:=2(=2a_{1}),\hskip 30.0pt\mu_{k}:=A\mu_{2^{i}a_{j}}=A^{i+1}\mu_{a_{j}},\;\;\;k\in I_{a_{j},i},\;\;\;0\leq i\leq d_{j}-1,\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}.

This means that iteratively on
I_{a_{j},i}
,
i\geq 1
, the sequence of quotients is given by multiplying the already defined quotients on
I_{a_{j},i-1}
with the constant
A
and
\mu
is constant on each
I_{a_{j},i}
. And the values on
I_{a_{j},0}
are obtained by multiplying
\mu_{a_{j}}
with
A
. In particular, we get

(3.11)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\mu_{b_{j}}=A^{d_{j}}\mu_{a_{j}}.

Next, on each
(b_{j},a_{j+1}]
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, we introduce
\mu
as follows:

(3.12)

\mu_{k}:=\sqrt{2}^{\frac{1}{2^{i}b_{j}}}\mu_{k-1},\;\;\;2^{i}b_{j}\leq k-1<k\leq 2^{i+1}b_{j},\hskip 20.0pt0\leq i\leq j-1,\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}.

Note: When considering in (
3.12
) the choices
i=j-1
and
k=2^{i+1}b_{j}=2^{j}b_{j}=a_{j+1}
this gives the value of
\mu_{a_{j+1}}
and so the definition of
M
is complete.

Since
\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{k}}=\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{2k-1}}\frac{\mu_{2k-1}}{\mu_{2k-2}}\cdots\frac{\mu_{k+1}}{\mu_{k}}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
gives a product of
k
-many factors, (
3.12
) yields

(3.13)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\forall\;0\leq i\leq j-1:\;\;\;\frac{\mu_{2^{i+1}b_{j}}}{\mu_{2^{i}b_{j}}}=\sqrt{2},

and which implies by iteration

(3.14)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\;\forall\;0\leq i\leq j-1:\;\;\;\frac{\mu_{2^{i+1}b_{j}}}{\mu_{b_{j}}}=2^{\frac{i+1}{2}}.

In particular, the choice
i=j-1
gives

(3.15)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\mu_{a_{j+1}}=2^{\frac{j}{2}}\mu_{b_{j}}.

Let us now show by induction the following consequence of (
3.9
) which is needed in the estimates below:

(3.16)

\forall\;j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}:\;\;\;\frac{a_{j}}{\mu_{a_{j}}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{j}}.

The case
j=1
follows by (
3.10
). Moreover, combining (
3.8
), (
3.11
) (
3.15
) with the induction hypothesis and finally with (
3.9
) gives for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}

\frac{a_{j+1}}{\mu_{a_{j+1}}}=\frac{2^{j}b_{j}}{2^{\frac{j}{2}}\mu_{b_{j}}}=\frac{2^{j}2^{d_{j}}a_{j}}{2^{\frac{j}{2}}A^{d_{j}}\mu_{a_{j}}}=\frac{a_{j}}{\mu_{a_{j}}}2^{\frac{j}{2}}\left(\frac{2}{A}\right)^{d_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\frac{j}{2}}}\left(\frac{2}{A}\right)^{d_{j}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{j+1}},

as desired.

Step IV
- Verification of the desired properties for
M
. We divide this step into several claims.

Claim a:

M\in\hyperlink{LCset}{\mathcal{LC}}
. This is obvious since
M_{0}=\mu_{0}=1
and
\frac{M_{1}}{M_{0}}=\mu_{1}=\mu_{a_{1}}=2
ensure normalization. Clearly
j\mapsto\mu_{j}
is by construction non-decreasing and satisfying
\lim_{j\rightarrow+\infty}\mu_{j}=+\infty
.

Claim b:

M
satisfies
(\operatorname{nq})
.

On the one hand, by recalling definition (
3.10
) and by (
3.16
) we have for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=a_{j}+1}^{b_{j}}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{j}-1}\sum_{k\in I_{a_{j},i}}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}=\sum_{i=0}^{d_{j}-1}\frac{2^{i}a_{j}}{A^{i+1}\mu_{a_{j}}}=\frac{a_{j}}{A\mu_{a_{j}}}\sum_{i=0}^{d_{j}-1}\left(\frac{2}{A}\right)^{i}\leq\frac{a_{j}}{A\mu_{a_{j}}}\sum_{i=0}^{+\infty}\left(\frac{2}{A}\right)^{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{j}}{A\mu_{a_{j}}}\frac{A}{A-2}\leq\frac{1}{2^{j}}\frac{1}{A-2}.

On the other hand, by (
3.8
), (
3.12
) and by combining (
3.9
) and (
3.16
) we get for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
:

\sum_{k=b_{j}+1}^{a_{j+1}}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sum_{k\in I_{b_{j},i}}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\leq\frac{a_{j+1}-b_{j}}{\mu_{b_{j}}}=\frac{b_{j}(2^{j}-1)}{A^{d_{j}}\mu_{a_{j}}}=\left(\frac{2}{A}\right)^{d_{j}}\frac{a_{j}(2^{j}-1)}{\mu_{a_{j}}}\leq\frac{2^{j}-1}{2^{j+\frac{j}{2}+1}}\leq\frac{1}{2^{\frac{j}{2}+1}}.

Summarizing, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq a_{1}}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}=\frac{1}{2}+\sum_{k>a_{1}}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{A-2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{2^{j}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\geq 1}\frac{1}{2^{\frac{j}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{A}{2(A-2)}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}-1}<+\infty,

and the claim is shown.

Claim c:

M
satisfies
(\operatorname{mg})
.

Recall that in order to conclude we have to show
\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{k}}<+\infty
and according to the definition of
M
we have to distinguish between several cases (also required in
Claim d
below):

(i)

For
k=1
we get
\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{k}}=\frac{\mu_{2a_{1}}}{\mu_{a_{1}}}=A
, see (
3.8
) and (
3.10
).

(ii)

When
k\in I_{a_{j},i}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
0\leq i\leq d_{j}-2
arbitrary, then we get
2k\in I_{a_{j},i+1}
and so
\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{k}}=A
, see again (
3.10
).

(iii)

When
k\in I_{a_{j},d_{j}-1}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
arbitrary, then
2k\in I_{b_{j},0}
and there exists
2\leq\ell\leq b_{j}
such that
b_{j}+\ell=2k
. This gives

\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{k}}=\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{b_{j}}}\frac{\mu_{b_{j}}}{\mu_{k}}=\sqrt{2}^{\frac{\ell}{b_{j}}},

see (
3.10
) and (
3.12
), and this equality implies the estimate
\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{k}}\leq\sqrt{2}
.

(iv)

When
k\in I_{b_{j},j-1}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
arbitrary, then
2k\in I_{a_{j+1},0}
and there exists
0\leq\ell\leq 2^{j-1}b_{j}-1
such that
k+\ell=a_{j+1}
. This implies

\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{k}}=\frac{\mu_{a_{j+1}}}{\mu_{k}}\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{a_{j+1}}}=\sqrt{2}^{\frac{\ell}{2^{j-1}b_{j}}}A,

see (
3.10
) and (
3.12
), and this equality yields the estimate
\frac{\mu_{2k}}{\mu_{k}}\leq\sqrt{2}A
.

(v)

Finally, let
k\in I_{b_{j},i}
with
0\leq i\leq j-2
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}

\displaystyle=\frac{b_{j}}{\mu_{b_{j}}}\frac{\sqrt{2}^{c_{j}-1}-1}{\sqrt{2}-1}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}-1}\frac{1}{j\log(j+1)}.

Since all appearing summands in the series under consideration are non-negative the previous estimate immediately verifies
\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{\mu_{k}}=+\infty
as desired.

Let us see that here we get
\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mu_{k}}{k}=+\infty
as well: By definition
\frac{\mu_{b_{j}}}{b_{j}}=\frac{\mu_{a_{j}}}{a_{j}}\left(\frac{A}{2}\right)^{d_{j}}\rightarrow+\infty

as
j\rightarrow+\infty
. By the comments made at the beginning of this section we are done.

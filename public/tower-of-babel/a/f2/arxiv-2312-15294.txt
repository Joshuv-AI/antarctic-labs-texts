On orbital stability of solitons for 2D Maxwell–Lorentz equations


Abstract
We prove the orbital stability of soliton solutions for 2D Maxwell–Lorentz system with extended charged particle. The solitons corresponds to the uniform motion and rotation of the particle. We reduce the corresponding Hamilton system by the canonical transformation via transition to a comoving frame. The solitons are the critical points of the reduced Hamiltonian. The key point of the proof is a lower bound for the Hamiltonian.

Introduction

We consider the 2D Maxwell–Lorentz equations with extended charged
spinning particle

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dot{E}(x,t)&\!=&\!J\nabla B(x,t)-[\dot{q}(t)-\omega(t)J(x-q(t))]\rho(x-q)\\
\dot{B}(x,t)&=&-\nabla\cdot JE(x,t),\quad\nabla\cdot E(x,t)=\rho(x-q(t)),\\
m\ddot{q}(t)&\!=&\!{\big\langle}E(x,t)+[J\dot{q}(t)+\omega(t)(x-q(t))]B(x,t),\rho(x-q(t)){\big\rangle}\\
I\dot{\omega}(t)&\!=&\!{\big\langle}(x-q(t))\cdot[JE(x,t)-\dot{q}(t)B(x,t)],\rho(x-q(t)){\big\rangle}\end{array}\right|,~~x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},~~t\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.1)

Here
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
,
E(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the electric field,
B(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
is the magnetic field,
\rho(x-q(t))
is the charge distribution of the extended
particle centered at a point
q(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
m
is the mass of the particle,
I>0
is its moment of inertia,
and
\omega(t)=\dot{\varphi}(t)
is the angular velocity of the particle rotation. The brackets
\langle,\rangle
denote the inner product in the Hilbert space
L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We choose the units where the speed of light is
c=1
.

Note that the 2D Maxwell–Lorentz equations can be introduced directly from 3D Maxwell–Lorentz system

[
18
, (2.39)–(2.41)]
by substituting
x=(x_{1},x_{2},0)
and

E(x,t)=(E_{1}(x,t),E_{2}(x,t),0),\quad B(x,t)=(0,0,B_{3}(x,t)),\quad q(t)=(q_{1}(t),q_{2}(t),0).

In the main body of the paper, we focus on the equations with a
nonrelativistic particle
,
and at the end, we comment on the corresponding modifications for the case of a
relativistic particle
.

We assume that the charge density
\rho(x)
is smooth and rotation-invariant, i.e.,

\rho\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\qquad\rho(x)=\rho_{1}(|x|).

(1.2)

Moreover, we assume that the total charge equals zero (neutrality of the particle):

\int\rho(x)dx=0.

(1.3)

The Maxwell potentials
A(x,t)=(A_{1}(x,t),A_{2}(x,t))
,
\Phi(x,t)
are introduced by

B(x,t)=\nabla\cdot JA,\qquad E(x,t)=-\dot{A}(x,t)-\nabla\Phi(x,t).

(1.4)

We choose the Coulomb gauge

\nabla\cdot A(x,t)=0
. The second line of (
1.1
) implies that
\Delta\Phi(x,t)=-\rho(x-q(t))
. Hence,

\Phi(x,t)=\Phi_{0}(x-q(t)),~~{\rm where}\qquad\Phi_{0}(y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\log|z-y|\rho(z)dz.

(1.5)

In the Maxwell potentials, the system (
1.1
) becomes

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\ddot{A}(x,t)&=&\Delta A(x,t)+{\cal P}\big([\dot{q}(t)-\dot{\varphi}(t)J(x-q(t))]\rho(x-q)\big)\\
m\ddot{q}(t)&=&{\big\langle}-\dot{A}(x,t)+[J\dot{q}(t)+\dot{\varphi}(t)(x-q(t))]\nabla\cdot JA(x,t),\rho(x\!-\!q(t)){\big\rangle}\\
I\ddot{\varphi}(t)&=&\!\!{\big\langle}(x\!-\!q(t))\cdot[-J\dot{A}(x,t)\!-\dot{q}(t)\nabla\cdot JA(x,t)],\rho(x\!-\!q(t)){\big\rangle}\end{array}\right|.

(1.6)

Here
{\cal P}
is the projection onto the space of solenoidal (divergence-free) vector fields.
In the Fourier space,

\widehat{{\cal P}a}(k)=\hat{a}(k)-\frac{\hat{a}(k)\cdot k}{k^{2}}k.

(1.7)

The system is invariant under the action of
the group of translations and rotations of the space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Accordingly, the system admits solitons of the form

S_{v,\omega}(t)=(A_{v,\omega}(x-vt),\dot{A}_{v,\omega}(x-vt),vt,v,\omega)

(1.8)

moving with constant speed
v
and rotating with constant angular velocity
\omega
.

The main result of present paper is the orbital stability of the solitons.
We establish the Lagrangian and the Hamiltonian structures of the system (
1.1
), and prove the conservations
of energy, linear and angular momenta.

The proof of the orbital stability of solitons relies on the reduction
of the system (
1.6
) by the canonical transformation via transition to a comoving frame.
The Hamiltonian of the reduced system depends on two parameters:
the total linear momentum
P
and the
angular momentum
M
, which are invariant in time.

The key point is the proof of the lower bound for the reduced Hamiltonian
with the fixed
P
and
M
.
The bound implies that the soliton is a strict global minimizer of the reduced Hamiltonian.
We show that this fact implies the orbital stability.
Moreover, the lower bound implies that the reduced system admits a unique
soliton for every
P,M\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Let us comment on related results.

Low-dimensional models are widely exploited
as useful models in the classical and quantum field theories. For example,
’t Hooft
[
4
]
established the confining for quarks in the meson theory in 1+1 space-time.
The 2D version of the Maxwell electrodynamics
was considered by Schwinger in
[
17
]
,
to show the possibility of the nonzero mass particles in the gauge theories.
In
[
3
]
, the Maxwell–Lorentz equations in 1D space were considered
in the context of plasma dynamics, when the charge and currents
correspond to a smooth distribution of the charged dust subject to the Lorentz dynamics.
The well-posedness is proved and the point charge asymptotics is analyzed.
The solitons of 2D Maxwell–Lorentz equations and their stability were not consider previously.

In
[
2
]
, Bambusi and Galgani proved the existence and orbital stability of solitons with velocities
|v|<1
for
the 3D Maxwell–Lorentz system without spinning. The proof relies on the transition to a comoving frame
and a lower bound for the reduced Hamiltonian.
The global convergence to solitons for the same system was proved in
[
5
]
.
In
[
13
]
, the global attraction to stationary states was established for a similar system with a
relativistic particle in presence of an external confining potential.

The results
[
5
,
14
,
13
]
provide the first rigorous proof of the radiation damping
in classical electrodynamics. All these results were obtained under the
Wiener-type condition
on the charge density
\rho
.
For the corresponding surveys, see
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
.

The global convergence to rotating solitons was established in
[
6
]
for solutions of the 3D Maxwell–Lorentz system
with
q(t)\equiv 0
in the case of sufficiently small charge density
\rho
.
This result was strengthened in
[
14
]
under a considerably weaker Wiener-type condition.

In
[
7
]
, the asymptotic completeness was proved for the scattering of solutions to the
Maxwell–Lorentz equations without spinning. The result refers to solutions which are close to a solitary manifold.

In
[
10
,
12
]
the orbital stability of solitons was studied for the 3D Maxwell–Lorentz system with moving and spinning particle.
The solitons move with a velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
|v|<1
and rotate with
an angular velocity
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
The results are obtained for solitons either with
\omega=0
or with
v=0
.
The proofs rely on the Hamiltonian–Poisson structure of the equations
which has been constructed in
[
11
]
.

2
Well-posedness

Let us introduce a phase space for the system (
1.6
). Denote by

\dot{H}^{1}
the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}
with the respect to the norm

\|A\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\|\nabla A\|_{L^{2}}
, where
L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let
{\cal F}^{0}
,
\dot{\cal F}^{1}
be the subspaces of solenoidal vector fields:

{\cal F}^{0}:=\{A\in L^{2}:{\rm div}A(x)\equiv 0\},\qquad\dot{\cal F}^{1}:=\{A\in\dot{H}^{1}:{\rm div}A(x)\equiv 0\}.

(2.1)

Denote

{\cal F}:=\dot{\cal F}^{1}\oplus{\cal F}^{0},\quad{\mathbb{Y}}:=\dot{\cal F}^{1}\oplus{\cal F}^{0}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2},\quad{\mathbb{W}}:=L^{2}\oplus H^{-1}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2}.

(2.2)

Proposition 2.1
.

i)
For any initial state

Y(0)=(A(x,0),\dot{A}(x,0),q(0),\dot{q}(0),\varphi(0),\dot{\varphi}(0))\in{\mathbb{Y}}
, there exists a unique
solution of the system
(
1.6
)
,

Y(t)=(A(x,t),\dot{A}(x,t),q(t),\dot{q}(t),\varphi(t),\dot{\varphi}(t))\in C(\mathbb{R},{\mathbb{Y}})\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R},{\mathbb{W}}).

(2.3)

ii)
The map

W(t):Y(0)\mapsto Y(t)

(2.4)

is continuous in
{\mathbb{Y}}
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

iii)
For
Y(0)\in{\mathbb{Y}}
, the energy
{\cal E}(t)
, linear momentum
P(t)=(P_{1}(t),P_{2}(t))
and angular momentum
M(t)
are conserved:

\displaystyle{\cal E}(t):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int[\dot{A}^{2}(x,t)+|\nabla A(x,t)|^{2}]dx+\frac{1}{2}m\dot{q}^{2}(t)+\frac{1}{2}I\dot{\varphi}^{2}(t)=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits,

(2.5)

\displaystyle P_{j}(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-{\big\langle}\dot{A}(x,t),\nabla_{j}A(x,t){\big\rangle}+m\dot{q}_{j}(t)+{\big\langle}\rho(x-q(t)),A_{j}(x,t){\big\rangle}=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits,~j=1,2,

(2.6)

\displaystyle M(t)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I\dot{\varphi}(t)-{\big\langle}[J(x-q(t))]\cdot A(x,t),\rho(x-q(t)){\big\rangle}=\mathop{\rm const}\nolimits.

(2.7)

iv)
Let

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A(x,0)\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\!\otimes\!\mathbb{R}^{2},\qquad\dot{A}(x,0)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\!\otimes\!\mathbb{R}^{2}\\
A(x,0)=0,\,\,\dot{A}(x,0)+\nabla\Phi_{0}(x-q(0))=0,\quad|x|>R_{0}\end{array}\right|

(2.8)

for some
R_{0}>0
. Then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A(x,t)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R})\!\otimes\!\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}A(x,t)|+|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\dot{A}(x,t)|\leq C_{\alpha}(1+|x|)^{-2-|\alpha|},\quad|x|>R(t)+1,\qquad\forall\alpha\end{array}\right|,

(2.9)

where

R(t)=R+|q(0)|+\overline{v}t+R_{\rho}
with

\overline{v}>0
defined by the relation
{\cal E}=\frac{1}{2}m\overline{v}^{2}
.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Proposition A.5 in
[
13
]
.
Namely, consider at first the case of smooth initial functions
A(x,0),\dot{A}(x,0)
with compact supports.
The elimination of the fields reduces
the system (
1.6
) to the system of nonlinear integral equations for
q(t)
and
\omega(t)
.
Then the existence and uniqueness of the solution

(q(t),\omega(t))

for small
|t|
follows by the
application of the contraction mapping principle.
The corresponding fields
A(\cdot,t),\dot{A}(\cdot,t)
are smooth and have compact supports, hence, the energy conservation
(
2.5
)
follows by standard
integration by parts. Therefore, the solution
Y(t)
can be extended to all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

The continuity of the map

W(t)
, as constructed
on the dense subspace of

{\mathbb{Y}}
, follows from the continuity
of the map
{\mathbb{Y}}\to C^{2}([0,t];\mathbb{R}^{2}\oplus\mathbb{R}^{2})
defined
as
Y(0)\mapsto(q(\cdot),\omega(\cdot))|_{[0,t]}
.
Now
i) and (
2.5
) follow
for general initial state
Y(0)\in{\mathbb{Y}}

by suitable approximations of
Y(0)
.
The conservation laws (
2.6
), and (
2.7
) will be proved later.

It remains to prove iv).
Note that
q(\cdot)\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}

and
\omega(\cdot)\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})\otimes\mathbb{R}^{2}

by (
2.3
)
and the last two equations of (
1.6
). This fact and
the first line of
(
2.8
) imply
the first line of (
2.9
)
due to the Kirchhoff integral representation of solutions
of the first equation of (
1.6
).
Further,
applying
-J\nabla
to both sides of the first equation in (
1.4
), and using
\nabla\cdot A=0
,
we obtain that

\Delta A(x,t)=-J\nabla B(x,t).

The second line of (
2.8
) implies that

E(x,0)=B(x,0)=0,\qquad|x|>R.

(2.10)

Moreover,
(
2.5
) implies that

|\dot{q}(t)|\leq\overline{v}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Hence,
|q(t)|\leq|q(0)|+\overline{v}|t|
.

Therefore, (
2.10
) implies that

E(x,t)=B(x,t)=0
, for
|x|>R(t)

by the 2D analogue of the integral representations (A.4) from
[
13
]
.
Hence,

A(x,t)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|y|<R(t)}J\nabla B(y,t)\log|x-y|dy=-\frac{1}{2\pi}J\nabla\int_{|y|<R(t)}B(y,t)\log|x-y|dy.

(2.11)

Now the second line of (
2.9
) follows.
∎

3
The Lagrangian structure

Here we show that, under assumption (
1.2
), sufficiently smooth solutions

X(t)=(A(t),q(t),\omega(t))
of the 2D system (
1.6
) satisfy the Hamilton least action principle.
The Lagrangian
L
is well known (cf.
[
15
, (28.6)]
:

L(A,\dot{A},q,\dot{q},\varphi,\dot{\varphi})=\frac{1}{2}\int[\dot{A}^{2}-(\nabla A)^{2}]dx+\frac{m\dot{q}^{2}}{2}+\frac{I\dot{\varphi}^{2}}{2}+{\big\langle}[\dot{q}-\dot{\varphi}J(x-q)]\cdot A,\rho(x-q){\big\rangle}

(3.1)

up to an additive constant depending on
\Phi
.
This Lagrangian is well defined
and Fréchet differentiable
on the extended Hilbert phase space
{\mathbb{Y}}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Under the assumption (
1.2
), the system (
1.6
)
with
\omega(t)=\dot{\varphi}(t)
is equivalent to the Euler–Lagrange equations

\dot{\Pi}=D_{A}L,\qquad\dot{p}=D_{q}L,\qquad\dot{M}=D_{\varphi}L,

(3.2)

where the canonical conjugate momenta are defined by

\Pi:=D_{\dot{A}}L,\qquad p:=D_{\dot{q}}L,\qquad M:=D_{\dot{\varphi}}L.

(3.3)

Proof.

All the calculations of variations are justified by

C^{2}
-approximations, which exist by Proposition
2.1
, iv).

Step i)
Differentiating (
3.1
), and using (
1.4
), we find
the momenta

\Pi=\dot{A},\qquad p=m\dot{q}+{\big\langle}A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle},\qquad M=I\dot{\varphi}-{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle}.

(3.4)

It remains to calculate the derivative
D_{A}L

on the right-hand side
of the first equation (
3.2
).
For any
h\in\dot{\cal F}^{1}
, we have

\frac{1}{2}{\big\langle}D_{A}\int(\nabla A(x))^{2}dx,h{\big\rangle}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\int|\nabla[A(x)+\varepsilon h(x)]|^{2}dx=\!\int\nabla A(x)\nabla h(x)dx=-{\big\langle}\Delta A,h{\big\rangle}.

Hence,

D_{A}L=\Delta A(q+y)+{\cal P}\big([\dot{q}-\dot{\varphi}Jy]\rho(y)\big).

(3.5)

Now the first equation of (
3.2
) coincides with the Maxwell equation (the first line of (
1.6
)).

Step ii)
Now we check the Newton equation (the second equation of (
1.6
)).
Differentiating (
3.1
) in
q_{k}
, we obtain

\displaystyle D_{q_{k}}L

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{q_{k}}{\big\langle}[\dot{q}-\dot{\varphi}Jy]\cdot A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle}={\big\langle}[\dot{q}-\dot{\varphi}Jy]\cdot\nabla_{k}A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle},~~k\!=\!1,2.

(3.6)

Hence, the corresponding Euler–Lagrange equation reads as the system

m\ddot{q}_{k}+{\big\langle}\dot{A}_{k}(q+\!y,t),\rho(y){\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}(\dot{q}(t)\!\cdot\!\nabla)A_{k}(q+\!y,t),\rho(y){\big\rangle}\!=\!{\big\langle}[\dot{q}\!-\!\dot{\varphi}Jy]\cdot\nabla_{k}A(q+\!y,t),\rho(y){\big\rangle}.

(3.7)

We should check that this system coincides with the second equation of (
1.6
)
with
\omega(t)=\dot{\varphi}(t)
:

m\ddot{q}_{k}(t)={\big\langle}-\dot{A}_{k}(q+y,t)+[(J\dot{q})_{k}(t)+\dot{\varphi}(t)y_{k}]\nabla\cdot JA(q+y,t),\rho(y){\big\rangle}.

(3.8)

It remains to prove that

-{\big\langle}(\dot{q}\cdot\nabla)A_{k},\rho{\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}[\dot{q}-\dot{\varphi}Jy]\cdot\nabla_{k}A,\rho{\big\rangle}={\big\langle}[(J\dot{q})_{k}+\dot{\varphi}y_{k}]\nabla\cdot JA,\rho{\big\rangle},~~k\!=\!1,2.

(3.9)

It suffices to consider the case
k=1
. Now (
3.9
) becomes

-{\big\langle}(\dot{q}_{1}\nabla_{1}+\dot{q}_{2}\nabla_{2})A_{1},\rho{\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}[\dot{q}_{1}-\!\dot{\varphi}y_{2}]\nabla_{1}A_{1},\rho{\big\rangle}+[\dot{q}_{2}+\!\dot{\varphi}y_{1}]\nabla_{1}A_{2},\rho{\big\rangle}={\big\langle}[\dot{q}_{2}+\dot{\varphi}y_{1}]\nabla\cdot JA,\rho{\big\rangle}.

After cancellations, this identity is equivalent to

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}{\big\langle}[-y_{2}\nabla_{1}+y_{1}\nabla_{2}]A_{1},\rho{\big\rangle}=\dot{\varphi}{\big\langle}A_{1},[y_{2}\nabla_{1}-y_{1}\nabla_{2}]\rho{\big\rangle}=0.

(3.10)

This identity follows by the rotational symmetry (
1.2
).

Step iii)
Finally, let us check that the last equation of (
3.2
) is equivalent to the last (torque) equation of the system (
1.6
).
The right-hand side of the last equation of (
3.2
)
vanishes since the Lagrangian (
3.1
) does not depend on
\varphi
.
Now, the expression for
M
from (
3.4
) implies

\dot{M}(t)=I\ddot{\varphi}(t)-{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot[\dot{A}(q+\!y,t)+(\dot{q}\cdot\nabla)A(q+\!y,t)],\rho(y){\big\rangle}=0.

(3.11)

So, the angular momentum is conserved:

M(t)=M,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.12)

To prove the equivalence
of the last equations of (
3.2
) and (
1.6
),
we should check that

-{\big\langle}(y_{1}\dot{q}_{1}+y_{2}\dot{q}_{2})(\nabla_{1}A_{2}-\nabla_{2}A_{1}),\rho{\big\rangle}={\big\langle}y_{2}[(\dot{q}_{1}\nabla_{1}+\dot{q}_{2}\nabla_{2})A_{1}]-y_{1}[(\dot{q}_{1}\nabla_{1}+\dot{q}_{2}\nabla_{2})A_{2}],\rho{\big\rangle}.

(3.13)

After cancellations, we arrive at

\dot{q}_{2}{\big\langle}(-y_{2}\nabla_{1}+y_{1}\nabla_{2})A_{2},\rho{\big\rangle}=\dot{q}_{1}{\big\langle}(-y_{1}\nabla_{2}+y_{2}\nabla_{1})A_{1},\rho{\big\rangle}.

Integrating by parts, and using the rotational symmetry (
1.2
), we
conclude that both parts of the last equation vanish.
∎

4
The conservation of linear momentum

The Lagrangian (
3.1
) is invariant under translations
x\mapsto x+a

and rotations
R(\theta):x_{1}+ix_{2}\mapsto e^{i\theta}(x_{1}+ix_{2})
of the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The rotation symmetry implies the conservation
(
3.12
) of the angular momentum
M
.
Let us calculate the conserved linear momentum
P
.
Consider the action of the group of translations

T_{a}:X=(A,q,\varphi)\mapsto T_{a}X=(A(x-a),q+a,\varphi),\qquad a\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^{2}.

The derivative of this map is

T^{\prime}_{a}:\dot{X}=(\dot{A},\dot{q},\dot{\varphi})\mapsto T_{a}^{\prime}\dot{X}=(\dot{A}(x-a),\dot{q},\dot{\varphi}).

It is easy to check that
L(T_{a}X,T_{a}^{\prime}\dot{X})=L(X,\dot{X})
.
Hence, we can apply
the Noether theorem on invariants
[
1
, p. 88]
to the one-parametric subgroups

T_{\varepsilon a}
with any
a\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
.
It suffices to consider

C^{2}
-approximations, which exist by
Proposition
2.1
.
Thus,
we obtain
the conservation
of the projection of the linear momentum

\displaystyle P(Y,\dot{Y})\cdot a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{\dot{Y}}L\cdot\frac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}T_{\varepsilon a}Y=(\dot{A},m\dot{q}+{\big\langle}\rho(x-q),A(x){\big\rangle},D_{\dot{\varphi}}L)\cdot(-a\cdot\nabla A,a,0)

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-{\big\langle}\dot{A},a\cdot\nabla A{\big\rangle}+m\dot{q}\cdot a+{\big\langle}\rho(x-q),A(x){\big\rangle}\cdot a.

Therefore, the conserved linear momentum is the vector

P=-{\big\langle}\Pi,\nabla_{*}A{\big\rangle}+p=-{\big\langle}\dot{A},\nabla_{*}A){\big\rangle}+m\dot{q}+{\big\langle}\rho(x-q),A(x){\big\rangle},

(4.2)

where the expression with
\nabla_{*}
is defined by

{\big\langle}\Pi,\nabla_{*}A{\big\rangle}_{j}={\big\langle}\Pi,\nabla_{j}A{\big\rangle},\quad j=1,2.

(4.3)

5
The Hamiltonian structure

The Hamiltonian structure is obtained through the Legendre transformation

[
1
,
16
]

(\dot{A},\dot{q},\dot{\varphi})\mapsto(\Pi,p,M),\quad H(A,q,\varphi,\Pi,p,M)={\big\langle}\Pi,\dot{A}{\big\rangle}+p\dot{q}+M\dot{\varphi}-L.

(5.1)

The formulas (
3.4
) give

\dot{A}=\Pi,\qquad\dot{q}=\frac{1}{m}(p-{\big\langle}A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle}),\qquad\dot{\varphi}=\frac{1}{I}(M+{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle}).

(5.2)

Hence, we obtain the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{2}\int[\Pi^{2}+|\nabla A|^{2}]dy+[m\dot{q}+{\big\langle}A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle}]\dot{q}+[I\dot{\varphi}-{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle}]\dot{\varphi}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\!\!-\frac{m\dot{q}^{2}}{2}-\frac{I\dot{\varphi}^{2}}{2}-{\big\langle}[\dot{q}-\dot{\varphi}Jy]\cdot A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle}=\frac{1}{2}\int[\Pi^{2}+|\nabla A|^{2}]dy+\frac{m\dot{q}^{2}}{2}+\frac{I\dot{\varphi}^{2}}{2},\qquad

which coincides with (
2.5
).
Here
\displaystyle\frac{m\dot{q}^{2}}{2}
and
\displaystyle\frac{I\dot{\varphi}^{2}}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{m\dot{q}^{2}}{2}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2m}(p-{\big\langle}A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle})^{2},\qquad\displaystyle\frac{I\dot{\varphi}^{2}}{2}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2I}(M+{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot A(q+y),\rho(y){\big\rangle})^{2}.

(5.4)

The Hamiltonian (

{\mathbb{Y}}
.
The system (

6
The canonical transformation to a comoving frame

Let us denote the fields in the “comoving frame” as

{\bf A}(y)=A(q+y),\qquad{\bm{\Pi}}(y)=\Pi(q+y),

(6.1)

and define the map of the phase space
{\mathbb{Y}}\oplus\mathbb{R}

(6.2)

where
P

).
Let us show that

(

{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}},\dot{\bf A}{\big\rangle}+P\dot{q}+M\dot{\varphi}={\big\langle}\Pi,\dot{A}{\big\rangle}+p\dot{q}+M\dot{\varphi}.

\dot{\bf A}(y,t)=\dot{A}(q+y,t)+\dot{q}(t)\cdot\nabla A(q+y,t).

6.3
) reduces to

{\big\langle}\Pi,\dot{q}\cdot\nabla A{\big\rangle}+P\cdot\dot{q}=p\dot{q},

which holds due to (
4.2
).

As a result, the canonical Hamiltonian equations (
5.5
)
in the variables
(A,\Pi,q,P,\varphi,M)
with the Hamiltonian (

)
are equivalent to the similar equations in the variables

({\bf A},{\bm{\Pi}},q,P,\varphi,M)

\displaystyle{\bf H}({\bf A},{\bm{\Pi}},P,M)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int[|{\bm{\Pi}}|^{2}+|\nabla{\bf A}|^{2}]dy+\frac{1}{2m}(P+{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}},\nabla_{*}{\bf A}{\big\rangle}-{\big\langle}{\bf A},\rho{\big\rangle})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2I}(M+{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot{\bf A}(y),\rho(y){\big\rangle})^{2},

where we used the expressions (

), (
4.2
)
and (

The equivalence follows from

Hence, the solutions of these systems

In other words, in the new variables, the Hamiltonian equations (

\dot{\bf A}=D_{\bm{\Pi}}{\bf H},\,\,\,\dot{\bm{\Pi}}=-D_{\bf A}{\bf H};\quad\dot{q}=D_{P}{\bf H},\,\,\,\dot{P}=-D_{q}{\bf H};\quad\dot{\varphi}=D_{M}{\bf H},\,\,\,\dot{M}=-D_{\varphi}{\bf H}.

Now the key point is that the Hamiltonian (

q
and
\varphi
. Hence, the equations for
q,P,\varphi
,
M

\displaystyle\dot{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D_{P}{\bf H}=\frac{1}{m}(P+{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}},\nabla_{*}{\bf A}{\big\rangle}-{\big\langle}{\bf A},\rho{\big\rangle}),\quad\dot{P}=0,

(6.7)

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}

\displaystyle D_{M}{\bf H}=\frac{1}{I}(M+{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot{\bf A}(y),\rho(y){\big\rangle}),\qquad\quad\,\,\,\,\dot{M}=0.

(6.8)

which also coincide with the formulas (

) and (

and
correspond to the conservation of

As the result, the system

\left\{\!\!\!\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{rcl}\dot{\bf A}&=&{\bm{\Pi}}+(\dot{q}\cdot\nabla){\bf A}\\

by (

8
The Lyapunov function

The stationary equations (
7.2
) mean that

D_{{\bf A}}{\bf H}_{P,M}({\bf S}_{v,\omega})=0,\quad D_{{\bm{\Pi}}}{\bf H}_{P,M}({\bf S}_{v,\omega})=0.

(8.1)

Hence, each soliton
{\bf S}_{v,\omega}
is a critical point of the reduced
Hamiltonian
{\bf H}_{P,M}
with the corresponding linear momentum
P=P_{v,\omega}
and
angular momentum
M=M_{v,\omega}
.
Moreover, it turns out that, the
soliton is a strict global minimizer of
{\bf H}_{P,M}
, which implies the orbital stability.

Lemma 8.1
.

The lower bound holds,

\displaystyle\delta{\bf H}_{P,M}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\bf H}_{P,M}({\bf A}_{v,\omega}+\delta{\bf A},{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega}+\delta{\bm{\Pi}})-{\bf H}_{P,M}({\bf A}_{v,\omega},{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega})

(8.2)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1-|v|}{2}(\|\delta{\bf A}\|_{\dot{\cal F}^{1}}^{2}\!+\|\delta{\bm{\Pi}}\|_{{\cal F}^{0}}^{2}),\qquad(\delta{\bf A},\delta{\bm{\Pi}})\!\in\!{\cal F}.

Proof.

Denote
\delta{\bf A}={\bf a}
,
\delta{\bm{\Pi}}=\bm{\pi}
.
Then
\nabla\cdot{\bf a}=0
, and so we have

\displaystyle\delta{\bf H}_{P,M}=\frac{1}{2}\int(|{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega}+\bm{\pi}|^{2}+|\nabla({\bf A}_{v,\omega}+{\bf a})|^{2})\,dy-\frac{1}{2}\int(|{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega}|^{2}+|\nabla{\bf A}_{v,\omega}|^{2})\,dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2m}(P+{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}},\nabla_{*}{\bf A}{\big\rangle}-{\big\langle}{\bf A},\rho{\big\rangle})^{2}-\frac{1}{2m}(P+{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega},\nabla_{*}{\bf A}_{v,\omega}{\big\rangle}-{\big\langle}{\bf A}_{v,\omega},\rho{\big\rangle})^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2I}(M+{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot{\bf A},\rho{\big\rangle})^{2}-\frac{1}{2I}(M+{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot{\bf A}_{v,\omega},\rho{\big\rangle})^{2}.

(8.3)

Evaluating, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta{\bf H}_{P,M}=\frac{1}{2}\int(|\bm{\pi}|^{2}+|\nabla{\bf a}|^{2})\,dy+\int({\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega}\cdot\bm{\pi}+\nabla{\bf A}_{v,\omega}\cdot\nabla{\bf a})\,dy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2m}\big(P+\!{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega},\nabla_{*}{\bf A}_{v,\omega}{\big\rangle}\!+\!{\big\langle}\bm{\pi},\nabla_{*}{\bf A}_{v,\omega}{\big\rangle}\!+\!{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega},\!\nabla_{*}{\bf a}{\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}\bm{\pi},\nabla_{*}{\bf a}{\big\rangle}\!-\!{\big\langle}{\bf A}_{v,\omega},\rho{\big\rangle}\!-\!{\big\langle}{\bf a},\rho{\big\rangle}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2m}\big(P+{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega},\nabla_{*}{\bf A}_{v,\omega}{\big\rangle}-{\big\langle}{\bf A}_{v,\omega},\rho{\big\rangle}\big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2I}\big(M+{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot[{\bf A}_{v,\omega}+{\bf a}],\rho{\big\rangle}\big)^{2}-\frac{1}{2I}\big(M+{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot{\bf A}_{v,\omega},\rho{\big\rangle}\big)^{2}.

After a rearrangement, we get

\displaystyle\delta{\bf H}_{P,M}\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!=\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{2}\int(|\bm{\pi}|^{2}+|\nabla{\bf a}|^{2})\,dy+\int({\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega}\cdot\bm{\pi}+\nabla{\bf A}_{v,\omega}\cdot\nabla{\bf a})\,dy

(8.4)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2m}[({\bf p}_{v,\omega}+\delta{\bf p})^{2}-{\bf p}_{v,\omega}^{2}]+\frac{1}{2I}[(M_{v,\omega}+\delta M)^{2}-M_{v,\omega}^{2}],

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}{\bf p}_{v,\omega}&\!\!:=&P\!\!+{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega},\nabla_{*}{\bf A}_{v,\omega}{\big\rangle}-{\big\langle}{\bf A}_{v,\omega}(y),\rho(y){\big\rangle}=mv\\
\delta{\bf p}&\!\!:=&\!\!{\big\langle}\bm{\pi},\nabla_{*}{\bf A}_{v,\omega}{\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega},\nabla_{*}{\bf a}{\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}\bm{\pi},\nabla_{*}{\bf a}{\big\rangle}-{\big\langle}{\bf a},\rho{\big\rangle}\\
M_{v,\omega}&\!\!:=&\!\!M+{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot{\bf A}_{v,\omega},\rho{\big\rangle}=I\omega\\
\delta M&\!\!:=&\!\!{\big\langle}[Jy]\cdot{\bf a},\rho{\big\rangle}\end{array}\right|.

(8.5)

It remains to calculate the integral

{\cal I}:=\int({\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega}\cdot\bm{\pi}+\nabla{\bf A}_{v,\omega}\cdot\nabla{\bf a})\,dy={\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega},\bm{\pi}{\big\rangle}-{\big\langle}\Delta{\bf A}_{v,\omega},{\bf a}{\big\rangle}.

By (
7.2
),

{\cal I}=-{\big\langle}(v\cdot\nabla){\bf A}_{v,\omega},\bm{\pi}{\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}(v\cdot\nabla){\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega},{\bf a}{\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}v\rho-\omega Jy\rho,{\bf a}{\big\rangle}

(8.6)

since
\nabla\cdot{\bf a}=0
.
Integrating by parts in (
8.6
), we obtain

{\cal I}=-{\big\langle}(v\cdot\nabla){\bf A}_{v,\omega},\bm{\pi}{\big\rangle}-{\big\langle}{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega},(v\cdot\nabla){\bf a}{\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}v\rho-\omega Jy\rho,{\bf a}{\big\rangle}=-v\cdot\delta{\bf p}-\omega\delta M+{\big\langle}\bm{\pi},(v\cdot\nabla){\bf a}{\big\rangle}.

(8.7)

Substituting into (
8.4
), we get

\displaystyle\delta{\bf H}_{P,M}\!\!

\displaystyle\!\!=

\displaystyle\!\!\frac{1}{2}\int(|\bm{\pi}|^{2}+|\nabla{\bf a}|^{2})\,dy+{\big\langle}\bm{\pi},(v\cdot\nabla){\bf a}{\big\rangle}

(8.8)

\displaystyle\!\!+\frac{1}{2m}[({\bf p}_{v,\omega}+\delta{\bf p})^{2}-{\bf p}_{v,\omega}^{2}]-v\cdot\delta{\bf p}+\frac{1}{2I}[(M_{v,\omega}+\delta M)^{2}-M_{v,\omega}^{2}]-\omega\delta M.\qquad\qquad

The last line can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{m}{2}\Big((v+\delta{\bf p}/m)^{2}-v^{2}-2v\cdot\delta{\bf p}/m\Big)+\frac{I}{2}\Big((\omega+\delta M/I)^{2}-\omega^{2}-2\omega\delta M/I\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{2}(\delta{\bf p}/m)^{2}+\frac{I}{2}(\delta M/I)^{2}\geq 0.

(8.9)

We have
|{\big\langle}\bm{\pi},(v\cdot\nabla){\bf a}{\big\rangle}|\leq|v||\bm{\pi}||\nabla{\bf a}|
.
Hence, (
8.8
) and (
8.9
) imply that

\displaystyle\delta{\bf H}_{P,M}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int(|\bm{\pi}|^{2}+|\nabla{\bf a}|^{2})\,dy+{\big\langle}\bm{\pi},(v\cdot\nabla){\bf a}{\big\rangle}\geq\frac{1}{2}\int(|\bm{\pi}|^{2}-2|v||\bm{\pi}||\nabla{\bf a}|+|\nabla{\bf a}|^{2})dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1-|v|}{2}\int(|\bm{\pi}|+|\nabla{\bf a}|)^{2}dy+\frac{|v|}{2}\int(|\bm{\pi}-\nabla{\bf a}||^{2}dy\geq\frac{1-|v|}{2}\int(|\bm{\pi}|+|\nabla{\bf a}|)^{2}dy.

∎

9
The orbital stability of solitons

Introduce the distance between two solutions
{\bf F}_{1}(y,t)
and
{\bf F}_{2}(y,t)
as

d({\bf F}_{1}(t),{\bf F}_{2}(t)):=\|{\bf F}_{1}(t)-{\bf F}_{2}(t)\|_{{\cal F}}+|\dot{q}_{1}(t)-\dot{q}_{2}(t)|+|\dot{\varphi}_{1}(t)-\dot{\varphi}_{2}(t)|,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},

(9.1)

where
\dot{q}_{j}(t)
and
\dot{\varphi}_{j}(t)
are defined by (
6.7
)–(
6.8
).
Recall that the reduced soliton (
1.8
) does not depend on time:

{\bf F}_{v,\omega}(y,t)={\bf S}_{v,\omega}(y):=({\bf A}_{v,\omega},{\bm{\Pi}}_{v,\omega}).

(9.2)

Definition 9.1
.

The soliton
{\bf S}_{v,\omega}
is orbitally stable if for any
\varepsilon>0
there is
a
\delta>0
such that
for any solution
{\bf F}
of the system (
6.11
),
the inequality

d({\bf F}(0),{\bf S}_{v,\omega})<\delta

(9.3)

implies

d({\bf F}(t),{\bf S}_{v,\omega})<\varepsilon,\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(9.4)

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
|v|<1
and let
\rho(x)
satisfy the conditions
(
1.2
)
and
(
1.3
)
.
Then the soliton
(
1.8
)
is orbitally stable.

Proof.

For the proof we develop the general scheme of
[
2
,
5
]
.
Namely, by Lemma
8.1
,
the soliton
{\bf S}_{v,\omega}
is a nondegenerate
global minimizer of the reduced Hamiltonian

{\bf H}_{P,M}
.
The inequality (
9.3
) with sufficiently small
\delta>0

implies that the linear and angular momenta
P^{*}
and
M^{*}
of the solution

{\bf F}(t)
are close to
P
and
M
, respectively:

|P^{*}-P|+|M^{*}-M|\to 0,\qquad\delta\to 0.

(9.5)

By Lemma
7.1
, for sufficiently small
\delta>0
, there exists a unique
soliton
{\bf S}_{v_{*},\omega_{*}}

with the total momentum
P^{*}
and angular momentum
M^{*}
,
which
is close to
{\bf S}_{v,\omega}
:

\|{\bf S}_{v_{*},\omega_{*}}-{\bf S}_{v,\omega}\|_{{\cal F}}\to 0,\qquad\delta\to 0.

Combining with (

), we get

\|{\bf F}(0)-{\bf S}_{v_{*},\omega_{*}}\|_{{\cal F}}\to 0,\qquad\delta\to 0.

(9.7)

Therefore,

|{\bf H}_{P_{*},M_{*}}({\bf F}(0))-{\bf H}_{P_{*},M_{*}}({\bf S}_{v_{*},\omega_{*}})|\leq\delta_{1},\qquad\delta_{1}\to 0\,\,\,{\rm as}\,\,\,\delta\to 0.

|{\bf H}_{P_{*},M_{*}}({\bf F}(t))-{\bf H}_{P_{*},M_{*}}({\bf S}_{v_{*},\omega_{*}})|\leq\delta_{1},\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

(9.9)

Therefore, the bound (
8.2
)
with

replaced by
v_{*},\omega_{*}
,
allows us to conclude that

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|{\bf F}(t)-{\bf S}_{v_{*},\omega_{*}}\|_{{\cal F}}\to 0\,\,\,{\rm as}\,\,\,\delta\to 0.

(9.10)

Finally, combined with (

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|{\bf F}(t)-{\bf S}_{v,\omega}\|_{{\cal F}}\to 0\,\,\,{\rm as}\,\,\,\delta\to 0.

(9.11)

|\dot{q}(t)-v|+|\dot{\varphi}(t)-\omega|\leq|\dot{q}(t)-v_{*}|+|\dot{\varphi}(t)-\omega_{*}|+|v_{*}-v|+|\omega_{*}-\omega|.

Indeed, the last two terms are small by (

) together with
(

)

10
The case of a relativistic particle

In this section we comment on the required modifications of our arguments in the case

(10.1)

\displaystyle\dot{\bf p}_{k}\!+\!{\big\langle}\dot{A}_{k}(q\!+\!y,t),\rho(y){\big\rangle}+{\big\langle}(\dot{q}(t)\!\cdot\!\nabla)A_{k}(q\!+\!y,t),\rho(y){\big\rangle}\!=\!{\big\langle}[\dot{q}\!-\!\dot{\varphi}Jy]\cdot\nabla_{k}A(q\!+\!y,t),\rho(y){\big\rangle},

H=\frac{1}{2}\int[\Pi_{A}^{2}\!+\!|\nabla A|^{2}]dx\!+\!\frac{m}{\sqrt{1\!-\!\dot{q}^{2}}}\!+\!\frac{I\dot{\varphi}^{2}}{2}.\qquad\qquad

(10.3)

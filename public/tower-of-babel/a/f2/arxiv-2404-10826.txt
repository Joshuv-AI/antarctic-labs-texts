d d -elliptic loci and the Torelli map
François Greer, Carl Lian

Abstract
. We show that two natural cycle classes on the moduli space of compact type stable maps to a varying elliptic curve agree. The first is the virtual fundamental class from Gromov-Witten theory, and the second is the Torelli pullback of the special cycle on 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} of principally polarized abelian varieties admitting an elliptic isogeny factor.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the moduli space of curves. This note is concerned with the loci of curves
C
admitting a cover
f:C\to E
, where
E
is an elliptic curve and
f
has degree
d
. The Hurwitz space
\mathcal{H}_{g/1,d}
of such covers can be compactified on the one hand by the space
\mathcal{A}dm_{g/1,d}
of admissible covers
[
10
,
1
]
, or alternatively by a relative moduli space of log stable maps
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}(\overline{\mathcal{E}},d)
to the fibers of the universal stable elliptic curve.

The resulting classes
[\mathcal{A}dm_{g/1,d}]\in A_{2g-2}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g})
are particularly interesting: they give cycle-valued quasi-modular forms in low genus
[
12
]
, but on the other hand are non-tautological in general
[
9
,
20
,
13
]
, so it is difficult to write down formulas for their classes. For covers of a
fixed
elliptic curve, quasi-modularity phenomena have been established in all genera
[
6
,
7
,
16
,
15
]
.

A basic observation is that if
f:C\to E
is a
d
-elliptic cover of smooth curves, then the induced map
h:E\to J(C)
on Jacobians has the property that the pullback of the principal polarization on
J(C)
has degree
d
on
E
. In this case,
J(C)
is isogenous to the product of
E
and a polarized abelian variety
Y
of dimension
g-1
. Therefore, it is natural to consider the “Noether-Lefschetz locus”
{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
of principally polarized abelian varieties along with a map of degree
d
from an elliptic curve, and its pullback under the Torelli map
{\mathsf{Tor}}:\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\ct}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
.

It is somewhat more convenient to pull back the Noether-Lefschetz loci by the
pointed
Torelli map
{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}:\mathcal{M}_{g,1}^{\ct}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
, although we discuss the unpointed case in Remark
3.15
.

.

We have a Cartesian diagram

is the moduli space of pointed stable maps

, for which

.

The statement of Theorem

has also appeared previously in the notes

.

Unlike the space of admissible covers, the space

fails to have the expected dimension of

in general. For example, if

is the stable curve obtained by gluing a curve

of genus

and an elliptic tail

at a node (away from

), then there exist stable maps

where

contracts the entirety of
C_{g-1}
to

, and restricts to an isogeny of degree

. However,

moves in a family of dimension
3g-3
.

On the other hand, the space
\mathcal{M}^{\ct,q}_{g,1}(\mathcal{E},d)
carries a virtual fundamental class obtained by Gysin pullback in the Cartesian diagram

\textstyle{\mathcal{M}_{g,1}^{\ct,q}(\mathcal{E},d)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

where

denotes the tautological section and
\mathcal{M}^{\ct}_{g,1}(\mathcal{E},d)
denotes the space of pointed stable maps which are no longer required to satisfy the property
f(p)=q

[\mathcal{M}^{\ct}_{g,1}(\mathcal{E},d)]^{\vir}

Theorem 1.2
.

We have
{\mathsf{Tor}}_{1}^{!}([{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}])=q^{!}([\mathcal{M}^{\ct}_{g,1}(\mathcal{E},d)]^{\vir})
in
A_{2g-1}(\mathcal{M}^{\ct}_{g,1}(\mathcal{E},d))
.

Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
provide a bridge between the intersection theory of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
and the Gromov-Witten theory of maps to a varying elliptic curve. A precise
connection of generating series is proposed in
[
17
]
. Projections, as defined in
[
5
]
, of the Noether-Lefschetz cycle classes have been computed by Iribar López
[
11
]
. The forthcoming work
[
11
]
also includes proofs of some of our auxiliary results, e.g. Lemma
3.4
and Proposition
3.6
. The Gromov-Witten theory of maps to a varying elliptic curve are also related to the genus 1 Gromov-Witten theory of the Hilbert scheme of points of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
by
[
14
,
18
]
.

It is natural to ask the extent to which Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 can be extended to the boundary. That is, for some compactification
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{g}
of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
admitting an extension of the Torelli map, whether the class of the closure (properly interpreted) of
{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}
pulls back to a virtual class on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{q}_{g,1}(\overline{\mathcal{E}},d)
or a closely related space. Such a statement would facilitate further calculations in Gromov-Witten theory. Formulating an analog of Theorem 1.1 at the boundary of
\mathcal{A}_{g}
already seems to be subtle. This question is currently under investigation.

2.
Moduli of stable maps and virtual classes

In this section, we construct virtual fundamental classes on spaces of stable maps to a varying elliptic curve, globalizing the usual constructions on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(E,d)
of stable maps to a fixed elliptic curve.

2.1.
Stable maps to a varying elliptic curve

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\pi:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{M}_{1,1}
be the universal family of elliptic curves with section
q:\mathcal{M}_{1,1}\to\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)
denote the space of
n
-pointed degree
d
stable maps
C\to E
, where
E
is a smooth elliptic curve. Formally, a map
S\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)
corresponds to a family of pre-stable curves
\mathcal{C}_{S}\to S
, a family of smooth elliptic curves
\mathcal{E}_{S}\to S
, and a map
f:\mathcal{C}_{S}\to\mathcal{E}_{S}
that is a pointed stable map on every fiber.

Let
\mu:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)\to\mathcal{M}_{1,1}
be the map remembering the target elliptic curve
E
. Let
\epsilon:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
be the map remembering the source curve, with unstable components contracted.

Let
\pi_{g,n}:\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)
be the universal curve, and let
f:\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)\to\mathcal{E}
be the universal stable map.

For

, let

denote the evaluation map remembering the image of the
i

\mathcal{E}
.

The fibers of
\mu
are the usual spaces of stable maps
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(E,d)
, and the maps
\epsilon,\pi_{g,n},f
similarly globalize the usual constructions.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f:(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})\to E
be a point of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)
. Then, the pointed curve
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
is stable.

Proof.

The pointed curve
(C,p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
can only fail to be stable if it contains a rational component mapping non-trivially to
E
. However, this is impossible when
E
is a smooth elliptic curve.
∎

Analogously to the absolute setting
[
2
]
, the relative virtual fundamental class
[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)]^{\vir}\in A_{2g-1+n}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d))
is constructed from a relative perfect obstruction theory

(R(\pi_{g,n})_{*}f^{*}T_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}_{1,1}})^{\vee}\to\mathbb{L}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)/(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times\mathcal{M}_{1,1})}

whose construction we recall. First, we have a map

\displaystyle f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}_{1,1}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{L}_{\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)/\mathcal{M}_{1,1}}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{L}_{\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)/(\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\times\mathcal{M}_{1,1})}

\displaystyle\cong(\pi_{g,n})^{*}\mathbb{L}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)/(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times\mathcal{M}_{1,1})},

where
\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}
is the universal curve over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Note that we have used that the diagram

\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{g,n}}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\times\mathcal{M}_{1,1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

is Cartesian, which is true by Lemma

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
with the stack of pre-stable curves

We therefore obtain

\displaystyle(R(\pi_{g,n})_{*}f^{*}T_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}_{1,1}})^{\vee}

\displaystyle=(R(\pi_{g,n})_{*}f^{*}\mathbb{L}_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}_{1,1}}^{\vee})^{\vee}

\displaystyle\to(R(\pi_{g,n})_{*}((\pi_{g,n})^{*}\mathbb{L}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathcal{E},d)/(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}\times\mathcal{M}_{1,1})})^{\vee})^{\vee}

By

to the fibers

We now construct a virtual fundamental class on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}^{q}(\mathcal{E},d)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}^{q}(\mathcal{E},d)
is obtained by truncating that of

to remove the one-dimensional deformations of

Formally, as on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}(\mathcal{E},d)
, we have a map of complexes on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{q}_{g,1}(\mathcal{E},d)

(R(\pi_{g}^{q})_{*}(f^{q})^{*}T_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}_{1,1}})^{\vee}\to\mathbb{L}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}^{q}(\mathcal{E},d)/\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}\times\mathcal{M}_{1,1}}.

We may compose on the left with the canonical truncation
\tau_{\geq-1}
to obtain a map

e_{\trunc}:(R^{1}(\pi_{g}^{q})_{*}(f^{q})^{*}T_{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{M}_{1,1}})^{\vee}[1]\to\mathbb{L}_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}^{q}(\mathcal{E},d)/\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}\times\mathcal{M}_{1,1}}

Proposition 2.5
.

The map

is a perfect obstruction theory, and thus gives rise to a

[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}^{q}(\mathcal{E},d)]^{\trunc}\in A_{2g-1}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,1}^{q}(\mathcal{E},d))

Proof.

We need to show that

is an isomorphism in degree 0 and surjection in degree
-1
. For any

, we have
H^{0}(C,f^{*}T_{E}(-p))=0
, so it follows that the map

to degree 0 is the isomorphism of zero objects, and the restriction to degree

3.
d
-elliptic maps of abelian varieties

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a principally polarized abelian variety (ppav) and
E
an elliptic curve. We say that a map

(resp. a map

) of abelian varieties has degree

(resp.

) is given by multiplication by

. We say that the map
h:X\to E
is
d
-elliptic
.

Note that
\deg(h)=\deg(h^{\vee})
. The degree of
h:E\to X
is also equal to the degree of the pullback of the principal polarization on
X
to
E
. We will pass freely between the map
h:X\to E
and its dual
h^{\vee}:E\to X
, identifying the two ppavs with their duals.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}
be the stack of maps
h:X\to E
of degree
d
, where
X
is a ppav of dimension
g
and
E
is an elliptic curve.

Abusing notation, let
\epsilon:{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
and
\mu:{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}\to\mathcal{A}_{1}
be the maps remembering the source and target, respectively.

Remark 3.3
.

The letters “NL” stand for “Noether-Lefschetz,” as the (dual) map

gives an exceptional algebraic cycle on
X
. We use the notation
{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}
to distinguish from the spaces
{\mathsf{NL}}_{g,d}
(see
[
17
]
) of
immersions

of degree

.

The moduli space

may be constructed as a closed substack of the relative Hom stack
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{X}_{g},\mathcal{X}_{1})\to\mathcal{A}_{g}\times\mathcal{A}_{1}
, where
\pi_{i}:\mathcal{X}_{i}\to\mathcal{A}_{i}
are the universal families.
Let
\mathcal{X}_{{\mathsf{NL}},g}=\mathcal{X}_{g}\times_{\mathcal{A}_{g}}{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}
be the pullback of the universal families over
{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}
, and let

Lemma 3.4
.

The morphisms
\epsilon:{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
and
(\epsilon,\mu):{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}\to\mathcal{A}_{g}\times\mathcal{A}_{1}
are proper.

Proof.

Let
t:\Spec(R)\to\mathcal{A}_{g}
be a morphism corresponding to a family
\widetilde{X}/\Spec(R)
of ppavs over a discrete valuation ring

. Let
\Frac(R)=K
be the fraction field of

, and let
\widetilde{t}:\Spec(K)\to{\widetilde{\mathsf{NL}}}_{g,d}
be a point lifting

corresponding to a

, where

Let

contains no rational curves. Then, possibly after a finite base change, the isogeny

H^{1}(B,T_{B})\otimes H^{0}(B^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{B^{\vee}})\oplus H^{0}(B,\mathcal{O}_{B})\otimes H^{1}(B^{\vee},T_{B^{\vee}})

of

is trivialized along both zero sections. Letting

be the projections of

to the two summands above, we have

.

Now, let

be the image of

under the map
\Def(B)\to\Def(A)

(t_{A},t_{B^{\vee}})\otimes c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{j})
. We have that
(t_{A},t_{B^{\vee}})
is supported on the Künneth components

H^{1}(A,T_{A})\otimes H^{0}(B^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{B^{\vee}})\oplus H^{0}(A,\mathcal{O}_{A})\otimes H^{1}(B^{\vee},T_{B^{\vee}})

, and that the projections
(c_{A}^{0},c_{A}^{1})
of

to the Künneth components

H^{0}(A,\Omega_{A})\otimes H^{1}(B^{\vee},\mathcal{O}_{B^{\vee}})\oplus H^{1}(A,\mathcal{O}_{A})\otimes H^{0}(B^{\vee},\Omega_{B^{\vee}})

of
H^{1}(A\times B^{\vee},\Omega_{A\times B^{\vee}})
are also given by pullback by
j
. By naturality of cup product, the product
t_{A}\cdot c^{0}_{A}+t_{B^{\vee}}\cdot c^{1}_{A}=0
is equal to zero. Therefore, the line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{j}
extends to the deformation
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}^{\vee}
corresponding to

, giving a deformation of

and therefore the desired natural map
\Def(j)\to\Def(B)

The composition
\Def(B)\to\Def(j)\to\Def(B)
is clearly equal to the identity by construction. Moreover, the kernel of the map
\Def(j)\to\Def(B)
is the space of deformations of
j

is an isomorphism, with inverse given by the map

∎

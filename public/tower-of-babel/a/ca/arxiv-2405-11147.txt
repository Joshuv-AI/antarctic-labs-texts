Abstract
. In this paper we extend Galbis’ elegant norm bounds for self-adjoint Toeplitz operators on the Fock space to bounded and integrable symbols which are non-radial. The main ingredients are a transplantation of the remarkable Nicola-Tilli isoperimetric inequality to the realm of Fock-Toeplitz operator theory and a two-dimensional adaption of Galbis’ integration and approximation arguments.

Primary 47B35; Secondary 30H20

1.
Introduction

Using the Hermite expansion, Galbis obtained in
[
2
]
inspiring norm bounds for self-adjoint Toeplitz operators with radial, bounded and integrable symbols on the Fock space defined on the complex plane. See also Grudsky and Vasilevski
[
4
]
for a related result. In this paper we extend Galbis’ norm bounds to the non-radial case. The main ingredient is a Gaussian isoperimetric inequality established recently by Nicola and Tilli in
[
6
]
, and for readers’ convenience we recall it as follows.

Theorem 1

(Nicola-Tilli,
[
6
]
)
.

For every
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})
with
||f||_{{\mathcal{F}}}=1
and every measurable set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
of finite measure, there holds

\int_{\Omega}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\,dA(z)\leq 1-e^{-|\Omega|},

where
|\Omega|
is the Lebesgue measure of
\Omega
.

Remark 2
.

For more recent progresses following this remarkable result (in different background geometry or its stability), we mention for example Kulikov
[
5
]
, Frank
[
1
]
, and Gómez-Guerra-Ramos-Tilli
[
3
]
(and the references therein).

In the statement of Theorem
1
, the Fock (or Bargmann-Fock) space
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})
is the Hilbert space consisting of those entire functions
f
such that

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)<+\infty,

where
dA(z)
denotes the Lebesgue measure. It is well known that
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})
admits a reproducing kernel
K(z,w)=e^{\pi\overline{w}z},
which means that

f(w)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}f(z)\overline{K(z,w)}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z),\ \ f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}).

For simplicity we denote
d\lambda(z)=e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}dA(z),
so
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}({\mathbb{C}},d\lambda).
The induced orthogonal projection

\mathbb{P}:L^{2}({\mathbb{C}},d\lambda)\to{\mathcal{F}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})

is the integral operator

\left(\mathbb{P}f\right)(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}f(w)\overline{K(w,z)}\ d\lambda(w).

For a measurable and bounded function
\varphi
on
{\mathbb{C}}
, the Toeplitz operator associated with symbol
\varphi
is defined as

T_{\varphi}(f)(z)=\mathbb{P}(\varphi f)(z)=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi(w)f(w)\overline{K(w,z)}\ d\lambda(w).

It is obvious that

T_{\varphi}:{\mathcal{F}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathcal{F}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}})

is a bounded operator and

\|T_{\varphi}(f)\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\|\varphi f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{C}},d\lambda)}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\cdot\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}.

The Toeplitz operator defined by a real valued symbol
\varphi
is self-adjoint. This can be seen via the following pairing identity

\langle T_{\varphi}(f),g\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}\varphi(z)f(z)\overline{g(z)}d\lambda(z)

for all
f,g\in{\mathcal{F}}^{2}({\mathbb{C}}).
In this case we have

(1)

\|T_{\varphi}\|=\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}=1}\left|\langle T_{\varphi}(f),f\rangle\right|\leq\sup_{\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}=1}\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}|\varphi(z)|\cdot|f(z)|^{2}d\lambda(z).

In order to estimate
||T_{\varphi}||
, we only need to estimate

\int_{{\mathbb{C}}}|\varphi(z)|\cdot|f(z)|^{2}d\lambda(z),

where
f\in\mathcal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that
||f||_{\mathcal{F}}=1
.
Using Theorem
1
of Nicola and Tilli, and adapting
[
2
, Lemma 2]
of Galbis, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\varphi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{C}})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{C}})
be a real-valued symbol. Then

(2)

\|T_{\varphi}\|\leq\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\|\varphi\|_{1}}{\|\varphi\|_{\infty}}\right)\right).

Remark 4
.

Examing the statements in Nicola and Tilli
[
6
]
, it is also transparent that the equality in (
2
) is attained by choosing
\varphi
as the indicator function of a disc with measure
\|\varphi\|_{1}
and the operator norm in (
1
) is saturated by
f(z)=K(z,w_{0})/K(w_{0},w_{0})
with
w_{0}
being the center of that disc.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

First, we prove the following simple but very useful integration lemma which is a two-dimensional adaption of
[
2
, Lemma 2]
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
(\Omega_{k})_{k=1}^{N}
be disjoint sets contained in
\mathbb{C}
with finite measure and
0\leq\varepsilon_{k}\leq 1
for every
1\leq k\leq N.
Then, for every
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that
||f||_{{\mathcal{F}}}=1
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{k}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)\leq 1-\exp\left(-\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{k}|\Omega_{k}|\right).

Proof.

We denote by
n
the number of indices
k
such that
0<\varepsilon_{k}<1
and we proceed by induction on
n.
For
n=0
, by Theorem
1
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)\leq 1-\exp\left(-\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\Omega_{k}|\right).

Let us now assume
n=1.
Let
1\leq j\leq N
be the coordinate with the property that
0<\varepsilon_{j}<1
and define function
\psi
by:

\displaystyle\psi(\varepsilon):=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\neq j}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)+\varepsilon\int_{\Omega_{j}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)

\displaystyle+\exp\left(-\sum_{k\neq j}|\Omega_{k}|-\varepsilon|\Omega_{j}|\right),\,\,\qquad 0\leq\varepsilon\leq 1.

We aim to prove
\psi(\varepsilon)\leq 1
. In fact,
\psi(0)\leq 1
and
\psi(1)\leq 1
follow from Theorem
1
. Moreover, the critical point
\varepsilon_{0}
of
\psi
satisfies

\int_{\Omega_{j}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)=|\Omega_{j}|\exp\left(-\sum_{k\neq j}|\Omega_{k}|-\varepsilon_{0}|\Omega_{j}|\right).

Hence

\displaystyle\psi(\varepsilon_{0})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq j}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)+\varepsilon_{0}|\Omega_{j}|\exp\left(-\sum_{k\neq j}|\Omega_{k}|-\varepsilon_{0}|\Omega_{j}|\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\exp\left(-\sum_{k\neq j}|\Omega_{k}|-\varepsilon_{0}|\Omega_{j}|\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\neq j}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)+\left(1+\varepsilon_{0}|\Omega_{j}|\right)\exp\left(-\sum_{k\neq j}|\Omega_{k}|-\varepsilon_{0}|\Omega_{j}|\right).

Since

1+\varepsilon_{0}|\Omega_{j}|\leq\exp\left(\varepsilon_{0}|\Omega_{j}|\right)

we conclude

\psi(\varepsilon_{0})\leq\sum_{k\neq j}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)+\exp\left(-\sum_{k\neq j}|\Omega_{k}|\right)\leq 1.

Let us assume that the lemma holds for
n=\ell
(
0\leq\ell<N
). Then we consider
n=\ell+1
and the function
\psi:[0,1]^{\ell+1}\to{\mathbb{R}}
defined by

\displaystyle\psi({\bm{\varepsilon}}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\ell+1}\varepsilon_{k}\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)+\sum_{j}\int_{\Omega_{j}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)

\displaystyle\qquad+\exp\left(-\sum_{k}\varepsilon_{k}|\Omega_{k}|-\sum_{j}|\Omega_{j}|\right)

for
{\bm{\varepsilon}}=(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{\ell+1}).
The induction hypothesis means that
\psi({\bm{\varepsilon}})\leq 1
whenever
{\bm{\varepsilon}}
is in the boundary of
[0,1]^{\ell+1}.
The lemma is proved after checking
\psi({\bm{\varepsilon}}_{0})\leq 1,
where
{\bm{\varepsilon}_{0}}
is a critical point of
\psi.
Proceeding as before,

\begin{array}[]{*2{>{\displaystyle}l}}\psi({\bm{\varepsilon}}_{0})&=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\ell+1}\varepsilon_{k}|\Omega_{k}|+1\right)e^{-\sum_{k}\varepsilon_{k}|\Omega_{k}|}e^{-\sum_{j}|\Omega_{j}|}+\sum_{j}\int_{\Omega_{j}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)\\
&\\
&\leq\exp\left(-\sum_{j}|\Omega_{j}|\right)+\sum_{j}\int_{\Omega_{j}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)\leq 1.\end{array}

By induction, we finish the proof of Lemma
5
.∎

Next we give a proof of Theorem
3
.

Proof.

After replacing
\varphi
by
\frac{\varphi}{\|\varphi\|_{\infty}}
if necessary we can assume that
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}=1.
According to (
1
), we aim to prove that

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(z)|\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)\leq 1-\exp(-||\varphi||_{1})

for every entire function
f\in\mathcal{F}^{2}(\mathbb{C})
such that
||f||_{\mathcal{F}}=1.

Let us first assume

(3)

\varphi=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varepsilon_{k}\chi_{\Omega_{k}},\ \left|\varepsilon_{k}\right|\leq 1,

where
(\Omega_{k})_{k=1}^{N}
are disjoint measurable sets in
\mathbb{C}
with finite measure. Then, Lemma
5
gives

\begin{array}[]{*2{>{\displaystyle}l}}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(z)|\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)&=\sum^{N}_{k=1}|\varepsilon_{k}|\int_{\Omega_{k}}|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)\\
&\\
&\leq 1-\exp\left(-\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\varepsilon_{k}||\Omega_{k}|\right)\\
&\\
&=1-\exp\left(-\|\varphi\|_{1}\right).\end{array}

Theorem
3
is proved for
\varphi
as in (
3
). Let us now assume that
\|\varphi\|_{\infty}=1
and
\varphi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{C}}).
Since
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{C})
, there is a sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n}
of simple functions as in (
3
) such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi_{n}(z)-\varphi(z)|\ dA(z)=0.

By triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(z)|\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi_{n}(z)|\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(z)-\varphi_{n}(z)|\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq 1-\exp\left(-\|\varphi_{n}\|_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(z)-\varphi_{n}(z)|\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z).

Because for a function
f\in\mathcal{F}^{2}
such that
||f||_{\mathcal{F}}=1
, we have

|f(z)|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\leq 1,

see for example
[
7
, Theorem 2.7]
or
[
6
, Proposition 2.1]
. So

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(z)-\varphi_{n}(z)|\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)\leq\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi_{n}(z)-\varphi(z)|\ dA(z).

Hence we obtain

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(z)|\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)\leq 1-\exp\left(-\|\varphi_{n}\|_{1}\right)+\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi_{n}(z)-\varphi(z)|\ dA(z).

Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\varphi(z)|\left|f(z)\right|^{2}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}}\ dA(z)\leq 1-\exp\left(-\|\varphi\|_{1}\right).

According to (
1
), this implies

\|T_{\varphi}\|\leq 1-\exp\left(-\|\varphi\|_{1}\right).

Thus the proof of Theorem
3
is finished.
∎

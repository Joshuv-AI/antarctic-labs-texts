Abstract
. We prove two supercongruences for specific truncated hypergeometric series. These include an uniparametric extension of a supercongruence that was recently established by Long and Ramakrishna. Our proofs involve special instances of various hypergeometric identities including Whipple’s transformation and the Karlsson–Minton summation.

1.
Introduction

Let
(a)_{n}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)
denote the Pochhammer symbol.
For complex numbers
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r}
and
b_{1},\ldots,b_{r}
,
the (generalized) hypergeometric series
{}_{r+1}F_{r}
is defined as

{}_{r+1}F_{r}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{0},&a_{1},&\ldots,&a_{r}\\[5.0pt]
&b_{1},&\ldots,&b_{r}\end{array};z\right]=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a_{0})_{k}(a_{1})_{k}\cdots(a_{r})_{k}}{k!(b_{1})_{k}\cdots(b_{r})_{k}}z^{k}.

Summation and transformation formulas for generalized hypergeometric series
play an important part in the investigation of supercongruences.
See, for instance,
[
7
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
16
,
17
,
19
]
. In particular,
Long and Ramakrishna
[
15
, Theorems 3 and 2]

proved the following two supercongruences:

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}\frac{\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)_{k}^{3}}{k!^{3}}\equiv\begin{cases}-\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{4}\pmod{p^{3}},&\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{4}$,}\\[10.0pt]
-\frac{p^{2}}{16}\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{1}{4}\right)^{4}\pmod{p^{3}},&\text{if $p\equiv 3\pmod{4}$,}\end{cases}

(1.1)

and

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(6k+1)\frac{\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{k}^{6}}{k!^{6}}\equiv\begin{cases}-p\,\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{9}\pmod{p^{6}},&\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{6}$,}\\[10.0pt]
-\frac{10}{27}p^{4}\,\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^{9}\pmod{p^{6}},&\text{if $p\equiv 5\pmod{6}$,}\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
\Gamma_{p}(x)
is the
p
-adic Gamma function.
The restriction of the supercongruence (
1.1
) modulo
p^{2}

was earlier established by Van Hamme
[
18
, Equation (H.2)]
.
The supercongruence (
1.2
) is even stronger than
a conjecure made by Van Hamme
[
18
, Equation (D.2)]

who asserted the corresponding supercongruence modulo

p^{4}
for
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
.
Long and Ramakrishna also mentioned that (
1.2
) does not hold
modulo
p^{7}
in general.

The first purpose of this paper is to prove the following supercongruence.
Note that the
r=\pm 1
cases partially confirm the
d=5
and
q\to 1
case
of
[
8
, Conjectures 1 and 2]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
r\leqslant 1
be an odd integer coprime with
5
.
Let
p
be a prime such that

p\equiv-\frac{r}{2}\pmod{5}
and
p\geqslant\frac{5-r}{2}
. Then

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(10k+r)\frac{\left(\frac{r}{5}\right)_{k}^{5}}{k!^{5}}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4}},

(1.3)

Recently, the second author
[
13
]

established the following supercongruence related to (
1.2
):

\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(6k-1)\frac{\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)_{k}^{6}}{k!^{6}}\equiv\begin{cases}\displaystyle 140p^{4}\,\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{9}\pmod{p^{5}},\quad&\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{6}$,}\\[15.0pt]
\displaystyle 378p\,\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{9}\pmod{p^{5}},\quad&\text{if $p\equiv 5\pmod{6}$,}\end{cases}

(1.4)

where
p
is a prime.

The second purpose of this paper is to give the following common generalization
of the second supercongruence in (
1.2
), restricted to modulo
p^{5}
,
and the first supercongruence in (
1.4
).

Theorem 2
.

Let
r\leqslant 1
be an integer coprime with
3
. Let
p
be
a prime such that
p\equiv-r\pmod{3}
and
p\geqslant 3-r
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(6k+r)\frac{\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)_{k}^{6}}{k!^{6}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(-1)^{r+1}\,80rp^{4}}{81}\cdot\frac{\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1+\frac{r}{3}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1+\frac{2r}{3}\right)^{3}\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1-\frac{r}{3}\right)^{4}}

\displaystyle\quad\;\times\sum_{k=0}^{1-r}\frac{(r-1)_{k}\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)_{k}^{3}}{(1)_{k}\left(\frac{2r}{3}\right)_{k}^{3}}\pmod{p^{5}}.

(1.5)

Letting
r=1
and
r=-1
in (
1.5
) and using (
1.12
) and
(
1.14
), we arrive at the
p\equiv 5\pmod{6}
case of (
1.2
)
modulo
p^{5}
and the
p\equiv 1\pmod{6}
case of (
1.4
), respectively.

Our proof of Theorem
1
will require Whipple’s well-poised

{}_{7}F_{6}
transformation formula (see
[
2
, p. 28]
):

\displaystyle{}_{7}F_{6}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}a,&1+\frac{1}{2}a,&b,&c,&d,&e,&-n\\[5.0pt]
&\frac{1}{2}a,&1+a-b,&1+a-c,&1+a-d,&1+a-e,&1+a+n\end{array};1\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(a+1)_{n}(a-d-e+1)_{n}}{(a-d+1)_{n}(a-e+1)_{n}}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}1+a-b-c,&d,&e,&-n\\[5.0pt]
d+e-a-n,&1+a-b,&1+a-c&\end{array};1\right],

where
n
is a non-negative integer, and Karlsson–Minton’s summation
formula (see, for example,
[
4
, Equation (1.9.2)]
):

{}_{r+1}F_{r}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}-n,&b_{1}+m_{1},&\ldots,&b_{r}+m_{r}\\[5.0pt]
&b_{1},&\ldots,&b_{r}\end{array};1\right]=0,

(1.10)

where
n,m_{1},\ldots,m_{r}
are non-negative integers and
n>m_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}
.
Our proof of Theorem
2
relies on a
{}_{7}F_{6}
transformation formula
slightly different from Whipple’s
{}_{7}F_{6}
transformation formula
(
1
), obtained as a result from combining (
1
)

transformation formula. The transformation was already utilized by the second author to prove
(

).

Furthermore, in order to prove Theorem
2
,
we require some properties of the
p
-adic Gamma function,
collected in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

[
3
, Section 11.6]

Suppose
p
is an odd prime and
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Then

\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}(0)=1,\quad\Gamma_{p}(1)=-1,

(1.11)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}(x)\Gamma_{p}(1-x)=(-1)^{a_{p}(x)},

(1.12)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{p}(x)\equiv\Gamma_{p}(y)\pmod{p}\quad\text{for $x\equiv y\pmod{p}$},

(1.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma_{p}(x+1)}{\Gamma_{p}(x)}=\begin{cases}-x\quad&\text{if $v_{p}(x)=0$,}\\
-1\quad&\text{if $v_{p}(x)>0$, }\end{cases}

(1.14)

where
a_{p}(x)\in\{1,2,\dots,p\}
with
x\equiv a_{p}(x)\pmod{p}
and

v_{p}(\cdot)
denotes the
p
-order.

Lemma 2
.

[
15
, Lemma 17, (4)]

Let
p
be an odd prime. If
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},n\in\mathbb{N}

such that none of
a,a+1,\dots,a+n-1
are in
p\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, then

(a)_{n}=(-1)^{n}\frac{\Gamma_{p}(a+n)}{\Gamma_{p}(a)}.

(1.15)

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

Motivated by the work of McCarthy and Osburn
[
16
]
and
Mortenson
[
17
]
, we take the following choice of parameters
in (
1
).
Let
a=\frac{r}{5}
,
b=\frac{r+5}{10}
,
c=\frac{r+3p}{5}
,

d=\frac{r+3ip}{5}
,
e=\frac{r-3ip}{5}
, and
n=\frac{3p-r}{5}
,
where
i^{2}=-1
. Then we conclude that

\displaystyle{}_{6}F_{5}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}\frac{r}{5},&1+\frac{r}{10},&\frac{r+3p}{5},&\frac{r+3ip}{5},&\frac{r-3ip}{5},&\frac{r-3p}{5}\\[5.0pt]
&\frac{r}{10},&1-\frac{3p}{5},&1-\frac{3ip}{5},&1+\frac{3ip}{5},&1+\frac{3p}{5}\end{array};1\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1+\frac{r}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}\left(1-\frac{r}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}}{\left(1-\frac{3ip}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}\left(1+\frac{3ip}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}}\,{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{5-r-6p}{10},&\frac{r+3ip}{5},&\frac{r-3ip}{5},&\frac{r-3p}{5}\\[5.0pt]
\frac{2r-3p}{5},&\frac{r+5}{10},&\frac{5-3p}{5}\end{array};1\right],

It is easy to see that, for
k\geqslant 0
and any
p
-adic integer
b
,

\left(a+bp\right)_{k}\left(a-bp\right)_{k}\left(a+bip\right)_{k}\left(a-bip\right)_{k}\equiv(a)_{k}^{4}\pmod{p^{4}}.

(2.5)

Hence, the left-hand side of (
2
) is congruent to

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{3p-r}{5}}\frac{\left(1+\frac{r}{10}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{r}{5}\right)_{k}^{5}}{\left(\frac{r}{10}\right)_{k}(1)_{k}^{5}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k=0}^{\frac{3p-r}{5}}(10k+r)\frac{\left(\frac{r}{5}\right)_{k}^{5}}{k!^{5}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{r}\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(10k+r)\frac{\left(\frac{r}{5}\right)_{k}^{5}}{k!^{5}}\pmod{p^{4}},

where we have used the fact that

\frac{\left(\frac{r}{5}\right)_{k}}{k!}\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for

\frac{3p-r}{5}<k\leqslant p-1
(the condition
p\geqslant\frac{5-r}{2}

in the theorem is to guarantee
\frac{3p-r}{5}\leqslant p-1
).
Since
\frac{3p-r}{5}\geqslant\frac{2p+r}{5}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1+\frac{r}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}\left(1-\frac{r}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}}{\left(1-\frac{3ip}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}\left(1+\frac{3ip}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1+\frac{r}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}\left(1-\frac{r}{5}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}}{\left(1+\frac{9p^{2}}{25}\right)_{\frac{3p-r}{5}}}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{p^{2}}.

Finally, by the congruences

\left(a+bip\right)_{k}\left(a-bip\right)_{k}\equiv\left(a+bp\right)_{k}\left(a-bp\right)_{k}\equiv(a)_{k}^{2}\pmod{p^{2}}

(2.6)

for any
p
-adic integer
b
, we obtain

\displaystyle{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{5-r-6p}{10},&\frac{r+3ip}{5},&\frac{r-3ip}{5},&\frac{r-3p}{5}\\[5.0pt]
\frac{2r-3p}{5},&\frac{r+5}{10},&\frac{5-3p}{5}\end{array};1\right]

\displaystyle\equiv{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{5-r-6p}{10},&\frac{r}{5},&\frac{r}{5},&\frac{r-3p}{5}\\[5.0pt]
\frac{2r-3p}{5},&\frac{r+5}{10},&\frac{5-3p}{5}\end{array};1\right]

\displaystyle\equiv{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{5-r-6p}{10},&\frac{r+p}{5},&\frac{r-p}{5},&\frac{r-3p}{5}\\[5.0pt]
\frac{2r-3p}{5},&\frac{r+5}{10},&\frac{5-3p}{5}\end{array};1\right]

\displaystyle=0\pmod{p^{2}},

where we have utilized Karlsson–Minton’s summation (
1.10
) with

n=\frac{3p-r}{5}
,
b_{1}=\frac{2r-3p}{5}
,
b_{2}=\frac{r+5}{10}
,

b_{3}=\frac{5-3p}{5}
,
m_{1}=\frac{1-r}{2}
,
m_{2}=\frac{2p+r-5}{10}
,
and
m_{3}=\frac{2p+r-5}{5}
in the last step.

Proof of Theorem
2

We can verify (
1.5
) for
r=1
and
p=2
by hand. In what follows,
we assume that
p
is an odd prime. Recall the following transformation
formula
[
13

\displaystyle{}_{7}F_{6}\!\left[\begin{matrix}t,&1+\frac{t}{2},&-n,&t-a,&t-b,&t-c,&1-t-m+n+a+b+c\\[5.0pt]
&\frac{t}{2},&1+t+n,&1+a,&1+b,&1+c,&2t+m-n-a-b-c\end{matrix};1\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{(1+t)_{n}(a+b+2-m-t)_{n}(a+c+2-m-t)_{n}(b+c+2-m-t)_{n}}{(1+a)_{n}(1+b)_{n}(1+c)_{n}(a+b+c+1-m-2t)_{n}}

\displaystyle\quad\;\times\frac{(a+b+1-m-t)(a+c+1-m-t)(b+c+1-m-t)}{(a+b+n+1-m-t)(a+c+n+1-m-t)(b+c+n+1-m-t)}

\displaystyle\quad\;\times{}_{4}F_{3}\!\left[\begin{matrix}-m,&\hskip-25.60747pt-n,&\hskip-25.60747pta+b+c+1-m-2t,&a+b+c+1+n-m-t\\[5.0pt]
&\hskip-28.45274pta+b+1-m-t,&a+c+1-m-t,&b+c+1-m-t\end{matrix};1\right].

(3.1)

Let
\zeta
be a fifth primitive root of unity.
Setting
m=1-r
,
t=\frac{r}{3}
,
n=\frac{2p-r}{3}
,
a=\frac{2p\zeta}{3}
,

b=\frac{2p\zeta^{2}}{3}
and

in (
3.1

3.1
) becomes

\displaystyle{}_{7}F_{6}\!\left[\begin{matrix}1+\frac{r}{6},&\frac{r}{3},&\frac{r-2p}{3},&\frac{r-2p\zeta}{3},&\frac{r-2p\zeta^{2}}{3},&\frac{r-2p\zeta^{3}}{3},&\frac{r-2p\zeta^{4}}{3}\\[5.0pt]
&\frac{r}{6},&1+\frac{2p}{3},&1+\frac{2p\zeta}{3},&1+\frac{2p\zeta^{2}}{3},&1+\frac{2p\zeta^{3}}{3},&1+\frac{2p\zeta^{4}}{3}\end{matrix};1\right]

p

\displaystyle\left(u+vp\right)_{k}\left(u+vp\zeta\right)_{k}\left(u+vp\zeta^{2}\right)_{k}\left(u+vp\zeta^{3}\right)_{k}\left(u+vp\zeta^{4}\right)_{k}\equiv(u)_{k}^{5}\pmod{p^{5}}.

\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)_{k}\equiv 0\pmod{p}

\displaystyle{}_{7}F_{6}\!\left[\begin{matrix}1+\frac{r}{6},&\frac{r}{3},&\frac{r-2p}{3},&\frac{r-2p\zeta}{3},&\frac{r-2p\zeta^{2}}{3},&\frac{r-2p\zeta^{3}}{3},&\frac{r-2p\zeta^{4}}{3}\\[5.0pt]

(3.2)

On the other hand, we determine the terminating hypergeometric series

\displaystyle\frac{(a+b+1-m-t)(a+c+1-m-t)(b+c+1-m-t)}{(a+b+n+1-m-t)(a+c+n+1-m-t)(b+c+n+1-m-t)}

\displaystyle\equiv 8\sum_{k=0}^{1-r}\frac{(r-1)_{k}\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)_{k}^{3}}{(1)_{k}\left(\frac{2r}{3}\right)_{k}^{3}}\pmod{p}.

(3.3)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{(1+t)_{n}(a+b+2-m-t)_{n}(a+c+2-m-t)_{n}(b+c+2-m-t)_{n}}{(1+a)_{n}(1+b)_{n}(1+c)_{n}(a+b+c+1-m-2t)_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1+\frac{r}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2r+2p(\zeta+\zeta^{2})}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2r+2p(\zeta+\zeta^{3})}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2r+2p(\zeta^{2}+\zeta^{3})}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}}{(-1)^{\frac{2p-r}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2p\zeta}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2p\zeta^{2}}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2p\zeta^{3}}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2p\zeta^{4}}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}}.

(3.5)

and

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{2r+2p(\zeta+\zeta^{2})}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2r+2p(\zeta+\zeta^{3})}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2r+2p(\zeta^{2}+\zeta^{3})}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{5p^{3}}{27}\left(1+\frac{2r+2p(\zeta+\zeta^{2})}{3}\right)_{\frac{p-2r-3}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2r+2p(\zeta+\zeta^{3})}{3}\right)_{\frac{p-2r-3}{3}}

\displaystyle\quad\;\times\left(1+\frac{2r+2p(\zeta^{2}+\zeta^{3})}{3}\right)_{\frac{p-2r-3}{3}}\left(\frac{3+p(2\zeta+2\zeta^{2}+1)}{3}\right)_{\frac{p+r}{3}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(-1)^{\frac{2p-r}{3}}10p^{4}}{81}\cdot\frac{\left(1+\frac{r}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r-3}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2r}{3}\right)^{3}_{\frac{p-2r-3}{3}}(1)^{3}_{\frac{p+r}{3}}}{(1)^{4}_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}}\pmod{p^{5}}.

(3.7)

It follows from (

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{p-1}(6k+r)\frac{\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)_{k}^{6}}{k!^{6}}

\displaystyle\quad\;\times\sum_{k=0}^{1-r}\frac{(r-1)_{k}\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)_{k}^{3}}{(1)_{k}\left(\frac{2r}{3}\right)_{k}^{3}}\pmod{p^{5}}.

By Lemmas

\displaystyle\frac{\left(1+\frac{r}{3}\right)_{\frac{2p-r-3}{3}}\left(1+\frac{2r}{3}\right)^{3}_{\frac{p-2r-3}{3}}(1)^{3}_{\frac{p+r}{3}}}{(1)^{4}_{\frac{2p-r}{3}}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{bb-6}}{=}\frac{(-1)^{\frac{2p-r}{3}+r}\,\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{2p}{3}\right)\Gamma_{p}\!\left(\frac{p}{3}\right)^{3}\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1+\frac{p+r}{3}\right)^{3}\Gamma_{p}(1)}{\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1+\frac{r}{3}\right)\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1+\frac{2r}{3}\right)^{3}\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1+\frac{2p-r}{3}\right)^{4}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{bb-3}}{\equiv}\frac{(-1)^{\frac{2p-r}{3}+r}\,\Gamma_{p}\!\left(0\right)^{4}\Gamma_{p}\left(1+\frac{r}{3}\right)^{2}\Gamma_{p}(1)}{\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1+\frac{2r}{3}\right)^{3}\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1-\frac{r}{3}\right)^{4}}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{bb-1}}{=}\frac{(-1)^{\frac{2p-r}{3}+r+1}\,\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1+\frac{r}{3}\right)^{2}}{\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1+\frac{2r}{3}\right)^{3}\Gamma_{p}\!\left(1-\frac{r}{3}\right)^{4}}.

(3.9)

The proof of (
1.5
) then follows from (
3.8
) and (
3.9
).

p

Conjecture 1
.

\displaystyle\quad\;\times\sum_{k=0}^{1-r}\frac{(r-1)_{k}\left(\frac{r}{3}\right)_{k}^{3}}{(1)_{k}\left(\frac{2r}{3}\right)_{k}^{3}}\pmod{p^{6}}.

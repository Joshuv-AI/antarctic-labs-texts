Correlated two-photon scattering in a one-dimensional waveguide coupled to two- or three-level giant atoms Preprint: APS/123-QED
Wenju Gu, He Huang, Zhen Yi, Lei Chen, Lihui Sun, Huatang Tan

Abstract
We investigate the two-photon scattering processes in a one-dimensional waveguide coupled to either a two-level or three-level giant atom. By manipulating the accumulated phase shift between the two coupling points, we are able to effectively modify the characteristics of these scattering processes. Utilizing the Lippmann-Schwinger formalism, we obtain the exact two-photon interacting scattering wavefunctions of these two systems. Additionally, analytical expressions for the incoherent power spectra and second-order correlations are derived. The incoherent spectrum, which is defined by the correlation of the bound state, provides valuable insights into photon-photon correlations. It serves as a useful indicator of the degree of photon-photon correlation between scattered photons. Furthermore, the second-order correlation function gives a direct measure of the photon-photon correlation. For photons scattered by the two-level giant atom, manipulating the accumulated phase shift allows for improvement of the photon-photon correlation and adjustment of the evolution of the second-order correlation. In the case of the three-level giant atom, the photon-photon correlation can be substantially increased. The photon-photon interaction and correlation distance of the scattered photons can be further enhanced by tuning the accumulated phase shift. Moreover, the statistical properties can be adjusted by the control field.

Introduction

Exploiting atom-photon interactions plays a critical role in the emerging field of waveguide quantum electrodynamics (QED)
Roy et al. 2017
;
Gu et al. 2017
;
Sheremet et al. 2023
;
Forn-Díaz et al. 2017
, which opens up novel opportunities for both fundamental physics
Chang et al. 2014
;
Shi et al. 2018
;
Fayard et al. 2021
and quantum information processing
Kannan et al. 2023
;
González-Tudela et al. 2015
;
Forn-Díaz et al. 2017
. The conventional atom-photon interaction has been studied within the small atom regime, where the interaction is localized due to the presence of atoms in a region significantly smaller than the wavelength of photons. However, recent investigations have focused on a new paradigm known as “giant atoms”, which exhibits nonlocal couplings. This has been achieved by coupling artificial atoms to propagating fields (e.g., surface acoustic waves) with wavelengths smaller than atomic sizes
Andersson et al. 2019
, or through meandering waveguides at separated points
Kannan et al. 2020
;
Vadiraj et al. 2021
. The introduction of nonlocal interactions enables the occurrence of various remarkable phenomena that are not achievable in the case of small atoms. These include frequency-dependent Lamb shifts and relaxation rates
Frisk Kockum et al. 2014
, waveguide-mediated decoherence-free subspaces
Kockum et al. 2018
;
Carollo et al. 2020
, nonexponential decay
Andersson et al. 2019
;
Longhi 2020
;
Guo et al. 2020a
;
Qiu et al. 2023
, oscillating bound states
Guo et al. 2020b
, and the enhanced spontaneous sudden birth of entanglement
Santos and Bachelard 2023
.

In waveguide QED systems, the presence of a one-dimensional (1D) continuum of modes allows for a broader range of field bandwidths and facilitates strong coupling to local emitters by reducing the mode volume
Roy et al. 2017
. The study of few-photon scattering is well-established in the strong-coupling regime, where the interaction between light and the emitter dominates over loss and dephasing effects. Various theoretical techniques have been developed to investigate few-photon scattering in the context of small atoms. These techniques include the real-space Bethe-ansatz method
Shen and Fan 2005a
;
Shen and Fan 2005b
;
Roy 2010
;
Roy 2011a
;
Roy 2013
, the Lehmann-Symanzik-Zimmermann reduction
Shi and Sun 2009
;
Shi et al. 2011
, the wave-packet evolution approach
Liao and Law 2010
;
Liao and Law 2013
, the Lippmann-Schwinger (LS) formalism
Zheng and Baranger 2013
;
Fang et al. 2014
;
Roy 2011b
, input-output formalism
Fan et al. 2010
;
Rephaeli et al. 2011
, as well as the Dyson series summation
Hurst and Kok 2018
.

The phenomenon of photon scattering in waveguide QED systems involving giant atoms has gained significant attention due to the unique interference effects
Zhao and Wang 2020
;
Feng and Jia 2021
;
Du et al. 2021
;
Yin et al. 2022
;
Cheng et al. 2022
;
Chen et al. 2022
;
Zhao et al. 2022
. It is worth noting that the majority of studies in this area primarily focus on single-photon interactions, with relatively limited exploration of few-photon scattering within the giant atom regime. The presence of nonlinearity in atomic systems would induce photon-photon correlations, and such photon-photon correlations can, in turn, influence the transmission and reflection of photons through phenomena like induced tunneling or blocking of photons
Zheng et al. 2012
;
Yi et al. 2023
;
Gu et al. 2022
;
Tang et al. 2020
. Therefore, gaining a clear understanding of the nature of photon-photon correlations is crucial to comprehending the functioning of various quantum devices, such as a switchable mirror
Redchenko et al. 2023
or a single-photon router
Hoi et al. 2012
. In the context of few-photon scattering, the wavefunctions typically exhibit a common structure: the two-particle plane wave with momenta of photons rearranged and the bound state. The plane-wave component arises from the coherent scattering, while the bound state component originates from the incoherent scattering. The bound state decays exponentially as the distance between the two photons increases, and is associated with the two-particle irreducible T-matrix in scattering theory
Xu et al. 2013
. Recently, the eigenstates of the scattering matrix have been successfully obtained for the even mode in a two-level giant system using a reasonable wavefunction hypothesis
Cheng et al. 2023
.

In this paper, we employ the LS formalism to analyze the two-photon scattering processes involving both two- and three-level giant atoms coupled to a 1D waveguide. By utilizing this approach, we are able to obtain the analytical two-photon interacting scattering wavefunctions for these systems. Additionally, the incoherent power spectrum is derived from the correlation of the bound state, with the total flux serving as an indicator of photon-photon correlation. The second-order correlation function provides a direct measure of photon-photon correlation. Through our analysis, we find that the accumulated phase shift can be utilized to enhance the photon-photon correlation and control the evolution of the second-order correlation for photons scattered by the two-level giant atom. In comparison to the two-level giant atom, the photon-photon correlation can be substantially enhanced for the system of the three-level giant atom. Moreover, we find that tuning the accumulated phase shift between the two coupling points allows for further improvement of photon-photon interaction and an increase in the decay distance of the second-order correlation. These properties can be explained by the poles of these systems. Furthermore, the statistics property of the scattering photons in the three-level giant atom system can be controlled by adjusting the control laser at the resonance condition. In the presence of the control field, the incident photons pass by the system coherently, thereby maintaining unchanged statistics. However, in the absence of the control field, the system effectively behaves as a two-level model, resulting in bunched transmitted photons and antibunched reflected photons.

The paper is organized as follows. In Sec.

We investigate various aspects, including the derivation of the two-photon interacting scattering eigenstate, the incoherent power spectrum, and the second-order correlation function. In Sec.
III
, we extend our analysis to the case of the three-level giant atom, where we examine similar quantities such as the two-photon interacting scattering eigenstate, the incoherent power spectrum, and the second-order correlation function. The conclusions drawn from our study are given in Sec.
IV
. Additionally, a summary of technical details relevant to the calculation of the incoherent power spectrum is presented in Appendix.

;

\hbar=1

\displaystyle\hat{H}=

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{0}-i\gamma_{e}/2\right)\hat{\sigma}_{ee}

\displaystyle-i\nu_{g}\int dx\left[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{R}(x)\partial_{x}\hat{a}_{R}(x)-\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{L}(x)\partial_{x}\hat{a}_{L}(x)\right]

\displaystyle+\frac{V}{2}\sum_{\alpha=R,L}\int dxM(x)\left[\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x)\hat{\sigma}^{-}+\hat{\sigma}^{+}\hat{a}_{\alpha}(x)\right].

(1)

Here

denotes the coupling points. The operators

The total excitation number is conserved for the Jaynes-Cummings model of interaction between the atom and waveguide field. Thus, in the single-excitation subspace, the eigenstate of the system can be written in the form

\displaystyle\left|{\phi_{1}(k)}\right\rangle_{\alpha}=

\displaystyle\int dx\left[\phi_{R}^{\alpha}(k,x)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{R}(x)+\phi_{L}^{\alpha}(k,x)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{L}(x)\right]\left|{0,g}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+u_{e}^{\alpha}(k)\left|{0,e}\right\rangle,

(2)

\alpha=\{R,L\}

k

\alpha

is the excitation amplitude of the atom, and
\left|{0,g}\right\rangle
denotes the vacuum state of the system. The probability amplitudes can be determined by the Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\left(\omega_{0}-k\right)u_{e}^{\alpha}(k)+\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma}{2}}\sum_{\alpha^{\prime}=R,L}\int dxM(x)\phi_{\alpha^{\prime}}^{\alpha}(k,x)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(-i\partial_{x}-k)\phi_{R}^{\alpha}(k,x)+\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma}{2}}M(x)u_{e}^{\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle(i\partial_{x}-k)\phi_{L}^{\alpha}(k,x)+\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma}{2}}M(x)u_{e}^{\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=0.

\displaystyle\phi_{R}^{R}(k,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{ikx}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\big\{\theta(-d/2-x)+t_{1}(k)\big[\theta(x+d/2)

\displaystyle-\theta(x-d/2)\big]+t_{2}(k)\theta(x-d/2)\big\},

\displaystyle\phi_{L}^{R}(k,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-ikx}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\big\{r_{1}(k)\theta(-d/2-x)

\displaystyle+r_{2}(k)\left[\theta(x+d/2)-\theta(x-d/2)\right]\big\},

\displaystyle\phi_{R}^{L}(k,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{ikx}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\big\{r_{1}(k)\theta(x-d/2)

\displaystyle+r_{2}(k)\left[\theta(x+d/2)-\theta(x-d/2)\right]\big\},

\displaystyle\phi_{L}^{L}(k,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-ikx}}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\big\{\theta(x-d/2)+t_{1}(k)\big[\theta(x+d/2)

\displaystyle-\theta(x-d/2)\big]+t_{2}(k)\theta(-d/2-x)\big\},

(4)

where

\displaystyle t_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{0}-k-i\Gamma/2\left(1+e^{ikd}\right)}{\omega_{0}-k-i\Gamma\left(1+e^{ikd}\right)},

\displaystyle t_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{0}-k+\Gamma\sin kd}{\omega_{0}-k-i\Gamma\left(1+e^{ikd}\right)},

\displaystyle r_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{i\Gamma\left(1+\cos kd\right)}{\omega_{0}-k-i\Gamma\left(1+e^{ikd}\right)},

\displaystyle r_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{i\Gamma/2\left(1+e^{ikd}\right)}{\omega_{0}-k-i\Gamma\left(1+e^{ikd}\right)},

\displaystyle u_{e}^{\alpha}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{-\sqrt{\Gamma/\pi}\cos(kd/2)}{\omega_{0}-k-i\Gamma\left(1+e^{ikd}\right)}.

(5)

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle I_{2}^{x}\otimes\left|{0}\right\rangle\left\langle{0}\right|+I_{1}^{x}\otimes\left|{d}\right\rangle\left\langle{d}\right|+I_{0}^{x}\otimes\left|{dd}\right\rangle\left\langle{dd}\right|,

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}\int dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}\left|{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{n}}\left\langle{x_{1}\cdots x_{n}}\right|,

\displaystyle\left|{\psi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left|{\phi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}+G^{R}(E)VI_{2}\left|{\psi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left|{\phi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}+UG^{R}(E)\left|{dd}\right\rangle\langle{dd}\left|{\psi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}.

\displaystyle\langle{dd}\left|{\psi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}=

\displaystyle\left(1-UG_{dd}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\times\langle{dd}\left|{\phi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}},

(11)

where
G_{dd}=\left\langle{dd}\right|G^{R}(E)\left|{dd}\right\rangle

{}_{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}\left\langle{x_{1}x_{2}}\right|

{}_{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}\langle x_{1}x_{2}\left|{\psi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}=_{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}\langle x_{1}x_{2}\left|{\phi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}

(12)

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}\int dk_{1}dk_{2}\left|{\phi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\left\langle{\phi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right|.

(13)

\displaystyle G_{dd}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}\int dk_{1}dk_{2}\frac{\langle dd\left|{\phi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}\left\langle{\phi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right|dd\rangle}{E-k_{1}-k_{2}+i0^{+}},

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\phi_{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}}^{\alpha_{1}}(k_{1},x_{1})\phi_{\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}^{\alpha_{2}}(k_{2},x_{2})

\displaystyle+\phi_{\alpha_{1}^{\prime}}^{\alpha_{2}}(k_{2},x_{1})\phi_{\alpha_{2}^{\prime}}^{\alpha_{1}}(k_{1},x_{2})\Big].

When the propagating time of photons between the coupling points is shorter than the atomic lifetime

\displaystyle G_{dd}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{E-2\omega_{0}+i2\Gamma^{\prime}},

\displaystyle G_{xd}^{RR}(x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{-\Gamma(1+\cos\vartheta)}{E-2\omega_{0}+i2\Gamma^{\prime}}e^{iEx_{c}}e^{i(E/2-\omega_{0})\left|{x}\right|-\Gamma^{\prime}\left|{x}\right|},

\displaystyle f_{RR}(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle f_{LL}(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-iEx_{c}}}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\left[r_{1}(k_{1})r_{1}(k_{2})\cos\Delta_{1}x+B_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x)\right],

\displaystyle f_{RL}(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{iEx/2}}{2\pi}\Big[t_{2}(k_{1})r_{1}(k_{2})e^{2i\Delta_{1}x_{c}}

\displaystyle+r_{1}(k_{1})t_{2}(k_{2})e^{-2i\Delta_{1}x_{c}}+2B_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x_{c})\Big],

\displaystyle B_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x)=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1+\cos\vartheta)^{2}e^{i(E/2-\omega_{0})|x|-\Gamma^{\prime}|x|}}{\left(E/2-\omega_{0}+i\Gamma^{\prime}\right)^{2}-\Delta_{1}^{2}},

(18)

where

corresponds to half of the energy difference between two incident photons. For the case of
\vartheta=0
, the interacting eigenstate can be simplified to the same form as that of the small two-level atom scenario with a single coupling point in Ref.
Shen and Fan 2007
by replacing
\Gamma
with
\Gamma/4
. The factor
4
arises from the constructive interference between two coupling points.

For the input of the two-particle plane wave, the scattering wavefunction exhibits a common constituent: the two-particle plane wave with rearranged momenta of the photons and the bound state. The plane wave originates from the coherent scattering, while the bound state originates from the incoherent scattering. As the distance between the two photons increases, the bound state decays exponentially. The bound state can also be referred to as the two-particle irreducible T-matrix in scattering theory
Xu et al. 2013
.

II.1
Incoherent power spectrum

The two-photon interacting eigenstate is composed of two components: the plane wave arising from coherent scattering, and the bound state arising from photon-photon interactions. To investigate their impacts on scattering processes, we first consider the power spectrum or resonance fluorescence, which is the Fourier transform of the first-order correlation function,

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}(\omega)=\int dte^{-i\omega t}\left\langle{\psi_{2}}\right|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x_{0})\hat{a}_{\alpha}(x_{0}+t)\left|{\psi_{2}}\right\rangle,

(19)

where
x_{0}
is the position of the detector located far away from the scattering region. Here
S_{\alpha}(\omega)
accounts for the spectral decomposition of the photons in the two-photon interacting wavefunction
\left|{\psi_{2}}\right\rangle
. The detailed derivation of the power spectrum is presented in Appendix A. In general, the power spectrum consists of the coherent and incoherent parts, i.e.,
S_{\alpha}(\omega)=S_{\alpha}^{\text{coh}}(\omega)+S_{\alpha}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)
. The coherent scattering component contributes a
\delta
-function, while the incoherent scattering component is determined by the correlation of the bound state in the wavefunction, as shown in Eq. (
53

To explore the physical implications of the incoherent power spectrum, we consider the case where
\alpha=R
, specifically focusing on the two-photon transmission state. In this case, the incoherent power spectrum can be expressed in the form

\displaystyle S_{R}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\left|{\Upsilon(\omega)}\right|^{2},

(20)

where

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\omega)=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1+\cos\vartheta)^{2}}{(E/2-\omega_{0}+i\Gamma^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(E-\omega_{0}-\omega+i\Gamma^{\prime})(\omega-\omega_{0}+i\Gamma^{\prime})}.

(21)

This expression is provided in Appendix A. When transforming the transmitted state from real space to frequency space, it can be represented as
t_{2}(\omega_{1})t_{2}(\omega_{2})\hat{a}_{R}^{\dagger}(\omega_{1})\hat{a}_{R}^{\dagger}(\omega_{2})\left|{0}\right\rangle+\frac{i}{\pi}\int d\omega\Upsilon(\omega)\hat{a}_{R}^{\dagger}(E-\omega)\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{R}(\omega)\left|{0}\right\rangle
.
The first term describes the independent propagation of the two photons, while the second term represents the final state of the two photons after undergoing inelastic scattering. According to the principle of energy conservation, the two scattered photons are always generated in pairs with frequencies of opposite signs. The coefficient
\Upsilon(\omega)
quantifies the generation of these photon pairs. Therefore, the incoherent power spectrum serves as a direct measure of the production of photon pairs with a frequency of
\omega
.

Furthermore, the total incoherent power spectrum is defined by

\displaystyle F(k)=

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha}\int d\omega S_{\alpha}^{\text{incoherent}}(\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\pi}\int dxB_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{*}(x)B_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x).

(22)

It serves as a quantitative measure for the overall strength of correlations, and also provides a direct measure of the bound-state term. Under the assumption of a narrow bandwidth of incident photons, where the spectral width of the wave packet is significantly smaller than
\Gamma
, the wave packet approaches to a
\delta
function. This implies that the incident photons have the equal frequency
k_{1}=k_{2}=k
. In this case, the expression for the total incoherent power spectrum can be simplified as

\displaystyle F(k)=\frac{4\Gamma^{3}(1+\cos\vartheta)^{3}}{\pi\left|{k-\omega_{0}+i\Gamma^{\prime}}\right|^{4}}=\frac{4}{\pi}\frac{\left|{B_{k_{1}k_{2}}(0)}\right|^{2}}{\Gamma(1+\cos\vartheta)},

(23)

which is consistent with the single coupling point case (by replacing
\Gamma
with
\Gamma/4
) when
\vartheta=0

Fang and Baranger 2015
.

Second-order correlation function

Next, we utilize the second-order correlation function to demonstrate the spatial interaction between photons

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[2\omega_{0}-\Delta-i\Gamma^{\prime}+\sqrt{(-\Delta+i\Gamma^{\prime})^{2}+\Omega^{2}}\Big],

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[2\omega_{0}-\Delta-i\Gamma^{\prime}-\sqrt{(-\Delta+i\Gamma^{\prime})^{2}+\Omega^{2}}\Big],

(39)

with
\Gamma^{\prime}=\Gamma(1+e^{i\vartheta})
. Furthermore, the functions
f_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
and
f_{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
are defined as

\displaystyle f_{1}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=

\displaystyle(2\lambda_{1}-E)(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{1}^{*})^{2}(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}^{*})^{2},

\displaystyle f_{2}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})=

\displaystyle(E-\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{1}^{*})(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{2}^{*})\left|{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}^{*}}\right|^{2}.

(40)

During the contour integration, we utilize the conditions
x_{1}>d/2
and
x_{2}>d/2
, and define
x=x_{1}-x_{2}
and
x_{c}=(x_{1}+x_{2})/2
. It can also be demonstrated that
G_{i}^{LL}(-x_{1},-x_{2})=G_{i}^{RL}(x_{1},-x_{2})=G_{i}^{LR}(-x_{1},x_{2})=G_{i}^{RR}(x_{1},x_{2})
for
i=1,2,3
, owing to the parity symmetry. Finally, the expression for the two-photon interacting eigenstate becomes

\displaystyle\left|{\psi_{2}(k_{1},k_{2})}\right\rangle_{RR}=

\displaystyle\int dx_{1}dx_{2}\Bigg[\frac{f_{RR}^{(\Lambda)}(x_{1},x_{2})}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{a}_{R}^{\dagger}(x_{1})\hat{a}_{R}^{\dagger}(x_{2})

\displaystyle+\frac{f_{LL}^{(\Lambda)}(x_{1},x_{2})}{\sqrt{2}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{L}(x_{1})\hat{a}_{L}^{\dagger}(x_{2})

\displaystyle+f_{RL}^{(\Lambda)}(x_{1},x_{2})\hat{a}_{R}^{\dagger}(x_{1})\hat{a}_{L}^{\dagger}(x_{2})\Bigg]\left|{0}\right\rangle.

(41)

The coefficients can be written as the sum of the plane-wave and bound-state terms

\displaystyle f_{RR}^{(\Lambda)}(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{iEx_{c}}}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\left[t_{2}(k_{1})t_{2}(k_{2})\cos\Delta_{1}x+B^{(2)}_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x)\right],

\displaystyle f_{LL}^{(\Lambda)}(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-iEx_{c}}}{\sqrt{2}\pi}\left[r_{1}(k_{1})r_{1}(k_{2})\cos\Delta_{1}x+B^{(2)}_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x)\right],

\displaystyle f_{RL}^{(\Lambda)}(x_{1},x_{2})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{iEx/2}}{2\pi}\Big[t_{2}(k_{1})r_{1}(k_{2})e^{2i\Delta_{1}x_{c}}

\displaystyle+r_{1}(k_{1})t_{2}(k_{2})e^{-2i\Delta_{1}x_{c}}+2B_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{(2)}(x_{c})\Big].

(42)

The bound-state term
B^{(2)}_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x)
at the resonance condition, i.e.,
\Delta=0
, can be expressed as

\displaystyle B_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{(2)}(x)=A_{+}e^{i(E/2-\gamma_{+})\left|{x}\right|}+A_{-}e^{i(E/2-\gamma_{-})\left|{x}\right|}.

(43)

Here,
\gamma_{\pm}
are the values of
\lambda_{1,2}
in Eq. (
39
) with
\Delta=0
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\pm}=\omega_{0}-i\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{2}\pm\frac{\sqrt{\Omega^{2}-{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{2}}}{2},

(44)

and the coefficients
A_{\pm}
are

\displaystyle A_{\pm}=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1+\cos\vartheta)^{2}}{2\sqrt{\Omega^{2}-{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{2}}}\Big[\pm(k_{1}+k_{2}-2\omega_{0})\frac{\Omega^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle+(k_{1}-\omega_{0})(k_{2}-\omega_{0})(\sqrt{\Omega^{2}-{\Gamma^{\prime}}^{2}}\mp i\Gamma^{\prime})\Big]

\displaystyle/\Big[(k_{1}-\gamma_{+})(k_{1}-\gamma_{-})(k_{2}-\gamma_{+})(k_{2}-\gamma_{-})\Big].

(45)

If
\Omega=0
, these coefficients can be reduced to the same form as those of the two-level giant atom derived above. In the following discussion, we also consider the narrow bandwidth limit for incident photons, where the frequencies of the incident photons are equal, i.e.,
k_{1}=k_{2}=k
.

III.1
Incoherent power spectrum

According to the previous discussion on the two-level giant atom system, the total incoherent power spectrum can be utilized as a metric for the photon-photon correlation because it originates from the correlated bound state. In the case of a three-level giant atom, the exact derivation of the total incoherent power spectrum
F(k)
is provided in Appendix A, which is

\displaystyle F(k)=\frac{8i}{\pi}\sum_{m,n=+,-}\frac{A_{m}^{*}A_{n}}{\gamma_{m}^{*}-\gamma_{n}}

(46)

The results are numerically shown in Fig.
6
. Notably, when the system reaches the perfect electromagnetically-induced transparency (EIT) window, i.e.,
k=\omega_{0}
, the value of
F(k)
becomes zero. This indicates that all photons are scattered coherently, and no bound state is formed in the wavefunction. This observation aligns with the expression of the bound-state term in Eq. (
43
), which becomes zero when
k=\omega_{0}
. Thus, it is consistent with the notion that photon-photon correlation and incoherent scattering are related. Additionally,
F(k=\omega_{0})=0
at the perfect transparency remains unaffected by the phase shift
\vartheta
, and even the number of identical atoms coupled to the waveguide
Fang and Baranger 2016
. This phenomenon is commonly referred to as fluorescence quenching
Zhou and Swain 1996
;
Rephaeli et al. 2011
.

III.2
Second-order correlation function

Likewise, to briefly illustrate the effect of the bound state on the transmitted and reflected field (
x_{1}>d/2
,
x_{2}>d/2
, and
x=x_{2}-x_{1}
) in the system of the three-level giant atom, we first examine the difference between the probability of the two-photon detection and the single-photon detection when
x=0
. Under the condition of
k_{1}=k_{2}=k
, we denote
\chi_{R}=2\pi^{2}\left|{f_{RR}^{(\Lambda)}(0)}\right|^{2}-\left|{t_{2}(k)}\right|^{4}
for the transmitted field and
\chi_{L}=2\pi^{2}\left|{f_{LL}^{(\Lambda)}(0)}\right|^{2}-\left|{r_{1}(k)}\right|^{4}
for the reflected field. If
\chi_{R}>0
, it indicates that the bound state enhances the transmission of two photons, resulting in a phenomenon known as photon-induced tunneling, which serves as a signature of photon bunching. Conversely, if
\chi_{R}<0
, it implies that the bound state can suppress the transmission of two photons, leading to photon blockade. In Fig.
8
, it shows that
\chi_{R}>0
, indicating that the transmitted photons are bunched, while
\chi_{L}<0
, suggesting that the reflected photons are antibunched. Therefore, the statistical properties of photons are determined by the interference between the plane-wave and bound-state terms.

Similarly, the normalized second-order correlation functions are utilized to investigate the photon-photon correlation of the transmitted and reflected field in the system. Upon performing calculations, the normalized correlation functions can be expressed in the form

\displaystyle g_{R}^{(2)}(x)=

\displaystyle\Bigg|1+\frac{A_{+}}{t_{2}(k_{1})t_{2}(k_{2})}e^{i(E/2-\gamma_{+})\left|{x}\right|}

\displaystyle+\frac{A_{-}}{t_{2}(k_{1})t_{2}(k_{2})}e^{i(E/2-\gamma_{-})\left|{x}\right|}\Bigg|^{2},

\displaystyle g_{L}^{(2)}(x)=

\displaystyle\Bigg|1+\frac{A_{+}}{r_{1}(k_{1})r_{1}(k_{2})}e^{i(E/2-\gamma_{+})\left|{x}\right|}

\displaystyle+\frac{A_{-}}{r_{1}(k_{1})r_{1}(k_{2})}e^{i(E/2-\gamma_{-})\left|{x}\right|}\Bigg|^{2}.

(47)

From the definition, it can be proven that at the perfect EIT window, where

, we have

due to the bound term in Eq. (

) being equal to zero. This implies that all of the photons are scattered coherently, without any structured correlation effects, which is consistent with the existence of a zero value for
F(k)
. It suggests that the statistical properties of the scattering photons can be adjusted by the control field in the three-level giant atom under the resonance condition. In the presence of the control field, the incident photons pass by the system coherently, thereby maintaining unchanged statistics. However, in the absence of the control field, the system effectively behaves as a two-level model, resulting in bunched transmitted photons and antibunched reflected photons. Actually, the generation of strong photon interactions indicated by the peak value of
F(k)
appears slightly away from the EIT condition
Roy and Bondyopadhaya 2014

x=0
, we have
g_{L}^{(2)}(0)=0
, indicating that the reflected photons are initially antibunched. As for the transmitted photons, the second-order correlation function at
x=0
is given by

\displaystyle g_{R}^{(2)}(0)=

\displaystyle\Bigg|1+\Gamma^{2}(1+\cos\vartheta)^{2}

\displaystyle\times\prod_{i=1}^{2}\frac{(k_{i}-\omega_{0})}{(\omega_{0}-k_{i})(\omega_{0}-k_{i}+\Gamma\sin\vartheta)-\frac{\Omega^{2}}{4}}\Bigg|^{2}.

(48)

It can be seen that when the incident photons have equal frequency, the transmitted photons are initially bunched. The analysis is consistent with the results presented in Fig.
8
and similar to that of the single coupling point
Fang and Baranger 2016
.

IV
Conclusions

In conclusion, we have employed the LS method to investigate the two-photon scattering processes involving two- and three-level giant atoms coupled to a 1D waveguide. We focus on effects of the accumulated phase shift acquired by photons as they travel between coupling points. The multiple coupling points of the giant atom give rise to interference effects that are absent in small atoms. Based on the analytical results for the total incoherent power spectra and second-order correlation functions of scattered photons, we have found that the accumulated phase shift can be utilized to enhance the strength and distance of photon-photon interactions. In the case of a two-level giant atom, photon-photon interactions are enhanced, and the evolution of the second-order correlation displays an oscillation between bunching and anti-bunching. Comparatively, the system of a three-level giant atom exhibits significantly increased photon-photon correlation, surpassing that of the two-level counterpart. The photon-photon interaction and the correlation distance can be further enhanced by tuning the accumulated phase shift between the two coupling points. These characteristics can be explained by analyzing the poles of the system.

Appendix A
Calculation of the incoherent power spectrum

A.1
The two-level giant atom

The power spectrum or resonance fluorescence is defined as the Fourier transform of the first-order coherence function

\displaystyle S_{\alpha}(\omega)=\int dte^{-i\omega t}\left\langle{\psi_{2}}\right|\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\alpha}(x_{0})\hat{a}_{\alpha}(x_{0}+t)\left|{\psi_{2}}\right\rangle,

(49)

where
x_{0}
is the position of a detector located far away from the scattering region. For example, considering
\alpha=R
, the first-order coherence in
\left|{\psi_{2}}\right\rangle
is

\displaystyle\left\langle{\psi_{2}}\right|\hat{a}_{R}^{\dagger}(x_{0})\hat{a}_{R}(x_{0}+t)\left|{\psi_{2}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\int dx^{\prime}f_{RR}^{*}(x_{0},x^{\prime})f_{RR}(x_{0}+t,x^{\prime}).

(50)

Under the condition of the narrow bandwidth for incident photons, they have equal frequency, i.e.,
k_{1}=k_{2}=k=E/2
, and

\displaystyle f_{RR}^{*}(x_{0},x^{\prime})f_{RR}(x_{0}+t,x^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{iEt/2}}{2\pi^{2}}\Big[\left|{t_{2}(E/2)}\right|^{4}+t_{2}^{2}(E/2)B_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{*}(x)

\displaystyle+t_{2}^{*2}(E/2)B_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x-t)+B_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{*}(x)B_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x-t)\Big],

(51)

with
x=x^{\prime}-x_{0}
. By substituting Eqs. (
50
) and (
51
) into Eq. (
19
), the power spectrum can be divided into two parts,

\displaystyle S_{R}(\omega)=S_{R}^{\text{coh}}(\omega)+S_{R}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega),

(52)

where the coherent part contains terms proportional to
\delta(0)\delta(\omega-E/2)
due to the use of delta-normalized plane waves. On the other hand, the incoherent part is related to the correlation of the bound-state term

\displaystyle S_{R}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\iint dtdxe^{i(E/2-\omega)t}B_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{*}(x)B_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x-t).

(53)

After the integration, the incoherent power spectrum becomes

\displaystyle S_{R}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)=\frac{4}{\pi^{2}}\left|{\Upsilon(\omega)}\right|^{2},

(54)

where

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\omega)=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma^{2}(1+\cos\vartheta)^{2}}{(E/2-\omega_{0}+i\Gamma^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(E-\omega_{0}-\omega+i\Gamma^{\prime})(\omega-\omega_{0}+i\Gamma^{\prime})}.

(55)

The total incoherent power spectrum is obtained by summing the right- and left-moving incoherent power spectra,

\displaystyle S^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)=S_{R}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)+S_{L}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega).

(56)

Here it can be proven that
S_{R}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)=S_{L}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)
. The total incoherent power can be used to measure the overall strength of photon-photon correlations to show the non-classical effects. It also provides a direct measure of the bound-state term. The definition of the total incoherent power spectrum is

\displaystyle F(k)=\int d\omega S^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)=\frac{4}{\pi}\int dxB_{k_{1}k_{2}}^{*}(x)B_{k_{1}k_{2}}(x).

(57)

Via integrating over
\omega
, it becomes

\displaystyle F(k)=\frac{4\Gamma^{3}(1+\cos\vartheta)^{3}}{\pi\left|{k-\omega_{0}+i\Gamma^{\prime}}\right|^{4}}.

(58)

A.2
The three-level giant atom

The incoherent power spectrum in the system of the three-level giant atom can be calculated by following the same procedure as that of the two-level giant atom, which yields

\displaystyle S_{R}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}}\left|{\sum_{m=+,-}\frac{A_{m}(E-2\gamma_{m})}{(E-\omega-\gamma_{m})(\omega-\gamma_{m})}}\right|^{2},

(59)

where
\gamma_{\pm}=\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\Omega^{2}-\Gamma^{\prime 2}}+\frac{i}{2}\Gamma^{\prime}
. By performing integration over the frequency
\omega
, the total incoherent power spectrum
F(k)=2\int d\omega S_{R}^{\text{incoh}}(\omega)
becomes

\displaystyle F(k)=\frac{8i}{\pi}\sum_{m,n=+,-}\frac{A_{m}^{*}A_{n}}{\gamma_{m}^{*}-\gamma_{n}}.

(60)

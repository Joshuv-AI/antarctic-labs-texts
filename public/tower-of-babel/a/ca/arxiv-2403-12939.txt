Hidden Zeros in Scaffolded General Relativity and Exceptional Field Theories
Yang Li 1 , Diederik Roest 1 , Tonnis ter Veldhuis 2

Abstract
It was recently discovered by Arkani-Hamed et al that the colour-ordered scattering amplitudes of Tr ( Φ 3 ) (\Phi^{3}) , the NLSM and scaffolded YM vanish at specific loci. We build on this observation and demonstrate that, beyond colour ordering, scattering amplitudes can display multiple instances of these hidden zeros. A first example are the flavour-ordered amplitudes of scaffolded General Relativity, corresponding to graviton exchange. The other two cases are multi-flavour DBI and the special Galileon. These are the natural generalisations of the NLSM with multiple instances of Adler’s zero. The zero loci naturally define factorisation limits; in all three cases these include a metric-coupled cubic scalar theory. We comment on the general picture of hidden zeros and the relation to the double copy.



I
Introduction

Positive geometry has been shown to encode rich properties of scattering amplitudes that are hidden from the Lagrangian prospective. For instance, the canonical form
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2017
of the kinematic associahedron
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2018
was found to be directly related to bi-adjoint scalar (BAS) amplitudes. Furthermore, its
\alpha^{\prime}
-generalization gives rise to amplitudes of stringy nature
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2021
. Much more recently, it was found that the amplitudes of
{\rm Tr}(\Phi^{3})
theory (that are contained in those of BAS) have a surprising property with a beautiful geometric structure: at all-loop and of any multiplicity, these follow from compact geometric rules on surfaces
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023a
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023b
. This framework of surfaceology has led to a series of interesting follow-up works
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023c
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024a
;
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2024b
;
Arkani-Hamed and Figueiredo 2024
.

Additionally, the tree-level amplitudes of
{\rm Tr}(\Phi^{3})
vanish on specific hypersurfaces in the space of Mandelstam variables
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023d
(as also found much earlier in string theory
D’Adda et al. 1971
). These loci naturally arise as ’causal diamonds’ in a two-dimensional kinematic mesh
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2022
, that is defined with respect to a specific colour ordering (and applies to the corresponding partial amplitude). On top of this, the loci give rise to new factorisation channels into lower-point amplitudes of the same theory.

Moreover, these minor miracles carry over to more intricate and interesting theories. Two of these were identified in
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023d
and involve colour ordering as well. The first is the scattering of pions, as described by the chiral non-linear sigma model (NLSM). The non-linear symmetry of these famously gives rise to the Adler zero in the soft limit
Adler 1965
. Interestingly, the zeros of Arkani-Hamed et al include and extend this soft-limit behaviour. The second theory describes external scalars interacting via gluon exchange. This is referred to as ’scaffolded Yang-Mills (YM)’ as these include the full YM amplitudes when stripping off the external scalars
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023c
.

In this letter, we will identify three theories that naturally generalise these results. The first two are higher-order variations of the Adler zero: the amplitudes of multi-Dirac-Born Infeld (DBI) and the special Galileon (SG) vanish in the soft limit as
p^{\sigma}
with
\sigma=2,3
, respectively
Cheung et al. 2017
. Similar to the Adler zero, we will demonstrate that their amplitudes vanish at multiple loci that include and extend these soft-limit behaviours. The third theory describes flavoured scalars interacting via graviton exchange, and can thus be seen as scaffolded General Relativity (GR).

Together, these six theories naturally span the set of scalar field theories that are related via the double copy procedure
Bern et al. 2008
;
Bern et al. 2010
;
Bern et al. 2019
;
Bern et al. 2022
. One can think of
{\rm Tr}(\Phi^{3})
as the zeroth copy, scaffolded YM and the NLSM as single copies, completed by scaffolded GR, multi-DBI and the special Galileon as double copies. We use the KLT formulation of these relations to demonstrate how the single-copy zeros carry over to the double copies, thus completing the web of exceptional scalar field theories with zeros in their amplitudes.

II
Colour ordering & single zeros

We first recap the results of
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023d

Tr
(\Phi^{3})

\displaystyle A^{\Phi^{3}}_{4}\sim\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}\sim\frac{u}{st}\,.

\displaystyle\alpha=(1,2)\,,\quad\beta=(4,5)\,,

III
Flavour ordering & double zeros

Going beyond colour-ordered partial amplitudes, we will demonstrate that scattering amplitudes can display multiple instance of the hidden zeros of
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023d
. As a first generalisation, we will retain some structure for the external scalars and require that they belong to a non-trivial group representation. Similar to the gluon exchange case, the full amplitudes at any multiplicity then splits up into different ’flavour structures’ that pair up the external scalars via a product of Kronecker delta functions. The coefficients of these structures will be referred to as flavour-ordered amplitudes. Without loss of generality, we will focus on the
\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}\cdots

Single scalar & triple zeros

Special Galileon -
The lowest-order contact interaction with three zeros is

\displaystyle A_{4}^{\rm SG}\sim stu\,,

(26)

Given the absence of colour and flavour structures, the special Galileon 6-pt amplitude vanishes in any causal diamond spanned by a square, with

\displaystyle\alpha=(i,j)\,,\quad\beta=(k,l)\,,

(27)

\displaystyle A_{6}^{\rm SG}\to

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{s_{1,2,3}}+\frac{1}{s_{1,5,6}}\right)s_{1,3}s_{2,3}\left(s_{1,3}+s_{2,3}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\times s_{1,6}s_{5,6}\left(s_{1,6}+s_{5,6}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{X_{B}}+\frac{1}{X_{T}}\right)A_{4}^{{\rm up,SG}}\times A_{4}^{{\rm down,SG}}\,.

(28)

This can be interpreted as a product of 4-pt SG amplitudes.

Looking at the skinny rectangles, the corresponding Mandelstams are an arbitrary selection of three variables from the soft Mandelstams with

\displaystyle\alpha=(1)\,,\quad\beta=(\{2,3,4,5,6\}_{|3|})\,.

VI
Conclusions

In this letter, we have shown that three double copy theories inherit amplitude zeros from their single copies: scaffolded GR, multi-DBI and the special Galileon. For the latter two, these zeros underlie their enhanced soft behaviours. Moreover, we have identified the extended Lagrangians that generate the mixed amplitudes from the near-zero factorization as covariantly coupled colorless
\Phi^{3}
, where the minimal coupling is to different metrics for the three cases. Finally, we prove that the inherited zeros of the double copy theories can be explained via the most general version of the KLT formula.

Independently of the double copy relations, one could wonder whether the different causal diamonds of
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023d
correspond to independent zeros and factorisations, or whether they imply each other. Indeed, the zero of skinny rectangles combined with the near-zero factorization imply the vanishing of height-two (
i=2)
causal diamonds. To see this, mentally split the height-two causal diamond into two strips of length
n-4
. One of these can be seen as the near-zero limit of an
n
-point skinny rectangle, and hence implies the factorisation into an
(n-1)
-point amplitude. The second strip of the original causal diamond is then the full skinny rectangle for this
(n-1)
-point amplitude, which therefore has to vanish.

Unfortunately, there appears to be an obstacle to generalising this argument to
i=3
and higher. When splitting these rectangles into smaller ones, the resulting shapes are
i-1
Mandelstam variables short of defining a lower causal diamond. For
i\geq 3
, one therefore misses more than one Mandelstam variables and the near-zero factorisation of
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2023d
does not apply. Possibly, this issue can be remedied by applying the new insights from
Cao et al. 2024
. In this work, it was found that the
{\rm Tr}(\Phi^{3})
amplitude factorizes under a 2-split of Mandelstam variables, which is exactly one row shorter than the entire zero locus. This might allow for an extension of the previous argument to all
i
; we leave a further investigation for future work.

Another interesting topic for future research is to look for a possible stringy generalization of the special Galileon. The SG inherits all the zeros from the NLSM via the KLT double copy, which arises from the closed/open string relation. We have checked via some examples that the mod square generalization
Arkani-Hamed et al. 2021
of the
{\rm Tr}(\Phi^{3})
stringy amplitude also admits zeros. For scaffolded GR, the stringy integral is the mod square generalisation of that of scaffolded YM. There is no known UV completion or string theory embedding of the SG, although higher-derivative corrections up to finite
{\cal O}(\alpha^{\prime})
has been found
Elvang et al. 2019
;
Bartsch et al. 2024
. It would be interesting to see whether the framework of surfaceology provides a new way to approach such a stringy generalization of the SG.

Finally, it is interesting to consider invariant polynomials that feature zero loci in a covariant manner as building blocks for amplitudes. Such a program will make manifest to what extent amplitudes are determined by their zero loci and pole structure.

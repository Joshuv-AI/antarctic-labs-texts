On the rainbow planar Turán number of paths
Ervin Győri, Ryan R. Martin, Addisu Paulos, Casey Tompkins, Kitti Varga

Abstract
An edge-colored graph is said to contain a rainbow- F F if it contains F F as a subgraph and every edge of F F is a distinct color. The problem of maximizing edges among n n -vertex properly edge-colored graphs not containing a rainbow- F F , known as the rainbow Turán problem, was initiated by Keevash, Mubayi, Sudakov and Verstraëte. We investigate a variation of this problem with the additional restriction that the graph is planar, and we denote the corresponding extremal number by ex 𝒫 ∗ ⁡ ( n , F ) \ex_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(n,F) . In particular, we determine ex 𝒫 ∗ ⁡ ( n , P 5 ) \ex_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(n,P_{5}) , where P 5 P_{5} denotes the 5 5 -vertex path.

Theorem 6
.

For

\ex_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(n,P_{k})=3n-6.

Proof.

First, let

be even. Take a double-wheel,
W_{n}=(K_{1}\cup K_{1})+C_{n-2}
. Note that
W_{n}
has two vertices of degree
n-2
and all the remaining vertices are of degree
4
. Let the
(n-2)
-cycle be
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}\dots v_{n-1}v_{n-2}v_{1}
and the two high-degree vertices be
u
and
w
.

We define an edge-coloring
c
of
W_{n}
, which uses
2n-2
distinct colors, as follows. Let
c(uv_{i})=a_{i}
and
c(wv_{i})=b_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,2,\dots,n-2\}
, and alternately color the edges of the
(n-2)
-cycle using two other colors
a
and
b
.

Now we show that with this edge-coloring,
W_{n}
is rainbow-
P_{k}
-free for any
k\geq 8
. Consider a path of length
7
in
W_{n}
. This path contains at most
4
edges which are not in the
(n-2)
-cycle. Thus, the number of edges which are on this cycle is at least
3
and hence the path is not rainbow. Since
W_{n}
is a maximal planar graph, we have
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(n,P_{k})=3n-6
.

Now let
n
be odd. In this case, take a maximal planar graph
H_{n}=K_{2}+P_{n-2}
. Let the path
P_{n-2}
be
v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}\dots v_{n-1}v_{n-2}
and let the remaining two vertices be
u
and
w
. Define an edge-coloring
c
of
H_{n}
, which uses
2n-3
distinct colors, as follows. Let
c(uv_{i})=a_{i}
for any
i\in\{2,3,\dots,n-3\}
, let
c(wv_{i})=b_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,2,3\dots,n-2\}
, let
c(uw)=d
, and alternately color the edges of the even cycle
uv_{1}v_{2}v_{3}\dots v_{n-2}u
using two other colors
a
and
b
.

Again, it is not difficult to see that
c
is a proper edge-coloring and any path of length
7
contains at least three edges of the
(n-1)
-cycle
uv_{1}v_{2}\dots v_{n-2}u
. Thus, every
P_{8}
of
H_{n}
is not rainbow. Since
H_{n}
is a maximal planar graph, we have
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(n,P_{k})=3n-6
.
∎

4
Outline of the proof of Theorem
7

From the extremal constructions discussed in the previous section, we have
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(n,P_{5})\geq\left\lfloor{{3n}/{2}}\right\rfloor
. In the upcoming sections, we prove
\ex_{\mathcal{P}}^{*}(n,P_{5})\leq\left\lfloor{{3n}/{2}}\right\rfloor
, thereby completing the proof of Theorem
7
.
The proof includes an induction argument and discharging methods.
To manage the flow of the proof, we break it into four sections.

In Section
5
, some basic preliminaries for the proof are given. The section examines some important graph structures together with proper edge-coloring schemes under the assumption that the graph avoids a rainbow-
P_{5}
.

In Section
6
, we analyze thoroughly the situations for which we can finish the proof by induction.
In this section, we also show why the planar Turán number and the rainbow planar Turán number of
P_{5}
behave quite differently.

Section
7
gives more refinements on the graph by avoiding vertices of degree at least five and two degree-
4
vertices sharing a common vertex. Moreover, in this section we show each degree-
4
vertex has at least two degree-
2
vertices in its neighbor or second neighbor.

We finish the proof in Section
8
.
In this section we define a modification
\tilde{d}
of the degree function, for which
\tilde{d}(v)\leq 3
holds for each vertex
v
of the graph.
This lets us to conclude the proof using the handshaking lemma.

6

From now on, by a graph

If

Theorem 10
.

If
G
contains two adjacent vertices

and

. We distinguish the the following three cases based on the

. Remember, we do not have two independent edges in

. Otherwise, we

. If
u_{2}

or

is a degree-
3

Let

Definition 8

Two vertex geometrically irreducible algebras
Grzegorz Bobiński, Grzegorz Zwara

Abstract
. We complete a classification of the two-vertex geometrically irreducible algebras. We also classify the algebras in new classes of hom- and ext-irreducible algebras.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
16G20, 14R20

1.
Introduction and main result

Throughout the paper
\Bbbk
denotes an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic.

Given a finite dimension
\Bbbk
-algebra
A
and a nonnegative integer
d
we denote by
\mod_{A}(d)
the variety of
d
-dimensional
A
-modules, which consists of the
\Bbbk
-algebra homomorphisms
A\to\mathbb{M}_{d\times d}(\Bbbk)
and parameterizes
A
-modules of dimension
d
. The algebra
A
is called geometrically irreducible if the connected components of
\mod_{A}(d)
are irreducible, for all
d
. Geometrically irreducible algebras have been studied in
[
BobSch2019a
,
BobSch2019b
,
Bob2021a
]
. In particular, it is conjectured in
[
BobSch2019b
]
that every geometrically irreducible algebra is a gluing (in a sense explained in
[
BobSch2019b
]
) of algebras with at most two simples. This conjecture has been confirmed for the algebras without shortcuts (
[
BobSch2019b
]
*Theorem 1.2). Consequently, the geometrically irreducible algebras with at most two simples seem to be of particular interest. The aim of this paper is to complete the classification of this class of algebras initiated in
[
BobSch2019b
,
Bob2021a
]
.

, let

For

(and
n\geq 1
), let

\rho^{(l)}:=\sum_{i=0}^{l}\varepsilon_{0}^{l-i}\alpha_{1}\varepsilon_{1}^{i}.

For
m\geq 2
and
n,l\geq 1
, let
A(n,m,l)
be the path algebra of the quiver
Q(n)
bounded by
\varepsilon_{0}^{m}
,
\varepsilon_{1}^{m}
and
\rho^{(l)}
. Similarly, for
m_{0},m_{1}\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
, let
A^{\prime}(n,m_{0},m_{1})
be the path algebra of the quiver
Q(n)
bounded by
\varepsilon_{0}^{m_{0}}
and
\varepsilon_{1}^{m_{1}}
.

The following theorem is the first main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be an algebra which has exactly two simples. Then
A
is geometrically irreducible if and only if
A
is Morita equivalent to one of the following algebras:

(1)

A(n,m,1)
, for some
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
, or

(2)

A(n,m,m-1)
, for some
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
, or

(3)

A^{\prime}(n,m_{0},m_{1})
, for some
m_{0},m_{1}\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
.

The main result of
[
BobSch2019b
]
(Theorem 1.1) states that if
A
is a geometrically irreducible algebra with exactly two simples, then (up to Morita equivalence)
A
is one of the algebras listed in Theorem
1.1
. On the other hand, it is relatively easy to see that the algebras
A^{\prime}(n,m_{0},m_{1})
,
m_{0},m_{1}\geq 1
and
n\geq 0
, are geometrically irreducible (see for example
[
BobSch2019b
]
*Proposition 3.3). Moreover,
[
Bob2021a
]
*Corollary 2.1 implies that the algebras
A(n,m,1)
,
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
, are also geometrically irreducible. Thus in order to prove Theorem
1.1
it suffices to prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

If
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
, then the algebra
A(n,m,m-1)
is geometrically irreducible.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we recall basic definitions, while in Section
3
we introduce classes of hom- and ext-irreducible algebras, which play crucial role in our proof of the main result presented in Section
4
. The final Section
5
contains a classification of the hom- and ext-irreducible algebras.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper, by
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathbb{N}_{+}
we denote the sets of integers, nonnegative integers, and positive integers, respectively. If
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
[i,j]
denotes the set of all
l\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
i\leq l\leq j
.

A quiver
Q
consists of a finite set
Q_{0}
of vertices, a finite set
Q_{1}
of arrows, and two maps
s,t\colon Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
, which assign to an arrow

\alpha\in Q_{1}
its starting and terminating vertex, respectively. An arrow
\alpha\in Q_{1}
is called a loop if
s\alpha=t\alpha
. If
\alpha
is an arrow in a quiver
Q
, then we define the degree
\deg\alpha
of
\alpha
by
\deg\alpha:=0
, if
\alpha
is a loop, and
\deg\alpha:=1
, otherwise. We denote by
Q_{1}^{(i)}
the set of arrows of degree
i
,
i=0,1
(in particular,
Q_{1}^{(0)}
is the set of loops in
Q
). By a path of length
l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
in
Q
we mean a sequence
\sigma=\alpha_{1}\cdots\alpha_{l}
of arrows such that
s\alpha_{i}=t\alpha_{i+1}
, for all
i\in[1,l-1]
. We put
s\sigma:=s\alpha_{l}
and
t\sigma:=t\alpha_{1}
, and call them the starting and the terminating vertex of
\sigma
, respectively. By the degree
\deg\sigma
of
\sigma
we mean
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\deg\alpha_{i}
. By an oriented cycle we mean a path
\sigma
of positive length such that
s\sigma=t\sigma
. For each vertex
x
of
Q
we also consider the trivial path
\mathbf{1}_{x}
at vertex
x
(i.e.,
s\mathbf{1}_{x}:=x=:t\mathbf{1}_{x})
of length (and degree)
0
. If
X\subseteq Q_{0}
, then we put
\mathbf{1}_{X}:=\sum_{x\in X}\mathbf{1}_{x}
.

With a quiver
Q
we associate its path algebra
\Bbbk Q
, which as a
\Bbbk
-vector space has a basis formed by all paths in
Q
and whose multiplication is induced by the composition of paths. By a relation
\rho
in
Q
we mean a linear combination of paths of length at least
2
with the same starting and terminating vertex (denoted by
s\rho
and
t\rho
, respectively). If
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\sigma_{i}
is a relation, where
\lambda_{1}
, …,
\lambda_{n}
are nonzero scalars and
\sigma_{1}
, …,
\sigma_{n}
pairwise different paths, the by the degree
\deg\rho
of
\rho
we mean
\min\{\deg\sigma_{i}:\text{$i\in[1,n]$}\}
. By a bound quiver we mean a pair
(Q,I)
consisting of a quiver
Q
and an ideal
I
of
\Bbbk Q
generated by relations, such that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
with
\sigma\in I
, for each path
\sigma
of length (at least)
n
. If
(Q,I)
is a bound quiver, then we call
\Bbbk Q/I
the path algebra of
(Q,I)
. If
A
is the path algebra of a bound quiver
(Q,I)
and the ideal
I
is generated by relations
\rho_{1}
, …,
\rho_{n}
, then we also say that
A
is the path algebra of
Q
bounded by
\rho_{1}
, …,
\rho_{n}
.

If
A
is an algebra, then there exists a bound quiver
(Q,I)
such that
A
and
\Bbbk Q/I
are Morita equivalent. Moreover, it follows from
[
Bon1991
]
that
A
is geometrically irreducible if and only if
\Bbbk Q/I
is geometrically irreducible.

Thus from now on we concentrate on path algebras of bound quivers. The quiver
Q
is uniquely determined by
A
and is called the Gabriel quiver of
A
. The algebra
A
is called weakly triangular, if there are no oriented cycles of positive degree in
Q
.

Let
(Q,I)
be a bound quiver. By a representation of
(Q,I)
we mean a collection
V
of finite dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector spaces
V_{x}
,
x\in Q_{0}
, together with
\Bbbk
-linear maps
V_{\alpha}\colon V_{s\alpha}\to V_{t\alpha}
,
\alpha\in Q_{1}
, such that
V_{\rho}
(defined in a natural way) vanishes for all relations
\rho\in I
. The collection
(\dim_{k}V_{x})\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
is called the dimension vector of
V
(and the elements of
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
are called dimension vectors). If
U
and
V
are representations of
(Q,I)
, then a homomorphism
f\colon V\to U
is a collection of
\Bbbk
-linear maps
f_{x}\colon V_{x}\to U_{x}
,
x\in Q_{0}
, such that
U_{\alpha}\circ f_{s\alpha}=f_{t\alpha}\circ V_{\alpha}
, for each
\alpha\in Q_{1}
. If
A
is the path algebra of
(Q,I)
then the category
\mod A
of (finite dimensional left)
A
-modules is equivalent to the category
\rep(Q,I)
of representations of
(Q,I)
. By abuse of language we call objects of
\rep(Q,I)
representations of
A
. If
x\in Q_{0}
then we denote by
S_{A}(x)
the simple representation at vertex
x
, i.e.,
S_{A}(x)_{x}:=\Bbbk
,
S_{A}(x)_{y}:=0
, for
y\neq x
, and
S_{A}(x)_{\alpha}:=0
, for
\alpha\in Q_{1}
.

According to
[
Bon1991
]
the above equivalence has its geometric counterpart. Namely, let
A
be the path algebra of a bound quiver
(Q,I)
. For a dimension vector
\mathbf{d}
we denote by
\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})
the set of representations
V
of
A
such that
V_{x}=\Bbbk^{d_{x}}
, for each
x\in Q_{0}
. One easily observes that
\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})
can be identified with a closed subset of
\prod_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}\mathbb{M}_{d_{t\alpha}\times d_{s\alpha}}(\Bbbk)
, thus carries a structure of an affine variety. Then results of
[
Bon1991
]
imply that
A
is geometrically irreducible if and only if
\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})
is irreducible, for each
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
. More precisely, if
d
is a nonnegative integer then the connected components of
\mod_{A}(d)
are (isomorphic to) the associated fiber bundles
\GL(d)\times_{\GL(\mathbf{d})}\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})
, for the dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
such that
\sum_{x\in Q_{0}}d_{x}=d
, where
\GL(\mathbf{d}):=\prod_{x\in Q_{0}}\GL(d_{x})
.

Let
A
be the path algebra of a bound quiver
(Q,I)
and
\mathbf{d}
a dimension vector. The group
\GL(\mathbf{d})
acts on
\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})
by conjugation, i.e., if
g\in\GL(\mathbf{d})
and
V\in\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})
, then
(g\ast V)_{\alpha}:=g_{t\alpha}V_{\alpha}g_{s\alpha}^{-1}
, for
\alpha\in Q_{1}
. Note that
g\in\GL(\mathbf{d})
can be viewed as an isomorphism
g\colon V\to g\ast V
.

We finish this subsection by listing basic properties of geometrically irreducible algebras proved in
[
BobSch2019a
]
*Section 3.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following hold for a geometrically irreducible algebra
A
with the Gabriel quiver
Q
.

(1)

A
is weakly triangular.

(2)

For each vertex
x\in Q_{0}
there exists at most one loop
\alpha\in Q_{1}
with
s\alpha=x
.

(3)

If
|Q_{0}|=1
, then
A\simeq k[x]/(x^{m})
. ∎

3.
Hom-, mono- and ext-irreducible algebras

Let
\Lambda
be an algebra. For dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
the set of triples
(V,W,f)
, such that
V\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e})
,
W\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d})
, and
f\in\Hom_{\Lambda}(V,W)
. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
has a natural structure of an affine variety. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
the subset of
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
consisting of the triples
(V,W,f)
such that
f
is a monomorphism. Note that
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
is an open subset of
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
. Moreover,
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
is nonempty if and only if
\mathbf{e}\leq\mathbf{d}
(i.e.,
e_{x}\leq d_{x}
, for each
x\in Q_{0}
). We say that
\Lambda
is hom-irreducible (mono-irreducible) if
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
(
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
, respectively) is irreducible, for all dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
(such that
\mathbf{e}\leq\mathbf{d}
, respectively). We have the following obvious observation.

Proposition 3.1
.

If an algebra
\Lambda
is hom-irreducible, then
\Lambda
is mono-irreducible. ∎

In order to define ext-irreducible algebras we need to recall an interpretation of extensions of
\Lambda
-modules in terms of cocycles. Fix the path algebra
\Lambda
of a bound quiver
(Q,I)
, dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
, and
V\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d})
and
U\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e})
. Let
\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d}}:=\prod_{\alpha\in Q_{1}}\mathbb{M}_{d_{t\alpha}\times e_{s\alpha}}(\Bbbk)
. For each
Z\in\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d}}
we have a representation
W^{V,Z,U}
of
Q
defined by:
W^{V,Z,U}_{x}:=\Bbbk^{d_{x}+e_{x}}(=\Bbbk^{d_{x}}\oplus\Bbbk^{e_{x}})
, for
x\in Q
, and
W^{V,Z,U}_{\alpha}:=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}V_{\alpha}&Z_{\alpha}\\
0&U_{\alpha}\end{smallmatrix}\right]
, for
\alpha\in Q_{1}
. Given an exact sequence

0\to V\xrightarrow{f}W\xrightarrow{h}U\to 0

of
\Lambda
-modules, there exists
Z\in\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d}}
and an isomorphism
g\colon W^{V,Z,U}\to W
, such that the following diagram is commutative

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mu}
\textstyle{W^{V,Z,U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{U\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{W\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{h}
\textstyle{U\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

where
\mu
and
\pi
are the natural maps. Commutativity of the above diagram means in particular that
f=g\circ\mu
. Moreover, if
W\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})
, then
g\in\GL(\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})
and
W=g\ast W^{V,Z,U}
. Let
\mathbb{Z}^{U,V}
be the set of
Z\in\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d}}
such that
W^{V,Z,U}\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})
. Then
Z\in\mathbb{Z}^{U,V}
if and only if
Z_{\rho}^{U,V}=0
, for each relation
\rho\in I
, where

Z_{\rho}^{U,V}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}\lambda_{i}V_{\alpha_{i,1}}\cdots V_{\alpha_{i,j-1}}Z_{\alpha_{i,j}}U_{\alpha_{i,j+1}}\cdots U_{\alpha_{i,l_{i}}},

provided
\rho=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i,1}\cdots\alpha_{i,l_{i}}
.

Given an algebra
\Lambda
and dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
we denote by
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
the set of triples
(U,V,Z)
such that
U\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e})
,
V\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d})
, and
Z\in\mathbb{Z}^{U,V}
. Obviously
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
has a structure of an affine variety and we call
\Lambda
ext-irreducible if the varieties
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
are irreducible, for all dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
.

The following proposition, whose proof uses a construction from
[
Bon1994
]
, is the main result of this section.

Proposition 3.2
.

An algebra
\Lambda
is ext-irreducible if and only if
\Lambda
is mono-irreducible.

Proof.

Assume first that
\Lambda
is ext-irreducible and fix dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
such that
\mathbf{e}\leq\mathbf{d}
. Consider the map
\Phi\colon\GL(\mathbf{d})\times\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e},\mathbf{e})\to\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
given by:

\Phi(g,(U,V,Z)):=(V,g\ast W^{V,Z,U},g\circ\mu),

for
g\in\GL(\mathbf{d})
,
V\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e})
,
U\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e})
, and
Z\in\mathbb{Z}^{U,V}
, where
\mu\colon V\to W^{V,Z,U}
is the natural inclusion. One easily checks that
\Phi
is a well-defined morphism of varieties. We show that
\Phi
is onto and this will imply that
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
is irreducible as an image of an irreducible set, since
\GL(\mathbf{d})
is irreducible and by assumption
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e},\mathbf{e})
is irreducible.

Let
(V,W,f)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
and
U:=\Coker f
. Without loss of generality we may assume
U\in\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}-\mathbf{e})
. We have an exact sequence

0\to V\xrightarrow{f}W\to U\to 0.

By remarks preceding the proposition, there exists
Z\in\mathbb{Z}^{U,V}
and
g\in\GL(\mathbf{d})
such that
W=g\ast W^{V,Z,U}
and
f=g\circ\mu
. In other words,
(V,W,f)=\Phi(g,(U,V,Z))
.

Now assume that
\Lambda
is mono-irreducible and fix dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
. Let
\mathcal{Y}
be the set of tuples
(V,W,f,h)
such that
(V,W,f)\in\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d},\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})
and
h\in\prod_{x\in Q_{0}}\mathbb{M}_{(d_{x}+e_{x})\times e_{x}}(\Bbbk)
, and
\mathcal{X}
be the subset of
\mathcal{Y}
consisting of the tuples
(V,W,f,h)
such that the collection
[f,h]:=([f_{x},h_{x}])_{x\in Q_{0}}\in\prod_{x\in Q_{0}}\mathbb{M}_{(d_{x}+e_{x})\times(d_{x}+e_{x})}(\Bbbk)
belongs to
\GL(\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{d})
. Then
\mathcal{Y}
is a vector bundle over
\mathcal{M}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{e}+\mathbf{d})
and
\mathcal{X}
is a nonempty open subset of
\mathcal{Y}
, hence irreducible.

We have obvious maps
\pi_{1,1}\colon\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})\to\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{d},\mathbf{d}}
,
\pi_{1,2}\colon\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})\to\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d}}
,
\pi_{2,1}\colon\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})\to\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{d},\mathbf{e}}
, and
\pi_{2,2}\colon\rep_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{d}+\mathbf{e})\to\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{e},\mathbf{e}}
, where
Q
is the Gabriel quiver of
\Lambda
. If
(V,W,f,h)\in\mathcal{X}
,
g:=[f,h]
, and
W^{\prime}:=g^{-1}\ast W
, then
\pi_{1,1}(W^{\prime})=V
,
\pi_{2,1}(W^{\prime})=0
, and
\pi_{1,2}(W^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{U,V}
, where
U:=\pi_{2,2}(W)
. Consequently, we may define
\Psi\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
by

\Psi(V,W,f,h):=(\pi_{2,2}([f,h]^{-1}\ast W),V,\pi_{1,2}([f,h]^{-1}\ast W)),

for
(V,W,f,h)\in\mathcal{X}
. Obviously
\Psi
is a regular map, which is easily seen to be onto, hence
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
is irreducible.
∎

We formulate the following obvious consequence of Propositions
3.1
and
3.2
.

Corolary 3.3
.

If an algebra
\Lambda
is hom-irreducible, then
\Lambda
is ext-irreducible. ∎

In Section
5
we classify the hom- and ext-irreducible algebras. In particular, we show that the hereditary algebras, which are not semisimple, are ext-irreducible, but not hom-irreducible.

4.
Proof of the main result

Throughout this section we fix
m\geq 2
. In order to simplify notation we put
A:=A(1,m,1)
and
B_{n}:=A(n,m,m-1)
, for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Moreover, by
A^{\prime}
we denote the path algebra of the quiver

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varepsilon_{0}}
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varepsilon_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\alpha}

bounded by
\varepsilon_{0}^{m}
,
\varepsilon_{1}^{m}
, and
\varepsilon_{0}\alpha-\alpha\varepsilon_{1}
. Finally, we put
\Lambda:=k[x]/(x^{m})
. Note that the dimension vectors for
\Lambda
are the elements of
\mathbb{N}
, while the dimension vectors for
A
,
A^{\prime}
and
B_{n}
are the pairs
(d,e)
with
d,e\in\mathbb{N}
. Furthermore, the correspondence given by

\varepsilon_{0}\mapsto\varepsilon_{0},\alpha\mapsto\alpha_{1},\varepsilon_{1}\mapsto-\varepsilon_{1},

induces an isomorphism
A^{\prime}\simeq A
. Since the algebra
A
is geometrically irreducible by
[
Bob2021a
]
*Corollary 1.2,
A^{\prime}
is geometrically irreducible as well.

The crucial point in the proof is the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
d,e\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the following hold.

(1)

\rep_{A^{\prime}}((d,e))\cong\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(e,d)
.

(2)

\rep_{B_{1}}((d,e))\cong\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(e,d)
.

Proof.

(
1
) Note that
\Lambda
is the path algebra of the quiver
Q
of the form

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varepsilon}

bounded by
\varepsilon^{m}
. Consequently,
\rep_{\Lambda}(d)
consists of the matrices
V\in\mathbb{M}_{d\times d}(\Bbbk)
such that
V^{m}=0
. Similarly,
\rep_{\Lambda}(e)
consists of the matrices
U\in\mathbb{M}_{e\times e}(\Bbbk)
such that
U^{m}=0
. Finally, if
V\in\rep_{\Lambda}(d)
and
U\in\rep_{\Lambda}(e)
, then
\Hom_{\Lambda}(U,V)
consists of the matrices

such that

. Consequently, we have an isomorphism
\rep_{A^{\prime}}((d,e))\cong\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(e,d)
given by

\rep_{A^{\prime}}((d,e))\ni M\to(M_{\varepsilon_{1}},M_{\varepsilon_{0}},M_{\alpha_{1}})\in\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(e,d).

(
2
) Note that
\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{e,d}=\mathbb{M}_{d\times e}(\Bbbk)
, while
\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(e,d)
consists of the triples
(V,U,Z)
with
V\in\rep_{\Lambda}(d)
,
U\in\rep_{\Lambda}(e)
, and
Z\in\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{e,d}
, such that
Z_{\varepsilon^{m}}^{U,V}=0
. By easy induction one shows that
Z^{U,V}_{\varepsilon^{l}}=\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}V^{l-1-i}ZU^{i}
, for
l\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. Consequently the map

\rep_{B_{1}}((d,e))\ni M\to(M_{\varepsilon_{1}},M_{\varepsilon_{0}},M_{\alpha_{1}})\in\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(e,d)

is easily seen to be an isomorphism.
∎

We have the following consequences.

Corolary 4.2
.

The algebra
\Lambda
is hom- and ext-irreducible.

Proof.

If
d,e\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(e,d)\cong\rep_{A^{\prime}}((d,e))
by Lemma
4.1
(
1
). Since
A^{\prime}
is geometrically irreducible,
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}(e,d)
is irreducible, thus
\Lambda
is hom-irreducible. Ext-irreducibility follows from Corollary
3.3
.
∎

Corolary 4.3
.

The algebra
B_{1}
is geometrically irreducible.

Proof.

Fix a dimension vector
(d,e)
. Since
\Lambda
is ext-irreducible by Corollary
4.2
and
\rep_{B_{1}}((d,e))\cong\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda}(e,d)
by Lemma
4.1
(
2
),
\rep_{B_{1}}((d,e))
is irreducible and the claim follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
. If
(d,e)
is a dimension vector, then

\rep_{B_{n}}((d,e))\cong\rep_{B_{1}}((d,e))\times\prod_{i=2}^{n}\mathbb{M}_{d\times e}(\Bbbk).

Since
B_{1}
is geometrically irreducible (Corollary
4.3
),
\rep_{B_{n}}((d,e))
is irreducible, thus
B_{n}
is geometrically irreducible.
∎

5.
Classification results

It is natural to ask about a classification of the hom- and ext-irreducible algebras. We solve this problem in this section.

We start with the following remark. One could define hom- and ext-irreducibility for not necessarily basic algebras, using module varieties instead of representation varieties
– as usual one requires that the connected components of corresponding varieties are irreducible. An analysis of these constructions, based on results of
[
Bon1991
]
, shows that notions of hom- and ext-irreducibility defined in this way coincides with these defined in Section
3
. More precisely, let
A
be the path algebra of a bound quiver
(Q,I)
and
\Lambda
an algebra Morita equivalent to
A
. Assume also that
S_{x}
,
x\in Q_{0}
, form a complete set of pairwise nonisomorphic simple
\Lambda
-modules such that the module
S_{x}
corresponds to the vertex
x
, for each
x\in Q_{0}
. For nonnegative integers
d
and
e
we define
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}^{\prime}(e,d)
to be the set of triples
(V,W,f)
, such that
V\in\mod_{\Lambda}(e)
,
W\in\mod_{\Lambda}(d)
, and
f\in\Hom_{\Lambda}(V,W)
. Then one shows (using results of
[
Bon1991
]
*Section 2) that the connected components of
\mathcal{H}_{\Lambda}^{\prime}(e,d)
are the associated fiber bundles
(\GL(e)\times\GL(d))\times_{(\GL(\mathbf{e})\times\GL(\mathbf{d}))}\mathcal{H}_{A}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
, for the dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
such that
\sum_{x\in Q_{0}}d_{x}\cdot\dim_{\Bbbk}S_{x}=d
and
\sum_{x\in Q_{0}}e_{x}\cdot\dim_{\Bbbk}S_{x}=e
. A similar statement also holds for ext-varieties, which in the module varieties case are defined in
[
Bon1994
]
*subsection 2.1. We leave details to the interested reader.

Consequently, we will work further with the path algebras of bound quivers, but formulate the results for arbitrary algebras.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, let
\Lambda_{m}:=k[x]/(x^{m})
. We have the following classification of the hom-irreducible algebras.

Theorem 5.1
.

Up to Morita equivalence, the algebras
\Lambda_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, are precisely the hom-irreducible connected algebras.

Proof.

We already know that the algebras
\Lambda_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, are hom-irreducible (Corollary
4.2
). It remains to show that they are the only hom-irreducible connected algebras.

Let
(Q,I)
be a bound quiver such that
A:=\Bbbk Q/I
is a hom-irreducible connected algebra. It is obvious that
A
is geometrically irreducible (
\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})=\mathcal{H}_{A}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{d})
). Suppose there exists an arrow
\alpha_{1}\colon x\to y
in
Q
with
x\neq y
. Since
A
is weakly triangular by Lemma
2.1
(
1
), there are no arrows
y\to x
in
Q
. Let
\alpha_{2},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\colon x\to y
be the remaining arrows from
x
to
y
in
Q
. Let
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
be the dimension vectors such that

d_{z}:=\delta_{x,z}+\delta_{y,z}\qquad\text{and}\qquad e_{z}:=\delta_{x,z},

for
z\in Q_{0}
, where
\delta_{u,v}
is the Kronecker delta.

Fix a loop
\alpha
in
Q
. Recall that there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that
\alpha^{n}\in I
. Consequently, if
W\in\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})
, then
W_{\alpha}^{n}=0
. Since
d_{z}\leq 1
, for each
z\in Q_{0}
, this implies
W_{\alpha}=0
. Similarly,
V_{\alpha}=0
, for each
V\in\rep_{A}(\mathbf{e})
.

Thus
\mathcal{H}_{A}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
is isomorphic to the variety

\{(b,a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\in\Bbbk^{n+1}:\text{$a_{1}b=\cdots=a_{n}b=0$}\},

which is easily seen to be reducible.

The above implies that
|Q_{0}|=1
(due to connectedness of
A
). Using again geometrical irreducibility of
A
we get
A\simeq\Lambda_{m}
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, by Lemma
2.1
(
3
).
∎

In order to classify the ext-irreducible algebras, we introduce the corresponding class of algebras. We call an algebra
A
a loop extension of a hereditary algebra, if
A
is the path algebra of a quiver
Q
bound by relations of degree
0
. Note that
A/\langle Q_{1}^{(0)}\rangle
is isomorphic to the path algebra of the quiver
(Q_{0},Q_{1}^{(1)})
(hence hereditary). In other words,
A
is obtained from a hereditary algebra (i.e., the path algebra of a quiver with no relations) by adding loops and relations involving only these loops. In particular
A
is weakly triangular. If additionally, for each vertex
x
of
Q
there is at most one loop
\alpha
such that
s\alpha=x
, then we call
A
a simple loop extension of a hereditary algebra. We show that the ext-irreducible algebras are exactly the simple loop extensions of hereditary algebras.

We start with the following.

Lemma 5.2
.

Every hereditary algebra is ext-irreducible.

Proof.

Let
A
be a hereditary algebra. Then
A=\Bbbk Q
, for some quiver
Q
. Consequently, if we fix dimension vectors
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
, then
\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})
and
\rep_{A}(\mathbf{e})
are affine spaces, and
\mathbb{Z}^{U,V}=\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d}}
, for all representations
V\in\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})
and
U\in\rep_{A}(\mathbf{e})
. Consequently,
\mathcal{E}_{A}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})=\rep_{A}(\mathbf{e})\times\rep_{A}(\mathbf{d})\times\mathbb{A}_{Q}^{\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d}}
is an affine space, thus irreducible.
∎

As a consequence we obtain the first part of our classification result.

Lemma 5.3
.

Every simple loop extension of a hereditary algebra is ext-irreducible.

Proof.

Let
A=\Bbbk Q/I
be a simple loop extension of a hereditary algebra. Let
H
be the path algebra of the quiver
(Q_{0},Q_{1}^{(1)})
. Next, for each loop
\varepsilon
, let
m_{\varepsilon}
be the minimal
m
such that
\varepsilon^{m}\in I
, and
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}:=k[x]/(x^{m_{\varepsilon}})
. Note that
I=\langle\varepsilon^{m_{\varepsilon}}:\text{$\varepsilon\in Q_{1}^{(0)}$}\rangle
.

Let
\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{e}
be dimension vectors. Then

\mathcal{E}_{A}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})=\mathcal{E}_{H}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})\times\prod_{\varepsilon\in Q_{1}^{(0)}}\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_{\varepsilon}}(e_{t\varepsilon},d_{s\varepsilon}).

Since
H
is ext-irreducible by Lemma
5.2
, and the algebras
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}
are ext-irreducible by Corollary
4.2
,
\mathcal{E}_{A}(\mathbf{e},\mathbf{d})
is irreducible, and the claim follows.
∎

In order to prove that the simple loop extensions of hereditary algebras are the only ext-irreducible algebras, we need additional facts on relations in (geometrically irreducible) algebras. Let
Q
be a quiver. Recall that a set
R
of relations is called minimal, if
\langle R\rangle\neq\langle R\setminus\{\rho\}\rangle
, for each
\rho\in R
. If
R
is a set of relations, then for
x,y\in R
we denote by
R_{x,y}
the set of
\rho\in R
such that
s\rho=x
and
t\rho=y
. The first fact is the following generalization of
[
Bon1983
]
*Proposition 1.2.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
A=\Bbbk Q/I
be a weakly triangular algebra. If
R
is a minimal set of relations generating
I
, then the cardinality
\#R_{x,y}
of
R_{x,y}
is independent of
R
, for all
x,y\in Q_{0}
,
x\neq y
. More precisely,

\#R_{x,y}=\dim_{\Bbbk}\Ext_{A}^{2}(S_{A}(x),S_{A}(y)).

Proof.

Let
J
be the ideal in
\Bbbk Q
generated by the arrows. Then according to
[
Bon1983
]
*Corollary 1.2

\dim_{\Bbbk}\Ext_{A}^{2}(S_{A}(x),S_{A}(y))=\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathbf{1}_{y}(I/(IJ+JI))\mathbf{1}_{x}.

Next, let
R^{(0)}
be the set of relations of degree
0
in
R
,
A^{\prime}=\Bbbk Q/\langle R^{(0)}\rangle
,
I^{\prime}=I/\langle R^{(0)}\rangle
, and
J^{\prime}=J/\langle R^{(0)}\rangle
. Since
x\neq y
,

\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathbf{1}_{y}(I/(IJ+JI))\mathbf{1}_{x}=\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathbf{1}_{y}(I^{\prime}/(I^{\prime}J^{\prime}+J^{\prime}I^{\prime}))\mathbf{1}_{x}.

Thus in order to finish the proof it suffices to show

\#R_{x,y}=\dim_{\Bbbk}\mathbf{1}_{y}(I^{\prime}/(I^{\prime}J^{\prime}+J^{\prime}I^{\prime}))\mathbf{1}_{x}.

In other words, it is enough to show that the residue classes of the elements of
R_{x,y}
form a basis of
\mathbf{1}_{y}(I^{\prime}/(I^{\prime}J^{\prime}+J^{\prime}I^{\prime}))\mathbf{1}_{x}
.

One easily sees that the residue classes of the elements of
R_{x,y}
span
\mathbf{1}_{x}(I^{\prime}/(I^{\prime}J^{\prime}+J^{\prime}I^{\prime}))\mathbf{1}_{y}
as a vector space (see for example the proof of the corresponding statement in the proof of
[
MarSalSte2021
]
*Theorem 4.1). Thus assume the residue classes of the elements of
R_{x,y}
are linearly dependent. This implies that there exists
\rho\in R_{x,y}
such that
\rho\in K^{\prime}+I^{\prime}J^{\prime}+J^{\prime}I^{\prime}
, where
K^{\prime}:=K/\langle R^{(0)}\rangle
and
K:=\langle R\setminus\{\rho\}\rangle
. Consequently,
K^{\prime}+I^{\prime}J^{\prime}+J^{\prime}I^{\prime}=I^{\prime}
. Note that
I^{\prime}J^{\prime}+J^{\prime}I^{\prime}
is the radical of
A^{\prime}
as an
A^{\prime}
-
A^{\prime}
-bimodule (here we use that
A
is weakly triangular, hence there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
with
\sigma\in\langle R^{(0)}\rangle
, for each path
\sigma
in
Q
of length
n
). Thus the Nakayama lemma (see for example
[
AndFul1992
]
*Corollary 15.13) implies
I^{\prime}=K^{\prime}
, i.e.,
I=K
, which contradicts the minimality of
R
.
∎

*Subsection 5.5.

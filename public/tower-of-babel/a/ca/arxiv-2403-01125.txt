Large Deviation Principle of Stochastic Evolution Equations with reflection
Zdzisław Brzeźniak, Qi Li, Tusheng Zhang

Abstract




Abstract.

In this paper, we establish a large deviation principle for stochastic evolution equations with reflection in an infinite dimensional ball. Weak convergence approach plays an important role.

Keywords.
Large deviations; Stochastic evolution equations; reflection; weak convergence.

1
Introduction

Let
H
be a seperable Hilbert space with the norm
|\cdot|
and inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
D:=B(0,1)
denotes the open unit ball on
H
. Let
A
be a self-adjoint, positive define operator on
H
and let
B
be a unbounded bilinear map from
H\times H
to

. Let
W
denote a real-valued Brownian Motion on a filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F,\mathbb{F}}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
satisfying the usual conditions. Consider the following stochastic evolution equations(SEEs) with reflection:

\left\{\begin{aligned} du(t)&+Au(t)dt=f(u(t))dt+B(u(t),u(t))dt+\sigma(u(t))dW(t)+dL(t),t\geq 0,\\
u(0)&=u_{0},\quad u_{0}\in\overline{D},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Where
u
is a
\overline{D}
-valued continuous stochastic process and
L
ia a
H
-valued continuous stochastic process, which plays the role of a local time.
\overline{D}
denotes the closed unit ball in the Hilbert space
H
.
f
and
\sigma
are measurable mappings.

Stochastic partial differential equations(SPDEs) with reflection can be used to model the evolution of random interfaces near a hard wall, see
[
12
]
. Existence and uniqueness of the above stochastic reflected problems were established in
[
6
]
. For the study of real-valued SPDEs with reflection we refer the readers to
[
16
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
18
]
and references therein.

The purpose of this paper is to establish a small noise large deviation principle(LDP) for the following SEEs with reflection:

\left\{\begin{aligned} du^{\varepsilon}(t)&+Au^{\varepsilon}(t)dt=f(u^{\varepsilon}(t))dt+B(u^{\varepsilon}(t),u^{\varepsilon}(t))dt+\sqrt{\varepsilon}\sigma(u^{\varepsilon}(t))dW(t)+dL^{\varepsilon}(t),t\geq 0,\\
u^{\varepsilon}(0)&=u_{0},\quad u_{0}\in\overline{D}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

Large deviations provides asymptotic estimates for probabilities of rare events and have wide applications in fields such as physics, communication engineering, biology, finance and computer science. There are a large number of papers devoted to LDP of SEEs and SPDEs driven by Brownian motions; see
[
2
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
and references therein. And one can also refer to
[
10
]
for LDP for Boussinesq equations;
[
13
]
for LDP for SEEs with small multiplicative noise and
[
14
]
for LDP for the stochastic shell model.

An effective approach to obtain the LDP is the weak convergence method introduced by Budhiraja, Dupuis and Maroulas in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. However, due to the singularity introduced by the reflection, proving the tightness of the perturbations of reflected SEEs required by the creteria in
[
1
]
is challenging. Instead, we will use a sufficient condition of the Budhiraja–Dupuis–Maroulas criteria proposed in
[
15
]
, which shifts the difficulty of proving tightness to a study of the continuity (with respect to the driving signals) of the deterministic skeleton equations associated with the reflected SEEs. Therefore, a crucial part of our work is to study the continuity of the deterministic reflection problems driven by the elements in the Cameron–Martin space of the driving Brownian motions.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce the precise assumptions of coefficients and the result of existence and uniqueness of solution for (
1.1
). In Section 3, we introduce the definition of LDP and the sufficient condition proposed in
[
15
]
. In Section 4, we study the skeleton reflection problems and establish the continuity of the deterministic reflection problems with respect to the driving signal. The proof of the large deviation principle will be presented in Section 5. And in Section 6 we give a example of damped Navier-Stokes Equations in the whole Euclidean domain
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

In this section we recall the sufficient condition proposed in

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a Polish space with the Borel

Definition 3.1

(Rate function) A function
I:\mathcal{E}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is called a rate function on
\mathcal{E}
, if for each
M<0

\{x\in\mathcal{E}:I(x)\leq M\}

\mathcal{E}

Definition 3.2

(Large deviation principle) Let

be a rate function on
\mathcal{E}
. A family

\mathcal{E}
-valued random elements is said to satisfy a large deviation principle on

(a) (Upper bound) For each closed subset

\mathcal{E}

\limsup\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\epsilon\textup{log}\mathbb{P}(X^{\epsilon}\in F)\leq-\inf\limits_{x\in F}I(x).

(b) (Lower bound) For each open subset
G
of
\mathcal{E}
,

\liminf\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}\epsilon\textup{log}\mathbb{P}(X^{\epsilon}\in G)\geq-\inf\limits_{x\in F}I(x).

The Cameron-Martin space associated with the Browian motion
\{W=W(t),t\in[0,T]\}
is isomorphic to the Hilbert space
K:=L^{2}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
with the inner product:

\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle_{K}:=\int_{0}^{T}h_{1}(s)h_{2}(s)ds.

(3.1)

Denote by
\|\cdot\|_{K}
the norm of K.
Let

denote the class of real-valued

-predictable processes

K

The set

endowed with the weak topology is a compact Polish space. Set
\widetilde{S}_{N}=\{\phi\in\widetilde{K};\phi(\omega)\in S_{N},\mathbb{P}
-
a.s.\}.

Recall the following sufficient condition from
[
15
]
.

Theorem 3.1

For

, let

6
LDP of Reflected Stochastic Navier Stokes Equations

An important application of this paper is to treat the stochastic Navier Stokes Equations on a two dimensional domains
D
, which can be unbounded, e.g., the whole Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. We will concentrate on this case and mostly follow a recent paper
[
4
]
.

For a natural number
d
and
p\in[1,\infty)
, let
L^{p}=L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the classical Lebesgue space of all
\mathbb{R}^{d}
-valued lebesgue measurable functions
v=(v^{1},\cdots,v^{d})
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with the following classical norm

\|v\|_{L^{p}}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{d}\|v^{k}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

(6.1)

For
p=\infty
, we set
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}=\text{max}_{k=1}^{d}\|v^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

Set
J^{s}=(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
. And define the generalized Sobolev spaces of divergence free vector distributions, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
, as

\displaystyle H^{s,p}

\displaystyle=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d}):\|J^{s}u\|_{L^{p}}<\infty\},

(6.2)

\displaystyle H^{s,p}_{\text{sol}}

\displaystyle=\{u\in H^{s,p}:\text{div }u=0\}.

It’s well-known that
J^{\sigma}
is an isomorphism between
H^{s,p}
and
H^{s-\sigma,p}
for
s\in mathbb{R}
and
1<p<\infty
. Moreover,
H^{s_{2},p}\subset H^{s_{1},p}
when
s_{1}<s_{2}
. For the Hilbert case
p=2
We set
H=H^{0,2}_{\text{sol}}
and, for
s\neq 0,H^{s}=H^{0,2}_{\text{sol}}
, so that
H^{s}
is a proper closed subspace of the classical Sobolev space usually denoted by the same symbol. In particular, we put

H=\{v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})):\text{div }v=0\}.

(6.3)

with scalar product inherited from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the duality bracket between
(H^{s,p})^{\prime}
and
H^{s,p}
spaces. Note that for
p\in[1,\infty)
, the space
(H^{s,p})^{\prime}
can be identified with
(H^{-s,p^{\ast}})
, where
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\ast}}=1
.

Now we define the operators appearing in the abstract formulation. Assume
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Let
A_{0}=-\Delta
; then
A_{0}
is a linear unbounded operator in
H^{s,p}
and bounded from
H{s+2,p}
to
H^{s,p}
. Moreover, the space
H^{s,p}_{\text{sol}}
are invariant w.r.t.
A_{0}
and the corresponding operator will be denoted by
A
. We can observe that
A
is a linear unbounded operator in
H^{s,p}
and bounded from
H^{s+2,p}_{\text{sol}}
to
H^{s,p}_{\text{sol}}
. The operator
-A_{0}
generates a contractive and analytic
C-0
-semigroup
\{e^{-tA}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
H^{s,p}
and therefore, the operator
-A
generates a contractive and analytic
C-0
-semigroup
\{e^{-tA}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
H^{s,p}_{\text{sol}}
. Moreover, for
t>0
the operator
e^{-tA}
is bounded from
H^{s,p}_{\text{sol}}
into
H^{s^{\prime},p}_{\text{sol}}

with
s^{\prime}>s
and there exists a constant
M
(depending on

) such that

\|e^{-tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}({H^{s,p}_{\text{sol}},H^{s^{\prime},p}_{\text{sol}}})}\leq M(1+t^{-(s^{\prime}-s)2}).

(6.4)

We have
A:H^{1}\rightarrow H^{-1}
as a linear bounded operator and

\langle Av,v\rangle=\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\quad v\in H^{1},

(6.5)

Where

\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\sum_{k-1}^{d}\|\nabla v^{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\quad v\in H^{1}.\\

(6.6)

Moreover,

\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}}=\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

(6.7)

We define a bounded trilinear form
b:H^{1}\times H^{1}\times H^{1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

b(u,v,z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(\xi)\cdot\nabla)v(\xi)\cdot z(\xi)d\xi,\quad u,v,z\in H^{1}.

(6.8)

and the corresponding bounded bilinear operator

via the trilinear form

\langle B(u,v),z\rangle=b(u,v,z),\quad u,v,z\in H^{1}.

(6.9)

This operator satisfies, for all

,

\langle B(u,v),z\rangle=-\langle B(u,z),v\rangle,\quad\langle B(u,v),v\rangle=0.

(6.10)

Finally, we define the noise forcing term. Given a real separable Hilbert space
K
we consider a
K
-cylindrical Wiener process
\{W(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
defined on a stochastic basis
(\Omega,\mathcal{F,\mathbb{F}}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})

(G1)

\exists L>0:\|\sigma(v_{1})-\sigma(v_{2})\|_{\gamma(K;H)}\leq L\|v_{1}-v_{2}\|_{H}

(6.11)

for all

K

H

We consider the stochastic damped Navier-Stokes equations, that is the equations of motion of a viscous incompressible fluid with two forcing terms, one is random and the other one is deterministic. These equations are

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}u+[-\nu\Delta u+\gamma u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p]dt=\sigma(u)\partial_{t}W+fdt,\\
&\text{div}u=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(6.12)

where the unknowns are the vector velocity

and the scalar pressure

on the right hand side is for the space correlated and white in time noise and

\sigma(u)

Projecting equations (

du(t)=[Au(t)+\gamma u(t)+B(u(t),u(t))]dt=\sigma(u(t))dW(t)+f(t)dt

with the initial condition

\mathcal{F}_{0}
-measurable random variable . Here

Now consider the reflected stochastic Navier-Stokes equation:

(6.15)

Recall

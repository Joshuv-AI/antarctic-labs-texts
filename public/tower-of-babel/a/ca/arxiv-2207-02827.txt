Electron induced nanoscale nuclear spin relaxation probed by hyperpolarization injection
William Beatrez, Arjun Pillai, Otto Janes, Dieter Suter, Ashok Ajoy

Abstract
We report on experiments that quantify the role of a central electronic spin as a relaxation source for nuclear spins in its nanoscale environment. Our strategy exploits hyperpolarization injection from the electron as a means to controllably probe an increasing number of nuclear spins in the bath, and subsequently interrogate them with high fidelity. Our experiments are focused on a model system of a nitrogen vacancy (NV) center electronic spin surrounded by several hundred C 13 {}^{13}\textrm{C} nuclear spins. We observe that the C 13 {}^{13}\textrm{C} transverse spin relaxation times vary significantly with the extent of hyperpolarization injection, allowing the ability to measure the influence of electron mediated relaxation extending over several nanometers. These results suggest interesting new means to spatially discriminate nuclear spins in a nanoscale environment, and have direct relevance to dynamic nuclear polarization and quantum sensors and memories constructed from hyperpolarized nuclei.



Introduction
– Coupled electron-nuclear spin systems are highly relevant to quantum information processing
Prokof’ev and Stamp 2000
;
Ladd et al. 2010
, quantum sensing
Degen et al. 2017
, and dynamic nuclear polarization (DNP)
Abragam and Goldman 1978
. Consider an electronic spin centrally located (position
r{=}0
) in a bath of nuclear spins within a magnetic field
B_{0}
(see Fig.
1
A-B), coupled to them via hyperfine interactions. The electrons are fast-relaxing (short
T_{1e}
), often on account of interactions with phonons
Jarmola et al. 2012
, and in turn they can serve as a relaxation source for the nuclear spins
Witzel and Sarma 2006
;
Bloembergen et al. 1947
;
Blumberg 1960
(schematically shown in Fig.
1

Hanson et al. 2008
;
Reiserer et al. 2016
;
Morton et al. 2008
, and is therefore important to quantify. It also plays a key role in determining rates of polarization transfer in DNP
Siaw et al. 2014
;
Saliba et al. 2017
;
Sarkar et al. 2021
. However, probing such relaxation influences, particularly in a spatially defined manner, is challenging. This is because experiments typically have very restricted access to spins in the bath,
i.e.
there is a limited possibility of spatially distinguishing the spins, other than a small shell where the nuclear resonance frequencies can be significantly shifted from those of the bulk
Blumberg 1960
;
Tse and Hartmann 1968
;
Horvitz 1971
;
Stern et al. 2021
;
Ramanathan 2008
. Previous nanoscale quantum sensing experiments have, for instance, been limited to proximal central spin relaxation effects in small (
<
20) spin networks
Dréau et al. 2014
;
Abobeih et al. 2019
.

In this paper, we report on experiments that study the effects of an electronic spin on nuclei over wider length scales, spanning several nanometers and involving several hundred nuclei. Our strategy (Fig.
1
D) exploits controllable hyperpolarized spin injection from the electron to the nuclear bath. The hyperpolarization time
\tau
is employed as a knob to tune length scales in the bath being probed; the polarization is carried over longer distances with increasing
\tau
. Simultaneously, the nuclei are subject to a RF driving protocol that permits continuous tracking of their magnetization over minute long periods with high signal-to-noise (SNR)

. The measurements reveal, surprisingly, that ensemble-averaged nuclear lifetimes
T_{2}^{\prime}
in the rotating frame increase dramatically with increasing polarization time
\tau

System and Protocol
– Experiments here are carried out in diamond as a model system, with central NV
Jelezko and Wrachtrup 2006
;
Manson et al. 2006
electronic spins surrounded by
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
nuclei (Fig.
1
A-B). At
{\approx}
1ppm, the NV centers are separated by
{\approx}12
nm, and
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
nuclei appear with lattice density
{\approx}
0.92 spins/nm
3
, yielding a relative NV:
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
ratio
{\sim}1{:}10^{4}

Ajoy et al. 2019a
;
Van Wyk et al. 1997
. In addition, the lattice hosts randomly positioned paramagnetic impurities (P1 centers) at a concentration
>
20ppm, which can also serve as relaxation sources (Fig.
1
A-B).

Experiments are conducted in three regimes (indicated in Fig.
1
D):
(I)
optically induced NV
{\rightarrow}{}^{13}\textrm{C}
spin injection for period
\tau
at low-field (
B_{\textrm{pol}}{=}36
mT),
(II)
rapid adiabatic transport to high field, and
(III)

{}^{13}\textrm{C}
interrogation at
B_{0}{=}7
T. For hyperpolarization (Fig.
1
E), we exploit a mechanism previously described in Ref.
Ajoy et al. 2018a
;
Ajoy et al. 2018b
;
Ajoy et al. 2020a
. Distant spins are polarized by spin diffusion driven by the internuclear dipolar Hamiltonian
Ramanathan 2008
;
Karabanov et al. 2015
;
Hovav et al. 2010
,
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{dd}}{=}\sum_{j<k}d_{jk}^{\textrm{CC}}(3I_{jz}I_{kz}-\vec{I_{j}}\cdot\vec{I_{k}})
, where
I_{j}
refer to
j^{\textrm{th}}
spin-1/2 operators, and coupling strengths
d_{jk}^{\textrm{CC}}{\propto}\gamma_{n}^{2}/r^{3}
, with magnetogyric ratio
\gamma_{n}{=}
10.7MHz/T, and
r
being the inter-nuclear distance. Bulk averaged
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
hyperpolarization under maximal conditions is
\varepsilon{\approx}0.3\%
.

Sample transport (regime
II
) occurs in
t_{s}{\approx}1\textrm{s}{\ll}T_{1n}
in a manner adiabatic with respect to the instantaneous
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
Larmor frequency, thereby preserving the hyperpolarization generated
Ajoy et al. 2019b
. Conversely, since
t_{s}{\gg}T_{1e}{\sim}1
ms
Popa et al. 2004
;
Jarmola et al. 2012
, the NV center rapidly loses hyperpolarization and ultimately returns to thermal polarization levels
{\approx}3
% in regime
III
(Fig.
1
F). Subsequently, the NV center predominantly serves as a point relaxation source for the nuclear bath (Fig.
1
C).

Ensemble
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
readout (regime
III
) employs a protocol described in Refs.
Ajoy et al. 2020b
;
Beatrez et al. 2021
.
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
spins are prepared along the transverse axis
\hat{\textbf{x}}
(
\rho_{I}{=}\varepsilon\sum_{j}I_{jx}
) on the Bloch sphere, and a train of spin-locking
\vartheta
-pulses are applied
Rhim et al. 1976
;
Rhim et al. 1973
.
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
nuclei are interrogated in windows between the pulses, allowing their dynamics to be continuously tracked with high SNR
Beatrez et al. 2022
. Signal obtained corresponds to measuring the survival probability in the
\hat{\textbf{x}}{\text{-}}\hat{\textbf{y}}
plane. The sequence operation can be described by the unitary
U(t)
. For sufficiently rapid pulsing duty cycle,
U(t){\approx}\exp(i{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{F}t)
, such that the inter-nuclear Hamiltonian is engineered to leading order in the Magnus expansion to

{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{F}{\approx}\sum_{j<k}d_{jk}^{\textrm{CC}}\left(\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{ff}}-\vec{I_{j}}\cdot\vec{I_{k}}\right)
,
where
\mathcal{H}_{\textrm{ff}}{=}I_{jz}I_{kz}+I_{jy}I_{ky}
is a flip-flop Hamiltonian
Beatrez et al. 2021
. Since
[\rho_{I}{,}{\mathcal{H}}^{(0)}_{F}]{=}0
commutes with the initial state, dipolar evolution is suppressed to leading order. As a result,
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
free induction decay lifetimes
T_{2}^{*}{\sim}1.5
ms (in the absence of spin-lock pulses), are significantly extended, here to
T_{2}^{\prime}{\gtrsim}
65.5s (see Fig.
2
A).

Theory
–
To describe the experimental observations, we construct a simple, semi-quantitative model for the nuclear polarization
p(r,t)
at coordinate
r
and time
t
. We assume centrosymmetry, a good approximation given the ensemble average in our experiments. We then model the dynamics using the differential equation,

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}p(r,t)=\frac{P_{0}}{r^{6}}-\frac{\kappa_{0}}{r^{6}}p(r,t)-\frac{1}{T_{1}}p(r,t)+D\nabla^{2}p(r,t)

(1)

where
P_{0}
denotes the rate of hyperpolarization injection and
\kappa_{0}
is the strength of spatially dependent relaxation due to the central NV center. The
1/r^{6}
scaling of the
P_{0}
term in Eq. (
1
) does not yield qualitatively different behavior compared to a
1/r^{3}
scaling but makes the equation better conditioned near
r{=}0
. In any case, the
r{\rightarrow}0
spatial regime and associated nuclear spin frozen core are outside the ambit of our experimental observations because of the relatively small
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
detection bandwidth (
{\approx}
32kHz) in our measurements. In contrast to the NV center (at
r{=}0
), we assume that the relatively dense P1 centers (
{>}
20ppm) serve as contributors to
background
relaxation of the
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
nuclei independent of their position; this is captured by the
T_{1}
term in Eq. (
1
). Finally, the last term denotes spin diffusion which we assume to be Fickian and Gaussian with a single constant
D
at all locations. This is a good approximation given the large number of
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
nuclei participating in the measured signal around every NV center
Ramanathan 2008
. For instance, a sphere of injected polarization with radius 4nm contains
{\approx}247
nuclear spins.

We solve Eq. (
1
) separately in the three regimes of the experiment (Fig.
1
D), with the solution for one regime setting the initial conditions for the next. Obtaining a precise estimate of the parameters in Eq. (
1
) is challenging and outside the scope of this paper; our goals instead are to obtain
qualitative
agreement with the experimental observations in Fig.
2
-Fig.
3
. We therefore make some simplifying assumptions to the parameters in Eq. (
1
). In regime
I
, we assume
P_{0}/\kappa_{0}{=}1/100
for simplicity (
P_{0}{=}0
in regime
III
). In regime
I
and
III
, we employ
T_{1}{=}q^{-1}T_{1,\textrm{LF}}
and
T_{1}{=}q^{-1}T_{1,\textrm{HF}}
respectively, where
T_{1,\textrm{LF}}{=}283
s and
T_{1,\textrm{HF}}{=}1520
s are the measured low and high-field bulk
{}^{13}\textrm{C}
lifetimes respectively at
\tau{=}60
s
Beatrez et al. 2022
, and
q
is a scaling factor that we employ as a free parameter in the fits. For Fig.
4
, we find good agreement with
q{=}6.75
. The latter assumption can be rationalized by the fact that (1) the individual contributions from P1 centers to
T_{1}
relaxation are hard to separate in the bulk
T_{1}
measurements; (2) longitudinal and transverse relaxation rates in regime
III
are expected to be proportional, but are in general, unequal, making the measured
T_{1,\textrm{HF}}
only approximately reflective of
T_{1}
in Eq. (
1
); and (3)
T_{1,\textrm{LF}}
is measured under dark conditions at a fixed
\tau{=}60
s, but the corresponding
T_{1}
in regime

Outlook
– These results open many interesting future avenues in a wide class of central spin systems. First, as Fig.
3

4

with respect to the central electron. Moving through time

2

4

Zhang and Cory 1998
;
Burgarth and Ajoy 2017
;
Ajoy et al. 2012
;
Boutis et al. 2004
, and the ability to rewritably engineer spin texture in the nuclear bath
King et al. 2012
;
Michal and Tycko 1999
. For instance, successive injection of positive and negative polarization from the NV center can result in non-equilibrium “shells” of nuclear polarization whose dynamics can be frozen for minute-long periods by decoupling. Ultimately, this engenders opportunities for exploiting mesoscale nuclear spin baths for quantum information science (QIS)
Foletti et al. 2009
and sensing
Sahin et al. 2021
;
Ajoy and Cappellaro 2012
;
Jaskula et al. 2019
;
Ledbetter et al. 2012
.

Our results illustrate the ability of nuclear spins to probe electronic relaxation processes, therefore providing a means to view the phonon density of states that dominate these relaxation mechanisms
Jarmola et al. 2012
;
Takahashi et al. 2008
. This is relevant to the emerging field of QIS with molecular systems
Bayliss et al. 2020
, where there is an important need to develop design rules for electronic lifetimes based on molecular vibronic properties
He et al. 2020
. It also has important implications for DNP, wherein the concentration and identity of electron spins can vastly affect nuclear polarization levels
Lange et al. 2012
. Concurrently, this suggests exciting potential for leveraging long nuclear
T_{2}^{\prime}
lifetimes in systems capable of “turning off” the electron spin after hyperpolarization injection
Capozzi et al. 2017
, for instance in molecules hosting triplet photoexcitable electrons
Henstra and Wenckebach 2014
;
Tateishi et al. 2014
;
Niketic et al. 2015
.

Conclusion
– In summary, we have experimentally measured the influence of a central electronic spin in relaxing the surrounding nuclear spin bath. We exploited controlled hyperpolarization injection as a knob to control length scales over which bath spins are probed, extending over several nanometers and hundreds of spins. Our work informs on a new means to probe nanoscale spin environments, and portends applications in quantum memories and sensors constructed out of hyperpolarized nuclei.

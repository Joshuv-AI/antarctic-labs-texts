at
p=n^{2-r}
has maximum 2-degree at most
O(\log n)
, so we can apply Lemma
6.2
to find a large linear subgraph
H\subseteq H_{n,p}^{r}
and the rest of the proof works out as before.
∎

7
Concluding Remarks

\bullet
Using arguments analogous to the ones used throughout the paper, it is not too difficult to show for any
F\in\mathcal{C}_{4}^{3}\setminus\{C_{4}^{3},\mathbf{F}\}
that
\mathrm{ex}(H,F)\geq\Delta^{-1/2-o(1)}e(H)
whenever
H
has maximum degree at most
\Delta
, and this is often tight by considering
H
to be the clique. However, our best bounds for
C_{4}^{3}
and
\mathbf{F}
given by Theorems
1.3
and
1.5
still have significant gaps.

\bullet
Theorem
1.5
shows that
\mathrm{ex}(H,\mathbf{F})\geq\Delta^{-3/5-o(1)}e(H)
for any host
H
with maximum degree at most
\Delta
. It is known that
\mathrm{ex}(n,\mathbf{F})=\Theta(n^{2})
, so taking
H=K_{\sqrt{\Delta}}^{3}
gives
\mathrm{ex}(H,\mathbf{F})=O(\Delta^{-1/2})e(H)
. It is unclear which of these bounds is closer to the truth, and we leave the following as an open problem.

Problem 7.1
.

Determine whether

\mathrm{ex}(H,\mathbf{F})\geq\Delta^{-1/2-o(1)}\cdot e(H)

whenever
H
is a 3-graph with maximum degree
\Delta
.

\bullet
While Proposition
1.4
gives essentially tight bounds for loose even cycles in linear hosts, our bounds are far from tight for general hosts. In particular, we do not know tight bounds for
C_{4}^{3}
.

Problem 7.2
.

Determine whether there exists a 3-graph
H
with maximum degree
\Delta
and

\mathrm{ex}(H,C_{4}^{3})\leq\Delta^{-3/4+o(1)}\cdot e(H).

A reasonable candidate for such an
H
is
H_{n,p}
with
p=n^{-2/3}
. It is conjectured by Mubayi and Yepremyan
[
21
]
that
\mathrm{ex}(H_{n,p}^{3},C_{4}^{3})\leq n^{4/3+o(1)}
a.a.s. at
p=n^{-2/3}
, and this can be rephrased as saying
\mathrm{ex}(H_{n,p}^{3},C_{4}^{3})\leq\Delta^{-3/4+o(1)}e(H)
a.a.s. Conversely, if there existed a method which improved upon the known lower bounds for
\mathrm{ex}(H_{n,p}^{3},C_{4}^{3})
, then it is possible that this method could also be used to improve lower bounds for general hosts
H
.

\bullet
Variants of Propositions
2.1
and
3.1
were used to prove essentially tight bounds on the relative Turán numbers for certain kinds of complete
r
-partite
r
-graphs
[
27
]
, and we suspect that these propositions will continue to be of use for future investigations into relative Turán numbers of hypergraphs.

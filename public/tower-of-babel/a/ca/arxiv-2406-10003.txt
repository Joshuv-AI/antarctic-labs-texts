Steady state, displacement current and spin polarization for massless fermion in a shear flow
Shu Lin, Ziyue Wang

Abstract
We consider spin polarization of massless fermions in a shear flow, whose complete contributions contain magnetization current and side-jump current known from collisional chiral kinetic theory. We argue that the side-jump current adopts interpretation of displacement current. We explicitly determine the displacement current contribution in the steady state reached in shear flow for a QED plasma. We find the displacement contribution enhances the magnetization contribution at small and large momenta, but leads to a suppression effect at intermediate momenta. Major differences from previous studies on collisional effect are: (i) the fermions are in the same steady state as the medium rather than being probes; (ii) Compton scattering and pair annihilation are also included in addition to the Coulomb scattering considered before.

2
Side-jump current as displacement current

Let us first recall the interpretation of the term
S^{{\mu}{\nu}}{\partial}_{\nu}f

[
25
]
. In the frame
n^{\mu}=(1,0,0,0)
, we have

\displaystyle S^{ij}{\partial}_{j}f=\nabla\times{\lambda}\hat{p}f=\nabla\times{\bf M},

(3)

with
M
being the magnetic moment of massless fermions. This leads to the interpretation of magnetization current. In Maxwell theory, the complete contribution to current also includes displacement current

\displaystyle{\bf j}={\partial}_{t}{\bf P}+\nabla\times{\bf M},

(4)

with
P
being the electric polarization. Unlike the magnetization current, which exists in non-interacting systems, the displacement current requires interaction. This is in line with the collision dependent side-jump current. We will argue that the side-jump current indeed adopts interpretation of displacement current. For simplicity, we consider homogeneous distribution in frame
n
, i.e.
{\partial}_{i}f=0
but
{\partial}_{t}f\neq 0
. In this case, we find the corresponding distribution function
f
being the same as the counterpart
{\bar{f}}
in no-jump frame according to Eq (16) of
[
24
]

\displaystyle{\bar{f}}-f=-\bar{{\Delta}}\cdot{\partial}f+\int_{BCD}\frac{{\bar{\Delta}}\cdot{\bar{n}}}{p\cdot{\bar{n}}}=0.

(5)

The first term on the RHS vanishes because
{\partial}_{i}f=0
and
\bar{\Delta}
has only nonvanishing spatial components in frame
n
. The second term vanishes identically. It follows that the kinetic equation takes the simple form as in the no-jump frame

\displaystyle p\cdot{\partial}f=\int_{BCD}C_{ABCD}.

(6)

The electric polarization comes from displacement of charge. A natural candidate for it would be
P^{i}={\bar{\Delta}}^{i}p_{0}f
with the side-jump identified as the displacement viewed in frame
n
and
p_{0}f
as density. However, since the side-jump depends on momentum of the other particle in the collision, we need to use an averaged quantity

\displaystyle P^{i}=\langle{\bar{\Delta}}^{i}\rangle p_{0}f.

(7)

The detail of the average will be clear soon. Now the displacement current becomes

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}P^{i}={\partial}_{t}\left(\langle{\bar{\Delta}}^{i}\rangle p_{0}f\right)=\langle{\bar{\Delta}}^{i}\rangle p_{0}{\partial}_{t}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\bar{\Delta}}^{i}\rangle\int_{BCD}C_{ABCD},

(8)

where in the second line we have used the kinetic equation. If we choose the average
\langle{\bar{\Delta}}^{i}\rangle=\frac{\int_{BCD}C_{ABCD}{\bar{\Delta}}^{i}}{\int_{BCD}C_{ABCD}}
, we easily confirm the displacement current interpretation of side-jump current:

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}P^{i}=\int_{BCD}C_{ABCD}{\bar{\Delta}}^{i}.

(9)

The difference between the right-handed and left-handed currents gives the spin polarization. The difference of the conducting current (first term on the RHS of (
1
)) corresponds to the spin carried by individual particles as
\int dp_{0}{\delta}(P^{2})p^{i}(f_{R}-f_{L})=\hat{p}^{i}(f_{R}-f_{L})/2
. The counterpart of the magnetization and displacement current correspond to gradient contributions needed to restore frame independence.

3
Steady state and collisional effect in a shear flow

Now we consider fermion in a steady state due to a shear flow. We choose the frame vector to be the fluid velocity vector
n^{\mu}=u^{\mu}

\displaystyle S^{i}_{\text{magn}}\sim\left(S_{{\lambda}=1/2}^{ij}-S_{{\lambda}=-1/2}^{ij}\right){\partial}_{j}f_{V}\sim-{\epsilon}^{ijk}p_{k}p_{l}{\partial}_{j}u_{l}f_{V}^{\prime}

\displaystyle p^{\mu}{\partial}_{\mu}f=C[f]\rightarrow p_{i}p_{j}{\sigma}_{ij}f_{V}^{\prime}={\delta}C,

Without the shear tensor, the collision term vanishes by detailed balance, so we have replaced

-f_{p}\Sigma^{>}_{\mu}(P)-(1-f_{p})\Sigma^{<}_{\mu}(P)

\Sigma^{>}_{\mu}(P)
contains contribution from Coulomb scattering, Compton scattering and annihilation at LL order, namely

-f_{p}\Sigma^{>}_{\mu}(P)
from the product of distribution functions in as loss term. Similar analysis allows us to identify the term
-(1-f_{p})\Sigma^{<}_{\mu}(P)
as gain term. Crucially they do not cancel in the steady state, which can be seen in the following explicit expression

\displaystyle-f_{p}\Sigma^{>}_{\mu}(P)-(1-f_{p})\Sigma^{<}_{\mu}(P)

\displaystyle=\;\;

\displaystyle 4e^{4}(2\pi)^{3}\int_{K,K^{\prime},P^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{4}\delta^{4}(P+K-P^{\prime}-K^{\prime})\delta(P^{\prime 2})\delta(K^{2})\delta(K^{\prime 2})

\displaystyle\times\Big[M_{\mu}^{Coul}\big(I_{ij}(\hat{p})\chi_{p}+I_{ij}(\hat{k})\chi_{k}-I_{ij}(\hat{k^{\prime}})\chi_{k^{\prime}}-I_{ij}(\hat{p^{\prime}})\chi_{p^{\prime}}\big)f^{\text{eq}}_{p}f^{\text{eq}}_{k}(1-f^{\text{eq}}_{k^{\prime}})(1-f^{\text{eq}}_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle+M_{\mu}^{Comp}\big(I_{ij}(\hat{p})\chi_{p}+I_{ij}(\hat{k})\gamma_{k}-I_{ij}(\hat{k^{\prime}})\chi_{k^{\prime}}-I_{ij}(\hat{p^{\prime}})\gamma_{p^{\prime}}\big)f^{\text{eq}}_{p}f^{\text{eq}}_{\gamma,k}(1-f^{\text{eq}}_{k^{\prime}})(1+f^{\text{eq}}_{\gamma,p^{\prime}})

(28)

where we have linearized in the shear tensor and defined the following quantities

\displaystyle M_{\mu}^{Coul}

\displaystyle=\frac{2N_{f}\cdot 2[P\cdot K(2K_{\mu}-Q_{\mu})]}{(Q^{2})^{2}},

\displaystyle M_{\mu}^{Comp}

\displaystyle=M_{\mu}^{anni}=-\frac{-K_{\mu}}{Q^{2}},

which can be viewed as Coulomb, Compton scattering and pair annihilation amplitude squares times
\bar{{\Delta}}_{\mu}
comparing to (

To make full use of Lorentz invariance, we rewrite (

with

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(u_{\mu}I_{\alpha\beta}T_{1}+\hat{P}_{\perp\mu}I_{\alpha\beta}T_{2}+J_{\mu,\alpha\beta}T_{3})f^{\text{eq}}_{p}(1-f^{\text{eq}}_{p}),

with the last structure defined as

The factor

is introduced for convenience.

In (

\displaystyle T_{3}

\displaystyle=

(36)

\displaystyle+\frac{(C_{b}-C_{f})\pi^{4}T^{2}}{48p}\frac{1+f^{\text{eq}}_{\gamma,p}}{1-f^{\text{eq}}_{p}},

with

5
Conclusion and Outlook

We have considered spin polarization of chiral fermion in a shear flow. We have made close connection with the collisional chiral kinetic theory, identifying conducting, magnetization and displacement current contributions to spin polarization. While each contribution is frame dependent, their sum is not. In the frame set by the fluid’s rest frame, we find the conducting current contribution vanishes. The magnetization current contribution widely used in phenomenological studies is independent of interaction. The displacement current contribution does depends on interaction. It is nonvanishing in the steady state reached in the shear flow. We determine this contribution with QED interaction, in which we have found nontrivial momentum dependence: the displacement current contribution enhances the magnetization current contribution for fermions at small and large momenta, but leads to a suppression effect at intermediate momenta. This might help understand the local polarization measurement of
{\Lambda}
hyperon in heavy ion collisions.

The massless limit plays a special role in our study. In this limit, the spin is enslaved by fermion momentum, leading to the vanishing of spin contribution to polarization in a shear flow. This will not be true for massive fermions. An obvious generalization is to consider massive fermions. A qualitative new feature is that the massive counterpart of the conducting current contribution needs to be determined dynamically. In the steady state reached in a shear flow, one can still determine it by solving the axial kinetic equation. We leave this for future studies.

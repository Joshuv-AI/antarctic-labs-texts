Superposition theorem for flexible grids
Antoine Marot, Noureddine Henka, Benjamin Donnot, Sami Tazi, IEEE members, Antoine Marot & Benjamin Donnot, Noureddine Henka & Sami Tazi

Abstract
Flexible grid topology has become a key enabler of flexibility in modern power grids, particularly for congestion management. Studying the effects of combinatorial topological changes is therefore of significant interest, though it remains computationally intensive in most cases. To address this, we revisit the superposition theorem, which has served as the foundation for the decomposition of numerous power system problems over the past decades, particularly those involving changes in generation and loads. However, its application has traditionally been restricted to fixed grid topologies, breaking down as soon as a topology change occurs. In this paper, we extend the superposition theorem to accommodate varying grid topologies by leveraging well-known distribution factors. This unified framework applies to all types of topological changes, including line disconnection and reconnection, as well as bus splitting and merging. We provide numerical experiments to validate our approach and highlight its advantages in terms of speed-up and interpretability. Finally, we demonstrate its application in two key use cases: remedial action search and updated security analysis following a topological change. Our results show that the proposed approach achieves more than an order-of-magnitude speed-up for real-sized grids compared to the fastest power flow solvers.

Introduction

To succeed at the energy system decarbonization and mitigate the effect of climate change, the largest emitting countries are undergoing a steep Energy Transition worldwide. Power systems are at the forefront of this transition, responsible for integrating a high share of renewable energy but also enabling greater electrification of usages, while accommodating for better efficiencies. For existing power grids, we are moving from a mostly predictable environment with known demand patterns, controllable generation, and middle life reliable infrastructure assets to an environment with a lot more uncertainties given intermittent generation, aging infrastructure requiring more outage management, active demand, and numerous market players

. In addition, more care must be taken to the development and use of resources given ecological considerations. Hence, more flexible operations with existing infrastructure are needed now. In particular, one could make the grid more flexible by dynamically adapting the grid topology, leveraging this overall but currently underutilized flexibility especially in meshed areas. Indeed, a change in topology changes the line power flows that one wants to monitor or control to avoid congestions. The topology can hence be regarded as an important variable. It can help, in particular, keep the grid safe from congestions while integrating renewables at scale

With regard to generalization, the second research avenue based on OPF and OTS has been paving the way towards it by aiming to frame things into a single optimization problem. Usage of mixed topological changes including line disconnections, bus splitting and merging, could already be found in

II
Extended superposition theorem framework

We consider a grid with generation g and load distribution d at
n_{bus}
electrical nodes, represented by the nodal power balance vector
P_{g-d}
. Branches connections at every node represent the grid topology that we denote by
T
. Our aim is to compute power flows
PF(P_{g-d},\mathrm{\textit{T}})
. In this paper, We restrict ourselves to the DC approximation
[
34
]
and assume that the grid remains connected into one single component.

II-A

Standard Superposition Theorem

Given a reference nodal power balance vector
P_{g-d}^{ref}
and k additions of generation and load changes
\delta P_{g-d}^{k}
(for instance a change of +10MW at a node and -10MW at another) summing up to target nodal power balance vector
P_{g-d}^{tgt}
, the standard ST provides a sum decomposition of resulting power flows under the same reference grid topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}
:

PF(P_{g-d}^{tgt},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})=PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{k}PF(\delta P_{g-d}^{k},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})\\
\text{with }P_{g-d}^{tgt}=P_{g-d}^{ref}+\sum_{k}\delta P_{g-d}^{k}

(1)

Thus, one can infer a target grid state without resolving the power flow equations again if the initial and unitary grid states are known. ST has proven very useful for decomposing the problem analytically, allowing for either more efficient computations or better interpretability when analyzing some grid phenomena. We aim to transpose this handy tool to topological changes.

II-B

Extended Superposition Theorem

To extend it similarly to topological changes, we start from
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}
as the reference topology to which we can apply unitary topological changes
\tau_{i}
each resulting in topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{i}}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}\odot\tau_{i}
. When combined in indifferent order, we reach a target topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}\odot\tau_{1}\odot...\odot\tau_{n_{\tau}}
. As illustrated in Figure
1
, we will prove the following Extended Superposition Theorem (EST):

PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt})=\alpha PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\tau}}\beta^{i}PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{i}})\\
\text{with }\alpha=1-\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\tau}}\beta^{i}

(2)

Note that this decomposition is a weighted linear combination instead of a pure linear one as before. This requires us to compute these weights. We will demonstrate that finding the
\beta^{i}
stems from solving a linear system of dimension the number of considered unitary changes
n_{\tau}
:

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}1&\text{tsf}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{2}}}_{l_{1}}&...&\text{tsf}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{n}}}_{l_{1}}\\
\text{tsf}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{1}}}_{l_{2}}&1&...&\text{tsf}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{n}}}_{l_{2}}\\
...&...&...&...\\
\text{tsf}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{1}}}_{l_{n}}&\text{tsf}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{2}}}_{l_{n}}&...&1\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\beta 1\\
\beta 2\\
...\\
\beta n\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
1\\
...\\
1\end{bmatrix}

(3)

Where
\text{tsf}^{T^{\tau_{i}}}_{l_{j}}
are Topology Sensitivity factors (TSF) reflecting the change in power flow of asset
l_{j}
after a unitary topology change
\tau_{i}
applied on asset
l_{i}
from
T^{ref}
:

\text{tsf}^{T^{\tau_{i}}}_{l_{j}}=\frac{x^{T^{ref}}_{l_{j}}-x^{T^{\tau_{i}}}_{l_{j}}}{x^{T^{ref}}_{l_{j}}}\text{with }x_{l_{j}}=pf_{l_{j}}\text{ or }\Delta\theta_{l_{j}}\text{ given }\tau_{i}

(4)

pf_{l_{j}}
is the power flowing in asset
l_{j}
and
\Delta\theta_{l_{j}}
the voltage angle difference at its extremities. Given
\sigma_{l_{j}}
this asset’s conductivity, they are tied to Ohm’s law when connected:

pf_{l_{j}}=\sigma_{l_{j}}\Delta\theta_{l_{j}}

(5)

Yet (
3
) and (
4
) only rely on the knowledge of reference and unitary topological change power flows.
\beta^{i}
coefficients are functions of those states only
\beta^{i}=f^{i}(PF(T^{ref}),PF(T^{\tau_{1}}),...,PF(T^{\tau_{n_{\tau}}}))
using simpler notation
PF(T)=PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}})
. This ensures that equation (
2
) is indeed a true superposition of these single states.
Considering that such measurements for assets switched and of interest solely are available, no other knowledge is needed, such as measurements on other assets, intrinsic grid properties, complete adjacency matrix or full topology information. This will make the computation frugal without resolving again any power flow equations.

Fig. 1
:
EST example on IEEE14, starting from a meshed topology (top left) to which 2 node splitting actions at substations 4 and 5 are applied (bottom right). Displayed EST coefficients are derived from initial and unitary action states.

This linear system form is generic to all four types of topological changes, as we will demonstrate in the next sections. Therefore this makes it a proper candidate for achieving a unified framework for combining them all in a single and direct computation scheme. In the next sections, we provide a step-by-step demonstration of all four types of change.

III
EST for line disconnections

In this section, we (re)-demonstrate with our formalism how this EST applies to line disconnections
o\in O
in the first place. It relates to well-known LODFs, and could have similarly been based on other well-known PTDFs.

We first set up the stage for this demonstration and later ones across the paper by revisiting a useful equivalent modeling.

III-A

Two equivalent models of line disconnections

We now describe an equivalent grid state model for disconnected lines that will be the foundation for EST demonstrations.
Disconnecting a line
o
resulting in topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{o}
is actually equivalent to virtually injecting a canceling flow
cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}
along that line
o
at virtual buses infinitely
close to the two line extremity buses
[
28
,
12
]
, while keeping this line virtually connected. To inject such a canceling flow, one can add a load and generator of its power value distributed at those two virtual buses. This also generalizes to multiple line disconnections that belong to a set
O
, resulting in topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}
, keeping all of these lines virtually connected but ensuring that their flow is null by injecting canceling flows at these lines
as in Figure
2
. Note that for multiple line disconnections, canceling flows are different from their initial line flows, as influences between the line flows have to be taken into account.

Fig. 2
:
Top left, power Flows in the reference topology for the two lines to disconnect. Right and bottom left, the two lines disconnected in two equivalent models: the standard one with physical line disconnections on top, and the canceling flow model at the bottom. The lines remains virtually connected but with equivalent null flow: the virtual induced flow
vf_{l}
gets canceled out by the reversed direction canceling flow
cf_{l}
.

Given
n_{O}
disconnected lines from
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}
, this canceling flow injections equivalence can be represented by:

PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})=PF({P^{\prime}}_{g-d}^{ref}+\sum_{o\in O}Cf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{o},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})

(6)

Cf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{o}
of dimension
n_{bus}+2n_{O}
is here the nodal injection vector form of
cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}
with non-null elements of positive and negative
cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}
at the virtual buses infinitely close to line
l
extremities, and null nodal values otherwise. Note that
{P^{\prime}}_{g-d}^{ref}
is
P_{g-d}^{ref}
augmented with
2n_{O}
null elements to match
Cf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{o}
dimension given
2n_{O}
virtual buses to consider.

The grid topology in the virtual injection model is
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}
as the lines remain virtually connected.
These models are equivalent, as they result in the same power flows. Given Ohm’s law, this also implies that voltage angles at nodes also remain the same under the same reference voltage angle node.
In this equivalent model, there exist in addition a ohm’s law induced virtual power flow
vf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{o}
at line
o
still virtually connected:

vf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}=\sigma_{o}\Delta\theta_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}

(7)

As this equivalent model aims at representing the system state following the disconnections of lines
o\in O
, the resulting virtual flows
vf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{o}
should be exactly compensated by their canceling flows as in Figure
2
:

vf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}-cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}=0\text{ for all }l\in O

(8)

This equivalent modeling is of interest since one can apply standard ST (
1
) to (
6
) such that:

PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})=PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{o\in O}PF(Cf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{o},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})

(9)

This is the foundational step for further deriving this EST for topological changes as we will see. However, we will need to derive
Cf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{o}
for all lines
o\in O
of interest.

III-B

Line disconnections demonstration

Let’s first consider the restricted topological changes case of line disconnections
o\in O
. We depart from a reference topology in which these lines are connected
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R}
and reach a topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}
with all lines disconnected
o\in O
.
The proposed EST in this case has already been indirectly demonstrated in the Generalize LODF paper
[
28
]
. Indeed, they compute multi-outage power flow solving a linear system of LODF, which is composed of reference and unitary outage power flow states.

To derive the linear system of equations to solve in our formalism, we work with the equivalent virtual injection models. In the one-line
o
disconnection case, the virtual flow is equivalent to the initial power flow
vf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus o}}}=pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}
. As from (
8
), it is canceled in the reverse direction by a canceling flow of a similar value
cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus o}}}=pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}
.

In the multiple line disconnection case, virtually injected canceling flows on other lines to disconnect (but virtually connected in the equivalent model) induce additional virtual flows on the line of interest as from (
9
):

PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt})=PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{o\in O}PF(Cf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}_{o},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})

(10)

To derrive the new powerflow terms, we use LODFs with effect on line
l
after line outage
o
:

lodf_{o,l}=\frac{pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus o}}}-pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}}{pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}}

(11)

LODF_{o}
is the vector form of outage
o
effect for all lines
l
.
Note that LODF remain constant for a given topology such as
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}
. This is also a well-defined term even when
pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}=0
. In fact, in that case, this is as if the line has already been disconnected from the start. Disconnecting it will not change the flows at all. Hence, the numerator will be null as well, and the resulting LODF will be null too.

In the equivalent modeling, as its topology remains
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}
, we can hence reuse the same LODF computed in
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}
.

Using
LODF
,
PF(Cf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}_{o},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})
is simply:

PF(Cf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}_{o},\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})=LODF_{o}\times cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}

(12)

This eventually leads by substitution to the equation:

PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt})=PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{o\in O}LODF_{o}\times cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}

(13)

As
pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}=0
for disconnected lines, this leads to the system of
n_{O}
independent canceling flow equations:

pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}+\sum_{o\in O}lodf_{o,l}\times cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}=0\text{ for all }l\in O\\
pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}+\sum_{o\in O,o\neq l}lodf_{o,l}\times cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}=cf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}\text{ as $lodf_{l,l}=-1$ }

(14)

From (
8
) the virtual induced flow
vf_{l}
value on line
l
is then:

vf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}=cf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}=pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}+\sum_{o\in O,o\neq l}lodf_{o,l}\times cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}\\

(15)

The other canceling flow contributions are added up to the initial flow
pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}
to result in the induced flow. Substituting LODF definition from (
11
) in (
12
) the EST is:

\begin{split}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt})&=PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{o\in O}(PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus o}})-PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}))\frac{cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}}{pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}}\\
&=\alpha\times PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{o\in O}\beta^{o}\times PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus o}})\end{split}\\
\text{with }\alpha=(1-\sum_{o\in O}\frac{cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}}{pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}})\text{ and }\beta^{l}=\frac{cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}}{pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}}

(16)

where we find that:

\alpha=1-\sum_{o\in O}\beta^{o}

(17)

Substituting
\beta^{o}
for
cf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}}
in (
14
), we have:

pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}+\sum_{o\in O}lodf_{o,l}\times\beta^{o}pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}=0\text{ for all }l\in O\\
pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}=-\sum_{o\in O}lodf_{o,l}\times\beta^{o}pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}\text{ for all }l\in O

(18)

And reusing LODF definition from (
11
) we recover (
3
):

pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}=\beta^{l}\times pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}+\sum_{o\in O,o\neq l}(\frac{pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}-pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus o}}}}{pf_{o}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}})\beta^{o}pf_{o}^{ref}\\
1=\beta^{l}\times 1+\sum_{o\in O,o\neq l}(1-\frac{pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus o}}}}{pf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}})\beta^{o}\text{ for all }l\in O\\
1=\beta^{l}\times 1+\sum_{o\in O,o\neq l}tsf_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus o}}}\beta^{o}\text{ for all }l\in O

(19)

We see from (
13
) or (
16
) that it solely relies here on reusing known quantities
PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})
,
PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus o}})
, and requires one to solve an additional linear system of equations of the size of the number of topological changes

IV
EST for Line reconnections

In this section, we consider line reconnections
\tau_{=l}
for lines
l\in R
to reach a target topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R}
departing from a reference topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}
in which those lines were initially not connected.
So we change states in a reverse order from Figure
2
: the reference state is the one with disconnected lines at the bottom, and the target state is with connected lines as at the top. Here we cannot reuse the linear system (
14
) as is, as there is no equivalent of
pf^{ref}_{l}
for initially disconnected lines
l
in this case. However, we can reuse the EST for line disconnections from previous section
III-B
, to prove it for reconnections such that:

PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt})=\alpha_{R}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}})\\
\text{with }\alpha_{R}=1-\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}

(20)

In the following, we will first demonstrate that this superposition exists and is unique. We will further identify the underlying linear system to solve, to derive the coefficients.

IV-
1
Demonstration

We first demonstrate the EST unicity and further its existence.

Proof.

Unicity
.
From (

), we can deduce that, when

This is due to line

flow being non-null only in topologies

and

in which the line is connected. For all the other topologies,

Given the uniqueness of powerflows, this proves the uniqueness of

after deriving the coefficients

When

is null, this is the case when reconnecting this single line does not affect the reference state, resulting in the same reference state

Proof.

Existence.

\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus l_{2}}}_{R}

l_{2}

\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R}\circ\tau_{\setminus l_{2}}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}\circ\tau_{=l_{1}}
.

So
PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus l_{2}}}_{R})=PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{1}}}_{O})
and
PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus l_{1}}}_{R})=PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{2}}}_{O})
.

This time
PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})=PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt})
is the superposed state we are looking for. So rearranging (

) leads to:

PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})=\frac{1}{\alpha_{O}}(PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})-\beta^{l_{1}}_{O}\times PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{2}}}_{R})-\beta^{l_{2}}_{O}\times PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{1}}}_{R}))\\
PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})=\alpha_{R}\times PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})+\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}\times PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}}_{O})\\
PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt})=\alpha_{R}\times PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}\times PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}})\\
\text{with }\alpha_{R}=\frac{1}{\alpha_{O}}\text{, }\beta_{R}^{l_{1}}=\frac{-\beta^{l_{2}}_{O}}{\alpha_{O}}\text{, }\beta_{R}^{l_{2}}=\frac{-\beta^{l_{1}}_{O}}{\alpha_{O}}

(23)

From (

), we have:

\frac{1}{\alpha_{O}}=1+\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}\frac{-\beta^{l_{i}}_{O}}{\alpha_{O}}

(24)

So we recover:

\alpha_{R}=1-\sum_{i\in\{1,2\}}\beta^{l_{i}}_{R}

(25)

This works out when
\alpha_{O}
is non null. As
\alpha_{R}
and
\beta^{l_{i}}
are all properly defined from (

) and cannot be infinite,
\alpha_{O}
is indeed never null.
For more than two line reconnections, we can
recursively
apply (

). We leave this complimentary demonstration to the Appendix

. By applying the same recursion, we find for any number of line reconnection the existence of an EST:

PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt})=\alpha_{R}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}})

Interpretation - canceling voltage angle difference equivalent model and LCDF

We find the same equations as for line disconnections, but considering
\Delta\theta_{l}
instead of
pf_{l}
.
An equivalent model is hence of canceling voltage angle difference instead of canceling flows. We recover the physical reasoning of a line reconnection equivalent model from
[
20
]
:

•

starting with the line disconnected in reference topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}
, inject some nodal power that cancels out the voltage angle difference
\Delta\theta_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}
at line
l
when disconnected originally

•

then physically reconnect line
l
. This does not induce any effect on the state as its flow remains null given that the voltage angle difference has been canceled out just before. It is closed without impacting the system.

•

finally remove the injected balance power to retrieve the desired target state, that is with line
l
reconnected

The problem hence reduces to finding those voltage angle difference injections. The same equations as for line disconnections therefore exist, one solely needs to replace
pf_{l}
by
\Delta\theta_{l}
. So analogously to LODF, we can define LCDF (line closing distribution factors) such as:

LCDF_{r,l}=\frac{\Delta\theta_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=r}}}-\Delta\theta_{l}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}}{\Delta\theta_{r}^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref}}}

(35)

And similarly to (
18
) the system of equations to solve could be rewritten and interpreted as:

\Delta\theta^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{l}+\sum_{r\in R}LCDF_{r,l}\times\beta^{r}\Delta\theta^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{r}=0

(36)

V-B

Node splitting and merging topological changes

A node-splitting change can be modeled through a null-impedance virtual line disconnection
[
35
]
in between the two target nodes and conversely a node merging as a virtual line reconnection. Physically, one could represent this virtual line as a coupling breaker opened or closed between 2 bus bars that represents the nodes. Previous ST demonstrations and systems of linear equations directly apply to those changes as no hypothesis or usage of grid properties such as line impedance were made, with only reliance on grid state knowledge.

For node merging,
\Delta\theta_{nodes}
between the two nodes to be merged can be used in the equations. For node splitting, the flow
pf_{nodes}^{T}
through the null-impedance line virtually connecting the two virtual nodes, not yet split, needs to be computed. It can be done based on line flows at the substation which results in a residual power flow at each virtual node, as they are not split yet:

pf_{nodes}^{T}=\sum_{l_{i}\in node_{1}}pf_{l_{i}}^{T}

(48)

As node splitting can be assimilated to line disconnection and node merging to line reconnection, they fall under the same system (
47
) which hence integrates all four types of topological changes.

VI-B

Topological Action Security Analysis

Security analysis (SA) is an application at the core of power system operations. It has been quite optimized over the years, reusing for instance the same KLU matrix factorization for all N-1 contingency computations. However, when a topological change is applied, one might need to be able to assess its robustness and recompute the SA quickly.

The EST can help reduce the computation time required for SA joined with topological actions. For experiments, for each grid under test, we select two random topological actions that do not break the grid. We then calculate the line outage SA for these two actions. Figure
4
compares the computation time for the resulting SA. LightSim compiled C++ solver, once the topological change is computed and applied once, reuses the corresponding matrix factorization to optimize computation. This is hence a challenging baseline for our straightforward python implementation.

Our EST’s python implementation is always several order of magnitude faster than pandapower, the python powerflow solver. For grids larger than 100 buses, the proposed approach exhibits faster computation compared to compiled power-flow solvers and scales linearly in the number of contingencies after this stage. We can observe that when considering a similar number of topological changes (a contingency plus topological action), the proposed technique solves an identical equation system regardless of the grid’s size, explaining this scalability. It becomes at least an order of magnitude faster for grids larger than 1000 buses in its standard industrial SA version with KLU. Even for the most optimized C++ SA version that relies on PTDFs in the specific case of DC approximation, we still observe a speed-up factor of 5 or more.

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we have demonstrated the existence and unicity of an Extended Superposition Theorem for all types of unitary topological changes and their mixture. We have seen the speed-up and interpretability it can bring to power flow computations and their analysis. In particular, applications for remedial action search and security analysis can already benefit from it. We believe that it can be of very generic use and a foundation for improvements in many applications as well as integrated in optimization formulations, such as in optimal power flow. -A

Multiple lines reconnection recursive demonstration

After the two-line reconnection case in section
IV
, we now consider the reconnection of three lines in
R=\{l_{1},l_{2},l_{3}\}
from a topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}
in which the three are disconnected to reach a topology
\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R}
in which they are all reconnected. We also consider the subsets of two reconnected lines defined as
R_{\setminus l_{i}}
for which the third line
l_{i}
remains disconnected. EST coefficients
\alpha_{R}
and
\beta^{l_{i}}_{R}
for superset
R
will be derived as a recursive formula from previous rank subsets
R_{\setminus l_{i}}
.

We start by reusing EST for line disconnections as previously:

PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})=\alpha_{O}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})+\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta^{l_{i}}_{O}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus l_{i}}}_{R})

(49)

Applying (
23
) for two line reconnections, since
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus l_{1}}}_{R}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R}\circ\tau_{\setminus l_{1}}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}\circ\tau_{=l_{2}}\circ\tau_{=l_{3}}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=\{l_{2},l_{3}\}}}_{O}
, we now have here :

\begin{split}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus l_{1}}}_{R})&=PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=\{l_{2},l_{3}\}}}_{O})\\
&=\alpha_{R_{\setminus l_{1}}}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})+\sum_{l_{j}\in R_{\setminus l_{1}}}\beta_{R_{\setminus l_{1}}}^{l_{j}}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{j}}}_{O})\end{split}

(50)

Transposing (
50
) to each
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{\setminus l_{i}}}
for
i\in{1,2,3}
and substituting it in (
49
) we obtain:

PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})=\alpha_{O}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})\\
+\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta^{l_{i}}_{O}(\alpha_{R_{\setminus l_{i}}}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})+\sum_{l_{j}\in R_{\setminus l_{i}}}\beta_{R_{\setminus l_{i}}}^{l_{j}}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{j}}}_{O}))

(51)

To finally reach:

PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})=\frac{1}{\alpha_{O}}((1-\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta^{l_{i}}_{O}(\alpha_{R_{\setminus l_{i}}}))PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})+\\
(-\beta^{l_{i}}_{O}\sum_{l_{j}\in R_{\setminus l_{i}}}\beta_{R_{\setminus l_{i}}}^{l_{j}})PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{j}}}_{O}))

(52)

We recover the ST equation:

PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})=\alpha_{R}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O})+\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}}_{O})\\
PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt})=\alpha_{R}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{ref})+\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}PF(\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}})\\
\text{with }\alpha_{R}=\frac{1-\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta^{l_{i}}_{O}\alpha_{R_{\setminus l_{i}}}}{\alpha_{O}}\\
\text{ and }\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}=\frac{-\beta^{l_{i}}_{O}\sum_{l_{j}\in R_{\setminus l_{i}}}\beta_{R_{\setminus l_{i}}}^{l_{j}}}{\alpha_{O}}\\

(53)

Again, this works out when
\alpha_{O}
is non-null. As
\alpha_{R}
and
\beta^{l_{i}}
are all properly defined from (
21
) and cannot be infinite,
\alpha_{O}
is in fact never null.

Reusing (
25
) we have:

\alpha_{R_{\setminus l_{i}}}=1-\sum_{j\in\{1,2,3\},j\neq i}\beta_{R_{\setminus l_{i}}}^{j}

(54)

Which we substitutes in (
53
) to recover:

\alpha_{R}=1-\sum_{i\in\{1,2,3\}}\beta^{l_{i}}_{R}

(55)

The same recursion applies for cases of more than 3 line disconnections, as all above equations hold for the next recursive ranks.

-B

Injected canceling flows cancel out

We here demonstrate that unknown injected canceling flows
cf_{l}
cancels out in the EST equation as required in section
IV-2
.

In the equivalent model of canceling flows (
6
), considering the base topology with lines reconnected in
\mathrm{\textit{T}}^{tgt}=\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R}
, equation (
26
) can be rewritten as:

PF(P_{g-d}^{ref},\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})=\alpha_{R}PF(P_{g-d}^{ref}+Cf^{T_{O}},\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})\\
+\sum_{l_{i}\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}PF(P_{g-d}^{ref}+Cf^{T_{O}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}}},\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{R})

(56)

Note the canceling flow additions on the right-hand sides to make equivalent power flows in this base topology
T_{R}
compared to the initially different topologies
T_{O}
and
T_{O}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}}
.

Using the standard ST, we further have:

PF(P_{g-d}^{ref}+Cf^{T_{O}})=PF(P_{g-d}^{ref})+PF(Cf^{T_{O}})

(57)

So we can rearrange (
56
):

(1-\alpha_{R}-\sum_{l\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}})PF(P_{g-d}^{ref})=PF(\alpha_{R}Cf^{T_{O}})\\
+\sum_{l\in R}PF(\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}Cf^{T_{O}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}}})

(58)

with left hand side null given (
55
):

(1-\alpha_{R}-\sum_{l\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}})=0

(59)

So right hand side is also null:

\begin{split}PF(\alpha_{R}Cf^{T_{O}})+\sum_{l\in R}PF(\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}Cf^{T_{O}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}}})&=0\\
PF(\alpha_{R}Cf^{T_{O}}+\sum_{l\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}Cf^{T_{O}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}}})&=0\end{split}

(60)

Null power flows all over the grid is only possible if all nodal power balance are null. This leads to:

\begin{split}\alpha_{R}Cf^{T_{O}}+\sum_{l\in R}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}Cf^{T_{O}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}}}&=0\\
\alpha_{R}cf^{\mathrm{\textit{T}}_{O}}_{l}+\sum_{l_{i}\in R_{\setminus l}}\beta_{R}^{l_{i}}cf^{T_{O}^{\tau_{=l_{i}}}}_{l}&=0\text{ for all $l\in R$ as $cf^{T_{O}^{\tau_{=l}}}_{l}=0$}\end{split}

(61)

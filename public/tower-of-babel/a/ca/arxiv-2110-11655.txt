Parafree graphs of groups with cyclic edge groups
Andrei Jaikin-Zapirain, Ismael Morales

Abstract
. We establish a combination theorem for parafree groups. These groups were introduced by Baumslag in the sixties. One of the current motivations for a better understanding of their structure is that they show up naturally in connection with Remeslennikov’s conjecture on the profinite rigidity of free groups. In this article, we determine when the fundamental group of a finite graph of groups with cyclic edge groups is parafree.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 20E06, Secondary: 16K40, 20C07, 20E18, 20E26

1.
Introduction

A group is said to be
parafree
if it is residually nilpotent and its quotients by the terms of its lower central series are the same as those of a free group. These groups were introduced by Baumslag
[
3
]
, who also constructed first examples of non-free parafree groups. From now on, we will assume that finite generation part of the definition of parafree groups.

Another motivation for studying parafree groups relates to the following central open problem in the theme of profinite rigidity, attributed to Remeslennikov
[
15
, Question 12]
: given a finitely generated residually finite group
G
with the same profinite completion as a free group
F
, does it follow that
G\cong F
? The first author
[
12
]
showed that such
G
must be a parafree group.

Many known examples of parafree groups are isomorphic to amalgamated free products or HNN extensions of free groups over cyclic edge groups
[
6
]
.
The purpose of this paper is to characterize when these constructions yield to parafree groups.

Our first result describes the exact circumstances under which an amalgamated free product with cyclic amalgam is parafree.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
U
and
V
be finitely generated groups,
1\neq u\in U
and
1\neq v\in V
. Consider the amalgamated free product
W=U\underset{u=v}{*}V
.
Then
W
is parafree if and only if the following three conditions hold.

(1)

The groups
U
and
V
are parafree.

(2)

The element
uv^{-1}
is not a proper power in the abelianization of
U*V
.

(3)

At least one of
u
or
v
is not a proper power in
U
or
V
, respectively.

A particular case of Theorem
1.1
, where
U
and
V
are free, follows from the work of Baumslag
[
4
]
and Azarov
[
1
]
. The case of HNN extensions with cyclic base groups is more intricate, and we obtain the following (less explicit) result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
U
be a finitely generated group,
u\in U\setminus\{1\}
and
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\langle u\rangle\to U
a monomorphism. Put
v=\alpha(u)
.
Consider the HNN extension
W=U*_{\alpha}
.
Then
W
is parafree if and only if the following four conditions hold.

(1)

The group
U
is parafree.

(2)

The element
uv^{-1}
is not a proper power in the abelianization of
U
.

(3)

At least one of
u
or
v
is not a proper power in
U
.

(4)

The image of the element
u
is non-trivial in some finite nilpotent quotient of
W
.

In the case where
U
is a parafree group with abelianisation isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, the last condition of the previous theorem can be replaced by a simpler one.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
U
be a finitely generated group with
r_{\ab}(U)=2
,
u\in U\setminus\{1\}
and
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\langle u\rangle\to U
a monomorphism. Put
v=\alpha(u)
.
Consider the HNN extension
W=U*_{\alpha}
.
Then
W
is parafree if and only if the following four conditions hold.

(1)

The group
U
is parafree.

(2)

The element
uv^{-1}
is not a proper power in the abelianization of
U
.

(3)

At least one of
u
or
v
is not a proper power in
U
.

(4)

The images of
u
and
v
in the abelianization of
U
generate a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

It is tempting to try to formulate a general criterion for the fundamental group of a graph of groups with cyclic edge groups to be parafree. Combining Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we derive the following result.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{G},\Gamma)
be a graph of groups over a finite
graph
\Gamma
and
W=\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\Gamma)
be its fundamental group. Assume that all vertex subgroups
\mathcal{G}(v)
(
v\in V(\Gamma)
) are finitely generated and all edge subgroups
\mathcal{G}(e)
(
e\in E(\Gamma)
) are cyclic. Then
W
is parafree if and only if the following four conditions hold.

(1)

All the vertex subgroups
\mathcal{G}(v)
(
v\in V(\Gamma)
) are parafree.

(2)

The abelianization of
W
is torsion-free of rank

r_{\ab}(W)=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{e\in E(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(e))+1-\chi(\Gamma),

where
\chi(\Gamma)=|V(\Gamma)|-|E(\Gamma)|
.

(3)

All the centralizers of non-trivial elements in
W
are cyclic.

(4)

For each non-trivial edge subgroup of
\mathcal{G}(e)
(
e\in E(\Gamma)
) there is a finite nilpotent quotient of
W
where the image of this edge subgroup is non-trivial.

It is relatively easy to see that the conditions presented in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are necessary for
W
to be parafree and they also imply that the quotients of
W
by the terms of its lower central series are the same as those of a free group. The difficult part in the proofs of both theorems is to show that
W
is residually nilpotent.
We establish this as an application of recent methods developed in
[
12
]
which were used for constructing abstract subgroups of free pro-
p
groups.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we describe the preliminary results which we use in the paper. Section
3
is devoted to construction of new examples of
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
-torsion-free
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules. In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
and their corollaries.

Preliminaries

2.1.
General notation for rings, groups and pro-
p
groups

All of our rings
R
will be associative and unitary. All ring homomorphisms will map
1\mapsto 1
. All
R
-modules are left

-modules, unless we say that we consider right

-modules.

Given a group

its commutator subgroup
and by

its abelianization. The terms of the lower central series of

are defined as
\gamma_{1}(G)=G
and
\gamma_{n+1}(G)=[\gamma_{n}(G),G]
if

and the terms of the

G_{1,p}=G
and
G_{n+1,p}=[G_{n,p},G]G_{n,p}^{p}
if

. We recall that when
{\bf G}
is a finitely generated pro-

\gamma_{k}{\bf G}
are closed normal subgroups of
{\bf G}
.

We denote by

the minimal number of generators of a group

and we put
r_{\ab}(G)=d(G_{\ab})
. Similarly, given a pro-
p
group
{\bf G}
, we denote by
d({\bf G})
the minimal number of topological generators of
{\bf G}
, which also equals the dimension of its
p
-abelianisation
{\bf G}/[{\bf G},{\bf G}]{\bf G}^{p}
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space.

As usual, we denote the free product in the category of pro-
p
groups by
\coprod
.

2.2.
Parafree groups

If
G
is a group we denote by
G_{\widehat{p}}
the pro-
p
completion of
G
. In this paper we will use the following characterization of parafree groups.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
G
be finitely generated residually nilpotent group. Then
G
is parafree if and only if
G_{\widehat{p}}
is a free pro-
p
group for every prime
p
. Moreover, a parafree group is residually-
p
for every prime
p
.

Proof.

A group is said to be weakly
p
-parafree if its quotients by the terms of its
p
-lower central series are the same as those of a free group. In these terms, a finitely generated
G
will be parafree if and only if it is residually nilpotent and it is weakly
p
-parafree for every prime
p
. We know from
[
14
, Corollary 2.9]
that a finitely generated
G
is weakly
p
-parafree if and only if it has free pro-
p
completion. This proves the first claim. The second claim of the proposition follows from the fact that finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent groups are residually-
p
for every prime
p

[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
.
∎

The following result provides a useful necessary condition for a group to be parafree.

Proposition 2.2
.

The centralizers of non-trivial elements of a parafree group are cyclic.

Proof.

Let
A
be the centralizer of a non-trivial element
u
of a parafree group
G
. By

[
5
, Theorem 4.2]
, we know that

(a)

a two-generated subgroup of
G
is free and

(b)

an abelian subgroup of
G
is cyclic.

Thus, by (a),
A
is locally cyclic, and, by (b),
A
is cyclic. ∎

2.3.
Group algebra and the augmentation ideal

Let
G
be a group and
k
a commutative ring. The group ring of
G
over
k
is denoted by
kG
. We denote by
I_{G}
the augmentation ideal of
\mathbb{Z}G
and by
kI_{G}=k\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{Z}}}I_{G}
the augmentation ideal of
kG
.

Given two groups
H\leq G
, we denote by
kI_{H}^{G}
the left ideal of
kG
generated by
kI_{H}
. Since
kG
is a natural free right

-module, it follows that the canonical map of
kG
-modules

kG\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{kH}}kI_{H}\longrightarrow kI_{H}^{G}

is an isomorphism.

2.4.
The fundamental groups of graphs of groups

We refer the reader to
[
8
]
for standard definitions and notions related to graph of groups and their fundamental groups.

By a

(\mathcal{G},\Gamma)
we mean a connected graph
\Gamma=(V(\Gamma),E(\Gamma),\iota,\tau)

\mathcal{G}
which assigns to each

\mathcal{G}(v)

of

and injective group homomorphism
t_{e}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\mathcal{G}(e)\to\mathcal{G}(\tau(e))

In this paper we work mostly with two particular cases: amalgamated free products and HNN extensions. The structure of the augmentation ideal of the group algebra of the fundamental group of a graph of groups is described in
[
7
, Lemma 6]
. We will describe this result in the cases that interest us.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
k
be a commutative ring.

(1)

Let
U
and

be two groups,
A
a subgroup of

and

where

if

and
p(b,c)=b+c
if
b\in kI_{U}^{W}
and

.

(2)

Let
U
be a group,

a subgroup of

and
\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}A\to U

W=U*_{\alpha}=\langle U,t\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}tg=\alpha(g)t\textrm{\ if }g\in A\rangle
.
Then there exists an exact of sequence of

-modules.

0\to kI_{\alpha(A)}^{W}\xrightarrow{\gamma}kI_{U}^{W}\oplus kW\xrightarrow{p}kI_{W}\to 0,

where
\gamma(a)=(a(1-t),a)

The following proposition will be used several times in the paper.

Proposition 2.4
.

[

Let

, i.e.

2.5.
The induced map between the augmentation ideals

Given a group homomorphism
\widetilde{G}\to G
, we obtain an induced map between the augmentation ideals

I_{\widetilde{G}}\to I_{G}
.
Here we will explain how one can derive information from this induced map back to the initial group homomorphism.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G
be a surjective group homomorphism of kernel
K
. Suppose that the natural homomorphism of
kG
-modules

\alpha\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}kG\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{k\widetilde{G}}}kI_{\widetilde{G}}\longrightarrow kI_{G},

defined by the
k
-linear extension of
a\otimes b\mapsto a\phi(b)
, is an isomorphism. Then

k\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K_{\ab}=0.

Proof.

Observe that by Shapiro’s lemma

k\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}K_{\ab}\cong H_{1}(K,k)\cong H_{1}(\widetilde{G},kG)=\Tor_{1}^{k\widetilde{G}}(kG,k).

Applying the right-exact functor
kG\otimes_{k\widetilde{G}}
to the exact sequence of left
k\widetilde{G}
-modules

0\to kI_{\widetilde{G}}\to k\widetilde{G}\to k\to 0,

we get the exact sequence

0\to\Tor_{1}^{k\widetilde{G}}(kG,k)\to k{G}\otimes_{k\widetilde{G}}kI_{\widetilde{G}}\xrightarrow{\alpha}kG\to k\to 0.

Since
\alpha
is injective,
\Tor_{1}^{k\widetilde{G}}(kG,k)=\{0\}
and we are done.
∎

2.6.
\D
-torsion-free modules

Let

be an embedding of the ring

into a division ring

. Let

be an

-module. We say that

is

-torsion-free
if the canonical map

The following provides a more flexible criterion for verifying whether a module is torsion-free.

Lemma 2.6
.

[
12
, Lemma 4.1]

Let
M
be an
R
-module. Then
M
is
\D
-torsion-free if and only if there exists a
\D
-module

and an injective homomorphism of
R
-modules

.

Given a homomorphism
R\to\D
, where
\D
is a division ring, we define

-dimension of

to be the dimension of the

-vector space

. We denote it by

.

Lemma 2.7
.

[
12
, Lemma 4.2]

Let

be an exact sequence of

-modules. Assume that

(1)

M_{1}
and
M_{3}
are
\D
-torsion-free;

(2)

\dim_{\D}M_{1}
and
\dim_{\D}M_{3}
are finite; and

(3)

\dim_{\D}M_{1}+\dim_{\D}M_{3}=\dim_{\D}M_{2}
.

Then
M_{2}
is also
\D
-torsion-free.

The following lemma will be used in the computation of

.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
12
, Lemma 4.3]

Let
M
be a
\D
-torsion-free

-module of finite

-dimension. Let

be a non-trivial

-submodule of

.

2.7.
The universal division rings of fractions of the group algebra of a subgroup of a free pro-
p
group

Let
{\bf F}
be a finitely generated free pro-
p
group. If
G
is an abstract subgroup of
{\bf F}
, then
it turns out that
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
has a universal division ring of fractions, denoted by
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
.
We will not give a formal definition of universal field of fractions which can be found in
[
13
]
, but we will describe the main properties of the embeddings
\mathbb{F}_{p}G\subseteq\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
, which we will use in this paper.

If
H
is a subgroup of
G
, then the division closure of
\mathbb{F}_{p}H
in
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
is the universal division ring of fractions of
\mathbb{F}_{p}H
, and, therefore, we will denote it by
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}H}
.

If
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
such that
G/N\cong\mathbb{Z}
, then
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
is a free
\mathbb{F}_{p}N
-module with the basis
\{t^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, where
t\in G
and
Nt
generates

. Thus
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
is isomorphic to a skew-polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}N[t^{\pm 1},\sigma]
with the indeterminate
t
and coefficients in
\mathbb{F}_{p}N
. Here
\sigma
is an automorphism of the ring
{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}
, induced from the automorphism of conjugation-by-
t
.

Let
R
be a subring of
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
generated by
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}
and

. It turns out that
\{t^{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}i\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is also a
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}
-basis of

(this is so called the
Hughes-free property
of the embedding
\mathbb{F}_{p}G\subseteq\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
, see
[
11
,
9
]
). thus

is isomorphic to
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}[t^{\pm 1},\sigma]
.

The following result gives a lower bound for the
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
-dimension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{G}
.

Proposition 2.9
.

[
12
, Corollary 3.7 and the discussion afterwards]

Let
G
be a finitely generated dense subgroup of a free pro-
p

. Then
\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{G}\geq d({\bf F}).

Moreover, if

is parafree and

, then
\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{G}=d({\bf F}).

The following result provides an important example of
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
-torsion-free-module.

Proposition 2.10
.

[
12
, Proposition 4.8]
Let
{\bf F}
be a free pro-
p

two subgroups of

, and

a maximal abelian subgroup of

2.8.
Embeddings of abstract groups into free pro-
p
groups

In this subsection we detail a method introduced in
[
12
]
for ensuring that a map from an abstract group
\widetilde{G}
to a free pro-
p
group is injective. We are particularly interested in the problem of producing families of parafree groups. Let
\widetilde{G}
be a candidate to being parafree, meaning that
\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}}
is free for every prime
p
. We want to study whether the canonical map
\widetilde{G}\longrightarrow\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}}
is an embedding for some suitable prime
p
. This would establish the residual nilpotence of
\widetilde{G}
and we could conclude that
\widetilde{G}
is parafree.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\widetilde{G}
be a finitely generated group,
{\bf F}
a finitely generated free pro-
p
group and
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\widetilde{G}\rightarrow{\bf F}
a group homomorphism. Suppose that we have the following conditions.

(1)

The image
G=\phi(\widetilde{G})
is dense in
{\bf F}
.

(2)

The
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-module
\mathbb{F}_{p}G\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}\widetilde{G}}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{\widetilde{G}}
is
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
-torsion-free and

\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}\mathbb{F}_{p}G\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}\widetilde{G}}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{\widetilde{G}}=d({\bf F}).

(3)

The kernel of
\phi
is free.

Then the map
\phi
is an embedding.

Proof.

We will prove that the surjective map
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\widetilde{G}\rightarrow G
verifies the assumption of Proposition
2.5
to deduce that
\ker\phi
has trivial
p
-abelianization. Since
\ker\phi
is free, the latter would imply that
\ker\phi=1
and the conclusion would follow.

It is clear that the natural homomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules

\mathbb{F}_{p}G\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}\widetilde{G}}}I_{\widetilde{G}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{G},

defined by
a\otimes b\mapsto a\phi(b)
, is surjective. If it was not injective, naming its kernel by
L
and naming
M=\mathbb{F}_{p}G\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}\widetilde{G}}}I_{\widetilde{G}}
, we would deduce, after applying Lemma
2.8
,

d({\bf F})=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}M>\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}(M/L)=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}(\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{G}),

which would contradict Proposition
2.9
.
∎

In the setting of our problem, that is, taking some
\widetilde{G}
with free pro-
p
completion and studying whether
\widetilde{G}\longrightarrow\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}}

is injective, we make a few comments about the three conditions of Proposition
2.11
. We take
G
to be the image of
\widetilde{G}
inside
\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}}
.

(1)

The first condition will be naturally ensured.

(2)

The second condition is the hardest part and requires the most technical arguments presented in Section
3
.

(3)

The third condition is natural from the point of view of the Bass-Serre theory. If we take
\widetilde{G}
to be the fundamental group of a graph of groups, and we ensure that
\ker\phi
intersects trivially every vertex subgroup, then the
\ker\phi
will necessarily be free.

3.

Proposition 3.1

Let

Then the
\mathbb{F}_{p}G

is
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
-torsion-free and

\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}M=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}H_{1}}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{1}}+\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}H_{2}}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{2}}-1.

Before giving the proof, we shall make an observation. If
A
is an abelian subgroup of
{\bf F}
, as occurs in the previous proposition, the universal division
\mathbb{F}_{p}A
-ring of fractions

is the field of fractions

of the commutative domain
\mathbb{F}_{p}A
. Given a nontrivial ideal

\mathbb{F}_{p}A

-vector space

is one-dimensional. This implies that

(1)

\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}A}}I=\dim_{Q_{\ore}(\mathbb{F}_{p}A)}Q_{\ore}(\mathbb{F}_{p}A)\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}A}}I=1.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We consider the
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-submodule of
M
defined by

L=(\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{A}^{G}\oplus\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{2}}^{G})/J.

We want to apply Lemma
2.7
to the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow M/L\rightarrow 0

of
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules.
Since
L\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{2}}^{G}\leq\mathbb{F}_{p}G
, then it is
\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
-torsion-free and

\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}L=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}H_{2}}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{2}}.

By the observation (
1

1=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}A}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{A}=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{A}^{G}=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}J.

So it is clear, by Lemma

, that

\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}M=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{1}}^{G}+\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{2}}^{G}-1=\\
\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}H_{1}}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{1}}+\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}H_{2}}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{2}}-1.

On the other side, the quotient

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{1}}^{G}/\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{A}^{G}

\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}
-torsion-free by Proposition

.
Again, by Lemma

\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}M/L=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{1}}^{G}-\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}G}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{A}^{G}=\dim_{\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}H_{1}}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H_{1}}-1.

Therefore, the short exact sequence

of
\mathbb{F}_{p}G
-modules satisfies the requirements of Lemma

∎

We now turn to the study of torsion-free modules that have the form of an augmentation ideal of a cyclic HNN extension.

Our point is to prove that a certain module, which may have the form

Lemma 3.2

Let

We can now state the second result of this section.

Claim 3.4

Since

\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}[N]}
(and hence in

Claim 3.5
.

We have the following equality of subsets of

(3)

We denote by

the conjugation

Claim 3.6
.

For all

, we have the following equality of subsets of

The same way we had the equality (
3

1\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H^{t^{-n}}}^{N}\,\,\bigcap\,\,\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}}(u^{t^{-n}}-1)=1\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}}\mathbb{F}_{p}N(u^{t^{-n}}-1).

Claim 3.7
.

We have the following equation of subsets of

1\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}}\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H}^{G}\,\,\bigcap\,\,\D_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}}\mathbb{F}_{p}G(v-1-t(u-1))=1\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{\mathbb{F}_{p}N}}\mathbb{F}_{p}G(v-1-t(u-1)).

The natural map

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\frac{\mathbb{F}_{p}I_{H}^{G}}{\mathbb{F}_{p}G(v-1-t(u-1))}}

4.
Proofs of the main results

4.1.
Amalgamated products

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\widetilde{H_{1}}
and
\widetilde{H_{2}}
be finitely generated groups. Let
1\neq u_{1}\in\widetilde{H_{1}}
and let
1\neq u_{2}\in\widetilde{H_{2}}
. Consider the following amalgamated product of cyclic amalgam

\widetilde{G}=\widetilde{H_{1}}\underset{u_{1}=u_{2}}{*}\widetilde{H_{2}}\cong\frac{\widetilde{H_{1}}*\widetilde{H_{2}}}{\langle\langle u_{1}u_{2}^{-1}\rangle\rangle}.

Then
\widetilde{G}
is parafree if and only if the three following conditions hold.

(1)

\widetilde{H_{1}}
and
\widetilde{H_{2}}
are parafree.

(2)

The element
u_{1}u_{2}^{-1}
of
\widetilde{H_{1}}*\widetilde{H_{2}}
is not a proper power in the abelianization of
\widetilde{H_{1}}*\widetilde{H_{2}}
.

(3)

There is at least one
i\in\{1,2\}
such that
u_{i}
is not a proper power in
\widetilde{H_{i}}
.

Remark
.

The condition (2) can be substituted by the condition

(2’)

r_{\ab}(\widetilde{G})=r_{\ab}(\widetilde{H_{1}})+r_{\ab}(\widetilde{H_{2}})-1
.

The proof of the theorem shows that the condition (3) can be substituted by the condition

(3’)

All the centralizers of non-trivial elements in
\widetilde{G}
are cyclic.

Proof.

We first prove that the conditions are necessary. Let us assume that
\widetilde{G}
is parafree.

Both
\widetilde{H_{1}}
and
\widetilde{H_{2}}
are subgroups of
\widetilde{G}
and hence they are residually nilpotent.
We want to show that, for all primes
p
,
\widetilde{H_{1}}_{\widehat{p}}
and
\widetilde{H_{2}}_{\widehat{p}}
are free. By Proposition
2.1
, this would imply that
\widetilde{H_{1}}
and
\widetilde{H_{2}}
are parafree.

Fix a prime
p
. Recall that the rank of a pro-
p
group
{\bf G}
equals the rank of its
p
-abelianisation
{\bf G}/[{\bf G},{\bf G}]{\bf G}^{p}
. From this, we observe that

(7)

d(\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}})\geq d(\widetilde{H_{1}}_{\widehat{p}})+d(\widetilde{H_{2}}_{\widehat{p}})-1.

Consider the closure
\overline{H_{i}}
of the image of
\widetilde{H_{i}}
under the canonical map
\widetilde{G}\to\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}}
. Since
\widetilde{G}
is parafree,
\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}}
is free pro-
p
, and so both
\overline{H_{1}}
and
\overline{H_{2}}
are free pro-
p
, because any closed subgroup of a free pro-
p
group is again free. Note that
\widetilde{H_{1}}
and
\widetilde{H_{2}}
generate
\widetilde{G}
. Hence the canonical map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\ordinarycolon}}\overline{H_{1}}\coprod\overline{H_{2}}\to\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}}
is onto.

Since
\widetilde{G}
is parafree, the images of
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
in
\widetilde{G_{\widehat{p}}}
are non-trivial. Therefore,
\ker f\neq\{1\}
.
We also recall that the groups
\overline{H_{1}}\coprod\overline{H_{2}}
and
\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}}
are free pro-
p
. So, by the Hopfian property, for the surjection
f
between free pro-
p
groups to have non-trivial kernel we must have

(8)

d(\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}})\leq d\left(\overline{H_{1}}\coprod\overline{H_{2}}\right)-1=d(\overline{H_{1}})+d(\overline{H_{2}})-1.

Moreover, since
\overline{H_{i}}
is a quotient of
\widetilde{H_{i}}_{\widehat{p}}
,
d(\overline{H_{i}})\leq d(\widetilde{H_{i}}_{\widehat{p}})
. The latter observation in combination with (
7
) and (
8

d(\overline{H_{i}})=d(\widetilde{H_{i}}_{\widehat{p}})
. The pro-
p
group
\overline{H_{i}}
is free, so the canonical map
\widetilde{H_{i}}_{\widehat{p}}\to\overline{H_{i}}
is an isomorphism. Hence
\widetilde{H_{i}}_{\widehat{p}}
is free pro-
p
and the first condition is proved.

The previous argument also shows that, for all primes
p
,

d(\widetilde{G}_{\widehat{p}})=d(\widetilde{H_{1}}_{\widehat{p}})+d(\widetilde{H_{2}}_{\widehat{p}})-1,

and this implies the second condition.

Finally, suppose that
u_{1}=v_{1}^{n_{1}}
in
\widetilde{H_{1}}
, and that
u_{2}=v_{2}^{n_{2}}
in
\widetilde{H_{2}}
with

.
Since
u_{i}\neq 1
and
\widetilde{H_{i}}
is torsion-free, then
\langle v_{i}\rangle
are infinite cyclic. Consider the subgroup
A
generated by
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
.
By Proposition
2.4
,
A
is isomorphic to
\langle v_{1}\rangle*_{v_{1}^{n_{1}}=v_{2}^{n_{2}}}\langle v_{2}\rangle
.
This group is non-abelian and belongs to the centralizer of
u_{1}=u_{2}
in
\widetilde{G}
. This contradicts Proposition
2.2
. This shows that the third condition holds.

We now verify that the three given conditions are sufficient. There is a canonical isomorphism

(9)

\widetilde{G}_{\ab}\cong\frac{\widetilde{H_{1}}_{\ab}\oplus\widetilde{H_{2}}_{\ab}}{u_{1}-u_{2}}.

From the fact that
u_{1}-u_{2}
is not a proper power in

\sum_{v\in V(\Delta_{1})}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Delta_{1})}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))+1-\chi(\Delta_{1})+\\
\sum_{v\in V(\Delta_{2})}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Delta_{2})}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))+1-\chi(\Delta_{2})-r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(e))=\\
\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))+1-\chi(\Gamma)

Since
r_{\ab}(W)=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))+1-\chi(\Gamma)
, we obtain that

r_{\ab}(U)=\sum_{v\in V(\Delta_{1})}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Delta_{1})}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))+1-\chi(\Delta_{1}),\\
r_{\ab}(V)=\sum_{v\in V(\Delta_{2})}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Delta_{2})}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))+1-\chi(\Delta_{2})\textrm{\ and\ }\\
r_{\ab}(W)=r_{\ab}(U)+r_{\ab}(V)-r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(e)).

Thus,
U
and
V
satisfy the four conditions of the corollary and, by the inductive hypothesis, they are parafree. If
\mathcal{G}(e)=\{1\}
, then
W\cong U*V
, and so,
W
is parafree. If
\mathcal{G}(e)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, then
W
is parafree by Theorem
4.1
and the remark afterwards.

If
\Gamma\setminus\{e\}=\Delta
is connected then
\chi(\Gamma)=\chi(\Delta)+1.
by Proposition
2.4
,

W\cong U*_{t_{e}}\textrm{\ where\ }U\cong\pi_{1}(\mathcal{G},\Delta).

We have that

r_{\ab}(W)\geq r_{\ab}(U)-r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(e))+1\geq\\
\sum_{v\in V(\Delta)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Delta)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))+1-\chi(\Delta)-r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(e))+1=\\
\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))+1-\chi(\Gamma).

By assumption,
r_{\ab}(W)=\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Gamma)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))+1-\chi(\Gamma)
. So we deduce that

r_{\ab}(U)=\sum_{v\in V(\Delta)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(v))-\sum_{f\in E(\Delta)}r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(f))-\chi(\Delta)\textrm{\ and\ }\\
r_{\ab}(W)=r_{\ab}(U)-r_{\ab}(\mathcal{G}(e))+1.

Thus,
U
satisfies the four conditions of the corollary and, by the inductive hypothesis, it is parafree. If
\mathcal{G}(e)=\{1\}
, then
W\cong U*\mathbb{Z}
, and so,
W
is parafree. If
\mathcal{G}(e)\cong\mathbb{Z}
, then
W
is parafree by Theorem
4.5
and the remark afterwards.

6
Discussion and Conclusions

Our results are summarized below:

1. Gauge invariance provides essential constraints on the amplitude of Eq. (
2.8
):

\displaystyle T^{5}_{\alpha,\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{iqx}\langle 0|T\left\{j^{\rm e.m.}_{\alpha}(x),\bar{u}\gamma_{\nu}\gamma_{5}b(0)\right\}|\bar{B}_{u}(p)\rangle.

(6.37)

We emphasize that for a consistent analysis of the
B\to l^{+}l^{-}l^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}
amplitude it is necessary to start with the
amplitude
(
6.37
) and properly parametrize this amplitude taking into account all constraints imposed by the electromagnetic
gauge invariance and analyticity. Taking into account these constraints leads to

\displaystyle F_{2A}(q^{\prime 2},q^{2}=0)=0,\quad F^{\prime}_{2A}(q^{\prime 2},q^{2}=0)=\frac{2Q_{B}f_{B}M_{B}}{M_{B}^{2}-q^{\prime 2}}.

These relations determine the behaviour of the differential decay rate

d^{2}\Gamma(B\to lll^{\prime}\nu^{\prime})/dq^{2}dq^{\prime 2}
at small
q^{2}
(see Eqs. (
4.27
) and (
4.28
)).

2. For the form factors
F_{V,1A,2A}(q^{\prime 2},q^{2})
, describing the amplitude of
B\to lll^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}
decay,
we obtained dispersion representations in
q^{2}
with one subtraction.
This allows us to take into account properly both the constraints imposed by gauge invariance
at small
q^{2}
and the contributions of vector mesons (
V
); the latter involve the weak
form factors describing
B\to V
decays.
Assuming that the light vector mesons
\rho^{0}
and
\omega
saturate the
spectral densities, we obtained analytic representations for the form factors in a
broad range of
q^{2}
and
q^{\prime 2}
.

Our assumption may seem oversimplified in comparison with a sophisticated analysis
of the form factors presented in
beneke2
. Moreover, our spectral representations saturated by
merely light vector mesons do not reproduce the correct
q^{2}
-behaviour and overshoot the form factors
at large values of
q^{2}
, where the form factors may be calculated using OPE
ims
;
beneke2
;
this means that our form factors do not produce realistic differential distributions at large values of
q^{2}
.

Nevertheless, our approach has a certain advantage compared to that of
beneke2
: Making use of the
once-subtracted
q^{2}
-dispersion representations allows us to take properly into account both the
gauge-invariance constraints
and the resonance contributions to the form factors: the latter may be calculated unambiguously
and are found to be nozero, see (
2.21
). On the other hand, in Ref.
beneke2

the resonance contributions to
F_{2A}
are omitted in order to satisfy the
gauge-invariance constraints.

A proper description of the resonance region of
q^{2}
is crucial as it produces
the bulk of the
B\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}l\nu
cross-section and nearly a half of the
B\to e^{+}e^{-}l\nu
cross section
(the other half comes from the region of small
q^{2}
). So, from the point
of view of obtaining numerical predictions, we find it eligible to trade the proper description of the region of

0<q^{2}<1
GeV
2
against overestimating the contribution of the region of large
q^{2}
which anyway,
even with our overshot form factors, contributes at less than a percent level.

3. We derived an explicit analytic expression for the differential distributions
d\Gamma/dq^{2}dq^{\prime 2}
in
B\to lll^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}

decays including the
O(m_{l}^{\prime 2}/q^{4})
terms which provide the most singular behaviour of the differential distribution
at small
q^{2}
. We then obtained numerical predictions for the differential and the
integrated branching ratios of the
B\to l^{+}l^{-}l^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}
decays.

Appendix A
Relations between the
B_{q}
and
\bar{B}_{q}
amplitudes

Here we derive the relations between the amplitudes of
B_{q}
and
\bar{B}_{q}
mesons.
Such relations are obtained by applying charge conjugation.

The fermion field transforms under charge conjugation
\hat{C}

(
\hat{C}^{2}=1
,
\hat{C}^{-1}=\hat{C}
) as follows
IZ
:

\displaystyle\hat{C}\psi\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta_{c}{\cal C}\bar{\psi}^{T},

(A.43)

\displaystyle\hat{C}\bar{\psi}\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\eta^{*}_{c}\psi^{T}({\cal C}^{T})^{-1},

(A.44)

|\eta_{c}|=1
, where the charge-conjugation matrix
{\cal C}
is defined by the relation

\displaystyle{\cal C}\gamma_{\mu}^{T}{\cal C}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma_{\mu}

(A.45)

and has the following properties:
{\cal C}^{T}=-{\cal C}
,
{\cal C}^{-1}=-{\cal C}
,
{\cal C}^{2}=-1
.
In the Dirac representation of the
\gamma
-matrices, one can choose
{\cal C}=i\gamma_{0}\gamma_{2}
leading to

\displaystyle{\cal C}\gamma_{5}^{T}{\cal C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma_{5},

(A.46)

\displaystyle{\cal C}(\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5})^{T}{\cal C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5},

(A.47)

\displaystyle{\cal C}(\sigma_{\mu\nu})^{T}{\cal C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mu\nu},

(A.48)

\displaystyle{\cal C}(\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5})^{T}{\cal C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}.

(A.49)

Making use of these relations, one obtaines the following expression for charge conjugation of bilinear currents
(of anticommuting) fermion operators:

\displaystyle\hat{C}(\bar{\psi}_{1}{\cal O}\psi_{2})\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{\psi}_{2}({\cal C}{\cal O}^{T}{\cal C})\psi_{1},

(A.50)

leading to

\displaystyle\hat{C}(\bar{\psi}_{1}\psi_{2})\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{2}\psi_{1},

(A.51)

\displaystyle\hat{C}(\bar{\psi}_{1}\gamma_{5}\psi_{2})\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{\psi}_{2}\gamma_{5}\psi_{1},

(A.52)

\displaystyle\hat{C}(\bar{\psi}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}\psi_{2})\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{\psi}_{2}\gamma_{\mu}\psi_{1},

(A.53)

\displaystyle\hat{C}(\bar{\psi}_{1}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\psi_{2})\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{\psi}_{2}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\psi_{1},

(A.54)

\displaystyle\hat{C}(\bar{\psi}_{1}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\psi_{2})\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{\psi}_{2}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\psi_{1},

(A.55)

\displaystyle\hat{C}(\bar{\psi}_{1}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}\psi_{2})\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{\psi}_{2}\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}\psi_{1}.

(A.56)

The
C
-conjugate states are related to each other as follows (no arbitrary phase is implied):

\displaystyle\hat{C}|B_{q}(p)\rangle=|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle,

(A.57)

The QCD vacuum state is
C
-invariant,
\hat{C}|0\rangle=|0\rangle
. So, if we are going to consider QCD
effects in the amplitudes, we can apply
C
-conjugation and relate to each other the amplitudes

\displaystyle\langle 0|\bar{q}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}b|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle=i\bar{f}_{B}p_{\mu},

(A.58)

\displaystyle\langle 0|\bar{b}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}q|B_{q}(p)\rangle=if_{B}p_{\mu}

(A.59)

and obtain the relation
f_{B}=\bar{f}_{B}
.
Similar relations may be obtained for more complicated amplitudes such as

\displaystyle\bar{T}^{{\cal O}}_{\alpha}(p,q)\equiv i\int dxe^{iqx}\langle 0|T\{j_{\alpha}^{\rm e.m.}(x),\bar{q}(0){\cal O}b(0)\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle,

(A.60)

\displaystyle T^{{\cal O}}_{\alpha}(p,q)\equiv i\int dxe^{iqx}\langle 0|T\{j_{\alpha}^{\rm e.m.}(x),\bar{b}(0){\cal O}q(0)\}|B_{q}(p)\rangle.

(A.61)

The parametrizations of these amplitudes are given via the same form factors but with appropriate sign
adjustments between (
A.60
) and (
A.61
). For instance, for the relation between the amplitudes
(we use in this Appendix the notation
\bar{T}
for the amplitudes containing
\bar{B}_{q}
-meson in the initial state)

\displaystyle\bar{T}_{\alpha\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{iqx}\langle 0|T\{j_{\alpha}^{\rm e.m.}(x),\bar{q}(0)\gamma_{\mu}b(0)\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle,

(A.62)

\displaystyle\bar{T}^{5}_{\alpha\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{iqx}\langle 0|T\{j_{\alpha}^{\rm e.m.}(x),\bar{q}(0)\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}b(0)\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle,

(A.63)

\displaystyle\bar{T}_{\alpha,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{iqx}\langle 0|T\{j_{\alpha}^{\rm e.m.}(x),\bar{q}(0)\sigma_{\mu\nu}b(0)\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle,

(A.64)

\displaystyle\bar{T}^{5}_{\alpha,\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\int dxe^{iqx}\langle 0|T\{j_{\alpha}^{\rm e.m.}(x),\bar{q}(0)\sigma_{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}b(0)\}|\bar{B}_{q}(p)\rangle,

(A.65)

and the corresponding amplitudes
T_{\alpha\mu},T^{5}_{\alpha\mu},T_{\alpha,\mu\nu}
, and
T^{5}_{\alpha,\mu\nu}
as defined according to
(
A.60
) and (
A.61
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{T}_{\alpha\mu}(p,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\mu}(p,q),

(A.66)

\displaystyle\bar{T}^{5}_{\alpha\mu}(p,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-T_{\alpha\mu}(p,q),

(A.67)

\displaystyle\bar{T}_{\alpha\mu\nu}(p,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\mu\nu}(p,q),

(A.68)

\displaystyle\bar{T}^{5}_{\alpha\mu\nu}(p,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{5}_{\alpha\mu\nu}(p,q).

(A.69)

In conclusion, the amplitudes (
A.60
) and (
A.61
) are related to each other by charge conjugation.

Dixmier Trace Formulas and Negative Eigenvalues of Schrödinger Operators on Curved Noncommutative Tori
Edward McDonald, Raphaël Ponge

Abstract
. In a previous paper we established Cwikel-type estimates on noncommutative tori and used them to get analogues in this setting of the Cwikel-Lieb-Rozenblum (CLR) and Lieb-Thirring inequalities for negative eigenvalues of fractional Schrödinger operators. In this paper, we focus on “curved” NC tori, where the role of the usual Laplacian is played by Laplace-Beltrami operators associated with arbitrary Riemannian metrics. The Cwikel-type estimates of our previous paper are extended to pseudodifferential operators and powers of Laplace-Beltrami operators. There are several applications of these estimates. First, we get L p L_{p} -versions of the usual formula for the trace of Ψ \Psi DOs on NC tori, i.e., for combinations of Ψ \Psi DOs with L p L_{p} -position operators. Next, we get L p L_{p} -versions of the analogues for NC tori Connes’ trace theorem and Connes’ integration formula. They give formulas for the NC integrals (a.k.a. Dixmier traces) of products of L p L_{p} -position operators with Ψ \Psi DOs or powers of the Laplace-Beltrami operators. Moreover, by combining our Cwikel-type estimates with suitable versions of the Birman-Schwinger principle we get versions of the CLR and Lieb-Thirring inequalities for negative eigenvalues of fractional Schrödinger operators associated with powers of Laplace-Beltrami operators and L p L_{p} -potentials. As in the original Euclidean case the Lieb-Thirring inequalities imply a dual Sobolev inequality for orthonormal families. Finally, we discuss spectral asymptotics and semiclassical Weyl’s laws for the our classes of operators on curved NC tori. This superseded a previous conjecture of [ 63 ] .

Introduction

Noncommutative tori are arguably the most well known examples of noncommutative spaces. In particular, they naturally appear in the noncommutative geometry approach to the quantum Hall effect
[
2
]
and topological insulators
[
11
,
72
]
.
Two-dimensional noncommutative tori arise from actions of
\mathbb{Z}
on the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}
by irrational rotations. More generally, an
n
-dimensional noncommutative torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
is the NC space whose
C^{*}
-algebra
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is generated by unitaries
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
subject to the relations,

U_{l}U_{j}=e^{2i\pi\theta_{jl}}U_{j}U_{l},\qquad j,l=1,\ldots,n,

where
\theta=(\theta_{jl})
is a given real anti-symmetric matrix. For
\theta=0
we recover the
C^{*}
-algebra
C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
of continuous functions on the ordinary torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. There is a natural
C^{*}
-action of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
on
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
generated by the canonical derivations
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
such that
\partial_{j}(U_{l})=i\delta_{jl}U_{j}
. The smooth NC torus
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is the precisely the
*
-algebra of the smooth elements for that action. The
L_{p}
-spaces
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
are the NC
L_{p}
-spaces associated with the standard (normalized) trace
\tau
of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.
We refer to Section
2
for the main background on noncommutative tori.

In
[
63
,
64
]
analogues for NC tori of the estimates of Cwikel
[
21
]
were derived. These Cwikel-type estimates were used in
[
63
]
to get versions for noncommutative tori of the Cwikel-Lieb-Rozenblum and Lieb-Thirring inequalities for negative eigenvalues of fractional Schrödinger operators associated with powers of the ordinary Laplacian
\Delta=-(\partial_{1}^{2}+\cdots\partial_{n}^{2})
. A semiclassical Weyl’s law for such operators was further conjectured in
[
63
]
. This is part of a recent revival of interest in Cwikel estimates (see, e.g.,
[
30
,
39
,
52
,
92
]
).

In this paper we deal with curved NC tori. Seminal articles of Connes-Tretkoff
[
20
]
and Connes-Moscovici
[
19
]
spurred an upsurge of activity on understanding the spectral geometry of curved noncommutative tori (see, e.g.,
[
18
,
22
,
26
,
27
,
29
,
34
,
45
,
50
,
59
,
68
,
91
]
and the references therein; see also
[
16
,
28
]
for recent surveys). By a curved NC torus it is meant a NC torus equipped with a Riemannian metric in the sense of
[
38
,
76
]
(see also Section
3
). The spectral geometry of a curved NC torus is then studied through the analysis of the corresponding Laplace-Beltrami operator. Until recently the main focus was on conformal deformations of the flat metric. The construction in
[
38
]
of the Laplace-Beltrami operator then made it possible to extend this program to arbitrary Riemannian metrics (see, e.g.,
[
59
,
68
,
91
]
).

Throughout this paper we focus on Laplace-Beltrami operators
\Delta_{g,\nu}
associated with an arbitrary Riemannian metric
g=(g_{ij})
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
and a smooth positive density
\nu
(i.e., a positive invertible element of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
).

In the terminology of
[
20
]

datum of
\nu
defines a “conformal” weight
\hat{\tau}_{\nu}(x):=(2\pi)^{n}{\tau}(x\nu)
. Associated with this weight is the

L_{2}
-space
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
given by the completion with respect to the inner product,

(1.1)

\left\langle x|y\right\rangle_{\nu}:=\hat{\tau}(xy^{*})=(2\pi)^{n}{\tau}(y^{*}\nu x),\qquad x,y\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),

where
\tau
is the standard trace of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. We have a natural
*
-representation
\lambda_{\nu}
of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
. For
\nu=1
this is just the left-regular representation of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
in the original
L_{2}
-space
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. The Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g,\nu}
is essentially selfadjoint on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
and has a compact resolvent belonging to the weak Schatten class
\mathscr{L}_{n/2,\infty}
(see Section
3
).

Cwikel-type estimates

The Cwikel-type estimates of
[
63
]
provide explicit upper bounds for the weak-Schatten quasi-norms of operators
\lambda(x)\Delta^{-n/2p}
and
\Delta^{-n/4p}\lambda(x)\Delta^{-n/4p}
in terms of suitable
L_{q}
-norms of
x
. They include explicit upper bounds for the best constants in those estimates (see also Section
4
).
Namely, if either
p\neq 2
and
q=\max(p,2)
, or
p=2<q
, then

(1.2)

\left\|\lambda(x)\Delta^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq c_{n}(p,q)\|x\|_{L_{q}}\qquad\forall x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),`

where
c_{n}(p,q)
is an explicit constant. There are similar estimates for the operators

\Delta^{-n/4p}\lambda(x)\Delta^{-n/4p}
if
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
(
loc. cit.
).

We extend the Cwikel-type estimates of
[
63
]
to operators
\lambda(x)P
and
Q^{*}\lambda(x)P
, where
P
and
Q
are negative order pseudodifferential operators (
\Psi
DOs) (see Proposition
4.11
for the precise statement). We refer to Section
2.5
(and the references therein) for the main background on
\Psi
DOs on NC tori. As an application of (
1.2
) we extend to operators of the form
\lambda(x)P
and
Q^{*}\lambda(x)P
, with
x
in suitable
L_{p}
-spaces, the usual trace-formula for
\Psi
DOs of order
<-n
(see Proposition
4.12
). Namely, if
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
P
is a
\Psi
DO of order
<-n
with symbol
\rho(\xi)
, then

\operatorname{Tr}\big[\lambda(x)P\big]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\tau\big[x\rho(k)\big].

We have a similar formula for operators
Q^{*}\lambda(x)P
, where
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p>1
, and
P
and
Q
are
\Psi
DOs of order
<-n/2
(
loc. cit.
).

We seek for curved versions of the Cwikel-type estimates of
[
63
]
. For the applications presented in this paper we need to state them in terms of quasi-norms associated with the weighted inner-product (
1.1
) rather than with the original inner product of
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. These two inner products are equivalent, and so this only affects the constants in those inequalities. The Cwikel estimates for
\Psi
DOs allow us to get Cwikel-estimates for powers of the Laplace-Beltrami operators
\Delta_{g,\nu}
(see Theorem
4.17
). Namely, if
p\neq 2
and
q=\max(p,2)
, or
p=2<q
, we have

(1.3)

\left\|\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\leq C_{g,\nu}(p,q)\|x\|_{L_{q}}\qquad\forall x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),

where
\|\cdot\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}
is
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
-quasi-norm with respect to the inner product (
1.1
). This includes explicit upper bounds for the best constants
C_{g,\nu}(p,q)
(
loc. cit.
). There are similar estimates for the compressions
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4q}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4q}
if
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
(
loc. cit.
).

Dixmier trace formulas

In the framework of noncommutative geometry
[
15
]
, the role of the integral is played by positive (normalized) traces on the weak trace class
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
(see also Section
5
). An important class of such traces is provided by Dixmier traces
[
24
]
(see also
[
15
,
61
,
70
]
). An operator
A\in\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
is called
measurable
(resp.,
strongly measurable
) if the value
\varphi(A)
is independent of the trace
\varphi
as it ranges over Dixmier traces (resp., positive normalized traces). Alternatively,
A
is measurable if and only if

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int A=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(N+1)}\sum_{j<N}\lambda_{j}(A)\ \text{exists},

where
\{\lambda_{j}(A)\}
is an eigenvalue sequence for
A
. In this case
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int A
is called the NC integral of
A
.

If
(M^{n},g)
is a closed Riemannian manifold, Connes’ trace theorem
[
14
]
asserts that every
\Psi
DO
P
of order
-n
on
M
is measurable and its NC integral is given by the integral of this principal symbol over the cosphere bundle
S^{*}M
(i.e., its NC residue trace). It was further shown that such operators are actually strongly measurable (see
[
46
]
). This result is also a consequence of the Weyl’s law for negative order
\Psi
DOs of Birman-Solomyak
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

One consequence of Connes’ trace theorem is the integration formula,

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\Delta_{g}^{-\frac{n}{4}}f\Delta_{g}^{-\frac{n}{4}}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int f\Delta_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2}}=\hat{c}(n)\int_{M}f\nu(g)(x)\qquad\forall f\in C^{\infty}(M),

where
\nu(g)(x)
is the Riemannian measure and
\hat{c}(n)=\frac{1}{n}(2\pi)^{-n}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
. This shows that the noncommutative integral recaptures the
Riemannian measure. The formula for
f\Delta_{g}^{-n/2}
actually holds for all
f\in L_{2}(M)
(see
[
46
]
). The formula for
\Delta_{g}^{-n/4}f\Delta_{g}^{-n/4}
further holds for any
f
in the Orlicz space
L\!\log\!L(M)
(see
[
79
,
94
]
; see also
[
71
]
). This result does not hold in general for functions in
L_{1}(M)
(see
[
46
]
).

Connes’ trace theorem and Connes’ integration formulas were extended to NC tori in
[
68
]
(see also
[
65
]
for the integration formula in the flat case). Namely, if
P
is a
\Psi
DO of order
-n
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
, then
P
is strongly measurable, and we have

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int P=\frac{1}{n}\tau\big[c_{P}\big],\qquad c_{P}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{-n}(\xi)d^{n-1}\xi,

where
\rho_{-n}(\xi)
is the principal symbol of
P
. The integration formula involves the spectral Riemannian density,

(1.4)

\tilde{\nu}(g):=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\xi|^{-n}_{g}d\xi,\qquad|\xi|_{g}^{2}:=\sum\xi_{i}\xi_{j}g^{ij},

where
g^{ij}
are the entries of the dual metric
g^{-1}=(g^{ij})
. If the entries of
g
pairwise commute with each other (e.g.,
g
is conformally flat), then
\tilde{\nu}(g)
is just
\sqrt{\det(g)}
. For all
x\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and for arbitrary positive smooth densities
\nu
, we have the integration formula,

(1.5)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}=c(n)\tau\big[x\tilde{\nu}(g)\big]\qquad\text{where}\ c(n)=\frac{1}{n}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|.

We use the Cwikel-type estimates of this paper to get
L_{p}
-versions of the above results. First, we look at operators of the form
\lambda(x)P
and
Q^{*}\lambda(x)P
where
P
and
Q
are negative order
\Psi
DOs (see Theorem
5.2
). More precisely, if
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
P
is a
\Psi
DO of order
-n
, then
\lambda(x)P
is strongly measurable, and we have

(1.6)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\lambda(x)P=\frac{1}{n}\tau\big[xc_{P}\big].

We have a similar result for operators of the form
Q^{*}\lambda(x)P
, where
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p>1
, and
P
and
Q
are
\Psi
DOs of order
-n/2
(
loc. cit.
).

The NC integral does not depend on the choice of the inner product. Therefore, by using the fact that powers of the Laplace-Beltrami operators
\Delta_{g,\nu}
are
\Psi
DOs allows us to get
L_{p}
-versions of the integration formula (
1.5
) (see Theorem
5.11
). More precisely, if
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\mu}^{-n/2}
is strongly measurable and its NC integral1 is computed by the formula (
1.5
). If
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p>1
, then
\Delta_{g,\mu}^{-n/4}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\mu}^{-n/4}
is strongly measurable as well, and we have

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}=c(n)\tau\big[x\tilde{\nu}(g)\big].

CLR and Lieb-Thirring inequalities

For Schrödinger operators
H_{V}:=\Delta+V
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, the CLR inequality
[
21
,
54
,
77
]
gives an upper-bound for the number of negative eigenvalues
N^{-}(H_{V})
(i.e., the number of bound states) in terms of the
L_{n/2}
-norm of the negative part
V_{-}
. Namely,

(1.7)

N^{-}(\Delta+V)\leq C_{n}\int|V_{-}(x)|^{\frac{n}{2}}dx.

The Lieb-Thirring inequalities
[
57
,
58
]
for
n\geq 2
and
\gamma>0
further provide upper bounds for the
\gamma
-moments of the negative eigenvalues. That is,

(1.8)

\sum\lambda_{j}^{-}(H_{V})^{\gamma}\leq L_{\gamma,n}\int|V_{-}(x)|^{\frac{n}{2}+\gamma}dx,

where
\{-\lambda_{j}^{-}(H_{V})\}
are the negative eigenvalues of
H_{V}
counted with multiplicity. We also have Lieb-Thirring inequalities for
n=1
and
\gamma\geq 1/2
(see
[
58
,
97
]
). The Lieb-Thirring inequality for
\gamma=1
is dual to a Sobolev inequality for (finite) orthonormal families in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. There are similar inequalities for fractional Schrödinger operators
\Delta^{n/2p}+V

(see, e.g.,
[
23
,
77
,
51
,
80
]
). All these inequalities have widespread applications in mathematical physics, including semiclassical Weyl’s laws for
L_{p}
-potentials and Lieb’s proof of the stability of matter (see
[
31
,
32
,
56
]
and the references therein).

In
[
63
]
we obtained analogues of the CLR and Lieb-Thirring inequalities for fractional Schrödinger operators
\Delta^{n/2p}+\lambda(V)
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
. This includes explicit upper bounds for the best CLR and LT constants. These inequalities were consequences of the Cwikel-type estimates (
1.2
) and suitable versions of the Birman-Schwinger principle. As in the Euclidean case the Lieb-Thirring inequality for
p=n/2
and
\gamma=1
is dual to a Sobolev inequality for orthonormal families.

Likewise, the curved Cwikel-type estimates (
1.3
) enable us to get CLR and Lieb-Thirring inequalities for fractional Schrödinger operators

H_{V}^{(p)}:=\Delta_{g,\nu}^{n/2p}+\lambda_{\nu}(V)
associated with powers of Laplace-Beltrami operators. As in
[
63
]
we get two CLR inequalities. First, we get an upper bound for the number of negative eigenvalues
N^{-}(H_{V}^{(p)})
(Theorem
6.1
). Namely, if
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
, then, for all
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(1.9)

N^{-}\big(H_{V}^{(p)}\big)-1\leq C_{g,\nu}(2p,2q)^{2p}\big(\big\|V_{-}\big\|_{L_{q}}\big)^{p},

where
V_{-}=\frac{1}{2}(|V|-V)
is the negative part of
V
and
C_{g,\nu}(2p,2q)
is the best constant in the inequality (
1.3
) for the pair
(2p,2q)
. As in
[
63
]
the
-1
summand in the l.h.s. of (
1.9
) arises from removing the dimension of the nullspace of
\Delta_{g,\nu}
(which is just
\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
).

As the nullspace
\ker\Delta_{g,\nu}=\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
is non-trivial, it is natural to restrict ourselves to its orthogonal complement in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
, i.e., the subspace,

\dot{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu):=\left\{u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta});\ \tau[u\nu]=0\right\}.

In other words,
\dot{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
consists of all
u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
that have zero mean value with respect to
\nu
. We denote by
\dot{\Delta}_{g,\nu}
and
\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V)
the restrictions to
\dot{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
of
{\Delta}_{g,\nu}
and
{\lambda}_{\nu}(V)
, respectively. We get a homogeneous upper bound for the number of negative eigenvalues of the operators
\dot{H}^{(p)}_{V}:=\dot{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{n/2p}+\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V)
(Theorem
6.3
). Namely, if
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
, then, for all
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(1.10)

N^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}\big)\leq C_{g,\nu}(2p,2q)^{2p}\big(\big\|V_{-}\big\|_{L_{q}}\big)^{p}.

There are various ways to derive the Lieb-Thirring inequalities (
1.8
) and the dual Sobolev inequality (see, e.g.,
[
31
,
32
,
56
]
). For
n\geq 3
, the simplest way is to deduce the Lieb-Thirring inequalities from the CLR inequality (
1.7
). This was the strategy used in
[
63
]
. Likewise, the CLR inequality (
1.10
) allows us to get Lieb-Thirring inequalities for
n\geq 3
(Theorem
6.5
).
Namely, if
\gamma>0
and
p>1
, then, for all
V=V^{*}\in L_{p+\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(1.11)

\sum\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}\big)^{\gamma}\leq L_{g,\nu}(p,\gamma)\tau\big[(V_{-})^{p+\gamma}\big],

where
\{-\lambda_{j}^{-}(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)})\}
are the negative eigenvalues of
\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}
, and the best constant
L_{g,\nu}(p,\gamma)
is such that

L_{g,\nu}(p,\gamma)\leq\gamma\frac{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(p+\gamma+1)}C_{g,\nu}(2p,2p)^{2p}.

As in the Euclidean case and in
[
63
]
, the Lieb-Thirring inequality (
1.11
) for
p=n/2
and
\gamma=1
is dual to a Sobolev inequality (Theorem
6.7
). Namely, for any orthonormal family
\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{N}\}
in
\dot{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
such that
\partial_{j}u_{\ell}\in{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
, we have

(1.12)

\sum_{\ell\leq N}\big\|du_{\ell}\|^{2}_{g,\nu}\geq K_{g,\nu}\tau\left[\rho^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\right],\qquad\text{where}\ \rho:=(2\pi)^{n}\sum_{\ell\leq N}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{*}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Here
du
is the differential of
u
and
\|\cdot\|_{g,\nu}
is the
L_{2}
-norm on 1-forms defined by
g
and
\nu
(see Section
3
and Section
6
for their precise definitions). Note that the constant
K_{g,\nu}
is independent of
N
. If we set
q=2(n+2)n^{-1}
, then for
N=0
the inequality becomes,

\big\|du_{0}\|^{2}_{g,\nu}\geq K_{g,\nu}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\nu)}\big)^{q},

where
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\nu)
is the
L_{q}
-space associated with
\nu
(see Section
3
). Furthermore, the best constant
K_{g,\nu}
is related to the best LT constant
L_{g,\nu}=L_{g,\nu}(n/2,1)
by

K_{g,\nu}=\frac{n}{n+2}\bigg(\frac{n+2}{2}L_{g,\nu}\bigg)^{-\frac{2}{n}}.

Spectral asymptotics

The original motivation for the Cwikel estimates on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
, came from a conjecture of Simon
[
84
]
regarding semiclassical Weyl’s laws for Schrödinger operators with
L_{p}
-potentials. Alternatively, the Birman-Schwinger principle reduces semiclassical Weyl’s laws to (classical) spectral asymptotics for compact operators. In the case of sufficiently smooth potentials the latter are special cases of a fairly general Weyl’s laws for negative order
\Psi
DOs of Birman-Solomyak
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
. We then can extend the results to non-smooth potentials by using Cwikel-type estimates and Birman-Solomyak’s perturbation theory (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
4
,
8
]
). Note also that Birman-Solomyak’s Weyl’ law implies a stronger form of Connes’ trace theorem (see, e.g.,
[
70
,
79
,
94
]
).

In
[
63
, Conjecture 8.8]
the authors conjectured a semiclassical Weyl’s laws for semiclassical (fractional) Schrödinger operators
h^{n/p}\Delta^{n/2p}+\lambda(V)
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
for suitable
L_{q}
-potentials
V
. In this paper, we give grounds for an analogue for (negative order)
\Psi
DOs on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
of Birman-Solomyak’s Weyl’s law (Conjecture
7.1
). Together with the Cwikel estimates of this paper this Weyl’s law implies the following spectral asymptotics (Theorem
7.3
). If
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
, then, for every
x=x^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(1.13)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{\pm}\left(\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\left(|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}x|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)_{\pm}^{p}\right]d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

where
\pm\lambda_{j}^{\pm}(\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p})
are the positive/negative eigenvalues of
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}
, and
|\xi|_{g}
is defined as in (
1.4
). This implies stronger forms of Connes’ trace theorem for NC tori from
[
69
]
and its
L_{p}
-version (
1.6
) (see Corollary
7.2
).

Thanks to the Birman-Schwinger principle the spectral asymptotics (
1.13
) imply a semiclassical Weyl’s law for fractional Schrödinger operators (Theorem
7.5
). Namely, if
p
and
q
are as above, then, for all
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}h^{n}N^{-}\left(h^{\frac{n}{p}}\Delta_{g,\nu}^{\frac{n}{2p}}+\lambda_{\nu}(V)\right)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\left(|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}V|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\right)_{-}^{p}\right]d\xi.

In particular, for the flat Euclidean metric
g_{0}=(\delta_{ij})
this delivers the conjecture of
[
63
]
mentioned above. A proof of that conjecture for
p=n/2
and
n\geq 3
is also given in
[
66
]
. However, the approach of
[
66
]
cannot be extended to non-flat metrics (see Remark
7.9
on this point).

Outline of the paper

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we review the main background on noncommutative tori, including their
L_{p}
-spaces, Sobolev spaces, and pseudodifferential operators.
In Section
3
, we recall the main facts regarding Riemannian metrics and Laplace-Beltrami operators on NC tori.
In Section
4
, we obtain Cwikel-type estimates for
\Psi
DOs and powers of Laplace-Beltrami operators. We use them to get
L_{p}
-trace formulas . In Section
5
, we establish
L_{p}
-versions of Connes’ trace theorem and Connes’ integration formulas for NC tori. In Section
6
, we establish the CLR and Lieb-Thirring inequalities for fractional Schrödinger operators associated with powers of Laplace-Beltrami operators and get the dual Sobolev inequality (
1.12
). In Section
7
, we discuss spectral asymptotics and semiclassical Weyl’s laws for fractional Schrödinger operators with
L_{p}
-potentials.

2.
Noncommutative Tori

In this section, we review the main definitions and properties of noncommutative
n
-tori,
n\geq 2
. We refer to
[
15
,
36
,
75
]
, and the references therein, for a more comprehensive account.

Throughout this paper, we let
\theta=(\theta_{jk})
be a real anti-symmetric
n\times n
-matrix, and denote by
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{n}
its column vectors. We also let
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
be the Hilbert space of
L_{2}
-functions on the ordinary torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n}/\penalty(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{n}
equipped with the inner product,

(2.1)

\left\langle\xi|\eta\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\xi(x)\overline{\eta(x)}dx,\qquad\xi,\eta\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

For
j=1,\ldots,n
, let
U_{j}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
be the unitary operator defined by

\left(U_{j}\xi\right)(x)=e^{ix_{j}}\xi\left(x+\pi\theta_{j}\right),\qquad\xi\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

We then have the relations,

(2.2)

U_{k}U_{j}=e^{2i\pi\theta_{jk}}U_{j}U_{k},\qquad j,k=1,\ldots,n.

The
noncommutative torus
is the noncommutative space whose
C^{*}
-algebra
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and von Neuman algebra
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
are generated by the unitary operators
U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
. For
\theta=0
we obtain the
C^{*}
-algebra
C(\mathbb{T}^{n})
of continuous functions on the ordinary
n
-torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and the von Neuman algebra
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
of essentially bounded measurable functions on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Note that (
2.2
) implies that
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
(resp.,
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
) is the norm closure (resp., weak closure) in
\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
of the linear span of the unitary operators,

U^{k}:=U_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots U_{n}^{k_{n}},\qquad k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

2.1.
GNS representation

Let
\tau:\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the state defined by

\tau(T)=\left\langle T1|1\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}(T1)(x)dx,\qquad T\in\mathscr{L}\left(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\right).

This induces a continuous tracial state on the von Neuman algebra
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that
\tau(1)=1
and
\tau(U^{k})=0
for
k\neq 0
.
The GNS construction allows us to get a
*
-representation of
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. More precisely, let
\left\langle\cdot|\cdot\right\rangle
be the inner product on
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
defined by

(2.3)

\left\langle u|v\right\rangle=\tau\left(uv^{*}\right),\qquad u,v\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

We then denote by
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
the Hilbert space given by the completion of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with respect to this inner product.

The action of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
on itself by left-multiplication then uniquely extends to a
*
-representation,

(2.4)

\lambda:L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\longrightarrow\mathscr{L}\big(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big).

For
\theta=0
we recover the Hilbert space
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with the inner product (
2.1
) and the representation of
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
by bounded multipliers.

The family
(U^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is actually an orthonormal basis of
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Thus, every
u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
can be uniquely written as

(2.5)

u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k},\qquad u_{k}:=\left\langle u|U^{k}\right\rangle,

where the series converges in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. For
\theta=0
we recover the Fourier series decomposition in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

2.2.
The smooth algebra
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})

The natural action of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
by translation gives rise to an action on
\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
. This induces a
*
-action
(s,u)\rightarrow\alpha_{s}(u)
on
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
given on the basis element
U^{k}
by

\alpha_{s}(U^{k})=e^{is\cdot k}U^{k}\qquad\text{for all $k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}$ and $s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$}.

This action is strongly continuous, and so we obtain a
C^{*}
-dynamical system
(C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\mathbb{R}^{n},\alpha)
. We are especially interested in the subalgebra
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
of smooth elements of this
C^{*}
-dynamical system (a.k.a.
smooth noncommutative torus
). Namely,

C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}):=\biggl\{u\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta});\ \alpha_{s}(u)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n};C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))\biggr\}.

The unitaries
U^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, are contained in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and so
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is a dense subalgebra of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Denote by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
the space of rapid-decay sequences with complex entries. In terms of the Fourier series decomposition (
2.5
) we have

C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})=\bigg\{u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k};(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})\bigg\}.

For
\theta=0
we recover the algebra
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
of smooth functions on the ordinary torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and the Fourier-series description of this algebra.

For
j=1,\ldots,n
, let
\partial_{j}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
be the derivation defined by

\partial_{j}(u)=\partial_{s_{j}}\alpha_{s}(u)|_{s=0},\qquad u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),

For
\theta=0
the derivation
\partial_{j}
is just the derivation
\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}
on
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. For
j,l=1,\ldots,n
, we have

\partial_{j}(U_{l})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}iU_{j}&\text{if $l=j$},\\
0&\text{if $l\neq j$}.\end{array}\right.

Given any multi-order
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
, we set
\delta^{\beta}=\delta_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots\delta_{n}^{\beta_{n}}
, wheres
\delta_{j}=\frac{1}{i}\partial_{j}
.
We endow
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with the locally convex topology defined by the semi-norms,

(2.6)

C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\ni u\longrightarrow\left\|\delta^{\beta}(u)\right\|,\qquad\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.

With the involution inherited from
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
this turns
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
into a (unital) Fréchet
*
-algebra. The Fourier series (
2.5
) of every
u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
converges in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with respect to this topology. In addition, it can be shown that
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is closed under holomorphic functional calculus (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
36
]
).

2.3.
L_{p}
-Spaces

The
L_{p}
-spaces of
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
are special instances of noncommutative
L_{p}
spaces associated with a semi-finite faithful normal trace on a von Neumann algebra
[
47
,
82
]
(see also
[
25
,
67
]
). We refer to
[
25
,
47
]
for the main background on noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces needed in this paper.

By definition
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is a von Neumann algebra of bounded operators on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
. Thus, a closed densely defined operator
T
on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
-affiliated if every unitary in the commutant of
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
in
\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
preserves the domain of
T
and commutes with
T
. Furthermore, as
\tau
is a finite faithful positive trace on
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
every such operator is
\tau
-measurable in the sense of
[
25
,
67
]
. Therefore, these operators form a
*
-algebra, where the sum and product of such operators are meant as the closures of their usual sum and product in the sense of unbounded operators (see
[
67
]
).

The space
L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
consists of all
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
-affiliated operators
x
on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that

\tau\left(|x|\right):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda d\tau(E_{\lambda})<\infty,

where
E_{\lambda}=\mathbbm{1}_{[0,\lambda]}(|x|)
is the spectral measure of
|x|
. We obtain a Banach space upon equipping
L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with the norm,

\|x\|_{L_{1}}:=\tau\big(|x|\big),\qquad x\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

We have a continuous inclusion with dense range of
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
into
L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. The trace
\tau
uniquely extends to a unitarily invariant continuous linear functional on
L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that

|\tau(x)|\leq\tau(|x|)=\|x\|_{1}\qquad\forall x\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

For
p>1
, the space
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
consists of all
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
-affiliated operators
x
on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
such that
|x|^{p}\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. This is a Banach space with respect to the norm,

\|x\|_{L_{p}}:=\tau(|x|^{p})^{1/p},\qquad x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

We also have a continuous inclusion with dense range of
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
into
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. In particular, for
p=2
the above definition is consistent with the previous definition of
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, since in both cases we get the completion of
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with respect to the same norm.

We have the following version of Hölder’s inequality.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
25
,
47
]
)
.

Suppose that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=r^{-1}\leq 1
. If
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
y\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
xy\in L_{r}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with norm inequality,

(2.7)

\|xy\|_{L_{r}}\leq\|x\|_{L_{p}}\|y\|_{L_{q}}.

This implies that, for every
p\geq 1
, the left multiplication of
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
on itself uniquely extends to an isometric representation,

\lambda:L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\longrightarrow\mathscr{L}\big(L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big).

For
p=2
we recover the
*
-representation (
2.4
).

2.4.
Sobolev spaces

Given any
s\geq 0
, the Sobolev space
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is defined by

(2.8)

W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}):=\Big\{u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k}\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta});\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}|u_{k}|^{2}<\infty\Big\}.

This is a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product and norm,

(2.9)

\left\langle u|v\right\rangle_{s}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}u_{k}\overline{v}_{k},\qquad\|u\|_{W_{2}^{s}}=\bigg(\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}|u_{k}|^{2}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that
W_{2}^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})=L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

Equivalently, let
\Delta=-(\partial_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\partial_{n}^{2})
be the Laplacian on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
. This is a non-negative selfadjoint operator on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with domain
W_{2}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. We have

\Delta\big(U^{k})=|k|^{2}U^{k},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

In particular,
\Delta
is isospectral to the Laplacian on the ordinary torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Set
\Lambda=(1+\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Given any
s\geq 0
, we have

W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}):=\Big\{u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta});\ \Lambda^{s}u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\Big\},\qquad\|u\|_{W_{2}^{s}}=\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{W_{2}^{0}}.

In particular,
\Lambda^{s}:W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is a unitary isomorphism. Note also (see
[
89
,
96
]
) that, given an integer
p\geq 0
, we have

W_{2}^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})=\Big\{u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta});\ \delta^{\alpha}u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\ \ \forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n},\,|\alpha|\leq p\big\}.

We mention the following versions of Sobolev’s embedding theorems.

Proposition 2.2

(see
[
96
, Theorem 6.6]
)
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
. For every
s\geq n(1/2-p^{-1})
, we have a continuous inclusion of
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
into
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. This inclusion is compact if the inequality is strict.

Proposition 2.3

(see
[
37
, Appendix A]
)
.

For any
s>n/2
, we have a compact inclusion of
W^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
into
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

2.5.
Pseudodifferential Operators on NC Tori

The pseudodifferential calculus on noncommutative tori is a special case of the pseudodifferential calculus for
C^{*}
-dynamical systems of Connes
[
12
]
and Baaj
[
1
]
. A detailed account on the calculus on NC tori can be found in
[
36
,
37
]
(see also
[
95
]
).

Definition 2.4

(Standard Symbols; see
[
1
,
12
]
)
.

\mathbb{S}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, consists of
C^{\infty}
-maps
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that, for all multi-orders
\alpha
and
\beta
, we have

(2.10)

\big\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\rho(\xi)\big\|\leq C_{\alpha\beta}\left(1+|\xi|\right)^{m-|\beta|}\qquad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Remark 2.5
.

\mathbb{S}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Fréchet space with respect to the topology generated by the semi-norms given by the best constants
C_{\alpha\beta}
in (
2.10
) (see
[
36
]
).

Definition 2.6

(Homogeneous Symbols)
.

S_{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, consists of
C^{\infty}
-maps
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash 0\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
that are homogeneous of degree
m
, i.e.,

\rho(t\xi)=t^{m}\rho(\xi)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash 0
and
t>0
.

Definition 2.7

(Classical Symbols; see
[
1
]
)
.

S^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, consists of
C^{\infty}
-maps
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
that admit an asymptotic expansion,

\rho(\xi)\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}\rho_{q-j}(\xi),\qquad\rho_{q-j}\in S_{q-j}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\sim
means that, for all
N\geq 0
and multi-orders
\alpha
,
\beta
, we have

(2.11)

\Big\|\delta^{\alpha}\partial_{\xi}^{\beta}\big(\rho-\sum_{j<N}\rho_{q-j}\big)(\xi)\Big\|\leq C_{N\alpha\beta}|\xi|^{q-N-|\beta|},

for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
|\xi|\geq 1
.

Remark 2.8
.

We have
S^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset\mathbb{S}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Example 2.9
.

Any polynomial map of the form
\rho(\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
,
a_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is in
S^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Given
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
. We let
P_{\rho}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
be the linear operator defined by

(2.12)

P_{\rho}u=(2\pi)^{-n}\iint e^{is\cdot\xi}\rho(\xi)\alpha_{-s}(u)dsd\xi,\qquad u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

The above integral is meant as an oscillating integral (see
[
36
]
). Equivalently, for all
u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}U^{k}
in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(2.13)

P_{\rho}u=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}u_{k}\rho(k)U^{k}.

In any case, we get a continuous linear operator
P_{\rho}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
(see
[
36
]
).

Definition 2.10
.

\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
m\in\mathbb{R}
, consists of all linear operators
P_{\rho}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with
\rho(\xi)
in
S^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
If
\rho\in S^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is such that
P=P_{\rho}
and
\rho\sim\sum_{j\geq 0}\rho_{q-j}
where
\rho_{q}
is non-zero, then
\rho_{q}
is called the principal symbol of
P.

While the symbol
\rho
is not determined uniquely by the operator
P_{\rho},
the principal symbol
\rho_{q}
is, see
[
37
, Remark 5.7]
.

Example 2.11
.

A differential operator on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
is of the form
P=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\lambda(a_{\alpha})\delta^{\alpha}
,
a_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
(see
[
12
,
15
]
). This is a
\Psi
DO of order
m
with symbol
\rho(\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}a_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
and principal symbol
\rho_{m}(\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}a_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
(see

3.
Riemannian Metrics and Laplace-Beltrami Operators on NC Tori

In this section, we review the main definitions and properties regarding Riemannian metrics and Laplace-Beltrami operators on NC tori as introduced in
[
38
,
76
]
.

3.1.
Riemannian metrics

In what follows, for
m\geq 1
, we denote by
\operatorname{GL}_{m}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
the group of invertible matrices in
M_{m}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
and denote by

\operatorname{GL}_{m}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
its subset of positive matrices. By a positive element of
M_{m}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
we mean a selfadjoint matrix with positive spectrum. For instance,
\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
consists of positive invertible elements of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

Let
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
be the free left-module over
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
generated by the canonical derivations
\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}
. This plays the role of the module of (complex) vector fields on the NC torus
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
(
cf
.
[
76
]
). As
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is a free module, the Hermitian metrics on
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
are in one-to-one correspondence with matrices
h\in\operatorname{GL}^{+}_{n}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
(see
[
38
,
76
]
). Namely, to any matrix
h=(h_{ij})
in
\operatorname{GL}^{+}_{n}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
corresponds the Hermitian metric,

(X,Y)_{h}:=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}X_{i}h_{ij}Y_{j}^{*},\qquad X=\sum_{i}X_{i}\partial_{i},\quad Y=\sum_{j}Y_{j}\partial_{j}.

On a
C^{\infty}
-manifold a Riemannian metric is a Hermitian metric on the module of vector fields that takes real-values on real vector fields and real differential forms. Equivalently, in local coordinates its matrix and its inverse both have real entries. For the NC torus
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n}
the role of real-valued smooth functions is played by selfadjoint elements of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 3.1

(
[
38
,
76
]
)
.

A
Riemannian metric
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
is a Hermitian metric on
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
whose matrix
g=(g_{ij})
is such that its entries
g_{ij}
and the entries of its inverse
g^{-1}=(g^{ij})
are selfadjoint elements of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
.

Remark 3.2
.

As usual we will often identify Riemannian metrics and their matrices.

Example 3.3
.

The standard flat metric is
g_{0}=(\delta_{ij})
.

Example 3.4
.

A conformal deformation of the flat metric is of the form
g=k^{2}g_{0}
with
k\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
. This is the kind of Riemannian metric considered in
[
19
,
20
]
.

Example 3.5

(Self-compatible Riemannian metrics
[
38
]
)
.

Let
g=(g_{ij})\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
be self-compatible in the sense of
[
38
]
, i.e., each entry
g_{ij}
commute with every other entry
g_{kl}
.
In this case, if the entries are selfadjoint, then the inverse
g^{-1}
has selfadjoint entries as well, and so
g
is a Riemannian metric. This example includes the conformally flat metrics of Example
3.4
. It also includes the
functional metrics
of
[
34
]
.

3.2.
Smooth densities

On ordinary manifolds densities (in the sense of differential geometry) are given in local coordinates by integration against positive smooth functions. On the NC torus

\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n}
the role of positive functions is played by positive invertible elements of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, i.e., elements of
\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
. Therefore, it is natural to think of any
\nu\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
as a smooth positive density on
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n}
.

Alternatively, if we think in terms of measures, we may focus on the corresponding weight
\hat{\tau}_{\nu}
on
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
given by

\hat{\tau}_{\nu}(x):=(2\pi)^{n}\tau[x\nu],\qquad x\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

We obtain a faithful weight. The associated
L_{2}
-space
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\nu)
is the completion of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with respect to the inner-product,

(3.1)

\left\langle x|y\right\rangle_{\nu}:=\hat{\tau}_{\nu}(xy^{*})=(2\pi)^{n}\tau\big[y^{*}\nu x\big],\qquad x,y\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Equivalently,
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\nu)
is the GNS representation space associated with
\hat{\tau}_{\nu}
for the opposite algebra
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})^{\textup{op}}
. We then define the
volume
of
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
with respect of
\nu
by

\operatorname{Vol}_{\nu}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}):=\hat{\tau}(1)=(2\pi)^{n}\tau[\nu].

The definition (
3.1
) implies that

\|x\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)}=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\tau\big[x^{*}\nu x\big]^{\frac{1}{2}}=\big\|(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\sqrt{\nu}x\big\|_{L_{2}},\qquad x\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Thus, a unitary isomorphism from
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
onto
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is provided by the left-multiplication operator,

\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big):=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\lambda\big(\sqrt{\nu})

In particular,
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\nu)
and
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
agree as topological vector spaces. Thus,
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
is just the Hilbert space
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with an equivalent inner product.

As
\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})
is a unitary isomorphism, we have a
*
-representation
\lambda_{\nu}:L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu))
given by

\lambda_{\nu}(x)=\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{-1}\lambda(x)\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)=\lambda\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big),\qquad x\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

In other words, we obtain a
*
-representation by composing the representation
\lambda
with the inner automorphism,

(3.2)

\sigma_{\nu}(x):=\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad x\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

More generally, for
p\in(1,\infty)
we denote by
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
the Banach space
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
equipped with the equivalent norm,

\|x\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)}:=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\big\|\sqrt{\nu}x\big\|_{L_{p}}.

In particular, as in the
p=2
case, the left-multiplication operator
\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{v})=(2\pi)^{n/2}\lambda(\sqrt{v})
provides us with
an isometric isomorphism from
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
onto
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

3.3.
Riemannian densities

On an ordinary Riemannian manifold
(M^{n},g)
the Riemannian density is given in local coordinates by integration against
\sqrt{\det(g(x))}
. The volume of
(M,g)
is then obtained as the integral of the Riemannian density.

Following
[
38
]
, given any
h\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
, its determinant is defined by

\det(h):=\exp\big[\operatorname{Tr}(\log h)\big]\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})),

where
\log h
is defined by holomorphic functional calculus and
\operatorname{Tr}:M_{n}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is the sum of the diagonal entries. This defines a positive invertible element of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
owing to the closedness of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
M_{n}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
under holomorphic functional calculus. We refer to
[
38
]
for an account on the main properties of this notion of determinant.
In particular, if
h=(h_{ij})
is self-compatible in the sense mentioned in Example
3.5
, we have

(3.3)

\det(h)=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m}}\varepsilon(\sigma)h_{1\sigma(1)}\cdots h_{m\sigma(m)}.

Given any Riemannian metric
g=(g_{ij})
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
its
Riemannian density
is defined by

\nu(g):=\sqrt{\det(g)}=\exp\big[\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\log(g)\right)\big]\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})).

The corresponding volume with respect to
\nu(g)
is simply denoted
\operatorname{Vol}_{g}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, i.e.,

\operatorname{Vol}_{g}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})=(2\pi)^{n}\tau\big[\nu(g)\big].

Example 3.6
.

For the flat metric
g_{0}=(\delta_{ij})
, we have
\nu(g_{0})=1
, and hence

\operatorname{Vol}_{g_{0}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})=(2\pi)^{n}\tau(1)=(2\pi)^{n}=|\mathbb{T}^{n}|.

Example 3.7
.

Let
g=k^{2}g_{0}
,
k\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
, be a conformal deformation of the flat Euclidean metric. Then

\nu(g)=k^{n}
, and so we have
\operatorname{Vol}_{g}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})=(2\pi)^{n}\tau[k^{n}]
.

3.4.
The Laplace-Beltrami operator

Let
g=(g_{ij})
be a Riemannian metric with inverse
g^{-1}=(g^{ij})
, and let

\nu\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
be a smooth positive density. As in
[
38
]
we define the
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
-module of
1
-forms
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
as the
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
-linear dual of the module of vector fields
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. That is, a
1
-form is just a
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
-linear map
\omega:\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. Here
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is a right
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
-module; the action of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is such that, for
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

\left\langle\omega a,X\right\rangle=a\left\langle\omega,X\right\rangle\qquad\forall X\in\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

A basis of
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is given by the
1
-forms
\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{n}
such that

\theta^{i}(\partial_{j})=\delta_{j}^{i},\qquad i,j=1,\ldots,n.

Thus, any
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
has a unique decomposition as
\omega=\sum\theta^{i}\omega_{i}
with
\omega_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. If
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
\omega a=\sum\theta^{i}\omega_{i}a
. We also observe that we have a left-action of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
on
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
given by

a\omega:=\sum\theta^{i}(a\omega_{i}),\qquad a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\quad\omega=\sum\theta^{i}\omega_{i}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

This turns
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
into a bimodule. In particular, the modular automorphism (
3.2
) lifts to a linear isomorphism
\sigma_{\nu}:\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
given by

\sigma_{\nu}(\omega):=\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\omega\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sum\theta^{i}\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{i}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big),\qquad\omega=\sum\theta^{i}\omega_{i}\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

By duality the Hermitian metric
(\cdot,\cdot)_{g}
on
\mathscr{X}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
defines a Hermitian metric
(\cdot,\cdot)_{g^{-1}}
on
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
given by

(\omega,\eta)_{g^{-1}}=\sum\eta_{i}^{*}g^{ij}\omega_{j},\qquad\omega=\sum\theta^{i}\omega_{i},\quad\eta=\sum\theta^{i}\eta_{i}.

We then endow
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with the inner-product,

(3.4)

\left\langle\omega|\eta\right\rangle_{g,\nu}:=\tau\big[(\sigma_{\nu}^{-1}(\omega),\sigma_{\nu}^{-1}(\eta))_{g^{-1}}\big]=\sum_{i,j}\tau\left[\eta_{i}^{*}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}g^{ij}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\omega_{j}\right],\qquad\omega,\eta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

The
exterior differential

d:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is given by

du:=\sum\theta^{i}\partial_{i}(u),\qquad u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Following
[
38
]
, the
Laplace-Beltrami operator

\Delta_{g,\nu}:C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is defined by

(3.5)

\left\langle\Delta_{g,\nu}u|v\right\rangle_{\nu}=\left\langle du|du\right\rangle_{g,\nu}\qquad\text{for all}\ u,v\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

If
\nu=\nu(g)
we simply denote it by
\Delta_{g}
. Equivalently, we have

(3.6)

\Delta_{g,\nu}u=-\nu^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\partial_{i}\big(\sqrt{\nu}g^{ij}\sqrt{\nu}\partial_{j}(u)\big),\qquad u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Example 3.8
.

For the flat metric
g_{0}=(\delta_{ij})
the operator
\Delta_{g_{0}}
is just the ordinary Laplacian
\Delta=-(\partial_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\partial_{n}^{2})
.

Example 3.9
.

Let
g=k^{2}\delta_{ij}
,
k\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
, be a conformally flat metric. In this case, we have

\Delta_{g}=k^{-2}\Delta-\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}k^{-n}\partial_{i}(k^{n-2})\partial_{i}.

In particular, in dimension
n=2
we get
\Delta_{g}=k^{-2}\Delta
. This is reminiscent of the conformal invariance of the Laplace-Beltrami operator in dimension 2.

In what follows, we set

(3.7)

|\xi|_{g}:=\big(\sum_{i,j}\xi_{i}g^{ij}\xi_{j}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Note that
|\xi|_{g}\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
if
\xi\neq 0
. In fact (see, e.g.,
[
38
, Corollary 3.1]
) there are constants
c_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
such that

(3.8)

c_{1}|\xi|^{2}\leq|\xi|_{g}^{2}\leq c_{2}|\xi|^{2}\qquad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Combining with (
3.6
) and Example
2.11
we immediately get the following result.

Proposition 3.10

(
[
38
]
)
.

The operator
\Delta_{g,\nu}
is an elliptic 2nd order differential operator whose principal symbol is equal to

\sigma_{\nu}(|\xi|^{2}_{g})=\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|_{g}^{2}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Thanks to this result the spectral theory for elliptic
\Psi
DOs applies (see, e.g.,
[
37
]
).
The following gathers the main spectral properties of
\Delta_{g,\nu}
.

Proposition 3.11

(
[
38
]
)
.

The following holds.

(1)

The operator
\Delta_{g,\nu}
with domain
W_{2}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is selfadjoint on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\nu)
and has compact resolvent.

(2)

The spectrum of
\Delta_{g,\nu}
consists of isolated non-negative eigenvalues with finite multiplicity.

(3)

Each eigenspace
\ker(\Delta_{g,\nu}-\lambda)
,
\lambda\in\operatorname{Sp}(\Delta_{g,\nu})
is a finite dimensional subspace of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. In particular,
\ker\Delta_{g,\nu}=\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
.

Thanks to the selfadjointness and positivity of
\Delta_{g,\nu}
, for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
we may define the power
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{s}
by Borel functional calculus. Equivalently,
if
(u_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 0}
is an orthonormal eigenbasis of
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
such that
\Delta_{g,\nu}u_{\ell}=\lambda_{\ell}u_{\ell}
and
u_{0}=1
, then

\Delta_{g,\nu}^{s}u_{0}=0,\qquad\Delta_{g,\nu}^{s}u_{\ell}=\lambda_{\ell}^{s}u_{\ell},\quad\ell\geq 1.

More generally, we can define complex powers
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{z}
for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
. We similarly define the powers
(1+\Delta_{g,s})^{z}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 3.12

(
[
49
,
68
]
)
.

For every
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the powers
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{s}
and
(1+\Delta_{g,s})^{s}
are operators in
\Psi^{2s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
whose principal symbols are both equal to
\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|_{g}^{2s}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Remark 3.13
.

For
\nu=\nu(g)
the result for
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{s}
is part of the contents of
[
68
, Lemma 10.3]
(see also
[
48
]
), but the proof remains valid for arbitrary smooth densities and for the powers
(1+\Delta_{g,s})^{s}
. More generally, it can be shown that the complex powers
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{z}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and

(1+\Delta_{g,s})^{z}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
, are holomorphic families of
\Psi
DOs of order
2z
in the sense of
[
53
,
69
]
(see
[
49
]
).

3.5.
The Sobolev spaces
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,v)

For
s\geq 0
, we denote by
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
the Sobolev space with Bessel potential
(1+\Delta_{g,\nu})^{s/2}
, i.e.,

W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu):=\left\{u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu);\ (1+\Delta_{g,\nu})^{\frac{s}{2}}u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\right\}.

It is equipped with the Hilbert norm,

\|u\|_{W^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)}:=\big\|(1+\Delta_{g,\nu})^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)},\qquad u\in W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu).

For
s=0
we recover the Hilbert space
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
. We denote by
W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
the antilinear dual of
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
. The duality pairing of
W^{-s}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
with
W^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
will be denoted by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle.

Note that
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}
induces a bounded operator
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\rightarrow W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
. By duality it further extends to a bounded operator
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}:W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
given by

(3.9)

\left\langle\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{s}{2}}u,v\right\rangle=\left\langle u|\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{s}{2}}v\right\rangle_{\nu},\qquad u\in W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu),\quad v\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu).

Proposition 3.14
.

For any
s\geq 0
, the Sobolev spaces
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
agree as topological vector spaces. By duality, their antilinear duals

W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
agree as topological vector spaces as well.

Proof.

For
s=0
we already know that
W_{2}^{0}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})=L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)=L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
agree as topological vector spaces. Let
s>0
. As
(1+\Delta_{g,\nu})^{\pm s/2}
are
\Psi
DOs of order
\pm s
, the composition
\Lambda^{s}(1+\Delta_{g,\nu})^{-s/2}
and its inverse

(1+\Delta_{g,\nu})^{s/2}\Lambda^{-s}
are
\Psi
DOs of order
0
, and hence are bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. We thus get an invertible bounded operator

\Lambda^{s}(1+\Delta_{g,\nu})^{-s/2}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with bounded inverse. It then follows that
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
agree as vector spaces and have equivalent norms. This gives the result.
∎

Combining this result with the Sobolev’s embeddings provided by Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
we obtain the following versions of those results.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
p\in[2,\infty)
. For every
s\geq n(1/2-p^{-1})
, we have a continuous inclusion of
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
into
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
. This inclusion is compact if the inequality is strict.

Corollary 3.16
.

For any
s>n/2
, we have a compact inclusion of
W^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
into
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

4.
Cwikel-Type Estimates

In this section, we extend the Cwikel-type estimates of
[
63
]
to pseudodifferential operators and powers of Laplace-Beltrami operators.

4.1.
Weak Schatten classes

We briefly review the main definitions and properties of weak Schatten classes. We refer to
[
33
,
85
]
for further details.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be a (separable) Hilbert space with inner product
\left\langle\cdot|\cdot\right\rangle
. The algebra of bounded linear operators on
\mathscr{H}
is denoted
\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{H})
and its norm is denoted
\|\cdot\|
. We also denote by
\mathscr{K}
its closed ideal of compact operators on
\mathscr{H}
.

Given any compact operator
T
, we denote by
(\mu_{j}(T))_{j\geq 0}
its singular value sequence, i.e.,
\mu_{j}(T)
is the
(j+1)
-th eigenvalue (counted with multiplicity) of the absolute value
|T|=\sqrt{T^{*}T}
. Recall that by the min-max principle we have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mu_{j}(T)

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\|T_{|E^{\perp}}\|;\ \dim E=j\right\}.

For
p\in(0,\infty)
, the weak Schatten class
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
is defined by

\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}:=\left\{T\in\mathscr{K};\ \mu_{j}(T)=\operatorname{O}\big(j^{-\frac{1}{p}}\big)\right\}.

We shall also use the notation
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}(\mathscr{H})
whenever we need to highlight the background Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
.

The weak Schatten class
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
is a two-sided ideal. We equip it with the quasi-norm,

(4.2)

\|T\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}:=\sup_{j\geq 0}\;(j+1)^{\frac{1}{p}}\mu_{j}(T),\qquad T\in\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}.

With this quasi-norm
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
is a quasi-Banach ideal. In particular, we have

\|ATB\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq\|A\|\|T\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\|B\|\qquad A,B\in\mathscr{L}(\mathscr{H}),\ T\in\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}.

For
p>1
the above quasi-norm is equivalent to norm, and so in this case
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
is a Banach ideal.

We also have a version of Hölder’s inequality for weak Schatten classes (see
[
33
,
85
,
93
]
). Namely, if
S\in\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
and
T\in\mathscr{L}_{q,\infty}
with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=r^{-1}
, then
ST\in\mathscr{L}_{r,\infty}
, and we have

(4.3)

\|ST\|_{\mathscr{L}_{r,\infty}}\leq p^{-\frac{1}{q}}q^{-\frac{1}{p}}(p+q)^{\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}}\|S\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\|ST\|_{\mathscr{L}_{q,\infty}}.

The above constant is sharp (see
[
93
]
). Note that for
p=q
it is equal to
4^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

4.2.
Cwikel-type estimates on flat NC tori

We briefly recall the Cwikel estimates on flat NC tori given in
[
63
]
.

The Hölder’s inequality for the
L_{p}
-spaces of
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
(
cf
. Proposition
2.1
) implies the following extension result for the left-regular representation

\lambda:L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathscr{L}(L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}))
,
p\geq 1
.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
47
]
)
.

Suppose that
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=r^{-1}\leq 1
. Then the left-regular representation uniquely extends to a linear isometry,

\lambda:L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\longrightarrow\mathscr{L}\big(L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),L_{r}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big).

In particular, if
p\geq 2
and
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=2^{-1}
, then we get a linear isometry,

\lambda:L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\longrightarrow\mathscr{L}\big(L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\big).

Given any
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
with
p\geq 2
, the above proposition asserts that
\lambda(x)
is a bounded operator from
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
to
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1/2
. In particular, we may regard it as an unbounded operator on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with domain
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
.

Note also that if
p>2
, then
q\in[2,\infty)
, and so by Proposition
2.2
we have a continuous embedding of
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
into
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
for
s\geq n(1/2-q^{-1})=n/p
. If
p=2
, then
q=\infty
and it follows from Proposition
2.3
that we have continuous embedding of
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
into
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
for
s>n/2=n/p
. Therefore, we arrive at the following result.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p\geq 2
, and assume that, either
s>n/p
, or
s=n/p
and
p>2
. Then
\lambda(x)
induces a bounded operator from
W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
to
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
, and so the operator
\lambda(x)\Delta^{-s/2}
is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

Given any
q\geq 1
, we denote by
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})^{*}
the anti-linear dual of
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, i.e., the space of continuous anti-linear forms on

L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. We observe that the left-regular regular representation of
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
can be regarded as an isometric linear map
\lambda:L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})^{*})
such that

\left\langle\lambda(x)u,v\right\rangle=\left\langle\lambda(x)u|v\right\rangle=\tau\big[v^{*}xu\big],\qquad x\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\quad u,v\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Lemma 4.3

(see
[
63
]
)
.

Suppose that
p^{-1}+2q^{-1}=1
. Then the left-regular representation uniquely extends to a continuous linear map
\lambda:L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow\mathscr{L}(L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})^{*})
such that

(4.4)

\left\langle\lambda(x)u,v\right\rangle=\tau\big[v^{*}xu\big]\qquad\forall x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\quad\forall u,v\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

In particular, if
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with
1\leq p<2
, then the above proposition shows that
\lambda(x)
makes sense as a bounded operator from
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
to
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})^{*}
, with
p^{-1}+2q^{-1}=1
, i.e.,
q^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}(1-p^{-1})
.

For
s>0
, by duality the continuous inclusion

W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\hookrightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
gives rise to a continuous embedding
\iota^{*}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that

\left\langle\iota^{*}u,v\right\rangle=\left\langle u|v\right\rangle,\qquad u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\quad v\in W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

This allows us to identify
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with a dense subspace of
W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

The operator
\Delta^{-s/2}
induces a bounded operator
\Delta^{-s/2}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow W^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. It uniquely extends to a bounded operator
\Delta^{-s/2}:W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
given by

\big\langle\Delta^{-\frac{s}{2}}u|v\big\rangle=\big\langle u,\Delta^{\frac{s}{2}}v\big\rangle,\qquad u\in W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\quad v\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Lemma 4.4

(see
[
63
]
)
.

Let
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p\geq 1
, and assume that, either
s>n/2p
, or
s=n/2p
and
p>1
. Then
\lambda(x)
uniquely extends to a bounded operator
\lambda(x):W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and so the operator
\Delta^{-s/2}\lambda(x)\Delta^{-s/2}
is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

To sum up the operators
\lambda(x)\Delta^{-s/2}
and
\Delta^{-s/2}\lambda(x)\Delta^{-s/2}
are bounded provided that
x
is in an appropriate
L_{p}
-space. As the following two results assert, they actually lie in suitable weak Schatten classes.

Proposition 4.5

(
[
63
, Theorem 6.1]
; see also
[
52
,
64
]
)
.

Suppose that
p\neq 2
and
q=\max(p,2)
, or
p=2<q
. If
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
\lambda(x)\Delta^{-n/2p}
is in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
, and we have

(4.5)

\big\|\lambda(x)\Delta^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq c_{n}(p,q)\|x\|_{L_{q}}.

Moreover, the best constant
c_{n}(p,q)
satisfies

c_{n}(p,q)\leq c_{0}(p,q)\nu_{0}(n)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where we have set

(4.6)

\displaystyle\nu_{0}(n):=\sup_{\lambda\geq 1}\lambda^{-\frac{n}{2}}\#\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus 0;\ |k|\leq\sqrt{\lambda}\right\},

(4.7)

\displaystyle c_{0}(p,q):=\left(\frac{130p}{p-2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad(p=q>2),\qquad c_{0}(p,q):=2^{\frac{1}{p}}(2-p)^{-\frac{1}{p}}\quad(p>2=q),

(4.8)

\displaystyle c_{0}(p,q):=2^{-\frac{1}{q}}q^{-\frac{1}{2}}(q-2)^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{2}}c_{0}(q,q)\quad(p=2<q).

Remark 4.6
.

For
p>2
the estimate (
4.5
) is obtained with an unspecified constant in
[
64
]
as a special case of the results of
[
52
]
.

Remark 4.7
.

The estimate (
4.5
) for
p=q=2
does not hold (see Remark
4.10
below).

Remark 4.8
.

The estimate (
4.5
) is a special case of more general Cwikel estimates for operators of the form
\lambda(x)g(-i\nabla)
with
g\in\ell_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
(see
[

). Here the operator
g(-i\nabla):L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is defined by

(4.9)

g(-i\nabla)U^{k}=g(k)U^{k},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}.

In particular, if

for

and

, then
g(-i\nabla)=\Delta^{-n/2p}
. The constants

for

in (

Proposition 4.9

(
[

)
.

Suppose that

and

, or
p=1<q
. If

, then

\Delta^{-{n}/{4p}}\lambda(x)\Delta^{-{n}/{4p}}
is in

, and we have

\big\|\Delta^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda(x)\Delta^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}c_{n}(2p,2q)^{2}\|x\|_{L_{q}}

where

is the best constant in the inequality (

) for the pair
(2p,2q)
. If

is positive, then the inequality holds without the

-factor.

.

\mathbb{T}^{n}

\theta=0

4.3.
Cwikel-type estimates for
\Psi
DOs

We shall now explain how to extend to pseudodifferential operators the
Cwikel-type estimates provided by Proposition
4.5
and Proposition
4.9
.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m>0
. We know from Proposition
2.19
that
P_{\rho}
extends to a bounded operator
P_{\rho}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow W_{2}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Let
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p\geq 2
, and assume that,
either
m>n/p
, or
m=n/p
and
p>2
. In any case Lemma
4.2
ensures that
\lambda(x)
induces a bounded operator from
W_{2}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
to
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and so the operator
\lambda(x)P_{\rho}
is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

If
\sigma(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then by Proposition
2.15
the formal adjoint
P_{\sigma}^{*}
of
P_{\sigma}
is
P_{\sigma^{\star}}
, where
\sigma^{\star}(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is given by (
2.15
). In particular,
P_{\sigma}^{*}
uniquely extends to a bounded operator
P_{\sigma}^{*}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow W_{2}^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. It then follows that
P_{\sigma}
uniquely extends to a bounded
P_{\rho_{2}}:W_{2}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that

(4.11)

\left\langle P_{\rho_{2}}u|v\right\rangle=\left\langle u,P_{\rho_{2}}^{*}v\right\rangle,\qquad u\in W_{2}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}),\qquad v\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

If
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p\geq 1
, and either
m>n/2p
, or
m=n/2p
and
p>1
, then Lemma
4.3
ensures that

\lambda(x)
uniquely extends to a bounded operator
\lambda(x):W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. It then follows that the operator

P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}
is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

In what follows, we denote by
\Pi_{0}
the orthogonal projection onto
\ker\Delta=\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
. That is,

\Pi_{0}u=\tau(u)1,\qquad u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Note that
\Pi_{0}
is a rank 1 operator.

We have the following extension to
\Psi
DOs of the Cwikel-type estimates (
4.5
) and (
4.10
).

Proposition 4.11

(Cwikel Estimates.
\Psi
DO Version)
.

The following hold.

(1)

Suppose that
p\neq 2
and
q=\max(p,2)
, or
p=2<q
. If
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
\rho(\xi)
is in
\mathbb{S}^{-n/p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then

\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\in\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
, and we have

(4.12)

\big\|\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1\big)\|P_{\rho}\|_{\mathscr{L}(L_{2},W_{2}^{n/p})}\|x\|_{L_{q}},

where
c_{n}(p,q)
is the best constant in (
4.5
).

(2)

Suppose that,
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
. If
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and
\rho(\xi)
and
\sigma(\xi)
are in
\mathbb{S}^{-n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\in\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
, and we have

(4.13)

\big\|P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\big(c_{n}(2p,2q)+1\big)^{2}\|P_{\sigma}\|_{\mathscr{L}(L_{2},W_{2}^{n/2p})}\|P_{\rho}\|_{\mathscr{L}(L_{2},W_{2}^{n/2p})}\|x\|_{L_{q}},

where
c_{n}(2p,2q)
is the best constant in (
4.5
) for the pair
(2p,2q)
. Moreover, if
x
is positive and
\rho=\sigma
, then the inequality holds without the
2^{1/p}
-factor.

Proof.

Suppose that, either
p\neq 2
and
q=\max(p,2)
, or
p=2<q
, and let
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Let us first prove (
4.12
) for
\rho(\xi)=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-n/2p}
, i.e.,
P_{\rho}=\Lambda^{-n/p}
. In the notation of (
4.9
) we have
\Lambda=g(-i\nabla)
with
g(k)=(1+|k|^{2})^{-n/4p}
, and so by Remark
4.8
and the results of
[
63
]
the operator
\lambda(x)\Lambda^{-n/p}
is in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
.

In fact, we have

(4.14)

\lambda(x)\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}=\lambda(x)(1-\Pi_{0})\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}+\lambda(x)\Pi_{0}\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}=\lambda(x)\Delta^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\cdot\Delta^{\frac{n}{2p}}\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}+\lambda(x)\Pi_{0}.

Here
\Delta^{\frac{n}{2p}}\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}
is a bounded operator of norm
\leq 1
. Thus, by Proposition
4.5
the operator
\lambda(x)\Delta^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\cdot\Delta^{\frac{n}{2p}}\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}
is in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
, and we have

(4.15)

\big\|\lambda(x)\Delta^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\cdot\Delta^{\frac{n}{2p}}\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq\big\|\lambda(x)\Delta^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\cdot\big\|\Delta^{\frac{n}{2p}}\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}\big\|\leq c_{n}(p,q)\|x\|_{L_{q}}.

Moreover, as
\lambda(x)\Pi_{0}
is an operator of rank
\leq 1
, it is contained in the weak Schatten class
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
. Furthermore, by the min-max principle (
4.1
) and the very definition of the weak Schatten class norm (
4.2
) we have

\big\|\lambda(x)\Pi_{0}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}=\mu_{0}\left(\lambda(x)\Pi_{0}\right)=\big\|\lambda(x)\Pi_{0}\big\|=\|\tau\|\|x\|_{L_{2}}\leq\|x\|_{L_{q}}.

Combining this with (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) then gives

(4.16)

\big\|\lambda(x)\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1\big)\|x\|_{L_{q}}.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{-n/p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. As the operator
P_{\rho}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow W_{2}^{n/p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is bounded, the composition
\Lambda^{n/p}P_{\rho}
is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Moreover, the very definition (
2.9
) of the norm of
W_{2}^{n/p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
implies that
\|\Lambda^{n/p}P_{\rho}\|=\|P_{\rho}\|_{\mathscr{L}(L_{2},W_{2}^{n/p})}
. As
\lambda(x)P_{\rho}=\lambda(x)\Lambda^{-n/p}\cdot\Lambda^{n/p}P_{\rho}
, we deduce that
that
\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\in\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
, and by using (
4.16
) we get

\big\|\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq\big\|\lambda(x)\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\big\|\Lambda^{\frac{n}{p}}P_{\rho}\|\leq\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1\big)\|x\|_{L_{q}}\|P_{\rho}\|_{\mathscr{L}(L_{2},W_{2}^{n/p})}.

This proves (
4.12
)

The proof of the 2nd part is similar to the proof of the 2nd part of
[
63
, Theorem 6.5]
. Suppose now that,
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
. Let
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and assume that
\rho(\xi)
and
\sigma(\xi)
are symbols in
\mathbb{S}^{-n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We may write
x=yz
with
y,z\in L_{2q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that
\|y\|_{L_{2q}}=\|z\|_{L_{2q}}=(\|x\|_{L_{q}})^{1/2}
(see, e.g.,
[
63
, Lemma 6.4]
). By the first part
\lambda(y)P_{\sigma}
and
\lambda(z)P_{\rho}
are in the weak Schatten class
\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}
. Moreover, by using (
4.11
) and arguing
along similar lines as that of the proof of
[
63
, Lemma 6.4]
it can be shown that

P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}=\big(\lambda(y^{*})P_{\sigma}\big)^{*}\lambda(z)P_{\rho}.

Thus, by the Hölder inequality (
4.3
) the operator
P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}
is in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
, and we have

(4.17)

\big\|P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\big\|\left(\lambda(y^{*})P_{\sigma}\right)^{*}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}}\big\|\lambda(z)P_{\rho}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}}.

By using (
4.12
) and the fact that
\|y^{*}\|_{L_{2q}}=\|y\|_{L_{2q}}=\|z\|_{L_{2q}}=(\|x\|_{L_{q}})^{1/2}
we get

\displaystyle\big\|\lambda(z)P_{\rho}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}}\leq\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1\big)\|z\|_{L_{2q}}=\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1\big)\big(\|x\|_{L_{2q}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\big\|\left(\lambda(y^{*})P_{\sigma}\right)^{*}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}}=\big\|\lambda(y^{*})P_{\sigma}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}}\leq\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1\big)\|y^{*}\|_{L_{2q}}=\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1\big)\big(\|x\|_{L_{2q}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining these inequalities with (
4.17
) gives (
4.13
).

If
x
is positive, then we may take
y=z=\sqrt{x}
. If in addition
\sigma=\rho
, then (
4.17
) becomes
P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}=(\lambda(\sqrt{x})P_{\sigma})^{*}(\lambda(\sqrt{x})P_{\sigma})
, and so we get

\big\|P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}=\big\|\big(\lambda(\sqrt{x})P_{\sigma}\big)^{*}\big(\lambda(\sqrt{x})P_{\sigma}\big)\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}=\big\|\lambda(\sqrt{x})P_{\sigma}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}}^{2}.

Combining this with the inequality (
4.12
) for
z=\sqrt{x}
gives the inequality (
4.13
) without the
2^{1/p}
-factor. The proof is complete.
∎

As a first application of the Cwikel-type estimates (
4.12
)–(
4.13
) we shall now explain how they enable to get
L_{p}
-versions of the trace formula (
2.16
).

Any operator in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
with
p<1
is trace class. Thus, Proposition
4.11
ensures that if
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
m>n
, then the operator
\lambda(x)P_{\rho}
is trace-class. Likewise, if
x\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
\rho(\xi)
and
\sigma(\xi)
are symbols in
\mathbb{S}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
m>n/2
, then Proposition
4.11
also ensures that
P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}
is trace-class. We then have the following extension of the trace formula (
2.16
).

Proposition 4.12

(
L_{p}
-Trace Formulas)
.

The following hold.

(1)

If
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m>n
, and
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then

(4.18)

\operatorname{Tr}\big[\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\tau\big[x\rho(k)\big].

(2)

If
x\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
\rho(\xi)
and
\sigma(\xi)
are in
\mathbb{S}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m>n/2
, then

(4.19)

\operatorname{Tr}\big[P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\tau\big[x\rho(k)\sigma(k)^{*}\big].

In particular, we have

(4.20)

\operatorname{Tr}\big[P_{\rho}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\tau\big[x|\rho(k)|^{2}\big].

Proof.

Let
\rho(\xi)\in\mathbb{S}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
m>n
, and
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. As
\{U^{k};\ k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
\rho(\xi)
takes values in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(4.21)

\operatorname{Tr}\big[\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left\langle\lambda(x)P_{\rho}(U^{k})|U^{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left\langle x\rho(k)U^{k}|U^{k}\right\rangle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\tau\big[(U^{k})^{*}x\rho(k)U^{k}\big].

Recall that
\tau
is a unitarily invariant functional on
L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and the basis elements
U^{k}
are unitary. Thus,
\tau[(U^{k})^{*}x\rho(k)U^{k}]=\tau[x\rho(k)]
, and so we get (
4.18
).

Suppose now that
x\in L_{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
\sigma(\xi)
are symbols in
\mathbb{S}^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
m>n/2
. In the same way as in (
4.21
) we have

(4.22)

\operatorname{Tr}\big[P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}\big]=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\left\langle P_{\sigma}^{*}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}(U^{k})|U^{k}\right\rangle.

In view of (
4.4
) and (
4.11
) we have

(4.23)

\left\langle P_{\sigma}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}(U^{k})|U^{k}\right\rangle=\left\langle\lambda(x)P_{\rho}(U^{k}),P_{\sigma}(U^{k})\right\rangle=\tau\left[[P_{\sigma}(U^{k})]^{*}xP_{\rho}(U^{k})\right].

As
P_{\rho}(U^{k})=\rho(k)U^{k}
and
P_{\sigma}(U^{k})=\sigma(k)U^{k}
, we get

\left\langle P_{\sigma}\lambda(x)P_{\rho}(U^{k})|U^{k}\right\rangle=\tau\left[(U^{k})^{*}\sigma(k)^{*}x\rho(k)U^{k}\right]=\tau\big[x\rho(k)\sigma(k)^{*}\big].

Combining this with (
4.22
) then gives (
4.19
). The proof is complete.
∎

4.4.
Curved Cwikel estimates

Let
g=(g_{ij})
be a Riemannian metric and
\nu\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
a smooth positive density on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
. We shall now explain how to extend the Cwikel estimates of Proposition
4.5
and Proposition
4.9
to powers of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g,\nu}
. Recall that
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}
,
s>0
, gives rise to bounded operators
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\rightarrow W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
and
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}:W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
.

As mentioned in Subsection
3.2
, the left-mutiplication operator
\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})
provides us with an isometric isomorphism from
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
onto

L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
. Combining this with Lemma
4.1
for
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=r^{-1}\leq 1
we get an isometric linear map,

\lambda_{\nu}:L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})\longrightarrow\mathscr{L}\big(L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu),L_{r}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\big),\qquad

where

(4.24)

\lambda_{\nu}(x)=\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{-1}\lambda(x)\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)=\lambda\big(\sigma_{\nu}(x)\big),\qquad x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}).

Here
\sigma_{\nu}(x)
is the inner automorphism (
3.2
). In particular, if
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p\geq 2
, and
p^{-1}+q^{-1}=1/2
, then
\lambda_{\nu}(x)
maps continuously
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
to
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
.

We are interested in operators of the form

\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}
and
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}
with
s>0
. For the applications we have in mind, it is important to regard them as operators on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
rather than as operators on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

In the same way as with Lemma
4.2
, by combining this with the continuous embeddings provided by Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
we get the following result.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p\geq 2
, and assume that, either
s>np^{-1}
, or
s=np^{-1}
and
p>2
. Then
\lambda_{\nu}(x)
induces a bounded operator

\lambda_{\nu}(x):W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n};\nu)
, and hence
\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}
is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
.

Remark 4.14
.

If
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p\geq 2
, and
s
are as in Lemma
4.13
, then (
4.24
) implies that

(4.25)

\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}=\lambda\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}.

Let
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)^{*}
,
q\geq 1
, be the anti-linear dual of
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
. By duality we have an isometric isomorphism

\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})^{*}:L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})^{*}\rightarrow L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)^{*}
given by

\big\langle\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{*}u,v\big\rangle=\big\langle u,\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)v\big\rangle,\qquad u\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})^{*},\quad v\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu).

Therefore, by Lemma
4.1
, if
p^{-1}+2q^{-1}=1
, then we have an isometric linear map,

\lambda_{\nu}:L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\longrightarrow\mathscr{L}\big(L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu),L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)^{*}\big),\qquad\lambda_{\nu}(x):=\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{*}\lambda(x)\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big).

More precisely, if
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
u,v\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
, then

(4.26)

\left\langle\lambda_{\nu}(x)u,v\right\rangle=\big\langle\lambda(x)\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)u,\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)v\big\rangle=(2\pi)^{n}\big\langle\lambda(x)\big(\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}u\big),\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}v\big\rangle=(2\pi)^{n}\tau\big[v^{*}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}u\big].

In particular, if
x\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then

\left\langle\lambda_{\nu}(x)u,v\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{n}\tau\left[v^{*}\nu\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)u\right]=\big\langle\lambda\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)u|v\big\rangle_{\nu}.

Moreover, in the same with Lemma
4.4
, by combining this with continuous embeddings provided by Proposition
2.2
and Proposition
2.3
we obtain the following result.

Lemma 4.15
.

Let
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p\geq 1
, and assume that, either
s>n/2p
, or
s=n/2p
and
p>1
. Then
\lambda_{\nu}(x)
uniquely extends to a bounded operator
\lambda_{\nu}(x):W_{2}^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)\rightarrow W_{2}^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta},g,\nu)
, and hence
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}
is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
.

Remark 4.16
.

Let
p
and
s
be as in Lemma
4.15
. The linear map
x\rightarrow\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}
is continuous from
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
to
\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu))
. If
x\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then by (
4.24
) we have

\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}=\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}\lambda\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s/2}
. The density of
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
into
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
then implies that

(4.27)

\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{s}{2}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{s}{2}}=\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{s}{2}}\lambda\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{s}{2}}\qquad\forall x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

For
p>0
we denote by
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}
the weak Schatten class
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu))
. We shall keep using the notation
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
for the weak Schatten class
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
. As
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
and
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
are the same Hilbert space with equivalent sinner products,
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}
and
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
are the same quasi-Banach ideal with equivalent quasi-norms. In fact, as
\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu}):L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is a unitary isomorphism, an isometric isomorphism from
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}
onto
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
is given by the map

T\longrightarrow\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})T\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})^{-1}={\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})T{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})^{-1}
. In particular,

\|T\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}=\big\|{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)T{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{-1}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\qquad\forall T\in\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}.

By Proposition
3.12
the power
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s}
is a
\Psi
DO of order
-2s
. Thus, if we disregard inner products, then by using (
4.25
) and (
4.27
) and Proposition
4.11
we see that
\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2p}
and
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}\lambda(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}
are operators in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
if
x
is in a suitable
L_{q}
-space, and we have explicit upper bounds for their
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
-quasi-norm. For our purpose, we regard these operators as operators on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
, and so we seek for estimates for their
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}
-quasi-norms.

Theorem 4.17

(Curved Cwikel Estimates)
.

The following hold.

(1)

Suppose that
p\neq 2
and
q=\max(p,2)
, or
p=2<q
. If
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2p}
is in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}
, and we have

(4.28)

\left\|\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\leq C_{g,\nu}(p,q)\big\|x\big\|_{L_{q}}.

Moreover, the best constant
C_{g,\nu}(p,q)
satisfies

C_{g,\nu}(p,q)\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1)\|\nu\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\Lambda^{\frac{n}{p}}\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\big\|_{\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu),L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))},

where
c_{n}(p,q)
is the best constant in the estimate (
4.5
).

(2)

Suppose that
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
. If
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then the operator
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}
is in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}
, and we have

(4.29)

\left\|\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}C_{g,\nu}(2p,2q)^{2}\big\|x\big\|_{L_{q}}.

where
C_{g,\nu}(2p,2q)
is the best constant in the estimate (
4.28
) for the pair
(2p,2q)
. Furthermore, the inequality holds without the
2^{1/p}
-factor if
x
is positive.

Proof.

Suppose that
p\neq 2
and
q=\max(p,2)
, or
p=2<q
. The operator
A:=\Lambda^{n/p}\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2p}
is a
\Psi
DO of order
0
, since

\Lambda^{n/p}
and
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2p}
are
\Psi
DOs of order
n/p
and
-n/p
, respectively. Thus,
A
is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. We actually regard it as a bounded operator
A:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Let
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. We already know that
\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2p}
is in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{-1}\lambda(x)\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{-1}\lambda\big(x\sqrt{\nu}\big)\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}A.

As
\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})^{-1}:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
is a unitary isomorphism and
A:L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is bounded, we get

\left\|\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left\|\lambda\big(x\sqrt{\nu}\big)\Lambda^{-\frac{n}{p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}}\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu),L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))}.

Combining this with the estimate (
4.12
) for
P_{\rho}=\Lambda^{-n/p}
gives

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1)\big\|x\sqrt{\nu}\big\|_{L_{q}}\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu),L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))}

\displaystyle\leq(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\big(c_{n}(p,q)+1)\|\nu\|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|x\big\|_{L_{q}}\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu),L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))}.

This proves (
4.28
).

Suppose now that either
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
. Let
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. As in the proof of Proposition
4.11
we may write

x=yz
with
y
and
z
in
L_{2q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
such that
\|y\|_{L_{2q}}=\|z\|_{L_{2q}}=(\|x\|_{L_{q}})^{1/2}
. If
u_{1},v_{1}\in W_{2}^{n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
, then by (
4.26
) we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\lambda_{\nu}(x)u_{1},v_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}\tau\big[v_{1}^{*}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}yz\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{1}\big]

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}\tau\left[\big(\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}y^{*}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}v_{1}\big)^{*}\nu\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}z\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{1}\big)\right]

\displaystyle=\left\langle\lambda_{\nu}(z)u_{1}|\lambda_{\nu}(y^{*})v_{1}\right\rangle_{\nu}.

Combining this with (
3.9
) we then see that, for all
u,v\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
, we have

\big\langle\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}u|v\big\rangle=\big\langle\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}u,\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}v\big\rangle=\big\langle\lambda_{\nu}(z)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}u|\lambda_{\nu}(y^{*})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}v\big\rangle.

This shows that

(4.30)

\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}=\left(\lambda_{\nu}(y^{*})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)^{*}\left(\lambda_{\nu}(z)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right).

We may now proceed as in the proof of Proposition
4.9
. By using (
4.30
) and the Hölder inequality (
4.3
) we get

\left\|\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\leq 2^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\lambda_{\nu}(y^{*})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\left\|\lambda_{\nu}(z)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}.

Thanks to the first part and the equalities
\|y^{*}\|_{L_{2q}}=\|y\|_{L_{2q}}=\|z\|_{L_{2q}}=(\|x\|_{L_{q}})^{1/2}
we get

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda_{\nu}(y^{*})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\leq c_{n}(2p,2q)\big\|y^{*}\big\|_{L_{2q}}=c_{n}(2p,2q)\big(\big\|x\big\|_{L_{2q}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\left\|\lambda_{\nu}(z)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\leq c_{n}(2p,2q)\big\|z\big\|_{L_{2q}}=c_{n}(2p,2q)\big(\big\|x\big\|_{L_{2q}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This gives the estimate (
4.29
).

If
x
is positive, then we may take
y=z=\sqrt{x}
. In this case (
4.30
) becomes

\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}=\left(\lambda_{\nu}(\sqrt{x})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)^{*}\left(\lambda_{\nu}(\sqrt{x})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right),

and so we have

\left\|\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}=\bigg(\left\|\lambda_{\nu}(y)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{2p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\bigg)^{2}

Combining this with the inequality (
4.28
) for
z=\sqrt{x}
gives the inequality (
4.29
) without the
2^{1/p}
-factor. The proof is complete.
∎

5.
Dixmier Trace Formulas

In this section, as applications of the Cwikel estimates of the previous section, we establish
L_{p}
-versions of the analogues for NC tori of Connes’ trace theorem and Connes’ integration formulas established in
[
68
]
.
This will show that a very wide class of operators built out of
\Psi
DOs and
L_{p}
-elements are actually integrable in the sense of NCG.

5.1.
Connes’ integration in noncommutative geometry

One of the main goals of noncommutative geometry
[
15
]
is to translate into the Hilbert space formalism of quantum mechanics the main tools of differential geometry. In this framework the notion of integral corresponds to positive traces on the weak trace class
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
. Such traces are annihilated by finite rank operators (see, e.g.,
[
61
]
) and are always continuous (see, e.g.,
[
68
, Remark 2.3]
), and so they are annihilated by the finite-rank operator closure
(\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty})_{0}
.

There is a whole zoo of positive traces on
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
(see, e.g.,
[
61
,
62
]
and the references therein). One important class of such traces is provided by Dixmier traces
\operatorname{Tr}_{\omega}:\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
(see
[
24
]
; see also
[
15
,
61
,
70
]
). We say that an operator
A\in\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
is
measurable
if the value of
\operatorname{Tr}_{\omega}(A)
is independent of the choice of the Dixmier trace. Equivalently (see
[
61
,
70
]
), the operator
A
is measurable if and only if it satisfies the following Tauberian condition,

(5.1)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int A:=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(N+1)}\sum_{j<N}\lambda_{j}(A)\quad\text{exists}.

Here
(\lambda_{j}(A))_{j\geq 0}
is any eigenvalue sequence of
A
such that

|\lambda_{0}(A)|\geq|\lambda_{1}(A)|\geq\cdots\geq|\lambda_{j}(A)|\geq\cdots\geq 0.

where each eigenvalue is repeating according to its (algebraic) multiplicity. The limit
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int A
is then called the
NC integral
of
A
.

As it turns out there are numerous positive traces on
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
that are not Dixmier traces (see, e.g.,
[
83
]
). Thus, it stands for reason to consider a stronger notion of measurability. We say that an operator
A\in\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
is
strongly measurable
(or
positively measurable
) if all positive normalized traces take on the same value on
A
. Here a trace
\varphi
on
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
is called normalized if

\big(A\geq 0\ \text{and}\ \lambda_{j}(A)=(j+1)^{-1}\big)\ \Longrightarrow\ \varphi(A)=1.

The Dixmier traces are positive normalized traces. Thus, if
A\in\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
is strongly measurable, then
A
is measurable and, for any positive normalized trace
\varphi
on
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
, we have

\varphi(A)=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int A=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\log(N+1)}\sum_{j<N}\lambda_{j}(A).

As mentioned above, any positive trace is continuous and is annihilated by
(\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty})_{0}
. It follows that the strongly measurable operators form a closed subspace of
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
containing
(\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty})_{0}
.

We refer to
[
83
, Section 7]
for a characterization of strongly measurable operators in terms of their eigenvalue sequences and for examples of measurable operators that are not strongly measurable.

It can be shown that every continuous trace on
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
is a linear combination of 4 positive traces (see
[
17
, Corollary 2.2]
). Therefore, in the definition of strong measurability we may substitute continuous (normalized) traces for positive (normalized) traces without altering the class of strongly measurable operators. This implies that strong measurability is insensitive to the choice of the inner product of the background Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
.

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold, and denote by
\Psi^{m}(M)
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the space of (classical)
\Psi
DOs of order
m
on
M
.
Connes’ trace theorem

[
14
]
asserts that every operator
P\in\Psi^{-n}(M)
is measurable, and we have

(5.2)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int P=\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{Res}(P),

where
\operatorname{Res}
is the noncommutative residue trace of Guillemin
[
35
]
and Wodzicki
[
98
]
. That is,

\operatorname{Res}(P)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{S^{*}M}\sigma_{-n}(P)(x,\xi)dxd^{n-1}\xi,

where
S^{*}M
is the unit cosphere bundle and
\sigma_{-n}(P)(x,\xi)
is the principal symbol of
P
. It was further shown by Kalton-Lord-Potapov-Sukochev
[
46
]
that every operator in
\Psi^{-n}(M)
is actually strongly measurable. These results can also be deduced from the Weyl’s law for negative order
\Psi
DOs of Birman-Solomyak
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
(see also Section
7
on this point).

The r.h.s. of (
5.2
) makes sense for any integer order
\Psi
DO, since the noncommutative residue is a trace on the algebra
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(M):=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi^{m}(M)
and is actually the unique trace up to constant multiple if
M
is connected. Therefore, using the r.h.s. allows us to extend the definition of the NC integral to
all
integer order
\Psi
DOs, including
\Psi
DOs that are not even bounded on
L_{2}(M,g)
.

Let
\Delta_{g}
be the Laplacian of
(M^{n},g)
. Specializing the above results to
P=f\Delta_{g}^{-n/2}
and
P=\Delta_{g}^{-n/4}f\Delta_{g}^{-n/4}
with
f\in C^{\infty}(M)

shows that these operators are strongly measurable. Moreover, we get
Connes’ integration formula
,

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\Delta_{g}^{-\frac{n}{4}}f\Delta_{g}^{-\frac{n}{4}}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int f\Delta_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2}}=\hat{c}(n)\int_{M}f(x)\sqrt{g(x)}d^{n}x,

where we have set
\hat{c}(n):=\frac{1}{n}(2\pi)^{-n}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
. This formula shows that the NC integral recaptures the Riemannian volume.

The above formula for
f\Delta_{g}^{-n/2}
actually holds for all
f\in L_{2}(M)
(see
[
46
]
). The formula for
\Delta_{g}^{-n/4}f\Delta_{g}^{-n/4}
further holds for any
f
in the Orlicz space
L\!\log\!L(M)
(see
[
79
,
94
]
; see also
[
71
]
). However, this result does not hold in general for functions in
L_{1}(M)
(see
[
46
]
).

5.2.
Integration formulas on NC tori

If
P\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, has symbol
\rho(\xi)\sim\sum\rho_{m-j}(\xi)
, then its
noncommutative residue
is defined by

\operatorname{Res}(P)=\tau[c_{P}],\qquad\text{where}\ c_{P}:=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{-n}(\xi)d^{n-1}\xi.

Here the integral is meant as a Riemann (surface) integral with values in the locally convex space
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. It can be shown that the noncommutative residue is a trace on the algebra
\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}):=\cup_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Psi^{\mathbb{Z}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and is the only one up to a constant multiple (see
[
69
]
; see also
[
53
]
).

Recall that any
\Psi
DO of order
\leq-n
is in the weak trace-class
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
. We have the following version for NC tori of Connes’s trace theorem.

Proposition 5.1

(see
[
68
]
)
.

If
P\in\Psi^{-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
P
is strongly measurable, and we have

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int P=\frac{1}{n}\operatorname{Res}(P).

More generally, given any
x\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, the operator
\lambda(x)P
is strongly measurable, and we have

(5.3)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int\lambda(x)P=\frac{1}{n}\tau\big[xc_{P}\big].

Recall that by the first part of Proposition
4.11
if
P\in\Psi^{-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
\lambda(x)P
is in
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
. By the 2nd part of the same Proposition, if
P,Q\in\Psi^{-n/2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p>1
, then
Q^{*}\lambda(x)P\in\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
.

The following theorem extends Proposition
5.1
to
L_{p}
-spaces.

Theorem 5.2
.

The following hold.

(1)

Let
P\in\Psi^{-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Then
\lambda(x)P
is strongly measurable, and we have

(5.4)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int\lambda(x)P=\frac{1}{n}\tau\big[xc_{P}\big].

(2)

Let
P,Q\in\Psi^{-n/2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p>1
. Then
Q^{*}\lambda(x)P
is
strongly measurable, and we have

(5.5)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int Q^{*}\lambda(x)P=\frac{1}{n}\tau\big[xc_{PQ^{*}}\big].

Proof.

Let
P\in\Psi^{-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Set
\Lambda(P)=\{\lambda(x)P;\ x\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\}
and
\Lambda_{2}(P)=\{\lambda(x)P;\ x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\}
. The first part of Proposition
4.11
and the density of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
ensure that
\Lambda_{2}(P)
is a subspace of
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
in which
\Lambda(P)
is a dense subspace. Proposition
5.1
asserts that
\Lambda(P)
is contained in the space of strongly measurable operators. As the latter is a closed subspace of
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
it follows that every operator in
\Lambda_{2}(P)
is strongly measurable.

By Proposition
5.1
the formula (
5.4
) holds for all
x\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. The r.h.s. of (
5.4
) is a continuous linear form on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. The l.h.s. is a continuous linear form on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
as well thanks to the 1st part of Proposition
5.1
and the continuity of the NC integral
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int
on the subspace (strongly) measurable operators. Combining this with the density of
C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
we then deduce that the formula (
5.4
) holds true for any
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. This proves the first part.

It remains to prove the 2nd part. Let
P,Q\in\Psi^{-n/2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Set

\Lambda^{\infty}(P,Q)=\{Q^{*}\lambda(x)P;\ x\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\}
and
\Lambda_{p}(P,Q)=\{Q^{*}\lambda(x)P;\ x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})\}
,
p>1
. As above, the 2nd part of Proposition
4.11
and the density of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
in
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
imply that
\Lambda_{p}(P,Q)
is a subspace of
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
that contains
\Lambda^{\infty}(P,Q)
as a dense subspace. Note that
\Lambda^{\infty}(P,Q)
is a subspace of
\Psi^{-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and so by Proposition
5.1
every operator in
\Lambda^{\infty}(P,Q)
is strongly measurable. As above, the fact that the strongly measurable operators form a closed subspace of
\mathscr{L}_{1,\infty}
then ensures that every operator in
\Lambda_{p}(P,Q)
is strongly measurable.

If
x\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
Q^{*}\lambda(x)P
is an operator in
\Psi^{-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and so by (
5.3
) we have

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int Q^{*}\lambda(x)P=\frac{1}{n}\tau\big[c_{Q^{*}xP}\big].

Let
\rho_{-n/2}(\xi)
and
\sigma_{-n/2}(\xi)
be the respective principal symbols of
P
and
Q
. The principal symbols of
Q^{*}P
and
Q^{*}xP
are
\sigma_{-n/2}(\xi)^{*}\rho_{-n/2}(\xi)
and
\sigma_{-n/2}(\xi)^{*}x\rho_{-n/2}(\xi)
, respectively. Thus,

\displaystyle\tau\big[c_{Q^{*}xP}\big]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\big[\sigma_{-\frac{n}{2}}(\xi)^{*}x\rho_{-\frac{n}{2}}(\xi)\big]d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\big[x\rho_{-\frac{n}{2}}(\xi)\sigma_{-\frac{n}{2}}(\xi)^{*}\big]d\xi

\displaystyle=\tau\bigg[x\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\rho_{-\frac{n}{2}}(\xi)\sigma_{-\frac{n}{2}}(\xi)^{*}d\xi\bigg]=\tau\big[xc_{PQ^{*}}\big].

This proves the equality (
5.5
) for
x\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. As with (
5.4
), the r.h.s. of (
5.5
) is a continuous linear form on
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. The 2nd part of Proposition
4.11
and the continuity of
\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int
ensure that the l.h.s. is a continuous linear form on
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
as well. The density of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
in
L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
then implies that both sides agree for all
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. This gives the 2nd part. The proof is complete.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The following hold.

(1)

If
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then the operator
\lambda(x)\Delta^{-n}
is strongly measurable, and we have

(5.6)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int\lambda(x)\Delta^{-\frac{n}{2}}=c(n)\tau\big[x\big],\qquad c(n):=\frac{1}{n}\big|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\big|.

(2)

If
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p>1
, then the operator
\Delta^{-n/4}\lambda(x)\Delta^{-n/4}
is strongly measurable, and we have

(5.7)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int\Delta^{-\frac{n}{4}}\lambda(x)\Delta^{-\frac{n}{4}}=c(n)\tau\big[x\big].

Proof.

We only need to compute
c_{\Delta^{-n/2}}
. Note that
\Delta^{-n/2}
is an operator in
\Psi^{-n}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
whose principal symbol is
|\xi|^{-n}
. This can be seen as a special case of Proposition
3.12
. A simpler way is to observe that (
2.13
) implies that
\Delta^{-n/2}=P_{\rho}
, where
\rho(\xi)
is any smooth function such that
\rho(\xi)=|\xi|^{-n}
for
|\xi|\geq 1
and
\rho(0)=0
. In any case, we get

(5.8)

c_{\Delta^{-\frac{n}{2}}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\xi|^{-n}d^{n-1}\xi=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}d^{n-1}\xi=\big|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\big|.

Therefore, applying the first part of Theorem
5.2
to
P=\Delta^{-n/2}
gives the first part. Applying the 2nd part of
Theorem
5.2
to
P=Q=\Delta^{-n/4}
gives the 2nd part. The proof is complete.
∎

5.3.
Curved Integration Formulas

Let
g=(g_{ij})
be a Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
and let
\nu\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
be a smooth positive density.

For ordinary Riemmanian manifolds the Riemannian density
\nu(g)=\sqrt{g}
naturally appears in Connes’s integration formula (
1
). As shown in
[
68
]
for curved NC tori what comes into play is

(5.9)

\tilde{\nu}(g):=\frac{1}{|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\xi|_{g}^{-n}d^{n-1}\xi,

where
|\xi|_{g}
is defined in (
3.7
).
It follows from the inequalities (
3.8
) that this defines a positive invertible element of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, i.e., a smooth positive density (see
[
68
]
). We call
\tilde{\nu}(g)
the
spectral Riemannian density
of
g
. We refer to
[
68
]
for a more detailed account on spectral Riemannian densities.

Example 5.5

(see
[
68
]
)
.

Assume that
g=(g_{ij})
is self-compatible in the sense mentioned in Example
3.5
. Then, we have

(5.10)

\tilde{\nu}(g)=\nu(g)=\sqrt{\det(g)},

where
\det(g)
is given by (
3.3
). In particular, if
g_{0}=k^{2}g
,
k\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
, is a conformally flat metric, then
\tilde{\nu}(g)=\nu(g)=k^{n}
.

Example 5.6
.

Suppose that
n=2
, and let
g
be of the form

g=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&b\end{pmatrix},\qquad a,b\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}\left(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\theta})\right).

We have

g=a^{\frac{1}{2}}\hat{g}a^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\text{where}\ \hat{g}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&a^{-\frac{1}{2}}ba^{-\frac{1}{2}}\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\hat{g}
is a self-compatible Riemannian metric. Therefore, by (
3.3
) and (
5.10
) we have

\det(\hat{g})=a^{-\frac{1}{2}}ba^{-\frac{1}{2}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{\nu}(\hat{g})=\sqrt{\det(\hat{g})}=\big(a^{-\frac{1}{2}}ba^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Furthermore, as
g^{-1}=a^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{g}^{-1}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}
, we have

|\xi|_{g}^{2}=\sum g^{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}=\sum a^{-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{g}^{ij}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}=a^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|_{\hat{g}}^{2}a^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, by the very definition (
5.9
) of
\tilde{\nu}(g)
we get

\tilde{\nu}(g)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\xi|_{g}^{-2}d\xi=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}a^{\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|_{\hat{g}}^{-2}a^{\frac{1}{2}}d\xi=a^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\nu}(\hat{g})a^{\frac{1}{2}}=a^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(a^{-\frac{1}{2}}ba^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}a^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This should be compared with
\nu(g)
, which in this case is
\exp(\log(a)+\log(b)).

In particular, if
[a,b]=0
we recover the formula
\tilde{\nu}(g)=\sqrt{ab}
predicted by (
5.10
). Note also that
a^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(a^{-\frac{1}{2}}ba^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}a^{\frac{1}{2}}
is the geometric mean of
a
and
b
in the sense of Pusz-Woronowicz
[
73
]
.

The integration formula for curved NC tori of
[
68
]
then can be stated as follows.

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}=c(n)\tau\big[x\tilde{\nu}(g)\big],\qquad c(n):=\frac{1}{n}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|.

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}=c(n)\tau\big[x\sqrt{\det(g)}\big],\qquad x\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Theorem 5.11
.

The following hold.

(1)

If
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
, then
\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}
is strongly measurable, and we have

(5.13)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}=c(n)\tau\big[x\tilde{\nu}(g)\big],\qquad c(n):=\frac{1}{n}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|.

(2)

If
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
,
p>1
, then
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}
is strongly measurable, and we have

(5.14)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}=c(n)\tau\big[x\tilde{\nu}(g)\big].

Proof.

As mentioned in Section
5.1
, strong measurability and the NC integral do not depend on the choice of the inner product. As
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
are the same Hilbert space with equivalent inner products, we may regard
\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}
and
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}
as operators in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

Let
x\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. By (
4.25
) we have
\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2}=\lambda(\nu^{-1/2}x\nu^{1/2})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2}
. Proposition
3.12
asserts that

\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2}
is an operator in
\Psi^{-n}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
. Therefore, by Proposition
5.2
the operator
\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2}
is strongly measurable, and we have

(5.15)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\lambda\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}=\frac{1}{n}\tau\left[(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}})c_{\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}}\right].

It remains to compute
c_{\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2}}
. We know by Proposition
3.12
that
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2}
has principal symbol
\nu^{-1/2}|\xi|_{g}\nu^{1/2}
. Thus,

c_{\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}|\xi|_{g}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}d^{n-1}\xi=\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\xi|_{g}d^{n-1}\xi\bigg)\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}=|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\nu}(g)\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Therefore, if we set
c(n)=n^{-1}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|
, then we get

(5.16)

\frac{1}{n}\tau\left[(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}})c_{\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}}\right]=c(n)\tau\left[\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\big(\nu^{-1/2}\tilde{\nu}(g)\nu^{1/2}\big)\right]=c(n)\tau\big[x\tilde{\nu}(g)\big].

Combining this with (
5.15
) gives (
5.13
).

Let
x\in L_{p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
p>1
. By (
4.27
) we have

\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}=\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}\lambda(\nu^{-1/2}x\nu^{1/2})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}
. We know by Proposition
3.12
that
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}\in\Psi^{-n/2}(\mathbb{T}_{\theta}^{n})
. Proposition
5.2
then ensures that
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}
is a strongly measurable operator, and we have

(5.17)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}\lambda\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4}}=\frac{1}{n}\tau\left[(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}})c_{\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{2}}}\right].

Note (
5.16
) holds for any

, and hence it holds for any

,

6.1.
CLR inequality for curved NC tori (1st version)

Suppose that
p>1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
, and let
V^{*}=V\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Lemma
4.15
ensures that
\lambda_{\nu}(V)
induces a bounded operator
\lambda_{\nu}(V):W_{2}^{-n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)\rightarrow W_{2}^{n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
. Thus, we get a quadratic form on
W_{2}^{n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
given by

Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}(u,v)=\left\langle\lambda_{\nu}(V)u,v\right\rangle,\qquad u,v\in W_{2}^{\frac{n}{2p}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Note that by (
4.26
) we have

(6.1)

Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}(u,v)=(2\pi)^{n}\tau\big[v^{*}\nu^{1/2}V\nu^{1/2}u\big],\qquad u,v\in W_{2}^{\frac{n}{2p}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

In particular,
Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}
is symmetric and
Q_{V}\geq 0
if
V\geq 0
.
More generally, we have

(6.2)

V_{1}\leq V_{2}\Longrightarrow\lambda_{\nu}(V_{1})\leq\lambda_{\nu}(V_{2}).

Our main focus is on the fractional Schrödinger operators,

H_{V}^{(p)}:=\Delta_{g,\nu}^{\frac{n}{2p}}+\lambda_{\nu}(V),\qquad p>0.

Note that
Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}
has same domain as the quadratic form of the operator
H_{0}^{(p)}=\Delta_{g,\nu}^{n/2p}
, i.e.,

Q_{H_{0}^{(p)}}(u,v)=\big\langle\Delta_{g,\nu}^{\frac{n}{4p}}u|\Delta_{g,\nu}^{\frac{n}{4p}}v\big\rangle=\big\langle\Delta_{g,\nu}^{\frac{n}{2p}}u,v\big\rangle,\qquad u,v\in W_{2}^{\frac{n}{2p}}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu),

where we regard
\Delta^{n/2p}
as an operator from
W_{2}^{-n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
to
W_{2}^{n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
. In particular,
H_{V}^{(p)}
makes sense as an operator from
W_{2}^{-n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
to
W_{2}^{n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
.

We have

\big(1+H_{0}^{(p)}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{\nu}(V)\big(1+H_{0}^{(p)}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=A\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(V)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}A,

where
A:=(1+H_{0}^{(p)})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{g,\nu}^{n/4p}=(\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/2p}+1)^{-1/2}
is a bounded operator on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
. Therefore, it follows from Theorem
4.17
that
(1+H_{0}^{(p)})^{-\frac{1}{2}}\lambda_{\nu}(V)(1+H_{0}^{(p)})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is in the weak Schatten class
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}
, and hence is compact. That is, the symmetric quadratic form
Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}
is
H^{(p)}_{0}
-form compact, and so it is
H^{(p)}_{0}
-form bounded with zero
H
-bound (see
[
87
, §7.8]
). Thus, by the KLMN theorem (see, e.g.,
[
74
,
81
]
) the restriction of
H_{V}^{(p)}
to
W_{2}^{n/p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
is a bounded from below selfadjoint operator on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
whose quadratic form is precisely
Q_{H_{0}^{(p)}}+Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}
.

As
H_{0}^{(p)}
has compact resolvent,
H^{(p)}_{V}
has compact resolvent as well, and hence it has pure discrete spectrum (see, e.g.,
[
87
, Theorem 7.8.4]
). We denote by
-\lambda_{j}^{-}(H^{(p)}_{V})
the
negative
eigenvalues of
H_{V}^{(p)}
in such a way that

\lambda_{0}^{-}\big(H_{V}^{(p)}\big)\geq\lambda_{1}^{-}\big(H_{V}^{(p)}\big)\geq\cdots>0,

where each eigenvalue is repeated according to multiplicity. We then introduce the counting function,

(6.3)

N^{-}\big(H_{V}^{(p)};\lambda\big):=\#\left\{j;\ \lambda_{j}^{-}\big(H_{V}^{(p)}\big)<\lambda\right\},\qquad\lambda\geq 0.

For
\lambda=0
we set
N^{-}(H_{V}^{(p)})=N^{-}(H_{V}^{(p)};0)
. Moreover, by Glazman’s lemma (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Theorem 10.2.3]
) we have

(6.4)

N^{-}(A;\lambda)=\max\left\{\dim F;\ F\in\mathscr{F}^{-}\big(H_{V}^{(p)};\lambda\big)\right\},

where
\mathscr{F}^{-}(H_{V}^{(p)};\lambda)
is the family of subspaces
F\subset W_{2}^{n/p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
such that
Q_{H_{V}^{(p)}}(u,u)<-\lambda\|u\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)}
for all
u\in F\setminus 0
. Combining this with (
6.2
) yields the monotonicity principle,

(6.5)

V_{1}\leq V_{2}\Longrightarrow N^{-}\big(H_{V_{2}}^{(p)};\lambda\big)\leq N^{-}\big(H_{V_{1}}^{(p)};\lambda\big)\quad\forall\lambda\geq 0.

In what follows, we let
V_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(|V|\pm V)
be the positive and negative parts of
V
, so that
V=V_{+}-V_{-}

Theorem 6.1

(CLR Inequality for Curved NC Tori; 1st Version)
.

Suppose that
q=\max(p,1)
if
p\neq 1
, or
p=1<q
. If
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then

(6.6)

N^{-}\left(H_{V}^{(p)}\right)-1\leq C_{g,\nu}(2p,2q)^{2p}\big(\big\|V_{-}\big\|_{L_{q}}\big)^{p},

where
C_{g,\nu}(2p,2q)
is the best constant in the inequality (
4.28
) for the pair
(2p,2q)
.

Proof.

The proof goes along merely the same lines as that of the proof of
[
63
, Theorem 8.1]
. As
V=V_{+}-V_{-}\geq-V_{-}
, the monotonicity principle (
6.5
) implies that

(6.7)

N^{-}\big(H_{V}^{(p)}\big)\leq N^{-}\big(H_{-V_{-}}^{(p)}\big).

Let
\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}
the orthogonal projection onto
\ker\Delta_{g,\nu}=\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
. That is,

\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}=\left\langle u|1\right\rangle_{\nu}1=\tau(u\nu)1,\qquad u\in L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu).

As
-V_{-}\leq 0
the Birman-Schwinger principle in the version provided by
[
63
, Theorem 7.9]
gives

N^{-}\big(H_{-V_{-}}^{(p)};\lambda\big)-N^{+}\big(\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}V_{-}\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}\big)\leq\left(\left\|\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(V)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\right)^{p},

where
N^{+}(\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}V_{-}\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)})
is the number of positive eigenvalues of
\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}V_{-}\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}
. As

\operatorname{rk}(\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}V_{-}\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)})\leq\operatorname{rk}(\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)})=1
, we have
N^{+}(\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}V_{-}\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)})\leq 1
. Thus,

N^{-}\big(H_{V}^{(p)}\big)-1\leq N^{-}\big(H_{-V_{-}}^{(p)};\lambda\big)-N^{+}\big(\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}V_{-}\Pi_{0}^{(\nu)}\big)\leq\left(\left\|\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(V_{-})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\right)^{p}.

Combining this with the Cwikel-type estimate (
4.29
) immediately gives the result.
∎

Remark 6.2
.

We have
Q_{H^{(p)}_{V}}(1,1)=\left\langle 1,\lambda_{\nu}(V)1\right\rangle=\hat{\tau}_{\nu}(V)
. Thus, if
\hat{\tau}_{\nu}(V)=(2\pi)^{n}\tau[V\nu]<0
, then the variational principle (
6.4
) implies that, in this case,
N^{-}(H_{V}^{(p)})
is always
\geq 1
.

6.2.
CLR inequality on curved NC tori (2nd version)

As
\Delta_{g,\nu}
has a non-trivial nullspace it is natural to restrict ourself to the orthogonal complement of
\ker\Delta_{g,\nu}=\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
, i.e., the subspace,

\dot{L}_{2}\big(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu\big):=\left\{u\in L_{2}\big(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu\big);\ \left\langle u|1\right\rangle_{\nu}=\tau[u\nu]=1\right\}.

Thus, we may regard
\dot{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
as the space of elements of
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
that have zero mean value with respect to
\nu
. For
s\geq 0
, we also set

\dot{W}^{s}_{2}\big(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu\big):=W^{s}_{2}\big(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu\big)\cap\dot{L}_{2}\big(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu\big).

In addition, we denote by
\dot{W}^{-s}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
the anti-linear dual of
\dot{W}^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
.

Let
\dot{\Delta}_{\nu,g}
be the compression of
\Delta_{g,\nu}
to
\dot{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Its domain is

\dot{W}^{s}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
, and it has same spectrum and eigenspaces as
\Delta_{g,\nu}
except for the eigenvalue
\lambda=0
.

Suppose that
p\neq 1
and
q=\max(p,1)
, or
p=1<q
, and let
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. Let
\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V):\dot{W}^{-n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)\rightarrow\dot{W}^{n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
be the compression of
\lambda_{\nu}(V)
to
\dot{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
. More precisely, we mean the operator given by

\big\langle\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V)u,v\big\rangle=Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}(u,v)=\left\langle\lambda_{\nu}(V)u,v\right\rangle,\qquad u,v\in\dot{W}^{\frac{n}{2p}}_{2}\big(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu\big).

That is, the quadratic form
Q_{\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V)}
is the restriction of
Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}
to
\dot{W}_{2}^{n/2p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
. In particular, the monotonicity principle (
6.5
) holds
verbatim
for the operators
\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V)
.

With respect to the orthogonal splitting
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)=\dot{L}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\oplus\mathbb{C}\cdot 1
we have

(6.8)

\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(V)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}=\begin{pmatrix}\dot{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V)\dot{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

In particular, we see that
\dot{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-{n}/{4p}}\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V)\dot{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-{n}/{4p}}
is in the weak Schatten class
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}(\dot{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu))
and has same
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
-quasi-norm as
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-{n}/{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(V)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-{n}/{4p}}
.
Thus, in the same way as with
H^{(p)}_{V}
the operator
\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}:=\dot{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{n/2p}+\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V)
restricts on

\dot{W}_{2}^{n/p}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
to a selfadjoint operator on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
which is bounded from below and has compact resolvent. We define its counting function as in (
6.3
). The monotonicity principle (
6.5
) holds
verbatim
for the operators
\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}
.

Theorem 6.3

(CLR Inequality on Curved NC Tori; 2nd Version)
.

Suppose that
p\neq 1
and
q=\max\{p,1\}
, or
p=1<q
. If
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then

(6.9)

N^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}\big)\leq C_{g,\nu}(2p,2q)^{2p}\big(\big\|V_{-}\big\|_{L_{q}}\big)^{p},

where
C_{g,\nu}(2p,2q)
is the best constant in the inequality (
4.28
) for the pair
(2p,2q)
.

Proof.

The proof follows along similar lines as that of Theorem
6.1
. In the same way as in (
6.7
) we have
N^{-}(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)})\leq N^{-}(\dot{H}_{-V_{-}}^{(p)})
. As
-V_{-}\leq 0
and
0
is not in the spectrum of
\dot{H}_{0}^{(p)}=\dot{\Delta}^{n/2p}_{g,\nu}
, the abstract Birman-Schwinger principle in the version of
[
10
]
(see also
[
63
,
87
]
) implies that

N^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}\big)\leq\left(\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\dot{\lambda}_{\nu}(V_{-})\dot{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}(\dot{L}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu))}\right)^{p}=\left(\left\|\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(V_{-})\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right\|_{\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}^{(\nu)}}\right)^{p}.

Combining this with the Cwikel-type estimate (
4.29
) immediately gives the result.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

We can recover from (
6.9
) the other CLR inequality (
6.6
), since, in the same way as in
[
63
, Remark 8.13]
, it can be shown that
N^{-}(H_{V}^{(p)})\leq N^{-}(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)})+1
.

6.3.
Lieb-Thirring inequalities

The Lieb-Thirring inequalities extend the CLR inequality (
6.9
) to
\gamma
-moments, i.e.,

\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}\big)^{\gamma},\qquad\gamma\geq 0.

In particular, for
\gamma=0
we recover the number of negative eigenvalues
N^{-}(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)})
.

Theorem 6.5

(Lieb-Thirring Inequalities on Curved NC Tori)
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
p>1
. If
V=V^{*}\in L_{p+\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then

(6.10)

\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}\big)^{\gamma}\leq L_{g,\nu}(p,\gamma)\tau\big[(V_{-})^{p+\gamma}\big],

where the best constant
L_{g,\nu}(p,\gamma)
satisfies

L_{g,\nu}(p,\gamma)\leq\gamma\frac{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(p+\gamma+1)}C_{g,\nu}(2p,2p)^{2p}.

Here
C_{g,\nu}(2p,2p)
is the best constant for the inequality (
4.28
) for the pair
(2p,2p)
.

Proof.

The proof goes along merely the same lines as that of the proof of
[
63
, Theorem 8.14]
. We only highlight the main steps. We have the standard formula,

\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}\big)^{\gamma}=\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\gamma-1}N^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)};t\big)dt.

For
t\geq 0
, the CLR inequality (
6.3
) gives

N^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)};t\big)=N^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V+t}^{(p)}\big)\leq C_{g,\nu}(2p,2p)^{2p}\tau\big[(V+t)_{-}^{p}\big].

Thus,

\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}^{(p)}\big)^{\gamma}\leq C_{g,\nu}(2p,2p)^{2p}\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\gamma-1}\tau\big[(V+t)_{-}^{p}\big]\,dt.

It is shown in the proof of
[
63
, Theorem 8.14]
that

\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\gamma-1}\tau\big[(V+t)_{-}^{p}\big]\,dt=\frac{\Gamma(p+1)\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(p+\gamma+1)}\tau\big[(V_{-})^{p+\gamma}\big].

This gives the result.
∎

Remark 6.6
.

By proceedings along the lines of
[
40
]
we can further get Lieb-Thirring inequalities for
p=1
with even better constants. See
[
63
, Remark 18.7]
for the discussion on this in the flat case.

6.4.
A Sobolev inequality

The formula (
3.4
) defines an inner product
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{g,\nu}
on the
\mathbb{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
-module of 1-forms
\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. We denote
\|\cdot\|_{g,\nu}
the corresponding norm. In particular, for all
u\in\mathbb{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

\big\|du\|^{2}_{g,\nu}=\left\langle du,du\right\rangle_{g,\nu}=\sum_{i,j}\tau\left[\partial_{i}(u)^{*}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}g^{ij}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{j}(u)\right].

Note that the above formula continues to make sense for all
u\in W_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
.

On
\mathbb{R}^{n}
the Lieb-Thirring inequality for
\gamma=1
and
p=n/2
is equivalent to a Sobolev inequality for orthonormal families (see
[
58
, Theorem 4]
; see
also
[
31
,
86
]
). Likewise, as a consequence of the above Lieb-Thirring inequalities we shall obtain the following Sobolev’s inequality.

Theorem 6.7

(Sobolev Inequality on Curved NC Tori)
.

Assume
n\geq 3
. For any family
\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{N}\}
in
\dot{W}_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
which is orthonormal in
\dot{L}_{2}s(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
, we have

(6.11)

\sum_{\ell\leq N}\big\|du_{\ell}\|^{2}_{g,\nu}\geq K_{g,\nu}\tau\left[\rho^{\frac{n+2}{n}}\right],\qquad\text{where}\ \rho:=(2\pi)^{n}\sum_{\ell\leq N}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{*}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}

In particular, if we set
q=2(n+2)n^{-1}
, then for
N=0
we get

(6.12)

\big\|du\|^{2}_{g,\nu}\geq K_{g,\nu}\big(\|u\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\nu)}\big)^{q}\qquad\forall u\in\dot{W}_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

Moreover, the best constant
K_{g,\nu}
is related to the best Lieb-Thirring constant
L_{g,\nu}=L_{g,\nu}(n/2,1)
by

(6.13)

K_{g,\nu}=\frac{n}{n+2}\bigg(\frac{n+2}{2}L_{g,\nu}\bigg)^{-\frac{2}{n}}.

Proof.

The proof goes along similar lines as that of the proof of
[
63
, Theorem 8.19]
. For any
V\in L_{n/2+1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, set
H_{V}:=H_{V}^{(n/2)}=\Delta_{g,\nu}+\lambda_{\nu}(V)
and
\dot{H}_{V}:=\dot{H}_{V}^{(n/2)}
. By the very definition (
3.5
) of
\Delta_{g,\nu}
we have

Q_{\Delta_{g,\nu}}(u,u)=\|du\|_{g,\nu}^{2}\qquad\forall u\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}).

This formula continues to hold for all
u\in W_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
. Moreover, by (
6.1
) we have

Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}(u,u)=\left\langle\lambda_{\nu}(V)u,u\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{n}\tau\big[u^{*}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}V\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}u\big]=(2\pi)^{n}\tau\big[\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}uu^{*}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}V\big].

Assume
V\geq 0
, and let
\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{N}\}
be any family in
\dot{W}_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
which is orthonormal in
\dot{L}_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\leq N}Q_{\dot{H}_{-V}}(u_{\ell},u_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\leq N}Q_{{H}_{-V}}(u_{\ell},u_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\leq N}Q_{\Delta_{g,\nu}}(u_{\ell},u_{\ell})-\sum_{\ell\leq N}Q_{\lambda_{\nu}(V)}(u_{\ell},u_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\leq N}\|du_{\ell}\|_{g,\nu}^{2}-\sum_{\ell\leq N}(2\pi)^{n}\tau\big[\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}u_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{*}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}V\big]

(6.14)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\leq N}\|du_{\ell}\|_{g,\nu}^{2}-\tau\big[\rho V\big],

where we have set
\rho:=(2\pi)^{n}\sum\nu^{1/2}u_{\ell}u_{\ell}^{*}\nu^{1/2}
. Moreover, as
\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{N}\}
is an orthonormal family, in the same way as in
the proof of
[
63
, Theorem 8.19]
(and in various other proofs of the Sobolev inequality), the variational principal for negative eigenvalues gives

(6.15)

\sum_{\ell\leq N}Q_{\dot{H}_{-V}}(u_{\ell},u_{\ell})\geq-\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{-V}\big).

As
-V\leq 0
, the Lieb-Thirring inequality (
6.10
) for
\gamma=1
and
p=n/2
delivers

\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{-V}\big)\leq L_{g,\nu}\tau\big[V^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\big].

Combining this with (
6.14
) and (
6.15
) leads to the inequality

\sum_{\ell\leq N}\|du_{\ell}\|_{g,\nu}^{2}\geq\tau\big[\rho V-L_{g,\nu}V^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\big].

The above inequality is true for all
0\leq V\in L_{\frac{n}{2}+1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. In the same way as in the proof of
[
63
, Theorem 8.19]
if we take

V=(\frac{1}{2}(n+2)L_{g,\nu})^{-2/n}\rho^{2/n}
, then
V\in L_{\frac{n}{2}+1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, and we have

\rho V-L_{g,\nu}V^{\frac{n}{2}+1}=\frac{n}{n+2}(\frac{1}{2}(n+2)L_{g,\nu})^{-2/n}\rho^{(n+2)/n}
. This gives the inequality (
6.11
) with a best constant
K_{g,\nu}
such that

(6.16)

K_{g,\nu}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}\bigg(\frac{n+2}{2}L_{g,\nu}\bigg)^{-\frac{2}{n}}.

Note that
\rho=\sum(\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})u_{\ell})(\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})u_{\ell})^{*}=\sum|(\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})u_{\ell})^{*}|^{2}
. Thus, if we set
q=2(n+2)n^{-1}
, then for
N=0
the inequality (
6.11
) means that, for all
u\in\dot{W}_{2}^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};g,\nu)
, we have

\|du\|^{2}_{g,\nu}\geq K_{g,\nu}\tau\left[\big|(\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})u)^{*}\big|^{q}\right].

Observe that

(6.17)

\tau\left[\big|(\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})u)^{*}\big|^{q}\right]=\big\|(\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})u)^{*}\big\|_{L_{q}}^{q}=\big\|\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})u\big\|_{L_{q}}^{q}=\big(\big\|u\big\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)}\big)^{q}.

Therefore, we obtain (
6.12
).

Conversely, let
V=V^{*}\in L_{n/2+1}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
and assume that
N^{-}(\dot{H}_{V})\neq 0
(otherwise the LT inequalities (
6.10
) are trivially satisfied).
Set
N=N^{-}(\dot{H}_{V})-1
, and let
\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{N}\}
be an orthonormal family of eigenvectors in
\dot{W}_{2}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})

such that
\dot{H}_{V}u_{j}=-\lambda_{j}^{-}(H_{V})u_{j}
for
j\leq N
. In particular, we have

\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}\big)=-\sum_{j\leq N}Q_{\dot{H}_{V}}(u_{j},u_{j})\leq-\sum_{j\leq N}Q_{\dot{H}_{-V_{-}}}(u_{j},u_{j}).

Combining this with (
6.14
) then gives

\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}\big)\leq-\sum_{j\leq N}\|du_{j}\|_{g,\nu}^{2}+\tau\big[\rho V_{-}\big].

Set
q=1+n/2
and
r=1+2/n
. Note that
q^{-1}+r^{-1}=1
. The Sobolev inequality (
6.11
) delivers

\sum_{j\leq N}\|du_{j}\|_{g,\nu}^{2}\geq K_{g,\nu}\tau\big[\rho^{r}\big].

Moreover, in the same way as in the proof of
[
63
, Theorem 8.19]
it can be shown that, for any
\epsilon>0
, we have

\tau\big[\rho V_{-}\big]\leq\frac{1}{q}\epsilon^{q}\tau\big[V_{-}^{q}\big]+\frac{1}{r}\epsilon^{-r}\tau\big[\rho^{r}\big].

Thus,

(6.18)

\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}\big)\leq\left(\frac{1}{r}\epsilon^{-r}-K_{g,\nu}\right)\tau\big[\rho^{r}\big]+\frac{1}{q}\epsilon^{q}\tau\big[V_{-}^{q}\big].

In the same way as in the proof of
[
63
, Theorem 8.19]
, for
\epsilon=(rK_{g,\nu})^{-1/r}
the first summand in the r.h.s. of (
6.18
) vanishes. In this case

q^{-1}\epsilon^{q}=\frac{2}{n+2}(\frac{n}{n+2}K_{g,\nu})^{-n/2}
, and so we get

\sum_{j\geq 0}\lambda_{j}^{-}\big(\dot{H}_{V}\big)\leq\frac{2}{n+2}\left(\frac{n}{n+2}K_{g,\nu}\right)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\!\!\!\cdot\tau\big[V_{-}^{q}\big].

This recovers the Lieb-Thirring inequality (
6.10
) for
\gamma=1
and
p=n/2
and shows that (
6.16
) is actually an equality. This completes the proof of (
6.13
).
∎

7.
Spectral Asymptotics

In this section, as a further application of the Cwikel-type estimates of this paper, we explain how these estimate lead to spectral asymptotics for Birman-Schwinger-type operators and fractional Schrödinger operators associated with powers of Laplace-Beltrami operator and
L_{p}
-potentials. In particular, this supersedes a conjecture in the prequel paper
[
63
]
.

In what follows, if
A
a selfadjoint compact operator we denote by
\pm\lambda_{j}^{\pm}(A)
its positive/negative eigenvalues in such a way that

\lambda_{0}^{\pm}(A)\geq\lambda_{1}^{\pm}(A)\geq\lambda_{2}^{\pm}(A)\geq\cdots>0,

where each eigenvalue is repeated according to multiplicity. In the 70s Birman-Solomyak
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
established a very general Weyl’s law for negative order
\Psi
DOs on closed manifolds and Euclidean spaces. By the Birman-Schwinger principle these asymptotics imply a semiclassical Weyl’s laws for fractional Schrödinger operators. They also imply a stronger form of Connes’ trace theorem (
5.2
). A “soft proof” of Birman-Solomyak’s Weyl’s law on closed manifolds is given in
[
70
]
.

We conjecture the following analogue for NC tori of Birman-Solomyak’s Weyl’s law.

Conjecture 7.1
.

Let
P=P^{*}\in\Psi^{-m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
,
m>0
, have principal symbol
\rho_{-m}(\xi)
, and set
p=nm^{-1}
. Then

(7.1)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{\pm}(P)=\left(\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\big(\rho_{-m}(\xi)_{\pm}\big)^{p}\right]d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In fact, Weyl’s laws for positive/negative eigenvalues for (selfadjoint) elliptic
\Psi
DOs are established in the forthcoming manuscript
[
49
]
. As a result,
the spectral asymptotics (
7.1
) hold if
P
is elliptic. We then can expect to extend them to general
\Psi
DOs by using the same kind of considerations as in
[
70
]
. However, as
[
49
]
is still in its final stage of preparation, we prefer to leave the above statement as a conjecture.

Corollary 7.2
.

Assume Conjecture
7.1
holds. Suppose that
p\neq 1
and
q=\max\{p,1\}
, or
p=1<q
, and set
m=n/2p
. Let
P\in\Psi^{m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
have principal symbol
\rho_{-m}(\xi)
. Then, for every
x=x^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{\pm}\big(P^{*}\lambda(x)P\big)=\left(\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\big[\left(\rho_{-m}(\xi)^{*}x\rho_{-m}(\xi)\right)_{\pm}^{p}\big]d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

For
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})_{\textup{sa}}:=\{x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta});\ x^{*}=x\}
set

\omega_{\pm}(x)=\left(\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\big[\left(\rho_{-m}(\xi)^{*}x\rho_{-m}(\xi)\right)_{\pm}^{p}\big]d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that
(x,\xi)\rightarrow\tau[(\rho_{-m}(\xi)^{*}x\rho_{-m}(\xi))_{\pm}^{p}]
are continuous functions on
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})_{\textup{sa}}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, because
(x,\xi)\rightarrow\rho_{-m}(\xi)^{*}x\rho_{-m}(\xi)
is a continuous map from
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})_{\textup{sa}}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
to
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})_{\textup{sa}}
and the functions
y\rightarrow\tau[y_{\pm}^{p}]=2^{-p}(\||y|\pm y\|_{L_{p}})^{p}
are continuous on
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})_{\textup{sa}}
(since
q\geq p
). It then follows that
\omega_{\pm}(x)
is a continuous function on
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})_{\textup{sa}}
.

If
x=x^{*}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
P^{*}\lambda(x)P
is a selfadjoint operator in
\Psi^{2m}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
whose principal symbol is
\rho_{m}(\xi)^{*}x\rho_{m}(\xi)
, and so by (
7.1
) we have

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{\pm}\big(P^{*}\lambda(x)P\big)=\omega_{\pm}(x).

By the 2nd part of Theorem
4.17
the map
x\rightarrow P^{*}\lambda(x)P
is a continuous linear map from
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
to
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
. Thus, if

x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})_{\textup{sa}}
and
(x_{\ell})_{\ell\geq 0}
is a sequence of selfadjoint elements of
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
converging to
x
in
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then
P^{*}\lambda(x_{\ell})P\rightarrow P^{*}\lambda(x)P
in
\mathscr{L}_{p,\infty}
. Birman-Solomyak’s perturbation theory (see
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
) then ensures that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{\pm}\big(P^{*}\lambda(x)P\big)=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{\pm}\big(P^{*}\lambda(x_{\ell})P\big)=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}\omega(x_{\ell})=\omega(x).

This proves the result.
∎

From now on we let
g=(g_{ij})
be a Riemannian metric and
\nu\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
a smooth positive density on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Assume Conjecture
7.1
holds. Suppose that
p\neq 1
and
q=\max\{p,1\}
, or
p=1<q
. For all
x=x^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(7.2)

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{\pm}\left(\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\left(|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}x|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)_{\pm}^{p}\right]d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Proof.

Recall that
\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu}):L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu)\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
is a unitary isomorphism. Under this isomorphism
\Delta_{g,\nu}
corresponds to the operator,

\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}:=\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)\Delta_{g,\nu}\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{-1}=\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\Delta_{g,\nu}\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

We get an elliptic 2nd order differential operator whose principal symbol is
\nu^{1/2}(\nu^{-1/2}|\xi|_{g}^{2}\nu^{1/2})\nu^{-1/2}=|\xi|^{2}_{g}
. It is formally selfadjoint with respect to the original inner product (
2.1
) of
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
. In particular, we get a selfadjoint operator on
L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
with domain
W_{2}^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.
If
s>0
, then
\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-s}=\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})^{-1}\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-s}\hat{\lambda}(\sqrt{\nu})
, and so by using Proposition
3.12
we see that
\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-s}
is a (selfadjoint)
\Psi
DO of order
-2s
whose principal symbol is
|\xi|_{g}^{-2s}
. This result remains valid for any power

\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{z}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

If
x\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then by using (
4.24
) we get

\displaystyle\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}

\displaystyle=\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)\cdot\lambda\big(\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)\cdot\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)

\displaystyle=\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big)^{-1}\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda(x)\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\hat{\lambda}\big(\sqrt{\nu}\big).

The l.h.s. and the lower r.h.s are both continuous maps from
L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
to
\mathscr{L}(L_{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta};\nu))
(
cf
. Remark
4.16
). Therefore, the above equalities continue to hold for all
x\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
.

It follows from all this that the operators
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}
and
\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}
have exactly the same positive and negative eigenvalues with the same multiplicities. As
\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-n/4p}
is a selfadjoint
\Psi
DO of order
-n/2p
whose principal symbol is
|\xi|_{g}^{-n/2p}
, by using Corollary
7.2
we get

\displaystyle\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{\pm}\left(\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j^{\frac{1}{p}}\lambda_{j}^{\pm}\left(\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda(x)\hat{\Delta}_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\left(|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}x|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)_{\pm}^{p}\right]d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

For
p=1
the spectral asymptotics (
7.2
) imply that
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}
is a measurable operator in the sense of (
5.1
). They also allow us to recover the integration formula (
5.14
). In fact, it can be further shown that Weyl’s laws of the form (
7.2
) imply that
\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-n/4}
is actually strongly measurable (see
[
70
]
). Thus, Theorem
7.3
yields a stronger form of Theorem
5.11
.

If
p
and
q
are as in Theorem
7.3
and
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, then we can define the (semiclassical) fractional Schrödinger operators
h^{n/p}\Delta_{g,\nu}^{n/2p}+\lambda_{\nu}(V)
,
h>0
, as in the previous section. The abstract form of the Birman-Schwinger principle
[
10
, Lemma 1.4]
implies that

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}h^{n}N^{-}\left(h^{\frac{n}{p}}\Delta_{g,\nu}^{\frac{n}{2p}}+\lambda_{\nu}(V)\right)=\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}j\lambda_{j}^{-}\left(\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\lambda_{\nu}(x)\Delta_{g,\nu}^{-\frac{n}{4p}}\right)^{p}.

This formula goes back to Birman-Solomyak (see, e.g.,
[
4
, Theorem 10.1]
and
[
8
, Appendix 6]
). Readers who are unfamiliar with the Birman-Schwinger principle may also find a proof of the above equality in
[
70
]
. Combining it with Theorem
7.3
we immediately arrive at the following semiclassical Weyl’s laws.

Theorem 7.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
7.3
, for all
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(7.3)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}h^{n}N^{-}\left(h^{\frac{n}{p}}\Delta_{g,\nu}^{\frac{n}{2p}}+\lambda_{\nu}(V)\right)=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\left(|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}V|\xi|_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\right)_{-}^{p}\right]d\xi.

In the case
\nu=1
and
g
is the flat Euclidean metric
g_{0}=(\delta_{ij})
, the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\Delta_{g,\nu}
is just the ordinary Laplacian
\Delta=-(\partial_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\partial_{n}^{2})
. In this case, Theorem
7.5
specializes to the following result.

Corollary 7.6
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
7.3
, for all
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(7.4)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}h^{n}N^{-}\left(h^{\frac{n}{p}}\Delta^{\frac{n}{2p}}+\lambda(V)\right)=c(n)\tau\big[(V_{-})^{p}\big],\qquad c(n):=\frac{1}{n}|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|.

Proof.

We have
|\xi|_{g_{0}}=|\xi|
, and so for
g=g_{0}
the r.h.s. of (
7.3
) is equal to

\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\left(|\xi|^{-\frac{n}{2p}}V|\xi|^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\right)_{-}^{p}\right]d\xi=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\left(V\right)_{-}^{p}\right]d\xi=\frac{1}{n}\tau\big[(V_{-})^{p}\big]|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|.

This gives the result.
∎

Remark 7.7
.

The semiclassical Weyl’s law (
7.4
) was conjectured in
[
63
]
. We refer to
[
66
]
for an alternative proof for
p=n/2
and
n\geq 3
. Note that the approach of
[
66
]
does not extend to non-flat Riemannian metrics or to the dimension
n=2
(see Remark
7.9
below).

It also worth looking at the special case of conformally flat metrics.

Corollary 7.8
.

Suppose that
g=k^{2}g_{0}
,
k\in\operatorname{GL}_{1}^{+}(C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}))
. Under the assumptions of Theorem
7.3
, for all
V=V^{*}\in L_{q}(\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta})
, we have

(7.5)

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}h^{n}N^{-}\left(h^{\frac{n}{p}}\Delta_{g}^{\frac{n}{2p}}+\lambda_{\nu(g)}(V)\right)=c(n)\tau\left[\left(k^{\frac{n}{2p}}Vk^{\frac{n}{2p}}\right)_{-}^{p}\right].

where
c(n)
is as in (
7.4
).

Proof.

As
g=k^{2}g_{0}
we have
|\xi|_{g}=|\xi|_{g_{0}}k^{-1}=|\xi|k^{-1}
, and so in this case the r.h.s. of (
7.3
) is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\left((k^{-1}|\xi|)^{-\frac{n}{2p}}V(k^{-1}|\xi|)^{-\frac{n}{2p}}\right)_{-}^{p}\right]d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\tau\left[\left(k^{\frac{n}{2p}}Vk^{\frac{n}{2p}}\right)_{-}^{p}\right]d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\tau\left[\left(k^{\frac{n}{2p}}Vk^{\frac{n}{2p}}\right)_{-}^{p}\right]|\mathbb{S}^{n-1}|.

The result then follows from Theorem
7.5
.
∎

Remark 7.9
.

For
p=n/2
the asymptotic (
7.5
) gives

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}h^{n}N^{-}\left(h^{2}\Delta_{g}+\lambda_{\nu(g)}(V)\right)=c(n)\tau\left[(kVk)_{-}^{\frac{n}{2}}\right].

As
\tilde{\nu}(g)=\nu(g)=k^{n}
, the integration formula (
5.14
) gives

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int(V_{-})^{\frac{n}{2}}\Delta_{g}^{-n/2}=c(n)\tau\left[(V_{-})^{\frac{n}{2}}k^{n}\right].

In general,
\tau[(V_{-})^{n/2}k^{n}]\neq\tau[(kVk)_{-}^{n/2}]
, and so we have

\lim_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}h^{n}N^{-}\left(h^{2}\Delta_{g}+\lambda_{\nu(g)}(V)\right)\neq\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int(V_{-})^{\frac{n}{2}}\Delta_{g}^{-\frac{n}{2}}.

This shows that the semiclassical Weyl’s law for spectral triples established in
[
66
]
need not hold for non-flat Riemannian metrics on
\mathbb{T}^{n}_{\theta}
.

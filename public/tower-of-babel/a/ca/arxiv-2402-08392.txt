Large Language Models as Minecraft Agents
Chris Madge, Massimo Poesio

Abstract
In this work we examine the use of Large Language Models (LLMs) in the challenging setting of acting as a Minecraft agent. We apply and evaluate LLMs in the builder and architect settings, introduce clarification questions and examining the challenges and opportunities for improvement. In addition, we present a platform for online interaction with the agents and an evaluation against previous works.

In previous research, deep neural methods were used to model both of these roles. These models used a CNN to model the world state, and an RNN to model the text input, with a similar decoder to produce the utterances

LLMs are arguably one of the most interesting advancements in natural language technology, if not the field of machine learning, in recent years. Some studies suggest that beyond their conversational ability (natural language understanding and generation

Our first contribution are LLM-based builder and architect agents.

Our second contribution is giving to these agents the ability to ask clarification questions

2
Related Work

2.1
The Original Architect and Builder Models for the Minecraft Task

Several models for both the
architect
and
builder
roles have been proposed.
The first
architect
model
Narayan-Chen et al. 2019
uses a seq2seq RNN that aside from the usual sequence of tokens as an input, also takes the current and target world state. This is decoded to the Architect utterance.

Following on from the Architect Model, the “Builder Action Prediction” model was created
Jayannavar et al. 2020
. This model took also combined a CNN with an RNN, although the decoder now predicted a series of actions the agent could follow.

2.2
IGLU

The Interactive Grounded Language Understanding shared tasks
(
Kiseleva et al. 2022a
;
Kiseleva et al. 2022b
)
were proposed to stimulate research on building interactive agents that learn to solve a task while provided with grounded natural language instructions in a collaborative environment.
The more recent edition of the shared task
Kiseleva et al. 2022b
involved two subtasks:
‘Building Structures’ (developing a conversational agent able to engage in the conversation), and ‘Asking Clarification Questions’ (developing a model able to ask clarification questions at the appropriate point).

The baseline
Skrynnik et al. 2022
provided for the 2022 IGLU task
Kiseleva et al. 2023
is split into two separate modules. One language component responsible for interpreting the natural language utterances and transforming them into simplified structured instructions, and a reinforcement learning component responsible for navigating the agent in the world and performing actions to realise those instructions.

Several builder models were entered in the IGLU 2022 competition task

Kiseleva et al. 2022b
. The creators of the competition reflect upon the most
notable winning submissions
Kiseleva et al. 2023
. The top
architecture took an end-to-end approach concatenating outputs from a
language model and a CNN, which is then fed into a transformer designed
specifically to improve and fine tune language models through reinforcement
learning. However, second and third place both made improvements on the
baselines architecture approach with further fine tuning on the T5 transformer model
Raffel et al. 2020
.

2.3
Using LLMs to generate Embodied AI in an Interactive Environment

There are many applications of LLMs as autonomous agents or embodied AI in interactive environments
Wang et al. 2023b
;
Xi et al. 2023
. Many of these do operate in a Minecraft world. However, they are targeted towards planning (e.g. dividing tasks to reach a final goal, including “crafting”/combining items, to reach that outcome)
Nottingham et al. 2023
;
Wang et al. 2023a
;
Zhu et al. 2023
, not the creation of 3D structures.

2.4
Generating and Responding to Clarification Questions

Generating and responding to clarification questions generative remains relatively unexplored for this task. There have been models that label utterances as requiring clarification
Shi et al. 2022
, or rank clarification questions appropriateness to an instruction in an information retrieval style

Kiseleva et al. 2023
;
Mohanty et al. 2023
, but only one generative model
Mehta et al. 2023
using BART
Lewis et al. 2019
. Whilst the notion of using modern LLMs such as Llama and GPT-4 for this purpose has been proposed, to our knowledge this is yet to be explored
Hromei et al. 2023
.

3
Methodology

In this work we created models for the “Collaborative Builder Task”
Narayan-Chen et al. 2019
in which two agents collaborate in a voxel based world to build a structure. The
architect
is given a target world structure, and provides natural language instructions to a
builder
, who cannot see the target structure, to place blocks to achieve that goal structure. Unlike the majority of previous approaches, both agents are able to provide and answer questions.

3.1
The Builder

The builder agent operates as follows:
The initial prompt to the LLM contains instructions for the task, constraints of the world (
min/max
for
x,y,z
), coordinate mappings (east/south/west/north) and requests that the LLM respond in a specified JSON format only (see
Builder prompt
for full prompt). Following the initial prompt, on each turn, the agent consumes an utterance (instruction) and produces a sequence of actions in addition to an optional clarification question in the originally specified format. This JSON encoded representation is used to directly update the world. If the result does not constitute a valid JSON response it is disregarded.
The builder is evaluated against IGLU RL baseline sub-task. We evaluate the agent based on its ability to interpret a natural language instruction and produce the correct 3D coordinates and colours for the target structure (not manoeuvre an agent in a 3D space to interact with the world and realise that target structure).

The closest work to our methodology is
Mehta et al. 2023
, similarly we focus solely on the language component of this task. We do not examine the task of manipulating an agent to place blocks as done in previous tasks
Narayan-Chen et al. 2019
;
Jayannavar et al. 2020
;
Shi et al. 2022
, as it is the language element that we are most interested in.

3.2
The Architect

The architect differs slightly from the builder.
The prompt, along with architect specific instructions, contains the target state of the world (see
Architect prompt
). Following the initial prompt the architect consumes every utterance given by the builder, along with the current state of the world encoded in a structured JSON format. The target structures are taken from the
Minecraft Dialogue Corpus

Narayan-Chen et al. 2019
and evaluated empirically through repeated interactions.

3.3
The LLMs

In this work we test against the LLMs: GPT-4; GPT-3.5; Llama2
Touvron et al. 2023
(the chat variation in various parameter sizes) and Vicuna
Chiang et al. 2023
. To our knowledge, the number of parameters used in the GPT models have not been formally released, although they are rumoured to be magnitudes greater than those used in the open models. The prompts used for both tasks are given in the appendix.

5
Conclusion

In this work we demonstrate LLMs acting as agents in a Minecraft-like block world task. The builder performs favourably over past bespoke models. In addition, the LLM has an existing built in capability to ask and answer questions. In future work, we will look at methods of improving openly available LLM models to close the gap with the fine tuned baseline, ranging from prompt engineering (e.g. introducing few-shot prompting etc.) to fine-tuning and other methods. Our early experiments with the architect does show some challenges, and further work needs to be done to give a quantitative evaluation against existing architect models.

Appendix

Architect prompt

You are an agent in a voxel world, where the most northernly point is 0,0,-5; the most westerly point -5,0,0; the most eastern point is 5,0,0; the most southern 0,0,5 and the y-axis is up and down, with y=0 being the minimum. Your task is to give instructions to a human to place blocks to achieve the target world state: <<target_world_state>> where the target world instructions are in the format [[x,y,z,color],...]. Give easy to interpret instructions, do not directly mention the coordinates. The builder will respond with the coordinates of the blocks they have placed in the same format. Don’t ask for coordinates, they will always be given. Avoid long instructions with multiple steps and start building the structure from the ground up.

Builder prompt

You are an agent in a voxel world, where the most northernly point is 0,0,-5; the most westerly point -5,0,0; the most eastern point is 5,0,0; the most southern 0,0,5 and the y-axis is up and down, with y=0 being the minimum. Describe the coordinates of the blocks their colours (must be one of: blue, yellow, green, orange, purple, red) and whether the action is to add or remove them, your confidence in your interpretation of the instruction and optionally a question if the instruction is potentially unclear, in the JSON format: {"add": [[x,y,z,color], ...], "remove": [[x,y,z,color], ...], "confidence": 0.0, "question": "..."}. Give the JSON only, no additional dialog.

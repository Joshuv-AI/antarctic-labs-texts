Victor Kac


Abstract
The first part of the paper is devoted to two descriptions of all polynomial tau-functions of the KP hierarchy: by a generalized Jacobi-Trudy formula, and a generalized Giambelli formula. We use the latter formula in the second part to obtain all polynomial tau-functions of the CKP hierarchy and its n n -reductions. In particular, for n = 3 n=3 we find all polynomial tau-functions of the Kaup-Kupershmidt hierarchy.

Introduction

The concept of a tau-function of a hierarchy of soliton equations, developed by the Kyoto school in early 80’s (see
[
21
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
9
]
) is very
useful for construction of solutions of these equations.

The geometric meaning of a tau-function is very simple: it is, up to a constant factor, a non-zero element of the orbit of a highest weight vector of a representation of an infinite-dimensional group
[
21
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
15
]
.

The first and the most famous example is the KP hierarchy, constructed as follows. Let
C\ell
be the associative algebra on generators
\psi^{+}_{j}
and
\psi^{-}_{j}
,
j\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
, subject to the relations

[\psi^{+}(z),\psi^{-}(w)]_{+}=\delta(z-w),\quad[\psi^{\pm}(z),\psi^{\pm}(w)]_{+}=0,

(1)

where

\psi^{\pm}(z)=\sum_{i\in\frac{1}{2}`+\mathbb{Z}}\psi_{i}^{\pm}z^{-i-\frac{1}{2}}

(2)

are the generating series, called the charged free fermionic fields,
and
\delta(z-w)=z^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\frac{z}{w}\right)^{n}
is the formal delta function.
Let
F
be an irreducible representation of the algebra
C\ell
, which admits a non-zero vector
|0\rangle
, such that

\psi^{\pm}_{j}|0\rangle=0,\ \text{for}\ j>0.

(3)

Let
GL_{\infty}
be the group
of matrices
(g_{ij})_{i,j\in{\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}}}
with entries in
\mathbb{C}
, which are
invertible and all, but a finite number of
g_{ij}-\delta_{ij}
, are
0
.
We obtain a representation
R
of this group on
F
by letting

R(I+aE_{ij})=1+a\psi^{+}_{-i}\psi^{-}_{j},\quad i,j\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z},\quad a\in\mathbb{C}.

(4)

Defining the charge decomposition

F=\bigoplus_{m\in{\mathbb{Z}}}F^{(m)},\

(5)

by letting

\text{charge}(|0\rangle)=0\ \text{and charge}(\psi^{\pm}_{j})=\pm 1,

we see that each
F^{(m)}
is an irreducible
highest weight
module over
GL_{\infty}
, and

|\pm m\rangle=\psi^{\pm}_{-\frac{2m-1}{2}}\cdots\psi^{\pm}_{-\frac{3}{2}}\psi^{\pm}_{-\frac{1}{2}}|0\rangle,\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

(6)

is a highest weight vector for
F^{(\pm m)}
.

The KP hierarchy in the fermionic picture
is defined as the following equation:

{\rm Res}_{z}\,\psi^{+}(z)\tau\otimes\psi^{-}(z)\tau=0,\quad\tau\in F^{(0)},

(7)

where
{\rm Res}_{z}\,\sum_{i}f_{i}z^{i}=f_{-1}
.
It is easy to show
[
15
]
that equation (
7
) holds for a non-zero

\tau\in F^{(0)}
if and only if
\tau
lies in the
R(GL_{\infty})
-orbit of
|0\rangle
.

A remarkable fact is that equation (
7
) can be converted in a collection of PDE’s, using bosonization of
F
. For this one introduces the free
bosonic field

\alpha(z)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{n}z^{-n-1}=:\psi^{+}(z)\psi^{-}(z):,

(8)

where, as usual,
:\psi^{+}_{i}\psi^{-}_{j}:=\psi^{+}_{i}\psi^{-}_{j}
if
i\leq j
, and

=-\psi^{-}_{j}\psi^{+}_{i}
if
i>j
. Then the
\alpha_{n}

satisfy the commutation relations of the
infinite Heisenberg Lie algebra

[\alpha_{m},\alpha_{n}]=m\delta_{m,-n},

(9)

and since
\alpha_{i}|0\rangle=0
for
i\geq 0
,
there exists an isomorphism

\sigma:F\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{C}[q,q^{-1},t_{1},t_{2},\ldots],

called the bosonization of
F
, which
is uniquely determined
by the following properties

\sigma(|m\rangle)=q^{m},\ \sigma\alpha_{0}\sigma^{-1}=q\frac{\partial}{\partial q},\ \sigma\alpha_{-i}\sigma^{-1}=it_{i}\ \mbox{and }\ \sigma\alpha_{i}\sigma^{-1}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}\ \mbox{for }i>0.

(10)

Furthermore, since
[\alpha_{k},\psi^{\pm}(z)]=\pm z^{k}\psi^{\pm}(z)
,
one can identify, under the isomorphism

\sigma
,
the charged free fermions with the vertex operator

\sigma\psi^{\pm}(z)\sigma^{-1}=q^{\pm 1}z^{\pm q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}}\exp\left(\pm\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}z^{i}\right)\exp\left(\mp\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}\frac{z^{i}}{i}\right).

(11)

Then equation (
7
) gets converted to the KP hierarchy of bilinear PDE’s on
\tau(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t_{1},t_{2},\ldots]
:

{\rm Res}_{z}\ \exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(t_{i}-{t^{\prime}}_{i})z^{i}\right)\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial{t^{\prime}}_{i}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}}\right)\frac{z^{-i}}{i}\right)\tau(t)\tau(t^{\prime})=0.

(12)

Here and further
t^{\prime}
denotes another copy of
t=(t_{1},t_{2},\ldots)
.

Next, equation (
12
) can be rewritten in terms of Lax type equations via
the dressing operators
P(t,\partial)
, where
\partial=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{1}}

[
21
]
.
This is a monic pseudodifferential operator, whose symbol is

P(t,z)=\frac{\exp(-\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{z^{-i}}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}})\tau(t)}{\tau(t)}.

The associated to
\tau(t)
Lax operator
L(t,\partial)
is defined as the pseudodifferential operator

L(t,\partial)=P(t,\partial)\circ\partial\circ P(t,\partial)^{-1}.

(13)

Then equation (
12
) on the tau-function
\tau(t)
is equaivalent to the following hierarchy
of Lax-Sato evolution PDE’s on

L(t,\partial)=\partial+\sum_{j>0}u_{j}(t)\partial^{-j}
:

\frac{\partial L(t,\partial)}{\partial t_{k}}=[(L(t,\partial)^{k})_{+},L(t,\partial)],\quad k=1,2,3,\ldots,

(14)

where the subscript
\ {}_{+}
, as usual, denotes the differential part of
L(t,\partial)^{k}
.

A famous result of Sato
[
21
]
is that all Schur polynomials
s_{\lambda}(t)

are tau-functions
of the KP hierarchy. Recall that the Schur polynolial
s_{\lambda}(t)
, associated to a partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{\ell}>0)
is defined by the Jacobi-Trudi formula
(see e.g.
[
20
]
, Section I.3):

s_{\lambda}(t)=\det\left(s_{\lambda_{i}+j-i}(t)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell},

(15)

where the elementary Schur polynomials
s_{j}(t)

are defined by the generating series

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}s_{j}(t)z^{j}=\exp\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}z^{i}.

(16)

In our paper
[
12
]
we proved that all polynomial tau-functions of the KP hierarchy are, up to a constant factor, of the form

\tau_{\lambda,c}(t)=\det\left(s_{\lambda_{i}+j-i}(t_{1}+c_{1i},t_{2}+c_{2i},t_{3}+c_{3i},\ldots)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq\ell},

(17)

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{\ell})
, and
c=(c_{ij})
is a

(\lambda_{1}+\ell-1)\times\ell
matrix over
\mathbb{C}
.
We call equation (
17
)
the generalized Jacobi-Trudi formula for polynomial KP tau-functions.

It is well known that, using the Frobenius notation

\lambda=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}|b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k})

for the partition
\lambda
, one can write the Schur polynomial
s_{\lambda}(t)

in the Giambelli form (see e.g.
[
20
]
, Section I.3):

s_{\lambda}(t)=\det\left(\chi_{(a_{i}|b_{j})}(t;t)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k},

(18)

where

\chi_{(a|b)}(t;t^{\prime}):=(-1)^{b}\sum_{n=0}^{b}s_{n+a+1}(t)s_{b-n}(-t^{\prime}),\qquad a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

(19)

The first new result of the paper is Theorem

5
in Section
3
, describing all polynomial KP tau-functions
by the generalized Giambelli formula (see also
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
):

\tau_{\lambda;c,d}(t)=\det\left(\chi_{(a_{i}|b_{j})}(t_{1}+c_{1i},t_{2}+c_{2i},\ldots;t_{1}+d_{1j},t_{2}+d_{2j},\ldots)\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq k},

(20)

where
c=(c_{ij})
is a
(a_{1}+b_{1}+1)\times k
matrix over
\mathbb{C}
and
d=(d_{ij})
is a
b_{1}\times k
matrix over
\mathbb{C}
.

In Section
4
, using the Jacobi-Trudi formalism, as in
[
16
]
, we construct more general KP tau-functions, and find a Jacobi-Trudi type formula for the
wave function

w^{+}(t,z)=P(t,\partial)\exp\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}t_{i}z^{i}

We also find analogous formulas in the framework of the Giambelli formalism.

Remark 3

The totality of tau-functions is independent of

5
The formulation of the CKP hierarchy

The group
SP_{\infty}
, the corresponding Lie algebra
sp_{\infty}
and its central extension
c_{\infty}
can be defined using the following bilinear form on
\mathbb{C}^{\infty}
, see e.g.
[
10
]
, Section 7.11:

(e_{i},e_{j})_{C}=(-1)^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\delta_{i,-j}.

(65)

Then

\displaystyle SP_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\{G\in GL_{\infty}|(G(v),G(w))_{C}=(v,w)_{C}\ \mbox{for all }v,w\in\mathbb{C}^{\infty}\},

\displaystyle sp_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\{g\in gl_{\infty}|(g(v),w)_{C}+(v,g(w))_{C}=0\ \mbox{for all }v,w\in\mathbb{C}^{\infty}\},

\displaystyle c_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\{g+\lambda K\in a_{\infty}|(g(v),w)_{C}+(v,g(w))_{C}=0\ \mbox{for all }v,w\in\mathbb{C}^{\infty}\},

The elements
C_{jk}=E_{-j,k}-(-1)^{j+k}E_{-k,j}
, with
j\geq k

form a basis of
sp_{\infty}
.

Note that

\displaystyle r(C_{jk})

\displaystyle=\psi^{+}_{j}\psi^{-}_{k}-(-1)^{j+k}\psi^{+}_{k}\psi^{-}_{j}\quad\mbox{and }\hat{r}(C_{jk})

\displaystyle=:\psi^{+}_{j}\psi^{-}_{k}:+(-1)^{j+k}:\psi^{-}_{j}\psi^{+}_{k}:.

This suggests to define an automorphism of the Clifford algebra
C\ell
:

\iota_{C}(\psi_{j}^{\pm})=(-1)^{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}\psi^{\mp}_{j}.

(66)

This induces via
\hat{r}
and the observation that

\iota_{C}(:\psi^{+}_{j}\psi^{-}_{k}:)=(-1)^{j+k}:\psi^{-}_{j}\psi^{+}_{k}:=-(-1)^{j+k}:\psi^{+}_{k}\psi^{-}_{j}:

the following involution on
a_{\infty}
:

\iota_{C}(E_{-j,k})=-(-1)^{j+k}E_{-k,j},\quad\iota_{C}(K)=K,

so that

c_{\infty}=\{g+\lambda K\in a_{\infty}|\iota_{C}(g)=g\}.

Let us study this automorphism and its consequences a bit better.
First of all

\iota_{C}({\rm Ann}_{\pm}|0\rangle)={\rm Ann}_{\mp}|0\rangle
and thus

\iota_{C}({\rm Ann}\,|0\rangle)={\rm Ann}\,|0\rangle
,
which makes it possible to define the automorphism also on the module
F
, namely define
\iota_{C}(|0\rangle)=|0\rangle
, and the rest is induced by (
66
).
Since
\iota_{C}(\psi^{\pm}(z))=\pm\psi^{\mp}(-z)
, we see that
\iota_{C}(F^{(m)})=F^{(-m)}
and we deduce that

\iota_{C}(\alpha(z))=\iota_{C}(:\psi^{+}(z)\psi^{-}(z)=:\psi^{+}(-z)\psi^{-}(-z):=\alpha(-z),

and hence that
\iota_{C}(\alpha_{k})=-(-1)^{k}\alpha_{k}
.
Moreover,

\iota_{C}(|m\rangle)=(-1)^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}|-m\rangle
.
Using the bosonization
\sigma:F\to B
, the automorphism
\iota_{C}
defines an automorphism, wich we also denote by
\iota_{C}
, of
B
.
Clearly
\iota_{C}(1)=1
, and we get that the operators on
B
satisfy

\iota_{C}(t_{i})=-(-1)^{i}t_{i},\ \iota_{C}(\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}})=-(-1)^{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{i}},\ \iota_{C}(q\frac{\partial}{\partial q})=-q\frac{\partial}{\partial q},\ \iota_{C}(q)=q^{-1}(-1)^{q\frac{\partial}{\partial q}}.

In particular,
F^{(0)}
is
\iota_{C}
invariant, and

is an invariant subspace for the representation
\hat{r}
, restricted to

.
Hence we want to look at

.
Let

.
This makes it possible to define a skewsymmetric form on
\Psi

7
Comparison with the polynomial solutions of BKP

It is interesting to compare the polynomial tau-functions of the CKP hierarchy and that of the BKP
hierarchy. The first observation is that both tau-functions are parametrized more or less by the same kind of permutations.
The ones of the CKP are parametrized by the self-conjugate partitions
\lambda=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}|a_{1},\ldots,a_{k})
,
where
a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{k}\geq 0
. Hence
\mu:=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k})
is an extended strict partition. We use the word extended because we allow
a_{k}
to be zero.
The polynomial solutions of the BKP hierarchy are parametrized by the same set of extended strict partitions, with the only additional restriction that
k
has to be even.

The second observation is that the solutions are expressed in terms of the polynomials:

\displaystyle\overline{\chi}_{M,N}(t,t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N}\left(\frac{1}{2}s_{M}(t)s_{N}(-t^{\prime})+\sum_{k=1}^{N}s_{M+k}(t)s_{N-k}(-t^{\prime})\right)

(95)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi_{(M,N)}(t,t^{\prime})-(-1)^{M}\frac{1}{2}s_{M}(t)s_{N}(-t^{\prime}).

Namely, one has (see
[
13
]
,
[
16
]
and
[
18
]
)

Euler-Rodrigues formula for three-dimensional rotation via fractional powers of matrices
Flank D. M. Bezerra, Lucas A. Santos

Abstract
. In this short paper, we review the Euler-Rodrigues formula for three-dimensional rotation via fractional powers of matrices. We derive the rotations by any angle through the spectral behavior of the fractional powers of the rotation matrix by π 2 \frac{\pi}{2} in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} about some axis. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010 : 37E45, 15A04, 47D03. Key words and phrases: Euler-Rodrigues formula; three-dimensional rotations; fractional powers; approximations.

Introduction

The Euler-Rodrigues formula describes the rotation of a vector in three dimensions, it was first discovered by Euler
[
4
]
and later rediscovered independently by Rodrigues
[
7
]
and it is related to a number of interesting problems in computer graphics, dynamics, kinematics, mathematics, and robotics, see Cheng and Gupta
[
2
]
and references therein.

Reviews of the Euler–Rodrigues formula in different mathematical forms can be found in the literature, see e.g., Dai
[
3
]
, Kahvecí, Yayli and Gök
[
5
]
and Mebius
[
6
]
. Here, we explored the geometric aspect of the classical Balakrishnan formula in
[
1
]
to obtain a new algorithm for the generation of a three-dimensional rotation matrix.

To our best knowledge, this treatment on the Euler–Rodrigues formula has not yet been explored in the literature.

2.
Three-dimensional rotations

Firstly, we present some facts of the theory of fractional powers of matrices. Secondly, we establish the main results of this paper; namely, we review the Euler-Rodrigues formula via the Balakrishnan formula on fractional powers of matrices.

2.1.
Fractional powers of operators

In this subsection, we recall some definitions and summarize without proofs results of the theory of fractional powers of matrices, in the sense of Balakrishnan
[
1
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
A\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
with no eigenvalues on
(-\infty,0)
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
A^{\alpha}=e^{\alpha\log A}
, where
\log A
is the principal logarithm.

Thanks to Balakrishnan
[
1
]
we following results are well-known.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
. We have

(i)

(2.1)

A^{\alpha}=\frac{\sin{\alpha\pi}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}^{\alpha-1}A(\lambda I+A)^{-1}d\lambda;

(ii)

Let
\beta
be real number, then

(A^{\alpha})^{\beta}=A^{\alpha\beta}.

2.2.
Main results

In this subsection, we present the main results of this paper. We explored the geometric aspect of the classical Balakrishnan formula (
2.1
) (see, e.g., Balakrishnan
[
1
]
) to obtain a new algorithm for the generation of three-dimensional rotation matrices. Here, the matrix representations of linear operators on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
are considered using the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
\mathbf{\hat{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
denotes a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}+n_{3}^{2}=1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The matrix which represents the rotation by an angle
\frac{\pi}{2}
about the axis
\mathbf{\hat{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
is given by

(2.2)

A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)=\left[\begin{matrix}n_{1}^{2}&n_{1}n_{2}-n_{3}&n_{1}n_{3}+n_{2}\\
n_{1}n_{2}+n_{3}&n_{2}^{2}&n_{2}n_{3}-n_{1}\\
n_{1}n_{3}-n_{2}&n_{2}n_{3}+n_{1}&n_{3}^{2}\end{matrix}\right].

Proof:

Choose two vectors,
\mathbf{\hat{l}}
and
\mathbf{\hat{m}}
, such that
\{\mathbf{\hat{l}},\mathbf{\hat{m}},\mathbf{\hat{n}}\}
is a right-handed orthonormal basis. Let
u=a\mathbf{\hat{l}}+b\mathbf{\hat{m}}+c\mathbf{\hat{n}}
, with
a,b,c\in\mathbb{R}
, be any vector to be rotated by an angle
\frac{\pi}{2}
counterclockwise about the axis
\mathbf{\hat{n}}
. The resulting vector
u^{\prime}
is the vector
u
with its component in the
\mathbf{\hat{l}},\mathbf{\hat{m}}
plane rotated by
\frac{\pi}{2}

\displaystyle u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-b\mathbf{\hat{l}}+a\mathbf{\hat{m}}+c\mathbf{\hat{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{n}}\times u+\langle u,\mathbf{\hat{n}}\rangle\mathbf{\hat{n}}.

Consider the standard basis
\{{\mathbf{\hat{e_{1}}},\mathbf{\hat{e_{2}}},\mathbf{\hat{e_{3}}}}\}
of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. If
u
is written as

u=u_{1}\mathbf{\hat{e_{1}}}+u_{2}\mathbf{\hat{e_{2}}}+u_{3}\mathbf{\hat{e_{3}}},

then

\displaystyle u^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{\hat{n}}\times u+\langle u,\mathbf{\hat{n}}\rangle\mathbf{\hat{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n_{2}u_{3}-n_{3}u_{2}+u_{1}n_{1}^{2}+u_{2}n_{1}n_{2}+u_{3}n_{1}n_{3})\mathbf{\hat{e_{1}}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(n_{3}u_{1}-n_{1}u_{3}+u_{1}n_{1}n_{2}+u_{2}n_{2}^{2}+u_{3}n_{2}n_{3})\mathbf{\hat{e_{2}}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(n_{1}u_{2}-n_{2}u_{1}+u_{1}n_{1}n_{3}+u_{2}n_{2}n_{3}+u_{3}n_{3}^{2})\mathbf{\hat{e_{3}}}.

Therefore, the matrix representation of this rotation is

A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)=\left[\begin{matrix}n_{1}^{2}&n_{1}n_{2}-n_{3}&n_{1}n_{3}+n_{2}\\
n_{1}n_{2}+n_{3}&n_{2}^{2}&n_{2}n_{3}-n_{1}\\
n_{1}n_{3}-n_{2}&n_{2}n_{3}+n_{1}&n_{3}^{2}\end{matrix}\right].\square

Remark 2.4
.

Thanks to the characterization in (
2.2
) of the matrix which represents the rotation by an angle
\frac{\pi}{2}
about the axis
\mathbf{\hat{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
we can obtain a matrix characterization of the linear semigroup generated by
A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)
, namely the uniformly continuous semigroup of bounded linear operators generated by
A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)
, denoted by
T(\cdot)
,
has the following explicit representation

\displaystyle T(t)=e^{tA(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2})}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(tA(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}))^{n}}{n!}=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n_{1}^{2}(e^{t}-\cos t)+\cos t&n_{1}n_{2}(e^{t}-\cos t)-n_{3}\sin t&n_{1}n_{3}(e^{t}-\cos t)+n_{2}\sin t\\
n_{1}n_{2}(e^{t}-\cos t)+n_{3}\sin t&n_{2}^{2}(e^{t}-\cos t)+\cos t&n_{2}n_{3}(e^{t}-\cos t)-n_{1}\sin t\\
n_{1}n_{3}(e^{t}-\cos t)-n_{2}\sin t&n_{2}n_{3}(e^{t}-\cos t)+n_{1}\sin t&n_{3}^{2}(e^{t}-\cos t)+\cos t\end{matrix}\right]

for any
t\geqslant 0
.

Remark 2.5
.

An explicit formula for the matrix elements of a general
3\times 3
rotation matrix can be find in Rodrigues
[
7
]
; namely, if
R(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)
denotes the a rotation by an angle
\theta
about an axis
\mathbf{\hat{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})

(n_{1}^{2}+n_{2}^{2}+n_{3}^{2}=1)
, whose elements are denoted by
R_{ij}(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)
, then we have the Rodrigues formula

(2.3)

R_{ij}(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)=\cos(\theta)\delta_{ij}+(1-\cos(\theta))n_{i}n_{j}-\sin(\theta)\epsilon_{ijk}n_{k},

where
\delta_{ij}
denotes the Kronecker delta, i.e.,

\delta_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if}\ i=j,\\
0,&\mbox{if}\ i\neq j,\end{cases}

and
\epsilon_{ijk}
denotes the Levi-Civita tensor, i.e.,

\epsilon_{ijk}=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if}\ (i,j,k)\in\{(1,2,3),(2,3,1),(3,1,2)\},\\
-1,&\mbox{if}\ (i,j,k)\in\{(3,2,1),(1,3,2),(2,1,3)\},\\
0,&\mbox{if}\ i=j,\ \mbox{or}\ j=k,\ \mbox{or}\ k=i,\end{cases}

which is called the angle-and-axis parameterization of the three-dimensional rotation matrix.

We wish to derive all the rotations by any angle
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
through the rotation by
\frac{\pi}{2}
and its fractional powers. In order to get this result we first explicit, in the following theorem, the fractional power, for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, of the rotation
A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2})
in Lemma
2.3
. It is one of the main results of this work.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2})
be the matrix that represents the rotation by an angle
\frac{\pi}{2}
about the axis
\mathbf{\hat{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
.
For
0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 1
, the fractional power of the rotation
A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2})
is given by

\begin{split}&A^{\alpha}\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)=\\
&\left[\begin{matrix}n_{1}^{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{1}n_{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))-n_{3}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{1}n_{3}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})))+n_{2}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\\
n_{1}n_{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+n_{3}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{2}^{2}(1-cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})+cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}&n_{2}n_{3}(1-cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})-n_{1}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\\
n_{1}n_{3}(1-cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})-n_{2}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{2}n_{3}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+n_{1}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{3}^{2}(1-cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})+\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\end{matrix}\right].\end{split}

Proof:

The proof consists of the explicit calculation of the fractional power of the operator
A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2})
through the formula (
2.1
) for
0<\alpha<1
.

(2.4)

A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)^{\alpha}=\frac{\sin(\alpha\pi)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\lambda}^{\alpha-1}A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\Big(\lambda I+A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\Big)^{-1}d\lambda,\ 0<\alpha<1.

Note that

\displaystyle\Big(\lambda I+A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\Big)^{-1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{2}+1)}\left[\begin{matrix}a^{2}(1-\lambda)+\lambda(1+\lambda)&ab(1-\lambda)+c(1+\lambda)&ac(1-\lambda)-b(1+\lambda)\\
ab(1-\lambda)-c(1+\lambda)&b^{2}(1-\lambda)+\lambda(1+\lambda)&bc(1-\lambda)+a(1+\lambda)\\
ac(1-\lambda)+b(1+\lambda)&bc(1-\lambda)-a(1+\lambda)&c^{2}(1-\lambda)+\lambda(1+\lambda)\end{matrix}\right]

and

\displaystyle A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\Big(\lambda I+A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\Big)^{-1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{2}+1)}\left[\begin{matrix}a^{2}\lambda(\lambda-1)+1+\lambda&ab\lambda(\lambda-1)-c\lambda(1+\lambda)&ac\lambda(\lambda-1)+b\lambda(1+\lambda)\\
ab\lambda(\lambda-1)+c\lambda(1+\lambda)&b^{2}\lambda(\lambda-1)+1+\lambda&bc\lambda(\lambda-1)-a\lambda(1+\lambda)\\
ac\lambda(\lambda-1)-b\lambda(1+\lambda)&bc\lambda(\lambda-1)+a\lambda(1+\lambda)&c^{2}\lambda(\lambda-1)+1+\lambda\end{matrix}\right].

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda(\lambda-1)}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{2}+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda+1}-\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+1}

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda+1}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{2}+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda^{2}+1}

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda(\lambda+1)}{(\lambda+1)(\lambda^{2}+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{\lambda^{2}+1}

from right side of the equation (
2.4
) and
A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\Big(\lambda I+A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)\Big)^{-1}
, and by (
2.1
) we obtain

\begin{split}&A^{\alpha}\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)=\\
&\left[\begin{matrix}n_{1}^{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{1}n_{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))-n_{3}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{1}n_{3}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})))+n_{2}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\\
n_{1}n_{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+n_{3}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{2}^{2}(1-cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})+cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}&n_{2}n_{3}(1-cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})-n_{1}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\\
n_{1}n_{3}(1-cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})-n_{2}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{2}n_{3}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+n_{1}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{3}^{2}(1-cos\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})+\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\end{matrix}\right].\end{split}

Finally, cases
\alpha=0
and
\alpha=1
are immediate, and the proof is complete.
\square

Corollary 2.7
.

The fractional power
A^{\alpha}(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2})
coincides with matrix
R(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})=[R_{ij}(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})]
, where
R_{ij}(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})
is given by (
2.3
), for
0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant 1
.

We are now in a position to give our definition for the rotation matrix by an angle
\theta
through fractional powers of the rotation by
\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Definition 2.8
.

The rotation by
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, denoted by
A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)
, is defined to be

(2.5)

A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta):=A^{\frac{2\theta}{\pi}}\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big).

Note that
A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2})
is such that the fractional power
A^{\alpha}(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2})
is well-defined for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. Theorem
2.6
states that the definition in (
2.5
) agrees with the classical one given by
Rodrigues formula
in (
2.3
) for
0\leq\theta\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
. The following theorem extends this result for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)
be the rotation defined in (
2.5
). Then

(2.6)

A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)=R(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)

for any
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof:
Firstly for
\theta\geqslant 0
, it is sufficient to show that (
2.6
) is satisfied for

\frac{(n-1)\pi}{2}\leq\theta\leq\frac{n\pi}{2},

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We proceed by induction. The case
n=1
follows from Theorem
2.6
. If we assume (
2.6
) for
n
, we can prove the result for
n+1
. Set

\frac{n\pi}{2}\leq\theta\leq\frac{(n+1)\pi}{2}

so that

\frac{(n-1)\pi}{2}\leq\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\frac{n\pi}{2}

Hence

(2.7)

A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)=A^{\frac{2\theta}{\pi}}\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)=A^{\frac{2\theta}{\pi}-1}\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)=A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta-\frac{\pi}{2})A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)

and by induction hypothesis

(2.8)

A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)=R\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)

combining (
2.7
) with (
2.8
) we obtain

\displaystyle A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta-\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)A\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)R\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},-\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)R\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)

above we use some basic properties of the Euler-Rodrigues formula.

Secondly, for
-\frac{\pi}{2}\leqslant\theta\leqslant 0
, and proceeding analogously to the proof of Theorem (
2.6
) we can obtain the expression

\begin{split}&A^{-\alpha}\Big(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\frac{\pi}{2}\Big)=\\
&\left[\begin{matrix}n_{1}^{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{1}n_{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+n_{3}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{1}n_{3}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})))-n_{2}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\\
n_{1}n_{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))-n_{3}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{2}^{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{2}n_{3}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+n_{1}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\\
n_{1}n_{3}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+n_{2}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{2}n_{3}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))-n_{1}\sin(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})&n_{3}^{2}(1-\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2}))+\cos(\frac{\alpha\pi}{2})\end{matrix}\right]\end{split}

and so the definition in (
2.5
) agrees with the classical one given by the Euler-Rodrigues formula in (
2.3
) for
-\frac{\pi}{2}\leqslant\theta\leqslant 0
. Finally, an analogous argument of induction as in the first part of this proof shows that (
2.5
) agrees with the Euler-Rodrigues formula in (
2.3
) for
\theta\leqslant 0
.
\square

Corollary 2.10
.

The family
\{A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta);\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
, where

\displaystyle A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)=

\displaystyle\left[\begin{matrix}n_{1}^{2}(1-\cos(\theta))+\cos(\theta)&n_{1}n_{2}(1-\cos(\theta))-n_{3}\sin(\theta)&n_{1}n_{3}(1-\cos(\theta))+n_{2}\sin(\theta)\\
n_{1}n_{2}(1-\cos(\theta))+n_{3}\sin(\theta)&n_{2}^{2}(1-\cos(\theta))+\cos(\theta)&n_{2}n_{3}(1-\cos(\theta))-n_{1}\sin(\theta)\\
n_{1}n_{3}(1-\cos(\theta))-n_{2}\sin(\theta)&n_{2}n_{3}(1-\cos(\theta))+n_{1}\sin(\theta)&n_{3}^{2}(1-\cos(\theta))+\cos(\theta)\end{matrix}\right]

is a uniformly continuous group on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with infinitesimal generator
G:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
given by

G=\left[\begin{matrix}0&-n_{3}&n_{2}\\
n_{3}&0&-n_{1}\\
-n_{2}&n_{1}&0\end{matrix}\right].

Proof:

That family
\{A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta);\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a group is an immediate consequence of the definition of
A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)
in (
2.5
). We obtain
G
easily from the definition of infinitesimal generator of a group

D(G)=\left\{u\in\mathbb{R}^{3};\lim_{\theta\to 0}\frac{A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)u-u}{\theta}\ \text{exists}\right\}

and

Gu=\lim_{\theta\to 0}\frac{A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)u-u}{\theta},\ \text{for any}\ u\in D(G).

Since
G
is a bounded linear operator, we conclude that
\{A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta);\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a uniformly continuous group on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
\square

Remark 2.11
.

In particular, we can obtain the explicit expression of the logarithm of rotations
A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)
thanks to the fact that the logarithm is the infinitesimal generator of the uniformly continuous group
\{A^{\alpha}(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta);\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
; namely, we have

\log A(\mathbf{\hat{n}},\theta)=\left[\begin{matrix}0&-\theta n_{3}&\theta n_{2}\\
\theta n_{3}&0&-\theta n_{1}\\
-\theta n_{2}&\theta n_{1}&0\end{matrix}\right].

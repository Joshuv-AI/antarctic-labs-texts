Refinement of quantum Markov states on trees


Abstract
In the present paper, we propose a refinement for the notion of quantum Markov states (QMS) on trees. A structure theorem for QMS on general trees is proved. We notice that any restriction of QMS in the sense of Ref. [ 6 ] is not necessarily to be a QMS. It turns out that localized QMS has the mentioned property which is called sub-Markov states , this allows us to characterize translation invariant QMS on regular trees. Mathematics Subject Classification : 46L53, 60J99, 46L60, 60G50. Key words : Quantum Markov state; Cayley tree; uniqueness.

2
Preliminaries

Let
T=(V,E)
be a locally finite tree. We fix a root
o\in V
. Two vertices
x
and
y
are
nearest neighbors
(denoted
x\sim y
) if they are joined through an edge (i.e.
<x,y>\in E
). A list
x\sim x_{1}\sim\dots\sim x_{d-1}\sim y
of vertices is called a
path
from
x
to
y
. The distance on the tree
d(x,y)
is the length of the shortest path from
x
to
y
.
The set of its
direct successors
of a given vertex
x\in V
is defined by

S(x):=\left\{y\in V\,\,:\,\,x\sim y\,\,\hbox{and}\,\,d(y,o)>d(x,o)\right\}

(1)

and its
k^{th}
successors w.r.t. the root
o
is defined by induction as follows

S_{1}(x):=S(x);

S_{k+1}(x)=S(S_{k}(x)),\,\,\forall k\geq 1.

The ”future” w.r.t. the vertex
x
is defined by:

S_{[m,n]}(x)=\bigcup_{k=m}^{n}S_{k}(x);\quad T(x)=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}S_{k}(x);\quad T^{{}^{\prime}}(x)=T(x)\setminus\{x\}.

(2)

In the homogeneous case (
|S(x)|=k
is constant w.r.t. the vertex
x
), the graph
T
coincides with the semi-infinite Cayley tree
\Gamma^{k}_{+}
of order
k
. In particular, if
k=1
the graph is reduced to the one-side integer lattice
\mathbb{N}
.

Let
x\in V
. If
o=x_{0}\sim x_{1}\sim\cdots x_{n}=x
is the unique edge-path with minimal length joining
o
and
x
, the set

P(x):=\{x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\}

(3)

represents the set of ”predecessors” of the vertex
x
w.r.t. the root
o
. The author is referred to
[
56
]
for a more detailed description of the hierarchical structure of rooted trees.

Define

\Lambda_{n}=S_{n}(o);\quad\Lambda_{[n,m]}:=S_{[n,m]}(0);\quad\Lambda_{[0,n]}:=S_{[0,n]}(0).

To each vertex
x
, we associate a
C^{*}
–algebra
\mathcal{A}_{x}
with identity
{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}_{x}
. For a given bounded region
\Lambda
, we consider the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}_{x}
. One can consider the following embedding

\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}\equiv\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}\otimes{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}_{\Lambda_{n+1}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n+1]}}.

The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}
can be viewed as a subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n+1]}}
. It follows the following quasi-local algebra.

\mathcal{A}_{V;\,loc}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{A}_{[0,n]}

(4)

and the quasi-local algebra

\mathcal{A}_{V}:=\overline{\mathcal{A}_{V;\,loc}}^{C^{*}}.

The set of states on a
C^{*}
–algebra
\mathcal{A}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
.

3
Quantum Markov chains and States on trees

Let us consider a triplet
{\mathcal{C}}\subset{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
of
unital
C^{*}
-algebras. Recall
[
1
]
that a
quasi-conditional expectation
with respect to the given triplet
is a completely positive (CP) linear map
\mathcal{E}\,:\,{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
such that
\mathcal{E}(ca)=c\mathcal{E}(a)
, for all
a\in{\mathcal{A}},\,c\in{\mathcal{C}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

[
7
,
13
]

Let
\varphi
be a state on
\mathcal{A}_{V}
. Then
\varphi
is called a
(backward) quantum Markov chain, associated with
\{\Lambda_{n}\}
, if
there exist a quasi-conditional expectation
E_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}
with
respect to the triple
\mathcal{A}_{{\Lambda}_{n-1]}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n+1]}}
for each

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and an initial state
\varphi_{0}\in S(\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{0}})
such that

\varphi=\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{0}\circ E_{\Lambda_{0]}}\circ E_{\Lambda_{1]}}\circ\cdots\circ E_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}

(5)

in the weak-* topology.

In
[
7
]
it was given a general definition of quantum Markov states which can be adopted to the considered setting as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

A quantum Markov chain
\varphi
is said to be quantum Markov state
with respect to the sequence
\{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_{j]}}\}
of
quasi-conditional expectations if one has

\varphi_{\lceil\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{j]}}}\circ\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_{j]}}=\varphi_{\lceil\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{j+1]}}},\ \ \forall j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(6)

Using this definition, in
[
7
]
non-homogeneous QMS has been characterized. To formulate that result let us recall some notations.

Let us assume that we have a locally faithful state
\varphi
on the quasi-local algebra
\mathcal{A}_{V}
. Then a
potential
h_{\Lambda_{n}}
is canonically defined for each finite subset
\Lambda_{n}
as follows

\varphi_{\lceil\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}}={\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}}(e^{-h_{\Lambda_{n}}}\cdot).

(7)

Such a set of potentials
h_{\Lambda_{n}}
satisfies normalization conditions

{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}}(e^{-h_{\Lambda_{n}}})=1.

Next result has been formulated and proved for trees in
[
42
]
.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
7
]

Let
\varphi
be a locally faithful state on
\mathcal{A}_{V}
. Then the following statements are
equivalent:

(i)

\varphi
is a QMS w.r.t. the sequence
\{\mathcal{E}_{\Lambda_{j]}}\}

of transition expectations;

(ii)

The sequence of potentials
\{h_{\Lambda_{n}}\}
associated to
\varphi
by (
7
), can be recovered by

h_{\Lambda_{n}}=H_{W_{0}}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}H_{W_{j},W_{j+1}}+\hat{H}_{W_{n}}

(8)

where the sequences
\{H_{W_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0},\;\{\hat{H}_{W_{j}}\}_{j\geq 0}

and
\{H_{W_{j},W_{j+1}}\}_{j\geq 0}
of self-adjoint operators localized in
\mathcal{A}_{W_{j}}
and

\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{j,j+1}}
,
respectively, and satisfying commutation relations

\displaystyle[H_{W_{n}},H_{W_{n},W_{n+1}}]=0,\quad[H_{W_{n},W_{n+1}},\hat{H}_{W_{n+1}}]=0,

\displaystyle[H_{W_{n}},\widehat{H}_{W_{n}}]=0,\quad[H_{W_{n},W_{n+1}},H_{W_{n+1},W_{n+2}}]=0.

(9)

We stress that one considers QMS with respect to the levels of the tree, then QMS can be associated with potential
given in Theorem
3.3
. However, the definition
3.4
does not take into account a finer structure of the tree.
This can be seen in the decomposition (
8
) since the terms
H_{W_{j},W_{j+1}}
are not specified.
Recently, in
[
42
]
we have considered QMS with finer structure of localized conditional expectations defined on the Cayley trees, then
the localization property (
28
) allowed us to explicitly find forms of
H_{W_{j},W_{j+1}}
and
\hat{H}_{W_{j}}
in terms of nearest neighbor and competing interactions, namely

\displaystyle H_{W_{j},W_{j+1}}=\sum_{x\in W_{j}}H_{x,\overrightarrow{S(x)}},\ \ \ \widehat{H}_{W_{j}}:=\sum_{x\in W_{j}}\widehat{H}_{x}.

(10)

We again emphasize that if one considers conditional expectations without localization
property, then the expression is impossible to be obtained. Therefore, it is natural to provide a conceptual definition QMS
which could cover such finer structure.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\varphi
be a quantum Markov chain on
\mathcal{A}_{V}
w.r.t. a sequence
\{E_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}\}_{n}
of quasi-conditional expectations.
If for each
x\in V
the restriction

E_{x}:=E_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}\lceil_{\mathcal{A}_{P(x)\cup\{x\}\cup S(x)}}

(11)

defines a quasi-conditional expectations w.r.t. the triplet
\mathcal{A}_{P(x)}\subset\mathcal{A}_{P(x)\cup\{x\}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{P(x)\cup\{x\}\cup\overrightarrow{S}(x)}
such that

\varphi_{\lceil\mathcal{A}_{\{x\}\cup\overrightarrow{S}(x)}}\circ E_{x}=\varphi_{\lceil\mathcal{A}_{P(x)\cup\{x\}\cup\overrightarrow{S}(x)}}

(12)

then
\varphi
is called (localized) quantum Markov state w.r.t. the family
\{E_{x}\}_{x\in L}
of local quasi-conditional expectations.

Remark 3.5
.

The above definition refines the notion of quantum Markov states on trees introduced in
[
42
]
for which the Markov property (
12
) is replaced by (
6
).
In the one-dimensional case these two definitions coincide with the usual notion quantum Markov states introduced in
[
3
]
.

3.1
Why localized quantum Markov states on trees are not reducible to the one dimensional case

In this section, we are going to demonstrate that localized QMS cannot be reduced to the one dimensional case. More precisely,
we explicitly consider the potential (
10
).

In what follows, we consider a semi-infinite
Cayley tree
\Gamma^{2}_{+}=(L,E)
of order two. Our starting

C^{*}
-algebra is the same
\mathcal{A}_{V}
but with
\mathcal{A}_{x}=M_{2}({\mathbb{C}})

for all
x\in V
.
By
"\mathrm{Tr}"
we denote the normalized on the local algebra that assigns
1
to the identity
{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}
. For a given bounded region
\Lambda
, the associated partial trace is defined by

\mathrm{Tr}_{\Lambda]}(a_{\Lambda}\otimes a_{\Lambda^{c}})=a_{\Lambda}\mathrm{Tr}(a_{\Lambda^{c}}).

Denote

{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(u)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\ \ \sigma^{(u)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\\
\end{array}\right).

(13)

For every vertices
(x,(x,1),(x,2))
we put

\displaystyle K_{<x,(x,i)>}=\exp\{\beta H_{x,(x,i)>}\},\ \ i=1,2,\ \beta>0,

(14)

\displaystyle L_{>(x,1),(x,2)<}=\exp\{J\beta H_{>(x,1),(x,2)<}\},\ \ J>0,

(15)

where

\displaystyle H_{<x,(x,i)>}=\frac{1}{2}\big({\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{x)}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(x,i)}+\sigma^{(x)}\sigma^{(x,i)}\big),

(16)

\displaystyle H_{>(x,1),(x,2)<}=\frac{1}{2}\big({\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(x,1)}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(x,2)}+\sigma^{(x,1)}\sigma^{(x,2)}\big).

(17)

The defined model is called the
Ising model with competing
interactions
per vertices
(u,(u,1),(u,2))
.

Therefore, one finds

\displaystyle K_{<u,v>}=K_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(u)}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(v)}+K_{3}\sigma^{(u)}\sigma^{(v)},

(18)

\displaystyle L_{>u,v<}=R_{0}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(u)}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(v)}+R_{3}\sigma^{(u)}\sigma^{(v)},

(19)

where

\displaystyle K_{0}=\frac{\exp{\beta}+1}{2},\ \ \ K_{3}=\frac{\exp{\beta}-1}{2},

\displaystyle R_{0}=\frac{\exp{(J\beta)}+1}{2},\ \ \ R_{3}=\frac{\exp{(J\beta)}-1}{2}.

Let

A_{(x,(x,1),(x,2))}:=K_{<x,(x,1)>}K_{<x,(x,2)>}L_{>(x,1),(x,2)<}

(20)

The operator
A_{(x,(x,1),(x,2))}
is localized on the algebra of observable associated with the ternary
(x,(x,1),(x,2))
.

A simple calculation leads to

\displaystyle A_{(x,(x,1),(x,2))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\gamma{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(x)}\otimes{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(x,1)}\otimes{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(x,2)}+\delta\sigma^{(x)}\otimes\sigma^{(x,1)}\otimes{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(x,2)}

(21)

\displaystyle+\delta\sigma^{(x)}\otimes{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(x,1)}\otimes\sigma^{(x,2)}+\eta{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(x)}\otimes\sigma^{(x,1)}\otimes\sigma^{(x,2)},

where

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma=K_{0}^{2}R_{0}+K_{3}^{2}.R_{3}=\frac{1}{4}[\exp{(J+2)\beta}+\exp{J\beta}+2\exp{\beta}],\\
\\
\delta=K_{0}K_{3}(R_{0}+R_{3})=\frac{1}{4}\exp{J\beta}[\exp{2\beta}-1],\\
\\
\eta=K_{0}^{2}R_{3}+K_{3}^{2}R_{0}=\frac{1}{4}[\exp{(J+2)\beta}+\exp{J\beta}-2\exp{\beta}].\\
\end{array}\right.

(22)

In
[
37
]
, we have found that

\mathrm{Tr}_{u]}\left(A_{(u,(u,1),(u,2))}^{*}{\bf 1}\!\!{\rm I}^{(u)}\otimes h^{(u,1)}\otimes h^{(u,2)}A_{(u,(u,1),(u,2))}\right)=h^{(u)}.

(23)

where
h^{(x)}=h_{\alpha}
,

h_{\alpha}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha\\
\end{array}\right)

(24)

here
\alpha=\frac{4}{\exp(2J\beta)(\exp(4\beta)+1)+2\exp(2\beta)}.

Define

\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{(u,(u,1),(u,2))}(a)=\mathrm{Tr}_{u]}\left(\alpha^{1/2}A_{(u,(u,1),(u,2))}^{*}aA_{(u,(u,1),(u,2))}\alpha^{1/2}\right).

(25)

One can see that the map
\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}
is an identity preserving transition expectation from
\mathcal{A}_{\{u\}\cup S(u)}
into

\mathcal{A}_{u}
. Then the map

E^{\alpha}_{u}=id_{P(u)}\otimes\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{(u,(u,1),(u,2))}

(26)

is a quasi-conditional expectation w.r.t. the triplet
\mathcal{A}_{P(x)}\subset\mathcal{A}_{P(x)\cup\{x\}}\subset\mathcal{A}_{P(x)\cup\{x\}\cup S(x)}
.

Let
\varphi_{\alpha}
be the quantum Markov chain associated with
\{E^{\alpha}_{u}\}
. Then

\varphi_{\alpha}(a)=\alpha^{2^{n}-1}\mathrm{Tr}\bigg(a\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}K_{[i,i+1]}K_{[i,i+1]}^{*}\bigg),\ \ \forall a\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{[0,n]}}.

(27)

where
K_{[i,i+1]}=\prod_{u\in\Lambda_{i}}A_{(u,(u,1),(u,2))}
.

The state

associated with the quasi-conditional expectations

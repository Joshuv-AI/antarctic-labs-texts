On the regularity theory for mixed local and nonlocal quasilinear parabolic equations
Prashanta Garain and Juha Kinnunen

Abstract
. We consider mixed local and nonlocal quasilinear parabolic equations of p p -Laplace type and discuss several regularity properties of weak solutions for such equations. More precisely, we establish local boundeness of weak subsolutions, lower semicontinuity of weak supersolutions as well as upper semicontinuity of weak subsolutions. We also discuss the pointwise behavior of the semicontinuous representatives. Our main results are valid for sign changing solutions. Our approach is purely analytic and is based on energy estimates and the De Giorgi theory.

1.
Introduction

We discuss regularity properties of weak solutions
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
for the mixed local and nonlocal quasilinear parabolic equation

(1.1)

\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{L}u(x,t)-\operatorname{div}\mathcal{B}(x,t,u,\nabla u)=g(x,t,u)\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

where
T>0
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
, with
N\geq 2
, is a bounded domain (i.e. bounded, open and connected set) and
\mathcal{L}
is an integro-differential operator of the form

(1.2)

\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=\text{P.V.}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(x,y,t,u(x,t),u(y,t)\big)K(x,y,t)\,dx\,dy\,dt,

where
P.V.
denotes the principal value and
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is measurable with respect to
(x,y,t)
and continuous with respect to
(u(x,t),u(y,t))
satisfying the growth condition

(1.3)

\begin{split}C_{1}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}\big(u(x,t)-u(y,t)\big)&\leq\mathcal{A}\big(x,y,t,u(x,t),u(y,t)\big)\\
&\leq C_{2}|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}\big(u(x,t)-u(y,t)\big)\end{split}

for some positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
.
We assume that
1<p<\infty
, unless otherwise mentioned.
The kernel
K
is symmetric in
x
and
y
such that, for some
0<s<1
and
\Lambda\geq 1
, we have

(1.4)

\frac{\Lambda^{-1}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\leq K(x,y,t)\leq\frac{\Lambda}{|x-y|^{N+ps}},

for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t\in(0,T)
. Here
\mathcal{B}(x,t,u,\zeta):\Omega\times(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a measurable function with respect to
(x,t)
and continuous with respect to
(u,\zeta)
such that

(1.5)

\mathcal{B}(x,t,u,\zeta)\zeta\geq C_{3}|\zeta|^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad|\mathcal{B}(x,t,u,\zeta)|\leq C_{4}|\zeta|^{p-1}

for almost every
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)
and for every
(u,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. We assume that the source function
g:\Omega\times(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is measurable with respect to
(x,t)
and continuous with respect to
u
which satisfies

(1.6)

|g(x,t,u)|\leq\alpha|u|^{l-1}+|h(x,t)|,

for every
(x,t,u)\in\Omega\times(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}
, where
1<l\leq\max\{2,p(1+\frac{2}{N})\}
,
\alpha\geq 0
and
h
is an integrable function to be made precise later.

There are several interesting equations that satisfy the conditions (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and (
1.5
).
The leading example of (
1.1
) is the mixed local and nonlocal parabolic
p
-Laplace equation

(1.7)

\partial_{t}u+a(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u-b\Delta_{p}u=0\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

with
a>0
and
b>0
. This equation is obtained by choosing

(1.8)

\mathcal{A}(x,y,t,u(x,t),u(y,t))=|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t)),

(1.9)

K(x,y,t)=a|x-y|^{-N-ps}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}(x,t,u,\nabla u)=b|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u.

This kind of equation appears in image processing, Lévy processes etc, see Dipierro-Valdinoci
[
25
]
and the references therein.
For
\mathcal{A}
as in (
1.8
) and
a=1
in (
1.9
), the operator
\mathcal{L}
defined by (
1.2
) becomes the fractional
p
-Laplace operator
(-\Delta_{p})^{s}
.
For
b=1
in (
1.9
),

\operatorname{div}\big(\mathcal{B}(x,t,u,\nabla u)\big)=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=\Delta_{p}u

is the
p
-Laplace operator.
We would like to emphasize that (
1.1
) also extends the following mixed Finsler and fractional
p
-Laplace equation

(1.10)

\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=\Delta_{\mathcal{F},p}\,u\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T),

where

(1.11)

\Delta_{\mathcal{F},p}\,u=\text{div}\big(\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)\big),

is the Finsler
p
-Laplace operator, with
\nabla_{\eta}
denoting the gradient operator with respect to the
\eta
variable. Here
\mathcal{F}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,\infty)
is the Finsler-Minkowski norm,
that is,
\mathcal{F}
is a nonnegative convex function in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
such that
\mathcal{F}(\eta)=0
if and only if
\eta=0
, and
\mathcal{F}
is even and positively homogeneous of degree
1
, so that

(1.12)

\mathcal{F}(t\eta)=|t|\mathcal{F}(\eta)\quad\text{for every $\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$ and $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

Then, it follows that
\mathcal{B}(x,t,u,\nabla u)=\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)^{p-1}\nabla_{\eta}\mathcal{F}(\nabla u)
satisfies the hypothesis (
1.5
), see Xia
[
41
, Chapter
1
]
. Various examples of Finsler-Minkowski norm
\mathcal{F}
can be found in, for example in Belloni-Ferone-Kawohl
[
4
]
, Xia
[
41
, p. 22–23]
and the references therein. A typical example of
\mathcal{F}
includes the
l^{q}
-norm defined by

(1.13)

\mathcal{F}(\eta)=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\eta_{i}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad q>1,

where
\eta=(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\ldots,\eta_{N})
. When
\mathcal{F}
is the
l^{q}
-norm as in (
1.13
), we have

(1.14)

\Delta_{\mathcal{F},p}\,u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\bigg(\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{k}}\Big|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{p-q}{q}}\Big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\Big|^{q-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\bigg).

For
q=2
in (
1.14
),
\Delta_{\mathcal{F},p}
becomes the usual
p
-Laplace operator
\Delta_{p}
. Moreover, for
q=p
in (
1.14
), the operator
\Delta_{\mathcal{F},p}
reduces to the pseudo
p
-Laplace operator, see Belloni-Kawohl
[
5
]
and therefore, equation (
1.10
) covers a wide range of mixed local and nonlocal problems and in particular, extends the following mixed pseudo and fractional
p
-Laplace equation

(1.15)

\partial_{t}u+(-\Delta_{p})^{s}u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(\Big|\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\Big|^{p-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\Big)\text{ in }\Omega\times(0,T).

Functional setting and auxiliary results

2.1.
Notation

The following notation will be used throughout the paper.
We denote the positive and negative parts of

by

The conjugate exponent of

The barred integral sign

We write

The dependencies of the constant
C

are indicated as
C=C(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{k})

For

of radius

\mathcal{Q}_{r,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0})=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\theta r^{p},t_{0}+\theta r^{p})

If

\mathcal{Q}_{r}(x_{0},t_{0})=\mathcal{Q}_{r,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0})
.
Moreover, we write
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)
with

.

2.2.
Sobolev spaces

Let
1<p<\infty
and assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an open connected set. Recall that the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(\Omega)
is the set of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}<\infty
, where

\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

If
0<p\leq 1
, we denote by
L^{p}(\Omega)
to be the set of measurable functions
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx<\infty
. We say that
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
if
u\in L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime})
for every
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
.
For
1<p<\infty
, the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
is defined by

W^{1,p}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}<\infty\},

where

\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
with zero boundary value is defined by

W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)=\{u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega):u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\}.

We present some known theory of fractional Sobolev spaces, see Di Nezza-Palatucci-Valdinoci
[
18
]
for more details.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
0<s<1
. Assume that
\Omega
is an open and connected set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is defined by

W^{s,p}(\Omega)=\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega):\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}<\infty\},

where

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\left(\int_{\Omega}|u(x)|^{p}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The fractional Sobolev space with zero boundary value is defined by

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)=\{u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\}.

For
0<s\leq 1
, the space
W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
is defined by requiring that a function belongs to
W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime})
for every
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
.
Here
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
denotes that
\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}
is a compact subset of
\Omega
.
The Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
and
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
, with
1<p<\infty
and
0<s\leq 1
, are reflexive Banach spaces, see
[
18
,
36
,
26
]
.

The next result asserts that the classical Sobolev space is continuously embedded in the fractional Sobolev space, see
[
18
, Proposition 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and assume that
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. There exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s)
such that

||u||_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}\leq C||u||_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}

for every
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.

The following result for the fractional Sobolev spaces with zero boundary value follows from
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<s<1
and assume that
\Omega
is a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. There exists a constant
C=C(N,p,s,\Omega)
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dx\,dy\leq C\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{p}\,dx

for every
u\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
Here we consider the zero extension of
u
to the complement of
\Omega
.

Next we introduce a tail space and a mixed parabolic tail that will be used throughout the paper. We refer to Brasco-Lindgren-Schikorra
[
10
]
for tail space.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
m_{1}>0
and
m_{2}>0
. We define a tail space
L_{m_{1}}^{m_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by

(2.1)

L_{m_{1}}^{m_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\bigg\{u\in L^{m_{2}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|^{m_{2}}}{1+|y|^{N+m_{1}}}\,dy<\infty\bigg\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{L_{m_{1}}^{m_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(y)|^{m_{2}}}{1+|y|^{N+m_{1}}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{m_{2}}}.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
and the interval
I=[t_{0}-T_{1},t_{0}+T_{2}]\subset(0,T)
.
We define the mixed parabolic tail by

(2.2)

\begin{split}\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(u;x_{0},r,I)&=\bigg(r^{p}\esssup_{t\in I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})}\frac{|u(y,t)|^{p-1}}{|y-x_{0}|^{N+ps}}\,dy\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.\end{split}

From the definitions above, it is clear that for any
v\in L^{\infty}(I,L^{p-1}_{ps}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, the parabolic tail
\mathrm{Tail}_{\infty}(v;x_{0},r,I)
is well defined.
For short, we write

\mathcal{A}\big(x,y,t,u(x,t),u(y,t)\big)=\mathcal{A}(u(x,y,t)).

Now we are ready to define the notion of weak solutions for the problem (
1.1
).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
g
satisfies the hypothesis (
1.6
) for
1<l\leq\max\{2,p(1+\frac{2}{N})\}
and
h\in L^{l^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega_{T})
. We say that
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\big)\cap C_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;L^{p-1}_{ps}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)
is a weak subsolution (or supersolution) of (
1.1
) if for every
\Omega^{\prime}\times(t_{1},t_{2})\Subset\Omega_{T}
and for every nonnegative
\phi\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})\big)\cap W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})\big)
, we have

(2.3)

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}u(x,t_{2})\phi(x,t_{2})\,dx-\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}u(x,t_{1})\phi(x,t_{1})\,dx-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}u(x,t)\partial_{t}\phi(x,t)\,dx\,dt\\
&+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathcal{A}\big(u(x,y,t)\big)\big(\phi(x,t)-\phi(y,t)\big)K(x,y,t)\,dx\,dy\,dt\\
&+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\mathcal{B}(x,t,u,\nabla u)\nabla\phi(x,t)\,dx\,dt\leq(\text{ or }\geq)\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}g(x,t,u)\phi(x,t)\,dx\,dt.\end{split}

Moreover, we say that

is a weak solution of (
1.1
) if the equality holds in (
2.3
) for every
\phi\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})\big)\cap W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime})\big)
without a sign restriction.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

and

Assume that
\Omega

(a)

If

(b)

Moreover, if
u\in L^{p}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}\big(0,T;L^{m}(\Omega)\big)
, then there exists a constant
C=C(p,m,N)>0

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{q}\,dx\,dt\leq C\bigg(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}\,dx\,dt\bigg)\bigg(\esssup_{0<t<T}\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)|^{m}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{p}{N}}.

For
a,k\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the functions
\zeta_{+}
and
\zeta_{-}

Lemma 2.10

Let

4.2.

Assume that
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega_{T}
and
r\in(0,1)
,
\theta>0
such that
\mathcal{Q}_{r,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0})=B_{r}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\theta r^{p},t_{0}+\theta r^{p})\Subset\Omega_{T}
. Let
a,\mu^{-}
and
M
be defined as in (
4.3
). Then the following De Giorgi type lemma shows that a weak supersolution satisfies the property
(\mathcal{D})
in Definition
4.5
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
1<p<\infty,\,0<s<1
and
g\equiv 0
in
\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\big)\cap C_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}\big(0,T;L^{p-1}_{ps}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\big)
is a weak supersolution of (
1.1
) in
\Omega_{T}
such that
u
is essentially bounded below in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)
and let

(4.9)

\lambda^{-}\leq\essinf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)}\,u.

Then there exists a constant
\nu=\nu(a,M,\mu^{-},\lambda^{-},\theta,N,p,s,\Lambda,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4})\in(0,1)
, such that if

|\{u\leq\mu^{-}+M\}\cap\mathcal{Q}_{r,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0})|\leq\nu|\mathcal{Q}_{r,\theta}(x_{0},t_{0})|,

then

u\geq\mu^{-}+aM\text{ a.e. in }\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{3r}{4},\theta}(x_{0},t_{0}).

Proof.

For
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, let

(4.10)

\begin{split}&k_{j}=\mu^{-}+aM+\frac{(1-a)M}{2^{j}},\quad\hat{k}_{j}=\frac{k_{j}+k_{j+1}}{2},\\
&r_{j}=\frac{3r}{4}+\frac{r}{2^{j+2}},\quad\hat{r}_{j}=\frac{r_{j}+r_{j+1}}{2},\\
&B_{j}=B_{r_{j}}(x_{0}),\quad\hat{B}_{j}=B_{\hat{r}_{j}}(x_{0}),\\
&\Gamma_{j}=(t_{0}-\theta r_{j}^{p},t_{0}+\theta r_{j}^{p}),\quad\hat{\Gamma}_{j}=(t_{0}-\theta\hat{r}_{j}^{p},t_{0}+\theta\hat{r}_{j}^{p})\\
&\mathcal{Q}_{j}=B_{j}\times\Gamma_{j},\quad\mathcal{\hat{Q}}_{j}=\hat{B}_{j}\times\hat{\Gamma}_{j},\quad A_{j}=\mathcal{Q}_{j}\cap\{u\leq k_{j}\}.\end{split}

Notice that
r_{j+1}<\hat{r}_{j}<r_{j}
,
k_{j+1}<\hat{k}_{j}<k_{j}
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and therefore, we have

B_{j+1}\subset\hat{B}_{j}\subset B_{j}
and
\Gamma_{j+1}\subset\hat{\Gamma}_{j}\subset\Gamma_{j}
.
Let
\{\Phi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{j})
be such that

(4.11)

0\leq\Phi_{j}\leq 1,\quad|\nabla\Phi_{j}|\leq C\frac{2^{j}}{r}\text{ in }\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi_{j}\equiv 1\text{ in }\mathcal{Q}_{j+1},

for some constant
C=C(N,p)>0
. Notice that, over the set
A_{j+1}=\mathcal{Q}_{j+1}\cap\{u\leq k_{j+1}\}
, we have
\hat{k}_{j}-k_{j+1}\leq\hat{k}_{j}-u
.
By integrating over the set
A_{j+1}
and using Lemma
2.8
(b), we obtain

(4.12)

\begin{split}(1-a)\frac{M}{2^{j+3}}|A_{j+1}|&\leq\int_{A_{j+1}}(\hat{k}_{j}-k_{j+1})\,dx\,dt\leq\int_{\mathcal{Q}_{j+1}}(\hat{k}_{j}-u)\,dx\,dt\leq\int_{\mathcal{\hat{Q}}_{j}}(u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}\Phi_{j}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq\bigg(\int_{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}}\big((u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}\Phi_{j}\big)^{p(1+\frac{2}{N})}\,dx\,dt\bigg)^{\frac{N}{p(N+2)}}|A_{j}|^{1-\frac{N}{p(N+2)}}\\
&\leq C(I+J)^{\frac{N}{p(N+2)}}\hat{K}^{\frac{1}{N+2}}|A_{j}|^{1-\frac{N}{p(N+2)}},\end{split}

for some constant
C=C(N,p)>0
, where

I=\int_{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}}|\nabla(u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}|^{p}\,dx\,dt,\quad J=\int_{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}}(u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}^{p}|\nabla\Phi_{j}|^{p}\,dx\,dt\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{K}=\esssup_{t\in\hat{\Gamma}_{j}}\int_{\hat{B}_{j}}(u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}^{2}\,dx.

Note that, due to the assumption (
4.9
), we know
\lambda^{-}\leq\essinf_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T)}\,u
, which gives

(4.13)

(u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}\leq(\mu^{-}+M-\lambda^{-})_{+}:=L\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T).

Estimate of
J
:
Using the properties of
\Phi_{j}
and (
4.13
), we get

(4.14)

\begin{split}J&=\int_{\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{j}}(u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}^{p}|\nabla\Phi_{j}|^{p}\,dx\,dt\leq C\frac{2^{jp}}{r^{p}}L^{p}|A_{j}|,\end{split}

for some constant
C=C(N,p)>0
.

Estimate of
I
and
\hat{K}
:
Let
\xi_{j}=\psi_{j}\eta_{j}
, where
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{j})
and
\{\eta_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset C_{c}^{\infty}(\Gamma_{j})
be such that

(4.15)

\begin{split}&0\leq\psi_{j}\leq 1,\quad|\nabla\psi_{j}|\leq C\frac{2^{j}}{r}\text{ in }B_{j},\quad\psi_{j}\equiv 1\text{ in }\hat{B}_{j},\quad\mathrm{dist}(\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{j},\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{j})\geq 2^{-j-1}r,\\
&0\leq\eta_{j}\leq 1,\quad|\partial_{t}\eta_{j}|\leq C\frac{2^{pj}}{\theta r^{p}}\text{ in }\Gamma_{j},\quad\eta_{j}\equiv 1\text{ in }\hat{\Gamma}_{j},\end{split}

for some constant
C=C(N,p)>0
. Note that
g\equiv 0
in
\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}
and for
\hat{k}_{j}<k_{j}
, we have
(u-k_{j})_{-}\geq(u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}
. Therefore, noting Lemma
2.9
and Remark
3.5
, we set
r=r_{j}
,
\tau_{1}=t_{0}-\theta\hat{r}_{j}^{p}
,
\tau_{2}=t_{0}+\theta r_{j}^{p}
,
\tau=\theta r_{j}^{p}-\theta\hat{r}_{j}^{p}
in Lemma
3.3
to obtain

(4.16)

\begin{split}&I+\hat{K}=\int_{\hat{\Gamma}_{j}}\int_{\hat{B}_{j}}|\nabla(u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}|^{p}\,dxdt+\esssup_{\hat{\Gamma}_{j}}\int_{\hat{B}_{j}}(u-\hat{k}_{j})_{-}^{2}\,dx\leq I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4},\end{split}

where

\begin{split}I_{1}&=C\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}{\max\{(u-k_{j})_{-}(x,t),(u-k_{j})_{-}(y,t)\}^{p}|\xi_{j}(x,t)-\xi_{j}(y,t)|^{p}}\,d\mu\,dt,\\
I_{2}&=C\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}^{p}|\nabla\xi_{j}|^{p}\,dx\,dt,\\
I_{3}&=C\esssup_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{j},\,t\in\Gamma_{j}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{j}}}{\frac{(u-k_{j})_{-}(y,t)^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}}\,dy\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}\xi_{j}^{p}\,dx\,dt\quad\text{and}\\
I_{4}&=C\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}^{2}|\partial_{t}\xi_{j}^{p}|\,dx\,dt,\end{split}

for some constant
C=C(N,p,s,\Lambda,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4})>0
.

Estimate of
I_{1}
:
Using (
4.13
), the properties of
\xi_{j}
and
r\in(0,1)
, we have

(4.17)

\begin{split}I_{1}&=C\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}{\max\{(u-k_{j})_{-}(x,t),(u-k_{j})_{-}(y,t)\}^{p}|\xi_{j}(x,t)-\xi_{j}(y,t)|^{p}}\,d\mu\,dt\\
&\leq C\frac{2^{jp}}{r^{ps}}L^{p}|A_{j}|\leq C\frac{2^{jp}}{r^{p}}L^{p}|A_{j}|,\end{split}

C=C(N,p,s,\Lambda,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4})>0
.

Estimate of
I_{2}
:
Again, using (
4.13
) and the properties of
\xi_{j}
, we deduce that

(4.18)

\begin{split}I_{2}&=C\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}^{p}|\nabla\xi_{j}|^{p}\,dx\,dt\leq C\frac{2^{jp}}{r^{p}}L^{p}|A_{j}|,\end{split}

C=C(N,p,s,\Lambda,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4})>0
.

Estimate of
I_{3}
:
Without loss of generality, we assume that
x_{0}=0
. Then for every
x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{j}
and every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{j}
, we observe that

(4.19)

\frac{1}{|x-y|}=\frac{1}{|y|}\frac{|x-(x-y)|}{|x-y|}\leq\frac{1}{|y|}(1+2^{j+3})\leq\frac{2^{j+4}}{|y|}.

Using (
4.13
),
r\in(0,1)
and
0\leq\xi_{j}\leq 1
, we have

(4.20)

\begin{split}I_{3}&=C\esssup_{(x,t)\in\mathrm{supp}\,\psi_{j},\,t\in\Gamma_{j}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{j}}}{\frac{(u-k_{j})_{-}(y,t)^{p-1}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}}\,dy\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}\xi_{j}^{p}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\frac{2^{j(N+p)}}{r^{p}}L^{p}|A_{j}|,\end{split}

for some constant
C=C(N,p,s,\Lambda,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4})>0
.

Estimate of
I_{4}
:
Using (
4.13
) along with the properties of
\xi_{j}
, we have

(4.21)

\begin{split}I_{4}&=C\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}^{2}|\partial_{t}\xi_{j}^{p}|\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\frac{2^{jp}}{\theta r^{p}}\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}(u-k_{j})_{-}^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\frac{2^{jp}}{\theta r^{p}}L^{2}|A_{j}|,\end{split}

for some constant
C=C(N,p,s,\Lambda,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4})>0
.
Inserting (
4.17
), (
4.18
), (
4.20
) and (
4.21
) in (

), we arrive at

(4.22)

\begin{split}I+\hat{K}\leq C\frac{2^{j(N+p)}}{r^{p}}L^{p}\Big(1+\frac{L^{2-p}}{\theta}\Big)|A_{j}|,\end{split}

for some constant
C=C(N,p,s,\Lambda,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4})>0
. Using (
4.14
) and (
4.22
) in (
4.12
), we obtain

(4.23)

\begin{split}|A_{j+1}|\leq C\frac{2^{j((N+p)^{2}+1)}L^{\frac{N+p}{N+2}}}{(1-a)M}\Big(1+\frac{L^{2-p}}{\theta}\Big)^{\frac{N+p}{p(N+2)}}\frac{|A_{j}|^{1+\frac{1}{N+2}}}{r^{\frac{N+p}{N+2}}},\end{split}

for some constant
C=C(N,p,s,\Lambda,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4})>0
. Dividing both sides of (
4.23
) by
|\mathcal{Q}_{j+1}|
and denoting by
Y_{j}=\frac{|A_{j}|}{|\mathcal{Q}_{j}|}
, we get

Y_{j+1}\leq 2Kb^{j}Y_{j}^{1+\delta},

where

K=\frac{C(\theta L^{N+p})^{\frac{1}{N+2}}}{2(1-a)M}\Big(1+\frac{L^{2-p}}{\theta}\Big)^{\frac{N+p}{p(N+2)}},\quad\delta=\frac{1}{N+2}\quad\text{and}\quad b=2^{(N+p)^{2}+1}>1.

We define
\nu=(2K)^{-\frac{1}{\delta}}b^{-\frac{1}{\delta^{2}}}
, which depends on

such that if

, then by Lemma

, we have
\lim_{j\to\infty}Y_{j}=0
. This completes the proof.

\begin{split}J_{1}&=C\Bigg(\int_{\Gamma_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}\int_{B_{j}}{\max\{(u-k_{j})_{-}(x,t),(u-k_{j})_{-}(y,t)\}^{p}|\xi_{j}(x,t)-\xi_{j}(y,t)|^{p}}\,d\mu\,dt,\\

4.18

4.20

4.27

4.29

Lemma 4.11
.

For

(4.32)

\begin{split}&k_{j}=\mu^{+}-aM-\frac{(1-a)M}{2^{j}}\end{split}

Estimate of

:

and (

Hilbert schemes of points on surfaces and multiple q q -zeta values
Mazen M. Alhwaimel, Zhenbo Qin

Abstract
. For a line bundle L L on a smooth projective surface X X and nonnegative integers k 1 , … , k N k_{1},\ldots,k_{N} , Okounkov [ 19 ] introduced the reduced generating series ⟨ ch k 1 L ⋯ ch k N L ⟩ ′ \big\langle{\rm ch}_{k_{1}}^{L}\cdots{\rm ch}_{k_{N}}^{L}\big\rangle^{\prime} for the intersection numbers among the Chern characters of the tautological bundles over the Hilbert schemes of points on X X and the total Chern classes of the tangent bundles of these Hilbert schemes, and conjectured that they are multiple q q -zeta values of weight at most ∑ i = 1 N ( k i + 2 ) \sum_{i=1}^{N}(k_{i}+2) . The second-named author further conjectured in [ 22 ] that these reduced generating series are quasi-modular forms if the canonical divisor of X X is numerically trivial. In this paper, we verify these two conjectures for ⟨ ch 2 L ⟩ ′ \big\langle{\rm ch}_{2}^{L}\big\rangle^{\prime} . The main approaches are to apply the procedure laid out in [ 23 ] and to establish various identities for multiple q q -zeta values and quasi-modular forms.

In the region

The integers

Multiple zeta values are series of the form

Multiple

[

,
5
,

For instance, we have

\displaystyle Z(4)=\sum_{n>0}\frac{q^{2n}}{(1-q^{n})^{4}},\quad Z(6)=\sum_{n>0}\frac{q^{3n}}{(1-q^{n})^{6}}.

(1.3)

The weight of

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
, and fix a subset
S\subset\mathbb{N}
.

(i)

Let
Q=\{Q_{s}(t)\}_{s\in S}

Q_{s}(0)=0
and
Q_{s}(1)\neq 0

s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\in S

with
\ell\geq 1

Z_{Q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{\ell}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{Q_{s_{i}}(q^{n_{i}})}{(1-q^{n_{i}})^{s_{i}}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[[q]].

Put
Z_{Q}(\emptyset)=1
, and define
Z(Q,S)
to be the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linear span
of the set

\{Z_{Q}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})|\,\ell\geq 0\text{ and }s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\in S\}.

(ii)

Define
\mathcal{MD}=Z(Q^{E},\mathbb{N})
where
Q^{E}=\{Q_{s}^{E}(t)\}_{s\in\mathbb{N}}

and

Q_{s}^{E}(t)=\frac{tP_{s-1}(t)}{(s-1)!}

with
P_{s}(t)
being the Eulerian polynomial defined by

\displaystyle\frac{tP_{s-1}(t)}{(1-t)^{s}}=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}d^{s-1}t^{d}.

(2.1)

We have
tP_{0}(t)=t
.
For

, the polynomial
tP_{s-1}(t)
has degree
s-1
.
For
s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\in\mathbb{N}
with
\ell\geq 1
,
define the Bachmann-Kühn series

\displaystyle[s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}]=Z_{Q^{E}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell})=\sum_{n_{1}>\cdots>n_{\ell}\geq 1}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{Q_{s_{i}}^{E}(q^{n_{i}})}{(1-q^{n_{i}})^{s_{i}}}.

(2.2)

(iii)

Define
\text{q}\mathcal{MD}
be the subspace of

linearly
spanned by

[s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}]
with
s_{1}>1
.

(iv)

Q_{s}^{O}(t)=\begin{cases}t^{s/2}&\text{if $s\geq 2$ is even;}\\
t^{(s-1)/2}(t+1)&\text{if $s\geq 3$ is odd.}\end{cases}

,

\displaystyle(s-1)!\cdot\frac{Q_{s}^{E}(t)}{(1-t)^{s}}=\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}d^{s-1}t^{d}.

\displaystyle(s-1)!\cdot[s]=(s-1)!\cdot\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{Q_{s}^{E}(q^{n})}{(1-q^{n})^{s}}=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}d^{s-1}q^{nd}

(2.4)

More generally, for

\displaystyle[s_{1},\ldots,s_{\ell}]=\frac{1}{(s_{1}-1)!\cdots(s_{\ell}-1)!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1}>\cdots>n_{\ell}\geq 1\\
d_{1},\ldots,d_{\ell}\geq 1\end{subarray}}d_{1}^{s_{1}-1}\cdots d_{\ell}^{s_{\ell}-1}q^{n_{1}d_{1}+\ldots+n_{\ell}d_{\ell}}.

(2.5)

Some examples of the Bachmann-Kühn series

Some examples of the Okounkov series

3.
Hilbert schemes of points on surfaces

In this section, we will review the Hilbert schemes of points
on surfaces and the geometric construction of
the Heisenberg operators by Grojnowski and Nakajima.
Furthermore, we will recall the concepts of generalized partitions
and the Chern character operators.

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex surface,
and
{X^{[n]}}
be the Hilbert scheme of
n
points in
X
.
An element in
{X^{[n]}}
is represented by a
length-
n

0
-dimensional closed subscheme
\xi
of
X
. For
\xi\in{X^{[n]}}
, let
I_{\xi}
be the corresponding sheaf of ideals. It
is well known that
{X^{[n]}}
is irreducible and smooth with dimension
2n
.
Define the universal codimension-
2
subscheme:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}=\{(\xi,x)\subset{X^{[n]}}\times X\,|\,x\in{\rm Supp}{(\xi)}\}\subset{X^{[n]}}\times X.

Denote by
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
the projections of
{X^{[n]}}\times X
to

{X^{[n]}}
and
X
respectively. A line bundle
L
on
X
induces
the tautological rank-
n
bundle
L^{[n]}
over
{X^{[n]}}
:

\displaystyle L^{[n]}=(p_{1}|_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}})_{*}\big(p_{2}^{*}L|_{\mathcal{Z}_{n}}\big).

(3.1)

Let
{\mathbb{H}}_{X}
be the direct sum of the cohomology groups of the Hilbert schemes
{X^{[n]}}
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{H}}_{X}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}H^{*}({X^{[n]}}).

For
m\geq 0
and
n>0
, let
Q^{[m,m]}=\emptyset
and define

Q^{[m+n,m]}
to be the closed subset:

\{(\xi,x,\eta)\in X^{[m+n]}\times X\times X^{[m]}\,|\,\xi\supset\eta\text{ and }\mbox{Supp}(I_{\eta}/I_{\xi})=\{x\}\}.

We recall from
[
13
,
18
]
the geometric construction of
the Heisenberg operators on the space
{\mathbb{H}}_{X}
.
Let
\alpha\in H^{*}(X)
. Set
\mathfrak{a}_{0}(\alpha)=0
.
For an integer
n>0
, the operator
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}(\alpha)\in{\rm End}({\mathbb{H}}_{X})
is
defined by

\mathfrak{a}_{-n}(\alpha)(a)=\tilde{p}_{1*}([Q^{[m+n,m]}]\cdot\tilde{\rho}^{*}\alpha\cdot\tilde{p}_{2}^{*}a)

for
a\in H^{*}(X^{[m]})
, where
\tilde{p}_{1},\tilde{\rho},\tilde{p}_{2}
are the projections of
X^{[m+n]}\times X\times X^{[m]}
to
X^{[m+n]},X,X^{[m]}
respectively. Define

\mathfrak{a}_{n}(\alpha)\in{\rm End}({\mathbb{H}}_{X})
to be
(-1)^{n}
times the
operator obtained from the definition of
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}(\alpha)
by switching the roles of
\tilde{p}_{1}
and
\tilde{p}_{2}
.
We often refer to
\mathfrak{a}_{-n}(\alpha)
(resp.
\mathfrak{a}_{n}(\alpha)
)
as the
creation
(resp.

) operator.
The following is from
[
13
,
18
]
. Our convention of the sign follows
[
17
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The operators
\mathfrak{a}_{n}(\alpha)
satisfy
the commutation relation:

\displaystyle\displaystyle{[\mathfrak{a}_{m}(\alpha),\mathfrak{a}_{n}(\beta)]=-m\;\delta_{m,-n}\cdot\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle\cdot{\rm Id}_{{\mathbb{H}}_{X}}}.

The space
{\mathbb{H}}_{X}
is an irreducible module over the Heisenberg
algebra generated by the operators
\mathfrak{a}_{n}(\alpha)
with a
highest weight vector
|0\rangle=1\in H^{0}(X^{[0]})\cong\mathbb{C}
.

In the above theorem, the Lie bracket is understood in the super
sense according to the parity of the degrees of the
cohomology classes involved, and

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=\int_{X}\alpha\beta.

When
\alpha
and
\beta
are divisors on
X
, we also use

\alpha\beta
to stand for
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle
.

It follows from
Theorem
3.1
that the space
{\mathbb{H}}_{X}
is linearly
spanned by all the Heisenberg monomials
\mathfrak{a}_{n_{1}}(\alpha_{1})\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{n_{k}}(\alpha_{k})|0\rangle

where
k\geq 0
and
n_{1},\ldots,n_{k}<0
.

Definition 3.2
.

(i)

A
generalized partition
of an integer

\lambda=(\cdots(-2)^{m_{-2}}(-1)^{m_{-1}}1^{m_{1}}2^{m_{2}}\cdots)

such that part

has multiplicity
m_{i}
,
m_{i}\neq 0
for
only finitely many
i

. Define

\ell(\lambda)=\sum_{i}m_{i}
,
|\lambda|=\sum_{i}im_{i}
,

|\lambda|_{+}=\sum_{i>0}im_{i}
,
s(\lambda)=\sum_{i}i^{2}m_{i}
,
and
\lambda^{!}=\prod_{i}m_{i}!
.

(ii)

Let
\alpha\in H^{*}(X)
and
k\geq 1
. Define

The following commutation relations can be found in

\displaystyle[\Gamma_{+}(L,x),\Gamma_{+}(L^{\prime},y)]=[\Gamma_{-}(L,x),\Gamma_{-}(L^{\prime},y)]=0,

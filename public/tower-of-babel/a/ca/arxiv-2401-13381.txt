Abstract
. We analyze p p -Laplace operators with degenerate elliptic coefficients. This investigation includes Grušin type p p -Laplace operators. We describe a separation phenomenon in elliptic and parabolic p p -Laplace type equations, which provides an illuminating illustration of simple jump discontinuities of the corresponding weak solutions. Interestingly validity of an isoperimetric inequality for considered setting does not imply continuity of elliptic equations. On the other hand, we are able to establish global L 1 L^{1} - L ∞ L^{\infty} -regularization and decay estimates of every mild solution of the parabolic Grušin type p p -Laplace equation.

Introduction

Studies of problems involving the
p
-Laplace operator are a central and
crucial part of the theory of Partial Differential Equations
(PDEs). Such operators are second order non-linear generalizations of
the standard Laplace operator, which is arguably fundamental and the
most significant linear elliptic operator. The
p
-Laplace equation has
attracted intense attention during the last fifty or so years and its
theory is by now developed and well understood, see
e.g.
[
31
,
20
,
21
,
1
]
and reference within.

Our main interest in this project is related to
p
-Laplace operators
corresponding to degenerate elliptic systems and geometry. It is a
worthy attention point because
p
-Laplace operators is of degenerate or singular form themselves. Indeed, in the Euclidean
\mathbb{R}^{d}
setting these
operators can be written down as

\Delta_{p}=\nabla\cdot\left(\lvert\nabla f\rvert^{p-2}\nabla f\right),

where
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\nabla f
is the standard Euclidean gradient,
compare (
20
) below. Clearly, for
p>2
this
divergence equation is degenerate when
|\nabla f|=0
, and becomes
singular in region where
\nabla f
vanishes. Hence considering
the degenerate elliptic system and the corresponding gradients for

p
-Laplace operators is especially interesting, which it plays a
crucial role in nonlinear potential theory (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
).

We specifically investigate (generalized) Grušin type
p
-Laplace
operators. Classical Grušhin-type operators are a class of linear
degenerate elliptic operators (cf. Example
2.1

i.)
)
that arise in the study of PDEs and functional inequalities (for
examples, in the Euler equation associated associated with a weighted
Sobolev inequality, see, e.g.,
[
32
]
or

[
23
]
). These operators often have specific forms and
properties that make them significant objects of our study. In the
classical Grušin space setting, the linear case

has been
studied extensively (see, e.g.,
[
33
,
35
,
37
,
22
]
), but
only a few results have been worked out in the general case

(see, e.g.,
[
6
,
30
]
). In
generalized Grušin
space
setting (see Example
2.1

ii.)

even in the linear case

only a few but crucial results are known;
these include the validity of a Nash inequality (see
[

) and

[
36

). To the best of our knowledge, similar results

In this article, we aim to change this by proving Nash and Sobolev

2.
Some preliminaries - our framework

The purpose of this section is to outline how to introduce the geometry
into the elliptic and parabolic
p
-Laplace type equations. For this, we
use the notion of Riemannian structures
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
.

2.1.
A Riemannian structure

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let the pair
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
consisting of the Euclidean
space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a symmetric, positive semi-definite matrix function

\bm{A}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}).

In the following, we refer to such a pair as a
Riemannian
structure
.

In order to explain the class of degenerate
diffusion operators of
p
-Laplace type

\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A}}f:=\nabla\cdot\left(\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}^{p-2}\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\right),

induced by a Riemannian structure
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
, we briefly summarize
the most relevant notions from Riemannian geometry. To do this, we begin
by considering the regular case.

2.1.1.
The elliptic setting.

If the matrix
\bm{A}
is assumed to be positive-definite on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
written
\bm{A}(x)>0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or more precisely,

(1)

\xi^{T}\bm{A}(x)\xi>0\qquad\text{for all
$x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$, $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}$.}

Then, the pair
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{g})
consisting of the Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the function
\bm{g}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\inner(\mathbb{R}^{d})
given by

(2)

\bm{g}(x)=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\bm{A}(x)}\qquad\text{for every $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,}

forms a classical
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold with (Riemannian)

metric

\bm{g}
. Here
\inner(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the set of inner
products on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The manifold
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{g})
is determined through
the matrix
\bm{A}
. Note, in (
2
) the inner products

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\bm{A}(x)}
are defined by

(3)

\langle X,Y\rangle_{\bm{A}(x)}:=\langle\bm{A}^{-1}(x)X,Y\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}

for every
X
,
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for
X
,
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the bracket

\langle X,Y\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}X_{i}\overline{Y}_{i}
refers
to the classical Euclidean inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and we write

\lvert X\rvert
or
\lvert X\rvert_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}=\sqrt{\langle X,X\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}}
to
denote the induced
Euclidean norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Remark
.

It is worth mentioning that in the framework outlined here, the
Euclidean inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}
could
be replaced by any other inner product provided by some

d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold.

Notation 2.1
.

The associated
norm

\lvert\cdot\rvert_{\bm{A}}
induced by the
matrix
\bm{A}
is then given by

(4)

\lvert X\rvert_{\bm{A}}=\sqrt{\langle X,X\rangle_{\bm{A}}}=\lvert\bm{B}^{-1}X\rvert_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}

where the matrix
\bm{B}
is the so-called
square root
of

\bm{A}
, meaning
\bm{B}
is the unique matrix of
\bm{A}
satisfying

(5)

\bm{B}^{2}(x)=\bm{A}(x)

for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Note, if
\bm{A}(x)>0
then also
\bm{B}
satisfies
\bm{B}(x)>0
.

2.1.2.
The gradient associated with a Riemannian structure

Next,
we introduce the notion of the gradient associated with a Riemannian
structure
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
. For this note, we write either

\nabla_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}
or, simply,
\nabla
and

\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}
in order to denote the
gradient

and the
partial derivatives
with respect to standard
Euclidean
coordinates.

Definition 2.1
.

For a given vector-field
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, that is,

X=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}

for some coefficients
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}\in\mathbb{R}
, and for every

f\in C^{1}
, the
action of
X
on
f
at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined by

Xf_{|x}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(x).

Remark
.

For given every
f\in C^{1}
and vector-field
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
the action
Xf_{|x}
in
local coordinates
can be
interpreted as

(6)

Xf_{|x}=\langle\nabla f(x),X\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}

Note, throughout this section, when we speak about
local
coordinates
then we mean
Euclidean coordinates
.

Definition 2.2
.

For a given Riemannian structure
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
and

f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we call
\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f
defined by

(7)

\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f(x),X\rangle_{\bm{A}(x)}=Xf_{|x}

for every vector-field
X
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the

gradient of
f
associated with the Riemannian structure

(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
, or shorter,
gradient of
f
associated with

\bm{A}
.

Applying (
6
) to (
7
), one sees that for
given
f\in C^{1}
, the gradient
\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f
in local
coordinates can be characterized by

\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f,X\rangle_{\bm{A}}=\langle\nabla f,X\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}

for every vector field
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and so, (
3
) yields that

\langle\bm{A}^{-1}\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f,X\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}=\langle\nabla f,X\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}

for every vector field
X\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

. Therefore, we have the following description of the gradient

\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f
in local coordinates.

Proposition 2.1

(The Gradient in local coordinates)
.

For a given Riemannian structure
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
and
f\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the
gradient

\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f
in local
coordinates can be rewritten by

(8)

\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f=\bm{A}\nabla f

Now, by the definition (
3
) of the inner products

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\bm{A}}
, one has that

\displaystyle\lvert\nabla_{\!\!\bm{A}}f\rvert^{2}_{\bm{A}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f,\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rangle_{\bm{A}}

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{A}^{-1}(\bm{A}\nabla f),\bm{A}\nabla f\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla f,\bm{A}\nabla f\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}

\displaystyle=\langle\bm{B}\nabla f,\bm{B}\nabla f\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}

\displaystyle=\lvert\bm{B}\nabla f\rvert^{2}_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}

for every
f\in C^{1}
. This shows, we have the following
characterization of the length of the gradient
\nabla_{\!\!\bm{A}}f
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For a given Riemannian structure
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
and
f\in C^{1}
,
one has that the
length
(associated with
\bm{A}
)

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}
of the gradient

\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f
is equivalent to

(9)

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}=\sqrt{\langle\nabla f,\bm{A}\nabla f\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}}

Moreover, in local coordinates

(10)

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}=\lvert\bm{B}\nabla f\rvert_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}=\sqrt{\langle\bm{B}\nabla f,\bm{B}\nabla f\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}}.

For convenience, we introduce the following notation.

Notation 2.2
.

For a given Riemannian structure
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
and
f\in C^{1}
, we set

\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}f=\bm{B}\nabla f,

where the matrix
\bm{B}
is the square root of
\bm{A}
.

The characterization (
10
) of the length

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}
in local coordinates in terms of the
square root
\bm{B}
of
\bm{A}
has the advantage to relax the
requirement
\bm{A}
being invertible.

Suppose,
\bm{A}=(a_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{d}

degenerate

For integers

coordinates is given by

(15)

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}^{2}=|x|^{\alpha}_{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\right\rvert^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x\rvert_{n}^{\beta_{j}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\right\rvert^{2}.

Note, the case
\alpha=0
is considered in the classical
Grušin type spaces mentioned above. In Section

8
, we consider the case
d=1+m
for

0\leq\alpha<2
and
\beta_{1}
, …,
\beta_{m}>0
. In this case,
the structure
\bm{A}
from (
14
) reduces to

\bm{A}=\begin{bmatrix}|x_{1}|^{\alpha}\,&0\\
0&\begin{bmatrix}|x_{1}|^{\beta_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&|x_{1}|^{\beta_{2}}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&|x_{1}|^{\beta_{m}}\end{bmatrix}_{m\times m}\end{bmatrix},

and the length
\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}
in local
coordinates is given by

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}^{2}=|x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\right\rvert^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}|x_{1}|^{\beta_{j}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y_{j}}\right\rvert^{2}.

This structure
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
leads to a separation phenomenon for

2\leq p<\infty
,
\frac{2}{p^{\mbox{}_{\prime}}}\leq\alpha<2
.

We emphasize that the case
\alpha\geq 2
is geometrically not
relevant because of geometric reasons. Indeed, consider a curve

\gamma\colon[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
connecting points
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
defined
by
\gamma(t)=t
. Then, obviously,
\gamma^{\prime}(t)=1
and according to
(
4
),

|\gamma^{\prime}(x)|_{\bm{A}(x)}=\langle 1,1\rangle_{\bm{A}(x)}^{1/2}=|x|^{-\alpha/2}

for every
x\in[a,b]
. Hence, the distance

(16)

d_{A}(a,b)=\int_{a}^{b}|\gamma^{\prime}(x)|_{\bm{A}(x)}\,dx=\int_{a}^{b}|x|^{-\alpha/2}dx

is finite for every
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
if
0<\alpha<2
, and

d_{A}(0,a)=\infty
for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
if
\alpha\geq 2
.

iii.)

(
Further generalization
) Let
d\geq 1
and
K
be a

(d-1)
-dimensional hyperplane in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
d_{K}
is the
distance defined in (
11
) then, for given exponents

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}\geq 0
, let
\bm{A}
be the diagonal
matrix defined by

\bm{A}=\begin{bmatrix}d_{K}^{\alpha_{1}}&0&0&0&\cdots&0\\
0&d_{K}^{\alpha_{2}}&0&0&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&0&d_{K}^{\alpha_{3}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&\cdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&\ddots&\ddots&0&d_{K}^{\alpha_{d}}\end{bmatrix}.

One easily verifies that the matrix
\bm{A}
satisfies
Assumption
2.1
with exponent

\gamma=\min\{\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}\}
.

iv.)

(
Heisenberg Group
) Let
d=3
and
\bm{A}
be the matrix given by

\bm{A}(x,y,z)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&y/2\\
0&1&-x/2\\
y/2&-x/2&(x^{2}+y^{2})/2\end{bmatrix}.

For this matrix, the gradient
\nabla_{\bm{A}}f
is the, in fact, the
horizontal gradient
associated with the
Heisenberg group
(cf., for instance,
[
14
]
). Note, the matrix
\bm{A}
does not satisfy Assumption
2.1
introduced in this paper. It is an interesting example of a sub-Riemannian structure
(\mathbb{R}^{3},\bm{A})
, but goes beyond the discussion of this paper. See for example the monograph
[
34
]
by Ricciotti concerning elliptic regularity theory of the
p
-Laplace equation in the Heisenberg group, and we refer to the recent regularity result
[
13
]
by Capogna, Citti, and Zhong for the parabolic
p
-Laplace equation in the Heisenberg group setting.

v.)

(
Spaces with monomial weights
) Now, for integers
d\geq 1
, and for
0\leq\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{d}<1
, let

\bm{A}
be the
d\times d
-diagonal matrix given by

(17)

\bm{A}=\begin{bmatrix}|x_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&|x_{2}|^{\alpha_{2}}&\ddots&0\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0\\
0&\cdots&0&|x_{d}|^{\alpha_{d}}\end{bmatrix},

where as before
|\cdot|
denotes the absolute value. It is easy
to see that

\displaystyle\xi^{T}\bm{A}(x)\xi

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}}\lvert\xi_{i}\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{|x_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}},\dots,|x_{d}|^{\alpha_{d}},1\}\,|\xi|^{2}

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus, the matrix
\bm{A}
satisfies Assumption
2.1
, and the
associated gradient
\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f
is given by

\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f=\begin{bmatrix}|x_{1}|^{\alpha_{1}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}},\dots,|x_{d}|^{\alpha_{d}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{d}}\end{bmatrix}.

This example of
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
was, for instance, mentioned in
[
12
]
by Cabré and Ros-Oton and in connection with other Sobolev inequalities with monomial weights. It is worth mentioning that as the degenerate Riemannian structure
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
discussed in Example ii.), also
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
given by (
17
) leads to a
separation phenomena
at
\{x_{i}=0\}
for every
i=1,\dots,d
provided
1\leq\alpha_{i}<2
. For further details to this topic, we refer to Section
8
.

vi.)

(
Poincaré half-plane
) Let
d=2
and
\bm{A}
be the diagonal matrix

\bm{A}=\begin{bmatrix}x^{2}&0\\
0&x^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Then
\bm{A}
is symmetric and satisfies hypothesis (
12
) with equality; namely, one has that

\xi^{t}\bm{A}\xi=x^{2}\xi_{1}^{2}+x^{2}\xi_{2}^{2}=x^{2}\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}.

Moreover, the associated gradient

\nabla_{\!\!\bm{A}}f=\begin{bmatrix}x^{2}&0\\
0&x^{2}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}f_{x}\\
f_{y}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}x^{2}\,f_{x}&x^{2}\,f_{y}\end{bmatrix}.

This example shows well that the matrix
\bm{A}
decides on the length of the geodesics. In fact, the metric
\bm{g}
induced by the matrix
\bm{A}
(cf (
2
)) coincides with the metric of two copies of the
Poincaré half-plane
.

3.
Lebesgue and mixed Sobolev spaces

Throughout this section, let
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
be a Riemannian structure
equipped with matrix functions
\bm{A}
satisfying the Assumption

2.1
.

The aim of this section is to outline the Lebesgue and

1^{\textrm{st}}
-order Sobolev spaces, which are used later on.

Notation 3.1
.

For
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and a given open subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we denote by
L^{q}(\Omega)
the standard Lebesgue
space with respect to the Lebesgue measure restricted on an open subset

\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and by
\lVert f\rVert_{q}
the associated norm. Further,
W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
refers to the space of all
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
with distributional partial derivative
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
for every
i=1,\dots,d
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq p
,
q\leq\infty
, and
\Omega
an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, we denote by
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
the
1^{\textrm{st}}
-order mixed
Sobolev space
of all
functions
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,1}_{loc}(\Omega)
such that

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. We equip the space

W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
with the sum norm

\lVert f\rVert_{W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)}=\lVert f\rVert_{q}+\lVert\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}\rVert_{p}.

Further, we denote by
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
the closure of the set
of test-functions
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
.

Notation 3.2
.

If
\bm{A}=\mathbb{I}_{d\times d}
is the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, then we
simply write
W^{1,(q,p)}(\Omega)
instead of

W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\mathbb{I}_{d\times d}}(\Omega)
. Further, if
q=p
then we write

W^{1,q}_{\bm{A}}(\Omega)
instead of
W^{1,(q,q)}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
1\leq p<\infty
. Then, the following statements
hold.

(1.)

The
1^{\textrm{st}}
-order mixed
Sobolev space
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
is a Banach space. In
particular,
W^{1,2}_{\bm{A}}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space.

(2.)

For
1<q
,
p<\infty
,
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
is reflexive.

(3.)

For
1\leq q
,
p<\infty
,
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
is separable.

Proof.

We begin by showing that
(1.)
holds. Let

(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a Cauchy sequence in

W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
. Since the space

W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into

L^{q}(\Omega)
, and since
L^{q}(\Omega)
is complete, there is a
function
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
such that
f_{n}\to f
in

L^{q}(\Omega)
. Now, for every

i=1,\,\dots,\,n
, and
m\geq 1
, set

(18)

\Omega_{m}=\Big\{x\in\Omega\,\Big|\,\min_{i=1,\dots,n}d_{K_{i}}(x)\geq 2^{-m},\;\lvert x\rvert<2^{m}\Big\}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. Since by assumption,
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a
Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
, there is an

N=N(\varepsilon)\geq 1
such that

\lVert\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f_{n_{1}}-\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f_{n_{2}}\rvert_{\bm{A}}\rVert_{p}<\varepsilon

for every integer
n_{1}
,
n_{2}\geq N
and so, by
(
9
) and (
12
), one see that

(19)

\tfrac{1}{2^{\frac{m}{2}p}}\int_{\Omega_{m}}\lvert\nabla(f_{n_{1}}-f_{n_{2}})\rvert^{p}_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{\Omega_{m}}\lvert\bm{B}\nabla(f_{n_{1}}-f_{n_{2}})\rvert^{p}_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon^{p}

for every integer
n_{1}
,
n_{2}\geq N
and every
m\geq 1
. Since

W^{1,(q,p)}(\Omega_{m})
is complete,
f_{n}\to f
in

L^{q}(\Omega)
, and since
\bm{B}
belongs to

L^{\infty}(\Omega_{m};\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
, we can conclude that

f\in W^{1,(q,p)}(\Omega_{m})
, and

\bm{B}\nabla f_{n}\to\bm{B}\nabla f
in

L^{p}(\Omega_{m};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, sending

n_{1}\to\infty
in

\int_{\Omega_{m}}\lvert\bm{B}\nabla(f_{n_{1}}-f_{n_{2}})\rvert^{p}_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon^{p}

yields that

\int_{\Omega_{m}}\lvert\bm{B}\nabla(f-f_{n_{2}})\rvert^{p}_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\varepsilon^{p}

for every
n_{2}\geq N
and every
m\geq 1
. By construction, we have that

\Omega_{m}\subseteq\Omega_{m+1}\quad\text{ and }\quad\bigcup_{m\geq 1}\Omega_{m}=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}

and
\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}K_{i}
has Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{d}
zero.
Thus, sending
m\to\infty
in the last inequality lead to

\lVert\lvert\nabla(f-f_{n_{2}})\rvert_{\bm{A}}\rVert_{p}\leq\varepsilon

for every
n_{2}\geq N
, implying that

f\in W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
and that

f_{n}\to f
in
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
. Since

(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
was an arbitrary Cauchy sequence, we have thereby
shown that
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
is Banach space. In particular,

W^{1,2}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
equipped with the inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{W^{1,2}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)}:=\int_{\Omega}f\,g\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}f,\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}g\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\mathrm{d}x

for every
f
,
g\in W^{1,2}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space.

Next, we outline the proof of claim
(3.)
by following
the standard argument (cf,
[
11
, Proposition 8.1]
). The
product space
E:=L^{q}(\Omega)\times L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is
reflexive if
1<q
,
p<\infty
, and separable if
1<q
,

p<\infty
. Further, the mapping

T:W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)\to E
defined by

Tf:=(f,\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}f)
is an isometry and by
(1.)
,
the image
T(W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega))
is a closed subspace of

E
. Thus, the fact that the space
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega)
is
reflexive and separable follows from the reflexivity and separability
of
T(W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A}}(\Omega))
.
∎

After introducing Sobolev spaces with respect to a Riemannian structure

(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
, it be natural to mention valid Poincaré and Sobolev
inequalities in this setting. But, Assumption
2.1
is too general
for the validity of such inequalities.

4.
Degenerate p-Laplace operators

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
be a Riemannian structure equipped with matrix
functions
\bm{A}
satisfying the Assumption
2.1
. Further, let

\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1<p<\infty
.

Then, the main object of this section is the
so-called
p
-Laplace operator

(20)

\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A}}f:=\nabla\cdot(\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert^{p-2}_{\bm{A}}\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f)\qquad\text{in $\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)$}

associated with
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
for
f\in W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A},loc}(\Omega)
, equipped with
homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
on
\partial\Omega
.

To realize the
2^{\textrm{nd}}
-order differential operator
\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A}}
, we consider the functional
\mathcal{E}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to[0,+\infty]
given by

(21)

\mathcal{E}(f):=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{p}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla_{\!\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x&\text{if $f\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)$,}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

for every
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Under the assumptions of this section, the functional
\mathcal{E}
given
by (
21
) is proper, densely defined, convex and lower
semi-continuous on
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

In the following, the matrix
\bm{B}
denotes the square root of

\bm{A}
as introduced in Notation
2.1
.

Proof.

The bulk of the proof of this proposition has been done in
Proposition
3.1
. Thus the reminding argument is quite
standard (see, for
instance,
[
17
,
15
,
26
,
25
]
). For
the sake of completeness, we provide the details. Let
\alpha>0
and

(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence in

D(\mathcal{E})=W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
converging in
L^{2}(\Omega)

to an element
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and satisfying

\mathcal{E}(f_{n})\leq\alpha
for all
n\geq 1
. Since the sequence

(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is bounded in
W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)

Proposition
3.1
yields that

f\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
and
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
admits
a subsequence, which, for simplicity, we denote again by itself, such
that
\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}f_{n}\rightharpoonup\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}f

weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, by (
10
) one has
that

\mathcal{E}(f)=\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}f\rvert_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}f\rvert_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq\alpha.

Therefore,
\mathcal{E}
is lower semi-continuous on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. The fact
that
\mathcal{E}
is densely defined follows from the fact that the set

C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
is contained in

D(\mathcal{E})=W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!A,0}(\Omega)
and dense in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

Next, we intend to compute the sub-gradient
\partial\mathcal{E}
in

L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Proposition 4.2
.

The restriction
\mathcal{E}_{|W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}}
of the
functional
\mathcal{E}
given by (
21
) is of the class
C^{1}
and its
sub-gradient operator
\partial\mathcal{E}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
is a
well-defined mapping
\partial\mathcal{E}:D(\partial\mathcal{E})\to L^{2}(\Omega)

given by

\partial\mathcal{E}(f)=-\nabla\cdot(\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert^{p-2}_{\!\bm{A}}\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f)\qquad\text{in
$L^{2}(\Omega)$}

for every
f\in D(\partial\mathcal{E})
, where the domain

D(\partial\mathcal{E})=\Big\{f\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)\,\Big|\,\begin{array}[c]{c}\exists\,h\in L^{2}(\Omega)\text{ s.t. }\forall\,\xi\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega),\\
\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f),\xi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}h\,\xi\,\mathrm{d}x\end{array}\Big\}.

Proof.

Recall that the matrix
\bm{B}
is symmetric and satisfies

\bm{B}^{2}=\bm{A}
. Moreover, one has that (
9
) and
(
10
). Thus, proceeding as in the classical case

\bm{A}=\mathbb{E}_{d\times d}
the identity matrix in

\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
(see, for instance,
[
25
]
) yields that the
restriction
\mathcal{E}_{|W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}}
of the functional

\mathcal{E}
given by (
21
) is continuously differentiable with
Fréchet-derivative

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f),\xi\rangle_{(W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0})^{\prime},W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}f\rvert^{p-2}_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}f,\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}\xi\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert^{p-2}_{\bm{A}}\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f,\nabla\xi\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x

for every
f\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
.

Now, let
f\in D(\partial\mathcal{E})
. Then,

f\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
and there is an element

h\in L^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying

(22)

\langle h,g-f\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mathcal{E}(g)-\mathcal{E}(f)\qquad\text{for every
$g\in L^{2}(\Omega)$}

Since
\mathcal{E}
is convex and differentiable, choosing
g=f+t\xi

in (
22
) for given
t>0
and

\xi\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
yields that

\langle h,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\inf_{t>0}\frac{\mathcal{E}(f+t\xi)-\mathcal{E}(f)}{t}=\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f),\xi\rangle_{(W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0})^{\prime},W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}}.

Further, taking
t<0
gives

\langle h,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{\mathcal{E}(f+t\xi)-\mathcal{E}(f)}{t}

and subsequently, letting
t\to 0-
leads to

(23)

\langle h,\xi\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f),\xi\rangle_{(W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0})^{\prime},W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}}.

Since (
23
) holds for every
\xi\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
,
h\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is the
unique extension of the distribution
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
This completes the proof of this proposition.
∎

The preceding proposition leads to the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

For a given Riemannian structure
(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
with
\bm{A}

satisfying Assumption
2.1
, we call the
2
nd-order
differential operator
\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A}}
given by
(
20
) the
p
-Laplace operator
associated
with
\bm{A}
. Further, for a given open subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
we call the operator

-\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}f:=\partial\mathcal{E}(f)\qquad\text{for every $f\in D(\partial\mathcal{E})$,}

where
\mathcal{E}
denotes the functional defined by (
21
), the

Dirichlet
p
-Laplace operator
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
associated
with
\bm{A}
.

4.1.
Examples of p-Laplace operators

As an application of the above given theory, we
consider the following example. We begin with
dimension
d=1
.

Example 4.1
.

Let
K=\{0\}
, and
\Omega=(a,b)\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be an open interval for
given
-\infty<a<0<b<\infty
. Then,
d_{K}(x)=\lvert x\rvert
for every

x\in\mathbb{R}
,
and the matrix
\bm{A}
has only one coefficient; namely,

\bm{A}=\Big[\lvert x\rvert^{\alpha}\Big]

for some given exponent
0<\alpha<2
. According to
(
8
), the associated gradient

\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f(x)=\lvert x\rvert^{\alpha}f^{\prime}(x):=\lvert x\rvert^{\alpha}\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}x}(x)

for every
x\in\Omega
and
f\in C^{1}
. Furthermore, the
functional
\mathcal{E}
introduced in (
21
) in
local coordinates

(see (
10
)) reduces to

(24)

\mathcal{E}(f)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{p}\int_{a}^{b}\lvert\lvert x\rvert^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f^{\prime}(x)\rvert^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x&\text{if $f\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(a,b)$,}\\[4.0pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

for every
f\in L^{2}(a,b)
and according to Proposition
4.1
,
the
Dirichlet
p
-Laplace operator

\Delta^{\!\bm{A}}_{p}
on

\Omega
associated with the Riemannian structure

(\mathbb{R},\lvert x\rvert^{\beta})
has the form

in

, and

\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}T_{n}(f)\to\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\qquad\text{in $L^{p}(\Omega)$.}

Next, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let

\Omega_{k}:=\{x\in\Omega\,|\,\dist(x,\partial\Omega)>\frac{1}{k}\}\cap\{|x|<k\}
.
Then,
\Omega_{k}\subseteq\Omega_{k+1}
,

\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega_{k}=\Omega
. Without loss of generality, we may
assume that
\Omega_{1}\neq\emptyset
. Then, let

\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
satisfy
0\leq\rho\leq 1
,
\rho\equiv 1

on the closure
\overline{\Omega_{1}}
,

\supp(\rho)\subseteq\Omega_{2}
, and

\lVert\rho\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=1
. Further, for every
k\geq 1
, set

\rho_{k}(x):=\rho(x/k)
for every
x\in\Omega
. Then, one has that

\rho_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
,
0\leq\rho_{k}\leq 1
,
\rho_{k}\equiv 1

on the closure
\overline{\Omega_{k}}
, and

\supp(\rho_{k})\subseteq\Omega_{2k}
. Hence, the test-function

T_{n}(f)\rho_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
and has compact support in

\Omega
. Moreover, for every compact subset
K
of
\Omega
, there is a

k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
K\subseteq\Omega_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
, and so

T_{n}(f)\rho_{k}=T_{n}(f)
for all
k\geq k_{0}
and
n\geq 1
. Therefore,

T_{n}(f)\rho_{k}\to f
in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
as
n
,
k\to\infty
. In addition,

\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}\big[T_{n}(f)\rho_{k}\big]=\bm{A}\nabla f\,\mathds{1}_{\{\lvert f\rvert\leq n\}}\,\rho_{k}+\frac{1}{k}\bm{A}\nabla\rho(\cdot/k)\,T_{n}(f)

for every
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Thus,

\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}\big[T_{n}(f)\rho_{k}\big]\to\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\qquad\text{in $L^{p}(\Omega)$}

as
n
,
k\to\infty
.

Now, let
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with compact

\supp(f)\subseteq\Omega
and

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Further, let

(\chi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of truncator functions given by

\chi_{n}(x_{1},y_{1},\dots,y_{m})=\begin{cases}0&\quad\text{if $x_{1}\leq\frac{1}{n}$,}\\
\frac{\log x_{1}+\log n}{\log n}&\quad\text{if $\frac{1}{n}<x_{1}\leq 1$,}\\
1&\quad\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

for every
x=(x_{1},y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then,
\chi_{n}(x)\to\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}(x)
as
n\to\infty
for every
x\in\Omega
, and by
Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem,
f\chi_{n}\to f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}
in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. In addition, one has that

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\left(f\chi_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\,\chi_{n}+\frac{1}{x_{1}\,\log n}\,f\,\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega\cap\{\frac{1}{n}<x_{1}<1\}},

\displaystyle\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\left(f\chi_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\,\chi_{n},

and so,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}\left(f\chi_{n}\right)(x)=(\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}f)(x)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}(x)

for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Next, let
n\geq 2
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f\chi_{n})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\left[|x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}}\left(f\chi_{n}\right)\right\rvert^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{j}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\left(f\chi_{n}\right)\right\rvert^{2}\right]^{\frac{p}{2}}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\left[|x_{1}|^{\alpha}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\,\chi_{n}+\frac{1}{x_{1}\,\log n}\,f\,\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega\cap\{\frac{1}{n}<x_{1}<1\}}\right\rvert^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 170.71652pt\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{j}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\,\chi_{n}\right\rvert^{2}\right]^{\frac{p}{2}}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{p}\int_{\Omega}\left[|x_{1}|^{\alpha}2\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\right\rvert^{2}+2\,\left\lvert\frac{|x_{1}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{x_{1}\,\log n}\,f\,\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega\cap\{\frac{1}{n}<x_{1}<1\}}\right\rvert^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 184.9429pt\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{j}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\right\rvert^{2}\right]^{\frac{p}{2}}\!\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{C_{p}}{p}\int_{\Omega}\left[|x_{1}|^{\alpha\frac{p}{2}}2^{\frac{p}{2}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\right\rvert^{p}+2^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\left\lvert\frac{|x_{1}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}{x_{1}\,\log n}\,f\,\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega\cap\{\frac{1}{n}<x_{1}<1\}}\right\rvert^{p}\right.

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 184.9429pt\left.+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{j}\frac{p}{2}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\right\rvert^{p}\right]\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{C_{p}2^{\frac{p}{2}}}{p}\left[\int_{\Omega}|x_{1}|^{\alpha\frac{p}{2}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\right\rvert^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega\cap\{\frac{1}{n}<x_{1}<1\}}\frac{|x_{1}|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}p}}{|x_{1}|^{p}\,(\log n)^{p}}\,|f|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 173.56198pt+\tfrac{C_{p}}{p}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{j}\frac{p}{2}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\right\rvert^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\tfrac{C_{p}2^{\frac{p}{2}}}{p}\left[\int_{\Omega}|x_{1}|^{\alpha\frac{p}{2}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\right\rvert^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{1}\int_{\Omega\cap\{x_{1}=r\}}\frac{|r|^{\frac{\alpha}{2}p}}{r^{p}\,(\log n)^{p}}\,|f|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}r\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 173.56198pt+\tfrac{C_{p}}{p}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{j}\frac{p}{2}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\right\rvert^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{C_{p}2^{\frac{p}{2}}}{p}\left[\int_{\Omega}|x_{1}|^{\alpha\frac{p}{2}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}\right\rvert^{p}\mathrm{d}x+\frac{|\supp(f)|\,\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}}{(\log n)^{p}}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{1}|r|^{(\frac{\alpha}{2}-1)p}\,\mathrm{d}r\right]

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 173.56198pt+\tfrac{C_{p}}{p}\int_{\Omega}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lvert x_{1}\rvert^{\beta_{j}\frac{p}{2}}\left\lvert\tfrac{\partial f}{\partial y_{i}}\right\rvert^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x

Note, for
p>1
and
p-p\frac{\alpha}{2}-1\leq 0
, or, equivalently,

\alpha\geq\frac{2}{p^{\mbox{}_{\prime}}}
, one has that

(50)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{(\log n)^{p}}\int_{\frac{1}{n}}^{1}r^{p(\frac{\alpha}{2}-1)}\,\mathrm{d}r=0.

If
p=1
, then (
50
) holds provided
\alpha>0
. Therefore, we
have shown that

\mathcal{E}(f\chi_{n})\to\mathcal{E}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}})\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

Similarly, let

(\hat{\chi}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a sequence of truncator functions given by

\hat{\chi}_{n}(x_{1},y_{1},\dots,y_{m})=\begin{cases}1&\quad\text{if $x_{1}\leq-1$,}\\
\frac{\log(-x_{1})+\log n}{\log n}&\quad\text{if $-1<x_{1}\leq-\frac{1}{n}$,}\\
0&\quad\text{if $x_{1}\geq-\frac{1}{n}$,}\end{cases}

for every
x=(x_{1},y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, a similar
computation shows that

\mathcal{E}(f\hat{\chi}_{n})\to\mathcal{E}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}})\qquad\text{as $n\to\infty$}

provided
p>1
and
\alpha>\frac{2}{p^{\mbox{}_{\prime}}}
. Now, since

\mathcal{E}(f\hat{\chi}_{n}+f\chi_{n})=\mathcal{E}(f\hat{\chi}_{n})+\mathcal{E}(f\chi_{n}),

sending
n\to\infty
yields that (
49
). Next, let
f
,
g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with compact

\supp(f)\subseteq\Omega
,
\supp(g)\subseteq\Omega^{+}
, and
,
\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}g\rvert_{\bm{A}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{E}(f+tg)-\mathcal{E}(f)}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{E}((f+tg)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}})-\mathcal{E}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}})}{t}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{\mathcal{E}((f+tg)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}})-\mathcal{E}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}})}{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{E}((f+tg)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}})-\mathcal{E}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}})}{t}

for every
t>0
, from where we can conclude that

\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f),g\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}),g\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}\rangle

Similarly, one obtains that

\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f),g\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}}\rangle=\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}}),g\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}}\rangle

for
g
with
\supp(g)\subseteq\Omega^{-}
. Now, for general

g\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
with compact
\supp(g)\subseteq\Omega
and

\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{A}}g\rvert_{\bm{A}}\in L^{p}(\Omega)
, one has that

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f),g\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f),g\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f),g\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}),g\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}\rangle+\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}}),g\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}),g\rangle+\langle\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}}),g\rangle,

concluding the proof of this lemma.
∎

With the above given lemma, we can now outline the proof of the main
result of this section, namely, Theorem
8.1
.

Proof of Theorem
8.1
.

For every constant
c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
,
f=c\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega}

is a continuous weak solution of the Grušin-type
p
-Laplace
equation (
47
) on
\Omega
. But due to Lemma
8.2
,

f^{+}:=c\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}
and
f^{-}:=c\,\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}}

are also weak solution of the Grušin-type
p
-Laplace
equation (
47
) on
\Omega
, which are having a simple jump
discontinuity along
y_{j}
crossing
\{x_{1}=0\}
for every

j=1,\dots,m
.
∎

8.2.
The parabolic equation

This section is dedicated to illustrate the separation phenomenon on
parabolic problems driven by
p
-Laplace operator. For this it is worth
noting the followin comment. In the following the
homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition on the (outer) boundary
\partial\Omega

of a given open subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
could be replaced with any
other kind of boundary conditions.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and
\mathcal{E}^{\Omega}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to[0,\infty]

be the functional realizing the negative
Dirichlet
p
-Laplace
operator

-\Delta^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p}
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
for an open
subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(cf. Example
4.2
). We
denote by
\{e^{t\Delta^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p}}\}_{t\geq 0}
be the semigroup
generated by
\Delta^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p}
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Since the effective
domain
D(\Delta^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p})
is a subset of

W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
, functions

f\in D(\Delta^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p})
vanish at the boundary

\partial\Omega
in a
weak sense
. However, even if

\Omega\cap\{x_{1}=0\}\neq\emptyset
, then function

f\in D(\Delta^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p})
can have the property that

\supp(f)\cap\{x_{1}=0\}\neq\emptyset
.

For the rest of this final section, suppose that
\Omega
is an
open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\Omega\cap\{x_{1}=0\}\neq\emptyset
.
Further, set
\dot{\Omega}:=\Omega^{+}\dot{\cup}\Omega^{-}
. Then,
equivalently, one has that

\dot{\Omega}=\Omega\setminus\{x_{1}=0\}
. Thus, the functional

\mathcal{E}^{\dot{\Omega}}:L^{2}(\Omega)\to[0,\infty]
given by

\mathcal{E}^{\dot{\Omega}}(f):=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{p}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla_{\!\!\bm{A}}f\rvert_{\bm{A}}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x&\text{if $f\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\dot{\Omega})$,}\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

is well-defined for every
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. It also follows from
Proposition
4.1
that
\mathcal{E}^{\dot{\Omega}}
is convex, proper, and
lower semi-continuous functional on
L^{2}(\Omega)
realizing the
negative Dirichlet
p
-Laplace operator
-\dot{\Delta}^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p}

on
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Let
\{e^{t\dot{\Delta}^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p}}\}_{t\geq 0}
denote the semigroup
generated by
\dot{\Delta}^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p}
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

Here, it is worth mentioning that in contrary to the
operator
\Delta^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p}
, functions

f\in D(\dot{\Delta}^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p})
vanish at the boundary

\partial\Omega^{+}\cup\partial\Omega^{-}
in a
weak sense
.

Next, we aim to show
that for the two functionals, one has that

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
p^{\mbox{}_{\prime}}=p/(p-1)
, and for

0\leq\alpha<2
,
\beta_{1}
, …,
\beta_{m}\geq 0
, let

(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
be the Riemannian structure given by (
45
). Further, let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

be an open subset such that
\Omega\cap\{x_{1}=0\}\neq\emptyset
. If

\frac{2}{p^{\mbox{}_{\prime}}}\leq\alpha<2
, then one has
that the two spaces
W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
and

W^{1,(q,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\dot{\Omega})
coincide and

(51)

\mathcal{E}^{\dot{\Omega}}=\mathcal{E}^{\Omega}.

Proof.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Then the proof of Lemma
8.2

shows that

f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\bm{A},0}(\Omega^{\pm})
,
and since
\dot{\Omega}:=\Omega^{+}\dot{\cup}\Omega^{-}
, it follows that

f\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\dot{\Omega})
. Moreover, by
Lemma
8.2
, one has that (
49
) for every

f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Since

W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\dot{\Omega})
is closed, we can conclude
that

W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)\subseteq W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\dot{\Omega})

and, in particular, that (
49
) holds for every

f\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, one
immediately sees that the reverse inclusion of

W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\dot{\Omega})
into
W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)
always
holds. Thus, we have thereby shown that

W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\Omega)=W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\dot{\Omega})

and by construction of
\mathcal{E}^{\Omega}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\dot{\Omega}}
that
(
51
) needs to hold.
∎

Now, the following result, is our main result of this final section.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
p^{\mbox{}_{\prime}}=p/(p-1)
, and for

0\leq\alpha<2
,
\beta_{1}
, …,
\beta_{m}\geq 0
, let

(\mathbb{R}^{d},\bm{A})
be the Riemannian structure given by (
45
). Further, let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}

be an open subset such that
\Omega\cap\{x_{1}=0\}\neq\emptyset
. If

\frac{2}{p^{\mbox{}_{\prime}}}\leq\alpha<2
, then for every
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, one has that

(52)

e^{t\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}[f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}]=[e^{t\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}f]\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}

and

(53)

e^{t\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}f=[e^{t\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}f]\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{+}}+[e^{t\Delta_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}f]\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{-}}

for every
t>0
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
. Due to the immediate smoothing
effect (
30
), one has that

e^{t\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}f\text{ and }e^{t\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}[f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}]\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\!\bm{A},0}(\dot{\Omega})

for every
t>0
. Hence and by the proof of Lemma
8.2
, one has that

\left[e^{t\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}f\right]\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}\in W^{1,(2,p)}_{\bm{A},0}(\Omega^{\pm}).

For keeping the next computation clear, we set

u(t)=e^{t\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}[f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}]\quad\text{and}\quad v(t)=e^{t\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}f

for every
t\geq 0
. Then, one sees that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert u(t)-v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}u(t)-\left[\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}v(t)\right]\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}},u(t)-v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad=\left\langle\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}u(t),u(t)-v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 71.13188pt-\left\langle\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}v(t),u(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}-v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad=-\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}u(t)\right\rvert_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}^{p-2}\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}u(t),\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}(u(t)-v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}})\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}v(t)\right\rvert_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}^{p-2}\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}v(t),\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}(u(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}-v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}})\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad=-\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}u(t)\right\rvert_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}^{p-2}\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}u(t),\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}(u(t)-v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}})\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\mbox{}\hskip 42.67912pt+\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}\right\rvert_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}^{p-2}\langle\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}},\nabla_{\!\bm{B}}(u(t)-v(t)\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}})\rangle_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\quad\leq 0

for a.e.
t>0
, where used the monotonicity of the power function

x\mapsto\lvert x\rvert^{p-2}_{\!\mathrm{Euc}}x
. From this, we can conclude that

e^{t\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}[f\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}]=\left[e^{t\dot{\Delta}_{p}^{\!\bm{A},D}}f\right]\mathds{1}_{\Omega^{\pm}}

for every
t\geq 0
, and as by Lemma
8.3
, the
functionals
\mathcal{E}^{\Omega}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\dot{\Omega}}
satisfy
(
51
), the two semigroups

\{e^{t\Delta^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p}}\}_{t\geq 0}
and

\{e^{t\dot{\Delta}^{\!\bm{A},D}_{p}}\}_{t\geq 0}
coincide. From this,
we can conclude the statements of this theorem.
∎

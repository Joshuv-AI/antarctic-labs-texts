A proof of the n ! 2 \frac{n!}{2} conjecture for hook shapes
Sam Armon

Abstract
A well-known representation-theoretic model for the transformed Macdonald polynomial H ~ μ ​ ( Z , t , q ) \widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q) , where μ \mu is an integer partition, is given by the Garsia-Haiman module ℋ μ \mathcal{H}_{\mu} . We study the n ! k \frac{n!}{k} conjecture of Bergeron and Garsia, which concerns the behavior of certain k k -tuples of Garsia-Haiman modules under intersection. In the special case that μ \mu has hook shape, we use a basis for ℋ μ \mathcal{H}_{\mu} due to Adin, Remmel, and Roichman to resolve the n ! 2 \frac{n!}{2} conjecture by constructing an explicit basis for the intersection of two Garsia-Haiman modules.



1
Introduction

The Macdonald polynomials
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
have become a central object of study in algebra, combinatorics, and geometry since their introduction by Macdonald in
[
Mac88
]
. Of particular import is the expansion of
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
in the Schur basis:

\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)=\sum_{\lambda}\widetilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(t,q)s_{\lambda}(Z).

The (since resolved)
Macdonald positivity conjecture
asserts that
\widetilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(t,q)
is a polynomial in
t
and
q
with nonnegative integer coefficients. This conjecture suggests that
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
should have a representation-theoretic interpretation as the character of a doubly graded symmetric group module, and indeed such an interpretation is given by the
Garsia-Haiman module

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
:

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Hai01
]
)
.

The transformed Macdonald polynomial
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
equals the bigraded Frobenius series of the Garsia-Haiman module
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
.

In fact, Garsia and Haiman were able to show that in order to establish this connection, it would be enough to prove that
\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})=n!
. Thus the problem was reduced to this
n!
conjecture
, which was later resolved by Haiman (
[
Hai01
]
).

This paper studies the related
\frac{n!}{k}
conjecture
of Bergeron and Garsia, which makes a similar assertion about the dimension of the intersection of certain
k
-tuples of Garsia-Haiman modules:

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
BG
]
)
.

Let
\lambda
be an integer partition of
n+1
, and let
\mu^{(1)},\ldots,\mu^{(k)}
be partitions of
n
each obtained from
\lambda
by removing a removable cell from the Young diagram of
\lambda
. Then,

\dim\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}_{\mu^{(i)}}\right)=\frac{n!}{k}.

We resolve the
\frac{n!}{k}
conjecture in the special case that
\lambda
has hook shape — the only nontrivial case is
k=2
since a hook has at most two removable cells, hence the title. The rest of the paper is organized as follows: after outlining some necessary prerequisites in Section
2
, we introduce the Garsia-Haiman module
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
in Section
3
. In particular, we define a basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
when
\mu
has hook shape; each basis element is indexed by a
standard filling
of
\mu
, and is given by a monomial which encodes particular inversions in the standard filling. This basis is simply a restatement of the
k
-th Artin basis of
[
ARR
]
, using the language of standard fillings rather than permutations.

Equipped with this basis, we proceed in Section
4
to construct an explicit basis for the intersection of two Garsia-Haiman modules with hook shape by defining a bijective map between particular subsets of standard fillings which leaves the corresponding basis element unchanged. Our bijection makes use of two “Foata-like” maps which preserve particular inversions in the first row and column of a standard filling of hook shape.

Let
Z=z_{1},z_{2},\ldots
denote a countably infinite set of variables. The
symmetric group

\mathfrak{S}_{n}
is the group of permutations of
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
, and we say that a function
f\in\mathbb{Q}[Z]
is
symmetric
if it is unchanged under the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action given by permuting indices, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Q}[Z]
denote the subring of symmetric functions. Many bases for
\Lambda
are known, but perhaps the most famous (and most useful from a representation-theoretic point of view) is the basis of
Schur functions

\{s_{\mu}\}
, where
\mu
ranges over the collection of all integer partitions.

Macdonald (
[
Mac88
]
) defined an exceptional new basis for the ring
\Lambda_{(t,q)}
of symmetric functions with coefficients in the field
\mathbb{Q}(t.q)
which simultaneously generalizes the Schur functions, Hall-Littlewood polynomials, and Jack symmetric functions, among others. Macdonald’s polynomials were not given by an explicit formula — rather, they were characterized by particular triangularity and orthogonality relations with respect to a
(t,q)
-deformation of the Hall inner product — but a certain transformed version
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
of the Macdonald polynomial has an elegant combinatorial description, proved in
[
HHL
]
. For any integer partition
\mu
,
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
may be described as a
(t,q)
-weighted sum over fillings of
\mu
, where the
t
- and
q
-weights are given by combinatorial statistics on fillings which were originally defined by Haglund (
[
Hag04
]
). See
[
Hag04
]
or
[
HHL
]
for the precise definition of these statistics.

Moreover,
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
has an elegant expansion in the Schur basis:

\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)=\sum_{\lambda}\widetilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(t,q)s_{\lambda}(Z).

(1)

The coefficients
\widetilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(t,q)
are
a priori
rational functions in
t
and
q
, but Macdonald conjectured that
\widetilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(t,q)\in\mathbb{N}[t,q]
; this
Macdonald positivity conjecture
was resolved in

, by realizing

as the doubly graded character multiplicities of a certain doubly graded

3
Garsia-Haiman modules

Garsia and Haiman (
[
GH93
]
) proposed the following representation-theoretic interpretation for
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
. Consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]=\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};y_{1},\ldots,y_{n}]
along with the diagonal
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action given by permuting the
X
- and
Y
-variables simultaneously and identically; that is,

\sigma\cdot f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n};y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=f(x_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,x_{\sigma(n)};y_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,y_{\sigma(n)})

for any

and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
. For
\mu\vdash n
, define the polynomial

\Delta_{\mu}=\det(x_{i}^{p_{j}-1}y_{i}^{q_{j}-1})_{i,j=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{Q}[X,Y],

where
\{(p_{1},q_{1}),\ldots,(p_{n},q_{n})\}
encode the coordinates of the cells of
\mu
, taken in any order. For instance, we label the cells of
\mu=(3,2)
as follows:

\Delta_{\mu}
is an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-alternating polynomial which is doubly homogeneous of
X
-degree
b(\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell(\mu)}(i-1)\mu_{i}
and of
Y
-degree
b(\mu^{\prime})=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell(\mu^{\prime})}(j-1)\mu^{\prime}_{j}
. Let

Definition 3.1

(
[
GH93
]
)
.

The
Garsia-Haiman module
is the quotient ring

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}=\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]/\mathfrak{I}_{\mu}

viewed as a doubly graded
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module, with the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action induced by the diagonal action on
\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]
.

Since
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is doubly graded, we have a direct sum decomposition

\mathcal{H}_{\mu}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{b(\mu)}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{b(\mu^{\prime})}(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})_{i,j},

where
(\mathcal{H}_{\mu})_{i,j}
denotes the submodule spanned by elements of total
X
-degree
i
and total
Y
-degree
j
.

Garsia and Haiman conjectured that the
Frobenius series
of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
(defined below) is precisely
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
. Due to known identities involving the usual Kostka numbers
K_{\lambda\mu}
, it is necessary that
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
afford a doubly graded version of the regular representation of
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, and in particular that the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is
n!
, for this identity to hold. Strikingly, this apparently weaker condition is sufficient to establish the desired result:

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Hai99
]
)
.

If the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is
n!
, then the bigraded Frobenius series of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
given by

Frob_{\mathcal{H}_{\mu}}(Z;t,q)=\sum_{i,j}t^{i}q^{j}ch((\mathcal{H}_{\mu})_{i,j}),

where

is the map which sends the irreducible
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-representation
S^{\lambda}
to the Schur function
s_{\lambda}
, equals the transformed Macdonald polynomial
\widetilde{H}_{\mu}(Z;t,q)
.

Thus the problem was reduced to this comparatively simpler
n!
conjecture
, which was later resolved by Haiman:

Theorem 3.2

(
[
Hai01
]
)
.

The dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
is
n!
.

This in turn proves the Macdonald positivity conjecture, since it realizes
\widetilde{K}_{\lambda\mu}(t,q)
as the doubly graded multiplicity of
S^{\lambda}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. The proof of the
n!
conjecture in
[
Hai01
]
, however, relies on deep algebro-geometric results, and it remains an open problem to construct an explicit basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
in general.

Many bases for
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
are known when
\mu
has hook shape, however (see
[
ARR
]
,
[

[

]

[

). We opt to work with the

-th Artin basis of
[

, which was also known to Garsia and Haiman in
[

. Using the following terminology, we reframe this basis in a form which is more amenable to our later constructions.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
SF(\mu)
denote the set of standard fillings of
\mu

to be a pair

where

. Similarly, define a
column inversion
to be a pair
S_{j,1}>S_{i,1}
for

(note the swapped indices). Denote the collection of row (resp. column) inversions in

\displaystyle\mathsf{rowInv}(S)

\displaystyle=\{(t,r):t>r,t\text{ left of }r\text{ in row $1$ of }S\},

\displaystyle\mathsf{colInv}(S)

\displaystyle=\{(d,c):d>c,d\text{ above }c\text{ in column $1$ of }S\}.

Definition 3.3
.

Given

by

\varphi_{S}(X,Y)=\prod_{(d,c)\in\mathsf{colInv}(S)}x_{d}\prod_{(t,r)\in\mathsf{rowInv}(S)}y_{r}

(cf.
[

]
, Definition 1.6).

(

.

The set

Remark 3.1
.

The theory of orbit harmonics developed by Garsia and Haiman (
[
GH
]
) lays the groundwork for a new, direct proof that
\{\varphi_{S}\}
forms a basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
, which we sketch here. Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}
be distinct rational numbers. To any standard filling
S
we associate an
orbit point

p_{S}=(\alpha_{i_{1}},\ldots,\alpha_{i_{n}};\beta_{j_{1}},\ldots,\beta_{j_{n}})
, and a polynomial

\psi_{S}=\prod_{(d,c)\in\mathsf{colInv}(S)}(x_{d}-\alpha_{\mathsf{row}(c)})\prod_{(t,r)\in\mathsf{rowInv}(S)}(y_{r}-\beta_{\mathsf{col}(t)}),

so that
\varphi_{S}
is the leading term of
\psi_{S}
. Garsia and Haiman proved that if

1.

the matrix
(\psi_{S}(p_{T}))
is nonsingular, and

2.

the Hilbert series of
\{\psi_{S}\}
is symmetric,

then the leading terms
\{\varphi_{S}\}
form a basis for
\mathcal{H}_{\mu}
. There exists a total ordering on standard fillings under which
(\psi_{S}(p_{T}))
is upper-triangular with nonzero diagonal entries (hence nonsingular), and the symmetry of the Hilbert series is evidenced by the fact that
\varphi_{S}(t,q)=t^{n(\mu)}q^{n(\mu^{\prime})}\varphi_{\widetilde{S}}(t^{-1},q^{-1})
, where
\widetilde{S}
is obtained from
S
by replacing the entry
i
with
n-i+1
.

4
A proof of the
\frac{n!}{2}
conjecture for hook shapes

Now equipped with an explicit basis for
\mathcal{H}_{(a,1^{\ell})}
, we can study the remarkable intersection properties of these modules which were conjectured in
[
BG
]
. In full generality, the
\frac{n!}{k}
conjecture
is:

Conjecture 4.1

(
[
BG
]
)
.

Let
\lambda\vdash n+1
, and let
\mu^{(1)},\ldots,\mu^{(k)}
be partitions of
n
each be obtained from
\lambda
by removing a removable cell from the Young diagram of
\lambda

\dim\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}_{\mu^{(i)}}\right)=\frac{n!}{k}.

has hook shape; in particular, the only nontrivial case is

[

]

\lambda=(a,1^{\ell})
is a partition of

with

and

— the conjecture is trivial otherwise — and let
\mu=(a,1^{\ell-1}),\rho=(a-1,1^{\ell})
be partitions of

obtained from

by removing a cell from the end of the first column, and from the end of the first row, respectively.

(
\frac{n!}{2}

For partitions

\dim(\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\rho})=\frac{n!}{2}.

$$D_{\mu}=\text{span}\{f(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial x_{n}};\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{1}},\ldots,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial y_{n}})\Delta_{\mu}:f\in\mathbb{Q}[X,Y]\},$$the span of all partial derivatives of all orders of $\Delta_{\mu}$; the map $\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\to D_{\mu}$ given by $f(X,Y)\mapsto f(\frac{\partial}{\partial X},\frac{\partial}{\partial Y})\Delta_{\mu}$ is an isomorphism of doubly graded $\mathfrak{S}_{n}$-modules (see \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Hai99}{}{}]}, Proposition 3.4).
\par It is worth pointing out that the original $\frac{n!}{k}$ conjecture in \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{BG}{}{}]} is stated in terms of $D_{\mu}$, rather than $\mathcal{H}_{\mu}$. Our basis for $\mathcal{H}_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{H}_{\rho}$ does not immediately yield a basis for $D_{\mu}\cap D_{\rho}$ in the sense that
$$\{\varphi_{S}(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial X},\tfrac{\partial}{\partial Y})\Delta_{\mu}:S\in SF_{<}(\mu)\}\neq\{\varphi_{T}(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial X},\tfrac{\partial}{\partial Y})\Delta_{\rho}:T\in SF_{<}(\rho)\}.$$In fact, experimental evidence suggests that these sets are disjoint.
\par As the above isomorphism is bidegree-complementing, the naive/optimistic conjecture would be that the complements $SF_{>}(\mu)$ and $SF_{>}(\rho)$ index a basis for $D_{\mu}\cap D_{\rho}$. However, it is not true in general that $\varphi_{S}(\frac{\partial}{\partial X},\frac{\partial}{\partial Y})\Delta_{\mu}\in D_{\rho}$ for arbitrary $S\in SF_{>}(\mu)$ (and vice-versa), either. Thus it appears to be nontrivial to construct an explicit basis for $D_{\mu}\cap D_{\rho}$ in the present paradigm.

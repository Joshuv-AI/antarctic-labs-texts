Abstract
. Recently, there are a great deal of work done which connects the Legendrian isotopic problem with contact invariants. The isotopic problem of Legendre curve in a contact 3 3 -manifold was studies via the Legendrian curve shortening flow which was introduced and studied by K. Smoczyk. On the other hand, in the SYZ Conjecture, one can model a special Lagrangian singularity locally as the special Lagrangian cones in ℂ 3 \mathbb{C}^{3} . This can be characterized by its link which is a minimal Legendrian surface in the 5 5 -sphere. Then in these points of view, in this paper we will focus on the existence of the long-time solution and asymptotic convergnce along the Legendrian mean curvature flow in higher dimensional η \eta -Einstein Sasakian ( 2 ​ n + 1 ) (2n+1) -manifolds under the suitable stability condition due to the Thomas-Yau conjecture.

In

[

the difficulty which states that most of the special Lagrangian tori fibration
have singularities, one can model them locally as special Lagrangian cones

\mathbb{S}^{5}

On the other hand, the standard Sasakian sphere
(\mathbb{S}^{2n+1},\alpha)

is the compact contactization of the Kähler projective space

(\mathbb{CP}^{n},\omega)
. Then the projection of the mean curvature vector
field of a Legendrian submanifold

Lagrangian submanifold in

Legendrian submanifold in the contactization is an immersed minimal Lagrangian
submanifold in

We first mention the Legendrian isotopic problem which connects with a great

The mean curvature flow is perhaps the most important geometric evolution
equation of submanifolds in Riemannian and Kähler manifolds. Intuitively,
a family of smooth immersions
F_{t}:L\rightarrow(M,g)

\begin{array}[c]{c}\frac{d}{dt}F_{t}=H\end{array}

and

this equation which belongs to the most important equations in Geometric
Analysis. This evolution equation is one of the most important equations in
geometric analysis. There are many results on this equation. For the case of
hypersurfaces, we refer to
[

interesting class in higher codimension is the Lagrangian mean curvature flow
which preserves the class of Lagrangian submanifolds if the ambient manifold
is a Kähler-Einstein manifold (
[
S1

In the case of Legendrian
n

(2n+1)
-manifolds, in

not preserve the Legendrian condition. Then it is the first paper by Smoczyk

flow which preserves the Legendrian condition if the ambient manifold is an

\eta
-Einstein Sasakian manifold. The main idea is to minimize the volume
energy in the class of Legendrian immersions and then establish such a
Legendrian mean curvature flow for Legendre submanifolds.

It was conjectured by Thomas-Yau
[

]
that for a compact Lagrangian
submanifold with the zero Maslov class in a Calabi-Yau Kähler manifold,
under some stability conditions, the Lagrangian mean curvature flow exists for
all time and converges to a special Lagrangian submanifold in its hamiltonian
deformation class. It is constructed by Neves
[

that there is a

isotopic to a Clifford torus

Lagrangian mean curvature flow with surgery, undergoing at most a finite
number of surgeries before flowing to a minimal Clifford torus in infinite time.

Lemma 3.6

Lemma 3.13

Let

Proof.

Let
\varphi(t,x)
be a eigenfunction of the Laplacian operator
\Delta
with

\begin{array}[c]{c}-\Delta\varphi=\lambda_{1}(t)\varphi,\text{ }\int_{L_{t}}\varphi^{2}d\mu_{t}=1.\end{array}

\lambda_{1}(t)

\begin{array}[c]{c}\lambda_{1}(t)=\int_{L_{t}}\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}d\mu_{t}.\end{array}

Taking derivative with respect to

(
3.27

\begin{array}[c]{c}\int_{L_{t}}[2\varphi\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\varphi^{2}|H|^{2}]d\mu_{t}=0.\end{array}

\begin{array}[c]{lll}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\lambda_{1}(t)&=&-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{L_{t}}\varphi\Delta\varphi d\mu_{t}\\

&=&\int_{L_{t}}[\lambda_{1}(2\varphi\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi-\varphi^{2}|H|^{2})-2H_{k}h^{kij}\varphi\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\varphi]d\mu_{t}\\
&=&\int_{L_{t}}-2H_{k}h^{kij}\varphi\nabla_{i}\nabla_{j}\varphi d\mu_{t}\\

From the Codazzi equation we have

\begin{array}[c]{c}\nabla_{i}h_{j}^{ik}-\nabla_{j}h_{i}^{ik}=-\frac{1}{2}g^{kl}R_{\alpha\beta}F_{l}^{\alpha}v_{j}^{\beta}=-\frac{1}{2}Kg^{kl}g_{\alpha\beta}F_{l}^{\alpha}v_{j}^{\beta}=0,\end{array}

\eta

\begin{array}[c]{lll}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\lambda_{1}(t)&=&2\int_{L_{t}}[H_{k}h^{kij}\nabla_{i}\varphi+(H_{k}h^{kij})_{i}\varphi]\nabla_{j}\varphi d\mu_{t}\\
&=&2\int_{L_{t}}[H_{k}h^{kij}\nabla_{i}\varphi+(\nabla_{i}H_{k}h^{kij}+H_{k}\nabla_{j}H^{k})\varphi]\nabla_{j}\varphi d\mu_{t}\\
&=&2\int_{L_{t}}[H_{k}h^{kij}\nabla_{i}\varphi+\nabla_{i}H_{k}h^{kij}\varphi]\nabla_{j}\varphi d\mu_{t}\\
&&-\int_{L_{t}}|H|^{2}(\left|\nabla\varphi\right|^{2}+\varphi\Delta\varphi)d\mu_{t}.\end{array}

Under the assumption (

\begin{array}[c]{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\lambda_{1}(t)\geq-2\Lambda\epsilon e^{-\gamma t}\lambda_{1}(t)-2\Lambda\epsilon e^{-\gamma t}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-2\epsilon^{2}e^{-2\gamma t}\lambda_{1}(t),\end{array}

\begin{array}[c]{l}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\geq-(\Lambda\epsilon e^{-\gamma t}+\epsilon^{2}e^{-2\gamma t})\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)-\Lambda\epsilon e^{-\gamma t}\end{array}

for

Thus, the first eigenvalue

will
satisfy

\begin{array}[c]{c}e^{-\frac{1}{2\gamma}(2\Lambda\epsilon e^{-\gamma t}+\epsilon^{2}e^{-2\gamma t})}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\geq e^{-\frac{1}{2\gamma}(2\Lambda\epsilon+\epsilon^{2})}\lambda_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}(0)+\frac{\Lambda\epsilon}{\gamma}\int_{1}^{e^{-\gamma t}}e^{-\frac{1}{2\gamma}(2\Lambda\epsilon u+\epsilon^{2}u^{2})}du,\end{array}

which implies

Theorem 4.1

Definition 4.2

In the following, we assume that

,
t\in(0,T_{0})
, are identically
zero and by the uniqueness of mean curvature flow we have

L_{s_{i},t}\rightarrow L_{\infty,t}=L_{0},\text{\ }t\in[0,T_{0}].

From (
4.9
), we can take
s_{i}\rightarrow 0
and
\delta_{i}\rightarrow 0

to yield

\begin{array}[c]{c}\int_{L_{s_{i},t_{i}}}|\Delta\frac{1}{s_{i}}\alpha_{s_{i},t_{i}}|^{2}d\mu_{s_{i},t_{i}}\leq(K+2+\delta_{i})\int_{L_{s_{i},t_{i}}}|\nabla\frac{1}{s_{i}}\alpha_{s_{i},t_{i}}|^{2}d\mu_{{}_{s_{i},t_{i}}},\end{array}

since
L_{0}
is minimal, we have

(4.10)

\begin{array}[c]{c}\int_{L_{0}}|\Delta\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{(0,t_{i})}|^{2}d\mu_{0}\leq(K+2)\int_{L_{0}}|\nabla\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{(0,t_{i})}|^{2}d\mu_{0}.\end{array}

On the other hand, from (
3.2
) the Legendrian angle
\alpha_{s,t}

satisfies

\begin{array}[c]{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\alpha_{s,t}=\Delta_{s,t}\alpha_{s,t}+(K+2)\alpha_{s,t}.\end{array}

Since
L_{0}
is minimal, we can take
s_{i}\rightarrow 0
to get

(4.11)

\begin{array}[c]{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}=\Delta_{0}\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}+(K+2)\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}.\end{array}

By the eigenvalue decomposition as in Lemma
4.4
, we have

\begin{array}[c]{c}\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{(s,t)=(0,0)}=-\Delta_{0}f_{0}-(K+2)f_{0}\text{ }\bot\text{ }E_{\lambda_{1}},\end{array}

where
E_{\lambda_{1}}
is the first eigenspace of the Laplacian operator

\Delta_{0}
on
L_{0}.
For any function
\phi\in E_{\lambda_{1}}
by
(
4.11
) we have

\begin{array}[c]{ccc}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{L_{0}}\phi\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}d\mu_{0}&=&\int_{L_{0}}\phi[\Delta_{0}\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}+(K+2)\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}]d\mu_{0}\\
&=&\int_{L_{0}}\left(\Delta_{0}\phi+(K+2)\phi\right)\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}d\mu_{0}=0,\end{array}

which show that

\begin{array}[c]{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\alpha_{s,t}|_{s=0}\text{ }\bot\text{ }E_{\lambda_{1}},\text{\ {for}\ }t\in[0,T_{0}].\end{array}

Since
X
is an essential Legendrian variation, we get

\begin{array}[c]{c}\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{(s,t)=(0,0)}=-\Delta_{0}f_{0}-(K+2)f_{0}\end{array}

is nonzero. Thus, by (
4.11
) we can see that
\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}
is nonzero and orthogonal to
E_{\lambda_{1}}
for all

t\in[0,T_{0}]
, which implies

\begin{array}[c]{c}\int_{L_{0}}|\Delta\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}|^{2}d\mu_{0}\geq\lambda_{2}\int_{L_{0}}|\nabla\frac{\partial\alpha_{s,t}}{\partial s}|_{s=0}|^{2}d\mu_{0},\end{array}

where the second eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}
is big than
\lambda_{1}=K+2
. This
is contradicts (
4.10
), and (
4.6
) is proved.

Now recall that by (
3.9
) in the proof of Lemma
3.5
with

H_{s,t}=\nabla\alpha_{s,t},
we have

\begin{array}[c]{lll}\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_{L_{s,t}}\left|H_{s,t}\right|^{2}d\mu_{{}_{s,t}}&\leq&\int_{L_{s,t}}[-(\Delta\alpha_{s,t})^{2}+(K+2+\Lambda\epsilon_{0})\left|H_{s,t}\right|^{2}]d\mu_{{}_{s,t}}\\
&=&\int_{L_{s,t}}[-(\Delta\alpha_{s,t})^{2}+(K+2+\Lambda\epsilon_{0})\left|\nabla\alpha_{s,t}\right|^{2}]d\mu_{{}_{s,t}}\\
&\leq&-(\delta_{0}-\Lambda\epsilon_{0})\int_{L_{s,t}}\left|H_{s,t}\right|^{2}d\mu_{{}_{s,t}},\end{array}

which is the inequality (
4.7
).
∎

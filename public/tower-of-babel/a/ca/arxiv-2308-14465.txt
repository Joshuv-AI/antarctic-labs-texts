A note on UFD \operatorname{UFD}
Mohsen Asgharzadeh

Abstract
. We search for principal ideals. As a sample, let R R be a strongly-normal, almost-factorial and complete-intersection local ring with a prime ideal 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} of height one. If 0 ​ p ​ t ​ ( R / 𝔭 ) ≥ dim R − 2 0pt(R/\mathfrak{p})\geq\dim R-2 , we show 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} is principal. .

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local 3-dimensional hypersurface ring such that

Let

A new proof of regular rings are UFD

In this section we apply some local cohomology and reprove a celebrated theorem of Auslander-Buchsbaum
[
10
]
.
We apply their strategy, but almost every thing is different from
[
10

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
R
be of depth at most two, and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)
. If
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})<\infty
, then
\mathfrak{p}
is principal.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}
. This says
R
is DVR, and the claim is clear. Now, we can assume
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}
. Then
0pt(R/\mathfrak{p})>0
. By Auslander-Buchsbaum formula,

0pt(R)=0pt(R/\mathfrak{p})+\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p}).

This gives

\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq 1
, and so
\mathfrak{p}
is free.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)
. If
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq 2
, then
\mathfrak{p}
is principal.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
d:=\dim(R)
.
By Lemma
3.1
, we may assume
0pt(R)\geq 3
. Following induction hypothesis,
\mathfrak{p}
is locally free over the punctured spectrum (see Fact
2.1
). Let
\mu_{r}:R\to R
defined via the assignment
x\mapsto rx
. There is a natural map

\pi:R\to\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p})
sending
r
into
\mu_{r}
. Since
R
is domain,
\mathfrak{p}
is torsion-free. Consequently, the map
\pi
is injective. Then we have

0\longrightarrow R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow C:=\operatorname{coker}(\pi)\longrightarrow 0\quad(+)

Since

\mathfrak{p}
is locally principal over the punctured spectrum,
C
is of finite length. By Grothendieck’s vanishing theorem
\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}(C)=0
. We plug this in the long exact sequence of local cohomology modules induced by
(+)
to deduce the following exact sequence

0=\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(C)\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}(R)\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}))\longrightarrow\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}(C)=0\quad(\dagger)

Apply this along with

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p})\hookrightarrow\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}(\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{p}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\dagger)}}{{=}}\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}(R)=0.

Since
\operatorname{pd}_{R}(\mathfrak{p})\leq 1
it is rigid. Let us apply Jothilingam’s result to deduce that
\mathfrak{p}
is free
*
*

*

or even without any use of Jothilingam.
.
∎

when is
\operatorname{Cl}(R)
cyclic?

Corollary 4.11
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
be a complete ring of depth at least
3
containing
\mathbb{Q}
and satisfying
(\operatorname{R}_{d-1})
where
d:=\dim R>4
. If
e(R)\leq 3
then
R
is hypersurface.

Proof.

The ring satisfies
(\operatorname{S}_{3})
. According to Fact
4.5

R
is Cohen-Macaulay.
If
e(R)\leq 2
the claim is well-known by Huneke. So, we may assume that
e(R)=3
. Following Abhyankar
\mu(\mathfrak{m})\leq d+2
.
Thanks to Proposition
4.8
,
R
is
\operatorname{UFD}
and following Murphy (see
[
19
, Theorem 12.3]
),
R
is Gorenstein.
Suppose on the way of contradiction that
R
is not hypersurface,
then
d+1<\mu(\mathfrak{m})\leq d+2
. This says that the ring is of
minimal multiplicity. Since the residue field is infinite, there is a system of parameter
\underline{x}:=x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
so that
\mathfrak{m}^{2}=(\underline{x})\mathfrak{m}
. Let us look at the Gorenstein ring
\overline{R}:=R/(\underline{x})
. It is easy to see
\overline{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}=0
. In other words,
\operatorname{Soc}(\overline{R})=\overline{\mathfrak{m}}
, which is 1-dimensional. Let
x_{d+1}\in\mathfrak{m}
be such that
\overline{x}_{d+1}
is a generator for
\operatorname{Soc}(\overline{R})
. This shows that
\mathfrak{m}:=(\underline{x},x_{d+1})
. Let
A:=k[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d+1}]]
and denote the natural
surjection
\pi:A\to R
sending
X_{i}
to
x_{i}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}:=\ker(\pi)
. Since
R
is
(\operatorname{S}_{2})
and
(\operatorname{R}_{1})
it is normal domain, in particular
\mathfrak{p}
is prime. As
A
is catenary,
\mathfrak{p}
is of height one. By
\operatorname{UFD}
property of regular rings there is some
f
such that
\mathfrak{p}=(f)
. We proved that
R=A/(f)
, i.e., it is hypersurface. This contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a strongly normal almost factorial complete-intersection ring and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)
. If
0pt(R/\mathfrak{p})\geq\dim R-2

is principal.

Proof.

The proof is proceed by induction on

d:=\dim(R)
. Suppose first
that

d<4
. Due to the
(R_{2})
condition, we may adopt the situation of Fact
5.1

Recall in this case that
\operatorname{Pic}(\operatorname{Spec}(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\operatorname{Cl}(R)

Since the ring is normal we deduce that

\operatorname{UFD}
, and in particular
\mathfrak{p}

Now, assume

\CI(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq 2
.
Since

(see

for all prime ideal

such that

. In the light of
[

, Cor 7.2]
we know
\operatorname{Cl}(R)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(S^{-1}R)

\operatorname{Cl}(S^{-1}R)
is torsion, i.e.,
S^{-1}R
is almost factorial.
Due to
inductive hypothesis, we deduce that

is locally principal over
\operatorname{Spec}(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}

Recall that
d>3
and
0pt(R/\mathfrak{p})\geq\dim R-2\geq 2

we deduce that

Discussion 5.4
.

An
R
-module
M
is called
totally reflexive
provided that:

i)

the natural map
M\rightarrow M^{**}
is an isomorphism,

ii)

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,R)=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M^{*},R)=0
for all
i\geq 1
.

The
Gorenstein dimension
of
M
, denoted
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}(M)
, is defined to be the infimum of all nonnegative integers
n
, such that there exists an exact sequence
0\rightarrow G_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow G_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0,
in which each
G_{i}
is a totally reflexive
R
-module.
Every finitely generated module over a Gorenstein ring has finite Gorenstein dimension. Moreover, if
R
is local and
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}(M)<\infty
, then it follows that
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}(M)=0ptR-0pt_{R}(M)
, and we call it Auslander-Bridger formula. If
\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}(k)<\infty
then
R
is Gorenstein.
For more details, see
[
10
]
.

Example 5.5
.

Let
R
be a
3
-dimensional Gorenstein ring which is not
\operatorname{UFD}
, e.g.
R=k[[x,y,u,v]]/(u^{2})
. Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)
be non-principal, e.g.,
\mathfrak{p}:=(u,v)
. Then
\operatorname{Gdim}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq 2
but
\mathfrak{p}
is not free.

Question 5.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)
be such that
\operatorname{Gdim}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq 2
. When is
\mathfrak{p}
totally reflexive?

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
R
be of depth at most two, and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)
. If
\operatorname{Gdim}(R/\mathfrak{p})<\infty
, then
\mathfrak{p}
is totally reflexive.

Proof.

Suppose first that
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}
. Then

\operatorname{Gdim}_{R}(k)<\infty
.
This shows
R
is Gorenstein, and
1
-dimensional as
\mathfrak{m}
is of height one.
In this case any ideal is totally reflexive.
Now, assume
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{m}
. Then
0pt(R/\mathfrak{p})>0
as
\mathfrak{p}
is prime. By Auslander-Bridger formula,

\operatorname{Gdim}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq 1
because
R
is of depth at most two. Thanks to
0\to\mathfrak{p}\to R\to R/\mathfrak{p}\to 0
we observe
\mathfrak{p}
is totally reflexive.
∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
R
be
(\operatorname{S}_{3})
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)
be such that
\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}
is
(\operatorname{S}_{3})
. If
\operatorname{Gdim}(R/\mathfrak{p})\leq 2
, then
\mathfrak{p}
is totally reflexive.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
d:=\dim R
. The case
d<3
is in Lemma
5.7
. So, we may assume
d>2
. Consequently,
0pt(R)\geq 3
. Recall that

\operatorname{Gdim}(R_{Q}/\mathfrak{p}R_{Q})\leq 2
for all
Q\in\operatorname{Var}(\mathfrak{p})\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
.
Thanks to inductive hypothesis

\mathfrak{p}R_{Q}
is totally reflexive for all
Q\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)\setminus\{\mathfrak{m}\}
. From this

E:=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\mathfrak{p},R)
is of finite length.
If a module
(-)
has finite
\operatorname{Gdim}
, then

\operatorname{Gdim}(-)=\sup\{i:\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(-,R)\neq 0\}
. So, it is enough to show
E=0
. There is a free module
F
and a totally reflexive module
T
such that the sequence

0\to T\to F\to\mathfrak{p}\to 0
is exact. This gives

0\to\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\to F^{\ast}\to T^{\ast}\to E\to 0
. Let us breakdown it into two short exact sequences

i)

0\to\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}\to F^{\ast}\to S\to 0
,

ii)

0\to S\to T^{\ast}\to E\to 0
.

ii)
yields that
0=\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(T^{\ast})\to\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(E)\to\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(S)\to\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(T^{\ast})=0,
and so
E=\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{0}(E)\cong\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(S)
. Since

0pt(R)>2
from i) we get that
\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{1}(S)\cong\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ast})
. Combining these, imply that
E\cong\operatorname{H}_{\mathfrak{m}}^{2}(\mathfrak{p}^{\ast})=0
.
∎

The above shows:

Let
R
be
3

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}^{1}(R)

\mathfrak{p}^{\ast}

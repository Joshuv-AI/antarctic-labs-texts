Embedding theorems for quantizable pseudo-Kähler manifolds
Andrea Galasso, Chin-Yu Hsiao

Abstract
Given a compact quantizable pseudo-Kähler manifold ( M , ω ) (M,\omega) of constant signature, there exists a Hermitian line bundle ( L , h ) (L,h) over M M with curvature − 2 ​ π ​ i ​ ω -2\pi i\,\omega . We shall show that the asymptotic expansion of the Bergman kernels for L ⊗ k L^{\otimes k} -valued ( 0 , q ) (0,q) -forms implies more or less immediately a number of analogues of well-known results, such as Kodaira embedding theorem and Tian’s almost-isometry theorem.

Embedding theorems, almost-isometry theorems

Introduction

Given a compact symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
such that
[\omega]\in H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
we can define a Hermitian line bundle
(L,h^{L})
having a unique holomorphic connection whose curvature is
R^{L}=-2\pi i\,\omega
. One can always pick an almost complex structure
J
compatible with
\omega
, in
[
SZ
]
under the assumption of
L
being positive a space of almost holomorphic sections
H^{0}_{J}(M,L^{\otimes k})
is defined and the authors prove symplectic analogues of standard results in complex geometry; particularly those involving the zero sets of sections and the corresponding maps they define into complex projective space. As far as the authors are aware, if the positivity assumption on
L
is dropped no such results are known and in fact one can not expect the dimensions of
H^{0}_{J}(M,L^{\otimes k})
to grow as in the positive case as
k
goes to infinity in general. From the other hand, since the curvature of the line bundle
L
in non-degenerate, suppose that it has constant signature
(n_{-},n_{+})
, one can try to replace the spaces
H^{0}_{J}(M,L^{\otimes k})
with spaces of harmonic
L^{\otimes k}
-valued
(0,n_{-})
-forms, this is suggested by results contained in
[
HM
]
and
[
MM
]
concerning the asymptotic expansion of the Bergman kernel for
(0,q)
forms for high tensor powers of
L
(see also
[
Hs
]
and
[
HM2
]
for the Szegő kernels for
(0,q)
forms). The aim of this paper is to define embeddings of
(M,\omega)
which are asymptotically symplectic; since the results in
[
HM
]
are presented in the holomorphic category we focus on the case when
J
is an integrable structure. We now recall some known results and state the main theorems.

. Thus, by noticing

\begin{split}&\forall k\,:\,\Phi_{k}^{(q)}(x_{k})=\Phi_{k}^{(q)}(y_{k})\iff\\
&\forall k,\,\,\exists\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\,:\,(S_{0,J_{0}}(x_{k}),\,\dots,\,S_{d_{k},J_{0}}(x_{k}))=\lambda_{k}\cdot(S_{0,J_{0}}(y_{k}),\,\dots,\,S_{d_{k},J_{0}}(y_{k}))\end{split}

(12)

we can also assume that for each harmonic
(0,q)
-forms
g_{k}\in H^{q}(M,L^{\otimes k})
, we have

\left|g_{k,J_{0}}(x_{k})\right|^{2}_{h^{L^{\otimes k}}}\geq\left|g_{k,J_{0}}(y_{k})\right|^{2}_{h^{L^{\otimes k}}},\ \ \mbox{for all $k\gg 1$}.

(13)

Assume that
\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}x_{k}=p
,
\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}y_{k}=q
,
p\neq q
.
Let
s
and
s_{1}
be local holomorphic trivializing sections of
L
defined on open sets
D\subset M
and
D_{1}\subset M
respectively,
p\in D
,
q\in D_{1}
,
\overline{D}\cap\overline{D}_{1}=\emptyset
.
From
[
HM1
, Lemma 3.7]
, we see that

P^{(q)}_{k,s,s_{1}}(x,y)=O(k^{-\infty})\ \ \mbox{on $D\times D_{1}$}.

(14)

From Theorem
2.1
and (
14
), we can find
g_{k}\in H^{q}(M,L^{\otimes k})
such that

\left|g_{k,J_{0}}(x_{k})\right|^{2}_{h^{L^{\otimes k}}}<\left|g_{k,J_{0}}(y_{k})\right|^{2}_{h^{L^{\otimes k}}},\ \ \mbox{for all $k\gg 1$}.

(15)

From (
13
) and (
15
), we get a contradiction. Therefore,
x_{k}
and
y_{k}
must converge to the same point
p\in M
. We can distinguish two cases: either
\limsup_{k}\sqrt{k}\lvert x_{k}-y_{k}\rvert=C>0
,
C\in]0,\infty]
or
\limsup_{k}\sqrt{k}\lvert x_{k}-y_{k}\rvert=0
. Here the distance
\lvert x_{k}-y_{k}\rvert
,
x_{k},y_{k}\in M
, is meant with respect to the Riemannian structure
{g}=(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle+\overline{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle})/2
on
TM
.

Let
s
be local holomorphic trivializing section of
L
defined on an open set
D\subset M
,
p\in D
,
\left|s\right|^{2}_{h^{L}}=e^{-2\phi}
.
Let us study the first case. For every
y_{k}\in D
, let us set

g_{k}(\cdot):=P_{k}^{(q)}(\cdot,\,y_{k})\overline{e}_{J_{0}}(y_{k})\in H^{q}(M,L^{\otimes k}).

On
D
, write
g_{k}=s^{k}\otimes\tilde{g}_{k}
,
\tilde{g}_{k}\in\Omega^{0,q}(D)
. Put
\hat{g}_{k}:=e^{-k\phi}\tilde{g}_{k}
.
In view of Theorem
2.1
, we have

\hat{g}_{k}(x)=e^{ik\,\Psi(x,y_{k})}b(x,y_{k},k)\overline{e}_{J_{0}}(y_{k})+O(k^{-\infty})\ \ \mbox{on $D$}.

Let
\hat{g}_{k,J_{0}}(x):=\langle\,\hat{g}_{k}(x)\,|\,\overline{e}_{J_{0}}(x)\,\rangle\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(D)
.
Notice that

\begin{split}&\left|\hat{g}_{k,J_{0}}(x_{k})\right|^{2}=\left|e^{ik\,\Psi(x_{k},y_{k})}\langle\,b(x_{k},y_{k},k)\overline{e}_{J_{0}}(y_{k})\,|\,\overline{e}_{J_{0}}(x_{k})\,\rangle\right|^{2}+O(k^{-\infty})\\
&\leq e^{-2k\,\mathrm{Im}\Psi({x}_{k},{y}_{k})}\left|\langle\,b({x}_{k},{y}_{k},k)\overline{e}_{J_{0}}(y_{k})\,|\,e_{J_{0}}(x_{k})\,\rangle\right|^{2}+O(k^{-\infty})\end{split}

and

\left|\hat{g}_{k,J_{0}}(y_{k})\right|^{2}=\left|\langle\,b({y}_{k},{y}_{k},k)\overline{e}_{J_{0}}(y_{k})\,|\,e_{J_{0}}(x_{k})\,\rangle\right|^{2}+O(k^{-\infty}).

Thus, by recalling that
x_{k},\,y_{k}\rightarrow p
, we get

\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\left|\hat{g}_{k,J_{0}}(x_{k})\right|^{2}}{k^{2n}}\leq e^{-2c\,C^{2}}\left|b_{0}(p,p)\right|^{2}<\left|b_{0}(p,p)\right|^{2}=\lim_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\left|\hat{g}_{k,J_{0}}(y_{k})\right|^{2}}{k^{2n}}

which contradicts (
13
), where
c>0
is a constant independent of
k
.

Now, let us focus on the second case.
Put
P^{(q)}_{k,s,J_{0},J_{0}}(x,y):=\langle\,P^{(q)}_{k,s}(x,y)e_{J_{0}}(y)\,|\,e_{J_{0}}(x)\,\rangle

and let
P^{(q)}_{k,s,J_{0},J_{0}}(x):=P^{(q)}_{k,s,J_{0},J_{0}}(x,x)
.
Let us set

f_{k}(t):=\frac{\left|P_{k,s,J_{0},J_{0}}^{(q)}(tx_{k}+(1-t)y_{k},y_{k})\right|^{2}}{P^{(q)}_{k,s,J_{0},J_{0}}(tx_{k}+(1-t)y_{k})P^{(q)}_{k,s,J_{0},J_{0}}(y_{k})}.

From Theorem
2.1
, it is then easy to prove that

f_{k}(t)\sim e^{-2k\,\mathrm{Im}\Psi(t{x}_{k}+(1-t){y}_{k},{y}_{k})}\cdot\left[1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}R_{k}(t)\right]

(16)

where
R_{k}=O(1)
and the symbol
\sim
means “has the same asymptotic as”. For ease of notation, let us set
F_{k}(t):=-2k\,\mathrm{Im}\Psi(t{x}_{k}+(1-t){y}_{k},{y}_{k})
for given
x_{k}
,
y_{k}
. Now, by applying the following formulas

\lvert F^{\prime}_{k}(t)\rvert=\lvert\langle-2k\,\mathrm{Im}\Psi^{\prime}_{x}(tx_{k}+(1-t)y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle\rvert\leq\frac{1}{c_{0}}k\lvert x_{k}-y_{k}\rvert^{2}

and

F^{\prime\prime}_{k}(t)=\langle-2k\,\mathrm{Im}\Psi^{\prime\prime}_{x}(tx_{k}+(1-t)y_{k},y_{k}),x_{k}-y_{k}\rangle<-c_{0}k\,\lvert x_{k}-y_{k}\rvert^{2}

for the computation of the second derivative of
f_{k}(t)
in equation (
16
), here
c_{0}
is a positive constant, and by using the assumption
\limsup_{k}\sqrt{k}\lvert x_{k}-y_{k}\rvert=0
, after some computations, we obtain that

\limsup_{k}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(t)}{k\,\lvert x_{k}-y_{k}\rvert^{2}}<0

(17)

for each
t
.

We now show that if we apply the assumption (
12
) for computing the
\limsup
appearing in (
17
) we get a different result. By Cauchy-Schwartz inequality we have
0\leq f_{k}(t)\leq 1
, for any
t\in[0,1]
and
f_{k}(0)=f_{k}(1)=1
. Thus, for each
k
, there is a
t_{k}\in[0,1]
such that
f^{\prime\prime}(t_{k})=0
. Hence,

\limsup_{k}\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(t)}{k\,\lvert x_{k}-y_{k}\rvert^{2}}\geq 0

which contradicts (
17
).

Eventually, we shall prove the last part of the theorem concerning pull-back of the Fubini-Study form, as a corollary we get that
\mathrm{d}\Phi^{(q)}_{k}
is injective and thus
\Phi^{(q)}_{k}
is an embedding (see
[
MM
, Lemma 5.16]
). Recall that the Fubini Study Kähler form on
\mathbb{CP}^{m}
is given in homogeneous coordinates
[w_{0}:\cdots:w_{m}]
by

\omega_{\mathrm{FS}}:=\frac{i}{2\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}\log\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m}\lvert w_{i}\rvert^{2}\right)\,.

(18)

Let us apply (
18
) to our setting; local holomorphic section
s
of
L
over an open set
D
in
M
. It induces a section
s^{k}
of the
k
-th tensor power of the line bundle
L^{\otimes k}
on
D
. By recalling (
2
), we have
S_{j,J_{0}}=f_{j,J_{0}}(z)\otimes s^{k}
for a smooth function
f_{j,J_{0}}
on
D
,
j=0,1,\ldots,d_{k}
. By definition (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\Phi^{(q)\,*}_{k}(\omega_{\mathrm{FS}})_{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi k}\partial\overline{\partial}\log\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d_{k}}\lvert f_{j,J_{0}}(z)\rvert^{2}\right)=\frac{i}{2\pi k}\partial\overline{\partial}\log\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d_{k}}\frac{\left|S_{j,J_{0}}(z)\right|^{2}_{h^{L^{\otimes k}}}}{\left|s^{k}(z)\right|^{2}_{h^{L^{\otimes k}}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\pi}\partial\overline{\partial}\phi+\frac{i}{2\pi k}\partial\overline{\partial}\log\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d_{k}}{\left|S_{j,J_{0}}(z)\right|^{2}_{h^{L^{\otimes k}}}}\right)\,.

Thus, the statement of the theorem follows from

\left\|\partial\overline{\partial}\log P^{(q)}_{k,s,J_{0},J_{0}}(x,x)\right\|_{\mathcal{C}^{\infty}}=O(1)

in view of the asymptotic expansion of
P^{(q)}_{k,s}(x,x)
.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Fix
p\in M
and let
s
be a local trivializing section of
L
defined on an open set

D
of
p
,
\left|s\right|^{2}_{h^{L}}=e^{-2\phi}
. Let
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n})
,
z_{j}=x_{2j-1}+ix_{2j}
,
j=1,\ldots,n
, be local coordinates of
M
defined on
D
. We take
z
and
s
so that (
9
) hold. Assume that
\lambda_{1}<0,\ldots,\lambda_{n_{-}}<0
,
\lambda_{n_{-}+1}>0,\ldots,\lambda_{n}>0
. We have
L_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}
at
p
,
j=1,\ldots,n
.
We need

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
u\in H^{q}(M,L^{\otimes k})
with
\left\|u\right\|_{k}=1
. On
D
, write
u=s^{k}\otimes\tilde{u}
,
\tilde{u}\in\Omega^{0,q}(D)
. Put
\tilde{u}_{J_{0}}(x):=\langle\,\tilde{u}(x)\,|\,\overline{e}_{J_{0}}(x)\,\rangle\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(D)
.
There is a constant
C>0
independent of
k
and
u
such that

\begin{split}&\left|(L_{t}\tilde{u}_{J_{0}})(p)\right|^{2}\leq Ck^{n},\ \ t=1,\ldots,n_{-},\\
&\left|(\overline{L}_{t}\tilde{u}_{J_{0}})(p)\right|^{2}\leq Ck^{n},\ \ t=n_{-}+1,\ldots,n.\end{split}

(19)

Proof.

Put
Z_{t}=L_{t}
,
t=1,\ldots,n_{-}
,
Z_{t}=\overline{L}_{t}
,
t=n_{-}+1,\ldots,n
. Fix
t\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
From Theorem
2.1
and off-diagonal expansion of
P^{(q)}_{k}
(see (
14
)), we have

\left|(Z_{t}\tilde{u}_{J_{0}})(p)-\int e^{ik\Psi(0,y)-k\phi(y)}\Bigr(r_{0}(y)k^{n+1}+r_{1}(y,k)\Bigr)\chi(y)\tilde{u}(y)\mathrm{dV}_{M}(y)\right|\leq C_{N}k^{-N},

(20)

for every
N>0
, where
C_{N}>0
is a constant independent of
k
,
u
and
r_{0}(y)=O(\left|y\right|^{2})
,

r_{1}(y,k)\in S^{n}_{{\rm cl\,}}(1;D,\mathbb{C}\boxtimes(T^{*0,q}M)^{*})
,
\chi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(D)
,
\chi=1
near
0
. From
{\rm Im\,}\Psi(0,y)\sim\left|y\right|^{2}
and some straightforward computations, we can check that

\begin{split}&\left|\int e^{ik\Psi(0,y)-k\phi(y)}\Bigr(r_{0}(y)k^{n+1}+r_{1}(y,k)\Bigr)\chi(y)\tilde{u}(y)\mathrm{dV}_{M}(y)\right|\\
&\leq C\left|\int e^{-ck\left|y\right|^{2}}(k^{2n+2}\left|y\right|^{4}+k^{2n})dy\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\hat{C}k^{n},\end{split}

(21)

where
C>0
,
c>0
,
\hat{C}>0
are constants independent of
k
and
u
.
From (
20
) and (
21
), we get (
19
).
∎

We can now prove Theorem
1.2
.
We may assume that
\sum^{d_{k}}_{j=0}\left|(Z_{t}\tilde{S}_{j,J_{0}})(p)\right|^{2}\neq 0
.
Let

u:=\frac{\sum^{d_{k}}_{j=0}S_{j}\overline{(Z_{t}\tilde{S}_{j,J_{0}})(p)}}{\sqrt{\sum^{d_{k}}_{j=0}\left|(Z_{t}\tilde{S}_{j,J_{0}})(p)\right|^{2}}}.

Then,
u\in H^{q}(M,L^{\otimes k})
with
\left\|u\right\|_{k}=1
. From Lemma
4.1
,
we get

\left|(Z_{t}\tilde{u}_{J_{0}})(p)\right|^{2}=\sum^{d_{k}}_{j=0}\left|(Z_{t}\tilde{S}_{j,J_{0}})(p)\right|^{2}\leq Ck^{n}.

The theorem follows.

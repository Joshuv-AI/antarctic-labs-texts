Homogenization of non-autonomous evolution problems for convolution type operators in random media.
A. Piatnitski, E. Zhizhina

Abstract
We study homogenization problem for non-autonomous parabolic equations of the form ∂ t u = L ⁡ ( t ) ​ u \partial_{t}u=L(t)u with an integral convolution type operator L ⁡ ( t ) L(t) that has a non-symmetric jump kernel which is periodic in spatial variables and stationary random in time. We show that the homogenized equation is a SPDE with a finite dimensional multiplicative noise.



1
Introduction and previous results

This paper deals with homogenization problem for a parabolic type equation of the form

\frac{\partial u^{\varepsilon}}{\partial t}\ =\ \frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int\limits_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}}a\Big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\Big)\ \mu_{\omega}\Big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon};\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\Big)\ (u^{\varepsilon}(y,t)-u^{\varepsilon}(x,t))dy,

(1)

here
\varepsilon>0
is a small parameter, the coefficient
\mu_{\omega}(\xi,\eta;\tau)
is periodic in spatial variables
\xi
and
\eta
, and stationary random in time, and the functions
\mu_{\omega}(\cdot)
and
a(\cdot)
are not assumed to be symmetric in spatial variables.
We study the asymptotic behaviour of solutions to the Cauchy problem for equation (
1
) with the initial
condition

u^{\varepsilon}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(2)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
It turns out that the asymptotic behaviour of solutions to the said Cauchy problem depends on the mixing properties of
\mu_{\omega}
.
In particular, if
\mu_{\omega}
has sufficiently good mixing properties, then the solution of the original equation converges in law in moving coordinates to a solution of the homogenized equation being a stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE).

It is worth noting that in the case of a symmetric kernel
a(x-y)\mu_{\omega}(x,y,t)
the homogenized operator is a deterministic second order parabolic operator with constant coefficients, see Remark
3.2
. Such a significant change in the behavior of the limit equation occurs due to the lack of symmetry of the kernel. A convolution type equation with a non-symmetric kernel is a natural non-local counterpart of a convection-diffusion problem with a non-zero convection (drift) term.

Homogenization problems for non-autonomous local convection-diffusion equations have been considered in
[
6
]
,
the coefficients of the operators being random stationary in time and periodic in spatial variables.
It was proved that under the assumption that the coefficients of the equation have good mixing properties the solution of the original problem converges in law to the solution of the limit stochastic partial differential equation in properly chosen moving coordinates.

The case of parabolic equations with a symmetric elliptic and a large potential (zero order term) has been treated in
[
2
]
.

In this paper we follow the scheme developed in the proof of the homogenization results presented in
[
6
]
,
however, due to the natural difference between differential and non-local convolution type operators the adaptation
of this scheme is rather non-trivial, it required new ideas and methods.

Averaging of parabolic differential equations of convection-diffusion type with large lower order terms and coefficients being periodic both in spatial and temporal variables were studied in
[
4
,
3
]
.
In
[
4
]
, the homogenization result was obtained under the assumption that the time oscillation is slower than the spacial one, and the effective drift is equal to zero.
In
[
3
]
, it was proved that the homogenization result holds in rapidly moving coordinates, and in the presence of zero order terms, homogenization takes place after factorization of solutions with the ground state of the corresponding cell problem.

In the autonomous non-local case when
\mu(\xi,\eta)
in equation (
1
) is a periodic non-symmetric function of spatial variables and does not depend on time, the corresponding Cauchy problem admits homogenization in moving coordinates
x^{\varepsilon}=x-\frac{b}{\varepsilon}t
, where
b\in{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}
is an effective drift, see
[
9
]
.
Since we borrow some constructions from this paper, we briefly recall here the model and the main result of
[
9
]
.
This paper deals with the Cauchy problems for an equation of the form (
1
) with the initial condition

u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
under the assumption that

\mu(x,y)
is periodic in
x
and

is non-negative,

.
It was shown that, under these conditions, the said Cauchy problem admits homogenization in moving coordinates, i.e.
there is a constant vector

such that for the solution

of this problem the following representation is valid:

with constant coefficients that reads

The effective drift

(3)

where

is the solution of equation

\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(q-\xi)\mu(q,\xi)\big(v_{0}(q)-v_{0}(\xi)\big)dq=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}v_{0}(\xi)d\xi=1.

(4)

It is proved in
[
9

that there exist two positive constants
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}>0
such that

0<\gamma_{1}\leq v_{0}(\xi)\leq\gamma_{2}<\infty
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

The homogenization problem for non-autonomous convolution type equations with coefficients periodic
in all variables including time
has been addressed in
[
11
]
.

In the present work we consider the case of non-autonomous random in time equations. More precisely, we assume that
the coefficient
\mu(\xi,z,s)
in (

s
with values
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2d})

In the second section we provide the assumptions on the coefficients and formulate the main homogenization
results of this work.
Section three deals with formal asymptotic expansion of a solution and various auxiliary ’’cell’’ problems.
Solutions of these problems play crucial role in deriving the homogenized problem and proving the convergence.
The proof of our homogenization results in the case of general stationary ergodic function

Section 4.
These results are then refined in Section 5 under the assumptions that

The special case of

is investigated in Section 6.
The proof of technical statements is given in Appendices.

2
Problem setup and main results

The goal of the present work is to study a homogenization problem for non-autonomous parabolic type problems with non-symmetric kernels in stationary ergodic in time and periodic in space media:

\begin{array}[]{l}H^{\varepsilon}_{\omega}u:=\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-L^{\varepsilon}_{\omega}u=0,\quad u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad\mbox{where}\\[5.69054pt]
(L^{\varepsilon}_{\omega}u)(x,t)\ =\ \frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d+2}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a\big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\big)\mu_{\omega}\big(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{y}{\varepsilon};\frac{t}{\varepsilon^{2}}\big)(u(y,t)-u(x,t))dy,\end{array}

(5)

and
a(z)
satisfies the following conditions:

a(z)\geqslant 0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}a(z)dz=1,\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{2}a(z)dz<\infty.

(6)

We assume that the periodic media evolves in time in a random manner. Namely, given a probability space
(\Omega,{\cal F},P)
, we define the random coefficient
\omega\mapsto\mu_{\omega}(x,y;t)
,
\omega\in\Omega
,
as a measurable mapping from
\Omega
to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d}\times\mathbb{R})
, and assume that

\mu_{\omega}(x,y;t)
is periodic in
x
and
y
with the period
[0,1)^{d}
and that
there exist positive constants
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
such that

0<\alpha_{1}\leq\mu_{\omega}(x,y;t)\leq\alpha_{2}\quad\mbox{ for all }\;x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;t\in\mathbb{R}.

(7)

We then suppose that the field
\mu_{\omega}(x,y;t)
is
stationary and ergodic
in time.
More precisely, it is assumed that there is a measure preserving dynamical system
\tau_{s}:\Omega\to\Omega
,

s\in\mathbb{R}
, such that:

\mu_{\omega}(x,y;t+s)=\mu_{\tau_{s}\omega}(x,y;t),\qquad t,s\in\mathbb{R},\;\omega\in\Omega.

This property is referred to as stationarity. In particular, this implies that the law of the random function

\mu_{\omega}(x,y;t)
is independent of
t
. Furthermore, we assume that the dynamical system
\tau_{s}
is ergodic,
that is if
A\subset\Omega
is such that
\tau_{s}A=A
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, then
P(A)=0
or
1
.

In addition to these general assumptions on
\mu_{\omega}(x,y;t)
, in the second part of Theorem
2.1
we also suppose that
\mu_{\omega}(x,y;t)
satisfies good
mixing conditions
. Under these mixing conditions a more precise
result on the homogenization of problem (
5
) is valid, see (
14
) below. We formulate here the mixing conditions in terms of the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{t}
associated with the random process
\mu_{\omega}
following the definition from
[
5
]
. The mapping
(\omega,t)\to\mu_{\omega}(x,y;t)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2d})
is
{\cal F}\otimes\mathbb{R}
-measurable, and we set

{\cal F}^{\mu}_{\leq t}=\sigma\{\mu(\cdot,\cdot,s):\ s\leq t\},\quad{\cal F}^{\mu}_{\geq t}=\sigma\{\mu(\cdot,\cdot,s):\ s\geq t\}.

The function
\alpha(t)
defined as

\alpha(t)=\alpha({\cal F}^{\mu}_{\leq 0},\ {\cal F}^{\mu}_{\geq t})=\sup\limits_{A\in{\cal F}^{\mu}_{\leq 0},\,B\in{\cal F}^{\mu}_{\geq t}}|P(A\cap B)-P(A)P(B)|

(8)

is called the strong mixing coefficient of
\mu
. We assume that

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\alpha^{1/2}(t)dt<\infty.

(9)

Denote by
u^{\varepsilon}_{\omega}(x,t)
a solution of the evolution problem

\frac{\partial u(x,t)}{\partial t}=L^{\varepsilon}u(x,t),\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(10)

t\in[0,T]
, where the operator
L^{\varepsilon}
is defined in (
5
).
The main result of the present work is the following statement:

Theorem 2.1
.

I. Let
a(z)
satisfy condition (
6
),
and assume that
\mu_{\omega}(x,y,t)
is a stationary ergodic process with values in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2d})
that
satisfies estimates (
7
) for all
\omega\in\Omega
.
Then there exists a positive definite symmetric matrix
\Theta
and a vector-valued stationary ergodic
process
\beta_{\omega}(t)\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
such that for any

and for a.e.
\omega\in\Omega

\|u^{\varepsilon}_{\omega}(x+\frac{b}{\varepsilon}\ t+G^{\varepsilon}_{0}(t),\ t)-u^{0}(x,\ t)\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T),\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\to 0,\quad\mbox{as }\;\varepsilon\to 0,

(11)

where
u^{0}(x,t)
is a solution of the Cauchy problem

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\Theta\cdot\nabla\nabla u,\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad t\in[0,T],

(12)

b=\mathbb{E}\beta_{\omega}(0)
, and

G^{\varepsilon}_{0}(t)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int\limits_{0}^{t}\mathop{\beta_{\omega}}\limits^{\circ}(\frac{s}{\varepsilon^{2}})ds=\varepsilon\int\limits_{0}^{t/\varepsilon^{2}}\mathop{\beta_{\omega}}\limits^{\circ}(\tau)d\tau=o(\varepsilon^{-1}),\quad\mathop{\beta_{\omega}}\limits^{\circ}(t)=\beta_{\omega}(t)-b.

(13)

II. If in addition the random field

satisfies the mixing conditions (
9
), then

u^{\varepsilon}_{\omega}(x+\frac{b}{\varepsilon}\ t,\ t)\ \mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{{\cal L}}\ u^{0}(x-\sigma W_{t},\ t),\quad t\in[0,T],\qquad\mbox{as }\;\varepsilon\to 0,

(14)

in the space

equipped with the strong topology;
here
\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{{\cal L}}
stands for the convergence in law,

denotes a standard Wiener process in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\sigma\sigma^{*}=2\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}\big(\mathop{\beta}\limits^{\circ}(0)\otimes\mathop{\beta}\limits^{\circ}(t)\big)\,dt.

Remark 2.1
.

It is worth noting that the function

is a solution of the following SPDE:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle dv^{0}(x,t)=A^{\mathrm{eff}}\cdot\nabla\nabla v^{0}(x,t)dt-\nabla v^{0}\cdot\sigma\,dW_{t}\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle v^{0}(x,0)=u^{0}(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

(15)

with

Indeed, it is sufficient to apply the Itô formula
to the function

and to use (
12

The more detailed formulation of Theorem
2.1
and its proof are given in Sections

-
5

In particular, all the effective characteristics of the limit problem are expressed explicitly in terms of

Denote by

Proof.

Combining the last two relations yields

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Our goal is to show that the limit

p_{\infty}(\xi,s)=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}p^{N}(\xi,s)

(117)

exists and
p_{\infty}(\xi,s)
is a stationary solution of the equation

.
According to estimate (

), for any

\{p^{N}(\cdot,s)\}
is a Cauchy sequence
in

, as
N\to\infty
. Therefore, the limit (
117

:

and we can show in the same way as above that

\|p^{N}(\cdot,s)-p_{\infty}(\cdot,s)\|\leq C_{7}\,e^{-\gamma_{0}(N-s)},\quad s<N.

Moreover, the convergence is uniform on any bounded interval

, i.e.

\lim_{N\to\infty}\,\sup_{s\in[-L,L]}\|p^{N}(\cdot,s)-p_{\infty}(\cdot,s)\|\to 0.

Due to (

p_{\infty}
inherits the bounds and normalization condition given in
(
35
)-(
37
). This yields (
44
).

Using (
44
) and the boundedness of operator

we conclude that

. Indeed,

\|p_{\infty}(\xi,t)-p_{\infty}(\xi,s)\|=\|\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{\tau}p_{\infty}(\xi,\tau)d\tau\|=\|\int_{s}^{t}A^{\ast}(\tau)p_{\infty}(\xi,\tau)d\tau\|\leq C_{8}\,|t-s|.

Since

is a stationary field, then

is also a stationary field for any

. Since
p_{\infty}(\cdot,s)=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}p^{N+s}(\cdot,s)
, we conclude that

is a stationary field. Thus,

p_{\infty}(\xi,s,\omega)=p_{\infty}(\xi,0,\tau_{s}\omega).

To prove the uniqueness of

to the problem

By the same arguments as in the proof of Lemma
3.2

We denote this constant by

. Assume first that

\|q(\cdot,0,\omega)\|\leqslant C_{6}\,e^{-\gamma_{0}N}\,\|q(\cdot,N,\omega)\|

.
Taking the average yields the following estimate:

\mathbb{E}\|q(\cdot,0,\omega)\|\leq C_{6}\,e^{-\gamma_{0}N}\ \mathbb{E}\|q(\cdot,N,\omega)\|

Due to the stationatity of

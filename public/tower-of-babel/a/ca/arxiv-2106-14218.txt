Ultra-spinning Chow’s black holes in six-dimensional gauged supergravity and their properties
Di Wu, Shuang-Qing Wu

Abstract
By taking the ultra-spinning limit as a simple solution-generating trick, a novel class of ultra-spinning charged black hole solutions has been constructed from Chow’s rotating charged black hole with two equal-charge parameters in six-dimensional 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 gauged supergravity theory. We investigate their thermodynamical properties and then demonstrate that all thermodynamical quantities completely obey both the differential first law and the Bekenstein-Smarr mass formula. For the six-dimensional ultra-spinning Chow’s black hole with only one rotation parameter, we show that it does not always obey the reverse isoperimetric inequality, thus it can be either sub-entropic or super-entropic, depending upon the ranges of the mass parameter and especially the charge parameter. This property is obviously different from that of the six-dimensional singly-rotating Kerr-AdS super-entropic black hole, which always strictly violates the RII. For the six-dimensional doubly-rotating Chow’s black hole but ultra-spinning only along one spatial axis, we point out that it may also obey or violate the RII, and can be either super-entropic or sub-entropic in general.

2.4
Thermodynamical quantities

Now we turn to investigate some thermodynamical aspects of the above ultra-spinning version
of the six-dimensional singly-rotating Chow’s black hole. Although the event horizon of the
above ultra-spinning black hole (
4
) is noncompact, it has a finite area and the
Bekenstein-Hawking entropy is one quarter of its horizon area:

\hat{S}=\frac{\hat{A}_{+}}{4}=\frac{1}{3}\pi\mu\,r_{+}(r_{+}^{3}+l^{2}r_{+}+q)\,,

(10)

where
r_{+}
is the location of the event horizon, which is the largest root of the equation

\hat{\Delta}(r_{+})=0

\kappa

on the event horizon

\hat{T}=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}=\frac{\hat{\Delta}^{\prime}(r_{+})}{4\pi\,r_{+}(r_{+}^{3}+l^{2}r_{+}+q)}\,.

(11)

Note that the above ultra-spinning Chow’s black hole is rotating with the speed of light at
infinity, so one must work in a co-rotating frame at the infinity. The angular velocity of
the event horizon is given by

\hat{\Omega}=-\,\frac{g_{t\phi}}{g_{\varphi\varphi}}\Big|_{r=r_{+}}=\frac{r_{+}l}{r_{+}^{3}+l^{2}r_{+}+q}\,.

(12)

Due to the well falling-off behaviors of the scalar field and the two-form potential as well
as its field strength three-form, the electric charge

calculated by using the following Gauss’ law integral:

\hat{Q}=\frac{1}{8\pi}\int\star d\hat{A}=\frac{1}{2}\mu\sqrt{q(2m+q)}\,,

(13)

and its corresponding electrostatic potential at the event horizon reads

\hat{\Phi}=\big(\hat{A}_{\mu}\chi^{\mu}\big)\big|_{r=r_{+}}=\frac{\sqrt{q(2m+q)}}{r_{+}^{3}+l^{2}r_{+}+q}\,,

(14)

where
\chi=\partial_{t}+\hat{\Omega}\partial_{\varphi}

event horizon, which is also used to define the above surface gravity

2.5
RII

Ten years ago, it was conjectured
PRD84-024037
that the AdS black hole satisfies the
following RII:

\mathcal{R}=\Big[\frac{(D-1)\mathcal{V}}{\omega_{D-2}}\Big]^{1/(D-1)}\Big(\frac{\omega_{D-2}}{A}\Big)^{1/(D-2)}\geq 1\,.

(29)

Here
\mathcal{V}
is the thermodynamic volume,
A
is the horizon area, and
\omega_{D-2}

stands for the area of the space orthogonal to constant (
t,r
)-surfaces. Equality is attained
for the Schwarzschild-AdS black hole, which implies that the Schwarzschild-AdS black hole has
the maximum entropy. In other words, for a given entropy, the Schwarzschild-AdS black hole owns
the least volume, thus it is most efficient in storing information.

It is worthy to examine whether the above ultra-spinning solution of the six-dimensional
singly-rotating Chow’s black hole violates the RII or not. Note that, due to the compatification
of
\varphi
-axis, the volume of the
S^{4}
-sphere in this
D=6
spacetime is:
\omega_{4}=4\pi\mu/3
. Substituting the thermodynamic volume:
\mathcal{V}=\hat{\mathcal{V}}=(4r_{+}^{3}+q)A_{+}/(20r_{+}^{2})
and the horizon area:
A=\hat{A}_{+}=4\pi\mu\,r_{+}(r_{+}^{3}+l^{2}r_{+}+q)/3

into the above isoperimetric ratio (
29
), we now get

\mathcal{R}=\frac{\big(1+qr_{+}^{-3}/4\big)^{1/5}}{\big(1+l^{2}r_{+}^{-2}+qr_{+}^{-3}\big)^{1/20}}\,.

(30)

In the uncharged AdS black hole case (
q=0
), the above ratio always complies with the
inequalities:
0\leq\mathcal{R}<1
, and the black hole is always super-entropic

PRL115-031101
. In the charged case (
q>0
), the conclusion will change drastically,
and we will show that this ratio can be equal to, larger than or small than unity, so the new
ultra-spinning charged black hole can be either sub-entropic or super-entropic, depending upon
the ranges of the mass and charge parameters. This property is remarkably different from that
of the singly-rotating Kerr-AdS
6
super-entropic black hole
PRL115-031101
;
JHEP0615096
,
which always strictly violates the RII. By the way, the ratio
\mathcal{R}
is monotonically
decreasing with
r_{+}/l
when
q>0

\displaystyle h_{yy}\simeq\frac{ql^{2}(y^{2}-z^{2})}{2(l^{2}-y^{2})^{2}(y^{2}-a^{2})r}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})\,,\qquad h_{zz}\simeq\frac{ql^{2}(z^{2}-y^{2})}{2(l^{2}-z^{2})^{2}(z^{2}-a^{2})r}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})\,,

\displaystyle h_{rr}\simeq-\,\frac{3ql^{2}}{2r^{5}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-7})\,,\qquad h_{\phi\phi}\simeq-\,\frac{q}{2a^{2}l^{2}\Xi^{2}r}(a^{2}-y^{2})(a^{2}-z^{2})+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})\,,

\displaystyle h_{\phi\eta}\simeq-\,\frac{2m}{al^{5}\Xi^{3}\,r^{3}}(a^{2}-y^{2})(l^{2}-y^{2})(a^{2}-z^{2})(l^{2}-z^{2})+\mathcal{O}(r^{-5})\,,

\displaystyle h_{\eta\eta}\simeq\frac{(2m+q)l^{2}-qa^{2}}{l^{8}\Xi^{2}r^{3}}(l^{2}-y^{2})^{2}(l^{2}-z^{2})^{2}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-5})\,.

It can be seen that they are well-behaved at infinity.

Using the formulae (
23
), one can straightforwardly compute the conserved AD mass and
two angular momenta as follows:

\displaystyle M=\mathcal{Q}[\partial_{t}]=\frac{\mu(1+\Xi)(2m+q\Xi)}{6\Xi^{2}}\,,\quad

(40)

\displaystyle J_{1}=\mathcal{Q}[\partial_{\phi}]=\frac{\mu\,ma}{3\Xi^{2}}\,,\quad J_{2}=\mathcal{Q}[\partial_{\eta}]=\frac{\mu(2m+q\Xi)l}{6\Xi}\,,\qquad

(41)

which are obviously accordant with the results obtained in the last subsection.

It is not difficult to certify that the above thermodynamical quantities completely obey both
the differential and the integral mass formulae:

\displaystyle dM=TdS+\Omega_{1}dJ_{1}+\Omega_{2}dJ_{2}+\Phi\,dQ+\mathcal{V}dP+Kd\mu\,,

(42)

\displaystyle M=\frac{4}{3}(TS+\Omega_{1}J_{1}+\Omega_{2}J_{2})+\Phi\,Q-\frac{2}{3}\mathcal{V}P\,,

(43)

by introducing the thermodynamic volume

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\mu\,ma^{2}}{15\Xi^{2}}+\frac{\pi\mu}{15\Xi}\Big(4r_{+}+\frac{q}{r_{+}^{2}}-\frac{qa^{2}l^{2}}{r_{+}^{2}}B_{+}-\frac{q^{2}a^{2}l^{2}}{r_{+}B_{+}^{2}}\Big)B_{+}

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\mu}{15\Xi^{2}r_{+}}\Big(\big[(1+3\Xi)r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}\big]B_{+}+qr_{+}(r_{+}^{2}+l^{2})+q^{2}-\frac{q^{2}a^{2}l^{2}\Xi}{B_{+}}\Big)\,,

and a new chemical potential

\displaystyle K=-\frac{2m+q\Xi}{12\Xi\,B_{+}}\big[(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})(r_{+}^{2}-l^{2})+qr_{+}\big]=\frac{q^{2}a^{2}}{6\Xi\,r_{+}B_{+}}-\frac{(r_{+}^{2}+a^{2})(r_{+}^{4}-l^{4})+2qr_{+}^{3}+q^{2}}{12l^{2}\Xi\,r_{+}}\,,

which are conjugate to the pressure
P=5/(4\pi\,l^{2})
and the variable
\mu
, respectively.

3.3
RII

Finally, we would like to check whether the ultra-spinning version of the six-dimensional
doubly-rotating Chow’s charged black hole violate the RII or not. After substituting the
second expression for the thermodynamic volume (
44
), the horizon area
A_{+}=4\pi\mu\,B_{+}/(3\Xi)
, and
\omega_{4}=4\pi\mu/3
into the isoperimetric ratio (
29
), now it reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\omega_{4}^{1/20}(5\mathcal{V})^{1/5}}{A_{+}^{1/4}}=\Big(\frac{15\mathcal{V}}{4\pi\mu}\Big)^{1/5}\Big(\frac{\Xi}{B_{+}}\Big)^{1/4}

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\big[(1+3\Xi)r_{+}^{2}+a^{2}\big]B_{+}+qr_{+}(r_{+}^{2}+l^{2})+q^{2}-q^{2}a^{2}l^{2}\Xi/B_{+}}{4\Xi^{2}r_{+}}\bigg)^{1/5}\Big(\frac{\Xi}{B_{+}}\Big)^{1/4}\,,

which approaches to a finite limit:
[(1+3\Xi)/4]^{1/5}/\Xi^{3/20}\geq 1
at
r_{+}=\infty
,
provided that
0<\Xi\leq 1
.

Clearly, due to the complicated expression of the thermodynamic volume
\mathcal{V}
since
now the solution is also characterized by another rotation parameter
a
, it is very hard to
analytically judge when the value of
\mathcal{R}
is equal to, larger than or less than unity.

4
Conclusion

Taking advantage of simplicity of a solution-generating trick via taking the ultra-spinning
limit, in this paper we have constructed the ultra-spinning cousins from Chow’s two equal-charge
rotating AdS black hole in six-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
,
SU(2)
gauged supergravity theory.

As a first step, we have exhaustively explored the ultra-spinning black hole in the singly-rotating
case about some of its properties, namely, thermodynamics, horizon geometry, and in particular,
the RII, since all the expressions are relatively simple and very convenient for our purpose.
We have presented all necessary thermodynamical quantities and demonstrated that they obey both
the differential and integral mass formulae. Remarkably, for the six-dimensional ultra-spinning
Chow’s charged black hole with only one rotation parameter, we have clearly shown that it does
not always satisfy the RII anymore, and it can be either sub-entropic or super-entropic, depending
upon the ranges of the solution parameters, especially the charge parameter. This property is
obviously different from the uncharged case — the singly-rotating Kerr-AdS
6
super-entropic
black hole, which always strictly violates the RII.

We then turn to consider the doubly-rotating case, namely, the six-dimensional ultra-spinning
Chow’s charged AdS black hole with an additional rotation parameter. We have explicitly shown
the effects of the charge and rotation parameters on the isoperimetric ratio and indicated that
the RII can be either violated or satisfied in the general case, and accordingly the ultra-spinning
solution can be either super-entropic or sub-entropic, depending upon on the ranges of the solution
parameters. A related subject is to extend the present work to investigate the two equal-charge
rotating black holes in five
PRD72-041901
and seven
CQG25-175010
dimensions. We
hope to report the related progress along this direction soon.

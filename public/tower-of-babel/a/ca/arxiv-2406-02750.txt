A Fast Compensated Algorithm for Computing Givens Rotations
Carlos F. Borges

Abstract
. We develop a very simple compensated scheme for computing very accurate Givens rotations. The approach is significantly more straightforward than the one in [ 4 ] , and the derivation leads to a very satisfying algorithm whereby a naively computed Givens rotation can be used to construct a correction to itself. It is also seen that this scheme continues to provide high accuracy even when built on a hypoteneuse calculation that is of lesser accuracy.

3.
A Compensated Approach to Givens Rotations

Although the true quantities
c
and
s
satisfy four mathematical conditions -

(2)

\displaystyle cs-sc

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(3)

\displaystyle c^{2}+s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1

(4)

\displaystyle cg-sf

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

(5)

\displaystyle cf+sg

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r

the computed quantities
\bar{c}
and
\bar{s}
may not. In fact, with the exception of condition
2
, which is satisfied by the computed quantities in any IEEE754 compliant FPS since multiplication is commutative, the remaining three conditions are frequently not satisfied by the computed values, no matter how accurately they are computed.

We examine the problem by writing the true values of
c
and
s
as simple additive perturbations to the computed values by setting

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{c}+\delta_{c}

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{s}+\delta_{s}

Conditions
3
,
4
,
5
become

\displaystyle(\bar{c}+\delta_{c})^{2}+(\bar{s}+\delta_{s})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle(\bar{c}+\delta_{c})g-(\bar{s}+\delta_{s})f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle(\bar{c}+\delta_{c})f+(\bar{s}+\delta_{s})g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r

which can be rearranged to the following set of three equations for the two unknown perturbations
\delta_{c}
and
\delta_{s}

(6)

\displaystyle 2\bar{c}\delta_{c}+2\bar{s}\delta_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\bar{c}^{2}-\bar{s}^{2}-\delta_{c}^{2}-\delta_{s}^{2}

(7)

\displaystyle-g\delta_{c}+f\delta_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{c}g-\bar{s}f

(8)

\displaystyle f\delta_{c}+g\delta_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r-\bar{c}f-\bar{s}g.

Experience indicates that the most useful approach to solving these equations, at least approximately, is to ignore the tiny quantities
\delta_{c}^{2}
and
\delta_{s}^{2}
in equation
6

8

4

Furthermore, dividing equation
7

g/r

\bar{c}

\bar{s}

(9)

\begin{bmatrix}\bar{c}&\bar{s}\\

\delta_{s}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}(1-\bar{c}^{2}-\bar{s}^{2})/2\\
(\bar{c}g-\bar{s}f)/r\end{bmatrix}

And this is solved, at least approximately, by simply applying the transpose of the originally computed Givens rotation. That is

(10)

\begin{bmatrix}\delta_{c}\\
\delta_{s}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\bar{c}&-\bar{s}\\
\bar{s}&\bar{c}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}(1-\bar{c}^{2}-\bar{s}^{2})/2\\
(\bar{c}g-\bar{s}f)/r\end{bmatrix}

It is critical to compute the quantities on the right hand side of
9
as accurately as possible since they both involve
significant
cancellation. To do this we can use three applications of a double length product such as the
2MultFMA()
algorithm (see
[
8
]
) and one additional
fma()

2MultFMA()
algorithm represents the product to two floating point numbers as a sum of two numbers of the same format. Specifically, given
a
and
b
in a specific floating point format, it finds
p
and
pp
in the same format so that
p+pp=ab
exactly. The algorithm from
[
8
]
is as follows

Algorithm 3
.

2MultFMA(a,b)

p=a*b

pp=fma(a,b,-p)

Using this device and then carefully summing will allow us to accurately compute the terms we need. This approach leads us to the following algorithm for a compensated Givens rotation:

Algorithm 4
.

Compensated DLARTG

r=hypot(f,g)

\bar{c}=f/r

\bar{s}=g/r

c_{1},c_{2}=2MultFMA(\bar{c},\bar{c})

s_{1},s_{2}=2MultFMA(\bar{s},\bar{s})

if

|c|\geq|s|

then

\epsilon_{Norm}=(1-c_{1}-s_{1}-c_{2}-s_{2})/2

else

\epsilon_{Norm}=(1-s_{1}-c_{1}-s_{2}-c_{2})/2

end

if

p,pp=2MultFMA(\bar{c},g)

\epsilon_{Orth}=(fma(-s,f,p)+pp)/r

\delta_{c}=\bar{c}*\epsilon_{Norm}-\bar{s}*\epsilon_{Orth}

\delta_{s}=\bar{s}*\epsilon_{Norm}+\bar{c}*\epsilon_{Orth}

\bar{c}=\bar{c}+\delta_{c}

\bar{s}=\bar{s}+\delta_{s}

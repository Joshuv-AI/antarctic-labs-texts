Affine statistical bundle modeled on a Gaussian Orlicz-Sobolev space
Giovanni Pistone

Abstract
. The dually flat structure of statistical manifolds can be derived in a non-parametric way from a particular case of affine space defined on a qualified set of probability measures. The statistically natural displacement mapping of the affine space depends on the notion of Fisher’s score. The model space must be carefully defined if the state space is not finite. Among various options, we discuss how to use Orlicz-Sobolev spaces with Gaussian weight. Such a fully non-parametric set-up provides tools to discuss intrinsically infinite-dimensional evolution problems. keywords Information geometry, Gaussian Orlicz-Sobolev space, Statistical Bundle, Exponential Manifold, Dually Flat Affine Manifold

Non-parametric statistical bundle

Professor S.-I. Amari clearly stated in a 1987 conference paper
[
3
]
the notion of a non-parametric fiber bundle in Information Geometry (IG). He says

A fibre bundle is constructed on a finite-dimensional parametric statistical model with a Hilbert space as the fibre space. The Hilbert space represents the tangent directions of the set of probability distributions in the function space. A pair of dual linear connections is introduced in the Hilbert bundle.

A related journal paper
[
6
]
shows the applications to the statistics of semi-parametric statistical models. R. E. Kass and P. W. Vos, in their 1997 monograph
[
25
, 10.3]
, further explain the construction by describing the tangent fiber
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
of a statistical model
\mathcal{M}
as a vector space of random variables:

The tangent space
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
is an inner product space of random variables having zero expectation and finite variance that describe
\mathcal{M}
at
p
. Finite-dimensional vector bundles enlarge
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
by adding other random variables, each of which has mean zero and finite variance. The Hilbert bundle carries this process to its conclusion by enlarging
T_{p}\mathcal{M}
to the space of all random variables having zero mean and finite variance.

I started to work on a non-parametric version of professor Amari’s ideas in the early nineties

If

In which sense does each fiber

is a convex set whose tangent space is the set

The Radon-Nikodym derivative

\int\ell(\theta)\,d\mu(\theta)=\int d\dot{\mu}(\theta)=0

\mu_{\theta}=p_{\theta}\cdot\mu

\ell(\theta)=\frac{\dot{p}_{\theta}}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{d}{d\theta}\log p_{\theta}

That is,

Conversely, let be given an element

Let us fix a reference measure

strictly positive probability densities. If the sample space is finite, all conditions outlined above are met. The Hilbert bundle is an expression of the tangent bundle where Fisher’s score expresses the velocity of one-dimensional curves.

If the sample space is not finite, one must introduce restrictions on both the set of probabilities and the random variables in the fiber. One option is to restrict to a convenient subset of positive densities

in such a way there is a vector space

S_{p}\mathcal{E}=\left\{u\in B(\mathcal{E})\,\middle|\,\int u\,p\,d\mu=0\right\}\ .

For

A similar but different approach will be used below. Both depend on the idea of finding a convenient class of random variables

For my purpose, the most important of professor Amari’s contributions has been the

The equations above clearly define a geometry of probability measures that is related but different from the previously studied Riemannian geometry based on the notion of Fisher-Rao information matrix taken as an expression of an inner product between tangent vectors. This new geometry originated, a least in the statistical community, with the idea of defining the geometry of curved exponential models as embedded in a larger exponential family

Such a theory has been known for a long time in statistical mechanics. The main difference is that R. Fisher and other statisticians of the same period used to think about parsimoniously parameterized models. In contrast, physicists such as Boltzmann and Gibbs used to think in terms of simple relations between statistical observables. The exponential family appears as a model with peculiar invariance properties in statistical mechanics.

2.1.
Orlicz spaces

Assume
\phi\in C[0,+\infty[
is null at 0,
\phi(0)=0
, strictly increasing, and
\lim_{u\to+\infty}\phi(u)=+\infty
. Let
\Phi
be its primitive function with
\Phi(0)=0
. We call such a function a Young function. The inverse function
\psi=\phi^{-1}
has the same properties as
\phi
, so that the primitive
\Psi
with
\Psi(0)=0
is again a Young function. The couple
(\Phi,\Psi)
, is a couple of conjugate Young functions. The relation is symmetric and we write both
\Psi=\Phi_{*}
and
\Phi=\Psi_{*}
. The Young inequality holds true,

\Phi(x)+\Psi(y)\geq xy\ ,\quad x,y\geq 0\ ,

and the Legendre equality holds true,

\Phi(x)+\Psi(\phi(x))=x\phi(x)\ ,\quad x\geq 0\ .

Here are my notations for specific cases we are going to use:

(3)

\displaystyle\Phi(x)={x^{p}}/p\ ,\quad\Psi(y)={y^{q}}/q\ ,\quad p,q>1\ ,\quad 1/p+1/q=1\ ;

(4)

\displaystyle\exp_{2}(x)=\mathrm{e}^{x}-1-x\ ,\quad(\exp_{2})_{*}(y)=(1+y)\log(1+y)-y\ ;

(5)

\displaystyle\cosh_{2}(x)=\cosh x-1\ ,\quad(\cosh_{2})_{*}(y)=\int_{0}^{y}\sinh^{-1}(v)\ dv\ ;

(6)

\displaystyle{\operatorname{gauss}_{2}}(x)=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\right)-1\ .

Given a Young function
\Phi
, and a probability measure
\mu
, the Orlicz space
L_{\Phi}\left(\mu\right)
is the Banach space whose closed unit ball is

\left\{f\in L^{0}(\mu)\,\middle|\,\int\Phi(\left|f\right|)\ d\mu\leq 1\right\}\ .

The corresponding Minkowski norm is usually called Luxemburg norm,

\left\|f\right\|_{L_{\Phi}\left(\mu\right)}=\inf\left\{\alpha>0\,\middle|\,\int\Phi(\alpha^{-1}\left|f\right|)\ d\mu\leq 1\right\}\ .

The Young inequality provides a separating duality
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\mu}=\int uv\ d\mu
of
L_{\Phi}\left(\mu\right)
and
L_{\Phi_{*}}\left(\mu\right)
such that
\left\langle u,v\right\rangle_{\mu}\leq 2\left\|u\right\|_{L_{\Phi}\left(\mu\right)}\left\|v\right\|_{L_{\Phi_{*}}\left(\mu\right)}
. The dual norm is called the Orlicz norm and is equivalent to the Luxembourg norm.

Domination relation between Young functions implies continuous injection properties for the corresponding Orlicz spaces. We say that
\Phi_{2}

eventually dominates

\Phi_{1}
, written
\Phi_{1}\prec\Phi_{2}
, if there is a constant

such that
\Phi_{1}(x)\leq\Phi_{2}(\kappa x)
for all

. As, in our case,
\mu
is a probability measure, the continuous embedding
L_{\Phi_{2}}\left(\mu\right)\to L_{\Phi_{1}}\left(\mu\right)
holds if, and only if,
\Phi_{1}\prec\Phi_{2}
. See proof in
[
1
, Th. 8.2]
. If
\Phi_{1}\prec\Phi_{2}
, then
(\Phi_{2})_{*}\prec(\Phi_{1})_{*}
. Looking at the examples above,

A special case occurs when there exists a function

for all

. This is true, for example, for a power function and in the case of the functions

The spaces corresponding to case (

2.3.
Calculus of the Gaussian space

From now on, the base probability space is the Gaussian probability space
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\gamma)
,
\gamma(z)=(2\pi)^{n/2}\exp\left(-\left|z\right|^{2}/2\right)
. Let us recall a few simple facts about the analysis of the Gaussian space, see
[
32
, Ch. V]
.

Let us denote by
C^{k}_{\text{poly}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
k=0,1,\dots
, the vector space of functions which are differentiable up to order
k
and which are bounded, together with all derivatives, by a polynomial. This class of functions is dense in
L^{2}(\gamma)
. For each couple
f,g\in C^{1}_{\text{poly}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, we have

\int{f(x)\ \partial_{i}g(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=\int{\delta_{i}f(x)\ g(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\ ,

where the divergence operator
\delta_{i}
is defined by
\delta_{i}f(x)=x_{i}f(x)-\partial_{i}f(x)
. Vector case is

\int{\nabla f(x)\cdot\nabla g(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=\int{f(x)\ \delta\cdot\nabla g(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\ ,\quad f,g\in C^{2}_{\text{poly}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\ ,

with
\delta\cdot\nabla g(x)=x\cdot\nabla g(x)-\Delta g(x)
.

Hermite polynomials
H_{\alpha}=\delta^{\alpha}1
provide an orthogonal basis for
L^{2}(\gamma)
such that
\partial_{i}H_{\alpha}=\alpha_{i}H_{\alpha-e_{i}}
,
e_{1}
the
i
-th element of the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In turn, this provides a way to prove that there is a closure of both operator
\partial_{i}
and
\delta_{i}
on a domain which is an Hilbert subspace of
L^{2}(\gamma)
. Such a space is denoted by
D^{2}
in
[
32
]
. Moreover, the closure of
\partial_{i}
is the infinitesimal generator of the translation operator,
[
33
,
11
]
. The space
D^{2}
is a Sobolev Space with Gaussian weight based on the
L^{2}
norm,
[
1
]
.

2.5.
Orlicz spaces with derivatives

By replacing the

-norm with a
{\cosh_{2}}

Let us start with a class of inequalities related to the classical Gauss-Poincaré inequality,

\int{\left(f(x)-\int{f(y)}\,\gamma(y)\,dy\,\right)^{2}}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\leq\int{\left|\nabla f(x)\right|^{2}}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\ ,

where

[

\left\|f-\overline{f}\right\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leq\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\right\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}
, where
\overline{f}=\int{f(y)}\,\gamma(y)\,dy\,
.

For
example
, if

is a probability density with respect to
\gamma
, then the
\chi^{2}
-divergence of

from
\gamma

D_{\chi^{2}}(P|\gamma)=\int{(p(x)-1)^{2}}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\leq\int{(\delta\cdot\nabla p(x))^{2}}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\ .

Generalizations of the Gauss-Poincaré inequality follow from the properties of the Ornstein-Uhlenbeck (OU) semigroup

P_{t}f(x)=\int{f(\mathrm{e}^{-t}x+\sqrt{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2t}}y)}\,\gamma(y)\,dy\,,\quad t\geq 0,\quad f\in C^{k}_{\text{poly}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\ ,

see
[
32
, V-1.5]
and
[
37
, 1.3]
. Notice that the OU semigroup interpolates between
P_{0}f=f
and
P_{\infty}f=\overline{f}
. If
X
,
Y
are independent standard Gaussian random variables in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then

X_{t}=\mathrm{e}^{-t}X+\sqrt{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2t}}Y,\quad Y_{t}=\sqrt{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2t}}X-\mathrm{e}^{-t}Y

are independent standard Gaussian random variables for all
t\geq 0
.
By the change of variable
(X,Y)\to(X_{t},Y_{t})
and Jensen’s inequality, it follows for each convex
\Phi
that

\int{\Phi(P_{t}f(x))}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\leq\int{\Phi(f(x))}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\ .

That is, for all
t\geq 0
, the mapping
f\mapsto P_{t}f
is non-expansive for the norm of each Orlicz space
L_{\Phi}\left(\gamma\right)
.

For all
\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}
convex and all
f\in C^{1}_{\text{poly}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, it holds

(7)

\int{\Phi\left(f(x)-\int{f(y)}\,\gamma(y)\,dy\,\right)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\leq\\
\iint\Phi\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\nabla f(x)\cdot y\right)\ \gamma(x)\gamma(y)\ dxdy=\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\iint\Phi\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\left|\nabla f(x)\right|z\right)\ \mathrm{e}^{-z^{2}/2}\gamma(x)\ dzdx=\\
\int{\widetilde{\Phi}\left(\left|\nabla f(x)\right|\right)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\ ,

where
\widetilde{\Phi}
is the convex function

\widetilde{\Phi}(a)=\int{\Phi\left(\frac{\pi}{2}az\right)}\,\gamma(z)\,dz\,\ .

The first example of convex function is
\Phi(s)=\mathrm{e}^{s}
, with
\widetilde{\Phi}(a)=\exp\left(\frac{\pi^{2}a^{2}}{8}\right)
, so that the inequality applied to
\frac{2\kappa}{\pi}f
becomes

(8)

\int{\exp\left(\frac{2\kappa}{\pi}\left(f(x)-\overline{f}\right)\right)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\leq\int{\exp\left(\frac{\kappa^{2}}{2}\left|\nabla f(x)\right|^{2}\right)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,

If the function

is Lipschitz with norm

The first case of bound for Orlicz norms we is the
\Phi(s)=s^{2p}

where

The cases
\Phi(a)=a^{2p}
are special in that we can use them in the proof the multiplicative property
\Phi(ab)=\Phi(a)\Phi(b)
. The argument generalizes to the case where the convex function
\Phi
is a Young function whose increase is controlled through a function

By using this bound in

\int{\Phi\left(\kappa\left(f(x)-\int{f(y)}\,\gamma(y)\,dy\,\right)\right)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\leq\int{\Phi\left(\left|\nabla f(x)\right|\right)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\ .

Assume now that

It is of particular interest the case of the Young function

with

\int{(\cosh-1)\left(\frac{2\kappa}{\pi}\left(f(x)-\overline{f}\right)\right)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\leq\\

Now, if

2\kappa/\pi\left\|f-\overline{f}\right\|_{L_{\cosh-1}\left(\gamma\right)}\leq 1

\left\|f-\overline{f}\right\|_{L_{\cosh-1}\left(\gamma\right)}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}\left\|\left|\nabla f\right|\right\|_{L_{\operatorname{gauss}_{2}}\left(\gamma\right)}\ .

In the following

There exists constants

(11)

(12)

We have, for all

Note that

\nabla P_{t}f(x)=\nabla\int{f(\mathrm{e}^{-t}x+\sqrt{1-\mathrm{e}^{-2t}}y)}\,\gamma(y)\,dy\,=\\

so that

Now,

f(x)-\overline{f}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\delta\cdot P_{t}\nabla f(x)\ dt\ .

As

the covariance of

\Cov_{\gamma}\left(f,g\right)=\\
\int{(f(x)-\overline{f})g(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=\int{(f(x)-\overline{f})(g(x)-\overline{g})}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\ .

It follows that for all

14

(15)

\Cov_{\gamma}\left(f,g\right)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t}\int{P_{t}\nabla f(x)\cdot\nabla g(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,\ dt\ .

We use here a result of

\left|\cdot\right|_{1}

The following

Given a couple of conjugate Young function

2.6.
Orlicz-Sobolev space with Gaussian weight

A reasonable option for our model space is to assume densities
f=\mathrm{e}^{u-K_{\gamma}(u)}\cdot\gamma
in the Gaussian maximal exponential family,
f\in\Maxexp\left(\gamma\right)
, and, moreover, assume differentiability in the form
u\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}}^{2}\left(\gamma\right)=L_{{\operatorname{gauss}_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
, that is,
u^{2}\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
, see
section

2.2
.

Precisely, the exponential and the mixture Orlicz-Sobolev-Gauss (OSG) spaces of interest are, respectively,

(16)

\displaystyle W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{v\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)\,\middle|\,\partial_{j}v\in L_{{\operatorname{gauss}_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)\right\}\ ,

(17)

\displaystyle W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{\eta\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)\,\middle|\,\partial_{j}f\in L_{{\operatorname{gauss}_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)\right\}\ ,

where
\partial_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,n
, is the partial derivative in the sense of distributions.

As
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
implies
\phi\gamma\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, for each
f\in W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)
it holds

\left\langle\partial_{j}f,\phi\right\rangle_{\gamma}=\left\langle\partial_{j}f,\phi\gamma\right\rangle=-\left\langle f,\partial_{j}(\phi\gamma)\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\gamma(u_{j}-\partial_{j})\phi\right\rangle=\left\langle f,\delta_{j}\phi\right\rangle_{\gamma}\ ,

with
\delta_{j}\phi=(u_{j}-\partial_{j})\phi
. Here, the
Stein operator

\delta_{i}
acts on
C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)

[
13
]
.

The meaning of both operators
\partial_{j}
and
\delta_{j}=(u_{j}-\partial_{j})
when acting on square-integrable random variables of the Gaussian space is well known. Still, here we are specifically interested in the action on OSG spaces. Let us denote by
C_{\mathrm{p}}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
the space of infinitely differentiable functions with polynomial growth of all derivatives. Polynomial growth implies the existence of
\gamma
-moments of all derivatives, hence
C_{\mathrm{p}}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\subset W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)
. If
f\in C_{\mathrm{p}}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
, then the distributional derivative and the ordinary derivative are equal and moreover
\delta_{j}f\in C_{\mathrm{p}}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
. For each
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
we have
\left\langle\phi,\delta_{j}f\right\rangle_{\gamma}=\left\langle\partial_{j}\phi,f\right\rangle_{\gamma}
.

The OSG spaces
W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
and

are both Banach spaces
[
35
, Sec. 10]
. The norm is the graph norm,

\displaystyle\left\|v\right\|_{W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)}=\left\|v\right\|_{L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|\partial_{j}v\right\|_{L_{{\operatorname{gauss}_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)}\ ,

\displaystyle\left\|\eta\right\|_{W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)}=\left\|\eta\right\|_{L_{{\cosh_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left\|\partial_{j}\eta\right\|_{L_{{\operatorname{gauss}_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)}\ .

In the cases of null integral,
eq.

12
shows that the second term only provides an equivalent norm for
W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
.

We review some relations between OSG spaces and Sobolev spaces without weight
[
1
]
in the following

. For each ball radius

,

(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\geq\gamma(x)\geq\gamma(x)(\left|x\right|<R)\geq(2\pi)^{-\frac{n}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{R^{2}}{2}}(\left|x\right|<R),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Let
\Omega_{R}
denote the open sphere of radius
R>0
and consider the restriction
u\mapsto u_{R}
of

to
\Omega_{R}
.

(1)

We have the continuous mappings

W^{1,(\cosh-1)}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\subset W^{1,(\cosh-1)}\left(\gamma\right)\rightarrow W^{1,p}(\Omega_{R}),\quad p\geq 1.

(2)

We have the continuous mappings

W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset W^{1,(\cosh-1)_{*}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\subset W^{1,(\cosh-1)_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)\rightarrow W^{1,1}(\Omega_{R}),\quad p>1.

Each

is a.s. Hölder of all orders on each

and hence a.s. continuous. The restriction

For

because

which is again a
p
-expectation. To minimize the Hyvärinen divergence we must minimize the
p
-expected value of the local score

S(q,x)=\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla\log q(x)\right|^{2}-\delta\cdot\nabla\log q(x)

If
p
and
q
belong to the maximal exponential model of
\gamma
, then
q=\mathrm{e}^{u-K(u)}
with
u\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
and
\int{u(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=0
. The local score becomes
\frac{1}{2}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}-\delta\cdot\nabla u
. To compute the
p
-expected value of the score with an independent sample of
p\cdot\gamma
, we have an interest to assume that the score is in
L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
, because this assumption implies the good convergence of the empirical means for all
p
. Assume, for
example
,
\nabla u\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}}^{2}\left(\gamma\right)=L_{{\operatorname{gauss}_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
. This implies directly
\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}\in L_{(\cosh-1)}\left(\gamma\right)
. Moreover, we must assume that the
L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
-norm of
\delta\cdot\nabla u
is finite. Under such assumptions, one hopes that the minimization of a suitable model of the sample expectation of the Hyvärinen score is consistent.

3.3.
Otto’s metric

This metric was originally defined in
[
38
]
. Let be given in the maximal exponential model of
\gamma
,
p\in\Maxexp\left(\gamma\right)
, and let
f
and
g
be given in the
p
-fiber of the statistical statistical bundle, that is,
f,g\in W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(p\right)=W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
and
\int{f(x)\,p}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=\int{g(x)\,p}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=0
. Otto’s inner product is

(f,g)\mapsto\langle\langle f,g\rangle\rangle_{p}=\int{\nabla f(x)\cdot\nabla g(x)\ p(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=\\
\int{f(x)\ \delta\cdot(p(x)\nabla g(x))}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=\left\langle f,\delta\cdot(p\nabla g)\right\rangle_{p}\ .

The LHS is well defined and continuous if
\nabla f,\nabla g\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}}^{2}\left(\gamma\right)
, because, in such a case,
\left|\nabla f\right|^{2},\left|\nabla g\right|^{2}\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)=L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(p\right)
. The RHS, if defined, is an inner product in
L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
. Note that the mapping
g\mapsto\delta\cdot(p\nabla g)
is 1-to-1 if
g
because by
\int{g(x)p(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=0
. The inverse of this mapping provides the natural gradient of Otto’s inner product in the sense of
[
4
]
and
[
28
]
.

3.4.
Boltzmann equation

The space-homogeneous Boltzmann operator with angular collision kernel
B(z,x)=\left|x^{\prime}z\right|
is discussed, for example, in
[
52
]
. I briefly show below how to use Gaussian Orlicz spaces in this context, see
[
41
]
and
[
29
]
.

The mixture bundle
\prescript{*}{}{S}\Maxexp\left(\gamma\right)
is the set of all couples
(f,\eta)
with
f\in\Maxexp\left(\gamma\right)
,
\eta\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)
, and
\int{\eta(x)\,f(x)}\,\gamma(x)\,dx\,=0
. The
∗
-notation recalls that the dual of
L_{{\cosh_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)
is
L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
. The Boltzmann operator is the mapping
\Maxexp\left(\gamma\right)\mapsto Q(f)
with

Q(f)(v)=\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\int_{S^{2}}(f(v-x{x^{\prime}}(v-w))f(w+x{x^{\prime}}(v-w))-f(v)f(w))\left|{x^{\prime}}(v-w)\right|\ \sigma(dx)\ dw\ ,

where
x^{\prime}
is the transpose of the column vector
x
,
S_{2}
is the unit sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and
\sigma
is the uniform probability on
S_{2}
. One can prove that
f\mapsto Q(f)/f
is a section of the mixture bundle. The Boltzmann equation can be seen as the equation
\accentset{\star}{f}=Q(f)/f
.

The smoothness of the Boltzmann section follows from a superposition of operators:

(1)

Product:
{\mathcal{E}}\left(f_{0}\right)\ni f\mapsto f\otimes f\in{\mathcal{E}}\left(f_{0}\otimes f_{0}\right)
;

(2)

Interaction:
{\mathcal{E}}\left(f_{0}\otimes f_{0}\right)\ni f\otimes f\mapsto g=Bf\otimes f\in{\mathcal{E}}\left(f_{0}\otimes f_{0}\right)
;

(3)

Conditioning:
{\mathcal{E}}\left(f_{0}\otimes f_{0}\right)\ni g\mapsto\int_{S_{2}}{g\circ A_{x}}\ {\sigma(dx)}\in{\mathcal{E}}\left(f_{0}\otimes f_{0}\right)
;

(4)

Marginalization.

There is a weak form of the Boltzmann section. Let
v,w
be a couple of velocities before collision and let us denote by
(v_{x},w_{x})
the velocities after collision, see
[
52
]
. For
f\in\Maxexp\left(\gamma\right)
and
g\in L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
, define the operator
A
with

Ag(v,w)=\int_{S^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}(g(v_{x})+g(w_{x}))}\ {\sigma(dx)}-\frac{1}{2}(g(v)+g(w))\ .

Ag
is in
L_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma^{\otimes 2}\right)
and
\left\langle g,Q(f)/f\right\rangle_{f}=\Expectation_{f\otimes f}\left[Ag\right]
.

3.5.
Other possible applications

The two cases below are open suggestions.

(1)

The transport problem
[
40
]
is discussed from the point of view compatible with IG in
[
31
,
34
]
. But it does not fit the strictly positive probability densities assumption. However, suppose the given margins belong to the exponential manifold. In that case, it is possible to consider the exponential sub-bundle with the given margins and discuss the gradient flow of the given optimization problem. This is done in a particular finite case in
[
46
]
.

(2)

The dual couple
W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}}\left(\gamma\right)
and
W^{1,2}_{{\cosh_{2}}_{*}}\left(\gamma\right)
define a couple of dual bundles on the set
\Maxexp\left(\gamma\right)
where velocity, acceleration, moment, gradient, Hessian, are all well defined from the affine structure. A real function on the exponential bundle is a Lagrangian function, and a real function on the mixture bundle is a Hamiltonian function. The conjugation relation holds, and the mechanic’s equations provide a dynamic picture of the statistical bundle. For example, one can consider the Lagrangian function, where entropy takes the role of the potential energy, and Fisher’s metric takes the role of the kinetic energy. This was done in the finite case in
[
44
]
and
[
18
]
.

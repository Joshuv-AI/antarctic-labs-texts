Abstract
. This paper investigates the time asymptotic stability of composite waves formed by two shock waves within the context of one-dimensional relaxed compressible Navier-Stokes equations. We establish the nonlinear stability of the composite waves consisting of two viscous shocks under the condition of having two small, independent wave strengths and the presence of small initial perturbations. Furthermore, the solutions of the relaxed system are observed to globally converge over time to those of the classical system as the relaxation parameter approaches zero. The methods are based on relative entropy, the a a -contraction with shifts theory and fundamental energy estimates. Keywords : Relaxed compressible Navier-Stokes equations; asymptotic stability; relaxation limit; composite waves of two shock wave; relative entropy; energy estimates AMS classification code : 76N15, 35B35, 35Q35



1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the one-dimensional isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations, complemented by Maxwell’s constitutive relations. The equations are formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{t}+(\rho u)_{x}=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+(\rho u^{2})_{x}+p(\rho)_{x}=\Pi_{x},\\
\tilde{\tau}(\rho)(\Pi_{t}+u\Pi_{x})+\Pi=\mu u_{x},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
. Here,
\rho
,
u
,
\Pi
represent fluid density, velocity and stress, respectively.
\mu>0
is the viscosity constant.
The pressure
p
is assumed to satisfy the usual
\gamma
-law,
p(\rho)=A\rho^{\gamma}
where
\gamma>1
denotes the adiabatic index and
A
is any positive constant.
Without loss of generality, we assume
A=1
in the sequel.

The constitutive relation
\eqref{1.1}_{3}
, first introduced by Maxwell in
[
35
]
, serves to delineate the connection between the stress tensor and velocity gradient for non-simple fluid. The relaxation parameter
\tilde{\tau}=\tilde{\tau}(\rho)
describe the time lag in response of the stress tensor to velocity gradient. In fact, even for simple fluid, water for example, the
time lag
does exists but it is very small ranging from 1 ps to 1 ns, see
[
34
,
40
]
. However, Pelton et al.
[
38
]
showed that such a
time lag
cannot be neglected, even for simple fluids, in the experiments of high-frequency (20GHZ) vibration of nano-scale mechanical devices immersed in water-glycerol mixtures. It was shown that, see also
[
2
]
, equation
\eqref{1.1}_{3}
provides a general formalism with which to characterize the fluid-structure interaction of nano-scale mechanical devies vibrating in simple fluids.

Assuming that
\tilde{\tau}(\rho)=\tau\rho
, where
\tau
is a positive constant, equation
\eqref{1.1}_{3}
reduces to

\displaystyle\tau\rho(\Pi_{t}+u\Pi_{x})+\Pi=\mu u_{x}.

(1.2)

From the standpoint of conservation laws, Freistühler initially proposed the constitutive equation (
1.2
) in
[
5
,
6
]
in multi-dimensional case. Under this assumption, equation (
1.2
) exhibits conservation properties by utilizing mass equation
\eqref{1.1}_{1}
, making it straightforward to define weak solutions. For ease of analysis, we restate the system (
1.1
) with the assumption
\tilde{\tau}(\rho)=\tau\rho
in Lagrangian coordinates as follows:

\begin{cases}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
u_{t}+p_{x}=\Pi_{x},\\
\tau\Pi_{t}+v\Pi=\mu u_{x},\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
v=\frac{1}{\rho}
denotes the specific volume per unit mass.

We are interested in the Cauchy problem to system (
1.3
) for the functions

\displaystyle(v,u,\Pi):[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}

with initial conditions

\displaystyle(v,u,\Pi)(0,x)=(v_{0},u_{0},\Pi_{0})(x)\rightarrow(v_{\pm},u_{\pm},0)\quad(x\rightarrow\pm\infty).

(1.4)

The large-time behavior of solutions to system (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) is closely
related to the Riemann problem of the associated
p
-system

\begin{cases}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=0,\end{cases}

(1.5)

with the Riemann initial data:

(v,u)(t=0,x)=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-}),\quad x<0,\\
(v_{+},u_{+}),\quad x>0.\end{cases}

(1.6)

We recall that there exists a unique intermediate state
(v_{m},u_{m})
connected to
(v_{-},u_{-})
and
(v_{+},u_{+})
by 1-shock wave and 2-shock wave, respectively. And it satisfies Rankine-Hugoniot condition

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sigma_{1}(v_{m}-v_{-})=-(u_{m}-u_{-}),\\
\sigma_{1}(u_{m}-u_{-})=p(v_{m})-p(v_{-}),\end{cases}\begin{cases}\sigma_{2}(v_{+}-v_{m})=-(u_{+}-u_{m}),\\
\sigma_{2}(u_{+}-u_{m})=p(v_{+})-p(v_{m}),\end{cases}

(1.7)

and Lax entropy condition

\displaystyle-\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{-})}<\sigma_{1}<-\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{m})},

(1.8)

\displaystyle\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{m})}<\sigma_{2}<\sqrt{-p^{\prime}(v_{+})}.

Then, the Riemann problem of the associated
p
-system has a composite wave solution
(\bar{v},\bar{u}):=(v_{1}^{s},u_{1}^{s})+(v_{2}^{s},u_{2}^{s})-(v_{m},u_{m})
, where 1-shock wave solution
(v_{1}^{s},u_{1}^{s})
and 2-shock wave solution
(v_{2}^{s},u_{2}^{s})
defined as follows (see
[
41
]
):

(v_{1}^{s},u_{1}^{s})(t,x)=\begin{cases}(v_{-},u_{-}),\quad x<\sigma_{1}t,\\
(v_{m},u_{m}),\quad x>\sigma_{1}t,\end{cases}(v_{2}^{s},u_{2}^{s})(t,x)=\begin{cases}(v_{m},u_{m}),\quad x<\sigma_{2}t,\\
(v_{+},u_{+}),\quad x>\sigma_{2}t.\end{cases}

If
\tau=0
, the system (
1.3
) reduces to classical compressible isentropic Navier-Stokes equations:

\begin{cases}v_{t}-u_{x}=0,\\
u_{t}+p(v)_{x}=\left(\mu\frac{u_{x}}{v}\right)_{x}.\end{cases}

(1.9)

The asymptotic behavior of solutions for system (
1.9
) and its non-isentropic counterpart has been extensively explored with a variety of initial conditions, as documented in
[
9
,
10
,
15
,
16
,
25
,
28
,
29
,
30
]
. Notably, for shock profile initial data, Matsumura and Nishihara
[
28
]
, Goodman
[
7
]
pioneered the establishment of the stability of traveling waves with sufficiently small initial disturbances and zero mass condition, by utilizing the anti-derivative method. Furthermore, Huang and Matsumura
[
16
]
demonstrated the asymptotic stability of a composite wave consisting of two viscous shocks within the Navier-Stokes-Fourier system, given that the shocks are of small magnitude and of the same order.

In a recent advancement, Kang, Vasseur and Wang
[
25
]
overcame the challenge of reconciling the standard anti-derivative method typically employed for viscous shock stability with the energy method used for rarefaction stability. They affirmed the stability of composite waves comprising both viscous shock and rarefaction by employing the method of relative entropy and the
a
-contraction with shifts theory. These methods was initially introduced by Bresch and Desjardins in
[
1
]
and further developed by Kang and Vasseur in
[
18
,
19
,
20
,
23
]
, with additional insights provided in
[
21
,
24
,
27
,
44
]
. Employing similar methodologies, Han, Kang and Kim
[
10
]
have recently established the uniform convergence toward a composite of two viscous shocks for system (
1.9
) with independently small amplitudes. The objective of the present paper is to extend the results in
[
10
]
to the context of the relaxed compressible Navier-Stokes equations (
1.9
).

For relaxed compressible Navier-Stokes equations, the time-asymptotic stability of both single viscous shock waves and composites of two rarefaction waves has been a subject of study. When
\tilde{\tau}(\rho)
is a constant, Hu-Wang
[
13
]
and Hu-Wang
[
14
]
respectively established the linear stability of the viscous shock wave and nonlinear stability of rarefaction waves. With
\tilde{\tau}=\tau\rho
, by checking Majda’s condition on the Lopatinski determinant and Zumbrun’s Evans function condition
[
31
,
32
,

]
, Freistühler
[

get the nonlinear stability of the viscous shock waves for system (
1.1
) with shock profile initial data. More recently, the authors
[
8

have shown the nonlinear stability of composite waves of viscous shock and rarefaction, where the method of relative entropy and the
a
-contraction with shifts theory were fully used.

In this paper, we studied the time asymptotic stability for composite waves of two viscous shocks to system (
1.3
)-(
1.4
). Note that the dissipation structure of relaxed system (
1.3
) is much weaker than that of classical system (
1.9
), thus the BD entropy used in
[
25
,
10
]
to prove the
a
-contraction property is not available for our system and energy estimates have new challenges. Moreover, compared to the results of
[
10
]
where cutoff functions were used to localize the diffusion term, we do not need this kind of operation. We shall follow the methods in
[
8
]
to overcome such difficulties. Here are our strategy. Instead of using BD entropy, we use of the special hyperbolic structure of the relaxed system and the relative entropy quantities with weight function and shifts to get the
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
estimates of
(v-\widetilde{v},u-\widetilde{u},\Pi-\widetilde{\Pi})
and weighted
L^{2}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
estimates of
(v-\widetilde{v},\Pi-\widetilde{\Pi})
(see Lemma
4.2
). We note that, unlike that in
[
25
,
10
]
where the dissipation estimates of the derivative of
(v-\widetilde{v})
were obtained for system (
1.3
), we do not have such estimate. Instead, we shall recover the dissipation estimate of
(v-\widetilde{v})
from high-order estimates and thus cutoff functions are not needed.

Our main theorem are stated as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let the relaxation parameter
\tau

\tau\leq\min\{\inf\limits_{z_{1}\in[v_{m},v_{-}]}\frac{\mu}{2|\sigma_{1}^{2}+p^{\prime}(z_{1})|},\inf\limits_{z_{2}\in[v_{m},v_{+}]}\frac{\mu}{2|\sigma_{2}^{2}+p^{\prime}(z_{2})|},1\}.

(1.10)

For a given constant state
(v_{+},u_{+})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
,
there exist constants
\delta_{0},\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that the following holds true.

For any constant states
(v_{-},u_{-})
and
(v_{m},u_{m})
satisfying (
1.7
) with

|v_{+}-v_{m}|+|v_{m}-v_{-}|\leq\delta_{0}.

Denote
(\widetilde{v}_{i},\widetilde{u}_{i},\widetilde{\Pi}_{i})(x-\sigma_{i}t)
the i-viscous shock solution that are the traveling wave solutions for (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) for each
i=1,2
.
Let
(v_{0},u_{0},\Pi_{0})
be any initial data such that

\sum\limits_{\pm}\left(\|(v_{0}-v_{\pm},u_{0}-u_{\pm})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\pm})}\right)+\|((v_{0})_{x},(u_{0})_{x})\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\sqrt{\tau}\|\Pi_{0}\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R})}<\varepsilon_{0},

(1.11)

where

.
Then, the initial value problem (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) has a unique global-in-time solution

. Moreover, there exist an absolutely continuous shift

(1.12)

\displaystyle u(t,x)-\left(\widetilde{u}_{1}(x-\sigma_{1}t-X_{1}(t))+\widetilde{u}_{2}(x-\sigma_{2}t-X_{2}(t))-u_{m}\right)\in C(0,+\infty;H^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,+\infty)}\left(\|v-\widetilde{v}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+\|u-\widetilde{u}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+\tau\|\Pi-\widetilde{\Pi}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(\|\left((v-\widetilde{v})_{x},(u-\widetilde{u})_{x}\right)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\|\Pi-\widetilde{\Pi}\|^{2}_{H^{2}}\right)\mathrm{d}t

.
In addition, as

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}

where

In addition, the shifts are well-separated in the following sense:

(v_{m}-\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}>\frac{v_{m}p^{\prime\prime}(v_{\ast})(v_{-}-\widetilde{v}_{1})(\widetilde{v}_{1}-v_{m})}{2\mu\sigma_{1}}\geq C(v_{-}-\widetilde{v}_{1})(\widetilde{v}_{1}-v_{m})\geq C\delta_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{1}-v_{m}).

Thus, it yields

\widetilde{v}_{1}-v_{m}\leq C\delta_{1}e^{-C\delta_{1}\xi_{1}}.

Combining the above estimates, one obtain

(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}\leq C\delta_{1}^{2}e^{-C\delta_{1}|\xi_{1}|}.

For high-order estimates, using (
2.5
) and (
1.10
), we have

|(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}\xi_{1}}|=\Big|\frac{(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}h((\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}})+\widetilde{v}_{1}h^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{1})(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}}{\mu\sigma_{1}+\tau\sigma_{1}h^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{1})}+\frac{\widetilde{v}_{1}h(\widetilde{v}_{1})\tau\sigma_{1}h^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{1})(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}}{\left(\mu\sigma_{1}+\tau\sigma_{1}h^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{1})\right)^{2}}\Big|\leq C\delta_{1}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}|.

Similarly, for
k=3,4
, we can get
|\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{k}\widetilde{v}_{1}|\leq C\delta_{1}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}|
.

Using
\eqref{2.2}_{1}
, we get

|\widetilde{u}_{1}-u_{-}|=|\sigma_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{1}-v_{-})|\leq C\delta_{1}e^{-C\delta_{1}|\xi_{1}|},\quad\xi_{1}<0,

and

|\widetilde{u}_{1}-u_{m}|=|\sigma_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{1}-v_{m})|\leq C\delta_{1}e^{-C\delta_{1}|\xi_{1}|},\quad\xi_{1}>0.

Similarly, one has

|(\widetilde{u}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}|=|\sigma_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}|\leq C\delta_{1}^{2}e^{-C\delta_{1}|\xi_{1}|},

and

|\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{k}\widetilde{u}_{1}|=|\sigma_{1}\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{k}\widetilde{v}_{1}|\leq C\delta_{1}|(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}|,

where
k=2,3,4
.

Substituting
\eqref{2.2}_{1}
and
\eqref{2.2}_{2}
into
\eqref{2.2}_{3}
, it yields

\widetilde{\Pi}_{1}=\frac{-\sigma_{1}^{3}\tau+\sigma_{1}\tau p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{1})-\mu\sigma_{1}}{\widetilde{v}_{1}}(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi},

(2.7)

and thus

|\widetilde{\Pi}_{1}|\leq C|(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi}|.

(2.8)

Taking the derivative of equation (

, we derive that

(\widetilde{\Pi}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}=\frac{\sigma_{1}^{3}\tau+\sigma_{1}\tau p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{1})-\mu\sigma_{1}}{\widetilde{v}_{1}}(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}\xi_{1}}+\frac{\sigma_{1}\tau p^{\prime\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{1})(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}(\widetilde{v}_{1})-(\sigma_{1}^{3}\tau+\sigma_{1}\tau p^{\prime}(\widetilde{v}_{1})-\mu\sigma_{1})(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}}{(\widetilde{v}_{1})^{2}}(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}.

Noting that
(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}\xi_{1}}\leq C\delta_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}

|(\widetilde{\Pi}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}|\leq C\delta_{1}(\widetilde{v}_{1})_{\xi_{1}}.

Similarly, for

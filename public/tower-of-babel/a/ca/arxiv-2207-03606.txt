One-Loop Corrections to Dihadron Production in DIS at Small x x
Filip Bergabo, Jamal Jalilian-Marian

Abstract
We calculate the one-loop corrections to dihadron production in Deep Inelastic Scattering (DIS) at small x x using the Color Glass Condensate formalism. We show that all UV and soft singularities cancel while the collinear divergences are absorbed into quark and anti quark-hadron fragmentation functions. Rapidity divergences lead to JIMWLK evolution of dipoles and quadrupoles describing multiple-scatterings of the quark anti-quark dipole on the target proton/nucleus. The resulting cross section is finite and can be used for phenomenological studies of dihadron angular correlations at small x x in a future Electron-Ion Collider (EIC).

\displaystyle-\frac{\frac{z(z_{1}-z)}{z_{1}}(z_{1}z_{2}+(z_{1}-z)(z_{2}+z))Q^{2}}{\Big[\mathbf{k}_{1}^{2}+(z_{1}-z)(z_{2}+z)Q^{2}\Big]\Big[\mathbf{k}_{2}^{2}+Q_{1}^{2}\Big]\left[\left(\mathbf{k}_{1}-\frac{z_{1}-z}{z_{1}}\mathbf{k}_{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{z(z_{1}-z)}{z_{2}z_{1}^{2}}\mathbf{k}_{2}^{2}+\frac{z}{z_{1}}(z_{1}-z)Q^{2}\right]}

(52)

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma_{14(2)}^{L}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}g^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{2}}{(2\pi)^{7}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}[S_{122^{\prime}1^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+1]K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}|Q_{1})e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2})}

\displaystyle+\frac{\frac{(z_{1}+z)(z_{2}-z)}{z_{1}z_{2}}(z_{1}z_{2}+(z_{1}+z)(z_{2}-z))}{\Big[\mathbf{k}_{1}^{2}+(z_{1}+z)(z_{2}-z)Q^{2}\Big]\left[\left(\mathbf{k}_{1}-\frac{z_{2}-z}{z_{2}}\mathbf{k}_{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{z(z_{2}-z)}{z_{1}z_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{k}_{2}^{2}+\frac{z}{z_{2}}(z_{2}-z)Q^{2}\right]}

\displaystyle-\frac{\frac{z(z_{2}-z)}{z_{2}}(z_{1}z_{2}+(z_{1}+z)(z_{2}-z))Q^{2}}{\Big[\mathbf{k}_{1}^{2}+(z_{1}+z)(z_{2}-z)Q^{2}\Big]\Big[\mathbf{k}_{2}^{2}+Q_{1}^{2}\Big]\left[\left(\mathbf{k}_{1}-\frac{z_{2}-z}{z_{2}}\mathbf{k}_{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{z(z_{2}-z)}{z_{1}z_{2}^{2}}\mathbf{k}_{2}^{2}+\frac{z}{z_{2}}(z_{2}-z)Q^{2}\right]}

These expressions constitute the full result for the one-loop corrections to inclusive quark anti-quark

3
) in the large

limit and ignored all subleading

terms. We have also used the following notation for the coordinate dependence of some of the Bessel functions:

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=\sqrt{z_{1}z_{2}\mathbf{x}_{12}^{2}+z_{1}z\mathbf{x}_{13}^{2}+z_{2}z\mathbf{x}_{23}^{2}},

\displaystyle X_{5}

\displaystyle X_{6}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{z_{1}(z_{2}-z)\mathbf{x}_{12}^{2}+z_{1}z\,\mathbf{x}_{13}^{2}+z(z_{2}-z)\mathbf{x}_{23}^{2}}.

(54)

Note that when
z\to 0
these all become

. The primed version
X^{\prime}
that appears in some real corrections is the same as
X
above but with
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\to\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime},\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}
.

IV
Divergences

The expressions for the one-loop corrections to quark anti-quark inclusive production cross section given above are formal, i.e. they contain divergences that need to be regulated and/or eliminated before one obtains a meaningful result. There are
4
categories of divergences:

\bullet
Ultraviolet (UV) divergences when loop momentum
\mathbf{k}\rightarrow\infty
or equivalently in coordinate space, when the transverse coordinate of the radiated gluon approaches the transverse coordinate
\mathbf{x}_{i}
of either quark or anti-quark when integrated, i.e.
\mathbf{x}_{3}\rightarrow\mathbf{x}_{i}
such that
|\mathbf{x}_{3}-\mathbf{x}_{i}|\rightarrow 0
. We will show that the real corrections are UV finite and that all UV divergences in the virtual corrections cancel against each other so that the virtual corrections are also UV finite by themselves.

\bullet
Soft divergences when
k^{\mu}\rightarrow 0
, which in this context corresponds to
both
transverse momentum in the loop
\mathbf{k}

and
the radiated gluon momentum fraction
z
go to zero simultaneously,
\mathbf{k},z\rightarrow 0
. Both the real and virtual corrections contain soft divergences, however all soft divergences cancel between real and virtual corrections.

\bullet
Collinear divergences when the radiated gluon momentum becomes parallel to either quark or anti-quark momentum, i.e. when the angle
\theta
between the radiated gluon
3
-momentum and its parent quark (anti-quark)
3
-momentum goes to zero at
finite

\mathbf{k}
and
z
(note that finite in this context means that
\mathbf{k}
is neither zero nor infinite). These appear, for example as

{1\over(p+k)^{2}}\sim{1\over(z_{1}\mathbf{k}-z\mathbf{p})^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{1-\cos\theta}
for radiation from a quark and similarly for radiation from a anti-quark (
z_{1},\mathbf{p}\rightarrow z_{2},\mathbf{q}
). These collinear divergences are absorbed into quark-hadron (anti quark-hadron) fragmentation functions which makes the fragmentation functions scale dependent, i.e. they evolve with the renormalization scale
\mu^{2}
.

\bullet
Rapidity divergences when the momentum fraction
z
of the gluon goes to zero while the transverse momentum
\mathbf{k}
of the gluon remains finite. These rapidity divergences are absorbed into (
x
) renormalization of the dipoles and quadrupoles making them energy/rapidity dependent and evolving according to the BK and JIMWLK evolution equations
Balitsky 1996
;
Kovchegov 2000
;
Jalilian-Marian
et al. 1997
;
Jalilian-Marian
et al. 1998a
;
Jalilian-Marian
et al. 1998b
;
Kovner et al. 2000
;
Iancu et al. 2001
;
Ferreiro et al. 2002
.

Here we give more details of the divergences and their cancellation/absorption into renormalized quantities. The easiest way to see cancellations of divergences is via power counting which is what we will use in this section. We will also provide results using dimensional regularization in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme which is the more common method. Using dimensional regularization raises issues when using spinor helicity methods as one changes the number of spacetime dimensions from
4
(where spinor helicity methods are formulated) to
4-2\epsilon
. There are various ways of dealing with this issue, here we will use the FDH (Four Dimensional Helicity) scheme which treats both internal and external states in
4
-dimensions. As expected the finite terms after regulating divergences will depend on the regularization scheme.

IV.1
UV divergences

For the real corrections (Eq.
32
-
41
) all momentum integrations have been performed and results are given in terms of a two-dimensional coordinate space integration
\differential^{2}\mathbf{x}_{3}
and an integration over momentum fraction
z
(relative to the LO result in Eq.
1
). In this context UV divergences manifest themselves as poles when

\mathbf{x}_{3}\rightarrow\mathbf{x}_{1}
or
\mathbf{x}_{3}\rightarrow\mathbf{x}_{2}
, i.e. when the transverse coordinate of the radiated gluon
\mathbf{x}_{3}
approaches that of the quark
\mathbf{x}_{1}
or the anti-quark
\mathbf{x}_{2}
. We focus on the quark case and consider the limit
\mathbf{x}_{3}\rightarrow\mathbf{x}_{1}
. The case of radiation from the anti-quark is identical.

We note that the only possible UV singularity in our expressions is in the gluon radiation kernel

\Delta_{ij}^{(3)}
which appears in all real corrections. Writing it as

\displaystyle\Delta^{(3)}_{ij}=\frac{\mathbf{x}_{3i}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{3j}}{\mathbf{x}_{3i}^{2}\mathbf{x}_{3j}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{3i}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{3j}^{2}}-\frac{{\mathbf{x}_{ij}^{2}}}{\mathbf{x}_{3i}^{2}\mathbf{x}_{3j}^{2}}\right],

(55)

we see that the integral of this kernel over
\mathbf{x}_{3}
is finite as
\mathbf{x}_{3}\rightarrow\mathbf{x}_{i}
due to cancellations between the first and third terms. Similarly, as
\mathbf{x}_{3}\rightarrow\mathbf{x}_{j}
there is a cancellation between the second and third terms. The other factors appearing in the real corrections are all non-singular in the UV limit. We therefore conclude that there are no UV divergences in the real corrections and that
all real corrections are UV finite
.

For the virtual corrections, several of the diagrams are UV divergent. However when combined they all cancel each other in the UV limit, specifically

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{5}+\differential\sigma_{11}\right]_{\text{UV}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{6}+\differential\sigma_{12}\right]_{\text{UV}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{9}+\differential\sigma_{10}+\differential\sigma_{14(1)}+\differential\sigma_{14(2)}\right]_{\text{UV}}=0.

(56)

The remaining virtual diagrams (7,8,13) are all UV finite. Therefore,
all UV divergences are canceled
when adding up all the relevant pieces of the differential cross section. The UV-finiteness of the production cross section holds for any value of
z
. We provide more details in Appendix
A
.

IV.2
Soft Divergences

Soft divergences appear when all components of a loop momentum go to zero, i.e.

k^{\mu}\rightarrow 0
which in this context means
\mathbf{k}
and
z

both

go to zero (in coordinate space this becomes
\mathbf{x}_{3}\to\infty
and
z\to 0
). Soft divergences in the real corrections generically
appear either as

\int{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}\over\mathbf{k}^{2}}\,e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\text{or}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\int{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}\over\mathbf{k}^{2}}

(57)

in the virtual corrections (after possibly shifting the transverse momentum).
In coordinate space soft divergences are contained in

\int\differential^{2}\mathbf{x}_{3}\,\Delta^{(3)}_{ij}={1\over 2}\,\int\differential^{2}\mathbf{x}_{3}\,\left[{1\over(\mathbf{x}_{3}-\mathbf{x}_{i})^{2}}+{1\over(\mathbf{x}_{3}-\mathbf{x}_{j})^{2}}-{(\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{j})^{2}\over(\mathbf{x}_{3}-\mathbf{x}_{i})^{2}\,(\mathbf{x}_{3}-\mathbf{x}_{j})^{2}}\right]

(58)

from which it is clear that the first two terms in
\Delta^{(3)}_{ij}
are
divergent when
\mathbf{x}_{3}\rightarrow\infty
. Adding all the singular parts
of the production cross section we get the following cancellations between the various terms,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{1\times 1}+2\,\differential\sigma_{9}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{2\times 2}+2\,\differential\sigma_{10}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{1\times 2}+\differential\sigma_{13(1)}+\differential\sigma_{13(2)}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{3\times 3}+\differential\sigma_{4\times 4}+2\,\differential\sigma_{3\times 4}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{1\times 3}+\differential\sigma_{1\times 4}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{2\times 3}+\differential\sigma_{2\times 4}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{5}+\differential\sigma_{7}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{6}+\differential\sigma_{8}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{11}+\differential\sigma_{14(1)}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0,

\displaystyle\left[\differential\sigma_{12}+\differential\sigma_{14(2)}\right]_{\text{soft}}=0.

(59)

The necessary relative factors of
2
in these expressions can be seen from Eq.
28
. These cancellations are easiest to see when the cross section is written in coordinate space in the soft
z
limit (Appendix
B
). It is worth noting that there are singular terms
\sim{1\over z}\,\log({1\over z})
which can be seen in dimensional regularization of the remaining integrals
which cancel between
\differential\sigma_{11,\text{soft}}
and
\differential\sigma_{14(1),\text{soft}}

and similarly between
\differential\sigma_{12,\text{soft}}
and
\differential\sigma_{14(2),\text{soft}}
(this is shown in Appendix
C
).
Thus there are no soft singularities (both
\mathbf{k},z\rightarrow 0
) left after adding
all the real and virtual terms.

IV.3
Rapidity Divergences

From eqs. (
32
-
53
) it is clear that the production
cross section contains terms which are singular in the

\int{\differential z\over z}
integral as
z\rightarrow 0
. This happens at finite
\mathbf{k}
(or
\mathbf{x}_{3}
)
so that rapidity divergences are different from the soft divergences that have been
shown to cancel among various terms. Following
Caucal et al. 2021
we
introduce a longitudinal momentum fraction factorization scale
z_{f}
and divide the
z
integration into
two regions:
z>z_{f}
and
z<z_{f}
(here we write the upper limit as
1
, in our expressions the upper limit is always either
z_{1}
or
z_{2}
).

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\differential z}{z}f(z)=\left\{\int_{0}^{z_{f}}\frac{\differential z}{z}+\int_{z_{f}}^{1}\frac{\differential z}{z}\,\right\}\,f(z).

(60)

In the region where
z>z_{f}
there
are formally no divergences left anymore (there are still collinear divergences in this
region but they will be absorbed into fragmentation functions - this is the topic of the next section) so the cross
section is completely finite. On the other hand in the second region where
z<z_{f}

we encounter the familiar rapidity divergence
y=\log(1/z)
which we expect will be absorbed into
JIMWLK evolution of the quadrupoles and dipoles. We now focus on the

z\rightarrow 0
limit of our expressions while
\mathbf{k}
(or equivalently
\mathbf{x}_{3}
)
is kept finite (see Appendix
B
).

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma_{\text{NLO}}^{L}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=\frac{2e^{2}g^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}}{(2\pi)^{10}}\int_{0}^{z_{f}}\frac{\differential z}{z}\int\differential^{10}\mathbf{x}\,K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{1})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}|Q_{1})e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{1^{\prime}1}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2})

\displaystyle\Bigg\{[S_{122^{\prime}1^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+1]\left(\frac{3}{\mathbf{x}_{31}^{2}}+\frac{3}{\mathbf{x}_{32}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{31^{\prime}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{32^{\prime}}^{2}}-\frac{8}{\mathbf{x}_{3}^{2}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{11^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{22^{\prime}}-2\tilde{\Delta}_{12}\right)

\displaystyle+[S_{12}S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+1]\left(\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{32}^{2}}-\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{32^{\prime}}^{2}}+2\tilde{\Delta}_{12^{\prime}}-2\tilde{\Delta}_{12}\right)

\displaystyle+[S_{11^{\prime}}S_{22^{\prime}}-S_{13}S_{23}-S_{1^{\prime}3}S_{2^{\prime}3}+1]\left(-\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{31}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{31^{\prime}}^{2}}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{32}^{2}}-\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{32^{\prime}}^{2}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{11^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{22^{\prime}}+2\tilde{\Delta}_{12^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+[S_{122^{\prime}3}S_{1^{\prime}3}-S_{1^{\prime}3}S_{2^{\prime}3}-S_{12}+1]\left(\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{32^{\prime}}^{2}}-\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{31^{\prime}}^{2}}+2\tilde{\Delta}_{11^{\prime}}-2\tilde{\Delta}_{12^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+[S_{1231^{\prime}}S_{2^{\prime}3}-S_{1^{\prime}3}S_{2^{\prime}3}-S_{12}+1]\left(\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{31^{\prime}}^{2}}-\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{32^{\prime}}^{2}}+2\tilde{\Delta}_{22^{\prime}}-2\tilde{\Delta}_{21^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+[S_{322^{\prime}1^{\prime}}S_{13}-S_{13}S_{23}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+1]\left(\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{31}^{2}}-\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{32}^{2}}+2\tilde{\Delta}_{12}\right)

\displaystyle+[S_{132^{\prime}1^{\prime}}S_{23}-S_{13}S_{23}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+1]\left(\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{32}^{2}}-\frac{2}{\mathbf{x}_{31}^{2}}+2\tilde{\Delta}_{12}\right)\Bigg\}.

(61)

Here we have defined

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{ij}=\frac{\mathbf{x}_{ij}^{2}}{\mathbf{x}_{3i}^{2}\mathbf{x}_{3j}^{2}}.

(62)

Inside the curly brackets, the first line comes from adding
\sigma_{1\times 1}+\sigma_{2\times 2}+2\sigma_{9}+2\sigma_{10}+2\sigma_{11}+2\sigma_{12}+2\sigma_{14(1)}+2\sigma_{14(2)}
. The second line is
2\sigma_{1\times 2}+2\sigma_{13(1)}+2\sigma_{13(2)}
. The third line is
\sigma_{3\times 3}+\sigma_{4\times 4}+2\sigma_{3\times 4}
. Fourth line is
2\sigma_{1\times 3}+2\sigma_{1\times 4}
. Fifth line is
2\sigma_{2\times 3}+2\sigma_{2\times 4}
. Sixth line is
2\sigma_{5}+2\sigma_{7}
, and the last line is
2\sigma_{6}+2\sigma_{8}
. Notice that cross terms have been doubled since we need to add their complex conjugate. To simplify the expression we use a combination of shifting
\mathbf{x}_{3}
where possible and using symmetry between primed and unprimed coordinates. We find that everything can be simplified down to the following expression.

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma_{\text{NLO}}^{L}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=\frac{2e^{2}g^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}}{(2\pi)^{10}}\,\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2})\int_{0}^{z_{f}}\frac{\differential z}{z}\int\differential^{10}\mathbf{x}\,K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{1})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}|Q_{1})

\displaystyle e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{1^{\prime}1}}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{2^{\prime}2}}\Bigg\{\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{12}+\tilde{\Delta}_{22^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{12^{\prime}}\right)S_{132^{\prime}1^{\prime}}S_{23}+\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+\tilde{\Delta}_{22^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{21^{\prime}}\right)S_{1^{\prime}321}S_{2^{\prime}3}

\displaystyle+\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{12}+\tilde{\Delta}_{11^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{21^{\prime}}\right)S_{322^{\prime}1^{\prime}}S_{13}+\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+\tilde{\Delta}_{11^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{12^{\prime}}\right)S_{32^{\prime}21}S_{1^{\prime}3}

\displaystyle-\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{11^{\prime}}+\tilde{\Delta}_{22^{\prime}}+\tilde{\Delta}_{12}+\tilde{\Delta}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}\right)S_{122^{\prime}1^{\prime}}-\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{12}+\tilde{\Delta}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{12^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{21^{\prime}}\right)S_{12}S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}

\displaystyle-\left(\tilde{\Delta}_{11^{\prime}}+\tilde{\Delta}_{22^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{12^{\prime}}-\tilde{\Delta}_{21^{\prime}}\ \right)S_{11^{\prime}}S_{22^{\prime}}-2\tilde{\Delta}_{12}\left(S_{13}S_{23}-S_{12}\right)-2\tilde{\Delta}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}\left(S_{1^{\prime}3}S_{2^{\prime}3}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}\right)\Bigg\}.

(63)

Here the first seven terms inside the curly bracket give the JIMWLK evolution of the quadrupole

S_{122^{\prime}1^{\prime}}
with the factorization scale
z_{f}
(See Eq.
5
in
Jalilian-Marian 2012
). The last two terms correspond to the rapidity evolution of the dipoles
S_{12}
and
S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}
as given by the BK equation
Balitsky 1996
;
Kovchegov 2000
. This demonstrates that the rapidity divergences can be absorbed into rapidity evolution of the LO cross section. The contribution of the
z
-integration above the factorization scale
z_{f}
is finite and constitutes part of the NLO corrections to the Leading Order cross section.

IV.4
Collinear divergences

IV.4.1
Leading Order dihadron production

We now focus on the collinear divergences present in some of the one-loop corrections in (Eq.
32
-
53
) and show that they can absorbed into evolution of quark-hadron and anti quark-hadron fragmentation functions. As this is completely new we will provide the full details of the calculation. We start by writing the inclusive dihadron production cross section in terms of the partonic cross section as

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\,\frac{1}{z_{h_{1}}^{2}z_{h_{2}}^{2}}\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to q\bar{q}X}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}D_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})D_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}}).

(64)

where
\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q}
are the quark and anti-quark transverse momenta while
\mathbf{p}_{h},\mathbf{q}_{h}
are the transverse momenta of the two produced hadrons. As partons and hadrons are assumed to be massless their rapidities are the same so that
y_{1}\equiv y_{p}=y_{h}
(and similarly for the anti-quark).
Hadronization is assumed to be describable in terms of a non-perturbative quark (or anti-quark)-hadron fragmentation function
D_{h/q}(z_{h_{1}})
which is then perturbatively renormalized (becomes
\mu^{2}
dependent) after absorption of the collinear divergences. The momentum fractions
z_{h_{1}},z_{h_{2}}
are defined via

p_{h_{1}}^{\mu}=z_{h_{1}}p^{\mu}
and
q_{h_{2}}^{\mu}=z_{h_{2}}q^{\mu}
so that
0<z_{h_{i}}<1
. Using our Leading Order result from Eq. (
1
) (only the longitudinal part) we can write this as

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma_{LO}^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\left[S_{122^{\prime}1^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+1\right]

\displaystyle K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{1})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}|Q_{1})\,e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}\,e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2})}

\displaystyle D_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})D_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})\,\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2}),

(65)

where
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
are related to hadronic momentum fractions by

\displaystyle z_{1}=\frac{p_{h}^{+}}{z_{h_{1}}l^{+}},\,\,\,\,\,\,z_{2}=\frac{q_{h}^{+}}{z_{h_{2}}l^{+}}.

(66)

Anticipating factorization of the one-loop corrections and to make our expression more compact we define the common factor
H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{1})
as

\displaystyle H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{1})\equiv\left[S_{122^{\prime}1^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+1\right]K_{0}\left(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{1}\right)\,K_{0}\left(|\mathbf{x}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}|Q_{1}\right)\,e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2})},

(67)

with
Q_{1}^{2}\equiv z_{1}(1-z_{1})\,Q^{2}
so that the common factor implicitly depends on
Q^{2}
. The Leading Order cross section can be then expressed in the following compact form

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma_{LO}^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H\left(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\delta\left(1-z_{1}-z_{2}\right)D_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})D_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}}).

(68)

IV.4.2
The virtual correction

First we’ll focus on the fragmentation of the quark into a hadron
h_{1}
. Treatment of anti quark fragmentation will be identical. Schematically, the fragmentation function receives corrections from the following diagrams:

Figure 3:
The full fragmentation function
D_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}},\mu^{2})
(solid black circle) can be written as the bare fragmentation function (faint gray circle) plus the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
corrections shown. These need to be added at the cross section level, and we’ll keep terms only up to
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
.

Here in Fig.
3
the first diagram after the equals sign corresponds to the leading order hadronic cross section computed in the previous subsection. The two
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
corrections correspond to our virtual correction
\sigma_{9}
and the real correction
\sigma_{1\times 1}
. So, let’s now compute the hadronic cross section contribution from the virtual correction
\sigma_{9}
(combining Eq.
46
with Eq.
64
).

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma_{9}^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=-\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})

\displaystyle\times\alpha_{s}C_{F}\int_{0}^{z_{1}}\frac{\differential z}{z}\left[\frac{z_{1}^{2}+(z_{1}-z)^{2}}{z_{1}^{2}}\right]\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{z}{z_{1}}\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2}).

(69)

Here we relaxed the large
N_{c}
approximation taking one factor of
N_{c}\to 2C_{F}
, and have also used
g^{2}\to 4\pi\alpha_{s}
. Let’s define a new variable for the
z
integration

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{z_{1}-z}{z_{1}}.

(70)

This defines
\xi
to be the fraction of longitudinal momentum that the quark carries after radiating the gluon. In terms of this, we get

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma_{9}^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=-\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})

\displaystyle\times\alpha_{s}\,C_{F}\,\int^{1}_{0}\differential\xi\,\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}\,\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\left(\mathbf{k}-(1-\xi)\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2}).

(71)

This can now be added to the leading order hadronic cross section. Note that we actually need to add
2\sigma_{9}
because it appears twice in the formula since it’s a cross term (Eq.
28
).

IV.4.3
The real correction

Looking at the relevant diagrams in Fig.
3
, the last one we need for the
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
corrections to the quark fragmentation function is the real correction
\sigma_{1\times 1}
.

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{L}_{1\times 1}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=\frac{2e^{2}g^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}^{2}z_{2}^{3}(1-z_{2})^{2}(z_{1}^{2}+(1-z_{2})^{2})}{(2\pi)^{8}z_{1}}\int\differential^{8}x\,K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{2})K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}|Q_{2})

\displaystyle\left[S_{122^{\prime}1^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+1\right]e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2})}\int\frac{\differential z}{z}\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{z}{z_{1}}\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2}-z).

(72)

We have rewritten the integral over the radiation kernel (in Eq.
32
) in momentum space using

\displaystyle e^{i\frac{z}{z_{1}}\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}\int\differential^{2}\mathbf{x}_{3}\Delta^{(3)}_{11^{\prime}}=\int\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{z}{z_{1}}\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}.

(73)

Looking at Eq.
64
we see that this correction contributes the following expression to the hadronic cross section.

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}_{1\times 1}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{2e^{2}g^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}^{2}z_{2}^{3}(1-z_{2})^{2}(z_{1}^{2}+(1-z_{2})^{2})}{(2\pi)^{8}z_{1}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}x\,K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{12}|Q_{2})

\displaystyle K_{0}(|\mathbf{x}_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}|Q_{2})\left[S_{122^{\prime}1^{\prime}}-S_{12}-S_{1^{\prime}2^{\prime}}+1\right]e^{i\mathbf{p}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{2}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{2})}

\displaystyle\int\frac{\differential z}{z}\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{z}{z_{1}}\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2}-z)D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}}).

(74)

Now we’ll write this using
H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})
defined in Eq.
67
and again relax the large
N_{c}
approximation taking one factor of
N_{c}
to
2C_{F}
, and also write
g^{2}=4\pi\alpha_{s}
.

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}_{1\times 1}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})

\displaystyle\times\alpha_{s}C_{F}\int\frac{\differential z}{z}\frac{(z_{1}+z)^{2}(z_{1}^{2}+(z_{1}+z)^{2})}{z_{1}^{4}}\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2}-z)\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{z}{z_{1}}\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}.

(75)

We factored out
(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}
to match the leading order expression and used the delta function to write
1-z_{2}
as
z_{1}+z
. We then perform a change of variables in the
z
integral by defining

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{z_{1}}{z_{1}+z}.

(76)

This
\xi_{1}
is then the fraction of the outgoing quark’s momentum relative to the parent quark that emitted the gluon (i.e.: the same definition as we used for the virtual correction).

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}_{1\times 1}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})

\displaystyle\times\alpha_{s}C_{F}\int\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi^{5}}\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}\delta(1-z_{2}-z_{1}/\xi)D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})\,\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{(1-\xi)}{\xi}\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}.

(77)

Now,
z_{1}
is no longer an external variable, as remarked earlier it can be written in terms of
z_{h_{1}}
using Eq.
66
. So one can then write
z_{1}
in terms of
z_{h_{1}}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}_{1\times 1}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}(p_{h}^{+})^{3}z_{2}^{3}}{(2\pi)^{7}(l^{+})^{3}z_{h_{1}}^{5}z_{h_{2}}^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})

\displaystyle\times\alpha_{s}C_{F}\int\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi^{5}}\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}\delta\left(1-z_{2}-\frac{p_{h}^{+}}{z_{h_{1}}l^{+}\xi}\right)D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})\,\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{(1-\xi)}{\xi}\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}.

(78)

Now that the
z_{h_{1}}
dependence is all explicit we make a substitution in the
z_{h_{1}}
integral, defining the new variable

\displaystyle z_{h_{1}}^{\prime}=z_{h_{1}}\xi,\,\,\,\,\,\,\differential z_{h_{1}}^{\prime}=\xi\differential z_{h_{1}}.

(79)

In order to complete this substitution, we need to take
z_{h_{1}}\to\frac{z_{h_{1}}^{\prime}}{\xi}
everywhere. This also means that the bounds on the
z_{h_{1}}^{\prime}
integral go from 0 to
\xi
. We’ll write this using a step function
\theta(\xi-z_{h_{1}}^{\prime})
so we can keep the explicit bounds from
0
to
1
.

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}_{1\times 1}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}(p_{h}^{+})^{3}z_{2}^{3}}{(2\pi)^{7}(l^{+})^{3}z_{h_{1}}^{\prime 5}z_{h_{2}}^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})

\displaystyle\times\alpha_{s}C_{F}\int\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}\left(\frac{z_{h_{1}}^{\prime}}{\xi}\right)\theta(\xi-z_{h_{1}}^{\prime})\delta\left(1-z_{2}-\frac{p_{h}^{+}}{z_{h_{1}}^{\prime}l^{+}}\right)\,\,\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{(1-\xi)}{\xi}\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}.

(80)

We can now write things back in terms of
z_{1}
to get rid of
p_{h}^{+}
and
l^{+}
to match the leading order and virtual correction. We can also use the step function to set the bounds on the
\xi
integral. Finally, we remove the primes from
z_{h_{1}}^{\prime}
since it’s an integration variable.

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}_{1\times 1}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{8e^{2}Q^{2}N_{c}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})

\displaystyle\times\delta\left(1-z_{1}-z_{2}\right)\alpha_{s}C_{F}\int_{z_{h_{1}}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}\left(\frac{z_{h_{1}}}{\xi}\right)\,\,\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\mathbf{k}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-\frac{(1-\xi)}{\xi}\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}.

(81)

IV.4.4
Evolution of the fragmentation function

Now we would like to add up the three terms in Fig.
3
. These terms are Eq.
68
,
71
, and
81
. Note that we also need to double Eq.
71
since it’s a cross term. Adding up these three gives the following,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2})

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})+2\alpha_{s}C_{F}\Bigg\{\int_{z_{h_{1}}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}\left(\frac{z_{h_{1}}}{\xi}\right)\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{e^{i\frac{\mathbf{k}}{\xi}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}_{1})}}{\left(\mathbf{k}-(1-\xi)\mathbf{p}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle-D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})\int_{0}^{1}\differential\xi\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}\int\frac{\differential^{2}\mathbf{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{(\mathbf{k}-(1-\xi)\mathbf{p})^{2}}\Bigg\}\Bigg].

(82)

Now we turn our attention to the two transverse momentum integrals. The one with the exponential comes from the real correction
\sigma_{1\times 1}
, and contains both a soft divergence,
\mathbf{k}\to 0
and
z\to 0
(equivalently
\xi\to 1
) at the same time, and a collinear divergence as
\mathbf{k}\to(1-\xi)\mathbf{p}
. The integral without an exponential (from the virtual correction
\sigma_{9}
) contains a soft divergence, a collinear divergence, and a UV divergence as
|\mathbf{k}|\to\infty
. We showed in section
IV.2
that the soft divergences cancel between these two terms. In section
IV.1
we also showed that the UV divergence in the virtual correction is canceled with other terms in the cross section. Therefore, the only divergence left to consider here is the collinear divergence in both integrals.

To see how regulating these divergences
leads to renormalization of the quark-hadron fragmentation function it is perhaps easiest to demonstrate this using an
explicit cutoff where the lower limit on
|\mathbf{k}|
is
\Lambda
and the upper limit is
\mu
.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2})

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})+\frac{\alpha_{s}C_{F}}{2\pi}\Bigg\{\int_{z_{h_{1}}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}\left(\frac{z_{h_{1}}}{\xi}\right)-\int_{0}^{1}\differential\xi\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})\Bigg\}\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\Bigg].

(83)

We would like to combine the terms inside the curly brackets, to do so we’ll define the quark-quark splitting function
P_{qq}(\xi)
:

\displaystyle P_{qq}(\xi)\equiv C_{F}\left[\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)_{+}}+\frac{3}{2}\delta(1-\xi)\right],

(84)

where the
+
distribution is defined inside an integral via

\displaystyle\int_{z}^{1}\differential\xi\frac{f(\xi)}{(1-\xi)_{+}}\equiv\int_{z}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{(1-\xi)}(f(\xi)-f(1))+f(1)\log(1-z).

(85)

This allows us to write Eq.
83
as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2})

\displaystyle\times\Bigg[D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}})+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}\Bigg\{\int_{z_{h_{1}}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}P_{qq}(\xi)D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}\left(\frac{z_{h_{1}}}{\xi}\right)\Bigg\}\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\Bigg].

(86)

Here we have used the following result:

\displaystyle\int_{z_{h_{1}}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}P_{qq}(\xi)D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}\left(\frac{z_{h_{1}}}{\xi}\right)=\int_{z_{h_{1}}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}\left(\frac{z_{h_{1}}}{\xi}\right)-\int_{0}^{1}\differential\xi\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}}).

(87)

To prove this, one first expands the
P_{qq}
function using Eq.
84
, then further expands the term with the plus distribution according to Eq.
85
. One of the terms is then exactly the first term on the right side of Eq.
87
. In the remaining term with a
\xi
integral one separates into two terms using
\int_{z_{h_{1}}}^{1}=\int_{0}^{1}-\int_{0}^{z_{h_{1}}}
. One can then use the fact that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{1-\xi}=\frac{(1+\xi^{2})}{(1-\xi)}+1+\xi.

(88)

The first term here paired with the
\int_{0}^{1}
integrals then gives exactly the second term on the right side of Eq.
87
. In all the other terms, the integral over
\xi
can be performed and these terms all cancel in the full expression.

Next, we can also combine with the leading order term (first term in the square brackets) by including a
\xi
integral that evaluates to
1
:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2})

\displaystyle\times\int_{z_{h_{1}}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}\left(\frac{z_{h_{1}}}{\xi}\right)\Bigg[\delta(1-\xi)+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}P_{qq}(\xi)\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\Bigg].

(89)

Then, we define the DGLAP evolved quark-hadron fragmentation function
D_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}},\mu^{2})
as

\displaystyle D_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}},\mu^{2})=\int_{z_{h_{1}}}^{1}\frac{\differential\xi}{\xi}D^{0}_{h_{1}/q}\left(\frac{z_{h_{1}}}{\xi}\right)\Bigg[\delta(1-\xi)+\frac{\alpha_{s}}{2\pi}P_{qq}(\xi)\log\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\Bigg],

(90)

in terms of which our expression becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\differential\sigma^{\gamma^{*}A\to h_{1}h_{2}X}_{LO+9+1\times 1}}{\differential^{2}\mathbf{p}_{h}\,\differential^{2}\mathbf{q}_{h}\,\differential y_{1}\,\differential y_{2}}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\differential z_{h_{2}}\frac{4e^{2}Q^{2}(z_{1}z_{2})^{3}N_{c}}{(2\pi)^{7}(z_{h_{1}}z_{h_{2}})^{2}}\int\differential^{8}\mathbf{x}\,H(\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{x},z_{2})

\displaystyle D^{0}_{h_{2}/\bar{q}}(z_{h_{2}})D_{h_{1}/q}(z_{h_{1}},\mu^{2})\delta(1-z_{1}-z_{2}).

(91)

It must be noted that here we used an explicit cutoff
\mu
to regulate these collinear divergences. However the most common way to handle these divergences is via dimensional regularization (dim reg). This is done in the next subsection.

IV.4.5
Evolution of the Fragmentation Function in Dimensional Regularization

As most parametrizations of the fragmentation functions are defined in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme it is important to relate our result with that given in the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme and using dimensional regularization as this would affect the finite pieces. The real integral can be regulated without complication via dimensional regularization.

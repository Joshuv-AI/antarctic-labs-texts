Introduction and main results

In present paper, we are interested in the following fractional system of Hamiltonian type:

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}u&=H_{v}(u,v)\;\;&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
(-\Delta)^{s}v&=H_{u}(u,v)&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
u&=v=0&&\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
s\in(0,1)
,
N>2s
,
H\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain. We will not focus on the regularity condition of the domain
\Omega
, and say simply that
\Omega
is smooth, even most results work with
C^{1,1}
boundary.
The study of system (
1.1
) is mainly motivated by the following classical Hamiltonian system:

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=H_{v}(u,v)\,&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
-\Delta v&=H_{u}(u,v)&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
u&=v=0&&\text{on}\penalty\ \partial\Omega.\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.2)

Formally the energy functional of (
1.2
) is

\mathcal{L}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\nabla vdx-\int_{\Omega}H(u,v)dx.

(1.3)

A crucial question is to decide on which space we will consider the functional
\mathcal{L}
. Let us look at the famous Lane-Emden system where
H(u,v)=\frac{|u|^{p+1}}{p+1}+\frac{|v|^{q+1}}{q+1}
,
p,q>0
, i.e.

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=|v|^{q-1}v\,&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
-\Delta v&=|u|^{p-1}u\,&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
u&=v=0\penalty\ \penalty\ &&\text{on}\penalty\ \partial\Omega.\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.4)

It is well known that the existence of positive solutions to (
1.4
) on a ball is decided by the position of
(p,q)
with respect to the critical hyperbola

p,q>0,\quad\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}=\frac{N-2}{N}.

(1.5)

A naive choice of the functional space for
\mathcal{L}
is
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{2}
. However, the Sobolev embedding greatly restricts the growth of
H
, and we could only work with
\max(p,q)\leq\frac{N+2}{N-2}
, hence many other choices of
H
were eliminated. Another difficulty is to deal with the strong indefiniteness of the quadratic part in
\mathcal{L}
, which is neither bounded from below nor from above on any subspace of
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{2}
with finite codimension.

To include more choices of
H
as for (
1.4
), people thought about functional spaces without symmetry in
u
and
v
, such as
W_{0}^{1,t}(\Omega)\times W_{0}^{1,\frac{t}{t-1}}(\Omega)
with
t>1
. But a new difficulty occurs since this choice is not a Hilbert space when
t\neq 2
, which prevents us from applying the linking theory due to Benci and Rabinowitz
[
3
]
.

As far as we are aware, Hulshof and van der Vorst
[
26
]
first used the interpolation space framework to deal with the system with
H(u,v)=F(u)+G(v)
, that is

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=g(v)\;\;&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
-\Delta v&=f(u)&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
u&=v=0&&\text{on}\penalty\ \partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.6)

They replaced the first integral in (
1.3
) by

\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{A}^{t}u\widetilde{A}^{2-t}vdx\quad\text{with}\;\;t\in(0,2),

where
\widetilde{A}^{t}:\widetilde{H}^{t}(\Omega)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
is a family of interpolation operators and
\widetilde{H}^{t}(\Omega)
is a family of interpolation spaces between
L^{2}(\Omega)
and
H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
. More precisely, for any

p,q>0,\quad\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>\frac{N-2}{N},

(1.7)

there exist some
t\in(0,2)
such that the embedding

\widetilde{H}^{t}(\Omega)\times\widetilde{H}^{2-t}(\Omega)\subset L^{p+1}(\Omega)\times L^{q+1}(\Omega)

is compact, which yields the existence of nontrivial solution to (
1.6
) under following conditions

•

(Subcritical)
f(u)=O(|u|^{p}),g(v)=O(|v|^{q})
as
|u|,|v|\to\infty
with
p,q>1
satisfying (
1.7
);

•

(Superlinear)
f(u)=o(|u|)
,
g(v)=o(|v|)
as
|u|,|v|\to 0
;

•

(
AR
condition)
f,g\in C(\mathbb{R})
,
f(0)=g(0)=0
, and there exist
\gamma>2
,
R>0
such that

0<\gamma F(u)\leq uf(u),\quad 0<\gamma G(v)\leq vg(v),\quad\forall\;|u|,|v|\geq R.

where
F(t):=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau\geq 0
,
G(t):=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau\geq 0
.

This generalized clearly the study of Lane-Emden system (
1.4
).

Later, de Figueiredo and Felmer also applied in
[
19
]
the interpolation space method to handle (
1.2
), with more general coupled
H
where
p,q
satisfy (
1.7
),

0\leq H(u,v)\leq C(|u|^{p+1}+|v|^{q+1}),

and
H
is superlinear at 0, that is

C(|u|^{p+1}+|v|^{q+1})\leq H(u,v)\;\;\text{with}\;\;pq>1,|u|+|v|<r\mbox{ for some }r>0.

In addition, Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz (
(AR)
for short) type condition (as
(H2)
below) was assumed. They proved then (
1.2
) admits a nontrivial solution if

(N-4)\max\{p,q\}<N+4.

Remark that to work with more general coupled
H
, we need to restrict the upper bound of
p,q
.

Clément and van der Vorst in
[
16
]
proposed another approach to study (
1.2
), their idea is to took advantage of dual method developed by Clarke, Ekeland and Temam
[
14
,
22
]
. Here the nonlinearity
H
is assumed strictly convex, subcritical (see (
1.7
)) and superlinear at 0. This dual method will transform (
1.2
) into a problem where the energy functional possesses a mountain pass structure. In fact, consider
the Legendre-Fenchel transform of
H
(see
[
22
]
,
[
37
, Chapter I, Section 6]
):

H^{*}(x)=\underset{w\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}{\sup}\big\{\langle w,x\rangle-H(x)\big\}\quad\mbox{ for any }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

one can obtain solutions to (
1.2
) by critical points of

\mathcal{J}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}{H}^{*}(u,v)dx-\int_{\Omega}v\mathcal{A}udx,\quad\forall\,(u,v)\in L^{1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)\times L^{1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)

(1.8)

where
\mathcal{A}
is the inverse of

\displaystyle-\Delta:W^{2,1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)\to L^{1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega).

(1.9)

If moreover
H
satisfies
(AR)
type condition (similar to
(H5)
and
(H7)
below), the existence of nontrivial solution to (
1.2
) was shown in
[
16
]
. This dual method was also used to handle system with critical growth, see Hulshof, Mitidieri and van der Vorst
[
25
]
.

Some other approaches exist. de Figueiredo, do Ó and Ruf
[
18
]
used Orlicz-space to obtain nontrivial solutions of (
1.6
), they replaced
W_{0}^{1,t}(\Omega)\times W_{0}^{1,\frac{t}{t-1}}(\Omega)
by Sobolev-Orlicz space
W_{0}^{1}L_{A}(\Omega)\times W_{0}^{1}L_{\widetilde{A}}(\Omega)
, where
A
is a
N
-function and
{\widetilde{A}}
is its Young-conjugate. Owing to the fact that this setting is not a Hilbert space, they used finite-dimensional approximation method. Their models contain also nonlinearites with nearly critical growth.
The Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction approach was also applied to problem (
1.6
), see for instance
[
33
,
32
]
. For more literature in this topic, we refer to
[
6
]
and references therein.

Coming back to the special case (
1.4
). In
[
15
]
, the existence of positive solution to (
1.4
) was firstly considered based on topological method. When
p,q>0
and
pq<1
, the uniqueness of positive solution was investigated in
[
17
]
. In
[
5
]
, (
1.4
) was reduced to the following single equation

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \Delta\big(|\Delta u|^{\frac{1}{p}-1}\Delta u\big)&=|u|^{q-1}u\penalty\ \penalty\ &&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
u&=\Delta u=0&&\text{on}\penalty\ \partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.10)

If
p,q>0
,
pq\neq 1
and subcritical as in (
1.7
), then (
1.4
) admits a positive classical ground state solution (it has minimal energy among all solutions), see
[
5
]
. If
\Omega
is a ball, we can use the Schwartz rearrangement to show that the ground state solution is radially symmetric. Furthermore, (
1.4
) has no positive solutions, if
\Omega
is star-shaped and
p,q>0
do not satisfy (
1.7
), see
[
30
]
.

To our best knowledge, for the fractional Laplacian case, although some special cases such as fractional Lane-Emden systems were studied (see
[
27
]
and references therein), there exists no study of (
1.1
) for general coupled subcritical nonlinearities so far. As mentioned above, a key step to handle (
1.1
) with the variational approach is to establish a suitable functional framework. Furthermore, in the Laplacian case, regardless of interpolation method, dual method, or reduced into a single equation, one needs the isomorphism given by (
1.9
). In the fractional Laplacian case, we need to find suitable functional space which plays the role of
W^{2,1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)
.

1.1
Weak solution and fractional spaces

Let
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain and
s\in(0,1)
, we denote

X_{0}^{s}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):u=0\penalty\ \mbox{ a.e.\penalty\ in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\;\;\text{and}\;\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}{\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}}dxdy<\infty\right\},

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}{\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}}dxdy\right)^{1/2}.

The embedding
X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{r}(\Omega)
is continuous for
r\in[1,\frac{2N}{N-2s}]
and compact for
r\in[1,\frac{2N}{N-2s})
, see
[
21
, Theorems 6.5, 7.1]
.

(-\Delta)^{s}
is an operator from
X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
into its dual space, namely

\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u,v\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}{\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}}dxdy,\quad\forall\,u,v\in X_{0}^{s}(\Omega).

Formally the energy functional associated to (
1.1
) is

\mathcal{K}(u,v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2N}}{\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}}dxdy-\int_{\Omega}H(u,v)dx.

(1.11)

As mentioned before, the crucial question is to find suitable functional space to work with
\mathcal{K}
, and
X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)^{2}
would not be the right one if we hope to handle more general functional
H
.

Next we recall the regularity result for the linear equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u\;=f\;\;\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\quad u=0\penalty\ \;\;\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

(1.12)

We denote

\delta(x):=\text{dist}(x,\partial\Omega),\quad x\in\Omega,

and

C_{\delta}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}):=\Big\{u\in C(\overline{\Omega}):\frac{u}{\delta^{s}}\mbox{ admits a continuous extension belonging to }C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})\Big\}.

Ros-Oton and Serra in
[
35
, Proposition 1.1, Theorem 1.2]
proved that

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{1,1}
domain and
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. There exists a unique
u\in X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
solving (
1.12
). Moreover
u\in C^{s}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C_{\delta}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
, and

\|u\|_{C^{s}(\overline{\Omega})}+\left\|\frac{u}{\delta^{s}}\right\|_{C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})}\leq C\|f\|_{\infty}

for some
0<\alpha<\min\{s,1-s\}
. The constants
\alpha
,
C
depend only on
\Omega
and
s
.

Here we shall consider weaker solution (see definition below) and choose the test function space as

\mathcal{T}_{s}(\Omega):=\{u\in X_{0}^{s}(\Omega):(-\Delta)^{s}u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)\}.

Hence
\mathcal{T}_{s}(\Omega)\subset C^{s}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C_{\delta}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
. Let

L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx):=\Big\{u\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|u|\delta^{s}dx<\infty\Big\}

be endowed with the norm
\|u\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx)}=|u\delta^{s}|_{1}
. In this paper,
|\cdot|_{r}
denotes always the norm of
L^{r}(\Omega)
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
N>2s
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx)
. We say that
u
is a
L^{1}
-weak solution to (
1.12
) if
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi dx,\quad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathcal{T}_{s}(\Omega).

(1.13)

Note that similar notions were given in
[
34
,
28
]
. We will prove in Proposition
2.3
that for any
f\in L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx)
, there exists a unique
L^{1}
-weak solution to (
1.12
). We give also the comparison principle and maximum principle for fractional Laplacian in
L^{1}
-weak sense, see Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
. Accordingly, we define

Definition 1.3
.

For
s\in(0,1)
and
N>2s
,
(u,v)
is said a
L^{1}
-weak solution to system (
1.1
) if
u,v\in L^{1}(\Omega)
,
H_{u}(u,v),H_{v}(u,v)\in L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx)
and for any
(\varphi,\psi)\in\mathcal{T}_{s}(\Omega)\times\mathcal{T}_{s}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}u(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}H_{v}(u,v)\varphi dx,\quad\int_{\Omega}v(-\Delta)^{s}\psi dx=\int_{\Omega}H_{u}(u,v)\psi dx.

If in addition
u,v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we call
(u,v)
a
classical solution
.

Another important functional space for us is the set of
u
such that
(-\Delta)^{s}u\in L^{r}(\Omega)
, namely for
r\geq 1
,

\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega):=\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega):\exists\;f\in L^{r}(\Omega)\mbox{ such that \eqref{L1weak} holds true}\}

(1.14)

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)}=|(-\Delta)^{s}u|_{r}.

We shall use
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
to play the role of
W^{2,r}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,r}(\Omega)
in the Laplacian case. In particular, we denote

\mathcal{H}^{2s}(\Omega)=\mathcal{W}^{2s,2}(\Omega).

Some (compact) embedding properties of
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
will be shown in Proposition
2.6
.

1.2
Fractional Hamiltonian system

Motivated by
[
26
,
19
]
, we apply firstly interpolation space method to study the system (
1.1
). For
0\leq\alpha\leq 2s
, consider the interpolation space

E^{\alpha}:=\Big\{u=\sum_{j\geq 1}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\in L^{2}(\Omega):\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\alpha}{s}}a_{j}^{2}<\infty\Big\},

(1.15)

where
\lambda_{j}
is the
j
-th eigenvalue of
(-\Delta)^{s}
with corresponding eigenfunction
\varphi_{j}
, and
\{\varphi_{j}\}
forms an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
. For
\alpha\in(0,2s)
, let
A^{\alpha}:E^{\alpha}\to L^{2}(\Omega)
be given by

A^{\alpha}u:=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\alpha}{2s}}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\in L^{2}(\Omega),\quad\forall\;u=\sum_{j\geq 1}a_{j}\varphi_{j}\in E^{\alpha}.

(1.16)

E^{\alpha}
is clearly a Hilbert space with the scalar product

(u,v)_{E^{\alpha}}=\int_{\Omega}A^{\alpha}uA^{\alpha}vdx=\sum_{j\geq 1}\lambda_{j}^{\frac{\alpha}{s}}\langle u,\varphi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}}\langle v,\varphi_{j}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

To handle the Hamiltonian system (
1.1
), we define

\mathbf{E}_{\alpha}:=E^{\alpha}\times E^{2s-\alpha},\quad\mbox{$\alpha\in(0,2s)$}.

Instead of considering
\mathcal{K}
over
X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)^{2}
, we consider the energy functional

\mathcal{E}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}A^{\alpha}uA^{2s-\alpha}vdx-\int_{\Omega}H(u,v)dx,\quad\forall\;(u,v)\in\mathbf{E}_{\alpha}.

(1.17)

The choice of
\mathbf{E}_{\alpha}
originates from three observations.

•

\mathcal{H}^{2s}(\Omega)=E^{2s}
, seeing Proposition
3.1
;

•

E^{\alpha}
can be embedded compactly into some
L^{r}(\Omega)
, seeing Proposition
2.6
and Remark
3.2
;

•

Every critical point of
\mathcal{E}
is a
L^{1}
-weak solution to (
1.1
), seeing Proposition
3.4
.

Applying a linking theorem in
[
23
]
, we show the following existence result, which extends the study for the Laplacian case in
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
N>2s
and
p,q>0
satisfy

1>\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>\frac{N-2s}{N},

(1.18)

and

(N-4s)\max\{p,q\}<N+4s.

(1.19)

Assume that
H\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
satisfies

(H1)

H\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
;

(H2)

There exists
R>0
such that

\frac{1}{p+1}H_{u}(u,v)u+\frac{1}{q+1}H_{v}(u,v)v\geq H(u,v)>0,\quad\forall\;|u|+|v|\geq R;

(1.20)

(H3)

There exist
r>0
,
C>0
such that

|H(u,v)|\leq C(|u|^{p+1}+|v|^{q+1}),\quad\forall\;|u|+|v|\leq r;

(1.21)

(H4)

There exists
C>0
such that for any
(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

|H_{u}(u,v)|\leq C\Big(|u|^{p}+|v|^{\frac{p(q+1)}{p+1}}+1\Big),\quad|H_{v}(u,v)|\leq C\Big(|v|^{q}+|u|^{\frac{q(p+1)}{q+1}}+1\Big).

(1.22)

Then, there exists a nontrivial classical solution to (
1.1
).

Note that Theorem
1.4
requires the assumption (
1.19
). For getting the existence of solutions to (
1.1
) in a more broad range of
p
,
q
, we will apply also the dual method. Three major difficulties subsist.

•

The first problem is still to construct suitable functional framework. We choose (see section
4
) the energy functional as follows

\mathcal{J}(f,g)=\mathcal{H}^{*}(f,g)-\int_{\Omega}g\mathcal{A}fdx,\quad\forall\,(f,g)\in X:=L^{1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)\times L^{1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)

(1.23)

where
\mathcal{A}
is the inverse of
(-\Delta)^{s}:\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)\to L^{1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{H}^{*}
denotes the Legendre-Fenchel transform of

\mathcal{H}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}H(u,v)dx,\quad\forall\;(u,v)\in X^{*}=L^{p+1}(\Omega)\times L^{q+1}(\Omega).

•

Secondly, we need to verify the differentiability of
\mathcal{J}
, and the correspondence between critical points of
\mathcal{J}
and solutions to (
1.1
). It should be mentioned that these arguments were not proven explicitly in
[
16
]
for the Laplacian case.

•

Finally, we need to check the compact embedding properties of
\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)
in order to check the Palais-Smale condition.

To deal with these difficulties, we use properties of Legendre-Fenchel transform (see Lemma
2.9
) to ensure the well-definedness of
\mathcal{J}
. For the differentiability of
\mathcal{J}
, we will prove in Lemma
4.5
that

\mathcal{H}^{*}(f,g)=\int_{\Omega}H^{*}(f,g)dx,\quad\forall\;(f,g)\in X,

where
H^{*}
is the Legendre-Fenchel transform of
H:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
. We use the fact

(\nabla H)^{-1}=\nabla H^{*}
(see Lemma
4.3
) which guarantees that
\mathcal{H}^{*}
is well defined and of class
C^{1}
over
X
. Moreover, under the superlinear growth assumption of
H
,
\mathcal{J}
has a mountain pass geometry, and
H_{z}^{*}(f,g):=\nabla H^{*}(f,g)
provides a weak solution to (
1.1
) given any critical point
(f,g)\in X
for
\mathcal{J}
, see Proposition
4.7
. The compact embeddings of
\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{p}}(\Omega)
are given in Proposition
2.6
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
N>2s
,
p,q>0
satisfy (
1.18
). Assume that
H\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
satisfies

(H5)

There exist positive numbers
C_{1},C_{2}
such that

\begin{split}&C_{1}|u|^{p+1}\leq H_{u}(u,v)u\leq C_{2}\left(|u|^{p+1}+|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}\right),\\
&C_{1}|v|^{q+1}\leq H_{v}(u,v)v\leq C_{2}\left(|v|^{q+1}+|u|^{\alpha}|v|^{\beta}\right),\end{split}

with

\frac{\alpha}{p+1}+\frac{\beta}{q+1}=1,\;\;\alpha,\beta>1;

(1.24)

(H6)

\nabla H

\langle\nabla H(u_{1},v_{1})-\nabla H(u_{2},v_{2}),\,(u_{1},v_{1})-(u_{2},v_{2})\rangle>0,\quad\mbox{for any disjoint }(u_{1},v_{1}),\,(u_{2},v_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2};

(H7)

There is

and positive numbers

,

Then there exists a nontrivial classical solution to (

).

Remark 1.6
.

(H5)

.
(H7)
is useful to prove that every Palais-Smale sequence of

Remark 1.7
.

The nonlinearities

But some differences exist between the two families of assumptions. For example, let

1.3
Fractional Lane-Emden system

As a special example, we revisit the fractional Lane-Emden system

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}u&=|v|^{q-1}v\,&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
(-\Delta)^{s}v&=|u|^{p-1}u\,&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
u&=v=0&&\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.26)

where
s\in(0,1)
,
p,q\in(0,\infty)
,
N>2s
. As for the classical Laplacian case, we consider subcritical exponents
p,q
, that is

p,q>0,\quad\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{1}{q+1}>\frac{N-2s}{N}.

(1.27)

Leite and Montenegro
[
27
]
showed the existence of positive viscosity solutions to (
1.26
) under the subcritical condition (
1.27
), they reduced (
1.26
) into a single equation and consider energy functional on
W_{0}^{1,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)\cap W^{2s,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)
.
Choi and Kim
[
13
,
12
]
studied the related problems with respect to spectral fractional Laplacian.

Different from
[
27
]
, we work with the functional space
\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)
.
Comparing with
[
27
]
, our setting avoid many regularity problems since every
\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)
solution is naturally a
L^{1}
-weak solution. More precisely, we consider

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}\left(|(-\Delta)^{s}u|^{\frac{1}{q}-1}(-\Delta)^{s}u\right)&=|u|^{p-1}u\;&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
u&=(-\Delta)^{s}u=0&&\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.28)

Definition 1.8
.

We call
u
an energy solution of (
1.28
), if
u\in\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{s}u|^{\frac{1}{q}-1}(-\Delta)^{s}u(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-1}u\varphi dx,\quad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega).

(1.29)

Note that the above terms are well defined since
(-\Delta)^{s}u\in L^{1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)
for any
u\in\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)
. Putting
v=|(-\Delta)^{s}u|^{\frac{1}{q}-1}(-\Delta)^{s}u
, we can prove that
(u,v)
is a classical solution to (
1.26
) if and only if
u
is an energy solution to (
1.28
), see Proposition
5.1
.

Moreover, solutions to (
1.28
) coincide with critical points of the following
C^{1}
functional in
\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{I}(u):=\frac{q}{q+1}\left|(-\Delta)^{s}u\right|_{1+\frac{1}{q}}^{1+\frac{1}{q}}-\frac{1}{p+1}|u|_{p+1}^{p+1}.

Consider the Nehari manifold associated to
\mathcal{I}
,

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{I}}:=\{u\in\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}:\langle\mathcal{I}^{\prime}(u),u\rangle=0\},

(1.30)

and the ground state level is defined as

c_{\mathcal{I}}:=\underset{u\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{I}}}{\inf}\mathcal{I}(u).

(1.31)

We establish the existence of positive solutions to (
1.26
) by showing that
c_{\mathcal{I}}
can be attained.

Theorem 1.9
.

Assume that
s\in(0,1)
,
N>2s
,
p,q
satisfy (
1.27
) and
pq\neq 1
. Then

(i)

(
1.26
) admits a positive classical ground state solution;

(ii)

If
pq<1
, the positive classical solution of (
1.26
) is unique;

(iii)

If
\Omega
is a ball, then (
1.26
) has a positive radially symmetric classical solution.

When
\Omega
is a ball and
pq<1
, the uniqueness of positive classical solution to (
1.26
) ensures that the solution is radially symmetric. For
pq>1
, we can not claim directly as in
[
5
]
that the ground state solution is radially symmetric, but we can work simply with
\mathcal{W}_{{\rm rad}}^{2s,r}(B_{R})
, the subset of radial functions in
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(B_{R})
. In fact, for
f\in C(\overline{B_{R}})
,
f\geq 0
, if
u
,
w
are respectively solutions of

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}u&=f&&\text{in}\penalty\ B_{R},\\
u&=0\penalty\ &&\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B_{R}},\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}\quad\quad\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}w&=f^{\#}&&\text{in}\penalty\ B_{R},\\
w&=0\penalty\ &&\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\overline{B_{R}},\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

where

denotes the radial decreasing rearrangement of

, we do not have always

, see
[
24
]
, while it is true for

.

Remark 1.10
.

For any classical solution
(u,v)
of (
1.26
), we have

\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q+1}dx=\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u,v\rangle=\langle(-\Delta)^{s}v,u\rangle=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p+1}dx.

Combining with

\mathcal{K}(u,v)=\mathcal{I}(u)

reaches the minimal energy among all classical solutions if

2.1
Basic properties for

-weak solutions

Here we show some elementary facts for

-weak solutions. Many of them were established for solutions in

-weak solutions.

Let us begin with the comparison principle. We say that

(-\Delta)^{s}u\leq(-\Delta)^{s}v
in the

-weak sense, if
u,v\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}(u-v)(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi dx\leq 0,\quad\forall\,\varphi\in\mathcal{T}_{s}(\Omega)\mbox{ with }(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi\geq 0.

(2.1)

By the definition of

fulfilled

Lemma 2.1
.

If

in the

-weak sense, then
u\leq v
a.e. in
\Omega
.

The next one is a Hopf lemma result for
L^{1}
-weak supersolutions.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
f\in L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx)
be nonnegative and

. Then there exists
C>0
depending only on
\Omega
,

and

such that for any

satisfying
(-\Delta)^{s}u\geq f
,
u>C\delta^{s}
a.e. in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Fix
f_{k}:=\min\{k,f\}
with
k
large enough such that
f_{k}\neq 0
. Let
u_{k}\subset X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
solve (ensured by Lemma
1.1
)

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{k}=f_{k}\;\;\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\quad u_{k}=0\;\;\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

(2.2)

As
f_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, by maximum principle (see
[
36
, Proposition 2.2.8]
,
[
8
, Theorem A.1]
) and Hopf’s lemma (see
[
11
, Proposition 2.7]
,
[

and
[

) for bounded source, there holds

for

. Using previous lemma, we get

, and finish the proof.
∎

Inspired by
[
10
]

-weak solution.

Proposition 2.3

Given any

L^{1}
-weak solution
u
to (
1.12
). Moreover, there exists
C=C(s,\Omega)>0
such that

|u|_{1}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx)}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Assume first
f\geq 0
, otherwise we decompose
f=f_{+}-f_{-}
. Let
\xi\in X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
be the solution of

(-\Delta)^{s}\xi=1\;\;\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\quad\xi=0\;\;\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.

(2.4)

For any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, set
f_{k}:=\min\{k,f\}
, and
u_{k}\geq 0
the solution of (
2.2
).
As

, we can use it as test function to (

for

|u_{k}|_{1}=\langle(-\Delta)^{s}\xi,u_{k}\rangle=\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u_{k},\xi\rangle=\int_{\Omega}f_{k}\xi dx\leq C\int_{\Omega}f_{k}\delta^{s}dx\leq C\|f\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx)}.

Similarly, considering

Assume that

2.2
Embedding and density

Here we expose some basic properties of
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
, including (compact) embedding results and density property, which will be important for constructing the variational framework.

Proposition 2.6
.

Assume that
s\in(0,1)
,
N>2s
,
r\geq 1
,
\Omega
is a smooth bounded domain. Then
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
is a Banach space and
\mathcal{T}_{s}(\Omega)
is dense in
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
. On the other hand, we have continuous embedding
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)\subset L^{\gamma}(\Omega)
for

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &1\leq\gamma<\frac{N}{N-2s},\;\;&&\text{if}\penalty\ r=1;\\
&1\leq\gamma\leq\frac{Nr}{N-2sr},\;\;&&\text{if}\penalty\ 1<r<\frac{N}{2s};\\
&1\leq\gamma<\infty,&&\text{if}\penalty\ 2sr=N;\\
&\gamma=\infty,&&\text{if}\penalty\ 2sr>N.\\
\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(2.5)

Moreover, the above embeddings are compact provided

1\leq\gamma<\frac{Nr}{N-2sr}\;\;\text{if}\penalty\ N>2sr;\quad\mbox{or}\;\;1\leq\gamma<\infty,\;\;\text{if}\penalty\ N\leq 2sr.

(2.6)

Proof.

The density of
\mathcal{T}_{s}(\Omega)
in
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
is obvious by definition and Lemma
1.1
.

The completeness of
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
is a simple fact. Let
\{u_{j}\}\subset\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
be a Cauchy sequence, by definition,
\{(-\Delta)^{s}u_{j}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{r}({\Omega})
, hence converges to
v\in L^{r}({\Omega})
. Using Proposition
2.3
, there is a unique
L^{1}
-weak solution
u
satisfying (
1.12
) with
f=v
. Clearly
u\in\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
, and
u_{j}
tends to
u
in
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
.

On the other hand, in virtue of Proposition
2.5
, the embedding of
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)\subset L^{\gamma}(\Omega)
is continuous if (
2.5
) holds true, we shall only prove that the embedding is compact provided (
2.6
).

Without loss of generality, we only consider the case
N>2sr
,
1<r
,
1\leq\gamma<\frac{Nr}{N-2sr}
. If
r\geq\frac{2N}{N+2s}
, then
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)\subset X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
is continuous by Proposition
2.5
. By Hölder inequality, fix any
1<\gamma<\frac{Nr}{N-2sr}
,

|u|_{{\gamma}}\leq|u|^{1-\theta}_{{1}}|u|^{\theta}_{{\frac{Nr}{N-2sr}}}\quad\mbox{with }\theta=\frac{Nr-\gamma(N-2sr)}{Nr-(N-2sr)}.

(2.7)

Since
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded in
L^{\frac{Nr}{N-2sr}}(\Omega)
and compactly embedded in
L^{1}(\Omega)
,
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{\gamma}(\Omega)
.

Suppose now
1<r<\frac{2N}{N+2s}
, we first claim that the embedding
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)\subset L^{1}(\Omega)
is compact. Indeed, let
\{u_{j}\}
be a bounded sequence in
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
, then
\{u_{j}\}
is bounded in
L^{\frac{Nr}{N-2sr}}(\Omega)
and the sequence
f_{j}=(-\Delta)^{s}u_{j}
is bounded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
. Let
A>0
to be chosen later, we denote
g_{j}=f_{j}\chi_{|f_{j}(x)|\leq A}
and
h_{j}=f_{j}\chi_{|f_{j}(x)|>A}
. By Hölder and Chebychev inequalities,

\displaystyle\|h_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx)}\leq C|h_{j}|_{1}\leq C|f_{j}|_{r}\Big(\int_{|f_{j}(x)|>A}dx\Big)^{1-\frac{1}{r}}\leq C^{\prime}A^{\frac{1}{r}-1}.

Let
\{v_{j}\}
and
\{w_{j}\}
be respectively
L^{1}
-weak solutions of

\left\{\begin{split}(-\Delta)^{s}v_{j}&=g_{j}&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
v_{j}&=0&&\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\\
\end{split}\right.\quad\left\{\begin{split}(-\Delta)^{s}w_{j}&=h_{j}&&\text{in}\penalty\ \Omega,\\
w_{j}&=0&&\text{in}\penalty\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\\
\end{split}\right.

(2.8)

Clearly
u_{j}=v_{j}+w_{j}
. Given any
\varepsilon>0
, we fix
A
large enough such that

|w_{j}|_{1}\leq C\|h_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega;\delta^{s}dx)}\leq C^{\prime}A^{\frac{1}{r}-1}\leq\varepsilon.

Moreover,
\{v_{j}\}
is bounded in
X_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
hence relatively compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Therefore
\{u_{j}\}
is precompact, or equivalently relatively compact in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, which means
\mathcal{W}^{2s,r}(\Omega)
is compactly embedded in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. Applying again the interpolation inequality (
2.7
),
\{u_{j}\}
is compact in
L^{\gamma}(\Omega)
for
1\leq\gamma<\frac{Nr}{N-2sr}
. So we are done.
∎

2.3
A linking theorem

The Linking theorem
2.8
below was given by Felmer
[
23
, Theorem 3.1]
, it is useful for the proof of Theorem
1.4
. For the sake of completeness, we recall also the definition of Palais-Smale condition.

Definition 2.7
.

For a Banach space
X
,
{\mathcal{I}}\in C^{1}(X,\mathbb{R})
is said satisfying the Palais-Smale condition at level
c\in\mathbb{R}

(
for short
(PS)_{c}
)
if any sequence
\{u_{j}\}\subset X
satisfying

{\mathcal{I}}(u_{j})\rightarrow c\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\mathcal{I}}^{\prime}(u_{j})\rightarrow 0\;\mbox{ in }\;X^{*}

admits a convergent subsequence in
X
. We say that
{\mathcal{I}}
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition
(
(PS)
for short
)
if
(PS)_{c}
is satisfied for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a Hilbert space such that
H=H_{1}\oplus H_{2}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{I}\in C^{1}(H)
satisfies
(PS)
condition and
\mathcal{I}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\langle Lz,z\rangle-\mathcal{J}(z)
, where

(i)

L:H\to H
is a bounded, self-adjoint linear operator and
L(H_{1})\subset H_{1}
;
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}:H\to H^{*}
is compact;

(ii)

There exist two linear, bounded, invertible operators
B_{1}
,
B_{2}:H\to H
such that
\widehat{B}_{\tau}=P_{2}B_{1}^{-1}e^{\tau L}B_{2}:H_{2}\to H_{2}
is invertible for any
\tau\geq 0
. Here
P_{2}:H\to H_{2}
is the projection along
H_{1}
and
e^{\tau L}=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\frac{(\tau L)^{n}}{n!}
;

(iii)

Let
e_{1}\in H_{1}
with
\|e_{1}\|=1
. Let
\rho>0
,
R_{1}>\frac{\rho}{\|B_{1}^{-1}B_{2}e_{1}\|}
,
R_{2}>\rho
and define

S=\{B_{1}z_{1}:z_{1}\in H_{1},\|z_{1}\|=\rho\},\quad Q=\{B_{2}(te_{1}+z_{2}):0\leq t\leq R_{1},z_{2}\in H_{2},\|z_{2}\|\leq R_{2}\}.

Suppose that
\mathcal{I}(z)\geq\sigma>0
on
S
and
\mathcal{I}(z)\leq 0
on
\partial Q
.

Then
\mathcal{I}
has a critical point
z_{0}\in H
such that
\mathcal{I}(z_{0})\geq\sigma
.

2.4
Legendre-Fenchel transform

Let
V
be a Banach space and
V^{*}
be its dual space. For a function
G:V\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,
G\not\equiv+\infty
, the function
G^{*}:V^{*}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
given by

G^{*}(u^{*})=\sup\{\langle u^{*},v\rangle-G(v):v\in V\},\quad\forall\,u^{*}\in V^{*},

(2.9)

is named the Legendre-Fenchel transform of
G
. The following are some basic properties of
G^{*}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
G^{*}
be the Legendre-Fenchel transform of
G
.

(a)

The Legendre-Fenchel transform reverses the order, that is,
\widetilde{G}\geq G
implies
\widetilde{G}^{*}\leq G^{*}
.

(b)

If
G\in C^{1}(V,\mathbb{R})
is convex and
G^{*}\in C^{1}(V^{*},\mathbb{R})
, then for
v\in V
,

Proposition 3.1

For any

.

Verification of

. We proceed by the following three steps.

Lemma 3.9
.

Under the assumption
(H4)
with subcritical

Proof.

Since

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that

-

Proof.

By Lemma

such that if

38

We define a homotopy

For any

By Brouwer’s invariance of domain theorem, it’s known that any continuous bijection of Euclidean space is indeed a homeomorphism; we can get this fact also by noting that

\mathcal{H}^{*}(f_{n},g_{n})-\langle(\mathcal{H}^{*})^{\prime}(f_{n},g_{n}),((1-\theta)f_{n},\theta g_{n})\rangle=c+o(1)\|(f_{n},g_{n})\|_{X}+o(1)

Proposition 5.1

Proof.

First, assume that

Next, since

(5.2)

\int_{\Omega}|(-\Delta)^{s}u|^{\frac{1}{q}-1}(-\Delta)^{s}u(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi dx=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{p-1}u\varphi dx\quad\forall\;\varphi\in\mathcal{W}^{2s,1+\frac{1}{q}}(\Omega).

We denote

In the sequel, we consider the functional

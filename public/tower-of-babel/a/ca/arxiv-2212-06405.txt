Twisted Thue equations with multiple exponents in fixed number fields
Tobias Hilgart tobias.hilgart@plus.ac.at

Abstract
Let K K be a number field of degree d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 and fix s s multiplicatively independent γ 1 , … , γ s ∈ K ∗ \gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{s}\in K^{*} that fulfil some technical requirements, which can be vastly simplified to ℚ \mathbb{Q} -linearly independence, given Schanuel’s conjecture. We then consider the twisted Thue equation | N K / ℚ ( X − γ 1 t 1 ⋯ γ s t s Y ) | = 1 , \left|N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\left(X-\gamma_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{s}^{t_{s}}Y\right)\right|=1, and prove that it has only finitely many solutions ( x , y , ( t 1 , … , t s ) ) \left(x,y,\left(t_{1},\dots,t_{s}\right)\right) with x ​ y ≠ 0 xy\neq 0 and ℚ ( γ 1 t 1 ⋯ γ s t s ) = K \mathbb{Q}\left(\gamma_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{s}^{t_{s}}\right)=K , all of which are effectively computable.

Introduction

One of the first non-binary parametrised Thue equations to ever be solved was

X^{3}-\left(a-1\right)X^{2}Y-\left(a+2\right)XY^{2}-Y^{3}=\pm 1,

done so by Thomas
[
Tho90
]
. If we express the polynomial equation in terms of its roots
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}
, which of course depend on the parameter
a
, then we can write this equivalently as

\left(X-\alpha_{1}Y\right)\left(X-\alpha_{2}Y\right)\left(X-\alpha_{3}Y\right)=\pm 1,

or
\left|N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\left(X-\alpha_{1}Y\right)\right|=1
, where
K=\mathbb{Q}\left(\alpha_{1}\right)
. This result was extended by Levesque and Waldschmidt
[
LW15
]
by twisting the equation by an exponential parameter
t
and showed that
\left|N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\left(X-\alpha_{1}^{t}Y\right)\right|=1
still only has finitely many integer solutions in
x,y,a,t
where
\left|y\right|>1
. They conjectured that this still holds, if the Thue equation is twisted by two exponents, i.e.
\left|N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\left(X-\alpha_{1}^{t}\alpha_{2}^{s}Y\right)\right|=1
, and it is reasonable to expect that if it works for two, it might work for finitely many – at least given sufficiently ”nice” conditions.

In a similar but different vein, Levesque and Waldschmidt
[
LW13
]
also showed the following: Let
K=\mathbb{Q}\left(\alpha\right)
, with embeddings
\Phi=\left\{\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\sigma}_{d}\right\}
into
\mathbb{C}
and
0<\nu<1
. Furthermore, let
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{K}
be an integral unit that fulfils
\mathbb{Q}\left(\alpha\varepsilon\right)=K
and

\left|\tilde{\sigma}_{1}(\alpha\varepsilon)\right|=\max_{i}\left(\left|\tilde{\sigma}_{i}(\alpha\varepsilon)\right|\right),\;\left|\tilde{\sigma}_{2}(\alpha\varepsilon)\right|\geq\max_{i}\left(\left|\tilde{\sigma}_{i}(\alpha\varepsilon)\right|\right)^{\nu}

(1)

for two distinct embeddings
\tilde{\sigma}_{1},\tilde{\sigma}_{2}\in\Phi
. Then for any solution
\left(x,y,\varepsilon\right)
of the inequality

\left|N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\left(x-\alpha\varepsilon\,y\right)\right|\leq m

the logarithms
\log\left|x\right|,\log\left|y\right|
, as well as the absolute logarithmic height
h(\alpha\varepsilon)
can be bounded by
m^{c}
for some effective constant

. They conjectured that the result would hold even without Condition (
1
). Proof of the finiteness of the number of solutions is already established in
[
LW12
]
but rests on Schmidt’s subspace theorem which does not allow for an effective upper bound for their heights.

For our result, we also fix the number field. The ”base” elements of the norm-form equation are fixed as well but can be chosen somewhat more freely. Our main result is as follows:

.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
d\geq 3

\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{s}\in K^{*}
be multiplicatively independent algebraic integers such that for each choice of

\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\log\left|\frac{\tilde{\sigma}_{d-2}(\gamma_{1})}{\tilde{\sigma}_{d-1}(\gamma_{1})}\right|&\cdots&\log\left|\frac{\tilde{\sigma}_{d-2}(\gamma_{s})}{\tilde{\sigma}_{d-1}(\gamma_{s})}\right|\end{pmatrix}=s,

has only finitely many integer solutions

and
\mathbb{Q}\left(\gamma_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{s}^{t_{s}}\right)=K

1.

\nu|p

\left|p\right|_{\nu}=p^{-d_{\nu}/d}

\nu|\infty

. In view of this normalisation, the product formula

\prod_{\nu}\left|\alpha\right|_{\nu}=1

holds for every

. The absolute height of

is then defined as

H(\alpha)=\prod_{\nu}\max\left(1,\left|\alpha\right|_{\nu}\right),

and the absolute logarithmic height as
h(\alpha)=\log H(\alpha)
. The absolute height is then equal to the Mahler measure
M(m_{\alpha})
of its minimal polynomial

, i.e. if

is the minimal polynomial of
\alpha\in K
, then

h(\alpha)=\frac{1}{n}\log M(m_{\alpha})=\frac{1}{n}\left(\left|a_{n}\right|+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log\max\left(1,\left|\alpha_{i}\right|\right)\right).

The next lemma goes back to a result of Tijdeman
[
Tij73
]
on the difference of consecutive numbers that are comprised of primes up to some given bound. We can adapt the statement to our setting with only small alterations and prove it almost analogously.

Lemma 1
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
d\geq s
and
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{s}\in K^{*}
multiplicatively independent. Let
\gamma=\gamma(t_{1},\dots,t_{s})=\gamma_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{s}^{t_{s}}
for non-zero integers
t_{1},\dots,t_{s}
.

Then for any two conjugates
\gamma^{(1)},\gamma^{(2)}
of
\gamma
with
M=\left|\gamma^{(1)}\right|>\left|\gamma^{(2)}\right|=m
there exists an effectively computable constant
c
independent of
t_{1},\dots,t_{s}
such that

M-m>\frac{M}{h(M)^{c}}.

Proof.

We start with moving the conjugation of
\gamma
down to the conjugations of the individual
\gamma_{i}
, i.e. if
\gamma^{(1)}
is the conjugation of
\gamma
under the embedding
\tilde{\sigma}
, let
\gamma_{i}^{(1)}=\tilde{\sigma}\left(\gamma_{i}\right)
, then we write

M-m=\left|\gamma^{(1)}\right|-\left|\gamma^{(2)}\right|=\left|\left(\gamma_{1}^{(1)}\right)^{t_{1}}\cdots\left(\gamma_{s}^{(1)}\right)^{t_{s}}\right|-\left|\left(\gamma_{1}^{(2)}\right)^{t_{1}}\cdots\left(\gamma_{s}^{(2)}\right)^{t_{s}}\right|,

and thus

1-\frac{m}{M}=1-\underbrace{\left|\left(\gamma_{1}^{(1)}\right)^{t_{1}}\cdots\left(\gamma_{s}^{(1)}\right)^{t_{s}}\left(\gamma_{1}^{(2)}\right)^{-t_{1}}\cdots\left(\gamma_{s}^{(2)}\right)^{-t_{s}}\right|}_{=:e^{\Lambda}}.

We want to use Proposition
1
to bound
\Lambda
. We have that
h(\gamma_{i})\ll 1
. If we also had
\left|t_{i}\right|\ll h(M)
, then we would have, by Proposition
1
, that
\left|\Lambda\right|>h(M)^{-c}
for some effectively computable
c
independent of the
t_{i}
, which is precisely the constant of the proposition multiplied with the heights
h(\gamma_{i})
. And if
\left|\Lambda\right|>h(M)^{-c}
, then
1-e^{\Lambda}\gg h(M)^{-c}
which then proves the assumption after multiplying the inequality with
M
(we can make the inequality
1-e^{\Lambda}\gg h(M)^{-c}
a strict
">"
by allowing for a larger constant
c
).

So what remains is to prove that
\left|t_{i}\right|\ll h(M)
. For that let
S
be the set of all places
\nu
of
K
for which
\left|\gamma^{(1)}\right|_{\nu}\neq 1
but in any case includes all non-archimedean places
\infty_{1},\dots,\infty_{d}
, that is
S=\left\{\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n},\infty_{1},\dots,\infty_{d}\right\}
.

In the spirit of an S-adic version of Minkowski spaces, we identify
\gamma
with the
\left|S\right|
-dimensional vector of logarithms of the individual valuations, i.e.

\gamma\mapsto\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|\gamma\right|_{\infty_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\log\left|\gamma\right|_{\infty_{d}}\\
\log\left|\gamma\right|_{\nu_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\log\left|\gamma\right|_{\nu_{n}}\end{pmatrix}}_{=:v}=t_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|\gamma_{1}\right|_{\infty_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\log\left|\gamma_{1}\right|_{\infty_{d}}\\
\log\left|\gamma_{1}\right|_{\nu_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\log\left|\gamma_{1}\right|_{\nu_{n}}\end{pmatrix}+\cdots+t_{s}\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|\gamma_{s}\right|_{\infty_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\log\left|\gamma_{s}\right|_{\infty_{d}}\\
\log\left|\gamma_{s}\right|_{\nu_{1}}\\
\vdots\\
\log\left|\gamma_{s}\right|_{\nu_{n}}\end{pmatrix}.

We further see that the right-hand side is of the form
v=\Gamma\left(t_{1},\dots,t_{s}\right)^{\top}
for the
(d+n)\times s
dimensional matrix
\Gamma
of the
\log\left|\gamma_{i}\right|_{\nu}
. Since
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{s}
are multiplicatively independent, the matrix
\Gamma
has full column-rank. We can thus multiply the equation with
\left(\Gamma^{\top}\Gamma\right)^{-1}\Gamma^{\top}
and apply the
l_{1}
norm. Using the consistency
\lVert Ax\rVert\leq\lVert A\rVert\cdot\lVert x\rVert
and hiding the matrix norms inside a constant
c
that does not depend on the
t_{i}
, this gives

c\cdot\left|v\right|_{l_{1}}\geq\left|\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}\\
\vdots\\
t_{s}\end{pmatrix}\right|_{l_{1}},

which of course implies
\left|t_{i}\right|\ll\left|v\right|_{l_{1}}
. For the final remaining estimate, note that to compute the height
h(\gamma)=\sum_{\nu}\max\left(\log\left|\gamma\right|_{\nu},0\right)
, we can ignore all the valuations where
\left|\gamma\right|_{\nu}=1
and thus sum precisely over all entries of the vector
v
and take only the positive values. But by the product formula, we have
\sum_{i=1}^{d+n}v_{i}=0
, i.e. the positive entries, which contribute to the height, and the negative that don’t, cancel. This in turn means that if we sum the absolute values, which is precisely what we do when calculating the
l_{1}
-norm
\left|v\right|_{l_{1}}
, it must be 2 times the sum of all positive (or negative, resp.) values, thus
\left|v\right|_{l_{1}}=2h(\gamma)
. This gives us
\left|t_{i}\right|\ll h(\gamma)
and thus proves the assertion.

Let us now call

\sigma_{a}=\min\left(\left|\sigma_{j}\right|,\left|\sigma_{k}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\log\left|\frac{\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{k}}{\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{l}}\right|=\log\frac{\sigma_{A}}{\sigma_{B}}+\log\left|\frac{1-\frac{\sigma_{a}}{\sigma_{A}}}{1-\frac{\sigma_{b}}{\sigma_{B}}}\right|,

Assume for now that

To that end, note that for any

\displaystyle\log\left|\beta_{i}\right|

\displaystyle=\log\left|x-\sigma_{j}y+y\left(\sigma_{j}-\sigma_{i}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\log\left|y\right|+\log\left|\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j}\right|+\log\left|1+\frac{\beta_{j}}{y\left|\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j}\right|}\right|

\displaystyle\ll\log\left|y\right|+\log\left|\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j}\right|.

. On the other hand, we have by Lemma

where we have, as a reminder,

by looking at the Mahler measure to compute the height, and
\log\left|\sigma_{1}\right|\ll t
by Equation (

\displaystyle\log\left|\beta_{i}\right|\ll\log\left|y\right|+t

. We also get the same bound for the positive quantity

(12)

and thus

(13)

for all

We return to our decomposition into powers of fundamental units,

Doing this for all

, and we only care that the unit rank

\underbrace{\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|\eta_{1}^{(1)}\right|&\cdots&\log\left|\eta_{r}^{(1)}\right|\\

\vdots\\

\log\left|\beta_{r}\right|\end{pmatrix}.

Since the

\max_{i}\left|b_{i}\right|\leq c_{4}\max_{i}\log\left|\beta_{i}\right|\ll t,

where

If we return to Equation (

. The heights of the

do not depend on

or the

.

We now have to check what happens if the linear form vanishes instead, i.e. if
\Lambda=0
. In this case, we have that
\frac{\beta_{l}}{\beta_{k}}=\frac{\sigma_{B}}{\sigma_{A}}
and we have to differentiate between four cases:

1.

Case

and thus

, which we again ruled out by requiring that

.

2.

Case

: This implies
\beta_{l}\sigma_{k}=\beta_{k}\sigma_{l}
, if we plug this into Siegel’s Identity (

\beta_{j}\left(\sigma_{k}-\sigma_{l}\right)=\beta_{l}\sigma_{j}+\beta_{k}\sigma_{j}

The fraction on the left-hand side is
\ll e^{-c_{6}t}
, which follows completely analogously to how we showed Equation (

. Thus,

\log\left|\frac{\beta_{k}}{\beta_{l}}\right|=\log\left(1-O\left(e^{-c_{6}t}\right)\right)\ll e^{-c_{6}t},

and the first equality also implies that
\log\left|\beta_{k}/\beta_{l}\right|\neq 0

Case

and thus

The first fraction on the left-hand side is

. The second fraction

, since
l
fulfils (

∗
⁣
∗

‣
3.1
) and

. This gives

Case

: This is analogous to the previous case, we now have

and
\beta_{l}\sigma_{j}=\beta_{k}\sigma_{l}

, while the second fraction is

, since

fulfils (

∗
⁣
∗

3.1

\left|\sigma_{j}\right|\geq\left|\sigma_{k}\right|
. This gives

4

Connection to Schanuel’s conjecture

If we assume Schanuel’s conjecture, then the full rank of the matrix
\Gamma
follows from the
\mathbb{Q}
-linear independency of
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{s}
. For sake of completeness, we state this as a second Theorem:

Theorem 2
.

Let
K
be a number field of degree
d\geq 3
and
s\leq d-2
. Let
\gamma_{1},\dots\gamma_{s}\in K^{*}
be multiplicatively independent and
\mathbb{Q}
-linearly independent algebraic integers.

If Schanuel’s conjecture holds, then the Thue equation

\left|N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}\left(X-\gamma_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{s}^{t_{s}}Y\right)\right|=1

has only finitely many integer solutions
\left(x,y,\left(t_{1},\dots,t_{s}\right)\right)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{N}^{s}
with
xy\neq 0
and
\mathbb{Q}\left(\gamma_{1}^{t_{1}}\cdots\gamma_{s}^{t_{s}}\right)=K
, all of which can be effectively computed.

Proof.

The proof for Case 1 of Theorem 1 can be carried over par for par. We adapt the proof for Case 2, i.e. that Equation (
15
) holds.

Assume that the matrix
\Gamma
does not have full rank, that there exist
x_{1},\dots,x_{s}
, not all zero such that

x_{1}\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|\frac{\gamma_{1}^{(1)}}{\gamma_{1}^{(d)}}\right|\\
\vdots\\
\log\left|\frac{\gamma_{1}^{(d-2)}}{\gamma_{1}^{(d)}}\right|\end{pmatrix}+\cdots+x_{s}\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|\frac{\gamma_{s\phantom{1}}^{(1)}}{\gamma_{s\phantom{1}}^{(d)}}\right|\\
\vdots\\
\log\left|\frac{\gamma_{s\phantom{1}}^{(d-2)}}{\gamma_{s\phantom{1}}^{(d)}}\right|\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

If we rewrite this slightly, then this means that

\begin{pmatrix}\log\left|\gamma_{1}^{(1)}\right|&\cdots&\log\left|\gamma_{s}^{(1)}\right|\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\log\left|\gamma_{1}^{(d-2)}\right|&\cdots&\log\left|\gamma_{s}^{(d-2)}\right|\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}\\
\vdots\\
x_{s}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda\\
\vdots\\
\lambda\end{pmatrix},

where
\lambda=x_{1}\log\left|\gamma_{1}^{(d)}\right|+\dots+x_{s}\log\left|\gamma_{s}^{(d)}\right|\neq 0
, since the
\gamma_{i}
are multiplicatively independent. Thus
\lambda
is a non-zero eigenvalue of the matrix on the left-hand side, which in turn means that
\lambda
and
\log\left|\gamma_{1}^{(i)}\right|,\dots,\log\left|\gamma_{s}^{(i)}\right|
are algebraically dependent. But Schanuel’s conjecture asserts that if
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{s}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
then
\log\left|\gamma_{1}\right|,\dots,\log\left|\gamma_{s}\right|
are algebraically independent over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
. This gives the contradiction and thus the full rank for the original matrix
\Gamma
.

We can then proceed analogously to the proof of Theorem 1 and derive
t\ll 1
and in further consequence
\left|x\right|,\left|y\right|\ll 1
.

∎

Abstract
. Inspired by Rumin’s work on a subcomplex in sub-Riemannian manifolds which is cohomologically equivalent to the de Rham complex, we present a more general construction that produces subcomplexes from any filtered cochain complex of finite depth and still computes the cohomology of the original filtered complex. A priori these subcomplexes depend not only on the filtration itself, but also on the choice of additional structures. However, we show that the construction only depends on the given filtration up to isomorphism. Finally, we show how such subcomplexes relate to spectral sequences, a cohomological machinery that arises naturally when considering a filtered complex.

In the current paper, we demonstrate how the Rumin complex appears as a specific instance of a more general method of generating subcomplexes that can be applied to any filtered cochain complex
(K,d)
. In order to produce such subcomplexes, one needs to make certain choices related to the filtration itself, such as the operator
d_{0}\colon K\to K
which we call a base differential of
(K,d)
, along with the subspaces
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, which will be referred to as the base transversal and cotransversal of
d_{0}
. However, the subcomplexes produced by any of these choices will all be isomorphic to each other, and they are all compute the same cohomology as the original filtered complex
(K,d)
. Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(K,d)
be a filtered cochain complex of finite depth with filtration
K=K_{0}\supset K_{1}\supset K_{2}\supset\cdots\supset K_{S}\supset K_{S+1}=0
and
dK_{j}\subset K_{j}
for any
j=0,\ldots,S
. Then there exists a direct sum decomposition

K=\mathcal{T}\oplus d\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P},

such that
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
is a subcomplex,
dK_{j}\subset d\mathcal{T}+K_{j+1}
for any
j=0,\ldots,S
, and the map

(\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j})/K_{j+1}\to K_{j}/K_{j+1}\ ,\quad\alpha\bmod K_{j+1}\mapsto d\alpha\bmod K_{j+1}\

is injective. Furthermore,
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
is isomorphic to
(K,d)
at the cohomology level.
Finally, if
K=\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\oplus d\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
is another such decomposition, then
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
and
(\tilde{\mathcal{P}},d|_{\tilde{\mathcal{P}}})
are isomorphic as cochain complexes.

Another cohomological machinery that arises when considering filtered complexes is that of spectral sequences. In this paper, we prove that a filtered cochain complex
(K,d)
of finite depth is a multicomplex, and as such it has further properties that allow us to describe each page of their spectral sequence explicitly. In this way, we are also able to clearly state the relationship between the the subcomplexes
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
that we introduce and the spectral sequence differential operators and quotients. In this sense, one can think of such subcomplexes as arising “naturally” from the filtration given.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Section

(K,d)

(K,d)

2.
Subcomplexes of general filtered complexes

2.1.
Base differential of filtered complexes

In this section, we present a general approach to finding subcomplexes of any filtered complex, which are isomorphic to the original complex at the level of cohomology. Our ideas are built on the work of graded forms presented by Rumin in
[
32
]
and
[
34
]
, and later used in
[
16
]
.

Let
(K,d)
be a cochain complex with differential operator
d
. Assume that we have a sequence of sub-complexes of finite depth, that is

(2.1)

K=K_{0}\supset K_{1}\supset K_{2}\supset\cdots\supset K_{S}\supset K_{S+1}=0\text{ with }dK_{j}\subset K_{j}\,.

We introduce the following definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a linear operator
d_{0}:K\to K
is
a base differential
of the filtered complex
(K,d)
if

(i)

d^{2}_{0}=0
,

(ii)

If
\alpha\in K_{j}
, then
(d-d_{0})\alpha\in K_{j+1}
,

Definition 2.2
.

Let
d_{0}:K\to K
be a base differential of
(K,d)
.

(i)

We say that a subspace
\mathcal{T}\subseteq K
is a base transversal for
d_{0}
if it satisfies

K=\ker d_{0}\oplus\mathcal{T}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j}=\{\alpha\in K_{j}\,:\,d_{0}\alpha\in K_{j}\text{
with
}d_{0}\alpha\neq 0\}\,.

(ii)

We say that a subspace
\mathcal{C}\subseteq K
is a base cotransversal for
d_{0}
with transversal
\mathcal{T}
if it satisfies

K=\image d_{0}\oplus\mathcal{C}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}\ .

Observe that by definition
d_{0}K_{j}\subset K_{j}
. Given a choice for a base differential
d_{0}
, one can always construct a corresponding base transversal
\mathcal{T}
and cotransversal
\mathcal{C}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Any filtered complex of finite depth
(K,d)
admits a base differential
d_{0}\colon K\to K
together with a base cotransversal and transversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Let us consider a filtered complex of finite depth as in (
2.1
). Since any vector subspace admits a complement, we can write

(2.2)

\displaystyle K_{j}=K_{(j)}\oplus K_{j+1}=K_{(j)}\oplus K_{(j+1)}\oplus K_{j+2}=\oplus_{k=0}^{S-j}K_{(j+k)}\,.

Using this direct sum decomposition, since
dK_{j}\subset K_{j}
, the action of the exterior differential
d
can be expressed as
d=d_{(0)}+d_{(1)}+\cdots+d_{(S)}
.
Moreover, since
(K,d)
is a complex, we have

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle d^{2}=\big(d_{(0)}+d_{(1)}+\cdots+d_{(S)}\big)\big(d_{(0)}+d_{(1)}+\cdots+d_{(S)}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{d_{(0)}^{2}}_{\in K_{(j)}}+\underbrace{d_{(0)}d_{(1)}+d_{(1)}d_{(0)}}_{\in K_{(j+1)}}+\underbrace{d_{(0)}d_{(2)}+d_{(1)}^{2}+d_{(2)}d_{(0)}}_{\in K_{(j+2)}}+\cdots+\underbrace{d_{(S)}^{2}}_{\in K_{(j+2S)}}

which, in particular, implies
d_{(0)}^{2}=0
and
(d-d_{(0)})K_{j}\subset K_{j+1}
. We can then define
d_{0}:=d_{(0)}
.

Since
d_{0}K_{(j)}\subset K_{(j)}
, we can find a complement
\mathcal{T}_{(j)}
to
\ker d_{0}\cap K_{(j)}
in
K_{(j)}
, as well as a complement
\mathcal{A}_{(j)}
to
d_{0}K_{(j)}
in
\ker d_{0}\cap K_{(j)}
. The subspaces of
K
given by

\mathcal{T}=\oplus_{j=0}^{S}\mathcal{T}_{(j)}\ ,\ \mathcal{C}=\mathcal{T}\oplus(\oplus_{j=0}^{S}\mathcal{A}_{(j)})\ \text{ with }\ \mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}

will be respectively a base transversal and base cotransversal to the base differential
d_{0}
.
∎

Once we have a base differential
d_{0}
and a choice for
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, we can define a partial inverse of the base differential as follows.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
d_{0}\colon K\to K
be a base differential of
(K,d)
, and let
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
be a base transversal and cotransversal for
d_{0}
. Then we can define the
inverse
map
d_{0}^{-1}\colon K\to K
of
d_{0}
as follows

(a)

\ker d^{-1}_{0}=\mathcal{C}
,

(b)

if
\alpha\in\image d_{0}
, then
d_{0}^{-1}\alpha
is the unique element in
\mathcal{T}
mapped to
\alpha
by
d_{0}
.

By construction
\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j}=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{T}\,:\,d_{0}\alpha\in K_{j}\text{ with }d_{0}\alpha\neq 0\}
, and so
d_{0}^{-1}K_{j}\subset K_{j}
. Moreover, by requiring
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, it follows that
(d_{0}^{-1})^{2}=0
. With these definitions in place, we are ready to produce the subcomplex
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
corresponding to the base differential
d_{0}
and the relative base transversal and cotransversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
.

We introduce the operator

P=\id-d_{0}^{-1}d-dd_{0}^{-1}.

Given
S
as in (
2.1
), we introduce the notation
P^{\infty}:=P^{S}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Given a base differential
d_{0}\colon K\to K
of
(K,d)
with base transversal and cotransversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, we define
\mathcal{P}\subset K
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}:=\image P^{\infty}\subset K\,.

Moreover, since
dP=Pd
, we have that
(\mathcal{P},d)
is a subcomplex of
(K,d)
and we will call this
the base subcomplex
of
d_{0}
.

Since the construction of the operator
P
depends not only on the base differential
d_{0}
, but also on the choice of base transversal and cotransversal for
d_{0}
, one should write
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{d_{0},\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}}
. However, in order to avoid cumbersome notation, once the map
d_{0}\colon K\to K
and the subspaces
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
have been fixed, we will simply write
\mathcal{P}=\image P^{\infty}\subset K
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{P},d)
be the base subcomplex of
d_{0}\colon K\to K
with transversal and cotransversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
.

(a)

If
\alpha\in K
is a closed form, then
P^{\infty}\alpha
is in the same cohomology class. In particular,

P^{\infty}:K\to\mathcal{P}

is an isomorphism at the cohomology level.

(b)

The surjection between the two complexes

K\to K/(\mathcal{T}+d\mathcal{T})

is an isomorphism at the cohomology level. Furthermore, one can write

K=\mathcal{T}\oplus d\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P}\ \ \text{with}\ \ker P^{\infty}=\mathcal{T}\oplus d\mathcal{T},

so the map

\alpha+\mathcal{T}+d\mathcal{T}\longmapsto P^{\infty}(\alpha)

is a well-defined isomorphism between the complexes
\big(K/(\mathcal{T}+d\mathcal{T}),d\big)
and
(\mathcal{P},d)
.

(c)

One can rewrite
\mathcal{P}\subset K
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in K\,:\,P(\alpha)=\alpha\}=\ker(dd_{0}^{-1}+d_{0}^{-1}d)=\ker d_{0}^{-1}\cap\ker(d_{0}^{-1}d)=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}\,:\,d\alpha\in\mathcal{C}\}\ .

In particular,
P^{k}=P^{\infty}
for any
k\geq S
.

Proof.

(a)

If
\alpha
is closed,
P\alpha=\alpha-dd_{0}^{-1}\alpha
. In other words,
\alpha
and
P\alpha
differ by an exact form. Applying this result iteratively, we have that
\alpha
and
P^{\infty}\alpha
are in the same cohomology class.

(b)

We will prove the first part of the statement by showing that there are no closed forms in
\mathcal{T}
. This observation follows from the fact that
\mathcal{T}=\image d_{0}^{-1}
, and so

d_{0}^{-1}d|_{\mathcal{T}}=\id|_{\mathcal{T}}+d_{0}^{-1}(d-d_{0})|_{\mathcal{T}},

which has inverse
\sum_{i=0}^{S}[(-d_{0}^{-1}(d-d_{0})|_{\mathcal{T}}]^{i}
. Hence,
\mathcal{T}
is transverse to
d\mathcal{T}
and
K\to K/(\mathcal{T}\oplus d\mathcal{T})
is an isomorphism at the level of cohomology. For the second part of the statement, we first observe that since
\mathcal{T}=\image d_{0}^{-1}
,

(2.3)

P(\alpha)=\alpha\mod\mathcal{T}+d\mathcal{T}.

Hence, if
P^{k}(\alpha)=0
, then we must have
\alpha\in\mathcal{T}+d\mathcal{T}
, giving us that
\ker P^{k}\subset\mathcal{T}+d\mathcal{T}
for any
k>0
. For the inclusion in the other direction, we first observe that for
\beta\in\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j}
, we have

P(\beta)=\beta-d_{0}^{-1}d\beta=-d_{0}^{-1}(d-d_{0})\beta\in\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j+1},

which also implies that
P(d\beta)=dP(\beta)\in d\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j+1}
. By the finiteness of the filtration, it follows that
P^{\infty}(\beta)=P^{\infty}(d\beta)=0
.

(c)

Introduce
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}=\{\alpha\in K\,:\,P(\alpha)=\alpha\}
. If
\alpha\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
, then
P^{\infty}(\alpha)=\alpha
by definition, so
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}\subset\mathcal{P}
. Conversely, if
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}
, then
\alpha=P^{\infty}(\beta)
, and furthermore, since
P(\beta)-\beta
is in
\mathcal{T}+d\mathcal{T}
by (
2.3
), we have

P(\alpha)-\alpha=P^{\infty}(P(\beta)-\beta)=0.

It follows that
P(\alpha)-\alpha=(dd_{0}^{-1}+d_{0}^{-1}d)\alpha=0
. We observe first that
dd_{0}^{-1}\alpha
is in
d\mathcal{T}
, while
d_{0}^{-1}d\alpha
is in
\mathcal{T}
and since these spaces are transverse, both
dd_{0}^{-1}\alpha
and
d_{0}^{-1}d\alpha
have to vanish. Furthermore, since there are no closed forms in
\mathcal{T}
, we must have
d_{0}^{-1}\alpha=0
and the result follows.

∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Given a base differential
d_{0}\colon K\to K
of
(K,d)
with transversal and cotransversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, we have the following direct sum decomposition for
K
:

K=\mathcal{T}\oplus d_{0}\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P}=\image d_{0}^{-1}\oplus\image d_{0}\oplus\mathcal{P}.

Finally,
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.6
(b), for any
\alpha\in K_{j}
, we have unique
\beta_{j},\gamma_{j}\in\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j}
,
\omega_{j}\in\mathcal{P}\cap K_{j}
, such that
\alpha=\beta_{j}+d\gamma_{j}+\omega_{j}
, and in particular we have that

\displaystyle\alpha=\beta_{j}+d_{0}\gamma_{j}+(d-d_{0})\gamma_{j}+\omega_{j}\ \text{ with }\ (d-d_{0})\gamma_{j}\in K_{j+1}\,.

Repeating this process on
(d-d_{0})\gamma_{j}
, we have that there exist unique
\beta_{j+1},\gamma_{j+1}\in\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j+1}
and
\omega_{j+1}\in\mathcal{P}\cap K_{j+1}
such that

\displaystyle\alpha=

\displaystyle\beta_{j}+d_{0}\gamma_{j}+\beta_{j+1}+d\gamma_{j+1}+\omega_{j}+\omega_{j+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta_{j}+\beta_{j+1}+d_{0}(\gamma_{j}+\gamma_{j+1})+(d-d_{0})\gamma_{j+1}+\omega_{j}+\omega_{j+1}\ ,\text{
with
}(d-d_{0})\gamma_{j+1}\in K_{j+2}\,.

Proceeding iteratively and using the fact that our filtered complex has finite length gives us the result.

By Theorem
2.6
(c), we have that
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{C}
, hence
\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{C}
, and the equality follows from the fact that both
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{P}
are transversal to
\image d_{0}=d_{0}\mathcal{T}
.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

Theorem
2.6
(b) and Corollary
2.7
imply that for any element
\alpha\in K
, there exists a unique way of expressing
\alpha
in terms of elements
\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{T}
and
\omega\in\mathcal{P}
.
Using the partial inverse
d_{0}^{-1}\colon K\to K
, one can also obtain an explicit formulation of these elements in terms of the given
\alpha\in K
.

(a)

For
\alpha\in K
given as
\alpha=\beta+d\gamma+\omega\in K
, with
\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{T}
,
\omega\in\mathcal{P}
, then

\omega=P^{\infty}(\alpha)\ ,\ \gamma=\sum_{i=0}^{S}[-d_{0}^{-1}(d-d_{0})]^{i}d^{-1}_{0}\alpha\ ,\ \beta=\alpha-d\gamma-\omega\ .

This follows from the fact that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P}
, so

d_{0}^{-1}\alpha=d_{0}^{-1}d\gamma=\gamma+d_{0}^{-1}(d-d_{0})\gamma\ \Longrightarrow\ \gamma=\sum_{i=0}^{S}[-d_{0}^{-1}(d-d_{0})]^{i}d^{-1}_{0}\alpha\,.

(b)

For
\alpha\in K
given as
\alpha=\beta+d_{0}\gamma+\omega\in K
, with
\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{T}
,
\omega\in\mathcal{P}
, then
\gamma=d_{0}^{-1}d_{0}\alpha
, and so

\omega=P^{\infty}(\alpha-d_{0}\gamma),\qquad\beta=(\id-P^{\infty})(\alpha-d_{0}\gamma)\ .

(c)

Observe that by Theorem
2.6
(c), there is a unique element
\beta
in each equivalence class
\alpha+\mathcal{T}+d\mathcal{T}
satisfying

d^{-1}_{0}\beta=0\quad\text{and}\quad d^{-1}_{0}d\beta=0\ .

(d)

Moreover, the subcomplex
(\mathcal{P},d)
is itself a filtered complex

\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{0}\supset\mathcal{P}_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{P}_{S}\supset\mathcal{P}_{S+1}=0\ \text{ with }\ \mathcal{P}_{j}=\mathcal{P}\cap K_{j}\ .

2.2.
Analogue of Rumin forms and Hodge decomposition

Given a base differential
d_{0}
of a filtered complex
(K,d)
with relative base transversal and cotransversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, let us introduce the subspace

\mathcal{E}_{0}=\ker d_{0}\cap\ker d_{0}^{-1}=\ker d_{0}\cap\mathcal{C}\subset K\ .

From the definition of
\mathcal{E}_{0}
, we readily get a direct sum decomposition of
\ker d_{0}
as
\ker d_{0}=\image d_{0}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{0}
. Moreover, the map

\Pi_{0}=\id-d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}-d_{0}^{-1}d_{0},

is a projection onto
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and

K=(\image d_{0}^{-1})\oplus(\image d_{0})\oplus\mathcal{E}_{0}=\mathcal{T}\oplus d_{0}\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{0}\ .

Proposition 2.9
.

The map
\Pi_{0}|_{\mathcal{P}}:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{E}_{0}
is a bijection with inverse
P^{\infty}|_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}:\mathcal{E}_{0}\to\mathcal{P}
. It follows that, if we define
d_{c}:\mathcal{E}_{0}\to\mathcal{E}_{0}
as

d_{c}\alpha=\Pi_{0}(P^{\infty}(d\alpha))=\Pi_{0}(dP^{\infty}(\alpha))\ ,

then
(\mathcal{E}_{0},d_{c})
and
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
are isomorphic as differential complexes. If we introduce a filtration on
\mathcal{E}_{0}
by imposing
\mathcal{E}_{0,j}=\mathcal{E}_{0}\cap K_{j}
, then
(\mathcal{E}_{0},d_{c})
and
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
are isomorphic as filtered complexes.

The notation
(\mathcal{E}_{0},d_{c})
is used to reflect Rumin’s notation in
[
34
]
.

Proof.

We observe that if
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}=\{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}\,:\,d\alpha\in\mathcal{C}\}
, then

P^{\infty}(\Pi_{0}(\alpha))=P^{\infty}(\alpha-d^{-1}_{0}d_{0}\alpha)=\alpha,

since
d_{0}^{-1}d_{0}\alpha\in\mathcal{T}
. On the other hand, if
\alpha\in\mathcal{E}_{0}
then

P^{\infty}(\alpha)=(\id-d_{0}^{-1}d)^{S}\alpha=\alpha\bmod\image d_{0}^{-1},

so
\Pi_{0}(P^{\infty}\alpha)=\alpha
. Observe that both
\Pi_{0}
and
P^{\infty}
preserve the filtration. It follows that if
d_{c}\alpha=\Pi_{0}(dP^{\infty}(\alpha))
then
(\mathcal{E}_{0},d_{c})
and
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
are by definition isomorphic complexes.
∎

We emphasize the following special case. Assume that we have a filtered complex
(K,d)
as in (
2.1
) with a Hilbert space structure, such that every subcomplex
K_{j}
is closed in
K
. We can then use the construction in the proof of Proposition
2.3
, to define
K_{j}=K_{(j)}\oplus K_{j+1}
where
K_{(j)}
is the orthogonal complement of
K_{(j+1)}
in
K_{j}
. We again define
d_{0}
as the linear operator such that for
\alpha\in K_{(j)}
, we have
d_{0}\alpha
as the projection of
d\alpha
to
K_{(j)}
. We make the final assumption that
\ker d_{0}
and
\image d_{0}
are both closed subspaces. Then one can take
\mathcal{T}=(\ker d_{0})^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{C}=(\image d_{0})^{\perp}
as base transversal and cotransversal for
d_{0}
. It is also possible to consider the formal adjoint
d_{0}^{*}
of
d_{0}
with respect to the inner product of such Hilbert space. We can then write a Hodge-type decomposition

K=\mathcal{T}\oplus d_{0}\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{0}=\image(d_{0}^{*})\oplus\image(d_{0})\oplus\ker(\Box_{0})\ ,\text{ with }\Box_{0}=d_{0}d_{0}^{*}+d_{0}^{*}d_{0}\ ,

and the subcomplex
(\ker\Box_{0},d_{c})
is isomorphic to
(K,d)
at the cohomology level (see also
[
16
]
and
[
18
]
).

2.3.
Invariance under choice of transversal and cotransversal

Let
d_{0}
be a base differential of the filtered cochain complex
(K,d)
, with base transversal and cotransversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
be a different choice of transversal and cotransversal for the same base differential
d_{0}
, and define the subcomplexes
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{d_{0},\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}=\mathcal{P}_{d_{0},\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}
relative to these two different choices. The following result holds.

Proposition 2.10
.

If
P^{\infty}=P^{S}
is defined with respect to the base transversal and cotransversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, then
P^{\infty}\circ\tilde{\Pi}_{0}|_{\tilde{\mathcal{P}}}
is an isomorphism between
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{P}
as filtered differential complexes.

Proof.

Let
d_{0}^{-1}
and
\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}
be the partial inverses of the base differential
d_{0}
, constructed using to
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
respectively.
We introduce
\Pi_{0}=\id-d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}-d_{0}^{-1}d_{0}
and
\tilde{\Pi}_{0}=\id-d_{0}\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}-\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}d_{0}
as the relative projections onto
\mathcal{E}_{0}=\ker d_{0}\cap\mathcal{C}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{0}=\ker d_{0}\cap\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
. We first see that, for any
\alpha\in\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{0}

\tilde{\Pi}_{0}(\Pi_{0}(\alpha))=\tilde{\Pi}_{0}(\alpha-d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}\alpha)=\alpha\ .

We have similar relations with
\Pi_{0}
and
\tilde{\Pi}_{0}
reversed. Hence, the following sequence of invertible maps

\tilde{\mathcal{P}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{\Pi}_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\tilde{\mathcal{E}}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Pi_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{E}_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle P^{\infty}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{P},

are all linear isomorphisms that preserve the filtration induced by the filtration (
2.1
) on
K
.

To conclude, it is sufficient to observe that for any
\alpha\in\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
, we have

\displaystyle P^{\infty}(\Pi_{0}(\tilde{\Pi}_{0}(\alpha)))=

\displaystyle P^{\infty}(\Pi_{0}(\alpha-\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}d_{0}\alpha))=P^{\infty}(\alpha-\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}d_{0}\alpha-d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}(\alpha-\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}d_{0}\alpha))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{\infty}(\alpha-\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}d_{0}\alpha)=P^{\infty}\circ\tilde{\Pi}_{0}(\alpha)\ .

∎

2.4.
Invariance under choice of base differential

We now look into the base differentials themselves. Let
d_{0}
be a base differential for a filtered complex
(K,d)
, with base transversal and cotransversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, and with
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{d_{0},\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}}
. Let
\tilde{d}_{0}
be a different choice of base differential of the same filtered complex
(K,d)
, with similar associated objects
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
. We then have the following result

Proposition 2.11
.

(a)

\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
are also base transversals and cotransversals for
\tilde{d}_{0}
.

(b)

If
P^{\infty}=P^{S}
is defined with respect to
d_{0}
and
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, then
P^{\infty}\circ\tilde{\Pi}_{0}|_{\tilde{\mathcal{P}}}
is an isomorphism of differential complexes between
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

(a)

Let us introduce the notation
\tilde{d}_{0}=d_{0}-N
, where the map
N=(d-\tilde{d}_{0})-(d-d_{0})
maps
K_{j}
into
K_{j+1}
. It follows that
\hat{N}:=d_{0}^{-1}N
is nilpotent with
\hat{N}^{S+1}=0
, meaning that
A:=\id-\hat{N}
is invertible. If we consider the projection map
d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}
applied to the image of
\tilde{d}_{0}
, we have that for any
\alpha\in K

\displaystyle d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}(\tilde{d}_{0}\alpha)=d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}(d_{0}\alpha-N\alpha)=d_{0}\alpha-d_{0}\hat{N}\alpha=d_{0}(\alpha-\hat{N}\alpha)

and so we get

\displaystyle d_{0}d^{-1}_{0}|_{\image(\tilde{d}_{0})}

\displaystyle:\image\tilde{d}_{0}\to\image d_{0}\ ,\ \tilde{d}_{0}\alpha\longmapsto d_{0}A\alpha\ .

The map given by the composition

\mathcal{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle A^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tilde{d}_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\tilde{d}_{0}\mathcal{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\image d_{0}\ ,

simplifies into
d_{0}\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathrm{image\,}d_{0}
, since for any
\alpha\in K
we have
d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}\tilde{d}_{0}A^{-1}\alpha=d_{0}AA^{-1}\alpha=d_{0}\alpha
. Since
d_{0}
is an invertible map from
\mathcal{T}
to
\image d_{0}
, this implies that
\tilde{d}_{0}|_{\mathcal{T}}
must be invertible as well.
Furthermore, by definition of base transversal for
d_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j}=

\displaystyle\{\alpha\in K_{j}:d_{0}\alpha\in K_{j}\text{ with }d_{0}\alpha\neq 0\}=\{\alpha\in K_{j}:d_{0}\alpha=d_{0}d_{0}^{-1}\tilde{d}_{0}A^{-1}\alpha\in K_{j}\text{ with }d_{0}\alpha\neq 0\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\alpha\in K_{j}:\tilde{d}_{0}A^{-1}\alpha\in K_{j}\text{ with
}\tilde{d}_{0}A^{-1}\alpha\neq 0\}=\{\alpha=A\beta\in K_{j}:\tilde{d}_{0}\beta\in K_{j}\text{ with $\tilde{d}_{0}\beta\neq 0\}$}

In other words,
\{\beta\in K_{j}:\tilde{d}_{0}\beta\in K_{j}\text{ with }\tilde{d}_{0}\beta\neq 0\}=A^{-1}(\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j})=\mathcal{T}\cap K_{j}
, and so
\mathcal{T}
is a base transversal for
\tilde{d}_{0}
as well. Finally, by construction we have
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
. Moreover the map

K\to\image\tilde{d}_{0},\qquad\alpha\mapsto\tilde{d}_{0}A^{-1}d_{0}^{-1}\alpha,

will be a projection onto
\image\tilde{d}_{0}
with kernel
\ker d_{0}^{-1}=\mathcal{C}
, implying that
\mathcal{C}
is transverse to
\image\tilde{d}_{0}
.

(b)

By (a) and Proposition
2.10
, it is sufficient to prove the result in the case where
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
equals
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
.
Continuing with the notation in (a), we observe that
\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}=A^{-1}d_{0}^{-1}
. Furthermore, if
\tilde{P}=\id-\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}d-d\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}
, then

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle=\{\tilde{P}(\alpha)=\alpha\,:\,\alpha\in K\}=\{\alpha\in K\,:\,\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}\alpha=0,\tilde{d}_{0}^{-1}d\alpha=0\}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in K\,:\,A^{-1}d_{0}^{-1}\alpha=0,A^{-1}d_{0}^{-1}d\alpha=0\}=\mathcal{P}

since
A^{-1}
is an invertible map of
\mathcal{T}
to itself. The result follows.

∎

2.5.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The existence of a splitting
K=\mathcal{T}\oplus d\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P}
follows from the existence of a base differential
d_{0}
of
(K,d)
as shown in Proposition
2.3
. The property
dK_{j}\subset d\mathcal{T}+K_{j+1}
is a direct consequence of the fact that for any
\alpha\in K_{j}

\displaystyle d\alpha

\displaystyle=d_{0}\alpha\bmod K_{j+1}=d_{0}\beta\bmod K_{j+1}=d\beta\bmod K_{j+1}\ ,\text{ where }\beta=d^{-1}_{0}d_{0}\alpha\in\mathcal{T}\ .

Conversely, assume that we have a decomposition
K=\mathcal{T}\oplus d\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P}
as described in Theorem
1.1
, corresponding to a base differential. Using the direct sum decomposition
K_{j}=\oplus_{i=0}^{S-j}K_{(j+i)}
presented in (
2.2
) and the fact that
dK_{j}\subset K_{j}
, the action of the exterior differential
d
can be expressed as
d=d_{(0)}+d_{(1)}+\cdots+d_{(S)}
.
Then by our assumptions on
\mathcal{T}
, for any
\alpha\in K_{j}
there exists a unique
\beta\in\mathcal{T}
such that

d\alpha=d_{(0)}\alpha\bmod K_{j+1}=d\beta\bmod K_{j+1}=d_{(0)}\beta\bmod K_{j+1}.

Hence,
\mathcal{T}
is a base transversal for the base differential
d_{(0)}
. By repeating the same reasoning of Corollary
2.7
, we get that
K=\mathcal{T}\oplus d_{(0)}\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P}
, and so
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{T}\oplus\mathcal{P}
gives a base cotransversal for
d_{(0)}
.

Once we define a partial inverse map
d_{(0)}^{-1}\colon\mathrm{image}\,d_{(0)}\to\mathcal{T}
, the map
P^{\infty}=(\id-d_{(0)}^{-1}d-dd_{(0)}^{-1})^{S}\colon K\to\mathcal{P}
gives us an isomorphism at the level of cohomology. Finally, the subcomplex
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
we have obtained in this way is isomorphic to any other base complex by Proposition
2.11
.

3.
Examples

We consider the following examples of filtered complexes
(K,d)
and their relative base complexes
(\mathcal{P},d)
.

3.1.
Manifolds with a negative filtration of the tangent bundle

Let
M
be a connected manifold. Assume that we have a filtration on the tangent bundle

TM=TM_{-s}\supset TM_{-s+1}\supset\cdots\supset TM_{-2}\supset TM_{-1}\supset TM_{0}=0\,,

where we are assuming
TM_{-s-k}=TM_{-s}=TM
for any
k\geq 0
.
This gives us a filtration

\bigwedge TM=(\bigwedge TM)_{-S}\supset(\bigwedge TM)_{-S+1}\supset\cdots\supset(\bigwedge TM)_{-1}\supset(\bigwedge TM)_{0}\supset 0\ ,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\text{where }S=\sum_{j=1}^{s}j\left(\rank(TM)_{-j}-\rank(TM)_{-j+1}\right)\,,

defined by imposing
\chi_{1}\wedge\chi_{2}\in(\bigwedge TM)_{-i-j}
, if
\chi_{1}\in(\bigwedge TM)_{-i}
and
\chi_{2}\in(\bigwedge TM)_{-j}
. We remark that from this convention
\mathbb{R}=\bigwedge^{0}TM
is in
(\bigwedge TM)_{0}
, but not in
(\bigwedge TM)_{-1}
.

By duality, we introduce
(\bigwedge T^{\ast}M)_{j}
defined as the subspace of the elements in
\bigwedge T^{*}M
that vanish on
(\bigwedge TM)_{-j+1}
, for any
j\geq 1
. We will denote by
\Omega_{j}
the space of all smooth sections of
(\bigwedge T^{\ast}M)_{j}
. Just like in the case of the filtration on
\bigwedge TM
,
\Omega_{0}
is the space of all smooth forms, so that
0
-forms
\Omega^{0}=C^{\infty}(M)
will belong to
\Omega_{0}
but not
\Omega_{1}
.

It is worth mentioning that, by definition, the filtration on
\bigwedge TM
gives rise to a dual decreasing filtration on
\bigwedge T^{*}M

\displaystyle\bigwedge T^{*}M=(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{0}\supset(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{1}\supset\cdots\supset(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{S}\supset(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{S+1}=0\,,

where
S
is the index given in (
3.1
). This filtration then naturally extends to the space of smooth forms as

\displaystyle\Omega=\Omega_{0}\supset\Omega_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\Omega_{S}\supset\Omega_{S+1}=0\,.

We introduce the following crucial assumption on our filtration:

(A)

\text{if }X\in\mathfrak{X}_{-i}\text{ and }Y\in\mathfrak{X}_{-j}\ ,\text{ then }[X,Y]\in\mathfrak{X}_{-i-j}

where we denote by
\mathfrak{X}_{-j}
the space of smooth sections of
TM_{-j}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

If (
A
) holds, then for
j=0,1,\dots,S
, we have
d\Omega_{j}\subset\Omega_{j}
.

Proof.

For
j=0
, the claim is trivial. Let
\alpha\in\Omega_{j}
be a
k
-form with
j\geq 1
and prove that also
d\alpha\in\Omega_{j}
.
For any
k+1
smooth vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1}
on
M
, the formula for the exterior differential on
\alpha
is given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle d\alpha(X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{k+1})=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\leq k+1}(-1)^{i-1}X_{i}\big(\alpha(X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{X}_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{k+1})\big)+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<l\leq k+1}(-1)^{i+l}\alpha\big([X_{i},X_{l}]\wedge X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{X}_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{X}_{l}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{k+1}\big)\,,

where the hat-symbol is used to indicate the terms omitted.

If
X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{X}_{-j+1}
, then for any
i=1,\ldots,k+1
, we have
X_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}_{-n_{i}}
with
n_{i}\geq 1
, and so
X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{X}_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{X}_{-m_{i}}
with
-m_{i}-n_{i}\geq-j+1
. This readily implies that
-m_{i}\geq-j+2
, that is
\mathfrak{X}_{-m_{i}}\subset\mathfrak{X}_{-j+1}
, and so the first addend of (
3.2
) vanishes, since by assumption
\alpha\in\Omega_{j}
.

Likewise, if
X_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}_{-n_{i}}
and
X_{l}\in\mathfrak{X}_{-n_{l}}
with both
n_{i},n_{l}\geq 1
, then the
(k-1)
-vector field
X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{X}_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{X}_{l}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{k+1}
will belong to
\mathfrak{X}_{-m_{i,l}}
with
-m_{i,l}\geq-j+1+n_{i}+n_{l}
. By assumption (
A
) we have that
[X_{i},X_{l}]\in\mathfrak{X}_{-n_{i}-n_{l}}
, and so

[X_{i},X_{l}]\wedge X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{X}_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge\hat{X}_{l}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{k+1}\in\mathfrak{X}_{-m_{i,l}-n_{i}-n_{l}}\subset\mathfrak{X}_{-j+1}

since
-m_{i,l}-n_{i}-n_{l}\geq-j+1+n_{i}+n_{l}-n_{i}-n_{l}\geq-j+1
, which implies that also the second addend of (
3.2
) vanishes, which concludes the proof.
∎

Given the decreasing filtration over the space of smooth forms, it is possible to express
\Omega
as a direct sum decomposition
\Omega=\Omega_{(0)}\oplus\cdots\oplus\Omega_{S}
, where the subspaces
\Omega_{(j)}
are taken so that
\Omega_{j}=\Omega_{(j)}\oplus\Omega_{j+1}
. We observe that if
\alpha\in\Omega_{j}
, then

d(f\alpha)=fd\alpha\mod\Omega_{j+1}.

Hence, we can define a
C^{\infty}(M)
-linear map
d_{0}\colon\Omega_{j}\to\Omega_{j}
such that, for any
\alpha\in\Omega_{(j)}
,

d_{0}\alpha:=d\alpha\mod\Omega_{j+1}\ \text{ so that }\ d_{0}\alpha\in\Omega_{(j)}\,,\text{ and }\ (d-d_{0})\alpha\in\Omega_{j+1}\ .

By tensoriality, the map
d_{0}\colon\Omega\to\Omega
induces a vector bundle morphism
\check{d}_{0}\colon\bigwedge T^{*}M\to\bigwedge T^{*}M
for which
\check{d}_{0}^{2}=0
. Moreover, we have that for any
\chi\in(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{j}
,
(\check{d}-\check{d}_{0})\chi\in(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{j+1}
, where
\check{d}\colon\bigwedge T^{*}M\to\bigwedge T^{*}M
is the vector bundle morphism induced by the exterior derivative
d\colon\Omega\to\Omega
.

The map
\check{d}_{0}
is therefore a base differential of the complex
(\bigwedge T^{*}M,\check{d})
, for which it is possible to construct
\check{\mathcal{T}}\subset\check{\mathcal{C}}\subset\bigwedge T^{*}M
a base transversal and cotransversal so that

\displaystyle\bigwedge T^{*}M=\ker\check{d}_{0}\oplus\check{\mathcal{T}}=\mathrm{image\,}\check{d}_{0}\oplus\check{\mathcal{C}}\,,\,\check{\mathcal{T}}\cap(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{j}=\{\chi\in(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{j}:\check{d}_{0}\chi\in(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{j}\text{ with }\check{d}_{0}\chi\neq 0\}.

It is therefore sufficient to take the spaces of smooth sections
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
of
\check{\mathcal{T}}\subset\check{\mathcal{C}}
to obtain a base transversal and cotransversal for
d_{0}\colon\Omega\to\Omega
. Indeed, the map
d_{0}\colon\Omega\to\Omega
is a base differential for the de Rham complex
(\Omega,d)
, since for any
\alpha\in\Omega_{j}
, we have
(d-d_{0})\alpha=(d-\check{d}_{0})\alpha\in\Omega_{j+1}
.

These definitions lead to well-defined base complexes
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})
as in Theorem
1.1
, and
(\mathcal{E}_{0},d_{c})
as in subsection
2.2
. In particular, the latter subcomplex coincides with the complex defined by Rumin in
[
32
]
and
[
34
]
, where

\bullet

grading of the tangent bundle is defined so that
TM_{-j-1}
is spanned by
TM_{-j}
brackets of vector fields with one taking value in
TM_{-j}
and the other in
TM_{-1}
;

\bullet

the subspaces
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{C}
are defined by taking the orthogonal complements with respect to an auxiliary Riemannian metric on
M
of
\ker d_{0}
and
\mathrm{image\,}d_{0}
respectively.

3.2.
Compact Riemannian manifolds and Hodge decomposition

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannianian manifold, and let
(\Omega,d)
be the de Rham complex of smooth differential forms. By the Hodge decomposition theorem, we have

(3.3)

\Omega=\image d\oplus\image d^{*}\oplus\mathcal{H},

where
\mathcal{H}=\ker(dd^{*}+d^{*}d)
is the space of harmonic forms, that is forms in the kernel of the Hodge-Laplacian, and
d^{*}\colon\Omega\to\Omega
denotes the codifferential.

Let us take the trivial filtration over the space of smooth forms,
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\supset\Omega_{1}=0
. In this case, we have that
d_{0}=d
, since
d_{0}\alpha=d\alpha\mod\Omega_{0}=d\alpha
for any
\alpha\in\Omega_{0}
. If we take
\mathcal{T}=\image d^{*}
and
\mathcal{C}=\image d^{*}\oplus\mathcal{H}
, then
P^{\infty}=P=\mathrm{id}-d^{*}d-dd^{*}
is the projection onto
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{H}
. In the resulting base complex
(\mathcal{P},d|_{\mathcal{P}})=(\mathcal{H},d|_{\mathcal{H}})
, the differential
d|_{\mathcal{H}}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is the trivial map, and so Theorem
1.1
implies that each cohomology class is uniquely represented by a harmonic form, as expected.

On non-compact manifolds, one can extend such construction by considering
L^{p}
-forms and obtain a similar result in the cases where we have an
L^{p}
-Hodge decomposition. See
[
11
,
12
,
26
,
14
]
for details on when such a decomposition exists.

3.3.
Compact Riemannian manifolds with non-positive filtration of the tangent bundle

We can combine the approaches of Section
3.1
and Section
3.2
to consider non-positive filtrations.
Consider a compact Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
of dimension
n
and assume we have a filtration of the tangent bundle
TM=TM_{-s}\supset TM_{-s+1}\supset\cdots\supset TM_{-1}\supset TM_{0}\supset 0
. We still require that assumption (
A
) holds for the vector fields on
M
.

Using the Riemannian metric, one can now express
TM
as a direct sum decomposition by defining orthogonal complements, that is
TM_{-j}=TM_{(-j)}\oplus_{\perp}TM_{-j+1}
for
j=-1,0,\dots,s
, so that

\displaystyle TM=TM_{(-s)}\oplus_{\perp}TM_{(-s+1)}\oplus_{\perp}\cdots\oplus_{\perp}TM_{(-1)}\oplus_{\perp}TM_{0}\,.

This orthogonal decomposition naturally extends to the space of smooth vector fields on one hand

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}=\mathfrak{X}_{(-s)}\oplus_{\perp}\mathfrak{X}_{(-s+1)}\oplus_{\perp}\cdots\oplus_{\perp}\mathfrak{X}_{(-1)}\oplus_{\perp}\mathfrak{X}_{(0)}\,,

and to the exterior algebra
\bigwedge TM
on the other

\displaystyle\bigwedge TM=(\bigwedge TM)_{(-S)}\oplus_{\perp}(\bigwedge TM)_{(-S+1)}\oplus_{\perp}\cdots\oplus_{\perp}(\bigwedge TM)_{(-1)}\oplus_{\perp}(\bigwedge TM)_{(0)}\,,

by imposing that if
X_{i}\in TM_{(-j_{i})}
then
X_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge X_{k}\in(\bigwedge TM)_{(-\sigma)}
where
\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{k}j_{i}
.

By repeating the same reasoning as in subsection
3.1
, by duality we obtain a decreasing filtration on
\bigwedge T^{*}M
and
\Omega
. In this particular case, one can define
(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{(j)}
as those elements in
\bigwedge T^{*}M
that vanish on
(\bigwedge TM)_{(-r)}
for any
r\neq j
, and then extend it to the space of smooth forms by imposing
\Omega_{(j)}
to be the space of smooth sections of
(\bigwedge T^{*}M)_{(j)}
.
As a result, we obtain an
L^{2}
-orthogonal decomposition of the space of smooth forms

\Omega=\Omega_{(0)}\oplus_{\perp}\Omega_{(1)}\oplus_{\perp}\cdots\oplus_{\perp}\Omega_{(S-1)}\oplus_{\perp}\Omega_{(S)}\,,

where
S=\sum_{j=1}^{s}j(\rank\Omega_{(j)})
coincides with the index (
3.1
).

Since assumption (
A
) still holds, we have that
d(\oplus_{k\geq j}\Omega_{(k)})\subset\oplus_{k\geq j}\Omega_{(k)}
(see Lemma
3.1
), and we can therefore express the exterior differential in terms of its action with respect to this orthogonal decomposition, that is
d=d_{(0)}+\cdots+d_{(s)}
, where
d_{(r)}(\Omega_{(j)})\subset\Omega_{(j+r)}
.

Once we define
d_{0}=d_{(0)}
, we readily obtain that it is a base differential of the de Rham complex
(\Omega,d)
(see also the proof of Proposition
2.3
). If we also take its formal adjoint
d_{0}^{*}=d_{(0)}^{*}
and consider

\mathcal{T}=(\ker d_{0})^{\perp}=\image d_{0}^{*},\qquad\mathcal{C}=(\image d_{0})^{\perp}=\ker d_{0}^{*}\,,

we obtain a base transversal and cotransversal of
d_{0}
, since
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
. Observe that now
d_{0}
and
d_{0}^{-1}
are no longer tensorial operators.

Similarly to what was mentioned in Section
3.2
, we can extend the same construction to
L^{p}
-forms on non-compact manifolds, provided that the exterior differential operator and its adjoint are closed operators on
L^{p}
-forms. Again, we refer to
[
19
, Section 1.4]
for conditions for when such requirements are satisfied.

3.4.
Forms vanishing at a point

Let
(M,\nabla)
be an affine manifold of dimension
n
and let
o\in M
be a fixed point. For
i
vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}
, we define the
i
-th covariant derivative iteratively by imposing
\nabla_{X_{1}}^{1}=\nabla_{X_{1}}
and

\nabla_{X_{1},\dots,X_{i}}^{i}=\nabla_{X_{1}}\nabla_{X_{2},\dots,X_{i}}^{i-1}-\nabla_{\nabla_{X_{1}}X_{2},\dots,X_{i}}^{i-1}-\cdots-\nabla_{X_{2},\dots,\nabla_{X_{1}}X_{i}}^{i-1}\,\text{ for }i\geq 2\,.

We note that the operator
\nabla_{X_{1},\dots,X_{i}}^{i}
is tensorial in all arguments.

Let us define the filtration
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\supset\Omega_{1}\supset\Omega_{2}\supset\cdots
over the space of smooth forms, where
\Omega_{j}
denotes the space of all forms
\alpha
satisfying

(3.4)

\nabla_{X_{1},\dots,X_{i}}^{i}\alpha|_{o}=0\ ,\ \text{for any $X_{1},\dots,X_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}$, and $i\leq j-\deg(\alpha)-1$}

with the convention that (
3.4
) reads as
\alpha|_{o}=0
in the case
i=0
, and we have no conditions when
j\leq\deg(\alpha)
.

This filtration is independent of the connection
\nabla
. In fact, it is straightforward to verify that for any local coordinate system
(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n})
defined on a neighbourhood
U
of the given point
o\in M
, one can express any
k
-form
\alpha\in\Omega_{j}
via the local chart
(U,\varphi)
with
\varphi\colon U\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

\displaystyle(\varphi^{-1})^{\sharp}\alpha|_{U}=\sum_{i_{1}<i_{2}<\cdots<i_{k}}\alpha_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}dx^{i_{1}}\wedge dx^{i_{2}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{i_{k}}\,,

where each
\alpha_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}}\in C^{\infty}(\varphi(U))
is a function that has a zero at
\varphi(o)
of degree at least
j-\deg(\alpha)
.

Let
S
be fixed and define
\Omega_{S}=\oplus_{j\geq S}\Omega_{j}
so that
\Omega_{S+1}=0
, in order to obtain a filtration of finite depth
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\supset\Omega_{1}\supset\cdots\supset\Omega_{S}\supset\Omega_{S+1}=0
. The following proposition can readily be obtained by applying the explicit formula of the exterior derivative (
3.2
).

Proposition 3.2
.

The finite filtration
\Omega=\Omega_{0}\supset\cdots\supset\Omega_{S}\supset\Omega_{S+1}=0
as defined in (
3.4
) is a filtered differential complex, that is,
d\Omega_{j}\subset\Omega_{j}
for
j=0,\ldots,S
.

The subspaces
\Omega_{j}
that make up this filtration can easily be re-expressed in terms of jet spaces. Indeed, let us denote by
\pi_{j}
the projection map that sends an arbitrary
k
-form
\alpha
to its
(j-k-1)
-jet at the point
o\in M
, where we are imposing
\pi_{j}(\alpha)=0
if
j-k<0
.
We then have that
\Omega_{j}=\ker\pi_{j}
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, as well as the series of inclusions
\ker\pi_{j}\subset\ker\pi_{j-1}
, and so
\Omega_{j}=\ker\pi_{j}
for
j=0,\ldots,S-1
, and
\Omega_{S}=\oplus_{j\geq S}\Omega_{j}=\oplus_{j\geq S}\ker\pi_{j}
.

Similarly as in the previous examples of differential complexes filtered, one can express
\Omega
as a direct sum decomposition

\Omega=\Omega_{(0)}\oplus\Omega_{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus\Omega_{(S-1)}\oplus\Omega_{S}
,
where the subspaces
\Omega_{(j)}
are defined so that
\Omega_{j}=\Omega_{(j)}\oplus\Omega_{j+1}
for each
j=0,\ldots,S-1
. For any
\alpha\in\Omega_{(j-1)}
, we define
d_{0}\alpha
as the component of
d\alpha
that still belongs to
\Omega_{(j)}
, and impose
d_{0}
to be the zero map on
\Omega_{S}
to obtain a base differential for the de Rham complex
(\Omega,d)
. We can then take
\mathcal{T}
to be some finite dimensional complement of
\ker d_{0}\cap(\Omega_{(0)}\oplus\cdots\oplus\Omega_{(S-1)})
and
\mathcal{C}=\Omega_{S}\oplus\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
, where
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
is a finite dimensional complement of
\image d_{0}
in
\Omega_{(0)}\oplus\cdots\oplus\Omega_{(S-1)}
containing
\mathcal{T}
. This gives us
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
a base transversal and cotransversal for this
d_{0}
.

3.5.
Graded Lie algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra of dimension
n
and let
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{gl}(V)
be a representation. On the space of linear forms

K=\oplus_{k=0}^{n}K^{k}\ ,\quad K^{k}=\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes V\ ,

one can define the operator
\tilde{d}:K^{k}\to K^{k+1}
by imposing that, for any
\alpha\in K^{k}
and for any
X_{1},\ldots,X_{k+1}

\displaystyle\tilde{d}\alpha(X_{1},\dots,X_{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq k+1}(-1)^{i-1}\rho(X_{i})\alpha(X_{1},\dots,\hat{X}_{i},\dots,X_{k+1})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{1\leq i<l<k+1}(-1)^{i+l}\alpha([X_{i},X_{l}],X_{1},\dots,\hat{X}_{i},\dots,\hat{X}_{l},\dots,X_{k+1})\ .

where the hat-symbol is used to indicate the terms that are omitted.
Just like the exterior differential defined in (
3.2
), this map
\tilde{d}
satisfies
\tilde{d}^{2}=0
.

Assume that we have a non-positive grading, that is a direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-s}
, where

(3.5)

\displaystyle\text{ if }X\in\mathfrak{g}_{-k}\text{ and }Y\in\mathfrak{g}_{-l}\ ,\ \text{ then }[X,Y]\in\mathfrak{g}_{-k-l}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-k-l+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\ .

We can also introduce an inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
such that this decomposition is given by orthogonal subspaces.
Then a finite filtration of
\mathfrak{g}
can be obtained by taking
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j=0}^{-s}\mathfrak{g}_{j}\supset\oplus_{j=0}^{-s+1}\mathfrak{g}_{j}\supset\cdots\supset\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}\supset\mathfrak{g}_{0}\supset 0
.

By repeating the same construction presented in subsections
3.1
and
3.3
, we obtain that this orthogonal decomposition naturally extends to the space
\bigwedge\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\otimes V
:

\displaystyle K^{k}=\wedge^{k}\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\otimes V=K^{k}_{(0)}\oplus K^{k}_{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus K^{k}_{(S-1)}\oplus K^{k}_{S}\ ,\text{ for each }k=0,\ldots,n\,,

where
S
is again defined using the same formula as in (
3.1
).

Our assumption of working with a non-positive grading (
3.5
) being equivalent to assumption (
A
) implies that we have a graded decomposition of the operator
\tilde{d}=\tilde{d}_{(0)}\oplus\tilde{d}_{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus\tilde{d}_{(s)}
, for which
\tilde{d}_{(0)}^{2}=0
(again, it is sufficient to follow the steps presented in subsection
3.3
). Therefore, the map
\tilde{d}_{0}:=\tilde{d}_{(0)}
is a base differential for the complex
(\bigwedge\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\otimes V,\tilde{d})
, and it is sufficient to take
\mathcal{T}:=(\ker\tilde{d}_{0})^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{C}=(\image\tilde{d}_{0})^{\perp}
as the relative base transversal and cotransversal.

4.
Spectral methods for the filtered subcomplex

In many of the examples considered in Section
3
, not only do we have a finite depth filtration of our complex
(K,d)
as in (
2.1
), but the complex is also given an additional grading
(K^{k})
,
k=0
,
1
,
\dots
.
This is indeed the case for our examples in Section
3
, where an element belongs to
K^{k}
if it is a
k
-form. We observe that the grading satisfies

(4.1)

dK^{k}\subseteq K^{k+1},\qquad K^{k}\subset K_{k},\qquad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\ .

A very natural question arises about the spectral sequences that can be constructed from such a filtration.
In this section we will see that, just like in the simpler case of Carnot groups
[
25
]
, any filtered complex of finite depth is also a multicomplex. As shown in
[
27
]
, the extra algebraic structure of a multicomplex allows us to obtain an explicit formulation of the differentials that arise from the spectral sequence obtained from this finite filtration. In this way, we are also able to compare these differentials with the projection map
P^{\infty}
introduced in Section
2
.

4.1.
Spectral sequence associated to a multicomplex

We will first include the basic definitions that we will use for the remainder of the section.

Definition 4.1
.

A multicomplex, also called a twisted (co)chain complex, is a
(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z})
-graded
k
-module
C
equipped with maps
d_{i}\colon C\to C
for
i\geq 0
of bidegree
|d_{i}|=(i,1-i)
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i+j=l}d_{i}d_{j}=0\ \text{ for all }\ l\geq 0\,.

For
C
a multicomplex and
(a,b)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
, we write
C_{a,b}
for the
k
-module in bidegree
(a,b)
.

Given a multicomplex
C
, one could consider different possible total complexes, however we will focus on the following one.

Definition 4.2
.

For a multicomplex
C
, its associated total complex
\mathrm{Tot}\,C
is the chain complex with

\displaystyle\left(\mathrm{Tot}\,C\right)_{k}=\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\leq 0\end{subarray}}C_{a,b}\bigg)\oplus\bigg(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a>0\end{subarray}}C_{a,b}\bigg)=\bigg(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
b\leq 0\end{subarray}}C_{a,b}\bigg)\oplus\bigg(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
b>0\end{subarray}}C_{a,b}\bigg)\,.

The differential on
\mathrm{Tot}\,C
is given for an arbitrary element
c\in\big(\mathrm{Tot\,C}\big)_{k}
by

(4.3)

\displaystyle(dc)_{a}=\sum_{i\geq 0}d_{i}(c)_{a-i}\,,

where
(c)_{a}
denotes the projection of
c\in\big(\mathrm{Tot}\,C\big)_{k}
to
C_{a,\bullet}=\prod_{b,a+b=k}C_{a,b}
.

In general, when working with
(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)_{k}
it is not always possible to consider the direct product total complex
\prod_{a+b=k}C_{a,b}
, as the formula (
4.3
) may involve infinite sums. However, since in this paper we are working on a complex of finite depth, the associated total complex takes the simpler form

(4.4)

\displaystyle\big(\mathrm{Tot}\,C\big)_{k}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\geq 0\end{subarray}}C_{a,b}\ ,\ \text{ with differential }(dc)_{a}=\sum_{i=0}^{S}d_{i}(c)_{a-i}\,.

Definition 4.3
.

Given a multicomplex
C
and its associated total complex
\mathrm{Tot}\,C
, we can define for each degree
k
the following subcomplexes

\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{F}_{p}\mathrm{Tot\,}C\big)_{k}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\geq p\end{subarray}}C_{a,b}\,.

By definition, as the value of
p
varies, the subcomplexes
\mathcal{F}_{p}\mathrm{Tot}\,C
form a filtration of
\mathrm{Tot}\,C
of finite depth:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tot}\,C=\mathcal{F}_{0}\mathrm{Tot}\,C\supset\mathcal{F}_{1}\mathrm{Tot}\,C\supset\cdots\supset\mathcal{F}_{S}\mathrm{Tot}\,C\supset\mathcal{F}_{S+1}\mathrm{Tot}\,C=0\,.

Let us now consider the spectral sequence associated with this filtered complex
\mathcal{F}_{p}\mathrm{Tot}\,C
. For
r\geq 0
, the
r^{th}
-page of the spectral sequence is a bigraded module
E_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)
with a map
\delta_{r}
of bidegree
(r,1-r)
for which
\delta_{r}\circ\delta_{r}=0
. Moreover, we have that the spaces
E_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)
can be expressed as the quotients

\displaystyle E_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)\cong\mathcal{Z}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)/\mathcal{B}^{p,\bullet}_{r}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)

where the
r
-cycles are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C):=\left\{c\in\mathcal{F}_{p}\mathrm{Tot}\,C\,:\,d\alpha\in\mathcal{F}_{p+r}\mathrm{Tot}\,C\right\}

and the
r
-boundaries are given by

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C):=\mathcal{Z}_{0}^{p+1,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)\ \text{
and
}\\
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C):=\mathcal{Z}_{r-1}^{p+1,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)+d\mathcal{Z}_{r-1}^{p-(r-1),\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)\ \text{
for
}r\geq 1\,.\end{cases}

Given an element
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)
, we will denote by
[x]_{r}
its image in
E^{p,\bullet}_{r}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)
, so that

\displaystyle\delta_{r}([x]_{r})=[dx]_{r}\ ,\ \text{
for any
}[x]_{r}\in E^{p,\bullet}_{r}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)\,.

Expanding on the expressions for
\mathcal{Z}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)
and
\mathcal{B}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)
, one can introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.4
.

Let
x\in C_{p,\bullet}
and let
r\geq 1
. We define the graded submodules
Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}
and
B^{p,\bullet}_{r}
of
C_{p,\bullet}
as follows:

\displaystyle x\in Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}\ \Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c}\text{
for
}1\leq j\leq r-1\ \text{ there exists }z_{p+j}\in C_{p+j,\bullet}\ \text{ such that }\\
d_{0}x=0\ \text{
and
}\ d_{m}x=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}d_{i}z_{p+m-i}\ \text{ for all }\ 1\leq m\leq r-1\end{array}

\displaystyle x\in B_{r}^{p,\bullet}\ \Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{c}\text{ for }1\leq k\leq r-1\ \text{ there exists }c_{p-k}\in C_{p-k,\bullet}\ \text{ such that}\\
x=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}d_{k}c_{p-k}\ \text{
and
}\\
0=\sum_{k=l}^{r-1}d_{k-l}c_{p-k}\ \text{
for
}\ 1\leq l\leq r-1\,.\end{array}

As shown already in
[
27
]
and
[
25
]
, the following holds.

Proposition 4.5
.

The map

\displaystyle\psi\colon\mathcal{Z}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot\,}C)/\mathcal{B}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,C)\longrightarrow Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}/B_{r}^{p,\bullet}

sending
[x]_{r}
to the class
[(x)_{p}]
is a well-defined isomorphism. Moreover, under this isomorphism, the
r^{th}
-differential of the spectral sequence corresponds to the following map

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\colon Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}/B_{r}^{p,\bullet}\to Z_{r}^{p+r,\bullet}/B_{r}^{p+r,\bullet}\ ,\ \partial_{r}\big([x]\big)=\bigg[d_{r}x-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}d_{i}z_{p+r-i}\bigg]

where
x\in Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}
and the elements
z_{p+j}\in C_{p+j,\bullet}
satisfy (
4.4
).

4.2.
Filtered complexes
(K,d)
of finite depth as multicomplexes

We apply the formalism of multicomplexes to a differential complex
(K,d)
having both a filtration
K_{0}\supset K_{1}\supset\cdots\supset K_{S}\supset K_{S+1}=0
and a grading
K=K^{0}\oplus\cdots\oplus K^{n}
satisfying (
4.1
).

Proposition 4.6
.

Any filtered complex
(K,d)
as described above is a multicomplex with maps
d_{(i)}\colon K\to K
of bidegree
|d_{(i)}|=(i,1-i)
with
i=0,\ldots,S
.

Proof.

As already pointed out in (
2.2
), we can express the complex
K
as a direct sum decomposition on each degree. For each degree
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have that

\displaystyle K^{k}=K_{0}^{k}=K_{(0)}^{k}\oplus K_{1}^{k}=K_{(0)}^{k}\oplus K^{k}_{(1)}\oplus K_{2}^{k}=K_{(0)}^{k}\oplus K^{k}_{(1)}\oplus\cdots\oplus K_{(S-1)}^{k}\oplus K^{k}_{S}\ .

This direct sum decomposition of
K
based on the filtration induces a decomposition of the original differential
d
, since
dK_{j}^{k}\subset K_{j}^{k+1}
. Indeed

\displaystyle d=d_{(0)}+d_{(1)}+\cdots+d_{(S)}\ \text{ where }\ d_{(i)}K_{(j)}^{k}\subset K_{(j+i)}^{k+1}\ ,\ i,j=0,\ldots,S\,.

Finally, since
(K,d)
is a complex, once we expand the formula
d^{2}=0
and gather all the terms according to their respective subspace
K_{(j)}
that they belong to, we get that for any
\alpha\in K_{(j)}^{k}

\displaystyle 0=

\displaystyle d^{2}\alpha=\big(d_{(0)}+d_{(1)}+\cdots+d_{(S)}\big)\big(d_{(0)}+d_{(1)}+\cdots+d_{(S)}\big)\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{d_{(0)}^{2}\alpha}_{\in K_{(j)}^{k+2}}+\underbrace{d_{(0)}d_{(1)}\alpha+d_{(1)}d_{(0)}\alpha}_{\in K_{(j+1)}^{k+2}}+\underbrace{d_{(0)}d_{(2)}\alpha+d_{(1)}^{2}\alpha+d_{(2)}d_{(0)}\alpha}_{\in K_{(j+2)}^{k+2}}+\cdots+\underbrace{d_{(S)}^{2}\alpha}_{\in K_{(j+2S)}^{k+2}}=\sum_{l=1}^{2S}\sum_{i+j=l}d_{(i)}d_{(j)}\alpha\,.

We then have that each addend
\sum_{i+j=l}d_{(i)}d_{(j)}\alpha
belongs to a different subspace
K_{(j+l)}^{k+2}
and so, by the direct sum decomposition
K_{j}^{k+2}=\bigoplus_{l=0}^{S}K_{(j+l)}^{k+2}
, we get that each addend will necessarily be zero.
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

Any filtered complex
(K,d)
of finite depth as in (
2.2
) admits a spectral sequence associated to the specific filtration considered, whose differentials at each step
r
have the following expression

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\colon Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}/B_{r}^{p,\bullet}\to Z_{r}^{p+r,\bullet}/B_{r}^{p+r,\bullet}\ ,\ \partial_{r}\big([x]\big)=\bigg[d_{(r)}x-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}d_{(i)}z_{p+r-i}\bigg]\ ,\ r=1,\ldots,S\,.

We stress that, given a filtered complex
(K,d)
of finite depth with base differential
d_{0}=d_{(0)}\colon K\to K
, once we fix a base transversal and cotransversal
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}
, it is possible to define the partial inverse
d_{0}^{-1}\colon K\to K
of
d_{0}
as in Definition
2.4
. Therefore, using this additional map, it is possible to rephrase the condition
x\in Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}
of (
4.4
) in terms of this operator
d_{0}^{-1}
, and require the
z_{p+j}\in K_{(p+j)}
to be elements in
\mathcal{T}
.

In other words, given
x\in Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}
, we have that there exist
z_{p+j}\in K_{(p+j)}\cap\mathcal{T}
with
1\leq j\leq r-1
such that

\displaystyle z_{p+1}=

\displaystyle d_{0}^{-1}d_{(1)}x

\displaystyle z_{p+2}=

\displaystyle d_{0}^{-1}(d_{(2)}x-d_{(1)}z_{p+1})=d_{0}^{-1}d_{(2)}x-d_{0}^{-1}d_{(1)}d_{0}^{-1}d_{(1)}x

\displaystyle z_{p+3}=

\displaystyle d_{0}^{(-1)}(d_{(3)}x-d_{(2)}z_{p+1}-d_{(1)}z_{p+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{0}^{-1}d_{(3)}x-d_{0}^{-1}d_{(2)}d_{0}^{-1}d_{(1)}x-d_{0}^{-1}d_{(1)}d_{0}^{-1}d_{(2)}x+d_{0}^{-1}d_{(1)}d_{0}^{-1}d_{(1)}d_{0}^{-1}d_{(1)}x\,.

If we introduce the multi-index notation

\displaystyle(d_{0}^{-1}d)_{I_{m}^{j}}:=(d_{0}^{-1}d_{(i_{1})})(d_{0}^{-1}d_{(i_{2})})\cdots(d_{0}^{-1}d_{(i_{m})})

where
I_{m}^{j}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{m})\in\mathbb{N}_{+}^{m}
for which
|I_{m}^{j}|=i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{m}=j
, then for any
j=1,\ldots,r-1
, we have the expression

(4.10)

\displaystyle z_{p+j}=\sum_{m=1}^{j}(-1)^{m-1}\sum_{I_{m}^{j}}(d_{0}^{-1}d)_{I_{m}^{j}}x\,.

Remark 4.8
.

Applying the definition of the subspaces
\mathcal{E}_{0}
and Proposition
2.9
, there is a clear correspondence between the subspaces
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{d_{0},\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}}
and the first page quotients of the spectral sequence, as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{d_{0},\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{C}}\cong\oplus_{p=0}^{S}E^{p,\bullet}_{1}\,.

Moreover, if we use the explicit expression of the
z_{p+j}\in K_{(p+j)}\cap\mathcal{T}
in terms of
x
given in (
4.10
), it is possible to obtain the following expression for the
r^{th}
-differential of the spectral sequence

\displaystyle\partial_{r}([x])=\bigg[d_{(r)}x-\sum_{i=1}^{r-1}d_{(i)}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{r-i}(-1)^{m-1}\sum_{I_{m}^{r-i}}(d_{0}^{-1}d)_{I_{m}^{r-i}}\bigg)x\bigg]

It is then a straightforward computation to check that the differential operator
dP^{\infty}
introduced in section
2
applied to any element
x\in Z_{1}^{p,\bullet}
coincides with the sum of the differentials
\partial_{r}
that appear in the multicomplex spectral sequence
generated by the filtration (
2.2
). This result is analogous to the main result contained in
[
25
]
, where considerations were only limited to the de Rham complex on Carnot groups.

5.
Three dimensional Lie groups with negative grading

We will include the following examples of the theory of Section
4
which also cover non-nilpotent Lie groups.
Let us consider the 3-dimensional simply connected Lie group
G_{\kappa}
, whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\kappa}=\spn_{\mathbb{R}}\{X,Y,Z\}
,which can be identified with the
\mathbb{R}
-module of left-invariant vector fields, has the following nontrivial bracket relations

\displaystyle[X,Y]=Z\ ,\ [Y,Z]=\kappa X\ ,\ [X,Z]=-\kappa Y\,.

Let us stress that
G_{1}
is isomorphic to
\SU(2)
,
G_{0}
coincides with the first Heisenberg group, while
G_{-1}
is the universal cover group of
\SL(2)
.

In this example, we will be showing the construction of the spectral sequence associated with the following “sub-Riemannian” grading:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{\kappa}=\mathfrak{g}_{-2}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-1}\ ,\text{ where }\mathfrak{g}_{-1}=\langle X,Y\rangle\text{ and }\mathfrak{g}_{-2}=\langle Z\rangle.

Let us denote by
\{X^{\ast},Y^{\ast},Z^{\ast}\}
the dual basis of
\{X,Y,Z\}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{\kappa}^{\ast}
.

Using this grading and following the same construction described in subsection
3.5
, we obtain a decreasing and finite filtration over the space
\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})
of smooth forms:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{0}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})=\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})\supset\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})\supset\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})\supset\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})\supset\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})\supset\mathcal{F}_{5}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})=\{0\}

where

•

\mathcal{F}_{0}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})=\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})
;

•

\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})=\spn_{C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})}\{X^{\ast},Y^{\ast},Z^{\ast},X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast},X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\}
;

•

\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})=\spn_{C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})}\{Z^{\ast},X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast},X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\}
;

•

\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})=\spn_{C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})}\{X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\}
;

•

\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})=\spn_{C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})}\{X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\}
;

•

\mathcal{F}_{5}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})=\{0\}
.

Following the notation adopted in Section
3.5
, we are considering the de Rham complex
(\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}),d)
as our filtered complex
(K,\tilde{d})
, where
\tilde{d}
is the exterior derivative and it admits the splitting
d=d_{0}+d_{(1)}+d_{(2)}
. This can be seen explicitly by writing down the action of
d
on an arbitrary 1-form
\alpha=fX^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}+hZ^{\ast}\in\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})=K^{1}
with
f,g,h\in C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})=K^{0}
:

\displaystyle d\big(fX^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}+hZ^{\ast}\big)=

\displaystyle df\wedge X^{\ast}+f\,dX^{\ast}+dg\wedge Y^{\ast}+g\,dY^{\ast}+dh\wedge Z^{\ast}+h\,dZ^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-YfX^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}-ZfX^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}-\kappa fY^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+XgX^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}-ZgY^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+

\displaystyle+\kappa gX^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}-hX^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}+XhX^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+YhY^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\underbrace{-hX^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}}_{d_{0}}+\underbrace{(Xg-Yf)X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}+XhX^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+YhY^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}}_{d_{(1)}}+

\displaystyle+\underbrace{(\kappa g-Zf)X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}-(\kappa f+Zg)Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}}_{d_{(2)}}\,.

Finally, the direct sum decomposition associated to such finite filtration is given by

\displaystyle\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})=K=K_{(0)}\oplus K_{(1)}\oplus K_{(2)}\oplus K_{(3)}\oplus K_{4}\,,

where

•

K_{(0)}=K^{0}=C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})
;

•

K_{(1)}=\spn_{C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})}\{X^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\}
;

•

K_{(2)}=\spn_{C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})}\{Z^{\ast},X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\}
;

•

K_{(3)}=\spn_{C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})}\{X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\}
;

•

K_{(4)}=\spn_{C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})}\{X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\}
.

We will now proceed to show the computations of the spectral sequence associated to the multicomplex that arises from this particular filtration, just as described in the previous section.
Notice that in our computations, for all the pages
r>0
, we will compute the subspaces
Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}
and
B_{r}^{p,\bullet}
, and use the isomorphism

\displaystyle E_{r}^{p,\bullet}=E_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}))=\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}))}{\mathcal{B}_{r}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}))}=\frac{Z_{r}^{p,\bullet}}{B_{r}^{p,\bullet}}\,.

Since the filtration over the space of smooth forms is nontrivial up to
S=4
, we are going to present and discuss the quotients that appear in the spectral sequence construction for each
p=0,\ldots,4
. In order to make the computations more understandable, we are also going to summarise the overall information at each page in a more pictorial way with the use of diagrams. We should stress that we chose not to follow the standard convention of such diagrams, where at each page
r
the quotient
E^{p,s}_{r}
is placed at position
p
along the horizontal axis and position
s
along the vertical one. In this way, each quotient
E_{r}^{p,s}
would be placed at the point
(p,s)
on the Cartesian plane. Instead, in our diagrams the level of the filtration is encoded along the vertical axis, and the degree of the forms along the horizontal axis, so that each quotient
E^{p,s}_{r}
will be placed at the point
(p+s,-p)
on the Cartesian plane. Finally, in order to simplify the notation, we will denote by
\langle\cdot\rangle
the span over the space of smooth functions
C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})
.

5.0.
Page-zero quotients
E_{0}^{p,\bullet}

At the zeroth page of the spectral sequence for
r=0
, one has the quotients:

\displaystyle E_{0}^{p,\bullet}=\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{0}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}))}{\mathcal{B}_{0}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}))}=\frac{\mathcal{F}_{p}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})}{\mathcal{F}_{p+1}\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa})}\,.

\mathbf{p=0}

•

E^{0,0}_{0}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})=K^{0}=\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})=\langle 1\rangle
;

•

E_{0}^{0,1}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})=[0]_{0}
;

•

E_{0}^{0,2}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})=[0]_{0}
;

•

E_{0}^{0,3}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})=[0]_{0}
;

\mathbf{p=1}

•

E_{0}^{1,-1}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})=0
;

•

E_{0}^{1,0}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})=K^{1}/K^{1}_{(2)}=\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})/\langle Z^{\ast}\rangle=\langle[X^{\ast}]_{0},[Y^{\ast}]_{0}\rangle
;

•

E_{0}^{1,1}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})=[0]_{0}
;

•

E_{0}^{1,2}=\mathcal{F}_{1}\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})=[0]_{0}
;

\mathbf{p=2}

•

E^{2,-2}_{0}=\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})=0
;

•

E_{0}^{2,-1}=\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})=\langle Z^{\ast}\rangle
;

•

E_{0}^{2,0}=\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})=K^{2}/K^{2}_{(3)}=\langle\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})/\langle X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\rangle=\langle[X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}]_{0}\rangle
;

•

E_{0}^{2,1}=\mathcal{F}_{2}\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})=[0]_{0}
;

\mathbf{p=3}

•

E_{0}^{3,-3}=\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})=0
;

•

E_{0}^{3,-2}=\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})=0
;

•

E_{0}^{3,-1}=\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})=K^{2}_{(3)}=\langle X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\rangle
;

•

E_{0}^{3,0}=\mathcal{F}_{3}\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})/\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})=[0]_{0}
;

\mathbf{p=4}

•

E_{0}^{4,-4}=\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{0}(G_{\kappa})=0
;

•

E_{0}^{4,-3}=\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{1}(G_{\kappa})=0
;

•

E_{0}^{4,-2}=\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{2}(G_{\kappa})=0
;

•

E_{0}^{4,-1}=\mathcal{F}_{4}\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})=K^{4}=\Omega^{3}(G_{\kappa})=\langle X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\rangle
.

These computations can be easily summarised in a more visual way using the following diagram

where we are also drawing the action of the operators
\partial_{0}=d_{0}\colon E_{0}^{p,\bullet}\to E^{p,\bullet}_{0}
.
Moreover, let us stress that such operator
\partial_{0}

E_{0}^{2,-1}
, as

\displaystyle\partial_{0}\colon E^{2,-1}_{0}\to E^{2,0}_{0}\ ,\ \partial_{0}(Z^{\ast})=d_{0}(Z^{\ast})=-X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}=-[X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}]_{0}\,.

5.1.
Page-one quotients
E_{1}^{p,\bullet}

Starting from page
r=1
, we are going to use the isomorphism

\displaystyle E_{1}^{p,\bullet}=\frac{\mathcal{Z}_{1}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}))}{\mathcal{B}_{1}^{p,\bullet}(\mathrm{Tot}\,\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}))}=\frac{Z_{1}^{p,\bullet}}{B_{1}^{p,\bullet}}\,,

where

\displaystyle Z_{1}^{p,\bullet}=

\displaystyle\{\alpha\in K_{(p)}:d_{0}\alpha=0\}\ \text{ and }B_{1}^{p,\bullet}=\{\beta\in K_{(p)}:\exists\ \gamma\in K_{(p)}\text{ such that }d_{0}\gamma=\beta\}\,.

\mathbf{p\neq 2}

As pointed out before, we have that
d_{0}
acts as the zero map on any
K_{(p)}
with
p\neq 2
, and so
E_{1}^{p,\bullet}=E^{p,\bullet}_{0}
.

\mathbf{p=2}

The map
d_{0}\colon K^{1}_{(2)}\to K^{2}_{(2)}

is an isomorphism, and so

\displaystyle Z_{1}^{2,-1}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in K^{1}_{(2)}:d_{0}\alpha=0\}=0\ \text{ and }\ B_{1}^{2,-1}=0

\displaystyle Z_{1}^{2,0}=

\displaystyle\{\alpha\in K^{2}_{(2)}:d_{0}\alpha=0\}=K^{2}_{(2)}\ \text{ and }\ B_{1}^{2,0}=K^{2}_{(2)}\,.

Therefore

•

E^{2,-2}_{1}=E^{2,-2}_{0}=0
;

•

E_{1}^{2,-1}=Z_{1}^{2,-1}/B_{1}^{2,-1}=0
;

•

E_{1}^{2,0}=Z_{1}^{2,0}/B_{1}^{2,0}=[0]_{1}
;

•

E_{1}^{2,1}=E_{0}^{2,1}=[0]_{0}
.

By the properties of the spectral sequence, for any
r>1
,
E_{r}^{2,\bullet}\subset E_{1}^{2,\bullet}
, and so
E^{2,\bullet}_{r}\cong 0
for any
r\geq 1
.

In this diagram of page one, we are also drawing the action of the operators
\partial_{1}\colon E_{1}^{p,\bullet}\to E_{1}^{p+1,\bullet}
, which have nontrivial action only on
E_{0}^{0,0}
and
E^{3,-1}_{1}
. Indeed

\displaystyle\partial_{1}

\displaystyle\colon E^{0,0}_{1}\to E_{1}^{1,0}\ ,\ \partial_{1}[f]=[d_{(1)}f]\ ,\ \forall\ f\in C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\colon

\displaystyle E_{1}^{3,-1}\to E_{1}^{4,-1}\ ,\ \partial_{1}[\alpha]=[d_{(1)}\alpha]\ ,\ \forall\ \alpha\in Z^{3,-1}_{1}\,.

In particular, for any
f\in C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})
, we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{1}[f]=[d_{(1)}f]=[XfX^{\ast}+YfY^{\ast}]=XfX^{\ast}+YfY^{\ast}\ \mathrm{mod}\,\langle Z^{\ast}\rangle\,,

while for any
\alpha=fX^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\in Z_{1}^{3,-1}=Z_{0}^{3,-1}=K_{(3)}^{2}
, we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{1}\big[fX^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\big]=\big[d_{(1)}\big(fX^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\big)\big]=(Xg-Yf)X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\,.

5.2.
Page-two quotients
E_{2}^{p,\bullet}

By definition, the subspaces
Z_{2}^{p,\bullet},B_{2}^{p,\bullet}\subset K_{(p)}
are given by

\displaystyle Z_{2}^{p,\bullet}=

\displaystyle\{\alpha\in K_{(p)}:d_{0}\alpha=0\text{ and }\exists\ \beta_{1}\in K_{(p+1)}\text{ s.t. }d_{1}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{1}\}

\displaystyle B_{2}^{p,\bullet}=

\displaystyle\{\beta\in K_{(p)}:\exists\ \gamma_{0}\in K_{(p)},\gamma_{1}\in K_{(p-1)}\text{ s.t. }\beta=d_{0}\gamma_{0}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{1}\text{ and }d_{0}\gamma_{1}=0\}

\mathbf{p=0}

The only nontrivial space of forms in
K_{(0)}
is the space of 0-forms, and so

\displaystyle Z_{2}^{0,0}=\{f\in C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})\mid d_{0}f=0\text{ and }\exists\,g\in K^{0}_{(1)}\text{ s.t. }d_{(1)}f=d_{0}g\}\,.

Since
K^{0}_{(1)}=0
, the condition
f\in Z_{2}^{0,0}
reads as
d_{(1)}f=0
, or in other words

\displaystyle f\in Z_{2}^{0,0}\longleftrightarrow d_{(1)}f=XfX^{\ast}+YfY^{\ast}=0\longleftrightarrow Xf\equiv Yf\equiv 0\,.

Moreover, since
Z=[X,Y]
, we have that
Z_{2}^{0,0}=\mathbb{R}
the constant functions, and so
E_{2}^{0,0}=Z_{2}^{0,0}=\mathbb{R}
. Finally, we know that the de Rham cohomology class in degree 0,
H^{0}(\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}),d)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
, and so by the properties of spectral sequences, we readily get that
E_{r}^{0,0}\cong H^{0}(G_{\kappa},d)\cong\mathbb{R}
for any
r\geq 2
.

\mathbf{p=1}

The only nontrivial space of forms in
K_{(1)}
is the space of 1-forms, and so

\displaystyle Z_{2}^{1,0}=\{\alpha\in K_{(1)}^{1}:d_{0}\alpha=0\text{ and }\exists\,\beta_{1}\in K_{(2)}^{1}\text{ s.t. }d_{(1)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{1}\}\,.

An arbitrary element
\alpha\in K_{(1)}^{1}
with
\alpha=fX^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}
satisfies
d_{0}\alpha=0
, moreover

\displaystyle d_{(1)}\alpha=(Xg-Yf)X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}=d_{0}[(Yf-Xg)Z^{\ast}]\,,\ \text{ where }(Yf-Xg)Z^{\ast}\in K^{1}_{(2)}

and so
Z_{2}^{1,0}=K^{1}_{(1)}
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle B_{2}^{1,0}=\{\beta\in K_{(1)}^{1}:\exists\,\gamma_{0}\in K_{(1)}^{0},\,\gamma_{1}\in K_{(0)}^{0}\text{ s.t. }\beta=d_{0}\gamma_{0}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{1}\text{ and }d_{0}\gamma_{1}=0\}

and since
K_{(1)}^{0}=0
, we have that an element
\beta=fX^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}\in B_{2}^{1,0}
if there exists
h\in K_{(0)}^{0}
such that

\displaystyle\beta=d_{(1)}h\text{ and }d_{0}h=0\longleftrightarrow\beta=d_{(1)}h=XhX^{\ast}+YhY^{\ast}\,.

Therefore, we have that
E_{2}^{1,0}=Z_{2}^{1,0}/B^{1,0}_{2}=K_{(1)}^{1}/d_{(1)}K_{(0)}^{0}=\langle[X^{\ast},Y^{\ast}]_{2}\rangle
.

\mathbf{p=2}

As already pointed out in the computations of the previous page, we have that
E_{2}^{2,\bullet}\cong 0
.

\mathbf{p=3}

The only nontrivial space of forms in
K_{(3)}
is the space of 2-forms, and so

\displaystyle Z_{2}^{3,-1}=\{\alpha\in K_{(3)}^{2}:d_{0}\alpha=0\text{ and }\exists\,\beta_{1}\in K_{(4)}^{2}\text{ s.t. }d_{(1)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{1}\}\,.

An arbitrary element
\alpha\in K_{(3)}^{2}
with
\alpha=fX^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}
satisfies
d_{0}\alpha=0
, moreover, since
K_{(4)}^{2}=0
, we have that
\alpha\in Z_{2}^{3,-1}
if
d_{(1)}\alpha=0
. Since also
d_{(2)}\alpha=0
for any
\alpha\in K_{(3)}^{2}
, we have that
d\alpha=d_{0}\alpha+d_{(1)}\alpha+d_{(2)}\alpha=d_{(1)}\alpha
, and so the space
Z_{2}^{3,-1}
coincides with the space of closed forms in
K_{(3)}^{2}
.
On the other hand,

\displaystyle\beta\in B_{2}^{3,-1}\longleftrightarrow\exists\ \gamma_{0}\in K_{(3)}^{1},\gamma_{1}\in K_{(2)}^{1}\text{ s.t. }\beta=d_{0}\gamma_{0}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{1}\text{ and }d_{0}\gamma_{1}=0\,.

We know that
K_{(3)}^{1}=0
, however, for any form
\gamma_{1}=fZ^{\ast}\in K_{(2)}^{1}
, we have
d_{0}\gamma_{1}=-fX^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\neq 0
, and so
B_{2}^{3,-1}=0
. Therefore,
E_{2}^{3,-1}=Z_{2}^{3,-1}/B_{2}^{3,-1}=Z_{2}^{3,-1}=\{\alpha\in K_{(3)}^{2}:d\alpha=0\}=\langle[X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast},Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}]_{2}\rangle
.

\mathbf{p=4}

Finally, the only nontrivial space of forms in
K_{(4)}
is the space of 3-forms, and so

\displaystyle Z^{4,-1}_{2}=\{\alpha\in K^{3}_{(4)}:d_{0}\alpha=0\text{ and }\exists\,\beta_{1}\in K_{(5)}^{3}\text{ s.t. }d_{(1)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{1}\}\,,

and since
K_{(5)}^{3}=K_{(5)}^{4}=0
, we have that
Z_{2}^{4,-1}=K_{(4)}^{3}
. On the other hand, the condition
\beta\in B_{2}^{4,-1}
reads

\displaystyle\beta\in B_{2}^{4,-1}\longleftrightarrow\exists\,\gamma_{0}\in K_{(4)}^{2},\gamma_{1}\in K_{(3)}^{2}\text{ s.t. }\beta=d_{0}\gamma_{0}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{1}\text{ and }d_{0}\gamma_{1}=0\,.

Since
K_{(4)}^{2}=0
, and
d_{0}\gamma_{1}=0
for any
\gamma_{1}\in K_{(3)}^{2}
, we get
B^{4,-1}_{2}=\{\beta\in K_{(4)}^{3}:\exists\,\gamma_{1}\in K_{(3)}^{2}\text{ s.t. }d_{(1)}\gamma_{1}=\beta\}=\{(Xg-Yf)X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\}
.

Therefore,

E_{2}^{4,-1}=Z_{2}^{4,-1}/B_{2}^{4,-1}=K_{(4)}^{3}/d_{(1)}K_{(3)}^{2}=[X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}]_{2}
.

It should be noted that any
\alpha\in Z_{2}^{4,-1}=K_{(4)}^{3}
is a closed form, since
d\alpha=0
. If
\alpha
is also exact, then there exists
\beta=\beta_{(2)}+\beta_{(3)}\in K^{2}
such that
d\beta=\alpha
. Here,
\beta_{(2)}\in K_{(2)}^{2}
and
\beta_{(3)}\in K_{(3)}^{2}
, where
\beta_{(2)}=d_{0}\gamma_{(2)}
for some
\gamma_{(2)}\in K_{(2)}^{1}
since the map
d_{0}\colon K_{(2)}^{1}\to K_{(2)}^{2}
is an isomorphism. Therefore

\displaystyle\alpha=d\beta=d_{(2)}\beta_{(2)}+d_{(1)}\beta_{(3)}=d_{(2)}d_{0}\gamma_{(2)}+d_{(1)}\beta_{(3)}=-d_{(1)}^{2}\gamma_{(2)}+d_{(1)}\beta_{(3)}\,.

Here, in the final equality, we use the fact that
(K,d)
is a multicomplex and
d_{(0)}d_{(2)}+d_{(1)}^{2}+d_{(2)}d_{(0)}=0
, and that
d_{(2)}\colon K_{(2)}^{1}\to K_{(4)}^{2}
is the zero map. Hence, for any exact form
\alpha\in Z_{2}^{4,-1}
there exists
\tilde{\beta}=\beta_{(3)}-d_{(1)}\gamma_{(2)}\in K_{(3)}^{2}
such that
\alpha=d_{(1)}\tilde{\beta}
. In other words,
E_{2}^{4,-1}
is isomorphic to
H^{3}(\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}),d)
, the de Rham cohomology class of
G_{\kappa}
in degree 3. In the case where
H^{3}(\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}),d)=0
, for example in the first Heisenberg group when
\kappa=0
, we have
E_{2}^{4,-1}\cong 0
, whereas if
H^{3}(\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}),d)\cong\mathbb{R}
, for example when
\kappa>0
, then
E_{2}^{4,-1}\cong\mathbb{R}
. Following the same reasoning as in the case of
\mathbf{p=0}
, we also have that
E^{4,-1}_{r}\cong H^{3}(\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}),d)
for any
r\geq 2
.

In this diagram of page two, we are showing the action of the operators
\partial_{2}\colon E_{2}^{p,\bullet}\to E_{2}^{p+2,\bullet}
. The only nontrivial map of such operators is
\partial_{2}\colon E_{2}^{1,0}\to E_{2}^{3,-1}
, so for any
\alpha\in Z_{2}^{1,0}=K_{(1)}^{1}

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\big[\alpha\big]=\big[d_{(2)}\alpha-d_{(1)}\beta_{1}\big]\ \text{ for some }\beta_{1}\in K_{(2)}^{1}\text{ s.t. }d_{(1)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{1}\,.

As already pointed out before, if we fix
\alpha=fX^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}
, it is sufficient to take
\beta_{1}=(Yf-Xg)Z^{\ast}\in K_{(2)}^{1}
in order to obtain
d_{(1)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{1}
, hence

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\big[fX^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}\big]=

\displaystyle\big[d_{(2)}(fX^{\ast}+gY^{\ast})-d_{(1)}(Yf-Xg)Z^{\ast}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[(\kappa g-Zf)X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}-(\kappa f+Zg)Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+

\displaystyle-X(Yf-Xg)X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}-Y(Yf-Xg)Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\kappa g-Zf-XYf+X^{2}g)X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+(YXg-Zg-Y^{2}f-\kappa f)Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}

One can also readily check that
\partial_{2}[\alpha]
is an element of
E_{2}^{3,-1}
, since

\displaystyle X(YXg-Zg-Y^{2}f-\kappa f)=Y(\kappa g-Zf-XYf+X^{2}g)\ ,\ \forall\,f,g\in C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})\,.

Moreover, the differential
\partial_{2}
is well-defined on the quotient
E_{2}^{1,0}
since for any
\beta\in B_{2}^{1,0}
we have
\beta=XhX^{\ast}+YhY^{\ast}
for some
h\in C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})
, and so

\displaystyle\partial_{2}[\beta]=

\displaystyle\big[d_{(2)}(XhX^{\ast}+YhY^{\ast})-d_{(1)}(YXh-XYh)Z^{\ast}\big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big[(\kappa Yh-ZXh)X^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}-(\kappa Xh+ZYh)Y^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+XZhX^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}+YZhY^{\ast}\wedge Z^{\ast}\big]=[0]\,.

5.3.
Page-three quotients
E_{3}^{p,\bullet}

By definition, we have that the subspaces
Z_{3}^{p,\bullet},B_{3}^{p,\bullet}\subset K_{(p)}
are given by

\displaystyle Z_{3}^{p,\bullet}=

\displaystyle\{\alpha\in K_{(p)}\mid d_{0}\alpha=0\,,\,\exists\ \beta_{1}\in K_{(p+1)},\beta_{2}\in K_{(p+2)}\text{ s.t. }d_{(1)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{1}\,,\,d_{(2)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{2}+d_{(1)}\beta_{1}\}

\displaystyle B_{3}^{p,\bullet}=

\displaystyle\{\beta\in K_{(p)}\mid\exists\ \gamma_{i}\in K_{(p-i)},\,i=0,1,2\text{ s.t. }\beta=\sum_{i=0}^{2}d_{(i)}\gamma_{i}\text{ and }d_{0}\gamma_{1}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{2}=d_{0}\gamma_{2}=0\}\,.

\mathbf{p=0}

As already pointed out in the computations of the previous page, we have that
E^{0,0}_{3}=E^{0,0}_{2}=\mathbb{R}
.

\mathbf{p=1}

We have already shown that an arbitrary element
\alpha=fX^{\ast}+gY^{\ast}\in K_{(1)}^{1}
belongs to
Z^{1,0}_{2}
since
d_{0}\alpha=0
and one can take
\beta_{1}=(Yf-Xg)Z^{\ast}\in K_{(2)}^{1}
such that
d_{(1)}\alpha=(Xg-Yf)X^{\ast}\wedge Y^{\ast}=d_{0}\beta_{1}
. Hence
\alpha\in Z^{1,0}_{3}
if there exists
\beta_{2}\in K_{(3)}^{1}
such that
d_{(2)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{2}+d_{(1)}\beta_{1}
, but since
K_{(3)}^{1}=0
, this means that

\alpha\in Z^{1,0}_{3}\longleftrightarrow\alpha\in K_{(1)}^{1}\text{ and }d_{(2)}\alpha=d_{(1)}\beta_{1}\text{ with }d_{(1)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{1}\,.

On the other hand,
\beta\in B_{3}^{1,0}
if there exist
\gamma_{i}\in K_{(1-i)}^{0}
, with
i=0,1,2
such that
\beta=d_{0}\gamma_{0}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{1}+d_{(2)}\gamma_{2}
and
d_{0}\gamma_{1}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{2}=d_{0}\gamma_{2}=0
. Since
K_{(-1)}^{0}=K_{(1)}^{0}=0
, we have that
\beta\in B_{3}^{1,0}
if
\exists\,h\in C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})
such that
\beta=d_{(1)}h
and
d_{0}h=0
, or in other words
B_{3}^{1,0}=B_{2}^{1,0}
.

To show that
E^{1,0}_{3}=Z_{3}^{1,0}/B_{3}^{1,0}\cong 0
, let us first consider the exterior differential of an arbitrary form
\alpha\in Z_{3}^{1,0}
:

\displaystyle d\alpha=d_{(0)}\alpha+d_{(1)}\alpha+d_{(2)}\alpha=0+d_{0}\beta_{1}+d_{(2)}\alpha=d\beta_{1}-d_{(1)}\beta_{1}-d_{(2)}\beta_{1}+d_{(2)}\alpha=d\beta_{1}

since
d_{(2)}\colon K_{(2)}^{1}\to K_{(4)}^{2}
is the zero map. This means that the form
d(\alpha-\beta_{1})=0
, that is
\alpha-\beta_{1}
is a closed form. We know that
H^{1}(\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}),d)=0
for any
\kappa
, and so
\alpha-\beta_{1}
is also exact, that is
\exists\,h\in C^{\infty}(G_{\kappa})
such that

\displaystyle\alpha-\beta_{1}=dh=d_{(1)}h+d_{(2)}h\ \longleftrightarrow\ \alpha=d_{(1)}h\in K_{(1)}^{1}\text{ and }\beta_{1}=d_{(2)}h\in K_{(2)}^{1}\,.

We have therefore shown that for any
\alpha\in Z_{3}^{1,0}
, there exists
h\in K_{(0)}^{0}
such that
d_{(1)}h=\alpha
, that is
Z_{3}^{1,0}=B_{3}^{1,0}
.

\mathbf{p=3}

By definition, we have that
\alpha\in Z_{3}^{3,-1}
if
d_{0}\alpha=0
and there exist
\beta_{1}\in K_{(4)}^{2}
and
\beta_{2}\in K_{(5)}^{2}
such that
d_{(1)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{1}
and
d_{(2)}\alpha=d_{0}\beta_{2}+d_{(1)}\beta_{1}
. Again, since
K_{(4)}^{2}=K_{(5)}^{2}=0
and
d_{0}\alpha=0
for any
\alpha\in K_{(3)}^{2}
, we have that
\alpha\in Z_{3}^{3,-1}
if
d_{(1)}\alpha=0
. In other words,
Z^{3,-1}_{3}=Z^{3,-1}_{2}
is the space of closed forms in
K_{(3)}^{2}
. On the other hand, we have
\beta\in B^{3,-1}_{3}
if there exist
\gamma_{i}\in K_{(3-i)}^{1}
with
i=0,1,2
such that
\beta=d_{0}\gamma_{0}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{1}+d_{(2)}\gamma_{2}
and
d_{0}\gamma_{1}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{2}=d_{0}\gamma_{2}=0
. Since
K_{(3)}^{1}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\beta\in B^{3,-1}_{3}\longleftrightarrow\exists\,\gamma_{1}\in K_{(2)}^{1},\,\gamma_{2}\in K_{(1)}^{1}\text{ s.t. }\beta=d_{(1)}\gamma_{1}+d_{(2)}\gamma_{2}\text{ and }d_{0}\gamma_{1}+d_{(1)}\gamma_{2}=d_{0}\gamma_{2}=0\,.

We now want to show that
B_{3}^{3,-1}=Z_{3}^{3,-1}
, and hence
E^{3,-1}_{3}\cong 0
. Since
H^{2}(\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}),d)=0
for any
\kappa
, we have that any
\alpha\in Z_{3}^{3,-1}
is a closed and hence exact form, that is
\exists\,\beta=\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}\in K^{1}
such that
d\beta=\alpha
. Here, we are taking
\beta_{1}\in K_{(1)}^{1}
and
\beta_{2}\in K_{(2)}^{1}
, and so

\displaystyle d\beta=d\beta_{(1)}+d\beta_{(2)}=d_{(1)}\beta_{(1)}+d_{(2)}\beta_{(1)}+d_{(0)}\beta_{2}+d_{(1)}\beta_{2}=\alpha\in K_{(3)}^{2}

which implies that
d_{(1)}\beta_{1}+d_{0}\beta_{2}=0
since it is an element of
K_{(2)}
, and
d_{0}\beta_{1}=0
since
d_{0}\colon K_{(1)}^{1}\to K_{(1)}^{2}
is the zero map.
For any
\alpha\in Z_{3}^{3,-1}
, it is therefore sufficient to take
\gamma_{2}=\beta_{1}\in K_{(1)}^{1}
and
\gamma_{1}=\beta_{2}\in K_{(2)}^{1}
to show that
\alpha\in B^{3,-1}_{3}
.

\mathbf{p=4}

As already pointed out in the computations of the previous page, we have that
E_{3}^{4,-1}=E_{3}^{4,-1}\cong H^{3}(\Omega^{\bullet}(G_{\kappa}),d)
.

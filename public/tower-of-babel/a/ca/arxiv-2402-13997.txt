THE DISTRIBUTION OF gcd ⁡ ( n , φ ⁡ ( n ) ) \gcd(n,\varphi(n)) Joshua Stucky


Abstract
Let φ ⁡ ( n ) \varphi(n) denote Euler’s phi function. In this paper, we study the distribution of the numbers ( n , φ ⁡ ( n ) ) (n,\varphi(n)) and their divisors. Our results generalize previous results of Erdős [ 2 ] and Pollack [ 6 ] .



1. Introduction

Let
\varphi(n)
denote Euler’s phi function. Szele
[
9
]
showed that the condition
(n,\varphi(n))=1
is equivalent to the uniqueness of a group of order
n
, and Erdős
[
2
]
subsequently showed that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,\varphi(n))=1\end{subarray}}1\sim\frac{x}{e^{\gamma}\log_{3}x}.

(1.1)

Here and throughout,
\log x=\log_{1}x
is the natural logarithm,
\log_{k}x=\log(\log_{k-1}(x))
is the
k
-fold iterated natural logarithm, and
\gamma
denotes the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Pollack
[
6
]
has given an asymptotic expansion for the sum (
1.1
) of the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,\varphi(n))=1\end{subarray}}1=\frac{x}{e^{\gamma}\log_{3}x}\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{c_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}\bigg)+O_{K}\left(\frac{x}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}\right)

(1.2)

for any integer
K\geq 1
, where
c_{k}
are certain arithmetic constants. Note that by Möbius inversion,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,\varphi(n))=1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{d\leq x}\mu(d)\mathcal{A}_{d}(x),

where
\mathcal{A}_{d}(x)
denotes the congruence sum

\mathcal{A}_{d}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
d\mid(n,\varphi(n))\end{subarray}}1.

In this paper, we consider the more general sums

S_{g}(x):=\sum_{d\leq x}g(d)\mathcal{A}_{d}(x),

where
g
is a multiplicative function. One could consider a variety of arithmetic functions
g
in the sum above. For clarity of presentation, we restrict our attention to bounded
g
. This is a fairly natural class of arithmetic functions that already allows us to give some interesting applications. Letting
f(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}g(d)
, we have

S_{g}(x)=\sum_{n\leq x}f((n,\varphi(n))).

Our main theorem, Theorem
1.1
, furnishes an asymptotic for the sum
S_{g}
. When
f
(or, equivalently,
g
) is suitably regular over primes, we obtain an asymptotic expansion of
S_{g}(x)
with a level of precision comparable to (
1.2
). By “suitably regular”, we mean that we can evaluate the partial sums
\sum_{p\leq x}g(p)p^{-1}
with a level of precision akin to the Prime Number Theorem. For all the specific
g
we consider,
g
will be essentially constant on primes in the sense that the following estimate holds: there are constants
x_{0},C_{1},C_{2}
such that for any
A>0
and all
x>x_{0}
, we have

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{g(p)}{p}=C_{1}\log_{2}x+C_{2}+O_{A}\left(\frac{1}{(\log x)^{A}}\right).

Note that if
f
is multiplicative with
f(n)\in[0,1]
for all
n
, then by Möbius inversion,
\left|g(n)\right|\leq 1
. In particular, Theorem
1.1
allows us to obtain information about the distribution of
(n,\varphi(n))
in any set
\mathcal{B}
whose indicator function is multiplicative. Examples of such sets include

•

sums of two squares (counted without multiplicity of representations)

•

r
th powers (e.g. squares, cubes, etc.)

•

r
-free numbers (e.g. squarefree or cubefree integers)

•

smooth numbers (i.e. integers free of large prime factors)

•

rough numbers (i.e. integers free of small prime factors)

If we instead take
g
itself to be the indicator function of a set
\mathcal{B}
such that
g
is multiplicative, we obtain information about the distribution of the divisors of
(n,\varphi(n))
that belong to
\mathcal{B}
. In particular, if
\mathcal{B}=\mathbb{N}
(i.e.
g(n)=1
), then we are able to calculate the average order of the divisor function
\tau
over
(n,\varphi(n))
(in fact, this was the original motivation for the present work). In Section
7
, we give a variety of applications of Theorem
1.1
.

To further motivate our results, consider the following heuristic argument. Let
P(d)
denote the largest prime factor of
d
. When
n
is of size
x
, the condition
d\mid(n,\varphi(n))
is roughly equivalent to
d\mid n
and
P(d)\leq\log_{2}x
. This phenomenon is apparent in Erdős’
[
2
]
proof of (
1.1
) and has been described more precisely by Erdős, Luca, and Pomerance (see Theorem 8 of
[
1
]
). Heuristically, then, we expect

S_{g}(x)\sim x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq x\\
P(d)\leq\log_{2}x\end{subarray}}\frac{g(d)}{d}.

(1.3)

When
g
is multiplicative and does not grow too quickly, we expect the sum to be well-approximated by the corresponding product over primes, and so we conjecture that

S_{g}(x)\sim x\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg).

(1.4)

Our main theorem establishes this conjecture in a quantitative form for bounded multiplicative functions.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g
be a multiplicative function such that
\left|g(n)\right|\leq G
for all
n
and some constant
G>0
. For any
K\geq 1
, as
x\to\infty
, we have

S_{g}(x)=x\bigg(\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)\exp\left(\mathcal{Q}_{g}(x)\right)+O_{G,K}\left(\frac{x}{(\log_{3}x)^{K}}\right).

where

\mathcal{Q}_{g}(x)=-\sum_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}+\sum_{p>\log_{2}x}\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right).

Since
g
is bounded, we immediately obtain the following corollary to Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let the hypotheses be as in Theorem
1.1
. Then

S_{g}(x)=x\bigg(\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)\left(1+O_{G}\left(\frac{1}{\log_{3}x}\right)\right)+O_{G,K}\left(\frac{x}{(\log_{3}x)^{K}}\right).

Proof.

Note that
t\mapsto t(\log x)^{1/t}
is decreasing for
t\leq\log_{2}x
. As well, since
1-e^{-t}\leq t
for all
t\geq 0
, we have

1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\leq\frac{\log_{2}x}{p}.

Thus

\left|\mathcal{Q}_{g}(x)\right|\leq G\frac{\pi(\log_{2}x)}{e\log_{2}x}+G\sum_{p>\log_{2}x}\frac{\log_{2}x}{p^{2}}\ll_{G}\frac{1}{\log_{3}x}.

∎

Remark.
It is possible for the product in Theorem
1.1
to be zero, as can be seen by choosing any multiplicative function
g
such that
g(2^{a})=-1
for
a\geq 1
. In this case, Theorem
1.1
only gives an upper bound for
S_{g}
, rather than an asymptotic. The author thanks Greg Martin for this observation.

2. Preliminaries

In this section, we collect together some preliminary results we will need in our analysis. We begin with the following simple lemma which gives a useful divisibility property for
\varphi
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any positive integers
a
and
b
, we have
\varphi(a)\mid\varphi(ab)
. In particular, if
d
is a positive integer with
d\nmid\varphi(ab)
, then
d\nmid\varphi(a)
.

Proof.

We write
b=b_{a}b^{\prime}
, where
b_{a}
is the largest divisor of
b
supported on the primes dividing
a
. Then

\varphi(ab)=\varphi(ab_{a})\varphi(b^{\prime})=b_{a}\varphi(a)\varphi(b^{\prime})

and the claim follows.
∎

We will need the following forms of Mertens’ theorems. These are straightforward consequences of the prime number theorem with classical error term
O(x\exp(-C\sqrt{\log x}))
.

Lemma 2.2
.

There is an absolute constant
c
such that, for all
x\geq 3
,

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p}=\log_{2}x+c+O(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log x})).

(2.1)

We also have

\prod_{p\leq x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}=e^{\gamma}\log x+O(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log x})).

(2.2)

Next, we need Rankin’s trick in the following form.

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
x\geq y\geq 10
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d>x\\
P(d)\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d}\ll\frac{(\log y)^{3}}{x^{\frac{1}{\log y}}}.

(2.3)

Proof.

Let
\sigma=\frac{1}{\log y}
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d>x\\
P(d)\leq y\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d}\leq\frac{1}{x^{\sigma}}\sum_{P(d)\leq y}\frac{1}{d^{1-\sigma}}=\frac{1}{x^{\sigma}}\prod_{p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{1-\sigma}}\right)^{-1}.

Since
y\geq 10
, (
2.2
) yields

\prod_{p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{1-\sigma}}\right)^{-1}\leq 20\prod_{10<p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{e}{p}\right)^{-1}\leq\prod_{10<p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-3}\ll(\log y)^{3}.

∎

In the course of proving our results (specifically Proposition
3.2
), we will need to use the Fundamental Lemma of Sieve Theory. Of the versions available, we will use the one due to Koukoulopolus (see
[
4
]
, Theorem 18.11). The following lemma, which follows from the fundamental lemma, is stated without proof in a slightly different form in
[
5
]
. For completeness, we include a short proof.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
and
Z
be such that
2\leq Z\leq X^{1/2e^{2}}
, and let
\mathcal{Q}
be a set of primes not exceeding
Z
. Let
(u,v]
be an interval with
v-u=X
. For each
q\in\mathcal{Q}
, choose a residue class
a_{q}\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ q\right)
. Let
N(u,v;\mathcal{Q})
be the number of integers
n\in(u,v]
for which
n\not\equiv a_{q}\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ q\right)
for any
q\in\mathcal{Q}
. Then

N(u,v;\mathcal{Q})=X\prod_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left(1+O\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\log X}{\log Z}\right)\right)\right).

Proof.

By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, we may choose an integer
a_{0}
such that
a_{q}\equiv a_{0}\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ q\right)
for each
q\in\mathcal{Q}
. We then sieve the sequence
n-a_{0}
since, for
q\in\mathcal{Q}
,
q\mid n-a_{0}
if and only if
n\equiv a_{0}\equiv a_{q}\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ q\right)
. Thus

N(u,v;\mathcal{Q})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u<n\leq v\\
(n-a_{0},\mathcal{Q})=1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u-a_{0}<n\leq v-a_{0}\\
(n,\mathcal{Q})=1\end{subarray}}1,

where the condition
(n,\mathcal{Q})=1
means that
n
is free of primes in
\mathcal{Q}
. To apply Theorem 18.11 of
[
4
]
, we note that for any
d
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u-a_{0}<n\leq v-a_{0}\\
n\equiv 0\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ d\right)\end{subarray}}1=\frac{X}{d}+O(1).

Moreover, for any
2\leq y_{1}<y_{2}\leq Z
, we have

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathcal{Q}\cap(y_{1},y_{2}]\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\leq\prod_{y_{1}<p\leq y_{2}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\log y_{2}}{\log y_{1}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{\log y_{1}}\right)\right)

by Lemma
2.2
. Thus Axioms 1 and 2 of Theorem 18.11 of
[
4
]
are satisfied. We take
u=\frac{\log X}{2\log Z}
in the theorem and note that
u^{-u/2}\leq e^{-u}
so long as
\log u\geq 2
. This is equivalent to
Z\leq X^{1/2e^{2}}
.

∎

To understand the product over primes appearing in Lemma
2.4
, we need the following result of Pomerance
[
8
]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
a,m
be positive integers with
(a,m)=1
and let
p_{a,m}
denote the least prime
p\equiv a\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ m\right)
. Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv a\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ m\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\log_{2}x}{\varphi(m)}+\frac{1}{p_{a,m}}+O\left(\frac{\log(2m)}{\varphi(m)}\right).

In particular,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq x\\
p\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ m\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\log_{2}x}{\varphi(m)}+O\left(\frac{\log(2m)}{\varphi(m)}\right).

3. Main Technical Propositions

In this section, we prove several results that we will use to evaluate sums involving the divisibility conditions
d\mid\varphi(n)
and
d\nmid\varphi(n)
. The first is a simple consequence of the Brun-Titchmarsh inequality.

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
x>1
and
p<x
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p\mid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1\ll\frac{x\log_{2}x}{p}.

Proof.

If
p\mid\varphi(n)
, then either
p^{2}\mid n
or
n
is divisible by a prime
q\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
. The Brun-Titchmarsh inequality and partial summation then gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p\mid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1\leq\frac{x}{p^{2}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{q}\ll\frac{x\log_{2}x}{p}.

∎

The following proposition allows us to evaluate asymptotically sums involving divisibility conditions on
\varphi(n)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
x
be sufficiently large. Denote by
\alpha
and
\beta
the functions

\alpha(d)=\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1-\frac{1}{p-1}\right),\qquad\beta(d)=\sum_{p\mid d}\frac{\log p}{p}.

(3.1)

Let
d
be a positive squarefree integer such that the following hold:

(i)

d
is odd and
1<d\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\log x}\right)
;

(ii)

\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}
is bounded.

Then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(\varphi(n),d)=1\end{subarray}}1=\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1-\alpha(d)}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\right)\right).

(3.2)

If
\omega(d)\leq K
for some
K\geq 1
, then

\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\ll_{K}\frac{\log p(d)}{p(d)}.

(3.3)

Proof.

Let

\mathcal{Q}=\bigcup_{p\mid d}\left\{q:q\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)\right\},\qquad Q(z)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in\mathcal{Q}\\
q\leq z\end{subarray}}q

and note that by the Chinese Remainder Theorem and Lemma
2.5
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\mid d\\
l>1\end{subarray}}\mu(l)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ l\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\mid d\\
l>1\end{subarray}}\mu(l)\bigg(\frac{\log_{2}x}{\varphi(l)}+O\left(\frac{\log l}{\varphi(l)}\right)\bigg)

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha(d))\log_{2}x+O\bigg(\sum_{l\mid d}\frac{\log l}{\varphi(l)}\bigg).

To estimate the error term, let

g_{d}(s)=\sum_{l\mid d}\frac{1}{\varphi(l)^{s}}.

By the well-known inequalities

\frac{l}{\log_{2}l}\ll\varphi(l)\leq l,

we have
\log l\asymp\log\varphi(l)
, and so the error term above is

\asymp\sum_{l\mid d}\frac{\log\varphi(l)}{\varphi(l)}=-g_{d}^{\prime}(1).

But for
d
squarefree,

g_{d}(s)=\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1+\frac{1}{(p-1)^{s}}\right)=\exp\bigg(\sum_{p\mid d}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{(p-1)^{s}}\right)\bigg),

and so

-g_{d}^{\prime}(1)=\bigg(\sum_{p\mid d}\frac{\log(p-1)}{p}\bigg)\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)=\frac{d}{\varphi(d)}\sum_{p\mid d}\frac{\log(p-1)}{p}<\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}.

Thus

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}=(1-\alpha(d))\log_{2}x+O\left(\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\right).

(3.4)

Let
J\geq 2e^{2}
be a parameter to be chosen. For the upper bound in (
3.2
), we note that the condition
(\varphi(n),d)=1

n

p\mid d
. In particular,
n
is free of all such primes
q\leq x^{1/J}
. By Lemma
2.4
with
z=x^{1/J}
, the number of integers up to
x

Q(z)
is

x\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{1/J}\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left(1+O\left(e^{-J}\right)\right).

To evaluate the product over primes, we first write

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{1/J}\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)=\exp\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{1/J}\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\bigg)=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{1/J}\\

q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q^{2}}\bigg)\bigg).

The error term is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{1/J}\\

and so

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{1/J}\\

q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}\bigg)\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p(d)}\right)\right).

By (

), the remaining sum over

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{1/J}\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}=(1-\alpha(d))(\log_{2}x-\log J)+O\left(\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\right).

and thus

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{1/J}\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)=\frac{J^{1-\alpha(d)}}{(\log x)^{1-\alpha(d)}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\right)\right),

(3.5)

since

\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\geq\beta(d)\geq\frac{1}{p(d)}.

for

. Therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(\varphi(n),d)=1\end{subarray}}1\leq\frac{J^{1-\alpha(d)}x}{(\log x)^{1-\alpha(d)}}\left(1+O\left(e^{-J}+\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\right)\right).

For the lower bound, note that if
(n,d)=1

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\

(n,dQ(x))=1\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,dQ(x^{1/J}))=1\end{subarray}}1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\

(3.6)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\

(n,Q(x))>1\\

Reasoning as before, the first sum is

\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}\frac{J^{1-\alpha(d)}x}{(\log x)^{1-\alpha(d)}}\left(1+O\left(e^{-J}+\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\right)\right).

The hypotheses of the proposition imply that

\frac{\varphi(d)}{d}=\exp\bigg(\sum_{p\mid d}\log\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\bigg)=\exp(O(\beta(d)))=1+O(\beta(d)),

and so

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,dQ(x^{1/J}))=1\end{subarray}}1=\frac{J^{1-\alpha(d)}x}{(\log x)^{1-\alpha(d)}}\left(1+O\left(e^{-J}+\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\right)\right).

To bound the second sum in (
3.6
), note that the conditions
(n,Q(x))>1
,
(n,Q(x^{1/J}))=1
induce a factorization
n=AB
, where
A
is the largest divisor of
n

Q(x)/Q(x^{1/J})
. Every such
A

A\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ l\right)
for some
l\mid d
,
l>1
. We fix
B
and
l
and count the number of corresponding
A
. Note that
1<A\leq x/B
and that every prime dividing
A
exceeds
x^{1/J}
. As well,
A=1+la
for some
a<x/lB
. If we assume for the moment that
\frac{\log x}{\log l}\geq 2J
, then
x/lB>x^{1/2J}
since
B<x^{1-1/J}
. For any prime
q\leq x^{1/2J}
with
q\nmid l
, we have
a\equiv\overline{l}(A-1)\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ q\right)
. Since every prime that divides
A
is at least
x^{1/J}
, we have
A\not\equiv 0\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ q\right)
, and so
a\not\equiv-\overline{l}\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ q\right)
. Applying Lemma
2.4
to
a
with
a_{q}\equiv-\overline{l}\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ q\right)
, the number of
A
corresponding to a given
B
and
l
is

\ll\frac{x}{lB}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x^{1/2J}\\
q\nmid l\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\leq\frac{x}{lB}\prod_{q\leq x^{1/2J}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)\prod_{q\mid l}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{-1}\ll\frac{xJ}{\varphi(l)B\log x}.

Summing over
l
, the number of
A
corresponding to a given
B
is

\ll\frac{xJ}{B\log x}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\mid d\\
l>1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(l)}.

Mertens’ theorem then gives

\displaystyle\sum\frac{1}{B}\leq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\not\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q}\right)

\displaystyle\ll\log x\exp\bigg(-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}\bigg)

\displaystyle\ll(\log x)^{\alpha(d)}

Therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,Q(x))>1\\
(n,Q(x^{1/J}))=1\end{subarray}}1\ll\frac{xJ}{(\log x)^{1-\alpha(d)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l\mid d\\
l>1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(l)}.

Letting
\theta(d)
denote the sum over
l
, we have shown

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(\varphi(n),d)=1\end{subarray}}1=\frac{J^{1-\alpha(d)}x}{(\log x)^{1-\alpha(d)}}\left(1+O\left(e^{-J}+\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}+J^{\alpha(d)}\theta(d)\right)\right).

Since
p(d)\geq 3
, we have

\alpha(d)=\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1-\frac{1}{p-1}\right)\geq\exp\bigg(-\sum_{p\mid d}\frac{2}{p-1}\bigg)\geq\exp\left(-\frac{4\beta(d)}{\log p(d)}\right)\geq 1-\frac{4\beta(d)}{\log p(d)},

and also

\displaystyle 1+\theta(d)=\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1+\frac{1}{p-1}\right)\leq\exp\bigg(\sum_{p\mid d}\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(\frac{2\beta(d)}{\log p(d)}\right)

\displaystyle=1+O\left(\frac{\beta(d)}{\log p(d)}\right).

That is, both
1-\alpha(d)
and
\theta(d)
are
O\left(\frac{\beta(d)}{\log p(d)}\right)
. If
p(d)\geq e^{2e^{2}}
, we choose
J=\log p(d)
so that the hypotheses of the proposition ensure that
\frac{\log x}{\log l}\geq 2J\geq 4e^{2}
for every
l\mid d
,
l>1
and
x
sufficiently large. Since
\alpha(d)\leq 1
, we then have
J^{\alpha(d)}\theta(d)\ll\beta(d)
and

J^{1-\alpha(d)}=\exp((1-\alpha(d))\log_{2}p(d))=1+O(\beta(d)).

Therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(\varphi(n),d)=1\end{subarray}}1=\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1-\alpha(d)}}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\beta(d)d}{\varphi(d)}\right)\right),

if
p(d)\geq e^{2e^{2}}
. If
p(d)<e^{2e^{2}}
, the
\beta(d)\gg 1
, and so (
3.2
) can only be an upper bound. In this case, the hypotheses of the proposition and (
3.4
) imply that there is an absolute constant
C>0
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq x\\
q\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}\geq(1-\alpha(d))\log_{2}x-C.

The desired upper bound then follows from Brun’s sieve, specifically in the form of Theorem 2.3 of
[
3
]
.

To prove (
3.3
), we simply note that if
\omega(d)\leq K
, then

\sum_{p\mid d}\frac{\log p}{p}\ll K\frac{\log p(d)}{p(d)}.

and

\frac{d}{\varphi(d)}=\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\leq\prod_{r=1}^{K}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{r}}\right)^{-1},

where
p_{r}
denotes the
r
th prime. We have
p_{r}\ll r\log r
, and so (
2.2
) gives

\prod_{r=1}^{K}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{r}}\right)^{-1}\ll\log p_{K}\ll\log K.

∎

The asymptotic formula (
3.2
) is not quite suitable for our purposes. To put it into a form more amenable for calculations, we need the following estimate for the function
\alpha(d)
appearing in Proposition
3.2
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

\alpha_{1}(d)=\sum_{p\mid d}\frac{1}{p-1}.

If
K\geq 1
and
\omega(d)\leq K
, then

\left|1-\alpha(d)-\alpha_{1}(d)\right|\leq\frac{4e^{K}}{p(d)^{2}}.

Proof.

Expanding the product in the definition of
\alpha(d)
and grouping the terms depending on the value of
\omega(g)
, we have

1-\alpha(d)=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\mid d\\
g>1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(g)}{\varphi(g)}=\alpha_{1}(d)-\sum_{k=2}^{\omega(d)}(-1)^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\mid d\\
\omega(g)=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\varphi(g)}.

The triangle inequality and binomial theorem then imply

\displaystyle\left|1-\alpha(d)-\alpha_{1}(d)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=2}^{\omega(d)}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{p\mid d}\frac{1}{p-1}\bigg)^{k}\leq\sum_{k=2}^{\omega(d)}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{\omega(d)}{p(d)-1}\right)^{k}\leq\frac{4}{p(d)^{2}}\sum_{k\geq 2}\frac{K^{k}}{k!}\leq\frac{4e^{K}}{p(d)^{2}}.

∎

Combining this with (
3.2
), we deduce the following consequences of Proposition
3.2
. These are the forms of Proposition
3.1
that we will use most often in applications.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let the notation and hypotheses be as in Proposition
3.2
and let
\alpha_{1}
be as in Lemma
3.3
. If
K\geq 1
and
\omega(d)\leq K
, and if
p(d)>2\sqrt{e^{K}\log_{2}x}
, then

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(\varphi(n),d)=1\end{subarray}}1=\frac{x}{(\log x)^{\alpha_{1}(d)}}\left(1+O_{K}\left(\frac{\log p(d)}{p(d)}+\frac{\log_{2}x}{p(d)^{2}}\right)\right),

(3.7)

We also have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
d\mid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1=x\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/(p-1)}}\right)+O_{K}\left(x\left(\frac{\log p(d)}{p(d)}+\frac{\log_{2}x}{p(d)^{2}}\right)\right)

(3.8)

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
d\nmid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1\ll x\sum_{p\mid d}(\log x)^{-1/(p-1)}.

(3.9)

Proof.

To prove (
3.7
), note that by Lemma
3.3
, we have, for some
\left|\theta\right|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1-\alpha(d)}}=\frac{1}{(\log x)^{\alpha_{1}(d)+4e^{K}\theta p(d)^{-2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\log x)^{\alpha_{1}(d)}}\exp\left(\frac{4e^{K}\theta\log_{2}x}{p(d)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\log x)^{\alpha_{1}(d)}}\left(1+O_{K}\left(\frac{\log_{2}x}{p(d)^{2}}\right)\right).

The estimate (
3.7
) now follows by inserting this into (
3.2
) and applying (
3.3
).

For (
3.8
), Möbius inversion gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
d\mid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{g\mid d}\mu(g)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(g,\varphi(n))=1\end{subarray}}1.

Applying (
3.7
), we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
d\mid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\mid d}\mu(g)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(g,\varphi(n))=1\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\end{subarray}}1+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\mid d\\
g>1\end{subarray}}\mu(g)\frac{x}{(\log x)^{\alpha_{1}(g)}}\left(1+O_{K}\left(\frac{\log p(g)}{p(g)}+\frac{\log_{2}x}{p(g)^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=x\prod_{p\mid d}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/(p-1)}}\right)+O_{K}\bigg(x\left(\frac{\log p(d)}{p(d)}+\frac{\log_{2}x}{p(d)^{2}}\right)\bigg).

To prove (
3.9
), note that if
d\nmid\varphi(n)
, then
p\nmid\varphi(n)
for some
p\mid d
. Thus

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
d\nmid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{p\mid d}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
p\nmid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1.

Applying (
3.7
) then gives (
3.9
).

∎

4. Cleaning and Decomposition of
S_{g}(x)

Recall that
g
is a multiplicative function bounded by 1. For notational simplicity, we suppress the subscript notation and write
S(x)=S_{g}(x)
. We then have

S(x)=\sum_{d\leq x}g(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
d\mid(n,\varphi(n))\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{d\leq x}g(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{d}\\
d\mid\varphi(nd)\end{subarray}}1.

To evaluate this sum, we need to perform some substantial cleaning and decomposition to remove inconvenient terms and isolate the portions of the variables
n
and
d
that govern the behavior of the sum. The net result of our manipulations, as well as the total error incurred as a result, can be seen in equation (
4.8
) at the end of this section.

For brevity, let
y=\log_{2}x
. As stated in the introduction, the condition
d\mid(n,\varphi(n))
is roughly equivalent to
d\mid n
and
P(d)\leq y
. Consequently, we expect the behavior of the congruence sums
\mathcal{A}_{d}(x)
to be governed primarily by the prime factors of
d
that are close to
y
. More precisely, it turns out that this behavior is governed by the prime factors
p
that are close to
y
on the logarithmic scale, i.e.

1-\varepsilon\leq\frac{\log p}{\log y}\leq 1+\varepsilon,

where the specific
\varepsilon
we consider depends on the desired precision (the parameter
K
) of the asymptotic expansion given in Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, we expect the prime factors
p
to affect the congruence sum
\mathcal{A}_{d}(x)
in different ways depending as
p>y
or
p<y
. This discussion motivates us to decompose the variable
d
in the following way. Let
U,V\in[1,\sqrt{y}]
be two parameters to be chosen and write

d=sm\ell,

(4.1)

where

•

P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}
,

•

p(m)>\frac{y}{V}
and
P(m)\leq Uy
,

•

p(\ell)>Uy
.

4.1. Initial Truncation

For technical reasons, it will be convenient to know that
d
is much smaller than
x
. As such, before introducing the above decomposition of
d
, we first show that the contribution of large
d
is negligible. Let

D=\exp((\log_{3}x)^{2}),\qquad D^{\prime}=(\log_{2}x)\exp(\sqrt{\log_{3}x}),

and let
S^{(1)}
be the sum
S
but restricted to
d\leq D
. We have

\left|S(x)-S^{(1)}(x)\right|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D<d\leq x\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{d}\\
P(d)\mid\varphi(nd)\end{subarray}}1=\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D<d\leq x\\
P(d)\leq D^{\prime}\end{subarray}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D<d\leq x\\
P(d)>D^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\bigg)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{d}\\
P(d)\mid\varphi(nd)\end{subarray}}1=\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2},

say. To estimate
\Sigma_{1}
, we use Rankin’s trick in the form of (
2.3
):

\Sigma_{1}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D<d\leq x\\
P(d)\leq D^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{d}\\
P(d)\mid\varphi(nd)\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d>D\\
P(d)\leq D^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{d}\ll x\frac{(\log D^{\prime})^{3}}{D^{1/\log D^{\prime}}}\ll\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log_{2}x}}.

To estimate
\Sigma_{2}
, we note that if
P(d)\mid\varphi(nd)
, then at least one of the following holds:

1.

P(d)^{2}\mid d
;

2.

P(d)\mid n
;

3.

P(d)\nmid n
and
P(d)\mid\varphi(n)
.

The contribution to
\Sigma_{2}
of the first case is

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D<d\leq x\\
P(d)>D^{\prime}\\
P(d)^{2}\mid d\end{subarray}}\sum_{n\leq\frac{x}{d}}1\leq\sum_{p>D^{\prime}}\frac{x}{p^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d>D/p^{2}\\
P(d)\leq p\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d}\ll x\sum_{p>D^{\prime}}\frac{\log p}{p^{2}}\ll\frac{x}{D^{\prime}}

and the contribution of the second case satisfies the same estimate. For the third case, we use Proposition
3.1
to see that this contribution is at most

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D<d\leq x\\
P(d)>D^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{d}\\
P(d)\mid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}D<d\leq x\\
P(d)>D^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\frac{x\log_{2}x}{dP(d)}\ll\sum_{p>D^{\prime}}\frac{x\log_{2}x}{p^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d>D\\
P(d)\leq p\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d}\ll\frac{x\log_{2}x}{D^{\prime}}.

Combining the three estimates above, we have

\Sigma_{2}\ll x\exp(-\sqrt{\log_{3}x}),

and so

S(x)=S^{(1)}(x)+O\left(x\exp(-\sqrt{\log_{3}x})\right)

(4.2)

since
\sqrt{\log_{2}x}\gg\exp(\sqrt{\log_{3}x})
.

4.2. Reduction to Primes Close to
y

Recall that we expect the behavior of the congruence sums
\mathcal{A}_{d}(x)
to be governed primarily by the prime factors of
d
that are close to
y
in the logarithmic scale. To make this precise, we begin by decomposing
S^{(1)}
using (
4.1
). We have

S^{(1)}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\leq\frac{D}{sm}\\
p(\ell)>Uy\end{subarray}}g(sm\ell)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm\ell}\\
sm\ell\mid\varphi(sm\ell n)\end{subarray}}1.

The contribution of
\ell>1
may be estimated in a similar manner as our truncation above: the summation conditions imply that
P(\ell)\mid(n,\varphi(n))
, and so arguing as before, the contribution to
S^{(1)}
of
\ell>1
is

\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<\ell\leq\frac{D}{sm}\\
p(\ell)>Uy\end{subarray}}\frac{x\log_{2}x}{sm\ell P(\ell)}\ll x\log_{2}x\bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq D\\
P(d)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{d}\bigg)\sum_{p>Uy}\frac{1}{p^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\geq 1\\
p(\ell)>Uy\\
P(\ell)\leq p\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\ell}\ll\frac{x}{U}.

Thus

S^{(1)}(x)=S^{(2)}(x)+O\left(x\exp(-\sqrt{\log_{3}x})+\frac{x}{U}\right),

(4.3)

where

S^{(2)}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}g(sm)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
sm\mid\varphi(smn)\end{subarray}}1.

Next, we show that we may ignore the condition
s\mid\varphi(smn)
with negligible error. We begin by writing

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
sm\mid\varphi(smn)\\
(n,m)=1\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
m\mid\varphi(smn)\\
(n,m)=1\end{subarray}}1-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
m\mid\varphi(smn)\\
s\nmid\varphi(smn)\\
(n,m)=1\end{subarray}}1.

To estimate the second sum, we apply (i) of Lemma
2.1
, ignore the conditions involving
m
, and then use (
3.9
) to see that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
m\mid\varphi(smn)\\
s\nmid\varphi(smn)\\
(n,m)=1\end{subarray}}1\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
s\nmid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1\ll\frac{x}{sm}\sum_{p\mid s}\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}.

Here we have used that the fact that
\log(x/sm)\asymp\log x
since
sm\leq D
. The contribution to
S^{(2)}
of these
n
is then

\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{sm}\sum_{p\mid s}\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\ll x\log y\sum_{p\leq\frac{y}{V}}\frac{1}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}.

Since the function
t\mapsto t(\log x)^{1/p}
is decreasing for
t\leq\log_{2}x
, the sum over primes is

\leq\pi\left(\frac{y}{V}\right)\frac{V}{y(\log x)^{V/y}}\ll\frac{1}{e^{V}\log y}.

Therefore

S^{(2)}(x)=S^{(3)}(x)+O\left(x\exp(-\sqrt{\log_{3}x})+\frac{x}{U}+\frac{x}{e^{V}}\right),

(4.4)

where

S^{(3)}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}g(sm)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
m\mid\varphi(mn)\\
(n,m)=1\end{subarray}}1.

Here the divisibility condition should actually be
m\mid\varphi(smn)
. However, since
p(m)>P(s)
, we have
(m,s\varphi(s))=1
, and therefore the two divisibility conditions are equivalent in our ranges of
s
and
m
.

4.3. Restriction to Squarefree Moduli

Next, we remove from
S^{(3)}
those
m
which are not squarefree as well as those
m
for which
(m,n)>1
. The contribution of those
m
which are not squarefree is at most

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{sp^{2}}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{smp^{2}}\ll\frac{xV}{y}\ll\frac{x}{\sqrt{y}}

by our restriction
V\leq\sqrt{y}
. This error may be absorbed into the error of (
4.4
), and we now consider the sum

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}g(sm)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
m\mid\varphi(mn)\end{subarray}}1=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}g(sm)\sum_{g\mid m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
m\mid\varphi(mn)\\
(n,m)=g\end{subarray}}1.

The contribution of
g>1
is trivially bounded by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\mid m\\
g>1\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{smg^{2}}\ll x\log y\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g>1\\
p(g)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(g)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{g^{2}}\ll\frac{xV\log y}{y},

and again this may be absorbed into the error term in (

). Thus

S^{(3)}(x)=S^{(4)}(x)+O\left(x\exp(-\sqrt{\log_{3}x})+\frac{x}{U}+\frac{x}{e^{V}}\right),

(4.5)

where

S^{(4)}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}g(sm)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
m\mid\varphi(mn)\\
(n,m)=1\end{subarray}}1.

4.4. Final Cleaning

Our goal is now to simplify the divisibility condition in the inner sum above. Heuristically, one expects that
(m,\varphi(m))
is the largest divisor of
m
supported on the primes
\leq\log_{2}m
(see Theorem of 8 of
[
1
]
). For
m
considered in the sums above, however, we have by the Prime Number Theorem that

\log_{2}m\leq\log_{2}\bigg(\prod_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}p\bigg)\sim\log y+\log(U+V)\ll\log y<\frac{y}{V}

if
y
is sufficiently large. Thus we expect
(m,\varphi(m))=1
for most
m
. Moreover, if
(m,n\varphi(m))=1
, then the conditions
m\mid\varphi(mn)
and
m\mid\varphi(n)
are equivalent. As such, we may replace the divisibility condition on
m
with
m\mid\varphi(n)
by making an error of at most the contribution from those
m
with
(m,\varphi(m))>1
.

To estimate this error, we note that since
m
is squarefree, if
(m,\varphi(m))>1
, then
m
is divisible by a pair of primes
p,q
such that
q\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)
. The contribution of these
m
to
S^{(4)}(x)
is bounded by

\displaystyle x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{s}\sum_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq y\\
q\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
pq\mid m\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}

\displaystyle\ll x\log y\sum_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\frac{1}{p}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\leq Uy\\
q\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ p\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{q}

\displaystyle\ll x\log y\log_{2}y\sum_{p>\frac{y}{V}}\frac{1}{p^{2}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{xV\log_{2}y}{y}.

This error may be absorbed into the error of (
4.4
). The condition
(n,m)=1
may now be removed at the cost of the same error we made when introducing it, and so

S^{(4)}(x)=S^{(5)}(x)+O\left(x\exp(-\sqrt{\log_{3}x})+\frac{x}{U}+\frac{x}{e^{V}}\right),

(4.6)

where

S^{(5)}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}g(sm)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
m\mid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1.

4.5. Final Truncation

We aim to apply Proposition
3.2
in the form of Corollary
3.4
. To do so, the variable
m
must have a bounded number of prime factors. Let
K\geq 1
be a fixed positive integer. We perform one last truncation and restrict our attention to those
m
with
\omega(m)\leq K
. We write

S^{(5)}(x)=S^{(6)}(x)+E(x),

where

S^{(6)}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
P(m)\leq y\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
\omega(m)\leq K\end{subarray}}g(sm)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq\frac{x}{sm}\\
m\mid\varphi(n)\end{subarray}}1

(4.7)

and
E(x)=S^{(5)}(x)-S^{(6)}(x)
. To estimate
E(x)
, we bound the sums over
n
trivially and use the binomial theorem to see that

\left|E(x)\right|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
\omega(m)\geq K+1\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{sm}\leq x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\geq 1\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{s}\sum_{k\geq K+1}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m>1\\
P(m)\leq Uy\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
\omega(m)=k\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{m}\ll x\log y\sum_{k\geq K+1}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\frac{1}{p}\bigg)^{k}.

Using (
2.1
) and the fact that
U,V\leq\sqrt{y}
, the sum over primes is

\log\left(\frac{\log(Uy)}{\log(y/V)}\right)+O\left(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log(y/V)})\right)\leq\mathcal{C}\frac{\log U+\log V}{\log y}.

for some positive absolute constant
\mathcal{C}
. Therefore

E(x)\ll x\log y\sum_{k\geq K+1}\frac{\mathcal{C}^{K}}{k!}\left(\frac{\log U+\log V}{\log y}\right)^{k}\ll_{K}x\log y\left(\frac{\log U+\log V}{\log y}\right)^{K+1}.

Combining all of our estimates in this section, we find that

S(x)=S^{(6)}(x)+O_{K}\left(x\exp(-\sqrt{\log_{3}x})+\frac{x}{U}+\frac{x}{e^{V}}+\log y\left(\frac{\log U+\log V}{\log y}\right)^{K+1}\right),

(4.8)

where
S^{(6)}
is given by (
4.7
).

5. Evaluation of
S^{(6)}(x)

We now apply Proposition
3.2
in the form of Corollary
3.4
to the inner sum over
n
in
S^{(6)}(x)
, and we have

\displaystyle S^{(6)}(x)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
\omega(m)\leq K\end{subarray}}\frac{g(sm)}{sm}\prod_{p\mid m}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log(x/sm))^{1/(p-1)}}\right)

\displaystyle+O_{K}\bigg(D+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\leq D\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}1<m\leq\frac{D}{s}\\
P(m)\leq Uy\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
\omega(m)\leq K\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{sm}\left(\frac{\log p(m)}{p(m)}+\frac{\log_{2}x}{p(m)^{2}}\right)\bigg).

Here the error term
O(D)
arises from the contribution of
m=1
. The error term contributes at most

\left(\frac{V\log y}{y}+\frac{V^{2}\log_{2}x}{y^{2}}\right)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 1\\
P(m)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}\frac{x}{m}\ll x\frac{V\log y(\log y+V)}{y}.

(5.1)

To evaluate the main term, we would like
\log x
in the product rather than
\log(x/sm)
. Note that

1-\frac{1}{(\log(x/sm))^{1/(p-1)}}=\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/(p-1)}}\right)\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log D}{p\log x}\right)\right)

and also

\prod_{p\mid m}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log D}{p\log x}\right)\right)=1+O\left(\frac{K\log D}{p(m)\log x}\right).

The contribution of the error term above may be absorbed into (
5.1
). Next, we extend the sums over
s
and
m
to all integers. Using Rankin’s trick in the form of (
2.3
), the contribution of
sm>D
is bounded by

x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n>D\\
P(n)\leq Uy\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}\ll\frac{x(\log y)^{3}}{D^{\frac{1}{\log(Uy)}}}\ll\frac{x}{(\log_{2}x)^{\frac{1}{4}}}.

The error term
O(D)
may be absorbed into this term, and so

\displaystyle S^{(6)}(x)

\displaystyle=x\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\geq 1\\
P(s)\leq\frac{y}{V}\end{subarray}}\operatornamewithlimits{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 1\\
P(m)\leq Uy\\
p(m)>\frac{y}{V}\\
\omega(m)\leq K\end{subarray}}\frac{g(sm)}{sm}\prod_{p\mid m}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/(p-1)}}\right)

\displaystyle+O_{K}\bigg(\frac{x}{(\log_{2}x)^{\frac{1}{4}}}+x\frac{V\log y(\log y+V)}{y}\bigg).

The condition
\omega(d)\leq K
may now be removed with the same error as when we introduced it, and the main term of
S^{(6)}(x)
may now be written as the product

x\prod_{p\leq\frac{y}{V}}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)\prod_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/(p-1)}}\right)\right).

(5.2)

We simplify the second product slightly by noting that

(\log x)^{-1/(p-1)}=(\log x)^{-1/p}\left(1+O\left(\frac{\log_{2}x}{p^{2}}\right)\right)=(\log x)^{-1/p}+O\left(\frac{\log_{2}x}{p^{2}}\right).

In the range of
p
in the second product, we then have

1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/(p-1)}}\right)=\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\right)\left(1+O\left(\frac{V}{p^{2}}\right)\right).

Then since

\prod_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\left(1+O\left(\frac{V}{p^{2}}\right)\right)=1+O\left(\frac{V^{2}}{y\log y}\right),

the product in (
5.2
) is

x\prod_{p\leq\frac{y}{V}}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)\prod_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\right)\left(1+O\left(\frac{V^{2}}{y\log y}\right)\right).

The contribution of the error term is

\ll\frac{V^{2}}{y\log y}\prod_{p\leq Uy}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\ll\frac{V^{2}}{y},

and this may be absorbed into (
5.1
). Thus

\displaystyle S^{(6)}(x)

\displaystyle=x\prod_{p\leq\frac{y}{V}}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)\prod_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+O_{K}\bigg(\frac{x}{(\log_{2}x)^{\frac{1}{4}}}+x\frac{V\log y(\log y+V)}{y}+\log y\left(\frac{\log U+\log V}{\log y}\right)^{K+1}\bigg).

Inserting the above calculations into (
4.8
), we find that

S(x)=x(\mathcal{P}(x)+\mathcal{E}(x)),

(5.3)

where

\mathcal{P}(x)=\prod_{p\leq\frac{y}{V}}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)\prod_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\right)

and

\left|\mathcal{E}(x)\right|\ll_{K}\exp(-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log_{3}x})+\frac{1}{U}+\frac{1}{e^{V}}+\log y\left(\frac{\log U+\log V}{\log y}\right)^{K+1}+\frac{V\log y(\log y+V)}{y}.

(5.4)

6. Conclusion

It remains to evaluate the product
\mathcal{P}(x)
. To do so, we first write

\mathcal{P}(x)=\mathcal{M}(x)\mathcal{P}_{1}(x)\mathcal{P}_{2}(x),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\leq y}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq y}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)^{-1}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{y<p\leq Uy}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\right).

Note that

\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)^{-1}=1-\frac{g(p)}{p}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right),

and so

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)^{-1}\left(1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right).

Thus

\mathcal{P}(x)=\mathcal{M}(x)\mathcal{P}_{1}^{*}(x)\mathcal{P}_{2}(x)+O\left(\frac{V}{y}\right),

where

\mathcal{P}_{1}^{*}(x)=\prod_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq y}\left(1-\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}\right).

We further investigate the products
\mathcal{P}_{1}^{*}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
by noting that if
p\leq y
, then

1+\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)=\exp\left(\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right),

and if
p>y
, then

1-\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}=\exp\left(-\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{1}^{*}(x)\mathcal{P}_{2}(x)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq y}\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}+\sum_{y<p\leq Uy}\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\bigg)\prod_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq Uy}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigg(-\sum_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq y}\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}+\sum_{y<p\leq Uy}\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\bigg)\left(1+O\left(\frac{V}{y\log y}\right)\right).

Arguing as in the proof of Corollary
1.2
, we have

\bigg|\sum_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq y}\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)-\sum_{y<p\leq Uy}\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}\bigg|\ll\frac{1}{\log_{3}x}.

The contribution of the error term may thus be absorbed into
\mathcal{E}(x)
in (
5.3
), and so

S(x)=x\bigg(\mathcal{M}(x)\exp\bigg(-\sum_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq y}\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}+\sum_{y<p\leq Uy}\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\bigg)+\mathcal{E}(x)\bigg),

where
\mathcal{E}(x)
satisfies (
5.4
). To complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we note that

\bigg|\sum_{p\leq\frac{y}{V}}\frac{g(p)}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}\bigg|\ll\frac{1}{e^{V}\log y}

(6.1)

and

\bigg|\sum_{p>Uy}\frac{g(p)}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\bigg|\ll\frac{1}{U\log y}.

(6.2)

As noted in the introduction,
\mathcal{M}(x)\ll\log y
, and so the contribution of the sums above may be absorbed into
\mathcal{E}(x)
. We have

\left|\mathcal{E}(x)\right|\ll_{K}\exp(-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\log_{3}x})+\frac{1}{U}+\frac{1}{e^{V}}+\log y\left(\frac{\log U+\log V}{\log y}\right)^{K+1}+\frac{V\log y(\log y+V)}{y},

and we conclude the proof of Theorem
1.1
by choosing

U=(\log_{3}x)^{K},\qquad V=\log_{3}x,

(6.3)

and so

\left|\mathcal{E}(x)\right|\ll_{K}x\frac{(\log_{4}x)^{K+1}}{(\log_{3}x)^{K}}\ll x\frac{1}{(\log_{3}x)^{K-1/2}},

say. Since
K
was arbitrary, this gives Theorem
1.1
.

7. Evaluation of Prime Sums

We now consider the expression given in Theorem
1.1
for a variety of arithmetic functions
g
. First, we need the following consequence of the Prime Number Theorem with classical error term. Throughout this section, we regard
K
is a fixed positive integer governing the precision of our asymptotic formulas.

Lemma 7.1
.

For any integer
K\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\frac{1}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log_{3}x}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{a_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{p>\log_{2}x}\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log_{3}x}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{b_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}\right),

where

\displaystyle a_{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(\log t)^{k}}{te^{t}}\,dt,

\displaystyle b_{k}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(-\log t)^{k}}{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{1/t}}\right)\,dt.

Proof.

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we set
y=\log_{2}x
and choose
U
and
V
as in (
6.3
), except with
K+2
in place of
K
in the choice of
U
. Then (
6.1
) and (
6.2
) give

\sum_{p\leq\frac{y}{V}}\frac{1}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}+\sum_{p>U\log_{2}x}\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)\ll\frac{1}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}.

To evaluate the remaining sums, we use the Prime Number Theorem in the form

\pi(t)=\int_{2}^{t}\frac{dz}{\log z}+R(t),

where

R(t)\ll t\exp(-C\sqrt{\log t}).

Integrating by parts, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\frac{y}{V}<p\leq y}\frac{1}{pe^{y/p}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\frac{y}{V}}^{y}\frac{1}{t(\log t)e^{y/t}}\,dt+O\left(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log y})\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{y<p\leq Uy}\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{pe^{y/p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{y}^{Uy}\frac{1}{t(\log t)}\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{y/t}}\right)\,dt+O\left(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log y})\right),

where the value of
C
is now slightly smaller. Changing variables
t\to\frac{y}{t}
in the first integral and
t\to yt
in the second yields

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{y}{V}}^{y}\frac{1}{t(\log t)e^{y/t}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log y}\int_{1}^{V}\frac{1}{te^{t}}\left(1-\frac{\log t}{\log y}\right)^{-1}dt,

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{Uy}\frac{1}{t(\log t)}\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{y/t}}\right)\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\log y}\int_{1}^{U}\frac{1}{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{1/t}}\right)\left(1+\frac{\log t}{\log y}\right)^{-1}dt.

If
\left|z\right|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\frac{1}{1-z}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}z^{k}+O\left(\frac{1}{z^{K+1}}\right)

uniformly in
K
. Since
t\leq V\leq U\leq\sqrt{y}
for
x
sufficiently large, we have

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{V}\frac{1}{te^{t}}\left(1-\frac{\log t}{\log y}\right)^{-1}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{1}{(\log y)^{k}}\int_{1}^{V}\frac{(\log t)^{k}}{te^{t}}\,dt+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{(\log y)^{K+1}}\right),

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{U}\frac{1}{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{1/t}}\right)\left(1+\frac{\log t}{\log y}\right)^{-1}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(\log y)^{k}}\int_{1}^{U}\frac{(\log t)^{k}}{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{1/t}}\right)dt+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{(\log y)^{K+1}}\right).

The tails of the integrals are

\int_{V}^{\infty}\frac{(\log t)^{k}}{te^{t}}\,dt\ll_{K}\frac{(\log V)^{k}}{Ve^{V}},\qquad\int_{U}^{\infty}\frac{(\log t)^{k}}{t}\left(1-\frac{1}{e^{1/t}}\right)\,dt\ll\frac{(\log U)^{k}}{U}.

Combining the above calculations now gives the claimed asymptotic expansions.

Remark.

and

7.1. Asymptotics for Indicator Functions

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a set of integers whose indicator function
f
is multiplicative. Then, as stated in the introduction, we have

f(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}g(d)

for a multiplicative function
g
with
\left|g(n)\right|\leq 1
for all
n
. More specifically, we have
g(p^{j})=f(p^{j})-f(p^{j-1})
for all
j\geq 1
, and in particular,
g(p)=f(p)-1
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{g}(x)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\frac{f(p)-1}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}+\sum_{p>\log_{2}x}\frac{f(p)-1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)

(7.1)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{f}(x)+\frac{1}{\log_{3}x}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{a_{k}-b_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}\right)

by Lemma
7.1
. Likewise

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{f(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{\gamma}\log_{3}x}\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{f(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)+O\left(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log_{3}x})\right)

by (
2.1
). Thus, for certain coefficients
c_{k}
, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,\varphi(n))\in\mathcal{B}\end{subarray}}1=\mathcal{F}(x)\frac{x}{e^{\gamma}\log_{3}x}\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{c_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}\bigg)+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}\right),

(7.2)

where

\mathcal{F}(x)=\bigg(\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{f(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)\exp(\mathcal{Q}_{f}(x)).

In fact, the coefficients
c_{k}
are exactly the coefficients in (
1.2
). We now specialize
f
to certain sets
\mathcal{B}
.

7.1.1.

If
\mathcal{B}
is the set of

, then

for all

\mathcal{Q}_{f}(x)=0
. Likewise

\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{f(p^{j})}{p^{j}}=\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{r}}\right)^{-1}=\zeta(r)+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log_{2}x)^{r-1}\log_{3}x}\right),

and so (
7.2
) gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,\varphi(n))=m^{r}\end{subarray}}1=\frac{\zeta(r)x}{e^{\gamma}\log_{3}x}\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{c_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}\bigg)+O_{K}\left(\frac{x}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}\right).

(7.3)

The above argument also works if

upon noting that

) by a different proof than that originally given by Pollack in

.

7.1.2.
r
-free Integers

If
\mathcal{B}
is the set of integers free of
r
th powers of primes, then
f(p^{j})=0
for
j\geq r
. In particular,
f(p)=1
, and so
\mathcal{Q}_{g}(x)=0
by (
7.1
). Thus

\displaystyle\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{f(p^{j})}{p^{j}}=\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\bigg(\sum_{j\leq r-1}\frac{1}{p^{j}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\gamma}x\log_{3}x}{\zeta(r)}+O\left(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log_{3}x})\right)

by (
2.2
). Therefore

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,\varphi(n))\ \text{is $r$-free}\end{subarray}}1=\frac{x}{\zeta(r)}+O_{K}\left(\frac{x}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}\right).

(7.4)

7.1.3. Sums of Squares

If
\mathcal{B}
is the set of integers representable as a sum of two squares, then

f(p^{j})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $p=2$ or $p\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)$},\\
1&\text{if $p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)$ and $j$ is even},\\
0&\text{if $p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)$ and $j$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Thus

\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{f(p^{j})}{p^{j}}=2\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq\log_{2}x\\
p\equiv 1\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq\log_{2}x\\
p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)^{-1}.

We rewrite the square of the sum as

\displaystyle\bigg(\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{f(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{-1}\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\left(1-\frac{\chi_{4}(p)}{p}\right)^{-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq\log_{2}x\\
p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)\end{subarray}}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi e^{\gamma}\log_{3}x}{2}\prod_{p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)^{-1}\left(1+O\left(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log_{3}x})\right)\right),

where
\chi_{4}
is the nontrivial Dirichlet character mod 4. Thus

\prod_{p\leq\log_{2}x}\bigg(\sum_{j\geq 0}\frac{g(p^{j})}{p^{j}}\bigg)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}B}{\sqrt{e^{\gamma}\log_{3}x}}\left(1+O\left(\exp(-C\sqrt{\log_{3}x})\right)\right),

where
B
is the usual Landau-Ramanujan constant given by

B^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)^{-1}.

To evaluate
\mathcal{Q}_{g}(x)
, we note that

\mathcal{Q}_{g}(x)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq\log_{2}x\\
p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>\log_{2}x\\
p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right).

The prime number theorem for primes
p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)
states that for
x
sufficiently large,

\pi(x;3,4)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{2}^{x}\frac{dt}{\log t}+O\left(t\exp(-C\sqrt{\log t})\right).

Arguing as in the proof of Lemma
7.1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq\log_{2}x\\
p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p(\log x)^{1/p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\log_{3}x}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{a_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p>\log_{2}x\\
p\equiv 3\,\left(\textnormal{mod}\ 4\right)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\log x)^{1/p}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\log_{3}x}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{b_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+2}}\right).

Combining all of the estimates above, we find that there are constants
d_{k}
such that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq x\\
(n,\varphi(n))\in\mathcal{B}\end{subarray}}1=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}Bx}{\sqrt{e^{\gamma}\log_{3}x}}\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{d_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}\bigg)+O_{K}\left(\frac{1}{(\log_{3}x)^{K+\frac{3}{2}}}\right).

(7.5)

7.2. The Average Number of Divisors of
(n,\varphi(n))

We conclude by studying the average number of divisors of
(n,\varphi(n))
, which was the original motivation for this work. In this case, we have
f(n)=\tau(n)
, so
g(n)=1
. Combining Theorem
1.1
and Lemma
7.1
, we have

\sum_{n\leq x}\tau((n,\varphi(n)))=e^{\gamma}x\log_{3}x\exp\bigg(\frac{1}{\log_{3}x}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\frac{b_{k}-a_{k}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}\bigg)+O_{K}\left(\frac{x}{(\log_{3}x)^{K}}\right).

In particular, there exist constants
c_{k}^{\prime}
such that

\sum_{n\leq x}\tau((n,\varphi(n)))=e^{\gamma}x\log_{3}x\bigg(1+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{c_{k}^{\prime}}{(\log_{3}x)^{k}}\bigg)+O_{K}\left(\frac{x}{(\log_{3}x)^{K}}\right).

(7.6)

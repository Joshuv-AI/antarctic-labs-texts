W-types in setoids Thanks: This work was partially funded by EPSRC grant EP/T000252/1.
Jacopo Emmenegger

Abstract
. We present a construction of W-types in the setoid model of extensional Martin-Löf type theory using dependent W-types in the underlying intensional theory. More precisely, we prove that the internal category of setoids has initial algebras for polynomial endofunctors. In particular, we characterise the setoid of algebra morphisms from the initial algebra to a given algebra as a setoid on a dependent W-type. We conclude by discussing the case of free setoids. We work in a fully intensional theory and, in fact, we assume identity types only when discussing free setoids. By using dependent W-types we can also avoid elimination into a type universe. The results have been verified in Coq and a formalisation is available on the author’s GitHub page.

1.
Introduction

The present paper is a contribution to the study of
models of extensional properties in intensional type theories
and is in particular concerned with W-types.
The W-type constructor in Martin-Löf type theory
[
ML84
,
ML82
]

produces an inductive type
whose terms can be understood as well-founded trees.
We provide a construction, verified in Coq,
of W-types in the setoid model
of extensional Martin-Löf type theory using dependent W-types
in the underlying intensional theory.
Although we work internally in intensional Martin-Löf type theory,
we present our results using the category-theoretic language.
More precisely, we consider a logical-framework presentation
of a dependent type theory with unit type,
\sum
-types,
\prod
-types
and dependent W-types and we show in
Section

4.2
that
the internal category of setoids
has initial algebras for polynomial endofunctors.
These were identified as a category-theoretic counterpart of W-types
by Moerdijk and Palmgren
[
MP00
]
.
Initiality of an algebra and the induction principle of the underlying type
are related via the notion of contractibility in
[
AGS17
]
.

Dependent W-type were introduced by Petersson and Synek
[
PS89
]
,
see also
[
NPS90
, Chapter 16]
,
to provide a constructor for general inductive data types,
and are also known as indexed W-types or tree types.
The dependent W-type constructor produces a family of mutually inductive types,
as opposed to the single inductive type produced by the ordinary W-type constructor.
Indeed, ordinary W-types are type-theoretically equivalent to dependent W-types
indexed over the unit type.
Gambino and Hyland
[
GH04
]
, see also
[
GK13
]
,
formulated a dependent version of polynomial endofunctors
and identified in their initial algebras
a category-theoretic counterpart of dependent W-types.

By setoid we mean a type equipped
with a type-valued total equivalence relation.
That these form a model of extensional Martin-Löf type theory that
interprets most type constructors
has been known for some time.
Most type constructors, as only recently Palmgren
[
Pal19
]

has provided us with a solution to the
long standing problem of interpreting a type universe,
and in fact a whole hierarchy,
in the setoid model.
As to W-types, it was again Palmgren who first showed
how to construct W-types for setoids
in intensional Martin-Löf type theory, see
[
Bre15
]
.
His approach uses extensively the elimination principle of W-types
into a type universe.
The novelty of our approach consists
in the use of dependent W-types instead.

We use dependent W-types in two crucial steps.
First, to define the (partial) equivalence relation on a W-type
that gives rise to the initial algebra setoid
W
.
Second, to characterise the setoid of morphisms of algebras
from the (candidate) initial algebra to a given algebra.
After these steps, it only takes an easy induction
to conclude initiality of
W
.
To briefly describe the steps where dependent W-types are used,
let us recall that we may regard terms of a W-type as well-founded trees:
a canonical tree is recursively specified providing its root and its immediate subtrees,

i.e.
other well-founded trees that are to be connected to the root.

The relation that we define in
3.1

stipulates that two trees are
matching if their roots are equal and
their immediate subtrees on equal branches are matching,
where by equal we mean the setoid equality.
By using dependent W-types,
this inductively defines a partial relation on the underlying W-type,
and we say that a tree is
extensional

if it is matching with itself.
Palmgren’s construction of W-types for setoids,
as well as the other constructions that we discuss below,
use the same relation, but constructed in different ways.
The initial algebra will be the setoid of extensional trees
with algebra map given by the constructor of the W-type.

Given an algebra
a
on a setoid
A
,
we then need to construct an algebra morphism
f
from
W
to
A
,
that is, a function commuting with the algebra maps,
and to show that it is the unique such.
Commutativity tells us, roughly, that
the action of
f
on an extensional tree
w
is determined,
via the algebra
a
, by the action of
f
on the immediate subtrees of
w
.
By using dependent W-types, we make precise in
Section

4.1
what it means
for a function on extensional trees
to be determined by its action on immediate subtrees,
and call these functions
telescopic
.
Actually, for the construction itself we find it more convenient
to speak of telescopic functions on immediate subtrees rather than on trees,
but here we can safely ignore the difference.
In
Section

4.1
we prove that a function on extensional trees
is an algebra morphism if and only if it is telescopic.

The characterisation in
Section

4.1
allows us to reduce the problem of finding
a unique algebra morphism, to the problem of finding a unique telescopic function.
To this aim, and thanks to the inductive nature of telescopic functions,
we directly use the elimination principle of dependent W-types.
In this sense, we believe that
Section

4.1
makes explicit,
in the case of setoids, the connection between the commutativity condition
for an algebra morphism out of the initial algebra,
and the inductive definition of the morphism itself.

In particular, a common aspect of arguments that construct W-types
is the use of the set, or setoid, of all subtrees of a tree,
usually obtained as the transitive closure of the immediate subtree relation.
This set may have a complicated construction in categories
and in intensional type theories,
but it is important for inductive arguments.
We can avoid dealing with transitive closures when proving
Section

4.1

since the definition of telescopic functions
only involves the setoid of immediate subtrees of a tree,
which has a natural and straightforward
definition as an image factorisation,
see
Sections

3.2
and
3.2
.

We also use dependent W-types to compare
the setoid of extensional trees with respect to discrete setoids
to the discrete setoid on the W-type of the underlying types.
In particular, we see in
Section

5.3

that every discrete tree with nodes from a 0-type,

i.e.
a type with decidable equality,
is extensional.
It seems that, without further assumptions,
not every discrete tree is extensional.
However, if function extensionality holds,
then it is possible to identify
the setoid of extensional trees on discrete setoids
with a subsetoid of discrete trees, see
Section

5.3
.

The setoid construction that we consider is an instance
of a quotient completion,
see
[
MR13
,
MR16
]
.
The author is aware of two other constructions of W-types
for quotient completions.
The first one, that we already mentioned,
was formulated by Palmgren for the setoid model in intensional Martin-Löf type theory and then adapted by Bressan
[
Bre15
]
to
minimalist type theory
[
Mai09
]
.
The argument requires a ‘large’ elimination principle for W-types,
in the sense that it must be possible to eliminate into a type universe
or a universe of propositions.
The second construction is due to van den Berg
[
Ber05
]

and it applies to exact completions of categories with finite limits.
In intensional Martin-Löf type theory, the assumption of finite limits is met
by the category of types
assuming function extensionality and Uniqueness of Identity Proofs,
by its full subcategory on the 0-types
assuming only function extensionality
[
UFP13
,
RS15
]
,
and by the e-category of types assuming only UIP
[
EP20
]
.
However, there is little hope that an internal category of types in a
fully intensional type theory would have ordinary finite limits.
Setoids are also closely related to groupoids.
Vidmar has given a construction of initial algebras
for polynomial endofunctors on groupoids
from initial algebras for polynomial endofunctors on sets
[
Vid18
]
.

In the next section we discuss the preliminaries needed for the construction.

Section

3
contains the construction of the algebra of
extensional trees and the definition of the setoid family of
immediate subtrees.
The proof of its initiality is in
Section

4
.
We conclude the paper with a discussion of the case of
extensional trees over discrete setoids in
Section

5
.
Each section begins with a brief overview of the content.

The Coq formalisation of the present paper is available on the author’s GitHub page
[
Emm18
]
and it includes

Sections

2.4
and
2.4
and
all numbered definitions and results in

Sections

2.5
,
3
,
4
and
5
,
except for
Section

2.5
,
a Coq proof of which can be found at
[
Pal12a
]
.

2.1.
The type-theoretic setting

We work in a logical framework formulation of
Martin-Löf type theory, see
[
NPS90
, Part III]
,
similar to the fragment of Coq that we used for the formalisation.
The logical framework consists of record types,

\prod
-types and a universe à la Russell
\mathsf{U}
,
which is the type
\mathsf{Set}
in the Coq formalisation.
Logic is interpreted according to the propositions-as-types interpretation.
When we wish to emphasise that a type is in
\mathsf{U}

we say that it is a
small type
.
Judgemental equality is denoted
\equiv
and
application of function terms will usually be denoted
by concatenation as in
f\,b
.
However, in the presence of multiple arguments we may adopt the notation with parenthesis
as in
ar(i,n,b)
.

The theory itself is then specified by declaring constants and equations.
Specifically, we require the universe
\mathsf{U}
to be closed under
\prod
-types,
and to contain the unit type
\mathsf{1}
,
\sum
-types,
and dependent W-types.
Ordinary W-types are recovered as dependent W-types over the unit type
\mathsf{1}
,
but in the Coq formalisation these are assumed to be primitive
for convenience.
In
Figures

1
and
2
we spell out
the constants and equations for (dependent) W-types
in the form of rules to increase readability.
In these rules, the premises of
W-FORM
and
DW-FORM

are left understood in the other rules.
We might as well drop (some) subscripts if they are clear from the context.
The system has
\eta
-conversion only for
\prod
-types.

Note that we do not require identity types.
These will be assumed only in
Section

5

for discussing the case of discrete setoids.
It is well-known that record types can be replaced with
\sum
-types
at the expense of readability.
In this case we would not need to assume that these coincide
with the
\sum
-types in the theory.

2.2.
W-types

W-types can be used to construct several inductive types,
including natural numbers and lists
[
Dyb97
]
,
and to give a constructive justifications
of certain theories of inductive definitions
[
Pal92
]
.
More generally, W-types provide a predicative counterpart to the notion of well-ordering.
Furthermore, they are instrumental in Aczel’s model of
Constructive Zermelo-Fraenkel set theory
[
Acz78
]
,
in the form of the type of iterative sets,
where they also allow to interpret the Regular Extension Axiom
which adds general inductive definitions to CZF
[
Acz86
]
.
The idea of a type of iterative sets is also central
in Palmgren’s interpretation of type universes in the setoid model
[
Pal19
]
.

By elimination of W-types, there are function terms

\mathsf{node}:\mathsf{W}_{B}\to A\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathsf{ist}:\prod_{w:\mathsf{W}_{B}}B\,(\mathsf{node}\,w)\to\mathsf{W}_{B}

such that
\mathsf{node}\,(\mathsf{sup}\,a\,f)\equiv a

and
\mathsf{ist}\,(\mathsf{sup}\,a\,f)\equiv f
.
Henceforth, we write
\mathsf{ist}_{w}
for
\mathsf{ist}\,w
.

We refer to terms of a W-type
\mathsf{W}_{B}
as
trees
,
to terms
a:A
as
nodes
,
to terms
b:B\,a
as
branches
and,
for every branch
b:B\,a
, to the term
\mathsf{ist}_{w}\,b
as the

immediate subtree of
w
on branch
b
.

In the set-theoretic interpretation of type theory,
W-types are sets of well-founded trees with labelled nodes.
The set
A
is the set of labels for the nodes
and, for each
a\in A
,
the set
B_{a}
consists of the branches out of the node with name
a
.
Trees are formed by providing a node
a\in A

and attaching other trees to the branches in
B_{a}
.
This procedure is formally specified by functions
f\colon B_{a}\to\mathsf{W}_{B}

which stipulate that the tree
f(b)
is attached to the branch
b\in B_{a}
.

2.3.
Dependent W-types

In the same vein,
dependent W-types can be understood as
free term algebras for infinitary
multi-sorted
signatures:
sorts are elements of
I
,
elements of
N_{i}
are function symbols with codomain sort
i

and the arity of a function symbol
a\in N_{i}
is given by the function

ar_{i,a}:B_{i,a}\to I
that maps each

b
-th argument of
a
to its sort.

Figure

4
depicts an element of a dependent W-type.
Compared to the graphical representation of trees in
Figure

3

the sort
i
of a function symbol

appears as an additional leg
pointing downwards out of the node corresponding to
a

and each branch

is labelled with the sort
ar(i,a,b)\in I
.
The dependent tree in
Figure

the signature containing two sorts
i_{0}
and
i_{1}
,
and three function symbols,

a_{0}
of sort
i_{0}
and
a_{1},a_{2}
of sort
i_{1}
,
such that
a_{0}
has empty arity,

a_{1}
has arity
i_{0}
and
i_{1}
,
and
a_{2}
has arity
i_{0}
and
i_{0}
.
This is formally specified by requiring that

,

,
a_{1},a_{2}\in N_{i_{1}}

A common presentation of the term in

Note that there must be at least one node with no branches,

i.e.

in order for these trees to be well-founded.
Indeed, in terms of signatures, these sets of trees consist of closed terms.
This is reflected in type theory:
whenever

is type-theoretically equivalent to the empty type

In order to make this precise,

or as

if propositional truncation is available,
in the sense that assuming either of them proves
that

is equivalent to
\mathsf{0}
.

Similarly to the non-dependent case,
using the elimination rule of dependent W-types,
we obtain for every

\displaystyle\mathsf{dnode}_{i}:\mathsf{DW}\,i\to N\,i

(1)

\displaystyle\mathsf{dist}_{i}:\prod_{(w:\mathsf{DW}\,i)}\prod_{(b:Br(i,\mathsf{dnode}_{i}\,w))}\mathsf{DW}\,ar(i,\mathsf{dnode}_{i}\,w,b),

(2)

such that
\mathsf{dnode}_{i}\,\mathsf{dsup}(i,n,f)\equiv n

and
\mathsf{dist}_{i}\,\mathsf{dsup}(i,n,f)\equiv f
.
We refer to
I
as the type of
indices
or sorts,
to
N
and

as the
node family
and the
branching family
, respectively,
and to

.

2.4.
Setoids and setoid families

A setoid is informally defined as a type together with
a type-theoretic equivalence relation on it.
This can be made precise in various different ways,

e.g.
considering partial relations,
or Prop-valued relations if a type of proposition is available,
or instead 0-types and mere relations.
See
[
BCP03
]
for a survey of possible definitions.
In our context we define a setoid as follows.

Definition
\thethm
.

A
setoid

A
is a list
(|A|,\approx_{A},r_{A},s_{A},t_{A})
where

|A|:\mathsf{U}
is a small type,
the type family
\approx_{A}:|A|\to|A|\to\mathsf{U}

is an equivalence relation with proofs of reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity
given by, respectively:

\displaystyle r_{A}:\prod_{a:|A|}a\approx_{A}a,\hskip 60.00009pts_{A}:\prod_{a,a^{\prime}:|A|}a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}\to a^{\prime}\approx_{A}a,

\displaystyle t_{A}:\prod_{a,a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime}:|A|}a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}\to a^{\prime}\approx_{A}a^{\prime\prime}\to a\approx_{A}a^{\prime\prime}.

The
type of setoids

\mathsf{Std}
is defined as a record on the types of

|A|,\approx_{A},r_{A},s_{A}
and
t_{A}
.

For a setoid
A
, we refer to the relation
\approx_{A}

as the
equality of
A
.

Since our theory has a unit type,
every small type
X
gives rise to a setoid on
X

with equality
\lambda a,a^{\prime}.\mathsf{1}
,
called the the
codiscrete setoid on
X
.

In
Section

5
we will have identity types available.
In this case every small type
X
can be equipped with the equality

\lambda x,x^{\prime}.x=_{X}x^{\prime}
given by the identity type.
This setoid is called the
discrete setoid on
X
.
More generally, a setoid
A
is
discrete
or (
free
) if

a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}
if and only if
a=_{|A|}a^{\prime}

for every
a,a^{\prime}:A
.

In the category of setoids that we will define below,
the codiscrete setoid over any inhabited type is a terminal object,
and it is isomorphic to the discrete setoid on
\mathsf{1}
if identity types are available.

Definition
\thethm
.

Let
A
and
B
be setoids.
A function term
f:|A|\to|B|
is

extensional (with respect to
\approx_{A}
and
\approx_{B}
)

if there is a term of type

\mathsf{ext}\,f\coloneqq\prod_{a,a^{\prime}:|A|}a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}\longrightarrow f\,a\approx_{B}f\,a^{\prime}.

(3)

We will refer to extensional function terms as extensional functions,
or simply as functions, and
we will write
|f|
for the underlying function term
of an extensional function
f
.

The setoid
A\Rightarrow B
of
extensional functions
from
A
to
B

has the type of extensional functions

\sum_{f:|A|\to|B|}\mathsf{ext}\,f

as underlying type and the equivalence relation

f\approx_{A\Rightarrow B}g\coloneqq\prod_{a:|A|}|f|\,a\approx_{B}|g|\,a

as equality.

In fact, in the Coq implementation we find it more convenient to define the type
of extensional functions as a record rather than as a
\sum
-type.
However this makes no essential difference.

Remark
\thethm
.

Both type families
\mathsf{ext}
and
\approx_{A\Rightarrow B}

defined in
2.4
are instances of a more general
binary relation on the type
|A|\to|B|
, namely

\mathsf{extgen}(f,g)\coloneqq\prod_{a,a^{\prime}:|A|}a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}\longrightarrow f\,a\approx_{B}g\,a^{\prime}.

It is easy to see that this is a partial equivalence relation on
|A|\to|B|

whose domain,
i.e.
the type of function terms
f
such that
\mathsf{extgen}(f,f)
,
is the type of extensional function
|A\Rightarrow B|
.
Furthermore, two extensional functions
f
and
g
are equal
if and only if the type
\mathsf{extgen}(|f|,|g|)
is inhabited.

In the rest of the paper we write
a:A
for a setoid
A
,
to mean
a:|A|
,
and we often not distinguish between
an extensional function
f:A\Rightarrow B

and its underlying function term
|f|:|A|\to|B|
.
We also write
f\mathop{\Rsh}\alpha:f\,a\approx_{B}f\,a^{\prime}

for the proof of extensionality of
f
applied to
\alpha:a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}
.
Occasionally, we also find it convenient
to drop the subscript from the equality of a setoid.
We do not expect these abuses of notation to lead to confusion.

Setoids provide us with a formulation of the notion of category
without equality on objects that was introduced by Aczel,
who formalised it in Lego
[
Acz93
]
.
See also
[
HS00
]
for a formalisation in Coq
that uses setoids with Prop-valued equalities.

Definition
\thethm
.

A
(locally small) e-category

\mathbb{A}
consists of
a type of objects
\Obj_{\mathbb{A}}

and a family of setoids

\Hom_{\mathbb{A}}:\Obj_{\mathbb{A}}\to\Obj_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathsf{Std}
,
of arrows,
together with function terms for identity and composition,
where the latter is extensional, in the sense that it has type

\prod_{a,b,c:\Obj_{\mathbb{A}}}\Hom_{\mathbb{A}}(b,c)\Rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{A}}(a,b)\Rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{A}}(a,c),

and with identity and associativity axioms formulated
using equalities of the family of setoids
\Hom_{\mathbb{A}}
.

An
e-functor
between two e-categories
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
consists of
a function term
F:\Obj_{\mathbb{A}}\to\Obj_{\mathbb{B}}

together with a term of type

\prod_{a,a^{\prime}:\Obj_{\mathbb{A}}}\Hom_{\mathbb{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\Rightarrow\Hom_{\mathbb{B}}(F\,a,F\,a^{\prime})

and proof terms witnessing that it is functorial
with respect to the equalities of the family of setoids
\Hom_{\mathbb{B}}
.

An
e-natural transformation
between two e-functors
F,G\colon\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{B}

consists of a term

n:\prod_{a:\Obj_{\mathbb{A}}}\Hom_{\mathbb{B}}(F\,a,G\,a)

whose action on
a:\Obj_{\mathbb{A}}
we denote as
n_{a}
, together with a term of type

\prod_{a,a^{\prime}:\Obj_{\mathbb{A}}}\prod_{f:a\Rightarrow a^{\prime}}G\,f\circ n_{a}\approx n_{a^{\prime}}\circ F\,f,

where
\approx
is the equality of
\Hom_{\mathbb{B}}(F\,a,G\,a^{\prime})
.

The names ‘e-category’, ‘e-functor’, etc. were introduced to distinguish
these concepts from the standard ones.
However, since these are the only formulations that appear in the present paper,
we will just say ‘category’ to mean ‘e-category’ and similarly for the others.

For a setoid
A
, the
discrete category

A^{\#}
on
A
is defined as follows.
Its type of objects is
|A|
and, for
a,a^{\prime}:A
,
its setoid of arrows from
a
to
a^{\prime}
is the codiscrete setoid on
the type
a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}
.
Since
\approx_{A}
is symmetric, the category
A^{\#}
is a groupoid,

i.e.
a category where every arrow is invertible.

Functors between two categories
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
and natural transformations between them
form a category
\mathsf{Fun}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
.
We denote the action of a functor
F
on an arrow
\alpha:\Hom_{\mathbb{A}}(a,a^{\prime})

as
F_{\alpha}:\Hom_{\mathbb{B}}(F\,a,F\,a^{\prime})
.

Setoids and extensional functions form a category
[
PW14
]
.

Definition
\thethm
.

The
category of setoids
is defined as follows.
The type of objects is the type of setoids
\mathsf{Std}
and the family of arrows is the family
\lambda A,B.A\Rightarrow B

of setoids of extensional functions.
Identity and composition are defined in the obvious way,
the latter will be denoted as
g\circ f
.
We abuse notation and denoted this category also as
\mathsf{Std}
.

For a setoid
A
, we define

\mathsf{Fam}\,A\coloneqq\mathsf{Fun}(A^{\#},\mathsf{Std})

and call a functor
B:\mathsf{Fam}\,A
a
setoid family over
A
.
We refer to the action of
B

on an arrow
\alpha:a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}
of
A^{\#}

as
transport along
\alpha
.
For
b:B\,a
and
b^{\prime}:B\,a^{\prime}
we define

b\approx_{\alpha}b^{\prime}\ \coloneqq\ B_{\alpha}\,b\approx_{B\,a^{\prime}}b^{\prime}.

(4)

For every extensional function
f:A^{\prime}\Rightarrow A

and every setoid family
B:\mathsf{Fam}\,A
,
there is a setoid family
f^{*}B:\mathsf{Fam}\,A^{\prime}
defined by

(f^{*}B)\,a\coloneqq B\,(f\,a)
and

(f^{*}B)_{\alpha}\coloneqq B_{(f\mathop{\Rsh}\alpha)}
.
This action extends to a functor from
\mathsf{Fam}\,A
to
\mathsf{Fam}\,A^{\prime}
.

Remark
\thethm
.

Besides the standard functoriality conditions on identities and composites,
setoid families enjoy an additional coherence.
Indeed, for every
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}:a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}
,
it is
\alpha\approx\alpha^{\prime}
as arrows in
A^{\#}
.
It follows by extensionality of
B
on arrows that

B_{\alpha}\approx_{(B\,a\Rightarrow B\,a^{\prime})}B_{\alpha^{\prime}}

for every
\alpha

\alpha^{\prime}
as above.
Indeed, setoid families in this sense are
the proof-irrelevant setoid families studied in
[
Pal12b
]
.
In particular, for every
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}:a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}
,

b:B\,a
and
b^{\prime}:B\,a^{\prime}
, it is

b\approx_{\alpha}b^{\prime}\longleftrightarrow b\approx_{\alpha^{\prime}}b^{\prime}

(5)

and, for every
\alpha:a\approx_{A}a
and
b,b^{\prime}:B\,a

It is an important feature of the category of sets in set theory

Theorem

.

For a setoid

Proof.

We describe the two functors,
leaving the straightforward verification of the equivalence to the reader.

Every setoid family

gives rise to a setoid

(\sum_{a:A}B\,a,\approx)

where the equality is

(a,b)\approx(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})\coloneqq\sum_{\alpha:a\approx_{A}a^{\prime}}b\approx_{\alpha}b^{\prime}.

The projection into
A
is extensional,
with extensionality proof also given by the first projection.
This defines an extensional function into
A
.

A natural transformation

from
B
to
C
is mapped
to the extensional function with underlying term

\lambda(a,b).(a,n_{a}\,b)
.
Its extensionality follows,
for every

, from

C_{\alpha}(n_{a}\,b)\approx_{C\,a^{\prime}}n_{a^{\prime}}\,(B_{\alpha}b)\approx_{C\,a^{\prime}}n_{a^{\prime}}\,b^{\prime}

which holds by naturality of

and extensionality of

.

Conversely, let

be a function.
Consider, for every
a:A
, the subsetoid of
B

on those

such that

,
i.e.
the type

\sum_{b:B}f\,b\approx_{A}a

with equality

(b,\_)\approx(b^{\prime},\_)\coloneqq b\approx_{B}b^{\prime}
.
This assignment extends to a setoid family
whose transport along

maps
a pair
(b,\beta)

to the pair
(b,\beta^{\prime})
, where
\beta^{\prime}:f\,b\approx_{A}a^{\prime}

is the concatenation of

and
\alpha

An arrow in
\mathsf{Std}/A
from

to some

is a function

such that

.
This gives rise to a natural transformation as follows.
For
a:A
, the underlying function term maps

(b,\beta)
to
(k\,b,\beta^{\prime})

where
\beta^{\prime}:g\,(k\,b)\approx_{A}a

is the concatenation of

This function term is clearly extensional as

As transport along

Recall that a category is

5.
Trees on discrete setoids

In this section we work with the additional assumption that
our theory has identity types
[

, Chapter 20.4]
,
see also
[
UFP13
, Chapter 1.12]
.
We unfold the definition of the setoid of extensional trees
in the case of a
discrete
setoid family,

i.e.
a setoid family coming from
an extensional function between discrete setoids.
We then compare it to the discrete setoid
on the W-type of the underlying type family.
The main result is
Section

5.3
.

In comparing the two, we will also make use of function extensionality.
This is because the functions of immediate subtrees of two matching trees
are only required to be pointwise equal,
as in the right-hand side of (
W2
).
Therefore, rather than to compare the two directly,
we prefer to identify another (total) equivalence relation on
\mathsf{W}_{|B|}
,
which is logically equivalent to the identity type on
\mathsf{W}_{|B|}

in the presence of function extensionality,
and to compare it to
\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{per}}_{B}
in the general case.

Recall that
x=_{X}x^{\prime}
denotes
the identity type of two terms
x,x^{\prime}:X
.
For a type family
Y:X\to\mathsf{U}
,
we denote as
Y_{\xi}:Y\,x\to Y\,x^{\prime}
the transport function term
given by identity elimination on
\xi:x=_{X}x^{\prime}
,
and we write
y=_{\xi}y^{\prime}
for the identity type

Y_{\xi}\,y=_{(Y\,x^{\prime})}y^{\prime}
.

5.1.
Discrete setoid families

We begin by identifying those setoid families that
correspond, under

2.4
, to functions between discrete setoids.

Definition
\thethm
.

A setoid family

is the transport function
of the underlying type family

of

The equivalence in

between the full subcategory of

on the functions into

N\coloneqq\lambda(w,w^{\prime}).\mathsf{node}\,w=_{X}\mathsf{node}\,w^{\prime}:\mathsf{W}_{Y}\times\mathsf{W}_{Y}\to\mathsf{U}

branching family

Br\coloneqq\lambda(w,w^{\prime}),p.\sum_{y,y^{\prime}}y=_{\xi}y^{\prime}:\prod_{I}N\,(w,w^{\prime})\to\mathsf{U}

and arity function

ar:=\lambda(w,w^{\prime}),p,(y,y^{\prime},q).(\mathsf{ist}_{w}\,y,\mathsf{ist}_{w^{\prime}}\,y^{\prime}):\prod_{I}\prod_{N}Br((w,w^{\prime}),p)\to\mathsf{U}.

It is possible to show that
\mathsf{ptEq}_{Y}
is a symmetric and transitive relation on
\mathsf{W}_{Y}

as for
\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{per}}_{B}
in

3.1
.
Reflexivity follows from the following lemma.

Lemma
\thethm
.

For every
w,w^{\prime}:\mathsf{W}_{Y}

w=_{\mathsf{W}_{Y}}w^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathsf{ptEq}_{Y}\,w\,w^{\prime}.

Thus we have a setoid
\widetilde{\mathsf{W}}_{Y}
of pointwise-equal trees,
that is the type
\mathsf{W}_{Y}
with
\mathsf{ptEq}
as equality,
together with an extensional function

(31)

from the discrete setoid on
\mathsf{W}_{Y}

which is in fact a (canonical) quotient map in
\mathsf{Std}
, cf.
Section

3.2
.

Proof of
Section

5.2
.

The proof is by induction on
w\equiv\mathsf{sup}\,x\,f
and
the inductive hypothesis tells us that if a tree is equal
to an immediate subtree of
w

By induction on
r:w=w^{\prime}
, it is enough to show that
\mathsf{ptEq}_{Y}\,w\,w
.
A canonical term is given taking the canonical proof of
x=_{X}x
and
proving that, for every

, if
y=_{(Y\,x)}y^{\prime}

then the trees
f\,y
and
f\,y^{\prime}
are pointwise equal.
This follows from the inductive hypothesis using the fact that every function term—
f
in particular, is extensional with respect to the identity type.
∎

Unsurprisingly,
it is for the converse that we need function extensionality.

Lemma
\thethm
.

Assume that the theory has function extensionality,

\mathsf{funext}
as given in the rule below.

\displaystyle\penalty X,Y:\mathsf{U}\hskip 20.00003ptf,g:X\to Y\hskip 20.00003ptH:\prod_{x:X}f\,x=_{Y}g\,x

\displaystyle\rule{0.0pt}{10.0pt}\mathsf{funext}\,H:f=_{(X\to Y)}g\hskip 20.00003pt

Then for every

Let
w\equiv\mathsf{sup}\,x\,f
and
w^{\prime}\equiv\mathsf{sup}\,x^{\prime}\,f
’.
The proof is by induction on the term
E:\mathsf{ptEq}\,w\,w^{\prime}
.
To conclude

it is enough to show that there is
\xi:x=_{X}x^{\prime}

such that
f=f^{\prime}\circ Y_{\xi}
.
The existence of

follows by definition of

.
By function extensionality and the inductive hypothesis it is then enough to show that,

the immediate subtrees

and
f^{\prime}\,(Y_{\xi}\,y)
are pointwise equal.
This also follows by definition of

It follows that, in the presence of function extensionality,

5.3.
Equivariant trees

Now we look for a characterisation of the setoid
W_{B}
of extensional trees
of a discrete setoid family
B
as subsetoid of
\widetilde{W}_{|B|}
,

i.e.
as a setoid of pointwise equal trees.

Definition
\thethm
.

Let
Y:X\to\mathsf{U}
be a type family.
A tree
w\equiv\mathsf{sup}\,x\,f:\mathsf{W}_{Y}
is
equivariant

if, for every
l:x=_{X}x
and every
y:Y\,x
,
the immediate subtrees
f\,(Y_{l}\,y)
and
f\,y
are pointwise equal,
and every immediate subtree of
w
is equivariant.

The type family
\mathsf{isEquivariant}:\mathsf{W}_{Y}\to\mathsf{U}
of proofs
that a tree is equivariant is defined as the
dependent W-type in
I\coloneqq\mathsf{W}_{Y}
with families of nodes and branches

N\coloneqq\lambda w.\prod_{(l:\mathsf{node}\,w=_{X}\mathsf{node}\,w)}\prod_{(y:Y\,(\mathsf{node}\,w))}\mathsf{ptEq}_{Y}(f\,(Y_{l}\,y),f\,y),\hskip 30.00005ptBr\coloneqq\lambda w,\_.Y\,(\mathsf{node}\,w)

and arity function

ar\coloneqq\lambda w,\_,y.\mathsf{ist}_{w}\,y.

Let
\mathsf{EqvTrees}_{Y}
be the subsetoid of
\widetilde{\mathsf{W}}_{Y}
on the equivariant trees,
that is to say, two equivariant trees are equal in
\mathsf{EqvTrees}_{Y}

if they are pointwise equal.

Let also
\mathsf{EqvTrees}^{=}_{Y}
be the subsetoid of
\mathsf{W}_{Y}
on the equivariant trees,
that is,
two equivariant trees
w
and
w^{\prime}
ar equal in
\mathsf{EqvTrees}^{=}_{Y}

if
w=_{\mathsf{W}_{Y}}w^{\prime}
.

Let
B:\mathsf{Fam}\,X
be a discrete setoid family
over the discrete setoid on
X:\mathsf{U}
.
Note first that, by its very definition,
for every
\xi:x=_{X}x^{\prime}
and every
b:B\,x
,
there is
l^{\prime}:x^{\prime}=_{X}x^{\prime}
such that

|B|_{l^{\prime}}\,(B_{\xi}\,b)=_{B\,x^{\prime}}|B|_{\xi}\,b.

(33)

Recall that we write
B_{\xi}
for the transport of the setoid family
B
,
and
|B|_{\xi}
for the transport of the underlying type family
|B|
—the former being given by definition and the latter
by elimination of the identity type.
The matching relation
\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{per}}_{B}
was defined in
3.1
.

Lemma
\thethm
.

Let
B:\mathsf{Fam}\,X
be a discrete setoid family
over the discrete setoid on
X:\mathsf{U}
.

(1)

For every
w,w^{\prime}:\mathsf{W}_{|B|}
,

\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{per}}_{B}\,w\,w^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathsf{ptEq}_{|B|}\,w\,w^{\prime}.

(2)

Every extensional tree is equivariant, i.e. for every
w:\mathsf{W}_{|B|}
,

\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{per}}_{B}\,w\,w\longrightarrow\mathsf{isEquivariant}\,w.

Proof.

(1)
The proof is by induction on

w\equiv\mathsf{sup}\,x\,f:\mathsf{W}_{|B|}
and the
inductive hypothesis tells us that if a tree is matching with an immediate
subtree
f\,b
of
w
, then it is pointwise equal to
f\,b
.

Let then
w^{\prime}\equiv\mathsf{sup}\,x^{\prime}\,f^{\prime}
be a tree an suppose that

w
and
w^{\prime}
are matching.
To prove that
w
and
w^{\prime}
are pointwise equal we need first to provide a proof

\xi:x=_{X}x^{\prime}
, which is given by
\alpha
in
Section

3.1
.
We then need to show that for every
b:B\,x
and
b^{\prime}:B\,x^{\prime}
,
if
B_{\xi}\,b\approx_{B\,x^{\prime}}b^{\prime}
, then the immediate subtrees
f\,b

and
f^{\prime}\,b^{\prime}
are pointwise equal.
By the inductive hypothesis, it is enough to show that the trees
f\,b

and
f^{\prime}\,b^{\prime}
are matching, which holds by definition.

(2)
The proof is again by induction on
w:\mathsf{W}_{|B|}
, into the type

\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{per}}_{B}\,w\,w\longrightarrow\mathsf{isEquivariant}\,w.

By the inductive hypothesis and the fact that every subtree of an extensional subtree
is extensional
3.1
,
it follows that every immediate subtree of
w
is equivariant.
To prove that
w
is equivariant,
it only remains to show that for every
l:x=_{X}x

and every
b:B\,x
,
the subtrees
f\,(|B|_{l}\,b)
and
f\,b
are pointwise equal.
By (1) just proved and the fact that

f
is extensional
3.1
,
it is enough to prove that
|B|_{l}\,b\approx_{B\,x}b
.
This is immediate by
Section

5.1

and standard properties of transport along identity proofs.
∎

It follows from
Section

5.3
that there is an extensional function

j_{B}:W_{B}\Rightarrow\mathsf{EqvTrees}_{|B|}

(34)

which is the identity on the underlying trees.
The next lemma proves that
j_{B}
is injective.

Lemma
\thethm
.

Let
B:\mathsf{Fam}\,X
be a discrete setoid family
over the discrete setoid on
X:\mathsf{U}
,
and let
w,w^{\prime}:\mathsf{W}_{|B|}
be equivariant trees.
Then

\mathsf{ptEq}_{|B|}\,w\,w^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{per}}_{B}\,w\,w^{\prime}.

Proof.

This is proven by induction on the proof
E
that

w\equiv\mathsf{sup}\,x\,f
is equivariant,
into the type

\prod_{w^{\prime}:\mathsf{W}_{Y}}\mathsf{isEquivariant}\,w^{\prime}\to\mathsf{ptEq}_{|B|}\,w\,w^{\prime}\to\mathsf{W}^{\mathsf{per}}_{B}\,w\,w^{\prime}.

Let then
w^{\prime}\equiv\mathsf{sup}\,x^{\prime}\,f^{\prime}
be equivariant and suppose that

w
and
w^{\prime}
are pointwise equal.
In particular, there is
\xi:x=_{X}x^{\prime}
.
So to prove that
w
and
w^{\prime}
are matching it is enough to show that
for every
b:B\,x
and
b^{\prime}:B\,x^{\prime}
,
if
B_{\xi}\,b\approx_{B\,x^{\prime}}b^{\prime}

then the immediate subtrees
f\,b
and
f^{\prime}\,b^{\prime}
are matching.
This follows by the inductive hypothesis once we know that

f^{\prime}\,b^{\prime}
is equivariant, and that
f\,b
and
f^{\prime}\,b^{\prime}

The former condition follows by
Section

5.3
.
For the latter we have

\begin{split}f\,b&\approx_{\mathsf{ptEq}}f^{\prime}(|B|_{\xi}\,b)\\
&\approx_{\mathsf{ptEq}}f^{\prime}\,(B_{\xi}\,b)\\
&\approx_{\mathsf{ptEq}}f^{\prime}\,b^{\prime}\end{split}

using, in order,
the fact that subtrees of
w
and
w^{\prime}
are pointwise equal by
Section

5.2
;
condition (
33
),
Section

5.2

and equivariance of
w^{\prime}
;
and
Section

5.1
,

Section

5.2
and equivariance of
w^{\prime}
.
∎

Theorem
\thethm
.

Let
B:\mathsf{Fam}\,X
be a discrete setoid family
over the discrete setoid on
X
.

(1)

The function
j_{B}
in (
34
) is a bijection between
the setoid of extensional trees

and the setoid of equivariant trees

|B|
.

(2)

),

with the subsetoid
\mathsf{EqvTrees}^{=}_{Y}
of the
(discrete setoid on the) W-type

.

(1)
This is immediate from

5.3
and
5.3
.

(2)
By

5.2
and
5.2

Even if assuming function extensionality does not seem to ensure, in general,
that the setoid

of extensional trees
with respect to a discrete setoid family

Corollary
\thethm
.

Let
B:\mathsf{Fam}\,X
be a discrete setoid family
over the discrete setoid on
X
,
and suppose that the type
X

(1)

Every tree in
\mathsf{W}_{|B|}
is extensional with respect to
B
.
In particular, the setoid
W_{B}
of extensional trees is in bijection
with the setoid
\widetilde{\mathsf{W}}_{|B|}

(2)

32

of extensional trees is in bijection with the

.

We only need to prove that every tree is extensional,

A tree is extensional if and only if it is equivariant by

5.3
,

is
equivariant, by induction on the tree and using the assumption that

∎

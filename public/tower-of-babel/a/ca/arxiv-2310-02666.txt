Introduction

Let
\mathcal{A}
be the family of all analytic normalized mappings

(1.1)

f(z)=\sum_{t=1}^{+\infty}a_{t}z^{t},~a_{1}:=1,

in the open unit disc

and
\mathcal{S}
is the subfamily of
\mathcal{A},
possessing univalent (schlicht) mappings. For

has an inverse

given by

f^{-1}(w)=w+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}t_{n}w^{n},~~|w|<r_{o}(f);\left(r_{o}(f)\geq\frac{1}{4}\right).

A typical problem in geometric function theory is to study a functional made up of combination of the coefficients of the original functions. For the positive integers
r,~n,
Pommerenke
[
19
]
characterized the
r^{th}
- Hankel determinant of
n^{th}
-order for

H_{r,n}(f)=\begin{array}[]{|cccc|}a_{n}&a_{n+1}&\cdots&a_{n+r-1}\\
a_{n+1}&a_{n+2}&\cdots&a_{n+r}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
a_{n+r-1}&a_{n+r}&\cdots&a_{n+2r-2}\end{array}.

This determinant is investigated by many authors for different combinations of
r
and
n
in (1.3) which yields various types of Hankel determinants such as
r=2
and
n=1
gives the famous Fekete-Szeg
\ddot{o}
functional mathematically denoted by

The estimation of the sharp bounds to the functional

is obtained for

in (1.3) is called as second order Hankel determinant for bounded turning, starlike and convex, symbolized as
\Re,

respectively fulfilling the conditions Re

\left\{\frac{zf^{\prime}(z)}{f(z)}\right\}>0
and Re

in the unit disc

were obtained by many authors which can be found in the literature (see e.g.,
[
6
,
7
]

In 1941 Ozaki

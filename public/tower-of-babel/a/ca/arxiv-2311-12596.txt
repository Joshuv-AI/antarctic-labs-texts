Extracting Many-Body Quantum Resources within One-Body Reduced Density Matrix Functional Theory
Carlos L. Benavides-Riveros, Tomasz Wasak, Alessio Recati

Abstract
Quantum Fisher information (QFI) is a central concept in quantum sciences used to quantify the ultimate precision limit of parameter estimation, detect quantum phase transitions, witness genuine multipartite entanglement, or probe nonlocality. Despite this widespread range of applications, computing the QFI value of quantum many-body systems is, in general, a very demanding task. Here we combine ideas from functional theories and quantum information to develop a novel functional framework for the QFI of fermionic and bosonic ground states. By relying upon the constrained-search approach, we demonstrate that the QFI matricial values can universally be determined by the one-body reduced density matrix (1-RDM), avoiding thus the use of exponentially large wave functions. Furthermore, we show that QFI functionals can be determined from the universal 1-RDM functional by calculating its derivatives with respect to the coupling strengths, becoming thus the generating functional of the QFI. We showcase our approach with the Bose-Hubbard model and present exact analytical and numerical QFI functionals. Our results provide the first connection between the one-body reduced density matrix functional theory and the quantum Fisher information.

Hubbard-like Hamiltonian.—
We begin with the Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\hat{h}+\hat{W}.

(1)

The one-body part
\hat{h}=-\sum_{\langle ij\rangle}t_{ij}\hat{b}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}
describes the tunneling, while the two-body term is of the general form

\displaystyle\hat{W}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ijkl}V_{ijkl}\hat{b}_{i}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}\hat{b}_{k}\hat{b}_{l}.

(2)

The operators
\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger}
(

) create (annihilate) a particle on site

V_{jjjj}=U

Dutta et al. 2015

\hat{J}^{ij}_{\alpha}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\hat{b}_{i}^{\dagger},\hat{b}_{j}^{\dagger})\,\sigma_{\alpha}\,(\hat{b}_{i},\hat{b}_{j})^{T}

\alpha=0,x,y,z
, are Pauli matrices. The two-body operator (
2
) can be rewritten, up to a one-body operator that can be incorporated into

\displaystyle\hat{W}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\sum_{ijkl}u_{\alpha\beta}^{ijkl}\{\hat{J}_{\alpha}^{ij},\hat{J}_{\beta}^{kl}\},

(3)

where
\{A,B\}=AB+BA
and
u_{\alpha\beta}^{ijkl}
are real coupling strengths subject to additional constraints stemming from the symmetry of the operators
\{\hat{J}_{\alpha}^{ij},\hat{J}_{\beta}^{kl}\}
.

Our central result is that (
3

\mathcal{F}[\bm{\gamma};\bm{u}]
, with
\bm{u}=\{u_{\alpha\beta}^{ij}\}

\mathcal{F}
and QFI) and then show this connection.

One-body reduced-density-matrix functional theory.—

Given a

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\bm{\gamma}]=\min_{\psi\rightarrow\bm{\gamma}}\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\hat{W}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}.

Quantum Fisher information.—
Consider a transformation of the density matrix
\rho
, describing the state of the quantum system, using a unitary operator
\hat{U}(\bm{\phi})
. The resulting state becomes
\rho(\bm{\phi})=\hat{U}(\bm{\phi})\rho\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\bm{\phi})
. The distance between these two density matrices (and the response of the quantum state to perturbations) can be quantified by the QFI. Specifically, for the response
\partial\rho(\bm{\phi})/\partial\phi_{a}\equiv(\rho\hat{L}_{a}+\hat{L}_{a}\rho)/2
, where
\hat{L}_{a}
is called the Symmetric Logarithmic Derivative and
\phi_{a}
is the
a
-th parameter of the vector
\bm{\phi}
, the QFIM is
M_{ab}=\mathrm{Tr}[\rho\hat{L}_{a}\hat{L}_{b}]
.
Since large QFI implies high sensitivity, originally the QFI was introduced in the context of quantum metrology and sensing via the quantum Cramér-Rao lower bound, which quantifies the ultimate precision limit in estimation protocols
Braunstein and Caves 1994
;
Holevo 2011
;
Degen et al. 2017
.

We parametrize the unitary with the angular-momentum operators, i.e.,
\hat{U}(\bm{\phi})=\exp(i\sum_{\alpha}\sum_{ij}\phi_{\alpha}^{ij}\hat{J}_{\alpha}^{ij})
. For pure states, which are considered in this work,
\rho=\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}
, the QFIM becomes the covariance matrix

\displaystyle M_{\alpha\beta}^{ijkl}[\psi]=2\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\{\hat{J}_{\alpha}^{ij},\hat{J}_{\beta}^{kl}\}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}-4\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\hat{J}_{\alpha}^{ij}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\hat{J}_{\beta}^{kl}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}.

(7)

An impediment to the calculation of this matrix is the prior knowledge of the quantum state, which involves computations of such complexity that hampers broad applicability. This problem, we will now see, can be circumvented by relying upon the 1-RDMs and the universal functional
\mathcal{F}
.

We note that the QFI is an entanglement measure
Pezzé and Smerzi 2009
;
Tóth 2012
: for a quantum system of
N
bosons or spins, the state exhibits at least
(m+1)
-particle entanglement
Hyllus et al. 2012
if the single-parameter QFI surpasses the quantum limit, e.g.,
\sum_{\alpha\beta}n_{\alpha}n_{\beta}M_{\alpha\beta}^{ijij}>sm^{2}+(N-sm)^{2}
, where
s
is an integer part of
N/m
, and
m=1,2,3,\ldots
quantifies entanglement depth; the vector
n
is normalized,
\sum_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}^{2}=1
. For
m=1
, the right-hand side, equal to
N
, is the standard quantum limit
Pezzè et al. 2018
. Similar inequality was derived for fermions
Costa de Almeida and Hauke 2021
. If the inequality is violated, the entanglement structure is not revealed.

Functional theory of QFIM.—
The central quantity of the framework presented above is the 1-RDM. Noteworthy, it already contains information about quantum correlations, via the correlation entropy
S[\bm{\gamma}]=-\mbox{Tr}[\bm{\gamma}\ln\bm{\gamma}]

Aikebaier et al. 2023a
;
Gersdorf et al. 1997
;
Tichy et al. 2011
;
Benavides-Riveros et al. 2014
. Surprisingly, 1-RDMFT is mainly focused on the goal of computing the ground-state energy and some associated observables
Pernal and Giesbertz 2016
;
Sutter and Giesbertz 2023
;
Giesbertz and Ruggenthaler 2019
;
Rodríguez-Mayorga et al. 2022
;
Benavides-Riveros and Marques 2019
;
Di Sabatino et al. 2021
;
Liebert and Schilling 2023
, but this powerful formalism has not been used to scrutiny multipartite entanglement or nonlocality. Indeed, the map
\bm{\gamma}\to\mbox{$|\psi[\bm{\gamma}]\rangle$}
can be used for the calculation of functionals of QFIM: by using Eq. (
7
) one can view QFIM as explicitly, universal functionals dependent on the 1-RDM:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}^{ijkl}[\bm{\gamma}]\equiv M_{\alpha\beta}^{ijkl}[\psi[\bm{\gamma}]].

(8)

Let’s emphasize that this functional is defined in a domain whose degrees of freedom do not scale with the number of particles. We now explain how
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}^{ijkl}
can be directly obtained from the universal functional
\mathcal{F}
.

Generation of QFI and reconstruction of
\mathcal{F}
.—

The universal functional is a function of the couplings
\bm{u}
, cf. Eq. (
6
). By applying the Hellmann-Feynman theorem to the derivative of the functional
Görling 1999
, we show, as detailed in Appendix
C
, that
\mathcal{M}
can be generated by the total energy functional
\mathcal{E}
by means of the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}^{ijkl}[\bm{\gamma};\bm{u}]=4\left[\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{E}[\bm{\gamma};\bm{u}]}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}^{ijkl}}\right)_{\!\!\bm{\gamma}}-\gamma_{\alpha}^{ij}\gamma_{\beta}^{kl}\right],

(9)

where
\bm{\gamma}
is kept fixed in the derivative and we indicated that
\mathcal{M}
depends on
\bm{u}
.
We note that one can substitute here
\mathcal{F}
instead of
\mathcal{E}
, but the latter is accessible experimentally.
This is our central result, which shows that, once the universal functional is determined as a function of the coupling strengths, it provides access to quantum resources quantified by the QFI without referring to the quantum state
|\psi[\bm{\gamma}]\rangle
.

Furthermore, the average of Eq. (
3
) with respect to
|\psi[\bm{\gamma}]\rangle
yields the relation

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\bm{\gamma};\bm{u}]=\sum_{\alpha\beta}\sum_{ijkl}u_{\alpha,\beta}^{ijkl}\left(\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{M}_{\alpha,\beta}^{ijkl}[\bm{\gamma};\bm{u}]+\gamma_{\alpha}^{ij}\gamma_{\beta}^{kl}\right).

(10)

Hence, the QFIM, together with its content about quantum resources, enters into the universal functional. These results show that the knowledge of the QFIM allows for full reconstruction of the universal 1-RDM functional.

QFIM functional theory for BEC.—
Another interesting limit of the two-well Bose-Hubbard model is the Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC) state, which has been used for quantum metrological tasks, spin squeezing and test Bell correlations
Schmied et al. 2016
;
Fadel et al. 2018
;
Ma et al. 2011
;
Dast et al. 2016
. Due to the Penrose-Onsager criterion
Penrose and Onsager 1956
, BEC states lie near the border of the Bloch sphere and it is convenient to define the functionals in terms of the angles
\theta
and
\varphi
, and the number of particles depleted from the condensate (i.e., away from the set of spin coherent states):
\delta=N/2-\gamma
(see Fig.

). Thus, the QFIM functionals can be expressed as functions of the spherical coordinates
\mathcal{M}_{\alpha\beta}(\delta,\theta,\varphi)
. In the neighborhood of the condensation point,
\mathcal{M}_{zz}
reads (see Appendix
B
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{zz}(\delta,\theta,\varphi)=\mathcal{M}_{zz}^{(0)}(\theta)-\mathcal{M}^{(1/2)}_{zz}(\theta,\varphi){\delta^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\mathcal{M}_{zz}^{(1)}(\theta)\delta+\mathcal{O}(\delta^{3/2}).

(16)

Here,
\mathcal{M}_{zz}^{(0)}(\theta)=N\sin^{2}(\theta)
represents the mean-field fluctuations which cannot surpass the standard quantum limit, i.e.,
\mathcal{M}_{zz}^{(0)}(\theta)\leqslant N
,
whereas the two beyond-mean field corrections scaling as
\delta^{1/2}
and
\delta
are
\mathcal{M}^{(1/2)}_{zz}(\varphi,\theta)=2\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos(\varphi)\sqrt{N(N-1)}
and
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}_{zz}(\theta)=8+2(N-6)\sin^{2}(\theta)
, respectively. The square-root scaling of the second term in Eq. (
16
) yields the diverging BEC force, that drives nonperturbatively the system away from the mean-field state
Benavides-Riveros et al. 2020
;
Liebert and Schilling 2021
;
Maciazek 2021
. Since the first term does not violate the standard quantum limit, only the next orders can contribute to genuine multipartite entanglement. In Fig.
3
an example of
\mathcal{M}_{zz}(\delta,\theta,\varphi)
is shown for
N=1000
and for depletion
\delta=0.1
. To surpass the standard quantum limit, the mean field contribution should be large, and thus
\theta\approx\pi/2
, so
\bm{\gamma}
lies close to the equator. Next, the third term in Eq. (
16
) is always positive, but is significantly overshadowed by the second term in the region
\cos\varphi>0
. However, in the region
\cos\varphi<0
it contributes positively, and we observe enhancement of entanglement.

Conclusion.—
In this paper we combined ideas from functional theories and quantum information to develop a functional approach to the QFI. In our formalism the elements of the QFI matrix are
functionals
of the 1-RDM, avoiding thus the exponential growth of the Hilbert space in which they are usually defined. We obtained two main results: (i)
the knowledge of the QFIM allows the full reconstruction of the universal functional of the 1-RDM
and (ii)
QFI functionals correspond to the derivatives of the 1-RDM functional with respect to the coupling strengths
, the latter being upgraded to the level of generating functional of the QFI functionals. These results show a so far unexplored ability of the 1-RDM functionals to detect genuine multipartite entanglement. Since in 1-RDM functional theory approach, we can freely adjust single-particle Hamiltonians to move in the landscape of the functionals, our work shows a novel way to extract many-body resources and to determine optimal sensing protocols
Reilly et al. 2023
.

We leave for future work to exploit our results in the context of quantum chemistry or condensed matter to investigate the use of strongly interacting systems for quantum metrological tasks.

Appendix A
Functional theory of quantum Fisher information for the two-site Bose-Hubbard model

We consider a system of
N
bosonic particles interacting by the following Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle\hat{W}=u\sum_{j=l,r}\hat{n}_{j}\left(\hat{n}_{j}-1\right).

(17)

We write the 1-RDMs in terms of the following components:
\gamma_{\alpha}=\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\hat{J}_{\alpha}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}
, for
\alpha=x,y,z
. In terms of the Pauli matrices
\sigma_{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}\sigma_{\alpha}\hat{\Psi},

(18)

with
\hat{\Psi}^{\dagger}\equiv(\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l},\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{r})
. A basis of the Hilbert space for
N
particles is the following:

\displaystyle\mbox{$|n,N-n\rangle$}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n!(N-n)!}}(b_{l}^{\dagger})^{n}(b_{r}^{\dagger})^{N-n}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}.

(19)

By denoting the real-valued expansion coefficients by
\alpha_{n}
and fixing the number of particles to 2, the 1-RDM reads
\gamma_{x}=\sqrt{2}\alpha_{1}(\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{2})
,
\gamma_{y}=0
, and
\gamma_{z}=(\alpha_{0}^{2}-\alpha_{2}^{2})
. It follows that
\sqrt{2}\gamma_{z}\alpha_{1}/\gamma_{x}=(\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{2})
and
\gamma_{x}/\sqrt{2}\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{0}+\alpha_{2}
. One can now write the amplitudes in terms of the 1-RDM. To show this, note first that

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{2}=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{1-(\gamma_{x}^{2}+\gamma_{z}^{2})}}{2(\gamma_{x}^{2}+\gamma_{z}^{2})}\gamma_{x}^{2}.

(20)

Due to the rotational symmetry of the result, we define
\gamma_{\rho}^{2}=\gamma_{x}^{2}+\gamma_{z}^{2}
, and
\gamma_{x}=\gamma_{\rho}\sin(\theta)
. One gets the expression:

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\rho}^{2}}\right)\sin^{2}(\theta).

(21)

Since
u
factories in the two-body interaction, the only important quantity is the sign of the interaction. As a result,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\bm{\gamma})=\min_{\psi\rightarrow\bm{\gamma}}\mbox{$\langle\psi|$}\hat{W}\mbox{$|\psi\rangle$}=\min\left\{2u\left[1-\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\rho}^{2}}\right)\sin^{2}(\theta)\right]\right\}=u\left[2-\left(1+{\rm sign}(u)\sqrt{1-\gamma_{\rho}^{2}}\right)\sin^{2}(\theta)\right].

(22)

By defining

\displaystyle\beta_{u}^{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+{\rm sign}(u)\sqrt{1-(\gamma_{x}^{2}+\gamma_{z}^{2})}}{\gamma_{x}^{2}+\gamma_{z}^{2}}\gamma_{x}^{2},

(23)

one can write all amplitudes in terms of
\bm{\gamma}
as follows:

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\gamma_{x}}{\sqrt{2}\beta_{u}}+\frac{\sqrt{2}\gamma_{z}\beta_{u}}{\gamma_{x}}\right),

(24)

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\gamma_{x}}{\sqrt{2}\beta_{u}}-\frac{\sqrt{2}\gamma_{z}\beta_{u}}{\gamma_{x}}\right).

(25)

With these results at hand, we can now compute the QFIM. First, let’s write explicitly the wave function:

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\psi(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\rangle$}=\alpha_{0}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\mbox{$|2,0\rangle$}+\beta_{u}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\mbox{$|1,1\rangle$}+\alpha_{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\mbox{$|0,2\rangle$},

(26)

with
\alpha_{0}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})
,
\beta_{u}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})
, and
\alpha_{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})
as defined in Eqs.
23
,
24
, and
25
, respectively. Recall the angular momentum matrices for this problem:

\displaystyle J_{x}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
1&0&1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad J_{y}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}i}\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\
-1&0&1\\
0&-1&0\end{pmatrix}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad J_{z}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(27)

Thus, all elements of QFIM can eventually be computed:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{zz}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=4\mbox{$\langle\psi(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})|$}\hat{J}_{z}^{2}\mbox{$|\psi(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\rangle$}-4\gamma_{z}^{2}=4\left[1-\beta_{u}^{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\right]-4\gamma_{x}^{2}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{yy}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=4\mbox{$\langle\psi(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})|$}\hat{J}_{y}^{2}\mbox{$|\psi(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\rangle$}-4\gamma_{y}^{2}=2\left[1+\beta_{u}^{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\right]-4\alpha_{0}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\alpha_{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{xx}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=4\mbox{$\langle\psi(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})|$}\hat{J}_{x}^{2}\mbox{$|\psi(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\rangle$}-4\gamma_{y}^{2}=2\left[1+\beta_{u}^{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\right]+4\alpha_{0}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\alpha_{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})-4\gamma_{x}^{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{xz}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=2\mbox{$\langle\psi(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})|$}\hat{J}_{z}\hat{J}_{x}+\hat{J}_{x}\hat{J}_{z}\mbox{$|\psi(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\rangle$}-4\gamma_{x}\gamma_{z}=2\sqrt{2}\beta_{u}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\left[\alpha_{0}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})-\alpha_{2}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})\right]-4\gamma_{x}\gamma_{z},

and
\mathcal{M}_{xy}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})=\mathcal{M}_{yz}(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{z})=0
.

Appendix B
Functional theory of quantum Fisher information for Bose-Einstein condensates

In this section, we first recap the known results for the two-site Bose-Hubbard model of Refs.
Benavides-Riveros et al. 2020
;
Maciazek 2021
and then extend them to compute the QFIM. The interacting Hamiltonian of the system is given in Eq. (
17
). We denote the 1-RDM by
\bm{\gamma}
and trace-normalize to the number of particles
N
. As
\bm{\gamma}=(\gamma_{x},\gamma_{y},\gamma_{z})
lies in the interior of a sphere of radius
N/2
, it can be expressed as a function of the spherical coordinates
(\rho,\theta,\alpha)
,
\bm{\gamma}\equiv\bm{\gamma}(\gamma_{\rho},\theta,\varphi)
. To be more specific, the cartesian components of the 1-RDM take the form:

\displaystyle\gamma_{z}(\gamma_{\rho},\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\rho}\cos(\theta),

\displaystyle\gamma_{x}(\gamma_{\rho},\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\rho}\sin(\theta)\cos(\varphi),

\displaystyle\gamma_{y}(\gamma_{\rho},\theta,\varphi)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\rho}\sin(\theta)\sin(\varphi).

(28)

Let us perform the following rotation of the original creation/annihilation operators (on the basis of sites):

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos{(\theta/2)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l}+e^{-i\varphi}\sin{(\theta/2)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{r}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e^{i\varphi}\sin{(\theta/2)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{l}+\cos{(\theta/2)}\hat{b}^{\dagger}_{r}\,.

(29)

In this new basis, states of the form

\mbox{$|N-n,n\rangle$}_{\rho}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{N-n)!n!}}(a^{\dagger}_{\rho})^{N-n}(a^{\dagger}_{\theta})^{n}\mbox{$|0\rangle$},.

map to 1-RDMs with the same
\varphi
and
\theta
, i.e.,
\gamma((N-2n)/2,\theta,\varphi)
. To see this notice that
\mbox{Tr}_{N-1}[\mbox{$|N-n,n\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle N-n,n|$}]=(N-n)\mbox{$|\rho\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\rho|$}+n\mbox{$|\theta\rangle$}\mbox{$\langle\theta|$}
. Using
2\cos{(\theta/2)}\sin{(\theta/2)}=\sin{\theta}

and
\cos^{2}{(\theta/2)}-\sin^{2}{(\theta/2)}=\cos{(\theta)}
, one easily confirm that

\displaystyle\gamma_{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mbox{$\langle N-n,n|$}b^{\dagger}_{l}b_{l}-b^{\dagger}_{r}b_{r}\mbox{$|N-n,n\rangle$}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(N-n)\mbox{$\langle 0|$}a_{\rho}(b^{\dagger}_{l}b_{l}-b^{\dagger}_{r}b_{r})a_{\rho}^{\dagger}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}+\frac{1}{2}n\mbox{$\langle 0|$}a_{\theta}(b^{\dagger}_{l}b_{l}-b^{\dagger}_{r}b_{r})a_{\theta}^{\dagger}\mbox{$|0\rangle$}=\frac{N-2n}{2}\cos(\theta)\,.

(30)

In the same way, one can see that

\gamma_{x}=\frac{N-2n}{2}\sin(\theta)\cos(\varphi)
and

\gamma_{y}=\frac{N-2n}{2}\sin(\theta)\sin(\varphi)
, which proves our statement.

By noticing that
\hat{n}_{l}=\cos^{2}{(\theta/2)}\hat{n}_{\theta}+\sin^{2}{(\theta/2)}\hat{n}_{\rho}-\cos{(\theta/2)}\sin{(\theta/2)}(e^{i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho}\hat{a}_{\theta}+e^{-i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta}\hat{a}_{\rho})

and
\hat{n}_{r}=\sin^{2}{(\theta/2)}\hat{n}_{\theta}+\cos^{2}{(\theta/2)}\hat{n}_{\rho}+\cos{(\theta/2)}\sin{(\theta/2)}(e^{i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho}\hat{a}_{\theta}+e^{-i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta}\hat{a}_{\rho})
, one gets for the Hamitonian (
17
) in the new basis:

\displaystyle\hat{n}^{2}_{r}+\hat{n}^{2}_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\cos^{4}{(\theta/2)}+\sin^{4}{(\theta/2)}\right)(\hat{n}^{2}_{\theta}+\hat{n}^{2}_{\rho})+2\cos^{2}{(\theta/2)}\sin^{2}{(\theta/2)}(e^{i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho}\hat{a}_{\theta}+e^{-i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta}\hat{a}_{\rho})^{2}+4\cos^{2}{(\theta/2)}\sin^{2}{(\theta/2)}\hat{n}_{\rho}\hat{n}_{\theta}

(31)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle(\cos^{2}{(\theta/2)}-\sin^{2}{(\theta/2)})\cos{(\theta/2)}\sin{(\theta/2)}\left[(e^{i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho}\hat{a}_{\theta}+e^{-i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta}\hat{a}_{\rho})\hat{n}_{\theta}+h.c.\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(\cos^{2}{(\theta/2)}-\sin^{2}{(\theta/2)})\cos{(\theta/2)}\sin{(\theta/2)}\left[(e^{i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho}\hat{a}_{\theta}+e^{-i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta}\hat{a}_{\rho})\hat{n}_{\rho}+h.c.\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)\right)(\hat{n}^{2}_{\theta}+\hat{n}^{2}_{\rho})+\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}{(\theta)}(e^{i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho}\hat{a}_{\theta}+e^{-i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta}\hat{a}_{\rho})^{2}+\sin^{2}{(\theta)}\hat{n}_{\rho}\hat{n}_{\theta}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\cos{(\theta)}\sin{(\theta)}\left[(e^{i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho}\hat{a}_{\theta}+e^{-i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta}\hat{a}_{\rho})(\hat{n}_{\theta}-\hat{n}_{\rho})+h.c.\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)\right)(\hat{n}^{2}_{\theta}+\hat{n}^{2}_{\rho})+\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}{(\theta)}\left[e^{2i\varphi}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{r})^{2}(\hat{a}_{\theta})^{2}+e^{-2i\varphi}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta})^{2}(\hat{a}_{\rho})^{2}\right]+\tfrac{1}{2}\sin^{2}{(\theta)}(N+4\hat{n}_{\rho}\hat{n}_{\theta})

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\cos{(\theta)}\sin{(\theta)}\left[(e^{i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho}\hat{a}_{\theta}+e^{-i\varphi}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta}\hat{a}_{\rho})(\hat{n}_{\theta}-\hat{n}_{\rho})+h.c.\right]\equiv\hat{h}_{2}(\theta,\varphi).

Using the Hamiltonian (
31
) we can compute the
exact universal functional close to the full BEC state along the radius
\gamma_{\rho}
, i.e., while keeping fixed the angles
\theta
and
\varphi
. In fact, close to that point, the wave function reads:

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}\rangle$}_{\rho}=\beta_{0}\mbox{$|N,0\rangle$}_{\rho}\pm\beta_{1}\mbox{$|N-2,2\rangle$}_{\rho}\,,

(32)

where
\beta_{0}
and
\beta_{1}
are taken real. Notice that the state (
32
) gives place to a 1-RDM with
\beta_{1}^{2}=N/4-\gamma_{\rho}/2
. At the same time, the expectation value of the particles outside the condensate is
\mbox{$\langle\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}|$}_{\rho}\hat{n}_{\theta}\mbox{$|\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}\rangle$}_{\rho}=2\beta_{1}^{2}
. Let us take this as the important parameter
\delta_{\rho}\equiv 2\beta_{1}^{2}=N/2-\gamma_{\rho}
.

Our goal is to compute the expectation value
\mbox{$\langle\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}|$}_{\rho}\hat{h}_{2}(\theta,\varphi)\mbox{$|\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}\rangle$}_{\rho}
. In order to do so we shall pre-compute the following expectation values:

\displaystyle\mbox{$\langle\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}|$}_{\rho}(\hat{n}^{2}_{\theta}+\hat{n}^{2}_{\rho})\mbox{$|\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}\rangle$}_{\rho}=N^{2}+4\beta_{1}^{2}(2-N)=N^{2}-2\delta_{\rho}(N-2)

(33)

\displaystyle\mbox{$\langle\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}|$}_{\rho}\hat{n}_{\rho}\hat{n}_{\theta}\mbox{$|\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}\rangle$}_{\rho}=\beta_{1}^{2}2(N-2)=(N-2)\delta_{\rho}\,.

(34)

Since
\mbox{$\langle N-2,2|$}_{\rho}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho})^{2}(\hat{a}_{\theta})^{2}\mbox{$|N,0\rangle$}_{\rho}=\sqrt{2N(N-1)}

we also have

\displaystyle\mbox{$\langle\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}|$}_{\rho}\left[e^{2i\varphi}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\rho})^{2}(\hat{a}_{\theta})^{2}+e^{-2i\varphi}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{\theta})^{2}(\hat{a}_{\rho})^{2}\right]\mbox{$|\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}\rangle$}_{\rho}

\displaystyle=\pm 2\cos(\varphi)\beta_{0}\beta_{1}\sqrt{2N(N-1)}

\displaystyle=\pm 2\cos(\varphi)\sqrt{\left(1-\frac{\delta_{\rho}}{2}\right)\delta_{\rho}}\sqrt{N(N-1)}.

(35)

Collecting (

) and (

\displaystyle\mbox{$\langle\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}|$}_{\rho}(\hat{n}^{2}_{l}+\hat{n}^{2}_{r})\mbox{$|\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}\rangle$}_{\rho}

\displaystyle\qquad=N^{2}-2(N-2)\delta_{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)[N(N-1)-6(N-2)\delta_{\rho}]\pm\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos(\varphi)\sqrt{N(N-1)}\sqrt{\delta_{\rho}\left(1-\frac{\delta_{\rho}}{2}\right)}\,.

(36)

After executing the constrained search for (
U>0
), this result can be written in a much more insightful way

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{N}(\delta_{\rho},\theta,\varphi)=\left[E^{0}(\theta)-\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos(\varphi)\sqrt{N(N-1)}\sqrt{\delta_{\rho}}+E^{1}(\theta)\delta_{\rho}+E^{3/2}(\theta,\varphi)\delta_{\rho}^{3/2}+\mathcal{O}(\delta_{\rho}^{5/2})\right],

(37)

where

E^{0}(\theta)=N(N-1)-\frac{1}{2}N(N-1)\sin^{2}(\theta)

E^{1}(\theta)=-2(N-2)+3(N-2)\sin^{2}(\theta)

E^{3/2}(\theta,\varphi)=\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{N(N-1)}\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos(\varphi)\,.

Now we should compute the expectation value of the operator

\displaystyle\hat{J}_{z}=\frac{1}{2}(b^{\dagger}_{l}b_{l}-b^{\dagger}_{r}b_{r})=\cos(\theta)\frac{1}{2}(a_{\rho}^{\dagger}a_{\rho}-a^{\dagger}_{\theta}a_{\theta})\,.

(38)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mbox{$\langle\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}|$}_{\rho}\hat{J}_{z}\mbox{$|\Psi_{\widetilde{\rm BEC}}\rangle$}_{\rho}=\left(\frac{N}{2}-\delta_{\rho}\right)\cos(\theta),

(39)

as expected from the definition of

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{zz}[\gamma]=2\mathcal{F}[\gamma]-4\gamma_{z}^{2}-N^{2}+2N=N\sin^{2}(\theta)-2\sin^{2}(\theta)\cos(\varphi)\sqrt{N(N-1)}\sqrt{\delta_{\rho}}+\tilde{E}^{1}(\theta)\delta_{\rho}+\mathcal{O}(\delta_{\rho}^{3/2}),

The rest of the matricial elements of the QFIM can be computed along similar lines.

Appendix C
Derivative of the functional

To prove Eq. (
9
) we first need to show that for ground states:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}[\bm{u};\bm{\gamma}]}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}=\mbox{$\langle\psi[\bm{\gamma}]|$}\frac{\hat{W}(\bm{u})}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}\mbox{$|\psi[\bm{\gamma}]\rangle$},

(41)

where we assume that the derivative keeps fixed
\bm{\gamma}
. To that aim,
let’s first use the fact that the Hamiltonian can be written as
\hat{H}(\bm{u})=\hat{h}+\hat{W}(\bm{u})
, where
\hat{h}
is the one-particle Hamiltonian. Thus, we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\bm{u};\gamma]

\displaystyle=\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\hat{W}(\bm{u})\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}=\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\hat{H}(\bm{u})\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}-\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\hat{h}\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}\,.

(42)

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}[\bm{u};\gamma]}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\hat{H}(\bm{u})\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\hat{h}\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}\,.

(43)

Due to the Hellmann–Feynman theorem the first term gives (recall that the wave functions are ground states)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\hat{H}(\bm{u})\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}=\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\frac{\partial\hat{H}(\bm{u})}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}\,.

(44)

The second term gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\hat{h}\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}\mbox{Tr}[\hat{h}\gamma]\,.

(45)

But since we have assumed that
\gamma
is kept fixed while performing the derivative, this term is zero. As a result,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}[\bm{u};\gamma]}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}=\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\frac{\partial\hat{H}(\bm{u})}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}=\mbox{$\langle\psi[\gamma]|$}\frac{\partial\hat{W}(\bm{u})}{\partial u_{\alpha\beta}}\mbox{$|\psi[\gamma]\rangle$}\,.

(46)

which is what we wanted to prove.

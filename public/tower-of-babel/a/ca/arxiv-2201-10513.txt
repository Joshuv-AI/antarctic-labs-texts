Solutions of Fermat-type partial differential-difference equations in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n}
Goutam Haldar

Abstract
. For two meromorphic functions f f and g g , the equation f m + g m = 1 f^{m}+g^{m}=1 can be regarded as Fermat-type equations. Using Nevanlinna theory for meromorphic functions in several complex variables, the main purpose of this paper is to investigate the properties of the transcendental entire solutions of Fermat-type difference and partial differential-difference equations in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . In addition, we find the precise form of the transcendental entire solutions in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} with finite order of the Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation ( ∂ f ⁡ ( z 1 , z 2 ) ∂ z 1 ) 2 + ( f ⁡ ( z 1 + c 1 , z 2 + c 2 ) − f ⁡ ( z 1 , z 2 ) ) 2 = 1 \displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}+(f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})-f(z_{1},z_{2}))^{2}=1 and f 2 ​ ( z 1 , z 2 ) + P 2 ​ ( z 1 , z 2 ) ​ ( ∂ f ⁡ ( z 1 + c 1 , z 2 + c 2 ) ∂ z 1 − ∂ f ⁡ ( z 1 , z 2 ) ∂ z 1 ) 2 = 1 , \displaystyle f^{2}(z_{1},z_{2})+P^{2}(z_{1},z_{2})\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}-\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}=1, where P ⁡ ( z 1 , z 2 ) P(z_{1},z_{2}) is a polynomial in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} . Moreover, one of the main results of the paper significantly improved the result of Xu and Cao [Mediterr. J. Math. (2018) 15:227 , 1-14 and Mediterr. J. Math. (2020) 17:8, 1-4].

Introduction

This paper mainly deals with the existence of transcendental entire solutions with finite order of Fermat-type difference and partial differential-difference equations in several complex variables. We adopt the standard notations of the Nevanlinna theory of meromorphic functions in one variable (see
[
10
,
15
,
40
]
). In the past several decades, considerable attention had been paid to the existence of entire solutions for Fermat-type equation
x^{n}+y^{m}=1
. Specially, in
1995

[
30
,
32
]
pointed out that the Fermat-type equation
x^{n}+y^{m}=1
, where

does not admit nontrivial solution in rational numbers for
m=n\geq 3

m=n=2

[
5

[

In

[

\displaystyle f^{n}+g^{m}=1

(1.1)

and proved the following interesting result.

Theorem A
.

[
37

There are no non-constant entire solutions of the functional equation
(\ref{e1.1})
if
m
,
n
are positive integers satisfying
1/m+1/n<1
.

In recent years, looking for non-constant entire as well as meromorphic solutions of Fermat-type difference as well as differential-difference equations have been studied extensively after the development of the difference analogous lemma of the logarithmic derivative by Halburd and Korhonen
[
7
,

]
, and Chiang and Feng
[

,

,

,

,
24
,

,
31
,
38
,
41
]

In view of Theorem A, Liu
et. al.

[
22
]
have showed that there is no finite order transcendental entire solution of Fermat-type difference equation
f^{n}(z)+f^{m}(z+c)=1
when
n>m>1
or
n=m>2
, and for the case
n=m=2
,
f(z)
must be of the form
f(z)=\sin(Az+B)
, where
c(\neq 0)
,
B\in\mathbf{C}
and
A=(4k+1)\pi/2c
,
k
is an integer. Later, in
2013
, Liu and Yang
[
19
]
extended this result by considering the Fermat-type difference equation
f^{2}(z)+P^{2}(z)f^{2}(z+c)=Q(z)
where
P(z)
and
Q(z)
are two non-zero polynomials.

In 2018, Xu and Cao
[
33
]
extended the above result to several complex variables as follows.

Theorem B
.

[
33
]

Let
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then any non-constant entire solution
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
with finite order of the Fermat-type difference equation
f(z)^{2}+f(z+c)^{2}=1
has the form
of
f(z)=\cos(L(z)+B)
, where
L
is a linear function of the form
L(z)=a_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}z_{n}
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
L(c)=-\pi/2-2k\pi

(k\in\mathbb{Z})
, and
B
is a constant on
\mathbb{C}
.

It would be interesting of finding the solutions as well as the precise form of the solutions of the equation considered in Theorem B. Henceforth, motivated by the results of Xu and Cao
[
33
]
, and Liu and Yang
[
19
]
, in this paper, we are mainly interested to generalize Theorem B by considering the following equation

\displaystyle f^{2}(z)+P^{2}(z)f^{2}(z+c)=Q(z),

(1.2)

where
P(z)
and
Q(z)
(with
Q(0)\neq 0
) are two non-zero polynomials in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. It is easy to see that (
1.2
) is a general setting of the equation
f(z)^{2}+f(z+c)^{2}=1
. Consequently, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f(z)
be a transcendental entire function defined on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
c=(c_{1},c_{2},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If

is a solution of (
1.2
) such that
T(r,P)=o(T(r,f))
and
T(r,Q)=o(T(r,f))
, then
P(z)=\pm 1
,
Q(z)
reduces to a constant and
f(z)
is of the form
f(z)=\cos(L(z)+B)
, where
L(z)=a_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}z_{n}
such that
L(c)=-\pi/2-2k\pi

(k\in\mathbb{Z})
, and
B
is a constant on
\mathbb{C}
.

Besides finding the solutions of Fermat-type difference equations, Fermat-type differential-difference equations are also studied by many researchers. For example, Xu and Cao
[
33
]
have investigated the entire solutions of Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{n}+f^{m}(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=1

.

[

Let
c=(c_{1},c_{2})
be a constant in
\mathbb{C}^{2}

(\ref{e1.3})
does not have any transcendental entire solution with finite order, where

In

[
35

generalized Theorem C by considering the following Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}+\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{2}}\right)^{n}+f^{m}(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=1

(1.4)

and proved the following result.

Theorem D
.

[
35
]

Let
c=(c_{1},c_{2})
be a constant in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
m
,
n

(i)

(ii)

As far as we know, there are few results about the complex difference and complex difference equations for the functions in several complex variables. Furthermore, it appears that the Fermat-type mixed partial differential-difference equations in several complex variables has not been addressed in the literature before. In order to generalize and also to establish a result which combines Theorem C and Theorem D, in our investigation, we consider the following partial differential-difference equation

\displaystyle(\partial^{I}f(z_{1},z_{2})+\partial^{J}f(z_{1},z_{2}))^{n}+f^{m}(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=1,

(1.5)

where

\displaystyle\partial^{I}f(z_{1},z_{2})=\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{|I|}f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial z_{2}^{\alpha_{2}}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\partial^{J}f(z_{1},z_{2})=\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{|J|}f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\partial z_{2}^{\beta_{2}}}

with
I=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
and
J=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
are two multi-index, where
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
,
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
are non-negative integers and
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. We denote by
\mid I\mid
to denote the length of
I
, that is,
\mid I\mid=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
. Similarly, for
J
also. Throughout this paper, we denote
z+w=(z_{1}+w_{1},z_{2}+w_{2})
for any
z=(z_{1},z_{2})
,

and
c=(c_{1},c_{2})

As a matter of fact, we prove the next result for any order Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation (
1.5

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
c=(c_{1},c_{2})
be a constant in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and
m
,
n
be two positive integers. If the Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation (
1.5

(i)

m>n
;

(ii)

n>m\geq 2

then (
1.5

In this regard, we recall here a result of Xu and Cao
[
33
]
in which the authors have investigated the solutions of the following Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)^{2}+f^{2}(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=1

(1.6)

and proved that the finite order transcendental entire solutions of (

Remark 1.1
.

By the following example one can observe that the concluding part of the result of Xu and Cao is not true in general.

Example 1.1
.

Let
f(z_{1},z_{2})=\sin(z_{1}+z_{2})
or
\cos(z_{1}+z_{2})
. Choose,

such that

f(z_{1},z_{2})
is a solution of the equation
(\ref{e2.3})
but

is not a function of
z_{1}
only.

In 2020, Xu and Cao
[
34
]
corrected
Theorem 1.2
of
[
33
]
and obtained the following result.

Theorem E
.

[
34
]

Let
c=(c_{1},c_{2})
be a constant in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then any transcendental entire solution with finite order of the Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation

has the form

are constant on

After a detailed study of the proof of result of Xu and Cao
[
33

and
[
34
, Theorem 1.1]
, a lacuna is found when the authors were trying to come up with a specific form of solution. For a transcendental entire function

with finite order satisfying the relation

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}+if(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})\right)\left(\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}-if(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})\right)=1,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}+if(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=e^{ip(z_{1},z_{2})}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}-if(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=e^{-ip(z_{1},z_{2})},

whereas the correct assumption will be

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}+if(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=\beta_{1}e^{\alpha p(z_{1},z_{2})}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}-if(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})=\beta_{2}e^{-\alpha p(z_{1},z_{2})},

where
\alpha
,
\beta_{1}
and

are complex constants in one variable such that

, and

is a polynomial. Therefore, in order to find out the complete characterization of the solutions of (
1.6

Question 1.1
.

What could be the precise form of solutions of the Fermat-type partial differential-difference equation
(\ref{e2.3})
?

We prove the following result which answers Question
1.1

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
c=(c_{1},c_{2})
be a constant in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then any transcendental entire solution with finite order of the partial differential-difference equation
(\ref{e2.3})
is of the form

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{A}{2i}\left(\beta_{1}e^{\alpha\left(\displaystyle\frac{i}{\alpha}Az_{1}+Bz_{2}+h(z_{2})\right)}-\beta_{2}e^{-\alpha\left(\displaystyle\frac{i}{\alpha}Az_{1}+Bz_{2}+h(z_{2})\right)}\right),

,

\alpha

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{A}{2i}\left(\beta_{1}e^{\alpha\left(\displaystyle\frac{i}{\alpha}Az_{1}+Bz_{2}+\text{constant}\right)}-\beta_{2}e^{-\alpha\left(\displaystyle\frac{i}{\alpha}Az_{1}+Bz_{2}+\text{constant}\right)}\right).

Let

,

in

2.
Key Lemmas

In this section, we present some necessary lemmas which will play key role to prove the main results of this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

[

Proof of Theorem

.

We discuss the whole proof into the following two cases.

Case 1:
Let

. Since

is entire, using Lemma
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle T(r,f(z_{1},z_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m(r,z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m\left(r,\frac{f(z_{1},z_{2})}{f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}\right)+m(r,f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))+\log 2

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m(r,f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle T(r,f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))+S(r,f).

By the Mohon’ko theorem for functions in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
(see
[
11
]
), an easy and straight forward computation shows that

\displaystyle mT(r,f(z_{1},z_{2}))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle mT(r,f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(r,f^{m}(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T(r,(\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)^{n}-1)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle nT(r,\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nm(r,\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle nm\left(r,\frac{\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f}{f}\right)+nm(r,f(z_{1},z_{2}))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle nm\left(r,\frac{\partial^{I}f}{f}\right)+m\left(r,\frac{\partial^{J}f}{f}\right)+nm(r,f(z_{1},z_{2}))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle nT(r,f(z_{1},z_{2}))+S(r,f).

Therefore, we must have

\displaystyle(n-m)T(r,f(z_{1},z_{2}))\leq S(r,f),

which contradicts to the fact that
f
is transcendental and also
m>n
.

Case 2:
Suppose

. In this case, it is easy to see that

. Let

, a simple computation shows that

\displaystyle(n-1)T(r,\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}(r,\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f-d_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle+S(r,\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{(\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)^{n}-1}\right)+S(r,\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{N}\left(r,\frac{1}{f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}\right)+S(r,\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T(r,f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))+S(r,f).

On the other hand, by Mohon’ko theorem for the functions in
\mathbf{C}^{2}

[
11
]
and (
1.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle mT(r,f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(r,f^{m}(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T(r,(\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)^{n}-1)+S(r,f)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nT(r,\partial^{I}f+\partial^{J}f)+S(r,f).

Thus we have

\displaystyle mT(r,f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))\leq\frac{n}{n-1}T(r,f(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2}))+S(r,f)

which is not possible since

is transcendental and

-\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}}e^{2\alpha p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}

are non-constants.

We also observe that

\displaystyle N\left(r,e^{\alpha 2p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}\right)=N\left(r,\frac{1}{e^{\alpha 2p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}}\right)=S(r,f),

\displaystyle N\left(r,\left(1-i\alpha\frac{\partial p(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)e^{\alpha ip(z_{1},z_{2})+\alpha p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=N\left(r,\frac{1}{\left(1-i\alpha\frac{\partial p(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)e^{\alpha ip(z_{1},z_{2})+\alpha p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}}\right)=S(r,f)

and

\displaystyle N\left(r,\left(1-i\alpha\frac{\partial p(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)e^{-\alpha ip(z_{1},z_{2})+\alpha p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}\right)

\displaystyle=N\left(r,\frac{1}{\left(1-i\alpha\frac{\partial p(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)e^{-\alpha ip(z_{1},z_{2})+\alpha p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}}\right)=S(r,f).

By Lemma
2.1
, we easily obtain

\displaystyle\left(1-i\alpha\frac{\partial p(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}\right)e^{ip(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})-ip(z_{1},z_{2})}=1,

(3.22)

which implies that

\displaystyle 1-i\alpha\frac{\partial p(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}=e^{ip(z_{1},z_{2})-ip(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}.

(3.23)

Again, using (
3

3.22
), we obtain

\displaystyle 1-i\alpha\frac{\partial p(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}=e^{\alpha p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})-ip(z_{1},z_{2})}.

(3.24)

Now we observe that L.H.S. of
(\ref{e4.21})
is a polynomial, whereas R.H.S. of it is transcendental. Therefore, we conclude that
\alpha p(z_{1},z_{2})-\alpha p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})
, and thus

must be constant.

Let

\displaystyle 1-i\alpha\frac{\partial p(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}=e^{ip(z_{1},z_{2})-ip(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})}=A,

(3.25)

where

is a non-zero complex constant in one variable.

), we get

\displaystyle 1-i\alpha\frac{\partial p(z_{1},z_{2})}{\partial z_{1}}=e^{\alpha p(z_{1}+c_{1},z_{2}+c_{2})-ip(z_{1},z_{2})}=\frac{1}{A}.

(3.26)

Therefore, it follows from (
3.25
) and (
3.26

A^{2}=1
.

Again, since

, we may assume that

\displaystyle p(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{A-1}{\alpha}iz_{1}+g(z_{2}),

where

is a polynomial in one variable
z_{2}
only.

From (
3.25

. This implies that

\displaystyle g(z_{2})-g(z_{2}+c_{2})=\frac{(A-1)i}{\alpha}c_{1}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log A.

Let

, where

is a polynomial in
z_{2}
only with degree greater than 1 or constant and

is a complex number in one variable.

Then

\displaystyle p(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{(A-1)i}{\alpha}z_{1}+Bz_{2}+h(z_{2})\;\;\text{and}

\displaystyle h(z_{2})-h(z_{2}+c_{2})=\frac{(A-1)i}{\alpha}z_{1}+Bz_{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}\log A.

If

, then from (

. If

must be constant and

.

From (

\displaystyle f(z_{1},z_{2})=\frac{1}{2i(A-1)}\left(\beta_{1}e^{i(A-1)z_{1}+\alpha Bz_{2}+\alpha h(z_{2})}-\beta_{2}e^{-(i(A-1)z_{1}+\alpha Bz_{2}+\alpha h(z_{2}))}\right),

where

In the special case whenever

,

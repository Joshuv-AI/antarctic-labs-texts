Optimal Feedback Controls of Stochastic Linear Quadratic Control Problems in Infinite Dimensions with Random Coefficients
Qi Lü, Tianxiao Wang

Abstract
It is a longstanding unsolved problem to characterize the optimal feedback controls for general linear quadratic optimal control problem of stochastic evolution equation with random coefficients. A solution to this problem is given in [ 21 ] under some assumptions which can be verified for interesting concrete models, such as controlled stochastic wave equations, controlled stochastic Schrödinger equations, etc. More precisely, the authors establish the equivalence between the existence of optimal feedback operator and the solvability of the corresponding operator-valued, backward stochastic Riccati equations. However, their result cannot cover some important stochastic partial differential equations, such as stochastic heat equations, stochastic stokes equations, etc. A key contribution of the current work is to relax the C 0 C_{0} -group assumption of unbounded linear operator A A in [ 21 ] and using contraction semigroup assumption instead. Therefore, our result can be well applicable in the linear quadratic problem of stochastic parabolic partial differential equations. To this end, we introduce a suitable notion to the aforementioned Riccati equation, and some delicate techniques which are even new in the finite dimensional case.

Introduction

In Control Theory, one of the fundamental issues
is to find optimal feedback controls, which are
particularly important in practical applications
since the main advantage of feedback controls is
to keep the corresponding control to be robust
with respect to (small) perturbation/
disturbance, which is usually unavoidable in
real world. Unfortunately, it is actually very
difficult to find optimal feedback controls for
many optimal control problems. So far, the most
successful attempt in this respect is that for
linear quadratic control problems (LQ problems
for short) which are extensively studied in
Control Theory. The study of LQ problems dates
back at least to

[
3
]
in which
the system is governed by a linear ordinary
differential equation. Later, in the seminal
work of
[
14
]
, the matrix-valued
Riccati equations were brought into LQ problems
to construct (linear) feedback controls. It is
well-known that, Kalman’s theory for LQ problems
is among the three milestones in modern (finite
dimensional) optimal control theory. Due to the
elegant and fruitful mathematical structure, LQ
problems were investigated extensively in the
literature for a variety of deterministic
control systems (e.g.,

[
2
,
4
]
).

LQ problems for controlled stochastic
differential equations (SDEs) was first studied
in
[
33
]
. More precisely, given
proper
(s,\eta)
, the state equation is
described as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle dX(t)=\big[AX(t)+Bu(t)\big]dt+\big[CX(t)+Du(t)\big]dW(t),\ \ t\in(s,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle X(s)=\eta,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

and the cost functional is defined as

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle{\cal J}(s,\eta;u(\cdot))=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{s}^{T}\big[\big\langle QX(t),X(t)\big\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}+\big\langle Ru(t),u(t)\big\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{m}}\big]dt+\big\langle GX(T),X(T)\big\rangle_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\Big].\end{array}

(1.2)

In the above,
W(\cdot)
is a standard Brownian
motion defined on complete filtered probability
space
(\Omega,{\cal F},{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{P}})
with

{\mathbb{F}}=\{{\cal F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
generated by

W(\cdot)
augmented by all
{\mathbb{P}}
-null sets. The
coefficients
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
, the control
variable
u(\cdot)
, and the state variable

X(\cdot)
are stochastic processes with suitable
measurability and integrability such that both
(
1.1
) and
(
1.2
) are well-defined.
The optimal control problem is to find (if
possible) a
\bar{u}(\cdot)
to minimize the cost
functional. There are a huge amount works
addressing the LQ problems for controlled SDEs
(see
[
5
,
8
,
19
,
28
,
29
,
30
,
33
]
and the
rich references therein). To simplify the notations, here and henceforth the sample point
\omega(\in\Omega)
and/or the
time variable
t(\in[0,T])
in the coefficients are often suppressed, in the case that no
confusion would occur.

Similar as the deterministic setting, to find the optimal feedback
controls, people introduce the following Riccati equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dP=-\big[PA+A^{\top}P+\Lambda C+C^{\top}\Lambda+C^{\top}PC+Q-L^{\top}K^{\dagger}L\big]dt+\Lambda dW(t)&\mbox{in }[0,T),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P(T)=G,\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where
K\equiv R+D^{\top}PD,

L=B^{\top}P+D^{\top}(PC+\Lambda),
and

K^{\dagger}
is the Moore-Penrose
pseudo-inverse of
K
, is introduced as
fundamental tools to represent the
linear feedback controls.
We point out
that (
1.3
) is actually
a backward stochastic differential
equation (BSDE), whose solvability is
highly challenging and nontrivial. Even
though the well-posedness is
guaranteed, as pointed in
[
19
, Remark
1.2]
, the solution is not fully qualified to
serve as the design of optimal feedback
controls. Naturally, one may ask a
question:

Is it possible to link
the existence of optimal feedback
control for LQ problems with the solvability of
(
1.3
)?

Along this
line, an affirmative answer was given
in
[
19
]
.

Now let us move to the linear quadratic for
stochastic evolution equations (SEEs), which is
the main concern of the current paper. Let
H

and
U
be separable Hilbert spaces, and
A
be
an unbounded linear operator (with domain
D(A)

on
H
), which generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup

\{e^{At}\}_{t\geq 0}
on
H
. Denote by
A^{*}

the adjoint operator of
A
, which generates the
adjoint
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{e^{A^{*}t}\}_{t\geq 0}

on
H
. Given proper
(s,\eta)
, consider the
following controlled linear SEE:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dx(t)=\big[(A+A_{1})x+Bu\big]dt+\big(Cx+Du\big)dW(t)\quad\mbox{in }(s,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle x(s)=\eta,\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

with the quadratic cost functional

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\cal J}(s,\eta;u(\cdot))&\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}{\mathbb{E}}\Big[\int_{s}^{T}\big(\big\langle Qx(t),x(t)\big\rangle_{H}+\big\langle Ru(t),u(t)\big\rangle_{U}\big)dt+\langle Gx(T),x(T)\rangle_{H}\Big].\end{array}

(1.5)

Here
u(\cdot)
is the control variable,

x(\cdot)
is the state variable, and the
coefficients (say,
A_{1}
,
B
,
C
and
D
) are
stochastic processes. Under proper conditions
(See Section 2), (
1.4
)
admits a unique solution and
(
1.5
) becomes
well-defined. The optimal control problem is to
find (if possible) a control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
to
minimize
{\cal J}
in (
1.5
).

SEEs are used to describe a lot of random
phenomena appearing in physics, chemistry,
biology, and so on. In many situations SEEs are
more realistic mathematical models than the
deterministic ones (e.g.

[
7
,
15
,
23
]
). Consequently,
there are many works addressing the optimal
control problems for SEEs. In particular, we
refer the readers to
[
1
,
11
,
17
,
21
,
23
]

and the rich references therein for LQ problems
for controlled SEEs.

To design optimal feedback controls for linear quadratic problem of
SEEs, similar to (
1.3
), we introduce the following
operator-valued Riccati equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dP=-\big[P(A+A_{1})+(A+A_{1})^{*}P+\Lambda C+C^{*}\Lambda\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\;\;\quad+C^{*}PC+Q-L^{*}K^{-1}L\big]dt+\Lambda dW(t)&\mbox{in }[0,T),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P(T)=G,\end{array}\right.

(1.6)

where
K\equiv R+D^{*}PD
,
L=B^{*}P+D^{*}(PC+\Lambda).

Besides the difficulties for the case
of finite dimensions, there exists
several new essentially ones in the
study of (
1.6
) when

\hbox{dim}H=\infty
:

•

There is no
stochastic integration/evolution
equation theory in general Banach
spaces that can be employed to handle
the (stochastic integral) term “
\Lambda dW(t)
” in (
1.6
)
effectively. Due to this difficulty,
there exist only a quite limited number
of works, see
[
1
,
9
,
10
]
and the
papers therein. We refer to
[
21
]
for the detailed introduction.

•

The appearance of
D
, and the resulting term
L^{*}K^{-1}L
lead to another essential difficulty in the study of (
1.6
). Notice that the second term is already quite subtle even in the finite dimensional case. Usually, people assume that
D=0
to avoid that difficulty (e.g.,
[
9
,
10
]
). We study the case that
D\neq 0
according to the following reasons:

On one hand, only when the controls/decisions could or would influence the scale of uncertainty do the stochastic problems differ from the deterministic
ones. On the other hand, once one put a control in the drift term, it will influence the diffusion term.

Generally speaking, to study the difficult
operator-valued stochastic differential
equations, people need to introduce suitable new
concept of solution. For example, in

[
20
,
22
]
, the
authors introduced the concept of transposition
solution to operator-valued, backward
stochastic (linear) Lyapunov equations to study
the maximum principle for optimal control
problems of SEEs. In
[
21
]
,
the authors defined a type of transposition
solution to treat (
1.6
), and
obtained equivalence between the existence of
optimal feedback operator and the solvability of
BSRE (
1.6
) in the sense of
transposition solution. We point out that such a
definition of transposition solution can bypass
the previous two difficulties. However, due to
the methodology limitation in obtaining BSREs by
the existence of optimal feedback operators, the
authors made a common yet technical assumption
of
A
, i.e.,
A
generates a
C_{0}
-group on

H
. In fact, they followed the forward-backward
stochastic system ideas (see e.g.

[
19
,
27
]

for the finite dimensional case), and therefore
used the inverse of solution to certain forward
stochastic differential equation to construct
the solution of Riccati equation. In the finite
dimensional case, the inverse of the solution to
the forward stochastic system is easy to see.
However, in the infinite dimensions, to achieve
such a goal, it requires
-A^{*}
generates a

C_{0}
-semigroup as well, which means that
A^{*}

generates a
C_{0}
-group. As a result, their
equivalence ruled out some important stochastic
partial differential equations, say stochastic
parabolic equation and stochastic stokes
equation for example.

In this paper, by supposing
A
generating a contraction semigroup,
we establish the equivalence between optimal feedback controls and
backward stochastic Riccati equations in infinite dimensions. As a
result, we not only have to deal with the two difficulties mentioned
in the last paragraph, but also need to overcome the methodology
limitation in
[
17
,
21
,
23
]
. Notice that all these obstacles can be well-treated
in the finite dimensional framework. To figure out these issues, let
us look at the following observation which holds in the finite
dimensional framework: the BSRE (
1.3
) can be
rewritten as a linear BSDE with a parameter
\Theta
:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dP=-\big[P(A+B\Theta)+(A+B\Theta)^{\top}P+(C+D\Theta)^{\top}P(C+D\Theta)+(C+D\Theta)^{\top}\Lambda\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\Lambda(C+D\Theta)+Q+\Theta^{\top}R\Theta\big]dt+\Lambda dW(t)\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P(T)=G,\end{array}\right.

(1.7)

where the parameter
\Theta
satisfies the following:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle D^{\top}PC+B^{\top}P+D^{\top}\Lambda+(R+D^{\top}PD)\Theta=0.\end{array}

(1.8)

This important fact was used in several existing papers under Markovian framework, see e.g.
[
16
, Theorem 3.3]
and
[
32
, Theorem 2.3]
for the mean-field stochastic LQ problems for SDEs, and
[
18
, Lemma 2.8]
for the mean-field stochastic LQ problem for SEEs.
Clearly, one advantage of (
1.7
) lies in its linearity in contrast with (
1.3
). As a tradeoff, one has to explore the additional relation (
1.8
).
In this article, we successfully apply this idea
into our infinite dimensional framework with
random coefficients and derive the new
equivalence between optimal feedback operators
and BSREs in the following three steps:

1.

We establish the well-posedness of an infinite dimensional version of (
1.7
), i.e., operator-valued backward stochastic Lyapunov equation (BSLE), in the sense of
H_{\lambda}
-transposition solution (inspired by
[
20
]
) (see Subsection 4.1).

2.

We provide a relationship
between the optimal feedback operator and the
operator-valued BSLE
(see Subsection 4.2). More precisely,
with
subtle choice of
v
, small
\varepsilon>0
such
that
t+\varepsilon\leq T
, we introduce control
variables
\Theta\bar{X}
and
\Theta X^{v,\varepsilon}+vI_{[t,t+\varepsilon]}
associated with
optimal feedback operator
\Theta
. At this moment, we point out one novel technical contribution. We refer to Remark
4.1
for more relevant details.

3.

Using the result established in step 2, we prove that the aforementioned
H_{\lambda}
-transposition solution of BSLE is just the transposition solution of BSREs.

At this very moment, let us summarize our contributions of the current article:

•

From the viewpoint of methodology, we develop some useful procedures to overcome the essential obstacles and limitation in the current relevant literature. Interestingly, some technologies happen to be new even in finite dimensional scenario.

•

From the viewpoint of conclusions, we considerably relax the
C_{0}
-group assumption of operator
A
and impose the
C_{0}
-semigroup assumption instead. This allows us to cover LQ problem for stochastic parabolic PDEs, which is one of the most important class of SPDEs.

It is worth noting that, besides the study of
optimal feedback controls for LQs,
operator-valued Riccati equations have other
applications (e.g.,
[
26
]
). We believe
that both the results and the methods in this
paper have other applications, such as the study
of open quantum systems with continuation
observation. Nevertheless, a detailed study for
it is beyond the scope of this paper and will be
done in our future works.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows.
In Section 2, we present some necessary
notations, important remarks and a complete
formulation of our optimal control problems. In
Section 3, we state our main result, i.e., the
characterization of optimal feedback operators
by means of well-posedness of operator-valued
backward stochastic Riccati equation in the
suitable transposition sense. In Section 4, we
first establish the well-posedness of
operator-valued backward stochastic Lyapunov
equation (BSLE for short) in Subsection 4.1,
then give a characterization of optimal
feedback operator via the operator-valued BSLE
in Subsection 4.2 and then demonstrate the proof
of the main result. In Section 5, we present an
illustrative example for LQ problem of
stochastic parabolic PDEs.

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad|L(\cdot,\cdot)|_{{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T))\big\}.\end{array}

(2.3)

We shall simply denote

\Upsilon_{p}({\cal X};{\cal X})
by

\Upsilon_{p}({\cal X})
.

Lemma 2.1

Assume that
{\cal X}
and
{\cal Y}
are separable
Hilbert spaces. For any
L\in\Upsilon_{p}({\cal X};{\cal Y})
, there exist a sequence

\{L_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{\infty}\big(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y}))\big)

such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}L_{n}v=Lv\quad\mbox{ in
}L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y})),\quad\forall\,v\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(\Omega;L^{\infty}(0,T;{\cal X})).

(2.4)

Proof
. Since
{\cal X}
(
resp.
{\cal Y}
) is a
separable Hilbert space, there is an orthomormal
basis
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
(
resp.
\{\tilde{e}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
) of
{\cal X}

(
resp.
{\cal Y}
). Denote by
{\cal X}_{n}
(
resp.
{\cal Y}_{n}
) the
subspace spanned by
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
(
resp.
\{\tilde{e}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{n}
) and by
\Gamma_{n}
(
resp.
\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n}
) the orthogonal projection operator
from
{\cal X}
(
resp.
{\cal Y}
) to
{\cal X}_{n}
(
resp.
{\cal Y}_{n}
).

Let
L_{n}=\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n}L\Gamma_{n}
. We first prove that

\{L_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})))
.

Since
L_{n}(t,\omega)\in{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})
for
a.e.
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
, we
have
L_{n}(t,\omega)\in{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})
for
a.e.
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
.
Moreover, from the definition of
L_{n}
,
we know that for a.e.
(t,\omega)\in[0,T]\times\Omega
,
L_{n}(t,\omega)
can be
regarded as an element in

{\cal L}({\cal X}_{n};{\cal Y}_{n})
.

Since
L(\cdot,\cdot)\!\in\mathbf{L}(L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(\Omega;L^{\infty}(0,T;{\cal X}));L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;

{\cal Y})))
, for any
x\in{\cal X}
, we have that

L_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)x\in L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y}_{n}))
. Noting that

{\cal L}({\cal X}_{n};{\cal Y}_{n})
is isomorphic to

{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
, we get that
L_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)\in L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,

T;{\cal L}({\cal X}_{n};{\cal Y}_{n})))
. Further, noting that

|L_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)|_{{\cal L}({\cal X}_{n};{\cal Y}_{n})}=|L_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)|_{{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})}\leq|L(\cdot,\cdot)|_{{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})},

it follows from

|L(\cdot,\cdot)|_{{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T))
that

|L_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)|_{{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T))
. This,
together with
L_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)\in L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal L}({\cal X}_{n};{\cal Y}_{n})))
, implies that

L_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)\in L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal L}({\cal X}_{n};{\cal Y}_{n})))
. Hence,

L_{n}(\cdot,\cdot)\in L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{\infty}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})))
.

For any
v\in L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}}}(\Omega;L^{\infty}(0,T;{\cal X}))
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle|L_{n}v-Lv|_{L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y}))}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle=|\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n}L\Gamma_{n}v-Lv|_{L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y}))}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leq|\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n}L\Gamma_{n}v-\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n}Lv|_{L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y}))}+|\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n}Lv-Lv|_{L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y}))}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\leq|L\Gamma_{n}v-Lv|_{L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y}))}+|\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n}Lv-Lv|_{L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y}))}.\end{array}

(2.5)

Since

\lim_{n\to\infty}|L\Gamma_{n}v-Lv|_{{\cal Y}}=0

and

|L\Gamma_{n}v-Lv|_{{\cal Y}}\leq 2|L|_{{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y})}|v|_{{\cal X}},

by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we get that

\lim_{n\to\infty}|L\Gamma_{n}v-Lv|_{L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y}))}=0.

(2.6)

Similarly, we can prove that

\lim_{n\to\infty}|\widetilde{\Gamma}_{n}Lv-Lv|_{L_{{\mathbb{F}}}^{2}(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal Y}))}=0.

(2.7)

Combining (
2.5
)–(
2.7
), we
obtain (
2.4
).

Remark 2.1

One can show that

L^{\infty}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\big(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y}))\big)\!\subset\!\Upsilon_{p}({\cal X};{\cal Y})
. For any
L(\cdot,\cdot)\!\in\Upsilon_{p}({\cal X};{\cal Y})
, one does not need to have

L(\cdot,\cdot)\in L^{\infty}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\big(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y}))\big)
.
The reason for introducing such set is the lack
of measurability for operator-valued functions
and processes appeared in concrete problems. For
example, generally speaking, a
C_{0}
-semigroup
is not measurable with respect to the time
variable (e.g.,
[
13
, Subsection
1.1.c]
). In such case, one
has to relax the condition on the measurability
of such functions and processes. Nevertheless,
as we shall see later, in some sense

\Upsilon_{p}({\cal X};{\cal Y})
is a nice “replacement”
of the space

L^{\infty}_{{\mathbb{F}}}\big(\Omega;L^{p}(0,T;{\cal L}({\cal X};{\cal Y}))\big)

in the study of stochastic LQ problems.

Now we put the following assumptions on the

(

)
The

(

(

The coefficients

\begin{cases}\displaystyle A_{1}(\cdot)\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\cal L}(H))),\quad B(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\cal L}(U;H))),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle C(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\cal L}(H))),\quad D(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\cal L}(U;H)),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle Q(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\cal S}(H))),\quad R(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\cal S}(U)),\quad G\in L^{\infty}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;$ ${\cal S}(H)),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle G\geq 0,\quad R>0,\quad Q\geq 0\mbox{

Under (
AS1
), the control system

) admits a unique

Problem (SLQ):
For each

Set

.

.

Remark 2.4

Note the set

depends on the
parameter
\lambda
. Once a

is

, namely,

depends on the choice of

Denote by

It is well-known that

is a Hilbert space itself
with the inner product

\big\langle F_{1},F_{2}\big\rangle_{{\cal L}_{2}(H;H_{\lambda}^{\prime})}\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\big\langle F_{1}e_{j},F_{2}e_{j}\big\rangle_{H_{\lambda}^{\prime}}.

Denote by
{\cal L}_{2}(H_{\lambda};H)
the set of all

to

.
Similarly,

Definition 2.1

An operator
\Theta(\cdot)\in\Upsilon_{2}(H;U)
is called an optimal
feedback operator for Problem (SLQ) if

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\cal J}(s,\eta;\Theta(\cdot)\bar{x}(\cdot))\leq{\cal J}(s,\eta;u(\cdot)),\\[5.69054pt]
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\forall\;(s,\eta)\in[0,T)\times L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{s}}(\Omega;H),\;\;u(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(s,T;U),\end{array}

(2.10)

where
\bar{x}(\cdot)=\bar{x}(\cdot\,;s,\eta,\Theta(\cdot)\bar{x}(\cdot))
solves

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle d\bar{x}(t)=\big[(A+A_{1})\bar{x}(t)+B\Theta\bar{x}(t)\big]dt+\big(C\bar{x}(t)+D\Theta\bar{x}(t)\big)dW(t)&\mbox{ in
}(s,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\bar{x}(s)=\eta.\end{array}\right.

(2.11)

,
\Theta(\cdot)
is

. For a fixed

) implies that

3
The statement of the main results

To characterize the optimal feedback operator,
we need the following operator-valued, backward
stochastic Riccati equation:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle dP=-\big[P(A+A_{1})+(A+A_{1})^{*}P+\Lambda C+C^{*}\Lambda\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\;\;\quad+C^{*}PC+Q-L^{*}K^{-1}L\big]dt+\Lambda dW(t)&\mbox{in }[0,T),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle P(T)=G,\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

where

K\equiv R+D^{*}PD,\qquad L=B^{*}P+D^{*}(PC+\Lambda).

(3.2)

Before presenting the main result, we need to recall the definition
of transposition solution
(P,\Lambda)
of (
3.1
). To this end,
let us consider the following two (forward) SEEs:

(3.3)

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle x_{2}(t)=\xi_{2}.\end{array}\right.

Here
t\in[0,T)
,
\xi_{1},\xi_{2}
are suitable

are

Put

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle C_{{\mathbb{F}},w}\big([0,T];L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\cal L}(H))\big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\triangle}}\!\Big\{P\!:\![0,T]\!\times\!\Omega\to\!{\cal L}(H)\,\Big|\,|P|_{{\cal L}(H)}\!\in\!L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T),\,\chi_{[t,T]}P(\cdot)\zeta\!\in\!C_{\mathbb{F}}([t,T];L^{2}(\Omega;H)),\ \forall\,\zeta\!\in\!L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{t}}(\Omega;H)\Big\},\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}}_{2}(H;H_{\lambda}^{\prime})\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\Delta}}}\big\{F\!\in\!{\cal L}_{2}(H;H_{\lambda}^{\prime})\;\big|\;F|_{H_{\lambda}},\mbox{ the restriction of }F\mbox{ on }H_{\lambda},\mbox{ is a Hilbert-Schmidt }\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\hskip 137.99603pt\mbox{ operator from }H_{\lambda}\mbox{ to }H,\mbox{ and }(F|_{H_{\lambda}})^{*}=F\big\},\end{array}

(3.5)

is a Hilbert space with the inner product inherited from

.

These spaces are Banach spaces with
the norms

|P|_{C_{{\mathbb{F}},w}\big([0,T];L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\cal S}(H))\big)}\mathop{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt=}\limits^{\Delta}}}\big||P|_{{\cal L}(H)}\big|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T)},\quad\forall P\in C_{{\mathbb{F}},w}\big([0,T];L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\cal S}(H))\big)

respectively.

Definition 3.1

We call
\big(P(\cdot),\Lambda(\cdot)\big)\in C_{{\mathbb{F}},w}\big([0,T];L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\cal S}(H))\big)\times L^{2}_{{\mathbb{F}},D,w}(0,T;{\cal S}(H))
a

conditions hold:

1)

K(t,\omega)\big(\equiv R(t,\omega)+D(t,\omega)^{*}P(t,\omega)D(t,\omega)\big)>0

;

2)
For any

,

\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in L^{4}_{{\cal F}_{t}}(\Omega;H)
,

\in L^{4}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(t,T;H_{\lambda}))
, with

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle g\in L^{\infty}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;C^{2m}(\overline{{\cal O}})),\quad q\geq 0,\;\;r>0,\;\;g\geq 0\;\mbox{ for a.e.
}(t,x,\omega)\in[0,T]\times{\cal O}\times\Omega.\end{cases}

(5.4)

From (
5.4
), one can check
that (
AS1
) holds.

Write
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
for the
eigenvalues of
A
and
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

the corresponding eigenvectors satisfying

|e_{j}|_{L^{2}({\cal O})}=1
for all
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
. It is
well known that
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

constitutes an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}({\cal O})
.
Hence, (
AS2
) holds.

Let
\lambda_{j}=|\mu_{j}|^{-\frac{m}{2}}
for

j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\lambda=\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
. Then

H_{\lambda}^{\prime}
is the completion of the
Hilbert space
L^{2}({\cal O})
with respect
to the norm

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle|f|_{H_{\lambda}^{\prime}}=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\mu_{j}|^{-m}(1+|\mu_{j}|)^{-2}|f_{j}|^{2}},\qquad\mbox{ for }f=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f_{j}e_{j}\in L^{2}({\cal O}).\end{array}

By the asymptotic distribution of
eigenvalues of
A
(e.g.,

[
24
, Chapter 1, Theorem 1.2.1 and
Remark 1.2.2]
), we have

\mu_{j}={\cal C}_{1}j^{\frac{2}{m}}+O(1)
for
some constant
{\cal C}_{1}>0
. Therefore,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle|I|^{2}_{{\cal L}_{2}(H;H_{\lambda}^{\prime})}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\big\langle e_{j},e_{j}\big\rangle_{H_{\lambda}^{\prime}}\leq 4\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\mu_{j}|^{-n}<\infty.\end{array}

Hence, the embedding from
L^{2}({\cal O})

to
H_{\lambda}^{\prime}
is Hilbert-Schmidt. From the
definition of
H_{\lambda}^{\prime}
, it follows that

H_{\lambda}=D(A^{\frac{m}{2}+1})
.

Let
\alpha\in C^{2m}(\overline{{\cal O}})
. For any
f\in H_{\lambda}
, one has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle|\alpha f|_{H_{\lambda}}&\displaystyle=|\alpha f|_{D(A^{{\frac{m}{2}+1}})}=\sqrt{|\alpha f|_{L^{2}({\cal O})}^{2}+|A^{{\frac{m}{2}+1}}(\alpha f)|_{L^{2}({\cal O})}^{2}}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\displaystyle\leq{\cal C}|\alpha|_{C^{2m}(\overline{{\cal O}})}|f|_{D(A^{\frac{m}{2}+1})}={\cal C}|\alpha|_{C^{2m}(\overline{{\cal O}})}|f|_{H_{\lambda}}.\end{array}

From this, and recalling
(
5.4
), we conclude that

A_{1}\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\cal L}(H_{\lambda})))
,

C\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;

L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\cal L}(H_{\lambda})))
,
G\in L^{\infty}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;{\cal L}(H_{\lambda}))
and

Q\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\cal L}(H_{\lambda})))
.
Similarly, we can prove that
A_{1}\in L^{1}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega;{\cal L}(H_{\lambda}^{\prime})))
,

C\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega;

{\cal L}(H_{\lambda}^{\prime})))
,
G\in L^{\infty}_{{\cal F}_{T}}(\Omega;{\cal L}(H_{\lambda}^{\prime}))
and

Q\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\cal L}(H_{\lambda}^{\prime})))
.
Thus, (
AS3
) holds.

Let
\widetilde{U}=D(A^{\frac{m}{2}+1})
. Then
\widetilde{U}

is dense in
L^{2}({\cal O})
. Let
\alpha\in C^{2m}(\overline{{\cal O}})
. For any
f\in\widetilde{U}
, one
has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle|\alpha f|_{H_{\lambda}}&\displaystyle=|\alpha f|_{D(A^{\frac{m}{2}+1})}=|A^{\frac{m}{2}+1}(\alpha f)|_{L^{2}({\cal O})}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr&\displaystyle\leq|\alpha|_{C^{2m}(\overline{{\cal O}})}|f|_{D(A^{\frac{m}{2}+1})}=|\alpha|_{C^{2m}(\overline{{\cal O}})}|f|_{\widetilde{U}}.\end{array}

From this, and using (
5.4
), we find
that
B\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\cal L}(\widetilde{U};H_{\lambda})))
,
D\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;

{\cal L}(\widetilde{U};H_{\lambda}))
and
R\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\cal L}(\widetilde{U}))
. Similarly, we can prove that
B\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega;L^{2}(0,T;{\cal L}(\widetilde{U}^{\prime};H_{\lambda}^{\prime})))
,

D\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\cal L}(\widetilde{U}^{\prime};H_{\lambda}^{\prime}))
and

R\in L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;{\cal L}(\widetilde{U}^{\prime}))
.
Therefore, (
AS4
) holds.

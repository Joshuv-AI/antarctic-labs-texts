Generic Global Rigidity in ℓ p \ell_{p} -Space and the Identifiability of the p p -Cayley-Menger Varieties
Tomohiro Sugiyama, Shin-ichi Tanigawa

Abstract
The celebrated result of Gortler-Healy-Thurston (independently, Jackson-Jordán for d = 2 d=2 ) shows that the global rigidity of graphs realised in the d d -dimensional Euclidean space is a generic property. Extending this result to the global rigidity problem in ℓ p \ell_{p} -spaces remains an open problem. In this paper we affirmatively solve this problem when d = 2 d=2 and p p is an even positive integer. A key tool in our proof is a sufficient condition for the d d -tangential weak non-defectivity of projective varieties due to Bocci, Chiantini, Ottaviani, and Vannieuwenhoven. By specialising the condition to the p p -Cayley-Menger variety, which is the ℓ p \ell_{p} -analogue of the Cayley-Menger variety for Euclidean distance, we provide an ℓ p \ell_{p} -extension of the generic global rigidity theory of Connelly. As a by-product of our proof, we also offer a purely graph-theoretical characterisation of the 2 2 -identifiability of an orthogonal projection of the p p -Cayley-Menger variety along a coordinate axis of the ambient affine space.



Keywords:
graph rigidity, generic global rigidity,
\ell_{p}
-spaces, Cayley-Menger varieties, identifiability, tangential weak defectivity

1
Introduction

Generic rigidity is the central idea in graph rigidity theory. A classical result due to Gluck
[
gluck
]
and Asimov-Roth
[
asimow1978rigidity
]
states that local rigidity is a generic property, meaning that a graph is
either locally rigid for almost all locations of vertices in the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space or
flexible for almost all locations of vertices in the same space.
A deeper result due to Gortler-Healy-Thurston
[
gortler2010characterizing
]
(independently, Jackson-Jordán
[
jackson2005connected
]
for
d=2
)
shows that global rigidity is also a generic property.
This fact is a cornerstone in recent developments of graph rigidity theory (see, e.g.,
[
JW
]
), and since then there have been many efforts to extend it to a wide range of global rigidity problems. However the current achievements in this research direction are not so extensive, and a generic property is known only for variants of Euclidean rigidity such as global rigidity on a cylinder
[
jackson2019
]
, on a flat torus
[
KST
]
, on linear subspaces
[
guler2021global
,
cruickshank2024global
]
, in pseudo-Euclidean spaces
[
GT
]
, or global rigidity problems under linear measurements such as direction rigidity.
There are also negative results for other measurement functions such as Euclidean inner product
[
JJT16
]
or Euclidean volume
[
Southgate
]
, where the corresponding global rigidity problem may not necessarily be a generic property.
Currently, there is no theory that characterizes which measurement functions give a global rigidity problem with the generic property.

In this paper, we consider the global rigidity problem in
\ell_{p}
-spaces, and show that global rigidity is a generic property in the
\ell_{p}
-plane if
p
is an even positive integer.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first generic global rigidity result for a high-degree polynomial measurement.
The major difficulty in extending the result of Euclidean rigidity to a general
\ell_{p}
-space is the lack of the theory of stress matrices.
This theory, originated by Connelly
[
connelly2005generic
]
, extensively uses the fact that the squared Euclidean distance is a quadratic polynomial, and there is no straightforward counterpart argument for non-quadratic distance functions.
We will overcome this difficulty using a tool in
[
cruickshank2023identifiability
]
.
Cruickshank, Mohammadi, Nixon, and the second author
[
cruickshank2023identifiability
]
have recently established a close connection between the global rigidity problem under a general measurement and the identifiability problem of secant varieties.
In this paper, we provide a more detailed exposition of this connection by specialising our focus to the
p
-Cayley-Menger variety, which is the
\ell_{p}
-analogue of the well-known Cayley-Menger variety for Euclidean distance.
It turns out that, when we apply a sufficient condition for the
d
-tangential weak non-defectivity (and the
d
-identifiability) by Borcci, Chiantini, Ottaviani, and Vannieuwenhoven
[
BCC
,
chiantini2014algorithm
]
to the
p
-Cayley-Menger variety, we obtain an
\ell_{p}
-analogue of Connelly’s sufficient condition.
We show that this condition is also necessary in the
\ell_{p}
-plane by using the inductive construction of 2-connected redundantly locally rigid graphs by Dewar, Hewetson, and Nixon
[
dewar2024uniquely
]
.

A by-product of our proof is a purely graph-theoretical characterisation of the
2
-identifiability of an axis-parallel projection of the
p
-Cayley-Menger variety.
While the identifiability of projective varieties has been an active topic in algebraic geometry,
there seems to be little knowledge on how the identifiability changes when a non-generic projection is applied.
We give a combinatorial and efficiently testable characterisation for when the
2
-identifiability of the
p
-Cayley-Menger variety is preserved by a projection along a coordinate axis of the ambient affine space.

Let us also review previous results on graph rigidity in
\ell_{p}
-space.
The theory of local rigidity in
\ell_{p}
-space was initiated by Kitson and Power
[
kitson2014infinitesimal
]
, who provided a neat characterisation of local rigidity in the
\ell_{p}
-plane.
The result of Kitson and Power has been extended to more general normed spaces
[
D21
,
K15
]
,
and the higher dimensional case has been also studied
[
dewarkitson2022
]
.
Global rigidity has not been well understood,
but recently Dewar, Hewetson, and Nixon
[
dewar2024uniquely
,
dewarnixon2022
]
gave a combinatorial characterisation of graphs that can be globally rigid in a generic realisation in an analytic normed space.
However, it is still not known whether the global rigidity is a generic property beyond Euclidean space.
We also note that having the generic property provides a significant advantage in the detailed analysis of global rigidity properties, such as global linkedness, see, e.g.,
[
jordan2024globally
]
for results in the Euclidean case.

The paper is organised as follows.
In Section
2
,
we introduce the basic notation and terminology on graph rigidity and then formally state our results.
In Section
3
, we explain the identifiability and tangential weak defectivity, key new tools in our global rigidity analysis,
and prove a Connelly-type sufficient condition for generic global rigidity in general dimensions.
As an application of the tools introduced in Section

, in Section

we give a sufficient condition for generic global rigidity in general dimensions in terms of generic local rigidity.
In Section
5
, we prove our main result, a characterisation of generic global rigidity in the
\ell_{p}
-plane.

.
For a point or a vector

denotes the

, we write

.
A point
\bm{x}

is sometimes regarded as a matrix of size

.

For a vector space

, let

.
For a matrix

.

2
Rigidity in
\ell_{p}
-space and main theorems

In this section, we shall first review preliminary results on graph rigidity and then state our main results.

2.1
Local and global rigidity

A
d
-dimensional
framework
is a pair
(G,\bm{p})
of a graph
G=(V,E)
and a map
\bm{p}:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The map
\bm{p}
is often referred to as a
point configuration
, and it is sometimes regarded as a point in
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
, where
n=|V|
.
Two
d
-dimensional frameworks
(G,\bm{p})
and
(G,\bm{q})
with the same underlying graphs are said to be
equivalent
if

\|\bm{p}(i)-\bm{p}(j)\|_{p}=\|\bm{q}(i)-\bm{q}(j)\|_{p}\qquad(ij\in E(G)),

and they are
congruent
if
there is an isometry
\iota
of
\ell_{p}^{d}
such that

\bm{q}(i)=\iota(\bm{p}(i))
for all
i\in V(G)
.
When
p\neq 2
,
\iota
is
a translation, a permutation of coordinates,
or a reflection along a coordinate axis.

A framework
(G,\bm{p})
is
globally rigid
in
\ell_{p}^{d}

if every framework equivalent to
(G,\bm{p})
is congruent,
and it is called
locally rigid
if this happens in a small neighbour of
\bm{p}
in
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
.
An equivalent way to define local rigidity is in terms of continuous motions: a framework
(G,\bm{p})
is not locally rigid if and only if
there is a continuous map
t\mapsto\bm{p}^{t}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
for
t\in[0,1]

such that
\bm{p}^{0}=\bm{p}
and
(G,\bm{p}^{t})
is equivalent but not congruent to
(G,\bm{p})
for
t>0
. See, e.g.,
[
kitson2014infinitesimal
]
for more details.

A key tool to analyse rigidity is the following
\ell_{p}
-analogue of the rigidity map (or the measurement map).
For a positive integer
d
and a real number
p
with
1<p<\infty
, define
f_{p}^{\times d}:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n\choose 2}
by

(f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p}))_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}((\bm{p}(i))_{k}-(\bm{p}(j))_{k})^{p}\qquad(\bm{p}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}).

(1)

For a graph
G=(V,E)
, define the projection map
\pi_{G}:\mathbb{R}^{n\choose 2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{E}
along coordinate axes onto
\mathbb{R}^{E}
, which is the subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{n\choose 2}
indexed by the elements in
E
.
Then
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p})
is the list of the
p
-th powered edge lengths of
(G,\bm{p})
when
p
is an even integer.
Hence we have the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
p
is an even positive integer with
p\neq 2
and
(G,\bm{p})
be a framework in
\ell_{p}^{d}
.
Then
(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid if and only if
any
\bm{q}
in the fibre of
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p})
under
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}
can be obtained from
\bm{p}
by a translation, a permutation of coordinates, and the reflections along coordinate axes.

2.2
Generic local rigidity

It is an NP-hard problem to decide the local/global rigidity of a given framework in a Euclidean space,
and a common tractable approach is to restrict our attention to generic frameworks. We say that a framework
(G,\bm{p})
or a point-configuration
\bm{p}:V(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
generic
if the set of coordinates of
\bm{p}
is algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.

For generic frameworks, local rigidity admits the following simple characterisation, which is the adaptation of a result of Gluck
[
gluck
]
or Asimov-Roth
[
asimow1978rigidity
]
to the
\ell_{p}
-setting.

Proposition 2.2
.

(Kitson and Power
[
kitson2014infinitesimal
]
)
Suppose
(G,\bm{p})
is a generic framework and let
p
be a real number with
1<p<\infty
and
p\neq 2
.
Then
(G,\bm{p})
is locally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
if and only if

\rank J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d})(\bm{p})=dn-d
.

The equivalence between two properties in Proposition
2.2

may not be valid if
(G,\bm{p})
is not generic.
A framework
(G,\bm{p})
is called
infinitesimally rigid
in
\ell_{p}^{d}

if
\rank J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d})(\bm{p})=dn-d
.

Proposition
2.2
implies that, for a graph
G
and a positive integer
d
,
either every generic framework
(G,\bm{p})
is locally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
or
no generic framework
(G,\bm{p})
is locally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
.
Hence, we may say that a graph
G
is
locally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
if a/every generic framework
(G,\bm{p})
is locally rigid. Since this is a property of graphs, it is natural to ask a combinatorial characterization. For
d=2
, Kitson and Power gave a concrete answer.

Theorem 2.3

(Kitson and Power
[
kitson2014infinitesimal
]
)
.

Suppose
p
is a real number with
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
p\neq 2
.
A graph
G
is locally rigid in the
\ell_{p}
-plane if and only if

G
is 2-tree-connected, i.e.,
G
contains two edge-disjoint spanning trees.

In general, a graph is said to be
k
-tree-connected
if it contains
k
edge-disjoint spanning trees.
Kitson and Power also conjectured that
d
-tree-connectivity characterises the
d
-dimensional local rigidity.
This problem is still open.

2.3
Generic global rigidity in Euclidean space

Before moving to global rigidity in
\ell_{p}
-space, we shall quickly review results in Euclidean space.

The central approach to the generic global rigidity problem is the theory of self-stresses due to Connelly
[
connelly2005generic
]
.
Given a
d
-dimensional framework
(G,\bm{p})
, a map
\omega:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called a
self-stress
(in Euclidean space) if
it satisfies the following equilibrium condition:

\sum_{v:uv\in E(G)}\omega(ij)(\bm{p}(j)-\bm{p}(i))=0\qquad(u\in V(G)).

(2)

A
stress matrix
of a framework
(G,\bm{p})
is the Laplacian matrix of
G
weighted by a self-stress. In general, for a graph
G
with the vertex set
V=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and an edge weight
\omega:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the weighted Laplacian
L_{G,\omega}
is an
n\times n
symmetric matrix with the entries defined by

L_{G,\omega}[i,j]=\begin{cases}\sum_{k:ik\in E(G)}\omega(ik)&(i=j)\\
-\omega(ij)&(i\neq j,ij\in E(G))\\
0&(i\neq j,ij\notin E(G)).\end{cases}

Weighted Laplacians and the equilibrium condition (
2
) are closely related. Indeed, given
(G,\bm{p})
and
\omega:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
\omega
is a self-stress of
(G,\bm{p})
if and only if
each coordinate vector of
(G,\bm{p})
(that is, the
n
-dimensional vector obtained by aligning
(\bm{p}(1))_{k},(\bm{p}(2))_{k},\dots,(\bm{p}(n))_{k}
) is in the kernel of
L_{G,\omega}
.
Moreover, any Laplacian has the all-one vector in its kernel.
Therefore, for any self-stress
\omega
of
(G,\bm{p})
,
the dimension of the kernel of
L_{G,\omega}
is at least one plus the dimension of the affine span of the points of
\bm{p}
.
In particular, if
(G,\bm{p})
is generic and
n\geq d+1
, then

\rank L_{G,\omega}\leq n-(d+1)
.

Theorem 2.4

(Gortler-Healy-Thurston
[
gortler2010characterizing
]
)
.

A generic framework
(G,\bm{p})
in the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space with
n\geq d+2
vertices
is globally rigid if and only if

(G,\bm{p})
has a self-stress
\omega
such that

\rank L_{G,\omega}=n-(d+1)
.

Since the condition of Theorem
2.4
is algebraic in the coordinates of
\bm{p}
, Theorem
2.4
implies that Euclidean global rigidity is a generic property of graphs.
Thus it enables us to extend the idea of graph local rigidity by Gluck, Asimov, and Roth to global rigidity, and we say that a graph
G
is
globally rigid
in the
d
-dimensional Euclidean space if a/every generic framework of
G
in
\ell_{2}^{d}
is globally rigid.
As with local rigidity, we can further ask a combinatorial characterization of global rigidity. For
d=2
, this question has been answered by Jackson and Jordán
[
jackson2005connected
]
,
and the higher dimensional characterisation problem is still open.
(In fact, the proof of Jackson and Jordán is independent from that of Gortler-Healy-Thurston in the sense that it also gives Theorem
2.4
for two-dimensional frameworks.)
A further refined analysis of global rigidity in terms of self-stresses can be found in
[
G23
]
.

2.4
Global rigidity problem in
\ell_{p}
-space

Characterising global rigidity is a challenging problem in general, but the following folklore fact states that the problem is easy in the 1-dimensional line.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(G,\bm{p})
be a framework in the
\ell_{p}
-line with
n\geq 2
vertices such that
\bm{p}
is injective.

•

If
(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid, then
G
is 2-connected.

•

If
(G,\bm{p})
is generic and
G
is 2-connected, then
(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid.

The necessary condition for generic global rigidity in general dimensions has been shown by Dewar, Hewetson, and Nixon
[
dewar2024uniquely
]
by adapting Hendrickson’s argument.
Although they have established the result in a more general non-Euclidean normed space, we only mention the result in the
\ell_{p}
-plane.

Theorem 2.6

(Dewar, Hewetson, and Nixon
[
dewar2024uniquely
]
)
.

Let
p
be an even positive integer with
p\neq 2

and let
(G,\bm{p})
be a generic framework in
\ell_{p}^{2}
with
n\geq 2
vertices.
If
(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid, then
G
is 2-connected and

G
is redundantly 2-tree-connected, i.e.,

G-e
is 2-tree-connected for every
e\in E(G)
.

The main theorem of the paper by Dewar, Hewetson, and Nixon
[
dewar2024uniquely
]
(which is also given in a general non-Euclidean normed plane) is that a graph satisfying the combinatorial necessary condition in Theorem
2.6
can be realised as a globally rigid generic framework.

Theorem 2.7

(Dewar, Hewetson, and Nixon
[
dewar2024uniquely
]
)
.

Let
p
be an even positive integer with
p\neq 2
and
G=(V,E)
be a graph with
n\geq 2
vertices.
Then there exists a globally rigid generic framework
(G,\bm{p})
in
\ell_{p}^{2}

if and only if
G
is 2-connected and
G
is redundantly 2-tree-connected.

2.5
Main rigidity theorems

The main result of this paper is the
\ell_{p}
-counterpart of Theorem
2.4
when
d=2
and
p
is even.
The result in particular implies that 2-dimensional global
\ell_{p}
-rigidity is a generic property of graphs.

To state our theorems,
we need to introduce a new notion about self-stresses.
Let
p
be a positive integer with
p\geq 2
.
For a
d
-dimensional framework
(G,\bm{p})
,
we define a
self-stress
of
(G,\bm{p})
as
\omega:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\sum_{j:ij\in E(G)}\omega(ij)(\bm{p}(j)-\bm{p}(i))^{p-1}=0\qquad(u\in V(G)),

(3)

or, equivalently,
\omega
is in the left kernel of
J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d})(\bm{p})
.
(Recall our convention that

(\bm{p}(j)-\bm{p}(i))^{p-1}
denotes the vector obtained by taking the
k
-th power of each coordinate of
\bm{p}(j)-\bm{p}(i)
.)
A
k
-th coordinated
self-stress is
\omega^{k}:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

\omega^{k}(ij):=\omega(ij)\left((\bm{p}(j))_{k}-(\bm{p}(i)_{k})\right)^{p-2}\qquad(ij\in E(G)).

(4)

for some self-stress
\omega:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
(G,\bm{p})
.

Now we consider the Laplacian
L_{G,\omega^{k}}
weighted by a
k
-th coordinated self-stress
\omega^{k}
.
Since
L_{G,\omega^{k}}
is a Laplacian matrix,
the all-one vector is in its kernel.
Also, since (
3
) and (
4
)
imply (
2
) in the
k
-th coordinate,
we have that
\rank L_{G,\omega^{k}}\leq n-2
.

We are now ready to state our main result.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
p
be an even positive integer with
p\neq 2
and let
(G,\bm{p})
be a generic

2
-dimensional framework with
n\geq 3
vertices.
Then
(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{2}
if and only if

(G,\bm{p})
has a
k
-th coordinated self-stress
\omega^{k}
for some
k

such that
\rank L_{G,\omega^{k}}=n-2
.

Since the condition of Theorem
2.8
is algebraic in the coordinates of
\bm{p}
, Theorem
2.8
implies that the global rigidity in the
\ell_{p}
-plane is a generic property of graphs.
Combining this with Theorem
2.7
, we have a Jackson and Jordán type characterisation in
\ell_{p}^{2}
, see Theorem
.

We strongly believe that the same characterisation is valid in general.

Conjecture 2.9
.

Let
p
be an even positive integer with
p\neq 2
,
d
be a positive integer, and
(G,\bm{p})
be a generic
d
-dimensional framework with
n\geq 3
vertices.
Then
(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
if and only if

(G,\bm{p})
has a
k
-th coordinated self-stress
\omega^{k}
for some
k

such that
\rank L_{G,\omega^{k}}=n-2
.

It should be noted that the proof of the sufficiency is valid in any dimension. This is stated in Theorem
3.6
and it offers an
\ell_{p}
-analogue of Connelly’s sufficient condition
[
connelly2005generic
]
.

Example 2.10
.

As a simplest example, consider the case when
G=K_{3}
and

(G,\bm{x})
is a 1-dimensional framework.
Then,

J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times 1})(\bm{x})=\begin{array}[]{cccc}&v_{1}&v_{2}&v_{3}\\
v_{1}v_{2}&(\bm{x}(v_{1})-\bm{x}(v_{2}))^{p-1}&(\bm{x}(v_{2})-\bm{x}(v_{1}))^{p-1}&0\\
v_{1}v_{3}&(\bm{x}(v_{1})-\bm{x}(v_{3}))^{p-1}&0&(\bm{x}(v_{3})-\bm{x}(v_{1}))^{p-1}\\
v_{2}v_{3}&0&(\bm{x}(v_{2})-\bm{x}(v_{3}))^{p-1}&(\bm{x}(v_{3})-\bm{x}(v_{2}))^{p-1}\\
\end{array}.

It is straightforward to check that

\displaystyle\omega

\displaystyle=(\omega(v_{1}v_{2}),\omega(v_{1}v_{3}),\omega(v_{2}v_{3}))

\displaystyle=((\bm{x}(v_{1})-\bm{x}(v_{2}))^{1-p},(\bm{x}(v_{3})-\bm{x}(v_{1}))^{1-p},(\bm{x}(v_{2})-\bm{x}(v_{3}))^{1-p})

is a unique self-stress of
(G,\bm{x})
up to constant, and the Laplacian matrix weighted by the first-coordinated stress is

L_{G,\omega^{1}}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{3})-\bm{x}(v_{1})}+\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{1})-\bm{x}(v_{2})}&-\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{1})-\bm{x}(v_{2})}&-\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{3})-\bm{x}(v_{1})}\\
-\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{1})-\bm{x}(v_{2})}&\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{1})-\bm{x}(v_{2})}+\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{2})-\bm{x}(v_{3})}&-\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{2})-\bm{x}(v_{3})}\\
-\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{3})-\bm{x}(v_{1})}&-\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{2})-\bm{x}(v_{3})}&\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{2})-\bm{x}(v_{3})}+\frac{1}{\bm{x}(v_{3})-\bm{x}(v_{1})}\end{pmatrix}.

L_{G,\omega^{1}}
has rank equal to
1=|V(G)|-2
. ∎

The second of our main results is the following sufficient condition in terms of local rigidity in general dimension.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
p
be an even positive integer with
p\neq 2
,
d
be a positive integer, and
(G,\bm{p})
be a generic
d
-dimensional framework with
n
vertices.
Then
(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
if

G
is 2-connected and
G
is locally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d+1}
.

An application of Theorem
2.11
is a sharp lower bound of the sampling probability for the Erdős-Rényi random graph to be globally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
.
For non-negative integers
n,M
, the Erdős-Rényi random graph
G(n,M)
is defined inductively by first setting
G(n,0)
to be the empty graph with
n
vertices
and then setting
G(n,M)
to be obtained from
G(n,M-1)
by adding a new edge taken uniformly randomly from the complement of
G(n,M-1)
.
Consider the following three random variables,

\displaystyle M_{\ell_{p}^{d}-LR}

\displaystyle:=\min\{M:\text{$G(n,M)$ is locally rigid in $\ell_{p}^{d}$}\},

\displaystyle M_{\ell_{p}^{d}-GR}

\displaystyle:=\min\{M:\text{$(G(n,M),\bm{p})$ is globally rigid in $\ell_{p}^{d}$ for every generic $\bm{p}$}\},\text{and}

\displaystyle M_{d}

\displaystyle:=\min\{M:\delta(G(n,M))=d\},

where
\delta(H)
denotes the minimum degree of a graph
H
.
Recently, Lew et al.
[
lew2023sharp
]
showed that

\mathrm{a.a.s.}

M_{\ell_{2}^{d}-LR}=M_{d}
and
M_{\ell_{2}^{d}-GR}=M_{d+1}
for Euclidean rigidity.
Their argument is still valid for local rigidity in
\ell_{p}
-space,
and hence Theorem
2.11
further implies the global rigidity threshold as follows.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
p
be an even positive integer with
p\neq 2
and
d
be a positive integer.
Then,
\mathrm{a.a.s.}

M_{\ell_{p}^{d}-LR}=M_{d}
and
M_{\ell_{p}^{d}-GR}=M_{d+1}
.

The proofs of Theorems
2.8
and
2.11
are given in Sections
5
and
4
, respectively.

2.6
Comments on the self-stress condition in Theorem
2.8

Theorem
2.8
(or Conjecture
2.9
) can be used to develop an efficient method for checking if a/every generic realisation of
G
is globally rigid.
This is an adaptation of a technique in Euclidean global rigidity
[
gortler2010characterizing
]
.

Let
(G,\bm{p})
be a generic locally rigid framework in
\ell_{p}^{d}
, and
G_{0}
be a minimally locally rigid spanning subgraph of
G
.
(Here a graph
H
is said to be minimally locally rigid if
H
is locally rigid and
H-e
is not locally rigid for any
e\in E(H)
.)
For each
e\in E(G)\setminus E(G_{0})
, there exists a unique self-stress
\omega_{e}
of
(G_{0}+e,\bm{p})
such that
\omega_{e}(e)=1
.
Observe that
\{\omega_{e}\}_{e\in E(G)\setminus E(G_{0})}
forms a basis of the linear space of self-stresses of
(G,\bm{p})
.
Hence, a self-stress
\omega
of
(G,\bm{p})
can be written, in a unique way, as

\omega=\sum_{e\in E(G)\setminus E(G_{0})}c_{e}\omega_{e}

(5)

for some real numbers
\bm{c}=\{c_{e}\}_{e\in E(G)\setminus E(G_{0})}
.
We say that
\omega
is
generic
with respect to
G_{0}
if

\{c_{e}\}_{e\in E(G)\setminus E(G_{0})}
is algebraically independent over the field
\mathbb{Q}(\bm{p})
generated by
\mathbb{Q}
and the entries of
\bm{p}
,
and
\omega
is
generic
if
it is generic with respect to some minimally locally rigid spanning subgraph
G_{0}
of
G
.

Since each entry of
\omega_{e}
is a rational function in the entries of
\bm{p}
, a self-stress is written as a rational map
\omega(\bm{p},\bm{c})
of
\bm{p}
and
\bm{c}
.
This implies that the entries in the Laplacian matrix
L_{G,\omega(\bm{p},\bm{c})^{k}}
are also rational functions of
\bm{p}
and
\bm{c}
.
Hence the rank of
L_{G,\omega(\bm{p},\bm{c})^{k}}
will be maximised whenever both
\bm{p}
and
\omega
are generic.
Also, such rational functions in
\bm{p}
are symmetric with respect to permutations of coordinates.
Thus, we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.13
.

(
\ell_{p}
-analogue of a theorem of Connelly and Whiteley
[
connelly2005generic
]
)
Let
p
be an integer with
p\geq 2
,
and
(G,\bm{p})
be a
d
-dimensional framework.
If
(G,\bm{p})
is infinitesimally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
, then

(G,\bm{q})
is infinitesimally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
for every generic framework
(G,\bm{q})
.
Moreover, if
(G,\bm{p})
has a self-stress
\omega
with
\rank L_{G,\omega^{k}}=|V(G)|-2
for some
k
, then
\rank L_{G,{\omega^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime}}}=|V(G)|-2
holds for every generic framework
(G,\bm{q})
, every generic self-stress
\omega^{\prime}
of
(G,\bm{q})
, and every
k^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,d\}
.

Proposition
2.13
says that, in order to confirm the stress condition in Theorem
2.8
or Conjecture
2.9
, it is sufficient to construct an infinitesimally rigid framework
(G,\bm{p})
(which may not be generic) that admits a self-stress
\omega
with
\rank L_{G,\omega^{k}}=|V(G)|-2
for some
k
.

We also remark the following simple property of weighted Laplacians.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and
\omega:E(G)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
be an edge weight.
Suppose
G
has a
1
-separator
(H_{1},H_{2})
,
that is,
G=H_{1}\cup H_{2}
,
|V(H_{1})\cap V(H_{2})|=1
, and

V(H_{1})\setminus V(H_{2})\neq\emptyset\neq V(H_{2})\setminus V(H_{1})
.
Let
\omega_{i}
be the restriction of
\omega
to
E(H_{i})
.

(i)

\rank L_{G,\omega}=\rank L_{H_{1},\omega_{1}}+\rank L_{H_{2},\omega_{2}}
.

(ii)

If
\omega
is a self-stress of a
d
-dimensional framework
(G,\bm{p})

and
\bm{p}|H_{i}
be the restriction of
\bm{p}
to
V(H_{i})
for
i=1,2
,
then
\omega_{i}
is a self-stress of
(H_{i},\bm{p}|H_{i})
.

Proof.

Denote the vertex in
V(H_{1})\cap V(H_{2})
by
v
,
and denote

L_{H_{i},\omega_{i}}=\begin{array}[]{ccc}&V(H_{i})\setminus\{v\}&v\\
V(H_{i})\setminus\{v\}&A_{i}&b_{i}^{\top}\\
v&b_{i}&c_{i}\\
\end{array}

Since
H_{i}
is connected and each entry of
\omega_{i}
is nonzero,
b_{i}
is not a zero vector.
Moreover, since the sum of columns (resp., rows) of
L_{H_{i},\omega_{i}}
is zero,
the rank of
A_{i}
is equal to that of
L_{H_{i},\omega_{i}}
.

By
G=H_{1}\cup H_{2}
,

L_{G,\omega}=\begin{array}[]{cccc}&V(H_{1})\setminus\{v\}&v&V(H_{2})\setminus\{v\}\\
V(H_{1})\setminus\{v\}&A_{1}&b_{1}^{\top}&0\\
v&b_{1}&c_{1}+c_{2}&b_{2}\\
V(H_{2})\setminus\{v\}&0&b_{2}^{\top}&A_{2}\\
\end{array}.

In this matrix,
adding all the columns of
V(H_{1})\setminus\{v\}
to that of
v

and then adding all the rows of
V(H_{1})\setminus\{v\}
to that of
v

result in

\begin{pmatrix}A_{1}&0&0\\
0&c_{2}&b_{2}\\
0&b_{2}^{\top}&A_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(6)

Hence,

\rank L_{G,\omega}=\rank A_{1}+\rank L_{H_{2},\omega_{2}}=\rank L_{H_{1},\omega_{1}}+\rank L_{H_{2},\omega_{2}}.

This completes the proof of (i).

To see (ii), recall the fact that
infinitesimal rigidity in
\ell_{p}
-space is invariant under translations of a framework. This implies that
the
d
columns associated with vertex
v
are redundant in the column space of
J(\pi_{H_{i}}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p}|H_{i}))
,
and the equilibrium condition (
3
) at
v
automatically holds if it holds at other vertices.
Hence, if
\omega
is a self-stress of
(G,\bm{p})
, then
\omega_{i}
is a self-stress of
(H_{i},\bm{p}|H_{i})
.
∎

Lemma
2.14
implies that
2-connectivity is necessary for the stress condition in Theorem
2.8
.

3
Identifiability and Global Rigidity

In this section we shall introduce tools from algebraic geometry,
which are used in the proofs of Theorems
2.8
and
2.11
.
The materials in this section can be understood as a detailed exposition of
[
cruickshank2023identifiability
, Section 7.2]
by specializing discussion to
\ell_{p}
-rigidity.

Since our tools are taken from the context of projective varieties over the complex field, we shall look at the affine cone of projective varieties over the complex field.
It should be noted that our rigidity results are established over the real field and we look at frameworks with real point configurations when referring to rigidity.

Let
{\cal V}
be an affine variety in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
.
The
d
-secant

S_{d}({\cal V})
of
{\cal V}
is defined as the Zariski closure of the union of linear subspaces spanned by
d
points in
{\cal V}
.
If
{\cal V}
is a cone, we have

S_{d}({\cal V})=\overline{\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{d}x_{k}:x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in{\cal V}\right\}},

where the upper bar denotes the Zariski closure.

For a point
x
in
{\cal V}
, let
T_{x}{\cal V}
be the tangent space of
{\cal V}
at
x
.
If
{\cal V}
is irreducible, a point
x
in
{\cal V}
is said to be
generic
if any polynomial with rational coefficients vanishing at
x
is vanishing at every point in
{\cal V}
.

3.1
Cayley-Menger varieties

For a positive integer
n
, define

f_{2}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n\choose 2}
by

(f_{2}(\bm{x}))_{ij}=(x_{i}-x_{j})^{2}\qquad(\bm{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n})

for each
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq n
.
When
\bm{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
f_{2}(\bm{x})
is the list of squared inter-point distances over points
x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}
in the real line.
Over
\mathbb{C}
, the Zariski closure of the image of
f_{2}
is known as the
Cayley-Menger variety

{\cal CM}_{n}
,
and the
d
-secant is called the
d
-dimensional Cayley-Menger variety.
The
d
-dimensional Cayley-Menger variety has been extensively studied in the context of rigidity theory, and as shown by Gortler, Healy, and Thurston
[
gortler2010characterizing
]
it plays a critical role in the analysis of generic global rigidity.

In this paper, we consider the
\ell_{p}
-analogue of the Cayley-Menger variety.
For a positive integer
p
, define

f_{p}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n\choose 2}
by

(f_{p}(\bm{x}))_{ij}=(x_{i}-x_{j})^{p}\qquad(\bm{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n})

for each
i,j
with
1\leq i<j\leq n
.
The Zariski closure of the image of
f_{p}
is called the
p
-Cayley-Menger variety

{\cal CM}_{n}^{p}
.

Since
f_{p}^{\times d}
is a polynomial map (defined in (
1
)), it can be also defined over
\mathbb{C}
.
Similarly, the projection map
\pi_{G}
is defined over
\mathbb{C}

by
\pi_{G}:\mathbb{C}^{n\choose 2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{E(G)}
.
For
\bm{p}\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{n}
,
let
\bm{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the
i
-th coordinate vector of
\bm{p}
.
Then

\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{x}_{i}).

This in particular implies that

\overline{{\rm image}\ \pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}}
is the
d
-secant of
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
.
(The above equation only implies

\overline{{\rm image}\ \pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}}\subseteq S_{d}(\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})})
,
but both sets are irreducible and have the same dimension,
so they are equal.)

Remark.

Although we have defined the
p
-Cayley-Menger variety for any positive integer
p
, its
d
-secant does not coincide with the image of the
\ell_{p}
-distance measurement map when
p
is odd.
Indeed, if
p
is odd, the one-dimensional
\ell_{p}
-distance measurement map with the
p
-th power becomes a piecewise polynomial map of the form
({\rm sign}(x_{i}-x_{j}))(x_{i}-x_{j})^{p}
.
Accordingly, our connection between
\ell_{p}
-global rigidity and the identifiability of the
p
-Cayley-Menger variety (given in Proposition
3.3
below) is currently limited to the case where
p
is even.
Extending this connection to the case where
p
is odd remains an open problem.

3.2
Defectivity

In this subsection, we shall first look at a relation between the local rigidity of graphs
G
and the defectivity of
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
.
The content of this section is described in more detail in
[
cruickshank2023identifiability
]
in a general form,
and we will provide an exposition by restricting our discussion to
{\cal CM}_{n}^{p}
for completeness.

Suppose an affine variety
\mathcal{V}
is a cone in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
.
Then the
d
-secant
S_{d}(\mathcal{V})
has dimension at most
\min\{d\dim\mathcal{V},m\}
.
The latter number is called the
expected dimension
, and
\mathcal{V}
is said to be
d
-defective
if
\dim S_{d}(\mathcal{V})
is smaller than the expected dimension.

When
{\cal V}=\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
,
the
d
-defectivity is directly related to local rigidity as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
p
be an integer with
p>2
.
Suppose
G
is a connected graph with
n
vertices and
|E(G)|\geq dn-d
.
Then
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
is not
d
-defective if and only if
G
is locally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
.

Proof.

Let
{\cal V}=\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
.
Then
\dim{\cal V}=n-1
as long as
G
is connected.
Hence, assuming that
G
is connected and
|E(G)|\geq dn-d
,

{\cal V}
is
d
-defective if and only if
\dim S_{d}(\mathcal{V})<dn-d
.

Note that
\dim S_{d}(\mathcal{V})
is equal to the dimension of the tangent space of
S_{d}(\mathcal{V})
at a generic point.
Since
S_{d}(\mathcal{V})=\overline{{\rm image}\ \pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}}
, for a generic
\bm{p}

the dimension of the tangent space of
S_{d}(\mathcal{V})
at
f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p})
is equal to

\rank J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d})(\bm{p})
.
Therefore,
{\cal V}
is
d
-defective if and only if

\rank J(\pi\circ f_{p}^{\times d})(\bm{p})<dn-d
.

When
p
is an integer with
p>2
, this condition is exactly the condition for the generic framework
(G,\bm{p})
not to be locally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
by Proposition
2.2
.
∎

3.3
Identifiability

Let
{\cal V}
be an affine variety in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
and suppose that

{\cal V}
is a cone.

{\cal V}
is called
d
-identifiable
if any generic point

x\in S_{d}({\cal V})
can be written as

x=\sum_{k=1}^{d}x_{k}
for unique
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in{\cal V}
up to a scaling of each
x_{i}
and a permutation of indices.

When
{\cal V}
is
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
and
\bm{p}
is generic,

\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p})
is a generic point in the
d
-secant of

\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
.
Using this interpretation, we reduce the global rigidity problem to the
d
-identifiability problem of
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
in Proposition
3.3
below.

We first check that the
d
-identifiablity imposes the following connectivity condition.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
p
be a positive integer,
d
be an integer with
d\geq 2
, and
G
be a graph with
n\geq 3
vertices.
If
\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})
is
d
-identifiable, then
G
is 2-connected.

Proof.

Consider a generic
d
-dimensional framework
(G,\bm{p})
.
Then
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p})
is a generic point in the
d
-secant of

\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
.
If
G
is not 2-connected, then by
n\geq 3
,
G
can be written as
G=H_{1}\cup H_{2}
such
that
E(H_{1})\cap E(H_{2})=\emptyset
and
|V(H_{1})\cap V(H_{2})|\leq 1
.
We only consider the case when
|V(H_{1})\cap V(H_{2})|=1
since the case when
|V(H_{1})\cap V(H_{2})|=0
is easier.

Let
v
be the vertex in
V(H_{1})\cap V(H_{2})
.
By translations, we may assume that
\bm{p}(v)
is at the origin.
We consider another framework
(G,\bm{q})
obtained by permutating the coordinates of
\bm{p}
only on
V(H_{2})\setminus V(H_{1})
(keeping the points of
V(H_{1})
fixed).
We have
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p})=\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{q})
,
while

\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{p}_{i})
and
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{q}_{i})
are different for the coordinate vectors
\bm{p}_{i}
and
\bm{q}_{i}
by
d\geq 2
.
Hence,
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
is not
d
-identifiable.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p
be an even positive integer,
d
be an integer with
d\geq 2
, and
(G,\bm{p})
be a generic framework with
n\geq 3
vertices.
If
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
is
d
-identifiable over
\mathbb{C}
, then
(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
.

Proof.

To see the global rigidity of
(G,\bm{p})
,
pick any framework
(G,\bm{q})
equivalent to
(G,\bm{p})
in
\ell_{p}^{d}
-space.
Let
\bm{p}_{i}
(resp.
\bm{q}_{i}
) be the
i
-th coordinate vector of
\bm{p}
(resp.
\bm{q}
) for each
i
.
Then

\sum_{i=1}^{d}\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{p}_{i})=\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p})=\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{q})=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{q}_{i}).

(7)

Since
\bm{p}
is generic,
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d}(\bm{p})
is a generic point in the
d
-secant of
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
.
Hence, by the
d
-identifiability of
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
,
(
7
) implies that there exists a permutation
\sigma
on
\{1,2\dots,d\}
such that
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{q}_{i})=\lambda_{i}\left(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{p}_{\sigma(i)})\right)
for some
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}
for each
i=1,\dots,d
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that the permutation
\sigma
is the identity.
Then

\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{q}_{i})=\lambda_{i}\left(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{p}_{i})\right)\quad\text{for each $i=1,\dots,d$}.

(8)

Suppose that
\lambda_{i}=1
for all
i
.
Then
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{q}_{i})=\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{p}_{i})
,
and hence
(G,\bm{p}_{i})
and
(G,\bm{q}_{i})
are equivalent as one-dimensional frameworks in
\ell_{p}^{1}
. Since
G
is 2-connected by Proposition
3.2
, one can apply Proposition
2.5
to show that
(G,\bm{q}_{i})
is congruent to
(G,\bm{p}_{i})
for all
i
.
Hence,
(G,\bm{q})
is congruent to
(G,\bm{p})
, and
(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid.

We now show that
\lambda_{i}=1
for all
i
.
Since
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
is
d
-identifiable,
the minimum vertex degree of
G
is at least
d
.
(Here we used the fact that, if the minimum degree of
G
is less than
d
, then
(G,\bm{p})
is not locally rigid
[
kitson2014infinitesimal
]
and hence a continuous motion of
(G,\bm{p})
gives a certificate that
\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})
is not
d
-identifiable.)
Pick any vertex
v
of
G
and let
u_{1},\dots,u_{d}
be distinct neighbours of
v
in
G
. Let
d_{ij}
be the
i
-th coordinate of
\bm{p}(u_{j})-\bm{p}(v)
for each
1\leq i,j\leq d
.
Then, the
vu_{j}
-th entry of
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{p}_{i})
is
d_{ij}^{p}
.
On the other hand, by (
8
),
the
vu_{j}
-th entry of
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{q}_{i})
is
\lambda_{i}d_{ij}^{p}
.
Substituting them into (
7
), we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{d}d_{ij}^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\lambda_{i}^{p}d_{ij}^{p}\qquad(j=1,\dots,d).

(9)

Since
\bm{p}
is generic, the set of
d_{ij}
over
1\leq i,j\leq d
is algebraically independent over
\mathbb{Q}
,
and hence the
d\times d
matrix
(d_{ij}^{p})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
is non-singular. Thus (
9
) implies
\lambda_{i}^{p}=1
for each
i
.
Since the entries of
\bm{p}
and
\bm{q}
are real and
p
is even,

\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{q}_{i})
and
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{p}_{i})
are both non-negative real numbers. Hence, by (
8
) and
\lambda_{i}^{p}=1
,
\lambda_{i}=1
follows.
This completes the proof.
∎

3.4
Tangential weak defectivity

The identifiability problem of varieties has been extensively studied in algebraic geometry.
In this paper, we shall use the idea of tangential weak defectivity introduced by Chiantini and Ottaviani
[
chiantini2012generic
]
to analyse the identifiability of
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
.

Let
{\cal V}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{m}
be the affine cone of a projective variety and
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
be generic points in
{\cal V}
.
The
d
-tangent contact locus
at
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
is defined by

C_{x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}{\cal V}:=\overline{\{y\in{\cal V}:\text{$y$ is non-singular, }T_{y}{\cal V}\subset\langle T_{x_{1}}{\cal V},\dots,T_{x_{d}}{\cal V}\rangle\}}.

Since
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
are generic,
the Terracini lemma implies
T_{\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}}S_{d}({\cal V})=\langle T_{x_{1}}{\cal V},\dots,T_{x_{d}}{\cal V}\rangle
.
Hence the
d
-tangent contact locus is equivalently defined as

C_{x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}{\cal V}:=\overline{\{y\in{\cal V}:\text{$y$ is non-singular, }T_{y}{\cal V}\subset T_{\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}}S_{d}({\cal V})\}},

Since
{\cal V}
is a cone,
\dim C_{x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}{\cal V}\geq 1
.

{\cal V}
is
d
-tangentially weakly defective
if
an irreducible component of
C_{x_{1},\dots,x_{d}}{\cal V}
that contains
x_{i}
has dimension at least two for some
i
.

Chiantini and Ottaviani
[
chiantini2012generic
]
showed that, if
{\cal V}
is not
d
-tangentially weakly defective, then
{\cal V}
is
d
-identifiable.
In terms of the
p
-Cayley-Menger varieties, we have the following.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
p
be an integer with
p>2
and
G
be a connected graph with
n\geq 3
vertices.
If
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
is not
d
-tangentially weakly defective,
then
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
is
d
-identifiable.

Since Proposition
3.4
is a key fact in this paper and it seems a useful tool in other rigidity problems, we are going to give a proof in Section
3.5
.

In view of Proposition
3.4
, the next question we should address is how we can check the
d
-tangential weak non-defectivity.
Bocci, Chiantini, Ottaviani, and Vannieuwenhoven
[
BCC
,
chiantini2014algorithm
,
chiantini2017generic
]
gave a linear algebraic technique to test this property for Segre varieties.
Interestingly, an adaptation of their idea to the
p
-Cayley-Menger varieties leads to our graph Laplacian condition in Section
2.5
.
The details are explained in the next lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
p
be an integer with
p>2
and
(G,\bm{p})
be a generic
d
-dimensional framework with
n\geq 2
vertices.
If
(G,\bm{p})
has a
k
-th coordinated self-stress
\omega^{k}
such that

\rank L_{G,\omega^{k}}=n-2
for all
k\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, then
\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
is not
d
-tangentially weakly defective.

Proof.

For simplicity we use the following notations.
For
i=1,\dots,d
, let
\bm{p}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the
i
-th coordinate vector of
\bm{p}
,
and let
x_{i}=\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{p}_{i})
.
Let
{\cal V}=\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
,
and
{\cal C}
be the
d
-tangent contact locus of
{\cal V}
at
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
.
Let
{\cal C}^{\prime}
be an irreducible component of
{\cal C}
that contains
x_{k}
.
Instead of proving
\dim{\cal C}^{\prime}=1
directly, we shall look at
(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p})^{-1}({\cal C}^{\prime}\cap{\cal V})
.
Since
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}
is invariant by the addition of a constant to each coordinate
and
\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}
is locally surjective around
\bm{p}
onto the image,
(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p})^{-1}({\cal C}^{\prime}\cap{\cal V})
has at least one larger dimension than
{\cal C}^{\prime}
.
So in order to prove
\dim{\cal C}^{\prime}=1
,
it is sufficient to prove that
(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p})^{-1}({\cal C}^{\prime}\cap{\cal V})
is contained in a two-dimensional variety.

Now, by the lemma assumption
(G,\bm{p})
has a
k
-th coordinated self-stress

\omega^{k}
such that
\rank L_{G,\omega^{k}}=n-2
.
By the definition of
k
-th coordinated self-stresses, there is a self-stress
\omega
of
(G,\bm{p})
from which
\omega^{k}
is constructed via (
4
).
Since the equilibrium condition (
3
) for
\omega
is equivalent to
being in the left kernel of
J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d})(\bm{p})
,
the fact that
\omega
is a self-stress of
(G,\bm{p})

implies that
\omega
is in the left kernel of
J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d})(\bm{p})
as a vector in
\mathbb{C}^{E(G)}
.
Moreover, the column space of
J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times d})(\bm{p})
is

\langle T_{x_{1}}{\cal V},T_{x_{2}}{\cal V},\dots,T_{x_{d}}{\cal V}\rangle
,
so we conclude that
\omega
is
in the orthogonal complement of

\langle T_{x_{1}}{\cal V},T_{x_{2}}{\cal V},\dots,T_{x_{d}}{\cal V}\rangle
.

For each
\bm{y}\in(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p})^{-1}({\cal C}^{\prime}\cap{\cal V})
,
we have

T_{\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{y})}{\cal V}\subseteq\langle T_{x_{1}}{\cal V},T_{x_{2}}{\cal V},\dots,T_{x_{d}}{\cal V}\rangle
by the definition of
{\cal C}
,
and thus
\omega
is in the orthogonal complement of
T_{\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}(\bm{y})}{\cal V}
. The latter condition is equivalent to saying that

\omega
is a self-stress of the one-dimensional framework
(G,\bm{y})
.
Hence, we obtain

(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p})^{-1}({\cal C}^{\prime}\cap{\cal V})\subseteq{\cal S}_{\omega}:=\{\bm{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\text{$\omega$ is a self-stress of $(G,\bm{y})$}\}.

By the definition of self-stresses,

\bm{y}\in{\cal S}_{\omega}
if and only if

\sum_{v:uv\in E(G)}\omega(uv)(\bm{y}(v)-\bm{y}(u))^{p-1}=0\qquad(u\in V(G)).

This is a system of
n
polynomial equations with
n
variables
\bm{y}(1),\bm{y}(2),\dots,\bm{y}(n)
.
Let
h_{\omega}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the polynomial map representing this system of polynomial equations.
Then
\bm{y}\in{\cal S}_{\omega}
if and only if
h_{\omega}(\bm{y})=0
.
So
{\cal S}_{\omega}
is an algebraic set.
Observe that
Jh_{\omega}(\bm{y})
is exactly the Laplacian matrix
L_{G,\omega^{\prime}}
of
G
weighted by
\omega^{\prime}
given by

\omega^{\prime}(uv):=(p-1)\cdot\omega(uv)\cdot(\bm{y}(v)-\bm{y}(u))^{p-2}\qquad(uv\in E(G)).

Hence,
Jh_{\omega}(\bm{p}_{k})=(p-1)L_{G,\omega^{k}}
.
By the lemma assumption,
\rank L_{G,\omega^{k}}=n-2
.
So
\dim\ker Jh_{\omega}(\bm{p}_{k})=2
.
Since
\dim\ker Jh_{\omega}(\bm{y})\geq 2
for any
\bm{y}\in{\cal S}_{\omega}
,
we conclude that

\bm{p}_{k}
is a non-singular point in
{\cal S}_{\omega}
,
and the irreducible component of
{\cal S}_{\omega}
that contains
\bm{p}_{k}
is two-dimensional.
This completes the proof.
∎

Combining the results so far, we have the following sufficient condition for global rigidity in
\ell_{p}^{d}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
p
be an even positive integer with
p\neq 2
,
d
be a positive integer, and
(G,\bm{p})
be a generic framework in
\ell_{p}^{d}
with
n\geq 3
vertices.
If
(G,\bm{p})
has a
k
-th coordinated self-stress
\omega^{k}
such that
\rank L_{G,\omega^{k}}=n-2
for all
k
, then

(G,\bm{p})
is globally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{d}
.

Proof.

Suppose the stress condition holds.
By Lemma
2.14
,
G
is 2-connected.
Also, by Lemma
3.5
,
{\cal V}=\overline{\pi_{G}({\cal CM}_{n}^{p})}
is not
d
-tangentially weakly defective.
By Proposition
3.4
,
{\cal V}
is
d
-identifiable.
Finally, by the 2-connectivity of

, and the

.
∎

Let

Then by definition

d:=(\bm{x}(v_{1})-\bm{x}(v_{2}))^{p-1}

By
\bm{x}^{\prime}(v_{0})=(\bm{x}^{\prime}(v_{1})+\bm{x}^{\prime}(v_{2}))/2
,
we can use elementary row operations to reduce

to the form

\left(\begin{array}[]{c | c}d&{\begin{array}[]{cc}-d&\ 0\end{array}}\\
\hline\cr O&J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times 1})(\bm{x})\end{array}\right).

Thus,
\rank J(\pi_{G^{\prime}}\circ f_{p}^{\times 1})(\bm{x}^{\prime})=\rank J(\pi_{G}\circ f_{p}^{\times 1})(\bm{x})+1=|V(G^{\prime})|-1
.

Next, using the self-stress
\omega
of
(G,\bm{x})
, we define a self-stress
\omega^{\prime}
of
(G^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})
as follows:

\omega^{\prime}(e)=\begin{cases}2^{p-1}\omega(v_{1}v_{2})&(e\in\{v_{0}v_{1},v_{0}v_{2}\})\\
\omega(e)&(e\in E(G)\setminus\{v_{1}v_{2}\}),\end{cases}

for
e\in E(G)
.
It is easy to see that
\omega^{\prime}\in\ker J(\pi_{G^{\prime}}\circ f_{p}^{\times 1})(\bm{x}^{\prime})^{\top}
, and hence
\omega^{\prime}
is a self-stress of
(G^{\prime},\bm{x}^{\prime})
.
Then, the Laplacian matrix
L_{G^{\prime},{\omega^{\prime}}^{1}}
is given by

L_{G^{\prime},{\omega^{\prime}}^{1}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}0&0\\
\hline\cr 0&L_{G,\omega^{1}}\\
\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}[]{c | c}\begin{array}[]{ccc}4\omega(v_{1}v_{2})&-2\omega(v_{1}v_{2})&-2\omega(v_{1}v_{2})\\
-2\omega(v_{1}v_{2})&\omega(v_{1}v_{2})&\omega(v_{1}v_{2})\\
-2\omega(v_{1}v_{2})&\omega(v_{1}v_{2})&\omega(v_{1}v_{2})\\
\end{array}&O\\
\hline\cr O&O\end{array}\right).

Since
\omega(v_{1}v_{2})\neq 0

L_{G^{\prime},{\omega^{\prime}}^{1}}
at the left-top corner.
The resulting matrix
L_{G^{\prime},{\omega^{\prime}}^{1}}/v_{0}
is exactly
L_{G,\omega^{1}}
.
Thus,
\rank L_{G^{\prime},{\omega^{\prime}}^{1}}=1+\rank L_{G^{\prime},{\omega^{\prime}}^{1}}/v_{0}=1+\rank L_{G,\omega^{1}}=|V(G^{\prime})|-2
.
∎

In a graph
G
, an inclusion-wise maximal 2-connected subgraph (having at least three vertices) is called a
2-connected component

and an inclusion-wise maximal 2-edge-connected subgraph (which may consist of a single vertex) is called a
2-edge-connected component
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
p
be an integer with
p>2
and
(G,\bm{x})
be a generic one-dimensional framework.
Then
(G,\bm{x})
has a
1
-st coordinated self-stress
\omega^{1}
such that
\rank L_{G,\omega^{1}}=|V(G)|-a-b
,
where
a
and
b
denote the number of 2-connected components and that of 2-edge-connected components in
G
, respectively.

Proof.

First suppose that
G
is 2-connected.
Then
a=b=1
.
It is a basic fact from graph theory that

G
can be constructed inductively from
K_{3}
by subdividing edges and adding edges keeping 2-connectivity.
By Proposition
2.2
,
G
and the intermediate graphs are redundantly locally rigid in
\ell_{p}^{1}
.
We have already seen in Example
2.10

that
K_{3}
satisfies the property of the statement.
Also, by the redundant rigidity of
G
and Lemma
4.3
, for every intermediate graph
H
and its generic point configuration, there exists a generic self-stress
\omega_{H}
such that
\rank L_{H,\omega_{H}^{1}}=|V(H)|-2=|V(H)|-a-b
.

Suppose secondly that
G
is not 2-connected but 2-edge-connected with at least two vertices.
Then the set of all 2-connected components form a tree structure (known as the block-cut-tree).
Applying Lemma
2.14
along this tree structure,
we obtain
\rank L_{G,\omega^{1}}=|V(G)|-a-1
for any generic framework
(G,\bm{x})
and any generic stress
\omega
of
(G,\bm{x})
.

Finally, we consider a general case.
Let
\omega
be a generic self-stress of
(G,\bm{x})
.
For any bridge
e
in
G
,
(G-e,\bm{x})
is not rigid
and hence
\omega(e)=0
holds.
Therefore, if we denote by
H
the graph obtained from
G
by removing all bridges of
G
, then
the restriction
\omega^{\prime}
of
\omega
to
E(H)
is a self-stress of
(H,\bm{x})
.
Since
\omega(e)=0
for every bridge
e
, we have

L_{G,\omega^{1}}=L_{H,\omega^{\prime 1}}
.
So
\rank L_{G,\omega^{1}}
is equal to the sum of the ranks of the Laplacian sub-matrices over all connected components in
H
.
Note that the set of connected components in
H
coincides with the set of 2-edge-connected components in
G
(some of which may consist of a single vertex).
If a 2-edge-connected component
C
has at least two vertices, then the weighted Laplacian has rank
|V(C)|-a_{C}-1
, where
a_{C}
denotes the number of 2-connected components in
C
.
If a 2-edge-connected component
C
consists of a single vertex,
then the corresponding weighted Laplacian has rank zero,
which is
|V(C)|-a_{C}-1
(by
a_{C}=0
).
Taking the sum of those values over all 2-edge-connected components, we obtain the desired rank formula.
∎

Step 1 has been already studied extensively

-extension

Let
G

by a

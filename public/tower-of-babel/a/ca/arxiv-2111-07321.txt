The discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem for the electrostatic 𝔭 {\mathfrak{p}} -capacity
Minhyun Kim, Taehun Lee

Abstract
. The Minkowski problem for electrostatic capacity characterizes measures generated by electrostatic capacity, which is a well-known variant of the Minkowski problem. This problem has been generalized to L p L_{p} Minkowski problem for 𝔭 {\mathfrak{p}} -capacity. In particular, the logarithmic case p = 0 p=0 relates to cone-volumes and therefore has a geometric significance. In this paper we solve the discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem for 1 < 𝔭 < n 1<{\mathfrak{p}}<n in the case where the support of the given measure is in general position.

1.
Introduction

A cornerstone of the Brunn–Minkowski theory is the Minkowski problem which asks if a given measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
arises as the surface area measure of a convex body.
This problem was completely solved by Minkowski himself
[
21
]
, Aleksandrov
[
1
,
2
]
, and Fenchel–Jessen
[
11
]
.
Precisely, if a measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is not concentrated on any closed hemisphere of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, then
\mu
is the surface area measure of a convex body if and only if the centroid of
\mu
is the origin, i.e.,
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\xi\,\mathrm{d}\mu(\xi)=0
.
The regularity of the solution has been studied by Nirenberg
[
22
]
, Cheng–Yau
[
8
]
, Pogorelov
[
23
]
, and Caffarelli
[
5
]
.

An important variant of the Minkowski problem initiated by Jerison
[
14
]
is the Minkowski problem for the electrostatic capacity.
In a similar way, it asks if a given measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
arises as the
electrostatic capacitary measure
. The electrostatic capacitary measure is defined by the differential of the capacity as the surface area measure is defined by the differential of the volume.
Jerison found the necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a convex body which are surprisingly identical to the corresponding conditions in the classical Minkowski problem (for volume).

A lot of research has been inspired by the work of Jerison. Namely,
L_{p}
Minkowski problem for the electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity, which will be described below, has been investigated intensively. The aim of this paper is to consider the logarithmic case
p=0
for discrete measures which relates to the cone-volumes
1
1

1

For a polytope that has the origin in its interior, the cone-volume of a face of the polytope is the volume of the convex hull of the face and the origin.
.

The result for the discrete Minkowski problem can be understood as prescribing the areas of faces of a polytope. From this perspective, a natural, important variant among other Minkowski type problems is the problem that prescribes the cone-volumes of a polytope instead of the surface areas.
The discrete Minkowski problem for the cone-volumes can be stated in the following way:

Discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem.

Let
u_{1},\cdots,u_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be unit vectors with a set of numbers
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{N}>0
. Find necessary and sufficient conditions on the set of unit vectors and the numbers so that there exists an
N
-faced polytope whose outer unit normals are
u_{1},\cdots,u_{N}
and the corresponding cone-volumes are
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{N}
.

Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang, and Zhang
[
4
]
solved the problem when the unit vectors are even, i.e.,
\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{N}\}=\{-u_{1},\cdots,-u_{N}\}
. In
[
28
]
, Zhu proved the problem in the case that the unit vectors are in
general position
(see
Definition

2.1
) and are not concentrated on any closed hemisphere. Later, the discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem was solved under a more general assumption that contains the two results above as special cases
[
3
]
.

It is worth noting that the logarithmic Minkowski problem for general measures was solved by Böröczky et al.
[
4
]
in the case of even measures. For non-even measures, Chen, Li, and Zhu
[
6
]
proved the existence of the solution under the same assumption as in
[
4
]
. We also note that there is no known conjecture characterizing cone-volume measures.

To describe the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem, we recall the notion of the
L_{p}
surface area. The surface area measure
S(K,\cdot)
of
K
appears in the celebrated Aleksandrov variational formula: for convex bodies
K
and
L
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}V(K+tL)}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0+}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L}(u)\,\mathrm{d}S(K,u),

where
V
is the
n
-dimensional volume. For an index
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the
L_{p}
surface area measure
S_{p}(K,\cdot)
is then defined by

\displaystyle S_{p}(K,\omega)=\int_{\omega}h_{K}(u)^{1-p}\,\mathrm{d}S(K,u)

for any Borel set
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
.

L_{p}
Minkowski problem.

Let
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Find necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
so that
\mu
is the
L_{p}
surface area measure
S_{p}(K,\cdot)
of a convex body
K
.

The
L_{1}
Minkowski problem is the classical Minkowski problem, and the
L_{0}
Minkowski problem is the logarithmic Minkowski problem. Another important special case is the
L_{-n}
Minkowski problem, also known as the centro-affine Minkowski problem.

Along with the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem for volume, there is a parallel
L_{p}
Minkowski-type problem.
This problem is concerned with the electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity of convex bodies, and is known as the
L_{p}
Minkowski problem for
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity.
To describe the problem, we recall the definition of the electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity and its variational formula.

Let
1<{\mathfrak{p}}<n
. For a compact set
K
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, the electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity is defined as

\displaystyle C_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)=\inf\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|\nabla u|^{\mathfrak{p}}\,\mathrm{d}x:u\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),u\geq\chi_{K}\right\},

where
\chi_{K}
is the characteristic function of
K
and
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
denotes the set of smooth functions with compact supports.
The case
{\mathfrak{p}}=2
is the classical electrostatic (or Newtonian) capacity of
K
.

As in the volume case, the differential of the electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity produces a geometric measure. Indeed, the electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacitary measure
\mu_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,\cdot)
of
K
appears in the Hadamard variational formula for
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity
[
10
]
: for convex bodies
K
and
L
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(K+tL)}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0+}=({\mathfrak{p}}-1)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L}(u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,u).

In particular, the formula implies the Poincaré
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity formula

\displaystyle C_{\mathfrak{p}}(K)=\frac{{\mathfrak{p}}-1}{n-{\mathfrak{p}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{K}(u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,u).

In a similar way as in the construction of the
L_{p}
surface area measure from the surface area measure, we introduce the
L_{p}
electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacitary measure.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
1<{\mathfrak{p}}<n
. Suppose that
K
is a convex body in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with the origin in its interior, and the measure
\mu_{p,{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,\cdot)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{p,{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,\omega)=\int_{\omega}h_{K}(u)^{1-p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,u)

for any Borel set
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. The measure
\mu_{p,{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,\cdot)
is called the
L_{p}
electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacitary measure.

Note that the
L_{p}
electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacitary measure appears in the
L_{p}
variational formula of the electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity
[
30
]
. Precisely, for convex bodies
K
and
L
with the origin in their interiors, it follows that for
p\geq 1

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(K+_{p}t\cdot_{p}L)}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0+}=\frac{{\mathfrak{p}}-1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}h_{L}(u)^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{p,{\mathfrak{p}}}(K,u).

We now state the following
L_{p}
Minkowski problem for the electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity.

L_{p}
Minkowski problem for
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity.

Let
p\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
1<{\mathfrak{p}}<n
, and
\mu
be a finite Borel measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. Find necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
so that
\mu
is the
L_{p}
electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacitary measure of a convex body
K
.

As mentioned above, Jerison
[
14
]
initiated the Minkowski type problem for the electrostatic capacity and solved the problem for the classical case
p=1
and
{\mathfrak{p}}=2
. The necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a convex body are the centroid of
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is the origin, which is identical to the corresponding conditions in the classical Minkowski problem (for volume). We point out that a solution for general measures was obtained by the existence of a solution for discrete measures and an approximation argument.

Recently, many authors extended Jerison’s work to other ranges of
(p,{\mathfrak{p}})
. For general measures, the
L_{p}
Minkowski problems for
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity were studied by Colesanti, Nyström, Salani, Xiao, and Zhang
[
10
]
for
p=1
and
1<{\mathfrak{p}}<n
; by Zou and Xiong
[
30
]
for
p>1
and
1<{\mathfrak{p}}<n
. We note that the existence results in these paper are also obtained from the existence results to the discrete case. For
0<p<1
and
1<{\mathfrak{p}}<2
, the problem for discrete measures was solved by Xiong, Xiong, and Xu
[
27
]
. As indicated in
[
30
]
, however, the logarithmic case
p=0
with
1<{\mathfrak{p}}<n
is still open although it is an important case.

In this paper we consider the case
p=0
for discrete measures.
More precisely, we solve the logarithmic Minkowski problem for
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity in the case of discrete measures whose support is in general position.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
1<\mathfrak{p}<n
, and
\mu
be a discrete measure on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
whose support is not concentrated on a closed hemisphere. Then
\mu
is the measure
\mu_{0,{\mathfrak{p}}}(P,\cdot)
of a polytope
P
whose outer unit normals are in general position if and only if the support of
\mu
is in general position. In that case, the polytope
P
contains the origin in its interior, and its unit normals are exactly the support of
\mu
.

The importance of the discrete
L_{p}
Minkowski problem for
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity follows from that the problem for general measures or for measures with density can be obtained by an approximation argument with discrete measures. Indeed, by first solving the polytopal case, an approximation argument provides a solution to the problem for general measures when
p\geq 1
and
1<{\mathfrak{p}}<n

[
14
,
10
,
30
]
. See also
[
6
]
for an approximation argument in the logarithmic (

) Minkowski problem.

2.
Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we assume that
n\geq 2
and
1<{\mathfrak{p}}<n
. In the Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
a
convex body
is a compact convex set with non-empty interior. We denote by
\mathcal{K}^{n}
the set of convex bodies in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and by
\mathcal{K}^{n}_{0}
the set of convex bodies with the origin
o
in their interior.

We write
x\cdot y
for the standard inner product of
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and
|x|=(x\cdot x)^{1/2}
for the standard norm of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. Let
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be the boundary of the unit ball
B_{1}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. For a set
A
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we write
\mathrm{Int}(A)
for the interior of
A
. We denote the volume, area, and diameter of
A
by
V(A)
,
\mathrm{Area}(A)
, and
\mathrm{diam}(A)
, respectively.

The
support function

h_{K}
of
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
is defined by

\displaystyle h_{K}(x)=\max\{x\cdot y:y\in K\}.

For
K,L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
, the
Hausdorff metric
is defined by

\displaystyle\delta(K,L)=\max_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|h_{K}(u)-h_{L}(u)|.

We define the
support hyperplane

H(K,u)
for
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
and
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
as

\displaystyle H(K,u)=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}:x\cdot u=h_{K}(u)\},

and then the
support set

F(K,u)
is defined by

\displaystyle F(K,u)=K\cap H(K,u).

A
polytope
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is the convex hull of a finite set of points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
with positive
n
-dimensional volume. If the convex hull of a subset of these points lies entirely on the boundary of the polytope and has positive
(n-1)
-dimensional volume, then it is called a
facet
of the polytope.

We write a discrete measure
\mu
of the form

\displaystyle\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\delta_{u_{i}},

where
\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{N}
are positive constants and
\delta_{u}
denotes the Dirac delta measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
defined by

\displaystyle\delta_{u}(A)=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if }u\in A,\\
0\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We need some definitions on the support of measures:

Definition 2.1
.

A finite subset
U
(with no less than
n
elements) of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is said to be
in general position
if any
n
elements of
U
are linearly independent.

Definition 2.2
.

A subset
U
of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is said to be
concentrated on a closed hemisphere
if there exists
u\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
such that
U\subset\{v\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}:u\cdot v\geq 0\}
.

Suppose that the unit vectors
u_{1},\cdots,u_{N}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
are not concentrated on any closed hemisphere of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})
the set of polytopes of the form
\cap_{i=1}^{N}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot u_{i}\leq a_{i}\}
for
a_{1},\dots,a_{N}\in{\mathbb{R}}
and by
\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})
the subset of
\mathcal{P}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})
whose element has exactly
N
facets. Note that any
P\in\mathcal{P}(u_{1},\cdots,u_{N})
has at most
N
facets, and the outer normals of
P
are a subset of
\{u_{1},\cdots,u_{N}\}
.

Note that for convex bodies
K,L\in\mathcal{K}^{n}
, the (first) mixed
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacity
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,L)
is defined as

C_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,L)=\frac{1}{n-{\mathfrak{p}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(K+tL)}{\mathrm{d}t}\bigg|_{t=0+}=\frac{\mathfrak{p}-1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}\int_{\omega}h_{L}(u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,u)

and by taking
K=L
, we obtain a measure corresponding to the cone-volume measure, namely for a Borel set
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,

\frac{\mathfrak{p}-1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}\int_{\omega}h_{K}(u)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(K,u).

Finally, we recall the Blaschke selection theorem (see
[
24
, Theorem 1.8.7]
).

Theorem 2.3

(Blaschke selection theorem)
.

Every bounded sequence of convex bodies has a subsequence that converges to a convex body.

3.
An extreme problem

Let us fix
N\geq n+1
and let
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be in general position and not concentrated on a closed hemisphere. Let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N}
be
N
positive real numbers. For a polytope
P\in\mathcal{P}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
and the discrete measure
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\delta_{u_{i}}
, we define a functional
\Phi_{\mu,P}:\mathrm{Int}(P)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\Phi_{\mu,P}(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\log(h_{P}(u_{i})-\xi\cdot u_{i}).

Note that an extreme problem for the functional
\Phi_{\mu,P}
with volume constraint has been widely used to establish logarithmic Minkowski problem (see, for instance,
[
4
,
28
,
3
,
6
]
). However, in this paper we consider the extreme problem

(3.1)

\inf\left\{\max_{\xi\in\mathrm{Int}(Q)}\Phi_{\mu,Q}(\xi):Q\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})~\text{and}~C_{\mathfrak{p}}(Q)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\right\}

with
\mathfrak{p}
-capacity constraint to prove
Theorem

1.2
.

Within this section, we prove that any minimizer of the problem (
3.1
) solves the discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem for
\mathfrak{p}
-capacity. Before we prove this, let us recall known results about the functional
\Phi_{\mu,P}
(see
[
28
, Section 3]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is in general position and not concentrated on a closed hemisphere. Let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N}
be
N
positive real numbers and define
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\delta_{u_{i}}
. If
P\in\mathcal{P}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
, then there exists a unique point
\xi(P)\in\mathrm{Int}(P)
such that

\Phi_{\mu,P}(\xi(P))=\max_{\xi\in\mathrm{Int}(P)}\Phi_{\mu,P}(\xi).

Moreover, if a sequence of polytopes
P_{m}\in\mathcal{P}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
converges to
P
with respect to the Hausdorff metric, then

\lim_{i\to\infty}\xi(P_{i})=\xi(P)

and

\lim_{i\to\infty}\Phi_{\mu,P_{i}}(\xi(P_{i}))=\Phi_{\mu,P}(\xi(P)).

Let us now prove that a minimizer of the extreme problem (
3.1
) solves the discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem for
\mathfrak{p}
-capacity.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u_{1},\dots,u_{N}
and
\gamma,\dots,\gamma_{N}
be given as in
Lemma

3.1
. If
P\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
satisfies
\xi(P)=o
,
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}
, and

(3.2)

\Phi_{\mu,P}(o)=\inf\left\{\max_{\xi\in\mathrm{Int}(Q)}\Phi_{\mu,Q}(\xi):Q\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})~\text{and}~C_{\mathfrak{p}}(Q)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\right\},

then
\mu_{0,\mathfrak{p}}(P,\cdot)=\mu
.

Proof.

Since
\xi(\lambda P)=\lambda\xi(P)
, we may assume that
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}=1
. Let
\delta_{1},\dots,\delta_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
and define

P_{t}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{N}\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot u_{i}\leq h_{P}(u_{i})+t\delta_{i}\}.

Since
P\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
, we choose
|t|
sufficiently small so that
P_{t}
is the polytope with exactly
N
facets. Let

\alpha(t)=C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})^{-1/(n-\mathfrak{p})}

and define
\tilde{P}_{t}=\alpha(t)P_{t}
. Then,
\tilde{P}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
,
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\tilde{P}_{t})=1
, and
\tilde{P}_{t}\to P
with respect to the Hausdorff metric as
t\to 0
. Since
P
is an optimizer of the extreme problem (
3.2
), we will compute the first variation of the functional.

We first have from
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
and

(3.3)

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}h_{P_{t}}(u_{i})=\delta_{i}

that

(3.4)

\begin{split}\alpha^{\prime}(0)&=-\frac{1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\\
&=-\frac{\mathfrak{p}-1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}h_{P_{t}}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(P,\xi)\\
&=-\frac{\mathfrak{p}-1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(P,\{u_{i}\}).\end{split}

We set
\xi(t):=\xi(\tilde{P}_{t})
and define

\Phi(t):=\Phi_{\mu,\tilde{P}_{t}}(\xi(t))=\max_{\xi\in\mathrm{Int}(\tilde{P}_{t})}\Phi_{\mu,\tilde{P}_{t}}(\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\log(\alpha(t)h_{P_{t}}(u_{i})-\xi(t)\cdot u_{i}).

Since the maximum is attained in an interior point
\xi(t)
, we obtain

(3.5)

0=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\frac{u_{i,j}}{\alpha(t)h_{P_{t}}(u_{i})-\xi(t)\cdot u_{i}}

for each
j=1,\dots,n
, where
u_{i,j}
is the
j
-th element of the vector
u_{i}
. In particular, when
t=0
we have

(3.6)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\frac{u_{i}}{h_{P}(u_{i})}=o

as vectors.

Let us next show that
\Phi
is differentiable at zero. We define avector valued function
F
by

F(t,\xi)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\frac{u_{i}}{\alpha(t)h_{P_{t}}(u_{i})-\xi\cdot u_{i}}.

Then, the Jacobian matrix of
F
at
(0,0)
is given by

\frac{\partial F}{\partial\xi}(0,0)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{h_{P_{t}}^{2}(u_{i})}u_{i}u_{i}^{T}.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonzero vector, then by the assumption that
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}
is in general position, there exists a vector
u_{i_{0}}\in\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}
such that
u_{i_{0}}\cdot x\neq 0
. Thus, we have

x^{T}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{h_{P_{t}}^{2}(u_{i})}u_{i}u_{i}^{T}\right)x=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\gamma_{i}}{h_{P_{t}}^{2}(u_{i})}(x\cdot u_{i})^{2}\geq\frac{\gamma_{i_{0}}}{h_{P_{t}}^{2}(u_{i_{0}})}(x\cdot u_{i_{0}})^{2}>0,

which indicates that the Jacobian matrix
\frac{\partial F}{\partial\xi}(0,0)
is positive definite. Therefore, by
\xi(0)=o
, (
3.5
), and the implicit function theorem,
\xi(t)
is differentiable at zero and so is
\Phi(t)
.

From the assumption that
\tilde{P}_{0}=P
is a minimizer of (
3.2
), we have
\Phi(0)\leq\Phi(t)
for sufficiently small
|t|
, which implies that
\Phi^{\prime}(0)=0
. Thus, by using (
3.3
), (
3.4
), and (
3.6
), we obtain

\begin{split}0=\Phi^{\prime}(0)&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\left(\frac{\alpha(0)}{h_{P}(u_{i})}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}h_{P_{t}}(u_{i})+\alpha^{\prime}(0)-\frac{\xi^{\prime}(0)\cdot u_{i}}{h_{P}(u_{i})}\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\left(\frac{\delta_{i}}{h_{P}(u_{i})}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}-1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{j}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(P,\{u_{j}\})\right)\\
&=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}\left(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{h_{P}(u_{i})}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}-1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma_{j}\right)\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(P,\{u_{i}\})\right).\end{split}

Since we assumed that
\sum_{j=1}^{N}\gamma_{j}=1
, we arrive at

\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}\left(\frac{\gamma_{i}}{h_{P}(u_{i})}-\frac{\mathfrak{p}-1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(P,\{u_{i}\})\right)=0.

Recalling that we have chosen
\delta_{i}
arbitrary, we conclude that

\frac{\mathfrak{p}-1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}h_{P}(u_{i})\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(P,\{u_{i}\})=\gamma_{i}

for all
i=1,\dots,n
. Therefore,
\lambda P
satisfies
\mu_{0,\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda P,\cdot)=\mu
, where
\lambda=(\frac{{\mathfrak{p}}-1}{n-{\mathfrak{p}}})^{-1/(n-{\mathfrak{p}})}
.
∎

4.
Compactness

In the previous section, we proved that any minimizer of the extreme problem (
3.1
) solves the discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem for
\mathfrak{p}
-capacity. In order to prove
Theorem

1.2
, we need to show that there exists a minimizer of the extreme problem (
3.1
). To this end, we provide a compactness result for this problem in this section. That is to say, we prove that a sequence of polytopes from
\mathcal{P}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
having a bounded
\mathfrak{p}
-capacity also has a bounded diameter.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is in general position and not concentrated on a closed hemisphere. If
\{P_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{P}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
is a sequence of polytopes satisfying
o\in P_{m}
and
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{m})=1
, then
\{P_{m}\}
is bounded.

A statement similar to
Theorem

4.1
with the assumption
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{m})=1
replaced by
V(P_{m})=1
was first proved by Zhu in
[
28
]
. The idea of proof of
Theorem

4.1
is to combine Zhu’s result and the isoperimetric inequality for
\mathfrak{p}
-capacity. Let us first recall the result in
[
28
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
28
, Theorem 4.3]

Let
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
be given as in
Theorem

4.1
. If
\{P_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{P}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
is a sequence of polytopes satisfying
o\in P_{m}
and
V(P_{m})=1
, then
\{P_{m}\}
is bounded.

We need the isoperimetric inequality for
\mathfrak{p}
-capacity for the proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
26
, Theorem 2.1]

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in(1,n)
and
P
be a convex body. Then,

\left(\frac{\mathrm{Area}(P)}{\mathrm{Area}(B_{1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\leq\left(\frac{\mathfrak{p}(n-1)}{n(\mathfrak{p}-1)}\right)^{\frac{\mathfrak{p}-1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\frac{C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P)}{C_{\mathfrak{p}}(B_{1})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}},

where
B_{1}
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We are now in a position to prove
Theorem

4.1
by using
Theorem

4.2
and
Theorem

4.3
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.1
.

Suppose that
\{P_{m}\}
is unbounded. Then, by
Theorem

4.2
, a sequence of volumes
\{V(P_{m})\}
is unbounded. By the isoperimetric inequality (for volume), there exists a dimensional constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(n)>0
such that

V(P_{m})^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq c_{1}\mathrm{Area}(P_{m})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

holds for every
m
. Moreover, by
Theorem

4.3
, we have

\mathrm{Area}(P_{m})^{\frac{1}{n-1}}\leq c_{2}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{m})^{\frac{1}{n-\mathfrak{p}}}

for some
c_{2}=c_{2}(n,\mathfrak{p})>0
. Thus, we conclude that
\{C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{m})\}
is unbounded, which leads us to a contradiction. Therefore,
\{P_{m}\}
is bounded.
∎

5.
Discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem for
\mathfrak{p}
-capacity

In this section, we prove the main result,
Theorem

1.2
, which follows from the existence of a minimizer of the extreme problem (
3.2
).

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is in general position and not concentrated on a closed hemisphere. Let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{N}
be
N
positive real numbers and define
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\delta_{u_{i}}
. Then, there exists a polytope
P\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
such that
\xi(P)=o
,
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}
, and

\Phi_{\mu,P}(o)=\inf\left\{\max_{\xi\in\mathrm{Int}(Q)}\Phi_{\mu,Q}(\xi):Q\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})~\text{and}~C_{\mathfrak{p}}(Q)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}\right\}.

In particular, the measure
\mu
is
L_{0}
electrostatic
{\mathfrak{p}}
-capacitary measure, i.e.,
\mu_{0,\mathfrak{p}}(P,\cdot)=\mu
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the theorem for the case
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\gamma_{i}=1
. Let us take a minimizing sequence
\{P_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
for

(5.1)

\inf\left\{\max_{\xi\in\mathrm{Int}(Q)}\Phi_{\mu,Q}(\xi):Q\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})~\text{and}~C_{\mathfrak{p}}(Q)=1\right\}.

Moreover, we may assume that
\xi(P_{m})=o
. Indeed, otherwise we may take
P_{m}-\xi(P_{m})
instead of
P_{m}
, which also minimizes (
5.1
) since
\Phi_{\mu,P_{m}}(\xi(P_{m}))=\Phi_{\mu,P_{m}-\xi(P_{m})}(\xi(P_{m}-\xi(P_{m})))
.

We know from
Theorem

4.1
that
\{P_{m}\}
is bounded. Thus, by Blaschke selection theorem,
Lemma

3.1
, and continuity of
C_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\mathcal{K}^{n}
, there exists a subsequence of
\{P_{m}\}
that converges to a convex set
P
satisfying
\xi(P)=o
,
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P)=1
, and

\Phi_{\mu,P}(o)=\inf\left\{\max_{\xi\in\mathrm{Int}(Q)}\Phi_{\mu,Q}(\xi):Q\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})~\text{and}~C_{\mathfrak{p}}(Q)=1\right\}.

Furthermore, it follows from the fact
P_{m}\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
that
\mathrm{supp}(P)\subset\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}
.

We now prove that
P
has a positive
n
-dimensional volume.
Otherwise
P\subset L
for some linear subspace
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathrm{dim}(L)=n-m
,
0<m\leq n
. Then, there exist
m+1
vectors
u_{i_{1}},\dots,u_{i_{m+1}}\in\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}
such that
u_{i_{j}}\in L^{\perp}
for
j=1,\dots,m+1
. Since
\mathrm{dim}(L^{\perp})=m
, any set of
n
vectors containing
u_{i_{1}},\dots,u_{i_{m+1}}
is linearly dependent, which contradicts to the assumption that
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{N}\}
is in general position. Therefore, we have
\mathrm{Int}(P)\neq\emptyset
.

To show that
P\in\mathcal{P}_{N}(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})
, we need to show that
P
has exactly
N
facets.
We first argue by contradiction that
F(P,u_{i})
are facets for all
i=1,\dots,N
. Assume that there is an index
i_{0}\in\{1,\dots,N\}
such that
F(P,u_{i_{0}})
is not a facet.
For
t>0
, we define the polytope
P_{t}
by

P_{t}=P\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x\cdot u_{i_{0}}\leq h_{P}(u_{i_{0}})-t\}

and let
\lambda=C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})^{-1/(n-\mathfrak{p})}
. Then, we have
C_{\mathfrak{p}}(\lambda P_{t})=1
and
\lambda P_{t}\to P
as
t\searrow 0
. Moreover, it follows from
Lemma

3.1
that
\xi(P_{t})\to\xi(P)=o\in\mathrm{Int}(P)
as
t\searrow 0
. We now choose
t>0
sufficiently small so that
P_{t}
has exactly one more facet than that of
P
and
h_{P}(u_{i})>\xi(P_{t})\cdot u_{i}+t
for all
i=1,\dots,N
.

We show that
\Phi_{\mu,\lambda P_{t}}(\xi(\lambda P_{t}))<\Phi_{\mu,P}(o)
, which contradicts the fact that
\Phi_{\mu,P}(o)
is a minimum. Let
d_{0}=\mathrm{diam}(P)
, then
d_{0}>h_{P}(u_{i_{0}})-\xi(P_{t})\cdot u_{i_{0}}>0
. Since
h_{P_{t}}(u_{i})=h_{P}(u_{i})
for all
i\neq i_{0}
, we have

\begin{split}&\prod_{i=1}^{N}(h_{\lambda P_{t}}(u_{i})-\xi(\lambda P_{t})\cdot u_{i})^{\gamma_{i}}\\
&=\lambda\prod_{i=1}^{N}(h_{P_{t}}(u_{i})-\xi(P_{t})\cdot u_{i})^{\gamma_{i}}\\
&=\lambda\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(h_{P}(u_{i})-\xi(P_{t})\cdot u_{i})^{\gamma_{i}}\right)\left(\frac{h_{P}(u_{i_{0}})-\xi(P_{t})\cdot u_{i_{0}}-t}{h_{P}(u_{i_{0}})-\xi(P_{t})\cdot u_{i_{0}}}\right)^{\gamma_{i_{0}}}\\
&\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N}(h_{P}(u_{i})-\xi(P_{t})\cdot u_{i})^{\gamma_{i}}\right)\frac{(1-t/d_{0})^{\gamma_{i_{0}}}}{C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})^{1/(n-\mathfrak{p})}}.\end{split}

If we define a function

g(t)=C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})^{\frac{1}{(n-\mathfrak{p})\gamma_{i_{0}}}}+\frac{1}{d_{0}}t-1,

then
g(0)=0
and

g^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{(n-\mathfrak{p})\gamma_{i_{0}}}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})^{\frac{1}{(n-\mathfrak{p})\gamma_{i_{0}}}-1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})+\frac{1}{d_{0}}.

By the variational formula for
\mathfrak{p}
-capacity
[
10
, Theorem 5.2]
, we have

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}=(\mathfrak{p}-1)\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}h_{P_{t}}(u_{i})\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(P,\{u_{i}\}).

Since
\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{i=0}h_{P_{t}}(u_{i})=0
for
i\neq i_{0}
,
\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{i=0}h_{P_{t}}(u_{i_{0}})=-1
, and
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(P,\{u_{i_{0}}\})=0
, we obtain that

\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})}{\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}=0.

Here we have used the fact that
\mu_{\mathfrak{p}}(P,\cdot)
is absolutely continuous with respect to
S(P,\cdot)
.
Thus,
g(t)>0
for sufficiently small
t>0
, which yields that

\frac{(1-t/d_{0})^{\gamma_{i_{0}}}}{C_{\mathfrak{p}}(P_{t})^{1/(n-\mathfrak{p})}}<1.

Therefore, we arrive at

\prod_{i=1}^{N}(h_{\lambda P_{t}}(u_{i})-\xi(\lambda P_{t})\cdot u_{i})^{\gamma_{i}}<\prod_{i=1}^{N}(h_{P}(u_{i})-\xi(P_{t})\cdot u_{i})^{\gamma_{i}},

which implies
\Phi_{\mu,\lambda P_{t}}(\xi(\lambda P_{t}))<\Phi_{\mu,P}(\xi(P_{t}))\leq\Phi_{\mu,P}(o)
. We now conclude that
P
has exactly
N
facets.
∎

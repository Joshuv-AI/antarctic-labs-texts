Dévissage for generation in derived categories
Souvik Dey, Pat Lank

Abstract
. This note is concerned with generation in the derived category of bounded complexes with coherent cohomology over a Noetherian scheme. We demonstrate a flavor of ‘dévissage’ by identifying two explicit collections of structure sheaves for closed subschemes that classically generate the bounded derived category. Amongst the two, one consists of those supported on the singular locus of the scheme. Moreover, building from the work of Aoki, we show the essential image of the derived pushforward along a proper surjective morphism of Noetherian schemes strongly generates the targets bounded derived category.

1.
Introduction

Recall a notion of generation for a triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
, which was introduced in
[
BvdB03
]
. A subcategory
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{T}
is said to
classically generate
if the smallest thick subcategory containing
\mathcal{S}
, denoted
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
, coincides with
\mathcal{T}
. This means every object in
\mathcal{T}
can be obtained from objects in
\mathcal{S}
using cones, shifts, finite coproducts, and retracts. Denote by
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n}
for the smallest full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
generated from
\mathcal{S}
using finite coproducts, shifts, retracts, and at most
n
cones. We will say
\mathcal{S}

strongly generates
if
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n}=\mathcal{T}
for some integer
n\geq 0
. In the special case
\mathcal{S}
consists of a single object
G
, then we write
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
as
\langle G\rangle
and
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n}
as
\langle G\rangle_{n}
.

The concept of generators has led to interesting invariants, as introduced in
[
ABIM10
,
Rou08
]
. There are important benefits a triangulated category enjoys in the presence of a single object which strongly generates, including representability results on cohomological functors
[
Rou08
]
. Those that are geometrically inclined might appreciate a connection between generation and openness of the regular locus for a Noetherian scheme
[
IT19
,
DL24
]
.

We will focus on the derived category of bounded complexes with coherent cohomology over a Noetherian scheme
X
, which is denoted by
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
. There has been considerable progress made in understanding instances where
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
can be generated by a single object. These cases have been studied for quasi-excellent separated schemes of finite Krull dimension
[
Aok21
,
Nee21
]
, Noetherian
J\textrm{-}2
schemes
[
ELS20
]
, Noetherian schemes admitting a separator
[
Jat21
]
, and various classes of affine Noetherian schemes
[
IT16
,
IT19
,
DLT23
]
. There have been efforts to explicitly identify such objects
[
Rou08
,
BIL+23
]
, including noncommutative techniques
[
BDL23
,
BDG17
,
DLR24
]
.

Our motivation is classical at heart. An intermediate step in arguments for generic freeness à la dévissage asserts that for any finitely generated module
M
over a commutative Noetherian ring
R
there exists a finite descending chain of submodules
\cdots\subseteq M_{1}\subseteq M_{0}=:M
whose corresponding quotients
M_{i}/M_{i+1}
are isomorphic to objects of the form
R/\mathfrak{p}
for some
\mathfrak{p}
is in
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
. This is observed in the proof of
[
Gro65
, Lemme 6.9.2]
. If interpreted in our context, this tells us that any finitely generated
R
-module can be obtained from the collection of modules of the form
R/\mathfrak{p}
where
\mathfrak{p}
is in
\operatorname{Spec}(R)
using cones, shifts, and direct sums in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\operatorname{Spec}(R))
.

This brings attention to our first result where we demonstrate a flavor of ‘dévissage’ for derived categories associated to schemes.

Theorem A
.

(see Theorem
3.3
)
Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. If
P
is a perfect complex on
X
with full support, then
D_{\operatorname{coh}}^{b}(X)
is classically generated by
P
and objects of the form
i_{\ast}\mathbb{L}i^{\ast}P
where
i\colon Z\to X
is a closed immersion from an integral scheme.

There is an interesting application of Theorem
A
when applied to the special case of
P=\mathcal{O}_{X}
. This tells us that any object in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
can be obtained, using finitely many cones, from
\mathcal{O}_{X}
and those of the form
i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
for
i\colon Z\to X
a closed immersion from an integral scheme. However, there is a refinement for Theorem
A
in the affine setting. Namely,
[
Tak14
]
tells us that
D^{b}({\operatorname{mod}}R)
is classically generated by
R
and
R/\mathfrak{p}
for
\mathfrak{p}
in the singular locus of
R
. Recall the singular locus of a Noetherian scheme
X
, denoted
\operatorname{sing}(X)
, is the collection of points
p
in
X
such that
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
is not regular.

Our next result tells us how this can globalized, where we leverage what is known affine locally.

Theorem B
.

(see Theorem
3.5
)
Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. If
P
is a classical generator for
\operatorname{perf}(X)
, then
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
is classically generated by
P
and the objects of the form
i_{\ast}i^{\ast}P
where
i\colon Z\to X
is a closed immersion from an integral scheme such that
i(Z)
is contained in
\operatorname{sing}(X)
.

The utility for Theorem
B
is demonstrated when the singular locus is finite as it allows for one to explicitly identify generators, see Example
3.7
for case of normal surfaces. We would like to note that similar statements have been observed for special cases where one has even further control using codimension of the closed subschemes, cf.
[
MS23
, Lemma 2.9]
and
[
Lan24
, Proposition 4.7]
. Theorem
B
has been under various guises in the literature for special settings, see Remark
3.6
for details.

A common method to detecting whether or not
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
can be generated by a single object is typically done by studying the behavior of the derived pushforward. These strategies are connected to Theorem
3.3
in an interesting way:
It is possible to show that
\langle\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle=D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
for
\pi\colon Y\to X
a proper surjective morphism
. By a different vein,
[
Aok21
]
demonstrated a general case where
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
can be strongly generated by a single object. This was accomplished using a notion of descendability for algebras in tensor triangulated categories
[
Mat16
,
BS17
]
.

The following builds a bit further on
[
Aok21
]
to tell us how to strengthen the connection of Theorem
3.3
and studying generation through the derived pushforward.

Theorem C
.

(see Theorem
3.11
)
If
\pi\colon Y\to X
is a proper surjective morphism of Noetherian schemes, then there is an integer
n
such that
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)=\langle\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
.

Proposition
C
shows that generators in the bounded derived category remain preserved under derived pushforward along proper surjective morphisms of Noetherian schemes, see Corollary
3.12
. This aids in explicitly identifying strong generators in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves for varieties over fields through alterations, see Example
3.13
. However, the consideration of generation by a derived pushforward of a morphism of schemes is very natural, and has been studied in the past.

An instance this phenomenon occurs is for a variety
X
with rational singularities over a field of characteristic zero. If
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
is a resolution of singularities, then
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}\colon D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})\to D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
is essentially dense, cf.
[
Har17
, Theorem 1.1]
,
[
Fav09
, Corollary 3.3.8]
, or
[
Kaw06
, Lemma 7.4]
. That is, any object
E
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
is a retract of
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}E^{\prime}
for some
E^{\prime}
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})
. Moreover, in the case of rational singularities,
[
MS23
]
observed that the essential image of
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}
generates
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
solely through iterated cones and shifts,
without retracts
, which holds important
K
-theoretic implications. However, the significance of Proposition
C
lies in its inclusion of retracts, which allows for a broader study of the bounded derived category for varieties beyond those with rational singularities.

On the other hand, if one is working in characteristic zero, then derived schemes are intimately related to what are called
differential graded schemes
, and so many our of results have an interesting interpretation in such context, cf.
[
Gai13
,
GR17a
,
GR17b
]
for details. In particular, Theorem
3.11
provides an alternative proof to related result in the literature in regards to proper descent for derived algebraic geometry over a field of characteristic zero, cf.
[
Gai13
, Corollary 8.1.3]
.

2.
Generation

First, we will briefly cover basic notions on generation in triangulated categories. For a further treatment on necessary background, the reader is referred to
[
BvdB03
,
Rou08
]
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category with shift functor
[1]\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
.

(1)

\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{S})
is the smallest strictly full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
\mathcal{S}
that is closed under shifts, finite coproducts, and retracts

(2)

\mathcal{S}
is said to be
thick
if it is closed under retracts and is a strictly full triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}

(3)

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
is the smallest thick subcategory in
\mathcal{T}
containing
\mathcal{S}

(4)

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{0}
is the full subcategory consisting of all objects isomorphic to the zero object

(5)

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{1}:=\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{S})

(6)

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n}:=\operatorname{add}\big(\{\operatorname{cone}(\varphi):\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n-1},\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{1})\}\big)
.

If
\mathcal{S}
consists of a single object
G
, then we write
\langle G\rangle
for
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
.

Remark 2.2
.

In the notation of Definition
2.1
, we have an exhaustive filtration for the smallest thick subcategory containing
\mathcal{S}
in
\mathcal{T}
:

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{0}\subseteq\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n}=\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
E
be an object of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
.

(1)

E
is said to be
finitely built
by
\mathcal{S}
if
E
is in
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle

(2)

\mathcal{S}
is said to
classically generate

\mathcal{T}
if
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle=\mathcal{T}
; if
\mathcal{S}
consists of a single object
G
, then
G
is called a
classical generator

(3)

\mathcal{S}
is said to
strongly generate

\mathcal{T}
if there exists
n\geq 0
such that
\langle\mathcal{T}\rangle_{n}=\mathcal{T}
; if
\mathcal{S}
consists of a single object
G
, then
G
is called a
strong generator
.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
. It is easy to verify for any object in
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
there exists an object
S
in
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{1}
such that
S
finitely builds
E
. We
will freely utilize this fact in Section
3
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme,

an object of

(1)

\operatorname{Supp}(E):=\bigcup^{\infty}_{j=-\infty}\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal{H}^{j}(E))

(2)

is
supported
on

\operatorname{Supp}(E)

(3)

3.
Results

This section will prove our main results and to start, we need something that is akin to generic freeness of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules on a Noetherian integral scheme. The following might be known to a few, but we offer an alternative proof for the sake of interest in our setting.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian integral scheme. If
E
and
G
are objects of
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
with full support, then there exists an open immersion
i\colon U\to X
such that
i^{\ast}E
is in
\langle i^{\ast}G\rangle_{1}
and
i^{\ast}G
is in
\langle i^{\ast}E\rangle_{1}
.

Proof.

Consider a nonempty open affine
U
in
X
. The set
S
of points
p
in
U
such that
E_{p}
is in
\langle G_{p}\rangle_{1}
is Zariski open, see
[
Let21
, Proposition 3.5]
. Clearly,
S
is nonempty as it contains the generic point of
X
. By the same reasoning, the set
T
of points
p
in
X
such that
G_{p}
is in
\langle E_{p}\rangle_{1}
is Zariski open and nonempty. Choose a nonempty open affine
W
in
S\cap T
. Let
j\colon W\to X
be the associated open immersion. It follows that
j^{\ast}E
is in
\langle j^{\ast}G\rangle_{1}
and
j^{\ast}G
is in
\langle j^{\ast}E\rangle_{1}
because for each
p
in
W
, one has
E_{p}
is in
\langle G_{p}\rangle_{1}
and
G_{p}
is in
\langle E_{p}\rangle_{1}
, see
[
Let21
, Corollary 3.4]
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

A variation of Lemma
3.1
has appeared in the proof of
[
Rou08
, Proposition 7.16]
for separated reduced schemes of finite type over a field.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. If
P
is a perfect complex on
X
with full support, then
D_{\operatorname{coh}}^{b}(X)
is classically generated by
P
and objects of the form
i_{\ast}i^{\ast}P
where
i\colon Z\to X
is a closed immersion from an integral scheme.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the collection consisting of
P
and objects of the form
i_{\ast}i^{\ast}P
where
i\colon Z\to X
is a closed immersion from an integral scheme. If
X=\emptyset
, then there is nothing to check, so without loss of generality,
X
is nonempty. We argue by Noetherian induction. Without loss of generality, assume the claim holds for any properly contained closed subscheme
Z
of
X
; it is possible to reduce to this case by
[
Rou08
, Lemma 7.40]
. We may further impose that
X
is reduced, see
[
Rou08
, Lemma 7.40]
or
[
Lan24
, Remark 2.23]
. Let
E
be an object of
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
.

If
E
has support properly contained in
X
, then the induction hypothesis tells us that
E
belongs to
\mathcal{S}
. Indeed, let
Z
be the reduced induced closed subscheme structure on
\operatorname{supp}(Z)
with corresponding ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}
. There is an integer
n
such that
\mathcal{I}^{n}E=(0)
, which ensures
E
is isomorphic to an object of the form
i_{\ast}E^{\prime}
for
E^{\prime}
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Z_{n})
and
i\colon Z_{n}\to X
the closed immersion associated to the ideal sheaf
\mathcal{I}^{n}
, see
[
Rou08
, Lemma 7.40]
or
[
Lan24
, Remark 2.23]
. Note that
i^{\ast}P
is a classical generator for
\operatorname{perf}(Z_{n})
, see
[
Sta23
,
Tag 0BQT
]
. The induction hypothesis ensures that
i_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Z_{n})
belongs to
i_{\ast}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
because
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Z_{n})=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is the collection consisting of
i^{\ast}P
and objects of the form
t_{\ast}(i\circ t)^{\ast}P
where
t\colon Z^{\prime}\to Z_{n}
is a closed immersion from an integral scheme. Note that
i_{\ast}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is contained in
\mathcal{S}
, and so, it follows that
E
belongs to
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
as claimed.

We will now consider the case where
E
has full support. Moreover, as objects in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
are finitely built by their cohomology sheaves, we can impose that
E
is a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module. First, lets consider the case where
X
is integral. We have that
E
is a coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module with full support on the integral scheme
X
. There exists a dense open immersion
j\colon U\to X
such that
j^{\ast}E
and
j^{\ast}P
finitely build one another in at most one cone in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(U)
, see Lemma
3.1
. If
U=X
, then we are done, so assume
U
is a properly contained subset of
X
. Note that
j^{\ast}E
is a direct summand of an object of the form
\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}j^{\ast}P^{\oplus r_{n}}[n]
, so we have an object
j^{\ast}E
and isomorphism
\phi\colon j^{\ast}A\oplus j^{\ast}E\to\oplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}j^{\ast}P^{\oplus r_{n}}[n]
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(U)
. There exists a roof in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}P^{\oplus r_{n}}[n]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A\oplus E}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

Observe that the cones of
f,g
are supported on the properly contained closed subset
Z
of
X
as the map
j^{\ast}\colon D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)\to D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(U)
is a Verdier localization. This ensures the objects

are in
\mathcal{S}
by our inductive hypothesis. Therefore,
B
belongs to
\mathcal{S}
, and hence, so does

.

Lastly, we will consider the case where
X
is not integral. Denote the irreducible components of
X=\operatorname{Supp}(E)
by
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
. There exists a short exact sequence of coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules:

where

and

, see

]
. Both objects

, and so, each belong to

Example 3.4

Let

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
X
be a Noetherian scheme. If
P
is a classical generator for
\operatorname{perf}(X)
, then
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
is classically generated by
P
and the objects of the form
i_{\ast}i^{\ast}P
where
i\colon Z\to X
is a closed immersion from an integral scheme such that
i(Z)
is contained in
\operatorname{sing}(X)
.

Proof.

Denote by
\mathcal{U}
for the collection of objects consisting of
P
and those of the form
i_{\ast}i^{\ast}P
where
i\colon Z\to X
a closed immersion from an integral scheme such that
i(Z)
is contained in
\operatorname{sing}(X)
. We will show that
\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle=D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{W}
is finitely built by
t^{\ast}P
for each closed immersion
t\colon W\to X

[
Sta23
,
Tag 0BQT
]
, and so,
t_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{W}
is finitely built by
t_{\ast}t^{\ast}P
. This ensures that
i_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{Z}
is contained in
\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle
for each closed immersion from an integral scheme such that
i(Z)
is contained in
\operatorname{sing}(X)
.

Consider an affine open cover
X=\bigcup^{n}_{i=1}U_{i}
with
s_{i}\colon U_{i}\to X
being the associated open immersions. Note that for each
1\leq i\leq n
, one has
\langle s^{\ast}_{i}\mathcal{U}\rangle
containing all objects of the form
p_{\ast}\mathcal{O}_{W}
where
p\colon W\to U_{i}
is a closed immersion where
W
such that
p(W)
is contained in
\operatorname{sing}(U_{i})
, including the structure sheaf. It follows that
\langle s^{\ast}_{i}\mathcal{U}\rangle=D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(U_{i})
because
U_{i}
is affine for each
1\leq i\leq n
, see
[
Tak14
, Corollary 4.3.(1)]
.

Let
E
be an object of
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
. There is an object
G_{i}
in
\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle_{1}
such that
s^{\ast}_{i}E
is finitely built by
s^{\ast}_{i}G_{i}
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(U_{i})
because
\langle s^{\ast}_{i}\mathcal{U}\rangle=D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(U_{i})
. Set
G=\oplus^{n}_{j=1}G_{i}
. It follows that
s^{\ast}_{j}E
is finitely built by
s^{\ast}_{j}G
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(U_{j})
for each
1\leq j\leq n
. This ensures that
E_{p}
is finitely built by
G_{p}
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
for each
p
in
X
. Hence,
E_{p}\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}K(p)
is finitely built by
G_{p}
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
for each
p
in
X
where
K(p)
is the Koszul complex on a minimal set of generators for the maximal ideal of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
. Indeed,
K(p)
is finitely built by
\mathcal{O}_{X,p}
, and tensoring with
E_{p}
tells us that
E_{p}\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}K(p)
is finitely built by
E_{p}
.

If
E_{p}\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}K(p)
is finitely built by
G_{p}
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\mathcal{O}_{X,p})
for each
p
in
X
, then
E
is finitely built by
P\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}G
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
, see
[
BIL+23
, Theorem 1.7]
. As
G
belongs to
\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle_{1}
, it is a direct summand of an object of the form
\oplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(i_{l,\ast}i^{\ast}_{l}P)^{\oplus r_{l}}[l]
. By tensoring with
P
and using projection formula, it follows that
P\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}\big(\oplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(i_{l,\ast}i^{\ast}_{l}P)^{\oplus r_{l}}[l])
is isomorphic to
\oplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(i_{l,\ast}i^{\ast}_{l}(P\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}P))^{\oplus r_{l}}[l]
. Note that
P\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}G
is a direct summand of
\oplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(i_{l,\ast}i^{\ast}_{l}(P\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}P))^{\oplus r_{l}}[l]
. As
P\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}P
is a classical generator for
\operatorname{perf}(X)
, we see that
\oplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(i_{l,\ast}i^{\ast}_{l}(P\overset{\mathbb{L}}{\otimes}P))^{\oplus r_{l}}[l]
is finitely built by
\oplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(i_{l,\ast}i^{\ast}_{l}P)^{\oplus r_{l}}[l]
. Therefore,
E
must be contained in
\langle\mathcal{U}\rangle
because it is finitely built by
\oplus_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}(i_{l,\ast}i^{\ast}_{l}P)^{\oplus r_{l}}[l]
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

(1)

It is important to assume
P
is a classical generator for
\operatorname{perf}(X)
in Theorem
3.5
. A possible relaxation might be imposing
P
be a perfect complex on
X
with full support, but we see that there are counterexamples, i.e.
P=\mathcal{O}_{X}
where
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k}
for
k
a field because
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is not a strong generator for
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{k})
and the singular locus is empty.

(2)

Theorem
3.5
has appeared in the different forms in the literature, but not in our generality. This includes the affine setting
[
Tak14
, Corollary 4.3]
, proof for
[
Rou08
, Theorem 7.38]
, and divisorial varieties
[
BFK14
, Lemma 4.13]
.

A possible utility for Theorem
3.5
in practice is when the singular locus is finite, which we demonstrate a special case below.

Example 3.7
.

Let
X
be a normal irreducible quasi-projective surface over a field. The singular locus of
X
must be finite, say with closed points
p_{1},\cdots,p_{n}
and associated closed immersions
\pi_{i}\colon\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(p_{i}))\to X
. If
\mathcal{L}
is an ample line bundle on
X
, then
\mathcal{L}\oplus(\oplus^{n}_{i=1}\pi_{i,\ast}\mathcal{O}_{\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(p_{i}))})
is a strong generator for
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
.

It is possible to show that
\langle\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle=D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
for
\pi\colon Y\to X
a proper surjective morphism by Noetherian induction coupled with Theorem
3.5
. We refine this to a stronger statement in what follows.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a proper surjective morphism of Noetherian schemes. For every object
K
in
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{-}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)
and integer
a
, there is a map
f\colon K\to L
with
L
in
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)
such that
\operatorname{cone}(f)
is in
D^{\leq a}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}}(X)
.

Proof.

Let
K:=\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}E
. Then
L=\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}(\tau_{\geq a-N}E)
fits the bill for some integer
N
.
∎

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a proper surjective morphism of Noetherian schemes. Let
n\geq 0
. For each
K
in
D^{-}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
which belongs to
\big(\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{-}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\big)^{\star n}
and integer
a
, there is a map
f\colon K\to L
with
L
an object of
\big(\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\big)^{\star n}
such that
\operatorname{cone}(f)
in
D^{<a}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}}(X)
.

Proof.

The case where
n=0
follows from Lemma
3.8
. We check the case for
n=1
, leaving the general case omitted as it follows similarly. There is a distinguished triangle in
D_{\operatorname{Qcoh}}(X)
:

A\to B\to K\to A[1]

where
A,B
are objects of
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{-}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)
. Then Lemma
3.8
ensures there is a commutative square:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{A^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B^{\prime}}}

where the vertical maps have cones in
D^{<a-1}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}}(X)
. This can be accomplished by choosing a map
B\to B^{\prime}
via Lemma
3.8
, and a map
A\to A^{\prime}
a sufficiently good approximation for which
A\to B^{\prime}
factors through
A^{\prime}
. Now extending the square above to a morphism of distinguished triangles, the base case follows.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a proper surjective morphism of Noetherian schemes. If
E
is an object of
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)\cap\langle\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{-}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
, then
E
belongs to
\langle\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
.

Proof.

The hypothesis tells us
E
is a retract of an object
K
which belongs to
\big(\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{-}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\big)^{\star n}
. Choose an integer
a
such that
E
belongs to
D^{>a}_{\operatorname{Qcoh}}(X)
. Consider the map
K\to L
obtained from Lemma
3.9
. We can check that
E
is a retract of
L
as the retraction
K\to E
factors through
L
for degree reasons.
∎

Theorem 3.11
.

If
\pi\colon Y\to X
is a proper surjective morphism of Noetherian schemes, then there is an integer
n
such that
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)=\langle\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
.

Proof.

There is an integer
n
such that each object of
D^{-}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
belongs to
\langle\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{-}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
, see argument for
[
Aok21
, Proposition 4.4]
. The desired claim follows from Lemma
3.10
.
∎

This gives the following immediate generalization of
[
Lan24
, Theorem E]
and
[
LO24
, Lemma 3.15]
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\to X
be a proper surjective morphism of Noetherian schemes. If
G
is a classical generator for
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)
, then
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}G
is a classical generator for
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
.

Proof.

Choose an integer
n
such that
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)=\langle\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)\rangle_{n}
, see Theorem
3.11
. Let
E
be an object of
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
. We can find an object
E^{\prime}
in
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y)
such that
E
is finitely built by
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}E^{\prime}
. However,
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}G
finitely builds
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}E^{\prime}
, and so,
E
belongs to
\langle D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)\rangle
. This completes the proof.
∎

Example 3.13
.

Let
X
be a variety over a field. Recall that
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
has finite Rouquier dimension, see
[
Rou08
, Theorem 7.38]
. There exists a proper surjective morphism
\pi\colon\widetilde{X}\to X
of varieties over
k
such that
\dim\widetilde{X}=\dim X
and
\widetilde{X}
is a regular quasi-projective variety, cf.
[
dJ96
, Theorem 4.1]
. Let
\mathcal{L}
be an ample line bundle on
\widetilde{X}
. By
[
Rou08
, Proposition 7.31]
, we know that
G:=\bigoplus^{\dim X}_{i=-\dim X}\mathcal{L}^{\otimes i}
is a strong generator for
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(\widetilde{X})
, and so
\mathbb{R}\pi_{\ast}G
is a strong generator for
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(X)
.

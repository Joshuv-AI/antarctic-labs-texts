Asymptotic Enumeration of Binary Contingency Tables and Comparison with Independence Heuristic
Da Wu

Abstract
. For parameters n , δ , B , C n,\delta,B,C , we obtained a sharp asymptotic formula for the number of ( n + ⌊ n δ ⌋ ) 2 (n+\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor)^{2} -dimensional binary contingency tables with non-uniform margins taking values of ⌊ B ​ C ​ n ⌋ \lfloor BCn\rfloor and ⌊ C ​ n ⌋ \lfloor Cn\rfloor . Furthermore, we compared our sharp asymptotics with the classical independence heuristic estimate and proved that the independence heuristic overestimates by a factor of e Θ ⁡ ( n 2 ​ δ ) e^{\Theta(n^{2\delta})} . Our comparison is based on the analysis of the correlation ratio and an explicit bound for the constant in Θ \Theta is also obtained.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

This paper studies the asymptotic enumeration of
binary contingency tables
and its connection with the classical
independence heuristic
introduced by I. J. Good
70
years ago
[
GC77
]
.

Binary contingency table is the set of
0
-
1
matrices with fixed row and column sums. Let
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
and
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be two positive integer vectors with same total sum of entries, i.e.
\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}=N
. Let

\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})=\left\{X=(X_{ij}):\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{ik}=r_{i},\sum_{k=1}^{m}X_{kj}=c_{j}\ \text{for all}\ 1\leq i\leq m,1\leq j\leq n\right\}

be the set of binary contingency tables with row margin
\mathbf{r}
and column margin
\mathbf{c}
. Since
X_{ij}\in\{0,1\}
, it is easy to see that
r_{i}\leq n,c_{j}\leq m
for all
i,j
. Binary contingency table has close connections with bipartite graphs with fixed degree sequence, see e.g.,
[
Wor19
]
, for historical review. It also arises as the structural constants in the ring of symmetric functions and representation theory of general linear groups, see
[
Mac98
]
.

Estimating the cardinality of
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})
is a fundamental problem in analytic combinatorics; see for instance,
[
GC77
]
and
[
Bar10
]
. At the very beginning, we have the following effortless estimate based on the so-called
independence heuristic
. Precisely speaking, fix
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
and
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
with
N=r_{1}+\ldots+r_{m}=c_{1}+\ldots+c_{n}
, and let
\mathcal{M}_{N}
be the set of
m\times n

0
-
1
matrices with total sum of entries
N
. Let
X
be the uniform sample from
\mathcal{M}_{N}
and consider the following two events:

\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{r}}=\{\text{$X$ has row sum $\mathbf{r}$}\}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{c}}=\{\text{$X$ has column sum $\mathbf{c}$}\}.

It follows from the definition that

\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{r}}\right)=\frac{|\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{r}}|}{|\mathcal{M}_{N}|}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{c}}\right)=\frac{|\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{c}}|}{|\mathcal{M}_{N}|}.

Assume
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{r}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{c}}
are independent, then

\frac{|\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{r}}|}{|\mathcal{M}_{N}|}\cdot\frac{|\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{c}}|}{|\mathcal{M}_{N}|}=\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{r}}\right)\mathbb{P}\left(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{c}}\right)=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{r}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{c}})=\frac{|\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})|}{|\mathcal{M}_{N}|}.

Therefore,

(1.1)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})|=\frac{|\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{r}}|\cdot|\mathcal{R}_{\mathbf{c}}|}{|\mathcal{M}_{N}|}=\binom{mn}{N}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n}{r_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\binom{m}{c_{j}}.

We call

(1.2)

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})=\binom{mn}{N}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n}{r_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\binom{m}{c_{j}}.

the
independence heuristic estimate
corresponding to margins
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{c}
.

In
1969
, O’Neil
[
O’N69
]
solved the problem for the case of large sparse binary matrices with
r_{i},c_{j}\leq(\log n)^{\frac{1}{4}-\varepsilon}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
. In
2010
, A. Barvinok
[
Bar10
]
used
permanents
and the
van der Waerden bound
for doubly-stochastic matrix to obtain an estimate for generic
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{c}
. This estimate has recently been improved in
[
BLP23
]
using the techniques of Lorentzian polynomials. Later on, Barvinok and Hartigan
[
BH13
]
used the
Maximal Entropy Principle
and local central limit theorem to obtain a more precise asymptotic formula under certain regularity conditions.

Our main focus is to first derive the asymptotics of
|\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})|
when both
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{c}
take two different linear values of
n
; see the precise definition in the next subsection. After that, we compared the results with the independence heuristic estimate (
1.2
) and showed that the independence heuristic estimate leads to a large overestimate by a factor of
e^{\Theta(n^{2\delta})}
. Our derivation of asymptotics follows closely the spirit of
[
LP22
]
and is mainly based on Barvinok’s asymptotic formula in
[
Bar10
]
and the author’s recent work
[
Wu22
]
on the limiting distribution of
Random Binary Contingency Tables
. Similar asymptotics for the uniform margin case can also be obtaned with the same techniques and the limiting distribution derived in
[
Wu23
]
.

1.2.
Setup and Statements of Main Results

For
0<\delta<1
,
0<B\leq\frac{1}{C}
and
0<C<1
, let

\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}=\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}:=(\underbrace{\lfloor BCn\rfloor,\ldots,\lfloor BCn\rfloor}_{\text{$\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor$ entries}},\underbrace{\lfloor Cn\rfloor\ldots,\lfloor Cn\rfloor}_{\text{$n$ entries}})\in\mathbb{N}^{[n^{\delta}]+n},

and let

\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C):=\mathcal{M}(\widetilde{\mathbf{r}},\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}).

Namely,
\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C)
is the set of
(\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor+n)^{2}
-dimensional binary matrices whose first
\lfloor n^{\delta}\rfloor
rows and columns have sum
\lfloor BCn\rfloor
and remaining
n
rows and columns have sum
\lfloor Cn\rfloor
.

The first main result of this paper is the sharp asymptotics of
|\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C)|
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
0<\delta<1
,
n\geq 1
,
0<C<\frac{3}{4}
and
0<B\leq\frac{1}{C}
,
and let
f(x):=x\log\frac{1}{x}+(1-x)\log\frac{1}{1-x}
. We have

\displaystyle\log|\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C)|

\displaystyle=f(C)n^{2}+\left[2f(BC)-(BC)\log\left(\frac{1-C}{C}\right)\right]n^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle+\left[f(z_{11}^{*})+z_{11}^{*}\log\left(\frac{1-C}{C}\cdot\frac{(BC)^{2}}{(1-BC)^{2}}\right)-\frac{B^{2}C}{2(1-C)}\right]n^{2\delta}

\displaystyle+O(n^{3\delta-1}+n\log n),

where
z_{11}^{*}=\frac{B^{2}(1-C)}{B^{2}-2B+1/C}
.

This theorem will be proved in the Section
2
. For now, we remark that the proof is based on the
Maximum Entropy Principle
and the function
f(x)
is Shannon-Boltzmann entropy of Bernoulli random variable with mean
x
; the coefficients in front of
n^{2},n^{1+\delta},n^{2\delta}
all come from the Bernoulli entropy.

Next, we study the relationship between
|\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C)|
and its corresponding independence heuristic. Recall that for margins
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
and
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
, the
independence heuristic estimate
is the following quantity,

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})=\binom{mn}{N}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n}{r_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\binom{m}{c_{j}},

where
N=\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}
is the total sum of entries. We denote

\mathcal{I}_{n,\delta}(B,C):=\mathcal{I}(\widetilde{\mathbf{r}},\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}).

To study the relation between
\mathcal{I}_{n,\delta}(B,C)
and
|\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C)|
, we consider their
Correlation Ratio

\rho_{n,\delta}(B,C)
. It is defined as

\rho_{n,\delta}(B,C):=\frac{|\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C)|}{\mathcal{I}_{n,\delta}(B,C)}.

Our next main result is on the asymptotic behaviour of
\rho_{n,\delta}(B,C)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
0<\delta<1
,
n\geq 1
,
0<C<\frac{3}{4}
and
0<B\leq\frac{1}{C}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2}}\log\rho_{n,\delta}(B,C)=0\qquad{and}\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{1+\delta}}\log\rho_{n,\delta}(B,C)=0.

Furthermore, let

\Delta_{B,C}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n^{2\delta}}\log\rho_{n,\delta}(B,C),

and we have the following explicit formula for
\Delta_{B,C}
:

\Delta_{B,C}=1-\frac{B^{2}C-2BC+1}{1-C}-\log\left(\frac{1-C}{B^{2}C-2BC+1}\right).

Moreover,

0\geq\Delta_{B,C}>\begin{cases}-\frac{1}{C}+\log\frac{1}{C}+1&\quad\text{when $0<C\leq\frac{1}{2}$},\\
-\frac{1}{1-C}+\log\frac{1}{1-C}+1&\quad\text{when $\frac{1}{2}<C<\frac{3}{4}$},\end{cases}

with
\Delta_{B,C}=0
if and only if
B=1
.

The behaviour of
\Delta_{B,C}
tells us that the independence heuristic overestimates the number of tables in
\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C)
. This matches Barvinok’s arguments on
cloned margins
, see
[
Bar10
]
for details. Probabilistically speaking, the events

\mathcal{R}_{n,\delta}(B,C)=\{\text{$0$-$1$ matrices has row sums $\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}$}\}

and

C_{n,\delta}(B,C)=\{\text{$0$-$1$ matrices has column sums $\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}$}\}

are asymptotically
negatively correlated
instead of being asymptotically independent.
\Delta_{B,C}
quantifies how far they are away from being independent. In fact, when
B=1
, i.e. all row sums and columns sums are equal, the independence heuristic provides the best estimate. As
B
moves away from
1
, meaning that the margins become less and less uniform, the independence heuristic overestimates by a factor of
e^{\Theta(n^{2\delta})}
.

It is really interesting for the readers to compare our results with the recent work of
[
LP22
]
on non-negative integer case. When the contingency tables are non-negative integer valued, the independence heuristic leads to a large undercounting, which is opposite to our binary case. The reason behind this phenomena remain mysterious. It would be nice if we can obtain the intermediate results on the contingency tables whose entries take values from
\{0,1,\ldots,k\}
for finite
k
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
and
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
be two positive integer vectors with total sum of entries. The
binary transportation polytope

\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})
is defined as

\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c}):=\left\{X=(X_{ij})\in[0,1]^{mn}:\sum_{k=1}^{n}X_{ik}=r_{i},\sum_{k=1}^{m}X_{kj}=c_{j},\forall i,j\right\}.

Barvinok introduced the following notion of
Typical Table
in
[
Bar10
]
.

Definition 2.1

(Typical Table)
.

Let
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
,
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be two positive integer vectors with total sum of entries. For each
X=(X_{ij})\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})
, let

g(X)=\sum_{i,j}f(X_{ij}),

where
f(x)=x\log\frac{1}{x}+(1-x)\log\frac{1}{1-x}
for
x\in(0,1)
. The
Typical Table

Z=(z_{ij})
is defined as the unique maximizer of
g
on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})
.

Remark 2.2
.

The function
g
is strictly concave so it attains a unique maximum in the interior of the binary transportation polytope. Hence typical table is well-defined.

Remark 2.3
.

The function
f(x)=x\log\frac{1}{x}+(1-x)\log\frac{1}{1-x}
is the Shannon-Boltzmann entropy of Bernoulli random variable with mean
x
. Therefore,
g(X)
can be viewed as the Bernoulli entropy of
X
and
Z
is the
maximal entropy matrix
on the polytope
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})
.

Remark 2.4
.

By symmetry, two entries in typical table are equal if they have the same margin conditions, i.e
z_{ij}=z_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}
if
r_{i}=r_{i^{\prime}},c_{j}=c_{j^{\prime}}
.

It turns out that typical table has close connection to the cardinality of
\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})
. The following theorem proved by Barvinok in
[
Bar10
]
plays a key role in our proof.

Theorem 2.5
.

Fix row margins
\mathbf{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
and column margins
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
and let
Z=(z_{ij})
be the typical table associated with
\mathcal{P}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})
. There exists some absolute constant
\gamma>0
such that

(2.1)

(mn)^{-\gamma(m+n)}e^{g(Z)}\leq|\mathcal{M}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})|\leq e^{g(Z)}.

Next, by
[
Wu22
, (2.3)]
and
[
Wu22
, Lemma
2.4
]
, we have the following asymptotics of entries of typical table
Z=(z_{ij})
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Fix
0\leq\delta<1
,
0<C<\frac{3}{4}
and
0<B\leq\frac{1}{C}

be the typical table for
\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C)

Proposition 2.7
.

Fix
0<\delta<1
,
0<C<\frac{3}{4}
and
0<B\leq\frac{1}{C}
. Let
Z=(z_{ij})
be the typical table for
\mathcal{M}_{n,\delta}(B,C)

\displaystyle g(Z)

\displaystyle=n^{2}f(z_{n+1,n+1})+2n[n^{\delta}]f(z_{1,n+1})+[n^{\delta}]^{2}f(z_{11})

\displaystyle=f(C)n^{2}+\left[2f(BC)-(BC)\log\left(\frac{1-C}{C}\right)\right]n^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle+\left[f(z_{11}^{*})+z_{11}^{*}\log\left(\frac{1-C}{C}\cdot\frac{(BC)^{2}}{(1-BC)^{2}}\right)-\frac{B^{2}C}{2(1-C)}\right]n^{2\delta}

\displaystyle+O(n^{3\delta-1})+O(n),

where

Proof.

By symmetry of typical table and marginal condition,

\begin{cases}[n^{\delta}]z_{11}+nz_{1,n+1}=[BCn]\\
[n^{\delta}]z_{1,n+1}+nz_{n+1,n+1}=[Cn]\end{cases}.

Therefore,

\begin{cases}([n^{\delta}]/n)z_{11}+z_{1,n+1}=[BCn]/n=BC+O(n^{-1})\\
([n^{\delta}]/n)z_{1,n+1}+z_{n+1,n+1}=[Cn]/n=C+O(n^{-1})\end{cases}

Let
z_{11}^{*}=\frac{B^{2}(1-C)}{B^{2}-2B+1/C}
, then

\displaystyle C-z_{n+1,n+1}

\displaystyle=z_{1,n+1}([n^{\delta}]/n)+O(n^{-1})

\displaystyle=(BC)([n^{\delta}]/n)-(BC-z_{1,n+1})([n^{\delta}]/n)+O(n^{-1})

\displaystyle=(BC)([n^{\delta}]/n)-z_{11}([n^{\delta}]/n)^{2}+O(n^{-1})

\displaystyle=(BC)([n^{\delta}]/n)-z_{11}^{*}([n^{\delta}]/n)^{2}-(z_{11}-z_{11}^{*})([n^{\delta}]/n)^{2}+O(n^{-1})

\displaystyle=(BC)([n^{\delta}]/n)-z_{11}^{*}([n^{\delta}]/n)^{2}+O(n^{3\delta-3}+n^{-1}).

Similarly,

\displaystyle BC-z_{1,n+1}

\displaystyle=([n^{\delta}]/n)z_{11}+O(n^{-1})

\displaystyle=z_{11}^{*}([n^{\delta}]/n)+(z_{11}-z_{11}^{*})([n^{\delta}]/n)+O(n^{-1})

\displaystyle=z_{11}^{*}([n^{\delta}]/n)+O(n^{2\delta-2}+n^{-1}).

Taylor expansion of

around

f(x)=f(a)+\log\left(\frac{1-a}{a}\right)(x-a)+\frac{1}{2(a-1)a}(x-a)^{2}+O\left(|x-a|^{3}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle f(z_{n+1,n+1})

\displaystyle=f(C)-\log\left(\frac{1-C}{C}\right)(BC)([n^{\delta}]/n)+\log\left(\frac{1-C}{C}\right)z_{11}^{*}([n^{\delta}]/n)^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{B^{2}C}{2(C-1)}([n^{\delta}]/n)^{2}+O\left(n^{3\delta-3}\right)+O\left(n^{\delta-1}\right)

\displaystyle f(z_{1,n+1})

\displaystyle=f(BC)-\log\left(\frac{1-BC}{BC}\right)z_{11}^{*}([n^{\delta}]/n)+\frac{1}{2(BC-1)BC}\left(z_{11}^{*}\right)^{2}([n^{\delta}]/n)^{2}

\displaystyle+O\left(n^{2\delta-2}\right)+O\left(n^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle f(z_{11})

\displaystyle=f\left(z_{11}^{*}\right)+O\left(n^{\delta-1}\right)+O\left(n^{-1}\right).

Therefore, we have that

\displaystyle g(Z)

\displaystyle=n^{2}f(z_{n+1,n+1})+2n[n^{\delta}]f(z_{1,n+1})+[n^{\delta}]^{2}f(z_{11})

\displaystyle=f(C)n^{2}+\left[2f(BC)-(BC)\log\left(\frac{1-C}{C}\right)\right]n^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle+\left[f(z_{11}^{*})+z_{11}^{*}\log\left(\frac{1-C}{C}\cdot\frac{(BC)^{2}}{(1-BC)^{2}}\right)-\frac{B^{2}C}{2(1-C)}\right]n^{2\delta}

\displaystyle+O(n^{3\delta-1})+O(n).

3.
Independence Heuristic Estimate

Using Stirling formula, we can deduce the asymptotics of
independence heuristic estimation
. Similar asymptotics of independence heuristic for non-negative integer-valued contingency tables can be found in
[
LP22
, Lemma 4.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Fix
0<\delta<1
,
0<C<1
and
0<B\leq\frac{1}{C}
,

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{I}_{n,\delta}(B,C)

\displaystyle=f(C)n^{2}+\left[2f(BC)-BC\log\left(\frac{1-C}{C}\right)\right]n^{1+\delta}

\displaystyle+\left[\frac{B^{2}C-4BC+2C}{2(1-C)}+\log(1-C)-2\log(1-BC)\right]n^{2\delta}

\displaystyle+O\left(n^{3\delta-1}+n\log n\right).

Proof.

By Stirling formula, we have that

\log\binom{a+b}{a}=(a+b)\log(a+b)-a\log a-b\log b+O\left(\log(a+b)\right).

Recall that for general margins
\mathbf{r}
and
\mathbf{c}
, the independence heuristic estimate takes the form

\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})=\binom{mn}{N}^{-1}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\binom{n}{r_{i}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\binom{m}{c_{j}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{I}(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{c})

\displaystyle=N\log N+(mn-N)\log(mn-N)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}\log r_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}(n-r_{i})\log(n-r_{i})

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}\log c_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(m-c_{j})\log(m-c_{j})+O\left((m+n)\log(mn)\right).

In our setup, when
\mathbf{r}=\widetilde{\mathbf{r}}
and
\mathbf{c}=\widetilde{\mathbf{c}}
, the dimension is
n+n^{\delta}
and the total sum of entries
N=BCn^{1+\delta}+Cn^{2}
. By Taylor expansion

\log(x+y)=\log y+\frac{x}{y}-\frac{x^{2}}{2y^{2}}+O\left(\frac{x^{3}}{y^{3}}\right),

we have that

\displaystyle\log(N)

\displaystyle=\log(Cn^{2}+BCn^{1+\delta})=\log(Cn^{2})+Bn^{\delta-1}-\frac{B^{2}}{2}n^{2\delta-2}+O\left(n^{3\delta-3}\right)

\displaystyle\log(mn-N)

\displaystyle=\log\left((1-C)n^{2}+(2-BC)n^{1+\delta}+n^{2\delta}\right)=\log\left[(1-C)n^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{2-BC}{1-C}\right)n^{\delta-1}+\frac{-2-2C+4BC-B^{2}C^{2}}{2(1-C)^{2}}n^{2\delta-2}+O\left(n^{3\delta-3}\right)

\displaystyle\log\left[(1-C)n+n^{\delta}\right]

\displaystyle=\log[(1-C)n]+\frac{n^{\delta-1}}{1-C}-\frac{n^{2\delta-2}}{2(1-C)^{2}}+O\left(n^{3\delta-3}\right)

\displaystyle\log\left[(1-BC)n+n^{\delta}\right]

\displaystyle=\log\left[(1-BC)n\right]+\frac{n^{\delta-1}}{1-BC}-\frac{n^{2\delta-2}}{2(1-BC)^{2}}+O\left(n^{3\delta-3}\right)

Therefore,
\log\mathcal{I}_{n,\delta}(B,C)
has the following expansion,

\displaystyle\log\mathcal{I}_{n,\delta}(B,C)

\displaystyle=\left(Cn^{2}+BCn^{1+\delta}\right)\left[\log(Cn^{2})+Bn^{\delta-1}-\frac{B^{2}}{2}n^{2\delta-2}\right]

\displaystyle+\left[(1-C)n^{2}+(2-BC)n^{1+\delta}+n^{2\delta}\right]\times

\displaystyle\left[\log((1-C)n^{2})+\left(\frac{2-BC}{1-C}\right)n^{\delta-1}+\frac{-2-2C+4BC-B^{2}C^{2}}{2(1-C)^{2}}n^{2\delta-2}\right]

\displaystyle-2BCn^{1+\delta}\log(BCn)-2n^{\delta}\left[(1-BC)n+n^{\delta}\right]\left[\log((1-BC)n)+\frac{n^{\delta-1}}{1-BC}-\frac{n^{2\delta-2}}{2(1-BC)^{2}}\right]

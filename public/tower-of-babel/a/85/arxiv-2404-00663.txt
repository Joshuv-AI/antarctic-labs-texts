Geometrizations of quantum groups and dual semicanonical bases.
Yingjin Bi

Abstract
. In this paper, we give a geometrization of semicanonical bases of quantum groups via Grothendieck groups of the derived categories of Lusztig’s nilpotent varieties. Additionally, we provide a characterization of the dual semicanonical bases using Serre polynomials of Grassmannians of modules over preprojective algebras.



1.
Introduction

The current study aims to extend the concept of semicanonical bases
\mathcal{S}
in the enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{n})
to the semicanonical bases
\mathcal{S}_{q}
in the quantum groups
U_{q}(\mathfrak{n})
such that
\mathcal{S}_{q=1}=\mathcal{S}
. In
[
Lus00
]
, Lusztig introduced the semicanonical bases of the enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{n})
based on Lusztig’s nilpotent varieties. Following Lusztig’s method, Geiss, Leclerc, and Schröer utilized modules over preprojective algebras to construct the dual semicanonical bases of the coordinate rings of unipotent groups
\mathbb{C}[N]
, see
[
GLS05
]
,
[
GLS07
]
.

It is essential to introduce dual semicanonical bases as they are connected to the cluster structure on the coordinate rings
\mathbb{C}[N]
of unipotent subgroups
N
through the representations of preprojective algebras, see
[
GLS05
]
. For quantum cluster algebras, in
[
GLS13
]
, Geiss, Leclerc, and Schröer demonstrated a quantum cluster algebra structure on the quantum coordinate rings
A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w))
for a specific Weyl group element
w\in W
. All cluster monomials in
A_{q}(\mathfrak{n}(w))
are part of the dual semicanonical bases. The conjecture that these cluster monomials also belong to the dual canonical bases was proven by Qin for the Dynkin case and all symmetrizable Kac-Moody types, see
[
Qin17
]
and
[
Qin20
]
. In
[
KKKO18
]
, Kang, Kim, Kashiwara, and Oh utilized
R
-matrices of modules over quiver Hecke algebras
R_{Q}
to validate this conjecture for symmetric Kac-Moody types. This conjecture establishes a relationship between dual canonical bases and dual semicanonical bases.

However, there is limited research on the quantum counterpart of dual semicanonical bases. One key question is how to characterize these dual semicanonical bases. Our primary result is describing dual semicanonical bases in terms of Serre polynomials of Grassmannians of modules over preprojective algebras. Building on Leclerc’s findings, we can depict the dual canonical bases using shuffle algebras. Similarly, we will present the explicit form of dual semicanonical bases using shuffle algebras.

Let us interpret our main results. For a dimension vector

of a quiver

, where

is the set of vertices of

2.
Derived categories of Lusztig’s nilpotent varieties

In this section, we review some concepts related to derived categories of constructible sheaves on Lusztig’s nilpotent varieties. For a variety
X
over
\mathbb{C}
, we say a sheaf
\mathcal{F}

constructible
if there is a decomposition of
X=\sum_{i}X_{i}
such that
X_{i}
is a constructible subset and
\mathcal{F}_{\mid X_{i}}
is constant for each
i
. For example, for a constructible subset
U\subset X
, the sheaf
\mathbf{I}_{U}
, defined by
\mathbf{I}_{U}(u)=1
for
u\in U
and
\mathbf{I}_{U}(v)=0
for
v\notin U
, is a constructible sheaf. We write the
D^{b}(X)
for the bounded derived category of constructible sheave on
X
.

If there exists an action of an algebraic group
G
on
X
, we refer to a constructible sheaf
\mathcal{F}
as
equivariant
if
\mathcal{F}_{\mid G\cdot x}
is constant for any
x\in X
. The derived category of equivariant constructible sheaves over
X
is denoted as
D_{G}^{b}(X)
. For a
G
-invariant morphism
f:X\to Y
, we can define
f_{!},f_{*}:D_{G}^{b}(X)\to D_{G}^{b}(Y)
and
f^{*},f^{!}:D_{G}^{b}(Y)\to D_{G}^{b}(X)
as detailed in
[
BL06
]
.

The Grothendieck group of
D_{G}^{b}(X)
is denoted as
K_{G}(X)
. It is a module over the ring
\mathbb{C}[q,q^{-1}]
defined by
q\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}[1]
, where
[1]
represents the shift of the complex
\mathcal{F}\in D_{G}^{b}(X)
. For a constant sheaf
\mathbf{I}_{X}
and the canonical projection
p:X\to pt
, we have
p_{!}\mathbf{I}_{X}=\sum_{i}\operatorname{H}_{i}(X)
in
K_{G}(pt)
where
\operatorname{H}_{\bullet}(X)
denotes the Borel-Moore homology of
X
.

2.1.
Constructible functions

Consider an algebraic variety
X
defined over the field of complex numbers. We refer to the space of all constructible functions
f:X\to\mathbb{Q}
as
M(X)
, which is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space. This means that
f^{-1}(a)
is constructible for every
a\in\mathbb{Q}
, and is empty for all but finitely many
a\in\mathbb{Q}
.

The map
\int_{X}:M(X)\to\mathbb{Q}
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map defined as
f\mapsto\int_{X}f=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Q}}a\chi(f^{-1}(a))
, where
\chi
represents the Euler characteristic. If there exists an algebraic group
G
that acts on
X
, we define
M_{G}(X)
as the
\mathbb{Q}
vector space comprising functions
f\in M(X)
such that
f
remains constant on every
G
-orbit within
X
.

Next, we introduce a map from
K_{G}(X)
to
M_{G}(X)
. For a complex
\mathcal{F}\in D_{G}^{b}(X)
, we define a constructible function
\Psi(\mathcal{F})
as

\Psi(\mathcal{F})(x):=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\dim\operatorname{H}^{i}(\mathcal{F})_{x}.

Since
\mathcal{F}
is a bounded complex,
\Psi(\mathcal{F})
is well-defined. This function induces a map as follows.

(2.1)

\begin{split}\Psi:K_{G}(X)&\to M_{G}(X)\\
\mathcal{F}&\mapsto\Psi(\mathcal{F})\end{split}

2.2.
Lusztig’s nilpotent varieties

In this section, we revisit the concept of Lusztig’s nilpotent varieties. Given a quiver
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})

\overline{Q}
by adding the opposing arrows
\bar{\alpha}

\varepsilon(\bar{\alpha})=-1

\bar{\alpha}\in Q_{1}^{op}

The

of

is defined as the quotient algebra

where

is the ideal generated by the equation

(2.2)

\sum\epsilon(\alpha)\alpha\bar{\alpha}=0.

For a dimension vector
\alpha
, we define
\Lambda_{\alpha}
as the variety of modules over
\Lambda_{Q}
with dimension vector
\alpha
. For a given path

for any
z\in\Lambda_{\alpha}
. An element

is deemed

if
z_{h}=0
for any path

of sufficient length. We abbreviate
\Lambda_{\alpha}
for the subvariety of
\Lambda_{\alpha}
consisting of nilpotent elements, if there is no any confusion.

The map

\Psi(\mathcal{F})\star\Psi(\mathcal{E})=\Psi(\mathcal{F}\star\mathcal{E})

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathcal{F}\star\mathcal{E})(x)=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{dim}\operatorname{H}_{i}(q_{!}r_{b}p^{*}(\mathcal{F}\boxtimes\mathcal{E}))_{x}

3.
Geometrization of semicanonical bases of quantum groups

Let us recall the definition of quantum groups. The quantum group of symmetric type is an
\mathbb{C}(q)
-algebra
U_{q}(\operatorname{\mathfrak{g}}_{Q})
generated by
e_{i},f_{i}
for
i\in Q_{0}
and
q^{h}
for
h\in P^{\vee}
subject to the following relations:

\displaystyle q^{0}=1,q^{h}q^{h^{\prime}}=q^{h+h^{\prime}}\quad\text{ for }h,h^{\prime}\in\mathrm{P}^{\vee},

\displaystyle q^{h}e_{i}q^{-h}=q^{\left\langle h,\alpha_{i}\right\rangle}e_{i},\quad q^{h}f_{i}q^{-h}=q^{-\left\langle h,\alpha_{i}\right\rangle}f_{i}\quad\text{ for }h\in\mathrm{P}^{\vee},i\in I,

\displaystyle e_{i}f_{j}-f_{j}e_{i}=\delta_{ij}\frac{t_{i}-t_{i}^{-1}}{q_{i}-q_{i}^{-1}},\quad\text{ where }t_{i}=q^{\mathrm{s}_{i}h_{i}},

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{2}(-1)^{r}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}2\\

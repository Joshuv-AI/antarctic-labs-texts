On emergent conformal symmetry near the photon ring
Bin Chen, Yehui Hou, Zezhou Hu

Abstract
In this note we revisit the emergent conformal symmetry in the near-ring region of warped spacetime. In particular, we propose a novel construction of the emergent near-ring s ​ l ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) QNM sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}} symmetry. We show that each eikonal QNM family falls into one highest-weight representation of this algebra, and s ​ l ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) QNM sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}} can be related to the near-ring isometry group s ​ l ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) ISO sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}} in a simple way. Furthermore we find that the coherent state space of s ​ l ​ ( 2 , ℝ ) QNM sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}} can be identified with the phase space of the photon ring.

The conformal symmetry plays an important role in studying black hole physics. In AdS

. Especially, for the higher dimensional black holes, the conformal symmetry does not present itself explicitly. In the case of the Kerr black hole, the symmetry either appears only in the isometry of the near horizon geometry of extreme Kerr (NHEK)
[
13
]
or emerges as the hidden conformal symmetry in the low frequency scattering
[

,

In the past few years, following the release of the black hole images taken by the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT) collaborations
[
16
]
, the physics encoded in the images has been intensely studied. Of particular interest is the black hole photon ring, which has a subring structure. This subring structure displays universal properties of the black holes, which are insensitive to the environment around the black holes
[

,

,

,
20
,
21

. The photon ring is produced by the unstably bound orbits of photons. Under a perturbation, the photons on the orbit could escape to infinity after orbiting many times. It turns out that these null trajectories provide eikonal approximations to the quasi-normal modes (QNMs)
[

,

,

,

The idea of holography of the photon ring was proposed in
[
27
]
very recently. On the one hand, it was shown that in the near-ring region of both Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes, there emerges a hidden conformal symmetry, referred to as
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{PR}}
, acting on the photon trajectories. More precisely, in the (reduced) phase space of null geodesics,
(T,\Psi,H,L)
for a Schwarzschild black hole or
(T,\Phi,\Psi,H,L,Q+L^{2})
for a Kerr black hole, the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{PR}}
algebra can be constructed via Poisson bracket. On the other hand, considering the massless wave propagation in the near-ring region, the wave equation reduces to the Schrödinger equation with an upside-down harmonic potential, which has a well-known conformal symmetry
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
. It turned out that
the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
algebra could be constructed by Heisenberg algebra, and the quasi-normal modes (QNMs) in the near-ring region can be described in a Fock space
[
28
,
27

. The eikonal
QNMs fall into the highest-weight representations of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}

[
2

and the warped black holes
[
29
,
30
,
31

. However, it is perplexing that the entire QNMs constructed in
[
27
]
are decomposed into
two
highest-weight representations of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
, with one tower of descendants having even overtone numbers and the other having odd overtone numbers.

In a more recent work, the study was generalized to the self-dual warped AdS
3
geometry
[
32
]
, which is similar to the NHEK geometry and has an exact
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
isometry group. It was showed that the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{PR}}
algebra in the phase space can be induced from
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
by simply contracting the Killing vectors with the momentum of the photon. The entire eikonal QNM spectrum can be constructed by using the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
symmetry as well. However, because each QNM overtone family has two highest-weight representations of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
in the near-ring region, the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
symmetry constructed from the Heisenberg algebra failed to match the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
symmetry.

In this note, we present a novel way to construct
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
in the near-ring region. For simplicity, our construction focuses on the self-dual warped AdS
3
geometry, but it can be applied to the near-ring regions of four-dimensional black holes as well. We show that within our construction the entire eikonal QNMs spectrum is described by only
one
highest-weight representation and consequently the newly constructed
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
match with the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
symmetry nicely. Moreover, we show that there is an exact correspondence between the near-ring QNMs and the points in the phase space of null geodesics, which may allow us to further identify the trajectories in the phase space with the coherent states of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
.

2
Constructions of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{PR}}

For simplicity, we show our construction in a self-dual warped AdS
3
geometry. The metric, which can be obtained from the near-horizon approximation of a near-extreme warped AdS
3
black hole
[
33
]
, is of the form

ds^{2}=l^{2}\left[\frac{-dT^{2}+dx^{2}}{\sinh^{2}x}+\Lambda^{2}\left(d\phi+\frac{dT}{\tanh x}\right)^{2}\right]\,,

(2.1)

where
l
is the (warped) AdS
3
radius,
T
and
x
are the time and radial coordinates, respectively. The parameter
\Lambda
is a warping factor.

The null geodesics in the above spacetime are described by the entire phase space (EPS) with the coordinates
(x^{\mu},p_{\mu})
,
x^{\mu}=T,x,\phi
,
p_{x}=p_{T},p_{x},p_{\phi}
, and a constrained Hamiltonian,
\mathcal{H}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}p_{\mu}p_{\nu}=0
. The Hamilton equations are

\{x^{\mu},\mathcal{H}\}_{\text{EPS}}=\frac{dx^{\mu}}{dt}\,,\quad\{p_{\mu},\mathcal{H}\}_{\text{EPS}}=\frac{dp_{\mu}}{dt}\,,

(2.2)

where
t
is the affine parameter, and the Poisson bracket is defined as
\{A,B\}_{\text{EPS}}=\frac{\partial A}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial B}{\partial p_{\mu}}-\frac{\partial B}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial A}{\partial p_{\mu}}
. Since the metric does not depend on
T
and
\phi
, there are two constants of motion, i.e., the energy and angular momentum,
H=-p_{T}
,
L=p_{\phi}
. Then, the equations of motion are

\displaystyle\big(\frac{l}{\sinh{x}}\big)^{2}\frac{dx}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm_{x}H\sqrt{V(x)}\,,

\displaystyle l^{2}\frac{dT}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H\sinh{x}(\sinh{x}+\lambda\cosh{x})\,,\,

\displaystyle l^{2}\frac{d\phi}{dt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H(\lambda\Lambda^{-2}-\lambda\cosh^{2}{x}-\sinh{x}\cosh{x})\,,

(2.3)

where
\pm_{x}
denotes the sign of
dx/dt
, and the radial potential reads

V(x)=(1+\lambda\coth{x})^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\Lambda^{2}\sinh^{2}{x}}\geq 0\,.

(2.4)

Note that we have introduced a dimensionless impact parameter,
\lambda=L/H
. The bound photon orbits are determined by

V(x)=V^{\prime}(x)=0\,,

(2.5)

which gives the double roots of the potential corresponding to the unstable bound photon orbits. After solving Eq. (
2.5
), we get

\hat{x}_{\pm}=\pm\tanh^{-1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}}}\,,\quad\hat{\lambda}_{\pm}=\mp\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}}}\,.

(2.6)

We assume
\Lambda
to be greater than
1
to ensure the existence of bound photon orbits. From now on, we only consider the outer orbit labeled by “
+
” and write
\hat{\lambda}=\hat{\lambda}_{+}
,
\hat{x}=\hat{x}_{+}
for simplicity.

The null geodesics can also be studied in a reduced phase space (RPS) with the coordinates
(x,\phi,p_{x},p_{\phi})
, the symplectic form
dp_{x}\wedge dx+dp_{\phi}\wedge d\phi
and the reduced Hamiltonian
H=-p_{T}
. From the first two equations in Eq. (
2
), we find that
T=T(x,p_{x},p_{\phi})
is canonically conjugate to
H
, that is,
\{T,H\}_{\text{RPS}}=1
. Thus, we choose
T
as the time function. Since we only concentrate on the RPS in the following, we omit the subscript RPS in the Poisson bracket for simplicity.

In addition, we can simplify the system further by introducing a new variable
[
32
]

\Psi(x,\phi,p_{x},p_{\phi})=\int d\phi+H\int\bigg[\coth{x}(1+\lambda\coth{x})-\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda^{2}\sinh^{2}{x}}\bigg]\frac{dx}{\sqrt{V(x)}}\,.

(2.7)

Then, the RPS can be described by the four variables
(T,\Psi,H,L)
with the symplectic form
dH\wedge dT+dL\wedge d\Psi
. The equations of motion can be rewritten as

\dot{T}=\{T,H\}=1\,,\quad\dot{\Psi}=\{\Psi,H\}=0\,,\quad\dot{L}=\{L,H\}=0\,,

(2.8)

where the “dot” represents the derivative with respect to
T
. Now we study the properties related to the bound photon orbit in the RPS described by
(T,\Psi,H,L)
. To characterize the deviation from the bound photon orbit, we define

\hat{H}=H-L/\hat{\lambda}\,.

(2.9)

The bound photon orbit has
\hat{H}=0
. As in
[
27
,
32
]
, the relation
\{T,H\}=\{T,\hat{H}\}=1
can be used to construct a
sl(2,\mathds{R})
algebra, which is generated by

H_{+}=\hat{H}\,,\quad H_{0}=-\hat{H}T\,,\quad H_{-}=\hat{H}T^{2}\,,

(2.10)

with the commutation relation

\{H_{p},H_{q}\}=(p-q)H_{p+q}\,,\qquad(p,q=+,0,-)\,.

(2.11)

Here, the quantity of physical interest is
T\hat{H}
. To see this, we define the finite action of
A
on
B
as

\displaystyle e^{-\alpha A}Be^{\alpha A}\equiv\sum_{n}\frac{(-\alpha)^{n}}{n!}\operatorname{ad}_{A}^{n}B\,,\quad\operatorname{ad}_{A}B\equiv\{A,B\}\,,

(2.12)

with both
A,B
being the functions in the RPS. Then, the action of
T\hat{H}
on
\hat{H}
is

e^{-\alpha T\hat{H}}\hat{H}e^{\alpha T\hat{H}}=e^{-\alpha}\hat{H}\,,

(2.13)

which means that
e^{-\alpha H_{0}}
is a finite dilation, leading to the attraction towards the bound photon orbit if
\alpha\char 62\relax 0
.

However, the construction of the
sl(2,\mathds{R})
algebra is not unique. Based on the relation
\{T,\hat{H}\}=1
, there exist the following constructions of
sl(2,\mathds{R})
algebra as well,

\displaystyle H_{+}=T\,,\quad H_{0}=T\hat{H}\,,\quad H_{-}=T\hat{H}^{2}\,,

(2.14)

or

\displaystyle H_{+}=\frac{1}{2}T^{2}\,,\quad H_{0}=\frac{1}{2}T\hat{H}\,,\quad H_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{H}^{2}\,,

(2.15)

and the automorphism of
sl(2,\mathds{R})

\displaystyle H_{\pm}\rightarrow H_{\mp}\,,\ H_{0}\rightarrow-H_{0}\,.

(2.16)

Note that all the constructions above have
H_{0}\propto T\hat{H}
. Moreover, all the constructions satisfy the same constraint

H_{0}^{2}=H_{+}H_{-}\,.

(2.17)

Since the algebra commute with
L
, a superselection sectors of the RPS with fixed
L
(sub-RPS) can be described by three generators
(H_{+},H_{0},H_{-})
with the constraint (
2.17
).

In addition, the relation
\{\Psi,L\}=1
also leads to similar constructions of
sl(2,\mathds{R})
algebra. For example, there is

H_{+}=\hat{L},\,\ H_{0}=-\hat{L}\Psi,\,\ H_{-}=\hat{L}\Psi^{2}

(2.18)

with
\hat{L}=L-\tilde{\lambda}H=-\tilde{\lambda}\hat{H}
describing the deviation from the bound photon orbit, which has
\hat{L}=0
.
Here, the dilation operator is
\Psi\hat{L}
, since under a finite action, we have

e^{-\alpha\Psi\hat{L}}\hat{L}e^{\alpha\Psi\hat{L}}=e^{-\alpha}\hat{L}\,.

(2.19)

The reason for the existence of two schemes, Eq (
2.13
) and Eq (
2.19
), is that the bound photon orbit is parameterized only by the impact parameter,
\lambda=L/H
.

3
Novel construction of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}

In this section, we discuss the properties of the near-ring QNMs introduced in
[
32
]
, and present a novel construction of the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
algebra of the QNM spectrum. Although the construction present here is based on the warped AdS
3
spacetime, it depends solely on the photon ring and the near-ring region. Thus, our construction can be extended to the four-dimensional Schwarzschild and Kerr black holes straightforwardly.

The isometry group of the warped AdS
3
spacetime Eq. (
2.1
) is generated by the following Killing vectors

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}=-\partial_{T}\,,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\pm}=e^{\pm T}(-\cosh x\partial_{T}\mp\sinh x\partial_{x}+\sinh x\partial_{\phi})\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{0}=\partial_{\phi}\,.

The first three vectors generate an
sl(2,\mathds{R})
algebra, referred to as
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
. The commutation relation is

[\mathcal{L}_{p},\mathcal{L}_{q}]=(p-q)\mathcal{L}_{p+q}\,,\qquad(p,q=+,0,-)\,.

(3.2)

We consider a free scalar field
\Phi(T,x,\phi)
minimally coupled with gravity. The equation of motion reads

\nabla^{2}\Phi=-\frac{1}{l^{2}}\left[-\mathcal{L}_{0}^{2}+\frac{\mathcal{L}_{+}\mathcal{L}_{-}+\mathcal{L}_{-}\mathcal{L}_{+}}{2}+\left(1-\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}\right)\mathcal{W}_{0}^{2}\right]\Phi=0\,,

(3.3)

where the Laplacian is rewritten in terms of the Casimir and commutes with the Lie-derivative along the vector fields defined in Eq. (
3.1
). Hence, the isometric symmetry is retained in the solution space of
\Phi
. As the translations along
T
and
\phi
are obvious Killing vectors, the eigenfunctions of Eq. (
3.3
) can be labeled by the eigenvalues of the operators

\mathcal{L}_{0}
and
\mathcal{W}_{0}
,

\Phi(T,\phi,x)=e^{im\phi}e^{(-i\hat{\omega}_{R}+\hat{\omega}_{I})T}\psi(x)\,,

(3.4)

where
m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2...
is the angular quantum number, and
\hat{\omega}=\hat{\omega}_{R}+i\hat{\omega}_{I}
is the complex frequency of the modes, with
\hat{\omega}_{R}
and
\hat{\omega}_{I}
the real and imaginary parts, respectively.

The QNMs require the ingoing condition at the horizon where
x\rightarrow+\infty
, and the outgoing condition at infinity where
x\rightarrow 0
. As shown in
[
32
]
, the conditions are satisfied by the highest-weight representation of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
,

\Phi_{m,n}(T,\phi,x)=e^{im\phi}e^{-(i\hat{\omega}_{R}+\frac{1}{2}+n)T}\psi_{m,n}(x)\,,

(3.5)

where
n=0,1,2...
is the overtone number, and

\hat{\omega}_{R}=-m\sqrt{1-\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}-\frac{1}{4m^{2}}}\,.

(3.6)

The fundamental mode
\Phi_{m,0}
is annihilated by
\mathcal{L}_{+}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{L}_{+}\Phi_{m,0}=0
, which means that it is the highest-weight state,

\mathcal{L}_{0}\Phi_{m,0}=\hat{h}\Phi_{m,0}=\bigg(\frac{1}{2}+i\hat{\omega}_{R}\bigg)\Phi_{m,0}\,.

(3.7)

The descendants
\Phi_{m,n}(n\char 62\relax 0)
can be obtained by acting
\mathcal{L}_{-}
on
\Phi_{m,0}
successively,

\Phi_{m,n}\propto\mathcal{L}_{-}^{n}\Phi_{m,0}\,.

(3.8)

We would not like to discuss the QNMs for generic
m
, so we do not need the specific form of
\psi_{m,n}(x)
, which were studied in detail in the section 2.3 of
[
32
]
.
From now on, we consider the eikonal limit of the QNMs with
m\gg 1
. The eikonal QNMs can be obtained by taking
|m|\rightarrow\infty
in (
3.5
), which leads to a simple form of the wave-function

\Phi_{m,n}=e^{-(n+\hat{h})T}e^{im\phi+imx}(\sinh x)^{\hat{h}}(\cosh x+\hat{\lambda}\sinh x)^{n}\,,

(3.9)

where the highest weight is

\hat{h}=1/2+i\hat{\omega}_{R}\,,\quad\hat{\omega}_{R}\approx m\hat{\Omega}\,

(3.10)

with
\hat{\Omega}=\hat{\lambda}^{-1}=-\sqrt{1-\Lambda^{-2}}
being the angular velocity of the (outer) bound photon orbit.

Next, we focus on the “near-ring region” of the eikonal QNMs, defined by

\displaystyle

\displaystyle|\delta x|\ll 1\,,

(3.11)

\displaystyle|m/\hat{\omega}_{R}-\hat{\lambda}|\ll 1\,,

\displaystyle|\hat{\omega}_{R}|\gg 1\,,

where
\delta x=x-\hat{x}_{+}
describing the deviation from the bound photon orbit.
In the warped AdS
3
spacetime, the last two conditions indicate the eikonal condition
|m|\gg 1
. Note that in the near-ring region, the eikonal limit holds only if
|\hat{\omega}_{R}|\delta x^{2}\gg 1
, which is the requirement that in this small region, the wavefront is still rapidly changing
[
34
]
. In this case, the eikonal QNMs in Eq. (
3.9
) become

\Phi^{\text{(NR)}}_{m,n}\approx c_{0}e^{-(n+\hat{h})T}e^{im\phi}\left(-\frac{\delta x}{\sinh\tilde{x}}\right)^{n}e^{-\frac{i}{2}m\hat{\Omega}k\delta x^{2}}\,,

(3.12)

where
k=(\Lambda^{2}-1)^{-1}
,
c_{0}=e^{im\tilde{x}}(\sinh{\td{x}})^{im\hat{\Omega}}
. The superscript “NR” denotes the near-ring approximation. To check that the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
still exists under such approximation, we act the Lie-derivatives on the QNMs in Eq. (
3.12
) and find

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}\Phi^{\text{(NR)}}_{m,n}=(n+\hat{h})\Phi^{\text{(NR)}}_{m,n}\,,

(3.13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{0}\Phi^{\text{(NR)}}_{m,n}=im\Phi^{\text{(NR)}}_{m,n}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{+}\Phi^{\text{(NR)}}_{m,n}\approx n\Phi^{\text{(NR)}}_{m,n-1}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{-}\Phi^{\text{(NR)}}_{m,n}\approx 2im\hat{\Omega}\Phi^{\text{(NR)}}_{m,n+1}\,.

In the last two equations, we have expanded
\delta x
in
\mathcal{L}_{\pm}
and used the eikonal conditions
|m|\gg 1
,
|\hat{\omega}_{R}|\delta x^{2}\gg 1
. Therefore, the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
is retained in the solution space of near-ring eikonal QNMs.

where

\hat{s}\approx-\sinh{\td{x}}\sqrt{2k\hat{\omega}_{R}}\,.

(3.20)

Based on the above analysis, we would like to propose a new construction of
sl(2,\mathds{R})
algebra by using the creation and annihilation operators.

In
[
27
]
, the authors have proposed a construction of
sl(2,\mathds{R})
which involved two highest-weight representations with the weights
1/4
and
3/4
, respectively. These two towers of modes must be interwoven to form the entire QNMs. We aim to solve this perplexing “two towers” problem in the following construction. Firstly, we define
\mathcal{N}(L_{\pm})
to be the number of
a_{-}
minus the number of
a_{+}
in
L_{\pm}
. Then, we require

\mathcal{N}(L_{-})=+1\,,\quad\mathcal{N}(L_{+})=-1\,.

(3.21)

To form an
sl(2,\mathds{R})
algebra,
L_{0}
should be of the form
a_{-}a_{+}+\hat{h}
so that

[L_{0},L_{p}]=-pL_{p}\,,\qquad(p=+,-)\,.

(3.22)

Note that we add
\hat{h}
to the expression of
L_{0}
to give the correct highest weight. Then, by virtue of the Heisenberg algebra, the generators satisfying Eq. (
3.21
) can always be written as

\displaystyle L_{+}

\displaystyle=F(a_{-}a_{+})a_{+}\,,

(3.23)

\displaystyle L_{0}

\displaystyle=a_{-}a_{+}+\hat{h}\,,

\displaystyle L_{-}

\displaystyle=a_{-}G(a_{-}a_{+})\,,

where
F(a_{-}a_{+}),G(a_{-}a_{+})
are two functions constrained by

[L_{+},L_{-}]=2L_{0}.

(3.24)

Acting both sides of Eq.(
3.24
) on the state
\Phi^{(\text{NR})}_{mn}
, we obtain

(n+1)F(n)G(n)-nF(n-1)G(n-1)=2(n+\hat{h})\,.

(3.25)

The general solution of the difference equation for
nF(n-1)G(n-1)
is

nF(n-1)G(n-1)=n^{2}-n+2\hat{h}n+C\,,

(3.26)

where
C
is a constant. The boundary condition at
n=0
gives
C=0
. Thus, the two functions
F
and
G
satisfy

F(a_{-}a_{+})G(a_{-}a_{+})=a_{-}a_{+}+2\hat{h}\,.

(3.27)

Therefore, the generators Eq. (
3.23
) form an
sl(2,\mathds{R})
algebra which we denote as
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
. So far, the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
algebra is totally constructed from the Heisenberg algebra regardless of the physical situation. However, we expect
L_{\pm}
to raise and lower the overtone numbers of

In the solution space of near-ring QNMs spanned by

as
|0\rangle
where we have suppressed the angular quantum number. With the help of the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) formula, a generic element of

group can be rewritten as

e^{\theta L_{-}}e^{AL_{0}}e^{BL_{+}}

|A,\theta\rangle=e^{\theta L_{-}}e^{AL_{0}}e^{BL_{+}}|0\rangle=e^{\hat{h}A+\theta L_{-}}|0\rangle\,.

(4.1)

The coherent state space of

5
Discussions

In this note, we discuss the realizations of the emergent conformal symmetries near the photon ring of a warped black hole. There are three types of conformal symmetries
[
32
]
: the isometry
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
, the emergent one
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{PR}}
in the phase space of the photon ring, and the other emergent one
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
in the solution space of high-frequency scattering in the near region. We managed to find a new construction of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
, which provides more transparent relations with
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
and
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{PR}}
. We showed that when acting the newly constructed
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
and
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
on the eikonal QNMs, we have the same results at the leading order, even though the two constructions are different. Consequently, the entire eikonal QNMs fall into the highest-weight representations of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
or equivalently of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{ISO}}
. We also showed that there is a correspondence between the coherent state space of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
and the phase space of the photon ring.

In our study, we focus on the warped spacetime in three dimensions. As discussed in the article
[
27
]
, the near-ring QNMs have emergent conformal symmetry in both the Schwarzschild and the Kerr black holes. The novel construction of the
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{QNM}}
is algebraic and can be applied to the four-dimensional black hole as well.

For the Schwarzschild black holes, the null geodesics are labeled by a single impact parameter,
\lambda=L/H
. Thus, Eq. (
2.13
) and Eq. (
2.19
) can be generalized to Schwarzschild black holes to construct
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{PR}}
algebra. These two schemes provide the same attraction flows toward the bound photon orbit. Moreover, there exists a correspondence between the coherent state space and the photon phase space for the Schwarzschild black hole.

However, for the Kerr black holes, two impact parameters are involved in the photon ring, which are
\lambda=L/H
,
\eta=Q/H^{2}
with
Q
the Carter constant. Hence, the construction of
sl(2,\mathds{R})_{\text{PR}}
in Kerr black holes necessarily requires the participation of the canonical pair
(T,H)
, which gives the right attraction flows towards the bound photon orbits, as shown in Fig.2 in
[
27
]
. Besides, as the photon phase space in Kerr is of six dimension, how to establish the correspondence between the coherent state space and the phase space is an interesting question.

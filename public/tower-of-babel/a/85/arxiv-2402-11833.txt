Kiyoon Eum

Abstract
. Let Ω \Omega be a bounded pseudoconvex domain in ℂ N \mathbb{C}^{N} . 1.
Introduction

Gaussian random polynomials and Gaussian random analytic functions serve as a means of introducing randomness in complex analysis, allowing for the study of the generic behavior of analytic objects. For instance, the distribution of zeros of random polynomials has been extensively studied in the literature, including
[
Ham56
]
and
[
SZ03
]
.

Another instance of introducing randomness in analysis comes from harmonic analysis. When it is difficult to construct an object satisfying some prescribed properties, it is often easier to define a random object that satisfies these properties almost surely. For related results and methods, see
[
Kah85
]
.

In
[
Blo05
]
and
[
BL15
]
, Bloom and Levenberg considered an asymptotic value of logarithms of random polynomials normalized by their degree and provided a sequence of random polynomials whose normalized logarithms converge to the pluricomplex Green function almost surely in the probability space of the sequence of random polynomials. More specifically, their construction involves a sequence of random polynomials of the form

(1.1)

f_{n}(z)=\sum_{|\nu|\leq n}a_{\nu}^{(n)}p_{\nu}^{(n)}(z)

where
a_{\nu}^{(n)}
are i.i.d. complex Gaussian random variables and
p_{\nu}^{(n)}(z)
are orthonormal basis of certain reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{n}
of polynomials. Under some condition on
\mathcal{H}_{n}
(see (
1.3
)), they proved that

(1.2)

\left(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|\right)^{*}=V_{K}(z)\;\;for\,all\;z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

where
V_{K}(z)
is the pluricomplex Green function for the regular compact set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
(in the sense of pluripotential theory). Here,
h^{*}
means the upper semi-continuous regularization of
h
. They also proved that in (
1.2
) the pointwise convergence can be replaced by
L^{1}_{loc}
convergence (
[
BL15
, Theorem 4.1, Theorem 4.2]
). See also
[
BD19
,
Dau21
]
for generalization of results in
[
Blo05
,
BL15
]
.

In this paper, we prove an analogous result of (
1.2
) for an arbitrary continuous plurisubharmonic (psh for short) function
u
on
\Omega
in place of
V_{K}
. The main ingredient of the construction in
[
BL15
]
was the following limiting behavior of the reproducing kernel
B_{n}(z,w)
of
\mathcal{H}_{n}
(
[
BL15
, Proposition 3.1]
):

(1.3)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{2n}\log B_{n}(z,z)=V_{K}(z)\;\;pointwisely.

For general psh function
u
on
\Omega
, Demailly
[
Dem92
]
constructed a sequence of reproducing kernel Hilbert space
(\mathcal{H}(nu))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
whose reproducing kernels satisfy (
1.3
) with
u
in place of
V_{K}
. We will define a sequence of Gaussian analytic functions(GAF’s) satisfying the analog of (
1.2
) using
(\mathcal{H}(nu))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and this will be our main result (Theorem
2
).

Theorem

(Main result)
.

Let
u
be arbitrary continuous psh function on
\Omega
and let
(f_{n})_{n}
be sequence of GAF’s defined in Definition
1
. Then

(1.4)

(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|)^{*}=u(z)\;\;for\,all\;z\in\Omega

and

(1.5)

\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|\rightarrow u\;\;in\;L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)\;\;as\;n\rightarrow\infty

hold almost surely.

Unlike the case in
[
BL15
]
, the property of analytic functions (Theorem
1
) is crucially used in the construction of the Gaussian analytic functions and the proof of the results because of the infinite dimensionality of our Hilbert spaces. See the comment before Theorem
2
.

As a corollary, we give a probabilistic proof for the deterministic theorem of Hörmander (Corollary
1
), stating that the family
\{m^{-1}\log|f|:f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega),\,m\in\mathbb{N}\}
is dense in
PSH(\Omega)
equipped with
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
-topology. We denote the space of psh functions on
\Omega
which are not identically
-\infty
by
PSH(\Omega)
and the space of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
by
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
. The proof of the main result and submean value inequality implies that the corresponding random variables induced by linear functionals such as
M_{v}(z,r):=\fint_{B(z,r)}v\,d\lambda
have a subexponential decay of tail probability.

Finally, we apply our main result to the distribution of zeros of Gaussian analytic functions. By
[
SZ08
]
, we know that the expected distribution of simultaneous zeros of independent
k
copies of
f_{n}
converges to
(dd^{c}u)^{k}
as a current, where
(dd^{c}u)^{k}
is defined as a positive closed current in the sense of Bedford-Taylor
[
BT82
]
. Using the main result, we can slightly improve this for
k=1
by proving convergence of zeros of
f_{n}
without taking expectation.

Remark
.

After uploading this article to arXiv, the author discovered that Gaussian analytic functions (GAF), or more precisely, Gaussian holomorphic sections on higher dimensional noncompact complex manifolds, have already been studied in
[
DLM25
]
and
[
BG24
]
. In particular, our definition of GAF coincides with equation (2.8) in
[

]
. However, while they assume smoothness and some further conditions on
u
(see Condition 1.2 in
[
DLM25
]
), we only assume
u
to be continuous. As a result, our result is not a special case of theirs. See also
[

]
.

2.
Probabilistic Preliminaries

We start with some basic properties of Gaussian random variables. For the definition of complex Gaussian random variables, see
[
HKP+09
]
, Section 2.1. We denote by
N(\mu,\sigma^{2})
and
N_{\mathbb{C}}(w,\sigma^{2})
the real Gaussian distribution and complex Gaussian distribution, respectively.

Proposition 1
.

Let
a_{j}\sim N_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\sigma_{j}^{2})
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
and
(a_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
are independent. Let
c\in\mathbb{C}
. Then

(i)
ca_{j}\sim N_{\mathbb{C}}(0,|c|^{2}\sigma_{j}^{2})

(ii)
\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\sim N_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sigma_{j}^{2})

(iii) If
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}^{2}<\infty
, then
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}\sim N_{\mathbb{C}}(0,\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}^{2})

As a consequence we have a following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(a_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables and
(c_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a
l^{2}
-sequence of numbers which is not identically zero. Then there exists a constant
C
independent of
(c_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
, such that

(2.1)

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}a_{j}|}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|^{2}}}<\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)<\frac{C}{n^{2}}

for all
n>1
.

Proof.

By Proposition
1
, we know that

(2.2)

\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}a_{j}}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|^{2}}}\sim N_{\mathbb{C}}(0,1).

Let
C>0
be an upper bound of the density function of
N_{\mathbb{C}}(0,1)
with respect to Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
. Then

(2.3)

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}c_{j}a_{j}|}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|c_{j}|^{2}}}<\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(|N_{\mathbb{C}}(0,1)|<\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)\leq\int_{\{|z|<1/n^{2}\}}Cd\lambda(z)\leq\frac{C\pi}{n^{4}}

where
d\lambda
denotes Lebesgue measure. Same reasoning applies to the real Gaussian case also. In fact as the proof shows,
\frac{1}{n}
instead of
\frac{1}{n^{2}}
in the inequality inside of
\mathbb{P}
would be enough for the complex Gaussian random variables.
∎

Next, we recall the definition of a Gaussian analytic function. In this paper, we only work on domains in complex Euclidian space. A stochastic process
X(x),x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N},
is a
Gaussian process
if all the finite-dimensional marginals of
X
are multivariate Gaussian. It is called
centered Gaussian process
if all of these multivariate Gaussians have a mean zero. It is called
Gaussian analytic function (GAF)
if it is centered and takes value in
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
. This definition coincides with the one given in
[
HKP+09
]
, Definition 2.2.1 when
N=1
.

As a Gaussian random variable is completely determined by its first and second moments, Gaussian process on
\Omega
is completely determined by its expectation and covariance functions. In particular, there exists at most one GAF for a given covariance function.

To prove the main result we need a following lemma. This is a Hilbert space version of Kolmogorov’s two-series theorem.

Lemma 2
.

Let
H
be a Hilbert space with Hilbert space norm
||\cdot||
. Assume that for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
X_{j}
is a
H
-valued random vector with
\mathbb{E}(X_{j})=0
,
\mathbb{E}||X_{j}||^{2}<\infty
. If
(X_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
are independent and
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}||X_{j}||^{2}<\infty
, then the series
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}
converges in
H
almost surely. In this case,
\mathbb{E}||\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}||^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}||X_{j}||^{2}

Proof.

See
[
Kah85
, Theorem 2, Chapter 3]
. The statement about
\mathbb{E}||\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}X_{j}||^{2}
follows from monotone convergence theorem.
∎

We end this section by mentioning a few remarks. The first one is that a countable intersection of events of probability one again has probability one. The second one is the Borel-Cantelli lemma, stating that for any sequence of events
\mathcal{A}_{n}
, if
\sum\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}_{n})<\infty
, then
\mathbb{P}(\;\mathcal{A}_{n}\;\;infinitely\;\;often\;)=0
. Lastly, throughout the paper, almost surely means almost surely in the probability space of the sequence of GAF’s or i.i.d. Gaussian random variables, unless otherwise specified. In any case the meaning will be obvious from the context.

3.
Complex Analytic Preliminaries

The importance of Lemma
2
in the study of Gaussian analytic functions relies on the following theorem of complex analysis :

Theorem 1
.

For any compact subset
K
of
\Omega
and every relatively compact open neighborhood
\omega
of
K
in
\Omega
, there are constants
C_{p}
for each
p\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(3.1)

\sup_{K}|f|\leq C_{p}||f||_{L^{p}(\omega,d\lambda)}

for all
f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Hölder’s inequality, we only need to prove it for
p=1
. For
p=1
, see
[
H9̈0
, Theorem 2.2.3]
.
∎

Throughout this paper, we assume
\Omega
to be a bounded pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
and
u
to be a psh function on
\Omega
. The following estimate of the weighted Bergman kernel will be used. See also the exposition by Błocki
[
Bło09
]
.

Proposition 2

(
[
Dem92
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Let
u
be a psh function on
\Omega
. For every
n>0
, let
\mathcal{H}(nu)
be the Hilbert space of holomorphic functions
f
on
\Omega
such that
\int_{\Omega}|f|^{2}e^{-2nu}d\lambda<\infty
and let
u_{n}=\frac{1}{2n}\log\sum_{j}|\sigma_{j}^{(n)}|^{2}
where
(\sigma_{j}^{(n)})
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}(nu)
. Then there are constants
L_{1},L_{2}>0
independent of
n
such that

(3.2)

u(z)-\frac{L_{1}}{n}\leq u_{n}(z)\leq\sup_{|\zeta-z|<r}u(\zeta)+\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{L_{2}}{r^{N}}

for every
z\in\Omega
and
r<d(z,\partial\Omega)
. In particular,
u_{n}
converges to
u
pointwisely and in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Note that
B_{n}(z,w)=\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(z)\overline{\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(w)}
are the reproducing kernels of
\mathcal{H}(nu)
. Since
u
is locally bounded from above, Theorem
1
implies
\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(z)\overline{\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(w)}
converges locally uniformly on
\Omega\times\Omega
.

We will need a notion of current for application in zeros of GAF’s. A current of degree
m
is a continuous linear functional on the space of test forms (i.e., smooth differential forms with compact support) of degree
2N-m
, where
2N
is a real dimension of the domain. For example, Schwartz distribution can be considered as current of degree
2N
. As
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
, complex structure induces a type on the space of differential forms and currents. A current of bidegree
(p,q)
is a continuous linear functional on the space of test forms of bidegree
(N-p,N-q)
. We can also define differential operators
d,d^{c},\partial,\bar{\partial}
on currents, where
d^{c}:=\frac{i}{2\pi}(\bar{\partial}-\partial)
. Current
T
is called closed current if
dT=0
.

Let us give few examples. If
u\in PSH(\Omega)
, then
dd^{c}u
is a closed positive (1,1) current on
\Omega
. For
Z\subset\Omega
a closed complex analytic subset of pure dimension

m
, an integration over
Z_{reg}
defines a closed positive current
[Z]
of bidegree
(N-m,N-m)
on
\Omega
. In particular, if
f
is a non constant holomorphic function on
\Omega
, its zero locus
Z_{f}
defines a positive closed current on
\Omega
which satisfies the Poincaré-Lelong formula (
5.1
) in the sense of currents. For more detailed introduction to currents in complex analysis, see
[
Dem97
]
.

4.
Definition and Convergence Results

The following lemma provides a convenient way of constructing GAF’s when we have explicit information on the covariance function. For example, it applies to Bergman-type kernel functions. This is due to
[
HKP+09
]
, Lemma 2.2.3., which is valid for
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
N\geq 1
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
(\varphi_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
be holomorphic functions on
\Omega
such that
\sum_{j}|\varphi_{j}|^{2}
converges locally uniformly. Let
(a_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
be an i.i.d. standard complex Gaussian random variables. Then
f(z)=\sum_{j}a_{j}\varphi_{j}(z)
almost surely converges locally uniformly on
\Omega
and hence defines a GAF. It has a covariance function
B(z,w)=\sum_{j}\varphi_{j}(z)\overline{\varphi_{j}(w)}
.

Proof.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\Omega
and
\omega
be a relatively compact open neighborhood of
K
in
\Omega
. By uniform convergence of
\sum_{j}|\varphi_{j}|^{2}
on
\omega
, we have
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}||\varphi_{j}||_{L^{2}(\omega)}^{2}<\infty
. Hence
\sum_{j}a_{j}\varphi_{j}(z)
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
2
with
a_{j}\varphi_{j}
in place of
X_{j}
. By Theorem
1
, this implies the uniform convergence of
\sum_{j}a_{j}\varphi_{j}(z)
on
K
. The rest of the statements are immediate.
∎

Now we can define our GAF’s.

Definition 1
.

Let
u
be a continuous psh function on
\Omega
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(\sigma_{j}^{(n)})_{j=1}^{\infty}
,
\mathcal{H}(nu)
as in Proposition
2
. We define GAF’s
f_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
by

(4.1)

f_{n}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}^{(n)}\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(z)

where
(a_{j}^{(n)})_{j=1}^{\infty}
are i.i.d. standard complex Gaussian random variables.

Since the invariant sum
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(z)\overline{\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(w)}
is such an important object in several complex variables, namely a (weighted) Bergman kernel, we would like to have a similar invariance for the sum in (
4.1
). Indeed by remark after the definition of GAF, this sum is independent of the choice of the orthonormal basis
(\sigma_{j}^{(n)})_{j=1}^{\infty}
. We denote their covariance functions by

(4.2)

B_{n}(z,w)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(z)\overline{\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(w)}

and use the notation
S_{n}(z):=\sqrt{B_{n}(z,z)}=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(z)|^{2}}
.

With this definition, we prove our main theorem. Note that except
Step 1
and
Step 2
, we closely follow the proof of Theorem 4.1 in
[
BL15
]
. The main technical difference comes from the fact that in
[
BL15
]
, the sum defining (
1.1
) is a finite sum, and hence Cauchy-Schwartz inequality can be applied to get an upper bound. However, in our case, the sum has infinite summands and
(a_{j}^{(n)})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is not
l^{2}
almost surely. Hence, we use a different method to get an upper bound, which is inspired by the proof of Lemma
3
. Namely, we consider
f_{n}
as a
L^{2}(B(z))
-valued random vector for some ball
B(z)
compactly contained in
\Omega
. The last part of Lemma
2
provides an explicit formula for the variance of this random vector, which is readily controlled by
S_{n}
, which in turn is controlled by Proposition
2
. In this regard, the independence assumption on the coefficients is crucial.

Theorem 2
.

Let
u
be arbitrary continuous psh function on
\Omega
and let
(f_{n})_{n}
be sequence of GAF’s defined in Definiton
1
. Then

(4.3)

(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|)^{*}=u(z)\;\;for\,all\;z\in\Omega

and

(4.4)

\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|\rightarrow u\;\;in\;L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)\;\;as\;n\rightarrow\infty

hold almost surely.

Proof.

Step 1.—
First we prove that for any
\varepsilon>0
and
z\in\Omega
, there exists
0<r<d(z,\partial\Omega)
such that

(4.5)

\frac{|f_{n}(\zeta)|}{S_{n}(\zeta)}\leq e^{\varepsilon n}

for all but finitely many
n
on
B(z,r)=\{|\zeta-z|<r\}
almost surely. By Borel-Cantelli lemma, it is enough to prove

(4.6)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{B(z,r)}\frac{|f_{n}(\zeta)|}{S_{n}(\zeta)}>e^{\varepsilon n}\right)<\infty

for some
r>0
. Using continuity of
u
, let
0<r_{2}<d(z,\partial\Omega)
so that

(4.7)

\text{osc}_{B(z,r_{2})}u=\sup_{B(z,r_{2})}u-\inf_{B(z,r_{2})}u<\varepsilon.

Then take
r,r_{1}
with
0<r<r_{1}<r_{2}
.
By Theorem
1
, we have

(4.8)

\sup_{B(z,r)}\frac{|f_{n}|}{S_{n}}\leq\frac{\sup_{B(z,r)}|f_{n}|}{\inf_{B(z,r)}S_{n}}\leq\frac{C_{2}||f_{n}||_{L^{2}(B(z,r_{1}))}}{\inf_{B(z,r)}S_{n}}.

By Chebyshev’s inequality and Lemma
2
,

(4.9)

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{||f_{n}||_{L^{2}(B(z,r_{1})}}{\inf_{B(z,r)}S_{n}}>e^{\varepsilon n}\right)\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}||f_{n}||^{2}_{L^{2}(B(z,r_{1}))}}{e^{2\varepsilon n}(\inf_{B(z,r)}S_{n})^{2}}=\frac{\sum_{j}\mathbb{E}||a_{j}^{(n)}\sigma_{j}^{(n)}||^{2}_{L^{2}(B(z,r_{1}))}}{e^{2\varepsilon n}(\inf_{B(z,r)}S_{n})^{2}}\\
=\frac{\sum_{j}||\sigma_{j}^{(n)}||^{2}_{L^{2}(B(z,r_{1}))}}{e^{2\varepsilon n}(\inf_{B(z,r)}S_{n})^{2}}\leq\frac{\lambda(B(z,r_{1}))\sup_{B(z,r_{1})}\sum_{j}|\sigma_{j}^{(n)}|^{2}}{e^{2\varepsilon n}(\inf_{B(z,r)}S_{n})^{2}}\\
=\frac{\lambda(B(z,r_{1}))\sup_{B(z,r_{1})}S_{n}^{2}}{e^{2\varepsilon n}(\inf_{B(z,r)}S_{n})^{2}}.\end{gathered}

Inequality (
3.2
) implies

(4.10)

e^{2n\inf_{B(z,r_{1})}u-L^{\prime}_{1}}\leq e^{2nu(\zeta)-L^{\prime}_{1}}\leq S_{n}^{2}(\zeta)\leq e^{2n\sup_{B(z,r_{2})}u+L^{\prime}_{2}}

on
B(z,r_{1})
for some constants
L^{\prime}_{1},L^{\prime}_{2}
independent of
\zeta
and
n
. Thus we have

(4.11)

\frac{\sup_{B(z,r_{1})}S_{n}^{2}}{e^{2\varepsilon n}(\inf_{B(z,r)}S_{n})^{2}}\leq e^{2n(\sup_{B(z,r_{2})}u-\inf_{B(z,r_{1})}u-\varepsilon)+L}\leq e^{-ln+L}

for some constants
l>0
and
L
. Since
\sum_{n}e^{-ln}<\infty
for
l>0
, this proves (
4.6
) and hence the claim. Note we have shown that

(4.12)

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{B(z,r)}\frac{|f_{n}|}{S_{n}}>e^{\varepsilon n}\right)\leq Ce^{-Dn}

for some
C,D>0
. This estimate will be used in Theorem
3
.

Step 2.—
Let
(K_{m})_{m=1}^{\infty}
be compact subsets of
\Omega
with
\Omega=\bigcup_{m}K_{m}
. Fix
K_{m}
and
\varepsilon>0
. For each
z\in K_{m}
, there exist
0<r_{z}<d(z,\partial\Omega)
and a random number
N_{z}<\infty
such that
\frac{|f_{n}(\zeta)|}{S_{n}(\zeta)}\leq e^{\varepsilon n}
on
B(z,r_{z})
for
n>N_{z}
almost surely, by
Step 1
. Since
K_{m}
is compact, there exist finitely many points
z_{1},...z_{M}
in
K_{m}
such that

K_{m}\subset\bigcup_{i}B(z_{i},r_{z_{i}})
. Then for
n>\max_{i}{N_{z_{i}}}
,
\frac{|f_{n}(\zeta)|}{S_{n}(\zeta)}\leq e^{\varepsilon n}
on
K_{m}
almost surely, and hence

(4.13)

\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}+\varepsilon

on
K_{m}
for
n>\max_{i}{N_{z_{i}}}
almost surely. Combined with inequality (
3.2
), this implies
(\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}|)_{n}
is almost surely locally uniformly bounded from above. Also by taking the
\limsup
, we get

(4.14)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}|\leq u+\varepsilon

on
K_{m}
almost surely. By letting
\varepsilon=q^{-1},q=1,2,...
and taking intersection of countably many events corresponding to each

, we get

(4.15)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}|\leq u

on
K_{m}
almost surely. Again taking intersection of countably many events corresponding to each
K_{m}
, we conclude that the inequality (
4.15
) holds on
\Omega
almost surely.

Step 3.—
Let
(z_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
be countable dense subset of
\Omega
. For each
z_{i}
, Lemma
1
implies

(4.16)

\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{|f_{n}(z_{i})|}{S_{n}(z_{i})}<\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{|\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}a_{j}^{(n)}\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(z_{i})|}{\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|\sigma_{j}^{(n)}(z_{i})|^{2}}}<\frac{1}{n^{2}}\right)<\frac{C}{n^{2}}.

Note that
S_{n}(z_{i})>0
by (
4.10
).
Since
\sum_{n}1/n^{2}<\infty
, by Borel-Cantelli lemma,

(4.17)

\frac{|f_{n}(z_{i})|}{S_{n}(z_{i})}\geq\frac{1}{n^{2}}

for all but finitely many
n
almost surely. Thus we get

(4.18)

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z_{i})|\geq u(z_{i})

almost surely. Taking intersection of countably many events corresponding to each
z_{i}
, we prove that (
4.18
) holds for all
z_{i}
almost surely.

Step 4.—
Here we prove (
4.3
). In what follows every statement is understood in almost sure sense. Define

(4.19)

F(z):=\left(\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|\right)^{*}.

By (
4.15
),

(4.20)

F\leq u^{*}=u\;\,on\;\Omega.

Also for each
z_{i}
,

(4.21)

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z_{i})|\geq u(z_{i})

by (
4.18
). Since
u
is continuous and
F
is upper semi-continuous, for any
z\in\Omega
we have

(4.22)

u(z)=\lim_{z_{i}\rightarrow z}u(z_{i})\leq\lim_{z_{i}\rightarrow z}F(z_{i})\leq F(z).

This proves (
4.3
). In fact we proved

(4.23)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z_{i})|=u(z_{i})

for all
z_{i}
.

Step 5.—
Lastly we prove (
4.4
). For convenience we collect what we have proved so far.

(4.24)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}|\;\text{is locally uniformly bounded from above ;}

(4.25)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|\leq u(z)\quad\forall z\in\Omega\,;

(4.26)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z_{i})|=u(z_{i})\quad\forall z_{i}.

By (
4.24
) the sequence
\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|
is compact in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
. By (
4.25
) and (
4.26
), every subsequential
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
limit is
\leq u
with equality on a dense subset
(z_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
. By the same argument as in (
4.22
), all limits are equal to
u
. Hence
\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|
converges to
u
in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
. This argument is due to Hörmander (
[
Hör07
]
, proof of Theorem 4.2.13).
∎

As a corollary we prove a well-known fact that any psh function
u
on
\Omega
can be approximated by functions of the form
N^{-1}\log|f|
with
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
.

Corollary 1

(
[
Hör07
, Theorem 4.2.13]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be a bounded pseudoconvex domain. Then
\{m^{-1}\log|f|:f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega),m\in\mathbb{N}\}
is dense in
PSH(\Omega)
equipped with
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
topology.

Proof.

We need to show that for arbitrary
u\in PSH(\Omega)
, there exists a sequence of
h_{n}\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
and
m_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
m_{n}^{-1}\log|h_{n}|
converges to
u
in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
. Since continuous psh functions are dense in
PSH(\Omega)
in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
-topology, we can assume
u
is continuous without loss of generality. Then Theorem
2
provides such sequence almost surely.
∎

When
N=1
and
\Omega
is simply connected, one can give a more elementary proof by approximating the measure
\frac{m}{2\pi}\Delta u
by integer point masses. See remark after Theorem 4.2.13 in
[
Hör07
]
.

Now we estimate the tail probability of random variables induced by classical linear functionals on
PSH(\Omega)
such as
M_{v}(z,r):=\fint_{B(z,r)}v\,d\lambda=\lambda(B(z,r))^{-1}\int_{B(z,r)}v\,d\lambda
,
C_{v}(z,r):=\fint_{\partial B(z,r)}v\,d\sigma=\sigma(\partial B(z,r))^{-1}\int_{\partial B(z,r)}v\,d\sigma
and
v_{\varepsilon}(z):=v*\rho_{\varepsilon}(z)
where
\rho_{\varepsilon}
is a standard mollifier. For simplicity we only prove the following theorem with
M_{v}(z,r)
, but it is clear that the same argument can be applied to other functionals. It is a straightforward consequence of Proposition
2
, subaveraging, and the proof of
step 1
of Theorem
2
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
u
and
(f_{n})_{n}
as in Theorem
2
. Given
z\in\Omega
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exist a constant
0<R<d(z,\partial\Omega)
such that for all
0<r<R
,

(4.27)

\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}|\,d\lambda-\fint_{B(z,r)}u\,d\lambda\right|>\varepsilon\right)\leq Ce^{-Dn}

holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
with some constants
C,D>0
.

Proof.

We only need to prove (
4.27
) for large enough
n
. By Proposition
2
we can let
n
large enough so that

(4.28)

\left|\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}\,d\lambda-\fint_{B(z,r)}u\,d\lambda\right|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

for a given
r>0
which will be specified later. Then

(4.29)

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}|-u\,d\lambda\right|>\varepsilon\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}|-\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}\,d\lambda\right|>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\\
\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{|f_{n}|}{S_{n}}\,d\lambda>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)+\,\mathbb{P}\left(\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{|f_{n}|}{S_{n}}\,d\lambda<-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right).\end{gathered}

We estimate each summand in (
4.29
) separately. For the first summand, (
4.12
) shows that there exists
0<R_{1}<d(z,\partial\Omega)
such that for
0<r<R_{1}
,

(4.30)

\begin{gathered}\mathbb{P}\left(\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{|f_{n}|}{S_{n}}\,d\lambda>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log\frac{|f_{n}|}{S_{n}}>\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\\
=\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{B(z,r)}\frac{|f_{n}|}{S_{n}}>e^{{\varepsilon n}/2}\right)\leq C_{1}e^{-D_{1}n}\end{gathered}

holds with some constants
C_{1},D_{1}>0
.

Now we move on to the second summand. Let
\delta>0
and
0<R_{2}<d(z,\partial\Omega)
so that
\varepsilon^{\prime}:=\varepsilon/2-\delta>0
and

(4.31)

\left|u(z)-\fint_{B(z,r)}u\,d\lambda\right|<\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{3}

for
0<r<R_{2}
.
Since
\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}
converges to
u
pointwisely and in
L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
by Proposition
2
, for each
0<r<R_{2}
we can let
n
large enough so that

(4.32)

\left|\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}(z)-u(z)\right|,\left|\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}(z)-u(z)\,d\lambda\right|<\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{3}

and hence

(4.33)

\left|\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}(z)-\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}\,d\lambda\right|<\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be the events defined by

(4.34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle:=\{\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|-\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}\,d\lambda<-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\}\,;

(4.35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle:=\{\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}(z)|>\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}(z)-\delta\}.

Then by submean value inequality, we have

(4.36)

\mathbb{P}\left(\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log|f_{n}|-\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}\,d\lambda<-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\leq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A})\leq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})+\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}^{c}).

For large enough
n
, (
4.33
) then implies

(4.37)

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B})\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}(z)-\fint_{B(z,r)}\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}\,d\lambda<\delta-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)=0.

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}^{c})
can be bounded in a similar way to Lemma
1
:

(4.38)

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{B}^{c})=\mathbb{P}\left(\frac{|f_{n}(z)|}{S_{n}(z)}\leq e^{-\delta n}\right)\leq C_{2}e^{-D_{2}n}

for some constants
C_{2},D_{2}>0
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark
.

The proof shows that the dependence of
C,D
on
0<r<R
only comes from finitely many small
n
which may differ for each
r
. Hence Theorem
3
holds asymptotically with the same
C,D
for all
0<r<R
in a
n\rightarrow\infty
limit.

5.
Application in Zeros

The exact formula for expected zero current for
k
independent Gaussian random holomorphic sections of line bundle is well known, and we only sketch the argument here for the reader’s convenience. See
[
SZ08
]
and
[
BS07
]
for rigorous derivations of the followings.

Let
f^{1}_{n},\cdots,f^{k}_{n}
be
k
independent copy of Gaussian analytic function defined by Definition
1
. For simplicity we suppress the lower index
n
. Define
Z_{f^{1},\cdots,f^{k}}
to be the current of integration over the zero set
\{z\in\Omega:f^{1}(z)=\cdots=f^{k}(z)=0\}
. By Bertini’s theorem, individual zero sets
Z_{f^{j}}
intersect each other transversely and
\{z\in\Omega:f^{1}(z)=\cdots=f^{k}(z)=0\}
is a complex submanifold of codimension
k
almost surely in the probability space of
k
-tuples of random multivariate GAF’s.

By Poincaré-Lelong formula, we have

(5.1)

Z_{f^{j}}=dd^{c}\log|f^{j}|.

Expected zero current
\mathbb{E}Z_{f_{j}}
then computed as follows. For
(N-1,N-1)
-test form
\chi
,

(5.2)

\begin{gathered}\langle\mathbb{E}Z_{f_{j}},\chi\rangle=\mathbb{E}\langle Z_{f_{j}},\chi\rangle=\mathbb{E}\langle dd^{c}\log|f^{j}|,\chi\rangle=\mathbb{E}\langle\log|f^{j}|,dd^{c}\chi\rangle\\
=\mathbb{E}\langle\log\frac{|f^{j}|}{S_{n}}+\log S_{n},dd^{c}\chi\rangle=\mathbb{E}\langle\log\frac{|f^{j}|}{S_{n}},dd^{c}\chi\rangle+\mathbb{E}\langle\log S_{n},dd^{c}\chi\rangle\\
=\langle\log S_{n},dd^{c}\chi\rangle=\langle dd^{c}\log S_{n},\chi\rangle.\end{gathered}

That is,
\mathbb{E}Z_{f^{j}}=dd^{c}\log S_{n}
. Note that the
\mathbb{E}\langle\log\frac{|f^{j}|}{S_{n}},dd^{c}\chi\rangle
term vanishes since
\frac{|f^{j}(z)|}{S_{n}(z)}
is standard complex Gaussian for all
z\in\Omega
by (
2.2
), hence its expectation is some constant independent of
z
. Various interchange of the order of integration can be justified. Formula for expected
k
simultaneous zero current
\mathbb{E}Z_{f^{1},\cdots,f^{k}}
follows from the fact that :

(5.3)

\mathbb{E}Z_{f^{1},\cdots,f^{k}}=\mathbb{E}Z_{f^{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathbb{E}Z_{f^{k}}=(\mathbb{E}Z_{f_{n}})^{k}.

This formula appears in Proposition 2.2 in
[
SZ08
]
. Heuristically, expectation splits since
Z_{f^{j}}
are independent. We collect the results in the following theorem.

Theorem 4
.

Let
Z_{f^{1},\cdots,f^{k}}
as defined above. Then we have

(5.4)

\frac{1}{n}Z_{f_{n}}\rightarrow dd^{c}u

almost surely. Also for
k=1,...,N
,

(5.5)

\frac{1}{n^{k}}\mathbb{E}Z_{f^{1},\cdots,f^{k}}=\left(dd^{c}\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}\right)^{k}\rightarrow(dd^{c}u)^{k}

in the sense of current.

Proof.

First claim follows from Theorem
2
and Poincaré-Lelong formula. For the second claim, we already showed the first identity. Since we assumed
u
to be continuous, (
3.2
) implies that
\frac{1}{n}\log S_{n}
converges to
u
locally uniformly. The conclusion follows from the continuity of the Bedford-Taylor product under locally uniform convergence of potentials.
∎

In the case of complex dimension
N=1
,
\frac{1}{n}Z_{f_{n}}
are (normalized) point processes converging to
\frac{1}{2\pi}\Delta u
in the sense of distributions. A statement about expectation is a direct consequence of Theorem 1 in
[
Sod00
]
and Proposition
2
.

It would be interesting to know whether we can replace
\mathbb{E}Z_{f^{1},\cdots,f^{k}}
by
Z_{f^{1},\cdots,f^{k}}
in (
5.5
) for
k>1
.

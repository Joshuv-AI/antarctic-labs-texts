Abstract
. We consider deterministic mean field games where the dynamics of a typical agent is non-linear with respect to the state variable and affine with respect to the control variable. Particular instances of the problem considered here are mean field games with control on the acceleration (see [ 5 , 15 , 19 ] ). We focus our attention on the approximation of such mean field games by analogous problems in discrete time and finite state space which fall in the framework of [ 32 ] . For these approximations, we show the existence and, under an additional monotonicity assumption, uniqueness of solutions. In our main result, we establish the convergence of equilibria of the discrete mean field games problems towards equilibria of the continuous one. Finally, we provide some numerical results for two MFG problems. In the first one, the dynamics of a typical player is nonlinear with respect to the state and, in the second one, a typical player controls its acceleration.

In the case of time-dependent MFGs, the articles

model equilibria of the game with a continuum of agents by a system of two Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) having the form

(1.1)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\partial_{t}v-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta v+H(t,x,\nabla_{x}v)&=&f(x,m(t))\quad\;\text{for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[4.0pt]
v(T,x)&=&g(x,m(T))\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[4.0pt]
\partial_{t}m-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\Delta m-\text{div}\left(\partial_{p}H(t,x,\nabla_{x}v)m\right)&=&0\quad\text{for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[4.0pt]
m(0)&=&m_{0}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}

where

\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,

(1.2)

H(t,x,p)=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{R}^{r}}\left\{-\langle p,b(t,a,x)\rangle-\ell_{0}(t,a,x)\right\}\quad\text{for all }(t,x,p)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

with
b\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

In this work we focus our attention on the first order case

The numerical approximation of the MFG system with non-local couplings (

In this work, we consider the approximation of deterministic MFGs where the dynamics of a typical agent is non-linear with respect to the state variable and affine with respect to the control variable, i.e. where
b
takes the form

(1.4)

b(t,a,x)=A(t,x)+B(t,x)a\quad\text{for all }(t,a,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

for some given functions
A\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
B\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}
. In particular, the dynamics that we consider covers the case of MFGs with control on the acceleration studied, at the continuous level, in
[
5
,
15
]
. We take the viewpoint of
[
37
]
, and the resulting discretization is an instance of the discrete MFG problem introduced in
[
32
]
. The key point is to observe that, under our assumptions, if the support of the initial distribution
m_{0}
of the agents is compact, then the set of optimal trajectories, and hence the support of any Lagrangian equilibrium, will be contained on a fixed compact set during the whole time interval
[0,T]
. Based on this a priori information on optimal trajectories, we construct a suitable discretization of the optimal control problem solved by a typical player by considering a finite time-space grid contained on a compact subset of
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the latter being independent of the discretization steps. Moreover, the proposed approximation takes advantage of the particular form of the dynamics in (
1.4
) and allows, at the fully-discrete level, to write the set of admissible feedback controls in terms of the state variables. The resulting scheme to approximate optimal control problems with control-affine dynamics seems to be new and, compared with standard SL schemes (see e.g.
[
29
]
), it reduces the complexity of computing interpolants over grids contained in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to the computation of interpolants over grids contained in
\mathbb{R}^{d-r}
. In order to obtain a discretization of the MFG problem, the previous scheme is coupled with a discretization of the FP equation which has a probabilistic interpretation in terms of a finite state Markov chain representing the evolution of a typical agent in the discrete framework. As usual in game theory, the computation of a discrete equilibrium, i.e. the solution to the fully-discrete scheme, can be written as a fixed point problem in terms of a
best response mapping
. Existence of at least one equilibrium is shown in Proposition
4.2
, while uniqueness is established in Proposition
4.1
under the standard Lasry-Lions monotonicity conditions on the couplings
f
and
g
. As in
[
37
]
, the (unique) equilibrium of the discrete MFG can be computed by using the fictitious play method provided that the aforementioned monotonicity conditions on
f
and
g
hold. Given a sequence of equilibria of the discrete MFG problems, corresponding to a sequence of discretization parameters tending to zero, we associate a sequence of probability measures on
C([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{d})
which is pre-compact for the topology of weak convergence. Our main result in Theorem
5.1
, valid in arbitrary dimensions, states that every accumulation point of this sequence is a MFG equilibrium in Lagrangian form. In particular, if system (
1.1
) is well-posed, then its unique solution is the limit of solutions to our scheme. Let us emphasize that in the particular case where
b

is given by (
1.3
), our scheme allows to approximate MFGs with cost functional that are much more general than those in
[
37
]
(see Remark
5.2
(ii) below). We illustrate our theoretical findings by considering two examples. In the first one, the problem has only one state variable,
b
is non-linear with respect to the state and affine with respect to the control. In the second example, we consider a problem with two states, position and velocity, and only one control, given by the acceleration. In both examples, the discrete MFGs problems are solved by using the fictitious play method.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. The next section introduces some basic definitions, states the MFG problem, fix our main assumptions, and provides existence and uniqueness results of a Lagrangian equilibrium. In Section
3
, we describe the approximation scheme that we propose for the optimal control problem underlying the MFG problem. We first derive some standard but important properties of a semi-discrete scheme and then we present the fully-discrete scheme, which is the cornerstone of the discretization of the MFG problem. Section
4
introduces the discretization of the MFG problem, provides existence and uniqueness results of an equilibrium for the discrete MFG, and discuss its numerical solution by using the fictitious play method. In Section
5
we state and prove our main result, which shows that accumulation points of equilibria of discrete MFGs, as the discretization parameters tend to zero, are equilibria in Lagrangian form of the continuous MFG problem. Two numerical tests that support our theoretical findings are presented in Section
6

3
and the proof of the uniqueness result stated in Section
2.1
.

3.
Approximation of the value function associated with an optimal control problem

In this section, we consider the approximation of a family of optimal control problems, depending on a fixed
m\in C\left([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\right)
. The resulting scheme, whose main properties are shown in the Appendix I of this work, will be the building block of the approximation of a MFG equilibrium that we propose in the next section.

For
\alpha\in L^{p}([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{r})
,
t\in[0,T)
, and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let us consider the equation

(3.1)

\dot{\gamma}(s)=A(s,\gamma(s))+B(s,\gamma(s))\alpha(s),\quad\text{for a.e. }s\in(t,T),\quad\gamma(t)=x.

Under assumption
(H3)
, (
3.1
) admits a unique solution. Let us define the value function
v\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(3.2)

v(t,x)=\inf\left\{\int_{t}^{T}\ell\left(s,\alpha(s),\gamma(s),m(s)\right){\rm d}s+g(\gamma(T),m(T))\,\bigg|\,\alpha\in L^{p}\left([0,T];\mathbb{R}^{r}\right),\;(\alpha,\gamma)\mbox{ satisfies \eqref{ecuacion_controlada}}\right\},

for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By
{\bf(H1)}
and
{\bf(H2)}
, the function
v
is well-defined and solves the following Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman equation in the viscosity sense (see e.g.
[
8
,
7
]
):

(3.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-\partial_{t}v(t,x)+H(t,x,\nabla v(t,x),m(t))&=&0\quad\text{for }(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\[6.0pt]
v(T,x)&=&g(x,m(T))\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

where, for all
t\in[0,T]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

H(t,x,p,\mu)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{r}}\left\{-\ell(t,\alpha,x,\mu)-\langle p,A(t,x)+B(t,x)\alpha\rangle\right\}.

Moreover,
[
26
, Theorem 2.1]
implies that
v
is the unique viscosity solution to (
3.3
).

3.1.
A semi-discrete scheme

Let
N_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
and set

(3.4)

\mathcal{I}=\{0,\ldots,N_{t}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{I}^{*}=\mathcal{I}\setminus\{N_{t}\}.

For any

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Gamma_{k,x}&=&\{\gamma=(\gamma_{k},\ldots,\gamma_{N_{t}})\;|\;(\forall\,j=k,\ldots,N_{t})\;\;\gamma_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\;\gamma_{k}=x\},\\[4.0pt]
\mathcal{A}_{k}&=&\{\alpha=(\alpha_{k},\ldots,\alpha_{N_{t}-1})\;|\;(\forall\,j=k,\ldots,N_{t}-1)\;\;\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{r}\}.\end{array}

Set

and

because it is the smallest set for which our approach allows to approximate the values
\{v(0,x)\;|\;x\in K_{0}\}
.

4.
Discretization of the MFG problem

We study in this section the existence and the uniqueness of solutions to a full-discretization of Problem
2.1
. At the end of this section, we also discuss the convergence of the
fictitious play
method
[
37
]
towards the solution of the discrete problem.

The definition of MFG equilibrium involves an optimal control problem solved by a “typical” player. Thus, in order to find a discrete version of Problem
2.1
, amenable to numerical computation, it is natural to first discretize the aforementioned optimal control problem. For this purpose, we will consider a variation of (
3.27
) with an additional entropy term that ensures the existence of a unique minimizer.

Let
N_{t}\in\mathbb{N}
,
N_{s}\in\mathbb{N}
, and
(\Delta t,\Delta x)\in\widehat{\Delta}
(see (
3.23
)). Recall that for any
(k,x)\in\mathcal{I}^{*}\times\mathcal{G}
, the sets
\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)
,
\mathcal{S}^{2}_{k+1}(x,\mathrm{y}_{1})
, for
\mathrm{y}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)
, and
\mathcal{S}_{k+1}(x)
are defined in (
3.21
). Let us define the family of sets
\{\mathcal{S}_{k}\,|\,k\in\mathcal{I}\}
by (
3.24
) with
K_{0}=\mathop{\rm supp}(m_{0})
. In order to discretize the initial distribution
m_{0}
, let us set

E(x)=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\;|\;|x-y|\leq\Delta x/2\right\}\quad\mbox{for}\;x\in\mathcal{G}.

By modifying
\Delta x
, if necessary, we can assume that
m_{0}(\partial E(x))=0
for all
x\in\mathcal{G}
. For a finite set
F
, let us define the (nonpositive) entropy function
\mathcal{E}_{F}\colon\mathcal{P}(F)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{E}_{F}(p)=\sum_{x\in F}p(x)\log(p(x))\quad\text{with the convention that $p(x)=p(\{x\})$ and $0\log(0)=0$}.

In what follows, we identify every
p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}_{k})
(
k\in\mathcal{I}
) with the probability measure
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}p(x)\delta_{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let
\varepsilon>0
, set
\mathcal{M}=\prod_{k\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}_{k})
and, for
M=(M_{k})_{k\in\mathcal{I}}\in\mathcal{M}
, define
\{V_{k}^{M}\,|\,k\in\mathcal{I}\}
by

(4.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}V_{k}^{M}(x)=\min\limits_{p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x))}\bigg\{\sum\limits_{\mathrm{y}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{1}(x)}p(\mathrm{y}_{1})\bigg[\Delta t\ell(t_{k},\alpha(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}),x,M_{k})\\[6.0pt]
\hskip 91.04872pt+I_{\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{2}(x,\mathrm{y}_{1})}[V_{k+1}^{M}(\mathrm{y}_{1},\cdot)](\mathbf{y_{2}}(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}))\bigg]+\varepsilon\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)}(p)\bigg\}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathcal{I}^{*},\,x\in\mathcal{S}_{k},\\[8.0pt]
V_{N_{t}}^{M}(x)=g(x,M_{N_{t}})\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{t}},\end{array}

where we recall that
\alpha(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1})
is defined in (
3.20
) for every
(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1})\in\mathcal{I}^{*}\times\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{G}_{r}
. For
k\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
,
x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
, and
y\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}
, we also set

(4.2)

P_{k}^{M}(x,y):=\begin{cases}p_{k}^{M}(x,\mathrm{y}_{1})\beta_{\mathrm{y}_{2}}(\mathbf{y_{2}}(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}))&\mbox{if $y\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}(x)$},\\[2.84526pt]
0&\mbox{if $y\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{k+1}(x)$},\end{cases}

where

(4.3)

\begin{array}[]{l}p_{k}^{M}(x,\cdot)\in\mathop{\rm argmin}\limits_{p\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{1}(x))}\bigg\{\sum\limits_{\mathrm{y}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{1}(x)}p(\mathrm{y}_{1})\bigg[\Delta t\ell(t_{k},\alpha(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}),x,M_{k})\\[10.0pt]
\hskip 170.71652pt+I_{\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{2}(x,\mathrm{y}_{1})}[V_{k+1}^{M}(\mathrm{y}_{1},\cdot)](\mathbf{y_{2}}(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}))\bigg]+\varepsilon\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)}(p)\bigg\}.\end{array}

Notice that, for every
k\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
,
x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
, we have
P_{k}^{M}(x,\cdot)\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}_{k+1})
and
\mathop{\rm supp}(P_{k}^{M}(x,\cdot))=\mathcal{S}_{k+1}(x)
. Finally, define the
best response mapping

{\bf br}\colon\mathcal{M}\ni M\mapsto{\bf br}(M)=\widehat{M}\in\mathcal{M}
, where
\widehat{M}
is given by

(4.4)

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\widehat{M}_{k+1}(y)&=&\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}P_{k}^{M}(x,y)\widehat{M}_{k}(x)\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathcal{I}^{*},\,y\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1},\\[13.0pt]
\hskip 9.67383pt\widehat{M}_{0}(x)&=&m_{0}(E(x))\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}.\end{array}

Remark 4.1
.

Notice that the presence of the entropy term in the right-hand side of (
4.3
) ensures that
p_{k}^{M}(x,\cdot)
is uniquely defined for all
k\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
,
x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
, and
M\in\mathcal{M}
, and
p_{k}^{M}(x,\mathrm{y}_{1})>0
for all
\mathrm{y}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{1}(x)
. Consequently, for every
\mathrm{y}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{1}(x)
, the application
\mathcal{M}\ni M\mapsto p_{k}^{M}(x,\mathrm{y}_{1})\in[0,1]
is continuous. Therefore, the best response mapping
{\bf br}
is well-defined and continuous.

Moreover, given
M\in\mathcal{M}
, setting
\widehat{M}={\bf br}(M)
, (
4.4
) and (
4.2
) imply

\widehat{M}_{k}(x)>0\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathcal{I},\,x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}.

The discretization of Problem
2.1
that we consider in this work is the following.

Problem 4.1
.

Find
M\in\mathcal{M}
such that
M={\bf br}(M)
.

The following proposition deals with the existence of solutions to Problem
4.1
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Problem
4.1
admits at least one solution
M^{*}\in\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is non-empty, convex and compact, and
{\bf br}
is continuous (see Remark
4.1
), the result follows from Brouwer’s fixed point theorem.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Since
\mathop{\rm supp}(m_{0})
is compact, (
3.25
) implies the existence of
C_{\infty}>0
, independent of
(\Delta t,\Delta x)\in\widehat{\Delta}
and
\varepsilon>0
, such that
\mathcal{S}_{k}\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{B}}(0,C_{\infty})
for all
k\in\mathcal{I}
. In particular, for any solution
M^{*}\in\mathcal{M}
to Problem
4.1
, the support of
M_{k}^{*}

(
k\in\mathcal{I}
)
is contained in
\overline{\mathrm{B}}(0,C_{\infty})
.

We now discuss the uniqueness of solutions to Problem
4.1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under
(H5)
, Problem
4.1
admits a unique solution.

Proof.

Suppose that
M
and
\bar{M}
solve Problem
4.1
. By (
4.1
) we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}V_{0}^{M}(x)&\leq&\Delta t\sum\limits_{\mathrm{y}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{1}(x)}p^{\bar{M}}_{0}(x,\mathrm{y}_{1})\big[\ell(t_{0},\alpha(0,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}),x,M_{0})+I_{\mathcal{S}^{2}_{1}(x,\mathrm{y}_{1})}[V_{1}^{M}(\mathrm{y}_{1},\cdot)](\mathbf{y_{2}}(0,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}))\big]\\[12.0pt]
&&\quad\quad+\varepsilon\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)}(p_{0}^{\bar{M}}(x,\cdot))\\[6.0pt]
&=&\Delta t\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{1}(x)}P^{\bar{M}}_{0}(x,y)\left(\ell(t_{0},\alpha(0,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}),x,M_{0})+V_{1}^{M}(y)\right)+\varepsilon\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)}(p_{0}^{\bar{M}}(x,\cdot)).\end{array}

By multiplying by
\bar{M}_{0}(x)
and summing over
x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}
, (
3.24
), (
4.2
), and (
4.4
) yield

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}\bar{M}_{0}(x)V_{0}^{M}(x)&\leq&\Delta t\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\bar{M}_{0}(x)P^{\bar{M}}_{0}(x,y)\ell(t_{0},\alpha(0,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}),x,M_{0})+\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\bar{M}_{1}(y)V_{1}^{M}(y)\\[12.0pt]
&&\quad+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}\bar{M}_{0}(x)\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)}(p_{0}^{\bar{M}}(x,\cdot)).\end{array}

Applying the same argument to
V_{1}^{M}(y)
and so forth, we obtain

(4.5)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}\bar{M}_{0}(x)V_{0}^{M}(x)&\leq&\Delta t\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}}\bar{M}_{k}(x)P_{k}^{\bar{M}}(x,y)\ell(t_{k},\alpha(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}),x,M_{k})\\[8.0pt]
&&+\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{t}}}\bar{M}_{N_{t}}(y)V^{M}_{N_{t}}(y)+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\bar{M}_{k}(x)\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)}(p_{k}^{\bar{M}}(x,\cdot)).\end{array}

Notice that Remark
4.1
implies that the inequality above is an equality if and only if
\bar{M}=M
. Thus, it holds that

(4.6)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}M_{0}(x)V_{0}^{M}(x)&=&\Delta t\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}}M_{k}(x)P_{k}^{M}(x,y)\ell(t_{k},\alpha(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}),x,M_{k})\\[8.0pt]
&&+\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{t}}}M_{N_{t}}(y)V^{M}_{N_{t}}(y)+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}M_{k}(x)\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)}(p_{k}^{M}(x,\cdot)).\end{array}

Now, let us suppose that
M\neq\bar{M}
. By interchanging the roles of
M
and
\bar{M}
in (
4.5
), we obtain

(4.7)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}M_{0}(x)V_{0}^{\bar{M}}(x)&<&\Delta t\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}}M_{k}(x)P_{k}^{M}(x,y)\ell(t_{k},\alpha(k,x,\mathrm{y}_{1}),x,{\bar{M}}_{k})\\[8.0pt]
&&+\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{t}}}M_{N_{t}}(y)V^{\bar{M}}_{N_{t}}(y)+\varepsilon\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}M_{k}(x)\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k+1}(x)}(p_{k}^{M}(x,\cdot)).\end{array}

By
(H5)
(i), (
4.6
), and (
4.7
), we get

(4.8)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}M_{0}(x)\left(V_{0}^{\bar{M}}(x)-V_{0}^{M}(x)\right)&<&\Delta t\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}M_{k}(x)\left(f(x,{\bar{M}}_{k})-f(x,M_{k}(x))\right)\\[8.0pt]
&&\quad+\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{t}}}M_{N_{t}}(y)\left(V^{\bar{M}}_{N_{t}}(y)-V_{N_{t}}^{M}(y)\right).\end{array}

Similarly, recalling that
M_{0}=\bar{M}_{0}
, we have that

(4.9)

\begin{array}[]{lll}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{0}}M_{0}(x)\left(V_{0}^{M}(x)-V_{0}^{\bar{M}}(x)\right)&<&\Delta t\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\bar{M}_{k}(x)\left(f(x,M_{k})-f(x,\bar{M}_{k}(x))\right)\\[8.0pt]
&&\quad+\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{t}}}\bar{M}_{N_{t}}(y)\left(V^{M}_{N_{t}}(y)-V_{N_{t}}^{\bar{M}}(y)\right).\end{array}

Noting that
V^{M}_{N_{t}}(y)=g(y,M_{N_{t}})
and
V^{\bar{M}}_{N_{t}}(y)=g(y,\bar{M}_{N_{t}})
, by adding (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{rcl}0&<&\Delta t\sum\limits_{k=0}^{N_{t}-1}\sum\limits_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\left(M_{k}(x)-\bar{M}_{k}(x)\right)\left(f(x,{\bar{M}}_{k})-f(x,M_{k}(x))\right)\\[8.0pt]
&&+\sum\limits_{y\in\mathcal{S}_{N_{t}}}\left(M_{N_{t}}(y)-\bar{M}_{N_{t}}(y)\right)\left(g(y,{\bar{M}}_{N_{t}})-g(y,M_{N_{t}})\right),\end{array}

which contradicts
(H5)
(ii).
∎

Besides the uniqueness of solutions to Problem
4.1
, the particular structure of
\ell
and the monotonicity assumptions on
f
and
g
in
(H5)
are important in order to approximate the unique solution
M^{*}
to Problem
4.1
. Indeed, if
(H5)
holds and
f
and
g
satisfy

(4.10)

\displaystyle|f(t,x,\mu_{1})-f(t,x,\mu_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{f}d_{1}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,T],\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\mu_{1},\,\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(4.11)

\displaystyle|g(x,\mu_{1})-g(x,\mu_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{g}d_{1}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\mu_{1},\,\mu_{2}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and we consider the
fictitious play
sequence

\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{0}\in\mathcal{M}\;\;\text{arbitrary},\;\quad(\forall\,n\geq 1)\quad\mathsf{M}^{n+1}={\bf br}(\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n}),\quad\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n+1}=\frac{n}{n+1}\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n}+\frac{1}{n+1}\mathsf{M}^{n+1},

then
[
37
, Theorem 3.2]
implies that

(\mathsf{M}^{n},\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n})\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}(M^{*},M^{*}).

5.
Convergence of solutions to the finite MFG problem towards a solution to Problem
2.1

Let
(N_{t}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
,
(N_{s}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
,
(\varepsilon_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
, and, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, set
\Delta t_{n}=T/N_{t}^{n}
,
\Delta x_{n}=1/N_{s}^{n}
,
\mathcal{I}^{n}=\{0,\ldots,N_{t}^{n}\}
,
\mathcal{I}^{n,*}:=\mathcal{I}^{n}\setminus\{N_{t}^{n}\}
,

, and
\mathcal{G}^{n}=\{i\Delta x_{n}\,|\,i\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\}
. We assume that
(\Delta t_{n},\Delta x_{n})\in\widehat{\Delta}
, where we recall that
\widehat{\Delta}
is defined in (
3.23
). For
k\in\mathcal{I}^{n,*}
and
x\in\mathcal{G}^{n}
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{1,n}(x)
,
\mathcal{S}^{2,n}_{k+1}(x,\mathrm{y}_{1})
, for
\mathrm{y}_{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{1,n}_{k+1}(x)
, and
\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{n}(x)
the sets defined in (
3.21
) associated with
\Delta t_{n}
and
\Delta x_{n}
. For
k\in\mathcal{I}^{n}
, the set
\mathcal{S}^{n}_{k}
is defined as in (
3.24
). Recall that Proposition
4.1
ensures the existence of at least one solution
M^{n}\in\mathcal{M}^{n}:=\prod_{k\in\mathcal{I}^{n}}\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}^{n}_{k})
to Problem
4.1
associated with the previous parameters. Denote by
\Gamma^{n}
the set of continuous functions
\gamma\colon[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{d}

\gamma(t^{n}_{k})\in\mathcal{S}_{k}^{n}

M^{n}_{k}=e_{t^{n}_{k}}\sharp\xi^{n}

below) that

. Finally, we denote by

4.1
) with

.

Remark 5.1
.

(i)
Notice that (
4.2
) implies that
\gamma\in\mathop{\rm supp}(\xi^{n})
if and only if for every
k\in\mathcal{I}^{n,*}
, we have
\gamma(t_{k}^{n})\in\mathcal{S}_{k}^{n}
and
\gamma(t_{k+1}^{n})\in\mathcal{S}_{k+1}^{n}(\gamma(t_{k}^{n}))
. Moreover, if
\gamma\in\mathop{\rm supp}(\xi^{n})
, then
\gamma
is absolutely continuous with

(5.3)

\dot{\gamma}(t)=\frac{\gamma(t_{k+1}^{n})-\gamma(t_{k}^{n})}{\Delta t_{n}}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathcal{I}^{n,*},\,t\in(t_{k}^{n},t_{k+1}^{n}).

(ii)
By definition of
\xi^{n}
we have

. Thus, the definition of
\Gamma^{n}
and Remark

imply that

\|\gamma\|_{\infty}\leq C_{\infty}\quad\text{for all }\gamma\in\mathop{\rm supp}(\xi^{n}).

Moreover, Lemma
5.1
below shows the existence of a constant

such that

\|\dot{\gamma}\|_{\infty}\leq D_{\infty}\quad\text{for all }\gamma\in\mathop{\rm supp}(\xi^{n}).

We can now state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
(H1)-(H4)
hold and that, as
n\to\infty
,
N_{t}^{n}\to\infty
,
N^{n}_{s}\to\infty
,
N^{n}_{t}/N^{n}_{s}\to 0
, and
\varepsilon_{n}=o\left(1/(N^{n}_{t}\log(N^{n}_{s}))\right)
. Then the following hold:

(i)

There exists at least one accumulation point
\xi^{*}
of
(\xi^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, with respect to the narrow topology in
\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
, and every such accumulation point solves Problem
2.1
.

(ii)

Let
(\xi^{n_{l}})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
be a subsequence of
\left(\xi^{n}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converging towards a MFG equilibrium

and set

,
\mathop{\rm supp}(m^{*}(t))\subset\overline{\mathrm{B}}(0,C_{\infty})
for all

,
M^{n_{l}}\to m^{*}
in
C([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
as

, and

(5.4)

\sup\left\{\left|V_{k}^{n_{l}}(x)-v^{*}(t_{k}^{n_{l}},x)\right|\;\big|\;k\in\mathcal{I}^{n_{l}},\;x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}^{n_{l}}\right\}\underset{l\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0,

where

is the value function (
3.2
) associated with
m^{*}
.

Remark 5.2
.

(i)
In addition to the assumptions of the previous theorem, let us assume that
(H5)
holds. If (
2.4
) holds, then Theorem
2.2
implies that the MFG equilibrium
\xi^{*}
is unique and hence the whole sequence
(\xi^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges

. In particular, we have

(5.5)

M^{n}\to m^{*}\quad\text{in }C([0,T];\mathcal{P}_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\;\;\text{as $n\to\infty$}.

(ii)
Notice that even in the particular case where the typical player controls directly its velocity, Theorem
5.1
significantly improves
[
37
, Theorem 4.1]
, where the cost function is assumed to satisfy some strong separability and regularity conditions. Indeed, for every
k\in\mathcal{I}^{*}
,

, and

, the value of

in (

) can be computed by using only the states

, which is a uniformly bounded set with respect to the discretization parameters. On the other hand, the space grid in
[

]
is unbounded with respect to the discretization parameters. This lack of compactness of the support of the scheme in
[

]
was the main issue in establishing the convergence result in
[

, Theorem 4.1]
for more general cost functions. For instance, the function

, with

being bounded from below by a strictly positive constant, Lipschitz continuous, and bounded, satisfies
(H1)
but it does not satisfy the assumptions in
[
37
]
.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
5.1

In order to prove Theorem
5.1
, we first show some preliminary results.

Lemma 5.1
.

There exists
D_{\infty}>0
, independent of

6.
Numerical results

In this section, we consider two instances of Problem
2.1
with monotone couplings. The first one deals with a one-dimensional problem and a nonlinear dynamics with respect to the space variable. In second instance, we approximate the MFG equilibrium of a two-dimensional problem in which a typical agent controls its acceleration (see e.g.
[
5
,
15
]
).

Given some discretization parameters
(\Delta t,\Delta x)\in(0,\infty)^{2}
, and
\varepsilon>0
, we approximate the solutions to the corresponding instances of Problem
4.1
by using the fictitious play algorithm recalled at the end of Section
4
. More precisely, in both examples we consider the following routine:

Data:

\mathsf{M}^{0}\in\mathcal{M}
and a tolerance parameter
\delta>0

e\leftarrow\delta+1

n\leftarrow 1

\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{1}\leftarrow\mathsf{M}^{0}

while

e>\delta

do

\mathsf{M}^{n+1}={\bf br}(\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n})

e\leftarrow|\mathsf{M}^{n+1}-\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n}|_{L^{1}}

\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n+1}=\frac{n}{n+1}\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n}+\frac{1}{n+1}\mathsf{M}^{n+1}

n\leftarrow n+1

end while

return

\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n-1}

Algorithm 1

The algorithm stops at iterate
n
if
|\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n}-{\bf br}(\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n})|_{L^{1}}\leq\delta
.

In (
1
), for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have set

|\mathsf{M}^{n+1}-\overline{\mathsf{M}}^{n}|_{L^{1}}=\frac{1}{N_{t}+1}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{t}}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}|\mathsf{M}_{k}^{n+1}(x)-\overline{\mathsf{M}}_{k}^{n}(x)|.

Inspired by the
modified fictitious play
iterates introduced in
[

returned by Algorithm
1

\delta>0

\mathsf{M}^{0}
for a subsequent application of the same algorithm with a smaller tolerance parameter. In this manner, we obtain better numerical performances, in terms of speed of convergence, than fixing a small tolerance parameter and applying Algorithm
1
only once.

Lemma 6.2
.

Given

and

,
m

k

Proof.

Given

(i), and

\delta>0

(6.8)

\Delta t\underline{\ell}\sum_{j=k}^{N_{t}-1}\left|\alpha^{n}_{j}\right|^{p}-(T-t_{k})C_{\ell}\leq J_{k,x}(0)+\delta-g(\gamma^{n}_{N_{t}})\leq(T-t_{k})C_{\ell}+\delta+L_{g}\left|\bar{\gamma}^{0}_{N_{t}}-\gamma^{n}_{N_{t}}\right|,

\begin{array}[]{lll}\left|\bar{\gamma}^{0}_{j+1}-\gamma^{n}_{j+1}\right|&\leq&\left|\bar{\gamma}^{0}_{j}-\gamma^{n}_{j}\right|+\Delta t\left|A(t_{j},\bar{\gamma}^{0}_{j})-A(t_{j},\gamma^{n}_{j})\right|+\Delta t\left|B(t_{j},\gamma^{n}_{j})\alpha^{n}_{j}\right|\\[10.0pt]

6.8
) and (
6.9

,

,
k
, and

Therefore, there exists at least one accumulation point

of

∎

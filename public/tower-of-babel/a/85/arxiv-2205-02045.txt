Duality in convex stochastic optimization
Teemu Pennanen, Ari-Pekka Perkkiö

Abstract
This paper studies duality and optimality conditions in general convex stochastic optimization problems introduced by Rockafellar and Wets in [ 28 ] . We derive an explicit dual problem in terms of two dual variables, one of which is the shadow price of information while the other one gives the marginal cost of a perturbation much like in classical Lagrangian duality. Existence of primal solutions and the absence of duality gap are obtained without compactness or boundedness assumptions. In the context of financial mathematics, the relaxed assumptions are satisfied under the well-known no-arbitrage condition and the reasonable asymptotic elasticity condition of the utility function. We extend classical portfolio optimization duality theory to problems of optimal semi-static hedging. Besides financial mathematics, we obtain several new frameworks in stochastic programming and stochastic optimal control.



Keywords.
Convex duality, stochastic programming, stochastic optimal control, financial mathematics

2
Integral functionals in duality

Convex duality is based on the theory of conjugate functions on dual pairs of locally convex topological vector spaces; see
[
27
]
. The first part of this section reviews spaces of random variables in separating duality with each other while the second part reviews conjugation of integral functionals on such spaces. This forms the functional analytic setting for the duality theory of stochastic optimization developed in the followup sections. For full generality, we make minimal assumptions on the spaces of random variables. The classical Lebesgue and Orlicz spaces,
L^{p}
and
L^{\Phi}
are covered as special cases but also many others that come up naturally e.g. in engineering and finance.

2.1
Dual spaces of random variables

Let
{\cal U}
and
{\cal Y}
be linear spaces of
\mathbbm{R}^{m}
-valued random variables in separating duality under the bilinear form

\langle u,y\rangle:=E[u\cdot y].

This means that
u\cdot y\in L^{1}
for all
u\in{\cal U}
and
y\in{\cal Y}
and that for every nonzero
u\in{\cal U}
, there exists a
y\in{\cal Y}
such that
\langle u,y\rangle\neq 0
and vice versa. As usual, we identify random variables that coincide almost surely so the elements of
{\cal U}
and
{\cal Y}
are actually equivalence classes of random variables that coincide almost surely. We will also assume that the spaces are
decomposable
and
solid
. Decomposability means that

1_{A}u+1_{\Omega\setminus A}u^{\prime}\in{\cal U}

for every
u\in{\cal U}
and
u^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}
while solidity means that if
\bar{u}\in{\cal U}
and
u\in L^{0}
are such that
|u^{i}|\leq|\bar{u}^{i}|
almost surely for every
i=1,\ldots,m
, then
u\in{\cal U}
; similarly for
{\cal Y}
. Solidity implies that

{\cal U}={\cal U}_{1}\times\cdots\times{\cal U}_{m}\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal Y}={\cal Y}_{1}\times\cdots\times{\cal Y}_{m},

where
{\cal U}_{i}
and
{\cal Y}_{i}
are solid decomposable spaces of real-valued random variables in separating duality under the bilinear form
(u_{i},y_{i})\mapsto E[u_{i}y_{i}]
. In particular,

u_{i}y_{i}\in L^{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\langle u,y\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}E[u_{i}y_{i}]\quad\forall u\in{\cal U},y\in{\cal Y}.

(1)

Given a solid space of real-valued random variables
{\cal U}_{0}
, the space
\{u\in L^{0}(\mathbbm{R}^{m})\mid|u|\in{\cal U}_{0}\}
is solid and it can be written as
{\cal U}_{0}^{m}
, the
m
-fold Cartesian product of
{\cal U}_{0}
. A solid space containing all constant functions is decomposable. The following shows that the converse does not hold.

Counterexample 1
.

Let
x\geq 1
be an unbounded real-valued random variable and
{\cal X}:=L^{\infty}+Lin(x1_{A}\mid A\in{\cal F})
. Then
{\cal X}
is decomposable, by construction, but not solid, since it does not contain
\sqrt{x}
.

Decomposable solid spaces of random variables in separating duality include Lebesgue spaces, Orlicz spaces, Marcinkiewich spaces paired with Lorentz spaces, spaces of finite moments
\|u\|_{L^{p}}
for all
p\in(1,\infty)
as well as the general class of Banach Function Spaces or, even more generally, locally convex function spaces; see
[
20
]
and its references. The spaces of continuous functions or various Sobolev spaces of functions on
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
fail to be decomposable or solid. The space
L^{0}
of all random variables is decomposable and solid but if
(\Omega,{\cal F},P)
is atomless, it cannot be paired with a nontrivial space of random variables. Indeed, if
y\in L^{0}
is nonzero, then there exists
\epsilon>0
and
A\in{\cal F}
such that
|y|1_{A}>\epsilon
and
P(A)
. Since the space is atomless, there exists
\eta<0
with
E[1_{A}\eta]=-\infty
. Choosing
u=1_{A}y\eta
, we get
E[u\cdot y]=-\infty
.

Given a topology on
{\cal U}
, the corresponding topological dual of
{\cal U}
is the linear space of all continuous linear functionals on
{\cal U}
. A topology is
compatible
with the bilinear form on
{\cal U}\times{\cal Y}
if every continuous linear functional can be expressed in the form

u\mapsto\langle u,y\rangle

for some
y\in{\cal Y}
. Such topologies can be characterized in terms of the “weak” and “Mackey” topologies associated with the bilinear form. The
weak topology

\sigma({\cal U},{\cal Y})
on
{\cal U}
is the topology generated by linear functionals
u\mapsto\langle u,y\rangle
where
y\in{\cal Y}
. Similarly for
{\cal Y}
. The
Mackey topology
is the topology generated by the sublinear functionals

\sigma_{D}(u):=\sup_{y\in D}\langle u,y\rangle,

where
D\subset{\cal Y}
is
\sigma({\cal Y},{\cal U})
-compact. Similarly for
{\cal Y}
. Given a topology on
{\cal U}
, the corresponding topological dual can be identified with
{\cal Y}
if and only if the topology is between
\sigma({\cal U},{\cal Y})
and
\tau({\cal U},{\cal Y})
. If
{\cal U}
is Fréchet (e.g. Banach) and
{\cal Y}
is its topological dual, then the
\sigma({\cal Y},{\cal U})
-compact sets are the bounded sets in
{\cal Y}
, so
\tau({\cal U},{\cal Y})
is the strong topology; see
[
10
]
.

The following is from
[
16
]
.

Lemma 2
.

We have
L^{\infty}\subseteq{\cal U}\subseteq L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}\subseteq{\cal Y}\subseteq L^{1}
and

\displaystyle\sigma(L^{1},L^{\infty})|_{\cal U}

\displaystyle\subseteq\sigma({\cal U},{\cal Y}),\quad\sigma({\cal U},{\cal Y})|_{L^{\infty}}\subseteq\sigma(L^{\infty},L^{1}),

\displaystyle\tau(L^{1},L^{\infty})|_{\cal U}

\displaystyle\subseteq\tau({\cal U},{\cal Y}),\quad\tau({\cal U},{\cal Y})|_{L^{\infty}}\subseteq\tau(L^{\infty},L^{1}).

The
L^{0}
-topology on
{\cal U}
is weaker than
\tau({\cal U},{\cal Y})
.

Given a decomposable space
{\cal U}
, its
Köthe dual
is the linear space

\{y\in L^{0}\mid u\cdot y\in L^{1}\quad\forall u\in{\cal U}\}.

This is the largest space of random variables that can be paired with
{\cal U}
with the bilinear form
(u,y)\mapsto E[u\cdot y]
. Clearly, the Köthe dual of a solid space is solid. The following is well-known, e.g., in Lebesgue and Orlicz spaces.

Lemma 3
.

Let
{\cal U}
and
{\cal Y}
be solid and
{\cal G}\subset{\cal F}
a
\sigma
-algebra such that
E^{\cal G}{\cal U}\subset{\cal U}
. The mapping
E^{\cal G}:{\cal U}\to{\cal U}
is weakly continuous if and only if
E^{\cal G}{\cal Y}\subset{\cal Y}
and in this case,

\langle E^{\cal G}u,y\rangle=\langle u,E^{\cal G}y\rangle\quad\forall u\in{\cal U},\ y\in{\cal Y}.

If
{\cal Y}
is the Köthe dual of
{\cal U}
, then
E^{\cal G}{\cal Y}\subset{\cal Y}
.

Proof.

If
u^{i}
,
y^{i}
,
(E^{\cal G}u)^{i}y^{i}
and
u^{i}(E^{\cal G}y)^{i}
are integrable, ?THM?
gives

E[E^{\cal G}u\cdot y]=E[(E^{\cal G}u)\cdot E^{\cal G}y]=E[u\cdot E^{\cal G}y].

(2)

Thus, if
E^{\cal G}{\cal U}\subset{\cal U}
and
E^{\cal G}{\cal Y}\subset{\cal Y}
, then, by (
1
), the function
u\mapsto E^{\cal G}u
is weakly continuous. On the other hand, if
E^{\cal G}:{\cal U}\to{\cal U}
is weakly continuous, then
u\mapsto E[E^{\cal G}u\cdot y]
is
\sigma({\cal U},{\cal Y})
-continuous for
y\in{\cal Y}
. Thus, there exists a
y^{\prime}\in{\cal Y}
such that
E[E^{\cal G}u\cdot y]=E[u\cdot y^{\prime}]
for all
u\in{\cal U}
. Since

, (
2

E[E^{\cal G}u\cdot y]=E[u\cdot E^{\cal G}y]\quad\forall u\in L^{\infty}.

Thus,
y^{\prime}=E^{\cal G}y
almost surely.

Assume now that
{\cal Y}
is the Köthe dual of
{\cal U}
and let

E^{\cal G}y\in{\cal Y}
. By solidity and linearity, we may assume that at most one component
y^{i}
of

is nonzero and that it is nonnegative. Then
E^{\cal G}y^{i}
is nonnegative. Since
{\cal Y}
is the Köthe dual, it suffices to show that
E[u^{i}(E^{\cal G}y^{i})]<\infty
for every nonnegative
u\in{\cal U}
. By ?THM?
,
E[u^{i}(E^{\cal G}y^{i})]=E[E^{\cal G}(u^{i})y^{i}]
, where the right side is finite, since
E^{\cal G}{\cal U}\subset{\cal U}
.
∎

Let
{\cal X}
and
{\cal V}
be decomposable solid spaces of
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
-valued random variables in separating duality under the bilinear form

(x,v)\mapsto E[x\cdot v].

A linear mapping
{\cal A}:{\cal X}\to{\cal U}
is
weakly continuous
if it is continuous with respect to the weak topologies. This means that
x\mapsto\langle{\cal A}x,y\rangle
is
\sigma({\cal X},{\cal V})
-continuous for all
y\in{\cal Y}
, or equivalently, there exists a linear mapping
{\cal A}^{*}:{\cal Y}\to{\cal V}
such that

\langle{\cal A}x,y\rangle=\langle x,{\cal A}^{*}y\rangle\quad\forall x\in{\cal X},\ y\in{\cal Y}.

The mapping
{\cal A}^{*}
is known as the
adjoint
of
{\cal A}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
A\in L^{0}(\mathbbm{R}^{m\times n})
be a random matrix such that
Ax\in{\cal U}
for all
x\in{\cal X}
. The linear mapping
{\cal A}:{\cal X}\to{\cal U}
defined pointwise by

{\cal A}x=Ax\quad a.s.

is weakly continuous if and only if
A^{*}y\in{\cal V}
for all
y\in{\cal Y}
, and in this case its adjoint is given pointwise by

{\cal A}^{*}y=A^{*}y\quad a.s.

If
{\cal V}
is the Köthe dual of
{\cal X}
, then
A^{*}y\in{\cal V}
for all
y\in{\cal Y}
,

Proof.

For any
x\in{\cal X}
and
y\in{\cal Y}
,

\langle{\cal A}x,y\rangle=E[(Ax)\cdot y]=E[x\cdot A^{*}y],

which proves the equivalence and the adjoint formula. The above equation implies that
x\cdot A^{*}y\in L^{1}
, so
A^{*}y\in{\cal V}
when
{\cal V}
is the Köthe dual of
{\cal X}
.
∎

2.2
Conjugates of integral functionals

This section studies convex integral functionals on paired decomposable spaces
{\cal U}
and
{\cal Y}
of random variables. More precisely, we take a normal integrand
h
and study the integral functionals
Eh:{\cal U}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
and
Eh^{*}:{\cal Y}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
defined by

Eh(u):=\int_{\Omega}h(u(\omega),\omega)dP(\omega)

and

Eh^{*}(y):=\int_{\Omega}h^{*}(y(\omega),\omega)dP(\omega).

The following two theorems are essentially reformulations of the main results in
[
25
]
. We give the simple proofs for completeness.

Theorem 5
.

If
h
is a convex normal integrand with
\mathop{\rm dom}\nolimits Eh\neq\emptyset
, then

(Eh)^{*}=Eh^{*}.

Moreover,
y\in\partial Eh(u)
if and only if
Eh(u)
is finite and
y\in\partial h(u)
almost surely.

Proof.

The first claim follows by applying
[
31
, Theorem 14.60]
to the normal integrand
h_{y}(u,\omega):=h(u,\omega)-u\cdot y(\omega)
, where
y\in{\cal Y}
. As to the second, we have
y\in\partial Eh(u)
if and only if
Eh(u)
is finite and
Eh(u)+(Eh)^{*}(y)=\langle u,y\rangle
. Since
(Eh)^{*}=Eh^{*}
by the first part, the equality holds, by Fenchel’s inequality, if and only if

h(u)+h^{*}(y)=u\cdot y

almost surely. This means that
y\in\partial h(u)
almost surely.
∎

Corollary 6
.

Let
h
be a convex normal integrand. The following are equivalent

1.

\mathop{\rm dom}\nolimits Eh\neq\emptyset
and
\mathop{\rm dom}\nolimits Eh^{*}\neq\emptyset
,

2.

Eh
is proper and closed,

3.

\mathop{\rm dom}\nolimits Eh\neq\emptyset
and there exists
y\in{\cal Y}
and
\alpha\in L^{1}
such that

\displaystyle h(u,\omega)\geq u\cdot y(\omega)-\alpha(\omega)

and imply that
Eh
and
Eh^{*}
are conjugates of each other and that
y\in\partial Eh(u)
if and only if
y\in\partial h(u)
almost surely.

Proof.

By ?THM?
, 1 implies 2. Assuming 2, there exists
u\in\mathop{\rm dom}\nolimits Eh
,
y\in{\cal Y}
and
a\in\mathbbm{R}
such that

Eh(u)\geq\langle u,y\rangle-a\quad\forall u\in{\cal U}

Thus, by ?THM?
,

a\geq(Eh)^{*}(y)=Eh^{*}(y).

By Fenchel’s inequality

h(u,\omega)+h^{*}(y,\omega)\geq u\cdot y,

so 3 holds with
\alpha(\omega)=h^{*}(y(\omega),\omega)
. If 3 holds,
Eh^{*}(y)\leq E\alpha
, so 1 holds. By ?THM?

Eh
and

are conjugates of each other and

implies

almost surely. If

are proper, so

Corollary 7

Given a closed convex valued measurable mapping

{\cal S}:=\{u\in{\cal U}\mid u\in S\ \text{a.s.}\}

This follows by applying ?THM?

Assume that

We will develop a duality theory for (

S
P

[

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm minimize}\limits\quad

\displaystyle Ef(x,\bar{u}):=\int f(x(\omega),\bar{u}(\omega),\omega)dP(\omega)\quad\mathop{\rm over}\ \text{$x\in{\cal X}_{a}$}

(
SP_{\cal X}
)

where
{\cal X}\subset L^{0}(\Omega,{\cal F},P;\mathbbm{R}^{n})
is a solid decomposable space of random paths and

{\cal X}_{a}:={\cal X}\cap{\cal N}.

We will assume that the parameter
\bar{u}
belongs to another solid decomposable space

of random variables. The general theory of convex duality will give a dual problem and optimality conditions for (

⁢
S

P
𝒳

S
P

We embed (

⁢
S

P
𝒳

) into the conjugate duality framework by introducing an additional parameter

and the extended real-valued convex function

defined by

We denote the associated optimum value function by

\varphi(z,u):=\inf_{x\in{\cal X}}\{Ef(x,u)\,|\,x-z\in{\cal N}\}.

We assume that
{\cal X}
is in separating duality with a solid decomposable space
{\cal V}\subset L^{0}(\Omega,{\cal F},P;\mathbbm{R}^{n})
and that
{\cal U}
is in separating duality with a solid decomposable space

\langle x,v\rangle:=E[x\cdot v]\quad\text{and}\quad\langle u,y\rangle:=E[u\cdot y].

Solidity implies that

{\cal X}={\cal X}_{0}\times\cdots\times{\cal X}_{T}\quad\text{and}\quad{\cal V}={\cal V}_{0}\times\cdots\times{\cal V}_{T},

where
{\cal X}_{t}
and
{\cal V}_{t}
are solid decomposable spaces of

-valued random variables in separating duality under the bilinear form
(x_{t},v_{t})\mapsto E[x_{t}\cdot v_{t}]
. It follows that

\langle x,v\rangle=\sum_{t=0}^{T}E[x_{t}\cdot v_{t}]\quad\forall x\in{\cal X},\ v\in{\cal V}

and

{\cal X}_{a}={\cal X}_{0}({\cal F}_{0})\times\cdots\times{\cal X}_{T}({\cal F}_{T}).

According to the general conjugate duality framework of
[
27
]
, the
dual problem
is the concave maximization problem

\mathop{\rm maximize}\limits\quad\langle\bar{u},y\rangle-F^{*}(0,p,y)\quad\mathop{\rm over}\ (p,y)\in{\cal V}\times{\cal Y}.

(
D
)

More explicit forms will be given below. By definition,

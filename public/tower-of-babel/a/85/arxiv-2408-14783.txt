Manifestly Covariant Worldline Actions from Coadjoint Orbits Part II: Twistorial Descriptions
TaeHwan OH

Abstract
Worldline actions for various twistor particles in AdS spacetimes are constructed from the coadjoint orbits of S ​ p ​ ( 4 , ℝ ) Sp(4,\mathbb{R}) , S ​ U ​ ( 2 , 2 ) SU(2,2) and O ∗ ​ ( 8 ) O^{*}(8) as constrained Hamiltonian systems. The constraints are associated with the coadjoint orbits of the dual groups, respectively identified as O ⁡ ( p , M − p ) O(p,M-p) , U ⁡ ( p , M − p ) U(p,M-p) and S ​ p ​ ( p , M − p ) Sp(p,M-p) . These actions are presented in a universal form by making use of the twistor variables taking value in ℝ \mathbb{R} , ℂ \mathbb{C} and ℍ \mathbb{H} , respectively. We find that the massless, massive and the conformal particles (namely the singletons) of any spins appear for the compact dual groups with p = 0 p=0 reproducing many of the results in earlier literature, whereas more exotic particle species, such as various tachyons, continuous spin particles and BdS particles appear for the non-compact dual groups.

2
Manifestly covariant worldline actions
from coadjoint orbits of a classical Lie group

Let us review the construction of manifestly covariant particle actions
from coadjoint orbits of a classical Lie group
[
1
]
.

2.1
Classical Lie Groups

Any classical Lie group is a subgroup of a general linear group

GL(N,\mathbb{F})
with
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{H}
,
which preserves a certain bilinear form:
We can define any classical Lie group as

B(b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{F})=\{\,A\in GL(N,\mathbb{F})\mid A^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,A=b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,\}\,,

(2.1)

where
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
is an element of
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
.
The dagger operation is defined as the composition
A^{\dagger}=(A^{t})^{*}

of the matrix transpose
t
and the
\mathbb{F}
conjugation
*
.
3
3

3

The
*
conjugation is the identity map
for
\mathbb{R}
, the complex conjugation for
\mathbb{C}
,
and the quaternionic conjugation for
\mathbb{H}
.

Up to a
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
conjugation,

b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
can be mapped to either Hermitian or anti-Hermitian
with respect to
\dagger
. A Hermitian
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
is in the conjugacy class of a flat metric of
(p,N-p)
signature:
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(p,N-p)}
.
An anti-Hermitian
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
is
in the conjugacy class of the symplectic matrix:

b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}=\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
. With different choices
of
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
, we find the full list of classical Lie groups,
besides the
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
itself:

\displaystyle B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(p,N-p)},\mathbb{R})=O(p,N-p)\,,\qquad

\displaystyle B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{R})=Sp(N,\mathbb{R})\,,

(2.2)

\displaystyle B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(p,N-p)},\mathbb{C})=U(p,N-p)\,,\qquad

\displaystyle B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{C})\cong U(\tfrac{N}{2},\tfrac{N}{2})\,,

(2.3)

\displaystyle B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(p,N-p)},\mathbb{H})=Sp(p,N-p)\,,\qquad

\displaystyle B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{H})\cong O^{*}(2N)\,.

(2.4)

In the right column,
N
is even for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
,
whereas it can be also an odd number for
\mathbb{H}
.
This is because we can take the second element of
\mathbb{H}

as a two-dimensional symplectic matrix:

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}=\mathsf{j}\,I_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
.
Since
\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
can be diagonalized as
\mathsf{i}\,\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(N/2,N/2)}
,
the group

B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{C})
is isomorphic to
U(N/2,N/2)
.
On the contrary, even though
\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
can still be diagonalized
as
\mathsf{i}\,\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(N/2,N/2)}
, the group
B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{H})

is not isomorphic to
Sp(N/2,N/2)
because the
\mathsf{i}
,
the first element of
\mathbb{H}
, does not commute with a quaternionic matrix.
See e.g.
[
52
, Prop. 9.3.2.]
for relevant discussions.

2.2
Twistor Groups

The classical Lie groups
B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F})

happen to be isomorphic to
AdS groups of special dimensions,

\displaystyle B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{R})=Sp(4,\mathbb{R})\cong\widetilde{SO}^{+}(2,3)\,,

\displaystyle B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{C})=U(2,2)\supset SU(2,2)\cong\widetilde{SO}^{+}(2,4)\,,

\displaystyle B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{H})=O^{*}(8)\cong\widetilde{SO}^{+}(2,6)\,,

(2.5)

and they
contain
GL(2,\mathbb{F})

isomorphic
with the Lorentz subgroups,

\displaystyle GL(2,\mathbb{R})\supset SL(2,\mathbb{R})\cong\widetilde{SO}^{+}\!(1,2)\,,

\displaystyle GL(2,\mathbb{C})\supset SL(2,\mathbb{C})\cong\widetilde{SO}^{+}\!(1,3)\,,

\displaystyle GL(2,\mathbb{H})\supset SL(2,\mathbb{H})\cong\widetilde{SO}^{+}\!(1,5)\,.

(2.6)

Here
\widetilde{SO}^{+}(p,q)
designates the double cover
of the orthochronous Lorentz group
SO^{+}(p,q)
.
Hence,
letting
|\mathbb{F}|
be
the real dimensions of
\mathbb{F}
,
the twistor group

B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F})

is the double cover of the AdS
|F|+2
isometry group, or equivalently the
(|\mathbb{F}|+1)
-dimensional
conformal group.
This isomorphism allows to describe half-integral spins,
which was not possible in the vectorial description
based on the Lie group
O(p,q)

[
1
]
,
and it is in the heart of twistor description of particles
and fields: see e.g.
[
5
,
28
]
.
We shall show how the twistor actions of various AdS particles
in the literature can be recovered, together with many new
particle actions,
from a universal expression by applying
the information of Lie group, that is
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
,
and the coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}^{G}_{\phi}
, which
will be specified by a representative element
\phi
.

All the twistor actions will be described
in an equal footing by taking the advantage of the natural inclusion
as groups of
4\times 4
matrices,

B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{R})\subset B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{C})\subset B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{H})\,,

(2.7)

with the
2\times 2
matrix Lorentz subgroups,

GL(2,\mathbb{R})\subset GL(2,\mathbb{C})\subset GL(2,\mathbb{H})\,.

(2.8)

This allows us to describe the corresponding twistor actions
in a universal expression in terms of
4M\,|\mathbb{F}|
variables,
where
M
is the rank of the dual group fixed by
the choice of the coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}^{G}_{\phi}
.

2.3
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
manifestly covariant worldline action

Let us now review the derivation of a manifestly covariant worldline action
with a
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
symmetry
from a coadjoint orbit of the Lie group
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
.
On the group manifold
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
, the matrix components
of
X^{a}{}_{b}\in\mathbb{F}
of an element
X\in GL(N,\mathbb{F})

serve a natural and global coordinate system,
and the left-invariant vector fields are given by

V=V^{a}{}_{b}\,X^{c}{}_{a}\,\frac{\partial}{\partial X^{c}{}_{b}}
.
The symplectic structure of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}GL(N,\mathbb{F})

prescribes the geometric action, which reads simply

S[X,P,A]=\int{\rm Tr}_{N}\left[P\,\mathrm{d}X+A\,(P\,X-\phi)\right]_{\mathbb{R}},

(2.9)

where
X
,
P
,
A
and
\phi
belong to
Mat_{N\times N}(\mathbb{F})
.
The real part
[-]_{\mathbb{R}}
stands for

\left[A\right]_{\mathbb{R}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(A+A^{\dagger}\right),

(2.10)

with
A^{\dagger}:=(A^{t})^{*}
, and the
(-)^{*}
stands for the conjugate in
\mathbb{F}
.
The real part does not do anything for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, but they are necessary
for the correct symplectic potentials for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}

and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{H}
. The constraint imposed
by the Lagrange multiplier
A
can be algebraically solved to give

S[X]=\int{\rm Tr}_{N}\left[\phi\,X^{-1}\mathrm{d}X\right]_{\mathbb{R}}\,.

(2.11)

If the coadjoint matrix
\phi
has a rank
M\leq N
,
we can transform it into a triangular form
and integrate out the components of
X
in the lower subspace

Mat_{N\times(N-M)}(\mathbb{F})
.
This brings the reduction of the action into the form,

S[X,P,A]=\int{\rm Tr}_{M}\left[P\,\mathrm{d}X+A\,(P\,X-\tilde{\phi})\right]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

(2.12)

where
X
,
P
and
A
takes value
in
Mat_{N\times M}(\mathbb{F})
,

Mat_{M\times N}(\mathbb{F})

and
Mat_{M\times M}(\mathbb{F})
, respectively.
The matrix
\tilde{\phi}
is the
M\times M

submatrix of the triangulized
\phi
,
and hence it belongs to
Mat_{M\times M}(\mathbb{F})
.
In the end, the action describes a
G=GL(N,\mathbb{F})

coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}^{G}_{\phi}
as a reduced phase space
inside
\mathbb{F}^{2MN}
where the constraints
are given through the moment maps

\tilde{\mu}(X,P)=P\,X\in Mat_{M\times M}(\mathbb{F})

generating
\tilde{G}=GL(M,\mathbb{F})
under Poisson bracket.
Note also that the moment maps

\mu(X,P)=X\,P\in Mat_{N\times N}(\mathbb{F})

associated with the original
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
symmetry
Poisson-commute with
\tilde{\mu}
.
The constraints
\tilde{\mu}(X,P)-\tilde{\phi}\approx 0

are associated with the dual
\tilde{G}=GL(M,\mathbb{F})

coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}^{\tilde{G}}_{\tilde{\phi}}
.

2.4
B(b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{F})
manifestly
covariant worldline action

The passage from
GL(N,\mathbb{F})
to
B(b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{F})

can be done by implementing the defining condition (
2.1
)
of
B(b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{F})
into the action (
2.11
)
as a constraint. The resulting action reads

\displaystyle S[X,A]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int{\rm Tr}_{N}\left[\phi\,X^{-1}\mathrm{d}X+A\,(X^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,X-b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)})\right]_{\mathbb{R}}

(2.13)

\displaystyle\cong

\displaystyle\int{\rm Tr}_{N}\left[\phi\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}^{\ \ -1}\,X^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,\mathrm{d}X+A\,(X^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,X-b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)})\right]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

where
\cong
designates the redefinition of
A

replacing
X^{-1}
by
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}^{\ \ -1}\,X^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
.
The constant matrix
\phi
belongs to

\mathfrak{b}(b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{F})=\{\,\xi\in Mat_{N\times N}(\mathbb{F})\,|\,\xi^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}+b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,\xi=0\,\}\,,

(2.14)

whereas
X
takes value in
Mat_{N\times N}(\mathbb{F})

after introducing the constraint.
If the matrix
\phi\in\mathfrak{b}(b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{F})

has a rank
M
, it can always be mapped to a
\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)}
as

\phi=T\,\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)}\,T^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,,

(2.15)

in terms of a transformation matrix
T\in Mat_{N\times M}(\mathbb{F})
.
Moreover, the mapping has a very simple rule:
we can always choose
T
such that

\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)}=(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)})^{-1}

for
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(p,N-p)}
,
and
\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)}=(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(q,M-q)})^{-1}

for
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}=\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}
.
In the latter case, the number
q
depends on
\phi
.
The real part
concerns mostly about the quaternion case
since in the real and complex cases
the Lagrangian is already real up to a boundary term.

After a suitable redefinition of
X
and
A

in terms of the transformation matrix
T

and integrating out the non-dynamical components of
X

in the subspace
Mat_{N\times(N-M)}(\mathbb{F})
,
the action becomes

S[X,A]=\int{\rm Tr}_{M}\left[\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)}\,X^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,\mathrm{d}X+A\left(X^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,X-\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)}^{\ \ -1}\,\tilde{\phi}\right)\right]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

(2.16)

where
X
takes value in
Mat_{N\times M}(\mathbb{F})

and
\tilde{\phi}
is given by

\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)}^{\ \ -1}\,\tilde{\phi}=T^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,T\,.

(2.17)

Here,
\tilde{\mu}(X)=X^{\dagger}\,b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)}\,X

are the moment maps
generating the dual symmetry
\tilde{G}=B(\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)},\mathbb{F})
,
and hence
\tilde{\phi}
is an element
of
\mathfrak{b}(\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)},\mathbb{F})
.
Note that the moment maps
\tilde{\mu}
of the dual group Poisson-commute with the moment maps

\mu(X)=X\,\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)}\,X^{\dagger}

generating the original symmetry
G=B(b_{\scriptscriptstyle(N)},\mathbb{F})
.

2.5
Worldline action
of twistor particles in AdS
|F|+2

With the choice
b_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)}=\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)}

and
(\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)})^{-1}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(q,M-q)}
,
the universal action (
2.16
) can be written
in a component form as

S[X,A]=\int\left[\eta^{IJ}\,(X^{A}{}_{J})^{*}\,\Omega_{AB}\,\mathrm{d}X^{B}{}_{I}+A^{IJ}\,\left((X^{A}{}_{J})^{*}\,\Omega_{AB}\,X^{B}{}_{I}-\eta_{JK}\,\tilde{\phi}^{K}{}_{I}\right)\right]_{\mathbb{R}},

(2.18)

where
\eta_{IJ}
are the components of
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(q,M-q)}
.
The dual group indices
I,J
take values in

\{1,\ldots,M\}
for
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)}
,

\{0,1,\ldots,M-1\}
for
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(1,M-1)}

and

\{0^{\prime},0,1\ldots,M-2\}
for
\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(2,M-2)}
.
These indices should not be confused with the spacetime coordinate indices.
What transforms covariantly under the spacetime symmetry
is the twistor variable with the indices
A,B
,
which takes 4 values. They can be further split into the undotted
and dotted bi-valued indices
\alpha,\beta
and
\dot{\alpha},\dot{\beta}
:

\Omega_{AB}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\delta^{\dot{\beta}}_{\dot{\alpha}}\\
\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}&0\end{pmatrix}\,,\qquad X^{A}{}_{I}=\binom{\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}}{\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}}\,,\qquad(X^{A}{}_{I})^{*}=\binom{(\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I})^{*}}{(\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I})^{*}}=\binom{\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}}{\bar{\pi}_{\alpha}{}_{I}}\,,

(2.19)

to result in the more familiar form of the twistor action,

S[\xi,\pi,A]=\int\left[\eta^{IJ}\,\Big(\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,J}\,\mathrm{d}\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}-\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{J}\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{\dot{\alpha}\,I}\Big)+A^{IJ}\,\left(\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,J}\,\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}-\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{J}\,\pi_{\dot{\alpha}\,I}-\tilde{\phi}_{JI}\right)\right]_{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.20)

Here,
(\tilde{\phi}_{IJ})^{*}=-\tilde{\phi}_{JI}

and
(A^{IJ})^{*}=-A^{JI}
under the
\mathbb{F}
conjugation.
For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
, the Lagrangian
without the projection to the real part

is already real up to a boundary term,
but for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{H}
, the (conj) term is necessary
for the reality of the Lagrangian.
Remark that
M

corresponds to the number of twistors.

The Poisson bracket of the action for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
reads

\{\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I},\bar{\pi}_{\beta\,J}\}=\eta_{IJ}\,\delta_{\beta}^{\alpha}\,,\qquad\{\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I},\pi_{\dot{\beta}\,J}\}=\eta_{IJ}\,\delta_{\dot{\beta}}^{\dot{\alpha}}\,,

(2.21)

where the complex variables and their complex conjugate
are treated independently as usual.
For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, we may either begin
with the real variables with

\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}=\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}\,,\qquad\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,I}=\pi_{\dot{\alpha}\,I}\,,

(2.22)

or work with the complex variables and treat
the above reality conditions as second class constraints.
For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{H}
,
we may use
the

Hermitian matrix representation of a quaternion, where
the Hermicity condition should be
imposed as second class constraints
(see Section

In fact, for a universal treatment

it is convenient to make use of the following properties of the real part

.
For any numbers

,

[a\,b]_{\mathbb{R}}=[b\,a]_{\mathbb{R}}=[a^{*}\,b^{*}]_{\mathbb{R}}=a_{0}\,b_{0}-\sum_{r=1}^{|\mathbb{F}|-1}a_{r}\,b_{r}\in\mathbb{R}\,,

(2.23)

where the real components

are given by

a=a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\,,\qquad a=a_{0}+a_{1}\,{\mathsf{i}}\in\mathbb{C}\,,\qquad a=a_{0}+a_{1}\,{\mathsf{i}}+a_{2}\,{\mathsf{j}}+a_{3}\,{\mathsf{k}}\in\mathbb{H}\,.

(2.24)

Then, the Poisson bracket can be expressed as

\big\{\,[a\,\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,\,[b\,\bar{\pi}_{\beta\,J}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,\big\}=\big\{\,[a\,\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,\,[b^{*}\,\pi_{\dot{\beta}\,J}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,\big\}=\frac{1}{2}\,\eta_{IJ}\,\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,[a\,b]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

(2.25)

for any
\mathbb{F}
numbers
a

.
The constraints imposed by the Lagrange multiplier

are

\tilde{\chi}_{IJ}=\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}-\tilde{\phi}_{IJ}\approx 0\,,

(2.26)

where
\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}
are the
\mathbb{F}
-valued functions given by

\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}=\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,I}\,\xi^{\alpha}{}_{J}-\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}\,\pi_{\dot{\alpha}\,J}\,,

(2.27)

and they are closed under the Poisson bracket,

\displaystyle\big\{\,[a\,\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,\,[b\,\tilde{\mu}_{KL}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,\big\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\eta_{JK}\,(b\,a)\,\tilde{\mu}_{IL}-\eta_{JL}\,(b^{*}\,a)\,\tilde{\mu}_{IK}-\eta_{KI}\,(b\,a^{*})\,\tilde{\mu}_{JL}+\eta_{LI}\,(b^{*}\,a^{*})\,\tilde{\mu}_{JK}\right]_{\mathbb{R}}\,.

(2.28)

These Poisson brackets
define the dual algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{b}(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(q,M-q)},\mathbb{F})
: the functions

\tilde{\mu}^{IJ}

are the moment maps of the dual algebra.

Note that for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, the real part does not play any role and the
numbers

can be factored out freely leaving
the usual Lie bracket of the orthogonal algebra
\mathfrak{so}(q,M-q)
.
For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
, the above is equivalent to the more usual expression,

\{\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}\,,\tilde{\mu}_{KL}\}=\eta_{JK}\,\tilde{\mu}_{IL}-\eta_{IL}\,\tilde{\mu}_{KJ}\,.

(2.29)

The moment maps
\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}
transform under the

conjugate as

(\tilde{\mu}_{IJ})^{*}=-\tilde{\mu}_{JI}\,,

(2.30)

and hence the symmetric and antisymmetric parts

\tilde{\mu}_{(IJ)}
and
\tilde{\mu}_{[IJ]}
have the property,

(\tilde{\mu}_{(IJ)})^{*}=-\tilde{\mu}_{(IJ)}\,,\qquad(\tilde{\mu}_{[IJ]})^{*}=\tilde{\mu}_{[IJ]}\,.

(2.31)

This shows that
each symmetric components
\tilde{\mu}_{(IJ)}
are respectively
zero, pure imaginary and pure quaternion for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{H}
.
Therefore, each
\tilde{\mu}_{(IJ)}
generates the Lie algebra
\hat{\mathbb{F}}

that we define as
\hat{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathfrak{o}(1)=0
,

\hat{\mathbb{C}}:=\mathfrak{u}(1)
and
\hat{\mathbb{H}}:=\mathfrak{sp}(1)
(in other words,
\hat{\mathbb{F}}=\mathbb{F}/\mathbb{R}
).

The moment maps
\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}
are
the image of the co-moment map
m

of the dual algebra
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{b}(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(q,M-q)},\mathbb{F})
,

m\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{*}\,R_{IJ}-a\,R_{JI}\right)\right]=[a\,\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,\qquad\forall a\in\mathbb{F}\,,

(2.32)

where the basis of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is the subset of
\mathbb{F}\,R_{IJ}
4
4

4

By

,
we mean the basis
\{R_{IJ}\}
,
\{R_{IJ},i\,R_{IJ}\}

and
\{R_{IJ},{\bf i}\,R_{IJ},{\bf j}\,R_{IJ},{\bf k}\,R_{IJ}\}

\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{H}
.

whose Lie brackets are inherited from the associative product,

R_{IJ}\,R_{KL}=\eta_{JK}\,R_{IL}\,.

(2.33)

The basis of the coadjoint space
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}=\mathfrak{b}(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(q,M-q)},\mathbb{F})^{*}

can be chosen in
\mathbb{F}\,{\cal R}^{IJ}

basis with
5
5

5

It is convenient to use the defining representations of

(R_{IJ})^{K}{}_{L}=\delta^{K}_{I}\,\eta_{JL}\,,\qquad({\cal R}^{IJ})^{K}{}_{L}=\eta^{IK}\,\delta^{J}_{L}\,,

(2.34)

so that the two are related by
{\cal R}^{IJ}=R^{IJ}=\eta^{IK}\,\eta^{JL}\,R_{KL}

and satisfy

\langle{\cal R}^{IJ}\,,R_{KL}\rangle={\rm Tr}\left(({\cal R}^{IJ})^{t}\,R_{KL}\right)=\delta^{I}_{K}\,\delta^{J}_{L}\,.

(2.35)

\langle a\,{\cal R}^{IJ}\,,\,b\,R_{KL}\rangle=[a^{*}\,b]_{\mathbb{R}}\,\delta^{I}_{K}\,\delta^{J}_{L}\,.

(2.36)

Hence, any elements

and

can be decomposed in the above basis as

\xi=\xi^{IJ}\,R_{IJ}\,,\qquad\tilde{\phi}=\tilde{\phi}_{IJ}\,{\cal R}^{IJ}\,,

(2.37)

with
\xi^{IJ}
and
\tilde{\phi}_{IJ}
in
\mathbb{F}
satisfying

(\xi^{IJ})^{*}=-\xi^{JI}\,,\qquad(\tilde{\phi}_{IJ})^{*}=-\tilde{\phi}_{JI}\,.

(2.38)

The bilinear form between

and

\langle\,\tilde{\phi}\,,\,\xi\,\rangle=\left[(\tilde{\phi}_{IJ})^{*}\,\xi^{IJ}\right]_{\mathbb{R}}={\rm Tr}_{M}\left[\tilde{\phi}^{\dagger}\ \xi\right]_{\mathbb{R}}\,.

(2.39)

The constraints
\chi_{IJ}=\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}-\tilde{\phi}_{IJ}\approx 0

are split into the first and second class constraints.
The first class constraints are the combinations
[\xi^{IJ}\,\chi_{IJ}]_{\mathbb{R}}
with

{\rm ad}^{*}_{[\xi^{IJ}\,R_{IJ}]_{\mathbb{R}}}\,\tilde{\phi}=0\,,

(2.40)

while the rest corresponding to

The dimension of the phase space, that is, the original coadjoint orbit

is

\displaystyle{\rm dim}({\cal O}_{\phi}^{G})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\,M\,|\mathbb{F}|-{\rm dim}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})-{\rm dim}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\tilde{\phi}})

(2.41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M+\frac{M(7-M)}{2}\,|\mathbb{F}|-{\rm dim}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}_{\tilde{\phi}})\,,

{\rm dim}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}})=\frac{M(M+1)}{2}\,|\mathbb{F}|-M\,.

(2.42)

As we shall see later, the possible values of

Now let us consider the spacetime symmetry which is generated by the moment maps,

L^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=\xi^{\alpha\,I}\,\bar{\pi}_{\beta\,I}\,,\quad P^{\alpha\dot{\beta}}=\xi^{\alpha\,I}\,\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\beta}}{}_{I}\,,\quad K_{\dot{\alpha}\beta}=\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{I}\,\bar{\pi}_{\beta\,I}\,,\quad\bar{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}=\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{I}\,\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\beta}}{}_{I}\,,

with the Poisson brackets,

\{\,[a\,L^{\alpha}{}]_{\beta}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,\,[b\,L^{\gamma}{}_{\delta}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,\}=\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\delta^{\alpha}_{\delta}\,(a\,b)\,L^{\gamma}{}_{\beta}-\delta^{\gamma}_{\beta}\,(b\,a)\,L^{\alpha}{}_{\delta}\right]_{\mathbb{R}},

(2.44)

\{\,[a\,P^{\alpha\dot{\beta}}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,\,[b\,K_{\dot{\gamma}\delta}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,\}=\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\delta^{\alpha}_{\delta}\,(a\,b)\,\bar{L}_{\dot{\gamma}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}+\delta^{\dot{\beta}}_{\dot{\gamma}}\,(b\,a)\,L^{\alpha}{}_{\delta}\right]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

(2.45)

\{\,[a\,\bar{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,\,[b\,\bar{L}_{\dot{\gamma}}{}^{\dot{\delta}}]_{\mathbb{R}}\,\}=\frac{1}{2}\,\left[\delta^{\dot{\beta}}_{\dot{\gamma}}\,(b\,a)\,\bar{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\delta}}-\delta_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\dot{\delta}}\,(a\,b)\,\bar{L}_{\dot{\gamma}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}\right]_{\mathbb{R}}\,.

They are the co-moment map image of the Lie algebra

generated by
the generators of the conformal algebra.

3
Classification of twistor particles in AdS
|F|+3

In this section, we determine the coadjoint matrices
\phi
corresponding
to different particle species. For each of
\phi
, we identify
(\tilde{b}_{\scriptscriptstyle(M)})^{-1}=\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(q,M-q)}
,
that defines the dual group, and the dual coadjoint matrix
\tilde{\phi}
.
The classification of coadjoint orbits has been already carried out in the
\mathfrak{so}(2,d)
basis.
Therefore, we simply need to transform the
\mathfrak{so}(2,d)
basis into the twistor basis.
The change of basis can be made using the spinor representations of
\mathfrak{so}(2,d)
.
We shall first find the spinor representations of Lorentz subalgebra
\mathfrak{so}(1,d-1)

and extend them to
\mathfrak{so}(2,d)
.

Let us begin with the
d=3
case,
\mathfrak{so}(1,2)\subset\mathfrak{gl}(2,\mathbb{R})
,
where the gamma matrices are indeed all real and given by

\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(1,2)}{\gamma}_{\mu}=\tau_{\mu}\,,

(3.1)

with

\tau_{0}=i\,\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\tau_{1}=\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\tau_{2}=\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(3.2)

The
\tau_{\mu}
matrices satisfy the (anti-)commutation relations,

[\tau_{\mu},\tau_{\nu}]=2\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}{}\,\tau^{\rho}\,,\qquad\{\tau_{\mu},\tau_{\nu}\}=2\,\eta_{\mu\nu}\,,

(3.3)

with the conventions,

\epsilon_{012}=1\,,\qquad\eta_{\mu\nu}={\rm diag}(-1,1,1)\,.

(3.4)

This choice of basis can be extended to the Clifford algebra
\mathfrak{Cl}(2,3)\supset\mathfrak{b}(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{R})
as

\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(2,3)}{\gamma}_{0^{\prime}}=\hat{\tau}_{0}\otimes 1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\,,\qquad\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(2,3)}{\gamma}_{\mu}=\hat{\tau}_{2}\otimes\tau_{\mu}\,,\qquad\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(2,3)}{\gamma}_{3}=\hat{\tau}_{1}\otimes 1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\,,

(3.5)

while keeping reality of the gamma matrices,
or to
\mathfrak{Cl}(1,5)\supset\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{H})
with the gamma matrices,

\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(1,5)}{\gamma}_{\mu}=\tau_{\mu}\otimes\check{\tau}_{1}\,,\qquad\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(1,5)}{\gamma}_{\omega}=\mathsf{q}_{\omega-3}\,1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\otimes\check{\tau}_{0}\quad[\omega=4,5,6]\,,

(3.6)

where
(\mathsf{q}_{1},\mathsf{q}_{2},\mathsf{q}_{3})=(\mathsf{i},\mathsf{j},\mathsf{k})
are the quaternion basis.
The first element
\mathsf{i}
can serve as
\sqrt{-1}
for the subalgebra
\mathfrak{Cl}(1,3)\supset\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{C})
.
We introduced different notations
\hat{\tau}_{\mu}=\check{\tau}_{\mu}=\tau_{\mu}
to make
the inclusion structure more clear.
Finally the largest Clifford algebra
\mathfrak{Cl}(2,6)\supset\mathfrak{b}(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{H})

is realized with the gamma matrices,

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(2,6)}{\gamma}_{0^{\prime}}=\hat{\tau}_{0}\otimes 1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\otimes\check{\tau}_{1}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(2,6)}{\gamma}_{\mu}=\hat{\tau}_{2}\otimes\tau_{\mu}\otimes\check{\tau}_{1}\,,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(2,6)}{\gamma}_{3}=\hat{\tau}_{1}\otimes 1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\otimes\check{\tau}_{1}\,,\qquad

\displaystyle\overset{\scriptscriptstyle(2,6)}{\gamma}_{\omega}=\mathsf{q}_{\omega-3}\,\hat{1}_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\otimes 1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\otimes\check{\tau}_{0}\,.

These gamma matrices are
8\times 8
with
\mathbb{H}
entries, but
the associated Lie algebra generators are all proportional to
\check{1}_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}
or
\check{\tau}_{2}

(no dependence on
\check{\tau}_{0}
and
\check{\tau}_{1}
),
and hence the last tensor product entry with
\check{\cdot}
can be dropped.
In the end, we identified the
spinor representations of all
\mathfrak{so}(2,6)\cong\mathfrak{b}(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{H})
generators.
For the realization of
\mathfrak{b}(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{H})
with
the standard form of
\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)}
,

\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)}=\hat{\tau}_{0}\otimes 1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}\\
-1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}&0\end{pmatrix},

(3.8)

we have to intertwine the representations as

J_{AB}=Q^{-1}\,\frac{[\gamma_{A},\gamma_{B}]}{4}\,Q\qquad[A,B=0^{\prime},\mu,\omega]\,,

(3.9)

where the intertwiner

is given by

Q=\begin{pmatrix}1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}&0\\
0&\tau_{0}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(3.10)

See Appendix
B
for
the explicit component expressions

.
The dual basis
{\cal J}^{AB}
of
\mathfrak{b}(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F})^{*}
can be also
represented by
4\times 4
matrices as

{\cal J}^{AB}=J^{BA}\,,

(3.11)

and satisfy

\langle{\cal J}^{AB},J_{CD}\rangle={\rm Tr}({\cal J}^{AB}\,J_{CD})=\delta^{[A}_{[C}\,\delta^{B]}_{D]}\,.

(3.12)

In the following,
we find simple
matrix expressions
of various coadjoint vectors
\phi

by making use of the matrix representation
of
{\cal J}^{AB}
introduced above.
We then diagonalize the matrices

to identify the dual groups

The matrix representation of each

determines the worldline action

into

)

Before enlisting our results, let us remark that

To address such limiting case, we perform

Moreover,

3.6
Particles on the conformal boundary

The twistor group

|F|+3

the conformal group in
|\mathbb{F}|+2
dimensions.
The conformal particles living in

very small

In this section,

•

The coadjoint orbit
corresponding to conformal scalar particle

\phi\,\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)}^{\ -1}=\epsilon\,{\cal J}^{+^{\prime}+}\,\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)}^{\ -1}=\frac{\epsilon}{4}\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\

\tilde{G}=B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(1)},\mathbb{F})

•

The coadjoint orbit of spinning singleton

\phi\,\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)}^{\ -1}=s\,({\cal J}^{0^{\prime}0}\pm{\cal J}^{12}+{\cal J}^{34}\mp{\cal J}^{56})\,\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)}^{\ -1}=\frac{s}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}+\mathsf{i}\,\tau_{0}&&\mp(\mathsf{i}\,1_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)}-\tau_{0})\\

\tilde{G}=B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(1)},\mathbb{F})
,

(3.96)

Note here that we use complex entries

\phi

the corresponding coadjoint orbit does not exist for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}

{\cal J}^{34}
and

For

Remark also that we included

to describe the particles of mixed symmetry spin
(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
by
the action with the constraints,

[\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}]=[s_{1}+s_{2},s_{1}-s_{3},s_{2}-s_{3}]\,.

(4.24)

For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
,
the label
m\geq s_{1}
in

[\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\,[m+s_{2},\ m-s_{1},\ s_{2}-s_{1}]\\
\,[s_{1}+s_{2},\ -m+s_{1},\ s_{2}-m]\end{array}\right..

(4.25)

corresponds to the mass of AdS
5
particle of
mixed symmetry spin
(s_{1},s_{2})
,
according to (
4.1
).
Note that
the two options for
[\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}]

describe exactly the same particle,
and they can be distinguished only by the
U(1)
shift value
2(s_{2}\mp(s_{1}-m))
.

•

B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F})

For
M=4
, we find
the action for a particle of
mass
m

s

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\,\Big[\,\delta^{IJ}\left(\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,I}\,\mathrm{d}\xi^{\alpha}{}_{J}-\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{J}\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}\right)+A^{(IJ)}\,\tilde{\mu}_{(IJ)}

(4.26)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,A^{[12]}\left(\tilde{\mu}_{[12]}-\frac{|m+s|}{2}\right)+A^{[34]}\left(\tilde{\mu}_{[34]}-\frac{|m-s|}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,A^{[13]}\,\tilde{\mu}_{[13]}+A^{[14]}\,\tilde{\mu}_{[14]}+A^{[23]}\,\tilde{\mu}_{[23]}+A^{[24]}\,\tilde{\mu}_{[24]}\Big]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

[

]
,
where the dual group symmetry

Again, for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{H}
,
with a
B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F})

the action can be transformed
so that the constraints with non-trivial shifts are

A^{11}\,(\tilde{\mu}_{11}-\mathsf{i}\,\ell_{1})+A^{22}\,(\tilde{\mu}_{22}-\mathsf{i}\,\ell_{2})+A^{33}\,(\tilde{\mu}_{33}-\mathsf{i}\,\ell_{3})+A^{44}\,(\tilde{\mu}_{44}-\mathsf{i}\,\ell_{4})\,,

(4.27)

where the case (

For

Note that

For

4.2
Non-compact dual group

In the case of non-compact dual groups,
we find various exotic particle species.
In this case,
we have very limited information about
unitary irreducible representations
because
they
are not of highest weight type
and
the relevant dual pair correspondence is much harder to analyze.
We can regard the situation
as the mileage of the classical analysis
for the representation theory.
Let us remind again that we have limited to the case with two labels for simplicity,
but the full cases can be treated by the same method.

•

B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(1,1)},\mathbb{F})

In this case, we find
short “massless” spinning tachyon
(
3.44
).
This is to be compared with
the massless spinning particles with
B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(2)},\mathbb{F})
,
which is the shortening point of massive spinning particle.
For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{H}
,
we also have BdS particles:
the short “massless” one with space-like spin (
3.71
)
and the one
with light-like momentum and spin
(
3.78
).
Note that for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}

with
B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(1,1)},\mathbb{C})=U(1,1)
,
the three cases
(
3.44
),
(
3.71
), and (
3.78
)
correspond respectively
to the two-sheet hyperboloid,
one-sheet hyperholoid, and cone
coadjoint orbits of
SU(1,1)
.

•

B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(2,1)},\mathbb{F})

This case includes
the maximal nilpotent orbit,
namely
the BdS particle of light-like momentum
and doubly-light-like spin
(
3.88
).
Besides it, we
find also large remnant orbits
(
3.56
) and (
3.59
)
appearing in the short “massless” limit
of the BdS particle (the
|m|<|s|
case
of massive spinning partlce) and the tachyon (
3.41
).
The latter two cases are to be compared with
the case of

B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(3)},\mathbb{F})
, namely
the large remnant orbit
appearing in the massless limit of massive spinning orbit.

•

B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(2,2)},\mathbb{F})

This case
includes
all kinds of “long” tachyons:
the ones with time-like spin (
3.42
),
space-like spin (
3.47
),
and light-like spin (
3.50
).
Moreover,
it contains
all kinds of “long” BdS particles:
the one appearing as the
|m|<|s|
subcase
of massive spinning particle,
the ones with
space-like momentum and space-like spin (
3.68
),
light-like momentum and space-like spin
(
3.75
)
and
space-like momentum and doubly-light-like spin
(
3.84
).

•

B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(3,1)},\mathbb{F})

Finally,
this case contains
the massive, massless and tachyonic
continuous spin particles (
3.53
).
The action reads

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\,\Big[\,\eta^{IJ}\left(\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,I}\,\mathrm{d}\xi^{\alpha}{}_{J}-\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{J}\,\mathrm{d}\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}\right)+A^{(IJ)}\,\tilde{\mu}_{(IJ)}

(4.30)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,A^{[01]}\left(\tilde{\mu}_{[01]}-|\nu|\right)-A^{[23]}\left(\tilde{\mu}_{[23]}-|s|\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\,A^{[02]}\,\tilde{\mu}_{[02]}+A^{[03]}\,\tilde{\mu}_{[03]}+A^{[12]}\,\tilde{\mu}_{[12]}+A^{[13]}\,\tilde{\mu}_{[13]}\Big]_{\mathbb{R}}\,,

where
I,J=0,1,2,3
and
\eta^{IJ}={\rm diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)
.
It can be viewed as
the twistorial counterpart
of the vectorial description
of the continuous spin particles
[
1
]
,
which are related to a variety of continuous spin fields identified by R. Metsaev

[
59
]
.
See also
[
60
]

for other vectorial models of (A)dS
4
continuous spin particles.

5
Dimensional reduction

Many of the twistor literatures
concern about massless and massive particles in flat spacetime,
Mink
|F|+2
.
In our construction, only conformal
particles are defined in

|\mathbb{F}|+2
dimensions,
namely
Mink
|F|+2
, AdS
|F|+2

and dS
|F|+2

boundaries
of AdS
|F|+3
.
These conformal particles are
massless but their spins are
rather special carrying the
(|\mathbb{F}|-1)
-row box Young diagram representation.
In fact, more general type of particles in
Mink
|F|+2
, AdS
|F|+2

and dS
|F|+2
can be obtained
from AdS
|F|+3
particles
by restricting the isometry
B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F})\cong\widetilde{SO}^{+}(2,|\mathbb{F}|+2)

to the subgroups
H_{\Lambda}
, that is,

H_{\Lambda=0}\cong\widetilde{ISO}^{+}(1,|\mathbb{F}|+1)
,

H_{\Lambda<0}\cong\widetilde{SO}^{+}(2,|\mathbb{F}|+1)
,
and
H_{\Lambda>0}\cong\widetilde{SO}^{+}(1,|\mathbb{F}|+2)
.
Remark that
the conformal particles,
namely singletons, are precisely the ones
which stay irreducible
under this restriction.
The restriction of
G=B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F})
to the subgroup
H_{\Lambda}
defines a new dual group
\tilde{H}_{\Lambda}
which include
the original dual group
\tilde{G}=B(\eta_{(q,M-q)},\mathbb{F})
.

(5.1)

The see-saw duality asserts that
the restriction of unitary irreducible representations on the left hand side
is governed by the restriction
on the right hand side, and vice versa. More precisely,
for the irreps
\pi^{G}_{\lambda}
of
G

and
\pi^{\tilde{H}_{\Lambda}}_{\tau}
of
\tilde{H}_{\Lambda}

branching respectively to
H_{\Lambda}

and
\tilde{G}
as

\pi^{G}_{\lambda}=\bigoplus_{\sigma}{\cal N}^{\sigma}_{\lambda}\,\pi^{H_{\Lambda}}_{\sigma}\,,\qquad\pi^{\tilde{H}_{\Lambda}}_{\tau}=\bigoplus_{\rho}\tilde{\cal N}^{\rho}_{\tau}\,\pi^{\tilde{G}}_{\rho}\,,

(5.2)

the multiplicities
{\cal N}^{\sigma}_{\lambda}

and
\tilde{\cal N}^{\rho}_{\tau}
are relate by

{\cal N}^{\sigma}_{\lambda}=\tilde{\cal N}^{\theta(\lambda)}_{\phi(\sigma)}\,,

(5.3)

where
\theta
and
\phi

are the bijective maps of the
(G,\tilde{G})
irreps and the
(H_{\Lambda},\tilde{H}_{\Lambda})
irreps, respectively.
Here, we consider the classical counterpart of the see-saw dual pair, which we may refer to as

symplectic see-saw reduction
.
The irreps correspond to coadjoint orbits,
and the restriction of an irrep down to
the irreps of a subgroup corresponds
to the foliation of a coadjoint orbits into
the coadjoint orbits of the subgroup.
Remark that the restriction to
H_{\Lambda=0}

to obtain massive twistor particles has been discussed already in
[
37
]
. Here,
we generalize the idea with extra emphasizes on see-saw dual pairs.

Let us consider the reductive subgroup

GL(2,\mathbb{F})
of
B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F})
,

L^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}=\xi^{\alpha}{}^{I}\,\bar{\pi}_{\beta\,I}\,,\qquad\bar{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}=\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{I}\,\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\beta}}{}_{I}\,.

(5.4)

We may separate each of
L^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}
and
\bar{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\beta}}

into the traceless and trace part:
the traceless part can be expressed
as

L_{(\alpha\beta)}=\xi_{(\alpha}{}^{I}\,\bar{\pi}_{\beta)\,I}\,,\qquad\bar{L}_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}=\pi_{(\dot{\alpha}}{}^{I}\,\bar{\xi}_{\dot{\beta})I}\,,

(5.5)

by lowering indices with the Levi-Civita symbols,
which are Lorentz invariant tensors
only for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
.
One combination of the traces is
the center of
GL(2,\mathbb{F})
,

D=L^{\alpha}{}_{\alpha}+\bar{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}=\xi^{\alpha\,I}\,\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,I}+\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{I}\,\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\beta}}{}_{I}\,,

(5.6)

which is nothing but the dilatation generator.
The other combination of the traces,

E=L^{\alpha}{}_{\alpha}-\bar{L}_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}=\xi^{\alpha\,I}\,\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,I}-\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{I}\,\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\beta}}{}_{I}\,,

(5.7)

takes value in
\hat{\mathbb{F}}
:
it vanishes for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
, generates
the central
U(1)
for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
.
For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{H}
, the three real generators in
E

are parts of the Lorentz subgroup
SL(2,\mathbb{H})
.
Since we consider the
|\mathbb{F}|+2
dimensional isometry subgroup of
B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F})
,
the dilatation generator
D
will be discarded from the
\mathfrak{h}_{\Lambda}
.
Moreover, among the translation and special conformal generators,

P^{\alpha\dot{\beta}}=\xi^{\alpha\,I}\,\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\beta}}{}_{I}\,,\qquad K_{\dot{\alpha}\beta}=\pi_{\dot{\alpha}}{}^{I}\,\bar{\pi}_{\beta\,I}

(5.8)

we retain only the linear combinations

P^{\alpha\dot{\beta}}+\Lambda\,K^{\dot{\beta}\alpha}\,.

(5.9)

As we consider a subgroup
H_{\Lambda}
,
the dual group enhances from
\tilde{G}
to

\tilde{H}_{\Lambda}
. This means
that on top of the
\tilde{G}
generators,

\tilde{\mu}_{IJ}=\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,I}\,\xi^{\alpha}{}_{J}-\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}\,\pi_{\dot{\alpha}\,J}\,,

(5.10)

we have additional generators that commute
with
H_{\Lambda}
.
It is not difficult to enlist all possible candidates because the invariance
under
L_{(\alpha\beta)}
and
\bar{L}_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}
implies that
the
\alpha,\beta
indices should be contracted:
the possible moment maps are

\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}\,\xi_{\alpha\,J}\,,\qquad\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,I}\,\xi^{\alpha}{}_{J}\,,\qquad\bar{\pi}^{\alpha}{}_{I}\,\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,J}\,,

(5.11)

and their
*
conjugates.
For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
, we can proceed to check the translational invariance to find that the additional dual generators are

M_{IJ}=\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}\,\xi_{\alpha\,J}-\Lambda\,\bar{\pi}^{\alpha}{}_{I}\,\bar{\pi}_{\alpha\,J}\,,\qquad\bar{M}_{IJ}=\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}\,\bar{\xi}_{\dot{\alpha}\,J}-\Lambda\,\pi^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}\,\pi_{\dot{\alpha}\,J}\,.

(5.12)

For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
,
the original dual generators form
O(p,M-p)
,
and real
M_{IJ}
are antisymmetric
hence span a
M(M-1)/2
dimensional space.
The whole dual group becomes a real form of
O_{M}\times O_{M}
. For the
p=0
example, we find
the sea-saw pairs for
\Lambda<0
and
\Lambda>0

The
M=1
case provides the conformal particles
having a very special restriction property: it branches into at most two irreps of the isometry subgroup.
For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{C}
,
it happens that the conformal particles cover all massless particles.

The
M=2
case
provides all “massive” particle species in
|\mathbb{F}|+2
dimensions, that is, those appearing in
the dimensional reduction of the most general “massless particle” in AdS
|F|+3
.
Here, we used the quotation mark for “massive” and “massless”,
because these cover not only the standard massive and massless particles with compact dual groups, but the analogue ones in tachyonic spectra or BdS with non-compact dual groups.

To reiterate, for
\Lambda\neq 0
,
the restriction
(
5.1
)
to 3d and 4d isometry groups leads, respectively, to the dual pairs,

(Sp(2,\mathbb{R})^{2},O(p,2-p)\times O(q,2-q))\,,\qquad(Sp(2,\mathbb{C}),O(2,\mathbb{C}))\,,

(5.15)

and

(Sp(4,\mathbb{R}),O(p,4-p))\,,\qquad(Sp(1,1),O^{*}(4))\,.

(5.16)

Therefore, one simply
recovers dual pairs of other type,
which describe
all possible “massive” particles in (A)dS
3
and (A)dS
4
, respectively.
For
\Lambda=0
,
the restriction provides
an equally good description,
even though it does not correspond
to a reductive dual pair (as the Poincaré group is not reductive).

Remark that
the restriction
(
5.1
)
to
H_{\Lambda=0}
reproduces
the 3d and 4d massive
twistor models

[
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
,
22
]
with the dual groups containing
respectively
O(2)
and
U(2)
:
these flat space twistor models
can be regarded as
the flat limit of
(A)dS massive twistors described by
the dual pairs
(
5.16
) with
p=0
.
The restriction also
reproduces
the 4d continuous spin
twistor model

[
47
,
48
,
49
]
,
with the dual group
containing
U(1,1)
.
This model can be regarded
as the flat limit of the AdS continuous spin twistor described by

(Sp(4,\mathbb{R}),O(3,1))
.

The particle description provided by the restriction (
5.1
)
has the manifest covariance of the full bulk Lorentz symmetry,
as oppose to the original particle descriptions based on
(B(\Omega_{\scriptscriptstyle(4)},\mathbb{F}),B(\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle(p,M-p)},\mathbb{F}))
having only manifest boundary Lorentz covariance: for the example of massive particles in AdS
4
, the original description makes use of real twistor
having only manifest 3d boundary Lorentz covariance, while
the restriction (
5.1
)
makes use of complex twistors having manifest covariance of the
4d bulk Lorentz symmetry.
Note however that the two descriptions are identical:
they are simply related by a
change of variables.

Let us also mention that
the quantization of twistor models leads to the oscillator representations governed by dual pair correspondences.
These representations are isomorphic to the spinor-helicity representations:
the massless
[
61
,

,

,

and massive

[

,

,
67

in flat space,
as well as their (A)dS counterparts

[

,

]

and
[

]
.

For
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{H}
,
the situation is different:
we should require the invariance under
E

(
5.7
) too, since it is a part of the Lorentz algebra.
We find that
M_{IJ}
no more commute with
E
.
If we had included
M_{IJ}
, which are

M(2M-1)
dimensional,
together with the original dual generators

Sp(p,M-p)
,
the enhanced dual group would had been
a real form of
GL_{2N}

which is dual to a real form of
GL_{4}

for instance,
U(2,2)
containing the AdS
5

or

5

The 6d isometry group
H_{\Lambda}

O^{*}(8)
, at the same time including
SU(2,2)
and
SL(2,\mathbb{H})

but not
U(2,2)
and

GL(2,\mathbb{H})

The impossibility of recovering
H_{\Lambda}
with
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{H}
as part of a dual pair
which in turn forms a seesaw pair with
\big(O^{*}(8),Sp(M)\big)

may be understood by recalling that the setup of Howe duality
is quite peculiar from the point of view of dualities
in representation theory. In general, a duality in the context
of representation theory designates a situation wherein
a
reducible
representation
V
of a given Lie group
G

is decomposed as a direct sum of tensor products of irreps
of
G
and irreps of its centralizer, say
\mathcal{A}
,
in a certain subalgebra of the endomorphism algebra of
V
,
such that pairs of irreps appear only once, and each
G
-irrep
appears with a unique irrep of the
\mathcal{A}
. The dual pair
correspondence, or Howe duality, corresponds to
V
being
the Fock space of
N
pairs of (bosonic) oscillators,
and the relevant subalgebra of its endomorphism algebra
being the
Weyl algebra
. On top of that,
if
G
is a reductive subgroup of
Sp(2N,\mathbb{R})
,
then its centralizer in the Weyl algebra can be characterized
using invariant theory for classical groups:
since by assumption
G
is a product of real forms
of the general linear, orthogonal, or symplectic groups
of various ranks, its invariant are generated by products
of the ‘elementary invariant tensors’ which are
the Kronecker delta, a metric of a given signature
and the canonical symplectic form. Since
Sp(2N,\mathbb{R})

is canonically embedded in the Weyl algebra as its subspace
of quadratic operators (in the oscillators), so is
G
,
and by the previous comment,
G
-invariant operators
are necessarily products of quadratic ones, the latter
being built using the elementary invariant tensors
to contract two oscillators. In other words, the centralizer
of
G
in the Weyl algebra is generated by quadratic invariants,
and therefore characterizing this centralizer boils down
to identifying the centralizer of
G
in
Sp(2N,\mathbb{R})
—the problem can be reduced to studying the quadratic subspace
of the Weyl algebra, i.e. the image of the symplectic group
in it. This latter aspect is the specificity of Howe duality,
and what makes it so appealing and efficient in many context.
However, the general problem of decomposing a space under
the joint action of a group
G
and its centralizer
(in a certain subalgebra of endomorphism),
though still well-posed, generically cannot be reduced
to the analysis of the centralizer of
G
in another
finite-dimensional Lie group like the symplectic group.

Consequently, one should not expect that the centralizer
of any Lie group
G
in the Weyl algebra is generated by
quadratic operator only, even when
G
is embedded
in quadratic operators: the number of oscillators
should be such that it allows the action of the elementary

G

of an invariant subspace. Such conditions were studied
by R. Howe in his seminal papers

\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{H}
is generated by a subgroup
contained in the same symplectic group as
O^{*}(8)

Appendix A
Quaternion-Symplectic structure

In this section,
we provide an alternative way to
handle the quaternion-symplectic structure.
Instead of expanding quaternions by
the typical basis
\mathsf{i}
,
\mathsf{j}
,
\mathsf{k}
,
we may use the
2\times 2
complex matrix representation
of quaternion:

q=\begin{pmatrix}q^{1}{}_{1}&q^{1}{}_{2}\\
q^{2}{}_{1}&q^{2}{}_{2}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bar{q}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{q}^{1}{}_{1}&\bar{q}^{1}{}_{2}\\
\bar{q}^{2}{}_{1}&\bar{q}^{2}{}_{2}\end{pmatrix},

(A.1)

with
\bar{q}^{i}{}_{j}=(q^{j}{}_{i})^{*}
and additional relations,

q^{1}{}_{1}=(q^{2}{}_{2})^{*}\,,\qquad q^{1}{}_{2}=-(q^{2}{}_{1})^{*}\,.

(A.2)

In this complex matrix description,
the dot product is given by the trace,

p\bullet q=\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{p}^{i}{}_{j}\,q^{j}{}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{q}^{i}{}_{j}\,p^{j}{}_{i}\,.

(A.3)

The relations (
A.2
) imposed on

\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}{}^{i}{}_{j}
and
\pi_{\dot{\alpha}\,I}{}^{i}{}_{j}

can be considered as second class constraints,
and hence can be imposed after all the computations
where we use the Poisson bracket (not the Dirac bracket) given by

\{\xi^{\alpha}{}_{I}{}^{i}{}_{k},\bar{\pi}_{\beta\,J}{}^{j}{}_{l}\}=\eta_{IJ}\,\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,\delta^{i}_{l}\,\delta^{j}_{k}\,,\qquad\{\bar{\xi}^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{I}{}^{i}{}_{k},\pi_{\dot{\beta}\,J}{}^{j}{}_{l}\}=\eta_{IJ}\,\delta^{\alpha}_{\beta}\,\delta^{i}_{l}\,\delta^{j}_{k}\,,

(A.4)

where
i,j,k,l=1,2

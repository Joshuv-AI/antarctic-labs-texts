Measurement of the dependence of the hadron production fraction ratios f \PQs / f \PQu f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQu} and f \PQd / f \PQu f_{\PQd}/f_{\PQu} on \PB meson kinematic variables in proton-proton collisions at s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV
*

Abstract
The dependence of the ratio between the \PBzs \PBzs and \PBp \PBp hadron production fractions, f \PQs / f \PQu f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQu} , on the transverse momentum ( \pt ) and rapidity of the \PB mesons is studied using the decay channels \PBzs → \PJGy ​ \PGf \PBzs\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PGf and \PBp → \PJGy ​ \PK + \PBp\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PK^{+} . The analysis uses a data sample of proton-proton collisions at a center-of-mass energy of 13 \TeV , collected by the CMS experiment in 2018 and corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 61.6 \fbinv . The f \PQs / f \PQu f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQu} ratio is observed to depend on the \PB \pt and to be consistent with becoming asymptotically constant at large \pt . No rapidity dependence is observed. The ratio of the \PBz \PBz to \PBp \PBp meson production fractions, f \PQd / f \PQu f_{\PQd}/f_{\PQu} , is also measured, for the first time in proton-proton collisions, using the \PBz → \PJGy \PKst 0 \PBz\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PKst{}^{0} decay channel. The result is found to be within one standard deviation of unity and independent of \pt and rapidity, as expected from isospin invariance.

When a
\PQb
quark is produced with sufficient momentum,
it undergoes a process, referred to as fragmentation or hadronization,
that results in the creation of a
\PQb
hadron.
The relative rates of the various flavors of
\PQb
hadrons are referred to as hadron production fractions
or fragmentation functions.
The fractions of
\PBp
,
\PBz
, and
\PBzs
mesons are denoted by
f_{\PQu}
,
f_{\PQd}
, and
f_{\PQs}
, respectively.
Experiments at the
\PB
factories use the characteristics of
\PB
meson production
at the
\PgUd
to set the scale of the measured
\PBp
and
\PBz
branching fractions,
allowing precision measurements at both
\PB
factories and hadron colliders.
For the
\PBzs
, however, the limited event samples at the
\PgUe
and uncertainties
in the fraction of
\PBzs
mesons produced do not allow the same approach.
As a result, precision
\PBzs
branching fraction measurements at hadron colliders
rely on ratios to
\PBp
or
\PBz
decay modes,
which require knowledge of
f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQu}
or
f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQd}
, respectively.
In fact, measurements of the rare decay
\PBzs\to\MM
,
which is used to search for physics beyond the standard model,
are currently limited by the uncertainty in

f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQu}

[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.
Besides providing information about the nature of the strong interaction,
precise
f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQu}
values are also needed to measure the
\PBzs\to\MM

and other
\PBzs
branching fractions.

A variety of measurements obtained from
\PZ
boson decays
by the LEP experiments have been combined by the Heavy Flavor Averaging Group
to obtain values for the hadron production fractions and the ratio
f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQd}

[
6
]
.
Subsequent central-rapidity measurements
by CDF (for pseudorapidity
\abs{\eta}<1
) in proton-antiproton collisions at
\sqrt{s}=1.96
\TeV

[

and

[

were compatible with the LEP result, with no evidence of a dependence on transverse momentum (
\pt

Instead, measurements of

in the forward-rapidity region (

by LHCb in
\Pp\Pp

\sqrt{s}=7,8

13

give strong evidence for a

\pt

\PB

This Letter presents an analysis aimed at establishing
if and how the relative
\PBzs

\PBp

\pt

\pt>12
\GeV

approximately complementary to that of the LHCb detector.
Additionally, we perform the first test of the isospin invariance

Throughout this Letter, charge-conjugate states are implicitly included,
and
\PKst{}^{0}
and
\PGf
represent the
\PKst{}
(892)
0
and
\PGfP
1020, respectively.
The
\PBp
and
\PBzs
mesons are reconstructed using the
\PBp\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PK^{+}
and
\PBzs\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PGf

with the
\PJGy

The respective event yields,
N_{\PBp}

The ratio of the efficiency-corrected meson yields,

{\mathcal{R}_{\PQs}}=(N_{\PBzs}/\epsilon_{\PBzs})\,/\,(N_{\PBp}/\epsilon_{\PBp})

is directly proportional to the
f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQu}

{\mathcal{R}_{\PQs}}=f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQu}\;\frac{\mathcal{B}(\PBzs\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PGf)\mathcal{B}(\PGf\!\to\!\PKp\PKm)}{\mathcal{B}(\PBp\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PK^{+})},

(1)

where
\mathcal{B}(\PBzs\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PGf)
,
\mathcal{B}(\PGf\!\to\!\PKp\PKm)
, and
\mathcal{B}(\PBp\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PK^{+})

are the branching fractions of the indicated decay channels;

\mathcal{B}(\PJGy\to\MM)
factor cancels in the ratio.
Given that the available measurements of the
\PBzs\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PGf
branching fraction
and of
f_{\PQs}
are correlated, we report measurements of
{\mathcal{R}_{\PQs}}
rather than of
f_{\PQs}/f_{\PQu}

The analysis also includes a measurement of the ratio
between the
\PBz
and
\PBp
hadron fractions,
f_{\PQd}/f_{\PQu}

using the
\PBz

\PBz\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PKst{}^{0}

\PKst{}^{0}

\PKst{}^{0}\!\to\!\Pgpm\PKp

Using notations analogous to those used above,

{\mathcal{R}_{\PQd}}=\frac{N_{\PBz}}{\epsilon_{\PBz}}\,\Bigg/\,\frac{N_{\PBp}}{\epsilon_{\PBp}}=f_{\PQd}/f_{\PQu}\;\frac{\mathcal{B}(\PBz\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PKst{}^{0})\mathcal{B}(\PKst{}^{0}\!\to\!\Pgpm\PKp)}{\mathcal{B}(\PBp\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PK^{+})}.

(2)

Under the assumption of strong isospin symmetry,
the
f_{\PQd}/f_{\PQu}
ratio is expected
to be independent of kinematic variables and identical to unity.
Given that the branching fractions used to calculate
f_{\PQd}/f_{\PQu}
from
{\mathcal{R}_{\PQd}}
are (independently) obtained from high-precision

\PB

we report the more relevant quantity
f_{\PQd}/f_{\PQu}

The CMS apparatus is a multipurpose detector
[
16
]

designed to trigger on and identify electrons, muons, photons,
and (charged and neutral) hadrons
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
.
A superconducting solenoid of 6
\unit
m internal diameter provides a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T.
Within the solenoid volume are the silicon pixel and strip tracker,
a crystal electromagnetic calorimeter, and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter.
Muons are measured in gas-ionization detectors embedded in the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid.
Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger system.
The first level, composed of custom hardware processors,
uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors to select events
at a rate of 100
\unit
kHz within a fixed latency of about 4
\mus

[
20
]
.
The second level,
consisting of a farm of processors running a faster version of the full event reconstruction software,
reduces the rate to around 1
\unit
kHz, before data storage
[
21
]
.

The events used in the analysis were selected by a trigger requiring
two oppositely charged muons with
\abs{\eta}<2.5
and
\pt>4
\GeV
,
a distance of closest approach between the two muons smaller than 0.5
\cm
,
a dimuon vertex fit
\chi^{2}
probability larger than 10%,
a dimuon invariant mass in the range 2.9–3.3
\GeV
,
and a transverse distance between the dimuon vertex and the beam axis,
L_{xy}
, larger than three times its uncertainty.
In addition, the dimuon
\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}
and transverse displacement vector,
\vec{L}_{xy}
,
must be aligned with each other:

{\cos\theta}\equiv{\vec{L}_{xy}\cdot\vec{p}_{\mathrm{T}}/(L_{xy}\,\pt)}>0.9
.
The trigger also requires a third track in the event,
compatible with being produced at the dimuon vertex, having
\pt>1.2
\GeV
and a transverse impact parameter significance larger than 2.
Finally, the dimuon-plus-track vertex fit
\chi^{2}
probability must be larger than 10%.

The charged tracks used to reconstruct the
\PB
mesons must pass high-purity criteria
[
19
]
,
have five or more hits in the silicon tracker, at least one of them in the pixel layers,
and have
\abs{\eta}<2.4
.
They must also match the tracks that triggered the data readout.
The muons used to reconstruct the
\PJGy
candidates must fulfill
soft-muon identification requirements
[
18
]
,
which include the (loose) matching between the track reconstructed in the silicon tracker
and the one reconstructed in the muon detectors.
They must also have
\pt>4
\GeV
and an impact parameter
smaller than 0.3
\cm
in the transverse plane and smaller than 20
\cm
along the beam axis.

The
\PJGy
candidate is combined with one track
to reconstruct
\PBp\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PK^{+}
decays
or with a pair of oppositely charged tracks
to reconstruct the
\PBzs\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PGf
and
\PBz\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PKst{}^{0}
decays.
All three tracks must have
\pt>1.2
\GeV
.
They are fitted together with the dimuon, imposing a common (secondary) vertex
(SV, the
\PB
meson decay point),
constraining the dimuon invariant mass to the
\PJGy
world-average mass,

m_{\PJGy}^{\mathrm{PDG}}

[
22
]
,
and assigning to each of the other tracks the
\Pgppm
or
\PKpm

Furthermore, the invariant mass of the pair of tracks must satisfy

\abs{M(\PKp\PKm)-m_{\PGf}^{\mathrm{PDG}}}<10
\MeV
for
\PBzs
decays and

\abs{M(\Pgp\PK)-m_{\PKst^{0}}^{\mathrm{PDG}}}<50
\MeV

\PBz

where
m_{\PGf}^{\mathrm{PDG}}
and
m_{\PKst^{0}}^{\mathrm{PDG}}
are,
respectively, the
\PGf

\PKst^{0}

[
22

In the latter case, lack of particle identification
implies that both combinations are possible (
\Pgpp\PKm
and
\PKp\Pgpm
);
if both match the mass window requirement, only the one with mass closer to

m_{\PKst^{0}}^{\mathrm{PDG}}

The primary vertex (PV) is selected among the several reconstructed
\Pp\Pp
collisions
as the one that minimizes the pointing angle of the
\PB
meson,
defined as the angle between the
\PB
momentum
and the vector joining the primary and secondary vertices.
The PV is refitted without the tracks of the
\PB

before computing the
\PB

We select
\PB
meson candidates with
12<\pt<70
\GeV

\abs{y}<2.4

For each decay channel, if more than one
\PB

The
{\mathcal{R}_{\PQs}}
and
{\mathcal{R}_{\PQd}}

by systematic uncertainties in the determination of the fitted signal yields

The systematic uncertainties affecting the signal yields are evaluated by
repeating the fits of the mass distributions in alternative conditions and computing
the difference between the obtained results and those of the baseline fit.
Two main variations of the fit model are independently considered:
first, the modeling of the signal peaks is changed from the default double-Gaussian
to a Student’s t-distribution
[
29
]
;
second, the combinatorial background is fitted by a first-order Chebyshev polynomial
instead of the baseline exponential function.
An additional systematic uncertainty in the
\PBz

of less than 1%, is evaluated by fitting the
\PJGy\,\PKst{}^{0}
mass distribution
changing the normalization of the “
\pi

relative to that of the
\PBz
signal term,
by the uncertainty in the default value,
which exclusively reflects the sizes of the MC event samples;
other systematic effects were found to be negligible.
The fit procedure itself is seen to provide unbiased results,
for each of the bins,
both for the central values and the uncertainties,
through a study involving fits of 1000 event samples randomly generated
using the nominal functions with the best fit parameters
and with sizes corresponding to the number of measured events.
The result is that the uncertainties in the fitted
\PBzs
,
\PBp
, and
\PBz

contribute systematic uncertainties to the

at a center-of-mass energy of 13
\TeV
, collected by CMS in 2018 and
corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 61.6
\fbinv
.
While no
{\mathcal{R}_{\PQs}}
dependence on the
\PB
meson rapidity is seen,
a strong variation is observed in the
12<\pt<18
\GeV
range, followed by a flat trend for higher
\pt
values.
The
f_{\PQd}/f_{\PQu}
ratio, measured
for the first time in proton-proton collisions
using the
\PBz\!\to\!\PJGy\,\PKst{}^{0}
decay channel,
is found to be compatible with unity
and independent of rapidity and
\pt
.
This is the first direct measurement of isospin invariance
in
\PB
meson production at hadron colliders.
The
\PQb
hadron
production fractions presented in this Letter also
provide a crucial input to measurements by ATLAS and CMS of the
\PBzs\to\MM
branching fraction.

Generalized Finch and Skea model compatible with observational data
Rinkal Patel, B.S.Ratanpal, D.M.Pandya

Abstract
We present an entirely new class of solutions to Einstein’s field equations that correspond to a static spherically symmetric anisotropic system by generalizing the Finch and Skea ansatz using the linear equation of state for the gravitational potential g r ​ r g_{rr} . Based on physical requirements, regularity condition, and stability, we make various assumptions about the model’s parameters. The exact solution generates a plausible model of a compact star PSR J0348+0432 that satisfies all physical criteria. The requirements for a well-behaved compact star are met, including regularity, equilibrium, causality, stability, energy, and compactness limitations.

Physical Viability and Stability:

Let us now examine the model’s physical viability and stability in light of the following.

6.1
Stability under three different forces

A star maintains static equilibrium in the presence of three forces: gravitational force
(F_{g})
, hydrostatic force
(F_{h})
, and anisotropic force
(F_{a})
. This condition is mathematically expressed as the Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff (TOV)(Tolman
[
57
]
, Oppenheimer and Volkoff
[
58
]
) equation, which is described by the conservation equation provided by

\nabla^{\mu}T_{\mu\nu}=0.

(32)

Now using the expression given in equation (
3
) into (
32
), one can obtain the following
equation:

\frac{-\nu^{\prime}}{2}(\rho+p_{r})-\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}+\frac{2}{r}(p_{\perp}-p_{r})=0.

(33)

This equation (
33
) can be written as

F_{g}+F_{h}+F_{a}=0,

(34)

where

F_{g}=\frac{-\nu^{\prime}}{2}(\rho+p_{r}),

F_{h}=-\frac{dp_{r}}{dr},

F_{a}=\frac{2}{r}(p_{\perp}-p_{r}).

The Fig.(
18
) shows that hydrostatics and anisotropic force are positive, but gravitational force is negative to keep the system in static equilibrium.

6.2
Adiabatic index for stability

The adiabatic index which is defined as

\Gamma=\left(\frac{\rho+p_{r}}{p_{r}}\right)\frac{dp_{r}}{d\rho}

(35)

is related to the stability of a relativistic anisotropic stellar configuration.
Any stellar configuration will remain stable if the adiabatic index is greater than 4/3, which physically characterises the stiffness of the EoS for a given density. Chandrasekhar
[
59
]
was the first to investigate this, and he demonstrated, using Eq.(
35
) in his work, that under general relativity, the Newtonian lower limit
\frac{4}{3}
has a significant effect. Later, several other researcher Heintzmann and Hillebrandt
[
60
]
, Hillebrandt and Steinmetz
[
61
]
, Barreto et al.
[
62
]
, Chan et al.
[
63
]
, Doneva and Yazadjiev
[
64
]
, Moustakidis
[
65
]
investigated the adiabatic index within a dynamically stable stellar system in the presence of an infinitesimal radial adiabatic perturbation.
In Fig.(
19
), we have shown the nature of Relativistic adiabatic index variation graphically for the star PSR J0348+0432 with different values of ’n’. Moreover the profile is monotonic increasing as well as greater than 4/3 everywhere inside the stellar interior.

6.3
Herrera condition for stability

We also know that the velocity of sound (both radial and transverse) must be less than the speed of light for a physically viable model, which is known as the causality requirement.
The velocity of sound, which is given by
v_{s}^{2}=\frac{dp}{d\rho}
, is one method for quantifying dense materials.
where p is the pressure and
\rho
is the matter density.
According to Lattimer and Prakash
[
66
]
causality requires an absolute limit on

v_{s}^{2}\leq 1
, and thermodynamic stability ensures that
v_{s}^{2}\geq 0.

There are two possible stable and two possible unstable regions: one with a positive gradient of anisotropy with respect to the radial variable r and other with a negative gradient. According to Abreu et al.
[
67
]
, it is obvious
0\leq\frac{dp_{r}}{dr}\leq 1,\;\;0\leq\frac{dp_{\perp}}{dr}\leq 1.

i.e
0\leq|v_{s\perp}^{2}-v_{sr}^{2}|\leq 1.

-1\leq|v_{s\perp}^{2}-v_{sr}^{2}|\leq 1\Rightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\ -1\leq|v_{s\perp}^{2}-v_{sr}^{2}|\leq 0&\mbox{potentially stable}\\
0\leq|v_{s\perp}^{2}-v_{sr}^{2}|\leq 1&\mbox{potentially unstable}\end{array}\right.

(36)

As a result, we may now investigate possible stable/unstable categories inside anisotropic models based on differences in sound propagation within the matter configuration. Those areas
v_{sr}^{2}\geq v_{s\perp}^{2}
that will be stable. On the other hand, if
v_{sr}^{2}\leq v_{s\perp}^{2}
everywhere within a matter distribution, no cracking will occur. From Fig.(
6
) we can observe that the graph of
\frac{dp_{r}}{d\rho}
is straight line at 0.2, which indicates
\frac{dp_{r}}{d\rho}=A
and A is taken 0.2, which is lies between 0 and 1. From Fig.(
7
) we observed that the graph of
\frac{dp_{\perp}}{d\rho}
, which is lies in range of 0 to 1. It is also noticed that from the Fig.(
20
), the matter configuration relation is stable as aforementioned since
v_{s\perp}^{2}-v_{sr}^{2}\leq 0
everywhere inside the star.

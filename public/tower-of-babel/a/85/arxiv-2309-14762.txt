GH-convergence of CAT ( 0 ) (0) -spaces: stability of the Euclidean factor
Nicola Cavallucci

Abstract
. We prove that if a sequence of geodesically complete CAT ( 0 ) (0) -spaces X j X_{j} with uniformly cocompact discrete groups of isometries converges in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense to X ∞ X_{\infty} , then the dimension of the maximal Euclidean factor splitted off by X ∞ X_{\infty} and X j X_{j} is the same, for j j big enough. In other words, no additional Euclidean factors can appear in the limit.



1.
Introduction

Given a CAT
(0)
-space
X
we denote by
\text{Eucl-rk}(X)
the dimension of the maximal Euclidean factor of
X
, i.e. the maximal
k
for which
X
splits metrically as
Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
: it is called the
Euclidean rank
of
X
.
The aim of this paper is to prove the following stability result.

Theorem A
.

Let
X_{j}
be a sequence of proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-spaces with discrete and
D_{0}
-cocompact groups
\Gamma_{j}<\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
. Suppose
X_{j}
converges in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to
X_{\infty}
. Then

\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{\infty})=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{j}).

The pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence needs basepoints on the spaces
X_{j}
and
X_{\infty}
in order to be defined. However, under the assumptions above, the limit
X_{\infty}
does not depend on the choice of the basepoints, see Section
2.1
. Theorem
A
is simpler if we assume that each
G_{j}
is
torsion-free
. Under this assumption, and more generally under the
nonsingularity
of the
\Gamma_{j}
’s, it was proved true by the author and A.Sambusetti in
[
CS23
, Corollary 7.17]
.

We will show a stronger result.

Theorem B
.

Let
X_{j}
be a sequence of proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-spaces with closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and
D_{0}
-cocompact groups
G_{j}<\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
. If
X_{j}
converges in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to
X_{\infty}
then

\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{\infty})=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{j}).

Theorem
B
will be consequence of the precise description of the behaviour of the groups
G_{j}
as in the statement given in
[
Cav23
]
. We remark that the difficulty in proving Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
is substantially the same. The motivations for that are well described in
[
Cav23
]
, in which it is shown that, even if the groups
G_{j}
’s are discrete, the limit
G_{\infty}<\textup{Isom}(X_{\infty})
is in general non-discrete. However it is always totally disconnected, provided the sequence
G_{j}
is non-collapsed. In
[
Cav23
]
it is proved that every sequence
G_{j}
as above can be transformed in a non-collapsed one without changing the isometry type of the spaces
X_{j}
, we refer to Section
2.2
for the details. Another motivation for studying the totally disconnected, unimodular case is provided by the decomposition of
[
CM09b
]
, where these groups appear naturally. We will come back to this at the end of the introduction.

Our proof of Theorem
B
will follow the same scheme of the proof of Theorem
A
in the torsion-free case given in
[
CS23
]
, overcoming the complications resulting from the total disconnection of the groups. One of the missing ingredients is an analogue, in our situation, of
[
CM19
, Theorem 2]
, which characterizes the Euclidean rank of a proper CAT
(0)
-space with a uniform lattice
\Gamma
as the maximal rank of a free abelian commensurated subgroup of
\Gamma
. We define the
abelian rank
of a locally compact group
G
as the maximal rank of an almost abelian, almost commensurated subgroup and we denote it by
\textup{Ab-rk}(G)
. The notions of almost abelianity and almost commensurability has been introduced in
[
Cav23
]
, see also Section
2.3
for more details. The generalization of
[
CM19
, Theorem 2]
is the following.

Theorem C
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-space and let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and cocompact. Then
\textup{Eucl-rk}(X)=\textup{Ab-rk}(G)
.

This allows to approach Theorem
B
by using the abelian rank. Ultimately what we will show is the next result.

Theorem D
.

Let
X_{j}
be a sequence of proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-spaces with closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and
D_{0}
-cocompact groups
G_{j}<\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
. Suppose
(X_{j}.G_{j})
converges in the equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to
(X_{\infty},G_{\infty})
with
G_{\infty}
totally disconnected. Then

\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{\infty})=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{j}).

A natural question is the following: what happens if we have a sequence of proper, geodesically complete,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-spaces
X_{j}
converging to
X_{\infty}
and we only know that
\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
is
D_{0}
-cocompact and unimodular for every
j
?
Here the unimodularity assumption of
\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
is very natural and it is satisfied as soon as
\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
possesses a lattice. The next example shows that in general the limit space
X_{\infty}
can split more Euclidean factors.

Example
.

Let
M
be a symmetric space of non-compact type equipped with a fixed Riemannian metric
g
of non-positive curvature. Notice that
\textup{Isom}(M)
is unimodular and transitive, so
0
-cocompact. Consider now the metric
\lambda g
on
M
with
\lambda\geq 1
: it is again a geodesically complete
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space whose isometry group is unimodular and
0
-cocompact. For
\lambda
going to
+\infty
this sequence of spaces converges in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
n
is the dimension of
M
.

Observe that there are no lattices with uniform codiameter along the spaces of this sequence, so the assumptions of Theorem
A
are not satisfied.

However, using the decomposition results of
[
CM09b
]
we also cited before, we can prove that the above example is in some sense the only thing that can happen.

Theorem E
.

Let
X_{j}
be a sequence of proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-spaces with
\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
unimodular and
D_{0}
-cocompact. Suppose
X_{j}
converges in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to
X_{\infty}
. Then each
X_{j}
splits as
M_{j}\times\mathbb{R}^{k_{j}}\times N_{j}
, where
M_{j}
is a symmetric space of non-compact type,
k_{j}=\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{j})
and
\textup{Isom}(N_{j})
is totally disconnected and unimodular. Moreover for every subsequence
\{j_{k}\}
for which
M_{j_{h}}
converges to a space
M_{\infty}
we have

\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{\infty})=\textup{Eucl-rk}(M_{\infty})+\lim_{h\to+\infty}\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{j_{h}}).

We end the introduction with a classical rigidity application of stability results as Theorem
B
, when coupled with some form of compactness.

Theorem F
.

Let
P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}>0
. Then there exists
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})>0
such that the following holds true. Let
X,Y
be proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-spaces with closed, totally disconnected, unimodular,
D_{0}
-cocompact groups
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
and
H<\textup{Isom}(Y)
. Let
x\in X
and
y\in Y
be points. If
d_{\textup{GH}}(\overline{B}(x,1/\varepsilon),\overline{B}(y,1/\varepsilon))\leq\varepsilon
then
\textup{Eucl-rk}(X)=\textup{Eucl-rk}(Y)
.

Here
d_{\textup{GH}}
denotes the Gromov-Hausdorff distance on the class of compact metric spaces up to isometry. Essentially if two space as in the statement have balls
of fixed radius
which are similar
up to a fixed error
then they must split the same Euclidean factor. This is an istance of a general principle: the geometric information of spaces as in the statement of Theorem
F
should be detected
looking at a fixed scale
. We will not pursue this naive idea further in this paper.

2.
Notation and recalls

We recall the essential tools we will need throughout the paper.

2.1.
\textup{CAT}(0)
-spaces, packing and isometries

In the paper
X
will denote a
proper
metric space with distance
d
.
A
geodesic
in
X
is an isometry
c\colon[a,b]\to X
, where
[a,b]
is an interval of
\mathbb{R}
. The
endpoints
of the geodesic
c
are the points
c(a)
and
c(b)
; a geodesic with endpoints
x,y\in X
is also denoted by
[x,y]
.
A
geodesic line
is an isometry
c\colon\mathbb{R}\to X
.
A metric space
X
is called
geodesic
if for every two points
x,y\in X
there is a geodesic with endpoints
x
and
y
.

A metric space
X
is called CAT
(0)
if it is geodesic and every geodesic triangle
\Delta(x,y,z)
is thinner than its Euclidean comparison triangle
\overline{\Delta}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
: that is, for any couple of points
p\in[x,y]
and
q\in[x,z]
we have
d(p,q)\leq d(\bar{p},\bar{q})
where
\bar{p},\bar{q}
are the corresponding points in
\overline{\Delta}(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
(see for instance
[
BH13
]
for the basics of CAT
(0)
-geometry).
A CAT
(0)
-space is
uniquely geodesic
: for every
x,y\in X
there exists a unique geodesic with endpoints
x
and
y
.

A CAT
(0)
-metric space
X
is
geodesically complete
if every geodesic can be extended to a geodesic line.

Let
\text{Isom}(X)
be the group of isometries of
X
, endowed with the compact-open topology: as
X
is proper, it is a topological, locally compact,
\sigma
-compact group.
A subgroup
G
is
totally disconnected
if it is totally disconnected as a subset of Isom
(X)
(with respect to the compact-open topology).
A closed group
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
is said to be
cocompact
if the quotient metric space
G\backslash X
is compact; in this case, we call
codiameter
of
G
the diameter of the quotient, and we will say that
G
is
D_{0}
-cocompact if it has codiameter at most
D_{0}
.
The
translation length
of
g\in\text{Isom}(X)
is by definition
\ell(g):=\inf_{x\in X}d(x,gx).

When the infimum is realized, the isometry
g
is called
elliptic
if
\ell(g)=0
and
hyperbolic
otherwise. The
free-systole
of a group
G<\textup{Isom}(X)

at a point

x\in X
is

\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,x):=\inf_{g\in G\setminus G^{\diamond}}d(x,gx),

where
G^{\diamond}
is the subset of all elliptic isometries of
G
. The
free-systole of

G
is accordingly defined as

\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,X)=\inf_{x\in X}\text{sys}^{\diamond}(G,x).

The next splitting theorem is due to P.E.Caprace and N.Monod.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space such that
\textup{Isom}(X)
is cocompact and unimodular. Then
X
splits metrically as
M\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times N
in such a way that
\textup{Isom}(X)=S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D
, with
\textup{Isom}(M)=S
,
\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})=\mathcal{E}_{k}
and
\textup{Isom}(N)=D
. Moreover

(a)

S
is an almost connected, semi-simple Lie group with no compact factor and trivial center,
M
is a simply connected, non-positively curved, symmetric Riemannian manifold;

(b)

k=\textup{Eucl-rk}(X)
;

(c)

D
is locally compact, totally disconnected, unimodular and cocompact with codiameter at most the codiameter of
\textup{Isom}(X)
.

Proof.

The action of
\textup{Isom}(X)
on
X
is minimal, i.e. there are no proper, convex, closed, invariant subsets, since
X
is geodesically complete and
\textup{Isom}(X)
is cocompact (cp.
[
CM09b
, Proposition 1.5]
). By
[
CM13
, Theorem M]
the fixed points at infinity of
\textup{Isom}(X)
are contained in the boundary of the Euclidean factor of
X
, by unimodularity. Therefore
\textup{Isom}(X)
has no fixed points at infinity since the isometries of the Euclidean factor have none. Hence
[
CM09b
, Theorem 1.6 and Addendum 1.8]
gives a canonical
\textup{Isom}(X)
-invariant splitting of
X
as
M\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times N
, where
M
and
S=\textup{Isom}(M)
are as in (a),
k=\textup{Eucl-rk}(X)
and
D=\textup{Isom}(N)
is totally disconnected. The fact that
\textup{Isom}(X)
splits as
S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D
follows for instance from
[
BH13
, Proposition I.5.3.(4)]
because the splitting is
\textup{Isom}(X)
-invariant.
D
is also cocompact with codiameter bounded by the codiameter of
\textup{Isom}(X)
, being the projection of
\textup{Isom}(X)
on
\textup{Isom}(N)
.
By
[
NB65
, Corollary p.85]
the product of locally compact groups is unimodular if and only if the factors are unimodular. Since
\textup{Isom}(X)
is unimodular we deduce that also
D
is unimodular.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

If
X
is a space as in Proposition
2.1
then it is equivalent to require that
\textup{Isom}(X)
is cocompact and unimodular or that there exists
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and cocompact. Indeed if
\textup{Isom}(X)
is unimodular and cocompact then the previous proposition applies and it is enough to take
G=\Gamma\times D
, where
\Gamma
is a lattice in
S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}
. Notice however that it is not possible in general to find such
G
with codiameter bounded in terms of the codiameter of
\textup{Isom}(X)
. This follows by comparing the Example in the introduction with Theorem
B
.

Viceversa if there exists
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
which is closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and cocompact then
\textup{Isom}(X)
is unimodular because the quotient space
\textup{Isom}(X)/G
is compact and
[
BdLHV08
, Corollary B.1.8]
applies. The fact that
\textup{Isom}(X)/G
is compact can be proved as follows. Fix
x_{0}\in X
, let
D
be the codiameter of the action of
G
on
X
and define the compact set
\overline{S}_{D}=\{h\in\textup{Isom}(X)\text{ s.t. }d(x_{0},hx_{0})\leq D\}
. For every
h\in\textup{Isom}(X)
we can find
g\in G
such that
d(hx_{0},gx_{0})\leq D
, i.e.
g^{-1}h\in\overline{S}_{D}
. In other words
\textup{Isom}(X)=G\cdot\overline{S}_{D}
, implying that
\textup{Isom}(X)/G
is compact.

Let
X
be a metric space and
r>0
.
A subset
Y
of
X
is called
r
-separated
if
d(y,y^{\prime})>r
for all
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
.
Given
x\in X
and
0<r\leq R
we denote by Pack
(\overline{B}(x,R),r)
the maximal cardinality of a
2r
-separated subset of
\overline{B}(x,R)
. Moreover we denote by Pack
(R,r)
the supremum of Pack
(\overline{B}(x,R),r)
among all points of
X
. Given
P_{0},r_{0}>0
we say that
X
is
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed
if Pack
(3r_{0},r_{0})\leq P_{0}
.
The packing condition should be thought as a metric, weak replacement of a Ricci curvature lower bound: for more details and examples see
[
CS21
]
and
[
CS20
]
. Moreover every metric space admitting a cocompact group of isometries is packed (for some
P_{0},r_{0}
), see the proof of
[
Cav22a
, Lemma 5.4]
. The packing condition has many interesting geometric consequences for complete, geodesically complete CAT
(0)
-spaces, as showed in
[
CS20
]
,
[
Cav21
]
and
[
Cav22b
]
.

2.2.
Gromov-Hausdorff convergence

An
isometric action
on a pointed space is a triple
(X,x,G)
where
X
is a proper metric space,
x\in X
is a basepoint and
G<\text{Isom}(X)
is a closed subgroup. An
equivariant isometry
between isometric actions of pointed spaces
(X,x,G)
and
(Y,y,H)
is an isometry
F\colon X\to Y
such that

–

F(x)=y
;

–

F_{*}\colon\textup{Isom}(X)\to\textup{Isom}(Y)
defined by
F_{*}(g)=F\circ g\circ F^{-1}
is an isomorphism between
G
and
H
.

The best known notion of convergence for isometric actions of pointed spaces is the
equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff
convergence, as defined by K. Fukaya in
[
Fuk86
]
:
we will write
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},x_{\infty},G_{\infty})

for a sequence
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})
of isometric actions converging in the equivariant pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to an isometric action
(X_{\infty},x_{\infty},G_{\infty})
.

Forgetting about the group actions and considering just pointed metric spaces
(X_{j},x_{j})
, this convergence reduces to the
pointed Gromov-Hausdorff
convergence: we will write
(X_{j},x_{j})\underset{\textup{pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},x_{\infty})
for a sequence of pointed metric spaces
(X_{j},x_{j})
converging to the pointed metric space
(X_{\infty},x_{\infty})
in this sense.
An equivalent approach uses ultralimits. We briefly recall it. A
non-principal ultrafilter

\omega
is a finitely additive measure on
\mathbb{N}
such that
\omega(A)\in\{0,1\}
for every
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
\omega(A)=0
for every finite subset of
\mathbb{N}
. Accordingly, we will write
\omega
-a.s.
or
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
in the usual measure theoretic sense.
Given a bounded sequence
(a_{j})
of real numbers and a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
there exists a unique
a\in\mathbb{R}
such that for every
\varepsilon>0
the set
\{j\in\mathbb{N}\text{ s.t. }|a_{j}-a|<\varepsilon\}
has
\omega
-measure
1
(cp.
[
DK18
, Lemma 10.25]
). The real number
a
is then called
the ultralimit of the sequence
a_{j}
and it is denoted by
\omega
-
\lim a_{j}
.

A non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
being given, one defines the
ultralimit pointed metric space

(X_{\omega},x_{\omega})=\omega
-
\lim(X_{j},x_{j})
of any sequence of pointed metric spaces
(X_{j},x_{j})
:

– first, one says that a sequence
(y_{j})
, where
y_{j}\in X_{j}
for every
j
, is
admissible
if there exists
M
such that
d(x_{j},y_{j})\leq M
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
;

– then, one
defines
(X_{\omega},x_{\omega})
as set of admissible sequences
(y_{j})
modulo the relation
(y_{j})\sim(y_{j}^{\prime})
if and only if
\omega
-
\lim d(y_{j},y_{j}^{\prime})=0
.

The point of
X_{\omega}
defined by the class of the sequence
(y_{j})
is denoted by
y_{\omega}=\omega
-
\lim y_{j}
.
Finally, the formula
d(\omega
-
\lim y_{j},\omega
-
\lim y_{j}^{\prime})=\omega
-
\lim d(y_{j},y_{j}^{\prime})
defines a metric on
X_{\omega}
which is called the ultralimit distance on
X_{\omega}
.

Using a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
, one can also talk of limits of isometries and of isometry groups of pointed metric spaces. A sequence of isometries
g_{j}
of pointed metric spaces
(X_{j},x_{j})
is
admissible
if there exists
M\geq 0
such that
d(x_{j},g_{j}x_{j})\leq M

\omega
-a.s. Every such sequence defines a limit isometry
g_{\omega}=\omega
-
\lim g_{j}
of
X_{\omega}=\omega
-
\lim(X_{j},x_{j})
by the formula:
g_{\omega}y_{\omega}=\omega
-
\lim g_{j}y_{j}
(
[
DK18
, Lemma 10.48]
).
Given a sequence of groups
G_{j}<\text{Isom}(X_{j})
we set

G_{\omega}=\{\omega\text{-}\lim g_{j}\text{ s.t. }g_{j}\in G_{j}\text{ for }\omega\text{-a.e.}(j)\}.

In particular the elements of
G_{\omega}
are ultralimits of admissible sequences.
One has a well-defined composition law on
G_{\omega}
(
[
Cav22a
, Lemma 3.7]
): if
g_{\omega}=\omega
-
\lim g_{j}
and
h_{\omega}=\omega
-
\lim h_{j}
we set
g_{\omega}\,\circ\,h_{\omega}:=\omega\text{-}\lim(g_{j}\circ h_{j}).

With this operation
G_{\omega}
becomes a group of isometries of
X_{\omega}
, which we call
the ultralimit group
of the sequence of groups
G_{j}
.
Notice that if
X_{\omega}
is proper then
G_{\omega}
is always a closed subgroup of isometries of
X_{\omega}

[
Cav22a
, Proposition 3.8]
.

In conclusion, a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
being given, for any sequence of isometric actions on pointed spaces
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})
there exists an
ultralimit isometric action
on a pointed space

(X_{\omega},x_{\omega},G_{\omega})=\omega\text{-}\lim(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j}).

The ultralimit approach and the Gromov-Hausdorff convergence are essentially equivalent.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Cav22a
]
, Proposition 3.13 & Corollary 3.14)
.

Let
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})
be a sequence of isometric actions of pointed spaces:

(i)

if
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},x_{\infty},G_{\infty})
, then
(X_{\omega},x_{\omega},G_{\omega})\cong(X_{\infty},x_{\infty},G_{\infty})
for every non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
;

(ii)

reciprocally, if
\omega
is a non-principal ultrafilter and
(X_{\omega},x_{\omega})
is proper, then
(X_{j_{k}},x_{j_{k}},G_{j_{k}})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\omega},x_{\omega},G_{\omega})
for some subsequence
\{{j_{k}}\}
.

Moreover, if for every non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
the ultralimit
(X_{\omega},x_{\omega},G_{\omega})
is equivariantly isometric to the same isometric action
(X,x,G)
, with
X
proper, then
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X,x,G)
.

The sequence
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})
is called
D
-cocompact
if each
G_{j}
is
D
-cocompact.
The ultralimit of a sequence of isometric actions on pointed spaces does not depend on the choice of the basepoints,
provided that the actions have uniformly bounded codiameter (cp.
[
CS23
, Lemma 7.3]
). Therefore, when considering the convergence of uniformily cocompact isometric actions,
we will often omit the basepoints.

Given three parameters
P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}>0
we denote by
\textup{CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td-u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
the class of triples
(X,x,G)
such that:

-

X
is a proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space;

-

x\in X
is a basepoint;

-

G<\textup{Isom}(X)
is a closed, totally disconnected, unimodular,
D_{0}
-cocompact group.

We also set
\textup{CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td-u}}(D_{0})=\bigcup_{P_{0},r_{0}>0}\textup{CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td-u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
.
We can always assume to have a uniform packing, as soon as we have pointed Gromov-Hausdorff convergence.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
CS23
, Proposition 7.5]
)
.

Suppose to have
X_{j}\in\textup{CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td-u}}(D_{0})
such that
X_{j}\underset{\textup{pGH}}{\longrightarrow}X_{\infty}
. Then
X_{j}\subseteq\textup{CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td-u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, for some
P_{0},r_{0}>0
. Moreover there exists
G_{\infty}<\textup{Isom}(X_{\infty})
such that, up to passing to a subsequence,
(X_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},G_{\infty})
.

Definition 2.5

(Standard setting of convergence)
.

We say that we are in the
standard setting of convergence
when we have a sequence

(X_{j},G_{j})

in
\textup{CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td-u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
such that

(X_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},G_{\infty})
.

The standard setting of convergence (or simply the sequence) is called:

-

non-collapsed
if
\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\textup{sys}^{\diamond}(G_{j},X_{j})>0
;

-

collapsed
if
\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\textup{sys}^{\diamond}(G_{j},X_{j})=0
.

Every collapsed sequence can be transformed in a non-collapsed one without changing the isometry type of the spaces.

Proposition 2.6

(
[
Cav23
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Let
P_{0},r_{0},D_{0}>0
. Then there exists
\Delta_{0}>0
such that the following holds true. Suppose
(X_{j},G_{j})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},G_{\infty})
with
(X_{j},G_{j})

in
\textup{CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td-u}}(P_{0},r_{0},D_{0})
. Then there exist
G_{j}^{\prime}<\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
,
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
such that
(X_{j},G_{j}^{\prime})\in\textup{CAT}_{0}^{\textup{td-u}}(P_{0},r_{0},\Delta_{0})
and
(X_{j},G_{j}^{\prime})\underset{\textup{eq-pGH}}{\longrightarrow}(X_{\infty},G_{\infty}^{\prime})
without collapsing.

By the characterization of collapsing sequences given in
[
Cav23
]
we have the following.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
Cav23
, Theorems 6.21 & 6.22]
)
.

In the standard setting of convergence we have that
G_{\infty}
is totally disconnected if and only if the sequence is non-collapsed. Moreover if it is the case then it is also unimodular.

Another important feature of non-collapsed sequences is the compactness of subgroups generated by small isometries.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
Cav23
, Corollary 6.13 and §6.4]
)
.

In the non-collapsed standard setting of convergence there exists
\sigma>0
such that for every
y_{j}\in X_{j}
the group generated by
\{g_{j}\in G_{j}\text{ s.t. }d(y_{j},g_{j}y_{j})\leq\sigma\}
is compact.

We end this section by explicitating the key lemma of the convergence theory developed in
[
CS23
]
and
[
Cav23
]
. It is a deep result, despite its proof is a straightforward consequence of
[
Cav23
]
. A direct proof of it without the machinery developed there is out of reach for the author.

Lemma 2.9
.

Suppose to be in the standard setting of convergence. Let
g_{j}\in G_{j}
be a sequence of admissible isometries defining the limit isometry
g_{\infty}\in G_{\infty}
. If
g_{j}
is elliptic for every
j
then
g_{\infty}
is elliptic.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
we can fix a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
and work with the ultralimit of the sequence
(X_{j},x_{j},G_{j})
.
The admissible condition means that
d(g_{j}x_{j},x_{j})\leq M
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
for some finite
M
. Let
y_{j}
be the projection of
x_{j}
on
\text{Fix}(g_{j})
. By
[
Cav23
, Corollary 6.17.(ii)]
we have
d(x_{j},y_{j})\leq N_{0}\cdot M
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
, where
N_{0}
is a constant depending only on
P_{0},r_{0}
and
D_{0}
. This means that the sequence
(y_{j})
is admissible and defines a limit point
y_{\omega}
which is fixed by
g_{\omega}
, so
g_{\omega}
is elliptic.
∎

2.3.
Almost abelian groups

In this part we recall the definitions of almost abelian and almost commensurated subgroup, as introduced in
[
Cav23
]
.

Definition 2.10
.

A locally compact group
G
is
almost abelian
if there exists a compact, open, normal subgroup
N\triangleleft G
such that
G/N
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually abelian.

For instance if a locally compact group
G
has a continuous surjective homomorphism
G\to\mathbb{Z}^{k}
with compact kernel then it is almost abelian.
Every almost abelian group has a well defined rank.

Definition 2.11

(
[
Cav23
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
G
be an almost abelian group. The rank of
G
, denoted by
\textup{rk}(G)
, is the unique integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that the group
G/N
is discrete, finitely generated and virtually abelian of rank
k
, for every compact, open, normal subgroup
N\triangleleft G
.

We now present an example of almost abelian groups. We will use this criterion in the proof of Theorem
D
.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
A
be a locally compact, compactly generated, totally disconnected group such that
\overline{[A,A]}
is compact. Then
A
is almost abelian.

Proof.

The group
\overline{[A,A]}
is compact and normal in
A
, so
A/\overline{[A,A]}
is a locally compact, compactly generated, totally disconnected, abelian group. By the classification of such groups (cp.
[
Mor77
, Theorem 24, p.85]
) it is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}\times K
for some
k\geq 0
and
K
compact. The subgroup
\{\textup{id}\}\times K
is open, compact and normal in
A/\overline{[A,A]}
. Let
\hat{K}
be the preimage of
K
under the projection map
\pi:A\to A/\overline{[A,A]}
. Then
\hat{K}
is open in
A
because
\pi
is continuous. It is also compact because the kernel of
\pi
is compact. Finally it is normal in
A
. Since
A/\hat{K}
is topologically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, then
A
is almost abelian.
∎

We recall that a subgroup
H
of a topological group
G
is cocompact if the space
G/H
is compact when endowed with the quotient topology.
Let
G
be a topological group and let
H<G
be a closed subgroup.
H
is said to be
almost commensurated
if the group
H\cap gHg^{-1}
is cocompact in both
H
and
gHg^{-1}
, for all
g\in G
. We recall the following fact.

Lemma 2.13

(
[
Cav23
, Lemma 4.2]
)
.

Let
G
be a topological group generated by a subset
S
. Let
H<G
be a closed subgroup. Suppose
H\cap sHs^{-1}
is cocompact in both
H
and
sHs^{-1}
for every
s\in S
. Then
H
is almost commensurated.

Almost abelian almost commensurated subgroups of isometries of CAT
(0)
-groups induce a splitting of the space in the following sense.

Proposition 2.14

(
[
Cav23
, Proposition 4.5]
)
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
CAT
(0)
-space and let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected and cocompact. Let
A<G
be a closed, almost abelian, almost commensurated subgroup of rank
k
. Then
X
splits isometrically and
G
-invariantly as
X=Y\times\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Moreover almost abelian groups act as lattices on some convex subsets.

Proposition 2.15

(
[
Cav23
, Proposition 3.12]
)
.

Let
X
be a proper, geodesically complete,
\textup{CAT}(0)
-space and let
G<\textup{Isom}(X)
be closed, totally disconnected and cocompact. Let
A<G
be an almost abelian subgroup of rank
k
. Then there exists a closed, convex,
A
-invariant subset
Y\subset X
isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
such that
A
acts a lattice on

.

2.4.
Lattices in Euclidean spaces

Euclidean spaces play a special role in the class of our
\textup{CAT}(0)
-spaces, as well highlighted in
[
CS23
]
and
[
Cav23
]
.
We will denote points in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
by a bold letter
v
and the origin with
{\bf O}
. A lattice of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a discrete, free abelian, cocompact group of isometries of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
.
It is well known that a lattice must act by translations on
\mathbb{R}^{k}
(see for instance
[
Far81
]
); so, alternatively, a lattice
\mathcal{L}
can be seen as the set of linear combinations with integer coefficients of
k
independent vectors
\bf b_{1},\ldots,\bf b_{k}
. A
basis

\mathcal{B}=\{\bf b_{1},\ldots,\bf b_{k}\}
of a lattice
\mathcal{L}
is a set of
k
independent vectors that generate
\mathcal{L}
as a group. Given a basis
\mathcal{B}
of a lattice
\mathcal{L}
and an element
{\bf v}\in\mathcal{L}
we denote by
\ell_{\mathcal{B}}({\bf v})
the classical word length of
{\bf v}
with respect to the generating set
\mathcal{B}
. We also set
\lambda(\mathcal{B}):=\max_{i}\|{\bf b_{i}}\|
.

There are many geometric invariants associated to a lattice
\mathcal{L}
, for instance:

– the
shortest generating radius
, that is

\lambda(\mathcal{L})=\inf\{r>0\text{ s.t. }\mathcal{L}\text{ contains }k\text{ independent vectors of length}\leq r\};

– the
shortest vector
, that is

\tau(\mathcal{L})=\inf\left\{\|{\bf v}\|\text{ s.t. }{\bf v}\in\mathcal{L}\setminus\{{\bf O}\}\right\}.

Notice that
\tau(\mathcal{L})
coincides with the free-systole of
\mathcal{L}
, while the codiameter of
\mathcal{L}
is at most
\sqrt{k}\cdot\lambda(\mathcal{L})
(cp. for instance
[
CS23
, §2.3]
). The notation is coherent in the sense that
\lambda(\mathcal{L})=\min_{\mathcal{B}\text{ basis of }\mathcal{L}}\lambda(\mathcal{B})
.

In the sequel we will need the next uniform estimate.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\tau,\lambda>0
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
R\geq 0
. There exists a constant
C=C(\tau,\lambda,k,R)
such that the following holds true. Let
\mathcal{L}
e a lattice of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with
\tau(\mathcal{L})\geq\tau
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be a basis of
\mathcal{L}
with
\lambda(\mathcal{B})\leq\lambda
. Let
{\bf v}\in\mathcal{L}
be an element with
\|{\bf v}\|\leq R
. Then
\ell_{\mathcal{B}}({\bf v})\leq C
.

Proof.

Suppose the thesis is not true and find lattices
\mathcal{L}_{j}
of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
, all satisfying
\tau(\mathcal{L}_{j})\geq\tau
, bases
\mathcal{B}_{j}=\{{\bf b_{1,j}},\ldots{\bf b_{k,j}}\}
of
\mathcal{L}_{j}
with
\lambda(\mathcal{B}_{j})\leq\lambda
and vectors
{\bf v_{j}}\in\mathcal{L}_{j}
with
\|{\bf v_{j}}\|\leq R
such that
\ell_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}({\bf v_{j}})\geq j
. As recalled above the groups
\mathcal{L}_{j}
have all codiameter at most
\sqrt{k}\cdot\lambda
and free-systole at least
\tau
. Fix a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
. A very special case of
[
CS23
, Theorem 7.10.(ii)]
says that the limit group
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
is again a lattice of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
and that the natural projection map
\pi_{j}\colon\mathcal{L}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{L}_{j}
,
{\bf v_{\omega}}=\omega
-
\lim{\bf v_{j}}\mapsto{\bf v_{j}}
is an isomorphism of groups for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. The sequences
{\bf b_{i,j}}
are admissible and define isometries
{\bf b_{i,\omega}}\in\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
. Since
\pi_{j}
is an isomorphism then
\mathcal{B}_{\omega}=\{{\bf b_{1,\omega}},\ldots{\bf b_{k,\omega}}\}
is a basis of
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
. The vector
{\bf v_{\omega}}:=\omega
-
\lim{\bf v_{j}}
is a well defined element of
\mathcal{L}_{\omega}
. Therefore it can be written as product of elements of
\mathcal{B}_{\omega}
, say
{\bf v_{\omega}}=\beta_{1}{\bf b_{1,\omega}}+\cdots+\beta_{k}{\bf b_{k,\omega}}
. Since
\pi_{j}
is an isomorphism then
{\bf v_{j}}=\beta_{1}{\bf b_{1,j}}+\cdots+\beta_{k}{\bf b_{k,j}}
, i.e.
\ell_{\mathcal{B}_{j}}({\bf v_{j}})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\beta_{i}|<j
if
j
is big enough, giving the contradiction.
∎

3.
The proof of Theorem
C

In this section we assume that
X
is a proper, geodesically complete, CAT
(0)
-space
X
and
G<\text{Isom}(X)
is closed, totally disconnected, unimodular and cocompact. Remark
2.2
says that
\textup{Isom}(X)
is unimodular and cocompact, so Proposition
2.1
applies. In particular
\textup{Isom}(X)=S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D
, where
S
is a semi-simple Lie group with trivial center and no compact factor,
\mathcal{E}_{k}=\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
and
D
is locally compact and totally disconnected. We generalize
[
CM19
, Lemma 6]
to the totally disconnected case.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D
with
S
semi-simple Lie group with trivial center and no compact factor,
\mathcal{E}_{k}=\textup{Isom}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
and
D
locally compact and totally disconnected.
Then every closed, cocompact, totally disconnected subgroup
G<S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D
commensurates an almost abelian subgroup of
G
of rank
k
.

Proof.

Let
Q<D
be a compact open subgroup. The intersection

G^{*}=G\cap(S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times Q)
is closed and totally disconnected. Indeed
G^{*}
is a closed subgroup of
G
, so its connected component
(G^{*})^{o}
is a closed, connected subgroup of
G
, therefore it must be trivial. Moreover
G^{*}
is cocompact in
S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times Q
. In order to prove it, we consider the continuous and open projection map
p\colon S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D\to(S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D)/G
. The set
p(S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times Q)
is closed in the compact space
(S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D)/G
, so compact as well. Moreover if
g_{1},g_{2}\in S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times Q
are such that
p(g_{1})=p(g_{2})
then
g_{2}^{-1}g_{1}\in G\cap(S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times Q)=G^{*}
. Therefore the induced map
p\colon(S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times Q)/G^{*}\to(S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D)/G
is well defined, continuous, closed and injective. We deduce that
(S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times Q)/G^{*}
is homeomorphic to its image through
p
which is a compact space, showing that
G^{*}
is cocompact in
S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times Q
. Furthermore
G^{*}
is commensurated by
G
. Indeed if
g=(g_{S},g_{\mathcal{E}_{k}},g_{D})\in G
then

gG^{*}g^{-1}\cap G^{*}=G\cap(S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times(g_{D}Qg_{D}^{-1}\cap Q))

and
g_{D}Qg_{D}^{-1}\cap Q
has finite index in
Q
because it is an open subgroup of the compact group
Q
. So
gG^{*}g^{-1}\cap G^{*}
has finite index in
G^{*}
and in
gG^{*}g^{-1}
.

Since
Q
is compact, the projection of
G^{*}
to
S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}
is closed, totally disconnected and cocompact. Indeed let
(g_{j,S},g_{j,\mathcal{E}_{k}},g_{j,Q})
be a sequence of elements of
G^{*}
such that
g_{j,S}
converges to
g_{\infty,S}\in S
and
g_{j,\mathcal{E}_{k}}
converges to
g_{\infty,\mathcal{E}_{k}}
. By compactness of
Q
we can suppose that also
g_{j,Q}
converges to some
g_{\infty,Q}
, up to pass to a subsequence. Therefore the sequence
(g_{j,S},g_{j,\mathcal{E}_{k}},g_{j,Q})
converges to
(g_{\infty,S},g_{\infty,\mathcal{E}_{k}},g_{\infty,Q})\in G^{*}
since
G^{*}
is closed. This shows that
(g_{\infty,S},g_{\infty,\mathcal{E}_{k}})
belongs to
p_{S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}}(G^{*})
, which is then closed. The cocompactness is clear. The group
p_{S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}}(G^{*})
is therefore locally compact, being closed in
S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}
. Since
G^{*}
is totally disconnected then it has a neighbourhood basis at the identity consisting of compact open subgroups. The map
p_{S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}}\colon G^{*}\to p_{S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}}(G^{*})
is a surjective, continuous homomorphism between locally compact groups, so it is open. This implies that also
p_{S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}}(G^{*})
has a neighbourhood basis at the identity consisting of compact open subgroups, so it is totally disconnected. The group
p_{S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}}(G^{*})
is a closed, totally disconnected subgroup of the Lie group
S\times A
, so it must be discrete. Since it is also cocompact it is a uniform lattice.
Upon replacing
G^{*}
by a
finite index subgroup, we may then assume by
[
CM09a
, Lemma 3.4 and 3.5]
that

G^{*}
possesses two normal subgroups
G^{*}_{S}
and
G^{*}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
, both commensurated by
G
, such that

G^{*}=G^{*}_{S}\cdot G^{*}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
and
G^{*}_{S}\cap G^{*}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}\subseteq Q
, where
G^{*}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}=G^{*}\cap(1\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times Q)
is a group whose projection to
\mathcal{E}_{k}
is a lattice.
The group
G^{*}_{\mathcal{E}_{k}}
is almost abelian of rank
k
, indeed it is a locally compact group with a surjective continuous homomorphism with values in
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
whose kernel is compact.
∎

We are ready to provide the

Proof of Theorem
C
.

As explained at the beginning of this section we know that
\textup{Isom}(X)=S\times\mathcal{E}_{k}\times D
, where
k=\textup{Eucl-rk}(X)
. The group
G
is cocompact in
\textup{Isom}(X)
(see Remark
2.2
), so Proposition
3.1
gives an almost abelian subgroup of
G
with rank
k
which is commensurated. This proves that
\textup{Eucl-rk}(X)\leq\textup{Ab-rk}(G)
. On the other hand Proposition
2.14
gives
\textup{Ab-rk}(G)\leq\textup{Eucl-rk}(X)
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Notice that the proof above gives something more: indeed it says that the abelian rank of a group
G
as in the statement coincides with the maximal rank of an almost abelian group which is commensurated, which is stronger than being almost commensurated. However we will not use it.

4.
The proofs of Theorems
D
,
B
and
F

We continue with the

Proof of Theorem
D
.

We are in the standard setting of convergence with basepoints
x_{j}
.
Since we are assuming that
G_{\infty}
is totally disconnected then we are in the non-collapsed case, by Proposition
2.7
. Theorem
C
says that
\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{j})=\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{j})
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
. Moreover if infinitely many
X_{j}
’s split a
\mathbb{R}^{k}
then also
X_{\infty}
splits a
\mathbb{R}^{k}
. This means that
\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{\infty})\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{j})
, and so
\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{\infty})\geq\limsup_{j\to+\infty}\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{j}).

The difficult part is to show that

\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{\infty})\leq\liminf_{j\to+\infty}\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{j}).

In order to do so we fix a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
. By Proposition
2.3
and
[
Cav22a
, Lemma 6.3]
it is enough to establish that

\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{\omega})\leq\omega\text{-}\lim\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{j}).

We set
k:=\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{\omega})
and we fix an almost abelian, almost commensurated subgroup
A<G_{\omega}
of rank
k
. Let
Y\subset X_{\omega}
be a closed, convex,
A
-invariant subset isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{k}
on which
A
acts as a lattice
\mathcal{L}
as in Proposition
2.15
. We fix elements
a_{1,\omega},\ldots,a_{k,\omega}\in A
forming a basis of
\mathcal{L}
. Each isometry
a_{i,\omega}
is by definition the limit of admissible isometries
a_{i,j}\in G_{j}
,
i=1,\ldots,k
.
We set
A_{j}:=\langle a_{1,j},\ldots,a_{k,j}\rangle
and we claim that
\overline{A_{j}}
is an almost abelian subgroup of rank
k
which is almost commensurated in
G_{j}
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. By definition this implies that
\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{j})\geq k
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
concluding the proof.

Step 1:

\overline{[A_{j},A_{j}]}
is compact for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
.

We fix a point
y_{\omega}=\omega
-
\lim y_{j}\in Y
. By assumption the commutators
[a_{i,\omega},a_{\ell,\omega}]
fix the point
y_{\omega}
for
1\leq i,\ell\leq k
. So we have
d([a_{i,j},a_{\ell,j}]y_{j},y_{j})\leq\sigma
for all
1\leq i,\ell\leq k
,
\omega
-a.s. Choosing
\sigma
as in Proposition
2.8
we have that all these commutators belong to the same compact subgroup of
G_{j}
,
\omega
-a.s. Therefore the closure of the group generated by them is compact.

Step 2:

\overline{A_{j}}
is almost abelian for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
.

The proof follows by Lemma
2.12
, once we have shown that
\overline{A_{j}}
is compactly generated and that
\overline{[\overline{A_{j}},\overline{A_{j}}]}
is compact.
The second condition is easier. Indeed by Step 1 we know that there exists a point
z_{j}\in X_{j}
fixed by
[A_{j},A_{j}]
. Now take two elements
g_{j},h_{j}\in\overline{A_{j}}
, so each of them is limit of isometries of
A_{j}
. The commutator
[g_{j},h_{j}]
is limit of commutators of elements of
A_{j}
, so it must fix
z_{j}
too. This shows that
[\overline{A_{j}},\overline{A_{j}}]
fixes the point
z_{j}
, so its closure is compact.

It remains to show that
\overline{A_{j}}
is compactly generated. We will prove that
\overline{A_{j}}=\langle A_{j},\overline{A_{j}}\cap B_{j}\rangle
, where
B_{j}:=\text{Stab}_{G_{j}}(x_{j})
. This would suffice since the set
A_{j}\cup(\overline{A_{j}}\cap B_{j})
is compact. Let
w_{n,j}
be a sequence of elements of
A_{j}
indexed by
n
and converging to some
w_{j}\in G_{j}
. Since the orbits of
G_{j}
are discrete (cp.
[
Cav23
, Theorem 2.6]
) we know that
w_{n,j}x_{j}=w_{j}x_{j}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
, for some
n_{0}
depending on
j
. In particular
w_{n_{0},j}^{-1}w_{n,j}x_{j}=x_{j}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. This means that we can write
w_{n,j}=w_{n_{0},j}\cdot b_{n,j}
for all
n\geq n_{0}
, for some
b_{n,j}\in B_{j}
. Since the sequence
w_{n,j}
converges to

then the sequence

converges to

. Therefore

. Since

we deduce that
w_{j}\in\langle A_{j},\overline{A_{j}}\cap B_{j}\rangle
. This shows that
\overline{A_{j}}\subseteq\langle A_{j},\overline{A_{j}}\cap B_{j}\rangle
. The other inclusion is trivial, so
\overline{A_{j}}=\langle A_{j},\overline{A_{j}}\cap B_{j}\rangle

Step 3:

\textup{rk}(\overline{A_{j}})=k
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
.

\overline{A_{j}}
is almost abelian by Step 2. First of all
\textup{rk}(\overline{A_{j}})\leq k
for every
j
. Indeed let
N_{j}\triangleleft\overline{A_{j}}
be a compact, open, normal subgroup such that
\overline{A_{j}}/N_{j}
is virtually abelian, finitely generated and discrete with rank
=\textup{rk}(\overline{A_{j}})
. The commutator group of
\overline{A_{j}}/N_{j}
is finite, being the image under the natural projection map of the compact group
\overline{[\overline{A_{j}},\overline{A_{j}}]}
. We quotient
\overline{A_{j}}/N_{j}
by its commutator subgroup and we obtain a finitely generated, abelian group of rank
=\textup{rk}(\overline{A_{j}})
on which
\overline{A_{j}}
surjects via a continuous homomorphism. Taking only the non-torsion part of this abelian group we can suppose that
\overline{A_{j}}
surjects via a continuous homomorphism onto
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
,
\ell=\textup{rk}(\overline{A_{j}})
. Since the image of
A_{j}
through this homomorphism is dense then it must be the whole
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
. Hence
\mathbb{Z}^{\ell}
can be generated by
k
elements, so
\textup{rk}(\overline{A_{j}})=\ell\leq k
.

The quantities
\textup{rk}(\overline{A_{j}})
belong to the finite set
\{0,\ldots,k\}
, so there exists
0\leq r\leq k
such that
\textup{rk}(\overline{A_{j}})=r
for
\omega
.a.e.
(j)
. Suppose
r<k
. By Proposition
2.15
we can find a closed, convex,
\overline{A_{j}}
-invariant subset
Y_{j}
of
X_{j}
isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{r}
on which
\overline{A_{j}}
acts as a lattice
\mathcal{L}_{j}
,
\omega
-a.s. The actions of
A_{j}
and
\overline{A_{j}}
coincide on
Y_{j}
, since the latter is discrete. Without loss of generality we can suppose that the first
r
elements
a_{1,j},\ldots,a_{r,j}
form a basis
\mathcal{A}_{j}
of
\mathcal{L}_{j}
. Recalling that the sequence is non-collapsed, i.e. we have a uniform lower bound on the length of every hyperbolic isometry of all the
G_{j}
’s, we can find
\tau>0
such that
\tau(\mathcal{L}_{j})\geq\tau
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. Moreover, since the isometries
a_{i,\omega}
are well defined, we can find
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\ell(a_{i,\omega})\leq\Lambda
for every
i=1,\ldots,k
. It is obvious that
\ell(a_{i,j})\leq 2\Lambda
for
i=1,\ldots,k
, for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. Moreover each
a_{i,j}
attains its minimum on
Y_{j}
by
[
BH13
, Proposition II.6.2]
. This means that
\lambda(\mathcal{A}_{j})\leq 2\Lambda

\omega
-a.s. Since
r<k
the following is true on
Y_{j}
:
a_{k,j}=\Pi_{i=1}^{r}a_{i,j}^{\alpha_{i,j}}
for some
\alpha_{i,j}
,
\omega
-a.s. Now observe that also
a_{k,j}
attains its minimum on
Y_{j}
, and so that
\|a_{k,j}\|\leq 2\Lambda
on
Y_{j}\cong\mathbb{R}^{r}
. We are in position to apply Proposition
2.16
: there exists a constant
C
, that does not depend on

, such that
\ell_{\mathcal{A}_{j}}(a_{k,j})\leq C
. This means in particular that we can choose the
\alpha_{i,j}
’s satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\alpha_{i,j}|\leq C
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. By triangle inequality the sequence of isometries
h_{j}:=a_{k,j}^{-1}\cdot\Pi_{i=1}^{r}a_{i,j}^{\alpha_{i,j}}
is admissible and defines the limit isometry
h_{\omega}=a_{k,\omega}^{-1}\cdot\Pi_{i=1}^{r}a_{i,\omega}^{\alpha_{i,\omega}}
, where
\alpha_{i,\omega}=\omega
-
\lim\alpha_{i,j}
. Moreover the

’s are all elliptic, so
h_{\omega}
is elliptic by Lemma
2.9
. But

belongs to
A
, so it attains its minimum on the closed, convex, invariant set

, again by
[
BH13
, Proposition II.6.2]
. Since
A
acts as a lattice on

, the only possibility is that

on
Y
. This means that
a_{k,\omega}=\Pi_{i=1}^{r}a_{i,\omega}^{\alpha_{i,\omega}}
on

\{a_{1,\omega},\ldots,a_{k,\omega}\}
generates a

\lim\overline{A_{j}}<G_{\omega}

In the proof of Step 2 we showed that every element

and an element of the compact group

, i.e.
g_{j}=\Pi_{i=1}^{k}a_{i,j}^{\alpha_{i,j}}\cdot b_{j}
with

g_{j}
with
g_{j}\in\overline{A_{j}}

for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. Since

fixes
x_{j}
this means
d(x_{j},\Pi_{i=1}^{k}a_{i,j}^{\alpha_{i,j}}x_{j})\leq M
for
\omega
-a.e.
(j)
. As in the proof of Step 3 take a subset
Y_{j}
of

\mathbb{R}^{k}
on which
\overline{A_{j}}
acts as a lattice

with

and basis

-a.e.

Theorem

Proof of Theorem
B
.

First of all we can suppose, up to passing to a subsequence, to be in the standard setting of convergence by Proposition
2.4
. Moreover by Proposition
2.6
we can change the action of the groups
G_{j}
without changing the spaces
X_{j}
in order to be in the non-collapsed standard setting of convergence. Observe that this change does not affect the thesis which involves only geometric properties of the spaces. By Proposition
2.7
the limit group
G_{\infty}
is totally disconnected. Theorem
C
says that
\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{j})=\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{j})
for every

. On the other hand Theorem
D
guarantees that
\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{\infty})=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\textup{Ab-rk}(G_{j})
. Therefore we conclude that
\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{\infty})=\lim_{j\to+\infty}\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{j})
.
∎

Theorem
A
is a particular case of Theorem
B
.

Proof of Theorem
F
.

If it is not the case we can find proper, geodesically complete,
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed spaces
X_{j},Y_{j}
with closed, totally disconnected, unimodular,
D_{0}
-cocompact groups
G_{j}<\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
and

and points

such that

but
\textup{Eucl-rk}(X_{j})\neq\textup{Eucl-rk}(Y_{j})
. By Proposition
2.4
the sequences

and

converge in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense to some spaces

We conclude with the proof of Theorem

Proof of Theorem
A
.

There exist

such that each

is
(P_{0},r_{0})
-packed (see for instance the proof of

with

symmetric Riemannian manifolds of non-positive curvature,

and
\textup{Isom}(N_{j})
totally disconnected and unimodular. Since
\textup{Isom}(X_{j})
is
D_{0}
-cocompact, so it is
\textup{Isom}(N_{j})
. Call
Y_{j}=\mathbb{R}^{k_{j}}\times N_{j}
. Let us take a subsequence

for which

. Since

and

. On

we consider a

Since

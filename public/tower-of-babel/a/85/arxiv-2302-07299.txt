Low temperature asymptotic expansion for classical O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) vector models
Alessandro Giuliani, Sébastien Ott

Abstract
. We consider classical O ⁡ ( N ) O(N) vector models in dimension three and higher and investigate the nature of the low-temperature expansions for their multipoint spin correlations. We prove that such expansions define asymptotic series, and derive explicit estimates on the error terms associated with their finite order truncations. The result applies, in particular, to the spontaneous magnetization of the 3D Heisenberg model. The proof combines a priori bounds on the moments of the local spin observables, following from reflection positivity and the infrared bound, with an integration-by-parts method applied systematically to a suitable integral representation of the correlation functions. Our method generalizes an approach, proposed originally by Bricmont and collaborators [ 6 ] in the context of the rotator model, to the case of non-abelian symmetry and non-gradient observables.



1.
Introduction

In 1976, in one of the most influential papers in mathematical statistical mechanics, Fröhlich-Simon-Spencer (FSS)
[
11
]
proved the existence
of orientational long range order for classical
O(N)
vector models in three or more dimensions. In 1981, in the special case of the
O(2)
model, also known as the rotator or XY model,
Bricmont-Fontaine-Lebowitz-Lieb-Spencer (BFLLS)

[
6
]
extended the
FSS result, proving, in particular, that the formal low-temperature expansion for its magnetization defines an asymptotic series; that is, the difference between
the magnetization at temperature
T
and the truncation of its low-temperature series at any finite order
n
is
o(T^{n})
as
T\to 0
. A direct generalization of this result to
O(N)
models with
N\geq 3
, including the
physically relevant case of the Heisenberg model, remained open since, due to the difficulty in extending the BFLLS method to the case of non-abelian rotational symmetry.
By ‘direct’, here, we mean by similar elementary methods, combining a priori bounds on the moments of local spin observables, based on reflection positivity and the related infrared bound
[
11
,
12
]
, with systematic integration-by-parts applied to a suitable integral representation of
the spin correlations. In this paper we provide such a direct, self-contained, proof of the asymptotic nature of the low-temperature expansion for the magnetization and other
spin correlation functions of classical
O(N)
vector models, for any
N\geq 3
. Technically, our proof is based on an extension of the BFLLS proof, capable of handling non-abelian symmetry and non-gradient observables.

Let us recall that there are other, more involved, approaches that can be used to investigate the low-temperature properties of classical
O(N)
vector models: we refer here to Balaban’s multiscale analysis
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
of its correlation functions
[
4
]
. Balaban’s construction implies, in particular,
that the low-temperature expansion for the magnetization is an asymptotic series; it actually provides much more detailed informations on the correlations at low temperatures, including their
large distance asymptotics and decay exponents. However, the construction is extremely involved and it is hard to extract from it explicit informations in a simple and direct way.

For this reason, it is important to develop simpler approaches to the problem, particularly in cases, such as the classical
O(N)
vector model, where the system displays reflection positivity,
which in turn implies several strong a priori bounds on correlation functions.
The proof described below shows that, even in models with non-abelian continuous symmetry,
there is no intrinsic obstacle in using the infrared bound to derive bounds on the low-temperature expansion for correlations.
However, even if our method allows us to control multipoint spin correlation at any fixed coordinates,
it does not provide informations on their asymptotic behavior as the relative distance between the spins diverges to infinity. We believe that, in order to compute the
critical exponents and the dominant asymptotics of spin correlations, multiscale analysis is
inevitable. It would be extremely nice to be able to use systematically reflection positivity within a multiscale scheme, thus simplifying Balaban’s approach, particularly in
connection with
the technically involved procedure related to the small/large field decomposition. It would also be nice to develop methods, inspired by the BFLLS approach, capable of
controlling the low-temperature series for the magnetization of reflection positive quantum spin systems, such as the 3D quantum XY model or quantum Hesienberg anti-ferromagnet.
These long term goals are behind the very motivations of the current work. It remains to be seen whether such challenging problems can be solved or at least attacked via
extensions of the current work, and we hope to come back to these problems in future publications.

1.3.
Main results

We are now ready to state the main result of this work.

Theorem 1.3
.

For any
n,K\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}:=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}
, and
\epsilon>0
there exists
C<\infty
such that for any
\alpha^{1},\cdots,\alpha^{N}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\alpha^{k}_{x}\leq K
, there are coefficients
a_{0},\cdots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\Big|\langle\prod_{x}\prod_{k}(S_{x}^{k})^{\alpha_{x}^{k}}\rangle-\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}T^{i}\Big|\leq CT^{n+1-\epsilon}

where the
a_{i}
s are given by an explicit inductive algorithm.

A Corollary of Theorem
1.3
and of our inductive algorithm is the following second order approximation of the magnetization. Recall that

\Delta
denotes the Laplacian on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
G=(-\Delta)^{-1}
its Green function. Recall also that
\{\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{k}\}_{k=1,\ldots,d}
denotes the canonical basis of unit vectors of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The discrete derivative of
G
acting (say) on its second coordinate,
\nabla^{\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{k}}_{x}G(x_{0},\cdot)
, was defined right before the statement of Proposition
1.1
. Similarly, we let

\nabla^{\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{k},\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{k^{\prime}}}_{x,y}G=G(x+\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{k},y+\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{k^{\prime}})-G(x,y+\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{k^{\prime}})-G(x+\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{k},y)+G(x,y)
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For
d\geq 3
and
N\geq 2
, the following second order expansion for the spontaneous magnetization of the
O(N)
vector model holds, as
T\to 0^{+}
:

(7)

\langle S_{0}^{N}\rangle=1-T\frac{(N-1)}{2}G(0,0)+T^{2}\frac{(N-1)}{2}\Big[\frac{(3N-5)}{4}G(0,0)^{2}\\
-\sum_{x_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{e\in\{\hat{\mathrm{e}}_{i},i=1,\cdots d\}}\big(\nabla_{x_{1}}^{e}G(0,\cdot)\big)^{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{x_{1},x_{1}}^{e,e}G+(N-2)G(0,0)\Big)\Big]+o(T^{2}).

Of course, similar formulas can be derived for third or higher order truncations, but we decided to spell out for illustrative purposes only the simplest non-trivial one.

A second immediate consequence of the proof of our main result is the following.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
S=(S_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
be distributed with respect to the measure
\mu
. Then the first
N-1
components of the rescaled field
\sqrt{\beta}S
, that is,
\sqrt{\beta}(S^{1},S^{2},\dots,S^{N-1})
, converge in law, as
\beta\to\infty
, to
N-1
independent Gaussian Free Fields on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

1.4.
Organization of the paper

We will prove Theorem
1.3
by introducing an inductive procedure to compute the coefficients in the Taylor series. This expansion follows the same scheme as in
[
6
]
: a regularized version of the Gaussian integration by part and a priori bounds on the moments of suitable observables, obtained by using the infrared bound. The main difference lies in the fact that for
N>2
, the
O(N)
model can not be written as a gradient perturbation of a Gaussian free field. This substantially complicates the picture and, as a result, we need to introduce a more involved procedure than the one of
[
6
]
,
particularly in regards to the procedure required for summing over the coordinates the remainder terms. The
paper is organized as follows: after having summarized the notations used in the paper in Section
2
, in Section
3
we introduce the coordinates we use to parametrize the spin space; then, using these coordinates, we express the Gibbs measure
as a perturbation of a Gaussian one; finally, we use this re-writing of the measure to derive the formal low-temperature expansion, whose structure will be used below, in the
description of the inductive procedure. In Section
4
, we explain how to use the infrared bound to derive a priori bound on the spin moments (this is
an adaptation of the method in
[
6
]
).
In Section
5
we describe the version of the Gaussian integration by parts to be used in the following. Finally, in Section
6
,
we describe the inductive procedure: we first illustrate the procedure for the computation of the spontaneous magnetization
at order
T^{2}
, thus proving Corollary
1.4
, and then discuss the general scheme at all orders, thus proving Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.5
.
Appendix
A
contains the proof of Proposition
1.2
.
The proofs of the priori bounds on the decay of correlations (which use reflection positivity in a
relatively standard manner) are collected in Appendix
B
, while Appendix
C

discusses some standard Gaussian estimates, and their implications for the
summation over the coordinates of certain connected Gaussian expectations, which systematically appear in the evaluation of the remainder terms.

3.3.
The finite order truncations of the low temperature expansion

If we denote by

(27)

\begin{split}&\langle a\rangle^{(n)}=\sum_{s_{0}=0}^{n}T^{s_{0}}\sum_{p,\tilde{p}}a_{s_{0}}^{p,\tilde{p}}\,\Big[\Phi(\phi^{p})\langle\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\rangle^{(n-s_{0})}+\mathds{1}_{n>s_{0}}\mathds{1}_{\|p\|_{1}>0}\sum_{s=1}^{n-s_{0}}T^{s}\sum_{k\geq 1}\tfrac{1}{k!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1},r_{1},\ldots,p_{k},r_{k}\geq 0:\\

&\qquad\cdot c_{r_{1}}\cdots c_{r_{k}}\sum_{x_{1},e_{1},\ldots,x_{k},e_{k}}\Phi\big(\phi^{p};(\nabla^{e_{1}}_{\!x_{1}}\phi)^{2r_{1}+2};\cdots;(\nabla^{e_{k}}_{\!x_{k}}\phi)^{2r_{k}+2}\big)\langle\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\mathcal{G}^{p_{l}}_{x_{l},e_{l}}\rangle^{(n-s-s_{0})}\Big],\end{split}

where, again, the sum over

can be restricted to even tuples.
Now, the terms
\langle\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\rangle^{(n-s_{0})}
in the right side
(and similarly
\langle\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\mathcal{G}^{p_{l}}_{x_{l},e_{l}}\rangle^{(n-s-s_{0})}

of
\langle\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\rangle^{(m)}

\tilde{p}
is an even tuple with non-negative elements.
If the order is zero, then
\langle 1\rangle^{(0)}=1
. More generally, if
\tilde{p}=0

\langle 1\rangle^{(m)}=1
, for all
m\geq 0
. Let us then consider a term

\langle\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\rangle^{(m)}
of positive order

\|\tilde{p}\|_{1}

Let us assume without loss of generality that
\|\tilde{p}^{1}\|
is even and positive, so that
\tilde{p}^{\geq 2}=(0,\tilde{p}^{2},\ldots,\tilde{p}^{N-2})

\|\tilde{p}^{\geq 2}\|_{1}\leq\|\tilde{p}\|_{1}-2

Using (
18

\prod_{x}\Big(\sqrt{\beta(1-T\|\tilde{u}_{x}\|^{2})}\sin\big(\sqrt{T}\phi_x\big)\Big)^{\tilde{p}^{1}_{x}}
in Taylor series in

in the form

(28)

\begin{split}&\phi^{\tilde{p}^{1}}\sum_{s\geq 0}(-1)^{s}T^{s}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(s_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}:\\

By applying formula (

•

-S_{0}^{k}

\langle S_{0}^{k}\rangle_{h}=0
and

\langle e^{a\sqrt{\beta}|S_{0}^{k}|}\rangle_{h}=\langle e^{a\sqrt{\beta}S_{0}^{k}}\mathds{1}_{S_{0}^{k}\geq 0}\rangle_{h}+\langle e^{-a\sqrt{\beta}S_{0}^{k}}\mathds{1}_{S_{0}^{k}<0}\rangle_{h}\leq 2\langle e^{-a\sqrt{\beta}S_{0}^{k}}\rangle_{h}.

Thus (
34
) holds for
k=1,\cdots,N-1
. Notice that this bound holds for any value of
T
.
For
k=N
, the lower bound (
6
) on
\langle S_{0}^{N}\rangle_{h}
, and
\langle S_{0}^{N}\rangle_{h}\geq 0
implies that

\langle S_{0}^{N}\rangle_{h}\geq\sqrt{\max(1-TNG_{00},0)}\geq\max(1-TNG_{00},0).

Plugging this bound in (
36
) with
k=N
, we find

e^{a^{2}G_{00}/2}\geq e^{a\sqrt{\beta}\langle S_{0}^{N}\rangle_{h}}\langle e^{-a\sqrt{\beta}S_{0}^{N}}\rangle_{h}\geq e^{-a\sqrt{T}NG_{00}}\langle e^{a\sqrt{\beta}(1-S_{0}^{N})}\rangle_{h}

so that

\langle e^{a\sqrt{\beta}(1-S_{0}^{N})}\rangle_{h}\leq e^{a^{2}G_{00}/2}e^{a\sqrt{T}NG_{00}}\leq e^{(a\sqrt{T}N+a^{2}/2)G_{00}},

proving (
35
).
∎

A direct consequence is

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\beta\geq 0
,
h\in D^{*}
and
b>0
. For any
1\leq k_{0}\leq N-1
,

(37)

\mu_{h}\big(\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}|S_{0}^{k}|^{2}\geq b^{2}\big)\leq 2e^{-b^{2}\beta/(2k_{0}^{2}G_{00})}.

If, additionally,
T\leq b/(2G_{00}N)
, then

(38)

\mu_{h}(S_{0}^{N}\leq 1-b)\leq e^{-b^{2}\beta/(8G_{00})}.

Proof.

Using the exponential version of Chebyshev’s inequality, Hölder’s inequality, and
(
34
), we find that, for any
a>0
,

\begin{split}\mu_{h}(\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}|S_{0}^{k}|^{2}\geq b^{2})&\leq\mu_{h}(\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}|S_{0}^{k}|\geq b)\\
&\leq e^{-ab\sqrt{\beta}}\langle e^{a\sqrt{\beta}\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}|S_{0}^{k}|}\rangle_{h}\\
&\leq e^{-ab\sqrt{\beta}}\prod_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\langle e^{ak_{0}\sqrt{\beta}|S_{0}^{k}|}\rangle^{1/k_{0}}_{h}\\
&\leq 2e^{-ab\sqrt{\beta}}e^{a^{2}k_{0}^{2}G_{00}/2}.\end{split}

Choosing
a=b\sqrt{\beta}/(k_{0}^{2}G_{00})
implies (
37
). Next, using
(
35
) and, again, the exponential version of Chebyshev’s inequality, for any
a>0
,

\mu_{h}(1-S_{0}^{N}\geq b)\leq e^{-ab\sqrt{\beta}}\langle e^{a\sqrt{\beta}(1-S_{0}^{N})}\rangle_{h}\leq e^{-ab\sqrt{\beta}}e^{(a\sqrt{T}N+a^{2}/2)G_{00}}.

Choosing
a=b\sqrt{\beta}/G_{00}-\sqrt{T}N
and
T\leq b/(2G_{00}N)
implies (
38
).
∎

4.2.
Bounds on the moments of the
(u,\theta)

The next Theorem is the main objective of this section. Its proof is a generalization of the one of the corresponding result in
[
6
]
,
namely
[
6
, Eq.(10) and Lemma 2]
. Here and below, with some abuse of notation, we denote by
\langle\ \rangle_{h}
the average
with respect to
\mu_{h}
, even when re-expressed in terms of the
(u,\theta)

S
(a similar convention will be used for

and
\langle\ \rangle
).

Theorem 4.3
.

There exist constants
C,C_{0},c>0
and
T_{0}>0
such that, if
T\leq T_{0}
, the following holds.

•

For any
k\in\{1,\cdots,N-2\}
and
a>0
,
\langle e^{a|\tilde{u}_{0}^{k}|}\rangle_{h}\leq 2e^{a^{2}G_{00}/2}
. Moreover, for any
n\geq 0
,

\langle|\tilde{u}^{k}_{0}|^{n}\rangle_{h}\leq C^{n}\sqrt{n!}.

•

\langle e^{|\phi_{0}|}\rangle_{h}\leq C
. In particular, for any
n\geq 0
,

|\langle|\phi_{0}|^{n}\rangle_{h}|\leq Cn!.

•

For any
F
function of
\phi,\tilde{u}
,

|\langle F\delta(\theta_{0}\pm\pi)\rangle_{h}|\leq\left\|F\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}C_{0}e^{h-c\beta},

where the sup-norm is computed over
\phi_{x}\in[-\sqrt{\beta}\pi,\sqrt{\beta}\pi]
and
\|\tilde{u}_{x}\|\leq\sqrt{\beta}
for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Proof.

The first inequality in the first item,
\langle e^{a|\tilde{u}_{0}^{k}|}\rangle_{h}\leq 2e^{a^{2}G_{00}/2}
, is a direct consequence of (
34
) and the definition of
\tilde{u}
. From such inequality, it follows that
\langle|\tilde{u}_{0}^{k}|^{n}\rangle_{h}\leq 2e^{a^{2}G_{00}/2}a^{-n}n!
. The choice
a=\sqrt{n/G_{00}}
implies the stated bound on

\langle|\tilde{u}_{0}^{k}|^{n}\rangle_{h}
.

To get the second item, write

\displaystyle\langle e^{\sqrt{\beta}|\theta_{0}|}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\langle e^{\sqrt{\beta}|\theta_{0}|}\mathds{1}_{|\theta_{0}|\leq\pi/2}\mathds{1}_{\|u_{0}\|\leq 1/2}\rangle_{h}+\langle e^{\sqrt{\beta}|\theta_{0}|}\mathds{1}_{|\theta_{0}|\leq\pi/2}\mathds{1}_{\|u_{0}\|>1/2}\rangle_{h}+\langle e^{\sqrt{\beta}|\theta_{0}|}\mathds{1}_{|\theta_{0}|>\pi/2}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle\leq\langle e^{\pi^{-1}\sqrt{3\beta}\sqrt{1-\|u_{0}\|^{2}}|\sin(\theta_0)|}\rangle_{h}+e^{\pi\sqrt{\beta}/2}\mu_{h}(\|u_{0}\|>1/2)+e^{\pi\sqrt{\beta}}\mu_{h}(|\theta_{0}|>\pi/2)

\displaystyle=\langle e^{\pi^{-1}\sqrt{3\beta}|S_{0}^{N-1}|}\rangle_{h}+e^{\pi\sqrt{\beta}/2}\mu_{h}\big({\sum_{k=1}^{N-2}|S^{k}_{0}|^{2}}>1/4\big)+e^{\pi\sqrt{\beta}}\mu_{h}(S_{0}^{N}\leq 0)

\displaystyle\leq 2e^{3G_{00}/(2\pi^{2})}+2e^{\pi\sqrt{\beta}/2}e^{-\beta/(8G_{00}(N-2)^{2})}+e^{\pi\sqrt{\beta}}e^{-\beta/(8G_{00})},

where in the last inequality we used (
34
), (
37
) and (
38
). If we now choose
\beta
large enough and recall the definition of
\phi
, we obtain, as desired, that

\langle e^{|\phi_{0}|}\rangle_{h}\leq C
for a suitable
C>0
.

Finally, we consider the term
\langle F\delta(\theta_{0}\pm\pi)\rangle_{h}
involving the delta function. Using the DLR equation and letting
S_{0}(u_{0})=\Pi(u_{0},\pi)=(u_{0}^{1},\cdots,u_{0}^{N-2},0,-\sqrt{1-\|u_{0}\|^{2}})
,

\langle F\delta(\theta_{0}\pm\pi)\rangle_{h}=\int d\mu_{h}(S_{i},i\neq 0)\frac{\int_{\|u_{0}\|\leq 1}du_{0}\,F(S,S_{0}(u_{0}))e^{\beta\sum_{i\sim 0}S_{0}(u_{0})\cdot S_{i}-\sqrt{1-\|u_{0}\|^{2}}h}}{\int d\nu_{N}(\tilde{S}_{0})e^{\beta\sum_{i\sim 0}\tilde{S}_{0}\cdot S_{i}+h\tilde{S}_{0}^{N}}}.

Define

J(S_{i},i\sim 0)=\sup_{\|u_{0}\|\leq 1}\Big(\int d\nu_{N}(\tilde{S}_{0})e^{\beta\sum_{i\sim 0}(\tilde{S}_{0}-S_{0}(u_{0}))\cdot S_{i}+h\tilde{S}_{0}^{N}}\Big)^{-1}\equiv\sup_{\|u_{0}\|\leq 1}J_{u_{0}}(S_{i},i\sim 0).

One has

|\langle F\delta(\theta_{0}\pm\pi)\rangle_{h}|\leq\left\|F\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle\infty}\frac{\pi^{N/2-1}}{\Gamma(N/2)}\int d\mu_{h}(S_{i},i\neq 0)J(S_{i},i\sim 0).

Now, for any event
A
measurable with respect to
\{S_{i}:i\sim 0\}
, one has

(39)

\int d\mu_{h}(S_{i},i\neq 0)J(S_{i},i\sim 0)\leq\sup_{(S_{i})_{i\sim 0}\in A}J(S_{i},i\sim 0)+\mu_{h}(A^{c})\sup_{(S_{i})_{i\sim 0}\in A^{c}}J(S_{i},i\sim 0).

We choose
A=\{S_{i}^{N}\geq 7/8,i\sim 0\}
. Study the first term. For any
(S_{i})_{i\sim 0}\in A
, any
u_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{N-2}
and
h\geq 0
, one has the bound

\begin{split}J_{u_{0}}(S_{i},i\sim 0)&\leq\Big(\int d\nu_{N}(\tilde{S}_{0})\mathds{1}_{\tilde{S}_{0}^{N}\geq 7/8}e^{\beta\sum_{i\sim 0}(\tilde{S}_{0}-S_{0}(u_{0}))\cdot S_{i}}\Big)^{-1}\\
&\leq\Big(\int d\nu_{N}(\tilde{S}_{0})\mathds{1}_{\tilde{S}_{0}^{N}\geq 7/8}e^{\beta 2d/32}\Big)^{-1}=Ce^{-\beta 2d/32}.\end{split}

for some constant
C>0
. Indeed, on the one hand, for
S_{i}^{N}\geq 7/8
and
\|u_{0}\|\leq 1
, by Cauchy-Schwartz,

\begin{split}S_{0}(u_{0})\cdot S_{i}&=\sum_{k=1}^{N-2}u_{0}^{k}S_{i}^{k}-\sqrt{1-\|u_{0}\|^{2}}S_{i}^{N}\leq\|u_{0}\|\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}(S_{i}^{k})^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\\
&\leq\|u_{0}\|\Big(1-\frac{49}{64}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\|u_{0}\|\cdot\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}.\end{split}

On the other hand, if
\tilde{S}^{N}_{0}\geq 7/8
and
S_{i}^{N}\geq 7/8
,

(40)

\tilde{S}_{0}\cdot S_{i}\geq\cos(2\arccos(7/8))=\frac{17}{32}.

We now turn to the second term in (
39
). First, by a union bound,

\mu_{h}(A^{c})\leq 2d\mu_{h}(1-S_{0}^{N}>1/8)\leq 2de^{-\beta/(8^{3}G_{00})},

where we assumed
T
sufficiently small and used (
38
). Moreover, for any
u_{0}\in\mathbb{B}^{N-2}
,

\begin{split}J_{u_{0}}(S_{i},i\sim 0)&\leq e^{h}\Big(\int d\nu_{N}(\tilde{S}_{0})\mathds{1}_{\|\tilde{S}_{0}-S_{0}(u_{0})\|\leq T}e^{\beta\sum_{i\sim 0}(\tilde{S}_{0}-S_{0}(u_{0}))\cdot S_{i}}\Big)^{-1}\\
&\leq e^{h+2d}\Big(\int d\nu_{N}(\tilde{S}_{0})\mathds{1}_{\|\tilde{S}_{0}-S_{0}(u_{0})\|\leq T}\Big)^{-1}\leq C^{\prime}e^{h}\beta^{N-1},\end{split}

for some
C^{\prime}>0
independent of
T
. Combining the two previous equations, we obtain the desired estimate on the second term in (
39
), which concludes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Taylor expansion of the spin correlations

Given
\alpha:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times[N]\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
\|\alpha\|_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\alpha^{k}_{x}
finite,
we let

be the spin observable

a_{\alpha}(S)=\prod_{x}\prod_{k}(S_{x}^{k})^{\alpha_{x}^{k}}.

In order to prove our main result, Theorem
1.3
, we are interested in computing
\langle a_{\alpha}(S)\rangle
for
\alpha
s such that
\|\alpha^{k}\|_{1}=\sum_{x}\alpha^{k}_{x}
are even, for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,N-1\}

(if any of these

is odd,

, due to the residual
O(N-1)
symmetry in the directions orthogonal to the magnetic field).
The main result of this subsection is the following:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\alpha
and
a_{\alpha}(S)
be defined as above. Then there exist coefficients
a_{\alpha,s}^{p,\tilde{p}}
, with
s\geq 0
and
p\in\mathfrak{P}
,

both even, such that, for any
n\geq 0
,

(41)

\langle a_{\alpha}(S)\rangle=\sum_{s=0}^{n}T^{s}\sum_{p,\tilde{p}}a_{\alpha,s}^{p,\tilde{p}}\langle\phi^{p}\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\rangle+R_{n+1}(\alpha),

where the sum over
p,\tilde{p}
in the right hand side runs over even tuples in
\mathfrak{P},\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}
,
a_{\alpha,s}^{p,\tilde{p}}=0
if
\|p\|_{1}+\|\tilde{p}\|_{1}\neq 2s
, and the remainder satisfies

(42)

|R_{n+1}(\alpha)|\leq C(n,\alpha)T^{n+1}

for some
C(n,\alpha)>0
.

Remark 4.1
.

The coefficients
a_{\alpha,s}^{p,\tilde{p}}
are explicitly computable in terms of the Taylor expansions of
\sqrt{1-x}
,
\sin x
and
\cos x
, see (
45
)-(
46
) below.

Proof.

We denote by
A_{\alpha}(\tilde{u},\phi)
the rewriting of
a_{\alpha}(S)
in the
(\tilde{u},\phi)
variables:

(43)

A_{\alpha}(\tilde{u},\phi)=\prod_{x}\prod_{k}\Big(\Pi_{k}(\sqrt{T}\tilde{u}_{x},\sqrt{T}\phi_{x})\Big)^{\alpha_{x}^{k}},

with
\Pi_{k}
the components of the function
\Pi
in (
10
). Letting
E_{\alpha}
be the event

E_{\alpha}=\{\tilde{u}\in(\sqrt{\beta}\mathbb{B}^{N-2})^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}:\|\tilde{u}_{x}\|\leq\sqrt{\beta}/2,\forall x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\alpha^{N-1}\cup\mathrm{supp}\,\alpha^{N}\},

and using (
37
), which implies that
\mu(E_{\alpha}^{c})=O(e^{-c\beta})
for some
c>0
, we find

(44)

\langle a_{\alpha}(S)\rangle=\langle\mathds{1}_{E_{\alpha}}\,A_{\alpha}(\tilde{u},\phi)\rangle+O(e^{-c\beta}).

By expanding each factor
\sqrt{1-T\|\tilde{u}_{x}\|^{2}}
,
\sin(\sqrt{T}\phi_x)
,
\cos(\sqrt{T}\phi_x)
appearing in the right hand side
of (

) in power series in

, as

(45)

\begin{split}&\sqrt{1-T\|\tilde{u}_{x}\|^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\infty)}}{{=}}\sum_{n\geq 0}T^{n}(-1)^{n}{1/2\choose n}\|\tilde{u}_{x}\|^{2n},\\
&\sin(\sqrt{T}\phi_x)=\sqrt{T}\phi_{x}\sum_{n\geq 0}T^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(2n+1)!}\phi_{x}^{2n},\\
&\cos(\sqrt{T}\phi_x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}T^{n}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(2n)!}\phi_{x}^{2n},\end{split}

we obtain the formal power series expansion for

, in the form

(46)

A_{\alpha}(\tilde{u},\phi)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\infty)}}{{=}}\sum_{s\geq 0}T^{s}\sum_{p,\tilde{p}}a_{\alpha,s}^{p,\tilde{p}}\phi^{p}\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}},

for suitable real coefficients

(the sum over
p,\tilde{p}
runs over even tuples in
\mathfrak{P},\tilde{\mathfrak{P}}
). By the Taylor remainder’s theorem, for any

, there exists

such that, for any
\tilde{u}
in the support of

,

\Big|A_{\alpha}(\tilde{u},\phi)-\sum_{s=0}^{n}T^{s}\sum_{p,\tilde{p}}a_{\alpha,s}^{p,\tilde{p}}\phi^{p}\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\Big|\leq C_{n,\alpha}T^{n+1}\sum_{x\in\mathrm{supp}\alpha}(\|\tilde{u}_{x}\|^{2(n+1)}+\phi_{x}^{2(n+1)}).

Therefore, using again (
37
) and the moments bounds in the first two items of Theorem
4.3
, we obtain (
41
)-(
42
), as desired. ∎

6.2.
Inductive hypotheses

Definition 6.1

(Inductive hypotheses
\mathcal{H}_{M,K}
)
.

We say that
\mathcal{H}_{M,K}
holds if for any
0<\epsilon<1
, and any integer
\alpha>M
, the two following hold:

\mathcal{H}_{M,K}^{1}
:

There exists
C=C(\epsilon,K,M)
such that: for any integer
0\leq n\leq M
,
p:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
even,
\tilde{p}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times[N-2]
even with
\frac{\left\|\tilde{p}\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}+\left\|p\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}}{2}\leq K
,

(59)

\big|\langle\phi^{p}\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\rangle-\langle\phi^{p}\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}\rangle^{(n)}\big|\leq CT^{n+1-\epsilon}.

\mathcal{H}_{M,K}^{2}
:

There exists
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(\epsilon,K,M,\alpha)
and
c_{K}
such that: for any integer
0\leq n\leq M
, even
\tilde{p}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times[N-2]\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, odd
p:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
, and odd
q:\mathbb{E}_{d}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
with
\frac{\left\|\tilde{p}\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}+\left\|p\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}+\left\|q\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}}{2}\leq K
,

(60)

\big|\langle\phi^{p}\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}(\nabla\phi)^{q}\rangle-\langle\phi^{p}\tilde{u}^{\tilde{p}}(\nabla\phi)^{q}\rangle^{(n)}\big|\leq\\
\leq C^{\prime}T^{\alpha}+C^{\prime}T^{n+1-\epsilon}\sum_{x\in\mathrm{supp}_{p}}\sum_{y\in\mathrm{supp}_{q}}\frac{\log^{2+n}(1+|x-y|)}{1+|x-y|}.

We say that
\mathcal{H}_{M,\infty}
holds if
\mathcal{H}_{M,K}
holds for every
K\geq 0
, and that
\mathcal{H}_{\infty,K}
holds if
\mathcal{H}_{M,K}
holds for every
M\geq 0
.

Remark 6.1
.

By linearity,
\mathcal{H}_{M,K}
implies a control over polynomials in
\tilde{u},\phi,\nabla\phi
. The constants in the error terms depend then on the coefficients of the polynomial.

Remark 6.2
.

We automatically have the validity of
\mathcal{H}_{\infty,0}
.

To lighten notation, we will not recall the domain and image of
p,q,\tilde{p}
. The main result of this section is

Theorem 6.1
.

\mathcal{H}_{M,K}
holds for any
M,K\geq 0
.

The proof of Theorem
6.1
will occupy the remainder of this section. Note that what we are in fact only interested in is the validity of
\mathcal{H}_{M,K}^{1}
.
\mathcal{H}_{M,K}^{2}
is only needed for technical reasons (which will be highlighted when proving the induction step for
\mathcal{H}_{M,K}^{1}
).

C^{\prime}\frac{\log^{k}(1+|y|)}{1+|y|}\sum_{|x|\leq|y|/2}(1+|x|)^{-d}\leq C\frac{\log^{k}(1+|y|)}{1+|y|}\log(1+|y|)
. In the same fashion, in the second sum,
|x|>|y|/2
so the second term is bounded from above by
C^{\prime}\frac{\log^{k}(1+|y|)}{(1+|y|)^{d}}\sum_{|x-y|<|y|/2}(1+|x-y|)^{-1}\leq C\frac{\log^{k}(1+|y|)}{1+|y|}
. In the last case, using Hölder’s inequality, we see that the third sum is bounded from above by

C^{\prime}\Big(\sum_{|x|>|y|/2}\frac{1}{(1+|x|)^{d+1}}\Big)^{\frac{d}{d+1}}\Big(\sum_{|x-y|\geq|y|/2}\frac{\log^{k(d+1)}(1+|x-y|)}{(1+|x-y|)^{d+1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{d+1}}\leq C\frac{\log^{k}(1+|y|)}{1+|y|}.

This proves the claim (
C
depends on
k,d
).
∎

In order to compare massive and massless propagators, we use Theorem
C.1
in combination with eq.(
78
) and the following Lemma.

Lemma C.3
.

Suppose
f_{m}(x)
is such that

•

\sup_{m\geq 0}|f_{m}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|)^{-\gamma}
,

•

|f_{m}(x)-f_{0}(x)|\leq Cm
,

for some
C,\gamma>0
. Then, for any
0<\gamma^{\prime}<\gamma
, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that

(83)

|f_{m}(x)-f_{0}(x)|\leq C^{\prime}m^{1-\gamma^{\prime}/\gamma}(1+|x|)^{-\gamma^{\prime}}.

Proof.

One has
|f_{m}(x)-f_{0}(x)|\leq|f_{m}(x)-f_{0}(x)|^{1-\gamma^{\prime}/\gamma}(2\sup_{m\geq 0}|f_{m}(x)|)^{\gamma^{\prime}/\gamma}
. From this, the two assumptions readily imply
(
83
).
∎

We conclude this subsection with a simple lemma that we used in many instances.

Lemma C.4
.

Let
a,b>0
be such that
a+b>d
. Then, there exists
C=C(a,b)
such that for any
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,

\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|x|)^{-a}(1+|x-u|)^{-b}\leq C\frac{\log(1+|u|)}{(1+|u|)^{a+b-{d}}}.

Proof.

Separate the sum over
x
into
\sum_{|x|\leq|u|/2}+\sum_{|x-u|<|u|/2}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|x|>|u|/2\\
|x-u|\geq|u|/2\end{subarray}}
. In the first sum, one has
|x-u|\geq|u|/2
, which allows us to bound from above the first term by
C\frac{\log(1+|u|)}{(1+|u|)^{a+b-d}}
(the log only appears if
a=d
). In the same fashion, in the second sum,
|x|>|u|/2
so the second term is bounded from above by
C\frac{\log(1+|u|)}{(1+|u|)^{a-d+b}}
(the log only appears if
b=d
). In the last case, one has
|x|\leq|x-u|+|u|\leq 3|x-u|
. So, the last sum is bounded from above by
C\sum_{|x|>|u|/2}(1+|x|)^{-a-b}\leq C(1+|u|)^{d-a-b}
.
∎

C.2.
Gaussian connected correlations

Let
a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and denote by
A=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
the corresponding multi-set, i.e., the collection of the elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
counted with their multiplicities, in the case that a given site of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
appears more than once in the list. One has the Wick formula

(84)

\Phi(\phi_{A})=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Pairings}(A)}\prod_{\{a_{1},a_{2}\}\in\pi}\Phi(\phi_{a_{1}}\phi_{a_{2}}),

where
\phi_{A}=\prod_{a\in A}\phi_{a}
, and

\mathrm{Pairings}(A)
is the set of partitions of
A
into two-elements classes.
Moreover, from (
25
) one also has the corresponding formula for connected correlations:
let
A_{i}=\{a_{1}^{i},\cdots,a_{k_{i}}^{i}\}
,
i=1,\cdots,n
, be multi-sets of sites. Then,

(85)

\Phi(\phi_{A_{1}};\cdots;\phi_{A_{n}})=\sum_{\pi\in\mathrm{Pairings}^{c}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{n})}\prod_{\{a_{1},a_{2}\}\in\pi}\Phi(\phi_{a_{1}}\phi_{a_{2}}),

where
\mathrm{Pairings}^{c}(A_{1},\cdots,A_{n})
is the subset of
\mathrm{Pairings}(A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{n})
consisting of the pairings
\pi
such that
the graph with vertex set
\{A_{1},\cdots,A_{n}\}
and edge set
F_{\pi}=\{\{A_{i},A_{j}\}:\exists\ a\in A_{i},b\in A_{j}\ \text{such that}\ \{a,b\}\in\pi\}
is connected. The same formulas extend (by linearity) to the case
A_{i}=A_{i}^{v}\sqcup A_{i}^{e}
with
A_{i}^{v}
a multi-set of sites and
A_{i}^{e}
a multi-set of edges (elements of
\mathbb{E}_{d}
) and
\phi_{A_{i}}
is replaced by
\phi_{A_{i}^{v}}(\nabla\phi)_{A_{i}^{e}}
.

Lemma C.5
.

Let
K,n\geq 0
be integers. Let
\epsilon>0
. Then, there exists
C=C(n,K,\epsilon)
such that for any
p,p^{\prime}:\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
odd, any
q:\mathbb{E}_{d}\to\mathbb{Z}_{+}
odd, and
r_{1},\cdots,r_{n}>0
integers with
\left\|p\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}+\left\|p^{\prime}\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}+\left\|q\right\|_{\scriptscriptstyle 1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}2r_{i}\leq K
,

(86)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\in\mathrm{B}_{+}\end{subarray}}\big|\Phi\big(\phi^{p}\phi^{p^{\prime}};(\nabla_{x_{1}}^{e_{1}}\phi)^{2r_{1}};\cdots;(\nabla_{x_{n}}^{e_{n}}\phi)^{2r_{n}}\big)\big|\leq C\sum_{x\in\mathrm{supp}_{p}}\sum_{y\in\mathrm{supp}_{p^{\prime}}}\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{d-2-\epsilon}},

and

(87)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\\
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}\in\mathrm{B}_{+}\end{subarray}}\big|\Phi\big(\phi^{p}(\nabla\phi)^{q};(\nabla_{x_{1}}^{e_{1}}\phi)^{2r_{1}};\cdots;(\nabla_{x_{1}}^{e_{n}}\phi)^{2r_{n}}\big)\big|\leq C\sum_{x\in\mathrm{supp}_{p}}\sum_{y\in\mathrm{supp}_{q}}\frac{1}{(1+|x-y|)^{d-1-\epsilon}}.

Proof.

For
n=0
the summands in the left sides should be interpreted as
|\Phi(\phi^{p}\phi^{p^{\prime}})|
and
|\Phi(\phi^{p}(\nabla\phi)^{q})|
, respectively. In this case,
the claim readily follows from
C.1
and (
84
). We therefore suppose
n\geq 1
. As the two claims are proved in exactly the same fashion, we prove only the first one and leave the adaptations to get the second to the reader. We will use the identity (
85
) together with Lemma
C.4
.

It will useful to use a graphical language: introduce a set of sites
\{0,0^{\prime},1,\cdots,n\}
corresponding to
\phi^{p},\phi^{p^{\prime}},(\nabla_{x_{1}}^{e_{1}}\phi)^{2r_{1}},\cdots,(\nabla_{x_{n}}^{e_{n}}\phi)^{2r_{n}}
. Edges will be given by the pairings in (
85
). See Figure
1
for an illustration with
n=4
.

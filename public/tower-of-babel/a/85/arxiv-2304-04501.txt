Abstract
. We show that the construction of the higher Gaudin Hamiltonians associated to the Lie algebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n \mathfrak{gl}_{n} admits an interpolation to any complex n n . We do this using the Deligne’s category 𝒟 t \mathcal{D}_{t} , which is a formal way to define the category of finite-dimensional representations of the group G ​ L n GL_{n} , when n n is not necessarily a natural number. We also obtain interpolations to any complex n n of the no-monodromy conditions on a space of differential operators of order n n , which are considered to be a modern form of the Bethe ansatz equations. We prove that the relations in the algebra of higher Gaudin Hamiltonians for complex n n are generated by our interpolations of the no-monodromy conditions. Our constructions allow us to define what it means for a pseudo-deifferential operator to have no monodromy. Motivated by the Bethe ansatz conjecture for the Gaudin model associated with the Lie superalgebra 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 n | n ′ \mathfrak{gl}_{n|n^{\prime}} , we show that a ratio of monodromy-free differential operators is a pseudo-differential operator without monodromy.

1.
Introduction

1.1.

Symmetric tensor categories (STC’s), see
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
, provide a natural framework for studying finite-dimensional representations
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb{K}}(G)
of a group
G
over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
. In this paper, we will consider only
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
. It is known that for any group
G
, there exists an affine group scheme
\widehat{G}
such that the category
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
is equivalent to the category
\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(\widehat{G})
of finite-dimensional (algebraic) representations of
\widehat{G}
. A natural question is whether there are examples of STC’s which are not equivalent to
\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(\widehat{G})
for some affine group scheme
\widehat{G}
. It is easy to see that the answer to this question is positive: categories of finite-dimensional representations of affine
super
group schemes provide new examples since dimensions of objects in such categories might take negative integer values. In fact, there are STC’s with objects of arbitrary complex dimension. Let
t\in\mathbb{C}
. In
[
4
, Example 1.27]
, Deligne and Milne introduced the category that will be denoted as
\mathcal{D}_{t}
in this paper. The category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
may be viewed as an interpolation
"\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(GL_{t})"
of the category
\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(GL_{n})
to arbitrary complex
n
. The object in
\mathcal{D}_{t}
that plays the role of the standard representation
\mathbb{C}^{n}
in
\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(GL_{n})
has dimension
t
(see, e.g.,
[
2
]
for details). If
t\notin\mathbb{Z}
, then
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is an STC, otherwise it is not abelian (which is necessary for being an STC in terminology of
[
8
]
), but rather Karoubian category, and we have a tensor functor
G_{n}:\mathcal{D}_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(GL_{n})
.
In
[
3
]
, Deligne also introduced similar interpolations
\mathcal{D}^{S}_{t}
,
\mathcal{D}^{O}_{t}
and
\mathcal{D}^{Sp}_{2t}
of the categories
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{n})

\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(O_{n})
and
\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(Sp_{n})
, respectively. The categories
\mathcal{D}_{t}
,
\mathcal{D}^{S}_{t}
,
\mathcal{D}^{O}_{t}
, and
\mathcal{D}^{Sp}_{t}
are sometimes called the Deligne’s categories.

As was shown in
[
6
]
, many Lie-theoretic constructions associated with classical Lie groups
GL_{n}
,
O_{n}
, or
Sp_{2n}
(e.g. the corresponding Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, symmetric dual pairs, Harish-Chandra modules, the Yangian
Y(\mathfrak{g})
) can be defined using the language of symmetric tensor categories. Then, replacing categories
\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(GL_{n})
,
\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(O_{n})
, and
\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(Sp_{2n})
with their super analogs, or with
\mathcal{D}_{t}
,
\mathcal{D}^{O}_{t}
, and
\mathcal{D}^{Sp}_{2t}
, respectively, one naturally obtains the generalization of these constructions to supergroups or more exotic generalizations, which collectively may be called ”Lie theory in complex rank”. In this paper, we show that the algebra of higher Hamiltonians (the Bethe algebra) of the quantum (rational) Gaudin model associated to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is one of such constructions, that is, it can be defined in categorical terms and admits an ”interpolation” to any complex
n
.

1.2.

The quantum Gaudin model associated to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
was first introduced in
[
11
]
, and it is a certain degeneration of the quantum spin chain. Fix
m
distinct complex numbers
z_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak z_{m}
. Let
e_{ij}
,
i,j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
be the standard basis of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, that is,
e_{ij}
is the matrix with
1
on the intersection of the row
i
and the column
j
and
0
everywhere else. The model is given by the following set of
m
commuting Hamiltonians:

H_{a}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b=1\\
b\neq a\end{subarray}}^{m}\frac{\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}e^{(a)}_{ij}e^{(b)}_{ji}}{z_{a}-z_{b}}\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m},\quad a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m,

where
e_{ij}^{(l)}=1\otimes\dots\otimes 1\otimes\underset{l-th}{e_{ij}}\otimes 1\otimes\dots\otimes 1
.

Let
V_{\nu}
denote the irreducible representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
of highest weight
\nu
. Fix a sequence
\bar{\nu}=(\nu^{(1)},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\nu^{(m)})
of integral dominant
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weights. The Hamiltonians
H_{a}
,
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
act on the tensor product
V(\bar{\nu})=V_{\nu^{(1)}}\otimes\dots\otimes V_{\nu^{(m)}}
. Let
V(\bar{\nu})^{sing}
be the space of singular vectors with respect to the action of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
on
V(\bar{\nu})
. The Hamiltonians commute with this action, and therefore, act on
V(\bar{\nu})^{sing}
. The algebra
\mathcal{B}^{sing}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
of higher Hamiltonians or ”conserved charges” of the quantum Gaudin model, which sometimes is also called the Bethe algebra, is the centralizer of
H_{a}
,
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
in the algebra
\operatorname{End}(V(\bar{\nu})^{sing})
of all linear transformations of the space
V(\bar{\nu})^{sing}
.

There is also the universal Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
, see
[
9
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
13
]
. The algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}
commutes with the copy of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
in
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
given by the inclusion
g\mapsto\sum_{a=1}^{m}1\otimes\dots\otimes 1\otimes\underset{a-th}{g}\otimes 1\otimes\dots\otimes 1
, and therefore, acts on
V(\bar{\nu})^{sing}
. The image of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
in
\operatorname{End}(V(\bar{\nu})^{sing})
under this action coincides with
\mathcal{B}^{sing}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
. The algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}
can be constructed using the center of the local completion of the universal enveloping algebra of the affine Lie algebra
\widehat{\mathfrak{gl}}_{n}
, see
[
9
]
. In this paper, we will use more explicit construction of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
given in
[
17
]
and
[
1
]
, see Section
3
for details. Also, instead of
\mathcal{B}^{sing}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
, we will consider the image
\mathcal{B}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
in
\operatorname{End}(V(\bar{\nu}))
since we have an isomorphism of algebras

\mathcal{B}^{sing}_{n}(\bar{\nu})\cong\mathcal{B}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
.

Now, let us return to the Deligne’s category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
. Indecomposable objects of
\mathcal{D}_{t}
are labeled by pairs of partitions. For each integral dominant
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight
\nu
, there is a pair of partitions
(\lambda,\mu)
such that
G_{n}(V_{(\lambda,\mu)})=V_{\nu}
, where
V_{(\lambda,\mu)}
is the indecomposable object corresponding to
(\lambda,\mu)
, and
G_{n}:\mathcal{D}_{n}\rightarrow\operatorname{Rep}^{alg}_{\mathbb{C}}(GL_{n})
is the functor mentioned above, see details in Section
2
.
For a sequence
\bar{\nu}=(\nu^{(1)},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\nu^{(m)})
of integral dominant
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weights, let
(\lambda^{(a)},\mu^{(a)})
,
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
be such that
G_{n}(V_{(\lambda^{(a)},\mu^{(a)})})=V_{\nu^{(a)}}
. Denote
V(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})=V_{(\lambda^{(1)},\mu^{(1)})}\otimes\dots\otimes V_{(\lambda^{(m)},\mu^{(m)})}
. In Section
4
, we construct an algebra
\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}(V(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}))
such that the functor
G_{n}
induces a homomorphism
\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{n}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
, which becomes an isomorphism for sufficiently large
n
.

1.3.

A standard problem in the theory of quantum integrable models is the problem of simultaneous diagonalization of higher Hamiltonians. One way to find common eigenvectors and eigenvalues of the elements of
\mathcal{B}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
is to use the algebraic Bethe ansatz, see
[
13
]
. Each eigenvector obtained in this way is associated with a solution of a system of algebraic equations called the Bethe ansatz equations. The Bethe ansatz conjecture states that all common eigenvectors can be obtained in this way.

In
[
9
]
, the Bethe ansatz equations for the Gaudin model associated with a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
were interpreted as ”no-monodromy” conditions on a certain space of
{}^{L}\mathfrak{g}
-opers
\operatorname{Op}(z_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak z_{m})
, where
{}^{L}\mathfrak{g}
is the Langlands dual of
\mathfrak{g}
, see also
[
10
]
. The Bethe ansatz conjecture was reformulated in
[
9
]
as follows: there is a bijection between the set of common eigenvectors of the higher Hamiltonians and the set of monodromy-free
{}^{L}\mathfrak{g}
-opers from
\operatorname{Op}(z_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak z_{m})
. This conjecture was proved for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
in
[
14
]
, and for any semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
in
[
16
]
.

In the case
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, we have
{}^{L}\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}
, and a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-oper from
\operatorname{Op}(z_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak z_{m})
may be viewed as a differential operator of the form

D=\partial_{u}^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\sum_{a=1}^{m}\frac{b_{ij}^{(a)}}{(u-z_{a})^{j}}\partial_{u}^{n-i},

where we denote
\partial_{u}=d/du
. Then the no-monodromy conditions discussed above are polynomial relations on the coefficients
b_{ij}^{(a)}
saying that for each
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, the differential operator
D
has formal series solutions of the form

f_{a,i}(u)=(u-z_{a})^{n+\nu_{i}^{(a)}-i}+c(u-z_{a})^{n+\nu_{i}^{(a)}-i+1}+\dots,

and that it has regular singularity at
\infty
, see details in Section
5
. Here,
\nu^{(a)}=(\nu_{1}^{(a)},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\nu_{n}^{(a)})
is the
a
-th
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weight in the sequence
\bar{\nu}
used in the construction of
\mathcal{B}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
.

Let
p_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak p_{d}
be the polynomials such that the no-monodromy conditions are written as
p_{k}(b_{ij}^{(a)})=0
,
k=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak d
. Consider the free commutative unital algebra
\mathcal{A}_{n}
with free generators
y_{ij}^{(a)}
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak i
,
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
. Let
J_{n}(\bar{\nu})
be the ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
generated by the elements
p_{k}(y_{ij}^{(a)})
,
k=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak d
. The Bethe ansatz conjecture was proved in
[
16
]
by proving a theorem, which in case of
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
may be formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

The algebras
B_{n}(\bar{\nu})
and
\mathcal{A}_{n}/J_{n}(\bar{\nu})
are isomorphic.

In the second part of the paper, we formulate and prove an analog of this theorem for the algebra
\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
with some restrictions on the sequence of bipartitions
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
, see formula (
4.2
). In this case, the order of the differential operator
D
becomes an arbitrary complex number, so
D
becomes a pseudo-differential operator, i. e., a formal series of the form

\partial_{u}^{t}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\sum_{a=1}^{m}\frac{b_{ij}^{(a)}}{(u-z_{a})^{j}}\partial_{u}^{t-i}.

Therefore, we consider an infinitely generated free commutative unital algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
with free generators
y_{ij}^{(a)}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 1}
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak i
,
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, and construct an ideal
\underline{J}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
in
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
such that for sufficiently large
n
, the projection of
\underline{J}_{t=n}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
to
\mathcal{A}_{n}
coincides with
J_{n}(\bar{\nu})
, where
\bar{\nu}
is such that
G_{n}(V_{(\lambda^{(a)},\mu^{(a)})})=V_{\nu^{(a)}}
. Then we prove that for all but finitely many
t
, the algebras
\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
and
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}/\underline{J}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
are isomorphic (Theorem
6.10
). The key observation is that the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}/\underline{J}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
is finitely generated and for sufficiently large integers
n
, the ideal
\underline{J}_{n}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
contains all elements
y_{ij}^{(a)}
with
i>n
, see Lemma
6.6
.

At the moment, it is unclear to us how to construct the ideal
\underline{J}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
for any
(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
(not necessarily satisfying condition (
4.2
)). We indicate some technical challenges that appear in this case in Remarks
4.2
and
6.5
.

1.4.

Our study of the algebra
\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
is in particular motivated by the Bethe ansatz conjecture for the Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n|n^{\prime}}
associated with the super Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n|n^{\prime}}
, see
[
15
]
,
[
12
]
. This conjecture relates eigenvectors of
\mathcal{B}_{n|n^{\prime}}
with pseudo-differential operators of order
n-n^{\prime}
, which are ratios of monodromy-free differential operators. These ratios are described by some polynomial relations on coefficients of pseudo-differential operators, and we expect that these relations are exactly the relations in the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n|n^{\prime}}
.

On the other hand, there is a full (but not faithful) tensor functor
G_{n|n^{\prime}}:\mathcal{D}_{n-n^{\prime}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb{C}}(GL_{n|n^{\prime}})
to the category of finite-dimensional representations of the supergroup
GL_{n|n^{\prime}}
such that the functor
G_{n}
discussed above is the special case
n^{\prime}=0
of
G_{n|n^{\prime}}
. The existence of the functor
G_{n|n^{\prime}}
is a consequence of a certain universal property of the Deligne’s category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
(see
[
2
, Proposition 3.5.1]
). In fact, it was shown in
[
5
]
that for
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, the abelian envelope of
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is a certain limit of the categories
\operatorname{Rep}_{\mathbb{C}}(GL_{n|n^{\prime}})
as
n,n^{\prime}\rightarrow\infty
,
n-n^{\prime}=t
. It is easy to see that the functor
G_{n|n^{\prime}}
induces a surjective homomorphism of algebras
\varphi_{n|n^{\prime}}:\,\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{n-n^{\prime}}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{n|n^{\prime}}
. Therefore, the ideal
\underline{J}_{n-n^{\prime}}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
together with the kernel of the homomorphism
\varphi_{n|n^{\prime}}
give all relations in the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n|n^{\prime}}
. In Section
7
, we prove that the relations given by the ideal
\underline{J}_{n-n^{\prime}}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
are satisfied by the ratios of monodromy-free differential operators arising in
[
12
]
. It follows that for proving the Bethe ansatz conjecture for the Gaudin model in super case, it is important to describe the kernel of
\varphi_{n|n^{\prime}}
. We would like to do this in our future studies.

1.5.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give the definition of the Deligne’s category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
and discuss its properties that will be used later. In Section
3
, we recall the construction of the Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, and present different generating sets of this algebra. In Section
4
, we introduce the Bethe algebra
\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
in the Deligne’s category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
as well as its generating sets analogous to the generating sets of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
introduced in Section
3
. Section
5
is devoted to the ”no-monodromy” conditions, that is, to the construction of the ideal
J_{n}(\bar{\nu})
and its relation to the Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
. We construct the ideal
\underline{J}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
and prove an analog of Theorem
1.1
for
\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
in Section
6
. We show how our interpolations of the no-monodromy conditions is related to ratios of differential operators in Section
7
.

2.
Preliminaries on the Deligne’s category
\mathcal{D}_{t}

2.1.

Let
f
and
f^{\prime}
be finite (possibly empty) words in two letters:
\bullet
and
\circ
. Following
[
2
]
, define an
(f,f^{\prime})
-diagram to be a graph satisfying the next five properties:

(1)

Each vertex is either
\bullet
or
\circ
.

(2)

The vertices are positioned in two rows so that in the top row, we obtain the word
f
, and in the bottom row, we obtain the word
f^{\prime}
.

(3)

Each vertex is adjacent to exactly one edge.

(4)

If an edge connects two vertices in the same row, then it connects a black vertex
\bullet
and a white vertex
\circ
.

(5)

If an edge connects two vertices in different rows, then it connects either two black or two white vertices.

Example
.

If
f=\bullet\quad\bullet\quad\circ\quad\bullet\quad\circ\quad\circ
and
f^{\prime}=\bullet\quad\bullet\quad\circ\quad\circ
, then

is an
(f,f^{\prime})
-diagram.

Fix
t\in\mathbb{C}
. Define a
\mathbb{C}
-linear strict monoidal category
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}
as follows.

•

Objects of
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}
are finite (possibly empty) words in letters
\bullet
and
\circ
.

•

If
f
and
f^{\prime}
are two objects in
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}
, then
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}}(f,f^{\prime})
is a vector space with a basis consisting of all
(f,f^{\prime})
-diagrams.

•

If
X
is an
(f,f^{\prime})
-diagram and
Y
is an
(f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime})
-diagram, define the composition
Y\circ X
as follows:

(1)

stack
X
atop
Y
so that the bottom row of
X
is identified with the top row of
Y
,

(2)

erase any loop that appeared in step (1),

(3)

multiply the obtained
(f,f^{\prime\prime})
-diagram by
t^{d}
, where
d
is the number of loops erased in step (2).

We extend the composition to any morphisms
X\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}}(f,f^{\prime})
and
Y\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}}(f^{\prime},f^{\prime\prime})
by linearity.

•

Tensor product is given by superposition of words or diagrams from left to right.

.

V

V^{*}
) for the object
\bullet
(resp.,

) of

. Notice that as a monoidal category,
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}

2.2.

Consider the additive envelope
(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0})^{add}
of the category
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}
. By definition,
(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0})^{add}
is a category with objects
X=(X_{j})
given by finite-length tuples of objects from
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}
and morphisms

\varphi:(X_{j})\rightarrow(Y_{i}),\quad\varphi=(\varphi_{ij}),\quad\varphi_{ij}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}}(X_{j},Y_{i})

given by matrices of morphisms from
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}
and composed via the matrix multiplication:

(\varphi\circ\psi)_{ij}=\sum_{k}\varphi_{ik}\circ\psi_{kj}.

The category
(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0})^{add}
is additive with biproducts given by concatenation of tuples. It also naturally inherits
\mathbb{C}
-linear and monoidal structures from the category
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}
.

By definition, the Deligne’s category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is the Karoubi envelope of
\mathcal{D}_{t}
. That is, objects of
\mathcal{D}_{t}
are pairs
(X,e)
, where
X
is an object from
\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}
,
e
is an idempotent in
\operatorname{End}_{(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0})^{add}}(X)
, and

\operatorname{Hom}((X,e),(Y,f))=\{\varphi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0})^{add}}(X,Y)|f\circ\varphi=\varphi=\varphi\circ e\}.

The composition of morphisms in
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is inherited from
(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0})^{add}
. Also, the category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
naturally inherits monoidal and additive structures from
(\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0})^{add}
.

2.3.

A partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
is an infinite non-increasing sequence of non-negative integers stabilizing at zero. Denote
|\lambda|=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}
. We will also write
\emptyset
for the partition
(0,0,\dots)
. A bipartition is a pair of partitions.

It was proved in
[
2
]
that any indecomposable object of the Deligne’s category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is isomorphic to
V_{(\lambda,\mu)}=(T^{r,s},e_{(\lambda,\mu)})
for some bipartition
(\lambda,\mu)
, where
r=|\lambda|
,
s=|\mu|
, and
e_{(\lambda,\mu)}
is a certain idempotent in
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{t}^{0}}(T^{r,s})
, see
[
2
, Section 4.3]
for details.

Notice that for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, the algebras
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}(V^{\otimes m})
and
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{t}}((V^{*})^{\otimes m})
are isomorphic to the group algebra
\mathbb{C}[S_{m}]
of the permutation group
S_{m}
. Under this isomorphism, the idempotents
e_{(\lambda,\emptyset)}
and
e_{(\emptyset,\lambda)}
with
|\lambda|=m
correspond to a scalar multiple of the Young symmetrizer
c_{\lambda}
(see, e.g.,
[
19
, Lecture 4]
for the definition of
c_{\lambda}
). In particular,
e_{(\lambda,\emptyset)}
and
e_{(\emptyset,\lambda)}
do not depend on the complex number
t
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let
V_{\text{\scalebox{0.7}{$\square$}}}
be the tautological representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
. Recall that a rational representation of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
is a subrepresentation of
V_{\text{\scalebox{0.7}{$\square$}}}^{\otimes r}\otimes(V_{\text{\scalebox{0.7}{$\square$}}}^{*})^{\otimes s}
for some
r,s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
. The Deligne’s category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
is an ”interpolation” of the category
\mathcal{R}_{n}
of rational representations of
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
in the sense that there exists an additive modular full essentially surjective functor
G_{n}:\mathcal{D}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{n}
, see
[
2
, Section 5]
for details.

For a bipartition
(\lambda,\mu)
, let
l_{\lambda}
and
l_{\mu}
be the numbers of nonzero elements in partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
, respectively. If
n\geqslant l_{\lambda}+l_{\mu}
, then the functor
G_{n}
sends the indecomposable object
V_{(\lambda,\mu)}
to the irreducible
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-module
V_{\nu}
of highest weight
\nu
defined by
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots)
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\dots)
as follows:

(2.1)

\nu=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\lambda_{l_{\lambda}},0,0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak 0,-\mu_{l_{\mu}},,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak-\mu_{2},-\mu_{1}).

If
n<l_{\lambda}+l_{\mu}
, then
G_{n}(V_{(\lambda,\mu)})=\{0\}
.

Fix
m
bipartitions
(\lambda^{(a)},\mu^{(a)})
,
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
.
Denote
\bar{V}=V_{(\lambda^{(1)},\mu^{(1)})}\otimes V_{(\lambda^{(2)},\mu^{(2)})}\otimes\dots\otimes V_{(\lambda^{(m)},\mu^{(m)})}
and
N=\sum_{a=1}^{m}(|\lambda^{(a)}|+|\mu^{(a)}|)
. Let
G_{n}^{\bar{V}}:\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(\bar{V})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{R}_{n}}(G_{n}(\bar{V}))
be the surjective homomorphism induced by the functor
G_{n}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For each
n\geqslant N
,
G_{n}^{\bar{V}}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Notice that
G_{n}^{\bar{V}}
is a restriction of
G_{n}^{\bar{W}}
, where

\bar{W}=V^{\otimes|\lambda^{(1)}|}\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes|\mu^{(1)}|}\otimes\dots\otimes V^{\otimes|\lambda^{(m)}|}\otimes(V^{*})^{\otimes|\mu^{(m)}|}.

Therefore, it is enough to show that
G_{n}^{\bar{W}}
is injective.

For any objects
A
,
B
, and
C
of
\mathcal{D}_{n}
or
\mathcal{R}_{n}
, we have
A\otimes B\cong B\otimes A
and
\operatorname{Hom}(A,B\otimes C)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(A\otimes C^{*},B)
. Thus, we have vector space isomorphisms
h_{1}:\,\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(\bar{W})\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{D}_{n}}(V^{\otimes N})
and
h_{2}:\,\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{R}_{n}}(G_{n}(\bar{W}))\rightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{R}_{n}}(V_{\text{\scalebox{0.7}{$\square$}}}^{\otimes N})=\mathbb{C}[S_{N}]
. The last equality follows from the Schur-Weyl duality and our assumption
n\geqslant N
. The map
h_{2}G_{n}^{\bar{W}}h_{1}^{-1}
sends different diagrams to different elements of
S_{N}
, therefore, it is injective, and the lemma follows.
∎

3.
The Bethe algebra of the Gaudin model associated with
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}

3.1.

Let
e_{ij}
,
i,j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
be the standard basis of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
, that is,
e_{ij}
is the matrix with
1
on the intersection of the row
i
and the column
j
and
0
everywhere else. Fix distinct complex numbers
z_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak z_{m}
. For each
i,j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, define a
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
-valued rational function
\mathcal{L}_{ij}(u)
of a variable
u
by the formula:

\mathcal{L}_{ij}(u)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\frac{e_{ij}^{(r)}}{u-z_{r}},

where
e_{ij}^{(r)}=1\otimes\dots\otimes 1\otimes\underset{r-th}{e_{ij}}\otimes 1\otimes\dots\otimes 1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}(u)
be an
n\times n
matrix with entries
\mathcal{L}_{ij}(u)
. Consider a differential operator

\operatorname{tr}\left(\partial_{u}-\mathcal{L}(u)\right)^{k}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}S_{kl}(u)\partial_{u}^{k-l}.

We have
S_{k0}(u)=1
, and

(3.1)

S_{kl}(u)=\sum_{r=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\frac{S_{klj}^{(r)}}{(u-z_{r})^{j}},\quad 1\leqslant l\leqslant k

for some
S_{klj}^{(r)}\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
. It is known, see for example,
[
1
]
, that the elements
S_{klj}^{(r)}
,
k\geqslant 1
,
1\leqslant l\leqslant k
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant l
,
1\leqslant r\leqslant m
commute with each other.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{n}
be the unital commutative subalgebra of
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
generated by the elements
S_{klj}^{(r)}
,
k\geqslant 1
,
1\leqslant l\leqslant k
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant l
,
1\leqslant r\leqslant m
. The algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}
is known as the algebra of higher Hamiltonians (the Bethe algebra) of the quantum rational Gaudin model, see
[
9
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
1
]
,
[
13
]
.

Recall that
V_{\nu}
denotes the finite dimensional representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
with highest weight
\nu
. Let
\bar{\nu}=(\nu^{(1)},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\nu^{(m)})
be a sequence of dominant integral
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weights. The Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}
acts on the tensor product
V(\bar{\nu})=V_{\nu^{(1)}}\otimes\dots\otimes V_{\nu^{(m)}}
. Denote by
\mathcal{B}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
the projection of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
in
\operatorname{End}(V(\bar{\nu}))
. In Section
4
below, we will construct an analog of the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}(\bar{\nu})
in the Deligne’s category
\mathcal{D}_{t}
.

3.2.

In what follows, we will need another generating set for the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, which we are going to present in this subsection.

For an
n\times n
matrix
A
with possibly non-commuting entries
a_{ij}
,
i,j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, define its column determinant
\operatorname{cdet}A
by the formula

\operatorname{cdet}A=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\operatorname{sign}(\sigma)\,a_{\sigma(1)1}a_{\sigma(2)2}\dots a_{\sigma(n)n}.

The matrix
A
is called a Manin matrix if
[a_{ij},a_{pq}]=[a_{pj},a_{iq}]
for all
i,j,p,q=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
.
It is known, see
[
1
]
, that
\partial_{u}-\mathcal{L}(u)
is a Manin matrix.

We will need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
A
is a Manin matrix. Denote
T_{k}=\operatorname{tr}A^{k}
. Let
\sigma_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\sigma_{n}
be the coefficients of the ”characteristic polynomial”:

\operatorname{cdet}(1+\alpha A)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sigma_{k}\alpha^{k}.

Then the Newton identities

\sigma_{k}=(-1)^{k+1}\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(-1)^{i}\sigma_{i}T_{k-i}

hold for every
k=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
.

The proof of the lemma can be found in
[
1
]
.

Consider a differential operator

(3.2)

\operatorname{cdet}\bigl[1+\alpha\bigl(\partial_{u}-\mathcal{L}(u)\bigr)\bigr]=\sum_{r=0}^{n}\sum_{s=0}^{r}\alpha^{r}\tilde{B}_{rs}(u)\partial_{u}^{r-s}.

The coefficients
\tilde{B}_{rs}(u)
are
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
-valued rational functions of
u
with possible poles only at
z_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak z_{m}
. For all
r=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
, we have
\tilde{B}_{r0}(u)=1
and

(3.3)

\tilde{B}_{rs}(u)=\sum_{a=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{s}\frac{\tilde{B}_{rsj}^{(a)}}{(u-z_{a})^{j}},\quad 1\leqslant s\leqslant r

for some
\tilde{B}_{rsj}^{(a)}\in U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
.

Applying Lemma
3.1
to the matrix
\partial_{u}-\mathcal{L}(u)
, we see that the elements
\tilde{B}_{rsj}^{(a)}
,
r=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
s=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak r
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak s
,
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
generate the Bethe algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Denote
B_{i}(u)\coloneqq\tilde{B}_{ii}(u)
and
B^{(a)}_{ij}\coloneqq\tilde{B}_{iij}^{(a)}
.

Proposition 3.2
.

The elements
B_{ij}^{(a)}
,
i=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
j=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak i
,
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
generate the algebra
\mathcal{B}_{n}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be the algebra of differential operators with respect to the variable
u
with
U(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})^{\otimes m}
-valued rational coefficients. Introduce a map
\Phi
:
\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}[\alpha^{-1}]

\Phi:\,\sum_{r}a_{r}(u)\partial_{u}^{r}\mapsto\sum_{r}a_{r}(u)(\partial_{u}+\alpha^{-1})^{r}.

It is straightforward to check that
\Phi
is a homomorphism of algebras. Therefore,

(3.4)

\begin{split}\Phi\bigl[\operatorname{cdet}\bigl(\partial_{u}-\mathcal{L}(u)\bigr)\bigr]&=\operatorname{cdet}\bigl[\Phi\bigl(\partial_{u}-\mathcal{L}(u)\bigr)\bigr]=\\
&=\alpha^{-n}\operatorname{cdet}\bigl[1+\alpha\bigl(\partial_{u}-\mathcal{L}(u)\bigr)\bigr].\end{split}

Using the relation

(\partial_{u}+\alpha^{-1})^{r}=\sum_{s=0}^{r}\binom{r}{s}\alpha^{-r+s}\partial_{u}^{s},

one can check that
\sum_{r=0}^{N}\sum_{s=0}^{M}a_{rs}\alpha^{-N+r}\partial_{u}^{s}\in\mathcal{D}[\alpha^{-1}]
belongs to the image of
\Phi
if and only if
a_{rs}=0
for
s>r
, and

a_{rs}=a_{r-s,0}\binom{N-r+s}{s}.

Since by (
3.4
), the differential operator
\alpha^{-n}\operatorname{cdet}\bigl[1+\alpha\bigl(\partial_{u}-\mathcal{L}(u)\bigr)\bigr]
belongs to the image of
\Phi
, we have

(3.5)

\tilde{B}_{rs}(u)=\tilde{B}_{ss}(u)\binom{n-s}{r-s}

for all
r=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
s=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak r
, and the proposition follows.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Notice that the generators
B_{ij}^{(a)}
of
\mathcal{B}_{n}

\bar{A}_{ij}
by crossing out columns and rows of

that contain elements

,

.

Proposition 5.1
.

The differential equation
Df=0
has solutions
f_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak f_{n}
of the form (
5.2
) if and only if (
5.3
) holds and

(5.5)

\det\bar{A}_{ij}=0,\quad\quad 1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n.

Proof.

Suppose that we have solutions
f_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak f_{n}
of the form (
5.2
). Then, as noted above, condition (
5.3
) holds. We may assume that for coefficients
a^{(i)}_{j}
of
f_{i}
, see formula (
5.2
), we have
a^{(i)}_{m_{k}-m_{i}}=0
for all
k>i
.

Equating the coefficients for
x^{m_{i}+l-n}
on the both sides of the relation
Df_{i}=0
, we have

(5.6)

\sum_{j=0}^{l-1}a^{(i)}_{j}r_{l-j}(m_{i}+j)+a^{(i)}_{l}r_{0}(m_{i}+l)=0.

For each
l=1,2,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m_{i}-m_{j}
such that
m_{i}+l\neq m_{k}
,

, denote by
\bar{P}_{il}
the principal minor of the matrix
\bar{A}_{ij}
with the last row

. Using the expansion of
\bar{P}_{il}
along the last column and induction on

, one checks that (
5.6
) implies

(5.7)

a^{(i)}_{l}=(-1)^{l}\bar{P}_{il}\left(\underset{k=1}{\overset{l}{{\prod}^{\prime}}}r_{0}(m_{i}+k)\right)^{-1},

where
\underset{k=1}{\overset{l}{{\prod}^{\prime}}}
denotes the product over

such that
m_{i}+k\neq m_{j}
for all

), we have

for any

, in particular
\underset{k=1}{\overset{l}{{\prod}^{\prime}}}r_{0}(m_{i}+k)\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, and the right hand side of formula (
5.7
) is well-defined.

Now, take

such that
m_{i}+l=m_{k}
for some

in formula (
5.6
). Because of condition (
5.3

But because of (
5.7
), the left hand side of (

) multiplied by
\underset{j=1}{\overset{l-1}{{\prod}^{\prime}}}r_{0}(m_{i}+j)

Conversely, assume that conditions (

Let

be the algebra of
\mathcal{A}
-valued rational functions of

. Let
\mathcal{D}\bigl(\mathcal{A}((x))\bigr)

be the algebras of differential operators with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}((x))
and
\mathcal{A}(u)
, respectively. For any

, define a map

\begin{split}L_{z}:\quad&\mathcal{D}\bigl(\mathcal{A}(u)\bigr)\rightarrow\mathcal{D}\bigl(\mathcal{A}((x))\bigr),\\
&\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}(u)\partial_{u}^{n-i}\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{z}\bigl[b_{i}(u)\bigr](x)\partial_{x}^{n-i}.\end{split}

Here,

is the Laurent series of
b_{i}(u)
at

, where we take

.

We will also need a map

defined by

L_{\infty}:\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}(u)\partial_{u}^{n-i}\mapsto\left(-\frac{1}{x^{2}}\right)^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{0}\bigl[b_{i}(u^{-1})\bigr](x)(-x^{2}\partial_{x})^{n-i}.

Now, let us take
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{n}
, where
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is the commutative algebra with free generators
y_{ij}^{(a)}
,

,

,
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
. Let
\bar{z}=(z_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak z_{m})
be a sequence of pairwise distinct complex numbers, and let
\bar{\nu}=(\nu^{(1)},\nu^{(2)},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\nu^{(m)})
be a sequence of dominant integral
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
-weights. Consider a differential operator

defined by the formula

(5.10)

D^{\mathbb{C}[y]}=\partial_{u}^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\sum_{a=1}^{m}\frac{y_{ij}^{(a)}}{(u-z_{a})^{j}}\partial_{u}^{n-i}.

Recall the polynomials

and matrices

. Notice that for each

is of the form (
5.1
). Taking

and matrices

,

Theorem 5.2
.

The kernel of the homomorphism

\begin{split}\varphi:\quad&\mathcal{A}_{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{n}(\bar{\nu})\\
&y^{(a)}_{ij}\mapsto B_{ij}^{(a)}(\bar{\nu})\end{split}

is the ideal

.

This theorem is one of the main results in the study of the Bethe ansatz method for the quantum Gaudin model. It is proved in
[
16
]
.

A result similar to Theorem
5.2
, can be found in work
[
14
]
, where it is proved that

is isomorphic to the algebra of functions on an intersection of Schubert cells. Points of this intersection correspond to kernels of the Fuchsian differential operators from

. Considering

as the algebra of functions on differential operators is more suitable for our goals since it is not clear how to generalize the intersection of Schubert cells to arbitrary complex

The interpolation of the no-monodromy conditions

Recall that
\bar{\lambda}
and
\bar{\mu}
are sequences of partitions satisfying condition (
4.2
).
In this section, we are going to prove an analog of Theorem
5.2
for the algebra
\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
.

For each
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, let us now think of
\mathcal{A}_{n}
as a subalgebra in the infinitely generated free commutative algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
with free generators
y_{ij}^{(a)}
,
1\leqslant a\leqslant m
,
i\geqslant 1
,
1\leqslant j\leqslant i
. Recall the generators
\underline{B}_{ij}^{(a)}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
,

,

,

of the algebra
\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu})
. Our goal is to find the kernel of the homomorphism

\begin{split}\underline{\varphi}_{t}:\quad&\mathcal{A}_{\infty}\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{B}}_{t}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}),\\
&y^{(a)}_{ij}\mapsto\underline{B}_{ij}^{(a)}(\bar{\lambda},\bar{\mu}).\end{split}

Recall that
l_{\lambda}^{(a)}
(resp.,
l_{\mu}^{(a)}
) is the number of nonzero elements in the partition
\lambda^{(a)}
(resp.,
\mu^{(a)}
). Denote
l^{(a)}=l_{\lambda}^{(a)}+l_{\mu}^{(a)}
.

Let
n
be an integer such that
n\geqslant\max_{a}l^{(a)}
. For each
a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak m
, let
\nu^{(a)}=(\nu^{(a)}_{1},\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak\nu^{(a)}_{n})
be a
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
weight defined by
\lambda^{(a)}
and
\mu^{(a)}
according to formula (
2.1
).

To find the kernel of the map
\underline{\varphi}_{t}
, we will first construct a generating set of the ideal
J_{n}(\bar{\nu})
that ”stabilizes for large
n
”.

In this section, for convenience, we will sometimes drop the upper index
(a)
assuming that it is equal to some fixed value, say,
a=1
, for example,
l\coloneqq l^{(1)}
,
r_{k}(\alpha)\coloneqq r_{k}^{(1)}(\alpha)
,
A_{ij}\coloneqq A^{(1)}_{ij}
, and so on.

Recall that

r_{k}(\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}b_{i,-i+k}\alpha^{\underline{n-i}},

where
\alpha^{\underline{j}}=\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)\dots(\alpha-j+1)
and
b_{i,-i+k}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
are the coefficients in the expansion

\sum_{a=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\frac{y^{(a)}_{ij}}{(u-z_{a})^{j}}=b_{i,-i}(u-z_{1})^{-i}+b_{i,-i+1}(u-z_{1})^{-i+1}+b_{i,-i+2}(u-z_{1})^{-i+2}+\dots.

For each
i=0,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak n
,
j\geqslant-i
, define
\tilde{b}_{ij}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}
by

r_{k}(\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\tilde{b}_{i,-i+k}(\alpha-l_{\mu})^{\underline{n-i}},

and consider polynomials
\tilde{r}_{k}(\alpha)
given by

\tilde{r}_{k}(\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{l+k}\tilde{b}_{i,-i+k}(\alpha-n+l_{\lambda}+k)^{\underline{l+k-i}},

where we assume that
\tilde{b}_{ij}=0
if

.

Notice that

(6.1)

\displaystyle(\alpha-l_{\mu})^{\underline{n-l-k}}\tilde{r}_{k}(\alpha)=r_{k}(\alpha)-\sum_{i=l+k+1}^{n}b_{i,-i+k}(\alpha-l_{\mu})^{\underline{n-i}},\quad

\displaystyle\text{if}\quad k+l\leqslant n,

(6.2)

\displaystyle\tilde{r}_{k}(\alpha)=(\alpha-n+l_{\lambda}+k)^{\underline{l+k-n}}r_{k}(\alpha),\quad

\displaystyle\text{if}\quad k+l\geqslant n.

For each
1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n
, let
M_{ij}
be the matrix constructed from
A_{ij}
by replacing each entry

with
(\alpha-n+l_{\lambda}+k-d)^{\underline{k-d-s}}\tilde{r}_{s}(\alpha)
, where

, and

is the number of the row containing the entry. Recall that the matrix
\bar{A}_{ij}
was constructed from

by deleting certain rows and columns. Let
\bar{M}_{ij}
be the matrix constructed from

in a similar way.

We will need the following two lemmas.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that
l_{\lambda}\geqslant 1
. Then for each

,
j<i\leqslant n
, we have

(6.3)

r_{0}(m_{n+1-j})=c_{1}\tilde{r}_{0}(m_{n+1-j})+M_{1},

(6.4)

\det\bar{A}_{n+1-i,n+1-j}=c_{2}\det\bar{M}_{n+1-i,n+1-j}+M_{2},

where
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
are nonzero complex numbers, and
M_{1}
,
M_{2}
belong to the ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
generated by
\tilde{b}_{q,-q+s}
,
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
,
q>l+s
.

Proof.

Since
l_{\lambda}\geqslant 1
, by condition (
4.2
), we have
l_{\mu}=0
and
l=l_{\lambda}
.
Since

, one can use formula (
6.1
) for

to see that

r_{0}(m_{n+1-j})=(m_{n+1-j})^{\underline{n-l}}\tilde{r}_{0}(m_{n+1-j})+M_{1},

where

belongs to the ideal in
\mathcal{A}_{n}
generated by
\tilde{b}_{q,-q+s}
,
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
,
q>l+s
.
For

, we have

, therefore

, and formula (
6.3
) is proved.

To prove formula (
6.4
), put

and fix some

. First, suppose that

. Using (

), one can see that up to summation with linear combinations of the elements

,

,

, the

-th row of

is the

-th row of

. For

. Therefore,

.

If

, then, using (
6.2
), one can see that up to summation with linear combinations of the elements
\tilde{b}_{q,-q+s}
,
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
,
q>l+s
, the
(d+1)
-th row of
M_{n+1-i,n+1-j}
is the
(d+1)
-th row of

multiplied by

. Since

, we have

∎

except the roots of the polynomials

On ratios of differential operators with no monodromy

7.1.
Pseudo-differential operators

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an associative unital commutative algebra, and let
\partial:\,\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
be a derivation on
\mathcal{A}
. Then a pseudo-differential operator of order
t\in\mathbb{C}
with coefficitents in
\mathcal{A}
is a formal series of the form

(7.1)

\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}a_{r}\partial^{t-r},\quad\quad a_{r}\in\mathcal{A},\quad a_{0}\neq 0.

Denote the set of all pseudo-differential operators with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}
as
\Psi\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
. One can add and multiply series of the form (
7.1
) in a usual way if for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
, we set

\partial^{\mu}a=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mu^{\underline{i}}}{i!}(\partial^{i}a)\partial^{\mu-i}.

It can be checked that this multiplication is well-defined and associative, thus, making
\Psi\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})
an associative algebra.

(7.30)

L_{z_{a}}(\bar{D}_{n}\bar{D}^{-1}_{n^{\prime}})\in\Psi\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A}((x)),(\lambda^{(a)}),\quad a=1,\allowbreak\dots,\allowbreak k.

By the construction of the ideals
J_{n}(\bar{\nu})

J_{n^{\prime}}(\bar{\eta})

\bar{D}_{n}

7.5

7.6

(7.31)

\bar{D}_{n}\bar{D}_{n^{\prime}}^{-1}\in\Psi\mathcal{D}_{\infty}(\mathcal{A}(u)).

By the definition of the ideal
\underline{J}_{n-n^{\prime}}
and its image
J_{n|n^{\prime}}

\mathcal{A}_{n|n^{\prime}}

J_{n|n^{\prime}}

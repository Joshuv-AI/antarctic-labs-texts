Quasi-Fuchsian vs Negative curvature metrics on surface groups
Ethan Fricker, Alex Furman

Abstract
. We compare two families of left-invariant metrics on a surface group Γ = π 1 ​ ( Σ ) \Gamma=\pi_{1}(\Sigma) in the context of course-geometry. One family comes from Riemannian metrics of negative curvature on the the surface Σ \Sigma , and another from quasi-Fuchsian representations of Γ \Gamma . We show that the Teichmuller space 𝒯 ⁡ ( Σ ) \operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma) is the only common part of these two families, even when viewed from the coarse-geometric perspective.

1.
Introduction and Statement of the main result

1.A.
Introduction and Background

Let
\Sigma
be a closed surface of genus at least two,
and
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
its fundamental group.
The Teichmuller space
\operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma)
has several equivalent descriptions:
as the moduli space of (i) complex structures, or (ii) conformal structures,
or (iii) Riemannian structures of constant curvature
-1
on
\Sigma
,
or as (iv) the space of discrete cocompact representations

\Gamma\to\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbf{R}})
, up to conjugation.
The latter two points of view can be extended as follows:

•

\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)
– the space of all Riemannian structures
of possibly variable negative curvature, up to isotopy and scaling.

•

\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)
– the space of all convex cocompact representations

\Gamma=\pi_{1}(\Sigma){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbf{C}})\cong\operatorname{Isom}^{+}({\mathbf{H}}^{3}),

up to conjugation.

Both
\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)
and
\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)
arise from convex cocompact isometric

\Gamma
-actions on CAT(-1) spaces:
the
\Gamma
-action by deck transformations on the
universal cover
(\tilde{\Sigma},d_{\tilde{g}})
in the Riemannian case,
and the
\Gamma
-action on
{\mathbf{H}}^{3}
in the quasi-Fuchsian case.

We can put these notions into an even broader context by looking at the space

\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
of equivalence classes
[d]
of
left-invariant metrics
d
on
\Gamma
obtained from restricting
the metric of the underlying
Gromov-hyperbolic space to a
\Gamma
-orbit.
Here two metrics
d,d^{\prime}
on
\Gamma
are equivalent if they are bounded distance
from each other after scaling:

d\sim d^{\prime}\qquad\textrm{if}\qquad\exists k,A:\quad|d^{\prime}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})-k\cdot d(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})|\leq A.

This perspective, introduced by the second author in
[
11
]

(see also more recent treatment in Bader–Furman
[
1
]
),
allows to observe possible ”geometries” of
\Sigma
from the ”outside” by
studying the corresponding classes
[d]\in\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
of metrics
d
on
\Gamma
.
The space
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
can be defined for a general non-elementary Gromov hyperbolic group
\Gamma
,
and
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
contains classes of metrics on
\Gamma
from various sources, such as
word metrics on
\Gamma
, Green metrics associated with symmetric generating
random walks on
\Gamma
(see Blachère–Haïssinsky–Mathieu
[
4
,
3
]
),
Anosov representations of
\Gamma
in higher rank simple Lie groups
(see Dey–Kapovich
[
10
]
), etc.

To avoid ambiguity in scaling we can normalize metrics
d
by the growth

h_{d}=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R}\log\#\left\{{\gamma\in\Gamma}\ |\ {d(\gamma,e)<R}\right\},

replacing
d
by
\hat{d}=h_{d}\cdot d
, so that
h_{\hat{d}}=1
.
For
\delta\in\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
we can define:

•

Marked Length Spectrum
\ell_{\delta}:\Gamma\to{\mathbf{R}}_{+}
given by the limit

\ell_{\delta}\left(\gamma\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\hat{d}(\gamma^{n},e)}{n}

where
\delta=[d]
and
\hat{d}=h_{d}\cdot d
.
Note that
\ell_{\delta}
is constant on conjugacy classes,
so we can write it as
\ell_{\delta}:\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}\to{\mathbf{R}}_{+}
.

•

Patterson-Sullivan-like
\Gamma
-invariant measure class

[\nu_{\delta}^{\rm PS}]
on
\partial\Gamma
(see Coorneart
[
7
]
,
and
[
11
,
1
]
).

•

Bowen–Margulis–Sullivan-like
\Gamma
-invariant Radon measure
m_{\delta}^{\rm BMS}

on the space
\partial^{(2)}\Gamma
of distinct pairs
(\xi,\eta)
of points on
\partial\Gamma
(see
[
11
,
1
]
).

In
[
11
]
(see also Bader–Furman
[
1
]
), it was shown that each
\delta\in\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}

is determined by each of these objects.
Furthermore, extending a prior work of Bader–Muchnik
[
2
]
, Garncarek
[
12
]

showed that for each
\delta\in\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
the quasi-regular unitary
\Gamma
-representation

\pi_{\delta}:\Gamma{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}U(\partial\Gamma,\nu_{\delta}^{\rm PS})

is irreducible, and that the map
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\hat{\Gamma}
,
\delta\mapsto\pi_{\delta}
,
is also injective.
Thus
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
can be embedded into any obe of the the following spaces:

{\mathbf{R}}_{+}^{\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}},\qquad\operatorname{Prob}(\partial\Gamma),\qquad{\rm Meas}_{\Gamma}\left(\partial^{(2)}\Gamma\right),\qquad\hat{\Gamma}.

The space
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
is also equipped with a natural metric:
given two classes
\delta=[d]
,
\delta^{\prime}=[d^{\prime}]
in
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
we can define the
(log) Lipschitz distance by

\rho_{\operatorname{Lip}}\left({\delta},{\delta^{\prime}}\right):=\log\left(\inf\left\{{\frac{K}{k}}\ |\ {\exists A,\ k\cdot d-A\leq d^{\prime}\leq K\cdot d+A}\right\}\right).

It is clear from the definition that
\rho_{\operatorname{Lip}}\left({-},{-}\right)
is symmetric
and satisfies the triangle inequality.
One can see that for any
a,b\in\Gamma\setminus\{e\}
one has

\left|\log\left(\frac{\ell_{\delta}(a)}{\ell_{\delta}(b)}:\frac{\ell_{\delta^{\prime}}(a)}{\ell_{\delta^{\prime}}(b)}\right)\right|\leq\rho_{\operatorname{Lip}}\left({\delta},{\delta^{\prime}}\right).

This shows that
\rho_{\operatorname{Lip}}\left({\delta},{\delta^{\prime}}\right)=0
implies
\ell_{\delta}=\ell_{\delta^{\prime}}
,
which occurs only when
\delta=\delta^{\prime}
. So
\rho_{\operatorname{Lip}}\left({},{}\right)
is indeed a metric on
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}

(see also a recent work of Cantrell–Tanaka
[
6
]
for a more
detailed picture).

1.B.
Riemannian and Quasi-Fuchsian structures on Surfaces

In this paper we focus on surface group
\Gamma=\pi_{1}(\Sigma)
and two specific
sources for
\delta\in\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
: namely
\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)
and
\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)
.

For the case of negatively curved Riemannian metric
g
on
\Sigma
,
fix
x\in\tilde{\Sigma}
and consider the metric on
\Gamma

d_{g,x}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}):=d_{\tilde{g}}(\gamma_{1}x,\gamma_{2}x).

Since
|d_{g,x}-d_{g,x^{\prime}}|\leq\operatorname{diam}(\Sigma,g)
the class
[d_{g,x}]

does not depend on the choice of
x\in\tilde{\Sigma}
,
and we can denote this class by
\delta_{g}=[d_{g,x}]
.
Note that
h_{d_{g,x}}
is the topological entropy of the geodesic flow
on the unit tangent bundle
T^{1}\Sigma
to
\Sigma
, and we assume that all
g\in\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)

are normalized so that
h_{d_{g,x}}=1
.
We have a map

(1.1)

i:\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)\ {\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\ \operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}.

The Marked Length Spectrum Rigidity Conjecture, that for surfaces
was proved by Otal
[
14
]
and Croke
[
8
]
,
asserts that a Riemannian structure
g
of variable negative curvature on a surface
\Sigma

is uniquely determined by the function
\ell_{g}:\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}\to{\mathbf{R}}
.
As a consequence, we obtain:

Proposition 1.1
.

The map
\mathscr{R}(\Sigma){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
,
i:g\mapsto\delta_{g}
, is injective.

Our second source of examples, are quasi-Fuchsian representations.
For
q\in\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)
choose a representation

\pi:\Gamma\to\operatorname{Isom}^{+}({\mathbf{H}}^{3})\cong\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbf{C}})

in this class and a point
y\in{\mathbf{H}}^{3}
and consider
the metric on
\Gamma
:

d_{\pi,y}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}):=d_{{\mathbf{H}}^{3}}\left(\pi(\gamma_{1}).y,\pi(\gamma_{2}).y\right).

The class
[d_{\pi,y}]
does not depend on the choice of
y\in{\mathbf{H}}^{3}

and remains unchanged if
\pi
is replaced by
a conjugate
\gamma\mapsto g\pi(\gamma)g^{-1}
;
thus we write
\delta_{q}
for
[d_{\pi,y}]
.
This gives a well defined map

(1.2)

j:\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)\ {\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\ \operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}.

One can deduce from a work of Burger
[
5
]
(or Dal’bo–Kim
[
9
]
),
the following.

Proposition 1.2
.

The map
\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma){\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
,
j:q\mapsto\delta_{q}
, is injective.

Hence one might view each of
\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)
and
\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)
as being
embedded in
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
.

Remark 1.3
.

We note in passing that the uniformization theorem allows us to view

\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)
as a bundle over
\operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma)
with fibers that can be identified
with the positive cone
C^{\infty}_{+}(\Sigma)/{\mathbf{R}}_{+}
; in particular
\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)
is connected.
One can show that the map (
1.1
) is continuous, and so the image
i(\mathscr{R}(\Sigma))

in
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
is connected.

Ahlfors and Bers showed that
\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)
can be identified with
\operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma)\times\operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma)
, and is in particular connected.
The map (
1.2
) can be shown to be continuous;
hence the image
j(\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma))
is a connected subset of
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
.

It is natural to wonder whether the intersection

i(\mathscr{R}(\Sigma))\cap j(\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma))\subset\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}

contains anything except for the image of
\operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma)
.
In other words, is it true that given a quasi-Fuchsian representation

\pi:\Gamma{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\longrightarrow}\limits}}\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbf{C}})

and a negatively curved metric
g
on the surface
\Sigma
, there exist constants
k,A
and points
x\in\tilde{\Sigma}
,
y\in{\mathbf{H}}^{3}
, so that

k\cdot d_{\tilde{g}}(\gamma.x,x)-A\leq d_{{\mathbf{H}}^{3}}(\pi(\gamma).y,y)\leq k\cdot d_{\tilde{g}}(\gamma.x,x)+A\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma)

only if
g
has constant curvature,
\pi
is conjugate into
\operatorname{PSL}_{2}({\mathbf{R}})
,
and
(\Sigma,g)
and
\pi
represent the same point in
\operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma)
?

Our main result answers this affirmatively.

Theorem A
.

The images of
\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)
and
\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)
in
\operatorname{\mathscr{D}_{\Gamma}}
have only
\operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma)

in common. Moreover, for any
q\in\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)\setminus\operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma)
there is

\alpha_{q}>0
so that

\rho_{\operatorname{Lip}}\left({\delta_{q}},{\delta_{g}}\right)\geq\alpha_{q}>0

for all
g\in\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)
.

The following natural question remains open.

Question 1.4
.

Is it true that for
g\in\mathscr{R}(\Sigma)\setminus\operatorname{\mathscr{T}}(\Sigma)
there exists
\beta_{g}>0

so that

\rho_{\operatorname{Lip}}\left({\delta_{q}},{\delta_{g}}\right)\geq\beta_{g}>0

for all
q\in\operatorname{\mathscr{Q}\mathscr{F}}(\Sigma)
?

Recall that given a point

Achievable Information-Energy Region in the Finite Block-Length Regime with Finite Constellations
Sadaf ul Zuhra, Samir M. Perlaza, H. Vincent Poor, Eitan Altman

Abstract
This paper characterizes an achievable information-energy region of simultaneous information and energy transmission over an additive white Gaussian noise channel. This analysis is performed in the finite block-length regime with finite constellations. More specifically, a method for constructing a family of codes is proposed and the set of achievable tuples of information rate, energy rate, decoding error probability (DEP) and energy outage probability (EOP) is characterized. Using existing converse results, it is shown that the construction is information rate, energy rate, and EOP optimal. The achieved DEP is, however, sub-optimal.

System Model

Consider a communication system formed by a transmitter, an information receiver (IR), and an energy harvester (EH). The objective of the transmitter is to simultaneously send information to the IR at a rate of
R

B

, the outputs of the channel are the random vectors

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{Y}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{N}}_{1},\mbox{ and }

(1a)

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{Z}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{x}}+{\boldsymbol{N}}_{2},

(1b)

where
n
is the duration of the transmission in channel uses; and the vectors
{\boldsymbol{Y}}=(Y_{1},Y_{2},\ldots,Y_{n})^{\sf{T}}\in\mathds{C}^{n}
and
{\boldsymbol{Z}}=(Z_{1},Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n})^{\sf{T}}\in\mathds{C}^{n}
are the inputs of the IR and the EH, respectively. The components of the random vectors
{\boldsymbol{N}}_{1}=(N_{1,1},N_{1,2},\ldots,N_{1,n})^{\sf{T}}\in\mathds{C}^{n}
and
{\boldsymbol{N}}_{2}=(N_{2,1},N_{2,2}
,
\ldots

\in

are independent and identically distributed. More precisely, for all
(i,j)\in\{1,2\}\times\{1,2,\ldots,n\}

is a complex circularly symmetric Gaussian random variable whose real and imaginary parts have zero means and variances

.
That is, for all

, for all
\boldsymbol{z}=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})^{\sf{T}}\in\mathds{C}^{n}

\displaystyle f_{\boldsymbol{Y}|\boldsymbol{X}}(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{n}f_{Y|X}(y_{t}|x_{t})\mbox{ and }

(2)

\displaystyle f_{\boldsymbol{Z}|\boldsymbol{X}}(\boldsymbol{z}|\boldsymbol{x})

\displaystyle f_{Z|X}(z_{t}|x_{t})=\frac{1}{\pi\sigma^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left|z_{t}-x_{t}\right|^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right).

(4b)

Within this framework, the two tasks of information and energy transmission must be accomplished.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{i}\cap\mathcal{D}_{j}=\phi,

\displaystyle|u_{t}(i)|\leqslant P,

Assume that the transmitter uses an
(n,M)
-code

\mathscr{C}\triangleq\{({\boldsymbol{u}}(1),\mathcal{D}_{1}),({\boldsymbol{u}}(2),\mathcal{D}_{2}),\ldots,({\boldsymbol{u}}(M),\mathcal{D}_{M})\},

(10)

\mathcal{D}_{i,t}

\mathds{C}
.
The information rate of any

To transmit message index
i

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}(\mathscr{C})

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle 1-\int_{\mathcal{D}_{i}}f_{\boldsymbol{Y}|\boldsymbol{X}}(\boldsymbol{y}|\boldsymbol{u}(i))\mathrm{d}\boldsymbol{y},

and the average DEP is given by

\displaystyle\gamma(\mathscr{C})

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\gamma_{i}(\mathscr{C}).

II-B

Energy Transmission

Given a channel output
z\in\mathds{C}
, the energy harvested from such channel output is given by a positive monotone increasing circularly symmetric function
g

g:\mathds{C}\rightarrow[0,+\infty),

(17)

with

The energy transmission task must ensure that a minimum average energy

Let

\displaystyle\theta_{i}(\mathscr{C},B)

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}[\bar{g}({\boldsymbol{Z}})<B|{\boldsymbol{X}}={\boldsymbol{u}}(i)],

where the probability is with respect to the probability density function

in (
3
); and the average EOP is given by

\displaystyle\theta(\mathscr{C},B)

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\theta_{i}(\mathscr{C},B).

(20)

This leads to the following refinement of Definition

.

Definition 3

(
(n,M,\epsilon,B,\delta)
-code)
.

An
(n,M,\epsilon)
-code

for the random transformation in (

-code

of the form in (

in (

). Such pmf is denoted by

where

in (

Definition 4

An

-code

III
Code Construction

The information-energy capacity region of SIET systems with finite constellations is defined as follows.

Definition 5
.

The information-energy capacity region
\mathcal{C}(n,\epsilon,\delta)
for the random transformation in (
1
) is the set of all information and energy transmission rate pairs
(R,B)\in\mathds{R}^{2}
for which there exists an
(n,M,\epsilon,B,\delta)
-code
\mathscr{C}
such that
\frac{\log M}{n}=R
, the average DEP
\lambda(\mathscr{C})\leq\epsilon
, and, the average EOP
\theta(\mathscr{C})\leq\delta

The process of characterizing an achievable information-energy region for SIET with finite constellations begins with the construction of an
\left(n,M\right)
-code.
Let the
\left(n,M\right)
-code
\mathscr{C}

\mathscr{C}\triangleq\left\{({\boldsymbol{u}}(1),\mathcal{D}_{1}),({\boldsymbol{u}}(2),\mathcal{D}_{2}),\ldots,({\boldsymbol{u}}(M),\mathcal{D}_{M})\right\}.

(25a)

The construction of the code begins with the construction of the channel input symbols. The set of channel input symbols is a modulation constellation represented by a finite subset of
\mathds{C}
.
Consider a constellation formed by
C

layers
, with
C\in\mathds{N}
. A layer is a subset of symbols in
\mathds{C}
that have the same magnitude. For all
c\in\{1,2,\ldots,C\}
, denote by
L_{c}\in\mathds{N}
the number of symbols in the
c
th
layer and let
A_{c}\in\mathds{R}^{+}
be the amplitude of the symbols in layer
c
. Denote such a layer by
\mathcal{U}(A_{c},L_{c})
. That is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(A_{c},L_{c})\triangleq\Big\{x_{c}^{\left(\ell\right)}=

(25b)

\displaystyle A_{c}\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\frac{2\pi}{L_{c}}\ell\right)\subseteq\mathds{C}:\ell\in\left\{0,1,2,\ldots,(L_{c}-1)\right\}\Big\},

where
\mathrm{i}
is the complex unit.
Using this notation, the constellation can be described by the following set:

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{c=1}^{C}\mathcal{U}(A_{c},L_{c}).

(25c)

Without any loss of generality, assume that

A_{1}>A_{2}>\ldots>A_{C}.

(25d)

The symbols in layer
c
of the form in (
25b
), are equally spaced along a circle of radius
A_{c}
. The constellation induced by the set
\mathcal{X}
is thus made up of points uniformly distributed along

The total number of symbols

in (

) for
\mathcal{X}
in (
25c
) is

L=\sum_{c=1}^{C}L_{c}.

(25e)

The construction of the
(n,M)
-code
\mathscr{C}
in (
25a
) is as follows.
For all
c\in\{1,2,\ldots,C\}
, let

be the frequency with which symbols of the
c
th
layer appear in the code. The resulting probability vector is denoted by

\boldsymbol{p}=\left(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{C}\right)^{\sf{T}},

(25f)

where, for all
c\in\{1,2,\ldots,C\}
,

p_{c}=\frac{1}{Mn}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_{c}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\sum_{t=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{\{u_{t}(i)=x_{c}^{(\ell)}\}}.

(25g)

The decoding set
\mathcal{G}_{c}^{(\ell)}
associated with symbol

is a circle of radius

centered at

. That is,

(25h)

The radii

,

are chosen such that the decoding regions are mutually disjoint. To ensure this, for all

the amplitudes

) satisfy the following

\displaystyle A_{c}-A_{c-1}\geq r_{c}+r_{c-1}.

(25i)

The vector formed by these radii is denoted by

\boldsymbol{r}=\left(r_{1},r_{2},\ldots,r_{C}\right)^{\sf{T}}.

(25j)

For all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,M\}
, the decoding region for codeword
\boldsymbol{u}(i)
is

\mathcal{D}_{i}=\mathcal{D}_{i,1}\times\mathcal{D}_{i,2}\times\ldots\times\mathcal{D}_{i,n},

(25k)

where
c\in\{1,2,\ldots,C\}
,

, and

are such that if,

This defines a family of
(n,M)
-codes denoted by

\displaystyle\sf{C\left(\mathcal{X},\boldsymbol{p},\boldsymbol{r}\right)},

with constellation
\mathcal{X}
in (

Comments on Optimality

The codes constructed in this work are optimal in the sense of the converse results of Theorem
1
except for the DEP
\epsilon
. Fig.
1
shows that the code
\mathscr{C}
achieves the optimal energy rate
B
and information rate

as given by the converse results albeit at a higher DEP
\epsilon
. Fig.
2
shows that the converse and achievable information-energy rate curves for

overlap. However, for the same information and energy rate pair, the DEP for the achievable curves is higher than that of the converse curves. The sub-optimality in DEP arises due to the sub-optimal choice of circular decoding regions in (

The proposed construction provides a method of building codes that meet the given energy and information rate, EOP, and DEP requirements. Building codes that achieve the optimal energy and information rate, EOP, and DEP requires optimizing set of channel input symbols
\mathcal{X}
. However, the problem of optimal input design even for the most well behaved channels
[

,
36
,

]
remains an open problem.

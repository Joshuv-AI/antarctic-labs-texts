Kinetic Derivation of the Hessian Geometric Structure in Chemical Reaction Systems Preprint: APS/123-QED
Tetsuya J. Kobayashi, Dimitri Loutchko, Atsushi Kamimura, and Yuki Sughiyama

Abstract
The theory of chemical kinetics form the basis to describe the dynamics of chemical systems. Owing to physical and thermodynamic constraints, chemical reaction systems possess various structures, which can be utilized to characterize important physical properties of the systems. In this work, we reveal the Hessian geometry which underlies chemical reaction systems and demonstrate how it originates from the interplay of stoichiometric and thermodynamic constraints. Our derivation is based on kinetics, we assume the law of mass action and characterize the equilibrium states by the detailed balance condition. The obtained geometric structure is then related to thermodynamics via the Hessian geometry appearing in a pure thermodynamic derivation. We demonstrate, based on the fact that both equilibrium and complex balanced states form toric varieties, how the Hessian geometric framework can be extended to nonequilibrium complex balanced steady states. We conclude that Hessian geometry provides a natural framework to capture the thermodynamic aspects of chemical reaction kinetics.



I
Introduction

Chemical kinetics constitutes the basis to describe various and complex behaviors of chemically implemented systems such as metabolic networks and intracellular signaling systems
Alon 2019
;
Mikhailov and Ertl 2017
.
Since the formulation and establishment of the law of mass action by C.M. Guldberg and P. Waage, the theories of chemical kinetics and chemical dynamics have been studied and developed using techniques from various different disciplines
Feinberg 2019
;
Alon 2019
;
Murray 2011
;
Epstein and Pojman 1998
;
Beard and Qian 2008
.

However, the theories of chemical dynamics are not always consistent with thermodynamics.
Yet, the consideration of thermodynamics is essential for constructing physically and thermodynamically sound kinetic theories.
For example, the combination of the law of mass action and the detailed balance condition leads to a kinetic characterization of the equilibrium state in a way that is consistent with chemical thermodynamics, as shown already in 1901 by Wegscheider
Wegscheider 1902
.
Since the framework of chemical kinetics does not necessarily obey the detailed balance condition, it enables the investigation not only of equilibrium systems but also of a wide range of nonequilibrium reaction systems.
Sparked by the pioneering work of Hill and Schnakenberg
Hill 2005
;
Hill 1966
;
Schnakenberg 1976
, a thermodynamic foundation has been in development for mass action systems out of equilibrium within the last decades
Beard and Qian 2008
;
Polettini and Esposito 2014
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
;
Rao and Esposito 2018a
;
Rao and Esposito 2018b
;
Avanzini et al. 2021
;
Ge and Qian 2016
, by employing the knowledge from stochastic thermodynamics
Schmiedl and Seifert 2007
.

In addition, the consideration of constraints of physical or thermodynamic origin also introduces additional intriguing structures into chemical kinetics.
Motivated by the work of Horn and Jackson
Horn and Jackson 1972
, who extended equilibrium states to complex balanced states, an algebro-geometric structure of chemical reaction systems was discovered and employed to study mass action systems in applied mathematics
Craciun et al. 2009
;
Craciun et al. 2019
;
Craciun and Sorea 2020
.
Other structures in chemical kinetics are also unveiled by using tools from graph theory, homological algebra, and others
Feinberg 2019
;
van der Schaft et al. 2013
;
Okada and Mochizuki 2016
;
Mochizuki and Fiedler 2015
;
Fiedler and Mochizuki 2015
;
Shinar and Feinberg 2010
;
Araujo and Liotta 2018
;
Hirono et al. 2021
.

Thus, clarification of the interrelation of chemical kinetics and thermodynamics can be a fruitful source of new physics and mathematics of chemical reaction systems
Polettini and Esposito 2014
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
;
Rao and Esposito 2018b
;
Gopalkrishnan et al. 2014
;
Craciun 2016
;
Dickenstein et al. 2019
;
Joshi and Shiu 2017
;
Otero-Muras et al. 2017
.

Recently, we found that Hessian geometry provides a natural framework for thermodynamics of chemical reaction systems and used the geometric structure to show that several important results, which were thus far derived only from kinetics, are of pure thermodynamic origin
Sughiyama et al. 2021
.
Nonetheless, it is important to clarify how the Hessian geometric structure is linked to chemical kinetics because chemical kinetics and thermodynamics were established historically in an interrelated manner and also because the majority of results for chemical reaction systems are based on mass action kinetics rather than thermodynamics.
This is achieved in this paper.

To this end, we find a close connection between the results of equilibrium systems obtained in algebraic geometry
Craciun et al. 2009
and the thermodynamics which is encoded in the Hessian structure
Sughiyama et al. 2021
.
This combination enables us to grasp the geometric structure of the whole state space, which clarifies the dualistic relation between stoichiometric constraints and thermodynamic constrains manifested as the flatness of the respective dual spaces.
Thereby, we generalize and extend the information geometric framework for chemical reaction networks limited to a single stoichiometric compatibility class
Yoshimura and Ito 2021
.

We derive the Hessian geometric structure
Shima 2007
, which purifies and generalizes some aspects of the information geometric one
Amari 2016
, by starting from the kinetic characterization of the chemical equilibrium state via the law of mass action and the detailed balance condition.
In the derivation, we show that the equilibrium states of a chemical reaction network are described by a toric variety
Craciun et al. 2009
.
The analytification of the toric variety plays a fundamental role throughout the paper as it constitutes a generalization of the exponential family well-known in statistics
Amari 1982
;
Amari 1985
;
Barndorff-Nielsen et al. 1986
;
Amari and Nagaoka 2000
;
Amari 2016
.
Building on the theory of exponential families, we extend the dually flat structure from information geometry
Amari and Han 1989
;
Okamoto et al. 1991
;
Amari and Nagaoka 2000
to chemical reaction networks.

The toric parameter representation of the equilibrium variety naturally leads to a dual space
\mathcal{Y}
, which is conjugate to the state space
\mathcal{X}
of molecular concentrations.
The Hessian geometric structure and associated convex potential functions
\varphi(\bm{x})
and
\varphi^{*}(\bm{y})
appear on the state space
\mathcal{X}
and its dual
\mathcal{Y}
: in the former, stoichiometric constraints form a linear coordinate system, whereas, in the latter, the equilibrium variety leads to the definition of a dual linear coordinate system.
Since the equilibrium variety is defined by the parameters of the reaction system, which are specified thermodynamically by the environmental variables, the dual space mathematically captures the role of thermal reservoirs attached to the system.
By comparison with the purely thermodynamic derivation in
Sughiyama et al. 2021
, the spaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are thermodynamically related to chemical density and chemical potentials.
The convex functions and associated Bregman divergences are mapped to the thermodynamic free energy of the system and the difference of total entropy.
We also show that the Hessian geometric framework can be naturally extended to the nonequilibrium complex balanced states because the both of equilibrium states and the complex balanced states are described by the same toric variety
Craciun et al. 2009
.

In the class of equilibrium systems, our thermodynamical results are special instances of the general theory derived from a purely thermodynamic argument.
In this aspect, this paper is supplementary to our accompanying paper
Sughiyama et al. 2021
.
However, owing to the mass action assumption, we find that the equilibrium manifold has the structure of an algebraic variety, which is not true in the general case.
Thereby, we establish a link to the extensive work carried out in real algebraic geometry
Craciun et al. 2009
.
Owing to this, we show how our results extend to nonequilibrium complex balanced steady-states, because they share the algebraic structure with the equilibrium states.
We expect this link between thermodynamics via Hessian geometry and algebraic geometry to be even more fruitful in the future.

To make the theory and results more accessible to researchers in chemical reaction network theory who are not necessarily familiar with information or Hessian geometry, we clarify several implicit and confusing identifications of different objects in conventional textbooks of information geometry
Amari 2016
.

This paper is organized as follows:
In Sec.

based on the stoichiometric constraints.
In Sec.

In Sec.

In Sec.

In Sec.

In the supplementary Sec.

Let

The vector

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\bm{x}}{\mathrm{d}t}=S\bm{j}(\bm{x};\bm{\theta}),

(1)

where

by eliminating the

III
Equilibrium variety and dual coordinate

The conserved quantities
\bm{\eta}
and the extent of chemical reaction
\bm{J}

\bm{\xi}

As verified in Eq.

Next, we show how dual coordinates

We point out that, to obtain the result, we will use the detailed balance condition together with the kinetic law of mass action to characterize the equilibrium state and the system.
In our accompanying paper, by contrast, we derive the same result only from a thermodynamic argument without these kinetic assumptions

We use the kinetic assumptions here because these assumptions prevail in chemical reaction network theory and stochastic thermodynamics

III.1
Equilibrium variety

The positive equilibrium states of the system (Eq.
1
) are the states that satisfy the detailed balance condition:

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta}):=\{\bm{x}>0|\bm{j}(\bm{x};\bm{\theta})=\bm{0}\}.

(8)

Note that
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})
can be empty if a specified
\theta
admits no equilibrium state.
We define the set of parameters
\Theta_{eq}
such that

\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})
is not empty if
\bm{\theta}\in\Theta_{eq}
.

To this end, we additionally assume that
\bm{j}^{\pm}(\bm{x};\bm{\theta})
satisfy the law of mass action:

\displaystyle j^{+}_{m}(\bm{x};\bm{\theta})=k^{+}_{m}\bm{x}^{\bm{\gamma}_{m}^{+}},\qquad j^{-}_{m}(\bm{x};\bm{\theta})=k^{-}_{m}\bm{x}^{\bm{\gamma}_{m}^{-}},

(9)

where
k^{\pm}_{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
are the rate constants of the
m
th forward and reverse reactions, respectively.
The integer vectors
\bm{\gamma}_{m}^{+},\bm{\gamma}_{m}^{-}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
specify reactants and products of the
m
th forward reaction.
Thus,
\bm{\gamma}_{m}^{-}-\bm{\gamma}_{m}^{+}=\bm{s}_{m}
where
\bm{s}_{m}
is the
m
th column vector of the stoichiometric matrix
S
.
For a pair of vectors
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\bm{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
, the exponential
\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}
represents the monomial

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{\bm{\alpha}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{N}x_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}.

(10)

We write Eq.
9
in a vector form as

\displaystyle\bm{j}^{+}(\bm{x};\bm{\theta})=\bm{k}^{+}\circ\bm{x}^{\Gamma^{+}},\qquad\bm{j}^{-}(\bm{x};\bm{\theta})=\bm{k}^{-}\circ\bm{x}^{\Gamma^{-}},

(11)

where
\bm{x}^{\Gamma}:=(\bm{x}^{\bm{\gamma}_{1}},\cdots,\bm{x}^{\bm{\gamma}_{M}})^{T}
and
\circ
is the component-wise product of vectors
4
4

4

If necessary, one may adopt the more general version
\bm{j}^{\pm}(\bm{x};\bm{\theta})=\bm{k}^{\pm}\circ\bm{h}(\bm{x})\circ\bm{x}^{\mp S^{T}}

\bm{h}(\bm{x})>0
.
.
Then
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta}):=\left\{\bm{x}>0|\bm{k}^{+}\circ\bm{x}^{\Gamma^{+}}=\bm{k}^{-}\circ\bm{x}^{\Gamma^{-}}\right\}.

(12)

Now,
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})
is an algebraic variety, i.e., the manifold defined as the zeros of algebraic equations
5
5

5

It should be noted that, hereafter, we use the analytifaction of the variety to work on differential geometric aspects of
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}

\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}
is given by algebraic equations (which are derived from the detailed balance condition).

Thus,
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})

In addition, we note that

III.2
Parameter conditions for equilibrium

Next, we derive the necessary and sufficient condition that the set of parameters
\Theta_{eq}
must satisfy to have non-empty
\mathcal{V}_{eq}(\bm{\theta})
for
\theta\in\Theta_{eq}
.
In other words, we characterize the parameter set that admits equilibrium states.
By rearranging
\bm{k}^{+}\circ\bm{x}^{\Gamma^{+}}=\bm{k}^{-}\circ\bm{x}^{\Gamma^{-}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ln\bm{K}:=\ln\frac{\bm{k}^{+}}{\bm{k}^{-}}=S^{T}\ln\bm{x},

(13)

where
\Gamma^{-}-\Gamma^{+}=S
was used.
From the Fredholm alternative, Eq.
13
has a solution if and only if
\ln\bm{K}\in\mathrm{Im}[S^{T}]
.
The condition
\ln\bm{K}\in\mathrm{Im}[S^{T}]
is an abstract representation of the Wegscheider condition
Wegscheider 1902
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
.
Thus, we can represent
\Theta_{eq}
as

\displaystyle\Theta_{eq}=\left\{\bm{\theta}=(\bm{k}^{\pm})|\bm{K}=\frac{\bm{k}^{+}}{\bm{k}^{-}},\,\ln\bm{K}\in\mathrm{Im}[S^{T}]\right\}.

(14)

Hereby,
\bm{K}
is the vector of equilibrium constants.
Thus, this representation means that, among all parameters in
\Theta_{eq}
, only the equilibrium constants
\bm{K}
are relevant for the existence of equilibrium states.
This is natural because the equilibrium state of a system should be characterized statically without specifying any kinetic information of the system.

III.3
Toric parameterization of the equilibrium variety

For a given
\bm{K}\in\Theta_{eq}
, there exists a particular solution
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}
of Eq.
13
.
Then the equilibrium variety
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{K})
, i.e. the set of
\bm{x}
that satisfies Eq.
13
, can be represented as

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{K})

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{x}|\ln\bm{x}=\ln\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}+U^{T}\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\eta}^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{\ell}\right\},

(15)

because
U
is a basis of
\mathrm{Ker}[S^{T}]
.
This representation is known as the affine toric parametrization of the variety
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{K})
.
In algebraic statistics,
U^{T}
is also called the design matrix of the toric variety.
A toric variety is characterized as being generated by a toric ideal, i.e., a prime binomial ideal in the coordinate ring of the ambient space
\mathbb{R}^{N}\supset\mathcal{X}

Sottile 2008
;
Eisenbud and Sturmfels 1996
;
Cox et al. 2011
.
Because the detailed balance condition is nothing but a set of binomial equations, a toric variety is a natural representation of the equilibrium states.

From Eq.
15
, we see that
\bm{\eta}^{*}
works as a coordinate of the variety
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{K})
.
However, similarly to the case of
\bm{x}_{0}
, the actual value of
\bm{\eta}^{*}
depends on the choice of
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}
, which is not uniquely specified because it is just a particular solution.
Among all
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}

\ln\bm{K}=S^{T}\ln\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}
, we choose one such that
(\ln\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}+\hat{\bm{y}})\in\mathrm{Ker}[S^{T}]^{\perp}
where
\hat{\bm{y}}
determines a reference point and can be associated with the standard chemical potential, as we will see later.
Because
\left<C^{*}\right>=\mathrm{Ker}[S^{T}]^{\perp}
, we can
write
\ln\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}=C^{*}\bm{\xi}^{*}-\hat{\bm{y}}
.
Since the equation

\displaystyle\ln\bm{K}=S^{T}\left(C^{*}\bm{\xi}^{*}-\hat{\bm{y}}\right)

(16)

uniquely determines
\bm{\xi}^{*}
if
\hat{\bm{y}}
is fixed and
\ln\bm{K}\in\mathrm{Im}S^{T}

\bm{\xi}^{*}
instead of

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\xi}^{*})=\left\{\bm{x}|\ln\bm{x}=C^{*}\bm{\xi}^{*}+U^{T}\bm{\eta}^{*}-\hat{\bm{y}}\right\}.

(17)

Now we introduce the space
\mathcal{Y}
obtained by the nonlinear transformation

\displaystyle\bm{y}=\ln\bm{x}+\hat{\bm{y}},

(18)

i.e.,

\mathcal{Y}:=\hat{\bm{y}}+\ln\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, any point on

can be linearly parametrized as

\displaystyle\bm{y}(\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*})=U^{T}\bm{\eta}^{*}+C^{*}\bm{\xi}^{*}.

(19)

Thus,

is a linear coordinate system of

By the reverse transformation of Eq.

can also be parametrized nonlinearly as

\displaystyle\bm{x}(\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*})=\exp\left[U^{T}\bm{\eta}^{*}+C^{*}\bm{\xi}^{*}-\hat{\bm{y}}\right].

(20)

As the form of the transformation in Eq.

is the space of chemical potentials, which is thermodynamically conjugate to the concentration space

Moreover,
\bm{x}(\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*})

Dual flat state space

We regard the state space
\mathcal{X}

Let
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{V}^{*}
be the dual vector space of
\mathbb{V}
and
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
be the bilinear form defined on
\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}^{*}
(Fig.

.
On
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}

4
:

\displaystyle\varphi(\bm{x})

\displaystyle:=\left[\ln\bm{x}-\hat{\bm{y}}-\bm{1}\right]^{T}\bm{x},

(23)

\displaystyle\varphi^{*}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle:=\bm{1}^{T}e^{\bm{y}+\hat{\bm{y}}}.

(24)

This yields the one-to-one Legendre duality between
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
and

\displaystyle=\partial\varphi(\bm{x}):=\left\{\frac{\partial\varphi(\bm{x})}{\partial\bm{x}}\right\}=\ln\bm{x}-\hat{\bm{y}},

\displaystyle\bm{x}

\displaystyle=\partial\varphi^{*}(\bm{y}):=\left\{\frac{\partial\varphi^{*}(\bm{y})}{\partial\bm{y}}\right\}=e^{\bm{y}+\hat{\bm{y}}},

such that

and

\displaystyle\varphi(\bm{x})+\varphi^{*}(\bm{y})-\left<\bm{x},\bm{y}\right>=0.

(27)

Thus, the pair

.

We use the following notation: a pair
(\bm{x},\bm{y})
is always treated as two Legendre dual coordinates (Fig.

).
Another pair with the same decoration, e.g.,
(\bm{x}^{\prime},\bm{y}^{\prime})
, will be treated as an another, generally distinct, Legendre dual pair.

and
\bm{\xi}^{*}
and
\bm{\eta}
and

\displaystyle=U^{*}\bm{y}=\bm{\eta}^{*},

(30)

\displaystyle\partial_{\bm{\xi}}\varphi(\bm{x}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi}))

\displaystyle=C^{T}\bm{y}=\bm{\xi}^{*},

(31)

IV.3
Bregman divergence

Using the Legendre dual potential functions, the Bregman divergence between two points
\bm{x}
and
\bm{x}^{\prime}
is defined as
Bregman 1967
;
Amari 2016
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle:=\varphi(\bm{x})-\varphi({\bm{x}^{\prime}})-\left<\partial\varphi(\bm{x}^{\prime}),\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}\right>.

(35)

Due to the convexity of
\varphi(\bm{x})
, the function
\mathcal{D}[\bm{x}^{\prime}\|\bm{x}]
is nonnegative and measures the extent of convexity as the deviation of
\varphi(\bm{x})
from its linear extrapolation
\varphi({\bm{x}^{\prime}})+\left<\partial\varphi(\bm{x}^{\prime}),\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}\right>
, evaluated at
\bm{x}^{\prime}
.
The minimum of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]
is
0
, which is achieved if and only if
\bm{x}=\bm{x}^{\prime}
.
A direct computation, using Eq.
23
and Eq.
24
, gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=\left(\ln\frac{\bm{x}}{\bm{x}^{\prime}}\right)^{T}\bm{x}-\bm{1}^{T}(\bm{x}-\bm{x}^{\prime}).

(36)

This indicates that
\mathcal{D}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]
is reduced to the generalized Kullback-Leibler divergence for positive measures on a discrete space for the specific functional form of
\varphi(\bm{x})
that originates from the law of mass action and the detailed balance condition.

Similarly, for
\bm{y}
and
\bm{y}^{\prime}
, the dual Bregman divergence is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{y}\|\bm{y}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle:=\varphi^{*}(\bm{y})-\varphi^{*}({\bm{y}^{\prime}})-\left<\partial\varphi^{*}(\bm{y}^{\prime}),\bm{y}-\bm{y}^{\prime}\right>.

(37)

If
\bm{y}
and
\bm{y}^{\prime}
are Legendre dual to
\bm{x}
and
\bm{x}^{\prime}
, respectively, the Bregman divergences satisfy the symmetry property

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]=\varphi(\bm{x})+\varphi^{*}(\bm{y}^{\prime})-\left<\bm{x},\bm{y}^{\prime}\right>=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{y}^{\prime}\|\bm{y}],

(38)

where
\varphi(\bm{x}^{\prime})+\left<\partial\varphi(\bm{x}^{\prime}),\bm{x}^{\prime}\right>=-\varphi^{*}(\bm{y}^{\prime})
was used.
Thus we are led to define
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}^{\prime}]
as (Fig.
4
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}^{\prime}]:=\varphi(\bm{x})+\varphi^{*}(\bm{y}^{\prime})-\left<\bm{x},\bm{y}^{\prime}\right>.

(39)

Note that
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{y}^{\prime}\|\bm{y}]
, and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}^{\prime}]
are just different representations of the same geometric quantity because
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
as well as
\bm{x}^{\prime}
and
\bm{y}^{\prime}
are in one-to-one correspondence by the Legendre transformation.
In the following, we abbreviate
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]
,
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{y}^{\prime}\|\bm{y}]
, and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}^{\prime}]
as
\mathcal{D}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]
,
\mathcal{D}[\bm{y}^{\prime}\|\bm{y}]
, and
\mathcal{D}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}^{\prime}]
, and switch between the three equivalent notations depending on the purpose.
We emphasize that

\displaystyle\bm{x}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]=\arg\min_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}^{\prime}].

(40)

As we will show later, the relation between the potential function
\varphi
and the Bregman divergence is the mathematical reason why the Kullback-Leibler divergence appears as the difference of the total entropy in equilibrium systems.

IV.4
Dual orthogonality

We demonstrate that the Bregman divergence and the mixed coordinate representation play a central role when determining the equilibrium state of the system.

Consider a the chemical reaction system (Eq.

\bm{K}\in\Theta_{eq}
and initial state
\bm{x}_{0}
.
Let
\bm{x}_{0}
be in stoichiometric polytope

Any point on

Similarly, any point on

can be written as

Then, from the definition of

and
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\xi}^{*})

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{p}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi}_{p})-\bm{x}_{eq}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi})

(41)

\displaystyle=U^{T}(\bm{\eta}_{q}^{*}-\bm{\eta}^{*}),

(42)

hold.
Here,
\bm{y}_{q}
and
\bm{y}_{eq}
are the Legendre duals of
\bm{x}_{q}
and
\bm{x}_{eq}
.
This yields the orthogonality

\displaystyle\left<\bm{x}_{p}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi}_{p})-\bm{x}_{eq}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi}),\bm{y}_{q}(\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*})-\bm{y}_{eq}(\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*})\right>=0,

following from

V
Differential Geometric Structure

In the previous section, we have obtained the geometric structure by exploiting only the information of the first derivative of
\varphi(\bm{x})
.
In this section, we explore the information contained in the second derivative of
\varphi(\bm{x})
, i.e., its Hessian.

V.1
Hessian and Fisher information

Because
\varphi(\bm{x})
is strongly convex, its Hessian (second derivative matrix)

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x}):=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi(\bm{x})}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}}\right)_{i,j}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\frac{1}{\bm{x}}\right),

(55)

is positive definite, which endows the tangent space of
\mathcal{X}
with a natural inner product structure:

\displaystyle\left<\Delta\bm{x},\Delta\bm{x}^{\prime}\right>_{\bm{x}}:=\left<G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})\Delta\bm{x},\Delta\bm{x}^{\prime}\right>,

(56)

where
\Delta\bm{x}
and
\Delta\bm{x}^{\prime}
are elements of the tangent space
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{X}
at
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}
.
Moreover,
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
is the bilinear form defined on
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{T}_{\bm{x}}^{*}\mathcal{X}
, where
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{X}
is the corresponding cotangent space.
The induced norm is
\left|\Delta\bm{x}\right|_{\bm{x}}:=\sqrt{\left<\Delta\bm{x},\Delta\bm{x}\right>_{\bm{x}}}
.
Also,
G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})
induces a mapping from the tangent space
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{X}
to the cotangent space
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{X}
via
\Delta\bm{y}=G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})\Delta\bm{x}\in\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{X}
.

The Hessian of
\varphi^{*}(\bm{y})
is computed as

\displaystyle G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y}):=\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi^{*}(\bm{y})}{\partial y_{i}\partial y_{j}}\right)_{i,j}=\mathrm{diag}\,e^{\bm{y}+\hat{\bm{y}}}

(57)

and satisfies
G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})=I
because
\bm{x}=e^{\bm{y}+\hat{\bm{y}}}
.
Thus,
G_{\mathcal{X}}^{-1}(\bm{x})=G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})
and
G_{\mathcal{Y}}^{-1}(\bm{y})=G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})
.
The inner product induced by
G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})
on the dual tangent space
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{y}}\mathcal{Y}
is

\displaystyle\left<\Delta\bm{y},\Delta\bm{y}^{\prime}\right>_{\bm{y}}:=\left<G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})\Delta\bm{y},\Delta\bm{y}^{\prime}\right>.

(58)

Because of the linear duality between
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, we have relations:
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{x}}\cong\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\bm{y}}
and
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\bm{x}}\cong\mathcal{T}_{\bm{y}}
.

In information geometry,
G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})
is known as Fisher information.
It is related to the infinitesimal change of the Kullback-Leibler divergence as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}+\Delta\bm{x}]

\displaystyle=\left<\Delta\bm{x},G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})\Delta\bm{x}\right>

(59)

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{y}+\Delta\bm{y}\|\bm{y}]

\displaystyle=\left<\Delta\bm{y},G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})\Delta\bm{y}\right>.

(60)

V.2
Fisher information for the dual and mixed coordinates

By inserting
\Delta\bm{x}=(U^{*})^{T}\Delta\bm{\eta}+C\Delta\bm{\xi}
or
\Delta\bm{y}=U^{T}\Delta\bm{\eta}^{*}+C^{*}\Delta\bm{\xi}^{*}
,
we obtain Fisher information matrices for
(\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi})
or
(\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*})
:

\displaystyle G_{\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}U^{*}G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})(U^{*})^{T}&U^{*}G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})C\\
C^{T}\,G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})(U^{*})^{T}&C^{T}G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})C\end{pmatrix}

(61)

\displaystyle G_{\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*}}(\bm{y})

\displaystyle:=\begin{pmatrix}UG_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})U^{T}&UG_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})C^{*}\\
(C^{*})^{T}\,G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})U^{T}&(C^{*})^{T}G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})C^{*}\end{pmatrix}

(62)

We can verify that
G_{\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*}}(\bm{y})
is the inverse matrix of
G_{\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})
by directly computing
G_{\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*}}(\bm{y})G_{\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})=I
where we use the fact that

\displaystyle P_{U}:=U^{T}U^{*},\qquad P_{C}:=C^{*}C^{T}

(63)

are orthogonal projection matrices and satisfy
P_{U}+P_{C}^{T}=I
.
The effective metric matrix can be further simplified by using mixed coordinates.
In this case
\Delta\bm{x}
is represented by

\displaystyle\Delta\bm{x}

\displaystyle=(U^{*})^{T}\Delta\bm{\eta}+G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})C^{*}\Delta\bm{\xi}^{*}

(64)

\displaystyle=G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{x})U^{T}\Delta\bm{\eta}^{*}+C\Delta\bm{\xi}.

(65)

The cross terms in
\mathrm{d}s^{2}
disappear due to the dual orthogolality:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}\sim

\displaystyle\left<\Delta\bm{\eta},G_{\bm{\eta}}(\bm{x})\Delta\bm{\eta}\right>+\left<\Delta\bm{\xi}^{*},G_{\bm{\xi}^{*}}(\bm{y})\Delta\bm{\xi}^{*}\right>,

(66)

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\left<\Delta\bm{\eta}^{*},G_{\bm{\eta}^{*}}(\bm{y})\Delta\bm{\eta}^{*}\right>+\left<\Delta\bm{\xi},G_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})\Delta\bm{\xi}\right>,

(67)

where
G_{\bm{\eta}}(\bm{x}):=U^{*}G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})(U^{*})^{T}
and
G_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x}):=C^{T}G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})C
are the diagonal blocks of
G_{\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})
(Eq.
61
), whereas
G_{\bm{\eta}^{*}}(\bm{y}):=UG_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})U^{T}
and
G_{\bm{\xi}^{*}}(\bm{y}):=(C^{*})^{T}G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})C^{*}
are those of
G_{\bm{\eta}^{*},\bm{\xi}^{*}}(\bm{y})
(Eq.
62
).
This is a tangent and cotangent space version of the generalized Pythagorean relation and the orthogolonality between the stoichiometric polytopes and equilibrium varieties.

9

9

Note that neither
G_{\bm{\eta}}(\bm{x})G_{\bm{\eta}^{*}}(\bm{y})=I
nor
G_{\bm{\xi}}(\bm{x})G_{\bm{\xi}^{*}}(\bm{y})=I
holds.

VI
Link to chemical thermodynamics

In this section, we clarify how the Hessian geometric structure and its constituents can be related to equilibrium chemical thermodynamics.
To this end, because the results were derived kinetically from the law of mass action and detailed balancing, we have to rederive the same results from thermodynamics, if at all possible, without assuming any kinetics.
This is achieved in our accompanying paper.
Here, we make the correspondence precise.
Refer to
Sughiyama et al. 2021
for the more general results derived from thermodynamics.

VI.1
Chemical potential and Free energy

The chemical potential of a dilute solution (or equivalently of an ideal gas) is given by

\displaystyle\bm{\mu}(\bm{x})=\hat{\bm{\nu}}^{o}(T)+RT\ln\bm{x},

(68)

where
T
is the temperature of the system,
R
is the gas constant, and
\hat{\bm{\nu}}^{o}(T)
is the standard chemical potential of
\bm{x}
10
10

10

We used
\hat{\bm{\nu}}^{o}(T)
for the standard chemical potential rather than more conventional
\hat{\bm{\mu}}^{o}(T)
to make the notation consistent with those in
Sughiyama et al. 2021
.
The vector
\bm{y}=\ln\bm{x}-\hat{\bm{y}}
is related to the chemical potential via

\displaystyle\bm{y}=\bm{\mu}/(RT),\qquad\hat{\bm{y}}=-\hat{\bm{\nu}}^{o}/(RT),

(69)

which corresponds to equation (63) in
Sughiyama et al. 2021
.
Thus,
\mathcal{Y}
is the space of chemical potentials, which is thermodynamically conjugate to the concentration space of molecular species
\mathcal{X}
.
The potential function
\varphi(\bm{x})
is associated with the Gibbs free energy of the system (without reservoir):

\displaystyle RT\varphi(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=RT\left[\ln\bm{x}-\hat{\bm{y}}-\bm{1}\right]^{T}\bm{x}

(70)

\displaystyle=\left[\bm{\mu}(\bm{x})-RT\bm{1}\right]^{T}\bm{x}=\mathcal{G}_{sys}(\bm{x})-\mathcal{G}_{0},

(71)

where
\mathcal{G}_{0}
is constant. This equation corresponds to equation (59) in
Sughiyama et al. 2021
under the additional assumption that there are no molecules exchanged with the reservoir. Thus, the system is closed.
Because
\mathcal{G}_{sys}(\bm{x})
is minimized for
\ln\hat{\bm{x}}=\hat{\bm{y}}
, which satisfies
\bm{\mu}(\hat{\bm{x}})=0
, such that
\hat{\bm{\nu}}^{o}
, or equivalently
\hat{\bm{y}}
, specifies the intrinsic equilibrium state that is attained if the system is closed and free from stoichiometric constraints.
We used the term Gibbs free energy for
\mathcal{G}_{sys}(\bm{x})
, following the convention of chemical thermodynamics
11
11

11

More precisely,
\mathcal{G}_{sys}(\bm{x})
is a Helmholtz free energy if the volume is predominantly determined by the non-reactive solvent as is assumed in the theory of chemical reaction systems (cf. Discussion in
Sughiyama et al. 2021
).
.

VI.2
Total entropy and Bregman divergence

Next, consider the case that the system is open but free from stoichiometric constraints, i.e.,
\mathrm{Ker}[S^{T}]=\{0\}
.
The total entropy of the system is related to
\varphi(\bm{x})
by

\displaystyle\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})=R\Omega\left[\left<\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res},\bm{x}\right>-\varphi(\bm{x})\right]+\mathrm{const},

(72)

where
\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}
is uniquely specified by the state of the chemical reservoir (see equation (41) in
Sughiyama et al. 2021
) through the equation

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{\mu}}^{T}O=-\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}^{T}S.

(73)

Here,
\tilde{\bm{\mu}}
is the chemical potential of molecules, which can be exchanged with reservoir and
O
is the stoichiometric matrix for these exchange reactions.
The existence of
\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}
follows from the equilibrium parameter condition (Eq.
14
) and Eq (78) of
Sughiyama et al. 2021
.
The uniqueness of
\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}
is assured by
\mathrm{Ker}[S^{T}]=\{0\}
.
If
\bm{x}=\tilde{\bm{x}}_{res}
, we have

\displaystyle\Sigma^{tot}(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{res};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})=R\Omega\varphi^{*}(\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})+\mathrm{const},

(74)

which indicates that
\varphi^{*}(\bm{y})
is the total entropy at the equilibrium point specified by the parameter
\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}
.
By using the relation between the potential
\varphi(\bm{x})
and the Bregman divergence
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}^{\prime}]
, the difference of total entropy between
\bm{x}
and
\bm{x}^{\prime}
can be associated with the difference of Bregman divergences

\displaystyle\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})

\displaystyle-\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x}^{\prime};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})

\displaystyle=-R\Omega\left[\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}]-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}^{\prime}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}]\right].

(75)

Without stoichiometric constraints,
\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})
is maximized at the corresponding equilibrium state
\bm{x}_{eq}

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}:=\arg\max_{\bm{x}}\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}).

(76)

By inserting
\bm{x}^{\prime}=\tilde{\bm{x}}_{res}
into Eq.
75
, where
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{res}
is the Legendre transform of
\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})

\displaystyle=\Sigma^{tot}(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{res};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})-R\Omega\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}].

(77)

Thus

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{eq}=\arg\min_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}]=\tilde{\bm{x}}_{res}

(78)

and the total entropy production is

\displaystyle\Delta\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x}\to\bm{x}_{eq};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})

\displaystyle:=\Sigma^{tot}(\tilde{\bm{x}}_{res};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})-\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})

\displaystyle=R\Omega\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}].

(79)

Equation (
78
) indicates that specifying a certain equilibrium parameter
\tilde{\bm{y}}\in\mathcal{Y}
by modulating the reservoir is equivalent to specifying the state
\tilde{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
that is the equilibrium state of the system under the reservoir parameter
\tilde{\bm{y}}
because of the one-to-one correspondence between
\tilde{\bm{x}}
and
\tilde{\bm{y}}
.

VI.3
Entropy production under constraints

If stoichiometric constraints exist, i.e.,
\mathrm{Ker}[S^{T}]\neq\{0\}
, then
\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res}
cannot be determined uniquely from the reservoir parameter by Eq.
73
.
Nevertheless, Eq.
72
has meaning.
Let
\tilde{\bm{y}}
be a particular solution satisfying Eq.
73
.
A system
\bm{x}(t)
with initial condition
\bm{x}_{0}
is restricted to the stoichiometric polytope
\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta}_{0})
which contains
\bm{x}_{0}
.
Then, the equilibrium state is characterized as

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{eq}=

\displaystyle\arg\max_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta}_{0})}\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{y}})

(80)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta}_{0})}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}].

(81)

Let
\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{X}}_{eq}(\tilde{\bm{\xi}}^{*})
be the equilibrium variety to which
\tilde{\bm{y}}
belongs, i.e.,
\tilde{\bm{x}}
, the Legendre transform of
\tilde{\bm{y}}
, satisfies
\tilde{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{X}}_{eq}(\tilde{\bm{\xi}}^{*})
.
Moreover, let
\bm{x}_{int}
be the intersection point of the polytope and the variety , i.e.,
\bm{x}_{int}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta}_{0})\cap\mathcal{V}^{\mathcal{X}}_{eq}(\tilde{\bm{\xi}}^{*})
.
As shown before, this intersection is unique and can be specified as
\bm{x}_{int}=\bm{x}_{int}(\bm{\eta}_{0},\tilde{\bm{\xi}}^{*})
.
For any
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta}_{0})
, the generalized Pythagorean relation holds:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}]=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}_{int}]+\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}_{int}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}].

(82)

Thus,

\displaystyle\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta}_{0})}\left[\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}_{int}]+\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}_{int}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}]\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\arg\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta}_{0})}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}_{int}]=\bm{x}_{int}.

(83)

This verifies that the equilibrium state
\bm{x}_{eq}
, which maximizes the total entropy, is characterized by the intersection point of the polytope and variety.
Also, the entropy production becomes

\displaystyle\Delta\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x}\to\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq};\tilde{\bm{y}})

\displaystyle:=\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x}_{eq};\tilde{\bm{y}})-\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x};\tilde{\bm{y}})

\displaystyle=R\Omega\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}_{eq}],

(84)

where the relevant part is independent of
\tilde{\bm{y}}
owning to the Pythagorean relation.
The convergence to
\bm{x}_{eq}
is then attributed to the second law, which was also provens kinetically for mass action system (Eq.
49
).
The independence of the choice of
\tilde{\bm{y}}
can be understood more clearly in
\mathcal{Y}
space from the dual variational equation:

\displaystyle\bm{y}_{eq}=

\displaystyle\underset{\tilde{\bm{y}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{Y}}_{eq}(\tilde{\bm{\xi}}^{*})}{\arg\min}\left[\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}_{int}]+\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}_{int}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}]\right]

\displaystyle=\underset{\tilde{\bm{y}}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{Y}}_{eq}(\tilde{\bm{\xi}}^{*})}{\arg\min}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}_{int}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}]=\bm{y}_{int},

(85)

which corresponds to Eq.
54
.
From the definition of
\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{Y}}_{eq}(\tilde{\bm{\xi}}^{*})
by Eq.
51
, any
\tilde{\bm{y}},\tilde{\bm{y}}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{Y}}_{eq}(\tilde{\bm{\xi}}^{*})
satisfies
(\tilde{\bm{y}}-\tilde{\bm{y}}^{\prime})^{T}S=0
.
Thus, the choice of a particular
\tilde{\bm{y}}
can only contribute to
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}_{int}\|\tilde{\bm{y}}]
, which is orthogonal to
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{y}_{int}]
.
In other words, if there exist stoichiometric constraints, the relevant quantity specified by the reservoir is no longer a point or value but a geometric object, i.e., the whole equilibrium variety .

VI.4
Linear response of total entropy

Finally, we investigate responses of the total entropy to infinitesimal changes of either
\bm{x}
or
\bm{y}_{res}
.
First, suppose that state
\bm{x}
is perturbed to
\bm{x}^{\prime}=\bm{x}+\Delta\bm{x}
. For a general perturbation, which is not restricted by stoichiometric constraints, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x}\to\bm{x}^{\prime};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})}{R\Omega}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}+\Delta\bm{x}\|\bm{x}_{eq}]-\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}_{eq}]

\displaystyle\approx\left<\Delta\bm{x},\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{eq}\right>.

(86)

Here,
\Delta\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{eq}
are treated as elements of
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
, respectively.
Moreover, we use an implicit identification of the base spaces with their tangent and cotangent spaces.
Because
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
inherit the affine structure of dual vector spaces
\mathbb{V}
and
\mathbb{V}^{*}
, and because the tangent and cotangent spaces are isomorphic to these vector spaces, and we can identify these spaces (non-canonically) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\bm{y}}\mathcal{Y}\cong\mathcal{T}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{X}\cong\mathbb{V}\supset\mathcal{X},\quad\quad\mathcal{T}^{*}_{\bm{x}}\mathcal{X}\cong\mathcal{T}_{\bm{y}}\mathcal{Y}\cong\mathbb{V}^{*}=\mathcal{Y}.

Because of this isomorphism, we can also regard
\Delta\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{eq}
as elements of tangent and cotangent spaces.
By combining this fact with the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\Delta\Sigma^{tot}(\bm{x}\to\bm{x}^{\prime};\tilde{\bm{y}}_{res})}{R\Omega}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left<\Delta\bm{x},\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{eq}\right>\right|

(87)

\displaystyle\leq\left|\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{eq}\right|_{\bm{y}}\left|\Delta\bm{x}\right|_{\bm{x}},

(88)

where
\left|\cdot\right|_{\bm{x}}
and
\left|\cdot\right|_{\bm{y}}
are the metrics on the tangent and cotangent spaces induced by the Fisher information (Eqs.
56
and
58
).
From this, we see that
\left|\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{eq}\right|_{\bm{y}}
is an upper bound of the sensitivity of entropy production proposed in
Yoshimura and Ito 2021
and that entropy production is maximized if
\Delta\bm{x}
is parallel to
\bm{y}-\bm{y}_{eq}
in the sense of linear duality.

VII
Complex-balanced systems

The Hessian geometric structure can also be extended beyond equilibrium chemical systems to complex balanced systems.

Complex balanced systems, introduced by Horn and Jackson
Horn and Jackson 1972
, are a class of nonequilibrium systems, which preserve several properties of equilibrium chemical systems.
A complex balanced system has a unique steady state called complex balanced state, which is also globally stable
Gopalkrishnan et al. 2014
;
Craciun 2016
.
Moreover, the generalized Kullback-Leibler divergence works as the Lyapunov function of the system
Horn and Jackson 1972
.
This similarity is partially attributed to the shared geometric structure between equilibrium and complex balanced systems.

To define the complex balanced state, we note that the stoichiometric matrix
S
can be decomposed as
S=-YB
.
Hereby,
B
is the incidence matrix of the oriented graph obtained by regarding the sets of reactants or products in the reaction network as vertices (also called complexes) and the reactions as edges.
The orientation of an edge is determined by the direction of the corresponding forward reaction.

Y
maps the complexes to the respective constituent molecular species.
We also assume that the reaction flux
\bm{j}^{\pm}(\bm{x}_{cb};\bm{\theta})
satisfies the law of mass action.

Then the set of complex balanced states is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta}):=\{\bm{x}_{cb}>0|B\bm{j}(\bm{x}_{cb};\bm{\theta})=\bm{0}\}.

(89)

Because
\bm{j}^{\pm}(\bm{x}_{cb};\bm{\theta})
consists of monominals,
\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})
is an algebraic variety.
As before, we define the parameter sets
\Theta_{cb}
in which
\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})
is non-empty, i.e.:

\displaystyle\Theta_{cb}:=\{\bm{\theta}|\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})\neq\emptyset\}.

(90)

Obviously,
\Theta_{eq}\subset\Theta_{cb}
and thus an equilibrium variety is a special class of complex balanced varieties.
Compared with the definition of an equilibrium variety,
B\bm{j}(\bm{x}_{cb};\bm{\theta})=\bm{0}
is in general not given by binomial equations.
Nevertheless,
\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})
is a toric variety, meaning that
B\bm{j}(\bm{x}_{cb};\bm{\theta})=\bm{0}
can be converted to binomial equations by appropriate algebraic manipulation similar to the Gaussian elimination for linear equations
Craciun et al. 2009
.
In other words, a complex balanced state is defined by hidden detailed balance (binomial) equations.
Thus, as a toric variety,
\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})
has an explicit parametrization in the same way as the equilibrium variety (Eq.
15
).
Moreover, the complex balanced variety has the same design matrix and thus is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{x}|\ln\bm{x}=\ln\tilde{\bm{x}}_{cb}+U^{T}\bm{\eta}^{*}\right\},

(91)

where
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{cb}
is determined by the actual values of kinetic parameters
Craciun et al. 2009
.
It should be noted that multiple parameter values
\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{\prime}\in\Xi_{\theta}
can generate the same variety
\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})=\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta}^{\prime})
.

As geometrical objects, the complex balanced variety and equilibrium variety are indistinguishable and therefore the embedding of each complex balanced variety into
\mathcal{X}
is parametrized analogously by
\bm{\xi}^{*}
. From now on, we write
\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\xi}^{*})
for the complex balanced variety embedded in
\mathcal{X}
.
As a result, the Hessian geometric structure of the equilibrium variety described in Sec.
IV
is preserved in the more general case of complex balance.

For example, for a given initial state
\bm{x}_{0}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta})
, the complex balanced steady state
\bm{x}_{cb}
is the intersection of the polytope
\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta})
, determined by the initial state, and the complex balanced variety
\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\xi}^{*})
:

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{cb}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta})\cap\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\xi}^{*}).

(92)

For the same reason as the equilibrium state,
\bm{x}_{cb}
is unique and the intersection is transversal
Craciun et al. 2009
.
By using the same potential function
\varphi(\bm{x})
as in Eq.
23
, define the dual space
\mathcal{Y}
, the Legendre transformed function
\varphi^{*}(\bm{x})
, the Bregman divergence
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
, the tangent and cotangent spaces
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{Y}
, and also the Fisher information given by
G_{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{x})
and
G_{\mathcal{Y}}(\bm{y})
.
In other words, we can naturally embed a
\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\theta})
into the Hessian geometric structure constructed for the equilibrium varieties.
As a result, the same Pythagorean relation as Eq.
46
holds for the complex balanced case:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}_{p}\|\bm{x}_{cb}]+\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}_{cb}\|\bm{x}_{q}]=\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}_{p}\|\bm{x}_{q}],

(93)

where
\bm{x}_{p}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta})
,
\bm{x}_{q}^{\mathcal{X}}\in\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\xi}^{*})
, and
\bm{x}_{cb}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta})\cap\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\xi}^{*})
.
Thus, the complex balanced state admits the variational characterizations

\displaystyle\bm{x}_{cb}(\bm{\eta},\bm{\xi}^{*})

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\bm{x}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta})}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]

for any fixed
\bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\xi}^{*})

(94)

\displaystyle=\arg\min_{\bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{cb}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\xi}^{*})}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}[\bm{x}\|\bm{x}^{\prime}]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\mbox{for any fixed $\bm{x}\in\mathcal{P}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{\eta})$}.

(95)

The analogous variational characterization also holds in the dual space.

While the geometric structure is inherited, the thermodynamic aspects are not.
For example, the space
\mathcal{Y}
may not admit the same interpretation as the chemical potential space.
The potential function
\varphi(\bm{x})
and Bregman divergence
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
are no longer associated with the thermodynamic potential function and the difference of total entropy.
All of this is rooted in the general lack of a thermodynamically consistent characterization of nonequilibrium states.
While there have been continuous attempts to achieve a thermodynamic characterization of complex balanced states
Ge and Qian 2016
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
;
Yoshimura and Ito 2021
, it is still an open problem.
We believe that the Hessian geometric structure can contribute to the resolution of the problem.

VIII
Stochastic thermodynamics on Graph

Finally, we describe how a class of models in stochastic thermodynamics is derived as a special case of our results.

A reversible Markov chain on a graph is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}p_{i}}{\mathrm{d}t}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[W_{i\|j}p_{j}-W_{j\|i}p_{i}\right]

(96)

is often employed as a model of stochastic thermodynamics
Hill 2005
;
Schnakenberg 1976
;
Seifert 2012
;
Ito 2018
.
Here,
i
is the index of vertices of the graph, and
N
is the total number of vertices.

W_{i\|j}
is the transition rate from state
j
to
i
.
Here, the transition from a vertex to itself is usually prohibited, i.e.,
W_{i\|i}=0
.
By assuming
W_{i\|j}>0
for
i\neq j
, the reversible Markov chain can be represented by a complete graph.
We label the edges by running variable
m
, and determine the orientation of each edge arbitrary.
Let
B
be the incidence matrix of the oriented graph.
We define functions
h(m)
and
t(m)
that return the index of head and tail vertices of the
m
th oriented edge, respectively.
Then, Eq.
96
can be mapped to a chemical rate equation (Eq.
1
) by defining
x_{i}=p_{i}
,
Y=I
,
S=-YB
, and

\displaystyle j^{+}_{m}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=W_{t(m)\|h(m)}x_{h(m)},

(97)

\displaystyle j^{-}_{m}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=W_{h(m)\|t(m)}x_{t(m)}.

(98)

All fluxes are linear in this case.
Because the graph is fully connected,
B
has the unique left null vector
\bm{1}
as
\bm{1}^{T}B=\bm{0}
.
Thus, the system has only one conserved quantity
\bm{\eta}=\bm{1}^{T}\bm{p}
, which reflects the conservation of total probability, i.e.,
\bm{1}^{T}\bm{p}=1
.
Thus
U=\bm{1}^{T}
, and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\eta)=\{\bm{x}|\eta=U\bm{x}\}=\{\bm{x}|\eta=\bm{1}^{T}\bm{x}\}
.
The stoichiometric polytope is nothing but the
N-1
dimensional simplex of probability distributions if we fix
\eta=1
.
Even if
B
is not fully connected, we typically assume that the graph is strongly connected to assure that Eq.
96
has a unique and globally stable steady state
\pi
by the Perron-Frobenius theorem.
In this case,
B
has the unique left null vector
\bm{1}
as well.

The detailed balance condition of Eq.
96
is typically defined as

\displaystyle W_{i\|j}\pi_{j}=W_{j\|i}\pi_{i}\quad\mbox{for all $i$ and $j$}.

(99)

This is equivalent to the detailed balance condition of the chemical rate equation:
j^{+}_{m}(\bm{x}_{eq})=j^{-}_{m}(\bm{x}_{eq})
for all
m
, i.e.,
\bm{j}(\bm{x}_{eq})=0
, whereby
\bm{x}_{eq}=\bm{\pi}
.
The equilibrium constant characterizing an equilibrium state becomes
K_{m}=W_{t(m)\|h(m)}/W_{h(m)\|t(m)}
, which also satisfies

\displaystyle\ln\bm{K}=-B^{T}\ln\bm{\pi}

(100)

as follows from the detailed balance condition.
Thus, the equilibrium variety can be defined for the Markov chain.
Its explicit parametrization (Eq.
15
) is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{K})

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{x}|\bm{x}=\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}\circ\exp[\bm{1}\eta^{*}]\right\}.

(101)

Here, we used that
U=\bm{1}^{T}
and that
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}
satisfies
\ln\bm{K}=S^{T}\ln\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}=-B^{T}\ln\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}
.
From Eq.
100
, choosing the identification
\tilde{\bm{x}}_{eq}=\bm{\pi}
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{K})

\displaystyle=\left\{\bm{x}|\bm{x}=\bm{\pi}\circ\exp[\bm{1}\eta^{*}]\right\}.

(102)

The variety
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{K})
is one-dimensional because the codimension of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\eta)
is one.
The intersection of
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{K})
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\eta)
is trivially
\bm{\pi}
if
\eta=1
.
This means that, in the case of stochastic dynamics on a graph, the Hessian structure is not evident because the equilibrium variety is just one-dimensional and the polytope is always fixed at
\eta=1
, i.e.,
\bm{1}^{T}\bm{p}=1
.
Under this constraint, the potential function is reduced to the conventional Kullback-Leibler divergence,
\varphi(\bm{p})=\bm{p}^{T}\ln(\bm{p}/\hat{\bm{p}})
, where we define
\ln\hat{\bm{p}}:=\hat{y}
.
The Legendre dual of
\bm{p}
is
\bm{y}=\partial\varphi(\bm{p})=\ln\bm{p}-\ln\hat{\bm{p}}
.
Thus, even though
\ln\bm{p}
looks as if it is just the logarithm of
\bm{p}
, the vectors
\bm{p}
and
\ln\bm{p}
should be discriminated as objects defined on different spaces.
The dual of
\varphi
is
\varphi^{*}(\bm{y})=\bm{1}^{T}e^{\bm{y}+\hat{\bm{y}}}
.
The Bregman divergence is now reduced to the conventional Kullback-Leibler divergence
\mathcal{D}[\bm{p}\|\bm{p}^{\prime}]=\bm{p}^{T}\ln(\bm{p}/\bm{p}^{\prime})
.
Even though
\varphi(\bm{p})
and
\mathcal{D}[\bm{p}\|\bm{p}^{\prime}]
are different quantities, both have the same expression as a KL divergence, which can cause confusion.
Finally, the Pythagorean relation does not provide nontrivial information because the polytope
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{X}}(\eta)
is fixed as the probability simplex and the variety
\mathcal{V}_{eq}^{\mathcal{X}}(\bm{K})
is one-dimensional.
Thus, the Hessian geometric structure is not fully exhibited under the restricted setting of a Markov chain model.
Nonetheless, the above discussion is beneficial because it allows to discriminate degenerate quantities that have different meanings, such as
\bm{p}
and
\ln\bm{p}
or
\varphi(\bm{x})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{X}}
and also to point out their locations in dual spaces
Ohga and Ito 2021a
.

IX
Discussion

In this work, we derived the Hessian geometric structure of chemical reaction systems which satisfy the law of mass action.
When establishing the geometry, a crucial role is played by the fact that the equilibrium and complex balanced states are given by toric varieties.
A purely thermodynamic argument in
Sughiyama et al. 2021
assures, however, that this geometric structure holds for a broader class of chemical reaction systems than those obeying mass action kinetics.
In the last century, there have been continuous attempts to extend the properties of equilibrium and complex balanced systems beyond the law of mass action in physics and applied mathematics
Avanzini et al. 2021
;
Craciun et al. 2019
;
Adamer and Helmer 2020
.
Thus, we expect that our results can provide a new theoretical basis for the development of chemical reaction network theory to such generalized models.

Our current approach relies on the fact that the state space of the system is represented by finite-dimensional vectors: the concentration vector in chemical reaction systems and the probability vector in the Markov chain model.
However, in reaction systems with a spatially inhomogeneous structure or in general stochastic thermodynamic models, the system is described by a positive or stochastic measure on a continuous space.
It is an open question whether the structure presented here can be extended to such cases.
It is naturally expected that information or Hessian geometry on an infinite-dimensional space becomes necessary to address the problem
Ay et al. 2017
;
Newton 2012
.

For the theory of information geometry and Hessian geometry
Amari 2016
;
Shima 2007
, chemical reaction systems provide a new and fertile field to apply and develop the theory.
While major applications of the theory have been restricted to statistics, information science, and other applied mathematical topics, more recently, applications to stochastic thermodynamics have been attempted
Crooks 2007
;
Ito 2018
;
Kolchinsky and Wolpert 2021
;
Ohga and Ito 2021a
.
Now, we add chemical reaction systems to the list.
In addition, from the viewpoint of real algebraic geometry, our results on chemical reaction systems may suggest a way to generalize Birch’s theorem to more general kinetic models
Craciun et al. 2019
.

The mathematical structure of chemical kinetics and chemical thermodynamics, since their establishment, has been continuously developed within various fields such as physics, applied mathematics, applied chemistry, and systems biology over the past century
Beard and Qian 2008
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
;
Aris 1965
;
Feinberg 2019
;
Alon 2019
.
However, the developments were mostly separated and shared only within the individual fields.
In applied mathematics, there is chemical reaction network theory by Feinberg
Feinberg 2019
, which is based on the work of Aris, Horn, and Jackson
Aris 1965
;
Horn and Jackson 1972
.
In real algebraic geometry, chemical reaction systems and toric geometry are becoming important research topics
Gopalkrishnan et al. 2014
;
Craciun 2016
;
Dickenstein et al. 2019
;
Joshi and Shiu 2017
.
In systems biology, a new theory emerged, which connects properties of reaction networks with the network topology
Shinar and Feinberg 2010
;
Okada and Mochizuki 2016
;
Mochizuki and Fiedler 2015
;
Fiedler and Mochizuki 2015
;
Hirono et al. 2021
.
In physics, network thermodynamics by Hill and Schnakenberg
Hill 2005
;
Schnakenberg 1976
and stochastic thermodynamics of chemical reaction theory by Qian and Esposito have been studied
Beard and Qian 2008
;
Rao and Esposito 2016
;
Qian and Ge 2021
.
Now, information and Hessian geometry can be added to this variety of applications.
Even though the theories have been developed to explain the same physical object, i.e., a chemical reaction system, the interrelationships between them are not clear yet.
It will be the next important step to integrate these theories from a unified perspective, which is expected to boost a further development of chemical reaction theory.

We note that a draft on a similar topic appeared on arXiv very recently (27 Dec 2021)
Ohga and Ito 2021b
.

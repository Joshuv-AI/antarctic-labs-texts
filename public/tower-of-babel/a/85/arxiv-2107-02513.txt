Abstract
. For any cardinal number κ \kappa and an index set Γ \Gamma , the Σ κ \Sigma_{\kappa} –product of real lines Σ κ ​ ( ℝ Γ ) \Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}) consists of all elements of ℝ Γ \mathbb{R}^{\Gamma} having < κ <\kappa many nonzero coordinates. A compact space K K is κ \kappa -Corson compact if it can be embedded into Σ κ ​ ( ℝ Γ ) \Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}) for some Γ \Gamma . The class of ( ω 1 \omega_{1} )-Corson compact spaces has been intensively studied over last decades. We discuss properties of κ \kappa -Corson compacta for various cardinal numbers κ \kappa as well as properties of related Boolean algebras and spaces of continuous functions. We present here a detailed discussion of the class of ω \omega -Corson compacta extending the results of Nakhmanson and Yakovlev [ NY ] . For κ > ω \kappa>\omega , our results on κ \kappa -Corson compact spaces are related to the line of research originated by Kalenda [ Ka2 ] and Bell and Marciszewski [ BM ] , and continued by Bonnet, Kubiś and Todorčević in their recent paper [ BKT ] .

1.
Introduction

Given an infinite cardinal number
\kappa
, a compact space
K
is
said to be
\kappa
-Corson compact
if, for some set
\Gamma
,

K
is homeomorphic to a subset of the
\Sigma_{\kappa}
–product of real lines, that is

\Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}:|\{\gamma:x(\gamma)\neq 0\}|<\kappa\}.

For
\kappa=\omega_{1}
this definition names the well-studied class of Corson compacta,
compact spaces that can be embedded into the familiar
\Sigma
-product of real lines, see e.g.
[
Ne
]
and
[
AMN
]
.
For arbitrary
\kappa
,
\kappa
-Corson compact spaces were first considered by Kalenda
[
Ka2
]

and in
[
BM
]
(where the terminology was slightly different).

The classes of
\kappa
-Corson compacta are a subject of a recent paper by Bonnet, Kubiś and
Todorčević
[
BKT
]
which motivated us to undertake our research that we report here.
Trying to examine them systematically one discovers quickly that the case
\kappa=\omega
is
quite special. In sections
3
–
6
we investigate
\omega
-Corson compacta
and a wider, somewhat more natural, class of compact spaces that can be embedded into
a
\sigma
-product of metrizable compacta. The latter
we call NY compacta to stress
the contribution of Nakhmanson and Yakovlev
[
NY
]
to the subject.

For regular
\kappa>\omega
there is a number of properties of
\kappa
-Corson compacta that
may be seen as natural analogues of known features of the usual Corson compact spaces.
However, if
\kappa
is a singular cardinal, then one faces number of problems that we were not able to
handle, with the exception of results from section
7
.

Here is the summary of our main results:

(A)

Extending results from
[
NY
]
, we give internal characterizations
of
\omega
-Corson compacta (Theorem
4.6
) and of NY compacta (Theorem
4.1
). We discuss the stability of these classes of compacta under countable products (Corollary
5.2
), continuous images (Corollary
5.3
), and the functor
P
which assigns to a compact space
K
the compact space
P(K)

of regular probability Borel measures equipped with the
weak^{\ast}
topology (Remark
5.11
). In
[
NY
]
it was proved that all NY compacta are hereditarily metacompact, in section
5
we investigate this covering property for Eberlein compacta not belonging to the class
\mathcal{NY}
(Example
5.5
, Theorem
5.9
).

(B)

We present some results on
\pi
-character and tightness of
\kappa
-Corson
compact space for arbitrary
\kappa>\omega
, answering two question of Kalenda
[
Ka2
]
.

(C)

We prove that for every uncountable regular
\kappa
the class
of
\kappa
-Corson compact spaces is stable under continuous images (Theorem
9.2
).
The result was first proved in
[
BM
]
for succesor cardinals and in
[
BKT
]
for
arbitrary regular
\kappa
by some model-theoretic approach. Our proof extends
the argument of Gul’ko
[
Gu
]
for
\kappa=\omega_{1}
.

(D)

In section
10
we partially solve a problem posed in
[
BKT
]
:
Theorem
10.3
says, in particular, that no
\sigma
-complete Boolean algebra of regular cardinality

\kappa
can have a
\kappa
-Corson compact Stone space.

(E)

In section
11
we discuss generalizations of a
result due to Pol
[
Po
]
,
characterizing Corson compacta

K
in terms of topological properties of function spaces

. We extend this characterization for
\kappa
-Corson compacta, where
\kappa
is regular (Theorem

), and use it to present yet another proof of the stability of the classes of these compacta under continuous images.

(F)

In section
12
we show that the question for
which
\kappa

\kappa
-Corson compact spaces is closed under the mentioned above functor

(of
forming the space of probability measures) is naturally related to the notion of a caliber of measures
(so is, typically, undecidable in the usual set theory). In turn, in the final section we discuss

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Terminology and notation

All topological spaces under consideration are assumed to be completely regular (Tikhonov).
As usual, the topological weight of a space
X
is denoted by
w(X)

(see section
7
for other cardinal invariants).

For a set
X
and
n\in\omega
, we use the standard notation

[X]^{n}=\{A\subseteq X:|A|=n\},\quad[X]^{\leq n}=\bigcup\{[X]^{k}:k\leq n\};

moreover
[X]^{<\omega}
stands for
\bigcup\{[X]^{k}:k<\omega\}
.

A family
\mathcal{U}
of subsets of a space
X

is
T_{0}
-separating if, for every pair of distinct points
x,y
of

X
, there is
U\in\mathcal{U}
containing exactly one of the
points
x,y
.

Given a family
\mathcal{U}
of subsets of a space
X
we define
{\rm ord}(x,\mathcal{U})=|\{U\in\mathcal{U}:x\in U\}|
for
x\in X
. We say that
\mathcal{U}
is
point-finite
if
{\rm ord}(x,\mathcal{U})<\omega
for all
x\in X
.

For a locally compact space
X
,
\alpha(X)
denotes the one point compactification of
X
. We denote the point at infinity of this compactification by
\infty_{X}
. For an infinite cardinal number
\kappa
,
D(\kappa)
denotes a discrete space of cardinality
\kappa
, and
A(\kappa)=\alpha(D(\kappa))
.

Recall that for any compact space
K
, its Aleksandrov duplicate
AD(K)
is defined as
the space
AD(K)=K\times 2
in which all the points
(x,1)
for
x\in K
are isolated
and basic neighborhoods of a point
(x,0)
have the form
(U\times 2)\setminus\{(x,1)\}
, where
U
is an open neighborhood of
x
in
K
.

A space
X
is
scattered
if no nonempty subset
A\subseteq X
is dense-in-itself.
For a scattered space
K
, its

Cantor-Bendixson height

ht(X)
is the minimal ordinal number

\alpha
such that the Cantor-Bendixson derivative
K^{(\alpha)}

of the space
K
is empty. The Cantor-Bendixson height of a
compact scattered space is always a nonlimit ordinal.

A space
X
is
metacompact
if every open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
has a point-finite open refinement
\mathcal{V}
. It is
\sigma
-metacompact
if every open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
X
has an open refinement
\mathcal{V}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\mathcal{V}_{n}
, where each family
\mathcal{V}_{n}
is point-finite.

For a space
X
, the sets of the form
f^{-1}((0,1])
, where
f:X\to[0,1]
is a continuous function, are called the
cozero sets
.

Recall that a space
X
is
strongly countable-dimensional
if
X
is a countable union of closed subspaces of finite covering dimension, see
[
En2
]
.

2.2.
\Sigma_{\kappa}
-products

We often write
I
for the closed unit interval
[0,1]
.
In the sequel,
\kappa
always stands for an infinite cardinal number.

Given
x
in the product space
\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}
, we write

\supp(x)=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:x(\gamma)\neq 0\}
.
Thus
\Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})
is the space of all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}
satisfying
|\supp(x)|<\kappa

and
\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})
is defined analogously.
Traditionally,
\Sigma_{\omega_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})
is written as
\Sigma(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})

and
\Sigma_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})
is written as
\sigma(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})
.
However, we need to specify more notation for the case
\kappa=\omega
.

Let
\{X_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
be a family of nonempty topological spaces, and let
a_{\gamma}
be
a fixed point in
X_{\gamma}
. The
\sigma
-product of the family

\{(X_{\gamma},a_{\gamma}):\gamma\in\Gamma\}
is defined as

\sigma(X_{\gamma},a_{\gamma},\Gamma)=\{(x_{\gamma})_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\in\prod_{\gamma\in\Gamma}X_{\gamma}:|\{\gamma\in\Gamma:x_{\gamma}\neq a_{\gamma}\}|<\omega\}\,.

If
X_{\gamma}=I
and
a_{\gamma}=0
, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
, then

\sigma(X_{\gamma},a_{\gamma},\Gamma)
is simply denoted by
\sigma(I,\Gamma)
.
Likewise,
if
X_{\gamma}=I^{\omega}
and
a_{\gamma}=(0,0,\dots)
, for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
then
\sigma(X_{\gamma},a_{\gamma},\Gamma)
is denoted by
\sigma(I^{\omega},\Gamma)
.

2.3.
Corson (
\kappa
-Corson) compacta and subclasses

As we have already mentioned, a compact space is said to be
\kappa
-Corson compact if it can be embedded into

\Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})
for some
\Gamma
.
To respect the existing tradition concerning the case
\kappa=\omega_{1}
, we write Corson compact rather than

\omega_{1}
-Corson compact.

Remark 2.1
.

We record here the following preliminary observations.

(a)

While defining
\kappa
-Corson compacta, one can clearly replace
\Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})
by

\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})
.

(b)

If
K
is
\kappa
-Corson compact, then
K
embeds into

\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})
where
\Gamma
is of size
w(K)
.

(c)

Every
compact space
K
is
\kappa
-Corson compact for
\kappa>w(K)
.

(d)

A compact space
K
is
\kappa
-Corson compact if and only if
there is a family
{\mathcal{F}}
of continuous functions
K\to[0,1]
that separates the points of
K
and

|\{f\in{\mathcal{F}}:f(x)\neq 0\}|<\kappa
for every
x\in K
.

The following result is a straightforward generalization of the well known
Rosenthal-type characterization of Corson compacta (see e.g.
[
BKT
]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\kappa
be an uncountable cardinal number. For a compact space
K
, the following
conditions are equivalent

(a)

K
is
\kappa
-Corson;

(b)

There exists a family
\mathcal{U}
consisting of cozero subsets of
K
which is
T_{0}
-separating, and

{\rm ord}(x,\mathcal{U})<\kappa
for all
x\in K
.

A space
K
is an
Eberlein
compact
if
K
is homeomorphic to a weakly compact subset of a Banach
space.
By the celebrated Amir-Lindenstrauss theorem, a compact space
K
is an Eberlein compactum if and only if
it can be embedded into

c_{0}(\Gamma)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}:\big|\{\gamma:|x(\gamma)|>\varepsilon\}\big|<\omega\mbox{ for every }\varepsilon>0\},

for some set
\Gamma
, see
[
Ne
]
.
As
c_{0}(\Gamma)\subseteq\Sigma_{\omega_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})
,
the class of Corson compact spaces contains all Eberlein compacta.

If
K
is homeomorphic to a weakly compact subset of a
Hilbert space, then
K
is said to be
uniform Eberlein
compact
. Note that the class of uniform Eberlein compacta contains all
metrizable compact spaces.

2.4.
Boolean algebras

For a Boolean algebra
\mathfrak{B}
we denote by
{\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B})
its Stone space (of all ultrafilters on
\mathfrak{B}
);
the Stone isomorphism

\mathfrak{B}\ni b\mapsto\widehat{b}=\{p\in{\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B}):b\in p\}

identifies
\mathfrak{B}
with the algebra of clopen subsets of
{\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B})
.

We say that a Boolean algebra
\mathfrak{B}
is
\kappa
-Corson
if
its Stone space
{\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B})
is
\kappa
-Corson compact. The property can be characterized internally
as follows.

Lemma 2.3
.

A Boolean algebra
\mathfrak{B}
is
\kappa
-Corson if and only if there is

G\subseteq\mathfrak{B}
that generates
\mathfrak{B}
and every centered subfamily
G_{0}\subseteq G
has size
<\kappa
.

The proof of the lemma above can be found in
[
BKT
]
; it is an obvious analogue
of the case
\kappa=\omega_{1}
which was already noted in
[
MP
, Lemma 2.2.]
.

2.5.
Function spaces

Given a compact space
K
, by
C(K)
we denote the Banach space of
continuous real-valued functions on
K
, equipped with the standard
supremum norm.
As usual, we identify the dual space
C(K)^{\ast}
with the space

M(K)
of signed Radon measures on
K
having finite variation
and write
{\mu}(g)
for
\int_{K}g\;{\rm d}\mu
. The symbol
M_{1}(K)
stands for the unit ball of

M(K)
, equipped with the
weak^{\ast}
topology inherited from
C(K)^{\ast}
.
Finally,
P(K)
is the subset of regular probability measures on
K
.

We will also consider function spaces of the form
C_{p}(K)
; in such a case
C(K)
is equipped with the pointwise
convergence topology.

3.
\omega
-Corson compact spaces and NY compacta

Motivated by the results by Nakhmanson and Yakovlev
[
NY
]
, we introduce here
the following notion.

Definition 3.1
.

We shall say that a compact space
K
is NY compact
or write
K\in\mathcal{NY}

if
K

can be embedded into some
\sigma
-product of metrizable compacta.

We start by the following easy observations.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
K
be a compact space
K
.

(a)

K
is
\omega
-Corson if and only if it can be embedded into some

\sigma
-product of metrizable finitely dimensional compacta.

(b)

K
is NY compact if and only if it can be embedded into the
\sigma
-product
\sigma(I^{\omega},\Gamma)
for some set
\Gamma
.

Proof.

The forward implication in (a) follows from Remark
2.1
(a).
To check the reverse implication, suppose that

\Xi
is an embedding of
K
into
\sigma(X_{\gamma},a_{\gamma},\Gamma)
, where
X_{\gamma}
’s
are compact metrizable spaces of finite dimension.

For every
\gamma\in\Gamma
there exist a natural number
k_{\gamma}
and
a homeomorphic embedding
\Phi_{\gamma}:X_{\gamma}\hookrightarrow[-1,1]^{k_{\gamma}}

such that
\Phi_{\gamma}(a_{\gamma})=\mathbf{0}^{k_{\gamma}}
; hence we can treat
K
as a subspace of
\sigma([-1,1],0,{\Gamma^{\prime}})
for a suitable set
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
We identify
[-1,1]
with
(\{0\}\times[0,1])\cup([0,1]\times\{0\})
by rotating
[-1,1]
over 90 degrees. In this way we turn a subspace of
\sigma([-1,1],0,{\Gamma^{\prime}})
into a subspace of
\Sigma_{\omega}([0,1]^{\Gamma^{\prime}\times 2})
.

Part (b) follows immediately from the fact that that any metrizable compactum can be embedded into the Hilbert cube
I^{\omega}
and
I^{\omega}
is homogeneous.
∎

Let us note that Proposition
3.2
(b) gives another equivalent definition of the
class
\mathcal{NY}
which will be often used below.

Proposition 3.3
.

Every
\omega
-Corson compact space is Eberlein compact and strongly countably dimensional.

Proof.

If
K
is a
\omega
-Corson compact space, then it is also Eberlein compact by the very definition.
To verify that second statement we can assume that
K\subseteq\sigma(I,\Gamma)
.

Note that

\sigma(I,\Gamma)=\bigcup_{n}A_{n}
where

A_{n}=\{x\in\sigma(I,\Gamma):|\supp(x)|\leq n\}.

Moreover, every
A_{n}
is a compact space of dimension
n
, see
[
EP
]
.
It follows that
\dim(A_{n}\cap K)\leq\dim A_{n}=n
and hence
K
is strongly countably dimensional.
∎

Proposition 3.4
.

A metrizable compact space
K
is
\omega
-Corson if and only if it is strongly countably dimensional.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Proposition
3.3

and the result from
[
En2
]
stating that the space
\sigma(I,\omega)

is universal for the class of separable metrizable spaces that
are strongly countably dimensional.
∎

It becomes clear that every
\omega
-Corson compact space is NY compact, while
the Hilbert cube is NY compact but not
\omega
-Corson compact.
We shall see later that there are zero-dimensional Eberlein compacta which are not

\omega
-Corson.
The assertion of Proposition
3.4
in fact
holds for NY compact spaces
K
, see Corollary
5.1
.

Remark 3.5
.

Given a Boolean algebra
\mathfrak{B}
and its Stone space
K={\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B})
, the following
conditions are equivalent:

(i)

K
is
\omega
-Corson compact;

(ii)

K
has a
T_{0}
-separating, point-finite family
\mathcal{U}
consisting of clopen subsets;

(iii)

K
is scattered Eberlein compact;

(iv)

K
is scattered Corson compact.

Indeed, (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) follows immediately from Lemma
2.3
and
(ii) implies that
K
can be embedded into some
\sigma(\{0,1\},\Gamma)
;
it is easy
to check that every compact subset of such a
\sigma
-product is scattered.
Then (iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii) is a result of Alster
[
Al
]
.

In connection with Remark
3.5
, we shall indicate that
the Rosenthal-type characterization of

\kappa
-Corson compacta given by Proposition
2.2

fails for
\kappa=\omega
.

Proposition 3.6
.

For a compact space
K
, the following
conditions are equivalent:

(a)

There exists a
T_{0}
-separating, point-finite family
\mathcal{U}
consisting of cozero subsets of
K
;

(b)

K
is a scattered Eberlein compact space.

In view of Remark
3.5
, Proposition
3.6

follows immediately from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

If a compact space
K
has a point-finite,
T_{0}
- separating family
\mathcal{U}
of open sets,
then
K
is scattered.

Proof.

Suppose that
K
is not scattered and let
A\subseteq K
be a nonempty subset
without isolated points. To arrive at contradiction,
we inductively construct nonempty open sets
V_{n}
such that
\overline{V_{n+1}}\subseteq V_{n}
and
|\{U\in\mathcal{U}:V_{n}\subseteq U\}|\geq n
for every
n
.

To start, pick
x,y\in A
,
x\neq y
.
There exists
V_{1}\in\mathcal{U}
such that
|V_{1}\cap\{x,y\}|=1
;
say
x\in V_{1}
. As
x
is not isolated in
A
,
there is
y_{1}\in A\cap V_{1}\setminus\{x\}
. In turn, there exists

U_{1}\in\mathcal{U}
separating
x
and
y_{1}
.
Assume, for instance, that
y_{1}\in U_{1}
and let
V_{2}
be an open set such that

y_{1}\in V_{2}\subseteq\overline{V_{2}}\subseteq U_{1}\cap V_{1}
.
Continuing in this fashion, we get the required sequences of
V_{n}
’s.

Now the set
F=\bigcap_{n}V_{n}=\bigcap_{n}\overline{V_{n}}
is
nonempty by compactness. If
z\in F
then
z
belongs to infinitely many elements of
the family
\mathcal{U}
, a contradiction.
∎

We close this section by a few observations on the stability of our classes under
some operations. It was proved in
[
Ma
, Proposition 2.8]
that
the Aleksandrov duplicate
AD(K)
of an (uniform) Eberlein compact space
K
is (uniform) Eberlein compact.
Using the same argument one can show a counterpart of this observation for
\kappa
-Corson and NY compacta.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any infinite cardinal number
\kappa
,
the Aleksandrov duplicate
AD(K)
of a
\kappa
-Corson compact (NY compact) space
K
is

\kappa
-Corson compact (NY compact).

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
\kappa>\omega
and let
K_{t},\ t\in T,
be a family of

\kappa
-Corson compact spaces (NY compact spaces).
Then the one point compactification
\alpha(\bigoplus_{t\in T}K_{t})
of the discrete union

\bigoplus_{t\in T}K_{t}
is
\kappa
-Corson compact (NY compact, respectively).

Proof.

We will prove this fact only for
\kappa
-Corson compacta (for NY compacta the proof is very similar).

Denote the point at infinity of
\alpha(\bigoplus_{t\in T}K_{t})
by
\infty
.
We can assume that
K_{t}\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma_{t}})
for some set
\Gamma_{t}
, and the sets
\Gamma_{t}
are pairwise disjoint and disjoint from
T
.

Consider

\Gamma=T\cup\bigcup_{t\in T}\Gamma_{t}\,.
Let
\psi:\alpha(\bigoplus_{t\in T}K_{t})\to\Sigma_{\kappa}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})
be defined by

\psi(x)(\gamma)=\begin{cases}x(\gamma)&\text{if }x\in K_{t}\,,\ \gamma\in\Gamma_{t}\,,\ t\in T\\
1&\text{if }x\in K_{t}\,,\ \gamma=t\,,\ t\in T\\
0&\text{if }x=\infty\,,\gamma\in\Gamma\end{cases}

for
x\in\alpha(\bigoplus_{t\in T}K_{t})
. A routine verification shows that
\psi
is an embedding.
∎

4.
Characterizations of
\omega
-Corson compacta and
NY
compacta

The main result of this section presents a characterization of
\omega
-Corson compact spaces
given in Theorem
4.6
. Our theorem builds on a characterization of the class

\mathcal{NY}
obtained by Nakhmanson and Yakovlev
[
NY
]
, which we expand by
adding another condition, see Theorem
4.1
(iv).

The following theorem offers several internal characterizations of compacta
from the class
\mathcal{NY}
.
Recall that a family
\mathcal{A}
of subsets of a topological space
X
is
closure preserving if
\overline{\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}}=\bigcup\{\overline{A}:A\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\}

for any subfamily
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For a compact space
K
, the following
conditions are equivalent:

(i)

K
is NY compact;

(ii)

there exists a
T_{0}
-separating family

\mathcal{U}=\bigcup\{\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}

consisting of cozero subsets of
K
, where each
\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}
is countable and the family
\{\bigcup\mathcal{U}_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
is point-finite;

(iii)

K
has a closure preserving cover consisting of metrizable compacta;

(iv)

K
is hereditarily metacompact and each nonempty subspace
A
of
K

contains a nonempty relatively open subspace
U
of countable weight.

\Leftrightarrow

To incorporate condition (iv) we need some preparations.
The proposition given below was also proved in

using
the equivalent assumption that the compact space

in question has a closure preserving cover

Proposition 4.2
.

If
K\in\mathcal{NY}
, then each nonempty subspace
A
of
K
contains a nonempty relatively open subspace
U
of countable weight.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
(b), we can assume that
K\subseteq\sigma(I^{\omega},\Gamma)
for some
\Gamma
. Obviously, it is enough to prove the assertion for the closure
\overline{A}
of a nonempty set
A\subseteq K
. Since
\overline{A}
is a compact subset of
\sigma(I^{\omega},\Gamma)
,
for simplicity we can assume that
\overline{A}=K
.

Suppose that there exists nonempty open set
U\subseteq K
such that
its every nonempty open subset has uncountable weight.
We can assume that
U
is a basic open set, that is

U=\{x\in K:x(\gamma)\in V_{\gamma}\mbox{ for }\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}\},

for some finite
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
and some family of open sets

\{V_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma_{0}\}
in
I^{\omega}
.

Note that for every countable
\Gamma^{\prime}\subseteq\Gamma
there must be

x\in U
and
\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma^{\prime}
such that

x(\gamma)\neq 0
. Indeed, otherwise
U
would be contained in a countable product of Hilbert’s cubes
so it would have a countable base.

Take
x\in U
and
\gamma_{1}\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{0}
such that

x(\gamma_{1})\neq 0
. Take open
G
with
x\in G\subseteq K

and such that for every
y\in G
we have
y(\gamma_{1})\neq 0
.
Then find open
W\subseteq K
such that

x\in W\subseteq\overline{W}\subseteq U
and put
U_{1}=W\cap G
.
Finally, set
\Gamma_{1}=\Gamma_{0}\cup\{\gamma_{1}\}
.
Then for every
y\in U_{1}
we have
y(\gamma_{1})\neq 0
and

x\in U_{1}\subseteq\overline{U_{1}}\subseteq U
.

Repeating the above argument, we can inductively construct nonempty open subsets
U_{n}
of
K

and distinct coordinates
\gamma_{n}\in\Gamma
such that

U_{n+1}\subseteq\overline{U_{n+1}}\subseteq U_{n}
and
if
z\in U_{n}
, then
z(\gamma_{n})\neq 0
. Clearly, by compactness,

F=\bigcap_{n}U_{n}=\bigcap_{n}\overline{U_{n}}\neq\emptyset
and
any
w\in F
has infinitely many nonzero coordinates, a contradiction.
∎

For the next lemma we slightly modify the argument used in

Lemma 4.3
.

If
K\in\mathcal{NY}
then for any family
\mathcal{V}
of open subsets of
K
, there exists a point-finite open refinement
\mathcal{U}

such that
\bigcup{\mathcal{U}}=\bigcup{\mathcal{V}}
and every
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
is
\sigma
-compact.

Proof.

Consider
K\subseteq\sigma(X_{\gamma},\xi_{\gamma},\Gamma)=Z
where

\{X_{\gamma}:\gamma\in\Gamma\}
is a family of compact metrizable spaces and

\xi_{\gamma}\in X_{\gamma}
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

Since, for a
\sigma
-compact
V\subseteq Z
, the intersection
V\cap K
is also
\sigma
-compact it is enough to prove that for any family
\mathcal{V}
of open subsets of
Z
, there exists a point-finite open refinement
\mathcal{U}

such that
\bigcup{\mathcal{U}}=\bigcup{\mathcal{V}}
and every
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
is
\sigma
-compact. Moreover, without loss of generality, we can assume that the family
\mathcal{V}
consists of basic open sets in
Z
.

For
B\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega}
, let
\pi_{B}:Z\to\prod_{\gamma\in B}X_{\gamma}
be the projection restricted to
Z
. Observe that, for each open set
U\subseteq\prod_{\gamma\in B}X_{\gamma}
, the set

\pi_{B}^{-1}(U)=U\times\sigma(X_{\gamma},\xi_{\gamma},\Gamma\setminus B)

is
\sigma
-compact being a product of

\sigma
-compact spaces: a
\sigma
-product and
an open subset of a metrizable compact space. In particular, each basic open set in
Z
is
\sigma
-compact, hence all
V\in\mathcal{V}
are
\sigma
-compact.

Given
B\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega}
, let

U(B)=\{x\in Z:\forall_{\gamma\in B}\ x(\gamma)\neq\xi_{\gamma}\}=\pi_{B}^{-1}(\prod_{\gamma\in B}(X_{\gamma}\setminus\{\xi_{\gamma}\})).

Every
U(B)
is open in
Z
and
\sigma
-compact, and the family
\{U(B):B\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega}\}
is point-finite.

For
B\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega}
, let

F(B)=\{x\in Z:x(\gamma)=\xi_{\gamma}\iff\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus B\}.

Observe that
\pi_{B}|F(B)
maps
F(B)
homeomorphically onto
\prod_{\gamma\in B}(X_{\gamma}\setminus\{\xi_{\gamma}\})
.
By the definition of a
\sigma
-product,
\bigcup\{F(B):B\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega}\}=Z
.

Let
\mathcal{K}(B)=\{V\cap F(B):V\in\mathcal{V}
} for
B\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega}
. Since
F(B)
is metrizable, there exists a point-finite family
\mathcal{M}(B)
of open subsets of
F(B)
, such that
\mathcal{M}(B)\prec\mathcal{K}(B)
and
\bigcup\mathcal{M}(B)=\bigcup\mathcal{K}(B)
. Each set
W\in\mathcal{M}(B)
is
\sigma
-compact because
F(B)
is
\sigma
-compact and metrizable.

Let

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(B)=\{\pi_{B}(W):W\in\mathcal{M}(B)\}.

Since
\pi_{B}|F(B)
is a homeomorphism,

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(B)
is a point-finite family of open sets in the product
\prod_{\gamma\in B}(X_{\gamma}\setminus\{\xi_{\gamma}\})
, hence the sets from
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}(B)
are also open in
\prod_{\gamma\in B}X_{\gamma}
.

Let
W
be an element of
M(B)
. Since
\mathcal{M}(B)\prec\mathcal{K}(B)
, there exists
V\in\mathcal{V}
such that
W\subseteq V\cap F(B)
. Let

\widetilde{W}=\pi^{-1}_{B}(\pi_{B}(W))\cap V,

then
\widetilde{W}\cap F(B)=W
, and
\pi_{B}(\widetilde{W})=\pi_{B}(W)
.

The set
\pi_{B}(W)
is
\sigma
-compact as a continuous image of a
\sigma
-compact set. The set
\pi^{-1}_{B}(\pi_{B}(W))=\pi_{B}(W)\times\sigma(X_{\gamma},\xi_{\gamma},\Gamma\setminus B)
is
\sigma
-compact being a product of
\sigma
-compact sets. Finally, the set
\widetilde{W}
is
\sigma
-compact as an intersection of two
\sigma
-compact sets.

Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\widetilde{W}:W\in\mathcal{M}(B)\};

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(B)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\pi^{-1}_{B}(\pi_{B}(W)):W\in\mathcal{M}(B)\}.

The family
\mathcal{R}(B)
is point-finite, because
M^{\prime}(B)
is point-finite, so
\mathcal{P}(B)
is point-finite as a shrinking of
\mathcal{R}(B)
. For each
W\in\mathcal{M}(B)
we have
W\subseteq F(B)
and
\widetilde{W}\subseteq U(B)
, therefore
\bigcup\mathcal{P}(B)\subseteq U(B)
.

Put
\mathcal{U}=\bigcup\{\mathcal{P}(B):B\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega}\}
; this family is point-finite because the family
\{U(B):B\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega}\}
and all families
\mathcal{P}(B),B\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega}
, are point-finite. Each set
\widetilde{W}\in\mathcal{U}
is open in Z and
\sigma
-compact.

From the definition of sets
\widetilde{W}
, it immediately follows that
\mathcal{U}\prec\mathcal{V}
.

Finally, we shall check that
\bigcup\mathcal{U}=\bigcup\mathcal{V}
. Let
x\in V\in\mathcal{V}
. Then
x\in F(B)
, for some
B
, so
x\in V\cap F(B)\in K(B)
. Because
\mathcal{M}(B)\prec\mathcal{K}(B)
and
\bigcup\mathcal{M}(B)=\bigcup\mathcal{K}(B)
, there exists
W\in\mathcal{M}(B)
such that
x\in W\subseteq V\cap F(B)
. As we observed before
\widetilde{W}\cap F(B)=W
, so
x\in\widetilde{W}
, that is
x\in\bigcup\mathcal{P}(B)
, therefore
x\in\bigcup\mathcal{U}
.
∎

Let us recall that one can adapt the Cantor-Bendixson rank of scattered spaces to
treat any topological property
\mathcal{P}
.
Namely, for any ordinal number
\alpha
we define the
\alpha
-th derivative

X^{(\alpha)}
of a given topological space
X
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
as follows:

(1)

X^{\prime}=X^{(1)}=X\setminus\bigcup\{U\subseteq X:U\mbox{ is open and has property }\mathcal{P}\}
;

(2)

X^{(\alpha+1)}=(X^{(\alpha)})^{\prime}
;

(3)

X^{(\alpha)}=\bigcap_{\beta<\alpha}X^{(\beta)}
for a limit ordinal
\alpha
.

If
X^{(\alpha)}=\emptyset
for some
\alpha
’s then the height
ht(X)
(with respect to
\mathcal{P}
)
is defined as
ht(X)=\min\{\alpha:X^{(\alpha)}=\emptyset\}
.
Note that if
X
is compact, then
ht(X)
is a successor ordinal number.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a hereditary topological property. For any topological spaces
Y\subseteq X
we have
Y^{(\alpha)}\subseteq X^{(\alpha)}
for every
\alpha
.

Proof.

Note first that
Y^{\prime}\subseteq X^{\prime}
. Indeed, if
x\in Y\setminus X^{\prime}
then there exists an open set

U\subseteq X
with property
\mathcal{P}
.
Then
U\cap Y
also has property
\mathcal{P}
and
x\in U\cap Y
which implies
x\notin Y^{\prime}
.

The general follows by induction on
\alpha
: for the successor step we apply the
above observation while the limit case is obvious.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
K
be a compact, hereditarily metacompact space and suppose that every nonempty
subspace
A
of
K
contains a nonempty, relatively open subspace
U
of countable weight.
Then
K\in\mathcal{NY}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be property of having countable weight.
Let
X^{(\alpha)}
be
\alpha
-th derivative of space
X
with respect to
property
\mathcal{P}
.
Note first that if
ht(K)=1
then

K=\bigcup\{U\subseteq K:U\mbox{ is open and }w(U)\leq\omega\}.

By compactness,
K
is then a finite union of open sets of countable weight,
and therefore
K
has countable weight itself.
Consequently,
K
can be embedded into
I^{\omega}
, so
K\in\mathcal{NY}
.

Observe that, by our assumption on
K
,
ht(K)
is well-defined so
we can check the assertion by induction on
ht(X)
.
Let
ht(X)=\alpha=\beta+1
and assume that the theorem holds for all compact spaces of smaller height.

The space
K^{(\beta)}
is of height 1; by the introductory remark, there is an embedding

i:K^{(\beta)}\to I^{\omega}
. Using the Tietze-Urysohn theorem,

i
can be extended to a continuous function
f:K\to I^{\omega}
.

For every
x\in K\setminus K^{(\beta)}
there is an open set
U_{x}\subseteq K

such that
\overline{U_{x}}\cap K^{(\beta)}=\emptyset
.
The space
\overline{U_{x}}
is hereditarily metacompact and,
by Lemma
4.4
,

(\overline{U_{x}})^{(\beta)}\subseteq K^{(\beta)}
.
Moreover,
\overline{U_{x}}\cap K^{(\beta)}=\emptyset
, so
(\overline{U_{x}})^{(\beta)}=\emptyset
.
It follows, that
ht(\overline{U_{x}})<\alpha
and, by the inductive assumption,

\overline{U_{x}}
can be embedded in
\sigma(I^{\omega},\Gamma_{x})
, for some
\Gamma_{x}
.

Now the family
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{x}:x\in K\setminus K^{\beta}\}
forms an open cover of

K\setminus K^{(\beta)}
. Hence, by hereditary metacompactness,
there exists a point-finite open cover
\mathcal{V}=\{V_{\xi}:\xi\in\kappa\}
of

K\setminus K^{\beta}
that is inscribed in
\mathcal{U}
.
By virtue of Lemma
4.3
, each set
V_{\xi}
has a point-finite cover
\mathcal{W}_{\xi}

consisting of open
\sigma
-compact sets.
The family
\mathcal{W}=\bigcup\{\mathcal{W_{\xi}}:\xi\in\kappa\}
is then a point-finite cover
of
K\setminus K^{(\beta)}
consisting of open
\sigma
-compact sets and
\mathcal{W}

is inscribed in
\mathcal{V}
.

It follows that for every
W\in\mathcal{W}
there is an embedding

g_{W}:\overline{W}\to\sigma(I^{\omega},\Gamma_{W})\subseteq(I^{\omega})^{\Gamma_{W}},

for some
\Gamma_{W}
.
Again, by the Tietze-Urysohn theorem,
g_{W}
can be extended to a continuous function

G_{W}:K\to(I^{\omega})^{\Gamma_{W}}
.

Note that for any open,
\sigma
-compact set
P\subseteq K
there exists a continuous function

h_{P}:K\xrightarrow{}[0,1]
such that
P=h_{P}^{-1}((0,1])
.
For every
W\in{\mathcal{W}}
we define
H_{W}:K\to I^{\omega}
by

H_{W}=h_{W}\cdot G_{W}
. Consider now the function

T=\Big(\mathop{\BTU}_{W\in\mathcal{W}}H_{W}\Big)\bigtriangleup\Big(\mathop{\BTU}_{W\in\mathcal{W}}h_{W}\Big)\bigtriangleup f:K\to\sigma(I^{\omega},\Gamma).

Each
x\in K
belongs to only finitely many elements of
\mathcal{W}
,
hence
H_{W}(x)=0=h_{W}(x)
except for a finite number of
W
’s.
Consequently,
the range of
T
is a subset of
\sigma(I^{\omega},\Gamma)
.

To complete the proof, it remains to prove that
T
is injective.
Consider any
x,y\in K
,
x\neq y
.

Case (1)

If
x,y\in K^{\beta}
then
T(x)\neq T(y)
since
f(x)\neq f(y)
.

Case (2)

If
x\in K^{(\beta)}
and
y\notin K^{(\beta)}
then there is

W\in\mathcal{W}
such that
y\in W
and
x\notin W
;
then
h_{W}(x)=0\neq h_{W}(y)
.

Case (3)

If
x,y\notin K^{(\beta)}
and
x,y\in W
for some
W\in\mathcal{W}

then either
h_{W}(x)\neq h_{W}(y)
(and so
T(x)\neq T(y)
)
or
h_{W}(x)=h_{W}(y)
but then then
H_{W}(x)\neq H_{W}(y)

because
G_{W}(x)=g_{W}(x)\neq g_{W}(y)=G_{W}(y)
.

Case (4)

The remaining case is

x\in W
and
y\notin W
for some
W\in\mathcal{W}
(or vice versa);
then
h_{W}(X)\neq 0=h_{W}(y)
so again
T(X)\neq T(y)
.

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

For a compact space
K
, the following
conditions are equivalent:

(i)

K
is
\omega
-Corson;

(ii)

K
has a closure preserving cover consisting of finite dimensional metrizable compacta;

(iii)

of

Here we again build on the results from
[

]

Proposition 4.7
.

Every
\omega
-Corson compact space
K
has a closure-preserving cover consisting of metrizable, compact, finitely dimensional sets.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, we can assume that
K
is a compact subspace of

\sigma(I,\Gamma)
, for some
\Gamma
.
For any finite
\Gamma_{0}\subseteq\Gamma
we put

A_{\Gamma_{0}}=\{x\in K:\forall_{\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{0}}\ x(\gamma)=0\},

and consider
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\Gamma_{0}}:\Gamma_{0}\in[\Gamma]^{<\omega)}\}
.
Then the family
\mathcal{A}
covers
K
and consists of finitely dimensional metrizable compacta
so it remains to prove that it is closure-preserving.

Since
K
is Eberlein compact, it is Frechet-Urysohn so, in particular,
the closure in
K
coincides with the sequential closure.
Therefore it suffices to check that
the union of any subfamily of
\mathcal{A}
is sequentially closed.
For that purpose
consider
x_{n}\in A_{\Gamma_{n}}\in\mathcal{A}
and assume that
the sequence of
x_{n}
converges to
x\in K
.

Suppose that
x\notin\bigcup_{n}A_{\Gamma_{n}}
;
then for every
n
there is
\gamma_{n}\notin\Gamma_{n}
such that

x(\gamma_{n})\neq 0
. Since
x\in K
, it has only finitely many nonzero coordinates and therefore
there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
such that
the set
N=\{n:\gamma_{n}=\gamma\}
is infinite.
Since
\gamma_{n}=\gamma\notin\Gamma_{n}
for
n\in N
, we get

0\neq x(\gamma)=\lim_{n\in N}x_{n}(\gamma)=\lim_{n\in N}x_{n}(\gamma_{n})=0,

a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
X
be a separable metrizable topological space and let
\mathcal{A}
be a
closure-preserving cover of the space
X
consisting of closed subsets.
Then there exists a countable subfamily
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
such that
\bigcup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}=X
.

Proof.

Suppose that every countable family
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
is not a cover of
X
.
Then we can construct by induction on
\alpha<\omega_{1}
countable subfamilies
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
such that for

\alpha<\beta<\omega_{1}
we have

\bigcup\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\subsetneq\bigcup\mathcal{A}_{\beta}
.
However, the space
Y=\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}(\bigcup\mathcal{A}_{\alpha})
is
separable and metrizable; therefore it does not allow
strictly ascending uncountable chains of closed sets, a contradiction.
∎

4.6
.

The implication (i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) is given by Proposition

.

To prove (ii)

(iii) note first that

and Lemma

,

, there exists an open, nonempty set

5.

-Corson compacta and

From the characterizations of
\omega
-Corson and NY compact space given in

we can easily derive the following strengthening of

.

Corollary 5.1
.

A NY compact space

is
\omega
-Corson if and only if it is strongly countably dimensional.

Proof.

The “only if” part follows immediately from Proposition
3.3
.

Let
K
be a NY compact space which is a countable union of finite dimensional compacta
A_{n}
.
We will show that
K
satisfies condition (iii) of Theorem
4.6
.
By condition (iv) of Theorem
4.1
,

K
is hereditarily metacompact. Similarly as in the proof of the implication (ii)
\Rightarrow
(iii) of
Theorem
4.1
, we only need to verify the second part of (iii)
for a nonempty closed subset
A
of
K
.

By Theorem
4.1
(iv), there exists a relatively open, nonempty set

V\subseteq A
such that
w(V)\leq\omega
. The set
V
is a Baire space
since it is an open subset of a compact space
A
. It follows, that for some
n
, the intersection

has a nonempty interior in

.
The set

The classes of
\omega
-Corson and NY compacta are clearly closed under finite products;
they are, however, not closed under taking nontrivial countable products.
Namely, from condition (iv) of Theorem
4.1

we can easily deduce the following fact which also allows one to produce
simple examples of Eberlein compacta which are not NY compact.

Corollary 5.2
.

For any sequence
(K_{n})_{n\in\omega}
of nonmetrizable Eberlein compacta, the product

\prod_{n\in\omega}K_{n}
does not belong to
\mathcal{NY}
.

Nakhmanson and Yakovlev noted that one can use condition (iii) of Theorem
4.1

to conclude the following.

Corollary 5.3
.

The class
\mathcal{NY}
is closed under taking continuous images.

Note that the class of
\omega
-Corson compact spaces is clearly not stable under
taking Hausdorff continuous images, as the Hilbert cube is a continuous image of the Cantor set
2^{\omega}
.

In the context of hereditary metacompactness of NY compacta,
the following result of Gruenhage
[
Gr
, Theorem 2.2]
is worth recalling
(here
\Delta=\{(x,x):x\in K\}
is the diagonal).

Theorem 5.4

(Gruenhage)
.

For a compact space
K
, the following
conditions are equivalent:

(i)

K
is Eberlein compact;

(ii)

K^{2}
is hereditarily
\sigma
-metacompact;

(iii)

K^{2}\setminus\Delta
is
\sigma
-metacompact.

We shall now mention two examples showing that one cannot omit any of the two properties
named by Theorem
4.1
(iv) and Theorem
4.6
(iii).

The first example is essentially due to Gruenhage, see his remarks
at the end of section 2 in
[
Gr
]
.
He described it as an inverse limit
X=\varprojlim X_{n}
of spaces
X_{n}
, where

X_{0}=A(\omega_{1})
,
X_{n+1}
is obtained from
X_{n}
,
by replacing each isolated point of
X_{n}
,
by a copy of
A(\omega_{1})
, and the bonding maps are the obvious quotient maps.
It is stated in
[

, without a proof, that the space
X^{2}\setminus\Delta
is metacompact.
We present below a different description of that space and prove that its every finite power is hereditarily
metacompact (Theorem
5.9
shows that we cannot improve this for infinite products).
Recall here that, in general, a square
L^{2}
of a hereditarily metacompact (even hereditarily Lindelöf)
compact space
L
need not to be hereditarily metacompact, which is witnessed by
the
double arrow
space
L
(cf.
[
En1
, 53.B(a)]
).

Recall also that
polyadic
spaces, introduced by Mrówka, are the continuous images of spaces of the form
A(\kappa)^{\lambda}
.

Example 5.5
.

There exists a zero-dimensional uniform Eberlein and polyadic compact space
K

such that
K\notin\mathcal{NY}
but
K^{n}
is hereditarily metacompact for every
n
.

Proof.

Consider the tree
T=\omega_{1}^{\omega}\cup\bigcup_{n\in\omega}\omega_{1}^{n}
with the standard order
\preceq
given by inclusion. For
s\in\omega_{1}^{n},\ n\in\omega
, let
V_{s}=\{t\in T:s\preceq t\}
(a wedge in
T
).

Our example
K
is the tree
T
equipped with the coarse wedge topology, i.e., the topology generated by the sets
V_{s},\ T\setminus V_{s}
for
s\in\omega_{1}^{n},\ n\in\omega
. Since
T
has only one minimal element, and every branch in
T
has a greatest element, the space
K
is compact (cf.
[
Ny2
, Theorem 3.4]
). From the definition of the topology on
T
it immediately follows that
K
is zero-dimensional.

To show that
K
is uniform Eberlein compact and polyadic it is enough to check that it is a continuous image of the product
A(\omega_{1})^{\omega}
. Indeed, one can easily verify that the map
\varphi:A(\omega_{1})^{\omega}\to K
defined by

\varphi((x_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty})=\begin{cases}(x_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}&\mbox{if }x_{i}\in\omega_{1}\mbox{ for all }i\in\omega\\
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{n-1}&\mbox{if }x_{n}=\infty\mbox{ and }x_{i}\in\omega_{1}\mbox{ for all }i<n\,,\end{cases}

for
(x_{i})_{i=0}^{\infty}\in A(\omega_{1})^{\omega}
, is a continuous surjection.

Let

\mathcal{B}=\{V_{s}\setminus\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}V_{t_{i}}:s\in\omega_{1}^{n},\ t_{0},\dots,t_{k}\in\omega_{1}^{n+1},\ n,k\in\omega\}\,.

The family
\mathcal{B}
is a base of
K
having the following properties
(observe that (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) and (3)
\Rightarrow
(4)):

(1)

u\in V_{s}\setminus\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}V_{t_{i}}
if and only if
s\preceq u
and
t_{i}\npreceq u
for
i\leq k
;

(2)

for a fixed
u\in T
, the collection
\mathcal{B}_{u}=\{U\in\mathcal{B}:u\in U\}
is closed under finite unions;

(3)

V_{s}\setminus\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}V_{t_{i}}\subseteq V_{s^{\prime}}\setminus\bigcup_{i=0}^{k^{\prime}}V_{t^{\prime}_{i}}

implies
s^{\prime}\prec s
or
s^{\prime}=s
and
\{t^{\prime}_{0},\dots,t^{\prime}_{k^{\prime}}\}\subset\{t_{0},\dots,t_{k}\}
;

(4)

for a fixed
u\in T
, the collection
\mathcal{B}_{u}
does not contain infinite strictly increasing chains.

Properties (2) and (4) imply that the base
\mathcal{B}
is

point-additively Noetherian
(cf.
[
Ny1
, Definition 1]
).
Therefore, Theorems 1 and 5 from
[
Ny1
]
yield that every finite product
K^{n}
is hereditarily metacompact.

Finally, the space
K
does not belong to
\mathcal{NY}
, since each nonempty open subset of
K
contains a copy of
A(\omega_{1})
, which violates the conclusion of Proposition
4.2
.
∎

Example 5.6
.

For the second example consider any scattered compact space

which is not Eberlein compact.
Then

A
of

.

Another relevant example comes from
[

:

Example 5.7
.

The product
A(\omega_{1})^{\omega}
is Eberlein compact, but not hereditarily metacompact.

As we show below, such a property is shared by all infinite product of nonmetrizable Eberlein compacta,
see Theorem
5.9
.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
K
be a compact, hereditarily metacompact topological space. If

\varphi:K\to L
is a continuous surjection then
L
is hereditarily metacompact as well.

Proof.

For any
B\subseteq L
,
by our assumption, the space
A=\phi^{-1}[B]
is metacompact.
By compactness of
K
, the mapping
\varphi
is closed and so is
the restriction of
\varphi
to
A

(if
F
is closed in
A
then
F=A\cap H
for some closed subset
H
of
K
;
then
\varphi[F]=\varphi[H]\cap B)
).
By Worrell’s theorem, see
[
En1
, Theorem 5.3.7]
,
metacompactness is preserved by closed mappings
so
B
is metacompact.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

If
(K_{n})_{n\in\omega}
is a sequence of nonmetrizable Eberlein compact spaces
then the product
\prod_{n\in\omega}K_{n}
is not hereditarily metacompact.

Proof.

It is well-known that a nonmetrizable Eberlein compactum is not
ccc
, so for each
n
,
there exists a family
\{U_{n}^{\alpha}:\alpha\in\omega_{1}\}
of pairwise disjoint, nonempty, open sets of
K_{n}
. Pick any
x^{\alpha}_{n}\in U_{n}^{\alpha}
. Consider

L_{n}=(K_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha\in\omega_{1}}U_{n}^{\alpha})\cup\{x_{n}^{\alpha}:\alpha\in\omega_{1}\}.

One can easily verify that the quotient space
L_{n}/(K_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}U_{n}^{\alpha})
obtained by identifying all points in
K_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}U_{n}^{\alpha}
is homeomorphic to
A(\omega_{1})
. Pick a homeomorphism
h_{n}
witnessing this. Let
q_{n}:L_{n}\to L_{n}/(K_{n}\setminus\bigcup_{\alpha<\omega_{1}}U_{n}^{\alpha})
be the quotient map, and let
f_{n}=h_{n}\circ q_{n}
. The map

\prod_{n\in\omega}f_{n}:\prod_{n\in\omega}L_{n}\to A(\omega_{1})^{\omega}

is a continuous surjection, and therefore, by Example
5.7
and Lemma
5.8
,
the product
\prod_{n\in\omega}K_{n}
is not hereditarily metacompact.
∎

Recall that the preorder
\leq^{*}
on
\omega^{\omega}
is defined by

f\leq^{*}g
if
f(n)\leq g(n)
for all but finitely many
n\in\omega
. A subset
A
of
\omega^{\omega}
is called
unbounded
if it is unbounded with respect to this preorder.
We refer below to the classical cardinal number:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}=\min\{|A|:A\text{ is an unbounded subset of }\omega^{\omega}\}\,.

It is well known that, for every
n\geq 1
, the statement
\mathfrak{b}=\omega_{n}
is consistent with
ZFC
, (cf.
[
vD
, Theorem 5.1]
).

The next result was proved in
[
Ma
]
for a proper subclass
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
(see Section
6
) of the class of NY compacta. We do not know if it can be proved for all Eberlein compact spaces.

Theorem 5.10
.

Assuming that
\mathfrak{b}>\omega_{1}
, each nonmetrizable compact space
K\in\mathcal{NY}
contains a closed nonmetrizable zero-dimensional subspace
L
.

Proof.

Our argument follows closely the proof of Theorem 4.15 in
[
Ma
]
.

Let
K\in\mathcal{NY}
; we can assume that
K\subseteq\sigma(I^{\omega},\Gamma)

for some set
\Gamma
. Since
K
is nonmetrizable, obviously the set
\Gamma
must be uncountable.
We can also assume that, for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
, there is
x_{\gamma}\in K
such that
x_{\gamma}(\gamma)\neq(0,0,\dots)
. Given
\gamma\in\Gamma
, the set

F_{\gamma}=\{\delta\in\Gamma:x_{\gamma}(\delta)\neq(0,0,\dots)\}

is finite and nonempty. Using the
\Delta
-system lemma we can find a finite set
A\subseteq\Gamma
and a set
S\subseteq\Gamma
of size
\omega_{1}
such that, for any distinct
\alpha,\beta\in S
,
F_{\alpha}\cap F_{\beta}=A
. Now, we can identify the product
(I^{\omega})^{\Gamma}
with the product
I^{\omega\times\Gamma}
, and apply Lemma 4.10 from
[
Ma
]
for the sets
X=\{x_{\gamma}:\gamma\in S\}
and
\Gamma_{0}=\omega\times A
.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

It follows from Proposition
4.2

that no nonmetrizable, compact convex subset
K
of a topological vector space
E
is NY compact because
every nonempty open set
V\subseteq K
contains a copy of the space
K
.
Indeed, for a fixed
x_{0}\in V
, by the continuity of the linear operations in
E
, and the compactness of
K
, the set
\{(1-\varepsilon)x_{0}+\varepsilon y:y\in K\}
- an affine copy of
K
, is contained in
V
, for suitably small
\varepsilon>0
.

In particular, the space
P(K)
of all probability Radon measures on a compact space
K
is NY compact if and only if
K
is metrizable (recall that
P(K)
is Eberlein compact if and only if
K
is Eberlein compact).

6.
Between
\omega
-Corson and
NY
compacta

One of the motivation for this section is to correct two false comments from the first author’s paper
[
Ma
]
.

We say that a compact space
K
belongs to the class
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
if, for some set
\Gamma
there is an embedding
\varphi:K\to\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}
and a countable subset
\Gamma_{0}
of
\Gamma
such that, for each
x\in K
, the set
\supp(\varphi(x))\setminus\Gamma_{0}
is finite (cf.
[
Ma
]
).

Proposition 6.1
.

A compact space
K
belongs to
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
if and only if it can be embedded into the product
I^{\omega}\times L
of the Hilbert cube
I^{\omega}
and some
\omega
-Corson compact space
L
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Each
\omega
-Corson compact space belongs to the class
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
, and each member of
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
is NY compact.

Again, the Hilbert cube witnesses the fact that the first implication in the above proposition cannot be reversed. The same is true for the second implication, see Example
6.4
below and Proposition
3.9
.

In
[
Ma
]
we made, without any justification, the following comment “One can even show that, for metrizable compacta
M_{n}
, the countable product of Aleksandrov duplicates
AD(M_{n})
is in
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
.” Corollary
5.2
shows that this does not hold true. Indeed,
the product
[AD(2^{\omega})]^{\omega}
is an example of a first-countable uniform Eberlein compact space which does not belong to the class
\mathcal{NY}
, hence is not in
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
(cf.
[
Ma
, Example 4.16]
).

The example below shows that another unexplained comment from
[
Ma
]
, claiming the any member of the class
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
, is uniform Eberlein is also false.

Example 6.3
.

Let
K
be a scattered Eberlein compact space which is not uniform Eberlein compact. Then
K
is
\omega
-Corson (hence belongs to
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
).

As we mentioned in the previous section, the class of
\omega
-Corson compact spaces is not stable under taking Hausdorff continuous images. One can prove the class
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
also lacks this property.

Example 6.4
.

The space
\alpha(D(\omega_{1})\times 2^{\omega})
is
\omega
-Corson (hence belongs to
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
), but its continuous image
\alpha(D(\omega_{1})\times I^{\omega})
does not belong to
\mathcal{EC}_{\omega c}
.

Proof.

The fact that
\alpha(D(\omega_{1})\times 2^{\omega})
is
\omega
-Corson is given by Proposition
3.9
.

Suppose that there is an embedding
\varphi:\alpha(D(\omega_{1})\times I^{\omega})\to\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}
and a countable subset
\Gamma_{0}
of
\Gamma
such that, for each
x\in\alpha(D(\omega_{1})\times I^{\omega})
, the set
\supp(\varphi(x))\setminus\Gamma_{0}
is finite. Let

A=\{x\in\alpha(D(\omega_{1})\times I^{\omega}):\varphi(x)|\Gamma_{0}=\varphi(\infty)|\Gamma_{0}\}\,,

where
\infty
denotes the point at infinity of
\alpha(D(\omega_{1})\times I^{\omega})
. Since
\Gamma_{0}
is countable, the set

is a
G_{\delta}
-subset of
\alpha(D(\omega_{1})\times I^{\omega})
containing

, hence it contains a copy of the Hilbert cube

. On the other hand, the projection
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma\setminus\Gamma_{0}}
restricted to
\varphi(A)
is an embedding and its image is contained in the

-product of intervals, which contradicts the fact that

is not strongly countably dimensional.
∎

We conclude the section with an observation related to the wider class of
\kappa
-Valdivia compact spaces
considered by Kalenda
[
Ka2
]
. The following provides a positive

Proposition 6.5
.

For every metrizable compactum

there is an embedding
f:K\to I^{\omega}

such that
f(K)\cap\sigma(I,\omega)
is dense in

.

Proof.

Let
K
be a metrizable compactum. We can assume that
K\subseteq I^{\omega}
.
Let
D
be a countable, dense subset of
K
, and
E
be a countable, dense subset of
I^{\omega}
, contained in
\sigma(I,\omega)
.
Put
F=D\cup E
. The Hilbert cube

is
countable dense
homogeneous
, i.e.,
given countable dense subsets

, there is a homeomorphism
h:I^{\omega}\to I^{\omega}

. Let

∎

7.
Some local properties

We consider here
\kappa
-Corson compacta for
\kappa>\omega
and discuss
their properties related to the usual cardinal invariants of topology.

Given a (topological) space
K
and
x\in K
,
\chi(x,K)
denotes the local character
of the space
K
at
x
(i.e. the minimal cardinality of a local base). Recall that
for every point
x
in a compact space
K
,
\chi(x,K)
is just the minimal size of a family of open
sets which intersection is
\{x\}
.
We write
\pi\chi(x,K)
for the
\pi
-character
of the space
K
at
x
(i.e. the minimal cardinality of a local
\pi
–base).
Similarily,
t(x,K)
denotes
the tightness of
K
at
x
(recall that
t(x,K)\leq\tau
means that whenever
X\subseteq K
and

x\in\overline{X}
, then there is
A\subseteq X
such that
|A|\leq\tau
and
x\in\overline{A}
).
The tightness of a space is
\leq\tau
if

t(x,K)\leq\tau
for every
x\in K
. The strong tightness of
K
is
\leq\tau
if
for every
X\subseteq K
and

x\in\overline{X}
there is
A\subseteq X
such that
|A|<\tau
and
x\in\overline{A}
.

Recall first that the following general fact holds even for singular
\kappa
,
compare
[
BKT
, Proposition 2.19]
.

Lemma 7.1
.

Given an uncountable
\kappa
, every
\kappa
-Corson compact space
K
contains
a point of character
<\kappa
.

Proof.

Assume that
K\subseteq{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})}
and consider the pointwise order
\leq
on elements of
K
.
It is easy to check that there is
x\in K
which is pointwise maximal, that is
y\in K
and

y(\gamma)\geq x(\gamma)
for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
imply
y=x
.
Then
x
is the only point in the intersection of the family of open sets

\{y\in K:y(\gamma)>x(\gamma)-1/n\},\gamma\in\supp(x),n\in\mathbb{N},

so there is a local base at
x\in K
of size
<\kappa
.
∎

We can now answer Question 1.22(ii) in
[
Ka2
]
; the fact given below was also
noted in
[
BKT
, Corollary 2.20]
.

Corollary 7.2
.

The space
[0,1]^{\kappa}
is
\kappa
–Corson for no
\kappa\geq\omega
.

Proof.

For
\kappa=\omega
this follows by Proposition
3.3
.
For
\kappa>\omega

, since

\chi(x,[0,1]^{\kappa})=\kappa

.
∎

To discuss the tightness we first recall
[
Ka2
, Lemma 1.19]
.

Lemma 7.3
.

For every
\kappa>\omega
and any
\Gamma
the space
{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})}
has strong tightness
\kappa
if and only if
\kappa
is regular.

Lemma 7.4
.

If
\kappa>\omega
is regular, then
\pi\chi(x,K)<\kappa
for every
\kappa
-Corson compact space

K
and every
x\in K
.

Proof.

Note first that Lemma
7.1
in fact implies that the points
x\in K
satisfying

\chi(x,K)<\kappa
form a dense subset

of

.
By Lemma

, for every
y\in K

such that

and

For every

pick a local base

at

Then

.
∎

The argument above is not applicable in the case of singular
\kappa
; nonetheless, the
same assertion holds.
The following result answers Question 1.22(1) from
[
Ka2
]
; here we
use an idea due to Tkačenko, see
[

.

Theorem 7.5
.

Let
\kappa
be an uncountable singular
cardinal, and let
K
be
\kappa
–Corson compact.
Then
\pi\chi(x,K)<\kappa
for every
x\in K
.

Consequently,
K
has strong tightness
\leq\kappa
.

Proof.

Consider again a compact space
K\subseteq{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})}
.
Let
\lambda={\rm cf}(\kappa)
, and let
\varphi:\lambda\to\kappa
be an increasing
function with an unbounded range. For any
\xi<\lambda
we put

A_{\xi}=\{x\in K:\;\pi\chi(x,K)\leq|\varphi(\xi)|\mbox{ and }|\supp(x)|\leq|\varphi(\xi)|\}.

Claim 1.

For every
\xi<\lambda
and
x\in K
, if
x\in\overline{A_{\xi}}
, then
\pi\chi(x,K)<\kappa
.

Indeed, let
\tau=\max(|\varphi(\xi)|,|{\rm supp}(x)|)
. By Lemma
7.3
, the space

\Sigma_{\tau^{+}}(\mathbb{R}^{\Gamma})
has tightness
\tau
.
Hence
x\in\overline{A_{\xi}}
lies
in the closure
of
\tau
many points
y
with
\pi\chi(y,K)\leq\tau
. This clearly implies that

\pi\chi(x,K)\leq\tau<\kappa
.

Claim 2.

K=\bigcup_{\xi<\lambda}\overline{A_{\xi}}
.

Suppose otherwise; let
K^{\prime}=\bigcup_{\xi<\lambda}\overline{A_{\xi}}
and
x\in K\setminus K^{\prime}
.
For every
\xi<\lambda
we may find a closed
G_{\delta}
set
Z_{\xi}\subseteq K
such that
x\in Z_{\xi}

and
Z_{\xi}\cap\overline{A_{\xi}}=\emptyset
. Write
Z=\bigcap_{\xi<\lambda}Z_{\xi}
.
Then
Z
is nonempty and, by Lemma
7.1
, there is
z\in Z
such that
\chi(z,Z)<\kappa
.
Choose
\xi
such that

\varphi(\xi)\geq\max(\chi(z,Z),|\supp(z)|,\lambda).

Note that

\pi\chi(z,K)\leq\chi(z,K)\leq\chi(z,Z)\cdot\lambda,

where the latter inequality follows from the fact that
Z
is an intersection of
\lambda
many open sets.
But then
z\in A_{\xi}
,
a contradiction with
z\notin K^{\prime}
.

It follows immediately from Claim 1 and Claim 2 that
\pi\chi(x,K)<\kappa
for every
x\in K
.
This clearly implies the final statement, for
if
L=\overline{X}\subseteq K
and
x\in L
, then
L
is
\kappa
–Corson, so

\pi\chi(x,L)<\kappa
, which implies that there is
Y\subseteq X
with
|Y|<\kappa
and
x\in\overline{Y}
.
∎

As we shall see later, it seems to be unclear if the class of

.

Corollary 7.6
.

For every uncountable

\pi\chi(y,L)<\kappa

Let

K

\kappa

It is well-known that there is closed

such that

and

for every nonempty open set

. Then

is again

By Lemma

and Theorem

,

.
It follows easily from irreducibility that

The following simple example comes from
[

Example 7.7

Fix an uncountable
\kappa

\leq\kappa

On the other hand, if
\kappa

with

Now the family
\mathcal{U}
of clopen sets

, where

and

separates points of
[0,\kappa]
and
{\rm ord}(x,\mathcal{U})<\kappa

so
[0,\kappa]
is
\kappa
-Corson by
Proposition
2.2
.

We enclose here some remarks on converging sequences.

Lemma 7.8
.

If a
\kappa
-Corson compact space
K
contains a point
x_{0}
such that

\chi(x_{0},K)=\kappa
, then
K
contains a nontrivial converging sequence.

Proof.

Consider a compact space
K\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma}).

Write
S_{0}=\supp(x_{0})
;
as
\chi(x_{0},K)=\kappa
, there is

x_{1}\in K
such that
x_{1}|S_{0}=x_{0}|S_{0}
, but
x_{1}\neq x_{0}
. Then we consider the
set
S_{1}=S_{0}\cup\supp(x_{1})
and repeat the argument.

In this fashion, we can define a sequence of points
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
K
,
and
S_{0}\subseteq S_{1}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq\Gamma
such that for every
n\geq 1

–

x_{n}\neq x_{0}
,

–

x_{n}|S_{n}\equiv x_{0}|S_{n}
, and

–

\supp(x_{n})\subseteq S_{n}
.

Then
x_{n}
converge to
x_{0}
: if
\gamma\in\bigcup_{n}S_{n}
, then
x_{n}(\gamma)=x_{0}(\gamma)

eventually holds; otherwise
x_{n}(\gamma)=0=x_{0}(\gamma)
.
∎

Note, however, that if the character of every point in a
\kappa
-Corson space is
<\kappa
, then

K
may contain
no converging sequence.
Such a phenomenon occurs for
\kappa=\mathfrak{c}^{+}
when we let
K
be
\beta\omega
(see Remark
2.1
(c)).

8.
Good sets

The concept of a good set of coordinates was widely used in analysing
the properties of classical Corson compacta, see
[
Ne
]
,
[
AMN
]
and
[
Ka2
]
.
The same concept can be applied to study
\kappa
-Corson compacta
for a regular uncountable
\kappa
.

Consider any fixed
K\subseteq[0,1]^{\Gamma}
; a set
A\subseteq K
is determined by coordinates
in
H\subseteq\Gamma
if for every
x\in A
and
y\in K
,
x|H=y|H
implies
y\in A
; we write

A\sim H
in such a case.
In the sequel, by a basic open set in
K
we mean a set of the form

\{y\in K:|y(\gamma)-\phi(\gamma)|<1/n\mbox{ for all }\gamma\in I\},

where
I\subseteq\Gamma
is finite and
\phi:I\to\mathbb{Q}
.
The following fact is standard.

Lemma 8.1
.

If
K
is a compact subset of
[0,1]^{\Gamma}
, then
every clopen subset of
K
is determined by finitely many coordinates.

If
K\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})
and
H\subseteq\Gamma
, then we write

\pi_{H}:K\to[0,1]^{\Gamma},

for the corresponding ‘projection’; precisely
\pi_{H}(x)(\gamma)=x(\gamma)
if
\gamma\in H
and

\pi_{H}(x)(\gamma)=0
otherwise.

Definition 8.1
.

Given
K\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})
,
a set
H\subseteq\Gamma
is said to be
good for

K
if
\pi_{H}[K]\subseteq K
.

The following observation follows easily by compactness.

Lemma 8.2
.

Given a compact space
K\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})
, the union of any chain of subsets of
\Gamma

which are good for
K
is also good.

The proof of the next lemma closely follows Benyamini’s argument for

\kappa=\omega_{1}
, see

[
Ne
, 6.24]
.

Lemma 8.3
.

Suppose that
\kappa
is a regular uncountable cardinal and let
\lambda\geq\kappa
.
Given a compact space
K\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\lambda})
,
for every

H\subseteq\lambda
with
|H|<\lambda
there is
G(H)\supseteq H
such that

|G(H)|<\lambda
and
G(H)
is good for
K
.

Proof.

Let
H_{0}=H
; we construct and increasing sequence
H_{n}\subseteq\lambda
as follows.
Given
H_{n}
, for every nonempty basic open set
U
in
K
depending on coordinates in
H_{n}

we pick
x_{U}\in U
and set

H_{n+1}=H_{n}\cup\bigcup_{U\sim H_{n}}\supp(x_{U}).

Then we put
G(H)=\bigcup_{n}H_{n}
.

If
\lambda=\kappa
, then
|H_{n}|<\kappa
and
|G(H)|<\kappa
by regularity of
\kappa
.
If
\lambda>\kappa
, then writing
\tau=\max(\kappa,|H|)
, we have

|H_{n}|\leq\tau
and so
|G(H)|\leq\tau<\lambda
.

Take any
x\in K
and consider
z=\pi_{G(H)}(x)
. If
V
is a basic neighborhood of

z\in{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\lambda})}
and
V\sim J\subseteq\lambda
, then for
I=J\cap G(H)
we have
I\subseteq H_{n}
for some
n

and we can write
V=U_{1}\cap U_{2}
, where
U_{1}\sim I
,
U_{2}\sim J\setminus I
.
Hence there is
x\in K
with
\supp(x)\subseteq H_{n+1}\subseteq G(H)
such that

x\in U_{1}\cap U_{2}=V
. This shows that
z\in\overline{K}=K
, and we are done.
∎

Lemma 8.4
.

Suppose that
\kappa
is a regular uncountable cardinal and let
\lambda\geq\kappa
.
Given a compact space
K\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\lambda})
,
and a continuous surjection
f:K\to L
,
for every

H\subseteq\lambda
with
|H|<\lambda
there is
G^{f}(H)\supseteq H
such that

(i)

|G^{f}(H)|<\lambda
and
G^{f}(H)
is good for
K
.

(ii)

for every
x,y\in K
, if
f(x)=f(y)
, then
f(\pi_{G^{f}(H)}(x))=f(\pi_{G^{f}(H)}(y))
.

Proof.

Firstly, consider a good set
G\subseteq\lambda

and let
K_{0}=\pi_{G}[K]\subseteq K
.
Suppose that
U_{1},U_{2}
are basic open sets in
K_{0}
depending on
G

and
f[\overline{U_{1}}]\cap f[\overline{U_{2}}]=\emptyset
.

Claim.

There is a finite set
I=I(U_{1},U_{2})\subseteq\lambda

and open sets
V_{1},V_{2}
in
K
determined by
I
such that

V_{i}\supseteq\overline{U_{i}}\mbox{ for }i=1,2\mbox{ and }f[V_{1}]\cap f[V_{2}]=\emptyset.

Indeed, take disjoint open sets
W_{i}
in
L,\ i=1,2
, separating
f[\overline{U_{1}}]
and
f[\overline{U_{2}}]
, and find sets
V_{i}
which are finite unions of basic open sets determined by a finite set
I\subseteq\lambda
, and satisfy

\overline{U_{i}}\subseteq V_{i}\subset f^{-1}(W_{i})
for
i=1,2
.

Using Claim we define
G_{0}\subseteq G_{1}\subseteq\ldots\lambda
as follows:
Start with
G_{0}=G(H)
; given
G_{n}
we let
H_{n}
be the union of
G_{n}

and all the finite sets
I(U_{1},U_{2})
where
U_{1},U_{2}
are as in Claim.
Then we put
G_{n+1}=G(H_{n})
.

Finally,
G^{f}(H)=\bigcup_{n}G_{n}
is as required. Indeed, such a set is good for
K

by Lemma
8.2
. Suppose
x,y\in K
are such that
f(x^{\prime})\neq f(y^{\prime})
, where

x^{\prime}=\pi_{G^{f}(H)}(x)
,
y^{\prime}=\pi_{G^{f}(H)}(y)
. Then there are basic neighborhoods

U_{1},U_{2}
of
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\pi_{G^{f}(H)}[K]
such that
f[\overline{U_{1}}]\cap f[\overline{U_{2}}]=\emptyset
.
Then
U_{1},U_{2}
are determined by a finite set
J\subseteq G^{f}[H]
;
we have
J\subseteq G_{n}
for some
n
and our construction at this step added coordinates showing that

f(x)\neq f(y)
.
∎

For the sake of section
10
we need to rephrase Lemma
8.3
as follows
(here the proof is very similar).

Lemma 8.5
.

Suppose that
\kappa
is a regular cardinal.
Given a compact space
K\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\kappa})
,
for every

H\subseteq\kappa
of size
<\kappa
there is
\xi<\kappa
such that

H
is contained in the interval
\{\beta:\beta<\xi\}
which is good for
K
.

In section
10
we also use the following easy observation.

Lemma 8.6
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma}
) and let
H\subseteq\Gamma
be a good set for
K
;
write
K^{\prime}=\pi_{H}[K]\subseteq K
. If
U
is a clopen subset of
K
, then
U\cap K^{\prime}=C\cap K^{\prime}
for some clopen set
C
determined by a finite subset of
H
.

9.
Continuous images

We have already seen that the class
\mathcal{NY}
is stable under continuous images while

\omega
-Corson compacta do not have such a property, see Corollary
5.3
and
the remark after it. Then the following theorem resolves the case
\kappa=\omega_{1}
.

Theorem 9.1
.

The class of Corson compact spaces is stable under continuous images.

The result was first proved by Gul’ko
[
Gu
]
and
by Michael & Rudin
[
MR
]
;
the proof in
[
MR
]
is a modification of the authors’ new argument for stability of the class
of Eberlein compacta under continuous images. However, according to
Kalenda
[
Ka2
, page 2]
that modification is not quite straightforward.
Quite different proofs of
9.1
were given by Gruenhage
[
Gr
]
(using
covering properties and topological games) and by Pol
[
Po
]

(using a characterization of Corson compacta
K
via properties of

C_{p}(K)
; see
[
Ka1
]
or
[
Ka2
]
).

Bell and Marciszewski
[
BM
, Theorem 7.1]
extended Pol’s approach
and proved that if
K
is
\tau^{+}
-Corson, then so is every
continuous image of
K
.

The following is Corollary 2.17 in
[
BKT
]
.

Theorem 9.2
.

For every regular cardinal number
\kappa
,
the class of
\kappa
-Corson compacta is stable under continuous images.

That result was obtained via model-theoretic approach. We give below a purely
topological argument for
9.2
— it closely follows
basic ideas from Gul’ko
[
Gu
]
(mentioned also in
[
Ne
, 6.26]
).
Namely, we shall prove by induction on
\lambda
the following.

Theorem 9.3
.

Given a regular cardinal number
\kappa
,
for every
\lambda
, if
K\subseteq{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\lambda})}
is compact and
L
is a continuous image of
K
,
then
L
is
\kappa
-Corson compact.

Proof.

Fix a compact space
K\subseteq{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\lambda})}
and a continuous surjection
f:K\to L
.

By Lemma
8.4
(and Lemma
8.2
), there is a family

\{G_{\xi}:\xi<\lambda_{0}\}
(where
\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda
,
note that
\lambda
may be singular)
of nonempty and strictly increasing subsets of
\lambda
such that
\lambda=\bigcup_{\xi<\lambda}G_{\xi}
and for every
\xi<\lambda_{0}

(i)

|G_{\xi}|<\lambda
;

(ii)

G_{\xi}
is good for
K
;

(iii)

for every
x,y\in K
, if
f(x)=f(y)
, then
f(\pi_{G_{\xi}}(x))=f(\pi_{G_{\xi}}(y))
;

(iv)

G_{\xi}=\bigcup_{\eta<\xi}G_{\eta}
whenever
\xi
is a limit ordinal.

Write
p_{\xi}=\pi_{G_{\xi}}
,
K_{\xi}=p_{\xi}[K]
and
p^{\eta}_{\xi}
for
p_{\xi}
restricted to

K_{\eta}
whenever
\xi<\eta
.
Note that every
p_{\xi}
is a retraction and
p_{\xi}\circ p_{\eta}=p_{\xi}
for
\xi<\eta
, so
we obtain the following inverse system

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{\xi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{\eta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\xi}_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\eta}_{\xi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\eta}}

Using
(ii)
above we will get the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{\xi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{\eta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\xi}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{\eta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\xi}_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\xi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p^{\eta}_{\xi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\eta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p_{\eta}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{\xi}_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q^{\eta}_{\xi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces q_{\eta}}

Here
f_{\xi}
is the restriction of
f
to
K_{\xi}
,
L_{\xi}=f[K_{\xi}]
and
the mapping
q_{\xi}
is defined by

q_{\xi}(f(x))=f(p_{\xi}(x));

note that property
(iii)
guarantees that the definition is correct.
Then every
q_{\xi}
is a retraction
L\to L_{\xi}
and
again, for
\xi<\eta
the bonding map
q^{\eta}_{\xi}
is
q_{\xi}
restricted to
L_{\eta}
.

For every
\xi<\lambda_{0}
we have
w(K_{\xi})\leq|G_{\xi}|<\lambda
, so
L_{\xi}
is
\kappa
-Corson
by the inductive assumption. Hence there is a family of functions
\Phi_{\xi}\subseteq C(L_{\xi})

which separates the points of
L_{\xi}
and satisfies

|\{f\in\Phi_{\xi}:f(x)\neq 0\}|<\kappa
for every
x\in L_{\xi}
(see Remark
2.1
).
Consider

\Phi=\{g\circ q_{0}:g\in\Phi_{0}\}\cup\{g\circ q_{\xi+1}-g\circ q_{\xi}:\xi<\lambda_{0},g\in\Phi_{\xi+1}\}\subseteq C(L);

we shall check that
\Phi
witnesses that
L
is
\kappa
-Corson compact too.

Note first that
x=\lim_{\xi<\lambda}p_{\xi}(x)
, and therefore

f(x)=\lim_{\xi<\lambda}f(p_{\xi}(x))=\lim_{\xi<\lambda}q_{\xi}(f(x))

for every
x\in K
.

Take
x,y\in K
such that
f(x)\neq f(y)
. If the points
f(x),f(y)
are in
L_{0}
, then some
g\in\Phi_{0}

separates them. Otherwise, the first
\eta
such that
q_{\eta}(f(x))\neq q_{\eta}(f(y))
must be
a successor ordinal; if
\eta=\xi+1
, then

f(x),f(y)
are separated by
g\circ q_{\xi+1}-g\circ q_{\xi}
for
g\in\Phi_{\xi+1}
separating
q_{\xi+1}(f(x)),q_{\xi+1}(f(y))
.

Given any
x\in K
, if

g\circ q_{\xi+1}(f(x))-g\circ q_{\xi}(f(x))\neq 0

for
g\in\Phi_{\xi+1}
, then

f(p_{\xi+1}(x))=q_{\xi+1}(f(x))\neq q_{\xi}(f(x))=f(p_{\xi}(x)),

so
p_{\xi+1}(x)\neq p_{\xi}(x)
.
We conclude that
\supp(x)\cap(G_{\xi+1}\setminus G_{\xi})\neq\emptyset
, which may happen

<\kappa
many times.
Hence the cardinality of
\varphi\in\Phi
with
\varphi(f(x))\neq 0
is

, and the proof is complete.
∎

Note that Theorem
9.2
says, in particular, that for regular
\kappa
,
every subalgebra of a
\kappa
-Corson algebra is again
\kappa
-Corson.
We do not know if the class of
\kappa
-Corson compacta is stable under continuous images also
for a singular
\kappa
. In particular, one can ask the following.

Problem 9.4
.

Is there a singular number
\kappa
and a subalgebra of a
\kappa
-Corson algebra which
is not
\kappa
-Corson?

10.
Basically disconnected spaces and beyond

We discuss here
[
BKT
, Question 5.2]
, a problem which can be stated as follows.

Problem 10.1
.

Can a
\sigma
-complete Boolean algebra
\mathfrak{B}
of size
\kappa
be

\kappa
-Corson?

Given an algebra
\mathfrak{B}
, the statement ‘
\mathfrak{B}
is
\sigma
-complete’ can be weakened
in various directions, see Koszmider and Shelah
[
KS
]
and the references therein.
In particular, the following properties were considered in
[
KS
]
.

Definition 10.1
.

A Boolean algebra
\mathfrak{B}
is said to have the subsequential
completeness property (SCP) if for
any sequence of pairwise disjoint
a_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}
there is infinite

N\subseteq\omega
such that the family
\{a_{n}:n\in N\}
has a least upper bound in
\mathfrak{B}
.

A Boolean algebra
\mathfrak{B}
is said to have the weak subsequential
separation property (WSSP) if for any sequence of pairwise disjoint
a_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}

there is
b\in\mathfrak{B}
such that
both of the sets

\{n\in\mathbb{N}:a_{n}\subseteq b\}
and
\{n\in\mathbb{N}:a_{n}\cap b=0\}

are infinite.

SCP was introduced by Haydon
[
Ha
]
in connection with the Grothendieck property of Banach spaces.
Clearly, we have the following implications (none of which is reversible, see
[
KS
]
)

\sigma
-complete
\longrightarrow
SCP
\longrightarrow
WSSP.

Recall that a compact space
K
is basically disconnected if the closure of
every open
F_{\sigma}
subset of
K
is open.
In particular, the Stone space of a
\sigma
-complete Boolean algebra
is basically disconnected. In turn, such a property of a compact space
K
can be weakened to saying
that
K
is an
F
-space, i.e. every two disjoint open
F_{\sigma}
subsets of
K
have disjoint closures.
Recall that there are connected
F
-spaces, e.g.
\beta([0,\infty))\setminus[0,\infty)
, see
[
GJ
, Theorem 14.27]
.

Concerning Problem
10.1
,
we note first a couple of easy answers.

Remark 10.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{B}
be a Boolean algebra with
|\mathfrak{B}|=\kappa
.

(a)

If
\mathfrak{B}
contains an independent family of size
\kappa
, then

{\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B})
maps continuously onto
2^{\kappa}
, so
{\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B})
is not
\kappa
-Corson
compact by Theorem
9.2
and Lemma
7.1

(for regular
\kappa
; if
\kappa
is singular, then use Lemma
7.6
).

(b)

In particular, if
\mathfrak{B}
is complete, then it is not
\kappa
-Corson, as
such
\mathfrak{B}
contains an independent family of full size by the Balcar-Franek theorem.
In the language of topology, no extremally disconnected compact space
of weight
\kappa
is
\kappa
-Corson compact.

(c)

The answer to Problem
10.1
is ‘no’ for
\kappa=\mathfrak{c}
.
Indeed, taking pairwise disjoint
a_{n}\in\mathfrak{B}^{+}
in a
\sigma
-complete algebra
\mathfrak{B}
and
an independent family
\mathcal{I}\subseteq P(\omega)
of cardinality
\mathfrak{c}
we get an independent family
\{a_{I}:I\in\mathcal{I}\}
,
where

a_{I}=\bigvee_{n\in I}a_{n},

and we argue as above.

(d)

More generally, if
\mathfrak{B}
has WSSP and
\kappa=\mathfrak{c}
, then
\mathfrak{B}
is not
\mathfrak{c}
-Corson.
This is a consequence of
[
KS
, Theorem 1.4]
, stating that such
\mathfrak{B}
must contain an
independent family of size
\mathfrak{c}
.

(e)

It is routine to check that
if
\mathfrak{B}
has WSSP (in particular, if
\mathfrak{B}
has SCP), then
K={\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B})

contains no nontrivial converging sequence.
This is a very particular version of
[
KS
, Proposition 2.4]

(related to the Grothendieck property of the underlying space of continuous functions).
The lack of converging sequences and Lemma
7.8
might give a short proof of
10.3
below
if we knew that the space
K
in question would contain a point of character
\kappa
.

The following provides a negative answer to Problem
10.1

in case
\kappa
in question is regular.

Theorem 10.3
.

If
\mathfrak{B}
is a Boolean algebra of regular size
\kappa
and
\mathfrak{B}
has SCP, then

\mathfrak{B}
is not
\kappa
-Corson.

Proof.

Suppose otherwise; we consider the Stone space
K={\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B})
which is then
a zero-dimensional compact space of weight
\kappa
and is
\kappa
-Corson compact
so, by Lemma
2.3
, we can assume that

K\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}(\{0,1\}^{\kappa})=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{\kappa}:|\supp(x)|<\kappa\}.

For a set
a\subseteq\kappa
we write
C(a)=\{x\in K:x|a\equiv 1\}
;
if
\xi<\kappa
, then
C(\{\xi\})
is simply denoted by
C(\xi)
.
Note that we can assume that
C(\xi)\neq\emptyset
for every
\xi<\kappa
.

Using Lemma
8.5
we define an strictly increasing function
g:\kappa\to\kappa
such that, for
every
\alpha<\kappa
, the initial segment
\{\xi:\xi<g(\alpha)\}
is good for
K
.
As an increasing union of good
sets is also good (Lemma

), we can assume that
g
is continuous, i.e.
g(\alpha)=\sup_{\beta<\alpha}g(\beta)

whenever
\alpha
is a limit ordinal. For simplicity, we shall write

\pi_{\alpha}=\pi_{\{\xi:\xi<g(\alpha)\}}\mbox{ and }K_{\alpha}=\pi_{\alpha}[K]\subseteq K.

Consider now a family
\mathcal{F}
of sets
a\subseteq\kappa
such that

(i)

a\subseteq g[\kappa]
,

(ii)

C(a)\neq\emptyset
, and

(iii)

C(a\cup\{\xi\})=\emptyset
for every
\xi>\max a
.

Claim 1.

Every
a\in\mathcal{F}
is finite.

To verify Claim 1, suppose that
a\in\mathcal{F}
contains a strictly increasing sequence of
\xi_{n}=g(\alpha_{n})
;
set
\xi=g(\alpha)
, where
\alpha=\sup_{n}\alpha_{n}
.
Then there is
x\in K
such that
x(\xi_{n})=1
for every
n
.
Now
\pi_{\alpha_{n}}(x)
is a sequence of distinct points in
K
, and

\lim_{n}\pi_{\alpha_{n}}(x)=\pi_{\alpha}(x)
, which contradicts the fact that
K
contains no converging
sequences, see Remark
10.2
(e).

Next we consider a set
S\subseteq\kappa
consisting of limit ordinals
\alpha<\kappa

having the following property:

\alpha=\sup_{n}\alpha_{n}
for some
\alpha_{n}<\alpha
and there are

a_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
such that
a_{n}\subseteq g(\alpha_{n+1})\setminus g(\alpha_{n})
for every
n
.
The following can be checked by a standard argument.

Claim 2.

The set
S
is stationary.

Note that, as
\mathfrak{B}
has SCP,
for every sequence of pairwise disjoint clopen sets
V_{n}\subseteq K
there
is infinite
N\subseteq\omega
such that

\overline{\bigcup_{n\in N}V_{n}}

is a clopen subset of
K
.

For
\alpha\in S
we pick a sequence of
a_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
witnessing that
\alpha
belongs to
S
;
passing to a subsequence if necessary and applying the above remark, we
can assume that

U_{\alpha}=\overline{\bigcup_{n\in N}C(a_{n})}

is a clopen subset of
K
.

Applying Lemma
8.6
we get the following.

Claim 3.

There is a function
f:S\to\kappa
such that, for every
\alpha\in S
,
f(\alpha)<\alpha

and
U_{\alpha}\cap K_{\alpha}=C_{\alpha}\cap K_{\alpha}
, where
C_{\alpha}
is a clopen set
determined by coordinates below
g(f(\alpha))
.

By the pressing down lemma,
f(\alpha)=\eta<\kappa
for
\alpha
from some stationary set
S_{0}\subseteq S
.
It follows that for
\alpha\in S_{0}
,
C_{\alpha}
is a clopen set determined by a finite number of
coordinates below
g(\eta)<\kappa
.
It follows that
C_{\alpha}=C
for a fixed set
C
and cofinally many
\alpha
’s.

We shall arrive at a contradiction considering
g(\eta)<\alpha<\beta
in
S
such that

(*)\quad U_{\alpha}\cap K_{\alpha}=C\cap K_{\alpha}\mbox{ and }U_{\beta}\cap K_{\beta}=C\cap K_{\beta}.

Indeed, take two sequences of finite sets
a_{n},b_{n}\in{\mathcal{F}}
witnessing that

\alpha,\beta
are in
S
, respectively.
Note that
J=\bigcup_{n}b_{n}\cap g(\alpha)
is finite, so there is
k
such that

a_{k}\subseteq g(\alpha)\setminus\max J
. Then
C(a_{k})
is disjoint from every
C(b_{n})
, and hence

C(a_{k})\cap U_{\beta}=\emptyset
. Moreover, we have
C(a_{k})\subseteq K_{\alpha}\subseteq K_{\beta}
, so
the second equation of (*) implies
C(a_{k})\cap C=\emptyset
.

On the other hand, we have
C(a_{k})\subseteq U_{\alpha}
, so
C(a_{k})\subseteq C
by the first equation of (*), and
this is in contradiction with
C(a_{k})\neq\emptyset
.
This shows that (*) cannot hold, and the proof is complete.
∎

.

Does the assertion of Theorem

remains true if we replace

11.
Function spaces
C_{p}(K)
on
\kappa
-Corson compacta
K

We briefly discuss in this section generalizations of a
result due to Pol
[
Po
, §3, Thm. 1.1]
(see
also
[
Gu
]
and
[
AP
]
). Pol’s theorem
characterizes Corson compacta

K
in terms of topological properties of function spaces
C_{p}(K)
. We extend this characterization for
\kappa
-Corson compacta, where
\kappa
is regular, and use it to give an alternative proof of Theorem
9.2
.

Given uncountable cardinal numbers
\lambda
and
\kappa
, by
L_{\kappa}(\lambda)

we denote the set
\lambda\cup\{\infty\}
topologized as follows:
all points
\alpha\in\lambda
are isolated in
L_{\kappa}(\lambda)
, and basic neighborhoods
of
\infty
have the form
\{\infty\}\cup(\lambda\setminus A)
where
A\subseteq\lambda
and
|A|<\kappa
.

By
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
we denote the class of all spaces which are continuous images of closed subsets of the countable product
L_{\kappa}(\lambda)^{\omega}
for some cardinal
\lambda
.
Pol’s result states that
K
is Corson compact if and only if
C_{p}(K)\in\mathcal{L}_{\omega_{1}}
.
In
[
BM
]
this result was generalized for
\kappa^{+}
-Corson compact spaces.

The next proposition is a generalization of Proposition 4.1 from
[
BM
]
and can be justified in exactly the same way.

Proposition 11.1
.

For every uncountable cardinal number
\kappa
, the class

\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
is closed under taking
continuous images, closed subspaces, countable products and countable unions.

Given an infinite cardinal number
\kappa
and a topological space
X
, we say that
X
is

\kappa
-Lindelöf if every open cover of
X
contains a subcover of cardinality less than
\kappa
. In this terminology
\omega_{1}
-Lindelöf spaces are usual Lindelöf spaces.

Corollary 4.2 from Noble
[
No
]
implies that, for every uncountable cardinal
\lambda
, the space

L_{\kappa}(\lambda)^{\omega}
is
\kappa
-Lindelöf, for every uncountable regular cardinal number
\kappa
, and it is
\kappa^{+}
-Lindelöf, for every singular
\kappa
. Hence we obtain

Proposition 11.2
.

Let
\kappa
be an uncountable cardinal number and
X
be an element of
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
. Then

(A)

if
\kappa
is regular, then
X
is
\kappa
-Lindelöf;

(B)

if
\kappa
is singular, then
X
is
\kappa^{+}
-Lindelöf.

The next theorem can be proved using the same arguments as in the proof of one implication in Theorem 6.1 from
[
BM
]
(cf.
[
Za
]
)

Theorem 11.3
.

Let
\kappa
be an uncountable cardinal and
K
be a
\kappa
-Corson compact space. Then the function space
C_{p}(K)
belongs to
\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
.

Corollary 11.4
.

Let
\kappa
be an uncountable cardinal and
K
be a
\kappa
-Corson compact space. Then

(A)

if
\kappa
is regular, then
C_{p}(K)
is
\kappa
-Lindelöf;

(B)

if
\kappa
is singular, then
C_{p}(K)
is
\kappa^{+}
-Lindelöf.

The following fact belongs to the folklore

Proposition 11.5
.

Let
\kappa
be an infinite cardinal and
[0,\kappa]
be the ordinal space equipped with the order topology. Then the function space
C_{p}([0,\kappa])
contains a closed discrete subspace of cardinality
\kappa
.

Proof.

One can easily verify that the subspace

\displaystyle\{f\in C_{p}([0,\kappa])\cap 2^{[0,\kappa]}:f(\kappa)=0\mbox{ and $f$ is nonincreasing}\}=\{\chi_{[0,\alpha]}:\alpha<\kappa\}\cup\{\chi_{\emptyset}\}

has the required properties.
∎

Since the space
[0,\kappa]
is
\kappa
-Corson for singular
\kappa
, and it is
\kappa^{+}
-Corson for regular
\kappa
, the above proposition shows that the results of Corollary
11.4
cannot be improved. In particular, this gives a negative answer to a part of Question 5.5 from
[
BKT
]
.

The next theorem generalizes Theorem 6.1 from
[
BM
]
. One implication in this result is given by Theorem
11.3
. To prove the reverse implication one needs to modify the arguments from the proof of
[
BM
, Theorem 6.1]
(which, in turn, follow the reasoning from
[
Po
]
). The details of the proof will be presented in
[
Za
]
.

Theorem 11.6
.

Let
\kappa
be an uncountable regular cardinal, and
K
be a compact space. Then

K
is a
\kappa
-Corson compact space if and only if
C_{p}(K)\in\mathcal{L}_{\kappa}
.

The next fact is well known

Proposition 11.7
.

If
L
is a continuous image of a compact space
K
, then the function space
C_{p}(L)
embeds as a closed subspace in
C_{p}(K)
.

Using Theorem
11.6
and Propositions
11.1
and
11.7
we can immediately obtain an alternative proof of Theorem
9.2
.

Problem 11.8
.

Does Theorem
11.6
hold true for any uncountable cardinal number
\kappa
?

12.
Measures on
\kappa
-Corson compacta

In this section, we discuss the stability of the classes of
\kappa
-Corson compacta under the functor

P
which assigns to a compact space
K
the compact space
P(K)

of regular probability Borel measures equipped with the
weak^{\ast}
topology.
Let us note that neither the class of
\omega
-Corson compacta nor
\mathcal{NY}
is stable
in this sense, see Remark
5.11
.

Remark 12.1
.

Recall that the space
P(K)
shares many topological properties with

M_{1}(K)
, the space of all signed measures from the unit ball in
C(K)^{\ast}
.
In particular,
M_{1}(K)
is a continuous image of

T\times P(K)\times P(K)
, where
T=\{(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|t|+|s|\leq 1\}
.

If
P(K)
is Corson compact, then so is
K
since it is homeomorphic to

\Delta_{K}=\{\delta_{x}:x\in K\}
. The reverse implication is not provable in ZFC.

Theorem 12.2
.

The following are equivalent

(i)

P(K)
is Corson compact for every Corson compact space
K
;

(ii)

M_{1}(K)
is Corson compact for every Corson compact space
K
;

(iii)

{\rm cov}(2^{\omega_{1}})>\omega_{1}
.

Here
{\rm cov}(2^{\omega_{1}})>\omega_{1}
means that the Cantor cube
2^{\omega_{1}}

cannot be covered by

\omega_{1}
sets that are null with respect to the usual product measure.
Such a statement follows from Martin’s axiom MA
(\omega_{1})
and is simply denied by CH.

The equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) is clear by
12.1
while (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(iii)
is a combination of
results from
[
AMN
]
and
[
KvM
]
.
Recall that under CH there are Corson compacta
K
for which
P(K)
is very far
from being Corson compact — it can be derived from a result due to Talagrand
[
Ta
]

that under CH there is a Corson compact space
K
such that

P(K)
contains a copy of
\beta\omega
.

We show below that Theorem
12.2
can
be naturally extended to
\kappa
-Corson compacta using
the following concept.

Definition 12.3
.

A regular cardinal
\kappa
is a caliber of Radon measures if, for every
compact space
K
, a measure
\mu\in P(K)
, and a family
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\}
in

Bor(K)
of
\mu
-positive sets, there is
x\in K
such that

\{\alpha<\kappa:x\in A_{\alpha}\}
has cardinality
\kappa
.

Basic properties of calibers of measures are discussed in
[
DPl
]
.
We recall here the most relevant facts.

Theorem 12.4
.

(a)

A regular cardinal
\kappa
is a caliber of Radon measures if and only if
it is a caliber of the usual product measure on
2^{\kappa}
.

(b)

The relation
{\rm cov}(2^{\omega_{1}})>\omega_{1}
is equivalent to saying that

\omega_{1}
is a caliber of Radon measures.

(c)

\mathfrak{c}^{+}
is a caliber of Radon measures.

Theorem 12.5
.

For a regular uncountable cardinal number
\kappa
the following are equivalent

(i)

P(K)
is
\kappa
-Corson compact for every
\kappa
-Corson compact space
K
;

(ii)

M_{1}(K)
is
\kappa
-Corson compact for every
\kappa
-Corson compact space
K
;

(iii)

For every
K\subseteq{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})}
and
\mu\in P(K)
there is
S\subseteq\Gamma
such that
|S|<\kappa
and

\mu\left(\{x\in K;\supp(x)\subseteq S\}\right)=1;

(iv)

\kappa
is a caliber of Radon measures.

Proof.

The equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) follows from
P(K)\subseteq M_{1}(K)
, Theorem
9.2

and Remark
12.1
.

For (i)
\Rightarrow
(iii) consider
K\subseteq{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\lambda})}
and
\mu\in P(K)
. It is not difficult to check that

\mu\in\overline{{\rm conv}\{\delta_{x}:x\in K\}}.

By Lemma
7.3
applied to
P(K)
, there is a set
D\subseteq K
such that
|D|<\kappa
and

\mu\in\overline{{\rm conv}\{\delta_{x}:x\in D\}}.

We shall check that the following set satisfies (iii):

S=\bigcup_{x\in D}\supp(x).

Write
C(\gamma)=\{x\in K:x(\gamma)=0\}
; if
\gamma\notin S

then
\delta_{x}(C(\gamma))=1
for every
x\in D
and hence

\nu(C(\gamma))=1
for every
\nu
from the set
{\rm conv}\{\delta_{x}:x\in D\}
.
Since
\mu
is in its closure, we have
\mu(C(\gamma))=1
as well.
Finally, the set

\{x\in K:\supp(x)\subseteq S\}=\bigcap_{\gamma\in\Gamma\setminus S}C(\gamma)

is an intersection of closed sets of full measure
\mu
so it has measure 1, by regularity of
\mu
.

To check (iii)
\Rightarrow
(i) take
K\subseteq{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})}
and consider
the family
{\mathcal{F}}\subseteq C(K)
of functions
f_{(I,\varphi)}
where

f_{(I,\varphi)}(x)=\prod_{\gamma\in I}x(\gamma)^{\varphi(\gamma)},\quad I\subseteq[\Gamma]^{<\omega},\varphi:I\to\mathbb{N}.

By the Stone-Weierstrass theorem,
the linear span of
{\mathcal{F}}
is norm dense in
C(K)
, and therefore the family
{\mathcal{F}}

distinguishes elements of
P(K)
. Given
\mu\in P(K)
, take a set
S
as in
(iii)
;
then
\mu(f_{(I,\varphi)})>0
implies
I\subseteq S
so, treating
{\mathcal{F}}

as a family of continuous functions on
P(K)
and using Remark
2.1
(d),
we conclude that
P(K)
is
\kappa
-Corson.

To argue for (iii)
\Rightarrow
(iv),
suppose that
\kappa
is not a caliber of a measure
\mu\in P(L)

for some compact space
L
. This is witnessed, in view of regularity of
\mu
, by
a family
\mathcal{A}=\{A_{\xi}:\xi<\kappa\}
of closed subsets of
L
such that
\mu(A_{\xi})>0
for every
\xi
and

\Big|\{\xi<\kappa:y\in A_{\xi}\}\Big|<\kappa,

for every
y\in L
. Consider the Boolean algebra
\mathfrak{B}
of subsets of
L
generated by
\mathcal{A}
.
Then
\mathfrak{B}
is a
\kappa
-Corson algebra
by Lemma
2.3
.
Moreover,

\theta:{\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B})\to 2^{\mathcal{A}},\theta(p)=\chi_{\widehat{A}}(p)\mbox{ for }p\in{\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B}),

is an embedding onto
K\subseteq\Sigma_{\kappa}(2^{\mathcal{A}})
. The measure
\mu
defines
a measure
\widehat{\mu}\in P({\rm ult}(\mathfrak{B}))
via the Stone isomorphism, and if we take
the image measure
\nu=\theta[\widehat{\mu}]\in P(K)
, then

\nu(\{x\in K:x(A)=1\}=\mu(A)>0,

for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
, so we have proved that (iii) does not hold.

Finally, we verify (iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii). If
K\subseteq{\Sigma_{\kappa}([0,1]^{\Gamma})}
and
\mu\in P(K)
, then the family
of sets of the form
C_{\gamma}=\{x\in K:x(\gamma)>0\}
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
, does
not contain
\kappa
many sets with nonempty intersection so, by
(iv)
,
the set
S=\{\gamma\in\Gamma:\mu(C_{\gamma})>0\}
must be of size
<\kappa

and hence
\mu
is supported by
\{x\in K:\supp(x)\subseteq S\}
.
∎

Corollary 12.6
.

For every
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
-Corson compact space
K
the spaces

P(K)
and
M_{1}(K)
are
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
-Corson compact.

13.
On Banach spaces
C(K)

Let us say that a class of compact spaces
{\mathcal{C}}
is B-stable if for any
L\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
any compact space
K
, the fact that the Banach spaces
C(K)
and
C(L)
are isomorphic implies

K\in{\mathcal{C}}
. Clearly, the class of metrizable compacta is B-stable, since

K
is metrizable if and only if
C(K)
is separable.
The class of Eberlein compacta is B-stable, as
L
is Eberlein compact if and only if

C(L)
is weakly compactly generated, which is
an isomorphic property, see e.g.
[
Ne
]
.

The class of
\omega
-Corson compact spaces
K
is not B-stable:

C(I)
and
C(I^{\omega})
are isomorphic by Miljutin’s theorem (cf. Semadeni
[
Se
]
),
while
I^{\omega}
is not
\omega
-Corson.
We do not know if the class
\mathcal{NY}
is B-stable.
The following open question has been around for several years,
see e.g.
[
AMN
, 3.9]
and
[
Ne
, 6.45]
.

Problem 13.1
.

Is the class of Corson compacta B-stable?

The problem has a relatively easy answer ‘yes’ if
\omega_{1}
is
a caliber of Radon measures. However, it is still unclear if
13.1
can be resolved within the usual axioms
of set theory; there are partial positive solutions in
[
Pl1
]
.
It seems natural to formulate a more general problem.

Problem 13.2
.

Given an uncountable cardinal number
\kappa
, is the class of
\kappa
-Corson compacta B-stable?

Again, the positive answer to
13.2
follows for
every regular number
\kappa
for which the class of
\kappa
-Corson compacta
is stable under
P(\cdot)
.
In fact, we have the following more general result.

Theorem 13.3
.

Suppose that a regular cardinal number
\kappa
is a caliber of Radon measures.
If
K
is
\kappa
-Corson compact and
T:C(L)\to C(K)
is an isomorphic embedding, then

L
is
\kappa
-Corson compact.

Proof.

We can assume that

c\cdot\|g\|\leq\|Tg\|\leq\|g\|
for some
c>0
and every
g\in C(L)
.
We consider the dual operator

T^{\ast}:M(K)\to M(L),\quad T^{\ast}\mu(g)=\mu(Tg)\mbox{ for }\mu\in M(K)\mbox{ and }g\in C(L).

Then
T^{\ast}
is surjective and
weak^{\ast}
–
weak^{\ast}

continuous; moreover,
T^{\ast}[M_{1}(K)]\supseteq c\cdot M_{1}[L]
.
By Theorem
12.5
,
M_{1}[K]
is
\kappa
-Corson compact, so

T^{\ast}[M_{1}(K)]
is also
\kappa
-Corson compact.
The space
L
embeds into
T^{\ast}[M_{1}(K)]
via the mapping
L\ni y\mapsto c\cdot\delta_{y}
,
and we are done.
∎

Corollary
12.6
and Theorem
13.3
yield the following.

Corollary 13.4
.

The class of
\mathfrak{c}^{+}
-Corson compacta is B-stable.

A Hilbert-Schmidt integral operator and the Weil distribution
Xian-Jin Li

Abstract
In this paper, a positive operator is given. It is shown that the product of this positive operator and the convolution operator is a trace class Hilbert-Schmidt integral operator and has nonnegative eigenvalues. A formula is given for the trace of this product operator. It seems that this product operator is the closest trace class integral operator which has nonnegative eigenvalues and is related to the Weil distribution. A relation is given between the trace of the product operator and the Weil distribution.

2. Nonnegativity of eigenvalues of
V(h)T

In this section we show that all eigenvalues of
V(h)T
are nonnegative.

Lemma 2.1

([5, Lemma 3.3 and Lemma 3.4, p. 2467])
E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}

is unitary on
L^{2}(C_{S})
, and its inverse is
E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}^{t}E_{S}^{-1}
.

Proof of Theorem 1.1

Since
S_{\Lambda}S_{\Lambda}=S_{\Lambda}
, we have

T=S_{\Lambda}\left(1-E_{S}Z_{\Lambda}E_{S}^{-1}\right)S_{\Lambda}.

By Lemma 2.1,
E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}

is a unitary operator on
L^{2}(C_{S})
. This implies that
E_{S}Z_{\Lambda}E_{S}^{-1}
is
an orthogonal projection on
L^{2}(C_{S})
as it is a nonzero self-adjoint idempotent.
So is
1-E_{S}Z_{\Lambda}E_{S}^{-1}
. Thus,

\langle(1-E_{S}Z_{\Lambda}E_{S}^{-1})F,F\rangle\geq 0

for
F\in L^{2}(C_{S})
. As
S_{\Lambda}
is bounded
linear operators on
L^{2}(C_{S})
, we have

\langle(1-E_{S}Z_{\Lambda}E_{S}^{-1})S_{\Lambda}F,S_{\Lambda}F\rangle\geq 0

for
F\in L^{2}(C_{S})
. It follows that

\displaystyle\langle TF,F\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S_{\Lambda}\left(1-E_{S}Z_{\Lambda}E_{S}^{-1}\right)S_{\Lambda}F,F\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle(1-E_{S}Z_{\Lambda}E_{S}^{-1})S_{\Lambda}F,S_{\Lambda}F\rangle\geq 0

for every element
F\in L^{2}(C_{S})
. Hence,
T
is a
positive operator on
L^{2}(C_{S})
.

This completes the proof of Theorem 1.1.
∎

Lemma 2.2

V(h)
is a positive operator on
L^{2}(C_{S})
.

Proof

Assume that
F
is any element in
L^{2}(C_{S})

with compact support. By definition,

V(h)F(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\lambda)\sqrt{|x/\lambda|}d^{\times}\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}g(|x/\lambda|y)g(y)dy.

If we make the change of variables
y\to|\lambda|y
then

\displaystyle\int_{C_{S}}V(h)F(x)\bar{F}(x)d^{\times}x

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{S}}\bar{F}(x)d^{\times}x\int_{C_{S}}F(\lambda)\sqrt{|x/\lambda|}d^{\times}\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}g(|x/\lambda|y)g(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{S}}\bar{F}(x)\sqrt{|x|}d^{\times}x\int_{C_{S}}F(\lambda)\sqrt{|\lambda|}d^{\times}\lambda\int_{0}^{\infty}g(|x|y)g(|\lambda|y)dy.

Since the above triple integral is absolute integrable as
F
and
g

are compactly supported, we can change order of integration to derive

\int_{C_{S}}V(h)F(x)\bar{F}(x)d^{\times}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{(\int_{C_{S}}F(x)g(|x|y)\sqrt{|x|}d^{\times}x)}(\int_{C_{S}}F(\lambda)g(|\lambda|y)\sqrt{|\lambda|}d^{\times}\lambda)dy\geq 0.

Since
S
is a finite set, compactly supported functions in

L^{2}(C_{S})
are dense in
L^{2}(C_{S})
.
As
V(h)
is bounded, we have

\langle V(h)F,\rangle F\geq 0

for all
F\in L^{2}(C_{S})
. That is,
V(h)
is a positive operator
on
L^{2}(C_{S})
.

This completes the proof of the lemma. ∎

Proof of Theorem 1.2

Since
T
is a positive operator on the
complex Hilbert space
L^{2}(C_{S})
by Lemma 2.2, [8, Theorem VI.9 on p. 196 and 4th
paragraph on p. 195] implies that there exists a positive bounded operator
B
on

L^{2}(C_{S})
, which is self-adjoint, satisfying that
T=B^{2}
.

Let
\lambda
be any eigenvalue of
V(h)T
on
L^{2}(C_{S})
.
Then a nonzero element
F\in L^{2}(C_{S})
exists such that

V(h)T(F)=\lambda F
. If
B(F)=0
, then

\lambda F=V(h)TF=V(h)B\cdot B(F)=0
. Hence
\lambda=0
as
F\neq 0
.
Thus it suffices to consider the case when
B(F)\neq 0
.

As
B
is bounded, we have
B(F)\in L^{2}(C_{S})
.
Assume that
B(F)\neq 0
. Since
V(h)T(F)=\lambda F
,
we have

. Thus

\lambda\langle B(F),B(F)\rangle_{L^{2}(C_{S})}=\langle\lambda B(F),B(F)\rangle=\langle BV(h)T(F),B(F)\rangle.

Since
B
is self-adjoint and
T=B^{2}
, we have

\langle BV(h)T(F),B(F)\rangle=\langle V(h)T(F),B^{2}(F)\rangle=\langle V(h)T(F),T(F)\rangle.

By Lemma 2.2,
V(h)
is a positive operator on
the space

. Hence

\langle V(h)T(F),T(F)\rangle_{L^{2}(C_{S})}\geq 0.

Therefore

\lambda\langle B(F),B(F)\rangle_{L^{2}(C_{S})}\geq 0.

Since

, we must have
\langle B(F),B(F)\rangle_{L^{2}(C_{S})}>0

as

is a Hilbert space. It follows that

.

This completes the proof of Theorem 1.2. ∎

E_{S}JE_{S}^{-1}F(x)=F(1/x)
by (3.1) and since

E_{S}J\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}=E_{S}JE_{S}^{-1}\cdot E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}
,
both
S_{\Lambda}
and
E_{S}J\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}
are bounded operators
on
L^{2}(C_{S})
. By Lemma 3.4, in order to show that
V(h)T
is of trace class
on
L^{2}(C_{S})
it suffices to show that

V(g)[S_{\Lambda},V(g^{*})]
and

V(g)[S_{\Lambda},V(g^{*})S_{\Lambda}E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}^{t}JE_{S}^{-1}]

are of trace class.

We can write

\displaystyle V_{S}(g)[S_{\Lambda},V_{S}(g^{*})]F(x)

[formula]

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{S}}\sqrt{|x/z|}g(|x/z|)d^{\times}z\int_{C_{S}}\left(S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)\right)g^{*}(|z/y|)\sqrt{|z/y|}F(y)d^{\times}y

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{S}}{g(|{x\over z}|)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}\over 1+|\log|z||}d^{\times}z\int_{C_{S}}{[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)]g^{*}(|{z\over y}|)\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\over(1+|\log|z||)^{-1}}F(y)d^{\times}y=ABF(x)

for

, where the operators
A
and

We find that

\displaystyle\int_{C_{S}\times C_{S}}\left|{g\left(|{x\over z}|\right)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}\over 1+|\log|z||}\right|^{2}d^{\times}xd^{\times}z

as

has a compact support. By Lemma 3.5,

is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator.

If

[S_{\Lambda}(|y|)-S_{\Lambda}(|z|)]g^{*}(|{z\over y}|)\neq 0,

then we must have
\sqrt{\mu}\leq|z/y|\leq\mu^{-1/2}
and
either

,

or
|z|>\Lambda^{-1}
,
|y|\leq\Lambda^{-1}
.

\Lambda^{-1}<|y|\leq|z|/\sqrt{\mu}\leq{1\over\sqrt{\mu}\Lambda}

and

\Lambda^{-1}\geq|z|\geq|y|\sqrt{\mu}>{\sqrt{\mu}\over\Lambda}.

If
|z|>\Lambda^{-1}

|y|\leq\Lambda^{-1}

\Lambda^{-1}<|z|\leq|y|/\sqrt{\mu}\leq{1\over\sqrt{\mu}\Lambda}

\Lambda^{-1}\geq|y|\geq\sqrt{\mu}|z|>{\sqrt{\mu}\over\Lambda}.

Therefore,

|z|\in({\sqrt{\mu}\over\Lambda},{1\over\Lambda}],\,\,|y|\in({1\over\Lambda},{1\over\Lambda\sqrt{\mu}}]\,\,\,\,\text{ or }\,\,\,\,|z|\in({1\over\Lambda},{1\over\Lambda\sqrt{\mu}}],\,\,|y|\in({\sqrt{\mu}\over\Lambda},{1\over\Lambda}].

[formula]

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{C_{S}\times C_{S}}\left|(1+|\log|z||)[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)]g^{*}(|{z\over y}|)\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\right|^{2}d^{\times}yd^{\times}z

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\sqrt{\mu}\over\Lambda}^{1\over\Lambda\sqrt{\mu}}(1+|\log|z||)^{2}d^{\times}z\int_{C_{S}}|g^{*}(|z/y|)|^{2}|z/y|d^{\times}y

It follows from Lemma 3.5 that
B
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator.

Thus
V(g)[S_{\Lambda},V(g^{*})]
is a product of two

,
B
on
L^{2}(C_{S})
, and hence is of trace class
on
L^{2}(C_{S})
by Lemma 3.3.

By Lemma 3.6, we can write

\displaystyle V(g)[S_{\Lambda},V(g^{*})S_{\Lambda}E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}^{t}JE_{S}^{-1}]F(x)=\int_{C_{S}}{g(|{x\over z}|)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}\over 1+|\log|z||}d^{\times}z

[formula]

\displaystyle\times\int_{C_{S}}{S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)\over(1+|\log|z||)^{-1}}\left(\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda{z\over y})d\lambda\right)F(y)d^{\times}y

for

. We have already shown above that

\int_{C_{S}\times C_{S}}\left|{g\left(|{x\over z}|\right)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}\over 1+|\log|z||}\right|^{2}d^{\times}xd^{\times}z<\infty.

Since
S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)\neq 0
only if either
\Lambda|z|>1
,

\Lambda|y|\leq 1
or
\Lambda|y|>1
,
\Lambda|z|\leq 1
.
That is,

|\log|z/y||=|\log|\Lambda z/\Lambda y||=|\log|\Lambda z|-\log|\Lambda y||=|\log|\Lambda z||+|\log|\Lambda y||.

[formula]

By Lemma 3.7

\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda{z\over y})d\lambda\ll_{S}\left|{\Lambda z\over\Lambda y}\right|^{{1\over 2}-c}.

If we choose

when
|\Lambda z/\Lambda y|<1
and choose

when

, by (3.7)

\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\left|\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda{z\over y})d\lambda\right|\ll_{S}{1\over(2+|\log|z/y||)^{3}}={1\over(1+|\log|\Lambda z||+1+|\log|\Lambda y||)^{3}}

for all

satisfying

.
It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{C_{S}\times C_{S}}\left|{S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)\over(1+|\log|z||)^{-1}}\left(\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda{z\over y})d\lambda\right)\right|^{2}d^{\times}yd^{\times}z

[formula]

\displaystyle\ll_{S}\int_{C_{S}}{(1+|\log|z||)^{2}\over(1+|\log|\Lambda z||)^{4}}d^{\times}z\int_{C_{S}}{1\over(1+|\log|\Lambda y||)^{2}}d^{\times}y<\infty.

Thus
V(g)[S_{\Lambda},V(g^{*})S_{\Lambda}E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}^{t}JE_{S}^{-1}]

is a product of two Hilbert-Schmidt operators by Lemma 3.5, and
hence is of trace class on

by Lemma 3.3.

This completes the proof of Theorem 1.3.

4. Trace of

In this section we compute the trace of

on

.
The
S
-local trace formula is first given in
[3, Theorem 4, p. 56] for the operator

\mathfrak{F}_{S}P_{\Lambda}\mathfrak{F}_{S}^{t}P_{\Lambda}
.
The author’s variant of it in [5, Theorem 3.16, p. 2481]

Lemma 4.1

([2, Corollary 3.2, p. 237]) Let

be a
\sigma
-finite

is continuous at

Lemma 4.2

V(h)T
is a Hilbert-Schmidt integral operator on

with
the kernel

\displaystyle k(x,y)=-\int_{C_{S}}{g(|{x\over z}|)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}\over 1+|\log|z||}\cdot{[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)]g^{*}(|{z\over y}|)\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\over(1+|\log|z||)^{-1}}S_{\Lambda}(y)d^{\times}z+\int_{C_{S}}g(|{x\over z}|)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}

\displaystyle\times\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}\right)_{u}\left[[S_{\Lambda}(z)-S_{\Lambda}({1\over|u|})]\sqrt{|zu|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda\right](y)S_{\Lambda}(y)d^{\times}z.

Proof

By (3.3) and (3.4),

[formula]

and

\displaystyle V(g)[S_{\Lambda},V(g^{*})]S_{\Lambda}F(x)

[formula]

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{S}}{g(|{x\over z}|)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}\over 1+|\log|z||}d^{\times}z\int_{C_{S}}{[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)]g^{*}(|{z\over y}|)\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\over(1+|\log|z||)^{-1}}S_{\Lambda}(y)F(y)d^{\times}y.

Since

E_{S}Jf(y)=\sqrt{|y|}\sum_{\xi\in O_{S}^{*}}{1\over|\xi y|}f({1\over\xi y})={1\over\sqrt{|y|}}\sum_{\gamma\in O_{S}^{*}}f({\gamma\over y})=E_{S}(f)({1\over y}),

by (3.6) we have

\displaystyle V(g)[S_{\Lambda},V(g^{*})S_{\Lambda}E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}^{t}JE_{S}^{-1}]E_{S}J\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}S_{\Lambda}F(x)=\int_{C_{S}}g(|{x\over z}|)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}d^{\times}z

\displaystyle\times\int_{C_{S}}[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)]\{\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda{z\over y})d\lambda\}\left(E_{S}J\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}S_{\Lambda}F\right)(y)d^{\times}y

\displaystyle\times\{\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda{z\over y})d\lambda\}\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}S_{\Lambda}F\right)({1\over y})d^{\times}y

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{S}}g(|{x\over z}|)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}d^{\times}z\int_{C_{S}}[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}({1\over|y|})]

\displaystyle\times\{\sqrt{|zy|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zy)d\lambda\}\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}S_{\Lambda}F\right)(y)d^{\times}y

for
F\in L^{2}(C_{S})
.

Since

[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)]\{\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda{z\over y})d\lambda\}\in L^{2}(C_{S})

for fixed
z
by (3.8), we have

[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}({1\over|y|})]\{\sqrt{|zy|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zy)d\lambda\}\in L^{2}(C_{S})

as a function of
y
. By Lemma 2.1,
E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}

is a unitary operator on
L^{2}(C_{S})
. This implies that

\langle E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}F,G\rangle_{L^{2}(c_{S})}=\langle F,E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}^{t}E_{S}^{-1}G\rangle_{L^{2}(C_{S})}

for

. From this identity we derive that

\displaystyle\int_{C_{S}}[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}({1\over|y|})]\{\sqrt{|zy|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zy)d\lambda\}\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}^{t}E_{S}^{-1}S_{\Lambda}F\right)(y)d^{\times}y

\displaystyle=\int_{C_{S}}\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}\right)_{u}\left[[S_{\Lambda}(z)-S_{\Lambda}({1\over|u|})]\sqrt{|zu|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda\right](y)S_{\Lambda}F(y)d^{\times}y.

Hence,

\displaystyle V(g)[S_{\Lambda},V(g^{*})S_{\Lambda}E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}^{t}JE_{S}^{-1}]E_{S}J\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}S_{\Lambda}F(x)=\int_{C_{S}}g(|{x\over z}|)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}d^{\times}z

[formula]

\displaystyle\times\int_{C_{S}}\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}\right)_{u}\left[[S_{\Lambda}(z)-S_{\Lambda}({1\over|u|})]\sqrt{|zu|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda\right](y)S_{\Lambda}F(y)d^{\times}y

for

for

, where the change order of integration

\displaystyle\int_{C_{S}}|\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}\right)_{u}\left[[S_{\Lambda}(z)-S_{\Lambda}({1\over|u|})]\sqrt{|zu|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda\right](y)S_{\Lambda}F(y)|d^{\times}y

is a Hilbert-Schmidt integral operator
on

, i.e.,

. Notice that

\displaystyle\int_{C_{S}\times C_{S}}|k(x,y)|^{2}d^{\times}xd^{\times}y\leq 2\int_{C_{S}\times C_{S}}\left|{g(|{x\over z}|)\sqrt{|{x\over z}|}\over 1+|\log|z||}\right|^{2}d^{\times}zd^{\times}x

\displaystyle\times\{\int_{C_{S}\times C_{S}}\left|{[S_{\Lambda}(|z|)-S_{\Lambda}(|y|)]g^{*}(|{z\over y}|)\sqrt{|{z\over y}|}\over(1+|\log|z||)^{-1}}S_{\Lambda}(y)\right|^{2}d^{\times}zd^{\times}y+\int_{C_{S}\times C_{S}}

\displaystyle\int_{C_{S}}\left|\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}\right)_{u}\left[[S_{\Lambda}(z)-S_{\Lambda}({1\over|u|})]\sqrt{|zu|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda\right](y)S_{\Lambda}(y)\right|^{2}d^{\times}y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{C_{S}}\left|\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}\right)_{u}\left[[S_{\Lambda}(z)-S_{\Lambda}({1\over|u|})]\sqrt{|zu|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda\right](y)\right|^{2}d^{\times}y

Lemma 4.3

Let
O_{p}=\{x\in Q_{p}:|x|_{p}\leq 1\}
and

B=\prod_{p\in S^{\prime}}p^{-1}O_{p}
. If
F(y)=f(|y|)
for some
function
f
, then

\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}F(y)\Psi_{S}(yx)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times B}F(y)\Psi_{S}(y|x|)dy.

Proof

Since the complement of
J_{S}
in
\mathbb{A}_{S}
is negligible,
as
|\xi|=1
for
\xi\in O_{S}^{*}
we can write

\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}F(y)\Psi_{S}(yx)dy=\sum_{\xi\in O_{S}^{*}}\int_{I_{S}}F(y)\Psi_{S}(yx\xi)dy.

Since
F(y)=f(|y|)
, if we change variables
y\to y|x|/x

with
|x|=(|x|,1,\cdots,1)
then

\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}F(y)\Psi_{S}(yx)dy=\sum_{\xi\in O_{S}^{*}}\int_{I_{S}}f(|y|)\Psi_{S}(y|x|\xi)dy=\sum_{\xi\in O_{S}^{*}}\varpi(\xi)\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-2\pi it|x|\xi}dt,

where
\varpi(\xi)
is given as in (3.2).

If
\xi\in O_{S}^{*}
satisfies
\varpi(\xi)\neq 0
,
then
\xi=l/k
for some integers
k,l\in\mathbb{N}_{S}

with square free
k
. Thus,
\xi\prod_{p\in S^{\prime}}O_{p}^{*}\subset B
. Hence,

\bigcup_{\xi\in O_{S}^{*},\varpi(\xi)\neq 0}\xi I_{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{*}\times B.

Conversely, if
x=(x_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{*}\times B
then there exists a
\xi\in O_{S}^{*}

such that
\xi^{-1}x\in I_{S}
, i.e.,
|\xi|_{p}=|x|_{p}\leq p
for
p\in S^{\prime}
.
By (3.2),
\varpi(\xi)\neq 0
. Hence,

x\in\bigcup_{\xi\in O_{S}^{*},\varpi(\xi)\neq 0}\xi I_{S}
. Thus,

\mathbb{R}^{*}\times B\subset\bigcup_{\xi\in O_{S}^{*},\varpi(\xi)\neq 0}\xi I_{S}.

Also if
\xi,\gamma
are distinct elements in
O_{S}^{*}
, then

\xi O_{S}^{*}
and
\gamma O_{S}^{*}
are disjoint sets. Therefore,

\mathbb{R}^{*}\times B=\bigcup_{\xi\in O_{S}^{*},\varpi(\xi)\neq 0}\xi I_{S},\text{ a disjoint union}.

Therefore,

\sum_{\xi\in O_{S}^{*},\varpi(\xi)\neq 0}\int_{I_{S}}F(y)\Psi_{S}(y|x|\xi)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times B}F(y)\Psi_{S}(y|x|\xi)dy.

That is,

\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}F(y)\Psi_{S}(yx)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times B}F(y)\Psi_{S}(y|x|)dy.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.4

We have

\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}\right)_{u}\left[\sqrt{|u|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda\right](y)={\sqrt{|y|}\over|z|}g(|{y\over z}|)S_{\Lambda}(y).

That is,

\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}\Psi_{S}(-uy)du\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}{1\over|z|}g({\lambda\over z})S_{\Lambda}(\lambda)\Psi_{S}(\lambda u)d\lambda={1\over|z|}g({y\over z})S_{\Lambda}(y).

Proof

By [5, Lemma 3.4 and Remark, p. 2467],

\left(e_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}e_{S}^{-1}\right)_{u}[\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda](y)=\left(\mathfrak{F}_{S}\right)_{u}[\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda](y).

It follows that

\displaystyle\left(E_{S}\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1}\right)_{u}[\sqrt{|u|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda](y)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{|y|}\left(\mathfrak{F}_{S}\right)_{u}[\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g(\lambda)S_{\Lambda}(\lambda z)\Psi_{S}(\lambda zu)d\lambda](y)=\sqrt{|y|}\left(\mathfrak{F}_{S}\right)_{u}[\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}{1\over|z|}g({\lambda\over z})S_{\Lambda}(\lambda)\Psi_{S}(\lambda u)d\lambda](y)

\displaystyle={\sqrt{|y|}\over|z|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}\Psi_{S}(-uy)du\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g({\lambda\over z})S_{\Lambda}(\lambda)\Psi_{S}(\lambda u)d\lambda.

Hence, the second stated identity follows from the first one.

By Lemma 4.3,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}\Psi_{S}(-uy)du\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}g({\lambda\over z})S_{\Lambda}(\lambda)\Psi_{S}(\lambda u)d\lambda

Lemma 6.2

Let

\displaystyle=\sqrt{|y|}\left(\mathfrak{F}_{S}\right)_{u}[\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}f(\lambda)\Psi_{S}(\lambda u)d\lambda](y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma\in O_{S}^{*}}\int_{I_{S}}f(\lambda)\Psi_{S}(\lambda\gamma u)d\lambda,

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{F}_{S}E_{S}^{-1})_{u}\{\sqrt{|u|}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}f(\lambda)\Psi_{S}(\lambda u)d\lambda\}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S}}\Psi_{S}(-uy)du\int_{I_{S}}f(\lambda)\Psi_{S}(\lambda u)d\lambda

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{2\pi iu_{r}y_{r}}du_{r}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S^{\prime}}}\Psi_{S^{\prime}}(-u_{b}y_{b})du_{b}\int_{\mathbb{A}_{S^{\prime}}}\phi(\lambda_{b})\Psi_{S^{\prime}}(\lambda_{b}u_{b})d\lambda_{b}

Corollary 6.3

We have

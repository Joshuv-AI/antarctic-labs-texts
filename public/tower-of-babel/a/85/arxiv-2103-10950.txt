Longitudinal dynamics and chiral symmetry breaking in holographic light-front QCD
Guy F. de Téramond, Stanley J. Brodsky

Abstract
The breaking of chiral symmetry in holographic light-front QCD is encoded in its longitudinal dynamics, with its chiral limit protected by the superconformal algebraic structure which governs its transverse dynamics. The scale in the longitudinal light-front Hamiltonian determines the confinement strength in this direction: It is also responsible for most of the light meson ground state mass consistent with the Gell-Mann-Oakes-Renner constraint. Longitudinal confinement and the breaking of chiral symmetry are found to be different manifestations of the same underlying dynamics as found in the ’t Hooft large- N C N_{C} QCD (1 + 1) model.

Introduction

In spite of the important progress of Euclidean lattice gauge theory, a basic understanding of the mechanism of color confinement and its relation to chiral symmetry breaking in QCD, two fundamental phenomena of strong interactions, has remained an unsolved problem. Recent developments based on superconformal quantum mechanics

;
Brodsky:2013ar
;
deTeramond:2014asa
;
Dosch:2015nwa
;
Brodsky:2014yha
;
Brodsky:2020ajy
. This new approach to nonperturbative QCD dynamics, holographic light-front QCD, leads to effective semi-classical relativistic bound-state equations for arbitrary spin
deTeramond:2013it
, and it incorporates fundamental properties which are not apparent from the QCD Lagrangian, such as the emergence of the hadron mass scale, the prediction of a massless pion in the chiral limit, and the remarkable connections between meson, baryon and tetraquark spectroscopy across the full hadron spectrum
Dosch:2015bca
;
Dosch:2016zdv
;
Nielsen:2018uyn
;
Nielsen:2018ytt

Sufian:2016hwn
;
deTeramond:2018ecg
;
Liu:2019vsn
, and also describe the running of the QCD coupling
\alpha_{s}(Q^{2})
in the nonperturbative domain
Brodsky:2010ur
;
Deur:2014qfa

II
Longitudinal and transverse dynamics in HLFQCD

We start with the light-front invariant QCD Hamiltonian

H^{LF}=P^{2}=P^{+}P^{-}-\mathbf{P}^{2}_{\perp}
,
P^{2}=P_{\mu}P^{\mu}=M^{2}
,

\displaystyle H^{LF}|\psi\rangle=M^{2}|\psi\rangle,

(1)

and compute
M^{2}
from the hadronic matrix element
\langle\psi(P’)|H^{LF}|\psi(P)\rangle=M^{2}\langle\psi(P’)|\psi(P)\rangle
, expanding the hadronic states in terms of their Fock components
deTeramond:2008ht
;
Brodsky:2014yha
. For the simplest hadronic bound-state, a two parton state with quark masses
m_{q}
and
m_{\bar{q}}
,

M^{2}=\\
\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\!\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}}{16\pi^{3}}\Big(\frac{\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{2}}{x(1-x)}+\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{(1-x)}\Big)\left|\psi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right|^{2}\\
+\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\!\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}}{16\pi^{3}}\,U(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\left|\psi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right|^{2},

(2)

with normalization

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\!\int\frac{d^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}}{16\pi^{3}}\,\left|\psi(x,\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right|^{2}=1.

(3)

The variable
x
is the LF longitudinal momentum fraction
x=k^{+}/P^{+}
and
\mathbf{k}_{\perp}
is the relative transverse momentum. Taking the Fourier transform, Eq. (
2
) can be expressed in terms of the transverse impact variable
\mathbf{b}_{\perp}
, conjugate to
\mathbf{k}_{\perp}
,

M^{2}=\\
\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\!\int\!d^{2}\mathbf{b}_{\perp}\psi^{*}(x,\mathbf{b}_{\perp})\Big(\frac{-\mathbf{\nabla}_{\mathbf{b}_{\perp}}^{2}}{x(1-x)}+\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{(1-x)}\Big)\psi(x,\mathbf{b}_{\perp})\\
+\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\!\int d^{2}\mathbf{b}_{\perp}\,\psi^{*}(x,\mathbf{b}_{\perp})U(x,\mathbf{b}_{\perp})\psi(x,\mathbf{b}_{\perp}),

(4)

with normalization

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\!\int d^{2}\mathbf{b}_{\perp}\,\left|\psi(x,\mathbf{b}_{\perp})\right|^{2}=1.

(5)

As noted in Refs.
Chabysheva:2012fe
;
Li:2015zda
, the LF Hamiltonian equation (
1
) leads to a longitudinal and a transverse light-front wave equation in the approximation where transverse and longitudinal dynamics are separated. To show this, we introduce the invariant LF transverse variable
\zeta^{2}=x(1-x)\mathbf{b}^{2}_{\perp}
, which is identified with the holographic AdS variable
z

Brodsky:2014yha
, and factor out the longitudinal, transverse and orbital dependence of the LF wave function
deTeramond:2008ht
;
Brodsky:2014yha

\displaystyle\psi(x,\zeta,\varphi)=e^{iL\varphi}X(x)\frac{\phi(\zeta)}{\sqrt{2\pi\zeta}},

(6)

in the approximation where the effective LF confinement potential is written as the sum of longitudinal and transverse components
Chabysheva:2012fe
;
Li:2015zda

\displaystyle U(x,\zeta)=U_{\perp}(\zeta)+U_{\parallel}(x).

(7)

The LF wave function normalization (
5
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!d\zeta\,\phi^{2}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=1,

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\frac{X^{2}(x)}{x(1-x)}

\displaystyle=1.

(9)

Following the procedure introduced in
deTeramond:2008ht
, we obtain for (
4
)

M^{2}=\\
\int_{0}^{\infty}\!d\zeta\,\phi^{*}(\zeta)\sqrt{\zeta}\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{d\zeta^{2}}-\frac{1}{\zeta}\frac{d}{d\zeta}+\frac{L^{2}}{\zeta^{2}}+U_{\perp}(\zeta)\right)\frac{\phi(\zeta)}{\sqrt{\zeta}}\\
+\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\,\chi^{*}(x)\left(\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{(1-x)}+U_{\parallel}(x)\right)\chi(x),

(10)

where
L
is the relative LF orbital angular momentum
L\equiv|L^{z}|_{max}
and

X(x)=\sqrt{x(1-x)}\,\chi(x)
, with the normalization of
\chi(x)
determined from (
9
)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\chi^{2}(x)=1.

(11)

In the chiral limit
X(x)\to\sqrt{x(1-x)}

Brodsky:2006uqa
, and therefore
\chi(x)=1
for zero quark masses.

Eq. (
10
) is thus equivalent to the semiclassical
LF transverse
deTeramond:2008ht
;
Brodsky:2014yha
and longitudinal
Chabysheva:2012fe
;
Li:2015zda
wave equations for mesons

\displaystyle\Big(-\frac{d^{2}}{d\zeta^{2}}-\frac{1-4L^{2}}{4\zeta^{2}}+U_{\perp}(\zeta)\Big)\phi(\zeta)

\displaystyle=M_{\perp}^{2}\phi(\zeta),

(12)

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{1-x}+U_{\parallel}(x)\Big)\chi(x)

\displaystyle=M_{\parallel}^{2}\,\chi(x),

(13)

with
M^{2}=M_{\perp}^{2}+M_{\parallel}^{2}
. Both equations (
12
) and (
13
) are relativistic and frame independent. We note that the separation of longitudinal and transverse equations (
12
) and (
13
) is natural in the light-front, since the quark masses are associated with the longitudinal component of the kinetic energy, not the transverse part
Chabysheva:2012fe
.

As noted in Refs.
Chabysheva:2012fe
;
Li:2015zda
, the longitudinal eigenvalue equation (
13
) for the longitudinal mass
M_{\parallel}^{2}
can be combined with the holographic LF transverse equation (
12
) for
M_{\perp}^{2}
to incorporate massive quarks. The longitudinal mass
M_{\parallel}^{2}
appears as a separation constant in the transverse equation (
12
), namely
M_{\perp}^{2}\!\to\!M^{2}\!-M_{\parallel}^{2}

Chabysheva:2012fe
. As a result, the structure of the superconformal equation in the transverse direction is not modified, even by heavy quark masses, as long as transverse and longitudinal dynamics can be separated.

II.1
HLFQCD equations for baryons

The semiclassical approximation to the light-front Hamiltonian for baryons is given by a system of two coupled linear differential equations for the chiral components
\psi_{+}
and
\psi_{-}
, which follow from the mapping of higher-dimensional wave equations for half-integer spin in AdS space to the light-front
deTeramond:2013it
;
Brodsky:2014yha

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{d\zeta}\psi_{-}-\frac{L+{\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}}{\zeta}\psi_{-}-V(\zeta)\psi_{-}

\displaystyle=M\psi_{+},

(14)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\zeta}\psi_{+}-\frac{L+{\textstyle{\frac{1}{2}}}}{\zeta}\psi_{+}-V(\zeta)\psi_{+}

\displaystyle=M\psi_{-},

(15)

with normalization

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!d\zeta\,\psi_{+}^{2}(\zeta)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!d\zeta\,\psi_{-}^{2}(\zeta)=1.

(16)

The system of linear equations (
14
) and (
15
) is equivalent to the system of second order light-front wave equations

\displaystyle\Big(-\frac{d^{2}}{d\zeta^{2}}-\frac{1-4L^{2}}{4\zeta^{2}}+U^{+}_{\perp}(\zeta)\Big)\psi_{+}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=M_{\perp}^{2}\psi_{+}(\zeta),

(17)

\displaystyle\Big(-\frac{d^{2}}{d\zeta^{2}}-\frac{1-4(L+1)^{2}}{4\zeta^{2}}+U^{-}_{\perp}(\zeta)\Big)\psi_{-}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=M_{\perp}^{2}\psi_{-}(\zeta),~~

(18)

where

\displaystyle U^{\pm}(\zeta)=V^{2}(\zeta)\pm V^{\prime}(z)+\frac{1+2L}{\zeta}V(\zeta).

(19)

It corresponds to LF angular momentum
L
and
L+1
.

II.2
Invariant mass ansatz

A simple ansatz to account for quark masses in HLFQCD was introduced in
Brodsky:2008pg
based on the arbitrary off-shell dependence of the LF wave function on the invariant mass squared: It controls the bound state according to the uncertainty principle in quantum mechanics. For a two-parton state this amounts to the substitution

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{2}}{x(1-x)}\to\frac{\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{2}}{x(1-x)}+\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{1-x},

(20)

in the ground-state Gaussian wave function to include the expression for the LF kinetic energy with quark masses: It is also the invariant mass squared
s=(p_{q}+p_{\bar{q}})^{2}
of the
q\bar{q}
pair. This substitution leads, upon exponentiation, to the longitudinal LF wave function
Brodsky:2008pg

\displaystyle\chi_{IM}(x)=\mathcal{N}\exp\Big(\!-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\Big[\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{1-x}\Big]\Big),

(21)

and thus to a natural factorization of the longitudinal and transverse components of the LFWF, independent of the actual value of the effective quark masses. The label IM stands for invariant mass, and
\mathcal{N}
is a normalization factor
Sandapen:2020nrn
. On the other hand it was shown in
Gutsche:2012ez
that the ansatz

\displaystyle\chi_{CSB}(x)\sim x^{a}(1-x)^{b},

(22)

obtained in
tHooft:1974pnl
;
Bergknoff:1976xr
, leads to explicit charge symmetry breaking (CSB) in holographic QCD models. The partonic mass shift contribution to hadron masses
Weisberger:1972hk
,

\displaystyle\Delta M^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\chi(x)\Big[\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{1-x}\Big]\chi(x),

(23)

used in
Brodsky:2008pg
and
Gutsche:2012ez
does not incorporate, however, the explicit contribution from a longitudinal potential to hadron masses required to satisfy the virial theorem.

III
Longitudinal and transverse effective confinement potentials

We briefly describe in this section the transverse confinement potential for mesons in HLFQCD and examine two particularly interesting models for longitudinal confinement: the well known ’t Hooft model
tHooft:1974pnl
in one-space and one-time directions, derived from first principles QCD in the large
N_{C}
limit, and the effective potential model introduced by Li, Maris, Zhao and Vary (LMZV)
Li:2015zda
, which generates a complete basis function in the longitudinal direction.

III.1
Transverse confinement potential

The transverse LF equation (
12
) has the same structure as the wave equations derived in AdS provided that one identifies
\zeta=z

deTeramond:2008ht
, the holographic fifth-dimensional coordinate of AdS. This precise mapping allows us to relate the LF confinement potential
U_{\perp}
to the dilaton profile which modifies AdS space
Brodsky:2014yha
. Conformal algebra underlies in LF holography the scale invariance of the QCD Lagrangian
Brodsky:2013ar
. It leads to the introduction of a scale
\lambda
and harmonic confinement,
U\sim\lambda\zeta^{2}
, maintaining the action conformal invariant
Brodsky:2013ar
;
deAlfaro:1976je
. The oscillator potential corresponds to a quadratic dilaton profile and thus to the emergence of linear Regge trajectories
Karch:2006pv
.

Extension to superconformal algebra leads to a specific connection between mesons, baryons and tetraquarks
deTeramond:2014asa
;
Dosch:2015nwa
;
Brodsky:2016yod
underlying the
SU(3)_{C}
representation properties, since a diquark cluster can be in the same color representation as an antiquark, namely
\bar{3}\in 3\times 3
. The meson wave function
\phi_{M}
, the upper and lower components of the baryon wave function,
\phi_{B\,\pm}\equiv\psi_{\pm}
, and the tetraquark wave function,
\phi_{T}
, can be arranged as a supersymmetric 4-plet matrix
Brodsky:2016yod
;
Zou:2018eam

\displaystyle|\Phi\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{M}^{\,(L+1)}&\phi_{B\,-}^{\,(L+1)}\\
\phi_{B\,+}^{\,(L)}&\phi_{T}^{\,(L)}\end{pmatrix},

(24)

with
H_{\perp}^{LF}|\Phi\rangle=M^{2}_{\perp}|\Phi\rangle
. The constraints from superconformal structure uniquely determine the form of the effective transverse confining potential for mesons, nucleons and tetraquarks
deTeramond:2014asa
;
Dosch:2015nwa
;
Brodsky:2016yod
, including critical constant terms:

\displaystyle U_{M\perp}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\zeta^{2}+2\lambda(L_{M}-1),

(25)

\displaystyle U^{+}_{B\perp}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\zeta^{2}+2\lambda(L_{B}+1),

(26)

\displaystyle U^{-}_{B\perp}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\zeta^{2}+2\lambda L_{B},

(27)

\displaystyle U_{T\perp}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\zeta^{2}+2\lambda(L_{T}+1),

(28)

and lead to the remarkable relations
L_{M}=L_{B}+1
,
L_{T}=L_{B}
. The superconformal algebra also requires the universality of Regge slopes with a unique scale
\lambda
for all hadron families, a well known fact in hadron physics, which holds to a good approximation.

The solution of the AdS wave equations for mesons and vector mesons does incorporate the dependence on the total quark spin,
S=0
for the
\pi
Regge trajectory and
S=1
for the
\rho
trajectory: It is given by the additional term
2\lambda S
,
S=0,1
, in the LF Hamiltonian. It leads, for example to the correct prediction for the
\pi-\rho
mass gap:
M_{\rho}^{2}-M^{2}_{\pi}=2\lambda
. In contrast, for baryons the quark spin interaction within a 2-quark cluster is not contained in the AdS action. To describe the quark spin-spin interaction, which distinguishes for example the nucleons from
\Delta
particles, we also include an identical term,
2\lambda S
,
S=0,1
in the LF baryon Hamiltonian to maintain hadronic supersymmetry. The final expression for the transverse mass spectrum for mesons, baryons and tetraquarks is given by
Brodsky:2016yod

\displaystyle M^{2}_{M\perp}

\displaystyle=4\lambda(n+L_{M})+2\lambda S,

(29)

\displaystyle M^{2}_{B\perp}

\displaystyle=4\lambda(n+L_{B}+1)+2\lambda S,

(30)

\displaystyle M^{2}_{T\perp}

\displaystyle=4\lambda(n+L_{T}+1)+2\lambda S,

(31)

with the same slope in
L
and
n
, the radial quantum number.

III.2
The t’Hooft model

As discussed by Chabysheva and Hiller
Chabysheva:2012fe
, it is natural to identify the potential for longitudinal dynamics with the potential which underlies the ’t Hooft model for large-
N_{C}
QCD in (1+1) dimensions. It has the same form as the instantaneous LF potential which appears from instantaneous gluon exchange in the
A^{+}=0
light-cone gauge in QCD (3+1)
Brodsky:1997de
.

In the ’t Hooft model
tHooft:1974pnl
the longitudinal equation (
13
) becomes the integral equation for the pion

\Big(\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{1-x}\Big)\chi(x)+\frac{g^{2}N_{C}}{\pi}P\!\int_{0}^{1}dx^{\prime}\frac{\chi(x)-\chi(x^{\prime})}{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}}\\
=M_{\parallel}^{2}\,\chi(x),

(32)

where the coupling
g
has dimensions of mass. To find an analytic solution to (
32
) the approximate solution

\displaystyle\chi(x)\sim x^{\beta_{1}}(1-x)^{\beta_{2}},

(33)

is chosen to cancel the end-point singularities
tHooft:1974pnl
. Expanding (
32
) near
x=\epsilon
we find

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{\pi m_{q}^{2}}{g^{2}N_{C}}-1+\pi\beta_{1}\cot(\pi\beta_{1})\Big]\epsilon^{\beta_{1}-1}=0.

(34)

It leads to the result
\beta_{1}=(3m_{\pi}^{2}/\pi g^{2}N_{C})^{1/2}
from the expansion of the transcendental equation (
34
) for
m_{q}^{2}/g^{2}N_{C}\ll 1
. Likewise, we obtain
\beta_{2}=(3m_{\bar{q}}^{2}/\pi g^{2}N_{C})^{1/2}
from the expansion of (
32
) at the upper bound
x=1-\epsilon
.

Integrating (
32
)

M_{\parallel}^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\Big(\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{x}+\frac{m_{\bar{q}}^{2}}{1-x}\Big)\chi^{2}(x)\\
+\frac{g^{2}N_{C}}{\pi}P\!\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{0}^{1}\!dx^{\prime}\,\frac{\chi(x)[\chi(x)-\chi(x’)]}{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}},

(35)

and using the approximate solution (
36
) in the limit of small quark masses we find for the pion mass

\displaystyle M_{\pi}^{2}=g\sqrt{\frac{\pi N_{C}}{3}}\,(m_{q}+m_{\bar{q}})+\mathcal{O}\!\left((m_{q}\!+m_{\bar{q}})^{2}\right),

(36)

using the Hellmann-Feynman theorem to evaluate the second integral in (
35
). It has the same linear dependence in the quark mass as the Gell-Mann-Oakes-Renner (GMOR) relation
GellMann:1968rz
. Spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking in the ’t Hooft model occurs in the limit
N_{C}\to\infty
followed by the limit
m_{q}\to 0

tHooft:1974pnl
;
Zhitnitsky:1985um
.

III.3
The LZMV longitudinal potential

The effective LMZV potential
Li:2015zda

\displaystyle U_{\parallel}(x)=-\sigma^{2}\frac{d}{dx}\Big(x(1-x)\,\frac{d}{dx}\Big)

(37)

was introduced in the context of basis light-front quantization (BLFQ)
Vary:2009gt
;
Li:2013cga
. It generates a complete basis of orthonormal functions from the solution of (
13
)

\displaystyle\chi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\kappa}(x)=Nx^{\alpha/2}(1-x)^{\beta/2}P_{\kappa}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(1-2x),

(38)

where
\alpha=2m_{q}/\sigma
,
\beta=2m_{\bar{q}}/\sigma
,
P_{\kappa}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
is a Jacobi polynomial of order
\kappa
and
N
is a normalization factor (Appendix
A
). The longitudinal basis can be combined with the holographic LFWF basis in transverse space
Brodsky:2014yha
to perform extensive numerical computations using the BLFQ methods
Li:2017mlw
;
Xu:2019xhk
;
Qian:2020utg
, as well other computations which require a complete basis function

, including numerical evaluation in quantum computers
Kreshchuk:2020aiq
.

The potential (
37
) leads to an oscillator in the longitudinal dimension which is combined with the transverse oscillator for heavy quark masses
m_{q},m_{\bar{q}}\to m_{Q},m_{\overline{Q}}

Li:2015zda
. The recovery of the rotational invariance of the oscillator in the nonrelativistic limit leads to a precise connection of the dimensionful longitudinal coupling
\sigma
to the transverse scale
\lambda
(Appendix
B
)

\displaystyle\sigma=\frac{\lambda_{Q}}{m_{Q}+m_{\overline{Q}}},

(39)

where
\lambda_{Q}
depends on the heavy quark mass
m_{Q}
,
\lambda\to\lambda_{Q}
, to keep the ratio (
39
) constant in the heavy quark mass domain (See
IV.2
). More relevant for our present purposes, in addition to its simplicity, is the observation that in the limit of small quark masses, the potential (
37
) leads to the approximate solution (
36
) obtained in
tHooft:1974pnl
;
Bergknoff:1976xr
, thereby incorporating the breaking of chiral symmetry in the LF holographic model consistent with the virial theorem.

IV
Extended HLFQCD model

The extension of HLFQCD to include quark masses described in this article combines the transverse and longitudinal dynamics as in
Chabysheva:2012fe
;
Li:2015zda
, but keeping the physically motivated invariant mass ansatz, which is required in order to incorporate the invariant off-mass-shell dependence of the full LFWF as in our previous work. In practice, we include the longitudinal dynamics by performing an expansion of the invariant mass LFWF (
21
) using the convenient basis of complete orthonormal eigenfunctions generated by the specific potential (
37
)

\displaystyle\chi_{IM}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}\exp\Big(-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{8\lambda}\Big[\frac{\alpha^{2}}{x}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{1-x}\Big]\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa}C_{\kappa}\,\chi_{\kappa}(x).

(40)

The expansion coefficients
C_{\kappa}
are computed from the overlap

\displaystyle C_{\kappa}^{\alpha,\beta}=\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\chi_{\kappa}^{\alpha,\beta}(x)\chi_{IM}(x),

(41)

with
\langle\chi_{IM}|\chi_{IM}\rangle=\sum_{\kappa}C_{\kappa}^{2}=1
.
A rapid convergence of the invariant mass LFWF was obtained in
Chabysheva:2012fe
;
Li:2015zda
using the basis wave function (
38
), with the lowest-index Jacobi polynomial giving the dominant contribution.

In terms of (
38
) the longitudinal contribution to a meson mass is given by

M_{\parallel}^{2}/\sigma^{2}=\int_{0}^{1}\!dx\,\chi_{IM}(x)\Big(-\frac{d}{dx}\Big(x(1-x)\,\frac{d}{dx}\Big)\\
+\frac{1}{4}\Big[\frac{\alpha^{2}}{x}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{1-x}\Big]\Big)\chi_{IM}(x)\\
=\sum_{\kappa}C_{\kappa}^{2}\,\nu^{2}(\kappa,\alpha,\beta),\hskip 90.0pt

(42)

expressed of a sum of the eigenvalues

\displaystyle\nu^{2}(\kappa,\alpha,\beta)={\textstyle{\frac{1}{4}}}(\alpha+\beta+2\kappa)(2+\alpha+\beta+2\kappa),

(43)

computed in Appendix
A
. In terms of the constituent quark masses the longitudinal mode expansion (
42
) can be expressed as

M_{\parallel}^{2}=\left(m_{q}+m_{\bar{q}}\right)^{2}+\sigma\left(m_{q}+m_{\bar{q}}\right)\\
+\sum_{\kappa=1}^{\infty}C_{\kappa}^{2}\left[(2\kappa\sigma\left(m_{q}+m_{\bar{q}}\right)+\kappa(\kappa+1)\sigma^{2}\right].

(44)

The rapid convergence of (
44
) in the basis function (
38
) found in
Chabysheva:2012fe
;
Li:2015zda
and discussed below in Sec.
V
, effectively ensures that the longitudinal mass squared does not grow as
\kappa^{2}
for large
\kappa
, which would contradict linear Regge behavior.

IV.1
Chiral symmetry breaking

The chiral limit follows directly from (
44
) since all the coefficients
C_{\kappa}
vanish for
\kappa\neq 0
in this limit (see (
57
)). We obtain for the pion mass

\displaystyle M^{2}_{\pi}=\sigma(m_{u}\!+m_{d})+\mathcal{O}\!\left((m_{u}\!+m_{d})^{2}\right),

(45)

VI
Conclusions and Outlook

The light-front semiclassical approximation described in this article incorporates the confinement strength in the longitudinal direction as well as the effective scale of chiral symmetry breaking as different manifestations of the same underlying dynamics. As such, it accounts for most of the meson mass, consistent with the GMOR relation. Following
Chabysheva:2012fe
we have separated LF longitudinal and transverse dynamics and adopted from
Li:2015zda
a potential which generates a convenient basis function in the longitudinal direction. It is responsible for breaking the chiral symmetry and restoring rotational symmetry in the limit of heavy quark masses. In practice, it allows us to reduce the highly complex and non-local four-dimensional LF QCD Hamiltonian to a set of two independent second order differential equations, consistent with the local structure of semiclassical AdS equations
Narayanan:2005gh
;
Katz:2007br
.

The mass dependence of the transverse scale
\lambda
found in
Dosch:2016zdv
;
Nielsen:2018ytt
is unchanged, therefore the Regge slopes are unmodified. The changes introduced by the new approach primarily modify the lowest meson state in each Regge trajectory, and therefore the value of the extracted effective quark masses. Our previous successful results for the Regge trajectories for light and heavy quark masses are basically unchanged.

The origin and physical interpretation of the longitudinal scale
\sigma
, which has the role of a condensate
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle
, remains to be explored, but as we have shown in (
47
), it is related to the dimensionful constant
g
in QCD
(1+1)
at large
N_{C}
. In lattice QCD the structure of the vacuum is sampled in the Euclidean region where non-trivial gauge field configurations provide a mechanism for symmetry breaking through the Banks-Casher relation,
\langle\bar{\psi}\psi\rangle=-\pi\rho(0)
, with
\rho(0)
the density or Dirac-zero modes
Banks:1979yr
;
McNeile:2012xh
. However, the relation between chiral symmetry breaking and confinement has remained elusive. In this context, it has been argued that the chiral condensate, usually viewed as a constant mass scale which fills all spacetime, is instead contained within hadrons, therefore a property of hadron dynamics
Brodsky:2012ku
;
Mannheim:2019lss
.

The fact that the nonzero pion mass is a consequence of longitudinal LF confinement is a remarkable result. One would expect, for example, from the two-dimensional ’t Hooft
tHooft:1974pnl
or Schwinger
Bergknoff:1976xr
models in light-front coordinates, that the pion mass from the mechanism of chiral symmetry breaking originates in the longitudinal component of the wave function
Zhitnitsky:1985um
, since the kinetic quark mass terms only depend on the longitudinal variable. However, this would not be the case if the transverse kinetic and potential energy of the pion would not exactly cancel as required by the superconformal structure of the transverse LF Hamiltonian. The pion plays a special role as a hadronic state of zero mass in the chiral limit. Since it does not have a baryonic partner, the pion breaks the meson-baryon hadron supersymmetry
Dosch:2015nwa
. In contrast, the proton mass (as well as the mass of radial and orbital hadron excited states) is generated by the addition of the transverse kinetic and transverse potential energy with a small contribution from the longitudinal dynamics, in agreement with the Regge phenomenology of the hadron mass spectrum.

Appendix A
Jacobi polynomials and solution to the longitudinal Hamiltonian equation

The Jacobi polynomials
P_{\kappa}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(z)
are solution of the differential equation

(1-z)^{-\alpha}(1+z)^{-\beta}\frac{d}{dz}\Big((1-z)^{\alpha+1}(1+z)^{\beta+1}\frac{d}{dz}\,u(z)\Big)\\
+\kappa(\kappa+a+b+1)u(z)=0,

(51)

which is orthogonal in the interval
[-1,1]
with weight
(1-z)^{\alpha}(1-z)^{\beta}
. Performing the change of variable
z=1-2x
we find

x^{-\alpha}(1-x)^{-\beta}\frac{d}{dx}\Big(x^{\alpha+1}(1-x)^{\beta+1}\frac{d}{dx}\,u(x)\Big)\\
+\kappa(\kappa+a+b+1)u(x)=0,

(52)

with the solution
P_{\kappa}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(1-2x)
orthogonal in the interval
[0,1]
with weight
x^{\alpha}(1-x)^{\beta}
.

Consider now the eigenvalue equation

\left(-\frac{d}{dx}\Big(x(1-x)\,\frac{d}{dx}\Big)+\frac{1}{4}\Big[\frac{\alpha^{2}}{x}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{1-x}\Big]\right)v(x)=\nu^{2}v(x).

(53)

Writing
v(x)=x^{\alpha/2}(1-x)x^{\beta/2}w(x)
and substituting in (
53
) we find that
w(x)=P_{\kappa}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(1-2x)
.
Therefore the normalized solution to (
53
)

\displaystyle\chi^{\alpha,\beta}_{\kappa}(x)=Nx^{\alpha/2}(1-x)^{\beta/2}P_{\kappa}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(1-2x),

(54)

with eigenvalues

\displaystyle\nu^{2}={\textstyle{\frac{1}{4}}}(\alpha+\beta+2\kappa)(2+\alpha+\beta+2\kappa),

(55)

and normalization

N=\sqrt{1+\alpha+\beta+2\kappa}\,\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(1+\kappa)\Gamma(1+\alpha+\beta+\kappa)}{\Gamma(1+\alpha+\kappa)\Gamma(1+\beta+\kappa)}},

(56)

given in terms of the Gamma function. If
\alpha=\beta=0
the solution (
54
) is reduced to

\displaystyle\chi^{0,0}_{\kappa}(x)=\sqrt{1+2\kappa}\,P_{\kappa}(1-2x),

(57)

where
P_{\kappa}(x)
is a Legendre polynomial.

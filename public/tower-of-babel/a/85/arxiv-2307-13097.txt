Geometric properties for a class of deformed trace functions
Frank Hansen

Abstract
We investigate geometric properties for a class of trace functions expressed in terms of the deformed logarithmic and exponential functions. We extend earlier results of Epstein, Hiai, Carlen and Lieb. them MSC2010 classification: 47A63; 15A90 Key words and phrases: trace function, deformed trace function, convexity, entropy.



1
Preliminaries

Tsallis
[
10
]
generalised in 1988 the standard Bolzmann-Gibbs entropy to a non-extensive quantity
S_{p}(\rho)
depending on a parameter
p.
In the quantum version it is given by

S_{p}(\rho)=\frac{1-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \rho^{p}}{p-1}\hskip 20.00003ptp\neq 1,

where
\rho
is a density matrix. It has the property that

S_{p}(\rho)\to S(\rho)

for
p\to 1,
where
S(\rho)=-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \rho\log\rho
is the von Neumann entropy.

1.1
The deformed logarithm and exponential

The Tsallis entropy may be written on a similar form

S_{p}(\rho)=-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \rho\log_{p}\rho,

where the deformed logarithm
\log_{p}
defined for positive
x
is given by

\log_{p}x=\int_{1}^{x}t^{p-2}\,dt=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{x^{p-1}-1}{p-1}&p\neq 1\\[10.76385pt]
\log x&p=1.\end{array}\right.

The deformed logarithm is also denoted the
p
-logarithm. The range of the
p
-logarithm is given by the intervals

\begin{array}[]{ll}\bigl(-(p-1)^{-1},\infty\bigr)&\text{for}\quad p>1\\[6.45831pt]
\bigl(-\infty,-(p-1)^{-1}\bigr)&\text{for}\quad p<1\\[6.45831pt]
\bigl(-\infty,\infty\bigr)&\text{for}\quad p=1.\end{array}

The inverse function
\exp_{p}
(denoted the
p
-exponential) is always positive and given by

\exp_{p}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(x(p-1)+1)^{1/(p-1)}&\text{for $p>1$ and $x>-(p-1)^{-1}$}\\[6.45831pt]
(x(p-1)+1)^{1/(p-1)}&\text{for $p<1$ and $x<-(p-1)^{-1}$}\\[6.45831pt]
\exp x&\text{for $p=1$ and $x\in\mathbf{R}.$ }\end{array}\right.

The
p
-logarithm and the
p
-exponential functions converge, respectively, to the logarithmic and the exponential functions for
p\to 1.

We note that

(1)

\frac{d}{dx}\log_{p}(x)=x^{p-2}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{and}\hskip 20.00003pt\frac{d}{dx}\exp_{p}(x)=\exp_{p}(x)^{2-p}\,.

We will also need the following lemma.

Lemma 1.1
.

Take arbitrary
p\in\mathbf{R}.
Independent of
x>0,
we have

\log_{p}x^{q}=q\log_{\alpha}x,

where
\alpha=1+q(p-1).
Furthermore, take arbitrary
q\neq 0
and set
\beta=1+(p-1)/q\,.
For any
x\in\mathbf{R}
in the domain of
\exp_{q}\,,
we obtain that
qx
is in the domain of
\exp_{\beta}
and that

(\exp_{p}x)^{q}=\exp_{\beta}(qx).

Proof.

We substitute
u=t^{1/q}
(thus
t=u^{q})
in

\log_{p}x^{q}=\int_{1}^{x^{q}}t^{p-2}\,dt

and note that
du=q^{-1}t^{(1-q)/q}\,dt.
Therefore,
dt=qt^{(q-1)/q}\,du
and thus

\log_{p}x^{q}=\int_{1}^{x}u^{q(p-2)}qu^{q-1}\,du=q\int_{1}^{x}u^{q(p-1)-1}\,du.

Since
q(p-1)-1=q(p-1)+1-2=\alpha-2,
the first statement follows. The definition of
\beta
implies
q/(p-1)=1/(\beta-1).
There are now four cases depending on
q>0,

q<0,

p>1
and
p<1.
In all four cases it follows that
qx
is in the domain of
\exp_{\beta}.
We finally obtain from the first result in the lemma that

\log_{\beta}(\exp_{p}x)^{q}=q\log_{p}(\exp_{p}x)=qx

and therefore
(\exp_{p}x)^{q}=\exp_{\beta}(qx).

∎

1.2
Convexity and min-max theorems

An important tool in our investigation is taken from convex analysis. These techniques are used in engineering, automatic control, signal processing, resource allocation, portfolio theory, and numerous other fields. We are in particular using that partial minimisation of a convex function is convex
[
2
, Section 3.2.5]
.
This technique was successfully applied by Carlen and Lieb in the investigation of trace functions
[
3
]
. We provide the proof as a convenience to the reader.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let
f\colon X\times Y\to\mathbf{R}
be a function of two variable and set

g(y)=\inf_{x\in X}f(x,y)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pth(y)=\sup_{x\in X}f(x,y)

for
x\in X.

(i)

If
f(x,y)
is jointly convex, then
g
is convex.

(ii)

If
f(x,y)
is convex in the second variable, then
h
is convex.

(iii)

If
f(x,y)
is concave in the second variable, then
g
is concave.

(iv)

If
f(x,y)
is jointly concave, then
h
is concave.

Proof.

Take
\varepsilon>0
and elements
y_{1},y_{2}\in Y.
Pick
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
such that

g(y_{1})\geq f(x_{1},y_{1})+\varepsilon\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptg(y_{2})\geq f(x_{2},y_{2})+\varepsilon.

Then

\begin{array}[]{l}g(\lambda y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2})\leq f(\lambda x_{1}+(1-\lambda)x_{2},\lambda y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2})\\[6.45831pt]
\leq\lambda f(x_{1},y_{1})+(1-\lambda)f(x_{2},y_{2})\leq\lambda g(y_{1})+(1-\lambda)g(y_{2})-\varepsilon,\end{array}

so
g
is convex. Pick to
\lambda y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2}\in Y
and
\varepsilon>0
an element
z\in X
such that

h(\lambda y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2})\leq f(x,\lambda y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2})+\varepsilon.

Then

\begin{array}[]{l}h(\lambda y_{1}+(1-\lambda)y_{2})\leq\lambda f(x,y_{1})+(1-\lambda)f(x,y_{2})+\varepsilon\\[6.45831pt]
\leq\lambda h(y_{1})+(1-\lambda)h(y_{2})+\varepsilon,\end{array}

so
h
is convex. The cases
(iii)
and
(iv)
follow by considering
-f(x,y).

∎

1.3
The Young tracial inequalities

The following inequalities are known as the tracial Young inequalities. We prefer to prove them as below.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
A
and
B
be positive definite matrices. Then

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ A^{p}B^{1-p}\leq p{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ A+(1-p){\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ B\hskip 20.00003pt0\leq p\leq 1

and

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ A^{p}B^{1-p}\geq p{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ A+(1-p){\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ B\hskip 20.00003ptp\leq 0,\,p\geq 1.

Proof.

Let first
0\leq p\leq 1.
We may write

\begin{array}[]{rl}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ A^{p}B^{1-p}&={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ L_{A}^{p}R_{B}^{1-p}I=S_{f}^{I}(A,B)\\[6.45831pt]
&\leq{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(pL_{A}+(1-p)R_{B}\bigr)I=p{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ A+(1-p){\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ B,\end{array}

where
f(t)=t^{p}
for
t>0,
and
L_{A}
and
R_{B}
are the left and right multiplication operators. The first equality above in terms of the quasi-entropy
S_{f}^{I}(A,B)
follows since
L_{A}
and
R_{B}
commute, and the first inequality in the proposition then follows from the geometric-arithmetic mean inequality. Since Jensen’s inequality reverses for the extensions of a chord (corresponding to the cases
p\leq 0
or
p\geq 1),
the second inequality of the proposition follows.
∎

2
Variational expressions

We take the following variational representations from our paper
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
with a slightly simplified proof.

Proposition 2.1
.

For positive definite operators
X
and
Y
we have

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ Y=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\max_{X>0}\bigl\{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{2-q}\left(\log_{q}X-\log_{q}Y\right)\bigr\},&q\leq 2,\\[10.76385pt]
\displaystyle\min_{X>0}\bigl\{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{2-q}\left(\log_{q}X-\log_{q}Y\right)\bigr\},&q>2.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We learned in Proposition
1.3
that

\begin{array}[]{rl}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{p}Y^{1-p}&\leq p{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X+(1-p){\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ Y,\qquad 0\leq p\leq 1,\\[8.61108pt]
{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{p}Y^{1-p}&\geq p{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X+(1-p){\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ Y,\qquad p\leq 0,\,p\geq 1.\end{array}

By combining the first inequality for
0\leq p<1
with the case
p>1
in the second, we obtain

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ Y\geq{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-\frac{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{p}Y^{1-p}}{1-p}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptp\geq 0,\,p\neq 1,

while the case
p\leq 0
gives the inequality

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ Y\leq{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-\frac{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{p}Y^{1-p}}{1-p}\,,\hskip 20.00003ptp\leq 0.

For
X=Y
the above inequalities become equalities.
Setting
q=2-p,
the first range (
p\geq 0,

p\neq 1)
is transformed to the range
(q\leq 2,

q\neq 1),
while the second range
(p\leq 0)
is transformed to the range
(q\geq 2).
Since
p=2-q
and
1-p=q-1
we obtain

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ Y=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\max_{X>0}\Bigl\{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-\frac{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{2-q}\left(X^{q-1}-Y^{q-1}\right)}{q-1}\Bigr\},&q\in(-\infty,2],\,q\neq 1,\\[10.76385pt]
\displaystyle\min_{X>0}\Bigl\{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-\frac{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{2-q}\left(X^{q-1}-Y^{q-1}\right)}{q-1}\Bigr\},&q\in[2,\infty).\end{array}\right.

By using the definition of the deformed logarithm we note that

\frac{X^{q-1}-Y^{q-1}}{q-1}=\log_{q}(X)-\log_{q}(Y),

and by inserting this in the expressions above, we obtain the desired statements of the proposition, except for
q=1.

We may finally let
q
tend to one in the first inequality and obtain the variational expression

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ Y=\max_{X>0}\Bigl\{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-{{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X\left(\log X-\log Y\right)}\Bigr\}

by continuity. This completes the proof.
∎

Note that the last statement in the above proof entails the inequality

S(X\mid Y)\geq{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ (X-Y)

for the relative quantum entropy
S(X\mid Y).

2.1
Further preliminaries

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
H
be an arbitrary matrix, take
L\geq 0,
and choose exponents
p
and
s
such that
s>0.

We consider the trace function

\psi_{L,H}(A)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(L+H^{*}A^{p}H\bigr)^{s}

defined in positive definite matrices.
\psi_{L,H}(A)
is convex (respectively concave) for arbitrary
H
and
L\geq 0,
if and only if it is convex (respectively concave) for arbitrary
H
and
L=0.

Proof.

By considering block matrices

\hat{H}=\begin{pmatrix}L^{1/2}&0\\
H&0\end{pmatrix}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\hat{A}=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\
0&A\end{pmatrix}

we obtain

\hat{H}^{*}\hat{A}^{p}\hat{H}=\begin{pmatrix}L+H^{*}A^{p}H&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Since
s>0
we obtain in addition

\bigl(\hat{H}^{*}\hat{A}^{p}\hat{H}\bigr)^{s}=\begin{pmatrix}\bigl(L+H^{*}A^{p}H\bigr)^{s}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},

since it is meaningful to set
0^{s}=0.
Indeed, for
\varepsilon>0
we have

\varepsilon^{s}=\exp\bigl(s\log\varepsilon\bigr),

and this quantity tends to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0.
Therefore,

\psi_{L,H}(A)=\psi_{0,\hat{H}}(\hat{A})
and the statement follows.
∎

If
s<0
there exist examples in two by two matrices such that
\psi_{0,H}(A)
is convex while
\psi_{L,H}(A)
is not.

3
The main trace function

Let
H
be an invertible contraction and
A
positive definite. Then

H^{*}\log_{p}(A)H>\frac{-1}{p-1}H^{*}H\geq\frac{-1}{p-1}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002ptp>1

and

H^{*}\log_{p}(A)H<\frac{-1}{p-1}H^{*}H\leq\frac{-1}{p-1}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002ptp<1.

Therefore,
H^{*}\log_{p}(A)H
belongs to the domain of the
p
-exponential. This is true even if
H
is not invertible since
\exp_{p}(0)=1.

Therefore,

\exp_{p}\bigl(L+H^{*}\log_{p}(A)H\bigr)

is well-defined and positive for arbitrary contractions
H
and
p\neq 1,
provided
L\geq 0
when
p>1,
and
L\leq 0
when
p<1.
In both cases we may define the trace function

(2)

\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \left[\exp_{p}\bigl(L+H^{*}\log_{p}(A)H\bigr)^{q}\right]

for arbitrary exponents
q.
We furthermore obtain the expression

(3)

\begin{array}[]{rl}\varphi_{p,q}^{L}(A)&=\displaystyle{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl[I+(p-1)L+(p-1)H^{*}\frac{A^{p-1}-I}{p-1}H\bigr]^{q/(p-1)}\\[10.76385pt]
&=\displaystyle{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl[I-H^{*}H+(p-1)L+H^{*}A^{p-1}H\bigr]^{q/(p-1)}.\end{array}

Note that
(p-1)L\geq 0
in both cases. By using Lemma
2.2
we obtain the following:

Corollary 3.1
.

Suppose
q/(p-1)>0.
Then
\varphi_{p,q}^{L}(A)
is convex (concave) if and only if the trace function
A\to{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(H^{*}A^{p}H\bigr)^{q/(p-1)}
is convex (concave).

We shall finally explore yet another expression for the main trace function. Given the expression in (
2
) and setting

(4)

\beta=1+\frac{p-1}{q}

we obtain

(5)

\varphi_{p,q}^{L}(A)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \exp_{\beta}\bigl(qL+qH^{*}\log_{p}(A)H\big),

where we used Lemma
1.1
.
By replacing
q
with
\beta
in Proposition
2.1
and setting

(6)

F(X,A)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{2-\beta}\left(\log_{\beta}X-\log_{\beta}Y\right),

where
Y=\exp_{\beta}\bigl(qL+qH^{*}\log_{p}(A)H\big),
we obtain that

(7)

\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\sup_{X>0}F(X,A)&\beta\leq 2,\\[10.76385pt]
\displaystyle\inf_{X>0}F(X,A)&\beta>2.\end{array}\right.

This is the main variational expression to be used. We next calculate

\begin{array}[]{l}F(X,A)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{2-\beta}\left(\log_{\beta}X-\log_{\beta}Y\right)\\[8.61108pt]
={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{2-\beta}\left(\log_{\beta}X-qL-qH^{*}\log_{p}(A)H\right)\\[8.61108pt]
=\displaystyle{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{2-\beta}\left(\frac{X^{\beta-1}-I}{\beta-1}-qL-qH^{*}\frac{A^{p-1}-I}{p-1}H\right)\\[12.91663pt]
=\displaystyle{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X-\frac{1}{\beta-1}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(X-X^{2-\beta}-(p-1)L-X^{2-\beta}H^{*}(A^{p-1}-I)H\bigr)\\[12.91663pt]
=\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{1}{\beta-1}\right){\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X+G(X,A),\end{array},

where we used
q/(p-1)=1/(\beta-1)
and set

(8)

G(X,A)=\frac{1}{\beta-1}{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \Bigl(X^{2-\beta}(I-H^{*}H+(p-1)L)+X^{2-\beta}H^{*}A^{p-1}H\Bigr).

The first term in
F(X,A)
is linear, so we only have to consider convexity or concavity of
G(X,A).
Note as before that
(p-1)L\geq 0.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H
be a contraction and consider for arbitrary real
q
the trace function
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
defined in (
2
).
If
q/(1-p)>0
and
\varphi_{p,q}^{L}(A)
is convex (respectively concave) for arbitrary contractions
H,
then so is the trace function
\varphi_{2-p,q}^{-L}(A).

Proof.

We may without loss of generality assume that
H
is invertible.
By using the calculation in (
3
) we obtain

(9)

t^{-q}\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(tA)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(t^{1-p}(I-H^{*}H+(p-1)L)+H^{*}A^{p-1}H\bigr)^{q/(p-1)}

for
t>0,
Thus, by letting
t\to 0
for
p<1
or letting
t\to\infty
for
p>1,
we obtain that the trace function

A\to{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(H^{*}A^{p-1}H\bigr)^{q/(p-1)}

is convex (respectively concave). It is no longer necessary to assume that
H
is a contraction, and since
H^{*}A^{p-1}H
is invertible, we can raise it to any non-zero exponent.
Therefore, by inversion we obtain that the trace function

A\to{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(H^{*}A^{1-p}H\bigr)^{q/(1-p)}

is convex (respectively concave) for arbitrary
H.
In particular, if
H
is a contraction we obtain by a small
calculation the identity

\varphi_{2-p,q}^{-L}(A)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(I-H^{*}H+(p-1)L+H^{*}A^{1-p}H\bigr)^{q/(1-p)}.

Since
q/(1-p)>0
we obtain from Lemma
3.2
that also
\varphi_{2-p,q}^{-L}(A)
is convex (respectively concave).
∎

3.1
The strategy of the proof

We shall determine parameter values
p
and
q
such that
F(X,A)
is either concave/convex or just concave/convex in the second variable. To do this we use that the functions
t\to t^{p}
are operator concave, if and only if
0\leq p\leq 1,
and operator convex, if and only if
-1\leq p\leq 0
or
1\leq p\leq 2.
It may be of interest to note that the same parameter conditions apply, if we only require matrix convexity or matrix concavity of order two, cf.
[
6
, Proposition 3.1]
.
We also make use of Lieb’s concavity theorem
[
8
, Corollary 1.1]
stating that the trace functions

(10)

(X,A)\to{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ X^{p}H^{*}A^{q}H

are concave if
p,q\geq 0
and
p+q\leq 1.
Ando’s theorem states
[
1
, Corollary 6.3]
that the trace function in (
10
) for an arbitrary matrix
H,
is convex for either
-1\leq p,q\leq 0,
or for

\hskip 10.00002pt-1\leq p\leq 0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt1-p\leq q\leq 2,

where obviously
p
and
q
may be interchanged in the condition.
Since
H=I
is a possibility, we realise that concavity of
\varphi_{p,q}(A)
requires
0\leq q\leq 1,
while convexity of
\varphi_{p,q}(A)
requires
q\leq 0
or
q\geq 1.

Since we intend to eventually use operator convexity/concavity of the function
t\to t^{p},
we are restricted to the cases

-1\leq p-1\leq 2\hskip 10.00002pt\text{or equivalently}\hskip 10.00002pt0\leq p\leq 3.

Note that if
\beta=1,
then
p=1.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
K
be a positive definite
n\times n
matrix, and let
H
be any
n\times n
matrix. We may define the operator map

\psi_{p}^{s}(A)=\bigl(K+H^{*}A^{p}H\bigr)^{s}

in positive definite
n\times n
matrices for exponents
p
and
s.
If
-1\leq p\leq 0
and
-1\leq s\leq 0,
then
\psi_{p}^{s}(A)
is concave.

Proof.

We first consider the case
-1\leq p\leq 0
and
s=-1.
Since

\psi_{p}^{-1}(A)=\bigl(K+H^{*}A^{p}H\bigr)^{-1}=K^{-1/2}\bigl(I+L^{*}A^{p}L\bigr)^{-1}K^{-1/2},

where
L=HK^{-1/2},
we may assume
K=I.
We may also without loss generality assume that
H
is invertible. We then obtain

\bigl(I+H^{*}A^{p}H\bigr)^{-1}=\frac{(H^{*}A^{p}H)^{-1}}{(H^{*}A^{p}H)^{-1}+I}=\frac{H^{-1}A^{-p}(H^{-1})^{*}}{H^{-1}A^{-p}(H^{-1})^{*}+I}

by an elementary calculation.
Since the map
A\to H^{-1}A^{-p}(H^{-1})^{*}
is concave and the function
t\to t(1+t)^{-1}
is operator monotone and operator concave, we obtain that
A\to\bigl(I+H^{*}A^{p}H\bigr)^{-1}
is concave. That is,
\psi_{p}^{-1}(A)
is concave. Since the function
t\to t^{\alpha}
is both operator monotone and operator concave for
0\leq\alpha\leq 1,
it follows that
\psi_{p}^{s}(A)
is concave for
-1\leq s\leq 0.

∎

The main theorem

Theorem 4.1
.

The trace function
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
defined in (
2
) has the following geometric properties depending on the matrix
L
and the parameters
p
and
q.

\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
is concave in positive definite
A
for

(11b)

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 1,

\displaystyle\,L\leq 0,

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\,q\leq 1.

(11c)

\displaystyle 1\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 2,

\displaystyle\,L\geq 0,

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\,q\leq 1.

\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
is convex in positive definite
A
for

(11d)

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 1,

\displaystyle\,L\leq 0,

\displaystyle\,q\leq 0.

(11e)

\displaystyle 1\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 2,

\displaystyle\,L\geq 0,

\displaystyle\,q\leq 0.

(11f)

\displaystyle 2\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 3,

\displaystyle\,L\geq 0,

\displaystyle\,q\geq 1.

Proof.

We divide the proof following the statement’s five cases.

(a)

Take
0\leq p<1,

L\leq 0,
and
1-p\leq q\leq 1.
Then

0\leq\beta=1+(p-1)/q\leq p<1.

Since
\varphi_{p,q}(A)=\sup_{X>0}F(X,A),
we may derive that
\varphi_{p,q}(A)
is concave if
F(X,A)
is jointly concave. Since
1<2-\beta\leq 2
the first term in
G(X,A)
is concave (note that
\beta<1).
Since
-1\leq p-1\leq 0
and
1-(p-1)\leq 2-\beta\leq 2,
we realise by Ando’s convexity theorem that
\varphi_{p,q}(A)
concave.

Next take
0\leq p\leq 1,L\leq 0,
and
0\leq q\leq 1-p.
That is
-1\leq s\leq 0,
where
s=q/(p-1)\,.
It then follows from (
3
) combined with Corollary
3.3
that

\varphi_{p,q}(A)
concave (even without the trace).

(b)

Take
1<p\leq 2,

L\geq 0,
and
0<q\leq p-1.
Then

\beta=1+\frac{p-1}{q}\geq 2

and thus
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)=\inf_{X>0}F(X,A).
We may thus derive that
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
is concave if
G(X,A)
is concave in the second variable. This is so since
0\leq p-1\leq 1.

Next, take
1<p\leq 2,

L\geq 0,
and
p-1\leq q\leq 1.
Then

1<p\leq\beta=1+\frac{p-1}{q}\leq 2

and thus
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)=\sup_{X>0}F(X,A).
We may thus derive that
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
is concave if
G(X,A)
is concave. Since
\beta>1
and
0\leq 2-\beta\leq 1,
the first term in
G(X,A)
is concave. The second term is concave by Lieb’s concavity theorem since
0\leq p-1\leq 1
and
2-\beta+p-1\leq 1.
The last inequality is satisfied since
p\leq\beta.
These two cases taken together proves
(b).

(c)

We first prove that
(d)
implies
(c).

Take
1<p<2,

L\geq 0,
and
q<0,
and note that
0\leq 2-p<1.
Since
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
is convex by
(d)
and
q/(1-p)>0,
we obtain by Lemma
3.2
that
\varphi^{-L}_{2-p,q}(A)
is convex. This is equivalent to saying that
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
is convex for
0\leq p\leq 1,

L\leq 0,
and
q<0.

(d)

By continuity we may assume
1<p\leq 2,

L\geq 0,
and
q<0.
Therefore,
\beta=1+(p-1)/q<1
and thus
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)=\sup_{X>0}F(X,A).
We obtain that
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
is convex, if
G(X,A)
is convex in the second variable. This is so since
\beta<1
and
0\leq p-1\leq 1.

(e)

Take
2\leq p\leq 3,

L\geq 0,
and
1\leq q\leq p-1.
Then

2\leq\beta=1+(p-1)/q\leq p\leq 3.

Since
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)=\inf_{X>0}F(X,A),
we may derive that
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
is convex if
G(X,A)
is convex. Since
-1\leq 2-\beta\leq 0
this follows by Ando’s convexity theorem if in addition

1-(2-\beta)\leq p-1\leq 2,

and this is satisfied since
\beta\leq p.

Take next
2\leq p\leq 3,

L\geq 0,
and
q\geq p-1.
Then

1<\beta=1+(p-1)/q\leq 2.

Since
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)=\sup_{X>0}F(X,A),
we may derive that
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A)
is convex if
G(X,A)
is convex in the second variable. But this is so since
\beta>1
and
1\leq p-1\leq 2.
These two cases taken together proves
(e).

∎

The special case
q=1
was proved in
[
9
, Corollary 2.3]
.

4.1
Comparison with the literature

The trace functions
\Upsilon_{p,q}(A)
were introduced and studied by Carlen and Lieb in
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
and later with a different definition (by setting
s=q/p)
in
[
5
, Proposition 5]
. We adopt and slightly generalise the latter definition by setting

(12)

\Upsilon^{K}_{p,s}(A)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(K+H^{*}A^{p}H\bigr)^{s},

where
K\geq 0,

H
is arbitrary, and
A
is positive definite. By replacing
p
with
p-1
and applying Corollary
3.1
, we obtain the following corollary to Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.2
.

The trace function
\Upsilon^{K}_{p,s}(A)
defined in (
12
) has the following geometric properties depending on the matrix
K
and the parameters
p
and
s.

\Upsilon^{K}_{p,q}(A)
is concave in positive definite
A
for

(13b)

\displaystyle-1\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 0,

\displaystyle p^{-1}\leq

\displaystyle\,s\leq 0.

(13c)

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 1,

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\,s\leq p^{-1}.

\Upsilon^{K}_{p,q}(A)
is convex in positive definite
A
for

(13d)

\displaystyle-1\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 0,

\displaystyle\,s\geq 0.

(13e)

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 1,

\displaystyle\,s\leq 0.

(13f)

\displaystyle 1\leq

\displaystyle\,p\leq 2,

\displaystyle\,s\geq p^{-1}.

Proof.

To a given
K\geq 0
we set
L=(p-1)^{-1}K.
Then
L\leq 0
for
p<1
and
L\geq 0
for
p>1.
By replacing
L
with
t^{p-1}L
in equation (
9
) we obtain

t^{-q}\varphi^{t^{p-1}L}_{p,q}(tA)={\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(t^{1-p}(I-H^{*}H)+(p-1)L+H^{*}A^{p-1}H\bigr)^{q/(p-1)}

and this expression tends to

{\rm Tr\hskip-1.99997pt}\penalty\ \bigl(K+H^{*}A^{p-1}H\bigr)^{q/(p-1)}=\Upsilon^{K}_{p-1,s}(A),\hskip 10.00002pts=\frac{q}{p-1}

by letting
t\to 0
in the case
p<1,
and letting
t\to\infty
in the case
p>1.
With these choices we realise that
\Upsilon^{K}_{p-1,s}(A)
has the same geometric properties as
\varphi^{L}_{p,q}(A).
We may now replace
p
with
p+1
and obtain that
\Upsilon^{K}_{p,s}(A)
has the same geometric properties as does
\varphi^{L}_{p+1,q}(A),
where
s=q/p\,.

In particular,

\Upsilon^{K}_{p,s}(A)
is concave for
-1\leq p\leq 0
and
0\leq q\leq 1,
equivalent to
p^{-1}\leq s\leq 0.

Furthermore,
\Upsilon^{K}_{p,s}(A)
is convex for
-1\leq p\leq 0
and

equivalent to
s\geq 0.
Likewise,
\Upsilon^{K}_{p,s}(A)
is convex for
0\leq p\leq 1
and
q\leq 0,
equivalent to
s\leq 0.
Finally,
\Upsilon^{K}_{p,s}(A)
is convex for
1\leq p\leq 2
and
q\geq 1,
equivalent to
s\geq p^{-1}.

∎

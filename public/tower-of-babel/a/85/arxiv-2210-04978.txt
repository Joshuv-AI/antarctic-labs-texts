Discrete homogeneity and ends of manifolds
Vitalij A. Chatyrko and Alexandre Karassev

Abstract
. It is shown that a connected non-compact metrizable manifold of dimension ≥ 2 \geq 2 is strongly discrete homogeneous if and only if it has one end (in the sense of Freudenthal compactification).

Primary 57S05; Secondary 54F15, 54D35

1.
Introduction

All spaces are assumed to be metrizable, and all maps continuous.
Recall that a topological space
X
is called
n
-homogeneous
for a positive integer
n
if
for any two subsets
A
and
B
of
X
with
|A|=|B|=n
there exists a homeomorphism
f\colon X\to X
, such that
f(A)=B

[
Bu
]
. Here, for a set
S
by

we denote its cardinality. Further,

is called
strongly
n
-homogeneous
if
any bijection

between two such subsets extends to a homeomorphism

.

Recall that a family of subsets
\mathcal{D}
of a topological space

is called

if each point

has a neighbourhood

which intersects at most one set from
\mathcal{D}
. If all elements of
\mathcal{D}
are singletons, we obtain a definition of a

of

. Note that this definition implies that all discrete subsets of

are closed in

The following stronger properties of topological spaces were considered in
[
ChK
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A space

is called

•

discrete homogeneous (br. DH) if for any two discrete subsets

and

of

;

and

of

and any bijection

onto itself.

Note that a compact space is (strongly) discrete homogeneous iff it is (strongly)
n
-homogeneous for any

However, any infinite discrete space

is strongly
n
-homogeneous for every positive integer

, but not discrete homogeneous.

We say that a homeomorphism

if

is the identity on

Recall also that a space
X
is called

By an

Remark 1.2
.

It was shown in

that a connected strongly locally homogeneous space, no two-point subset of which separates it, is strongly

It was shown in

In

In this paper we answer the above question.

2.
Ends of spaces

Definition 2.1
.

A space is called a generalized continuum if it is locally compact,
\sigma
-compact, connected, and locally connected space.

Note that in the literature one can find a slightly different definition of generalized continuum. Namely, the local connectedness condition is often omitted.

Example 2.2
.

Any connected manifold is a generalized continuum.

Let
X
be a generalized continuum as in Definition
2.1
.
There exists a sequence (generally speaking, not unique) of compact subsets
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
X
such that
X_{n}\subset{\rm int}X_{n+1}
for all
n=1,2,\dots
and
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}=X
.

Definition 2.3
.

By an end of
X
with respect to
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
we mean a sequence of subsets
\{Y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that for all
n
the set
Y_{n}
is a non-empty connected component of
X\setminus X_{n}
, and
Y_{n+1}\subset Y_{n}
.

It is easy to see that if a generalized continuum is compact than it has no ends.

We call a connected component
C
of
X\setminus X_{n}

bounded
if there exists
m
such that
C\subset X_{m}.
Otherwise,
C
is called
unbounded
.

Remark 2.4
.

Below we summarize some facts about connected components of
X\setminus X_{n},

n=1,2,\dots

(a)

The number of connected components
C
of
X\setminus X_{n}
such that
C\cap(X\setminus X_{n+1})\neq\varnothing

is finite.

(b)

If
X
is non-compact, there exists at least one unbounded component of
X\setminus X_{n}
.

(c)

If
X
is non-compact, each connected component of
X\setminus X_{n+1}
is contained in some connected component of
X\setminus X_{n}
, and each unbounded component of
X\setminus X_{n}
contains at least one unbounded component of
X\setminus X_{n+1}
. This implies that any non-compact generalized continuum has at least one end.

Remark 2.5
.

(a)

A generalized continuum has no ends iff it is compact.

(b)

A generalized continuum has at most one end iff each of its compact subsets is contained in a compact subset with connected complement.

The collection of all ends of
X
is denoted by
E(X)
. Let
F(X)=X\cup E(X)
be the disjoint union of
X
and the set of its ends. For each open set
U
let

E_{U}=\{E=\{Y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in E(X)\colon Y_{n}\subset U\mbox{ for some }n\}.

One can introduce a base of topology on
F(X)
consisting of sets of the form
E_{U}\cup U
for all open subsets
U
of
X.
It turns out that
F(X)
, endowed with this topology, is a compactification of
X
, called the Freudenthal compactification
[
F
]
. Moreover,
F(X)
and
E(X)
do not depend on the choice of the sequence
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}.
Note that if
|E(X)|=1
then
F(X)
is the Alexandrov’s one-point compactification of
X
.

The following fact is well-known. We include its proof for completeness.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X
be a generalized continuum and
f\colon X\to X
a homeomorphism. There exists a homeomorphism
\overline{f}\colon F(X)\to F(X),
extending
f.

Proof.

For all
x\in X
put
\overline{f}(x)=f(x).
Let
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of compact subsets
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
X
such that
X_{n}\subset{\rm int}X_{n+1}
for all
n=1,2,\dots
and
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}X_{n}=X
. Then
\{f(X_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence with the same properties. Further, if
E=\{Y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an end of
X
with respect to
\{X_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
then
\{f(Y_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an end of
X
with respect to

\{f(X_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}.
We let
\overline{f}(E)=\{f(Y_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}.
It is straightforward to check that
\overline{f}
is an autohomeomorphism of
F(X).

∎

Lemma 3.1

Let

∎

For the proof of the following see e.g.

Definition 3.3

A space

Let

4.
Discrete homogeneity

In this section, we state and prove our results.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a generalized continuum and
E=\{Y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an end of
X
. Then there exists a countable discrete subset
A
of
X
such that
\overline{A}\cap E(X)=\{E\}
, where
\overline{A}
denotes the closure of
A
in
F(X).

Proof.

For each
n=1,2,\dots
pick a point
a_{n}\in Y_{n}
and let
A=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}.
It is easy to verify that
A
satisfies the conclusion of the lemma.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

If, for a generalized continuum
X
,
|E(X)|>1
then
X
is not discrete homogeneous.

Proof.

Consider
a,b\in E(X)
such that
a\neq b
. By Lemma
4.1
, there exist two discrete countable subsets
A
and
B
of
X
such that
\overline{A}\cap E(X)=\{a\}
and
\overline{B}\cap E(X)=\{b\}
(here, for a subset
Y
of
X
, by
\overline{Y}
we denote the closure of
Y
in
F(X)
). Suppose there is a homeomorphism
f\colon X\to X
such that
f(A\cup B)=A
. Then, for a homeomorphism
\overline{f}\colon F(X)\to F(X)
extending

we have
\overline{f}(a)=a=\overline{f}(b)
, contradiction.
∎

For a metric space
X
,

and

B(x,r)=\{y\in X\colon{\rm dist}(x,y)\leq r\}.
For a collection
\mathcal{S}
of sets by

we denote the union of all sets in
\mathcal{S}

Theorem 4.3
.

An
m
-dimensional connected manifold

,

, is strongly discrete homogeneous if and only if it has at most one end.

Proof.

The “only if” part follows from Proposition
4.2
. For the other direction, let

be an

|E(M)|\leq 1
. If

has no ends, then by Remark

(c)

is compact, and hence by Remark

strongly discrete homogeneous. Assume now that

has exactly one end. Fix a metric on

[
V
]

Let

Since

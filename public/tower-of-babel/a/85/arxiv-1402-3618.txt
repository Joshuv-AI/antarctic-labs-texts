Derived Witt-Dévissage Formalism
Satya Mandal

Abstract




Abstract
:
In this article we establish some formalism of Derived
Witt-Dévissage theory for resolving subcategories of abelian categories.
Results directly apply to noetherian schemes.

1
Introduction

This is another one of a series of articles
(
[
MS1
,
MS2
,
MS3
]
) dedicated to study of derived Witt groups.
Our interest in derived Witt groups emanates from
our interest in Chow-Witt groups,
as obstruction groups for projective modules.
We would refrain from repetition of the
introductory comments and the
background discussion provided in (
[
MS1
]
).

In (
[
MS1
]
), we proved dévissave theorems
for Cohen-Macaulay affine schemes, extending the
corresponding theorem of Balmer and Walter (
[
BW
]
)
on regular schemes.
In this paper, we prove analogous dévissave theorems
for noetherian schemes
X
, with the hypothesis that
any coherent sheaf on
X
is a quotient of a locally free
sheaf on
X
. In particular, we remove the Cohen-Macaulay
condition in (
[
MS1
]
).
In the process of doing so, it became clear that
the arguments could be made formal enough,
so that they work for resolving subcategories
of abelian categories. The concept of resolving subcategories
dates back to the paper of Auslander and Bridger (
[
AB
]
).
More recently, there has been considerable amount of activities
(
e.g.

[
T1
,
T2
]
)
on resolving subcategories of
the category
Mod(A)
of finitely generated modules over
noetherian commutative rings
A
.
Much of it is directed toward the classification of such resolving
subcategories under various conditions, which is encompassed by
similar attempts to classify variety of types
of subcategories of the module categories.
The work of Benson, Iyengar and Krause (
e.g.

[
BIK
]
)
would be one of the stimulus,
where they consider subcategories of group algebras.
To the best of our knowledge, very little literature is
available on resolving subcategories of abelian categories.
We give a version of the dévissage theorem for resolving subcategories
of abelian categories, which directly applies to noetherian schemes.
From our perspective,
the results on Witt groups of noetherian schemes constitute
the essence of this article.
However, the formal version of the results
on resolving subcategories unifies the theory and
has its significance by its own rights.
In this introduction, we will
state the versions of the results for resolving subcategories,
as follows.

Suppose
{\mathscr{V}}
is a resolving subcategory (see definition
3.11
)
of an abelian category
{\mathcal{C}}
. Let
\omega
be an object
in
{\mathcal{C}}
with an injective resolution. For objects

M
in
{\mathcal{C}}
, denote
M^{*}:=Hom(M,\omega)
. Assume

{\mathscr{V}}
inherits a duality structure from
∗
and

{\mathscr{V}}
is totally
\omega
-reflexive (see definition
7.1
).
The following is a list of a few notations and facts.

1.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{V}},\omega)
denote the full subcategory of
objects in
{\mathcal{C}}
with finite
{\mathscr{V}}
-dimension.
Assume
d:=\max\{\dim_{{\mathscr{V}}}M:M\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{V}},\omega)\}<\infty
.

2.

Let

{\mathcal{A}}(\omega):=\{M\in{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{V}},\omega):Ext^{i}(M,\omega)=0~~\forall~i<d\}.

It follows that
M\mapsto M^{\vee}:=Ext^{d}(M,\omega)
is a
duality on
{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)
.

3.

D^{b}({\mathscr{V}})
would denote the bounded derived category of
complexes of objects in
{\mathscr{V}}
and
D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)}({\mathscr{V}})

would be the subcategory of complexes with homologies in

{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)
.

4.

It is a standard fact (see
3.13
)
that there is a canonical functor

\zeta:{\mathcal{B}}({\mathscr{V}},\omega)\longrightarrow D^{b}({\mathscr{V}})
,
given by (choices of)
{\mathscr{V}}
-resolutions.

5.

The restriction of
\zeta:{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)\longrightarrow D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)}({\mathscr{V}})

is a duality preserving functor.

First, we prove isomorphisms of Witt groups, as follows.

W({\mathcal{A}}(\omega),^{\vee},\pm\tilde{\varpi})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}W(D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)}({\mathcal{A}}(\omega),^{\vee},\pm\tilde{\varpi}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}W(D^{b}({\mathcal{A}}(\omega),^{\vee},\pm\tilde{\varpi})).\\

It follows from a theorem of Balmer (
[
B3
]
) that the
first and the last groups are isomorphic.
We further prove that the functor
\zeta
induces isomorphims
of Witt groups

W_{St}^{+}({\mathcal{A}}(\omega))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}W^{d}\left(D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)}({\mathscr{V}}),*,1,\varpi\right),\quad W_{St}^{-}({\mathcal{A}}(\omega))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}W^{d-2}\left(D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)}({\mathscr{V}}),*,1,-\varpi\right)

where subscript "St" corresponds to "standard"
sign convention of the duality.
Also for
n=d-1,d-3
, we have

W^{n}\left(D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}(\omega)}({\mathscr{V}},*,1,\pm\varpi)\right)=0
.
By 4-periodicity all the shifted Witt groups are determined.

These results can be applied to
noetherian schemes
X
, with
{\mathcal{C}}=Coh(X)
and
{\mathscr{V}}
as the
subcategory of locally free sheaves on
X
, provided
{\mathscr{V}}

is a resolving subcategory. This will be the case for a wide variety
of schemes
X
(see
[
H
]
),
including those that have an ample invertible sheaf. In these applications,
we assume

d:=\dim X=\max\{depth({\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}):x~{\rm is~a~closed~point}\}

(see remark
6.5
).
As a consequence, the following decomposition theorem follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
X
is a noetherian scheme, with
\dim X=d
, as in
(
2.1
) and
X^{(d)}
will denote the
set of all closed points of codimension
d
in
X
. We assume

d=\max\{depth({\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}):x~{\rm is~a~closed~point}\}=\dim X
.
Then, the homomorphisms

W^{d}\left(D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}(X)}({\mathscr{V}}(X)),*,1,\varpi\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}W^{d}(D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X,x})}({\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}),*,1,\varpi)\qquad{\rm and}

W^{d-2}\left(D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}(X)}({\mathscr{V}}(X)),*,1,-\varpi\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{x\in X^{(d)}}W^{d-2}(D^{b}_{{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{O}}_{X,x})}({\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}),*,1,-\varpi)

are isomorphims.

When
X
is regular, this is a theorem of Balmer and Walter (
[
BW
]
).
In this case,
first isomorphism
follows from such a decomposition of the corresponding
categories
and the second isomorphism would have zeros on both sides.

Before we conclude this introduction, we comment on the sense of direction of this series of articles (
[
MS1
,
MS2
,
MS3
]
).
For noetherian schemes
X
as above,
we will give a Gersten-Witt like complex of the "relative" Witt groups

W^{p}({\mathcal{D}}^{p}(X),{\mathcal{D}}^{p+1}(X))
, where
{\mathcal{D}}^{p}(X)
denotes the subcategory of the finite derived category

D^{b}(X)
, of complexes

{\mathcal{E}}_{\bullet}
with finite locally free dimension homologies

{\mathcal{H}}_{i}({\mathcal{E}}_{\bullet})
and

co\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{i}({\mathcal{E}}_{\bullet}))\geq p
. The "relative" Witt groups are defined by forming
a group of isometry classes of
S
-spaces, as in (
[
B2
]
). These results will appear subsequently.

We close this introduction,
with a few comments on the lay out of this article.
As would be expected,
the results on Witt groups of noetherian schemes would follow
from that of the resolving subcategories.
However, we made a choice to
give complete proofs for the former (§
5
,
6
)
and the proofs of the results on resolving subcategories
(§
7
) would follow similarly.
Throughout in §
5
,
6
, we remind the readers
that our proofs are formal enough to apply to §
7
.
We also give a proof of the existence of the functor

\zeta:{\mathcal{B}}(X)\longrightarrow D^{b}({\mathscr{V}}(X))
in §
3
. While
this is a standard fact among the experts, we were unable to find
references that would be of any help for the readership of this article.
While the main isomorphism theorem was dealt with in §
5

, we summarize
our results on noetherian schemes in §
6
.
In §
7
, we state our results on
resolving subcategories.

3
The functor by resolution

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme, as in (
2.1
).
When
X=Spec(A)
is affine, it is not difficult to
see that there is a natural functor

\zeta:Coh(X)\longrightarrow K^{\geq 0}({\mathscr{V}}(X))
, given by
(
choice of
)
projective resolutions of finitely generated
A
-modules
M
in
Coh(X)
.
If
M\in{\mathcal{B}}
has finite projective dimension then

\zeta(M)\in K^{b}({\mathscr{V}}(X))
.
When
X

is not affine, morphisms
f:{\mathcal{F}}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{G}}
in
Coh(X)

do not naturally lift to morphisms
of the corresponding
{\mathscr{V}}(X)
-resolutions
of
{\mathcal{F}}
and
{\mathcal{G}}
.

However,
even when
X
is not affine
there are, in stead functors

\zeta:{\mathcal{B}}\longrightarrow D^{b}({\mathscr{V}}(X))
,
(resp.
\zeta:{\mathcal{B}}(X)\longrightarrow D^{\geq 0}({\mathscr{V}}(X))
)
to the derived category,
given by (
the choices
) of
{\mathscr{V}}(X)
-resolutions
of objects in
{\mathcal{B}}
(resp. of
Coh(X)
).
While this fact is well known among the experts,
there is no suitable reference,
to the best of our knowledge, that is accessible
to a wider community.
The purpose of this section is to give a proof of this fact
for the benefit of such readers.
We point out in §
3.1
,
that one can define and prove the existence of
such a functor in the context of resolving subcategories of
abelian categories.

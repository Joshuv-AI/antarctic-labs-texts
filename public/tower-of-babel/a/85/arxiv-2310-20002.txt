Partial regularity for 𝑩 ​ 𝑽 𝓑 \bm{BV^{\mathcal{B}}} minimizers
Federico Franceschini

Abstract
. We prove an ε \varepsilon -regularity theorem for B ​ V ℬ BV^{\mathcal{B}} minimizers of strongly ℬ \mathcal{B} -quasiconvex functionals with linear growth, where ℬ \mathcal{B} is an elliptic operator of the first order. This generalises to the B ​ V ℬ BV^{\mathcal{B}} setting the analogous result for B ​ V BV functions by F. Gmeineder and J. Kristensen [Arch. Rational Mech. Anal. 232 (2019)]. The results of this work cannot be directly derived from the ℬ = ∇ \mathcal{B}=\nabla case essentially because of Ornstein’s “non-inequality”. This adaptation requires an abstract local Poincaré inequality and a fine Fubini-type property to avoid the use of trace theorems, which in general fail when ℬ \mathcal{B} is elliptic.

1.1.1.
The operator
\mathcal{B}
.

We start fixing
\mathcal{B}
, an elliptic operator with constant coefficients, homogeneous of order 1 from
\mathbf{R}^{m\times n}
to
\mathbf{R}^{N}
. That is to say, for each
v\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n},\mathbf{R}^{m})
we set

\mathcal{B}v:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}B_{j}\partial_{j}v,\text{ for some linear maps }B_{j}\colon\mathbf{R}^{m}\to\mathbf{R}^{N}.

By

\ker\widehat{\mathcal{B}}[\xi]=\{0\}
for all
\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, where the
symbol
is the linear map between
\mathbf{R}^{m}
and
\mathbf{R}^{N}
defined as

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}[\xi]:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}B_{j}\text{ for each }\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{n},

so necessarily
m\leq N
.
We also define the

of
\mathcal{B}
as

\Lambda_{\mathcal{B}}:=\bigcup_{\xi\neq 0}\ran\widehat{\mathcal{B}}[\xi]\ \subset\mathbf{R}^{N}.

1.1.2.
Functions with bounded
\mathcal{B}
-variation

This space of functions arises naturally when looking at distributional limits of sequences

having a bound on
\|\mathcal{B}v_{k}\|_{L^{1}}
.

For an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
, define

where
\mathcal{M}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{n})
is the space of
\mathbf{R}^{N}
-valued Borel measures with finite total variation in
\Omega
. By a famous result of Ornstein (seee
[

,
KK16
]
),
BV^{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)\subsetneq BV(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{m})
unless
\mathcal{B}=\nabla
1
1

1

That is to say, (unless) there is a linear map

such that
\nabla u=p(\mathcal{B}u)
.
.

1.1.3.
Functionals defined on measures

We explain the meaning of

\int_{\Omega}f(\mathcal{B}u)

when
u\in BV^{\mathcal{B}}
and
f\colon\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
has linear growth

(H1)

|f(y)|\leq L\langle y\rangle\text{ for all }y\in\mathbf{R}^{N},

and

(1.1)

Here,

and

is the japanese bracket.

For all

consider the functional

(1.2)

\mathcal{F}[v,\Omega]:=\int_{\Omega}f(\mathcal{B}v(x))\,dx,

which can be extended

2

This extension is continuous with respect to the

where we decomposed the measure

with respect to the Lebesgue measure. We will then denote

\int_{\Omega}f(\mathcal{B}v)=\mathcal{F}[v,\Omega]\text{ for all }v\in BV^{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega),

(without the

1.1.4.
\mathcal{B}
-quasiconvexity

Following
[
FM99
]
, we say that a continuous function

is
\mathcal{B}
-quasiconvex if, for all

f(y)\leq\int_{Q}f(y+\mathcal{B}\varphi(x))\,dx\text{ for all }\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(Q,\mathbf{R}^{m}),

where

is the unit cube.

\mathcal{B}

\mathcal{F}[\cdot,\Omega]
will be weakly
∗

We say that

\mathcal{B}
-quasiconvex if there is

such that

(H2)

f-\ell\langle\cdot\rangle\text{ is $\mathcal{B}$-quasiconvex.}

Strong quasiconvexity is a natural assumption in the framework of minimization problems: it is a necessary condition if we want

to be

1.4
.

1.1.5.
Excess

We will prove regularity of local minimizers of
\mathcal{F}[\cdot,\Omega]
in the balls of
\Omega
where a suitable energy denisity (called the

[
DG61

) is smaller than a parameter

which does not depend on the particular solution.

In our situation the right definition of excess in a ball

(1.3)

\Phi(x_{0},R):=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}R^{n}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}E\big(\mathcal{B}u-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\mathscr{L}^{n}\big),

where

and

\Phi

Under the further regularity assumption on the lagrangian

we are going to show

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

) and (

be a local minimizer of
\mathcal{F}[\cdot,\Omega]

\int_{\Omega}f(\mathcal{B}u)\leq\int_{\Omega}f(\mathcal{B}u+\mathcal{B}\varphi)\text{ for all }\varphi\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{m}).

Then for every
\alpha\geq 1
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
there is a critical threshold
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,\gamma,\mathcal{B},f^{\prime\prime},L/\ell)>0
such that the following implication holds. If

B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\quad\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha,\quad\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon,

then

and

[\nabla^{2}u]_{C^{\gamma}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}\leq CR^{-\gamma}\sqrt{\Phi(x_{0},R)}.

for some
C=C({{\alpha,\gamma,\mathcal{B},f^{\prime\prime},L/\ell}})
.

Some remarks are in order.

Remark 1.2
.

Non-convex Lagrangians

Remark 1.3

Local minimizers

Remark 1.4

Assumption (

\mathcal{F}[\cdot,\Omega]
is coercive, that is for all sequences

with fixed boudnary values on

Then necessarily there is some

1.2.
Comparison with the full gradient case

Of course there is (a unique)

and

Still, our result cannot be reduced to the

This fundamental difference can be worked out under the assumption that

A posteriori, the main differences with respect to

We also remark that this adaptation would be straightforward if we assumed a much stronger ellipticity condition on

2.1.
General notation

We work in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
with its standard Euclidean structure, denote with
B_{r}
the balls centred at the origin of radius
r>0
.
\Omega
will always denote an open, bounded set with Lipschitz boundary.

When

we denote differentiation by apexes

f^{\prime}(x)[z]:=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}f(x+tz),\quad f^{\prime\prime}(x)[z,z]:=\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=0}f(x+tz).

Similarly the action of a bilinear map
Q

is denoted by
Q[z,z^{\prime}]
.

We denote with
\mathscr{D}
the space of test functions and with
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}
the space of distributions.

We denote with

the space of continuous compactly supported functions and with
C_{0}
its closure in the uniform topology.

The space
\mathcal{M}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})
of
\mathbf{R}^{N}
-valued Borel measures on
\Omega
with finite total variation, will be identified with

\mathcal{M}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})\simeq C_{0}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})^{*}.

Similarly we have

\mathcal{M}_{loc}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})\simeq C_{c}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})^{*}\text{ and }\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbf{R}^{N})\simeq C(\overline{\Omega},\mathbf{R}^{N})^{*}.

We denote with the angular bracket
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
these dualities, using the standard scalar product on
\mathbf{R}^{N}
.

We will use the the trace spaces
W^{s,p}
defined by the Gagliardo seminorms

[u]_{W^{s,p}(B_{1})}:=\left(\int_{B_{1}}\int_{B_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/p},

we also need the sphere version

[u]_{W^{s,p}(\partial B_{1})}:=\left(\int_{\partial B_{1}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n-1+sp}}\,d\sigma_{x}\,d\sigma_{y}\right)^{1/p}.

In estimates we write

meaning that, if one fixes the parameters

2.2.
Functionals defined on measures

We introduce notation to deal with functionals defined on measures. We refer to
[
AFP00
]
for background in measure theory.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that continuous function
f\colon\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
belongs to
\mathbf{E}_{1}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})
if the limit

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{f(x,tz)}{t}=:f^{\infty}(x,z)\text{ exists in }\mathbf{R},\text{ locally uniformly in }x\in\overline{\Omega},z\in\mathbf{R}^{N}.

The function
f^{\infty}(\cdot,\cdot)
is called the “strong recession function”.

We remark that, by definition,
f^{\infty}\colon\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
is continuous and positively one-homogeneous in its second argument.

For any
f\in\mathbf{E}_{1}
and any
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbf{R}^{N})
, we take the decomposition of
\mu
with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mu=\mu^{ac}(x)\mathscr{L}^{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Omega+\mu^{s}
and further decompose the singular part
\mu^{s}
in terms of its own total variation
\mu^{s}=\frac{d\mu^{s}}{d|\mu^{s}|}(x)\,|\mu^{s}|
. Then we define for any Borel set
A\subset\overline{\Omega}

(2.1)

\int_{A}f(x,\mu):=\int_{A}f(x,\mu^{ac}(x))\,dx+\int_{A}f^{\infty}\Big(x,\frac{d\mu^{s}}{d|\mu^{s}|}(x)\Big)\,d|\mu^{s}|(x).

The same construction can of course be carried out for every Radon measure
\mu\in C_{0}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})^{*}
and Borel set
A\Subset\Omega
.

We can now define a suitable notion of strong convergence of measures.
Define
E:\mathbf{R}^{N}\to\mathbf{R}
by

(2.2)

E(z):=\sqrt{1+|z|^{2}}-1=\langle z\rangle-1,

it is easily checked that
E
belongs to
\mathbf{E}_{1}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})
and it has the nice property of being strictly convex. Furthermore, simple computations shows that

(2.3)

\int_{\overline{\Omega}}E(\mu)=|\tilde{\mu}|(\overline{\Omega})\quad\text{ where }\tilde{\mu}:=\left(\mu,\mathscr{L}^{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\Omega\right)\in C(\overline{\Omega},\mathbf{R}^{N}\times\mathbf{R})^{*}.

Definition 2.2

(Area-strict convergence)
.

Given
\mu
and
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbf{N}}
in
\mathcal{M}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})
we say that
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbf{N}}
converges “area-strictly” to
\mu
in
\Omega
, and write
\mu_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle E}}{{\rightharpoondown}}\mu
, as
j\to+\infty
, provided
\mu_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mu

Intuitively (

, this ensures continuity of the functional

for all

For every

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(x,\mu_{j})=\int_{\overline{\Omega}}f(x,\mu),

\Omega
.

If we know something more about the limit measure

2.4.
Erhenpreis fundamental principle

We state a very general result concerning the solvability of (possibly overdetermined) systems of PDEs with constant coefficients.

Consider the set
M_{\mathcal{B}}
of all constant-coefficients differential operators

\alpha\colon C^{\infty}({\mathbf{R}^{n}},\mathbf{R}^{m})\to C^{\infty}({\mathbf{R}^{n}})

such that
\alpha\circ\mathcal{B}\equiv 0
. We will call
M_{\mathcal{B}}
the “module of compatibility conditions” of
\mathcal{B}
. The following remarkable result is contained in
[
Pal70
, Theorem 1, Chapter 7]
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
V\subset{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
be an open convex set and
{M}_{\mathcal{B}}
its module of compatibility conditions. If
f\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(V,\mathbf{R}^{m})
satisfies

(2.6)

\alpha(f)=0\text{ in }\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(V)\text{ for every }\alpha\in{M}_{\mathcal{B}},

then there exists
u\in\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(V,X)
such that
\mathcal{B}u=f
in
\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(V,\mathbf{R}^{m})
.

Remark 2.7
.

Then
{M}_{\mathcal{B}}
has a natural structure of
\mathbf{R}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]
-module via the natural action:

p\cdot\alpha:=p(\partial/\partial x_{1},\ldots,\partial/\partial x_{n})\circ\alpha,\qquad\text{ for all }p\in\mathbf{R}[\xi],\alpha\in{M}_{\mathcal{B}}.

Since
{M}_{\mathcal{B}}
is a submodule of the Noetherian module
\mathbf{R}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}]
, it is finitely generated by some operators
\{\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{\ell}\}
. This means that the compatibility condition (
2.6
) in this theorem can be checked only for
\mathcal{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{A}_{\ell}
.

Remark 2.8
.

This theorem is a far reaching generalization of Poincaré’s Lemma: a closed form (that is a form that satisfies the compatibility conditions
d\omega=0
) is in fact exact, provided the domain is simple enough (for example convex always works).
A quicker account of this theory can be found in
[
H“”or90
, Chapter 7]
.

2.5.
The space

Let us collect some properties of the space

.
Recall that

BV^{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{m})\colon\mathcal{B}u\in C_{0}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})^{*}\right\}.

The space

is defined similarly requiring that
\mathcal{B}u\in C_{c}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})^{*}
.

Given some sequence
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbf{N}}\subset BV^{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
and
u\in BV^{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
, we say that:

•

\{u_{j}\}
converges weakly
∗
to
u
in
BV^{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{{loc}}}(\Omega)
provided

u_{j}\to u\text{ in }L^{1}_{{\text{{loc}}}}(\Omega)\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{B}u_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\mathcal{B}u\text{ in }C_{c}(\Omega,\mathbf{R}^{N})^{*};

•

\{u_{j}\}
converges area-strictly to
u
in
BV^{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega)
provided
\lim_{j}\|u-u_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}=0
and

\int_{\Omega}E(\mathcal{B}u_{j})\to\int_{\Omega}E(\mathcal{B}u),\quad\text{ that is }\mathcal{B}u_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle E}}{{\rightharpoondown}}\mathcal{B}u\text{ in }\Omega.

The area-strict closure of

is denoted by

.

BV^{\mathcal{B}}
functions can be approximated by smooth ones in the following sense.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
u\in BV^{\mathcal{B}}_{{\text{{loc}}}}(\Omega)
and
\{\phi_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
be a family of standard mollifiers, extend

to zero outside
\Omega
and set
u_{\varepsilon}:=u*\phi_{\varepsilon}

3.20
)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{B}}f^{\prime\prime}_{y_{0}}(0)[\mathcal{B}\Phi_{0},\mathcal{B}V]

(by
3.17
)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,L}\int_{\mathbf{B}}E(\mathcal{B}\tilde{U})|\mathcal{B}\Phi_{0}|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\mathcal{B}}\|\nabla\Phi_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{B})}\int_{\mathbf{B}}E(\mathcal{B}\tilde{U})

(by
3.19
)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{q,\mathcal{B}}\left(\int_{\mathbf{B}}E(V)\right)^{1/q^{\prime}}\int_{\mathbf{B}}E(\mathcal{B}\tilde{U}),

dividing we get

\int_{\mathbf{B}}E(V)\,dx\lesssim_{\alpha,q,\mathcal{B},L}\left(\int_{\mathbf{B}}E(\mathcal{B}\tilde{U})\right)^{q},

changing variables back to the ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
we finally find
3.12
.
∎

3.4.
Excess decay and iteration

In the previous section we derived two key inequalities for a minimizer
u
of
\mathcal{F}
, with the previous notations they roughly look like

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{R/2}}E(\mathcal{B}u)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Caccioppoli}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{R}}E\left(\frac{u}{R}\right)\,dx,

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{R}}E\left(\frac{u-h}{R}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Linearized Euler}}}{{\lesssim}}

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{R}}E(\mathcal{B}u)\right)^{q},

where
h
is the
\mathcal{B}
-affine replacement of
u
in
B_{R}
and the averages are always taken with respect to the Lebesgue measure. The idea is to link these inequalities to obtain some nonlinear estimate that can be iterated on smaller balls. As in other regularity results we wish to control a suitable “excess” function: in our set-up the right definition is

(3.21)

\mathcal{E}(x_{0},R):=\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}E\left(\mathcal{B}u-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right),

where we recall that for every non-neglegible and bounded Borel set
U
we use the notation

\left(\mathcal{B}u\right)_{U}:=\frac{\mathcal{B}u(U)}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(U)}.

When the center
x_{0}
is fixed we will also use the shorthand
\mathcal{E}(x_{0},R)=\mathcal{E}(R)
. Then we have

Proposition 3.6

(Preliminary Decay)
.

Let
u\in BV^{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbf{R}^{n}})
be a minimizer of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. Fix a positive threshold
\alpha>0
, any exponent
1<q<n/(n-1)
and any ball
B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
such that

(3.22)

\left|\left(\mathcal{B}u\right)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \left|\mathcal{B}u-\left(\mathcal{B}u\right)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|(B_{R}(x_{0}))\leq\omega_{n}R^{n}.

Then there is a large constant
c_{\text{\emph{dec}}}=c_{\text{\emph{dec}}}(\alpha,q,\mathcal{B},L,\ell)
such that

(3.23)

\mathcal{E}(\sigma R)\leq c_{\text{\emph{dec}}}\left(\sigma^{n+2}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}{\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(R)}{\omega_{n}R^{n}}\right)}^{q-1}\right)\,\mathcal{E}(R),

for any
\sigma\in(0,1/10)
.

Proof.

In this proof we shall denote by
C
a generic constant depending only on
\alpha,q,L/\ell,\mathcal{B},n
. In particular, we will keep track of the dependence of the constants from
R
and
\sigma
. Since we work at a fixed center we will forget about
x_{0}
.

Step 1.
We fix any linear map
A\in\mathbf{R}^{m}\otimes\mathbf{R}^{n}
such that
\mathcal{B}(Ax)=(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}}
. This is possible because
(\mathcal{B}u)_{R}\in\Lambda
.

Step 2.
Choose any
a_{0}\in\ker(\mathcal{B},B_{R})\subset C^{\infty}(B_{R})
such that

\big[u-Ax-a_{0}\big]_{W^{1-1/p,p}(B_{R/2})}\lesssim_{\mathcal{B}}R^{\frac{n+1}{p}-n}|\mathcal{B}u-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}}|\left(B_{R}\right),

this is possible thanks to the local Poincaré inequality
Proposition

2.12
applied to the function
u(x)-Ax
in the ball
B_{R}
.

Step 3.
Using Lemma
2.13
on
u-Ax-a_{0}
we find a radius
r^{*}\in\left(\frac{4}{10}R,\frac{5}{10}R\right)
such that
\partial B_{r^{*}}
is a good sphere for
u-Ax-a_{0}
(and thus for
u
),
|\mathcal{B}u|(\partial B_{r^{*}})=0
and

\displaystyle\big[\ u-Ax-a_{0}\big]_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial B_{r^{*}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{n}{R^{-1/p}}\big[u-Ax-a_{0}\big]_{W^{1-1/p,p}(B_{R/2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\mathcal{B}}R^{-n(1-1/p)}|\mathcal{B}u-(\mathcal{B}u)_{R}|\left(B_{R}\right).

Setting
\tilde{u}(x):=u(x)-a_{0}(x)-Ax\in BV^{\mathcal{B}}_{loc}(B_{R})
we proved

[{\tilde{u}}]_{W^{1-1/p,p}\partial B_{r^{*}}}\lesssim_{\mathcal{B}}R^{-n(1-1/p)}|\mathcal{B}\tilde{u}|(B_{R}).

Step 4.
Set
a(x):=a_{0}(x)+Ax\in\aff(\mathcal{B},B_{R})
and notice that

|\mathcal{B}a|=|\mathcal{B}(a_{0}+Ax)|=|0+(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}}|\leq\alpha.

We apply now the linearization procedure (Proposition
3.5
) in the ball
B_{r*}
using
A
as
\mathcal{B}
-affine map: the choice of
r^{*}
ensures assumption
(i)
, the last estimate ensures
(ii)
. Thus we find a map
h\in C^{\infty}\cap W^{1,p}(B_{r*},\mathbf{R}^{m})
which solves the system

\begin{cases}\mathcal{B}^{*}\left(f^{\prime\prime}(\mathcal{B}a).\mathcal{B}h\right)=0&\text{ in }B_{r^{*}},\\
\tr_{\partial B_{r^{*}}}h=u\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$|$}_{\partial B_{r^{*}}}&\text{ on }\partial B_{r^{*}},\end{cases}

and satisfies

(3.24)

\displaystyle\|\nabla\tilde{h}\|_{L^{p}(B_{r*})}\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B},L}[{\tilde{u}}]_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial B_{r^{*}})}\lesssim_{\mathcal{B}}R^{-n(1-1/p)}|\mathcal{B}\tilde{u}|(B_{R}),

(3.25)

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r^{*}}}E\Big(\frac{\tilde{u}-\tilde{h}}{r^{*}}\Big)\,dx\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B},L}\Big(\fint_{B_{r^{*}}}E(\mathcal{B}\tilde{u})\Big)^{q}=\Big(\frac{\mathcal{E}(r^{*})}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(B_{r^{*}})}\Big)^{q},

where we set for brevity
\tilde{h}:=h-a
.

Step 6.
Consider the affine map
H(x):=\tilde{h}(x_{0})+\nabla\tilde{h}(x_{0})(x-x_{0})
. Then

|\mathcal{B}(a+H)(x)|\leq\alpha+C_{0}\ \ \ \text{ for all }x\in B_{R},

for some constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(\mathcal{B})
. We have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}(a+H)|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{B}a|+|\mathcal{B}H|\lesssim_{\mathcal{B}}|(\mathcal{B}u)_{R}|+|\nabla\tilde{h}(x_{0})|\leq\alpha+\sup_{x^{\prime}\in B_{r^{*}/2}}|\nabla\tilde{h}(x^{\prime})|,

but then Theorem
2.20
and (
3.24
) give

\displaystyle\sup_{x^{\prime}\in B_{r^{*}/2}}|\nabla\tilde{h}(x^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B},\Lambda/\lambda}\Big(\fint_{B_{r^{*}}}|\nabla\tilde{h}|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B},\Lambda/\lambda}R^{-n/p}[u]_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial B_{r^{*}})}\lesssim_{\mathcal{B}}R^{-n}|\mathcal{B}\tilde{u}|(B_{R})\lesssim 1.

Step 7.
Fix any
\sigma\in(0,1/10)
. We will prove the decay bounding
\mathcal{E}(\sigma R)
in terms of
\mathcal{E}(R)
, linking the Caccioppoli inequality and the harmonic approximation.

Exploiting the quasi-minimality property of the mean (Lemma
2.22
) we get

\mathcal{E}(\sigma R)=\int_{B_{\sigma R}}E(\mathcal{B}u-(\mathcal{B}u)_{\sigma R})\leq 4\int_{B_{\sigma R}}E(\mathcal{B}u-\mathcal{B}(a+H))=4\int_{B_{\sigma R}}E(\mathcal{B}(\tilde{u}-H)).

Now we link this estimate with the Caccioppoli inequality (applied on the ball
B_{\sigma R}
with map
a+H\in\aff_{\mathcal{B}}(B_{R})
and threshold
\alpha+C_{0}
) and the triangular inequality:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\sigma R)

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{B_{\sigma R}}E(\mathcal{B}(\tilde{u}-H))\lesssim_{\alpha}\int_{B_{2\sigma R}}E\Big(\frac{\tilde{u}-H}{2\sigma R}\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\alpha}\underbrace{\int_{B_{2\sigma R}}E\Big(\frac{\tilde{u}-\tilde{h}}{2\sigma R}\Big)\,dx}_{:=\bm{I}}+\underbrace{\int_{B_{2\sigma R}}E\Big(\frac{\tilde{h}-H}{2\sigma R}\Big)\,dx}_{:=\bm{II}}

Where we used that
\tilde{h}
is well defined on
B_{2\sigma R}\subset B_{r^{*}}
. We estimate the two integrals, let us deal with the first using Lemma
2.21
and (
3.25
)

\displaystyle\bm{I}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2\sigma R}}E\left(\frac{\tilde{u}-\tilde{h}}{r^{*}}\frac{r^{*}}{2\sigma R}\right)\,dx\leq\left(\frac{r^{*}}{2\sigma R}\right)^{2}\int_{B_{2\sigma R}}E\left(\frac{\tilde{u}-\tilde{h}}{r^{*}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\int_{B_{r^{*}}}E\left(\frac{\tilde{u}-\tilde{h}}{r^{*}}\right)\,dx\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B}}\frac{{r^{*}}^{n}}{\sigma^{2}}\,{\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(r^{*})}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(B_{r^{*}})}\right)}^{q}\lesssim\frac{\mathcal{E}(R)}{\sigma^{2}}\,{\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}(R)}{R^{n}}\right)}^{q-1}.

For the second term we use that
\tilde{h}
behaves like an harmonic function. Using Taylor’s theorem and Theorem
2.20

(b)
we have

\sup_{B_{2\sigma R}}{|\tilde{h}-H|}\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B}}\sigma^{2}R^{2}\sup_{B_{r^{*}/2}}|\nabla^{2}\tilde{h}|\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B},\Lambda/\lambda}\sigma^{2}R\Big(\fint_{B_{r^{*}}}|\nabla\tilde{h}|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{1/p},

then using (
3.24
) as in Step 6 we find

\Big(\fint_{B_{r^{*}}}|\nabla\tilde{h}|^{p}\,dx\Big)^{1/p}\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B},\Lambda/\lambda}R^{-n}|\mathcal{B}\tilde{u}|(B_{R}).

Integrating over
B_{2\sigma R}
we get exploiting Lemma
2.21
and Jensen’s inequality

\displaystyle\bm{II}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2\sigma R}}E\Big(\frac{\tilde{h}-H}{2\sigma R}\Big)\,dx\leq\sigma^{n}R^{n}E\Big(C\sigma\frac{|\mathcal{B}\tilde{u}|(B_{R})}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(B_{R})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C^{2}\sigma^{n+2}R^{n}\left(\frac{|\mathcal{B}\tilde{u}|(B_{R})}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(B_{R})}\right)^{2}\lesssim\sigma^{n+2}R^{n}E\Big(\frac{|\mathcal{B}\tilde{u}|(B_{R})}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(B_{R})}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sigma^{n+2}R^{n}\fint_{B_{R}}E(\mathcal{B}\tilde{u})\lesssim\sigma^{n+2}\mathcal{E}(R).

Combining these two estimates we have (
3.23
).
∎

The next step is to iterate this decay to prove that there is a “critical threshold
\varepsilon_{0}
” such that, if
\mathcal{E}(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon_{0}R^{n}
, then
\mathcal{E}(x_{0},r)\lesssim r^{n+\gamma}
when
r\to 0^{+}
. Since this estimate will hold also for
x
near
x_{0}
too, we will be able to employ Campanato’s integral characterization of Hölder continuity. We prove a decay of the normalized excess of a ball
B_{R}\Subset\Omega
:

\Phi(x_{0},R):=\frac{\mathcal{E}(x_{0},R)}{\mathscr{L}^{n}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}=\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}E\left(\mathcal{B}u-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right).

Proposition 3.7

(Excess decay)
.

Let
u\in BV^{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbf{R}^{n}})
be a minimizer of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
a fixed exponent. Then for every
\alpha>0
there exists a critical threshold
\varepsilon_{\text{\emph{crit}}}=\varepsilon_{\text{\emph{crit}}}(\alpha,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell)>0
such that the following implication holds: if

(3.26)

B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\quad\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha,\quad\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon_{\text{crit}},

then

(3.27)

\Phi(x_{0},r)\lesssim_{{\alpha,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell}}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\gamma}\Phi(x_{0},R)\quad\text{ for every }r\in(0,R).

Proof.

Fix
u,q,\alpha
and
\gamma
as in the hypothesis. Let us denote with

C:=c_{\text{decay}}(\alpha+1,q:=1+1/n,\mathcal{B},L,\ell)

the constant given by Proposition
3.6
relative to
\alpha+1
, this ensures

\begin{cases}B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha+1,\\
\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\mathcal{B}u-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq 1\end{cases}\Rightarrow\Phi(x_{0},\sigma R)\leq C\,\left(\sigma^{n+2}+\sigma^{-2}\Phi(x_{0},R)^{1/n}\right)\Phi(x_{0},R),

for every
0<\sigma<1/10
.

Step 1. We now fix two values
\sigma_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
, both depending only on
\{\alpha+1,q,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell\}
such that the following holds:

(3.28)

\begin{cases}B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha+1,\\
\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon_{0}\end{cases}\Rightarrow\Phi(x_{0},\sigma_{0}R)\leq\sigma_{0}^{2\gamma}\Phi(x_{0},R).

The choices are

\sigma_{0}:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{20},3C^{-\frac{1}{n-2(1-\gamma)}}\right\},\quad\varepsilon_{0}:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{3},\left(\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2(1+\gamma)}}{3C}\right)^{n}\right\},

in order to reduce
3.28
to Proposition
3.6
just use Lemma
2.23
:

\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon_{0}\leq 1/3\Rightarrow\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\mathcal{B}u-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)}\leq 1.

We remark that
\varepsilon_{\text{crit}}
is yet to be chosen and differs from
\varepsilon_{0}
.

Step 2. We inspect how the hypothesis of
3.28
behaves when passing from
B_{R}
to
B_{\sigma_{0}R}
, that is to say we notice that

(3.29)

\begin{cases}B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon_{0},\\
\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha<\alpha+1\end{cases}\Rightarrow\begin{cases}B_{\sigma_{0}R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\Phi(x_{0},\sigma_{0}R)\leq\sigma_{0}^{2\gamma}\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon_{0},\\
\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{\sigma_{0}R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha+\sigma^{-n}_{0}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)},\end{cases}

the only nontrivial verification being

(triangular)

\displaystyle\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{\sigma_{0}R}(x_{0})}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|+\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{\sigma_{0}R}(x_{0})}-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|

(monotonicity)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha+\sigma^{-n}_{0}\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\left|\mathcal{B}u-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|

( by Lemma
2.23
)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha+\sigma^{-n}_{0}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)}.

In order to apply again
3.28
on
B_{\sigma_{0}R}(x_{0})
we need an additional smallness condition on the initial excess

(3.30)

\sigma^{-n}_{0}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)}\leq 1.

This is of course possible replacing
\varepsilon_{0}
with some smaller
\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{0}
. Nevertheless we want to iterate this argument infinite times so we must ensure that
\varepsilon_{k}
does not go down to
0
. Luckily this can be done, we think it is more clear to provide the next step of the iteration instead of a formal induction.

Step 3. Suppose that
3.30
is true, then we apply
3.28
on
B_{\sigma_{0}R}(x_{0})
and get

\displaystyle\begin{cases}B_{\sigma_{0}R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\Phi(x_{0},\sigma_{0}R)\leq\sigma_{0}^{2\gamma}\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon_{0},\\
\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{\sigma_{0}R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha+\sigma^{-n}_{0}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)}\leq\alpha+1,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}B_{\sigma_{0}^{2}R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\Phi(x_{0},\sigma_{0}^{2}R)\leq\sigma_{0}^{2\gamma}\Phi(x_{0},\sigma_{0}R),\\
\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{\sigma_{0}^{2}R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha+\sigma^{-n}_{0}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)}+\sigma^{-n}_{0}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},\sigma_{0}R)},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}B_{\sigma_{0}^{2}R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\Phi(x_{0},\sigma_{0}^{2}R)\leq\sigma_{0}^{4\gamma}\Phi(x_{0},R),\\
\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{\sigma_{0}^{2}R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha+(1+\sigma_{0}^{\gamma})\sigma^{-n}_{0}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)},\end{cases}

In order to apply
3.28
on
B_{\sigma_{0}^{2}R}(x_{0})
we need a further smallness assumption on the excess

(1+\sigma_{0}^{\gamma})\sigma^{-n}_{0}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)}\leq 1,

this requirement is slightly stronger than
3.30
. Going on in this fashion one easily devise the pattern: the condition

\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\sigma_{0}^{k\gamma}\right)\sigma^{-n}_{0}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)}\leq 1,

is enough for infinitely many steps and, since the series converges, we can set

\varepsilon_{\text{crit}}:=\min\left\{\varepsilon_{0};\frac{1}{3}\sigma_{0}^{2n}\left(\sum_{k\in\mathbf{N}}\sigma_{0}^{k\gamma}\right)^{-2}\right\}.

With this choice we have

(3.31)

\begin{cases}B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha,\\
\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon_{\text{crit}}\end{cases}\Rightarrow\Phi(x_{0},\sigma_{0}^{k}R)\leq\sigma_{0}^{2\gamma k}\Phi(x_{0},R),\quad k\in\mathbf{N},

and this gives the Hölder estimate
3.27
by discrete interpolation.
∎

We can finally prove

Theorem 3.8

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
u\in BV^{\mathcal{B}}({\mathbf{R}^{n}})
be a minimizer of
\overline{\mathcal{F}}
and
\gamma\in(0,1)
some fixed exponent. Then for every
\alpha>0
there exists a critical threshold
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell)>0
such that the following implication holds: if

(3.32)

B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\quad\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha,\quad\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon,

then
\mathcal{B}u\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B_{R/2}(x_{0})\ll\mathscr{L}^{n}
,
\mathcal{B}u\in C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))
and

(3.33)

\left[\mathcal{B}u\right]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B},L,\ell}R^{-\gamma}\sqrt{\Phi(x_{0},R)}.

Proof.

We start with two simple estimates that relates the oscillation of nested balls, whose centers do not necessarily agree. Given
B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega
there holds

\displaystyle\Phi(x,R/2)\leq 2^{n+2}\,\Phi(x_{0},R)

\displaystyle\text{ for every }x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0}),

\displaystyle\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R/2}(x)}\right|\leq\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|+2^{n}\sqrt{3\Phi(x_{0},R)}

\displaystyle\text{ for every }x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0}).

These are simply proven by Lemmas
2.22
,
2.23
and the quasi-triangular inequality. So defining
\varepsilon
as

(3.34)

\varepsilon:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{3\cdot 2^{n}};\frac{\varepsilon_{\text{crit}}(\alpha+1,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell)}{2^{n+2}}\right\},

these two estimates gives us

\begin{cases}B_{R}(x_{0})\Subset\Omega,\Phi(x_{0},R)\leq\varepsilon,\\
\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\right|\leq\alpha\end{cases}\Rightarrow\begin{cases}B_{R/2}(x)\Subset\Omega,\Phi(x,R/2)\leq\varepsilon_{\text{crit}}(\alpha+1,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell),\\
\left|(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{R/2}(x)}\right|\leq\alpha+1,\end{cases}

so that we can apply Proposition
3.7
on the ball
B_{R/2}(x)
with exponent
\gamma
and threshold
\alpha+1
and find:

\Phi(x,r)\lesssim_{{\alpha,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell}}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\gamma}\,\Phi(x_{0},R)\quad\text{ for every }r\in(0,R/2),

but the constant involved does not depend on the center
x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0})
so there holds

\Phi(x,r)\lesssim_{{\alpha,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell}}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\gamma}\,\Phi(x_{0},R)\quad\text{ for every }x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0}),r\in(0,R/2).

This in particular gives us that for every
x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0})
we have

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\frac{|\mathcal{B}^{s}u|(\overline{B}_{r}(x))}{r^{n}}\lesssim\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\gamma}\,\Phi(x_{0},R)=0,

so by standard results about upper densities of measures (see Theorem 2.56 in
[
AFP00
]
) we get
\mathcal{B}u\ll\mathscr{L}^{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}B_{R/2}(x_{0})
, so we can write
\mathcal{B}u=\mathcal{B}u(x)\in L^{1}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}),\mathbf{R}^{N})
. We now show that
\mathcal{B}u
belongs to the Campanato space
\mathcal{L}^{n+\gamma}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))
, in fact by Lemma
2.23
we have

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x)}\left|\mathcal{B}u(x^{\prime})-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{r}(x)}\right|\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Phi(x,r)^{2}+2\Phi(x,r)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{{\alpha,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell}}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{4\gamma}\Phi(x_{0},R)^{2}+\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\gamma}\Phi(x_{0},R)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{2\gamma}\Phi(x_{0},R).

We conclude that

\sup_{x\in B_{R/2}(x_{0}),0<r<R/2}r^{-n-\gamma}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left|\mathcal{B}u(x^{\prime})-(\mathcal{B}u)_{B_{r}(x)}\right|\,dx^{\prime}\lesssim_{{\alpha,\gamma,\mathcal{B},L,\ell}}\frac{\sqrt{\Phi(x_{0},R)}}{R^{\gamma}},

so by the integral characterization of Hölder continuity we get
3.33
:

\left[\mathcal{B}u\right]_{C^{0,\gamma}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}\sim_{n,\gamma}\left[\mathcal{B}u\right]_{\mathcal{L}^{1,n+\gamma}(B_{R/2}(x_{0}))}\lesssim_{\alpha,\mathcal{B},L,\ell}R^{-\gamma}\sqrt{\Phi(x_{0},R)}.

∎

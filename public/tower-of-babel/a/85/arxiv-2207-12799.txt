MODULAR PAULSEN PROBLEM AND MODULAR PROJECTION PROBLEM
K. MAHESH KRISHNA

Abstract




Abstract
: Based on the solution of
Paulsen Problem
by Kwok, Lau, Lee, and Ramachandran [
STOC’18-Proceedings of the 50th Annual ACM SIGACT Symposium on Theory of Computing, 2018
] and independently by Hamilton, and Moitra [
Isr. J. Math., 2021
] we study Paulsen Problem and Projection Problem in the context of Hilbert C*-modules.
We show that for commutative C*-algebras, if Modular Paulsen Problem has a solution, then Modular Projection Problem also has a solution. We formulate the problem of operator scaling for matrices over C*-algebras.

Keywords
: Frame, C*-algebra, Hilbert C*-module, invariant basis number, Paulsen Problem, Projection Problem, Operator Scaling Problem.

Mathematics Subject Classification (2020)
: 42C15, 46L05.

1.
Introduction

We begin from the definition of frames and various special sub classes of it for finite dimensional Hilbert spaces which originated from the work of Duffin and Schaeffer
[
40
,
34
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

[
51
,
15
]

A collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathbb{K}
(
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
) is said to be a
frame
for
\mathcal{H}
if there exist
a,b>0
such that

\displaystyle a\|h\|^{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle h,\tau_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq b\|h\|^{2},\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{H}.

Definition 1.2
.

[
51
,
15
]

A frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is said to be a

(i)

Parseval frame
if

\displaystyle h=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle h,\tau_{j}\rangle\tau_{j},\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{H}.

(ii)

tight frame
if there exists
a>0
such that

\displaystyle a\|h\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\langle h,\tau_{j}\rangle|^{2},\quad\forall h\in\mathcal{H}.

(iii)

unit norm frame
if
\|\tau_{j}\|=1
, for all
1\leq j\leq n
.

From a frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for
\mathcal{H}
we get the following three operators. We use
\mathcal{H}
to denote a finite dimensional Hilbert space. We denote the identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
by
I_{\mathcal{H}}.

(i)

Analysis operator
\theta_{\tau}:\mathcal{H}\ni h\mapsto\theta_{\tau}h\coloneqq(\langle h,\tau_{j}\rangle)_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
.

(ii)

Synthesis operator
\theta_{\tau}^{*}:\mathbb{K}^{n}\ni(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\mapsto\theta_{\tau}^{*}(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{H}
.

(iii)

Frame operator
S_{\tau}:\mathcal{H}\ni h\mapsto S_{\tau}h\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle h,\tau_{j}\rangle\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{H}
.

It then follows that
S_{\tau}=\theta_{\tau}^{*}\theta_{\tau}
and the frame operator is positive and invertible. Therefore
\{S_{\tau}^{-1/2}\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a Parseval frame for
\mathcal{H}
. It is known that in this way the construction of Parseval frame is difficult due to inversion and square root process of frame operator. On the other side, given a collection, it is easy (even not always) to verify that whether it is a frame than to verify that it is a Parseval frame. This leads to the following notions.

.

[
20
]

A Parseval frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called an
equal norm Parseval frame
if

\displaystyle\|\tau_{j}\|^{2}=\frac{d}{n},\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

.

[

A frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called an
\varepsilon

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)I_{\mathcal{H}}\leq S_{\tau}\leq(1+\varepsilon)I_{\mathcal{H}}.

Definition 1.5
.

[
20

A frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is called an
\varepsilon

(
\varepsilon<1
) if

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\frac{d}{n}\leq\|\tau_{j}\|^{2}\leq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{d}{n},\quad\forall 1\leq j\leq n.

Definition 1.6

[

A frame for a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space

\varepsilon
-nearly equal norm and

Following notion is often used to measure the distance between two collections in Hilbert space.

2.
Modular Paulsen Problem and Modular Projection Problem

Originated from the work of Kaplansky
[
63
]
for commutative C*-algebras and developed from the work of Paschke
[
84
]
and Rieffel
[
88
]
for non commutative C*-algebras, through a development of half century, it became evident that Hilbert C*-modules play a prominent role in the non commutative geometry
[
73
,
95
,
77
]
. This demanded a necessity of developing frame theory for Hilbert C*-modules. In their fundamental paper Frank and Larson introduced the notion of frames for Hilbert C*-modules
[
42
]
. In this paper we only consider the following particular Hilbert C*-module. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a unital C*-algebra,
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathcal{A}^{d}
be the left module over
\mathcal{A}
w.r.t. natural operations. Modular
\mathcal{A}
-inner product on
\mathcal{A}^{d}
is defined as

\displaystyle\langle(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d},(y_{j})_{j=1}^{d}\rangle\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}y_{j}^{*},\quad\forall(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d},(y_{j})_{j=1}^{d}\in\mathcal{A}^{d}.

Hence the norm on
\mathcal{A}^{d}
becomes

\displaystyle\|(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d}\|\coloneqq\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{d}x_{j}x_{j}^{*}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall(x_{j})_{j=1}^{d}\in\mathcal{A}^{d}.

Standard orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{A}^{d}
is denoted by
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
.

Definition 2.1
.

[
42
]

A collection
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
in
\mathcal{A}^{d}
is said to be a (modular)
frame
for
\mathcal{A}^{d}
if there exist real
a,b>0
such that

\displaystyle a\langle x,x\rangle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle x,\tau_{j}\rangle\langle\tau_{j},x\rangle\leq b\langle x,x\rangle,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{A}^{d}.

A frame
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
for
\mathcal{A}^{d}
is said to be
Parseval
if

\displaystyle\langle x,x\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle x,\tau_{j}\rangle\langle\tau_{j},x\rangle,\quad\forall x\in\mathcal{A}^{d}.

It has to be noted that the theory of frames for Hilbert C*-modules behaves mysteriously compared to the theory of frames for Hilbert spaces
[
8
,
59
,
85
,
10
,
76
,
11
]
. Thus every result of Hilbert space frame theory has to be rechecked for Hilbert C*-modules. Through detailed working, Frank and Larson obtained the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
42
]

Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be a frame for
\mathcal{A}^{d}
. Then

(i)

Analysis homomorphism
\theta_{\tau}:\mathcal{A}^{d}\ni x\mapsto\theta_{\tau}x\coloneqq(\langle x,\tau_{j}\rangle)_{j=1}^{n}\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
is adjointable and bounded below.

(ii)

Synthesis homomorphism
\theta_{\tau}^{*}:\mathcal{A}^{n}\ni(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\mapsto\theta_{\tau}^{*}(a_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{A}^{d}
is surjective

(iii)

Frame homomorphism
S_{\tau}:\mathcal{A}^{d}\ni x\mapsto S_{\tau}x\coloneqq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle x,\tau_{j}\rangle\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{A}^{d}
is self-adjoint positive and invertible.

(iv)

S_{\tau}=\theta_{\tau}^{*}\theta_{\tau}
.

(v)

P_{\tau}\coloneqq\theta_{\tau}S_{\tau}^{-1}\theta_{\tau}^{*}
is a projection onto
\theta_{\tau}(\mathcal{A}^{d})
.

\displaystyle\inf\{\operatorname{dist}^{2}(\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}):\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\text{ is an equal inner product Parseval frame for }\mathcal{A}^{d}\}\leq f(\varepsilon,n,d).

Moreover, whether
f
depends on
n
?

Theorem
1.13
says that solution of Paulsen problem exists for Hilbert spaces. However, the proof of Theorem
1.13
as given by Hadwin can not be executed for C*-algebras. Therefore, we first ask the following question.

Problem 2.13
.

Whether there exists a solution to the modular Paulsen problem?

Since Hilbert C*-modules are generalizations of Hilbert spaces, we must have the following result (see Proposition
1.14
).

Proposition 2.14
.

Modular Paulsen function
f
, if it exists, satisfies

\displaystyle f(\varepsilon,n,d)\geq\varepsilon^{2}d,\quad\forall\varepsilon>0,\forall d\in\mathbb{N}.

We formulate Problem
1.20
to Hilbert C*-modules as follows.

Problem 2.15
.

(
Modular projection problem)
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a C*-algebra with invariant basis number property. Let
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
be the standard orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{A}^{d}
. Find the function
g:(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\to[0,\infty)
satisfying the following: If
P:\mathcal{A}^{d}\to\mathcal{A}^{d}
is an orthogonal projection of rank
n
satisfying

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\frac{n}{d}\leq\langle Pe_{k},Pe_{k}\rangle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n}{d},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq d,

then there exists an orthogonal projection

with

\displaystyle\langle Qe_{k},Qe_{k}\rangle=\frac{n}{d},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq d,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle Pe_{k}-Qe_{k},Pe_{k}-Qe_{k}\rangle\right\|\leq g(\varepsilon,n,d).

Moreover, whether

First natural question is the following.

Problem 2.16
.

Whether there is a relation between modular Paulsen problem and modular projection problem?

We answer Problem
2.16
partially. More precisely, we show that if we can solve modular Paulsen problem, then we can solve modular projection problem. Our developments are motivated from the arguments given in
[
20
]
. First we derive a lemma.

Theorem 2.17
.

Assume that the C*-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is commutative. Let
\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
and
\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
be two Parseval frames for
\mathcal{A}^{d}
. If

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}^{2}(\{\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n})=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\tau_{j}-\omega_{j}\rangle\right\|<\varepsilon,

then

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}^{2}(\{\theta_{\tau}\tau_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n},\{\theta_{\omega}\omega_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n})=\left\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\theta_{\tau}\tau_{j}-\theta_{\omega}\omega_{j},\theta_{\tau}\tau_{j}-\theta_{\omega}\omega_{j}\rangle\right\|<4\varepsilon.

Proof.

Let
1\leq j\leq n
. Then

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}\tau_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle e_{k},\quad\theta_{\omega}\omega_{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\omega_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle e_{k}.

We now note the following. If
a,b
are any two elements of a C*-algebra, then
(a+b)(a+b)^{*}\leq 2(aa^{*}+bb^{*}).
In fact,

\displaystyle 2(aa^{*}+bb^{*})-(a+b)(a+b)^{*}

\displaystyle=2aa^{*}+2bb^{*}-aa^{*}-ab^{*}-ba^{*}-bb^{*}

\displaystyle=aa^{*}+bb^{*}-ab^{*}-ba^{*}=a(a^{*}-b^{*})-b(a^{*}-b^{*})

\displaystyle=(a-b)(a-b)^{*}\geq 0.

Therefore

\displaystyle\langle\theta_{\tau}\tau_{j}-\theta_{\omega}\omega_{j},\theta_{\tau}\tau_{j}-\theta_{\omega}\omega_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle-\langle\omega_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle)(\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}\rangle-\langle\omega_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle)^{*}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle+\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle)(\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle+\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle)^{*}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle^{*}+2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\omega_{k}\rangle^{*}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle^{*}+2\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\tau_{j}-\omega_{j}\rangle.

We now sum by varying
j
and use commutativity of the C*-algebra to get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\theta_{\tau}\tau_{j}-\theta_{\omega}\omega_{j},\theta_{\tau}\tau_{j}-\theta_{\omega}\omega_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle^{*}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\tau_{j}-\omega_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle\langle\tau_{j},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle^{*}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\tau_{j}-\omega_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{k}-\omega_{k},\tau_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\tau_{j}-\omega_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=4\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle\tau_{j}-\omega_{j},\tau_{j}-\omega_{j}\rangle.

By taking norm we get the conclusion.
∎

Theorem 2.18
.

Assume that the C*-algebra is commutative. If Modular Paulsen Problem has a solution, then Modular Projection Problem also has a solution.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a commutative C*-algebra,
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
be the standard orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{A}^{d}
. Assume that Modular Paulsen Problem has a solution. We wish to show that Modular Projection Problem also has a solution. Let
P:\mathcal{A}^{d}\to\mathcal{A}^{d}
be an orthogonal projection of rank
n
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle(1-\varepsilon)\frac{n}{d}\leq\langle Pe_{k},Pe_{k}\rangle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\frac{n}{d},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq d.

Since
P
is a projection, it is then clear that
\{Pe_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
is a modular Parseval frame for
P(\mathcal{A}^{d})
. Note that Inequality (
1
) tells that
\{Pe_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
is an
\varepsilon
-nearly equal inner product Parseval frame for
P(\mathcal{A}^{d})
(note that rank of this module is
n
). Since Modular Paulsen Problem has a solution, there is an equal inner product Parseval frame
\{\omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
for
P(\mathcal{A}^{d})
satisfying

\displaystyle\operatorname{dist}^{2}(\{Pe_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d},\{\omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d})=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle Pe_{k}-\omega_{k},Pe_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle\right\|\leq f(\varepsilon,d,n).

Define
Q\coloneqq P_{\omega}=\theta_{\omega}\theta_{\omega}^{*}:\mathcal{A}^{d}\to\mathcal{A}^{d}
. Then
Q
is an orthogonal projection and
Qe_{k}=\theta_{\omega}\theta_{\omega}^{*}e_{k}=\theta_{\omega}\omega_{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq d
. Since
\{\omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{d}
is an equal inner product Parseval frame for
P(\mathcal{A}^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle\langle Qe_{k},Qe_{k}\rangle=\langle\theta_{\omega}\omega_{k},\theta_{\omega}\omega_{k}\rangle=\langle\omega_{k},\omega_{k}\rangle=\frac{n}{d},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq d.

Define
\tau_{k}\coloneqq Pe_{k}
for all
1\leq k\leq d
. Then we find that

\displaystyle\theta_{\tau}Pe_{k}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle Pe_{k},\tau_{j}\rangle e_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle Pe_{k},Pe_{j}\rangle e_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\langle Pe_{k},e_{j}\rangle e_{j}=Pe_{k},\quad\forall 1\leq k\leq d.

Now using Theorem
2.17
we get

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle Pe_{k}-Qe_{k},Pe_{k}-Qe_{k}\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle Pe_{k}-\omega_{k},Pe_{k}-\omega_{k}\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum_{k=1}^{d}\langle\theta_{\tau}Pe_{k}-\theta_{\omega}e_{k},\theta_{\tau}Pe_{k}-\theta_{\omega}e_{k}\rangle\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 4f(\varepsilon,d,n).

Therefore Modular Projection Problem holds.
∎

Corollary 2.19
.

Assume that the C*-algebra is commutative. If Modular Paulsen function
f
exists, then modular projection function
g
also exists and

\displaystyle g(\varepsilon,n,d)\leq 4f(\varepsilon,n,d),\quad\forall(\varepsilon,n,d)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}.

Quantum equilibration and measurements – bounds on speeds, Lyapunov exponents, and transport coefficients obtained from the uncertainty relations and their comparison with experimental data
Saurish Chakrabarty, Zohar Nussinov

Abstract
We discuss our recent study of local quantum mechanical uncertainty relations in quantum many body systems. These lead to fundamental bounds for quantities such as the speed, acceleration, relaxation times, spatial gradients and the Lyapunov exponents. We additionally obtain bounds on various transport coefficients like the viscosity, the diffusion constant, and the thermal conductivity. Some of these bounds are related to earlier conjectures, such as the bound on chaos by Maldacena, Shenker and Stanford while others are new. Our approach is a direct way of obtaining exact bounds in fairly general settings. We employ uncertainty relations for local quantities from which we strip off irrelevant terms as much as possible, thereby removing non-local terms. To gauge the utility of our bounds, we briefly compare their numerical values with typical values available from experimental data. In various cases, approximate simplified variants of the bounds that we obtain can become fairly tight, i . e . i.e. , comparable to experimental values. These considerations lead to a minimal time for thermal equilibrium to be achieved. Building on a conjectured relation between quantum measurements and equilibration , our bounds, far more speculatively, suggest a minimal time scale for measurements to stabilize to equilibrium values.

2
Bounds from local uncertainty relations in many body systems

We consider a macroscopic system
\Lambda
of
N_{\Lambda}
particles, with a density matrix
\rho_{\Lambda}
, whose dynamics is
governed by the time independent Hamiltonian
H_{\Lambda}
.
The rate of change of an arbitrary local operator
Q_{i}^{H}
in the Heisenberg picture is

\displaystyle{\frac{dQ_{i}^{H}}{dt}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[H_{\Lambda},Q_{i}^{H}\right]}
.
The subscript
i
can be thought of as a particle index.
We note that we can replace
H_{\Lambda}
in the above expression by the local Heisenberg picture Hamiltonian
\tilde{H}_{i}^{H}

which represents only the portion of
H_{\Lambda}
containing terms that do not commute with our
chosen local operator
Q_{i}^{H}
. With this,

\displaystyle{\frac{dQ_{i}^{H}}{dt}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[\tilde{H}_{i}^{H},Q_{i}^{H}\right]}
.
Next, we use the textbook type quantum uncertainty relation which is trivially provable to be valid
\Big(
via,
e.g.
, the use of Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities for Hilbert-Schmidt (trace) type inner products satisfying the inner product positive semi-definite property
\big({\sf Tr}(\rho_{\Lambda}A^{\dagger}A)\geq 0\big)
associated with the density matrices
\rho_{\Lambda}
providing expectation values
\Big)
for general mixed states,

\displaystyle{\sigma_{A}~\sigma_{B}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|\big\langle[A,B]\big\rangle\right|}
.
Here,
A
and
B
are any two operators and

\displaystyle{\sigma_{A}^{2}=\left\langle(A-\langle A\rangle)^{2}\right\rangle}
,

\displaystyle{\langle A\rangle\equiv\mbox{Tr}\left(\rho_{\Lambda}A\right)}
.
Using this,

\displaystyle{\left|\left\langle\frac{dQ_{i}^{H}}{dt}\right\rangle\right|\leq\frac{2}{\hbar}\sigma_{\tilde{H}_{i}^{H}}\sigma_{Q_{i}^{H}}}
.
Now we focus on the value of
\sigma_{\tilde{H}_{i}^{H}}^{2}
when averaged over the entire system and consider the particular case of
\rho_{\Lambda}
defining a macroscopic thermal system at a temperature
T
for which the variances may be evaluated. For a translationally invariant
system in thermal equilibrium, the variance
\left(\sigma_{\tilde{H}_{i}^{H}}\right)^{2}\equiv k_{B}T^{2}C_{v,i}
(defining an effective local heat capacity
C_{v,i}
) assumes the same value of each
i
. (The energy variance of the full many body Hamiltonian
H_{\Lambda}
is given by
k_{B}T^{2}C^{(\Lambda)}_{v}
with
C^{(\Lambda)}_{v}
the heat capacity of the global system
\Lambda
.) Putting everything together,

\displaystyle\overline{\left(\frac{\left\langle\frac{dQ^{H}}{dt}\right\rangle^{2}}{\sigma_{Q^{H}}^{2}}\right)}\leq\frac{4k_{B}T^{2}C_{v,i}}{\hbar^{2}},

(1)

where
\overline{X}\equiv\frac{1}{N_{\Lambda}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N_{\Lambda}}X_{i}
.
Even though the right hand side of Eq.
1
is independent of the spatial index
i
, we have kept it to underscore that
C_{v,i}
is an effective
local
heat capacity.

2.1
Upper bound on the relaxation rate (Lower bound on the relaxation time)

The left hand side of Eq.
1
is, dimensionally, the square of the
relaxation rate associated with the operator
Q_{i}^{H}
. This leads to a bound on the
relaxation rate,

\displaystyle\tau_{Q}^{-1}\leq\frac{2T\sqrt{k_{B}C_{v,i}}}{\hbar}.

(2)

At high temperatures, when the equipartition theorem applies,
i.e.
,

C_{v,i}={\cal O}(k_{B})
, this inequality becomes,

\tau_{Q}^{-1}\leq{\cal O}\left(2k_{B}T/\hbar\right)
,
implying that,

\displaystyle{\tau_{Q}\geq{\cal O}\left(\hbar/2k_{B}T\right)}
.

2.2
Upper bound on particle speeds and lower bounds on particle displacements

Choosing the operator
Q_{i}^{H}

\alpha^{\mbox{\tiny{th}}}
Euclidean component

\displaystyle{\overline{\left(\left\langle\frac{dr_{\alpha}^{H}}{dt}\right\rangle^{2}\Big/\sigma_{r_{\alpha}^{H}}^{2}\right)}\leq\frac{4k_{B}T^{2}C_{v,i}}{\hbar^{2}}}

Here,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\overline{\left\langle\frac{dr_{\alpha}^{H}}{dt}\right\rangle^{2}}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}k_{B}T\sigma_{r_{\alpha}^{H}}}{\hbar}.

(3)

A related bound for the expectation value of the square of the velocity components can also be
obtained using a similar analysis.

Thus, at high temperatures,

\displaystyle{\overline{\left\langle\left(\frac{dr_{\alpha}^{H}}{dt}\right)^{2}\right\rangle}\leq\frac{2\left(k_{B}T\right)^{2}\sigma_{r_{\alpha}^{H}}^{2}}{\hbar^{2}}}

The advantage of this relation is that in the classical limit, the left hand side takes the
value
\frac{k_{B}T}{m}

\displaystyle\sigma_{r_{\alpha}^{H}}^{2}\geq\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2mk_{B}T}=\frac{\lambda_{T}^{2}}{4\pi},

(4)

\lambda_{T}
being the thermal de Broglie wavelength.

Conclusions

Our local quantum uncertainty based bounds on the relaxation times in equilibrated quantum systems
[
12
,
10
]
are intimately related to conjectured Matsubara like Planckian time
scales
[

These bounds may further relate to possible fundamental limits on measurement and equilibration times (a conjectured connection between measurement and equilibration was briefly reviewed). Our lower bound on the shear viscosity is closely connected to proposed bounds on the viscosity to entropy density ratio
[

,

Our upper bound on the shear viscosity in equilibrated systems, that follows from the

[
12
]

[

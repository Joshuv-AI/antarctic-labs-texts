Invariant measures and global well-posedness for a fractional Schrödinger equation with Moser-Trudinger type nonlinearity
Jean-Baptiste Casteras, Léonard Monsaingeon

Abstract
In this paper, we construct invariant measures and global-in-time solutions for a fractional Schrödinger equation with a Moser-Trudinger type nonlinearity i ​ ∂ t u = ( − Δ ) α ​ u + 2 ​ β ​ u ​ e β ​ | u | 2 for ( x , t ) ∈ M × ℝ i\partial_{t}u=(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+2\beta ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad(x,t)\in\ M\times\mathbb{R} (E) on a compact Riemannian manifold M M without boundary of dimension d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 . To do so, we use the so-called Inviscid-Infinite-dimensional limits introduced by Sy (’19) and Sy and Yu (’21). More precisely, we show that if s > d / 2 s>d/2 or if s ≤ d / 2 s\leq d/2 and s ≤ 1 + α s\leq 1+\alpha , there exists an invariant measure μ s \mu^{s} and a set Σ s ⊂ H s \Sigma^{s}\subset H^{s} containing arbitrarily large data such that μ s ​ ( Σ s ) = 1 \mu^{s}(\Sigma^{s})=1 and that ( E ) is globally well-posed on Σ s \Sigma^{s} . Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the construction of invariant measures and global-in-time solutions for the following fractional Schrödinger equation with defocusing Moser-Trudinger type nonlinearity

i\partial_{t}u=(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+2\beta ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad(x,t)\in\ M\times\mathbb{R}.

(1.1)

Here
\alpha>0
and
\beta>0
are fixed parameters, and
M
is a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary of dimension
d\geq 1
.

This equation has been introduced in
[
LLT
]
for
\alpha=1
to describe a self-focusing laser beam whose radius is much greater than the vacuum wavelength.
Equation (
1.1
) is also interesting from a purely mathematical point of view since when
d=2
the nonlinearity is energy critical, where the energy is

E(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}+\int_{M}e^{\beta|u|^{2}}\,dx.

(1.2)

To illustrate this point, let us consider the fractional Schrödinger equation with pure power nonlinearity

i\partial_{t}u=(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+|u|^{p-1}u.

(1.3)

Recall that it satisfies two conservation laws, in the sense that the mass and the
p
-energy

M(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad E_{p}(u)=\dfrac{1}{2}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}+\dfrac{1}{p+1}\|u\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1}

are preserved along the evolution, at least formally.
Moreover, (
1.3
) enjoys a scaling invariance

u_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(\lambda^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}x,\lambda t),\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad\lambda>0

and therefore the critical
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}
Sobolev regularity is given by the critical exponent

s_{c}=\dfrac{d}{2}-\dfrac{2\alpha}{p-1}.

The power-NLS is said energy super-critical (resp. energy critical, resp. energy subcritical) if
s_{c}>\alpha
(resp.
s_{c}=\alpha
, resp.
s_{c}<\alpha
).
Viewing formally the exponential nonlinearity as an infinite-degree polynomial
e^{\beta|u|^{2}}u=\sum\dfrac{\beta^{k}}{k!}|u|^{2k}u
we see that the critical Sobolev scale for (
1.1
) is given by
p=\infty
, namely

s_{c}=\dfrac{d}{2}.

As a consequence (
1.1
) is energy critical in dimension
d=2
for the standard diffusion
\alpha=1
.
When
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}
, this equation has been intensively studied see
[
CIMM09
,
IMMN12
]
.
We also refer to
[
Caz79
,
Nak15
,
WH07
]
for related equations with the same type of nonlinearity.
In particular, the global well-posedness of (
1.1
) for small initial data and defocusing nonlinearity has been obtained in
[
CIMM09
]
when
M=\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\alpha=1
.
In the same setting, a scattering result for small data when
\beta=4\pi
was proved in
[
IMMN12
]
.
On the other hand, when
M
is a compact manifold, it is interesting to investigate the behavior of (
1.1
) since scattering should not be expected.
In fact, one expects that generic solutions should exhibit some energy cascade from low to high frequencies, which should presumably be detected by the unboundedness
\limsup_{|t|\rightarrow\infty}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}=+\infty
for large enough
s
.
Our main goal in this paper is to construct some invariant measures, prove probabilistic global well-posedness for (
1.1
), and obtain some information about the long-time behavior of solutions (or rather, the possible growth rate of their Sobolev norms).

The first result in this direction was obtained by Bourgain
[
Bou94
]
for a nonlinear Schrödinger equation in dimension
d=1
.
The main obstruction to obtaining global solutions at this low level of regularity is the lack of conservation laws.
In order to tackle this issue, Bourgain was able to use the Gibbs measure constructed by Lebowitz, Rose, and Speer
[
LRS88
]
(supported on
H^{1/2-}
) in order to obtain bounds for some
N
-dimensional Galerkin projections of the equation (which were already used in the construction of the Gibbs measure).
Most notably, these bounds were independent of the dimension
N
.
Comparing the full problem with its finite-dimensional projections, Bourgain then managed to prove the global existence of solutions.
To some extent, invariant measures allow conservation laws to survive even in low regularity, at least in a statistical sense.

Concerning our specific equation, Gibbs measures were constructed by Robert in
[
Rob
]
.
More precisely, let us set up some notations.
We denote the potential

V_{\beta}(u)=\int_{M}e^{\beta|u|^{2}}dx

and the reference Gaussian measure is (at least formally)

``\,\mu_{\alpha}(du)=\prod\limits_{n\geq 0}\exp\left(-\dfrac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{|\lambda_{n}|}\right)^{2\alpha}|\hat{u}_{n}|^{2}\right)d\hat{u}_{n}\,",

(1.4)

where
\hat{u}_{n}=\langle u,e_{n}\rangle
is the
n
-th Fourier coefficient in the
L^{2}
expansion of
u
on the Hilbert basis
\{e_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
given by the Laplace-Beltrami eigenfunctions with eigenvalues
-\lambda_{n}
.
Notice that
\mu_{\alpha}
is the law of the
L^{2}
-valued random variable

u_{\alpha}^{\omega}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\dfrac{g_{n}(\omega)}{(1+|\lambda_{n}|)^{\alpha}}e_{n},

where
g_{n}
is a family of independent standard complex-valued Gaussians on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
.
In
[
Rob
]
it is shown that, if
\alpha>d/2
then

\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(du)=\dfrac{e^{-V_{\beta}(u)}}{\int_{L^{2}}e^{-V_{\beta}(u)}\mu_{\alpha}(du)}\mu_{\alpha}(du)

is a well-defined probability measure supported on
H^{(\alpha-\frac{d}{2})^{-}}(M)
and is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu_{\alpha}
.
Let us point out that the condition
\alpha>d/2
is really necessary for the existence of the Gibbs measure, and that the support
\supp\rho_{\alpha,\beta}
necessarily corresponds to rough regularity.

Robert then uses this Gibbs measure in order to construct global solutions to (
1.1
):
for fixed
\alpha>d/2
, small enough
0<\beta<\beta_{0}
, and any
0<s<\alpha-\frac{d}{2}
, there exists a random variable
u
with values in
C(\mathbb{R};H^{s})
solving (
1.1
) in the distributional sense, and
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}
remains invariant over time.
In the stronger dispersion regime
\alpha>d
, one can in fact retrieve more information and prove that, for any
0<\beta\ll\beta_{0}
, the evolution (
1.1
) is
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}
-almost surely
globally
well-posed and that the Gibbs measure
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}
is flow-invariant.
More precisely, for fixed
\alpha>d
and any
s\in(\frac{d}{2},\alpha-\frac{d}{2})
, the flow is always locally well-posed and there exists a set
\Sigma^{s}\subset H^{s}
of full
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}
-measure such that, for any
u_{0}\in\Sigma^{s}
, the flow
\phi^{t}(u_{0})
is globally defined and
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}
is invariant under
\phi^{t}
.
Invariance is understood here in the sense that, for any
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}
-measurable set
A\subset\Sigma^{s}
, there holds
\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(\phi^{t}(A))=\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(A)
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
For weak dispersions
d/2<\alpha\leq d
, let us stress that even the
local
well-posedness is problematic (in particular uniqueness poses serious issues).

An alternative approach to the construction of Gibbs measures was developed by Kuksin
[
Kuk04
]
for the
2
-d Euler equation, and adapted by Kuksin and Shirikyan
[
KS04
]
for the cubic Schrödinger equation.
This is known as the
fluctuation-dissipation method
, and consists in considering a stochastic perturbation of the original equation, enjoying suitable enhanced dissipation properties and possessing a stationary measure for any given viscosity parameter.
Using a compactness argument, one wishes to show that this family of invariant measures has a weak limit as the viscosity vanishes, and that this limit is as expected an invariant measure for the original equation.
As a byproduct of the construction, suitable estimates (uniform in the level of noise) can be used next to prove long-time existence results.
We refer to
[
FS21
,
KS12
,
Sy18
,
Lat20
,
SYc
,
SYd
]
for related results.

Sy
[
Sya
]
and Sy and Yu
[
SYb
]
combined these two approaches into what they call
Inviscid-Infinite-dimensional limits
, and constructed invariant measures for a class of energy-supercritical NLS equations with pure-power nonlinearities.
This method consists in using a fluctuation-dissipation argument on
N
-dimensional Galerkin approximations.
In doing so, one obtains first a stationary measure for any fixed viscosity
\sigma>0
and dimension
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
An invariant measure for the original equation is then retrieved by taking first an inviscid limit
\sigma\to 0
and then an infinite-dimensional limit
N\to\infty
.
This is exactly the method that we implement in this work, with several technical improvements and difficulties arising here due to the exponential nonlinearity.

Throughout the whole paper we focus on the energy critical and supercritical cases only, i-e
\alpha\leq d/2
. (The subcritical case can be handled by standard fixed point methods.)
In the high regularity setting,
s>d/2
, our main result reads

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\alpha\leq d/2
, and fix
s>d/2
.
There exist an increasing concave function
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
, a probability measure
\mu^{s}\in\mathfrak{p}(L^{2})
supported on
H^{s}
and a set
\Sigma^{s}\subset H^{s}
such that

•

\mu^{s}(\Sigma^{s})=1

•

equation (
1.1
) is globally well-posed on
\Sigma^{s}

•

the induced flow
\phi^{t}
leaves the measure
\mu^{s}
invariant

•

we have

\int_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\mu^{s}(du)<\infty.

•

the set
\Sigma^{s}
contains data of arbitrarily large size,
\mu^{s}(\{\|u\|_{H^{s}}>K\})>0
for all
K>0

•

for any fixed
r<s
and all
u_{0}\in\Sigma^{s}
we have

\|\phi_{t}(u_{0})\|_{H^{r}}\leq C\zeta(1+\ln(1+|t|))\qquad\text{for all }t\in\mathbb{R},

for some
C=C(u_{0},r)
.

Upon closer inspection, our construction actually yields a rough but explicit upper bound of the form
\zeta(z)\sim C\sqrt{|z|}
for large
|z|
and some
C
depending on the various parameters.
(See the proof of Proposition
3.8
for details.)

In the low regularity regime
s\leq d/2
we will establish

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\alpha\leq 1
, and fix
s\leq\min\{d/2,1+\alpha\}
.
There exists a probability measure
\mu^{s}\in\mathfrak{p}(L^{2})
supported on
H^{s}
, a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
, and a
L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};H^{s})\cap C(\mathbb{R};H^{s-\alpha})
-valued stochastic process
u
such that

•

u=u^{\omega}
is a global distributional solution of (
1.1
) for
\mathbb{P}
-a.a.
\omega\in\Omega
;

•

\mu^{s}
is invariant in the sense that the law of
u(t)
is
\mu^{s}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}

•

We have

\int_{L^{2}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\mu^{s}(dv)<\infty.

•

\supp\,\mu^{s}
contains data of arbitrarily large size,
\mu^{s}(\{\|u\|_{H^{s}}>K\})>0
for all
K>0
.

Remark 1.3
.

The
C(\mathbb{R};H^{r-\alpha})
continuity of a.a. trajectory
u^{\omega}
appears here for technical convenience, and it seems plausible that trajectories should in fact be continuous for the
H^{s}
topology.

Although the statement of Theorem
1.2
is very similar in spirit with Theorem
1.1
, it is worth pointing out that the very notion of invariance is slightly different and that the rigorous statement is more probabilistic in nature than for high regularity.
Indeed, for
s>d/2
the global solutions in Theorem
1.1
will be somehow constructed by hand, and the induced flow
u(t)=\phi^{t}(u_{0})
is deterministically well-posed at least for small times.
However, at the very low level of
H^{s}
regularity involved in Theorem
1.2
above, even uniqueness of solutions to (
1.1
) may fail (in addition to existence), so the very notion of local flow is delicate.
The stochastic setting here somehow allows for an implicit selection of solutions (arising from a passage to the infinite-dimensional limit
N\to\infty
in our construction), hence the statement does not even mention flow-maps and must be weakened in order to tackle this issue.
Apart from that, the only but major difference compared with Theorem
1.1
is that we lose quantitative control over the
H^{r}
growth of solutions.

Observe that Theorem
1.1
holds in the whole supercritical range
\alpha\in(0,d/2]
.
This is no longer the case for Theorem
1.2
, since the proof relies at some point on a Córdoba-Córdoba type lemma crucially requiring
\alpha\leq 1
.
This restriction seems artificial, as one expects that higher dispersion should simplify the analysis, and indeed this is a purely technical obstacle due to our specific construction.
We point out that, also for technical reasons, we were not able to cover the full range of Sobolev exponents in the low regularity regime, and
s\in(1+\alpha,d/2]
is not covered in Theorem
1.2
if
d/2>1+\alpha
.
The very same gap also appears in Sy’s work
[
SYc
]
for the same reasons.

As far as optimality is concerned, let us stress that (i) for the unregularized equation (
1.1
) there are a priori only two conserved quantities
E(u),M(u)
, (ii) given the choice of a dissipation operator
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{s}
involved in our construction by fluctuation-dissipation (to be described shortly), the best one can hope to control is the variation
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}
of
E,M
in the direction of
\mathcal{L}_{s}
, and (iii) our current choices of
\mathcal{L}_{s}
control at best the
H^{s}
norm, and no better.
This strongly suggests that our measures constructed in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
should be somehow optimal, in the sense that they should satisfy
\int_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{H^{r}}^{2}\mu^{s}(du)=\infty
for any
r>s
and should thus be
H^{s}
critical for given
s
.
(We will not discuss any further this delicate optimality issue in this work.)

Finally, we wish to emphasize that our results allow for arbitrary exponential rates
\beta>0
in the nonlinearity, contrarily to
[
Rob
]
.
It is also worth stressing that we restrict here to defocusing nonlinearities: Indeed, a cornerstone of our analysis will consist in retrieving good statistical control on some suitable dissipation functionals
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}
(to be defined later), thus allowing conservation laws to survive in low regularity and yielding sufficient compactness throughout the successive inviscid-infinite-dimensional limits.
In the focusing case these functionals fail to be coercive, and the whole analysis collapses.

In this work we shall only address the existence of invariant measures and the associated almost-sure global well-posedness.
An interesting and more difficult question is to derive qualitative properties of these measures, e.g. the dimensionality of the supports and the absolute continuity with respect to the Gaussian measure (
1.4
).
These issues are left for future work.

Structure of the proof

Let us now describe informally our Inviscid-Infinite-dimensional construction, based on Sy
[
Sya
]
and Sy and Yu
[
SYb
]
.
Let
E_{N}
be the space generated by the first
N
eigenvalues of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M
, and
P_{N}
the orthogonal projector thereupon.
On
E_{N}
, we define a Brownian motion and white noise by

\zeta_{N}(t,x)=\sum_{|m|\leq N}a_{m}\beta_{m}(t)e_{m}(x)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\eta_{N}(t,x)=\dfrac{d}{dt}\zeta_{N}(t,x),

where
(a_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an arbitrary family of complex numbers (to be well-chosen later on and satisfying some decay conditions) and
\beta_{m}
is a sequence of independent standard real Brownian motions.
We consider the following (finite-dimensional) SDE

\partial_{t}u=-i\left[(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+P_{N}\left(2\beta ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right)\right]-\sigma^{2}\mathcal{L}(u)+\sigma\eta_{N},

(1.5)

where
\sigma>0
is a small viscosity parameter.
Here
\mathcal{L}
is a (deterministic) dissipation operator to be well-chosen below, and the Brownian motion of course induces stochastic fluctuations in the system.
Clearly as
\sigma\to 0
and
N\to\infty
this is a perturbation of (
1.1
), and note that scaling
\sigma^{2}\mathcal{L}
and
\sigma\eta_{N}
is consistent with Itô’s formula.
For technical reasons we will choose different dissipation operators
\mathcal{L}
, tailored to either strong or low regularity regimes.
In any case the choice of
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{s}
will be subordinated to the prior choice of a fixed Sobolev exponent
s>0
, thus targeting a chosen
H^{s}
regularity.

We will prove that (
1.5
) is always stochastically well-posed on
E_{N}
(see Definition
3.1
), and standard finite-dimensional Bogoliubov-Krylov arguments will allow to produce a stationary measure
\mu^{s}_{\sigma,N}
on
E_{N}
.
The energy and mass dissipation

\mathcal{E}(u)=E^{\prime}(u;\mathcal{L}(u))\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathcal{M}(u)=M^{\prime}(u;\mathcal{L}(u))

in the direction of
\mathcal{L}
will be our two fundamental functionals, yielding barely sufficient compactness and
H^{s}
control throughout the whole analysis.
More precisely, Itô’s formula will yield

\int_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}(u)\mu^{s}_{\sigma,N}(du)\leq C\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\int_{L^{2}}\mathcal{M}(u)\mu^{s}_{\sigma,N}(du)\leq C

uniformly in
\sigma,N
.
Owing to our specific choices of
\mathcal{L}
(for both strong and low regularities), the resulting functionals
\mathcal{E},\mathcal{M}
will enjoy suitable coercivity and give just enough compactness on the family of stationary measures
\left\{\mu^{s}_{\sigma,N}\right\}_{\sigma,N}
.
Taking first the limit
\sigma\rightarrow 0
and then
N\rightarrow\infty
, we establish the weak convergence of
\mu^{s}_{\sigma,N}
(in
H^{r}
for all
r<s
) to a measure
\mu^{s}
satisfying the same estimates.
Fine-tuning the Brownian coefficients
(a_{m})_{m}
, one can moreover slightly modify
\mu^{s}
in order to get initial data with arbitrarily large
H^{s}
norm, which guarantees in particular that
\mu^{s}
is not trivially concentrated on the zero solution.

At this stage it remains to construct global solutions for a large-enough class of initial data
u_{0}\in\Sigma^{s}
(with
\mu^{s}(\Sigma^{s})=1
), and this is when the construction starts to really differ for strong and weak regularities.

For high regularities
s>d/2
we choose to include a strong damping term
\mathcal{L}(u)=(\dots)+G(\|u\|_{H^{s}})u
in the dissipation operator, for some function
G:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
growing sufficiently fast in order to overtake the exponential nonlinearity.
The Sobolev embedding
H^{s}\subset L^{\infty}
then allows to control the
H^{s}
norms of the projected solutions uniformly in
N,\sigma
.
This puts us in position of implementing Bourgain’s strategy
[
Bou94
]
to construct a full
\mu^{s}
-measure set
\Sigma^{s}\subset H^{s}
on which (
1.1
) is globally well-posed.
The invariance of
\mu^{s}
will naturally follow from some strong convergence of the projected flow-map
\phi^{N}_{t}
towards its unprojected counterpart
\phi^{t}
.
Let us point out that this construction also provides a quantitative estimate on the slow logarithmic growth of the
H^{r}
-norm of the global solutions constructed here, inherited from the control for finite
N
.

For low regularities
s\leq d/2
the failure of the Sobolev embedding
H^{s}\not\subset L^{\infty}
prevents from controlling the nonlinearity in
H^{s}
, thus one cannot expect to control the growth of solutions even for the projected equation.
In order to circumvent this issue we choose now to include the nonlinearity
\mathcal{L}(u)=(\dots)+P_{N}\left(ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right)
in the dissipation operator.
This will result in coercivity
\mathcal{M}(u)\gtrsim\int|u|^{2}e^{\beta|u|^{2}}
in the mass dissipation functional, which in turn will allow a statistical control
\int_{L^{2}}\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}\mu^{s}_{\sigma,N}(du)
for the nonlinearity in any finite time interval.
However, we only managed to control some residual crossed terms via a suitable Cordóba-Cordóba inequality requiring
s\leq\alpha+1
as well as2
\alpha\leq 1
, and our analysis unfortunately does not cover the range
s\in(\alpha+1,d/2]
(we were simply not able to find a dissipator
\mathcal{L}
allowing to control the nonlinearity while dominating the crossed terms.)
At this stage, a soft but very flexible Skorokhod-type argument by Burq, Thomann and Tzvetkov
[
BTT18
]
allows to conclude that
\sigma^{s}=\lim\sigma^{s}_{\sigma,N}
is invariant, as the limit of invariant measures for which the nonlinearities
P_{N}(u_{N}e^{\beta|u_{N}|^{2}})\to ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}
enjoy sufficient convergence (precisely owing to Skorokhod’s representation theorem).
We also refer to
[
AC90
,
DPD02
]
for earlier implementations of this strategy in a fluid-mechanical context.
We stress that, for such weak regularities
s\leq d/2
, we do not obtain quantitative control on the growth of
H^{r}
-norm of the solutions.
As already pointed out, this is the only significant difference between Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.1
.

The plan of this paper is the following:
in Section
2
we establish several basic properties satisfied by solutions of finite-dimensional Galerkin projections of (
1.1
).
In particular, we prove existence of a unique global solution in
C(\mathbb{R};H^{s})
.
Moreover, for strong regularities
s>d/2
we derive suitable quantitative bounds on the
H^{r}
norms in fixed time interval, uniformly in
N
.
In the same regime, we also obtain some continuity properties of the flow-map
\phi^{t}
associated with (
1.1
) and of its projected approximation.

Section
3
focuses on the strong regularity
s>d/2
and actually contains the core of the paper: we carry out the full technical details for the above construction and prove Theorem
1.1
.
This section is the most involved of the paper, as a significant part will be recycled in the next section (mostly the construction of the invariant measure by compactness arguments).

Section
4
is devoted to the low regular setting, Theorem
1.2
.
As most of the technical details are similar to the previous section, we will not give the full details for the construction of the measure
\mu^{s}
.
We will of course highlight the differences and give a full proof for the significantly different construction of global solutions as well as for the invariance of the measure.

Finally, some technical and auxiliary results are collected in the Appendix.

2
Preliminaries and Galerkin projections

Let
0=\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{m}\leq\ldots
be the eigenvalues of the Laplace-Beltrami operator
-\Delta
on
M
.
For notational convenience we write
\lambda_{-m}=\lambda_{m}
.
We denote by
(e_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
the corresponding eigenfunctions and by
e_{-m}
the eigenfunction
ie_{m}
, so that
(e_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a Hilbert basis for
L^{2}(M;\mathbb{C})
.

We denote by
\|.\|
the standard
L^{2}
norm with corresponding scalar product

(u,v)=\mathcal{R}\int_{M}u\bar{v}\,dx,

where
\mathcal{R}
stands for the real part.
For
u=\sum u_{m}e_{m}
expressed in this Hilbert basis, the standard Sobolev norm reads

\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}=\|u\|^{2}+\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}=\sum\limits_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}(1+\lambda_{m}^{s})|u_{m}|^{2},

Let now
E_{N}
be the subspace of
L^{2}
spanned by
(e_{m})_{|m|\leq N}
, and let
P_{N}
be the orthogonal projection onto
E_{N}
.
On the finite-dimensional space
E_{N}
, we have the norm equivalence

c\|u\|\leq\|u\|_{L^{q}}\leq C_{N,q}\|u\|

and

\|u\|\leq\|u\|_{H^{s}}\leq\left(1+\lambda_{N}^{s/2}\right)\|u\|

for any
q\in(2,\infty]
and
s\in(0,\infty)
.

For
\gamma>0
the spectral powers of the Laplacian are defined as

(-\Delta)^{\gamma}u=\sum\lambda_{m}^{\gamma}u_{m}e_{m}\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad u=\sum u_{m}e_{m},

and we recall that

and

commute.
On

we define the dispersive propagator

by

S(t)u=e^{-it(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}u=\sum e^{-it\lambda_{m}^{\alpha}}u_{m}e_{m},

and we recall that it is unitary in the sense that

\|S(t)u\|_{H^{s}}=\|u\|_{H^{s}}\qquad\mbox{for all}\qquad s\geq 0,\,u\in H^{s}.

In this setting, we consider now the Galerkin projection of (
1.1
)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u=(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+P_{N}\left(2\beta ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right),\\
u(0)=P_{N}u_{0},\end{cases}

(2.1)

which is nothing but a nonlinear ODE on the finite-dimensional space

(note that

maps

to itself).

Let us start with various technical preliminaries on the well-posedness of (
1.1
) and (
2.1
).
For fixed

and

we write

X_{T}^{r}=C([-T,T];H^{r}(M))\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad\|u\|_{X^{r}_{T}}=\sup\limits_{t\in[-T,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{r}}.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix

.
For any
R>0
, there exists a time
T>0
such that, for every

and

, there exists a unique solution

to (

).
This time can be taken of the form

(2.2)

for some small

and large

depending on

only.
Moreover, this solution is in fact global-in-time and satisfies

\|u\|_{X_{T}^{s}}=\sup_{t\in[-T,T]}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\leq 2R.

(2.3)

The important point here is that we have a quantitative control (
2.3
) on the
H^{s}
norm in any fixed time interval uniformly in

.

Proof.

Let
u_{0}\in B_{R}(H^{s})
.
For

we set

F(u)=S(t)P_{N}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)P_{N}\left(2\beta ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right)d\tau,

and observe of course that

solves (

) with initial datum

if and only if it is a fixed point for

.
We have, using the fact that

is unitary in

and Lemma

,

\displaystyle\|F(u)\|_{X_{T}^{s}}\leq\|P_{N}u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+2\beta\int_{0}^{T}\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+C\beta T\|u\|_{X^{s}_{T}}e^{C\beta\|u\|_{X^{s}_{T}}^{2}}.

Taking
T\lesssim\dfrac{c}{\beta e^{C\beta R^{2}}}
for

small enough and

large enough (only depending on

\|F(u)\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\frac{1}{2}\|u\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\leq 2R
and thus

maps

to itself.

Let now

be two functions in

.
By Lemma

and because

is unitary on

, we have

\|F(u_{1})-F(u_{2})\|_{X_{T}^{s}}=\left\|2i\beta\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)P_{N}\left(u_{1}e^{\beta|u_{1}|^{2}}-u_{2}e^{\beta|u_{2}|^{2}}\right)d\tau\right\|_{X^{T}_{s}}\\
\leq C\beta\int_{0}^{T}\left\|u_{1}e^{\beta|u_{1}|^{2}}-u_{2}e^{\beta|u_{2}|^{2}}\right\|_{X^{s}_{T}}d\tau\\
\leq C\beta Te^{C\beta\left(\|u_{1}\|^{2}_{X_{T}^{s}}+\|u_{2}\|_{X^{s}_{T}}^{2}\right)}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X^{s}_{T}}\leq C\beta Te^{8C\beta R^{2}}\|u_{1}-u_{2}\|_{X^{s}_{T}}.

(2.4)

This shows that

is a contraction on

, again as soon as

for some small

and large

depending on

only.
By the Banach fixed point Theorem we conclude that there exists a unique solution

to (

) in

.

Finally, at least for

there holds
\left(i(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u,u\right)=0
and
\left(iP_{N}\left(ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right),u\right)=\left(iue^{\beta|u|^{2}},u\right)=0
, whence the conservation of mass

along solutions of (

On the finite-dimensional space

this guarantees that

remains in a fixed compact set (a ball of radius

), hence blowup in finite time is excluded and

is thus global-in-time.
∎

In the low regularity setting we have

Proposition 2.2

Proof.

The proof is quite similar to the previous one, with the major difference that

Taking

∎

We denote by

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix

, let

for some

, and choose

as in (

If

is any sequence such that

in

then

\|\phi^{t}(u_{0})-\phi_{N}^{t}(u_{0,N})\|_{X_{T}^{r}}\to 0\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{as }N\to\infty.

(2.5)

Moreover both flows are locally Lipschitz continuous, in the sense that if

and

as before, then

\|\phi^{t}(u_{0})-\phi^{t}(u^{\prime}_{0})\|_{X_{T}^{r}}\leq C\|u_{0}-u^{\prime}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}

(2.6)

and

\|\phi_{N}^{t}(u_{0})-\phi_{N}^{t}(u^{\prime}_{0})\|_{X_{T}^{r}}\leq C\|u_{0}-u^{\prime}_{0}\|_{H^{r}}

(2.7)

for some

Proof.

Let us begin with (
2.5
).
Note first that, if
N
is large enough, then
u_{0},u_{0,N}\in B_{2R}
hence
\phi^{t}(u_{0})
and
\phi^{t}_{N}(u_{0,N})
are both defined at least for times

and both remain in

(Propositions

and
2.3
).
Using Duhamel’s formula, we have, for any

\displaystyle\phi^{t}(u_{0})

\displaystyle=S(t)u_{0}-2i\beta\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})e^{\beta|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})|^{2}}d\tau,

\displaystyle\phi_{N}^{t}(u_{0,N})

\displaystyle=S(t)u_{0,N}-2i\beta\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)P_{N}\left(\phi_{N}^{\tau}(u_{0,N})e^{\beta\left|\phi_{N}^{\tau}(u_{0,N})\right|^{2}}\right)d\tau.

Taking the difference, we get

\displaystyle\phi^{t}(u_{0})-\phi_{N}^{t}(u_{0,N})

\displaystyle=S(t)(u_{0}-u_{0,N})-2i\beta\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)(1-P_{N})\left(\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})e^{\beta|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})|^{2}}\right)d\tau

\displaystyle+2i\beta\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)P_{N}\left[e^{\beta\left|\phi^{\tau}_{N}(u_{0,N})\right|^{2}}\phi_{N}^{\tau}(u_{0,N})-e^{\beta|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})|^{2}}\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})\right]d\tau.

Using that

is unitary in
H^{r}
, exploiting Lemma

to control the last integral, and recalling that

remain contained in a fixed ball

for times

, we get

\displaystyle\|\phi^{t}(u_{0})-\phi_{N}^{t}(u_{0,N})\|_{H^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}-u_{0,N}\|_{H^{r}}+C\int_{0}^{T}\left\|(1-P_{N})\left[\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})e^{\beta|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})|^{2}}\right]\right\|_{H^{r}}d\tau

\displaystyle+Ce^{16C\beta R^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})-\phi_{N}^{\tau}(u_{0,N})\right\|_{H^{r}}d\tau.

(2.8)

Because
f(\tau)=\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})e^{\beta|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})|^{2}}\in H^{r}
it is easy to see, for fixed

, that we have pointwise convergence

\left\|(1-P_{N})\left[\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})e^{\beta|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})|^{2}}\right]\right\|_{H^{r}}\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0\qquad\mbox{for a.e. }\tau\in[-T,T],

as the remainder

of an absolutely convergent series.
The uniform bound

I_{N}=\int_{0}^{T}\left\|(1-P_{N})\left[\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})e^{\beta|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})|^{2}}\right]\right\|_{H^{r}}d\tau\xrightarrow[N\to\infty]{}0.

Our previous estimate (

\|\phi^{t}(u_{0})-\phi_{N}^{t}(u_{0,N})\|_{H^{r}}\leq{\varepsilon}_{N}+C\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\phi_{N}^{\tau}(u_{0,N})-\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})\right\|_{H^{r}}d\tau

with

as

.
Grönwall’s inequality leads to

\|\phi^{t}(u_{0})-\phi_{N}^{t}(u_{0,N})\|_{H^{r}}\leq{\varepsilon}_{N}e^{CT}

and (

For (

) we take the difference

\phi^{t}(u_{0})-\phi^{t}(u_{0}^{\prime})=S(t)(u_{0}-u_{0}^{\prime})\\
-2i\beta\int_{0}^{t}S(t-\tau)\left(\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})e^{\beta|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})|^{2}}-\phi^{\tau}(u_{0}^{\prime})e^{\beta|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0}^{\prime})|^{2}}\right)d\tau.

Because we know that
\phi^{\tau}(u_{0}),\phi^{\tau}(u_{0}^{\prime})\in B_{2R}(H^{r})
for all

, and

is unitary, our Lemma

allows to control

\|\phi^{t}(u_{0})-\phi^{t}(u_{0}^{\prime})\|_{H^{r}}\leq\|u_{0}-u_{0}^{\prime}\|_{H^{r}}+C\int_{0}^{t}\|\phi^{\tau}(u_{0})-\phi^{\tau}(u_{0}^{\prime})\|_{H^{r}}d\tau

The proof of (

3
High regularity

In this section we focus exclusively on the high regularity regime
s>d/2
, and recall that we only consider the energy critical and supercritical cases
\alpha\leq d/2
.
The reader can think of the
H^{s}
scale as being fixed once and for all in this section, and we will rather use the
H^{r}
notation for any other Sobolev spaces,
r<s
.
Let us first set-up our fluctuation-dissipation framework.

3.1
Fluctuation-dissipation and a priori estimates

On
E_{N}
, we define a Brownian motion and a white noise by

\zeta_{N}(t,x)=\sum_{|m|\leq N}a_{m}\beta_{m}(t)e_{m}(x)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\eta_{N}(t,x)=\dfrac{d}{dt}\zeta_{N}(t,x),

(3.1)

where
(a_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}
is an arbitrary family of complex numbers (satisfying some decay conditions to be precised soon),
\beta_{m}
is a sequence of independent standard real Brownian motions with respect to a filtration
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
and defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
.

Since
s>d/2
and we consider the energy supercritical case only
\alpha\in(0,d/2]
, we have in particular
s>\alpha
.
We define the dissipation operator

\mathcal{L}(u)=\left[(-\Delta)^{s-\alpha}+G(\|u\|_{H^{s}})\right]u

(3.2)

for some smooth, nondecreasing function
G:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

G(\rho)=c\rho^{2}e^{\Lambda\beta\rho^{2}}

(3.3)

for some small
c>0
and large
\Lambda>0
.
Here one can think of
\Lambda
to be as large as needed, in fact we will at times adjust
\Lambda\geq C
for various constants
C=C(s)
related to the Sobolev embedding via the exponential constant in Lemma
A.1
.

For some small parameter
\sigma>0
and fixed exponent
\alpha>0
, consider the Stochastic PDE

\partial_{t}u=-i\left[(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+P_{N}\left(2\beta ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right)\right]-\sigma^{2}\mathcal{L}(u)+\sigma\eta_{N}.

(3.4)

We think of
\mathcal{L}
as a deterministic regularizing dissipation (hence the minus sign), and the white noise induces stochastic fluctuations in the system.
As already pointed out, the scaling
\sigma^{2}\mathcal{L}
and
\sigma\eta_{N}
is consistent with Itô’s formula and will be crucial in order to retrieve enough compactness estimates and take the limit
\sigma\to 0^{+}
later on.

By solutions of the SPDE, we mean here:

Definition 3.1

(Stochastic global well-posedness)
.

We say that (
3.4
) is stochastically globally well-posed on
E_{N}
if the following properties hold:

(i)

for any
E_{N}
-valued random variable
u_{0}
independent of
\mathcal{F}_{t}

•

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.a.
\omega
there exists a solution
u=u^{\omega}\in C(\mathbb{R}^{+};E_{N})
of (
3.4
) with initial datum
u_{0}=u_{0}^{\omega}
in the integral sense, i-e

u(t)=u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\left[-i\left((-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+P_{N}\left(2\beta ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right)\right)-\sigma^{2}\mathcal{L}(u)\right]d\tau+\sigma\zeta_{N}(t)

(3.5)

with equality as elements of
C(\mathbb{R}^{+};E_{N})
.

•

if

are two such solutions with the same initial data

.

(ii)

(pathwise continuity w.r.t. initial data)

\mathbb{P}
-a.a.
\omega\in\Omega
, we require that

\lim_{u_{0}\rightarrow u_{0}^{\prime}}u^{\omega}(.\,;u_{0})=u^{\omega}(.\,;u_{0}^{\prime})\ \quad\mbox{in}\quad\ C_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{+};E_{N}),

where

and

are deterministic data in

are the corresponding solutions of (

) for fixed
\omega

(iii)

the

-valued stochastic process

is adapted to the filtration
\sigma(u_{0},\mathcal{F}_{t}).

Owing to (

), any such solution, if any, is unique.
Also, such a process solves the finite-dimensional
forward
Itô SDE

du=\left(-i\left[(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u+P_{N}\left(2\beta ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right)\right]-\sigma^{2}\mathcal{L}(u)\right)dt+\sigma d\zeta_{N}

in the classical sense.

Proposition 3.2
.

Equation (
3.4
) is stochastically globally well-posed on
E_{N}
in the sense of Definition
3.1
.

Proof.

We will construct the solution in the form
u=z+v
, where
z
will first contain all the Brownian fluctuation and

To this end let us first introduce

z(t)=\sigma\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i(t-\tau)(-\Delta)^{\alpha}}d\zeta_{N}(\tau).

(3.6)

This is well-defined for
\mathbb{P}
-a.a.
\omega\in\Omega
as a function of time with values in

, and it is the unique solution to the Itô SDE

\begin{cases}dz=-i(-\Delta)^{{\alpha}}zdt+{\sigma}d\zeta_{N},\\

We also have that

Because

\left({\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|z(t)\|\right)^{2}\leq{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|z(t)\|^{2}\leq 2{\mathbb{E}}\,\|z(T)\|^{2}=2\sigma^{2}T.

(3.8)

For any fixed
\omega\in\Omega

\begin{cases}\dfrac{dv}{dt}=-i\left[(-\Delta)^{{\alpha}}v+2\beta P_{N}\left((v+z)e^{\beta|v+z|^{2}}\right)\right]\\
\hskip 56.9055pt-\sigma^{2}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s-{\alpha}}+G(\|v+z\|_{H^{s}})\Big](v+z),\\

(3.9)

where
u_{0}=u_{0}^{\omega}
and

are considered as given, deterministic data for fixed

.
A straightforward application of the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem guarantees existence and uniqueness of a local-in time solution

.
Also, observe that

.

As a first step, let us prove that,

of (

Using (

(v,i(-\Delta)^{\alpha}v)=0

\left(u,iP_{N}(ue^{\beta|u^{2}|})\right)=\left(u,iue^{\beta|u^{2}|}\right)=0
for

, we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(v,\dfrac{dv}{dt}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(v,-2i\beta(v+z)e^{\beta|v+z|^{2}}\right)-\sigma^{2}\Big[\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s-{\alpha}}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}G(\|v+z\|_{H^{s}})\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\sigma^{2}G(\|v+z\|_{H^{s}})(v,z)

\displaystyle=-\left(z,-2i\beta(v+z)e^{\beta|v+z|^{2}}\right)-\sigma^{2}\Big[\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s-{\alpha}}}^{2}+\|v\|^{2}G(\|v+z\|_{H^{s}})\Big]

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\sigma^{2}G(\|v+z\|_{H^{s}})(v,z)

and (

Next, let us show that

Writing

and recalling from (

Subtracting the two equations

\partial_{t}w_{k}=-i\left[(-\Delta)^{{\alpha}}w_{k}+2\beta P_{N}\left(ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}-u_{k}e^{\beta|u_{k}|^{2}}\right)-2\beta P_{N}\left(w_{k}e^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right)\right]\\
+2i\beta P_{N}\left(z_{k}e^{\beta|u_{k}|^{2}}\right)-\sigma^{2}_{k}\Big[(-\Delta)^{s-{\alpha}}u_{k}+G(\|u_{k}\|_{H^{s}})u_{k}\Big],

(3.28)

with initial datum

.
Recall from (

)(
3.12

and

Since on the finite dimensional space

.
We write

We have moreover

ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}-u_{k}e^{\beta|u_{k}|^{2}}=\sum\limits_{p\geq 0}\dfrac{\beta^{p}}{p!}(|u|^{2p}u-|v_{k}+z_{k}|^{2p}(v_{k}+z_{k}))\\

where

By (

) we find in any fixed time-interval

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}\left(\frac{1}{2}\|w_{k}\|^{2}\right)\leq 0+2\beta\left(\|w_{k}\|^{2}\|F(u,v_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}+\|w_{k}\|\|z_{k}\|\|\tilde{F}(v_{k},z_{k})\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\right)\\
+2\beta\|w_{k}\|\|z_{k}\|e^{\beta\|u_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}}+\sigma_{k}^{2}\|w_{k}\|\left(\|u_{k}\|_{H^{\frac{s-\alpha}{2}}}+G(\|u_{k}\|_{H^{s}})\|u_{k}\|\right).

) of

.

.

where

3.2
The large dimension limit
N\to\infty

Proposition 3.7
.

There exist a measure
\mu^{s}\in\mathfrak{p}(L^{2})
such that, up to some subsequence (not relabeled here),

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mu^{s}_{N}=\mu^{s},\qquad\mbox{weakly over }H^{r}\mbox{ for all }r<s.

Moreover, we have

\int_{L^{2}}\mathcal{M}(u)\mu^{s}(du)=\dfrac{A^{0}}{2},

(3.30)

and

\int_{L^{2}}\left[\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}+\left(\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha}}^{2}+\int_{M}u^{2}e^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right)G(\|u\|_{H^{s}})\right]\mu^{s}(du)\\
\leq C\left(A^{\alpha}+A^{\frac{d-1}{2}}(1+A^{0})\right).

(3.31)

for some
C
only depending on the various parameters.

Note that, for finite
N
, the measure
\mu^{s}_{N}\in\mathfrak{p}(L^{2})
is supported on the finite-dimensional space
E_{N}
, and can therefore be viewed unambiguously as a probability measure over
H^{r}
for any
r\geq 0
.
For definiteness and consistency we consider the limit
\mu^{s}
as a probability measure over
L^{2}
as well, but it is part of our statement that
\mu^{s}
can also be considered as a probability measure on
H^{r}
for any
r<s
.

Proof.

From (
3.24
) and (
3.14
) we see that

c\int_{L^{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}\mu^{s}_{N}(du)\leq\int_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}(u)\mu^{s}_{N}(du)\leq C,

and from (
3.23
) combined with (
3.15
)

\int_{L^{2}}\|u\|^{2}\mu^{s}_{N}(du)\leq\int_{L^{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s-\alpha}}\mu^{s}_{N}(du)\leq\int_{L^{2}}\mathcal{M}(u)\mu^{s}_{N}(du)\leq C.

Whence

\int_{L^{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\mu^{s}_{N}(du)\leq C

(3.32)

uniformly in
N
.
By Chebyshev’s inequality we have thus
\mu^{s}_{N}(B_{R}^{c}(H^{s}))\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}
, and since
B_{R}(H^{s})
is compactly embedded in
L^{2}
we see that the sequence
\{\mu^{s}_{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
is tight over
L^{2}
.
Prokhorov’s Theorem guarantees that, up to extraction of a subsequence if needed,
\mu^{s}_{N}\rightharpoonup\mu^{s}
for some weak limit
\mu^{s}\in\mathfrak{p}(L^{2})
.
Actually, for any
r<s
the embedding
B_{R}(H^{s})
into
H^{r}
is compact, hence the very same argument shows that
\mu^{s}_{N}
is weakly compact over
H^{r}
for any
r<s
.
By uniqueness of the limit we see that the weak convergence holds for any such
r
along this particular subsequence.
For the remaining estimates one can proceed exactly as in the proof of Proposition
3.6
, with the minor difference that the various functions
\mathcal{M}(u),\|u\|^{2}\dots
appearing in the integrals are not continuous anymore but only lower semicontinuous (with respect to the

topology, which is enough in order to safely apply Lemma
A.5
).
∎

Now that we have a natural candidate
\mu^{s}=\lim\mu^{s}_{N}
as a “good” probability measure for (
1.1
), it remains to construct global solutions and to show that
\mu^{s}
is indeed invariant for the corresponding flow.
Here we carry out most of the preparatory technical work.

A key issue will be to prove that the

norms grow slowly enough (logarithmically in time) for

.
In order to measure this growth in the correct time scale, let us introduce the monotone increasing function

\tilde{G}(\rho)=\ln(1+G(\rho))\qquad\mbox{for}\qquad\rho\geq 0.

For the reader’s convenience it is worth anticipating that the growth function
\zeta
in Theorem
1.1
will be
\zeta=2\tilde{G}^{-1}
, roughly speaking.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
d/2<r<s
,
N\geq 0
.
For any

there exists a set
\Sigma_{N,r}^{i}\subset E_{N}

for some

.
Moreover for all

\|\phi_{N}^{t}(u_{0})\|_{H^{r}}\leq 2\tilde{G}^{-1}(1+i+\ln(1+|t|))\hskip 28.45274pt\forall\,t\in\mathbb{R}.

One can think here of

that there is a large set on which we have an explicit logarithmic

Proof.

For

, we set

in the

It may help to think of

Let

be the local time of existence from Proposition

\Sigma_{N,r}^{i,j}=\bigcap\limits_{k=0}^{k_{j}}\phi_{N}^{-kT}(B_{N,r}^{i,j})\qquad\mbox{with}\qquad k_{j}=\left\lfloor e^{j}/T\right\rfloor.

(3.35)

On can think of this as the set of initial data allowing to maintain a logarithmic control up to large times

and modulo a fixed delay

.
Using the invariance of
\mu_{N}^{s}
, we get

\mu_{N}^{s}\left(E_{N}\setminus\Sigma_{N,r}^{i,j}\right)=\mu_{N}^{s}\left(\bigcup\limits_{k\leq k_{j}}E_{N}\setminus\phi_{N}^{-kT}(B_{N,r}^{i,j})\right)\\
\leq\sum\limits_{k\leq k_{j}}\mu_{N}^{s}\left(E_{N}\setminus\phi_{N}^{-kT}(B_{N,r}^{i,j})\right)=\sum\limits_{k\leq k_{j}}\mu_{N}^{s}\left(\phi_{N}^{-kT}\left(E_{N}\setminus B_{N,r}^{i,j}\right)\right)\\

We claim now that

G(\|u\|_{H^{r}})\leq C(\mathcal{E}(u)+1)

for some

.
Indeed, we first observe from
\int_{M}|u|^{2}e^{\beta|u|^{2}}\geq\|u\|^{2}

Note that, in dimension

\alpha<r<s
.
By standard interpolation there exists

=G(\|u\|_{H^{s}})\left(\dfrac{\|u\|_{H^{r}}^{1/\theta}}{\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}}\right)^{2}\leq G(\|u\|_{H^{s}})\|u\|_{H^{\alpha}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{c}(\mathcal{E}(u)+C).

On the other hand, since

Back to our main argument: From (

{\mathbb{E}}_{\mu^{N}_{s}}\exp\left(\tilde{G}(\|u\|_{H^{r}})\right)\leq C

, and Chebyshev’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\mu_{N}^{s}(E_{N}\setminus\Sigma_{N,r}^{i,j})

\displaystyle\leq\left(k_{j}+1\right)\dfrac{1}{e^{2(i+j)}}\int\limits_{\tilde{G}(\|u\|_{H^{r}})\geq 2(i+j)}\exp(\tilde{G}(\|u\|_{H^{r}}))\mu^{s}_{N}(du)

).

) we have

, hence

4
Low regularity

In this section we consider the case

As already mentioned, the main obstacle in this case is the fact that the existence time for solutions to the projected equation (

As a consequence we will lose any quantitative control on the growth of

In order to circumvent this difficulty we still implement a fluctuation-dissipation approach, but with a modified dissipation operator

Roughly speaking, the new dissipation will allow to gain an extra coercivity and control the exponential nonlinearity (which was previously achieved through a control on the

4.1
Compactness estimates and stationary measure

In this section, we take

4.2
Construction of global solutions

The goal is now to pass to the limit
N\to\infty
, and this is where the Skorokhod-type argument
[
AC90
,
BTT18
,
DPD02
]
comes into play.
To this end, let
u_{0}
be an
E_{N}
-valued random variable distributed according to
\mu^{s}_{N}\in\mathfrak{p}(L^{2})
just constructed, and let
u_{N}(t)=\phi^{t}_{N}(u_{0})
.
As a first step we work in a finite time interval
t\in[-T,T]
for some fixed large
T>0
.
One can canonically consider the processes
(u_{N}(t))_{t\in[-T,T]}
as a
C([-T,T],H^{s-\alpha})
-valued random variable.
Let
\nu^{s}_{T,N}
be its law.
By invariance of
\mu^{s}_{N}
we have

\mu^{s}_{N}=\left.\nu^{s}_{T,N}\right|_{t=t_{0}}\qquad\mbox{for all}\qquad t_{0}\in[-T,T].

(4.9)

Consider the following functional spaces

X_{T}^{s}=L^{2}([-T,T];H^{s})\cap H^{1}\Big([-T,T];\big(\dot{H}^{s-\alpha}+L^{2})\big)\Big)

and

Y_{T}^{s}=C([-T,T];H^{s-\alpha}),

and note that
X_{T}^{s}
is compactly embedded into
Y_{T}^{s}
.

Proposition 4.2
.

We have

\int_{Y^{s}_{T}}\left(\|u\|_{X_{T}^{s}}^{2}+\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\right)\nu_{T,N}^{s}(du)\leq CT

(4.10)

for some constant
C
independent of
N
.

Proof.

From
-i\partial_{t}u_{N}=(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u_{N}+2\beta P_{N}u_{N}e^{\beta|u_{N}|^{2}}
we have obviously

\|\partial_{t}u_{N}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s-\alpha}+L^{2}}\leq\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u_{N}\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s-\alpha}}+\left\|2\beta P_{N}u_{N}e^{\beta|u_{N}|^{2}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

hence by (
4.9
) and the invariance of
\mu^{s}_{N}

\int_{Y^{s}_{T}}\|u\|_{X_{T}}^{2}\nu_{T,N}^{s}(du)=\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{L^{2}}\left(\|u_{N}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|\partial_{t}u_{N}(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s-\alpha}+L^{2}}\right)\nu^{s}_{T,N}\big|_{t}(du)dt\\
\leq\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{L^{2}}\left(\|u_{N}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u_{N}(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s-\alpha}}+4\beta^{2}\left\|P_{N}u_{N}(t)e^{\beta|u_{N}(t)|^{2}}\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)\nu^{s}_{T,N}\big|_{t}(du)dt\\
=\int_{-T}^{T}\int_{L^{2}}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha}u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s-\alpha}}+4\beta^{2}\left\|P_{N}ue^{\beta|u(t)|^{2}}\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)\mu^{s}_{N}(du)dt\\
\leq(1+4\beta^{2})2T\int_{L^{2}}\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|u\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\left\|P_{N}ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right)\mu^{s}_{N}(du).

By (
4.8
) this latter integral is bounded uniformly in
N
and the first part of our estimate follows.

In order to control now the
L^{1}_{t}L^{1}_{x}
norm of the nonlinearity, we first argue just as before to write

\int_{Y^{s}_{T}}\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\nu_{T,N}^{s}(du)=2T\int_{L^{2}}\int_{M}\left|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right|dx\,\mu_{N}^{s}(du).

Let now
\Phi(z)
be the monotone increasing, convex function from Lemma
A.4
with
b=\beta
and
c=1
, defined implicitly as
\Phi(ue^{\beta|u|^{2}})=u^{2}e^{\beta|u|^{2}}
.
By Jensen’s inequality and (

) we have

\Phi\left(\int_{L^{2}}\int_{M}\left|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right|\frac{dx}{Vol(M)}\,\mu_{N}^{s}(du)\right)\leq\int_{L^{2}}\int_{M}\Phi\left(ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right)\frac{dx}{Vol(M)}\,\mu_{N}^{s}(du)\\
=\int_{L^{2}}\int_{M}|u|^{2}e^{\beta|u|^{2}}\frac{dx}{Vol(M)}\,\mu_{N}^{s}(du)\leq C

uniformly in
N
, hence

\int_{Y^{s}_{T}}\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{T}L^{1}_{x}}\nu_{T,N}^{s}(du)\leq 2T\,Vol(M)\Phi^{-1}(C)

(4.11)

and the proof is complete
∎

Chebyshev’s inequality in (
4.10
) gives
\nu^{s}_{T,N}(B_{R}(X^{s}_{T})^{c})\leq\frac{C}{R^{2}}
uniformly in
N
, hence by the Prokhorov theorem there exists a weak limit

\nu^{s}_{T}=\lim\limits_{N\to\infty}\nu^{s}_{T,N}\qquad\mbox{in}\qquad\mathfrak{p}(Y_{T}^{s})

(up to a subsequence

, not relabeled here.)
By lower semicontinuity in (
4.10
) we have moreover

\int_{Y^{s}_{T}}\left(\|u\|_{X_{T}^{s}}^{2}+\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}_{t}L^{1}_{x}}\right)\nu_{T}^{s}(du)\leq CT

(4.12)

By Skorokhod’s representation theorem, there exists a
Y_{T}
-valued stochastic process
u
distributed according to
\nu^{s}_{T}\in\mathfrak{p}(Y_{T}^{s})

Next, we want to show that

is a distributional solution of (

\omega

Let us now deal with the first term

Write for simplicity

The remaining terms

Lemma A.1
.

Let
s>d/2
.
There exists a constant
C=C(s,d)

\left\|e^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq e^{C\beta\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}}e^{C\beta\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}}

and

\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}-ve^{\beta|v|^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq Ce^{C\beta\left(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|v\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)}\|u-v\|_{H^{s}}.

Proof.

First, let us recall that, for all
u,v\in H^{s}
, we have

\|uv\|_{H^{s}}\leq C(\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v\|_{H^{s}})\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|v\|_{H^{s}}

(A.1)

for some
C=C(s)
.
Using the previous inequality repeatedly, we get

\left\|e^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}=\left\|\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\beta^{k}|u|^{2k}}{k!}\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\beta^{k}}{k!}\left\||u|^{2k}\right\|_{H^{s}}\\
\leq\sum_{k\geq 0}\dfrac{\beta^{k}}{k!}\left(C\|u\|_{H^{s}}\right)^{2k}=e^{C\beta\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}},

and as a consequence we obtain as well

\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}}\left\|e^{\beta|u|^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq C\|u\|_{H^{s}}e^{C\beta\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}}.

In order to estimate
ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}-ve^{\beta|v|^{2}}
we first exploit the algebraic identity

|u|^{2k}u-|v|^{2k}v=(u-v)P_{k}(u,\bar{u},v,\bar{v})
, where
P_{k}
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
2k
defined recursively by
P_{k}=|u|^{2k}+|v|^{2k}+uvP_{k-1}
and
P_{0}=1
.
It is not difficult to check that
\|P_{k}\|_{H^{s}}\leq C^{k}(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|v\|^{2}_{H^{s}})^{k}
, hence

\left\|ue^{\beta|u|^{2}}-ve^{\beta|v|^{2}}\right\|_{H^{s}}=\left\|\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}}{k!}(|u|^{2k}u-|v|^{2k}v)\right\|_{H^{s}}\\
=\left\|\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}}{k!}(u-v)P_{k}\right\|_{H^{s}}\leq\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}\frac{\beta^{k}}{k!}C\|(u-v)\|_{H^{s}}\|P_{k}\|_{H^{s}}\\
\leq C\left\|u-v\right\|_{H^{s}}\sum\limits_{k\geq 0}\frac{(\beta C)^{k}}{k!}(\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\|v\|^{2}_{H^{s}})^{k}=C\left\|u-v\right\|_{H^{s}}e^{C\beta\left(\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\left\|v\right\|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)}

and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

For
\gamma\in(0,1]
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
, we have

\left\langle fe^{|f|^{2}},(-\Delta)^{\gamma}f\right\rangle\geq\left\|e^{|f|^{2}/2}\right\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{\gamma}}.

Proof.

Let
f=a+ib
.
Applying twice the previous lemma with
\Phi(r)=r^{2}/2
, separately to the real-valued functions

\mathcal{R}\left[(a+ib)e^{(a^{2}+b^{2})/2}(-\Delta)^{\gamma}(a-ib)\right]=e^{(a^{2}+b^{2})/2}(a(-\Delta)^{\gamma}a+b(-\Delta)^{\gamma}b)\\

Lemma A.4
.

, and set

f^{\prime}(u)\geq 1
.
As a consequence
f^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

C^{\infty}

Changing variables

The only thing left to check is that

Lemma A.8

(modified Arzelà-Ascoli)
.

Let
(X,\mathsf{d})
be a Polish space,
K\subset X
a compact set, and
f_{n}:K\subset X\to X
an equicontinuous sequence of functions converging pointwise

f_{n}(x)\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}f(x)\qquad\mbox{for all }x\in K.

Then

\sup\limits_{x\in K}\mathsf{d}(f_{n}(x),f(x))\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0.

Proof.

Fix
{\varepsilon}>0
.
By equicontinuity there is
\delta>0
such that
\mathsf{d}(f_{n}(x),f_{n}(y))+\mathsf{d}(f(x),f(y))\leq{\varepsilon}/2
as soon as
\mathsf{d}(x,y)\leq\delta
.
Take a finite cover
K\subset\cup_{i}B(x_{i},\delta)
of radius
\delta
.
For
x\in K
we have by triangle inequality

\mathsf{d}(f_{n}(x),f(x))\leq\mathsf{d}(f_{n}(x),f_{n}(x_{i}))+\mathsf{d}(f(x),f(x_{i}))+\mathsf{d}(f_{n}(x_{i}),f(x_{i}))\\
\leq{\varepsilon}/2+\mathsf{d}(f_{n}(x_{i}),f(x_{i}))

for some suitable
i
such that
x\in B(x_{i},\delta)
, and the claim follows.
∎

Explaining the 𝑹 𝑲 ( ∗ ) R_{K^{(*)}} anomalies and the CDF 𝑴 𝑾 M_{W} in Flavorful Top Seesaw Models with Gauged 𝑼 ​ ( 𝟏 ) 𝑳 ⁡ ( − 𝑹 ) U(1)_{L(-R)}
Yi Chung

Abstract
A new heavy Z ′ Z^{\prime} vector boson provides a possible explanation for the neutral current B anomalies and points to the scale where we expect the solution to the hierarchy problem to appear. In this paper, we explore a modified Top Seesaw model based on the global symmetry breaking of U ​ ( 3 ) L U(3)_{L} to U ​ ( 2 ) L U(2)_{L} . The symmetry is partially gauged such that the breaking introduces not only a composite Higgs doublet but also a TeV-scale Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson. The U ​ ( 3 ) L / U ​ ( 2 ) L U(3)_{L}/U(2)_{L} Top Seesaw model predicts a light Higgs boson as well as a large top Yukawa coupling. Additional U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} symmetry is gauged such that a flavorful TeV-scale Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson is introduced to address the B anomalies. The existence of a heavy vector-like top quark and Z − Z ′ Z-Z^{\prime} mixing breaks the custodial symmetry, which predicts a heavier W W mass as observed by the CDF collaboration. Two possible candidates for the U ​ ( 1 ) ′ U(1)^{\prime} symmetry corresponding to the Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson, U ​ ( 1 ) L U(1)_{L} and U ​ ( 1 ) L − R U(1)_{L-R} , are discussed in detail. The parameter space for the models to address both anomalies is presented. The direct searches of the Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson from dilepton channels are also studied. Based on the assumptions, a TeV-scale Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson is still viable and can be probed in the near future.

2.1
Scalar potential and vacuum structure

For a realistic potential to realize the desired mechanism, we follow the construction in

\displaystyle V_{\Phi}=M_{\Phi}^{2}\,\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\text{Tr}[(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}]+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}(\text{Tr}[\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi])^{2}~.

(4)

Assuming the coupling of the strong interaction is greater than the critical coupling, then the coefficient of quadratic term

\displaystyle V_{\not{R}}=\delta M_{TT}^{2}\Phi_{T}^{\dagger}\Phi_{T}+\delta M_{tt}^{2}\Phi_{t}^{\dagger}\Phi_{t}+(M_{Tt}^{2}\Phi_{T}^{\dagger}\Phi_{t}+\text{ h.c.}).

(5)

\displaystyle\Delta\mathcal{L}=-m_{Tt}\bar{T^{\prime}_{L}}\,t^{\prime}_{R}+\text{ h.c.}~\implies~V_{\text{tadpole}}=-C_{Tt}S_{t}+\text{ h.c.},~\text{where}~C_{Tt}\propto{m_{Tt}}\,\Lambda^{2}~.

M_{Z^{\prime}}=\sqrt{\left(Q_{S_{T}}\,g_{Z^{\prime}}u_{T}\right)^{2}+\left(Q_{S_{t}}\,g_{Z^{\prime}}u_{t}\right)^{2}+\left(Q_{H_{T}}\,g_{Z^{\prime}}v\right)^{2}}\approx\left(2\,g_{Z^{\prime}}f\right)^{2},

(23)

where
Q_{i}
is the charge of the scalar
i
. For future convenience, we define the
Z^{\prime}
scale

f^{\prime}\equiv\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}}{g_{Z^{\prime}}}\approx 2f~,

(24)

which is relevant in the study of the
Z^{\prime}
phenomenology in the following sections. The relation is determined by the charge of the dominant vacuum, i.e.
\langle S_{T}\rangle
, which in this case carries charge
|Q_{S_{T}}|=2
.

Next, we consider the
U(1)^{\prime}
based on
L-R
, where the
L
is the same as the
U(1)_{L}
in eq. (
18
) and the
R
comes from the generator

U(1)_{R}:~\begin{pmatrix}-2&0\\
0&~2~\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(25)

Under the
U(1)_{L-R}
, the charge of each fermion becomes

\displaystyle T^{\prime}_{L}:-2,~\quad q^{\prime}_{L}=(t^{\prime}_{L},b^{\prime}_{L}):1,~\quad T^{\prime}_{R}:2,~\quad t^{\prime}_{R}:-2~.

(26)

The reason for this setup is based on the issue in the scalar potential discuss in section
2.1
. The realistic potential requires the mass term
m_{Tt}\,\bar{T^{\prime}_{L}}\,t^{\prime}_{R}
as shown in (
6
). However, the mass
m_{Tt}
should originate from a new scale which might break the
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry directly, unless the combination
\bar{T^{\prime}_{L}}\,t^{\prime}_{R}
is neutral under the
U(1)^{\prime}
, which requires
t^{\prime}_{R}
carrying charge
Q_{t^{\prime}_{R}}=-2
. On the other hand, the combination
\bar{T^{\prime}_{R}}\,T^{\prime}_{L}
must carry a nonzero charge to be broken, which requires
Q_{T^{\prime}_{R}}\neq-2
. Therefore, the simplest choice is having an additional
U(1)_{R}
as shown in eq. (
25
) from
\tau_{3}
inside
U(2)_{R}
, under which
t^{\prime}_{R}
and
T^{\prime}_{R}
carry opposite charges so the both conditions are satisfied.

Under the new assumption, the interaction is now extended to the right-handed fermions as given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}^{\text{quark}}=g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\,(

\displaystyle\,-2\bar{T^{\prime}_{L}}\gamma^{\mu}T^{\prime}_{L}+\,\bar{T^{\prime}_{L}}\gamma^{\mu}t^{\prime}_{L}+\bar{b^{\prime}}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}b^{\prime}_{L}\,+2\bar{T^{\prime}}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}T^{\prime}_{R}-\,2\bar{t^{\prime}}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}t^{\prime}_{R}\,)

(27)

\displaystyle=g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\,(

\displaystyle\,(-2c_{L}^{2}+s_{L}^{2})\,\bar{T^{\prime}_{L}}\gamma^{\mu}T^{\prime}_{L}+(-2s_{L}^{2}+c_{L}^{2})\,\bar{t}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}t_{L}+\bar{b}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}b_{L}

\displaystyle+(-3c_{L}s_{L})\,\bar{T}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}t_{L}+(-3c_{L}s_{L})\,\bar{t}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}T_{L}

\displaystyle+(2c_{R}^{2}-2s_{R}^{2})\,\bar{T^{\prime}}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}T^{\prime}_{R}+(2s_{R}^{2}-2c_{R}^{2})\,\bar{t}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}t_{R}

\displaystyle+(-4c_{R}s_{R})\,\bar{T}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}t_{R}+(-4c_{R}s_{R})\,\bar{t}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}T_{R})

(28)

The lepton sector is similar with simple transformation as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}^{\text{lepton}}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}^{\text{quark}}\,(\,T,\,t,\,b\,\to N,\,\nu_{\tau},\,\tau\,)

(29)

The charges of the scalars under
U(1)_{L-R}
are given by

\displaystyle S_{T}:-4,~\quad S_{t}:0,~\quad H_{T}:-1,~\quad H_{t}:3~.

(30)

We can again derive the mass of
Z^{\prime}
boson as

M_{Z^{\prime}}=\sqrt{\left(Q_{S_{T}}\,g_{Z^{\prime}}u_{T}\right)^{2}+\left(Q_{S_{t}}\,g_{Z^{\prime}}u_{t}\right)^{2}+\left(Q_{H_{T}}\,g_{Z^{\prime}}v\right)^{2}}\approx\left(4\,g_{Z^{\prime}}f\right)^{2},

(31)

and the
Z^{\prime}
scale of
U(1)_{L-R}
as

f^{\prime}\equiv\frac{M_{Z^{\prime}}}{g_{Z^{\prime}}}\approx 4f~,

(32)

where we get a different factor because now
|Q_{S_{T}}|=4
.

Notice that either case is not perfectly preserved in the complete scalar potential.
U(1)_{L}
is also broken by
-C_{Tt}S_{t}
tadpole term and
U(1)_{L-R}
is broken by
M_{Tt}^{2}\Phi_{T}^{\dagger}\Phi_{t}
term. The source of these violating terms will directly break the corresponding

from another scale, which will introduce additional

mass terms and affect our analysis. In this work, we assume the origin of these
U(1)^{\prime}
violating terms comes from the scale lower than the breaking scale
f
such that the
M_{Z^{\prime}}
we derive is still valid. Under the assumption, we then have the
Z^{\prime}
boson and composite Higgs come from the same scale.

4
M_{W}
and electroweak precision tests

An obvious shortage of
SU(3)/SU(2)
CHMs is the lack of custodial symmetry protection, which will modify the measurement in the electroweak sector. On the other hand, it could provide an explanation for the deviation in the electroweak sector, too. Especially, the latest
W
boson measurement by the CDF collaboration
CDF:2022hxs
shows a
7\sigma
discrepancy. These electroweak precision tests (EWPTs) are usually expressed in terms of
S
,
T
, and
U
parameters
Peskin:1990zt
;
Peskin:1991sw
. The
U
is usually fixed to zero because it is suppressed by an additional factor
M_{\rm new}^{2}/m_{Z}^{2}
, which is typically small for heavy new physics. The
W
mass deviation is also interpreted in terms of these oblique parameters in many following analyses
Lu:2022bgw
;
Strumia:2022qkt
;
deBlas:2022hdk
;
Bagnaschi:2022whn
;
Asadi:2022xiy
, which point out that a larger
T
parameter is preferred. In this section, we will discuss the contribution to the
S
and
T
parameters from the new physics in our model.

4.1
The
S
parameter

The leading contribution to the
S
parameter comes from the mixing between the SM gauge bosons and the composite vector resonances
\rho
, which act as the gauge boson partners. The resulting value is estimated to be
Agashe:2003zs
;
Agashe:2005dk
;
Giudice:2007fh

\displaystyle\Delta S\sim c_{S}~4\pi\frac{v^{2}}{M_{\rho}^{2}}\sim c_{S}~0.03\left(\frac{5\text{ TeV}}{M_{\rho}}\right)^{2},

(33)

where
c_{S}
is an
\mathcal{O}(1)
factor and
M_{\rho}
is the mass of the vector resonances
\rho
.

There is also a contribution from the nonlinear Higgs dynamics due to the deviations of the Higgs couplings, which result in an incomplete cancellation of the electroweak loops
Barbieri:2007bh
;
Grojean:2013qca
. This contribution is given by

\displaystyle\Delta S\sim\frac{1}{12\pi}\text{ ln}\left(\frac{M_{\rho}^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}\right)~\frac{v^{2}}{f^{2}}\sim 0.05\text{ ln}\left(\frac{M_{\rho}}{m_{h}}\right)~\frac{v^{2}}{f^{2}}~,

(34)

which is proportional to
v^{2}/f^{2}
and depends logarithmically on
M_{\rho}
.

4.2
The
T
parameter

The
T
parameter parametrizes the amount of custodial
SU(2)
breaking. In our model, The leading contribution to the
T
parameter comes from the loop involving the vector-like top quark
T
, which is given by
Chivukula:1998wd

\Delta T=\frac{3s_{L}^{2}}{16\pi^{2}\alpha v^{2}}\left[s_{L}^{2}M_{T}^{2}+4c_{L}^{2}\frac{M_{T}^{2}m_{t}^{2}}{M_{T}^{2}-m_{t}^{2}}\text{ ln}\left(\frac{M_{T}}{m_{t}}\right)-(2-2s_{L}^{2})m_{t}^{2}\right]~.

(35)

Substituting the relation we have in our model, we can rewrite it as

\Delta T\approx\frac{3}{16\pi^{2}\alpha f^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}y_{T}^{2}v^{2}+4m_{t}^{2}\text{ ln}\left(\frac{y_{T}f}{\sqrt{2}m_{t}}\right)-2m_{t}^{2}\right]~.

(36)

Another important contribution comes from the
Z-Z^{\prime}
mixing, which will shift the
Z
boson mass and lead to a positive contribution to the
T
parameter as
Allanach:2022bik

\displaystyle\Delta T\sim\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{4Q_{H}^{2}g_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}{g^{2}+g^{\prime 2}}\frac{m_{Z}^{2}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)\sim\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{Q_{H_{T}}^{2}}{Q_{S_{T}}^{2}}\frac{v^{2}}{f^{2}}\right)~.

(37)

This contribution depends not only on the scale
f
but also on the ratio
|Q_{H_{T}}/Q_{S_{T}}|
, which is
1/2
for
U(1)_{L}
and
1/4
for
U(1)_{L-R}
. Therefore, a larger contribution is expected for
U(1)_{L}
compared to
U(1)_{L-R}
, which will end up as a stronger bound on the scale
f
.

There is also a contribution from the modifications of the Higgs couplings to gauge bosons due to the nonlinear effects of the pNGB Higgs as
Barbieri:2007bh
;
Grojean:2013qca

\displaystyle\Delta T\sim-\frac{3}{16\pi\text{ cos }\theta_{W}^{2}}\text{ ln}\left(\frac{M_{\rho}^{2}}{m_{h}^{2}}\right)~\frac{v^{2}}{f^{2}}\sim-0.16\text{ ln}\left(\frac{M_{\rho}}{m_{h}}\right)~\frac{v^{2}}{f^{2}}~,

(38)

which again depends on
v^{2}/f^{2}
and is logarithmically sensitive to
M_{\rho}
. The contribution can partially cancel the other two positive contributions. Summing up all the contributions, we can then compare them with the experimental results Especially, we would like to find the required amount to explain the CDF
M_{W}
measurement.

5
Flavor Phenomenology

The discussions in the previous section give us the bounds on the symmetry breaking scale
f
from the electroweak precision tests. Next, we discuss the constraints from flavor physics, mainly due to the new
Z^{\prime}
boson. Because of its third-generation-philic nature, the relevant processes are largely from
B
meson and
\tau
lepton. Especially, it might provide an explanation for the observed neutral current B anomalies.

To simplify the analysis, we follow
Chung:2021ekz
;
Chung:2021xhd
and assume all the nontrivial flavor violating processes originated from the left-handed sector. Under this assumption, there is no difference between the two
U(1)^{\prime}
because they share the same structure in the relevant left-handed part, which is given by the Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\,(\,\bar{b}_{L}^{f}\gamma^{\mu}b_{L}^{f}+\bar{\tau}_{L}^{f}\gamma^{\mu}\tau_{L}^{f}\,)~.

(41)

The
b_{L}^{f}
and
\tau_{L}^{f}
are still in the flavor basis and both relevant fields carry
Q=1
.

To study the flavor phenomenology of the
Z^{\prime}
boson, we need to extend the third-generation interaction to cover all the generations. Due to the observation of the nontrivial CKM matrix and PMNS matrix, we know the third-generation-philic interaction in the flavor basis can in general be transferred to the light generations by the rotation between flavor and mass basis.

In the flavor basis, the interaction terms are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\,(\,\bar{D}_{L}^{f}\gamma^{\mu}Q_{D_{L}}^{f}D_{L}^{f}+\bar{E}_{L}^{f}\gamma^{\mu}Q_{E_{L}}^{f}E_{L}^{f}\,)~,

(42)

where
D
represents the three down-type quarks,
E
represents the three charged leptons, and
f
denotes the flavor basis. The charge matrices are given by

Q_{D_{L}}^{f}=Q_{E_{L}}^{f}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&1\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(43)

Next, we need the mixing matrices
U_{F_{L}}
to transform them from the flavor basis
F^{f}_{L}
to the mass basis
F^{m}_{L}
, which satisfy
F^{f}_{L}=U_{F_{L}}F^{m}_{L}
. The charge matrices in the mass eigenstate are then given by

Q_{F_{L}}^{m}=U_{F_{L}}^{\dagger}Q_{F_{L}}^{f}U_{F_{L}}.

(44)

Following the analysis in
Chung:2021ekz
;
Chung:2021xhd
, we only focus on the rotation matrices between the second and third generation of the down-type quarks,
U_{D_{L}}
, and charged leptons,
U_{E_{L}}
. The rotation matrices are given by

U_{D_{L}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\text{cos}~\theta_{d}&\text{sin}~\theta_{d}\\
0&-\text{sin}~\theta_{d}&\text{cos}~\theta_{d}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad U_{E_{L}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\
0&\text{cos}~\theta_{e}&\text{sin}~\theta_{e}\\
0&-\text{sin}~\theta_{e}&\text{cos}~\theta_{e}\\
\end{pmatrix}~.

(45)

The resulting interaction terms are given in the mass basis as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}=g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\,(\,\bar{D}_{L}^{m}\gamma^{\mu}Q_{D_{L}}^{m}D_{L}^{m}+\bar{E}_{L}^{m}\gamma^{\mu}Q_{E_{L}}^{m}E_{L}^{m}\,)~,

(46)

where
D_{L}^{m}=(d_{L},s_{L},b_{L})
,
E_{L}^{m}=(e_{L},\mu_{L},\tau_{L})
, and the charge matrices are

Q_{D_{L}}^{m}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\text{sin}^{2}\,\theta_{d}&-\,\text{sin}\,\theta_{d}\,\text{cos}\,\theta_{d}\\
0&-\,\text{sin}\,\theta_{d}\,\text{cos}\,\theta_{d}&\text{cos}^{2}\,\theta_{d}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad Q_{E_{L}}^{m}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&\text{sin}^{2}\,\theta_{e}&-\,\text{sin}\,\theta_{e}\,\text{cos}\,\theta_{e}\\
0&-\,\text{sin}\,\theta_{e}\,\text{cos}\,\theta_{e}&\text{cos}^{2}\,\theta_{e}\\
\end{pmatrix}~.

(47)

Then, we can write down all the couplings with the
Z^{\prime}
boson for the left-handed fermions. To study the B anomalies, two of them,
Z^{\prime}sb
and
Z^{\prime}{\mu\mu}
are especially important so we define the corresponding couplings as
g_{sb}
and
g_{\mu\mu}
. Moreover, we can extract the charge and mixing part of the couplings and rewrite the couplings as

\displaystyle g_{sb}=-\,g_{Z^{\prime}}\epsilon_{sb}

\displaystyle\quad\text{where}\quad\epsilon_{sb}=\,\text{sin}\,\theta_{d}\,\text{cos}\,\theta_{d}~,

(48)

\displaystyle g_{\mu\mu}=g_{Z^{\prime}}\epsilon_{\mu\mu}

\displaystyle\quad\text{where}\quad\epsilon_{\mu\mu}=\text{sin}^{2}\,\theta_{e}~.

(49)

We will show later that the constraints from flavor physics can be put on the three key parameters: the
Z^{\prime}
scale
f^{\prime}
, the mixings
\epsilon_{sb}
, and
\epsilon_{\mu\mu}
.

With these specified mixing matrices, we can then explore the consequence in flavor physics, especially the parameter space allowed to explain the neutral current B anomalies. Also, the constraints from other low energy experiments, including neutral meson mixings and lepton flavor violating decays, are discussed in this section.

5.1

To explain the observed neutral current B anomalies, an additional contribution on the process

Besides, we also consider the value

5.2
Neutral Meson Mixing

Next, we discuss the constraints from other measurements. The strongest constraints on the

solution come from neutral meson mixings. In our specified mixing matrices, the mixings between the first generation down-type quark and other generations are not presented, so there are no additional contributions on
K^{0}-\bar{K}^{0}
and

\Delta\mathcal{L}_{B_{s}}=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{g_{sb}^{2}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}(\bar{s}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}b_{L})(\bar{s}_{L}\gamma_{\mu}b_{L})~.

(54)

Following the calculation in

C_{B_{s}}\equiv\frac{\Delta M_{s}}{\Delta M_{s}^{SM}}\approx 1+\left(5576\text{ TeV}^{2}\right)\,\left(\frac{g_{sb}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}}\right)^{2},

(55)

The measurement of mixing parameter
HFLAV:2019otj
compared with SM prediction by sum rule calculations
King:2019lal
gives a strong upper bound at 95% C.L. as

\left|\frac{g_{sb}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{194~\text{TeV}}\implies\epsilon_{sb}\leq\frac{f^{\prime}}{194~\text{TeV}}~.

(56)

Next, we can combine it with the requirement from eq. (

to the lower bound on

(57)

where the value in the denominator corresponds to

respectively. The transformation implies that, for the

, the required

Moreover, because there is a maximum value for

5.3

In the lepton sector, there are also strong constraints from flavor changing neutral currents. The off-diagonal term in the lepton charge matrix

will introduce lepton flavor violating decays. Since we only specified the rotation between the second and the third generation, the most important effects show up in

\displaystyle BR(\tau\to 3\mu)

\displaystyle=3.28\times 10^{-4}\,\left(\frac{1\text{ TeV}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{4}\epsilon_{\mu\mu}^{3}(1-\epsilon_{\mu\mu})~.

(59)

The current bound on the branching ratio is
<2.1\times 10^{-8}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1\text{ TeV}}{f^{\prime}}\right)^{4}\epsilon_{\mu\mu}^{3}(1-\epsilon_{\mu\mu})<6.4\times 10^{-5}~.

(60)

7
Collider searches

Several new degrees of freedom are introduced in our model, including the heavy Higgs doublet and new vector-like fermions. However, in this section, we only focus on the new
Z^{\prime}
boson, which should be the lightest among them. Also, the properties of the
Z^{\prime}
boson are partially fixed by the requirements to explain the two anomalies, which makes it predictable and a good target for direct searches.

The analyses of EWPTs and flavor physics from previous sections already put constraints on the mixings, the scale
f
and
f^{\prime}=M_{Z^{\prime}}/g_{Z^{\prime}}
. Direct searches, on the other hand, can provide the lower bound on the mass
M_{Z^{\prime}}
directly. A general
Z^{\prime}
collider search has been discussed in
Allanach:2019mfl
;
Chung:2021xhd
, so in this section, we will focus on the parameter spaces available in this model, which are shown in the figure
2
.

The direct searches depend on the
Z^{\prime}
properties. In this work, we consider two types of
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry with different interaction terms, which lead to very different properties, especially the branching ratio. Also, the allowed parameter spaces are different. Therefore, we will discuss them separately.

Another undetermined issue is the neutrino sector. The properties of the vector-like neutrino
N
and the right-handed neutrino
\nu_{R}
will both affect the
Z^{\prime}
phenomenology. However, to determine them, a complete theory of neutrino mass is required, which is beyond the scope of this work. In the following analysis, we assume a Dirac-type neutrino such that
\nu_{R}
is light and thus the decay
Z^{\prime}\to\bar{\nu_{R}}\nu_{R}
is allowed. On the other hand, for the vector-like neutrino
N
, we assume
M_{N}>M_{Z^{\prime}}
such that
Z^{\prime}\to NN
and
Z^{\prime}\to N\nu
are both forbidden. Therefore, both new vector-like fermions
T
and
N
are heavier than the
Z^{\prime}
boson so we can focus on the
Z^{\prime}
interaction with the SM fermions in the following discussion.

7.1
The
Z^{\prime}
boson of
U(1)_{L}
gauge symmetry

Starting from the
U(1)_{L}
, the interaction between
Z^{\prime}
and the SM fermions is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}^{\text{SM}}=\,g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\,((-2s_{L}^{2}+c_{L}^{2})\,\bar{t}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}t_{L}+\bar{b}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}b_{L}+(-2s_{L}^{2}+c_{L}^{2})\,\bar{\nu_{\tau}}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}{\nu_{\tau}}_{L}+\bar{\tau}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}\tau_{L})~.

(63)

Since the angle
\theta_{L}
is small, it is approximately given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}^{\text{SM}}\approx g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\,(\,\bar{t}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}t_{L}+\bar{b}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}b_{L}+\,\bar{\nu_{\tau}}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}{\nu_{\tau}}_{L}+\bar{\tau}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}\tau_{L})~,

(64)

where the
Z^{\prime}
boson couples universally with all the thrid-generation SM left-handed fermions in the flavor basis. Using the specified matrices discussed in section
5
, we can further transform the interaction to the mass basis.

The couplings to the first and second generation quarks are negligible due to the small mixing angles between flavor and mass basis. Therefore, the dominant production comes from the process
b_{L}\bar{b}_{L}
fusion with the production cross section given by

{\sigma}(b_{L}\bar{b}_{L}\to Z^{\prime})\equiv{g_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\cdot\sigma_{b_{L}b_{L}}(M_{Z^{\prime}})~,

(65)

where the coupling dependence is taken out. The
\sigma_{b_{L}b_{L}}
(as a function of
M_{Z^{\prime}}
) is determined by the left-handed bottom-quark parton distribution functions
Martin:2009iq
;
Alwall:2014hca
.

The partial width of the
Z^{\prime}
boson decaying into Weyl fermion pairs
\bar{f_{i}}f_{j}
is given by

\Gamma_{ij}=\frac{C}{24\pi}g_{ij}^{2}M_{Z^{\prime}}~,

(66)

where
C
counts the color degree of freedom and
g_{ij}
is the coupling of
Z^{\prime}\bar{f_{i}}f_{j}
vertex. In the limit that all
m_{f}
are negligible, we get the total relative width as

\frac{\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{3\pi}g_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\sim 11\,\%\cdot g_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}~.

(67)

The narrow width approximation is valid up to
g_{Z^{\prime}}\sim 1.0
.

The dominant decay channels are the diquarks channel of the third generation with

Br(t\bar{t})\sim Br(b\bar{b})\sim 37.5\%~.

(68)

However, the main constraint is expected to be from dilepton channels which are rather clean. Based on the mixing matrices we specified, the branching ratios are given as

\displaystyle Br(\tau\tau)\sim 12.5~(1-\epsilon_{\mu\mu})^{2}~\%~,

(69)

\displaystyle Br(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})\sim 12.5\%\quad\implies\quad

\displaystyle Br(\mu\tau)\sim 25.0~\epsilon_{\mu\mu}(1-\epsilon_{\mu\mu})~\%~,

(70)

\displaystyle Br(\mu\mu)\sim 12.5~\epsilon_{\mu\mu}^{2}~\%~.

(71)

From the production cross section and the branching ratios we got, we can calculate the cross section for the dilepton final state. For example, for the dimuon final state, the contour line for the cross section are straight lines in figure
1
with a fixed ratio
\epsilon_{\mu\mu}/f^{\prime}
. The minimal value of
\epsilon_{\mu\mu}/f^{\prime}
that corresponds to the strong blue (purple) line in figure
1
gives the smallest cross section for
b_{L}\bar{b}_{L}\to Z^{\prime}\to\mu\mu
process.

Z^{\prime}
in this model is accidentally the same as the one in
Chung:2021ekz

Chung:2021xhd
due to the same
\sigma\times Br
, which results in a similar bound. However, it is different for other dilepton channels.
The lower bound on
M_{Z^{\prime}}
can then be derived by comparing with the experimental results from the ATLAS

7.2
The
Z^{\prime}
boson of
U(1)_{L-R}

The interaction between the
Z^{\prime}
of
U(1)_{L-R}
symmetry and the SM fermions, assuming both angles
\theta_{L}
and
\theta_{R}
are negligible, is approximately given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{int}}^{\text{SM}}\approx g_{Z^{\prime}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\,(\,\bar{t}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}t_{L}+\bar{b}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}b_{L}-2\,\bar{t}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}t_{R}+\,\bar{\nu_{\tau}}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}{\nu_{\tau}}_{L}+\bar{\tau}_{L}\gamma^{\mu}\tau_{L}-\,2\,\bar{\nu_{\tau}}_{R}\gamma^{\mu}{\nu_{\tau}}_{R}\,)~.

There are additional couplings with right-handed top quark and
\tau
neutrino compared to the

The dominant production process is the same as the
Z^{\prime}
boson from

symmetry, which comes from the process

. The cross section is also given by

{\sigma}(b_{L}\bar{b}_{L}\to Z^{\prime})\equiv{g_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\cdot\sigma_{b_{L}b_{L}}(M_{Z^{\prime}})~.

(74)

The total relative width in the limit that all
m_{f}
are negligible is

\frac{\Gamma_{Z^{\prime}}}{M_{Z^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{\pi}g_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}\sim 32\,\%\cdot g_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}~,

The dominant decay channel now becomes

Br(t\bar{t})\sim 62.5\%~\quad Br(\nu\bar{\nu})\sim 20.8\%~\quad Br(b\bar{b})\sim 12.5\%~.

(76)

\displaystyle Br(\tau\tau)\sim 4.16~(1-\epsilon_{\mu\mu})^{2}~\%~,

(77)

\displaystyle Br(\ell^{+}\ell^{-})\sim 4.16\%\quad\implies\quad

\displaystyle Br(\mu\tau)\sim 8.32~\epsilon_{\mu\mu}(1-\epsilon_{\mu\mu})~\%~,

(78)

\displaystyle Br(\mu\mu)\sim 4.16~\epsilon_{\mu\mu}^{2}~\%~.

(79)

8
Conclusions

The naturalness principle so far is still the strongest motivation for particle physicists to look for TeV-scale new physics. Although lack of evidence from any direct searches, two anomalies appear from the precision measurement and both point to new degrees of freedom at the TeV scale. One is the long-standing neutral current B anomalies, which can trace back to almost ten years ago. The other is the CDF
M_{W}
measurement, which is presented early this year with large significance. A desired UV model at the TeV scale should be able to address all three problems together.

To explain all of them, we consider a
U(3)_{L}/U(2)_{L}
Top Seesaw model with an additional
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge symmetry. The Higgs doublet arises as pNGBs of the global symmetry breaking
U(3)_{L}\to U(2)_{L}
, which is naturally lighter than the breaking scale
f\sim
few TeVs. Moreover, the top seesaw mechanism can naturally generate the top Yukawa coupling with
y_{t}\sim 1
. The fifth pNGB is eaten by the
U(1)^{\prime}
gauge boson, which results in a heavy
Z^{\prime}
boson. Two possible candidates for
U(1)^{\prime}
symmetry,
U(1)_{L}
and
U(1)_{L-R}
, are discussed in this work. This third-generation-philic
Z^{\prime}
boson can provide an explanation for the neutral current B anomalies. The lack of custodial symmetry, which used to be the fault of the model, generically predicts a heavier
M_{W}
as observed by the CDF collaboration.

The viable parameter spaces preferred by the two anomalies are discussed in detail. The breaking scale
f
between 3.0 (2.4) TeV and 5.8 (4.6) TeV in the
U(1)_{L(-R)}
model can provide an explanation for the CDF
M_{W}
measurement. Combined with the requirement from NCBAs and the two main constraints from FCNCs,
B_{s}-\bar{B}_{s}
mixing and
\tau\to 3\mu
, which put the bounds on the
Z^{\prime}
scale
f^{\prime}
, the mixing
\epsilon_{sb}
and
\epsilon_{\mu\mu}
, we find that only the
Z^{\prime}
boson from the
U(1)_{L}
gauge symmetry can address both anomalies at the same time. On the other hand, the
U(1)_{L-R}
model can only address the conditions either with a smaller
|C_{LL}|
or an even heavier
M_{W}
.

Direct searches of the new
Z^{\prime}
boson are also studied. The bound on the
M_{Z}^{\prime}
for the
U(1)_{L}
model is
1.25
TeV. A sub-TeV
Z^{\prime}
boson is also possible if
|C_{LL}|
is
2\sigma
lower. For the
U(1)_{L-R}
case, considering the smaller
|C_{LL}|
, there is no bound from the direct searches due to its small branching ratio in the dimuon channel. The most important message from the bounds is: we are just about to explore the most relevant parameter space in this type of
Z^{\prime}
model. Different from the flavor universal new physics, which is almost ruled out up to several TeV by the first two runs of LHC, the third-generation-philic particles are just about to be tested in Run 3 and the upcoming HL-LHC. Together with updates on NCBAs and
M_{W}
, the mystery of the TeV-scale UV-complete model might be unveiled.

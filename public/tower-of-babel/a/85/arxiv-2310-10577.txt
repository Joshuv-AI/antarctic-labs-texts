Introduction

Let
s\in(0,1)
and
N\geq 1
. The present paper is devoted to nondegeneracy properties of solutions to the problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u+\lambda u=u^{p}\quad\text{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\qquad u>0\quad\textrm{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\qquad u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and to the Dirichlet problem

(1.2)

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}u+\lambda u=u^{p}\quad\text{ in ${B}$},\qquad u>0\quad\textrm{in ${B}$},\qquad u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)

where
B:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,|x|<1\}
is the unit ball. Here
(-\Delta)^{s}
denotes the fractional Laplacian of order
s
, which, under appropriate smoothness and integrability assumptions on the function
u
, is pointwisely given by

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x)=c_{N,s}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}(x)}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dy

with
c_{N,s}=2^{2s}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}s\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{\Gamma(1-s)}
. Moreover,
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the usual fractional Sobolev space endowed with the scalar product

(v_{1},v_{2})\mapsto\langle v_{1},v_{2}\rangle_{s}=[v_{1},v_{2}]_{s}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v_{1}v_{2}dx

and induced norm
v\mapsto\|v\|_{s}=\sqrt{[v]^{2}_{s}+\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}}
, where

(1.3)

[v_{1},v_{2}]_{s}=c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(v_{1}(x)-v_{1}(y))(v_{2}(x)-v_{2}(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy

and
[v]_{s}^{2}:=[v,v]_{s}
. In addition, we let
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)
denote the closed subspace of functions
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
u\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B
. Furthermore, we assume that
\lambda>0
in case of (
1.1
) and
\lambda>-\lambda_{1}(B)
in case of (
1.2
), where
\lambda_{1}(B)
denotes the first Dirichlet eigenvalue of
(-\Delta)^{s}
for
s\in(0,1)
. Finally, we assume that
p\in(1,2^{*}_{s}-1)
with
2^{*}_{s}
being the critical fractional Sobolev exponent given by

2^{*}_{s}=\frac{2N}{N-2s}\,\,\textrm{ if }2s<N\qquad\textrm{ and }\qquad 2^{*}_{s}=\infty\,\,\textrm{ if }2s\geq 1=N.

A priori, it is natural to consider (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) in weak sense. A positive function
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is called a weak solution of (
1.1
) if

[u,v]_{s}+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p}v\,dx\qquad\text{for all $v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}

Similarly, a positive function
u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)
is called
a weak solution of (
1.2
) if

[u,v]_{s}+\lambda\int_{B}uv\,dx=\int_{B}u^{p}v\,dx\qquad\text{for all $v\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)$.}

It is well known (see e.g.
[
23
, Proposition 3.1]
) that positive (weak) solutions to (
1.1
) belong to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, are radially symmetric about a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and are strictly decreasing in
|x-x_{0}|
. We also recall that, by
[
32
]
, every (weak) solution
u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)
of (
1.2
) is contained in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and therefore
[
33
]
and
[
32
]
imply that
u\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{\infty}{(B)}
. Moreover, by
[
26
]
,
u
is a radial function which is strictly decreasing in its radial variable (see also
[
6
]
for a different argument but with
\lambda=0
).

In the local case
s=1
, the uniqueness of solutions to problems (
1.2
) and (
1.1
) (up to translations) is a classical result and has been established by Kwong in his seminal paper
[
30
]
, see also
[

In contrast, for semilinear fractional equations, the uniqueness and nondegeneracy of specific classes of solutions is a very challenging problem, and few results are available up to now. One of the main reasons for this is the lack of ODE techniques which are highly useful in the case
s=1
.
It was first proved by Amick and Toland in
[
3
]
, based on complex analytic methods, that (

) has a unique solution in the special case

,

and

, see also
[
2

for an alternative proof. Much later, Frank, Lenzmann and Silvestre

proved uniqueness and nondegeneracy of Morse index one solutions to (

) up to translations in the general case, extending an earlier result of Frank and Lenzmann for the case

to all dimensions, and extending also results of

to all

1.2

The first purpose of the present paper is to show that all positive solutions of (
1.2
) are nondegenerate in nonradial directions, and that the same is true for (
1.1
) up to the directional derivatives of
u
. In order to formulate these statements rigorously, we consider the subspaces

H^{s}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\>:\>\text{$w$ radially symmetric}\}

and

H^{s}_{nr}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\bigl[H^{s}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\bigr]^{\perp}:=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\>:\>\text{$\langle u,w\rangle_{s}=0$ for all $w\in H^{s}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})$.}\}.

Moreover, we let
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{r}(B)
resp.
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{nr}(B)
denote the subspaces of

H^{s}_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
H^{s}_{nr}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, respectively of functions
w
with
w\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus B
. We then have the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s\in(0,1)
,
N\geq 1
and
1<p<2^{*}_{s}-1
.

(i)

Let

satisfy (

Then there exists
\overline{\Lambda}>p

(1.4)

[w]_{s}^{2}+\lambda\int_{B}w^{2}dx\geq\overline{\Lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p-1}w^{2}dx\qquad\textrm{ for all $w\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{nr}(B)$.}

(ii)

Let
u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfy (
1.1
). Then there exists
\overline{\Lambda}>p
such that

(1.5)

[w]_{s}^{2}+\lambda\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w^{2}dx\geq\overline{\Lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p-1}w^{2}dx\qquad\textrm{ for all $w\in{\mathcal{M}}_{u}$ ,}

where

{\mathcal{M}}_{u}:=\Bigl\{w\in H^{s}_{nr}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\,:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p-1}(\partial_{x_{i}}u)w\,dx=0\quad\textrm{for all $i=1,\dots,N$}\Bigr\}.

Let us explain in detail why Theorem
1.1
should be regarded as a result on nonradial nondegeneracy. For this, let
u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)
be a fixed radial solution of (
1.2
). The linearization of (
1.1
) at
u
is then given by
the equation

(1.6)

(-\Delta)^{s}w+\lambda w-pu^{p-1}w=0\qquad\textrm{ in $B$,}\qquad\qquad w\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B),

which is closely related to the weighted eigenvalue problem

(1.7)

(-\Delta)^{s}w+\lambda w=\Lambda u^{p-1}w\qquad\textrm{ in $B$,}\qquad\qquad w\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)

with eigenvalue parameter
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. In particular, if
\Lambda=p
is not an eigenvalue of (
1.7
), then (
1.6
) has no nontrivial solution and
u
is nondegenerate. We note that the compact embedding
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(B;u^{p-1}dx)
implies that the set of eigenvalues of (
1.7
) is given as a sequence
0<\Lambda_{1}<\Lambda_{2}\leq\dots
, with each eigenvalue being counted with multiplicity. Since
(-\Delta)^{s}
commutes with rotations, eigenfunctions corresponding to the eigenvalues
\Lambda_{i}
can be chosen to be contained in one of the spaces
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{r}(B)
and
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{nr}(B)
. Moreover, we note that
\Lambda_{1}
is a simple eigenvalue with an eigenspace spanned by a positive radial eigenfunction. All other eigenfunctions of (
1.7
) are orthogonal to the first eigenfunction with respect to the scalar product in
L^{2}(B;u^{p-1}dx)
, and therefore they have to change sign. Since
u
solves (
1.7
) with
\Lambda=1
and is positive, we conclude a posteriori that
\Lambda_{1}=1
is the first eigenvalue of (
1.7
).

It is now natural to call

radially

1.6
) does not admit nontrivial solutions in
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{r}(B)
,
{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{nr}(B)
, respectively. In particular,
u

nonradially nondegenerate
if
\Lambda=p
is not an eigenvalue of (
1.7
) admitting a nonradial eigenfunction

Our proof of Theorem

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u+u=u^{p}\quad\text{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\qquad u>0\quad\textrm{ in $\mathbb{R}^{N}$},\qquad u\in H^{1/2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

Moreover, the optimal constants

3.
A Picone type identity

The following new Picone-type result will be one of our main tools in the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open set of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\alpha>0
. Let
V\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
and
v\in C^{2s+\alpha}(\Omega)\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};(1+|x|)^{-N-2s})
satisfy

(3.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}v=Vv\qquad\textrm{ in $\Omega$}.

Let
e
be a unit vector of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and let
\sigma_{e}
denote the reflection with respect to
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,x\cdot e=0\}
.
Suppose that
v>0
on
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,x\cdot e>0\}
and
v=-v\circ\sigma_{e}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then for any
w\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C_{c}(\Omega)
satisfying
w=-w\circ\sigma_{e}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\frac{w}{v}\in C_{c}(\Omega)
, we have

(3.2)

[w]_{s}^{2}-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}Vw^{2}dx=\int_{\{x\cdot e>0\}}\int_{\{y\cdot e>0\}}H_{w,v}^{e}(x,y)dxdy,

where
H_{w,v}^{e}\geq 0
on
\{x\cdot e>0\}\cap\{y\cdot e>0\}
is given by

(3.3)

H_{w,v}^{e}(x,y):=c_{N,s}{v(x)v(y)}\left[w(x)/v(x)-w(y)/v(y)\right]^{2}\times\left(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{|\sigma_{e}(x)-y|^{N+2s}}\right).

Proof.

We assume without loss of generality that
e=e_{1}
. We first note that for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
x_{1}\not=0
and
y_{1}\not=0
, we have

\displaystyle(w(x)-w(y))^{2}-

\displaystyle w^{2}(x)\frac{(v(x)-v(y))}{v(x)}+w^{2}(y)\frac{(v(x)-v(y))}{v(y)}

(3.4)

\displaystyle=v(x)v(y)\left(w(x)/v(x)-w(y)/v(y)\right)^{2}.

Moreover, by assumption,
w/v\in C_{c}(\Omega)
, and thus

c_{N,s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{w^{2}(x)}{v(x)}\frac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy=\int_{\Omega}\frac{w^{2}(x)}{v(x)}\,(-\Delta)^{s}v(x)dx=\int_{\Omega}V(x)w^{2}(x)dx<\infty

by (
3.1
). From this and a change of variable, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(w(x)-w(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dydx-\frac{2}{c_{N,s}}\int_{\Omega}V(x)w^{2}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{v(x)v(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\left(w(x)/v(x)-w(y)/v(y)\right)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle=2\int_{\{x_{1}>0\}\cap\{y_{1}>0\}}\frac{v(x)v(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\left(w(x)/v(x)-w(y)/v(y)\right)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle-2\int_{\{x_{1}>0\}\cap\{y_{1}>0\}}\frac{v(x)v(y)}{((x_{1}+y_{1})^{2}+|\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y}|^{2})^{(N+2s)/2}}\left(w(x)/v(x)-w(y)/v(y)\right)^{2}dxdy.

Here, we used that
x_{1}\mapsto v(x_{1},\cdot)
is odd and
x_{1}\mapsto\frac{w}{v}(x_{1},\cdot)
is even. It follows, from this, that

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(w(x)-w(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dydx-\frac{2}{c_{N,s}}\int_{\Omega}Vw^{2}dx=\frac{2}{c_{N,s}}\int_{\{x_{1}>0\}\cap\{y_{1}>0\}}H^{e}_{w,v}(x,y)dxdy,

where, for all
(x,y)\in\{x_{1}>0\}\times\{y_{1}>0\}
,

\displaystyle H^{e}_{w,v}(x,y):=c_{N,s}{v(x)v(y)}

\displaystyle\left[w(x)/v(x)-w(y)/v(y)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\times\left(|x-y|^{-N-2s}-((x_{1}+y_{1})^{2}+|\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y}|^{2})^{-(N+2s)/2}\right).

By the triangular inequality and the fact that
v(x)v(y)>0
for all
x,y\in\{x_{1}>0\}\cap\{y_{1}>0\}
, we deduce that
H^{e}_{w,v}\geq 0
on
\{x_{1}>0\}\cap\{y_{1}>0\}
. Hence (
3.2
) follows.
∎

4.1.
Nonradial nondegeneracy in the ball case

This section is devoted to the proof of Part (i) of Theorem
1.1
. By the remarks after
Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to prove the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
w\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{nr}(B)
be an eigenfunction of (
1.7
) corresponding to an eigenvalue
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then
\Lambda>p
.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that
\Lambda\leq p
. For a unit vector
\nu
, we define the hyperplane
T_{\nu}:=\{x\cdot\nu=0\}
and we denote by
\sigma_{\nu}
the reflection with respect to
T_{\nu}
. Since
w
is nonradial, there exists a unit vector
\nu
with the property that

{\widetilde{w}}:=\frac{w-w\circ\sigma_{\nu}}{2}\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}(B)\setminus\{0\}.

Moreover, since
u
is radial,
{\widetilde{w}}
also solves
(-\Delta)^{s}{\widetilde{w}}+{\widetilde{w}}=\Lambda u^{p-1}{\widetilde{w}}
in
B
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\nu=e_{1}
, so
{\widetilde{w}}
is odd with respect to reflection of the
x_{1}
-variable. By Lemma
2.1
(i), we have
v:=-\partial_{x_{1}}u\in C^{\infty}(B)\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
v
is a pointwise solution
of the equation
(-\Delta)^{s}v+\lambda v=pu^{p-1}v
in
B
which is odd with respect to the reflection at
\{x_{1}>0\}
and satisfies

v\geq 0\quad\text{in $\{x_{1}>0\}$}\qquad\text{and}\qquad v\not\equiv 0\quad\text{in $\{x_{1}>0\}\cap B$.}

Indeed, these inequalities follow since
v(x)=-{x_{1}}\frac{\partial_{r}u(x)}{|x|}
and
\partial_{r}u\leq 0
,
\partial_{r}u\not\equiv 0
in
B\setminus\{0\}
.

Now, by Lemma
8.3
from the appendix, we have
v>0
in
\{x_{1}>0\}\cap B
and
\partial_{x_{1}}v>0
on
\{x_{1}=0\}\cap B
. Consequently,

(4.1)

x\mapsto\frac{v(x)}{x_{1}}\qquad\text{extends to a positive $C^{\infty}$-function on $B$.}

Next we note that, by fractional elliptic regularity,
{\widetilde{w}}\in C^{\infty}(B)\cap L^{\infty}(B)
.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the functions

(4.2)

\zeta_{k}(x)=1-\chi(k(1-|x|)),

where
\chi
is given by (
4.6
),
and we put
w_{k}:=\zeta_{k}{\widetilde{w}}\in C^{1}_{c}(B)
. Therefore
w_{k}/v\in C_{c}(B)
by (
4.1
). We can thus
apply Lemma
3.1
to obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle[w_{k}]^{2}+\lambda\int_{B}w_{k}^{2}dx-p\int_{B}u^{p-1}w_{k}^{2}dx=\int_{\{x_{1}>0\}\cap\{y_{1}>0\}}H_{w_{k},v}^{e_{1}}(x,y)dxdy.

By

16

, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{(w_{k}(x)-w_{k}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dydx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{({\widetilde{w}}(x)-{\widetilde{w}}(y))^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dydx\quad\textrm{as $k\to\infty$. }

In addition, by the dominated convergence theorem,

\int_{B}u^{p-1}w_{k}^{2}dx\to\int_{B}u^{p-1}{\widetilde{w}}^{2}dx\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{B}w_{k}^{2}dx\to\int_{B}{\widetilde{w}}^{2}dx\qquad\text{as $k\to\infty$.}

We can thus apply Fatou’s lemma in (

) to deduce that

\displaystyle 0=[{\widetilde{w}}]_{s}^{2}+\lambda\int_{B}{\widetilde{w}}^{2}dx-\Lambda\int_{B}u^{p-1}{\widetilde{w}}^{2}dx\geq

\displaystyle[{\widetilde{w}}]_{s}^{2}+\lambda\int_{B}{\widetilde{w}}^{2}dx-p\int_{B}u^{p-1}{\widetilde{w}}^{2}dx

(4.4)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\{x_{1}>0\}\cap\{y_{1}>0\}}H^{e_{1}}_{{\widetilde{w}},v}(x,y)dxdy,

where, recalling (
3.3
),

\displaystyle H^{e_{1}}_{\widetilde{w},v}(x,y):=c_{N,s}{v(x)v(y)}\left[\widetilde{w}(x)/v(x)-\widetilde{w}(y)/v(y)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\times\left(|x-y|^{-N-2s}-((x_{1}+y_{1})^{2}+|\widetilde{x}-\widetilde{y}|^{2})^{-(N+2s)/2}\right)\geq 0\quad\textrm{for all $x,y\in\{x_{1}>0\}\cap\{y_{1}>0\}$ .}

From this, (
4.4
) and the assumption
\Lambda\leq p
, we deduce
\Lambda=p
and that equality holds in (
4.4
). This implies that
H^{e_{1}}_{w,v}\equiv 0
on
{\{x_{1}>0\}\cap\{y_{1}>0\}}
and thus
w\not\equiv 0
is proportional to
v=\partial_{x_{1}}u
on
\{x_{1}>0\}
. This is impossible because
\partial_{x_{1}}u
is unbounded as
|x|\to 1
on
\{x_{1}>0\}
by Lemma
2.1
(i). We thus conclude that
\Lambda>p
.
∎

4.2.
Nonradial nondegeneracy in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{N}

This section is devoted to the proof of Part (ii) of Theorem
1.1
. Hence we assume from now on that
u=u(|x|)
is a fixed radial solution of (
1.1
). By the remarks after Theorem
1.1
, it suffices to prove the following.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
w\in H^{s}_{nr}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be an eigenfunction of (
1.9
) corresponding to an eigenvalue
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. Then
one of the following alternatives hold.

(i)

\Lambda>p
.

(ii)

\Lambda=p
, and
w\in\spann\{\partial_{x_{1}}u,\dots,\partial_{x_{N}}u\}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Lambda\leq p
. By elliptic regularity we have
w\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We fix a unit vector
\nu
and consider, as before, the reflection
\sigma_{\nu}
at the hyperplane
T_{\nu}:=\{x\cdot\nu=0\}
. Moreover, we define

{w^{\nu}}:=\frac{w-w\circ\sigma_{\nu}}{2}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Clearly since
u
is radial, then
{w^{\nu}}
solves
(-\Delta)^{s}{w^{\nu}}+{w^{\nu}}=\Lambda_{2}u^{p-1}{w^{\nu}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
By Lemma
2.1
(ii), we have
v:=-\nabla u\cdot\nu\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
v
is a pointwise solution
of the equation
(-\Delta)^{s}v+v=pu^{p-1}v
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
which is odd with respect to the reflection at
\nu
and
v\gneqq 0
on
\{x\cdot\nu>0\}
. Applying Lemma
8.3
from the appendix, we have
v>0
in
\{x\cdot\nu>0\}
and
\nabla v\cdot\nu>0
on
\{x\cdot\nu=0\}
. Therefore,

(4.5)

x\mapsto\frac{v(x)}{x\cdot\nu}\qquad\text{extends to a positive $C^{\infty}$-function on $\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

We let

(4.6)

\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(-2,2),\quad\textrm{ with $0\leq\chi\leq 1$ on $\mathbb{R}$ and $\chi\equiv 1$ on $(-1,1)$}.

For
R\in\mathbb{N}
, we define
w_{R}^{\nu}(x)=w^{\nu}(x)\chi(|x|/R)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and we note that
w_{R}/v\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
thanks to (
4.5
).
Applying Lemma
3.1
, we get

(4.7)

\displaystyle[w_{R}^{\nu}]_{s}^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(w_{R}^{\nu})^{2}dx-p\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p-1}(w_{R}^{\nu})^{2}dx=\int_{\{x\cdot\nu>0\}\cap\{y\cdot\nu>0\}}H_{w_{R}^{\nu},v}^{\nu}(x,y)dxdy.

It is not difficult to see that
\|w_{R}^{\nu}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to\|w^{\nu}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
as

. On the other hand by the dominated convergence theorem, as

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(w_{R}^{\nu})^{2}dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(w^{\nu})^{2}dx\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p-1}(w_{R}^{\nu})^{2}dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p-1}(w^{\nu})^{2}dx.

We thus apply Fatou’s lemma, recalling (

(4.8)

(\Lambda-p)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{p-1}(w^{\nu})^{2}dx\geq\int_{\{x\cdot\nu>0\}\cap\{y\cdot\nu>0\}}H_{w^{\nu},v}^{\nu}(x,y)dxdy\geq 0.

Since
\Lambda\leq p

\Lambda=p
and
H_{w^{\nu},v}^{\nu}\equiv 0
on
\{x\cdot\nu>0\}\cap\{y\cdot\nu>0\}
. In view of (

), this implies that there exists

such that

\frac{w(x)-w\circ\sigma_{\nu}(x)}{2}=\kappa_{\nu}\nabla u(x)\cdot\nu=\kappa_{\nu}\partial_{r}u(x)\frac{\nu\cdot x}{|x|}\qquad\textrm{ for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

We recall that this holds for an arbitrary unit vector
\nu\in S^{N-1}
. Moreover, a posteriori we find that

\kappa_{\nu}=\|\nabla u\cdot\nu\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w\nabla u\cdot\nu\,dx\qquad\text{for $\nu\in S^{N-1}$,}

so the function
\nu\mapsto h(\nu):=\kappa_{\nu}
is smooth on
S^{N-1}
. Moreover, the function
\partial_{r}u
is radial and nonpositive, so we may write
\partial_{r}u(x)=-U(|x|)
with a function
U:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
. We may then apply Corollary

in the appendix to obtain the representation

(4.9)

w(x)=w_{*}(x)+h_{*}\partial_{r}u(x)\frac{\nu_{*}\cdot x}{|x|}=w_{*}(x)+h_{*}\nabla u(x)\cdot\nu_{*}\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}

with a fixed vector

, a constant

and a radial function

. Since

it follows that

. Hence

∎

Remark 4.3
.

We note that the above argument to prove Theorem

Let

h
takes its maximum at
\nu_{max}=e_{N}:=(0,\dots,0,1)
.

We first consider the case
N=2
. We then write
x_{\theta}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\in S^{1}
for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and regard all functions as
2\pi
-periodic functions of the angle
\theta
. For a fixed angle
\theta
, we consider
\nu_{\theta}:=(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)
. Then the reflection at the hyperplane
T=T_{\nu_{\theta}}:=\{x\in S^{1}\>:\>x\cdot\nu_{\theta}=0\}
corresponds in the angle coordinate to the map

\vartheta\mapsto 2\theta-\vartheta

Setting

\tilde{w}(\theta)=w(x_{\theta})=w(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\tilde{h}(\theta)=h(\nu_{\theta})=h(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta),

we may then reformulate assumption (
8.1
) as

\displaystyle\frac{w(\theta+s)-w(\theta-s)}{2}=\tilde{h}(\theta)\>x_{\theta}\cdot\nu_{\theta}=\tilde{h}(\theta)\>(\cos(\theta+s),\sin(\theta+s))\cdot(-\sin\theta,\cos\theta)

\displaystyle=\tilde{h}(\theta)\Bigl(\sin(\theta+s)\cos\theta-\cos(\theta+s)\sin\theta\Bigr)=\tilde{h}(\theta)\sin s\quad\text{for $\theta,s\in\mathbb{R}.$}

Differentiating in
\theta
at
s=0
gives

(8.3)

\partial_{\theta}w(\theta)=\tilde{h}(\theta)\qquad\text{for all $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Consequently, we may reformulate the assumption again as

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\theta-s}^{\theta+s}\tilde{h}(\tau)d\tau=\tilde{h}(\theta)\sin s\quad\text{for $\theta,s\in\mathbb{R}.$}

Differentiating this identity three times in
s
gives

\frac{\tilde{h}^{\prime\prime}(\theta+s)+\tilde{h}^{\prime\prime}(\theta-s)}{2}=-\tilde{h}(\theta)\cos s\quad\text{for $\theta,s\in\mathbb{R}.$}

Evaluating at
s=0
gives
\tilde{h}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=-\tilde{h}(\theta)
, from which we deduce that

(8.4)

\tilde{h}(\theta)=h_{max}\cos\theta\qquad\text{for $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$.}

Here we used the fact that
\tilde{h}
takes its maximum at zero, since
h
takes its maximum at
\nu_{max}=(0,1)
by assumption. Combining (
8.3
) and (
8.4
) gives

\displaystyle w(x_{\theta})

\displaystyle=\tilde{w}(\theta)=\tilde{w}(\frac{\pi}{2})+h_{max}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\theta}\cos\vartheta d\vartheta=\tilde{w}(\frac{\pi}{2})+h_{max}(\sin\theta-1)

\displaystyle=w(\nu_{max})+h_{max}(x_{\theta}\cdot\nu_{max}-1)\qquad\text{for $\theta\in\mathbb{R}$,}

which gives (
8.2
) in the case
N=2
.

In the general case
N\geq 2
, we may repeat the above argument in the
2
-dimensional subspace
\spann\{e_{*},e_{N}\}
, where
e_{*}\in S^{N-2}\times\{0\}
is chosen arbitrarily. This then yields (
8.2
) for arbitrary
z\in S^{N-1}
.
∎

Corollary 8.2
.

Let
w\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
h\in C^{2}(S^{N-1})
and
U:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be functions with the property that

\frac{w(x)-w(\sigma_{\nu}(x))}{2}=h(\nu)U(|x|)\,\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu\qquad\text{for every $\nu\in S^{N-1},x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}$.}

Let, moreover,
\nu_{max}\in S^{N-1}
be a point with
h_{max}:=h(\nu_{max})=\max\limits_{S^{N-1}}h
. Then we have

w(x)=[w(|x|\nu_{max})-h_{max}\,U(|x|)]+h_{max}\,U(|x|)\>\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nu_{max}\qquad\text{for $x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}$.}

In particular,
w
is a sum of a radial function and an odd function with respect to the reflection
\sigma_{\nu_{max}}
.

Proof.

It suffices to apply Lemma
8.1
, for fixed
r>0
, to the functions

S^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad z\mapsto w(rz)

in place of
w
and

S^{N-1}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\nu\mapsto U(r)h(\nu)

in place of
h
.
∎

8.2.
A Hopf type lemma

In this section, we provide a variant of the fractional Hopf type lemma in
[
36
, Prop. 2.2]
under somewhat weaker assumptions.

Lemma 8.3
.

Let
H_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\>:\>x_{1}>0\}
,
T:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\>:\>x_{1}=0\}
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded set of class
C^{2}
which is symmetric with respect to reflection of the
x_{1}
-coordinate, and let
\Omega_{+}:=\Omega\cap H_{+}
. Moreover, let
c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\alpha>0
and
v\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};(1+|x|)^{-N-2s})\cap C^{2s+\alpha}(\Omega)
satisfy

(8.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} (-\Delta)^{s}v+c(x)v\geq 0\qquad\text{in $\Omega_{+}$,}\\
v\geq 0\qquad\text{in $H_{+},$}\\
v\not\equiv 0\qquad\text{in $\Omega_{+}$,}\\
v(-x_{1},x^{\prime})=-v(x)\qquad\text{for $x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}\end{aligned}\right.

Then we have

v>0\quad\text{in $\Omega_{+}$}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{v(t,x^{\prime})}{t}>0\qquad\text{for every $(0,x^{\prime})\in T\cap\Omega$.}

Proof.

In the case where, in addition,
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the conclusion is contained in
[
20
, Cor. 3.3]
and
[
36
, Prop. 2.2]
. In fact,
[
36
, Prop. 2.2]
also assumes the equality
(-\Delta)^{s}v+c(x)=0
in
\Omega^{+}
, but the proof given there only requires that
(-\Delta)^{s}v+c(x)\geq 0
in
\Omega^{+}
. To get rid of the additional assumption
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we use a cut-off argument. So we let
v\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N};(1+|x|)^{-N-2s})\cap C^{2s+\alpha}(\Omega)
satisfy (
8.5
), and we let
\tilde{x}\in\Omega
with
\tilde{x}_{1}\geq 0
. We choose a radial cut-off function
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
with
0\leq\psi\leq 1
in such a way that there exists a (sufficiently large) neighborhood
U\subset\Omega
of
\tilde{x}
with
\psi\equiv 1
in
U
and
v\not\equiv 0
in
U_{+}:=U\cap H_{+}
. We may assume also that
U
is symmetric with respect to the
x_{1}
-coordinate. For
x\in U_{+}
we then have

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}(\psi v)(x)=(-\Delta)^{s}v(x)+(-\Delta)^{s}((\psi-1)v)(x)

\displaystyle\geq-c(x)v(x)+c_{N,s}f(x)

\displaystyle=-c(x)(\psi v)(x)+c_{N,s}f(x),

where

f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus U}\frac{(1-\psi)(y)v(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus U)\cap H_{+}}(1-\psi)(y)v(y)\Bigl(\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}-\frac{1}{|x-\bar{y}|^{N+2s}}\Bigr)\,dy\geq 0

and we write
\bar{y}=(-y_{1},y^{\prime})
for
y=(y_{1},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Here we have used the oddness of the function
y\mapsto(1-\psi)(y)v(y)
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{s}(\psi v)+c(x)(\psi v)\geq 0\qquad\text{in $U_{+}$,}

\displaystyle\psi v\geq 0\qquad\text{in $H_{+},$}

\displaystyle\psi v\not\equiv 0\qquad\text{in $U_{+}$,}

\displaystyle(\psi v)(-x_{1},x^{\prime})=-(\psi v)(x)\qquad\text{for $x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$.}

Since moreover
\psi v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
[
20
, Cor. 3.3]
gives
v(\tilde{x})=\psi v(\tilde{x})>0
if
\tilde{x}\in\Omega_{+}
, and
[
36
, Prop. 2.2]
gives

\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{v(t,x^{\prime})}{t}=\liminf_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{(\psi v)(t,x^{\prime})}{t}>0\quad\text{if $\tilde{x}=(0,x^{\prime})\in\Omega\cap T$.}

The claim thus follows.
∎

8.3.
A topological lemma

As before, we let
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}:=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\>:\>t>0\}
. The following topological lemma was used in the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Lemma 8.4
.

Let
x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}<x_{4}
be real numbers. Suppose that
\gamma,\eta:[0,1]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
are continuous curves such that
\gamma(0)=(x_{1},0)
,
\gamma(1)=(x_{3},0)
,
\eta(0)=(x_{2},0)
,
\eta(1)=(x_{4},0)
. Then the curves
\gamma
and
\eta
intersect in
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}}
, i.e. there exists
t,\tilde{t}\in(0,1)
with
\gamma(t)=\eta(\tilde{t})
.

This lemma is a variant of
[
24
, Lemma D.1]
. In contrast to
[
24
, Lemma D.1]
, we do not assume that the curves
\gamma
and
\eta
are simple (i.e., injective), and we do not require that
\gamma(t),\eta(t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
for
t\in(0,1)
. Clearly, under the more general assumptions of Lemma
8.4
, the intersection point may also lie on
\partial\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. A full proof of Lemma
8.4
, based on the intersection number of two curves, can already be found in
[
25
, p. 554–556]
, and it is very likely that there are earlier references for this lemma which we are not aware of.

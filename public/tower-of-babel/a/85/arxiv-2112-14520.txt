Existence and Multiplicity of solutions for a Dirichlet problem in fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
Pablo Ochoa, Analía Silva, Maria José Suarez Marziani

Abstract
. In this paper, we first prove the existence of solutions to Dirichlet problems involving the fractional g g -Laplacian operator and lower order terms by appealing to sub- and supersolution methods. Moreover, we also state the existence of extremal solutions. Afterwards, and under additional assumptions on the lower order structure, we establish by variational techniques the existence of multiple solutions: one positive, one negative and one with non-constant sign.

1.
Introduction

Let us consider the following non local and non standard growth problem

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccll}(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u=f(x,u)&&\text{in}~~\Omega,\\
\,\,\,\,\,u=0&&\text{in}~~\Omega^{c},\end{array}\right.

where
s\in(0,1)
,
\Omega
is a smooth and bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
is the fractional
g
-Laplacian defined for sufficiently smooth functions
u
as

(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u(x)=2p.v\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{dy}{|x-y|^{n+s}},

where
p.v.
stands for ’in principal value’ and
G^{\prime}=g
is an
N
-function. We recall the definition of
N
-function and its properties in Section 2. For a complete guide of this setting we mention the now classical book
[
17
]
.

The fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and their relation with the g-Laplacian operator were discussed in
[
14
]
. These spaces are the appropriate functional framework for
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
. For the reader convenience, we include some basic definitions and properties in Section 2.

Throughout the paper, we consider that
f
has a subcritical growth in the sense of the Orlicz-Sobolev embedding
[
2
]
. The main feature on the non linear term
f
is that no oddness condition is imposed.

As far as we know, the existence and multiplicity of solutions for equations involving the fractional
g
-Laplacian operator have been approached recently. Indeed, in
[
4
]
, they provide existence of a non-negative solution for fractional
g
-Laplacian problems with a source
f(x,u)
having a power-growth in
u
. Moreover, in
[
5
]
the existence of a nodal solution, that is, a changing-sing solution, was given. Regarding multiple solutions for
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
, we quote the work
[
7
]
, for the existence of two non-trivial solutions, and
[
6
]
, where infinitely many solutions are obtained for a class of non-local Orlicz-Sobolev Schrödinger equations.

Inspired by
[
11
]
, we obtain an existence result using the sub-supersolution method. More precisely,
we consider a sub-supersolution pair
(\underline{u},\overline{u})
and we define the set
S
(\underline{u},\overline{u})
as follows

\text{{S}}(\underline{u},\overline{u})=\left\{u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\colon\text{$u$ is a solution of \eqref{1.1}},~\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}\right\}.

One of the main result of the article states that if
(\underline{u},\overline{u})
is a sub-supersolution pair of (
1
), then there exists
u\in

S

(\underline{u},\overline{u})
, and hence (
1
) admits a solution.

Finally, we show, under some growth conditions in the source, the existence of three different nontrivial solutions for equation (
1
). More precisely, these solutions are one positive, one negative and one with non-constant sign. The method that we employ was introduced in
[
26
]
and was useful in different context, see for example
[
9
,
25
,
12
,
18
,
13
]
for the local case and
[
8
]
for the nonlocal context. This technique consists in restricting the functional associated to (
1
) to three different manifolds constructed by sign and normalization conditions. Unlike
[
26
]
, here we do not appeal to the Ljusternik-Schnirelman theory. Instead, we use the Ekeland variational principle to prove the existence of a critical point of each restricted functional which turns out to be a critical point of the unrestricted functional and, consequently, is also a weak solution of (
1
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we collect some basic facts of Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and the fractional
g
-Laplacian used throughout the manuscript. Section 3 is devoted to the study of the set
S

(\underline{u},\overline{u})
. Finally, in section 4 we prove the multiplicity of weak solutions for (
1
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
N-functions

In this section we introduce basic definitions and preliminary results related to Orlicz spaces. For more details see
[
17
]
. We start recalling the definition of an N-function.

Definition 1
.

A function
G\colon\mathbb{R}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
is called an N-function if it admits the representation

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau,

where the function
g
is right-continuous for
t\geq 0
, positive for
t>0
, non-decreasing, and satisfies the conditions

g(0)=0,\quad g(\infty)=\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)=\infty.

We extend
G
to
\mathbb{R}
as an even function. By
[
17
, Chapter 1, Sec. 5]
, an N-function has also the following properties:

(1)

G
is continuous, convex and even.

(2)

G
is super-linear at zero and at infinite, that is

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow 0}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=0

and

\displaystyle\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{G(x)}{x}=\infty.

Indeed, the above conditions serve as an equivalent definition of N-functions.

An important property for N-functions is the following:

Definition 2
.

We say that the N-function G satisfies the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition if there exists
C>2
such that

G(2x)\leq CG(x)\,\,\text{~~~for all~~}x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

According to
[
17
, Theorem 4.1, Chapter 1]
a necessary and sufficient condition for an N-function to satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition is that there is
p^{+}>1
such that

(2)

\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

Associate to
G
is the N-function complementary to it which is defined as follows:

(3)

\widetilde{G}(t):=\sup\left\{tw-G(w)\colon w>0\right\}.

The definition of the complementary function assures that the following Young-type inequality holds

(4)

at\leq G(t)+\widetilde{G}(a)\text{ for every }a,t\geq 0.

By
[
17
, Theorem 4.3, Chapter 1]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for the N-function
\widetilde{G}
complementary to
G
to satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition is that there is
p^{-}>1
such that

p^{-}\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)},~~~~~\forall\,t>0.

From now on, we will assume that the N-function
G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)d\tau
satisfies the following growth behavior:

(5)

1<p^{-}\leq\frac{tg(t)}{G(t)}\leq p^{+}<\infty,~~~~~\forall t>0.

Hence,
G
and
\widetilde{G}
both satisfy the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition. Observe that (
5
) holds for all
t\in\mathbb{R},\,t\neq 0.
Another useful condition (which implies (
5
)) is the following:

(6)

p^{-}-1\leq\dfrac{tg^{\prime}(t)}{g(t)}\leq p^{+}-1,~~~~~\forall t>0.

Again, (
6
) holds for any
t\in\mathbb{R},\,t\neq 0.

Examples of functions
G
verifying the above conditions are:

•

G(t)=|t|^{p}
,
p>1
;

•

G(t)=|t|^{p}+|t|^{q}
,
p,q>1
;

•

G(t)=t^{p}(|\log(t)|+1)
,
p>(3+\sqrt{5})/2
;

•

G(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(p|s|^{p-2}s(|\log(s)|+1)+\frac{|s|^{p-2}s}{1+|s|}\right)\,ds
, where
p^{+}=p+1
and
p^{-}=p-1
(see Remark 1 in
[
23
]
).

As a final assumption, we will finally impose that
g^{\prime}
is non-decreasing.

2.2.
Orlicz spaces

Now, we introduce the definition of the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. For more details on this functional setting, see
[
14
]
.

Definition 3
.

Let
s\in(0,1)

and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. We define

L^{G}(\Omega):=\left\{u\colon\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\,u\,\,is~~measurable\,\,and\,\,\rho_{G,\Omega}(u)<\infty\right\}

and

W^{s,G}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in L^{G}(\Omega)\colon\rho_{s,G,\Omega}(u)<\infty\right\},

where the modulars
\rho_{G,\Omega}
and
\rho_{s,G,\Omega}
are defined as

\rho_{G,\Omega}(u):=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}G\left(|u(x)|\right)dx,

\rho_{s,G,\Omega}(u):=\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega\times\Omega}G\left(\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)d\mu,\\

with

d\mu:=\dfrac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{n}}.

The norm associated to these spaces is the Luxemburg type norm

||u||_{s,G,\Omega}:=||u||_{G,\Omega}+[u]_{s,G,\Omega}

where

||u||_{G,\Omega}:=\displaystyle\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\rho_{G,\Omega}\left(\dfrac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}

and

\left[u\right]_{s,G,\Omega}:=\displaystyle\inf\left\{\lambda>0\colon\rho_{s,G,\Omega}\left(\dfrac{u}{\lambda}\right)\leq 1\right\}.

When
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we will omit the dependence on
\Omega
in the above quantities.

For further reference, we state the Hölder inequality.

Lemma 4
.

For
u\in L^{G}(\Omega)
and
v\in L^{\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
there holds

\int_{\Omega}|uv|dx\leq 2||u||_{G,\Omega}||v||_{\widetilde{G},\Omega}.

The topological dual space of
W^{s,G}(\Omega)
is denoted by
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
. We recall that when the N-function
G
satisfies the
\bigtriangleup_{2}
condition, then
L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are reflexive, separable Banach spaces (see
[
1
, Chapter 8]
,
[
14
, Proposition 2.11]
and
[
17
, Theorem 8.2]
).

Also, to include boundary values, we define the space

W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\colon u=0~~\text{in}~~\Omega^{c}\right\},

and the space of test functions

E:=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\subset W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

where the closure is taking with respect to the norm of
\|\cdot\|_{s,G}
. Observe that
E\subset W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Next, we mention some useful lemma for N-functions, which allows comparison with power functions.

Lemma 5
.

[
3
, Lemma 2.5]

Let

\xi^{-}(t)=\min\left\{t^{p^{-}},t^{p^{+}}\right\}

and

\xi^{+}(t)=\max\left\{t^{p^{-}},t^{p^{+}}\right\}.

Then,

(1)

\xi^{-}(||u||_{G,\Omega})\leq\rho_{G,\Omega}(u)\leq\xi^{+}(||u||_{G,\Omega})
, for
u\in L^{G}(\Omega)
.

(2)

\xi^{-}(\left[u\right]_{s,G})\leq\rho_{s,G}(u)\leq\xi^{+}(\left[u\right]_{s,G})
, for
u\in W^{s,G}(\Omega)
.

Now, we introduce a notion of comparison between
N
- functions.

Definition 6
.

Given two
N
-functions
A
and
B
, we say that
B
is essentially larger than
A
, denoted by

A\ll B,
if for any
c>0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{A(ct)}{B(t)}=0.

In order for the Sobolev immersion theorem to hold, it is necessary that G verifies the following two conditions:

(G1)

\displaystyle\int_{M}^{\infty}\left(\frac{t}{G(t)}\right)^{\frac{s}{n-s}}dt=\infty
for some
M
.

(G2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}\left(\frac{t}{G(t)}\right)^{\frac{s}{n-s}}dt<\infty

for some
\delta>0
.

Observe that when
G(t)=|t|^{p}
, then assumptions (G1) and (G2) are satisfied if
sp<n
. More examples can be seen in Remark 1 in
[
23
]
.

Given
G
satisfying (G1) and (G2) we define its Orlicz-Sobolev conjugate as

(7)

G_{\frac{n}{s}}(t)=G\circ H^{-1}(t),

where

H(t)=\left(\int_{0}^{t}\left(\frac{\tau}{G(\tau)}\right)^{\frac{s}{n-s}}d\tau\right)^{\frac{n-s}{n}}.

Hence, we have the following embedding theorem, see
[
2
]
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
G
be an N-function satisfying (
5
), (G1) and (G2), and let
G_{\frac{n}{s}}
be defined in (
7
). Then the embedding
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{G_{\frac{n}{s}}}(\Omega)
is continuous. Moreover, the N-function
G_{\frac{n}{s}}
is optimal in the class of Orlicz spaces.
Finally, given any N-function
B
, the embedding
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{B}(\Omega)
is compact if and only if
B\ll G_{\frac{n}{s}}
.

2.3.
Elementary inequalities

In this subsection we collect some elementary inequalities for N-functions
G
satisfying (
5
). We give the proofs of those which, up to our knowledge, are new in the literature.

Lemma 8
.

[
14
, Lemma 2.9]

Let
G
be an N-function satisfying (
5
) such that
g=G^{\prime}
. Then

\widetilde{G}(g(t))\leq(p^{+}-1)G(t)

holds for any
t\geq 0
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Then we have

(8)

G\left(|a_{+}-b_{+}|\right)\leq(a_{+}-b_{+})g(a-b),

where
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
a\geq 0
and
b\leq 0
, then

(9)

G(|a_{+}-b_{+}|)=G(a_{+})\leq\frac{a_{+}}{p^{-}}g(a_{+})\leq(a_{+}-b_{+})g(a-b),

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
g=G^{\prime}
is non-decreasing.
The case
b_{+}\geq 0
and
a_{+}=0
is similar.

Now, let
a_{+},b_{+}>0
, i.e.
a,b>0
. Then, if
a-b=a_{+}-b_{+}\geq 0
, we obtain from (
9
) that

G(|a_{+}-b_{+}|)=G(a_{+}-b_{+})\leq\frac{1}{p^{-}}(a_{+}-b_{+})g(a_{+}-b_{+})\leq(a_{+}-b_{+})g(a-b).

If
a-b=a_{+}-b_{+}<0
, and recalling that g is odd, we deduce

G(|a_{+}-b_{+}|)=G(b_{+}-a_{+})\leq\frac{1}{p^{-}}(b_{+}-a_{+})g(b_{+}-a_{+})\leq(a_{+}-b_{+})g(a-b).

∎

Lemma 10
.

Let
G
be an
N
-function satisfying whose derivative
g
satisfies (
6
).
Then, for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
it holds

g^{\prime}(a-b)(a_{+}-b_{+})(a_{+}-b_{+})\leq(p^{+}-1)g(a-b)(a_{+}-b_{+}).

Proof.

The lemma is true if
a,b<0
. Suppose that
a,b\geq 0
. If
a\geq b
, then, by (
6
),

g^{\prime}(a-b)(a_{+}-b_{+})(a_{+}-b_{+})=g^{\prime}(a-b)(a-b)(a_{+}-b_{+})\leq(p^{+}-1)g(a-b)(a_{+}-b_{+}).

If
a<b
,

g^{\prime}(a-b)(a_{+}-b_{+})(a_{+}-b_{+})=g^{\prime}(b-a)(b-a)(b_{+}-a_{+})\leq(p^{+}-1)g(b-a)(b_{+}-a_{+}).

On the other hand, if
a<0
and
b>0
, we have

g^{\prime}(a-b)(a_{+}-b_{+})(a_{+}-b_{+})=g^{\prime}(a-b)(-b)(-b_{+})\leq g^{\prime}(b-a)(b-a)(b_{+}-a_{+})

and we conclude as before. The case
a>0
and
b<0
is treated similarly.
∎

Lemma 11
.

[
20
, Lemma 2.1]

Assume (
6
) and that
g^{\prime}
is non-decreasing. There exists
C>0
such that

(10)

g(b)-g(a)\geq Cg(b-a),

for all
b\geq a
.

Remark 12
.

Observe that as
g
is odd

g(b)-g(a)\leq Cg(b-a),\qquad\mbox{ for all }b\leq a.

Lemma 13
.

Assume (
6
) and that
g^{\prime}
is non-decreasing. Let
a,b,c
and
d
be real numbers so that
a-b=c-d
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

G(|a-b|)\leq C(g(c)-g(d))(a-b).

Proof.

First, we consider the case
a>b
, using (
5
) and (
10
)

\displaystyle G(|a-b|)

\displaystyle=G(a-b)\leq\frac{1}{p^{-}}g(a-b)(a-b)=\frac{1}{p^{-}}g(c-d)(a-b)

\displaystyle\leq C(g(c)-g(d))(a-b).

The case
b>a
follows analogously appealing to Remark
12
.
∎

2.4.
The fractional g-Laplacian operator.

In this section we recall some elementary properties of the fractional
g
-Laplacian.
This operator is well defined between
W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and its dual space
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In fact, in
[
14
, Theorem 6.12]
the following representation formula is provided

\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u,v\rangle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu,

for any
v\in W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

We give some properties of the operator
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
in the next result. We point out that the monotonicity of
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
has been obtained in
[
4
, Lemma 3.4]
under a more restrictive assumption.

Lemma 14
.

(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}\colon W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a monotone, continuous, and an
(S)_{+}
-operator. Moreover, it admits a continuous inverse.

Proof.

The continuity of
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
follows from Hölder inequality. Observe that to prove that it is monotone and an
(S)_{+}
-operator, it is enough by
[
27
, Example 27.2]
to state that
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
is uniformly monotone.
Observe that for
u,v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
, we obtained thanks to Lemma
13
that

(11)

\begin{array}[]{rl}\rho_{s,G}(u-v)&=\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}G\left(\dfrac{(u-v)(x)-(u-v)(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\,d\mu\\
&=\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}G\left(\bigg|\dfrac{(u-v)(x)-(u-v)(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\bigg|\right)\,d\mu\\
&\leq C\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u-(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}v,u-v\right\rangle.\end{array}

Thus, by Lemma
5
, it follows that

\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u-(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}v,u-v\right\rangle\geq C\min\left\{\|u-v\|^{p^{+}-1}_{s,G},\|u-v\|^{p^{-}-1}_{s,G}\right\}\|u-v\|_{s,G}.

We conclude that
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
is uniformly monotone. To prove that it admits a continuous inverse is enough to show that is coercive, hemicontinuous and uniformily monotone (see for instance Theorem 26.A in
[
27
]
). In fact,

\frac{\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u,u\right\rangle}{[u]_{s,G}}\geq\frac{p^{-}\rho_{s,G}(u)}{[u]_{s,G}}\geq p^{-}\min\left\{[u]_{s,G}^{p^{-}-1},[u]_{s,G}^{p^{+}-1}\right\}

so, taking limit as
[u]_{s,G}
goes to infinity, we obtain that the fractional
g
-Laplacian is coercive.
Since the real function
t\to\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}(u+tv),w\right\rangle
is continuous in
[0,1]
for any
u,v,w\in W^{s,G}(\Omega)
we obtain that
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
is hemicontinuous. This ends the proof.

∎

3.
Solutions in a sub-supersolution interval

In this section we extend the results obtained in
[
11
]
to the Orlicz fractional setting. More precisely, we focus in proving the existence of solutions for (
1
). We assume that the function
f
satisfies the following hypothesis:

(\textbf{H}_{0})

f\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function such that for a.e
x\in\Omega
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}

|f(x,t)|\leq c_{0}(1+|b(t)|),~~~~~~

where
c_{0}>0
and
b(t)=B^{\prime}(t)
, with
B
an N-function satisfying (
5
) and
G\ll B\ll G_{\frac{n}{s}}
.

We point out that this kind of hypothesis has been used before in the literature, see for example
[
3
]
.

Now, we are in position to prove our first lemma.

Lemma 18
.

Suppose that
f
satisfies
(\textbf{H}_{0})
. Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in

W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

(i)

if
u_{1},u_{2}
are supersolutions of (
1
), then
u^{*}=\min\left\{u_{1},u_{2}\right\}
is also a supersolution;

(ii)

if
u_{1},u_{2}
are subsolutions of (
1
), then
u_{*}=\max\left\{u_{1},u_{2}\right\}
is a subsolution as well.

Proof.

We prove
(i)
, the proof of
(ii)
is analogous. We have for
i=1,2
and for all
v\in E_{+}
that

(13)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccll}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{i}(x)-u_{i}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{v(x)-v(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\geq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{i})vdx,\\
u_{i}\geq 0\mbox{ in }\Omega^{c}.\end{array}\right.

Set
u^{*}=\min\left\{u_{1},u_{2}\right\}\in W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then
u^{*}\geq 0
in
\Omega^{c}
.
Moreover, we consider the following sets

A_{1}=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon u_{1}(x)<u_{2}(x)\right\},\quad A_{2}=A_{1}^{c}.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
, and define for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
the truncation function

{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccll}0&&if~~t\leq 0,\\
\dfrac{t}{\varepsilon}&&if~~0<t<\varepsilon,\\
1&&if~~t\geq\varepsilon.\end{array}\right.

The mapping
{\tau_{\varepsilon}}\colon\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is Lipschitz continuous, non-decreasing,
0\leq{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(t)\leq 1
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and

(14)

{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{A_{1}},\qquad 1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{A_{2}}

a.e in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
. For simplicity, we denote

{\tau_{\varepsilon}}:={\tau_{\varepsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1})

and

1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}:=1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1}).

Let now
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)_{+}
. Using
{\tau_{\varepsilon}}\varphi
and
1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}\varphi
as test functions in (
13
), we obtain

(15)

\begin{split}I&:=\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)\varphi(x)-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(y)\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\\
&\quad+\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{(1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}})(x)\varphi(x)-(1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}})(y)\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\\
&\geq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{1}){\tau_{\varepsilon}}\varphi dx+\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{2})(1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}})\varphi dx:=II.\end{split}

We get, for
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
,

\begin{array}[]{llr}&I=\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)]d\mu&(A)\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(y)]d\mu&(B)\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{2}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}[{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)]d\mu&(C)\\
&-\displaystyle\iint_{A_{2}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(y)]d\mu&(D)\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)]d\mu&(E)\\
&-\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(y)]d\mu&(B)\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{2}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)]d\mu&(F)\\
&-\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{2}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)]d\mu&(C)\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{2}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}[{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(y)]d\mu&(D)\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{2}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(y)]d\mu&(G)\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{2}\times A_{2}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu&(H).\end{array}

Observe first that
(E),(F),(G)\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
by (
14
). Also, getting together the integrals with equal letters, we have

\displaystyle I=\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)]d\mu

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{1}}\left[g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)-g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\right]\dfrac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}[{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)-{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(y)]d\mu

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{2}}\left[g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}-g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right]{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x)d\mu

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{2}\times A_{1}}\left[g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}-g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right]{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(y)d\mu

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{2}\times A_{2}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu

\displaystyle+\textbf{o}(1)

\displaystyle=(A)+(B)+(C)+(D)+(H).

We have that
(B)
is negative, since
g
is non-decreasing and since for all
x,y\in A_{1}

u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)\geq u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)

if and only if

{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(y)={\tau_{\varepsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1})(y)\geq{\tau_{\varepsilon}}(u_{2}-u_{1})(x)={\tau_{\varepsilon}}(x).

On the other hand, for all
x\in A_{1}
,
y\in A_{2}
, there holds

u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)\leq u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(y)\leq u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y),

and thus, using again that
g
is non-decreasing, we get

g\left(\frac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\leq g\left(\frac{u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\leq g\left(\frac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right).

Then,
(C)
is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{2}}\left[g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right]\tau_{\varepsilon}(x)d\mu.

Analogously, for all
x\in A_{2}
,
y\in A_{1}
we can bound
(D)
by

\displaystyle\iint_{A_{2}\times A_{1}}\left[g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right]\tau_{\varepsilon}(y)d\mu.

So we have

\begin{array}[]{llr}I&\leq\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\tau_{\varepsilon}(x)d\mu\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{1}\times A_{2}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\tau_{\varepsilon}(x)d\mu\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{2}\times A_{1}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{1}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\tau_{\varepsilon}(y)d\mu\\
&+\displaystyle\iint_{A_{2}\times A_{2}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{2}(x)-u_{2}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\,+\textbf{o}(1)\\
&\to\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u^{*}(x)-u^{*}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu,\quad\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0$}.\end{array}

Now in order to estimate II, we use the Dominated Convergence Theorem. Observe that the mayorant is obtained thanks to
(\textbf{H}_{0})
and the definition of
{\tau_{\varepsilon}}
. In fact,

|f(\cdot,u_{1}){\tau_{\varepsilon}}\varphi|\leq c_{0}(1+|b(u_{1})|)\varphi,

|f(\cdot,u_{2})(1-{\tau_{\varepsilon}})\varphi|\leq c_{0}(1+|b(u_{2})|)\varphi.

So, passing to the limit as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
,

(16)

\begin{array}[]{ll}II\to\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x,u^{*})\varphi dx.\end{array}

Hence, taking
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
in (
15
), we get for all
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)_{+}
that

\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\frac{u^{*}(x)-u^{*}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\geq\int_{\Omega}f(x,u^{*})\varphi dx.

Finally by the definition of
E
, we obtain that the previous inequality holds for all test functions in
E_{+}
. Thus,
u^{*}
is a supersolution of (
1
), as we want to prove.
∎

Our next lemma proves that
S
(\underline{u},\overline{u})
is not empty.

Lemma 19
.

Let
(\underline{u},\overline{u})
be a sub-supersolution pair of (
1
) with
f
such that verifies
\textbf{H}_{0}
. Then, there exists
u\in
S
(\underline{u},\overline{u})
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we let
A=(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}\colon W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
. By Lemma
14
,
A
is monotone and hemicontinuous. Thanks to
[
22
, Lemma 2.98
(i)
]
we know that also
A
is pseudomonotone.

We consider a truncation of
f
defined as

\widetilde{f}(x,t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccll}f(x,\underline{u}(x))&&if~~t\leq\underline{u}(x),\\
f(x,t)&&if~~\underline{u}(x)<t<\underline{u}(x),\\
f(x,\overline{u}(x))&&if~~t\geq\overline{u}(x),\end{array}\right.

for all
(x,t)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}
. Observe that
\widetilde{f}
does not satisfy
\textbf{H}_{0}
in general, however
\widetilde{f}\colon\Omega\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory function which satisfies the following bound for a.e.
x
and all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(17)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{f}(x,t)|\leq c_{0}(1+|b(\overline{u}(x))|+|b(\underline{u}(x))|)\in L^{\widetilde{B}}(\Omega).

We define
D\colon W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\to W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
as

\langle D(u),v\rangle=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{f}(x,u)v~dx,

for all
u,v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. Observe that
D
is well defined since by (
17
) and
G\ll B
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left|-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{f}(x,u)v~dx\right|&\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{f}(x,u)||v|~dx\\
&\leq 2||\widetilde{f}(x,u)||_{\widetilde{B},\Omega}||v||_{B,\Omega}\\
&\leq C||\widetilde{f}(x,u)||_{\widetilde{B},\Omega}||v||_{s,G,\Omega}\\
&<\infty.\end{array}

Moreover,
D
is linear and continuous.

We now prove that
D
is strongly continuous. Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence such that
u_{n}\rightharpoonup u
in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
, and take any subsequence of
\{D(u_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that we still denote by
\{D(u_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. Passing to a further subsequence if necessary, we have
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{B}(\Omega)
,
u_{n}(x)\to u(x)
and
|u_{n}(x)|\leq h(x)
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, for some
h\in L^{B}(\Omega)
. Therefore, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by
\textbf{H}_{0}
and Lemma
8
we have for a.e.
x\in\Omega

\begin{array}[]{ll}|\widetilde{f}(x,u_{n})-\widetilde{f}(x,u)|&\leq|\widetilde{f}(x,u_{n})|+|\widetilde{f}(x,u)|\\
&=2c_{0}+c_{0}|b(\overline{u}_{n})|+c_{0}|b(\underline{u}_{n})|+c_{0}|b(\overline{u})|+c_{0}|b(\underline{u})|\in L^{\widetilde{B}}(\Omega).\end{array}

Observe that by continuity of
f(x,\cdot)
we have
\widetilde{f}(x,u_{n})\to\widetilde{f}(x,u)
a.e. Moreover, for all
v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
,

(18)

\begin{array}[]{ll}|\langle D(u_{n})-D(u),v\rangle|&\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{f}(x,u_{n})-\widetilde{f}(x,u)||v|dx\\
&\leq 2||\widetilde{f}(x,u_{n})-\widetilde{f}(x,u)||_{\widetilde{B},\Omega}||v||_{B,\Omega}\\
&\leq C||\widetilde{f}(x,u_{n})-\widetilde{f}(x,u)||_{\widetilde{B},\Omega}||v||_{s,G,\Omega}.\\
\end{array}

By dominated convergence Theorem, the right-hand side of (
18
) tends to 0 uniformly for any
v
with
\|v\|_{s,G,\Omega}\leq 1
. Hence
D(u_{n})\to D(u)
in
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
. Therefore, any subsequence of
\{D(u_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
has a further subsequence that converges to
D(u)
. Hence, the whole sequence
\{D(u_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
D(u)
and
D
is strongly continuous. Thanks to
[
22
, Lemma 2.98
(ii)
]
,
D
is pseudomonotone. Moreover,
A+D
is pseudomonotone by
[
27
, Proposition 27.7]
.

Now we prove that
A+D
sends bounded sets into bounded set. We already stated that
A
is bounded. Regarding
D
, observe that

\begin{array}[]{ll}||D(u)||_{-s,\widetilde{G}}&=\displaystyle\sup_{||v||_{s,G,\Omega}\leq 1}\langle D(u),v\rangle\\
&=\displaystyle\sup_{||v||_{s,G,\Omega}\leq 1}-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{f}(x,u)vdx\\
&\leq\displaystyle\sup_{||v||_{s,G,\Omega}\leq 1}2||\widetilde{f}(x,u)||_{\widetilde{B},\Omega}||v||_{B,\Omega}\\
&\leq C\displaystyle\sup_{||v||_{s,G,\Omega}\leq 1}||\widetilde{f}(x,u)||_{\widetilde{B},\Omega}||v||_{s,G,\Omega}\\
&\leq C\|c_{0}(1+|b(\overline{u})|+|b(\underline{u})|)\|_{\widetilde{B},\Omega}.\\
\end{array}

Thus,
D
sends bounded sets into bounded sets.

It remains to check that
A+D
is coercive. In fact, for all
u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
with
||u||_{s,G}\geq 1
we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\langle A(u)+D(u),u\rangle}{||u||_{s,G}}&=\dfrac{\langle A(u),u\rangle}{||u||_{s,G}}+\dfrac{\langle D(u),u\rangle}{||u||_{s,G}}\\
&\geq\dfrac{p^{-}\rho_{s,G}(u)}{||u||_{s,G}}-\dfrac{1}{||u||_{s,G}}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{f}(x,u)u~dx\\
&\geq\dfrac{p^{-}\xi^{-}(\left[u\right]_{s,G})}{||u||_{s,G}}-\dfrac{1}{||u||_{s,G}}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\widetilde{f}(x,u)||u|~dx\\
&\geq p^{-}\left[u\right]_{s,G}^{p^{-}-1}-\dfrac{2}{||u||_{s,G}}||\widetilde{f}(x,u)||_{\widetilde{B},\Omega}||u||_{B,\Omega}\\
&\geq p^{-}\left[u\right]_{s,G}^{p^{-}-1}-\dfrac{C}{||u||_{s,G}}||\widetilde{f}(x,u)||_{\widetilde{B},\Omega}||u||_{s,G}\\
&\geq p^{-}\left[u\right]_{s,G}^{p^{-}-1}-C.\end{array}

Observe that this goes to infinity when
\left[u\right]_{s,G}\to\infty
, as we want to prove.

Appealing to
[
22
, Theorem 2.99]
, the equation

(19)

A(u)+D(u)=0\text{~~~~~in $W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)$}

has a solution
u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Next, we prove that in
\Omega

(20)

\underline{u}\leq u\leq\overline{u}.

Observe that (
20
) holds in
\Omega^{c}
. Now we use
(u-\overline{u})_{+}\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)_{+}
as a test function in (
19
). Then

\langle A(u)+D(u),(u-\overline{u})_{+}\rangle=\langle A(u),(u-\overline{u})_{+}\rangle+\langle D(u),(u-\overline{u})_{+}\rangle=0.

Thus,

\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u,(u-\overline{u})_{+}\rangle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{f}(x,u)(u-\overline{u})_{+}~dx.

Observe now that in the support of
(u-\overline{u})_{+}
we have
\widetilde{f}(x,u)=f(x,\overline{u})
. Hence,

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u,(u-\overline{u})_{+}\rangle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x,\overline{u})(u-\overline{u})_{+}~dx\\
&\leq\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{\overline{u}(x)-\overline{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{(u-\overline{u})_{+}(x)-(u-\overline{u})_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\\
&=\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}\overline{u},(u-\overline{u})_{+}\rangle,\end{array}

where we also used that
\overline{u}
is a supersolution of (
1
), so

(21)

\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u-(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}\overline{u},(u-\overline{u})_{+}\rangle\leq 0.

In the next calculations, we first use (
8
) with

a=\dfrac{(u-\overline{u})(x)}{|x-y|^{s}}

and

b=\dfrac{(u-\overline{u})(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}.

Also, by (
10
) and the fact that
u(x)-u(y)\leq\overline{u}(x)-\overline{u}(y)
if and only if

(u-\overline{u})_{+}(x)\leq(u-\overline{u})_{+}(y)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\rho_{s,G}\left((u-\overline{u})_{+}\right)=\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}G\left(\dfrac{|(u-\overline{u})_{+}(x)-(u-\overline{u})_{+}(y)|}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)d\mu\\
&\leq\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{(u-\overline{u})(x)-(u-\overline{u})(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{(u-\overline{u})_{+}(x)-(u-\overline{u})_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\\
&\leq\dfrac{1}{C}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left[g\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)-g\left(\dfrac{\overline{u}(x)-\overline{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\right]\dfrac{(u-\overline{u})_{+}(x)-(u-\overline{u})_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\\
&=\dfrac{1}{C}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{(u-\overline{u})_{+}(x)-(u-\overline{u})_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\\
&-\dfrac{1}{C}\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{\overline{u}(x)-\overline{u}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{(u-\overline{u})_{+}(x)-(u-\overline{u})_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\\
&=C^{\prime}\left[\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u-(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}\overline{u},(u-\overline{u})_{+}\rangle\right].\end{array}

Consequently, by (
21
), it follows
(u-\overline{u})_{+}=0
. Analogously we prove
u\geq\underline{u}
, so the inequality (
20
) holds.

Finally, using (
20
) in (
19
) we see that
u\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
solves (
1
) in the sense of Definition
16
(since
E\subset W^{s,G}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
) and
u\in S(\underline{u},\overline{u})
as we want to prove.
∎

We end the section giving a result related to extremal elements in

. For that, we will need two preliminary results. The proofs below are similar to those from
[

]
. However, we write them for convenience of the reader.

We recall the following definition.

Definition 20
.

We say that a partially ordered set
(S,\leq)
is
downward directed
(resp.
upward directed
) if for all
u_{1},u_{2}\in S
there exists
u_{3}\in S
such that
u_{3}\leq u_{1},u_{2}
(resp.
u_{3}\geq u_{1},u_{2}
).

Also, we say that
S
is
directed
if it is both downward and upward directed.

Lemma 21
.

Let
(\underline{u},\overline{u})
be a sub-supersolution pair of (
1
) with
f
such that verifies
\textbf{H}_{0}
. Then,
S(\underline{u},\overline{u})
is directed.

Proof.

We prove that
S(\underline{u},\overline{u})
is downward directed. Let
u_{1},u_{2}\in S(\underline{u},\overline{u})
, then in particular
u_{1},u_{2}
are supersolutions of (
1
). Set
u^{*}=\min\left\{u_{1},u_{2}\right\}\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
, then by Lemma
18

u^{*}
is a supersolution of (
1
) and
\underline{u}\leq u^{*}
. By Lemma
19
there exists
u_{3}\in S(\underline{u},u^{*})
, in particular
u_{3}\in S(\underline{u},\overline{u})
and
u_{3}\leq u^{*}
.

In the same way we see that
S(\underline{u},\overline{u})
is upward directed. And then
S(\underline{u},\overline{u})
is directed.
∎

Lemma 22
.

Let
(\underline{u},\overline{u})
be a sub-supersolution pair of (
1
) with
f
such that verifies
\textbf{H}_{0}
. Then
S(\underline{u},\overline{u})
is compact in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
S(\underline{u},\overline{u})
, then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
v\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)

(22)

\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u_{n},v\rangle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{n})v~dx

and
\underline{u}\leq u_{n}\leq\overline{u}
. Using
u_{n}\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
as a test function in (
22
), we have by
\textbf{H}_{0}

\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u_{n},u_{n}\rangle&\leq\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|f(x,u_{n})||u_{n}|~dx\\
&\leq c_{0}\left[\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|u_{n}|dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|b(u_{n})||u_{n}|~dx\right]\\

M_{2}
is also a smooth sub-manifold of
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.

We next consider
M_{3}
. For
u\in M_{3}
, we have

\begin{split}&\left\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(u),u\right\rangle=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g^{\prime}\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\dfrac{u_{+}(x)-u_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mu\\
&\quad+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{u_{+}(x)-u_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu-\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)u_{+}dx-\int_{\Omega}f_{u}(x,u)u_{+}^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

Appealing to the fact that

sign(a-b)=sign(a_{+}-b_{+}),

for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
a-b,a_{+}-b_{+}\neq 0
, and using (
6
), it follows that

\begin{split}&\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g^{\prime}\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\dfrac{u_{+}(x)-u_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mu\\
&\qquad+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{u_{+}(x)-u_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\,d\mu\\
&\qquad\leq p^{+}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{u_{+}(x)-u_{+}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu\\
&\qquad=p^{+}\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u,u_{+}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Hence, recalling that
u\in M_{1}
and proceeding as in (
31
), we obtain that

\left\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(u),u\right\rangle<0.

Similarly, by the fact that

sign(a-b)\neq sign(a_{-}-b_{-}),

for all
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
a-b,a_{-}-b_{-}\neq 0
, using (
6
) and the choice of
c_{3}
in (
\textbf{H}_{2}
), it follows that

\left\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{2}(u),u\right\rangle\leq p^{-}\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u,u_{+}\right\rangle-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)u_{-}dx-\int_{\Omega}f_{u}(x,u)u_{-}^{2}dx<0.

Therefore
M_{3}
is a smooth sub-manifold of
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.
∎

Lemma 31
.

The sets
K_{i}
are complete.

Proof.

We consider
K_{3}
, the proofs for
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are similar. Let
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be a Cauchy sequence in
K_{3}
, then
u_{k}\rightarrow u
in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. In particular,
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u_{k}\to(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u
in
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
and
u_{k_{+}}\rightharpoonup u_{+}
in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
. So,

(32)

\begin{split}&\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u_{k},u_{k_{+}}\right\rangle\\
&\quad=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left[\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u_{k}-(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u,u_{k_{+}}\right\rangle+\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u,u_{k_{+}}\right\rangle\right]\\
&\quad=\left\langle(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u,u_{+}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Moreover, using the compact embedding
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{B}(\Omega)
, we get
u_{k_{+}}\rightarrow u_{+}
in
L^{B}(\Omega)
and hence by
(\mathbf{H}_{0})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{k})u_{k_{+}}dx\rightarrow\int_{\Omega}f(x,u)u_{+}dx.

Hence, recalling Lemma
28
, we get
u_{+}\in M_{1}
. Similarly,
u_{-}\in M_{2}
, and thus we obtain
u\in K_{3}
. This shows that
K_{3}
is complete.
∎

For the next result, we recall the definition of tangent space of Banach manifolds.

Definition 32
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
M\subset X
be a Banach manifold. The tangent space at
u
of
M
is

T_{u}M=\{v\colon\exists\alpha:(-1,1)\to M\quad\alpha(0)=u\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\alpha^{\prime}(0)=v\}.

We recall that in Banach manifolds,
T_{u}M
is a closed linear subspace of
X
.

Lemma 33
.

For every
u\in M_{i}
we obtain the direct decomposition

T_{u}W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)=T_{u}M_{i}\oplus span\{u_{+},u_{-}\}.

Moreover, the projection onto the first component in this decomposition is uniformly continuous on bounded sets of
M_{i}
.

Proof.

We show the descomposition of
M_{1}
.
Let
v\in T_{u}W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
and
v=v_{1}+v_{2}
, where
v_{2}=\alpha u_{+}
and
v_{1}=v-v_{2}
. We are interesting in choosing
\alpha
so that
v_{1}\in T_{u}M_{1}
,

\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(u),v\rangle=\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(u),\alpha u_{+}\rangle+\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(u),v_{1}\rangle.

If we choose

\alpha=\dfrac{\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(u),v\rangle}{\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(u),u_{+}\rangle},

then
\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(u),v_{1}\rangle=0
.
So, we obtain

T_{u}W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)=T_{u}M_{1}\oplus span\{u_{+}\},

where
M_{1}=\left\{u\colon\varphi_{1}(u)=0\right\}
and
T_{u}M_{1}=\left\{v\colon\langle\varphi^{\prime}_{1}(u),v\rangle=0\right\}
.
Analogously,

T_{u}W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)=T_{u}M_{2}\oplus span\{u_{-}\}

and

T_{u}W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)=T_{u}M_{3}\oplus span\{u_{+},u_{-}\}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

In the next lemma, we prove that the unrestricted functional
\Phi
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition, that is, if
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
is a sequence such that
\Phi(u_{j})
is uniformly bounded and
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{j})\rightarrow 0
strongly in
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
, then
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
contains a strongly convergent subsequence. The arguments of the proof are standard. However, we write it for convenience of the reader.

Lemma 34
.

The unrestricted functional
\Phi
satisfies the Palais-Smale condition.

Proof.

Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
be a Palais-Smale sequence, i.e
\Phi(u_{j})
is uniformly bounded and
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{j})\rightarrow 0
strongly in
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
. Since
\Phi(u_{j})
is uniformly bounded, using Lemma
27
,
\{u_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
.

We define
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{j}):=\psi_{j}
, then

\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{j}),z\rangle=\langle\psi_{j},z\rangle,~~for~all~z\in W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega).

Also,

\langle\Phi^{\prime}(u_{j}),z\rangle=\displaystyle\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g\left(\dfrac{u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}\right)\dfrac{z(x)-z(y)}{|x-y|^{s}}d\mu-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x,u_{j})zdx.

Then,
v
is a weak solution of problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccll}\qquad(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}v=f(x,u_{j})+\psi_{j}&&\text{in}~~\Omega,\\
v=0&&\text{in}~~\Omega^{c}.\end{array}\right.

Observe that by
(\textbf{H}_{0})
,
f_{j}:=f(x,u_{j})+\psi_{j}\in W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
.

Now, we define the operator
T\colon W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)\rightarrow W^{s,G}_{0}(\Omega)
, as
T(h):=u
, where
u
is a weak solution of problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccll}(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}u=h&&\text{in}~~\Omega,\\
\quad\quad\,\,\,\,\,\,\,u=0&&\text{in}~~\Omega^{c}.\end{array}\right.

By Lemma
11
we know that
(-\bigtriangleup_{g})^{s}
is continuous and that admits a continuous inverse on
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
. It is enough to see that
f_{j}\rightarrow f(\cdot,u(\cdot))
in
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
. As
\Phi^{\prime}(u_{j})\rightarrow 0
strongly in
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
, we only need to check that
\{f(x,u_{j})\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
has a subsequence that strongly converges in
W^{-s,\widetilde{G}}(\Omega)
, the proof of this fact can be found in
[
3
, Lemma 3.5]
. This finishes the proof.

Theorem 37

Under assumptions
(\mathbf{H}_{0})–(\mathbf{H}_{2})

\Phi(u)\geq c[u]^{p^{+}}_{s,G}

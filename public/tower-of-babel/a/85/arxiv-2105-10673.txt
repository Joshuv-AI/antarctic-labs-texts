Estimation and numerical validation of inf-sup constant for bilinear form ( p , div ​ 𝒖 ) (p,\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u})
V. Jain, M. Gerritsma

Abstract
We give a derivation for the value of inf-sup constant for the bilinear form ( p , div ​ 𝒖 ) \left(p\;,\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right) . We prove that the value of inf-sup constant is equal to 1.0 in all cases and is independent of the size and shape of the domain. Numerical tests for validation of inf-sup constants is performed using finite dimensional spaces defined in [ 1 ] on two test domains i) a square of size Ω = [ 0 , 1 ] 2 \Omega=[0,1]^{2} , ii) a square of size Ω = [ 0 , 2 ] 2 \Omega=[0,2]^{2} , for varying mesh sizes and polynomial degrees. The numeric values are in agreement with the theoretical value of inf-sup term.



1
Introduction

Numerical schemes for mixed finite element methods
should result in stable bounded solutions.
The stability of finite element discretization is governed by the inf-sup criterion for bilinear forms.
The objective of this paper is to derive and validate the inf-sup stability constant for the bilinear form of the divergence term.
This is of importance because it often appears as
pressure constraint
term in discretization of fluid mechanics equations.
For given
\bm{u}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega\right)
,
p\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
, the bilinear form is give by

b\left(p,\bm{u}\right)=\left(p\;,\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right)\;.

(1)

The inf-sup constant
\beta
for (
1
) is given by

\beta=\inf_{p\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\sup_{\bm{u}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega\right)}\frac{\left(p\;,\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right)}{\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\|\bm{u}\|_{H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega\right)}}\;.

(2)

Quite some work has been done to approximate the value of inf-sup constant see for eg.
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

In this work we measure the norm of the velocity field in the space orthogonal to kernel of the divergence operator
and prove that the value of the inf-sup constant
\beta=1.0
.
For numerical tests we use finite dimensional spaces defined in
[
1
]
.
The validation study is performed on three different domains: i) unit square
\Omega=[0,1]^{2}
; ii) square
\Omega=[0,2]^{2}
, for varying mesh sizes,
h
, and polynomial degrees
N=1,2,3
.

The numerical values are in agreement with the derived values of
\beta
, with maximum errors shown of the order of
10^{-6}
.

2
Derivation of inf-sup constant
\beta

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be an open, bounded domain.

We will use the finite dimensional spaces
and the divergence operator
defined in
[
1
, §3]
:
D\left(\Omega\right)\subset H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega\right)
,
\widetilde{S}\left(\Omega\right)\subset L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
,
S\left(\Omega\right)\subset L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
,
and
\mathbb{E}^{2,1}
the discrete representation of the divergence operator
.
Let
K=\mathrm{Ker}\ \mathbb{E}^{2,1}
,
H=\mathrm{Ker}\left({\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\intercal}\right)
.
The discrete inf-sup condition is then given by

\beta=\inf_{p\in H^{\perp}}\sup_{\bm{u}\in K^{\perp}}\frac{\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)^{\intercal}{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\top}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)}{\|\bm{u}\|_{H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}\;,

(3)

where
\mathcal{N}^{x}
denotes the degrees of freedom in our finite element, i.e. the vector of expansion coefficients
.
Here
p\in\widetilde{S}(\Omega)\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\bm{u}\in D(\Omega)\subset H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)
and the vectors
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)
and
\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)
are the expansion coefficients.
The norm of
p\in\widetilde{S}(\Omega)
is

\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}={\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)}^{\intercal}{\mathbb{M}^{(2)}}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)\;,

(4)

the norm of
\bm{u}\in H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega\right)
is

\|\bm{u}\|^{2}_{H\left(\mathrm{div};\Omega\right)}={\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)}^{\intercal}\left(\mathbb{M}^{(1)}+{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\intercal}\mathbb{M}^{(2)}\mathbb{E}^{2,1}\right)\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)\;,

and the norm of
\bm{u}\in K^{\perp}\subset D(\Omega)
is

\|\bm{u}\|_{K^{\perp}}^{2}=\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)^{\intercal}{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\intercal}\mathbb{M}^{(2)}{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)=\|\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\;.

(5)

In the continuous case, using Cauchy Schwartz inequality, we have, for all
p\in L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
and
\bm{u}\in K^{\perp}

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{\left(p,\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u}\right)}{\|\bm{u}\|_{K^{\perp}}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}\leq\frac{\|\mathrm{div}\ \bm{u})\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}{\|\bm{u}\|_{K^{\perp}}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}=1\;.}

(6)

If this inequality holds for all
p
and
\bm{u}
it should also hold when we take the infimum over
\widetilde{S}(\Omega)
and the supremum over
D(\Omega)
, from which we conclude that
\beta\leq 1
.

Now, for an arbitrary vector field
\bm{u}^{*}
, we have

\sup_{u\in K^{\perp}}\frac{\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)^{\intercal}{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\intercal}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)}{\|\bm{u}\|_{H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}\geq\frac{\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}^{*}\right)^{\intercal}{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\intercal}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)}{\|\bm{u}^{*}\|_{H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}\;,

(7)

If we now take for
\bm{u}^{*}
, the vector field with expansion coefficients which satisfy
\mathbb{M}^{(2)}\mathbb{E}^{2,1}\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}^{*}\right)=\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)
, then
\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}=\|\bm{u}^{*}\|_{H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}
and the numerator
\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)^{\intercal}\mathbb{E}^{\intercal}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)=\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}=\|\bm{u}^{*}\|_{H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}
.
If we insert these estimates in (
7
) we have

\sup_{\bm{u}\in K^{\perp}}\frac{\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)^{\intercal}{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\intercal}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)}{\|\bm{u}\|_{H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}\geq\frac{\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)^{*\intercal}{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\intercal}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)}{\|\bm{u}^{*}\|_{H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}=\frac{\|\bm{u}^{*}\|_{H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}{\|\bm{u}^{*}\|_{H(\mathrm{div};\Omega)}\|p\|_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}=1\;,

(8)

which shows that
\beta\geq 1
.
From (
6
) and (
8
) we conclude that
\beta=1
in the discrete setting.
This value is independent of the mesh size or polynomial degree, so this value is also the inf-sup constant for
h\rightarrow 0
. This value is also independent of the domain
\Omega
.

3
Evaluation of numeric inf-sup constant
{\beta}_{h}

In this section we will follow
[
5
, §3.4.3]
to evaluate the inf-sup constant.
Let
S_{x}
and
S_{y}
be the two symmetric and (semi-)positive definite matrices, such that

\displaystyle S_{x}^{\intercal}S_{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\intercal}\mathbb{M}^{(2)}\mathbb{E}^{2,1}\;,

(9)

\displaystyle S_{y}^{\intercal}S_{y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathbb{M}^{(2)}}^{-1}\;.

(10)

We can write the norms in (
4
) and (
5
) as

\displaystyle||\bm{u}||_{K^{\perp}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)}^{\intercal}{\mathbb{E}^{2,1}}^{\intercal}\mathbb{M}^{(2)}\mathbb{E}^{2,1}\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)={\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)}^{\intercal}S_{x}^{\intercal}S_{x}\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)=||S_{x}\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)||_{E}\;,

(11)

\displaystyle||p||_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)}^{\intercal}{\mathbb{M}^{(2)}}^{-1}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)={\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)}^{\intercal}S_{y}^{\intercal}S_{y}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)=||S_{y}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)||_{E}\;,

(12)

where
||\cdot||_{E}
is the Eucledian vector norm.

Let
M=S_{y}^{-1}\ \mathbb{E}^{2,1}\ S_{x}^{-1}
and its singular value decomposition be given by

M=S_{y}^{-1}\ \mathbb{E}^{2,1}\ S_{x}^{-1}=V\Sigma U\;.

(13)

Now we can write
\mathbb{E}^{2,1}
as

\mathbb{E}^{2,1}=S_{y}S_{y}^{-1}\ \mathbb{E}^{2,1}\ S_{x}^{-1}S_{x}=S_{y}MS_{x}=S_{y}V\Sigma US_{x}\;.

(14)

Also, let

\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)=S_{x}^{-1}U^{\intercal}\bm{x}\;,\qquad\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)=S_{y}^{-1}Vy\;.

(15)

Now, if we substitute
\bm{u}
,
p
from (
15
) and
\mathbb{E}^{2,1}
from (
14
) in the RHS term of (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\inf_{p\in H^{\perp}}\sup_{\bm{u}\in K^{\perp}}\frac{{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}^{0}\left(p\right)}^{\intercal}\mathbb{E}^{2,1}\mathcal{N}^{1}\left(\bm{u}\right)}{||\bm{u}||_{K^{\perp}}||p||_{L^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in\left(\mathrm{Ker}\ \Sigma^{\intercal}\right)^{\perp}}\sup_{\bm{x}\in\left(\mathrm{Ker}\ \Sigma\right)^{\perp}}\frac{y^{\intercal}V^{\intercal}S_{y}^{-1}S_{y}V\Sigma US_{x}S_{x}^{-1}U^{\intercal}\bm{x}}{||S_{x}{S_{x}}^{-1}U^{\intercal}\bm{x}||_{E}||S_{y}{S_{y}}^{-1}Vy||_{E}}

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf_{y\in\left(\mathrm{Ker}\ \Sigma^{\intercal}\right)^{\perp}}\sup_{\bm{x}\in\left(\mathrm{Ker}\ \Sigma\right)^{\perp}}\frac{y^{\intercal}\Sigma\bm{x}}{||\bm{x}||_{E}||y||_{E}}:=\beta_{h}\;.

where in the last step we used
[
5
, Prop 3.4.3]
which states that there exists a positive constant
{\beta}_{h}
that is equivalent to the smallest positive singular value of the matrix,
M=S_{y}^{-1}E^{2,1}S_{x}^{-1}
.

5
Conclusions

In this paper, we derive a theoretical estimate for the discrete inf-sup formulation and validate the value of the constant using finite dimensional spaces defined in
[
1
]
.

The theoretical proof of the inf-sup term becomes straight forward when we use the appropriate norm on
K^{\perp}
space, see (
5
).

We evaluate the constant for
two
different test cases, i) a unit square domain
\Omega=[0,1]^{2}
, ii) a square domain
\Omega=[0,2]^{2}
.
It is shown that for all the cases the numerical value
is in agreement with the theoretical value
.

A Mass Model for the Lensing Cluster SDSS J1004+4112: Constraints From the Third Time Delay
R. Forés-Toribio, J. A. Muñoz, C. S. Kochanek, E. Mediavilla

Abstract
We have built a new model for the lens system SDSS J1004+4112 including the recently measured time delay of the fourth quasar image. This time delay has a strong influence on the inner mass distribution of the lensing cluster ( ρ ∝ r − α \rho\propto r^{-\alpha} ) allowing us to determine α = 1.18 − 0.03 ​ ( − 0.18 ) + 0.02 ​ ( + 0.11 ) \alpha=1.18^{+0.02(+0.11)}_{-0.03(-0.18)} at the 68% (95%) confidence level in agreement with hydrodynamical simulations of massive galaxy clusters. We find an offset between the brightest cluster galaxy (BCG) and the dark matter halo of 3.8 − 0.7 ​ ( − 1.3 ) + 0.6 ​ ( + 1.4 ) 3.8^{+0.6(+1.4)}_{-0.7(-1.3)} kpc at 68% (95%) confidence which is compatible with other galaxy cluster measurements. As an observational challenge, the estimated time delay between the leading image C and the faint (I=24.7) fifth image E is roughly 8 yr.



I
Introduction

The lens system SDSS J1004+4112 was the first example of a quasar lensed by a cluster
(
2003Natur.426..810I
)
. The source and lens redshifts are
z_{s}=1.73
and
z_{l}=0.68
, respectively. This leads to an image separation of 15″ and large magnified images of the host galaxy. It is the second largest quasar lens after SDSS J1029+2623
(
2006ApJ...653L..97I
)
. Since its discovery, many additional observational constraints have been obtained: more galaxy cluster members
(
2004ApJ...605...78O
)
, the central quasar image
(
2005PASJ...57L...7I
;
2008PASJ...60L..27I
)
, other background lensed sources
(
2005ApJ...629L..73S
;
2009MNRAS.397..341L
;
2010PASJ...62.1017O
)
, the velocity dispersion of the brightest galaxy cluster
(
2008PASJ...60L..27I
)
and the time delays between quasar images A, B, and C
(
2007ApJ...662...62F
;
2008ApJ...676..761F
)
.

As new observational constraints were measured, increasingly sophisticated mass models of the lens cluster were made using both parametric
(
2003Natur.426..810I
;
2004ApJ...605...78O
;
2007ApJ...662...62F
;
2010PASJ...62.1017O
)
and nonparametric
(
2004AJ....128.2631W
;
2006ApJ...652L...5S
;
2009MNRAS.397..341L
;
2015PASJ...67...21M
)
approaches. Recently, the time delay of the fourth quasar image
(
Munoz_submitted
)
has been measured at 2457 days between images D and C, and it is over one year longer than predicted
(
2010PASJ...62.1017O
)
. Indeed, the ability of the models to predict the unmeasured time delays has been remarkably poor.

Apart from the models of the mass distribution, this system has been widely studied not only in the optical but also from X-rays to radio
(
2006A&A...454..493L
;
2006ApJ...647..215O
;
2009ApJ...702..472R
;
2011ApJ...739L..28J
;
2012ApJ...755...24C
;
2021MNRAS.505L..36M
)
. However, there are still issues that remain unsolved like the origin of the enhanced asymmetric wings in the broad emission lines of quasar image A
(
2004ApJ...610..679R
;
2006ApJ...645L...5G
;
2006A&A...454..493L
;
2012ApJ...755...82M
;
2018ApJ...859...50F
;
2020A&A...634A..27P
;
2021A&A...653A.109F
)
.

Here we revisit the mass model making use of the newly measured time delay. The new delay should help to constrain the inner mass profile of the cluster halo and to look for deviations from a Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) profile
(
2006PASJ...58..271K
;
2010PASJ...62.1017O
)
. The paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
we present the available observational constraints. In Section
we describe the modeling method and the components of the mass model. Section
presents the results and they are discussed in Section
. Throughout this paper we assume a flat
\Lambda
CDM cosmology with
\Omega_{M}=0.26
,
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.74
and
H_{0}
=72 km s
{}^{-}1
Mpc
-1
.

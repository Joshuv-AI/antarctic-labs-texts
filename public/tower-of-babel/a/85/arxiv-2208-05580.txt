The weak elliptic Harnack inequality revisited Thanks: Supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (No. 11871296).
Jiaxin Hu, Zhenyu Yu

Abstract
. In this paper we firstly derive the weak elliptic Harnack inequality from the generalized capacity condition, the tail estimate of jump measure and the Poincaré inequality, for any regular Dirichlet form without killing part on a measure metric space, by using the lemma of growth and the John-Nirenberg inequality. We secondly show several equivalent characterizations of the weak elliptic Harnack inequality for any (not necessarily regular) Dirichlet form. We thirdly present some consequences of the weak elliptic Harnack inequality. Dedicated to the memory of Professor Ka-Sing Lau.

1.
Introduction and main results

In 1961, Moser showed in
[
36
]
that the following
elliptic Harnack inequality
, denoted by
(\mathrm{H})
, is true: for any
compact
D^{\prime}
in a domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for any
function
u
which is non-negative, harmonic (with respect to the symmetric,
uniformly elliptic divergence-form operator) in
D
, we have

\sup_{D^{\prime}}u\leq C\inf_{D^{\prime}}u,

where
C=C(D^{\prime},D)\geq 1
is a constant depending only on
D^{\prime},D
. The importance of this inequality is that the constant
C
is
independent of function
u
(but may depend on two domains
D^{\prime}
,
D
). If further
D^{\prime},D
are two concentric balls, for example, if
D=B(x,R)
and
D^{\prime}=B(x,R/2)
, then

\sup_{B(x,R/2)}u\leq C\inf_{B(x,R/2)}u,

(1.1)

where the constant
C\geq 1
is independent not only of function
u
, but
also of ball
B
. The inequality (
1.1
) says that a function, which is
both non-negative and harmonic in a ball, is nearly constant around the
center. The reader may consult a book
[
39
, Theorem 2.1.1]
for more details.

A symmetric, uniformly elliptic operator gives arise to a strongly local,
regular Dirichlet form in the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx)
(see
for example
[
19
, Chapter 1]
on the basic
theory of Dirichlet forms on a Hilbert space). The elliptic Harnack
inequality plays an important role in analysis, for example, in showing the
uniformly local Hölder continuity of harmonic functions, or in obtaining
the lower estimate of the heat kernel, for a given Dirichlet form on a
metric space.

Since the Moser’s celebrated paper
[
36
]
, there has been
an increasing interest in the study on the Harnack inequality for local
Dirichlet forms. In 1972, Bombieri and Giusti
[
10
]
used
the geometric analysis to prove a Harnack inequality for elliptic
differential equations on minimal surfaces. In 1980, Safonov
[
37
]
obtained the elliptic Harnack inequalities for partial
differential operators in non-divergence form. After that, the elliptic
Harnack inequality was extended in various settings, see for example, by
Benedetto and Trudinger
[
15
, Theorem 3]
in 1984 for
De Giorgi classes on Euclidean spaces, by Biroli and Mosco
[
8
]
in 1995 for a certain class of local
Dirichlet forms on discontinuous media, by Strum
[
41
, Proposition 3.2]
in 1996 for time-dependent local Dirichlet forms on
compact metric spaces, and by Cabré
[
12
]
in 1997
for non-divergence elliptic operators on Riemannian manifolds with
non-negative curvature. In 2005, Barlow
[
2
, Theorem 2]

showed that the elliptic Harnack inequality is equivalent to an annulus-type
Harnack inequality for Green’s functions in the context of random walks on
graphs. In 2015, Grigor’yan, Hu and Lau
[
24
]

gave an equivalent characterization for the elliptic Harnack inequality and
the mean exit time estimate combined, for any strongly local, regular
Dirichlet form on a metric measure space, by using a more general Poincaré inequality and the generalized capacity inequality (see also an
earlier work
[
22
]
). In 2018, Barlow and Murugan

[
4
]
showed that the elliptic Harnack inequality is
stable under bounded perturbations for strongly local, regular Dirichlet
forms on a length metric space, but assuming the existence of Green
function. Recently, this result has been improved by Barlow, Chen and
Murugan in
[
3
]
, without assuming the existence of
Green functions and a length but assuming the relative ball-connectedness.

The Harnack inequality above is investigated only for local Dirichlet forms.
In recent years, the people have begun to study the elliptic Harnack
inequality for non-local operators or non-local Dirichlet forms. It can be
imagined that the classical Harnack inequality like the version (
1.1
)
no longer holds for non-local operators (see, for example
[
5
, Section 3]
and
[
18
, Theorem 2.2]
for
\alpha
-stable
processes). Instead, a weak Harnack inequality different from (
1.1
)
should take place. In this direction, the reader may refer to
[
16
, Theorem
1.2]
,
[
17
]
and
[
18
, Theorem 1.6]
for non-local integro-differential operators,

[
34
]
for the fractional non-local linearized
Monge-Ampère equation, and
[
13
]
for pure jump
type Dirichlet forms.

In this paper, we are concerned with the weak elliptic Harnack inequality
under a more general framework (We do not touch the parabolic Harnack
inequality in this paper). Our underlying space is a metric measure space,
which may be bounded or unbounded, and our Dirichlet form is mixed, which
may be local or non-local, whose jump kernel may not exist. The main results
of this paper are as follows:

•

to establish the weak elliptic Harnack inequality for local or
non-local regular Dirichlet forms (Theorem
1.8
below);

•

to study the relationship among different versions of the weak
elliptic Harnack inequality appearing in the literature (Theorem
1.9
below).

Let

\displaystyle(\mathrm{LG})\text{ \ (see Section \ref{Sect-LG}),}

(1.22)

\displaystyle(\mathrm{VD})+(\mathrm{LG})+(\mathrm{Cap}_{\leq})+(\mathrm{PI})

Liu and Murugan
[
31

show that the parabolic
Harnack inequality implies the existence of the jump kernel

for a
pure jump regular Dirichlet form. A natural question arises whether the weak
elliptic Harnack inequality also implies the existence of the jump kernel.
The answer is negative. In fact, the paper
[

has given an example on the ultra-metric space where
the jump measure satisfies both conditions
(\mathrm{PI})
and
(\mathrm{TJ})
(noting that condition

automatically holds since it
follows directly from condition

and the ultra-metric
property), but the jump kernel does not exist. By Theorem
1.8

above, the weak elliptic Harnack inequality is true, however, the jump
kernel does not exist in this case. We will give the details in Section
7
.

Let us explain the idea of proving the weak elliptic Harnack inequality in
Theorem
1.8
. The proof essentially consists of the following two
steps (under the case when
f\equiv 0
).

(1)

To obtain the so-called
measure-to-point lemma
as follows: for
some
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and for any non-negative superharmonic function

u
in a ball
B
, there exists a constant
\eta>0
, depending only on
\varepsilon
but independent of the ball
B
and the function
u
, such
that

\frac{\mu(B\cap\{u>1\})}{\mu(B)}\geq\varepsilon\text{ \ }\Rightarrow\text{ \ }\inf_{\frac{1}{2}B}u\geq\eta.

(1.24)

(2)

To obtain the so-called
crossover lemma
as follows: there exist
three universal numbers
p,\delta
in
(0,1)
and
C>0
such that, for any
non-negative superharmonic function
u
in a ball
B_{R}
, any concentric
ball
B_{r}
of
B_{R}
with
0<r\leq\delta R
and for any positive number
\lambda\geq w(B_{R})T_{\frac{3}{4}B_{R},B_{R}}(u_{-})
,

\left(\fint_{B_{r}}(u+\lambda)^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}(u+\lambda)^{-p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\leq C.

(1.25)

The implication (
1.24
) says that, if the occupation

measure
of a superlevel set

\{u\geq a\}\text{ \ for }a>0

in a ball
B
for a function
u
, which is non-negative, superharmonic in
B
, is bounded from below by a constant
\varepsilon
, then the function
u

should be also bounded from below by a positive number
\eta a
at almost
all
points
near the center.

The measure-to-point lemma is essentially the same as the
Lemma of
growth
introduced by Landis in
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
in studying solutions of elliptic second order PDEs (local
Dirichlet form) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This Lemma of growth has been
reformulated and extended to the case for pure jump type (non-local)
Dirichlet forms on the metric measure space in
[
20
, Lemma 4.1]
, see also a forthcoming paper
[
21
]
for
mixed (either local or non-local) Dirichlet forms defined by (

) without killing part (cf. Lemma
3.5
below). An alternative
version of Lemma of growth for pure jump type (non-local) Dirichlet forms on
metric space was stated in
[
13
, Proposition 3.6]
.

We remark that the measure-to-point lemma is originated from the work by
Moser
[
35
, Theorem 2]
, and developed by Krylov and
Safonov
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
,

[
37
]
. The reader may consult the reference
[

for the classical case.

Once the measure-to-point lemma has been established, one needs further to
show the crossover lemma (
1.25
), where the Poincaré inequality comes
into a stage. To achieve (

), one needs to show that, for any

that is superharmonic and

and for any positive number

from below by a tail (in the case of local Dirichlet forms, any number

will be fine), the logarithm function

belongs to the space

, for some number

that is independent of

and ball

. After that, the
rest of the proof is standard: one makes use of Lemma

in
Appendix for an exponential function

\exp\left(\frac{c}{b}g\right)\text{ \ for any }b\geq||g||_{\mathrm{BMO}}

for

, which is valid from the John-Nirenberg
inequality (see Lemma

in Appendix), and we are eventually led

) (see Lemma

below).

Besides the version of the weak elliptic Harnack inequality stated in

and term as conditions

,

,

,

.

.

Let

2.
Faber-Krahn inequality and Dirichlet heat kernel

In this section, we show that for a regular Dirichlet form
without killing part on a metric space, if the measure satisfies conditions
(\mathrm{VD})
and
(\mathrm{RVD})
, then the Poincaré inequality
implies the Faber-Krahn inequality. Although this conclusion is known to the
expert, there is no a direct proof in the literature, and we will give a
self-contained proof for convenience. Here we do not assume the existence of
the jump kernel, neither the independence of point for the scaling function
w
. Our result can be viewed as an extension of the previous work
[
24
, Theorem 5.1]
for a local Dirichlet form for
the doubling measure, and
[
6
, Lemmas 5.2, 5.3]
for
a non-local Dirichlet form for the Ahlfors-regular measure. See also
[
11
, Proposition 3.4.1]
. As a by-product, we
derive that the Dirichlet heat kernel
p_{t}^{B}(x,y)
exists and satisfies
an upper bound, for any ball
B
of radius less than
\sigma\overline{R}
.

We introduce condition
(\mathrm{Nash}_{B})
, which is the
Nash
inequality on a ball
B
.

Definition 2.1

(condition
(\mathrm{Nash}_{B})
)
.

We say that condition
(\mathrm{Nash}_{B})
holds if there exist three
positive constants
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
\nu,C
such that for any metric
ball
B
of radius
r\in(0,\sigma\overline{R})
and any
u\in\mathcal{F}(B)
,

||u||_{2}^{2+2\nu}\leq\frac{C}{\mu(B)^{\nu}}||u||_{1}^{2\nu}\left(||u||_{2}^{2}+w(B)\mathcal{E}(u,u)\right).

(2.1)

We remark that constants
C
and
\nu,\sigma
are all independent of ball
B
and function
u
.

We show that the Poincaré inequality implies the Nash inequality on a
ball.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
is a regular Dirichlet
form in
L^{2}(M,\mu)
without killing part. If conditions
(\mathrm{VD})

and
(\mathrm{PI})
are satisfied, then condition
(\mathrm{Nash}_{B})

holds, that is,

(\mathrm{VD})+(\mathrm{PI})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{Nash}_{B}).

Proof.

Since the proof is quite long, we divided into two steps.

Step

1
. We show that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for
all
s>0
and all
u\in\mathcal{F}\cap L^{1}
with
||u||_{1}>0

||u_{s}||_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{C||u||_{1}^{2}}{\underset{z\in\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}{\inf}V(z,s)},

(2.2)

where
u_{s}(x)
is the average of function
u
over a ball
B(x,s)
, that
is,

u_{s}(x)=\frac{1}{V(x,s)}\int_{B(x,s)}u(z)\mu(dz)\ \text{\ for}\ x\in M,s>0.

The proof is motivated by
[
38
, Theorem 2.4]
. At
this step, we do not need condition
(\mathrm{PI})
. To this end, let
||u||_{1}>0
, and denote by

A_{s}:=\{x\in M:d(x,\mathrm{supp\,}(u))<s\},

the
s
-neighborhood of the support of
u
. Clearly, we see that
u_{s}(x)\equiv 0
when
x
lies outside the set
A_{s}
, since
u(z)=0
for
z\in B(x,s)\subset M\setminus\mathrm{supp\,}(u)
. It follows that

||u_{s}||_{\infty}\leq\frac{||u||_{1}}{\underset{x\in A_{s}}{\inf}V(x,s)}\leq\frac{2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu}||u||_{1}}{\underset{x\in\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}{\inf}V(x,s)},

(2.3)

where we have used the fact that for any
x\in A_{s}
,

\frac{1}{\underset{x\in A_{s}}{\inf}V(x,s)}\leq\frac{2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu}}{\underset{x\in\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}{\inf}V(x,s)},

since there exists a point
z\in\mathrm{supp\,}(u)
such that
d(z,x)<s
,
and thus by (
1.3
)

\frac{V(z,s)}{V(x,s)}\leq C_{\mu}\left(\frac{d(z,x)+s}{s}\right)^{d_{2}}\leq C_{\mu}\left(\frac{s+s}{s}\right)^{d_{2}}=2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu},

(2.4)

from which,

\frac{1}{\underset{x\in A_{s}}{\inf}V(x,s)}=\underset{x\in A_{s}}{\sup}\frac{1}{V(x,s)}\leq 2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu}\underset{z\in\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}{\sup}\frac{1}{V(z,s)}=\frac{2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu}}{\underset{x\in\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}{\inf}V(x,s)}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle||u_{s}||_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{A_{s}}\frac{1}{V(x,s)}\left(\int_{B(x,s)}|u(z)|\mu(dz)\right)\mu(dx)

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{s}}\frac{1}{V(x,s)}\left(\int_{\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}|u(z)|1_{B(x,s)}(z)\mu(dz)\right)\mu(dx)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}|u(z)|\left(\int_{A_{s}}\frac{1_{B(x,s)}(z)}{V(x,s)}\mu(dx)\right)\mu(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}|u(z)|\left(\int_{A_{s}\cap B(z,s)}\frac{1}{V(x,s)}\mu(dx)\right)\mu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}|u(z)|\frac{V(z,s)}{\underset{x\in B(z,s)}{\inf}V(x,s)}\mu(dz)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}|u(z)|\underset{x\in B(z,s)}{\sup}\frac{V(z,s)}{V(x,s)}\mu(dz)\leq 2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu}||u||_{1},

(2.5)

since for any
z\in\mathrm{supp\,}(u)
and any
x\in B(z,s)
,

\frac{V(z,s)}{V(x,s)}\leq 2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu}

by virtue of (
2.4
). Therefore, it follows from (
2.3
), (
2.5
) that

||u_{s}||_{2}^{2}\leq||u_{s}||_{\infty}||u_{s}||_{1}\leq\frac{(2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu})^{2}||u||_{1}^{2}}{\underset{z\in\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}{\inf}V(z,s)},

thus showing (
2.2
) with
C:=(2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu})^{2}
.

Step

2
. We show that condition
(\mathrm{Nash}_{B})
holds. We
assume that condition
(\mathrm{PI})
holds.

Fix a ball
B:=B(x_{0},r)
with
r\in(0,\frac{\overline{R}}{\kappa})
,
where constant
\kappa
is the same as in condition
(\mathrm{PI})
. Let
s\in(0,\frac{\overline{R}}{2\kappa})
be a number to be determined later
on, and fix a function
u\in\mathcal{F}(B)\cap L^{1}(M,\mu)
. Since
M
is
separable, there is a countable family of points
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}

such that
M\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}B(y_{i},s)
. By the doubling
property, we can find a subsequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
such that
M=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}B_{i}
with
B_{i}:=B(x_{i},s)
, and
\{\frac{1}{5}B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
are pairwise
disjoint (see
[
25
, Theorem 1.16]
). The over-lapping
number
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}1_{2\kappa B_{i}}
is bounded by some integer
N_{0}
depending only on
\kappa
and
C_{\mu}
, that is,
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}1_{2\kappa B_{i}}\leq N_{0}
. From this, we have for any
measurable function
g\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\iint_{(2\kappa B_{i})\times M}g(x,y)J(dx,dy)

\displaystyle=\iint_{M\times M}g(x,y)\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}1_{2\kappa B_{i}}(x)J(dx,dy)

\displaystyle\leq N_{0}\iint_{M\times M}g(x,y)J(dx,dy).

(2.6)

We estimate the term
||u-u_{s}||_{2}^{2}
by

\displaystyle||u-u_{s}||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{i}}|u(x)-u_{s}(x)|^{2}\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\left(\int_{B_{i}}(|u(x)-u_{2B_{i}}|^{2}+|u_{2B_{i}}-u_{s}(x)|^{2})\mu(dx)\right)\eqqcolon 2(I_{1}+I_{2}).

(2.7)

For
I_{1}
, we have by condition
(\mathrm{PI})
,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{i}}|u(x)-u_{2B_{i}}|^{2}\mu(dx)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{2B_{i}}|u(x)-u_{2B_{i}}|^{2}\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}w(x_{i},2s)\left\{\int_{2\kappa B_{i}}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle+\iint_{(2\kappa B_{i})\times(2\kappa B_{i})}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(dx,dy)\right\}.

(2.8)

For
I_{2}
, note that for any
x\in B_{i}=B(x_{i},s)
, the function
1_{B(x,s)}(z)=0
when
z\in(2B_{i})^{c}\subset B(x,s)^{c}
. Using the
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and condition
(\mathrm{PI})
, we have for any
x\in B_{i}

\displaystyle|u_{s}(x)-u_{2B_{i}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{M}\frac{1_{B(x,s)}(z)}{V(x,s)}(u(z)-u_{2B_{i}})\mu(dz)\Big|^{2}\leq\int_{M}\frac{1_{B(x,s)}(z)}{V(x,s)}|u(z)-u_{2B_{i}}|^{2}\mu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2B_{i}}\frac{1}{V(x,s)}|u(z)-u_{2B_{i}}|^{2}\mu(dz)\leq\frac{2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu}}{V(x_{i},s)}\int_{2B_{i}}|u(z)-u_{2B_{i}}|^{2}\mu(dz)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cw(x_{i},2s)}{V(x_{i},s)}\left\{\int_{2\kappa B_{i}}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle+\iint_{(2\kappa B_{i})\times(2\kappa B_{i})}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(dx,dy)\right\},

(2.9)

where we have used the fact that for any
x\in B_{i}
,

\frac{V(x_{i},s)}{V(x,s)}\leq C_{\mu}\left(\frac{d(x_{i},x)+s}{s}\right)^{d_{2}}\leq 2^{d_{2}}C_{\mu}

by virtue of (
1.3
). Therefore, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{i}}(u_{2B_{i}}-u_{s}(x))^{2}\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{i}}\frac{Cw(x_{i},2s)}{V(x_{i},s)}\left\{\int_{2\kappa B_{i}}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle+\iint_{(2\kappa B_{i})\times(2\kappa B_{i})}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(dx,dy)\right\}\mu(dx)

\displaystyle=C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}w(x_{i},2s)\left\{\int_{2\kappa B_{i}}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle+\iint_{(2\kappa B_{i})\times(2\kappa B_{i})}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(dx,dy)\right\}.

(2.10)

Combining (
2.8
) and (
2.10
), we conclude from (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle||u-u_{s}||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(I_{1}+I_{2})

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}w(x_{i},2s)\left\{\int_{2\kappa B_{i}}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle+\iint_{(2\kappa B_{i})\times(2\kappa B_{i})}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(dx,dy)\right\}

(2.11)

for a positive constant
C
depending only on the constants from condition
(\mathrm{VD})
(independent of
u,s,B_{i}
).

Since
u\in\mathcal{F}(B)
, if
2\kappa B_{i}\subset B^{c}
, we see that
u(x)=u(y)=0
when
x,y\in 2\kappa B_{i}
, thus
1_{2\kappa B_{i}}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle=0
, and so the integral in the above summation
vanishes. In other words, the summation in (

) is taken only
over the indices
i
such that
(2\kappa B_{i})\cap B\neq\emptyset
. Set

Q:=\sup_{i:(2\kappa B_{i})\cap B\neq\emptyset}w(x_{i},s).

(2.12)

Since
w(x_{i},2s)\leq C_{2}2^{\beta_{2}}w(x_{i},s)
by using (
1.5
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}w(x_{i},s)\iint_{(2\kappa B_{i})\times(2\kappa B_{i})}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(dx,dy)

\displaystyle\leq Q\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\iint_{(2\kappa B_{i})\times M}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(dx,dy)

\displaystyle\leq Q\cdot N_{0}\iint_{M\times M}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(dx,dy)\text{ \ (using (\ref{eq:vol_9}))}

\displaystyle=N_{0}Q\mathcal{E}^{(J)}(u,u).

(2.13)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}w(x_{i},s)\int_{2\kappa B_{i}}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle

\displaystyle\leq Q\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{2\kappa B_{i}}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle=Q\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}1_{2\kappa B_{i}}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle

\displaystyle\leq N_{0}Q\int_{M}d\Gamma^{(L)}\langle u\rangle=N_{0}Q\mathcal{E}^{(L)}(u,u).

(2.14)

Therefore, combining (
2.13
) and (

), we conclude
from (

) that for all

\in(0,\frac{\overline{R}}{2\kappa})

(2.15)

It is left to estimate
Q
for any

.
We distinguish two cases when

or not.

Indeed, let
z_{0}\in(2\kappa B_{i})\cap B

\frac{w(x_{i},s)}{w(z_{0},s)}=\frac{w(x_{i},s)}{w(x_{i},2\kappa s)}\cdot\frac{w(x_{i},2\kappa s)}{w(z_{0},s)}\leq C_{1}^{-1}\left(\frac{s}{2\kappa s}\right)^{\beta_{1}}\cdot C_{2}\left(\frac{2\kappa s}{s}\right)^{\beta_{2}}=c^{\prime}(\kappa),

Thus,

. From this, we

(2.16)

Plugging (

||u-u_{s}||_{2}^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\beta_{1}}w(x_{0},r)\mathcal{E}(u)

if

||u_{s}||_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{C||u||_{1}^{2}}{\underset{x\in\mathrm{supp\,}(u)}{\inf}V(x,s)}\leq C\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{d_{2}}\frac{||u||_{1}^{2}}{V(x_{0},r)}.

(2.18)

Therefore, we conclude from (

), (

) that for

<r\wedge\frac{\overline{R}}{2\kappa}

\displaystyle||u||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(||u-u_{s}||_{2}^{2}+||u_{s}||_{2}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\beta_{1}}w(x_{0},r)\mathcal{E}(u)+\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{d_{2}}\frac{||u||_{1}^{2}}{V(x_{0},r)}\right).

On the other hand, if

||u||_{2}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\beta_{1}}||u||_{2}^{2}.

(2.20)

Summing up (

\displaystyle||u||_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left(\frac{s}{r}\right)^{\beta_{1}}\left(w(x_{0},r)\mathcal{E}(u)+||u||_{2}^{2}\right)+\left(\frac{r}{s}\right)^{d_{2}}\frac{||u||_{1}^{2}}{V(x_{0},r)}\right)

(2.21)

We minimize the right-hand side of (

\in(0,\frac{\overline{R}}{2\kappa})

\in(0,\frac{\overline{R}}{2\kappa})
.

thus showing that

for all

. Hence, condition
(\mathrm{Nash}_{B})
holds with
\sigma=\frac{1}{\kappa}
and

from which, we see that, using the fact that

The proof is complete.

We derive the on-diagonal upper bound of the Dirichlet heat kernel on any

Lemma 2.3

Let

be a regular Dirichlet form in

,

3.
A refinement of lemma of growth

In this section we shall derive the lemma of growth for any
two concentric balls
B,\delta B
with

, which is a refinement
of the version stated in a forthcoming paper
[
21
]
, see also
[
20
, Lemma 4.1]
. The lemma of growth will follow from
conditions
(\mathrm{VD})
,
(\mathrm{Gcap})
,
(\mathrm{FK})
,
(\mathrm{TJ})
. The basic tool in the proof is to use the celebrated De-Giorgi iteration
technique for occupation measures (instead of for
L^{2}
-norms). Although
the idea is essentially the same as in
[
21
]
,
[
20
, Proof of Lemma
4.1]
, we sketch the proof for the reader’s
convenience.

Before we address the lemma of growth, we give the following preliminary.
For each
n\geq 1
, let
F_{n}
be a function on
[0,\infty)
given by

F_{n}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\left(r+\sqrt{r^{2}+\frac{1}{n^{2}}}\right)-\frac{1}{2n}\text{ \ for }r\in(-\infty,\infty).

(3.1)

Clearly,
F_{n}(0)=0
, and for any
r\in(-\infty,\infty)
,

\displaystyle 0\leq F_{n}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\frac{r}{\sqrt{r^{2}+n^{-2}}}\right)\leq 1\text{,\ \ }

\displaystyle 0\leq F_{n}^{\prime\prime}(r)=\frac{1}{2n^{2}(r^{2}+n^{-2})^{3/2}}\leq\frac{n}{2},

\displaystyle F_{n}(r)\rightrightarrows r_{+}\ \ \text{uniformly in }(-\infty,\infty)\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty.

(3.2)

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
be a regular Dirichlet form in
L^{2}(M,\mu)
without killing part and let
F_{n}
be given by (
3.1
).
Then for any
u\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cap L^{\infty}
and any
0\leq\varphi\in\mathcal{F}\cap L^{\infty}
,

\mathcal{E}(u_{+},\varphi)\leq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}(u,F_{n_{k}}^{\prime}(u)\varphi)

(3.3)

for a subsequence
\{n_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of
\{n\}_{n\geq 1}
.

Proof.

Note that the functions
F_{n}(u)
,
F_{n}^{\prime}(u)\varphi
belong to
\mathcal{F}\cap L^{\infty}
for each
n\geq 1
by using Proposition
8.4
in Appendix. Since
\varphi\geq 0
in
M
, we have

\mathcal{E}(F_{n}(u),\varphi)\leq\mathcal{E}(u,F_{n}^{\prime}(u)\varphi)\text{ \ (}n\geq 1\text{)}

(3.4)

by using (
8.2
) in Appendix.

Write
u=v+a
for some
v\in\mathcal{F}
and
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
F_{n}(v+a)-F_{n}(a)
is a normal contraction of
v\in\mathcal{F}
, we have

f_{n}:=F_{n}(u)-F_{n}(a)=F_{n}(v+a)-F_{n}(a)\in\mathcal{F}\text{ \ and \ }\mathcal{E}(f_{n},f_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}(v,v).

Since
(v+a)_{+}-a_{+}
is also a normal contraction of
v\in\mathcal{F}
,
we also have

f:=u_{+}-a_{+}=(v+a)_{+}-a_{+}\in\mathcal{F}\text{.}

On the other hand, by the dominated convergence theorem,

f_{n}\overset{L^{2}}{\longrightarrow}f\ \ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty.

Since
f_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
and

\sup_{n}\mathcal{E}(f_{n},f_{n})\leq\mathcal{E}(v,v)<\infty,

there exists a subsequence
\left\{f_{n_{k}}\right\}_{k\geq 1}
converging
to
f
weakly in terms of the energy norm
\mathcal{E}
by using Lemma
8.5
in Appendix. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(u_{+},\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(f+a_{+},\varphi)=\mathcal{E}(f,\varphi)=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}(f_{n_{k}},\varphi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}(F_{n_{k}}(u)-F_{n_{k}}(a),\varphi)=\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}(F_{n_{k}}(u),\varphi)\leq\limsup_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}(u,F_{n_{k}}^{\prime}(u)\varphi)

by virtue of (
3.4
), thus showing (
3.3
). The proof is complete.
∎

6.
Consequences of weak Harnack inequality

In this section, we look at two consequences of the weak Harnack inequality.
One is that we obtain the Hölder continuity of any harmonic function if
conditions
(\mathrm{wEH})
and
(\mathrm{TJ})
hold for any regular
Dirichlet form without killing part, see Lemma
6.2
below. The Hölder continuity of harmonic functions was investigated in various settings,
see for example
[
40
, Theorem 5.3]
for a certain
class of integro-differential equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see also
[
18
, Theorem 1.7]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
under a weaker assumption), and
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
for a pure-jump Dirichlet form. Here we have
extended this conclusion to a more general situation where the jump kernel
does not necessarily exist. Although the proof is standard, we sketch the
proof for completeness of this paper.

The other consequence of the weak Harnack inequality is that we can obtain a
Lemma of growth for any globally non-negative, superharmonic function (Lemma
6.4
below), which leads to a lower bound of the mean exit time on a
ball (Lemma
6.3
below). The lower bound of the mean exit time plays an
important role in obtaining the heat kernel estimate.

Recall that for an open subset
\Omega
of
M
, a function
u\in\mathcal{F}
is
harmonic
in
\Omega
if for any non-negative
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(\Omega)
,

\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)=0.

For any ball
B\subseteq M
and any function
u\in L^{\infty}(B,\mu)
, we
define

\eosc_{B}u:=\esup_{B}u-\einf_{B}u.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
be a regular Dirichlet form
in
L^{2}(M,\mu)
without killing part. If conditions
(\mathrm{wEH})
and
(\mathrm{TJ})
hold, then there exist two constants
\beta\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that, for any
x_{0}\in M
,
0<r<\sigma\overline{R}
and any
harmonic function
u
in
B(x_{0},r)
,

\eosc_{B(x_{0},\rho)}u\leq C||u||_{L^{\infty}}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\beta},\ \ 0<\rho\leq r.

(6.1)

We remark that constants
C,\beta
are independent of
\overline{R},u,x_{0},r,\rho
.

Proof.

Fix a ball
B\left(x_{0},r\right)
for
0<r<\sigma\overline{R}
. Set

B_{\rho}:=B\left(x_{0},\rho\right)\text{ \ for any }\rho>0.

Let
u
be a harmonic function in
B_{r}
. Without loss of generality, we
assume that
||u||_{L^{\infty}(M)}<\infty
. Let

M_{0}:=||u||_{L^{\infty}}\text{, \ \ }m_{0}:=\einf_{M}u\text{, \ }K:=M_{0}-m_{0}

so that
0\leq K\leq 2\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

We will construct two sequences
\{m_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
,
\{M_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}

of positive numbers such that for each
n
,

\displaystyle m_{n-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle m_{n}\leq M_{n}\leq M_{n-1}\text{ \ \ and \ \ }M_{n}-m_{n}=K\theta^{-n\beta},

\displaystyle m_{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle u(x)\leq M_{n}\quad\text{ for any }x\in B_{r\theta^{-n}},

(6.2)

where
\theta,\beta
are two constants to be determined so that

\theta\geq\delta^{-1}\text{, \ \ }\beta\in(0,1)\text{, \ \ and \ }\frac{2-\lambda}{2}\theta^{\beta}\leq 1,

(6.3)

where
\lambda:=(2^{1+1/p}C_{H})^{-1}\in(0,1)
and
p,\delta\in(0,1)

and
C_{H}\geq 1
come from condition
(\mathrm{wEH})
. Once this is true,
then we are done by noting that (
6.1
) follows, since for any
0<\rho<r
, there is some integer
j\geq 0
such that

\theta^{-j-1}\leq\frac{\rho}{r}<\theta^{-j},

from which, we see by (
6.2
) that

\eosc_{B_{\rho}}u\leq\eosc_{B_{r\theta^{-j}}}u\leq M_{j}-m_{j}=K\theta^{-j\beta}\leq 2\theta^{\beta}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\frac{\rho}{r}\right)^{\beta}.

We will show (
6.2
) inductively. Indeed, assume that there exists an
integer
k\geq 1
such that (
6.2
) holds for any
n\leq k-1
. We need
to construct
m_{k},M_{k}
such that (
6.2
) still holds for
n=k
and
for
\theta,\beta
satisfying (
6.3
).

To do this, set for any
x\in M

v(x)=\left(u(x)-\frac{M_{k-1}+m_{k-1}}{2}\right)\frac{2\theta^{(k-1)\beta}}{K}.

(6.4)

Clearly, we have by (
6.2
) for
n=k-1
that

|v(x)|\leq\frac{M_{k-1}-m_{k-1}}{2}\frac{2\theta^{(k-1)\beta}}{K}=\frac{K\theta^{-(k-1)\beta}}{2}\frac{2\theta^{(k-1)\beta}}{K}=1

(6.5)

for almost all
x\in B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}
.

Note that for any point
y\in B(x_{0},r\theta^{-(k-1)})^{c}
, there is some
integer
j\geq 1
such that

r\theta^{-k+j}\leq d\left(y,x_{0}\right)<r\theta^{-k+j+1}.

For simplicity, set
M_{-n}=M_{0}
and
m_{-n}=m_{0}
for any
n\geq 1
. By (
6.2
), for any
y\in B(x_{0},r\theta^{-(k-j-1)})\setminus B(x_{0},r\theta^{-(k-j)})
(
j\geq 1
),

\displaystyle\frac{K}{2\theta^{(k-1)\beta}}v(y)

\displaystyle=u(y)-\frac{M_{k-1}+m_{k-1}}{2}\leq M_{k-j-1}-\frac{M_{k-1}+m_{k-1}}{2}

\displaystyle=M_{k-j-1}-m_{k-j-1}+m_{k-j-1}-\frac{M_{k-1}+m_{k-1}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq M_{k-j-1}-m_{k-j-1}-\frac{M_{k-1}-m_{k-1}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq K\theta^{-(k-j-1)\beta}-\frac{K}{2}\theta^{-(k-1)\beta},

from which, it follows that

v(y)\leq 2\theta^{j\beta}-1\leq 2\left(\frac{d\left(y,x_{0}\right)}{r\theta^{-k}}\right)^{\beta}-1\text{ \ for any }y\in B(x_{0},r\theta^{-(k-1)})^{c}.

(6.6)

On the other hand, we similarly have that, for any
y\in B(x_{0},r\theta^{-(k-j-1)})\setminus B(x_{0},r\theta^{-(k-j)})
(
j\geq 1
),

\displaystyle\frac{K}{2\theta^{(k-1)\beta}}v(y)

\displaystyle=u(y)-\frac{M_{k-1}+m_{k-1}}{2}\geq m_{k-j-1}-\frac{M_{k-1}+m_{k-1}}{2}

\displaystyle=m_{k-j-1}-M_{k-j-1}+M_{k-j-1}-\frac{M_{k-1}+m_{k-1}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\left(M_{k-j-1}-m_{k-j-1}\right)+\frac{M_{k-1}-m_{k-1}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-K\theta^{-(k-j-1)\beta}+\frac{K}{2}\theta^{-(k-1)\beta},

which gives that

v(y)\geq 1-2\theta^{j\beta}\geq 1-2\left(\frac{d\left(y,x_{0}\right)}{r\theta^{-k}}\right)^{\beta}\text{ \ for any }y\in B(x_{0},r\theta^{-(k-1)})^{c}.

(6.7)

We distinguish two cases: either

\mu(B_{r\theta^{-k}}\cap\left\{v\leq 0\right\})\geq\mu(B_{r\theta^{-k}})/2,

(6.8)

or

\mu(B_{r\theta^{-k}}\cap\left\{v>0\right\})\geq\mu(B_{r\theta^{-k}})/2.

(6.9)

If (
6.8
) holds, we will show that for almost every
z\in B_{r\theta^{-k}}

v(z)\leq 1-\lambda.

(6.10)

Temporally assume that (
6.10
) holds true. Then by (
6.4
), we see
that for any point
z\in B_{r\theta^{-k}}
,

\displaystyle u(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{K}{2\theta^{(k-1)\beta}}v(z)+\frac{M_{k-1}+m_{k-1}}{2}\leq\frac{K(1-\lambda)}{2\theta^{(k-1)\beta}}+\frac{M_{k-1}+m_{k-1}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{K(1-\lambda)}{2}\theta^{-(k-1)\beta}+\frac{M_{k-1}-m_{k-1}}{2}+m_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{K(1-\lambda)}{2}\theta^{-(k-1)\beta}+\frac{K}{2}\theta^{-(k-1)\beta}+m_{k-1}\text{ \ (using (\ref{601}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2-\lambda)\theta^{\beta}}{2}K\theta^{-k\beta}+m_{k-1}\leq K\theta^{-k\beta}+m_{k-1},

where in the last inequality we have used the fact that
\frac{2-\lambda}{2}\theta^{\beta}\leq 1
in (
6.3
). Therefore, setting

m_{k}=m_{k-1}\text{ \ \ and \ \ }M_{k}=m_{k}+K\theta^{-k\beta}\leq M_{k-1},

we obtain that
m_{k}\leq u(z)\leq M_{k}
for a.e
z\in B_{r\theta^{-k}}
,
thus showing that (
6.2
) holds when
n=k
, which finishes the induction
step from
n\leq k-1
to
n=k
in the case when (
6.8
) holds.

We turn to show (
6.10
). Indeed, consider
h:=1-v
. Clearly, the
function
h
is harmonic in
B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}
and also is non-negative
in
B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}
by using (
6.5
). Applying
(\mathrm{wEH})
to
the function
h
in
B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}
and

, we find that

\left(\fint_{B_{r\theta^{-k}}}h^{p}du\right)^{1/p}\leq C_{H}\left(\einf_{B_{r\theta^{-k}}}h+w\left(x_{0},r\theta^{-k}\right)T_{\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}},B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}(h_{-})\right),

(6.11)

where we have used the fact that
\theta^{-1}\leq\delta
so that
r\theta^{-k}\leq\delta\cdot r\theta^{-(k-1)}
. Note that by (
6.8
),

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B_{r\theta^{-k}}}h^{p}du\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\mu\left(B_{r\theta^{-k}}\right)}\int_{B_{r\theta^{-k}}\cap\left\{v\leq 0\right\}}(1-v)^{p}du\right)^{1/p}

(6.12)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mu(B_{r\theta^{-k}}\cap\left\{v\leq 0\right\})}{\mu\left(B_{r\theta^{-k}}\right)}\right)^{1/p}\geq 2^{-1/p}.

Also note that by (

h_{-}(y)=(1-v(y))_{-}=(v(y)-1)_{+}\leq 2\left[\left(\frac{d\left(y,x_{0}\right)}{r\theta^{-k}}\right)^{\beta}-1\right]

for any

.
From this, we have by condition

\displaystyle T_{\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}},B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}(h_{-})

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}\int_{B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}^{c}}h_{-}(y)J(x,dy)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{x\in\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}\int_{B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}^{c}}\left[\left(\frac{d\left(y,x_{0}\right)}{r\theta^{-k}}\right)^{\beta}-1\right]J(x,dy)

\displaystyle=2\sup_{x\in\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{r\theta^{-k+j+1}}\backslash B_{r\theta^{-k+j}}}\left[\left(\frac{d\left(y,x_{0}\right)}{r\theta^{-k}}\right)^{\beta}-1\right]J(x,dy)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sup_{x\in\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}\int_{B_{r\theta^{-k+j+1}}\backslash B_{r\theta^{-k+j}}}\left(\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1\right)J(x,dy)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1\right)\sup_{x\in\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}\int_{B_{r\theta^{-k+j}}^{c}}J(x,dy)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1\right)\sup_{x\in\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}\int_{B(x,r\theta^{-k+j}/4)^{c}}J(x,dy)

\displaystyle\leq 2C\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1\right)\sup_{x\in\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}\frac{1}{w(x,r\theta^{-k+j}/4)},

(6.13)

where

is the same constant as in (

). Since

\frac{w(x,r\theta^{-k+j}/4)}{w(x_{0},r\theta^{-k})}\geq\frac{w(x,r\theta^{-k+j})}{C_{2}4^{\beta_{2}}w(x_{0},r\theta^{-k})}\geq\frac{C_{1}\theta^{j\beta_{1}}}{C_{2}4^{\beta_{2}}}

for any

),
it follows from (

w\left(x_{0},r\theta^{-k}\right)T_{\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}},B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}(h_{-})\leq\frac{2CC_{2}4^{\beta_{2}}}{C_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1}{\theta^{j\beta_{1}}}.

(6.14)

Therefore, substituting (

), (
6.14
) into (
6.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\einf_{B_{r\theta^{-k}}}h

\displaystyle\geq\left(C_{H}2^{1/p}\right)^{-1}-w\left(x_{0},r\theta^{-k}\right)T_{\frac{3}{4}B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}},B_{r\theta^{-(k-1)}}}(h_{-})

\displaystyle\geq\left(C_{H}2^{1/p}\right)^{-1}-\frac{2CC_{2}4^{\beta_{2}}}{C_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1}{\theta^{j\beta_{1}}}.

(6.15)

Since
\theta^{-1}\leq\delta
, we see that for any
\beta\in\left(0,\beta_{1}/2\right)

\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}\theta^{-j\beta_{1}}\left(\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1\right)\leq\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}\theta^{-j\beta_{1}}\theta^{(j+1)\beta_{1}/2}=\frac{\theta^{-l\beta_{1}/2}}{1-\theta^{-\beta_{1}/2}}\leq\frac{\delta^{l\beta_{1}/2}}{1-\delta^{\beta_{1}/2}}\leq\left(\frac{8CC_{2}4^{\beta_{2}}}{C_{1}}C_{H}2^{1/p}\right)^{-1},

provided that the number
l
is sufficiently large, which depends only on
\delta
but is independent of
\beta,\theta
. For such a number
l
, we
now choose
\beta\in\left(0,\beta_{1}/2\right)
to be so small that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{l}\theta^{-j\beta_{1}}\left(\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta^{-\beta_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\left(\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1\right)\leq l\theta^{-\beta_{1}}\left(\theta^{(l+1)\beta}-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle l\delta^{\beta_{1}}\left(\theta^{(l+1)\beta}-1\right)\leq\left(\frac{8CC_{2}4^{\beta_{2}}}{C_{1}}C_{H}2^{1/p}\right)^{-1}.

It follows that

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1}{\theta^{j\beta_{1}}}=\sum_{j=1}^{l}\theta^{-j\beta_{1}}\left(\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1\right)+\sum_{j=l+1}^{\infty}\theta^{-j\beta_{1}}\left(\theta^{(j+1)\beta}-1\right)\leq 2\left(\frac{8CC_{2}4^{\beta_{2}}}{C_{1}}C_{H}2^{1/p}\right)^{-1},

from which, we see by (
6.15
) that

\einf_{B_{r\theta^{-k}}}h\geq\left(C_{H}2^{1/p}\right)^{-1}-\frac{2CC_{2}4^{\beta_{2}}}{C_{1}}\cdot 2\left(\frac{8CC_{2}4^{\beta_{2}}}{C_{1}}C_{H}2^{1/p}\right)^{-1}=\left(2C_{H}2^{1/p}\right)^{-1}=\lambda.

Therefore,
v\leq 1-\lambda
in
B_{r\theta^{-k}}
, thus showing (
6.10
) when (
6.8
) is satisfied, as desired.

It remains to consider the case when (
6.9
) is satisfied. We need to
show

v\geq-1+\lambda\text{ \ in }B_{r\theta^{-k}}\text{.}

(6.16)

Indeed, consider the function
h=1+v
. Similar to the argument above,
setting
M_{k}=M_{k-1}
and
m_{k}=M_{k}-K\theta^{-k\beta}
, one can
obtain (
6.16
). The proof is complete.
∎

Recall that the operator
\mathcal{L}^{\Omega}

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
be a regular Dirichlet form in
L^{2}
. Then

(\mathrm{VD})+(\mathrm{Cap}_{\leq})+(\mathrm{FK})+(\mathrm{wEH})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{E}_{\geq})+(\mathrm{E}_{\leq}).

(6.22)

Proof.

Note that by
[
24
, Theorem 9.4 p.1542]

(\mathrm{FK})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{E}_{\leq}),

(6.23)

(observing that we only use condition
(\mathrm{FK})
at this stage).

It remains to show the implication

(\mathrm{VD})+(\mathrm{Cap}_{\leq})+(\mathrm{FK})+(\mathrm{wEH})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{E}_{\geq}).

(6.24)

Let
\delta
be the same constant as in condition
(\mathrm{wEH})
. Without
loss of generality, assume that
\delta<\frac{2}{3}
. Let
B:=B(x_{0},R)

be a ball in
M
with
R<\sigma\overline{R}
. Let
u
be the unique weak
solution such that

\mathcal{E}(u,\varphi)=(1_{\delta B},\varphi)\text{ \ for any }0\leq\varphi\in\mathcal{F}(B).

(6.25)

It is known that
u\in\mathcal{F}(B)
,
u\geq 0
in
M
, and
u
is
superharmonic in
B

[

, Lemma 5.1]
. Applying (
1.16
) in condition

on the function

, and with
f=0
,
r=\delta R
,
and noting that
u_{-}=0

\left(\fint_{\delta B}u^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\leq C_{H}\einf_{\delta B}u.

(6.26)

On the other hand, we have by condition
(\mathrm{Cap}_{\leq})

\mathcal{E}(\phi,\phi)\leq C\frac{\mu(B)}{w(B)}

(6.27)

for some

Taking
\varphi=\phi
in (

we see that

\mathcal{E}(u,\phi)=(1_{\delta B},\phi)=\int_{\delta B}\phi d\mu=\mu(\delta B)\geq C_{\mu}^{-1}\delta^{d_{2}}\mu(B).

(6.28)

Taking
\varphi=u
in (

\mathcal{E}(u,\phi)\leq\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(u,u)\mathcal{E}(\phi,\phi)}=\sqrt{(1_{\delta B},u)}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(\phi,\phi)}\leq C\sqrt{\int_{\delta B}ud\mu}\sqrt{\frac{\mu(B)}{w(B)}}.

(6.29)

Therefore, combining (

6.29

\int_{\delta B}ud\mu\geq C\mu(B)w(B).

Since by (

||u||_{L^{\infty}}\leq||E^{B}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq Cw(B),

we conclude by (

\displaystyle\int_{\delta B}ud\mu

\displaystyle=\int_{\delta B}u^{p}\cdot u^{1-p}d\mu\leq\left(Cw(B)\right)^{1-p}\int_{\delta B}u^{p}d\mu=\left(Cw(B)\right)^{1-p}\mu(\delta B)\fint_{\delta B}u^{p}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}w(B)^{1-p}\mu(B)(\einf_{\delta B}u)^{p}\leq C^{\prime\prime}w(B)^{1-p}\mu(B)(\einf_{\delta B}E^{B})^{p},

thus showing (

Finally, we show that the weak elliptic Harnack inequality also implies a
Lemma of growth, termed
condition

(\mathrm{LG}_{0})
, for any
global non-negative superharmonic
function.

We say that
condition

(\mathrm{LG}_{0})
holds if there exist four
constants
\sigma,\epsilon_{0},\eta,\delta\in(0,1)
such that, for any
ball
B:=B(x_{0},R)
with radius
R\in(0,\sigma\overline{R})
and for any

that is superharmonic in

and

, if

\frac{\mu(\delta B\cap\{u<a\})}{\mu(\delta B)}\leq\epsilon_{0}

(6.31)

for some

, then

\einf_{\delta B}u\geq\eta a.

(6.32)

We remark that the superharmonic function
u
in condition
(\mathrm{LG}_{0})
is required to be non-negative
globally
in
M
, instead of
being non-negative
locally
in condition

(\mathrm{LG})
given
in Definition
3.2
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F})
be a regular Dirichlet form in
L^{2}
. Then

(\mathrm{wEH})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{LG}_{0}).

(6.33)

Proof.

Let
u\in\mathcal{F}^{\prime}\cap L^{\infty}
be superharmonic in
B
and
non-negative globally in
M
. Assume that (
6.31
) holds, namely,

\frac{\mu(\delta B\cap\{u\geq a\})}{\mu(\delta B)}=1-\frac{\mu(\delta B\cap\{u<a\})}{\mu(\delta B)}\geq 1-\epsilon_{0}.

Since
u_{-}=0
in
M
, we see that

T_{\frac{3}{4}B_{R},B_{R}}(u_{-})\equiv 0.

Applying (
1.16
) with
r=\delta R
and
f\equiv 0
, it follows
that

\einf_{\delta B}u\geq C_{H}^{-1}\left(\fint_{\delta B}u^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\geq C_{H}^{-1}a\left(\frac{\mu(\delta B\cap\{u\geq a\})}{\mu(\delta B)}\right)^{1/p}\geq C_{H}^{-1}(1-\epsilon_{0})^{1/p}a,

thus showing that (
6.32
) is true with

∎

Lemma
6.3
above gives a direct, simpler proof of obtaining a lower
bound of the mean exit time from the weak elliptic Harnack inequality. We
remark that this conclusion can also be obtained in a more indirect way,
without recourse to condition
(\mathrm{FK})
. Indeed, the implication

(\mathrm{VD})+(\mathrm{Cap}_{\leq})+(\mathrm{LG}_{0})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{E}_{\geq})

has been proved in a forthcoming paper
[
21
]
for any regular
Dirichlet form in
L^{2}
. Combining this with (
6.33
), we have

(\mathrm{VD})+(\mathrm{Cap}_{\leq})+(\mathrm{wEH})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{VD})+(\mathrm{Cap}_{\leq})+(\mathrm{LG}_{0})\Rightarrow(\mathrm{E}_{\geq}),

from which, we also obtain condition
(\mathrm{E}_{\geq})
from the weak
elliptic Harnack inequality but without using
(\mathrm{FK})
. We do need
condition
(\mathrm{FK})
in Lemma
6.3
, not only in deriving condition

(\mathrm{E}_{\leq})
but also in deriving condition
(\mathrm{E}_{\geq})
.

7.
An example

In this section we give an example to illustrate Theorem
1.8
. We show that the assumptions
(\mathrm{VD})
,
(\mathrm{RVD})
,
(\mathrm{Gcap})
,
(\mathrm{TJ})
,
(\mathrm{PI})
are all satisfied so that
the weak Harnack inequality holds, but the jump kernel does not exist. This
example is essentially taken from
[
6
, Section 15]
,
see also
[
21
]
.

Example 7.1

(Ultra-metric space)
.

Let
\beta,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be three positive
numbers. Let
\left(M_{i},d_{i},\mu_{i}\right)
for
i=1,2
be two
ultrametric spaces, where
d_{i}
is an ultra-metric:

d_{i}(x,y)\leq\max\{d_{i}(x,z),d_{i}(z,y)\}\text{ \ for all }x,y,z\in M_{i},

and the measure
\mu_{i}
is Ahlfors-regular:

C^{-1}r^{\alpha_{i}}\leq\mu_{i}\left(B\left(x_{i},r\right)\right)\leq Cr^{\alpha_{i}}\text{ for all }x_{i}\in M_{i}\text{ and all }r>0

(7.1)

for some constant
C\geq 1
. Let
J_{i}
be a function on
M_{i}\times M_{i}

for
i=1,2
such that for
\mu_{i}
-almost all
x_{i},y_{i}\in M_{i}
,

J_{i}\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)=d_{i}\left(x_{i},y_{i}\right)^{-\left(\alpha_{i}+\beta\right)}.

(7.2)

Consider the product space
M:=M_{1}\times M_{2}
equipped with
product measure
\mu:=\mu_{1}\times\mu_{2}
and the metric

d(x,y):=\max\left\{d_{1}\left(x_{1},y_{1}\right),d_{2}\left(x_{2},y_{2}\right)\right\}\text{ for }x=\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right),y=\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)\text{ in }M.

Clearly,
(M,d,\mu)
is an ultrametric space and for any point
x=\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)
in
M
, the metric ball
B(x,r)
in
M
can be written
as

B(x,r)=B(x_{1},r)\times B(x_{2},r)\text{ for any }r>0.

(7.3)

From this, we see that for any point
x=\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right)
in
M

and any
r>0
,

V(x,r)=\mu(B(x,r))=\mu_{1}\left(B\left(x_{1},r\right)\right)\mu_{2}\left(B\left(x_{2},r\right)\right)\asymp r^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}=r^{\alpha},

(7.4)

where
\alpha:=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
. For simplicity, let the scaling
function
w(x,r)
be defined by

w(x,r)=a(x)r^{\beta}\ \ \text{for any point}\ x\in M\ \text{and any }\ r>0,

where
a(x)
is a measurable function on
M
with
C^{-1}\leq a(x)\leq C

for all
x\in M
(
C\geq 1
). Clearly, such a function
w
satisfies (
1.5
) and

C^{-1}r^{\beta}\leq w(x,r)\leq Cr^{\beta}.

(7.5)

Define the measure
J
on
\mathcal{B}(M\times M)
by
J(dx,dy)=J(x,dy)\mu(dx)
, where
J(x,dy)
is a transition function on
M\times\mathcal{B}(M)
given by

J(x,dy)=J_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})\mu_{1}(dy_{1})\delta_{x_{2}}(dy_{2})+J_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})\mu_{2}(dy_{2})\delta_{x_{1}}(dy_{1})

(7.6)

for any points
x=\left(x_{1},x_{2}\right),y=\left(y_{1},y_{2}\right)

in
M
, where
\delta_{b}(dx)
is the Dirac measure concentrated at point
b
. By (
7.4
) and (
7.6
), we have for any
r>0
and any point
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in M
,

\displaystyle\int_{B(x,r)^{c}}J(x,dy)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x,r)^{c}}\left(J_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})\mu_{1}(dy_{1})\delta_{x_{2}}(dy_{2})+J_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})\mu_{2}(dy_{2})\delta_{x_{1}}(dy_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(x_{1},r)^{c}}J_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})\mu_{1}(dy_{1})+\int_{B(x_{2},r)^{c}}J_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})\mu_{2}(dy_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r^{\beta}}+\frac{C}{r^{\beta}}=\frac{2C}{r^{\beta}}\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{w(x,r)}\text{ \ \ \ \ (using (\ref{e1}), (\ref{e2}) and (\ref{ew}))},

(7.7)

which is exactly condition
(\mathrm{TJ})
.

Let
\left(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\right)
be a Dirichlet form in
L^{2}\left(M,\mu\right)
defined by

\mathcal{E}(u,v)=\iint_{M\times M}\left(u\left(x\right)-u\left(y\right)\right)\left(v\left(x\right)-v\left(y\right)\right)J\left(x,dy\right)\mu(dx),\quad u,v\in\mathcal{F},

where the space
\mathcal{F}
is the closure of the set

\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}\mathbf{1}_{B_{i}}:n\in\mathbb{N},c_{i}\in\mathbb{R},B_{i}\text{ is a compact ball }\right\}

under the inner product

\sqrt{\mathcal{E}(\cdot,\cdot)+(\cdot,\cdot)_{L^{2}\left(M,\mu\right)}}.

By
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
, the form
\left(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{F}\right)
is regular and non-local. By (
7.4
),
the measure
\mu
satisfies conditions
(\mathrm{VD})
and
(\mathrm{RVD})
, whilst condition
(\mathrm{Gcap})
automatically holds since it follows
directly from condition
(\mathrm{TJ})
and the ultrametric property. Hence,
conditions
(\mathrm{VD})
,
(\mathrm{RVD})
,
(\mathrm{Gcap})
,
(\mathrm{TJ})
in Theorem
1.8
are satisfied.

It remains to verify condition
(\mathrm{PI})
. Indeed, let
B:=B(x^{\prime},r)
be a metric ball in
M
. Writing up
x^{\prime}=(x_{0},y_{0})
with
x_{0}\in M_{1}
,
y_{0}\in M_{2}
, we see
B=B(x_{0},r)\times B(y_{0},r)
by using (
7.3
). By (
7.6
), (
7.2
)

\displaystyle\int_{B}\int_{B}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(x,dy)\mu(dx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B}\left\{\int_{B(x_{0},r)}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{d_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})^{\alpha_{1}+\beta}}\mu_{1}(dy_{1})\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\int_{B(y_{0},r)}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(x_{1},y_{2}))^{2}}{d_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})^{\alpha_{2}+\beta}}\mu_{2}(dy_{2})\right\}\mu(dx).

The first integral on the right-hand side is estimated as follows: for any
(x_{1},x_{2})\in B(x_{0},r)\times B(y_{0},r)
,

\displaystyle\int_{B(x_{0},r)}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{d_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})^{\alpha_{1}+\beta}}\mu_{1}(dy_{1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{B(x_{0},r)}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{r^{\alpha_{1}+\beta}}\mu_{1}(dy_{1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{-1}\int_{B(y_{0},r)}\int_{B(x_{0},r)}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{r^{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\beta}}\mu_{1}(dy_{1})\mu_{2}(dy_{2})\ \ \text{(using (\ref{e1}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{-1}\int_{B}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{r^{\alpha+\beta}}\mu(dy)

by using the fact that
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}=\alpha
, from which, we have

\int_{B}\int_{B(x_{0},r)}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{d_{1}(x_{1},y_{1})^{\alpha_{1}+\beta}}\mu_{1}(dy_{1})\mu(dx)\geq C^{-1}\int_{B}\int_{B}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{r^{\alpha+\beta}}\mu(dy)\mu(dx).

Similarly, the second integral is estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{B}\int_{B(y_{0},r)}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(x_{1},y_{2}))^{2}}{d_{2}(x_{2},y_{2})^{\alpha_{2}+\beta}}\mu_{2}(dy_{2})\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{-1}\int_{B}\int_{B}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(x_{1},y_{2}))^{2}}{r^{\alpha+\beta}}\mu(dy)\mu(dx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C^{-1}\int_{B}\int_{B}\frac{(u(y_{1},y_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{r^{\alpha+\beta}}\mu(dx)\mu(dy)\text{ \ (swapping }(x_{1},x_{2})\text{
with }(y_{1},y_{2})\text{).}

Therefore, we conclude from above that, using the elementary inequality
a^{2}+b^{2}\geq(a+b)^{2}/2
,

\displaystyle\int_{B}\int_{B}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}J(x,dy)\mu(dx)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{-1}\left\{\int_{B}\int_{B}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{r^{\alpha+\beta}}\mu(dy)\mu(dx)\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\int_{B}\int_{B}\frac{(u(y_{1},y_{2})-u(y_{1},x_{2}))^{2}}{r^{\alpha+\beta}}\mu(dx)\mu(dy)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{-1}\int_{B}\int_{B}\frac{(u(x_{1},x_{2})-u(y_{1},y_{2}))^{2}}{2r^{\alpha+\beta}}\mu(dx)\mu(dy)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C^{\prime}r^{-\beta}}{\mu(B)}\int_{B}\int_{B}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}\mu(dx)\mu(dy)\ \ \text{(using (\ref{e3}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C^{\prime}r^{-\beta}\int_{B}(u-{u_{B}})^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{w(B)}\int_{B}(u-{u_{B}})^{2}d\mu\ \ \text{(using (\ref{ew})),}

thus showing that condition
\mathrm{(PI)}
with
\kappa

=1
is
satisfied.

Therefore, all the hypotheses in Theorem
1.8
are satisfied,
and the weak elliptic Harnack inequality follows. We mention that the jump
kernel does not exist by (
7.6
) in this case.

Lemma 8.5
.

Let

\mathcal{E}(f)\leq\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{E}(f_{n}).

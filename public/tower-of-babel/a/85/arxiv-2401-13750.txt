1.
Introduction

Let
k,\ell
be integers
\geq 1.
In this paper we consider the operator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}:=(-\Delta)^{\ell}+|x|^{2k}
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and the corresponding semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}
for
\gamma>0
. The operators
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
are known as
anharmonic oscillators
and they play an important role in quantum physics, particularly when modelling vibrations for suitable families of molecules. Relevant cases such as the quartic, quartic-quadratic and sextic potentials
V(x)
associated to the one dimensional Hamiltonian
-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}+V(x)
are of interest in the analysis of specific quantum systems of molecules. For instance in the analysis of water molecules, under some circumstances, the harmonic oscillator fails to be even an approximate description. Therein a successful model is the quartic-quadratic oscillator given by a potential
V(x)
of the form
ax^{4}+bx^{2}
, where
a,b
are suitable constants. This model is, in particular, useful for treating low-frequency vibrations in small ring molecules
[
43
]
. Indeed, there is a bulk of recent literature on the anharmonic oscillators of quartic and sextic order in the study of molecular vibrations (cf.
[
4
,
20
,
36
]
and the references therein). We point out that, we can also consider more general operators than
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
allowing lower order terms with respect to each one of the symbols
|\xi|^{2\ell}
and
|x|^{2k}
. Such operators were considered in
[
12
]
. Furthermore, more generally we consider positive fractional powers
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}
of our anharmonic oscillators. We associate to the operator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
a suitable Hörmander metric
g=g^{k,\ell}
on the phase-space, an appropriate modulation weight
v
and study the corresponding boundedness properties on modulation spaces
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
for
0<p,q\leq\infty
. The fractional powers of the anharmonic oscillator are well defined for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, since in the scale of
S(m,g)
classes the operator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
turns out to be elliptic with respect to the metric
g=g^{k,\ell}
in the sense of
[
8
]
. The main results in this work extend the main ones in
[
5
,
6
]
for the special case of the harmonic oscillator
\mathcal{H}=-\Delta+|x|^{2}=\mathcal{A}_{1,\,1}
.

At this point the reader might wonder: since the natural operator in the context of quantum mechanics is the unitary group
e^{-it\mathcal{H}}
when
\mathcal{H}=-\Delta+V(x)
, why to study the semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{H}}
? Indeed, the usefulness of heat semigroups in quantum mechanics has been shown as long as 40 years ago and was well pointed out by B. Simon in
[
38
]
. For instance, when looking at the
L^{p}
properties of the eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{H}=-\Delta+V(x)
, if
\mathcal{H}\psi=E\psi
and
\psi\in L^{2}
, one observes that since
e^{-t\mathcal{H}}\psi=e^{-tE}\psi
, then
\psi\in e^{-t\mathcal{H}}(L^{2})
. Thus, a mapping property such as
e^{-t\mathcal{H}}:L^{2}\rightarrow L^{\infty}
can be used to deduce that
\psi
belongs also to
L^{\infty}
.
The works of B. Simon and R. Carmona on Schrödinger semigroups have deeply influenced the research on these semigroups, their applications are diverse and the literature is currently huge.
See for instance
[
1
,
15
,
39
,
40
,
29
,
30
]
, for more accounts on this matter. Thus, the Schrödinger semigroups are relevant for the study of
L^{p}
properties of eigenfunctions of Schrödinger operators and especially for the so-called
ground state
. By using them several quantities associated to the ground state can be analized, and in particular, they are useful in the quantum field theory
[
23
,
41
]
.
On the other hand, spectral properties for anharmonic oscillators have been investigated by many researchers for a long time. The case of polynomial potentials of even order has been studied by Helffer and Robert in
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
; the one-dimensional anharmonic oscillator with non smooth potential
\mathcal{L}=-\frac{d^{2\ell}}{dx^{2\ell}}+|x|
was recently considered in
[
16
]
. Herein we will consider mapping properties between modulation spaces
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}
for the semigroup corresponding to
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
.

The modulation spaces were introduced by H. Feichtinger in 1983 (cf.
[
21
]
) and have been intensively investigated in the last decades. We refer the reader to the survey
[
22
]

by Feichtinger for a historical account on the development of such spaces and a good account of the literature.
Modulation spaces have found numerous applications in various problems in linear
and nonlinear partial differential equations, see
[
37
]

for a recent survey. See also
[
17
]
for some recent applications of modulation spaces to nonlinear PDEs.
Here, in particular, we will apply our estimates
on modulation spaces to obtain the global well-posedness for a class
of nonlinear parabolic equations associated with
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}
.

The investigation of the global well-posedness for linear and nonlinear parabolic equations is an area of intensive research.
The well-posedness for a class of parabolic equations within the setting of Weyl-Hörmander calculus has been studied in
[
19
]
. In
[
18
,
33
]
, the authors discussed estimates of the heat propagator
e^{-t(-\Delta)^{\beta}}
with
\beta>0
of fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\beta}
for modulation spaces and Lebesgue spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and studied the global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem for the nonlinear fractional heat equation. In this case, the heat propagator is a Fourier multiplier on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, therefore, using the standard results on the boundedness of Fourier multipliers one can deduce the desired estimates. In the present paper, we are dealing with the anharmonic semigroups
e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}
for
\gamma>0
which are not, in general, Fourier multipliers. Therefore, we mainly depend on the Weyl-Hörmander
S(M,g)
symbol calculus. Our proof strategy shares similarities with that of
[
5
,
6
]
, but our proofs are significantly more intricate. This complexity arises due to the generality of the considered fractional anharmonic oscillators
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}
. Recently, there has been a great interest in investigating the control problem involving the anharmonic oscillators and their fractional counterparts. In particular, the null-controllability and spectral inequalities for the evolution equations associated with the (fractional) anharmonic oscillators were widely studied in
[
2
,
3
,
32
]
and references therein. It is interesting to record that the null-controllability of evolution equations involving the harmonic oscillator is different from the null-controllability of evolution equations involving anharmonic oscillators (see
[
9
,
32
]
). In the works
[
13
,
14
]
, the authors investigated the boundedness of spectral and Fourier multipliers associated with anharmonic oscillators.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we briefly present the necessary background on modulation spaces and the
S(m,g)
calculus. In Section
3
we establish our main result regarding the mapping properties on modulation spaces. We start first by introducing a suitable modulation weight function and deducting the main mapping properties we are addressing. Finally, in Section
4
we present an application to the global well-posedness for non-linear heat equations involving fractional anharmonic oscillators on modulation spaces.

2.
Preliminaries: Modulation spaces and Weyl-Hörmander calculus

In this section we briefly review some basics on modulation spaces and and the Weyl-Hörmander calculus. We refer the reader to Gröchenig’s book
[
24
]
for the basic definitions and properties of modulation spaces. We also refer to

[
28
, Section 18.5]
,
[
28
,
31
,
7
,
35
]
for a more detailed discussion on the Weyl-Hörmander calculus.

We start by recalling the notion of modulation spaces and we refer the reader to the Chapter 11 of
[
24
]
for a detailed exposition.
A
modulation weight function
is a non-negative, locally integrable function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
. Since we will also consider the notion of weight associated to a Hörmander metric, here in order to distinguish from them we additionally labeled the term
modulation
for the ones corresponding to the setting of modulation spaces.
A modulation weight function
v
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
is called
submultiplicative,
if

v(x+y)\leq v(x)v(y)\mbox{ for all }x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}.

(2.1)

A modulation weight function
w
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
is
v
-moderate,
if

w(x+y)\leq v(x)w(y)\mbox{ for all }x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}.

(2.2)

In particular the weights of polynomial type play an important role. They are of the form

v_{s}(x,\xi)=(1+|x|^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{s/2}.

(2.3)

The
v_{s}
-moderated weights (for some
s
) are called
polynomially moderated
.

Given a modulation weight
w
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
,
0\leq p,q\leq\infty,
and a window
g\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),
the modulation space
\mathcal{M}_{w}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
consists of the temperate distributions
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{w}^{p,q}}:=\|V_{g}f\|_{L_{w}^{p,q}}:=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}|V_{g}f(x,\xi)|^{p}w(x,\xi)^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty,

(2.4)

with suitable modification when
p=\infty
or
q=\infty,

where

V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}f(y)\,\overline{g(y-x)}e^{-iy\cdot\xi}dy,

(2.5)

denotes the short-time Fourier transform of
f
with respect to
g
at the point
(x,\xi)
. The modulation space
\mathcal{M}_{w}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
endowed with the above quasi-norm becomes a quasi-Banach space, independent of
g\neq 0
. For
p,q\geq 1,
the modulation space
\mathcal{M}_{w}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is a Banach space and the quasi-norm becomes a norm. In this case, the dual of
\mathcal{M}_{w}^{p,q}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is
\mathcal{M}_{\frac{1}{w}}^{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),
where
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
are the Lebesgue conjugates of
p
and
q,
respectively. If
p_{1}\leq p_{2},\,q_{1},\leq q_{2}
and
w_{1}\geq w_{2}
then the embedding
\mathcal{M}_{w_{1}}^{p_{1},q_{1}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{w_{2}}^{p_{2},q_{2}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
holds.

We now recall basic notions of the Weyl-Hörmander calculus and refer the reader to
[
28
,
31
,
7
]
for more detailed discussions.

The Weyl quantisation of
a\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the operator
a^{w}(x,D):\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined by

\displaystyle a^{w}(x,D)u(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}a((x+y)/2,\xi)u(y)dyd\xi,\,\,u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

More generally, we will consider a family of
t
-quantisations of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and for any
t\in\mathbb{R},
defined by

a_{t}(x,D)f(x):=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\langle x-y,\xi\rangle}a(tx+(1-t)y,\xi)u(y)dyd\xi.

Thus, the Weyl quantisation of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
corresponds to the case
t=\frac{1}{2}
i.e.
a_{\frac{1}{2}}(x,D)=a^{w}(x,D).
The case
t=1
corrresponds to the Kohn-Nirenberg quantisation.

a(x,D)u(x)=a_{1}(x,D)u(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\langle x,\xi\rangle}a(x,\xi)\widehat{u}(\xi)d\xi.

We now recall the definition of a Hörmander metric. By looking at our anharmonic oscillators, this concept will be crucial in particular to deduce some good properties for the fractional powers of
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
.

Definition 2.1

(Hörmander’s metric)
.

For
X\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, let
g_{X}(\cdot)
be a positive definite quadratic form on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. We say that

g(\cdot)
is a Hörmander metric if the following three conditions are satisfied:

I.

Continuity or slowness.
There exist a constant
C>0
such that

g_{X}(X-Y)\leq C^{-1}\Longrightarrow\left(\frac{g_{X}(T)}{g_{Y}(T)}\right)^{\pm 1}\leq C,

for all
T\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}\setminus\{0\}
.

II.

Uncertainty principle.
In terms of the symplectic form
\sigma(Y,Z):=\langle z,\eta\rangle-\langle y,\zeta\rangle
,
we define

g_{X}^{\sigma}(T):=\sup_{W\neq 0}\frac{\sigma(T,W)^{2}}{g_{X}(W)}.

We say that
g
satisfies the
uncertainty principle

if

\lambda_{g}(X)=\inf_{T\neq 0}\left(\frac{g_{X}^{\sigma}(T)}{g_{X}(T)}\right)^{1/2}\geq 1,

for
all
X,T\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

III.

Temperateness.
We say that
g
is temperate if there exist
\overline{C}>0
and
J\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left(\frac{g_{X}(T)}{g_{Y}(T)}\right)^{\pm 1}\leq\overline{C}(1+g_{Y}^{\sigma}(X-Y))^{J},

for all
X,Y,T\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
.

Let
g
be a Hörmander metric.
The Planck function
associated to
g
is defined by

h_{g}(X)^{2}=\sup_{T\neq 0}\frac{g_{X}(T)}{g_{X}^{\sigma}(T)}.

Note that
h_{g}(X)=(\lambda_{g}(X))^{-1}
. So,

Definition 2.2

(
g
-weight)
.

Let
M:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a function. Then,

•

we say that

M
is
g
-
continuous
if there exists
\tilde{C}>0

such that

g_{X}(X-Y)\leq\frac{1}{\tilde{C}}\Longrightarrow\left(\frac{M(X)}{M(Y)}\right)^{\pm 1}\leq\tilde{C}.

•

we say that

M
is
g
-
temperate
if there exist
\tilde{C}>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}

such that

\left(\frac{M(X)}{M(Y)}\right)^{\pm 1}\leq\tilde{C}(1+g_{Y}^{\sigma}(X-Y))^{N}.

We will say that
M
is a
g
-
weight
if it is
g
-continuous and
g
-temperate.

Given a Hörmander metric
g
and a
g
-weight, we can now define the corresponding classes of symbols.

Definition 2.3
.

For a Hörmander metric
g
and a
g
-weight
M
, the class
S(M,g)
consists of all smooth functions
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
such that for any integer
k
there exists
C_{k}>0
, such that for all

X,T_{1},...,T_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
we have

|\sigma^{(k)}(X;T_{1},...,T_{k})|\leq C_{k}M(X)\prod_{i=1}^{k}g_{X}^{1/2}(T_{i}).

(2.6)

The notation
\sigma^{(k)}
stands for the
k^{th}
derivative of
\sigma
and
\sigma^{(k)}(X;T_{1},...,T_{k})
denotes the
k^{th}
derivative of
a
at
X
with respect to the directions
T_{1},...,T_{k}
.
For
\sigma\in S(M,g)
we denote by
\parallel\sigma\parallel_{k,S(M,g)}
the minimum
C_{k}
satisfying the above inequality. The class
S(M,g)
becomes a Fréchet space endowed with the family of seminorms
\parallel\cdot\parallel_{k,S(M,g)}
.

3.
Mapping properties of anharmonic fractional heat semigroup on modulation spaces

In this section, we establish the boundedness on modulation spaces for a class of pseudo-differential operators including the negative powers of our anharmonic oscillators. We first introduce the specific setting regarding those spaces by choosing a suitable modulation weight function, and secondly an appropriate class of symbols by defining a Hörmander metric adapted to the operators
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
.

We start by introducing a modulation weight function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
for integers
k,\ell\geq 1
. First we define
\tilde{v}
by

\tilde{v}(x,\xi):=(1+|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell}),

for all
x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

We note that for all
x,y,\xi,\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
and a suitable
C>1
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{v}(x,\xi)\tilde{v}(y,\eta)=

\displaystyle\,(1+|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell})(1+|y|^{k}+|\eta|^{\ell})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,1+|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell}+1+|y|^{k}+|\eta|^{\ell}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,1+|x|^{k}+|y|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell}+|\eta|^{\ell}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,C^{-1}(1+|x+y|^{k}+|\xi+\eta|^{\ell})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,C^{-1}\tilde{v}(x+y,\xi+\eta).

Hence, by defining
v=C\tilde{v}
, we obtain the submultiplicativity of
v
. From now on we will only consider modulation spaces
\mathcal{M}_{w}^{p,q}
corresponding to modulation weights of the form
w=v^{m}
for
m\in\mathbb{R}
. When
m=0,
we simply write
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}.

On the other hand, we will associate to the operator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
the following metric on the phase-space:

g:=g^{(k,\ell)}=\frac{dx^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{1}{k}}}+\frac{d\xi^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{1}{\ell}}}.

(3.1)

The fact that this metric is indeed a Hörmander metric has been proved in
[
11
]
, where the authors established spectral properties for the operators
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
, by studying Schatten-von Neumann properties of their negative powers. The fact that
1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell}
is a
g
-weight follows, and in particular we have

|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell}\in S(1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell},g).

We observe that the uncertainty parameter for our metric is given by

\lambda_{g}=(1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{k+\ell}{2k\ell}}.

(3.2)

Therefore the
Planck function

g
and the modulation weight
v
are related by

h_{g}^{-1}=\lambda_{g}\simeq v^{\frac{k+\ell}{k\ell}}.

(3.3)

We note that the exponent
{\frac{k+\ell}{2k\ell}}
in (
3.2
)
also arises in the study of asymptotic formulas for the eigenvalue counting function of our anharmonic oscillators, as we will clarify later on.

The Hörmander symbol classes
S((1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{m}{2}},g)
for
m\in\mathbb{R}
, can also be defined in a more intrinsic way:

If
a\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
we will say that
a\in\Sigma_{k,\ell}^{m}
if

|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha\beta}(1+|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell})^{m-\frac{|\beta|}{k}-\frac{|\alpha|}{\ell}}\,,

(3.4)

holds for all multi-indices
\alpha,\beta
and for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
This characterisation of
S((1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{m}{2}},g)=\Sigma_{k,\ell}^{m}
was derived in
[
11
]
.

The space of symbols
\Sigma_{k,\ell}^{m}=S((1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{m}{2}},g)
becomes a Fréchet space by introducing the following seminorms:

\|a\|_{\alpha,\beta}:=\inf\{C_{\alpha\beta}:\eqref{sigmacl}\,\,\mbox{ holds}\}.

(3.5)

It will also be useful to point out that our symbol classes can also be embedded into the ones introduced by Nicola and Rodino in
[
34
]
, by taking
k_{0}=\max\{k,\ell\}
. Indeed, we have

g=g^{(k,\ell)}\leq\frac{dx^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{1}{k_{0}}}}+\frac{d\xi^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{1}{k_{0}}}}.

(3.6)

Hence, by taking
\Phi(x,\xi)=\Psi(x,\xi)=(1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{1}{k_{0}}},\,
and
M(x,\xi)=1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell}
, it is not difficult to see that
\Phi
and
\Psi
are sub-linear and temperate weights. Consequently,
M
is a temperate weight. Then, by considering the class
S(M;\Phi,\Psi)
in the sense of
[
34
]
, it is clear that,

S((1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{\frac{s}{2}},g)\subset S(M^{\frac{s}{2}};\Phi,\Psi).

(3.7)

It is crucial to point out that the densely defined anharmonic oscillator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell}
on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
is invertible. Indeed, since the operators
(-\Delta)^{\ell}
and
|x|^{2k}
are strictly positive, then the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell}
are strictly positive. On the other hand, by Theorem 4.2.9 of
[
34
]
, it follows that the spectrum of the closure
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{k,\ell}
in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
only consists of a discrete sequence of eigenvalues diverging to
\infty
. Therefore,
0
does not belong to the spectrum of
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{k,\ell}
. Moreover, the eigenvalues of
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{k,\ell}

have all finite multiplicity and the eigenfunctions belong to
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}),
and
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
has an orthonormal basis consisting of eigenfunctions of
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{k,\ell}
.

We also note that our anharmonic oscillators are
g
-elliptic in the sense of
[
8
]
and we can define the real powers
\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell}^{\gamma}
for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
of the operators
\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell}
. The strong uncertainty principle in that setting also holds.

The Sobolev space
H_{k,\ell}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and for
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
is defined as

H_{k,\ell}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}):\|(\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell})^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}<\infty\right\}.

The space
H_{k,\ell}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
will be called the anharmonic-Sobolev space of order
s
relative to
k,\ell.

For
k=1
and
\ell=1,

H_{k,\ell}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the well-known Shubin-Sobolev space (also known as Hermite-Sobolev space).
The space
H_{k,\ell}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Hilbert space equipped with the sesquilinear form

\langle u,v\rangle:=\langle(\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell})^{\frac{s}{2}}u,(\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell})^{\frac{s}{2}}v\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}.

Also, the dual space of
H_{k,\ell}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
H_{k,\ell}^{-s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

The following lemma characterises the anharmonic-Sobolev space. The proof of this lemma is verbatim to the proof of

by using the weight

the embedding and duality properties of

Lemma 3.1

For all

we have

\mathcal{M}_{v^{m}}^{2,2}=H_{k,\ell}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(3.8)

with equivalent norms.

Using Lemma

, the inclusion relations given in Theorem 2.4.17 of

and Hölder’s inequality, we deduce that for every

for all

Recall that
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the modulation space
\mathcal{M}_{v^{m}}^{p,q}
with

We can now state the boundedness on modulation spaces for

-quantizations of symbols in the setting of the above classes.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

,

and

. Then

extends to a bounded operator, with operator norm depending only on a finite
number of seminorms of
a
in
\Sigma_{k,\ell}^{-m}
.

Proof.

We consider the following modulation weights
\omega_{1}(x,\xi)=1
,
\omega_{2}(x,\xi)=(1+|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell})^{m}

(x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
and
\omega_{0}(x,\xi,\eta,y)=(1+|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell}+|y|^{k}+|\eta|^{\ell})^{m},

(x,\xi,y,\eta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. An application of Theorem 3.1 of
[
42
]
will give the boundedness of

a_{t}(x,D):\mathcal{M}^{p,\,q}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{v^{m}}^{p,\,q},

for all

, provided
a\in\mathcal{M}_{\omega_{0}}^{\infty,\,r}({\mathbb{R}}^{2n})
for
r\leq\min\{1,p,q\}
.

In order to see that in fact

, for

, we first write:

e^{-iW\cdot Y}=(1+|w|^{2k}+|\tau|^{2\ell})^{-N}\left((\frac{1}{2}+(-\Delta_{y})^{k})^{N}+(\frac{1}{2}+(-\Delta_{\eta})^{\ell})^{N}\right)e^{-iW\cdot Y},

(3.10)

where we have written
Y=(y,\eta),W=(w,\tau)
.

We also note that for a suitable
C>0
we have

C^{-1}(1+|x|+|\xi|)^{\tau_{0}}\leq 1+|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell}\leq C(1+|x|+|\xi|)^{\tau_{1}},

(3.11)

where
\tau_{0}=\min\{k,\ell\}
and
\tau_{1}=\max\{k,\ell\}
.

We now plug (

) into the formula (

) for the short-time Fourier transform of the symbol

on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2n}
. A repeated integration by parts and using the fact that

give us that the following holds: for every

there exists

|V_{g}a(Z,W)|\leq C_{N}(1+|z|^{k}+|\gamma|^{\ell})^{-m}(1+|w|^{k}+|\tau|^{\ell})^{-2N},

(3.12)

for all

and where we have written

.

From (
3.11
) and (

) we can conclude the proof by taking

which gives the finiteness of the desired modulation norm in

for the symbol

.
∎

We now consider the special case of the positive powers of the anharmonic oscillator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
and the corresponding semigroup.
We will require some knowledge of the spectrum of the anharmonic oscillator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}=(-\Delta)^{\ell}+|x|^{2k}
. We will denote by
\lambda_{j}
the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
and arrange them in increasing order with the smallest eigenvalue denoted by
\lambda_{0}>0
. The estimates at the end of Section 5 in
[

give the asymptotics for the eigenvalues

:

\lambda_{j}\sim C_{k,\ell}j^{\frac{2k\ell}{n(k+\ell)}},\,\mbox{ as }\,j\rightarrow\infty.

(3.13)

We denote by
\Phi_{j}
an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions corresponding to the eigenvalues
\lambda_{j}
. We also denote by
d_{j}
the dimension of the eigenspace
H_{j}
corresponding to
\lambda_{j}
. Thus, with respect to the orthogonal projections
P_{j}
over each

we have

\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}P_{j}f,\hskip 11.38092ptP_{j}f=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{j}}\langle f,\Phi_{j_{i}}\rangle\Phi_{j_{i}},

where

denotes the inner product in
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
.

We can define the fractional powers
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}
of
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
by means of the spectral theorem

\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}f=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{\gamma}P_{j}f.

Having this, we can say that the anharmonic-Sobolev space
H_{k,\ell}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all those functions

such that

\|f\|_{H_{k,\ell}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}:=\|(\mathcal{A}_{k,\ell})^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}^{s}\|P_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}<\infty.

(3.14)

The next result about the fractional anharmonic oscillator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}:=((-\Delta)^{\ell}+|x|^{2k})^{\gamma}
will be essential in the proof of the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\gamma>0
and let
k,\ell
be integers
\geq 1.
The fractional anharmonic oscillator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}:=((-\Delta)^{\ell}+|x|^{2k})^{\gamma}
is a pseudo-differential operator with real Weyl symbol
a_{\gamma}:=\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}(x,\xi)\in\Sigma^{2\gamma}_{k,\ell}.
More precisely, the symbol
a_{\gamma}
is given by

\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}(x,\xi):=(|\xi|^{2\ell}+|x|^{2k})^{\gamma}+r(x,\xi),\quad|\xi|^{\ell}+|x|^{k}\geq 1,

(3.15)

for some

r\in\Sigma^{2\gamma-(\frac{k+\ell}{k\ell})}_{k,\ell}.

Proof.

We first note that from (
3.2
), the Planck function corresponding to our metric
g
satisfies

h_{g}=(1+|x|^{2k}+|\xi|^{2\ell})^{-\frac{k+\ell}{2k\ell}}\leq C(1+|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell})^{-\frac{k+\ell}{k\ell}}\leq C(1+|x|+|\xi|)^{-\frac{(k+\ell)\min\{k,\ell\}}{k\ell}},

and the strong uncertainty principle holds for
g
in the sense of
[
34
]
.

On the other hand, since the Hörmander metric
g
is split and due to the form of the symbol
\sigma:=|\xi|^{2\ell}+|x|^{2k}
, it is not difficult to see that their corresponding
\sigma_{t}(x,D)
quantizations coincide up to lower order terms by Theorem 2.3.12 of
[
31
]
. The operator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}
is positive and Theorem 4.3.6 of
[
34
]
applied to the class

regarding the positive powers of

, gives the desired result. Indeed, the Weyl symbol
a_{\gamma}=\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}(x,\xi)
satisfies the following estimate for the first remainder

\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}(x,\xi)-(|\xi|^{2\ell}+|x|^{2k})^{\gamma}\in\Sigma^{2\gamma-(\frac{k+\ell}{k\ell})}_{k,\ell},

for
|\xi|^{\ell}+|x|^{k}\geq 1

As we have seen, the operator
A=\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}
for
\gamma>0
, is a pseudo-differential operator with positive, elliptic Weyl symbol in

as in (

define an orthonormal basis of

the

associated to
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}
for

e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-t\lambda_{j}^{\gamma}}P_{j}f.

Moreover, thanks to Theorem 4.5.1 of
[
34
]
, the operator

is a pseudo-differential operator with the Weyl symbol satisfying a family of inequalities that will be useful in the next theorem.

We can now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\gamma>0,

0<p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\leq\infty
and let
k,\ell
be integers
\geq 1.
Define
\tilde{p},\tilde{q}
and
\sigma
as follows:

\frac{1}{\tilde{p}}:=\text{max}\left\{\frac{1}{p_{2}}-\frac{1}{p_{1}},0\right\},\quad\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}:=\text{max}\left\{\frac{1}{q_{2}}-\frac{1}{q_{1}},0\right\}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\sigma:=\frac{n}{2\gamma}\Big(\frac{1}{k\tilde{p}}+\frac{1}{\ell\tilde{q}}\Big).

Then the semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}
associated to the

\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}:=((-\Delta)^{\ell}+|x|^{2k})^{\gamma}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, satisfies the following estimate, for every

\|e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(t)\|f\|_{{\mathcal{M}}^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(3.17)

where

C(t)=C^{\prime}\begin{cases}t^{-\sigma}\quad&0<t\leq 1,\\
e^{-t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\quad&t\geq 1,\end{cases}

for some positive constant

where
\lambda_{0}
is the smallest eigenvalue of the anharmonic oscillator

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps, in the first step we consider

Case

. The modulation spaces obey some inclusion relations that allow us to reduce the proof to consider
p_{2}

and
q_{2}

\min\{q_{1},q_{2}\}

and
q_{2}\leq q_{1}
.

On the other hand, since the operator

for

, its

-ellipticity following from (
3.15
). We can then apply
Theorem 4.5.1 of
[

]
to the heat kernel

e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}
. The operator
e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}
has a Weyl symbol

and we write

. For every

, the symbol

belongs to a bounded subset of

If

is such that

, applying Theorem

\|(1+t^{N}\omega^{N})V_{g}(e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f)\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p_{1},q_{1}}},

for a suitable constant

.

Thus, in order to obtain (
3.17
), it will be enough to prove that

\|F\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}}\leq C(t)\|(1+t^{N}\omega^{N})F\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}},

(3.19)

with
C(t)
as in the statement and for all measurable functions

.

By Hölder inequality and since
\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{p}}
,
\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{\tilde{q}}
, we have

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}}=

\displaystyle\|(1+t^{N}\omega^{N})^{-1}(1+t^{N}\omega^{N})F\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(1+t^{N}\omega^{N})^{-1}\|_{L^{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}}\|(1+t^{N}\omega^{N})F\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}}.

Now, to estimate
\tilde{C}(t)=\|(1+t^{N}\omega^{N})^{-1}\|_{L^{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}}
, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\tilde{C}(t)^{\tilde{q}}

\displaystyle=\|(1+t^{N}\omega^{N})^{-1}\|_{L^{\tilde{p},\tilde{q}}}^{\tilde{q}}=\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+t^{N}(1+|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell})^{2\gamma N})^{-\tilde{p}}dx\right)^{\frac{\tilde{q}}{\tilde{p}}}d\xi

\displaystyle=\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+(t^{\frac{1}{2\gamma}}(|x|^{k}+|\xi|^{\ell}))^{2\gamma N})^{-\tilde{p}}dx\right)^{\frac{\tilde{q}}{\tilde{p}}}d\xi

The change of variables

and

\displaystyle\tilde{C}(t)^{\tilde{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+(|\tilde{x}|^{k}+|\tilde{\xi}|^{\ell})^{2\gamma N})^{-\tilde{p}}t^{-\frac{n}{2\gamma k}}d\tilde{x}\right)^{\frac{\tilde{q}}{\tilde{p}}}t^{-\frac{n}{2\gamma\ell}}d\tilde{\xi}

3.19
) and hence the estimate (
3.17
) for the case

Case

. In view of the following representation

e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-t\lambda_{j}^{\gamma}}P_{j}f,

3.17

\|P_{j}f\|_{M^{p_{2},q_{2}}}\leq C^{\prime}\lambda_{j}^{m}\|f\|_{M^{p_{1},q_{1}}}.

for all

for some

and

To prove (

(3.22)

Indeed, it is easy to see this by a simple calculation as follows:

\displaystyle\|P_{j}f\|_{H^{m}_{k,\ell}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\frac{m}{2}}P_{j}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}^{m}\|P_{k}(P_{j}f)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

(3.25)

where we have used the fact that the integral in penultimate inequality is decreasing in

so its value for

can not bigger than that of

4.
Applications to global well-posedness of the nonlinear heat equation for the anharmonic oscillator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}

In this section, our main aim is to investigate the global well-posedness of the following nonlinear fractional heat equation associated with the anharmonic oscillator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}u=\lambda|u|^{2\beta}u,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(4.1)

for
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},
where
\beta\in\mathbb{N},

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma>0.

We begin by recalling some well-known properties of modulation space
[
21
,
5
]
. The following result is concerned with the algebra property of modulation spaces.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
p_{i},q_{j}\in[0,1]
for
1\leq i\leq m
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{i}}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{q_{i}}=m-1+\frac{1}{q_{0}}.
Then, for some
C>0,
we have

\Bigg\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}\Bigg\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p_{0},q_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p_{i},q_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The next result easily follows from Lemma
4.1
and the embedding of modulation spaces (for proof, see e.g.
[
5
]
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
p,q,r\in[1,\infty]
and
\frac{1}{r}+2\beta=\frac{2\beta+1}{q}
for
\beta\in\mathbb{N}.

Then the following multi-linear estimate holds:

\||f|^{2\beta}f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta+1}.

Now, we state and prove the main result of this section concerning the global wellposedness of nonlinear heat equations associated with the fractional anharmonic oscillator.

Theorem 4.3
.

Consider the nonlinear heat equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u+\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}u=\lambda|u|^{2\beta}u,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(4.2)

for
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{n},
where
\beta\in\mathbb{N},

\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
and
\gamma>0.
Let
p,q\in[1,\infty]
be such that
2\beta+1\leq q^{\prime}
and
\frac{\beta n}{\gamma\ell}<q^{\prime}.
Then, for a small initial data
u_{0},
that is, there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}}\leq\epsilon,
the problem (
4.2
) admits a global solution
u
in
L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{M}^{p,q}).
In addition to this, if
p<\infty,

u\in C([0,\infty),\mathcal{M}^{p,q}).

Moreover, if
\epsilon
is sufficiently small then we get the exponential decay in time, in particular,
u\in Y,
where the space
Y
is defined as

Y:=\left\{u\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{M}^{p,q})\Big|\Big\|e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\infty))}<+\infty\right\}

(4.3)

equipped with norm

\|u\|_{Y}:=\Big\|e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}([0,\infty))},

(4.4)

where
\lambda_{0}
is the smallest eigenvalue of the anharmonic oscillator
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}.

Proof.

Using Duhamel’s principle we can write the solution of
u
of (
4.2
) in the following integral form

\displaystyle u(t)=e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}u_{0}+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}(|u(s)|^{2\beta}u(s))\,ds.

(4.5)

Now, with
p,q
as in the statement of the theorem, from Theorem
3.4
we have

\displaystyle\|e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{\prime}\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(4.6)

for all
t\geq 0
and some positive constant
C^{\prime}.
Also, for any
r\geq q
, by setting
p_{1}=p_{2}=p
and
q_{1}=q\leq r=q_{2}
and so
\sigma=\frac{n}{2\gamma\ell}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)
in Theorem
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle\|e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C^{\prime}\begin{cases}t^{-\sigma}\|f\|_{M^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\quad 0<t\leq 1,\\
e^{-t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|f\|_{M^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}&\quad t\geq 1.\end{cases}

(4.7)

for some
C^{\prime}>0.

By the assumption
2\beta+1\leq q^{\prime},
so we can pick
1\leq r\leq\infty
such that
\frac{2\beta+1}{q}=\frac{1}{r}+2\beta
since
2\beta\leq\frac{2\beta+1}{q}\leq 2\beta+1
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}.
Also, the assumption
\frac{\beta n}{\gamma\ell}<q^{\prime}
yields that
\sigma=\frac{n}{2\gamma\ell}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)<1.

Let us now estimate the nonhomogeneous part of the integral (
4.5
). An application of Minkowski’s inequality with (
4.7
) gives

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle(|u(s)|^{2\beta}u(s))\,ds\Bigg\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{-(t-s)\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}(|u(s)|^{2\beta}u(s))\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\sigma}\|(|u(s)|^{2\beta}u(s))\|_{M^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,ds&\quad 0<t\leq 1,\\
\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|(|u(s)|^{2\beta}u(s))\|_{M^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,ds&\quad t\geq 1.\end{cases}

(4.8)

Now, we use Lemma
4.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle(|u(s)|^{2\beta}u(s))\,ds\Bigg\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\sigma}\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta+1}\,ds&\quad 0<t\leq 1,\\
\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta+1}\,ds&\quad t\geq 1.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime\prime}\|u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,t],\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{2\beta+1}\begin{cases}\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-\sigma}\,ds&\quad 0<t\leq 1,\\
\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\tau\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\,ds&\quad t\geq 1.\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\|u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{2\beta+1},

(4.9)

because the integral given in the penultimate inequality is finite with the given condition
\sigma<1.

As we are going to use the Banach contraction argument for the proof, let us set the operator of our interest

\mathfrak{T}u:=e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}u_{0}+\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}(|u(s)|^{2\beta}u(s))\,ds

(4.10)

for
u
in the closed ball

B_{R}:=\{u\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})):\|u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq R\}

in
L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
of radius
R
and centered at origin.

Claim:
We claim that the operator
\mathfrak{T}
is a contraction map from
B_{R}
into
B_{R}
for some suitable
R>0.

We first notice that it follows from (
4.6
) and (
4
) that for some
C_{2}>0,
we have the following estimate for
\mathfrak{T}
in
B_{R},

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{T}u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u(s)\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}^{2\beta+1}\right),

(4.11)

which by assuming
\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{R}{2C_{2}},
for now, yields that

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{T}u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{2}+C_{2}R^{2\beta+1},

(4.12)

as
u\in B_{R}.

Now, to make sure that
\mathfrak{T}u\in B_{R},
we must choose
R
such
C_{2}R^{2\beta+1}<\frac{R}{2},
that is,
R<\left(\frac{1}{2C_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}.
Therefore, choosing
\epsilon=\frac{R}{2C_{2}}
with
R<\left(\frac{1}{2C_{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}
we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{T}u\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{2}+\frac{R}{2}=R,

(4.13)

implying that
\mathfrak{T}u\in B_{R}.

Next, we show that the map
\mathfrak{T}
is a contraction. For this, by repeating similar arguments as above for
u,v\in B_{R}
and using the following inequality

|u|^{2\beta}u-|v|^{2\beta}v\leq C_{3}(|u|^{2\beta-1}+|v|^{2\beta-1})|u-v|,

we have, for
C_{4}>0,
that

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{T}u-\mathfrak{T}v\|_{L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\,\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\leq

\displaystyle C_{4}\left(\|u\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta-1}+\|v\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta-1}\right)\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}^{\prime}R^{2\beta-1}\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(4.14)

Hence, by taking
R>0
sufficiently small and therefore
\epsilon
, for example
R<\left(\frac{1}{2C_{4}^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2\beta-1}},
we conclude that
\mathfrak{T}
is a contraction on
B_{R},
establishing our claim.

Therefore, using Banach fixed point theorem, we conclude that
\mathfrak{T}
has a unique fix point in
B_{R}
which is the solution of nonlinear problem (
4.2
).

Additionally, let us assume that
p<\infty
. Our objective is to demonstrate that, under this assumption, the unique solution
u\in L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
obtained from (
4.2
) as described earlier is continuous with respect to
t
. This implies that
u\in C([0,\infty),\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. The condition
2\beta+1\leq q^{\prime}
also implies that
q<\infty
. Let us assume for a moment that the semigroup
{e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}}
is strongly continuous on
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
then we repeat the similar calculation and the Banach contraction argument as above by replacing
L^{\infty}([0,\infty),\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
by
C([0,\infty),\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
to get the desired conclusion. It is evident from (
4.6
) that it is enough to show that, for every
f
in the dense subset of
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),
the map
t\mapsto e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f
is continuous with values in
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
in order to ensure that
e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}
is a strongly continuous semigroup on
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

To establish the continuity of the map
t\mapsto e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f
with values in
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
f
within the dense subset of
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we leverage the inclusion relation (
3.6
) and apply a certain abstract argument from
[
34
, Page 194]
. This involves utilizing the fact that the semigroup
e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}
exhibits strong continuity on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\beta}
commutes with
e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}
for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
. Consequently, the mapping
t\mapsto e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\beta}f=\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\beta}e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f
is continuous with values in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for every
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
.
By referring to
[
34
, Page 194]
, we infer that the seminorms
p_{\beta}(f):=\|\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\beta}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
for
\beta\in\mathbb{N}
define an equivalent family of seminorms on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Consequently, the mapping
t\mapsto e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f
exhibits continuity with values in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and by extension, when treated as a
\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
-valued function. This conclude that for
p<\infty,
the unique solution
u
of (
4.2
) is in
C([0,\infty),\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Now we proceed to deduce that by choosing
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small we can get the desired rate of decay of
u.
Again, from Theorem
3.4
it follows that

\|e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{0}e^{-t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for some
C_{0}>0,
which implies that

\|e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}f\|_{Y}\leq C_{0}\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(4.15)

where the space
Y
is defined by (
4.3
).

Similar to estimate (
4
), we have

e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\Bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}(|u(s)|^{2\beta}u(s))\,ds\Bigg\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{0}^{\prime}e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{t}C(t-s)\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta+1}\,ds,

(4.16)

where
C(t)
for
t>0
is given by

C(t)=C^{\prime}\begin{cases}t^{-\sigma}\quad&0<t\leq 1,\\
e^{-t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\quad&t\geq 1,\end{cases}

(4.17)

for some positive constant
C^{\prime}.
To estimate the integral on the right-hand side, we set

T_{1}:=\{s\in[0,t]:t-s<1\}
and
T_{2}:=\{s\in[0,t]:t-s\geq 1\}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\int_{0}^{t}C(t-s)\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta+1}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad=e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\int_{T_{1}}C(t-s)\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta+1}\,ds+e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\int_{T_{2}}C(t-s)\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta+1}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\int_{T_{1}}(t-s)^{-\sigma}(e^{-s\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}e^{-s\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{2\beta+1}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+C_{1}e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\int_{T_{2}}e^{-(t-s)\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2\beta+1}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\int_{T_{1}}(t-s)^{-\sigma}e^{-s(2\beta+1)\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}(e^{s\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{2\beta+1}\,ds

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+C_{1}\int_{T_{2}}e^{2\beta s\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}(e^{s\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|u(s)\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})})^{2\beta+1}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|u(s)\|_{Y}^{2\beta+1}\int_{T_{1}}(t-s)^{-\sigma}e^{-s(2\beta+1)\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\,ds+C_{1}\|u(s)\|_{Y}^{2\beta+1}\int_{T_{2}}e^{-2\beta s\lambda_{0}^{\beta}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}e^{t\lambda_{0}^{\gamma}}\|u(s)\|_{Y}^{2\beta+1}\int_{0}^{1}\tau^{-\sigma}\,d\tau+C_{1}\|u(s)\|_{Y}^{2\beta+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-2\beta s\lambda_{0}^{\beta}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\|u(s)\|_{Y}^{2\beta+1},

(4.18)

where we have used that
\beta>0
and
\sigma<1
by the hypothesis in the statement of the theorem.
Therefore, putting together (
4.15
), (
4.16
) and (
4
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{T}u\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{-t\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}u_{0}\|_{Y}+\Big\|\lambda\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-s)\mathcal{A}_{k,\,\ell}^{\gamma}}(|u(s)|^{2\beta}u(s))\,ds\Big\|_{Y}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\left(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{M}^{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|u(s)\|_{Y}^{2\beta+1}\right).

Finally, we can argue as before using Banach’s contraction argument to get the desired result. ∎

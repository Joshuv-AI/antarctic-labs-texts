Compact Bloch mappings on the complex unit disc
A. Jiménez-Vargas, D. Ruiz-Casternado

Abstract
. The known duality of the space of Bloch complex-valued functions on the open complex unit disc 𝔻 \mathbb{D} is addressed under a new approach with the introduction of the concepts of Bloch molecules and Bloch-free Banach space of 𝔻 \mathbb{D} . We introduce the notion of compact Bloch mapping from 𝔻 \mathbb{D} to a complex Banach space and establish its main properties: invariance by Möbius transformations, linearization from the Bloch-free Banach space of 𝔻 \mathbb{D} , factorization of their derivatives, inclusion properties, Banach ideal property and transposition on the Bloch function space. We state Bloch versions of the classical theorems of Schauder, Gantmacher and Davis–Figiel–Johnson–Pełczyński.

1.
Introduction

The study of the compactness of nonlinear mappings has been a subject of much interest as a first step to extend results from Linear Functional Analysis to the nonlinear setting. In this direction,
various authors established some results of the classical theory such as Schauder, Gantmacher and Gantmacher–Nakamura Theorems in different nonlinear environments: for instance, R. M. Aron and M. Schottenloher
[
5
]
, J. Mujica
[
17
]
and R. Ryan
[
20
]
in both holomorphic and polynomial settings; J. Batt
[
7
]
in the nonlinear bounded context; S. Yamamuro
[
22
]
in the differentiable framework; and J. M. Sepulcre, M. Villegas-Vallecillos and the first author
[
12
]
in the Lipschitz setting.

In this paper, we will address the study of this topic in the setting of Bloch mappings. Let
\mathbb{D}
be the open complex unit disc,
X
be a complex Banach space and
\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
denote the space of all holomorphic mappings from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
.

The
normalized Bloch space

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
is the Banach space of all mappings
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
f(0)=0
such that

p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=\sup\left\{(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}<\infty,

endowed with the
Bloch norm

p_{\mathcal{B}}
. The
normalized little Bloch space

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D},X)
is the norm-closed linear subspace of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
formed by all those mappings
f
so that

\lim_{|z|\to 1^{-}}(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|=0.

To simplify, we will write
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
instead of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
and
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{C})
, respectively. Note that the set of all polynomials is dense in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
.

The properties of Bloch functions on
\mathbb{D}
are well known (see
[
1
]
) and they have been studied by some authors to investigate problems of geometric function theory (see
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
14
,
18
,
23
]
). To paraphrase R. M. Timoney
[
21
]
:
one of the reasons that Bloch functions are interesting is that they can be characterized in many different ways and, so, they arise in many different contexts.

The properties of local compactness of holomorphic mappings on complex Banach spaces were studied in
[
5
,
20
]
. A holomorphic mapping
f\colon X\to Y
is said to be
locally (weakly) compact
if every point
x\in X
has a neighborhood
U_{x}
such that
f(U_{x})
is relatively (weakly) compact in
Y
. The study of holomorphic mappings on Banach spaces with a relatively (weakly) compact range was initiated by J. Mujica
[
17
]
.

The novelty of our approach in this paper lies in using the Bloch range of a function in place of its local or global range. By the
Bloch range
of a function
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
, we mean the set
\left\{(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z)\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}\subseteq X
. Clearly, a holomorphic mapping
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is Bloch if its Bloch range is a bounded subset of
X
and this motivates us to introduce the following concepts: a holomorphic mapping
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is said to be
(weakly) compact Bloch
if its Bloch range is a relatively (weakly) compact subset of
X
. Observe that if
X
is finite-dimensional, then every Bloch mapping is indeed both compact and weakly compact. Among others properties, we emphasize that both linear spaces of compact Bloch mappings and weakly compact Bloch mappings from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
are
Möbius-invariant
and
Banach ideals
.

One of the key elements in our work is the concept of
Bloch atom of
\mathbb{D}
, namely, the function
\gamma_{z}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}
with
z\in\mathbb{D}
defined by
\gamma_{z}(f)=f^{\prime}(z)
. These elementary Bloch molecules of
\mathbb{D}
generate a norm-closed linear subspace of the dual space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, denoted
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
and called
Bloch-free Banach space of
\mathbb{D}
because of a
universal extension property
of this space. The elements of the linear hull generated by Bloch atoms of
\mathbb{D}
will be referred to as
Bloch molecules of
\mathbb{D}
.

This paper is organized as follows. Section
2
contains a complete study of the concepts introduced. It is well known that
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is a dual Banach space
[
2
,
3
]
. Section
3
is devoted to the study of the duality of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
under a different approach since we prove that
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is the unique strongly predual of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
(compare to
[
18
, Corollary 2]
). In Section
4
, we see that
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is not a mere predual of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
but provides a procedure for linearizing the derivative of vector-valued Bloch mappings: for every complex Banach space
X
and every mapping
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, there exists a unique bounded linear operator
S_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\to X
such that
S_{f}\circ\Gamma=f^{\prime}
, where
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is the holomorphic mapping that maps
z
to
\gamma_{z}
(compare to
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
). Furthermore,
\left\|S_{f}\right\|=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
.

In Section
5
, we show that the concept of compact (weakly compact, finite-rank, approximable) Bloch mapping from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
is in concordance with the corresponding property of the associated bounded linear operator
S_{f}
from
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
to
X
. Furthermore, we apply the Davis–Figiel–Johnson–Pełczyński theorem
[
8
]
to factor the derivative of a weakly compact Bloch mapping through reflexive spaces. We complete this section dealing the problem as to when
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
has the approximation property. Finally, we introduce the notion of transpose mapping of a Bloch mapping, and present the Bloch analogues of the Schauder, Gantmacher and Gantmacher–Nakamura Theorems.

We now fix some notation.
X
and
Y
will always denote complex Banach spaces. To indicate that
X
and
Y
are isometrically isomorphic, we will write
X\cong Y
. We denote by
\mathrm{id}_{X}
the identity map of
X
. As usual,
B_{X}
,
S_{X}
and
X^{*}
denote the closed unit ball, the unit sphere and the topological dual space of
X
, respectively. For a set
A\subseteq X
,
\mathrm{Int}(A)
,
\mathrm{abco}(A)
and
\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(A)
stand for the interior, the absolutely convex hull and the norm-closed absolutely convex hull of
A
in
X
, respectively. If
E
and
F
are locally convex Hausdorff spaces,
\mathcal{L}(E;F)
or
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
denote the linear space of all continuous linear operators from
E
into
F
. Unless stated otherwise, if
E
and
F
are Banach spaces, we will understand that they are endowed with their canonical norm topology.
\mathbb{T}
stands for the set of all uni-modular complex numbers.

2.
Bloch-free Banach space of the complex unit disc

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{C}
. Let us recall that a function
f\colon\Omega\to X
is
holomorphic
if

f^{\prime}(a):=\lim_{z\to a}\frac{f(z)-f(a)}{z-a}

exists for all
a\in\Omega
. Let
\mathcal{H}(\Omega,X)
denote the space of holomorphic mappings from
\Omega
to
X
.

Let us recall the concept of peaking function in the setting of Bloch function spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq 1
is said to peak at a point
z\in\mathbb{D}
if
(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z)=1
, and for each open disc
D(z,\varepsilon)\subseteq\mathbb{D}
with
\varepsilon>0
, exists
\delta>0
such that
(1-|w|^{2})|f^{\prime}(w)|\leq 1-\delta
for all
w\in\mathbb{D}\backslash D(z,\varepsilon)
.

Colloquially, if
f
peaks at
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have that
(1-|w|^{2})|f^{\prime}(w)|
is uniformly less than 1 when
w
is away from
z
.

Proposition 2.2
.

For every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, the function
f_{z}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

f_{z}(w)=\frac{(1-|z|^{2})w}{1-\overline{z}w}\qquad(w\in\mathbb{D}),

belongs to the normalized little Bloch space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{z})=1
, and peaks at
z
.

Proof.

Note that
f_{z}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
with
f_{z}(0)=0
and

f^{\prime}_{z}(w)=\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{(1-\overline{z}w)^{2}}\qquad(w\in\mathbb{D}).

Since

(1-|w|^{2})|f^{\prime}_{z}(w)|=\frac{(1-|w|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}{|1-\overline{z}w|^{2}}=\frac{|1-\overline{z}w|^{2}-|z-w|^{2}}{|1-\overline{z}w|^{2}}=1-\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|^{2}\leq 1

for all
w\in\mathbb{D}
, it follows that
f_{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{z})\leq 1
. Further,
(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}_{z}(z)=1=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{z})
. From the inequality

(1-|w|^{2})|f^{\prime}_{z}(w)|=(1-|w|^{2})\frac{1-|z|^{2}}{|1-\overline{z}w|^{2}}\leq(1-|w|^{2})\frac{1}{|1-|z||w||^{2}}\qquad(w\in\mathbb{D}),

we infer that

\lim_{|w|\to 1^{-}}(1-|w|^{2})\left|f_{z}^{\prime}(w)\right|=0,

and thus
f_{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
. Finally, let
0<\varepsilon<1
. If
|w|<1
and
|w-z|\geq\varepsilon
, we have

(1-|w|^{2})|f^{\prime}_{z}(w)|=1-\left|\frac{z-w}{1-\overline{z}w}\right|^{2}\leq 1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{4},

and so
f_{z}
peaks at
z
.
∎

The first step to construct a Bloch molecule on the normalized Bloch space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is the following concept.

Definition 2.3
.

For each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, a Bloch atom of
\mathbb{D}
is the function
\gamma_{z}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\gamma_{z}(f)=f^{\prime}(z)\qquad(f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})).

We now show that
\gamma_{z}
is an element of the topological dual space of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then
\gamma_{z}
is in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
with
\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|=1/(1-|z|^{2})
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\gamma_{z}
is linear and

\left|\gamma_{z}(f)\right|=\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq\frac{p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)}{1-|z|^{2}}

for all
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. Hence
\gamma_{z}
is continuous with
\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|\leq 1/(1-|z|^{2})
. To see that
\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|=1/(1-|z|^{2})
, take the function
f_{z}\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. Since
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{z})=1
, we have

\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|\geq\left|\gamma_{z}(f_{z})\right|=\left|f^{\prime}_{z}(z)\right|=\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}.

∎

The preceding proposition justifies the following concept.

Definition 2.5
.

A normalized Bloch atom of
\mathbb{D}
is the function
\widehat{\gamma}_{z}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\widehat{\gamma}_{z}=(1-|z|^{2})\gamma_{z}.

We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
the set of all normalized Bloch atoms of
\mathbb{D}
, that is,

\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})=\left\{\widehat{\gamma}_{z}\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}.

We now introduce the concept of Bloch-free Banach space of
\mathbb{D}
. This terminology will be justified by a
universal extension property
of this space (see Theorem
4.1
).

Definition 2.6
.

The Bloch-free Banach space over
\mathbb{D}
, denoted
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, is the norm-closed linear hull of
\left\{\gamma_{z}\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
, that is,

\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})=\overline{\mathrm{lin}}\left\{\gamma_{z}\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}.

The elements of
\mathrm{lin}\{\gamma_{z}\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
are referred to as Bloch molecules of
\mathbb{D}
.

In consistency with the notation of Bloch atoms, we consider the mapping
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{D}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
defined by

\Gamma(z)=\gamma_{z}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

In order to prove its holomorphy, let us recall that given a Banach space
X
, a subset
N\subseteq B_{X^{*}}
is said to be
norming for
X
if

\left\|x\right\|=\sup\left\{\left|x^{*}(x)\right|\colon x^{*}\in N\right\}\qquad(x\in X).

Notice that
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is norming for
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
since

p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=\sup\left\{(1-|z|^{2})\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}=\sup\left\{\left|\widehat{\gamma}_{z}(f)\right|\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}

for every
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. A mapping
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is said to be
locally bounded
if

\sup\left\{\left\|f(z)\right\|\colon z\in K\right\}<\infty

for every compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{D}
.

Proposition 2.7
.

\Gamma
is a holomorphic mapping from
\mathbb{D}
to
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
with
\Gamma^{\prime}(z)(f)=f^{\prime\prime}(z)
for all
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Start by noting that for each
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, the
evaluation mapping

J_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathbb{C}
, defined by

J_{f}(\gamma)=\gamma(f)\qquad(\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})),

is linear and continuous with
\left\|J_{f}\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. It is clear that
\{J_{f}\colon f\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}\}
is a norming set for
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
since
\left\|\gamma\right\|=\sup\{\left|J_{f}(\gamma)\right|\colon f\in B_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})}\}
for each
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
.
On the other hand, given a compact set
K\subseteq\mathbb{D}
, there exists
r_{K}\in(0,1)
such that
|z|\leq r_{K}
for all
z\in K
.
Using Proposition
2.4
, we have

\left\|\Gamma(z)\right\|=\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|=\frac{1}{1-|z|^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{1-(r_{K})^{2}}

for all
z\in K
, and thus

\sup\left\{\left\|\Gamma(z)\right\|\colon z\in K\right\}\leq\frac{1}{1-(r_{K})^{2}}.

Hence
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is locally bounded. Since
J_{f}\circ\Gamma=f^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
for all
f\in B_{\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D})}
, then
\Gamma
is holomorphic by applying
[
4
, Proposition A.3]
.

Finally, given
a\in\mathbb{D}
and
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, we have

\left(\frac{\Gamma(z)-\Gamma(a)}{z-a}\right)(f)=\frac{\Gamma(z)(f)-\Gamma(a)(f)}{z-a}=\frac{f^{\prime}(z)-f^{\prime}(a)}{z-a}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D},\,z\neq a),

hence

\lim_{z\to a}\left(\frac{\Gamma(z)-\Gamma(a)}{z-a}\right)(f)=f^{\prime\prime}(a),

and thus
\Gamma^{\prime}(a)(f)=f^{\prime\prime}(a)
. ∎

We gather some properties of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
in the following.

Remarks 2.8
.

(1)

Since
\mathbb{D}
is separable and the mapping
\Gamma\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is continuous, the set
\Gamma(\mathbb{D})
is separable, hence its norm-closed linear hull,
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, is also separable.

(2)

\Gamma(\mathbb{D})
is a linearly independent subset of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
. Indeed, assume that
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}=0
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{D}
with
z_{j}\neq z_{k}
if
j,k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
j\neq k
. Then
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(z_{k})=0
for all
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. For each
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, let
Q_{j}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
be the polynomial defined by

Q_{j}(z)=\prod_{k=1,\\
k\neq j}^{n}\frac{z-z_{k}}{z_{j}-z_{k}},

and let
P_{j}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
be a polynomial such that
P^{\prime}_{j}=Q_{j}
and
P_{j}(0)=0
. Then
P_{j}
is in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
with
P^{\prime}_{j}(z_{k})=\delta_{jk}
for all
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, where
\delta
is the Kronecker delta. It follows that

0=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}P^{\prime}_{j}(z_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\delta_{jk}=\lambda_{j}

for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, as we wanted.

We also will use the following easy fact through the paper.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and let
F\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Then there exists a mapping
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
f(0)=0
such that
f^{\prime}=F
.

Proof.

Define
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
by

f(z)=\int_{0}^{z}F(s)\ ds\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

where the integral is the Bochner integral of the function
F\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
along some path in
\mathbb{D}
from
0
to
z
. First, notice that
f
does not depend on the path chosen since if
\alpha_{z},\beta_{z}
are paths in
\mathbb{D}
from
0
to
z
and
\phi\in X^{*}
, by classical Cauchy’s Theorem for star-shaped domains we have

\int_{\alpha_{z}}(\phi\circ F)(s)\ ds=\int_{\beta_{z}}(\phi\circ F)(s)\ ds,

that is,

\phi\left(\int_{\alpha_{z}}F(s)\ ds\right)=\phi\left(\int_{\beta_{z}}F(s)\ ds\right),

by the properties of the Bochner integral, and since
X^{*}
separates the points of
X
, it follows that

\int_{\alpha_{z}}F(s)\ ds=\int_{\beta_{z}}F(s)\ ds.

Clearly,
f(0)=0
. Now, given
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\phi\in X^{*}
, we have

(\phi\circ f)(z)=\phi\left(\int_{0}^{z}F(s)\ ds\right)=\int_{0}^{z}(\phi\circ F)(s)\ ds,

and again Cauchy’s Theorem for star-shaped domains gives

\phi(f^{\prime}(z))=(\phi\circ f)^{\prime}(z)=(\phi\circ F)(z)=\phi(F(z)),

and this yields that
f^{\prime}(z)=F(z)
.
∎

3.
Duality of complex-valued Bloch function spaces

Every Bloch molecule
\gamma
of
\mathbb{D}
has at least a representation as a linear combination of Bloch atoms of
\mathbb{D}
of the form
\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
and
z_{k}\in\mathbb{D}
for
k=1,\ldots,n
.

The norm on
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
will be the restriction of the norm of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
but it is convenient to regard that the restriction on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
of the norm of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
coincides with the following
projective norm
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For each
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
, define

\pi(\gamma)=\inf\left\{\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z_{k}\right|^{2}}\colon\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}\right\},

the infimum being taken over all representations of
\gamma
. Then
\pi
is a norm on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
. In fact,
\pi(\gamma)=\left\|\gamma\right\|
.

Proof.

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
and let
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}
be a representation of
\gamma
. Since
\gamma
is linear and

\left|\gamma(f)\right|=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}(f)\right|=\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(z_{k})\right|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|\lambda_{k}\right|\left|f^{\prime}(z_{k})\right|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\lambda_{k}|}{1-|z_{k}|^{2}}

for all
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, we deduce that

\left\|\gamma\right\|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z_{k}\right|^{2}}.

Since this holds for every representation of
\gamma
, it follows that
\left\|\gamma\right\|\leq\pi(\gamma)
. Suppose that
\pi(\gamma)=0
. Since
\left\|\gamma\right\|\leq\pi(\gamma)
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|
is a norm on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
, then
\gamma=0
.

We check that
\pi(\lambda\gamma)=\left|\lambda\right|\pi(\gamma)
. If
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, then
\lambda\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(\lambda\lambda_{k})\gamma_{z_{k}}
and so

\pi(\lambda\gamma)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda\lambda_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z_{k}\right|^{2}}=\left|\lambda\right|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z_{k}\right|^{2}}.

Since the representation of
\gamma
is arbitrary, this implies that
\pi(\lambda\gamma)\leq\left|\lambda\right|\pi(\gamma)
. If
\lambda=0
, we have
\pi(\lambda\gamma)=0=\left|\lambda\right|\pi(\gamma)
.
Assume that
\lambda\neq 0
. Similarly, we have
\pi(\gamma)=\pi(\lambda^{-1}(\lambda\gamma))\leq\left|\lambda^{-1}\right|\pi(\lambda\gamma)
, thus
\left|\lambda\right|\pi(\gamma)\leq\pi(\lambda\gamma)
and hence
\pi(\lambda\gamma)=\left|\lambda\right|\pi(\gamma)
.

We show that
\pi(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\leq\pi(\gamma)+\pi(\gamma^{\prime})
for all
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. Then there are representations
\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}
and
\gamma^{\prime}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{k}\gamma_{z^{\prime}_{k}}
such that
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}<\pi(\gamma)+\varepsilon/2
and
\sum_{k=1}^{m}\lambda^{\prime}_{k}\gamma_{z^{\prime}_{k}}<\pi(\gamma^{\prime})+\varepsilon/2
. We can concatenate these representations to get a representation
\sum_{k=1}^{n+m}\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{k}\gamma_{z^{\prime\prime}_{k}}
for
\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}
taking

(\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{k},z^{\prime\prime}_{k})=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}(\lambda_{k},z_{k})&\text{ if }&k=1,\ldots,n,\\
(\lambda^{\prime}_{k-n},z^{\prime}_{k-n})&\text{ if }&k=n+1,\ldots,n+m.\end{array}\end{cases}

It follows that

\displaystyle\pi(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n+m}\frac{\left|\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z^{\prime\prime}_{k}\right|^{2}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z^{\prime\prime}_{k}\right|^{2}}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{n+m}\frac{\left|\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z^{\prime\prime}_{k}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z_{k}\right|^{2}}+\sum_{k=n+1}^{n+m}\frac{\left|\lambda^{\prime}_{k-n}\right|}{1-\left|z^{\prime}_{k-n}\right|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\left|\lambda_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z_{k}\right|^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\frac{\left|\lambda^{\prime}_{k}\right|}{1-\left|z^{\prime}_{k}\right|^{2}}<\pi(\gamma)+\pi(\gamma^{\prime})+\varepsilon.

By the arbitrariness of
\varepsilon
, we deduce that
\pi(\gamma+\gamma^{\prime})\leq\pi(\gamma)+\pi(\gamma^{\prime})
. Hence
\pi
is a norm on
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
.

To prove that
\left\|\gamma\right\|\geq\pi(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
, suppose by contradiction that
\left\|\upsilon\right\|<1<\pi(\upsilon)
for some
\upsilon\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
. Denote

B=\left\{\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\colon\pi(\gamma)\leq 1\right\}.

Clearly,
B
is a convex and closed subset of
\left(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D})),\pi\right)
. Applying the Hahn–Banach Separation Theorem to
B
and
\{\upsilon\}
, we obtain a functional
\eta\in\left(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D})),\pi\right)^{*}
such that

1=\|\eta\|=\sup\left\{\mathrm{Re}(\eta(\gamma))\colon\gamma\in B\right\}<\mathrm{Re}(\eta(\upsilon)).

Define
F\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
by

F(z)=\eta(\gamma_{z})\qquad\left(z\in\mathbb{D}\right).

Notice that
F=\eta\circ\Gamma
is holomorphic because
\Gamma
is holomorphic and
\eta
is linear and continuous. Now, by Lemma
2.9
, there exists a holomorphic function
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
f^{\prime}(z)=\eta(\gamma_{z})
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
f(0)=0
. Since

(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|=(1-|z|^{2})\left|\eta(\gamma_{z})\right|\leq(1-|z|^{2})\left\|\eta\right\|\pi(\gamma_{z})=\left\|\eta\right\|=1

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we get that
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq 1
. For any
\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
, we get

\gamma(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(z_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\eta(\gamma_{z_{k}})=\eta\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}\right)=\eta(\gamma).

Therefore

\left\|\upsilon\right\|\geq|\upsilon(f)|\geq\mathrm{Re}(\upsilon(f))=\mathrm{Re}(\eta(\upsilon)),

so
\left\|\upsilon\right\|>1
, and this is a contradiction.
∎

The following theorem shows that
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is a predual of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is isometrically isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
, via the mapping
\Lambda\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
given by

\Lambda(f)(\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(z_{k})\qquad(f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),\;\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))).

Its inverse is the mapping
\Lambda^{-1}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
defined by

\Lambda^{-1}(\phi)(z)=\int_{[0,z]}\phi(\gamma_{w})\ dw\qquad\left(\phi\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*},\;z\in\mathbb{D}\right).

Proof.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. Define the mapping
\Lambda_{0}(f)\colon\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\to\mathbb{C}
by

\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(z_{k})\qquad(\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}).

Clearly,
\Lambda_{0}(f)
is linear. Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
and let
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}
be any representation of
\gamma
. Since

\left|\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)\right|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\lambda_{k}|\left|f^{\prime}(z_{k})\right|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\lambda_{k}|}{1-|z_{k}|^{2}},

we obtain that
\left|\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)\right|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\pi(\gamma)
, that is,
\left|\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)\right|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\left\|\gamma\right\|
by Lemma
3.1
. Hence
\Lambda_{0}(f)
is continuous with
\left\|\Lambda_{0}(f)\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
.

By the denseness of
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
in
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, there exists a unique continuous function
\Lambda(f)
from
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
to
\mathbb{C}
that extends
\Lambda_{0}(f)
. Further,
\Lambda(f)
is linear and
\left\|\Lambda(f)\right\|=\left\|\Lambda_{0}(f)\right\|
. Let
\Lambda\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
be the mapping so defined. Clearly,
\Lambda
is linear. In order to see that it is a surjective isometry, let
\phi
be an element of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
. Define
F\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
by

F(z)=\phi(\gamma_{z})\qquad\left(z\in\mathbb{D}\right).

Notice that
F=\phi\circ\Gamma\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D})
.
Now, by Lemma
2.9
, there exists a holomorphic function
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
with
f(0)=0
such that
f^{\prime}(z)=\phi(\gamma_{z})
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Since

(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(z)|=(1-|z|^{2})\left|\phi(\gamma_{z})\right|\leq(1-|z|^{2})\left\|\phi\right\|\left\|\gamma_{z}\right\|=\left\|\phi\right\|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we deduce that
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\left\|\phi\right\|
. For any
\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
, we get

\Lambda(f)(\gamma)=\Lambda_{0}(f)(\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(z_{k})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\phi(\gamma_{z_{k}})=\phi\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}\right)=\phi(\gamma).

Hence
\Lambda(f)=\phi
on the dense subspace
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
and, consequently,
\Lambda(f)=\phi
. Moreover,
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\left\|\phi\right\|=\left\|\Lambda(f)\right\|
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We can now use the classic notation of the dual pairing between
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
:

\left\langle\gamma,f\right\rangle=\gamma(f)\qquad(\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),\;f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})).

Corollary 3.3
.

The closed unit ball of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
coincides with the norm-closed absolutely convex hull of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\subseteq S_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}
, it is immediate that
\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq B_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}
. Conversely, suppose that there is a
\upsilon\in B_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}\setminus\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
. By the Bipolar Theorem, there exists a
\phi\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
such that
\left|\phi(\gamma)\right|\leq 1<\left|\phi(\upsilon)\right|
for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{abco}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
. By Theorem
3.2
,
\phi=\Lambda(f)
for some
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. It follows that

(1-|z|^{2})\left|f^{\prime}(z)\right|=\left|\Lambda(f)(\widehat{\gamma}_{z})\right|\leq 1<\left|\Lambda(f)(\upsilon)\right|\leq\left\|\Lambda(f)\right\|\left\|\upsilon\right\|\leq\left\|\Lambda(f)\right\|=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and we arrive at a contradiction. Hence
B_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
, as required.
∎

We have just seen that
B_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}=\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
. In fact, a stronger approximation result is valid: every element of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
can be represented as a series of normalized Bloch atoms, and one may choose the sum of the coefficients to be arbitrarily close to the norm.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exist sequences
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{1}
with
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{n}\right|<\left\|\gamma\right\|+\varepsilon
and
(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{D}^{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\gamma=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}}.

Moreover,

\left\|\gamma\right\|=\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{n}\right|\colon\gamma=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}},\ (\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{1},\;(z_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathbb{D}^{\mathbb{N}}\right\}.

The dual pairing satisfies the formula

\left\langle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}},f\right\rangle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})f^{\prime}(z_{n})\qquad\left(f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\right).

Proof.

Let
A=\left\{z_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
be a dense countable subset of
\mathbb{D}
and consider the normed space
\ell_{1}(A)
of all functions
f\colon A\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f(z_{n})|<\infty
with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f(z_{n})|.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
e_{z_{n}}\colon A\to\mathbb{C}
be the function defined by
e_{z_{n}}(w)=\chi_{z_{n}w}
, where
\chi
is the Kronecker delta. Clearly,
e_{z_{n}}\in\ell_{1}(A)
with
\left\|e_{z_{n}}\right\|_{1}=1
. Given
f\in\ell_{1}(A)
, the series
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(z_{n})\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}}
is absolutely convergent since

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left\|f(z_{k})\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{k}}\right\|=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|f(z_{k})\right|\left\|\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{k}}\right\|=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left|f(z_{k})\right|\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Since
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is a Banach space, then the series
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}f(z_{n})\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}}
converges in
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
and therefore we can consider the mapping
T\colon\ell_{1}(A)\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
defined by

T(f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(z_{n})\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}}\qquad\left(f=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(z_{n})e_{z_{n}}\in\ell_{1}(A)\right).

Clearly,
T\in\mathcal{L}(\ell_{1}(A),\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
with
\left\|T\right\|\leq 1
. Now, note that
T(e_{z_{n}})=\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}

and therefore

B_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}=\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))\subseteq\overline{T(B_{\ell_{1}(A)})}.

By a standard Open Mapping Theorem argument (see Lemma 2.24 and the remark which follows it in
[
9
]
), we actually have

\mathrm{Int}(B_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})})\subseteq T(\mathrm{Int}(B_{\ell_{1}(A)})).

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
and
\varepsilon>0
. Since
\gamma/(||\gamma||+\varepsilon)\in\mathrm{Int}(B_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})})
, there exists
f\in\ell_{1}(A)
with
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|f(z_{n})|<1
such that

\gamma/(||\gamma||+\varepsilon)=T(f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(z_{n})\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}}.

Take
\lambda_{n}=(||\gamma||+\varepsilon)f(z_{n})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and the first assertion is proved. The second assertion follows from the first one and the fact that

\left\|\gamma\right\|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left\|\lambda_{n}\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}}\right\|=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|\lambda_{n}\right|

for any such expression of
\gamma
as in the statement.

To check the dual pairing formula, consider such a representation of
\gamma
,
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{n}}
. Given
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, we must see that the series
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{n}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})f^{\prime}(z_{n})
converges to
\gamma(f)
. Denote, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\widehat{\gamma}_{z_{k}}\in\mathrm{lin}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
. Since
\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}(1-|z_{k}|^{2})f^{\prime}(z_{k})=\gamma_{n}(f)
, we have

\left|\gamma(f)-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}(1-|z_{k}|^{2})f^{\prime}(z_{k})\right|=|\gamma(f)-\gamma_{n}(f)|\leq\left\|\gamma-\gamma_{n}\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\qquad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

Since
||\gamma-\gamma_{n}||\to 0
, we get
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}(1-|z_{n}|^{2})f^{\prime}(z_{n})=\gamma(f)
.
∎

The isometric isomorphism
\Lambda\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
in Theorem
3.2
allows to consider the weak* topology on
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, that is, the topology

\left\{\Lambda^{-1}(U)\colon U\text{ is open in }\left(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*},w^{*}\right)\right\}.

On bounded subsets of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, the weak* topology on
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
agrees with the topology of pointwise convergence as we see in the next result whose proof is straightforward.

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
.

(1)

If
(f_{i})_{i\in I}\to f
weak* in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, then
(f_{i})_{i\in I}\to f
pointwise on
\mathbb{D}
.

(2)

If
(f_{i})_{i\in I}
is bounded in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
(f_{i})_{i\in I}\to f
pointwise on
\mathbb{D}
, then
(f_{i})_{i\in I}\to f
weak* in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
.
\hfill\Box

If
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic function, then the Pick–Schwarz Lemma asserts that

(1-|z|^{2})|h^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1-|h(z)|^{2}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

Let
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
and
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. Clearly,
(f\circ h)(0)=0
and

(1-|z|^{2})|(f\circ h)^{\prime}(z)|=(1-|z|^{2})|f^{\prime}(h(z))|h^{\prime}(z)|\leq(1-|h(z)|^{2})|f^{\prime}(h(z))|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, and thus
f\circ h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f\circ h)\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. So we have the following.

Proposition 3.6
.

For each holomorphic function
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
h(0)=0
, the composition operator
C_{h}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, defined by
C_{h}(f)=f\circ h
for all
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, is linear and continuous with
||C_{h}||\leq 1
. Moreover,
||C_{h}||\geq p_{\mathcal{B}}(C_{h}(\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{D}}))=p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)
.
\hfill\Box

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
. There exists a unique operator
\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
such that
\widehat{h}\circ\Gamma=h^{\prime}\cdot(\Gamma\circ h)
. In fact,
(\widehat{h})^{*}=\Lambda\circ C_{h}\circ\Lambda^{-1}
and therefore
||\widehat{h}||=||C_{h}||
.

Proof.

First, we claim that
\Lambda\circ C_{h}\circ\Lambda^{-1}
is weak*-to-weak* continuous from
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
into itself. Let
(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
be a net in
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
and
\phi\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
. Assume that
(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}\to\phi
weak* in
\mathcal{G}(D)^{*}
. By
[
16
, Corollary 2.6.10]
,
(\phi_{i})_{i\in I}
is norm-bounded in
\mathcal{G}(D)^{*}
. Clearly,
(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i}))_{i\in I}\to\Lambda^{-1}(\phi)
weak* in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. Hence
(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i}))_{i\in I}\to\Lambda^{-1}(\phi)
pointwise on
\mathbb{D}
by Corollary
3.5
(1). In particular,
(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i})\circ h)_{i\in I}\to\Lambda^{-1}(\phi)\circ h
pointwise on
\mathbb{D}
. Since
(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i})\circ h)_{i\in I}
is norm-bounded because

p_{\mathcal{B}}(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i})\circ h)\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i}))=\left\|\Lambda(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i}))\right\|=\left\|\phi_{i}\right\|

for all
i\in I
, Corollary
3.5
(2) guarantees that
(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i})\circ h)_{i\in I}\to\Lambda^{-1}(\phi)\circ h
weak* in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, that is,
(C_{h}(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i})))_{i\in I}\to C_{h}(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi))
weak* in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
. Finally,
(\Lambda(C_{h}(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi_{i}))))_{i\in I}\to\Lambda(C_{h}(\Lambda^{-1}(\phi)))
weak* in
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
and this proves our claim.

By
[
16
, Corollaries 3.1.11 and 3.1.5]
, there is a unique operator
\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
such that
(\widehat{h})^{*}=\Lambda\circ C_{h}\circ\Lambda^{-1}
. It is clear that
||\widehat{h}||=||(\widehat{h})^{*}||=||C_{h}||
. Given
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we have

(\widehat{h})^{*}(\Lambda(f))(\gamma_{z})=(\Lambda(f)\circ\widehat{h})(\gamma_{z})=\Lambda(f)(\widehat{h}(\gamma_{z})),

and

\displaystyle\Lambda\circ C_{h}\circ\Lambda^{-1}(\Lambda(f))(\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=\Lambda(C_{h}(f))(\gamma_{z})=\Lambda(f\circ h)(\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=(f\circ h)^{\prime}(z)=f^{\prime}(h(z))h^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\Lambda(f)(\gamma_{h(z)})h^{\prime}(z)=\Lambda(f)(h^{\prime}(z)\gamma_{h(z)}).

Hence

\Lambda(f)(\widehat{h}(\gamma_{z}))=\Lambda(f)(h^{\prime}(z)\gamma_{h(z)})

for all
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. By the surjectivity of
\Lambda\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
, this means that

\phi(\widehat{h}(\gamma_{z}))=\phi(h^{\prime}(z)\gamma_{h(z)})

for all
\phi\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Since
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
separates points of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, this implies that
\widehat{h}(\gamma_{z})=h^{\prime}(z)\gamma_{h(z)}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, that is,
\widehat{h}\circ\Gamma=h^{\prime}\cdot(\Gamma\circ h)
.
∎

Let us recall (see
[
19
, B.3]
) that if
X
and
Y
are Banach spaces and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, then
T
is called a
metric injection or an isometry
if
\left\|T(x)\right\|=\left\|x\right\|
for all
x\in X
, and
T
is called a
metric surjection
if
T
is surjective and
\left\|T(x)\right\|=\inf\left\{\left\|y\right\|\colon T(y)=T(x)\right\}
for all
x\in X
.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function such that
h(0)=0
.

(1)

If
g\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic function with
g(0)=0
, then
\widehat{g\circ h}=\widehat{g}\circ\widehat{h}
.

(2)

\widehat{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{D}}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}
.

(3)

If
h
is bijective and
h^{-1}
is holomorphic, then
\widehat{h}
is a topological isomorphism from
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
onto itself with
(\widehat{h})^{-1}=\widehat{h^{-1}}
.

(4)

\widehat{h}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is a metric surjection if and only if
C_{h}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is a metric injection.

(5)

4.
Linearization of derivatives of Banach-valued Bloch mappings

Next we state the universal extension property of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every complex Banach space
X
and every Bloch mapping
f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
with
f(0)=0
, there exists a unique bounded linear operator
S_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\to X
such that
S_{f}\circ\Gamma=f^{\prime}
, that is, the diagram

\mathbb{D}
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
X
f^{\prime}
\Gamma
S_{f}

commutes. Furthermore,
\left\|S_{f}\right\|=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Define the mapping
R_{f}\colon\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))\to X
by

R_{f}(\gamma)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}f^{\prime}(z_{k})\qquad\left(\gamma=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\lambda_{k}\gamma_{z_{k}}\right).

Clearly,
R_{f}
is linear and
R_{f}\circ\Gamma=f^{\prime}
.
Since

\left\|R_{f}(\gamma)\right\|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}|\lambda_{k}|\left\|f^{\prime}(z_{k})\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|\lambda_{k}|}{1-|z_{k}|^{2}}\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\pi(\gamma)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\left\|\gamma\right\|

for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
, it follows that
R_{f}
is continuous with
\left\|R_{f}\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. Since
\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
is norm-dense in
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, there exists a unique continuous mapping
S_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\to X
such that
S_{f}(\gamma)=R_{f}(\gamma)
for all
\gamma\in\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
. Further,
S_{f}
is linear and
\left\|S_{f}\right\|=\left\|R_{f}\right\|
. Hence
S_{f}\circ\Gamma=f^{\prime}
and
\left\|S_{f}\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. Conversely, we have
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\left\|S_{f}\right\|
because

\left\|S_{f}\right\|\geq\left\|S_{f}(\widehat{\gamma}_{z})\right\|=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|S_{f}(\gamma_{z})\right\|=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|(S_{f}\circ\Gamma)(z)\right\|=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Theorem

allows to identify the spaces
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)

Corollary 4.2

The mapping

is an isometric isomorphism from

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)

We may study holomorphic mappings on Banach spaces in terms of power series expansions (see
[
4
, Appendix A]
).

Let
X
be a Banach space and let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}
be an open set. If
f\colon\Omega\to X
is holomorphic, by Taylor’s Theorem for vector-valued holomorphic mappings,
for each
a\in\Omega
there exist a closed disc
\overline{D}(a,r_{a})\subseteq\Omega
and a sequence
\{a_{m}^{(a)}\}_{m\geq 0}
in
X
given by

a_{m}^{(a)}:=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|z-a|=r_{a}}\frac{f(z)}{(z-a)^{m+1}}\ dz,

such that

where the series converges uniformly for

. This series is known as the

at

and by analogy with the complex case, it is represented as

f(z)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}d^{m}f(a)(z-a).

Proposition 4.4
.

\mathbb{C}
is topologically isomorphic to a complemented subspace of
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

Let

Since

is holomorphic, let

S(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=r}\frac{\Gamma(wz)}{w^{2}}\ dw\qquad(z\in\mathbb{C}),

where

\displaystyle=S_{f}\circ d^{1}\Gamma(0)(z)=d^{1}(S_{f}\circ\Gamma)(0)(z)

5.
Bloch mappings with compact type Bloch range

A linear operator between Banach spaces
T\colon X\to Y
is said to be
compact (weakly compact)
if
T(B_{X})
is relatively compact (respectively, relatively weakly compact) in
Y
. We denote by
\mathcal{K}(X,Y)
,
\mathcal{W}(X,Y)
,
\mathcal{F}(X,Y)
and
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(X,Y)
the spaces of compact linear operators, weakly compact linear operators, bounded finite-rank linear operators and approximable linear operators from
X
into
Y
, respectively.

In this section, we introduce and study the analogues of these concepts in the setting of Bloch spaces.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The Bloch range of
f
, denoted
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
, is the set

\left\{(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z)\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}\subseteq X.

Note that
f
is Bloch if and only if
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
is a bounded subset of
X
.

The mapping
f
is said to be compact Bloch (weakly compact Bloch) if
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
is a relatively compact (resp., relatively weakly compact) subset of
X
.

We denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D},X)
the linear spaces of compact Bloch mappings and weakly compact Bloch mappings from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
that preserve the zero, respectively.

Other classes of Bloch mappings closely related to the previous ones are the following.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
X
a complex Banach space. A Bloch mapping
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
has finite dimensional Bloch rank if the linear hull of the set
\left\{(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z)\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}
is a finite dimensional subspace of
X
. In that case, we define the Bloch rank
\mathcal{B}
-
\mathrm{rank}(f)
of
f
to be the dimension of this subspace.

A Bloch mapping
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
is said to be approximable if it is the limit in the Bloch norm
p_{\mathcal{B}}
of a sequence of finite-rank Bloch mappings of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
.

We denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D},X)
and
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
the spaces of finite-rank Bloch mappings and approximable Bloch mappings
f
from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
such that
f(0)=0
, respectively.

5.1.
Invariance by Möbius transformations

The
Möbius group of
\mathbb{D}
, denoted by
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, consists of all one-to-one holomorphic functions
\phi
that map
\mathbb{D}
onto itself. Each
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
has the form
\phi=\lambda\phi_{a}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
and
a\in\mathbb{D}
, where

\phi_{a}(z)=\frac{a-z}{1-\overline{a}z}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D}).

It is easy to check that

\left|\phi_{a}^{\prime}(z)\right|=\frac{1-|a|^{2}}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}}

and

1-|\phi_{a}(z)|^{2}=\frac{(1-|a|^{2})(1-|z|^{2})}{|1-\overline{a}z|^{2}}=(1-|z|^{2})\left|\phi_{a}^{\prime}(z)\right|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

Let us recall (see
[
3
]
) that for a complex Banach space
X
, a linear space
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)
of holomorphic mappings from
\mathbb{D}
into
X
endowed with a seminorm
p_{\mathcal{A}}
is said to be
Möbius-invariant
if

(1)

\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)\subseteq\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{D},X)
and there exists a constant
c>0
such that
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq cp_{\mathcal{A}}(f)
for all
f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)
,

(2)

f\circ\phi\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
p_{\mathcal{A}}(f\circ\phi)=p_{\mathcal{A}}(f)
for all
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
and
f\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D},X)
.

For
f\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},X)
and
\phi\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})
, we have

(1-|z|^{2})(f\circ\phi)^{\prime}(z)=(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(\phi(z))\phi^{\prime}(z)=(1-\left|\phi(z)\right|^{2})f^{\prime}(\phi(z))\frac{\phi^{\prime}(z)}{|\phi^{\prime}(z)|}

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Hence
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f\circ\phi)\subseteq\mathbb{T}\,\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
, and therefore, if
f
is (weakly) compact Bloch, it is clear that
f\circ\phi
is (weakly) compact Bloch with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f\circ\phi)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. Hence we have proved the following.

Proposition 5.3
.

The spaces of compact Bloch mappings and weakly compact Bloch mappings from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
are Möbius-invariant.
\hfill\qed

5.2.
Linearization and factorization

We now study the relationship between the Bloch compactness of
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
and the compactness of its linearization
S_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

f
is compact Bloch.

(2)

S_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\to X
is compact.

In this case,
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=\left\|S_{f}\right\|
. Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto S_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
.

Proof.

First, using Theorem
4.1
, we derive the relations:

\displaystyle\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=S_{f}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))

\displaystyle\subseteq S_{f}(B_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})})=S_{f}(\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})))

\displaystyle\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(S_{f}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})))=\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)).

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: If
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, we deduce that
S_{f}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
with
\left\|S_{f}\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
by applying the second inclusion above and the fact that
\overline{\mathrm{abco}}(\overline{S_{f}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))})
is compact in
X
by Mazur’s Compactness Theorem.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: If
S_{f}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
, we obtain that
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\left\|S_{f}\right\|
by using the first inclusion above. This completes the proof of the equivalence and its consequence.

The last assertion of the statement follows immediately using Corollary
4.2
and what was proved above.
∎

As an application of Theorem
5.4
, we show that the derivatives of the members of the Bloch space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}
are generated by composition with the Banach operator ideal
\mathcal{K}
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is compact Bloch.

(2)

f^{\prime}=T\circ g^{\prime}
, where
Y
is a complex Banach space,
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
and
T\in\mathcal{K}(Y,X)
.

In this case,
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=\inf\{\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\}
, where the infimum is taken over all factorizations of
f^{\prime}
as above, and this infimum is attained at
T=S_{f}
and
g=\Gamma
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: If
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, we have the factorization
f^{\prime}=S_{f}\circ\Gamma
, where
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is a complex Banach space,
S_{f}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
by Theorems
4.1
and
5.4
. Now, by Lemma
2.9
, there is a holomorphic mapping
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
with
h(0)=0
satisfying that
h^{\prime}(z)=\Gamma(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Since

(1-|z|^{2})\left\|h^{\prime}(z)\right\|=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|\Gamma(z)\right\|=1

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, we get that
h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)=1
. Thus we obtain the required factorization
f^{\prime}=S_{f}\circ h^{\prime}
. Moreover, we have
\inf\left\{\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\right\}\leq\left\|S_{f}\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(h)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: Assume that
f^{\prime}=T\circ g^{\prime}
, where
Y
is a complex Banach space,
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
and
T\in\mathcal{K}(Y,X)
. Since
g^{\prime}=S_{g}\circ\Gamma
by Theorem
4.1
, it follows that
f^{\prime}=T\circ S_{g}\circ\Gamma
which implies that
S_{f}=T\circ S_{g}
, and thus
S_{f}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
by the ideal property of
\mathcal{K}
. By Theorem
5.4
, we obtain that
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with

p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=\left\|S_{f}\right\|=\left\|T\circ S_{g}\right\|\leq\left\|T\right\|\left\|S_{g}\right\|=\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(g),

and taking the infimum over all representations of
f^{\prime}
, we deduce that
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\inf\{\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\}
.
∎

The Davis–Figiel–Johnson–Pełczyński Factorization Theorem
[
8
]
states that any weakly compact linear operator between Banach spaces factors through a reflexive Banach space. We now extend this result to weakly compact Bloch mappings.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is Bloch weakly compact.

(2)

S_{f}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\to X
is weakly compact.

(3)

There exist a reflexive Banach space
Y
, an operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(Y,X)
and a mapping
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
such that
f^{\prime}=T\circ g^{\prime}
.

Furthermore,
f\mapsto S_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
.

Proof.

Since the norm closure and weak closure of the convex hull of a subset of a normed space coincide
[
16
, Corollary 2.5.18]
and the norm-closed convex hull of a weakly compact subset of a Banach space is itself weakly compact
[
16
, Theorem 2.8.14]
, a similar proof to that of Theorem
5.4
yields that
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
.

If (2) holds, applying the Davis–Figiel–Johnson–Pełczyński Theorem, there exist a reflexive Banach space
Y
and operators
T\in\mathcal{L}(Y,X)
and
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),Y)
such that
S_{f}=T\circ S
. Since
S\circ\Gamma\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},Y)
, by Lemma
2.9
we can find a mapping
g\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},Y)
with
g(0)=0
such that
g^{\prime}=S\circ\Gamma
. It follows that
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
since

(1-|z|^{2})\left\|g^{\prime}(z)\right\|=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|S(\Gamma(z))\right\|\leq(1-|z|^{2})\left\|S\right\|\left\|\Gamma(z)\right\|=\left\|S\right\|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Moreover,
f^{\prime}=S_{f}\circ\Gamma=T\circ S\circ\Gamma=T\circ g^{\prime}
, and this proves (3).

Finally, (3) implies (1) because
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=T(\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(g))
,
where
T
is weak-to-weak continuous
and
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(g)

is relatively weakly compact in
Y
since it is a bounded subset of the reflexive Banach space
Y
.
∎

Let us recall that if
A
is a set and
X
is a linear space, then a mapping
f\colon A\to X
is said to have
finite dimensional rank
if the linear hull of its range is a finite dimensional subspace of
X
in whose case the
rank of
f
, denoted by
\mathrm{rank}(f)
, is defined as the dimension of
\mathrm{lin}(f(A))
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

f
has finite dimensional Bloch rank.

(2)

f^{\prime}
has finite dimensional rank.

(3)

S_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
has finite rank.

In that case,
\mathrm{lin}(f^{\prime}(\mathbb{D}))=S_{f}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
and
\mathcal{B}
-
\mathrm{rank}(f)=\mathrm{rank}(f^{\prime})=\mathrm{rank}(S_{f})
. Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto S_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
.

Proof.

First, observe that

\mathrm{lin}\left\{(1-|z|^{2})f^{\prime}(z)\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}=\mathrm{lin}\left\{f^{\prime}(z)\colon z\in\mathbb{D}\right\}

for any function
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
and
\mathcal{B}
-
\mathrm{rank}(f)=\mathrm{rank}(f^{\prime})
follow from this observation. We now prove
(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
. If
f^{\prime}
has finite dimensional rank, then
\mathrm{lin}(f^{\prime}(\mathbb{D}))
is finite dimensional, hence it is closed in
X
. Using Theorem
4.1
, we have

S_{f}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))=S_{f}(\overline{\mathrm{lin}}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D})))\subseteq\overline{S_{f}(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D})))}=\overline{\mathrm{lin}}(S_{f}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D})))=\overline{\mathrm{lin}}(f^{\prime}(\mathbb{D}))=\mathrm{lin}(f^{\prime}(\mathbb{D}))

and hence
S_{f}
has finite rank. Conversely, if
S_{f}
has finite rank, then
f^{\prime}
has finite dimensional rank since

\mathrm{lin}(f^{\prime}(\mathbb{D}))=\mathrm{lin}(S_{f}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D})))=S_{f}(\mathrm{lin}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D})))\subseteq S_{f}(\overline{\mathrm{lin}}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D})))=S_{f}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})).

∎

5.3.
Inclusions

Note that
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D},X)
is a linear subspace of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. In fact, we have:

Proposition 5.8
.

Every approximable Bloch mapping from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
is compact Bloch.

Proof.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Then there is a
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D},X)
such that
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{n}-f)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Since
S_{f_{n}}\in\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
by Theorem
5.7
,
\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)\subseteq\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
and
\left\|S_{f_{n}}-S_{f}\right\|=\left\|S_{f_{n}-f}\right\|=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f_{n}-f)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
by Corollary
4.2

, we deduce that
S_{f}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
by
[
16
, Corollary 3.4.9]
, and so
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
by Theorem
5.4
.
∎

An application of Theorem
5.7
and Corollary
4.2
shows the connection of an approximable Bloch mapping with its linearization:

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Then
f
is approximable Bloch if and only if
S_{f}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
is approximable. Furthermore, the mapping
f\mapsto S_{f}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\overline{\mathcal{F}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\overline{\mathcal{F}}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
.
\hfill\qed

Let us recall that a Banach space
X
is said to have the
approximation property
if given a compact set
K\subseteq X
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is an operator
T\in\mathcal{F}(X,X)
such that
\left\|T(x)-x\right\|<\varepsilon
for every
x\in K
. On the approximation property in holomorphic function spaces, we refer to results of Aron and Schottelonher
[
5
]
and Mujica
[
17
]
.

Grothendieck
[
10
]
proved that a dual Banach space
X^{*}
has the approximation property if and only if given a Banach space
Y
, an operator
S\in\mathcal{K}(X,Y)
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is an operator
T\in\mathcal{F}(X,Y)
such that
\left\|T-S\right\|<\varepsilon
. Since
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is a dual space, combining the Grothendieck’s result with Theorems
5.7
and
5.4
, we next give a necessary and sufficient condition for the space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
have the approximation property.

Corollary 5.10
.

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
has the approximation property if and only if, for each complex Banach space
X
, every compact Bloch mapping from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
is approximable Bloch.
\hfill\qed

5.4.
Banach ideal property

We now formalize the notion of an ideal of Bloch mappings with a definition inspired by the notion of operator ideal between Banach spaces
[
19
]
.

Definition 5.11
.

An ideal of normalized Bloch mappings (or simply, a normalized Bloch ideal) is a subclass
\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}
of the class of all normalized Bloch mappings
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}
such that for each complex Banach space
X
, the components

\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X):=\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)

satisfy:

(I1)

\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
is a linear subspace of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
,

(I2)

For any
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x\in X
, the mapping
g\cdot x\colon z\mapsto g(z)x
from
\mathbb{D}
to
X
is in
\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
,

(I3)

The ideal property: if
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
,
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
where
Y
is a complex Banach space, then
T\circ f\circ h
belongs to
\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
.

A normalized Bloch ideal
\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}
is said to be normed (Banach) if there is a function
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}\colon\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}\to\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
such that for every complex Banach space
X
, the following three conditions are satisfied:

(N1)

(\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X),\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}})
is a normed (Banach) space with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\leq\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
,

(N2)

\left\|g\cdot x\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}=p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
for all
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x\in X
,

(N3)

If
Y
is a complex Banach space,
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
,
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
and
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, then
\left\|T\circ f\circ h\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}\leq\left\|T\right\|\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}
.

A normed normalized Bloch ideal
[\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}]
is said to be:

(I)

Injective if for any mapping
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, any complex Banach space
Y
and any metric injection
\iota\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
, we have that
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}=\left\|\iota\circ f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}
whenever
\iota\circ f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
.

(S)

Surjective if for any complex Banach space
X
, any
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
and any holomorphic function
\pi\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
with
\pi(0)=0
such that
\widehat{\pi}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
is a metric surjection, we have that
f\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}=\left\|f\circ\pi\right\|_{\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}}
whenever
f\circ\pi\in\mathcal{I}^{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}}(\mathbb{D},X)
.

The condition imposed on
\widehat{\pi}
in the surjectivity property is closely related to isometric composition operators on
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, studied by Martín and Vukotić
[
15
]
.

Remark 5.12
.

In view of Corollary
3.8
and
[
15
, Theorem 1.1]
, if
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic function such that
h(0)=0
, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\widehat{h}\colon\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
is a metric surjection.

(2)

C_{h}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is a metric injection.

(3)

Either
h
is a rotation of the identity or
h
has the property (M): for every
a\in\mathbb{D}
there exists a sequence
(z_{n})
in
\mathbb{D}
such that
(|z_{n}|)\to 1
,
(h(z_{n}))\to a
, and
((1-|z_{n}|^{2})|h^{\prime}(z_{n})|/(1-|h(z_{n})|^{2}))\to 1
.

We present some examples of such normalized Bloch ideals.

Proposition 5.13
.

[\widehat{\mathcal{B}},p_{\mathcal{B}}]
is a Banach ideal of normalized Bloch mappings. Furthermore, if
X
and
Y
are complex Banach spaces,
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
is a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
and
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, then
S_{T\circ f\circ h}=T\circ S_{f}\circ\widehat{h}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.2
,
(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X),p_{\mathcal{B}})
is a Banach space. Given
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x\in X
, it is clear that
g\cdot x\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is holomorphic with
(g\cdot x)^{\prime}=g^{\prime}\cdot x
and
(g\cdot x)(0)=g(0)x=0
. Moreover,
g\cdot x
is Bloch with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(g\cdot x)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
. Now, let
T
,
h
and
f
be as in the statement. Clearly,
(T\circ f\circ h)(0)=0
and
T\circ f\circ h\in\mathcal{H}(\mathbb{D},Y)
with

(T\circ f\circ h)^{\prime}=T\circ(f\circ h)^{\prime}=T\circ[h^{\prime}\cdot(f^{\prime}\circ h)],

and since

(1-|z|^{2})|h^{\prime}(z)|\leq 1-|h(z)|^{2}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{D})

by the Pick–Schwarz Lemma, we deduce that
T\circ f\circ h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(T\circ f\circ h)\leq\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. This completes the proof of the first assertion of the statement. To prove the second assertion, note that, by Corollary
3.7
, there is an operator
\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
with
||\widehat{h}||\leq 1
such that
\widehat{h}\circ\Gamma=h^{\prime}\cdot(\Gamma\circ h)
. Since

\displaystyle(T\circ f\circ h)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=T\circ[h^{\prime}\cdot(f^{\prime}\circ h)]=T\circ[h^{\prime}\cdot(S_{f}\circ\Gamma\circ h)]

\displaystyle=T\circ[S_{f}(h^{\prime}\cdot(\Gamma\circ h))]=T\circ[S_{f}\circ(\widehat{h}\circ\Gamma)]

\displaystyle=(T\circ S_{f}\circ\widehat{h})\circ\Gamma

with
T\circ S_{f}\circ\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),Y)
, we get that
S_{T\circ f\circ h}=T\circ S_{f}\circ\widehat{h}
by Theorem
4.1
.
∎

Proposition 5.14
.

[\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}},p_{\mathcal{B}}]
is a Banach ideal of normalized Bloch mappings whenever
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K},\mathcal{W},\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. Furthermore, the ideal
[\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}},p_{\mathcal{B}}]
is surjective and injective for
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K},\mathcal{W}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a complex Banach space and
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K},\mathcal{W},\overline{\mathcal{F}}
. We will show that
(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathbb{D},X),p_{\mathcal{B}})
enjoys the required conditions:

(N1)
(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathbb{D},X),p_{\mathcal{B}})
is a Banach space by Theorems
5.4
,
5.6
and
5.9
.

(N2) Given
g\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
and
x\in X
, it is clear that
\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(g\cdot x)=\mathrm{rang}_{\mathcal{B}}(g)x
and thus
g\cdot x\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\mathbb{D},X)\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(g\cdot x)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(g)\left\|x\right\|
.

(N3) Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
,
h\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function with
h(0)=0
and
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Then
S_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
by Theorems
5.4
,
5.6
and
5.9
, hence
S_{T\circ f\circ h}=T\circ S_{f}\circ\widehat{h}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),Y)
by Proposition
5.13
and the operator ideal property of
\mathcal{I}
, and thus
T\circ f\circ h\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
with

p_{\mathcal{B}}(T\circ f\circ h)=\left\|S_{T\circ f\circ h}\right\|=\left\|T\circ S_{f}\circ\widehat{h}\right\|\leq\left\|T\right\|p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)

again by the aforementioned theorems.

Assume now that
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{K},\mathcal{W}
.

(I) Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
and let
\iota\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
be a metric injection, where
Y
is a complex Banach space. Assume that
\iota\circ f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathbb{D},Y)
. Then
\iota\circ S_{f}=S_{\iota\circ f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),Y)
. Since the operator ideal
[\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|]
is injective by
[
19
, 4.6.12]
, it follows that
S_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
with
\left\|S_{f}\right\|=\left\|\iota\circ S_{f}\right\|
or, equivalently,
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(\iota\circ f)
.

(S) Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
and let
\pi\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
be a holomorphic function with
\pi(0)=0
such that
\widehat{\pi}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}))
is a metric surjection. Assume that
f\circ\pi\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Since
S_{f}\circ\widehat{\pi}=S_{f\circ\pi}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
and the operator ideal
[\mathcal{I},\left\|\cdot\right\|]
is surjective by
[
19
, 4.7.13]
, we have that
S_{f}\in\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
with
\left\|S_{f}\right\|=\left\|S_{f}\circ\widehat{\pi}\right\|
, hence
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{I}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f\circ\pi)
.
∎

Using Theorem
5.7
, a proof similar to that of Proposition
5.14
yields the following.

Proposition 5.15
.

[\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{F}},p_{\mathcal{B}}]
is a surjective and injective normed normalized Bloch ideal.
\hfill\Box

5.5.
Transposition

We state the analogues for Bloch mappings of the results due to Schauder, Gantmacher and Nakamura on the compactness and weak compactness of the adjoint of a bounded linear operator between Banach spaces.

The first step is to present a Bloch version of the concept of adjoint operator. Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Given
x^{*}\in X^{*}
, we have
(x^{*}\circ f)(0)=0
and

\displaystyle(1-|z|^{2})\left\|(x^{*}\circ f)^{\prime}(z)\right\|

\displaystyle=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|x^{*}(f^{\prime}(z))\right\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-|z|^{2})\left\|x^{*}\right\|\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\left\|x^{*}\right\|

for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Hence
x^{*}\circ f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(x^{*}\circ f)\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)\left\|x^{*}\right\|
. This justifies the following.

Definition 5.16
.

Given
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, the Bloch transpose of
f
is the mapping
f^{t}\colon X^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
, given by

f^{t}(x^{*})=x^{*}\circ f\qquad(x^{*}\in X^{*}).

Clearly,
f^{t}
is linear and continuous with
||f^{t}||\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. In fact,
||f^{t}||=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. Indeed, for
0<\varepsilon<p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
, take
z\in\mathbb{D}
such that
(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|>p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)-\varepsilon
. By the Hahn–Banach Theorem, there exists
y^{*}\in X^{*}
with
\left\|y^{*}\right\|=1
such that
\left|y^{*}(f^{\prime}(z))\right|=\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|
. We have

\displaystyle\left\|f^{t}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\sup_{x^{*}\neq 0}\frac{p_{\mathcal{B}}(f^{t}(x^{*}))}{\left\|x^{*}\right\|}\geq\frac{p_{\mathcal{B}}(y^{*}\circ f)}{\left\|y^{*}\right\|}

\displaystyle\geq(1-|z|^{2})\left|y^{*}(f^{\prime}(z))\right|=(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|>p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)-\varepsilon.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, one obtains
||f^{t}||\geq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
, as desired. Finally, note that

\displaystyle(\Lambda\circ f^{t})(x^{*})(\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=\Lambda(f^{t}(x^{*}))(\gamma_{z})=\Lambda(x^{*}\circ f)(\gamma_{z})

\displaystyle=(x^{*}\circ f)^{\prime}(z)=x^{*}(f^{\prime}(z))

\displaystyle=x^{*}(S_{f}(\gamma_{z}))=(S_{f})^{*}(x^{*})(\gamma_{z})

for all
x^{*}\in X^{*}
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
, where
(S_{f})^{*}\colon X^{*}\to\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*}
is the adjoint operator of
S_{f}
. Since
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})=\overline{\mathrm{lin}}(\Gamma(\mathbb{D}))
, we deduce that
\Lambda\circ f^{t}=(S_{f})^{*}
and thus
f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}
. So we have:

Proposition 5.17
.

If
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
, then
f^{t}\in\mathcal{L}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
with
||f^{t}||=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
and
f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}
.
\hfill\Box

We next see that the mapping
f\mapsto f^{t}
identifies
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with the subspace of
\mathcal{L}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
formed by all weak*-to-weak* continuous linear operators from
X^{*}
into
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
16
, Corollary 3.1.12]
).

Proposition 5.18
.

f\mapsto f^{t}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),w^{*}))
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Hence
f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),w^{*}))
by Theorem
4.1
and
[
16
, Theorem 3.1.11]
.
We have
||f^{t}||=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
by Proposition
5.17
. It remains to show the surjectivity of the mapping in the statement. Take
T\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),w^{*}))
. Then the mapping
\Lambda\circ T
belongs to
\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*},w^{*}))

and, by
[
16
, Theorem 3.1.11]
, there is a
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
such that
S^{*}=\Lambda\circ T
. By Corollary
4.2
, there exists
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
such that
S_{f}=S
. Hence
T=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}=f^{t}
, as desired.
∎

The equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
in the next result is a version of Schauder Theorem for compact Bloch mappings.

Theorem 5.19
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is compact Bloch.

(2)

f^{t}\colon X^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is compact.

(3)

f^{t}\colon X^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is bounded-weak*-to-norm continuous.

(4)

f^{t}\colon X^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is compact and bounded-weak*-to-weak continuous.

(5)

f^{t}\colon X^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is compact and weak*-to-weak continuous.

Proof.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
: applying Theorem
5.4
, the Schauder Theorem
[
16
, Theorem 3.4.15]
and
[
16
, Proposition 3.4.10]
, we have

\displaystyle f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow S_{f}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(S_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{K}(X^{*},\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{K}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})).

(1)\Leftrightarrow(3)
: similarly, one obtains

\displaystyle f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow S_{f}\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(S_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})),

by Theorem
5.4
and
[
16
, Theorem 3.4.16]
.

(3)\Leftrightarrow(4)\Leftrightarrow(5)
: it follows directly from
[
13
, Proposition 3.1]
.
∎

We are now in position to identify
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with the subspace of
\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),w^{*}))
consisting of all bounded-weak*-to-norm continuous linear operators from
X^{*}
into
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proposition 5.20
.

f\mapsto f^{t}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Then
f^{t}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
by Theorem
5.19
and
||f^{t}||=p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
by Proposition
5.17
. To prove the surjectivity, take
T\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
. Then
\Lambda\circ T\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*})
. If
Q_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}
denotes the natural injection from
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
into
\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{**}
, then
Q_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}(\gamma)\circ\Lambda\circ T\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\mathbb{C})
for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
and, by
[
16
, Theorem 2.7.8]
,
Q_{\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})}(\gamma)\circ\Lambda\circ T\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});\mathbb{C})
for all
\gamma\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})
, that is,
\Lambda\circ T\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*},w^{*}))
by
[
16
, Corollary 2.4.5]
. Hence
\Lambda\circ T=S^{*}
for some
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
by
[
16
, Theorem 3.1.11]
. Note that
S^{*}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*})
and this means that
S\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
by
[
16
, Theorem 3.4.16]
. Now,
S=S_{f}
for some
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
by Theorem
5.4
. Finally, we have
T=\Lambda^{-1}\circ S^{*}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}=f^{t}
.
∎

The following result provides both versions of Gantmacher and Gantmacher–Nakamura Theorems for weakly compact Bloch mappings.

Theorem 5.21
.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D},X)
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

f\colon\mathbb{D}\to X
is weakly compact Bloch.

(2)

f^{t}\colon X^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is weakly compact.

(3)

f^{t}\colon X^{*}\to\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
is weak*-to-weak continuous.

Proof.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
: we have

\displaystyle f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D},X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow S_{f}\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(S_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{W}(X^{*},\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*})

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{W}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})),

by Theorem
5.6
, the Gantmacher Theorem
[
16
, Theorem 3.5.13]
and
[
16
, Proposition 3.5.11]
.

(1)\Leftrightarrow(3)
: similarly,

\displaystyle f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D},X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow S_{f}\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(S_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*},w))

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),w))

by Theorem
5.6
, the Gantmacher–Nakamura Theorem
[
16
, Theorem 3.5.14]
and
[
16
, Corollary 2.5.12]
.
∎

We next identify
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D},X)
with the subspace of
\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),w^{*}))
formed by all weak*-to-weak continuous linear operators from
X^{*}
into
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proposition 5.22
.

f\mapsto f^{t}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),w))
.

Proof.

In view of Theorem
5.21
and Proposition
5.17
, we only need to show that the mapping in the statement is surjective. Let
T\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),w))
. Then
\Lambda\circ T\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*},w))
by
[
16
, Theorem 2.5.11]
, and this last set is contained in the space
\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*},w^{*}))
.
It follows that
\Lambda\circ T=S^{*}
for some
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
by
[
16
, Theorem 3.1.11]
. Hence
S^{*}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*},w))
and, by the Gantmacher–Nakamura Theorem,
S\in\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
. Now,
S=S_{f}
for some
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbb{D},X)
by Corollary
5.6
. Finally,
T=\Lambda^{-1}\circ S^{*}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ(S_{f})^{*}=f^{t}
, as desired.
∎

We conclude by identifying the little Bloch space
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D},X)
with the space of bounded-weak*-to-norm continuous linear operators from
X^{*}
to
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
. In its proof we will apply a criterion for compactness in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
due to K. Madigan and A. Matheson
[
14
]
.

Theorem 5.23
.

f\mapsto f^{t}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D},X)
onto
\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D}))
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D},X)
and let us prove that
f^{t}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D}))
. We have

(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|=(1-|z|^{2})\sup_{x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}}\left|x^{*}(f^{\prime}(z))\right|=\sup_{x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|(x^{*}\circ f)^{\prime}(z)\right|

for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
. For each
x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}
, we deduce that
f^{t}(x^{*})\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
with
p_{\mathcal{B}}(f^{t}(x^{*}))\leq p_{\mathcal{B}}(f)
. Hence
f^{t}(B_{X^{*}})
is a bounded subset of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
. Since we also have

\lim_{|z|\to 1}\sup_{x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|(x^{*}\circ f)^{\prime}(z)\right|=\lim_{|z|\to 1}(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|=0,

Lemma 1 in
[
14
]
asserts that the set
f^{t}(B_{X^{*}})
is relatively compact in
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
, that is,
f^{t}
is in
\mathcal{K}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D}))
. Moreover,
f^{t}
belongs to
\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}),w^{*}))
by Proposition
5.18
. Hence
\Lambda\circ f^{t}\in\mathcal{K}(X^{*},\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*})\cap\mathcal{L}((X^{*},w^{*});(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*},w^{*}))
. Then there is an
S\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
for which
S^{*}=\Lambda\circ f^{t}
by
[
16
, Theorem 3.1.11]
. Hence
S^{*}\in\mathcal{K}(X^{*},\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D})^{*})
and, by the Schauder Theorem,
S\in\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{D}),X)
. Hence
f^{t}=\Lambda^{-1}\circ S^{*}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
by
[
16
, Theorem 3.4.16]
. Since
f^{t}(X^{*})\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
, we conclude that
f^{t}\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D}))
, as expected.

By Proposition
5.17
, the mapping of the statement is a linear isometry. It remains to show that it is onto. For this take
T\in\mathcal{L}((X^{*},bw^{*});\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D}))
. By Proposition
5.20
, there exists
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{\mathcal{K}}(\mathbb{D},X)
such that
T=f^{t}
. Let us prove that
f
actually belongs to
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D},X)
. Since
T\in\mathcal{K}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{D}))
by Theorem
5.19
and
T(X^{*})\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D})
, we deduce that
T\in\mathcal{K}(X^{*},\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D}))
. Then, by applying again
[
14
, Lemma 1]
we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{|z|\to 1}(1-|z|^{2})\left\|f^{\prime}(z)\right\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{|z|\to 1}\sup_{x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|x^{*}(f^{\prime}(z))\right|

\displaystyle=\lim_{|z|\to 1}\sup_{x^{*}\in B_{X^{*}}}(1-|z|^{2})\left|(T(x^{*}))^{\prime}(z)\right|=0,

and so
f\in\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{0}(\mathbb{D},X)
.
∎

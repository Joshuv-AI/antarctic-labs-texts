New phenomena in deviation of Birkhoff integrals for locally Hamiltonian flows
Krzysztof Frączek, Minsung Kim

Abstract
. We consider smooth locally Hamiltonian flows on compact surfaces of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 to prove their deviation of Birkhoff integrals for smooth observables. Our work generalizes results of Forni [ 10 ] and Bufetov [ 2 ] which prove the existence of deviation spectrum of Birkhoff integrals for observables whose jets vanish at sufficiently high order around fixed points of the flow. They showed that ergodic integrals can display a power spectrum of behaviours with exactly g g positive exponents related to the positive Lyapunov exponents of the cocycle so-called Kontsevich-Zorich, a renormalization cocycle over the Teichmüller flow on a stratum of the moduli space of translation surfaces. Our paper extends the study of the spectrum of deviations of ergodic integrals beyond the case of observables whose jets vanish at sufficiently high order around fixed points. We prove the existence of some extra terms in deviation spectrum related to non-vanishing of the derivatives of observables at fixed points. The proof of this new phenomenon is based on tools developed in the recent work of the first author and Ulcigrai [ 8 ] for locally Hamiltonian flows having only (simple) non-degenerate saddles. In full generality, due to the occurrence of (multiple) degenerate saddles, we introduce new methods of handling functions with polynomial singularities over a full measure set of IETs.

The main result of this paper concerns the deviation of ergodic averages for a.a. locally Hamiltonian flows that allow saddles of any multiplicity

multi-valued Hamiltonian flows
.

2.1.1.
Relation with closed
1
-forms

Such flows are in
1-1
correspondence with smooth closed real-valued
1
-forms. For every vector field
X
preserving
\omega
let
\eta:=\imath_{X}\omega=\omega(X,\,\cdot\,)
be the corresponding
1
-form, where
\imath_{X}
denotes the contraction operator. Since
\eta
is a smooth closed
1
-form (i.e.
d\eta=0
), for any
p\in M
and any simply connected neighbourhood
U
of
p
there exists a smooth (local Hamiltonian) map (unique up to additive constant)
such that
dH=\eta
on
U
. It follows that the flow
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
is the (local) solution of the Hamiltonian equation
p^{\prime}=X(p)
with
dH=\omega(X,\,\cdot\,)
.
In local coordinates, if
\omega(x,y)=V(x,y)dx\wedge dy
then the Hamiltonian equation is of the form
x^{\prime}=\frac{\partial H}{\partial y}/V
,

y^{\prime}=-\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}/V
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Fix}(\psi_{\mathbb{R}})
the
set of fixed points of
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
, i.e. the set of zeros of the form
\eta
.
If the form
\eta
is Morse, locally differential of a
Morse function
(i.e. the Hessian at every fixed point is non-zero), it corresponds to either a center or a simple saddle. More precisely, by Morse lemma, for every fixed point
\sigma\in\mathrm{Fix}(\psi_{\mathbb{R}})
there exists a local chart
(x,y)
in a neighborhood
U_{\sigma}
of
\sigma

such that
H(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}
or
H(x,y)=2xy
. In this paper, we permit the appearance of
degenerate
fixed points, i.e. saddles of multiplicity
m>2
such that the corresponding Hamiltonian function is of the form
H(x,y)=\Im(x+iy)^{m}
. More precise description of the local behaviour of
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
around multi-saddles is postponed to §
8
.

2.1.2.
Minimality vs. minimal components

For more detailed explanation of the space of locally Hamiltonian flows and the partition of the surface into invariant components, we refer the reader to
[
21
]
.
We call a
saddle connection
an orbit of
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
running from a saddle to another saddle. A
saddle loop
is a saddle connection
joining the same saddle.
If every fixed point in
\mathrm{Fix}(\psi_{\mathbb{R}})
is isolated,
M
splits into a finite number of
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
-invariant surfaces (with boundary) so that every such surface is a
minimal component
of
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
(every orbit, except of fixed points, is dense in the component) or is a periodic component (its interior is filled by periodic orbits and its boundary consists of saddle connections).

Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the set of smooth locally Hamiltonian flows (or equivalently smooth closed
1
-forms) on
M
with isolated zeros.
The set
\mathcal{F}
is equipped with a topology by considering smooth perturbations
of closed smooth
1
-forms. For any vector
\overline{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{s})
of natural numbers
\geq 2
and any
c\leq\sum_{i=1}^{s}(m_{i}-1)
, denote by
\mathcal{F}_{\overline{m},c}

the set of smooth locally Hamiltonian flows with
c
centers and
s
saddles of multiplicity
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{s}
. By the Poincaré-Hopf Theorem,
c-\sum_{i=1}^{s}(m_{i}-1)=2-2g
.
A measure-theoretical notion of typicality on
\mathcal{F}
(on each
\mathcal{F}_{\overline{m},c}
separately) is defined by the cohomology class of the 1-form
\eta
,
so called
Katok fundamental class
(introduced by Katok in
[
13
]
).
Let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n}
be a base of
H_{1}(M,\mathrm{Fix}(\psi_{\mathbb{R}}),\mathbb{R})
, where
n=2g+s+c-1
.
Let us consider the period map

\Theta(\psi_{\mathbb{R}})=\Big(\int_{\gamma_{1}}\eta,\ldots,\int_{\gamma_{n}}\eta\Big)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

which is well-defined in a neighbourhood of
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\overline{m},c}
.
The
\Theta
-pullback of the Lebesgue measure class (i.e. class of sets with zero measure) gives the desired measure class on
\mathcal{F}_{\overline{m},c}
. When we use the expression
a.e. locally Hamiltonian flow
below we mean full measure in each
\mathcal{F}_{\overline{m},c}
with respect to the corresponding measure class.
We distinguish a subset
\mathcal{F}_{\min}=\bigcup_{\overline{m}}\mathcal{F}_{\overline{m},0}\subset\mathcal{F}
and the corresponding measure class. Then a.e. flow
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{F}_{\min}
is minimal.
On the other hand, every
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\mathcal{F}_{\min}
has a nontrivial splitting into minimal and periodic components.
Then we only deal this with the flow
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
restricted to any of its minimal component
M^{\prime}\subset M
.

We should mention that mixing properties of a.e. non-degenerate locally Hamiltonian flow restricted to minimal components
are fully described in
[
4
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
. When degenerate saddles appear, mixing was proved by Kochergin in
[
15
]
.
On the other hand, understanding more subtle spectral properties of locally Hamiltonian flows seems to be still in its infancy.
Only recently the first results have appeared for the singular spectrum in
[
3
]
and the countable Lebesgue spectrum in
[
6
]
.

2.2.
Interval exchange transformations (IET)

To define an IET we adopt the notation
from
[
28
]
. Let
\mathcal{A}

be a
d
-element alphabet and let
\pi=(\pi_{0},\pi_{1})
be a pair of
bijections
\pi_{\varepsilon}:\mathcal{A}\to\{1,\ldots,d\}
for
\varepsilon=0,1
.
For every
\lambda=(\lambda_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{\mathcal{A}}
let

|\lambda|:=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\lambda_{\alpha}
,
I:=\left[0,|\lambda|\right)
and for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle I_{\alpha}:=[l_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}),\text{ where
}l_{\alpha}=\sum_{\pi_{0}(\beta)<\pi_{0}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\beta},\;\;\;r_{\alpha}=\sum_{\pi_{0}(\beta)\leq\pi_{0}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\beta}

\displaystyle I^{\prime}_{\alpha}:=[l^{\prime}_{\alpha},r^{\prime}_{\alpha}),\text{ where
}l^{\prime}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\pi_{1}(\beta)<\pi_{1}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\beta},\;\;\;r^{\prime}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\pi_{1}(\beta)\leq\pi_{1}(\alpha)}\lambda_{\beta}.\

The
interval exchange transformation

T=T_{(\pi,\lambda)}
given by the data
(\pi,\lambda)
is the
orientation preserving piecewise isometry

T_{(\pi,\lambda)}:I\to I
which,
for each
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, maps the interval
I_{\alpha}

isometrically onto the interval
I^{\prime}_{\alpha}
. Clearly
T

preserves the Lebesgue measure on
I
.

Denote by

\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{A}}
the subset of irreducible pairs,
i.e. such that

\pi_{1}\circ\pi_{0}^{-1}\{1,\ldots,k\}\neq\{1,\ldots,k\}
for
1\leq k<d
. We will always assume that
\pi\in\mathcal{S}^{0}_{\mathcal{A}}
. The IET
T_{(\pi,\lambda)}
is
explicitly given by
T(x)=x+w_{\alpha}
for
x\in I_{\alpha}
, where

w=\Omega_{\pi}\lambda
and
\Omega_{\pi}
is the matrix

[\Omega_{\alpha\,\beta}]_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}}
given by

\Omega_{\alpha\,\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}+1&\text{ if
}\pi_{1}(\alpha)>\pi_{1}(\beta)\text{ and
}\pi_{0}(\alpha)<\pi_{0}(\beta),\\
-1&\text{ if }\pi_{1}(\alpha)<\pi_{1}(\beta)\text{ and
}\pi_{0}(\alpha)>\pi_{0}(\beta),\\
0&\text{ in all other cases.}\end{array}\right.

We use also an alternative description of IET. Let
\widehat{I}=(0,|I|]
and denote by
\widehat{T}_{(\pi,\lambda)}:\widehat{I}\to\widehat{I}
the
exchange of the intervals

\widehat{I}_{\alpha}:=(l_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}]
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
,
i.e.
\widehat{T}_{(\pi,\lambda)}x=x+w_{\alpha}
for
x\in\widehat{I}_{\alpha}
.

Let
End(T)=\{l_{\alpha},r_{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\}
stand for the set of end points of the intervals

I_{\alpha},\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
.
A pair
{(\pi,\lambda)}

satisfies the
Keane condition
(see
[
14
]
) if

T_{(\pi,\lambda)}^{m}l_{\alpha}\neq l_{\beta}
for all
m\geq 1

and for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{A}
with
\pi_{0}(\beta)\neq 1
.

2.3.
Cocycles and special flows over IETs

Let
T:I\to I
be an ergodic IET. Each measurable function
\varphi:I\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
determines an

additive cocycle

\varphi^{(\,\cdot\,)}(\,\cdot\,)
for
T
so that

\varphi^{(n)}(x):=\sum_{0\leq k<n}\varphi(T^{k}(x))\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 0\text{ and }x\in I.

Let

g:I\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\}
be an integrable function such that

\underline{g}=\inf_{x\in I}g(x)>0
. Denote by
(T^{g}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}=T^{g}_{\mathbb{R}}

the
special flow
built over the IET
T
and under the
roof function

g
acting on

I^{g}:=\{(x,r)\in I\times{\mathbb{R}}:0\leq r<g(x)\}

so that
T^{g}_{t}(x,r)=(x,r+t-g^{(n)}(x))
, where
n
is the unique
integer number with
g^{(n)}(x)\leq r+t<g^{(n+1)}(x).

Locally Hamiltonian flows are represented as special flows.
Let us consider a restriction of a locally Hamiltonian flow
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
on
M
to its minimal component
M^{\prime}\subset M
.
Let
I\subset M^{\prime}
be any transversal smooth curve with its standard parametrization
\gamma:[0,|I|]\to I
, i.e.
\int_{0}^{\gamma(s)}\eta=s

for
s\in[0,|I|]
, where
\eta

the closed
1
-form
\eta
associated with the flow
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
. By minimality,
I
is a global transversal and the first return map
T:I\to I
is an IET in standard coordinates on
I
.
Moreover,
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
restricted to
M^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the special flow
T^{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
, where
g:I\to{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\}
is the first return time map.
The roof function has logarithmic (polynomial) singularities derived from non-degenerate (degenerate) saddles. A detailed description of these relations is presented in Theorem
4.1
.

Let
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
be an integrable map. We study the asymptotics of ergodic integrals
\int_{0}^{T}f(\psi_{t}x)\,dt
using only the return times to the curve
I
. More precisely,
for every
x\in I
we deal with
\varphi_{f}(x):=\int_{0}^{g(x)}f(\psi_{t}x)\,dt
3
3

3

In fact, the roof function
g
can be obtained by choosing
f=1
, i.e
g=\varphi_{1}
.
. Then the asymptotic of the cocycle
\varphi^{(n)}(x)
gives almost full information about the growth of the ergodic integrals.
The cocycle
\varphi_{f}:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
has also logarithmic and polynomial singularities depending on the multiplicity of saddles, which is described in Theorem
4.1
.

The same strategy can be applied to any special flow
T^{g}_{\mathbb{R}}
and any integrable function
f:I^{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. Then
\varphi_{f}:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
given by
\varphi_{f}(x)=\int_{0}^{g(x)}f(x,r)\,dr
.
By Fubini’s theorem,
\varphi_{f}
is well-defined for a.e.

,
is integrable and

\int_{I}\varphi_{f}(x)\,dx=\int_{I^{g}}f(x,r)\,dx\,dr.

Remark 2.1
.

In the reduction of the locally Hamiltonian flow
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
to the special flow
T^{g}_{\mathbb{R}}

I_{\alpha}
exchanged by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\widetilde{\pi}_{\varepsilon}(\alpha)&=&\pi_{\varepsilon}(\alpha)\text{ for all }\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\text{ and }\\

Kontsevich-Zorich cocycle and its accelerations

If an IET
T
satisfies Keane’s condition, then

also satisfies Keane’s condition and we can generate a sequence of IETs

This defines a multiplicative cocycle

and taking values in

Let

. For every

is the

. Then

is the first return map of

to the interval

.

For every

let
Z(k+1):=A^{(n_{k+1}-n_{k})}(\mathcal{R}^{n_{k}}(T))^{t}
. We then have

By following notations from

let

Q(k,l)=Z(l)\cdot Z(l-1)\cdot\dotsc\cdot Z(k+2)\cdot Z(k+1)=A^{(n_{l}-n_{k})}(\mathcal{R}^{n_{k}}(T))^{t}.

Then,
Q(k,l)\in SL_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathbb{Z}})
and
\lambda^{(k)}=Q(k,l)^{t}\lambda^{(l)}
. We write

In what follows, the norm of a vector is defined as the sum of

|I^{(k)}|\leq|I^{(l)}|\left\|Q(k,l)\right\|.

\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
0\leq a<1
.
Let us consider any
C^{1}
-map
f:J\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
with
J:=(x_{0},x_{1}]
such that
|f^{\prime}(x)(x-x_{0})^{1+a}|\leq C
for
x\in(x_{0},x_{1}]
.
Then for every
s\in(x_{0},x_{1}]
, we have

(4.10)

\displaystyle|f(s)-m(f,J)|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{a(s-x_{0})^{a}}+\frac{2a-1}{a(1-a)}\frac{1}{|J|^{a}}\right),\quad\text{ if }0<a<1

(4.11)

\displaystyle|f(s)-m(f,J)|\leq C\left(\log\frac{|J|}{s-x_{0}}+1\right),\quad\text{ if }a=0.

Moreover, for every
0\leq a<1
we have

(4.12)

\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}\left|f(s)-m(f,J)\right|\,ds\leq\frac{2C}{(1-a)|J|^{a}}.

Proof.

For all
t,s\in(x_{0},x_{1}]
and
a\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle|f(s)-f(t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{s}^{t}f^{\prime}(u)du\right|\leq C\left|\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{(u-x_{0})^{1+a}}du\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left|-\frac{(t-x_{0})^{-a}}{a}+\frac{(s-x_{0})^{-a}}{a}\right|.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|f(s)-m(f,J)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|J|}\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}\left|-\frac{(t-x_{0})^{-a}}{a}+\frac{(s-x_{0})^{-a}}{a}\right|dt

\displaystyle=C\left(\frac{2}{1-a}\frac{(s-x_{0})^{1-a}}{|J|}+\frac{(s-x_{0})^{-a}}{a}-\frac{(x_{1}-x_{0})^{-a}}{a(1-a)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{1}{a(s-x_{0})^{a}}+\frac{2a-1}{a(1-a)}\frac{1}{|J|^{a}}\right),

which gives (
4.10
).
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|J|}

\displaystyle\int_{J}\left|f(s)-m(f,J)\right|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|J|}\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}\left(\frac{2}{1-a}\frac{(s-x_{0})^{1-a}}{|J|}+\frac{(s-x_{0})^{-a}}{a}-\frac{(x_{1}-x_{0})^{-a}}{a(1-a)}\right)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{2C}{(1-a)(2-a)|J|^{a}}\leq\frac{2C}{(1-a)|J|^{a}},

which gives (
4.12
) when
0<a<1
.

Similarly, if
a=0
then

\displaystyle|f(s)-f(t)|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{s}^{t}f^{\prime}(u)du\right|\leq\left|\int_{s}^{t}\frac{C}{u-x_{0}}du\right|=C\left|\log\frac{t-x_{0}}{s-x_{0}}\right|.

It follows that

\displaystyle\left|f(s)-m(f,J)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|J|}\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}\left|\log\frac{t-x_{0}}{s-x_{0}}\right|dt=C\left(\log\frac{|J|}{s-x_{0}}-1+2\frac{s-x_{0}}{|J|}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\log\frac{|J|}{s-x_{0}}+1\right),

which gives (
4.11
).
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}\left|f(s)-m(f,J)\right|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|J|}\int_{x_{0}}^{x_{1}}\left(\log\frac{|J|}{s-x_{0}}-1+2\frac{s-x_{0}}{|J|}\right)\,ds=C,

which gives (
4.12
) when
a=0
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

By (
4.10
) and (
4.11
), we also have

(4.13)

\left|f(x_{1})-m(f,J)\right|\leq\frac{C}{(1-a)|J|^{a}}\text{ for every }0\leq a<1.

In particular, specific bounds for averaged function
\varphi
on
I_{\alpha}
are given as follows.

Lemma 4.5
.

Assume that
0\leq a<1
and
\varphi\in\operatorname{P_{a}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
.
Then for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and
x\in IntI_{\alpha}
we have

(4.14)

|\varphi(x)|\leq\left\|\mathcal{M}(\varphi)\right\|+p_{a}(\varphi)\left(\frac{1}{a\min\{x-l_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}-x\}^{a}}+\frac{2^{a+2}}{a(1-a)|I_{\alpha}|^{a}}\right)\\

if
0<a<1
and

(4.15)

|\varphi(x)|\leq\left\|\mathcal{M}(\varphi)\right\|+p_{a}(\varphi)\left(\log\frac{|I_{\alpha}|}{2\min\{x-l_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}-x\}}+2\right)

if
a=0
.

Proof.

By (
4.13
) applied to
f=\varphi
restricted to
J=(l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}]
and
[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha})
, we have

\displaystyle|\varphi(m_{\alpha})-m(\varphi,[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}])|\leq\frac{2^{a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{1-a}\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|^{a}},

\displaystyle|\varphi(m_{\alpha})-m(\varphi,[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}])|\leq\frac{2^{a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{1-a}\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|^{a}}.

Therefore

|m(\varphi,[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}])-m(\varphi,[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}])|\leq\frac{2^{a+1}p_{a}(\varphi)}{1-a}\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|^{a}}.

As
m(\varphi,I_{\alpha})=(m(\varphi,[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}])+m(\varphi,[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}]))/2
, it follows that

(4.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|m(\varphi,[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}])-m(\varphi,I_{\alpha})|&=\frac{|m(\varphi,[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}])-m(\varphi,[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}])|}{2}\leq\frac{p_{a}(\varphi)2^{a}}{(1-a)|I_{\alpha}|^{a}},\\
|m(\varphi,[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}])-m(\varphi,I_{\alpha})|&\leq\frac{p_{a}(\varphi)2^{a}}{(1-a)|I_{\alpha}|^{a}}.\end{split}

If
0<a<1
then we can apply (
4.10
) to
f=\varphi
restricted to
J=(l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}]
and
[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha})
and taking
C=p_{a}(\varphi)
. This gives

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}])|

\displaystyle\leq p_{a}(\varphi)\left(\frac{1}{a(x-l_{\alpha})^{a}}+\frac{2^{a+1}}{a(1-a)|I_{\alpha}|^{a}}\right),\quad\text{ if }x\in(l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}],

\displaystyle|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}])|

\displaystyle\leq p_{a}(\varphi)\left(\frac{1}{a(r_{\alpha}-x)^{a}}+\frac{2^{a+1}}{a(1-a)|I_{\alpha}|^{a}}\right),\quad\text{ if }x\in[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}).

Together with (
4.16
) this yields (
4.14
).

If
a=0
then we can apply (
4.11
) to
f=\varphi
restricted to
J=(l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}]
and
[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha})
and taking
C=p_{a}(\varphi)
. This gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}&|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}])|\leq p_{a}(\varphi)\left(\log\frac{|I_{\alpha}|}{2(x-l_{\alpha})}+1\right),\quad\text{ if }x\in(l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}],\\
&|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}])|\leq p_{a}(\varphi)\left(\log\frac{|I_{\alpha}|}{2(r_{\alpha}-x)}+1\right),\quad\text{ if }x\in[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}).\end{split}

Together with (
4.16
) this yields (
4.15
).
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For every
0\leq a<1
,
\varphi\in\operatorname{P_{a}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
we have

(4.17)

\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|}\int_{I_{\alpha}}|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,I_{\alpha})|\,dx\leq\frac{2^{2+a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{1-a}\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|^{a}}.

Proof.

By (
4.12
) applied to
f=\varphi
restricted to
J=(l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}]
and
[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|}\int_{[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}]}|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}])|\,dx\leq\frac{2^{a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{1-a}\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|^{a}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|}\int_{[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}]}|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}])|\,dx\leq\frac{2^{a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{1-a}\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|^{a}}.

In view of (
4.16
), it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|}\int_{[l_{\alpha},m_{\alpha}]}|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,I_{\alpha})|\,dx\leq\frac{2^{1+a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{1-a}\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|^{a}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|}\int_{[m_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}]}|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,I_{\alpha})|\,dx\leq\frac{2^{1+a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{1-a}\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|^{a}}.

Summing up, we obtain

\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|}\int_{I_{\alpha}}|\varphi(x)-m(\varphi,I_{\alpha})|\,dx\leq\frac{2^{2+a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{1-a}\frac{1}{|I_{\alpha}|^{a}},

which completes the proof.
∎

We finish the section by introducing a lower bound for function
\varphi
. This will be crucial in handling some lower bounds of renormalized cocycles and Birkhoff integrals. (See the part V in the proof of Theorem
1.1
in §
10
.)

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose that
\varphi:J\to{\mathbb{R}}
(
J=(x_{0},x_{1}]
) is an integrable
C^{1}
-function such that

0<c\leq|(x-x_{0})^{1+a}\varphi^{\prime}(x)|\text{ for all }x\in J.

Then there exists a sub-interval
\widehat{J}\subset J
such that

(4.18)

|\widehat{J}|\geq|J|/4\text{ and }|\varphi(x)|\geq\frac{c}{4|J|^{a}}\text{ for all }x\in\widehat{J}.

Proof.

We first show that for every
\xi>0
we have

(4.19)

Leb\{x\in J:|\varphi(x)|\leq\xi\}\leq\frac{2\xi|J|^{1+a}}{c}.

Note that
\varphi
is strictly monotonic. We focus on the strictly decreasing case, i.e.
-(x-x_{0})^{1+a}\varphi^{\prime}(x)\geq c
for all
x\in J
. Then
\varphi(x)\to+\infty
as
x\searrow x_{0}
. The proof in the strictly increasing case follows by the same way.
Suppose that the set
\{x\in J:|\varphi(x)|\leq\xi\}
is not empty. Then it is an interval
[y_{1},y_{2}]\subset J

such that
\varphi(y_{1})=\xi
and
\varphi(y_{2})=\xi_{2}\geq-\xi
. It follows that

\displaystyle y_{2}-y_{1}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{-1}(\xi_{2})-\varphi^{-1}(\xi)=-\int_{\xi_{2}}^{\xi}(\varphi^{-1})^{\prime}(x)\,dx=-\int_{\xi_{2}}^{\xi}\frac{1}{\varphi^{\prime}(\varphi^{-1}(x))}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\xi_{2}}^{\xi}\frac{(\varphi^{-1}(x)-x_{0})^{1+a}}{c}\,dx\leq\frac{|J|^{1+a}}{c}(\xi-\xi_{2})\leq\frac{2\xi|J|^{1+a}}{c}.

Applying (
4.19
) to
\xi=\frac{c}{4|J|^{a}}
, we have that
\{x\in J:|\varphi(x)|\leq\frac{c}{4|J|^{a}}\}
is an interval
whose length is at most

\frac{2\frac{c}{4|J|^{a}}|J|^{1+a}}{c}=\frac{|J|}{2}.

Since
\{x\in J:|\varphi(x)|\geq\frac{c}{4|J|^{a}}\}
consists of at most two intervals and its measure is at least
|J|/2
,
one of these intervals satisfies (
4.18
).
∎

5.
Renormalization of cocycles

In this section we review a renormalization operator on cocycles over IETs derived from the renormalizations of an IETs given by accelerated Rauzy-Veech induction.

5.1.
Special Birkhoff sums

Assume that an IET
T:I\rightarrow I
satisfies Keane’s condition.
For any
0\leq k<l
and any measurable cocycle
\varphi:I^{(k)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
for the IET

T^{(k)}:I^{(k)}\to I^{(k)}
, denote by

S(k,l)\varphi:I^{(l)}\to{\mathbb{R}}
the renormalized cocycle for

T^{(l)}
given by

S(k,l)\varphi(x)=\sum_{0\leq i<Q_{\beta}(k,l)}\varphi((T^{(k)})^{i}x)\text{ for }x\in I^{(l)}_{\beta}.

We write
S(k)\varphi
for
S(0,k)\varphi
and we adhere to the
convention that
S(k,k)\varphi=\varphi
. Sums of this form are
usually called
special Birkhoff sums
. If
\varphi
is
integrable then

(5.1)

\|S(k,l)\varphi\|_{L^{1}(I^{(l)})}\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k)})}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{I^{(l)}}S(k,l)\varphi(x)\,dx=\int_{I^{(k)}}\varphi(x)\,dx.

Clearly,
S(k,l)\Gamma^{(k)}=\Gamma^{(l)}
and
S(k,l)
is the linear automorphism of
{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{A}}
whose matrix in the canonical basis is
Q(k,l)
.

Recalling the definition of
C^{\pm}_{\alpha}
(see Definition
4
), the following lemma is simply mimics of Lemma 3.1 and 3.3 in
[
7
]
. Since its proof
proceeds in the same way as the proofs of the mentioned lemmas in
[
7
]
, we omit it.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
\varphi\in\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
. For every
k>0
we have
S(k)\varphi\in\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I^{(k)}_{\alpha})
and there exists a permutation
\chi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}
such that

C^{+}_{\alpha}(S(k)\varphi)=C^{+}_{\alpha}(\varphi)\text{ and }C^{-}_{\alpha}(S(k)\varphi)=C^{-}_{\chi(\alpha)}(\varphi)

for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
. Moreover, there are distinct
\alpha_{*}
,
\alpha^{*}
in
\mathcal{A}
such that

\displaystyle\big\{T^{j}l^{(k)}_{\alpha}:0\leq j<Q_{\alpha}(k)\big\}\cap End(T)=\{l_{\alpha}\}\text{ for every }\alpha\in\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle\big\{\widehat{T}^{j}r^{(k)}_{\alpha}:0\leq j<Q_{\alpha}(k)\big\}\cap End(T)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\{r_{\chi(\alpha)}\}&\text{if }\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\{\alpha_{*},\alpha^{*}\}\\
\big\{r_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)},r_{\pi_{1}^{-1}(d)}\big\}&\text{if }\alpha=\alpha^{*}\\
\emptyset&\text{if }\alpha=\alpha_{*}.\end{array}\right.

In view of the above Lemma, for all
0\leq k<l
the operator
S(k,l)

maps
\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I^{(k)}_{\alpha})
into
\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I^{(l)}_{\alpha})
. The following three results give estimates for the increase
p_{a}
along the sequence of renormalized cocycles.

Proposition 5.2
.

Suppose that
T=T_{(\pi,\lambda)}
satisfies Keane’s condition. Then for every
0<a<1
,
\varphi\in\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and
k\geq 1
,

(5.2)

p_{a}(S(k)\varphi)\leq\left(2+2d\zeta(1+a)\left(\frac{\max\{|I^{(k)}_{\alpha}|:\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\}}{\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\alpha}|:\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\}}\right)^{1+a}\right)p_{a}(\varphi).

Proof.

Suppose that
x\in(l_{\beta}^{(k)},m_{\beta}^{(k)}]
(
m_{\beta}^{(k)}
is the middle point of
I_{\beta}^{(k)}
) for some
\beta\in\mathcal{A}
.
By Lemma
5.1
, there exists
0\leq j_{\beta}<Q_{\beta}(k)
such that

l_{\beta}=T^{j_{\beta}}l^{(k)}_{\beta}
. It follows that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j_{\beta}}x)(x-l_{\beta}^{(k)})^{1+a}|&=|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j_{\beta}}x)(T^{j_{\beta}}x-T^{j_{\beta}}l_{\beta}^{(k)})^{1+a}|\\
&=|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j_{\beta}}x)(T^{j_{\beta}}x-l_{\beta})^{1+a}|\leq p_{a}(\varphi).\end{split}

For any
0\leq j<Q_{\beta}(k)
,
j\neq j_{\beta}
denote by
e_{\alpha_{j}}
an element of the set
End(T)
that is the closest to
T^{j}x
,
more precisely
e_{\alpha_{j}}=l_{\alpha_{j}}
if
T^{j}x\in(l_{\alpha_{j}},m_{\alpha_{j}}]
and
e_{\alpha_{j}}=r_{\alpha_{j}}
if
T^{j}x\in(m_{\alpha_{j}},r_{\alpha_{j}})
. Then

(5.4)

|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j}x)(T^{j}x-e_{\alpha_{j}})^{1+a}|\leq p_{a}(\varphi).

Since
T^{j}x\in T^{j}(l_{\beta}^{(k)},m_{\beta}^{(k)}]
with
j\neq j_{\beta}
and the elements of
End(T)
lie on the boundary of intervals

T^{i}I^{(k)}_{\gamma}
for
0\leq i<Q_{\gamma}(k)
,
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

(5.5)

|T^{j}x-e_{\alpha_{j}}|\geq\min\{\tfrac{1}{2}|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|,\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\gamma}|:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\}\}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\gamma}|:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\}.

Suppose that for some
j\neq j^{\prime}
we have
\alpha_{j}=\alpha_{j^{\prime}}=\alpha
. Since
T^{j}x\in T^{j}I^{(k)}_{\beta}
and
T^{j^{\prime}}x\in T^{j^{\prime}}I^{(k)}_{\beta}
and they are equally distant from the ends of the intervals, we have

(5.6)

|(T^{j}x-e_{\alpha_{j}})-(T^{j^{\prime}}x-e_{\alpha_{j^{\prime}}})|=|T^{j}x-T^{j^{\prime}}x|\geq|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|.

In view of (
5.4
), it follows that for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and
e_{\alpha}\in I_{\alpha}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<Q_{\beta}(k)\\
j\neq j_{\beta},e_{\alpha_{j}}=e_{\alpha}\end{subarray}}|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j}x)|

\displaystyle\leq p_{a}(\varphi)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<Q_{\beta}(k)\\
j\neq j_{\beta},e_{\alpha_{j}}=e_{\alpha}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{|T^{j}x-e_{\alpha}|^{1+a}}

\displaystyle\leq p_{a}(\varphi)\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{1}{\big|\tfrac{1}{2}\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\gamma}|:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\}+l|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|\big|^{1+a}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{1+a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\gamma}|:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\}^{1+a}}\sum_{l\geq 1}\frac{1}{l^{1+a}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\zeta(1+a)2^{1+a}p_{a}(\varphi)}{\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\gamma}|:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\}^{1+a}}.

Therefore, by (
5.3
), we have

\displaystyle|S(k)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}(x)(x-l^{(k)}_{\beta})^{1+a}|=\Big|\sum_{0\leq j<Q_{\beta}(k)}\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j}x)(x-l^{(k)}_{\beta})^{1+a}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j_{\beta}}x)(x-l^{(k)}_{\beta})^{1+a}|+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha\in\mathcal{A}\\
e_{\alpha}\in\{l_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}\}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<Q_{\beta}(k)\\
j\neq j_{\beta},e_{\alpha_{j}}=e_{\alpha}\end{subarray}}|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j}x)|(|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|/2)^{1+a}

\displaystyle\leq p_{a}(\varphi)\left(1+2d\zeta(1+a)\left(\frac{|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|}{\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\gamma}|:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\}}\right)^{1+a}\right).

For the other cases, we assume that
x\in(m_{\beta}^{(k)},r_{\beta}^{(k)})
for some
\beta\in\mathcal{A}\setminus\{\alpha_{*},\alpha^{*}\}
or
\beta\in\{\alpha_{*},\alpha^{*}\}
.
For the former case, by Lemma
5.1
, there exists
0\leq j_{\beta}<Q_{\beta}(k)
such that

r_{\chi(\beta)}=\widehat{T}^{j_{\beta}}r^{(k)}_{\beta}
. It follows that

(5.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j_{\beta}}x)(r_{\beta}^{(k)}-x)^{1+a}|&=|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j_{\beta}}x)(\widehat{T}^{j_{\beta}}r_{\beta}-T^{j_{\beta}}x)^{1+a}|\\
&=|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j_{\beta}}x)(r_{\chi(\beta)}-T^{j_{\beta}}x)^{1+a}|\leq p_{a}(\varphi).\end{split}

The estimate from above of
|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j}x)|
for
j\neq j_{\beta}
is the same as previous case. Therefore we obtain

(5.8)

|S(k)\varphi^{\prime}(x)(r^{(k)}_{\beta}-x)^{1+a}|\leq p_{a}(\varphi)\left(1+2d\zeta(1+a)\left(\frac{|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|}{\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\gamma}|:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\}}\right)^{1+a}\right).

For the latter cases, if
x\in(m_{\alpha^{*}}^{(k)},r^{(k)}_{\alpha^{*}})
, then
by Lemma
5.1
, there exists
0\leq j_{\alpha^{*}}<Q_{{\alpha^{*}}}(k)-1
such that

r_{\pi_{1}^{-1}(d)}=\widehat{T}^{j_{\alpha^{*}}}r^{(k)}_{\alpha^{*}}
and
r_{\pi_{0}^{-1}(d)}=\widehat{T}^{j_{\alpha^{*}}+1}r^{(k)}_{\alpha^{*}}
. It follows that
the estimate (
5.7
) from above of
|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j}x)|
for
j\neq j_{\alpha^{*}},j_{\alpha^{*}}+1
is the same as (
5.3
) in the first case.

Lastly, if
x\in(m_{\alpha_{*}}^{(k)},r^{(k)}_{\alpha_{*}})
, then by Lemma
5.1
again,
\{\widehat{T}^{j}r^{(k)}_{\alpha_{*}}:0\leq j<Q_{\alpha}(k)\}\cap End(T)=\emptyset
.
Since the elements of
End(T)
lie on the boundary of intervals

T^{i}I^{(k)}_{\gamma}
for
0\leq i<Q_{\gamma}(k)
,
\gamma\in\mathcal{A}
, it follows that for every
0\leq j<Q_{\alpha_{*}}(k)
we have

\operatorname{dist}(T^{j}x,End(T))\geq\min\{\tfrac{1}{2}|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|,\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\gamma}|:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\}\}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\gamma}|:\gamma\in\mathcal{A}\}.

Repeating the same argument as in the first case, we obtain similar bound of (
5.8
).
∎

For
a=0
, the proof mimics the idea applied in the estimate for logarithmic singularity type from
[

and
[

.
The proof is carried out in each sub-cases as in the proof of Proposition

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose that

(5.9)

.

For every IET
T
satisfying
there exists

such that
for all

and for every function

We finish this section by giving a lower bound derived from the proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose that
T=T_{(\pi,\lambda)}
satisfies Keane’s condition and
0<a<1
. Assume that
\varphi\in\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
are such that
C^{+}_{\alpha}(\varphi)\neq 0
or
C^{-}_{\alpha}(\varphi)\neq 0
. Then choose
\delta>0
so that

\displaystyle|(x-l_{\alpha})^{1+a}\varphi^{\prime}(x)|\geq|C^{+}_{\alpha}(\varphi)|/2\text{ for all }x\in(l_{\alpha},l_{\alpha}+\delta]\text{ or}

\displaystyle|(r_{\alpha}-x)^{1+a}\varphi^{\prime}(x)|\geq|C^{-}_{\alpha}(\varphi)|/2\text{ for all }x\in[r_{\alpha}-\delta,r_{\alpha}).

If
k\geq 1
is such that
|I^{(k)}|\leq\delta
then for every
x\in(l^{(k)}_{\alpha},m^{(k)}_{\alpha}]
we have

|S(k)\varphi^{\prime}(x)|\geq\frac{|C^{+}_{\alpha}|}{2(x-l^{(k)}_{\alpha})^{1+a}}-\frac{2^{2+a}d\zeta(1+a)p_{a}(\varphi)}{|I^{(k)}_{\alpha}|^{1+a}}\left(\frac{\max\{|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|:\beta\in\mathcal{A}\}}{\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|:\beta\in\mathcal{A}\}}\right)^{1+a},

or for every
x\in[m^{(k)}_{\chi^{-1}(\alpha)},r^{(k)}_{\chi^{-1}(\alpha)})
we have

|S(k)\varphi^{\prime}(x)|\geq\frac{|C^{-}_{\alpha}|}{2(r^{(k)}_{\chi^{-1}(\alpha)}-x)^{1+a}}-\frac{2^{2+a}d\zeta(1+a)p_{a}(\varphi)}{|I^{(k)}_{\chi^{-1}(\alpha)}|^{1+a}}\left(\frac{\max\{|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|:\beta\in\mathcal{A}\}}{\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|:\beta\in\mathcal{A}\}}\right)^{1+a}.

Proof.

We focus only on the case
C^{+}_{\alpha}(\varphi)\neq 0
. The other case can be treated in the same way. By the proof of Proposition
5.2
,
for every
x\in(l^{(k)}_{\alpha},m^{(k)}_{\alpha}]
we have

\Big|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq j<Q_{\beta}(k)\\
j\neq j_{\alpha}\end{subarray}}\varphi(T^{j}x)\Big|\leq\frac{2^{2+a}d\zeta(1+a)p_{a}(\varphi)}{|I^{(k)}_{\alpha}|^{1+a}}\left(\frac{\max\{|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|:\beta\in\mathcal{A}\}}{\min\{|I^{(k)}_{\beta}|:\beta\in\mathcal{A}\}}\right)^{1+a}

and

|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j_{\alpha}}x)(x-l_{\alpha}^{(k)})^{1+a}|=|\varphi^{\prime}(T^{j_{\alpha}}x)(T^{j_{\alpha}}x-l_{\alpha})^{1+a}|\geq|C^{+}_{\alpha}|/2.

This yields our claim.
∎

6.
Correction operator

In this section we define the whole family of correction operators
\mathfrak{h}_{j}:\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})\rightarrow U_{j}\subset H(\pi)
for
0\leq a<1
with
2\leq j\leq g+1

determined by
\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda_{1}a<\lambda_{j-1}
.
These operators are generalizations of the correction operator introduced in
[
8
]
for the cocycles with logarithmic singularities.
The correction operator allows us to correct a cocycle with polynomial singularities by a piecewise
constant function, so that we have better control over the growth of the special Birkhoff sums of the corrected cocycle.

6.1.
Correction operator for cocycles with polynomial singularities

Recall the projection operator
\mathcal{M}
defined on
L^{1}(I)
in (
4.9
). For every step of (accelerated) renormalization let us consider the corresponding projection operators on the vector space, i.e. for every
k\geq 0
let

\mathcal{M}^{(k)}:\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})\rightarrow\Gamma^{(k)}\quad\text{be given by}\quad{\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(\varphi)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}m(\varphi,I)\chi_{I^{(k)}_{\alpha}}}.

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
T
satisfies
. For any
0\leq a<1
take
2\leq j\leq g+1
so that
\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda_{1}a<\lambda_{j-1}
. There exists a bounded linear operator

\mathfrak{h}_{j}:\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})\rightarrow U_{j}
such that for any
\tau>0
there exists a constant
C=C_{\tau}\geq 1
such that for every
\varphi\in\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
with
\mathfrak{h}_{j}(\varphi)=0
we have

(6.1)

\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi)\|\leq C\left(\big(K^{a,j,\tau}_{k}+C^{a,j,\tau}_{k}\big)p_{a}(\varphi)+\|Q_{E_{j}}(k)\|\frac{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(0)})}}{|I^{(0)}|}\right).

In view of Proposition
3.6
and the fact that
\|Q_{E_{j}}(k)\|=O(e^{(\lambda_{j}+\tau)k})\leq O(e^{(\lambda_{1}a+\tau)k})
(see (
3.6
) combined with (
3.5
)), we have the following result.

Corollary 6.2
.

Assume that
T
satisfies
. Then for every
\varphi\in\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
with
\mathfrak{h}_{j}(\varphi)=0
, for every small enough
\tau>0
, there exists a FDC-acceleration such that

(6.2)

\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi)\|=O(e^{(\lambda_{1}a+\tau)k}).

Corollary
6.2
plays a crucial role in determining the growth rate of ergodic integrals in §
7
.
Before starting the proof of Theorem
6.1
, we provide some basic inequalities regarding the operator
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}
.

Note that, by definition and (
4.17
),

(6.3)

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(\varphi)\big\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k)})}\leq 2\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k)})}

(6.4)

\displaystyle\big\|\varphi-\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(\varphi)\big\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k)})}\leq\frac{2^{2+a}d}{1-a}p_{a}(\varphi)|I^{(k)}|^{1-a}.

As for every
h\in\Gamma^{(k)}
,

(6.5)

\frac{|I^{(k)}|\left\|h\right\|}{\kappa}\leq\min_{\beta\in\mathcal{A}}|I_{\beta}^{(k)}|\left\|h\right\|\leq\left\|h\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k)})}\leq|I^{(k)}|\left\|h\right\|,

this gives

(6.6)

\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(\varphi)\|\leq\frac{2\kappa}{|I^{(k)}|}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k)})}.

Let us consider a linear operator

P^{(k)}_{0}:\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha}^{(k)})\rightarrow\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha}^{(k)})
given by

P^{(k)}_{0}(\varphi)=\varphi-\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(\varphi).

In view of (
6.4
) and (
2.4
), for all
k\geq 0
we have

(6.7)

\frac{1}{|I^{(k)}|}\big\|P^{(k)}_{0}(\varphi)\big\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k)})}\leq\frac{2^{2+a}d}{1-a}\frac{p_{a}(\varphi)}{|I^{(k)}|^{a}}\leq\frac{2^{2+a}d}{(1-a)|I|^{a}}\|Q(k)\|^{a}p_{a}(\varphi).

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

Let us denote
v_{k}:=\mathcal{M}^{(k)}\circ S(k)(\varphi)
.
Direct calculation shows that

\displaystyle\big(S(k,k+1)

\displaystyle\circ P^{(k)}_{0}\circ S(k)(\varphi)-P_{0}^{(k+1)}\circ S(k,k+1)\circ S(k)(\varphi)\big)

\displaystyle=-S(k,k+1)\circ\mathcal{M}^{(k)}\circ S(k)(\varphi)+\mathcal{M}^{(k+1)}\circ S(k+1)(\varphi)

\displaystyle=-Z(k+1)v_{k}+v_{k+1}.

By (
5.1
), (
6.7
), (
5.10
) and (
2.4
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I^{(k+1)}|}\big\|S(k,k+1)\circ P^{(k)}_{0}\circ S(k)(\varphi)\big\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k+1)})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{|I^{(k+1)}|}\big\|P^{(k)}_{0}\circ S(k)(\varphi)\big\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k)})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|I^{(k)}|}{|I^{(k+1)}|}\frac{2^{2+a}d}{(1-a)|I|^{a}}\left\|Q(k)\right\|^{a}p_{a}(S(k)\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}\langle\|Q(k)\|\rangle^{a}\|Z(k+1)\|p_{a}(\varphi),

and similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I^{(k+1)}|}\big\|P_{0}^{(k+1)}\circ S(k+1)(\varphi)\big\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k+1)})}\leq C_{a}\langle\|Q(k+1)\|\rangle^{a}p_{a}(\varphi).

Therefore, for any
0\leq a<1
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|I^{(k+1)}|}\left\|Z(k+1)v_{k}-v_{k+1}\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(k+1)})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{a}\left(\langle\|Q(k)\|\rangle^{a}\|Z(k+1)\|+\langle\|Q(k+1)\|\rangle^{a}\right)p_{a}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{a}\langle\|Q(k)\|\rangle^{a}\|Z(k+1)\|p_{a}(\varphi).

By (
6.5
), this gives

(6.8)

\left\|Z(k+1)v_{k}-v_{k+1}\right\|\leq\kappa C^{\prime}_{a}\langle\|Q(k)\|\rangle^{a}\|Z(k+1)\|p_{a}(\varphi).

For any sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathcal{A}}
, let
\Delta x_{k+1}=x_{k+1}-Z(k+1)x_{k}
for
k\geq 0
and
\Delta x_{0}=x_{0}
.
Then, by telescoping,

(6.9)

\displaystyle x_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}Q(j,k)\Delta x_{j}.

Letting
v_{k}=\mathcal{M}^{(k)}\circ S(k)(\varphi)
, by (
6.6
) and (
6.8
),

(6.10)

\left\|\Delta v_{0}\right\|\leq\frac{2\kappa}{|I^{(0)}|}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(0)})}\text{ and }\left\|\Delta v_{k+1}\right\|\leq\kappa C^{\prime}_{a}\langle\|Q(k)\|\rangle^{a}\|Z(k+1)\|p_{a}(\varphi).

Now we claim that for any
\tau>0
, there exists a vector
v\in U_{j}^{(0)}
such that

(6.11)

\left\|Q(k)v-v_{k}\right\|\leq O\big((p_{a}(\varphi)+\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}(I)})(K^{a,j,\tau}_{k}+C^{a,j,\tau}_{k})\big).

For every
k\geq 0
let
e_{k}=P^{(k)}_{E_{j}}v_{k}\in E_{j}^{(k)}
and
u_{k}=P^{(k)}_{U_{j}}v_{k}\in U_{j}^{(k)}
. Then
v_{k}=u_{k}+e_{k}
. Since
Z(k+1)(E_{j}^{(k)})=E_{j}^{(k+1)}
and
Z(k+1)(U_{j}^{(k)})=U_{j}^{(k+1)}
we have

\displaystyle\Delta u_{k+1}=u_{k+1}-Z(k+1)u_{k}=P^{(k+1)}_{U_{j}}\Delta v_{k+1}

\displaystyle,\ \Delta e_{k+1}=e_{k+1}-Z(k+1)e_{k}=P^{(k+1)}_{E_{j}}\Delta v_{k+1},

\displaystyle\Delta u_{0}=u_{0}=P^{(0)}_{U_{j}}\Delta v_{0}

\displaystyle,\ \Delta e_{0}=e_{0}=P^{(0)}_{E_{j}}\Delta v_{0}.

In view of (
6.10
) and (
3.15
), we have

(6.12)

\displaystyle\left\|\Delta u_{0}\right\|\leq C\left\|\Delta v_{0}\right\|\leq\frac{2C\kappa}{|I^{(0)}|}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(0)})},\quad\left\|\Delta e_{0}\right\|\leq C\left\|\Delta v_{0}\right\|\leq\frac{2C\kappa}{|I^{(0)}|}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(0)})},

and for every
k\geq 1
we have

(6.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\Delta u_{k}\right\|&\leq C_{a,\tau}\|Z(k)\|\langle\|Q(k-1)\|\rangle^{a}\|Q(k)\|^{\tau}p_{a}(\varphi),\\
\left\|\Delta e_{k}\right\|&\leq C_{a,\tau}\|Z(k)\|\langle\|Q(k-1)\|\rangle^{a}\|Q(k)\|^{\tau}p_{a}(\varphi).\end{split}

Let us consider an infinite series

. For any

small enough, by (

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|&e_{k}\|\leq\sum_{0\leq l\leq k}\|Q(l,k)\Delta e_{l}\|\leq\sum_{0\leq l\leq k}\|Q|_{E_{j}}(l,k)\|\|\Delta e_{l}\|\\

Since
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)(v))=Q(k)v
, this gives

(6.16)

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)(\varphi-v))\big\|

\displaystyle\leq O\Big((K^{a,j,\tau}_{k}+C^{a,j,\tau}_{k})p_{a}(\varphi)+\|Q|_{E_{j}}(k)\|\frac{\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(0)})}}{|I^{(0)}|}\Big).

Let us consider the linear operator
\mathfrak{h}_{j}:\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})\rightarrow U_{j}^{(0)}\subset H(\pi)
, called the
correction operator
given by

\mathfrak{h}_{j}(\varphi)=v=\sum_{l\geq 0}Q(0,l)^{-1}\circ P^{(l)}_{U_{j}}\circ\big(\mathcal{M}^{(l)}\circ S(l)-Z(l)\circ\mathcal{M}^{(l-1)}\circ S(l-1)\big)(\varphi),

where
\mathcal{M}^{(-1)}=0
. Since
\mathfrak{h}_{j}(\varphi)=\lim_{l\to\infty}Q(0,l)^{-1}\circ P^{(l)}_{U_{j}}\circ\mathcal{M}^{(l)}\circ S(l)(\varphi)
, by Remark

,

is independent of the choice of

and its FDC-acceleration.
By (

), we have

\left\|\mathfrak{h}_{j}(\varphi)\right\|\leq C^{\prime}_{a,\tau}\Big(\frac{2\kappa}{|I^{(0)}|}\left\|\varphi\right\|_{L^{1}(I^{(0)})}+K^{a,j,\tau}_{0}p_{a}(\varphi)\Big)\leq C^{\prime}_{a,\tau}\Big(\frac{2\kappa}{|I^{(0)}|}+K^{a,j,\tau}_{0}\Big)\left\|\varphi\right\|_{a},

thus, the operator

is bounded.
Hence, if

, then, by (

).

7.1.
Reduction of Birkhoff integrals to Birkhoff
sums

Given an IET
T:I\to I
and an integrable function

with

\underline{g}=\inf_{x\in I}g(x)>0
, we consider the special flow
(T^{g}_{t})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}
acting on

For every integrable function

For every
u\geq\underline{g}
let
I_{u}
be a subset of
I
such that

g(x)\leq u
for every

. Moreover, for every

let

A_{u}^{s}:=\{(x,r)\in I^{g}:x\in I_{u}\}\setminus\{T^{g}_{-t}(x,0):x\in I\setminus I_{u},\,0\leq t\leq s\}\subset I^{g}.

For every
(x,r)\in I^{g}
and
s>0

be the number of crossing the interval
I
by the orbit segment

Then
0\leq n(x,r,s)\leq s/\underline{g}+1

We recall the following elementary Lemma that relates the Birkhoff integrals of
f

for the flow
(T_{t}^{g})_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}

for the IET

[

]

Lemma 7.1

(Lemma 7.1 in
[
8
]
)
.

For every integrable map
f:I^{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
s>0
and
u\geq\underline{g}
if

(7.1)

T^{i}x\in I_{u}\quad\text{for all}\quad 0\leq i\leq n(x,r,s),

and

(7.2)

\Big|\int_{0}^{s}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt\Big|\leq|\varphi_{f}^{(n(x,r,s))}(x)|+2\chi(u).

We next estimate
\varphi_{f}^{(n)}(x)
using the decomposition
into special Birkhoff sums introduced by Zorich in
[

Let
T:I\to I
be an arbitrary IET satisfying Keane’s condition.
For every
x\in I

Proposition 7.2

(see
[
32
]
or
[

]

For every

Since the sequence

\big(\min_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}Q_{\alpha}(m)\big)_{m\geq 0}

increases to the infinity

m(n)=m(n,T):=\max\{m(x,n):x\in I\}\quad\text{is well-defined.}

Lemma 7.3
.

Assume that
T
satisfies
and
\varphi:I\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is a bounded map such that
for some
\tau>0
and the corresponding FDC-acceleration we have
\|S(k)\varphi\|_{\sup}=O(e^{\lambda k})
for some

. Then

\|\varphi^{(n)}\|=O(n^{(\lambda+\tau)/\lambda_{1}})

Proof.

By Zorich’s standard argument for the decomposition

[

\|\varphi^{(n)}\|_{\sup}\leq 2\sum_{l=1}^{m(n)}\left\|Z(l+1)\right\|\left\|S(l)\varphi\right\|_{\sup}.

By Proposition

, if
T
satisfies
, by (

) and (
3.13

Since

and

|\varphi^{(n)}(x)|=O\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{m(n)}e^{(\lambda+\tau)l}\Big)=O(e^{(\lambda+\tau)m(n)})=O(n^{(\lambda+\tau)/\lambda_{1}}).

If
\varphi_{f}

Proposition 7.4

(Proposition 7.3 in

8
]
)

For every integrable map

and

Suppose that

Lemma 7.5
.

Suppose that
T
satisfies
and for every small enough

\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi_{f})\|=O(e^{b\lambda_{1}(1+\tau)k}).

Then for every
\tau>0

C=C_{\tau}\geq 1
such that for every

and

(7.5)

\left|\int_{0}^{s}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))dt\right|\leq Cu^{(1+\tau)\frac{b}{a}}.

Proof.

Let
u\geq s^{a}

then

It follows that

I^{(k)}(u)\neq\emptyset\Rightarrow|I^{(k)}|>cu^{-1/a}\Rightarrow k\leq\frac{1}{a\lambda_{1}}\log(C^{\prime}u).

Moreover, if
x\in I^{(k)}(u)\cap I_{\alpha}^{(k)}
, then
x\in[l^{(k)}_{\alpha}+cu^{-1/a},r^{(k)}_{\alpha}-cu^{-1/a}]
.
In view of (
4.14
), (
5.2
), (
7.4
), (
2.4
) and (
3.9
), it follows that
for every
x\in I^{(k)}(u)
,

\displaystyle|(S(k)\varphi_{f})(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi_{f})\|+p_{b}(S(k)\varphi_{f})\Big(\frac{1}{b{(cu^{-1/a})}^{b}}+\frac{2^{b+2}}{b(1-b)|I^{(k)}|^{b}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq O(e^{b\lambda_{1}(1+\tau)k})+O(p_{b}(\varphi_{f}))\Big(\frac{u^{b/a}}{b{c}^{b}}+\frac{2^{b+2}}{b(1-b)}O\big(e^{b\lambda_{1}(1+\tau)k}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq O\big(u^{(1+\tau)\frac{b}{a}}\big)+O\big(u^{\frac{b}{a}}\big)=O\big(u^{(1+\tau)\frac{b}{a}}\big).

Therefore, by (
7.2
) and (
7.3
), for every
(x,r)\in A^{s}_{u}
we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{s}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\chi(u)+2\sum_{k\geq 0,\,I^{(k)}(u)\neq\emptyset}\|Z(k+1)\|\|S(k)\varphi_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(I^{(k)}(u))}

\displaystyle=O(u^{\frac{b}{a}})+O\Big(u^{(1+\tau)\frac{b}{a}}\sum_{0\leq k\leq\frac{\log(C^{\prime}u)}{a\lambda_{1}}}e^{\tau k}\Big)

\displaystyle=O(u^{\frac{b}{a}})+O\Big(u^{(1+\tau)\frac{b}{a}}u^{\frac{\tau}{\lambda_{1}}\frac{1}{a}}\Big)=O\Big(u^{(1+\tau(1+\frac{1}{b\lambda_{1}}))\frac{b}{a}}\Big).

∎

7.2.
Estimates of ergodic integrals

In this section, we show
L^{1}
and a.e. bounds of the Birkhoff integrals for special flows. These bounds are related to the growth of the sequence
\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi)\|
.
It is the first step toward complete deviation formula in §
10
. The main tools used in this section come from Lemma
7.5
.

Recall that
0<b\leq a<1
,
g\in{\operatorname{P_{a}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and
f:I^{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is an integrable map such that
\varphi_{f}\in{\operatorname{P_{b}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and
\varphi_{|f|}\in{\operatorname{\widehat{P}_{b}}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
. Moreover,
c>0
is chosen so that

x\in I_{u}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}[l_{\alpha}+cu^{-1/a},r_{\alpha}-cu^{-1/a}]\Longrightarrow g(x)\leq u.

For every
s>0
let

B_{s^{a}}:=\{(x,r)\in I^{g}:x\in I\setminus I_{s^{a}},0\leq r<g(x)-s\}.

Lemma 7.6
.

There exists
C\geq 1
such that for all
s^{a}\leq u_{1}<u_{2}
we have

(7.6)

Leb\big((A_{u_{2}}^{s}\setminus A_{u_{1}}^{s})\setminus B_{s^{a}}\big)\leq Cs(u_{1}^{-1/a}-u_{2}^{-1/a}).

Proof.

First note that

\displaystyle(A_{u_{2}}^{s}\setminus A_{u_{1}}^{s})\setminus B_{s^{a}}

\displaystyle\subset\bigcup_{0\leq t\leq s}T^{g}_{-t}\big((I_{u_{2}}\setminus I_{u_{1}})\times\{0\}\big)

\displaystyle\quad\cup\big\{(x,r)\in I^{g}:x\in I_{u_{2}}\setminus I_{u_{1}},g(x)-s\leq r<g(x)\big\}.

Therefore

Leb\big((A_{u_{2}}^{s}\setminus A_{u_{1}}^{s})\setminus B_{s^{a}}\big)\leq 2s\,Leb(I_{u_{2}}\setminus I_{u_{1}}).

Since
I_{u_{2}}\setminus I_{u_{1}}
is the union of
2d
disjoint intervals of the same length
c(u_{1}^{-1/a}-u_{2}^{-1/a})
,
we have

2s\,Leb(I_{u_{2}}\setminus I_{u_{1}})=4dcs(u_{1}^{-1/a}-u_{2}^{-1/a}).

This gives (
7.6
).
∎

Lemma 7.7
.

There exists
C\geq 1
such that

(7.7)

\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}\,dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(B_{s^{a}})}\leq Cs^{b}\text{ for all }s\geq 1.

Proof.

First note that for every
x\in I\setminus I_{s^{a}}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{g(x)-s}

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{s}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt\Big|\,dr\leq\int_{0}^{g(x)-s}\int_{r}^{r+s}|f(x,t)|\,dt\,dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{g(x)}\Big(\int_{\max\{0,t-s\}}^{\min\{t,g(x)-s\}}|f(x,t)|\,dr\Big)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{g(x)}(\min\{t,g(x)-s\}-\max\{0,t-s\})|f(x,t)|\,dt.

As
\min\{t,g(x)-s\}-\max\{0,t-s\}\leq s
, we have

\int_{0}^{g(x)-s}\Big|\int_{0}^{s}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt\Big|\,dr\leq s\int_{0}^{g(x)}|f(x,t)|\,dt=s\varphi_{|f|}(x).

It follows that

\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}\,dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(B_{s^{a}})}=\int_{I\setminus I_{s^{a}}}\int_{0}^{g(x)-s}\Big|\int_{0}^{s}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt\Big|\,dr\,dx\leq s\int_{I\setminus I_{s^{a}}}\varphi_{|f|}(x)\,dx.

As
\varphi_{|f|}\in\operatorname{\widehat{P}_{b}}
, there exists
C>0
such that for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and every
x\in I_{\alpha}
we have

\varphi_{|f|}(x)\leq\frac{C}{\min\{x-l_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}-x\}^{b}}.

Since
I\setminus I_{s^{a}}
is the union of
2d
intervals of length
cs^{-1}
with ends at
l_{\alpha}
,
r_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{I\setminus I_{s^{a}}}\varphi_{|f|}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq 2dC\int_{0}^{cs^{-1}}x^{-b}\,dx=\frac{2dCc^{1-b}}{1-b}s^{b-1}.

Therefore

\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}\,dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(B_{s^{a}})}\leq\frac{2dCc^{1-b}}{1-b}s^{b}.

∎

Now we obtain the
L^{1}
-bound for special flows under the condition for the growth rate of the renormalized and projected cocycles
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi)
.

Theorem 7.8
.

Suppose that
T
satisfies
,
0<b\leq a<1
,
g\in{\operatorname{P_{a}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
, and
f:I^{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is an integrable map such that
\varphi_{f}\in{\operatorname{P_{b}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and
\varphi_{|f|}\in{\operatorname{\widehat{P}_{b}}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
.
Assume that for every
\tau>0
,

\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi_{f})\|=O(e^{b\lambda_{1}(1+\tau)k}).

Then for every
\tau>0
we have

(7.8)

\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(I^{g})}=O\big(s^{(1+\tau)b}).

Proof.

Let
(u_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be any strictly increasing sequence diverging to
+\infty
and such that
u_{0}=s^{a}
. Then

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(I^{g})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(A^{s}_{u_{0}})}Leb(A^{s}_{u_{0}})+\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(B_{u_{0}})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{\infty}((A^{s}_{u_{j}}\setminus A^{s}_{u_{j-1}})\setminus B_{u_{0}})}Leb\big((A^{s}_{u_{j}}\setminus A^{s}_{u_{j-1}})\setminus B_{u_{0}}\big).

In view of (
7.5
), (
7.7
) and (
7.6
), we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{\infty}(A^{s}_{u_{0}})}\leq Cu_{0}^{(1+\tau)\frac{b}{a}}=Cs^{(1+\tau)b};

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{\infty}((A^{s}_{u_{j}}\setminus A^{s}_{u_{j-1}})\setminus B_{u_{0}})}\leq Cu_{j}^{(1+\tau)\frac{b}{a}};

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}\,dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(B_{s^{a}})}\leq Cs^{b};

\displaystyle Leb\big((A^{s}_{u_{j}}\setminus A^{s}_{u_{j-1}})\setminus B_{u_{0}})\big)\leq Cs(u_{j-1}^{-1/a}-u_{j}^{-1/a}).

Hence

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(I^{g})}

\displaystyle\leq Cs^{(1+\tau)b}+Cs^{b}+sC^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}u_{j}^{(1+\tau)\frac{b}{a}}(u_{j-1}^{-1/a}-u_{j}^{-1/a}).

Let us consider a strictly decreasing sequence of positive numbers
(v_{n})_{n\geq 0}
given by
v_{n}:=u_{n}^{-1/a}
.
Then
v_{0}=s^{-1}
and
v_{n}\to 0
as
n\to+\infty
and

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(I^{g})}

\displaystyle\leq 2Cs^{(1+\tau)b}+sC^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}v_{j}^{-(1+\tau)b}(v_{j-1}-v_{j}).

Passing through all possible sequences
(u_{n})_{n\geq 0}
and taking the infimum of values standing on the right hand side, this gives

\displaystyle\Big\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}dt\Big\|_{L^{1}(I^{g})}

\displaystyle\leq 2Cs^{(1+\tau)b}+sC^{2}\int_{0}^{s^{-1}}x^{-(1+\tau)b}\,dx

\displaystyle=2Cs^{(1+\tau)b}+\frac{sC^{2}}{1-(1+\tau)b}s^{(1+\tau)b-1}=O\big(s^{(1+\tau)b}\big).

∎

The following measure estimations are key ingredients for Borel-Cantelli argument applied to prove a.e. pointwise upper bound for Birkhoff integrals.

Lemma 7.9
.

There exists
C>0
such that for all
1\leq s<s^{\prime}
and
\tau>0
we have

(7.9)

\displaystyle Leb\big((A^{s}_{s^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\big)\leq Cs^{-\tau}+Cs^{-(1+\tau)(1-a)};

(7.10)

\displaystyle Leb\big((A^{s^{\prime}}_{{s^{\prime}}^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\setminus(A^{s}_{s^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\big)\leq C(s^{\prime})^{-(1+\tau)}(s^{\prime}-s).

Proof.

Recall that

(A^{s}_{s^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}=\bigcup_{0\leq t\leq s}T^{g}_{-t}((I\setminus I_{s^{(1+\tau)a}})\times\{0\})\cup\{(x,r)\in I^{g}:x\in I\setminus I_{s^{(1+\tau)a}}\},

where

I\setminus I_{s^{(1+\tau)a}}=\bigcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}[l_{\alpha},l_{\alpha}+cs^{-(1+\tau)})\cup(r_{\alpha}-cs^{-(1+\tau)},r_{\alpha}].

As
I\setminus I_{{s^{\prime}}^{(1+\tau)a}}\subset I\setminus I_{s^{(1+\tau)a}}
, it follows that

(A^{s^{\prime}}_{{s^{\prime}}^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\setminus(A^{s}_{s^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\subset\bigcup_{s<t\leq s^{\prime}}T^{g}_{-t}((I\setminus I_{{s^{\prime}}^{(1+\tau)a}})\times\{0\}).

Therefore

Leb\big((A^{s^{\prime}}_{{s^{\prime}}^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\setminus(A^{s}_{s^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\big)\leq(s^{\prime}-s)Leb(I\setminus I_{{s^{\prime}}^{(1+\tau)a}})=(s^{\prime}-s)2dc(s^{\prime})^{-(1+\tau)},

which gives (
7.10
).
Moreover, we have

Leb\big((A^{s}_{s^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\big)\leq sLeb(I\setminus I_{s^{(1+\tau)a}})+\int_{I\setminus I_{s^{(1+\tau)a}}}g(x)\,dx.

As
g\in\operatorname{\widehat{P}_{b}}
, there exists
C>0
such that for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and every
x\in I_{\alpha}
we have

g(x)\leq\frac{C}{\min\{x-l_{\alpha},r_{\alpha}-x\}^{a}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{I\setminus I_{s^{(1+\tau)a}}}g(x)\,dx\leq 2dC\int_{0}^{cs^{-(1+\tau)}}x^{-a}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2dCc^{1-a}}{1-a}s^{-(1+\tau)(1-a)}.

As
sLeb(I\setminus I_{s^{(1+\tau)a}})=s2dcs^{-(1+\tau)}=2dcs^{-\tau}
, this gives (
7.9
).
∎

Now we prove a.e. pointwise upper bound of Birkhoff integrals for special flows under some restriction on the growth rate of renormalized and projected cocycles
\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi_{f})
.

Theorem 7.10
.

Suppose that
T
satisfies
,
0<b\leq a<1
,
g\in{\operatorname{P_{a}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and
f:I^{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is an integrable map such that
\varphi_{f}\in{\operatorname{P_{b}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and
\varphi_{|f|}\in{\operatorname{\widehat{P}_{b}}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
.
Assume that for every
\tau>0
,

\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi_{f})\|=O(e^{b\lambda_{1}(1+\tau)k}).

Then for a.e.
(x,r)\in I^{g}
we have

(7.11)

\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{\log|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}(x,r)\,dt|}{\log s}\leq b.

Proof.

Fix
\tau>0
small enough. In view of Lemma
7.5
, there exists
C_{\tau}\geq 1
such that for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

(7.12)

\frac{\log|\int_{0}^{m}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))dt|}{\log m}\leq(1+\tau)^{2}b+\frac{C_{\tau}}{\log m}\text{ for every }(x,r)\in A^{m}_{m^{(1+\tau)a}}.

Moreover, in view of Lemma
7.9
, there exists
C>0
such that for every
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have

\displaystyle Leb\big(\bigcup_{n\geq m}(A^{n}_{n^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\big)

\displaystyle=Leb\big((A^{m}_{m^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\big)+\sum_{n\geq m}Leb\big((A^{n+1}_{(n+1)^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\setminus(A^{n}_{n^{(1+\tau)a}})^{c}\big)

\displaystyle\leq Cm^{-\tau}+Cm^{-(1+\tau)(1-a)}+\sum_{n\geq m}C(n+1)^{-(1+\tau)}((n+1)-n)

\displaystyle\leq Cm^{-\tau}+Cm^{-(1+\tau)(1-a)}+\frac{C}{\tau}m^{-\tau}.

Hence

Leb\big(\bigcup_{m\geq 1}\bigcap_{n\geq m}A^{n}_{n^{(1+\tau)a}}\big)=1.

In view of (
7.12
), it follows that for a.e.
(x,r)\in I^{g}
we have

\limsup_{m\to+\infty}\frac{\log|\int_{0}^{m}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))dt|}{\log m}\leq(1+\tau)^{2}b.

As we can choose
\tau>0
arbitrary small, this gives

\limsup_{m\to+\infty}\frac{\log|\int_{0}^{m}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))dt|}{\log m}\leq b\text{ for a.e. }(x,r)\in I^{g}.

Hence

\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{\log|\int_{0}^{s}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))dt|}{\log s}\leq\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{\log\big(|\int_{0}^{[s]}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))dt|+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(I^{g})}\big)}{\log[s]}\leq b

for a.e.
(x,r)\in I^{g}
.
∎

The following corollary will be essential in proving a
L^{1}
-lower bound of Birkhoff integrals in the next section.

Corollary 7.11
.

Suppose
T
satisfies
,
0<b<1
and
\varphi\in{\operatorname{P_{b}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
such that

\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi)\|=O(e^{b\lambda_{1}(1+\tau)k})\text{ for every }\tau>0.

Then for a.e.
x\in I
we have

(7.13)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log|\varphi^{(n)}(x)|}{\log n}\leq b.

In particular, for every
\varphi\in{\operatorname{P_{b}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
for a.e.
x\in I
we have

(7.14)

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log|\varphi^{(n)}(x)-n\mu(\varphi)|}{\log n}\leq\max\left\{b,\frac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\right\},

where
\mu(\varphi)=\int_{I}\varphi(y)\,dy
.

Proof.

Let us consider the constant roof function
g:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
,
g\equiv 1
. Choose an integrable map
f:I^{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\varphi_{f}=\varphi
and
\varphi_{|f|}=|\varphi_{f}|\in{\operatorname{P_{b}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
(we can set
f(x,r)=\varphi(x)
for
(x,r)\in I^{g}
). Since
g\in{\operatorname{P_{b}G}}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
, by Theorem
7.10
applied to
s=g^{(n)}(x)=n
, we have

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log|\varphi^{(n)}(x)|}{\log n}=\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log\big|\int_{0}^{g^{(n)}(x)}f\circ T^{g}_{t}(x,r)\,dt\big|}{\log g^{(n)}(x)}\leq b.

Denote by
\Gamma_{0}\subset\Gamma
the space of zero mean piecewise constant maps, i.e.
h\in\Gamma_{0}
if
\langle h,\lambda\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{d}h_{i}\lambda_{i}=0
.
As
h_{1}\not\in\Gamma_{0}
,
h_{2},\ldots,h_{g}\in\Gamma_{0}
, we have

(7.15)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\|Q(k)h\|}{k}\leq\lambda_{2}\text{ for every }h\in\Gamma_{0}.

In view of Corollary
6.2
, there exists
\sum_{1\leq i<j}\beta_{i}h_{i}\in U_{j}
such that

(7.16)

\Big\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}\Big(S(k)\Big(\varphi-\sum_{1\leq i<j}\beta_{i}h_{i})\Big)\Big)\Big\|=O(e^{b\lambda_{1}(1+\tau)k})\text{ for every }\tau>0.

By (
7.13
), for every
\tau>0
we have
\big(\varphi-\sum_{1\leq i<j}\beta_{i}h_{i}\big)^{(n)}(x)=O(n^{(b+\tau)})
for a.e.
x\in I
.
Taking
\tau>0
such that

, by the ergodicity of
T
, we obtain

\int_{I}\Big(\varphi-\sum_{1\leq i<j}\beta_{i}h_{i}\Big)(x)\,dx=0.

It follows that
\mu(\varphi)-\sum_{1\leq i<j}\beta_{i}h_{i}\in\Gamma_{0}
. In view of (
7.15
), this gives

\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{\log\|Q(k)(\mu(\varphi)-\sum_{1\leq i<j}\beta_{i}h_{i})\|}{k}\leq\lambda_{2}.

By (

\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)(\varphi-\mu(\varphi)))\|=\|S(k)(\varphi-\mu(\varphi))\|=O(e^{(\max\{\lambda_{2},b\lambda_{1}\}+\tau)k}).

Applying again (
7.13
), we get (
7.14
).
∎

7.3.
Pure power deviation

In this section we relate the growth of the accelerated KZ cocycle with the deviation of special flows for functions associated with homology elements.
In particular, it is mostly devoted to prove the lower bound of Birkhoff integrals in a.e. and
L^{1}
-norm.
In the following proposition, we reprove Bufetov’s result about pure deviations a.e. Here the
L^{1}
-estimates account for the novelty of this result.

Proposition 7.12
.

Suppose that the IET
T:I\to I
satisfies
.
Assume that
0<a<1
,
g\in\operatorname{P_{a}G}(\sqcup_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}I_{\alpha})
and
f:I^{g}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a bounded function such that there exists
K>0
for which
f(x,r)=0
for
r\geq K
and
\varphi_{f}=h=(h_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in\mathcal{A}}\in H(\pi)
.
Suppose that there exists
\lambda>0
such that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\frac{\log\|Q(k)h\|}{k}=\lambda.

Then

(7.17)

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{\log|\int_{0}^{s}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt|}{\log s}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{1}}\text{ for a.e. }(x,r)\in I^{g},

(7.18)

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\to+\infty}\frac{\log\|\int_{0}^{s}f\circ T^{g}_{t}\,dt\|_{L^{1}}}{\log s}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{1}}.

Proof.

First note that for every
\tau>0
,

(7.19)

\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)\varphi_{f})\|=\|\mathcal{M}^{(k)}(S(k)h)\|\leq\|Q(k)h\|=O(e^{k(\lambda+\tau)}).

As
\varphi_{|f|}
is bounded, Theorem
7.8
and
7.10
yield the inequalities
\leq
in (
7.17
) and (
7.18
).

To show the reverse inequalities, note that for every
n\geq 1
and
(x,r)\in I^{g}
we have

(7.20)

\int_{0}^{g^{(n)}(x)-r}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt=\int_{r}^{g^{(n)}(x)}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,0))\,dt=\varphi^{(n)}_{f}(x)-\int_{0}^{r}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,0))\,dt.

Moreover, for every
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
we have

(7.21)

\varphi^{(Q_{\alpha}(k))}_{f}(x)=(Q(k)h)_{\alpha}\text{ if }x\in\bigcup_{0\leq i<p_{k}}T^{i}I^{(k)}_{\alpha}.

Indeed, if
x\in I^{(k)}_{\alpha}
then

\varphi^{(Q_{\alpha}(k))}_{f}(x)=S(k)\varphi_{f}(x)=(Q(k)h)_{\alpha}.

Now suppose that
x=T^{i}y
so that
y\in I^{(k)}_{\alpha}
and
0\leq i<p_{k}
.
Then

\varphi^{(Q_{\alpha}(k))}_{f}(x)-\varphi^{(Q_{\alpha}(k))}_{f}(y)=\varphi^{(Q_{\alpha}(k))}_{f}(T^{i}y)-\varphi^{(Q_{\alpha}(k))}_{f}(y)=\sum_{0\leq l<i}(\varphi_{f}(T^{l}T^{Q_{\alpha}(k)}y)-\varphi_{f}(T^{l}y)).

Since
y,T^{Q_{\alpha}(k)}y\in I_{\alpha}^{(k)}
and, by (
RT
),
\{T^{j}I^{(k)}_{\alpha}:{0\leq j<p_{k}}\}
is a Rokhlin tower of intervals,
for every
0\leq i<p_{k}
the points
T^{i}y
and
T^{i}T^{Q_{\alpha}(k)}y
belong to the same interval
I_{\beta}
.
As
\varphi_{f}=h
is constant
on each interval
I_{\beta}
,
\beta\in\mathcal{A}
, it follows
that

\varphi^{(Q_{\alpha}(k))}_{f}(x)=\varphi^{(Q_{\alpha}(k))}_{f}(y)=(Q(k)h)_{\alpha}.

Choose
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and a subsequence
(k_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that

(7.22)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log|(Q(k_{n})h)_{\alpha}|}{k_{n}}=\lambda.

In view of (
RT
),

(7.23)

Leb\Big(\bigcup_{0\leq i<p_{k_{n}}}T^{i}I^{(k_{n})}_{\alpha}\Big)\geq\frac{\delta|I|}{\kappa}\text{ for all }n\geq 1.

By the ergodicity of
T
, for a.e.
x\in I
, passing to a further subsequence, we have

x\in\bigcup_{0\leq i<p_{k_{l_{n}}}}T^{i}I^{(k_{l_{n}})}_{\alpha}\text{ for all }n\geq 1

and

(7.24)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{g^{(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}(x)}{Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}})}=\mu(g):=\int_{I}g(y)\,dy.

In view of (
7.21
), this gives

(7.25)

\varphi^{(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}_{f}(x)=(Q(k_{l_{n}})h)_{\alpha}\text{ for every }n\geq 1.

For every
r\geq 0
with
(x,r)\in I^{g}
, let us consider a sequence
(\tau_{n})_{n\geq 1}
given by

\tau_{n}=g^{(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}(x)-r.

Then, by (
7.20
) and (
7.25
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\log\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt\right|}{\log\tau_{n}}=\frac{\log\left|(Q(k_{l_{n}})h)_{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{r}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,0))\,dt\right|}{\log(g^{(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}(x)-r)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\log\left|(Q(k_{l_{n}})h)_{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{r}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,0))\,dt\right|}{k_{l_{n}}}\frac{\log(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}{\log(g^{(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}(x)-r)}\frac{k_{l_{n}}}{\log(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}.

Moreover, by (
3.14
), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}{k_{l_{n}}}=\lambda_{1};

by (
7.24
), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}{\log(g^{(Q_{\alpha}(k_{l_{n}}))}(x)-r)}=1;

by (
7.22
), we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\left|(Q(k_{l_{n}})h)_{\alpha}-\int_{0}^{r}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,0))\,dt\right|}{k_{l_{n}}}=\lambda.

It follows that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt\right|}{\log\tau_{n}}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{1}},

which completes the proof of (
7.17
).

We now turn to
L^{1}
-estimate of the lower bound. Let us consider a new sequence
(\tau_{n})_{n\geq 1}
given by
\tau_{n}=Q_{\alpha}(k_{n})\mu(g)
.
Take any
\max\{\lambda_{2}/\lambda_{1},a\}<\zeta<1
and
\tau>0
such that
\zeta(\lambda/\lambda_{1}+2\tau)\lambda_{1}/\lambda<1
. Since

\limsup_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\log|g^{(n)}(x)-n\mu(g)|}{\log n}<\zeta

(see Corollary
7.11
) and by the ergodic theorem, there exist
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
J\subset I
such that
Leb(I\setminus J)<|I|{\delta}/(2\kappa)

and for every

and
x\in J
we have

(7.26)

|g^{(n)}(x)-n\mu(g)|<n^{\zeta}\text{ and }n\mu(g)/2<g^{(n)}(x)<2n\mu(g).

Suppose that

(x,r)\in D_{n}:=\Big(J\cap\bigcup_{0\leq i<p_{k_{n}}}T^{i}I^{(k_{n})}_{\alpha}\Big)\times[0,\underline{g}]

and
Q_{\alpha}(k_{n})\geq 2N+1
. As
Leb(I\setminus J)<|I|{\delta}/(2\kappa)
, by (
7.23
), we have
Leb(D_{n})>\underline{g}|I|\delta/(2\kappa)=:\bar{\delta}>0
.
Moreover

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt-(Q(k_{n})h)_{\alpha}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{r}^{\tau_{n}+r}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,0))\,dt-\int_{0}^{g^{(Q_{\alpha}(k_{n}))}(x)}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,0))\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2r\|f\|_{C^{0}}+\left|\int_{\tau_{n}}^{g^{(Q_{\alpha}(k_{n}))}(x)}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,0))\,dt\right|.

Denote by

the unique natural number such that

By assumption,

It follows that

\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt-(Q(k_{n})h)_{\alpha}\right|\leq(2r+K)\|f\|_{C^{0}}+|\varphi_{f}^{(Q_{\alpha}(k_{n}))}(x)-\varphi_{f}^{(l_{n}(x))}(x)|.

Since

, there exists

such that

.
Therefore,

, we have
N\leq Q_{\alpha}(k_{n})/2-1\leq l_{n}(x)
.
Hence by (
7.26
),

\displaystyle-l_{n}(x)^{\zeta}

\displaystyle<g^{(l_{n}(x))}(x)-l_{n}(x)\mu(g)\leq Q_{\alpha}(k_{n})\mu(g)-l_{n}(x)\mu(g)

\displaystyle<g^{(l_{n}(x)+1)}(x)-l_{n}(x)\mu(g)<(l_{n}(x)+1)^{\zeta}+\mu(g).

It follows that there exists
C>0
such that for every
(x,r)\in D_{n}
(with
Q_{\alpha}(k_{n})\geq 2N+1
) we have

|Q_{\alpha}(k_{n})-l_{n}(x)|\leq C(Q_{\alpha}(k_{n}))^{\zeta}.

Then for every

, by (
7.27
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}f(T^{g}_{t}(x,r))\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\geq|(Q(k_{n})h)_{\alpha}|-C^{\prime}(Q_{\alpha}(k_{n}))^{\zeta(\lambda/\lambda_{1}+2\tau)}.

As

, it follows that

\frac{\log\|\int_{0}^{\tau_{n}}f\circ T^{g}_{t}\,dt\|_{L^{1}(I^{g})}}{\log\tau_{n}}\geq\frac{\log\bar{\delta}\big(|(Q(k_{n})h)_{\alpha}|-C^{\prime}(Q_{\alpha}(k_{n}))^{\zeta(\lambda/\lambda_{1}+2\tau)}\big)}{\log\mu(g)Q_{\alpha}(k_{n})}.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log(Q_{\alpha}(k_{n}))^{\zeta(\lambda/\lambda_{1}+2\tau)}}{\log|(Q(k_{n})h)_{\alpha}|}=\zeta(\lambda/\lambda_{1}+2\tau)\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda}<1.

Therefore,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\big(|(Q(k_{n})h)_{\alpha}|-C^{\prime}(Q_{\alpha}(k_{n}))^{\zeta(\lambda/\lambda_{1}+2\tau)}\big)}{\log|(Q(k_{n})h)_{\alpha}|}=1.

Hence, by (

) and (

∎

8.
Local analysis around saddles

In this section we mainly prove some local relations between regularity of the function
f:M\to{\mathbb{R}}
around fixed
points and the types of singularity for the associated cocycle
\varphi_{f}:I\to{\mathbb{R}}
. This analysis is one of
our main result of this paper that was not studied in the related literatures beforehand in such a comprehensive way.
The main results of this chapter play a key role in proving Theorem
4.1
and its extension in §
9
.
In fact, we generalize the approach developed for simple saddles in
[
7
]
(related to logarithmic singularity type) to multi-saddle type.

Let
M^{\prime}\subset M
be a minimal component of a locally Hamiltonian flow
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
associated with a closed 1-form
\eta
.
Let
I\subset M^{\prime}
be its transversal curve equipped with a standard parametrization. Recall that a parametrization of curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
is
standard
if
\gamma:I\to M
if
\eta(d\gamma)=1
.
In the standard coordinates, the first return map
T:I\to I
is an IET.

For every saddle
\sigma\in\mathrm{Fix}(\psi_{\mathbb{R}})
of multiplicity
m=m_{\sigma}\geq 2
let
(x,y)
be a singular chart in a neighborhood
U_{\sigma}
of
\sigma
. Then the corresponding local Hamiltonian is of the form
H(x,y)=\Im(x+iy)^{m}
. If the
\psi_{\mathbb{R}}
-invariant area form
\omega=V(x,y)dx\wedge dy
, then the corresponding local Hamiltonian equation in
U_{\sigma}
is of the form

\frac{dx}{dt}=\frac{\frac{\partial H}{\partial y}(x,y)}{V(x,y)}=\frac{m\Re(x+iy)^{m-1}}{V(x,y)}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{dy}{dt}=-\frac{\frac{\partial H}{\partial x}(x,y)}{V(x,y)}=-\frac{m\Im(x+iy)^{m-1}}{V(x,y)},

so

(8.1)

X(x,y)=X_{1}(x,y)+iX_{2}(x,y)=\frac{m\overline{(x+iy)^{m-1}}}{V(x,y)}

and

\eta=m\Im(x+iy)^{m-1}\,dx+m\Re(x+iy)^{m-1}\,dy.

Therefore, a
C^{1}
-curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to U_{\sigma}
is standard if and only if

(8.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}1=\eta_{\gamma(t)}\gamma^{\prime}(t)&=m\Im(\gamma(t))^{m-1}\Re\gamma^{\prime}(t)+m\Re(\gamma(t))^{m-1}\Im\gamma^{\prime}(t)\\
&=\Im\big(m(\gamma(t))^{m-1}\gamma^{\prime}(t)\big)=\Im\left(\frac{d}{dt}(\gamma(t))^{m}\right).\end{split}

For every
f\in C^{m}(M)
and any
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{2}
with
|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\leq m
let

\partial_{\sigma}^{\alpha}(f)=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}(f\cdot V)}{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}x\partial^{\alpha_{2}}y}(0,0)
.

Lemma 8.1
.

For every
f\in C^{m}(M)
and any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{2}
with
|\alpha|\leq m-2
we have

\partial_{\sigma}^{\alpha}(f)=\partial_{\sigma}^{\alpha}(f\circ\psi_{t})\text{ for every }t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Proof.

First note that for every
(x,y)\in U_{\sigma}\cap\psi_{-t}(U_{\sigma})
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}((f\cdot V)\circ\psi_{t})(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\partial(f\cdot V)}{\partial x}(\psi_{t}(x,y))}{V(\psi_{t}(x,y))}(V\cdot X_{1})(\psi_{t}(x,y))

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\frac{\partial(f\cdot V)}{\partial y}(\psi_{t}(x,y))}{V(\psi_{t}(x,y))}(V\cdot X_{2})(\psi_{t}(x,y)).

Therefore, by induction

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}x\partial^{\alpha_{2}}y}((f\cdot V)\circ\psi_{t})(x,y)=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}x\partial^{\alpha_{2}}y}\frac{d}{dt}((f\cdot V)\circ\psi_{t})(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}W_{\beta,1}(t,x,y)\frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial^{\beta_{1}}x\partial^{\beta_{2}}y}(V\cdot X_{1})(\psi_{t}(x,y))

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}W_{\beta,2}(t,x,y)\frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial^{\beta_{1}}x\partial^{\beta_{2}}y}(V\cdot X_{2})(\psi_{t}(x,y)).

As
V\cdot X_{1}
and
V\cdot X_{2}
are homogenous polynomials of degree
m-1
, we have

\frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial^{\beta_{1}}x\partial^{\beta_{2}}y}(V\cdot X_{1})(0,0)=\frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial^{\beta_{1}}x\partial^{\beta_{2}}y}(V\cdot X_{2})(0,0)=0\text{ if }|\beta|\leq m-2.

It follows that

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}x\partial^{\alpha_{2}}y}((f\cdot V)\circ\psi_{t})(0,0)=0\text{ for all }t\in{\mathbb{R}}\text{ and }|\alpha|\leq m-2.

Hence

\partial_{\sigma}^{\alpha}(f\circ\psi_{t})=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}x\partial^{\alpha_{2}}y}((f\cdot V)\circ\psi_{t})(0,0)=\frac{\partial^{|\alpha|}}{\partial^{\alpha_{1}}x\partial^{\alpha_{2}}y}(f\cdot V)(0,0)=\partial_{\sigma}^{\alpha}(f).

∎

Let
G_{0}:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the principal
m
-th root map, i.e.
G_{0}(re^{is})=r^{1/m}e^{is/m}
if
s\in[0,2\pi)
, and let
\omega\in{\mathbb{C}}
be the principal
2m
-th root of unity.

Definition 5
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
denote by

the pre-image of the square

by the map

of

.

Let us consider four curves that parametrize some incoming and outgoing segments of the boundary of

,

For every interval

the corresponding angular sector

Lemma 8.2
.

The following statements hold:

(i) The maps

\gamma_{\pm}^{out}(s)

(iii) for every

\int_{0}^{\tau(s)}f(\psi_{t}(\gamma_{\pm}^{in}(s)))\,dt=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}\frac{(f\cdot V)(G_{0}(u,s))}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{m-1}{m}}}du.

Proof.

As

\Im(\gamma_{\pm}^{in}(s)^{m})=\Im(G_{0}(-\varepsilon+is)^{m})=s,\quad\Im(\gamma_{\pm}^{out}(s)^{m})=\Im(G_{0}(\varepsilon+is)^{m})=s,

in view of (

\gamma_{\pm}^{in}

Since the map
z\mapsto z^{m}
is a bijection between

, and

G_{0}
is its inverse, let us consider a local flow

on

restricted to

\displaystyle=m\,\psi_{t}(G_{0}(z))^{m-1}\frac{d}{dt}\psi_{t}(G_{0}(z))=m\,\psi_{t}(G_{0}(z))^{m-1}X(\psi_{t}(G_{0}(z)))

\displaystyle=m^{2}\frac{|\psi_{t}(G_{0}(z))|^{2(m-1)}}{V(\psi_{t}(G_{0}(z)))}=m^{2}\frac{|\tilde{\psi}_{t}(z)|^{\frac{2(m-1)}{m}}}{V\circ G_{0}(\tilde{\psi}_{t}(z))}.

It follows that the interval

is the incoming and
\{(\varepsilon,s):s\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\}
is the outgoing

.

to the flow

Then

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau(s)}f\circ G_{0}\big(\Re\tilde{\psi}_{t}(-\varepsilon,s),s\big)\,dt.

,

,

or

is homogenous of degree

and analytic on

-th root and

-th root of unity, then
G
is either
\omega^{l}G_{0}\text{ or }\omega^{l}\overline{G}_{0}\text{ for some }0\leq l<2m.

Let
f:{D}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a bounded Borel map where
D
is the pre-image of the square
[-1,1]\times[-1,1]

by the map
z\mapsto z^{m}
. For every
a\geq 1/2
let us consider the map
\varphi=\varphi_{f,a}:(0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
given by

(8.5)

\varphi(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(G(u,s))}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du.

Remark 8.4
.

Note that for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{f,a,\varepsilon}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{f(G(u,s))}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(G(u/\varepsilon,s))}{((u/\varepsilon)^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2a-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(\varepsilon^{-1/m}G(u,\varepsilon s))}{(u^{2}+(\varepsilon s)^{2})^{a}}\,du=\varepsilon^{2a-1}\varphi_{f\circ\varepsilon^{-1/m},a}(\varepsilon s).

Therefore

s^{2a}\varphi^{\prime}_{f,a,\varepsilon}(s)=(\varepsilon s)^{2a}\varphi^{\prime}_{f\circ\varepsilon^{-1/m},a}(\varepsilon s).

Notice that

s^{2a-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{((\frac{u}{s})^{2}+1)^{a}}\frac{du}{s}=\int_{0}^{1/s}\frac{dx}{(x^{2}+1)^{a}}.

Let us recall the definitions of Gamma function
\Gamma(z)
and Beta function
B(x,y)

B(x,y):=\int_{0}^{1}t^{x-1}{(1-t)}^{y-1}dt,\quad\Gamma(z):=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{z-1}e^{-x}dx,

and let us denote

\Gamma_{a}:=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{dx}{(x^{2}+1)^{a}}=\frac{1}{2}B(\frac{1}{2},a-\frac{1}{2})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\Gamma(a-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(a)}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\frac{\Gamma(a-\frac{1}{2})}{\Gamma(a)}.

Then, for every
a>1/2
,

(8.6)

\displaystyle s^{2a-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du\leq\Gamma_{a}\text{ for all }s\in(0,1],\text{ and}

(8.7)

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du=\Gamma_{a}.

If
a=1/2
then

(8.8)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du=\int_{0}^{1/s}\frac{dx}{\sqrt{x^{2}+1}}=\log\Big(\frac{1}{s}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{s^{2}}+1}\Big)\leq\log\frac{3}{s}\leq 2+|\log s|.

In view of (
8.6
) and (
8.8
), for every
s\in(0,1]
,

(8.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} s^{2a-1}\varphi_{|f|,a}(s)&\leq\|f\|_{\sup}\Gamma_{a}\ &\text{ if }\ a\geq 1/2,\\
\varphi_{|f|,a}(s)&\leq\|f\|_{\sup}(2+|\log s|)\ &\text{ if }\ a=1/2.\end{aligned}

In the following lemmas, we find the upper bound of
\varphi^{\prime}
by
C^{k}
-norms of the function
f
and the order of vanishing at the saddle.

Lemma 8.5
.

Suppose that
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
C^{1}
-map. For every
1/2\leq a\leq 1
we have

|s^{2a}\varphi^{\prime}(s)|\leq 2\|f\|_{C^{1}}\Gamma_{a+\frac{m-1}{2m}}.

Moreover,

\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a}\varphi^{\prime}(s)=-2af(0,0)\Gamma_{a+1}.

Proof.

First note that
\varphi
is a
C^{1}
-function on
(0,1]
with

(8.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\varphi^{\prime}(s)&=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(G(u,s))\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s}(u,s)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(G(u,s))\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s}(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du\\
&\quad-2a\int_{0}^{1}\frac{sf(G(u,s))}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}\,du.\end{split}

As
(G_{1}+iG_{2})^{m}=\pm u\pm is
, we have

(8.11)

m(G_{1}+iG_{2})^{m-1}\Big(\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s}+i\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s}\Big)=\pm i.

Hence

\Big|\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s}+i\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s}\Big|=\frac{1}{m|G_{1}+iG_{2}|^{m-1}}=\frac{1}{m(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{m-1}{2m}}}.

It follows that

(8.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Big|&\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(G(u,s))\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s}(u,s)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(G(u,s))\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s}(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du\Big|\\
&\leq\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}}{m}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+\frac{m-1}{2m}}}\,du\leq\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}}{m}\frac{\Gamma_{a+\frac{m-1}{2m}}}{s^{2a-\frac{1}{m}}}\leq\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}}{m}\frac{\Gamma_{a+\frac{m-1}{2m}}}{s^{2a}}\end{split}

and

\Big|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{sf(G(u,s))}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}\,du\Big|\leq\|f\|_{C^{0}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{s}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}\,du\leq{\|f\|_{C^{0}}}\frac{\Gamma_{a+1}}{s^{2a}}.

It follows that

|\varphi^{\prime}(s)|\leq\Big(2a\|f\|_{C^{0}}\Gamma_{a+1}+\frac{\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}}{m}\Gamma_{a+\frac{m-1}{2m}}\Big)\frac{1}{s^{2a}}\leq 2\|f\|_{C^{1}}\Gamma_{a+\frac{m-1}{2m}}\frac{1}{s^{2a}}.

Since
f
is of class
C^{1}
, we have

|f(G(u,s))-f(0,0)|\leq\|f\|_{C^{1}}\|G(u,s)\|\leq\|f\|_{C^{1}}(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{1}{2m}}.

Moreover, by (
8.6
),

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{1}{2m}}}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}\,du\leq\frac{\Gamma_{a+1-\frac{1}{2m}}}{s^{2a+1-\frac{1}{m}}}.

Therefore, in view of (
8.10
), (
8.12
), we have

\displaystyle\Big|

\displaystyle s^{2a}\varphi^{\prime}(s)+2af(0,0)s^{2a+1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq s^{2a}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(G(u,s))\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s}(u,s)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(G(u,s))\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s}(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du\right|

\displaystyle\quad+2as^{2a+1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|f(0,0)-f(G(u,s))|}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}du

\displaystyle=s^{2a}\|f^{\prime}\|_{C^{0}}\frac{\Gamma_{a+\frac{m-1}{2m}}}{s^{2a-\frac{1}{m}}}+2as^{2a+1}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{1}{2m}}}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}\,du

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{C^{1}}\Gamma_{a+\frac{m-1}{2m}}s^{\frac{1}{m}}+2as^{2a+1}\|f\|_{C^{1}}\frac{\Gamma_{a+1-\frac{1}{2m}}}{s^{2a+1-\frac{1}{m}}}=O(s^{\frac{1}{m}}).

Hence

\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a}\varphi^{\prime}(s)=-\lim_{s\to 0}2af(0,0)s^{2a+1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}=-2af(0,0)\Gamma_{a+1}.

∎

Lemma 8.6
.

Assume that
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a
C^{m}
-map,
1/2\leq a\leq 1
and let
k
be a natural number such that
k\leq m(2a-1)
.
Suppose that
f^{(j)}(0,0)=0
for
0\leq j<k
. Then

|s^{2a-\frac{k}{m}}\varphi^{\prime}(s)|\leq\frac{\|f\|_{C^{k}}\Gamma_{1}}{(k-1)!}\text{ for all }s\in(0,1].

Moreover, if
k<m(2a-1)
then

s^{2a-\frac{k}{m}-1}\varphi_{|f|,a}(s)\leq\frac{\|f\|_{C^{k}}\Gamma_{{a-\frac{k}{2m}}}}{k!}\text{ for all }s\in(0,1]

and if
k=m(2a-1)
, then

\varphi_{|f|,a}(s)\leq\frac{\|f\|_{C^{k}}}{k!}(2+|\log s|)\text{ for all }s\in(0,1].

Proof.

By assumption,

\displaystyle|f(G(u,s))|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}\|G(u,s)\|^{k}\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{k}{2m}},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(G(u,s))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|(\partial f/\partial x)^{(k-1)}\|_{C^{0}}}{(k-1)!}\|G(u,s)\|^{k-1}\leq\frac{\|(\partial f/\partial x)^{(k-1)}\|_{C^{0}}}{(k-1)!}(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{k-1}{2m}},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(G(u,s))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|(\partial f/\partial y)^{(k-1)}\|_{C^{0}}}{(k-1)!}\|G(u,s)\|^{k-1}\leq\frac{\|(\partial f/\partial y)^{(k-1)}\|_{C^{0}}}{(k-1)!}(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{k-1}{2m}}.

Therefore

\varphi_{|f|,a}(s)\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{k}{2m}}}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}du=\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a-\frac{k}{2m}}}du

and, by (
8.10
),

\displaystyle|\varphi^{\prime}(s)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{m(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{k-1}{2m}}}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{m-1}{2m}}(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}du

\displaystyle\quad+2as\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{k}{2m}}}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}du

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{m(k-1)!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+\frac{m-k}{2m}}}du+2as\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1-\frac{k}{2m}}}du.

As
k\leq m(2a-1)
, we have
a+1-\frac{k}{2m}>a+\frac{m-k}{2m}=1+\frac{(2a-1)m-k}{2m}\geq 1
.
By (
8.6
), this gives

\displaystyle|\varphi^{\prime}(s)|\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{m(k-1)!}\frac{\Gamma_{a+\frac{m-k}{2m}}}{s^{2a-\frac{k}{m}}}+2as\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}\frac{\Gamma_{a+1-\frac{k}{2m}}}{s^{2a+1-\frac{k}{m}}}\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{(k-1)!}\frac{\Gamma_{1}}{s^{2a-\frac{k}{m}}}.

If additionally
k<m(2a-1)
then
a-\frac{k}{2m}>\frac{1}{2}
. By (
8.6
) again,

\displaystyle\varphi_{|f|,a}(s)\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a-\frac{k}{2m}}}du\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}\frac{\Gamma_{a-\frac{k}{2m}}}{s^{2a-1-\frac{k}{m}}}.

On the other hand, if
k=m(2a-1)
then
a-\tfrac{k}{2m}=\tfrac{1}{2}
and, by (
8.8
),

\displaystyle\varphi_{|f|,a}(s)\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a-\frac{k}{2m}}}du\leq\frac{\|f^{(k)}\|_{C^{0}}}{k!}(2+|\log s|).

∎

8.2.
Quantities
C^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{f})

In this section, we develop some tools that help compute the non-vanishing quantities
C^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{f})
.

Definition 6
.

For every real
\beta
let
C_{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2})
be the space of
continuous homogenous functions
H:{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}\to{\mathbb{C}}
of degree
\beta
such that
H(u,s)=O(\|(u,s)\|^{\beta})
.

For every real
a
such that
2a-\beta>1
and
H\in C_{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2})
let

\Gamma_{a}(H):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{H(x,1)}{(x^{2}+1)^{a}}\,dx.

As
{H(x,1)}/{(x^{2}+1)^{a}}=O(1/(1+x)^{2a-\beta})
, the quantity
\Gamma_{a}(H)
is well-defined and

(8.13)

s^{2a-\beta-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{H(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{H(u/s,1)}{((u/s)^{2}+1)^{a}}\,\frac{du}{s}=\int_{0}^{1/s}\frac{H(x,1)}{(x^{2}+1)^{a}}\,dx\to\Gamma_{a}(H).

Note that if
H(u,s)=\tilde{H}(u,s)/\|(u,s)\|^{\max\{-\beta,0\}}
and
\tilde{H}:{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\times{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{C}}

is continuous homogenous of degree
\max\{\beta,0\}
then
H\in C_{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2})
.

Lemma 8.7
.

Assume that
2a-\beta>1
,
H\in C_{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2})
is of class
C^{1}
and
\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial y}\in C_{\beta-1}({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2})
. Then

(8.14)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{a}(\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial y})=2a\Gamma_{a+1}(H)-(2a-1-\beta)\Gamma_{a}(H).

Proof.

Note that for every
y>0
,

\Gamma_{a}(H)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{H(x,1)}{(x^{2}+1)^{a}}\,dx=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{H(x/y,1)}{(x^{2}/y^{2}+1)^{a}}\,\frac{dx}{y}=y^{2a-1-\beta}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{H(x,y)}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{a}}\,dx.

As
H\in C_{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2})
and
\tfrac{\partial H}{\partial y}\in C_{\beta-1}({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2})
, by differentiating with respect to
y
, we get

\displaystyle-(2a-1-\beta)y^{\beta-2a}\Gamma_{a}(H)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dy}y^{1+\beta-2a}\Gamma_{a}(H)=\frac{d}{dy}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{H(x,y)}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{a}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{\frac{\partial H}{\partial y}(x,y)}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{a}}\,dx-2ay\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{H(x,y)}{(x^{2}+y^{2})^{a+1}}\,dx.

Taking
y=1,
this yields (
8.14
).
∎

Recall that, by Lemma
8.5

for any
C^{1}
-map
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
with
f(0,0)\neq 0
and for every
1/2\leq a\leq 1
we have

\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a}\varphi^{\prime}(s)=-2af(0,0)\Gamma_{a+1}.

In the next preliminary lemmas, we find precise asymptotics of
\varphi^{\prime}
at zero also when the function
f
and some of its derivatives vanish at the saddle.
This plays a crucial role in calculating the quantity
C^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\varphi_{f})
explicitly in §
9
.

Lemma 8.8
.

Let
k
be an integer number such that
0\leq k\leq m(2a-1)
.
Suppose that
f:D\to{\mathbb{R}}
is of class
C^{k+1}
and
f^{(j)}(0,0)=0
for
0\leq j<k
. Then

(8.15)

\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a-\frac{k}{m}}\varphi^{\prime}(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{j}\partial y^{k-j}}(0,0)\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(G),

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(G)=-\frac{2a-1-\frac{k}{m}}{k}\Gamma_{a}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})-2a\frac{k-1}{k}\Gamma_{a+1}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})\text{ if }k\geq 1

and

\Gamma_{a}^{0,0}(G)=-2a\Gamma_{a+1}
.

Proof.

If
k=0
, then (
8.15
) follows directly from Lemma
8.5
.

Assume that
k\geq 1
. Then,
by assumptions,

\displaystyle f(G(u,s))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{j}\partial y^{k-j}}(0,0)G_{1}^{j}(u,s)G_{2}^{k-j}(u,s)+O(\|G(u,s)\|^{k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{j}\partial y^{k-j}}(0,0)G_{1}^{j}(u,s)G_{2}^{k-j}(u,s)+O((u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{k+1}{2m}}).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(G(u,s))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{j+1}\partial y^{k-1-j}}(0,0)G_{1}^{j}(u,s)G_{2}^{k-1-j}(u,s)+O((u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{k}{2m}}),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(G(u,s))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{j}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{j}\partial y^{k-j}}(0,0)G_{1}^{j}(u,s)G_{2}^{k-1-j}(u,s)+O((u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{k}{2m}}).

As
\|G(u,s)\|=\|(u,s)\|^{\frac{1}{m}}=(u^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{1}{2m}}
and
\|\frac{\partial G}{\partial s}(u,s)\|=\frac{\|(u,s)\|^{-\frac{m-1}{m}}}{m}=\frac{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{-\frac{m-1}{2m}}}{m}
, it follows that

(8.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{ds}\frac{f(G(u,s))}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}&=\frac{\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(G(u,s))\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s}(u,s)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(G(u,s))\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s}(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}-2a\frac{sf(G(u,s))}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}\\
&=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{j}\partial y^{k-j}}(0,0)\frac{\big(\frac{j}{k}G_{1}^{j-1}G_{2}^{k-j}\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s}+\frac{k-j}{k}G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-1-j}\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s}\big)(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\\
&\quad-2as\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\partial^{k}f}{\partial x^{j}\partial y^{k-j}}(0,0)\frac{(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}\\
&\quad+O\Big(\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+\frac{m-k-1}{2m}}}\Big)+O\Big(\frac{s}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1-\frac{k+1}{2m}}}\Big).\end{split}

Since
G_{1}^{j-1}G_{2}^{k-j}\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s}
,
G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j-1}\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s}
are homogenous of degree
\frac{k-m}{m}<2a-1
and
G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j}
is homogenous of degree
\frac{k}{m}<2(a+1)-1
, by (
8.13
) we have

(8.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a-\frac{k}{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(G_{1}^{j-1}G_{2}^{k-j}\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s})(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du=\Gamma_{a}(G_{1}^{j-1}G_{2}^{k-j}\tfrac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s}),\\
\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a-\frac{k}{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j-1}\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s})(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a}}\,du=\Gamma_{a}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j-1}\tfrac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s}),\\
\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a-\frac{k}{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{s(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})(u,s)}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1}}\,du=\Gamma_{a+1}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j}).\end{split}

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a-\frac{k+1}{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+\frac{m-k-1}{2m}}}\,du=\Gamma_{a+\frac{m-k-1}{2m}},

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0}s^{2a-\frac{k+1}{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{s}{(u^{2}+s^{2})^{a+1-\frac{k+1}{2m}}}=\Gamma_{a+1-\frac{k+1}{2m}}.

In view of (
8.16
) and (
8.17
), this gives the statement (
8.15
) with

\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(G)=\frac{j}{k}\Gamma_{a}(G_{1}^{j-1}G_{2}^{k-j}\tfrac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial s})+\frac{k-j}{k}\Gamma_{a}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-1-j}\tfrac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial s})-2a\Gamma_{a+1}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j}).

In view of (
8.14
) applied to
H=G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j}
(which is homogenous of degree
k/m
), we get

\displaystyle\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(G)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\Gamma_{a}\big(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial s}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})\big)-2a\Gamma_{a+1}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})

\displaystyle=\frac{2a}{k}\Gamma_{a+1}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})-\frac{2a-1-\frac{k}{m}}{k}\Gamma_{a}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})-2a\Gamma_{a+1}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})

\displaystyle=-\frac{2a-1-\frac{k}{m}}{k}\Gamma_{a}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})-2a\frac{k-1}{k}\Gamma_{a+1}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j}).

∎

Let
G_{0}:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}_{\geq 0}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the principal branch of the
m
-th root and let
\omega
and
\omega_{0}
be the principal
2m
-th and
4m
-th root of unity respectively.

Lemma 8.9
.

Let
1/2<a\leq(m-1)/m
,
1\leq k<(2a-1)m
and
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{k}
are real numbers not all equal to
0
. Then there exists
0\leq l<2m

such that

\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}\big(\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(\omega^{l}G_{0})+\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(\omega^{l+1}\overline{G_{0}})\big)\neq 0.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{G}:C_{k/m}({\mathbb{R}}_{>0}^{2})\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the linear operator given by

\mathfrak{G}(H):=-\frac{2a-1-\frac{k}{m}}{k}\Gamma_{a}(H)-2a\frac{k-1}{k}\Gamma_{a+1}(H).

Then
\overline{\mathfrak{G}(H)}=\mathfrak{G}(\overline{H})
and
\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(G)=\mathfrak{G}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})
.

Suppose that, contrary to our claim,

\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}\big(\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(\omega^{l}G_{0})+\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(\omega^{l+1}\overline{G_{0}})\big)=0\text{ for every }0\leq l<2m.

the linear space of homogenous polynomial of degree

The space

coincides with the subspace

of complex homogenous polynomials

of the form

such that
\overline{c}_{j}=c_{k-j}
for

. For every

denote by

.

As

is non-zero by assumptions, the corresponding polynomial

is also non-zero.
Note that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}\Gamma_{a}^{k,j}(G)&=\sum_{j=0}^{k}a_{j}\mathfrak{G}(G_{1}^{j}G_{2}^{k-j})={\mathfrak{G}}(Q(G_{1},G_{2}))\\
&=\mathfrak{G}(\widehat{Q}(G,\overline{G}))=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}\mathfrak{G}(G^{j}\overline{G}^{k-j}).\end{split}

As
k<m(2a-1)\leq m-2
, in view of (
8.18
) and (
8.19
), for every

we have

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}c_{j}\Big(\mathfrak{G}\big((\omega^{l}G_{0})^{j}(\overline{\omega^{l}G_{0}})^{k-j}\big)+\mathfrak{G}\big((\omega^{l+1}\overline{G_{0}})^{j}(\overline{\omega^{l+1}\overline{G_{0}}})^{k-j}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\omega^{-kl}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\omega^{2jl}c_{j}\big(\mathfrak{G}(G_{0}^{j}\overline{G_{0}}^{k-j})+\omega^{2j-k}\mathfrak{G}(\overline{G_{0}}^{j}{G_{0}}^{k-j})\big).

Let us consider the matrix

This gives

and

is non-zero, there exists

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\omega_{0}^{k-2j}\Big(\mathfrak{G}\big(G_{0}^{j}\overline{G_{0}}^{k-j}\big)+\omega_{0}^{2j-k}\overline{\mathfrak{G}\big({G^{j}_{0}}\overline{G_{0}}^{k-j}\big)}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{G}\big((\omega^{-1}_{0}G_{0})^{j}\overline{(\omega^{-1}_{0}G_{0})}^{k-j}\big)+\overline{\mathfrak{G}\big({(\omega^{-1}_{0}G_{0})}^{j}\overline{(\omega^{-1}_{0}G_{0})}^{k-j}\big)}.

Hence

Since

is the principal

-th root, for all

we have

. Hence

As

we have

which is a contradiction.

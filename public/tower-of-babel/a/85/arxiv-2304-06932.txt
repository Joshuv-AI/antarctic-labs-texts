The Grothendieck Ring of Certain Non-Noetherian Group-Graded Algebras via K K -Series
Nathaniel Gallup

Abstract
. With the goal of computing the Grothendieck group of certain multigraded infinite polynomial rings and the K K -series of infinite matrix Schubert spaces, we introduce a new type of Γ \Gamma -graded k k -algebra (which we call a PDCF algebra) and a new type of graded module (a BDF module) over said algebra. Since infinite polynomial rings are not Noetherian and the modules of interest are not finitely generated, we compensate by requiring certain finiteness properties of the grading group Γ \Gamma and of the grading itself. If R R is a PDCF Γ \Gamma -graded k k -algebra, we prove that every projective BDF Γ \Gamma -graded R R -module is free, and that when the graded maximal ideal of R R is generated by a regular sequence, a BDF analog of the Hilbert Syzygy Theorem holds: every BDF R R -module has a free resolution of BDF R R -modules which, though not finite, has the property that each graded piece is eventually zero. We use this to show that the Grothendieck group of projective BDF R R -modules (defined similarly to K 0 ​ ( R ) K_{0}(R) ) is isomorphic to the Grothendieck group of all BDF R R -modules (defined similarly to G 0 ​ ( R ) G_{0}(R) ). We describe this Grothendieck group explicitly by using K K -series to give an isomorphism with a certain space of formal Laurent series. Finally we give a BDF version of Serre’s formula for the product in the Grothendieck group, making it into a ring.



1.
Introduction

In
[
Gal21
]
we introduced a type of infinite-dimensional flag space
\text{Fl}_{\mathbb{N}}
which is a
k
-functor (i.e. a functor from the category of
k
-algebras to the category of sets, for
k
a field). This functor is covered disjointly by Schubert subfunctors
X^{\circ}_{\sigma}
which are indexed by permutations
\sigma
of
\mathbb{N}
, and which are quotients
\text{sh}(B\sigma B)/B
of (the fppf-sheafification of) the coset spaces
B\sigma B
. The affine analogs of these spaces, called matrix Schubert spaces, are quotients of a Cox ring with infinitely many columns (i.e. a polynomial ring
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}
where
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots
is an increasing sequence of natural numbers, and
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
are such that
i\leq n_{j}
) by an ideal
\mathfrak{d}_{\sigma}
generated by certain minors. There is a torus action of
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
(the abelian group of
\mathbb{N}
-indexed integer sequences with finitely many nonzero entries) on the
k
-functor represented by
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}
which corresponds to a multigrading of
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}
by
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
and since the ideal
\mathfrak{d}_{\sigma}
is also multigraded, it corresponds to an equivariant sheaf on this
k
-functor. This makes
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}/\mathfrak{d}_{\sigma}
into a
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
-graded
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}
-module. The goal of this paper is to compute the
K
-theory (i.e. Grothendieck group) of this multigraded polynomial ring. A forthcoming paper will then compute the
K
-series of
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}/\mathfrak{d}_{\sigma}
.

Much of the classical theory in the case of finite-dimensional flag varieties (see
[
MS05
]
for a thorough treatment) uses the fact that a polynomial ring in finitely many variables over a field is Noetherian and hence the matrix Schubert variety
k[x_{i,j}\mid 1\leq i,j\leq n]/\mathfrak{d}_{\sigma}
(with
\sigma\in S_{n}
) is finitely generated. But of course polynomial rings in infinitely many variables are not Noetherian and the ideal
\mathfrak{d}_{\sigma}
in this case is not finitely generated. Miraculously, however, the
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
-grading we care about has some other finiteness properties which still allow us to define a well-behaved graded Grothendieck group containing these modules. In particular, every graded piece of
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}
is finite-dimensional (such rings and modules are called
finite-dimensionally graded
), the
0
th graded piece of
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}
is just
k
(such graded rings are called
connected
), the graded support of
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}
is
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{(\mathbb{N})}
which is a pointed submonoid of
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
, and for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
, there are only finitely many
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
which are in the support of
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}
and satisfy
\beta_{i}\leq\alpha_{i}
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
(we call this property
downward finite
). Finally, the graded maximal ideal
\bigoplus_{\alpha>0}(k[x_{i,j}]_{n})_{\alpha}
is generated by the regular sequence consisting of all of the variables
x_{i,j}
.

It turns out that these properties are all that is needed define a Grothendieck group of a (not necessarily Noetherian) graded
k
-algebra which is an integral domain. Specifically, if
\Gamma
is an abelian group and
R
is such a
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra with support
Q\subseteq\Gamma
, then we say that
R
is
pointed
if
q+q^{\prime}=0
implies
q=q^{\prime}=0
for
q,q^{\prime}\in Q
,
downward finite
if for all
g\in\Gamma
the set
\{h\in Q\mid h\leq_{Q}g\}
is finite (the partial order on
\Gamma
is defined by setting
Q
to be the nonnegative elements),
connected
if
R_{0}=k
, and
finite-dimensionally graded
if
\dim_{k}(R_{g})
is finite for all
g\in\Gamma
(this condition is needed to define Hilbert series and
K
-series and is automatic if
R
is finitely generated as a
k
-algebra). We call algebras satisfying these conditions
PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebras
. Furthermore if
M
is a (not necessarily finitely generated)
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, we say that it is
finitely bounded below
if its
\Gamma
-support is bounded below by finitely many elements (the purpose of this condition is to guarantee closure of our category under tensor product), and we call such a module
M
a
BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
if additionally its support is downward finite and it is also finite-dimensionally graded (these conditions are automatic for finitely generated modules).

BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules over a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra share a shocking number of properties with finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-graded modules over a Noetherian
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-graded
k
-algebra. Preliminarily, the category
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma}
of such modules is abelian and closed under tensor product in the category of all
R
-modules. Furthermore, recall that the Quillen–Suslin theorem (
[
Qui76
]
,
[
Sus76
]
) says that every finitely-generated projective module over a polynomial ring is free. If one is satisfied with just finitely generated
graded
modules over a positively graded ring, then the proof that all such projective modules are free is substantially easier (see
[
MM11
]
, Section 2). We use a similar technique to that found in the latter reference to prove the following analogous result for BDF modules.

Theorem
.

Every projective BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module is free.

Recall the Hilbert Syzygy Theorem states that every finitely generated module over a Noetherian regular local ring or over a polynomial ring in finitely many variables has a finite free resolution. This is of course not true for BDF modules, but using the existence of minimal free resolutions of BDF modules, we adapt the proof given in
[
Eis13
]
(which uses
\operatorname{Tor}
) to obtain the following analog.

Theorem

(BDF Hilbert Syzygy Theorem)
.

If
R
is a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra with graded maximal ideal generated by a regular sequence, then every BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module has a resolution of BDF
\Gamma
-graded free modules with the property that each graded piece is eventually zero.

Classically, there are two common constructions of the Grothendieck group of a Noetherian ring. In the first,
K_{0}(R)
is defined to be the Grothendieck group completion of the set of isomorphism classes of finitely generated projective
R
-modules which is a commutative monoid under direct sum. In the second,
G_{0}(R)
is defined to be the free abelian group on the set of isomorphism classes of
all
finitely generated
R
-modules, modulo the relation
[M]-[N]+[L]=0
whenever there is a short exact sequence
0\to M\to N\to L\to 0
. Then there is an obvious map
K_{0}(R)\to G_{0}(R)
, and when
R
is either Noetherian regular local or a polynomial ring in finitely many variables, then the existence of a finite projective resolution gives an inverse to this map (see
[
Wei13
]
).

We make analogous definitions for BDF modules and prove the analogous result. Specifically we define
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
to be the Grothendieck group completion of the set of isomorphism classes of BDF
\Gamma
-graded projective
R
-modules, and
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
to be the free abelian group on the set of isomorphism classes of
all
BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules, modulo the same relation as before. We then prove:

Theorem
.

If
R
is a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra with graded maximal ideal generated by a regular sequence, the map
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
is an isomorphism.

Recall that homotopy invariance tells us that the Grothendieck ring
G_{0}(S)
of a polynomial ring
S=k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
in finitely many variables is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. If one is interested instead in graded modules over
S
with the standard
\mathbb{Z}
-grading, then the map
G_{\mathbb{Z},0}(S)\to\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
given by sending
[M]
to its
K
-polynomial is an isomorphism, where the latter denotes all Laurent polynomials. More generally, if
S
is
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-multigraded with graded support
Q
, the same
K
-polynomial map
G_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},0}(S)\to\mathbb{Z}[Q][\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
is again an isomorphism, where the latter denotes all Laurent polynomials in variables indexed by
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(see
[
MS05
]
Chapter 8). We give the following analogous result for BDF modules, with
\mathbb{Z}[Q][\mathbb{Z}^{d}]
replaced by
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
, the formal Laruent series with variables indexed by
\Gamma
still with finitely bounded below, but now also with downward finite, support. Note that in the Noetherian case,
K
-
polynomials
are honest Laurent polynomials, but because our modules are not finitely generated, we must pass to
K
-
series
.

Theorem
.

The map
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
, which sends
[F]
to its
K
-series, is an isomorphism.

Classically, the tensor product makes
K_{0}(R)
into a ring (called the Grothendieck ring) and the corresponding product in
G_{0}(R)
is given by Serre’s formula
[M]\cdot[N]=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)]
(see
[
Ser00
]
), the latter sum begin finite by the Hilbert Syzygy Theorem. For BDF modules, the tensor product does still make
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
into a ring, but in order to interpret Serre’s formula, we must make sense of an in infinite sum of elements of
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
. To that end we define a certain directed filtration of subgroups on
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
making it into a linear topological group, and show that the sum
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)]
converges. Finally we prove the following theorem:

Theorem
.

The isomorphisms
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}\cong K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\cong G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
are ring isomorphisms with the product in the first given by the usual product of formal Laurent series, in the second given by the tensor product, and in the third given by Serre’s formula.

2.1.
Families

In this paper, a

of elements of a set
X
is a function
a:I\to X
where
I
is an index set. We denote the image of
i\in I
under
a
by

and, to distinguish families from sets, we denote this family by

.

2.2.
Graded Rings

Throughout this paper
k
will denote a field
and
\Gamma
will denote an abelian group
. A
\Gamma
-grading
of a commutative
k
-algebra
R
is a function which assigns to every element
g\in\Gamma
a
k
-submodule
R_{g}\subseteq R
such that
R=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}R_{g}
and for all
g,h\in\Gamma
we have
R_{g}R_{h}\subseteq R_{g+h}
. A
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra
is a
k
-algebra
R
together with a
\Gamma
-grading of
R
. Hence we can write any element
r\in R
as a sum
r=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}r_{g}
where
r_{g}\in R_{g}
and only finitely many of the
r_{g}
’s are nonzero. Sine we will never put a topology on
R
and therefore cannot take sums with more than finitely many nonzero terms, we will often fail to mention the nonzero condition explicitly. Throughout this paper
R
will denote a
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra
which, for reasons explained in Section
3
,
we will always assume is an integral domain
(unless otherwise stated).

Example 1
.

When
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}
we obtain the usual notion of a graded ring.

Proposition 1
.

R_{0}
is a subalgebra of
R
.

Proof.

By definition
R_{0}
is a
k
-subspace and we also have
R_{0}\cdot R_{0}\subseteq R_{0}
hence
R_{0}
is closed under multiplication. Furthermore we can write
1=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}r_{g}
where
r_{g}\in R_{g}
and so for any
h\in\Gamma
we have
r_{h}=r_{h}\cdot 1=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}r_{h}r_{g}
. The LHS is in
R_{h}
, so the RHS must also be by the uniqueness of the direct sum. But
r_{h}r_{g}\in R_{h+g}
and the latter is equal to
R_{h}
if and only if
g=0
. Therefore
r_{g}r_{h}=0
for all
g\neq 0
and
r_{0}r_{h}=r_{h}
. In particular we have

\displaystyle r_{0}=r_{0}\cdot 1=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}r_{0}r_{g}=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}r_{g}=1.

Hence
1\in R_{0}
and so
R_{0}
is a subalgebra as desired.
∎

2.3.
Graded Modules

A
\Gamma
-grading
of an
R
-module
M
is a function which assigns to every element
g\in\Gamma
a
k
-submodule
M_{g}\subseteq M
such that
M=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}M_{g}
and for all
g,h\in\Gamma
we have
R_{g}M_{h}\subseteq M_{g+h}
. In particular
R
is itself a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module. Elements of
M
which are in
M_{g}
for some
g\in\Gamma
are called
homogeneous of degree
g
and if
m\in M_{g}
we write
\deg(m)=g
.

One can shift the grading of any graded module: if
M
is a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module and
g\in\Gamma
, then we denote by
M(g)
the
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module which is equal to
M
as an
R
-module but whose grading is given by
M(g)_{h}=M_{g+h}
.

Proposition 2
.

Let
M
be a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module. The following are equivalent for a submodule
N\subseteq M
.

(1)

N
is generated by homogeneous elements.

(2)

N=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}N\cap M_{g}
.

(3)

For any
n\in N
if we write
n=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}m_{g}
where
m_{g}\in M_{g}
then
m_{g}\in N
for all
g\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

((1)
\implies
(2)). By hypothesis the family
(N\cap M_{g}\mid g\in\Gamma)
is independent, and if
N
is generated by homogeneous elements, then every element of
N
can be written as a sum of homogeneous elements of
N
, so this family spans as well.

((2)
\implies
(3)). If we have
n=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}m_{g}
where
m_{g}\in M_{g}
, then because the family
(N\cap M_{g}\mid g\in\Gamma)
spans
M
, we can also write
n=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}n_{g}
where
n_{g}\in N\cap M_{g}
. Subtraction yields
0=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}m_{g}-n_{g}
with
m_{g}-n_{g}\in M_{g}
. But because the family
(M_{g}\mid g\in\Gamma)
is independent, it follows that
m_{g}=n_{g}\in N\cap M_{g}
as desired.

((3)
\implies
(1)). Given
n\in N
, since
M=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}M_{g}
, we can write
n=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}m_{g}
where
m_{g}\in M_{g}
. Then by hypothesis we have
m_{g}\in N\cap M_{g}
, so
N
is generated by the homogeneous elements
N\cap M_{g}
for
g\in\Gamma
.
∎

A submodule which satisfies the conditions of Proposition
2
is called
\Gamma
-graded
or just
graded
for short. An ideal of
R
is
\Gamma
-graded
if it is as a submodule of
R
. We now collect a technical results that we will need later.

Proposition 3
.

Let
M
be a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, and
N\subseteq M
a graded submodule. Then there is a
\Gamma
-grading of
M/N
given by
M/N=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}(M_{g}+N)/N
, where we identify
(M_{g}+N)/N
with the submodule of
M/N
spanned by elements of the form
m_{g}+N
for
m_{g}\in M_{g}
.

Proof.

Given
g,h\in\Gamma
, it is clear that
(M_{h}+N)/N
is a
k
-subspace of
M/N
and that
R_{g}\cdot(M_{g}+N)/N\subseteq(M_{g+h}+N)/N
. Furthermore it is also obvious that
M/N=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}(M_{g}+N)/N
. To show that the family
((M_{g}+N)/N\mid g\in\Gamma)
of
k
-subspaces of
M/N
is independent, we compute:

0=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}m_{g}+N=\left[\sum_{g\in\Gamma}m_{g}\right]+N\implies\sum_{g\in\Gamma}m_{g}\in N

Because
N
is graded, for all
g\in\Gamma
it follows that
m_{g}\in N
and hence
m_{g}+N=0
as desired.
∎

Proposition 4
.

Let
M
be a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, and
I\subseteq R
a graded ideal. Then
IM
is a graded submodule of
M
.

Proof.

Given
x=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}i_{j}m_{j}\in IM
, because
I
is graded, we can write
i_{j}=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}(i_{j})_{g}
and because
M
is graded we can write
m_{j}=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}(m_{j})_{g}
. Then we can write
x=\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\sum_{g,h\in\Gamma}(i_{j})_{g}(m_{j})_{h}
and
(i_{j})_{g}(m_{j})_{g}\in IM\cap M_{g+h}
, hence

is generated by homogeneous elements of the form

where
i\in I
is homogeneous and
m\in M
is homogeneous, so it is a graded submodule.
∎

2.4.
Homomorphisms of Graded Modules

If
M
and
N
are two
\Gamma
-graded

-modules we say that an

is
\Gamma
-graded
(or just

\varphi(M_{g})\subseteq N_{g}

.

Let

be
\Gamma
-graded

\ker\varphi
is a graded submodule of

\operatorname{im}\varphi

y_{g}\in(\operatorname{im}\varphi)_{g}
for some

such that
\varphi(x_{g})=y_{g}

Proof.

If
x\in\ker\varphi
and we write
x=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}x_{g}
then we have
0=\varphi(x)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\varphi(x_{g})
and since
\varphi
is graded, we have
\varphi(x_{g})\in N_{g}
. Because the family
(N_{g}\mid g\in\Gamma)
is independent, it follows that
\varphi(x_{g})=0
, and hence
x_{g}\in\ker f
as desired.

If

\sum_{g\in\Gamma}y_{a}=y=\varphi(x)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\varphi(x_{g})
. Since

∎

2.5.
Multigraded Polynomial Rings

In this paper we will mainly be concerned with
\Gamma
-graded polynomial rings
S:=k[x_{i}\mid i\in I]
such that the monomials
x^{\alpha}
(where
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{(I)}
) are all homogeneous (a
\Gamma
-multigrading
). Note that the degree of a monomial in the multigraded setting is completely determined by the degrees of the variables themselves.

Example 2
.

The main example we will be interested in throughout the paper involves the following polynomial ring which can be thought of as a Cox ring (see
[
HK00
]
) with infinitely many columns in the variable matrix. Suppose that
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots
is any sequence of natural numbers. We denote by
k[x_{i,j}]_{n}
the ring of polynomials in the variables
x_{i,j}
where
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
and
i\leq n_{j}
. We picture this as a matrix
X
with
(i,j)
entry equal to
x_{i,j}
whose
j
th column has zeros after the
(n_{j},j)
entry.

Define a
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
-multigrading on
k[x_{i,j}\mid i,j\in\mathbb{N}]_{n}
by
\deg(x_{i,j})=e_{j}
, where we denote the standard basis on
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
by
(e_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N})
. We denote the elements of
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
by
\vec{a}=(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots)
and recall that the parentheses in the notation “
\mathbb{N}
” require that
\vec{a}
has only finitely many nonzero entries.

3.
Finite Dimensionally Graded Rings and Modules

In this paper we will study polynomial rings in infinitely many variables which, we note, are not finitely generated as algebras over
k
. Because of this we will need to place other finiteness conditions on our rings and modules. In particular we will study the Grothendieck group of
R
using the Hilbert series of a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
M
and will therefore need
M_{g}
to be a finite-dimensional
k
-vector space for all
g\in\Gamma
. We call such a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
finite-dimensionally graded
(some authors call this
modest
). We certainly want
R
to be a finite-dimensionally graded module over itself and in this section we give a combination of (typically easy to check) conditions on
R
that will guarantee this.

If
M
is a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, we define the
\Gamma
-support
(or just support) of
M
to be the set
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)=\{g\in\Gamma\mid M_{g}\neq 0\}
. In the case when
M=R
, we define the
image monoid

Q
of the grading to be the submonoid of
\Gamma
generated by the set
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R)
. Note that by Proposition
1
,
1\in R_{0}
, hence
0\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R)
. However if
R
is
not
a domain, it is not always true that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R)
is closed under addition, as the following example shows.

Example 3
.

3.1.
Conditions on the Image Monoid

We now discuss the finiteness conditions we require on the image monoid of
R
, and begin with a brief summary of some necessary monoid terminology. Recall that a
monoid
consists of a set
Q
together with a binary operation on
Q
which is associative and has a (necessarily unique) identity. The monoid is called
commutative
if the binary operation is commutative. In this paper all monoids will be commutative, hence we use “
+
” to denote the binary operation and “
0
” to denote the identity.

A monoid
Q
is called
cancelative
if
p+u=q+u
implies
p=q
for all
p,q,u\in Q
. Any abelian group has an underlying commutative monoid and if
Q
is a submonoid of an abelian group
\Gamma
, it is automatically cancelative since every element of
Q
has an inverse in
\Gamma
. Conversely any cancelative commutative monoid can be embedded as a submonoid of an abelian group (see
[
Wei13
]
Chapter II Section 1).

Definition 1
.

A monoid
Q
is called
pointed
if
p+q=0
implies
p=q=0
for all
p,q\in Q
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
Q
be a pointed submonoid of an abelian group
\Gamma
. Define a relation
\leq_{Q}
on
\Gamma
by
g\leq_{Q}h
if and only if there exists
q\in Q
such that
g+q=h
. Then
\leq_{Q}
is a partial order.

Proof.

To check reflexivity, note that
g=g+0
and
0\in Q
, so
g\leq_{Q}g
. For antisymmetry note that if
g\leq_{Q}h
and
h\leq_{Q}g
then there exists
p,q\in Q
such that
b=a+p
and
a=b+q
. Then we have
a=a+p+q
so by cancelativity we have
0=p+q
and by pointedness of
Q
we have
p=q=0
, hence
g=h
. Finally for transitivity, if
g\leq_{Q}h
and
h\leq_{Q}u
then there exist
p,q\in Q
such that
h=g+p
and
u=b+q
, hence we have
u=g+(p+q)
and since
Q
is closed under addition,
p+q\in Q
, hence
g\leq_{Q}u
as desired.
∎

Note that for any
q\in Q
, we have
0\leq_{Q}q
. Furthermore, if
Q
is a pointed submonoid of an abelian group
\Gamma
and
g\leq_{Q}h
then
g+q=h
for some
q\in Q
and hence for any
u\in\Gamma
we have
g+u+q=h+u
, so
g+u\leq_{Q}h+u
. Thus
(\Gamma,\leq_{Q})
is a partially ordered group.

Definition 2
.

We say that a subset
U
of a poset
(P,\leq)
is
downward finite
if for all
p\in P
the set
\{u\in U\mid u\leq p\}
is finite.
If
\Gamma
is an abelian group and
Q\subseteq\Gamma
is a pointed submonoid, we say that a subset
U\subseteq\Gamma
is
downward finite
if it is with respect to the partial order
\leq_{Q}
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
\Gamma
be an abelian group and
Q\subseteq\Gamma
a pointed submonoid. Then
Q
itself is downward finite if and only if for all
p\in Q
the set
\{q\in Q\mid q\leq_{Q}p\}
is finite.

Proof.

The forward direction is clear. For the reverse, given
g\in\Gamma
if
\{q\in Q\mid q\leq_{Q}g\}
is empty it is certainly finite and if it is nonempty then there exists
q\in Q
such that
q\leq_{Q}g
so by definition there is some
q^{\prime}\in Q
with
q+q^{\prime}=g
which implies
g\in Q
, hence by hypothesis the set
\{q\in Q\mid q\leq_{Q}g\}
is finite.
∎

Definition 3
.

We say that a
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra
R
is
connected
if
R_{0}=k
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
R
be a connected
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra with image monoid
Q
which is pointed and downward finite. Let
S\subseteq R
be a set of homogeneous elements which generate
R
as a
k
-algebra. If
S_{g}:=R_{g}\cap S
is finite for all
g\in\Gamma
, then
R
is finite-dimensionally graded.

Proof.

Given
q\in Q
, every
r\in R_{q}
can be written as a polynomial in finitely many of the elements of
S
, say
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
, with coefficients in
k
. Because each element of
S
is homogeneous, each monomial in this polynomial is also homogeneous, hence we can write
r=f_{1}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})+\ldots+f_{m}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
where
f_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
is a
k
-linear combination of monomials each of which lies in
R_{q_{i}}
for
q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\in Q
distinct. We may assume without loss of generality that
q_{1}=q
. By independence of the family
(R_{g}:g\in\Gamma)
of
k
-subspaces of
R
, we must have
r=f_{1}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
and
f_{i}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})=0
for all
i>1
. Suppose that
s_{i}\in R_{q_{i}}
. Given any
1\leq i\leq n
, we may assume that
s_{i}
appears with nonzero exponent in at least one of the monomials in the expansion
r=f_{1}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
(otherwise we can simply leave it out of the list
s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}
at the beginning). Then if
\gamma s_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\ldots s_{n}^{\ell_{n}}
for
\gamma\in k
is such a monomial (i.e. with
\ell_{i}>0
), we have
q=\ell_{1}q_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{n}q_{n}
, and hence
\ell_{i}q_{i}\leq_{Q}q
, so since
\ell_{i}>0
, we have
q_{i}\leq_{Q}q
.

This implies that the monomials
s_{1}^{\ell_{1}}\ldots s_{n}^{\ell_{n}}
with
q=\ell_{1}q_{1}+\ldots+\ell_{n}q_{n}
and
s_{i}\in\bigcup_{p<_{Q}q}S_{p}
form a
k
-spanning set for
R_{q}
. Since
Q
is downward finite, the set
\{p\in Q\mid p<_{Q}q\}
is finite, and by hypothesis
S_{p}
is finite for all
p\in Q
, so
\dim_{k}(R_{q})
is finite.
∎

Definition 4
.

We call a
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra

that is connected and finite-dimensionally graded and whose image monoid is pointed and downward finite a
PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra
.

Example 4
.

Let

be a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded

. We make

into a
\Gamma

and

for all

. Then

which is obviously a pointed and downward finite submonoid of

,

We now check that the polynomial ring

4.
Finiteness Conditions on Graded Modules

Having defined, in the previous section, the finiteness conditions on
k
-algebra
R
that we will need, we now turn to finiteness conditions on modules. These will be important to guarantee that the resulting category of modules is closed under tensor product. We begin with the conditions on the support.

Definition 5
.

We say that a subset
U
of a poset
(P,\leq)
is
finitely bounded below
if there exists
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in P
such that for all
u\in U
there exists
1\leq i\leq n
such that
p_{i}\leq u
. In this case we say that
U
is
bounded below by
\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\}
. If
Q
is a pointed submonoid of an abelian group
\Gamma
, then we say that
U\subseteq\Gamma
is
finitely bounded below
if it is with respect to the partial order
\leq_{Q}
.

Note 1
.

In Section 8.2 of
[
MS05
]
, for
Q
a submonoid of an abelian group
\Gamma
, a Laurent series
a=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}\textbf{t}^{g}
is said to be
supported on finitely many translates of
Q
if the support of
a
is contained in
(h_{1}+Q)\cup\ldots\cup(h_{n}+Q)
for some
h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\in\Gamma
. As long as
Q
is pointed, this is equivalent to the support of
a
being a finitely bounded below subset of
\Gamma
with respect to
\leq_{Q}
. We use the latter terminology because we often refer to finitely bounded below subsets in a context broader than the support of a series or module.

Example 6
.

Consider the abelian group
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{N}}
with pointed submonoid
Q=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}
. The subset
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{\mathbb{N}}
is finitely bounded below, since
(0,0,\ldots)
is a lower bound, but not downward finite since the set of elements less than
(1,1,\ldots)
contains all of the standard basis vectors
e_{i}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots)
.

Example 7
.

Consider the abelian group
\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
with pointed submonoid
Q=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{(\mathbb{N})}
. The subset
U=\{e_{i}-e_{i+1}\mid i\in\mathbb{N}\}
is downward finite because given any
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots)\in\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
there exists
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n_{i}=0
for all
i\geq\ell
and then it cannot be that
e_{i}-e_{i+1}\leq_{Q}(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots)
for
i\geq\ell
. But
U
is not finitely bounded below since if
(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots)\in\mathbb{Z}^{(\mathbb{N})}
were smaller than infinitely many elements of
U
, infinitely many of the
m_{i}
’s would have to be negative.

If
R
is a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra and
M
a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, we say that
M
is
finitely bounded below
or that it is
downward finite
if
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)\subseteq\Gamma
satisfies the corresponding property.

Definition 6
.

If
M
is finitely bounded below, downward finite, and finite-dimensionally graded, we call it a
BDF

\Gamma
-graded
R
-module. We denote by
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
the full subcategory of the category of
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules consisting of the BDF modules.

Note 2
.

In Theorem 8.20 of
[
MS05
]
it is proved that in the multigraded case, finitely generated modules are finitely bounded below. It is easy to see in the more general case of modules over a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra that finitely generated modules are in fact BDF.

In the rest of this section, we will prove various results about the category
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
, and we begin by showing that it contains the ring
R
itself.

Proposition 9
.

If
R
is a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra then
R
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module.

Proof.

By definition of PDCF,
Q=\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R)
is downward finite. Furthermore for any
q\in Q
,
0\leq_{Q}q
and therefore
Q
is finitely bounded below. Again by definition of PDCF,
R
is a finite-dimensionally graded
k
-algebra, hence it is a finite-dimensionally graded
R
-module.
∎

Next we show that
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is closed under shifting of the grading.

Proposition 10
.

If
R
is a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra and
M
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module and
g\in\Gamma
then
M(g)
is also a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module.

Proof.

Note that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M(g))=\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)-g
. Therefore if
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)
is bounded below by
\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}
,
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M(g))
is bounded below by
\{g_{1}-g,\ldots,g_{n}-g\}
. Similarly if
h\in\Gamma
, then because
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)
is downward finite, the set
\{u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)\mid u\leq_{Q}h+g\}
is finite, hence the translation
\{u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)\mid u\leq_{Q}h+g\}-g
is also finite, but this set is equal to
\{v\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M(g))\mid v\leq_{Q}h\}
, which is therefore also finite. Finally given any
h\in\Gamma
,
M(g)_{h}=M_{g+h}
which is a finite dimensional
k
-vector space by hypothesis.
∎

Finally we show that
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is an abelian category, preceded by several necessary lemmas.

Lemma 1
.

Let
(P,\leq)
be a poset.

(1)

If
U\subseteq P
is finitely bounded below and
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
then
U^{\prime}
is also finitely bounded below.

(2)

If
U\subseteq P
is downward finite and
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
then
U^{\prime}
is also downward finite.

Proof.

(1)

If
U\subseteq P
is finitely bounded below then there exists
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
such that for all
u\in U
there exists
1\leq i\leq n
with
p_{i}\leq u
. As
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
, it is true the for all
u^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
there exists
1\leq i\leq n
such that
p_{i}\leq u^{\prime}
.

(2)

Given
p\in P
, we have that the set
\{u\in U\mid u\leq p\}
is finite, but because
U^{\prime}\subseteq U
it follows that
\{u^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}\mid u^{\prime}\leq p\}
is a subset of the aforementioned set and is therefore also finite.

∎

Lemma 2
.

If
R
is a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra,
M
is a BDF
R
-module, and
N\subseteq M
is a graded submodule, then
N
and
M/N
are also BDF
R
-modules.

Proof.

For any
g\in\Gamma
,
N_{g}=N\cap M_{g}
is a subspace of
M_{g}
. Therefore
\dim_{k}(N_{g})\leq\dim_{k}(M_{g})
, so since
M
is finite-dimensionally graded, so is
N
. Furthermore
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(N)\subseteq\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)
, so by Lemma
1
,
N
is finitely bounded below and downward finite.

Next, for any
g\in\Gamma
, there is a surjective
k
-linear map from
M_{g}
to
(M/N)_{g}=(M_{g}+N)/N
which sends
m_{g}\mapsto m_{g}+N
, the kernel of which is clearly
N_{g}
. Therefore
(M/N)_{g}
is isomorphic as a
k
-vector space to
M_{g}/N_{g}
, which is a quotient of a finite-dimensional vector space. Hence
M/N
is finite-dimensionally graded. Furthermore, this implies that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M/N)\subseteq\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)
, so again by Lemma
1
,
M/N
is finitely bounded below and downward finite.
∎

Theorem 2
.

Let
R
be a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra. Then
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is an abelian category (see
[
Sta21
]
for the definition of an abelian category).

Proof.

The category of
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules is an abelian category (see
[
NVO04
]
Section 2.2). Since
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is a full subcategory, it is a pre-additive, meaning its sets of morphisms are abelian groups and composition is bilinear. The zero module with the unique grading is clearly BDF. Finally finite direct sums of BDF modules are BDF since the support of a direct sum of graded modules is a union of the supports and finite union preserves being finitely bounded below and downward finite. Therefore
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is an additive category.

Now if
M,N
are BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules and
\varphi:M\to N
is a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module homomorphism, by Proposition
5
,
\ker\varphi\subseteq M
and
\operatorname{im}\varphi\subseteq N
are graded submodules, therefore by Lemma
2
,
\ker\varphi
and
\operatorname{coker}\varphi
are BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules. Thus
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is closed under kernels and cokernels and hence is a pre-abelian category. Finally, the natural map
M/\ker\varphi\to\operatorname{im}\varphi
is an isomorphism in the category of
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules (because this category is abelian) and because
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is a full subcategory, the inverse of this isomorphism is also in
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
, so abelianity follows.
∎

Note 3
.

We will henceforth assume that
R
denotes a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra that is a domain
unless otherwise stated.

.

We now show that if

5.
Tensor Product of BDF Modules

The goal of this section is to show that
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is not just an abelian category but is closed in the category of
R
-modules under tensor product. This will be important for two reasons in future sections: the first is that it will be needed to define minimal free resolutions of BDF modules and the second is that it will give us a product on the Grothendieck group.

5.1.
Hilbert Series

In order to show that the support of the tensor product of two BDF modules is again finitely bounded below and downward finite, we need to give the definition of the Hilbert series of a graded module, and prove that the product of two such series is well-defined.

Given an abelian group
\Gamma
, we define
\mathbb{Z}^{\Gamma}
to be the set of functions
a:\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}
and we identify
a
with its generating function
a(\textbf{t})=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}\textbf{t}^{g}
. We would like to define the product of two such series by the following formula.

(1)

\left(\sum_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}\textbf{t}^{g}\right)\left(\sum_{h\in\Gamma}b_{h}\textbf{t}^{h}\right)=\left(\sum_{u\in\Gamma}c_{u}\textbf{t}^{u}\right)

where
c_{u}=\sum_{g+h=u}a_{g}b_{h}
, but this is not well-defined in general since the sum defining
c_{u}
could be infinite. We restrict to a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{\Gamma}
on which this multiplication is actually well-defined.

Definition 7
.

Define
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
to be the subset of elements
a(\textbf{t})=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}\textbf{t}^{g}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\Gamma}
such that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(a):=\{g\in\Gamma\mid a_{g}\neq 0\}
is downward finite and finitely bounded below.

We think of elements of
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
as analogs of formal Laurent series. We now show that multiplication of two such series is well-defined and that
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
is closed under this product, but first we need several lemmas.

Lemma 3
.

Let
V,W\subseteq\Gamma
be finitely bounded below and downward finite. Given any
u\in\Gamma
, the sets
U=\{(g,h)\mid g\in V,h\in W,g+h\leq_{Q}u\}\subseteq\Gamma\times\Gamma
,
U_{1}=\{g\in V\mid\exists h\in W,g+h\leq_{Q}u\}
, and
U_{2}=\{h\in W\mid\exists g\in V,g+h\leq_{Q}u\}
are all finite.

Proof.

Consider the projection functions
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}:U\to\Gamma
which send
(g,h)
to
g
and
h
respectively. Given any
h\in\pi_{2}(U)
, there exists
g\in V
such that
g+h\leq_{Q}u
and since
V
is finitely bounded below, there exists
1\leq i\leq n
such that
g_{i}\leq_{Q}g
, which gives
g_{i}+h\leq_{Q}g+h\leq_{Q}u
and hence that
h\leq_{Q}u-g_{i}
. Since
W
is downward finite, there are only finitely many elements of
\{h\in W\mid h\leq_{Q}u-g_{i}\}
and since there are only finitely many
i
’s, it follows that
\pi_{2}(U)
is finite. Note that
\pi_{2}(U)=U_{2}
. A similar argument shows that
\pi_{1}(U)=U_{1}
is finite. But
U\subseteq\pi_{1}(U)\times\pi_{2}(U)
and since the latter is finite, so is the former.
∎

Lemma 4
.

If
V,W\subseteq\Gamma
are finitely bounded below and downward finite, then
V+W
is as well.

Proof.

Since
V
and
W
are finitely bounded below by hypothesis, there are subsets
\{g_{1},\ldots g_{n}\}
,
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}\}
such that every element of
V
is larger than an element of the former, and every element of
W
is larger than an element of the latter. Hence given
v+w\in V+W
there exists
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
such that
g_{i}\leq_{Q}v
and
h_{j}\leq_{Q}w
. Adding these inequalities together yields
g_{i}+h_{j}\leq_{Q}v+w
and hence every element of
V+W
is larger than one of the elements of the set
\{g_{i}+h_{j}\mid 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m\}
.

Given any
u\in\Gamma
, Lemma
3
implies that the set
U=\{(g,h)\mid g\in V,h\in W,g+h\leq_{Q}u\}
is finite, but the set
\{g\in V+W\mid g\leq_{Q}u\}
is the image of
U
under the map
(g,h)\mapsto g+h
, so this set is finite too.
∎

If
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
, by Lemma
3
, all but finitely many of the terms in the sum
c_{u}=\sum_{g+h=u}a_{g}b_{h}
from Equation
1
are zero, so the product of two elements of
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
exists in
\mathbb{Z}^{\Gamma}
. We now show that this product is actually again in
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
.

Lemma 5
.

Given
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
, we have that
ab\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
.

Proof.

If
u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(ab)
then there must exist
g\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(a)
and
h\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(b)
such that
g+h=u
, i.e.
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(ab)\subseteq\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(a)+\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(b)
. By Lemma
4
, because
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(a)
and
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(b)
are finitely bounded below and downward finite,
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(a)+\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(b)
is too. Hence by Lemma
1
,
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(ab)
is also finitely bounded below and downward finite.
∎

It is easy to see that the multiplication defined by Equation
1
makes
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
into a commutative ring with multiplicative identity equal to
1=\textbf{t}^{0}
. We can now define Hilbert functions of BDF modules.

Definition 8
.

If
M
is a finite-dimensionally
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, the
Hilbert function
of
M
is the function
\Gamma\to\mathbb{N}
given by
g\mapsto\dim_{k}(M_{g})
. The associated generating function
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\dim_{k}(M_{g})\textbf{t}^{g}
is called the
Hilbert series
of
M
. By definition
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)
is an element of
\mathbb{Z}^{\Gamma}
. Note that if
M
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module then
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
.

5.2.
Tensor Product of BDF Modules

We are now in a position to define the
\Gamma
grading on the tensor product of two BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules and show that the result is still BDF.

Proposition 11
.

Let
M
and
N
be BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules. Then
M\otimes_{R}N
is a
\Gamma
-graded BDF
R
-module with
(M\otimes_{R}N)_{u}=\text{span}_{k}(x\otimes y\in M\otimes_{R}N\mid x\in M_{g},y\in N_{h},g+h=u)
for
u\in\Gamma
.

Proof.

First we show that
M\otimes_{R}N=\bigoplus_{u\in\Gamma}(M\otimes_{R}N)_{u}
. Note that as
k
-vector spaces we have
M=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}M_{g}
and
N=\bigoplus_{h\in\Gamma}N_{h}
. Therefore because tensor product commutes with direct sum, we have
M\otimes_{k}N=\bigoplus_{g,h\in\Gamma}M_{g}\otimes_{k}N_{h}=\bigoplus_{u\in\Gamma}\left[\bigoplus_{g+h=u}M_{g}\otimes_{k}N_{h}\right]
. Therefore
M\otimes_{k}N
is a
\Gamma
-graded
k
-vector space with
(M\otimes_{k}N)_{u}=\bigoplus_{g+h=u}M_{g}\otimes_{k}N_{h}
. Let
L
be the
k
-subspace of
M\otimes_{k}N
spanned by elements of the form
rx\otimes y-x\otimes ry
where
r\in R
and
x\in M
,
y\in N
are all homogeneous. There is a
k
-linear map
\varphi:M\otimes_{k}N\to M\otimes_{R}N
sending
x\otimes y\mapsto x\otimes y
and clearly
L
is contained in the kernel of
\varphi
, so we obtain a
k
-linear map
\overline{\varphi}:(M\otimes_{k}N)/L\to M\otimes_{R}N
. Conversely we define a map
\psi^{\prime}:M\times N\to(M\otimes_{k}N)/L
by sending
(x,y)\mapsto\overline{x\otimes y}
. This is
R
-balanced because given
r=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}r_{g}\in R
,
x=\sum_{h\in\Gamma}x_{h}\in M
, and
y=\sum_{u\in\Gamma}y_{u}\in N
, we have that

\psi^{\prime}(rx,y)=\overline{(rx)\otimes y}=\overline{\sum_{g,h,u\in\Gamma}r_{g}x_{h}\otimes y_{u}}=\overline{\sum_{g,h,u\in\Gamma}x_{h}\otimes r_{g}y_{u}}=\overline{x\otimes(ry)}=\psi^{\prime}(x,ry).

We therefore obtain a map
\psi:M\otimes_{R}N\to(M\otimes_{k}N)/L
which is clearly the inverse of
\overline{\varphi}
, hence
\overline{\varphi}
is an isomorphism.

Now if
r\in R_{g}
,
x\in M_{h}
, and
y\in N_{u}
, then
rx\otimes y\in M_{g+h}\otimes_{k}N_{u}
and
x\otimes ry\in M_{g}\otimes_{k}N_{h+u}
, and therefore
rx\otimes y-x\otimes ry\in(M\otimes_{k}N)_{g+h+u}
, hence
L
is spanned by homogeneous elements and is therefore a graded submodule. Thus
(M\otimes_{k}N)/L
is a
\Gamma
-graded
k
-vector space. Using the
k
-linear isomorphism
(M\otimes_{k}N)/L\cong M\otimes_{R}N
from above and the fact that image of
(M\otimes_{k}N)_{u}
under
\varphi
is exactly
(M\otimes_{R}N)_{u}=\text{span}_{k}(x\otimes y\in M\otimes_{R}N\mid x\in M_{g},y\in N_{h},g+h=u)
, we obtain that
M\otimes_{R}N=\bigoplus_{u\in\Gamma}(M\otimes_{R}N)_{u}
so indeed
M\otimes_{R}N
is a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module.

Now we use the Hilbert series machinery developed above to show that
M\otimes_{R}N
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module. We begin with
M\otimes_{k}N
. By definition, for any
u\in\Gamma
,
(M\otimes_{k}N)_{u}=\bigoplus_{g+h=u}M_{g}\otimes_{k}N_{h}
and by Lemma
3
since
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)
and
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(N)
are finitely bounded below and downward finite, the set
\{(g,h)\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)\times\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(N)\mid g+h=u\}
is finite, and since
M
and
N
are finite-dimensionally graded,
M_{g}
and
N_{h}
are finite-dimensional vector spaces, so
(M\otimes_{k}N)_{u}
is a finite-dimensional vector space of dimension
\sum_{g+h=u}\dim_{k}(M_{g})\dim_{k}(N_{h})
. Hence
M\otimes_{k}N
is a finite-dimensionally graded
k
-vector space with Hilbert series given by
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M\otimes_{k}N)=\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(N)
. By Lemma
5
we have that
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M\otimes_{k}N)\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
, and hence
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M\otimes_{k}N)
is finitely bounded below and downward finite. Since
M\otimes_{R}N
is a quotient of
M\otimes_{k}N
by a graded subspace, it follows that
M\otimes_{R}N
is finite-dimensionally graded too, and that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M\otimes_{R}N)
is finitely bounded below and downward finite as well.
∎

Note 4
.

A similar proof to that of Proposition
11
shows that if
R
is a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra and
M_{1},\ldots,M_{n}
are BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules, then if we define
(M_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}M_{n})_{g}=\text{span}_{k}(x_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes x_{n}\mid x_{i}\in M_{h_{i}},\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}=g)
for
g\in\Gamma
, then
M_{1}\otimes_{R}\ldots\otimes_{R}M_{n}
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module.

Finally we record the fact that the tensor product of two graded maps is graded, which will be useful later.

Proposition 12
.

Let
M,M^{\prime},N,N^{\prime}
be BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules, and let
\varphi:M\to M^{\prime}
,
\psi:N\to N^{\prime}
be graded
R
-linear maps. Then
\varphi\otimes\psi:M\otimes_{R}N\to M^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N^{\prime}
is also graded.

Proof.

By Proposition
11
,
(M\otimes_{R}N)_{u}
is spanned by elements of the form
x\otimes y
where
\deg(x)+\deg(y)=u
. Given
x\otimes y\in M\otimes_{R}N
with
x\in M_{g}
,
y\in N_{h}
and
g+h=u
, since
\varphi
and
\psi
are graded, we have that
\varphi(x)\in M^{\prime}_{g}
and
\psi(y)\in N^{\prime}_{h}
, and hence
[\varphi\otimes\psi](x\otimes y)=\varphi(x)\otimes\psi(y)\in(M^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N^{\prime})_{u}
. Hence indeed
\varphi\otimes\psi
sends
(M\otimes_{R}N)_{u}
to
(M^{\prime}\otimes_{R}N^{\prime})_{u}
as desired.
∎

6.
Free and Projective BDF Modules

In this section we’ll show that every projective BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module is in fact free. We begin by describing
\Gamma
-graded free modules. To build these, we will want to take infinite direct sums of shifted copies of
R
, but we must place restrictions on such direct sums to insure that the resulting free module is still BDF. The following definition gives the necessary restriction.

Definition 9
.

Let
Q
be a pointed submonoid of an abelian group
\Gamma
. A family of elements
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)
of
\Gamma
is called a
BDF family
if the set
\{g_{i}\mid i\in I\}\subseteq\Gamma
is downward finite and finitely bounded below and for each
g\in\Gamma
, the set
\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}
is finite. If
M
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, a family of homogenous elements
(m_{i}\mid i\in I)
of
M
is called a
BDF family
if each
m_{i}
is nonzero and
(\deg(m_{i})\mid i\in I)
is a BDF family of elements of
\Gamma
.

We now show that the direct sum of a BDF family of shifted copies of
R
is again BDF.

Proposition 13
.

If
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)
is a BDF family of elements of
\Gamma
then the free module
F=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module.

Proof.

First note that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(F)=\bigcup_{i\in I}\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))
. By hypothesis the set
\{g_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is finitely bounded below, say by
\{h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}\}
, and we claim that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(F)
is also bounded below by this set. Then given
u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(F)
, there exists
i\in I
such that
u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))=\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R)+g_{i}
and hence we have that
u-g_{i}\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R)
so
0\leq_{Q}u-g_{i}
, which gives
g_{i}\leq_{Q}u
. But because
\{g_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is bounded below by
\{h_{1}\ldots,h_{n}\}
, then there exists
1\leq j\leq n
such that
h_{j}\leq_{Q}g_{i}
, and hence
h_{j}\leq_{Q}u
, as desired.

We now claim that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(F)
is downward finite. Given
g\in\Gamma
, we have that
\{u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(F)\mid u\leq_{Q}g\}=\bigcup_{i\in I}\{u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))\mid u\leq_{Q}g\}
. But if
u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))
, then as above
g_{i}\leq_{Q}u
so if we also have
u\leq_{Q}g
, it follows that
g_{i}\leq_{Q}g
. However by hypothesis there are only finitely many
g_{i}
with
g_{i}\leq_{Q}g
, say
g_{i_{1}}\ldots,g_{i_{n}}
. Hence we have that
\{u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(F)\mid u\leq_{Q}g\}=\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{n}\{u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i_{\ell}}))\mid u\leq_{Q}g\}
. Since
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R)
is downward finite, by Proposition
10
,
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i_{\ell}}))
is also downward finite, and hence
\{u\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(F)\mid u\leq_{Q}g\}
is a finite union of finite sets.

Finally we claim that
F
is finite dimensionally graded. By definition we have that
F_{h}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})_{h}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R_{h-g_{i}}
. By definition of
Q=\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R)
,
R_{h-g_{j}}=0
unless
h-g_{j}\geq_{Q}0
which is equivalent to
g_{j}\leq_{Q}h
. Since the set
\{g_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is downward finite, there are only finitely many such
g_{j}
. By hypothesis, for each of these
g_{j}
, there are only finitely many
i\in I
such that
g_{i}=g_{j}
. Therefore the direct sum
\bigoplus_{i\in I}R_{h-g_{i}}
is in fact a finite direct sum of finite-dimensional
k
-vector spaces as desired.
∎

For each
i\in I
, we denote the image of
1\in R(g_{i})
under the inclusion map
R(g_{i})\hookrightarrow F
by
e_{i}
so that
(e_{i}\mid i\in I)
is a homogeneous basis for
F
. Note that if we forget the grading,
F=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(g_{i})
is equal to the free
R
-module
R^{(I)}
.

In order to show that projective BDF modules are free, we will need to use the fact that for every BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
M
, there exists a free BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
F
and a graded surjection
F\to M
such that the images of a homogeneous basis for
F
are a
minimal
BDF
R
-spanning family for
M
. That such a map exists for a given BDF family in
M
is easy to see, as the following lemma shows.

Lemma 6
.

Given a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
M
and a BDF family
(x_{i}\mid i\in I)
of elements of
M
, there exists a BDF
\Gamma
-graded free
R
-module
F=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})
and a graded
R
-linear map
\varphi:F\to M
such that the image of the basis
(e_{i}\mid i\in I)
of
F
is
(x_{i}\mid i\in I)
. In particular if
(x_{i}\mid i\in I)

R
-spans
M
then this map is surjective.

Proof.

For any
i\in I
, let
g_{i}=\deg(x_{i})
. Because
(x_{i}\mid i\in I)
is a BDF family, Proposition
13
implies that
F=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})
is a
\Gamma
-graded free
R
-module. Then we can define an
R
-linear map
\varphi:F\to M
by sending
e_{i}\mapsto x_{i}
which is graded because
\deg(e_{i})=g_{i}=\deg(x_{i})
.
∎

We must now prove the existence of a minimal BDF
R
-spanning family in any BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
M
. Unsurprisingly first we need a BDF
\Gamma
-graded version of Nakayama’s Lemma, preceded by a necessary lemma about the unique graded maximal ideal of
R
and the graded residue field.

Lemma 7
.

The set
R_{+}:=\bigoplus_{q>_{Q}0}R_{q}
is the unique graded maximal ideal of
R
and
R/R_{+}\cong k
in the category of PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebras (
k
is a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra as in Example
4
).

Proof.

Define a function
(-)_{0}:R\to k
by sending
r=\sum_{q\in Q}r_{q}\mapsto r_{0}
. This is a
\Gamma
-graded
k
-linear map, and it respects multiplication because if
r=\sum_{p\in Q}r_{p}
and
s=\sum_{q\in Q}s_{q}
then
(rs)_{0}=\sum_{p+q=0}r_{p}s_{q}
and since
Q
is pointed, if
p+q=0
then
p=0
and
q=0
, hence
(rs)_{0}=r_{0}s_{0}
. Therefore
(-)_{0}
is a graded surjective
k
-algebra homomorphism whose kernel is clearly
R_{+}
, so
R_{+}
is a graded maximal ideal. Furthermore suppose that
I=\bigoplus_{q\in Q}R_{q}\cap I
is a graded ideal of
R
. If
R_{0}\cap I\neq 0
then
I
contains a unit and so is equal to
R
. Hence if
I
is proper, it must be that
R_{0}\cap I=0
and hence
I\subseteq R_{+}
. It follows that
R_{+}
is the unique graded maximal ideal of
R
.

∎

The previous lemma shows that we can view PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebras much like local rings and this analogy will be very advantageous. In particular it gives our BDF analog of the graded version of Nakayama’s Lemma, preceded by a lemma about the well-foundedness of downward finite sets.

Lemma 8
.

If a subset
U
of a poset
(P,\leq)
is downward finite then
(U,\leq)
is well-founded.

Proof.

Suppose that
U
is not well-founded. Then there exists a strictly infinite descending chain
u_{1}>u_{2}>\ldots
in
U
and so clearly the set
\{u\in U\mid u\leq u_{1}\}
is not finite, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 9

(BDF
\Gamma
-graded Nakayama’s Lemma)
.

If
R
is a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra,
M
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, and
R_{+}M=M
, then
M=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
M\neq 0
. Then
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)
is non-empty and downward finite, so it has a minimal element with respect to
\leq_{Q}
by Lemma
8
, call it
h
. Note that
R_{+}M=\sum_{q>_{Q}0,g\in\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)}R_{q}M_{g}
, but
R_{q}M_{g}\subseteq M_{g+q}
and since
q>_{Q}0
it follows that
g+q>_{Q}g
. Hence every element of
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R_{+}M)
must be strictly larger than some element of
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)
. In particular,
h\notin\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(R_{+}M)
, which shows that
R_{+}M\neq M
.
∎

We need one more technical lemma before we can discuss minimal
R
-spanning families. It describes how tensoring a BDF module
M
with
k\cong R/R_{+}
as an
R
-module is the same as quotienting
M
by
R_{+}M
. The graded
k
-algebra homomorphism
(-)_{0}:R\to k
from the proof of Lemma
7
makes
k
into a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module with multiplication
R\times k\to k
given by sending
r,\gamma\mapsto r_{0}\gamma
. Then
k
is in fact a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module because
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(k)=\{0\}
is clearly finitely bounded below and downward finite.

Lemma 10
.

For any BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
M
there is a graded
R
-linear isomorphism
M\otimes_{R}k\cong M/R_{+}M
.

Proof.

First we define a map
\varphi:M\times k\to M/R_{+}M
which sends
x,\gamma\mapsto\gamma\overline{x}
. This map is obviously
k
-bilinear and it is
R
-balanced because given
r=\sum_{q\in Q}r_{q}\in R
we have

\varphi(rx,\gamma)=\gamma\overline{\sum_{q\in Q}r_{q}x}\overset{(1)}{=}\gamma\overline{r_{0}x}=\gamma r_{0}\overline{x}=\varphi(x,r_{0}\gamma)=\varphi(x,r\gamma)

where (1) follows because
\sum_{q\in Q}r_{q}x=r_{0}x+\sum_{q>0}r_{q}x
and the latter term is in
R_{+}M
. Hence we obtain an
R
-linear map
\tilde{\varphi}:M\otimes_{R}k\to M/R_{+}M
. The degree of
x\otimes\gamma
is
\deg(x)+\deg(\gamma)=\deg(x)=\deg(\overline{x})
so this map is indeed graded. On the other hand we define a map
\psi:M\to M\otimes_{R}k
by sending
x\mapsto x\otimes 1
which is clearly
R
-linear and the kernel of
\psi
contains
R_{+}M
since given
rx
where
r\in R_{q}
for
q>0
and
x\in M_{g}
we have
\psi(rx)=rx\otimes 1=x\otimes r\cdot 1
, but
r\cdot 1=0
since
r\in R_{+}
, hence
\psi(rx)=0
. Since
R_{+}M
is spanned by elements of the same form as
rx
, it follows that
\ker\psi\subseteq R_{+}M
. Hence we obtain a map
\overline{\psi}:M/R_{+}M\to M\otimes_{R}k
. The maps
\tilde{\varphi}
and
\overline{\psi}
are obviously inverses of each other.
∎

We are now finally in a position to discuss minimal
R
-spanning families. The following proposition describes them in detail.

Proposition 14
.

Let
M
be a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module,
(x_{i}\mid i\in I)
be a BDF family of elements of
M
with
\deg(x_{i})=g_{i}
,
F=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})
, and
\varphi:F\to M
be the
R
-linear map sending
e_{i}\mapsto x_{i}
.

(1)

The following are equivalent:

(a)

(\overline{x_{i}}\mid i\in I)

k
-spans
M/R_{+}M
.

(b)

(x_{i}\mid i\in I)

R
-spans
M
.

(c)

\operatorname{im}\varphi=M
.

(2)

If
(\overline{x}_{i}\mid i\in I)
is
k
-independent then
\ker\varphi\subseteq R_{+}F
.

(3)

The following are equivalent:

(a)

(\overline{x_{i}}\mid i\in I)
is a
k
-basis of
M/R_{+}M
.

(b)

(x_{i}\mid i\in I)
is a minimal
R
-spanning family for
M
in the sense that no proper subfamily
(x_{j}\mid j\in J)
with
J\subsetneq I

R
-spans
M
.

(c)

(x_{i}\mid i\in I)

R
-spans
M
and
\ker\varphi\subseteq R_{+}F
.

Proof.

(1)

(b)
\iff
(c) is obvious. To prove (a)
\implies
(c), suppose that
(\overline{{}_{i}}\mid i\in I)

k
-spans
M/R_{+}M
. Let
C
be the cokernel of
\varphi
so that we obtain an exact sequence
F\to M\to C\to 0
. Since the functor
-\otimes_{R}k
is right exact, we obtain an exact sequence
F\otimes_{R}k\to M\otimes_{R}k\to C\otimes_{R}k\to 0
. We claim that
\varphi\otimes\text{id}
is surjective. Indeed, by the proof of Lemma
10
, under the isomorphism
M/R_{+}M\cong M\otimes_{R}k
, the element
\overline{x_{i}}
corresponds to

6.1.
Minimal Free Resolutions of BDF Modules

We conclude this section with an application of the theory of minimal spanning sets we just developed to the existence of minimal free resolutions. These are free resolutions which map homogeneous bases onto minimal generating sets, and therefore, in some sense have the smallest possibly syzygy modules. They will be of importance to us when proving the BDF Hilbert Syzygy Theorem.

Lemma 11
.

Let

\Gamma
-graded

-module and consider the following free resolution in the category

(1)

\operatorname{im}\varphi_{n}\subseteq R_{+}F_{n-1}

For every

Proof.

(1)
\iff
(2). First of all, for any any

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{n}\otimes_{R}k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{n-1}\otimes_{R}k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{n}/R_{+}F_{n}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{n-1}/R_{+}F_{n-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi_{n}\otimes\text{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{n-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{\varphi}_{n}}

(1)

(3). Given

Lemma 14
.

If
M

\Gamma

R

\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M(-g))=\textbf{t}^{g}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)

Lemma 15
.

If
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)

Lemma 16
.

Let
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)
be a BDF family of elements of
\Gamma
and let

. In particular

By definition

and

if and only if

. Therefore the direct sum can be taken over the set

since it is equal to a direct sum over the empty set. Conversely, if there exists

then

so

.

Theorem 4

.

be an PDCF
\Gamma

\Gamma
-graded

-module

\Gamma
-finite free resolution of

.

Proof.

If
(r_{i}\mid i\in\mathbb{N})
is a homogeneous regular sequence on

then by Proposition

\Gamma
-finite free resolution of

. Tensoring the Koszul complex with

which is
\Gamma
-finite by Corollary

, and the
i
th homology of which is
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(k,M)
. By Proposition
15
, there exists a minimal BDF
\Gamma
-graded free resolution
F_{\bullet}
of
M
. Tensoring with
k
gives a complex
F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}k
the
i
th homology of which is
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,k)
. But by Proposition

, we have that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,k)_{g}\cong\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(k,M)_{g}
for all

, hence the former is zero if and only if the latter is. Since
K_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}M
is
\Gamma
-finite, for all

there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
[K_{i}\otimes_{R}M]_{g}=0
, and hence
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(k,M)=0
, and hence
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,k)_{g}=0
for all

.

Because the resolution
F_{\bullet}
of
M
is minimal, the differentials of the complex
F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}k
are all
0
by Lemma
11
, and hence
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(M,k)_{g}=(F_{i}\otimes_{R}k)_{g}
. But by Lemma
17
,
(F_{i}\otimes_{R}k)_{g}=0
if and only if
(F_{i})_{g}=0
, therefore
F_{\bullet}
is a
\Gamma
-finite free resolution of
M
as desired.
∎

9.
Projective
\Gamma
-Dimension of a BDF Module

Although we will not need it in the sequel, we give an application of the previous section to defining the graded projective dimension of a BDF module and we show that it is equal to a certain graded torsion dimension, just as in the ungraded finitely generated case (see
[
Eis13
]
Corollary 19.5).

Let
M
be a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module. The
projective
\Gamma
-dimension
of
M
is the function
\text{pd}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M):\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
which sends
g\in\Gamma
to the minimum
n
such that there exists a BDF projective resolution
P_{\bullet}
of
M
with
(P_{n})_{g}\neq 0
but
(P_{i})_{g}=0
for all
i>n
. The
torsion
\Gamma
-dimension
of
M
is the function
\text{td}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M):\Gamma\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}
which sends
g\in\Gamma
to the minimum
n
such that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{n}(k,M)_{\leq g}\neq 0
but
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(k,M)_{\leq g}=0
for all
i>n
. In
[
MS05
]
the
i
th graded Betti number of
M
in degree
g
is defined to be
\beta_{i,g}(M)=\dim_{k}\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(k,M)_{g}
, so in this language the torsion dimension
\text{td}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)
is just the first
n
such that all Betti numbers
\beta_{n,h}(M)
for
h\leq_{Q}g
vanish.

Proposition 22
.

If
M
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, then for each
g\in\Gamma
, we have
\text{pd}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)=\text{td}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)
.

Proof.

First we show that
\text{pd}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)\geq\text{td}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)
. This is trivially true if
\text{pd}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)=\infty
, so suppose that
\text{pd}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)=n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then there exits a BDF free resolution
F_{\bullet}
of
M
such that
(F_{i})_{g}=0
for all
i>n
. Tensoring with
k
yields a complex
F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}k
which, by Lemma
17
, has the property that
(F_{i}\otimes_{R}k)_{\leq g}=0
for all
i>n
so
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(k,M)_{\leq g}=0
for all
i>n
.

Next we show that
\text{pd}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)\leq\text{td}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)
. Again this is trivial if
\text{td}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)=\infty
so suppose that
\text{td}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)=n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. By Proposition
15
, there exists a minimal BDF
\Gamma
-graded free resolution
F_{\bullet}
of
M
. Tensoring with
k
gives a complex
F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}k
whose differentials are all
0
. It follows that
\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(k,M)_{g}=(F_{i}\otimes_{R}k)_{g}
. Note that by Lemma
17
,
(F_{i}\otimes_{R}k)_{\leq g}=0
if and only if
(F_{i})_{g}=0
, and for
i>n
we have that the former is
0
so the latter is too, implying that
\text{pd}_{R}^{\Gamma}(M)(g)\leq n
.
∎

10.
K
-series of Graded Modules

The rest of the paper will be dedicated to defining the Grothendieck group of our abelian category
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
and proving it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]\{\Gamma\}
. This isomorphism is obtained by computing the
K
-series of a BDF module, which is a slightly altered version of the Hilbert series. In this section we will introduce
K
-series and, by turning the set of such into a linear topological module, show that a free BDF module is determined by both its Hilbert series and its
K
-series

10.1.
Definition of
K
-series

Lemma 18
.

Let
R
be a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra. Then
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
is a unit and
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)=a=\sum_{g\in Q}a_{g}\textbf{t}^{g}
, and note that we may take the sum over
Q
instead of
\Gamma
by definition of
Q
. Now because
R_{0}=k
, it follows that
a_{0}=\dim_{k}(R_{0})=1
. Since
Q
is well-founded by hypothesis, we can use well-founded recursion to define an element
b=\sum_{g\in Q}a_{g}\textbf{t}^{g}\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
which will be the inverse of
a
. Let
b_{0}=1
. Then suppose that for some
g\in Q
we have defined
b_{h}
for all
h\in Q
with
h<_{Q}g
. Then we let

b_{g}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in Q\smallsetminus\{0\}\\
u+v=g\end{subarray}}b_{u}a_{v},

which makes sense because if
u,v\in Q\smallsetminus\{0\}
and
u+v=g
then
u<_{Q}g
hence
b_{u}
is already defined by induction.

Having defined
b
, we let
c=ab
and we note first that
c_{0}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in Q\\
u+v=0\end{subarray}}a_{u}b_{v}=a_{0}b_{0}=1
because
Q
is pointed by hypothesis. For
h\in Q
with
h\neq 0
, we have

c_{h}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in Q\\
u+v=h\end{subarray}}a_{u}b_{v}=b_{h}a_{0}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,v\in Q\smallsetminus\{0\}\\
u+v=h\end{subarray}}b_{u}a_{v}=0.

Therefore
c=1
as desired. From the definition of
b
it is clear that
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]
(
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]
denotes the Laurent series which are supported on
Q
).
∎

Definition 13
.

The
K
-series
of a
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
M
is defined to be
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(M)=\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)\cdot\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}
.

An important property of
K
-series is that they are additive, as described in the following result.

Proposition 23
.

Hilbert series and
K
-series are additive i.e. if
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
is an exact sequence of BDF
R
-modules then
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(L)-\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)+\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(N)=0
and
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(L)-\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(M)+\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(N)=0
.

Proof.

If
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
is an exact sequence of BDF
R
-modules then because kernels and images of graded module homomorphisms are graded submodules, it follows that
0\to L_{g}\to M_{g}\to N_{g}\to 0
is an exact sequence of
k
-vector spaces for all
g\in\Gamma
. This implies that
\dim_{k}(L_{g})-\dim_{k}(M_{g})+\dim_{k}(N_{g})=0
, which is exactly the coefficient of
\textbf{t}^{g}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(L)-\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)+\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(N)
. Hence the latter is
0
. Multiplying by
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}
gives the final desired result.
∎

10.2.
Linear Topological Modules

Here we give a brief summary of the basics of linear topological modules that we will need to define a topology on
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
. A
directed filtration
of an
R
-module
M
consists of a non-empty directed set
D
(i.e. a set with a reflexive and transitive binary operation where every pair of elements has an upper bound) together with an order-reversing function
F:D\to\text{Sub}_{R}(M)
(where
\text{Sub}_{R}(M)
denotes the set of
R
-submodules of
M
and is a poset under inclusion). By definition, if
d,e\in D
then there exists
t\in D
with
d\leq t
and
e\leq t
, and hence
F_{d}\supseteq F_{t}
and
F_{e}\supseteq F_{t}
, implying that
F_{t}\subseteq F_{d}\cap F_{e}
. A
filtered
R
-module
is an
R
-module with a directed filtration on it.

Recall that a
topological
R
-module
is an
R
-module
M
with a topology on it such that addition
M\times M\to M
which sends
m,n\mapsto m+n
, and scalar multiplication
R\times M\to M
which sends
r,m\mapsto rm
is continuous (where
R
is given the discrete topology).

Proposition 24
.

Let
(F,D)
be a directed filtration of an
R
-module
M
.

(1)

The set
B=\{a+F_{d}\mid a\in\Gamma,d\in D\}
is a base for a topology on
M
which we call the
F
-adic
topology.

(2)

The
F
-adic topology makes
M
into a topological
R
-module.

Proof.

(1)

Clearly
B
covers
M
since
D
is non-empty and for any
x\in M
and any
d\in D
, we have that
x\in x+F_{d}
. Next, given
x+F_{d}
and
y+F_{e}
with
x,y\in M
and
d,e\in D
, if
z\in(x+F_{d})\cap(y+F_{e})
then by hypothesis there exists
t\in D
such that
F_{t}\subseteq F_{d}\cap F_{e}
. Clearly then we have
z+F_{t}\subseteq z+F_{d}=x+F_{d}
and similarly
z+F_{t}\subseteq y+F_{e}
, hence
z+F_{t}\subseteq(x+F_{d})\cap(y+F_{e})
as desired.

(2)

We first show that addition
M\times M\to M
is continuous. Suppose that
x+y\in z+F_{d}
. Then
x+F_{d}
and
y+F_{d}
are neighborhoods of
x
and
y
respectively and
(x+F_{d})+(y+F_{d})=x+y+F_{d}=z+F_{d}
, so
(x+F_{d})\times(y+F_{d})
is a neighborhood of
(x,y)
in
M\times M
which maps into
z+F_{d}
under
+
, hence indeed addition is continuous. Finally to show that scalar multiplication is continuous, suppose
rx\in z+F_{d}
. Then we have that
r(x+F_{d})=rx+F_{d}=z+F_{d}
. Hence
\{r\}\times(x+F_{d})
is a neighborhood of
(r,x)
which maps into
z+F_{d}
under scalar multiplication as desired.

∎

Definition 14
.

A topological
R
-module
M
is called a
linear topological
R
-module
if there exists a directed filtration of
M
whose associated topology agrees with that on
M
.

We now discuss convergence of series in a linear topological module. Let
(F,D)
be a directed filtration of an
R
-module
M
. A net
(x_{i}\mid i\in I)
in
M
(i.e. a family of elements of
M
indexed by a directed set
I
) converges to
x\in M
if and only if for all
d\in D
there exists
i\in I
such that if
j\geq i
, then
x_{j}-x\in F_{d}
. If
(x_{a}\mid a\in A)
is a family of elements of
M
, by definition the series
\sum_{a\in A}^{\infty}x_{a}
converges to
x
if its net of partial sums
(\sum_{b\in B}x_{b}\mid B\in\text{Fin}(A))
does, where
\text{Fin}(A)
denotes the set of finite subsets of
A
which is directed by containment. Explicitly this means for all
d\in D
there exists a finite set
B\subseteq A
such that if
B\subseteq C\subseteq A
and
C
is finite then
x-\sum_{c\in C}x_{c}\in F_{d}
.

Later we will need to know that series in our linear topological modules converge to at most one point. The following result gives us a method of checking when this is true.

Proposition 25
.

Let
(F,D)
be a directed filtration of an
R
-module
M
. Then the
F
-adic topology on
M
is Hausdorff if and only if every net
(x_{i}\mid i\in I)
in
M
converges to at most one point.

Proof.

This is true for general topological spaces, see
[
Kel75
]
Chapter 2, Theorem 3.
∎

Furthermore, for linear topological modules, the Hausdorff condition can be checked easily simply by taking the intersection of the defining filtration.

Proposition 26
.

If
(F,D)
is a directed filtration of an
R
-module
M
then the
F
-adic topology on
M
is Hausdorff if and only if
\bigcap_{d\in D}F_{d}=0
.

Proof.

See
[
Mat89
]
Section 8.
∎

10.3.
The Topology of
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}

We now apply the theory of linear topological modules that we just developed to our study of
K
-series. First we note that there is an injective ring homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\to\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
which makes
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
into a
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]
module. Now, given any
g\in\Gamma
, define
Z_{g}
to be the set of series
a\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
with
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(a)\cap(-\infty,g]=\emptyset
where
(-\infty,g]=\{h\in\Gamma\mid h\leq_{Q}g\}
.

Proposition 27
.

Z_{g}
is a
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]
submodule of
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
.

Proof.

It is obvious that
Z_{g}
is a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
. Given
a\in Z_{g}
and
b\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]
, for any
u\in\Gamma
with
u\leq_{Q}g
by definition we have that
(ab)_{u}=\sum_{g+h=u}a_{g}b_{h}
. If
h\notin Q
then
b_{h}=0
so
a_{g}b_{h}=0
. But if
h\in Q
then
g+h=u
implies that
g\leq_{Q}u\leq_{Q}g
and hence
a_{g}=0
. Therefore
(ab)_{u}=0
and
ab\in Z_{g}
.
∎

Given any subset
U
of
\Gamma
, define
(-\infty,U]=\{g\in\Gamma\mid\exists u\in U,g\leq_{Q}u\}
. Then let
\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\Gamma)
be the set of finite subsets
U\subseteq\Gamma
with the relation
U\leq U^{\prime}
if and only if for all
u\in U
there exists
u^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
such that
u\leq_{Q}u^{\prime}
. For each
U\subseteq\Gamma
, define
Z_{U}
to be the subset consisting of series
f\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
with
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(f)\cap(-\infty,U]=\emptyset
. Note that for any subset
U\subseteq\Gamma
we have that
Z_{U}=\bigcap_{u\in U}Z_{u}
, hence
Z_{U}
is a
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]
-submodule of
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
for all
U\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\Gamma)
.

Proposition 28
.

The set
\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\Gamma)
is directed and the function
\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\Gamma)\to\text{Sub}_{\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]}(\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\})
sending
U\mapsto Z_{U}
is a directed filtration.

Proof.

The relation is clearly reflexive and transitive, and for any such
U
and
U^{\prime}
,
U\cup U^{\prime}
is an upper bound for
U
and
U^{\prime}
which is also finite. If
U\leq U^{\prime}
, then clearly
(\infty,U]\subseteq(-\infty,U^{\prime}]
, so for any
f\in Z_{U^{\prime}}
we have that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(f)\cap(-\infty,U^{\prime}]=\emptyset
so certainly
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(f)\cap(-\infty,U]=\emptyset
and hence
f\in Z_{U}
. Hence the map is order-reversing.
∎

Proposition 29
.

We have that
\bigcap_{U\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\Gamma)}Z_{U}=0
. As a result the
Z
-adic topology on
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
is Hausdorff and nets in
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
converge to at most one point.

Proof.

Suppose that
f\in\bigcap_{U\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\Gamma)}Z_{U}
. Then given any
g\in\Gamma
, we have that
f\in Z_{g}
so in particular
f_{g}=0
. Since
g
was arbitrary, it follows that
f=0
. The desired conclusions follow from Propositions
25
and
26
.
∎

Our main application of this topology is to compute the
K
-series of a free BDF module. We begin with two technical lemmas regarding the multiplication of convergent series by elements of the ring, and then prove the desired resut over the course of the next three lemmas.

Lemma 19
.

If
(F,D)
is a directed filtration of an
R
-module
M
then for any
r\in R
, multiplication by
r
gives a continuous function
M\to M
.

Proof.

Multiplication by
r
is equal to the composition of the continuous functions
M\to R\times M
given by
m\mapsto(r,m)
and scalar multiplication
R\times M\to M
.
∎

Lemma 20
.

If
(F,D)
is a directed filtration of an
R
-module
M
and a series
\sum_{a\in A}^{\infty}x_{a}
in
M
converges to
x
then the series
\sum_{a\in A}^{\infty}rx_{a}
converges to
rx
.

Proof.

By Lemma
19
, multiplication by
r
is a continuous function
M\to M
, and continuous functions preserve convergence of nets, hence the net
(r\sum_{b\in B}x_{b}\mid B\in\text{Fin}(A))
converges to
rx
, but because each
B
is a finite set, we obtain
r\sum_{b\in B}x_{b}=\sum_{b\in B}rx_{b}
, so this is actually the net of partial sums of the series
\sum_{a\in A}^{\infty}rx_{a}
.
∎

Lemma 21
.

If
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)
is a BDF family of elements of
\Gamma
then
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i}))=\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))
, i.e. the RHS converges to the LHS in the
Z
-adic topology.

Proof.

Let
F=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})
. We first show that
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(F)=\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))
. Let
U\subseteq\Gamma
be a finite set. Because
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)
is a BDF family, the set
\{g_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is downward finite, hence for each
u\in U
the set
\{g_{i}\mid g_{i}\leq_{Q}u\}
is finite and furthermore for each
g_{i}
in this set, the set
\{j\in I\mid g_{j}=g_{i}\}
is also finite. Since there are only finitely many elements of
U
, the set
B=\{i\in I\mid\exists g_{i}\in(-\infty,U]\}
is finite. If
C\subseteq I
is finite and
B\subseteq C
, we claim that
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(F)-\sum_{i\in C}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))\in Z_{U}
. First note that because
C
is finite, we have that
\sum_{i\in C}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))=\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(\bigoplus_{i\in C}R(-g_{i}))
.

Then if
g\in(-\infty,U]
, by Lemma
16
,
\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})\right)_{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in I,g_{i}\leq_{Q}g}R(-g_{i})_{g}
, and similarly
\left(\bigoplus_{i\in C}R(-g_{i})\right)_{g}=\bigoplus_{i\in C,g_{i}\leq_{Q}g}R(-g_{i})_{g}
. But if
g_{i}\leq_{Q}g
then
i\in B\subseteq C
, hence these two direct sums are taken over the same set, i.e.
\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})\right)_{g}=\left(\bigoplus_{i\in C}R(-g_{i})\right)_{g}
. In particular their dimensions are the same. Since
g
was an arbitrary element of
(-\infty,U]
, the Hilbert series
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(F)
and
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(\bigoplus_{i\in C}R(-g_{i}))
agree on
(-\infty,U]
, so indeed their difference is in
Z_{U}
. It follows that
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(F)=\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))
as claimed.

Finally, using the fact that
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]
and Lemma
20
in step (1), we can compute the desired result:

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})\right)=\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{=}\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))=\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i})).

∎

Lemma 22
.

Let
M
be a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module, and
g\in\Gamma
. Then
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(M(-g))=\textbf{t}^{g}\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(M)
and in particular we have
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(R(-g))=\textbf{t}^{g}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
14
we have that
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M(-g))=\textbf{t}^{g}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)
, so we compute:

\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(M(-g))=\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M(-g))\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}=\textbf{t}^{g}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(M)\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}=\textbf{t}^{g}\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(M)

In particular this implies that
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(R(-g))=\textbf{t}^{g}\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(R)=\textbf{t}^{g}\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}=\textbf{t}^{g}
.
∎

Lemma 23
.

If
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)
is a BDF family of elements of
\Gamma
then
\sum_{i\in I}\textbf{t}^{g_{i}}=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{g}=\#\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}
so that
f=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}
. Given any finite set
U\subseteq\Gamma
, because
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)
is a BDF family, the set
B=\{i\in I\mid g_{i}\in(-\infty,U]\}
is finite. If
C\subseteq\Gamma
is finite and
B\subseteq C
then we have that
f-\sum_{i\in C}\textbf{t}^{g_{i}}\in Z_{U}
because for any
g\in(-\infty,U]
, we have that
f_{g}
is the size of the set
\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}
, but this set is contained in
B
and hence in
C
, so there are exactly
f_{g}
terms of the sum
\sum_{i\in C}\textbf{t}^{g_{i}}
equal to
\textbf{t}^{g}
.
∎

We are now able to compute the
K
-series of a free BDF module.

Proposition 30
.

If
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)
is a BDF family of elements of
\Gamma
then
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i}))=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}
.

Proof.

We compute:

\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i}))\overset{(1)}{=}\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(R(-g_{i}))\overset{(2)}{=}\sum_{i\in I}\textbf{t}^{g_{i}}\overset{(3)}{=}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}

Here (1) follows from Lemma
21
, (2) follows from Lemma
22
, and (3) follows from Lemma
23
.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain that free BDF modules are determined by their
K
-series.

Corollary 5
.

If
F
and
F^{\prime}
are free BDF
R
-modules then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(F)=\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(F^{\prime})

(2)

\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)=\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F^{\prime})

(3)

F\cong F^{\prime}

Proof.

One obtains (1)
\iff
(2) by multiplying by
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)
or
\mathcal{H}_{\Gamma}(R)^{-1}
. Also (3)
\implies
(1) is obvious. We now show
(2)\implies(3)
. Given BDF
\Gamma
-graded free modules
F=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})
and
F^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{j\in J}R(-h_{j})
with
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)=\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F^{\prime})
, by Proposition
30
, we have that
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}
and
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F^{\prime})=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{j\in J\mid h_{j}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}
, and hence for each
g\in\Gamma
, we have
\#\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}=\#\{j\in J\mid h_{j}=g\}
, so we obtain a bijection between the standard bases of
F
and
F^{\prime}
, yielding
F\cong F^{\prime}
.
∎

11.
The Grothendieck Group of a Graded Ring

For the category of finitely generated ungraded or graded modules over a fixed ring there are two typical ways to define the Grothendieck group of a commutative ring
R
which agree in favorable circumstances, for example when the ring is a Noetherian regular local ring, or a polynomial ring in finitely many variables. One the one hand,
K_{0}(R)
(or
K_{\Gamma,0}(R)
in the graded case) is defined to be the Grothendieck group of the commutative monoid of isomorphism classes of finitely generated projective (graded)
R
-modules with addition given by
[P]+[Q]=[P\oplus Q]
. On the other hand,
G_{0}(R)
(or
G_{\Gamma,0}(R)
in the graded case) is defined to be the quotient of the free abelian group on the set of isomorphism classes of all finitely generated (graded)
R
-modules modulo the subgroup generated by expressions of the form
[L]-[M]+[N]
when there exists a short exact sequence
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
. (See
[
Wei13
]
for details). In this section we give analogs of these definitions for BDF
\Gamma
-graded modules and show that they agree for PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebras whose ideal maximal graded ideal
R_{+}
is generated by a regular sequence.

11.1.
The Group
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
of a PDCF
\Gamma
-Graded
k
-Algebra

We begin by recalling the Grothendieck group of a commutative monoid. If
Q
is a commutative monoid, then there exists an abelian group
K
(unique up to a unique isomorphism) and a monoid homomorphism
i:Q\to K
which satisfies the following universal property: For all abelian groups
A
and all monoid homomorphisms
\varphi:Q\to A
there exists a unique group homomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}:K\to A
such that
\varphi=\tilde{\varphi}\circ i
. Then
K
is called the
Grothendieck group
of the commutative monoid
Q
.

One construction of the Grothendieck group of
Q
is to define an equivalence relation
\sim
on
Q\times Q
by letting
(q_{1},q_{2})\sim(p_{1},p_{2})
if and only if there exists
g\in Q
such that
q_{1}+p_{2}+g=p_{1}+q_{2}+g
. The equivalence class of
(q,p)\in Q\times Q
is denoted by
[q,p]
, and
K
is defined to be the set of equivalence classes
Q\times Q/\sim
together with the binary operation
[q,p]+[q^{\prime},p^{\prime}]=[q+q^{\prime},p+p^{\prime}]
.

We will need the following proposition which describes when the Grothendieck group of a commutative monoid is trivial.

Proposition 31
.

Let
Q
be a commutative monoid. Then its Grothendieck group
K
is trivial if and only if for all
p,q\in Q
there exists
g\in Q
such that
p+g=q+g
.

Proof.

Since the Grothendieck group of
Q
is unique up to a unique isomorphism we may assume that
K=Q\times Q/\sim
, and we note that the identity of
K
is
[0,0]
. We have that
K
is trivial if and only if for all
p,q\in Q
,
(p,q)\sim(0,0)
which is true if and only if for all
p,q\in Q
there exists
g\in Q
such that
p+g=q+g
.
∎

We define
R\text{-}\textbf{PMod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
to be the category of
projective
BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules. By Theorem
3
, every such module is actually free. Now define
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
to be the Grothendieck group of the commutative monoid consisting of the set of isomorphism classes of objects in
R\text{-}\textbf{PMod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
, together with the operation
[M]+[N]=[M\oplus N]
. We note that by Proposition
31
this group is not trivial because
R
and
R\oplus R
are both free BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules, if there existed an object
M
of
R\text{-}\textbf{PMod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
with
R\oplus M\cong R\oplus R\oplus M
as
R
-modules then we would have
R_{0}\oplus M_{0}\cong R_{0}\oplus R_{0}\oplus M_{0}
as
k
-vector spaces which is impossible as
R_{0}
and
M_{0}
are both finite-dimensional and
\dim_{k}(R_{0})=1
. Recall that the famous Eilenberg swindle shows that the Grothendieck group of the monoid consisting of
all
projective
R
-modules is trivial. We have avoided this by considering only finite-dimensionally graded modules.

Note 5
.

The category
R\text{-}\textbf{PMod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is split exact (which can be embedded in the abelian category
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
), and therefore we could have equivalently defined
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
to be the free abelian group on the set of isomorphism classes of elements of
R\text{-}\textbf{PMod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
modulo the subgroup generated by sums of the form
[L]-[M]+[N]
whenever there is a short exact sequence
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
(see
[
Wei13
]
Example 7.1.2).

Proposition 32
.

Every element of
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
can be written as
[F]-[F^{\prime}]
where
F
and
F^{\prime}
are free BDF
R
-modules.

Proof.

By definition every element of
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
is of the form
[F_{1}]+\ldots+[F_{n}]-[F^{\prime}_{1}]-\ldots-[F^{\prime}_{m}]
for
F_{i}
and
F^{\prime}_{j}
free BDF
R
-modules. By definition of addition in the Grothendieck group, this element is equal to
[F]-[F^{\prime}]
where
F=F_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus F_{n}
and
F^{\prime}=F^{\prime}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus F^{\prime}_{m}
. Again
F
and
F^{\prime}
are free BDF
R
-modules, as desired.
∎

Theorem 5
.

Let
R
be a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra. There is an isomorphism
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\overset{\sim}{\to}\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
given by sending
[F]\mapsto\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)
.

Proof.

By the universal property of Grothendieck groups, to define a group homomorphism
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
it suffices to define a map from the commutative monoid of isomorphism classes of free
\Gamma
-graded BDF
R
-modules to
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
that respects direct sum, and by Proposition
23
we have
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F\oplus F^{\prime})=\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)+\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F^{\prime})
so such a map does indeed exist. To show injectivity, we note that if
[F]-[F^{\prime}]\mapsto 0
then
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)=\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F^{\prime})
hence by Corollary
5
,
F\cong F^{\prime}
and so
[F]=[F^{\prime}]
in
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
.

For surjectivity, let
a=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}a_{g}\textbf{t}^{g}\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
be such that
a_{g}\geq 0
for all
g\in\Gamma
and define
I
to be the subset of
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(a)\times\mathbb{N}
containing the elements
(g,n)
such that
n\leq a_{g}
. Define a function
h:I\to\Gamma
by
(g,n)\mapsto g:=h_{(g,n)}
, so that we get a family
(h_{i}\mid i\in I)
of elements of
\Gamma
. We claim that this is a BDF family. Indeed, for each
g\in\Gamma
, we have
\#\{i\in I\mid h_{i}=g\}=a_{g}
and the set
\{h_{i}\mid i\in I\}
is exactly
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(a)
which is finitely bounded below and downward finite by definition of
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
. Therefore by Proposition
13
,
F:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-h_{i})
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module.

Then under our map
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
, we have that
[F]\mapsto\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)
and the latter is equal to
\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{i\in I\mid h_{i}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}
by Proposition
30
. Since
a=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{i\in I\mid h_{i}=g\}
as well, we have
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)=a
.

Surjectivity follows since every element of
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
can be written as
a-a^{\prime}
where
a_{g},a^{\prime}_{g}\geq 0
.
∎

11.2.
The Group
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
of a Graded Ring

Define
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
to be the free abelian group on the set of isomorphism classes of objects in
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
, modulo the subgroup generated by expressions of the form
[L]-[M]+[N]
whenever there is a short exact sequence
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
. We now prove a series of lemmas which culminate in the result that
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
and
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
are isomorphic. We begin by defining the map that will become this isomorphism.

Lemma 24
.

Let
R
be a PDCF
k
-algebra. There exists a group homomorphism
\Phi:K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
which sends the isomorphism class
[F]
in
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
of a free module
F
to the residue of
[F]
in the quotient
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
.

Proof.

There is a function
\varphi
from the set of isomorphism classes of objects in
R\text{-}\textbf{PMod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
to
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
which sends an isomorphism class
[F]
to its residue in the quotient
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
. This is well-defined because
R\text{-}\textbf{PMod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
is a subcategory of
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
, so if modules are isomorphic in the former, they are also isomorphic in the latter. Furthermore, it is a monoid homomorphism because given free modules
F
and
F^{\prime}
in
R\text{-}\textbf{PMod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
, we have that
\varphi([F]+[F^{\prime}])=\varphi([F\oplus F^{\prime}])=[F\oplus F^{\prime}]
and since there is an exact sequence
0\to F\to F\oplus F^{\prime}\to F^{\prime}\to 0
in
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
we have that
[F\oplus F^{\prime}]=[F]+[F^{\prime}]
in
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
, and the latter is equal to
\varphi([F])+\varphi([F^{\prime}])
. Therefore the universal property of the Grothendieck group of a monoid gives the desired group homomorphism
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
.
∎

Next we give a technical lemma, followed by a lemma which allows us to express classes of modules
[M]\in G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
in terms of free modules using
\Gamma
-finite free resolutions.

Lemma 25
.

If
\ldots\to M_{2}\to M_{1}\to M_{0}\to 0
is a
\Gamma
-finite sequence in
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
then for any collection of submodules
Q_{n}\subseteq M_{n}
any subset
U\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
\bigoplus_{n\in U}Q_{n}
is also in
R\text{-}\textbf{Mod}_{\Gamma}^{\text{BDF}}
.

Proof.

First of all if
Q_{n}\subseteq M_{n}
then we have that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(\bigoplus_{n\in U}Q_{n})\subseteq\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(\bigoplus_{n\in U}M_{n})
. Furthermore we also have
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(\bigoplus_{n\in U}M_{n})\subseteq\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}((M_{n})_{g})
and the latter is finitely bounded below and downward finite by (GF1), hence the former is as well by Lemma
1
. Furthermore, each
Q_{n}
is finite-dimensionally graded, hence for all
g\in\Gamma
,
(Q_{n})_{g}
is a finite-dimensional
k
-vector space. By definition
(\bigoplus_{n\in U}(Q_{n})_{g}=\bigoplus_{n\in U}(Q_{n})_{g}
, but by (GF2) there are only finitely many
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq}0
for which
(M_{n})_{g}
is nonzero hence the same is true for
(Q_{n})_{g}
since
(Q_{n})_{g}\subseteq(M_{n})_{g}
. Therefore this direct sum is also finite-dimensional and we obtain that
\bigoplus_{n\in U}Q_{n}
is finite-dimensionally graded as desired.
∎

Lemma 26
.

If
M
is a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module and
F_{\bullet}
is a
\Gamma
-finite free resolution of
M
, then in
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
we have
[M]=[\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n}]-[\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}]
.

Proof.

If
\ldots\to F_{1}\overset{\varphi_{1}}{\to}F_{0}\overset{\varphi_{0}}{\to}M\to 0
is a
\Gamma
-finite free resolution of
M
, consider the map
\varphi:=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\varphi_{2n}:\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n}\to M\oplus\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}
. Note that both the domain and codomain of
\varphi
are BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules by Lemma
25
. The kernel of this map is
\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\ker\varphi_{2n}
and the cokernel is
\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}/\operatorname{im}\varphi_{2n+2}
. Therefore we have the following exact sequence.

0\to\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\ker\varphi_{2n}\to\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n}\overset{\varphi}{\to}M\oplus\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}\to\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}/\operatorname{im}\varphi_{2n+2}\to 0

So in
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
we have

(2)

[\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}/\operatorname{im}\varphi_{2n+2}]-[M\oplus\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}]+[\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n}]-[\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\ker\varphi_{2n}]=0.

However, we compute:

\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}/\operatorname{im}\varphi_{2n+2}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}/\ker\varphi_{2n+1}\cong\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\operatorname{im}\varphi_{2n+1}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\ker\varphi_{2n}.

Therefore the first and last terms of Equation
2
cancel giving
[M\oplus\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}]=[\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n}]
. The LHS of this equation is equal to
[M]\oplus[\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}]
, therefore subtraction yields
[M]=[\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n}]-[\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}]
.

∎

Finally we arrive at the isomorphism between the two Grothendieck groups.

Theorem 6
.

Let
R
be a PDCF
\Gamma
-graded
k
-algebra with the property that
R_{+}
is generated by a homogeneous regular sequence. The group homomorphism
\Phi:K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
described in Lemma
24
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Given
[M]\in G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
, by the BDF Hilbert Syzygy Theorem (Theorem
4
), there exists a BDF
\Gamma
-finite free resolution
\ldots\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to M\to 0
of
M
, and then by Lemma
26
we have that
[M]=[F]-[F^{\prime}]
in
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
, where
F=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n}
and
F^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{2n+1}
. Since
F
and
F^{\prime}
are both free,
[F]-[F^{\prime}]
is an element of
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
and we have that
\Phi([F]-[F^{\prime}])=[M]
so
\Phi
is surjective.

On the other hand, because
K
-series are additive (Corollary
23
), the function which sends the isomorphism class of a BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-module
M
to its
K
-series
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(M)
defines, via the universal property of
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
, a group homomorphism
\Psi:G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
. Composing this with the isomorphism
\Theta:\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}\to K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
of Theorem
5
, we obtain a group homomorphism
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
which is a left-inverse of
\Phi
, since the composition
\Theta\circ\Psi\circ\Phi
sends
[F]\in K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
to
[F]\in G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
to
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}([F])\in\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
back to
[F]\in K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
. Since
\Phi
has a left inverse, it is is injective.
∎

Definition 15
.

We call the group
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\cong G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
the
BDF
\Gamma
-graded Grothendieck group
of
R
.

12.
Ring Structure on the Grothendieck Group

In this section we define the product which makes the Grothendieck group into a ring. We begin with two preliminary results which lead to the definition of this product on
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
, then we give a BDF version of Serre’s formula to define the product on
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
.

Lemma 27
.

If
(g_{i}\mid i\in I)
and
(h_{j}\mid j\in J)
are BDF families of elements of
\Gamma
then
(g_{i}+h_{j}\mid(i,j)\in I\times J)
is also a BDF family.

Proof.

Let
U=\{g_{i}\mid i\in I\}
and
V=\{h_{j}\mid j\in J\}
. We have that
\{g_{i}+h_{j}\mid(i,j)\in I\times J\}=U+V
and because
U
and
V
are finitely bounded below and downward finite by hypothesis, by Lemma
4
the LHS is too. Given any
c\in\Gamma
the set
T=\{(g,h)\in U\times V\mid g+h=c\}
is finite by Lemma
3
. Then we compute:

\displaystyle\{(i,j)\in I\times J\mid g_{i}+h_{j}=c\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{(g,h)\in T}\{(i,j)\in I\times J\mid(g_{i},h_{j})=(g,h)\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{(g,h)\in T}\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}\times\{j\in J\mid h_{j}=h\}.

By hypothesis, the sets
\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}
and
\{j\in J\mid h_{j}=h\}
are finite, and since
T
is finite, this union is finite as well.
∎

Proposition 33
.

If
F
and
F^{\prime}
are free BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules then
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F\otimes_{R}F^{\prime})=\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F^{\prime})

Proof.

If
F=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})
and
F=\bigoplus_{j\in J}R(-h_{j})
, then because tensor product commutes with direct sum, we can compute

F\otimes_{R}F^{\prime}=\left(\bigoplus_{i\in I}R(-g_{i})\right)\otimes_{R}\left(\bigoplus_{j\in J}R(-h_{j})\right)\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I,j\in J}R(-g_{i})\otimes_{R}R(-h_{j})\cong\bigoplus_{i\in I,j\in J}R(-(g_{i}+h_{j}))

The family
(g_{i}+h_{j}\mid(i,j)\in I\times J)
of elements of
\Gamma
is BDF by Lemma
27
, so by Proposition
30
, we have that
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F\otimes_{R}F^{\prime})=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{(i,j)\in I\times J\mid g_{i}+h_{j}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}
. On the other hand, again by Proposition
30
we also have that
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}
and that
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F^{\prime})=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\#\{j\in J\mid h_{j}=g\}\textbf{t}^{g}
, so by definition
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F)\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(F^{\prime})=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\left(\sum_{a+b=g}\#\{j\in J\mid g_{i}=a\}\cdot\#\{j\in J\mid h_{j}=b\}\right)\textbf{t}^{g}
. But there is an obvious bijection
\{(i,j)\in I\times J\mid g_{i}+h_{j}=g\}\leftrightarrow\bigsqcup_{a+b=g}\{i\in I\mid g_{i}=a\}\times\{j\in J\mid h_{j}=b\}
, so we obtain the desired result.
∎

Corollary 6
.

The binary operation
[F],[F^{\prime}]\mapsto[F\otimes_{R}F^{\prime}]
gives a well-defined commutative ring structure on
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
with multiplicative identity equal to
[R]
and the isomorphism
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
given in Theorem
5
is a ring map.

Proof.

In fact because the map
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
given by
K
-series is a group isomorphism, it follows that this binary operation is well-defined and indeed gives a commutative ring structure on
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
. Since
\mathcal{K}_{\Gamma}(R)=1
,
[R]
is the multiplicative identity of
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
.
∎

12.1.
BDF Serre’s Formula

To make sense of Serre’s formula in the BDF setting, we must place a linear topology on
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
that corresponds to the
Z
-adic topology on
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
. Given a finite set
U\subseteq\Gamma
, define
Z^{G}_{U}
to be the subgroup of
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
generated by the set of all
[M]\in G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
such that
\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M)\cap[U,-\infty)=\emptyset
. This gives a directed filtration
Z^{G}
on
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
which corresponds exactly to the
Z
filtration on
\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
under the isomorphism
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to\mathbb{Z}[[Q]]\{\Gamma\}
. Hence the
Z^{G}
-adic topology is also Hausdorff so all series converge to at most one element.

If
R
is a Noetherian ring, it is well-known that if
M_{\bullet}
is a
bounded
chain complex of finitely generated modules, then the Euler characteristic of the complex in
G_{0}(R)
depends only on its homology, i.e.
\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[M_{i}]=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[H_{i}(M)]
(see
[
Wei13
]
, Chapter II, Proposition 6.6). The following lemma is a BDF version of that result for
\Gamma
-finite
complexes.

Lemma 28
.

If
\ldots\to M_{2}\overset{\varphi_{2}}{\to}M_{1}\overset{\varphi_{1}}{\to}M_{0}\overset{\varphi_{0}}{\to}0
is a
\Gamma
-finite chain complex of BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules then
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[M_{i}]
and
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[H_{i}(M_{\bullet})]
both converge to
[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}M_{2i}]-[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}M_{2i+1}]
in the
Z^{G}
-adic topology on
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
.

Proof.

First of all, because the sequence is
\Gamma
-finite, in particular
V:=\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}\text{Supp}_{\Gamma}(M_{i})\subseteq\Gamma
is finitely bounded below and downward finite. Hence given
U\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\Gamma)
, there are only finitely many
g\in(-\infty,U]\cap V
. Therefore again since the sequence is
\Gamma
-finite, there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
(M_{i})_{g}=0
for all
g\in(-\infty,U]
and
i\geq n
. We may assume without loss of generality that
i
is odd, the even case being similar. In
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
the following holds.

[\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\infty}M_{2j}]-[\bigoplus_{j=0}^{\infty}M_{2j+1}]-\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}[M_{j}]=[\bigoplus_{j=(i+1)/2}^{\infty}M_{2j}]-[\bigoplus_{j=(i+1)/2}^{\infty}M_{2j+1}]

However, since
i\geq n
, it follows that
\left(\bigoplus_{j=(i+1)/2}^{\infty}M_{2j}\right)_{g}=\bigoplus_{j=(i+1)/2}^{\infty}(M_{2j})_{g}=0
, hence
\bigoplus_{j=(i+1)/2}^{\infty}M_{2j}\in Z^{G}_{U}
. Similarly
\bigoplus_{j=(i+1)/2}^{\infty}M_{2j+1}\in Z^{G}_{U}
, so their difference is in
Z^{G}_{U}
as desired.

Now given
U\in\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}(\Gamma)
, if we choose
n
such that
(M_{i})_{g}=0
for all
g\in(-\infty,U]
and
m\geq n
, then we compute:

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}[H_{j}(M_{\bullet})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}[\ker\varphi_{j}/\operatorname{im}\varphi_{j+1}]=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}([\ker\varphi_{j}]-[\operatorname{im}\varphi_{j+1}])

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}([\ker\varphi_{j}]-[M_{j+1}/\ker\varphi_{j+1}])=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}([\ker\varphi_{j}]-[M_{j+1}]+[\ker\varphi_{j+1}])

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{=}[M_{0}]-[M_{1}]+[M_{2}]-\ldots-(-1)^{m}[M_{m+1}]+(-1)^{m}[\ker\varphi_{m+1}]

\displaystyle=[M_{0}]-[M_{1}]+[M_{2}]-\ldots-(-1)^{m}[\operatorname{im}\varphi_{m+1}]

Here (1) follows from cancelation and the fact that
\ker\varphi_{0}=M_{0}
. Therefore we have that
\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}[M_{j}]-\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}[H_{j}(M_{\bullet})]=(-1)^{m}[\operatorname{im}\varphi_{m+1}]
. But because
m\geq n
it follows that
(M_{m})_{g}=0
, and hence
(\operatorname{im}\varphi_{m+1})_{g}=0
, for all
g\in(-\infty,U]
, so
[\operatorname{im}\varphi_{m+1}]\in Z^{G}_{U}
. Therefore the difference of the series
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[M_{i}]
and
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[H_{i}(M_{\bullet})]
converges to
0
, so they converge to the same element in the
Z^{G}_{U}
-adic topology.
∎

Lemma 29
.

Let
M
and
N
be BDF
\Gamma
-graded
R
-modules. If
F_{\bullet}
and
G_{\bullet}
are
\Gamma
-finite BDF free resolutions of
M
and
N
respectively, then
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)]
converges to
[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j})\oplus(F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1})]-[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{jm})\oplus(F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1})]
in the
Z^{G}
-adic topology on
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
.

Proof.

We form the double complex
(F_{\bullet}\to M\to 0)\otimes_{R}(G_{\bullet}\to N\to 0)
as in Figure
1
and note, as in the proof of Proposition
21
, each column of this complex other than the right-most is exact, and is therefore a free resolution of its bottom term. Taking the direct sum of all columns with bottom term
F_{2i}\otimes_{R}N
we obtain an exact sequence
\ldots\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{1}\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{0}\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}\otimes_{R}N\to 0
whose terms are BDF by Lemma
25
because the rows of the double complex are BDF
\Gamma
-finite, and which is
\Gamma
-finite itself because by Lemma
13
, the double complex is
\Gamma
-finite. Similarly the direct sum of the odd columns also gives a
\Gamma
-finite exact sequence
\ldots\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{1}\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{0}\to\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}N\to 0
. Since these are in fact free resolutions, by Lemma
26
we have
[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}\otimes_{R}N]=[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j}]-[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1}]
and similarly
[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}N]=[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{2j}]-[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1}]
. Therefore by Lemma
28
we can compute as follows.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)]=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[H_{i}(F_{\bullet}\otimes_{R}N)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[F_{i}\otimes_{R}N]

\displaystyle=[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}\otimes_{R}N]-[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}N]

\displaystyle=\left([\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j}]-[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1}]\right)-\left([\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{2j}]-[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1}]\right)

\displaystyle=[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j})\oplus(F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1})]-[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{jm})\oplus(F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1})].

∎

Corollary 7

(BDF Serre’s Formula)
.

The binary operation
[M]\cdot[N]=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)]
gives a well-defined commutative ring structure on
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
with multiplicative identity equal to
[R]
and the isomorphism
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\overset{\sim}{\to}G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
given in Theorem
6
is a ring map.

Proof.

Under the isomorphism
\Theta\circ\Psi:G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)\to K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
given in the proof of Theorem
6
, we have that
\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)]=[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j})\oplus(F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1})]-[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{jm})\oplus(F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1})]
maps to the class in
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
with the same representatives. But in
K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
, using the ring structure defined in Corollary
6
, we compute:

\displaystyle[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j})\oplus(F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1})]-[\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{\infty}(F_{2i+1}\otimes_{R}G_{2j})\oplus(F_{2i}\otimes_{R}G_{2j+1})]

\displaystyle=\left([\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i}]-[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}F_{2i+1}]\right)\cdot\left([\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}G_{2i}]-[\bigoplus_{i=0}^{\infty}G_{2i+1}]\right)

The latter is product of the images of
[M]
and
[N]
under
\Theta\circ\Psi
. Since this is an isomorphism, the binary operation
[M]\cdot[N]=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{i}[\operatorname{Tor}_{i}(M,N)]
does indeed give a ring structure on
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
and
\Theta\circ\Psi
is an isomorphism of rings.
∎

Definition 16
.

K^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
together with the ring structure given by Corollary
6
, or equivalently
G^{\text{BDF}}_{\Gamma,0}(R)
together with the ring structure given by Corollary
7
, is called the
BDF
\Gamma
-graded Grothendieck ring
of
R
.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the existence of solutions for the linearly coupled Choquard system with potentials

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u+V_{1}(x)u=\mu_{1}(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u+\beta(x)v,\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v+V_{2}(x)v=\mu_{2}(I_{\alpha}\star|v|^{q})|v|^{q-2}u+\beta(x)u,\end{aligned}\right.\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.1)

under the constraint

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u^{2}dx=\xi^{2},~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v^{2}dx=\eta^{2},

(1.2)

where
I_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{|x|^{N-\alpha}},~\alpha\in(0,N),~1+\frac{\alpha}{N}<p,~q<\frac{N+\alpha}{N-2},~\mu_{1}>0,~\mu_{2}>0
and
\beta(x)
is a fixed function.

If
\beta(x)=0
and
V_{1}(x)=V_{2}(x)=0
, then the system (
1.1
) can be reduced to the scalar Choquard equation

\displaystyle-\Delta u+\lambda u=\mu(I_{\alpha}\star|u|^{p})|u|^{p-2}u.

(1.3)

Physical motivations arise from the case
N=3
and
\alpha=2
. In 1954, Pekar
[
29
]
described a polaron
at rest in the quantum theory. In 1976, to model an electron trapped in its own hole, Choquard
[
24
]
considered (
1.3
) as an approximation to Hartree-Fock theory of one-component plasma. In particular cases, Penrose
[
30
]
investigated the selfgravitational collapse of a quantum mechanical wave function. The system of weakly coupled equations has been widely considered in recent years and it has applications especially in nonlinear optics
[
26
,
27
]
. Furthermore, nonlocal nonlinearities have attracted considerable interest
as a means of eliminating collapse and stabilizing multidimensional solitary waves. It appears naturally
in optical systems
[
25
]
and is known to influence the propagation of electromagnetic waves in plasmas
[
8
]
.

In (
1.1
), if
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are given, some literature call it as fixed frequency problem. There are some related articles. In
[
40
]
, the authors
studied the existence and nonexistence results for a class of linearly coupled Choquard system (
1.1
) with
V_{1}(x)=V_{2}(x)=0
for critical case. In
[
39
]
,
the authors were concerned with the following linearly coupled Choquard-type system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u+\lambda_{1}u=\mu_{1}(I_{\alpha}\star F(u))F^{\prime}(u)+\beta v,\\
&-\Delta v+\lambda_{2}v=\mu_{2}(I_{\alpha}\star F(v))F^{\prime}(v)+\beta u,\end{aligned}\right.\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Under some suitable conditions on
F^{\prime}
, the existence of vector solutions was proved by using variational methods. Moreover, they proved the asymptotic behavior of the solutions as
\beta\to 0^{+}
.

Condition (
1.2
) is called as the normalization condition, which imposes a normalization on the
L^{2}
-masses of
u
and
v
. The solutions to the Schrödiger system (
1.1
) under the constraint (
1.2
) are usually referred as normalized solutions. In order to obtain the solution to the fractional Schrödiger system (
1.1
) satisfying the normalization condition (
1.2
), one need to consider
the critical point of the functional
E_{V}(u,v)
on
S_{\xi}\times S_{\eta}
(see (
1.4
) and (
1.5
)). And then,
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
appear as Lagrange multipliers with respect to the mass constraint, which cannot be determined a priori, but are part of the unknown. Some literature called this problem as fixed mass problem.

Up to our knowledge, there is no paper about the normalized solutions of linearly coupled Choquard system. In our paper, we first consider the normalized solutions of linearly coupled Choquard system (

) without potentials, and then consider the normalized solutions of linearly coupled Choquard system (

&-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta(x)uvdx.\end{split}

In this paper, the essential difficulty is to obtain the
L^{2}
-strong convergence of critical sequence due to the constraint (

).

For
1+\frac{\alpha}{N}<p,q<1+\frac{\alpha+2}{N}
and
V_{1}(x)=V_{2}(x)=0
, the key step is proved the subadditivity of

(see Lemma

), combining with Brézis-Lieb lemma, it can lead to the

-strong convergence of minimizing sequence of

For

and

, we construct the mountain pass structure and obtain the bounded Palais-Smale sequence satisfying Pohozev identity in limit case. In addition, the sign of Lagrange multipliers plays an important role in

-strong convergence of Palais-Smale sequence. Hence the difficulty can be turned into showing the sign of Lagrange multipliers. By

and Pohozev identity in limit case, we can verify the sign of one of Lagrange multipliers. But for the other, by the fact the linearly coupled system (
1.1
) has no semi-trivial solutions, we can obtain the sign of the other Lagrange multiplier. Therefore, the
L^{2}
-strong convergence of Palais-Smale sequence is established.

For

and

, if

and

are trapping potentials, then

-strong convergence of Palais-Smale sequence can be given by the compact embedding lemma, which can be seen in
[
31
]
. If

or

is not trapping potential, then the key step is to establish the split lemma for

, combining with Brézis-Lieb lemma, it can lead to the

-strong convergence of minimizing sequence of

-supercritical case. Indeed, by comparing the energy, the proper linking geometry can be constructed and the bounded Palais-Smale sequence

,

Lemma 3.1

E(u,v)

Proof.

By (

\displaystyle B(u,p)\leq C(N,\alpha,p)\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2p\delta_{p}}\xi^{2p(1-\delta_{p})},

\displaystyle B(v,q)\leq C(N,\alpha,q)\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2q\delta_{q}}\eta^{2q(1-\delta_{q})},

where
\delta_{p}=\frac{N(p-1)-\alpha}{2p}
. Thus,

\displaystyle E(u,v)\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2})-\frac{\mu_{1}C(N,\alpha,p)\xi^{2p(1-\delta_{p})}}{2p}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2p\delta_{p}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mu_{2}C(N,\alpha,q)\eta^{2q(1-\delta_{q})}}{2q}\|\nabla v\|_{2}^{2q\delta_{q}}-\beta\xi\eta.

Since
1+\frac{\alpha}{N}<p,q<1+\frac{\alpha+2}{N}
,
2p\delta_{p}<2
and

. Hence we complete the proof.
∎

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta u_{n}v_{n}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta(u_{n}-u_{0})(v_{n}-v_{0})+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta u_{0}v_{0}=o(1)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\beta u_{0}v_{0}.

\displaystyle E_{V}(u_{n},v_{n})\geq

\displaystyle E_{V}(u_{0},v_{0})+F_{\mu_{1}}(w_{0}(\cdot-y_{n}))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V_{1}(x)|w_{0}(\cdot-y_{n})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+F_{\mu_{1}}(u_{n}-u_{0}-w_{0}(\cdot-y_{n}))+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V_{1}(x)|u_{n}-u_{0}-w_{0}(\cdot-y_{n})|^{2}dx.

Since
|y_{n}|\rightarrow\infty,~\lim\limits_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}V_{1}(x)=0
and

, we can conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V_{1}(x)|w_{0}(x-y_{n})|^{2}dx\rightarrow 0\text{~and}~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V_{1}(x)|u_{n}-u_{0}-w_{0}(x-y_{n})|^{2}dx\rightarrow 0,

as
n\rightarrow\infty
, which implies that

\displaystyle E_{V}(u_{n},v_{n})\geq E_{V}(u_{0},v_{0})+F_{\mu_{1}}(w_{0}(\cdot-y_{n}))+F_{\mu_{1}}(u_{n}-u_{0}-w_{0}(\cdot-y_{n}).

Hence

\displaystyle e_{V}(\xi,\eta)\geq e_{V}(\xi_{1},\eta)+m(\xi_{2},\mu_{1})+m(\delta_{1},\mu_{1}).

By Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
5.5
, we can see that

\displaystyle e_{V}(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=e_{V}(\xi_{1},\eta)+m(\xi_{2},\mu_{1})+m(\delta_{1},\mu_{1})

\displaystyle>e_{V}(\xi_{1},\eta)+m(\xi_{2}+\delta_{1},\mu_{1})

\displaystyle\geq e_{V}(\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}+\delta_{1},\eta)

\displaystyle=e_{V}(\xi,\eta),

which is a contradiction. Hence
\delta_{1}=0
.

The global Cauchy problem for the Euler–Riesz equations
Young-Pil Choi, Jinwook Jung, Yoonjung Lee

Abstract
. We completely resolve the global Cauchy problem for the multi-dimensional Euler–Riesz equations, where the interaction forcing is given by ∇ ( − Δ ) − σ / 2 ρ \nabla(-\Delta)^{-\sigma/2}\rho for some σ ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \sigma\in(0,2) . We construct the global-in-time unique solution to the Euler–Riesz system in a H s H^{s} Sobolev space under a smallness assumption on the initial density and a dispersive spectral condition on the initial velocity. Moreover, we investigate the algebraic time decay of convergences for the constructed solutions. Our results cover the both attractive and repulsive cases as well as the whole regime σ ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \sigma\in(0,2) .

35Q31, 76N10.

1.
Introduction

In the present work, we are interested in the global-in-time Cauchy problem for the Euler–Riesz equations:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho u)=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\cr&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\nabla\cdot(\rho u\otimes u)+c_{P}\nabla(\rho^{\gamma})=\lambda\rho\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}\rho,\end{aligned}

subject to initial data:

(\rho(x,0),u(x,0))=(\rho_{0}(x),u_{0}(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\rho=\rho(x,t)
and
u=u(x,t)
denote the density and velocity of the fluid at time
t
and position
x
, respectively.
Here, the Riesz operator
\Lambda^{s}
is defined by
(-\Delta)^{s/2}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, and in the current work we concentrate on the case

\sigma\in(0,\min\{d,2\}).

Note that the case
\sigma=2
corresponds to the classical Euler–Poisson system. Here,
\gamma>1
,
c_{P}
is a nonnegative constant, and
\lambda
is a constant. Depending on the sign of
\lambda
, the forcing term describes the attractive (
\lambda>0
) and repulsive (
\lambda<0
) interactions.

The pressureless case
(c_{P}=0)
is introduced and rigorously derived in
[
35
]
as a mean-field limit of the second-order system corresponding to Newton’s law with Riesz interactional potential. The rigorous derivation of the system (
1.1
) with the isothermal pressure
(\gamma=1)
from the Vlasov–Riesz–Fokker–Planck-type equation is studied in
[
13
]
. The relation between (
1.1
) and the fractional porous medium equation is also investigated in
[
10
]
through a relaxation limit.

One of the main difficulties in analyzing the Euler system arises from the finite-time singularity formation. More precisely, no matter how smooth and small the initial data are, the equations may develop a singularity in finite time. In the repulsive case
(\lambda<0)
, the system (
1.1
) has a dissipative effect from the interactions. However, even in that case, it is known that the system (
1.1
) has a singularity formation. We refer to
[
6
,
9
,
32
,
33
,
34
]
for the study of the finite-time breakdown of smoothness of solutions to the Euler, Euler–Poisson, or Euler–Riesz equations. Thus, for the global-in-time existence of solutions, it is natural to take into account solutions with lower regularities, for instance, measure-valued solutions, entropy weak solutions
[
3
,
4
,
18
,
25
,
31
,
37
]
. We refer to
[
8
]
and references therein for a general survey of the Euler equations.

Some of the previous works on the global existence of regular solutions for the Euler–Poisson system, (
1.1
) with
\sigma=2
, can be summarized as follows. In the case of zero background state, the critical thresholds phenomena, that lead to global regularity or finite-time blow-up of regular solutions, in the one-dimensional Euler–Poisson system with and without pressure are observed in
[
36
]
and
[
19
]
, respectively. In the presence of the constant background state, the global-in-time existence of regular solutions to the one-dimensional Euler-Poisson is studied in
[
21
]
, and two and three-dimensional cases are dealt with in
[
20
,
27
,
30
]
.

For the Euler–Riesz system (
1.1
), the local-in-time well-posedness is established in a recent work
[
9
]
. Roughly speaking, the interaction force in (
1.1
) can be regarded as
\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}\rho\sim|\nabla|^{1-\sigma}\rho
in terms of regularity. This observation shows that establishing the well-posedness theory of (
1.1
) is more challenging in the case
\sigma\in(0,1)
. In fact, this regularity issue is already addressed in
[
7
,
11
,
12
]
for the Vlasov–Riesz system. Since the case
\sigma=1
plays a critical value and has physical meaning as well, motivated from
[
2
,
5
,
26
]
, we call that particular case the Manev potential and divide the regime
0<\sigma<2
into two parts:

(i)

1\leq\sigma<2
(sub-Manev potential case) and

(ii)

0<\sigma<1
(super-Manev potential case).

In the current work, we study the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of regular solutions to the system (
1.1
). In the presence of linear damping in velocity and constant background state, the global-in-time existence and uniqueness of regular solutions are obtained in
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
in the repulsive and attractive interaction cases, respectively. However, in the undamped case, as mentioned above it is not expected to have the global existence of regular solutions for general initial data due to the lack of dissipations. Thus, inspired by
[
1
,
17
,
23
,
24
]
, we analyze sufficient conditions on initial data that ensure the global-in-time existence of regular solutions. More precisely, we consider a sufficiently small, regular initial density and a
dispersive
spectral condition on the initial velocity that causes fluid particles to spread. In this framework, the global-in-time Cauchy problem for the Euler system with sub-Manev interactions, i.e. (
1.1
) with
c_{P}>0
,
\lambda=\pm 1
, and
\sigma\in[1,2)
is discussed in a recent work
[
16
]
.

1.1.
Main results

Our main results are two-fold. First, we investigate the global well-posedness of the pressureless, repulsive Euler-Riesz equations, i.e. the system (
1.1
) with
c_{P}=0
and
\lambda=-1
. Secondly, we investigate the global well-posedness of Euler–Riesz equations with pressure, i.e. the system (
1.1
) with
c_{P}>0
.

To state our results, we consider the Burgers’ equation:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+v\cdot\nabla v=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

\displaystyle v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

For
s>\frac{d}{2}+1
, we define the space
E^{s}
as

E^{s}:=\{z\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}):\nabla z\in L^{\infty}\mbox{ and }\nabla^{2}z\in H^{s-2}\}.

Here,
\nabla^{k}
denotes the
k
-th order gradient.

We then recall from
[
1
]
the following result for the Burgers’ equation.

Proposition 1.1
.

Suppose that
v_{0}\in E^{s}
satisfies

(1.3)

\mbox{there exists}\quad\varepsilon>0\quad\mbox{such that}\quad\mbox{dist(\emph{Sp}(}Dv_{0}(x)),\mathbb{R}_{-})\geq\varepsilon\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\emph{Sp}A
denotes the spectrum of a matrix
A
.
Then the following holds:

(i)

The system (
1.2
) has a global-in-time classical solution corresponding to the initial datum
v_{0}(x)
such that

\nabla^{2}v\in\mathcal{C}^{j}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s-2-j})\quad\mbox{for }\ j=0,1.

(ii)

Moreover, there exists a function
K\in\mathcal{C}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that the relation

(1.4)

\nabla v(x,t)=\frac{\mathbb{I}_{d}}{1+t}+\frac{K(x,t)}{(1+t)^{2}}

holds for any
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Here,
\mathbb{I}_{d}
stands for the
d\times d
identity matrix and
K
satisfies

(1.5)

\|K(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq k_{\ell}(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\ell}\quad\mbox{for any }\ 0<\ell\leq s-1

for some constant
k_{\ell}>0
. In particular, we get

(1.6)

\nabla\cdot v(x,t)=\frac{d}{1+t}+\frac{\mbox{\rm Tr}K(x,t)}{(1+t)^{2}}.

Here, the symbol
Tr
denotes the trace of a matrix.

(iii)

If
\nabla^{2}v_{0}
is bounded, then we have

\|\nabla^{2}v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(1+t)^{-3}.

1.1.1.
Pressureless case

In the absence of pressure, i.e.
c_{P}=0
, by introducing
n:=\sqrt{\rho}
, we reformulate the system (
1.1
) as

(1.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n+u\cdot\nabla n+\frac{1}{2}n\nabla\cdot u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u=\lambda\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})

with the initial data:

(n(x,0),u(x,0))=(n_{0}(x):=\sqrt{\rho_{0}(x)},u_{0}(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Note that in the pressureless and attractive Riesz interaction case, i.e.
c_{P}=0
and
\lambda=1
, we would deduce from the linear analysis that (
1.7
) is ill-posed when
\sigma<2
, see
[
9
, Section 2]
for details. Therefore, in the absence of pressure, we only deal with the repulsive case. For simplicity of presentation, we take
\lambda=-1
. Then our main theorem in the pressureless and repulsive case is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\sigma\in(0,\min\{d,2\})
, and
s>\max\left\{2,\frac{d}{2}+1\right\}
. Suppose that the initial data
(n_{0},u_{0})
satisfy

(i)

there exists
v_{0}\in E^{s+1}
satisfying (
1.3
) and
u_{0}-v_{0}
is small in
H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}
and

(ii)

n_{0}
is small in
H^{s}
.

Then there exists a unique global-in-time regular solution
(n,u-v)
to (
1.7
) satisfying

n\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s})\quad\mbox{and}\quad u-v\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}).

Furthermore, one also has the following temporal decay estimates: for any
t\geq 0

\|n(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\ell-\frac{\sigma}{2}-\min\left\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{2}\right\}}\quad\forall\,\ell\in[0,s]

and

\|(u-v)(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\ell-\min\left\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{2}\right\}}\qquad\forall\,\ell\in\left[0,s+\frac{\sigma}{2}\right].

Here
C>0
is independent of
t
.

Due to the strong singularity in the Riesz interaction potential, it is required to find some cancellations of top-order terms in the estimates. Clearly, it depends on the reformulation and regularity of solution spaces. We discover that the transformation
n=\sqrt{\rho}
and the regularity of solutions
(n,u-v)\in H^{s}\times H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}
well capture the required cancellations of terms with highest-order derivatives. Here the sharp commutator estimates and various Sobolev inequalities are crucially employed. In addition to that, we analyze the stabilizing effect from the repulsive Riesz interaction potential to get a proper dissipation rate for
n
. Then this together with using a dispersive effect from the condition on the initial velocity
v_{0}\in E^{s}
provides the global regularity of solutions and the temporal decay estimates.

Remark 1.1
.

Here, we provide some remarks on the temporal decay.

(i)

We can rewrite the temporal decay of
n
in Theorem
1.1
as

(1.8)

\|n(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\ell-\min\left\{1-\frac{d-\sigma}{2},\,0\right\}}

for
\ell\in[0,s]
. When
d>\sigma+2
, equivalently
1-\frac{d-\sigma}{2}<0
, (
1.8
) does not give any decay estimate when
\ell\leq\frac{d-\sigma}{2}-1
. However, once we use the conservation of mass:

\|n(t)\|_{L^{2}}=\|n_{0}\|_{L^{2}}

(see Lemma
3.1
below), and combine this with (
1.8
) when
\ell=s
to yield

(1.9)

\|n(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\ell-\min\left\{\frac{\ell}{s}\left(1-\frac{d-\sigma}{2}\right),\,0\right\}}

via a simple interpolation.

(ii)

From Theorem
1.1
and (
1.9
), we use the interpolation argument to obtain temporal decay estimates in
L^{p}
for
2\leq p\leq\infty
:

\|(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{p}-\min\left\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{2}\right\}}

and

\|n(t)\|_{L^{p}}\leq C(1+t)^{-(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p})-\min\left\{\frac{1}{s}(\frac{d}{2}-\frac{d}{p})(1-\frac{d-\sigma}{2}),\,0\right\}}

for
t\geq 0
.

1.1.2.
The case with pressure

We next consider the pressured case, i.e.
c_{P}>0
. We set
c_{P}=1
without loss of generality. In this case, we introduce the sound speed
n:=\kappa\rho^{\tilde{\gamma}}
with

\kappa=\frac{2\sqrt{\gamma}}{\gamma-1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{\gamma}=\frac{\gamma-1}{2},

and reformulate system (
1.1
) as the following symmetric form:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n+u\cdot\nabla n+\tilde{\gamma}n\nabla\cdot u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u+u\cdot\nabla u+\tilde{\gamma}n\nabla n=\lambda\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}n^{\frac{1}{\tilde{\gamma}}}

with initial data

(n(x,0),u(x,0))=(n_{0}(x):=\kappa(\rho_{0}(x))^{\tilde{\gamma}},\,u_{0}(x)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Before we state our main results for the system (
1.1
) with
c_{P}=1
, we intrdouce the following assumption
(\mathcal{A})
on the initial data and parameters
s,\gamma
, and
\sigma
:

(
\mathcal{A}
1)

There exists
v_{0}\in E^{s+1}
satisfying (
1.3
) and
u_{0}-v_{0}
is small in
H^{s}
.

(
\mathcal{A}
2)

n_{0}
is small in
H^{s}
.

(
\mathcal{A}
3)

If
\frac{2}{\gamma-1}
is not an integer, assume that

s<\frac{2}{\gamma-1}+\sigma-\frac{1}{2}.

We first state our main theorem for system (
1.1
) with the attractive or repulsive sub-Manev interactions (
1\leq\sigma<2
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\sigma\in[1,2)
, and
s>\frac{d}{2}+1
. Suppose that the initial data
(n_{0},v_{0})
and system parameters
s,\gamma
, and
\sigma
satisfy standing assumption
(\mathcal{A})
. Then there exists a unique global-in-time regular solution
(n,u-v)
to (
1.10
) regardless of the sign of
\lambda
satisfying

n\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s})\quad\mbox{and}\quad u-v\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}_{+};H^{s}).

Furthermore, we have the following temporal decay estimates:

\|n(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\ell-\min\left\{1,\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{2}\right\}}

and

\|(u-v)(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\ell-\min\left\{1,\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{2}\right\}}

for all
\ell\in[0,s]
and
t\geq 0
. Here
C>0
is independent of
t
.

.

We provide some remarks on the result of Theorem

.

(i)

Since we are assuming

, we naturally exclude the case

.

(ii)

The case
\sigma=2

(iii)

For the case
\sigma\in[1,2]
, authors in

under the same conditions on

, and

. Compared to that result, our result deals with the system (

) in a usual Sobolev space, and we are also able to provide the

for

.

Finally, in the super-Manev potential case (
0<\sigma<1
), we separately present our main results in the repulsive (i.e.
\lambda=-1
) and attractive (i.e.
\lambda=1
) cases, respectively.

Theorem 1.3

(repulsive case)
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\sigma\in(0,1)
, and
s>\max\{2+\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{d}{2}+1\}

, and

Then there exists a unique global-in-time regular solution

to (

\|n(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\ell-\min\left\{1,\,\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{2},\,\frac{d-\sigma}{2}\right\}}

\|(u-v)(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\ell-\min\left\{1,\,\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{2},\,\frac{d-\sigma}{2}\right\}}

for all
\ell\in[0,s]
and
t\geq 0
. Here
C>0
is independent of
t
.

Theorem 1.4

(attractive case)
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
s>\max\{3-\frac{\sigma}{2},\frac{d}{2}+1\}
. Suppose that the initial data
(n_{0},v_{0})
and system parameters

and

satisfy standing assumption
(\mathcal{A})
. We further assume that

\|n(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\ell-\min\left\{1,\,\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{2}\right\}}

and

\|(u-v)(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{\ell}}\leq C(1+t)^{\frac{d}{2}-\ell-\min\left\{1,\,\frac{d(\gamma-1)}{2}\right\}}

. Here

is independent of

.

As briefly mentioned above, in the super-Manev potential case
(0<\sigma<1)
, we lose some differentiability of the interaction force
\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}\rho
. Thus, the classical energy estimates as in the sub-Manev case would not be directly applicable to that case. In the repulsive case, we utilize the arguments developed in Section
1.1.1
for the pressureless case; we combine
\dot{H}^{s}
-estimates of
(n,u-v)
with the weighted
\dot{H}^{s-\frac{\sigma}{2}}
-estimates of
n
to cancel out the repulsive interaction force term arising from the
\dot{H}^{s}
-estimates of
u-v
. On the other hand, the attractive case is more delicate, and it is essential to use a dissipation effect from the pressure appropriately to handle the attractive super-Manev interaction forces. Since the interaction potential is of a slightly lower order than the pressure, we employ the
regularity-tuning
iteration argument developed in
[
15
]
, see Section
4.2
for details. This enables us to handle the attractive super-Manev interaction term by the pressure term. Again, the proofs strongly rely on the energy method based on the sharp commutator estimates for the fractional Laplacian and various Sobolev embeddings.

1.2.
Organization of the paper

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2

and

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present several technical lemmas that will be used throughout this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

(i)

Let
s>0
,
r\in(1,\infty)
and
p_{1},p_{2},q_{1},q_{2}\in(1,\infty]
.
Then we have

(2.1)

\|\Lambda^{s}(fg)\|_{L^{r}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|g\|_{L^{q_{1}}}+\|f\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\|\Lambda^{s}g\|_{L^{q_{2}}}

for
f\in\dot{W}^{s,p_{1}}\cap L^{p_{2}}
and
g\in L^{q_{1}}\cap\dot{W}^{s,q_{2}}
, where

\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}=\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}
.

(ii)

Let
s>0
. If
f\in\dot{W}^{1,\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
and
g\in L^{\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{s-1}
, then we obtain

(2.2)

\|[f,\Lambda^{s}]g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}.

(iii)

Let
s>1
. If
f\in\dot{W}^{2,\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
and
g\in L^{\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{s-2}
, then we get

(2.3)

\|[f,\Lambda^{s}]g-s\nabla f\cdot\Lambda^{s-2}\nabla g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla^{2}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{s-2}}.

(iv)

Let
0<\sigma<1
. If
\Lambda^{1-\sigma}f\in L^{\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}}
and
g\in\dot{W}^{\frac{\sigma}{2},2}
,
then we deduce

(2.4)

\displaystyle\|[\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}},f]g\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{1-\sigma}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+1-\sigma}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}g\|_{L^{2}}.

(v)

Let
1\leq s<2
, then the following inequality holds

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|[\Lambda^{s},f]g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{1}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}

for
\Lambda f,\nabla f\in L^{\infty}
, and
g\Lambda^{s}f\in L^{2}
.
Moreover, when
0<s<1
, we have

\|[\Lambda^{s},f]g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{s_{1}}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-s_{1}}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}

for any
s_{1}<s
.

Here
a\lesssim b
represents that there is a positive constant such that
a\leq Cb
.

Proof.

For the Kato–Ponce inequality (i) we refer to
[
22
]
.
The inequalities (ii) and (iii) are found in
[
1
,
17
]
.
The commutator estimates of the form (iv) and (v) follow from the fractional Leibniz inequality obtained in
[
29
]
.
In fact, it follows from
[
29
]
that

\|\Lambda^{s}\partial_{x_{i}}(fg)-f\Lambda^{s}\partial_{x_{i}}g-g\Lambda^{s}\partial_{x_{i}}f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{s_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|\Lambda^{s_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}}

holds for
-1<s<0
and
s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,s+1]
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in[2,\infty]
with
s+1=s_{1}+s_{2}
and
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}
. Applying this with
s_{1}=1-\sigma
,
s_{2}=\frac{\sigma}{2}
,
p_{1}=\infty
, and
p_{2}=2
, then we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\left(fg\right)-f\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}g\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\left(fg\right)-f\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}g-g\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{1-\sigma}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}}f\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{\sigma}}}\|g\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{2d}}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{1-\sigma}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+1-\sigma}}\|\Lambda^{\frac{\sigma}{2}}g\|_{L^{2}}

thanks to the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality.
Thus we obtain (
2.4
).

To show (v), we recall that
the following inequality holds for
s>0
and
p\in(1,\infty)
:

\|\Lambda^{s}(fg)-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq s_{1}}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\partial^{\alpha}f\Lambda^{s,\alpha}g-\sum_{|\beta|<s_{2}}\frac{1}{\beta!}\partial^{\beta}g\Lambda^{s,\beta}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{s_{1}}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s_{2}}g\|_{L^{p}}

whenever
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 0
with
s_{1}+s_{2}=s
. Here,
\alpha,\beta
denote the multi-index and
\widehat{\Lambda^{s,\alpha}h}:=i^{-|\alpha|}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}(|\xi|^{s})\hat{h}(\xi)
.
In the case
1\leq s<2
, we use the above with taking

to get

\displaystyle\|[\Lambda^{s},f]g\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{s}(fg)-\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 1}\frac{1}{\alpha!}\partial^{\alpha}f\Lambda^{s,\alpha}g-g\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{|\alpha|=1}\|\partial^{\alpha}f\Lambda^{s,\alpha}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{1}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{1}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-1}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}

from the fact that
\|\Lambda^{s,\alpha}g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{s-\alpha}g\|_{L^{2}}
.
On the other hand, in the case
0<s<1
, it is not difficult to check that a similar process yields

\displaystyle\|[\Lambda^{s},f]g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\Lambda^{s_{1}}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{s-s_{1}}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|g\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{2}}

for

.
∎

For the uniqueness of solutions, we need to estimate the term

due to the use of the inequality (

). Here, we would like point out that

cannot be controlled by

. To handle this difficulty, we provide the following lemma which, in particular, gives

.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
s>0
. If
f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+s+\varepsilon}\cap\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+s-\varepsilon}
, then we have

\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+s+\varepsilon}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+s-\varepsilon}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

From
[
28
, Lemma 2.1]
, it follows that

\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\||\xi|^{s}\hat{f}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\||\xi|^{\frac{d}{2}+s+\varepsilon}\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\||\xi|^{\frac{d}{2}+s-\varepsilon}\hat{f}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}=C\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+s+\varepsilon}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+s-\varepsilon}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

by Young’s inequality.
∎

Due to the structure of our reformulation, we also need to employ the Sobolev inequality for the composition of functions presented in the lemma below. For the proof, we refer to
[
1
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\geq 1
and
0\leq s<\alpha+1/2
. If
f\in L^{\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{s}
, then we have

\||f|^{\alpha}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\alpha-1}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.

Finally, we provide a Grönwall-type lemma below.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
Y\in AC(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfies the following differential inequality

(2.6)

\frac{d}{dt}Y(t)+\frac{a}{1+t}Y(t)\leq C^{\ast}\left(Y^{2}(t)+\frac{Y(t)}{(1+t)^{2}}+c_{P}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{Y^{b_{i}+1}(t)}{(1+t)^{1-c_{i}}}\right)\quad\text{a.e t}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}

for some
C^{\ast}=C^{*}(N)>0
, any positive integer
N
and
c_{P}=0,1
.
If
a>1
,
b_{i}>0
, and
c_{i}<ab_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,N
,
then there exists
M=M(a,b_{i},c_{i},C^{\ast})
such that if
Y(0)\leq M
, then we have

Y(t)\leq\frac{2e^{\frac{C^{\ast}t}{1+t}}}{(1+t)^{a}}Y(0),\quad\forall t\geq 0.

Proof.

The case
N=1
is already shown in
[
1
, Lemma 4.1]
.
Following a similar argument as in
[
1
]
, we extend it to any positive integer
N
.
Let us set

Z(t):=(1+t)^{a}e^{-\frac{C^{*}t}{1+t}}Y(t)

and then (
2.6
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Z(t)

\displaystyle\leq C^{*}(1+t)^{a}e^{-\frac{C^{*}t}{1+t}}Y^{2}(t)+C^{*}e^{-\frac{C^{*}t}{1+t}}c_{P}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1+t)^{a+c_{i}-1}Y^{b_{i}+1}(t)

\displaystyle=C^{*}(1+t)^{-a}e^{\frac{C^{*}t}{1+t}}Z^{2}(t)+C^{*}c_{P}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1+t)^{c_{i}-1-ab_{i}}e^{\frac{C^{*}b_{i}t}{1+t}}Z^{b_{i}+1}(t).

To use the bootstrap argument, for any
T>0
we assume that

(2.7)

Z(t)\leq 2Z_{0}\quad\forall\,t\in[0,T],

where
Z_{0}:=Z(0)
.
Then it yields that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Z(t)\leq 4C^{*}(1+t)^{-a}e^{C^{*}}Z_{0}^{2}+Cc_{P}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1+t)^{c_{i}-1-ab_{i}}e^{C^{*}b_{i}}(2Z_{0})^{b_{i}+1}.

Taking integration over
[0,T]
, we obtain

(2.8)

\displaystyle Z(t)

\displaystyle\leq Z_{0}+\frac{4C^{*}e^{C^{*}}}{a-1}Z_{0}^{2}(1-(1+t)^{1-a})+c_{P}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{2^{b_{i}+1}C^{*}e^{C^{*}b_{i}}}{ab_{i}-c_{i}}Z_{0}^{b_{i}+1}(1-(1+t)^{c_{i}-ab_{i}})

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+\frac{4C^{*}e^{C^{*}}}{a-1}Z_{0}+c_{P}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{2^{b_{i}+1}C^{*}e^{C^{*}b_{i}}}{ab_{i}-c_{i}}Z_{0}^{b_{i}}\Big)Z_{0}

for
a>1
and
ab_{i}>c_{i}
.
When
Z_{0}=0
, it is trivial to get (
2.7
). Otherwise, we take
Z_{0}>0
sufficiently small so that

\frac{4C^{*}e^{C^{*}}}{a-1}Z_{0}+c_{P}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{2^{b_{i}+1}C^{*}e^{C^{*}b_{i}}}{ab_{i}-c_{i}}Z_{0}^{b_{i}}<1.

if
b_{i}>0
. Hence, (
2.8
) implies (
2.7
) for any
T>0
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Pressureless Euler–Riesz system

In this section, we show a priori estimates for the system (
1.7
) with repulsive case
\lambda=-1
.
First, for simplicity, we set
w:=u-v
, then the system (
1.7
) can be rewritten as follows:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}n+w\cdot\nabla n+v\cdot\nabla n+\frac{1}{2}n\nabla\cdot w+\frac{1}{2}n\nabla\cdot v=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+w\cdot\nabla w+v\cdot\nabla w+w\cdot\nabla v=-\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}n^{2}.

3.1.
A priori estimates

We first provide a priori estimates for smooth solutions to (
3.1

3.1.1.
H^{s}\times H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}
bound estimates

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(n,w)
be a smooth solution to (
3.1
) decaying sufficiently fast at infinity. Then we have

\frac{d}{dt}\|n\|_{L^{2}}=0,

\frac{d}{dt}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1-\frac{d}{2}}{1+t}\|w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\|w\|_{L^{2}}}{(1+t)^{2}}+\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})\|_{L^{2}},

and for
s>1
,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}X_{s,\sigma}+\frac{s+\min\{0,\frac{\sigma}{2}+1-\frac{d}{2}\}}{1+t}X_{s,\sigma}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}X_{s,\sigma}+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\frac{X_{s,\sigma}}{(1+t)^{2}}+\frac{\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{2+s-\frac{d}{2}}}+\frac{\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{2+s+\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{d}{2}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{(\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}+\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}})}{(1+t)^{3}}+\frac{\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{s+1-\frac{d}{2}}}+\frac{\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{s+1+\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{d}{2}}},

where

X_{s,\sigma}^{2}:=\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}+4\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}
.

Proof.

First, we deduce from
\eqref{npER}_{1}
that

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|n|^{2}\,dx=0.

To estimate
\|w\|_{L^{2}}
, we see that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|w|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w(w\cdot\nabla w)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w(v\cdot\nabla w)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w(w\cdot\nabla v)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla\cdot w)|w|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla\cdot v)|w|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{1+t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|w|^{2}\,dx-\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w(K\cdot w)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}w\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\frac{d}{2}-1}{1+t}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{(1+t)^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})\|_{L^{2}}

which gives the desired result.

Next, we show the highest-order estimates for
(n,w)
.
By applying
\Lambda^{s}
to (
3.1
)
1
, we estimate

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda^{s}n|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n(n\nabla\cdot\Lambda^{s}w)\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n\,(w\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s}n)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n(v\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s}n)\,dx+s\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n\,\partial_{k}v\cdot\nabla\partial_{k}\Lambda^{s-2}n\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n\,\Lambda^{s}(n\nabla\cdot v)\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n\,[w,\Lambda^{s}]\cdot\nabla n\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n[n,\Lambda^{s}]\nabla\cdot w\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n\,\Big([v,\Lambda^{s}]\cdot\nabla n-s\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}\partial_{k}v\cdot\nabla\partial_{k}\Lambda^{s-2}n\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{7}I_{i}.

First, direct computations using (
1.6
) give

I_{1}\leq C\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad I_{2}\leq\frac{\frac{d}{2}}{1+t}\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}+\frac{C}{(1+t)^{2}}\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}.

For

, (
1.4
) implies

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}\partial_{k}v\cdot\Lambda^{-2}\nabla\partial_{k}z

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{1+t}\Lambda^{-2}\Big(-\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}\partial_{kk}z\Big)+\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}\frac{K_{k}}{(1+t)^{2}}\cdot\Lambda^{-2}\nabla\partial_{k}z

\displaystyle=-\frac{z}{1+t}+\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}\frac{K_{k}}{(1+t)^{2}}\cdot\Lambda^{-2}\nabla\partial_{k}z,

where

denotes the

Then we combine this with

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=s\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n\,\partial_{k}v\cdot\nabla\partial_{k}\Lambda^{s-2}n\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{s}{1+t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda^{s}n|^{2}\,dx+\frac{s}{(1+t)^{2}}\sum_{1\leq k\leq d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}n\,K_{k}\cdot\Lambda^{s-2}\nabla\partial_{k}n\,dx

where

due to (

For the rest, we use (

Next, we apply

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Lambda^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}w|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}w\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s}(n^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}w\,(w\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}w)\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}w(v\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}w)\,dx

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

To eliminate the last term on the left-hand side of the above, we use the integration by part to observe that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}w\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s}(n^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}w\cdot\Lambda^{s}(n\nabla n)\,dx

\displaystyle=2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\Lambda^{s}w\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{s}n\,dx-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{s}w\cdot[n,\Lambda^{s}]\nabla n\,dx

and thus we obtain

where we used (
2.2
). Thus it follows that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}+\frac{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}+1-\frac{d}{2}}{1+t}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}n\Lambda^{s}n\nabla\cdot\Lambda^{s}w\,dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\frac{\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{2+s+\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{d}{2}}}+\frac{\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}}{(1+t)^{3}}+\frac{\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{s+1+\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{d}{2}}}\right)\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}.

Finally, we set

X_{s,\sigma}^{2}:=\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}+4\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2},

and from (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}X_{s,\sigma}^{2}+\frac{s+\min\{0,\frac{\sigma}{2}+1-\frac{d}{2}\}}{1+t}X_{s,\sigma}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}X_{s,\sigma}^{2}+\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}X_{s,\sigma}+\frac{X_{s,\sigma}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{2+s-\frac{d}{2}}}+\frac{\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}}{(1+t)^{3}}+\frac{\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{s+1-\frac{d}{2}}}\right)X_{s,\sigma}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{2+s-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}+\frac{\|w\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}}{(1+t)^{3}}+\frac{\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{s+1-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}\right)X_{s,\sigma},

and this gives the desired estimate.
∎

3.1.2.
Temporal decay estimates

We next investigate a priori temporal decay estimates of solutions. For this, inspired by
[
1
,
17
]
,
we introduce

n_{\ell_{1},p_{1}}(t):=(1+t)^{\ell_{1}+\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{d}{p_{1}}-1}\|n(t)\|_{\dot{W}^{\ell_{1},p_{1}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad w_{\ell_{2},p_{2}}(t):=(1+t)^{\ell_{2}-\frac{d}{p_{2}}-1}\|w(t)\|_{\dot{W}^{\ell_{2},p_{2}}}

for
\ell_{1}\in[0,s]
,
\ell_{2}\in[0,s+\frac{\sigma}{2}]
, and
p_{1},p_{2}\in[2,\infty]
. For simplicity, we write
n_{p_{1}}:=n_{0,p_{1}}
and
w_{p_{2}}:=w_{0,p_{2}}
.
We also define

Y_{s,\sigma}(t):=\left(w_{s+\frac{\sigma}{2},2}^{2}(t)+4n_{s,2}^{2}(t)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad Z(t):=n_{2}(t)+w_{2}(t)+Y_{s,\sigma}(t).

Then the following lemma is obtained by using the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
s>\frac{d}{2}+1
, we have the following relations:

\displaystyle n_{\infty}(t)\lesssim n_{s,2}^{\frac{\frac{d}{2}}{s}}(t)\,n_{2}^{1-\frac{\frac{d}{2}}{s}}(t),\hskip 65.0ptw_{1,\infty}(t)\lesssim w_{s+\frac{\sigma}{2},2}^{\frac{\frac{d}{2}+1}{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}(t)\,w_{2}^{1-\frac{\frac{d}{2}+1}{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}(t),

\displaystyle n_{1,\infty}(t)\lesssim n_{s,2}^{\frac{\frac{d}{2}+1}{s}}(t)\,n_{2}^{1-\frac{\frac{d}{2}+1}{s}}(t),\hskip 40.0ptw_{s,2}(t)\lesssim w_{s+\frac{\sigma}{2},2}^{\frac{s}{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}(t)\,w_{2}^{1-\frac{s}{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}(t),

\displaystyle n_{s-1,2}(t)\lesssim n_{s,2}^{\frac{s-1}{s}}(t)\,n_{2}^{\frac{1}{s}}(t),\hskip 60.0ptw_{s-1+\frac{\sigma}{2},2}(t)\lesssim w_{s+\frac{\sigma}{2},2}^{\frac{s-1}{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}(t)\,w_{2}^{1-\frac{s-1}{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}(t),

\displaystyle n_{\frac{d}{\sigma-1}}(t)\lesssim n_{s,2}^{\frac{\frac{d}{2}-(\sigma-1)}{s}}(t)\,n_{2}^{\frac{s-\frac{d}{2}+\sigma-1}{s}}(t)\quad\mbox{when}\quad\sigma>1.

In particular, each term on the left-hand side of the inequalities above is bounded by
CZ(t)
with a constant
C>0
independent of
t
.

Then our main result of this subsection can be stated as follows.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
be satisfied.
For
T>0
, suppose that
(n,w)
is a smooth solution to (
3.1
) on the time interval
[0,T]
decaying sufficiently fast at infinity. Then we have

\frac{d}{dt}Z(t)+\frac{C_{d,\sigma}}{1+t}Z(t)\lesssim Z(t)^{2}+\frac{Z(t)}{(1+t)^{2}},

where
C_{d,\sigma}=1+\min\{\frac{d-\sigma}{2},\,1\}>0
.

Proof.

First, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that

\frac{d}{dt}n_{2}+\frac{1+\frac{d-\sigma}{2}}{1+t}n_{2}=0

and

\frac{d}{dt}w_{2}+\frac{2}{1+t}w_{2}\lesssim w_{1,\infty}w_{2}+\frac{w_{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}+(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

Here, we divide it into two cases,
0<\sigma\leq 1
and
1<\sigma<2
.
In particular for
1<\sigma<2
, the condition on
\sigma
naturally implies
d\geq 2
.

\bullet
(Case A:
0<\sigma\leq 1
)
Here, we use the Gagliardo–Nirenberg interpolation inequality and (
2.1
) to get

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|n^{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\frac{1-\sigma}{s}}\|n^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{1-\sigma}{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)^{\frac{1-\sigma}{s}}\left(\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}\|n\|_{L^{2}}\right)^{1-\frac{1-\sigma}{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{1+\frac{d}{2s}}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{1-\frac{d}{2s}}\right)^{\frac{1-\sigma}{s}}\left(\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\frac{d}{2s}}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{d}{2s}}\right)^{1-\frac{1-\sigma}{s}}

\displaystyle=C\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{\frac{\frac{d}{2}+(1-\sigma)}{s}}\|n\|_{L^{2}}^{2-\frac{\frac{d}{2}+(1-\sigma)}{s}}

which implies

(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim n_{s,2}^{\frac{\frac{d}{2}+(1-\sigma)}{s}}n_{2}^{2-\frac{\frac{d}{2}+(1-\sigma)}{s}}.

Hence, we deduce from the above estimates that

(3.4)

\frac{d}{dt}(n_{2}+w_{2})+\frac{C_{d,\sigma}}{1+t}(n_{2}+w_{2})\lesssim\frac{Z(t)}{(1+t)^{2}}+Z(t)^{2}.

\bullet
(Case B:
1<\sigma<2
)
In this case, we first use Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality to get

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|n^{2}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sigma-1}{d}}}}\lesssim\|n\|_{L^{2}}\|n\|_{L^{\frac{d}{\sigma-1}}},

and use Lemma
3.2
to yield

\displaystyle(1+t)^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}(n^{2})\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim n_{2}\,n_{\frac{d}{\sigma-1}}\lesssim n_{s,2}^{\frac{\frac{d}{2}-(\sigma-1)}{s}}n_{2}^{2-\frac{\frac{d}{2}-(\sigma-1)}{s}}.

Thus, we also have (
3.4
) in this case.

Finally, the highest-order estimate in Lemma
3.1
implies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}Y_{s,\sigma}+\frac{1+\min\{1,\,\frac{d-\sigma}{2}\}}{1+t}Y_{s,\sigma}

\displaystyle\lesssim w_{1,\infty}Y_{s,\sigma}+n_{1,\infty}w_{s,2}+\frac{Y_{s,\sigma}+n_{\infty}+w_{\infty}+n_{s-1,2}+w_{s-1+\frac{\sigma}{2},2}+n_{1,\infty}+w_{1,\infty}}{(1+t)^{2}}.

By Lemma
3.2
, it immediately follows that

\frac{d}{dt}Y_{s,\sigma}+\frac{C_{d,\sigma}}{1+t}Y_{s,\sigma}\lesssim Z(t)^{2}+\frac{Z(t)}{(1+t)^{2}}.

Therefore, we collect all the estimates to yield the desired result.
∎

3.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Now, we present the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

\bullet
(Global-in-time existence and temporal decay) First, we combine Lemma
3.1
with the arguments in
[
16
]
to deduce the local-in-time existence of regular solutions to (
3.1
). For the global-in-time existence, we deduce from Proposition
3.1
that

(3.5)

\frac{d}{dt}Z(t)+\frac{C_{d,\sigma}}{1+t}Z(t)\leq C_{0}\left(Z^{2}(t)+\frac{Z(t)}{(1+t)^{2}}\right)

for some constant
C_{0}>0
independent of
t
.
Here,
C_{d,\sigma}=1+\min\{1,\frac{d-\sigma}{2}\}
.
If

Z(0)\sim\|n_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|w_{0}\|_{H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}

is sufficiently small,
then by applying Lemma
2.4
to (
3.5
) with
a=C_{d,\sigma}>0
, we deduce

Z(t)\leq\frac{2e^{\frac{C_{0}t}{(1+t)}}}{(1+t)^{C_{d,\sigma}}}Z(0)\quad\forall\,t>0.

This shows that the desired global existence and temporal decay estimates in Theorem
1.1
.

\bullet
(Uniqueness) Here, we show the stability in
L^{2}
-Sobolev spaces which also implies the uniqueness. For
i=1,2
, let
(n_{i},w_{i})
be the solution to (
3.1
) constructed in the above on the time interval
[0,T]
corresponding to the initial data
(n_{i,0},w_{i,0})
. Then we first have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(n_{1}-n_{2})(w_{1}+v)\cdot\nabla(n_{1}-n_{2})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(n_{1}-n_{2})(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\nabla n_{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(n_{1}-n_{2})^{2}\nabla\cdot(w_{2}+v)\,dx-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(n_{1}-n_{2})n_{1}\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2})\,dx.

Here, integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\nabla(w_{1}+v)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla(w_{2}+v)\|_{L^{\infty}})\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|\nabla n_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\|n_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}),

where we used Young’s inequality and
C=C(T)
is a positive constant.
We also see

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot[(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\nabla(w_{2}+v)]\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot[(w_{1}+v)\cdot\nabla(w_{1}-w_{2})]\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}((n_{1})^{2}-(n_{2})^{2})\,dx

so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\nabla(w_{1}+v)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla(w_{2}+v)\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}((n_{1})^{2}-(n_{2})^{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

Note that

\displaystyle\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}((n_{1})^{2}-(n_{2})^{2})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|(n_{1}-n_{2})(n_{1}+n_{2})\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sigma-1}{d}}}}&\mbox{if}&\sigma\in[1,2),\\[11.38109pt]
\|(n_{1}-n_{2})(n_{1}+n_{2})\|_{\dot{H}^{1-\sigma}}&\mbox{if}&\sigma\in(0,1).\end{array}\right.

In the case
\sigma\in[1,2)
, the Sobolev embedding
\dot{H}^{\sigma-1}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sigma-1}{d}}}
implies

\|\nabla\Lambda^{-\sigma}((n_{1})^{2}-(n_{2})^{2})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}.

For the case
\sigma\in(0,1)
, we get the similar result by using (
2.1
). Thus, we obtain

(3.6)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(4\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\leq C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

The above estimate (
3.6
) forces us to estimate higher-order derivatives of solutions. Below, we provide the estimates of
\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
and
\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}
. Here we remark that there is a difference of
\frac{\sigma}{2}
-regularity between
n_{1}-n_{2}
and
w_{1}-w_{2}
like our solution space. In order to close the estimates of uniqueness, it is required to cancel top-order terms which appear in the estimates for
\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
and
\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}
.

We begin with the estimate for
\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})\partial_{i}(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\nabla n_{2}\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\partial_{i}\nabla n_{2}\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})\partial_{i}(w_{1}+v)\nabla\cdot(n_{1}-n_{2})\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})]^{2}\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2})\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})\partial_{i}n_{1}\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2})\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})(n_{1}-n_{2})\nabla\cdot\partial_{i}(w_{1}+w_{2}+2v)\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})(n_{1}+n_{2})\nabla\cdot\partial_{i}(w_{1}-w_{2})\,dx.

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1}}+\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\|(w_{1}-w_{2})\partial_{i}\nabla n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+C\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{i}(n_{1}-n_{2})(n_{1}+n_{2})\nabla\cdot\partial_{i}(w_{1}-w_{2})\,dx.

Note that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\|(w_{1}-w_{2})\partial_{i}\nabla n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}&\mbox{if}&d=1,\\[5.69054pt]
\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{2}{\delta}}}\|\nabla^{2}n_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{2}{1-\delta}}}&\mbox{if}&d=2,\\[5.69054pt]
\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d}}}}\|\nabla^{2}n_{2}\|_{L^{d}}&\mbox{if}&d\geq 3.\end{array}\right.

From the following Sobolev embeddings

H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad{H}^{s}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{2}\qquad\text{when }d=1,

\dot{H}^{1-\delta}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2}{\delta}}\quad\text{and}\quad{H}^{s}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{2+\delta}\hookrightarrow\dot{W}^{2,\frac{2}{1-\delta}}\qquad\text{when }d=2

for small
\delta>0
with
s>2+\delta
, and

\dot{H}^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d}}}\quad\text{and}\quad H^{s}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+1}\cap\dot{H}^{2}\hookrightarrow\dot{W}^{2,d}\qquad\text{when }d\geq 3

for
s>1+\frac{d}{2}
, we arrive at

\|(w_{1}-w_{2})\partial_{i}\nabla n_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1}}.

Hence we use Young’s inequality to obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(n_{1}+n_{2})\nabla(n_{1}-n_{2})\cdot\nabla(\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2}))\,dx.

We also estimate
\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}[(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\nabla(w_{2}+v)]\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}[(w_{1}+v)\cdot\nabla(w_{1}-w_{2})]\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\nabla\Lambda^{1-\frac{\sigma}{2}}((n_{1})^{2}-(n_{2})^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle=:\sum_{i=1}^{3}J_{i}.

For
J_{1}
, one uses (
2.1
) and the similar arguments employed in (
3.7
) to get

(3.9)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}[(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\nabla(w_{2}+v)]\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla(w_{2}+v)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\nabla(w_{2}+v)\|_{L^{2}}&\mbox{if}&d=1\\
\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{2}{\delta}}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\nabla(w_{2}+v)\|_{L^{\frac{2}{1-\delta}}}&\mbox{if}&d=2\\
\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d}}}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\nabla(w_{2}+v)\|_{L^{d}}&\mbox{if}&d\geq 3\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\leq C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}},

where we used

w_{2}\in H^{s+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad v\in\dot{H}^{2+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\cap\dot{H}^{2+\delta+\frac{\sigma}{2}}\cap\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+1+\frac{\sigma}{2}},

and
\delta>0
is taken to be small so that
s>2+\delta
.
Thus, we have

J_{1}\leq C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}.

Here
C=C(T)
is a positive constant.

For
J_{2}
, we apply (
2.5
) with
f=w_{1}+v
,
g=\nabla(w_{1}-w_{2})
, and
s=1+\frac{\sigma}{2}
so that

(3.10)

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(w_{1}-w_{2})\cdot\Big(\left[\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}},\,w_{1}+v\right]\cdot\nabla(w_{1}-w_{2})\Big)\,dx

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\cdot(w_{1}+v)|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(w_{1}-w_{2})|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C(\|\Lambda(w_{1}+v)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla(w_{1}+v)\|_{L^{\infty}})\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(w_{1}+v)\cdot\nabla(w_{1}-w_{2})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|w_{1}+v\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+1-\varepsilon}}^{1/2}\|w_{1}+v\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}+1+\varepsilon}}^{1/2}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle+C\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}(w_{1}+v)\|_{L^{\frac{2d}{\sigma}}}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, where we used Lemma
2.2
and
\dot{H}^{\frac{d}{2}-\frac{\sigma}{2}}\hookrightarrow L^{\frac{2d}{\sigma}}
.

For
J_{3}
, one uses the argument used in (
3.7
) to get

\displaystyle\|\nabla\cdot(\nabla(n_{1}+n_{2})(n_{1}-n_{2}))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla(n_{1}-n_{2})\cdot\nabla(n_{1}+n_{2})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(n_{1}-n_{2})\nabla^{2}(n_{1}+n_{2})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}},

and this implies

\displaystyle J_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2})\Lambda^{2}((n_{1}-n_{2})(n_{1}+n_{2}))\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla(\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2}))\cdot\nabla[(n_{1}-n_{2})(n_{1}+n_{2})]\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla(\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2}))\cdot(n_{1}+n_{2})\nabla(n_{1}-n_{2})\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2})\nabla\cdot(\nabla(n_{1}+n_{2})(n_{1}-n_{2}))\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla(\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2}))\cdot(n_{1}+n_{2})\nabla(n_{1}-n_{2})\,dx+C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}.

Thus, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}+C\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla(\nabla\cdot(w_{1}-w_{2}))\cdot(n_{1}+n_{2})\nabla(n_{1}-n_{2})\,dx,

and we deduce from (
3.8
) that

(3.11)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(4\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{2}+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{\dot{H}^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}\right)\leq C(\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}).

Therefore, we combine (
3.6
) with (
3.11
) to yield

\frac{d}{dt}\left(4\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}\right)\leq C(\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}),

and subsequently Grönwall’s lemma gives

\|n_{1}-n_{2}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|w_{1}-w_{2}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}\leq C(\|n_{1,0}-n_{2,0}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|w_{1,0}-w_{2,0}\|_{H^{1+\frac{\sigma}{2}}}^{2}),

which implies the desired uniqueness result.

For the

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}+\frac{\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}+\left(\frac{\|n\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{2+s-\frac{d}{2}}}+\frac{\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}}{(1+t)^{3}}+\frac{\|\nabla n\|_{L^{\infty}}}{(1+t)^{s+1-\frac{d}{2}}}\right)\|n\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}

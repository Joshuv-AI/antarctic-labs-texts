Abstract
. We study compressible types in the context of (local and global) NIP. By extending a result in machine learning theory (the existence of a bound on the recursive teaching dimension), we prove density of compressible types. Using this, we obtain explicit uniform honest definitions for NIP formulas (answering a question of Eshel and the second author), and build compressible models in countable NIP theories.



1.
Introduction

By the Sauer-Shelah lemma, if a formula
\phi(x;y)
is NIP, then the number of
\phi
-types over a finite set
A
is bounded by a polynomial in the cardinality of
A
. For a stable formula, this is a consequence of definability of types: one only needs to specify the parameters involved in the definition. In dense linear orders, the reason for this phenomenon is different: for any finite set
A
and element
b
, the
\leq
-type of
b
over
A
is implied by its restriction to some subset
A_{0}
of size 2: the information of the full type can be
compressed
down to this subset of bounded size. A
\leq
-type over an infinite set cannot in general be compressed down to a finite set, however finite parts of it can be uniformly compressed; following
[
23
]
, we call such a type
compressible
(
Definition

2.11
). We expect NIP formulas to exhibit a combination of those two behaviours.

For NIP theories one manifestation of this philosophy is the result from
[
23
]
that an arbitrary type has a generically stable part up to which it is compressible. Distal structures are (NIP) structures in which every type is compressible and hence this decomposition is trivial. For stable theories, compressible types turn out (
Lemma

4.8
) to be precisely types which are
l-isolated
, that is, isolated formula by formula. These play a role in Shelah’s classification theory; one key property is that in a countable stable theory, an l-atomic model exists over any set
[
18
, IV.2.18(4),3.1(5),3.2(1)]
. In this paper, we think of compressibility as an isolation notion and investigate its properties by analogy with the stable case. In order to obtain similar model-construction results, we need two basic properties: density of compressible types and transitivity of compressibility.

Density of compressible types over a set
A
means that every formula over
A
extends to a complete compressible type over
A
. We prove this for countable NIP theories (
Corollary

3.21
) by first considering the local setting of a single NIP formula
\phi
, and showing that any finite partial
\phi
-type extends to a complete compressible
\phi
-type (
Corollary

3.9
). This is a combinatorial argument based on the proof by Chen, Cheng, and Tang
[
3
]
of a bound on the “recursive teaching dimension” of a finite set system in terms of its VC-dimension. The existence of such a bound was used in
[
7
]
to prove uniform definability of types over finite sets (UDTFS) for an NIP formula in an arbitrary theory. We generalise this result (answering
[
7
, Question 28]
) by showing uniformity of honest definitions for NIP formulas, which was previously known only assuming NIP for the whole theory
[
5
, Theorem 11]
. For this, we first show that an arbitrary
\phi
-type
p
is a
rounded average
of finitely many compressible types (
Theorem

5.17
). The rounded average of the compression schemes of these types gives an honest definition for
p
.

In fact, it turns out that full consistency of
p
is not required here: for large enough
k
we get uniform honest definitions for
k
-consistent families of instances of
\phi
, which we dub
k
-hypes
(
Corollary

5.30
). Using this, we also obtain in
Theorem

5.36
uniform definability of
\phi
-types which are
pseudofinite
in the sense that their positive and negative parts are pseudofinite (
Definition

5.34
).

In order to prove transitivity, namely that
\operatorname{tp}(AB/C)
is compressible when
\operatorname{tp}(A/BC)
and
\operatorname{tp}(B/C)
are, we return to the global setting of an NIP theory and use the type-decomposition theorem from
[
23
]
. We show in
Proposition

6.23
that compressibility can be
rescoped
to an arbitrary subset of the domain: if
\operatorname{tp}(a/B)
is compressible and
C\subseteq B
, then
\operatorname{tp}(a/C)
is compressible in the language with constants for elements of
B
. We deduce transitivity in
Proposition

6.25
.

Finally, we conclude that for countable NIP theories (or even countable theories naming any set of constants) one can construct models which are compressible over arbitrary sets (
Propositions

6.29
and
6.30
). We give several applications:

\bullet
Given a definable unary set
X
whose induced structure is stable, and any model
N
of the theory of the induced structure, there is a model
M
of
T
such that
X(M)=N
and moreover, if
N^{\prime}\succ N
then there is
M^{\prime}\succ M
such that
X(M^{\prime})=N^{\prime}
. This is
Corollary

6.33
.

\bullet
If the theory is not stable, we can extend models without realising any non-algebraic generically stable type (
Corollaries

6.39
and
6.40
).

\bullet
We analyse compressiblity in ACVF, showing that then any model
M
containing
A
whose residue field is algebraic over
A
is compressible over
A
(
Example

6.41
).

2.3.
VC-dimension and NIP

Definition 2.3

(VC-dimension)
.

Let

. The pair

We say that

.

If no such

Definition 2.4
.

Suppose

\mathcal{L}
-theory and

Say

The theory

Definition 2.5
.

Suppose
T
is an
\mathcal{L}
-theory and
\phi(x,y)
is an NIP formula. Let
\operatorname{vc}(\phi)
be the VC-dimension of
\{\phi(M^{x},a)\mid a\in M^{y}\}
, where

is any (some) model of

. Note that this definition depends on the partition of variables.

Let
{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}
be the partitioned formula

(it is the same formula with the partition reversed). Let
\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\phi)=\operatorname{vc}({\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}})
be the
dual

.

Fact 2.6
.

[
21

Suppose

is a VC-class on

\mathcal{F}^{*}=\{\{s\in\mathcal{F}\mid x\in s\}\mid x\in X\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F})

Remark 2.7
.

By

is NIP, and
\operatorname{vc}({\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}})=\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\phi)<2^{\operatorname{vc}(\phi)+1}

By

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\binom{s}{\leq k}=\sum_{i\leq k}\binom{s}{i}
, and let

Remark 2.9
.

For all

,

Indeed,

\binom{n}{\leq k}^{n}\geq 2^{n}

Similarly, to show that

\binom{k}{\leq k}^{n}=2^{kn}\geq 2^{k}

Lemma 2.10

Let

Let

For each

2.4.
Compressible types

Here we will review the basic properties of compressible types.

Definition 2.11
.

A type
p(x)\in S(A)
is
compressible
if for any formula
\phi(x,y)
there is a formula
\psi(x,z)
such that for every finite set
A_{0}\subseteq A
, there is some

such that

•

\psi(x,c)\in p
and

•

\psi(x,c)\vdash(p\restriction\phi)|_{A_{0}}
(i.e., it implies the set
\{\phi(x,a)\mid\phi(x,a)\in p,\;a\in A_{0}^{y}\}\cup\{\neg\phi(x,a)\mid\phi(x,a)\notin p,\;a\in A_{0}^{y}\}
).

Suppose

. Given

any tuple, we let
(A,a)
be the structure with universe

and the induced structure coming from

\phi(x,y)
, there is a relation

interpreted by

for any

. Note that if

then

such that

are isomorphic over

This construction preserves useful information on the type

is

if for every formula

is definable and

Fact 2.12
.

[
23
, Lemma 3.2]
If
\operatorname{tp}(a/A)
is compressible and
(A^{\prime},a^{\prime})\equiv(A,a)
, then so is
\operatorname{tp}(a^{\prime}/A^{\prime})

Compactness gives the following equivalent definition of compressibility:

Fact 2.13
.

The type

is compressible iff for any (some)

-saturated elementary extension

and any formula

In fact, this was the original definition of compressibility in

We give another useful characterisation of compressible types.
Recall that two types

over

over

A type

Fact 2.14
.

[

, Lemma 3.3]

(

-saturated extension

(2)

(3)

.

(

A type

Let

The other direction follows from

3.
Density of (local) compressibility

Here we will prove that (local) compressible types are dense. In
Section

3.1
we prove an abstract version of this dealing with set systems of finite VC-dimension (generalising
[
3
, Lemma 4]
to infinite sets).
Then in
Section

3.2
we deduce that locally compressible types are dense for NIP formulas, and in
Section

3.1.
Compressibility for set systems of finite VC-dimension

Let

Let

Definition 3.1

•

Remark 3.2

This terminology is originally inspired by, but does not precisely agree with, the terminology around compression schemes in the statistical learning literature.

Suppose

4.

[

, we get the following fact.

Fact 4.18
.

If

is a model and

is stable then

It follows that when the base is a model,

5.
Rounded averages of compressible types and applications

Let
\operatorname{Maj}
be the majority rule Boolean operator, i.e., for truth values
P_{0},\dots,P_{n-1}
, let

\operatorname{Maj}_{i<n}P_{i}=\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{0}\subseteq n\\
|I_{0}|>n/2\end{subarray}}\bigwedge_{i\in I_{0}}P_{i}.

We just write
\operatorname{Maj}_{i}
if
n
is clear.

More generally, for
\alpha\in(0,1)
, let
\operatorname{Maj}^{\alpha}
be the “greater than an
\alpha
-fraction” Boolean operator, i.e.,

\operatorname{Maj}^{\alpha}_{i<n}P_{i}=\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{0}\subseteq n\\
|I_{0}|>\alpha n\end{subarray}}\bigwedge_{i\in I_{0}}P_{i}.

Definition 5.1
.

Suppose
\phi(x,y)
is a formula,
B\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{y}
and
p_{0}(x),\ldots,p_{n-1}(x)\in S_{\phi}(B)
.

The
rounded average
of
p_{0}(x),\ldots,p_{n-1}(x)\in S_{\phi}(B)
is the following (possibly inconsistent) collection of formulas

\operatorname{rAvg}(p_{i}\mid i<n):=\{\phi(x,b)^{\epsilon}\mid b\in B,\epsilon<2,\operatorname{Maj}_{i<n}(\phi(x,b)^{\epsilon}\in p_{i}(x))\}.

More generally, for
\alpha\in[\frac{1}{2},1)
, the
\alpha
-rounded average
is the set

\operatorname{rAvg}_{\alpha}(p_{i}\mid i<n):=\{\phi(x,b)^{\epsilon}\mid b\in B,\epsilon<2,\operatorname{Maj}^{\alpha}_{i<n}(\phi(x,b)^{\epsilon}\in p_{i}(x))\}.

The main result of this section is:

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\phi(x,y)
be an NIP formula and suppose
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
.
Then there exist
n
and
k
depending only on
\operatorname{vc}(\phi)
and
\alpha
such that for
A\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{y}
,
any
p\in S_{{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}}(A)
is the
\alpha
-rounded average of
n
types in

S_{{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}\downarrow k}(A)
.

(We give a more precise and general statement in
Theorem

5.17
, allowing a partial type
\pi(x)
.)

We give some applications:

•

Uniformity of honest definitions for NIP formulas, see
Definition

5.22
. This is
Corollary

5.23
.

•

Uniform definability of pseudofinite types, see
Theorem

5.36
.

5.1.
Superdensity

In this section we isolate a sufficient condition for proving

, which uses the

-theorem (see

Definition 5.3
.

Suppose
q\leq p<\omega
. A set system

-property

.

[
15
]

(The

-theorem) There exists a function

such that for any

, if

We isolate from the proof of

the following immediate generalisation of the

-theorem to infinite set systems.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\phi(x,y)
be NIP.
Let
p\geq q>\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\phi)
be integers, and let
N=N_{\operatorname{pq}}(p,q)
.
Let
A\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{x}
and
B\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{y}
.
Suppose that
\phi(A,b)\neq\emptyset
for every
b\in B
,
and that for every

with
|B_{0}|=p
there exists
B_{1}\subseteq B_{0}
with
|B_{1}|=q
such that for some

\vDash\bigwedge_{b\in B_{1}}\phi(a,b)
.

Then
\{\bigvee_{i<N}\phi(x_{i},b)\mid b\in B\}

is finitely satisfiable in
A
.

Proof.

By the definition of finite satisfiability, it suffices to see this in the case that

is finite; but this case is a direct consequence of the
(p,q)
-theorem (
5.4
).
∎

Suppose
\phi(x,y)
is a formula,
A\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{x}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
. For variables
\bar{x}=(x_{i}\mid i<N)
of the same sort of

, we denote by

the space of
\Delta
-types in
\bar{x}
over
\mathcal{U}^{y}
which are finitely satisfiable in

, where

then for any

, the product

is the partial type
q(\bar{x})\cup p(y)\cup\{\phi(x_{i},y)^{\epsilon_{i}}\mid i<N\}
where
\epsilon_{i}<2
is the truth value of
\phi(a_{i},b)
for some (any)

and

. Note that this is well-defined.

For

,

and

, we consider the following condition:

(\dagger)_{b,N,S}

\left\{\begin{minipage}[276.00105pt]\vskip 5.0ptFor every
$q(\bar{x})\in S^{\bar{x}}_{{\phi},\text{$A$-fs}}(\mathcal{U}^{y})$ where $\bar{x}=(x_{i}\mid i<N)$,
there is $p\in S$
such that
$$q(\bar{x})\otimes p(y)\vdash\bigwedge_{i<N}(\phi(x_{i},y)\leftrightarrow\phi(x_{i},b)).$$\vskip 5.0pt\end{minipage}\right.

Definition 5.6
.

Suppose
\phi(x,y)
is a formula and
A\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{x}
. A set

is
superdense
in

if

holds for every
b\in\mathcal{U}^{y}
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark 5.7
.

By considering realised types in
A
, it follows that any superdense set

is also dense.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
and let
d\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
(1-\alpha)n>2^{d+1}-1
, and let
N=N_{\operatorname{pq}}(n,2^{d+1})
. Fix some formula
\phi(x,y)
such that
\operatorname{vc}(\phi)\leq d
.
Suppose
A\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{x}
,
b\in\mathcal{U}^{y}
and
S\subseteq S_{{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}}(A)
satisfy
(\dagger)_{b,N,S}
.

Then
r:=\operatorname{tp}_{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}(b/A)
is the
\alpha
-rounded average of
n
elements of
S
.

Proof.

Since
\alpha\geq 1/2
,
r=\operatorname{rAvg}_{\alpha}(p_{i}\mid i<n)
iff for all
a\in A
,
|\{i<n\mid\phi(a,y)\in r\Leftrightarrow\phi(a,y)\in p_{i}\}|>\alpha n
. Let
\phi^{\prime}(x,y)=\phi(x,y)\leftrightarrow\phi(x,b)
(as a formula over
\mathcal{U}
). In this notation, we must prove that for some
p_{0},\ldots,p_{n-1}\in S
, for all
a\in A
,
|\{i<n\mid p_{i}(y)\vdash\phi^{\prime}(a,y)\}|>\alpha n
.

Note that
\operatorname{vc}(\phi^{\prime})=\operatorname{vc}(\phi)\leq d
; indeed,
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi
shatter the same subsets of
\mathcal{U}^{x}
.

Now assume the conclusion fails. In particular,
r\notin S
(else it is the rounded average of
n
copies of itself).
Let
B\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{y}
be a set of realisations of the types in
S
. By assumption, for every
B_{0}\subseteq B
with
|B_{0}|=n
, there exists
a\in A
and
B_{1}\subseteq B_{0}
with
|B_{1}|\geq(1-\alpha)n>2^{d+1}-1\geq\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\phi^{\prime})
such that

\neg\phi^{\prime}(a,c)
holds for all

(the last inequality follows from

2.7
).

Note that for

we have
\operatorname{tp}_{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}(b^{\prime}/A)\neq\operatorname{tp}_{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}(b/A)
since
\operatorname{tp}_{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}(b/A)\notin S
, so
\neg\phi^{\prime}(A,b^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
.

So

5.5
applies to
\neg\phi^{\prime}
(with

,
q=2^{d+1}
; note that
\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\phi^{\prime})=\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\neg\phi^{\prime})
) and hence

is finitely satisfiable in
A
, where
\bar{x}=(x_{i}\mid i<N)
.

Extend
\pi
to

(formally, first extend
\pi
to a global type finitely satisfiable in
A
, and then restrict to a global
\{\phi(x_{i},y)\mid i<N\}
-type

. Then note that

implies
\pi
).
Then for all

, we have

. However,
(\dagger)_{b,N,S}
implies that for some

,

, contradiction.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

When

,

and

.

Remark 5.10
.

From the proof of

, we get something slightly stronger (under the same assumptions): either

, or

is an

.

Remark 5.11
.

admits a partial converse, for an arbitrary formula
\phi(x,y)
and any

:
letting
\bar{x}=(x_{i}\mid i<N)
, if

is the rounded average of

,
then for every

and even in a uniform way, as in the proof of

by

. In the finite case we can already conclude the following, basically because superdensity is the same as density when

is finite.

.

Fix
\alpha\in[1/2,1)
and some

. Then there are

(depending only on

) such that if
\phi(x,y)
is a formula with

, then for any finite

-rounded average of

Let

be as in

, and let

As a corollary we retrieve UDTFS. First recall the definition.

(UDTFS)
.

We say that

has

such that for every finite set

with

the following holds: for every

there exist

such that

Every formula with UDTFS is easily NIP (see e.g., the proof of Theorem 14 in
[
7
]
). The proof of UDTFS for NIP formulas in
[
7
]
roughly goes by showing

5.12
with

, and deducing UDTFS from that (this is not stated explicitly in this language, see the proof of Theorem 14, (1) implies (2), (3) there). We omit the details here since we will prove uniformity of honest definitions in

The formula

We do point out that the proof here is, at least conceptually, simpler than the proof in
[

]
: both proofs use the finite version of

, but here the only other ingredient is the

-theorem, while there both the VC-theorem and von Neumann’s minimax theorem are used.

5.2.
A variant of Ramsey’s theorem for finite subsets

Here we will prove a variant of Ramsey’s theorem for finite subsets of a cardinal. This result generalises
Remark

3.3
for
(\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa),\subseteq)
in the same way that Ramsey’s theorem generalises the pigeonhole principle. It will be used in the proof of
Theorem

5.2
.

For a partial order
(X,\leq)
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
X^{n}_{<}=\{(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n-1})\in X^{n}\mid x_{0}<\ldots<x_{n-1}\}
be the set of ordered chains of size
n
(in short,
n
-chains
).

For
\kappa
a cardinal, let
\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)
be the set of finite subsets of
\kappa
, partially ordered by inclusion.

Say
f:\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)
is

strictly increasing
if

s\subsetneq t\Rightarrow f(s)\subsetneq f(t)
for all
s,t\in\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)
, and say
f
is

cofinal
if for all
s\in\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)
there is
t\in\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)
such that
f(t)\supseteq s
. Note that the image of an
n
-chain under a strictly increasing map is an
n
-chain.

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
\kappa
be an infinite cardinal,
0<n\in\mathbb{N}

and let
c:\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)^{n}_{<}\rightarrow r<\omega
be a finite colouring of the
n
-chains.
Then there is a
strictly increasing
cofinal map
f:\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)\rightarrow\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)
such that the image of all
n
-chains
f(\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)^{n}_{<})
is monochromatic, i.e.,
|(c\circ f)(\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)^{n}_{<})|=1
.

Proof.

Denote by
M
the structure
(\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa),\subseteq,(P_{k})_{k<r})
where
P_{k}=c^{-1}(k)\subseteq M^{n}_{<}
for
k<r
. Let
N\succ M
be an
|M|^{+}
-saturated extension. Let
\pi=\{x\supsetneq s\mid s\in M\}
. As
\pi
is finitely satisfiable in
M
, there is
q\in S_{\text{$M$-fs}}(N)
extending
\pi
. Let
(a_{n-1},\dots,a_{0})\vDash q^{(n)}|_{M}
and let
\bar{a}=(a_{0},\dots,a_{n-1})
. Note that
\bar{a}\in N^{n}_{<}
(because for all
a\vDash q|_{M}
and all
b\in M
,
b\subsetneq a
), and hence for some
k<r
,
\bar{a}\in P^{N}_{k}
. We claim that this colour
k
works.

For
m<\omega
, let
S_{m}=\{s\in\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)\mid|s|=m\}
. By recursion on
m<\omega
we define
f\restriction S_{m}
such that for any
s\in S_{m}
:

(i)

for any
1\leq i\leq n
and
i
-chain
s_{0}\subsetneq\ldots\subsetneq s_{i-1}=s
,
(f(s_{0}),\dots,f(s_{i-1}),a_{i},\dots,a_{n-1})\in P_{k}^{N}
.

(ii)

f(s)\supseteq s
and
f(s)\supsetneq f(t)
for all
t\subsetneq s
.

The construction is possible because
q
is finitely satisfiable in
M
and since there only finitely many conditions to fulfill for each
s\in\mathcal{P}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\kappa)
(and because of the choice of
\bar{a}
):
given
s\in S_{m}
and a chain as in (i), by induction we have
q(x)\vdash P_{k}(f({s_{0}}),\dots,f(s_{i-2}),x,a_{i},\dots,a_{n-1})
; since there are only finitely many such chains to consider and also
q(x)\vdash x\supsetneq f(t)\cup s
for any
t\subsetneq s
, we can find
f(s)
by finite satisfiability.

Now the
i=n
case of (i) implies that the image under
f
of any
n
-chain has colour
k
. Meanwhile (ii) implies that
f
is cofinal, and that
f
is strictly increasing.
∎

5.3.
The proofs of superdensity and of
Theorem

5.2

In this section we will prove
Theorem

5.2
, by proving superdensity of compressible types in a uniform way.

Proposition 5.16

(Superdensity of
\star
-compressible types)
.

Define
k_{\operatorname{sd}}:\mathbb{N}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
by
k_{\operatorname{sd}}(1,d):=k_{\operatorname{comp}}(d)+2d+2
,
and
k_{\operatorname{sd}}(n,d):=n\cdot k_{\operatorname{sd}}(1,2^{B_{\operatorname{vc}}(n,2^{d+1})+1})
for
n\neq 1
(see
Definition

2.8
).

Let
\phi(x,y)
be a formula,
d\in\mathbb{N}
, and assume that
\operatorname{vc}(\phi)\leq d
.
Let
A\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{x}
.
Let
\pi(y)
be a (small) partial type.
Let
b\in\pi(\mathcal{U})
.
For
0<n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
S=S_{{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}\downarrow k_{\operatorname{sd}}(n,d)}^{\pi}(A)
.

Then
(\dagger)_{b,n,S}
holds.

Proof.

Let
\bar{x}=(x_{i}\mid i<n)
and let
q(\bar{x})\in S^{\bar{x}}_{{\phi},\text{$A$-fs}}(\mathcal{U}^{y})
.

We first reduce to the case

(*)

n=1\text{ and }q\vdash\phi(x,b).

For
i<n
, let
\epsilon_{i}<2
be such that
q\vdash\phi(x_{i},b)^{\epsilon_{i}}
.
Let
\phi^{\prime}(\overline{x},y)=\bigwedge_{i<n}\phi(x_{i},y)^{\epsilon_{i}}
,
and consider the global
\phi^{\prime}
-type
q^{\prime}(\overline{x})\in S^{\overline{x}}_{\phi^{\prime}}(\mathcal{U}^{y})
implied by
q
,
which is finitely satisfiable in
A
.

Now
\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\neg\phi)=\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\phi)
, and so by
Lemma

2.10
,
and
Remark

2.7
, we have

\operatorname{vc}(\phi^{\prime})<2^{\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\phi^{\prime})+1}\leq 2^{B_{\operatorname{vc}}(n,\operatorname{vc}^{*}(\phi))+1}<2^{B_{\operatorname{vc}}(n,2^{\operatorname{vc}(\phi)+1})+1}\leq 2^{B_{\operatorname{vc}}(n,2^{d+1})+1}=:d^{\prime}
. (Note that
B_{\operatorname{vc}}
is increasing in the second variable.)
Note that
q^{\prime}\vdash\phi^{\prime}(\overline{x},b)
.

Let
k=k_{\operatorname{sd}}(1,d^{\prime})
.
Assuming the proposition in the
Eq.

*
case,
we obtain

p^{\prime}\in S_{(\phi^{\prime})^{\operatorname{opp}}\downarrow k}^{\pi}(A^{n})
such that

q^{\prime}(\overline{x})\otimes p^{\prime}(y)\vdash\phi^{\prime}(\overline{x},y)
.

Claim
.

p^{\prime}
implies a complete type
p\in S_{{\phi^{\operatorname{opp}}}\downarrow k_{\operatorname{sd}}(n,d)}^{\pi}(A)
(which implies
p^{\prime}
).

Proof.

We first show that if
a\in A
then
p^{\prime}(y)\vdash\phi(a,y)^{\epsilon}
for some
\epsilon
.

Since
q^{\prime}(\overline{x})\otimes p^{\prime}(y)\vdash\phi^{\prime}(\overline{x},y)
and since
q^{\prime}
is finitely satisfiable in
A
, there is some
(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1})\in A^{n}

Compressibility as an isolation notion

In this section we study properties of compressibility seen as an isolation notion (mostly) under NIP, and in particular as a way to construct models analogous to constructible models in totally transcendental theories. Towards that we prove a transitivity result for compressibility in

6.1.
Monotonicity

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose

are finite tuples. If
\operatorname{tp}(cb/A)
is compressible then so are
\operatorname{tp}(c/Ab)
and
\operatorname{tp}(b/A)
.

Proof.

We start by showing that
\operatorname{tp}(c/Ab)
is compressible. Suppose

is a tuple of variables such that

. Given a formula
\phi(x,y)
, let
\Phi
be the set of all formulas of the form
\psi(x,z,y)
we get from substituting variables from

by variables from

in
\phi
.
Fix some formula
\psi(x,z,y)\in\Phi

\zeta_{\psi}(xz,w)
that compresses
\operatorname{tp}_{\psi}(cb/A)
(with the partition
\psi(xz,y)
). This means that for any

there is some

such that
\operatorname{tp}(cb/A)\vdash\zeta_{\psi}(xz,a_{\psi,A_{0}})\vdash\operatorname{tp}_{\psi}(cb/A_{0})

Then we have that
\operatorname{tp}(c/Ab)\vdash\bigwedge_{\psi\in\Phi}\zeta_{\psi}(x,ba_{\psi,A_{0}})\vdash\operatorname{tp}_{\phi}(c/A_{0}b)
, and this shows that
\operatorname{tp}(c/Ab)
is compressible.

Now we show that
\operatorname{tp}(b/A)
is compressible. Fix some formula
\phi(z,y)
. Suppose
\zeta(xz,w)
compresses
\operatorname{tp}_{\phi}(cb/A)
. Fix some
A_{0}\subseteq_{\operatorname{fin}}A
and suppose

is such that
\operatorname{tp}(cb/A)\vdash\zeta(xy,a)\vdash\operatorname{tp}_{\phi}(cb/A_{0})

Note that if

is such that
\vDash\forall x,z(\zeta(x,z,a)\rightarrow\phi(z,a_{0})^{\epsilon})

,

\vDash\forall z(\exists x\zeta(x,z,a)\rightarrow\phi(z,a_{0})^{\epsilon})

\operatorname{tp}(b/A)\vdash\exists x\zeta(x,z,a)\vdash\operatorname{tp}_{\phi}(b/A_{0})
.
∎

Proposition 6.18
.

Let
(X,<)
be any (small) infinite linearly ordered set. A type

is compressible if and only if
for every

if

,
then

Moreover, if

has no first element then

.

Proof.

That this condition implies compressibility follows from

(3

can be taken from

(where

is saturated enough).

For the converse, assume compressibility and let

be as in the proposition. Let

and

Let

Applying

(ii),(iv)) to

Hence

Let

Suppose

,

By

Example 6.26
.

Proposition

6.25
is false without NIP. For example, let
T
be the theory of the countable atomless Boolean algebra. Let
A
be a countable set of pairwise disjoint elements
\{a_{i}\mid i<\omega\}
. For
i<\omega
, let
b_{i}=\bigcup a_{<i}
, and let
B=A\cup\{b_{i}\mid i<\omega\}
. Let

contain all the elements from
A
. Let
C=Bc
. Then
C
is compressible over
B
,
B
is compressible over
A
, but
C
is not compressible over
A
.

Indeed, to show the first statement, it is enough to see
that
p:=\operatorname{tp}(c/B)
is compressible by
Lemma

6.6
. This is true since
\zeta(x,z):=(z<x)\land(x\neq 1)
compresses it: for every finite
B_{0}\subseteq B
, let
i<\omega
be such that
\bigcup B_{0}\leq b_{i}
. Then
p\vdash(b_{i}<x)\land(x\neq 1)\vdash p|_{B_{0}}
by quantifier elimination.

Since every tuple from
B
is in the definable closure of
A
,
B
is compressible over
A
(every finite tuple is isolated).

Finally,
C
is not compressible over
A
, since compressibility is monotonic (
Lemma

6.1
) and

is not compressible over

. Why? Suppose

compresses

. Let

, and assume

is such that

holds and
\psi(x,d)\vdash b_{|z|+2}\leq x
. Let

be such that

. Then
(\bigcup d<x)\land(x\neq 1)\vdash\psi(x,d)
(since this implies

), so

but

holds, contradiction.

Note that this example shows that in

, weak compressibility is different from compressibility (see
3.23
for the definition). Namely,
\operatorname{tp}(c/A)
is weakly compressible (as witnessed by

) but not compressible.

6.4.
Compressible models and applications

In this section,
T^{\prime}
is a countable NIP theory with monster model
\mathcal{U}
in the language
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
,
F\subseteq\mathcal{U}
is some small subset, and
T=\operatorname{Th}(\mathcal{U}_{F})
in the language
\mathcal{L}:=\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(F)
. In other words,
T
is a complete theory we get by naming constants in a countable NIP theory (whose monster model is still denoted by
\mathcal{U}
, abusing notation).

Definition 6.28
.

Say
B
is
compressibly constructible over
A
if
B
can be enumerated as
B=(b_{i})_{i<\alpha}
for some ordinal
\alpha
, such that
\operatorname{tp}(b_{i}/Ab_{<i})
is compressible for all
i<\alpha
.

As with other isolation notions, the existence of compressibly constructible models follows straightforwardly from density. In fact this is an instance of the abstract result
[
18
, Theorem IV.3.1(5)]
, but we give the proof.

Proposition 6.29
.

For any set
A
, there exists a model
M\supseteq A
which is compressibly constructible over
A
and of cardinality
\leq|A|+|T|
.

Moreover, if
B
is compressibly constructible over
A
then there is some model
M\supseteq B
which is compressibly constructible over
A
and of cardinality
\leq|B|+|T|
.

Proof.

Since
A
is compressibly constructible over
A
, it is enough to prove the “moreover” part.

Let
\lambda=|B|+|T|
.
Construct an increasing chain
(B_{i})_{i<\omega}
as follows. Let
B_{0}=B
, and given
B_{i}
, let
(\theta^{i}_{j})_{j<\lambda}
enumerate all consistent formulas over
B_{i}
. Construct a sequence
(b^{i}_{j})_{j<\lambda}
inductively by letting
b^{i}_{j}\vDash\theta^{i}_{j}
be such that
\operatorname{tp}(b^{i}_{j}/B_{i}b^{i}_{<j})
is compressible, using
Corollary

6.24
(with
B=F
). Let
B_{i+1}=B_{i}\cup\{b^{i}_{j}\mid j<\lambda\}
. Finally,
M:=\bigcup_{i<\omega}B_{i}
is as required, by Tarski-Vaught.
∎

Thanks to
Proposition

6.25
, we also have the following instance of
[
18
, Theorem IV.3.2(1)]
.

Proposition 6.30
.

If
B
is compressibly constructible over

, then
B
is compressible over

.

Proof.

Suppose

is an enumeration witnessing that

is compressibly constructible. Prove by induction on

that

is compressible over

. For the successor steps use

, and for the limit steps use

So compressible models exist over arbitrary sets. We give some applications.

6.4.1.
Realising models of a stable part

Suppose

is some small set. Recall that
the induced structure on

is the structure

whose universe is

with the language consisting of a relation

for each
\mathcal{L}
-formula

, where

.

Lemma 6.31
.

Suppose

is such that
\operatorname{Th}(A_{\operatorname{ind}})
is stable (or just has stable quantifier-free formulas). Then there exists an l-atomic model

over

: for every finite tuple

from

,
\operatorname{tp}(b/A)
is l-isolated.

Proof.

By

4.8
(i.a), a compressible model over

is l-atomic over

, so this follows directly from

and

.
∎

The next corollary deduces that the reduct map to a stable sort is surjective, and moreover elementary embeddings in the reduct theory can be lifted. Thanks to Anand Pillay and Martin Hils for suggesting that we consider this problem.

Remark 6.32
.

Suppose
X
is a
\emptyset
-definable set,
and let
T_{X}:=\operatorname{Th}(X(\mathcal{U})_{\operatorname{ind}})
.
If
T_{X}
is stable then

is
(uniformly) stably embedded by
[

, Proposition 3.19]
. It
follows that stability of

is equivalent to assuming that

is stable
as a partial type in the sense of

.

Corollary 6.33
.

Suppose
X
is a
\emptyset
-definable subset of a sort of
\mathcal{U}
, and let
T_{X}:=\operatorname{Th}(X(\mathcal{U})_{\operatorname{ind}})
.
Suppose
T_{X}
is stable.
Let
N\vDash T_{X}
.
Then there exists
M\vDash T
such that
N=X(M)_{\operatorname{ind}}
.

Moreover, if
N_{1}\prec N_{2}\vDash T_{X}
, and
M_{1}\vDash T
is such that
X(M_{1})=N_{1}
, then there is
M_{2}\succ M_{1}
such that
X(M_{2})=N_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
X(\mathcal{U})
is a saturated model of
T_{X}
, we may assume that
N\prec X(\mathcal{U})
and hence
N=A_{\operatorname{ind}}
where
A\subseteq X(\mathcal{U})
is the universe of

. By
Lemma

6.31
, there exists
M\prec\mathcal{U}
which is l-atomic over

. In particular,

is l-atomic over

, and so

by

.

Now for the “moreover” part. Work in the language
\mathcal{L}(M_{1})
(adding constants for
M_{1}
). Then
N_{1}
can be enriched to a model
N_{1}^{\prime}\prec X(\mathcal{U}_{M_{1}})_{\operatorname{ind}}
of
T_{X}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{Th}(X(\mathcal{U}_{M_{1}})_{\operatorname{ind}})
. By
Remark

6.32
,
X(\mathcal{U})
is stably embedded, and since

this expansion of

is conservative: all new relations are already definable with parameters from

. Using these definitions, we can enrich

to

in such a way that

, and in particular,

.

Now,

is still stable, so we may apply the first part (recall that we allow naming any number of constants in

) and get some

such that

. Letting

be the reduct to

(forgetting the constants for

), we get that

and

as required (this is a bit subtle, since what we really get is that there is an elementary embedding from

to

, but then we can change

∎

Remark 6.34
.

The assumption that
X
is a subset of a sort of

is needed to make sense of the statement (otherwise, if

is e.g., a set of pairs and

under the canonical bijection.

Example 6.35
.

Let
M=(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{Q},<),\mathbb{Q},<)
in the language
\mathcal{L}
which includes a predicate

for

, a predicate

for the group of automorphisms

is just the order, because given two increasing tuples

(there is some automorphism

6.4.2.
Extensions preserving a stable part

Corollary 6.37
.

Suppose
A
is a set such that if
M
is a model containing
A
, then
M
contains an infinite chain in some
M
-definable preorder.

Then there are arbitrarily large models which are compressible over
A
.

Proof.

There exists a compressible model over
A
by
Propositions

6.30
and
6.29
. It is enough to show that any such model
M
can be strictly extended to a compressible model over
A
. By assumption
M
contains an infinite chain in some definable preorder. By
Remark

4.9
, there is some
c
such that
\operatorname{tp}(c/M)
is compressible but not l-isolated, so
c\notin M
. Apply (the “moreover” part of)
Proposition

6.29
to get some compressibly constructible model
N
over
M
containing
Mc
. Then
N
is compressible over
M
by
Proposition

6.30
. By transitivity (
Proposition

6.25
),
N
is compressible over
A
.
∎

Example 6.38
.

The following example shows that
Corollary

6.37
requires the assumption that every model containing
A
contains an infinite chain; mere instability does not suffice. Let
\mathcal{L}=\{<\}
where
<
is a binary relation symbol. Let
T
say that
<
is a partial order, that comparability is an equivalence relation having exactly one class of size
n
for each
0<n<\omega
, and that each class has maximal and minimal elements and is discretely ordered (every non-maximal element has a successor and every non-minimal element has a predecessor). Let
M
be the
\mathcal{L}
-structure with universe
\{(i,j)\mid i\in\mathbb{N},j\leq i\}
and with
<^{M}=\{(i,j_{1}),(i,j_{2})\in M^{2}\mid i\in\mathbb{N},j_{1}<j_{2}\}
. Then
M\vDash T
, and in fact every model of
T
is a disjoint union of (a copy of)
M
and infinite chains, each discretely ordered with a minimal and maximal element. Hence any two saturated models of
T
are isomorphic and thus
T
is complete. Moreover,
T
has quantifier elimination after adding the predecessor and successor functions by a back-and-forth argument. It follows that
T
is NIP (by e.g. showing that
x<y
is NIP as there is a polynomial bound on every
(x<y)
-type over a finite subset of
M
).

Suppose
c\in\mathcal{U}
. Then
\operatorname{tp}(c/M)
is generically stable (see
Definition

4.16
). Indeed, suppose
c\notin M
and let
p
be a global coheir extending
\operatorname{tp}(c/M)
. Then any Morley sequence
I:=(a_{i})_{i<\omega}
of
p
over
M
must be such that
a_{i},a_{j}
are incomparable for
i\neq j
. Thus
I
is an indiscernible set by quantifier elimination, and hence
p
is generically stable by NIP and the “moreover” part of
4.13
.

Thus, if
\operatorname{tp}(c/M)
is compressible then it is l-isolated by
Proposition

4.14
and hence realised by
Remark

4.7
. Hence
M
is the only model compressible over
M
and the conclusion of
Corollary

6.37
does not hold.

Corollary 6.39
.

Let
M\vDash T
, and suppose that
<
is an
M
-definable preorder on an
M
-definable set
D
and suppose that there is an infinite

Then:

(i)

There exists

such that if

. (See

(ii)

For any cardinal

with

such that if

and
\operatorname{tp}(a/M)

.

In particular, there are arbitrarily large elementary extensions

of

such that if

M
-definable and stable (see

4.4
), then

(ii) follows from (i) by taking the union of a suitably long elementary chain. We prove (i).

By
Remark

4.9
there is some

such that

. Apply (the “moreover” part of)

6.29
to get some compressibly constructible model
N\succ M
over

containing

4.14
, if

The “in particular” part follows since for any

6.4.3.
Compressible types in ACVF

Example 6.41
.

If
\mathcal{U}\vDash\operatorname{ACVF}
and
A\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{\operatorname{eq}}
,
then any model
M
containing
A
whose residue field is algebraic over
A
is compressible over
A
; i.e.,
if
M\prec\mathcal{U}
,
A\subseteq M^{\operatorname{eq}}
, and
k(M)=\operatorname{acl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}(A)\cap k
,
where
k=k(\mathcal{U})
denotes the residue field,
then
M
is compressible over
A
.

To see this, consider first a 1-type
\operatorname{tp}(a/B)
over an
\operatorname{acl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}
-closed set
B=\operatorname{acl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}(B)\subseteq\mathcal{U}^{\operatorname{eq}}
, where
a\in\mathcal{U}
. We show that
\operatorname{tp}(a/B)
is compressible iff
\operatorname{dcl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}(Ba)\cap k=B\cap k
.
By unique Swiss cheese decompositions
[
12
, Theorem 3.26]
,
\operatorname{tp}(a/B)
is determined by knowing which of the balls defined over
B
contain
a
, i.e.,
\operatorname{tp}(a/B)
is implied by
\operatorname{tp}_{\in}(a/B)
where
x\in y
is the element relation between the valued field and the sort of balls (closed and open).
One sees directly that this
\in
-type is compressible unless
\operatorname{tp}(a/B)
is the generic type of a closed ball
\alpha
over
B
which contains infinitely many open balls over
B
of radius
\gamma:=\operatorname{radius}(\alpha)
.
In that case, let
\beta_{1}\neq\beta_{2}
be distinct open subballs of
\alpha
over
B
of radius
\gamma
(the existence of these two balls is our only use of our assumption that there are infinitely many such), and consider the map
\beta\mapsto\operatorname{res}(\frac{\beta-\beta_{1}}{\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}})
for
\beta
an open subball of
\alpha
of radius
\gamma
. This is a well-defined injective map to
k
defined over
B
, so genericity of
\operatorname{tp}(a/B)
(and the fact that
B=\operatorname{acl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}(B)
) implies that the image under this map of the open ball around
a
of radius
\gamma
is not in
B\cap k
, so we have
\operatorname{dcl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}(Ba)\cap k\supsetneq B\cap k
.
Conversely, if
\operatorname{tp}(a/B)
is compressible, then since the residue field is a pure stably embedded algebraically closed field, we must have
\operatorname{dcl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}(Ba)\cap k=B\cap k
.

The compressibility of
M
over
A
if
k(M)=\operatorname{acl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}(A)\cap k
now follows by first taking a compressible construction sequence which alternates taking
\operatorname{acl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}
-closure and adding a single new element of
M
, and then applying
Proposition

6.30
.

If
A\cap k
is infinite then conversely
k(M)=\operatorname{acl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}(A)\cap k
is necessary for compressibility of
M
over
A
, but this fails in general;
for example, if
A
is finite, then any model containing
A
is of course compressible over
A
.

Remark 6.42
.

The argument in
Example

6.41
is based on ideas from
[
2
]
, and combining it with
[
2
, Remark 3.12]
actually yields an alternative proof of
[
2
, Theorem 5.6]
. Indeed, suppose
K
is a valued subfield of
\mathcal{U}
with
\operatorname{res}(K)
finite. Taking an elementary extension we may assume that
(\mathcal{U},K)
is sufficiently saturated. Let

. Then
k(K^{\operatorname{alg}})=\operatorname{acl}^{{\operatorname{eq}}}(A)\cap k
is the algebraic closure of the prime field, so by
Example

6.41
,
K^{\operatorname{alg}}
, and in particular
K
, is compressible over
A
. By
[
2
, Remark 3.12]
, it follows that
K
is distal in
\mathcal{U}
in the sense defined in that paper. The same argument applies to

defined in
[
2
, Theorem 5.6]
. However, this proof does not yield bounds on the exponents of the resulting distal cell decompositions, whereas such bounds are obtained in
[
2
, Remark 6.2]
as a consequence of the more elementary methods of that paper.

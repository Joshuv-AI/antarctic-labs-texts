Abstract
. We prove a “twist-compatibility” result for p p -adic families of cohomology classes associated to symmetric spaces. This shows that a single family of classes (lying in a finitely-generated Iwasawa module) interpolates classical cohomology classes of many different weights, including twists by Grössencharacters of possibly non-trivial infinity-type. This subsumes and generalises a number of prior results relating to Euler systems and p p -adic L L -functions, and we conclude with some novel applications to Euler systems for GSp 4 \GSp_{4} , GSp 4 × GL 2 \GSp_{4}\times\GL_{2} , and GSp 4 × GL 2 × GL 2 \GSp_{4}\times\GL_{2}\times\GL_{2} .

1.
Introduction

This article is a sequel to the article
[
Loe21b
]
of the first author. In the previous article, we constructed norm-compatible families of cohomology classes for Shimura varieties and other symmetric spaces, associated to pairs of reductive groups satisfying an “open orbit” condition. This very general construction subsumes a number of well-known results in Iwasawa theory, including the norm-compatibility of modular symbols and Heegner points for
\GL_{2}
, as well as more recent constructions such as the Euler system for
\GSp_{4}
constructed in
[
LSZ22
]
.

In this paper, we consider varying the algebraic weight defining the coefficient system for our cohomology. Our main result,
theorem

5.2.3
, shows that the norm-compatible families constructed in
[
Loe21b
]
interpolate classical cohomology classes of many different weights (including twists by Grössencharacters of possibly non-trivial infinity-type). Several specific instances of this result are already known – see
remark

5.2.6
below – and form an important ingredient in the proofs of “explicit reciprocity laws” for Euler systems and
p
-adic
L
-functions. The general theorem proved here subsumes all of these earlier results, and we conclude with several new applications of this theory to Euler systems for
\GSp_{4}
,
\GSp_{4}\times\GL_{2}
, and
\GSp_{4}\times\GL_{2}\times\GL_{2}
.

2.
Control theorems for ordinary cohomology

The aim of this section is to prove a general control result (
theorem

2.6.2
) for “
Q
-ordinary” cohomology groups associated to a general parabolic subgroup
Q
. Our formulation is heavily influenced by the work of Urban
[
Urb05
]
in the setting of Coleman theory; however, unlike Urban we obtain integral results, without inverting
p

Q
-ordinarity rather than just
Q
-finite-slope. These results are adapted from the second author’s 2022 University of Warwick PhD thesis.

2.1.
Setup

Let
p
be a prime. We consider the following situation:

•

\mathcal{G}
is a connected reductive group over
\mathbf{Q}
.

•

We assume there exists an unramified
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-model
G
of
\mathcal{G}
, i.e. a reductive group scheme
G
over
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
together with an isomorphism
\mathcal{G}\times_{\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}\cong G\times_{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}\mathbf{Q}_{p}

•

is a parabolic subgroup of
G
, and
\bar{Q}
a choice of opposite parabolic; thus

is a Levi subgroup of
Q

\overline{N}\times L\times N
is an open subscheme of
G
over
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
, where

is the unipotent radical of

(and similarly

). Note that

\mathbf{Q}

•

S
denotes the torus
L/L^{\mathrm{der}}
, and
X^{\bullet}(S)
its character lattice.
We let
X_{+}^{\bullet}(S)
be the cone of dominant weights (relative to
Q
).

•

A
is the maximal
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-split subtorus in the centre of
L
.

We choose a torus
S^{0}\subset S
, and let
L^{0}
and
Q^{0}
be its preimages in

and

respectively (so

). We write

for the profinite abelian group

\mathbf{Z}_{p}

is

We shall also make the following global assumption (Milne’s “Axiom (SV5)”):

Assumption 2.1.1
.

The torus
\mathcal{Z}=Z(\mathcal{G})^{\circ}
(the identity compononent of the centre of

) is isogenous to the product of a
\mathbf{Q}
-split torus and an

.

The assumption (SV5) can be weakened, but at the cost of more complicated statements: very roughly, if

]

2.2.
Algebraic representations

Let us fix a maximal torus
T\subset L
, and a finite extension
K/\mathbf{Q}_{p}
, with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}
, such that
T
splits over
K
. We can and do assume that
K
is unramified (since
G
has a smooth integral model).

For
\lambda\in X^{\bullet}_{+}(S)
, there is a unique isomorphism class of irreducible representations
(\rho_{\lambda},V_{\lambda})
of
G_{/K}
of highest weight
\lambda
(with respect to any choice of Borel contained in
Q
). A representative of this isomorphism class can be constructed using the Borel–Weil theorem, as the space of all polynomials

\{f\in K[G]:f(\overline{n}\ell g)=\lambda(\ell)f(g)\quad\forall\overline{n}\in\overline{N},\ell\in L,g\in G\},

with
G
acting by right-translation. In particular, the highest weight space of
V_{\lambda}
has a unique basis vector
f_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{hw}}
whose restriction to the big Bruhat cell
\overline{N}LN
is given by
\overline{n}\ell n\mapsto\lambda(\ell)
. This is also the highest
relative
weight space of
V_{\lambda}
, i.e. the highest-weight eigenspace for the action of
A
.

2.3.
Integral lattices

Let
\lambda\in X^{\bullet}_{+}(S)
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

An
admissible lattice
in
V_{\lambda}
is an
\mathcal{O}
-lattice
\mathcal{L}\subset V_{\lambda}
which is invariant under
G_{/\mathcal{O}}
(i.e.
G(R)
preserves
\mathcal{L}\otimes R
for every
\mathcal{O}
-algebra
R
), and whose intersection with the highest-weight subspace is
\mathcal{O}\cdot f_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{hw}}
.

We recall the following standard facts, which can be found in
[
Lin92
]
for example:

(a)

There are only finitely many admissible lattices in
V_{\lambda}
.

(b)

Every admissible lattice is the direct sum of its intersections with the weight spaces of
V^{G}_{\lambda}
.

(c)

There are unique maximal and minimal admissible lattices
\mathcal{L}^{\max}
and
\mathcal{L}^{\min}
such that every admissible
\mathcal{L}
satisfies
\mathcal{L}^{\max}\supseteq\mathcal{L}\supseteq\mathcal{L}^{\min}
.

(d)

If we realise
V_{\lambda}
via the Borel–Weil construction as above, then the maximal admissible lattice
\mathcal{L}^{\max}
is given by the intersection of
V_{\lambda}
with the coordinate ring of
G_{/\mathcal{O}}
.

(e)

The dual of an admissible lattice in
V_{\lambda}
is an admissible lattice in
V_{\lambda}^{\vee}
. In particular, the dual of the maximal lattice is the minimal lattice and vice versa.

We shall henceforth always write
V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}
for the
maximal
admissible lattice, realised via the Borel–Weil construction as above, and
V^{\min}_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}
for the minimal lattice.

Remark 2.3.2
.

Note that if
G=\GL_{2}
and
V_{\lambda}=\Sym^{n}(K^{2})
is the
n
-th symmetric power of the standard representation, then
\Sym^{n}(\mathcal{O}^{2})
is the maximal lattice, and the module
\operatorname{TSym}^{n}(\mathcal{O}^{2})
of symmetric tensors (which features prominently in
[
KLZ17
]
) is the minimal lattice.

Definition 2.3.3
.

Let
\Sigma^{+}
denote the monoid of cocharacters
\eta\in X_{\bullet}(A)
which are dominant with respect to
Q
, so that
\langle\eta,\phi\rangle\geqslant 0
for every relative root
\phi
. We let
\Sigma^{++}\subset\Sigma^{+}
be the ideal of elements that are strictly dominant, so
\langle\eta,\phi\rangle>0
for all
\phi
.

Lemma 2.3.4
.

If
\eta\in\Sigma^{+}
, then the endomorphism
p^{\langle\eta,\lambda\rangle}\rho_{\lambda}\left(\eta(p)^{-1}\right)
of
V_{\lambda}
acts on every weight space as a non-negative power of
p
, and hence restricts to an endomorphism of
\mathcal{L}
for any admissible lattice
\mathcal{L}
. If
\eta\in\Sigma^{++}
then this endomorphism acts as a strictly positive power of
p
on every weight space except the highest one.

Proof.

This is an easy explicit check.
∎

2.4.
Cohomology of symmetric spaces

Let us fix a prime-to-
p
level group
K^{p}
, which we shall suppose to be
neat
.
1
1

1

See
[
Pin90
, §0.1]
for a definition of this condition (which implies that the intersection of
K^{p}
with any conjugate of
G(\mathbf{Q})
is torsion-free) and a proof that any open compact subgroup contains a neat subgroup of finite index.
We define
H^{*}(U,\mathcal{O})
, for
U\subset G(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
open compact, to mean
one
of the following:

•

Betti cohomology of the locally symmetric space
Y_{G}(K^{p}U)
, viewed as a real manifold, with coefficients in
\mathcal{O}
.

•

Supposing
\mathcal{G}
to be equipped with a choice of Shimura datum: the étale cohomology (with
\mathcal{O}
coefficients) of the canonical model of the Shimura variety of level
K^{p}U
, base-extended from the reflex field
E
to
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
(so the resulting module has a
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/E)
-action).

•

Again in the Shimura-variety setting, and supposing that the Shimura datum is of abelian type, and
\mathcal{Z}
splits over a CM field: the étale cohomology of the canonical integral model of the Shimura variety over
\mathcal{O}_{E}[1/S]
, where
E
is the reflex field, and
S
is a sufficiently large finite set of primes. In this case, we may also allow arbitrary cyclotomic twists of the coefficients.

One can also consider cohomology with compact support (in any of the above settings). More generally, since we may regard an algebraic representation of
G
as a coefficient sheaf (for any of the above cohomology theories), we can make sense of
H^{*}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}})
for any
\lambda
, as long as we assume
U\subseteq G(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
.

Remark 2.4.1
.

For the existence of canonical integral models of abelian-type Shimura varieties, see
[
Lov17
, Theorem 2.2.1]
; it suffices to take
S
to contain
p
and all places where
K^{p}
is not hyperspecial. (In particular,
S
can be chosen independently of
U
.)

Note that in all three cases the groups
H^{*}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}})
are finitely-generated over
\mathcal{O}
. For Betti cohomology, and for étale cohomology over
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
, this is well-known. The case of étale cohomology of
\mathcal{O}_{E}[1/S]
-models is slightly more delicate. We can express the étale cohomology of the integral model as the limit of a Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence whose
E_{2}
page is
H^{i}(E^{S}/E,H^{j}_{\text{{\'{e}t}}}(Y_{G}(K^{p}U)_{\overline{\mathbf{Q}}},\dots))
, where
E^{S}
is the maximal algebraic extension of
E
unramified outside
S
. As the functors
H^{i}(E^{S}/E,-)
map finitely-generated
\mathcal{O}
-modules to finitely-generated
\mathcal{O}
-modules, and vanish for
i\gg 0
, the result follows.

Remark 2.4.2
.

This would not work if we worked with the canonical model over
E
, rather than over
\mathcal{O}_{E}[1/S]
, since the Galois cohomology functors
H^{i}(E,-)
without ramification restrictions do not send finitely-generated modules to finitely-generated modules.

2.5.
Hecke actions

For general
\lambda
, the functors
H^{*}(-,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}})
are
Cartesian cohomology functors
for
G(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
, in the sense of
[
Loe21b
, Definition 2.1.3]
; that is, given open compacts
U,V\subseteq G(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
and
g\in G(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
such that
g^{-1}Ug\subseteq V
, we have pullback maps
[g]^{*}:H^{*}(V,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}})\to H^{*}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}})
, and pushforward maps
[g]_{*}
in the opposite direction, satisfying an appropriate compatibility relation involving double cosets.

However, if the coefficients are non-trivial, they are typically not cohomology functors for the whole of
G(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
since this group does not act on the lattice
V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}
. We shall work around this by renormalising our Hecke operators using
lemma

2.3.4
.

Definition 2.5.1
.

Suppose
U\subseteq G(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
. Then, for any
\lambda\in X^{\bullet}_{+}(S)
and
\eta\in\Sigma^{+}
, we can define a normalised Hecke operator acting on
H^{*}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}})
by

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\eta}=p^{h}[U\eta(p^{-1})U],\qquad h={\langle\lambda,\eta\rangle}.

Remark 2.5.2
.

Recall
\eta
is a cocharacter
\mathbf{G}_{m}\to A
, so
\eta(p^{-1})\in A(\mathbf{Q}_{p})\subseteq G(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
. In the special cases of this theory we have previously worked out, the scalar factor
p^{h}
appearing above is hidden from view, since we had always chosen
\eta
and
\lambda
such that
\langle\eta,\lambda\rangle=0
.

Definition 2.5.3
.

We say an open compact subgroup
U\subset G(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
has an
Iwahori decomposition
(with respect to
Q
) if we have
U=\bar{N}_{U}L_{U}N_{U}
, where
N_{U}=U\cap N(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
etc, and
N_{U}
and
\bar{N}_{U}
are the products of their intersections with the relative root spaces for
A
.

The prototypical examples are the parahoric subgroups
J=\{g\in G(\mathbf{Z}_{p}):g\bmod p\in Q(\mathbf{F}_{p})\}
and its opposite
\bar{J}
.

Proposition 2.5.4
.

If
U
has an Iwahori decomposition, then the operators
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\eta}
for varying
\eta
define an action of
\Sigma^{+}
on
H^{*}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}})
; and if we choose any
\eta_{0}\in\Sigma^{++}
, the
anti-ordinary idempotent

\mathop{e_{Q}^{\prime}}=\lim_{k\to\infty}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\eta_{0}})^{k}

is independent of the choice of
\eta_{0}
, and cuts out the maximal direct summand on which the operators
\mathcal{T}_{\eta}^{\prime}
are all invertible.

Remark 2.5.5
.

We will show in
remark

2.7.6
below that
\mathop{e_{Q}^{\prime}}H^{*}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}})
in fact depends only on
U\cap Q(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
.

We use the term “anti-ordinary” since our Hecke operators are defined using the image of
p^{-1}
under a dominant cocharacter, rather than
p
. In the familiar case when
G=\GL_{2}
and
Q
is the upper-triangular Borel, this means we do not work with the Hecke operator
U_{p}
which acts as
\sum a_{n}q^{n}\mapsto\sum a_{np}q^{n}
on
q
-expansions, but rather its transpose
U_{p}^{\prime}
.

Proposition 2.5.6
.

For any
U
with an Iwahori decomposition, the natural map

\mathop{e_{Q}^{\prime}}H^{i}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}^{\min})\to\mathop{e_{Q}^{\prime}}H^{i}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}),

induced by the inclusion
V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}^{\min}\hookrightarrow V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}
, is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Both
V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}^{\min}
and
V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}
are direct sums of their weight spaces for
A
. The inclusion map is an isomorphism on the highest relative weight space (by definition). On each lower weight space,
p^{\langle\lambda,\eta_{0}\rangle}\rho_{\lambda}(\eta_{0}(p)^{-1})
acts as a positive power of
p
, so some power of this map will annihilate the quotient of the weight spaces in the two lattices. Thus, if
r\gg 0
(depending on
\lambda
), the operator
(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\eta_{0}})^{r}
annihilates the cohomology of the quotient.
∎

2.6.
Iwasawa cohomology and control theorems

Definition 2.6.1
.

For
i\geqslant 0
, and
\lambda\in X^{\bullet}_{+}(S)
, we set

H^{i}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(Q^{0},V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}})=\varprojlim_{U}H^{i}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}),

where
U
varies over open compact subgroups of
G(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
containing
Q^{0}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
, and the inverse limit is taken relative to the pushforward maps.

One checks that the levels
U
having an Iwahori decomposition are cofinal in the above inverse system, and the operators
\{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\eta}:\eta\in\Sigma^{+}\}
are compatible with the pushforward maps and hence act on the inverse limit.

In this section and the next, we prove the following result, generalising results due to Ohta
[
Oht99
,
Oht00
]
for Betti or étale cohomology of modular curves to arbitrary reductive groups:

Theorem 2.6.2
.

(i)

The modules
\mathop{e_{Q}^{\prime}}H^{i}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(Q^{0},\mathcal{O})
are finitely-generated over
\mathcal{O}[[\mathfrak{S}]]
, and vanish for
i\gg 0
.

(ii)

Let
U
be any open compact subgroup of
G(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
such that
U\supseteq Q^{0}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
and
U
admits an Iwahori decomposition. For any
\lambda\in X^{\bullet}_{+}(S/S_{0})
, there is a convergent spectral sequence (supported in the second quadrant
i\leqslant 0,j\geqslant 0
):

E_{2}^{ij}=\operatorname{Tor}_{-i}^{\mathcal{O}[[\mathfrak{S}_{U}]]}\Big(\mathop{e_{Q}^{\prime}}H^{j}_{\mathrm{Iw}}(Q^{0},\mathcal{O}),\mathcal{O}[-\lambda]\Big)\Rightarrow\mathop{e_{Q}^{\prime}}H^{i+j}(U,V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}),

where
\mathfrak{S}_{U}
is the image of
U\cap Q(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
in
\mathfrak{S}
, and
\mathcal{O}[-\lambda]
denotes
\mathcal{O}
with
\mathfrak{S}_{U}
acting via the algebraic character
-\lambda
. This spectral sequence is compatible with the action of Hecke operators away from
p
, and with the operators
\{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\eta}:\eta\in\Sigma^{+}\}
.

We shall give the proof below assuming that
H^{*}(-)
denotes Betti cohomology. The remaining cases follow readily from this, as follows. One may define all the spaces and maps for a finite truncation of the coefficients, using general functoriality properties of étale cohomology. Then the remaining assertions – that the inverse limits are finitely-generated, and that the moment map in (ii) is an isomorphism on the ordinary part – can be checked using Artin’s comparison theorem to relate Betti cohomology and étale cohomology over
\overline{\mathbf{Q}}
, and the Hochschild–Serre spectral sequence in order to descend to the cohomology of the integral model over
\mathcal{O}_{E}[1/S]
.

Remark 2.6.3
.

In the above formulation, we have assumed that
\lambda
factors through the quotient
S/S_{0}
, and in particular is trivial on
L^{\mathrm{der}}
. More generally, we can fix any
\nu\in X^{\bullet}_{+}(T)
(not necessarily trivial on
L^{\mathrm{der}}
) and study the cohomology groups
\mathop{e_{Q}^{\prime}}\cdot\varprojlim_{U}H^{i}(U,V_{\nu,\mathcal{O}})
; one can show that these are finite-type
\mathcal{O}[[\mathfrak{S}]]
-modules which interpolate the cohomology groups
\mathop{e_{Q}^{\prime}}\cdot H^{i}(U,V_{\lambda+\nu,\mathcal{O}})
as
\lambda
varies over characters of
S/S_{0}
(so
\lambda+\nu
varies over a coset of
X^{\bullet}(S/S_{0})
in
X^{\bullet}(T)
). We shall stick to the case
\nu=0
here for ease of notation, but the proof in the general case is very similar.

2.7.
Proof of the control theorem

For brevity we set
R\coloneqq\mathcal{O}[[\mathfrak{S}]]
. Let us choose an element
\eta\in\Sigma^{++}
and set
\tau=\eta(p)
.

Groups

Let
Q^{0}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})\subset U\subset G(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
be an open compact subgroup admitting an Iwahori decomposition

U=\bar{N}_{U}\times L_{U}\times N_{0},

and for
r\geqslant 0
set

U^{(r)}=\tau^{-r}U\tau^{r}\cap U.

Note that we have
U^{(r)}=\bar{N}_{U}^{(r)}\times L_{U}\times N_{0}
for all
r
, where
\bar{N}_{U}^{(r)}=\tau^{-r}\bar{N}_{U}\tau^{r}
; in particular
\bigcap_{r\geqslant 0}U^{(r)}=U\cap Q(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
.

Betti cohomology complexes

For an open compact subgroup
K=K^{p}U\subset G(\mathbf{A}_{\mathrm{f}})
the symmetric space
Y(K)
is given by a finite disjoint union

Y(K)=\bigsqcup_{i}\mathcal{X}/\Gamma_{i}.

where
\Gamma_{i}\subset G(\mathbf{Q})^{+}
are arithmetic subgroups, and
\mathcal{X}=G(\mathbf{R})^{+}/\left(K_{\infty}Z(\mathbf{R})\cap G^{+}(\mathbf{R})\right)
with
K_{\infty}
a maximal compact subgroup of
G(\mathbf{R})
. If
K
is neat then
\mathcal{X}/\Gamma_{i}
has the structure of a smooth manifold. This space satisfies
\pi_{1}\left(\mathcal{X}/\Gamma_{i}\right)=\Gamma_{i}
and thus

H^{j}(K,V_{\lambda})=\bigoplus_{i}H^{j}(\Gamma_{i},V_{\lambda}).

Let
M
be a
\mathbf{Z}[K]
-module with
K
acting through its projection to
U
. By the theory of Borel–Serre (
[
BS73
]
; see also
[
Urb05
, §4.2.1]
), the cohomology groups
H^{i}(\Gamma_{i},M)
vanish for
i\gg 0
, and are computed by a complex
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}(\Gamma_{i},M)
of
\mathbf{Z}[\Gamma_{i}]
-modules, depending functorially on
M
, each term of which is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of copies of
M
. We write

\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}(U,M):=\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}(\Gamma_{i},M).

This complex computes the Betti cohomology of
Y(K)
. If
M
has an action of
\tau^{-1}
compatible with the
K
-action then we can define an action of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}_{\eta}
on
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}(K,M)
lifting the action on cohomology. There are many such liftings but these differ by a chain homotopy and thus induce the same morphism in the derived category.

Modules of continuous functions

For
\lambda\in X_{+}^{\bullet}(S/S_{0})
we define
U
-modules

\displaystyle C_{\lambda,U}

\displaystyle=\{f:U\to\mathcal{O}:f\ \text{continuous},\ f(n\ell g)=\lambda^{-1}(\ell)f(g)\ \forall n\in N_{0},\ell\in L_{U},g\in U\}

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{univ}}

\displaystyle=\{f:U\to\mathcal{O}:f\ \text{continuous},\ f(n\ell g)=f(g)\ \forall n\ell\in Q^{0}(\mathbf{Z}_{p}),g\in U\}

with
U
acting by right translation.

Note that, since we have
U=N_{0}\times L_{U}\times\bar{N}_{U}
, restriction to
\bar{N}_{U}
is an isomorphism between
C_{\lambda,U}
and the space of continuous functions on
\bar{N}_{U}
, for any
\lambda
(but the action of
U
on this space depends on
\lambda
). Similarly, functions in
C_{\mathrm{univ}}
are uniquely determined by their restriction to
L_{U}\times\bar{N}_{U}
, and this restriction must transform trivially under left-translation by
L_{U}\cap L^{0}
, giving an isomorphism between

3.
Branching laws for algebraic representations

We recall the setup considered in
[
Loe21b
]
, where we consider pushforward of “Eisenstein-type” cohomology classes from a group
H
to a larger group
G
. We shall fix an embedding
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}
of reductive groups over
\mathbf{Q}
, extending to an embedding
H\hookrightarrow G
of reductive group schemes over
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
.

3.1.
Flag varieties

We fix choices of subgroups
Q,S
etc as in §
2.1
, both for
G
and for
H
; and we distinguish between them using subscripts, so
Q_{G}
and
Q_{H}
are parabolics in
G
and
H
respectively. Note that we do not assume any direct compatibility between these (e.g. we do not suppose that
Q_{H}=Q_{G}\cap H
etc).
We are interested in the action of
H
, and its subgroup
Q_{H}^{0}
, on the flag variety
\mathcal{F}=G/\overline{Q}_{G}
. We assume that there exists
u\in\mathcal{F}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
such that

(A)

the
Q_{H}^{0}
-orbit of
u
is Zariski-open in
\mathcal{F}
, and

(B)

the image of
\overline{Q}_{G}\cap u^{-1}Q_{H}^{0}u
under the projection
\overline{Q}_{G}\twoheadrightarrow L_{G}\twoheadrightarrow S_{G}
is contained in the subtorus
S_{G}^{0}
.

Remark 3.1.1
.

Note that the validity of (B) is independent of the choice of representative
u
in (A), since
\overline{Q}_{G}\cap u^{-1}Q_{H}^{0}u
is well-defined up to conjugation (and
S_{G}
is commutative).

3.2.
Highest-weight representations

Proposition 3.2.1
.

For any
\lambda\in X^{\bullet}_{+}(S_{G}/S_{G}^{0})
, we have
\dim(V^{G}_{\lambda})^{Q_{H}^{0}}\leqslant 1
. If this dimension is 1, then
(V_{\lambda}^{G})^{Q_{H}^{0}}
is spanned by the unique polynomial
\br_{\lambda}\in K[G]
(a “branching polynomial”) such that
\br_{\lambda}\left(\overline{q}u^{-1}q^{\prime}\right)=\lambda(\overline{q})
for
\overline{q}\in\overline{Q}_{G}
and
q^{\prime}\in Q_{H}^{0}
.

Proof.

Suppose
f\in(V_{\lambda}^{G})^{Q_{H}^{0}}
. Then the restriction of
f
to the subvariety
\overline{Q}_{G}u^{-1}Q_{H}^{0}
of
G
is uniquely determined by
f(u^{-1})
. Since
\overline{Q}_{G}u^{-1}Q_{H}^{0}
is open (and
G
is connected), this implies that
f
itself is uniquely determined, as an element of
K[G]
, by
f(u^{-1})
. In particular, if
f
is non-zero, then we can scale it so that
f(u^{-1})=1
.
∎

Remark 3.2.2
.

Conversely, if
S_{G}^{0}
is exactly the image of
\overline{Q}_{G}\cap u^{-1}Q_{H}^{0}u
in
S_{G}
, then any weight
\lambda\in X^{\bullet}_{+}(S_{G})
such that
(V^{G}_{\lambda})^{Q_{H}^{0}}\neq 0
must lie in
X^{\bullet}_{+}(S_{G}/S_{G}^{0})
. However, it is convenient not to require
S_{G}^{0}
to have this minimality property.

Definition 3.2.3
.

We shall say that a weight
\lambda\in X^{\bullet}(S_{G}/S_{G}^{0})
is

-admissible
if
(V_{\lambda}^{G})^{Q_{H}^{0}}\neq 0
.

If this holds, then

must act on

via a weight

, and this weight must be dominant (since it is the highest weight of the

, which is finite-dimensional). We denote this weight by

, or just

if

Remark 3.2.4
.

The natural map

\frac{Q_{H}\cap u\overline{Q}_{G}u^{-1}}{Q_{H}^{0}\cap u\overline{Q}_{G}u^{-1}}\to Q_{H}/Q_{H}^{0}

is injective, and for dimension reasons, it must in fact be an isomorphism. The left-hand side maps naturally to

; so we can characterise

as the pullback of
\lambda
to this quotient.

Proposition 3.2.5
.

If
\lambda
is
Q_{H}^{0}
-admissible, then there exists a unique homomorphism

This follows from Frobenius reciprocity:

is isomorphic to the co-induced module

.

If
\lambda
is a

-admissible weight, then

Since

3.3.

If

-admissible weight, we thus have two canonical vectors in

, and the

-invariant vector

. We also have a canonical linear functional
\psi_{\lambda}
on

, given by evaluation at

to its highest-weight space (i.e. it is a lowest-weight vector of weight

for the contragredient representation

Proposition 3.3.1
.

We have
\psi_{\lambda}(f_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{hw}})=\psi_{\lambda}(u^{-1}\cdot\br_{\lambda})
.

Proof.

Both sides are, by definition, equal to 1.

Corollary 3.3.2
.

Let
\eta\in\Sigma^{++}
. If
\lambda
is
Q_{H}^{0}
-admissible, then for any

we have

p^{hr}\rho_{\lambda}(\eta(p)^{-r})\cdot(f_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{hw}}-u^{-1}\cdot\br_{\lambda})\in p^{r}V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}},

where

as above.

Proof.

Since
V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}
is stable under the action of
G
, it is certainly stable under the action of
\mathbf{G}_{m,/\mathbf{Z}_{p}}
defined by

, and hence is equal to the direct sum of its eigenspaces for the powers of

. So to prove that
p^{h}\rho_{\lambda}(\eta(p)^{-1})

to itself, it suffices to show that the eigenvalues of
\rho_{\lambda}(\eta(p)^{-1})
on

are in

is the highest weight of

To prove the second statement, it suffices to take

acts on these with eigenvalues in

∎

5.

5.1.
Definitions

Let

Since

and these have the property

(2)

f_{\lambda,{r+1}}^{\mathrm{hw}}\bmod p^{r}=\pr_{V_{r+1},V_{r}}^{\star}\left(f_{\lambda,r}^{\mathrm{hw}}\right)\quad\in H^{0}(V_{r+1},V_{\lambda,r}).

Definition 5.1.1
.

The

of weight

is the map

\mom^{\lambda}_{G}\ \ :\ \ H^{c}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\left(Q_{G}^{0},\mathcal{O}\right)\longrightarrow H^{c}_{\mathrm{Iw}}\left(Q_{G}^{0},V_{\lambda,\mathcal{O}}\right)

which is well-defined by (

By construction this map commutes with the action of the Hecke operator

(and the moment maps for the two lattices are compatible).

Theorem 5.2.1

The above diagram is commutative.

Remark 5.2.2

As in

Proof.

From the definition of the moment maps, it suffices to prove the following claim: suppose we define
z_{H}^{[\mu]}=\mom_{H}^{\mu}(z_{H}^{[0]})
and apply the constructions above in weight 0 and in weight
\lambda

we have the following equality of elements of

:

\xi_{G,r}^{[\lambda]}\bmod p^{r}=(\xi_{G,r}^{[0]}\bmod p^{r})\cup f_{\lambda,r}^{\mathrm{hw}}.

Recall that
V_{r}=\tau^{-r}U_{r}\tau^{r}
. We thus have an isomorphism
\tau^{r}:Y_{G}(U_{r})\to Y_{G}(V_{r})
, and our (integrally normalised) pushforward map

H^{c}(Y_{G}(U_{r}),V_{\lambda,r})\longrightarrow H^{c}(Y_{G}(U_{r}),(\tau^{r})^{*}V_{\lambda,r})\xrightarrow[\cong]{\ (\tau^{r})_{\star}\ }H^{c}(Y_{G}(V_{r}),V_{\lambda,r}),

where the first map is given by the morphism of sheaves

corresponding to the endomorphism

of

Since

is an admissible lattice, it is the direct sum of its intersections with the weight spaces of

for the action of

. So it suffices to prove that

as elements of

∎

6.1.

It will be convenient to work with a slightly different family of subgroups. Let

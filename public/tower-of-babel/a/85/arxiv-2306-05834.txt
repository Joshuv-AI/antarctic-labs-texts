On spectrum of sample covariance matrices from large tensor vectors
Wangjun Yuan

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the limiting empirical spectral distribution (LSD) of sums of independent rank-one k k -fold tensor products of n n -dimensional vectors as k , n → ∞ k,n\to\infty . Assuming that the base vectors are complex random variables with unit modular, we show that the LSD is the Marčenko-Pastur law. Comparing with the existing results, our limiting setting allows k k to grow much faster than n n . Consequently, we obtain the necessary and sufficient conditions for Marčenko-Pastur law to serve as the LSD of our matrix model. Our approach is based on the moment method.

Introduction

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathbf{y}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where
\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\}
is a family of i.i.d. centered random variables with unit variance, and let
\{\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{(l)}:1\leq\alpha\leq m,1\leq l\leq k\}
be a family of i.i.d. copies of
\mathbf{y}
.
For
1\leq\alpha\leq m
, define
Y_{\alpha}=\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbf{y}_{\alpha}^{(k)}
yielding a
k
-fold tensor product. We identify each
Y_{\alpha}
as an
n^{k}
-dimensional vector, and denote by
Y=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
. Let
\{\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\ldots\}
be a sequence of real numbers, we consider the sum of
m
independent rank-
1
Hermitian matrices:

\displaystyle M_{n,k,m}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{m}\tau_{\alpha}Y_{\alpha}Y_{\alpha}^{*},

(1.1)

which is an
n^{k}\times n^{k}
Hermitian matrix.

In statistics, the model (
1.1
) is called the sample covariance matrix. It helps to understand the population covariance matrix. The limiting empirical spectral distribution (LSD) of
M_{n,k,m}
can serve as a test statistics. The simplest case
k=1
, which corresponds to the population vector with i.i.d. entries, was well-studied in the literature. Under appropriate moment conditions on
\xi_{1}
, the LSD was obtained in the seminal paper
[
11
]
when
n\to+\infty
and
m/n^{k}\to c
for some positive constant
c
. When
\tau_{\alpha}=1
for all
\alpha
, the resulting LSD is the famous Marčenko-Pastur law. This probability distribution has a density function given by

\displaystyle p(x)=\dfrac{\sqrt{((1+\sqrt{c})^{2}-x)(x-(1-\sqrt{c})^{2})}}{2\pi x}{\bf 1}_{[(1-\sqrt{c})^{2},(1+\sqrt{c})^{2}]}(x)+(1-c)\delta_{0}(dx){\bf 1}_{0<c<1}.

(1.2)

Further details can be found in
[
2
,
3
]
.
Subsequently, numerous works on the Marčenko-Pastur law have emerged in the literature, including
[
4
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
, and the reference therein. Later, the necessary and sufficient conditions on
Y_{1}
were carried out in
[
14
]
to ensure that the Marčenko-Pastur law is the LSD of
M_{n,1,m}
when
\tau_{\alpha}=1
for all
\alpha
.

Another motivation to study the model (
1.1
) comes from quantum information theory.
We consider a classical probability problem of distributing
m
balls randomly into
n
bins. Using the terminology of matrices, this classical probability problem is equivalent to the spectrum of the
M_{n,1,m}
when
Y_{\alpha}
is chosen randomly from the canonical basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The eigenvalues of the
M_{n,1,m}
are the frequency of occurrence of vectors
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
in the family
\{Y_{\alpha}:1\leq\alpha\leq m\}
.
It was considered as a quantum analog for
M_{n,1,m}
if the vector
Y_{\alpha}
is picked randomly from the unit sphere on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
A modified version of the quantum problem, which was introduced in
[
1
]
, is to choose the vector
Y_{1}
from the random product states in
(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{\otimes k}
. When
k
and
m/n^{k}
are fixed, and the base vector
\mathbf{y}
is Gaussian,
[
1
]
established the convergence in expectation of the ESD towards the Marčenko-Pastur law (
1.2
) as
n\to+\infty
by computing the moments.
In the realm of quantum physics and quantum information theory, it is natural to explore systems with a multitude of quantum states.
The paper
[
16
]
characterizes the quantum entanglement of structured random states and studies the spectral density of the reduced state when the number of the quantum states
k
is large.
Besides, the asymptotic behavior of the average entropy of entanglement for elements of an ensemble of random states associated with a graph was studied in
[
6
]
when the dimension of the quantum subsystem is large.

The cases
k\geq 2
and
k=1
are very different. The tensor structure appears when
k\geq 2
, which results in the dependence of the entries of
Y_{\alpha}
and non-unitary invariant. In a recent work
[
10
]
, the LSD for the
k
-fold tensor model
M_{n,k,m}
was explored when
k
is large. In the special case where
\tau_{\alpha}\equiv 1
, the LSD precisely follows the Marčenko-Pastur law (
1.2
). A central limit theorem (CLT) is also established for a class of linear spectral statistics following the approach of
[
9
]
when
k=2
. The main setup in
[
10
]
is that the number of tensor folds (
k
) must be sufficiently small in comparison to the spatial dimension (
n
). More precisely,
k/n\to 0
is required for the validity of the LSD. In a more recent study,
[
7
]
considered the case where
k=O(n)
for the model (
1.1
). Under the condition
k/n\to d
, they derived the limiting moment sequence of the empirical spectral distribution (ESD) of
M_{n,k,m}
, assuming the finiteness of the all moments of
\xi_{1}
. It is interesting that, the fourth moment of
\xi_{1}
contributes to the limiting moment sequence. If
\tau_{\alpha}\equiv 1
, the resulting limiting moment sequence corresponds to the Marčenko-Pastur law (
1.2
) when either
d=0
or
d>0
and the fourth moment of
\xi_{1}
is 1.

In the present paper, we study the model (
1.1
) with
\xi_{1}
chosen from the unit circle on the complex plane. Our focus lies in the scenario where
k
goes to infinity much faster than the setting in
[
7
]
. We derive the limiting moment sequence of the ESD of
M_{n,k,m}
when
n,k
goes to infinity. When
\tau_{\alpha}\equiv 1
, the limiting moment sequence reveals that the LSD is exactly Marčenko-Pastur law (
1.2
). From the point of view of probability theory, we remove the restriction on the speed of
k
approaching infinity, as required in
[
10
,
7
]
. Our results even allows that
k/n
does not have a limit when
n\to\infty
. In practical terms, one would anticipate that the dimension of a system remains fixed and is reused multiple times, which leads to the scenario that
k
tends to infinity while

is fixed. The study of the model (
1.1
) with fixed

remains an open question, and we plan to address this case in our future work. As an variant of this real-world scenario, the results in this paper apply to the setting where
k\gg n\gg 1
.

We would like to remark that the tensor
Y_{\alpha}
introduced above is the non-symmetric random tensor model. Instead of considering the tensor product of i.i.d. vectors, the
k
-fold tensor product
\mathbf{y}^{\otimes k}
of the same random vector
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is known as the symmetric random tensor model. The limiting spectral distribution of the Hermitian matrix (

) constructed by i.i.d. copies of

]
.

Throughout the paper, we assume that the parameters
m,n,k
grow towards infinity following the proportion

\displaystyle\dfrac{m}{n^{k}}\to c

(1.3)

for some constant
c\in(0,\infty)
. The following theorem is the first main results of the paper, where we establish the convergence in expectation of the moments.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M_{n,k,m}
be in (
1.1
) with
|\xi_{1}|=1
. Suppose that for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tau_{j}^{q}\to m_{q}^{(\tau)},\quad m\to+\infty.

(1.4)

Assume that (
1.3
) holds. Then for any fixed
p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n,k\to+\infty}\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\big]=\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)}\right).

Here,
\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)
is the frequency of
t
in the sequence
\alpha
and is given by (
2.2
), and
\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
is a set of sequences that is defined in Lemma
2.1
.

Our approach is based on the method of moments. We associate graphs to each terms of the moment, and compute the moment by distinguishing the graphs that contributes to the limit.
The class
\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
of sequences corresponds to the largest leading terms, while the sequences in
\alpha\notin\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
are negligible.
It’s important to note that the tensor structure introduces a power of
k
in the moment calculation.
For the case
k=1
, the limit of the moment sequence can be obtained by counting the size of
\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
, since only the leading terms in the sum of
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
contribute to the limit. We refer the interested readers to
[
3
]
*Section 3.1.3. This is also true whenever
k=o(n)
. See
[
7
]
*Section 4.
For the case
k=O(n)
studied in
[
7
]
, besides the first leading term, the second leading term also contributes to the limit.
In our current setting, where
k
can grow much faster, all terms associated with
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
may contribute to the limit. Hence, we need to characterize all the possible graphs associate with
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
. This is the main novelty in methodology of the present paper.

In the next theorem, we strengthen Theorem
1.1
from convergence in expectation to almost sure convergence.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that the conditions in Theorem
1.1
hold. Suppose that
k=k(n)
is a function of
n
and tends to infinity as
n\to\infty
. Then for any fixed
p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)}\right),

almost surely.

After establishing the limiting moment sequence, it is nature to inquire whether this sequence uniquely characterizes a probability measure. The following corollary provides a condition that guarantees the uniqueness of the probability measure corresponding to the moment sequence, which leads to the almost sure convergence of the ESD of
M_{n,k,m}
.

Corollary 1.1
.

Assume that the conditions in Theorem
1.1
hold. Suppose that there exists a positive constant
A
, such that
|m_{q}^{(\tau)}|\leq A^{q}q^{q}
for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists a probability measure
\mu
whose moment sequence is

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{p}\mu(dx)=\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)}\right),\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}.

(1.5)

Moreover, suppose that
k=k(n)
is a function of
n
and tends to infinity when
n\to\infty
, then the ESD of
M_{n,k,m}
converges almost surely to
\mu
.

In particular, if
\tau_{\alpha}=1
for all
1\leq\alpha\leq m
, then the ESD of
M_{n,k,m}
converges almost surely to the Marčenko-Pastur law (
1.2
).

By combining the results in
[
10
]
*Theorem 1.2,
[
7
]
*Theorem 2.1 and Corollary
1.1
, we immediately obtain the following necessary and sufficient condition for Marčenko-Pastur law (
1.2
) to be the LSD of the model (
1.1
) when
\tau_{\alpha}\equiv 1
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
M_{n,k,m}
be in (
1.1
) with
\tau_{\alpha}=1
for all
1\leq\alpha\leq m
. Then the ESD of
M_{n,k,m}
converges almost surely to the Marčenko-Pastur law (
1.2
) if and only if either
k=o(n)
or
|\xi_{1}|=1
.

Proposition 2.2

For any

is a

Proof.

If

is not a single graph, then all edges must coincide with at least one other edges. If we remove the orientation and glue all the coincide edges, it results in non-directed connected graph with at most

. For
1\leq k\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle S(n,k)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\dfrac{(-1)^{k-i}i^{n}}{i!(k-i)!}.

2.

For positive integers
n\geq k>1
, we have

\displaystyle S(n+1,k)=S(n,k-1)+kS(n,k).

3.

For positive integers
n
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}S(n,k)\cdot x(x-1)\ldots(x-k+1)=x^{n}.

3
Convergence of spectral moments

3.1
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We compute the moment

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\big].

for any
p\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
By convention,
\alpha_{p+1}=\alpha_{1}
. We have

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\big]=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\alpha_{t}}\right)\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathrm{Tr}\big(Y_{\alpha_{1}}^{*}Y_{\alpha_{2}}Y_{\alpha_{2}}^{*}\cdots Y_{\alpha_{p}}Y_{\alpha_{p}}^{*}Y_{\alpha_{p+1}}\big)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\alpha_{t}}\right)\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\prod_{l=1}^{k}\mathrm{Tr}\left(\left(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{1}}^{(l)}\right)^{*}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{2}}^{(l)}\left(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{1}}^{(l)}\right)^{*}\ldots\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{(l)}\right)\Bigg]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\alpha_{t}}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\mathrm{Tr}\left(\left(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{1}}^{(1)}\right)^{*}\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{2}}^{(1)}\left(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{1}}^{(1)}\right)^{*}\ldots\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{p+1}}^{(1)}\right)\Bigg]\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}=1}^{m}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\alpha_{t}}\right)\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i^{(1)},\ldots,i^{(p)}=1}^{n}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\bigg(\overline{\big(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{t}}^{(1)}\big)_{i^{(t)}}}\big(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{t+1}}^{(1)}\big)_{i^{(t)}}\bigg)\right]\right)^{k},

(3.1)

where we used the i.i.d. setting in the third equality.

For two sequences
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{p})\in[m]^{p}
and
i=(i^{(1)},\ldots,i^{(p)})\in[n]^{p}
, let

\displaystyle E(i,\alpha)=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{t=1}^{p}\bigg(\overline{\big(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{t}}^{(1)}\big)_{i^{(t)}}}\big(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{t+1}}^{(1)}\big)_{i^{(t)}}\bigg)\right].

(3.2)

By the i.i.d. setting,
E(i,\alpha)=E(i^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
if the two sequences
i
and
\alpha
are equivalent to
i^{\prime}
and
\alpha^{\prime}
, respectively. By (
3.1
) and (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathbb{E}\big[\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\big]=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{J}_{s,p}(m)}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\alpha_{t}}\right)\left(\sum_{r=1}^{p}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{J}_{r,p}(n)}E(i,\alpha)\right)^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}}\left(\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\varphi(\alpha_{t})}\right)\left(\sum_{r=1}^{p}n\cdots(n-r+1)\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}}E(i,\alpha)\right)^{k}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2},

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\varphi(\alpha_{t})}\right)\left(\sum_{r=1}^{p}n\cdots(n-r+1)\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}}E(i,\alpha)\right)^{k},

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\varphi(\alpha_{t})}\right)\left(\sum_{r=1}^{p}n\cdots(n-r+1)\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}}E(i,\alpha)\right)^{k}.

Note that the component of the base vector satisfies

\displaystyle\left(\mathbf{y}_{\beta}^{(l)}\right)_{i}\overline{\left(\mathbf{y}_{\beta}^{(l)}\right)_{i}}=\dfrac{1}{n},\quad\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\left(\mathbf{y}_{\beta}^{(l)}\right)_{i}\right)^{p}\right]\right|\leq\dfrac{1}{n^{p/2}}.

(3.4)

Recall the definition of paired graph and single graph in Definition
2
. For any sequence
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}
and
i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}E(i,\alpha)=n^{-p},&g(i,\alpha)\mathrm{\ is\ a\ paired\ graph,}\\
E(i,\alpha)=0,&g(i,\alpha)\mathrm{\ is\ a\ single\ graph,}\\
|E(i,\alpha)|\leq n^{-p},&g(i,\alpha)\mathrm{\ otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

(3.5)

Firstly, we deal with
I_{1}
. For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
, by Proposition
2.1
, formula (
3.5
) and Proposition
2.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}}E(i,\alpha)=

\displaystyle n^{-p}\cdot\#\{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}:g(i,\alpha)\ \mathrm{is\ paired\ graph}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}n^{-p}\cdot S(p+1-s,r),&r\leq p+1-s,\\
0,&r>p+1-s,\end{cases}

where we use the notation
\#S
for the number of elements in the set
S
. Thus, it follows from Lemma
2.4
that

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{p}n\cdots(n-r+1)\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}}E(i,\alpha)=n^{-p}\sum_{r=1}^{p+1-s}n\cdots(n-r+1)\cdot S(p+1-s,r)=n^{1-s}.

Hence,

\displaystyle I_{1}=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\left(\dfrac{m}{n^{k}}\right)^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\dfrac{1}{m^{s}}\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\varphi(\alpha_{t})}\right).

(3.6)

Next, we deal with
I_{2}
. For any
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}
, by Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
2.2
, if the graph
g(i,\alpha)
is not a single graph for
i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}
, then
r+s\leq p
. Hence, by (
3.5
), we establish

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{r=1}^{p}n\cdots(n-r+1)\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}}E(i,\alpha)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{p-s}n^{-p+r}\cdot\#\{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}:g(i,\alpha)\ \mathrm{is\ not\ a\ single\ graph}\}.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle|I_{2}|\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left|\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\varphi(\alpha_{t})}\right|\left|\sum_{r=1}^{p}n\cdots(n-r+1)\sum_{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}}E(i,\alpha)\right|^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{p}\left(\dfrac{m}{n^{k}}\right)^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left|\dfrac{1}{m^{s}}\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\varphi(\alpha_{t})}\right|

\displaystyle\times\left|\sum_{r=1}^{p-s}n^{-p+r+s-1}\cdot\#\{i\in\mathcal{C}_{r,p}:g(i,\alpha)\ \mathrm{is\ not\ a\ single\ graph}\}\right|^{k}.

(3.7)

Note that the assumption (
1.4
) implies the following convergence:

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{m^{s}}\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\varphi(\alpha_{t})}\to\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)},\quad m\to\infty.

Hence, under the limiting setting (
1.3
), when
n,k\to\infty
, we can deduce from (
3.6
) and (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle I_{1}\to\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)}\right),\quad I_{2}\to 0.

(3.8)

Therefore, the proof is concluded by taking limit
n,k\to\infty
in (
3.1
) and using (
3.8
).

3.2
Proof of Theorem
1.2

For any
p\in\mathbb{N}
, for
k\geq 2
, we compute the variance

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\right).

The idea is similar to
[
7
]
, and is sketched below. By the computation of
[
7
]
*Section 3.2, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\right)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{2k}}\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in[m]^{p}\atop\alpha\cap\beta\not=\emptyset}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\alpha_{t}}\tau_{\beta_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[\left(\sum_{i,j\in[n]^{p}}E^{\prime}(i,\alpha;j,\beta)\right)^{k}-\left(\sum_{i,j\in[n]^{p}}E(i,\alpha)E(j,\beta)\right)^{k}\right],

where
E(\cdot,\cdot)
is given in (
3.5
), and
E^{\prime}(i,\alpha;j,\beta)
is defined by

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(i,\alpha;j,\beta)=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{t=1}^{p}\bigg(\overline{\big(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{t}}^{(1)}\big)_{i^{(t)}}}\big(\mathbf{y}_{\alpha_{t+1}}^{(1)}\big)_{i^{(t)}}\overline{\big(\mathbf{y}_{\beta_{t}}^{(1)}\big)_{j^{(t)}}}\big(\mathbf{y}_{\beta_{t+1}}^{(1)}\big)_{j^{(t)}}\bigg)\right].

Next, we join the two graphs
g(i,\alpha)
and
g(j,\beta)
together and keep the coincident edges. We denote by
g(i,\alpha)\cup g(j,\beta)
the resulting graph. If there is an edge in the graph
g(i,\alpha)\cup g(j,\beta)
that does not coincide with any other edges, then this edge must belong to
g(i,\alpha)
or
g(j,\beta)
, which implies

\displaystyle E^{\prime}(i,\alpha;j,\beta)=E(i,\alpha)E(j,\beta)=0.

Thus, we only need to consider the indices such that all edges in
g(i,\alpha)\cup g(j,\beta)
coincide with other edges. Noting that
\alpha\cap\beta\not=\emptyset
, the graph
g(i,\alpha)\cup g(j,\beta)
is connected with
4p
edges. Hence, if we remove orientation and glue coincident edges for the graph
g(i,\alpha)\cup g(j,\beta)
, it results in a non-directed connected graph with at most
2p
edges, which implies that

\displaystyle|(\alpha,\beta)|+|(i,j)|\leq 2p+1.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\right)=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{n^{2k}}\sum_{s=1}^{2p}\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{C}_{s,2p}\atop\alpha\cap\beta\not=\emptyset}\left(\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\varphi(\alpha_{t})}\tau_{\varphi(\beta_{t})}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left[\left(\sum_{r=1}^{2p+1-s}n\ldots(n-r+1)\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}_{r,2p}}E^{\prime}(i,\alpha;j,\beta)\right)^{k}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad-\left(\sum_{r=1}^{2p+1-s}n\ldots(n-r+1)\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}_{r,2p}}E(i,\alpha)E(j,\beta)\right)^{k}\right].

By (
3.4
), for any sequence
\alpha,\beta,i,j
, it holds that

\displaystyle|E^{\prime}(i,\alpha;j,\beta)|,\ |E(i,\alpha)E(j,\beta)|\leq n^{-2p}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left|\sum_{r=1}^{2p+1-s}n\ldots(n-r+1)\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}_{r,2p}}E(i,\alpha)E(j,\beta)\right|,\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\left|\sum_{r=1}^{2p+1-s}n\ldots(n-r+1)\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}_{r,2p}}E^{\prime}(i,\alpha;j,\beta)\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{2p+1-s}n^{r-2p}\sum_{(i,j)\in\mathcal{C}_{r,2p}}1\leq C_{p}n^{1-s}(1+o_{n}(1)),

where
C_{p}
is a positive number that only depends on
p
, and
o_{n}(1)
is a quantity that tends to 0 as
n\to\infty
.
Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{2C_{p}^{k}}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=1}^{2p}\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{C}_{s,2p}\atop\alpha\cap\beta\not=\emptyset}\left(\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{p}\tau_{\varphi(\alpha_{t})}\tau_{\varphi(\beta_{t})}\right)n^{-ks}(1+o_{n}(1))^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2C_{p}^{k}}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=1}^{2p}\left(\dfrac{m}{n^{k}}\right)^{s}\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{C}_{s,2p}\atop\alpha\cap\beta\not=\emptyset}\left(\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{s}\dfrac{1}{m}\tau_{\varphi(t)}^{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)+\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\beta)}\right)(1+o_{n}(1))^{k}.

By assumption (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\varphi\in\mathcal{I}_{s,m}}\prod_{t=1}^{s}\dfrac{1}{m}\tau_{\varphi(t)}^{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)+\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\beta)}\to\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)+\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\beta)}^{(\tau)},\quad m\to\infty.

Together with (
1.3
), we establish

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}\left(\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\right)\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{2C_{p}^{k}}{n^{k}}\sum_{s=1}^{2p}c^{s}\sum_{(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathcal{C}_{s,2p}\atop\alpha\cap\beta\not=\emptyset}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)+\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\beta)}^{(\tau)}\right)(1+o_{n}(1))^{k+1+s}.

Therefore, for
k\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 2}\mathrm{Var}\left(\dfrac{1}{n^{k}}\mathrm{Tr}M_{n,k,m}^{p}\right)<+\infty.

The proof is concluded by Borel-Cantelli’s Lemma, noting that
k=k(n)
tends to infinity as
n\to\infty
.

3.3
Proof of Corollary
1.1

We start with the uniqueness of
\mu
. We have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)}\right)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}A^{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}A^{\sum_{t=1}^{s}\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{p}\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)\right)^{\sum_{s=1}^{p}\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}.

By definition (
2.2
), for
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}
, we have
\sum_{t=1}^{s}\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)=p
. Hence, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)}\right)\right|\leq A^{p}p^{p}\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}1=A^{p}p^{p}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\dfrac{c^{s}}{p}\binom{p}{s-1}\binom{p}{s}.

Using the inequality
\binom{p}{s}\leq 2^{p}
for all
0\leq s\leq p
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)}\right)\right|\leq 4^{p}A^{p}p^{p}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\dfrac{c^{s}}{p}\leq 4^{p}A^{p}(1+c)^{p}p^{p}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}\left(\prod_{t=1}^{s}m_{\mathrm{deg}_{t}(\alpha)}^{(\tau)}\right)\right|^{-1/p}\geq\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\left(4^{p}A^{p}(c+1)^{p}p^{p}\right)^{-1/p}=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{4A(c+1)p}=+\infty.

Therefore, the Carleman’s condition is satisfied, which implies that there exists a unique probability measure
\mu
whose moments are given by (
1.5
).

The uniqueness of the probability measure
\mu
corresponding to the moments in (
1.5
) guarantees the almost sure convergence of the ESD of
M_{n,k,m}
towards
\mu
.

If
\tau_{\alpha}=1
for all
1\leq\alpha\leq m
, then the condition (
1.4
) holds with
m_{q}^{(\tau)}=1
for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
. In this case, the moment sequence (
1.5
) for
\mu
becomes

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}x^{p}\mu(dx)=\sum_{s=1}^{p}c^{s}\sum_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}_{s,p}^{(1)}}1=\sum_{s=1}^{p}\dfrac{c^{s}}{p}\binom{p}{s-1}\binom{p}{s},\quad\forall p\in\mathbb{N}_{+},

where we use Lemma
2.1
in the last equality. By
[
3
]
*Lemma 3.1, this moment sequence coincides with the moment sequence of the Marčenko-Pastur law (
1.2
). Therefore, the uniqueness of
\mu
implies that
\mu
is exactly the Marčenko-Pastur law (
1.2
).

Recall that for infinite matrices

In the same way one can define the Schur–Hadamard product of two finite matrices of the same size.

Let

B\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}\quad\Longrightarrow A\star B\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}.

We use the notation

for the space of Schur multipliers of
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}

We refer the reader to
[
GK1
]

[
BS
]
for properties of Schatten-von Neumann classes
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}
. It is well known that for
p\geq 1

{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}
is a Banach space and
\|\cdot\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}
is a norm. For
p<1
, the class
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}
is a quasi-Banach space. More precisely, for
p<1
,
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}
is a

p
-Banach space
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}
is a
p
-norm
, i.e.,

\|T+R\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}^{p}\leq\|T\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}^{p}+\|R\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}^{p},\quad T,~R\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p},

see, for example,
[
Pe2
]
, App. 1, for the proof of this triangle inequality for
p<1
.

It follows from the closed graph theorem that if
A\in\mathfrak{M}_{p}
, then

\|A\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sup\{\|A\star B\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}:~B\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p},~\|B\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\leq 1\}<\infty.

It is easy to see that
\mathfrak{M}_{p}
is a Banach space for
p\geq 1
and it is a
p
-Banach space for
0<p<1

Obviously, all finite matrices are Schur multipliers of

(the
p
-norm

We denote by

{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}

2.
The Besov classes
\boldsymbol{B_{p}^{1/p}}

For the reader’s convenience we give here the definition of the Besov classes

\big(B_{p}^{1/p}\big)_{+}
of functions in
B_{p}^{1/p}
that are analytic in the unit disk
{\mathbb{D}}
.

Definition.
Let
v
be an infinitely differentiable function on
(0,\infty)
such that

v\geq{\boldsymbol{0}}
,
\operatorname{supp}v=\big[\frac{1}{2},2\big]
and

\sum_{j\geq 0}v\big(2^{-j}x\big)=1\quad\mbox{for}\quad x\geq 1.

Let
0<p<\infty
. For a function
\varphi
analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
we say that

\varphi\in\big(B_{p}^{1/p}\big)_{+}
if

\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{n}\|\varphi*V_{n}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}<\infty,

where

\displaystyle V_{n}(z)=\sum_{j>0}v\big(2^{-n}j\big)z^{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varphi*V_{n}=\sum_{j>0}\widehat{\varphi}(j)\widehat{V}_{n}(j)z^{j}.

(2.1)

Among various equivalent
p
-norms on
\big(B_{p}^{1/p}\big)_{+}
we can select the following one:

\|\varphi\|_{B_{p}^{1/p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}2^{n}\|\varphi*V_{n}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

3.
\boldsymbol{L^{p}}
estimates for certain trigonometric polynomials

We start this section with an elementary observation. Since clearly,
|f(0)|\leq\|f\|_{H^{p}}
,
p\in(0,\infty]
for
f\in H^{p}
, it follows that

\mbox{if}\quad f(z)=\sum\limits_{j=m}^{n}\widehat{f}(j)z^{j},\quad\mbox{then}\quad|\widehat{f}(m)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\widehat{f}(n)|\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}}\quad p>0.

We need sharp estimates of the
p
-norms in
L^{p}
of certain trigonometric polynomials whose coefficients are expressed in terms of the values of an infinitely differentiable function with compact support at the points of the grid
\{\frac{k}{m}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
.

Let
q
be an infinitely differentiable function on
{\mathbb{R}}
with compact support, we define the family
Q_{m}
,
m\geq 1
, of trigonometric polynomials by

\displaystyle Q_{m}(z)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}q\left(\frac{k}{m}\right)z^{k},\quad m\geq 1.

(3.1)

It was established in
[
A
]
, Ch 4 that for
p\in(0,1]
, there exists a positive number
C_{p}
such that

\|Q_{m}\|_{L^{p}}\leq C_{p}m^{1-1/p},\quad m\geq 0.

We are going to improve this estimate and show that one can select
C_{p}
so that it remains bounded as
p
approaches 1.

Denote by
{\mathscr{F}}
and
{\mathscr{F}}^{*}
the Fourier transform and the inverse Fourier transform defined by

({\mathscr{F}}f)(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-2\pi{\rm i}xt}\,dx,\quad({\mathscr{F}}^{*}f)(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{2\pi{\rm i}xt}\,dx.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0<p\leq 1
and let
q
be an infinitely differentiable function on
{\mathbb{R}}
with compact support.
Then the trigonometric polynomials
Q_{m}
given by (
3.1
) admit the estimate

\|Q_{m}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq m^{1-1/p}\|{\mathscr{F}}q\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}.

Proof.
We define the operator
R
from
L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})
to
L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})
by

(Rf)\big(e^{2\pi{\rm i}t}\big)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}f(t+k).

Clearly, for
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{Rf}(n)=({\mathscr{F}}f)(n),\quad n\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

(3.2)

It is easy to see that

\|Rf\|^{p}_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{0}^{1}|f(t+k)|^{p}\,dt=\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}^{p}.

Put now

f={\mathscr{F}}^{*}q\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{m}(t)=mf(mt),\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

It follows from (
3.2
) that
(Rf_{m})(t)=Q_{m}\big(e^{2\pi{\rm i}t}\big)
,
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Thus,

\|Q_{m}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big|mf(mt)|^{p}\,dt\right)^{1/p}=m^{1-1/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}=m^{1-1/p}\|{\mathscr{F}}q\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}})}.\quad\blacksquare

Corollary 3.2
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
for each
p_{0}\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\sup_{p\in[p_{0},1]}m^{1/p-1}\|Q_{m}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}<\infty.

(3.3)

We can improve now Lemma 4.2 of
[
AP3
]
that shows to what extent the
L^{p}
-quasinorm of a trigonometric polynomial of a given degree can jump when we apply the Riesz projection. The improvement below
gives us an estimate uniform in
p
as
p
approaches 1.

Recall that the Riesz projection

{\mathbb{P}}_{+}
and
{\mathbb{P}}_{-}
on the set of trigonometric polynomials are defined by

({\mathbb{P}}_{+}\psi)(z)=\sum_{j\geq 0}\widehat{\psi}(j)z^{j}\quad\mbox{and}\quad({\mathbb{P}}_{-}\psi)(z)=\sum_{j<0}\widehat{\psi}(j)z^{j}.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
q
be an infinitely differentiable function on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\operatorname{supp}q=[-1,1],\quad q(t)>0\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\in(-1,1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad q(0)=1.

Consider the trigonometric polynomials
Q_{m}
,
m\geq 1
, of degree
m-1
defined by (
3.1
). Then
for each
p_{0}\in(0,1)
, there exists a positive number
d
such that and a sequence of trigonometric polynomials
Q_{m}
,
m\geq 1
, of degree
m-1
such that

\frac{\|{\mathbb{P}}_{+}Q_{m}\|_{L^{p}}}{\|Q_{m}\|_{L^{p}}}\geq dm^{1/p-1}

for every
p\geq p_{0}
.

Proof.
Clearly,

\deg Q_{m}=m-1
and
({\mathbb{P}}_{+}Q_{m})(0)=1
. It is also easy to see that

\|{\mathbb{P}}_{+}Q_{m}\|_{L^{p}}\geq|({\mathbb{P}}_{+}Q_{m})(0)|=1.

Denote the supremum of the left-hand side of (
3.3
) by
s
. Let
p\in[p_{0},1)
.
Then by (
3.3
),

\frac{\|{\mathbb{P}}_{+}Q_{m}\|_{L^{p}}}{\|Q_{m}\|_{L^{p}}}\geq s^{-1}m^{1/p-1}.\quad\blacksquare

Estimates of the
\boldsymbol{p}
-norms in
\boldsymbol{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}

In this section we recall certain facts on Hankel matrices (Hankel operators) that will be used in §
6
. We also obtain sharp
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}

For a function
\varphi
analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
, we denote by
\Gamma_{\varphi}
the
Hankel matrix

\Gamma_{\varphi}=\{\widehat{\varphi}(j+k)\}_{j,k\geq 0},

where
\widehat{\varphi}(j)
is the
j
th Taylor coefficient of
\varphi
. We refer the reader to the book
[
Pe3
]
for basic definitions and properties of Hankel operators.

By Nehari’s theorem, the matrix
\Gamma_{\varphi}
induces a bounded linear operator on the sequence space

if and only if there exists a function
\psi
in
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}})
such that their Fourier coefficients satisfy the equalities
\widehat{\varphi}(j)=\widehat{\psi}(j)
,
j\geq 0
. Here
\widehat{\psi}(j)
is the
j
th Fourier coefficient of
\psi
.

We will need the following criterion for the membership of
\Gamma_{\varphi}
in the Schatten–von Neumann class
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}
:

Let
0<p<\infty
and let
\varphi
be a function analytic in
{\mathbb{D}}
. Then

\Gamma_{\varphi}\in{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\varphi\quad\mbox{belongs to the Besov class}\quad B_{p}^{1/p}.

This was established in

[
Pe1
]
for
p\geq 1
. For
p<1
, this was proved in
[
Pe2
]
, see also
[
Pek
]
and
[
S
]
for alternative proofs. We also refer the reader to the book
[
Pe3
]
, see Ch.6, § 3, in which a detailed presentation of this material is given.

Note that to prove the sufficiency of the condition
\varphi\in B_{p}^{1/p}

for the membership of the Hankel operator
\Gamma_{\varphi}
in
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}

in the case
0<p\leq 1
, the following inequality was established in
[
Pe1
]
and
[
Pe2
]
, see also
the book
[
Pe3
]
, Ch.6, § 3:

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{\varphi}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\leq 2^{1/p-1}m^{1/p}\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}}

(4.1)

for an arbitrary analytic polynomial
\varphi
of degree at most

.

The following fact is an improvement of Theorem 3.1 of
[

]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
0<p_{0}<1
. Then there exists a positive number
d
such that for
p\in[p_{0},1)
and
for a polynomial
\varphi
is of the form

\varphi(z)=\sum_{j=2^{n-1}+1}^{2^{n+1}-1}\widehat{\varphi}(j)z^{j},

the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle d2^{(n+1)/p}\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}}\leq\|\Gamma_{\varphi}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}\leq 2^{(n+1)/p}\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}}.

(4.2)

Proof.
The upper estimate for
\|\Gamma_{\varphi}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}
in (
4.2
) is an immediate consequence of

).

To obtain the lower estimate for

the reasoning given in the proof of Lemma 3.9 of Chapter 6 of the book

]
together with Theorem

.
implies that

\displaystyle 2^{k}\|\varphi*V_{k}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq\operatorname{const}\|\Gamma_{\varphi}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}^{p},\quad k\geq 1.

(4.3)

It follows from the definition of

given in (

) that for

\varphi=\varphi*V_{n-1}+\varphi*V_{n}+\varphi*V_{n+1}.

Thus, we can conclude from (

\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}}^{p}\leq\sum_{j=-1}^{1}\|\varphi*V_{n+j}\|^{p}_{L^{p}}\leq\operatorname{const}\cdot 2^{n+1}\|\Gamma_{\varphi}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}^{p}.

This implies the lower estimate for

in (

).
\blacksquare

Let

and let

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{\varphi}\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{p}}\leq(2m)^{1/p-1}\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}}.

5.
The behaviour of the
\boldsymbol{p}
-norms in
\boldsymbol{H^{p}}
of the analytic Dirichlet kernel

We define the analytic Dirichlet kernel
D_{n}^{+}
by

D_{n}^{+}(z)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}z^{j},\quad n\geq 1.

We will see in §
6
that the
p
-norms
\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{p})}
admit sharp lower and upper estimates in terms of the
p
-norms
\|D_{n}^{+}\|_{H^{p}}
. The following theorem gives us sharp upper and lower estimates for

\|D_{n}^{+}\|_{H^{p}}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq p<1
.
Then

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}(1-e^{-1})^{2}}{4\pi}\min\big\{(1-p)^{-1},\log n\big\}\leq\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}\leq\min\big\{2(1-p)^{-1},\log 5n\big\}.

(5.1)

Proof.
Let us first proceed to the upper estimate for
\|D_{n}^{+}\|_{H^{p}}
.
We have

|D^{+}_{n}(e^{it})|=\frac{|1-e^{int}|}{|1-e^{it}|}=\frac{|\sin nt/2|}{|\sin t/2|}\leq\frac{\pi|\sin nt/2|}{|t|}\quad\text{for}\quad t\in[-\pi,\pi].

Let
p\in(0,1)
. Then

\displaystyle\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\leq\pi^{p-1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{|\sin nt/2|^{p}\,dt}{t^{p}}\leq\pi^{p-1}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{dt}{t^{p}}=\frac{1}{1-p}

(5.2)

and

\displaystyle\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}\leq\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{|\sin nt/2|\,dt}{t}=\int_{0}^{\pi n/2}\frac{|\sin x|\,dx}{x}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\int_{1}^{\pi n/2}\frac{dx}{x}=1+\log(\pi n/2)=\log(e\pi n/2)\leq\log 5n.

Thus, we have proved that

\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\leq\min((1-p)^{-1},(\log 5n)^{p}).

Note that
p\mapsto(1-p)^{1-1/p}
is a a decreasing function on
(0,1)
,

\lim_{p\to 0}(1-p)^{1-1/p}=e
and
\lim_{p\to 1}(1-p)^{1-1/p}=1
.
Hence, (
5.2
) implies

\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}\leq(1-p)^{1-1/p}(1-p)^{-1}\leq e(1-p)^{-1}.

It is also clear that for
p\in\big[\frac{1}{2},1)
,

\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}\leq 2(1-p)^{-1}.

Thus,

\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}\leq\min(2(1-p)^{-1},\log 5n).

Let us obtain now the lower estimate for
\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}
. Clearly,
\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}\geq 1
for all
n\geq 1
and all
p>0
.
Besides,
\|D^{+}_{1}\|_{H^{p}}=1
for all
p>0
.
Suppose now that
n\geq 2
.

We have

|D^{+}_{n}(e^{it})|=\frac{|1-e^{int}|}{|1-e^{it}|}=\frac{|\sin nt/2|}{|\sin t/2|}\geq\frac{2|\sin nt/2|}{|t|}\quad\text{for}\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\geq\frac{2^{p}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{|\sin nt/2|^{p}\,dt}{t^{p}}=\frac{2n^{p-1}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi n/2}\frac{|\sin x|^{p}\,dx}{x^{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n^{p-1}}{\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}(k-1)}^{\frac{\pi}{2}k}\frac{|\sin x|^{p}\,dx}{x^{p}}.

To estimate the integral
\int\limits_{\frac{\pi}{2}(k-1)}^{\frac{\pi}{2}k}\dfrac{dx}{x^{p}}
for given

k\in\mathbb{N}
, we put

E_{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\Big\{x\in\Big[\frac{\pi}{2}(k-1),\frac{\pi}{2}k\Big]:2\sin^{2}x\geq 1\Big\}.

Clearly,
E_{k}
is an interval of length
\pi/4
and

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{\pi}{2}(k-1)}^{\frac{\pi}{2}k}\frac{|\sin x|^{p}\,dx}{x^{p}}\geq 2^{-p/2}\int_{E_{k}}\frac{dx}{x^{p}}\geq 2^{-p/2}\frac{\pi}{4}\min_{x\in E_{k}}x^{-p}\geq 2^{-p/2}\frac{\pi}{4}\Big(\frac{\pi}{2}k\Big)^{-p}=2^{p/2-2}\pi^{1-p}k^{-p}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\geq\frac{2^{p/2-1}n^{p-1}}{\pi^{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{-p}\geq\frac{2^{p/2-1}n^{p-1}}{\pi^{p}}\int_{1}^{n+1}\frac{dt}{t^{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{p/2-1}n^{p-1}}{\pi^{p}(1-p)}((n+1)^{1-p}-1)\geq\frac{2^{p/2-1}(1-n^{p-1})}{\pi^{p}(1-p)}.

If
(1-p)\log n\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\geq\frac{2^{p/2-1}(1-n^{p-1})}{\pi^{p}(1-p)}=\frac{2^{p/2-1}(1-e^{(p-1)\log n})}{\pi^{p}(1-p)}\geq\frac{2^{p/2-1}(1-e^{-1})}{\pi^{p}(1-p)}.

Thus, for
p\in[1/2,1)
we have

\displaystyle\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}\geq\frac{2^{1/2-1/p}(1-e^{-1})^{1/p}}{\pi(1-p)^{1/p}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}(1-e^{-1})^{2}}{4\pi(1-p)^{1/p}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}(1-e^{-1})^{2}}{4\pi(1-p)}

if
(1-p)\log n\geq 1
.

Now let
(1-p)\log n\leq 1
. Then

\displaystyle\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\geq\frac{2^{p/2-1}(1-n^{p-1})}{\pi^{p}(1-p)}=\frac{2^{p/2-1}(1-e^{(p-1)\log n})}{\pi^{p}(1-p)\log n}\log n\geq\frac{2^{p/2-1}(1-e^{-1})\log n}{\pi^{p}}.

Hence, for
p\in[1/2,1]
, we have

\displaystyle\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}\geq\frac{2^{1/2-1/p}(1-e^{-1})^{1/p}(\log n)^{1/p}}{\pi}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}(1-e^{-1})^{2}}{4\pi}\log n.\quad\blacksquare

6.
The behaviour of
\boldsymbol{\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{p})}}
versus the behavior of
\boldsymbol{\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}}

In this section we reduce the estimation of
\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{p})}
to the estimation of
\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}
.
The following theorem is the main result of the section.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq p<1
. Then there exists a positive number
c
such that

\displaystyle cn^{1/p-1}\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}\leq\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{p})}=\|\chi_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{p}}=\|\Delta_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{p}}\leq(2n)^{1/p-1}\|D^{+}_{n}\|_{H^{p}}.

(6.1)

Proof.
The upper estimate in (
6.1
) for
\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{p})}
for all
p\in(0,1)
was mentioned in
[
AP3
]
and follows immediately from (
4.4
).

It suffices to establish the lower estimate for
n=2^{k}
. This is clear from (
5.1
).

Let us proceed to the lower estimate for
\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{p})}
in (
6.1
). Let
q
be a nonnegative infinitely differentiable even function on
{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\operatorname{supp}q=[-1,1],\quad q(t)>0\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\in(-1,1)

and

\displaystyle q(t)+q(t-1)=1\quad\mbox{for}\quad t\in[0,1].

(6.2)

Consider the trigonometric polynomials
Q_{m}
defined by (
3.1
).

It is more convenient for our purpose to consider instead of the Riesz projection
{\mathbb{P}}_{+}
the projection

{\mathbb{P}}^{+}
defined on the class of trigonometric polynomials by

({\mathbb{P}}^{+}\psi)(z)=\sum_{j\geq 1}\widehat{\psi}(j)z^{j}.

It follows immediately from (
6.2
)
that

\displaystyle\bar{z}{\mathbb{P}}^{+}Q_{m+1}+z^{m}{\mathbb{P}}_{-}Q_{m+1}=D_{m}^{+}.

(6.3)

Put

P_{k}(z)=z^{2^{k}}Q_{2^{k-1}}(z),\quad k\geq 1,

P_{k}^{-}=z^{2^{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{-}Q_{2^{k-1}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad P_{k}^{+}=z^{2^{k}}{\mathbb{P}}^{+}Q_{2^{k-1}}.

It is easy to see that

P_{k}^{-}=P_{k}*D^{+}_{2^{k}}.

Thus,

\Gamma_{P_{k}^{-}}=\Gamma_{P_{k}}\star\Delta_{2^{k}-1}.

Let
m=2^{k-1}-1
. We have by (
6.3
)

\displaystyle P_{k}^{+}+z^{2^{k-1}}P_{k}^{-}=z^{2^{k}+1}D^{+}_{2^{k-1}-1}.

(6.4)

It follows from the fact that
q
is an even function that

\|P_{k}^{+}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}=\|P_{k}^{-}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}

and so by (
6.4
),

\displaystyle\|D^{+}_{2^{k-1}-1}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\leq\|P_{k}^{+}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}+\|P_{k}^{-}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}=2\|P_{k}^{-}\|_{H^{p}}^{p}.

(6.5)

By Theorem
4.1
, there exists a positive number
d
such that
inequalities (
4.2
) hold for
\varphi=P_{k}
and
\varphi=P_{k}^{-}
.

Thus,

\|\Delta_{2^{k}}\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{p}}\geq\operatorname{const}\frac{\|\Gamma_{P_{k}^{-}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}}{\|\Gamma_{P_{k}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}}\geq\operatorname{const}\frac{\|P_{k}^{-}\|_{H^{p}}}{\|P_{k}\|_{H^{p}}}.

Applying (
6.5
), we obtain

\|\Delta_{2^{k}}\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{p}}\geq\operatorname{const}\frac{\|D^{+}_{2^{k-1}}\|_{H^{p}}}{\|P_{k}\|_{H^{p}}}\geq\operatorname{const}2^{k(1/p-1)}\|D^{+}_{2^{k-1}}\|_{H^{p}}

by Theorem
3.1
. It remains to observe that

\|D^{+}_{2^{k-1}}\|_{H^{p}}\geq\operatorname{const}\|D^{+}_{2^{k}}\|_{H^{p}}

which follows easily from Theorem
5.1
.
This completes the proof.
\blacksquare

7.
The main result

We are ready now to state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\frac{1}{2}\leq p<1
. Then there exists a positive number
c
such that

\displaystyle cn^{1/p-1}\min\big\{(1-p)^{-1},\log n\big\}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{p})}=\|\chi_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{p}}=\|\Delta_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{M}_{p}}

\displaystyle\leq(2n)^{1/p-1}\min\big\{2(1-p)^{-1},\log 5n\big\}.

Clearly, Theorem
7.1
is an immediate consequence of Theorems
6.1

and
5.1
.

Let now
n\geq 3
and
1-(\log n)^{-1}\leq p<1
. Then there exist positive numbers
k
and
K
such that

k\log n\leq\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{p})}\leq K\log n

which implies the well known fact that

k\log n\leq\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{1})}\leq K\log n.

Remark.
We have observed in
[
AP3
]
that if we consider the triangular projection
{\mathscr{P}}_{n}^{\mathscr{K}}
on the space of
n\times n
block matrices whose entries are bounded linear operators on a Hilbert space
{\mathscr{K}}
, then

\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}^{\mathscr{K}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}\to{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}=\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}\to{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}},

and so in the statement of Theorem
7.1
we can replace
\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}\to{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}
by

\|{\mathscr{P}}_{n}^{\mathscr{K}}\|_{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}\to{\boldsymbol{S}}_{p}}
.

CONSTRICTING ELEMENTS AND THE GROWTH OF QUASI-CONVEX SUBGROUPS
by Xabier LEGASPI

Abstract




Abstract
. —
Given a group
G
acting on a geodesic metric space, we consider a preferred collection of paths of the space – a path system – and study the spectrum of relative exponential growth rates and quotient exponential growth rates of the infinite index subgroups of
G
which are quasi-convex with respect to this path system. If
G
contains a constricting element with respect to the same path system, we are able to determine when the first kind of growth rates are strictly smaller than the growth rate of
G
, and when the second kind of growth rates coincide with the growth rate of
G
. Examples of applications include relatively hyperbolic groups,
CAT(0)
groups and hierarchically hyperbolic groups containing a Morse element.

1

Introduction

Recall that an isometric action of a group
G
on a metric space
(X,d)
is
proper
if any
ball

B_{G}(x,r)
of
G
based at
x\in X
of radius
r\geqslant 0
is finite, where

B_{G}(x,r)=\{g\in G\colon d(x,gx)\leqslant r\}.

Let
o\in X
. Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. The
relative exponential growth rate
of
H
is

\omega_{H}=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}log|B_{G}(o,r)\cap H|.

The
quotient exponential growth rates
of
H
are

\omega_{G/H}=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}log|\mathcal{L}(B_{G}(o,r))|,\quad\omega_{H\backslash G}=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r}log|\mathcal{R}(B_{G}(o,r))|,

where
\mathcal{L}\colon G\twoheadrightarrow G/H
and
\mathcal{R}\colon G\twoheadrightarrow H\backslash G
are the natural maps respectively on the set of left cosets
G/H
and on the set of right cosets
H\backslash G
. Note that the numbers
\omega_{H}
,
\omega_{G/H}
and
\omega_{H\backslash G}
do not depend on the choice of the point
o\in X
. The original motivation for our work was the following problem:

Problem 1.1
. —

Determine the groups
G
acting properly on metric spaces
X
and subgroups
H
of
G
which are solution to the system of equations

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega_{H}<\omega_{G},\\
\omega_{G/H}=\omega_{H\backslash G}=\omega_{G}.\end{cases}

We see from the definition that
0\leqslant\max\,\{\omega_{H},\omega_{G/H},\omega_{H\backslash G}\}\leqslant\omega_{G}
. In the extreme case in which
H
is a finite index subgroup of
G
, one can easily prove that

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega_{H}=\omega_{G},\\
\omega_{G/H}=\omega_{H\backslash G}=0\leqslant\omega_{G}.\end{cases}

In general, it is very hard to obtain any estimation of the relative and quotient exponential growth rates of the infinite index subgroups of
G
. However, we know
[
DFW19
]
,
[
Ant21
]
,
[
GR20
]
that if
G
is a non-virtually cyclic group acting geometrically on a hyperbolic space
X
and
H
is an infinite index quasi-convex subgroup of
G
, then

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega_{H}<\omega_{G},\\
\omega_{G/H}=\omega_{H\backslash G}=\omega_{G}.\end{cases}

In particular, the authors of
[
DFW19
]
and
[
Ant21
]
were highly influenced by J. Cannon’s arguments based on automatic structures and regular languages. This fact also influenced others that partially extended the hyperbolic result
[
CRSZ20
]
.

In this article we go beyond the hyperbolic case and we obtain elementary proofs of two main results (
section 1
and
section 1
) that do not require the theory of regular languages and automatas. We will be interested in groups acting properly on geodesic metric spaces conditioned by a very general notion of <<non-positive curvature>> introduced by A. Sisto in
[
Sis18
]
—
containing a constricting element with respect to a path system
— while the infinite index subgroups object of our study will satisfy a very general notion of <<convex cocompactness>> —
quasi-convexity with respect to a path system
.

First of all, we will mention two applications. Later we will give an informal explanation of our general setting as the result of a natural generalisation of these applications. We expect that this will be enough to understand our main theorems stated right after that. We will give another application at the end.

(A) Groups acting properly with a strongly contracting element.
Members of this class contain elements that “behave like” a loxodromic isometry in a hyperbolic space – in a strong way. Let
\delta\geqslant 0
. A
subset

A
of
X
is
\delta
-
strongly contracting
if the diameter of the nearest-point projection on
A
of any ball of
X
not intersecting
A
is less than
\delta
. An
element

g
of
G
is
\delta
-
strongly contracting
if it has infinite order and there exists
o\in X
such that the orbit
\langle g\rangle o
is
\delta
-strongly contracting.

In his seminal paper M. Gromov introduced the concept of
\delta
-hyperbolic space
[
Gro87
]
. He observed that most of the large scale features of negative curvature can be described in terms of thin triangles. Nowadays, there are plenty of reformulations of the
\delta
-hyperbolicity. In particular, H. Masur and Y. Minsky gave one by describing geodesics in terms of strong contraction:

Example 1.2
. —

A geodesic metric space
X
is hyperbolic if and only if there exists
\delta\geqslant 0
such that any geodesic segment of
X
is
\delta
-strongly contracting
[
MM99
, Theorem 2.3]
.

The following are some subclasses of groups acting properly with a strongly contracting element:

(I)

\mathbf{H}=
“
G
is a group acting properly with a loxodromic element on a hyperbolic space
X
.” In
\mathbf{H}
, an element is loxodromic if and only if it is strongly contracting. See
[
CDP90
]
.

(II)

\mathbf{RH}=
“
G
is a relatively hyperbolic group acting with a hyperbolic element on a locally finite Cayley graph
X
of
G
.” In
\mathbf{RH}
, hyperbolic elements are strongly contracting. See
[

and
[

.

(III)

\mathbf{CAT_{0}}=
“
G
is a group acting properly with a rank-one element on a proper

space

.” In
\mathbf{CAT_{0}}
, rank-one elements are strongly contracting. See
[

, Theorem 5.4]
,
[

]

[

.

(IV)

\mathbf{MCG_{T}}=
“

3g+p-4>0
acting on its Teichmüller space endowed with the Teichmüller metric.” In

(V)

\mathbf{GSC}=
“

of

with respect to

(VI)

\mathbf{Gar}=
“

is the quotient of a

-pure Garside group of finite type by its center, acting with a Morse element on the Cayley graph

of

with respect to the Garside generating set.” In

[

.

An appropriate notion of convex cocompactness in this setting is just the usual quasi-convexity. A
subgroup

H
of
G
is
quasi-convex
if there exist
o\in X
and
\eta\geqslant 0
such that any geodesic of
X
with endpoints in

Theorem 1.3
. —

If

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega_{H}<\omega_{G},\\

(B) Hierarchically hyperbolic groups.
Let
MCG(\Sigma_{g,p})
be the mapping class group of an orientable surface
\Sigma_{g,p}
of genus
g
and

marked points of complexity

to

acts with a strongly contracting element on any of its locally finite Cayley graphs or not. Maybe the candidates that come to mind are the pseudo-Anosov elements, and evidence suggest that not all of them are strongly contracting: K. Rafi and Y. Verberne have constructed a generating set

The reader should note that the metric space where they act with a hierarchically hyperbolic structure is any of their locally finite Cayley graphs:

(I)

Mapping class groups of finite type surfaces

]
.

(II)

Right-angled Artin groups
[

.

(III)

(IV)

Now consider the following notion of convex cocompactness. A
subgroup

of

if there exists

,

-quasi-geodesic of

Theorem 1.4
. —

If
G
is a non-virtually cyclic hierarchically hyperbolic group acting on a locally finite Cayley graph

is an infinite index Morse subgroup of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega_{H}<\omega_{G},\\
\omega_{G/H}=\omega_{H\backslash G}=\omega_{G}.\end{cases}

We know that pseudo-Anosov elements of mapping class groups are Morse with respect to any word metric
[

]
, which allows us to obtain a more concrete statement:

Corollary 1.5
. —

If
G
is the mapping class group of a surface of genus

acting on a locally finite Cayley graph
X
of
G
, and
H
is an infinite index Morse subgroup of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\omega_{H}<\omega_{G},\\
\omega_{G/H}=\omega_{H\backslash G}=\omega_{G}.\end{cases}

Now that we gave the big picture, we will give a technical definition that encapsulates the classes discussed so far. In order to do so, we make two observations. On the one hand, the strong contraction property can be reformulated in the following way. An element
g
of
G
is strongly contracting if and only if it has infinite order and there exists
o\in X
such that any geodesic segment of
X
joining any pair of points
x,y\in X
whose projections
\pi_{g}(x)
and
\pi_{g}(y)
via a nearest-point projection
\pi_{g}\colon X\to\langle g\rangle o
are far away passes near these two projections
[
ACT15
, Proposition 2.9]
. On the other hand, mapping class groups – or more generally, hierarchically hyperbolic groups – come with hierarchy paths, a family of special quasi-geodesics encoding substantial information about the geometry of the space and easier to work with than the set of all (quasi-)geodesics. For these reasons, in order to define very general notions of non-positive curvature and convex cocompactness, we will be considering path systems, introduced by A. Sisto in
[
Sis18
]
:

Definition 1.6

(Path system group)
. —

Let
\mu\geqslant 1
,
\nu\geqslant 0
. A
(\mu,\nu)
-
path system group

(G,X,\Gamma)
is a group
G
acting properly on a geodesic metric space
X
together with a
G
-invariant collection
\Gamma
of paths of
X
satisfying:

(PS1)

\Gamma
is closed under taking subpaths.

(PS2)

For every
x,y\in X
, there exists
\gamma\in\Gamma
joining
x
to
y
.

(PS3)

Every element of
\Gamma
is a
(\mu,\nu)
-quasi-geodesic.

We refer to
\Gamma
as
(\mu,\nu)
-
path system
.

We fix
\mu\geqslant 1
,
\nu\geqslant 0
and a
(\mu,\nu)
-path system group
(G,X,\Gamma)
for the following definitions. Let
\delta\geqslant 0
. We say that an element
g
of
G
is
\delta
-
constricting
if it has infinite order and there exist
o\in X
and a coarse nearest-point projection
\pi_{g}\colon X\to\langle g\rangle o
with the property that any
\gamma\in\Gamma
joining any two points
x,y\in X
such that
d(\pi_{g}(x),\pi_{g}(y))>\delta
passes through the
\delta
-neighbourhoods of the points
\pi_{g}(x)
and
\pi_{g}(y)
. See
section 2
. In particular we have the following examples:

Example 1.7
. —

(I)

An infinite order element of
G
is strongly contracting if and only if it is constricting with respect to the set of all the geodesic segments of
X

[
ACT15
, Proposition 2.9]
.

(II)

Assume that
G
is also hierarchically hyperbolic. An infinite order element
g
of
G
is Morse if and only if for every
\kappa\geqslant 1
, there exists
\delta\geqslant 0
such that
g
is
\delta
-constricting with respect to the set of all the
\kappa
-hierarchy paths. See
[
RST21
, Theorem E]
and
[
BHS20
, Lemma 1.27]
.

Let
\eta\geqslant 0
. A subgroup
H
of
G
is
\eta
-
quasi-convex
if there exists
o\in X
such that any
\gamma\in\Gamma
with endpoints in
Ho
is contained in the
\eta
-neighbourhood of
Ho
. See

.

Finally, we state the main results of this article.
section 1
and
section 1
are special cases. Our first result generalises
[
Yan19
, Theorem 4.8]
and
[
DFW19
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3]
. The
Poincaré series

\mathcal{P}_{H}(s)
based at
o\in X
of a subgroup
H
of
G
is defined as

\forall\,s\geqslant 0,\quad\mathcal{P}_{H}(s)=\sum_{h\in H}e^{-sd(o,ho)}

and modifies its behaviour at the relative exponential growth rate
\omega_{H}
: the series diverges if
s<\omega_{H}
and converges if
s>\omega_{H}
. In
s=\omega_{G}
the series can converge or diverge depending on the nature of
H
. This behaviour is independent of the point
o\in X
. We say that the subgroup
H
is
divergent
if
\mathcal{P}_{H}(s)
diverges at
s=\omega_{H}
.

Theorem 1.8
. —

Let
(G,X,\Gamma)
be a path system group. Assume that
G
contains a constricting element. Let
H
be an infinite index subgroup of
G
. Assume that the following conditions are true:

(I)

\omega_{H}<\infty
.

(II)

H
is divergent.

(III)

H
is quasi-convex.

Then
\omega_{H}<\omega_{G}
.

We make the following remark. Under the hypothesis of
section 1
, one may ask if there is a growth gap

\sup_{H}\omega_{H}<\omega_{G},

where the supremum is taken among the infinite index subgroups
H
of
G
satisfying (I), (II) and (III). In our context, the answer is yes: there is a growth gap when
G
is a hyperbolic group with Kazhdan’s property (T)
[
CDST18
, Theorem 1.2]
. However, one can show that there is no growth gap among free groups
[
DFW19
, Theorem 9.4]
or fundamental groups of compact special cube complexes
[
LW20
, Theorem 1.5]
. The answer to our context could be different if one studied semigroups instead of subgroups
[
Yan19
, Theorem A]
.

In
[
Gro87
, 5.3.C]
, M. Gromov stated that in a torsion-free hyperbolic group
G
, any infinite index quasi-convex subgroup
H
is a free factor of a larger quasi-convex subgroup. Gromov’s ideas were later developed by G. N. Arzhantseva in
[
Arz01
, Theorem 1]
. More recently, J. Russell, D. Spriano and H. C. Tran generalised her result to the context of groups with the <<Morse local-to-global property>>
[
RST19
, Corollary 3.5]
. In our context, we have obtained the following, in which there is no torsion-free assumption. We will see that
section 1
is, in part, a consequence of this result:

Theorem 1.9
. —

Let
(G,X,\Gamma)
be a path system group. Assume that
G
contains a constricting element
g_{0}
. Let
H
be an infinite index quasi-convex subgroup of
G
. There exist an element
g\in G
conjugate to a large power of
g_{0}
and a finite extension
E
of
\langle g\rangle
such that the intersection
H\cap E
is finite and the natural morphism
H\ast_{H\cap E}\langle g,H\cap E\rangle\to G
is injective.

According to our
section 2
(6), the subgroup generated by a constricting element is always Morse, and in particular quasi-convex. Hence we obtain a weak version of the Tits alternative:

Corollary 1.10
. —

Let
(G,X,\Gamma)
be a path system group. Assume that
G
contains a constricting element. Then, either
G
is virtually cyclic or contains a free subgroup of rank two.

To the best of our knowledge, the previous corollary has not been recorded for the class of groups acting properly with a strongly contracting element. Besides, the Tits alternative is known for hierarchically hyperbolic groups
[
DHS17
, Theorem 9.15]
.

In our second result we generalise
[
Ant21
, Theorem 3]
and
[
GR20
, Theorem 2]

Theorem 1.11
. —

Let
(G,X,\Gamma)
be a path system group. Assume that
G

2

Path system geometry

This section is devoted to present the notations and vocabulary of the main geometric objects of this article. We formalise our notions of <<convex-cocompactness>> and <<non-positive curvature>>.

2.1. Metric spaces. —
Let
X
be a metric space. Given two points
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we write
d(x,x^{\prime})
for the distance between them. The
ball of

X
of center
x\in X
and radius
r\geqslant 0
is defined as

B_{X}(x,r)=\{y\in X\colon\,d(x,y)\leqslant r\}.

The
distance between a point

x\in X

and a subset

Y\subseteq X
is defined as

d(x,Y)=\inf\,\{d(x,y)\colon\,y\in Y\}.

Let
\eta\geqslant 0
. The
\eta
-
neighbourhood
of a subset
Y\subseteq X
is defined as

Y^{+\eta}=\{x\in X\colon\,d(x,Y)\leqslant\eta\}.

The

Growth estimation criteria

In this section, we fix a group
G
acting properly on a metric space
X
and a subgroup
H\leqslant G
. The goal is to establish simple criteria so that we can check if
H
is a solution to the system of equations

\begin{cases}\omega_{H}<\omega_{G},\\
\omega_{G/H}=\omega_{H\backslash G}=\omega_{G}.\end{cases}

Our criterion to estimate the relative exponential growth rate is basically
[
DPPS11
, Criterion 2.4]
. The statement that we actually need is more specific, so we will give a proof for the convenience of the reader. Recall that a subgroup
H\leqslant G
is
divergent
if its Poincaré series
\mathcal{P}_{H}(s)
diverges at
s=\omega_{H}
.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
DPPS11
, Criterion 2.4]
)
. —

Assume that the following conditions are true:

(I)

\omega_{H}<\infty
.

(II)

H
is divergent.

(III)

There exist subgroups
K\leqslant G
and
F\leqslant H\cap K
so that
F
is a proper finite subgroup of
K
and the natural morphism
\phi\colon H\ast_{F}K\to G
is injective.

Then
\omega_{H}<\omega_{G}
.

Remark 3.2
. —

In the proof below, note that the relative exponential growth rate makes sense for any subset of
G
, as it does the notion of Poincaré series.

Since
H
is divergent, in particular it is infinite and hence
H-F
is non-empty. Since
F
is a proper subgroup of
K
, there exists
k\in K-F
. Denote by
U
the set of elements of
H\ast_{F}K
that can be written as words that alternate elements of
H-F
and
k
, always with an element of
H-F
at the beginning and with a
k
at the end. The inequality
\omega_{\phi(U)}\leqslant\omega_{G}
can be deduced from the definition. It is enough to prove that there exists
s_{0}\geqslant 0
such that
\omega_{H}<s_{0}\leqslant\omega_{\phi(U)}
. Let
o\in X
. Since
\omega_{H}<\infty
, the interval
(\omega_{H},\infty)
is non-empty. Since
H
is divergent, there exists
s_{0}\in(\omega_{H},\infty)
such that
\sum_{h\in H-F}e^{-s_{0}d(o,hko)}>1
; otherwise one obtains a contradiction with the divergence of
H
.

In order to obtain the inequality
s_{0}\leqslant\omega_{\phi(U)}
, it suffices to show that the Poincaré series
\mathcal{P}_{\phi(U)}(s)=\sum_{g\in\phi(U)}e^{-sd(o,go)}
diverges at
s=s_{0}
. Since
\phi\colon H\ast_{F}K\to G
is injective, we have

\mathcal{P}_{\phi(U)}(s)\geqslant\sum_{m\geqslant 1}\sum_{h_{1},\cdots,h_{m}\in H-F}e^{-sd(o,h_{1}kh_{2}k\cdots h_{m}ko)}.

By the triangle inequality, for every
m\geqslant 1
and for every
h_{1},\cdots,h_{m}\in H-F
, we have
d(o,h_{1}kh_{2}k\cdots h_{m}ko)\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{m}d(o,h_{i}ko)
. Thus,

\sum_{h_{1},\cdots,h_{m}\in H-F}e^{-sd(o,h_{1}kh_{2}k\cdots h_{m}ko)}\geqslant\left[\sum_{h\in H-F}e^{-sd(o,hko)}\right]^{m}.

We see that
\mathcal{P}(s_{0})=\infty
follows from the claim.
∎

We continue with the quotient exponential growth rates. First, observe this fact:

Proposition 3.3
. —

We have
\omega_{G/H}=\omega_{H\backslash G}
.

Let
\mathcal{L}\colon G\twoheadrightarrow G/H
and
\mathcal{R}\colon G\twoheadrightarrow H\backslash G
be the natural maps. Let
x\in X
and

. We recall that

\mathcal{L}(B_{G}(x,r))=\{gH\in G/H\colon\,d(x,gx)\leqslant r\},

\mathcal{R}(B_{G}(x,r))=\{Hg\in H\backslash G\colon\,d(x,gx)\leqslant r\}.

Note that for every
g\in G
, we have
gH\in\mathcal{L}(B_{G}(x,r))
if and only if
Hg^{-1}\in\mathcal{R}(B_{G}(x,r))
. Hence
|\mathcal{L}(B_{G}(x,r))|=|\mathcal{R}(B_{G}(x,r))|
. Therefore
\omega_{G/H}=\omega_{H\backslash G}
.

Definition 3.4
. —

Let
\phi\colon G\to G
. We say that
G
is
\phi
-

G/H
if there exist
\theta\geqslant 0
and
x\in X
satisfying the following conditions:

(CQ1)

For every

\phi(u)H=\phi(v)H
, then
d(\phi(u)x,\phi(v)x)\leqslant\theta
.

(CQ2)

For every
u\in G
,
d(ux,\phi(u)x)\leqslant\theta
.

Proposition 3.5
. —

If there exist
\phi\colon G\to G
such that
G
is
\phi
-coarsely
G/H
, then
\omega_{G}=\omega_{G/H}
.

The inequality
\omega_{G/H}\leqslant\omega_{G}
can be deduced from the defintion. Assume that there exist
\phi\colon G\to G
such that
G
is
\phi
-coarsely
G/H
for

and
\theta\geqslant 0
.

Claim
. —

There exist
\kappa\geqslant 1
such that for every

,

|B_{G}(x,r)|\leqslant\kappa|\mathcal{L}(B_{G}(x,r+\theta))|.

Let
\kappa=|B_{G}(x,3\theta)|
. Let
r\geqslant 0
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{L}}\colon G\rightarrow G/H,u\mapsto\phi(u)H
. Note that the ball

can be decomposed as the disjoint union of the sets
\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}(\hat{\mathcal{L}}(u))
such that
\hat{\mathcal{L}}(u)\in\hat{\mathcal{L}}(B_{G}(x,r))
. Hence,

|B_{G}(x,r)|\leqslant\sum_{\hat{\mathcal{L}}(u)\in\hat{\mathcal{L}}(B_{G}(x,r))}|\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}(\hat{\mathcal{L}}(u))|.

It suffices to estimate the size of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}(B_{G}(x,r))
and the size of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}(\hat{\mathcal{L}}(u))
, for every

. First we prove that
|\hat{\mathcal{L}}(B_{G}(x,r))|\leqslant|\mathcal{L}(B_{G}(x,r+\theta))|
. Let
u\in B_{G}(x,r)
. By the triangle inequality,

d(x,\phi(u)x)\leqslant d(x,ux)+d(ux,\phi(u)x).

By the hypothesis (CQ2), we have
d(ux,\phi(u)x)\leqslant\theta
. Hence
d(x,\phi(u)x)\leqslant r+\theta
. Consequently,
\hat{\mathcal{L}}(B_{G}(x,r))\subseteq\mathcal{L}(B_{G}(x,r+\theta))
. Now we prove that for every
u\in G
, we have
|\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{-1}(\hat{\mathcal{L}}(u))|\leqslant\kappa

u\in G

\displaystyle d(x,u^{-1}vx)=d(ux,vx)\leqslant d(ux,\phi(u)x)+d(\phi(u)x,\phi(v)x)+d(\phi(v)x,vx).

\hat{\mathcal{L}}(u)=\hat{\mathcal{L}}(v)

4

Buffering sequences

In this section, we fix constants
\mu\geqslant 1
,
\nu\geqslant 0
and a
(\mu,\nu)
-path system space
(X,\Gamma)
. Despite the fact that our space
X
does not carry any global geometric condition, we still can obtain some control through constricting subsets. We could ignore the <<wild regions>> if, for instance, we were able to <<jump>> from one constricting subset to another. The buffering sequences below encapsulate this idea. In fact, the proofs of our main results consist essentially in building up some particular buffering sequences. W. Yang had already introduced this concept for piece-wise geodesics in
[
Yan19
]
.

Definition 4.1
. —

Let
\delta\geqslant 0
,
\varepsilon\geqslant 0
,
L\geqslant 0
. Let
\mathbf{A}
be a collection of subsets of
X
. A finite sequence of subsets
Y_{0},A_{1},Y_{1},\cdots,A_{n},Y_{n}\subseteq X
where
Y_{0}
and
Y_{n}
are the only possible empty sets is
(\delta,\varepsilon,L)
-
buffering on

\mathbf{A}
if for every
i\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
the set
A_{i}
belongs to
\mathbf{A}
and there exists a
\delta
-constricting map
\pi_{A_{i}}\colon X\to A_{i}
with the following properties whenever
Y_{i}
and
Y_{i-1}
are non-empty:

(BS1)

\max\{\operatorname{diam}_{A_{i}}(A_{i+1}),\operatorname{diam}_{A_{i+1}}(A_{i})\}\leqslant\varepsilon
if
i\neq n
.

(BS2)

\max\,\{\operatorname{diam}_{A_{i}}(Y_{i-1}),\operatorname{diam}_{A_{i}}(Y_{i})\}\leqslant\varepsilon
.

(BS3)

\max\,\{d(A_{i},Y_{i-1}),d(A_{i},Y_{i})\}\leqslant\varepsilon
.

(BS4)

d_{A_{i}}(Y_{i-1},Y_{i})\geqslant L
.

Figure 2:
An example of a
buffering sequence in the Poincaré disk model. In this example, the sets
A_{i}
are subpaths of length
\geqslant L
of a given bi-infinite geodesic
\alpha
. Each set
Y_{i}
is the collection of geodesics that are orthogonal to the geodesic segment of
\alpha
that is between
A_{i}
and
A_{i+1}
. In particular, the sets
Y_{i}
are convex. For more intuition, one could interpret this picture on a tree.

What makes buffering sequences remarkable is that they satisfy a variant of
Behrstock inequality
. We will find a direct application of the following inequality later in the study of the quotient exponential growth rates:

Proposition 4.2
. —

For every
\delta\geqslant 0
,
\varepsilon\geqslant 0
, there exists
\theta\geqslant 0
with the following property. Let

be a
(\delta,\varepsilon,0)

\min\,\{d_{A}(x,Y),d_{B}(x,Y)\}\leqslant\theta.

Let
\delta\geqslant 0
,
\varepsilon\geqslant 0
. Let
\theta_{0}=\theta_{0}(\delta)\geqslant 0
be the constant of
section 2
. Let
\theta\gg\theta_{0}+1
. Its exact value will be precised below. Let
A,Y,B\subseteq X
be a
(\delta,\varepsilon,0)

\{A,B\}
. Let
x\in X
. By symmetry, it suffices to show that if
d_{A}(x,Y)>\theta
, then
d_{B}(x,Y)\leqslant\theta
. Assume that
d_{A}(x,Y)>\theta
. Let
a\in A
such that
d_{B}(x,a)\leqslant d_{B}(x,A)+1
. Let
b\in B
. Let

. By (BS3), we have
\max\,\{d(A,Y),d(B,Y)\}\leqslant\varepsilon

\max\,\{d_{A}(b,\pi_{B}(q)),d_{A}(q,p),d_{B}(a,\pi_{A}(p)),d_{B}(p,y)\}\leqslant\varepsilon.

Coarse nearest-point projection

Coarse Lipschitz map

Claim

d_{A}(x,B)>\theta_{0}

By the triangle inequality,

d_{A}(x,b)\geqslant d_{A}(x,p)-d_{A}(b,\pi_{B}(q))-d_{A}(\pi_{B}(q),q)-d_{A}(q,p).

Moreover,

\delta\geqslant 0

Quasi-convexity in the Intersection–Image property

In this section, we fix constants

,
\nu\geqslant 0

Proposition 5.1

For every

,

\varepsilon_{1}\geqslant 0
,

\max\,\{d_{A}(x,x^{\prime}),d_{A}(y,y^{\prime})\}\leqslant\mu(\varepsilon_{2}+1)+\theta_{0}.

Given a torsion-free hyperbolic group

\mu\geqslant 1

(\mu,\nu)

Definition 6.1

(Quasi-convex element)

Let
\eta\geqslant 0

(QE1)

\eta

.

\eta
-quasi-convex subset

The main result of this section is the following.

Proposition 6.2
. —

Let
\eta\geqslant 0
. Assume that

\eta
-quasi-convex element

(H,Y)

\eta
-quasi-convex subgroup of

. Then:

(I)

For every

\operatorname{diam}(uA\cap Y)>\theta

(II)

Remark 6.3

Under the notation of (II), when

The rest of the section is devoted to the proof of

Definition 6.4
. —

Let

\frac{1}{\kappa}d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})-\lambda\leqslant d_{Z}(\phi(y),\phi(y^{\prime}))\leqslant\kappa d_{Y}(y,y^{\prime})+\lambda.

We start with a variant of Milnor-Schwarz Theorem. If
U

Lemma 6.5

For every
\eta\geqslant 0

Lemma 6.6

\max\,\{d(\pi_{uA}(ufy),uf\pi_{A}(y)),d(uf\pi_{A}(y^{\prime}),\pi_{uA}(ufy^{\prime}))\}\leqslant\theta_{0}.

Since the element
y^{\prime}
is arbitrary, we obtain
d_{uA}(y,ufY)>\theta
.
∎

(\delta,\varepsilon,L)

section 4

above, for every

\displaystyle\pi_{u_{i}A}(u_{i+1}A)

\displaystyle=\pi_{v_{i}A}(u_{i+1}A),

\displaystyle\pi_{u_{i+1}A}(u_{i}A)

\displaystyle=\pi_{u_{i+1}A}(v_{i}A).

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}_{v_{i}A}(u_{i+1}A)

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}_{u_{i+1}A}(v_{i}A)

\displaystyle\operatorname{diam}_{A}(h_{i}^{-1}A)

\displaystyle\pi_{u_{i}A}(v_{i-1}Y)

\displaystyle\pi_{u_{i}A}(v_{i}Y)

\max\,\{d(u_{i}A,v_{i-1}Y),d(u_{i}A,v_{i}Y)\}=\max\,\{d(u_{i}A,u_{i}Y),d(v_{i}A,v_{i}Y)\}\leqslant d(A,Y)\leqslant\varepsilon.

Quantum state transfer between twins in weighted graphs
Stephen Kirkland, Hermie Monterde, Sarah Plosker

Abstract
Twin vertices in simple unweighted graphs are vertices that have the same neighbours and, in the case of weighted graphs with possible loops, the corresponding incident edges have equal weights. In this paper, we explore the role of twin vertices in quantum state transfer. In particular, we provide characterizations of periodicity, perfect state transfer, and pretty good state transfer between twin vertices in a weighted graph with respect to its adjacency, Laplacian and signless Laplacian matrices. As an application, we provide characterizations of all simple unweighted double cones on regular graphs that exhibit periodicity, perfect state transfer, and pretty good state transfer.



Keywords:
quantum state transfer, twin vertices, adjacency matrix, Laplacian matrix, signless Laplacian matrix

The graphs

For instance, there are infinitely many double cones known to exhibit adjacency and Laplacian perfect state transfer between their apexes (which are twins)
[

,
1

[

[

In this paper, we provide a systematic approach to analyzing the properties of quantum state transfer between twin vertices in weighted graphs with possible loops. In particular, we focus on connected graphs. In the case of a disconnected graph, our results may be applied to its connected components.

In Section
2
, we use the algebraic properties of graphs with twins to show that their transition matrices have a certain form. We then use this to provide an upper bound on the probability of state transfer between twins

and

3

The results in this section may prove to be of independent interest in graph theory; however, we are only interested in building up the machinery to apply to our work on quantum state transfer between twins herein. The Section
4
is devoted to a characterization of periodicity in twin vertices. We highlight our result in this section regarding the exact minimum period of a periodic vertex in a weighted graph with possible loops. In particular, we show that if the size of the eigenvalue support of a periodic vertex is at least three, then its minimum period exceeds
2\pi/(\lambda-\mu)
, where
\lambda
and
\mu
are the largest and smallest eigenvalues in the eigenvalue support. This result implies that the bound on the minimum period provided by Godsil
[
27

is tight if and only if the size of the eigenvalue support is two. Section
5

In particular, we provide a characterization of perfect state transfer between twins, which then allows us to identify all connected and disconnected double cones on regular graphs whose apexes admit adjacency and signless Laplacian perfect state transfer. These double cones provide infinitely many examples of graphs that exhibit perfect state transfer and pretty good state transfer between twin vertices. Lastly, in Section
6

The remainder of the present section is allotted to basic definitions and notation.

Let
X
be a connected weighted graph with possible loops. Since
M
is real symmetric, we can write

M=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}E_{j},

(1)

in its spectral decomposition, where the
\lambda_{j}

is the orthogonal projection matrix onto the eigenspace associated with
\lambda_{j}
. If the eigenvalues are not indexed, then we also denote by

the orthogonal projection matrix corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda

. The matrix

X
. Taking the exponential of

yields the following transition matrix

U(t)=e^{itM}

(2)

of the
(continuous-time) quantum walk
on

with respect to
M

and
\tau

is a complex symmetric unitary matrix, and so

\tau

\sum_{j=1}\lvert U(\tau)_{u,j}\rvert^{2}=1
for any vertex

of
X
. For this reason, if

and

are vertices of

, then

Here are some important properties associated with state transfer. If

, then we say that

(PST) occurs from

to

at time

, and if

, then we say that

at time
\tau
. The minimum positive

\lvert U(\tau)_{u,v}\rvert^{2}=1
, respectively

. Note that these various types of quantum state transfer depend on the matrix

U_{L}(t)_{u,v}=\pm U_{Q}(t)_{u,v}

\theta\in\sigma_{u}(M)

Vertices

The following result imposes a particular form on the transition matrices of graphs with twin vertices.

Theorem 1
.

Vertices
u
and
v
are twins in
X
if and only if for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
U(t)_{u,u}=U(t)_{v,v}
and
U(t)_{w,u}=U(t)_{w,v}
for all

. Moreover, if

and

are twins in

U(t)_{u,u}\neq U(t)_{v,u}

Proof.

To prove necessity, let

and

be twin vertices in

, there exists an automorphism

of

and

implies that

for all

. Now, by Lemma

,

and

are cospectral, and thus,

for any

. To prove sufficiency, suppose

for all

. A simple computation reveals that

is an eigenvector for

for any

. Consequently,
\textbf{e}_{u}-\textbf{e}_{v}
is an eigenvector for

∎

implies that
\lvert U(t)_{u,v}\rvert=\lvert U(t)_{u,w}\rvert
whenever
w\in T\backslash\{u,v\}
. If
\lvert U(t)_{u,v}\rvert=1
, then
\lvert U(t)_{u,w}\rvert=1
for all
w\in T\backslash\{u,v\}
, a contradiction because
U(t)
is unitary. Thus,
\lvert U(t)_{u,v}\rvert<1
, and by (
6
),
U(t)_{u,u}\neq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
∎

Now, suppose
X
has
n\geq 3
vertices and
T
is a set of twins in
X
. If
u,v\in T
and we let
U(t)_{u,u}=a
and
U(t)_{u,v}=b
, then labelling the vertices so that those in
T
appear first, Theorem
1
yields

U(t)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}U_{1}&U_{2}\\
U_{2}^{T}&*\\
\end{array}\right]

(7)

where
U_{1}=(a-b)I_{\lvert T\rvert}+bJ_{\lvert T\rvert}
,
a\neq b
and the columns of
U_{2}^{T}
are identical. Since
U(t)
is unitary, we have

\lvert U(t)_{u,u}\rvert^{2}+\left(\lvert T\rvert-1\right)\lvert U(t)_{u,v}\rvert^{2}+\sum_{w\notin T}\lvert U(t)_{u,w}\rvert^{2}=1

(8)

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, if
\lvert T\rvert\geq 3
, Corollary
1
(3) implies that
\lvert U(t)_{u,u}\rvert^{2}>0
, and so (
8
) implies that

\lvert U(t)_{u,v}\rvert^{2}<\frac{1}{\lvert T\rvert-1}.

These considerations yield the following result.

Corollary 2
.

Assume
X
has
n\geq 3
vertices and let
T
be a set of twins in
X
. Then
U(t)
assumes the form in (
7
), and (
8
) holds. Moreover, if
u,v\in T
, then
\lvert U(t)_{u,v}\rvert^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lvert T\rvert-1}
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and this inequality is strict whenever
\lvert T\rvert\geq 3
.

If
X
is connected and
T=V(X)
, then Corollary
2
yields
U(t)=(a-b)I+b\textbf{J}
, which explains the form of the transition matrix of
K_{n}
.

In
[
25
]
, Godsil posed the problem: find examples of cospectral vertices
u
and
v
such that for some constant
\delta>0
,
\lvert U(t)_{u,v}\rvert<1-\delta
for all
t
. We address this problem by using the above corollary. Take any graph with a set of twins
T
with
\lvert T\rvert\geq 3
. Then any two vertices
u,v\in T
are cospectral by Lemma
2
, and Corollary
2
gives us
\lvert U(t)_{u,v}\rvert^{2}<\frac{1}{\lvert T\rvert-1}=1-\delta
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\delta=\frac{\lvert T\rvert-2}{\lvert T\rvert-1}
. Next, we have the following consequence of (
8
).

Corollary 3
.

Assume
X
has
n\geq 3
vertices and let
T
be a set of twins in
X
. Then no vertex in
T
can be involved in pretty good state transfer with a vertex that is not in
T
. Moreover, if
\lvert T\rvert\geq 3
, then any vertex in
T
cannot be involved in pretty good state transfer with any vertex in
X
.

3
Joins

The exploration of joins with respect to quantum state transfer is not entirely new (see for instance,
[
2
,
3
]
). Here, we provide a systematic approach for studying quantum state transfer between twin vertices that arise from joining either a complete or empty graph with another graph (possibly regular). The results we develop here are instrumental in completing the discussion of double cones in Section
5
. We start by surveying the eigenvalue supports of vertices in joins of simple unweighted graphs, beginning with the adjacency matrix.

Lemma 3
.

Let
X
be a
k
-regular graph on
m\geq 2
vertices and
Y
be an
\ell
-regular graph on
n\geq 1
vertices. Define
\lambda^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(k+\ell\pm\sqrt{D}\right)
and
D=(k-\ell)^{2}+4mn
. Consider
Z=X\vee Y
, and let
u\in V(X)
and
w\in V(Y)
. If one of
X
and
Y
is not complete, then
\lambda^{\pm}\in\sigma_{u}(A)
,
k\in\sigma_{u}(A)
if and only if
X
is disconnected, and no eigenvalue of
A(Y)
is contained in
\sigma_{u}(A)
. The following also hold.

1.

If

and
Y\neq K_{n}
, then
\sigma_{u}(A)=\{\lambda^{\pm},-1\}
.

2.

Let
X\neq K_{m}
. If
X
is connected, then
\sigma_{u}(A)=\{\lambda^{\pm}\}\cup\sigma_{u}(A(X))\backslash\{k\}
. Otherwise,
\sigma_{u}(A)=\{\lambda^{\pm}\}\cup\sigma_{u}(A(X))
. In particular, if
X=O_{m}
, then
\sigma_{u}(A)=\{\lambda^{\pm},0\}
, and if we add that
Y=O_{1}
, then
\sigma_{w}(A)=\{\pm\sqrt{m}\}
.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{m}=k
be the eigenvalues of
A(X)
and
\mu_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{n}=\ell
be the eigenvalues of
A(Y)
. Using
[
16
, Equation (12.2.1)]
, the spectral decomposition of
A(Z)
is

A(Z)=\lambda^{+}E_{\lambda^{+}}+\lambda^{-}E_{\lambda^{-}}+\sum_{\lambda\neq\lambda_{m}}\lambda\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}F_{\lambda}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\end{array}\right]+\sum_{\mu\neq\mu_{n}}\mu\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&F_{\mu}\end{array}\right],

(9)

where the last two terms are absent whenever both
X
and

are complete graphs, the last term in the above sum is absent if

,

,

if

E_{\lambda^{\pm}}=\frac{1}{\pm m\sqrt{D}(k-\lambda^{\mp})}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}(k-\lambda^{\mp})^{2}\textbf{J}_{m}&m(k-\lambda^{\mp})\textbf{J}_{m,n}\\
m(k-\lambda^{\mp})\textbf{J}_{n,m}&m^{2}\textbf{J}_{n}\end{array}\right].

Moreover,
k
is an eigenvalue of
A(Z)
with orthogonal projection matrix
\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}F_{k}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\end{array}\right]
such that

if and only if

is disconnected. From these considerations, the first and second statements follow immediately.
∎

For the Laplacian case, we have the following.

Lemma 4
.

Let
X
and
Y
be graphs on
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1
vertices, respectively. Consider
Z=X\vee Y
, and let

and

. The following hold in

.

1.

If

, then
\sigma_{u}(L)=\{0,m+n\}
.

2.

Let
X\neq K_{m}
. If
X
is connected, then
\sigma_{u}(L)=\{0,m+n,\lambda+n:0<\lambda\in\sigma_{u}(L(X))\}
. Otherwise,
\sigma_{u}(L)=\{0,m+n,n,\lambda+n:0<\lambda\in\sigma_{u}(L(X))\}
. In particular, if
X=O_{m}
, then
\sigma_{u}(L)=\{0,m+n,n\}
, and if we add that
Y=O_{1}
, then
\sigma_{w}(L)=\{0,m+1\}
.

Proof.

Let
0=\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{m}
be the eigenvalues of
L(X)
and
0=\mu_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{n}
be the eigenvalues of
L(Y)
. Using
[
1
, Equation 31]
, the spectral decomposition of

is given by

L(Z)=\frac{1}{m+n}(0)\textbf{J}_{m,n}+(m+n)E_{m+n}+\sum_{\lambda\neq\lambda_{1}}(\lambda+n)\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}F_{\lambda}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\end{array}\right]+\sum_{\mu\neq\mu_{1}}(\mu+m)\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&F_{\mu}\end{array}\right]

(10)

where
E_{m+n}=\frac{1}{mn(m+n)}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}n^{2}\textbf{J}_{m}&-mn\textbf{J}_{m,n}\\
-mn\textbf{J}_{n,m}&m^{2}\textbf{J}_{n}\end{array}\right]
, the third (resp., fourth) term is absent if
X=K_{m}
(resp.,
Y=K_{n}
), the fourth term is absent if
n=1
,
F_{0}=E_{0}-\frac{1}{m}\textbf{J}_{m}
and
F_{\lambda}=E_{\lambda}
whenever
\lambda>0
. Moreover,
n
is an eigenvalue of
L(Z)
with orthogonal projection matrix
\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}F_{0}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\end{array}\right]
such that
F_{0}\textbf{e}_{u}\neq 0
if and only if

is disconnected. From these considerations, conclusions 1-2 are straightforward.
∎

With respect to the Laplacian matrix, Alvir et al. calculated the transition matrix of a join of two simple unweighted graphs which are not necessarily regular
[
1
, Fact 8]
. More recently, Coutinho and Godsil provided a similar result for the case of the adjacency matrix with the additional condition that the graphs joined are regular
[
16

. For our next result, we derive the transition matrix of a join of two simple unweighted regular graphs with respect to the signless Laplacian matrix.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X
be a
k
-regular graph on
m\geq 2
vertices, and
Y
be an
\ell
-regular graph on
n\geq 1
vertices. Let

,

,
\lambda^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(2k+2\ell+n+m\pm\sqrt{D}\right)
and consider the matrix

in (
13

,

and

U_{Q}(t)=e^{it\lambda^{+}}E_{\lambda^{+}}+e^{it\lambda^{-}}E_{\lambda^{-}}+\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}e^{itn}U_{X}(t)-\frac{e^{it(2k+n)}}{m}\textbf{J}_{m}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&e^{itm}U_{Y}(t)-\frac{e^{i(2\ell+m)}}{n}\textbf{J}_{n}\end{array}\right].

(11)

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{m}=2k
be the eigenvalues of

and
\mu_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{n}=2\ell
be the eigenvalues of

\textbf{v}_{j}
is an eigenvector of

corresponding to
\lambda_{j}
for

and
\textbf{v}_{j}
is orthogonal to
1
, then

is an eigenvalue of

with corresponding eigenvector
\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\textbf{v}_{j}\\
\textbf{0}\end{array}\right]
for

. Similarly, if
\textbf{w}_{j}
is an eigenvector of

corresponding to
\mu_{j}
for

and
\textbf{w}_{j}
is orthogonal to

, then

is an eigenvalue of

with corresponding eigenvector
\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{w}_{j}\end{array}\right]
for

. Using equitable partitions, one can show that the remaining two eigenvalues of

are
\lambda^{\pm}
with corresponding eigenvectors
\textbf{v}^{\pm}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\left(p\pm\sqrt{D}\right)\textbf{1}_{m}\\
2m\textbf{1}_{n}\end{array}\right]
. Thus, we may write

Q(Z)=\lambda^{+}E_{\lambda^{+}}+\lambda^{-}E_{\lambda^{-}}+\sum_{\lambda\neq\lambda_{m}}(\lambda+n)\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}F_{\lambda}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\end{array}\right]+\sum_{\mu\neq\mu_{n}}(\mu+m)\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&F_{\mu}\end{array}\right]

(12)

where
F_{2k}=E_{2k}-\frac{1}{m}\textbf{J}_{m}
,

whenever

, and

E_{\lambda^{\pm}}=\frac{1}{m\left[\left(p\pm\sqrt{D}\right)^{2}+4mn\right]}\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\left(p\pm\sqrt{D}\right)^{2}\textbf{J}_{m}&2m\left(p\pm\sqrt{D}\right)\textbf{J}_{m,n}\\

(13)

Moreover,

is an eigenvalue of

with orthogonal projection matrix
\left[\begin{array}[]{ccccc}F_{2k}&\textbf{0}\\
\textbf{0}&\textbf{0}\end{array}\right]
such that

if and only if

is disconnected. Using (

U_{X}(t)=\frac{e^{i2kt}}{m}\textbf{J}_{m}+\sum_{\lambda\neq 2k}e^{it\lambda}F_{\lambda}

∎

We note that the last two terms in (

,

4
Periodicity

Denote the minimum period of a periodic vertex
u
by
\rho
. Then every period
\tau
of
u
is an integer multiple of
\rho
. Moreover, if PST occurs between
u
and
v
at time
\tau
, then both of them are periodic at time
2\tau
. The converse of this is not necessarily true as periodic vertices need not exhibit PST. However, it is shown by Godsil in
[
27
]
that if
u
is periodic and there is PST between
u
and
v
, then the minimum PST time between
u
and
v
is
\rho/2
, and PST occurs between
u
and
v
at every odd multiple of
\rho/2
. Now, since periodicity is a necessary condition for PST, to characterize PST between twin vertices, we first need to characterize periodic twin vertices. To do this, we state the famous Ratio Condition due to Godsil
[
26
]
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X
be a weighted graph, possibly with loops. The following are equivalent.

1.

Vertex
u
of
X
is periodic.

2.

For all
\lambda_{p},\lambda_{q},\lambda_{r},\lambda_{s}\in\sigma_{u}(M)
with
\lambda_{r}\neq\lambda_{s}
, we have

\dfrac{\lambda_{p}-\lambda_{q}}{\lambda_{r}-\lambda_{s}}\in\mathbb{Q}.

(14)

If in addition we assume that
\phi(M,t)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
, then
u
is periodic if and only if either (i)
\sigma_{u}(M)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
, or (ii) every
\lambda_{j}\in\sigma_{u}(M)
is of the form
\lambda_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a+c_{j}\sqrt{\Delta}\right)
, where
a
,
c_{j}
and
\Delta>1
are integers such that
\Delta
is square-free, and the difference between any two eigenvalues in
\sigma_{u}(M)
is an integer multiple of
\sqrt{\Delta}
.

Eigenvalue supports are known to contain at least two elements
[
41
, Proposition 2.8]
. The following result determines the minimum period of periodic vertices.

Theorem 4
.

Let
u
and
v
be vertices in
X
, and
\sigma_{u}(M)=\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
with
\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}
.

1.

If
\lvert\sigma_{u}(M)\rvert=2
, then
u
is periodic with
\rho=\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
.

2.

If
\lvert\sigma_{u}(M)\rvert\geq 3
and
u
is periodic, then
\rho=\frac{2\pi q}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
, where
q=\operatorname{lcm}(q_{2},\ldots,q_{n})
and each
q_{j}
is an integer such that
\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}=\frac{p_{j}}{q_{j}}
for some integer
p_{j}
such that
\text{gcd}(p_{j},q_{j})=1
.

Proof.

The first statement is straightforward, and so we only prove the second. Let us suppose that
u
is periodic with
\sigma_{u}(M)=\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
for some
n\geq 3
. Then Theorem
3
holds, and we may let
\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}=\frac{p_{j}}{q_{j}}
, where each
p_{j}
and
q_{j}
are integers such that
\text{gcd}(p_{j},q_{j})=1
. The fact that
u
is periodic is equivalent to the existence of a time
t
and unit
\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
such that

U(t)\textbf{e}_{u}=\gamma\textbf{e}_{u}.

The spectral decomposition of
U(t)
in (
4
) allows us to write the above equation as
\sum_{j}e^{it\lambda_{j}}E_{j}e_{u}=\sum_{j}\gamma E_{j}e_{u}
. Equivalently,
e^{it(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{j})}=1
, which holds if and only if for each
j\geq 2
, we have
t(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{j})=2k\pi
for some integer
k
. Now, suppose
\rho=\frac{2\pi z}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
for some
z\in\mathbb{R}
. Since
\text{gcd}(p_{j},q_{j})=1
for each
j\geq 2
, we get that
\rho(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{j})=2\pi z\left(\frac{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}\right)=\frac{2\pi zp_{j}}{q_{j}}
is an integer multiple of
2\pi
if and only if
z
is the minimum integer such that each
q_{j}
divides
z
. Therefore,
z=q
, where
q=\operatorname{lcm}(q_{2},\ldots,q_{n})
and so
\rho=\frac{2\pi q}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
.
∎

If
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are the largest and smallest eigenvalues in
\sigma_{u}(M)
, then Godsil showed that
\rho\geq\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
(see
[
27
, Lemma 3.4]
). However, if
\lvert\sigma_{u}(M)\rvert\geq 3
, then
q
in Theorem
4
(2) satisfies
q>1
, and so
\rho>\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
. Thus, the inequality
\rho\geq\frac{2\pi}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
is tight if and only if
\lvert\sigma_{u}(M)\rvert=2
. We also note that while
q
depends on the choice of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, the minimum period
\rho
in Theorem
4
does not.

We say that a subset
W\subseteq V(X)
is
periodic
if each vertex in
W
is periodic and there exists a time
\tau>0
such that
\lvert U(\tau)_{u,u}\rvert=1
for each
u\in W
. The minimum time such that
W
is periodic is called the
minimum period

\rho
of
W
. In particular, if
W=V(X)
, then we say that
X
is periodic. As each vertex in
W
is periodic, it follows that
\rho
is an integer multiple of the minimum periods of the vertices in
W
. It is also immediate that if all vertices in
W
have the same eigenvalue support, then
W
is periodic if and only if one of its vertices is periodic, and as a consequence, each vertex has the same minimum period
\rho,
which equals the minimum period of
W
.

Now, if
T
is a set of twins in
X
, then Lemma
2
implies that the vertices in
T
are pairwise cospectral, and thus, they all have the same eigenvalue support. Combining this with Lemma
1
and
[
28
, Theorem 6.1]
yields a characterization of periodic twin vertices whenever
\phi(M,t)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\phi(M,t)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
and
T
be a set of twins in
X
with
\sigma_{u}(M)=\{\theta,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\}
for each
u\in T
. Then
T
is periodic if and only if
\lambda_{j}=\theta+b_{j}\sqrt{\Delta}
for each
j
, where
\theta
is given in (
5
),
b_{j}
is an integer, and either
\Delta=1
or
\Delta>1
is a square-free integer. Moreover, if
T
is periodic, then each
u\in T
has minimum period
\rho=2\pi/g\sqrt{\Delta}
, where
g=\operatorname{gcd}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{r})
.

Example 1
.

Let
n\geq 2
and consider the star
K_{1,n}\cong O_{n}\vee O_{1}
with set of leaves
T=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\}
. Then
T
is a set of twins in
K_{1,n}
and Lemma
3
(2b) yields
\sigma_{u_{j}}(A)=\{\theta,\pm\sqrt{n}\}
for each

, where
\theta=0
. Moreover, Lemma
4
(2) gives us
\sigma_{u_{j}}(L)=\{1,0,n+1\}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. Invoking Theorem
5
and (
3
), we conclude that
T
is periodic with respect to
M\in\{A,L,Q\}
.

We now use Theorem
5
to characterize adjacency periodic twin vertices in simple unweighted joins of the form

, where

is either
K_{m}
or

, and
Y
is regular.

Theorem 6
.

Let

,

, and

-regular graph on

and

1.

If

and

, then

.

2.

Let

. Then

is adjacency periodic if and only if either

(a)

and

, in which case

, or

(b)

, where

,

if

, and

.

Moreover, if

is adjacency periodic if and only if

is a perfect square and

is integral.

Proof.

Since 1 is clear, it suffices to show 2. Let

, and suppose

5
Perfect state transfer

We first state an important observation due to Dave Morris
[
27
, Lemma 13.1]
.

Lemma 5
.

If pretty good state transfer occurs between
u
and
v
, then
u
and
v
are strongly cospectral.

It is well-known that two vertices are strongly cospectral if and only if they are cospectral and parallel. Since twin vertices are cospectral by Lemma
2
, it follows that twin vertices are strongly cospectral if and only if they are parallel. Next, we state the Corollaries 3.10 and 3.14 in
[
41
]
respectively, which will prove useful in this section.

Lemma 6
.

Let
T
be a set of twins in
X
. If
\lvert T\rvert\geq 3
, then each vertex
v\in T
is not parallel, and hence not strongly cospectral, with any vertex
z\neq u
.

Lemma 7
.

Let
T=\{u,v\}
be a set of twins in
X
, and consider
\theta
in (
5
). If
\Omega
is an orthogonal set of eigenvectors for
\theta
such that
\textbf{e}_{u}-\textbf{e}_{v}\in\Omega
, then
u
and
v
are strongly cospectral if and only if (i)
\lvert\Omega\rvert=1
or (ii)
\textbf{w}^{T}\textbf{e}_{u}=\textbf{w}^{T}\textbf{e}_{v}=0
for all
\textbf{w}\in\Omega\backslash\{\textbf{e}_{u}-\textbf{e}_{v}\}
. Moreover, if
u
and
v
are strongly cospectral, then
\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)=\{\theta\}
, and
u
and
v
cannot be strongly cospectral to any

.

If
u
and
v
are are strongly cospectral, then
\lvert\sigma_{u}(M)\rvert\geq 3

[
41
, Theorem 3.4]
. Thus, if
u
and
v
are twins that are strongly cospectral, then Lemma
7
yields
\lvert\sigma_{uv}^{+}(M)\rvert\geq 2
and
\lvert\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)\rvert=1
. A related result of Coutinho and Liu says that if

and

are strongly cospectral and
\lvert\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)\rvert=1
, then

and

are twins
[

, Lemma 3.1]
. Thus, if

and

are strongly cospectral with respect to

, then

and

are twins if and only if
\lvert\sigma_{uv}^{-}(M)\rvert=1
.

It is known that PST is monogamous
[
33
]
. However, PGST is not, as shown by the Cartesian product of
P_{2}
and
P_{3}
provided by Pal and Bhattacharjya in
[
43
, Example 4.1]
which exhibits pairwise adjacency PGST between four vertices. For the weighted case, Johnston et al. provided a graph that exhibits Laplacian PGST from one vertex to three distinct vertices
[
31
, Example 2]
. However, from Lemma
6
, if PGST occurs between vertices in a set of twins
T
, then
\lvert T\rvert=2
. Combining this with Lemma
7
, we conclude that a vertex
u
with a twin in
X
can only pair up with at most one vertex

to exhibit PGST. That is, PGST is monogamous when it involves a vertex with a twin.

Making use of Lemma
7
and a characterization of PST by Coutinho
[
18
, Theorem 2.4.4]
, we obtain the following characterization of PST between twin vertices.

Theorem 9
.

Let
T=\{u,v\}
be a set of twins in
X
and suppose
\sigma_{u}(M)=\{\theta,\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{r}\}
, where
\theta
is given in (

). Then perfect state transfer occurs between

and

if and only if

1.

E_{\theta}\textbf{e}_{u}=-E_{\theta}\textbf{e}_{v}
,
E_{j}\textbf{e}_{u}=E_{j}\textbf{e}_{v}

j=1,\ldots,r
; and

2.

there exists a time
\tau
such that for each

, an odd
m_{j}
exists such that

\tau(\lambda_{j}-\theta)=m_{j}\pi.

(15)

In addition, the minimum time that perfect state transfer occurs between

and

is
\tau=\frac{\pi q}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}}
, where

is an integer given in Theorem
4
.

Denote the largest power of two that divides an integer

by
\nu_{2}(b)
. With the assumption in Theorem
9
, we further suppose that
\phi(M,t)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
. Applying the well-known characterization of PST due to Coutinho
[

, Theorem 2.4.4]
for the case that

Let

and

be a set of twins in

For each

For each

, where

In Theorem

We illustrate Theorem

Example 2
.

Theorem 11

Let
Y
be a
\ell
-regular graph on
n\geq 1

1.

Adjacency perfect state transfer occurs between the apexes of

(a)

\ell=0
and

(mod 4);

(b)

\ell>0

; or

(c)

\ell>0

\nu_{2}(s)>1
,

\nu_{2}(n)=1

\ell
is odd,
\nu_{2}(n)>\nu_{2}(s)

Let
u
and

be the apexes of

[

,

and

A
and

1
and
7
, we get that
\sigma_{uv}^{-}(A)=\{0\}
and
\sigma_{uv}^{-}(Q)=\{n\}
. We divide our discussion into two cases: the adjacency case and the signless Laplacian case.

We begin by proving the first statement. By Lemma
3
(2), we get
\sigma_{u}(A)=\{\lambda^{\pm},0\}
, where
\lambda^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ell\pm\sqrt{\ell^{2}+8n}\right)
. If
\ell=0
, then
\sigma_{u}(A)=\left\{\pm\sqrt{2n},0\right\}
, where
\nu_{2}(\sqrt{2n})=\nu_{2}(-\sqrt{2n})
. By Theorem
10
, PST occurs between
u
and
v
with minimum time
\tau=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2n}}
, and so (i) holds. Now, let
\ell>0
. By Theorem
10
, adjacency PST occurs between
u
and
v
if and only if
\ell^{2}+8n
is a perfect square and

\nu_{2}(\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}+8n})=\nu_{2}(\ell-\sqrt{\ell^{2}+8n}).

(16)

Now,
\ell^{2}+8n
is a perfect square if and only if
8n=4s(\ell+s)
for some integer
s
such that
s(\ell+s)
is even. Making use of (
16
), we get
\nu_{2}(\ell+s)=\nu_{2}(s)
which implies that
\nu_{2}(\ell)>\nu_{2}(s)
. As
\nu_{2}(s)\geq 0
and
\nu_{2}(n)=\nu_{2}\left(\frac{s(\ell+s)}{2}\right)=\nu_{2}(s)+\nu_{2}(\ell+s)-1=2\nu_{2}(s)-1\geq 0
, we obtain
\nu(s)\geq 1
. Therefore, (ii) is true. Lasly, invoking Theorem
10
yields the minimum PST time
\tau=\frac{\pi}{2g}
, where
g=\operatorname{gcd}(\ell+\sqrt{\ell^{2}+8n},\ell-\sqrt{\ell^{2}+8n})
.

Next, we show the second statement. By Corollary
4
(2b), we get
\sigma_{u}(Q)=\{\lambda^{\pm},\theta\}
, where
\lambda^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(2\ell+n+2\pm\sqrt{(2\ell+n+2)^{2}-8\ell n})
and
\theta=n
in Theorem
10
. We have the following cases.

•

Let
\ell=0
so that
\sigma_{u}(Q)=\{n,n+2,0\}
. Since we can write
n+2=\theta+2
and

, we obtain
\nu_{2}(-n)=\nu_{2}(2)=1
if and only if

for some odd

, or equivalently,

(mod 4). Applying Theorem
10
, we get signless Laplacian PST between

and
v
if and only if

(mod 4), in which case the minimum PST time is
\tau=\frac{\pi}{2}
.

•

Let
\ell>0
so that we can write
\lambda^{\pm}=\theta+\frac{1}{2}\left(2\ell-n+2\pm\sqrt{(2\ell+n+2)^{2}-8\ell n}\right)
. Invoking Theorems

(2) and
10
(2), we need either

or

is a perfect square. If

, then

, and hence, Theorem
10
yields signless Laplacian PST between

and

with minimum time

. Now, suppose
(2\ell+n+2)^{2}-8\ell n
is a perfect square. By Theorem

(3), signless Laplacian PST occurs between

and
v
if and only if

\nu_{2}\left(2\ell-n+2+\sqrt{(2\ell+n+2)^{2}-8\ell n}\right)=\nu_{2}\left(2\ell-n+2-\sqrt{(2\ell+n+2)^{2}-8\ell n}\right).

(17)

Using conjugation, (
17
) yields
\nu_{2}\left((2\ell-n+2\pm\sqrt{(2\ell+n+2)^{2}-8\ell n})^{2}\right)=\nu_{2}(8n)
. Thus,

\nu_{2}(n)
is odd and we may write (

\nu_{2}\left(2\ell-n+2\pm\sqrt{(2\ell+n+2)^{2}-8\ell n}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nu_{2}(n)+3\right).

Since
(2\ell+n+2)^{2}-8\ell n

for some integer

such that

is even, i.e.,

. Since

is even,

is also even. This allows us to write

, and so we may write (

\nu_{2}\left(\ell+1-\frac{s}{2}\right)+1=\nu_{2}(n-s)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nu_{2}(n)+1\right).

(19)

Let

,

and

for odd

,

and

whenever

. Both subcases contradict (

–

Let
\nu_{2}(s)>1
and
\nu_{2}(n)=1
so that
\nu_{2}(n-s)=1
. If
\ell
is even, then
\nu_{2}(\ell+1-\frac{s}{2})=0
. Otherwise,
\nu_{2}(\ell+1-\frac{s}{2})>0
. Thus, (

) holds if and only if
\ell
is even.

–

Let
\nu_{2}(n)>\nu_{2}(s)
so that
\nu_{2}(n)\geq 3
. Then
\nu_{2}(n-s)=\nu_{2}(s)
, and (

) holds if and only if

\nu_{2}\left(\ell+1-\frac{s}{2}\right)+1=\nu_{2}(s)=\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{2}(n)+1)\geq 2.

(20)

If
\ell
is even, then
\nu_{2}(\ell+1-\frac{s}{2})=0
, a contradiction to (

). Now, suppose
\ell
is odd and let
\ell+1=2^{\nu_{2}(\ell+1)}d

*

If
\nu_{2}(\ell+1)>\nu_{2}(s)-1

\nu_{2}(\ell+1-\frac{s}{2})=\nu_{2}(s)-1

\nu_{2}(s)=\frac{1}{2}(\nu_{2}(n)+1)
.

*

If
\nu_{2}(\ell+1)=\nu_{2}(s)-1
, then
\nu_{2}(\ell+1-\frac{s}{2})>\nu_{2}(s)-1

) fails.

*

If
\nu_{2}(\ell+1)<\nu_{2}(s)-1

, and so (

) fails.

Combining the two cases above, we get PST occurring between

and

whenever

for some integer

, and either (i)
\nu_{2}(s)>1
,

Thus, we have covered all cases.

Long Time Behaviour of the Discrete Volume Preserving Mean Curvature Flow in the Flat Torus
Daniele De Gennaro, Anna Kubin

Abstract
We show that the discrete approximate volume preserving mean curvature flow in the flat torus 𝕋 N \mathbb{T}^{N} starting near a strictly stable critical set E E of the perimeter converges in the long time to a translate of E E exponentially fast. As an intermediate result we establish a new quantitative estimate of Alexandrov type for periodic strictly stable constant mean curvature hypersurfaces. Finally, in the two dimensional case a complete characterization of the long time behaviour of the discrete flow with arbitrary initial sets of finite perimeter is provided.



1
Introduction

We consider the geometric evolution of sets called
the volume preserving mean curvature flow
. The
classical mean curvature flow
is defined as a flow of sets
(E_{t})_{0\leq t\leq T}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
following the motion law

v_{t}=-H_{E_{t}}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial E_{t},

where
v_{t}
denotes the component of the velocity relative to the outer normal vector of
\partial E_{t}
and
H_{E}
is the mean curvature of the set
E
.
In order to include the volume constraint, one can consider the following velocity

v_{t}=\overline{H}_{E_{t}}-H_{E_{t}}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial E_{t}

(1.1)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, where
\overline{H}_{E_{t}}
denotes the average of
H_{E_{t}}
over
\partial E_{t}
.

The defined geometric evolution is called
volume constrained mean curvature flow
or
volume preserving mean curvature flow
. One can observe that the volume of the evolving sets is indeed preserved during the evolution and that the perimeters of the sets
E_{t}
are non-increasing.

This geometric flow has been used to describe some types of solidification processes and coarsening phenomena in physical systems. For example, one can consider mixtures that, after a first relaxation time, can be described by two subdomains of nearly pure phases far from equilibrium, evolving in a way to minimize the total interfacial area between the phases while keeping their volume constant (further details on the physical background can be found in
[
6
,
20
]
, see also the introduction of
[
19
]
).
Moreover, some variants of the volume-preserving mean curvature flow were also applied in the context of shape recovery in image processing in
[
8
]
.

One of the main mathematical difficulties of the volume preserving mean curvature flow is the non-local nature of the functional given by the constraint. Moreover, the generated flow may present singularities of different kinds, even in a finite time-span and even if the initial data is smooth. For example, we can see merging or collision of near sets, pinch-offs or shrinking of connected components to points. There exist examples of singular solutions even in the two dimensional case, see
[
16
,
17
]
. After the onset of singularities,
the classical or smooth formulation of the flow (
1.1
) ceases to hold and needs to be replaced by a weaker one.
Due to the lack of a comparison principle, a natural approach is the minimizing movement approach proposed independently by Almgren, Taylor and Wang in
[
2
]
and by Luckhaus and Sturzenhecker in
[
14
]
for the unconstrained case and adapted to the volume-preserving setting in
[
21
]
.

We briefly recall the scheme in the volume contrained setting.
First of all we define a discrete-in-time approximation of the flow that will be called the
discrete (volume-preserving) flow
. Given any initial set
E_{0}
and a time-step
h>0
we define iteratively
E_{h}^{0}:=E_{0}
and for all
n\geq 0

E_{h}^{n+1}\in\text{argmin}\left\{P(F)+\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{F\triangle E_{h}^{n}}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E_{h}^{n}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x\ :\ F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N},\,|F|=|E_{0}|\right\},

where
\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E_{h}^{n}}
is the distance function from the set
\partial E_{h}^{n}
. We can define for every
t\geq 0
, the approximate flow by
E_{h}(t):=E_{h}^{[t/h]}
.
It can be proved (see
[
19
, Proposition 2.2]
) that the discrete flow is well defined.
Any limit point of this flow as the time-step
h
converges to zero will be called a
flat flow
.
As for the classical mean curvature flow, this approach produces global-in-time solutions as shown in
[
21
]
. The existence of such global solutions then permits to analyse the equilibrium configurations reached in the long time asymptotics.

The long time behaviour of the volume preserving mean curvature flow has been previously studied only in some particular cases, when the existence of global smooth solutions could be ensured by choosing suitably regular initial sets. For example one can consider uniformly convex and nearly spherical initial sets (see
[
9
,
10
]
), or
C^{\infty}-
regular initial sets that are
H^{3}-
close to strictly stable critical sets in the three and four dimensional flat torus (see
[
22
]
).
For more general initial data, the long time behaviour in the context of flat flows of convex and star-shaped sets (see
[
5
,
12
]
) has been characterized only up to (possibly diverging in the case of
[
5
]
) translations.
In
[
19
]
the authors characterized the long-time limits of the discrete-in-time approximate flows constructed by the Euler implicit scheme introduced in
[
2
,
14
]
under the volume constraint in arbitrary space dimension. They proved that the discrete flow starting from an arbitrary bounded initial set converges exponentially fast to a finite union of disjoint balls with equal radii. The same authors and collaborators were also able to send the discretization parameter
h
to 0 in the preprint
[
11
]
, in the case
N=2
.
Indeed, an explicit penalization is used in order to enforce the volume constraint.

In this paper the long-time convergence analysis is developed in the flat torus
\mathbb{T}^{N}
for the discrete flow. In such framework the class of possible long-time limits is much richer as it includes not only union of balls with equal radii but also different type of critical sets for the perimeter.
The notion of
strictly stable critical set
is crucial to our result; for the precise definition we refer to Section
2
, but it can be summarized as a regular, critical set for the perimeter (i.e. with a constant mean curvature boundary) with strictly positive (volume-constrained) second variation. The main result of the paper is the theorem below. It provides a complete characterization of the long-time behaviour of the discrete mean curvature flow in the flat torus starting near a strictly stable critical set. Moreover, an estimate on the convergence speed is provided.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E
be a strictly stable critical set in the flat torus. Then there exist
\delta^{*}=\delta^{*}(E)>0
and
h^{*}=h^{*}(E)>0
with the following property: if
h<h^{*}
and
E_{0}\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
is a set of finite perimeter satisfying

|E_{0}|=|E|,\qquad\overline{E}_{0}\subset(E)_{\delta^{*}},

then every discrete volume preserving mean curvature flow
(E_{h}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
starting from
E_{0}
converges to a translate of
E
in
C^{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and the convergence is exponentially fast.

We would like to give some details to highlight the major differences between our results and the analysis carried out in
[
22
]
. In the aforementioned work, the author studied the flat flow, albeit in low dimension (
N\leq 4
). In the article, it was assumed the initial set to be a
C^{\infty}-
deformation of a strictly stable critical set, close in the
H^{3}-
sense to the latter set. Under these assumptions, it was proved the exponential convergence of the flat flow to a translated of the strictly stable critical set. We remark that our result addresses the long time behaviour of the discrete flow but holds in much weaker hypotheses: we only assume the initial set to be of finite perimeter and close in the Hausdorff sense to a strictly stable critical set. Moreover, our result holds in every dimension and we are also able to provide the complete characterization of the long-time behaviour starting from any initial set in dimension
N=2
. In order to state the precise result in the two-dimensional case we first introduce the following notation.

We will call
lamella
any connected set in
\mathbb{T}^{2}
whose
1-
periodic extension in
R^{2}
is a stripe bounded by two parallel lines.
Our final result in two dimension is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
h
,
m>0
and an initial set
E_{0}\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
with finite perimeter and such that
|E_{0}|=m
.
Let
(E_{h}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a discrete flow starting from
E_{0}
and let
P_{\infty}
be the limit of the non-increasing sequence
P(E_{h}^{n})
. Then either one of the following holds:

i)

(E_{h}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a disjoint union of
l
discs of equal radii and total area
m
, where
l=\pi^{-1}(4m)^{-1}P_{\infty}^{2}\in\mathbb{N}
;

ii)

\left((E_{h}^{n})^{c}\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a disjoint union of
l
discs of equal radii and total area
1-m
, where
l=\pi^{-1}(4-4m)^{-1}P_{\infty}^{2}\in\mathbb{N}
;

iii)

(E_{h}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to a disjoint union of
l
lamellae of total area
m
, with the same slope and
l\leq P_{\infty}/2
.
Moreover, the equality
l=P_{\infty}/2\in\mathbb{N}
holds if and only if the limit is given by vertical or horizontal lamellae.

In all cases the convergence is exponentially fast in
C^{k}
for every
k\in N
.

1.1
Comments about the proof of the main results

The first step towards proving our main result Theorem
1.1
is Proposition
5.2
. More precisely, we prove the convergence up to translations of any discrete flow, starting Hausdorff-close to a strictly stable critical set
E
, to the latter set. Such a convergence holds in the
C^{k}-
norm for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Since at this point we can not rule out that different subsequences of the discrete flow may converge to different translates of
E
, the subsequent step consists in proving the convergence of the whole flow to a unique translate of the set
E
(with exponential rate).

In order to prove Proposition
5.2
we first show (see Step 1 of the proof of the aforementioned proposition) that every long-time limit of the flow is a critical point of the perimeter. When the ambient space is
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, this implies that the limit points can only be balls or finite union of balls with the same radii. However, in the periodic setting, we may end up with different critical points of the perimeter. Indeed, already in the three dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{3}
we find a wealth of different critical points in addition to balls: for example, lamellae, cylinders and gyroids (see Figure
1
) .

Figure 1:
The critical points in
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. Balls, cylinders, gyroids and
3-
dimensional lamellae.

We then exploit the strict stability of
E
(Proposition
4.6
) to ensure that the flow remains
L^{1}
-close up to translations to the set
E
. To conclude, a regularity argument shows that the convergence in
L^{1}
of the flow to a regular stable set implies the convergence in
C^{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, thus proving Proposition
5.2
.

The proof of Proposition
4.6
is based on the following idea: from a stability result in
[
1
]
, one can estimate the
L^{1}-
distance (up to translations) of a set
F
from a strictly stable critical set
E
in terms of the differences of the perimeters, provided that the
L^{1}-
distance between
E
and
F
remains below a certain threshold. Moreover, a counterexample shows that the Hausdorff-closeness assumption can not be weakened to
L^{1}-
closeness, as we will discuss in details in Subsection
4.3
.

In order to establish the uniqueness of the limit and, therefore, the main Theorem
1.1
, Section
5.2
is devoted to proving the convergence of the barycenters of the evolving sets. A crucial intermediate result consists in generalizing the Alexandrov-type estimate
[
19
, Theorem 1.3]
(see also
[
13
]
) to the flat torus. This result provides a stability inequality for
C^{1}-
normal deformations of strictly stable critical sets in the periodic setting. It could also be seen as an higher-order Łojasiewicz-Simon inequality for the perimeter functional.
We briefly give some definitions to present some further details. Given a set
E
of class
C^{1}
and a function
f:\partial E\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial E)}
is sufficiently small, the
normal deformation

E_{f}
of the set
E
is defined as

\partial E_{f}:=\{x+f(x)\nu_{E}(x)\ :\ x\in\partial E\},

where
\nu_{E}
is the normal outer vector of
E
. A normal deformation
E_{f}
is said to be of class
C^{k}
if
E
is of class
C^{k}
and

f\in C^{k}(\partial E)
. The result proved in
[
19
]
is the following.

Theorem
.

There exist
\delta\in(0,1/2)
and
C>0
with the following property: for any
f\in C^{1}(\partial B)\cap H^{2}(\partial B)
such that
\|f\|_{C^{1}(\partial B)}\leq\delta
,
|E_{f}|=\omega_{N}
and
\textnormal{bar}(E_{f})=0
, we have

\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial B)}\leq C\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial B)}.

We are able to show that in the periodic setting the above quantitative estimate holds with
B
replaced by any strictly stable critical set. More precisely, we have the following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a strictly stable critical set.
There exist
\delta\in(0,1/2)
and
C>0
with the following property: for any
f\in C^{1}(\partial E)\cap H^{2}(\partial E)
such that
\|f\|_{C^{1}(\partial E)}\leq\delta
and satisfying

\left|\int_{\partial E}f\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right|\leq\delta\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)},\qquad\left|\int_{\partial E}f\nu_{E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right|\leq\delta\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)},

(1.2)

we have

\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial E)}\leq C\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}.

(1.3)

We will prove in details in Section
3
that the conditions (
1.2
) have a geometric explanation. Indeed, the first one ensures that
|E_{f}|\approx|E|
, up to higher-order error terms, and the second one,
for some choices of
E
, is implied by imposing
\text{bar}(E_{f})\approx\text{bar}(E)
.

We finally remark that the estimate (
1.3
) is optimal for what concerns the power of the norms, see
[
19
, Remark 1.5]
.

The last section of the paper is devoted to the two-dimensional case. This particular choice of the dimension is purely technical and it is motivated by the availability of a complete characterization of the critical points of the perimeter in the two-dimensional flat torus. In this setting we are able to prove the exponential convergence of the flow starting from any initial set to either a finite union of balls or a finite union of lamellae or the complement of these configurations.

2
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{T}^{N}:=\mathbb{R}^{N}/\mathbb{Z}^{N}
be the
N-
dimensional torus, that is the quotient space
\mathbb{R}^{N}/\sim
where
\sim
is the equivalence relation given by
x\sim y
if and only if
x-y\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}
. We can define the distance between two points
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
simply by

\textnormal{dist}(x,y)=\min_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}}|(x+z)-y|.

The definition of functional spaces on the torus is straightforward: for example,
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
can be identified with the subspace of
W^{k,p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of functions that are one-periodic with respect to all coordinate directions. When we need to be specific about functions on the torus, it is often convenient to give coordinates to
\mathbb{T}^{N}
via the unit cube
Q=[0,1)^{N}
.

Firstly, we recall the definition of functions of bounded variation in the periodic setting. We say that a function
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
is of bounded variation if its total variation is finite, that is

|Du|=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}u\,\textnormal{div}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}x:\varphi\in{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}C^{1}}}(\mathbb{T}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ |\varphi|\leq 1\right\}<+\infty.

We denote the space of such functions by
BV(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. We say that a measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
is of finite perimeter in
\mathbb{T}^{N}
if its characteristic function
\chi_{E}\in BV(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. The perimeter
P(E)
of
E
in
\mathbb{T}^{N}
is nothing but the total variation
|D\chi_{E}|(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. We refer to Maggi’s book
[
15
]
for a more complete reference about sets of finite perimeter and their properties.

We recall the following notation.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
E
be a set of class
C^{1}
.
Given a function
f:\partial E\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial E)}
is sufficiently small, we set

\partial E_{f}:=\{x+f(x)\nu_{E}(x):x\in\partial E\}

(2.1)

and we call
E_{f}
the normal deformation of
E
induced by
f
.

With a slight abuse of notation, we give the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
E
be a set of class
C^{1}
.
Let
X(\partial E)
denote a functional space that can either be
L^{p}(\partial E)
,
W^{k,p}(\partial E)
,
C^{k,\alpha}(\partial E)
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\geq 1
and
\alpha\in[0,1]
. For any
F=E_{f}
with
f\in X(\partial E)
, we set

\textnormal{dist}_{X}(F,E)=\|f\|_{X(\partial E)}.

We recall the classical definition of
C^{1,\alpha}-
convergence of sets.

Definition 2.3
.

Given
\alpha\in[0,1]
, a sequence
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
C^{1,\alpha}-
regular sets is said to converge in
C^{1,\alpha}
to a set
E
if:

•

for any
x\in\partial E
, up to rotations and relabelling the coordinates, we can find a cylinder
C=B^{\prime}\times(-1,1)
, where
B^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
is the unit ball centred at the origin, and functions
f,f_{n}\in C^{1,\beta}(B^{\prime};(-1,1))
such that for
n
large enough, it holds

\displaystyle(E-x)\cap C

\displaystyle=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in B^{\prime}\times(-1,1)\ :\ x_{N}\leq f(x^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle(E_{n}-x)\cap C

\displaystyle=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in B^{\prime}\times(-1,1)\ :\ x_{N}\leq f_{n}(x^{\prime})\};

•

it holds

f_{n}\to f\quad\text{in }C^{1,\alpha}(B^{\prime}).

The following is a simple rephrasing of a classical result concerning the
C^{1,\alpha}-
convergence of
\Lambda-
minimizers of the perimeter (see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 4.2]
).

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\Lambda>0
and let
E
be a set of class
C^{2}
. Then for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,E)>0
with the following property: for every
\Lambda-
minimizer
F
such that
|E\triangle F|\leq\delta
, then
F
is of class
C^{1,1/2}
and

\textnormal{dist}_{C^{1,\beta}}(E,F)\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for}\quad\beta\in(0,1/2).

We now recall some preliminary results from
[
1
]
regarding the second variation of the perimeter in the flat torus.
Firstly, we fix some notation. Let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a set of class
C^{2}
and let
\nu_{E}
be its exterior normal.
Throughout the section, when no confusion is possible, we shall omit the subscript
E
and write
\nu
instead of
\nu_{E}
. Given a vector
X
, its tangential part on
\partial E
is defined as
X_{\tau}=X-(X\cdot\nu)\nu
. In particular, we will denote by
D_{\tau}
the tangential gradient operator given by
D_{\tau}\varphi=(D\varphi)_{\tau}
. We also recall that the second fundamental form
B_{E}
of
\partial E
is given by
D_{\tau}\nu
, its eigenvalues are called principal curvatures and its trace is called mean curvature, and we denote it by
H_{E}
.

Let
X:\mathbb{T}^{N}\to{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{R}^{N}}}
be a vector field of class
C^{2}
. Consider the associated flow
\Phi:\mathbb{T}^{N}\times(-1,1)\to\mathbb{T}^{N}
defined by
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}=X(\Phi),\ \Phi(\cdot,0)=Id
. We define the
first and second variation of the perimeter at
E
with respect to the flow
\Phi
to be respectively the values

\dfrac{\,\textnormal{d}}{\,\textnormal{d}t}\Big\lvert_{t=0}P(E_{t}),\quad\dfrac{\,\textnormal{d}^{2}}{\,\textnormal{d}t^{2}}\Big\lvert_{t=0}P(E_{t})

where
E_{t}=\Phi(\cdot,t)(E).
It is a classical result of the theory of sets of finite perimeter (see
[
15
]
) that the the first variation of the perimeter has the following expression

\dfrac{\,\textnormal{d}}{\,\textnormal{d}t}\Big\lvert_{t=0}P(E_{t})=\int_{\partial^{*}E}H_{E}\nu_{E}\cdot X\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1},

where
H_{E}
is the (weak) scalar curvature of
E
. The following equation for the second variation of the perimeter holds.

Theorem 2.5

(Theorem 3.1 in
[
1
]
)
.

If
E
,
X
and
\nu
are as above, we have

\displaystyle\dfrac{\,\textnormal{d}^{2}}{\,\textnormal{d}t^{2}}\Big\lvert_{t=0}P(E_{t})=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}\left(|D_{\tau}(X\cdot\nu)|^{2}-|B_{E}|^{2}(X\cdot\nu)^{2}\right)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}-\int_{\partial E}H_{E}\textnormal{div}_{\tau}(X_{\tau}(X\cdot\nu))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial E}H_{E}(\textnormal{div}X)(X\cdot\nu)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

Remark 2.6
.

We remark that the last two integral in the above expression vanish when
E
is a critical set for the perimeter and if
|\Phi(\cdot,t)(E)|=|E|
for all
t\in[0,1]
. Indeed, if
E
is a regular critical set for the perimeter then its curvature is constant, therefore the second integral vanishes. Moreover, if the flow
\Phi
is volume-preserving then it can be shown (see equation (2.30) in
[
7
]
) that

0=\dfrac{\,\textnormal{d}^{2}|E_{t}|}{\,\textnormal{d}t^{2}}=\int_{\partial E}(\textnormal{div}X)(X\cdot\nu)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

Hence, if
\Phi
is a volume-preserving variation of a regular critical set
E
we have

\dfrac{\,\textnormal{d}^{2}}{\,\textnormal{d}t^{2}}\Big\lvert_{t=0}P(E_{t})=\int_{\partial E}\left(|D_{\tau}(X\cdot\nu)|^{2}-|B_{E}|^{2}(X\cdot\nu)^{2}\right)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=:\delta^{2}P(E)[X\cdot\nu_{E}].

We remark that due to the translation invariance of the perimeter functional, the second variation degenerates along flows of the form
\Phi(x,t)=x+t\eta,
where

\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. In view of this it is convenient to introduce the subspace
T(\partial E)
of
\tilde{H}^{1}(\partial E):=\left\{\varphi\in H^{1}(\partial E):\int_{\partial E}\varphi\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=0\right\}
generated by the functions
\nu_{i},

i=1,\dots,N
. Its orthogonal subspace, in the
L^{2}-
sense, will be denoted by
T^{\perp}(\partial E)
and is given by

T^{\perp}(\partial E)=\left\{\varphi\in\tilde{H}^{1}(\partial E):\int_{\partial E}\varphi\nu_{i}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=0,\ i=1,\dots,N\right\}.

Definition 2.7
.

We say that a regular critical set
E
is a strictly stable set if it has positive second variation of the perimeter, in the sense that

\delta^{2}P(E)[\varphi]>0,\qquad\forall\varphi\in T^{\perp}(\partial E)\setminus\{0\}.

The following result ensures that the second variation of a strictly stable set
E
is coercive on the subspace
T^{\perp}(\partial E).

Lemma 2.8

(Lemma 3.6 in
[
1
]
)
.

Assume that
E
is a strictly stable set, then

m_{0}:=\inf\{\delta^{2}P(E)[\varphi]:\varphi\in T^{\perp}(\partial E),\,\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\partial E)}=1\}>0

and

\delta^{2}P(E)[\varphi]\geq m_{0}\|\varphi\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\partial E)}\quad\forall\varphi\in T^{\perp}(\partial E).

Moreover, from the Step 1 in the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 3.9]
we obtain also the following result.

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume that
E
is a strictly stable set, then

\inf\left\{\delta^{2}P(E)[\varphi]:\varphi\in\tilde{H}^{1},\ \|\varphi\|_{H^{1}(\partial E)}=1,\ \left|\int_{\partial E}\varphi\nu_{E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right|\leq\delta\right\}\geq\dfrac{m_{0}}{2},

where the constant
m_{0}
is the one in Lemma
2.8
.

In the proof of our main result we will also need the following key lemma which shows
that any set
F
sufficiently close to
E
can be translated in such a way that the resulting set
\tilde{F}
satisfies
\partial\tilde{F}=\{x+\varphi(x)\nu_{E}(x):x\in\partial E\}
, with
\varphi
having a suitably small projection on
T(\partial E)
.

Lemma 2.10

(Lemma 3.8 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be of class
C^{3}
and let
p>N-1
. For every
\delta>0
there exist
C>0
and
\eta_{0}>0
such that if
F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
satisfies
\partial F=\{x+\psi(x)\nu_{E}(x):x\in\partial E\}
for some
\psi\in C^{2}({\partial E})
with
\|\psi\|_{W^{2,p}(\partial E)}\leq\eta_{0}
, then there exist
\sigma\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
and
\varphi\in W^{2,p}(\partial E)
with the properties that

|\sigma|\leq C\|\psi\|_{W^{2,p}(\partial E)},\quad\|\varphi\|_{W^{2,p}(\partial E)}\leq C\|\psi\|_{W^{2,p}(\partial E)}

and

\partial F+\sigma=\{x+\varphi(x)\nu_{E}(x):x\in\partial E\},\quad\left\lvert\int_{\partial E}\varphi\nu_{E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right\rvert\leq\delta\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}.

Let
E,F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be measurable sets. We define

\alpha(E,F):=\min_{x\in\mathbb{T}^{N}}|E\triangle(F+x)|.

In one of the main results of
[
1
]
the authors proved that the distance
\alpha(\cdot,\cdot)
between a set and a strictly stable set can be bounded by the square root of the difference of their perimeters.

Theorem 2.11

(Corollary 1.2 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a strictly stable set. Then, there exist
\sigma=\sigma(E)
,
C=C(E)>0
such that

C\alpha^{2}(E,F)\leq P(F)-P(E)

for all
F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
with
|F|=|E|
and
\alpha(E,F)<\sigma
.

3
A quantitative generalized Alexandrov Theorem

In this section, we will prove that local minimizers of the perimeter in the flat torus satisfy a quantitative Alexandrov-type estimate. We reproduce some arguments similar to the ones used in the proof of Theorem 1.3 in
[
19
]
.
In this section, we consider
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
a strictly stable set. Thanks to some classical results for sets of finite perimeter (see for example
[
15
, Theorem 27.4]
), the previous hypothesis implies that
E
is connected and it is of class
C^{\infty}
.

First of all, we compute the
(N-1)-
Jacobian of the map

\Phi:\partial E\to\partial E_{f}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\qquad x\mapsto x+f(x)\nu_{E}(x).

Given
x\in\partial E
, we choose an orthonormal basis

\mathcal{B^{\prime}}:=\left\{v_{1}(x),\dots,v_{N-1}(x)\right\}

of
T_{x}E
such that in this basis the second fundamental form of
E
,
B_{E}(x):T_{x}E\to T_{x}E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
has the following expression

B_{E}(x)=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{1}(x)&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\kappa_{N-1}(x)\\
0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix},

where
\kappa_{1}(x),\ldots,\kappa_{N-1}(x)
are the principal curvatures of
E
in
x
.
We then complete
\mathcal{B^{\prime}}
to a basis
\mathcal{B}
of the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the normal vector
v_{N}(x):=\nu_{E}(x)
.
In the following, to simplify the notation, we will drop the dependence on
x
.
The tangential differential
of
\Phi
with respect to the basis
\mathcal{B}
is given by

D\Phi=I+\nu_{E}\otimes\nabla f+fD\nu_{E},

where
I
is the immersion
T_{x}E\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\nabla f
is the tangential gradient of
f
and
D\nu_{E}
is the tangential differential of
\nu_{E}
.
Given the regularity of
\partial E
, we recall that
D\nu_{E}
is equal to
B_{E}
.
Moreover, by definition of
\mathcal{B}
, we have that

(\nu_{E}\otimes\nabla f)(v_{i},v_{j})=\delta_{N,i}\,\nabla f\cdot v_{j},\quad i=1,\ldots,N,\,\,j=1,\ldots,N-1.

Thanks to the previous observations we obtain

D\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}1&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&1\\
0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}0&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&0\\
\partial_{v_{1}}f&\dots&\partial_{v_{N-1}}f\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{1}f&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&\kappa_{N-1}f\\
0&\dots&0\end{pmatrix},

thus we find the following expression

D\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}1+\kappa_{1}f&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&1+\kappa_{N-1}f\\
\partial_{v_{1}}f&\dots&\partial_{v_{N-1}}f\end{pmatrix}.

(3.1)

By Binet formula, the Jacobian
J\Phi
can be explicitly computed as

\displaystyle J\Phi

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1+\kappa_{i}f)^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}(\partial_{v_{j}}f)^{2}\prod_{i\neq j}(1+\kappa_{i}f)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1+\kappa_{i}f)\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{(\partial_{v_{j}}f)^{2}}{(1+\kappa_{j}f)^{2}}\right)^{1/2}.

(3.2)

To show the previous formula, we characterize the minors of
D\Phi
. If we omit the
N-
th row of
D\Phi
, we obtain the minor

M_{N}=\begin{pmatrix}1+\kappa_{1}f&&\\
&\ddots&\\
&&1+\kappa_{N-1}f\end{pmatrix},

if we omit the
i-
th row of
D\Phi
for
1\leq i\leq N-1
, we obtain the minor

M_{i}=\begin{pmatrix}1+\kappa_{1}f&&&&&&\\
&\ddots&&&&&&\\
&&&1+\kappa_{i-1}f&&&&\\
&&&&1+\kappa_{i+1}f&&&\\
&&&&&&\ddots&&\\
&&&&&&&1+\kappa_{N-1}f\\
\partial_{v_{1}}f&\dots&&\partial_{v_{i-1}}f&\partial_{v_{i+1}}f&&\dots&\partial_{v_{N-1}}f\end{pmatrix}.

We then deduce (
3.2
) by explicitly computing

\det(M_{N})^{2}=\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1+\kappa_{i}f)^{2},\quad\det(M_{i})^{2}=(\partial_{v_{i}}f)^{2}\prod_{j\neq i}(1+\kappa_{j}f)^{2}.

The previous formula for
J\Phi
allows us to calculate some quantities that will be useful later on. Observe that, if
\|f\|_{C^{1}}
is small enough, the map
\Phi
is a diffeomorphism from
\partial E
to
\Phi(\partial E)=\partial E_{f}
, and thus the tangential differential
D\Phi:T_{x}E\to T_{\Phi(x)}E_{f}
is a surjective map. In particular, this allows us to calculate the normal
vector

\nu_{E_{f}}
in
\Phi(x)
. We remark that a vector
v
orthogonal to every column of (
3.1
) is a normal vector to the whole tangent space
T_{\Phi(x)}E_{f}
, therefore a possible
v
is given by

v=-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{\partial_{v_{i}}f}{1+\kappa_{i}f}v_{i}+\nu_{E},

where the sign of the component along
\nu_{E}
is taken positive so that the case
f=0
is consistent with the orientation of
\nu_{E}
.
Since
|v|\geq 1
, by normalizing
v
we obtain the normal
vector

\nu_{E_{f}}=\left(\nu_{E}-\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{\partial_{v_{i}}f}{1+\kappa_{i}f}v_{i}\right)\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{(\partial_{v_{j}}f)^{2}}{(1+\kappa_{j}f)^{2}}\right)^{-1/2},

(3.3)

moreover, we remark that

\nu_{E}\cdot\nu_{E_{f}}=\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{(\partial_{v_{j}}f)^{2}}{(1+\kappa_{j}f)^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}.

(3.4)

We can now compute explicitly the formula for the first variation of the perimeter.

Lemma 3.1
.

Setting
Q:=\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{(\partial_{v_{j}}f)^{2}}{(1+\kappa_{j}f)^{2}}\right)^{1/2}
, the following formulas hold true:

1.

If
f\in L^{\infty}(\partial E)\cap H^{1}(\partial E)
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
sufficiently small, then

P(E_{f})=\int_{\partial E}Q\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1+\kappa_{i}f)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

2.

If
f\in L^{\infty}(\partial E)\cap H^{1}(\partial E)
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}
sufficiently small, then the first variation
\delta P(E_{f})[\varphi]
exists for all
\varphi\in C^{1}(\partial E)
and is given by

\displaystyle\delta P(E_{f})[\varphi]=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}\varphi\,Q\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\kappa_{i}\prod_{j\neq i}(1+\kappa_{j}f)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial E}\dfrac{1}{Q}\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1+\kappa_{i}f)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{\partial_{v_{j}}\varphi\,\partial_{v_{j}}f}{(1+k_{j}f)^{2}}-\varphi\,\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{k_{j}\,(\partial_{v_{j}}f)^{2}}{(1+k_{j}f)^{3}}\right)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

(3.5)

Proof.

The first formula is a straightforward consequence of the area formula

P(E_{f})=\int_{\partial E_{f}}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\int_{\partial E}J\Phi\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

and of the expression of the Jacobian
J\Phi
in (
3.2
). Now, (
3.5
) easily follows by taking the derivatives

\dfrac{d}{d\varepsilon}\Big|_{\varepsilon=0}\ P(E_{f+\varepsilon\varphi})

in the first formula.
∎

In the following, with
C
we will refer to a positive constant, possibly changing from line to line, and we will specify its explicit dependence when needed.

Remark 3.2
.

We observe that, if
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial E)}
is small enough and
|E_{f}|=|E|
, then there exists a constant
C>0
, only depending on
E
, such that

\left|\int_{\partial E}f(x)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)\right|\leq C\int_{\partial E}f(x)^{2}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x).

(3.6)

Firstly, since
\partial E
is regular, for every
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small there exists a tubular neighborhood
\mathcal{N}
of
\partial E
such that
\mathcal{N}
is diffeomorphic to
\partial E\times(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
via the diffeomeorphism
\Psi(x,t)=x+\nu_{E}(x)t
.
The Jacobian of
\Psi
is given by

J\Psi(x,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1+\kappa_{i}(x)t).

(3.7)

Secondly, if
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial E)}
is small enough, we remark that the condition
|E_{f}|=|E|
is equivalent to

0=|E_{f}|-|E|=\int_{\partial E}\int_{0}^{f(x)}J\Psi(x,t)\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x).

Then, we can conclude that

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}\int_{0}^{f(x)}J\Psi(x,t)\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}f(x)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)+\int_{\partial E}\int_{0}^{f(x)}(J\Psi(x,t)-1)\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}f(x)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)+\int_{\partial E}\int_{0}^{f(x)}(H_{E}(x)\,t+o(t))\,\textnormal{d}t\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x),

that implies (
3.6
) for a constant depending only on
N
and the principal curvatures of
E
.

We are now able to prove the following stability result; it ensures that the second variation of the perimeter remains strictly positive for small normal deformations of a strictly stable set
E
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix
N\geq 2
.
There exists
\delta=\delta(E)>0
small such that, if
f\in L^{\infty}(\partial E)\cap H^{1}(\partial E)
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial E)}\leq\delta
,

\left\lvert\int_{\partial E}f(x)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)\right\rvert\leq\delta\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\lvert\int_{\partial E}f(x)\nu_{E}(x)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)\right\rvert\leq\delta\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)},

(3.8)

then we have

\delta^{2}P(E)[f]=\int_{\partial E}(|\nabla f(x)|^{2}-|B_{E}(x)|^{2}f(x)^{2})\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)\geq\frac{m_{0}}{8}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial E)}^{2},

where
m_{0}
is the constant given by Lemma
2.8
.

Proof.

Set
g=f-\bar{f}
, where
\bar{f}=\fint_{\partial E}f\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
, then
g
has zero average and, by the first inequality in (
3.8
), we have

\bar{f}^{2}=\frac{1}{P(E)^{2}}\left(\int_{\partial E}f\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right)^{2}\leq C\delta^{2}\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\partial E)}.

(3.9)

If
\delta
is sufficiently small, from (
3.9
) we obtain

\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}=\|f-\bar{f}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}-\bar{f}^{2}P(E)\geq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}\left(1-C\delta^{2}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}.

Using the previous inequality, (
3.9
) again and the second inequality in (
3.8
) we infer that the function
g
satisfies

\left\lvert\int_{\partial E}g\nu_{E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\int_{\partial E}f\nu_{E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right\rvert+\left\lvert\int_{\partial E}\bar{f}\nu_{E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right\rvert\leq C\delta\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}.

Then, we can apply Lemma
2.9
to obtain

\delta^{2}P(E)[g]\geq\dfrac{m_{0}}{2}\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial E)}^{2},

provided
\delta
small enough. We conclude

\displaystyle\delta^{2}P(E)[f]

\displaystyle=\delta^{2}P(E)[g]-\delta^{2}P(E)[g]+\delta^{2}P(E)[f]

\displaystyle=\delta^{2}P(E)[g]-2\bar{f}\int_{\partial E}|B_{E}(x)|^{2}f(x)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)+\bar{f}^{2}\int_{\partial E}|B_{E}(x)|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{m_{0}}{2}\|g\|_{H^{1}(\partial E)}^{2}-C|\bar{f}|\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}\geq\dfrac{m_{0}}{2}(\|g\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}+\|\nabla g\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2})-C\delta\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{m_{0}}{4}(\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2})-C\delta\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}\geq\dfrac{m_{0}}{8}\|f\|_{H^{1}(\partial E)}^{2},

up to taking
\delta
smaller if needed, and where the constant
C>0
only depends on
E
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Remark
3.2
ensures that the conclusion of the previous lemma also holds if we replace the hypothesis
|\int_{\partial E}f\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}|\leq\delta\|f\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}
with
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\partial E)}
small enough and
|E_{f}|=|E|
.

We are now able to prove the generalized version of the quantitative Alexandrov’s inequality in the periodic setting, Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

First of all we notice that, if we take the constant
C
in (
1.3
) to be bigger than
\sqrt{P(E)/2}
, then it is enough to consider only the case
\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}\leq 1
.

Set
p=x+f(x)\nu_{E}(x)
and let
\varphi\in C^{1}(\partial E)
, by the definition of scalar mean curvature
H_{E_{f}}
and a change of coordinates we obtain

\delta P(E_{f})[\varphi]=\int_{\partial E}(H_{E_{f}}\nu_{E_{f}})(p)\cdot\nu_{E}\,\varphi\,J\Phi\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

(3.10)

Combining (
3.10
), (
3.2
) and (
3.4
) we obtain

\delta P(E_{f})[\varphi]=\int_{\partial E}H_{E_{f}}\varphi\,J\Phi\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\dfrac{(\partial_{v_{j}}f)^{2}}{(1+\kappa_{j}f)^{2}}\right)^{-1/2}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\int_{\partial E}H_{E_{f}}\varphi\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}(1+\kappa_{i}f)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

In the following, with a slight abuse of notation, with the symbol
O(g)
we will mean any function
h
of the form
h(x)=r(x)g(x)
, where
|r(x)|\leq C
for all
x\in\partial E
and
C
is a constant depending only on
N
and
E
.

By a simple Taylor expansion we have

\delta P(E_{f})[\varphi]=\int_{\partial E}H_{E_{f}}\varphi\,\left(1+H_{E}f+O(f^{2})\right)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

(3.11)

From (
3.5
) and again by Taylor expansion, we obtain

\displaystyle\delta P(E_{f})[\varphi]=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}\left(H_{E}+f\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\kappa_{i}\sum_{s\neq i}\kappa_{s}+O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2})\right)\varphi\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial E}(\nabla f+h)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}\left(H_{E}+fH^{2}_{E}-|B_{E}|^{2}f+O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2})\right)\varphi\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial E}(\nabla f+h)\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

(3.12)

where
\nabla f,\nabla\varphi
are respectively the tangent gradient of
f,\varphi
on
\partial E
and
h
is a vector field satisfying
|h|\leq C(|f|+|\nabla f|^{2}))|\nabla f|
.
Set
R=O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2})
, by comparing (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) we infer that

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}(\nabla f\cdot\nabla\varphi-|B_{E}|^{2}f\varphi)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}(H_{E_{f}}-H_{E})\left(1+H_{E}f+R\right)\varphi\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial E}(h\cdot\nabla\varphi+(O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\varphi)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

(3.13)

Testing (
3.13
) with
\varphi=1
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}(H_{E_{f}}-H_{E})\left(1+H_{E}f+R\right)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\int_{\partial E}(O(|f|)+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1},

then, for
\delta
sufficiently small, using Hölder inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\overline{H}_{E_{f}}-H_{E}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|-\fint_{\partial E}(H_{E_{f}}-H_{E})(H_{E}f+R)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}+\fint_{\partial E}(O(|f|)+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\fint_{\partial E}(H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}})(H_{E}f+R)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right|+\left|\fint_{\partial E}(\overline{H}_{E_{f}}-H_{E})(H_{E}f+R)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right|

\displaystyle\ \ +\int_{\partial E}(O(|f|)+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq\delta\,\dfrac{|H_{E}|+C\delta}{P(E)}\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}}+\delta\left(|H_{E}|+C\delta\right)|\overline{H}_{E_{f}}-H_{E}|

\displaystyle\ \ +\int_{\partial E}(O(|f|)+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1},

with
C=C(N,E)
since
\delta\leq 1
.
For
\delta
small enough, recalling that
\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}}\leq 1
, the previous inequality implies

\frac{1}{2}|\overline{H}_{E_{f}}-H_{E}|\leq C\delta\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}}+\int_{\partial E}(O(|f|)+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\leq C\delta.

(3.14)

Using the bound
\|f\|_{C^{1}}\leq\delta
and the definition of
h
we easily see that

h\cdot\nabla f=\delta\,O(|\nabla f|^{2}).

Testing (
3.13
) with
\varphi=f
, using Hölder’s inequality and by the previous remark, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}(|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle-|B_{E}|^{2}f^{2})\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\int_{\partial E}\left(H_{E_{f}}-H_{E}\right)(1+H_{E}f+R)f\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle+\delta\int_{\partial E}(O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int(H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}})(1+H_{E}f+R)f\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}+\int(\overline{H}_{E_{f}}-H_{E})(1+H_{E}f+R)f\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle+\delta\int_{\partial E}(O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}+|\overline{H}_{E_{f}}-H_{E}|\int(1+H_{E}f+R)f\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle+\delta\int_{\partial E}(O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,C\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}+|\overline{H}_{E_{f}}-H_{E}|\int(f+O(f^{2})+fO(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle+\delta\int_{\partial E}(O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

(3.15)

By (
3.6
), (
3.14
) and by Hölder inequality, we obtain

|\overline{H}_{E_{f}}-H_{E}|\int(f+O(f^{2})+fO(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\leq\delta\int_{\partial E}(O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2})).

Finally, by the above inequality, (
3.6
) again and by combining (
3
) with (
3.14
)
we deduce that, for any
\eta>0
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\partial E}(|\nabla f|^{2}-|B_{E}|^{2}f^{2})\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}}\|f\|_{H^{1}}+\delta\int_{\partial E}(O(f^{2})+O(|\nabla f|^{2}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{\eta}C^{2}\|H_{E_{f}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\eta\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+C\delta\|f\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

(3.16)

The conclusion then follows combining (
3.16
) with Lemma
3.3
and taking
\delta
and
\eta
sufficiently small.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

For some particular choices of the set
E
, a geometric explanation of the condition

\left\lvert\int_{\partial E}f\nu_{E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right\rvert\leq\delta\|f\|_{L^{2}}

(3.17)

can be found. It is the case for the ball, the cylinder or the lamella. For example consider
E=B_{r}
, the case where
E
is a cylinder or a lamella being analogous. We show that in this case condition (
3.17
) follows from enforcing

\text{bar}(E_{f})=\text{bar}(B_{r})=0.

Indeed, consider the case
r=1
for simplicity, the barycenter in polar coordinates is given by

0=\dfrac{1}{(N+1)\omega_{N}}\int_{\partial B}(1+f)^{N+1}x\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N+1}

and thus, by a simple Taylor expansion, we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B}\left(1+(N+1)f+\frac{1}{2}Rf^{2}\right)x\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

\displaystyle=(N+1)\int_{\partial B}f(x)x\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial B}xRf^{2}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

where
|R(x)|\leq C(N)
for every
x\in\partial B.

We can then estimate

\left\lvert\int_{\partial B}f(x)x\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right\rvert\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

provided
\|f\|_{C^{1}}\leq\delta
and the conclusion follows recalling
\nu_{B}(x)=x
.

4
Uniform
L^{1}-
estimate on the discrete flow

In this section we give the precise definition of the discrete volume preserving flow in the flat torus and we study some of its properties. In particular, we prove Proposition
4.6
that will play a crucial role in the proof of our main result.

4.1
Discrete volume preserving mean-curvature flow

Let
E\neq\emptyset
be a measurable subset of
\mathbb{T}^{N}
. In the following we will always assume that
E
coincides with its Lebesgue representative.
Fixed
h>0
,
m\in(0,1)
, we consider the minimum problem

\min\left\{P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F}\textnormal{sd}_{E}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x:F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N},\,\lvert F\rvert=m\right\},

(4.1)

where
\textnormal{sd}_{E}(x):=\textnormal{dist}_{E}(x)-\textnormal{dist}_{\mathbb{T}^{N}\setminus E}(x)
is the signed distance from the set
E
. Observe that the minimum problem (
4.1
) is equivalent to the problem

\min\left\{P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F\triangle E}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x:F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N},\,\lvert F\rvert=m\right\}.

For every
F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
, we set

J_{h}^{E}(F):=P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F\triangle E}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x=:P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(F,E),

(4.2)

with a little abuse of notation we will sometimes denote by
J_{h}^{E}
also the functional

F\mapsto P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F}\textnormal{sd}_{E}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x

and, when no ambiguity arises, we will write
J_{h}
instead of
J_{h}^{E}
.

By induction we can now define the
discrete-in-time, volume preserving mean curvature flow

(E_{h}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and we will refer to it as the
discrete flow.

Let
E_{0}\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a measurable set such that
\lvert E_{0}\rvert=m
, we define
E_{h}^{1}
as a solution of (
4.1
) with
E_{0}
instead of
E
, i.e.

E_{h}^{1}\in\textnormal{argmin}\left\{P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F}\textnormal{sd}_{E_{0}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x:F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N},\,\lvert F\rvert=m\right\}.

Assume that
E_{h}^{k}
is defined for
1\leq k\leq n-1
, we define
E_{h}^{n}
as a solution of (
4.1
) with
E
replaced by
E_{h}^{n-1}
, i.e.

E_{h}^{n}\in\textnormal{argmin}\left\{P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F}\textnormal{sd}_{E_{h}^{n-1}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x:F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N},\,\lvert F\rvert=m\right\}.

Remark 4.1
.

We start by remarking that the sequence of the perimeters along the discrete flow is non-increasing. Indeed, from the minimality of
E_{h}^{n}
and considering
E^{n-1}_{h}
as a competitor we obtain

P(E_{h}^{n})\leq P(E_{h}^{n})+\frac{1}{h}\int_{E^{n-1}_{h}\triangle E^{n}_{h}}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E^{n-1}_{h}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x\leq P(E^{n-1}_{h}).

From this simple remark we observe that, even if the starting set of the flow
E_{0}
is not of finite perimeter, the perimeters of the sets
E_{h}^{n}
are uniformly bounded by a constant that only depends on the dimension
N
, the fixed volume
m
and
h
. Given any set
E_{0}
of volume
m
, consider the cube
Q_{m}
of the same volume. From the minimality of
E_{h}^{1}
and using
Q_{m}
as a competitor we obtain

\displaystyle P(E^{1}_{h})

\displaystyle\leq P(Q_{m})+\frac{1}{h}\int_{E_{0}\triangle Q_{m}}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E_{0}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x-\frac{1}{h}\int_{E_{0}\triangle E_{1}^{h}}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E_{0}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq P(Q_{m})+\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\sqrt{N}=C(N,m,h),

where we estimated
\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E_{0}}\leq\text{diam}(\mathbb{T}^{N})=\sqrt{N}
.

We recall some preliminary results that can be found in
[
19
]
. If not otherwise stated, their original proofs can be easily adapted to the periodic case, the major difference being that in our case we work in the flat torus, which is compact, thus simplifying some arguments.
First of all, we observe that that the problem (
4.1
) admits solutions via the standard method of the calculus of variations.

The regularity properties of the discrete flow are investigated in the following proposition. Some of the results are classical, others follow from
[
19
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
h,

m
,
M>0
and let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a set with
\lvert E\rvert=m
and
P(E)\leq M
. Then, any solution
F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
to (
4.1
) satisfies the following regularity properties:

i)

There exist
c_{0}=c_{0}(N)>0
and a radius
r_{0}=r_{0}(m,h,N,M)>0
such that for every
x\in\partial^{*}F
and
r\in(0,r_{0}]
we have

\lvert B_{r}(x)\cap F\rvert\geq c_{0}r^{N}\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert B_{r}(x)\setminus F\rvert\geq c_{0}r^{N}.

In particular,
F
admits an open representative whose topological boundary coincides with the closure of its reduced boundary, i.e.
\partial F=\overline{\partial^{*}F}
.

ii)

There exists
\Lambda=\Lambda(m,h,N,M)>0
such that
F
is a
\Lambda
-minimizer of the perimeter, that is

P(F)\leq P(F^{\prime})+\Lambda\lvert F\triangle F^{\prime}\rvert

for all measurable set
F^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
.

iii)

The following Euler-Lagrange equation holds: there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
X\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{N},\mathbb{T}^{N})
we have

\int_{\partial^{*}F}\dfrac{\textnormal{sd}_{E}}{h}X\cdot\nu_{F}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}+\int_{\partial^{*}F}\textnormal{div}_{\tau}X\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\lambda\int_{\partial^{*}F}X\cdot\nu_{F}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}.

(4.3)

iv)

There exists a closed set
\Sigma
,
whose Hausdorff dimension is less than or equal to
N-8
, such that
\partial^{*}F=\partial F\setminus\Sigma
is an
(N-1)
-submanifold of class
C^{2,\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
with

\lvert H_{F}(x)\rvert\leq\Lambda,\quad\text{for all }x\in\partial F\setminus\Sigma.

v)

There exists
k_{0}=k_{0}(m,h,N,M)\in\mathbb{N}
and
s_{0}=s_{0}(m,h,N,M)>0
such that
F
is made up of at most
k_{0}
connected components having mutual Hausdorff distance at least
s_{0}
.

The following result characterizes the stationary sets of the discrete scheme.
The last assertion of the proposition is a technical result that will be employed in the proof of Lemma
4.4
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Every stationary set
E
for the discrete flow is a critical set of the perimeter.

Viceversa, if
E
is a
regular
critical set of the perimeter, then there exists
h^{*}=h^{*}(E)>0
such that, for every
h<h^{*}
, the volume preserving discrete flow starting from
E
is unique and given by
E_{h}^{n}=E.

Moreover, if
E
is a strictly stable set then it is also the unique volume-constrained minimizer of the functional

\tilde{J}_{h}(F):=P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F}\textnormal{dist}_{E}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x.

Proof.

The first statement is an immediate consequence of (
4.3
). Since
E
is a stationary point for the discrete flow, it satisfies

\int_{\partial^{*}E}\textnormal{div}_{\tau}X\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}=\lambda\int_{\partial^{*}E}X\cdot\nu_{E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

for all
X\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{T}^{N},\mathbb{T}^{N})
, i.e.
E
is a critical point for the perimeter.

The second part follows using the same argument of the proof of
[
19
, Proposition 3.2]
. Indeed, recall that the second variation has the following expression

\partial^{2}J_{h}(E)[\varphi]=\int_{\partial E}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\left(\dfrac{1}{h}-|B_{E}|^{2}\right)\varphi^{2}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1},

which is positive if
h
is small enough. Then we procede as in the proof of
[
19
, Proposition 3.2]
.

Analogously, we prove that
E
is the unique volume-constrained minimizer of
\tilde{J}_{h}
. Firstly, observe that, by Theorem
2.11
,
E
is a strict local
L^{1}
-minimizer of the perimeter and it is a global minimizer of the second term in
\tilde{J}_{h}
. Therefore, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\tilde{J}_{h}(E)<\tilde{J}_{h}(F)

for all measurable set
F
such that
|F|=|E|
and
|E\triangle F|\leq\varepsilon
, i.e.
E
is an isolated local minimizer for
\tilde{J}_{h}
in
L^{1}
with the volume constraint, with minimality neighbourhood uniform with respect to
h
. Now, given any sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
going to zero, let
F_{n}
be a volume constrained minimizer of
J_{h_{n}}
; we then easily deduce that
|E\triangle F_{n}|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, and therefore, for
n
large enough,
|E\triangle F_{n}|\leq\varepsilon
. The strict minimality of
E
therefore implies
F_{n}=E
.

∎

4.2
Uniform
L^{1}
estimate

In this subsection we prove a uniform
L^{1}-
estimate on the discrete flow starting from an initial set
E_{0}
sufficiently “close” to a strictly stable set of the perimeter. We will devote the next subsection to a discussion upon the hypotheses of the estimate. Before we recall the definition of Hausdorff distance and some of its properties, for a complete reference see e.g.
[
3
, Section 4.4]
,
[
18
, Section 10.1]
.

Given a set
C\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
, we denote by
(C)_{\delta}
the
\delta
fattened of
C
, that is the set

\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{N}:\textnormal{dist}_{C}(x)\leq\delta\}.

Let
C_{1},

C_{2}\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be closed sets, we define the
Hausdorff distance
between
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
as

\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(C_{1},C_{2}):=\inf\left\{\rho>0:C_{1}\subset(C_{2})_{\rho},\,C_{2}\subset(C_{1})_{\rho}\right\}.

Given
C_{n},

C
closed sets in
\mathbb{T}^{N}
, we say that
(C_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
C
in the Hausdorff distance and we write
C_{n}\overset{H}{\to}C
, if
\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(C_{n},C)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We recall that the space of closed subsets of a compact set equipped with the Hausdorff metric is compact (see e.g
[
3
, Theorem 4.4.15]
or
[
18
, Proposition 10.1]
) and also that the convergence in the Hausdorff distance is equivalent to the uniform convergence of the respective distance functions, i.e.

C_{n}\overset{H}{\to}C\quad\iff\quad\textnormal{dist}_{C_{n}}\to\textnormal{dist}_{C}\quad\text{uniformly}.

In the following, given two open smooth sets
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
, we will denote by
\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(E_{1},E_{2})
the Hausdorff distance between their closures.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a strictly stable set and let
\varepsilon>0
. Then, there exist
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,E)>0
and
h^{*}=h^{*}(E)>0
such that, for every
h<h^{*}
and for every set
E_{0}
satisfying

|E_{0}|=|E|,\qquad\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(\overline{E}_{0},\overline{E})\leq\delta,

we have

|E\triangle F|\leq\varepsilon,

where
F
is a solution of (
4.1
) with
E_{0}
replacing
E
.

Proof.

Let
h^{*}=h^{*}(E)
be the constant given by Proposition
4.3
so that, for every
h<h^{*}
,
E
is the unique volume-constrained global minimizer of the functional

\tilde{J}_{h}(G):=P(G)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{G}\textnormal{dist}_{E}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x.

(4.4)

Fix
h<h^{*}
and let
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of sets satisfying

|E_{n}|=|E|,\qquad\overline{E}_{n}\overset{H}{\to}\overline{E}.

(4.5)

Consider
F_{n}
a solution of (
4.1
) with
E_{n}
replacing
E
. We claim that

F_{n}\overset{L^{1}}{\to}E.

If we prove the claim, the conclusion easily follows.

First, Remark
4.1
ensures that
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of sets with uniformly bounded perimeters, with the bound depending only on
N,m,h
. Therefore, there exist
F
a set of finite perimeter such that
|F|=m
and a (unrelabelled) subsequence of
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

F_{n}\overset{L^{1}}{\to}F.

Now, let
K
be a compact subset of
\mathbb{T}^{N}
such that, up to a subsequence, we have

\overline{E_{n}^{c}}\overset{H}{\to}K.

From the second property in (
4.5
) we easily deduce that
(\overline{E})^{c}\subset K
, and therefore
K^{c}\subset\overline{E}
. In particular, this inclusion implies that

\int_{K^{c}}\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x=\int_{E}\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x\geq\int_{G}\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x

for every
G\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
.
Setting

\bar{J}_{h}(G):=P(G)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{G}(\textnormal{dist}_{E}(x)-\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x))\,\textnormal{d}x,

from the previous remark and from the fact that
E
is the unique minimizer of (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{h}(G)

\displaystyle=\tilde{J}_{h}(G)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{G}\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle>\tilde{J}_{h}(E)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{G}\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{J}_{h}(E)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{E}\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x=\bar{J}_{h}(E),

for any measurable set
G\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
with
|G|=|E|
. Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{h}(F)

\displaystyle=P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F}\left(\textnormal{dist}_{E}(x)-\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x)\right)\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}P(F_{n})+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F}\left(\textnormal{dist}_{E}(x)-\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x)\right)\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(P(F_{n})+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F_{n}}\left(\textnormal{dist}_{E_{n}}(x)-\textnormal{dist}_{E_{n}^{c}}(x)\right)\,\textnormal{d}x\right)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(P(E)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{E}\left(\textnormal{dist}_{E_{n}}(x)-\textnormal{dist}_{E_{n}^{c}}(x)\right)\,\textnormal{d}x\right)

\displaystyle=P(E)-\frac{1}{h}\int_{E}\textnormal{dist}_{K}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x=\bar{J}_{h}(E)

where we exploited the lower-semicontinuity of the perimeter and the minimality of
F_{n}
.
Since
E
is the unique volume-constrained minimizer of
\bar{J}_{h}
, the set
F
must coincide with
E
and this concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

We remark that under the hypotheses of Lemma
4.4
we could have just assumed the one-sided inclusion

\overline{E}_{0}\subset(E)_{\delta^{*}}

instead of

\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(\overline{E}_{0},\overline{E})\leq\delta

for a suitable
\delta^{*}\leq\delta
. Indeed, let
\delta_{n}\to 0
and
E_{n}\subset(E)_{\delta_{n}}
such that
|E_{n}|=|E|
. We prove that
\overline{E}_{n}
converges to
\overline{E}
in the sense of Kuratowski (and thus with respect to Hausdorff).
Let
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence such that
x_{n}\in\overline{E}_{n}
and
x_{n}\to y
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
y_{n}\in E
such that
|x_{n}-y_{n}|\leq\delta_{n}.
Therefore, for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n_{0}
such that, for
n\geq n_{0}
, we have

|y_{n}-y|\leq|y_{n}-x_{n}|+|x_{n}-y|\leq\delta_{n}+\varepsilon,

that is
y_{n}\to y
. Since
(y_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset E
, we have
y\in\overline{E}
.

Fix now
y\in\overline{E}
. Assume by contradiction that there exists
\delta>0
such that
\textnormal{dist}_{E_{n}}(y)>\delta
, i.e. it doesn’t exist a sequence of elements in
\overline{E}_{n}
converging to
y
. From this (and up to subsequences) it follows

E_{n}\subset(E)_{\delta_{n}}\setminus B_{\delta}(y)\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}|E_{n}|\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}|(E)_{\delta_{n}}\setminus B_{\delta}(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}|(E)_{\delta_{n}}\setminus\left(B_{\delta}(y)\cap E\right)|

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}|(E)_{\delta_{n}}|-|B_{\delta}(y)\cap E|=m-|B_{\delta}(y)\cap E|

which is a contradiction.

We are now able to prove the main estimate that will be used in the proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Proposition 4.6

(Uniform
L^{1}-
estimate)
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a strictly stable set. Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist
\delta^{*}=\delta^{*}(\varepsilon,E)>0
and
h^{*}=h^{*}(E)>0
with the following property: for every
h<h^{*}
, if
E_{0}
is a measurable set such that

|E_{0}|=|E|,\qquad\overline{E}_{0}\subset(E)_{\delta^{*}},

then the discrete flow
(E_{h}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
starting from
E_{0}
satisfies

\alpha(E,E^{n}_{h})\leq\varepsilon

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Fix
h<h^{*}
, where
h^{*}=h^{*}(E)
is the constant given by Lemma
4.4
and let
\sigma=\sigma(E)
,
C=C(E)
be the constants of Theorem
2.11
.
Moreover, let

\delta:=\delta(\sigma,E)
be the constant given by Lemma
4.4
with
\sigma
replacing
\varepsilon
.
Set
\delta^{*}\leq\delta
to be chosen later and consider
E_{0}
such that

|E_{0}|=|E|,\qquad\overline{E}_{0}\subset(E)_{\delta^{*}}.

Recall that, from Remark
4.5
and from the hypothesis
\overline{E}_{0}\subset(E)_{\delta^{*}}
, without loss of generality, we can assume
\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(\overline{E}_{0},\overline{E})\leq\delta^{*}
.
Moreover, by the regularity of
E
, we can also suppose
\alpha(E_{0},E)\leq\tilde{C}\delta^{*}
, for a suitable constant
\tilde{C}>0
that only depends on
E
.
From Lemma
4.4
we have that

|E_{h}^{1}\triangle E|\leq\sigma.

(4.6)

Let
x_{0}
be such that
\alpha(E_{0},E)=\lvert E_{0}\triangle(E+x_{0})\rvert
. By choosing
E+x_{0}
as a competitor for the minimality of
E_{h}^{1}
and estimating dist
{}_{\partial E_{0}}\leq\text{diam}(\mathbb{T}^{N})=\sqrt{N}
, we find

P(E_{h}^{1})-P(E)\leq\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{E_{0}\triangle(E+x_{0})}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E_{0}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x\leq\dfrac{\sqrt{N}}{h}\alpha(E_{0},E)\leq\frac{\sqrt{N}}{h}\tilde{C}\delta^{*}.

By (
4.6
), we can apply Theorem
2.11
and the previous estimate to obtain

\displaystyle\alpha(E_{h}^{1},E)\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{C}}\sqrt{P(E_{h}^{1})-P(E)}\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{C}}\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{N}}{h}\alpha(E,E_{0})}\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{C}}\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{N}}{h}\tilde{C}\delta^{*}}\leq\min\{\sigma,\delta,\varepsilon\},

where we have chosen
\delta^{*}
such that
\delta^{*}\leq Ch\left(\min\{\sigma,\delta,\varepsilon\}\right)^{2}/(\tilde{C}\sqrt{N}).

Since
E_{h}^{1}
is a
\Lambda-
minimizer and
E
is regular, up to taking
\delta^{*}
smaller, the classical regularity theory for
\Lambda-
minimizers (see Theorem
2.4
) implies

\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(\partial E_{h}^{1},\partial E+x_{1})\leq\delta,

where
x_{1}
is such that
\alpha(E_{h}^{1},E)=|E_{h}^{1}\triangle(E+x_{1})|
.

Now we iterate the procedure: by induction, suppose that

\alpha(E_{h}^{n-1},E)\leq\min\{\sigma,\delta,\varepsilon\},\qquad\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(\partial E_{h}^{n-1},\partial E+x_{n-1})\leq\delta

(4.7)

where
x_{n-1}
is such that
|E_{h}^{n-1}\triangle(E+x_{n-1})|=\alpha(E_{h}^{n-1},E)
. Observe that the second inequality in (
4.7
) implies that
\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(\overline{E}_{h}^{n-1},\overline{E}+x_{n-1})\leq\delta
, therefore
E_{h}^{n-1}
and
E+x_{n-1}
satisfy the hypotheses of Lemma
4.4
and thus

|E_{h}^{n}\triangle(E+x_{n-1})|\leq\sigma.

Observe that by definition
\alpha(E_{h}^{n},E+x_{n-1})=\alpha(E_{h}^{n},E)
. Now, by Theorem
2.11
and the monotonicity of the perimeters along the discrete flow we obtain

\displaystyle\alpha(E_{h}^{n},E)

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{C}}\sqrt{P(E_{h}^{n})-P(E)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{C}}\sqrt{P(E_{h}^{1})-P(E)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{C}}\sqrt{\dfrac{\sqrt{N}}{h}\tilde{C}\delta^{*}}\leq\min\{\sigma,\delta,\varepsilon\}.

Again, thanks to the choice of
\delta^{*}
, the hypotheses of Theorem
2.4
are satisfied and thus

\,\textnormal{d}_{H}(\partial E_{h}^{n},\partial E+x_{n})\leq\delta,

where
x_{n}
is such that
\alpha(E_{h}^{n},E)=|E_{h}^{n}\triangle(E+x_{n})|
. This concludes the proof.
∎

4.3
Some remarks on the hypothesis of the
L^{1}-
estimate

In this subsection we show that Proposition
4.6
does not hold if we weaken the hypothesis of closeness in the Hausdorff distance between the starting set
E_{0}
and the strictly stable set
E
. In particular, we prove that the sole hypothesis of closeness in
L^{1}
and in perimeter is not enough. We remark that a modification of this example yields the same result in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Fix
h>0
and
G\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
. Recall that, for any set
F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
such that
|F|=|G|
, we have set

J_{h}^{G}(F):=P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\mathcal{\int}_{F\triangle G}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial G}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x.

(4.8)

Proposition 4.7
.

There exist
m>0
and a sequence
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
with the following properties:
|E_{n}|=m
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
P(E_{n})
is uniformly bounded and, letting
F_{n}
be any volume-constrained minimizer of (
4.8
) with
E_{n}
instead of
G
, we have

E_{n}\overset{L^{1}}{\to}E,\quad P(E_{n})\to P(E)\quad\text{but}\quad F_{n}\overset{L^{1}}{\to}F,

where
E
is a lamella and
F
is such that
|E\triangle F|>0.

Proof.

Let
m>0
such that the ball of volume
m
has perimeter strictly less than the one of the lamella of the same volume; we remark that for every smaller volume
m^{\prime}\leq m
the same property holds.
Let
E
be a lamella of measure
m
, recall that
E
is a strictly stable set of the perimeter in
\mathbb{T}^{N}
.
From the assumption on
m
it follows that
E
is only a local minimizer of the perimeter and not a global one.

Step 1.
Firstly, we construct a sequence
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
E_{n}\to E
in
L^{1}
and
\partial E_{n}\to\mathbb{T}^{N}
in the Hausdorff distance.
We define
E_{n}
by adding to
E
some balls contained in
\mathbb{T}^{N}\setminus E
and of overall small volume, and by subtracting to
E
balls contained in
E
with the same overall volume.

Recall that
\mathbb{T}^{N}=[0,1]^{N}/\mathbb{Z}^{N}
.
In the following, with a little abuse of notation, we will identify
\mathbb{T}^{N}
and
[0,1)^{N}
.
We define

\displaystyle I_{n}:

\displaystyle=\left\{\underline{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}^{N}:\,0\leq k_{i}\leq 2^{n}-1\quad\forall i=1,\ldots N\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}:

\displaystyle=\left\{Q_{n,\underline{k}}:=\Big[0,\frac{1}{2^{n}}\Big)^{N}+\frac{\underline{k}}{2^{n}}:\,\underline{k}\in I_{n}\right\},

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Up to choosing
m
smaller, we can assume that
m=1/2^{s}
for some
s\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, we can suppose, up to translations, that
E=[0,1)^{N-1}\times(0,1/2^{s})
, thus for
n\geq s
we have

E=\textnormal{Int}\left(\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in I_{n},\,\,0\leq k_{N}\leq 2^{n-s}-1}Q_{n,\underline{k}}\right),

where
\textnormal{Int}(\cdot)
denotes the interior of a set in
\mathbb{T}^{N}
.
For every
n\geq s
and

\underline{k}\in I_{n}
, we consider the balls
B_{n,\underline{k}}\subset Q_{n,\underline{k}}
centered in the center of the cube
Q_{n,\underline{k}}
and of radius
r_{n,\underline{k}}
chosen in such a way that

\left\lvert\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in I_{n},\,\,0\leq k_{N}\leq 2^{n-s}-1}B_{n,\underline{k}}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in I_{n},\,\,2^{n-s}\leq k_{N}\leq 2^{n}-1}B_{n,\underline{k}}\right\rvert.

(4.9)

Moreover, we can also take the radii
r_{n,\underline{k}}
sufficiently small so that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\lvert\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in I_{n}}B_{n,\underline{k}}\right\rvert=0,\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}P\left(\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in I_{n}}B_{n,\underline{k}}\right)=0.

(4.10)

Set now

\displaystyle A_{n}

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in I_{n},\,\,0\leq k_{N}\leq 2^{n-s}-1}B_{n,\underline{k}}\subset\textnormal{Int}\left(\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in I_{n},\,\,0\leq k_{N}\leq 2^{n-s}-1}Q_{n,\underline{k}}\right)=E,

\displaystyle C_{n}

\displaystyle:=\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in I_{n},\,\,2^{n-s}\leq k_{N}\leq 2^{n}-1}B_{n,\underline{k}}\subset\bigcup_{\underline{k}\in I_{n},\,\,2^{n-s}\leq k_{N}\leq 2^{n}-1}Q_{n,\underline{k}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}\setminus E.

Define
E_{n}=(E\cup C_{n})\setminus A_{n}
and observe that, by (
4.9
), we have
|E_{n}|=|E|
. Now, by (
4.10
), we also obtain

E_{n}\overset{L^{1}}{\to}E\quad\textnormal{ and }\quad P(E_{n})\to P(E).

Observe that, from the definition of
A_{n}
and
C_{n}
, we have that

(\partial A_{n})_{\sqrt{N}/2^{n}}\cup(\partial C_{n})_{\sqrt{N}/2^{n}}=\mathbb{T}^{N}

and therefore the set
\partial E_{n}=\partial E\cup\partial C_{n}\cup\partial A_{n}
converges in the Hausdorff metric to the whole
\mathbb{T}^{N}
as
n\to+\infty
. Therefore we have constructed a sequence
(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
that satisfies

E_{n}\overset{L^{1}}{\to}E,\qquad P(E_{n})\to P(E),\qquad\partial E_{n}\overset{H}{\to}\mathbb{T}^{N}.

(4.11)

Step 2.
Let
E_{n}
be the sets previously defined.
We consider the space
X=\{F\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}\,:\,F\text{ is measurable}\}
endowed with the
L^{1}-
distance, i.e.
\textnormal{dist}_{L^{1}}(F,G)=|F\triangle G|
for every
F,G\in X
. We extend our functional in the following way

\tilde{J}^{E}_{h}(F):=\begin{cases}J_{h}^{E}(F)&\text{if }P(F)<\infty,\ |F|=m,\\[4.30554pt]
+\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and we set
J_{n}:=\tilde{J}_{h}^{E_{n}}
. We then prove the
\Gamma-
convergence of the functionals
J_{n}
to the perimeter functional in
X
, that is

\Gamma(X)-\lim_{n\to\infty}J_{n}=P.

(4.12)

We can clearly restrict ourselves to consider sets of finite perimeter and volume
m
, otherwise the result is trivial. For any given set
F
of measure
m
and finite perimeter we choose the sequence constantly equal to
F
as a recovery sequence for
F
. Indeed, by (
4.11
) we have

J_{n}(F)=P(F)+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F\triangle E_{n}}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E_{n}}\to P(F).

We now prove the
\liminf
inequality. Given a sequence
F_{n}
that converges to
F
in
L^{1}
, by the
L^{1}-
semicontinuity of the perimeter, we have

P(F)\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}P(F_{n})\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(P(F_{n})+\frac{1}{h}\int_{F_{n}\triangle E_{n}}\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E_{n}}\right)

and thus (
4.12
) is proved.
Therefore, thanks to the equi-coercivity of the functionals
J_{n}
, any sequence of volume-constrained global minimizers of
J_{n}
converges in
L^{1}
, up to a subsequence, to a volume-constrained global minimizer of the perimeter in the torus. Let
(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of global minimizers of the functional
J_{n}
and let
F
be such that
F_{n}\to F
in
L^{1}
. We know that
F
is a global minimizer of the perimeter and that by the choice of
m
the lamella is not a global minimizer. Therefore it must hold
|E\triangle F|>0
.
∎

5
Convergence of the flow

In this section, we will prove the main result of the paper concerning the convergence of the discrete flow that mainly relies on Proposition
4.6
.

5.1
Convergence of the flow up to translations

We start by recalling
[
19
, Lemma 3.6]
: it will be used in the proof of the following proposition.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(E_{h}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a volume preserving discrete flow starting from
E_{0}
and let
E_{h}^{k_{n}}
be a subsequence such that
E^{k_{n}}_{h}+\tau_{n}\to F
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
for some set
F
and a suitable sequence
(\tau_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}.
Then
\textnormal{dist}_{\partial E^{k_{n}-1}_{h}}(\cdot+\tau_{n})\to\textnormal{dist}_{\partial F}
uniformly.

In the following proposition we characterize the long-time behaviour up to translations of the discrete mean curvature flow in the flat torus starting near a regular strictly stable set.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a strictly stable set. Then there exist
\delta^{*}=\delta^{*}(E)>0
and
h^{*}=h^{*}(E)>0
with the following property: if
h<h^{*}
and
E_{0}\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
is a set of finite perimeter satisfying

|E_{0}|=|E|,\qquad\overline{E}_{0}\subset(E)_{\delta^{*}},

then, for every discrete flow
(E_{h}^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
starting from
E_{0}
, there exists a sequence of translations
\tau_{n}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
such that

E_{h}^{n}+\tau_{n}\to E\quad\text{in}\quad C^{k},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be sufficiently small and let
\delta^{*}=\delta^{*}(\varepsilon,E)
,
h^{*}=h^{*}(E)
be the constants given by Proposition
4.6
.
Fix
E_{0}
an initial set satisfying
|E|=|E_{0}|
and
\overline{E}_{0}\subset(E)_{\delta^{*}}
. It is enough to show that any (unrelabelled) subsequence of the discrete flow starting from
E_{0}
admits a further subsequence converging in
C^{k}
and up to translations to
E
.
We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1.
(Existence and regularity of a limit point) From Proposition
4.2
we remark that, for
n\geq 1
, the sets
E_{h}^{n}
are uniform
\Lambda-
minimizers with uniformly bounded, non-increasing perimeters. Therefore, by the compactness of (uniform)
\Lambda-
minimizers, we can conclude that there exists a subsequence
(E_{h}^{k_{n}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and a
\Lambda-
minimizer
E_{h}^{\infty}
such that

E_{h}^{k_{n}}\overset{L^{1}}{\to}E_{h}^{\infty},\quad P(E_{h}^{k_{n}})\to P(E_{h}^{\infty}),\quad\textnormal{sd}_{E_{h}^{k_{n}-1}}\to\textnormal{sd}_{E_{h}^{\infty}}\ \text{uniformly}.

Let
G
be a set of finite perimeter such that
|G|=m
. By the minimality of
E_{h}^{k_{n}}
we have

P(E_{h}^{k_{n}})+\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{E_{h}^{k_{n}}}\textnormal{sd}_{E_{h}^{k_{n}-1}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x\leq P(G)+\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{G}\textnormal{sd}_{E_{h}^{k_{n}-1}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x

and, taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, we obtain

P(E_{h}^{\infty})+\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{E_{h}^{\infty}}\textnormal{sd}_{E_{h}^{\infty}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x\leq P(G)+\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{G}\textnormal{sd}_{E_{h}^{\infty}}(x)\,\textnormal{d}x.

We have thus proved that
E_{h}^{\infty}
is a fixed point for the discrete flow and thus, by Proposition
4.3
, it is a critical point for the perimeter.

Let
\tau_{\infty}\in\text{argmin}_{x}|(E_{h}^{\infty}+x)\triangle E|
.
By Proposition
4.6
we have
\alpha(E,E_{h}^{k_{n}})\leq\varepsilon
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Now, up to taking
\varepsilon
smaller, Theorem
2.4
and the smoothness of
E
, yields both the
C^{1,\beta}
-closeness between
E_{h}^{\infty}+\tau_{\infty}
and
E
, and the
C^{1,\beta}
regularity of
E_{h}^{\infty}+\tau_{\infty}
(and thus of
E_{h}^{\infty}
), for every
\beta\in(0,1)
. From Proposition
4.2

(iv)
it follows that
E_{h}^{\infty}
is of class
C^{2,\beta}
, therefore we conclude that
E_{h}^{\infty}
has constant classical mean curvature and thus it is of class
C^{\infty}
. To conclude, the smoothness of
E^{\infty}_{h}
allow us to use Theorem
2.4
to improve the convergence of the subsequence to

E_{h}^{k_{n}}\to E^{\infty}_{h}\quad\text{in}\quad C^{1,\beta}

(5.1)

and to ensure that the sets
E^{k_{n}}_{h}
are of class
C^{1,\beta}
for
n
large enough.

Step 2.
(Convergence in
C^{2,\beta}
of the flow and
C^{2,\beta}-
closeness to
E
) In this step we we will prove that
E^{\infty}_{h}
is
C^{2,\beta}-
close to
E
and that the convergence of
E^{k_{n}}_{h}
to
E^{\infty}_{h}
is in
C^{2,\beta}
. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\alpha(E,E^{\infty}_{h})=|E\triangle E^{\infty}_{h}|
so that the translation introduced by the previous step does not appear.

First of all we remark that, owing to the compactness of
\partial E^{\infty}_{h}
, it suffices to show that the result holds locally. By a compactness argument and the definition of convergence of sets in
C^{1,\beta}
(Definition
2.3
), up to rotations and relabelling the coordinates, we can find a cylinder
C=B^{\prime}\times(-L,L)
, where
B^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
is a ball centred at the origin, and functions
f_{\infty},f_{n}\in C^{1,\beta}(B^{\prime};(-L,L))
describing locally
\partial E^{\infty}_{h}\cap C
and
\partial E^{k_{n}}_{h}\cap C
respectively. We remark that the convergence (
5.1
) now reads as

f_{k_{n}}\to f_{\infty}\quad\text{in}\quad C^{1,\beta}(B^{\prime}).

(5.2)

We now prove that the curvatures
H_{E^{k_{n}}_{h}}
of the sequence
E^{k_{n}}_{h}
are converging in
C^{0,\beta}
to the curvature of
E^{\infty}_{h}
in the following sense

H_{E^{k_{n}}_{h}}(\cdot,f_{k_{n}}(\cdot))\to H_{E^{\infty}_{h}}(\cdot,f_{\infty}(\cdot))\quad\text{in}\quad C^{0,\beta}(B^{\prime}).

(5.3)

We will follow an argument used in Step 3 of the proof of
[
1
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Since we described
\partial E^{k_{n}}_{h}\cap C
as a graph, the following formula for the curvature of
\partial E_{h}^{k_{n}}
holds

\textnormal{div}\left(\dfrac{\nabla f_{k_{n}}(\cdot)}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla f_{k_{n}}(\cdot)|^{2}}}\right)=H_{E^{k_{n}}_{h}}(\cdot,f_{k_{n}}(\cdot))\quad\text{on}\quad B^{\prime}

(5.4)

and an analogous formula holds for
\partial E^{\infty}_{h}
. From (
5.4
) and the Euler-Lagrange equation (
4.3
), by integrating on
B^{\prime}
, we then obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{k_{n}}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(B^{\prime})

\displaystyle-\dfrac{1}{h}\int_{B^{\prime}}sd_{E^{k_{n}-1}_{h}}(x^{\prime},f_{k_{n}}(x^{\prime}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x^{\prime})

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\prime}}H_{E^{k_{n}}_{h}}(x^{\prime},f_{k_{n}}(x^{\prime}))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\prime}}\textnormal{div}\left(\dfrac{\nabla f_{k_{n}}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla f_{k_{n}}(x^{\prime})|^{2}}}\right)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B^{\prime}}\dfrac{\nabla f_{k_{n}}(y)}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla f_{k_{n}}(y)|^{2}}}\cdot y\,\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-2}(y),

where we set
y=x^{\prime}/|x^{\prime}|
and integrated by parts in the last line. We can then exploit the convergence (
5.2
) and the formula (
5.4
) for the curvature of
E^{\infty}_{h}
to prove

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B^{\prime}}\dfrac{\nabla f_{k_{n}}(y)}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla f_{k_{n}}(y)|^{2}}}\cdot y\,\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-2}(y)

\displaystyle\to\int_{\partial B^{\prime}}\dfrac{\nabla f_{\infty}(y)}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla f_{\infty}|^{2}}(y)}\cdot y\,\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-2}(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{B^{\prime}}\textnormal{div}\left(\dfrac{\nabla f_{\infty}(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla f_{\infty}(x^{\prime})|^{2}}}\right)\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=H_{E^{\infty}_{h}}\,\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(B^{\prime}).

Now, Lemma
5.1
ensures that
sd_{E^{k_{n}-1}_{h}}\to sd_{E^{\infty}_{h}}
uniformly and we can use the convergence (
5.2
) to obtain

sd_{E^{k_{n}-1}_{h}}((\cdot,f_{k_{n}}(\cdot)))\to sd_{E^{\infty}_{h}}((\cdot,f_{\infty}(\cdot)))=0\quad\text{uniformly on }B^{\prime},

since
\partial E^{\infty}_{h}\cap C=\{(x^{\prime},f_{\infty}(x^{\prime})):x^{\prime}\in B^{\prime})\}
by definition. Therefore we find

\int_{B^{\prime}}sd_{E^{k_{n}-1}_{h}}((x^{\prime},f_{k_{n}}(x^{\prime})))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x^{\prime})\to\int_{B^{\prime}}sd_{E^{\infty}_{h}}((x^{\prime},f_{\infty}(x^{\prime})))\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x^{\prime})=0.

We then conclude that (
5.5
) converges to
H_{E^{\infty}_{h}}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(B^{\prime})
and thus it must hold

\lambda_{k_{n}}\to H_{E^{\infty}_{h}}.

From (
4.3
), the previous result and the fact that the signed distance functions are all equi-lipschitz, we conclude that for any
\beta\in(0,1)
, the sequence
(H_{E^{k_{n}}_{h}}(\cdot,f_{k_{n}}(\cdot)))
is bounded in
C^{0,\beta}(B^{\prime})
and thus it converges uniformly to
H_{E^{\infty}_{h}}(\cdot,f_{\infty}(\cdot))
. This proves the convergence (
5.3
).

We remark that the previous result also hold if we describe the sets of the flow
E_{h}^{k_{n}}
as normal deformations of
E^{\infty}_{h}
, that is there exist functions
\varphi_{k_{n}}:\partial E^{\infty}_{h}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
E_{h}^{k_{n}}=(E_{h}^{\infty})_{\varphi_{k_{n}}}
. In this case the convergence (
5.1
) reads as

\varphi_{k_{n}}\to 0\quad\text{in}\quad C^{1,\beta}(\partial E^{\infty}_{h}),

and this and Lemma
5.1
ensure that

sd_{E^{k_{n}-1}_{h}}(\cdot+\varphi_{k_{n}}(\cdot)\nu_{E^{\infty}_{h}}(\cdot))\to sd_{E^{\infty}_{h}}(\cdot)=0\quad\text{uniformly on }\partial E^{\infty}_{h}.

Now, the convergence of the curvatures reads as

H_{E^{k_{n}}_{h}}(\cdot+\varphi_{k_{n}}(\cdot)\nu_{E^{\infty}_{h}}(\cdot))\to H_{E^{\infty}_{h}}(\cdot)\quad\text{in}\quad C^{0,\beta}(\partial E^{\infty}_{h}).

We can then apply directly
[
1
, Lemma 7.2]
to obtain that the subsequence
E^{k_{n}}_{h}
is converging to
E^{\infty}_{h}
in
C^{2,\beta}
.

To prove the
C^{2,\beta}-
closeness of the limit point we argue by contradiction. Assume that a sequence of limit points
(E^{\infty,l}_{h})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
is converging in
C^{1,\beta}
to
E
but there exists
\sigma>0
such that

\textnormal{dist}_{C^{2,\beta}}(E,E^{\infty,l}_{h})>\sigma

for every
l
large enough. Again, we describe locally
\partial E^{\infty,l}_{h}
and
\partial E
as graphs of suitable functions
f_{\infty,l},f:B^{\prime}\to(-L,L)
and we can repeat the same argument previously employed to prove that

H_{E^{\infty,l}_{h}}((\cdot,f_{\infty,l}(\cdot)))\to H_{E}((\cdot,f(\cdot)))\qquad\text{in }C^{0,\beta}(B^{\prime}).

This time the argument is simpler, since the limit points are stationary sets for the perimeter and thus their Euler-Lagrange equation is

H_{E^{\infty,l}_{h}}=\lambda_{E^{\infty,l}_{h}}\in\mathbb{R}\quad\text{on}\quad\partial E^{\infty,l}_{h}.

Again, Lemma 7.2 in
[
1
]
yields the desired contradiction.

Step 3.
(Uniqueness up to translations and
C^{k}
convergence) By the previous step we can find a suitable function
\varphi_{\infty}\in C^{2,\beta}(\partial E)
such that
E^{\infty}_{h}=E_{\varphi_{\infty}}
. Up to introducing a further translation given by Lemma
2.10
, the hypotheses of Theorem
1.3
are satisfied and thus

\|\varphi_{\infty}\|_{H^{1}(\partial E)}\leq C\|H_{E^{\infty}_{h}}-\overline{H}_{E^{\infty}_{h}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}=0,

since the set
E^{\infty}_{h}
is a stationary set for the perimeter. Therefore
E^{\infty}_{h}
is a translated of the set
E
.

A standard bootstrap method based on the elliptic regularity theory combined with the Euler-Lagrange equation (
4.3
) yields the convergence in
C^{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

5.2
Exponential convergence of the whole flow

In this subsection we will prove that the translations introduced in Proposition
5.2
decay to zero exponentially fast. In order to prove this result we will estimate the decay of the dissipations via a dissipation-dissipation inequality, which in turn relies on the quantitative Alexandrov type estimate established in Theorem
1.3
. We start by recalling some preliminary results from
[
19
]
.

The following lemma is an adaptation to our case of
[
19
, Lemma 3.8]
. Its proof can be found in the Appendix.

Lemma 5.3

(
A priori
estimates)
.

Let
\eta>0
and let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a strictly stable set. There exists
\delta>0
with the following property: if
f_{1},f_{2}\in C^{1}(\partial E)
with
\|f_{i}\|_{C^{1}(\partial E)}\leq\delta
and
|E_{f_{i}}|=|E|
for
i=1,2
we have

\displaystyle C_{1}(1-\eta)\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(E_{f_{1}},E_{f_{2}})\leq C_{1}(1+\eta)\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(5.6)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1-\eta}{2}\int_{\partial{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}E_{f_{1}}}}}\textnormal{sd}^{2}_{E_{f_{2}}}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(E_{f_{1}},E_{f_{2}})\leq\dfrac{1+\eta}{2}\int_{\partial{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}E_{f_{1}}}}}\textnormal{sd}^{2}_{E_{f_{2}}}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}

(5.7)

\displaystyle|\textnormal{bar}(E_{f_{1}})-\textnormal{bar}(E_{f_{2}})|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq\frac{C_{2}}{C_{1}(1-\eta)}\mathcal{D}(E_{f_{1}},E_{f_{2}})

(5.8)

for suitable constants
C_{1},\,C_{2}>0
.

The following lemma proves the crucial dissipation-dissipation inequality (
5.10
) (see
[
19
, Lemma 3.9]
). This result will play a central role in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Its proof is based on the Alexandrov-type estimate contained in Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
h>0
and let
E\subset\mathbb{T}^{N}
be a strictly stable set. There exist constants
C,

\delta>0
with the following property: for any pair of normal deformations
E_{f_{1}},

E_{f_{2}}
with
f_{i}\in C^{2}(\partial E),

\|f_{i}\|_{C^{1}(\partial E)}\leq\delta
, and such that
|E_{f_{2}}|=|E|,

|\int_{\partial E}\nu_{E}f_{2}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}|\leq\delta\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}
and

H_{E_{f_{2}}}+\dfrac{\textnormal{sd}_{E_{f_{1}}}}{h}=\lambda\quad\text{on}\quad\partial E_{f_{2}}

(5.9)

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\mathcal{D}(E,E_{f_{2}})\leq C\mathcal{D}(E_{f_{2}},E_{f_{1}}).

(5.10)

Proof.

By Theorem
1.3
, for
\delta
sufficiently small, we get

\begin{split}\|f_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}&\leq C\|H_{E_{f_{2}}}-\overline{H}_{E_{f_{2}}}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}\leq C\|H_{E_{f_{2}}}-\lambda\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}^{2}\\
&\leq 2C\|H_{E_{f_{2}}}-\lambda\|_{L^{2}(\partial E_{f_{2}})}^{2}=\frac{2C}{h^{2}}\int_{\partial E_{f_{2}}}\textnormal{sd}_{E_{f_{1}}}^{2}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1},\end{split}

where the third inequality follows by bounding the Jacobian of the change of variables by
2
(see (
3.2
)). By combining the previous inequalities with (
5.6
) and (
5.7
), we obtain the thesis.
∎

We are now able to prove our main result.
The proof relies on our previous result Proposition
5.2
, however this time we have to show that the translations introduced converge to an appropriate translation
\xi
. To achieve this result, we will obtain in Step 1 some estimates on the dissipations along the flow by comparing the energy with a suitable competitor. Once the (exponential) decay of the dissipations is proved, the convergence of the translations follows (see Step 2). The last step is devoted to prove the exponential convergence of the sets.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
h^{*}>0
,
\delta^{*}>0
and
(\tau_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be given by Proposition
5.2
. Fix
h<h^{*}
and set
E_{n}:=E_{h}^{n}
. We split the proof in three steps.

Step 1.
(Exponential convergence of dissipations) Testing the minimality of
E_{n}
with
E_{n-1}
we obtain

P(E_{n})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})\leq P(E_{n-1}).

Recalling that
P(E_{n})\to P(E)
and summing the previous inequality from
n+1
to
+\infty
we get

\sum_{k=n+1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{k},E_{k-1})\leq P(E_{n})-P(E).

(5.11)

We will now construct a suitable competitor to estimate the dissipation at the step
n-1
with the difference of perimeters.
Since, by Proposition

, we have

E_{n}+\tau_{n}\to E\quad\text{in}\quad C^{k}\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N},

(5.12)

the translated sets of the flow, for

large enough, can be written as normal deformations of the set

, that is there exists

such that

E_{n}+\tau_{n}=E_{g_{n}},

where
E_{g_{n}}
was defined in (

).
The convergence (
5.12
) then reads as
g_{n}\to 0
in
C^{k}
as
n\to\infty
. Let
\sigma_{n}
be the translations introduced by Lemma
2.10
with
E_{n}+\tau_{n}
instead of
F
. From the convergence in
C^{k}
of
E_{n}+\tau_{n}
to
E
, we deduce that
\sigma_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Therefore, setting

F_{n}:=E_{n}+\tau_{n}+\sigma_{n},

we have that
F_{n}\to E
in
C^{k}
and
F_{n}=E_{f_{n}}
with
f_{n}:\partial E\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\left|\int_{\partial E}f_{n}\nu_{E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}\right|\leq\delta\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f_{n}\|_{W^{2,p}(\partial E)}\leq C\|g_{n}\|_{W^{2,p}(\partial E)}

for
p>N-1
. Consider now the competitor

\mathscr{E}_{n}:=E-\tau_{n-1}-\sigma_{n-1}.

From the minimality of

we easily deduce

P(E_{n})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})\leq P(\mathscr{E}_{n})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{E}_{n},E_{n-1})=P(E)+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E,E_{n-1}+\tau_{n-1}+\sigma_{n-1})

(5.13)

where we used the translational invariance of the dissipations. From Lemma
5.1
we obtain that the sequence
E_{n-2}+\tau_{n-1}+\sigma_{n-1}
converges in
C^{k}
to the same limit of
E_{n-1}+\tau_{n-1}+\sigma_{n-1}
, that is to
E
. In particular, for
n
large enough we can write
E_{n-2}+\tau_{n-1}+\sigma_{n-1}=E_{\psi}
for a suitable function

(depending on

) and with
\|\psi\|_{C^{1}(\partial E)}
small. From Lemma
5.10
we can then estimate the right hand side of (

) with

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(E,E_{n-1}+\tau_{n-1}+\sigma_{n-1})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(E,F_{n-1})=\mathcal{D}(E,E_{f_{n-1}})\leq C\mathcal{D}(E_{f_{n-1}},E_{\psi})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1}+\tau_{n-1}+\sigma_{n-1},E_{n-2}+\tau_{n-1}+\sigma_{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2}).

From the previous inequality and (
5.13
) we obtain

P(E_{n})-P(E)=P(E_{n})-P(\mathscr{E}_{n})\leq\dfrac{C}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2}).

(5.14)

Now, (
5.11
) and (

) yield

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n-1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{k},E_{k-1})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{k},E_{k-1})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{C+1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\dfrac{C+1}{h}\left(\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2})+\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})\right).

We then apply Lemma
5.5
below (with
l=2
) to conclude

\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})\leq\left(1-\dfrac{1}{C+1}\right)^{n/2}\left(P(E_{0})-P(E)\right).

(5.15)

Step 2.
(Exponential convergence of barycenters)
Set

b=\left(1-\dfrac{1}{C+1}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\in(0,1).

(5.16)

From (
5.12
) and Lemma
5.1
both the sequences
(E_{n}+\tau_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(E_{n-1}+\tau_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converge in

to

. Therefore, for
n
large enough, there exist some functions

such that

E_{n}+\tau_{n}=E_{f_{1,n}},\quad E_{n-1}+\tau_{n}=E_{f_{2,n}}

and

as

for

. From (

) and (

) we can estimate for

\displaystyle|\text{bar}(E_{n})-\text{bar}(E_{n-1})|

\displaystyle=|\text{bar}(E_{n}+\tau_{n})-\text{bar}(E_{n-1}+\tau_{n})|

\displaystyle=|\text{bar}(E_{f_{1,n}})-\text{bar}(E_{f_{2,n}})|

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(E_{f_{1,n}},E_{f_{2,n}})}=\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(P(E_{0})-P(E)\right)^{1/2}b^{n}.

In turn, the above estimate implies that

satisfies the Cauchy condition, thus the whole sequence admits a limit
\bar{\xi}\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
. Moreover, the convergence is exponentially fast in the sense that

|\text{bar}(E_{f_{1,n}})-\bar{\xi}|\leq\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}|\text{bar}(E_{f_{1,n}})-\text{bar}(E_{f_{2,n}})|\leq C\left(P(E_{0})-P(E)\right)^{1/2}\dfrac{b^{n}}{1-b}

for
n
large enough.
Recalling (
5.12
) we thus conclude that there exists a suitable translation
\xi\in\mathbb{T}^{N}
such that for every

E_{n}\to E-\xi\quad\text{in }C^{k}\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

Step 3.
(Exponential convergence of the sets) By the previous step we can write, for
n
large, the boundaries of the evolving sets as radial graphs of the limit set
E-\xi
. Precisely, for
n
large enough there exist functions
f_{n}
such that

E_{n}+\xi=E_{f_{n}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|f_{n}\|_{C^{k}(\partial E)}\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty.

(5.17)

From (
5.6
) and for

\|f_{n}-f_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}\leq 2\sqrt{\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})}
and thus, recalling (

\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}\leq\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\|f_{n}-f_{n-1}\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}\leq\left(P(E_{0})-P(E)\right)^{1/2}\dfrac{b^{n}}{1-b}

(5.18)

where

is as in (
5.16
). The above estimate yields the exponential decay of the

norms of the radial graphs. We recall the well-known Gagliardo-Nieremberg inequality: for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
there exists

such that, if

\|D^{k}g\|_{L^{2}(\partial E)}\leq C\|D^{2k}g\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}(\partial E)}\|g\|^{1/2}_{L^{2}(\partial E)}

(5.19)

where

[

.
Now, by (

) for every

for every

∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

.

6
Two-dimensional case

In this section, we completely characterize the long-time behaviour of the discrete flow in dimension two.
This particular choice for the dimension is purely technical and can be justified as follows. In the two-dimensional flat torus we have a complete characterization of the critical points of the perimeter: they consist in unions of disjoint discs (having the same area) or in unions of disjoint lamellae (possibly having different areas), or their complements. It turns out that these sets are all strictly stable. This allows us to conclude that either the connected components of any limit point of the discrete flow or the ones of their complements are strictly stable sets. We remark that in higher dimension this could not be true anymore.

Fix
h
,
m>0
and let
(E^{n}_{h})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a flow with initial set
E_{0}\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
such that
|E_{0}|=m
. We recall that, by Proposition
4.2
, there exists
s_{0}>0
such that the distance between the connected components of the set
E_{h}^{n}
is at least
s_{0}
. Moreover, the proposition also provides a bound from below on the diameter of the connected components. Set

P_{\infty}:=\lim_{n}P(E_{h}^{n})

as the limit of the monotone sequence of the perimeters along the discrete flow.
Let
F
be any possible limit point of the sequence
(E_{n}^{h})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
. We observe that if
F
is a union of discs then its number of connected components must be
\pi^{-1}P_{\infty}^{2}/(4m)
and therefore the form of the limit point is uniquely determinated up to translations. Analogously, if
F
is the complement of a union of discs,
F^{c}
is made of
\pi^{-1}P_{\infty}^{2}/(4-4m)
connected components and thus it is uniquely determinated up to translations of its complement. In the case when
F
is a union of lamellae the number of connected components is
, in general, less than or equal to

P_{\infty}/2
, and we have no information on the area of the single components.

Since we will consider
h
as a fixed parameter, from now on we will denote by
E_{n}
the set
E_{h}^{n}
.

Remark 6.1

(Remarks on the uniform
C^{1,\alpha}-
closeness to limit points)
.

We remark that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
n\geq n_{0}
it holds

|E_{n}\triangle\bigcup_{i=1}^{l_{n}}F_{i,n}|\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{or}\quad|E_{n}^{c}\triangle\bigcup_{i=1}^{L_{n}}F_{i,n}|\leq\varepsilon,

(6.1)

where, in the first case,
\bigcup_{i=1}^{l_{n}}F_{i,n}
is a union of disjoint lamellae or a union of disjoint discs, with
F_{i,n}
having the same mass of the
i-
th connected component of
E_{n}
;
l_{n}
is either
less than or equal to

P_{\infty}/2
if

, are lamellae or
l_{n}=\pi^{-1}P_{\infty}^{2}/(4m)
if they are discs; in the second case,

is a union of disjoint discs, with

having the same mass of the
i-
th connected component of

and
L_{n}=\pi^{-1}P_{\infty}^{2}/(4-4m)
.
This can be easily proved recalling that any subsequence of the flow admits a further subsequence converging in

to a set of the aforementioned form.

Moreover, the classical regularity theory of
\Lambda-
minimizers implies that the previous result can be improved. Consider, for the sake of simplicity, that

satisfies the first inequality in (
6.1
)(the other case is analogous). Then one can prove that for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that for every

it holds

E_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l_{n}}(F_{i,n})_{f_{i,n}}\quad\text{where}\quad f_{i,n}\in C^{1,\alpha}(\partial F_{i,n}),\ \|f_{i,n}\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\partial F_{i,n})}\leq\varepsilon.

(6.2)

Remark 6.2
.

In this remark, we identify
\mathbb{T}^{2}
with the unit square

We prove that for a fixed
M>0
there exists a finite number of slopes such that, for any lamella

having one of those slopes, we have

.

Fix a lamella

. Let

be one of the two components of the boundary of

, and suppose that
(0,0)\in a

is a closed curve in

coprime or equal to

or

The lamella

Since

Our claim follows since in the disc of radius

In the following lemma we characterize the geometric form of any limit point of the discrete flow.

Lemma 6.3

(Uniqueness of the form of the limit)
.

Fix

\mathscr{E}_{B}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l_{B}}B_{i}

(6.3)

any disjoint union of

Remark 6.4

(

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma

, we can assume that all the limit points of the flow are sets either of the form

,
\mathscr{E}_{B^{c}}
or

(see (

), (

), (

To conclude we need to prove that the whole sequence converges in

In the case when the limit points are of the form

, the proof follows the same spirit of
[
19
, Theorem 3.4]
, but it is easier since we work in a compact space. The case when the limit points are of the form

instead of

Now, suppose that the limit points are of the form

We begin by observing that any subsequence of the flow admits a further subsequence converging in

to a union of disjoint lamellae. Firstly, we prove the exponential decay of the dissipations. Testing the minimality of

with

P(E_{n})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})\leq P(E_{n-1}).

Summing for

\sum_{s=n+1}^{+\infty}\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{s},E_{s-1})\leq P(E_{n})-P_{\infty}.

(6.8)

With the notation previously introduced, for every
\varepsilon

large enough such that (
6.2

be the sets given by (
6.2

,

.

We will now construct a suitable competitor to estimate the dissipation at the step

n
large enough consider the competitor

\mathscr{L}_{n}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}F_{i,n-1}.

We remark that, by definition and for

.
By Proposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{D}(E_{i,n},E_{i,n-1}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathscr{L}_{n},E_{n-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{D}(F_{i,n-1},E_{i,n-1}).

Testing the minimality of
E_{n}
with
\mathscr{L}_{n}
and using the previous equality we have

P(E_{n})+\frac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})\leq P(\mathscr{L}_{n})+\frac{1}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{D}(F_{i,n-1},E_{i,n-1}).

(6.10)

Recalling Remark
6.4
and equations (
6.2
) and (
6.7
), we then obtain that the connected components of both
E_{n-1}
and
E_{n-2}
are small normal
C^{1,\alpha}-
deformations of the connected components of
\mathscr{L}_{n-1}
. Thus we can assume that both
E_{i,n-1}
and
E_{i,n-2}
can be described as normal deformation of
F_{i,n-1}
for
i=1,\dots,k
. Let
f_{i,n-1}
and
f_{i,n-2}
be the functions (having small
C^{1,\alpha}-
norms) that describe respectively these deformations.
Now, recalling Lemma
5.10
, we can estimate

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(F_{i,n-1},E_{i,n-1})=

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(F_{i,n-1},(F_{i,n-1})_{f_{i,n-1}})\leq C\mathcal{D}((F_{i,n-1})_{f_{i,n-1}},(F_{i,n-1})_{f_{i,n-2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\mathcal{D}(E_{i,n-1},E_{i,n-2}).

Thus, from equations (
6.9
) and (
6.10
) we get

P(E_{n})-P_{\infty}=P(E_{n})-P(\mathscr{L}_{n})\leq\frac{C}{h}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mathcal{D}(E_{i,n-1},E_{i,n-2})=\frac{C}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2})

and then (
6.8
) clearly yields

\displaystyle\sum_{s=n-1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{s},E_{s-1})=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=n+1}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{s},E_{s-1})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq P(E_{n})-P_{\infty}+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C+1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{C+1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n-1},E_{n-2})+\dfrac{1}{h}\mathcal{D}(E_{n},E_{n-1})\right).

We can then conclude using the same arguments of
[
19
, Theorem 3.4]
.
∎

Appendix A
Appendix

We present the proof of Lemma
5.3
for the reader’s convenience. It is slightly different from the proof of
[
19
, Lemma 3.8]
.

Proof of Lemma
5.3
.

The proof of equations (
5.6
) and (
5.7
) are quite analogous to the corresponding ones in
[
19
]
. We recall it for the sake of completeness and to highlight the minor differences between the two versions.

We start by observing that for any
\eta^{\prime}>0
, if
\delta
is sufficiently small, then for every
p_{0}\in\partial E_{f_{2}}
the boundary of
E_{f_{2}}
in a small disc must be contained in a cone

\partial E_{f_{2}}\cap B_{4\bar{\delta}}(p_{0})\subset G:=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ :|(y-p_{0})\cdot\nu_{E}(p_{0})|^{2}\leq\dfrac{\eta^{\prime 2}}{1+\eta^{\prime 2}}|y-p_{0}|^{2}\right\}.

(A.1)

We then divide the rest of the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
If
\delta
is small enough , for every point
p=\lambda p_{0}\in B_{2\delta}(p_{0})\ (\lambda>0),
we have that

\dfrac{1}{1+\eta^{\prime}}|p-p_{0}|\leq\textnormal{dist}(p,\partial E_{f_{2}})\leq|p-p_{0}|.

Indeed the second inequality is trivial by definition, since
p_{0}\in\partial E_{f_{2}}
. Concerning the first one, set
q\in\partial E_{f_{2}}
such that
\textnormal{dist}(p,\partial E_{f_{2}})=|p-q|
, in particular
|p-q|\leq|p-p_{0}|\leq 2\delta
. From (
A.1
) we infer that
q\in G
and thus we have

\textnormal{dist}(p,\partial E_{f_{2}})\geq\textnormal{dist}(p,G)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{1+\eta^{\prime 2}}}|p-p_{0}|\geq\dfrac{1}{1+\eta^{\prime}}|p-p_{0}|

where we used the explicit formula for the projection of a point on a cone. If
p_{0}:=s+f_{2}(s)\nu_{E}(s)\in\partial E_{f_{2}}
with
s\in\partial E
, we set

p_{t}:=p_{0}+t\dfrac{f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)}{|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|}\nu_{E}(s)\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,c|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|]

for an appropriate constant
c
such that the quantities defined are regular. We deduce that

\dfrac{1}{1+\eta^{\prime}}t\leq\textnormal{dist}(p_{t},\partial E_{f_{2}})\leq t.

(A.2)

Keeping the same notation and using the coarea formula (also recall (
3.7
)), we infer that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(E_{f_{1}},E_{f_{2}})

\displaystyle=\int_{E_{f_{1}}\triangle E_{f_{2}}}\textnormal{dist}(x,\partial E_{f_{2}})\,\textnormal{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s)\int_{0}^{c|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|}\textnormal{dist}(p_{t},\partial E_{f_{2}})J\Phi(s,t)\,\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s)\int_{0}^{c|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|}\textnormal{dist}(p_{t},\partial E_{f_{2}})\,\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{\partial E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s)\int_{0}^{c|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|}\textnormal{dist}(p_{t},\partial E_{f_{2}})(J\Phi(s,t)-1)\,\textnormal{d}t.

(A.3)

Recalling that for every
s\in\partial E
we have that
J\Phi(s,\cdot)-1
is Lipschitz continuous with constant
H_{E}
, for
\delta
small enough and using (
A.2
), we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(E_{f_{1}},E_{f_{2}})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\delta H_{E})\int_{\partial E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s)\int_{0}^{c|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|}t\,\textnormal{d}t

(A.4)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1+\delta H_{E}}{2}c^{2}\int_{\partial E}|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s),

(A.5)

from which the second inequality in (
5.6
) follows by taking
\delta
small enough. On the other hand, by (
A.2
) we also have

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(E_{f_{1}},E_{f_{2}})

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{1-\delta H_{E}}{1+\eta^{\prime}}\int_{\partial E}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s)\int_{0}^{c|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|}t\,\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-\delta H_{E}}{1+\eta^{\prime}}c^{2}\int_{\partial E}|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s),

(A.6)

from which the first inequality in (
5.6
) follows by taking
\eta^{\prime}
and
\delta
small enough.

Step 2.
The inequalities (
5.7
) and (
5.8
) are now easy consequences. Indeed, by (
A.2
) we have that, for every
p_{1}=(1+f_{1}(s))\nu_{E}(s)\in\partial E_{f_{1}}
, it holds

\dfrac{c}{1+\eta^{\prime}}|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|\leq\textnormal{dist}(p_{1},E_{f_{2}})\leq c|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|.

Therefore (
5.7
) follows from (
A.5
) and (
A.6
), by taking
\eta^{\prime}
and
\delta
smaller if needed and using the same change of coordinates used previously (recall that
J\Phi
and its inverse are estimated from above by
1+C\delta
for a suitable constant
C>0
).

Finally, we prove (
5.8
). For
\delta
small enough, we can bound the Jacobian by
2
and therefore we obtain

\displaystyle|\text{bar}(E_{f_{1}})-\text{bar}(E_{f_{2}})|\,|E|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{E_{f_{1}}\setminus E_{f_{2}}}x\,\textnormal{d}x-\int_{E_{f_{2}}\setminus E_{f_{1}}}x\,\textnormal{d}x\right|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{\partial E\cap\{f_{1}>f_{2}\}}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s)\int_{f_{2}(s)}^{f_{1}(s)}(s+t\nu_{E}(s))J\Phi(s)\,\textnormal{d}t

\displaystyle\,\,\,-\int_{\partial E\cap\{f_{1}<f_{2}\}}\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s)\int_{f_{1}(s)}^{f_{2}(s)}(s+t\nu_{E}(s))J\Phi(s)\,\textnormal{d}t\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq 2\left|\int_{\partial E}\big(2s+(|f_{1}(s)|+|f_{2}(s)|)\nu_{E}(s)\big)\,|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)|\,\textnormal{d}\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(s)\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|f_{1}-f_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

and the conclusion follows from (
5.6
).
∎

Stratification of Derived Categories of Tate Motives
David Rubinstein

Abstract
. We classify the localizing tensor ideals of the derived categories of mixed Tate motives over certain algebraically closed fields. More precisely, we prove that these categories are stratified in the sense of Barthel, Heard and Sanders. A key ingredient in the proof is the development of a new technique for transporting stratification between categories by means of Brown–Adams representability, which may be of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

The theory of stratification for a rigidly-compactly generated tensor triangulated category
\mathcal{T}
developed by Barthel, Heard and Sanders
[
BHS23b
]
provides a framework for classifying the localizing ideals of
\mathcal{T}
.  At its heart is a support theory
Supp
for
\mathcal{T}
that takes value in the Balmer spectrum of compact objects
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
. This support theory is given by tensoring with certain tensor idempotent objects
g(\mathcal{P})\in\mathcal{T}
associated to each
\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
. Explicitly, we have

Supp(t)=\{\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c}):t\otimes g(\mathcal{P})\neq 0\}

for each
t\in\mathcal{T}
. The category
\mathcal{T}
is
stratified
if this support theory provides an order-preserving bijection between the collection of localizing tensor ideals of
\mathcal{T}
and the collection of all subsets of
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
.
In this paper, we prove that the derived category of mixed Tate motives over certain algebraically closed fields is stratified. Our starting point is the computation of their Balmer spectra by Gallauer
[
Gal19
]
:

Theorem 1.1

(Gallauer)
.

The Balmer spectrum of
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z)^{c}
is the following topological space:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{m_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{m_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{m_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{e_{2}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{e_{3}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{e_{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdots}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{m_{0}}}

We will explain what these primes are in Theorem
3.12
. Our main theorem is the following (Theorem
8.1
):

Theorem 1.2
.

The category
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z)
is stratified.

There are many consequences of a category
\mathcal{T}
being stratified (see, e.g.
[
BHS23b
, Part III]
). For example, if
\mathcal{T}
is stratified and
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
satisfies a mild topological assumption, then the abstract telescope conjecture holds (see
[
BHS23b
, Theorem 9.11]
). This topological condition holds for
Spc(DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z)^{c})
for example, so as a corollary to the above theorem, we get (Theorem
8.2
):

Theorem 1.3
.

Every smashing tensor ideal of
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z)
is compactly generated.

Proving that a category is stratified amounts to establishing two properties: (a) the ‘local-to-global’ principle, which morally says every object can be reconstructed from its local pieces; and (b), the ‘minimality’ property, which says the localizing ideals generated by the tensor idempotents
g(\mathcal{P})
are minimal. Importantly, Barthel, Heard and Sanders, building off the earlier work of Stevenson, prove that if the spectrum is noetherian, as the above spectrum of Tate motives is for example, the local-to-global principle always holds, and so the problem reduces to proving the minimality property. Moreover, minimality is a local condition, so the bulk of this paper is devoted to proving that minimality holds at the unique closed points in the corresponding local categories for each prime in
Spc(DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z)^{c})
. While there are infinitely many primes in
Spc(DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z)^{c})
, there are qualitatively only 3 distinct primes to consider:
m_{0},e_{p},\text{ and }m_{p}
.

The local category associated to the generic point
m_{0}
is equivalent to the category of rational motives
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)
. We prove this category is stratified in §
4
using an extension of work on t-structures for rational motives by Peter and Levine
[
Pet13
,
Lev93
]
.

For the heights one and two primes,
e_{p}\text{ and }m_{p}
, we use a result similar in spirit to the quasi-finite descent of
[
BCH+23
]
to reduce to the following two local categories:

(1)

DTM^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)
: which corresponds to
e_{p}
(see §
5
), and

(2)

DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)
: which corresponds to
m_{p}
(see §
7
).

Stratification of
DTM^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)
is an immediate consequence of the so-called ‘Rigidity Theorem’ for étale motives with finite coefficients (see Theorem
5.2
). On the other hand, in proving
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)
is stratified in §
7
, we are led naturally to the following question, which may be of independent interest.

Question 1.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
are two rigidly-compactly generated tensor-triangulated categories with a tt-equivalence between their compact parts
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{c}\simeq\mathcal{T}_{2}^{c}
. If
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is stratified, does it follow that
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is stratified as well?

In §
6
we investigate this question through the lens of Brown–Adams representability. More precisely, given a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category
\mathcal{T}
, we study the module category
\mathcal{A}:=Add((\mathcal{T}^{c})^{op},\mathcal{A}b)
along with the restricted Yoneda functor

\displaystyle h:\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle t\xrightarrow{}\hat{t}:=Hom(-,t)|_{\mathcal{T}^{c}}.

The essential image of restricted Yoneda lies in the subcategory of homological functors, and in certain examples a much stronger relationship holds. For example, Adams showed in
[
Ada71
]
that for the stable homotopy category
\mathcal{T}=\text{SH}
, every homological functor
\mathcal{H}:(\mathcal{T}^{c})^{op}\to\mathcal{A}b
is the restriction to
\mathcal{T}^{c}
of a representable functor on
\mathcal{T}
, and every map between homological functors is induced by a (non-unique) map between the representing spectra. Due to this historical example, a tt-category is said to satisfy Brown–Adams representability if it satisfies those above two properties. Determining whether or not a category satisfies Brown–Adams representability is a challenging problem in general. Importantly for us however, Neeman, generalizing a theorem of Brown, showed in
[
Nee97
]
that a sufficient condition is that the subcategory of compact objects
\mathcal{T}^{c}
is equivalent to a countable category.

The category of modules
\mathcal{A}
, and its relation to
\mathcal{T}
, has also been studied by Balmer, Krause and Stevenson in a series of recent papers
[
BKS19
,
BKS20
,
Bal20a
,
Bal20b
]
. In particular, they use
\mathcal{A}
to define a new spectrum, the so-called homological spectrum
Spc^{h}(\mathcal{T}^{c})
. Associated to each ‘homological prime’
\beta\in Spc^{h}(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is a certain pure-injective object
E_{\beta}\in\mathcal{T}
, and these can be used to define a new support theory for
\mathcal{T}
. These
E_{\beta}
objects are often much better behaved than the analogous
g(\mathcal{P})
objects are. For example, they are often field objects (see Remark
6.12
). Our main theorem for this section is the following (see Theorem
6.25
):

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
are two rigidly-compactly generated tensor-triangulated categories with a tt-equivalence between their compact parts
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{c}\simeq\mathcal{T}_{2}^{c}
. Suppose further that:

(1)

\mathcal{T}_{1}
is stratified;

(2)

\mathcal{T}_{1}^{c}
is equivalent to a countable category;

(3)

The
E_{\beta}
objects in
\mathcal{T}_{1}
are field objects; and

(4)

For every nonzero homological functor
\hat{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
there exists a nonzero map
\hat{E}_{\beta}\otimes\hat{x}\to\hat{t}
for some
\beta\in Spc^{h}(\mathcal{T}_{1}^{c})
and compact
x\in\mathcal{T}_{1}^{c}
.

Then
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is also stratified.

As mentioned above, we use this theorem to prove that
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)
is stratified. The compact part of this category is known to be equivalent to the (bounded) derived category of filtered
\mathbx Z/p
vector spaces (see Chapter
7
), and this category is well understood and amenable to the techniques of stratification. In particular, it satisfies the hypothesis of the above theorem. This allows us to conclude that
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)
is stratified (see Theorem
7.11
), and hence deduce that minimality holds at the height two prime
m_{p}
.

A final comment to make is that, while we are only considering motives over
\overline{\mathbx Q}
in this paper, our results hold for other algebraically closed fields satisfying a certain vanishing condition, as in
[
Gal19
, Hypothesis 6.6]
. In particular, this vanishing condition holds for
\overline{\mathbx F}_{p}
, see
[
Gal19
, Remark 6.8]
. Moreover, our results are conjectured to hold for
\mathbx C
. More specifically, the conjecture is as follows: for any real closed field
\mathbx F
, there is a canonical surjective comparison map (see
[
Bal10
, §5]
)

\rho:Spc(DTM(\mathbx F,\mathbx Z)^{c})\to Spec(\mathbx Z)

and so the Balmer spectrum can be found by identifying the fibers of this map. For fields satisfying the vanishing hypothesis, the fiber of the generic point
(0)
is known to be a singleton (for example, the fiber of
(0)
is
m_{0}
for the case
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z)^{c}
), whereas the fiber is only conjecturally a singleton for
\mathbx F=\mathbx C
(see
[
BG22
, Remark 11.8]
). If the fiber is a singleton as conjectured, then our proofs hold in their entirely for
\mathbx C
as well.

2.
Balmer–Favi Support and Stratification

Throughout the paper
\mathcal{T}
will denote a rigidly-compactly generated tensor-triangulated category and we will write
\mathcal{T}^{c}
for the subcategory of compact-rigid objects. We assume that the reader has a general understanding of tt-geometry as in
[
Bal05
]
and the theory of stratification as in
[
BHS23b
]
. Let us briefly remind the reader of the relevant definitions and results we shall use for this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
be a prime. We say
\mathcal{P}
is
weakly visible
if
\{\mathcal{P}\}
is the intersection of a Thomason subset and the complement of a Thomason subset. We say
\mathcal{P}
is
visible
if its closure is Thomason. Note that a visible prime is weakly visible. If every prime
\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is weakly visible (respectively visible) we say
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is weakly noetherian (respectively noetherian).

Associated to each weakly visible prime
\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is a nonzero tensor idempotent object
g(\mathcal{P})
in
\mathcal{T}

[
BF11
,
BHS23b
]
. These objects are used to define a support theory on
\mathcal{T}
:

Definition 2.2

(Balmer–Favi)
.

Suppose that
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is weakly noetherian. The Balmer–Favi support of an object
t\in\mathcal{T}
is

Supp(t):=\{\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c}):t\otimes g(\mathcal{P})\neq 0\}.

Remark 2.3
.

Let us make a quick remark about these
g(\mathcal{P})
objects in a special case. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category, and suppose
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
consists of two, connected points

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{M}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}.}

That is, a closed point
\mathcal{M}
and a generic point
\mathcal{P}
. Consider the idempotent triangle

e_{\mathscr{M}}\to\mathbb{1}\to f_{\mathscr{M}}\to\Sigma e_{\mathscr{M}}

associated to the finite localization for
Y=\{\mathcal{M}\}
. We have that

(1)

g(\mathscr{M})=e_{\mathscr{M}}
, and

(2)

g(\mathcal{P})=f_{\mathscr{M}}
.

We will use this observation later in §
7
.

Remark 2.4
.

The Balmer-Favi support extends the universal Balmer support for compact objects. That is, for
x\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
we have
Supp(x)=supp(x)
; see
[
BHS23b
, Lemma 2.18]
.

Definition 2.5

(Local-to-Global Principle)
.

Suppose that
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is weakly noetherian. We say
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the
local-to-global principle
if

Locid(t)=Locid(t\otimes g(\mathcal{P}):\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c}))

for any
t\in\mathcal{T}
.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that if
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the local-to-global principle, then it also satisfies the so-called detection property:

Supp(t)=\varnothing\iff t=0.

Remark 2.7
.

While there is no general characterization for when the local-to-global principle holds, a sufficient condition is that
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is noetherian. This is established in
[
BHS23b
, Theorem 3.22]
, building off the work of Stevenson
[
Ste13
,
Ste17
]
.

Now we come to the main characterization of stratification established in
[
BHS23b
, Theorem 4.1]
:

Theorem 2.8

(Stratification)
.

Suppose
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is weakly noetherian. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

The local-to-global principle holds for
\mathcal{T}
and for each prime
\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
we have that
Locid(g(\mathcal{P}))
is a minimal localizing tensor ideal of
\mathcal{T}
.

(b)

For all
t\in\mathcal{T}
,
Locid(t)=Locid(g(\mathcal{P}):\mathcal{P}\in Supp(t))
.

(c)

The map

\displaystyle\{\text{Localizing ideals of }\mathcal{T}\}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{Supp}\{\text{Subsets of }Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\mapsto Su

\displaystyle pp(\mathcal{L}):=\bigcup_{t\in\mathcal{L}}Supp(t)

is a bijection.

Definition 2.9
.

We say that
\mathcal{T}
is
stratified
if any of the equivalent conditions (a)-(c) from above hold. We will say that
\mathcal{T}
has “
minimality at
\mathcal{P}
”, or satisfies the minimality property at
\mathcal{P}
, if
Locid(g(\mathcal{P}))
is a minimal localizing ideal. Importantly, the minimality property is a condition that can be checked locally:

Theorem 2.10
.

[
BHS23b
, Proposition 5.2]

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the local-to-global principle and has weakly noetherian spectrum. Then
\mathcal{T}
has minimality at
\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
if and only if the local category
\mathcal{T}/Locid(\mathcal{P})
has minimality at its unique closed point.

Remark 2.11
.

Thus, assuming the local-to-global principle holds, to establish
\mathcal{T}
is stratified it suffices to check if each local category
\mathcal{T}/Locid(\mathcal{P})
has minimality at its unique closed point.

Remark 2.12
.

The theory of stratification is amenable to various descent techniques (see, for example
[
BCH+23
,
BCHS23
]
). Let us close out this section with a slight modification of ‘quasi-finite descent’
[
BCHS23
, Theorem 17.16]
that we will use throughout the paper.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
be rigidly-compactly generated tt-categories. A coproduct preserving tt-functor
f^{*}:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{S}
is called a
geometric functor
. We denote the right adjoint by
f_{*}
(which exists by Brown–Neeman Representability, see for example
[
BDS16
]
).

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
f^{*}:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{S}
be a geometric functor and let
\varphi:=Spc(f^{*}):Spc(\mathcal{S}^{c})\to Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
denote the induced map on spectra. Assume that:

(1)

\mathcal{T}
is local and satisfies the detection property;

(2)

f_{*}(\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{S}})\in\mathcal{T}
is compact;

(3)

There exists a unique
\mathcal{P}\in Spc(\mathcal{S}^{c})
such that
\varphi(\mathcal{P})=\mathscr{M}
is the unique closed point in
Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
.

Then minimality at
\mathcal{P}
implies minimality at
\mathscr{M}
.

Proof.

Our first step will be to show that
f^{*}
is conservative on all objects supported on
\mathscr{M}
. Let
Supp(t)\subseteq\{\mathscr{M}
} and suppose
f^{*}(t)=0
. Then
0=f_{*}f^{*}(t)\simeq f_{*}(\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{S}})\otimes t
. Hence

\varnothing=Supp(f_{*}(\mathbb{1})\otimes t)=supp(f_{*}(\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{S}}))\cap Supp(t)

where the second equality holds from the half-tensor product formula (see
[
BHS23b
, Lemma 2.18]
). Since
\mathcal{S}\neq 0
we have that
\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{S}}=f^{*}(\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{T}})\neq 0
and so
f_{*}(\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{S}})\neq 0
since
f_{*}
is conservative on the image of
f^{*}
(see
[
BCHS23
, Remark 13.9]
). Now
supp(f_{*}(\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{S}}))
is closed since we are assuming
f_{*}(\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{S}})
is compact, and so it contains the unique closed point
\mathscr{M}
, which forces
Supp(t)=\varnothing
. Hence we conclude that
t=0
since
\mathcal{T}
satisfies the detection property. This establishes that
f^{*}
is conservative on on all objects supported on
\mathscr{M}
. Now suppose
0\neq t\in Locid(g({\mathscr{M}}))
and note that

0\neq f^{*}(t)\in Locid(f^{*}(g({\mathscr{M}})))=Locid(g(\mathcal{P}))

where
f^{*}(g({\mathscr{M}}))=g(\varphi^{-1}(\{\mathscr{M}\}))=g(\mathcal{P})
by
[
BS17
, Proposition 5.11]
and by the hypothesis on the pre-image of
\{\mathscr{M}\}
. Since we are assuming minimality at
\mathcal{P}
we have an equality

Locid(f^{*}(t))=Locid(f^{*}(g(\mathscr{M}))).

Hence we get
f_{*}f^{*}(g({\mathscr{M}}))\in f_{*}(Locid(f^{*}(t)))\subseteq Locid(t)
by
[
BCHS23
, 13.4]
. Finally we compute:

\displaystyle g({\mathscr{M}})

\displaystyle\in Locid(g({\mathscr{M}})\otimes f_{*}(\mathbb{1}))\text{ (by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{BHSStrat}{}{}, Lemma~3.7]})}

\displaystyle=Locid(f_{*}f^{*}(g({\mathscr{M}})))

\displaystyle\subseteq Locid(t)

which establishes minimality at the prime
\mathscr{M}
as desired.
∎

3.
Derived Categories of Motives

In this section we recall the computation of the Balmer spectra of certain motivic categories by Martin Gallauer. We will follow
[
CD19
,
CD16
]
for the general theory of motives, and will also refer heavily to
[
Gal19
]
and the references therein.

Definition 3.1
.

We fix a commutative ring
R
, and the field
\overline{\mathbx Q}
. The derived category of motives over
\overline{\mathbx Q}
is the tt-category, denoted
DM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)
, that is constructed out of the derived category of Nisnevich sheaves with transfers of
R
–modules on the category of smooth, finite type
\overline{\mathbx Q}
–schemes; see
[
CD19
, 11.1.1, 11.1.2]
for further details.

Remark 3.2
.

This category comes equipped with a functor

R(-):Sm/\overline{\mathbx Q}\to DM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)

that sends a smooth
\overline{\mathbx Q}
-scheme
X
to its underlying ‘motive’, which is denoted
R(X)
. Moreover, the construction of
DM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)
inverts the motive of the projective line, which we denote by
R(1)
. For any integer
n
, we then denote the ‘Tate-twist’ of weight
n
by
R(n):=R(1)^{\otimes n}
, and one gets that
R(i)\otimes R(j)=R(i+j)
for all
i,j\in\mathbx Z
.

Definition 3.3
.

The category of Tate motives, denoted
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)
, is defined as
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)=Loc(R(n):n\in\mathbx Z)
. It is a rigidly-compactly generated tensor-triangulated category; see for example
[
San22
, Example 5.17]
and the references therein.

Remark 3.4
.

Given a ring homomorphism
R\to R^{\prime}
there is an induced geometric functor
\gamma^{*}:DM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)\to DM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R^{\prime})

which restricts to a geometric functor on Tate motives
\gamma^{*}:DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)\to DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R^{\prime})
.
The right adjoint
\gamma_{*}
of this geometric functor is always conservative (see
[
CD16
, 5.4.2]
). Moreover, if
R^{\prime}\in D(R)^{c}
, then
\gamma_{*}
preserves compact objects
[
Gal19
, §3]
.

There is a similar, parallel story to tell about the so-called étale motives:

Definition 3.5
.

We again fix a commutative ring
R
, and the field
\overline{\mathbx Q}
. The derived category of
étale
motives over
\overline{\mathbx Q}
is the tt-category denoted
DM^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)
, that is constructed out of the derived category of
étale
sheaves with transfers of
R
–modules on the category of smooth , finite type
\overline{\mathbx Q}
schemes; see
[
CD16
]
and
[
Gal19
, §4]
for further details.

This category again comes equipped with a ‘
étale
’ motive functor

R^{\text{ét}}(-):Sm/\overline{\mathbx Q}\to DM^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)

and we again have invertible étale Tate objects
R^{\text{ét}}(n)
arising from the étale motive of the projective line.

Definition 3.6
.

The derived category of
étale
Tate motives is the tt-category
DTM^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)
=
Loc(R^{\text{ét}}(n):n\in\mathbx Z)
. When
R
is any localization or quotient of
\mathbx Z
, this category is also rigidly-compactly generated (see
[
Gal19
, §4]
).

Remark 3.7
.

Given a ring map
R\to R^{\prime}
, we again have a geometric functor
\gamma^{*}:D(T)M^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)\to D(T)M^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},R^{\prime})
with conservative right adjoint
\gamma_{*}
which preserves compacts whenever
R^{\prime}\in D(R)^{c}
.

Remark 3.8
.

Essentially since every Nisnevich sheaf is also an
étale
sheaf, there is a canonical ‘
étalification
’ tt-functor

\alpha_{\text{ét}}:DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)\to DTM^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},R)

that commutes with change of coefficients. Moreover, whenever
\mathbx Q\subseteq R
this functor is an equivalence of categories
[
CD19
, 16.1.2]
.

An important result by Gallauer provides another deep connection between étale Tate motives and Tate motives:

Theorem 3.9
.

[
Gal19
, Theorem C.4]

Let
p
be a prime number and let
\beta_{p}:\mathbx Z/p(0)\to\mathbx Z/p(1)
denote the Bott map. Consider the geometric functor

\gamma^{*}:DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z_{(p)})\to DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)

induced by the ring map
\mathbx Z_{(p)}\to\mathbx Z/p
and let

\gamma_{*}:DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)\to DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z_{(p)})

denote its right adjoint. Then the étalification functor

\alpha_{\text{ét}}:DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z_{(p)})\to DTM^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z_{(p)})

is a finite localization with kernel
Locid(\gamma_{*}(cone(\beta_{p})))
. In particular, we have an equivalence

DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z_{(p)})/Locid(\gamma_{*}(cone(\beta_{p})))\simeq DTM^{\text{ét}}(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z_{(p)})

Armed with this, we can now describe Gallauer’s computations of the Balmer spectra of Tate motives. Let us first state the spectrum for finite coefficients:

Proposition 3.10
.

[
Gal19
, Proposition 8.2]

Let
\beta_{p}:\mathbx Z/p(0)\to\mathbx Z/p(1)
denote the Bott map. Then
Spc(DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)^{c})
consists of two connected points:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Spc(DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)^{c})}=}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(0)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\langle cone(\beta_{p})\rangle}

The last result needed before the main computation by Gallauer is the following computation of the spectrum with rational coefficients in
[
Pet13

4.
Minimality at the Height Zero Prime

In this section, we will prove minimality holds at the the height zero prime
m_{0}
.

Remark 4.1
.

Recall that the height zero prime
m_{0}
is obtained from the algebraic localization going from integral to rational coefficients. That is

DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z)/Locid(m_{0})\simeq DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)

By Theorem
2.10
, to prove that minimality holds at the prime
m_{0}
it suffices to prove minimality holds at the unique prime in
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)
. Let us now recall the computation of
Spc(DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)^{c})
by Peter
[
Pet13
]
.

Remark 4.2
.

To orient the reader for why we are interested in the following definitions: our goal will be to eventually produce a triangulated equivalence between
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)
and the derived category of the heart of a t-structure. Such an equivalence can be given, provided the t-structure satisfies extra properties, which we now explain.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
\textbf{t}=(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
denote a t-structure on a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category
\mathcal{T}
and let us denote the heart by
\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
. The t-structure
t
is said to be:

(a)

Non Degenerate
if

\bigcap_{k\in\mathbx Z}\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{U}=0=\bigcap_{k\in\mathbx Z}\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{V}

(b)

Smashing
if the coaisle
\mathcal{V}
is closed under coproducts.

(c)

Compatible
if
\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}\otimes\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
and
\mathbb{1}\in\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
. In this case
\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
is itself a tensor-abelian category, whose tensor structure is exact in both variables. Moreover the unit of
\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
is just the same unit as in
\mathcal{T}
(see
[
Pet13
, Remark 3.7]
).

(d)

Strongly hereditary
if
hom_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\Sigma^{i}Y)=0
for
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
and
i\geq 2
. Note that if
t
is strongly hereditary then
\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
is a hereditary abelian category, that is
Ext^{i}(M,N)=0
for all
i\geq 2
and
M,N\in\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
, see
[
Lev93
, Proposition 1.6]
.

Remark 4.4
.

A t-structure
t
is smashing if and only if the cohomology functor
\mathcal{H}^{0}:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
preserves coproducts, see for example
[
AHMV17
, Lemma 3.3]
.

Lemma 4.5
.

If a t-structure
t
is strongly hereditary, then, for all
M,N\in\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
and for all
i\in\mathbx Z
we have
Ext^{i}(M,N)\simeq hom_{\mathcal{T}}(M,\Sigma^{i}N)
.

Proof.

Indeed, for
i=0
we have
Ext^{0}(M,N)=hom_{\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}}(M,N)=hom_{\mathcal{T}}(M,N)
since
\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
is a full subcategory. For
i=1
we have
Ext^{1}(M,N)=hom_{\mathcal{T}}(M,\Sigma N)
for any heart
\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
(see
[
BBD82
]
). For
i\geq 2
we have
Ext^{i}(M,N)=0=hom_{\mathcal{T}}(M,\Sigma^{i}N)
by assumption and since
\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
is a hereditary category. Finally for
i<0
we always have
Ext^{i}(M,N)=0
and
hom_{\mathcal{T}}(M,\Sigma^{i}N)=0
since every t-structure
t
has this vanishing condition.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

One has the following two inclusion maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(\mathcal{\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{D(\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\mathcal{T}}}

A very natural question to ask is if we can extend the inclusion of
\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}
into
\mathcal{T}
into a triangulated functor on
D(\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}})
(called the realization functor)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{D(\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\mathcal{T}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists real}

in such a way that
real(i_{D(\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}})})\simeq i_{\mathcal{T}}
? For the bounded derived category, an affirmative answer is reached provided there is a so-called ‘f-category’ living above
\mathcal{T}
(see
[
Bei06
, Appendix]
). Such a condition is not too strict in practice, as Modoi
[
Mod19
]
proved that every triangulated category which is the underlying category of a stable derivator admits such an f-category. In particular, as every stable model category gives rise to a stable derivator (see for example
[
Vir19
, Example 1.11]
), any triangulated category that arises as the homotopy category of a stable model category admits an f-category.

Remark 4.7
.

In
[
Vir19
]
, Virili is able to construct an unbounded realization functor for a t-structure with some assumptions about some higher structures living above
\mathcal{T}
. Let us briefly set the stage for the following theorem. Suppose that
\mathcal{T}
arises as the base of a strong, stable derivator
\mathbb{D}
, that is
\mathcal{T}=\mathbb{D}(e)
, and let
t
be a t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
. Then Virili is able to construct a morphism of derivators

real_{\textbf{t}}:Ch_{\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}}\to\mathbb{D}

where
Ch_{\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}}
is the derivator defined by
Ch_{\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}}(I)=Ch((\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}})^{I})
for any diagram category
I
. Moreover, this morphism of derivators takes quasi-isomorphisms to isomorphisms (
[
Vir19
, Theorem 6.7]
), so, assuming the derived category
D(\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}})
exists, the morphism factors through the derivator underlying the derived category to give a morphism of derivators

real_{\textbf{t}}:D_{\mathcal{T}^{\text{\Heart}}}\to\mathbb{D}

Moreover, assuming the t-structure satisfies certain properties we can say more about this morphism. We summarize all this below, rephrased in the language of triangulated categories.

Theorem 4.8
.

[
Vir19
, §6.3]

Suppose
\mathcal{T}
arises as the base of a strong and stable derivator
\mathbb{D}
(for example,
\mathcal{T}
can be the homotopy category of a stable model category), and let
t
be a t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
. Denote by

real^{b}:D^{b}(\mathcal{A})\to\mathcal{T}

the bounded realization functor (which exists by
[
Mod19
]
). Then we can lift the bounded realization functor to an unbounded one

Moreover, suppose that

(a)

t
is non-degenerate,

(b)

is fully faithful, and

(c)

Then the unbounded realization functor remains fully faithful and is coproduct preserving.

Remark 4.9
.

We will now introduce the t-structure that exists for

[

Definition 4.10

Define

It follows that

Remark 4.13
.

Let

. The cohomology functor associated to the t-structure

Definition 4.14
.

[
Lev93
, Definition 1.4]

Let

to be the full, additive category generated by

for

\mathcal{T}

X\in\mathcal{T}

.

Remark 4.15
.

Under the equivalence

we have that the pair
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}_{a},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}_{a})
on

.

Remark 4.16
.

[
Lev93
, §4]

To spell it out a little more, we have that:

\displaystyle X\in\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0}\iff gr_{a}(X)\simeq\coprod_{n\geq 0}\Sigma^{n}\mathbx Q(\frac{a}{2})^{m_{n}}\text{ for all }a\in\mathbx Z

\displaystyle X\in\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0}\iff gr_{a}(X)\simeq\coprod_{n\leq 0}\Sigma^{n}\mathbx Q(\frac{a}{2})^{m_{n}}\text{ for all }a\in\mathbx Z

Theorem 4.17
.

[
Lev93
, Theorem 4.2]
,
[
Pet13
, Lemma 3.3]

The pair
(\mathcal{T}^{\leq 0},\mathcal{T}^{\geq 0})
is a t-structure on
\mathcal{T}
. Moreover,

(a)

The t-structure is non-degenerate, compatible and strongly hereditary.

(b)

The heart
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
is generated as a full abelian category closed under extensions and coproducts by

.

(c)

admits a functorial filtration by sub-objects

Parts (a)-(c) are
[

and
[

, the description of the aisle and co-aisle in the preceding remark, and that
gr_{a}
commutes with coproducts (recall Remark

).
∎

Corollary 4.18
.

There is an equivalence of triangulated categories

real:D(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T})\simeq DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q).

Proof.

We can apply Theorem
4.8
to get an exact and coproduct preserving functor
real:D(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T})\to DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)
. To prove that

is fully faithful, since
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
generates
D^{b}(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T})
as a triangulated category, it suffices to prove that

\displaystyle Ext^{i}(M,N)\xrightarrow{real^{b}}

\displaystyle hom_{DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)}(real^{b}(M),real^{b}(N)[i])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip 1.42271pthom_{DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)}(M,N[i])

is a bijection for objects
M,N\in\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
. Since the t-structure is strongly-hereditary, this follows from Lemma

. Hence

is fully faithful, which implies

is fully faithful by Theorem

. Thus the essential image is a localizing subcategory of
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)
. This category contains all the Tate twists by Theorem

(b). Since the Tate twists generate

we get that the essential image is everything.
∎

This equivalence immediately gives us the following:

Corollary 4.19
.

Every object
t\in DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)
is isomorphic to
t\simeq\coprod_{i}\Sigma^{-i}H^{i}(t)

Proof.

This isomorphism holds in

because
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
is heriditary, see for example
[

]
. The result thus follows for

since

is a coproduct preserving equivalence, and is just the identity on the heart.
∎

We now turn to classifying the localizing ideals of
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)
. We will closely follow Peter’s approach of computing the thick tensor ideals of
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)^{c}
.

Remark 4.20
.

Let us fix some notation for the rest of this section. We have the additive functor

\displaystyle\phi:\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)\to\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle X\longrightarrow\coprod_{i}H^{i}(X)

and the inclusion functor

\iota:\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}\hookrightarrow DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q).

Note that the functor

is also a tensor-functor, because the t-structure is compatible; see
[

]
. Following the terminology of Peter we say

is a coherent tensor ideal of

if it is closed under extensions, kernels, cokernels, and tensoring by arbitrary elements of
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
. Let us denote by

to be the set of coherent tensor ideals of
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
closed under coproducts.

Proposition 4.21
.

The maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Localizing ideals of }DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Q)\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Coh^{\coprod}(\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi^{*}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}=\iota^{-1}(\mathcal{L})

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle=\phi^{-1}(\mathcal{M})

are bijective functions inverse to each other.

This is the unbounded analogue of
[

, Theorems 4.4, 4.11]
. We include a sketch of the argument here for the readers convenience. The first step is to prove
\phi^{*}
is well defined, so let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
be a localizing ideal of
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
. The same proof as in
[

, Theorem 4.4]
shows that

is closed under extensions and tensors. It is closed under coproducts because the cohomology functors preserve coproducts since the t-structure is smashing, and because
\mathcal{M}
is closed under coproducts by assumption. Now we observe that since

we have

, so we get that
\iota^{*}
is well defined on the image of
\phi^{*}
, and is left inverse to it. Thus, it remains to show
\phi^{*}
is surjective. To do so, we can apply
[

, Theorem 4.11]
after noting that we still have an isomorphism

for any

.
∎

Remark 4.22
.

We can now complete our last reduction step: we continue to denote

. For each

The functor

\displaystyle gr:=\coprod_{a\in\mathbx Z}gr_{a}[-a]:\mathcal{T}\to D(\mathbx Q)

\displaystyle X\mapsto\coprod_{a\in\mathbx Z}gr_{a}(X)[-a]

is a tensor-triangulated functor.

If we restrict the domain of this functor to just the heart, we obtain the following
[
Lev93
, Corollary 4.3]
:

Proposition 4.24
.

The functor
gr|_{\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}}:\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}\to D(\mathbx Q)
gives an equivalence of
\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
with a tensor subcategory of

.

With these two propositions in hand, we can run the exact argument as in

to conclude:

.

The only coherent tensor ideals closed under coproducts of

are the

\mathcal{A}\mathcal{T}
.

Theorem 4.26
.

The category

is stratified.

Proof.

Since

Again keeping in mind Remark

Corollary 4.27
.

Minimality holds at the height zero prime

.

5.

In this section we establish minimality for the height one prime

Remark 5.1
.

From Proposition

is equivalent to establishing minimality at the closed point in

Combining Theorems

, we get that

. To do so, we will consider the geometric functor

induced by the ring map

.

This category

Theorem 5.2

(Rigidity Theorem)
.

There is a tt-equivalence

Proof.

This is
[

, Theorem 4.5.2]
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

The category

is stratified.

Proof.

As the derived category of a field is, of course, stratified, we obtain the same for

. We can then apply
[

, Proposition 5.22]
to conclude that

is itself a tt-field, and thus is also stratified (see
[

, Theorem 18.4]
).
∎

Remark 5.4
.

On spectra the geometric functor (

6.
Brown–Adams Representability and Stratification

In this section, we return to Question
1.4
from the introduction in order to prove minimality holds at the height two prime
m_{p}
in Chapter
7
. To remind the reader, we suppose we are given two rigidly-compactly generated tt-categories
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}
, where
\mathcal{T}_{1}
is stratified and that we have a tt-equivalence between their compact parts
\mathcal{T}_{1}^{c}\simeq\mathcal{T}_{2}^{c}
. Our question is whether this then implies that
\mathcal{T}_{2}
is stratified as well. While we are not able to answer this question in full generality, we are able to achieve positive results assuming our category satisfies Brown–Adams Representability, which we now explain.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a rigidly-compactly generated tt-category. The
category of modules
on
\mathcal{T}
is the Grothendieck abelian category

\mathcal{A}:=Mod(\mathcal{T}^{c}):=Add((\mathcal{T}^{c})^{op},Ab)

of contravarient additive functors from
\mathcal{T}^{c}
to abelian groups. The subcategory of finitely presented modules
\mathcal{A}^{fp}:=mod(\mathcal{T}^{c})
coincides with the usual Freyd-envelope of
\mathcal{T}^{c}

[
Nee01
, Chapter 5]
. For relevant information about this category of modules, we will be primarily following the recent series of papers by Balmer, Krause and Stevenson
[
Bal20a
,
BKS20
,
Bal20b
,
BKS19
]
.

Remark 6.1
.

We have the restricted Yoneda functor

\displaystyle h:\hskip 2.84544pt

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle t\xrightarrow{}\hat{t}:=Hom(-,t)|_{\mathcal{T}^{c}}

which fits into the commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}^{c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}^{fp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

Note that restricted Yoneda is no longer an embedding in general, due to the potential existence of so-called phantom maps. Nevertheless,

is conservative, that is
\hat{t}=0\implies t=0
, because
\mathcal{T}
is compactly generated.

Definition 6.2
.

For

we write

(1)

to be the smallest Serre subcategory containing
S
closed under coproducts and suspension.

(2)

the smallest Serre subcategory containing

closed under coproducts and tensor products (and hence is automatically closed under suspension, see
[

, Remark 2.2]

We summarize the facts we will need about
\mathcal{A}
below. This can be found in
[

, §§2-3]

(1)

\mathcal{A}
inherits a suspension

(2)

\mathcal{A}

is symmetric monoidal, and moreover
\hat{t}\otimes-
is exact and colimit preserving for any

.

Definition 6.3
.

Sitting inside of

is the full subcategory of

, consisting of those contravariant functors that send exact triangles to long exact sequences. Clearly,

. Moreover, in certain important historic examples there has been a much stronger relationship between the essential image of restricted Yoneda and the subcategory of homological functors:

Theorem 6.4
.

[

]

Let
\mathcal{T}=\text{SH}
denote the stable homotopy category, and
\mathcal{T}^{c}
the category of finite spectra. Then any homological functor

\mathcal{H}:(\mathcal{T}^{c})^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{A}b

for some

. Moreover, any natural transformation

is induced by some (non-unique) map

Remark 6.5
.

Neeman, Keller and Christensen have investigated the extent to which this result generalizes to other rigidly-compactly generated tt-categories in
[

,
CKN01
]
. Following their terminology, we say that

(BRO)

If every
\mathcal{H}\in Hol(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is isomorphic to

for some
t\in\mathcal{T}
.

(BRM)

If every natural transformation
h(t)\to h(s)

t\to s
.

Remark 6.6
.

It follows from results of Beligiannis (see
[

Definition 6.7
.

We say that

if condition (BRM) holds.

Remark 6.8
.

Neeman shows
[

is equivalent to a countable category (that is, a category with only countably many objects and morphisms between them).

Remark 6.9

Suppose

is a compactly generated tt-category that satisfies Brown–Adams representability. Then it follows (see
[
Nee97
, Remark 3.2]
) that any isomorphism

in

is induced by an isomorphism

in
\mathcal{T}
.

Let us now connect this back to the theory of stratification:

[

Remark 6.15

Let us assume that

. Moreover, we have the following two equivalences:

Theorem 7.3
.

[
SS16
, 3.16]

There is an equivalence of tt-categories between
D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)
and
D(\mathbx Z^{op}Mod(\mathbx Z/p))
.

Proposition 7.4
.

[
Gal19
, Propositions 7.7, 7.9]

There is an equivalence of triangulated categories

pos:D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)^{c}\xrightarrow{\sim}DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)^{c}

that induces a bijection of thick tensor ideals.

Remark 7.5
.

The proof of this last proposition uses the existence of a strongly hereditary t-structure on
D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)^{c}

[
Gal18
, Lemma 7.6]
. Moreover, to obtain the bijection of thick tensor ideals between the two categories, Gallauer only needed to show the functor is tensor on a certain subcategory of
D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)^{c}
. In particular, the functor is tensor on the heart of the t-structure. Since the t-structure is strongly hereditary, and generates
D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)^{c}
, every object is a finite sum of shifts of objects in the heart, see for example
[
Hub16
]
. Using this, one can show that the functor is really tensor everywhere.

Corollary 7.6
.

The functor
pos:D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)^{c}\to DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)^{c}
is a tensor triangulated equivalence.

Remark 7.7
.

Let
M\in\mathbx Z^{op}Mod(\mathbx Z/p)
. Associated to
M
is the graded
\mathbx Z/p[\beta]
-module
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbx Z}M_{n}
where
\beta
has degree
-1
and acts by
\beta:M\to M(1)
. Conversely, given a graded
\mathbx Z/p[\beta]
-module
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbx Z}M_{n}
we get a presheaf sending
n
to
M_{n}
with transition maps given by
\beta:M_{n}\to M_{n-1}
. This provides a tensor-equivalence between the Grothendieck categories
\mathbx Z^{op}Mod(\mathbx Z/p)
and
Mod_{gr}(\mathbx Z/p[\beta])
, see for example
[
DS13
, Lemma. 2.2]
and the references therein.

Remark 7.8
.

Hence, by combining Theorem
7.3
and Remark
7.7
, we obtain a tt-equivalence between
D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)
and
D(Mod_{gr}(\mathbx Z/p[\beta]))
. The tt-geometry of graded
\mathbx Z/p[\beta]
-modules has been studied by Stevenson and Dell’Ambrogio, and more recently by Barthel, Heard and Sanders. Let us summarize what is needed for us below:

Theorem 7.9
.

[
DS13
,
BHS23a
]

The category
\mathcal{T}:=D(Mod_{gr}(\mathbx Z/p[\beta]))
is stratified. As a consequence,
D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)
is stratified as well. Moreover, we have that:

(1)

\mathcal{T}^{c}
is countable, and hence satisfies Brown–Adams representability.

(2)

The natural map
\varphi:Spc^{h}(\mathcal{T}^{c})\xrightarrow{\sim}Spc(\mathcal{T}^{c})
is a homeomorphism. Moreover, the ‘naive’ homological support

Supp^{h}_{\text{naive}}(t):=\{\beta\in Spc^{h}(\mathcal{T}^{c}):E_{\beta}\otimes t\neq 0\}

corresponds with the actual homological support.

(3)

Letting
p
denote the unique prime corresponding to
\beta\in Spc^{h}(\mathcal{T}^{c})
, the homological primes
E_{\beta}\simeq k(p)
are field objects in
\mathcal{T}
.

(4)

For all nonzero
t\in\mathcal{T}
there exists a non-zero map from
E_{\beta}\otimes x\to t
for some
\beta\in Spc^{h}(\mathcal{T}^{c})
and
x\in\mathcal{T}^{c}.

Proof.

That
\mathcal{T}
is stratified is
[
DS13
, Theorem 5.7]
. Part
(2)
is a general consequence of stratification, see
[
BHS23a
, Theorem 4.7]
and the remark after it. Part
(3)
is
[
BHS23a
, Example 5.3]
and
[
DS13
, Lemma 4.2]
. Finally, part
(4)
is
[

, Proposition 4.7]
.
∎

Proposition 7.10
.

Continue to let
\mathcal{T}:=D(Mod_{gr}(\mathbx Z/p[\beta]))
and denote by
\mathcal{A}=Add((\mathcal{T}^{c})^{op},\mathcal{A}b)
the category of modules on
\mathcal{T}
as in §
6
. Then for all non-zero homological functors
0\neq\hat{t}\in\mathcal{A}
there exists a non-zero map
\hat{E}_{\beta}\otimes\hat{x}\to\hat{t}
for some
\beta\in Spc^{h}(\mathcal{T}^{c})
and
x\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
.

Proof.

As the corresponding statement holds for
\mathcal{T}
by Theorem
7.9
, we really have to just show there are no phantom maps out of
E_{\beta}\otimes x
for any
\beta\in Spc^{h}(\mathcal{T}^{c})
and
x\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
. To do so, first note that the graded ring
R:=\mathbx Z/p[\beta]
is a graded-local, regular, noetherian ring (see
[
BH93
, §1.5]
for example). Letting
\mathfrak{m}
denote the unique maximal ideal in
R
, we have
E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}\simeq k(\mathfrak{m})\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
because
R
is graded-regular. Now let
0\neq t\in\mathcal{T}
be arbitrary. If there is a nonzero map
f:E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}\otimes x\to t
for some
x\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
, then the map
\hat{f}:\hat{E}_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}\otimes\hat{x}\to\hat{t}
remains nonzero since there can be no phantom maps out of compact objects. In this case the proof is complete.

Otherwise, assume there are no-nonzero maps
E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}\otimes x\to t
for any compact object
x\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
. This implies that
[E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}},t]=0
, which, since
E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
, tells us that
[E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}},\mathbb{1}]\otimes t=0
. Since
E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}
is a direct summand of
E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}\otimes[E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}},\mathbb{1}]\otimes E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}
this forces
E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}\otimes t=0
and so
E_{\beta_{\mathscr{M}}}\notin Supp^{h}(t)
. Again using that
\mathcal{T}
is stratified, we conclude that
\mathscr{M}\notin Supp(t)
. Now recall from Remark
2.3
that
g(\mathscr{M})=e_{\mathscr{M}}
and
g(\mathcal{P})=f_{\mathscr{M}}
, and so we get
e_{\mathscr{M}}\otimes t=0
, and
t\simeq t\otimes f_{\mathscr{M}}
. Let
f^{*}:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}
be the finite localization associated to
\mathcal{P}
. Recall that
f_{*}
is fully faithful, and that
f_{*}(\mathbb{1})=f_{\mathscr{M}}
. Then using the projection formula we get
f_{*}f^{*}(t)=t\otimes f_{\mathscr{M}}\simeq t
. Moreover, Gallauer showed that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}\simeq D(\mathbx Z/p)
as tensor-triangulated categories (see
[
Gal18
, Lemma 3.7, Lemma 5.3]
). Indeed, when thinking of
\mathcal{T}
as the category of filtered
\mathbx Z/p
-vector spaces, the functor
f^{*}:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}
corresponds to forgetting the filtration. Now, by assumption we know that there is a nonzero map
\alpha:E_{\beta_{\mathcal{P}}}\otimes x\to t
for some compact
x
. Our goal is to show that
\hat{\alpha}
remains nonzero in
\mathcal{A}
. To do so, consider the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{P}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hat{f}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

Since
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{P}}
is the derived category of a field, it is phantomless, and so to show that
\hat{\alpha}
is nonzero in
\mathcal{A}
, by the commutativity of the above diagram, it suffices to show that
f^{*}(\alpha)\neq 0
. To do so, let
\eta
denote the counit of the adjunction
f^{*}\dashv f_{*}
. Recall that since
f_{*}
is fully faithful
\eta
is an isomorphism. Finally, consider the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{E_{\beta_{\mathcal{P}}}\otimes x}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{*}f^{*}(E_{\beta_{\mathcal{P}}}\otimes x)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f_{*}f^{*}(t)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{*}f^{*}(\alpha)}

This implies that
f_{*}f^{*}(\alpha)\neq 0
and so
f^{*}(\alpha)\neq 0
as desired.
∎

This allows us to use the results from §
6
to immediately conclude:

Theorem 7.11
.

The category
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)
is stratified.

Proof.

We have a tt-equivalence
D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)^{c}\simeq DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)^{c}
where
D_{fil}(\mathbx Z/p)
is stratified, the subcategory of compact objects is countable, and whose homological primes

are field objects by Theorem

. The last proposition gives us the final assumption needed to apply Theorem
6.25
to conclude that
DTM(\overline{\mathbx Q},\mathbx Z/p)

∎

As a result, keeping in mind Remark

Minimality holds at the height 2 prime
